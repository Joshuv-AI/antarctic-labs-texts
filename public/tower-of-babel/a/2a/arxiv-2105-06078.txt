General Tail Bounds for Random Tensors Summation: Majorization Approach
Shih Yu Chang, Yimin Wei

Abstract
In recent years, tensors have been applied to different applications in science and engineering fields. In order to establish theory about tail bounds of the tensors summation behavior, this work extends previous work by considering the tensors summation tail behavior of the top k k -largest singular values of a function of the tensors summation, instead of the largest/smallest singular value of the tensors summation directly (identity function) explored in [ 5 ] . Majorization and antisymmetric tensor product tools are main techniques utilized to establish inequalities for unitarily norms of multivariate tensors. The Laplace transform method is integrated with these inequalities for unitarily norms of multivariate tensors to give us tail bounds estimation for Ky Fan k k -norm for a function of the tensors summation. By restricting different random tensor conditions, we obtain generalized tensor Chernoff and Bernstein inequalities.

(37)

If
f
is a single variable function, the notation

represents a tensor function with respect to the tensor
\mathcal{C}

Theorem 14
.

Let
\Omega,\nu,\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}_{\tau}
be defined as the beginning part of Section
3.1
, and
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be a non-decreasing convex function, we have following two equivalent statements:

\displaystyle\vec{\lambda}(\mathcal{C})\prec_{w}\int_{\Omega^{r}}\vec{\lambda}(\mathcal{D}_{\tau})d\nu^{r}(\tau)\Longleftrightarrow\left\|f(\mathcal{C})\right\|_{\rho}\leq\int_{\Omega}\left\|f(\mathcal{D}_{\tau})\right\|_{\rho}d\nu(\tau),

(38)

where
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\rho}
is the unitarily invariant norm defined in Eq. (
23
).

Proof:

We assume that the left statement of Eq. (
38
) is true and the function
f
is a non-decreasing convex function. From Lemma
12
, we have

\displaystyle\vec{\lambda}(f(\mathcal{C}))=f(\vec{\lambda}(\mathcal{C}))\prec_{w}f\left(\int_{\Omega^{r}}\vec{\lambda}(\mathcal{D}_{\tau})d\nu^{r}(\tau)\right).

(39)

From the convexity of

\displaystyle f\left(\int_{\Omega^{r}}\vec{\lambda}(\mathcal{D}_{\tau})d\nu^{r}(\tau)\right)\leq\int_{\Omega^{r}}f(\vec{\lambda}(\mathcal{D}_{\tau}))d\nu^{r}(\tau)=\int_{\Omega^{r}}\vec{\lambda}(f(\mathcal{D}_{\tau}))d\nu^{r}(\tau).

(40)

Then, we obtain

[
14
]

\displaystyle\left\|f(\mathcal{C})\right\|_{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\rho\left(\int_{\Omega^{r}}\vec{\lambda}(f(\mathcal{D}_{\tau}))d\nu^{r}(\tau)\right)

(41)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\rho(\vec{\lambda}(f(\mathcal{D}_{\tau})))d\nu(\tau)=\int_{\Omega}\left\|f(\mathcal{D}_{\tau})\right\|_{\rho}d\nu(\tau).

Therefore, the right statement of Eq. (

On the other hand, if the right statement of Eq. (

\displaystyle\exp\int_{\Omega^{r}}\log\vec{\lambda}(\mathcal{D}_{\tau})d\nu^{r}(\tau);

\displaystyle\left\|f(\mathcal{C})\right\|_{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\int_{\Omega}\log\left\|f(\mathcal{D}_{\tau})\right\|_{\rho}d\nu(\tau);

(49)

\displaystyle\left\|g(\mathcal{C})\right\|_{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left\|g(\mathcal{D}_{\tau})\right\|_{\rho}d\nu(\tau).

(50)

) and Eq. (

\Longrightarrow
Eq. (

\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}_{\tau}
are positive Hermitian tensors, and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}\log\left(\frac{\left\|\mathcal{C}^{-p}\right\|_{1}}{r}\right)\leq\frac{1}{p}\log\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\left\|\mathcal{D}_{\tau}^{-p}\right\|_{1}}{r}d\nu(\tau)\right).

(96)

Then, we will get

\displaystyle\frac{-\log\det\nolimits_{H}(\mathcal{C})}{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{p\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{p}\log\left(\frac{\left\|\mathcal{C}^{-p}\right\|_{1}}{r}\right)

(97)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{p\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{p}\log\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\left\|\mathcal{D}_{\tau}^{-p}\right\|_{1}}{r}d\nu(\tau)\right)=_{1}\frac{-\int_{\Omega}\log\det\nolimits_{H}(\mathcal{D}_{\tau})d\nu(\tau)}{r},

which will prove the identity for Eq. (
95
) when
k=r
. The equality in
=_{1}
will be proved by the following Lemma

\hfill\Box

Lemma 18
.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{\tau}
be nonnegative Hermitian tensors with
\int_{\Omega}\left\|\mathcal{D}_{\tau}^{-p}\right\|_{\rho}d\nu(\tau)<\infty
for any
p>0
, then we have

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{p\rightarrow 0}\left(\frac{1}{p}\log\int_{\Omega}\frac{\left\|\mathcal{D}_{\tau}^{-p}\right\|_{1}}{r}d\nu(\tau)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{r}\int_{\Omega}\log\det\nolimits_{H}(\mathcal{D}_{\tau})d\nu(\tau)

(98)

Proof:

Because
\int_{\Omega}\left\|\mathcal{D}_{\tau}^{-p}\right\|_{\rho}d\nu(\tau)<\infty
, we have that
\mathcal{D}_{\tau}
are positive Hermitian tensors for
\tau
almost everywhere in
\Omega
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{p\rightarrow 0}\left(\frac{1}{p}\log\int_{\Omega}\frac{\left\|\mathcal{D}_{\tau}^{-p}\right\|_{1}}{r}d\nu(\tau)\right)

\displaystyle=_{1}

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{p\rightarrow 0}\frac{\int_{\Omega}\frac{-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}\log\lambda_{i}(\mathcal{D}_{\tau})}{r}d\nu(\tau)}{\int_{\Omega}\frac{\left\|\mathcal{D}_{\tau}^{-p}\right\|_{1}}{r}d\nu(\tau)}

(99)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-1}{r}\int_{\Omega}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}\log\lambda_{i}(\mathcal{D}_{\tau})d\nu(\tau)

\displaystyle=_{2}

\displaystyle\frac{-1}{r}\int_{\Omega}\log\det\nolimits_{H}(\mathcal{D}_{\tau})d\nu(\tau),

where
=_{1}
is from L’Hopital’s rule, and
=_{2}
is obtained from
\det\nolimits_{H}
definition.

3.3
Multivaraite Tensor Norm Inequalities

In this section, we will apply derived majorization inequalities for tensors to multivariate tensor norm inequalities which will be used to bound random tensor concentration inequalities in later sections. We will begin to present a Lie-Trotter product formula for tensors.

Lemma 19
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
(\mathcal{L}_{k})_{k=1}^{m}
be a finite sequence of bounded tensors with dimensions
\mathcal{L}_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}
, then we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{m}\exp(\frac{\mathcal{L}_{k}}{n})\right)^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}\mathcal{L}_{k}\right)

(100)

Proof:

We will prove the case for
m=2
, and the general value of
m
can be obtained by mathematical induction.
Let
\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2}
be bounded tensors act on some Hilbert space. Define
\mathcal{C}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\exp((\mathcal{L}_{1}+\mathcal{L}_{2})/n)
, and
\mathcal{D}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\exp(\mathcal{L}_{1}/n)\star_{M}\exp(\mathcal{L}_{2}/n)
. Note we have following estimates for the norm of tensors
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}
:

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{C}\right\|,\left\|\mathcal{D}\right\|\leq\exp\left(\frac{\left\|\mathcal{L}_{1}\right\|+\left\|\mathcal{L}_{2}\right\|}{n}\right)=\left[\exp\left(\left\|\mathcal{L}_{1}\right\|+\left\|\mathcal{L}_{2}\right\|\right)\right]^{1/n}.

(101)

From the Cauchy-Product formula, the tensor
\mathcal{D}
can be expressed as:

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp(\mathcal{L}_{1}/n)\star_{M}\exp(\mathcal{L}_{2}/n)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\mathcal{L}_{1}/n)^{i}}{i!}\star_{M}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\mathcal{L}_{2}/n)^{j}}{j!}

(102)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}n^{-l}\sum_{i=0}^{l}\frac{\mathcal{L}_{1}^{i}}{i!}\star_{M}\frac{\mathcal{L}_{2}^{l-i}}{(l-i)!},

then we can bound the norm of
\mathcal{C}-\mathcal{D}
as

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{C}-\mathcal{D}\right\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left([\mathcal{L}_{1}+\mathcal{L}_{2}]/n\right)^{i}}{i!}-\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}n^{-l}\sum_{i=0}^{l}\frac{\mathcal{L}_{1}^{i}}{i!}\star_{M}\frac{\mathcal{L}_{2}^{l-i}}{(l-i)!}\right\|

(103)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}k^{-i}\frac{\left([\mathcal{L}_{1}+\mathcal{L}_{2}]\right)^{i}}{i!}-\sum_{m=l}^{\infty}n^{-l}\sum_{i=0}^{l}\frac{\mathcal{L}_{1}^{i}}{i!}\star_{M}\frac{\mathcal{L}_{2}^{l-i}}{(l-i)!}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k^{2}}\left[\exp(\left\|\mathcal{L}_{1}\right\|+\left\|\mathcal{L}_{2}\right\|)+\sum_{l=2}^{\infty}n^{-l}\sum_{i=0}^{l}\frac{\left\|\mathcal{L}_{1}\right\|^{i}}{i!}\cdot\frac{\left\|\mathcal{L}_{2}\right\|^{l-i}}{(l-i)!}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{2}}\left[\exp\left(\left\|\mathcal{L}_{1}\right\|+\left\|\mathcal{L}_{2}\right\|\right)+\sum_{l=2}^{\infty}n^{-l}\frac{(\left\|\mathcal{L}_{1}\right\|+\left\|\mathcal{L}_{2}\right\|)^{l}}{l!}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2\exp\left(\left\|\mathcal{L}_{1}\right\|+\left\|\mathcal{L}_{2}\right\|\right)}{n^{2}}.

For the difference between the higher power of
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
, we can bound them as

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{C}^{n}-\mathcal{D}^{n}\right\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\mathcal{C}^{m}(\mathcal{C}-\mathcal{D})\mathcal{C}^{n-l-1}\right\|

(104)

\displaystyle\leq_{1}

\displaystyle\exp(\left\|\mathcal{L}_{1}\right\|+\left\|\mathcal{L}_{2}\right\|)\cdot n\cdot\left\|\mathcal{L}_{1}-\mathcal{L}_{2}\right\|,

where the inequality
\leq_{1}
uses the following fact

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{C}\right\|^{l}\left\|\mathcal{D}\right\|^{n-l-1}\leq\exp\left(\left\|\mathcal{L}_{1}\right\|+\left\|\mathcal{L}_{2}\right\|\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\leq\exp\left(\left\|\mathcal{L}_{1}\right\|+\left\|\mathcal{L}_{2}\right\|\right),

(105)

based on Eq. (
101
). By combining with Eq. (
103
), we have the following bound

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{C}^{n}-\mathcal{D}^{n}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2\exp\left(2\left\|\mathcal{L}_{1}\right\|+2\left\|\mathcal{L}_{2}\right\|\right)}{n}.

(106)

Then this lemma is proved when
n
goes to infity.
\hfill\Box

Below, new multivariate norm inequalities for tensors are provided according to previous majorization theorems.

Theorem 20
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{N}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{N}}
be positive Hermitian tensors for
1\leq i\leq n
with Hermitian rank
r
,
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\rho}

\rho
. For any continous function

such that

is convex on
\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\left\|f\left(\exp\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\log\mathcal{C}_{i}\right)\right)\right\|_{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\log\left\|f\left(\left|\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{C}_{i}^{1+\iota t}\right|\right)\right\|_{\rho}\beta_{0}(t)dt,

(107)

where
\beta_{0}(t)=\frac{\pi}{2(\cosh(\pi t)+1)}
.

For any continous function
g(0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
such that
x\rightarrow g(e^{x})
is convex on
\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|g\left(\exp\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\log\mathcal{C}_{i}\right)\right)\right\|_{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left\|g\left(\left|\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{C}_{i}^{1+\iota t}\right|\right)\right\|_{\rho}\beta_{0}(t)dt.

(108)

Proof:

From Hirschman interpolation theorem
[
36
]
and
\theta\in[0,1]
, we have

\displaystyle\log\left|h(\theta)\right|\leq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\log\left|h(\iota t)\right|^{1-\theta}\beta_{1-\theta}(t)dt+\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\log\left|h(1+\iota t)\right|^{\theta}\beta_{\theta}(t)dt,

(109)

where
h(z)
be uniformly bounded on
S\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\{z\in\mathbb{C}:0\leq\Re(z)\leq 1\}
and holomorphic on
S
. The term

is defined as :

\displaystyle\beta_{\theta}(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\frac{\sin(\pi\theta)}{2\theta(\cos(\pi t)+\cos(\pi\theta))}.

(110)

Let

\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{N}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{N}}

. From Hirschman interpolation theorem, we then have following interpolation theorem for tensor-valued function:

\displaystyle\log\left\|H(\theta)\right\|\leq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\log\left\|H(\iota t)\right\|^{1-\theta}\beta_{1-\theta}(t)dt+\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\log\left\|H(1+\iota t)\right\|^{\theta}\beta_{\theta}(t)dt.

(111)

\displaystyle\log\left\|\left|\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{C}_{i}^{\theta}\right|^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\right\|\leq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\log\left\|\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{C}_{i}^{1+\iota t}\right\|\beta_{\theta}(t)dt.

\displaystyle\left|\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left(\wedge^{k}\mathcal{C}_{i}\right)^{\theta}\right|^{\frac{1}{\theta}}=\wedge^{k}\left|\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{C}^{\theta}_{i}\right|^{\frac{1}{\theta}},

If Eq. (

\displaystyle\left\|f\left(\left|\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{C}_{i}^{\theta}\right|^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\right)\right\|_{\rho}

Ky Fan

We begin by introducing following two lemmas about Ky Fan

-norm inequalities for the product of tensors (Lemma

Lemma 21
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{N}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{N}}
with Hermitian rank
r
and let
p_{i}
be positive real numbers satisfying
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{p_{i}}=1
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\left|\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{C}_{i}\right|^{s}\right\|_{(k)}\leq\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m}\left(\left\|\left|\mathcal{C}_{i}\right|^{sp_{i}}\right\|_{(k)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{i}}}\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\left\|\left|\mathcal{C}_{i}\right|^{sp_{i}}\right\|_{(k)}}{p_{i}}

(120)

where
s\geq 1
and
k\in\{1,2,\cdots,r\}
.

Proof:

Since we have

\displaystyle\left\|\left|\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{C}_{i}\right|^{s}\right\|_{(k)}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}\left(\left|\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{C}_{i}\right|^{s}\right)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\lambda^{s}_{j}\left(\left|\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{C}_{i}\right|\right)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\sigma^{s}_{j}\left(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{C}_{i}\right),

(121)

where we have orders for eigenvalues as
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots
, and singular values as
\sigma_{1}\geq\sigma_{2}\geq\cdots
.

From Lemma
11
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{C}_{i}\right)^{\wedge k}\right\|=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{k}\sigma_{j}\left(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{C}_{i}\right).

(122)

From the fact that the norm is submultiplicative and majorization theory, we will have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\sigma^{s}_{j}\left(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{C}_{i}\right)\leq\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\left(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m}\sigma^{s}_{j}(\mathcal{C}_{i})\right).

(123)

Then, we can apply Hölder’s inequality to Eq. (
123
) and obtain

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\left(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m}\sigma^{s}_{j}(\mathcal{C}_{i})\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\sigma^{sp_{i}}_{j}(\mathcal{C}_{i})\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{i}}}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\lambda^{sp_{i}}_{j}(\left|\mathcal{C}_{i}\right|)\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{i}}}

(124)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}(\left|\mathcal{C}_{i}\right|^{sp_{i}})\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{i}}}=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m}\left(\left\|\left|\mathcal{C}_{i}\right|^{sp_{i}}\right\|_{(k)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{i}}}

The second inequality in Eq. (
120
) is obtained by applying Young’s inequality to numbers
\left\|\left|\mathcal{C}_{i}\right|^{sp_{i}}\right\|_{(k)}
for
1\leq i\leq m
. This completes the proof.
\hfill\Box

Lemma 22
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{N}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{N}}
with Hermitian rank
r
, then we have

\displaystyle\left\|\left|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{C}_{i}\right|^{s}\right\|_{(k)}\leq m^{s-1}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\left\|\left|\mathcal{C}_{i}\right|^{s}\right\|_{(k)}

(125)

where
s\geq 1
and
k\in\{1,2,\cdots,r\}
.

Proof:

Since we have

\displaystyle\left\|\left|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{C}_{i}\right|^{s}\right\|_{(k)}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}\left(\left|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{C}_{i}\right|^{s}\right)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\lambda^{s}_{j}\left(\left|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{C}_{i}\right|\right)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\sigma^{s}_{j}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{C}_{i}\right).

(126)

where we have orders for eigenvalues as
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots
, and singular values as
\sigma_{1}\geq\sigma_{2}\geq\cdots
.

From Theorem G.1.d. in
[
24
]
and Theorem 3.2 in
[
21
]
, we have Fan singular value majorization inequaliies:

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\sigma_{j}(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{C}_{i})\leq\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\sigma_{j}(\mathcal{C}_{i})\right),

(127)

where
k\in\{1,2,\cdots,s\}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\sigma^{s}_{j}(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{C}_{i})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\sigma_{j}(\mathcal{C}_{i})\right)^{s}\leq m^{s-1}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\sigma^{s}_{j}(\mathcal{C}_{i})\right)

(128)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m^{s-1}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\sigma^{s}_{j}(\left|\mathcal{C}_{i}\right|)\right)=m^{s-1}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\sigma_{j}(\left|\mathcal{C}_{i}\right|^{s})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m^{s-1}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\left\|\left|\mathcal{C}_{i}\right|^{s}\right\|_{(k)}

\hfill\Box

k
-norm probability bound for a function of tensors summation.

Theorem 23
.

Consider a sequence
\{\mathcal{X}_{j}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{N}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{N}}\}

(129)

where

. Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\left\|g\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\mathcal{X}_{j}\right)\right\|_{(k)}\geq\theta\right)\leq~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

\displaystyle(n+1)^{s-1}\inf\limits_{t,p_{j}}e^{-\theta t}\left(ka_{0}^{s}+\sum\limits_{l=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\frac{a^{ls}_{l}\mathbb{E}\left\|\exp\left(p_{j}lst\mathcal{X}_{j}\right)\right\|_{(k)}}{p_{j}}\right).

\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{p_{j}}=1

p_{j}>0

Let
t>0
be a parameter to be chosen later. Then

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\left\|g\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\mathcal{X}_{j}\right)\right\|_{(k)}\geq\theta\right)=\mathrm{Pr}\left(\left\|\exp\left(tg\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\mathcal{X}_{j}\right)\right)\right\|_{(k)}\geq\exp\left(\theta t\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq_{1}\exp\left(-\theta t\right)\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\exp\left(tg\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\mathcal{X}_{j}\right)\right)\right\|_{(k)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq_{2}\exp\left(-\theta t\right)\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|g\left(\exp\left(t\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\mathcal{X}_{j}\right)\right)\right\|_{(k)}\right)

(131)

where

uses Markov’s inequality,
\leq_{2}
requires condition provided by Eq. (
129
).

We can further bound the expectation term in Eq. (

) as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|g\left(\exp\left(t\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\mathcal{X}_{j}\right)\right)\right\|_{(k)}\right)\leq_{3}\mathbb{E}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left\|g\left(\left|\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m}e^{(1+\iota\tau)t\mathcal{X}_{j}}\right|\right)\right\|_{(k)}\beta_{0}(\tau)d\tau

\displaystyle\leq_{4}(n+1)^{s-1}\left(ka_{0}^{s}+\sum\limits_{l=1}^{n}a^{ls}_{l}\mathbb{E}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left\|\left|\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m}e^{(1+\iota\tau)t\mathcal{X}_{j}}\right|^{ls}\right\|_{(k)}\beta_{0}(\tau)d\tau\right),~~~

(132)

where
\leq_{3}
from Eq. (
108
) in Theorem
20
,
\leq_{4}
is obtained from function
g
definition and Lemma
22
. Again, the expectation term in Eq. (
4.1
) can be further bounded by Lemma
21
as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left\|\left|\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m}e^{(1+\iota\tau)t\mathcal{X}_{j}}\right|^{ls}\right\|_{(k)}\beta_{0}(\tau)d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\left\|\left|e^{t\mathcal{X}_{j}}\right|^{p_{j}ls}\right\|_{(k)}}{p_{j}}\beta_{0}(\tau)d\tau

(133)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\mathbb{E}\left\|e^{p_{j}lst\mathcal{X}_{j}}\right\|_{(k)}}{p_{j}}.

Note that the final equality is obtained due to that the integrand is indepedent of the variable
\tau
and

.

Finally, this theorem is established from Eqs. (

), (
4.1
), and (

4.2
Generalized Tensor Chernoff Bound

Folliowing lemma is about Ky Fan
k
-norm bound for the exponential of a random tensor with the constraint of the maximum singular value.

Lemma 24
.

Given a nonnegative Hermitian random tensor
\mathcal{X}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{N}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{N}}
with

6
Conclusions

In this work, we generalize previous work by considering the tail bound behavior of the top
k
-largest singular values of a function of random tensors summation. In previous work, we only considered the tail bound behavior of the largest singular value of tensors summation directly (identity function). Majorization and antisymmetric tensor products are our main gadgets used to derive bounds for unitarily norms of multivariate tensors. Then, we apply Laplace transform method to these bounds of unitarily norms of multivariate tensors to obtain Ky Fan
k
-norm concentration inequalities for a function of tensors summation. Under this approach, generalized tensor Chernoff and Bernstein inequalities are special cases of Ky Fan
k
-norm concentration inequalities obtained by restricting different random tensors conditions.

Possible future works include considering a more general unitarily invariant norm instead of Ky Fan
k
-norm in our tail bounds and other functions of random tensors summation besides the power of polynomials.

1.1.
Preliminaries

Let
I={\mathbb{R}}
or
I=[0,\infty)

is denoted a complex Banach space. The abbreviations
{\mathcal{C}}_{b}(I:X)

where

stand for the spaces of bounded continuous functions

and continuous functions

respectively. Both spaces are Banach ones endowed with the sup-norm. The space of
X

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}C}(I:X)

\displaystyle\equiv\bigl\{y:I\rightarrow X:y\in{\mathcal{C}}\bigl((t_{i},t_{i+1}]:X\bigr),

\displaystyle t_{i}\neq 0,\,\,\mbox{for all}\,\,i\in{\mathbb{N}},\,\,y(t_{i}^{-})=y(t_{i})\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,y(t_{i}^{+})\mbox{ exist for any }i\in{\mathbb{N}}\bigr\},

where the symbols

at the point

,

is a Banach space endowed with the sup-norm.

For an operator family

of a bounded linear operators on a Banach space

it is said that is a strongly continuous semigroup of bounded linear operators (shortly

(i)

(iii)

.

Let

-periodic function (see

. By

we denote the set of all piecewise continuous and

-periodic functions, i.e.,

\displaystyle{\Phi}_{\omega,\rho}=\bigl\{y\,:\,y\in{\mathcal{P}C}([0,\infty):X)\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,y(\cdot+\omega)=\rho y(\cdot)\bigr\}.

We continue the investigations started in
[

]

-periodic solutions of the following abstract integro-differential impulsive equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\dot{y}(t)=Ay(t)+f\Biggl(t,y(t),\int\limits_{0}^{t}g(t,s,y(t))\,ds\Biggr),\quad t\neq{\tau}_{k},\,\,k\in{\mathbb{N}};\\
&\Delta y|_{t={\tau}_{k}}=B_{k}y(t)+d_{k},\quad\quad k\in{\mathbb{N}},\end{aligned}\right.

where

is the infinitesimal generator of a strongly continuous semigroup of bounded linear operators

For all

\displaystyle g(t+\omega,s,\rho y)=\rho g(t,s,y).

(A7)

2.
Nonhomogeneous linear impulsive problem

Of concern is the following equation

(2.1)

y^{\prime}(t)=f\Biggl(t,y(t),\int\limits_{0}^{t}g(t,s,y(t))\,ds\Biggr)

accompanied with the conditions

(2.2)

\displaystyle f\Biggl(t+\omega,\rho y,\rho\int\limits_{0}^{t+\omega}g(t,s,y)\,ds\Biggr)=\rho f\Biggl(t,y,\int\limits_{0}^{t}g(t,s,y)\,ds\Biggr)

and

(2.3)

\displaystyle g(t+\omega,s,\rho y)=\rho g(t,s,y).

We need the following auxiliary lemma:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f
be continuous and locally Lipschitzian in last two coordinates. If

f\Biggl(t+\omega,\rho y(t),\rho\int\limits_{0}^{t+\omega}g(t,s,y(t))\,ds\Biggr)=\rho f\Biggl(t,y(t),\int\limits_{0}^{t}g(t,s,y(t))\,ds\Biggr),\quad t\geq 0,

then
y(t)
is a solution of equation

y^{\prime}(t)=f\Biggl(t,y(t),\int_{0}^{t}g(t,s,y(t))\,ds\Biggr)

satisfying
t\in{\Phi}_{\omega,\rho}
if and only if we have

\displaystyle y(\omega)=\rho y(0).

Proof.

Let
y\in{\Phi}_{\omega,\rho}
. By the definition of
{\Phi}_{\omega,\rho}
, we have
y(t+\omega)=\rho y(t)
for all
t\geq 0
. Put
t=0
, so
y(\omega)=\rho y(0)
.
Now, let
y(\omega)=\rho y(0).
Set

x(t):={\rho}^{-1}y(t+\omega)
,
t\geq 0
.
Using (
2.2
)-(
2.3
), we have

\displaystyle x^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle={\rho}^{-1}y^{\prime}(t+\omega)

\displaystyle={\rho}^{-1}f\Biggl(t+\omega,y(t+\omega),\int\limits_{0}^{t+\omega}g(t+\omega,s,y(t+\omega))\,ds\Biggr)

\displaystyle={\rho}^{-1}f\Biggl(t+\omega,\rho{\rho}^{-1}y(t+\omega),\int\limits_{0}^{t+\omega}g(t+\omega,s,\rho{\rho}^{-1}y(t+\omega))\,ds\Biggr)

\displaystyle={\rho}^{-1}f\Biggl(t+\omega,\rho x(t),\int\limits_{0}^{t+\omega}g(t,s,\rho x(t))\,ds\Biggr)

\displaystyle={\rho}^{-1}\rho f\Biggl(t+\omega,x(t),\int\limits_{0}^{t}g(t,s,y(t))\,ds\Biggr)

\displaystyle=f\Biggl(t+\omega,x(t),\int\limits_{0}^{t}g(t,s,y(t))\,ds\Biggr).

Additionally,

\displaystyle x(0)={\rho}^{-1}y(\omega)=y(0).

Now, both
y(t)
and
x(t)
,
t\geq 0
, satisfy (
2.1
) and
y(0)=x(0)
. From the uniqueness of the solutions, we conclude that
x(t)=y(t)
, so
y(t+\omega)=\rho y(t)
,
t\geq 0
.
∎

At the very beginning of our work, we consider the homogeneous linear impulsive evolution equation.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(A1)–(A3)
hold. Then the homogeneous linear impulsive evolution equation

(2.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\dot{y}(t)=Ay(t),\quad t\neq{\tau}_{k},\,\,k\in{\mathbb{N}}\\
&\Delta y|_{t={\tau}_{k}}=B_{k}y(t)+d_{k},\quad\quad k\in{\mathbb{N}},\end{aligned}\right.

has a solution
y\in{\Phi}_{\omega,\rho}
if and only if
y(\omega)=\rho y(0)
or

\displaystyle\Biggl(\rho-T(\omega)\Biggl(\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k}){\Biggr)}\Biggr)y(0)=\sum\limits_{0<{\tau}_{i}<\omega}T(\omega-{\tau}_{i})\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i({\tau}_{i},\omega)}(E+B_{k})d_{i}.

Proof.

The direct implication is obvious. We prove the opposite direction. Let us assume that
y(\omega)=\rho y(0)
. The solution of (
2.4
), with

is given by the formula (

\displaystyle y(t)

\displaystyle=T(t)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,t)}(E+B_{k})y(0)+\int\limits_{0}^{t}T(t-{\tau})\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(\tau,t)}(E+B_{k})f(\tau)\,d{\tau}

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{0<{\tau}_{i}<t}T(t-{\tau}_{i})\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i({\tau}_{i},t)}(E+B_{k})d_{i},\quad t\geq 0.

For every
t\geq 0
and
t\neq{\tau}_{k}
for any

we have:

\displaystyle y(t

\displaystyle+\omega)

\displaystyle=T(t+\omega)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,t+\omega)}(E+B_{k})y(0)+\sum\limits_{0<{\tau}_{i}<t+\omega}T(t+\omega-{\tau}_{i})\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i({\tau}_{i},t+\omega)}(E+B_{k})d_{i}

\displaystyle=T(t)T(\omega)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,t)}(E+B_{k})\cdot\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})y(0)

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{0<{\tau}_{i}<t+\omega}T(t)T(\omega-{\tau}_{i})\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(\omega,t+\omega)}(E+B_{k})\cdot\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i({\tau}_{i},t+\omega)}(E+B_{k})d_{i}

\displaystyle=T(t)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,t)}(E+B_{k})

\displaystyle\times{\Biggl(}T(\omega)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})y(0)+\sum\limits_{0<{\tau}_{i}<\omega}T(\omega-{\tau}_{i})\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i({\tau}_{i},\omega)}(E+B_{k})d_{i}{\Biggr)}

∎

\displaystyle\leq M^{2}\max\Biggl\{\Biggl\|\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})^{2}\Biggr\|,1\Biggr\}\cdot\Biggl\|\Biggl(\rho-T(\omega)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})\Biggr)^{-1}\Biggr\|\frac{e^{\gamma(t+\omega)}-e^{\gamma t}}{\gamma}

\displaystyle+M\max\Biggl\{\Biggl\|\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})\Biggr\|,1\Biggr\}\cdot\frac{e^{\gamma t}-1}{\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq\Biggl\{M\max\Biggl\{\Biggl\|\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})^{2}\Biggr\|,1\Biggr\}\cdot\Biggl\|\Biggl(\rho-T(\omega)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})\Biggr)^{-1}\Biggr\|e^{\gamma\omega}

\displaystyle+\max\Biggl\{\Biggl\|\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})\Biggr\|,1\Biggr\}\Biggr\}M\frac{e^{\gamma\omega}-1}{\gamma}.

For
\gamma=0
, we have

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\omega}\|H(t,\tau)\|\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq M^{2}\max\Biggl\{\Biggl\|\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})^{2}\Biggr\|,1\Biggr\}\cdot\Biggl\|\Biggl(\rho-T(\omega)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})\Biggr)^{-1}\Biggr\|\cdot\int\limits_{0}^{\omega}e^{\gamma(\omega+t-\tau)}\,d\tau

\displaystyle+M\max\Biggl\{\Biggl\|\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})\Biggr\|,1\Biggr\}\cdot\int\limits_{0}^{t}e^{\gamma(t-\tau)}\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\Biggl\{M\max\Biggl\{\Biggl\|\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})^{2}\Biggr\|,1\Biggr\}\cdot\Biggl\|\Biggl(\rho-T(\omega)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})\Biggr)^{-1}\Biggr\|

\displaystyle+\max\Biggl\{\Biggl\|\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})\Biggr\|,1\Biggr\}\Biggr\}M\omega.

∎

With an additional condition from the previous lemma, we can state the following:

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
(A1)–(A4)
hold, and let

\Biggl(\rho-T(\omega)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})\Biggr)^{-1}T(t)=T(t)\Biggl(\rho-T(\omega)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})\Biggr)^{-1},\quad t\geq 0.

Then we have

\int\limits_{0}^{\omega}\|H(t,\tau)\|\,d\tau\leq C^{\prime}_{2}\equiv\left\{\begin{aligned} &M\Biggl\|\Biggl(\rho-T(\omega)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})\Biggr)^{-1}\Biggr\|\cdot\max\Biggl\{\Biggl\|\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})\Biggr\|,1\Biggr\}\\
&\times\max\{\|T\|,1\}\frac{e^{\gamma\omega}-1}{\gamma},\quad\gamma\neq 0;\\
&M\Biggl\|\Biggl(\rho-T(\omega)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})\Biggr)^{-1}\Biggr\|\cdot\max\Biggl\{\Biggl\|\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})\Biggr\|,1\Biggr\}\\
&\times\max\{\|T\|,1\}\omega,\quad\gamma=0,\end{aligned}\right.

for any
t\in(0,\omega)
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\omega}\|H(t,\tau)\|\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{0}^{t}\|T\|\cdot\Biggl\|\Biggl(\rho-T(\omega)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})\Biggr)^{-1}\Biggr\|\cdot\|T(t-\tau)\|\cdot\Biggl\|\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(\tau,t)}(E+B_{k})\Biggr\|\,d\tau

\displaystyle+\int\limits_{t}^{\omega}\|T(t+\omega-\tau)\|\cdot\Biggl\|\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,t)+i(\tau,\omega)}(E+B_{k})\Biggr\|\cdot\Biggl\|\Biggl(\rho-T(\omega)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})\Biggr)^{-1}\Biggr\|\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq M\Biggl\|\Biggl(\rho-T(\omega)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})\Biggr)^{-1}\Biggr\|\max\Biggl\{\Biggl\|\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})\Biggr\|,1\Biggr\}

\displaystyle\times\Biggl(\int\limits_{0}^{t}\|T\|e^{\gamma(t-\tau)}\,d\tau+\int\limits_{t}^{\omega}e^{\gamma(t+\omega-\tau)}\,d\tau\Biggr).

Now, we have two subcases:
\gamma\neq 0
and
\gamma=0
.
For
\gamma\neq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\omega}\|H(t,\tau)\|\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq M\Biggl\|\Biggl(\rho-T(\omega)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})\Biggr)^{-1}\Biggr\|\max\Biggl\{\Biggl\|\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})\Biggr\|,1\Biggr\}

\displaystyle\times\Biggl(\int\limits_{0}^{t}\|T\|e^{\gamma(t-\tau)}\,d\tau+\int\limits_{t}^{\omega}e^{\gamma(t+\omega-\tau)}\,d\tau\Biggr)

\displaystyle\leq M\Bigg\|\Biggl(\rho-T(\omega)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})\Biggr)^{-1}\Biggr\|\max\Biggl\{\Biggl\|\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})\Biggr\|,1\Biggr\}

\displaystyle\times\Biggl(\|T\|\frac{e^{\gamma t}-1}{\gamma}+\frac{e^{\gamma\omega}-e^{\gamma t}}{\gamma}\Biggr)

\displaystyle\leq M\Biggl\|\Biggl(\rho-T(\omega)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})\Biggr)^{-1}\Biggr\|\max\Biggl\{\Biggl\|\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})\Biggr\|,1\Biggr\}

\displaystyle\times\max\{\|T\|,1\}\Biggl(\frac{e^{\gamma t}-1}{\gamma}\Biggr).

For
\gamma=0
, we have

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\omega}\|H(t,\tau)\|\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq M\Biggl\|\Biggl(\rho-T(\omega)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})\Biggr)^{-1}\Biggr\|\max\Biggl\{\Biggl\|\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})\Biggr\|,1\Biggr\}

\displaystyle\times\Biggl(\int\limits_{0}^{t}\|T\|e^{\gamma(t-\tau)}\,d\tau+\int\limits_{t}^{\omega}e^{\gamma(t+\omega-\tau)}\,d\tau\Biggr)

\displaystyle\leq M\Biggl\|\Biggl(\rho-T(\omega)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})\Biggr)^{-1}\Biggr\|\max\Biggl\{\Biggl\|\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})\Biggr\|,1\Biggr\}\max\{\|T\|,1\}\omega.

∎

3.
(\omega,{\rho})
-periodic solutions of integro–differential impulsive problem

In this section, we consider the following abstract integro-differential impulsive equation:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\dot{y}(t)=Ay(t)+f\Biggl(t,y(t),\int\limits_{0}^{t}g(t,s,y(t))\,ds\Biggr),\quad t\neq{\tau}_{k},\,\,k\in{\mathbb{N}};\\
&\Delta y|_{t={\tau}_{k}}=B_{k}y(t)+d_{k},\quad\quad k\in{\mathbb{N}}.\end{aligned}\right.

We are going to prove, by using the Banach fixed point theorem and the Schauder fixed point theorem, that under certain conditions, we have the existence and uniqueness of solutions of (
1.1
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(A1)–(A7)
and
(A9)
hold. If
0<LC_{2}<1
, where
L=(L_{f}(\nu)+M_{1}L_{g}(\nu))
and
M_{1}
is certain constant, then the equation (
1.1
) has a unique
(\omega,\rho)
-periodic solution
y\in{\Phi}_{\omega,\rho}
, satisfying

\displaystyle\|y\|\leq\frac{\|f\|_{0}C_{2}+C_{1}}{1-LC_{2}},

where

\|f\|_{0}:=\Biggl\|f\Biggl(\cdot,0,\int_{0}^{\cdot}g(\cdot,s,0)\,ds\Biggr)\Biggr\|_{\infty}=\max\limits_{t\in[0,\omega]}\Biggl\|f\Biggl(t,0,\int\limits_{0}^{t}g(t,s,0)\,ds\Biggr)\Biggr\|.

Proof.

Note that, if
y\in{\Phi}_{\omega,\rho}
, then
f(\cdot,y,\int_{0}^{\cdot}g(\cdot,s,y)\,ds)\in{\Phi}_{\omega,\rho}
. Keeping in mind Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
2.3
, we need to solve the fixed point problem

\displaystyle y(t)=\int\limits_{0}^{\omega}H(t,\tau)f\Biggl(\tau,y(\tau),\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}g(\tau,s,y(\tau))\,ds\Biggr)\,d\tau+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}H(t,{\tau}_{i})d_{i},\quad t\in[0,\omega].

We define the operator
R
on the space
\Psi
as

\displaystyle(Ry)(t):=\int\limits_{0}^{\omega}H(t,\tau)f\Biggl(\tau,y(\tau),\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}g(\tau,s,y(\tau))\,ds\Biggr)\,d\tau+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}H(t,{\tau}_{i})d_{i}.

We will show that
R:\Psi\rightarrow\Psi
is a contraction mapping. Let
y_{1},\ y_{2}\in{\Psi}.
Using Lemma
2.7
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\bigl\|(Ry_{1})(t)-(Ry_{2})(t)\bigr\|

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{0}^{\omega}\|H(t,\tau)\|\cdot\Biggl\|f\Biggl(\tau,y_{1}(\tau),\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}g(\tau,s,y_{1}(\tau))\,ds\Biggr)-f\Biggl(\tau,y_{2}(\tau),\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}g(\tau,s,y_{2}(\tau))\,ds\Biggr)\Biggr\|\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{0}^{\omega}\|H(t,\tau)\|\cdot L_{f}(\nu)\Biggl(\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|

\displaystyle+\Biggl\|\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}g(\tau,s,y_{1}(\tau))\,ds-\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}g(\tau,s,y_{2}(\tau))\,ds\Biggr\|\Biggr)\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{0}^{\omega}\|H(t,\tau)\|\,d\tau\cdot L_{f}(\nu)\Biggl(\bigl\|y_{1}-y_{2}\bigr\|+\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}\bigl\|g(\tau,s,y_{1}(\tau))-g(\tau,s,y_{2}(\tau))\bigr\|\,ds\,d\tau\Biggr)

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{0}^{\omega}\|H(t,\tau)\|\cdot L_{f}(\nu)\Biggl(\bigl\|y_{1}-y_{2}\bigr\|+L_{g}(\nu)\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}\bigl\|y_{1}-y_{2}\bigr\|\,ds\Biggr)

\displaystyle\leq(L_{f}(\nu)+M_{1}L_{g}(\nu))\cdot\bigl\|y_{1}-y_{2}\bigr\|\cdot\int\limits_{0}^{\omega}\|H(t,\tau)\|\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq LC_{2}\cdot\bigl\|y_{1}-y_{2}\bigr\|,

where
L:=L_{f}(\rho)+M_{1}L_{g}(\rho)
. So,

\displaystyle\bigl\|(Ry_{1})-(Ry_{2})\bigr\|\leq LC_{2}\bigl\|y_{1}-y_{2}\bigr\|,\quad 0<LC_{2}<1.

Hence, the uniqueness of the solution of (
1.1
) follows by the Banach contraction mapping principle.
Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\|y\|=\|Ry\|

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{0}^{\omega}\|H(t,\tau)\|\cdot\Biggl\|f\Biggl(\tau,y(\tau),\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}g(\tau,s,y(\tau))\,ds\Biggr)\Biggr\|\,d\tau+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\bigl\|H(t,{\tau}_{i})d_{i}\bigr\|

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{0}^{\omega}\|H(t,\tau)\|\cdot\Biggl\|f\Biggl(\tau,y(\tau),\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}g(\tau,s,y(\tau))\,ds\Biggr)-f\Biggl(\tau,0,\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}g(\tau,s,0)\Biggr)\,ds\Biggr\|\,d\tau

\displaystyle+\int\limits_{0}^{\omega}\|H(t,\tau)\|\cdot\Biggl\|f\Biggl(\tau,0,\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}g(\tau,s,0)\,ds\Biggr)\Biggr\|\,d\tau+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\bigl\|H(t,{\tau}_{i})d_{i}\bigr\|

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{0}^{\omega}L_{f}(\nu)\Biggl(\|y\|+\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}\|g(\tau,s,y(\tau))-g(\tau,s,0)\|\,ds\Biggr)\,d\tau

\displaystyle+\int\limits_{0}^{\omega}\|H(t,\tau)\|\cdot\Biggl\|f\Biggl(\tau,0,\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}g(\tau,s,0)\Biggr)\,ds\Biggr\|\,d\tau+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\bigl\|H(t,{\tau}_{i})d_{i}\bigr\|

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{0}^{\omega}\|H(t,{\tau})\|\cdot L_{f}(\nu)\Biggl(\|y\|+M_{1}L_{g}(\nu)\|y\|\Biggr)\,d\tau+\|f\|_{0}\int\limits_{0}^{\omega}\|H(t,\tau)\|d\tau

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\bigl\|H(t,{\tau}_{i})d_{i}\bigr\|\leq\|y\|\Biggl(L_{f}(\nu)+M_{1}L_{g}(\nu)\Biggr)C_{2}+\|f\|_{0}C_{2}+C_{1}.

We obtain:

\displaystyle\|y\|\leq\frac{\|f\|_{0}C_{2}+C_{1}}{1-\bigl(L_{f}(\nu)+M_{1}L_{g}(\nu)\bigr)C_{2}}=\frac{\|f\|_{0}C_{2}+C_{1}}{1-LC_{2}},

so the proof is finished.
∎

Now we will state and prove the following result:

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
(A1)–(A6)
and
(A8)–(A10)
hold. If
0<\beta C_{2}<1
, then (
1.1
) has a
(\omega,\rho)
-periodic solution
y\in{\Phi}_{\omega,\rho}
.

Proof.

We consider the operator
R
defined like in the proof of the previous theorem on
B_{l}=\{y\in\Psi\,:\,\|y\|\leq l\}
and the constant
l
is given by
l=\frac{\alpha C_{2}+C_{1}}{1-\beta C_{2}}
. We are going to prove the statement of the theorem in the following steps:

Step 1.
We show that
R(B_{l})\subset B_{l}
. Let
y\in B_{l}
be arbitrary, and let
t\in[0,\omega]
. Then we have:

\displaystyle\|Ry(t)\|

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{0}^{\omega}\|H(t,\tau)\|\cdot\Biggl\|f\Biggl(\tau,y(\tau),\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}g(t,s,y(\tau))\Biggr)\,ds\Biggr\|\,d\tau+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\bigl\|H(t,{\tau}_{i})d_{i}\bigr\|

\displaystyle\leq\beta\int\limits_{0}^{\omega}\bigl\|H(t,{\tau}_{i})\bigr\|\cdot\|y(\tau)\|\,d\tau+\alpha\int\limits_{0}^{\omega}\|H(t,\tau)\|\,d\tau

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\bigl\|H(t,{\tau}_{i})\bigr\|\cdot\|d_{i}\|\leq\beta C_{2}\|y\|+\alpha C_{2}+C_{1}=l,

implying
\|Ry(t)\|\leq l
, so
R(B_{l})\subset B_{l}
for any
t\in[0,\omega]
. Here the constants
\alpha
and
\beta
are from the assumption (A8).

Step 2.
We prove that the operator
R
is continuous on
B_{l}
. Let
(y_{n})
be a Cauchy sequence such that
y_{n}\rightarrow y
, when
n\rightarrow\infty
in
B_{l}
. Let

f_{n}=f\Biggl(t,y_{n}(t),\int\limits_{0}^{t}g(t,s,y_{n}(t))\,ds\Biggr)
,
t\in[0,\omega]
and
f=f\Biggl(t,y(t),\int\limits_{0}^{t}g(t,s,y(t))\,ds\Biggr)
. It is clear that
f_{n}\rightarrow f
, when
y_{n}\rightarrow y
, for any
t\in[0,\omega]
. Now,

\displaystyle\bigl\|(Ry_{n})(t)-(Ry)(t)\bigr\|

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{0}^{\omega}\|H(t,\tau)\|\cdot\Biggl\|f\Biggl(t,y_{n}(t),\int\limits_{0}^{t}g(t,s,y_{n}(t))\,ds\Biggr)-f\Biggl(t,y(t),\int\limits_{0}^{t}g(t,s,y(t))\,ds\Biggr)\Biggr\|\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{0}^{\omega}\|H(t,\tau)\|\cdot\|f_{n}-f\|\,d\tau\leq C_{2}\|f_{n}-f\|.

Hence,
R
is continuous operator on
B_{l}
.

Step 3.
We show that
R(B_{l})
is relatively compact set. Since
R(B_{l})\subset B_{l}
, it follows that
R(B_{l})
is uniformly bounded. Now, we show that the operator
R
is an equicontinuous operator. For any
t_{1},t_{2}\in[0,\omega]
and
y\in B_{l}
, using (A8), we have

\displaystyle\bigl\|(Ry)(t_{2})-(Ry)(t_{1})\bigr\|

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{0}^{\omega}\bigl\|H(t_{2},\tau)-H(t_{1},\tau)\bigr\|\cdot\Biggl\|f\Biggl(\tau,y(\tau),\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}g(\tau,s,y(\tau))\,ds\Biggr)\Biggr\|\,d\tau

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\bigl\|H(t_{2},{\tau}_{i})-H(t_{1},{\tau}_{i})\bigr\|\cdot\|d_{i}\|

\displaystyle\leq(\alpha+\beta\|y\|)\int\limits_{0}^{\omega}\bigl\|H(t_{2},\tau)-H(t_{1},\tau)\bigr\|\,d\tau+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\bigl\|H(t_{2},{\tau}-i)-H(t_{1},{\tau}_{i})\bigr\|\cdot\|d_{i}\|.

We have:

\bigl\|H(t_{2},\tau)-H(t_{1},\tau)\bigr\|=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Biggl\|T(t_{2})\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,t_{2})}(E+B_{k})\Biggl(\rho-T(\omega)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})\Biggr)^{-1}T(\omega-\tau)\\
&\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(\tau,\omega)}(E+B_{k})+T(t_{2}-\tau)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(\tau,t_{2})}(E+B_{k})\\
&-T(t_{1})\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,t_{1})}(E+B-k)\Biggl(\rho-T(\omega)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})\Biggr)^{-1}\\
&\times T(\omega-\tau)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(\tau,\omega)}(E+B_{k})-T(t_{1}-\tau)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(\tau,t_{1})}(E+B_{k})\Biggr\|,\\
&\quad 0<\tau<t_{1}<t_{2};\\
&\Biggl\|T(t_{2})\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,t_{2})}(E+B_{k})\Biggl(\rho-T(\omega)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})\Biggr)^{-1}T(\omega-\tau)\\
&\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(\tau,\omega)}(E+B_{k})-T(t_{1})\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,t_{1})}(E+B_{k})\Biggl(\rho-T(\omega)\\
&\times\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})\Biggr)^{-1}\cdot T(\omega-\tau)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(\tau,\omega)}(E+B_{k})\Biggr\|,\quad t_{1}<t_{2}<\tau<\omega.\end{aligned}\right.

If
0<\tau<t_{1}<t_{2}
, then we have

\displaystyle\bigl\|H(t_{2},\tau)-H(t_{1},\tau)\bigr\|

\displaystyle\leq\Biggl\|T(t_{2})\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,t_{2})}(E+B_{k})+T(t_{2}-\tau)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(\tau,t_{2})}(E+B_{k})T(\omega-\tau)

\displaystyle\times\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(\tau,\omega)}(E+B_{k})+T(t_{2}-\tau)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(\tau,t_{2})}(E+B_{k})

\displaystyle-T(t_{1})\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,t_{1})}(E+B_{k})+T(t_{2}-\tau)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(\tau,t_{2})}(E+B_{k})

\displaystyle\times T(\omega-\tau)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(\tau,\omega)}(E+B_{k})-T(t_{1}-\tau)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(\tau,t_{1})}(E+B_{k})\Biggr\|

\displaystyle\leq\bigl\|T(t_{2})-T(t_{1})\bigr\|\cdot\Biggl\|\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})^{2}\Biggr\|\cdot\Biggl\|\Biggl(\rho-T(\omega)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})\Biggr)^{-1}\Biggr\|

\displaystyle\times Me^{\gamma(\omega-\tau)}+\bigl\|T(t_{2}-\tau)-T(t_{1}-\tau)\bigr\|\cdot\Biggl\|\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})\Biggr\|.

Using (A10), we obtain
\|H(t_{2},\tau)-H(t_{1},\tau)\|\rightarrow 0
as
t_{2}\rightarrow t_{1}
.

If
t_{1}<t_{2}<\tau<\omega
, we have

\displaystyle\|H(t_{2},\tau)-H(t_{1},\tau)\|

\displaystyle=\Biggl\|T(t_{2})\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,t_{2})}(E+B_{k})\Biggl(\rho-T(\omega)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})\Biggr)^{-1}T(\omega-\tau)

\displaystyle\times\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(\tau,\omega)}(E+B_{k})-T(t_{1})\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,t_{1})}(E+B_{k})

\displaystyle\times\Biggl(\rho-T(\omega)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})\Biggr)^{-1}T(\omega-\tau)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(\tau,\omega)}(E+B_{k})\Biggr\|

\displaystyle\leq\|T(t_{2})-T(t_{1})\|\cdot\Biggl\|\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})^{2}\Biggr\|

\displaystyle\times\Biggl\|\Biggl(\rho-T(\omega)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{i(0,\omega)}(E+B_{k})\Biggr)^{-1}\Biggr\|Me^{\gamma(\omega-\tau)}.

Again, using (A10), we obtain that
\|H(t_{2},\tau)-H(t_{1},\tau)\|\rightarrow 0
as
t_{2}\rightarrow t_{1}
. Therefore, for any
t_{1},t_{2}\in[0,\omega]
, we have
H(t_{2},\tau)\rightarrow H(t_{1},\tau)
, when
t_{2}\rightarrow t_{1}
. This implies that
\|(Ry)(t_{2})-(Ry)(t_{1})\|\rightarrow 0
when
t_{2}\rightarrow t_{1}
, hence the operator
R
is an equicontinuous operator.

Step 4.
We show that
R
maps a bounded set into a precompact set in
X
. We consider the approximate operator
R_{\varepsilon}
on
B_{l}
as following

\displaystyle(R_{\varepsilon})(t)=\int\limits_{0}^{\omega}H(t-\varepsilon,\tau)f\Biggl(\tau,y(\tau),\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}g(\tau,s,y(\tau))\,ds\Biggr)\,d\tau+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}H(t-\varepsilon,{\tau}_{i})d_{i},\quad t\in[0,\omega].

Let

K=\{(Ry)(t)\,:\,t\in[0,\omega]\}

and

\displaystyle K_{\varepsilon}=T(\varepsilon)\bigl\{(R_{\varepsilon}y)(t)\,:\,t\in[0,\omega]\bigr\},\quad\varepsilon\in(0,\omega).

Since
K
is bounded and (A10) holds, we obtain that
K_{\varepsilon}
is precompact.
Next,

\displaystyle\bigl\|(R_{\varepsilon}y)(t)-(Ry)(t)\bigr\|

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{0}^{\omega}\|H(t,\tau)-H(t-\varepsilon,\tau)\|\cdot\Biggl\|f\Biggl(\tau,y(\tau),\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}g(\tau,s,y(\tau))\,ds\Biggr)\Biggr\|\,d\tau

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\bigl\|H(t,{\tau}_{i})-H(t-\varepsilon,{\tau}_{i})\bigr\|\cdot\|d_{i}\|

\displaystyle\leq(\alpha+\beta l)\int\limits_{0}^{\omega}\|H(t,\tau)-H(t-\varepsilon,\tau)\|\,d\tau

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\bigl\|H(t,{\tau}_{i})-H(t-\varepsilon,{\tau}_{i})\bigr\|\cdot\|d_{i}\|.

Since for any
t_{1},t_{2}\in(0,\omega)
,
H(t_{2},\tau)\rightarrow H(t_{1},\tau)
as
t_{2}\rightarrow t_{1}
, we have

\|(R_{\varepsilon}y)(t)-(Ry)(t)\|\rightarrow 0

as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
. Hence,
K
can be approximated by a precompact set
K_{\varepsilon}
with arbitrary accuracy. So,
K
itself is a precompact set in
X
, i.e.,
R
maps a bounded set into a precompact set. The Arzelà–Ascoli theorem implies the compactness of the operator
R
, so the statement of the theorem follows by applying the Schauder fixed point theorem.
∎

Abstract
. In this paper, we first study infinitesimal deformations of a Lie conformal algebra and a Lie conformal algebra with a module (called an 𝖫𝖢𝖬𝗈𝖽 \mathsf{LCMod} pair), which lead to the notions of Nijenhuis operator on the Lie conformal algebra and Nijenhuis structure on the 𝖫𝖢𝖬𝗈𝖽 \mathsf{LCMod} pair, respectively. Then by adding compatibility conditions between Nijenhuis structures and 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators, we introduce the notion of an 𝒪 ​ N \mathrm{\mathcal{O}N} -structure on an 𝖫𝖢𝖬𝗈𝖽 \mathsf{LCMod} pair and show that an 𝒪 ​ N \mathrm{\mathcal{O}N} -structure gives rise to a hierarchy of pairwise compatible 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators. In particular, we show that compatible 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators on a Lie conformal algebra can be characterized by Nijenhuis operators on Lie conformal algebras. Finally, we introduce the notions of conformal r r -matrix-Nijenhuis structure and symplectic-Nijenhuis structure on the Lie conformal algebra and study their relations.

Preliminaries on Lie conformal algebra

Definition 2.1
.

A
Lie conformal algebra

A
is a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module endowed with a
\mathbb{C}
-bilinear map
A\times A\rightarrow A[\lambda]
, denoted by

(5)

\displaystyle[\partial a_{\lambda}b]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\lambda[a_{\lambda}b],

(6)

\displaystyle{[a_{\lambda}b]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-[b_{-\lambda-\partial}a],

(7)

\displaystyle{[a_{\lambda}[b_{\mu}c]]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[[a_{\lambda}b]_{\lambda+\mu}c]+[b_{\mu}[a_{\lambda}c]],\quad\forall~a,b,c\in A.

A Lie conformal algebra is called

if it is finitely generated as a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module.

The ordinary examples of finite Lie conformal algebra is the Virasoro and current Lie conformal algebras.

Example 2.2
.

The Virasoro Lie conformal algebra
{\rm Vir}
is the simplest nontrivial example of Lie conformal algebras, which is defined by

{\rm Vir}=\mathbb{C}[\partial]a,\quad[a_{\lambda}a]=(\partial+2\lambda)a.

Example 2.3
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
be a Lie algebra. The current Lie conformal algebra associated with
\mathfrak{g}
is defined by

{\rm Cur}~\mathfrak{g}=\mathbb{C}[\partial]\otimes\mathfrak{g},\quad[a_{\lambda}b]=[a,b],\quad\forall~a,b\in\mathfrak{g}.

In the following, we recall a class of Lie conformal algebras named quadratic Lie conformal algebras.

Definition 2.4
.

If a Lie conformal algebra
A=\mathbb{C}[\partial]V
with a free
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module and the
\lambda
-bracket is of the following form:

[a_{\lambda}b]=\partial u+\lambda v+w,\quad\forall~a,b\in V,

where

.

Recall that a
Novikov algebra
is a pair
(A,\circ)
, where
A
is a vector space and

is a bilinear multiplication satisfying that for all

(8)

\displaystyle(a\circ b)\circ c

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a\circ c)\circ b,

(9)

\displaystyle(a\circ b)\circ c-a\circ(b\circ c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(b\circ a)\circ c-b\circ(a\circ c).

Recall that a
Gel’fand-Dorfman bialgebra
is a triple
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\circ)
such that
(A,[\cdot,\cdot])
is a Lie algebra,
(A,\circ)
is a Novikov algebra and they satisfy the following condition:

(10)

[a\circ b,c]+[a,b]\circ c-a\circ[b,c]-[a\circ c,b]-[a,c]\circ b=0.

Proposition 2.5

(
[

A Lie conformal algebra

It is not hard to see that Virasoro Lie conformal algebra is a quadratic Lie conformal algebra, which is corresponding to the Gel’fand-Dorfman bialgebra

3.1.
Nijenhuis operators on Lie conformal algebras

Let

\lambda

[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot]

\omega:A\otimes A\rightarrow A[\lambda]

By a direct calculation, we can deduce that

(21)

\displaystyle\{\partial a_{\lambda}b\}_{\omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\lambda\{a_{\lambda}b\}_{\omega},

(22)

\displaystyle\{a_{\lambda}b\}_{\omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\{b_{-\lambda-\partial}a\}_{\omega},

(23)

\displaystyle\{a_{\lambda}\{b_{\mu}c\}_{\omega}\}_{\omega}-\{b_{\mu}\{a_{\lambda}c\}_{\omega}\}_{\omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\{a_{\lambda}b\}_{\omega_{\lambda+\mu}}c\}_{\omega},

and

(24)

\{[a_{\lambda}b]_{\lambda+\mu}c\}_{\omega}+[\{a_{\lambda}b\}_{\omega_{\lambda+\mu}}c]-\{a_{\lambda}[b_{\mu}c]\}_{\omega}-[a_{\lambda}\{b_{\mu}c\}_{\omega}]+\{b_{\mu}[a_{\lambda}c]\}_{\omega}+[b_{\mu}\{a_{\lambda}c\}_{\omega}]=0.

By (

\displaystyle\{a_{\lambda}(\partial b)\}_{\omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\lambda+\partial)\{a_{\lambda}b\}_{\omega}.

We see that (

If

\mathcal{O}
-operator. It is not hard to see that

[u_{\lambda}v]^{T+T\circ S}=[u_{\lambda}v]^{T}+[u_{\lambda}v]^{T\circ S}=[u_{\lambda}v]^{T}+[u_{\lambda}v]_{S}^{T}.

Thus we have

\begin{split}&(T+T\circ S)([u_{\lambda}v]^{T+T\circ S})\\
=&T([u_{\lambda}v]^{T})+T\circ S([u_{\lambda}v]_{S}^{T})+T\circ S([u_{\lambda}v]^{T})+T([u_{\lambda}v]_{S}^{T})\\
=&T([u_{\lambda}v]^{T})+T\circ S([u_{\lambda}v]_{S}^{T})+T\circ S([u_{\lambda}v]^{T})\\
&+T([S(u)_{\lambda}v]^{T}+[u_{\lambda}S(v)]^{T}-S[u_{\lambda}v]^{T})\\
=&T([u_{\lambda}v]^{T})+T\circ S([u_{\lambda}v]_{S}^{T})+T([S(u)_{\lambda}v]^{T}+[u_{\lambda}S(v)]^{T})\\
=&[T(u)_{\lambda}T(v)]+[T\circ S(u)_{\lambda}T\circ S(v)]+[T\circ S(u)_{\lambda}T(v)]+[T(u)_{\lambda}T\circ S(v)]\\
=&[(T+T\circ S)(u)_{\lambda}(T+T\circ S)(v)],\end{split}

which implies that
T+T\circ S
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator.

Similar to the properties of
\mathrm{\mathcal{O}N}
-structures on Lie algebras given in
[
19
]
, we also have

Lemma 4.13
.

Let
(T,S,N)
be an
\mathrm{\mathcal{O}N}
-structure on an
\mathsf{LCMod}
pair
(A,[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot];\rho)
. Then

\rm(i)

for all
k,i\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(66)

T_{k}[u_{\lambda}v]_{S^{k+i}}^{T}=[T_{k}(u)_{\lambda}T_{k}(v)]_{N^{i}};

\rm(ii)

for all
k,i\in\mathbb{N}
such that
i\leq k
,

(67)

[u_{\lambda}v]^{T^{k}}=[u_{\lambda}v]_{S^{k}}^{T}=S^{k-i}[u_{\lambda}v]^{T_{i}},

where
T_{k}=T\circ S^{k}=N^{k}\circ T
and set
T_{0}=T
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Lemmas 4.8 and 4.9 in
[
19
]
.

Proposition 4.14
.

Let
(T,S,N)
be an
\mathrm{\mathcal{O}N}
-structure on an
\mathsf{LCMod}
pair
(A,[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot];\rho)
. Then all
T_{k}=N^{k}\circ T
are
\mathcal{O}
-operators on the
\mathsf{LCMod}
pair
(A;\rho)
and for all
k,l\in\mathbb{N}
,
T_{k}
and
T_{l}
are compatible.

Proof.

By (
66
) and (
67
) with
i=0
, we have

T_{k}[u_{\lambda}v]^{T_{k}}=[T_{k}(u)_{\lambda}T_{k}(v)],

which implies that
T_{k}
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator.

For the second assertion, we only need to prove that
T^{k}+T^{k+i}
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator for all
k,i\in\mathbb{N}
. By (
67
), we have

[u_{\lambda}v]^{T_{k}+T_{k+i}}=[u_{\lambda}v]^{T_{k}}+[u_{\lambda}v]^{T_{k+i}}=[u_{\lambda}v]^{T_{k}}+[u_{\lambda}v]_{S^{i}}^{T_{k}}.

Thus, we have

\begin{split}&(T_{k}+T_{k+i})([u_{\lambda}v]^{T_{k}+T_{k+i}})\\
=&T_{k}([u_{\lambda}v]^{T_{k}})+T_{k}([u_{\lambda}v]_{S^{i}}^{T_{k}})+T_{k+i}([u_{\lambda}v]^{T_{k}})+T_{k+i}([u_{\lambda}v]_{S^{i}}^{T_{k}})\\
=&T_{k}([u_{\lambda}v]^{T_{k}})+T_{k+i}([u_{\lambda}v]^{T_{k}})+T_{k+i}([u_{\lambda}v]_{S^{i}}^{T_{k}})\\
&+T_{k}([S^{i}(u)_{\lambda}v]^{T_{k}}+[u_{\lambda}S^{i}(v)]^{T_{k}}-S^{i}[u_{\lambda}v]^{T_{k}})\\
=&T_{k}([u_{\lambda}v]^{T_{k}})+T_{k+i}([u_{\lambda}v]_{S^{i}}^{T_{k}})+T_{k}([S^{i}(u)_{\lambda}v]^{T_{k}})+T_{k}([u_{\lambda}S^{i}(v)]^{T_{k}})\\
=&[T_{k}(u)_{\lambda}T_{k}(v)]+[T_{k+i}(u)_{\lambda}T_{k+i}(v)]+[T_{k+i}(u)_{\lambda}T_{k}(v)]+[T_{k}(u)_{\lambda}T_{k+i}(v)]\\
=&[(T_{k}+T_{k+i})(u)_{\lambda}(T_{k}+T_{k+i})(v)],\end{split}

which implies that
T_{k}+T_{k+i}
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator. Thus
T_{k}
and
T_{l}
are compatible for all
k,l\in\mathbb{N}
.

Compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operators can give rise to
\mathrm{\mathcal{O}N}
-structures.

Theorem 4.15
.

Let
T,T_{1}:V\rightarrow A
be two
\mathcal{O}
-operators on an
\mathsf{LCMod}
pair
(A,[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot];\rho)
. Suppose that
T
is invertible. If
T
and
T_{1}
are compatible, then

\rm(i)

(T,S=T^{-1}\circ T_{1},N=T_{1}\circ T^{-1})
is an
\mathrm{\mathcal{O}N}
-structure on the
\mathsf{LCMod}
pair
(A,[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot];\rho)
;

\rm(ii)

(T_{1},S=T^{-1}\circ T_{1},N=T_{1}\circ T^{-1})
is an
\mathrm{\mathcal{O}N}
-structure on the
\mathsf{LCMod}
pair
(A,[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot];\rho)
.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.10
,
N=T_{1}\circ T^{-1}
is a Nijenhuis operator on
A
.

Since
T
and
T_{1}
are compatible, we have

\displaystyle[T(u)_{\lambda}T_{1}(v)]+[T_{1}(u)_{\lambda}T(v)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T(\rho(T_{1}(u))_{\lambda}(v)-\rho(T_{1}(v))_{-\lambda-\partial}(u))

\displaystyle+T_{1}(\rho(T(u))_{\lambda}(v)-\rho(T(v))_{-\lambda-\partial}(u)).

Because of
T_{1}=T\circ S
, we get

(68)

\begin{split}[T(u)_{\lambda}TS(v)]+[TS(u)_{\lambda}T(v)]=&T(\rho(TS(u))_{\lambda}(v)-\rho(TS(v))_{-\lambda-\partial}(u))\\
&+TS(\rho(T(u))_{\lambda}(v)-\rho(T(v))_{-\lambda-\partial}(u)).\end{split}

Since
T
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator, we have

(69)

\begin{split}[T(u)_{\lambda}TS(v)]+[TS(u)_{\lambda}T(v)]=&T(\rho(T(u))_{\lambda}(S(v))-\rho(TS(v))_{-\lambda-\partial}(u)\\
&+T(\rho(TS(u))_{\lambda}(v)-\rho(T(v))_{-\lambda-\partial}(S(u)).\end{split}

By (
68
), (
69
) and the fact that
T
is invertible, we have

(70)

S(\rho(T(u))_{\lambda}(v)-\rho(T(v))_{-\lambda-\partial}(u))=\rho(T(u))_{\lambda}(S(v))-\rho(T(v))_{-\lambda-\partial}(S(u)).

Replacing
v
with
S(v)
, we get

(71)

S(\rho(T(u))_{\lambda}(S(v))-\rho(T\circ S(v))_{-\lambda-\partial}(u))=\rho(T(u))_{\lambda}(S^{2}(v))-\rho(T\circ S(v))_{-\lambda-\partial}(S(u)).

Since
T
and
T\circ S
are
\mathcal{O}
-operators, we have

TS[u_{\lambda}v]^{T\circ S}=[TS(u)_{\lambda}TS(v)]=T[S(u)_{\lambda}S(v)]^{T}.

By the fact that
T
is invertible, we have

S[u_{\lambda}v]^{T\circ S}=[S(u)_{\lambda}S(v)]^{T}.

which implies that

(72)

S(\rho((TS)(u))_{\lambda}(v)-\rho(TS(v))_{-\lambda-\partial}(u))=\rho((TS)(u))_{\lambda}(S(v))-\rho((TS)(v))_{-\lambda-\partial}(S(u)).

By (
71
) and (
72
), we have

S(\rho(T(u))_{\lambda}(S(v)))-\rho(T(u))_{\lambda}(S^{2}(v))-S(\rho((T\circ S)(u))_{\lambda}(v))+\rho((T\circ S)(u))_{\lambda}(S(v))=0.

By the fact that
N\circ T=(T_{1}\circ T^{-1})\circ T=T_{1}=T\circ S
, we have

S(\rho(T(u))_{\lambda}(S(v)))-\rho(T(u))_{\lambda}(S^{2}(v))-S(\rho(N\circ T)(u))_{\lambda}(v))+\rho((N\circ T)(u))_{\lambda}(S(v))=0.

Since
T
is invertible and let
a=T(u)
, we have

S(\rho(a)_{\lambda}(S(v)))-\rho(a)_{\lambda}(S^{2}(v))-S(\rho(N(a))_{\lambda}(v))+\rho(N(a))_{\lambda}(S(v))=0.

Thus
(N,S)
is a Nijenhuis structure on an
\mathsf{LCMod}
pair
(A;\rho)
.

(i) It is obvious that
N\circ T=T\circ S
. By (
70
), we have

\begin{split}[u_{\lambda}v]_{S}^{T}-[u_{\lambda}v]^{T\circ S}&=\rho(T(u))_{\lambda}(S(v))-\rho(T(v))_{-\lambda-\partial}(S(u))-S(\rho(T(u))_{\lambda}(v)-\rho(T(v))_{-\lambda-\partial}(u))\\
&=0,\end{split}

which implies that
[u_{\lambda}v]_{S}^{T}=[u_{\lambda}v]^{T\circ S}
. Therefore,
(T,S=T^{-1}\circ T_{1},N=T_{1}\circ T^{-1})
is an
\mathrm{\mathcal{O}N}
-structure on the
\mathsf{LCMod}
pair
(A,[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot];\rho)
.

(ii) It is obvious that
T_{1}\circ S=N\circ T_{1}
. By (
72
), we have

\begin{split}&[u_{\lambda}v]_{S}^{T_{1}}-[u_{\lambda}v]^{T_{1}\circ S}\\
=&\rho(T_{1}(u))_{\lambda}(S(v))-\rho(T_{1}(v))_{-\lambda-\partial}(S(u))-S(\rho(T_{1}(u))_{\lambda}(v)-\rho(T_{1}(v))_{-\lambda-\partial}(u))\\
=&\rho((T\circ S)(u))_{\lambda}(S(v))-\rho((T\circ S)(v))_{-\lambda-\partial}(S(u))-S(\rho((T\circ S)(u))_{\lambda}(v)-\rho((T\circ S)(v))_{-\lambda-\partial}(u))\\
=&0,\end{split}

which implies that
[u_{\lambda}v]_{S}^{T_{1}}=[u_{\lambda}v]^{T_{1}\circ S}
. Thus
(T_{1},S=T^{-1}\circ T_{1},N=T_{1}\circ T^{-1})
is an
\mathrm{\mathcal{O}N}
-structure on the
\mathsf{LCMod}
pair
(A,[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot];\rho)
.

Proposition 4.16
.

Let
(T,N,S)
be an
\mathrm{\mathcal{O}N}
-structure on an
\mathsf{LCMod}
pair
(A;\rho)
. If
T
is invertible, then
(T,N^{k},S^{k})
and
(T_{k}=N^{k}\circ T,N^{k},S^{k})
are
\mathrm{\mathcal{O}N}
-structures for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Since
(T,N,S)
is an
\mathrm{\mathcal{O}N}
-structure on the
\mathsf{LCMod}
pair
(A;\rho)
, by Proposition
4.14
,
T
and
T_{k}=N^{k}\circ T
are compatible
\mathcal{O}
-operators. Then by the condition that
T
is invertible and Theorem
4.15
, the conclusion follows.

5.
Conformal
r
-matrix-Nijenhuis structures and symplectic-Nijenhuis structures on Lie conformal algebras

In this section, we introduce the notions of conformal
r
-matrix-Nijenhuis structure and symplectic-Nijenhuis structure on a Lie conformal algebra. Furthermore, we study their connections with
\mathrm{\mathcal{O}N}
-structures on
\mathsf{LCMod}
pairs.

5.1.
Conformal
r
-matrix-Nijenhuis structures on Lie conformal algebras

Let
V
be free and of finite rank as a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module. Then
V\cong{({V^{*}}^{c})^{*}}^{c}
through the
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module homomorphism
v\mapsto v^{**},
where
v^{**}
is given by

v^{**}_{\lambda}(\alpha)=\alpha_{-\lambda}(v),\quad\forall~\alpha\in{V^{*}}^{c}.

Suppose
A
is a finite Lie conformal algebra which is free as a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module. Then we have

A\otimes A\cong A^{\ast c\ast c}\otimes A\cong{\rm Chom}(A^{\ast c},A).

Define

\langle\alpha,a\rangle_{\lambda}=\alpha_{\lambda}(a),\quad and\quad\langle\alpha\otimes\beta,a\otimes b\rangle_{(\lambda,\mu)}=\langle\alpha,a\rangle_{\lambda}\langle\beta,b\rangle_{\mu},

where
a,b\in A
and
\alpha,\beta\in A^{*c}
. Then for any
r\in A\otimes A
with
r=\sum_{i}a_{i}\otimes b_{i}\in A\otimes A
, we associate a conformal linear map
r^{\sharp}\in{\rm Chom}(A^{\ast c},A)
as follows:

(73)

r^{\sharp}_{\lambda}(\alpha)=\sum_{i}\langle\alpha,a_{i}\rangle_{-\lambda-\partial}b_{i},\quad\forall~\alpha\in A^{\ast c},

which is equivalent to

(74)

\langle\beta,r^{\sharp}_{-\mu-\partial}(\alpha)\rangle_{\lambda}=\langle\alpha\otimes\beta,r\rangle_{(\mu,\lambda)},\quad\forall~\alpha,\beta\in A^{\ast c}.

Set
r^{21}=\sum_{i}b_{i}\otimes a_{i}
. We say
r
is
skew-symmetric
if
r=-r^{21}
.

We say that
r
is
non-degenerate
if
r_{\lambda}^{\sharp}=r^{\sharp}_{\lambda}|_{\lambda=0}
and
r_{0}^{\sharp}
is a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module isomorphism from
A^{\ast c}
to
A
. In this case, (
74
) becomes

(75)

\langle\beta,r_{0}^{\sharp}(\alpha)\rangle_{\lambda}=\langle\alpha\otimes\beta,r\rangle_{(-\lambda,\lambda)}\quad\forall~\alpha,\beta\in A^{\ast c}.

Definition 5.1
.

(
[
22
]
)

Let
A
be a Lie conformal algebra. An element
r\in A\otimes A
is called a
conformal
r
-matrix
if
\llbracket r,r\rrbracket\equiv 0~{\rm mod}~\partial^{\otimes^{3}}
in
A\otimes A\otimes A
, where
\partial^{\otimes^{3}}=\partial\otimes 1\otimes 1+1\otimes\partial\otimes 1+1\otimes 1\otimes\partial.

There is a close relationship between conformal
r
-matrices and
\mathcal{O}
-operators.

Lemma 5.2
.

(
[
16
]
)

Let
A
be a finite Lie conformal algebra. Then,
r
is a skew-symmetric conformal
r
-matrix if and only if
r^{\sharp}_{0}=r^{\sharp}_{\lambda}|_{\lambda=0}
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator on the
\mathsf{LCMod}
pair
(A;\mathrm{ad}^{*})
.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
r
be a skew-symmetric conformal
r
-matrix and
N:A\rightarrow A
a Nijenhuis operator on a finite Lie conformal algebra A. A pair
(r,N)
is called a
conformal
r
-matrix-Nijenhuis structure
on the Lie conformal algebra
A
if for all
\alpha,\beta\in A^{\ast c}
, the following conditions are satisfied:

(76)

\displaystyle{N\circ r_{\lambda}^{\sharp}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{r_{\lambda}^{\sharp}\circ N^{\ast}},

(77)

\displaystyle{[\alpha_{\lambda}\beta]^{N\circ r_{0}^{\sharp}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{[\alpha_{\lambda}\beta]_{N^{\ast}}^{r_{0}^{\sharp}}}.

There is a close relationship between conformal
r
-matrix-Nijenhuis structures and
\mathrm{\mathcal{O}N}
-structures on Lie conformal algebras.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
r
be a skew-symmetric conformal
r
-matrix and
N:A\rightarrow A
a Nijenhuis operator on a Lie conformal algebra
A
. If
(r,N)
is a conformal
r
-matrix-Nijenhuis structure on
A
, then
(r_{0}^{\sharp},N,S=N^{\ast})
is an
\mathrm{\mathcal{O}N}
-structure on the
\mathsf{LCMod}
pair
(A;\mathrm{ad}^{\ast})
.

Proof.

It is straightforward.

The following proposition shows that if the conformal
r
-matrix
r
is determined by
r_{0}^{\sharp}
, the conformal
r
-matrix-Nijenhuis structure is equivalent to the
\mathrm{\mathcal{O}N}
-structure on the
\mathsf{LCMod}
pair
(A;\mathrm{ad}^{\ast})
.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
r
be a skew-symmetric conformal
r
-matrix with
r_{\lambda}^{\sharp}=r_{0}^{\sharp}
and
N:A\rightarrow A
a Nijenhuis operator on a Lie conformal algebra
A
. Then
(r,N)
is a conformal
r
-matrix-Nijenhuis structure on
A
if and only if
(r_{0}^{\sharp},N,S=N^{\ast})
is an
\mathrm{\mathcal{O}N}
-structure on the
\mathsf{LCMod}
pair
(A;\mathrm{ad}^{\ast})
.

Proof.

Since
r_{\lambda}^{\sharp}=r_{0}^{\sharp}
,
{N\circ r_{\lambda}^{\sharp}}={r_{\lambda}^{\sharp}\circ N^{\ast}}
holds if and only if
{N\circ r_{0}^{\sharp}}={r_{0}^{\sharp}\circ N^{\ast}}
holds. The rest is straightforward.

Proposition 5.6
.

Assume that
(r,N)
is skew-symmetric conformal
r
-matrix-Nijenhuis structure on
A
and set
r=\sum_{i}a_{i}\otimes b_{i}
. Then
r_{N}=\sum_{i}a_{i}\otimes N(b_{i})
is skew-symmetric. Furthermore,
r
and
r_{N}
are compatible conformal
r
-matrices in the sense that any linear combination of
r
and
r_{N}
is still a conformal
r
-matrix.

Proof.

By the fact that the Lie conformal algebra
A
is finite,
r
is skew-symmetric if and only if

\langle\beta\otimes\alpha,r\rangle_{(\mu,\lambda)}=-\langle\alpha\otimes\beta,r\rangle_{(\lambda,\mu)},\quad\forall~\alpha,\beta\in A^{*c},

which is also equivalent to

\langle\alpha,r^{\sharp}_{-\mu-\partial}(\beta)\rangle_{\lambda}=-\langle\beta,r^{\sharp}_{-\lambda-\partial}(\alpha)\rangle_{\mu}.

Furthermore, by (
74
) and
{N\circ r_{\lambda}^{\sharp}}={r_{\lambda}^{\sharp}\circ N^{\ast}}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\alpha\otimes\beta,r_{N}\rangle_{(\lambda,\mu)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\alpha\otimes N^{*}\beta,r\rangle_{(\lambda,\mu)}=\langle N^{*}\beta,r^{\sharp}_{-\mu-\partial}(\alpha)\rangle_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle\alpha,r^{\sharp}_{-\lambda-\partial}(N^{*}\beta)\rangle_{\mu}=-\langle\alpha,Nr^{\sharp}_{-\lambda-\partial}(\beta)\rangle_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle N^{*}\alpha,r^{\sharp}_{-\lambda-\partial}(\beta)\rangle_{\mu}=-\langle\beta\otimes N^{*}\alpha,r\rangle_{(\lambda,\mu)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle\beta\otimes\alpha,r_{N}\rangle_{(\mu,\lambda)},

which implies that
r_{N}
is skew-symmetric.

By Theorem
5.4
and Proposition
4.12
,
(r_{N}^{\sharp})_{0}=(r_{N}^{\sharp})_{\lambda}|_{\lambda=0}
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator on the
\mathsf{LCMod}
pair
(A;\mathrm{ad}^{*})
and, furthermore,
r^{\sharp}_{0}
and
(r_{N}^{\sharp})_{0}
are compatible. By Lemma
5.2
,
r_{N}
is a conformal
r
-matrix. It is straightforward to check that the
\mathcal{O}
-operators
r^{\sharp}_{0}
and
(r_{N}^{\sharp})_{0}
are compatible if and only if the conformal
r
-matrices
r
and
r_{N}
are compatible. The conclusion follows.

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
(r,N)
be a conformal
r
-matrix-Nijenhuis structure on a conformal algebra
A
and and set
r=\sum_{i}a_{i}\otimes a_{j}
. Then for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
r_{N^{k}}=\sum_{i}a_{i}\otimes N^{k}(b_{i})\in A\otimes A
are pairwise compatible skew-symmetric conformal
r
-matrices.

Proof.

Similar to the proof of Proposition
5.6
, the conclusion follows from Theorem
5.4
and Proposition
4.14
.

5.2.
Symplectic-Nijenhuis structures on Lie conformal algebras

Recall that a
2
-form on a Lie conformal algebra
A
is a
\mathbb{C}
-bilinear map
\omega:A\otimes A\rightarrow\mathbb{C}[\lambda]
satisfies the following conditions:

(78)

\displaystyle\{\partial a_{\lambda}b\}_{\omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\lambda\{a_{\lambda}b\}_{\omega},

(79)

\displaystyle\{a_{\lambda}\partial b\}_{\omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\{a_{\lambda}b\}_{\omega},

(80)

\displaystyle\{a_{\lambda}b\}_{\omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\{b_{\lambda}a\}_{\omega},\forall~a,b,c\in A.

A
2
-form
\omega
on a finite Lie conformal algebra
A
is called
non-degenerate
if
\omega^{\natural}
defined by

(81)

\{a_{\lambda}b\}_{\omega}=\langle\omega^{\natural}(a),b\rangle_{\lambda}

is a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module isomorphism from
A
to
A^{\ast c}
.

Recall that a
2
-cocycle on a Lie conformal algebra
A
with coefficients in the trivial module is a
2
-form
\omega
on
A
satisfying
\mathrm{d}^{T}\omega=0
, i.e.

(82)

\displaystyle\{a_{\lambda}[b_{\mu}c]\}_{\omega}-\{b_{\mu}[a_{\lambda}c]\}_{\omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{[a_{\lambda}b]_{\lambda+\mu}c\}_{\omega},\quad\forall~a,b,c\in A.

Definition 5.8
.

(
[
18
]
)

A
symplectic structure
on a Lie conformal algebra
(A,[\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot])
is a non-degenerate
2
-cocycle
\omega
on
A
.

Lemma 5.9
.

(
[
16
]
)

Let
A
be a Lie conformal algebra. Then
r\in A\otimes A
is a skew-symmetric and non-degenerate
r
-matrix if and only if the bilinear form
\omega
defined by

(83)

\{a_{\lambda}b\}_{\omega}=\langle(r_{0}^{\sharp})^{-1}(a),b\rangle_{\lambda},\quad a,b\in A

is a symplectic structure on
A
, where
r_{0}^{\sharp}\in Chom(A^{\ast c},A)
is given by (
75
).

By Lemmas
5.2
and
5.9
, we have

Corollary 5.10
.

Let
A
be a Lie conformal algebra and
\omega
a
2
-form on
A
. Then
\omega
is a symplectic structure on the Lie conformal algebra
A
if and only if
(\omega^{\natural})^{-1}:A^{\ast c}\rightarrow A
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator on the
\mathsf{LCMod}
pair
(A;\mathrm{ad}^{\ast})
.

Definition 5.11
.

Let
\omega
be a symplectic structure and
N
a Nijenhuis operator on a Lie conformal algebra
A
. Then
(\omega,N)
is called a
symplectic-Nijenhuis structure
on the Lie conformal algebra
A
if for all
a,b\in A
,

(84)

\{N(a)_{\lambda}b\}_{\omega}=\{a_{\lambda}N(b)\}_{\omega}

and
\omega_{N}:A\otimes A\rightarrow\mathbb{C}[\lambda]
defined by

(85)

\{a_{\lambda}b\}_{\omega_{N}}=\langle\omega^{\natural}(N(a)),b\rangle_{\lambda}

is also a
2
-cocycle.

Note that (
84
) implies that
\omega_{N}
is a
2
-form on the Lie conformal algebra
A
.

Recall that a
symplectic structure
on a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
is a nondegenerate
2
-cocycle
\omega\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, i.e.

\omega([x,y],z)+\omega([y,z],x)+\omega([z,x],y)=0,\quad\forall~x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Let
\omega
be a symplectic structure and
N
a Nijenhuis operator on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Recall that a
symplectic-Nijenhuis structure
on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is a pair
(\omega,N)
satisfying that for
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
,

\omega(N(x),y)=\omega(x,N(y))

and
\omega_{N}:\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
defined by

\omega_{N}(x,y)=\omega(N(x),y)

is also a
2
-cocycle on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.

Example 5.12
.

Let
(\omega,N)
be a symplectic-Nijenhuis structure on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}

(\tilde{\omega},\tilde{N})
is a symplectic-Nijenhuis structure on the current Lie conformal algebra
{\rm Cur}~\mathfrak{g}=\mathbb{C}[\partial]\otimes\mathfrak{g}
, where
\tilde{\omega}
is defined by

(86)

\{f(\partial)x_{\lambda}g(\partial)y\}_{\tilde{\omega}}=f(-\lambda)g(\lambda)\omega(x,y),\quad x,y\in\mathfrak{g},f(\partial),g(\partial)\in\mathbb{C}[\partial],

and
\tilde{N}
is given by (
35
).

Example 5.13
.

Given a Lie conformal algebra
A=\mathbb{C}[\partial]a\oplus\mathbb{C}[\partial]b
with the
\lambda
-bracket given by

[a_{\lambda}a]=(\partial+2\lambda)a,\quad[a_{\lambda}b]=(\partial+k\lambda+l)b,\quad[b_{\lambda}b]=0,\quad k,l\in\mathbb{C}.

Define
\rho:A\rightarrow{\rm gc}(A)
by

\rho(a)_{\lambda}(a)=(\partial+\lambda+m)a,\quad\rho(a)_{\lambda}(b)=(\partial+k\lambda+l)b,\quad\rho(b)_{\lambda}(a)=\rho(b)_{\lambda}(b)=0,

where
m\in\mathbb{C}
. It is straightforward to check that
\rho
gives a module of
A
over
A
. Consider the semi-direct Lie conformal algebra
D=A\ltimes_{\rho^{*}}A^{*c}
,
\omega
defined by

\displaystyle\{a_{\lambda}b\}_{\omega}=0,\quad\{a_{\lambda}a^{*}\}_{\omega}=-1,\quad\{a_{\lambda}b^{*}\}_{\omega}=0,\quad\{b_{\lambda}a^{*}\}_{\omega}=0,\quad\{b_{\lambda}b^{*}\}_{\omega}=-1

is a symplectic structure on the Lie conformal algebra
D
, where
\{a^{*},b^{*}\}
is the dual
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-basis of
A^{*c}
. Then
N:D\rightarrow D
defined by

N(a)=k_{1}a,\quad N(b)=k_{2}b,\quad N(a^{*})=k_{1}a^{*},\quad N(b^{*})=k_{2}b^{*}

is a Nijenhuis operator on the Lie conformal algebra
D
. Furthermore,
(\omega,N)
is a symplectic-Nijenhuis structure on the Lie conformal algebra
D
.

Example 5.14
.

Given a Lie conformal algebra
A=\mathbb{C}[\partial]a\oplus\mathbb{C}[\partial]b
with the
\lambda
-bracket given by

[a_{\lambda}a]=0,\quad[a_{\lambda}b]=\lambda a,\quad[b_{\lambda}b]=\partial b+2\lambda b.

Then
\omega
defined by

\displaystyle\{a_{\lambda}b\}_{\omega}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}b

is a symplectic structure on the Lie conformal algebra
A
. It is obvious that
N:A\rightarrow A
defined by

N(a)=k_{1}a,\quad N(b)=k_{1}b

is a Nijenhuis operator on the Lie conformal algebra
A
. Furthermore,
(\omega,N)
is a symplectic-Nijenhuis structure on the Lie conformal algebra
A
.

Proposition 5.15
.

If
(\omega,N)
is a symplectic-Nijenhuis structure on
A
, then for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\omega_{N^{k}}
is closed, i.e.
\mathrm{d}^{T}\omega_{N^{k}}=0
, where
\omega_{N^{k}}:A\otimes A\rightarrow\mathbb{C}[\lambda]
is defined by

(87)

\{a_{\lambda}b\}_{\omega_{N^{k}}}=\langle\omega^{\natural}(N^{k}(a)),b\rangle_{\lambda}.

Proof.

By a direct calculation, for
a,b,c\in A
, we have

\displaystyle\{a_{\lambda_{1}}b_{\lambda_{2}}c\}_{\mathrm{d}^{T}\omega_{N^{k+1}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{N(a)_{\lambda_{1}}b_{\lambda_{2}}c\}_{\omega_{N^{k}}}+\{a_{\lambda_{1}}N(b)_{\lambda_{2}}c\}_{\omega_{N^{k}}}-\{N(a)_{\lambda_{1}}N(b)_{\lambda_{2}}c\}_{\omega_{N^{k-1}}}

\displaystyle+\{\big(N\big([N(a)_{\lambda_{1}}(b)]+[a_{\lambda_{1}}N(b)]-N[a_{\lambda_{1}}b]\big)-[N(a)_{\lambda_{1}}N(b)]\big)_{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}}N^{k-1}(c)\}_{\omega}.

Since
N
is a Nijenhuis operator on the Lie conformal algebra
A
, the above equality implies

\{a_{\lambda_{1}}b_{\lambda_{2}}c\}_{\mathrm{d}^{T}\omega_{N^{k+1}}}=\{N(a)_{\lambda_{1}}b_{\lambda_{2}}c\}_{\omega_{N^{k}}}+\{a_{\lambda_{1}}N(b)_{\lambda_{2}}c\}_{\omega_{N^{k}}}-\{N(a)_{\lambda_{1}}N(b)_{\lambda_{2}}c\}_{\omega_{N^{k-1}}}.

By induction on
k
, the conclusion follows.

There is a close relationship between symplectic-Nijenhuis structures and
\mathrm{\mathcal{O}N}
-structures.

Theorem 5.16
.

Let
\omega:A\otimes A\rightarrow\mathbb{C}[\lambda]
be a non-degenerate
2
-form and
N:A\rightarrow A
a Nijenhuis operator on a finite Lie conformal algebra
A
. Then
(\omega,N)
is a symplectic-Nijenhuis structure on
A
if and only if
((\omega^{\natural})^{-1},N,S=N^{\ast})
is an
\mathrm{\mathcal{O}N}
-structure on the
\mathsf{LCMod}
pair
(A;\mathrm{ad}^{\ast})
.

Proof.

Assume that
(\omega,N)
is a symplectic-Nijenhuis structure on
A
. By (
80
), we have

\langle\omega^{\natural}(a),b\rangle_{\lambda}=-\langle\omega^{\natural}(b),a\rangle_{\lambda}.

Since
\omega
is non-degenerate, letting
a=(\omega^{\natural})^{-1}(\alpha)
and
b=(\omega^{\natural})^{-1}(\beta)
, we also have

(88)

\langle\alpha,(\omega^{\natural})^{-1}(\beta)\rangle_{\lambda}=-\langle\beta,(\omega^{\natural})^{-1}(\alpha)\rangle_{\lambda}.

By (
84
), letting
a=(\omega^{\natural})^{-1}(\alpha)
and
b=(\omega^{\natural})^{-1}(\beta)
, we have

\langle\omega^{\natural}(N(\omega^{\natural})^{-1}(\alpha)),(\omega^{\natural})^{-1}(\beta)\rangle_{\lambda}=-\langle\alpha,N((\omega^{\natural})^{-1}(\beta))\rangle_{\lambda}.

Furthermore, by (
88
), we have

\langle\alpha,(\omega^{\natural})^{-1}(N^{*}\beta)\rangle_{\lambda}=\langle\alpha,N((\omega^{\natural})^{-1}(\beta))\rangle_{\lambda},

which implies that
(\omega^{\natural})^{-1}\circ N^{*}=N\circ(\omega^{\natural})^{-1}
holds.

By Corollary
5.10
,
\omega
is a symplectic structure on the Lie conformal algebra
A
if and only if
(\omega^{\natural})^{-1}:A^{\ast c}\rightarrow A
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator on the
\mathsf{LCMod}
pair
(A;\mathrm{ad}^{\ast})
. In the following, we need to show that

(89)

[\alpha_{\lambda}\beta]^{N\circ(\omega^{\natural})^{-1}}=[\alpha_{\lambda}\beta]^{(\omega^{\natural})^{-1}}_{N^{*}},\quad\forall~\alpha,\beta\in A^{*c}.

By the fact that
\omega
is a
2
-cocycle, letting
a=(\omega^{\natural})^{-1}(\alpha)
and
b=(\omega^{\natural})^{-1}(\beta)
, we have

\displaystyle\langle[\alpha_{\lambda}\beta]^{N\circ(\omega^{\natural})^{-1}}-[\alpha_{\lambda}\beta]^{(\omega^{\natural})^{-1}}_{N^{*}},c\rangle_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{ad}^{*}((\omega^{\natural})^{-1}(\alpha))_{\lambda}N^{*}(\beta)-\mathrm{ad}^{*}((\omega^{\natural})^{-1}(\beta))_{-\lambda-\partial}N^{*}(\alpha)-N^{*}(\mathrm{ad}^{*}((\omega^{\natural})^{-1}(\alpha))_{\lambda}\beta)

\displaystyle+N^{*}(\mathrm{ad}^{*}((\omega^{\natural})^{-1}(\beta))_{-\lambda-\partial}\alpha),c\rangle_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{ad}^{*}(a)_{\lambda}N^{*}\omega^{\natural}(b)-\mathrm{ad}^{*}(b)_{-\lambda-\partial}N^{*}\omega^{\natural}(a)-N^{*}(\mathrm{ad}^{*}(a)_{\lambda}\omega^{\natural}(b))+N^{*}(\mathrm{ad}^{*}(b)_{-\lambda-\partial}\omega^{\natural}(a)),c\rangle_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{ad}^{*}(a)_{\lambda}N^{*}\omega^{\natural}(b)-\mathrm{ad}^{*}(b)_{\mu-\lambda}N^{*}\omega^{\natural}(a)-N^{*}(\mathrm{ad}^{*}(a)_{\lambda}\omega^{\natural}(b))+N^{*}(\mathrm{ad}^{*}(b)_{\mu-\lambda}\omega^{\natural}(a)),c\rangle_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\{b_{\mu-\lambda}N[a_{\lambda}c]\}_{\omega}+\{a_{\lambda}N[b_{\mu-\lambda}c]\}_{\omega}+\{b_{\mu-\lambda}[a_{\lambda}N(c)]\}_{\omega}-\{a_{\lambda}[b_{\mu-\lambda}N(c)]\}_{\omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\{b_{\mu-\lambda}N[a_{\lambda}c]\}_{\omega}+\{a_{\lambda}N[b_{\mu-\lambda}c]\}_{\omega}-\{[a_{\lambda}b]_{\mu}N(c)\}_{\omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\{N(b)_{\mu-\lambda}[a_{\lambda}c]\}_{\omega}+\{N(a)_{\lambda}[b_{\mu-\lambda}c]\}_{\omega}-\{N[a_{\lambda}b]_{\mu}c\}_{\omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\{b_{\mu-\lambda}[a_{\lambda}c]\}_{\omega_{N}}+\{a_{\lambda}[b_{\mu-\lambda}c]\}_{\omega_{N}}-\{[a_{\lambda}b]_{\mu}c\}_{\omega_{N}}.

Since
\omega_{N}
is a
2
-cocycle, (
89
) follows. Thus
((\omega^{\natural})^{-1},N,S=N^{\ast})
is an
\mathrm{\mathcal{O}N}
-structure on the
\mathsf{LCMod}
pair
(A;\mathrm{ad}^{\ast})
.

The converse can be proved similarly. We omit the details.

By Proposition
5.5
and Theorem
5.16
, we have

2

5.6

5.17

r

5.7

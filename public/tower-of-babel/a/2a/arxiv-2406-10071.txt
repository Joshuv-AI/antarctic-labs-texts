Right-preordered groups from a categorical perspective
Maria Manuel Clementino, Andrea Montoli

Abstract
. We study the categorical properties of right-preordered groups, giving an explicit description of limits and colimits in this category, and studying some exactness properties. We show that, from an algebraic point of view, the category of right-preordered groups shares several properties with the one of monoids. Moreover, we describe split extensions of right-preordered groups, showing in particular that semidirect products of ordered groups have always a natural right-preorder.

1.
Introduction

In
[
8
]
preordered groups have been studied from a categorical point of view. A preordered group is a group equipped with a preorder (i.e. a reflexive and transitive relation) which is compatible with the group operation both on the left and on the right, in the sense that the group operation is monotone with respect to such preorder. In particular, limits and colimits in the category
OrdGrp
of preordered groups and monotone group homomorphisms have been explicitly described, and some exactness properties of
OrdGrp
have been explored. From an algebraic point of view,
OrdGrp
does not share with the category
Grp
of groups the strong properties that allow the intrinsic interpretation of homological algebra, like being a protomodular
[
3
]
or a Mal’tsev
[
6
]
category. From this perspective,
OrdGrp
turns out to be more similar to the category
Mon
of monoids, where such properties hold only “locally”, i.e. for some “good” objects. In the case of
{\rm\bf OrdGrp}
, such good objects are those preordered groups whose preorder is symmetric (and hence an equivalence relation). Another interesting aspect of this relatively weak algebraic context is that, on a split extension of groups, whose kernel and codomain are preordered groups, there may be many compatible preorders, turning it to a split extension in
{\rm\bf OrdGrp}
, or none.

The wider class of the so-called right- (or left-)preordered groups is also interesting for applications in various mathematical fields. These are groups equipped with a preorder which is compatible with the group operation only on the right (or on the left): if
a\leq b
then, for all
c
,
a+c\leq b+c
(we are using the additive notation although our groups need not be abelian). In fact, right-orders on a group are related to actions of the group on the real line (see, e.g.,
[
13
,
7
]
). Moreover, right-ordered groups are used to give a description of the free lattice-ordered groups on given groups
[
12
]
. Following a similar spirit, in
[
11
]
spaces of right-orders on partially ordered groups have been related to spectral spaces of lattice-ordered groups. In
[
14
]
several important examples of preorders on groups that are compatible with the group operation only on one side are described.

The aim of this paper is to extend the study made in
[
8
]
to the category
ROrdGrp
whose objects are the right-preordered groups and whose arrows are the monotone group homomorphisms. In particular, we observe that
ROrdGrp
is isomorphic to the category whose objects are pairs
(G,M)
, where
G
is a group and
M
is a submonoid of
G
, and whose arrows are group homomorphisms that (co)restrict to the submonoids. Using the good properties of the forgetful functors from
ROrdGrp
to, on the one hand,
Grp
and, on the other hand, the category
Ord
of preordered sets and monotone maps, as well as the ones of the functor associating to every right-preordered group its positive cone, we give a description of limits and colimits in
{\rm\bf ROrdGrp}
, and we show that this category has the same exactness properties as
{\rm\bf OrdGrp}
. Moreover, we characterize the “good” objects, from an algebraic point of view, proving that they are still the groups equipped with a (right-compatible) equivalence relation. Finally, we explore the possible compatible right-preorders on split extensions, showing that, as for
OrdGrp
, all such preorders are bounded by the product preorder (a pair is positive if and only if both components are) and the so-called lexicographic preorder. We prove that the existence of a compatible right preorder is equivalent to the fact that the lexicographic one is compatible (extending a result of
[
10
]
). Using the semidirect product construction, we exhibit examples of split extensions which admit compatible right-preorders without admitting preorders that are compatible on both sides.

The category
ROrdGrp
of right-preordered groups

A
right-preordered group
is a group
X
together with a preorder (i.e. a reflexive and transitive relation)
\leq
such that

\forall\ x,y,z\in X,\qquad x\leq y\quad\Rightarrow\quad x+z\leq y+z.

A morphism of right-preordered groups is a monotone group homomorphism. We denote the category of right-preordered groups and their morphisms by
{\rm\bf ROrdGrp}
. We point out that, when the group is abelian, the seemingly weaker condition of being right-preordered coincides with being a preordered group.

A right-preorder on a group
X
determines a submonoid of
X
, namely
P_{X}=\{x\in X\,|\,x\geq 0\}
, also known as the
positive cone
of
X
.

Proposition 2.1
.

For a group
X
, right-preorders on
X
are in bijective correspondence with submonoids of
X
.

Proof.

If
\leq
is a right-preorder on
X
, then, if
x\geq 0
and
y\geq 0
,
x+y\geq 0+y\geq 0
, hence
P_{X}=\{x\in X\,|\,x\geq 0\}
is a submonoid of
X
.

Conversely, given a submonoid
M
of
X
, define
\leq
on
X
by
x\leq y
if
y-x\in M
. Then
\leq
is clearly reflexive and transitive:
x\leq y
and
y\leq z
implies
y-x\in M
and
z-y\in M
, hence
(z-y)+(y-x)=z-x\in M
, that is,
x\leq z
. Moreover, for every
x,y,z\in X
, if
y-x\in M
then
y+z-z-x=(y+z)-(x+z)\in M
; that is,
x\leq y
implies
x+z\leq y+z
as claimed.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

(1)

Topologicity of

(2)

Both

(3)

The forgetful functor

ROrdGrp
is a regular category.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(iv): according to Lemma
3.3
, we only need to show that
P_{Y}
is a gregarious monoid. Suppose there is an element
b\in P_{Y}
for which there are no

with

. Let
X=\langle b\rangle
be the subgroup of

generated by
b
, with the induced preorder, and
j\colon X\hookrightarrow Y
the inclusion. As a (right-)preordered group,
X
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
with its usual order, namely
P_{X}=\{nb\ |\ n\in\mathbb{N}\}
. Consider then the following right-hand side pullback in
{\rm\bf ROrdGrp}
:

\textstyle{Y\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\langle 1,0\rangle}
\textstyle{Y\times X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{1\times j}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{2}}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\langle j,1\rangle}
\scriptstyle{j}
\textstyle{Y\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\langle 1,0\rangle}
\textstyle{Y\times Y\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{2}}
\textstyle{Y.\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\langle 1,1\rangle}

We show that the positive cone of
Y\times X
(which is
P_{Y}\times P_{X}
) contains strictly the submonoid
P
of
Y\times X
generated by
\langle 1,0\rangle(P_{Y})
and
\langle j,1\rangle(P_{X})
; this would prove that the upper point in the pullback above is not strong, contradicting the assumption. In particular, we show that
(0,b)\notin P
. The elements of
P
are of the form

(y_{1},0)+(n_{1}b,n_{1}b)+(y_{2},0)+(n_{2}b,n_{2}b)+\ldots+(y_{k},0)+(n_{k}b,n_{k}b)

for some

,

. If

, then we should have

\begin{cases}y_{1}+n_{1}b+y_{2}+n_{2}b+\ldots+y_{k}+n_{k}b=0\\

but this is possible only if there is a unique

such that

, while all the others

’s are

. So we would get

y_{1}+y_{2}+\ldots+y_{i}+b+y_{i+1}+\ldots+y_{k}=0,

which is against our assumption on
b
.
∎

4.
Split extensions in
{\rm\bf ROrdGrp}

Any split extension

(4.i)

\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{k}
\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{p}
\textstyle{B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{s}

in
{\rm\bf ROrdGrp}
is in particular a split extension in
{\rm\bf Grp}
. Hence
A
, as a group, is isomorphic to the semidirect product
X\rtimes_{\varphi}B
w.r.t.
the action
\varphi
of
B
on
X
given by
\varphi_{b}(x)=k^{-1}(s(b)+k(x)-s(b))
. Furthermore, in
{\rm\bf Grp}
the split extension (
4.i
) is isomorphic to
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\langle 1,0\rangle}
\textstyle{X\rtimes_{\varphi}B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{B}}
\textstyle{B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\langle 0,1\rangle}
. Hence every split extension (
4.i
) in
{\rm\bf ROrdGrp}
is isomorphic to a split extension of the form

(4.ii)

\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\langle 1,0\rangle}
\textstyle{X\rtimes_{\varphi}B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{B}}
\textstyle{B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\langle 0,1\rangle}

where
X\rtimes_{\varphi}B
is equipped with a preorder which makes (
4.ii
) a split extension in
{\rm\bf ROrdGrp}
. We call such
right-preorders

compatible
. We already know from
[
8
, Section 5]
that, given a split extension (
4.ii
) in
{\rm\bf Grp}
with
X
and
B
preordered groups, there may be more than one compatible preorder in
X\rtimes_{\varphi}B
, and there may be none. It follows immediately that in
{\rm\bf ROrdGrp}
there is no uniqueness of compatible right-preorders. We will see next that, analogously, existence is not guaranteed.

As in
{\rm\bf OrdGrp}
(see
[
8
,
10
]
for details), the positive cone
P
of a compatible right-preorder must contain

P_{\rm prod}=P_{X}\times P_{B}=\{(x,b)\in X\rtimes_{\varphi}B\,|\,x\geq 0\mbox{ and }b\geq 0\},

and must be contained in

P_{\rm lex}=\{(x,b)\in X\rtimes_{\varphi}B\,|\,b>0\mbox{ or }(b\sim 0\mbox{ and }x\geq 0)\}.

Proposition 4.1
.

If
P
is a compatible cone in
X\rtimes_{\varphi}B
, then
P_{\rm prod}\subseteq P\subseteq P_{\rm lex}
.

Proof.

The morphism
\langle 1,0\rangle\colon X\to X\rtimes_{\varphi}B
is a kernel in
{\rm\bf ROrdGrp}
if and only if
(x\in P_{X}\,\Leftrightarrow\,(x,0)\in P)
, while
\langle 0,1\rangle\colon B\to X\rtimes_{\varphi}B
is monotone if and only if
(0,b)\in P
whenever
b\in P_{B}
. Hence
P_{\rm prod}\subseteq P
. Moreover, monotonicity of
\pi_{B}
gives that
b\in P_{B}
whenever
(x,b)\in P
. If
b\sim 0
then
-b\in P_{B}
, and so
(0,-b)\in P
; therefore, if
(x,b)\in P
, then
(x,0)=(x,b)+(0,-b)\in P
, hence
x\in P_{X}
.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

For a split extension (
4.ii
) in
{\rm\bf Grp}
with
(X,P_{X})
and
(B,P_{B})
right-preordered groups, the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

P_{\rm lex}
is a compatible right-preorder;

(ii)

there is a compatible right-preorder
P
;

(iii)

(\forall b\in P_{B})\;(\exists b^{\prime}\in P_{B}\;b+b^{\prime}\sim 0)\Rightarrow\varphi_{b}\mbox{ monotone}
.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) is obvious.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii): suppose
b,b^{\prime}\in P_{B}
, and let
x\in P_{X}
; then
(0,b)+(x,b^{\prime})=(\varphi_{b}(x),b+b^{\prime})
must belong to
P
. If
b+b^{\prime}\sim 0
then necessarily
\varphi_{b}(x)\geq 0
as claimed.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(i): we need to show that
P_{\rm lex}
is a submonoid of
X\rtimes_{\varphi}B
; let
(x,b),(y,b^{\prime})\in P_{\rm lex}
; then
(x,b)+(y,b^{\prime})=(x+\varphi_{b}(y),b+b^{\prime})
belongs trivially to
P_{\rm lex}
when
b+b^{\prime}\not\sim 0
; if
b+b^{\prime}\sim 0
then, by (iii),
\varphi_{b}(y)\geq 0
and consequently
x+\varphi_{b}(y)\geq\varphi_{b}(y)\geq 0
.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

If
B
is a right-ordered group (i.e., with an antisymmetric preorder), then the right-preorder
P_{\rm lex}
is compatible.

One can also identify the split extensions admitting
P_{\rm prod}
as a compatible cone.

Proposition 4.4
.

For a split extension (
4.ii
) in
{\rm\bf Grp}
, with
(X,P_{X})
and
(B,P_{B})
right-preordered groups, the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

P_{\rm prod}
is a compatible right-preorder.

(ii)

(\forall b\in P_{B})\;\varphi_{b}
is monotone.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii): If
b\in P_{B}
and
x\in P_{X}
, then
(0,b)+(x,0)=(\varphi_{b}(x),b)\in P_{\rm prod}
, hence
\varphi_{b}(x)\in P_{X}
and therefore
\varphi_{b}
is monotone.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i): Given
x,y\in P_{X}
and
a,b\in P_{B}
,
(x,a)+(y,b)=(x+\varphi_{a}(y),a+b)\in P_{\rm prod}
since
a+b\in P_{B}
and
x+\varphi_{b}(y)\in P_{X}
by (ii).
∎

Remark 4.5
.

We do not know whether
(b\geq 0\;\wedge\;\exists b^{\prime}\geq 0\;b+b^{\prime}\sim 0)
is equivalent to
b\sim 0
. This is the case when
B
is a preordered group.

A comparison of Corollary
4.3
above with Theorem 3.2 of
[
10
]
gives us the following

Corollary 4.6
.

When
B
is an ordered group and
\varphi_{b}
is not monotone for some
b\in B
, then
P_{\rm lex}
– and every possible compatible right-preorder on
X\rtimes_{\varphi}B
– makes it a right-preordered group but not a preordered one.

Examples
4.7
(2,3) below are concrete instances of the situation described in the previous corollary.

Examples 4.7
.

(1)

As shown in
[
8
, Example 5.8]
, the split extension

\textstyle{(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{N})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\langle 1,0\rangle}
\textstyle{\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{2}}
\textstyle{(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{N})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\langle 0,1\rangle}

has an uncountable number of compatible (right-)preorders (here every right-preorder is a preorder on

since the group is abelian).

(2)

Consider the split extension

(4.iii)

\textstyle{(\mathbb{Z},P)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\langle 1,0\rangle}
\textstyle{\mathbb{Z}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathbb{Z}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

with

When

.

When

(3)

We can extend the previous situation, providing plenty of examples of right-(pre)ordered groups which are not (pre)ordered. Let us describe explicitly some of them. We recall that an action of a group
B
on a group
X
can be expressed as a group homomorphism from
B
to the automorphism group
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
. When
X
is the additive group of rationals,
\mathrm{Aut}(X)=(\mathbb{Q}\setminus\{0\},\cdot)
. Hence, an action of
\mathbb{Z}
on
\mathbb{Q}
, namely a group homomorphism
\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Q}\setminus\{0\}
, is uniquely determined by the image of
1
, i.e. by a non-zero rational number

. Let us then consider the split extension

\textstyle{(\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{Q}^{+})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\langle 1,0\rangle}

\textstyle{(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{N})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

where
\varphi

, i.e.
\varphi_{b}(x)=q^{b}x
. The lexicographic preorder (actually, order) on
\mathbb{Q}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathbb{Z}
is always right-compatible, since
(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{N})
is an ordered group (i.e. the preorder is antisymmetric), but when

5.
Further comments

The passage from preordered groups to right-preordered groups as outlined here can be carried out to the more general context of
V
-groups, when
V
is a commutative and unital quantale, as studied in
[
9
]
. We take this opportunity to point out that in the statement (ii) of Proposition 3.1 of our paper
[
9
]
a condition is missing. Indeed, a
V
-group is a group which has a
V
-category structure both left- and right-invariant under shifting, and this is the notion studied in
[
9
]
. Condition (ii) of Proposition 3.1 of
[
9
]
gives a notion of right-invariant
V
-group, or simply right-
V
-group, that may be interesting to explore in future work.

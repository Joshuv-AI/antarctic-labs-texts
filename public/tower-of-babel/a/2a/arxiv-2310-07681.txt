Murmurations
Nina Zubrilina

Abstract
We establish a case of the surprising correlation phenomenon observed in the recent works of He, Lee, Oliver, Pozdnyakov, and Sutherland between Fourier coefficients of families of modular forms and their root numbers.

This discovery stemmed from the use of machine learning and computational techniques and did not explain the mathematical source of this phenomenon, referred to as "murmurations" due to its visual similarity to bird flight patterns:

The goal of this paper is to establish this bias in families of modular forms of square-free level with arbitrary fixed weight and root number. We show the following:

Theorem 1
.

Let
H^{\text{new}}(N)
be a Hecke basis for trivial character weight
k
cusp newforms for
\Gamma_{0}(N)
with
f\in H^{\text{new}}(N)
normalized to have lead coefficient
1
. Let
\varepsilon(f)
be the root number of
f

a_{f}(p)
be the

f
, and let
\lambda_{f}(p):=a_{f}(p)/p^{(k-1)/2}

and
P
be parameters going to infinity with
P
prime; assume further that that
Y=(1+o(1))X^{1-\delta_{2}}
and
P\ll X^{1+\delta_{1}}
for some
\delta_{2},\delta_{1}>0
with
2\delta_{1}<\delta_{2}<1
. Let
y:=P/X
. Then:

\displaystyle\frac{\sum^{\square}_{\begin{subarray}{I}N\in[X,X+Y]\end{subarray}}\sum_{f\in H^{\text{new}}(N)}\lambda_{f}(P)\sqrt{P}\varepsilon(f)}{\sum^{\square}_{\begin{subarray}{I}N\in[X,X+Y]\end{subarray}}\sum_{f\in H^{\text{new}}(N,k)}1}

\displaystyle=D_{k}A\sqrt{y}+(-1)^{k/2-1}D_{k}B\sum_{1\leq r\leq 2\sqrt{y}}c(r)\sqrt{4y-r^{2}}U_{k-2}\left(\frac{r}{2\sqrt{y}}\right)

\displaystyle-D_{k}\delta_{k=2}\pi y+\mathrm{O}_{\varepsilon}\left(X^{-\delta^{\prime}+\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{P}\right)

where
U_{k-2}
is the Chebyshev polynomial given by

U_{n}(\cos\theta):=\frac{\sin((n + 1) \theta)}{\sin\theta},

\delta^{\prime}:=\min\{\delta_{2}/2-\delta_{1},1/9+\delta_{2}/9-\delta_{1}\},

A:=\prod_{p}\left(1+\frac{p}{(p+1)^{2}(p-1)}\right),

B:=\prod_{p}\frac{p^{4}-2p^{2}-p+1}{(p^{2}-1)^{2}},

D_{k}:=\frac{12}{(k-1)\pi\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{2}+p}\right)},

c(r):=\prod_{p|r}\left(1+\frac{p^{2}}{p^{4}-2p^{2}-p+1}\right),

and
\sum^{\square}
denotes a sum over square-free parameters. In particular, for any
\delta_{1}<2/9
, one can find
\delta_{2}
for which
\delta^{\prime}>0

We define

M_{k}(y):=D_{k}A\sqrt{y}+(-1)^{k/2-1}D_{k}B\sum_{1\leq r\leq 2\sqrt{y}}c(r)\sqrt{4y-r^{2}}U_{k-2}\left(\frac{r}{2\sqrt{y}}\right)-D_{k}\delta_{k=2}\pi y

to be the weight
k

The formula above arises from an application of the Eichler-Selberg trace formula to the composition of Hecke and Atkin-Lehner operators, which allows us to reinterpret the sum in terms of class numbers. We then compute class number averages in short intervals by means of the class number formula.

We remark that the exponents in the statement are far from optimal, as our goal here is only to get a power saving error term in a range up to
X^{a}
for some

. The restriction to square-free levels is a technical one, as the trace formula simplifies greatly when the level is square-free. From the computations of Sutherland, it appears that the resulting density functions are slightly perturbed when one considers all levels, but they share key properties with the ones above.

.

Let

Theorem 4
.

Let

Murmurations are a feature of the the one-level density transition range. The Katz-Sarnak philosophy (
[
6
]
) predicts that averages as in Theorem

behave differently when

and

P\sim N
describe the phase transition between these. The unusual (for
k>2
) normalization of the coefficients above also arises naturally from this interpretation. For a more detailed discussion of this connection, we point the reader to
[
11
]
.

Computing similar averages weighted "harmonically" (i.e., by the value at
1
of the symmetric square
L

y

\sqrt{y}
, at the origin.

Murmurations for elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
are not explained by these results, as they constitute a very sparse subset of weight
2
modular forms. We point out that computational observations of the aforementioned authors make a compelling case that this phenomenon is very sensitive to the ordering by conductor, and disappears almost entirely when the curves are ordered by naive height,
j
-invariant, or discriminant.

Trace Formula Setup

Given a square-free positive integer
N
and a prime
P\nmid N
, let
H^{\text{new}}(N,k)
denote a basis of the space
S^{\text{new}}(N,k)
of weight
k

\Gamma_{0}(N)
, and let
a_{f}(P)=\lambda_{f}(P)P^{(k-1)/2}
denote the eigenvalue under the
P

denote the root number of

(recall

under the Atkin-Lehner involution

\sum_{f\in H^{\text{new}}(N,k)}a_{f}(P)\varepsilon(f)
as the trace of the operator
(-1)^{k/2}T_{p}\circ W_{N}
on

and apply the corresponding trace formula.

Such a trace formula was first derived by Yamauchi in
[
16
]
; the result contained a computational error which was later corrected by Skoruppa and Zagier (
[
12
]
). This formula (section
2
, formula (
7

on the full space of cusp forms

; as the authors point out in the discussion leading to formula
(5)
, oldforms coming from

contribute to the trace only when
N/M
is a square. Since we are restricting ourselves to
N

Theorem

(Skoruppa-Zagier (
[
12
]
, section
2
, formulas (

) and (

))
.

For

,

\displaystyle\sum_{f\in H^{\text{new}}(N,k)}\sqrt{P}\lambda_{f}(P)\varepsilon(f)

\displaystyle=\frac{H_{1}(-4PN)}{2}+(-1)^{k/2-1}U_{k-2}\left(\frac{r\sqrt{N}}{2\sqrt{P}}\right)\sum_{0<r\leq 2\sqrt{P/N}}H_{1}(r^{2}N^{2}-4PN)

\displaystyle-\delta_{k=2}(P+1).

Here the Hurwitz class number
H_{1}(-d)
is the number of equivalence classes with respect to

of positive-definite binary quadratic forms of discriminant
-d
weighed by the number of automorphisms (i.e., with forms corresponding to multiples of

and

with the error term disappearing if

.

Assume from now on that

. The square factors of

are
1
and

\displaystyle\sum_{f\in H^{\text{new}}(N)}\sqrt{P}\lambda_{f}(P)\varepsilon(f)

\displaystyle=\frac{h(-4PN)}{2}+\frac{h(-PN)}{2}-\delta_{k=2}P+\mathrm{O}(1).

\displaystyle+(-1)^{k/2-1}U_{k-2}\left(\frac{r\sqrt{N}}{2\sqrt{P}}\right)\sum_{1\leq r\leq 2\sqrt{\frac{P}{N}}}\sum_{d^{2}|r^{2}N-4P}h(N(r^{2}N-4P)/d^{2}).

(1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Res}_{s=1}\mathcal{L}_{\chi}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\zeta(2)}\prod_{p|m}\frac{1}{1+1/p}

Since there are no other poles to the right of the line
1/2
, shifting the contour to the line
\real(s)=1/2+\varepsilon,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{I}N\leq Z\end{subarray}}\mu^{2}(N)\chi(N)

\displaystyle=\frac{Z}{\zeta(2)}\prod_{p|m}\frac{p}{p+1}+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{L}_{\chi}(s)\frac{Z^{s}}{s}ds+\mathrm{O}_{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{Z^{1+\varepsilon}}{T}\right),

where the integration contour
\mathcal{C}
as shown in Figure
6(a)
. Similarly, if
\chi
is non-principal, there are no poles inside of the contour, and

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{I}N\leq Z\end{subarray}}\mu^{2}(N)\chi(N)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{L}_{\chi}(s)\frac{Z^{s}}{s}ds+\mathrm{O}_{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{Z^{1+\varepsilon}}{T}\right).

It remains to bound the contour integral terms.

(a)

Integration contour
\mathcal{C}

From the Euler product expansion for
L(s,\chi)
it is immediate that for
s=\sigma+it,\sigma>1
, one has
\absolutevalue{L(s, \chi)}\geq\zeta(2\sigma)/\zeta(\sigma)
, and thus
\absolutevalue{1/L(2s, \chi^2)}\ll_{\varepsilon}1
everywhere on the above contour. Moreover, from the above discussion, we also have
H_{\chi}(s)\ll 1
. Hence

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{L}_{\chi}(s)\frac{Z^{s}}{s}ds\ll_{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathcal{C}}\absolutevalue{L(s, \chi))}\frac{Z^{s}}{s}ds.

Next, let
\chi^{\prime}
be the the real primitive character of modulus
m_{0}<m
that gives rise to
\chi
. For
\sigma>0
,

L(s,\chi)=L(s,\chi^{\prime})\prod_{p|m}(1+\chi^{\prime}(p)p^{-s})

and thus for
s\in\mathcal{C}
,

\absolutevalue{L(s, \chi)}\leq\absolutevalue{ L(s, \chi')}\prod_{p|m}(1+p^{-1/2-\varepsilon})\ll m^{\varepsilon}\absolutevalue{ L(s, \chi')}

From result of Davenport (
[
2
]
) that for
\chi^{\prime}
primitive and
s=\sigma+it
,
\sigma\in[0,1]
, we have

\absolutevalue{L(s, \chi')}\ll((\absolutevalue{t}+1)m_{0})^{1/2-\sigma/2}

Thus,

\int_{1/2+\varepsilon-iT}^{1/2+\varepsilon+iT}\absolutevalue{L(s, \chi')) \frac{Z^s}{s}}ds\ll Z^{1/2+\varepsilon}m^{1/4-\varepsilon/2}\int_{-T}^{T}\frac{t^{1/4-\varepsilon/2}}{\absolutevalue{t}+1}dt\ll Z^{1/2+\varepsilon}\int_{1}^{T}t^{-3/4-\varepsilon}dt\ll m_{0}^{1/4-\varepsilon/2}Z^{1/2+\varepsilon}T^{1/4-\varepsilon}

Next, let
\sigma\geq 1
and
s=\sigma+it,t>1
, and let

A(u):=\sum_{n\leq u}\chi^{\prime}(n).

If
\chi
is principal (i.e., if
L(s,\chi^{\prime})=\zeta(s)
) one has
\absolutevalue{L(s, \chi')}\ll\log\absolutevalue{t}
. Indeed, for an integer
N=\lfloor T\rfloor
, Abel summation gives

\zeta(s)=\sum_{n\leq N}n^{-s}-N^{s-1}+\int_{N}^{\infty}\frac{su}{u^{s+1}}du-\int_{N}^{\infty}\frac{s\{u\}}{u^{s+1}}du=\sum_{n\leq N}n^{-s}+\mathrm{O}(1)+\mathrm{O}\left(T/N\right)=\mathrm{O}(\log T).

On the other hand, if
\chi
is non-principal, one has

\absolutevalue{A(u)}\ll\sqrt{m_{0}}\log m_{0}

by the Polya-Vinogradov inequality; hence, by partial summation,

\absolutevalue{\sum_{n \geq N} n^{-s} \chi'(n)}=\absolutevalue{-A(N) N^{-s} + \int_N^\infty\frac{s A(u)}{u^{s + 1}} d u}\ll\frac{\sqrt{m_{0}}\log m_{0}}{N}+\absolutevalue{t}\sqrt{m_{0}}\log m_{0}\int_{N}^{\infty}\frac{du}{u^{s+1}}\ll\frac{\absolutevalue{t}\sqrt{m_{0}}\log m}{N},

so setting
N=\sqrt{m_{0}}\absolutevalue{t}
, we see that

\absolutevalue{L(s, \chi')}\ll\absolutevalue{\sum_{n < N} \chi'(n) n^{-s}}+\absolutevalue{t}\sqrt{m_{0}}\log m_{0}/N\ll\log\absolutevalue{m_0t}.

To summarize,

\displaystyle\int_{1/2+\varepsilon+iT}^{1+\varepsilon+iT}\absolutevalue{L(s, \chi')) \frac{Z^s}{s} }ds

\displaystyle\ll\int_{1/2+\varepsilon}^{1}\frac{Z^{\sigma}}{T}(Tm_{0})^{1/2-\sigma/2}d\sigma+\log(Tm_0)\int_{1}^{1+\varepsilon}\frac{Z^{\sigma}}{T}d\sigma

\displaystyle\ll\frac{\sqrt{m_{0}}}{\sqrt{T}}\int_{1/2+\varepsilon}^{1}\left(\frac{Z}{\sqrt{Tm_{0}}}\right)^{\sigma}d\sigma+\frac{\varepsilon Z^{1+\varepsilon}\log m_{0}T}{T}

and similarly for the other horizontal contour. Finally, taking
T=Z^{2/5}/m_{0}^{1/5}
yields the desired error term.
∎

Lemma 6.8
.

One has

\sum_{n\leq Z}\mu^{2}(n)\varphi(n)=\frac{Z^{2}}{2\zeta(2)}\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{2}+p}\right)+\mathrm{O}(Z^{8/5+\varepsilon})

Proof.

Consider the associated Dirichlet series

\mathcal{L}_{\chi}(s):=\prod_{p}\left(1+\frac{p-1}{p^{2}}\right)=\frac{\zeta(s-1)}{\zeta(2s-2)}\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{s}+p}\right).

Applying Perron’s formula again,

\sum_{N\leq Z}\mu^{2}(N)\chi(N)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{2+\varepsilon-iT}^{2+\varepsilon+iT}\mathcal{L}_{\chi}(s)\frac{Z^{s}}{s}ds+\mathrm{O}_{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{Z^{1+\varepsilon}}{T}\right)

Shifting the contour to the line
\real(s)=3/2+\varepsilon
picks up the pole of
\zeta(s)
at
s=2
with residue agreeing with the main term, and no other poles. In the region
\real(s)\geq 3/2+\varepsilon
, the function
\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{s}+p}\right)
is bounded absolutely. Similarly,
\absolutevalue{1/\zeta(2s-2)}\ll_{\varepsilon}1
in this region. Hence it suffices to bound the contour integrals

\int_{1/2+\varepsilon-iT}^{1/2+\varepsilon+iT}\absolutevalue{\zeta(s)Z^{s+1}/s}ds
and
\int_{1/2+\varepsilon\pm iT}^{1+\varepsilon\pm iT}\absolutevalue{\zeta(s)Z^{s+1}/s}ds
. By the convexity bound we have
\zeta(1/2+\varepsilon+it)\ll(1+\absolutevalue{t})^{1/2-\sigma/2}
; hence, for the first integral

\int_{1/2+\varepsilon-iT}^{1/2+\varepsilon+iT}\absolutevalue{\zeta(s)Z^{s+1}/s}ds\ll Z^{3/2+\varepsilon}\int_{1}^{T}t^{1/4-\varepsilon/2-1}dt=Z^{3/2+\varepsilon}T^{1/4-\varepsilon/2}.

Similarly, on the vertical parts of the contour inside the critical strip, one has

\int_{1/2+\varepsilon+iT}^{1+iT}\absolutevalue{\zeta(s)Z^{s+1}/s}ds\ll\int_{1/2+\varepsilon}^{1}Z^{t+1}\frac{T^{1/2-t/2}}{T}dt\ll\frac{Z^{2}}{T}\int_{0}^{1/2}T^{-t/2}\ll Z^{2}/T,

and for
\sigma\geq 1
,
\zeta(s)=\mathrm{O}(\log T)
gives

\int_{1+iT}^{1+\varepsilon+iT}\absolutevalue{\zeta(s)Z^{s+1}/s}ds\ll\varepsilon Z^{2+\varepsilon}/T^{1-\varepsilon}.

Taking
T=Z^{2/5}
concludes the calculation.

∎

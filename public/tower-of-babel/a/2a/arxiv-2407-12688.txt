Abstract
. We focus on the permutation polynomials of the form L ⁡ ( X ) + Tr m 3 ​ m ​ ( X ) s L(X)+{\rm Tr}_{m}^{3m}(X)^{s} over 𝔽 q 3 {\mathbb{F}}_{q^{3}} , where 𝔽 q {\mathbb{F}}_{q} is the finite field with q = p m q=p^{m} elements, p p is a prime number, m m is a positive integer, Tr m 3 ​ m {\rm Tr}_{m}^{3m} is the relative trace function from 𝔽 p 3 ​ m {\mathbb{F}}_{p^{3m}} to 𝔽 p m {\mathbb{F}}_{p^{m}} , L ⁡ ( X ) L(X) is a linearized polynomial over 𝔽 q 3 {\mathbb{F}}_{q^{3}} , and s > 1 s>1 is a positive integer. 1.
Introduction

For a prime power
q=p^{m}
, let
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
denote the finite field with
q
elements,
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
the multiplicative cyclic group of all non-zero elements of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, and
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[X]
the ring of polynomials over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
in the indeterminate
X
. A polynomial
f(X)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[X]
is a permutation polynomial (PP) if the induced map
c\mapsto f(c)
is a bijection from
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
to itself. It may be noted that permutation polynomials were first studied by Hermite
[
6
]
over the finite fields of prime order. Later, Dickson
[
2
]
investigated PPs over arbitrary finite fields. For any PP
f(X)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[X]
, there exists a unique polynomial
f^{-1}(X)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[X]
modulo
X^{q}-X
such that
f^{-1}(f(X))\equiv X\pmod{X^{q}-X}
. The polynomial
f^{-1}
is referred to as the compositional inverse of
f
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. Finding PPs and their compositional inverses holds significant importance from both mathematical and practical perspectives. In fact, PPs and their compositional inverses have a wide range of applications in cryptography
[
17
,
18
]
, coding theory
[
3
,
7
]
, and combinatorial design theory
[
4
]
. In 1991, Mullen
[
16
]
posed a problem concerning the computation of the coefficients of the compositional inverses of permutation polynomials. The significance of explicitly determining the compositional inverses of PPs stems from the fact that PPs and their inverses have plethora of applications. For example, in a block cipher with a Substitution-Permutation Network (SPN) structure, the compositional inverse of the substitution box (S-box) is crucial throughout the decryption process. Generally, constructing PPs and determining their compositional inverses is a challenging problem that has garnered the attention of many researchers.

We now provide a brief account of the development and motivation behind the specific form of permutation polynomials that we are considering in this paper. In 2003, Helleseth and Zinoviev
[
5
]
studied PPs of the form
\left(\frac{1}{X^{2}+X+\delta}\right)^{2^{\ell}}+X
to establish new Kloosterman sums identities over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m}}
, where
\ell\in\{0,1\}
and
\delta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m}}
. Inspired by their work, Yuan and Ding
[
28
,
30
]
initiated an investigation for PPs of the form

f_{1}(X):=(X^{p^{i}}-X+\delta)^{s}+L(X)
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, where
i,s
are positive integers,
\delta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and
L(X):=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\alpha_{i}X^{p^{i}}
is a linearized polynomial in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[X]
. Following this, several classes of PPs of the form
f_{1}(X)
have been constructed in the literature
[
11
,
32
,
34
,
35
,
36
]
. The study of permutation polynomials of the shape
f_{1}(X)
may be divided into two specific types of PPs, namely,
f_{2}(X):=\left(X^{p^{i}}-X+\delta\right)^{s_{1}}+\left(X^{p^{i}}-X+\delta\right)^{s_{2}}+X
, and
f_{3}(X):=\left({\rm Tr}_{m}^{n}(X)+\delta\right)^{s}+L(X)
, where

are positive integers such that

and
\delta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. It turns out that Zeng, Zhu, Li, and Liu
[
33
]
proposed several classes of PPs of the form

over finite fields of even characteristic. Subsequently, Li, Wang, Li and Zeng
[
13
]
, as well as Liu, Xie, Liu and Zou
[
15
]
, investigated these types of PPs over finite fields of odd characteristic. In
[
31
]
, Zeng, Tian and Tu explored PPs of the form
f_{3}(X)
over finite fields of characteristic
2
, where
\delta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
,
L(X)={\rm Tr}_{m}^{n}(X)+X
or
X
,
and the exponent
s
satisfies either of the two conditions:

s(2^{m}+1)\equiv{2^{m}+1}~\pmod{2^{n}-1}
or
s(2^{m}-1)\equiv{2^{m}-1}\pmod{2^{n}-1}
.
Later, Li, Wang, Wu and Zhu
[
14
]
proposed many new classes of PPs of the forms

f_{4}(X):=\left({\rm Tr}_{m}^{2m}(X)^{k_{1}}+\delta\right)^{s_{1}}+\left({\rm Tr}_{m}^{2m}(X)^{k_{2}}+\delta\right)^{s_{2}}+X
, and
f_{5}(X):=\left({\rm Tr}_{m}^{2m}(X)^{k}+\delta\right)^{s}+X
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{2m}}
. Furthermore, Li and Cao
[
9

constructed several classes of PPs of the form
f_{1}(X)
and

with the parameter

over

. Recently, Li and Cao
[

introduced some classes of PPs of the form

and

over

with

Motivated by the work of several authors, as discussed above, we consider it worthwhile to explore these types of PPs further. Specifically, we focus our attention on the PPs studied by Zeng, Tian and Tu

2.
Preliminaries

This section consists of some basic definitions and an important result that will be used in the subsequent sections.

Definition 2.1
.

For two positive integers
m
and
n
with
m\mid n
, we use
{\rm Tr}_{m}^{n}(.)
to denote the (relative) trace function from
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}
to
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{m}}
, which is defined as follows

{\rm Tr}_{m}^{n}(X):=\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\frac{n-m}{m}}X^{p^{mi}}.

Definition 2.2
.

A polynomial of the form

L(X)=\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\alpha_{i}X^{q^{i}}

with coefficients in an extension field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is called
q
-linearized polynomial over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
.

Definition 2.3
.

For a prime
p
and for an integer
a\in\mathbb{Z}
, the
p
-adic valuation of
a
is defined as follows

\nu_{p}(a):=\max\{e\in\mathbb{Z}\mid p^{e}\;\mbox{divides}\;a\},

where
\mathbb{Z}
is the set of integers.

Definition 2.4
.

For an odd prime
p
and for
c\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
, we define the Legendre symbol as follows

\eta(c):=\left(\frac{c}{p}\right)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }c\text{ is quadratic residue modulo }p\text{ and }c\not\equiv 0\pmod{p},\\
-1&\text{if }c\text{ is nonquadratic residue modulo }p\text{ and }c\not\equiv 0\pmod{p},\\
0&\text{if }c\equiv 0\pmod{p}.\end{cases}

Definition 2.5
.

For a positive integer
d
, the unit circle of order
d
is denoted by
\mu_{d}
and is defined as

\mu_{d}:=\{X\in\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}:X^{d}=1\},

where
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}
is the algebraic closure of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.

The following lemma, which is crucial to this paper and will be frequently used in Section
3
, is stated below after defining some notations that will be used in its statement.

For positive integers
i
with
1\leq i\leq n
, let
m_{i}
’s be positive integers,
u_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
, and
a_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
with
a_{i}^{q^{n-1}+q^{n-2}+\cdots+q^{2}+q+1}=1
. We now introduce the following notations:

A_{i}(X):=X^{q^{n-1}}+a_{i}X^{q^{n-2}}+a_{i}^{1+q^{n-1}}X^{q^{n-3}}+\cdots+a_{i}^{1+q^{n-1}+q^{n-2}+\cdots+q^{2}}X,

D_{1}:=\begin{pmatrix}1&u_{2}&\cdots&u_{n}\\
a_{1}^{m_{1}q}&u_{2}^{q}a_{2}^{m_{2}q}&\cdots&u_{n}^{q}a_{1}^{m_{n}q}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
a_{1}^{m_{1}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}q^{i}}&u_{2}^{q^{n-1}}a_{2}^{m_{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}q^{i}}&\cdots&u_{n}^{q^{n-1}}a_{2}^{m_{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}q^{i}}\end{pmatrix},

1&a_{n}&a_{n}^{1+q^{n-1}}&\cdots&a_{n}^{1+q^{n-1}+q^{n-2}+\cdots+q^{n-j}}&\cdots&a_{n}^{1+q^{n-1}+q^{n-2}+\cdots+q^{2}}\end{pmatrix}.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let

3.
Permutation polynomials of the form

over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{3}}
for infinitely many positive integers

with the condition that

and we propose six such classes of PPs.

Now, we prove the following two lemmas which will be used in the proof of Theorem
3.4
. These two lemmas essentially give an exact count for the number of solutions in
\mu_{q^{2}+q+1}
of two special types of equations, where
\mu_{q^{2}+q+1}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{3}}
is the unit circle of order

. Unless otherwise stated, note that in Lemma

, Lemma

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
q=2^{m}
,
h(X)=X^{q+1}+(A+1)X+A\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{3}}[X]
with
A\neq 0
. Then

(1)

h(X)
has only one root in
\mu_{q^{2}+q+1}
if
A^{q+1}+A+1\neq 0
, and

(2)

h(X)
has all
q+1
roots in
\mu_{q^{2}+q+1}
if
A^{q+1}+A+1=0
,

where
\mu_{q^{2}+q+1}:=\{X\in{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q^{3}}:X^{q^{2}+q+1}=1\}.

Proof.

It is clear that
1
is always a root of
h(X)
in
\mu_{q^{2}+q+1}
.

Case 1.
First, we assume that
A^{q+1}+A+1\neq 0
. Now, if possible, suppose that
\alpha\in\mu_{q^{2}+q+1}\setminus\{1\}
is a root of
h(X)
, that is,

(3.1)

\alpha^{q+1}+(A+1)\alpha+A=0.

By raising
q
th power to Equation (
3.1
), we obtain

(3.2)

\alpha^{q^{2}+q}+(A^{q}+1)\alpha^{q}+A^{q}=0.

Now, multiply Equation (
3.2
) by
\alpha
, we get

\alpha^{q^{2}+q+1}+(A^{q}+1)\alpha^{q+1}+A^{q}\alpha=0.

Using the value of
\alpha^{q+1}
from Equation (
3.1
) in the above equation, we obtain

(A^{q+1}+A+1)\alpha+(A^{q+1}+A+1)=0.

Since
A^{q+1}+A+1\neq 0
, we get
\alpha=1
, which is a contradiction.

Case 2.
Now, suppose
A^{q+1}+A+1=0
and
\beta
is any root of
h(X)
, that is,

(3.3)

\beta^{q+1}+(A+1)\beta+A=0.

Since
A^{q+1}+A+1=0
implies
A^{q^{2}+q+1}=1
. Thus, Equation (
3.3
) can be written as

A^{q^{2}+q+1}\beta^{q+1}+A^{q+1}\beta+A=0

which implies

(A^{q}\beta)^{q+1}+A^{q}\beta+1=0,

as
A\neq 0.
So, we have
A^{q}\beta\in\mu_{q^{2}+q+1}
. Hence
\beta\in\mu_{q^{2}+q+1}
as
A\in\mu_{q^{2}+q+1}
, which completes the proof.

∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
q=2^{m}
,
m
be odd. Then
X^{q+1}+(A+1)X^{q}+A=0
has no solution in
\mu_{q^{2}+q+1}\setminus\{1\}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{3}}
, where
A\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
.

Proof.

Let
h(X)=X^{q+1}+(A+1)X^{q}+A
. It is clear that
h(1)=0
. Now, suppose
\alpha\in\mu_{q^{2}+q+1}\setminus\{1\}
such that
h(\alpha)=0
, that is,

(3.4)

\alpha^{q+1}+(A+1)\alpha^{q}+A=0.

We raise power
q^{2}
to Equation (

(3.5)

(A+1)\alpha^{q+1}+A\alpha^{q}+1=0.

Now by adding Equation (
3.4
) and Equation (
3.5

(3.6)

\alpha^{q+1}+\frac{\alpha^{q}}{A}+1+\frac{1}{A}=0.

We now add Equation (
3.4

\alpha^{q}(A+1+\frac{1}{A})+A+1+\frac{1}{A}=0

(3.7)

\alpha^{q}(A^{2}+A+1)+A^{2}+A+1=0.

Note that

Hence Equation (

∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let

be positive integers such that

,

(1)

if

,
f_{1}(X)
is a PP if and only if
a\neq 1
and
\gcd(s,q-1)=1
,

(2)

if

and if

f_{1}(X)
is a PP if and only if
a\neq 1
and
\gcd(s,q-1)=1
.

Proof.

We first express
f_{1}(X)
into the form as that of

given in Lemma
2.6
. Then the determinants
\det D_{1}
and

corresponding to

\det D_{2}=a^{(2^{k}+1)}a^{2^{k}q^{2}}+a+a^{2^{k}}+a^{(2^{k}+1)}a^{q^{2}}+a^{1+q^{2}}+a^{2^{k}}a^{2^{k}q^{2}}.

Case 1.
Let
q\equiv 2\pmod{3}
. Assume that
f_{1}(X)
is a PP over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{3}}
, we have to show that
a\neq 1
and
\gcd(s,q-1)=1
. If possible, suppose that
a=1
, we get
\det D_{1}=0
and
\det D_{2}=0
. Therefore, by Lemma
2.6
,
f_{1}(X)
is not a PP over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{3}}
. Now, if
\gcd(s,q-1)\neq 1
, then again by Lemma
2.6
,
f_{1}(X)
is not a PP over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{3}}
. Hence,

and

Conversely, suppose that
a\neq 1
and
\gcd(s,q-1)=1
. In view of Lemma
2.6
, it is enough to show that
\det D_{1}\neq 0
and
\det D_{2}\neq 0
. Suppose on the contrary, that
\det D_{1}=0
, that is,

a^{(2^{k}+1)q}a^{2^{k}q^{2}}+a^{q}+a^{2^{k}q}+a^{(2^{k}+1)q}a^{q^{2}}+a^{q+q^{2}}+a^{2^{k}q}a^{2^{k}q^{2}}=0.

By substituting

and

in the above expression, we obtain
x^{2^{k}}y^{2^{k}}+1+x^{2^{k}-1}+x^{2^{k}}y+y+x^{2^{k}-1}y^{2^{k}}=0
. We now add

on both sides so as to get

Further, since

and

\left(\frac{x(y+1)}{x+1}\right)^{2^{k}-1}=1.
Also,

is a

as

If

a^{q+q^{2}}=1
, which leads to a contradiction. Now, if

(3.8)

a^{q+1}+\frac{\omega}{\omega+1}a+\frac{1}{\omega+1}=0.

Thus, by letting

(3.9)

\mu_{q^{2}+q+1}\setminus\{1\}

\det D_{2}\neq 0

4.1.
Compositional inverses of permutation polynomials

As alluded to in the introduction, Mullen
[
16
]
proposed the problem of computing the coefficients of the compositional inverses of permutation polynomials. In block ciphers, a permutation is often used as an S-box to build the confusion layer during the encryption process and the inverse
is needed while decrypting the cipher. In general, it is not easy to obtain the explicit expression for compositional inverses of PPs, except for several well-known classes of PPs such as linearized polynomials
[
22
]
and Dickson polynomials
[
12
]
. The reader is referred to an excellent survey by Wang
[
21
]
on the various methods and their unification for computing compositional inverses of permutation polynomials. The problem of explicitly determining the compositional inverses of permutation polynomials is rather challenging. Here, we provide the coefficients of the compositional inverses of the permutation polynomials obtained in Section
3
.

The following proposition provides the compositional inverse for the permutation polynomial of the form given in Lemma

and can be gleaned from the proof of

Definition 4.2

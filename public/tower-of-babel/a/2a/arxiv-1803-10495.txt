Warped product pointwise bi-slant submanifolds of Kenmotsu manifolds
Shyamal Kumar Hui ∗ , Joydeb Roy and Tanumoy Pal

Abstract
. The present paper deals with the study of warped product pointwise bi-slant submanifolds of Kenmotsu manifolds with an example. The characterization for such submanifold is also discussed. An inequality of such submanifold is obtained and its equality case is also considered.

2.
Preliminaries

An odd dimensional smooth manifold
\bar{M}^{2m+1}
is said to be an almost contact metric manifold
[
5
]
if it admits a
(1,1)

tensor field
\phi
, a vector field
\xi
, an
1
-form
\eta
and a Riemannian metric
g
which satisfy

(2.1)

\phi\xi=0,\ \ \ \eta(\phi X)=0,\ \ \ \phi^{2}X=-X+\eta(X)\xi,

(2.2)

g(\phi X,Y)=-g(X,\phi Y),\ \ \ \eta(X)=g(X,\xi),\ \ \ \eta(\xi)=1,

(2.3)

g(\phi X,\phi Y)=g(X,Y)-\eta(X)\eta(Y)

for all vector fields
X,Y
on
\bar{M}
.

An almost contact metric manifold
\bar{M}^{2m+1}(\phi,\xi,\eta,g)
is said to be Kenmotsu manifold if the following conditions hold
[
23
]
:

(2.4)

\bar{\nabla}_{X}\xi=X-\eta(X)\xi,

(2.5)

(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi)(Y)=g(\phi X,Y)\xi-\eta(Y)\phi X,

where
\bar{\nabla}
denotes the Riemannian connection of
g
.

Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional submanifold of a Kenmotsu manifold
\bar{M}
. Throughout the paper we assume that
the submanifold
M
of
\bar{M}
is tangent to the structure vector field
\xi
.

Let
\nabla
and
\nabla^{\bot}
be the induced connections on the tangent bundle
TM
and the normal bundle
T^{\bot}M
of
M
respectively.
Then the Gauss and Weingarten formulae are given by

(2.6)

\bar{\nabla}_{X}Y=\nabla_{X}Y+h(X,Y)

and

(2.7)

\bar{\nabla}_{X}V=-A_{V}X+\nabla_{X}^{\bot}V

for all
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)
and
V\in\Gamma(T^{\bot}M)
, where
h
and
A_{V}
are second fundamental form and the shape operator
(corresponding to the normal vector field
V
) respectively for the immersion of
M
into
\bar{M}
. The second fundamental form

h
and the shape operator
A_{V}
are related by
g(h(X,Y),V)=g(A_{V}X,Y)

for any
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)
and
V\in\Gamma(T^{\bot}M)
, where g is the Riemannian metric on
\bar{M}
as well as on
M
.

The mean curvature
H
of
M
is given by
H=\frac{1}{n}\text{trace}\ h
. A submanifold of a Kenmotsu manifold
\bar{M}
is said to be totally umbilical if
h(X,Y)=g(X,Y)H
for any
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)
. If
h(X,Y)=0
for all
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)
, then
M
is totally geodesic and if
H=0
then
M
is minimal in
\bar{M}
.

Let
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}\}
be an orthonormal basis of the tangent bundle
TM
and
\{e_{n+1},\cdots,e_{2m+1}\}
an orthonormal basis of the normal bundle

T^{\bot}M
. We put

(2.8)

h_{ij}^{r}=g(h(e_{i},e_{j}),e_{r})\ \text{and}\ \ \|h\|^{2}=g(h(e_{i},e_{j}),h(e_{i},e_{j})),

for
r\in\{n+1,\cdots,2m+1\}
.

For a differentiable function
f
on
M
, the gradient
\boldsymbol{\nabla}f
is defined by

(2.9)

g(\boldsymbol{\nabla}f,X)=Xf,

for any
X\in\Gamma(TM)
. As a consequence, we get

(2.10)

\|\boldsymbol{\nabla}f\|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(e_{i}(f))^{2}.

For any
X\in\Gamma(TM)
and
V\in\Gamma(T^{\bot}M)
, we can write

(2.11)

\displaystyle\text{(a)}\ \phi X=PX+QX,\ \ \text{(b)}\ \phi V=bV+cV

where
PX,\ bV
are the tangential components and
QX,\ cV
are the normal components.

A submanifold
M
of an almost contact metric manifold
\bar{M}
is said to be invariant if
\phi(T_{p}M)\subseteq T_{p}M
, for every
p\in M
and
anti-invariant if
\phi T_{p}M\subseteq T^{\bot}_{p}M
, for every
p\in M

[
4
]
.

A submanifold
M
of an almost contact metric manifold
\bar{M}
is said to be slant if for each non-zero vector
X\in T_{p}M
, the angle
\theta
between
\phi X

and
T_{p}M
is a constant, i.e. it does not depend on the choice of
p\in M
.

Definition 2.1
.

[
29
]

A submanifold
M
of an almost contact metric manifold
\bar{M}
is said to be pointwise slant if for any non-zero vector
X\in T_{p}M
at
p\in M
,
such that
X
is not proportional to
\xi_{p}
, the angle
\theta(X)
between
\phi X
and
T_{p}^{*}M=T_{p}M-\{0\}
is independent of the choice of non-zero

X\in T_{p}^{*}M
.

For pointwise slant submanifold
\theta
is a function on
M
, which is known as slant function of
M
. Invariant and anti-invariant submanifolds are particular cases of pointwise slant submanifolds with slant function
\theta=0
and
\frac{\pi}{2}
respectively. Also a pointwise slant submanifold
M
will be slant if and only if
\theta
is constant on
M
, Thus a pointwise slant submanifold is proper if neither
\theta=0,\frac{\pi}{2}
nor constant.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
29
]

Let
M
be a submanifold of an almost contact metric manifold
\bar{M}
such that
\xi\in\Gamma(TM)
. Then,
M
is pointwise slant if and
only if

(2.12)

P^{2}=\cos^{2}\theta(-I+\eta\otimes\xi),

for some real valued function
\theta
defined on the tangent bundle
TM
of
M
.

If
M
be a pointwise slant submanifold of an almost contact metric manifold
\bar{M}
the following relations holds:

(2.13)

\displaystyle g(PX,PY)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos^{2}\theta\{g(X,Y)-\eta(X)\eta(Y)\}

(2.14)

\displaystyle g(QX,QY)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sin^{2}\theta\{g(X,Y)-\eta(X)\eta(Y)\}

(2.15)

\displaystyle bQX

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sin^{2}\theta\{-X+\eta(X)\xi\},\ \ \ cQX=-QPX

for any
X,\ Y\in\Gamma(TM)
.

We now recall the following:

Theorem 2.2
.

[
16
]
[Hiepko’s Theorem].
Let
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
be two orthogonal distribution on a Riemannian manifold
M
. Suppose that
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
both are involutive such that
\mathcal{D}_{1}
is a totally geodesic foliation and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
is a spherical foliation. Then
M
is locally isometric to a non-trivial warped product
M_{1}\times_{f}M_{2}
, where
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are integral manifolds of
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
, respectively.

3.
Pointwise bi-slant submanifolds

In this section, we define and
study pointwise bi-slant submanifold of Kenmotsu manifold.

Definition 3.1
.

[
13
]

A submanifold
M
of an almost contact metric manifold
\bar{M}
is said to be a pointwise bi-slant submanifold if there exists a pair of orthogonal distribution
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
of
M
at the point
p\in M
such that

(a)
TM=\mathcal{D}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{D}_{2}\oplus\{\xi\}
,

(b)
\phi(\mathcal{D}_{1})\bot\mathcal{D}_{2}\oplus\{\xi\}
,

(c) the distribution
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
are pointwise slant with slant functions
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
, respectively.

The pair
\{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\}
of slant functions is called the bi-slant function and if
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\neq 0,\frac{\pi}{2}
and
both
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}
are not constant on
M
, then
M
is called proper pointwise bi-slant submanifold.

If
M
is a pointwise bi-slant submanifold of
\bar{M}
then for any
X\in\Gamma(TM)
we have

(3.1)

X=T_{1}X+T_{2}X

where
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
are the projections from
TM
onto
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
, respectively.

If we put
P_{1}=T_{1}\circ P
and
P_{2}=T_{2}\circ P
then from (
3.1
), we get

(3.2)

\phi X=P_{1}X+P_{2}X+QX,

for
X\in\Gamma(TM)
. From Theorem 2.1, we get

(3.3)

P_{i}^{2}X=\cos^{2}\theta_{i}\{-X+\eta(X)\xi\},\ \ X\in\Gamma(TM),\ \ i=1,2.

For a proper pointwise bi-slant submanifold
M
of a Kenmotsu manifold
\bar{M}
, the normal bundle of
M
is decomposed as

(3.4)

T^{\bot}M=Q\mathcal{D}_{1}\oplus Q\mathcal{D}_{2}\oplus\nu,

where
\nu
is a
\phi
-invariant normal subbundle of
M
.

Now, we find the following lemma for a pointwise bi-slant submanifold
M
of Kenmotsu manifold
\bar{M}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
M
be a pointwise bi-slant submanifold of
\bar{M}
with pointwise slant distributions
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
with distinct slant function
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
, respectively. Then

(3.5)

\displaystyle(\sin^{2}\theta_{1}-\sin^{2}\theta_{2})g(\nabla_{X}Y,Z)=g(A_{QP_{2}Z}Y-A_{QZ}P_{1}Y,X)

\displaystyle+g(A_{QP_{1}Y}Z-A_{QY}P_{2}Z,X)

and

(3.6)

\displaystyle(\sin^{2}\theta_{2}-\sin^{2}\theta_{1})g(\nabla_{Z}W,X)=g(A_{QP_{2}W}X-A_{QW}P_{1}X,Z)

\displaystyle+g(A_{QP_{1}X}W-A_{QX}P_{2}W,Z)-\eta(X)g(Z,W),

for any
X,\ Y\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}_{1}\oplus\{\xi\})
and
Z,\ W\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}_{2})
.

Proof.

For any
X,\ Y\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}_{1}\oplus\{\xi\})
and
Z\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}_{2})
, we have

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{X}Y,Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\phi\bar{\nabla}_{X}Y,\phi Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi Y,\phi Z)-g((\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi)Y,Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}P_{1}Y,\phi Z)+g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}QY,\phi Z)-\eta(Y)g(\phi X,\phi Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g(\phi\bar{\nabla}_{X}P_{1}Y,Z)+g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}QY,P_{2}Z)+g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}QY,QZ)-\eta(Y)g(X,Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}P_{1}^{2}Y,Z)-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}QP_{1}Y,Z)+g((\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi)P_{1}Y,Z)

\displaystyle+g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}QY,P_{2}Z)-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}QZ,\phi Y)+g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}QZ,P_{1}Y)-\eta(Y)g(X,Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos^{2}\theta_{1}g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}Y,Z)-\sin 2\theta_{1}X(\theta_{1})g(Y,Z)-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}QP_{1}Y,Z)

\displaystyle+g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}QY,P_{2}Z)+g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}bQZ,Y)+g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}cQZ,Y)

\displaystyle-g((\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi)QZ,Y)+g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}QZ,P_{1}Y)-\eta(Y)g(X,Z)

Using (
2.5
), (
2.7
), (
2.15
), orthogonality of the distributions and symmetry of the shape operator, the above equation reduces to

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{X}Y,Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos^{2}\theta_{1}g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}Y,Z)+g(A_{QP_{1}Y}Z,X)-g(A_{QY}P_{2}Z,X)

\displaystyle+\sin^{2}\theta_{2}g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}Y,Z)+g(A_{QP_{2}Z}Y,X)-g(A_{QZ}P_{1}Y,X),

from which the relation (
3.5
) follows. Also, for
X\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}_{1})
and
Z,\ W\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}_{2})
, we have

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{Z}W,X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\phi\bar{\nabla}_{Z}W,\phi X)+\eta(X)g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}W,\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}\phi W,\phi X)-g((\bar{\nabla}_{Z}\phi)W,\phi X)-\eta(X)g(W,\bar{\nabla}_{Z}\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}P_{2}W,\phi X)+g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}QW,\phi X)-\eta(X)g(Z,W)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g(\phi\bar{\nabla}_{Z}P_{2}W,X)+g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}QW,P_{1}X)

\displaystyle+g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}QW,QX)-\eta(X)g(Z,W)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}P_{2}^{2}W,X)-g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}QP_{2}W,X)+g((\bar{\nabla}_{Z}\phi)P_{2}W,X)

\displaystyle+g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}QW,P_{1}X)-g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}QX,\phi W)

\displaystyle+g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}QX,P_{2}W)-\eta(X)g(Z,W)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos^{2}\theta_{2}g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}W,X)-\sin 2\theta_{2}Z(\theta_{2})g(W,X)-g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}QP_{2}W,X)

\displaystyle+g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}QW,P_{1}X)+g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}bQX,W)+g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}cQX,W)

\displaystyle-g((\bar{\nabla}_{Z}\phi)QX,W)+g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}QX,P_{2}W)-\eta(X)g(Z,W)

Using (
2.5
), (
2.7
), (
2.15
), orthogonality of the distributions and symmetry of the shape operator,
the above equation reduces to

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{Z}W,X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos^{2}\theta_{2}g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}W,X)+g(A_{QP_{2}W}X,Z)-g(A_{QW}P_{1}X,Z)

\displaystyle+\sin^{2}\theta_{1}g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}W,X)+g(A_{QP_{1}X}W,Z)

\displaystyle-g(A_{QX}P_{2}W,Z)-\eta(X)g(Z,W),

) follows.

4.
Warped product pointwise bi-slant submanifolds

Definition 4.1
.

[
6
]

Let
(N_{1},g_{1})
and
(N_{2},g_{2})
be two Riemannian manifolds with Riemannian metric
g_{1}

and
g_{2}
respectively and
f
be a positive definite smooth function on
N_{1}
. The warped product
of
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
is the Riemannian manifold
N_{1}\times_{f}N_{2}=(N_{1}\times N_{2},g)

(4.1)

g=g_{1}+f^{2}g_{2}.

A warped product manifold
N_{1}\times_{f}N_{2}
is said to be trivial if the warping function

is constant.

Let

be a warped product manifold, then we have
[
6
]

(4.2)

\displaystyle\nabla_{U}X=\nabla_{X}U=(X\ln f)U,

for any
X
,
Y\in\Gamma(TN_{1})
and
U\in\Gamma(TN_{2})
.

Now we construct an example of warped product pointwise bi-slant submanifold of Kenmotsu manifold.

\displaystyle\qquad\|h\|^{2}\geq 2q\csc^{2}\theta_{1}\bigg(\cos^{2}\theta_{1}+\cos^{2}\theta_{2}\bigg)\bigg\{\|\boldsymbol{\nabla}^{\theta_{1}}lnf\|^{2}-1-\sum_{r=1}^{p}(e_{r}lnf)^{2}\bigg\},

where
\boldsymbol{\nabla}^{\theta_{1}}\ln f
denotes the gradient of
\ln f
along
M_{1}
,
2q=
dim
M_{2}
and
\theta_{1},\ \theta_{2}
are the slant angles of
M_{1}

and
M_{2}
, respectively.

(ii) If the equality sign of (
6.1
) holds, then
M_{1}
is totally geodesic and
M_{2}
is totally umbilical in
\bar{M}
.

Proof.

From the definition of
h
, we have

\|h\|^{2}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}g(h(e_{i},e_{j}),h(e_{i},e_{j}))=\sum_{r=n+1}^{2m+1}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}g(h(e_{i},e_{j}),e_{r})^{2}.

Now we decompose the above relation for the frames of
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
, as follows

(6.2)

\displaystyle\|h\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{r=n+1}^{2m+1}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2p+1}g(h(e_{i},e_{j}),e_{r})^{2}+2\sum_{r=n+1}^{2m+1}\sum_{i=1}^{2p+1}\sum_{j=1}^{2q}g(h(e_{i},e^{*}_{j}),e_{r})^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{r=n+1}^{2m}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2q}g(h(e^{*}_{i},e^{*}_{j}),e_{r})^{2}

Since
M
is mixed totally geodesic, the second term in the right hand side of (
6.2
) is zero and decomposing the remaining terms according to (
3.4
), we obtain

(6.3)

\displaystyle\|h\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{r=n+1}^{n+2q}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2p+1}g(h(e_{i},e_{j}),e_{r})^{2}+\sum_{r=n+2q+1}^{2n-1}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2p+1}g(h(e_{i},e_{j}),e_{r})^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{r=2n}^{2m+1}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2p+1}g(h(e_{i},e_{j}),e_{r})^{2}+\sum_{r=n+1}^{n+2p}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2q}g(h(e^{*}_{i},e^{*}_{j}),e_{r})^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{r=n+2p+1}^{2n-1}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2q}g(h(e^{*}_{i},e^{*}_{j}),e_{r})^{2}+\sum_{r=2n}^{2m+1}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2q}g(h(e^{*}_{i},e^{*}_{j}),e_{r})^{2}.

Next by removing the
\nu
components in (
6.3
), we have

(6.4)

\displaystyle\|h\|^{2}\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{2q}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2p+1}g(h(e_{i},e_{j}),\widetilde{e}_{r})^{2}+\sum_{r=1}^{2p}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2p+1}g(h(e_{i},e_{j}),\hat{e}_{r})^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{r=1}^{2p}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2q}g(h(e^{*}_{i},e^{*}_{j}),\widetilde{e}_{r})^{2}+\sum_{r=1}^{2q}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2q}g(h(e^{*}_{i},e^{*}_{j}),\hat{e}_{r}).

Since we could not find any relation for
g(h(e_{i},e_{j}),\hat{e}_{r})
for any
i,j=1,2,\cdots,2p+1
,
r=1,2,\cdots,2p
. And
g(h(e^{*}_{i},e^{*}_{j}),\widetilde{e}_{r})
, for any
i,j,r=1,2,\cdots,2q
therefore, we leave the
2
nd and
4
th terms and get

(6.5)

\|h\|^{2}\geq\sum_{r=1}^{2q}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2p+1}g(h(e_{i},e_{j}),\widetilde{e}_{r})^{2}+\sum_{r=1}^{2p}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2q}g(h(e^{*}_{i},e^{*}_{j}),\hat{e}_{r})^{2}.

In view of (
4.13
), (
6.5
) yields

(6.6)

\|h\|^{2}\geq\sum_{r=1}^{2q}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2p+1}g(h(e_{i},e^{*}_{j}),\tilde{e}_{r})^{2}+\sum_{r=1}^{2p}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2q}g(h(e^{*}_{i},e^{*}_{j}),\hat{e}_{r})^{2}.

Since
M
is mixed totally geodesic, we have

(6.7)

g(h(e_{i},e_{j}),e_{r})=0,

for every
i,\ j=1,\cdots,2p+1,\ j=n+1,\cdots,2q
.

By virtue of (
6.7
),we have from (
6.6
) that

(6.8)

\|h\|^{2}\geq\sum_{r=1}^{2p}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2q}g(h(e^{*}_{i},e^{*}_{j}),\hat{e}_{r})^{2}.

Thus, by using the orthonormal frame fields of
Q\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
Q\mathcal{D}_{2}
, we derive

(6.9)

\displaystyle\|h\|^{2}\geq\csc^{2}\theta_{1}\sum_{r=1}^{p}\sum_{i,j=1}^{q}g(h(e^{*}_{i},e^{*}_{j}),Qe_{r})^{2}

\displaystyle+\csc^{2}\theta_{1}\sec^{2}{\theta}_{2}\sum_{r=1}^{p}\sum_{i,j=1}^{q}g(h(P_{2}e^{*}_{i},e^{*}_{j}),Qe_{r})^{2}

\displaystyle+\csc^{2}\theta_{1}\sec^{2}{\theta}_{2}\sum_{r=1}^{p}\sum_{i,j=1}^{q}g(h(e^{*}_{i},P_{2}e^{*}_{j}),Qe_{r})^{2}

\displaystyle+\csc^{2}\theta_{1}\sec^{4}{\theta}_{2}\sum_{r=1}^{p}\sum_{i,j=1}^{q}g(h(P_{2}e^{*}_{i},P_{2}e^{*}_{j}),Qe_{r})^{2}

\displaystyle+\csc^{2}\theta_{1}\sec^{2}{\theta}_{1}\sum_{r=1}^{p}\sum_{i,j=1}^{q}g(h(e^{*}_{i},e^{*}_{j}),QP_{1}e_{r})^{2}

\displaystyle+\csc^{2}\theta_{1}\sec^{2}{\theta}_{1}\sec^{2}{\theta}_{2}\sum_{r=1}^{p}\sum_{i,j=1}^{q}g(h(P_{2}e^{*}_{i},e^{*}_{j}),QP_{1}e_{r})^{2}

\displaystyle+\csc^{2}\theta_{1}\sec^{2}{\theta}_{1}\sec^{2}{\theta}_{2}\sum_{r=1}^{p}\sum_{i,j=1}^{q}g(h(Pe^{*}_{i},P_{2}e^{*}_{j}),QP_{1}e_{r})^{2}

\displaystyle+\csc^{2}\theta_{1}\sec^{2}{\theta}_{1}\sec^{4}{\theta}_{2}\sum_{r=1}^{p}\sum_{i,j=1}^{q}g(h(P_{2}e^{*}_{i},P_{2}e^{*}_{j}),QP_{1}e_{r})^{2}.

Using (
4.14
)-(
4.16
), (
4.20
)-(
4.24
) and (
6.7
) in (
6.9
), we get

(6.10)

\displaystyle\|h\|^{2}\geq

\displaystyle\csc^{2}\theta_{1}\sum_{r=1}^{p}\sum_{i,j=1}^{q}(P_{1}e_{r}lnf)^{2}g(e^{*}_{i},e^{*}_{j})^{2}

\displaystyle+\csc^{2}\theta_{1}\sum_{r=1}^{p}\sum_{i,j=1}^{q}(P_{1}e_{r}lnf)^{2}g(e^{*}_{i},e^{*}_{j})^{2}

\displaystyle+\csc^{2}\theta_{1}\sec^{2}\theta_{1}\cos^{2}\theta_{2}\sum_{r=1}^{p}\sum_{i,j=1}^{q}(P_{1}e_{r}lnf)^{2}g(e^{*}_{i},e^{*}_{j})^{2}

\displaystyle+\csc^{2}\theta_{1}\sec^{2}\theta_{1}\cos^{2}\theta_{2}\sum_{r=1}^{p}\sum_{i,j=1}^{q}(P_{1}e_{r}lnf)^{2}g(e^{*}_{i},e^{*}_{j})^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2q\csc^{2}\theta_{1}[1+\sec^{2}\theta_{1}\cos^{2}\theta_{2}]\sum_{r=1}^{p}(P_{1}e_{r}lnf)^{2}.

Now,

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{\nabla}^{\theta_{1}}\ln f\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{2p+1}(e_{r}lnf)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{p}(e_{r}lnf)^{2}+\sum_{r=1}^{p}(\sec\theta_{1}P_{1}e_{r}lnf)^{2}+(\xi lnf)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{p}(e_{r}lnf)^{2}+\sec^{2}\theta_{1}\sum_{r=1}^{p}(P_{1}e_{r}lnf)^{2}+1.

\displaystyle\ \ \text{as}\ \ (\xi lnf)=1

From the above relation, we get

(6.11)

\sum_{r=1}^{p}(P_{1}e_{r}lnf)^{2}=\cos^{2}\theta_{1}\left[\|\boldsymbol{\nabla}^{\theta_{1}}lnf\|^{2}-1-\sum_{r=1}^{p}(e_{r}lnf)^{2}\right].

Using (
6.11
) in (
6.10
), we get (
6.1
).

If equality of (
6.1
) holds, then from the leaving third term of (
6.3
),
we get
g(h(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{1}),\nu)=0
, which implies that
h(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{1})\bot\nu
, i.e.,

(6.12)

h(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{1})\subset Q\mathcal{D}_{1}\oplus Q\mathcal{D}_{2}.

and from the leaving second term in (
6.4
), we find

g(h(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{1}),Q\mathcal{D}_{1})=0
, which implies that
h(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{1})\bot Q\mathcal{D}_{1}
, i.e.,

(6.13)

h(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{1})\subset Q\mathcal{D}_{2}\oplus\nu.

Also, from (
4.13
) and (
6.7
), we get
h(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{1})\bot Q\mathcal{D}_{2}
, i.e.,

(6.14)

h(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{1})\subset Q\mathcal{D}_{1}\oplus\nu.

From (
6.12
)-(
6.14
), we conclude that

(6.15)

h(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{1})=0.

Since
M_{1}
is totally geodesic in
M
(
[
6
]
,
[
8
]
), from (
6.15
), we can say that
M_{1}
is totally geodesic in
\bar{M}
.

Similarly, for the leaving sixth term in (
6.3
), we get
g(h(\mathcal{D}_{2},\mathcal{D}_{2}),\nu)=0
, which implies that

h(\mathcal{D}_{2},\mathcal{D}_{2})\bot\nu
, i.e.,

(6.16)

h(\mathcal{D}_{2},\mathcal{D}_{2})\subset Q\mathcal{D}_{2}\oplus\nu.

Also, for the leaving fourth term in (
6.4
), we obtain
g(h(\mathcal{D}_{2},\mathcal{D}_{2}),Q\mathcal{D}_{2})=0
, which implies that

h(\mathcal{D}_{2},\mathcal{D}_{2})\bot Q\mathcal{D}_{2}
i.e.,

(6.17)

h(\mathcal{D}_{2},\mathcal{D}_{2})\subset Q\mathcal{D}_{1}.

From (
6.16
) and (
6.17
), we get

(6.18)

h(\mathcal{D}_{2},\mathcal{D}_{2})\subset Q\mathcal{D}_{1}.

Moreover, using (
6.7
) in (
6.15
), we obtain

(6.19)

g(h(Z,W),QX)=(P_{1}X\ln f)g(Z,W)+\{(X\ln f)-\eta(X)\}g(Z,P_{2}W),

for any
X\in\Gamma(TM_{1})
and
Z,\ W\in\Gamma(TM_{2})
.
By Polarization of (
6.19
), we obtain

(6.20)

g(h(Z,W),QX)=(P_{1}X\ln f)g(Z,W)+[(X\ln f)-\eta(X)]g(W,P_{2}Z).

Subtracting (
6.20
) from (
6.19
), we get

(6.21)

g(h(Z,W),QX)=(P_{1}X\ln f)g(Z,W).

From (
6.18
), (
6.21
) and the fact that
M_{2}
is totally umbilical in
M
(
[
6
]
,
[
8
]
), we can conclude that
M_{2}
is totally umbilical
in
\bar{M}
. Thus the proof of the theorem is complete.

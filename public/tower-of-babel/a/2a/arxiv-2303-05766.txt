Higher Ext-groups in the triple product case
Li Cai, Yangyu Fan

Abstract
. In this short note, we compute higher extension groups for all irreducible representations and deduce the multiplicity formula for finite length representations in triple product case.



1.
Introduction

Let
F
be a
p
-adic field.
Let
L/F
be a cubic étale extension and
D/F
be a quaternion algebra. Let
G={\mathrm{Res}}_{L/F}D^{\times}
and
H=D^{\times}
.
Note that the intersection of the center
Z_{G}
of
G
with

H
is
Z_{H}={\mathbb{G}}_{m}
. Denote by
{\mathrm{Rep}}(F^{\times}\backslash G(F))
the category of smooth
F^{\times}\backslash G(F)
-representations.

In this short note, we prove that the higher Ext groups vanish

{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{F^{\times}\backslash H(F)}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}})=0,\quad i>0.

for any generic
\pi\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(F^{\times}\backslash G(F))
.
Combining with results from
the local trace formula approach, we obtain
a multiplicity formula for any irreducible
\pi\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(F^{\times}\backslash G(F))
.

To state the result,
we introduce more notations. For any irreducible
\pi\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(F^{\times}\backslash G(F))
, consider its geometric multiplicity

m_{\mathrm{geo}}(\pi)=\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}}|W(H,T)|^{-1}\int_{F^{\times}\backslash T(F)}c_{\pi}(t)D^{H}(t)dt

where

•

the support
{\mathcal{T}}={\mathcal{T}}(G,H)

is a set of tori in
H
. If
D
is split,
{\mathcal{T}}
consists of
F^{\times}
and the nonsplit maximal tori in
H
.
If
D
is non-split,
{\mathcal{T}}
consists of the nonsplit maximal tori in
H
.

•

W(H,T)=N_{H}(T)/Z_{H}(T)
.

•

c_{\pi}
is the regularized character on the semi-simple locus of
G(F)

(See
[
12
, Definition 2.5]
).

•

D^{H}(t)=|\det(1-{\mathrm{ad}}(t))|_{{\mathfrak{h}}/{\mathfrak{h}}_{t}}|
is the Weyl discriminant on the semi-simple
locus of
H(F)
.

•

the measure
dt
is normalized so that the volume
{\mathrm{Vol}}(F^{\times}\backslash T(F),dt)=1
.

Via the local trace formula approach, Wan proves the following multiplicity formula
for tempered representations:

Theorem 1.1

(Wan
[
11
]
, C.2 and C.3)
.

For any irreducible tempered
\pi\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(F^{\times}\backslash G(F))
,

m(\pi):=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}{\mathrm{Hom}}_{H(F)}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}})=m_{\mathrm{geo}}(\pi).

Remark 1.2
.

In fact, the above multiplicity formula holds for generic representations combining the result of Prasad
[
8
,
9
]

(See
[
11
, Remark C. 2.3]
).

In general, it is conjectured in
[
10
, Conjecture 7.1]
(for the Gan-Gross-Prasad models)
and
[
12
, Conjecture 7.6]
(for all spherical pairs) that
the multiplicity formula should hold for all
\pi\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(F^{\times}\backslash G(F))
of finite length
with the multiplicity
m(\pi)
replaced by the Euler-Poincaré number

{\mathrm{EP}}_{F^{\times}\backslash H(F)}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}}):=\sum_{i\geq 0}(-1)^{i}\dim_{\mathbb{C}}{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{F^{\times}\backslash H(F)}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}}).

Philosophically, such multiplicity formula can be viewed as a kind of Riemann-Roch theorem (See
[
10
, Remark 7.2]
).

Example 1.3
.

Assume
D
is split and
L=E\oplus F
where
E/F
is a quadratic field extension. Let
\pi=\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2}\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(F^{\times}\backslash G(F))
with

•

\pi_{1}=I_{B(E)}^{{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(E)}\chi_{1}\boxtimes\chi_{2}\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(F^{\times}\backslash{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(E))
being the normalized parabolic induction for characters
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}:\ E^{\times}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
such that
\chi_{1}\chi_{2}|_{F^{\times}}=1
;

•

\pi_{2}={\mathbb{C}}\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(F^{\times}\backslash{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F))
being the trivial representation.

Then

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{F^{\times}\backslash H(F)}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}})={\mathrm{Hom}}_{F^{\times}\backslash{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F)}(\pi_{1},{\mathbb{C}}).

Set
\chi^{\prime}:=\chi_{1}{\overline{\chi_{2}}}
where
-
denotes the Galois conjugation with respect to

E/F
. It is known that (see e.g.
[
6
, Theorem 5.2]
)
m(\pi)\leq 1
and the equality holds if and only if

\chi_{1}|_{F^{\times}}=\chi_{2}|_{F^{\times}}=1
or
\chi^{\prime}=1
.

On the other hand, by a property of the regularized characters of parabolic inductions
[
12
, Proposition 2.7]

m_{\mathrm{geo}}(\pi)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{F^{\times}\backslash E^{\times}}c_{\pi}(t)D^{H}(t)dt=\frac{1}{2}\int_{F^{\times}\backslash E^{\times}}(\chi^{\prime}(t)+\chi^{\prime}(\bar{t}))dt.

In particular,
m_{\mathrm{geo}}(\pi)\leq 1
and the equality holds if and only if
\chi^{\prime}=1
.

Therefore, in the case
\chi^{\prime}\not=1
and
\chi_{1}|_{F^{\times}}=\chi_{2}|_{F^{\times}}=1
,

m(\pi)\not=m_{\mathrm{geo}}(\pi).

This is compatible with Theorem
1.1
since
\pi_{2}
(hence
\pi
) is neither generic nor tempered.

The following is the main result of this paper.

Theorem 1.4
.

For any irreducible
\pi\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(F^{\times}\backslash G(F))
, generic if
D
is split,

{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{F^{\times}\backslash H(F)}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}})=0,\quad i\geq 1.

Moreover, for any
\pi\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(F^{\times}\backslash G(F))
of finite
length, the multiplicity formula holds

\mathrm{EP}_{F^{\times}\backslash H(F)}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}})=m_{\mathrm{geo}}(\pi).

Remark 1.5
.

Previously,

•

The vanishing result is known for the Whittaker model: if
G
is a connected quasi-split reductive
group over
F
,
B=TN
a Borel subgroup of
G
,
\psi
a generic character on
N(F)
, then for
any irreducible representation
\pi\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(G(F))
and any
i\geq 1
,
by
[
10
, Proposition 2.8]

{\mathrm{Ext}}_{N(F)}^{i}(\pi,\psi^{-1})\cong{\mathrm{Ext}}_{G(F)}^{i}\left(i_{N(F)}^{G(F)}\psi,\pi^{\vee}\right)\cong{\mathrm{Ext}}_{1}^{i}(\pi_{N,\psi},{\mathbb{C}})=0.

For the Gan-Gan-Prasad models of general linear groups, the vanishing result is
due to Chan-Savin
[
5
]
.

•

The only known cases of
[
12
, Conjecture 7.6]

are the group case
[
10
, Proposition 2.1(4)]
,
the Whittaker models
[
12
, Section 8.1]
,
and the Gan-Gross-Prasad models for general linear groups (
[
10
, Theorem 4.2]

and note that in this case the support
{\mathcal{T}}={\mathcal{T}}(G,H)
of
m_{\mathrm{geo}}
is
\{1\}
).

We explain the proof of Theorem
1.4
.
In fact, it is known that
{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{F^{\times}\backslash H(F)}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}})=0

for any
\pi
when
i\geq 2
(see
[
10
, Proposition 2.9]
, also Proposition
2.1
(1) below) and
{\mathrm{Ext}}^{1}_{F^{\times}\backslash H(F)}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}})=0
if
\pi
is supercuspidal (see
[
3
, Theorem 5.4.1]
, also
Proposition
2.1
(1) below). The proof of the vanishing result
is reduced to showing that
{\mathrm{Ext}}^{1}_{F^{\times}\backslash H(F)}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}})=0

for non-supercuspidal
\pi
. For this,
we apply standard tools: the geometric lemma of Bernstein-Zelevinsky
and the Schneider-Stuhler duality
(
[
7
, Theorem 2]
, see also Theorem
2.2
below).
Once the vanishing results are available,
the multiplicity formula for finite length representations
is deduced from the tempered version (Theorem
1.1
)
by noting both sides are additive and constant in an unramified twisting family.

In fact, we compute
{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{F^{\times}\backslash H(F)}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}})

for all irreducible
\pi\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(F^{\times}\backslash G(F))
and all
i
.
The results for
L=E\oplus F
together with the Schneider-Stuhler duality implies the following complete classification of irreducible

{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F)
-subrepresentations of irreducible
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(E)
-representations,
which is analogous to
[
7
, Proposition 9.1]
for the case
L=F\oplus F\oplus F
(in fact, there is an extra central character condition in
[
7
, Proposition 9.1]
:

\pi=\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2}\boxtimes\pi_{3}
with
\pi_{3}
having trivial central character. This condition is dropped here).

Proposition 1.6
.

Assume
L=E\oplus F
and
D
is split.
For
\pi=\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2}\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(F^{\times}\backslash G(F))
irreducible,

\pi_{2}^{\vee}
is a
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F)
-subrepresentation of
\pi_{1}
if and only if

•

\pi_{1}=\xi\circ\det
and
\pi_{2}=\xi^{-1}|_{F^{\times}}\circ\det
; or

•

\pi_{2}^{\vee}
is supercuspidal, and appears as a quotient of
\pi_{1}|_{{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F)}
; or

•

\pi_{2}={\mathrm{St}}_{F}\otimes\xi
,
\pi_{1}=I_{B(E)}^{{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(E)}\chi_{1}\boxtimes\chi_{2}

such that
\xi\chi_{1}|_{F^{\times}}=\xi\chi_{2}|_{F^{\times}}=1
and
\xi_{E}\chi^{\prime}\neq 1
.
Here
\xi_{E}=\xi\circ N_{E/F}
,
\chi^{\prime}=\chi_{1}{\overline{\chi_{2}}}
and
{\mathrm{St}}_{F}
is the Steinberg representation for
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F)
.

2.
The proof

For any reductive group
\Gamma
over
F
and any center character
\omega:\ Z_{\Gamma}(F)\to{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
, let
{\mathrm{Rep}}(\Gamma(F),\omega)
denote the full subcategory of
{\mathrm{Rep}}(\Gamma(F))
consisting of objects on which
Z_{\Gamma}(F)
acts by
\omega
. For any objects
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(\Gamma(F),\omega)\subset{\mathrm{Rep}}(\Gamma(F))
, let
{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(\Gamma(F),\omega)}(\pi,\pi^{\prime})
(resp.
{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{\Gamma(F)}(\pi,\pi^{\prime})
) be the
i
-th extension group in the category
{\mathrm{Rep}}(\Gamma(F),\omega)
(resp.
{\mathrm{Rep}}(\Gamma(F))
).
Let
\Gamma^{\prime}(F)
be a closed subgroup of the
p
-adic group
\Gamma(F)
with modulus character
\delta_{\Gamma^{\prime}}
. Note that when
\Gamma^{\prime}
is reductive,
\delta_{\Gamma^{\prime}}
is trivial. Denote by
I_{\Gamma^{\prime}(F)}^{\Gamma(F)}
(resp.
i_{\Gamma^{\prime}(F)}^{\Gamma(F)}
) the normalized (resp. compact) induction. Note that for any
\sigma\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(\Gamma^{\prime}(F))
,
(i_{\Gamma^{\prime}(F)}^{\Gamma(F)}\sigma)^{\vee}=I_{\Gamma^{\prime}(F)}^{\Gamma(F)}\sigma^{\vee}

where
-^{\vee}
stands for smooth dual in the proper category. Moreover if
\Gamma^{\prime}=MN
is a parabolic subgroup with Levi factor
M
, denote by
J_{N}
the normalized Jacquet functor for
\Gamma^{\prime}
.

We record some basic properties of Ext-groups which are frequently used in the following.

Proposition 2.1
.

We have the following results for the Ext-groups.

(1)

For any irreducible
\pi\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(\Gamma(F))
, smooth
\pi^{\prime}\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(\Gamma(F))
and any
i>\mathrm{split\ rank\ of}\ Z_{\Gamma}

{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{\Gamma(F)}(\pi,\pi^{\prime})\cong{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{\Gamma(F)}(\pi^{\prime},\pi)=0.

If
\pi
is supercuspidal with central character
\omega
, then
\pi
is both projective and injective in
{\mathrm{Rep}}(\Gamma(F),\omega)
. In particular,
for any
i\geq 1

{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(\Gamma(F),\omega)}(\pi,\pi^{\prime})\cong{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(\Gamma(F),\omega)}(\pi^{\prime},\pi)=0.

(2)

For any
\pi\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(\Gamma(F),\omega)
,
\sigma\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(\Gamma(F),\omega^{-1})
and any
i\geq 0

{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{Z_{\Gamma}(F)\backslash\Gamma(F)}(\pi\otimes\sigma,{\mathbb{C}})\cong{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(\Gamma(F),\omega)}(\pi,\sigma^{\vee})\cong{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(\Gamma(F),\omega^{-1})}(\sigma,\pi^{\vee}).

(3)

The restriction functor

{\mathrm{Rep}}(\Gamma(F))\to{\mathrm{Rep}}(\Gamma^{\prime}(F))

sends projective objects to projective objects, the normalized induction functor
I_{\Gamma^{\prime}(F)}^{\Gamma(F)}:\ {\mathrm{Rep}}(\Gamma^{\prime}(F))\to{\mathrm{Rep}}(\Gamma(F))
sends injective objects to injective objects. Moreover, for any
\sigma\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(\Gamma^{\prime}(F))
,
\pi\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(\Gamma(F))

and any
i\geq 0

{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{\Gamma(F)}\left(\pi,I_{\Gamma^{\prime}(F)}^{\Gamma(F)}\sigma\right)\cong{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{\Gamma^{\prime}(F)}\left(\pi,\sigma\otimes\delta_{\Gamma^{\prime}}^{1/2}\right).

When
\Gamma^{\prime}=MN
is a parabolic subgroup with Levi factor
M
, the normalized Jacquet functor
J_{N}:\ {\mathrm{Rep}}(\Gamma(F))\to{\mathrm{Rep}}(M(F))
sends projective objects to projective objects. For any
\sigma\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(M(F))
and any
i\geq 0

{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{\Gamma(F)}\left(\pi,I_{\Gamma^{\prime}(F)}^{\Gamma(F)}\sigma\right)\cong{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{M(F)}(J_{N}(\pi),\sigma).

Proof.

The property of supercuspidal representations can be found in
[
3
, Theorem 5.4.1]
. Other results are summarized in
[
10
, Section 2]
.
∎

For any
\pi\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(\Gamma(F),\omega)
irreducible, let
d(\pi)
(resp.
d^{\prime}(\pi)
) be the split rank of
Z_{M}
(resp.
Z_{M}\cap[\Gamma,\Gamma]
), where
M
is any Levi subgroup carrying the cuspidal support of
\pi
. The key ingredient for computing higher Ext groups is the following Schneider-Stuhler duality theorem.

Theorem 2.2

(Theorem 1,2 in
[
7
]
)
.

Let
\pi\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(\Gamma(F),\omega)
be any irreducible representation and
D(\pi)
be the Aubert-Zelevinsky involution of
\pi
. Then

•

for any
\pi^{\prime}\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(\Gamma(F))
,
{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{\Gamma(F)}(\pi,\pi^{\prime})=0
for
i>d(\pi)
and for
0\leq i\leq d(\pi)
, there is a non-degenerate pairing

{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{\Gamma(F)}(\pi,\pi^{\prime})\times{\mathrm{Ext}}^{d(\pi)-i}_{\Gamma(F)}(\pi^{\prime},D(\pi))\to{\mathrm{Ext}}_{\Gamma(F)}^{d(\pi)}(\pi,D(\pi))\cong{\mathbb{C}},

•

for any
\pi^{\prime}\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(\Gamma(F),\omega)
,
{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(\Gamma(F),\omega)}(\pi,\pi^{\prime})=0
for
i>d^{\prime}(\pi)
and for
0\leq i\leq d^{\prime}(\pi)
, there is a non-degenerate pairing

{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(\Gamma(F),\omega)}(\pi,\pi^{\prime})\times{\mathrm{Ext}}^{d^{\prime}(\pi)-i}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(\Gamma(F),\omega)}(\pi^{\prime},D(\pi))\to{\mathrm{Ext}}_{{\mathrm{Rep}}(\Gamma(F),\omega)}^{d^{\prime}(\pi)}(\pi,D(\pi))\cong{\mathbb{C}}.

Remark 2.3
.

In general, the non-degeneracy means that if
\pi^{\prime}=\varinjlim_{n}\pi_{n}^{\prime}
is the inductive limit of finite generated
\Gamma(F)
-submodules
\pi_{n}^{\prime}
, then

{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{\Gamma(F)}(\pi,\pi^{\prime})=\varinjlim_{n}{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{\Gamma(F)}(\pi,\pi_{n}^{\prime}),\quad{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{\Gamma(F)}(\pi^{\prime},D(\pi))=\varprojlim_{n}{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{\Gamma(F)}(\pi_{n}^{\prime},D(\pi))

is the inductive limit (resp. projective limit) of finite dimensional
{\mathbb{C}}
-spaces and the pairing is direct limit of perfect pairings on these finite dimensional spaces.

In the triple product case, the result of Aizenbud-Sayag
[
1
]
guarantees all the Ext-groups below are finite dimensional and the non-degeneracy has the usual meaning.

In the following, we shall concentrate on the triple product case.

The case
D
non-split is straightforward.

Proposition 2.4
.

Assume
D
is non-split. Then for any
\pi\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(F^{\times}\backslash G(F))
irreducible,

{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{F^{\times}\backslash H(F)}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}})=0,\quad i>0.

Proof.

Note that
Z_{H}\backslash H
is anisotropic. Then by Proposition
2.1
(1),
{\mathbb{C}}
is injective and the statement follows. ∎

To deal with the
D
split case, we need to consider
the following Waldspurger toric case.

Lemma 2.5
.

Assume
D
is split. Let
K\subset D
be an étale quadratic
F
-algebra and embed
F^{\times}
into
D^{\times}(F)\times K^{\times}
diagonally. Then for any generic irreducible
\pi\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(F^{\times}\backslash(D^{\times}(F)\times K^{\times})\big)
,

{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{F^{\times}\backslash K^{\times}}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}})=0,\quad i\geq 1.

Proof.

Assume
\pi=\sigma\boxtimes\xi
with
\sigma\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(D^{\times}(F))
and
\xi\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(K^{\times})
. If
K/F
is a field extension, then

{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{F^{\times}\backslash K^{\times}}(\sigma\boxtimes\xi,{\mathbb{C}})=0
for
i\geq 1
by Proposition
2.1
(1).

If
K=F\oplus F
,
{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{F^{\times}\backslash K^{\times}}(\sigma\boxtimes\xi,{\mathbb{C}})=0
for
i\geq 2
by Proposition
2.1
(1).
For
i=1
, one can apply Theorem
2.2
to
{\mathbb{C}}\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(F^{\times}\backslash K^{\times})
, where
d({\mathbb{C}})=1
and
D({\mathbb{C}})={\mathbb{C}}
. One has

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}{\mathrm{Ext}}^{1}_{F^{\times}\backslash K^{\times}}(\sigma\boxtimes\xi,{\mathbb{C}})=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}{\mathrm{Hom}}_{F^{\times}\backslash K^{\times}}({\mathbb{C}},\sigma\boxtimes\xi)=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}{\mathrm{Hom}}_{K^{\times}}(\xi^{-1},\sigma).

Identify
K^{\times}
with the split torus
T(F)
and let
{\mathcal{K}}(\sigma)
be the Kirillov model of
\sigma
. Write
\xi=\xi_{1}\boxtimes\xi_{2}
. Then by
[
2
, Proposition 4.7.2]
,

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{T(F)}(\xi^{-1},{\mathcal{K}}(\sigma)|_{T})=\{\phi\in{\mathcal{K}}(\sigma)\ |\ \phi(ax)=\xi_{1}^{-1}(a)\phi(x)\text{ for any }a\in F^{\times}\}=0.

∎

Assume
D
is split. By Proposition
2.1
(1),
{\mathrm{Ext}}^{i}_{F^{\times}\backslash H(F)}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}})=0
for
i\geq 2
. In the following, we compute
{\mathrm{Ext}}^{1}_{F^{\times}\backslash H(F)}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}})
case by case. Let
T\subset B\subset{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}
be the diagonal torus and the group of upper triangular matrices respectively,
{\mathrm{St}}_{F}
be the Steinberg representation for
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F)
and
|\cdot|_{F}
be the norm character on
F^{\times}
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Assume
D
is split and
L/F
is a cubic field extension. Then for
\pi\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(F^{\times}\backslash G(F))
irreducible,
{\mathrm{Ext}}^{1}_{F^{\times}\backslash H(F)}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}})=0
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.1
(1,3)(the modulus character of
H
is trivial), when
\pi
is supercuspidal,

{\mathrm{Ext}}^{1}_{F^{\times}\backslash H(F)}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}})={\mathrm{Ext}}^{1}_{F^{\times}\backslash G(F)}(\pi,I_{H(F)}^{G(F)}{\mathbb{C}})=0.

If
\pi
is one-dimensional, then by Theorem
2.2
for
{\mathbb{C}}\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(F^{\times}\backslash H(F))
, where
D({\mathbb{C}})={\mathrm{St}}_{F}
and
d({\mathbb{C}})={\mathbb{C}}
,

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}{\mathrm{Ext}}^{1}_{F^{\times}\backslash H(F)}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}})=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}{\mathrm{Hom}}_{H(F)}({\mathrm{St}}_{F},\pi)=0.

Note that when
\pi
is a special series, then
\pi\hookrightarrow I_{B(L)}^{{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(L)}\chi_{1}\boxtimes\chi_{2}
with
\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{-1}=|\cdot|_{L}
. Thus it suffices to show that for
\pi=I_{B(L)}^{{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(L)}\chi\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(F^{\times}\backslash G(F))
where
\chi=\chi_{1}\boxtimes\chi_{2}
with
\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{-1}\neq|\cdot|_{L}^{-1}
,

{\mathrm{Ext}}^{1}_{F^{\times}\backslash H(F)}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}})=0.

By the geometric lemma (see
[
9
, Section 6.1]
), there exists an exact sequence of
F^{\times}\backslash H(F)
-representations

0\to i_{F^{\times}}^{H(F)}{\mathbb{C}}\to\pi\to I_{B(F)}^{H(F)}\chi\delta_{B}\to 0

where
\delta_{B}
is the modulus character of
B(F)
. The short exact sequence induces a long exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to

\displaystyle{\mathrm{Hom}}_{F^{\times}\backslash H(F)}(I_{B(F)}^{H(F)}\chi\delta_{B},{\mathbb{C}})\to{\mathrm{Hom}}_{F^{\times}\backslash H(F)}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}})\to{\mathrm{Hom}}_{F^{\times}\backslash H(F)}(i_{F^{\times}}^{H(F)}{\mathbb{C}},{\mathbb{C}})\to

\displaystyle{\mathrm{Ext}}^{1}_{F^{\times}\backslash H(F)}(I_{B(F)}^{H(F)}\chi\delta_{B},{\mathbb{C}})\to{\mathrm{Ext}}^{1}_{F^{\times}\backslash H(F)}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}})\to{\mathrm{Ext}}^{1}_{F^{\times}\backslash H(F)}(i_{F^{\times}}^{H(F)}{\mathbb{C}},{\mathbb{C}})\to 0.

By Proposition
2.1
(2,3),

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{F^{\times}\backslash H(F)}(i_{F^{\times}}^{H(F)}{\mathbb{C}},{\mathbb{C}})={\mathbb{C}},\quad{\mathrm{Ext}}^{1}_{F^{\times}\backslash H(F)}(i_{F^{\times}}^{H(F)}{\mathbb{C}},{\mathbb{C}})=0.

Note that

•

if
\pi
is reducible,
I_{B(F)}^{H(F)}\chi\delta_{B}
is irreducible;

•

if
\pi
is irreducible, we may assume
I_{B(F)}^{H(F)}\chi\delta_{B}
is irreducible by replacing
\chi
by
\chi^{w}
, the twisting of
\chi
by
w=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}
, if necessary.

Thus

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{F^{\times}\backslash H(F)}(I_{B(F)}^{H(F)}\chi\delta_{B},{\mathbb{C}})=0.

Moreover by Theorem
2.2
for
{\mathbb{C}}\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(F^{\times}\backslash H(F))
, where
D({\mathbb{C}})={\mathrm{St}}_{F}
and
d({\mathbb{C}})=1
,

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}{\mathrm{Ext}}^{1}_{F^{\times}\backslash H(F)}(I_{B(F)}^{H(F)}\chi\delta_{B},{\mathbb{C}})=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}{\mathrm{Hom}}_{F^{\times}\backslash H(F)}({\mathrm{St}}_{F},I_{B(F)}^{H(F)}\chi\delta_{B})=0.

Consequently,
{\mathrm{Hom}}_{H(F)}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}})={\mathbb{C}}
and
{\mathrm{Ext}}^{1}_{F^{\times}\backslash H(F)}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}})=0
.
∎

Proposition 2.7
.

Assume
D
is split and
L=E\oplus F
. For
\pi=\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2}\in{\mathrm{Rep}}(F^{\times}\backslash G(F))
irreducible,
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}{\mathrm{Ext}}^{1}_{F^{\times}\backslash H(F)}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}})\leq 1
with the equality holds if and only if

•

\pi_{2}=\xi\circ\det
,
\pi_{1}=I_{B(E)}^{{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(E)}\chi_{1}\boxtimes\chi_{2}
such that

Abstract
We study the optimal bailout dividend problem with transaction costs for an insurance company, where shareholder payouts align with the arrival times of an independent Poisson process. In this scenario, the underlying risk model follows a spectrally negative Lévy process. Our analysis confirms the optimality of a periodic ( b 1 , b 2 ) (b_{1},b_{2}) -barrier policy with classical reflection at zero. This strategy involves reducing the surplus to b 1 b_{1} when it exceeds b 2 b_{2} at the Poisson arrival times and pushes the surplus to 0 whenever it goes below zero. Keywords: Dividends; capital injection; spectrally negative Lévy process; scale function; periodic barrier strategy. 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification: 91B30; 60G51; 93E20

Introduction

In the context of de Finetti’s dividend problem with transaction costs and capital injection, the aim is to identify an optimal strategy merging dividend payments and capital injection in order to maximize the expected net present value (NPV) involving the subtraction of fixed transaction costs and capital injection expenses from dividend payouts. According to the study by Junca et al.
[
7
]
(also see
[
17
]
), an effective approach for solving this problem is through a reflected
(b_{1},b_{2})
-barrier policy, paired with classical reflection at zero, when the (uncontrolled) surplus process of an insurance company follows a spectrally negative Lévy process. This type of strategy entails reducing the surplus to
b_{1}
when it exceeds
b_{2}
(where
0\leq b_{1}<b_{2}<\infty
), and pushing the surplus to
0
whenever it goes below zero.

Within the insurance literature, where periodic observation models have been widely studied (e.g.,
[
1
,
2
]
), and recognizing the practical nature of dividend-payout decisions occurring at discrete times, this paper builds on the problem mentioned above by introducing a periodic dividend constraint. This adjustment stipulates that dividend payouts to shareholders are made on the arrival times of an independent Poisson process
N^{r}
with intensity
r>0
, while the management of capital injection follows the classical bailout restriction. This entails continuous injection of capital over time to maintain the non-negativity of the controlled process uniformly in time. With the underlying risk model following a spectrally negative Lévy process, our main aim is to establish that the periodic
(b_{1},b_{2})
-barrier policy with classical reflection at zero is optimal for the problem of maximizing the expected NPV across all the admissible periodic-classical strategies. The periodic
(b_{1},b_{2})
-barrier policy stipulates decreasing the surplus to
b_{1}
when it exceeds
b_{2}
at the arrival times of
N^{r}
and injecting capital to set the surplus at
0
whenever it becomes negative.

In previous works, assuming a spectrally negative Lévy model, Noba et al.
[
12
]
studied the bailout dividend problem with periodic dividend payments, excluding transaction costs. They established the optimality of the periodic
b
-barrier policy alongside the classical reflection at zero for this scenario. The periodic
b
-barrier policy involves paying out to the shareholders the excess between the surplus and the barrier
b
at the arrival times of
N^{r}
. Moreover, Avanzi et al.
[
4
]
showed the optimality of the periodic
(b_{1},b_{2})
-barrier policy for the optimal dividend problem involving periodic dividend payments and transaction costs, without capital injection. Our paper builds on and extends these earlier findings in the field.

Since under the periodic
(b_{1},b_{2})
-barrier policy with classical reflection at zero, the surplus is pushed down to
b_{1}
when it exceeds the level
b_{2}
at each arrival time of the Poisson process
N^{r}
, and the strategy pushes the surplus to
0
whenever it goes below zero, the controlled process associated with this strategy can be constructed akin to the Lévy process with Parisian reflection above and classical reflection below introduced in
[
15
]
; for more details see Subsection
2.2
. The latter appears in studies related to problems of stochastic control with periodic strategies such as in
[
4
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
, as its fluctuation identities can efficiently determine the expected NPV of barrier strategies by means of scale functions, enabling the application of the ‘guess and verify’ method to effectively address those optimization problems. However, since the fluctuation identities have not been studied before for a Lévy processes under a periodic
(b_{1},b_{2})
-barrier policy with classical reflection at zero, we will study these identities as a preliminary step to obtain the expected NPV of periodic
(b_{1},b_{2})
-barrier policies with the classical reflection at zero.

Our approach for confirming optimality can be outlined as follows:

(1)

The expected NPV under a periodic
(b_{1},b_{2})
-barrier policy with classical reflection at
0
is explicitly written in terms of the

-scale functions; see Proposition
4.1
. Subsequently, we employ the ‘guess and verify’ procedure, commonly used in the literature.

(2)

The candidate optimal barrier
\pi^{0,\mathbf{b}^{*}}
, with
\mathbf{b}^{*}=(b^{*}_{1},b^{*}_{2})
, is selected based on the conjecture that its expected NPV
v^{\mathbf{b}^{*}}_{\alpha}
, given in (
4.1
), satisfies a smoothness condition at the point
b^{*}_{2}
and that
v^{\mathbf{b}^{*}\prime}_{\alpha}
evaluated at
b^{*}_{1}
becomes one. We then proceed to verify the optimality of this selected barrier strategy by showing that its expected NPV satisfies the corresponding variational inequalities. In order to check the optimality of
\pi^{0,\mathbf{b}^{*}}
, it is important to highlight that we compute the expected value of the Laplace transform of the first down-crossing time for the Lévy process under a periodic
(b_{1},b_{2})
-barrier policy with classical reflection at
0
mentioned before; see Remark
6.5
and Lemma
A.3
. This identity will help us to confirm the monotonicity of
v^{\mathbf{b}^{*}}_{\alpha}
. Furthermore, checking that

is less or equal to the first derivative of the value function of the problem with periodic-classical strategies without transaction costs (studied by Noba et al.
[

2
Formulation of the problem

We assume that the surplus process of an insurance company, in the absence of control, is modelled by a spectrally negative Lévy process
X=\{X(\mathpzc{t}):\mathpzc{t}\geq 0\}
, which is defined on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbbm{P})
, and
\mathbbm{F}\raisebox{0.4pt}{$:$}\hskip-2.84526pt=\{\mathcal{F}_{\mathpzc{t}}:\mathpzc{t}\geq 0\}
is the completed and right-continuous filtration generated by
X
. Recall that a spectrally negative Lévy process is a process that has càdlàg paths, stationary and independent increments and that only jumps downwards. We assume that
X
has non-monotone trajectories to avoid trivial cases.

Throughout this paper, we will denote by
\mathbbm{P}_{x}
the law of
X
, when
X(0)=x\in\mathbbm{R}
, and
\mathbbm{E}_{x}
represents the associated expectation operator. For convenience, we write
\mathbbm{P}\equiv\mathbbm{P}_{0}
and
\mathbbm{E}\equiv\mathbbm{E}_{0}
, when
X(0)=0
.

Denote by
\psi:[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbbm{R}
to the Laplace exponent of
X
, i.e.

\mathbbm{E}\big[\expo^{\theta X(\mathpzc{t})}\big]=:\expo^{\psi(\theta)t},\qquad t,\theta\geq 0,

given by the
Lévy-Khintchine formula

\psi(\theta)\raisebox{0.4pt}{$:$}\hskip-2.84526pt=\mu\theta+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\theta^{2}+\int_{(-\infty,0)}\big(\expo^{\theta z}-1-\theta z\mathbf{1}_{\{z>-1\}}\big)\Pi(\mathrm{d}z),\quad\theta\geq 0,

where
\mu\in\mathbbm{R}
,
\sigma\geq 0
, and
\Pi
is the Lévy measure of
X
defined on
(-\infty,0)
satisfying

\int_{(-\infty,0)}(1\land z^{2})\Pi(\mathrm{d}z)<\infty.

It is known that
X
has paths of bounded variation if and only if
\sigma=0
and
\int_{(-1,0)}|z|\Pi(\mathrm{d}z)<\infty
. Its Laplace exponent is given by

\psi(\theta)=c\theta+\int_{(-\infty,0)}\big(\expo^{\theta z}-1\big)\Pi(\mathrm{d}z),\quad\theta\geq 0,

where
c\raisebox{0.4pt}{$:$}\hskip-2.84526pt=\gamma-\int_{(-1,0)}z\Pi(\mathrm{d}z)>0
since we omitted the case of monotone paths. For more details about general Lévy processes, the reader can consult e.g.
[
9
]
.

2.1
Bailout optimal dividend problem with Poissonian decision times and a fixed transaction cost

Assume that the insurance company pays dividends to the shareholders at the arrival times
\mathcal{T}_{r}\raisebox{0.4pt}{$:$}\hskip-2.84526pt=\{T(i);i\geq 1\}
of a Poisson process
N^{r}=\{N^{r}(\mathpzc{t});\mathpzc{t}\geq 0\}
with intensity
r>0
and independent of
X
. The process
N^{r}
is defined on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbbm{H},\mathbbm{P})
, where
\mathbbm{H}=\{\mathcal{H}_{t}\}_{\mathpzc{t}\geq 0}
is the right-continuous complete filtration generated by
(X,N^{r})
. In this case, the dividend strategy
L_{r}^{\pi}
has the following form

\displaystyle L_{r}^{\pi}(\mathpzc{t})=\int_{[0,\mathpzc{t}]}\nu^{\pi}(\mathpzc{s})\mathrm{d}N^{r}(\mathpzc{s}),\qquad\text{$\mathpzc{t}\geq 0$,}

(2.1)

for some càglàd process
\nu^{\pi}
adapted to the filtration
\mathbbm{H}
.

Additionally, the shareholders must inject capital into the insurance company to avoid the event of ruin. The total capital injected to the insurance company until time
\mathpzc{t}
is represented by the process
R_{r}^{\pi}=\{R_{r}^{\pi}(\mathpzc{t}):\mathpzc{t}\geq 0\}
, which is non-decreasing, right-continuous, and
\mathbbm{H}
-adapted, with
R_{r}^{\pi}(0-)=0
. Notice that capital injection can be made continuously in time. In addition, the process
R_{r}^{\pi}
must satisfy

\mathbbm{E}_{x}\bigg[\int_{[0,\infty)}\expo^{-q\mathpzc{t}}\mathrm{d}R_{r}^{\pi}(\mathpzc{t})\bigg]<\infty,\quad x\geq 0,

(2.2)

where
q>0
represents the rate of discounting.
The corresponding controlled process associated with the strategy
\pi=(L^{\pi}_{r},R^{\pi}_{r})
is given by
U_{r}^{\pi}(0-)=X(0)
and

\displaystyle U_{r}^{\pi}(\mathpzc{t})\raisebox{0.4pt}{$:$}\hskip-2.84526pt=X(\mathpzc{t})-L_{r}^{\pi}(\mathpzc{t})+R_{r}^{\pi}(\mathpzc{t}),\quad t\geq 0.

We denote by
\mathcal{A}
the set of strategies
\pi
satisfying (
2.1
)–(
2.2
) with the additional constraint that the surplus of the insurance company has to be non-negative uniformly in time i.e.
U_{r}^{\pi}(\mathpzc{t})\geq 0
for all
t\geq 0
a.s. We call a strategy
\pi

admissible
if
\pi\in\mathcal{A}
.

Given
\alpha>0
and
\beta>1
, the constant costs per unit of dividend payments and capital injected respectively, our aim is to maximize the expected net present value (NPV)

v^{\pi}_{\alpha}(x)\raisebox{0.4pt}{$:$}\hskip-2.84526pt=\mathbbm{E}_{x}\bigg[\int_{[0,\infty)}\expo^{-q\mathpzc{t}}\mathrm{d}\bigg[L_{r}^{\pi}(\mathpzc{t})-\alpha\sum_{0\leq\mathpzc{s}\leq\mathpzc{t}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\Delta L^{\pi}_{r}(\mathpzc{s})\neq 0\}}\bigg]-\beta\int_{[0,\infty)}\expo^{-q\mathpzc{t}}\mathrm{d}R_{r}^{\pi}(\mathpzc{t})\bigg],\quad x\geq 0,

(2.3)

over all
\pi\in\mathcal{A}
, where
\Delta L^{\pi}_{r}(\mathpzc{s})\raisebox{0.4pt}{$:$}\hskip-2.84526pt=L^{\pi}_{r}(\mathpzc{s})-L^{\pi}_{r}(\mathpzc{s}-)
. Hence, our goal is to find the value function of the problem

v_{\alpha}(x)\raisebox{0.4pt}{$:$}\hskip-2.84526pt=\sup_{\pi\in\mathcal{A}}v^{\pi}_{\alpha}(x),\quad x\geq 0,

(2.4)

and obtain an optimal strategy
\pi^{*}\in\mathcal{A}
whose expected NPV,
v^{\pi^{*}}_{\alpha}
, coincides with
v_{\alpha}
if such a strategy exists.

Throughout the paper, we assume the following additional condition.

Assumption 2.1
.

We assume that
\psi^{\prime}(0+)=\mathbbm{E}[X(1)]>-\infty
.

2.2
Periodic-classical barrier strategies

To solve the problem mentioned above, we will study some properties of the expected NPVs of the strategies
\pi^{0,\mathbf{b}}
which are conformed by a periodic
\mathbf{b}
-barrier strategy, with
\mathbf{b}\raisebox{0.4pt}{$:$}\hskip-2.84526pt=(b_{1},b_{2})
such that
0\leq b_{1}<b_{2}
, and a capital injection process
R^{0,\mathbf{b}}_{r}
which is given by the classical reflection at zero. The reason for doing this, is because our main purpose is to verify that an optimal strategy for the problem (
2.4
) is of the periodic-classical
\mathbf{b}
-barrier type.

So, given the barrier
\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},b_{2})
, with
0\leq b_{1}<b_{2}
, the strategy
\pi^{0,\mathbf{b}}
and the (controlled) surplus process
U_{r}^{0,\mathbf{b}}
are constructed as follows.

Let
R^{0,\mathbf{b}}_{r}(\mathpzc{t})\raisebox{0.4pt}{$:$}\hskip-2.84526pt=\bigg[-\inf_{0\leq\mathpzc{s}\leq\mathpzc{t}}X(\mathpzc{s})\bigg]\vee 0
for
\mathpzc{t}\geq 0
, then we have

\displaystyle U_{r}^{0,\mathbf{b}}(\mathpzc{t})=X(\mathpzc{t})+R^{0,\mathbf{b}}_{r}(\mathpzc{t}),\quad 0\leq t<\widehat{T}_{\mathbf{b}}(1),

where
\widehat{T}_{\mathbf{b}}(1)\raisebox{0.4pt}{$:$}\hskip-2.84526pt=\inf\{T(i):\;X(T(i)-)+R^{0,\mathbf{b}}_{r}(T(i)-)>b_{2}\}
. Here and throughout of the paper, let
\inf\emptyset=\infty
.
The process then jumps down by
X(\widehat{T}_{\mathbf{b}}(1))+R^{0,\mathbf{b}}_{r}(\widehat{T}_{\mathbf{b}}(1))-b_{1}

so that
U_{r}^{0,\mathbf{b}}(\widehat{T}_{\mathbf{b}}(1))=b_{1}
. For
\widehat{T}_{\mathbf{b}}(1)\leq t<\widehat{T}_{\mathbf{b}}(2)\raisebox{0.4pt}{$:$}\hskip-2.84526pt=\inf\{T(i)>\widehat{T}_{\mathbf{b}}(1):\;U_{r}^{0,\mathbf{b}}(T(i)-)>b_{2}\}
,
U_{r}^{0,\mathbf{b}}(\mathpzc{t})
is the process reflected at
0
of the process
(X(\mathpzc{t})-X(\widehat{T}_{\mathbf{b}}(1))+b_{1};t\geq\widehat{T}_{\mathbf{b}}(1))
. The process
U_{r}^{0,\mathbf{b}}
can be constructed by repeating this procedure.
It is clear that it admits a decomposition

\displaystyle U_{r}^{0,\mathbf{b}}(\mathpzc{t})=X(\mathpzc{t})-L_{r}^{0,\mathbf{b}}(\mathpzc{t})+R_{r}^{0,\mathbf{b}}(\mathpzc{t}),\quad t\geq 0.

Notice that for
\mathbf{b}
, the strategy
\pi^{0,\mathbf{b}}\raisebox{0.4pt}{$:$}\hskip-2.84526pt=(L_{r}^{0,\mathbf{b}},R_{r}^{0,\mathbf{b}})
is admissible for the problem described at the beginning of this section, since (
2.2
) holds by Assumption
2.1
and Proposition
4.1
. We denote its expected NPV
v^{\pi^{0,\mathbf{b}}}_{\alpha}
, defined in (
2.3
), by
v^{\mathbf{b}}_{\alpha}
.

Since the expression of
v_{\alpha}^{\mathbf{b}}
is given trough the
q
-scale functions, we shall give some preliminaries about them. Additionally, we shall mention about the optimal bailout dividend problems without transaction costs when dividends are made periodical in time. This problem was studied by Noba et al.
[
12
]

Preliminaries

For each
q\geq 0
, there exists a function
W^{(q)}
, called the
q
-scale function such that
W^{(q)}(x)=0
for
x\in(-\infty,0)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\theta x}W^{(q)}(x)\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{\psi(\theta)-q},\quad\theta>\Phi(q),

(3.1)

where

\Phi(q)\raisebox{0.4pt}{$:$}\hskip-2.84526pt=\sup\{\lambda\geq 0:\psi(\lambda)=q\}.

(3.2)

We also define, for
x\in\mathbbm{R}

\displaystyle\overline{W}^{(q)}(x)\raisebox{0.4pt}{$:$}\hskip-2.84526pt=\int_{0}^{x}W^{(q)}(y)\mathrm{d}y,\quad\overline{\overline{W}}^{(q)}(x)\raisebox{0.4pt}{$:$}\hskip-2.84526pt=\int_{0}^{x}\overline{W}^{(q)}(y)\mathrm{d}y,

\displaystyle Z^{(q)}(x)\raisebox{0.4pt}{$:$}\hskip-2.84526pt=1+q\overline{W}^{(q)}(x),\quad\overline{Z}^{(q)}(x)\raisebox{0.4pt}{$:$}\hskip-2.84526pt=\int_{0}^{x}Z^{(q)}(z)\mathrm{d}z=x+q\int_{0}^{x}\int_{0}^{z}W^{(q)}(w)\mathrm{d}w\mathrm{d}z.

Notice that
{\overline{W}}^{(q)}(x)=\overline{\overline{W}}^{(q)}(x)=0
,
Z^{(q)}(x)=1
and
\overline{Z}^{(q)}(x)=x
for
x\leq 0
, due to
W^{(q)}(x)=0
for
x\in(-\infty,0)
.

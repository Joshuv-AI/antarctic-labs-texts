m_{j,k,min}=\operatornamewithlimits{argmin}_{m\in\mathcal{K}\mbox{ with }(k,m)\in\mathcal{E}_{j,\cdot}}\hat{\beta}_{j,k}-\hat{\beta}_{j,m}.

Let
L_{j,k,min}=\{m\in\mathcal{K}:\hat{\beta}_{j,m}=\hat{\beta}_{j,m_{j,k,min}}\mbox{ and there is a connected path }(l,l+1,\dots,m_{min},\dots,l+q)\mbox{ s.t. either }(l_{i},l_{i+1})\in\mathcal{F}\mbox{ or }(l_{i+1},l_{i})\in\mathcal{F}\mbox{ and }\hat{\beta}_{j,l_{i}}=\hat{\beta}_{j,l_{i+1}}\mbox{ for }i=0,\dots,q\}
, in other words,
L_{k,j,min}
is the indices of all coefficients which have been fused to each other along a path of pairwise fusings and have their coefficients equal to
\hat{\beta}_{j,m_{j,k,min}}
. Then similar to the previous KKT conditions and with
G_{j,k}
defined as in the proof of selection consistency, we have

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{0}={}

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}-\sum_{m\in L_{j,1,min}}{\boldsymbol{X}_{j}^{\top}}(\boldsymbol{Y}_{\cdot,m}-\boldsymbol{X}\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\cdot,m})\\
\vdots\\
-\sum_{m\in L_{j,k_{0},min}}{\boldsymbol{X}_{j}^{\top}}(\boldsymbol{Y}_{\cdot,m}-\boldsymbol{X}\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\cdot,m})\\
\end{pmatrix}+\lambda_{1}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{v}_{j,1}\\
\vdots\\
\mathbf{v}_{j,k_{0}}\\
\end{pmatrix}+\lambda_{2}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{b}_{j,1}\\
\vdots\\
\mathbf{b}_{j,k_{0}}\\
\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\equiv{}

\displaystyle\Phi+\lambda_{1}\mathbf{v}+\lambda_{2}\mathbf{b},

where

\mathbf{v}_{j,k^{\prime}}=N\cdot\sum_{m\in L_{j,k^{\prime},min}}\sum_{G\in\mathcal{G}\mbox{ s.t. }m\in G}\lambda_{G,j}t_{j,m,G},

and

\displaystyle\mathbf{b}_{j,k^{\prime}}={}

\displaystyle N\cdot\sum_{m\in L_{j,k^{\prime},min}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\in\mathcal{K}\mbox{ s.t. }(l,m)\in\mathcal{F}\mbox{ or }(m,l)\in\mathcal{F}\\
\mbox{ and }\beta_{j,l}^{0}\neq\beta_{j,m}^{0}\end{subarray}}\lambda_{2,j,l,m}\cdot r_{j,m,l}

(13)

\displaystyle+N\cdot\sum_{m\in L_{j,k^{\prime},min}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\in\mathcal{K}\mbox{ s.t. }(l,m)\in\mathcal{F}\mbox{ or }(m,l)\in\mathcal{F}\\
\mbox{ and }\beta_{j,l}^{0}=\beta_{j,m}^{0},\hat{\beta}_{j,m_{j,k^{\prime},min}}<\hat{\beta}_{j,l}\end{subarray}}\lambda_{2,j,l,m}\cdot r_{j,m,l}.

(14)

As before,
||N^{-1/2}\Phi||_{2}=O_{p}(1)
. By similar arguments as before, each term in
{N}^{-1/2}\mathbf{v}_{j,k^{\prime}}
converges to zero in probability and each term in (
13
) divided by
\sqrt{N}
converges to 0 in probability, whereas the terms in (
14
) divided by
\sqrt{N}
converge to
-\infty
in probability and hence we have arrived at a similar contradiction as in the proof of selection consistency. Repeating these arguments for an appropriately defined
L_{k,j,max}
results in the conclusion that for
(l,m)\in\mathcal{E}_{\cdot,j}
we have
P((l,m)\in\hat{\mathcal{E}}_{\cdot,j})\to 0
and hence we have completed the proof.

∎

Appendix B.2.1
Data generation

For each replication of the simulation, we generate data under model (
), where
\mathbf{x}_{i}
are generated as i.i.d. Bernoulli
(0.2)
random variables. The responses are generated as a multivariate ordered probit model where the observed responses
\mathbf{y}_{i}
take 8 levels determined by an unobserved latent response
\mathbf{y}^{*}_{i}
, which is generated as
\mathbf{y}^{*}_{i}={\boldsymbol{\beta}^{0}}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{i}\text{ for }i=1,\dots,N
. The
j
th observed response for the
i
th individual is then determined by

\mathbf{y}_{ij}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if}\ \mathbf{y}^{*}_{ij}\leq-2\\
2,&\text{if}\ -2<\mathbf{y}^{*}_{ij}\leq-1\\
3,&\text{if}\ -1<\mathbf{y}^{*}_{ij}\leq-1/2\\
4,&\text{if}\ -1/2<\mathbf{y}^{*}_{ij}\leq 0\\
5,&\text{if}\ 0<\mathbf{y}^{*}_{ij}\leq 1/2\\
6,&\text{if}\ 1/2<\mathbf{y}^{*}_{ij}\leq 1\\
7,&\text{if}\ 1<\mathbf{y}^{*}_{ij}\leq 2\\
8,&\text{if}\ \mathbf{y}^{*}_{ij}>2.\\
\end{cases}

The error vectors
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{i}
for the latent responses are generated as i.i.d. multivariate normal random variables with mean vector
\boldsymbol{0}
and covariance matrix
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}=[\sigma_{\epsilon jk}]_{j,k=1}^{K}
, where
\sigma_{\epsilon jk}=4\left(0.5^{|j-k|}\right)
. The dimensionality of the outcome/error vector is
K=8
and the outcomes form 3 groups, with the first three outcomes forming group 1, the fourth and fifth forming group 2, and the last three outcomes forming group 3. The variable effects
\boldsymbol{\beta}^{0}
are generated in the exact same manner as in the simulation study in the main text, which for reference is

\boldsymbol{\beta}^{0}=\begin{pmatrix}\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\smash{\overbrace{\phantom{\begin{matrix}\xi_{1,1}\xi^{G}_{1,1}\eta_{1,1}&\xi_{1,2}\xi^{G}_{1,1}\eta_{1,2}&\xi_{1,3}\xi^{G}_{1,1}\eta_{1,3}\end{matrix}}}^{\text{$G_{1,1}$}}}$}\xi_{1,1}\xi^{G}_{1,1}\eta_{1,1}&\xi_{1,2}\xi^{G}_{1,1}\eta_{1,2}&\xi_{1,3}\xi^{G}_{1,1}\eta_{1,3}&\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\smash{\overbrace{\phantom{\begin{matrix}\xi_{1,4}\xi^{G}_{1,2}\eta_{1,4}&\xi_{1,5}\xi^{G}_{1,2}\eta_{1,5}\end{matrix}}}^{\text{$G_{1,2}$}}}$}\xi_{1,4}\xi^{G}_{1,2}\eta_{1,4}&\xi_{1,5}\xi^{G}_{1,2}\eta_{1,5}&\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\smash{\overbrace{\phantom{\begin{matrix}\xi_{1,6}\xi^{G}_{1,3}\eta_{1,6}&\xi_{1,7}\xi^{G}_{1,3}\eta_{1,7}&\xi_{1,8}\xi^{G}_{1,3}\eta_{1,8}\end{matrix}}}^{\text{$G_{1,3}$}}}$}\xi_{1,6}\xi^{G}_{1,3}\eta_{1,6}&\xi_{1,7}\xi^{G}_{1,3}\eta_{1,7}&\xi_{1,8}\xi^{G}_{1,3}\eta_{1,8}\\
\vdots&&&&&&&\vdots\\
\xi_{z,1}\xi^{G}_{z,1}\eta_{z,1}&\xi_{z,2}\xi^{G}_{z,1}\eta_{z,2}&\xi_{z,3}\xi^{G}_{z,1}\eta_{z,3}&\xi_{z,4}\xi^{G}_{z,2}\eta_{z,4}&\xi_{z,5}\xi^{G}_{z,2}\eta_{z,5}&\xi_{z,6}\xi^{G}_{z,3}\eta_{z,6}&\xi_{z,7}\xi^{G}_{z,3}\eta_{z,7}&\xi_{z,8}\xi^{G}_{z,3}\eta_{z,8}\\
0&\cdots&&&&&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&&&&&&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&&&&&\cdots&0\end{pmatrix},

where the last
p-z
rows of
\boldsymbol{\beta}^{0}
have all elements as 0, the terms
\xi_{j,k}\sim
Bernoulli
(0.9)
induce sparsity at the individual effect level, the terms
\xi^{G}_{j,1},\xi^{G}_{j,2},\xi^{G}_{j,3}\sim
Bernoulli
(1-p_{\text{HS}})

\eta_{j,k}
are distributed i.i.d. uniformly from
\{-1,-0.5,-0.25,-0.125,0.125,0.25,0.5,1\}

j

\eta_{j,k}
for
k

p_{\text{GE}}/2
and independently, the terms
\eta_{j,k}
for all outcomes
k=1,\dots,8
are set to be all equal to
\eta_{j,1}
with probability
p_{\text{GE}}/2
. The latter process induces effects for some variables within a group to be equal to each other and induces effects for some variables to be equal for all outcomes.

We explore dimensions of
p=50,100,
and
200

From relative Rota-Baxter operators and relative averaging operators on Lie algebras to relative Rota-Baxter operators on Leibniz algebras: a uniform approach
Rong Tang, Yunhe Sheng, Friedrich Wagemann

Abstract
. In this paper, first we construct two subcategories (using symmetric representations and antisymmetric representations) of the category of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Leibniz algebras, and establish the relations with the categories of relative Rota-Baxter operators and relative averaging operators on Lie algebras. Then we show that there is a short exact sequence describing the relation between the controlling algebra of relative Rota-Baxter operators on a Leibniz algebra with respect to a symmetric (resp. antisymmetric) representation and the controlling algebra of the induced relative Rota-Baxter operators (resp. averaging operators) on the canonical Lie algebra associated to the Leibniz algebra. Finally, we show that there is a long exact sequence describing the relation between the cohomology groups of a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Leibniz algebra with respect to a symmetric (resp. antisymmetric) representation and the cohomology groups of the induced relative Rota-Baxter operator (resp. averaging operator) on the canonical Lie algebra.



1.
Introduction

The purpose of this paper is to provide a unified approach to study relative Rota-Baxter operators and relative averaging operators on Lie algebras.

1.1.
Relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie algebras

G. Baxter introduced the concept of a Rota-Baxter commutative algebra
[
8
]
in his study of fluctuation theory in probability. A Rota-Baxter operator of weight zero on an associative algebra
(A,\cdot)
is a linear map
\mathcal{R}:A\,\rightarrow\,A
such that

(\mathcal{R}x)\cdot(\mathcal{R}y)=\mathcal{R}((\mathcal{R}x)\cdot y+x\cdot(\mathcal{R}y)),\quad\forall x,y\in A.

These operators with general weights have been found many applications in recent years, including the algebraic approach of Connes-Kreimer
[
13
]
to renormalization of perturbative quantum field theory, quantum analogue of Poisson geometry
[
48
]
, twisting on associative algebras
[
49
]
, dendriform algebras and associative
Yang-Baxter equations
[
1
]
. The notion of Rota-Baxter operators on Lie algebras was introduced independently in the 1980s as the operator form of the classical Yang-Baxter equation. A linear operator
\mathcal{R}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is called a Rota-Baxter operator of weight zero if the following condition is satisfied:

[\mathcal{R}(x),\mathcal{R}(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathcal{R}([\mathcal{R}(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,\mathcal{R}(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}),\quad\forall~x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Moreover, Kupershmidt introduced the notion of a relative Rota-Baxter operator (also called an
{\mathcal{O}}
-operator) on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to arbitrary representation in
[
29
]
.
Note that a skew-symmetric classical
r
-matrix is a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie algebra with respect to the coadjoint representation. Relative Rota-Baxter operators play important roles in the study of integrable systems
[
6
,
40
]
, provide solutions of the classical Yang-Baxter equation in the semidirect product Lie algebra and give rise to pre-Lie algebras
[
5
]
. See
[
21
]
for more details. Recently, the deformation and homotopy theory of relative Rota-Baxter Lie algebras and relative Rota-Baxter associative algebras were established in
[
15
,
30
,
44
,
45
]
.

1.2.
Averaging operators on Lie algebras

An averaging operator on an associative algebra
(A,\cdot)
is a linear operator
\mathcal{R}:A\,\rightarrow\,A
such that

(\mathcal{R}x)\cdot(\mathcal{R}y)=\mathcal{R}((\mathcal{R}x)\cdot y)=\mathcal{R}(x\cdot(\mathcal{R}y)),\quad\forall x,y\in A.

In the last century, many studies on averaging operators were done for various special algebras, such as function spaces, Banach algebras, and the topics and methods were largely
analytic
[
7
,
12
,
24
,
39
]
. Recently, it was found that commutative double algebra structures
[
20
]
on a vector space
V
can be described by symmetric averaging operators on the associative algebra
\mathrm{End}(V)
. Averaging operators can be defined on algebras over arbitrary binary operad
[
1
]
. In particular,
a linear operator
\mathcal{R}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is called an averaging operator if the following condition is satisfied:

[\mathcal{R}(x),\mathcal{R}(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathcal{R}([\mathcal{R}(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}),\quad\forall~x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

There is a close relation between the classical Yang-Baxter equation, conformal algebras and averaging operators on Lie algebras
[
26
]
.

Averaging operators on Lie algebras are also called embedding tensors in some mathematical physics literatures. Embedding tensors and their associated tensor hierarchies provide an
algebraic and efficient way to construct supergravity theories and further to construct higher gauge theories (see e.g.
[
10
,
23
,
28
,
42
]
). Recently the controlling algebra and the cohomology theory of embedding tensors were established in
[
41
]
using the higher derived bracket. Meanwhile the cohomology and homotopy theory of averaging associative algebras were studied in
[
52
]
.

The concept of Rota-Baxter operators and averaging operators were further studied on the level of algebraic operads in
[
3
,
35
,
36
,
37
,
50
,
51
]
. The action of the Rota-Baxter operator on
a binary quadratic operad splits the operad, and the action of the averaging operator on a binary quadratic operad
duplicates the operad. Since the splitting and duplication processes are in Koszul duality, one can regard the Rota-Baxter operator and averaging operator to be Koszul dual to each other.

1.3.
Relative Rota-Baxter operators on Leibniz algebras

Leibniz algebras were first discovered by Bloh who called them D-algebras
[
9
]
. Then Loday rediscovered this algebraic structure and called them Leibniz algebras with the motivation in the study of the periodicity in algebraic K-theory
[
31
,
33
,
34
]
. Averaging operators on Lie algebras give rise to Leibniz algebras, which can be viewed as the duplication of Lie algebras. From the viewpoint of the operad theory,
leibniz algebras are defined as the algebras over the
Leibniz
operad which is the duplicator of the
Lie
operad
[
36
]
. More intrinsically, let
\mathcal{P}
be a binary quadratic operad. The algebra duplicates by an averaging operator on the
\mathcal{P}
-algebra is a
\mathrm{Du}(\mathcal{P})
-algebra. On the other hand, the algebra splits by a Rota-Baxter operator on the
\mathcal{P}
-algebra is a
\mathrm{BSu}(\mathcal{P})
-algebra. Since
\mathrm{Du}(\mathcal{P})
is the Koszul dual of
\mathrm{BSu}(\mathcal{P}^{!})

[
36
]
,
the duplication construction of an averaging operator is a kind of Koszul dual of the splitting construction of a Rota-Baxter operator
[
3
,
36
]
. The (co)homology and homotopy theories of Leibniz algebras were established in
[
18
,
33
,
38
,
2
]
. Recently Leibniz algebras were studied from different aspects due to applications in both mathematics and physics.
In particular, integration of Leibniz algebras were studied in
[
11
,
14
]
and deformation quantization of Leibniz algebras was studied in
[
16
]
. As the underlying algebraic structures of embedding tensors and Courant algebroids, Leibniz algebras also have application in higher gauge theories
[
10
,
28
,
43
]
and homogeneous spaces
[
25
]
. The notion of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Leibniz algebras was introduced in
[
46
]
to study Leibniz bialgebras. Moreover, the cohomology theory of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Leibniz algebras was given in
[
47
]
and used to classify linear deformations and formal deformations.

1.4.
Outline of the paper

In this paper, we propose a unified approach to study relative Rota-Baxter operators and relative averaging operators on Lie algebras using relative Rota-Baxter operators on Leibniz algebras. First we observe that there are forgetful functors from the categories of relative Rota-Baxter operators and relative averaging operators on Lie algebras to the category of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Leibniz algebras. Conversely, we construct two functors from certain subcategories of the category of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Leibniz algebras to the categories of relative Rota-Baxter operators and relative averaging operators on Lie algebras, and show that they are left adjoint for the aforementioned forgetful functors. Then we show that the controlling algebras of relative Rota-Baxter operators and relative averaging operators on Lie algebras can be derived from the controlling algebra of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Leibniz algebras. Finally, we give a long exact sequence to describe the relation between the cohomology groups of a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Leibniz algebra with respect to a symmetric (resp. antisymmetric) representation and the cohomology groups of the induced relative Rota-Baxter operator (resp. averaging operator) on the canonical Lie algebra. The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, using symmetric representations and antisymmetric representations, we construct two subcategories of the category of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Leibniz algebras, and establish the relations with the categories of relative Rota-Baxter operators and relative averaging operators on Lie algebras. In Section
3
, we show that there is a short exact sequence describing the relation between the controlling algebra of relative Rota-Baxter operators on a Leibniz algebra with respect to a symmetric representation and the controlling algebra of the induced relative Rota-Baxter operators on the canonical Lie algebra. For relative averaging operators, we establish a similar result. In Section
4
, first we introduce the Loday-Pirashvili cohomology of a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie algebra and then use a long exact sequence to describe the relation between the cohomology groups of a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Leibniz algebra with respect to a symmetric representation and the Loday-Pirashvili cohomology groups of the induced relative Rota-Baxter operator on the canonical Lie algebra. Similarly, there is a long exact sequence describing the relation between the cohomology groups of a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Leibniz algebra with respect to an antisymmetric representation and the cohomology groups of the induced relative averaging operator on the canonical Lie algebra.

In this paper, we work over an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic 0 and all the vector spaces are over
\mathbb{K}
and finite-dimensional.

2.
Relations between the categories
\mathsf{AO}\mathsf{Lie}
,
\mathsf{RBO}\mathsf{Lie}
and
\mathsf{RBO}\mathsf{Leibniz}

In this section, we establish the relations between certain subcategories of the category of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Leibniz algebras and the category of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie algebras as well as the category of relative averaging operators on Lie algebras.

2.1.
Relative Rota-Baxter operators and relative averaging operators

Definition 2.1
.

(
[
29
]
)

A
relative Rota-Baxter operator
on a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{g}})
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho)
is a linear map
T:V\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
satisfying the following quadratic constraint:

(1)

[Tu,Tv]_{\mathfrak{g}}=T\big(\rho(Tu)(v)-\rho(Tv)(u)\big),\quad\forall u,v\in V.

Let
T:V\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
(resp.
T^{\prime}:V^{\prime}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
) be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{g}})
(resp.
(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime},\{-,-\}_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}})
) with respect to the representation
(V;\rho)
(resp.
(V^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})
). A
homomorphism
from
T
to
T^{\prime}
is a pair
(\phi,\varphi)
, where
\phi:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
is a Lie algebra homomorphism,
\varphi:V\longrightarrow V^{\prime}
is a linear map such that

(2)

\displaystyle T^{\prime}\circ\varphi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi\circ T,

(3)

\displaystyle\varphi\rho(x)(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho^{\prime}(\phi(x))(\varphi(u)),\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g},u\in V.

In particular, if
\phi
and
\varphi
are invertible, then
(\phi,\varphi)
is called an
isomorphism
.

We denote by
\mathsf{RBO}\mathsf{Lie}
the category of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie algebras.

Definition 2.2
.

(
[
1
,
39
]
)

A
relative averaging operator
on a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{g}})
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho)
is a linear map
T:V\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
satisfying the following quadratic constraint:

(4)

[Tu,Tv]_{\mathfrak{g}}=T\big(\rho(Tu)(v)\big),\quad\forall u,v\in V.

Let
T:V\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
(resp.
T^{\prime}:V^{\prime}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
) be a relative averaging operator on the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{g}})
(resp.
(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime},\{-,-\}_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}})
) with respect to the representation
(V;\rho)
(resp.
(V^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})
). A
homomorphism
from
T
to
T^{\prime}
is a pair
(\phi,\varphi)
, where
\phi:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
is a Lie algebra homomorphism,
\varphi:V\longrightarrow V^{\prime}
is a linear map such that

(5)

\displaystyle T^{\prime}\circ\varphi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi\circ T,

(6)

\displaystyle\varphi\rho(x)(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho^{\prime}(\phi(x))(\varphi(u)),\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g},u\in V.

In particular, if
\phi
and
\varphi
are invertible, then
(\phi,\varphi)
is called an
isomorphism
.

We denote by
\mathsf{AO}\mathsf{Lie}
the category of relative averaging operators on Lie algebras.

A (left)
Leibniz algebra
is a vector space
\mathfrak{G}
together with a bilinear operation
[-,-]_{\mathfrak{G}}:\mathfrak{G}\otimes\mathfrak{G}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{G}
such that

\displaystyle[x,[y,z]_{\mathfrak{G}}]_{\mathfrak{G}}=[[x,y]_{\mathfrak{G}},z]_{\mathfrak{G}}+[y,[x,z]_{\mathfrak{G}}]_{\mathfrak{G}},\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{G}.

A
representation
of a Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{G},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{G}})
is a triple
(V;\rho^{L},\rho^{R})
, where
V
is a vector space,
\rho^{L},\rho^{R}:\mathfrak{G}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{gl}(V)
are linear maps such that for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{G}
,

\displaystyle\rho^{L}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{G}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\rho^{L}(x),\rho^{L}(y)],

\displaystyle\rho^{R}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{G}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\rho^{L}(x),\rho^{R}(y)],

\displaystyle\rho^{R}(y)\circ\rho^{L}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\rho^{R}(y)\circ\rho^{R}(x).

Here
[-,-]
is the commutator Lie bracket on
\mathfrak{gl}(V)
. There are two important kinds of representations of Leibniz algebras:

•

a representation
(V;\rho^{L},\rho^{R})
is called
symmetric
if
\rho^{R}=-\rho^{L}
;

•

a representation
(V;\rho^{L},\rho^{R})
is called
antisymmetric
if
\rho^{R}=0
.

For any representation
(V;\rho^{L},\rho^{R})
of a Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{G}
, we define a vector subspace
V_{\rm anti}
which is spanned by all elements
\rho^{L}(x)v+\rho^{R}(x)v
with
x\in\mathfrak{G}
and
v\in V
. Directly from
\rho^{R}(y)\circ\rho^{L}(x)=-\rho^{R}(y)\circ\rho^{R}(x)
for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{G}
, we deduce that
(V_{\rm anti};\rho^{L},\rho^{R})
is an antisymmetric representation. The quotient representation
V/V_{\rm anti}
is then symmetric, denoted by
V_{\rm sym}
, and we have an exact sequence

(7)

0\to V_{\rm anti}\to V\to V_{\rm sym}\to 0.

Now we recall the notion of a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Leibniz algebra.

\displaystyle T^{\prime}\circ\varphi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi{\rho^{L}}(x)(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rho^{L}}^{\prime}(\phi(x))(\varphi(u)),

(11)

\displaystyle\varphi{\rho^{R}}(x)(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rho^{R}}^{\prime}(\phi(x))(\varphi(u)).

In particular, if
\phi
and
\varphi
are invertible, then
(\phi,\varphi)
is called an
isomorphism
from
T
to
T^{\prime}
.

We denote by
\mathsf{RBO}\mathsf{Leibniz}
the category of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Leibniz algebras. Moreover, we denote by
\mathsf{RBO}\mathsf{LeibnizS}
and
\mathsf{RBO}\mathsf{LeibnizA}
the categories of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Leibniz algebras with respect to symmetric representations and antisymmetric representations respectively.

2.2.
Relation to crossed modules

Note that relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie algebras and Leibniz algebras
T:V\to\mathfrak{g}
resp.
T:V\to\mathfrak{G}
as well as relative averaging operators on Lie algebras
T:V\to\mathfrak{g}
give rise to a bracket on
V
.

For a relative Rota-Baxter operator
T:V\to\mathfrak{g}
on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, the bracket

[v,w]_{T}:=\rho(Tv)w-\rho(Tw)v

for all
v,w\in V
renders
V
a Lie algebra such that
T:V\to\mathfrak{g}
becomes a morphism of Lie algebras.

In the same vein, given a relative Rota-Baxter operator
T:V\to\mathfrak{G}
on a Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{G}
, the bracket

[v,w]_{T}:=\rho^{L}(Tv)w+\rho^{R}(Tw)v

for all
v,w\in V
renders
V
a Leibniz algebra such that
T:V\to\mathfrak{G}
becomes a morphism of Leibniz algebras.

Still in the same vein, given a relative averaging operator
T:V\to\mathfrak{g}
on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, the bracket

[v,w]_{T}:=\rho(Tv)w

for all
v,w\in V
renders
V
a Leibniz algebra such that
T:V\to\mathfrak{g}
is a morphism of Leibniz algebras.

The next proposition shows that with an equivariance condition, the action of
\mathfrak{g}
(resp.
\mathfrak{G}
) on V becomes an action by derivations with respect to these brackets. This kind of equivariance condition is present, for example, in Loday-Pirashvili’s tensor category of linear maps, see
[
32
]
.

Proposition 2.4
.

(1)

Let
T:V\to\mathfrak{g}
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie algebra and suppose that
T
is equivariant in the sense that
T(\rho(x)v)=[x,Tv]_{\mathfrak{g}}
for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}
and all
v\in V
. We deduce that
\rho(x)
for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}
is a derivation of the Lie algebra
(V,[-,-]_{T})
. Thus, we obtain that
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Der}(V)
is homomorphism of Lie algebras.

(2)

Let
T:V\to\mathfrak{G}
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Leibniz algebra and suppose that
T
is equivariant in the sense that
T(\rho^{L}(x)v)=[x,Tv]_{\mathfrak{G}}
and
T(\rho^{R}(x)v)=[Tv,x]_{\mathfrak{G}}
for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}
and all
v\in V
. Then
\mathfrak{G}
acts on
(V,[-,-]_{T})
by derivations (in the Leibniz sense), i.e. for all
x\in\mathfrak{G}
and all
v,w\in V

\displaystyle\rho^{L}(x)[v,w]_{T}=[\rho^{L}(x)v,w]_{T}+[v,\rho^{L}(x)w]_{T},

\displaystyle\rho^{R}(x)[v,w]_{T}=[v,\rho^{R}(x)w]_{T}-[w,\rho^{R}(x)v]_{T},

\displaystyle[\rho^{L}(x)v,w]_{T}+[\rho^{R}(x)v,w]_{T}=0.

We deduce that the pair
(\rho^{L}(x),\rho^{R}(x))
for all
x\in\mathfrak{G}
is a biderivation
[
31
]
of the Leibniz algebra
(V,[-,-]_{T})
.
For relative averaging operators, equivariance will imply that the left action is by derivations, but defining the right operation to be the opposite of the left action or zero will not render this an action by derivations in the Leibniz sense in general.

Note that this proposition does not give rise to crossed modules, because the requirements
\rho(Tv)w=[v,w]
in the Lie case and
\rho^{L}(Tv)w=[v,w]=\rho^{R}(w)v
in the Leibniz case for
v,w\in V
are not fullfilled. In the case of relative averaging operators, this requirement is fullfilled, but the action is not by derivations in general.
On the other hand, it is well known that crossed modules of Lie algebras provide examples of relative averaging operators, see Example 2.14 in
[
41
]
.

for the adjoint representation

\rho^{R}(x)=[-,x]

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
(\mathfrak{G},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{G}})
be a Leibniz algebra. Then

We call
\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}}
the

associated to the Leibniz algebra

Observe that

The equivalence class of

\mathfrak{G}.
We denote by

(12)

Let

be a representation of a Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{G},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{G}})

\displaystyle\rho^{L}(\mathsf{Leib}(\mathfrak{G}))=0.

\displaystyle\theta(\bar{x})=\rho^{L}(x),\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{G},

such that following diagram of Leibniz algebra homomorphisms commute:

\textstyle{\mathfrak{G}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathrm{pr}}
\scriptstyle{\rho^{L}}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

It implies that

Proposition 2.6
.

Let

be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Leibniz algebra

Applying the projection

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{pr}\circ T(\theta(\mathrm{pr}\circ T(u))v-\theta(\mathrm{pr}\circ T(v))u),

is a relative Rota-Baxter operator on the canonical Lie algebra
\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}}
with respect to the representation

∎

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
T:V\longrightarrow\mathfrak{G}
(resp.

Theorem 2.8
.

from the category

Let

Remark 2.9
.

The functors
G:\mathsf{RBO}\mathsf{LeibnizS}\to\mathsf{RBO}\mathsf{Lie}
and
F:\mathsf{RBO}\mathsf{Lie}\to\mathsf{RBO}\mathsf{LeibnizS}
are induced by those establishing the equivalence of categories between modules over a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and symmetric Leibniz representations over
\mathfrak{g}
, viewed as a Leibniz algebra. From this, one may deduce that the pair
(F,G)
constitutes almost an adjoint equivalence. In fact,
F
is fully faithful, because the counit of the adjunction
\epsilon:G\circ F\to{\rm{Id}}
is a natural isomorphism for each object of
\mathsf{RBO}\mathsf{Lie}
. On the other hand, the unit of the adjunction
\eta:{\rm{Id}}\to F\circ G
is not an isomorphism. For an object
T:V\to\mathfrak{G}
in
\mathsf{RBO}\mathsf{LeibnizS}
,
(F\circ G)(T)
corresponds to viewing
\mathrm{pr}\circ T:V\to\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}}
again as an object in
\mathsf{RBO}\mathsf{LeibnizS}
. We have a natural relative Rota-Baxter operator homomorphism
(\mathrm{pr},{\rm{Id}}_{V})
from
T
to
\mathrm{pr}\circ T

\textstyle{V\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{{\rm{Id}}_{V}}
\scriptstyle{T}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{G}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathrm{pr}}
\textstyle{V\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathrm{pr}\circ T}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}},}

but it is not an isomorphism in general.

Note that one may add the data which arises from the passage from
\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}}
to
\mathfrak{G}
to the category
\mathsf{RBO}\mathsf{Lie}
in order to make it an equivalence of categories. The data consists of the isomorphism class of an antisymmetric representation of
\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}}
(corresponding to
\mathsf{Leib}(\mathfrak{G})
) and of the Loday-Pirashvili cohomology class of a
2
-cocycle characterizing the abelian extension

(16)

\displaystyle 0\to\mathsf{Leib}(\mathfrak{G})\to\mathfrak{G}\to\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}}\to 0.

Given a relative Rota-Baxter operator
T:V\to\mathfrak{g}
together with a
\mathfrak{g}
-module
A
(viewed as an antisymmetric representation of the Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{g}
) and a
2
-cocycle
\alpha\in Z^{2}(\mathfrak{g},A)
, we obtain a relative Rota-Baxter operator
T:V\to\mathfrak{G}
for the Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{G}
constructed from
(\mathfrak{g},A,\alpha)
with
A=\mathsf{Leib}(\mathfrak{G})
and
V
viewed as a symmetric representation of
\mathfrak{G}
, because
\mathsf{Leib}(\mathfrak{G})
acts trivially from the left and thus also trivially from the right as the representation
V
is symmetric. This construction depends on the choice of the cocycle and the module
A
, but becomes natural when passing to cohomology and isomorphism classes.

3.
Relations between the controlling algebras

In this section, we establish the relation between the controlling algebra of relative Rota-Baxter operators on a Leibniz algebra with respect to a symmetric (resp. antisymmetric) representation and the controlling algebra of the relative Rota-Baxter (resp. averaging) operators on the canonical Lie algebra.

A permutation
\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}
is called an
(i,n-i)
-shuffle
if
\sigma(1)<\cdots<\sigma(i)
and
\sigma(i+1)<\cdots<\sigma(n)

A degree

Let

on the graded vector space

where

\displaystyle P\bar{\circ}Q=\sum_{k=1}^{p+1}P\circ_{k}Q,

\displaystyle(P\circ_{k}Q)(x_{1},\cdots,x_{p+q+1})

3.1.

Let

is the same as the semidirect product Leibniz algebra

. We denote this semidirect product multiplication by
\mu.
Recall that the Nijenhuis-Richardson bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{NR}}
associated to the direct sum vector space
\mathfrak{g}\oplus V
gives rise to a graded Lie algebra
(\oplus_{k\geq 1}\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{k}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V),\mathfrak{g}\oplus V),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{NR}})
. The graded Lie algebra
({\mathcal{C}}^{*}(V,\mathfrak{g}),\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
is also obtained via the derived bracket
[
27
]
:

\left\llbracket P,Q\right\rrbracket=(-1)^{m-1}[[\mu,P]_{\mathsf{NR}},Q]_{\mathsf{NR}},\quad\forall P\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{m}V,\mathfrak{g}),Q\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}V,\mathfrak{g}).

Since
(\oplus_{k\geq 1}\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{k}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V),\mathfrak{g}\oplus V),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{NR}})
is a graded Lie subalgebra of
(\oplus_{n\geq 1}\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{n}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V),\mathfrak{g}\oplus V),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{B}})
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\left\llbracket P,Q\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{m-1}[[\mu,P]_{\mathsf{NR}},Q]_{\mathsf{NR}}=(-1)^{m-1}[[\mu,P]_{\mathsf{B}},Q]_{\mathsf{B}}=\{P,Q\}_{V},

which implies that
({\mathcal{C}}^{*}(V,\mathfrak{g}),\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
is a subalgebra of
(C^{*}(V,\mathfrak{g}),\{-,-\}_{V})=\mathsf{C}_{\mathfrak{g}}(V;\rho,-\rho)
.
∎

4.
Relations between the cohomologies

In this section, we establish the relations between the cohomology groups of a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Leibniz algebra with respect to a symmetric (resp. antisymmetric) representation and the cohomology groups of the induced relative Rota-Baxter (resp. averaging) operator on the canonical Lie algebra.

4.1.
Cohomology of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Leibniz algebras

Definition 4.1
.

(
[
33
]
)
Let
(V;\rho^{L},\rho^{R})
be a representation of a Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{G},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{G}})
.
The
Loday-Pirashvili cohomology
of
\mathfrak{G}
with coefficients in
V
is the cohomology of the cochain complex
(C^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{G},V)=\oplus_{k=0}^{+\infty}C^{k}(\mathfrak{G},V),\partial)
, where
C^{k}(\mathfrak{G},V)=\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{k}\mathfrak{G},V)
and the coboundary operator

\partial:C^{k}(\mathfrak{G},V)\longrightarrow C^{k+1}(\mathfrak{G},V)

is defined by

\displaystyle(\partial f)(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{i+1}\rho^{L}(x_{i})f(x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{k+1})+(-1)^{k+1}\rho^{R}(x_{k+1})f(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k})

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k+1}(-1)^{i}f(x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{j-1},[x_{i},x_{j}]_{\mathfrak{G}},x_{j+1},\cdots,x_{k+1}),

for all
x_{1},\cdots,x_{k+1}\in\mathfrak{G}
. The resulting cohomology is denoted by
\mathcal{HL}^{*}(\mathfrak{G},V)
.

Theorem 4.2
.

(
[
47
]
)

Let
T
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Leibniz
algebra
(\mathfrak{G},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{G}})
with respect to
(V;\rho^{L},\rho^{R})

[-,-]_{T}:\otimes^{2}V\,\rightarrow\,V
by

(46)

\displaystyle[u,v]_{T}=\rho^{L}(Tu)v+\rho^{R}(Tv)u,\quad u,v\in V.

Then
(V,[-,-]_{T})

\varrho^{L},\varrho^{R}:V\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{G})
by

(47)

\displaystyle\varrho^{L}(u)x=[Tu,x]_{\mathfrak{G}}-T\rho^{R}(x)u,\quad\varrho^{R}(u)x=[x,Tu]_{\mathfrak{G}}-T\rho^{L}(x)u,\quad\forall u\in V,x\in\mathfrak{G}.

Then
(\mathfrak{G};\varrho^{L},\varrho^{R})
is a representation of the Leibniz algebra
(V,[-,-]_{T})
.

Let
\partial_{T}:C^{n}(V,\mathfrak{G})\longrightarrow C^{n+1}(V,\mathfrak{G})
be the corresponding Loday-Pirashvili coboundary operator of the Leibniz algebra
(V,[-,-]_{T})
with coefficients in the representation
(\mathfrak{G};\varrho^{L},\varrho^{R})
. More precisely,
\partial_{T}:C^{n}(V,\mathfrak{G})\longrightarrow C^{n+1}(V,\mathfrak{G})

\displaystyle(\partial_{T}f)(v_{1},\cdots,v_{n+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i+1}[Tv_{i},f(v_{1},\cdots,\hat{v}_{i},\cdots,v_{n+1})]_{\mathfrak{G}}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i+1}T\rho^{R}(f(v_{1},\cdots,\hat{v}_{i},\cdots,v_{n+1}))v_{i}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{n+1}[f(v_{1},\cdots,v_{n}),Tv_{n+1}]_{\mathfrak{G}}+(-1)^{n}T\rho^{L}(f(v_{1},\cdots,v_{n}))v_{n+1}

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n+1}(-1)^{i}f(v_{1},\cdots,\hat{v}_{i},\cdots,v_{j-1},\rho^{L}(Tv_{i})v_{j}+\rho^{R}(Tv_{j})v_{i},v_{j+1},\cdots,v_{n+1}).

4.2.
The Loday-Pirashvili cohomology of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie algebras

In
[
44
]
the authors established a cohomology theory of a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie algebra as the Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology of a Lie algebra. Thus we will refer to it as the Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology of a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie algebra. More precisely, let
T
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie
algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho)
.
Define
\varrho:V\longrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
by

\varrho(u)(x):=[Tu,x]+T\rho(x)(u),\;\;\forall x\in\mathfrak{g},u\in V.

Then
(\mathfrak{g};\varrho)
is a representation of the Lie algebra
(V,[-,-]_{T})
on the vector space
\mathfrak{g}
. We denote by
(\mathfrak{C}^{*}(V,\mathfrak{g}),d_{\mathsf{CE}})
the Chevalley-Eilenberg cochain complex of the Lie algebra
(V,[-,-]_{T})
with coefficients in the representation
(\mathfrak{g};\varrho)
, where
\mathfrak{C}^{*}(V,\mathfrak{g})=\oplus_{k\geq 0}\mathfrak{C}^{k}(V,\mathfrak{g})
and
\mathfrak{C}^{k}(V,\mathfrak{g})=\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{k}V,\mathfrak{g})
. The cohomology of the cochain complex
(\mathfrak{C}^{*}(V,\mathfrak{g}),d_{\mathsf{CE}})
is called the
Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology of the relative Rota-Baxter operator

T
. Denote the set of
k
-cocycles by
\mathcal{Z}^{k}_{\mathsf{CE}}(T)
and the set of
k
-coboundaries by
\mathcal{B}^{k}_{\mathsf{CE}}(T)
. Denote by

(49)

\mathcal{H}^{k}_{\mathsf{CE}}(T)=\mathcal{Z}^{k}_{\mathsf{CE}}(T)/\mathcal{B}^{k}_{\mathsf{CE}}(T),\quad k\geq 0,

the corresponding
k
-th cohomology group.

As explained in Section
2
, a relative Rota-Baxter operator
T:V\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
on a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{g}})
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho)
can be viewed as a special relative Rota-Baxter operator on the Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{g}})
with respect to the symmetric representation
(V;\rho,-\rho)
. Thus, the above approach to define the cohomology of a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Leibniz algebra can be applied to define a cohomology of a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie algebra. More precisely, let
T:V\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{g}})
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho)
. Then
(V,[-,-]_{T})
is a Leibniz algebra, where the Leibniz bracket
[-,-]_{T}
is defined by

[u,v]_{T}=\rho(Tu)v-\rho(Tv)u,\quad\forall u,v\in V.

Moreover,
(\mathfrak{g};\varrho^{L},\varrho^{R})
is a symmetric representation of the Leibniz algebra
(V,[-,-]_{T})
, where
\varrho^{L},\varrho^{R}:V\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
is defined by

\varrho^{L}(u)x=[Tu,x]_{\mathfrak{g}}+T\rho(x)u,\quad\varrho^{R}(u)x=[x,Tu]_{\mathfrak{g}}-T\rho(x)u.

Let
\partial_{T}:C^{n}(V,\mathfrak{g})\longrightarrow C^{n+1}(V,\mathfrak{g})
be the corresponding Loday-Pirashvili coboundary operator of the Leibniz algebra
(V,[-,-]_{T})
with coefficients in the symmetric representation
(\mathfrak{g};\varrho^{L},\varrho^{R})
.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
T:V\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{g}})
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho)
. The cohomology of the cochain complex
(\oplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}C^{n}(V,\mathfrak{g}),\partial_{T})
is called the
Loday-Pirashvili cohomology of the relative Rota-Baxter operator

T
.

We denote the set of
k
-cocycles by
\mathcal{Z}^{k}_{\mathsf{LP}}({T})
, the set of
k
-coboundaries by
\mathcal{B}^{k}_{\mathsf{LP}}({T})
and the
k
-th cohomology group by

(50)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{k}_{\mathsf{LP}}({T})=\mathcal{Z}^{k}_{\mathsf{LP}}({T})/\mathcal{B}^{k}_{\mathsf{LP}}({T}).

Remark 4.6
.

Note that the monomorphism
\mathfrak{C}^{k}(V,\mathfrak{g})=\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{k}V,\mathfrak{g})\to C^{k}(V,\mathfrak{g})=\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{k}V,\mathfrak{g})
induced from the natural epimorphism
\otimes^{k}V\to\wedge^{k}V
induces isomorphisms

\mathcal{H}^{0}_{\mathsf{CE}}({T})\cong\mathcal{H}^{0}_{\mathsf{LP}}({T})\,\,\,{\rm and}\,\,\,\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mathsf{CE}}({T})\cong\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mathsf{LP}}({T})

and a long exact sequence

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{2}_{\mathsf{CE}}({T})\to\mathcal{H}^{2}_{\mathsf{LP}}({T})\to\mathcal{H}_{\rm rel}^{0}({T})

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{3}_{\mathsf{CE}}({T})\to\mathcal{H}^{3}_{\mathsf{LP}}({T})\to\mathcal{H}_{\rm rel}^{1}({T}\to\cdots

where
\mathcal{H}_{\rm rel}^{\bullet}({T})
is the cohomology of the relative complex

C_{\rm rel}^{\bullet}(V,\mathfrak{g})
which is by definition (up to a degree shift) the cokernel of the monomorphism

\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{k}V,\mathfrak{g})\to\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{k}V,\mathfrak{g})
. These matters as well as a spectral sequence linking
\mathcal{H}_{\rm rel}^{\bullet}({T})
and
\mathcal{H}_{\mathsf{LP}}^{\bullet}({T})
in still another way can be found in
[
18
]
.
In particular, Theorem 2.6 in
[
18
]
shows how the vanishing of Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology implies the vanishing of Loday-Pirashvili cohomology.

Remark 4.7
.

Note furthermore that the comparison between Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomologies and Loday-Pirashvili cohomologies makes sense more generally for any relative Rota-Baxter operator
T:V\to\mathfrak{G}
over a Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{G}
as soon as the representation of
\mathfrak{G}
on
V
is symmetric, because then the bracket
[-,-]_{T}
on
V
is a Lie bracket.

In case the induced representation of
(V,[-,-]_{T})
on
\mathfrak{G}
is not symmetric, one may use the short exact sequence

0\to\mathfrak{G}_{\rm anti}\to\mathfrak{G}\to\mathfrak{G}_{\rm sym}\to 0

and the relation of Loday-Pirashvili cohomology with values in an antisymmetric representation to the cohomology with values in a symmetric representation (see
[
18
]
) in order to reduce the computations to the case of symmetric coefficients.

Let
T:V\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{G}
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{G}
with respect to a symmetric representation
(V;\rho^{L},\rho^{R}=-\rho^{L})
. By Proposition
2.6
,
\bar{T}:=\mathrm{pr}\circ T:V\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}}
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}}
with respect to the representation
(V;\theta)
given by (
15
). Now we establish the relationship between the cohomology groups of the relative Rota-Baxter operator
T
and the Loday-Pirashvili cohomology groups of the relative Rota-Baxter operator
\bar{T}.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
T:V\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{G}
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{G}
with respect to a symmetric representation
(V;\rho^{L},\rho^{R}=-\rho^{L})
. Then
(\mathsf{Leib}(\mathfrak{G});\varrho^{L},\varrho^{R})
is a subrepresentation of
(\mathfrak{G};\varrho^{L},\varrho^{R})
.

Proof.

Since
\rho^{R}=-\rho^{L}
, by (
14
), we have

\displaystyle\varrho^{L}(u)[y,y]_{\mathfrak{G}}=[Tu,[y,y]_{\mathfrak{G}}]_{\mathfrak{G}}+\rho^{L}([y,y]_{\mathfrak{G}})u=[[Tu,y]_{\mathfrak{G}},y]_{\mathfrak{G}}+[y,[Tu,y]_{\mathfrak{G}}]_{\mathfrak{G}},

for all
y\in\mathfrak{G}
, which implies that
\varrho^{L}(u)(\mathsf{Leib}(\mathfrak{G}))\subset\mathsf{Leib}(\mathfrak{G}).

By the fact that
\mathsf{Leib}(\mathfrak{G})
is contained in the left center of the Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{G}
, we have

\displaystyle\varrho^{R}(u)[y,y]_{\mathfrak{G}}=[[y,y]_{\mathfrak{G}},Tu]_{\mathfrak{G}}-T\rho^{L}([y,y]_{\mathfrak{G}})u=0,

which implies that
\varrho^{R}(u)(\mathsf{Leib}(\mathfrak{G}))=0\subset\mathsf{Leib}(\mathfrak{G})
. Therefore,
(\mathsf{Leib}(\mathfrak{G});\varrho^{L},\varrho^{R})
is a subrepresentation of
(\mathfrak{G};\varrho^{L},\varrho^{R})
.
∎

Note that even though the Leibniz algebra
(V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{T})
becomes a Lie algebra if
T
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{G}
with respect to a symmetric representation, the representation given in Theorem
4.2
is still a representation of
(V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{T})
as a Leibniz algebra, and
\varrho^{R}\neq-\varrho^{L}.
But the quotient representation
(\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}};\bar{\varrho}^{L},\bar{\varrho}^{R})
is symmetric, where

\bar{\varrho}^{L}:V\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}})
and
\bar{\varrho}^{R}:V\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}})
are given by

(51)

\bar{\varrho}^{L}(u)\bar{x}=\overline{\varrho^{L}(u)x},\quad\bar{\varrho}^{R}(u)\bar{x}=\overline{\varrho^{R}(u)x},\quad\forall u\in V,x\in\mathfrak{G}.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
T:V\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{G}
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{G}
with respect to a symmetric representation
(V;\rho^{L},\rho^{R}=-\rho^{L})
. Then the quotient representation
(\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}};\bar{\varrho}^{L},\bar{\varrho}^{R})
is a symmetric representation of
(V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{T})
as a Leibniz algebra, i.e.
\bar{\varrho}^{R}=-\bar{\varrho}^{L}
.

Proof.

For all
u\in V,x\in\mathfrak{G}
, we have

\displaystyle\varrho^{L}(u)x+\varrho^{R}(u)x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[Tu,x]_{\mathfrak{G}}-T\rho^{R}(x)u+[x,Tu]_{\mathfrak{G}}-T\rho^{L}(x)u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[Tu,x]_{\mathfrak{G}}+[x,Tu]_{\mathfrak{G}}\in\mathsf{Leib}(\mathfrak{G}).

Thus, we deduce that
\bar{\varrho}^{R}=-\bar{\varrho}^{L}
.
∎

Let
\overline{\partial}_{{T}}:C^{n}(V,\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}})\longrightarrow C^{n+1}(V,\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}})
be the corresponding Loday-Pirashvili coboundary operator of the Leibniz algebra
(V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{T})
with coefficients in the quotient representation
(\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}};\bar{\varrho}^{L},\bar{\varrho}^{R})
.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
T
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{G},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{G}})
with respect to a symmetric representation
(V;\rho^{L},\rho^{R}=-\rho^{L})
. Then

\overline{\partial}_{{T}}=\partial_{\bar{T}}.

That is, the Loday-Pirashvili cochain complex
(\oplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}C^{n}(V,\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}}),\partial_{\bar{T}})
associated to the relative Rota-Baxter operator
\bar{T}
and the cochain complex
(\oplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}C^{n}(V,\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}}),\overline{\partial}_{T})
obtained by using the quotient representation
(\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}};\bar{\varrho}^{L},\bar{\varrho}^{R})
are the same.

Proof.

Obviously, the spaces of
n
-cochains are the same. Thus we only need to show that the induced Leibniz algebra structure
[-,-]_{\bar{T}}
on
V
by the relative Rota-Baxter operator
\bar{T}
is the same as
[-,-]_{{T}}
, and the representations
(\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}};\varrho^{L},\varrho^{R})
and
(\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}};\bar{\varrho}^{L},\bar{\varrho}^{R})
are the same.

For all
u,v\in V
, by (
15
), we have

[u,v]_{\bar{T}}=\theta(\bar{T}u)v-\theta(\bar{T}v)u=\rho^{L}(Tu)v-\rho^{L}(Tv)u=[u,v]_{T}.

For any
u\in V,x\in\mathfrak{G}

4.3.
Cohomology of relative averaging operators on Lie algebras

As explained in Section
2
, a relative averaging operator
T:V\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
on a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{g}})
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho)
can be viewed as a special relative Rota-Baxter operator on the Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{g}})
with respect to the antisymmetric representation
(V;\rho,0)
. Thus, the above approach to define the cohomology of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Leibniz algebras can be applied to define the cohomology of relative averaging operators. More precisely, let
T:V\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
be a relative averaging operator on a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{g}})
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho)
. Then
(V,[-,-]_{T})
is a Leibniz algebra, where the Leibniz bracket
[-,-]_{T}
is defined by

[u,v]_{T}=\rho(Tu)v,\quad\forall u,v\in V.

Moreover,
(\mathfrak{g};\varrho^{L},\varrho^{R})
is a representation of the Leibniz algebra
(V,[-,-]_{T})
, where
\varrho^{L},\varrho^{R}:V\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
is defined by

\varrho^{L}(u)x=[Tu,x]_{\mathfrak{g}},\quad\varrho^{R}(u)x=[x,Tu]_{\mathfrak{g}}-T\rho(x)u.

Let
\partial_{T}:C^{n}(V,\mathfrak{g})\longrightarrow C^{n+1}(V,\mathfrak{g})
be the corresponding Loday-Pirashvili coboundary operator of the Leibniz algebra
(V,[-,-]_{T})
with coefficients in the representation
(\mathfrak{g};\varrho^{L},\varrho^{R})
. More precisely,
\partial_{T}:C^{n}(V,\mathfrak{g})\longrightarrow C^{n+1}(V,\mathfrak{g})
is given by

\displaystyle(\partial_{T}f)(v_{1},\cdots,v_{n+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i+1}[Tv_{i},f(v_{1},\cdots,\hat{v}_{i},\cdots,v_{n+1})]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{n+1}[f(v_{1},\cdots,v_{n}),Tv_{n+1}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+(-1)^{n}T\rho(f(v_{1},\cdots,v_{n}))v_{n+1}

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n+1}(-1)^{i}f(v_{1},\cdots,\hat{v}_{i},\cdots,v_{j-1},\rho(Tv_{i})v_{j},v_{j+1},\cdots,v_{n+1}).

See

Example 4.17
.

Let
\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C})
be the Lie algebra of all
n\times n

\mathbb{C}
. Then
T={\rm{Id}}:\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C})\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C})
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C})
with respect to the representation
(\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C});\rho)

\rho:\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C})\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C}))

\rho(A)B=AB,\quad\forall A,B\in\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C}).

Then

is the original Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C})

(\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C});\varrho)
is a representation of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C})
, where
\varrho:\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C})\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C}))
is defined by

(57)

\varrho(A)B:=[TA,B]+T\rho(B)(A)=AB=\rho(A)B,\;\;\forall A,B\in\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C}).

Thus, the cohomology of the relative Rota-Baxter operator

is the Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology of
\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C})
with coefficients in the representation
(\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C});\rho)

is the direct sum

, where the factor

is the center and consists of diagonal matrices. The Hochschild-Serre formula
[
22
]
reads then

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\mathsf{CE}}^{n}(\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C}),\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C}))=\bigoplus_{p+q=n}\mathcal{H}_{\mathsf{CE}}^{p}(\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C}))\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\mathsf{CE}}^{q}({\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C}))^{\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C})}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}_{\mathsf{CE}}^{n}(\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C}))\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\mathsf{CE}}^{0}({\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C}))^{\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C})}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{\mathsf{CE}}^{n-1}(\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C}))\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\mathsf{CE}}^{1}({\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C}))^{\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C})}.

The invariant space
\mathcal{H}_{\mathsf{CE}}^{0}({\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C}))^{\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C})}
is zero, because
\mathcal{H}_{\mathsf{CE}}^{0}({\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C}))=0
. Furthermore, we have
\mathcal{H}_{\mathsf{CE}}^{1}({\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C}))=0
, because all elements are coboundaries. Therefore all the cohomology groups are zero.

Let
(\mathfrak{G},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{G}})
be a Leibniz algebra. Then the natural projection

T=\mathrm{pr}:\mathfrak{G}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}}

gives a relative averaging operator on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}}
with respect to the representation
(\mathfrak{G};\rho)
, where
\rho:\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{G})
is defined by

\rho(\bar{x})y=[x,y]_{\mathfrak{G}},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{G}.

Then
(\mathfrak{G},[-,-]_{T})
is the original Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{G},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{G}})
. Moreover,
(\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}};\varrho^{L},\varrho^{R})
is a representation of the Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{G},[-,-]_{T})
, where
\varrho^{L},\varrho^{R}:\mathfrak{G}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}})
is defined by

\varrho^{L}(x)\bar{y}=[\bar{x},\bar{y}]_{\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}}},\quad\varrho^{R}(x)\bar{y}=[\bar{y},\bar{x}]_{\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}}}-\overline{[y,x]_{\mathfrak{G}}}=0.

Thus, the cohomology of the relative averaging operator
T
is the Loday-Pirashvili cohomology of
(\mathfrak{G},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{G}})
with coefficients in the antisymmetric representation
(\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}};\varrho^{L},\varrho^{R})
.

Let now
(\mathfrak{G},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{G}})
be a finite-dimensional semisimple Leibniz algebra over a field of characteristic zero. By Theorem 4.3 of
[
18
]
, we obtain that
\mathcal{HL}^{p}(\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}})=\{0\}
for
p\geq 2
and an exact sequence

0\to(\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}})_{\mathrm{anti}}\to\mathcal{HL}^{0}(\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}})\to\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}}^{\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}}}\to\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathfrak{G}}(\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}})\to\mathcal{HL}^{1}(\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}})\to 0.

As
\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}}
is already antisymmetric and
\mathcal{HL}^{0}(\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}})=\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}}
, the first (non-trivial) map in this sequence is an isomorphism. Furthermore
\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}}^{\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}}}=\{0\}
, because
\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{Lie}}
is semisimple. Therefore the last (non-trivial) map in this sequence is also an isomorphism (as in Lemma 1.4 of
[
18
]
).

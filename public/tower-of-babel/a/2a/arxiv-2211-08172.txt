A study on Horn matrix functions and its confluent cases
Ravi Dwivedi

Abstract
In this paper, we give the matrix version of Horn’s hypergeometric function and its confluent cases. We also discuss the regions of convergence, the system of matrix differential equations of bilateral type, differential formulae and infinite summation formulae satisfied by these hypergeometric matrix functions. We also give the certain integral representation of these hypergeometric matrix functions. The study of these 23 matrix functions leads to completing the matrix generalization of Horn’s list of 34 hypergeometric series. Keywords : Horn’s hypergeometric functions, Matrix functional calculus. AMS Subject Classification : 15A15, 33C65.

Let

\|A\|

\|A\|=\max_{x\neq 0}\frac{\|{Ax}\|_{2}}{\|x\|_{2}}=\max\{\,\sqrt{\lambda}\mid\lambda\in\sigma(A^{*}A)\,\},

(2.1)

where for any vector
x
in the
r
-dimensional complex space,
\|{x}\|_{2}=(x^{*}x)^{\frac{1}{2}}
is the Euclidean norm of
x
and
A^{*}
denotes the transposed conjugate of
A
. If

and

are holomorphic functions of the complex variable
z
, which are defined in an open set
\Omega

A
is a matrix in
\mathbb{C}^{r\times r}
with
\sigma(A)\subset\Omega
, then from the properties of the matrix functional calculus
[
3
]
, it follows that

f(A)g(A)=g(A)f(A).

(2.2)

Furthermore, if
B\in\mathbb{C}^{r\times r}
is a matrix for which
\sigma(B)\subset\Omega
, and if
AB=BA
, then

f(A)g(B)=g(B)f(A).

(2.3)

The reciprocal gamma function
\Gamma^{-1}(z)=1/\Gamma(z)
is an entire function of the complex variable
z
. The image of
\Gamma^{-1}(z)
acting on
A
, denoted by
\Gamma^{-1}(A)
, is a well defined matrix. If

is invertible for all integers

, then the reciprocal gamma function is defined as

\Gamma^{-1}(A)=A(A+I)\dots(A+(n-1)I)\Gamma^{-1}(A+nI),\ n\geq 1.

(2.4)

The Pochhammer symbol

,

, is defined as

(z)_{n}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if $n=0$}\\
z(z+1)\dots(z+n-1),&\text{if $n\geq 1$}.\end{cases}

(2.5)

By application of the matrix functional calculus, the Pochhammer symbol for

is given by

(A)_{n}=\begin{cases}I,&\text{if $n=0$}\\
A(A+I)\dots(A+(n-1)I),&\text{if $n\geq 1$}.\end{cases}

(2.6)

This gives

(A)_{n}=\Gamma^{-1}(A)\ \Gamma(A+nI),\qquad n\geq 1.

(2.7)

If

is such that

for all eigenvalues

of

can be expressed as

\Gamma(A)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}\,t^{A-I}\,dt.

3.1
Regions of convergence

We now determine the convergence of these matrix functions. To obtain so, we extend the well known technique develop by Horn given in
[
17
]
. Consider the hypergeometric matrix series

\displaystyle F(x,y)=\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{C}_{m,n}\,x^{m}y^{n},

(3.40)

which gives

\displaystyle\|F(x,y)\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}\|\mathcal{C}_{m,n}\|\,|x|^{m}\,|y|^{n},

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{A}_{m,n}\,|x|^{m}\,|y|^{n}.

(3.41)

Define

\displaystyle\rho(m,n)=\left|\lim_{u\to\infty}f(mu,nu)\right|^{-1},\quad m>0,n\geq 0,

(3.42)

\displaystyle\sigma(m,n)=\left|\lim_{u\to\infty}g(mu,nu)\right|^{-1},\quad m\geq 0,n>0,

(3.43)

where
f(m,n)=\frac{\mathcal{A}_{m+1,n}}{\mathcal{A}_{m,n}}
and
g(m,n)=\frac{\mathcal{A}_{m,n+1}}{\mathcal{A}_{m,n}}
. Now one can proceed in the same way as by Horn to find the region of convergence for Horn matrix functions. We will start by finding the region of convergence of Horn matrix function
G_{1}
defined in (
3.1

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
A
,
B
and
B^{\prime}
be matrices in
\mathbb{C}^{r\times r}
. Then the matrix function
G_{1}
defined in (
3.1
) converges absolutely for

,
|x|\leq r
,
|y|\leq s
.

Proof.

Consider the matrix series

\displaystyle G_{1}(A,B,B^{\prime};x,y)=\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}(A)_{m+n}(B)_{n-m}(B^{\prime})_{m-n}\frac{x^{m}\,y^{n}}{m!\,n!}.

(3.44)

This implies

\displaystyle\|G_{1}(A,B,B^{\prime};x,y)\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}(\|A\|)_{m+n}(\|B\|)_{n-m}(\|B^{\prime}\|)_{m-n}\frac{|x|^{m}\,|y|^{n}}{m!\,n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{A}_{m,n}|x|^{m}\,|y|^{n}.

(3.45)

So, we have

\displaystyle f_{m,n}=\frac{\mathcal{A}_{m+1,n}}{\mathcal{A}_{m,n}}=\frac{(\|A\|+m+n)\,(\|B^{\prime}\|+m-n)}{(\|B\|+n-m-1)\,(m+1)},

(3.46)

\displaystyle g_{m,n}=\frac{\mathcal{A}_{m,n+1}}{\mathcal{A}_{m,n}}=\frac{(\|A\|+m+n)\,(\|B\|+m-n)}{(\|B^{\prime}\|+m-n-1)\,(n+1)}

(3.47)

and

\displaystyle\rho(m,n)=\left|\lim_{u\to\infty}f(mu,nu)\right|^{-1}=\frac{n}{m+n},

(3.48)

\displaystyle\sigma(m,n)=\left|\lim_{u\to\infty}g(mu,nu)\right|^{-1}=\frac{m}{m+n}.

(3.49)

Therefore the region of convergence is given by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}=\{(r,s)\mid 0<r<\rho_{1,0}\cap 0<s<\sigma_{0,1}\}=K[1,1],

(3.50)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{Z}=\left\{(r,s)\mid\forall(m,n)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}:0<r<\frac{n}{m+n}\cup 0<s<\frac{m}{m+n}\right\}.

(3.51)

Eliminating
m
and

3.51
) gives required region of absolute convergence.

\displaystyle(A)_{m}\,(C)_{m}^{-1}=\Gamma(C)\,\Gamma^{-1}(A)\,\Gamma^{-1}(C-A)\int_{0}^{1}t^{A+(m-1)I}(1-t)^{C-A-I}dt,\quad AC=CA

(3.61)

3.60

\displaystyle G_{1}(A,B,B^{\prime};x,y)

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
A
,
B
and
B^{\prime}
be matrices in
\mathbb{C}^{r\times r}
. Then the Horn matrix function

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{r}}{\partial x^{r}}G_{1}(A,B,B^{\prime};x,y)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{r}\,(A)_{r}\,G_{1}(A+rI,B-rI,B^{\prime}+rI;x,y)\,(I-B)_{r}^{-1}\,(B^{\prime})_{r},\quad BB^{\prime}=B^{\prime}B;

(4.1)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{r}}{\partial y^{r}}G_{1}(A,B,B^{\prime};x,y)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{r}\,(A)_{r}\,G_{1}(A+rI,B+rI,B^{\prime}-rI;x,y)\,(B)_{r}\,(I-B^{\prime})_{r}^{-1},\quad BB^{\prime}=B^{\prime}B;

(4.2)

\displaystyle\left(x^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\right)^{r}\ [x^{A+(r-1)I}G_{1}(A,B,B^{\prime};x,xy)]=x^{A+rI}\,(A)_{r}\,G_{1}(A+rI,B,B^{\prime};x,xy);

(4.3)

\displaystyle\left(y^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right)^{r}\ [y^{A+(r-1)I}G_{1}(A,B,B^{\prime};xy,y)]=y^{A+rI}\,(A)_{r}\,G_{1}(A+rI,B,B^{\prime};xy,y);

(4.4)

\displaystyle\left(x^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\right)^{r}\ [G_{1}(A,B,B^{\prime};x,\frac{y}{x})\,x^{B^{\prime}+(r-1)I}]=G_{1}(A,B,B^{\prime}+rI;x,\frac{y}{x})\ x^{B^{\prime}+rI}\,(B^{\prime})_{r};

(4.5)

\displaystyle\left(y^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right)^{r}\ [G_{1}(A,B,B^{\prime};\frac{x}{y},y)\,y^{B+(r-1)I}]=G_{1}(A,B+rI,B^{\prime};\frac{x}{y},y)\,y^{B+rI}\,(B)_{r}.

(4.6)

Proof.

From equation (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x}G_{1}(A,B,B^{\prime};x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}(A)_{m+n}(B)_{n-m}(B^{\prime})_{m-n}\ \frac{\partial}{\partial x}\frac{x^{m}\,y^{n}}{m!\,n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1,n=0}^{\infty}(A)_{m+n}(B)_{n-m}(B^{\prime})_{m-n}\ \frac{x^{m-1}\,y^{n}}{(m-1)!\,n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}(A)_{m+n+1}(B)_{n-m-1}(B^{\prime})_{m-n+1}\ \frac{x^{m}\,y^{n}}{m!\,n!}

\displaystyle=(-1)(A)_{1}\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}(A+I)_{m+n}(B-I)_{n-m}(B^{\prime}+I)_{m-n}\ \frac{x^{m}\,y^{n}}{m!\,n!}

\displaystyle\quad\times(I-B)_{1}^{-1}(B^{\prime})_{1}

\displaystyle=(-1)(A)_{1}\,G_{1}(A+I,B-I,B^{\prime}+I;x,y)(I-B)_{1}^{-1}(B^{\prime})_{1}.

Iterating this process
r
-times, we get required formula (
4.1

4.2

This section deals with the study of infinite summation formulae satisfied by Horn matrix functions and their confluent forms. In the theorem given below, we give the infinite summation formulae satisfied by first Horn matrix function
{G}_{1}(A,B,B^{\prime};x,y)
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
A
,
B
and
B^{\prime}
be matrices in
\mathbb{C}^{r\times r}
. Then the Horn matrix function
{G}_{1}(A,B,B^{\prime};x,y)
satisfies the following infinite summation formulae

\displaystyle(1-t)^{-A}G_{1}\left(A,B,B^{\prime};\frac{x}{1-t},\frac{y}{1-t}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(A)_{n}}{n!}G_{1}(A+nI,B,B^{\prime};{x},y)t^{n},\quad|t|<1;

(5.1)

\displaystyle G_{1}\left(A,B,B^{\prime};{x}{(1-t)},\frac{y}{1-t}\right)\,(1-t)^{-B}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}G_{1}(A,B+nI,B^{\prime};{x},y)\,\frac{(B)_{n}}{n!}\,t^{n},\quad BB^{\prime}=B^{\prime}B,|t|<1;

(5.2)

\displaystyle G_{1}\left(A,B,B^{\prime};\frac{x}{1-t},{y}{(1-t)}\right)(1-t)^{-B^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}G_{1}(A,B,B^{\prime}+nI;{x},y)\,\frac{(B^{\prime})_{n}}{n!}\,t^{n},\quad|t|<1.

(5.3)

Proof.

From the definition of Horn’s matrix function
{G}_{1}(A,B,B^{\prime};x,y)
, we have

\displaystyle(1-t)^{-A}G_{1}\left(A,B,B^{\prime};\frac{x}{1-t},\frac{y}{1-t}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l,m=0}^{\infty}(1-t)^{-(A+(l+m)I)}(A)_{l+m}(B)_{m-l}(B^{\prime})_{l-m}\frac{x^{l}\,y^{m}}{l!\,m!}.

(5.4)

Using the matrix identities, for
|t|<1
,
(1-t)^{-(A+(l+m)I)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(A+(l+m)I)_{n}}{n!}t^{n}
and
(A+(l+m)I)_{n}(A)_{l+m}=(A)_{n}(A+nI)_{l+m}
, we get

\displaystyle(1-t)^{-A}G_{1}\left(A,B,B^{\prime};\frac{x}{1-t},\frac{y}{1-t}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l,m,n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(A)_{n}}{n!}(A+nI)_{l+m}(B)_{m-l}(B^{\prime})_{l-m}\frac{x^{l}\,y^{m}}{l!\,m!}\,t^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(A)_{n}}{n!}G_{1}(A+nI,B,B^{\prime};{x},y)t^{n},\quad|t|<1.

(5.5)

It completes the proof of (
5.1
). Similarly, the other infinite summation can be proved.
∎

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we studied the Horn functions and its confluent cases with the matrices as parameters. We discuss the regions of convergence and give the system of partial differential equations of bilateral type satisfy by these matrix functions. We also determine certain integral representation of these matrix functions. In last, we give the differential formulae and infinite summation formulae induced from these matrix functions. These matrix functions will enrich the literature in theory of special functions and are capable to find the new applications in mathematics as well as in physics.

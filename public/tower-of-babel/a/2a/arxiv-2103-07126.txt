On the relations between the zeros of a polynomial and its Mahler measure
M. Ounaies, G. Rhin, J.-M. Sac-Épée

Abstract
In this work, we are dealing with some properties relating the zeros of a polynomial and its Mahler measure. We provide estimates on the number of real zeros of a polynomial, lower bounds on the distance between the zeros of a polynomial and non-zeros located on the unit circle and a lower bound on the number of zeros of a polynomial in the disk { | x − 1 | < 1 } \{|x-1|<1\} .

Introduction

For a polynomial
P\in\mathbb{C}[x]
of the form

P(x)=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{d}a_{j}x^{j}=\displaystyle a_{d}\prod_{k=1}^{d}(x-\mu_{k}),a_{d}\not=0,

its Mahler measure
[
13
]
is given by

M(P)=\exp\Big(\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log|P(e^{it})|dt\Big).

Using the Jensen’s formula
[
10
]
, it can be expressed as

M(P)=|a_{d}|\displaystyle\prod_{|\mu_{k}|\geq 1}|\mu_{k}|.

For an algebraic number
\alpha
, its Mahler measure
M(\alpha)
is that of its minimal polynomial in
\mathbb{Z}[x]
. We refer the reader to
[
23
]
for a survey of results on Mahler measure of algebraic numbers.

When
P\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
, clearly
M(P)\geq 1
and by Kronecker’s theorem
[
11
]
, the only irreducible monic integer polynomials
P
such that
M(P)=1
, apart from the monomial
x
, are cyclotomic polynomials. In other words, the only algebraic numbers
\alpha
such that
M(\alpha)=1
are
0
and the roots of unity.

In a paper from 1933
[
12
]
, Lehmer exhibited the polynomial
P_{L}(x)=x^{10}+x^{9}-x^{7}-x^{6}-x^{5}-x^{4}-x^{3}+x+1
whose Mahler measure
M(P_{L})=1.176280\dots
and raised the question whether there exists
P\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
such that
1<M(P)<M(P_{L})
.

This question is still open and the existence of a constant
\epsilon>0
such that all
P\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
satisfy either
M(P)=1
or
M(P)>1+\epsilon
is now known as Lehmer’s conjecture.

For irreducible noncyclotomic polynomials
P\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
of degree
d
, E. Dobrowolski
[
4
]
showed a lower bound of the form

M(P)>1+C\left(\frac{\log\log d}{\log d}\right)^{3},

and it is the best unconditional lower bound for
M(P)
known thus far, up to the constant
C
.

A polynomial
P
of degree
d
is said to be self-reciprocal if
\displaystyle P(x)=x^{d}P\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)
. C. Smyth
[
22
]
proved an important theorem in the positive direction of Lehmer’s conjecture:

Theorem 1.1
.

[Smyth]
If
P\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
is not self-reciprocal and satisfies
P(0)P(1)\not=0
, then
M(P)\geq\theta_{0}
, where
\theta_{0}=1.324717\dots
is the real root of the polynomial
x^{3}-x-1
.

Let us also cite a theorem due to A. Schinzel
[
20
]
:

Theorem 1.2
.

[Schinzel]
If
P
is a monic integer polynomial of degree
d
whose all roots are real and such that
P(0)P(1)P(-1)\not=0
, then

M(P)\geq\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}.

We will denote by
E_{\theta}
the set of monic irreducible polynomials
P\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
such that
1<M(P)<\theta\leq\theta_{0}
, where
\theta_{0}
is as in in Theorem
1.1
. Many authors have focused on the computational search for such polynomials. D. Boyd
[
2
,
3
]
gave a complete list of polynomials in
E_{1.3}
up to degree 20. This list was extended by Mossinghoff
[
15
]
up to degree 24, then by Flammang, Rhin and Sac-Épée
[
8
]
with exhaustive results up to degree 40 and by Mossinghoff, Rhin and Wu
[
18
]
who provided an exhaustive list up to degree 44.

M. Mossinghoff
[
17
]
maintains a website that collects all known polynomials in
E_{1.3}
of degree less than 180. New polynomials have been added to the list available on this website, from the works of Rhin and Sac-Épée
[
19
]
, Mossinghoff, Pinner and Vaaler
[
16
]
, and El Otmani, Maul, Rhin and Sac-Épée
[
6
]
.

In the present work, we are dealing with estimates about the zeros of a polynomial with complex coefficients in relation with its Mahler measure.

Because of their importance for Lehmer’s conjecture, we are interested in applying some of our results from Sections 4, 5 and 6 to obtain information on the zero sets of polynomials in
E_{\theta}
.

The paper is organised as follows:
Section 2 provides notations, preliminary results and first properties of
E_{\theta}
.

Section 3 deals with lower bounds on
|\mu-\omega|
where
P(\mu)=0
,
|\omega|=1
,
P(\omega)\not=0
. Subsection 3.1 focusses on integer polynomials while Subsection 3.2 deals with real or complex polynomials, mainly self-reciprocal. The lower bounds involve either
M(P)
or
L(P)
.

A. Dubickas
[
5
]
showed that for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
D_{0}(\varepsilon)
such that for all monic, irreducible, non-cyclotomic polynomials
P\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
of degree
d\geq D_{0}(\varepsilon)
and for all
\mu\in P^{-1}(0)
,

|\mu-1|>\exp\left(-\left(\frac{\pi}{4}+\varepsilon\right)\sqrt{d\log d\log M(P)}\right).

(1.1)

Our Proposition
3.2
improves on (
1.1
) by substituting the constant
\frac{\pi}{8}
to
\frac{\pi}{4}
in the special case where
P
is self-reciprocal.

For polynomials
P\in\mathbb{C}[x]
of degree
d
, Theorem
3.3
provides the following inequality for all
\mu\in P^{-1}(0)
:

|\mu-1|\geq\frac{|P(1)|}{edL(P)}.

In particular, if all the coefficients of
P
are positive, then

|\mu-1|\geq\frac{1}{ed}.

In Section 4, we generalise Theorem
1.2
to polynomials in
\mathbb{C}[x]
and we strengthen the dependence on
P(0)
,
P(-1)
and
P(1)
. Our Theorem
4.1
states that for monic polynomials
P\in\mathbb{C}[x]
of degree
d
having
m\geq 1
real zeros and such that
P(0)P(1)P(-1)\not=0
, the following holds:

M(P)\geq\left(\frac{|P(1)P(-1)|^{\frac{1}{m}}+\left(4^{\frac{d}{m}}|P(0)|^{\frac{2}{m}}+|P(1)P(-1)|^{\frac{2}{m}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{2^{\frac{d}{m}}}\right)^{\frac{m}{2}}.

In Section 5, we give upper bounds for the number of real zeros of
\displaystyle P(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}a_{j}x^{j}\in\mathbb{C}[x]
by terms involving (besides its degree) its Mahler measure or its length
\displaystyle L(P)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}|a_{j}|
.

A. Dubickas
[
5
]
showed that for polynomials
P\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
of degree
d
satisfying
P(0)P(-1)P(1)\not=0
, there is a constant
\alpha>0
such that

|P^{-1}(0)\cap\mathbb{R}|\leq\alpha\sqrt{d\log d\log M(P)}.

(1.2)

Corollary
5.4
allows us to improve (
1.2
) by eliminating the term
\sqrt{\log d}
in the special case where
\log L(P)\leq\sqrt{d\log M(P)}
.

In Section 6, we focus on integer polynomials. The main result is the following lower bound for the Mahler measure of a monic irreducible polynomial
P\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
of degree
d\geq D_{0}(\varepsilon)
and satisfying
\log M(P)=o(\frac{d}{\log d})
:

\log M(P)\geq\left(C-\varepsilon\right)\frac{d}{K(P)^{2}\log d}\ \text{ with }C=\frac{4}{\pi^{2}}\log^{2}\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}\right)

and where we have denoted by
K(P)=\min\left(\left|P^{-1}(0)\cap\{|x-1|<1\}\right|,\left|P^{-1}(0)\cap\{|x+1|<1\}\right|\right)
.

2
Notations and preliminary results

Consider a polynomial
P(x)=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{d}a_{j}x^{j}\in\mathbb{C}[x]
of degree
d
. In our estimates in relation with
P
, besides
M(P)
, we will use the following quantities:

\begin{split}&H(P)=\max_{0\leq j\leq d}|a_{j}|,\ \ \ L(P)=\sum_{j=0}^{d}|a_{j}|,\\
&\|P\|=\sup_{|x|=1}|P(x)|,\ \ \ L_{2}(P)=\left(\sum_{j=0}^{d}|a_{j}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

The height
H(P)
and the length
L(P)
were introduced in
[
13
]
by K. Mahler who compared them to
M(P)
, showing the inequalities

\begin{split}&|a_{j}|\leq M(P){d\choose j}\ \ (j=0,\ldots,d),\\
&M(P)\leq L_{2}(P)\leq L(P)\leq 2^{d}M(P),\\
&H(P)\leq 2^{d-1}M(P),\ \ \ M(P)\leq(d+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}H(P).\\
\end{split}

(2.1)

Using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we also have

|P(1)|\leq\|P\|\leq L(P)\leq(d+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}L_{2}(P)

(2.2)

and by Parseval formula,

L_{2}(P)=\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|P(e^{i\theta})|^{2}d\theta\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\|P\|.

(2.3)

Define the reciprocal of
P
by

P^{*}(x)=x^{d}P\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)=\sum_{j=0}^{d}a_{d-j}x^{j}.

Clearly,
P
is self-reciprocal iff
P=P^{*}
.

Let
P(x)=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{d}a_{j}x^{j}=\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{d}(x-\mu_{k})\in\mathbb{C}[x]
,
a_{d}=1
,
a_{0}\not=0
.
We will denote by

Q(x)=a_{0}^{-1}P(x)P^{*}(x)=\prod_{k=1}^{d}(x-\mu_{k})\prod_{k=1}^{d}(x-\mu_{k}^{-1})=\sum_{j=0}^{2d}A_{j}x^{j}.

(2.4)

Then
Q
is monic and self-reciprocal and

M(Q)=|a_{0}|^{-1}M(P)^{2},\ Q(1)=a_{0}^{-1}P(1)^{2},\ Q(-1)=(-1)^{d}a_{0}^{-1}P(-1)^{2}.

(2.5)

Besides, the coefficients are given by

A_{j}=a_{0}^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{j}a_{k}a_{d-j+k}\ \ (j=0,\ldots,d)

and they satisfy

\sum_{j=0}^{d}|A_{j}|\leq|a_{0}^{-1}|\sum_{j=0}^{d}\sum_{k=0}^{j}|a_{k}||a_{d-j+k}|=|a_{0}^{-1}|\sum_{k=0}^{d}\left(|a_{k}|\sum_{j=k}^{d}|a_{j}|\right)\leq|a_{0}^{-1}|L(P)^{2}.

(2.6)

For a set
A\subset\mathbb{C}
, we will denote by
|P^{-1}(0)\cap A|
the number of zeros of
P
belonging to
A
counted with multiplicities.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
r>1
and
P
be a monic polynomial in
\mathbb{C}[x]
. Then

|P^{-1}(0)\cap\{|x|>r\}|<\frac{\log{M(P)}}{\log r}.

Proof.

Put
N=|P^{-1}(0)\cap\{|x|>r\}|
. Then

r^{N}<\prod_{\mu\in P^{-1}(0)\cap\{|x|>r\}}|\mu|\leq\prod_{\mu\in P^{-1}(0)\cap\{|x|\geq 1\}}|\mu|=M(P).

∎

For
1<\theta<\theta_{0}
, where
\theta_{0}
is given by Theorem
1.1
, let

E_{\theta}
be the set of integer monic irreducible polynomials
P
such that
1<M(P)\leq\theta
and satisfying conditions c1 and c2:

c1.

P
is primitive, i.e., it cannot be written as
Q(x^{m})
with
m\geq 2
,
Q\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
,

c2.

a_{j_{0}}>0
, where
j_{0}=\min\{j\geq 1\text{ such that }a_{j}\not=0\}
. This condition is satisfied either by
P(x)
or by
P(-x)
.

Conditions c1 and c2 are usually added by the authors looking for polynomials with small Mahler measure via computational methods in order to avoid redundancy, since

M(P(x))=M(P(-x))\text{ and }M(P(x^{m}))=M(P(x)).

Remark 2.2
.

•

For any
P\in E_{\theta}
and
\mu\in P^{-1}(0)
,
P
is the minimal polynomial of
\mu
in
\mathbb{Z}[x]
.

•

By Theorem
1.1
, all polynomials of
E_{\theta}
are self-reciprocal.

•

Any self-reciprocal polynomial of odd degree is divisible by
(x+1)
. Thus all polynomials in
E_{\theta}
are of even degree.

•

By Kronecker’s theorem, the cyclotomic polynomials are excluded of
E_{\theta}
because their Mahler measure is equal to
1
.

The first properties satisfied by the zero set
\displaystyle Z_{\theta}=\bigcup_{P\in E_{\theta}}P^{-1}(0)
are listed in the proposition below.

Proposition 2.3
.

1.

All
P^{-1}(0)
,
P\in E_{\theta}
are disjoint and they contain only simple zeros.

2.

For all
P\in E_{\theta}
,
P^{-1}(0)
is symmetric with regard to
\{|x|=1\}
and
\{\Im x=0\}
.

3.

Z_{\theta}\subset\{\frac{1}{\theta}\leq|x|\leq\theta\}
.

4.

Z_{\theta}\cap\{\Im x\not=0\}\subset\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{\theta}}\leq|x|\leq\sqrt{\theta}\}
.

5.

For all
P\in E_{\theta}
of degree
2n
, and for all
r>1
,

|P^{-1}(0)\cap\{\frac{1}{r}\leq|x|\leq r\}|>2(n-\frac{\log\theta}{\log r}).

6.

Z_{\theta}
does not contain any root of unity.

7.

Z_{\theta}
does not intersect the imaginary axe.

Proof.

1.

This follows immediately after the uniqueness of the minimal polynomial of an algebraic integer.

2.

This is because
P(x)=0\Leftrightarrow P(\bar{x})=0\Leftrightarrow P(1/x)=0
.

3.

Let
P\in E_{\theta}
. By symmetry of
P^{-1}(0)
with regards to
\{|x|=1\}
, it suffices to note that if
x\in P^{-1}(0)\cap\{|x|>1\}
then
|x|\leq M(P)\leq\theta
.

4.

Let
P\in E_{\theta}
. By symmetry of
P^{-1}(0)
with regards to
\{|x|=1\}
and
\{\Im x=0\}
, it suffices to note that if
x\in P^{-1}(0)\cap\{|x|>1\}\cap\{\Im x\not=0\}
then
|x|^{2}\leq M(P)\leq\theta
.

5.

This is a consequence of Proposition
2.1
and 2.

6.

The minimal polynomials corresponding to the roots of unity are cyclotomic polynomials, which are excluded from
E_{\theta}
.

7.

Assume that for some
y\in\mathbb{R}
and
\displaystyle P=\sum_{j=0}^{2n}a_{j}x^{j}\in E_{\theta}

(a_{2n}\not=0)
,
P(iy)=0
. Then
\displaystyle i\Im(P(iy))=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_{2k+1}(iy)^{2k+1}=0
. But at least one of the coefficients
a_{2k+1}
is not zero by condition c1. This would contradict the fact that
P
is the minimal polynomial of
iy
.

∎

3
Distance between zeros of a polynomial and non-zeros of modulus one

On Figure 1, we notice that the roots of the polynomials in
E_{1.3}
seem to stay far from the roots of unity. The results given in this section, when specialised to polynomials in
E_{\theta}
,
\theta\leq\theta_{0}
, allow us to estimate from below the distance between the zeros of
P\in E_{\theta}
and the roots of unity. Most of our results deal with polynomials which are self-reciprocal of even degree like the ones in
E_{\theta}
.

3.1
Lower bounds involving the Mahler measure

For an algebraic number
\mu\not=1
of degree
d
, Liouville inequality gives:

|\mu-1|\geq 2^{1-d}M(\mu)^{-1}.

We prove the following Liouville-type inequalities:

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume that
P\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
is monic, self-reciprocal of degree
2n
and does not vanish at the roots of unity. If
P(\mu)=0
and

\omega^{m}=1
or
\omega^{m}=-1

(m\in\mathbb{N}^{*})
, then

\text{ If }\mu\in\mathbb{R}\text{ or }|\mu|=1,\ \ |\mu-\omega|\geq m^{-1}2^{1-n}M(P)^{-\frac{m}{2}},

\text{ If }\mu\notin\mathbb{R}\text{ and }\ |\mu|\not=1,\ \ |\mu-\omega|\geq m^{-1}2^{1-\frac{n}{2}}M(P)^{-\frac{m}{4}},

Proof.

By substituting
P(-x)
to
P(x)
, we may assume that
\omega^{m}=1
and by substituting
\bar{\omega}
to
\omega
, we may assume that
\Im\mu\geq 0
. We divide
P^{-1}(0)\cap\{|x|\geq 1,\ \Im x\geq 0\}
into two sets:

\begin{split}&\mu\in A\Leftrightarrow P(\mu)=0,\ |\mu|=1,\Im\mu>0\text{ or }|\mu|>1,\Im\mu=0,\\
&\mu\in B\Leftrightarrow P(\mu)=0,\ |\mu|>1,\Im\mu>0.\end{split}

Then
P^{-1}(0)=\{\mu,\mu^{-1},\ \mu\in A\}\cup\{\mu,\mu^{-1},\bar{\mu},\bar{\mu}^{-1},\ \mu\in B\}
and

\prod_{\mu\in P^{-1}(0)}|1-\mu^{m}|=M(P)^{m}\prod_{\mu\in A}|1-\mu^{-m}|^{2}\prod_{\mu\in B}|1-\mu^{-m}|^{4}.\\

\displaystyle\prod_{\mu\in P^{-1}(0)}(1-\mu^{m})
is a symmetric polynomial in the zeros of
P
thus it is an integer and as
P
does not vanish at the roots of unity, it is a non-zero integer. We deduce that

\begin{split}1\leq M(P)^{\frac{m}{2}}\prod_{\mu\in A}|1-\mu^{-m}|\prod_{\mu\in B}|1-\mu^{-m}|^{2}.\\
\end{split}

For all
\mu\in A\cup B
, we have
|1-\mu^{-m}|\leq 2
thus

\forall\mu\in A,\ \ 1\leq M(P)^{\frac{m}{2}}2^{n-1}|1-\mu^{-m}|,

\forall\mu\in B,\ \ 1\leq M(P)^{\frac{m}{2}}2^{n-2}|1-\mu^{-m}|^{2}.

To obtain the desired estimate for all
\mu\in A\cup B
, we use the following

|1-\mu^{-m}|\leq|\bar{\omega}-\mu^{-1}|\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}|\mu^{-j}|\leq m|\bar{\omega}-\mu^{-1}|=m|\mu|^{-1}|\omega-\mu|\leq m|\omega-\mu|.

If
|\mu|<1
, we apply the former to
\bar{\mu}^{-1}
. and we use
|1-\bar{\mu}^{m}|\leq m|\omega-\mu|
.
∎

For integer polynomials of small Mahler measure, there are better lower bounds due to Mignotte and Waldschmitt
[
14
]
. They showed that for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
D_{0}(\varepsilon)
such that for
d\geq D_{0}(\varepsilon)

|\mu-1|>\exp\left(-(1+\varepsilon)\sqrt{d\log d\log M}\right).

This was improved by Dubickas
[
5
]
as follows:

|\mu-1|>\exp\left(-\left(\frac{\pi}{4}+\varepsilon\right)\sqrt{d\log d\log M}\right).

(3.1)

With a minor modification in Dubickas proof of Theorem 1 in
[
5
]
, we obtain a better result than (
3.1
) in the special case when
P
is self-reciprocal. This is displayed in the next proposition.

Proposition 3.2
.

Assume that
P\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
is monic, irreducible, self-reciprocal of degree
d
and satisfies
M(P)>1
. Let
P(\mu)=0
and

\omega^{m}=1
or
\omega^{m}=-1

(m\in\mathbb{N}^{*})
. Then for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
D_{0}(\varepsilon)
such that for
d\geq D_{0}(\varepsilon)

|\omega-\mu|\geq\exp\left(-\left(\frac{\pi\sqrt{m}}{8}+\varepsilon\right)\sqrt{d\log d\log M(P)}\right).

If
\mu\notin\mathbb{R}
and
|\mu|\not=1
, then

|\omega-\mu|\geq\exp\left(-\left(\frac{\pi\sqrt{m}}{16}+\varepsilon\right)\sqrt{d\log d\log M(P)}\right).

3.2
Lower bounds involving the coefficients

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
P\in\mathbb{C}[x]
be of degree
d\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. Let
\mu
be a zero of
P
with multiplicity
m_{\mu}
and
|\omega|=1
. Then,

|\mu-\omega|\geq d^{-1}\left(e^{-1}\frac{|P(\omega)|}{L(P)}\right)^{\frac{1}{m_{\mu}}}.

In particular, if all the coefficients of
P
are positive, then

|\mu-1|\geq d^{-1}e^{-\frac{1}{m_{\mu}}}.

If
|P(\omega)|=\|P\|
, then

|\mu-\omega|\geq d^{-1}\left(e^{-1}(d+1)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{m_{\mu}}}.

Proof.

We may assume
P(\omega)\not=0
.
For all
\theta\in[0,2\pi]
,

|P(\omega+d^{-1}e^{i\theta})|\leq(1+d^{-1})^{d}L(P)\leq eL(P).

Let
\mu_{k}
be the distinct zeros of
P
with respective multiplicities
m_{k}
. Applying Jensen’s formula to
\frac{P(x)}{P(\omega)}
in the disk
\{|x-\omega|<d^{-1}\}
, we find:

\sum_{|\mu_{k}-\omega|\leq d^{-1}}m_{k}\log\frac{d^{-1}}{|\mu_{k}-\omega|}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log\left|\frac{P(\omega+d^{-1}e^{i\theta})}{P(\omega)}\right|d\theta\leq\log\frac{eL(P)}{|P(\omega)|}.

For each
k
, either
|\mu_{k}-\omega|\geq d^{-1}\geq d^{-1}\frac{|P(\omega)|}{eL(P)}
or, using the estimate above,

\left(d|\mu_{k}-\omega|\right)^{m_{k}}\geq\frac{|P(\omega)|}{eL(P)}.

In the case where all the coefficients of
P
are positive,
P(1)=L(P)
.

In the case where
P(\omega)=\|P\|
, we use the inequalities (
2.2
) and (
2.3
):

L(P)\leq(d+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|P\|=(d+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}|P(\omega)|.

∎

The rest of this subsection deals with self-reciprocal polynomials. As any self-reciprocal polynomial of odd degree is the product of
(x+1)
and a self-reciprocal polynomial of even degree, we will only consider polynomials of degree
d=2n
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
P(x)=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{2n}a_{j}x^{j}
be a self-reciprocal polynomial in
\mathbb{C}[x]
of degree
2n
. Let
|\omega|=1
,
P(\omega)\not=0
and
\rho\in(0,1)
. Then

\begin{split}\sum_{\mu\in P^{-1}(0),|\mu-\omega|\leq\rho}\log\frac{\rho}{|\mu-\omega|}\leq\log\left(1+\frac{\rho(1+(1-\rho)^{-n})}{|P(\omega)|}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(n-j)|a_{j}|\right).\end{split}

(3.2)

If
\omega\in\{-1,1\}
,

\begin{split}\sum_{\mu\in P^{-1}(0),|\mu-\omega|\leq\rho}\log\frac{\rho}{|\mu-\omega|}\leq\log\left(1+\frac{\rho^{2}(1-\rho)^{-n}}{|P(\omega)|}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(n-j)^{2}|a_{j}|\right).\end{split}

(3.3)

Proof.

Jensen’s formula applied to the function
\frac{x^{-n}P(x)}{P(\omega)}
in the disk
\{|x-\omega|<\rho\}
gives

\sum_{\mu\in P^{-1}(0),|\mu-\omega|\leq\rho}\log\frac{\rho}{|\mu-\omega|}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log\left|\frac{(\omega+\rho e^{i\theta})^{-n}P(\omega+\rho e^{i\theta})}{P(\omega)}\right|d\theta.

(3.4)

Let
x
be a non-zero complex number. A straightforward computation shows that

\begin{split}x^{-n}P(x)&=\bar{\omega}^{n}P(\omega)-\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}a_{j}{\bar{\omega}}^{n-j}(x^{n-j}-\omega^{n-j})(x^{j-n}-\omega^{n-j}).\end{split}

We use

\begin{split}\left|(x^{n-j}-\omega^{n-j})(x^{j-n}-\omega^{n-j})\right|&=|x-\omega||x^{j-n}-\omega^{n-j}|\left|\sum_{k=0}^{n-j-1}x^{k}\omega^{-1-k}\right|\\
&=|x-\omega|\left|\sum_{k=0}^{n-j-1}(x^{k+j-n}\omega^{-1-k}-x^{k}\omega^{n-j-1-k})\right|\\
&\leq|x-\omega|(n-j)(1+\max(|x|^{-n},|x|^{n})\end{split}

and we readily obtain

\left|\frac{x^{-n}P(x)}{P(\omega)}\right|\leq 1+\frac{|x-\omega|(1+\max(|x|^{-n},|x|^{n})}{|P(\omega)|}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(n-j)|a_{j}|.

(3.5)

(
3.2
) follows from (
3.4
), (
3.5
) and the fact that for all
\theta\in[0,2\pi]
,

1-\rho\leq|\omega+\rho e^{i\theta}|\leq 1+\rho\leq(1-\rho)^{-1}.

(3.6)

In the case where
\omega\in\{1,-1\}
, we write

\begin{split}\left|(x^{n-j}-\omega^{n-j})(x^{j-n}-\omega^{n-j})\right|&=|x|^{-1}|x-\omega|^{2}\left|\sum_{k=0}^{n-j-1}x^{k}\omega^{-1-k}\right|\left|\sum_{k=0}^{n-j-1}x^{-k}\omega^{-1-k}\right|\\
&\leq|x-\omega|^{2}(n-j)^{2}\max(|x|^{-n},|x|^{n})\end{split}

which gives the inequality

\left|\frac{x^{-n}P(x)}{P(\omega)}\right|\leq 1+\frac{|x-\omega|^{2}\max(|x|^{n},|x|^{-n})}{|P(\omega)|}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(n-j)^{2}|a_{j}|.

(3.7)

(
3.3
) follows from (
3.4
), (
3.6
) and (
3.7
).
∎

Theorem 3.5
.

\end{split}

(3.8)

Besides, if

\frac{|\mu|}{|\mu-\omega|^{2}}\leq(\frac{n}{\log\delta}+1)^{2}+\frac{\delta}{|P(\omega)|}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(n-j)^{2}|a_{j}|,

(3.11)

Proof.

3.4

4
A lower bound for the Mahler measure of complex polynomials

A result due to Schinzel
[
20
]
states that any monic polynomial
P\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
of degree
d
whose all zeros are real (resp. positive) and such that
P(-1)P(1)\not=0
and
|P(0)|=1
satisfies

M(P)\geq\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}\ (\text{resp. }M(P)\geq\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}\right)^{d}).

V. Flammang
[
7
]
generalised Schinzel’s theorem by proving that any monic
P\in\mathbb{R}[x]
of degree
d
whose all zeros are real (resp. positive) and such that
P(0)\not=0
,
|P(1)|\geq 1
,
|P(-1)|\geq 1
satisfies

M(P)\geq\left(\frac{1+\left(4|P(0)|^{\frac{2}{d}}+1\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{2}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}\ (\text{resp. }M(P)\geq\left(\frac{1+\left(4|P(0)|^{\frac{1}{d}}+1\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{2}\right)^{d}).

J. Garza
[
9
]
showed that for monic polynomials
P\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
satisfying
P(0)P(-1)P(1)\not=0
and having
m\geq 1
real zeros, the following holds:

M(P)\geq\left(\frac{1+\left(4^{\frac{d}{m}}+1\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{2^{\frac{d}{m}}}\right)^{\frac{m}{2}}.

Our next theorem generalises the three above results to polynomials in
\mathbb{C}[x]
. It also strengthens the dependence on
P(0)
,
P(1)
and
P(-1)
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
P\in\mathbb{C}[x]
be a monic polynomial of degree
d\geq 2
.
Assume that
P
has
m\geq 1
real zeros and that
P(0)P(1)P(-1)\not=0
, then

M(P)\geq\left(\frac{|P(1)P(-1)|^{\frac{1}{m}}+\left(4^{\frac{d}{m}}|P(0)|^{\frac{2}{m}}+|P(1)P(-1)|^{\frac{2}{m}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{2^{\frac{d}{m}}}\right)^{\frac{m}{2}}

(4.1)

with equality if and only if
P
is of the form

P=(z-i)^{d_{1}}(z+i)^{d_{2}}(z-a)^{d_{3}}(z+a)^{d_{4}}(z-a^{-1})^{d_{5}}(z+a^{-1})^{d_{6}},\ \ a>1.

(4.2)

If
P
has
n\geq 1
positive zeros and
P(0)P(1)\not=0
, then

M(P)\geq\left(\frac{|P(1)|^{\frac{1}{n}}+\left(4^{\frac{d}{n}}|P(0)|^{\frac{1}{n}}+|P(1)|^{\frac{2}{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{2^{\frac{d}{n}}}\right)^{n}

(4.3)

with equality if and only if
P
is of the form

P=(z-a)^{d_{1}}(z-a^{-1})^{d_{2}},\ \ a>1.

Proof.

Let
Q=P(0)^{-1}PP^{*}
defined by (
2.4
). Let
\{\beta_{k}\}_{k=1}^{m}
be the real zeros of
Q
outside
[-1,1]
and
\{\delta_{k}\}_{k=1}^{f}
its non-real zeros such that either
|\delta_{k}|>1
or
|\delta_{k}|=1
,
\Im\delta_{k}>0
. Then
d=m+f
and

\begin{split}Q(1)&=\prod_{k=1}^{f}(1-\delta_{k})(1-\delta_{k}^{-1})\prod_{k=1}^{m}(1-\beta_{k})(1-\beta_{k}^{-1})\\
Q(-1)&=\prod_{k=1}^{f}(1+\delta_{k})(1+\delta_{k}^{-1})\prod_{k=1}^{m}(1+\beta_{k})(1+\beta_{k}^{-1})\\
|Q(1)Q(-1)|^{\frac{1}{2}}&=M(Q)\prod_{k=1}^{f}|1-\delta_{k}^{-2}|\prod_{k=1}^{m}(1-\beta_{k}^{-2})\\
\end{split}

Like in the proof of Schinzel’s theorem, we use the inequality
[
20
]
[Lemma 3]

\prod_{k=1}^{m}(1-\beta_{k}^{-2})\leq\left(1-\left(\prod_{k=1}^{m}\beta_{k}^{-2}\right)^{\frac{1}{m}}\right)^{m}\leq\left(1-M(Q)^{-\frac{2}{m}}\right)^{m},

which follows from the concavity of the function
t\mapsto\log(1-e^{-t}),t>0
. We obtain

\begin{split}|Q(1)Q(-1)|^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq M(Q)2^{d-m}\left(1-M(Q)^{-\frac{2}{m}}\right)^{m}.\end{split}

(4.4)

We deduce that

M(Q)^{\frac{2}{m}}-2^{1-\frac{d}{m}}|Q(1)Q(-1)|^{\frac{1}{2m}}M(Q)^{\frac{1}{m}}-1\geq 0

and solving the inequality
t^{2}-2^{1-\frac{d}{m}}|Q(1)Q(-1)|^{\frac{1}{2m}}t-1\geq 0
, we find

2^{\frac{d}{m}}M(Q)^{\frac{1}{m}}\geq|Q(1)Q(-1)|^{\frac{1}{2m}}+\left(4^{\frac{d}{m}}+|Q(1)Q(-1)|^{\frac{1}{m}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We conclude the proof of (
4.1
) by using (
2.5
).

Equality in (
4.1
) is equivalent to equality in (
4.4
). It is attained iff

\prod_{k=1}^{f}\frac{2}{|1-\delta_{k}^{-2}|}=\left(1-M(Q)^{-\frac{2}{m}}\right)^{-m}\prod_{k=1}^{m}(1-\beta_{k}^{-2})=1,

which gives

\delta_{1}^{2}=\ldots=\delta_{f}^{2}=-1,\ \ \ \beta_{1}^{2}=\ldots=\beta_{m}^{2}=a^{2}\text{ with }a>1.

For the proof of (
4.3
), let
\{\beta_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n}
be the positive zeros of
Q
and
\{\beta_{k}\}_{k=n+1}^{m}
be the negative zeros of
Q
. Using once again the concavity of the function
t\mapsto\log(1-e^{-t}),t>0
, we have

\begin{split}|Q(1)|^{\frac{1}{2}}&=M(Q)^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{k=1}^{f}|1-\delta_{k}^{-1}|\prod_{k=1}^{n}(1-\beta_{k}^{-1})\prod_{k=n+1}^{m}(1-\beta_{k}^{-1})\\
&\leq M(Q)^{\frac{1}{2}}2^{d-n}\prod_{k=1}^{n}(1-\beta_{k}^{-1})\\
&\leq M(Q)^{\frac{1}{2}}2^{d-n}\left(1-M(Q)^{-\frac{1}{n}}\right)^{n}.\end{split}

(4.5)

We find

M(Q)^{\frac{1}{n}}-2^{1-\frac{d}{n}}|Q(1)|^{\frac{1}{2n}}M(Q)^{\frac{1}{2n}}-1\geq 0.

We proceed as in the end of the proof of (
4.1
) by substituting
M(Q)^{\frac{1}{2n}}
to
M(Q)^{\frac{1}{m}}
and
|Q(1)|^{{\frac{1}{2n}}}
to
|Q(1)Q(-1)|^{\frac{1}{2m}}
.

Equality in (
4.3
) is equivalent to equalities in (
4.5
). It is attained iff

\prod_{k=1}^{f}\frac{2}{|1-\delta_{k}^{-1}|}\prod_{k=n+1}^{m}\frac{2}{|1-\beta_{k}^{-1}|}=\left(1-M(Q)^{-\frac{1}{n}}\right)^{-n}\prod_{k=1}^{m}(1-\beta_{k}^{-1})=1,

which gives

f=0,\ \ \ n=m=d,\ \ \ \beta_{1}=\ldots=\beta_{n}=a\text{ with }a>1.

∎

5
The number of real zeros

5.1
Bounds involving the Mahler measure

A. Dubickas
[
5
]
showed that for polynomials
P\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
of degree
d
such that
P(0)P(-1)P(1)\not=0
, there is a constant
D_{0}
such that for all
d\geq D_{0}
,

|P^{-1}(0)\cap\mathbb{R}|\leq 1.085\sqrt{d\log d\log M(P)}.

(5.1)

A bound of this size does not apply to polynomials in
\mathbb{R}[x]
. For example, the polynomials of the form
P=\left(x-d^{\frac{1}{d}}\right)^{d}
satisfy
M(P)=d
,

\sqrt{d\log d\log M(P)}=\sqrt{d}\log d\text{ \ \ while }\ \ |P^{-1}(0)\cap\mathbb{R}|=d=\frac{d\log M(P)}{\log d}\ .

Our next theorem shows that the bound
\displaystyle\frac{d\log(2M(P))}{\log d}
holds, up to a small multiplicative constant, for polynomials in
P\in\mathbb{C}[x]
such that
|P(1)P(-1)|\geq 1
and
|P(0)|\geq 1
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
P\in\mathbb{C}[x]
be a monic polynomial of degree
d\geq 2
. Assume
P(0)P(1)P(-1)\not=0
. Then

\begin{split}|P^{-1}(0)\cap\mathbb{R}|&\leq\max\left(\frac{d\log 2+\log|P(0)|-\log\left|P(1)P(-1)\right|}{-\log\left(\sinh\left(\frac{\log d}{d}\right)\right)},\frac{d\log\left(M(P)^{2}|P(0)|^{-1}\right)}{\log d}\right).\\
\end{split}

(5.2)

We note that
\displaystyle-\log\left(\sinh\left(\frac{\log d}{d}\right)\right)\sim\log d
.

Remark 5.2
.

The equality in (
5.2
) is attained by polynomials of the form (
4.2
) with
a=d^{\frac{1}{d}}
.
If we denote by
m=|P^{-1}(0)\cap\mathbb{R}|
, they satisfy

M(P)^{2}|P(0)|^{-1}=d^{\frac{m}{d}},\ \ |P(0)|^{-1}|P(1)P(-1)|=2^{d}\left(\sinh\left(\frac{\log d}{d}\right)\right)^{m}.

Thus

\frac{d\log 2+\log|P(0)|-\log\left|P(1)P(-1)\right|}{-\log\left(\sinh\left(\frac{\log d}{d}\right)\right)}=\frac{d\log\left(M(P)^{2}|P(0)|^{-1}\right)}{\log d}=m.

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Let us repeat the beginning of the proof of Theorem
4.1
with the same notations. We assume that
\displaystyle m\geq\frac{d\log M(Q)}{\log d}

and we use inequality (
4.4
):

\begin{split}|Q(1)Q(-1)|^{\frac{1}{2}}&\leq M(Q)2^{d-m}\left(1-M(Q)^{-\frac{2}{m}}\right)^{m}\\
&=2^{d}\left(\sinh\left(\frac{\log M(Q)}{m}\right)\right)^{m}\\
&\leq 2^{d}\left(\sinh\left(\frac{\log d}{d}\right)\right)^{m}\\
\end{split}

We deduce that

m\leq\frac{d\log 2-\frac{1}{2}\log\left|Q(1)Q(-1)\right|}{-\log\left(\sinh\left(\frac{\log d}{d}\right)\right)}.

The result follows by using (
2.5
).

∎

5.2
Bounds involving the Mahler measure and the length

A theorem announced by E. Schmidt in 1932 states that for polynomials of the form
P(x)=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{d}a_{j}x^{j}
,
a_{0}a_{d}\not=0
,
a_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
, there is an absolute constant
C>0
such that

|P^{-1}(0)\cap\mathbb{R}|^{2}\leq C\,d\log\left(\frac{L(P)}{\sqrt{|a_{0}a_{d}|}}\right).

(5.3)

I. Schur
[
21
]
gave an elementary proof of (
5.3
) and showed that the best possible constant
C
is
4
.

P. Borwein, T. Erdélyi and G. Kós
[
1
]
showed that whenever
|a_{0}|=|a_{d}|=1
,
|a_{j}|\leq 1
, there is an absolute constant
C>0
such that

|P^{-1}(0)\cap\mathbb{R}|\leq C\sqrt{d}.

Our next result gives an upper bound depending both on the Mahler measure and on the length of the polynomial.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
P\in\mathbb{C}[x]
be a monic polynomial of degree
d
. Assume
P(0)P(1)P(-1)\not=0
. Then

\begin{split}|P^{-1}(0)\cap\mathbb{R}|&\leq 2\sqrt{c}\left(d\log\frac{M(P)^{2}}{|P(0)|}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+(c+1)\log\frac{M(P)^{2}}{|P(0)|}\\
&+(\log c)^{-1}\log(1+d^{2})+(\log c)^{-1}\log\left(\frac{L(P)^{2}}{2|P(1)|^{2}}+\frac{L(P)^{2}}{2|P(-1)|^{2}}\right),\\
\end{split}

(5.4)

where
c>1
is such that
c\log c=1+c\ \ (c=3.594...)
.

Proof.

Once again we use
Q=P(0)^{-1}PP^{*}
and we may assume that
M(Q)>1
otherwise there are no real zeros at all.
We apply Lemma
3.4
to
Q
with
\rho=\frac{\gamma}{\gamma+1}
,
\gamma=\sqrt{\frac{(c+1)\log c\log M(Q)}{d}}
and first with
\omega=1
:

\begin{split}2\log c\,|Q^{-1}(0)\cap\{1<x\leq 1+\frac{\rho}{c}\}|&\leq\sum_{\underset{1<\mu\leq 1+\frac{\rho}{c}}{Q(\mu)=0}}\left(\log\frac{\rho}{|\mu-1|}+\log\frac{\rho}{|\mu^{-1}-1|}\right)\\
&\leq\sum_{\underset{|\mu-1|\leq\rho}{Q(\mu)=0}}\log\frac{\rho}{|\mu-1|}\\
&\leq\log\left(1+\frac{\rho^{2}(1-\rho)^{-d}d^{2}}{|Q(1)|}\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}|A_{k}|\right).\end{split}

(5.5)

Repeating the argument with
\omega=-1
,

2\log c\,|Q^{-1}(0)\cap\{-1-\frac{\rho}{c}\leq x<-1\}|\leq\log\left(1+\frac{\rho^{2}(1-\rho)^{-d}d^{2}}{|Q(-1)|}\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}|A_{k}|\right).

(5.6)

Adding (
5.5
) and (
5.6
) and using the inequality
(1+u)(1+v)\leq\left(1+\frac{u+v}{2}\right)^{2}
,

\begin{split}\log c\,|Q^{-1}(0)\cap\{1<|x|\leq 1+\frac{\rho}{c}\}|&\leq\log\left(1+d^{2}\rho^{2}(1-\rho)^{-d}\left(\frac{1}{2|Q(1)|}+\frac{1}{2|Q(-1)|}\right)\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}|A_{k}|\right)\\
&\leq\log\left(\left(\frac{1}{2|Q(1)|}+\frac{1}{2|Q(-1)|}\right)\sum_{k=0}^{d}|A_{k}|\right)+\log(1+d^{2}\rho^{2})-d\log(1-\rho)\\
\end{split}

Now we use the inequality
-\log(1-\rho)\leq\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}=\gamma
, (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) to obtain

\log c|Q^{-1}(0)\cap\{1<|x|\leq 1+\frac{\rho}{c}\}|\leq\log\left(\frac{L(P)^{2}}{2|P(1)|^{2}}+\frac{L(P)^{2}}{2|P(-1)|^{2}}\right)+\log(1+d^{2})+\gamma d.

On the other hand, by Proposition
2.1
and the inequality
\log(1+\frac{\rho}{c})\geq\frac{\rho}{c+1}
,

|Q^{-1}(0)\cap\{|x|>1+\frac{\rho}{c}\}|\leq\frac{\log M(Q)}{\log(1+\frac{\rho}{c})}\leq\log M(Q)(c+1)\rho^{-1}=\log M(Q)(c+1)(1+\gamma^{-1})

\leq(c+1)\log M(Q)+\sqrt{c\log M(Q)d}.

Finally,

\begin{split}|P^{-1}(0)\cap\mathbb{R}|&=|Q^{-1}(0)\cap\{|x|>1\}|\\
&\leq(\log c)^{-1}\log\left(\frac{L(P)^{2}}{2|P(1)|^{2}}+\frac{L(P)^{2}}{2|P(-1)|^{2}}\right)+\\
&+(\log c)^{-1}\log(1+d^{2})+(c+1)\log M(Q)+2\sqrt{c\log M(Q)d}.\\
\end{split}

We conclude the proof of (
5.4
) by applying (
2.5
).
∎

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
P\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
be of degree
d
. Assume
P(0)P(1)P(-1)\not=0
. Then

\begin{split}|P^{-1}(0)\cap\mathbb{R}|&\leq 2\sqrt{2c\,d\log M(P)}+2(c+1)\log M(P)\\
&+(\log c)^{-1}\log(1+d^{2})+2(\log c)^{-1}\log L(P),\end{split}

(5.7)

where
c>1
is such that
c\log c=1+c\ \ (c=3.594...)
.

If moreover,
\log L(P)\leq c_{1}\sqrt{d\log M(P)}
for some constant
c_{1}>0
, then for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
D_{0}(\varepsilon)
such that for
d\geq D_{0}(\varepsilon)
,

|P^{-1}(0)\cap\mathbb{R}|\leq(c_{2}+\varepsilon)\sqrt{d\log M(P)},

(5.8)

where
c_{2}=2\left(\sqrt{2c}+c+1+(\log c)^{-1}c_{1}\right)
.

Remark 5.5
.

Inequality (
5.8
) improves the bound (
5.1
) given by Dubickas in the particular case where
L(P)\leq c_{1}\sqrt{d\log M(P)}
.

Proof.

Let
P=a_{d}x^{d}+\ldots+a_{0}
with
a_{0},\ldots,a_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
a_{d}\not=0
and
P(0)P(1)P(-1)\not=0
. We apply Theorem
5.3
to the polynomial
\tilde{P}=a_{d}^{-1}P
. Inequality (
5.7
) follows after observing that

\begin{split}\frac{M(\tilde{P})^{2}}{|\tilde{P}(0)|}&=\frac{M(P)^{2}}{a_{d}|\tilde{P}(0)|}\leq M(P)^{2}\\
\frac{L(\tilde{P})^{2}}{2|\tilde{P}(1)|^{2}}+\frac{L(\tilde{P})^{2}}{2|\tilde{P}(-1)|^{2}}&=\frac{L(P)^{2}}{2|P(1)|^{2}}+\frac{L(P)^{2}}{2|P(-1)|^{2}}\leq L(P)^{2}.\end{split}

For the proof of (
5.8
), we may assume that
M(P)>1
, otherwise by Kronecker’s theorem
P
has no real zeros. Then,
we know from
[
4
]
that for some constant
\sigma>0
,

\log M(P)\geq\sigma\left(\frac{\log\log d}{\log d}\right)^{3}.

(5.9)

Thus for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
D_{0}(\varepsilon)
such that for
d\geq D_{0}(\varepsilon)
,

(\log c)^{-1}\log(1+d^{2})\leq\varepsilon\sqrt{d\log M(P)}.

On the other hand, we may also assume that
\log M(p)\leq d
because otherwise
\sqrt{d\log M(P)}\geq d
and (
5.8
) is again trivially satisfied.

Since
\log M(p)\leq\sqrt{d\log M(p)}
, (
5.8
) follows immediately from (
5.7
) and the assumption that
\log L(P)\leq c_{1}\sqrt{d\log M(P)}
.
∎

6
The number of zeros in
\{|x-1|<1\}
.

In
[
5
]
, A. Dubickas used a Vandermonde determinant which, in its simplified form is defined by

d(x)=\det(x^{(j-1)l})_{1\leq j,l\leq N}=\prod_{1\leq v<u\leq N}(x^{u}-x^{v})\ \ \ (N\in\mathbb{N}^{*}).

Following the lines of his work, we put

R(x)=|d(x)|\prod_{1\leq v<u\leq N}|x|^{-v}=\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}|x^{j}-1|^{N-j}.

In
[
5
]
, the author applies Hadamard’s inequality to obtain:

R(x)\leq\max(1,|x|)^{\frac{(N-1)N(N+1)}{6}}N^{\frac{N}{2}}.

(6.1)

Lemma 6.1
.

Assume
P\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
is monic, has degree
d
and does not vanish at the roots of unity. Then for all integer
N\geq 2
,

|P(1)|\leq N^{\frac{d}{N-1}}M(P)^{\frac{N+1}{3}}.

Remark 6.2
.

•

By Kronecker’s theorem, either
P=x^{d}
or
M(P)>1
.

•

By (
2.1
) and (
2.3
), we already had
|P(1)|\leq 2^{d}M(P)
. We retrieve this inequality if we put
N=2
in Lemma
6.1
.

Proof.

Let
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{d}
be the zeros of
P
. Then

\begin{split}R(\mu_{k})&=|\mu_{k}-1|^{\frac{(N-1)N}{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}\left|\sum_{l=0}^{j-1}\mu_{k}^{l}\right|^{N-j}\\
\end{split}

and

\prod_{k=1}^{d}R(\mu_{k})=|P(1)|^{\frac{(N-1)N}{2}}\prod_{k=1}^{d}\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}\left|\sum_{l=0}^{j-1}\mu_{k}^{l}\right|^{N-j}.

Observing that

\prod_{k=1}^{d}\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}\left(\sum_{l=0}^{j-1}\mu_{k}^{l}\right)^{N-j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{*},

and using the inequality (
6.1
), we deduce that

\begin{split}|P(1)|^{\frac{(N-1)N}{2}}&\leq\prod_{k=1}^{d}R(\mu_{k})\leq N^{\frac{dN}{2}}M(P)^{\frac{(N-1)N(N+1)}{6}}.\end{split}

The result follows easily.
∎

We will need the following result due to Zhang
[
25
]
and for which Zagier gave an elementary proof in
[

]

Theorem 6.3
.

[Zhang, Zagier]Let
\omega
be a primitive
6
th root of unity. Let
P\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
be a polynomial of degree
d
such that
P(0)P(1)P(\omega)\not=0
.
Denote
P_{*}(x)=P(1-x)
. Then

M(P)M(P_{*})\geq\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
P\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
be monic, irreducible, of degree
d
and satisfying
0<\log M(P)\leq\phi(d)
where
\phi(d)=o(\frac{d}{\log d})
. Then for
all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
D_{0}(\varepsilon)
such that for
d\geq D_{0}(\varepsilon)
,

|P^{-1}(0)\cap\{|x-1|<1\}|\geq\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\log\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}-\varepsilon\right)\sqrt{\frac{d}{\log d\log M(P)}}.

This leads to the lower bound

\log M(P)\geq\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\log\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}-\varepsilon\right)^{2}\frac{d}{K(P)^{2}\log d},

(6.2)

where
K(P)=\min\left(\left|P^{-1}(0)\cap\{|x-1|<1\}\right|,\left|P^{-1}(0)\cap\{|x+1|<1\}\right|\right)
.

Proof.

We apply Lemma
6.1
with

\displaystyle N=\left[\sqrt{\frac{d\log d}{\log M(P)}}\right]+2.
We obtain

\begin{split}\frac{N+1}{3}\log M(P)&\leq\log M(P)+\frac{1}{3}\sqrt{d\log d\log M(P)},\\
d\frac{\log N}{N-1}&\leq\sqrt{\frac{d\log M(P)}{\log d}}\log\left(2\sqrt{\frac{d\log d}{\log M(P)}}\right)\\
&=\sqrt{d\log d\log M(P)}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\log(4\log d)}{2\log d}\right)-\sqrt{\frac{d}{\log d}}\sqrt{\log M(P)}\log\sqrt{\log M(P)}\\
&\leq\sqrt{d\log d\log M(P)}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\log(4\log d)}{2\log d}\right)+e^{-1}\sqrt{\frac{d}{\log d}}.\\
\end{split}

|P(1)|\leq M(P)\exp\left(\sqrt{d\log d\log M(P)}\left(\frac{5}{6}+\frac{\log(4\log d)}{2\log d}\right)+\sqrt{\frac{d}{\log d}}\right).

Denote by
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{d}
the zeros of
P
.
By Theorem
6.3
, we have

\prod_{|1-\mu_{k}|\geq 1}|1-\mu_{k}|\geq\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}M(P)^{-1}.

We immediately deduce that

\begin{split}\prod_{|1-\mu_{k}|<1}|1-\mu_{k}|&\leq|P(1)|\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}\right)^{-\frac{d}{2}}M(P)\\
&\leq M(P)^{2}\exp\left({\sqrt{d\log d\log M(P)}}\left(\frac{5}{6}+\frac{\log(4\log d)}{2\log d}\right)+\sqrt{\frac{d}{\log d}}-\frac{d}{2}\log\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}\right)\right)\\
\end{split}

(6.3)

Denote by
J=|P^{-1}(0)\cap\{|x-1|<1\}|
and let

(6.4)

As the same apply to

∎

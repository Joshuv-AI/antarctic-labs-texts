A toolbox for black hole scattering
Nico Groenenboom

Abstract
Hawking’s free field theory is expected to break down after Page time. In previous work, we have shown that a primary dynamical reason for this breakdown is the dominance of graviton fluctuations of the horizon that mediate scattering processes. In this article, we present a toolbox for such ‘black hole scattering’ computations. The toolbox comprises of explicit expressions for the graviton propagator near the horizon in an angular momentum basis for all angular momentum modes of either parity, the leading interaction rules, and most importantly a rewriting of the theory in terms of a scalar theory with an interesting four-vertex. We demonstrate how this rewriting drastically reduces the number of diagrams to be calculated in the original formulation. Finally and perhaps most remarkably, we observe that the black hole entropy appears to emerge from the multiplicity of external legs of the dominant 2 → 2 ​ N 2\rightarrow 2N amplitudes in this theory.

Introduction

Hawking argued that black holes violate predictability in the laws of physics when free scalar fields propagate on a black hole background

. A natural question to ask is whether this conclusion would change if the scalar fields were to interact with the inevitably (quantum) fluctuating black hole spacetime. To answer such a question, it is imperative that calculations in the presence of a fluctuating black hole horizon are doable. We have shown in recent years that several relevant calculations can be done
[

that teach us interesting (near-horizon) physics. It is the aim of the present article to summarise these developments, extend them, and present a comprehensive toolbox for further calculations.

The calculations of interest are those of scattering amplitudes. The predominant question of interest is, can we calculate the amplitude of
N
particles emerging from a Schwarzschild black hole when
M
particles are thrown into it? The interactions governing the scattering processes are mediated by graviton fluctuations of the black hole horizon. To leading order, these gravitons couple to the stress tensor of the minimally coupled scalar field resulting in three-vertices of interest. Extension to higher order interactions is both desirable and straightforward with the tools we develop, although explicit calculations are likely to be hard to do in complete generality.

Several questions may arise when scattering amplitudes in curved spacetimes are considered. We list some of them that are rather natural in the context of black holes here and provide conceptual answers:

•

Black holes break translational invariance. How could we then possibly compute Feynman diagrams in momentum space? While four-momenta are indeed hard to define, exploiting the spherical symmetry of the background, they can be traded for two-momenta (along the longitudinal directions) and angular momentum labels (after integrating the sphere out). Of course, the longitudinal part of the metric is not flat, but its conformal flatness allows us to trade the curvature for potentials. And the longitudinal momenta are defined after this Weyl rescaling, without loss of generality.

•

How are kinematic variables defined if the spacetime is curved? After the Weyl rescaling described above to exploit the conformal flatness of the longitudinal part of the Schwarzschild metric, kinematic variables are defined with the help of the longitudinal two-momenta arising from the flat part of the metric. The price we pay is of course that the curvature effects have to be traded for potentials.

•

The black hole contains a large number of degrees of freedom. But perturbation theory is likely to breakdown when scattering processes involving many external legs are considered. So, are we likely to learn much at all from black hole scattering? While perturbation theory with many external legs does breakdown in the vacuum

Isn’t gravity (perturbatively) non-renormalisable? How far can computing amplitudes take us? While it is true that ultraviolet physics will kick in at some stage, one of the remarkable features of black hole scattering is that as long as centre of mass energies of scattering satisfy

Therefore, there is a lot more to be understood in the infrared than one might have expected. These are all effects that can be explicitly calculated as deviations from Hawking’s free field theory.

With those questions answered, we now list a few results of black hole scattering that may be considered as significant successes.

Successes of black hole scattering to date

•

Black hole eikonal:
As alluded to above, the emergence of a new eikonal phase near the horizon that is different from, but somewhat analogous to, the flat space eikonal is noteworthy. In flat space, the eikonal phases emerges at large impact parameters when scattering energies are trans-Planckian

Black holes have interesting time scales associated with them, scrambling time and Page time are universal examples. The success of black hole scattering is not all-encompassing. There certainly are shortcomings, some of which may be more important and/or harder to rectify than others.

Some shortcomings so far

•

In its strict sense, it is not clear how one must properly define the S-matrix in the presence of a black hole. In particular, how Hawking radiation may be incorporated into the asymptotic out states is an interesting concern. We essentially turn a blind eye to this issue, and compute scattering amplitudes as if asymptotic states were well-defined. A consequence of this is that while the corrections to Hawking’s picture are directly calculable in this approach, incorporating Hawking’s leading order Bogoliubov transformations require additional care.

•

An analytic expression for the graviton propagator in a black hole background is unavailable. Therefore, we resort to an approximate version in the near-horizon region. For several observables, this sufficiently captures the dominant physics at low energies. Nevertheless, it would be of particular interest to understand the limitations of this approximation with care.

•

Analyses of scattering processes have been limited to the leading three-vertex arising from the graviton coupled to the minimally coupled scalar stress-tensor. Vertex corrections, classical corrections from graviton self-interactions, higher order interactions, other gauge and matter fields have all yet to be studied.

•

Another obvious shortcoming of the black hole scattering program is a technical one. All calculations so far have only been done in the presence of a large black hole. Calculations in the presence of a time dependent background are analytically intractable. While the scale at which these effects become is not entirely clear, it is certainly of interest to exhaustively understand what questions necessitate these effects to be studied.

. So, a natural guess for the longitudinal diffeomorphism would be

\displaystyle\bar{\xi}_{a}~=~-\dfrac{V_{a}}{2V^{2}}KY_{0}+f\left(x,y\right)\epsilon_{ab}V^{b}Y_{0}\,.

(2.25)

To ensure that the second term indeed fixes the trace degree of freedom, we demand that
\delta h_{ab}=-\eta^{ab}\mathfrak{h}_{ab}
which can be worked out to be

\displaystyle\left(V\cdot\partial\right)f+f\left(\nabla\cdot V\right)~=~-\eta^{ab}\mathfrak{h}_{ab}~\eqqcolon~-\mathfrak{h}\,.

(2.26)

In Schwarzschild coordinates, this equation can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\partial_{r}f+\dfrac{\left(\nabla\cdot V\right)}{V^{r}}f~=~-\dfrac{\mathfrak{h}}{V^{r}}\,,

(2.27)

which has a solution with the following integral representation

\displaystyle f~=~-I\left(r\right)\int\limits_{R}^{r}\dfrac{\mathfrak{h}\left(r^{\prime},t\right)}{I\left(r^{\prime}\right)V^{r}\left(r^{\prime}\right)}\text{d}r^{\prime}\quad\text{with}\quad I\left(r\right)~=~\text{Exp}\left[-\int\limits_{R}^{r}\dfrac{\left(\nabla\cdot V\right)\left(r^{\prime}\right)}{V^{r}\left(r^{\prime}\right)}\text{d}r^{\prime}\right]\,.

(2.28)

2.1.1
Traceless gauge

As we have demonstrated above, in the traceless gauge, the only remaining off-shell degrees of freedom are the diagonal longitudinal modes

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}_{0}^{ab}~=~\begin{pmatrix}\mathfrak{h}_{0}^{xx}&0\\
0&\mathfrak{h}_{0}^{yy}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(2.29)

The quadratic action is

\displaystyle S~=~\dfrac{1}{4}\int\text{d}^{2}x~\mathfrak{h}_{0}^{ab}\Delta^{-1}_{abcd}\mathfrak{h}_{0}^{cd}\,,

(2.30)

where the quadratic operator is given by

\displaystyle\Delta^{-1}_{abcd}~

\displaystyle=~\dfrac{1}{2}\mu^{2}\left(\eta_{ac}x_{[b}\partial_{d]}+\eta_{bd}x_{[a}\partial_{c]}\right)+\dfrac{1}{2}\mu^{2}\bigr(\eta_{ab}\eta_{cd}-\eta_{ac}\eta_{bd}-\eta_{ad}\eta_{bc}\bigr)\,.

(2.31)

This is the operator that was found in
[
4
]
for the even modes, only made traceless (as defined in Appendix

) to fix the redundant gauge degree of freedom. In momentum space the operator reads

\displaystyle\Delta^{-1}_{abcd}~

\displaystyle=~\dfrac{1}{2}\mu^{2}\left(\eta_{ac}k_{[b}\partial^{k}_{d]}+\eta_{bd}k_{[a}\partial^{k}_{c]}\right)+\dfrac{1}{2}\mu^{2}\bigr(\eta_{ab}\eta_{cd}-\eta_{ac}\eta_{bd}-\eta_{ad}\eta_{bc}\bigr)\,

(2.32)

where
\partial^{k}_{a}
is the derivative with respect to
k_{a}
. In order to invert this operator, we begin by observing that time-translational invariance (in Schwarzschild coordinates) or dilation invariance (under
x\rightarrow\lambda x
and

in Kruskal-Szekeres coordinates) implies that the most general form of the inverse is symmetric in the first and last two indices, in addition to being symmetric under exchange of the first two indices with the last two:

\displaystyle\Delta_{abcd}~

\displaystyle=~A\left(k^{2}\right)\eta_{ab}\eta_{cd}+B\left(k^{2}\right)\eta_{a(c}\eta_{d)b}+C\left(k^{2}\right)\left(\eta_{ab}k_{c}k_{d}+\eta_{cd}k_{a}k_{b}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+D\left(k^{2}\right)\left(\eta_{a(c}k_{d)}k_{b}+\eta_{b(c}k_{d)}k_{a}\right)+E\left(k^{2}\right)k_{a}k_{b}k_{c}k_{d}\,.

(2.33)

In addition, to respect the traceless gauge, we require the inverse to be traceless (as discussed in Appendix
B
) such that

\displaystyle\Delta^{-1}_{abcd}\Delta^{cdef}~

\displaystyle=~\delta^{(e}_{a}\delta^{f)}_{b}-\dfrac{1}{2}\eta_{ab}\eta^{ef}

\displaystyle=~\dfrac{B\mu^{2}}{2}\eta_{ab}\eta^{ef}-\left(B\mu^{2}+\dfrac{\mu^{2}Dk^{2}}{4}\right)\delta_{a}^{(e)}\delta_{b}^{f)}+\dfrac{\mu^{2}D}{4}\left(\eta_{ab}k^{e}k^{f}+\eta^{ef}k_{a}k_{b}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad-\left(\dfrac{\mu^{2}D}{2}+\mu^{2}Ek^{2}\right)k_{(a}\delta_{b)}^{(e}k^{f)}+\mu^{2}Ek_{a}k_{b}k^{e}k^{f}\,.

(2.34)

where in the second equality, we plugged in
4
4

4

Notice that
\partial^{k}_{a}F\left(k^{2}\right)=2F^{\prime}k_{a}
and so on for other similar terms. This implies that
k_{[a}\partial^{k}_{b]}F\left(k^{2}\right)=2F^{\prime}k_{[a}k_{b]}=0
allowing us to drop all derivative terms acting on scalar functions.
the general form (
2.1.1
). The solution to this equation is given by

B\left(k^{2}\right)~=~-\dfrac{1}{\mu^{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad D\left(k^{2}\right)~=~0~=~E\left(k^{2}\right)\,,

(2.35)

with arbitrary functions
A\left(k^{2}\right)
and

to be traceless and are given by

A\left(k^{2}\right)~=~\dfrac{1}{2\mu^{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad C\left(k^{2}\right)~=~0\,.

(2.36)

Therefore, the propagator is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\ell=0}^{abcd}~=~\dfrac{1}{2\mu^{2}}\left(\eta^{ab}\eta^{cd}-\eta^{ac}\eta^{bd}-\eta^{ad}\eta^{bc}\right)\,.

(2.37)

Evidently, the propagator has no momentum dependence. This is owed to the fact that there are only two off-shell degrees of freedom in the monopole and therefore, imposing equations of motion, we are left with no dynamical modes.

2.2
Propagator for the dipole mode:

Before fixing any gauge, the dipole mode

\displaystyle h^{-}_{1m,\mu\nu}~

(2.41)

&&&r^{2}(K-G)\sin^{2}\theta\end{pmatrix}Y_{1}^{m}\,.

(2.42)

2.2.1
Propagator for the even harmonics

We see from (
2.42
) that the only transverse scalar mode is
K-G
which we may conveniently just call
K
. Therefore, we have

h^{+}_{1m,\mu\nu}~=~\begin{pmatrix}H_{xx}&H_{xy}&h^{+}_{x}\partial_{\theta}&h^{+}_{x}\partial_{\phi}\\
&H_{yy}&h^{+}_{y}\partial_{\theta}&h^{+}_{y}\partial_{\phi}\\
&&r^{2}K&0\\
&&&r^{2}K\sin^{2}\theta\end{pmatrix}Y_{1}^{m}\,.

(2.43)

Of the four gauge degrees of freedom to be fixed, three are even modes. The convenient choice would be to gauge fix the transverse scalar and
h^{+}_{a}
which we do by the following choice

\xi_{a}~=~\left(\dfrac{1}{2}r^{2}\partial_{a}K-h^{+}_{a}\right)Y_{1}^{m}\quad\text{and}\quad\xi_{A}~=~-\dfrac{1}{2}r^{2}K\partial_{A}Y_{1}^{m}\,,

(2.44)

to reduce the even mode to

h^{+}_{1m,\mu\nu}~=~\begin{pmatrix}H_{xx}&H_{xy}&0&0\\
&H_{yy}&0&0\\
&&0&0\\
&&&0\end{pmatrix}Y_{1}^{m}\,.

(2.45)

The remaining degree of freedom must be removed from the odd mode. The quadratic action for the even wave is therefore

\displaystyle S~=~\sum_{m=-1}^{1}\dfrac{1}{4}\int\text{d}^{2}x~\mathfrak{h}_{1m}^{ab}\Delta^{-1}_{abcd}\mathfrak{h}_{1m}^{cd}\,,

(2.46)

where

\displaystyle\Delta^{-1}_{abcd}~

\displaystyle=~\dfrac{\mu^{2}}{2}\left(\eta_{ac}x_{[b}\partial_{d]}+\eta_{bd}x_{[a}\partial_{c]}+\eta_{ab}x_{(c}\partial_{d)}-\eta_{cd}x_{(a}\partial_{b)}\right)+2\mu^{2}\bigr(\eta_{ab}\eta_{cd}-\eta_{a(c}\eta_{d)b}\bigr)\,.

(2.47)

To find the propagator, we first observe that the even mode carries three offshell degrees of freedom and the odd mode comes with one (after gauge fixing the other). This implies that upon imposing equations of motion, there are no dynamical degrees of freedom left. So, we expect that the derivative terms in the quadratic operator will not contribute to the propagator. So, we may use the general ansatz (
2.1.1
) but without requiring tracelessness. Therefore, we find

\displaystyle\Delta^{-1}_{abcd}\Delta^{cdef}~

\displaystyle=~\delta^{(e}_{a}\delta^{f)}_{b}

\displaystyle=~2\mu^{2}\left(A+B\right)\eta_{ab}\eta^{ef}-2\mu^{2}B\delta_{a}^{(e}\delta^{f)}_{b}+\text{derivative terms}\,.

(2.48)

where we used the momentum space representation (
x\to i\partial^{k}
and
\partial\to-ik
) in the second equality. The solution is found to be

\displaystyle A\left(k^{2}\right)~

\displaystyle=~\dfrac{1}{2\mu^{2}}\,,\quad B\left(k^{2}\right)~=~-\dfrac{1}{2\mu^{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad C\left(k^{2}\right)~=~D\left(k^{2}\right)~=~E\left(k^{2}\right)~=~0\,.

(2.49)

So, the propagator is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{1m}^{abcd}~=~\dfrac{1}{4\mu^{2}}\bigr(2\eta^{ab}\eta^{cd}-\eta^{ac}\eta^{bd}-\eta^{ad}\eta^{bc}\bigr)\,.

(2.50)

2.2.2
Propagator for the odd harmonics

The action for the odd dipole mode can be read off from the general near-horizon action (
C.24
)

\displaystyle S_{1,m}~=~-\int\text{d}^{2}x~\mathfrak{h}^{a}\left(\partial_{a}\partial_{b}-\eta_{ab}\partial^{2}-\dfrac{3}{2}\mu^{2}x_{a}\partial_{b}+\dfrac{3}{2}\mu^{2}x_{b}\partial_{a}+\dfrac{3}{2}\mu^{2}\eta_{ab}\right)\mathfrak{h}^{b}\,.

(2.51)

A convenient choice of gauge to fix the redundant degree of freedom in the odd mode
h^{a}
is a Lorenz-like gauge since it is a spin one field in question. We choose
\partial_{a}h^{a}=0
whose corresponding diffeomorphism is given by
\xi_{A}=-F\left(x\right){\epsilon_{A}}^{B}\partial_{B}
, where
F
is given by a solution to

\displaystyle\partial^{2}F\left(x\right)-2V^{a}\partial_{a}F\left(x\right)-2F\left(x\right)\partial_{a}V^{a}~=~-\partial_{a}\mathfrak{h}^{a}\,.

(2.52)

Owing to the gauge choice, the first and third terms in the quadratic operator vanish identically. The fourth term can be integrated by parts as

\displaystyle\int\text{d}^{2}x~\mathfrak{h}^{a}x_{b}\partial_{a}\mathfrak{h}^{b}~

\displaystyle=~\int\text{d}^{2}x~\mathfrak{h}^{a}\partial_{a}\left(x_{b}\mathfrak{h}^{b}\right)-\int\text{d}^{2}x~\mathfrak{h}^{a}\eta_{ab}\mathfrak{h}^{b}

\displaystyle=~-\int\text{d}^{2}x~\mathfrak{h}^{b}x_{b}\partial_{a}\mathfrak{h}^{a}-\int\text{d}^{2}x~\mathfrak{h}^{a}\eta_{ab}\mathfrak{h}^{b}\,,

(2.53)

where the first integral drops due to the gauge choice and the second term cancels the last term in (
2.51
), leaving us with

\displaystyle S_{1,m}~=~\int\text{d}^{2}x~\mathfrak{h}^{a}\eta_{ab}\partial^{2}\mathfrak{h}^{b}\,.

(2.54)

The propagator is therefore easily found to be the familiar one for a massless spin one field

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{1m}^{ab}~=~\dfrac{\eta^{ab}}{k^{2}-i\epsilon}\,.

(2.55)

In the generalised Regge-Wheeler gauge of
[
29

30
]
,
V^{a}h_{a}=0
, the quadratic operator takes the following non-invertible form:
\partial_{a}\partial_{b}-\partial^{2}\eta_{ab}
. However, defining
h^{a}=h_{-}\epsilon^{ab}V_{b}
results in a propagator for the field
h_{-}
. It would be interesting to understand the apparent gauge-dependent emergent dynamics for what is arguably a non-propagating mode
[
29
,
30
]
.

3
Interactions with matter

In this section, we will proceed to (minimally) couple the theory we have considered so far, to matter (with an action we label
S_{M}
). The leading interaction term takes the form

\displaystyle S_{int}~

\displaystyle=~\dfrac{1}{2}\kappa\int\text{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}~h^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu}\quad\text{with}\quad T_{\mu\nu}~\coloneqq~\dfrac{-2}{\sqrt{-g}}\dfrac{\delta S_{M}}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}\,.

(3.1)

For the even modes, using (
2.58
), we have

\displaystyle S^{+}_{int}~

\displaystyle=~\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell m\end{subarray}}\dfrac{1}{2}\kappa\int\text{d}^{2}x\left(r^{2}A\left(x,y\right)\right)\text{d}\Omega_{2}~\left(g^{ac}g^{bd}H_{cd}T_{ab}+Kg^{AB}T_{AB}\right)Y_{\ell}^{m}

\displaystyle=~\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell m\end{subarray}}\dfrac{1}{2}\kappa\int\text{d}^{2}x\left(r^{2}A\left(x,y\right)\right)\text{d}\Omega_{2}~\left(\eta^{ac}\eta^{bd}\dfrac{A\left(x,y\right)}{rA\left(x,y\right)^{2}}\mathfrak{h}_{cd}T_{ab}+\dfrac{\mathcal{K}}{r}g^{AB}T_{AB}\right)Y_{\ell}^{m}

\displaystyle=~\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell m\end{subarray}}\dfrac{1}{2}\kappa\int\text{d}^{2}x~r~\text{d}\Omega_{2}~\left(\mathfrak{h}^{ab}T_{ab}+\mathcal{K}g^{AB}T_{AB}\right)Y_{\ell}^{m}\,,

(3.2)

where in the second line, we used the field definitions
rH_{ab}=A\left(x,y\right)\mathfrak{h}_{ab},~rK=\mathcal{K}
and the fact that
A\left(x,y\right)g^{ab}=\eta^{ab}
. Considering a minimally coupled scalar field, we have

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}~

\displaystyle=~\partial_{\mu}\tilde{\phi}\partial_{\nu}\tilde{\phi}-\dfrac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\rho}\tilde{\phi}\partial^{\rho}\tilde{\phi}\,.

(3.3)

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle T_{ab}~

\displaystyle=~\partial_{a}\tilde{\phi}\partial_{b}\tilde{\phi}-\dfrac{1}{2}g_{ab}\partial_{\rho}\tilde{\phi}\partial^{\rho}\tilde{\phi}

\displaystyle=~\left(\dfrac{1}{r}\partial_{a}\phi+\phi\partial_{a}\dfrac{1}{r}\right)\left(\dfrac{1}{r}\partial_{b}\phi+\phi\partial_{b}\dfrac{1}{r}\right)-\dfrac{1}{2}g_{ab}\left(\dfrac{1}{r}\partial_{\rho}\phi+\phi\partial_{\rho}\dfrac{1}{r}\right)\left(\dfrac{1}{r}\partial^{\rho}\phi+\phi\partial^{\rho}\dfrac{1}{r}\right)

\displaystyle=~\dfrac{1}{r^{2}}\left(\partial_{a}\phi\partial_{b}\phi-\dfrac{1}{2}\eta_{ab}\eta^{cd}\partial_{c}\phi\partial_{d}\phi-\dfrac{\eta_{ab}}{2A\left(x,y\right)r^{2}}\gamma^{CD}\partial_{C}\phi\partial_{D}\phi\right)+\dots

\displaystyle\sim~\dfrac{1}{R^{2}}\left(\partial_{a}\phi\partial_{b}\phi-\dfrac{1}{2}\eta_{ab}\eta^{cd}\partial_{c}\phi\partial_{d}\phi-\dfrac{1}{2}\eta_{ab}\gamma^{CD}\hat{\partial}_{C}\phi\hat{\partial}_{D}\phi\right)

\displaystyle\sim~\dfrac{1}{R^{2}}\left(\partial_{a}\phi\partial_{b}\phi-\dfrac{1}{2}\eta_{ab}\eta^{cd}\partial_{c}\phi\partial_{d}\phi\right)~\eqqcolon~\dfrac{1}{R^{2}}\tilde{T}_{ab}\,,

(3.4)

where we redefined the scalar field as
\tilde{\phi}\to\frac{1}{r}\phi
in the second line. The terms represented by the dots evidently drop out in the near-horizon limit
r\sim R
,
A\left(x,y\right)\sim 1
. In the third line, we also used that
g^{CD}=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\gamma^{CD}
where
\gamma^{CD}
is the inverse of the round metric on the unit two-sphere. In the fourth line, we defined the rescaled transverse derivatives
\hat{\partial}_{A}\coloneqq\frac{1}{R}\partial_{A}
in the near horizon limit. The transverse effects are, therefore, naturally suppressed in the near-horizon limit by an additional
\frac{1}{R}
factor in comparison to the longitudinal momenta. Therefore, in the fifth line, we ignore the last term of the fourth line and arrive at the energy momentum tensor purely along the longitudinal directions.

Similarly, for the transverse and mixed components of the stress tensor, we have

\displaystyle g^{AB}T_{AB}~

\displaystyle=~g^{AB}\partial_{A}\tilde{\phi}\partial_{B}\tilde{\phi}-g^{ab}\partial_{a}\tilde{\phi}\partial_{b}\tilde{\phi}-g^{CD}\partial_{C}\tilde{\phi}\partial_{D}\tilde{\phi}

\displaystyle=~-\dfrac{1}{A\left(x,y\right)}\eta^{ab}\left(\dfrac{1}{r}\partial_{a}\phi+\phi\partial_{a}\dfrac{1}{r}\right)\left(\dfrac{1}{r}\partial_{b}\phi+\phi\partial_{b}\dfrac{1}{r}\right)

\displaystyle\sim~-\dfrac{1}{R^{2}}\eta^{ab}\partial_{a}\phi\partial_{b}\phi

(3.5)

\displaystyle T_{aA}~

\displaystyle=~\partial_{a}\tilde{\phi}\partial_{A}\tilde{\phi}~\sim~\dfrac{1}{R}\partial_{a}\phi\hat{\partial}_{A}\phi\,.

(3.6)

Therefore, in the near-horizon limit, the interaction term for the even graviton takes the following form:

\displaystyle S^{+}_{int}~

\displaystyle=~\dfrac{\kappa}{2R}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell m\\
\ell_{1}m\\
\ell_{2}m\end{subarray}}\int\text{d}^{2}x~\text{d}\Omega_{2}~\Biggr[\mathfrak{h}^{ab}\left(\partial_{a}\phi_{\ell_{1},m_{1}}\partial_{b}\phi_{\ell_{2},m_{2}}-\dfrac{1}{2}\eta_{ab}\partial_{c}\phi_{\ell_{1},m_{1}}\partial^{c}\phi_{\ell_{2},m_{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-\mathcal{K}\eta^{ab}\partial_{a}\phi_{\ell_{1},m_{1}}\partial_{b}\phi_{\ell_{2},m_{2}}\Biggr]Y_{\ell}^{m}Y_{\ell_{1}}^{m_{1}}Y_{\ell_{2}}^{m_{2}}

\displaystyle=~\dfrac{\gamma}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell m\\

where
\kappa^{2}=8\pi G
. To find the entropy associated with the multiplicity of the external states, we need knowledge of the relevant centre of mass energy of the scattering process to be considered. Lacking a first principle derivation, there are several arguments to be made in favour of the choice
E=M_{BH}
:

•

The canonical energy scale of the system we would like to probe is indeed
M_{BH}
.

•

The Eisenbud-Wigner time-delay associated with elastic
2\rightarrow 2
scattering has been shown to be scrambling time, agreeing with the expectation from
[
11
]
, only if we set
E=M_{BH}

[
5
]
. This is perhaps the strongest indication in favour of this choice.

•

In similar vein, the Eisenbud-Wigner time-delay associated with inelastic
2\rightarrow 2N
scattering has been shown to be Page time when
E=M_{BH}

[
5
]
. An interpretation of this result is that when
E=M_{BH}
energy is thrown into the black hole, it doubles the energy contained in the black hole. After the scattering process, momentum conservation implies that all the energy that went in has returned and therefore, the black hole has halved in size returning to its original mass. It is therefore natural that the half-life time of the black hole is how long one has to wait for information to return.

Therefore, with
E=M_{BH}
, we have

\displaystyle N_{max}~=~\dfrac{2}{e}4\pi GM_{\text{BH}}^{2}~=~\dfrac{2}{eG}\dfrac{4\pi R^{2}}{4}~=~\dfrac{2}{eG}A~=~\dfrac{2}{e}S_{BH}\,.

(6.4)

Inserting this into (
6.2
), we find

\displaystyle S_{\Omega}~\sim~S_{BH}\dfrac{2\log\left(4\right)}{e}\,.

(6.5)

The proportionality factor is evidently of order one. With very little input, it is remarkable that such a result emerges!

Summary

In this article, we have developed a toolbox that can be used to compute scattering amplitudes in the presence of a fluctuating black hole background. In particular, we found the graviton propagator near the horizon of a Schwarzschild black hole in a partial wave basis for all angular momentum modes of either parity. We also showed that not all graviton modes are interacting at leading (three-point) order and found the propagator for the interacting modes. We then found that this theory can be rewritten (without loss of generality) as a scalar theory with a particular four-vertex that captures the elastic
2\rightarrow 2
graviton exchange. We demonstrated that this rewriting is exceptionally effective for computations, dramatically reducing the number of computations in comparison to the original formulation.

This toolbox can be used for many interesting computations including all scattering amplitudes of interest. In addition to addressing some of the shortcomings mentioned in the introduction, it would also be interesting to address the issue of antipodal identification that appears to be a natural boundary condition to glue the future of the past horizon and the past of the future horizon together, for consistency

. Extensions to incorporate other standard model fields and charged particles is another straightforward application. In upcoming work, we hope to address several such applications that rely on the the tools developed in this article.

Appendix A
Background metric and choice of coordinates

In this paper, we work in Kruskal-Szekeres coordinates that are defined as

\displaystyle xy~

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~2R^{2}\left(1-\dfrac{r}{R}\right)e^{\tfrac{r}{R}-1}\,,

(A.1)

\displaystyle x/y~

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~e^{2\tau}\,,

(A.2)

where
r\left(x,y\right)
is implicitly defined,
\tau=\tfrac{t}{2R}
and
R=2GM
is the Schwarzschild radius (whose inverse we call
\mu=1/R
). We will often write these coordinates as a two-vector
x^{a}=\{x,y\}
with small Latin letters denoting the longitudinal coordinates and capital Latin letters denoting the transverse coordinates on the sphere. We will also work in natural units
\hbar=c=1
. The Schwarzschild metric in these coordinates is given by

\displaystyle\text{d}s^{2}~

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~-2A(r)\text{d}x\text{d}y+r^{2}\left(x,y\right)\text{d}\Omega^{2}_{2}\,,

(A.3)

\displaystyle A(r)~

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\dfrac{R}{r}e^{1-\tfrac{r}{R}}\,.

(A.4)

where we employ the mostly plus signature. To leading order in the near horizon region, we have
r\sim R
and therefore
A\sim 1
resulting in the product space

\displaystyle\text{d}s^{2}~=~-2\text{d}x\text{d}y+R^{2}\text{d}\Omega^{2}_{2}\,.

(A.5)

Christoffel symbols:

The non-vanishing Christoffel symbols of the Schwarzschild metric in Kruskal-Szekeres coordinates are given by

\displaystyle\Gamma^{c}_{ab}~

\displaystyle=~\delta^{c}_{(a}U_{b)}-\dfrac{1}{2}g_{ab}U^{c}

\displaystyle\Gamma^{C}_{AB}~=~\delta^{C}_{(A}W_{B)}-g_{AB}W^{C}

(A.6)

\displaystyle\Gamma^{A}_{Ba}~

\displaystyle=~V_{a}\delta^{A}_{B}

\displaystyle\Gamma^{a}_{AB}~=~-V^{a}g_{AB}\,,

(A.7)

where
V_{a}=\partial_{a}\log r
,
U_{a}=\partial_{a}\log A
and
W_{A}=\partial_{A}\log\sin\theta
. The rest of the symbols
\Gamma_{ab}^{A}=0=\Gamma_{aA}^{b}
vanish identically.

The Riemann tensor:

Using the definition

R^{\rho}_{\text{ }\mu\sigma\nu}~=~\partial_{\sigma}\Gamma^{\rho}_{\mu\nu}-\partial_{\nu}\Gamma^{\rho}_{\mu\sigma}+\Gamma^{\rho}_{\sigma\kappa}\Gamma^{\kappa}_{\mu\nu}-\Gamma^{\rho}_{\mu\kappa}\Gamma^{\kappa}_{\sigma\nu}

(A.8)

the only non-vanishing components of the Riemann tensor are related to the following components by symmetries

\displaystyle R_{xyxy}~

\displaystyle=~\partial_{x}\partial_{y}\log A\,,

(A.9)

\displaystyle R_{\theta\phi\theta\phi}~

\displaystyle=~r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\left(1+\dfrac{2\partial_{x}r\partial_{y}r}{A}\right)\,,

(A.10)

\displaystyle R_{aAbB}~

\displaystyle=~g_{AB}S_{ab}\,,

(A.11)

where in the last line we defined a new tensor

\displaystyle S_{ab}~

\displaystyle\coloneqq-\nabla_{(a}V_{b)}-V_{a}V_{b}~=~-\tilde{\nabla}_{(a}V_{b)}-V_{a}V_{b}\,.

(A.12)

In the second equality above, we defined
\tilde{\nabla}
which is the covariant derivative only along the longitudinal directions. This second equality holds because
\Gamma_{ab}^{C}=0
.

A.1
The antisymmetric Levi-Civita tensor

Here we define the antisymmetric tensor on the transverse two-sphere as

\displaystyle\epsilon_{AB}=r^{2}\sin\theta\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}\,,

(A.13)

with
\epsilon_{\theta\phi}=r^{2}\sin\theta=-\epsilon_{\phi\theta}
. Indices are raised and lowered with the usual round metric on the two-sphere. Therefore we have

\displaystyle\epsilon_{A}^{\text{ }\text{ }B}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\sin\theta\\
-\csc\theta&0\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\epsilon^{AB}=\frac{1}{r^{2}\sin\theta}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}\,.

(A.14)

Similarly, in the near horizon region
r\sim R
, we have the antisymmetric tensor in the longitudinal directions

\displaystyle\epsilon^{ab}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\epsilon_{ab}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}\,.

(A.15)

Appendix B
The group of traceless tensor operators

In this section, we consider the group
\bar{G}
consisting of all invertible rank four tensors that are (symmetric) traceless in their first and last pair of indices. For
M,N\in\bar{G}
the elements in the group are defined by

\displaystyle\eta^{ab}M_{abcd}~

\displaystyle=~0~=~M_{abcd}\eta^{cd}\,.

(B.1)

The group operation is given by

\displaystyle\left(M\cdot N\right)_{abef}~\equiv~M_{abcd}N^{cd}_{\text{ }\text{ }\text{ }\text{ }ef}\,,

(B.2)

where closure (
M\cdot N\in\bar{G}
) is guaranteed by the observation that
M\cdot N
is still traceless over
ab
and
ef
. Of course,
\bar{G}
is a subgroup of the group
G
that consists of
all
invertible rank four tensors. The question of interest now is what the identity element
\bar{I}\in\bar{G}
is, since this might differ from the identity element
I\in G
given by
I_{ab}^{cd}=\delta_{(a}^{c}\delta_{b)}^{d}
. Indeed we require
\bar{I}
to be traceless, but
I
clearly isn’t. The obvious correction would be to make it traceless

\displaystyle\bar{I}_{ab}^{cd}~=~\delta_{(a}^{c}\delta_{b)}^{d}-\dfrac{1}{2}\eta_{ab}\eta^{cd}\,.

(B.3)

It can now be checked that

\displaystyle\eta^{ab}\bar{I}_{ab}^{cd}~=0~=~\bar{I}_{ab}^{cd}\eta_{cd}\quad\text{and}\quad\left(\bar{I}\cdot M\right)_{abef}~=~\bar{I}_{ab}^{cd}M_{cdef}~=~M_{abef}

(B.4)

hold, since
M
is traceless. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle M^{-1}\cdot M~=~M^{-1}_{abcd}M^{cd}_{\text{ }\text{ }\text{ }\text{ }ef}~=~\bar{I}_{abef}~=~\bar{I}\,.

(B.5)

where we used that
\bar{I}_{abef}=\bar{I}_{efab}
which ensures that there is no ambiguity between using covariant or contravariant indices.

Appendix C
Calculation of the propagator for the odd harmonics

We begin with the first line of the action (
2
):

\displaystyle S~

\displaystyle=~-\dfrac{1}{2}\int\text{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}h^{\mu\nu}\left[\dfrac{1}{2}\left(2\nabla^{\rho}\nabla_{(\mu}h_{\nu)\rho}-\Box h_{\mu\nu}-\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}h\right)-~\dfrac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\biggr(\nabla^{\rho}\nabla^{\sigma}h_{\rho\sigma}-\Box h\biggr)\right]

\displaystyle=~-\dfrac{1}{2}\int\text{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}h^{\mu\nu}\left[G^{(1)}_{\mu\nu}\right]\,,

(C.1)

where
G^{(1)}_{\mu\nu}
is the first order variation of the Einstein tensor. Into this action, we will now plug in the odd harmonics

h^{-}_{aA}~\eqqcolon~h_{a}\eta_{A}\quad\text{with}\quad\eta_{A}~\coloneqq~-{\epsilon_{A}}^{B}\partial_{B}Y_{\ell}^{m}\,.

(C.2)

First, we notice that
\eta_{A}
does not depend on the longitudinal coordinates while
h_{a}
is independent of the transverse spherical coordinates. Since
g_{aA}=0
and
h^{-}=g^{\mu\nu}h^{-}_{\mu\nu}=0
, we have

\displaystyle G^{(1),-}_{aA}~

\displaystyle=~\dfrac{1}{2}g^{\rho\sigma}\bigr(\nabla_{\rho}\nabla_{a}h^{-}_{A\sigma}+\nabla_{\rho}\nabla_{A}h^{-}_{a\sigma}\bigr)-\dfrac{1}{2}\Box h^{-}_{aA}\,.

(C.3)

We now use the familiar property
[\nabla_{\rho},\nabla_{\sigma}]h_{\mu\nu}=-R^{\tau}_{\text{ }\text{ }\mu\rho\sigma}h_{\tau\nu}-R^{\tau}_{\text{ }\text{ }\nu\rho\sigma}h_{\tau\mu}
, to rewrite the above as

\displaystyle G^{(1),-}_{aA}~

\displaystyle=~\nabla_{(a}L_{A)}-\dfrac{1}{2}g^{\rho\sigma}\bigr({R^{\tau}}_{A\rho a}h^{-}_{\tau\sigma}+{R^{\tau}}_{\sigma\rho a}h^{-}_{\tau A}+{R^{\tau}}_{a\rho A}h^{-}_{\tau\sigma}+{R^{\tau}}_{\sigma\rho A}h^{-}_{\tau a}\bigr)-\dfrac{1}{2}\Box h^{-}_{aA}\,,

(C.4)

where we defined
L_{\mu}\coloneqq\nabla^{\nu}h^{-}_{\mu\nu}
. Using the Riemann tensor components written out in Appendix
A
and writing out the covariant derivatives, we find

\displaystyle G^{(1),-}_{aA}~=~\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{a}-2V_{a}\right)L_{A}+\dfrac{1}{2}\partial_{A}L_{a}+S_{ab}h^{b}\eta_{A}-\dfrac{1}{2}\Box h^{-}_{aA}\,,

(C.5)

where we used
R_{AaBb}h_{-}^{bB}~=~S_{ab}h^{b}\eta_{A}
from the definition of the quantity
S_{ab}
in Appendix
A
. We will now calculate the quantities
L_{\mu}
and the
\Box
term. The first quantity to calculate is

\displaystyle L_{A}~

\displaystyle=~g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}h^{-}_{A\nu}

\displaystyle=~g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}h^{-}_{A\nu}-g^{\mu\nu}\Gamma_{\mu A}^{\rho}h^{-}_{\rho\nu}-g^{\mu\nu}\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\rho}h^{-}_{A\rho}

\displaystyle=~\eta_{A}g^{ab}\left(\partial_{a}+2V_{a}\right)h_{b}\,,

(C.6)

where we inserted
h_{-}^{aA}=h^{a}\eta^{A}
and the relevant Christoffel symbols to arrive at the last line.
Similarly,

\displaystyle L_{a}~

\displaystyle=~g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}h^{-}_{a\nu}

\displaystyle=~g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}h^{-}_{a\nu}-g^{\mu\nu}\Gamma_{\mu a}^{\rho}h^{-}_{\rho\nu}-g^{\mu\nu}\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\rho}h^{-}_{a\rho}

\displaystyle=~h_{a}\dfrac{1}{\sin\theta}g^{AB}\partial_{A}\left(\sin\theta\eta_{B}\right)

\displaystyle=~h_{a}\hat{\nabla}^{A}\eta_{A}

\displaystyle=~-h_{a}\hat{\nabla}^{A}\epsilon_{BC}\hat{\nabla}^{C}Y_{\ell m}

\displaystyle=~0\,,

(C.7)

where in the second last line, we identified the covariant derivative on the two-sphere and to arrive at the last line, we observe that the covariant derivatives acting on
Y_{\ell m}
are in competition with the antisymmetry of
\epsilon_{BC}
.

The term containing the d’Alembertian can be written as

\displaystyle\Box h^{-}_{aA}~

\displaystyle=~\Box h^{-}_{aA}-\partial^{c}\left(\Gamma_{ca}^{b}h^{-}_{bA}\right)-\partial^{\nu}\left(\Gamma_{\nu A}^{B}h^{-}_{Ba}\right)-g^{\mu\nu}\Gamma^{\tau}_{\mu\nu}\left(\partial_{\tau}h^{-}_{aA}-\Gamma_{\tau a}^{b}h^{-}_{bA}-\Gamma_{\tau A}^{B}h^{-}_{Ba}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad-g^{\mu\nu}\Gamma_{\mu a}^{b}\partial_{\nu}h^{-}_{bA}+g^{\mu\nu}\Gamma_{\mu a}^{\tau}\Gamma_{\nu\tau}^{b}h^{-}_{bA}-g^{\mu\nu}\Gamma_{\mu A}^{B}\partial_{\nu}h^{-}_{Ba}

\displaystyle\qquad+g^{\mu\nu}\Gamma_{\mu A}^{\tau}\Gamma_{\nu\tau}^{B}h^{-}_{Ba}+2g^{\mu\nu}\Gamma_{\mu a}^{\tau}\Gamma_{\nu A}^{\kappa}h^{-}_{\kappa\tau}\,.

(C.8)

We now insert
h^{-}_{aA}=h^{-}_{Aa}=h_{a}\eta_{A}
and separate the longitudinal terms from the transverse ones. This gives

\displaystyle\Box h^{-}_{aA}~

\displaystyle=~\eta_{A}\biggr[g^{cd}\partial_{c}\partial_{d}h_{a}-\partial^{c}\left(\Gamma_{ac}^{b}h_{b}\right)-\partial^{b}\left(V_{b}h_{a}\right)-g^{\mu\nu}\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{b}\partial_{b}h_{a}+g^{\mu\nu}\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{c}\Gamma_{ac}^{b}h_{b}+g^{\mu\nu}\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{b}V_{b}h_{a}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\Gamma_{ac}^{b}\partial^{c}h_{b}+g^{cd}\Gamma^{e}_{ca}\Gamma^{b}_{de}h_{b}-2V_{a}V^{b}h_{b}-V_{b}\partial^{b}h_{a}+2V^{c}\Gamma_{ca}^{b}h_{b}-2V_{a}V^{b}h_{b}\biggr]

\displaystyle\qquad+h_{a}\biggr[g^{BC}\partial_{B}\partial_{C}\eta_{A}-\partial^{C}\left(\Gamma^{B}_{AC}\eta_{B}\right)-g^{\mu\nu}\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{B}\partial_{B}\eta_{A}+g^{\mu\nu}\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{C}\Gamma^{B}_{AC}\eta_{B}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\Gamma_{AC}^{B}\partial^{C}\eta_{B}+g^{CD}\Gamma_{CA}^{E}\Gamma^{B}_{DE}\eta_{B}\biggr]\,.

(C.9)

Writing longitudinal covariant derivatives with a tilde and transverse ones with a hat, this can be compactly written as

\displaystyle\Box h^{-}_{aA}~

\displaystyle=~\eta_{A}\tilde{\Box}h_{a}-4\eta_{A}V_{a}V^{b}h_{b}-2\eta_{A}V^{b}V_{b}h_{a}-\eta_{A}\left(\partial^{b}V_{b}\right)h_{a}+h_{a}\hat{\Delta}\eta_{A}\,,

(C.10)

where
\hat{\Delta}
is the Laplacian on the two sphere.

C.1
Integrating the two-sphere out

Piecing the above terms together, we have:

\displaystyle G^{(1),-}_{aA}~

\displaystyle=~\dfrac{1}{2}\eta_{A}\biggr[-\tilde{\Box}h_{a}+\left(\tilde{\nabla}_{a}-2V_{a}\right)\left(\tilde{\nabla}_{b}+2V_{b}\right)h^{b}+2S_{ab}h^{b}+4V_{a}V_{b}h^{b}+2V^{2}h_{a}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\left(\tilde{\nabla}\cdot V\right)h_{a}\biggr]+\dfrac{1}{2}h_{a}\hat{\Box}\eta_{A}\,.

(C.11)

We notice that

\displaystyle\hat{\Box}\eta_{A}~

\displaystyle=~-\epsilon^{AB}\eta^{CD}\hat{\nabla}_{C}\hat{\nabla}_{D}\hat{\nabla}_{B}Y_{\ell m}

\displaystyle=~-\epsilon^{AB}\left(\eta^{CD}\hat{\nabla}_{C}\hat{\nabla}_{B}\hat{\nabla}_{D}Y_{\ell m}\right)

\displaystyle=~-\epsilon^{AB}\left(\hat{\nabla}_{B}\hat{\Box}Y_{\ell m}-\eta^{CD}{\hat{R}^{E}}_{~DCB}\hat{\nabla}_{E}Y_{\ell m}\right)

\displaystyle=~-\epsilon^{AB}\left(\hat{\nabla}_{B}\hat{\Box}Y_{\ell m}+{\hat{R}^{E}}_{~B}\hat{\nabla}_{E}Y_{\ell m}\right)

\displaystyle=~-\epsilon^{AB}\left(\hat{\nabla}_{B}\hat{\Box}Y_{\ell m}+\hat{R}\hat{\nabla}_{B}Y_{\ell m}\right)

\displaystyle=~-\dfrac{\lambda-2}{r^{2}}\eta_{A}\,,

(C.12)

where the second equality is allowed because the spherical harmonics are scalar functions on the sphere, and in the last equality, we used that the Ricci tensor of the two sphere satisfies
\hat{R}_{AB}=\frac{1}{2}\hat{R}g_{AB}=\frac{1}{r^{2}}g_{AB}
.

Therefore, the action for the odd harmonics can now be written as

\displaystyle S~

\displaystyle=~-\sum_{\ell,m,\ell^{\prime},m^{\prime}}\int\text{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}h^{a}_{\ell m}\eta^{A}_{\ell m}\left[G^{(1),-}_{aA,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}\right]

\displaystyle=~-\sum_{\ell,m,\ell^{\prime},m^{\prime}}\dfrac{1}{2}\int\text{d}^{2}x~A\left(x,y\right)r\left(x,y\right)^{2}\int\text{d}\Omega_{2}~\eta^{A}_{\ell m}\eta_{A,\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad h^{a}_{\ell m}\biggr[\left(-\tilde{\Box}+2V^{c}V_{c}+\left(\tilde{\nabla}\cdot V\right)+\dfrac{\lambda-2}{r^{2}}\right)g_{ab}+\left(\tilde{\nabla}_{a}-2V_{a}\right)\left(\tilde{\nabla}_{b}+2V_{b}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+2S_{ab}+4V_{a}V_{b}\biggr]h^{b}_{\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=~-\dfrac{\lambda-1}{2}\sum_{\ell,m}\int\text{d}^{2}x~A\left(x,y\right)h^{a}_{\ell m}\biggr[\left(-\tilde{\Box}+2V^{c}V_{c}+\left(\tilde{\nabla}\cdot V\right)+\dfrac{\lambda-2}{r^{2}}\right)g_{ab}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\left(\tilde{\nabla}_{a}-2V_{a}\right)\left(\tilde{\nabla}_{b}+2V_{b}\right)+2S_{ab}+4V_{a}V_{b}\biggr]h^{b}_{\ell m}\,,

(C.13)

where in the third equality, we inserted the definition of
\eta_{A}
and applied Stokes’ theorem before integrating over the sphere. Remarkably, the action is proportional to
\lambda-1=\ell^{2}+\ell
. Therefore, the contribution of these odd modes for large
\ell
is heavily suppressed in comparison to the even modes.

C.2
Weyl transformation and the near-horizon approximation

The quadratic operator in (
C.1
) reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{ab}~

\displaystyle=~A\left(x,y\right)\biggr[\left(-\tilde{\Box}+2V^{c}V_{c}+\left(\tilde{\nabla}\cdot V\right)+\dfrac{\lambda-2}{r^{2}}\right)g_{ab}+\left(\tilde{\nabla}_{a}-2V_{a}\right)\left(\tilde{\nabla}_{b}+2V_{b}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+2S_{ab}+4V_{a}V_{b}\biggr]

\displaystyle=~A\left(x,y\right)\biggr[\left(-\tilde{\Box}+2V^{c}V_{c}+\left(\tilde{\nabla}\cdot V\right)+\dfrac{\lambda-2}{r^{2}}\right)g_{ab}+\tilde{\nabla}_{a}\tilde{\nabla}_{b}+2\left(\tilde{\nabla}_{a}V_{b}\right)+2V_{b}\tilde{\nabla}_{a}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-2V_{a}\tilde{\nabla}_{b}+2S_{ab}\biggr]

\displaystyle=~A\left(x,y\right)\biggr[\left(-\tilde{\Box}+2V^{c}V_{c}+\left(\tilde{\nabla}\cdot V\right)+\dfrac{\lambda-2}{r^{2}}\right)g_{ab}+\tilde{\nabla}_{a}\tilde{\nabla}_{b}+2\left(\tilde{\nabla}_{a}V_{b}\right)+2V_{b}\tilde{\nabla}_{a}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-2V_{a}\tilde{\nabla}_{b}-2V_{a}V_{b}\biggr]

where in the third equality, we plugged in the definition of
S_{ab}
from (
A.12
). Given the complicated background spacetime, this operator is not invertible. However, we will now exploit the conformal flat nature of the metric
g_{ab}=A\left(x,y\right)\eta_{ab}
to make the following field redefinition

h_{a}~=~\sqrt{A\left(x,y\right)}\,\mathfrak{h}_{a}\,.

(C.15)

This transforms the quadratic operator as

\mathcal{D}_{ab}~\longrightarrow~\sqrt{A\left(x,y\right)}\mathcal{D}_{ab}\sqrt{A\left(x,y\right)}\,.

(C.16)

In order to cycle the
\sqrt{A\left(x,y\right)}
from the far right in the above equation to the other side, we will make extensive use of the following identity

\partial_{a}\sqrt{A\left(x,y\right)}~=~\sqrt{A\left(x,y\right)}\left(\partial_{a}+\dfrac{1}{2}U_{a}\right)\,.

(C.17)

We will now write all quantities appearing in the odd harmonic action (
C.1
) in terms of the Weyl transformed field and the flat metric
\eta_{ab}
. Therefore, in what follows, we will use the symbol “
\rightarrow
” to show the step where the Weyl transformation and replacement of
g_{ab}
by
A\left(x,y\right)\eta_{ab}
have been made.

The first term of interest is

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}^{a}\tilde{\nabla}^{b}h_{b}~

\displaystyle=~\partial^{a}\partial^{b}h_{b}

\displaystyle\to~A\sqrt{A}\dfrac{1}{A}\partial^{a}\dfrac{1}{A}\partial^{b}\sqrt{A}~\mathfrak{h}_{b}

\displaystyle=~\left(\partial^{a}-\dfrac{1}{2}U^{a}\right)\left(\partial^{b}+\dfrac{1}{2}U^{b}\right)\mathfrak{h}_{b}\,.

(C.18)

As desired there are only flat space derivatives and potential terms that are artefacts of the curvature. Next, we consider

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{a}\tilde{\nabla}^{a}h_{b}~

\displaystyle=~g^{ac}\tilde{\nabla}_{a}\tilde{\nabla}_{c}h_{b}

\displaystyle=~g^{cd}\partial_{c}\partial_{d}h_{b}-U_{c}\partial^{c}h_{b}-U_{b}\partial^{c}h_{c}+U^{a}\partial_{b}h_{a}-\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\partial^{c}U_{c}\right)h_{b}-\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\partial^{c}U_{b}\right)h_{c}+\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{b}U_{c}\right)h^{c}

\displaystyle\to~g^{cd}\left(\partial_{c}+\dfrac{1}{2}U_{c}\right)\left(\partial_{d}+\dfrac{1}{2}U_{d}\right)\mathfrak{h}_{b}-U_{c}\left(\partial^{c}+\dfrac{1}{2}U^{c}\right)\mathfrak{h}_{b}-U_{b}\left(\partial^{c}+\dfrac{1}{2}U^{c}\right)\mathfrak{h}_{c}

\displaystyle\qquad+U^{c}\left(\partial_{b}+\dfrac{1}{2}U_{b}\right)\mathfrak{h}_{c}-\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\partial^{c}U_{c}\right)\mathfrak{h}_{b}-\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\partial^{c}U_{b}\right)\mathfrak{h}_{c}+\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{b}U_{c}\right)\mathfrak{h}^{c}

\displaystyle=~\eta_{bc}\biggr[\partial^{2}-\dfrac{1}{4}U_{d}U^{d}\biggr]\mathfrak{h}^{c}+\biggr[-U_{b}\partial_{c}+U_{c}\partial_{b}\biggr]\mathfrak{h}^{c}\,,

(C.19)

where in the second equality, we used that
g^{cd}\Gamma^{e}_{cb}\Gamma^{a}_{de}=0
. Finally we consider the single derivative terms:

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}^{a}h_{b}~

\displaystyle=~\partial^{a}h_{b}-\dfrac{1}{2}U_{b}h^{a}-\dfrac{1}{2}U^{a}h_{b}+\dfrac{1}{2}\delta_{b}^{a}U^{c}h_{c}

\displaystyle\to~\partial^{a}\mathfrak{h}_{b}-\dfrac{1}{2}U_{b}\mathfrak{h}^{a}+\dfrac{1}{2}\delta_{b}^{a}U^{c}\mathfrak{h}_{c}\,.

(C.20)

Putting these results together gives the following operator:

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{ab}~

\displaystyle\to~\partial_{a}\partial_{b}+U_{[a}\partial_{b]}-4V_{[a}\partial_{b]}+\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{a}U_{b}\right)-\dfrac{1}{4}U_{a}U_{b}-2V_{a}V_{b}

\displaystyle\qquad+\eta_{ab}\left(-\partial^{2}+\dfrac{1}{4}U_{d}U^{d}-V_{c}U^{c}+2V_{c}V^{c}+\left(\partial_{c}V^{c}\right)+A\left(x,y\right)\dfrac{\lambda-2}{r^{2}}\right)\,.

(C.21)

The biggest advantage of all the manipulations done so far is that the theory is now entirely defined in two dimensional flat space with the Minkowski metric
\eta_{ab}
. The remaining derivatives are all partial derivatives with the curvature traded for complicated potential terms. This allows us the luxury of defining familiar a Fourier transforms. So far all calculations were exact. The problem still remains that the operator is not invertible analytically. To remedy this problem, we will employ a near-horizon approximation to find an inverse near the horizon.

The near-horizon approximation

To leading order, near the horizon we have
r\sim R
. This implies that the longitudinal coordinates satisfy
x,y\ll R
or equivalently
\mu x_{a}\ll 1
. The potentials now become

\displaystyle V_{a}~\sim~\dfrac{1}{2}\mu^{2}x_{a}\quad\text{and}\quad U_{b}~\sim~-\mu^{2}x_{b}\,.

(C.22)

The quadratic operator therefore simplifies to

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{ab}~=~\partial^{a}\partial^{b}-\eta^{ab}\partial^{2}-3\mu^{2}x^{[a}\partial^{b]}+\mu^{2}\eta^{ab}\left(\lambda-\dfrac{3}{2}\right)\,.

(C.23)

Therefore, the action for the odd modes can be written as

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell,m}S_{\ell,m}~=~\sum_{\ell,m}-\dfrac{\lambda-1}{2}\int\text{d}^{2}x~\mathfrak{h}^{a}\left(\partial_{a}\partial_{b}-\eta_{ab}\partial^{2}-3\mu^{2}x_{[a}\partial_{b]}+\mu^{2}\eta_{ab}\left(\lambda-\dfrac{3}{2}\right)\right)\mathfrak{h}^{b}\,.

(C.24)

The quadratic operator is now easy to invert, using ideas developed in

4
]
.

C.3
Propagator for the odd harmonics

To invert the operator, we first perform a Fourier transform, resulting in

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{ab}~

\displaystyle=~\eta^{ab}\left(k^{2}+\mu^{2}\left(\lambda-\dfrac{3}{2}\right)\right)-k^{a}k^{b}-3\mu^{2}k^{[a}\partial_{k}^{b]}\,,

(C.25)

where we used that

\displaystyle\int\text{d}^{2}x\mathfrak{h}^{a}\left(-3\mu^{2}x_{[a}\partial_{b]}\right)\mathfrak{h}^{b}~

\displaystyle=~\int\text{d}^{2}x\int\text{d}^{2}k\int\text{d}^{2}k^{\prime}\mathfrak{h}^{a}\left(k\right)\mathfrak{h}^{b}\left(k^{\prime}\right)e^{ik\cdot x}\left(-3\mu^{2}x_{[a}\partial_{b]}\right)e^{ik^{\prime}\cdot x}

\displaystyle=~\int\text{d}^{2}x\int\text{d}^{2}k\int\text{d}^{2}k^{\prime}\mathfrak{h}^{a}\left(k\right)\mathfrak{h}^{b}\left(k^{\prime}\right)e^{ik\cdot x}\left(-3i\mu^{2}x_{[a}k^{\prime}_{b]}\right)e^{ik^{\prime}\cdot x}

\displaystyle=~\int\text{d}^{2}x\int\text{d}^{2}k\int\text{d}^{2}k^{\prime}\mathfrak{h}^{a}\left(k\right)\mathfrak{h}^{b}\left(k^{\prime}\right)e^{ik\cdot x}\left(-3\mu^{2}k^{\prime}_{[b}\partial^{k^{\prime}}_{a]}\right)e^{ik^{\prime}\cdot x}

\displaystyle=~\int\text{d}^{2}x\int\text{d}^{2}k\int\text{d}^{2}k^{\prime}e^{i(k+k^{\prime})\cdot x}\mathfrak{h}^{a}\left(k\right)\left(3\mu^{2}\partial^{k^{\prime}}_{[a}k^{\prime}_{b]}\right)\mathfrak{h}^{b}\left(k^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle=~\left(2\pi\right)^{2}\int\text{d}^{2}k^{\prime}\mathfrak{h}^{a}\left(-k^{\prime}\right)\left(-3\mu^{2}k^{\prime}_{[a}\partial^{k^{\prime}}_{b]}\right)\mathfrak{h}^{b}\left(k^{\prime}\right)\,,

(C.26)

where in the second equality we rewrote the partial derivative, in the third, we commuted
x
and
k
before rewriting
x
as a partial derivative with respect to
k
, integrated by parts in the fourth, and used antisymmetry in the final equality.

We would now like to find the Green’s function
\mathcal{G}^{bc}
that satisfies

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{ab}\,\mathcal{G}^{bc}~=~\delta_{a}^{c}\,.

(C.27)

Dilation invariance of the background
x\to\lambda x,\,y\to\lambda^{-1}y
corresponds to time translation invariance of the black hole background. This suggests the following form for the Green’s function

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{bc}~=~F\left(k^{2}\right)\left(\eta^{bc}+G\left(k^{2}\right)k^{b}k^{c}\right)\,,

(C.28)

where
F,\,G
are to be determined. Acting with the operator
\mathcal{D}
on
\mathcal{G}
gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{ab}\,\mathcal{G}^{bc}~

\displaystyle=~F\delta_{a}^{c}\left(k^{2}+\mu^{2}\left(\lambda-\dfrac{3}{2}\right)\right)+Fk_{a}k^{c}\left(G\mu^{2}\left(\lambda-\dfrac{3}{2}\right)-1\right)

\displaystyle\qquad-3\mu^{2}\biggr(k_{[a}\partial_{k}^{c]}F+k_{[a}\partial^{k}_{b]}\left(FGk^{b}k^{c}\right)\biggr)\,.

(C.29)

Action of the the derivatives on
F,\,G
can be worked out using the chain rule resulting in
\partial_{a}F=2F^{\prime}k_{a}
and
\partial_{a}G=2G^{\prime}k_{a}
. However, since all derivatives contain an antisymmetrisation, we see that derivatives on the scalar factors vanish:

\displaystyle k_{[a}\partial_{k}^{c]}F~=~2F^{\prime}k_{[a}k^{c]}~=~0\,.

(C.30)

Therefore, the total contribution from the derivatives is given by

\displaystyle k_{[a}\partial_{k}^{c]}F+k_{[a}\partial^{k}_{b]}\left(k^{b}k^{c}FG\right)~

\displaystyle=~FGk_{[a}\delta^{b}_{b]}k^{c}+FGk_{[a}\delta^{c}_{b]}k^{b}

\displaystyle=~\dfrac{1}{2}FG\biggr(k_{a}k^{c}\delta^{b}_{b}-k_{b}k^{c}\delta_{a}^{b}+k_{a}k^{b}\delta^{c}_{b}-k^{2}\delta^{c}_{a}\biggr)

\displaystyle=~FGk_{a}k^{c}-\dfrac{1}{2}FGk^{2}\delta^{c}_{a}\,.

(C.31)

Inserting this contribution into the defining equation gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{ab}\,\mathcal{G}^{bc}~

\displaystyle=~F\delta_{a}^{c}\left(k^{2}+\mu^{2}\left(\lambda-\dfrac{3}{2}\right)+\dfrac{3}{2}\mu^{2}Gk^{2}\right)+Fk_{a}k^{c}\left(G\mu^{2}\left(\lambda-\dfrac{9}{2}\right)-1\right)~=~\delta_{a}^{c}\,.

(C.32)

The solution to this equation is easily seen to be given by

\displaystyle F~=~\dfrac{\lambda-\frac{9}{2}}{\lambda-3}\dfrac{1}{k^{2}+\mu^{2}\frac{\left(\lambda-\frac{3}{2}\right)\left(\lambda-\frac{9}{2}\right)}{\lambda-3}}\quad\text{and}\quad G~=~\dfrac{1}{\mu^{2}\left(\lambda-\frac{9}{2}\right)}\,.

(C.33)

We now finally have the propagator for the odd harmonics which reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{ab}~=~\dfrac{\lambda-\frac{9}{2}}{\lambda-1}\dfrac{1}{k^{2}\left(\lambda-3\right)+\mu^{2}\left(\lambda-\frac{3}{2}\right)\left(\lambda-\frac{9}{2}\right)}\left(\eta^{ab}+\dfrac{k^{a}k^{b}}{\mu^{2}\left(\lambda-\frac{9}{2}\right)}\right)\,.

(C.34)

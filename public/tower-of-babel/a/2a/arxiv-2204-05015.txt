Compactly generated tensor t-structures on the derived category of a Noetherian scheme
Umesh V Dubey, Gopinath Sahoo

Abstract
. We introduce a tensor compatibility condition for t-structures. For any Noetherian scheme X X , we prove that there is a one-to-one correspondence between the set of Thomason filtrations and the set of aisles of compactly generated tensor compatible t-structures on the derived category of X X . This generalizes the earlier classification of compactly generated t-structures for commutative rings to schemes. Hrbek and Nakamura have reformulated the famous telescope conjecture for t-structures. As an application of our main theorem, we prove that a tensor version of the conjecture is true for separated Noetherian schemes.

Introduction

The classification of subcategories in terms of subsets of the ambient space, in the setting of
derived categories, first appeared in the work of Hopkins. Inspired by
the analogous result in the finite stable homotopy category
[
HS98
, Theorem 7]
, Hopkins
obtained that for a
commutative ring
R
there is a one-to-one correspondence between thick subcategories of
perfect complexes on
R
and the specialization closed subsets of
\mathrm{Spec}R

[
Hop87
, Theorem 11]
. Neeman intrigued by this parallel between the stable homotopy category and the
derived category conducted further investigation. He obtained the classification of localizing
subcategories of the unbounded derived category
\mathbf{D}(R)
, which on restricting to the
perfect complexes
\mathrm{{Perf}}{(R)}
provides Hopkins’ theorem
[
Nee92
, Theorem 1.5]
.
However, Neeman showed all this is true for Noetherian rings and that extra care is needed for
arbitrary rings; see
[
Nee92
, Example 4.1]
.

Thomason took a step further and generalized the results of Hopkins and Neeman to schemes.
For similar classification to hold in the case of schemes he observed that a tensor compatible
condition on thick subcategories of
\mathrm{{Perf}}{(X)}
has to be considered; a condition which all
thick subcategories satisfy in the affine situation. His second important observation was that
for the non-Noetherian case specialization closed subsets were to be replaced by subsets
stronger than specialization closed; such subsets are now being called Thomason subsets
after him; see Definition
3.16
. Thus he obtained for a quasi-compact
quasi-separated scheme
X
, a bijective
correspondence between thick
\otimes
-ideals of
\mathrm{{Perf}}{(X)}
and Thomason subsets of
X

[
Tho97
, Theorem 3.15]
, thereby, closing the gap pointed out by Neeman and
establishing the correct bijective correspondence for arbitrary commutative rings; see his
commentaries
[
Tho97
, History 3.17]
. Similar classifications in the situation of
Noetherian formal schemes
[
ATJLSS04
, Theorem 4.12]
and for Noetherian graded rings

[
DS13
, Theorem 5.8]
have been achieved.

All these works consider suitable triangulated
subcategories in the derived category or perfect complexes. Another important class of
subcategories, not necessarily triangulated, is the class
of t-structures introduced in
[
BBD82
]
by Beĭlinson et al. in their study of
perverse sheaves. Stanley showed the class of t-structures on
\mathbf{D}(\mathbb{Z})
is not a
set
[
Sta10
, Corollary 8.4]
and therefore it is not feasible to classify all t-structures of the
unbounded derived category in terms of subsets of
\mathrm{Spec}R
. However, a subclass of
t-structures on
\mathbf{D}(R)
, precisely the compactly generated ones, has been classified in
terms of filtrations of specialization closed subsets by Alonso et al.
[
ATJLS10
, Theorem 3.11]
,
for
R
Noetherian. Hrbek has extended this classification to arbitrary commutative rings,

[
Hrb20
, Theorem 5.1]
. Although he obtained a one-to-one bijection between the set of
compactly generated t-structures and the set of Thomason filtrations, unlike the
Noetherian case, it is not clear that the aisle of such a t-structure is determined by
cohomological supports; see the introduction to section 5 of
[
Hrb20
]
.

In this article, we generalize the classification of compactly generated t-structures to
Noetherian schemes. First, we introduce the notion of tensor t-structures; an almost similar
notion has been studied in
[
ATJLSS03
, §5]
with a different goal in mind. Such t-structures
localize well to the open subsets. For a Noetherian scheme
X
we prove:

Theorem 0.1
.

There is a bijective correspondence between the set of aisles of compactly generated
tensor t-structures on
\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)
and the set of Thomason filtrations of
X
.

(For more details see Theorem
4.11
.)

Though our approach is close to Thomason’s proof of
[
Tho97
, Theorem 3.15]
in spirit,
our proof differs in two aspects. Since the aisle of a tensor t-structure is rarely a
\otimes
-ideal,
one of the key ingredients of
[
Tho97
]
- the Tensor Nilpotence Theorem or any naive
variation of it, is not helpful in our case. Instead, we use a local global principle to obtain
Lemma
4.9
the counterpart of
[
Tho97
, Lemma 3.14]
. Another
obstacle was extending perfect complexes on an open subscheme to perfect complexes on
the ambient scheme along an aisle. Thomason and Trobaugh in their seminal
paper
[
TT90
]
give a criterion for the extension of perfect complexes using
K_{0}
groups;
see
[
TT90
, Lemma 5.6.2]
. In the case of t-structures, one needs to consider the supports
of cohomology sheaves component-wise, instead of their union as in the stable case. Thus,
we needed to have extensions of perfect complexes with some boundedness
assumption. This we achieve after closely inspecting and later modifying various results of

[
TT90
, §5]
; see section
3.2
.

A Bousfield subcategory of a triangulated category is smashing if the corresponding
localization functor preserves coproducts. The telescope conjecture asks if all such smashing
subcategories are compactly generated. The question was originally asked by Ravenel for the
stable homotopy category of spectra
[
Rav84
]
, in this case, the answer remains elusive.
However, in the
case of algebraic triangulated categories, there are many positive results. The first one is due
to Neeman
[
Nee92
, Corollary 3.4]
. For a detailed discussion on the history of the
conjecture and various positive results in this direction see the introduction of
[
Ant14
]
.
Since Bousfield subcategories correspond to stable t-structures, it is natural to seek a
reformulation of the conjecture which encompasses t-structures. Hrbek and Nakamura have
formulated the telescope conjecture for homotopically smashing t-structures in the language of
derivators; see
[
HN21
, Question A.7]
. They proved for a commutative Noetherian ring
R

the telescope conjecture for homotopically smashing t-structures is true, more precisely, any
homotopically smashing t-structure on
\mathbf{D}(R)
is compactly generated

[
HN21
, Theorem 1.1]
. As an application of Theorem
4.11
, we obtain, for a
separated Noetherian scheme
X
:

Theorem 0.2
.

Any homotopically smashing tensor t-structure on
\mathbf{D}(\mathrm{Qcoh}(X))
is
compactly generated.
(For more details see §
5
)

This provides yet another proof of the tensor telescope conjecture in this case; see Remark

5.10
and also
[
BF11
, Corollary 6.8]
.

In section
1
we discuss the basic facts about t-structures, we recall that
a t-structure is completely determined by its aisle. The notion of compactly generated
t-structure appears in various guises. Though all these notions are known to be
equivalent, a proof seems to be missing in the literature. Hence we give a complete treatment
of such t-structures in this section. In section
2
we introduce tensor t-structures
and
\otimes
-aisles of a tensor triangulated category and collect some basic facts about them.
We show the relation between the new notion of
\otimes
-aisle and the earlier notion of
\otimes
-ideal; see Proposition
2.9
. In the third section, for a Noetherian scheme
X
, we
prove that the associated subcategory of a
Thomason filtration is a compactly generated
\otimes
-aisle of
\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)
; see Theorem

3.20
. In section
4
, we prove the main classification result.
In the last section, we discuss the telescope conjecture for homotopically smashing
t-structures and prove that a tensor version of the conjecture is true for separated Noetherian
schemes.

1.
Preliminaries

Convention

We always assume a triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
has all
small coproducts unless otherwise stated.

1.1.
Basics on t-structures

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category. The notion of t-structure
is introduced by Beĭlinson, Bernstein, and Deligne in
[
BBD82
, Définition 1.3.1]
.

Definition 1.1
.

A
t-structure
on
\mathcal{T}
is a pair of full subcategories
(\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{T}^{\geq 0})

satisfying the following conditions :

(t1)

For
A\in\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}
and
B\in\mathcal{T}^{\geq 0}[-1]
,
\mathrm{Hom}(A,B)=0
;

(t2)

\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}[1]\subset\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}
and
\mathcal{T}^{\geq 0}[-1]\subset\mathcal{T}^{\geq 0}
;

(t3)

For any
T\in\mathcal{T}
, there is a distinguished triangle

A\rightarrow T\rightarrow B\rightarrow A[1]

such that
A\in\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}
and
B\in\mathcal{T}^{\geq 0}[-1]
.

The triangle in (t3) is unique and we call it the t-decomposition triangle of
T
for
(
\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}
,
\mathcal{T}^{\geq 0}
). The full subcategory
\mathcal{H}
=
\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}

\cap

\mathcal{T}^{\geq 0}
of
\mathcal{T}
is called
the
heart
of the t-structure (
\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}
,
\mathcal{T}^{\geq 0}
). It is an abelian category and there is a
natural cohomological functor
H^{0}:\mathcal{T}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
.

In
[
KV88
]
Keller and Vossieck observed that the pair (
\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}
,
\mathcal{T}^{\geq 0}
) is completely
determined by the subcategory
\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}
. They characterized such subcategories and termed
them as
aisles
of
\mathcal{T}
. We recall the precise definition of aisle
[
KV88
, 1.1]
.

Definition 1.2
.

A full subcategory
\mathcal{U}
is an
aisle
of
\mathcal{T}
if it satisfies the following conditions:

(a1)

closed under positive shifts, i.e.
\mathcal{U}[1]\subset\mathcal{U}
;

(a2)

closed under extensions, i.e. for each distinguished triangle

A\to B\to C\to A[1]

in
\mathcal{T}
,
if
A
and
C
are in
\mathcal{U}
then
B
is in
\mathcal{U}
;

(a3)

the inclusion
\mathcal{U}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}
admits a right adjoint denoted by
\tau_{\mathcal{U}}^{\leq}
.

The functor
\tau_{\mathcal{U}}^{\leq}
is called the truncation functor associated with
\mathcal{U}
.

For any subcategory
\mathcal{U}
of
\mathcal{T}
, we denote
\mathcal{U}^{\perp}
to be the full subcategory
consisting of objects
B\in\mathcal{T}
such that
\mathrm{Hom}(A,B)=0
for all
A\in\mathcal{U}
.
Analogously we define
{}^{\perp}\mathcal{U}
to be the full subcategory of objects

B\in\mathcal{T}
such that
\mathrm{Hom}(B,A)=0
for all
A\in\mathcal{U}
.

The assignments, (
\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}
,
\mathcal{T}^{\geq 0}
)
\mapsto

\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}
and

\mathcal{U}

\mapsto
(
\mathcal{U}
,
\mathcal{U}
⊥
[1]) give a mutually inverse bijective correspondence
between the aisles of
\mathcal{T}
and the t-structures on
\mathcal{T}
; see
[
KV88
, §1]
. The
subcategory
(\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0})^{\perp}=\mathcal{T}^{\geq 0}[-1]
is called the coaisle of the t-structure
(
\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}
,
\mathcal{T}^{\geq 0}
).

We define a few notions weaker than aisle; these have been introduced in
[
ATJLSS03
, §1]
.

Definition 1.3
.

A full subcategory
\mathcal{U}
is a
preaisle
of
\mathcal{T}
if
\mathcal{U}
is closed under positive
shifts and extensions, that is,
\mathcal{U}
satisfies (a1) and (a2). We call a preaisle
stable

if it is closed under negative shifts. A preaisle is a

thick preaisle
if it is closed under taking direct summands in
\mathcal{T}
. A preaisle
that is closed under taking small coproducts in
\mathcal{T}
is called a
cocomplete preaisle
.

A cocomplete preaisle is thick by the Eilenberg swindle argument;
see
[
ATJLSS03
, Corollary 1.4.]
. By
[
ATJLSS03
, Lemma 1.3.]
aisles are cocomplete preaisles.

1.2.
Compactly generated t-structures

A triangulated subcategory
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathcal{T}
is
thick
if it is closed
under direct summands. A thick subcategory is
localizing
if it is closed under taking
coproducts in
\mathcal{T}
. If
\mathcal{S}

\subset

\mathcal{T}
is a class of objects, then

\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle
denotes the smallest localizing subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
containing
\mathcal{S}
;
see for instance
[
Nee01
, Definition 1.12]
. Following similar customs, we denote the
smallest cocomplete preaisle containing
\mathcal{S}
by
\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle^{\leq 0}
and call it the
cocomplete
preaisle generated by
\mathcal{S}
.

An object
C\in

\mathcal{T}
is
compact
if for any collection of objects
\{A_{i}\}

in
\mathcal{T}
the natural map

\coprod_{i}\mathrm{Hom}(C,A_{i})\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(C,\coprod_{i}A_{i})

is an
isomorphism. We denote the full subcategory of compact objects of
\mathcal{T}
by
\mathcal{T}^{c}
;
the subcategory
\mathcal{T}^{c}
is a thick subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
. In contrast to our convention

\mathcal{T}^{c}
need not have coproducts.

Definition 1.4
.

A preaisle
\mathcal{U}
is
compactly generated
if
\mathcal{U}
=
\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle^{\leq 0}
for a set of compact
objects
\mathcal{S}
. We say an aisle is
compactly generated
if it is a compactly generated
preaisle. A t-structure is
compactly generated
if the aisle of the
t-structure is compactly generated.

We state a result of Keller and Nicolás.

Proposition 1.5

(
[
KN13
, Theorem A.7]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a set of compact objects of
\mathcal{T}
. Then,

(1)

\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle^{\leq 0}
is an aisle of
\mathcal{T}
.

(2)

Every object
A
of
\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle^{\leq 0}
fits in a triangle

\coprod_{i\geq 0}A_{i}\rightarrow A\rightarrow\coprod_{i\geq 0}A_{i}[1]\rightarrow\coprod_{i\geq 0}A_{i}[1]

where
A_{i}
is an
i
-fold extension of small coproducts of non negative shifts
of objects of
\mathcal{S}
.

Now we bring another auxiliary notion from
[
ATJLS10
, 1.1, page. 316]
which will be helpful in
characterizing compactly generated preaisles. Let
\mathcal{U}
be a preaisle of
\mathcal{T}
. We say
\mathcal{U}

is a
total
preaisle of
\mathcal{T}
if
{}^{\perp}(\mathcal{U}^{\perp})

=\mathcal{U}
. Next, we include some results which
are probably well known but have not appeared explicitly in the literature.

Lemma 1.6
.

Aisles are total preaisles.

Proof.

[
ATJLSS03
, Proposition 1.1 (i)]
.
∎

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a class of objects of
\mathcal{T}
. Consider the collection
of objects
\{S[n]\mid S\in\mathcal{S}\text{ and }n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\}
we denote it by

\mathcal{S}
[\mathbb{N}]
.

Lemma 1.7
.

The subcategory
{}^{\perp}(\mathcal{S}[\mathbb{N}]^{\perp})
is a cocomplete total preaisle of
\mathcal{T}

Proof.

For any collection of objects
\mathcal{Y}\subset\mathcal{T}
, we have
\mathcal{Y}\subset((^{\perp}\mathcal{Y})^{\perp})
, replacing
\mathcal{Y}
by
(\mathcal{Y}^{\perp})
we get
(\mathcal{Y}^{\perp})\subset((^{\perp}(\mathcal{Y}^{\perp}))^{\perp})
. Applying left
⟂
we get
{}^{\perp}((^{\perp}(\mathcal{Y}^{\perp}))^{\perp})\subset(^{\perp}(\mathcal{Y}^{\perp}))
.
Again for any collection
\mathcal{Y}
we have
\mathcal{Y}\subset

{}^{\perp}(\mathcal{Y}^{\perp})
,
replacing
\mathcal{Y}
by
(^{\perp}(\mathcal{Y}^{\perp}))
we get
(^{\perp}(\mathcal{Y}^{\perp}))\subset

{}^{\perp}((^{\perp}(\mathcal{Y}^{\perp}))^{\perp})
.
This proves

(^{\perp}(\mathcal{Y}^{\perp}))=

{}^{\perp}((^{\perp}(\mathcal{Y}^{\perp}))^{\perp}).

Replacing
\mathcal{Y}
by the class of objects
\mathcal{S}[\mathbb{N}]
we get

(^{\perp}(\mathcal{S}[\mathbb{N}]^{\perp}))=

{}^{\perp}((^{\perp}(\mathcal{S}[\mathbb{N}]^{\perp}))^{\perp}).
Also
note that for a class of objects
\mathcal{Y}
of
\mathcal{T}
, if
\mathcal{Y}[-1]\subset\mathcal{Y}
then

{}^{\perp}\mathcal{Y}
is a cocomplete preaisle. Since
\mathcal{S}[\mathbb{N}]^{\perp}[-1]\subset\mathcal{S}[\mathbb{N}]^{\perp}
therefore
{}^{\perp}(\mathcal{S}[\mathbb{N}]^{\perp})
is a cocomplete total preaisle of
\mathcal{T}
.

∎

Lemma 1.8
.

The subcategory
{}^{\perp}(\mathcal{S}[\mathbb{N}]^{\perp})
is the smallest total preaisle containing
\mathcal{S}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
1.7
, the subcategory
{}^{\perp}(\mathcal{S}[\mathbb{N}]^{\perp})
is a total preaisle.
Suppose
\mathcal{U}
is a total preaisle containing
\mathcal{S}
. As
\mathcal{U}
is closed under positive
shifts
\mathcal{S}[\mathbb{N}]\subset\mathcal{U}
, hence
{}^{\perp}(\mathcal{S}[\mathbb{N}]^{\perp})\subset

{}^{\perp}(\mathcal{U}^{\perp})=\mathcal{U}
. This proves the claim.
∎

Lemma 1.9
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a set of compact objects of
\mathcal{T}
. Then,

\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle^{\leq 0}={{}^{\perp}(\mathcal{S}[\mathbb{N}]^{\perp})}.

Proof.

Recall that
\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle^{\leq 0}
is the smallest cocomplete preaisle containing
\mathcal{S}
. Now

{}^{\perp}(\mathcal{S}[\mathbb{N}]^{\perp})
is a cocomplete preaisle by Lemma
1.7
, hence
\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle^{\leq 0}

\subset

{}^{\perp}(\mathcal{S}[\mathbb{N}]^{\perp})
. We know
\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle^{\leq 0}
is an aisle by Proposition
1.5
. Since aisles
are total preaisles by Lemma
1.6
,
\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle^{\leq 0}
is a total preaisle containing
\mathcal{S}
.
Lemma
1.8
says
{}^{\perp}(\mathcal{S}[\mathbb{N}]^{\perp})
is the smallest total preaisle containing
\mathcal{S}

therefore we get the reverse inclusion
{}^{\perp}(\mathcal{S}[\mathbb{N}]^{\perp})

\subset

\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle^{\leq 0}
.
∎

Proposition 1.10
.

Let
\mathcal{U}
be a cocomplete preaisle of
\mathcal{T}
.
The following are equivalent:

(1)

The cocomplete preaisle
\mathcal{U}
is compactly generated.

(2)

There is a set of compact objects
\mathcal{S}
such that
\mathcal{U}
=
\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle^{\leq 0}
.

(3)

There is a set of compact objects
\mathcal{S}
such that
\mathcal{U}
=
{}^{\perp}(\mathcal{S}[\mathbb{N}]^{\perp})
.

(4)

There is a set of compact objects
\mathcal{S}

\subset

\mathcal{U}
such that for any

A\in\mathcal{U}

if

\mathrm{Hom}(S,A)=0

for every
S\in\mathcal{S}[\mathbb{N}]
then
A\cong 0
.

(5)

The pair
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{U}^{\bot}[1])
is a compactly generated t-structure.

Proof.

(1)
\Leftrightarrow
(2) follows from Definition
1.4
.
Lemma
1.9
shows (2)
\Leftrightarrow
(3). Suppose
A\in\mathcal{U}
and
\mathrm{Hom}(S,A)=0
for all
S\in\mathcal{S}[\mathbb{N}]
, this means
A\in\mathcal{S}[\mathbb{N}]^{\perp}
. By Lemma

[
ATJLSS03
, Lemma 3.1]
we have

\mathcal{S}[\mathbb{N}]^{\perp}=\mathcal{U}^{\perp}
hence
A\in\mathcal{U}\cap\mathcal{U}^{\perp}
. So
we get
A\cong 0
; this proves (3)
\Rightarrow
(4).

Next, we prove (4)
\Rightarrow
(2). Note that
\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle^{\leq 0}

\subset

\mathcal{U}
and

\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle^{\leq 0}
is an aisle by Proposition
1.5
. Let
A\in\mathcal{U}
.
Consider the t-decomposition triangle of
A
for
\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle^{\leq 0}
,

\tau_{\mathcal{S}}^{\leq}A\rightarrow A\rightarrow\tau_{\mathcal{S}}^{>}A\rightarrow\tau_{\mathcal{S}}^{\leq}A[1].

As
\mathcal{U}
is closed under extension
\tau_{\mathcal{S}}^{>}A
is in
\mathcal{U}
. Since
\tau_{\mathcal{S}}^{>}A
is in
\mathcal{S}[\mathbb{N}]^{\perp}
by (4)
\tau_{\mathcal{S}}^{>}A\cong 0
. Therefore
the map
\tau_{\mathcal{S}}^{\leq}A\rightarrow A
is an isomorphism and hence
A\in\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle^{\leq 0}
.
This
proves
\mathcal{U}
=
\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle^{\leq 0}
.

A compactly generated preaisle
\mathcal{U}
is an aisle by Proposition
1.5
. By
the bijective correspondence between aisles and t-structures and by Definition

1.4
, we get (5)
\Leftrightarrow
(1).
∎

1.3.
Smashing t-structures

A thick subcategory
\mathcal{C}
is
Bousfield
if the inclusion
\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{T}
admits a
right adjoint; see
[
Kra10
, 4.9]
. Therefore Bousfield subcategories are precisely the stable
aisles. A Bousfield subcategory
\mathcal{C}
is
smashing
if the subcategory

\mathcal{C}^{\perp}
is localizing; for other characterizations see
[
Kra10
, Proposition 5.5.1]
.
In view of this we say an aisle
\mathcal{U}
is
smashing
if
\mathcal{U}^{\perp}
is closed
under coproducts; also see
[
SŠV17
, Definition 7.1]
. A t-structure
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}[1])
is
called
smashing
if the coaisle
\mathcal{V}
is closed under taking coproducts.

Proposition 1.11
.

Compactly generated t-structures are smashing t-structures.

Proof.

Suppose
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}[1])
is a compactly generated t-structure and
\mathcal{U}=

{}^{\perp}(\mathcal{S}[\mathbb{N}]^{\perp})
.
Let
\{A_{i}\}
be a collection of objects in
\mathcal{V}
. To show
\coprod_{i}A_{i}\in\mathcal{V}
it is enough to show
\mathrm{Hom}(S,\coprod_{i}A_{i})=0
for all
S\in\mathcal{S}[\mathbb{N}]
. Since

S\in\mathcal{S}[\mathbb{N}]
is compact we have
\mathrm{Hom}(S,\coprod_{i}A_{i})=\coprod_{i}\mathrm{Hom}(S,A_{i}).

As
A_{i}\in\mathcal{V}
we have
\mathrm{Hom}(S,A_{i})=0
and this proves the claim.
∎

2.
Tensor t-structures

We recall the definition of tensor triangulated category from
[
HPS97
, Definition A.2.1]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
tensor triangulated category

(\mathcal{T},\otimes,\mathbf{1})
is a triangulated
category with a compatible closed symmetric monoidal structure. This means there is a
functor
-\otimes-\colon\mathcal{T}\times\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}
which is triangulated in both the
variables and satisfies certain compatibility conditions. Moreover, for each
B\in\mathcal{T}
the
functor
-\otimes B
has a right adjoint which we denote by
\mathscr{H}om(B,-)
. The
functor
\mathscr{H}om(-,-)
is triangulated in both the variables, and for any
A
,
B
,
and
C
in
\mathcal{T}
we have natural isomorphisms
\mathrm{Hom}(A\otimes B,C)\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(A,\mathscr{H}om(B,C))
.

Suppose
\mathcal{T}
is given with a preaisle
\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}
such that
\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}\otimes\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}\subset\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}
and
\mathbf{1}\in\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}
. We introduce the following definition which is motivated from
[
ATJLSS03
, §5]
; a similar situation is also considered in
[
Nee18
, §3]
.

Definition 2.2
.

A preaisle
\mathcal{U}
of
\mathcal{T}
is a
\otimes
-preaisle
(with respect to
\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}
) if
\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}\otimes\mathcal{U}\subset\mathcal{U}
. An aisle is called a
\otimes
-aisle if it is a
\otimes
-preaisle. A t-structure is
a
tensor t-structure
if the aisle of the t-structure is a
\otimes
-aisle.

Remark 2.3
.

Though we define tensor t-structures in this generality, for the derived categories (example

\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)
) we always take tensor t-structures with respect to the aisle of the
standard t-structure.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}[1])
be a t-structure on
\mathcal{T}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}[1])
is a tensor t-structure.

(2)

\mathcal{U}
is a
\otimes
-aisle.

(3)

\mathscr{H}om(\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{V})\subset\mathcal{V}
.

Proof.

(1)
\Leftrightarrow
(2) is by definition. Let
A\in\mathcal{U}
,
B\in\mathcal{V}
, and
X\in\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}
. Then from the adjunction isomorphism we have

\mathrm{Hom}(A\otimes X,B)\cong\mathrm{Hom}(A,\mathscr{H}om(X,B)).

This proves (2)
\Leftrightarrow
(3).

∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{\mathcal{T}}^{\leq 0}
be generated by a set of objects
\mathcal{K}
, that is,
\mathcal{\mathcal{T}}^{\leq 0}
=

\langle\mathcal{\mathcal{K}}\rangle^{\leq 0}
. Then a preaisle
\mathcal{U}
of
\mathcal{T}
is a
\otimes
-preaisle if and only if

\mathcal{K}

\otimes

\mathcal{U}

\subset\mathcal{U}
.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathcal{K}

\otimes

\mathcal{U}

\subset\mathcal{U}
. We define
\mathcal{B}
=
\{X\in\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}\mid X\otimes\mathcal{U}\subset\mathcal{U}\}
. If
X\in\mathcal{B}
then
X[1]\in\mathcal{B}
, as
\mathcal{U}
is closed under positive shifts. Suppose we
have a triangle
X\rightarrow Y\rightarrow Z\rightarrow X[1]
with
X
and
Z
in
\mathcal{B}
. Since
\mathcal{U}
is closed
under extension, we get
Y\in\mathcal{B}
.
This proves
\mathcal{B}
is a preaisle. Again cocompleteness of
\mathcal{U}
implies

\mathcal{B}
is cocomplete. Since
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{B}
we get

\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}\subset\mathcal{B}
which proves
\mathcal{U}
is
\otimes
-preaisle. The converse follows
easily.
∎

Proposition 2.6
.

If
\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}=\langle\mathbf{1}\rangle^{\leq 0}
then every cocomplete preaisle of
\mathcal{T}
is
a
\otimes
-preaisle. In particular, all t-structures are tensor t-structures.

Proof.

Follows from Lemma
2.5
.
∎

We recall the definitions of
\otimes
-ideal
and
coideal
; see for instance

[
HPS97
, Definition 1.4.3]
.

Definition 2.7
.

A thick subcategory
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathcal{T}
is a
\otimes
-ideal of
\mathcal{T}
if
\mathcal{T}\otimes\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{C}
. We say
\mathcal{C}
is a
coideal
of
\mathcal{T}

if
\mathscr{H}om(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{C})\subset\mathcal{C}
.

Recall that a preaisle is called stable if it is closed under negative shifts. We say a t-structure is

stable
if the aisle is closed under negative shifts. Note that for a stable t-structure the
coaisle is closed under positive shifts.

Lemma 2.8
.

Suppose
\langle\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}\rangle=\mathcal{T}
. If a cocomplete
\otimes
-preaisle is stable, then it
is a
\otimes
-ideal of
\mathcal{T}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{U}
be a stable cocomplete
\otimes
-preaisle of
\mathcal{T}
. We define
\mathcal{B}
=
\{X\in\mathcal{T}\mid X\otimes\mathcal{U}\subset\mathcal{U}\}
. Using similar arguments as in Lemma
2.5
, we can observe that
\mathcal{B}
is a localizing subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
. Since

\mathcal{B}

contains

\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}
we get
\langle\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}\rangle=\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{B}
. This proves
\mathcal{U}
is a

\otimes
-ideal.
∎

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a tensor triangulated category with a preaisle
\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}
such that

\langle\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}\rangle=\mathcal{T}
. If
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}[1])
is a stable t-structure on

\mathcal{T}
, then the following are equivalent:

(1)

(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}[1])
is a stable tensor t-structure on
\mathcal{T}
.

(2)

The aisle
\mathcal{U}
is a
\otimes
-ideal of
\mathcal{T}
.

(3)

The coaisle
\mathcal{V}
is a coideal of
\mathcal{T}
.

Proof.

(1)
\Leftrightarrow
(2) follows from Lemma
2.8
. (2)
\Leftrightarrow
(3)
follows from the adjunction isomorphism - same as the proof of
Proposition
2.4
.
∎

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a tensor triangulated category and
\mathcal{S}
be a class of objects of
\mathcal{T}
. We
denote the smallest cocomplete
\otimes
-preaisle containing
\mathcal{S}
by
\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle^{\leq 0}_{\otimes}
. The
following result is a version of
[
HR17
, Lemma 1.1]
in the present context.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
be two tensor triangulated categories with preaisles

\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}
and
\mathcal{{T}^{\prime}}^{\leq 0}
respectively.
Let
F:\mathcal{T}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
be a coproduct preserving tensor triangulated functor.
We assume
F
to be right-t-exact, that is,
F(\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0})\subset\mathcal{T}^{\prime\leq 0}
.
Then the following hold:

(1)

If
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}
is a cocomplete
\otimes
-preaisle of
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
,
then the full subcategory
F^{-1}(\mathcal{U}^{\prime})
of
\mathcal{T}
is a cocomplete

\otimes
-preaisle.

(2)

If
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{T}
is a class of objects, then
\langle F(\mathcal{S})\rangle^{\leq 0}_{\otimes}=\langle F(\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle^{\leq 0}_{\otimes})\rangle^{\leq 0}_{\otimes}.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}
be a cocomplete preaisle of
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
. If
A\in F^{-1}(\mathcal{U}^{\prime})
, then
F(A)\in\mathcal{U}^{\prime}
so
F(A)[1]\in\mathcal{U}^{\prime}
and this implies
A[1]\in F^{-1}(\mathcal{U}^{\prime})
. Given a triangle
A\rightarrow B\rightarrow C\rightarrow A[1]
in
\mathcal{T}
with
A
and
C
in
F^{-1}(\mathcal{U}^{\prime})
. We have
F(A)\rightarrow F(B)\rightarrow F(C)\rightarrow F(A)[1]
in
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
with
F(A)
and
F(C)
in
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}
hence
F(B)\in\mathcal{U}^{\prime}
, and we get
B\in F^{-1}(\mathcal{U}^{\prime})
. This proves
F^{-1}(\mathcal{U}^{\prime})
is a preaisle of
\mathcal{T}
. The fact
F
preserves coproducts will imply
that if
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}
is cocomplete then
F^{-1}(\mathcal{U}^{\prime})
is cocomplete. Let

\mathcal{U}^{\prime}
be a cocomplete
\otimes
-preaisle of
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
. For any
A\in F^{-1}(\mathcal{U}^{\prime})
and
B\in\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}
, we have
F(A\otimes B)=F(A)\otimes F(B)\in\mathcal{U}^{\prime}
and hence
A\otimes B\in F^{-1}(\mathcal{U}^{\prime})
. This
shows
F^{-1}(\mathcal{U}^{\prime})
is a cocomplete
\otimes
-preaisle.

Now part (2). For any class of objects
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{T}
, we have

\langle F(\mathcal{S})\rangle^{\leq 0}_{\otimes}\subset\langle F(\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle^{\leq 0}_{\otimes})\rangle^{\leq 0}_{\otimes}
. By (1),
F^{-1}(\langle F(\mathcal{S})\rangle^{\leq 0}_{\otimes})
is a cocomplete
\otimes
-preaisle. Since
\mathcal{S}\subset F^{-1}(\langle F(\mathcal{S})\rangle^{\leq 0}_{\otimes})
we have
\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle^{\leq 0}_{\otimes}\subset F^{-1}(\langle F(\mathcal{S})\rangle^{\leq 0}_{\otimes})
. This implies
F(\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle^{\leq 0}_{\otimes})\subset\langle F(\mathcal{S})\rangle^{\leq 0}_{\otimes}
. Therefore,
\langle F(\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle^{\leq 0}_{\otimes})\rangle^{\leq 0}_{\otimes}\subset\langle F(\mathcal{S})\rangle^{\leq 0}_{\otimes}
.

∎

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a rigidly compactly generated tensor triangulated category in the sense of
Balmer
[
BF11
, 1.1]
. In particular,
\mathbf{1}\in\mathcal{T}^{c}
and the tensor product

\otimes
restricts to
\mathcal{T}^{c}
. In this case, we can prove a tensor triangulated analogue of
Proposition
1.5
.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a rigidly compactly generated tensor triangulated category with a preaisle

\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}
.
Suppose
\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}
is compactly generated and
\mathcal{T}^{c}\cap\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}=\mathcal{K}
.
Let
\mathcal{S}
be a set of compact objects of
\mathcal{T}
. Then,

(1)

\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle^{\leq 0}_{\otimes}
is an aisle of
\mathcal{T}
.

(2)

Every object
A
of
\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle^{\leq 0}_{\otimes}
fits in a triangle

\coprod_{i\geq 0}A_{i}\rightarrow A\rightarrow\coprod_{i\geq 0}A_{i}[1]\rightarrow\coprod_{i\geq 0}A_{i}[1]

where
A_{i}
is an
i
-fold extension of small coproducts of non negative shifts
of objects of
\mathcal{K}\otimes\mathcal{S}
.

Proof.

If we show

\langle\mathcal{K}\otimes\mathcal{S}\rangle^{\leq 0}=\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle^{\leq 0}_{\otimes}

then
by Proposition
1.5
, our claim follows. Since
\mathcal{K}\otimes\mathcal{S}\subset\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle^{\leq 0}_{\otimes}
, we have
\langle\mathcal{K}\otimes\mathcal{S}\rangle^{\leq 0}\subset\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle^{\leq 0}_{\otimes}
.

For convenience, we denote
\langle\mathcal{K}\otimes\mathcal{S}\rangle^{\leq 0}
by
\mathcal{U}
.
Let
\mathcal{A}
=
\{X\in\mathcal{U}\mid\mathcal{K}\otimes X\subset\mathcal{U}\}
.
As in the proof of Lemma
2.5
, it can be checked that
\mathcal{A}
is
a cocomplete preaisle. Since
\mathcal{K}\otimes\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{K}
we have

\mathcal{K}\otimes\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{A}
, and this proves
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{U}
. Which means

\mathcal{K}\otimes\mathcal{U}\subset\mathcal{U}
now by Lemma
2.5
we get

\mathcal{U}
is a tensor preaisle. Since
\mathbf{1}\in\mathcal{K}
we have
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{U}
,
this shows
\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle^{\leq 0}_{\otimes}\subset\mathcal{U}
.

∎

3.
A class of tensor t-structures in
\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)

Let
X
be a quasi-compact quasi-separated scheme. We denote the derived category of
complexes of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-modules with quasi-coherent cohomology by
\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)
.
The derived category
(\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X),\otimes^{L}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}},{\mathcal{O}}_{X})
is a tensor triangulated
category with the derived tensor product
\otimes^{L}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}
and the structure sheaf

{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
as the unit. The full subcategory of complexes whose cohomologies vanish in
positive degree
\mathbf{D}_{qc}^{\leq 0}(X)
is a preaisle of
\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)
.

We define the
\otimes
-preaisles of
\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)
with respect to the standard preaisle

\mathbf{D}_{qc}^{\leq 0}(X)
.

In the affine situation when
X=\mathrm{Spec}R
, note that
R
generates the preaisle

\mathbf{D}^{\leq 0}(R)
. Hence by Proposition
2.6
every cocomplete
preaisle of
\mathbf{D}(R)
is a
\otimes
-preaisle. This has been well known; see for instance

[
ATJLS10
, Proposition 1.10]
also
[
Hrb20
, Proposition 2.2]
.

The following is a characterization of
\otimes
-preaisles in
\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)
. For separated schemes
with an ample family of line bundles (a.k.a. divisorial), see
[
ATJLSS03
, Proposition 5.1]
for a
stronger result; note that what we call
\otimes
-aisle has been termed as
0-rigid
in

[
ATJLSS03
, §5]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{U}
be a cocomplete preaisle of
\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

The preaisle
\mathcal{U}
is a
\otimes
-preaisle.

(2)

For every
G\in\mathcal{U}
and
F\in\mathbf{D}_{qc}^{\leq 0}(X)
we have

F\otimes^{L}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}G\in\mathcal{U}
.

(3)

For every
G\in\mathcal{U}
and
F
a quasi-coherent
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module, we have

F\otimes^{L}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}G\in\mathcal{U}
.

(4)

For every
G\in\mathcal{U}
and
F
a flat quasi-coherent
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module, we have
F\otimes^{L}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}G\in\mathcal{U}
.

Proof.

(1)
\Leftrightarrow
(2) follows from the definition. The implications (2)
\Rightarrow
(3)
\Rightarrow
(4) are obvious. We prove (4)
\Rightarrow
(2). Let
F\in\mathbf{D}_{qc}^{\leq 0}(X)
, we can replace
F
by a K-flat resolution say
E
, such that

E
is in
\mathbf{D}_{qc}^{\leq 0}(X)
and each component
E^{i}
is a flat
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-
module
[
LH09
, Proposition 2.5.5]
. For each integer
n
we denote
\sigma^{\geq n}E
the brutal truncation of
E
from below. Note that
E
is the colimit of the directed
system
\{\sigma^{\geq n}E\}
with the obvious maps.

For any
G\in\mathcal{U}
, to show
E\otimes^{L}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}G\in\mathcal{U}
it is
enough to show for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\sigma^{\geq-n}E\otimes^{L}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}G\in\mathcal{U}
since
\mathcal{U}
is cocomplete. We prove by induction on
n
. We have the following
distinguished triangle for each
n
,

\sigma^{\geq-(n-1)}E\rightarrow\sigma^{\geq-n}E\rightarrow E^{-n}[n]\rightarrow\sigma^{\geq-(n-1)}E[1].

Here
E^{-n}[n]
is the complex which is zero everywhere except at
-n
th entry, where it is
precisely
E^{-n}
: the
-n
th component of the complex E. As
E^{-n}
is a
flat
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module and
\mathcal{U}
is closed under positive shifts, by (4) we have
E^{-n}[n]\otimes^{L}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}G\in\mathcal{U}
. Now by induction hypothesis we assume
\sigma^{\geq-(n-1)}E\otimes^{L}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}G\in\mathcal{U}
, this proves the claim since
\mathcal{U}
is closed
under extensions.

∎

3.1.
Filtrations of supports and associated subcategories

Let
E
be a complex in
\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)
. The
cohomological support
of
E

[
Tho97
, Definition 3.2]
is defined to be the subspace

\mathrm{Supph}(E)=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{Supp}(H^{n}(E)).

We introduce an auxiliary notation,

\mathrm{Supph}^{\geq i}(E)=\bigcup_{j\geq i}\mathrm{Supp}(H^{j}(E)).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
A\rightarrow B\rightarrow C\rightarrow A[1]
be a distinguished triangle in
\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)
. Then,

\mathrm{Supph}^{\geq i}(B)\subset\mathrm{Supph}^{\geq i}(A)\bigcup\mathrm{Supph}^{\geq i}(C).

Proof.

From the long exact cohomology sequence we have

\mathrm{Supp}(H^{j}(B))\subset\mathrm{Supp}(H^{j}(A))\bigcup\mathrm{Supp}(H^{j}(C)).

Now taking union over
j\geq i
the result follows.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

For any
E\in\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)
, we have

\mathrm{Supph}^{\geq i}(E[1])\subset\mathrm{Supph}^{\geq i}(E).

Proof.

Note that
\mathrm{Supph}^{\geq i}(E[1])=\mathrm{Supph}^{\geq i+1}(E)
. And

\mathrm{Supph}^{\geq i+1}(E)\subset\mathrm{Supph}^{\geq i}(E)
follows from the definition of
\mathrm{Supph}^{\geq i}(-)
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\{E_{\alpha}\}
be a set of objects in
\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)
. Then,

\mathrm{Supph}^{\geq i}(\oplus_{\alpha}E_{\alpha})=\bigcup_{\alpha}\mathrm{Supph}^{\geq i}(E_{\alpha}).

Proof.

It is enough to show
\mathrm{Supp}(H^{i}(\oplus_{\alpha}E_{\alpha}))=\bigcup_{\alpha}\mathrm{Supp}(H^{i}(E_{\alpha}))
. As cohomology commutes with direct sums we have
H^{i}(\oplus_{\alpha}E_{\alpha})\cong\oplus_{\alpha}H^{i}(E_{\alpha})
so

\mathrm{Supp}(H^{i}(\oplus_{\alpha}E_{\alpha}))=\mathrm{Supp}(\oplus_{\alpha}H^{i}(E_{\alpha}))=\bigcup_{\alpha}\mathrm{Supp}(H^{i}(E_{\alpha})).

∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
B\in\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)
. For any flat quasi-coherent
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module
F
and
n\geq 0
, we
have

\mathrm{Supph}^{\geq i}(F[n]\otimes^{\textbf{L}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}B)\subset\mathrm{Supph}^{\geq i}(B).

Proof.

First we prove for
n=0
. Since
F
is a flat
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module we have
F\otimes^{\textbf{L}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}B=F\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}B
. The flatness of
F
implies
H^{i}(F\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}B)\cong F\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}H^{i}(B)
. Now
\mathrm{Supp}(F\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}H^{i}(B))\subset\mathrm{Supp}(H^{i}(B)),
hence we get

\mathrm{Supp}(H^{i}(F\otimes^{\textbf{L}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}B))\subset\mathrm{Supp}(H^{i}(B)).

And this
proves

\mathrm{Supph}^{\geq i}(F\otimes^{\textbf{L}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}B)\subset\mathrm{Supph}^{\geq i}(B).

Next for
n>1
. By Lemma
3.3
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Supph}^{\geq i}(F[n]\otimes^{\textbf{L}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}B)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Supph}^{\geq i}((F\otimes^{\textbf{L}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}B)[n])

\displaystyle\subset\mathrm{Supph}^{\geq i}(F\otimes^{\textbf{L}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}B).

Now, applying
n=0
case we get

\mathrm{Supph}^{\geq i}(F[n]\otimes^{\textbf{L}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}B)\subset\mathrm{Supph}^{\geq i}(B).

∎

Definition 3.6
.

A
filtration of supports of
X
is a function
\phi
from
\mathbb{Z}
with values in the set of
subsets of
X
such that
\phi(i+1)\subset\phi(i)
for each
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Let
\phi
be a filtration of supports of
X
. The subcategory of
deriveX
associated to

\phi
and denoted by
\mathcal{U}_{\phi}

is the full subcategory containing objects
E
such that
\mathrm{Supp}(H^{i}(E))\subset\phi(i)
for
each
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\phi
be a filtration of supports of
X
and
\mathcal{U}_{\phi}
be the associated
subcategory of
\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)
. Then
\mathcal{U}_{\phi}
is a cocomplete
\otimes
-preaisle of
\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)
.

Proof.

Since
\phi
is a decreasing function
E\in\mathcal{U}_{\phi}
if and only if
\mathrm{Supph}^{\geq i}(E)\subset\phi(i)
for each
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. By Lemma
3.3
if
E\in\mathcal{U}_{\phi}
,
then
E[1]\in\mathcal{U}_{\phi}
. Using Lemma

3.2
we can observe that
\mathcal{U}_{\phi}
is closed under extensions, and
Lemma
3.4
implies
\mathcal{U}_{\phi}
is cocomplete. Now to show

\mathcal{U}_{\phi}
is a
\otimes
-preaisle, by Lemma
3.1
, it is
enough to show for any flat
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module
F
and any

B\in\mathcal{U}_{\phi}
we must have
F\otimes^{L}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}B\in\mathcal{U}_{\phi}
. This
follows from
n=0
case of Lemma
3.5
.
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
B

\in\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)
, and
E
be a perfect complex in
\mathbf{D}_{qc}^{\leq 0}(X)
.
Then,

\mathrm{Supph}^{\geq i}(E\otimes^{\textbf{L}}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}B)\subset\mathrm{Supph}^{\geq i}(B).

Proof.

First, we observe that the brutal truncation of a perfect complex is perfect. For each positive
integer
n
, we have the triangle coming from brutal
truncation,

\sigma^{\geq-(n-1)}E\rightarrow\sigma^{\geq-n}E\rightarrow E^{-n}[n]\rightarrow\sigma^{\geq-(n-1)}E[1].

The
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module
E^{-n}
is flat. Tensoring with
B
we get the triangle,

\sigma^{\geq-(n-1)}E\otimes^{\textbf{L}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}B\rightarrow\sigma^{\geq-n}E\otimes^{\textbf{L}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}B\rightarrow E^{-n}[n]\otimes^{\textbf{L}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}B\rightarrow\sigma^{\geq-(n-1)}E[1]\otimes^{\textbf{L}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}B.

As
E
is perfect and
X
is quasi-compact there is an
N
such that

\sigma^{\geq-N}E=E
.
In view of Lemma
3.2
and by induction on the length of
E
, it is now
enough to show for any flat
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module
F
and positive integer
n
,

\mathrm{Supph}^{\geq i}(F[n]\otimes^{\textbf{L}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}B)\subset\mathrm{Supph}^{\geq i}(B)
;
which has been shown in
3.5
. This proves the claim.

∎

3.2.
Extending perfect complexes along a tensor preaisle

Let
X
be a quasi-compact quasi-separated scheme with an ample family of line bundles. Let

U\subset X
be a quasi-compact open subset and
Z
be a closed subset of
X
with

X\setminus Z
quasi-compact.

The following proposition is an improvement of
[
TT90
, Proposition 5.4.2]
, we remove
both the Noetherian hypothesis and the boundedness assumption on
F
.
Also we observe that the map can be extended along a
\otimes
-preaisle.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let

since
\mathcal{U}
is a tensor preaisle,
K\otimes E\in\mathcal{U}
and we get a contradiction.

∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}[1])
be a tensor t-structure on
\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)
. Then

(\mathcal{U}|_{U},\mathcal{V}|_{U}[1])

is
a t-structure on
\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(U)
. In particular, for any
A\in\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)
the triangle

j^{*}\tau_{\mathcal{U}}^{\leq}A\rightarrow j^{*}A\rightarrow j^{*}\tau_{\mathcal{U}}^{>}A\rightarrow j^{*}\tau_{\mathcal{U}}^{\leq}A[1],

is a t-decomposition triangle of
j^{*}A
in
\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(U)
.

Proof.

To show
(\mathcal{U}|_{U},\mathcal{V}|_{U}[1])
is a t-structure
it is enough to get a t-decomposition triangle for each
B\in\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(U)
.
For any
B\in\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(U)
, take
j_{*}B
and its t-decomposition
triangle for
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}[1])

\tau^{\leq}_{\mathcal{U}}j_{*}B\rightarrow j_{*}B\rightarrow\tau^{>}_{\mathcal{U}}j_{*}B\rightarrow\tau^{\leq}_{\mathcal{U}}j_{*}B[1].

Applying
j^{*}
we get a triangle in
\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(U)
. Since
j^{*}j_{*}B\cong B
we get

j^{*}\tau^{\leq}_{\mathcal{U}}j_{*}B\rightarrow B\rightarrow j^{*}\tau^{>}_{\mathcal{U}}j_{*}B\rightarrow j^{*}\tau^{\leq}_{\mathcal{U}}j_{*}B[1].

By Lemma
4.5

j^{*}\tau^{>}_{\mathcal{U}}j_{*}B\in\mathcal{V}|_{U}
and by definition of
\mathcal{U}|_{U}

we have
j^{*}\tau^{\leq}_{\mathcal{U}}j_{*}B\in\mathcal{U}|_{U}
, hence the above triangle gives a
t-decomposition of
B
for
(\mathcal{U}|_{U},\mathcal{V}|_{U}[1])
.

Now for any
A\in\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)
applying
j^{*}
and the argument as above, we get the
second half of the claim.
∎

Let
R

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
A
and
B
be
two perfect complexes on
X
. If

\mathrm{Supph}^{\geq i}(A)\subset\mathrm{Supph}^{\geq i}(B)
for each
i
, then
A\in\langle B\rangle^{\leq 0}_{\otimes}
.

Proof.

Consider the t-decomposition of
A
with respect to the aisle
\langle B\rangle^{\leq 0}_{\otimes}

\tau_{B}^{\leq}A\rightarrow A\rightarrow\tau_{B}^{>}A\rightarrow\tau_{B}^{\leq}A[1].

Let
U
be an open affine subset of
X
, and
j:U\rightarrow X
be the open immersion. Applying

j^{*}
to the above triangle we get

j^{*}\tau_{B}^{\leq}A\rightarrow j^{*}A\rightarrow j^{*}\tau_{B}^{>}A\rightarrow j^{*}\tau_{B}^{\leq}A[1].

Now using Lemma
4.6
and the Lemma
4.8

we conclude the map
j^{*}\tau_{B}^{\leq}A\rightarrow j^{*}A
is an isomorphism. As the map

\tau_{B}^{\leq}A\rightarrow A
is locally an isomorphism on every open affine subset of
X
, it is an isomorphism.
This proves
A\in\langle B\rangle^{\leq 0}_{\otimes}
.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
Z
be a closed subset of
X
. Then there is a perfect complex

E\in\mathrm{Perf}^{\leq 0}_{Z}(X)
such that
\mathrm{Supp}(H^{0}(E))=Z
.

Proof.

Consider an open affine cover of
X
say

\{U_{\alpha}=\mathrm{Spec}R_{\alpha}\}
. We take a Koszul complex
K_{\alpha}

on
R_{\alpha}
such that
\mathrm{Supph}(K_{\alpha})=Z\cap U_{\alpha}
. By Proposition

3.14
there is an extension of
\oplus_{i}(K_{\alpha}[i])^{\oplus\binom{n}{i}}
;
say
E_{\alpha}
. Now take
E=\bigoplus_{\alpha}E_{\alpha}
, it is easy to check

\mathrm{Supp}(H^{0}(E))=Z
, and by our construction
E\in\mathrm{Perf}^{\leq 0}_{Z}(X)
.
∎

We denote the collection of Thomason filtrations of
X
by
{\sf{Thom}^{fil}}(X)
and the collection
of compactly generated
\otimes
-aisles of
\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)
by
{\sf{Aisle^{cp}_{\otimes}}}(X)
.

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
X
be a Noetherian scheme. There is a one-to-one
correspondence between
{\sf{Thom}^{fil}}(X)
and
{\sf{Aisle^{cp}_{\otimes}}}(X)
. More precisely, the maps

\Phi:{\sf{Aisle^{cp}_{\otimes}}}(X)\rightarrow{\sf{Thom}^{fil}}(X),

\mathcal{U}\mapsto\varphi_{\mathcal{U}},\hskip 14.22636pt(\ref{Phi U}),

\Psi:{\sf{Thom}^{fil}}(X)\to{\sf{Aisle^{cp}_{\otimes}}}(X),

\phi\mapsto\mathcal{U}_{\phi},\hskip 14.22636pt(\ref{U phi}),

are inverse of each other.

Proof.

The maps are well defined by Lemma
4.3
and Theorem

3.20
.
First, we show
\Phi\circ\Psi=
Id.
Given
\phi\in{\sf{Thom}^{fil}}(X)
we have
\phi(i)=\bigcup_{\lambda}Z_{\lambda}
where

Z_{\lambda}
’s are closed subsets of
X
. For a fixed
Z_{\lambda}
by Lemma

4.10

we have a perfect complex
E_{\lambda}
and
E_{\lambda}[i]\in\Psi(\phi)
, this
proves the claim.

To show
\Psi\circ\Phi
= Id, we first observe
\mathcal{U}\subset\Psi\circ\Phi(\mathcal{U})
.
For the reverse inclusion, we will show that any compact object
A\in\Psi\circ\Phi(\mathcal{U})

is in
\mathcal{U}
. By Lemma
4.2
we have compact objects
\{B_{\alpha}\}\subset\mathcal{U}
such
that
\mathrm{Supph}^{\geq i}(A)\subset\bigcup_{\alpha}\mathrm{Supph}^{\geq i}(B_{\alpha})
.

Since
A
is perfect,
\mathrm{Supph}^{\geq i}(A)
is a closed subset of
X
. As
X
is Noetherian
there are finitely many irreducible components of
\mathrm{Supph}^{\geq i}(A)
say

Z_{1}
,
Z_{2}
,
\cdots,Z_{n}
. Now, we take
B_{i}
such that the generating point of

Z_{i}
lies in
\mathrm{Supph}^{\geq i}(B_{i})
. Therefore,
we have a finite collection
\{B_{i}\}^{n}_{i=1}
of
\{B_{\alpha}\}
such that

\mathrm{Supph}^{\geq i}(A)\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{Supph}^{\geq i}(B_{i})
. So

\mathrm{Supph}^{\geq i}(A)\subset\mathrm{Supph}^{\geq i}(\oplus_{i=1}^{n}B_{i})
, since

\oplus_{i=1}^{n}B_{i}\in\mathcal{U}
, by
Lemma
4.9
we conclude
A\in\mathcal{U}
. This proves the theorem.

∎

5.
Tensor telescope conjecture for t-structures

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a Grothendieck abelian category. A subcategory
\mathcal{V}
of
\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{G})
is

closed under homotopy colimits
if for any directed system
\{A_{i}\}
in

\mathbf{C}(\mathcal{G})
with all
A_{i}
in
\mathcal{V}
, the colimit of the directed system
\{A_{i}\}
in

\mathbf{C}(\mathcal{G})
belongs to
\mathcal{V}
; see
[
HN21
, Fact 2.1]
, for the definition in the
general setting of derivators see
[
HN21
, A.1]
. A t-structure
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}[1])
on

\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{G})
is
homotopically smashing
if the coaisle
\mathcal{V}
is closed under
homotopy colimits. Recall that we say a t-structure is smashing if the coaisle is closed under
coproducts.

Proposition 5.1
.

Every homotopically smashing t-structure on
\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{G})
is smashing.

Proof.

[
SŠV17
, Proposition 7.2]
.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

Smashing t-structures in general are not homotopically smashing see
[
SŠV17
, Example 8.2]
. However, in the case of stable t-structures these two notions coincide; see

[
HN21
, A.5]
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Every compactly generated t-structure on
\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{G})
is homotopically smashing.

Proof.

[
SŠV17
, Proposition 7.2]
.
∎

The telescope conjecture for t-structures asks if every homotopically smashing t-structure on

\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{G})
is
compactly generated, that is, if the converse of Proposition
5.3
is true. When

\mathcal{G}
is
\mathrm{Mod}
-
R
for
R
Noetherian ring, Hrbek and Nakamura have proved the
following:

Theorem 5.4

(
[
HN21
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

Any homotopically smashing t-structure on
\mathbf{D}(R)
is
compactly generated.

For a separated Noetherian scheme

X
and the derived category of quasi-coherent sheaves
\mathbf{D}(\mathrm{Qcoh}(X))
,
which is equivalent to
\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)
, we prove
the following:

Theorem 5.5
.

Any homotopically smashing tensor t-structure on
\mathbf{D}(\mathrm{Qcoh}(X))
is
compactly generated.

First, we prove some lemmas. In this section
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}[1])
will always mean a
homotopically smashing tensor t-structure on
\mathbf{D}(\mathrm{Qcoh}(X))
and

U
is an open affine subset of
X
.

Lemma 5.6
.

The restriction of
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}[1])
to
U
, that is,
(\mathcal{U}|_{U},\mathcal{V}|_{U}[1])
is a
homotopically smashing t-structure on
\mathbf{D}(\mathrm{Qcoh}(U))
.

Proof.

Recall
\mathcal{U}|_{U}=\langle j^{*}\mathcal{U}\rangle^{\leq 0}
and

\mathcal{V}|_{U}=(\mathcal{U}|_{U})^{\perp}
.
By Lemma
4.6

(\mathcal{U}|_{U},\mathcal{V}|_{U}[1])
is a t-structure on

\mathbf{D}(\mathrm{Qcoh}(U))
. Now we will show
(\mathcal{U}|_{U},\mathcal{V}|_{U}[1])
is
homotopically smashing. Let
\{A_{i}\}
be a directed system in
\mathcal{V}|_{U}
. By adjunction
isomorphism, if
A_{i}\in\mathcal{V}|_{U}
then
j_{*}A_{i}\in\mathcal{V}
. Consider the directed system

\{j_{*}A_{i}\}
in
\mathcal{V}
, since

\mathcal{V}
is closed under homotopy colimits we have
\varinjlim j_{*}A_{i}\in\mathcal{V}
. Since

j^{*}
is an exact functor and
j^{*}j_{*}A_{i}\cong A_{i}
the colimit of the system
\{A_{i}\}
is

j^{*}(\varinjlim j_{*}A_{i})
which by Lemma
4.5
is in

\mathcal{V}|_{U}
.

∎

Lemma 5.7
.

If the graded support of
\mathcal{U}
is
\phi
, then the graded support of
\mathcal{U}|_{U}

is
\phi|_{U}
.

Proof.

Consider
\mathcal{U}_{(\phi|_{U})}
the associated subcategory of the filtration
\phi|_{U}
in

\mathbf{D}(\mathrm{Qcoh}(U))
, it is a cocomplete preaisle by Proposition
3.7
.
We have
j^{*}A\in\mathcal{U}_{(\phi|_{U})}
for any
A\in\mathcal{U}
therefore

\mathcal{U}|_{U}=\langle j^{*}\mathcal{U}\rangle^{\leq 0}\subset\mathcal{U}_{(\phi|_{U})}
. So the graded
support of
\mathcal{U}|_{U}
is contained in
\phi|_{U}
. Next, given
x\in\phi|_{U}(i)
, we have

x\in\phi(i)
which means there is an object
E
in
\mathcal{U}
such that
x\in\mathrm{Supp}(H^{i}(E))
.
As
j^{*}E\in\mathcal{U}|_{U}
we get the graded support of
\mathcal{U}|_{U}
is
\phi|_{U}
.
∎

Lemma 5.8
.

If the graded support of
\mathcal{U}
is
\phi
, then

\mathcal{U}|_{U}=\mathcal{U}_{(\phi|_{U})}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.6
we know
\mathcal{U}|_{U}
is homotopically
smashing. As
U
is affine, Theorem
5.4
implies
\mathcal{U}|_{U}
is compactly
generated. By Lemma
5.7
the graded support of
\mathcal{U}|_{U}
is
\phi|_{U}
. By the classification of compactly generated t-structures, we conclude

\mathcal{U}|_{U}=\mathcal{U}_{(\phi|_{U})}
.
∎

Lemma 5.9
.

If the graded support of
\mathcal{U}
is
\phi
then
\phi
is a Thomason filtration.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.8
we have
\mathcal{U}|_{U}=\mathcal{U}_{(\phi|_{U})}
and

\mathcal{U}|_{U}
is compactly generated. Since the graded support of a compactly generated
aisle is a Thomason filtration we get
\phi|_{U}
is a Thomason
filtration. In the Noetherian case, Thomason subsets and specialization
closed subsets are the same. Specialization closed is a local property therefore the graded
support of
\mathcal{U}
is a Thomason filtration.

∎

Proof of Theorem
5.5
.

We denote the graded support of
\mathcal{U}
by
\phi
and consider
\mathcal{U}_{\phi}
the
subcategory associated with
\phi
. Clearly,
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathcal{U}_{\phi}
.

For
A\in\mathcal{U}_{\phi}
, consider the following t-decomposition triangle for
\mathcal{U}

\tau^{\leq}_{\mathcal{U}}A\rightarrow A\rightarrow\tau^{>}_{\mathcal{U}}A\rightarrow\tau^{\leq}_{\mathcal{U}}A[1].

By Lemma
4.6
restricting to
U
gives a t-decomposition triangle for

\mathcal{U}|_{U}

j^{*}\tau^{\leq}_{\mathcal{U}}A\rightarrow j^{*}A\rightarrow j^{*}\tau^{>}_{\mathcal{U}}A\rightarrow j^{*}\tau^{\leq}_{\mathcal{U}}A[1].

Since
j^{*}A\in\mathcal{U}_{(\phi|_{U})}
and
\mathcal{U}|_{U}=\mathcal{U}_{(\phi|_{U})}
the map

j^{*}\tau^{\leq}_{\mathcal{U}}A\rightarrow j^{*}A
is an isomorphism. As it is true for each open affine
subset of
X
,
we get
\tau^{\leq}_{\mathcal{U}}A\rightarrow A
is an isomorphism. Thus

A\in\mathcal{U}
and this proves
\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{U}_{\phi}
. By Lemma
5.9
,

\phi
is a Thomason filtration. Now by Theorem
4.11
, the
subcategory
\mathcal{U}_{\phi}
is a compactly generated
\otimes
-aisle. Therefore
\mathcal{U}
is
compactly generated.
∎

Remark 5.10
.

By Remark
5.2
and Proposition
2.9
, the aisle of a
homotopically smashing stable tensor t-structure on
\mathbf{D}(\mathrm{Qcoh}(X))
is a
smashing
\otimes
-ideal of
\mathbf{D}(\mathrm{Qcoh}(X))
. Therefore, Theorem

5.5
provides another proof of the tensor telescope conjecture for
separated Noetherian schemes.

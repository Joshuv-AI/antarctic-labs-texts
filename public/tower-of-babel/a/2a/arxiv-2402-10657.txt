Existence of a minimizer to the particle number-Casimir functional for the Einstein-Vlasov system
Håkan Andréasson

Abstract
In 2001 Wolansky [ 13 ] introduced a particle number-Casimir functional for the Einstein-Vlasov system. Two open questions are associated with this functional. First, a meaningful variational problem should be formulated and the existence of a minimizer to this problem should be established. The second issue is to show that a minimizer, for some choice of the parameters, is a static solution of the Einstein-Vlasov system. In the present work we solve the first problem by proving the existence of a minimizer to the particle number-Casimir functional. On the technical side, it is a main achievement that we are able to bypass the non-compactness of minimizing sequences by new arguments in both v v -space and x x -space, which might have several further applications. We note that such compactness results for the Einstein-Vlasov system have been absent in the literature, whereas similar results have been known in the Newtonian case. We also provide arguments which give strong support that minimizers corresponding to small masses are static solutions of the Einstein-Vlasov system. Furthermore, our analysis leads us to propose a new stability criterion for static solutions: We conjecture that a static solution for which the Casimir-binding energy is positive is stable for mass-preserving perturbations.



1
Introduction

The Einstein-Vlasov system is a PDE system from Mathematical General Relativity,
where Einstein’s field equations are used jointly with a kinetic description of the matter
to model the time evolution of a collection of non-colliding objects (‘particles’);
the objects to be described in astrophysics include stars, galaxies or galaxy clusters.
The system simplifies considerably, if some symmetry assumptions are added,
like spherical symmetry.

It is well-known that there exists a large number of spherically symmetric
static (i.e., time-independent) solutions to the Einstein-Vlasov system
[
12
,
2
]
.
Whether or not a subclass of these solutions can be obtained as minimizers of a variational problem,
as should be expected from general physics principles,
has however remained an open question. The main reason why this is of interest is related to stability.
In the Newtonian analogue, i.e., for the Vlasov-Poisson system, stability of a class of static solutions
was first shown by Guo and Rein as a consequence of their result that the solutions are minimizers
of an energy-Casimir functional
[
10
]
. Many quite general results on stability for the Vlasov-Poisson system
have since then been obtained, see
[
11
,
12
]
and the references therein.

For the Einstein-Vlasov system it was claimed by Wolansky in
[
13
]
that a class of static solutions
of the Einstein-Vlasov system are minimizers to a particle number-Casimir functional. However, the proof
in
[
13
]
contains serious flaws as observed in
[
4
]
, and hence the problem was still open.
It naturally splits into two parts: Firstly, one needs to formulate a variational problem
and to show that a minimizer to this variational problem does exist. The second part is to prove that a minimizer,
for some choices of the parameters, is a static solution of the Einstein-Vlasov system.

The main purpose of the present work concerns part one, i.e., we set up a variational problem
and establish that a minimizer to this problem exists. To show existence of a minimizer to a variational problem
often requires that compactness properties of the minimizing sequence can be derived. Indeed, this is central
in the Vlasov-Poisson case
[
10
]
, but in the relativistic case such compactness results
have so far been absent in the literature.
An essential part of the present paper is about obtaining such compactness results for minimizing sequences.
Moreover, we provide arguments which give strong support that a class of minimizers are static solutions;
we are however not able to fully prove this. Furthermore, our existence proof leads us to a new criterion
which we conjecture to be sufficient for stability.

We now specify in detail the problem we solve and we also include some more comments on the Newtonian case,
since there are some fundamental differences between the relativistic and the non-relativistic systems.
Let the Casimir function be defined as

\chi(f)=\frac{k}{k+1}\,f^{1+\frac{1}{k}}

for
k\in]0,2]
. The functional we are dealing with is

{\cal D}(f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\lambda}\,(\chi(f)-\alpha f)\,dx\,dv,

(1.1)

where
\alpha>0
,
\lambda=\lambda_{f}
is given by

e^{-2\lambda}=1-\frac{2m}{r}

and
m=m_{f}
is the mass function defined as

m(r)=\int_{|x|\leq r}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f\,dx\,dv.

We refer to this functional as the particle number-Casimir functional (introduced by Wolansky
[
13
]

and also used in
[
5
]
), since
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\lambda}\,f\,dx\,dv

represents the particle number. We point out that
\mathcal{D}(f)
is a conserved quantity along solutions
f=f(t)

of the full Einstein-Vlasov system. From now on we fix
\alpha=1
,
but let us nevertheless explain the reason for including a general
\alpha
in (
1.1
).
The goal is to show that a minimizer of
\mathcal{D}
over a certain set
{\cal A}
of admissible functions
is a static solution that has the form

f_{0}(r,w,\ell)=\Big(1-Ce^{\mu_{0}(r)}\sqrt{1+w^{2}+\ell^{2}/r^{2}}\Big)_{+}^{k},

(1.2)

where
r=|x|
,
w=\frac{x\cdot v}{r}
and
\ell=|x\wedge v|
are the spherical coordinates
and
e^{\mu_{0}(r)}
is one of the metric coefficients.
A family of static solutions of this form can be parametrized by the central red shift
[
6
,
9
]
.
If the mass and the radius of the spatial support of the solutions for such a sequence is plotted (the ‘mass-radius diagram’),
then a spiral is obtained
[
6
]
. A solution of the form (
1.2
)
can thus not have an arbitrary large mass. However, if
f_{0}

is a static solution with induced mass
M
, then another static solution can be generated
by applying the scaling
\tilde{f}(x,v)=\gamma^{2}f_{0}(\gamma x,v)
, see Lemma
5.1
ahead.
The induced mass of
\tilde{f}
is
M/\gamma
. Hence, if we want to find static solutions with large masses as minimizers,
then the corresponding particle number-Casimir functional (
1.1
) needs to be modified, i.e.,
one needs to have a different value than
\alpha=1
. This will result in a slightly different form of
f_{0}
which depends
on
\alpha
, cf.
[
13
]
. For minimizing the functional
{\cal D}
,
it is however sufficient to deal with just one
\alpha
, which we take to be
\alpha=1
.

We will consider functions
f=f(x,v)\geq 0
of
x,v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
that are spherically symmetric,
and our goal is to minimize
{\cal D}

over an appropriate class of functions
{\cal A}
. For
f\in{\cal A}

we will require the mass constraint

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f\,dx\,dv=M.

(1.3)

We note that also
M
is a conserved quantity of the Einstein-Vlasov system.
This constraint will not be sufficient to enforce the mass function
m=m_{f}
to have

2m/r
stay away from
1
. Without a restriction on
m/r
, the functional can be shown to be unbounded from below.
This is in stark contrast to the Newtonian case, where the corresponding functional is bounded from below
and a
global
minimizer exists.
For this reason we will need to add another condition,
which we choose to be of the form
\rho(r)=\rho_{f}(r)\leq\sigma_{0}
for
r\in[0,\infty[
, where

\rho(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f(x,v)\,dv

(1.4)

denotes the density induced by
f
, and
\rho(x)=\rho(r)
by abuse of notation.
Here another important technical difference
to the Vlasov-Poisson case arises: For Einstein-Vlasov (
1.4
) and (
1.3
)
only gives
\rho\in L^{1}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, whereas the density
\rho_{{\rm VP}}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(x,v)\,dv

for Vlasov-Poisson is know to satisfy
\rho_{{\rm VP}}\in L^{5/3}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

To summarize, we consider the convex set
{\cal A}
given by

\displaystyle{\cal A}={\cal A}_{M,\sigma_{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\{f=f(x,v)\geq 0\,\,\mbox{is measurable and spherically symmetric},

(1.5)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f\,dx\,dv=M,\,\,\rho(r)\leq\sigma_{0}\,\,\mbox{for}\,\,r\in[0,\infty[\Big\},

where
M>0
and
\beta\in]0,\frac{1}{2}[
are fixed, and for technical reasons

0<\sigma_{0}\leq\min\Big\{1,\frac{3\beta^{3}}{4\pi M^{2}}\Big\}.

(1.6)

It is shown in Lemma
9.2
below that for
f\in\mathcal{A}
it holds that
m/r\leq\beta
.
Let us point out that the formulation of the variational problem by Wolansky in
[
13
]

is different from ours. In fact, Wolansky eventually considers another functional
(defined in terms of a suitable Lagrangian). However, in both formulations a restriction on
m/r
is necessary.
This restriction introduces additional ‘boundaries’ of the set
{\cal A}
, and the minimization problem
is turned into an obstacle problem, which is one reason for the flaws in
[
13
]
.

We want to minimize
\mathcal{D}
over the set
\mathcal{A}
and we thus define

I=\inf_{f\in{\cal A}}{\cal D}(f).

(1.7)

It is not difficult to establish that
I<0
; see Lemma
4.2
ahead.
The main purpose of this paper is to show that
I
is attained,
in the sense that there exists a function
f_{0}\in{\cal A}
such that
{\cal D}(f_{0})=I
.
This will be achieved under a further assumption,
which we call the Casimir-binding-energy condition (CBEC).

Definition 1.1

We say that the Casimir-binding-energy condition (CBEC) holds, if

I=\inf_{f\in{\cal A}}{\cal D}(f)<-M.

Analytically we are able to show that (CBEC) holds for all
k\in]0,2]
, cf. Lemma
4.2
.
The reason for the terminology ‘Casimir-binding-energy’ condition
is due to the definition of the binding energy as

E_{b}(f)=M_{0}-M=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\lambda}\,f\,dx\,dv-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}f\,dx\,dv.

As mentioned above,
M_{0}
is a conserved quantity called the particle number, or rest mass.
We thus find it natural to call the quantity

E_{Cb}(f)=-\mathcal{D}(f)-M=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\lambda}\,(f-\chi(f))\,dx\,dv-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}f\,dx\,dv

the Casimir-binding energy.
We note that both the binding energy and the Casimir-binding energy
are conserved quantities. Moreover, due to
f\geq 0
we have
E_{b}(f)\geq E_{Cb}(f)
, so that the (CBEC) condition
implies that a minimizer
f_{0}
(which we show that exists) has the property that
E_{b}(f_{0})>0
.
It is known that perturbations of static solutions for which
E_{b}(f)>0
have better stability properties
than in the case where
E_{b}(f)<0
, although the condition
E_{b}(f)>0
is certainly not sufficient for stability
[
6
,
9
]
.
In the literature, the strong binding energy hypothesis
[
14
]
claims that the first local maximum
of the binding energy along a sequence of static solutions signals the onset of instability.
This claim has recently been investigated carefully using numerical simulations, and counterexamples were found,
see
[
9
]
. There is however strong support that the
weak
binding energy hypothesis holds, which says
that static solutions up to the first local maximum of the binding energy along a sequence are stable.
To decide whether or not a steady state has the property that it is situated before the first local maximum
of the binding energy along a sequence requires that such a sequence of static solutions can be generated.
We propose a more direct criterion that can be straightforwardly checked,
without the need of having a sequence of static solutions. The class of perturbations that we address have the property that they leave the total mass unchanged, and the perturbed density function
f
off a static solution
f_{0}
is also required to be non-negative. We call this class mass-preserving perturbations.

Conjecture:
Let
f_{0}
be an isotropic static solution of the form (
1.2
).
If
\mathcal{D}(f_{0})<-M
, where
M
is the induced mass of
f_{0}
, then
f_{0}
is stable for mass-preserving perturbations.

In Section
8
a more detailed discussion of the (CBEC) is given and some comments about the stated conjecture are also provided.

Let us end this introduction by commenting about the relation between stability and the existence of a minimizer.
As mentioned before, for the Vlasov-Poisson system stability is a consequence
of the fact that a static solution is obtained as a minimizer of an energy-Casimir functional over a certain set
(which we simply denote by
\mathcal{A}_{{\rm VP}}
without being more specific).
A perturbation
f
of a static solution
f_{0}
will stay in the set
\mathcal{A}_{{\rm VP}}
as it evolves,
and this fact is crucial for deducing stability. In the relativistic case, a perturbation of a static solution
will not necessarily remain in the set
\mathcal{A}
due to the constraint
\rho\leq\sigma_{0}
.
Hence, if a static solution is a minimizer of the particle number-Casimir functional,
it is necessary to control the evolution of a perturbation of the static solution
in a much more precise fashion to make a conclusion about stability.
This is highly non-trivial and again points at the fundamental difference between the Newtonian and the relativistic case.
Nevertheless, this provides us with another method to approach the non-linear stability problem.

This paper is organized as follows. In the next section the main results and some ideas of the proofs
are outlined. A new method to obtain minimizing sequences that decay in
v
-space
is presented in Section
3
, and it relies on a rearrangement argument.
This property is crucial for deriving further features of the limiting function
f_{0}
of the minimizing sequence,
see Section
4
. By using the Casimir-binding-energy condition (CBEC),
the compact support of
f_{0}
in
x
-space can be proved in Section
5
. In addition,
the existence of a minimizer
f_{0}\in\mathcal{A}
is shown in this section. A variational inequality satisfied by
f_{0}

is derived in Section
6
. As a consequence of this inequality we obtain that the minimizer
has compact support also in
v
-space. In Section
7
, a connection to the results in
[
5
]

is made by proving that the minimizer necessarily takes a certain form, of which a static solution is a special case.
A particularly transparent way to state the variational inequality is also derived, which clarifies under which conditions a minimizer
is a static solution. An argument is also given which gives strong support to the belief
that the minimizer is a static solution, provided that the mass
M
is sufficiently small.
A discussion of the Casimir-binding-energy condition (CBEC) is contained in Section
8
, as well as a discussion of the conjecture on stability that we propose.
Section
9
has some technical results,
in particular, it is shown that the ‘Casimir part’ of the functional
\mathcal{D}
is convex
for
k\in]0,2]
.

2
Main results and ideas of proofs

The minimization problem (
1.7
), (
1.5
), (
1.1
)
is quite non-standard. Certainly a minimizing sequence
(f_{n})\subset{\cal A}

will be bounded in
L^{1+1/k}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
, and we can extract a subsequence
(that is not relabeled) such that

f_{n}\rightharpoonup f_{0}\,\,\mbox{in}\,\,L^{1+1/k}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})

(2.1)

as
n\to\infty
for an appropriate limit function
f_{0}\in L^{1+1/k}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
.
However, it is far from obvious why we should have
f_{0}\in{\cal A}

and
{\cal D}(f_{0})=I
. There is also no direct concentration compactness principle
available that could be of help at this point.

In this paper we will first introduce a new technique that improves minimizing sequence
by mass rearrangement in
v
-space. This procedure should be thought of
as two ‘machines’, with the feature that you stick in a function with certain properties,
then turn the crank, and out comes another function that has better properties.
The first machine modifies
f
into
\hat{f}
and adds the condition
\hat{f}(x,v)\leq 1
,
and in addition we have
\rho_{\hat{f}}=\rho_{f}
as well as
{\cal D}(\hat{f})\leq{\cal D}(f)
.
Then the second machine takes
\hat{f}
into some
\tilde{f}

so that
\rho_{\tilde{f}}=\rho_{\hat{f}}
and
{\cal D}(\tilde{f})\leq{\cal D}(\hat{f})

as well as
\tilde{f}(x,v)\leq 1
. The newly gained property of
\tilde{f}
is

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{|v|\geq P+1}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,\tilde{f}\,dx\,dv\leq\frac{2}{P^{1/4}},\quad P\geq P_{0}+1,

(2.2)

for a certain
P_{0}>0
depending only on
k
,
M
and
\beta
.
If we apply this procedure along the minimizing sequence,
we thus may suppose that additionally
f_{n}(x,v)\leq 1
holds, as well as (
2.2
) for all
f_{n}

(with uniform constants). It turns out that (
2.2
) is sufficient,
together with the weak convergence (
2.1
), to prove the following facts
about the limit function
f_{0}\in L^{1+1/k}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

Lemma 2.1

The following assertions hold:

•

0\leq f_{0}\leq 1
;

•

f_{0}
is spherically symmetric;

•

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{|v|\geq P+1}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f_{0}\,dx\,dv\leq\frac{2}{P^{1/4}},\quad P\geq P_{0}+1
;

•

m_{f_{n}}(r)\to m_{f_{0}}(r)
as
n\to\infty
for
r\in[0,\infty[
,
in particular
m_{f_{0}}(r)/r\leq\beta
for
r\in]0,\infty[
;

•

\rho_{f_{0}}(r)\leq\sigma_{0}
for
r\in[0,\infty[
;

•

\lambda_{f_{n}}(r)\to\lambda_{f_{0}}(r)
as
n\to\infty
for
r\in[0,\infty[
.

While Lemma
2.1
is satisfying, and (
2.2
) is very useful to control the loss of mass in
v
-space,
we need an additional input to make sure that also no mass is lost in
x
-space,
in the sense that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f_{0}\,dx\,dv=M

for the limit function
f_{0}
. Observe that we already know that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f_{0}\,dx\,dv=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{|v|\leq P+1}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f_{0}\,dx\,dv+{\cal O}(P^{-1/4})
,
which serves as a substitute for compact support in
v
.
To close the argument, we will be able to determine the potential loss of mass
in
x
-space by comparison to a function
h\in{\cal A}
such that
{\cal D}(h)<0

and
|D(h)|>M
; such a function
h
must exist due to (CBEC).

Lastly, it is also possible to prove that in fact
f_{0}
must have compact support,
both in
x
and in
v
. Hence we arrive at the following main result of this paper.

Theorem 2.2

Let
k\in]0,2]
. Then (CBEC) is verified
and there is a function
f_{0}\in L^{1+1/k}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})

that is a minimizer, i.e., we have
f_{0}\in{\cal A}
and
{\cal D}(f_{0})=I
.
Furthermore,
f_{0}\leq 1
, and there are
R_{0},S_{0}>0
such that
f_{0}(x,v)=0
for a.e.
|x|\geq R_{0}
,
v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

and for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
|v|\geq S_{0}
.

Up to the compact support property in
v
-space,
the proof of this theorem is completed in Section
5
, cf. Theorem
5.10
.
To establish that the minimizer has compact support in
v
-space we make use of the variational inequality
satisfied by
f_{0}
, as outlined in Section
6
. The compact support property in
v
-space
is proven in Corollary
6.3
.

The important question whether or not the minimizer from Theorem
2.2

is a static solution of the Einstein-Vlasov system is considered in Section
7
.
By making use of the variational inequality satisfied by
f_{0}
and the results in
[
5
]
, we will find that

f_{0}(r,w,\ell)=\Big(1-\tilde{\varepsilon}(r)\sqrt{1+w^{2}+\ell^{2}/r^{2}}\Big)_{+}^{k}

for the function
\tilde{\varepsilon}(r)=(G^{\prime})^{-1}(-4\pi\kappa\rho_{0}(r))
,
where
\kappa=\frac{k+1}{16\pi^{2}}
and

G(\varepsilon)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\xi^{2}\,{(1-\varepsilon\sqrt{1+\xi^{2}})}_{+}^{k+1}\,d\xi,\textrm{for }\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}.

This information can be used to rewrite the variational inequality as

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d}{dr}\Big(e^{\lambda_{0}+\mu_{0}}(m_{g}-m_{0})\Big)\,U\,dr\geq 0.

(2.3)

Here
g\in\mathcal{A}
is free to be taken such that

{\rm supp}\,g\subset{\rm supp}\,f_{0}
, and
\mu_{0}
and
\lambda_{0}

are the induced metric functions for the minimizer
f_{0}
. In addition,

U(r)=-\left\{\begin{array}[]{c@{\quad:\quad}l}e^{-\mu_{0}(r)}\tilde{\varepsilon}(r)&r\in[0,R_{0}]\\
0&r\in]R_{0},\infty[\end{array}\right.,

where
R_{0}
denotes the end of the
r
-support of
f_{0}
.
It turns out that if
U(r)={\rm const.}
, then
f_{0}
is a static solution. Hence, if the inequality in (
2.3
)
could be turned into an equality, the claim would follow from integration by parts, if
U
was differentiable.
If the minimizer stays away from the boundary specified in the set
\mathcal{A}
, i.e.,
if the induced density satisfies
\rho_{0}(r)<\sigma_{0}
,
then all of this can be made rigorous. We give an argument that for small mass
M
, the induced density
\rho_{0}

must at least partially stay away from of the boundary.
Up to such technical issues, we thus provide strong support
that a minimizer corresponding to a small mass is a static solution of the Einstein-Vlasov system.

3
Rearrangement in
v
-space

Throughout this section we fix
M>0
and
\beta\in]0,\frac{1}{2}[
. Let

\displaystyle\tilde{{\cal A}}_{M,\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\{f=f(x,v)\geq 0\,\,\mbox{is measurable and spherically symmetric},

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f\,dx\,dv=M,\,\,\frac{m(r)}{r}\leq\beta\,\,\mbox{for}\,\,r\in]0,\infty[,\,\,\rho(r)\leq 1\,\,\mbox{for}\,\,r\in[0,\infty[\Big\}.

Since it will be needed below, we remark that the function

\phi(s)=\chi(s)-s=\frac{k}{k+1}\,s^{1+1/k}-s,\quad s\in[0,\infty[,

(3.2)

has its zeros at
s=0
and
s=(\frac{k+1}{k})^{k}
, attains its negative
minimal value
\phi(1)=-\frac{1}{k+1}
at
s=1

and decreases in
[0,1]
.

We improve a given
f\in\tilde{{\cal A}}_{M,\beta}

(to be thought of as a member of a minimizing sequence
for
{\cal D}
) in several steps.

Lemma 3.1

Let
f=f(x,v)\geq 0
be spherically symmetric
and so that
f\in\tilde{{\cal A}}_{M,\beta}
, i.e.,

(a)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f\,dx\,dv=M
;

(b)

m(r)/r\leq\beta
for
r\in]0,\infty[
;

(c)

\rho(r)\leq 1
for
r\in[0,\infty[
.

Then there is a spherically symmetric function

\hat{f}=\hat{f}(x,v)\geq 0
such that

•

\rho_{\hat{f}}=\rho_{f}
, in particular (a)-(c) holds for
\hat{f}

and
\hat{f}\in\tilde{{\cal A}}_{M,\beta}
;

•

{\cal D}(\hat{f})\leq{\cal D}(f)
;

•

\hat{f}(x,v)\leq 1
for
(x,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

Proof :
To construct
\hat{f}
, we define the sets

E(x)=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:f(x,v)>1\}

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and also

\rho_{D}(x)=\int_{E(x)}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,(f(x,v)-1)\,dv.

Then
0\leq\rho_{D}(x)\leq\rho(x)\leq 1
. Let
H=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:a<|v|<b\}

for
a=\sqrt[3]{\frac{1}{4\pi}}
and
b=\sqrt[3]{\frac{25}{4\pi}}
,
and moreover

H_{<}(x)=\Big\{v\in H:f(x,v)<\frac{1}{2}\Big\},\quad H_{>}(x)=\Big\{v\in H:f(x,v)\geq\frac{1}{2}\Big\}

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. We have
|H|=(4\pi/3)(b^{3}-a^{3})=8
for the Lebesgue measure of
H
,
and since
H=H_{<}(x)\cup H_{>}(x)
is a disjoint union, also
|H_{<}(x)|+|H_{>}(x)|=8
.
We claim that

(x,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
;

•

we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{|v|\geq P+1}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,\tilde{f}\,dx\,dv\leq\frac{2}{P^{1/4}},\quad P\geq P_{0}+1.

(3.19)

Proof :
From
f
we can pass to
\hat{f}

by applying Lemma
3.1
, and hence we may suppose
w.l.o.g. that already
f
has the additional property that

f(x,v)\leq 1\quad\mbox{for}\quad(x,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}.

(3.20)

Now we need to distinguish a few cases.

Case 1:
We have

P_{0}^{1/4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{|v|\geq P_{0}}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f\,dx\,dv\geq 1.

Then (a)-(e) from Lemma
3.2
are satisfied
for
f
and
P_{0}
. Thus there is
\tilde{f}
such that

\rho_{\tilde{f}}=\rho_{f}
,
{\cal D}(\tilde{f})\leq{\cal D}(f)
,

{\rm supp}(\tilde{f})\subset\{(x,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}:|v|\leq P_{0}+1\}

and
\tilde{f}(x,v)\leq 1
pointwise;
no further property of
\tilde{f}
will be needed here.
To prove (
3.19
), let
P\geq P_{0}+1
. If
|v|\geq P
,
then
\tilde{f}(x,v)=0
and hence

P^{1/4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{|v|\geq P}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,\tilde{f}\,dx\,dv=0.

Case 2:
We have

P_{0}^{1/4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{|v|\geq P_{0}}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f\,dx\,dv<1

and

P^{1/4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{|v|\geq P}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f\,dx\,dv\leq 1,\quad P\geq P_{0},

holds for
f
. Since

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{|v|\geq P+1}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f\,dx\,dv\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{|v|\geq P}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f\,dx\,dv

we may simply take
\tilde{f}=f
in this case.

Case 3:
We have

P_{0}^{1/4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{|v|\geq P_{0}}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f\,dx\,dv<1

and there is a
P_{1}\geq P_{0}
such that

P_{1}^{1/4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{|v|\geq P_{1}}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f\,dx\,dv>1

is verified. Then
P_{1}>P_{0}
, and since the function

\varphi(P)=P^{1/4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{|v|\geq P}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f\,dx\,dv

is continuous on
[P_{0},P_{1}]
, there is a first
\hat{P}\in]P_{0},P_{1}[

such that

\hat{P}^{1/4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{|v|\geq\hat{P}}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f\,dx\,dv=1.

In particular,

P^{1/4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{|v|\geq P}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f\,dx\,dv\leq 1,\quad P\in[P_{0},\hat{P}].

(3.21)

For
f
and
\hat{P}
we can apply Lemma
3.2

to obtain a density
\tilde{f}
such that

\rho_{\tilde{f}}=\rho_{f}
,
{\cal D}(\tilde{f})\leq{\cal D}(f)
,

{\rm supp}(\tilde{f})\subset\{(x,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}:|v|\leq\hat{P}+1\}

and
\tilde{f}(x,v)\leq 1
pointwise. In addition, we can use that

\hat{P}^{1/4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\hat{P}\leq|v|\leq\hat{P}+1}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,\tilde{f}\,dx\,dv\leq 1.

(3.22)

To show (
3.19
), let
P\geq P_{0}+1
. If
P\geq\hat{P}
and
|v|\geq P+1
,
then
|v|\geq\hat{P}+1
, and thus the integral in question vanishes
by the support properties of
\tilde{f}
. Hence we may assume that

P_{0}+1\leq P\leq\hat{P}
holds.
First we will consider the case where
P+1\leq\hat{P}
,
so that we have
P_{0}\leq P<P+1\leq\hat{P}<\hat{P}+1
. We write

\displaystyle P^{1/4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{|v|\geq P+1}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,\tilde{f}\,dx\,dv

(3.23)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P^{1/4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{P+1\leq|v|\leq\hat{P}+1}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,\tilde{f}\,dx\,dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P^{1/4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{P+1\leq|v|\leq\hat{P}}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,\tilde{f}\,dx\,dv+P^{1/4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\hat{P}\leq|v|\leq\hat{P}+1}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,\tilde{f}\,dx\,dv\qquad

Now

P^{1/4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{P+1\leq|v|\leq\hat{P}}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f\,dx\,dv\leq P^{1/4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{|v|\geq P}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f\,dx\,dv\leq 1

by (
3.21
), as
P\leq\hat{P}
.
The second term in (
3.23
) is also bounded by
1
,
since
P^{1/4}\leq\hat{P}^{1/4}
and we may use (
3.22
).
It remains to consider the case where
P+1\geq\hat{P}
,
so that
P_{0}\leq P\leq\hat{P}\leq P+1\leq\hat{P}+1
. Then

\displaystyle P^{1/4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{|v|\geq P+1}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,\tilde{f}\,dx\,dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P^{1/4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{P+1\leq|v|\leq\hat{P}+1}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,\tilde{f}\,dx\,dv

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\hat{P}^{1/4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\hat{P}\leq|v|\leq\hat{P}+1}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,\tilde{f}\,dx\,dv

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 1,

once again by (
3.22
).

Remark 4.1

If (
1.6
) holds,

,
where

To see this,
\rho(r)\leq\sigma_{0}\leq 1
for

by the definition of

. Recalling

We recall that

Lemma 4.2

If
f\in{\cal A}
, then
{\cal D}(f)\geq-c_{\beta}M

for
c_{\beta}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-2\beta}}
;
in particular,
I
is well-defined. Furthermore,

I<0.

In addition, for every exponent
k>0
there exists a function
h\in{\cal A}

such that
{\cal D}(h)<0
and
|{\cal D}(h)|>M
.

Proof :
For
f\in{\cal A}
we have
1\leq e^{\lambda}\leq c_{\beta}
,
as in (
3.10
), and also recall Remark
4.1
. Therefore

{\cal D}(f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\lambda}\,(\chi(f)-f)\,dx\,dv\geq-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\lambda}\,f\,dx\,dv\geq-c_{\beta}M.

To show that
I
is negative, consider

\tilde{f}=A\,{\bf 1}_{[0,a]}(|x|)\,{\bf 1}_{[0,b]}(|v|)

for
A\in]0,1]
and
a,b>0
. Then

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,\tilde{f}\,dv=A\,{\bf 1}_{[0,a]}(r)\,\theta(b),

\displaystyle\theta(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\pi\int_{0}^{b}s^{2}\sqrt{1+s^{2}}\,ds=\frac{\pi}{2}\Big(b\sqrt{1+b^{2}}(1+2b^{2})-\arcsin b\Big).

This gives the mass function

\tilde{m}(r)=4\pi\int_{0}^{r}s^{2}\tilde{\rho}(s)\,ds=\left\{\begin{array}[]{c@{\quad:\quad}c}\frac{4\pi}{3}\,A\,\theta(b)\,r^{3}&r\leq a\\[4.30554pt]
\frac{4\pi}{3}\,A\,\theta(b)\,a^{3}&r\geq a\end{array}\right.,

and hence the condition
\tilde{f}\in{\cal A}
is equivalent to

\frac{4\pi}{3}\,A\,\theta(b)\,a^{3}=M\quad\mbox{and}\quad A\,\theta(b)\leq\sigma_{0}\leq\min\Big\{1,\frac{3\beta^{3}}{4\pi M^{2}}\Big\}.

(4.1)

In addition, we obtain

e^{\tilde{\lambda}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\frac{2\tilde{m}(r)}{r}}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{c@{\quad:\quad}c}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\frac{8\pi}{3}\,A\,\theta(b)\,r^{2}}}&r\leq a\\[8.61108pt]
\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\frac{8\pi}{3}\,A\,\theta(b)\,\frac{a^{3}}{r}}}&r\geq a\end{array}\right..

Since
\tilde{f}=A
on its support and
\chi(A)-A=-A(1-\frac{k}{k+1}\,A^{\frac{1}{k}})<0

due to
A\leq 1
, this yields

\displaystyle{\cal D}(\tilde{f})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\tilde{\lambda}}(\chi(\tilde{f})-\tilde{f})\,dx\,dv

(4.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-A\Big(1-\frac{k}{k+1}\,A^{\frac{1}{k}}\Big)\,\frac{16\pi^{2}}{3}\,b^{3}\int_{0}^{a}\frac{r^{2}}{\sqrt{1-\frac{8\pi}{3}\,A\,\theta(b)\,r^{2}}}\,dr<0.

Note that here explicitly, for
r\in[0,a]
,

\frac{2\tilde{m}(r)}{r}=\frac{8\pi}{3}\,A\,\theta(b)\,r^{2}\leq\frac{8\pi}{3}\,A\,\theta(b)\,a^{2}\leq 2\beta<1

for
r\leq a
, as predicted by Lemma
9.2
, using that (
4.1
) yields

\theta(b)=\frac{3M}{4\pi Aa^{3}}
and
\frac{3M}{4\pi a^{3}}=A\theta(b)\leq\frac{3\beta^{3}}{4\pi M^{2}}

\frac{8\pi}{3}\,A\,\theta(b)\,a^{2}=\frac{2M}{a}\leq 2\beta.

The condition (
4.1
) is easy to satisfy: We will fix
A=A(k)
depending only on
k
below.
Then first take
b>0
small enough such that

holds
and thereafter put

and

by (

), so that in particular

.

For the last assertion, the integral (
4.2
) can be evaluated explicitly.
It is found that, with

, one has

{\cal D}(\tilde{f})=-A\Big(1-\frac{k}{k+1}\,A^{\frac{1}{k}}\Big)\,\frac{8\pi^{2}}{3}\,\frac{b^{3}}{c^{3}}\,\Big(\arcsin(ca)-ca\sqrt{1-c^{2}a^{2}}\Big).

Thus if (
4.1
) holds, the condition

is equivalent to

4\Big(1-\frac{k}{k+1}\,A^{\frac{1}{k}}\Big)\,\frac{\arcsin(ca)-ca\sqrt{1-c^{2}a^{2}}}{c^{3}a^{3}}>\frac{b\sqrt{1+b^{2}}(1+2b^{2})-\arcsin b}{b^{3}}.

(4.3)

As

, the expansions are

\displaystyle\arcsin(x)-x\sqrt{1-x^{2}}=\frac{2}{3}\,x^{3}+{\cal O}(x^{5}),

\displaystyle x\sqrt{1+x^{2}}(1+2x^{2})-\arcsin x=\frac{7}{3}\,x^{3}+{\cal O}(x^{5}),

and we already know from above that we can take

and
a=(\frac{3M}{4\pi A\theta(b)})^{1/3}
to make sure that
\tilde{f}\in{\cal A}
.
From the expansions we deduce as

\displaystyle\theta(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{2}\,\Big(\frac{7}{3}\,b^{3}+{\cal O}(b^{5})\Big)=\frac{7\pi}{6}\,b^{3}+{\cal O}(b^{5}),

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{8\pi}{3}\,A\theta(b)}=\sqrt{A}\,\frac{2\sqrt{7}\pi}{3}\,b^{3/2}\,(1+{\cal O}(b^{2})),

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{3M}{4\pi A\theta(b)}\Big)^{1/3}=A^{-1/3}\,\Big(\frac{9M}{14\pi^{2}}\Big)^{1/3}\,\frac{1}{b}\,(1+{\cal O}(b^{2})),

\displaystyle ca

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A^{1/6}\,\frac{2\sqrt{7}\pi}{3}\,\Big(\frac{9M}{14\pi^{2}}\Big)^{1/3}b^{1/2}\,(1+{\cal O}(b^{2})),

so that also

Thus if

Lemma 4.4

;

•

a.e.

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{X}\int_{V}f_{n}(x,v)\,dx\,dv=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{X}\int_{V}f_{n}(Ax,Av)\,dx\,dv

\displaystyle\int_{X}\int_{V}f_{0}(Ax,Av)\,dx\,dv

as

is spherically symmetric.

If
P\geq P_{0}+1
,

and

\lambda_{f_{n}}(r)\to\lambda_{f_{0}}(r)
pointwise, note first that

e^{\lambda_{f_{n}}}-e^{\lambda_{f_{0}}}=\frac{2}{r}\,\frac{e^{2\lambda_{f_{n}}}e^{2\lambda_{f_{0}}}}{e^{\lambda_{f_{n}}}+e^{\lambda_{f_{0}}}}\,(m_{f_{n}}-m_{f_{0}}),

(4.10)

as is verified by a direct calculation. From above we know
\lim_{n\to\infty}m_{f_{n}}(r)=m_{f_{0}}(r)

for every
r>0
. Since, owing to (
3.10
),
1\leq e^{2\lambda_{f_{n}}}\leq c_{\beta}^{2}

and
1\leq e^{2\lambda_{f_{0}}}\leq c_{\beta}^{2}
, the claim follows.

\Box

5
The Casimir-binding-energy condition and existence of a minimizer

We continue to consider

{\cal D}(f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\lambda}\,(\chi(f)-f)\,dx\,dv,

(5.1)

which is (
1.1
).

First we study the scaling properties of the system; also see
[
9
]
.
Henceforth we will assume that
k\in]0,2]
.

Lemma 5.1

Suppose that
f=f(x,v)\geq 0
is measurable
and spherically symmetric such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}f\,dx\,dv=M
. For
\gamma>0
define

f_{\gamma}(x,v)=\gamma^{2}f(\gamma x,v).

Then

M_{\gamma}=\gamma^{-1}M,\quad\rho_{\gamma}(r)=\gamma^{2}\rho(\gamma r),\quad m_{\gamma}(r)=\gamma^{-1}m(\gamma r),\quad\lambda_{\gamma}(r)=\lambda(\gamma r),

and if furthermore
\gamma\leq 1
, then also

{\cal D}(f_{\gamma})\leq\gamma^{-1}\,{\cal D}(f).

Proof :
By changing variables as
\tilde{x}=\gamma x
,
d\tilde{x}=\gamma^{3}dx
,
we calculate

M_{\gamma}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}f_{\gamma}\,dx\,dv=\gamma^{2}\gamma^{-3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}f(\tilde{x},v)\,d\tilde{x}\,dv=\gamma^{-1}M.

Moreover,

\rho_{\gamma}(r)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}f_{\gamma}(x,v)\,dv=\gamma^{2}\rho(\gamma r)

yields

m_{\gamma}(r)=4\pi\int_{0}^{r}s^{2}\rho_{\gamma}(s)\,ds=4\pi\gamma^{2}\int_{0}^{r}s^{2}\rho(\gamma s)\,ds=4\pi\gamma^{-1}\int_{0}^{\gamma r}\tau^{2}\rho(\tau)\,d\tau=\gamma^{-1}m(\gamma r).

This in turn leads to

e^{-2\lambda_{\gamma}(r)}=1-\frac{2m_{\gamma}(r)}{r}=1-\frac{m(\gamma r)}{\gamma r}=e^{-2\lambda(\gamma r)},

so that
\lambda_{\gamma}(r)=\lambda(\gamma r)
.
It follows that

\displaystyle{\cal D}(f_{\gamma})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\lambda_{\gamma}}\,(\chi(f_{\gamma})-f_{\gamma})\,dx\,dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\lambda(\gamma r)}\,\Big(\frac{k}{k+1}\,\gamma^{2(1+\frac{1}{k})}\,f(\gamma x,v)^{1+\frac{1}{k}}-\gamma^{2}f(\gamma x,v)\Big)\,dx\,dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\lambda(\tilde{r})}\,\Big(\frac{k}{k+1}\,\gamma^{\frac{2}{k}-1}\,f(\tilde{x},v)^{1+\frac{1}{k}}-\gamma^{-1}f(\tilde{x},v)\Big)\,d\tilde{x}\,dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\cal D}(f)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\lambda(\tilde{r})}\,\Big(\frac{k}{k+1}\,[\gamma^{\frac{2}{k}-1}-1]\,f(\tilde{x},v)^{1+\frac{1}{k}}-[\gamma^{-1}-1]f(\tilde{x},v)\Big)\,d\tilde{x}\,dv.

Since
k\in]0,2]
we have
\frac{2}{k}-1\geq 0
, and hence
\gamma^{\frac{2}{k}-1}-1\leq 0

due to
\gamma\leq 1
. Therefore we obtain

\displaystyle{\cal D}(f_{\gamma})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\cal D}(f)+(\gamma^{-1}-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\lambda}\,(-f)\,dx\,dv

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\cal D}(f)+(\gamma^{-1}-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\lambda}\,(\chi(f)-f)\,dx\,dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma^{-1}\,{\cal D}(f),

as claimed.
\Box

Lemma 5.2

Let
f\in{\cal A}
and
\varepsilon,\eta,R>0
be such that
\frac{2M}{R}\leq 1-\frac{1}{(1+\eta)^{2}}
,

f\leq 1,\quad{\cal D}(f)\leq I+\varepsilon\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Delta M=\int_{|x|\geq R}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f\,dx\,dv\in]0,M[

are satisfied. If
f_{1}={\bf 1}_{\{|x|\leq R\}}f
, then

\frac{\Delta M}{M-\Delta M}\,|{\cal D}(f_{1})|\leq(1+\eta)\Delta M+\varepsilon.

Proof :
Let
f_{2}={\bf 1}_{\{|x|\geq R\}}f
.
Since
f\leq 1
we have
\chi(f)-f\leq 0
pointwise,
recall (
3.2
). Therefore in particular
\chi(f_{1})-f_{1}\leq 0
pointwise
and
\chi(f_{2})-f_{2}\leq 0
pointwise. If
r\leq R
, then

\displaystyle m_{f_{1}}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\pi\int_{0}^{r}s^{2}\rho_{f_{1}}(s)\,ds=\int_{|x|\leq r}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f_{1}(x,v)\,dx\,dv

(5.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{|x|\leq r}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f(x,v)\,dx\,dv=m_{f}(r),

and hence also

e^{\lambda_{f_{1}}(r)}=e^{\lambda_{f}(r)},\quad r\leq R.

This gives

\displaystyle|{\cal D}(f)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\lambda_{f}(r)}\,(f-\chi(f))\,dx\,dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{|x|\leq R}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\lambda_{f}(r)}\,(f-\chi(f))\,dx\,dv+\int_{|x|\geq R}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\lambda_{f}(r)}\,(f-\chi(f))\,dx\,dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{|x|\leq R}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\lambda_{f_{1}}(r)}\,(f_{1}-\chi(f_{1}))\,dx\,dv+\int_{|x|\geq R}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\lambda_{f}(r)}\,(f-\chi(f))\,dx\,dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|{\cal D}(f_{1})|+\int_{|x|\geq R}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\lambda_{f}(r)}\,(f-\chi(f))\,dx\,dv.

If
r\geq R
, then
\frac{2M}{R}\leq 1-\frac{1}{(1+\eta)^{2}}
leads to

e^{\lambda_{f}(r)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\frac{2m_{f}(r)}{r}}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\frac{2M}{R}}}\leq 1+\eta.

It follows that

\displaystyle\int_{|x|\geq R}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\lambda_{f}(r)}\,(f-\chi(f))\,dx\,dv

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\eta)\int_{|x|\geq R}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f\,dx\,dv

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\eta)\int_{|x|\geq R}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f\,dx\,dv\leq(1+\eta)\Delta M.

As a consequence,

|{\cal D}(f)|\leq|{\cal D}(f_{1})|+(1+\eta)\Delta M.

(5.3)

Next we apply the scaling property from Lemma
5.1

to
f_{1}
,
M
replaced by the total mass
M-\Delta M
of
f_{1}

and
\gamma=\frac{M-\Delta M}{M}\in]0,1[
to obtain
a spherically symmetric function
f_{1,\gamma}\geq 0
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}f_{1,\gamma}\,dx\,dv=\gamma^{-1}(M-\Delta M)=M

and also

{\cal D}(f_{1,\gamma})\leq\frac{M}{M-\Delta M}\,{\cal D}(f_{1})\leq 0.

From
\rho_{f_{1,\gamma}}(r)=\gamma^{2}\rho_{f_{1}}(\gamma r)\leq\rho_{f_{1}}(\gamma r)\leq\sigma_{0}

we deduce that
f_{1,\gamma}\in{\cal A}
, so that

{\cal D}(f)-\varepsilon\leq I\leq{\cal D}(f_{1,\gamma})\leq\frac{M}{M-\Delta M}\,{\cal D}(f_{1}).

Taking minus signs, this can be rewritten as

\frac{M}{M-\Delta M}\,|{\cal D}(f_{1})|\leq|{\cal D}(f)|+\varepsilon.

Using (
5.3
), we arrive at

\frac{\Delta M}{M-\Delta M}\,|{\cal D}(f_{1})|\leq(1+\eta)\Delta M+\varepsilon,

as was to be shown.

\Box

Now we turn to the Casimir-binding-energy condition (CBEC),
as stated in Definition
1.1
.

Remark 5.3

(a) Observe that (CBEC) holds if and only if
there is a function

h\in{\cal A}
such that
{\cal D}(h)<0
and

|{\cal D}(h)|>M.

(b) According to (a) and Lemma
4.2
, (CBEC) is verified
in particular for
k\in]0,2]
.

Corollary 5.4

Let
h
be as in Remark
5.3
(a) and
\delta>0

such that
|{\cal D}(h)|\geq M+\delta
. Next define

\eta=\min\Big\{1,\frac{\delta}{6M}\Big\}

and take

R\geq\max\Big\{\frac{8M}{3},\frac{2M}{1-\frac{1}{(1+\frac{\delta}{6M})^{2}}}\Big\}.

(5.4)

If
g\in{\cal A}
satisfies

g\leq 1,\quad{\cal D}(g)\leq I+\eta(\Delta M)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Delta M\in]0,M[,

where

\Delta M=\int_{|x|\geq R}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,g\,dx\,dv,

then

\Delta M\geq\frac{\delta}{18}.

In particular, if
(f_{n})\subset{\cal A}
is a minimizing sequence
such that
f_{n}\leq 1
and

\lim_{n\to\infty}{(\Delta M)}_{n}=\varepsilon_{0}

for some
\varepsilon_{0}\in]0,M[
, where

{(\Delta M)}_{n}=\int_{|x|\geq R_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f_{n}\,dx\,dv\quad\mbox{with}\quad R_{n}\geq\max\Big\{\frac{8M}{3},\frac{2M}{1-\frac{1}{(1+\frac{\delta}{6M})^{2}}}\Big\},

(5.5)

then

\varepsilon_{0}\geq\frac{\delta}{18}.

Proof :
We can apply Lemma
5.2
with
f=g
and
\varepsilon=\eta(\Delta M)
,
since
\frac{2M}{R}\leq 1-\frac{1}{(1+\eta)^{2}}
. It follows that

\frac{\Delta M}{M-\Delta M}\,|{\cal D}(g_{1})|\leq(1+\eta)\Delta M+\varepsilon=(1+2\eta)\Delta M

for
g_{1}={\bf 1}_{\{|x|\leq R\}}\,g
, and hence

|{\cal D}(g_{1})|\leq(M-\Delta M)(1+2\eta).

Now we distinguish two cases.
Case 1:
|{\cal D}(h)|\leq|{\cal D}(g_{1})|+\frac{\delta}{6}
. Then

\displaystyle M+\delta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\mathcal{D}(h)|\leq|{\cal D}(g_{1})|+\frac{\delta}{6}\leq(M-\Delta M)(1+2\eta)+\frac{\delta}{6}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M(1+2\eta)+\frac{\delta}{6}\leq M+\frac{\delta}{2},

which is a contradiction.
Case 2:
|{\cal D}(h)|\geq|{\cal D}(g_{1})|+\frac{\delta}{6}
.
If
r\leq R
, then

m_{g_{1}}(r)=4\pi\int_{0}^{r}s^{2}\rho_{g_{1}}(s)\,ds=\int_{|x|\leq r}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}g_{1}(x,v)\,dx\,dv=\int_{|x|\leq r}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}g(x,v)\,dx\,dv=m_{g}(r),

and hence also
\lambda_{g_{1}}(r)=\lambda_{g}(r)
for
r\leq R
.
Therefore we obtain

\displaystyle{\cal D}(h)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\cal D}(g_{1})-\frac{\delta}{6}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{|x|\leq R}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\lambda_{g_{1}}}(\chi(g_{1})-g_{1})\,dx\,dv-\frac{\delta}{6}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{|x|\leq R}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\lambda_{g}}(\chi(g)-g)\,dx\,dv-\frac{\delta}{6}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\cal D}(g)+\int_{|x|\geq R}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\lambda_{g}}(g-\chi(g))\,dx\,dv-\frac{\delta}{6}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\cal D}(g)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\frac{2M}{R}}}\int_{|x|\geq R}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}g\,dx\,dv-\frac{\delta}{6}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\cal D}(g)+(1+\eta)\Delta M-\frac{\delta}{6}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle I+\eta(\Delta M)+(1+\eta)\Delta M-\frac{\delta}{6}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I+(1+2\eta)\Delta M-\frac{\delta}{6}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle I+3\Delta M-\frac{\delta}{6}.

Since
h\in{\cal A}
, we have
I\leq{\cal D}(h)
, which implies that

\Delta M\geq\frac{\delta}{18}
.

For the second part,
R_{n}
satisfies (
5.4
) by hypothesis.
Due to the fact that
(f_{n})
is a minimizing sequence,
we have
\lim_{n\to\infty}{\cal D}(f_{n})=I
.
Then
{(\Delta M)}_{n}\to\varepsilon_{0}>0
shows that
{\cal D}(f_{n})\leq I+\eta{(\Delta M)}_{n}

is verified for
n
large enough. In addition,
\varepsilon_{0}\in]0,M[

yields that also
{(\Delta M)}_{n}\in]0,M[
for
n
sufficiently large. Thus the first part applies to deduce that
{(\Delta M)}_{n}\geq\frac{\delta}{18}

for large
n
, so that
\varepsilon_{0}\geq\frac{\delta}{18}
is found in the limit
n\to\infty
.

\Box

The last part of the previous result can be sharpened as follows.

Let
h
be as in Remark
5.3
(a) and
\delta>0

such that
|{\cal D}(h)|\geq M+\delta
. Let
(R_{n})
be a sequence such that

(5.6)

Then there is no minimizing sequence
(f_{n})\subset{\cal A}
such that
f_{n}\leq 1
and

\lim_{n\to\infty}{(\Delta M)}_{n}\in]0,M[,

Proof :
Since
\delta>0
has the property that
|{\cal D}(h)|\geq M+\delta
,
there is no loss of generality in assuming that
\delta<20M
,
as otherwise
\delta
could be replaced by a smaller number.
Assuming the contrary of what is stated,
let
\varepsilon_{0}=\lim_{n\to\infty}{(\Delta M)}_{n}\in]0,M[
.
We distinguish two cases. Case 1:
\varepsilon_{0}\leq\frac{\delta}{20}
.
From Corollary
5.4
we however deduce that
\frac{\delta}{18}\leq\varepsilon_{0}\leq\frac{\delta}{20}
,
which is a contradiction. Case 2:
\varepsilon_{0}>\frac{\delta}{20}
. Then we may assume that

For every fixed

we consider the continuous function
\kappa_{n}(R)=\int_{|x|\geq R}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f_{n}\,dx\,dv
.
Then

and

.
Hence there is a
\tilde{R}_{n}>R_{n}
such that

. For

{(\widetilde{\Delta M})}_{n}=\int_{|x|\geq\tilde{R}_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f_{n}\,dx\,dv

we then get
{(\widetilde{\Delta M})}_{n}=\kappa_{n}(\tilde{R}_{n})=\frac{\delta}{20}
for

.
Furthermore, since
\tilde{R}_{n}>R_{n}
, also
\tilde{R}_{n}
satisfies (
5.5
).
As
\frac{\delta}{20}<M
by the remark at the beginning of the argument,

to yield
\frac{\delta}{20}\geq\frac{\delta}{18}
,
which is impossible.

\Box

From now on we take
(f_{n})\subset{\cal A}
to be a minimizing sequence for
{\cal D}

with the additional property (
4.6
), and

Lemma 5.6

For every
R>0
we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{|x|\leq R}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f_{n}\,dx\,dv=\int_{|x|\leq R}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f_{0}\,dx\,dv

(5.7)

and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{|x|\leq R}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\varphi\,f_{n}\,dx\,dv=\int_{|x|\leq R}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\varphi\,f_{0}\,dx\,dv

(5.8)

for every bounded and continuous function
\varphi=\varphi(x,v)\geq 0
.

Proof :
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
P\geq P_{0}+1
we use (
4.6
) and Lemma
4.4

to bound

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{|x|\leq R}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f_{n}\,dx\,dv-\int_{|x|\leq R}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f_{0}\,dx\,dv\bigg|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{|x|\leq R}\int_{|v|\leq P+1}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f_{n}\,dx\,dv+\int_{|x|\leq R}\int_{|v|\geq P+1}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f_{n}\,dx\,dv

\displaystyle-\,\int_{|x|\leq R}\int_{|v|\leq P+1}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f_{0}\,dx\,dv-\int_{|x|\leq R}\int_{|v|\geq P+1}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f_{0}\,dx\,dv\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{|x|\leq R}\int_{|v|\leq P+1}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f_{n}\,dx\,dv-\int_{|x|\leq R}\int_{|v|\leq P+1}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f_{0}\,dx\,dv\bigg|+\frac{4}{P^{1/4}}.

Given
\varepsilon>0
choose
P\geq P_{0}+1
large enough so that

.
Since
|\ldots|\to 0
as

for the first term on the right-hand side

with the property that

for

, which establishes (

To show (
5.8
), one can proceed in an analogous way, using that this time

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{|x|\leq R}\int_{|v|\geq P+1}\varphi\,f_{n}\,dx\,dv-\int_{|x|\leq R}\int_{|v|\geq P+1}\varphi\,f_{0}\,dx\,dv\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{|x|\leq R}\int_{|v|\geq P+1}\varphi\,f_{n}\,dx\,dv+\int_{|x|\leq R}\int_{|v|\geq P+1}\varphi\,f_{0}\,dx\,dv

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\|\varphi\|}_{\infty}\int_{|x|\leq R}\int_{|v|\geq P+1}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f_{n}\,dx\,dv+{\|\varphi\|}_{\infty}\int_{|x|\leq R}\int_{|v|\geq P+1}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f_{0}\,dx\,dv

\displaystyle\leq

for the error term. For the other term, the weak convergence may still be used,
owing to the fact that
{\bf 1}_{\{|x|\leq R\}}{\bf 1}_{\{|v|\leq P+1\}}\varphi\in{L^{1+1/k}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\ast}
.

\Box

. Let

5.3
(a) and

\Box

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{|x|\leq R}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f_{n}\,dx\,dv=0

from (

h

5.3
(a) and

such that

. In particular,

If

In order to establish that

, due to Lemma

has mass

.
Suppose that
m=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}f_{0}\,dx\,dv<M

, then in fact

. Let

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{|x|\leq R}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f_{0}\,dx\,dv

\displaystyle\int_{|x|\leq R_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f_{0}\,dx\,dv=m.

Lemma 5.9

\displaystyle\int_{|x|\leq R}\int_{|v|\leq P+1}|e^{\lambda_{f_{n}}}-e^{\lambda_{f_{0}}}|\,f_{n}\,dx\,dv+\int_{|x|\geq R}\int_{|v|\leq P+1}|e^{\lambda_{f_{n}}}-e^{\lambda_{f_{0}}}|\,f_{n}\,dx\,dv

.

Lemma 6.1

If

\int\int\Big[e^{\lambda_{0}}(\chi^{\prime}(f_{0})-1)(g-f_{0})+e^{3\lambda_{0}}\,(\chi(f_{0})-f_{0})\,\frac{m_{g}-m_{0}}{r}\Big]\,dx\,dv\geq 0

Proof :
Let
g\in{\cal A}
. From the convexity of
{\cal A}

and the minimality of
f_{0}
we can infer that
\varphi(t)={\cal D}(tg+(1-t)f_{0})\geq{\cal D}(f_{0})=\varphi(0)
for

. Therefore by definition of
{\cal D}

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\lambda_{0}}\,(\chi(f_{0})-f_{0})\,dx\,dv\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\lambda_{tg+(1-t)f_{0}}}\,\Big(\chi(tg+(1-t)f_{0})-f_{0}-t(g-f_{0})\Big)\,dx\,dv

for

1\leq e^{\lambda_{g}}\leq c_{\beta}

\Big|\frac{m_{g}(r)}{r}-\frac{m_{0}(r)}{r}\Big|\leq\frac{4\pi}{r}\,\Big(\int_{0}^{r}s^{2}\,(\rho_{g}(s)+\rho_{0}(s))\,ds\Big)\leq\frac{8\pi\sigma_{0}}{3r}\,r^{3}\leq\frac{8\pi}{3}\,\sigma_{0}.

On the other hand, if
r\geq 1
, then

\Big|\frac{m_{g}(r)}{r}-\frac{m_{0}(r)}{r}\Big|\leq\frac{4\pi}{r}\,\int_{0}^{r}s^{2}\,(\rho_{g}(s)+\rho_{0}(s))\,ds\leq 2M.

2e^{2\lambda_{0}(r)}\Big|\frac{m_{g}(r)-m_{0}(r)}{r}\Big|\leq 2c_{g}^{2}\,\Big(\frac{8\pi}{3}\,\sigma_{0}+2M\Big)=:C_{0}

in any case. Henceforth we restrict the range of
t

to
t\in[0,1]\cap[0,\frac{1}{2C_{0}}]
. Since
m_{f}
is linear in
f
,
we can write pointwise a.e.

\displaystyle e^{-2\lambda_{tg+(1-t)f_{0}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\frac{2m_{tg+(1-t)f_{0}}}{r}=1-\frac{2m_{0}}{r}-2t\,\frac{m_{g}-m_{0}}{r}=e^{-2\lambda_{0}}-2t\,\frac{m_{g}-m_{0}}{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-2\lambda_{0}}\Big(1-2t\,e^{2\lambda_{0}}\frac{m_{g}-m_{0}}{r}\Big).

6.3
) and

e^{\lambda_{tg+(1-t)f_{0}}}=e^{\lambda_{0}}\frac{1}{(1-2t\,e^{2\lambda_{0}}\frac{m_{g}-m_{0}}{r})^{1/2}}.

We have

\frac{1}{(1-x)^{1/2}}=1+\frac{1}{2}\,x+\psi(x)x^{2},\quad|\psi(x)|\leq C,\quad x\in\Big[0,\frac{1}{2}\Big].

Therefore we may continue

\displaystyle e^{\lambda_{tg+(1-t)f_{0}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{\lambda_{0}}\Big(1+t\,e^{2\lambda_{0}}\frac{m_{g}-m_{0}}{r}+\psi\Big(2t\,e^{2\lambda_{0}}\frac{m_{g}-m_{0}}{r}\Big)\Big[2t\,e^{2\lambda_{0}}\frac{m_{g}-m_{0}}{r}\Big]^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{\lambda_{0}}\Big(1+t\,e^{2\lambda_{0}}\frac{m_{g}-m_{0}}{r}+t^{2}\Psi\Big),

where
|\Psi(t,r)|\leq C
is uniformly bounded. Next,

\displaystyle\chi(tg+(1-t)f_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\chi(f_{0})+t\chi^{\prime}(f_{0})(g-f_{0})+\int_{0}^{t(g-f_{0})}\Big(\chi^{\prime}(f_{0}+s)-\chi^{\prime}(f_{0})\Big)\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\chi(f_{0})+t\chi^{\prime}(f_{0})(g-f_{0})+t(g-f_{0})\,\Phi,

\displaystyle\Phi(t,x,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\chi^{\prime}(f_{0}(x,v)+\tau(g(x,v)-f_{0}(x,v)))-\chi^{\prime}(f_{0}(x,v))\Big)\,d\tau.

Going back to (
6.2
) and using the previous expansions, we get
for
t\in]0,\frac{1}{2C_{0}}]
, after some simplification,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\lambda_{0}}\bigg(\Big[(\chi^{\prime}(f_{0})-1)(g-f_{0})+e^{2\lambda_{0}}(\chi(f_{0})-f_{0})\,\frac{m_{g}-m_{0}}{r}+(g-f_{0})\Phi\Big]

\displaystyle\hskip 60.00009pt+\,t\Big[e^{2\lambda_{0}}\frac{m_{g}-m_{0}}{r}\,(g-f_{0})(\chi^{\prime}(f_{0})-1+\Phi)+(\chi(f_{0})-f_{0})\Psi\Big]

.

since the other terms are similar or easier. For a.e.

as

,

(r,w,\ell)\in Q=[r_{1},r_{2}]\times[w_{1},w_{2}]\times[\ell_{1},\ell_{2}]

.

, then

for all

.
For the mass functions it follows

and in particular

.

.
If

(r,w,\ell)\not\in Q

.

\Box

Let

R_{0}=\inf\{R>0:f_{0}(r,w,\ell)=0\,\,\mbox{for a.e.}\,\,r\in[R,\infty[,w\in\mathbb{R},\ell\in[0,\infty[\}.

Next we show a further support property of

Lemma 7.6

Suppose that
\rho_{0}(r)\leq\sigma_{0}-\delta_{0}

.
Then there is no interval
]a,b[

.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m(a)+4\pi^{2}\int_{a}^{r}F(s)\,ds=m_{0}(a)+4\pi^{2}\int_{a}^{r}F_{0}(s+\delta)\,ds

Here we used that

\rho(r)=\frac{\pi}{r^{2}}\,F(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{c@{\quad:\quad}l}\frac{\pi}{r^{2}}\,F_{0}(r)&r\in[0,a]\\[4.30554pt]

.

,

. Lastly, we observe that

\displaystyle{\cal D}(f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\pi^{2}\int_{0}^{R_{0}-\delta}\frac{G(r)}{\sqrt{1-\frac{2m(r)}{r}}}\,dr

\displaystyle 4\pi^{2}\int_{0}^{a}\frac{G_{0}(r)}{\sqrt{1-\frac{2m_{0}(r)}{r}}}\,dr+4\pi^{2}\int_{a}^{R_{0}-\delta}\frac{G(r)}{\sqrt{1-\frac{2m(r)}{r}}}\,dr=:T_{1}+T_{2}.

\Box

In the following lemma we show that there are minimizers that are not constant

Lemma 7.7

Let
\beta
be sufficiently small so that

. Let
M=\sqrt{3\beta^{3}/4\pi}

so that
\sigma_{0}=1
, cf. (

). If
\rho_{0}=\sigma_{0}=1
on
[0,r_{\ast}]
, then

.

Proof :

Assume that
\rho_{0}=\sigma_{0}=1
on
[0,M/\beta]
.
Let

. Since

we have

\int_{|v|\leq K}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}f_{0}\,dv\leq\int_{|v|\leq K}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,dv\leq\frac{\sigma_{0}}{2}=\frac{1}{2},

which implies that

M_{<}=4\pi\int_{0}^{M/\beta}r^{2}\int_{|v|\leq K}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}f_{0}\,dv\,dr\leq\frac{M}{2}.

Clearly, we have that

. From (

) we get

Hence we obtain

\Box

Remark 7.8

In the argument above we used that

, at least on a large set of its support. This fact can be used to improve the result, so that

8
On the Casimir-binding-energy condition (CBEC)

In this section we discuss the relevance of the Casimir-binding-energy condition (CBEC) for static solutions, we find strong evidence that static solutions that are not too relativistic will satisfy this condition.
We also comment on the stability conjecture proposed in the introduction.

Let us start by deriving a couple of general results for static solutions.

Lemma 8.1

Suppose that
f_{0}
is a static solution of the form (
7.9
), i.e.,

f_{0}=(1-e^{\mu_{0}(r)-\mu_{0}(R_{0})}{\cal E})_{+}^{k}
for
{\cal E}=\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}=\sqrt{1+w^{2}+\ell^{2}/r^{2}}
. Then

{\cal D}(f_{0})=-4\pi e^{-\mu_{0}(R_{0})}\int_{0}^{R_{0}}r^{2}(p_{0}+\rho_{0})\,e^{\mu_{0}+\lambda_{0}}\,dr.

Proof :
From the proof of Corollary
7.3
it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\chi(f_{0})-f_{0})\,dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{r^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\ell^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}dw\,\frac{d}{dw}\,[(\chi^{\prime}(f_{0})-1)w]\,f_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\pi}{r^{2}}\,e^{\mu_{0}(r)-\mu_{0}(R_{0})}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\ell^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}dw\,\frac{d}{dw}\,[{\cal E}w]\,f_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\pi}{r^{2}}\,e^{\mu_{0}(r)-\mu_{0}(R_{0})}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\ell^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}dw\,\Big(\frac{w^{2}}{\cal E}+{\cal E}\Big)\,f_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(p_{0}+\rho_{0})\,e^{\mu_{0}(r)-\mu_{0}(R_{0})}.

The claim follows by inserting this expression into the formula for
{\cal D}
.

\Box

Lemma 8.2

Suppose that
f_{0}=(1-e^{\mu_{0}(r)-\mu_{0}(R_{0})}{\cal E})_{+}^{k}
. Then

\frac{1}{2}\,e^{-\mu_{0}(R_{0})}M\leq|{\cal D}(f_{0})|\leq e^{-\mu_{0}(R_{0})}M.

Proof :
From
[
1
, equ. (10)]
it follows that

4\pi\int_{0}^{R_{0}}r^{2}(\rho_{0}+p_{0}+q_{0})e^{\mu_{0}+\lambda_{0}}\,dr=M.

(8.1)

Here
q_{0}
denotes twice the tangential pressure.
From this relation and Lemma
8.1
we can derive bounds for
|{\cal D}(f_{0})|
. We immediately have

|{\cal D}(f_{0})|\leq e^{-\mu_{0}(R_{0})}M,

and furthermore, since
\rho_{0}\geq p_{0}+q_{0}
,

\displaystyle|{\cal D}(f_{0})|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 4\pi e^{-\mu_{0}(R_{0})}\int_{0}^{R_{0}}r^{2}\rho_{0}\,e^{\mu_{0}+\lambda_{0}}\,dr

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2\pi e^{-\mu_{0}(R_{0})}\int_{0}^{R_{0}}r^{2}(\rho_{0}+p_{0}+q_{0})\,e^{\mu_{0}+\lambda_{0}}\,dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\,e^{-\mu_{0}(R_{0})}M,

which completes the argument.

\Box

We note that in our case the pressure is isotropic and we have
q_{0}=2p_{0}
, so that

4\pi\int_{0}^{R_{0}}r^{2}(\rho_{0}+3p_{0})\,e^{\mu_{0}+\lambda_{0}}\,dr=M.

From Lemma
8.2
it first seems that highly relativistic solutions for which

e^{-\mu_{0}(R_{0})}=\frac{1}{1-\frac{2M}{R_{0}}}

is large should be good candidates to satisfy the (CBEC). Note that
2M/R_{0}<8/9

for any static solution of the Einstein-Vlasov system, cf.
[
3
]
,
whereas for isotropic solutions an analytic bound is not known, but numerical simulations indicate that
2M/R_{0}<1/2

in that case. We now argue why it is misleading that highly relativistic solutions are the best candidates.
For, let us consider highly relativistic isotropic solutions where the extreme case
2M/R_{0}=1/2
is attained.
Then Lemma
8.2
gives the lower bound

|{\cal D}(f_{0})|\geq\frac{1}{2}\,e^{-\mu_{0}(R_{0})}M=\frac{M}{\sqrt{2}}.

On the other hand, for static solutions that are not very relativistic,
but close to being Newtonian, we have
p_{0}\ll\rho_{0}
, so that

\displaystyle|{\cal D}(f_{0})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\pi e^{-\mu_{0}(R_{0})}\int_{0}^{R_{0}}r^{2}(p_{0}+\rho_{0})\,e^{\mu_{0}+\lambda_{0}}\,dr

\displaystyle\simeq e^{-\mu_{0}(R_{0})}4\pi\int_{0}^{R_{0}}r^{2}(\rho_{0}+3p_{0})\,e^{\mu_{0}+\lambda_{0}}\,dr=e^{-\mu_{0}(R_{0})}M>M.

Hence a static solution that is not too relativistic is a good candidate for satisfying the (CBEC).
Now, it is straightforward to numerically check (CBEC) on particular static solutions, and indeed
we find that solutions that are not too relativistic do verify the condition.

In the introduction we conjecture that isotropic static solutions which satisfy the (CBEC) are stable with respect to mass-preserving perturbations.
An interesting observation is that when
k\ll 1
, the Casimir-binding energy
E_{Cb}
and the binding energy
E_{b}
almost coincide. If the conjecture is true it indicates that when
k=0
, the sign of
E_{b}
alone determines whether a static solution is stable or unstable. The first author investigated, together with Gerhard Rein, stability of static shells in
[
6
]
. (Note that the conjecture we pose concerns isotropic solutions rather than shells, which are anisotropic, but a comparison is nevertheless relevant.) In
[
6
]
the perturbations had the form
f=Af_{0}
, where
A<1
or
A>1
, which we here call amplitude-perturbations. From the results in Table 1 in
[
6
]
it follows that the sign of
E_{b}
does not determine stability in the case of amplitude-perturbations; if
A>1
there are several cases when the static solution is unstable even if
E_{b}>0
. (The binding energy in
[
6
]
is in fact the fractional binding energy but the sign is the same as of
E_{b}
.) Now, the case
A>1
does not preserve the mass and such a perturbation is outside the scope of our conjecture. In a very limited study we have investigated the case
k=0
for shells and we
do
find numerical support that for mass-preserving perturbations the sign of
E_{b}
alone determines the stability properties. An interesting aspect of this is that in the numerical study
[
8
]
, another type of perturbations were considered which are called dynamically accessible perturbations. The results in
[
8
]
indicate that the stability properties for dynamically accessible perturbations and amplitude-perturbations are similar in the following sense: if an amplitude-perturbation with
A>1
is unstable then a dynamically accessible perturbation is also unstable if the perturbation is such that the particles of the perturbation initially go inward, i.e. the steady state is pushed towards collapse rather than towards dispersion.
In our situation with mass-preserving perturbations we have therefore tried perturbations of both types, i.e. perturbations that push towards collapse and towards dispersion. In both cases we find numerical support that a shell with
k=0
, and such that
E_{b}>0
, is stable in accordance with our conjecture. This indicates an essential difference between mass-preserving perturbations and dynamically accessible perturbations. A more extensive numerical study will be carried out in future work to fully understand this intriguing issue.

9
Some technical results

9.1
A convexity property

The following observation is a crucial ingredient of the minimization argument,
and this fact seems not to have been noticed so far.

Lemma 9.1

Let
k\in]0,2]
. Then the functional

f\mapsto\Psi(f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\lambda}\,\chi(f)\,dx\,dv

is convex.

Proof :
Let
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
. To begin with,

e^{\lambda_{f}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\frac{2m}{r}}}.

Since
m=m_{f}
is linear in
f
, we obtain

D(e^{\lambda_{f}})(\varphi)=\frac{1}{r}\,\Big(1-\frac{2m_{f}}{r}\Big)^{-3/2}m_{\varphi}=\frac{1}{r}\,e^{3\lambda_{f}}\,m_{\varphi}

for the derivative. Thus

D\Psi(f)(\varphi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\bigg(\frac{1}{r}\,e^{3\lambda_{f}}\,m_{\varphi}\,\chi(f)+e^{\lambda_{f}}\,\chi^{\prime}(f)\varphi\bigg)\,dx\,dv.

In addition,
D(e^{3\lambda_{f}})(\varphi)=\frac{3}{r}\,e^{5\lambda_{f}}\,m_{\varphi}
.
Hence, for the second derivative,

D^{2}\Psi(f)(\varphi,\varphi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\bigg[\frac{3}{r^{2}}\,e^{5\lambda_{f}}\,m_{\varphi}^{2}\,\chi(f)+\frac{2}{r}\,e^{3\lambda_{f}}\,m_{\varphi}\,\chi^{\prime}(f)\varphi+e^{\lambda_{f}}\,\chi^{\prime\prime}(f)\varphi^{2}\bigg]\,dx\,dv.

(9.1)

Using
\chi(f)=\frac{k}{k+1}\,f^{1+1/k}
,
\chi^{\prime}(f)=f^{1/k}

and
\chi^{\prime\prime}(f)=\frac{1}{k}\,f^{1/k-1}
,
the relation (
9.1
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle D^{2}\Psi(f)(\varphi,\varphi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{k}{k+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\lambda_{f}}f^{1/k-1}\bigg[\frac{3}{r^{2}}\,e^{4\lambda_{f}}\,m_{\varphi}^{2}\,f^{2}+\frac{2}{r}\,e^{2\lambda_{f}}\,m_{\varphi}\,\Big(1+\frac{1}{k}\Big)f\varphi+\frac{1}{k}\Big(1+\frac{1}{k}\Big)\varphi^{2}\bigg]\,dx\,dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{k}{k+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\lambda_{f}}f^{1/k-1}\bigg[\bigg(\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\Big(1+\frac{1}{k}\Big)\varphi+\sqrt{3}\,\frac{1}{r}\,e^{2\lambda_{f}}m_{\varphi}f\bigg)^{2}+\frac{(2-k)}{3k}\Big(1+\frac{1}{k}\Big)\varphi^{2}\bigg]\,dx\,dv,

which is positive, owing to
2-k\geq 0
. Thus
\Psi
is convex.

\Box

9.2
A lemma on
2m/r

Lemma 9.2

Let
M=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\,f\,dx\,dv
be the mass and

\sigma_{M}=\frac{3}{32\pi M^{2}}.

If
\rho(r)\leq\sigma\leq\sigma_{M}
for all
r
, then

\frac{2m(r)}{r}\leq\bigg(\frac{\sigma}{\sigma_{M}}\bigg)^{1/3}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad r.

In particular, if
\sigma<\sigma_{M}
, then

\frac{2m(r)}{r}\leq\bigg(\frac{\sigma}{\sigma_{M}}\bigg)^{1/3}<1\quad\mbox{for all}\quad r.

Furthermore, if
\rho(r)\leq\sigma_{M}
for all
r
and

\frac{2m(r)}{r}=1\quad\mbox{for some}\quad r=r_{\ast},

(9.2)

then
r_{\ast}=2M
.

Proof :
To establish the first claim,
let
r_{1}=(\frac{3M}{4\pi\sigma})^{1/3}
. From the assumption we have

m(r)=4\pi\int_{0}^{r}\eta^{2}\,\rho(\eta)\,d\eta\leq\frac{4\pi}{3}\,\sigma\,r^{3}

for all
r
. Hence, if
r\leq r_{1}
, then we get

\frac{2m(r)}{r}\leq\frac{8\pi}{3}\,\sigma\,r^{2}\leq\frac{8\pi}{3}\,\sigma\,r_{1}^{2}=\frac{8\pi}{3}\,\sigma\,\bigg(\frac{3M}{4\pi\sigma}\bigg)^{2/3}=\bigg(\frac{\sigma}{\sigma_{M}}\bigg)^{1/3}.

(9.3)

On the other hand, if
r\geq r_{1}
, then also

\frac{2m(r)}{r}\leq\frac{2M}{r}\leq\frac{2M}{r_{1}}=2M\bigg(\frac{4\pi\sigma}{3M}\bigg)^{1/3}=\bigg(\frac{\sigma}{\sigma_{M}}\bigg)^{1/3}.

(9.4)

For the second assertion, suppose that
\rho(r)\leq\sigma_{M}
for all
r

and that (
9.2
) holds. We are going to take
\sigma=\sigma_{M}

in the first part of the argument. If we had
r_{\ast}<r_{1}
,
then repeating (
9.3
) with the strict inequality
would imply

1=\frac{2m(r_{\ast})}{r_{\ast}}<1,

which is impossible. Accordingly, we must have
r_{\ast}\geq r_{1}
. But then
(
9.4
) leads to

1=\frac{2m(r_{\ast})}{r_{\ast}}\leq\frac{2M}{r_{\ast}}\leq 1,

which gives

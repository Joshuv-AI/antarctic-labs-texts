Unitary Constraints on Semiclassical Schwarzschild Black Holes in the Presence of Island
Dong-Hui Du, Wen-Cong Gan, Fu-Wen Shu, and Jia-Rui Sun

Abstract
We reconsider D ≥ 4 D\geq 4 dimensional asymptotically flat eternal Schwarzschild black hole, and focus on the situation where the inner boundary of the radiation region is chosen to be near the horizon (i.e. β ≪ 1 \beta\ll 1 ). The tension between the near-horizon condition and the short-distance approximation emerges in large dimensions in [ J ​ H ​ E ​ P ​ 06 ​ ( 2020 ) ​ 085 ] [JHEP06(2020)085] . We remove this tension by introducing a more proper near horizon condition, thus the resulting island solution is well-behaved in any D ≥ 4 D\geq 4 dimensional spacetime. Interestingly, a novel constraint is obtained in this situation as required by the existence of the island solution, which directly leads to the constraints on the size of the Schwarzschild black hole, the position of the inner boundary for the radiation region, or the value of c ⋅ G ~ N c\cdot\tilde{G}_{N} in any D ≥ 4 D\geq 4 dimension. When considering the large D D limit, the constraint on the size of the Schwarzschild black hole obtained in this situation is in agreement with the result given in [ P h y s . R e v . D 102 ( 2020 ) 2 , 026016 ] [Phys.Rev.D102(2020)2,026016] . We interpret these as the unitary constraints implied by the presence of island in semiclassical gravity.

6
Conclusion and discussion

In this paper, we redefine a proper near-horizon condition in higher dimensions, which avoids the ill behavior of short-distance approximation in large dimensions under the near-horizon condition adopted in Ref.
Hashimoto:2020cas
. The general island solution for
{\alpha}<{\beta}\ll 1
is investigated in any
D\geq 4
dimension. An analytical island solution (
20
) is obtained by assuming
{\alpha}\ll{\beta}/n\ll 1
, accompanying with the constraint (
21
). And the existence of the island solution for general condition
{\alpha}<{\beta}\ll 1
is also confirmed, meanwhile a more general constraint (
26
) is obtained by the existence of the island solution in this case. We discuss the constraint (
26
) for situation
{\beta}\ll 1
from three equivalent aspects: constraint (
30
) on the size of the black holes, constraint (
33
) on the position of the inner boundary of the radiation region, and constraint (
35
) on the value of
c\cdot\tilde{G}_{N}
. Moreover, the large dimension behavior of constraint (
31
) on the size of black hole is in agreement with the constraint in Ref.
FDN
. These results are simply required by the presence of the island in situation
{\beta}\ll 1
, which can be understood as the unitary constraints in semiclassical gravity. The entanglement entropy of the radiation follows the Page curve and the Page time is obtained for
{\alpha}<{\beta}\ll 1
. While some doubts about the estimation of the scrambling time by
\Delta t
are raised in this case. For special case
{\alpha}\ll{\beta}/n\ll 1
,
\Delta t
gives the result as the scrambling time at leading order, i.e.
\Delta t\simeq t_{\text{scr}}
, as similarly obtained in Ref.
Hashimoto:2020cas
for
\tilde{{\alpha}}\ll\tilde{{\beta}}\ll 1
. However, for general
{\alpha}<{\beta}\ll 1
, we find
\Delta t\neq t_{\text{scr}}
generally, and
\Delta t
is highly dependent on position of the inner boundary of the selected radiation region.

The existence of the constraint (
26
) implied by the island solution for
{\alpha}<{\beta}\ll 1
is surprising, which reveals some aspects from the unitarity in semiclassical gravity once we admit the island formalism. Some implications are shown in the paper, but much remains to be further investigated. We expect similar results can be obtained for other types of black holes, such as dynamical Schwarzschild black holes and the Reissner-Nordström black holes and so on. In this paper, we only consider the situation where the inner boundary of the radiation region is near the horizon, i.e.
{\beta}\ll 1
, and the constraint (
26
) is only valid for this situation. It is possible that there exists a more general and completed constraint for any
{\beta}>0
by requiring the existence of the island solution. However, the calculation of the entanglement entropy in general
D\geq 4
dimensional Schwarzschild black hole spacetime for any
{\beta}>0
is still a hard task. One may also consider another special situation where the inner boundary of radiation region is chosen to be far from the horizon, i.e.
{\beta}\gg 1
, which is not considered in our paper. As the results of Refs.
Arefeva:2021kfx
;
Arefeva:2022guf
;
Arefeva:2022cam
;
Ageev:2022hqc
;
He:2021mst
strongly indicate that there may also be a unitary constraint required by the existence of the island solution for situation
{\beta}\gg 1
. Moreover, when
{r_{h}}/{l_{p}}\sim(c\cdot\chi)^{{1\over n+1}}
(or equivalently
{cl_{p}^{n+1}}/{r_{h}^{n+1}}\sim 1/\chi
), we find no consistent island solution for situation
{\beta}\ll 1
, as we think it has been out of the semiclassical validity. This even occurs for a large black hole with size
r_{h}\sim n^{{3}/{2}}l_{p}\gg l_{p}
in large dimension limit. The studies of large dimension gravity may help provide some insights into the future studies of quantum-gravity effects.

Appendix A
Short-distance approximation under original and new near-horizon condition

The island solution in higher dimensional spacetime has been studied in Ref.
Hashimoto:2020cas
when inner boundary of the radiation region was chosen to be near the horizon, and the boundary of the island was assumed to be outside and near the horizon. In which the near-horizon condition was defined by
\tilde{{\alpha}}<\tilde{{\beta}}\ll 1
with parameters
\tilde{{\alpha}}\equiv\sqrt{{(r_{a}-r_{h})}/{r_{h}}}>0
and
\tilde{{\beta}}\equiv\sqrt{{(r_{b}-r_{h})}/{r_{h}}}>0
, thus
\tilde{{\beta}}-\tilde{{\alpha}}<\tilde{{\beta}}\ll 1
. However, we would like to point out that this near-horizon condition is not sufficient if one wants to simultaneously take a well-behaved short-distance approximation in large dimension case when applying the formula (
3
).

In any high dimension
D=n+3\geq 4
,
n
can also be treated as a variable and we need be careful when taking short-distance approximation under the original near-horizon condition
\tilde{{\beta}}-\tilde{{\alpha}}<\tilde{{\beta}}\ll 1
. To see the subtleties, we look at the geodesic distance and take the expansions for small
\tilde{{\alpha}},\tilde{{\beta}}
(
\tilde{{\alpha}}
is at most the order of
\tilde{{\beta}}
), thus

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{r_{a}}^{r_{b}}{dr\over\sqrt{1-\left({r_{h}\over r}\right)^{n}}}

(44)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{r_{h}\over\sqrt{n}}\left[\ 2(\tilde{{\beta}}-\tilde{{\alpha}})+{1\over 6}(n+1)(\tilde{{\beta}}^{3}-\tilde{{\alpha}}^{3})\right.

\displaystyle\left.+{1\over 240}(n+1)(n-7)(\tilde{{\beta}}^{5}-\tilde{{\alpha}}^{5})+\ ...\ \right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{2r_{h}(\tilde{{\beta}}-\tilde{{\alpha}})\over\sqrt{n}}\left(1+\mathcal{O}(n\tilde{{\beta}}^{2})\right),

where
``..."
are the higher order terms, and in the last equality we used
n\gg 1
to specify the order in large dimensions. Note that when
n
is very large, a tighter condition
\tilde{{\beta}}\ll{1}/{\sqrt{n}}
(or
n\tilde{{\beta}}^{2}\ll 1
) is required in order to effectively approximate
L
by the first order term of
\tilde{{\alpha}},\tilde{{\beta}}
. This requires a much stronger constraint than the condition
\tilde{{\beta}}\ll 1
if
n\rightarrow\infty
. The short-distance approximation used in Ref.
Hashimoto:2020cas
, i.e.
L\simeq 2r_{h}(\tilde{{\beta}}-\tilde{{\alpha}})/\sqrt{n}
, only behaves well when the dimension is far below a critical dimension
n_{c}={1}/{\tilde{{\beta}}^{2}}
with a small but finite
\tilde{{\beta}}
. And huge corrections of order
\mathcal{O}(n\tilde{{\beta}}^{2})
would be required once
n\sim n_{c}
, even not taking
n\rightarrow\infty
. This tension between the original near-horizon condition and the short-distance approximation in large dimension case is always existing if we take a small but finite
\tilde{{\beta}}
. This short-distance approximation in terms of small but finite
\tilde{{\alpha}},\tilde{{\beta}}
is highly constrained by the dimension, and we need to find a more proper near-horizon condition which gets rid of this large dimension constraint.

The above problem can be avoided by using the coordinate
\rho
. Defining
{\alpha}\equiv\sqrt{\rho_{a}-1}>0
and
{\beta}\equiv\sqrt{\rho_{b}-1}>0
, where
\rho_{a}={(r_{a}/r_{h})}^{n}
and
\rho_{b}={(r_{b}/r_{h})}^{n}
. Also we assume boundary of the island is outside and near the horizon. And we take
{\alpha}<{\beta}\ll 1
as the new near-horizon condition with horizon at
\rho=1
, thus
{\beta}-{\alpha}<{\beta}\ll 1
. Considering the geodesic distance in coordinate
\rho
and taking the expansions for small
{\alpha},{\beta}
(
{\alpha}
is at most the order of
{\beta}
), i.e.

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{r_{a}}^{r_{b}}{dr\over\sqrt{1-\left({r_{h}\over r}\right)^{n}}}=\int_{\rho_{a}}^{\rho_{b}}{r_{h}\rho^{{1\over n}}d\rho\over n\sqrt{\rho(\rho-1)}}

(45)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{r_{h}\over n^{4}}\left[2n^{3}({\beta}-{\alpha})-{1\over 3}n^{2}(n-2)({\beta}^{3}-{\alpha}^{3})\right.

\displaystyle\left.+{1\over 20}n(n-2)(3n-2)({\beta}^{5}-{\alpha}^{5})+\ ...\ \right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{2r_{h}({\beta}-{\alpha})\over n}\left(1+{\cal O}({\beta}^{2})\right),

where
``..."
represent the higher order terms, and in the last equality we used
n\gg 1
to specify the order in large dimensions. One can see the condition
{\beta}\ll 1
is sufficient to ensure the validity of taking the first-order approximation of small but finite
{\alpha},{\beta}

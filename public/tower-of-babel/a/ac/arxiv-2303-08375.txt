Auxiliary Splines Space Preconditioning for B-Splines Finite Elements: The case of 𝑯 ⁡ ( 𝒄 ​ 𝒖 ​ 𝒓 ​ 𝒍 , Ω ) \displaystyle\bm{H}(\bm{curl},\Omega) and 𝑯 ⁡ ( d ​ i ​ v , Ω ) \displaystyle\bm{H}(div,\Omega) elliptic problems
A. El Akri, K. Jbilou, A. Ratnani

Abstract
This paper presents a study of large linear systems resulting from the regular B \displaystyle B -splines finite element discretization of the 𝒄 ​ 𝒖 ​ 𝒓 ​ 𝒍 − 𝒄 ​ 𝒖 ​ 𝒓 ​ 𝒍 \displaystyle\bm{curl}-\bm{curl} and 𝒈 ​ 𝒓 ​ 𝒂 ​ 𝒅 − d ​ i ​ v \displaystyle\bm{grad}-div elliptic problems on unit square/cube domains. We consider systems subject to both homogeneous essential and natural boundary conditions. Our objective is to develop a preconditioning strategy that is optimal and robust, based on the Auxiliary Space Preconditioning method proposed by Hiptmair et al. [ 40 ] . Our approach is demonstrated to be robust with respect to mesh size, and we also show how it can be combined with the Generalized Locally Toeplitz (GLT) sequences analysis presented in [ 49 ] to derive an algorithm that is optimal and stable with respect to spline degree. Numerical tests are conducted to illustrate the effectiveness of our approach.

Introduction

The
Isogeometric Analysis
(IgA) is a mathematical approach that combines
Finite Element Methods
(FEMs) with
Computer-Aided Design
(CAD) to design and analyze the numerical approximation of
Partial Differential Equations
(PDEs). Like FEM, IgA formulates problems through variational methods and specifies a finite-dimensional subspace for the solution. However, IgA employs the same functions used to describe the underlying domain, typically
\displaystyle B
-spline or Non-Uniform Rational
\displaystyle B
-spline (NURBS) functions commonly used in CAD. This approach offers several advantages over FEM, including exact geometry, which eliminates geometric approximation errors, and the use of
\displaystyle B
-spline functions, which makes higher
\displaystyle C^{p}
-continuous interpolation more practical than standard Lagrange and Hermite polynomials used in FEM.

The field of IgA has rapidly developed in recent years, with significant contributions since the pioneering work by Hughes in 2005
[
43
]
. This approach has been applied in various areas, including electromagnetism
[
13
,
15
,
57
]
, incompressible fluid dynamics
[
11
]
, fluid-structure interaction
[
6
,
42
]
, structural and contact mechanics
[
47
,
62
]
, plasmas physics problems
[
58
]
, and kinetic systems
[
1
,
4
,
22
]
, among others. For a comprehensive overview, readers can refer to the review paper by Da Veiga et al.
[
23
]
and Cottrell et al.
[
21
]
.

Despite the large success of the method, it is important to note that dealing with higher-order IgA finite elements can be challenging. Specifically, using higher-order
\displaystyle B
-spline functions can generate huge, sparse, ill-conditioned matrices. Although the discrete systems produced by IgA methods are typically better conditioned than those produced by standard finite elements, their condition numbers cannot be uniformly bounded with respect to the discretization parameter
\displaystyle h
, and can even grow rapidly as
\displaystyle h
approaches zero. For example, this is the case for the Full-Wave problem with high wave numbers
[
50
]
. (See also
[
35
]
for explicit bounds of the spectral condition number in the case of the Poisson equation). As a result, direct solvers are not suitable for IgA discrete systems, and even standard iterative methods may fail. Preconditioning is therefore necessary to obtain convergence in a reasonable amount of time.

The literature offers several techniques to address the problem of preconditioning IgA discrete systems. These include overlapping Schwarz preconditioners
[
24
,
25
]
, non-overlapping decomposition methods
[
7
,
12
,
26
,
27
]
, FETI-type preconditioners
[

,
54
]
, multilevel algorithms

, multigrid methods
[
30
,
34
]
, and preconditioning based on the solution of Sylvester equations
[
60
]
.

A review of the current state of the art indicates a growing interest in developing efficient and rapid IgA preconditioning techniques in recent years. However, most research has focused on scalar elliptic problems, with limited technical generalizations to linear elasticity systems. To the best of our knowledge, only a few papers
[
49
,
50
]
have studied
\displaystyle\bm{H}(\bm{curl})
and
\displaystyle\bm{H}(div)
problems. In these works, the construction of solvers exploits a detailed spectral analysis of the involved matrices based on the theory of the Generalized Locally Toeplitz (GLT) sequences. However, results of practical interest can be precisely developed only if addressed to specific models. In contrast, this paper presents a more general and systematic approach, providing abstract techniques that can be applied to a broader range of problems.

We shall consider two model problems; the
\displaystyle\bm{curl}-\bm{curl}
problem: finds a vector field
\displaystyle\bm{u}\,:\,\overline{\Omega}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that

\bm{curl}\,\bm{curl}\,\bm{u}+\tau\bm{u}=\bm{f},\quad\text{in }\Omega,

(1)

and the
\displaystyle\bm{grad}-div
problem: finds
\displaystyle\bm{u}\,:\,\overline{\Omega}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that

-\bm{gard}\,div\,\bm{u}+\tau\bm{u}=\bm{f},\quad\text{in }\Omega,

(2)

and
subject to both homogeneous natural and essential boundary conditions and where
\displaystyle\bm{f}\,:\,\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a vector field and
\displaystyle\tau
is a small positive parameter. The variational forms of these problems can be written in unified form as follows: Finds
\displaystyle\bm{u}\in\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{D},\Omega)
such that

(D\bm{u},D\bm{v})_{\left(L^{2}(\Omega)\right)^{3}}+\tau(\bm{u},\bm{v})_{\left(L^{2}(\Omega)\right)^{3}}=(\bm{f},\bm{v})_{\left(L^{2}(\Omega)\right)^{3}},\quad\forall v\in\mathcal{H}(\bm{D},\Omega),

(3)

\displaystyle D\in\{\bm{curl},div\}
and the space
\displaystyle\\
mathcal{H}(\mathcal{D},\Omega)
fulfill the boundary conditions on the case of essential boundary conditions (see the next section for a precise definition).

Preconditioning for these types of problems is particularly challenging. This is because, as pointed out in
[
40
]
, the operator
\displaystyle\mathcal{D}
has a large null space. Unlike the scalar Laplacian operator, which has a null space of dimension one, the kernel of
\displaystyle\mathcal{D}
has infinite dimension. Another challenge when discretizing (
3

\displaystyle\tau\rightarrow 0
. While the continuous problem is well-posed, discrete stability can only be achieved with very fine meshes, which leads to a rapidly growing spectral condition number as
\displaystyle\tau
approaches
\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{D},\Omega)
but also be robust with respect to the parameter
\displaystyle\tau

Over the last decades, a promising technique, called Auxiliary Space Preconditioning (ASP) method

The basic idea of ASP is to transfer the original problem to an auxiliary space where it is easier to solve, then transfer the solution back to the original space and correct the error between the auxiliary space and the full space by applying a smoothing scheme. The choice of the auxiliary space is typically based on a stable decomposition of the space
\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{D},\Omega)
, known as a regular decomposition
[
8
,
10
,
20
,
40
,
53
,
64
]
. However, the main challenge in developing the method lies in adapting these regular space decompositions to the discrete level.

The ASP method has already been successfully applied to various preconditioning problems for large-scale finite element systems. In this paper, we extend the method to the isogeometric context, building upon the work presented in
[
40
]
. As a first step, we assume that
\displaystyle\Omega=(0,1)^{d}

\displaystyle d=2
or
\displaystyle 3

2.2
Auxiliary Space Preconditioning (ASP) Method

This subsection provides a brief overview of the Auxiliary Space Preconditioning (ASP) method. For a more detailed discussion, the reader is refereed to
[
17
,
39
,
40
,
45
,
46
,
52
,
63
]
and the references therein.

Let
\displaystyle V
be a Hilbert space with an inner product
\displaystyle a:V\times V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. The ASP method involves three main components: auxiliary spaces, transfer operators, and a smoother. The auxiliary spaces, denoted as
\displaystyle W_{i}
for
\displaystyle i=1,\cdots,I
, are equipped with inner products
\displaystyle a_{i}:W_{i}\times W_{i}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. The transfer operators are linear operators
\displaystyle\pi_{i}:W_{i}\rightarrow V
that map the auxiliary spaces to
\displaystyle V
. The smoother is an inner product
\displaystyle s:V\times V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
that is distinct from
\displaystyle a
and is often provided by a relaxation method such as the Jacobi or symmetric Gauss-Seidel schemes.

Given these components, the ASP preconditioner is constructed as

B=S^{-1}+\sum_{i=1}^{I}\pi_{i}\circ{A}_{i}^{-1}\circ\pi_{i}^{*},

where
\displaystyle S
and
\displaystyle A_{i}
are linear operators corresponding to the inner products
\displaystyle s
and
\displaystyle a_{i}
, respectively, and
\displaystyle\circ
denotes composition of linear operators. The adjoint operator of
\displaystyle\pi_{i}
is denoted as
\displaystyle\pi_{i}^{*}
.

Under appropriate assumptions, we prove that
\displaystyle B
is a valid preconditioner for
\displaystyle A
. Specifically, we have the following result (see
[
40
, Theorem 2.2]
):

Theorem 2.3
.

Assume that there are some nonnegative constants
\displaystyle\beta_{j}
and
\displaystyle\gamma
such that

(i)

The continuity of
\displaystyle\pi_{j}
with respect to the graph norms:

a\left(\pi_{j}(w_{j}),\pi_{j}(w_{j})\right)\leq\beta_{j}\sum_{i=1}^{I}a_{i}(w_{j},w_{j}),\quad\forall w_{j}\in W_{j}.

(ii)

The continuity of
\displaystyle s^{-1}
:

a(v,v)\leq\gamma\,s(v,v),\quad\forall v\in V.

(iii)

Existence of a stable decomposition of
\displaystyle V
: for each
\displaystyle v\in V
, there exist
\displaystyle\widetilde{v}\in V
and
\displaystyle w_{i}\in W_{i}
such that

v=\widetilde{v}+\sum_{i=1}^{I}\pi_{i}w_{i},

with estimate

s(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{v})+\sum_{i=1}^{I}a_{i}(w_{i},w_{i})\leq\eta\,a(v,v),

for some nonnegative (small) constant
\displaystyle\eta
.

Then we have the following estimate for the spectral condition number of the preconditioned operator

\kappa(BA)\leq\eta\left(\gamma+\sum_{i=1}^{I}\beta_{i}\right).

The above result highlights the central importance of stable regular decompositions in constructing an efficient auxiliary space preconditioner. In this work, we focus on the discrete case, which requires adapting the regular decomposition of Theorem
2.2
to the discrete level. As a first step, we introduce the discrete spaces in the next section.

In this section, we introduce a discrete counterpart of the functional space

in the context of Isogeometric Analysis

. We begin by recalling some basic properties of
\displaystyle B
-spline functions and then proceed to construct the IgA discretization of
\displaystyle\bm{curl}
and
\displaystyle div
operators. For an introduction to the subject, we refer the reader to standard textbooks on the topic

Let
\displaystyle T=(t_{1},t_{2},\ldots,t_{m})
be a knot vector, which is a non-decreasing sequence of real numbers. The
\displaystyle i
-th
\displaystyle B
-spline of order
\displaystyle p\in\mathbb{N}
is defined recursively using the
Cox-de Boor formula
as follows:

B_{i,0}(t)=\begin{cases}1\quad&\text{if }t_{i}\leq t<t_{i+1},\\
0\quad&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

B_{i,p}(t)=\frac{t-t_{i}}{t_{i+p}-t_{i}}B_{i,p-1}(t)+\frac{t_{i+p+1}-t}{t_{i+p+1}-t_{i+1}}B_{i+1,p-1}(t),

for
\displaystyle i=1,\ldots,n=m-p-1
, where a fraction with zero denominator is assumed to be zero. Following
[
14
]
, we introduce also the vector
\displaystyle U=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{N})
of breakpoints where
\displaystyle N
is the number of knots without repetition and the regularity vector
\displaystyle\bm{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{N})\in\mathbb{N}^{N}
in such a way that for each
\displaystyle i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
, the
\displaystyle B
-spline function
\displaystyle B_{i,p}
is
\displaystyle\alpha_{i}
continuously derivable at the breakpoint
\displaystyle u_{i}
. Note that
\displaystyle\alpha_{i}=p-r_{i}
where
\displaystyle r_{i}
is the multiplicity of the break point
\displaystyle u_{i}
. Throughout the paper, we will only consider
non-periodic
knot vectors

T=(\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{p+1},t_{p+2},\ldots,t_{m-p-1},\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{p+1}),

and we suppose that
\displaystyle 1\leq r_{i}\leq p
. In this way we guarantee that
\displaystyle 0\leq\alpha_{i}\leq p
where the minimal regularity
\displaystyle\alpha_{i}=0
corresponds to a continuity at knot
\displaystyle u_{i}
. This allow us to introduce the uni-variate spline spaces

\mathcal{S}^{p}_{\bm{\alpha}}=span\left\{B_{i,p}\,:\,i=1,\ldots,n\right\},\quad\mathcal{S}^{p}_{\bm{\alpha},0}=span\left\{B_{i,p}\,:\,i=2,\ldots,n-1\right\}.

Note that all the elements of space
\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{p}_{\bm{\alpha},0}
vanish at the boundary of
\displaystyle(0,1)
(by definition). Hence, the space is suited for dealing with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions.

These definitions can be generalized to the multivariate case
\displaystyle\Omega=(0,1)^{3}
by
tensorization
: With a tridirectional knot vector
\displaystyle\bm{T}=T_{1}\times T_{2}\times T_{3}
at hand, where

T_{i}=(\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{p_{i}+1},t_{i,p_{i}+2},\ldots,t_{i,m_{i}-p_{i}-1},\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{p_{i}+1}),\quad m_{i},p_{i}\in\mathbb{N},\;i=1,2,3,

is an open univariate knot vector, we define the
tensor-product spline
space by

\bm{\mathcal{S}}^{p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}}_{\bm{\alpha}_{1},\bm{\alpha}_{2},\bm{\alpha}_{3}}=\mathcal{S}^{p_{1}}_{\bm{\alpha}_{1}}\otimes\mathcal{S}^{p_{2}}_{\bm{\alpha}_{2}}\otimes\mathcal{S}^{p_{3}}_{\bm{\alpha}_{3}},

where
\displaystyle\bm{\alpha}_{i}
is the regularity vector related to knot
\displaystyle T_{i}
, with
\displaystyle i=1,2,3
. However, we shall also assume our mesh to be
locally quasi-uniform
, meaning, there exists a constant
\displaystyle\theta\geq 1
such that for all
\displaystyle i\in\{1,2,3\}
we have

\frac{1}{\theta}\leq\frac{h_{i,j_{i}}}{h_{i,j_{i}+1}}\leq\theta,\quad j_{i}=1,\ldots,N_{i}-2,

where
\displaystyle N_{i}
is the number of
\displaystyle T_{i}
-knots without repetition and
\displaystyle h_{i,j_{i}}=t_{i,j_{i}+1}-t_{i,k_{j_{i}}}
, with
\displaystyle k_{j_{i}}=\max\{l\,:\,t_{l}<t_{i,j_{i}+1}\}
.

With these notations, the
\displaystyle 3
-
\displaystyle d
approximations spaces are given by (see, e.g.
[
14
,
23
]
)

Lemma 3.1
.

For every
\displaystyle\bm{\varphi}\in\bm{X}(\Omega)
such that
\displaystyle\mathcal{D}\bm{\varphi}\in V_{h}(\mathcal{D}^{+},\Omega)
, we have

(i)

\displaystyle\Pi_{h}^{\mathcal{D}}\bm{\varphi}
is well-defined.

(ii)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}\bm{\varphi}=\mathcal{D}\left(\Pi_{h}^{\mathcal{D}}\bm{\varphi}\right)
.

(iii)

\displaystyle\left\|\bm{\varphi}-\Pi_{h}^{\mathcal{D}}\bm{\varphi}\right\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim h\|\bm{\varphi}\|_{\bm{X}(\Omega)}
.

Proof.

First insertion is a consequence of the fact that
\displaystyle\bm{X}(\Omega)\subset\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{D},\Omega)
. Concerning (ii), using the commutativity of Diagram (
2.5
), we obtain

\mathcal{D}\left(\Pi_{h}^{\mathcal{D}}\bm{\varphi}\right)=\Pi_{h}^{\mathcal{D}^{+}}\left(\mathcal{D}\bm{\varphi}\right).

We now use
\displaystyle\mathcal{D}\bm{\varphi}\in\bm{V}_{h}(\mathcal{D}^{+},\Omega)
to obtain (ii). Estimate (iii) follows from Theorem
2.6
.∎

Lemma 3.2
.

For each
\displaystyle\bm{u}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}(\mathcal{D},\Omega)
, there exist
\displaystyle\bm{\varphi}\in\bm{X}(\Omega)
and
\displaystyle\bm{\phi}_{h}\in V_{h}(\mathcal{D}^{-},\Omega)
such that

\bm{u}_{h}=\Pi_{h}^{\mathcal{D}}\bm{\varphi}+\mathcal{D}^{-}\bm{\phi}_{h},

(9)

with estimates

\|\bm{\varphi}\|_{\bm{X}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|\mathcal{D}\bm{u}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)},\quad\|\bm{\varphi}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|\bm{u}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)},

(10)

\text{and}\quad\|\Pi_{h}^{\mathcal{D}}\bm{\varphi}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\mathcal{D}^{-}\bm{\phi}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|\bm{u}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}.

(11)

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle\bm{u}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}(\mathcal{D},\Omega)
. According to Theorem
2.2
, there exists
\displaystyle\bm{\varphi}\in\bm{X}(\Omega)
such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\mathcal{D}\bm{\varphi}=\mathcal{D}\bm{u}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}(\mathcal{D}^{+},\Omega),\\
\|\bm{\varphi}\|_{\bm{X}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|\mathcal{D}\bm{u}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)},\\
\|\bm{\varphi}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|\bm{u}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}.\end{array}\right.

(12)

We now apply Lemma
3.1
and obtain

\mathcal{D}\bm{u}_{h}=\mathcal{D}\bm{\varphi}=\mathcal{D}\left(\Pi_{h}^{\mathcal{D}}\bm{\varphi}\right),

hence,

\bm{u}_{h}-\Pi_{h}^{\mathcal{D}}\bm{\varphi}\in\textbf{ker}\left(\mathcal{D}\mid_{\bm{V}_{h}(\mathcal{D},\Omega)}\right)=\mathcal{D}^{-}\left(V_{h}(\mathcal{D}^{-},\Omega)\right).

Therefore, there exists
\displaystyle\bm{\phi}_{h}\in V_{h}(\mathcal{D}^{-},\Omega)
such that
\displaystyle\bm{u}_{h}-\Pi_{h}^{\mathcal{D}}\bm{\varphi}=\mathcal{D}^{-}\bm{\phi}_{h}
, which yields (
9
). Property (
10
) follows from estimates in (
12
).

We now show (
11
). We write

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\Pi_{h}^{\mathcal{D}}\bm{\varphi}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\Pi_{h}^{\mathcal{D}}\bm{\varphi}-\bm{\varphi}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\bm{\varphi}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\displaystyle h\|\bm{\varphi}\|_{\bm{X}(\Omega)}+\|\bm{\varphi}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\displaystyle h\|\mathcal{D}\bm{u}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\bm{u}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)},

where in the last estimate we have used first and second estimates in (
12
). Moreover, using the inverse inequality

\|\mathcal{D}\bm{u}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim h^{-1}\|\bm{u}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)},

(13)

we get

\|\Pi_{h}^{\mathcal{D}}\bm{\varphi}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|\bm{u}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}.

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\mathcal{D}^{-}\bm{\phi}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\bm{u}_{h}-\Pi_{h}^{\mathcal{D}}\bm{\varphi}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\bm{u}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\Pi_{h}^{\mathcal{D}}\bm{\varphi}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|\bm{u}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)},

and inequality (
11
) is proved.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Every
\displaystyle\bm{\varphi}\in\bm{X}(\Omega)
admits a stable approximation
\displaystyle\bm{\varphi}_{h}\in\bm{X}_{h}(\Omega)
satisfying

\|\bm{\varphi}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|\bm{\varphi}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)},\quad\text{and}\quad h^{-1}\|\bm{\varphi}-\bm{\varphi}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\bm{\varphi}_{h}\|_{\bm{X}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|\bm{\varphi}\|_{\bm{X}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle\bm{\varphi}:=\left(\varphi^{1},\varphi^{2},\varphi^{3}\right)\in\bm{X}(\Omega)
and define

\bm{\varphi}_{h}=\left(\Pi_{h}^{\bm{grad}}\varphi^{1},\Pi_{h}^{\bm{grad}}\varphi^{2},\Pi_{h}^{\bm{grad}}\varphi^{3}\right)\in\bm{X}_{h}(\Omega).

According to Theorem
2.6
, we have:

\|\varphi^{k}-\Pi_{h}^{\bm{grad}}\varphi^{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|\varphi^{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\quad k=1,2,3,

(14)

\|\varphi^{k}-\Pi_{h}^{\bm{grad}}\varphi^{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim h\|\varphi^{k}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)},\quad k=1,2,3,

(15)

and

\|\varphi^{k}-\Pi_{h}^{\bm{grad}}\varphi^{k}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|\varphi^{k}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)},\quad k=1,2,3.

(16)

Using (

), we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\bm{\varphi}-\bm{\varphi}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\|\varphi^{1}-\Pi_{h}^{\bm{grad}}\varphi^{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\varphi^{2}-\Pi_{h}^{\bm{grad}}\varphi^{2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\varphi^{3}-\Pi_{h}^{\bm{grad}}\varphi^{3}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\displaystyle\tau>0
. Every
\displaystyle\bm{u}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}(\mathcal{D},\Omega)
has a decomposition

\bm{u}_{h}=\bm{w}_{h}+\Pi_{h}^{\mathcal{D}}\bm{\varphi}_{h}+\mathcal{D}^{-}\bm{\phi}_{h},

(17)

where
\displaystyle\bm{w}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}(\mathcal{D},\Omega)
,
\displaystyle\bm{\varphi}_{h}\in\bm{X}_{h}(\Omega)
and
\displaystyle\bm{\phi}_{h}\in V_{h}(\mathcal{D}^{-},\Omega)
with estimate

(h^{-2}+\tau)\,\|\bm{w}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\bm{\varphi}_{h}\|_{\bm{X}(\Omega)}^{2}+\tau\|\bm{\varphi}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\tau\|\mathcal{D}^{-}\bm{\phi}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\lesssim\|\bm{u}_{h}\|_{A_{\mathcal{D}}}^{2},

(18)

with notation

\|\bm{u}_{h}\|_{A_{\mathcal{D}}}^{2}=\|\mathcal{D}\bm{u}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\tau\|\bm{u}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle\bm{u}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}(\mathcal{D},\Omega)
. Using lemma (
3.2
) we can find

\displaystyle\bm{\varphi}\in\bm{X}(\Omega)
and
\displaystyle\bm{\phi}_{h}\in V_{h}(\mathcal{D}^{-},\Omega)
with properties

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\bm{u}_{h}=\Pi_{h}^{\mathcal{D}}\bm{\varphi}+\mathcal{D}^{-}\phi_{h}\\
\|\bm{\varphi}\|_{\bm{X}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|\mathcal{D}\bm{u}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\\
\|\bm{\varphi}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|\bm{u}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\\
\|\Pi_{h}^{\mathcal{D}}\bm{\varphi}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\mathcal{D}^{-}\bm{\phi}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|\bm{u}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)},\end{array}\right.

(19)

and let
\displaystyle\bm{\varphi}_{h}\in\bm{X}_{h}(\Omega)
be the stable approximation of
\displaystyle\bm{\varphi}
, given by Lemma
3.3
. We define

\bm{w}_{h}=\Pi_{h}^{\mathcal{D}}(\bm{\varphi}-\bm{\varphi}_{h}).

In this way, using the decomposition in (
19
), we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bm{u}_{h}=\Pi_{h}^{\mathcal{D}}\bm{\varphi}+\mathcal{D}^{-}\bm{\phi}_{h}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Pi_{h}^{\mathcal{D}}(\bm{\varphi}-\bm{\varphi}_{h})+\Pi_{h}^{\mathcal{D}}\bm{\varphi}_{h}+\mathcal{D}^{-}\bm{\phi}_{h}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bm{w}_{h}+\Pi_{h}^{\mathcal{D}}\bm{\varphi}_{h}+\mathcal{D}^{-}\bm{\phi}_{h},

and decomposition (
17
) is proved. In order to show (
18
), we need to perform careful estimates. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle h^{-1}\|\bm{w}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle h^{-1}\|\Pi_{h}^{\mathcal{D}}(\bm{\varphi}-\bm{\varphi}_{h})\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

(20)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\displaystyle h^{-1}\|\bm{\varphi}-\bm{\varphi}_{h})\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|\bm{\varphi}\|_{\bm{X}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|\mathcal{D}\bm{u}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)},

where in the last estimate we have used the first inequality in (
19
). Moreover, using the inverse inequality (
13
) we get

\|\bm{w}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim h\|\mathcal{D}\bm{u}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|\bm{u}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}.

(21)

Concerning the component
\displaystyle\bm{\varphi}_{h}
, we use the first and the second inequalities in (
19
) to obtain

\|\bm{\varphi}_{h}\|_{\bm{X}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|\bm{\varphi}\|_{\bm{X}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|\mathcal{D}\bm{u}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)},

(22)

and

\|\bm{\varphi}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|\bm{\varphi}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|\bm{u}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}.

(23)

Combining (
20
)–(
23
) together with third estimate in (
19
) we obtain the desired estimate (
18
). This complete the proof of the proposition.
∎

Proposition
3.4
forms the basis for applying the auxiliary space theory described in Section
2.2
. It offers a strategy for selecting suitable auxiliary spaces and projections, as discussed in the next subsection, and provides clear instructions for choosing a smoothing operator, which must satisfy the following condition:

s(\bm{w}_{h},\bm{w}_{h})\approx(h^{-2}+\tau)\,\|\bm{w}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Typically, a smoother such as Jacobi or Gauss-Seidel is used, which depends on the choice of the bases of the discrete spaces, similar to the multigrid method.

Next, we will improve estimate (
18
) to apply a Jacobi smoothing method, by first constructing suitable bases for the discrete spaces. We adopt the following set of basis functions, as proposed in
[
16
,
23
,
57
]
:

\mathcal{B}(\bm{grad})=\Big\{B_{i_{1},p_{1}}\otimes B_{i_{2},p_{2}}\otimes B_{i_{3},p_{3}}:\quad 1\leq i_{l}\leq n_{l},\quad l=1,2,3\Big\},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(\bm{curl})=\left\{(D_{i_{1},p_{1}-1}\otimes B_{i_{2},p_{2}}\otimes B_{i_{3},p_{3}})\bm{e}_{1}:\;1\leq i_{1}\leq n_{1}-1,\,1\leq i_{l}\leq n_{l},\,l=2,3\right\}

We will make use of the following
\displaystyle L^{2}
-stability of spline basis functions (see

).

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\displaystyle(B_{i,p})_{1\leq i\leq n}
and
\displaystyle(D_{j,p})_{1\leq j\leq n-1}
denote, respectively, the
\displaystyle B
-spline and the Curry–Schoenberg spline bases associated to knot vector
\displaystyle T
. Then, we have

h\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}^{2}\approx\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}B_{i,p}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2},\quad\quad\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}d_{j}^{2}\approx\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}d_{j}D_{j,p-1}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2},

for all vectors
\displaystyle(b_{1},\cdots,b_{n})
and
\displaystyle(d_{1},\cdots,d_{n-1})
.

In particular,

\left\|B_{i,p}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\approx h,\quad\left\|D_{j,p-1}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\approx 1,\quad\forall 1\leq i\leq n,\quad\forall 1\leq j\leq n-1.

A direct consequence of this theorem is the following stability result:

Corollary 3.6
.

The bases
\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(D)
are
\displaystyle L^{2}
-stables, i.e

\left\|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\\
\bm{v}_{r}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{D})\end{subarray}}c_{r}\,\bm{v}_{r}\right\|^{2}_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\approx\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\\
\bm{v}_{r}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{D})\end{subarray}}c_{r}^{2}\left\|\bm{v}_{r}\right\|^{2}_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)},

(24)

for any vector
\displaystyle(c_{r})\in\mathbb{R}^{\#\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{D})}
.

Proof.

From Theorem
3.5
we deduce that for all vectors
\displaystyle(b_{1},\cdots,b_{n})
and
\displaystyle(d_{1},\cdots,d_{n-1})
we have

\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}^{2}\left\|B_{i,p}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\approx h\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}^{2}\approx\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}B_{i,p}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2},

and

\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}d_{j}^{2}\left\|D_{j,p-1}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\approx\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}d_{j}^{2}\approx\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}d_{j}D_{j,p-1}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}.

The bound (
24
) follows by tensorization and applying last estimates on each coordinate.
∎

We prove the following stable decomposition result.

Theorem 3.7

(Hitpmair-Xu decomposition)
.

Let
\displaystyle\tau>0
. For each
\displaystyle\bm{u}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}(\mathcal{D},\Omega)
, there exit
\displaystyle\bm{w}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}(\mathcal{D},\Omega)
,
\displaystyle\bm{\varphi}_{h}\in\bm{X}_{h}(\Omega)
and
\displaystyle\bm{\phi}_{h}\in V_{h}(\mathcal{D}^{-},\Omega)
such that

\bm{u}_{h}=\bm{w}_{h}+\Pi_{h}^{\mathcal{D}}\bm{\varphi}_{h}+\mathcal{D}^{-}\bm{\phi}_{h}.

(25)

In addition, expanding the component
\displaystyle\bm{w}_{h}
on the basis
\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{D})

\bm{w}_{h}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\\
\bm{v}_{r}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{D})\end{subarray}}c_{r}\,\bm{v}_{r},

we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\\
\bm{v}_{r}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{D})\end{subarray}}c_{r}^{2}\left\|\bm{v}_{r}\right\|^{2}_{A_{\mathcal{D}}}+\|\bm{\varphi}_{h}\|_{\bm{X}(\Omega)}^{2}+\tau\|\bm{\varphi}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\mathcal{D}^{-}\phi_{h}\|_{A_{\mathcal{D}}}^{2}\lesssim\|\bm{u}_{h}\|_{A_{\mathcal{D}}}^{2}.

(26)

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle\bm{w}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}(\mathcal{D},\Omega)
,
\displaystyle\bm{\varphi}_{h}\in\bm{X}_{h}(\Omega)
, and
\displaystyle\bm{\phi}_{h}\in V_{h}(\mathcal{D}^{-},\Omega)
the components given by Proposition
3.4
. By remaking that

\|\mathcal{D}^{-}\bm{\phi}_{h}\|_{A_{\mathcal{D}}}^{2}=\|\mathcal{D}\,\left(\mathcal{D}^{-}\bm{\phi}_{h}\right)\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\tau\,\|\mathcal{D}^{-}\bm{\phi}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\tau\|\mathcal{D}^{-}\bm{\phi}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

we need only to estimate the first term in (
26
). Using (
24
) we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\\
\bm{v}_{r}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{D})\end{subarray}}c_{r}^{2}\left\|\bm{v}_{r}\right\|^{2}_{A_{\mathcal{D}}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\\
\bm{v}_{r}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{D})\end{subarray}}c_{r}^{2}\left\|\mathcal{D}\bm{v}_{r}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\tau\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\\
\bm{v}_{r}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{D})\end{subarray}}c_{r}^{2}\left\|\bm{v}_{r}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\displaystyle(h^{-2}+\tau)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\\
\bm{v}_{r}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{D})\end{subarray}}c_{r}^{2}\left\|\bm{v}_{r}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\displaystyle(h^{-2}+\tau)\left\|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\\
\bm{v}_{r}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{D})\end{subarray}}c_{r}\,\bm{v}_{r}\right\|^{2}_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle(h^{-2}+\tau)\|\bm{w}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)},

and we conclude the proof using (
18
).

∎

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}=div

3.7
in the case of the

Corollary 3.8

(Hitpmair-Xu decomposition for
\displaystyle\bm{H}(div,\Omega)
)
.

Let
\displaystyle\tau>0
. For each
\displaystyle\bm{u}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}(div,\Omega)
, there exit
\displaystyle\bm{w}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}(div,\Omega)

\displaystyle\bm{z}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}(\bm{curl},\Omega)
and
\displaystyle(\bm{\varphi}_{h},\bm{\psi}_{h})\in\bm{X}_{h}(\Omega)^{2}
such that

\bm{u}_{h}=\bm{w}_{h}+\Pi_{h}^{div}\bm{\varphi}_{h}+\bm{curl}\,\bm{z}_{h}+\bm{curl}\,\bm{\psi}_{h}.

(27)

In addition, expanding the components
\displaystyle\bm{w}_{h}
and
\displaystyle\bm{z}_{h}
on the bases
\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(div)
and
\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(\bm{curl})
, respectively,

\bm{w}_{h}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\\
\bm{v}_{r}\in\mathcal{B}(div)\end{subarray}}c_{r}\,\bm{v}_{r},\quad\bm{z}_{h}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\\
\bm{v}_{r}\in\mathcal{B}(\bm{curl})\end{subarray}}d_{r}\,\bm{v}_{r},

we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\\
\bm{v}_{r}\in\mathcal{B}(div)\end{subarray}}c_{r}^{2}\left\|\bm{v}_{r}\right\|^{2}_{A_{div}}+\|\bm{\varphi}_{h}\|_{\bm{X}(\Omega)}^{2}+\tau\|\bm{\varphi}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

(28)

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,1,1}-----}+\tau\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\\
\bm{v}_{r}\in\mathcal{B}(\bm{curl})\end{subarray}}d_{r}^{2}\left\|\bm{curl}\,\bm{v}_{r}\right\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\tau\left\|\bm{\psi}_{h}\right\|_{\bm{X}(\Omega)}^{2}\lesssim\|\bm{u}_{h}\|_{A_{div}}^{2},

Proof.

Using Theorem
3.7
, we can find
\displaystyle\bm{w}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}(div,\Omega)
,
\displaystyle\bm{\varphi}_{h}\in\bm{X}_{h}(\Omega)
and
\displaystyle\bm{\phi}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}(\bm{curl},\Omega)
such that

\bm{u}_{h}=\bm{w}_{h}+\Pi_{h}^{div}\bm{\varphi}_{h}+\bm{curl}\,\bm{\phi}_{h},

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\\
\bm{v}_{r}\in\mathcal{B}(div)\end{subarray}}c_{r}^{2}\left\|\bm{v}_{r}\right\|^{2}_{A_{div}}+\|\bm{\varphi}_{h}\|_{\bm{X}(\Omega)}^{2}+\tau\|\bm{\varphi}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\bm{curl}\,\phi_{h}\|_{A_{div}}^{2}\lesssim\|\bm{u}_{h}\|_{A_{div}}^{2}.

Using however Proposition
3.4
, there exist

\displaystyle\bm{z}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}(\bm{curl},\Omega)
,
\displaystyle\bm{\psi}_{h}\in\bm{X}_{h}(\Omega)
and
\displaystyle\Psi_{h}\in V_{h}(\bm{grad},\Omega)
such that

with estimate

(30)

It follows that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bm{u}_{h}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bm{w}_{h}+\Pi_{h}^{div}\bm{\varphi}_{h}+\bm{curl}\,\bm{z}_{h}+\bm{curl}\left(\Pi_{h}^{\bm{curl}}\bm{\psi}_{h}\right)+\bm{curl}\,\bm{grad}\,\Psi_{h}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bm{w}_{h}+\Pi_{h}^{div}\bm{\varphi}_{h}+\bm{curl}\,\bm{z}_{h}+\bm{curl}\,\bm{\psi}_{h},

where in the last equality we have used the fact that
\displaystyle\bm{curl}\left(\Pi_{h}^{\bm{curl}}\bm{\psi}_{h}\right)=\bm{curl}\,\bm{\psi}_{h}
and
\displaystyle\bm{curl}\,\bm{grad}\,\Psi_{h}=0

We now prove bound (

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\\
\bm{v}_{r}\in\mathcal{B}(div)\end{subarray}}c_{r}^{2}\left\|\bm{v}_{r}\right\|^{2}_{A_{div}}+\|\bm{\varphi}_{h}\|_{\bm{X}(\Omega)}^{2}+\tau\|\bm{\varphi}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\tau\|\bm{curl}\,\phi_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\lesssim\|\bm{u}_{h}\|_{A_{div}}^{2}.

(31)

But, using (
30

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\\
\bm{v}_{r}\in\mathcal{B}(\bm{curl})\end{subarray}}d_{r}^{2}\left\|\bm{curl}\,\bm{v}_{r}\right\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\bm{\psi}_{h}\right\|_{\bm{X}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\displaystyle h^{-2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\\
\bm{v}_{r}\in\mathcal{B}(\bm{curl})\end{subarray}}d_{r}^{2}\left\|\bm{v}_{r}\right\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\bm{\psi}_{h}\right\|_{\bm{X}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\displaystyle h^{-2}\left\|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\\

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle h^{-2}\|\bm{z}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\bm{\psi}_{h}\right\|_{\bm{X}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\bm{curl}\,\bm{\phi}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

and we conclude the proof combining this last estimate together with (

Following the same notations of Section

, let us consider
\displaystyle V=\bm{V}_{h}(\bm{curl},\Omega)
equipped with the bilinear form

for

and

\displaystyle\bm{X}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\bm{X}_{h}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\bm{X}_{h}(\Omega)

Remark 3.9
.

In two dimensions, we have two distinct curl operators: the scalar curl operator, defined as
\displaystyle curl\,\bm{u}=\frac{\partial u_{1}}{\partial x_{2}}-\frac{\partial u_{2}}{\partial x_{1}}
, and the vector
\displaystyle\bm{curl}
operator, defined as
\displaystyle\bm{curl}\,u=\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{2}},-\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{1}}\right)
. The present analysis refers to the scalar
\displaystyle curl
operator. However, the vector
\displaystyle\bm{curl}
operator is used in the preconditioning of the two-dimensional
\displaystyle\bm{H}(div,\Omega)
problem addressed in the following subsection.

The following fundamental result demonstrates the mesh-independence of the preconditioner
\displaystyle\bm{B}_{\bm{curl}}
, at least when a Jacobi smoothing scheme is used. This result is a direct consequence of Theorem
3.7
and Theorem
2.3
.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
\displaystyle\tau>0
and suppose that the smoothing operator
\displaystyle s
is given by (
32
). Then, the spectral condition number
\displaystyle\kappa\left(\bm{B}_{\bm{curl}}\bm{A}_{\bm{curl}}\right)
is bounded, with respect to
\displaystyle h
and
\displaystyle\tau
.

Proof.

We will verify the assumptions of Theorem
2.3
. To do so, we use Theorem
2.6
and the following estimate (found, for instance, in
[
14
]
):

\|\Pi_{h}^{\bm{curl}}\bm{w}_{h}\|_{\bm{H}(\bm{curl},\Omega)}\lesssim\|\bm{w}_{h}\|_{\bm{H}(\bm{curl},\Omega)},\quad w_{h}\in W_{1}.

From this estimate, we can see that the first inequality in (i) holds with a constant
\displaystyle\beta_{1}
that is independent of
\displaystyle h
. The second inequality in (i) holds with a constant
\displaystyle\beta_{2}=1
, which is a consequence of the relation
\displaystyle\bm{curl}\circ\bm{grad}=0
.

To prove the inequality in (ii), we express any
\displaystyle\bm{w}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}(\bm{curl},\Omega)
as

\bm{w}_{h}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{r}\\
\bm{v}_{\bm{r}}\in\mathcal{B}(\bm{curl})\end{subarray}}c_{\bm{r}}\,\bm{v}_{\bm{r}}.

We have

\displaystyle\displaystyle a(\bm{w}_{h},\bm{w}_{h})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\bm{curl}\,\bm{w}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\tau\|\bm{w}_{h}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{r}\\
\bm{v}_{\bm{r}}\in\mathcal{B}(\bm{curl})\end{subarray}}c_{\bm{r}}\,\bm{curl}\,\bm{v}_{\bm{r}}\right\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\tau\left\|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{r}\\
\bm{v}_{\bm{r}}\in\mathcal{B}(\bm{curl})\end{subarray}}c_{\bm{r}}\,\bm{v}_{\bm{r}}\right\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{\mathcal{Q}\in\mathcal{Q}_{h}}\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{K}c_{k}\,\bm{curl}\,\bm{v}_{k}\right\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\tau\sum_{\mathcal{Q}\in\mathcal{Q}_{h}}\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{K}c_{k}\,\bm{v}_{k}\right\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle K\sum_{\mathcal{Q}\in\mathcal{Q}_{h}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}c_{k}^{2}\|\bm{curl}\,\bm{v}_{k}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\tau K\sum_{\mathcal{Q}\in\mathcal{Q}_{h}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}c_{k}^{2}\|\bm{v}_{k}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle K\,s(\bm{w}_{h},\bm{w}_{h}),

where
\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{h}
denotes the parametric Bézier mesh,
\displaystyle Q
a generic Bézier element and the constant
\displaystyle K
is the number of basis functions whose support interacts with
\displaystyle Q
. The constant
\displaystyle K
depends only on the degrees of the spline bases.

Finally, the last assumption (iii) follows from Theorem
3.7
.

∎

5
Conclusions

In this work, we have proposed the use of the Auxiliary Space Preconditioning method (ASP) as a preconditioning strategy for linear
\displaystyle\bm{H}(\bm{curl},\Omega)
and
\displaystyle\bm{H}(div,\Omega)
elliptic problems in Isogeometric Analysis (IgA). Our contributions include a uniform, stable, and regular decomposition of the IgA discrete spaces, and a proof of the mesh-independent effectiveness of the preconditioners using abstract theory by R. Hiptmair and J. Xu
[
40
]
. Our numerical simulations, conducted in two and three spatial dimensions, have confirmed the practical usefulness of our approach. Specifically, we have shown that our preconditioner significantly reduces the spectral condition number and that the number of conjugate gradient iterations required for convergence is independent of the discretization parameter. Additionally, we have demonstrated that the resulting algorithm can be easily extended to a
\displaystyle p
-stable algorithm. Our results suggest that the proposed preconditioning method is a promising candidate for future applications in isogeometric analysis.

We conclude this paper by presenting some future research perspectives selected from several possible ones. First, our results only apply to a parametric domain. Therefore, extending our method to more general physical domains is currently under development and will be the subject of future work. Another research direction involves extending our results to the case of variant coefficients, which is a wide-open subject and of great interest to investigate. Finally, our numerical tests indicate that the ASP-GLT algorithm has been highly effective. Therefore, a theoretical study of this algorithm would be beneficial, particularly with respect to the choice of solver in the GLT smoothing step, which needs more investigation.

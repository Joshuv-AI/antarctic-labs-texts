Explicit Low-Bandwidth Evaluation Schemes for Weighted Sums of Reed-Solomon-Coded Symbols
Han Mao Kiah, Wilton Kim, Stanislav Kruglik, San Ling, Huaxiong Wang School of Physical and Mathematical Sciences, Nanyang Technological University, Singapore email: {hmkiah, wilt0002, stanislav.kruglik, lingsan, hxwang}@ntu.edu.sg

Abstract
Motivated by applications in distributed storage, distributed computing, and homomorphic secret sharing, we study communication-efficient schemes for computing linear combinations of coded symbols. Specifically, we design low-bandwidth schemes that evaluate the weighted sum of ℓ \ell coded symbols in a codeword 𝒄 ∈ 𝔽 n \boldsymbol{c}\in\mathbb{F}^{n} , when we are given access to d d of the remaining components in 𝒄 \boldsymbol{c} . Formally, suppose that 𝔽 \mathbb{F} is a field extension of 𝔹 \mathbb{B} of degree t t . Let 𝒄 \boldsymbol{c} be a codeword in a Reed-Solomon code of dimension k k and our task is to compute the weighted sum of ℓ \ell coded symbols. In this paper, for some s < t s<t , we provide an explicit scheme that performs this task by downloading d ⁡ ( t − s ) d(t-s) sub-symbols in 𝔹 \mathbb{B} from d d available nodes, whenever d ≥ ℓ ​ | 𝔹 | s − ℓ + k d\geq\ell|\mathbb{B}|^{s}-\ell+k . In many cases, our scheme outperforms previous schemes in the literature. Furthermore, we provide a characterization of evaluation schemes for general linear codes. Then in the special case of Reed-Solomon codes, we use this characterization to derive a lower bound for the evaluation bandwidth.



1
Introduction

As applications in distributed storage and distributed computation become more prevalent,
communication bandwidth
has become a critical performance metric of codes.
In such distributed settings, data is represented as a vector
{\boldsymbol{x}}=\mathbb{F}^{k}
for some finite field
\mathbb{F}
.
To protect against
erasures
, the data is then encoded into a codeword
\boldsymbol{c}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})\in\mathbb{F}^{n}
and each codeword symbol
c_{i}
is stored in one of
n
nodes or servers.
The beautiful
Reed-Solomon
(RS) code
[
RS60
]
allows one to recover
{\boldsymbol{x}}
by accessing any available
k
nodes. In other words, even if
n-k
nodes fail, we are able to recover the data. Unfortunately, this solution is not ideal in the scenario where only one node fails.
In this case, to repair a single node failure, i.e. to recover one codeword symbol, we download
k
codeword symbols. This is costly as
k
is usually much greater than one!
Typically, the total amount of information downloaded is referred to as the
repair bandwidth
and the repair bandwidth problem was first introduced in
[
DGW+10
]
. Since then, there has been a flurry of research to design new codes that reduce this repair bandwidth
[
BKV+18
,
LO18
]
.
Of interest to this paper is the pioneering work of Guruswami and Wootters
[
GW17
]
, where the authors revisit the ubiquitous Reed-Solomon codes and demonstrate that the repair bandwidth can be reduced dramatically when more than
k
nodes are available.
Later on, their methods were extended in a variety of scenarios, including multiple erasures and different parameter regimes
[
GW17
,
DM17
,
DD17
,
DDKM18
,
DDK+21
,
SW22
,
CT22
,
BBD+22
]
.

In this paper, we consider the case where any
\ell
nodes are unavailable.
Instead of recovering the contents of these
\ell
nodes, we recover a
weighted sum
or linear combination of them.
This is motivated by applications in distributed computation.
Specifically, in this setting, we have a computation task
{\cal T}
that is split into
\ell
smaller subtasks
{\cal T}_{1},{\cal T}_{2},\ldots,{\cal T}_{\ell}
, and we distribute these subtasks to
\ell
servers to compute locally.
However, if there is a straggler among these
\ell
servers, that is, a server fails to complete its subtask, we are unable to determine
{\cal T}
.
To mitigate this
straggler
problem, we employ coding and use
n>\ell
servers.
Specifically, we design a code over finite field
\mathbb{F}
and
n-\ell
additional subtasks
{\cal T}_{\ell+1},{\cal T}_{\ell+2},\ldots,{\cal T}_{n}
such that the following holds for any codeword
\boldsymbol{c}=(c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{n})
: we have
c_{i}
corresponds to the result of subtask
{\cal T}_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
.
As before, we distribute these
n
codeword symbols to
n
servers.
If the code is chosen to be some code that corrects
n-d
erasures, then we are able to determine
c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{\ell}
as long as we have the results of any
d
nodes.
Typically, for most computation tasks, the result of
{\cal T}
is obtained from a weighted sum of
c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{\ell}
.
Hence, the result of
{\cal T}
is
one
symbol in
\mathbb{F}
.
As before, we see that it is rather wasteful if we simply download
d
symbols from the
d
available nodes. Therefore, our central task is to reduce this amount of downloaded information, which we refer to as
evaluation bandwidth
.

1.2
Our Contributions

Therefore, our task can be stated as follows. Consider a codeword
\boldsymbol{c}=(c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{n})
of a Reed-Solomon code of length
n
and dimension
k
.
Without loss of generality, we consider the first
\ell
positions and fix
\ell
nonzero coefficients
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\ldots,\kappa_{\ell}\in\mathbb{F}
.
Then given any
d\leq n
nodes, our objective is to design schemes that evaluate the sum
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathsf{S}}\triangleq\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\kappa_{i}c_{i}
by downloading as little information as possible from these
d
nodes.
For ease of exposition, we assume that these
d
nodes are distinct from the nodes containing
c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{\ell}
.

(A)

Explicit low-bandwidth evaluation schemes for Reed-Solomon codes
.
In Section
3
, we provide two explicit schemes to recover
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathsf{S}}=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\kappa_{i}c_{i}
from some
d
available nodes. We explicitly formulate the information to be downloaded in terms of
trace polynomials
and
subspace polynomials
.
While our methods build upon the frameworks of
[
GW17
,
DDKM18
,
DDK+21
]
, a key feature of our schemes is that they can be adjusted according to the parameters:

\ell
(the number of coded symbols in the sum
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathsf{S}}
) and

d
(the number of available nodes).
As expected, the evaluation bandwidth of our schemes is smaller when
\ell
is smaller, or when
d
is bigger. Specifically, the evaluation bandwidths of the two schemes are as follows.

\bullet

Scheme 1
: Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field extension of
\mathbb{B}
of degree
t
. Whenever
d\geq\ell|\mathbb{B}|^{t-1}-\ell+k
, we can recover
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathsf{S}}
by downloading
d
subsymbols (in
\mathbb{B}
), or
d\lceil\log_{2}|\mathbb{B}|\rceil
bits, from any
d
available nodes.

\bullet

Scheme 2
: Suppose further that
W=\{0,w_{1},\ldots,w_{|\mathbb{B}|^{s}-1}\}
is a
\mathbb{B}
-linear subspace of
\mathbb{F}
with dimension
s
. Whenever
d\geq\ell|\mathbb{B}|^{s}-\ell+k
, we can recover
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathsf{S}}
by downloading
d(t-s)
subsymbols (in
\mathbb{B}
), or
d(t-s)\lceil\log_{2}|\mathbb{B}|\rceil
bits, from any
d
available nodes.

(B)

Lower bound for the evaluation bandwidth for Reed-Solomon codes
. By modifying the techniques in
[
MBW19
]
and
[
LWJ19
]
, we provide a characterization of
\mathbb{B}
-linear evaluation schemes for general linear codes.
Then in the special case of Reed-Solomon codes, we use this characterization to determine the required evaluation bandwidth. As with our evaluation schemes, the lower bound depends on both
\ell
and
d
. In the case where
\ell=1
and
d=n-1
, we recover the bounds obtained in
[
GW17
,
DM17
]
. Unfortunately, the bound is not tight in general (see Figures
1
and
2
, and Tables
1
and
2
).
Nevertheless, this is the first nontrivial
\ell
- and
d
-dependent bound, and is also a lower bound on the repair bandwidth of multiple erasures (studied in
[
DDKM18
,
MBW19
]
).

Preliminaries

Let
[n]
denote the set of integers
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
and let
\mathbb{F}
denote a finite field.
An
\mathbb{F}
-linear code

[n,k]
code
\mathcal{C}
is an
\mathbb{F}
-linear subspace of
dimension

k
. Each element of
\cal C
is called a
codeword
.
The orthogonal complement to
\mathcal{C}
is called the
dual code

\mathcal{C}^{\perp}
.
Then by definition, for each
\boldsymbol{c}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})\in\mathcal{C}
and
\boldsymbol{c}^{\perp}=(c_{1}^{\perp},\ldots,c_{n}^{\perp})\in\mathcal{C}^{\perp}
, we have that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}c_{i}^{\perp}=0
.

Of interest to this paper is the ubiquitous Reed-Solomon codes
[
RS60
,
DNNM+22
]
.

Definition 1
.

Let
\mathbb{F}[x]
denote the ring of polynomials over
\mathbb{F}
. The
Reed-Solomon code

{\rm RS}(\mathcal{W},k)
of dimension
k
with evaluation points
\mathcal{W}=\{\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{n}\}\subseteq\mathbb{F}
and code rate
R=\frac{k}{n}

is defined as:

{\rm RS}(\mathcal{W},k)=\left\{(f(\omega_{1}),\ldots,f(\omega_{n})):f\in\mathbb{F}[x],\,\deg(f)<k\right\}.

On the other hand, the
generalized Reed-Solomon code

{\rm GRS}(\mathcal{W},k,\boldsymbol{\lambda})
of dimension
k
with evaluation points
\mathcal{W}
and multiplier
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in\mathbb{F}^{n}
, where
\lambda_{i}\neq 0
for all
i\in[n]
, is defined as:

{\rm GRS}(\mathcal{W},k,\boldsymbol{\lambda})=\left\{(\lambda_{1}f(\omega_{1}),\ldots,\lambda_{n}f(\omega_{n})):f\in\mathbb{F}[x],\,\text{deg}(f)<k\right\}.

Clearly, the generalized Reed-Solomon code
{\rm GRS}(\mathcal{W},k,\boldsymbol{\lambda})
with multiplier vector
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(1,\ldots,1)
is a Reed-Solomon code
\textrm{RS}(\mathcal{W},k)
with the same set of evaluation points and dimension. Another important fact is that the dual of
\textrm{RS}(\mathcal{W},k)
is
\textrm{GRS}(\mathcal{W},n-k,\boldsymbol{\lambda})
, where the multiplier vector can be explicitly given as (see, for example
[
MS77
, Ch. 10, 12]
)

\lambda_{i}^{-1}=\prod_{j\in[n]\setminus\{i\}}(\omega_{i}-\omega_{j}).

(1)

For convenience, when it is clear from context,
f(x)
denotes a polynomial of degree at most
k-1
corresponding to a codeword in
{\rm RS}(\mathcal{W},k)
,
while
r(x)
denotes a polynomial of degree at most
n-k-1
corresponding to a dual codeword from
{\rm GRS}(\mathcal{W},n-k,\boldsymbol{\lambda})
.
Our low-bandwidth schemes rely on the existence of low-degree polynomials
r(x)
and the following relation

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}f(\omega_{i})r(\omega_{i})=0.

(2)

In what follows, we refer to polynomials
r(x)
as
parity-check polynomials
for
\mathcal{C}
. Of interest, we employ
trace polynomials
and
subspace polynomials
. Formally, we describe our evaluation framework in the next subsection.

2.1
Trace Recovery Framework

Suppose that
\mathbb{F}
is a field extension of
\mathbb{B}
of degree
t>1
.
Throughout this paper, we refer to the elements of
\mathbb{F}
as
symbols
and
the elements of
\mathbb{B}
as
sub-symbols
. We also denote the nonzero elements of
\mathbb{F}
as
\mathbb{F}_{*}
.
As mentioned in the introduction, a remarkable property of Reed-Solomon codes is the following: for any Reed-Solomon code of dimension
k
, we can reconstruct the entire word
\boldsymbol{c}
by accessing any
k
symbols in
\boldsymbol{c}
.
This is also known as the
maximum distance separable (MDS)
property.

Fix
\ell\geq 1
and some
\ell
-tuple
\boldsymbol{\kappa}\in\mathbb{F}_{*}^{\ell}
.
In this work, we are interested in recovering the weighted sum
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathsf{S}}=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\kappa_{i}
by accessing some
d\geq k
available nodes. The classical approach downloads
k
symbols from any
k
-subset of the
d
available nodes. Over
\mathbb{B}
, the number of sub-symbols downloaded is
kt
and the question we want to address is: can we download less than
kt
sub-symbols, especially when
d
is much greater than
k
?

Let us formally define a weighted-sum evaluation scheme for general
\mathbb{F}
-linear code.

Definition 2
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a degree-
t
field extension of
\mathbb{B}
.
Let
\ell
and
d
be integers and
\mathcal{C}
be an
\mathbb{F}
-linear code of length
\ell+d
.
We fix a set of coefficients
\boldsymbol{\kappa}\in\mathbb{F}_{*}^{\ell}
.
A
(\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mathcal{C})
-
weighted-sum evaluation scheme
or shortly
(\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mathcal{C})
-
evaluation scheme
is defined to be a collection of
d
functions

\displaystyle g_{j}\;:\;\mathbb{F}\to\mathbb{B}^{b_{j}}\text{ for }j\in[d]

(3)

such that for all codewords
\boldsymbol{c}\in\mathcal{C}
, the weighted sum
\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\kappa_{i}c_{i}
can be computed using values in the set
\{g_{j}(c_{\ell+j})\,:\,j\in[d]\}
.
The
evaluation bandwidth

b
is given by
\sum_{j=1}^{d}b_{j}
.
If the functions
g_{j}(j\in[d])
along with the function that determines the weighted sum
\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\kappa_{i}c_{i}
are all
\mathbb{B}
-linear, we say that the
(\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mathcal{C})
-evaluation scheme is
\mathbb{B}
-linear.

In this work, we limit our attention to
\mathbb{B}
-linear evaluation schemes only.
To this end, we consider the
trace function

{\rm Tr}:\mathbb{F}\to\mathbb{B}
defined as follows:

{\rm Tr}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}x^{|\mathbb{B}|^{i}}\text{ for }x\in\mathbb{F}.

(4)

It is straightforward to see that the trace function is
\mathbb{B}
-linear.
Furthermore,
{\rm Tr}(x)
is a polynomial in
x
with degree
|\mathbb{B}|^{t-1}
.
Hence, in this paper, we also refer to
{\rm Tr}(x)
as a
trace polynomial
.
We also refer the interested reader to Lidl and Niedderreiter
[
LN96
, Ch. 2]
for an in-depth overview of the properties of the trace function. Actually, one crucial fact is that all
\mathbb{B}
-linear maps defined over
\mathbb{F}
can be described in terms of trace functions.

Thus far, we have a function that maps symbols in
\mathbb{F}
to sub-symbols in
\mathbb{B}
.
Next, we describe a procedure to recover a symbol in
\mathbb{F}
using sub-symbols in
\mathbb{B}
.
If we view
\mathbb{F}
as a
\mathbb{B}
-linear subspace of dimension
t
, we can define a
\mathbb{B}
-basis
\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{t}\}
for
\mathbb{F}
.
Furthermore, there exists a
trace-dual basis

\{\widetilde{u}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{u}_{t}\}
for
\mathbb{F}
such that
{\rm Tr}(u_{i}\widetilde{u}_{j})=1
if
i=j
, and
{\rm Tr}(u_{i}\widetilde{u}_{j})=0
, otherwise. The following result plays a crucial role in our evaluation framework.

Proposition 1
.

[
LN96
, Ch. 2]

Let
\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{t}\}
be a
\mathbb{B}
-basis of
\mathbb{F}
. Then there exists a trace-dual basis
\{\widetilde{u}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{u}_{t}\}
and we can write each element

as

x=\sum_{i=1}^{t}{\rm Tr}(u_{i}x)\widetilde{u}_{i}.

Hence, following
[
GW17
]
, our strategy to evaluate
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathsf{S}}
is to compute

{\rm Tr}(u_{i}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathsf{S}})\,(i\in[t])
by utilizing as little symbols in
\mathbb{B}
as possible.

2.2
Related Work

As mentioned earlier, our problem of interest bears similarities with certain previous work.
Namely, one can obtain the evaluation of a weighted sum by employing a scheme that either
repairs multiple erasures
or
evaluates a weighted sum of all data symbols
.
Hence, in this subsection, we review state-of-the-art schemes for these scenarios and reformulate their results in terms of
(\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mathcal{C})
-evaluation schemes.

•

Low-bandwidth repair of multiple erasures
.
Low-bandwidth repair for Reed-Solomon codes was initiated by the pioneering paper of Guruswami and Wootters
[
GW17
]
. In this work, Guruswami and Wootters introduced the trace repair framework and designed an optimal repair schemes for the case of single erasure. Specifically, they used trace polynomials as parity-check polynomials and formed equations to determine the traces to download.

Later, in place of trace polynomials, Dau and Milenkovic studied the use of subspace polynomials as parity-check polynomials and designed another class of optimal repair schemes
[
DM17
]
. This setup was then generalized to the case of two and three erasures in centralized and decentralized repair models in papers
[
DDKM18
,
DDK+21
,
MBW19
]
.
The trade-off between the sub-packetization and bandwidth for multiple erasures was investigated in
[
LWJ19
,
CV21
]
.
Herein, we focus on the case of small sub-packetization level (of the order
\log(n)
).

We also do not impose restrictions on the set of evaluation points and can choose them to be any set of specific size. This is noticeably different from related secret-sharing schemes in which each secret is represented by a single field symbol (see, for example,
[
DLWX22
]
and the references wherein).
Specifically, in
[
DLWX22
]
, the evaluation points, which correspond to secrets, are required to satisfy certain algebraic properties.

Now, suppose that we have a low-bandwidth scheme that repairs
\ell
erasures by downloading information from any
d
available nodes.
Then for
\boldsymbol{\kappa}\in\mathbb{F}_{*}^{\ell}
, we can first use the repair scheme to recover the coded symbols
c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{\ell}
and then compute the weighted sum
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathsf{S}}=\sum_{i\in[\ell]}\kappa_{i}c_{i}
.
Therefore, we obtain an
(\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mathcal{C})
-evaluation scheme with the same bandwidth.
In the following theorem, we summarize the state-of-the-art results for low-bandwidth repair of multiple erasures.

Theorem 2

(
[
MBW19
, Theorems 2 and 3]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{A}
be two disjoint subsets of distinct points in
\mathbb{F}
with
|\mathcal{B}|=\ell
and
|\mathcal{A}|=d
and let
\boldsymbol{\kappa}\in\mathbb{F}_{*}^{\ell}
.
For
\ell\leq k\leq\ell+d

\mathcal{C}
be the Reed-Solomon code
{\rm RS}(\mathcal{B}\cup\mathcal{A},k)

Suppose that
\mathbb{F}
is a field extension of

with degree

(i)

If
s\leq t

-evaluation scheme with bandwidth

d(t-s)\left\lceil\log_{2}|\mathbb{B}|\right\rceil

(ii)

If

Remark 1
.

Of interest are two parameter regimes for the repair of MDS codes that depends on the degree of field extension

.
The first one, more commonly studied for regenerating codes, is when

is significantly larger than

. In this regime, we have the famous cut-set (lower) bound
[

on repair bandwidth and there are codes attaining it for one or several erasures

Another regime, more natural for practical settings, is when

In our work, we focus on this parameter regime, that is,

.
Recently, Lenz

(\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mathcal{C})
-evaluation scheme with the same bandwidth. The following theorem formalizes such a result. We defer the explanation on why

Communication-Efficient Secret Sharing Schemes.

In secret sharing, shares of a secret
S
is distributed to
d
participants
so that the secret can be recovered with a certain amount
k
of shares.
Secret sharing scheme was first proposed independently by Shamir
[
Sha79
]
and Blakley
[
BLA79
]
.
Shamir’s scheme
[
Sha79
]
is essentially a Reed-Solomon code.
Here, a secret polynomial of degree at most
k-1
whose constant term is
S
is chosen.
Then the polynomial of degree is evaluated at
d
distinct nonzero points and distributed to the participants.
Using polynomial properties, we see that
k
participants can recover
S
via interpolation,
while any
k-1
participants cannot obtain any information on
S
.
In
[
BM85
]
, this notion of
ramp schemes
was introduced.
Here, we have two thresholds are
k
and
k^{\prime}
: any
k
participants can recover the secret
S
,
while any subset of at most
k^{\prime}
participants obtain no information about
S
.
Many research has been done to improve ramp scheme, for instance, to construct a communication efficient ramp scheme (see
[
HLKB16
,
HB17
,
WW08
]
),
or to prevent partial information from being recovered explicitly (see
[
IY06
,
Yam86
,
Mat15
,
EK20
]
).

Another extension is known as
Homomorphic Secret Sharing
,
which aims to evaluate a function
F
of the secrets
s_{1},\ldots,s_{N}
from
N
clients
[
BGI16
,
BGI+18
]
.
Similar to
[
SW22
]
, we can employ our schemes for single-client HSS (when
N=1
) with secret
S=\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{\ell}\}
and
F(\boldsymbol{\kappa},S)=\sum_{i\in[\ell]}\kappa_{i}c_{i}
.
Specifically, we can set
{\cal B}=\{\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{\ell}\}
and
{\cal A}=\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d}\}
be two disjoint subsets of distinct points in
\mathbb{F}
.
The shares are computed by using a polynomial
f
of degree
k-1
satisfying
f(\beta_{i})=c_{i}
, for all
i\in[\ell]
,
and we distribute
f(\alpha_{1}),\ldots,f(\alpha_{d})
to the
d
participants.
Hence, the secret together with the shares form a codeword of
{\rm RS}({\cal B}\cup{\cal A},k)
and we can apply our result to compute
F(\boldsymbol{\kappa},s)
. The important difference is that in HSS, the location of codesymbols for which we want to find the weighted sum is fixed, but our results have no such restriction.

Henceforth,
\mathbb{F}

with degree

Hence, each element in
\mathbb{F}

\mathbb{B}

(t\times d)

over
\mathbb{\mathbb{B}}

Then we use
{\rm rank}_{\mathbb{B}}(\boldsymbol{M})
to denote the rank of

over
\mathbb{B}

We also adopt the following matrix-vector notation.
For a matrix

to denote the entry in the

-th column.
Moreover, we use

Definition 4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{\ell+d}
be an
\mathbb{F}
-linear code.
Let us fix
\boldsymbol{\kappa}\in\mathbb{F}_{*}^{\ell}

Then an

-

(C1)

are codewords in the dual code

.

(C2)

For

, the following holds

\left(\prod_{i\neq 1}\kappa_{i}\right)\boldsymbol{M}[1,:]=\left(\prod_{i\neq 2}\kappa_{i}\right)\boldsymbol{M}[2,:]=\cdots=\left(\prod_{i\neq\ell}\kappa_{i}\right)\boldsymbol{M}[\ell,:].

(20)

For convenience, we set
\boldsymbol{M}_{0}=(\prod_{i\neq 1}\kappa_{i})\boldsymbol{M}[1,:]
.
Furthermore, we have
{\rm rank}_{\mathbb{B}}\boldsymbol{M}_{0}=t
.

(C3)

We have that

\sum_{j\in[d]}{\rm rank}_{\mathbb{B}}\boldsymbol{M}[\ell+j,:]=b.

(21)

Using this notion of evaluation matrix, we characterize when a code

admits an evaluation scheme with certain bandwidth.

Theorem 7
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{\ell+d}

\mathbb{F}
-linear code and

.

-evaluation matrix with bandwidth

Lemma 8

Let
\mathcal{C}

Proof.

The result immediately follows from the fact that the dual of the Reed-Solomon code is a generalized Reed-Solomon code with multipliers given by (

Let

Lemma 9

The quantity

\Omega=\frac{1}{|\mathbb{F}|-1}\sum_{i\in[d]}(|\mathbb{B}|^{t-b_{i}}-1).

Proof.

First, observe that
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in S_{\omega}
if and only if

belongs to the null space of the matrix
[p_{1}(\omega),\ldots,p_{t}(\omega)]
.
In other words, for

, we have
\sum_{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{B}^{t}\setminus\{\boldsymbol{0}\}}\mathbb{I}({\boldsymbol{x}}\in S_{\omega})=|\mathbb{B}|^{t-b_{\omega}}-1

[p_{1}(\omega),\ldots,p_{t}(\omega)]

Now, if
\omega\in\mathcal{B}

\sum_{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{B}^{t}\setminus\{\boldsymbol{0}\}}\sum_{\omega\in\mathcal{B}\cup\mathcal{A}}\mathbb{I}({\boldsymbol{x}}\in S_{\omega})=\sum_{\omega\in\mathcal{B}\cup\mathcal{A}}\sum_{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{B}^{t}\setminus\{\boldsymbol{0}\}}\mathbb{I}({\boldsymbol{x}}\in S_{\omega})=\sum_{i\in[d]}(|\mathbb{B}|^{t-b_{i}}-1)\,.

Lemma 10

We have the following inequality:

\Omega\leq(l+d)-k-1.

Proof.

Now,
{\boldsymbol{x}}^{*}\in S_{\omega}

6
Conclusion and Open Problems

We investigate the problem of efficiently computing linear combinations of coded symbols.
We characterize the evaluation process in terms of an evaluation matrix for any
\mathbb{F}
-linear codes.
For Reed-Solomon codes, we propose explicit evaluation schemes as well as a lower bound on recovery bandwidth.
Several open questions remain, including:

(A)

closing the gap between the lower bound on evaluation bandwidth and an explicit construction;

(B)

developing low-bandwidth evaluation schemes for other classes of functions, for example, low-degree polynomials;

(C)

extending the framework to other parameter regimes, for example, when
t
is extremely large
[
TYB19
]
or over prime fields
[
CT22
]
;

(D)

ensuring robustness, that is, ensuring correct evaluation in the presence of erroneous nodes.

Appendix A
Proof of the Form of Theorem
3

In this appendix, we discuss on why
[
SW22
, Theorem 19]
can be written as Theorem
3
. For the case of
|{\cal B}|=\ell
,
|{\cal A}|=d
,
n=|\mathbb{B}|^{t}
from
[
SW22
, Theorem 19]
, we have four conditions to be fulfilled:

(a)

\delta\geq\gamma+\frac{1}{|\mathbb{B}|}
,

(b)

\epsilon\leq 1-\frac{k}{|\mathbb{B}|^{t}}
,

(c)

n-d=|I|<\gamma n
, and

(d)

(\epsilon-\delta)|\mathbb{B}|
is an integer.

From conditions (a)-(c), we obtain

\displaystyle\epsilon-\delta\leq 1-\frac{k}{|\mathbb{B}|^{t}}-\gamma-\frac{1}{|\mathbb{B}|}<1-\frac{k}{|\mathbb{B}|^{t}}-\left(1-\frac{d}{|\mathbb{B}|^{t}}\right)-\frac{1}{|\mathbb{B}|}=\frac{d-k}{|\mathbb{B}|^{t}}-\frac{1}{|\mathbb{B}|},

(27)

which implies

\displaystyle(\epsilon-\delta)|\mathbb{B}|<\frac{d-k}{|\mathbb{B}|^{t-1}}-1.

(28)

Let
s=(\epsilon-\delta)|B|\in\mathbb{Z}
(from condition (d)), then we can write (
28
) as

\displaystyle d\geq|\mathbb{B}|^{t-1}(s+1)+k+1.

(29)

Combining it with the fact that
d+\ell\leq n
, we translate
[
SW22
, Theorem 19]
to Theorem
3
.

Appendix B
Characterization of Evaluation Schemes using Evaluation Matrices

In this appendix, we complete the proof of Theorem
7
.
In other words, we demonstrate the necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of low-bandwidth evaluation schemes.

[\Leftarrow]
First, we prove the sufficient condition.
That is, using an evaluation matrix, we construct the functions
g_{j}:\;\mathbb{F}\to\mathbb{B}^{b_{j}}
for
j\in[d]

such that
\sum_{j\in[t]}b_{j}=b
.
Hence, we obtain an
(\boldsymbol{\kappa},\mathcal{C})
-weighted-sum evaluation scheme with bandwidth
b
.

Now, using Condition (C1) in Definition
4
, the
m
-th column
\boldsymbol{M}[:,m]
belongs to the dual code
\mathcal{C}^{\perp}
for all
m\in[t]
. This means that, for all
\boldsymbol{c}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{d},c_{d+1},\ldots,c_{\ell+d})\in\mathcal{C}
, we have that

\sum_{i\in[\ell+d]}c_{i}\boldsymbol{M}[i,m]=0\text{, or, }\sum_{i\in[\ell]}c_{i}\boldsymbol{M}[i,m]=-\sum_{j\in[d]}c_{\ell+j}\boldsymbol{M}[\ell+j,m].

Next, we multiply the equation by
\Lambda\triangleq\prod_{i\in[\ell]}\kappa_{i}
. Then using Condition (C2), we have that

\Lambda\boldsymbol{M}[i,m]=\kappa_{i}\boldsymbol{M}_{0}[1,m]
, and so,

\sum_{i\in[\ell]}\kappa_{i}c_{i}\boldsymbol{M}_{0}[m]=-\sum_{j\in[d]}\Lambda c_{\ell+j}\boldsymbol{M}[\ell+j,m].

Applying trace function to both sides of the equation above and utilizing its linearity, we obtain

{\rm Tr}\left(\left(\sum_{i\in[\ell]}\kappa_{i}c_{i}\right)\boldsymbol{M}_{0}[m]\right)=-{\rm Tr}\left(\sum_{j\in[d]}\Lambda c_{\ell+j}\boldsymbol{M}[\ell+j,m]\right).

For
j\in[d]
, we set
b_{j}\triangleq{\rm rank}_{\mathbb{B}}(\boldsymbol{M}[\ell+j,:])
and form a
\mathbb{B}
-basis
\chi_{1}^{j},\ldots,\chi_{b_{j}}^{j}
corresponding to the column space of
\boldsymbol{M}[\ell+j,:]
.
Then for
m\in[t]
, we can write
\Lambda c_{\ell+j}\boldsymbol{M}[\ell+j,l]
as a
\mathbb{B}
-linear combination of elements in
\chi_{1}^{j},\ldots,\chi_{b_{j}}^{j}
. So, for some
a_{j,1,m},\ldots,a_{j,b_{j},m}\in\mathbb{B}
, we have that

{\rm Tr}\Big(\Lambda c_{\ell+j}\boldsymbol{M}[\ell+j,m]\Big)=a_{j,1,m}{\rm Tr}\left(c_{\ell+j}\Lambda\chi_{1}^{j}\right)+\cdots+a_{j,b_{j},m}{\rm Tr}\left(c_{\ell+j}\Lambda\chi_{b_{j}}^{j}\right).

(30)

Now, for
j\in[d]
, we define the function

g_{j}(x):\;\mathbb{F}\to\mathbb{B}^{b_{j}},\;x\mapsto\left({\rm Tr}\left(x\Lambda\chi_{1}^{j}\right),\ldots,{\rm Tr}\left(x\Lambda\chi_{b_{j}}^{j}\right)\right).

(31)

Then we observe that for all
m\in[t]
,
{\rm Tr}(\Lambda c_{\ell+j}\boldsymbol{M}[\ell+j,l])
can be computed using
g_{j}(c_{\ell+j})
and
a_{j,1,m},\ldots,a_{j,b_{j},m}
. Then using (
30
), we can compute
{\rm Tr}\left(\left(\sum_{i\in[\ell]}\kappa_{i}c_{i}\right)\boldsymbol{M}_{0}[m]\right)
for all
m\in[t]
.

Finally, from Condition (C2), we have that the rank of the matrix
\boldsymbol{M}_{0}
is
t
.
In other words,
\{\boldsymbol{M}_{0}[m]:m\in[t]\}
forms a
\mathbb{B}
-basis.
Then Proposition
1
states there exists a trace-dual basis
\{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}_{0}}[m]:m\in[t]\}
and we have that

\sum_{i\in[\ell]}\kappa_{i}c_{i}=\sum_{m\in[t]}{\rm Tr}\left(\left(\sum_{i\in[\ell]}\kappa_{i}c_{i}\right)\boldsymbol{M}_{0}[m]\right)\widetilde{\boldsymbol{M}_{0}}[m].

It remains to determine the evaluation bandwidth.
This follows from the definition of
g_{j}
and Condition (C3).
Specifically, since
{\rm rank}_{\mathbb{B}}(\boldsymbol{M}[\ell+j,:])=b_{j}
for
j\in[d]
,
we have that the evaluation bandwidth is
\sum_{j=1}^{d}b_{j}=b
.

[\Rightarrow]
Here, we prove the necessary condition. That is, we construct the evaluation matrix
\boldsymbol{M}
using the functions
g_{j}
. Let us choose a basis
\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{t}\}
of
\mathbb{F}
over
\mathbb{B}
, its trace-dual basis
\{\tilde{u}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{u}_{t}\}
and write down
\sum_{i\in[\ell]}\kappa_{i}c_{i}
as a
\mathbb{B}
-linear combination of elements of the latter one, namely

\sum_{i\in[\ell]}\kappa_{i}c_{i}=\sum_{m=1}^{t}\zeta_{m}\tilde{u}_{m},

(32)

where
\zeta_{m}\in\mathbb{B}
for all
m\in[t]
. The fact that the considered recovery scheme is
\mathbb{B}
-linear means that coefficients
\zeta_{m}
are
\mathbb{B}
-linear combinations of replies
\{g_{j}(c_{\ell+j}):\;j\in[d]\}
. As a result, we have that

\zeta_{m}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\eta_{j}^{m}g_{j}(c_{\ell+j}),

where
\eta_{j}^{m}\in\mathbb{B}
.

As all functions
g_{j}
are
\mathbb{B}
-linear maps from
\mathbb{F}
to
\mathbb{B}^{b_{j}}
, we can define
g_{j}(c_{\ell+j})
as follows

g_{j}(c_{\ell+j})=\gamma_{1}^{j}\textrm{Tr}(\theta_{1}^{j}c_{\ell+j})+\cdots+\gamma_{b_{j}}^{j}\textrm{Tr}(\theta_{b_{j}}^{j}c_{\ell+j}),

where
\{\theta_{1}^{j},\ldots,\theta_{b_{j}}^{j}\}\in\mathbb{F}
are linearly independent as vectors over
\mathbb{B}
and
\{\gamma_{1}^{j},\ldots,\gamma_{b_{j}}^{j}\}\in\mathbb{B}
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\zeta_{m}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}[\xi_{m,j,1}\textrm{Tr}(\theta_{1}^{j}c_{\ell+j})+\cdots+\xi_{m,j,b_{j}}\textrm{Tr}(\theta_{b_{j}}^{j}c_{\ell+j})],

where
\xi_{m,j,l}=\eta_{j}^{m}\gamma_{l}
and
\xi_{m,j,l}\in\mathbb{B}
for all
l\in[t]
.
Let us multiply both sides of (
32
) by
u_{m}
, where
m\in[t]
, and apply trace function from
\mathbb{F}
to
\mathbb{B}
. As a result we have

\textrm{Tr}\left(\sum_{i\in[\ell]}\kappa_{i}c_{i}u_{m}\right)=\zeta_{1}\textrm{Tr}(\tilde{u}_{1}u_{m})+\cdots+\zeta_{m}\textrm{Tr}(\tilde{u}_{m}u_{m})+\cdots+\zeta_{t}\textrm{Tr}(\tilde{u}_{t}u_{m}),

but bases
\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{t}\}
and
\{\tilde{u}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{u}_{t}\}
are trace-dual. Hence,

\textrm{Tr}\left(\sum_{i\in[\ell]}\kappa_{i}c_{i}u_{m}\right)=\zeta_{m}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\xi_{m,j,1}\textrm{Tr}(\theta_{1}^{j}c_{\ell+j})+\cdots+\xi_{m,j,b_{j}}\textrm{Tr}(\theta_{b_{j}}^{j}c_{\ell+j})\right).

Employing the fact that
\{\xi_{m,j,1},\ldots,\xi_{m,j,b_{j}}\}\in\mathbb{B}
we have

\textrm{Tr}\left(\sum_{i\in[\ell]}\kappa_{i}c_{i}u_{m}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\textrm{Tr}\left((\xi_{m,j,1}\theta_{1}^{j}+\cdots+\xi_{m,j,b_{j}}\theta_{b_{j}}^{j})c_{\ell+j}\right).

We can define
\mu_{m,j}=\xi_{m,j,1}\theta_{1}^{j}+\cdots+\xi_{m,j,b_{j}}\theta_{b_{j}}^{j}
, where
\mu_{m,j}\in{\rm span}_{\mathbb{B}}\{\theta_{1}^{j},\ldots,\theta_{b_{j}}^{j}\}
. Note that we have chosen
\theta_{1}^{j},\ldots,\theta_{b_{j}}^{j}
so that
{\rm rank}_{\mathbb{B}}\{\mu_{1,j},\ldots,\mu_{t,j}\}=b_{j}
as otherwise the recovery scheme can be trivially improved by replacing
\{\theta_{1}^{j},\ldots,\theta_{b_{j}}^{j}\}
with basis of
\{\mu_{1,j},\ldots,\mu_{t,j}\}
over
\mathbb{B}
that contains less than
b_{j}
elements. Consequently,

\textrm{Tr}\left(\sum_{i\in[\ell]}\kappa_{i}c_{i}u_{m}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\textrm{Tr}(\mu_{m,j}c_{\ell+j}).

(33)

Let
\{\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{t}\}
be a basis of
\mathbb{F}
over
\mathbb{B}
and
\{\tilde{\delta}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{\delta}_{t}\}
be its trace-dual basis. Due to the linearity of code
\mathcal{C}
over
\mathbb{F}
, (
32
) is also valid for codeword
(\delta_{l}c_{1},\ldots,\delta_{l}c_{\ell+d})
. Hence for all
l\in[t]
we have

{\rm Tr}\left(\left[\sum_{i\in[\ell]}\kappa_{i}c_{i}\right]u_{m}\delta_{l}\right)=\sum_{j\in[d]}{\rm Tr}(\mu_{mj}c_{\ell+j}\delta_{l}).

Multiplying both sides by
\tilde{\delta}_{l}
, we obtain

{\rm Tr}\left(\left[\sum_{i\in[\ell]}\kappa_{i}c_{i}\right]u_{m}\delta_{l}\right)\tilde{\delta}_{l}=\sum_{j\in[d]}{\rm Tr}(\mu_{mj}c_{\ell+j}\delta_{l})\tilde{\delta}_{l}.

Taking sums over all
l\in[t]
, we have

u_{m}\sum_{i\in[\ell]}\kappa_{i}c_{i}=\sum_{j\in[d]}\mu_{mj}c_{\ell+j}.

(34)

According to (
34
), for all
m\in[t]
,
\left(u_{m}\kappa_{1},\ldots,u_{m}\kappa_{\ell},-\mu_{m(\ell+1)},\ldots,-\mu_{m(\ell+d)}\right)
are codewords of the dual code and we can form the columns of recovery matrix from them. In such a case the latter has the following form

\boldsymbol{M}=\left[\begin{matrix}\kappa_{1}u_{1}&\ldots&\kappa_{1}u_{t}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\kappa_{\ell}u_{1}&\ldots&\kappa_{\ell}u_{t}\\
-\mu_{1,\ell+1}&\ldots&-\mu_{t,\ell+1}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
-\mu_{1,\ell+d}&\ldots&-\mu_{t,\ell+d}\end{matrix}\right].

It is clear that
\frac{1}{\kappa_{1}}\boldsymbol{M}[1,:]=\cdots=\frac{1}{\kappa_{\ell}}\boldsymbol{M}[\ell,:]=[u_{1},\ldots,u_{t}]
and has full rank over
\mathbb{B}
.
Also, for
j\in[d]
, we have that
{\rm rank}_{\mathbb{B}}\boldsymbol{M}[\ell+j,:]={\rm rank}_{\mathbb{B}}(-\mu_{1,\ell+j},\ldots-\mu_{t,\ell+j})=b_{j}
. Therefore, we have
\sum_{j\in[d]}{\rm rank}_{\mathbb{B}}\boldsymbol{M}[\ell+j,:]=\sum_{j\in[d]}b_{j}=b
.

Encoding impredicative hierarchy of type universes with variables
Yoan Géran

Abstract
Logical frameworks can be used to translate proofs from a proof system to another one. For this purpose, we should be able to encode the theory of the proof system in the logical framework. The Lambda Pi calculus modulo theory is one of these logical frameworks. Powerful theories such as pure type systems with an infinite hierarchy of universes have been encoded, leading to partial encodings of proof systems such as Coq, Matita or Agda. In order to fully represent systems such as Coq and Lean, we introduce a representation of an infinite universe hierarchy with an impredicative universe and universe variables where universe equivalence is equality, and implement it as a terminating and confluent rewrite system.

Level variables

We extend the syntax of the levels with variables.

Definition 8

(Levels)
.

A level is a term of the grammar

t\coloneqq 0\mid s(t)\mid\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(t,t}}\right)\mid\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(t,t}}\right)

where
x
is an element of a countable set of variables
\mathcal{X}
.
We denote by
\mathcal{L}
the set of the levels, and we say that
t
is a concrete levels if
t

does not contain any variable.

Definition 9

(Valuation)
.

A valuation is a function
\sigma\colon\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{N}
.

Definition 10
.

Let
\sigma\colon\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{N}
be a a valuation. We define
inductively the value of a level
t
over
\sigma
,
denoted as
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\llbracket t}}\right\rrbracket_{\sigma}

with

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\llbracket 0}}\right\rrbracket_{\sigma}=0\qquad\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\llbracket s(t)}}\right\rrbracket_{\sigma}=s(\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\llbracket t}}\right\rrbracket_{\sigma})\qquad\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\llbracket x}}\right\rrbracket_{\sigma}=\sigma(x)

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\llbracket\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(t_{1},t_{2}}}\right)}}\right\rrbracket_{\sigma}=\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\vphantom{\mathord{\big(}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\llbracket t_{1}}}\right\rrbracket_{\sigma},\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\llbracket t_{2}}}\right\rrbracket_{\sigma}}}\right)\qquad\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\llbracket\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(t_{1},t_{2}}}\right)}}\right\rrbracket_{\sigma}=\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\vphantom{\mathord{\big(}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\llbracket t_{1}}}\right\rrbracket_{\sigma},\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\llbracket t_{2}}}\right\rrbracket_{\sigma}}}\right)

We use the same symbol
s
,
\operatorname*{max}
, and
\operatorname{imax}
for the syntax of the levels and the functions of the
natural numbers. However, levels are abstract terms and are interpreted through valuations.
Besides, the concrete levels can clearly be identified as the natural numbers which justifies
the use of the same symbol and permits to see the interpretation as a function that

concretizes
a level, turning it into a concrete level.

Definition 11

(Level comparison)
.

Let
t_{1},t_{2}\in\mathcal{L}
. We say that
t_{1}\leqslant_{\mathcal{L}}t_{2}

if for all valuations
\sigma
,

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\llbracket t_{1}}}\right\rrbracket_{\sigma}\leqslant\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\llbracket t_{2}}}\right\rrbracket_{\sigma}
.
In the same way, we say that
t_{1}=_{\mathcal{L}}t_{2}
if for all valuations

\sigma
,
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\llbracket t_{1}}}\right\rrbracket_{\sigma}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\llbracket t_{2}}}\right\rrbracket_{\sigma}
.
Hence
t_{1}=_{\mathcal{L}}t_{2}
if and only if
t_{1}\leqslant_{\mathcal{L}}t_{2}
and
t_{2}\leqslant_{\mathcal{L}}t_{1}
.

With level variables, the equivalence is no more the syntactic equality and the above embedding does not reflect it anymore:
\operatorname{\text{{{max}}}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x,y}}\right)
and
\operatorname{\text{{{max}}}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(y,x}}\right)
are not convertible and adding rules for that would lead to a non-terminating system. In the same way, commutativity and
equivalences such as
\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x,x}}\right)=_{\mathcal{L}}x
or
\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(s(x),x)}}\right)=_{\mathcal{L}}s(x)
are hard to express, and the impredicatity introduces other:
\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x,x}}\right)=_{\mathcal{L}}x
,
\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x,y}}\right),x}}\right)=_{\mathcal{L}}\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x,y}}\right)
, etc.

And yet, a correct embedding of the levels should reflect these equivalences. For instance, a term of the
universe
\operatorname{Type}_{x}
is also a term of the universe
\operatorname{Type}_{\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x,x}}\right)}
, and then we should be able to
identify the universes such as
\operatorname{Type}_{x}
and
\operatorname{Type}_{\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x,x}}\right)}
. This paper presents a new embedding
that faithfully represents levels with variables.

Related work

Some solutions have been studied in the predicative case. The big issue is the associativiy and commutativity of the
\operatorname*{max}
symbol. In
[
20
]
, Genestier solved this problem to encode
Agda
’s universe polymorphism. For that, he used rewriting modulo associativity and commutativity (AC). The idea,
also mentionned in a draft of Voevodsky
[
33
]
, is to represent each level as

\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(n,n_{1}+x_{1},\ldots,n_{k}+x_{k}}}\right)
where
n\geqslant\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}}}\right)
. Besides, if there exists
i\neq j
such that
x_{j}=x_{i}
, we simplify the term and keep only

\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(n_{i},n_{j}}}\right)+x_{i}
. Then, we obtain a minimal representation of terms of the
\operatorname*{max}
-successor algebra.

Blanqui gives another presentation of this algebra in
[
9
]
, with an encoding without matching modulo AC. However, this solution requires to keep the level in
some AC canonical form, and can then require the modification of the
\lambda\Pi/\equiv
type-checker.

The
\operatorname{imax}
-successor algebra is less studied and we do not know easy ways to reflect its equalities.
A confluent encoding is proposed in
[
4
]
, but it does not fully reflect the equalities; for instance, the levels
\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x,y}}\right),x}}\right)
and
\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x,y}}\right)
are not convertible.
Besides, Férey designed a non-confluent encoding of universe polymorphism in
[
17
]
.

Contribution and outline

We introduce a new representation for the levels, using the idea presented above in the predicative case: find a set of subterms such that any level can be expressed as a maximum of subterms. They should be easily comparable to simplify
\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u,v}}\right)
into
u
if
u\leqslant_{\mathcal{L}}v
and obtain minimal
representations, and they should ensure the uniqueness property:

\displaystyle\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}}}\right)=_{\mathcal{L}}\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}}}\right)\iff\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}}}\right\}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}}}\right\}.

Intuitively, the subterms should be very basic and simple: a subterm
u
must not be equivalent to a maximum of other subterms. With this representation, we obtain a deep understanding of the
\operatorname{imax}
-successor algebra,
and an easy procedure-decision for the level inequality problem.

In the
\cref
sec-level-transformation, we study the semantic of the
\operatorname{imax}
operator and establish a suitable set of subterms. Then, in the
\cref
sec-uniqueness, we introduce the minimal representation and shows that equivalent terms have the same minimal representation. And the
\cref
sec-implementation is dedicated to the implementation of this representation into the
\lambda\Pi/\equiv
as a first-order confluent and terminating rewrite system. An implementation in
Dedukti
is available on
https://gitlab.crans.org/geran/dedukti-level-implementation
.

In this section, we study the

2.1
Levels as maximum

The very first step is to show that any level can be express as a maximum of levels
that do not contain any
\operatorname*{max}
, that is the principle of our idea of representation.
The succesor can be distributed over
\operatorname*{max}
, the two next propositions show
how to distribute
\operatorname{imax}
over
\operatorname*{max}
.

Proposition 12
.

For all
u,v,w\in\mathcal{L}
,
\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u,\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(v,w}}\right)}}\right)=_{\mathcal{L}}\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u,v}}\right),\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u,w}}\right)}}\right)
.

Proposition 13
.

For all
u,v,w\in\mathcal{L}
,

\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u,v}}\right),w}}\right)=_{\mathcal{L}}\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u,w}}\right),\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(v,w}}\right)}}\right)
.

Then, any level can then be expressed as a maximum of levels without
\operatorname*{max}
.
Note that for this, we consider that
\operatorname*{max}
takes a set of levels as argument.
We obtain this theorem.

Theorem 14
.

For all
t\in\mathcal{L}
, there exists
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}
in the grammar

t\coloneqq 0\mid s(t)\mid\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(t,t}}\right)
such that

t=_{\mathcal{L}}\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}}}\right)
.

2.2
Simplification of the levels

We can now focus on levels without maximum. The uniqueness property sought for the representation requires the subterms to be very basic, and then search to simplify
the levels.

The main issue is
\operatorname{imax}
: its asymmetry complicates its interaction with other symbols. The previous equalities show how to remove the interaction between
\operatorname{imax}
and
\operatorname*{max}
, now, we will study the interactions between
\operatorname{imax}
and the other symbols.
The goal is to restrict the localisation of the
\operatorname{imax}
symbols to specific parts of the levels in order to understand and control their influence on the levels semantic.

Firstly, we recall these equalities that are direct consequences of the
semantic of
\operatorname{imax}
. They permit to deal with
0
and the successor.

Proposition 15
.

For all
u,v\in\mathcal{L}
,
\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u,0}}\right)=_{\mathcal{L}}0
and

\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u,s(v)}}\right)=\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u,s(v)}}\right)
.

And we show how to remove
\operatorname{imax}
symbol in second argument of

\operatorname{imax}
.

Proposition 16
.

For all
u,v,w\in\mathcal{L}
,

\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u,\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(v,w}}\right)}}\right)=_{\mathcal{L}}\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u,w}}\right),\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(v,w}}\right)}}\right)
.

Thus, we can consider that the second argument of a
\operatorname{imax}
is always
a variable. It is more complicated to directly enforce the form of its
first arguments, but we can obtain one restriction by distributing
the successor over the
\operatorname{imax}
. However, we cannot do it as directly
as we distribute the successor over the
\operatorname*{max}
, as shown in the
next example.

Example 17
.

We show that
s(\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(y,x}}\right))\not=_{\mathcal{L}}\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(s(y),s(x)}}\right)

by considering a valuation
\sigma
such that
\sigma(x)=0
and

\sigma(y)=1
.

Proposition 18
.

For all
u,v,w\in\mathcal{L}
,
s(\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(v,w}}\right))=_{\mathcal{L}}\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(s(w),\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(s(v),w}}\right)}}\right)
.

Finally, all of these propositions leads to this grammar restriction.

Theorem 19
.

For all
t\in\mathcal{L}
, there exists
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}
in the grammar

t\coloneq s^{k}(x)\mid s^{k}(0)\mid\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(t,x}}\right)
such that

t=_{\mathcal{L}}\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}}}\right)
.

Remark 20
.

For all
t
in this grammar, there exists
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathcal{X}
,
and
v=s^{k}(0)
or
v=s^{k}(x)
such that

t=\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\cdots\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(v,x_{1}}}\right),x_{2})\cdots)}}\right),x_{n-1}}}\right),x_{n}}}\right)
.

2.3
Introducting new levels

Here, we continue the simplification of the levels under
\operatorname*{max}
in order to
find simple enough terms to reach the uniqueness property. Indeed, the terms of the
grammar of
\cref
th-first_grammar are still not enough.

Example 21
.

Let us consider
t=\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x,y}}\right),\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(y,x}}\right)}}\right)
Then,

t=_{\mathcal{L}}\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x,y}}\right)
.

To better understand the issue, we should have a deep understanding of the semantic of

\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x,y}}\right)
. It means that we always consider the value of
y
, but we only consider
the value of
x
if
y
is not zero: the position of the variables are essential in the levels. But, taking into account
\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(y,x}}\right)
leads to offset this, and the value of
x
and
y

are always considered.

Here, we come to a solution that consists to introduce new symbols that permits to represent the current levels, without taking into account the order of the variables.

Definition 22

(Sublevels)
.

We define new symbols
\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}
and
\operatorname{\mathcal{B}}
.
\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}
takes as argument a set of variables,
an integer and a variable, and
\operatorname{\mathcal{B}}
takes as argument a set of variables
and an integer. They have the following semantic.

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\llbracket\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(E,x,S}}\right)}}\right\rrbracket_{\sigma}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0&\text{if there exists $y\in E$ such that $\sigma(y)=0$}\\
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\llbracket x}}\right\rrbracket_{\sigma}+S&\text{else}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\llbracket\operatorname{\mathcal{B}}(E,S)}}\right\rrbracket_{\sigma}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0&\text{if there exists $y\in E$ such that $\sigma(y)=0$}\\
S&\text{else}\end{cases}

In the next proposition, we show that any level of the grammar of

\cref
th-first_grammar can be written as a maximum of terms of this new grammar.

Proposition 23
.

If
t=\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\cdots\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(s^{k}(y),x_{1}}}\right))\cdots)}}\right),x_{n-1}}}\right),x_{n}}}\right)
, then

t=_{\mathcal{L}}\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\emptyset,x_{n},0}}\right),\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{x_{n}}}\right\},x_{n-1},0}}\right),\ldots,\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}}}\right\},x_{n},0}}\right),\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}}}\right\},y,k}}\right)}}\right).

And if
t=\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\cdots\operatorname{imax}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(s^{k}(0),x_{1}}}\right))\cdots)}}\right),x_{n-1}}}\right),x_{n}}}\right)
,
then

t=_{\mathcal{L}}\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\emptyset,x_{n},0}}\right),\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{x_{n}}}\right\},x_{n-1},0}}\right),\ldots,\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}}}\right\},x_{n},0}}\right),\operatorname{\mathcal{B}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}}}\right\},k}}\right)}}\right).

The two equivalences are quite similar, the difference being in the very last subterm
of the
\operatorname*{max}
which is a
\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}
in the first case (we have to take into account
s^{k}(y)

that is to say
k+y
) and a
\operatorname{\mathcal{B}}
in the second one (
k
is taken into account with
a
\operatorname{\mathcal{B}}
).

The sublevels that we search for our representation are elements of this grammar.
We just have two slightly modifications, two simplifications to make.
The first one is illustrated by this example.

Example 24
.

With
t_{1}=\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\emptyset,x,0}}\right)
and
t_{2}=\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{x}}\right\},x,0}}\right)
, we have

t_{1}=_{\mathcal{L}}t_{2}
since for all substitution
\sigma
,
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\llbracket t_{1}}}\right\rrbracket_{\sigma}=\sigma(x)=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\llbracket t_{2}}}\right\rrbracket_{\sigma}
.

The issue here is the fact that the second argument of a
\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}
symbol does not necesarily
appear in its first argument. This creates equalities as shown in the next proposition.

Proposition 25
.

Let
x\in\mathcal{X}
,
E\subset\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{x}}\right\}
and
S\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(E,x,S}}\right)=_{\mathcal{L}}\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(E\cup\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{x}}\right\},x,S}}\right),\operatorname{\mathcal{B}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(E,S}}\right)}}\right).

Then, we will only consider elements of the form
\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(E,x,S}}\right)
such that
x\in E
.

The second modification is related to the representation of
0
. Indeed, for all

E\subset\mathcal{X}
,
\operatorname{\mathcal{B}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(E,0}}\right)=_{\mathcal{L}}0
, and we should then keep at most one of them.
However, since we already have
0=_{\mathcal{L}}\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\emptyset}}\right)
, we can remove all of them.

And we end up with this set of sublevels which permits to express any level of
\mathcal{L}
.

Definition 26
.

We denote by
\mathcal{L}_{\text{s}}
the set of the sublevels that check the following conditions.

1.

\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(E,x,S}}\right)\in\mathcal{L}_{\text{s}}\iff x\in E
,

2.

\operatorname{\mathcal{B}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(E,S}}\right)\in\mathcal{L}_{\text{s}}\iff S>0
.

Theorem 27
.

Let
t\in\mathcal{L}
. Then there exists
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\in\mathcal{L}_{\text{s}}
such
that
t=_{\mathcal{L}}\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}}}\right)
.

Remark 28
.

By convenience, we will note
u_{i}\in t
if there exists
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\in\mathcal{L}_{\text{s}}
such
that
t=_{\mathcal{L}}\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}}}\right)
.

3
A minimal representation

In the previous section, we find a set
\mathcal{L}_{\text{s}}
and show that any level can be represented
as a maximum of elements of
\mathcal{L}_{\text{s}}
. The goal of this one is to show that any level
has a minimal representation as maximum of elements of
\mathcal{L}_{\text{s}}
and that this representation
is unique. Intuitevely, the sublevels of a minimal representation should be incomparable
(else one of the sublevels could be removed).

Definition 29

(Minimal representation)
.

Let
t
be a term. We say that
t
is a minimal representation if and
only if there exists
u1\ldots,u_{n}\in\mathcal{L}_{\text{s}}
such
that

1.

t=\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}}}\right)
,

2.

forall
i\neq j
,
u_{i}
and
u_{j}
are incomparable.

We denote by
\mathcal{L}_{r}
the set of the minimal representations.

Of course, any level
t
has a minimal representation. We can express

t
as a maximum of elements of
\mathcal{L}_{\text{s}}
(by
\cref
th-sublevels) and we
remove elements while thy are not incomparabe. The challenging part
is its uniqueness. To show it, we study the core of the definition
of a minimal representation: the sublevel comparison.

Theorem 30

(Sublevels comparison)
.

Elements of
\mathcal{L}_{\text{s}}
are compared as follows.

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(E,x,S}}\right)\not\leqslant_{\mathcal{L}}\operatorname{\mathcal{B}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(F,K}}\right)

(1)

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathcal{B}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(E,S}}\right)\leqslant_{\mathcal{L}}\operatorname{\mathcal{B}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(F,K}}\right)\iff F\subset E\land S\leqslant K

(2)

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathcal{B}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(E,S}}\right)\leqslant_{\mathcal{L}}\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(F,x,K}}\right)\iff(F\subset E\land S\leqslant K+1)

(3)

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(E,x,S}}\right)\leqslant_{\mathcal{L}}\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(F,y,K}}\right)\iff F\subset E\land x=y\land S\leqslant K

(4)

As a corollary, we get that the sublevel equivalence is a syntactic equality, which is quite
natural; the uniqueness property would be impossible otherwise.

Corollary 31
.

Let
t_{1},t_{2}\in\mathcal{L}_{\text{s}}
. Then
t_{1}=_{\mathcal{L}}t_{2}\iff t_{1}=t_{2}
.

proof
We have
t_{1}=_{\mathcal{L}}t_{2}\iff t_{1}\leqslant_{\mathcal{L}}t_{2}\land t_{2}\leqslant_{\mathcal{L}}t_{1}
, and
we conclude with the
\cref
th-comparison_sublevel.

And we can show the uniqueness of the minimal representation.
First, we show that two equivalent minimal representations have the same

\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}
.

Proposition 32
.

Let
t_{1},t_{2}\in\mathcal{L}_{r}
such that
t_{1}=_{\mathcal{L}}t_{2}
.
Then

\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(E,x,S}}\right)\in t_{1}\iff\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(E,x,S}}\right)\in t_{2}.

Proof.

Let us note

\displaystyle t_{1}=\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(E_{0},x_{0},S_{0}}}\right),\ldots,\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(E_{n},x_{n},S_{n}}}\right),\operatorname{\mathcal{B}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(G_{0},T_{0}}}\right),\ldots,\operatorname{\mathcal{B}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(G_{p},T_{p}}}\right)}}\right)

\displaystyle t_{2}=\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(F_{0},y_{0},K_{0}}}\right),\ldots,\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(F_{m},y_{m},K_{m}}}\right),\operatorname{\mathcal{B}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(H_{0},L_{0}}}\right),\ldots,\operatorname{\mathcal{B}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(H_{q},L_{q}}}\right)}}\right).

Let
\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(E,x,S}}\right)
be an sublevel of
t_{1}
. We consider
\sigma
such that

\sigma(y)=\begin{cases}\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(S_{0},\ldots,S_{n},K_{0},\ldots,K_{m},T_{0},\ldots,T_{p},L_{0},\ldots,L_{q}}}\right)+1&if $y=x$\\
1&if $y\in E\setminus\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{x}}\right\}$\\
0&else\end{cases}

We have
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\llbracket t_{1}}}\right\rrbracket_{\sigma}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\llbracket\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(E,x,S}}\right)}}\right\rrbracket_{\sigma}=S+\sigma(x)
and then

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\llbracket t_{2}}}\right\rrbracket_{\sigma}=S+\sigma(x)
. Then, there exists

\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(F,y,K}}\right)
in
t_{2}
such that
F\subset E\cup\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{x}}\right\}=E

(else
F
contains a variable
z
such that
\sigma(z)=0
) and

\sigma(y)+K=\sigma(x)+S
or there exists
\operatorname{\mathcal{B}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(F,K}}\right)
in
t_{2}

such that
K=\sigma(x)+S
. Since

\sigma(x)>\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(S_{0},\ldots,S_{n},K_{0},\ldots,K_{m}}}\right)
, we deduce that
it is the first case and
y=x
.

Then, there is
(F,x,S)
in
t_{2}
with
F\subset E
and
\sigma(y)+K=\sigma(x)+S
. If
F\subsetneq E
, then by the same reasoning, we show that
there exists
\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(G,x,S}}\right)\in t_{1}
with
G\subset F\subsetneq E
. But,
by
\cref
def-normal, it is impossible to have
\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(E,x,S}}\right)
and
\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(G,x,S}}\right)
in

t_{1}
with
G\subset E
since they are comparable.

Then
E=F
and
\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(E,x,S}}\right)
is also an element of
t_{2}
.
∎

And we show the same for the
\operatorname{\mathcal{B}}
.

Proposition 33
.

Let
t_{1},t_{2}\in\mathcal{L}_{r}
such that
t_{1}=_{\mathcal{L}}t_{2}
.
Then

\operatorname{\mathcal{B}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(E,S}}\right)\in t_{1}\iff\operatorname{\mathcal{B}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(E,S}}\right)\in t_{2}.

Proof.

Let us note

\displaystyle t_{1}=\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(E_{0},x_{0},S_{0}}}\right),\ldots,\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(E_{n},x_{n},S_{n}}}\right),\operatorname{\mathcal{B}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(G_{0},T_{0}}}\right),\ldots,\operatorname{\mathcal{B}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(G_{p},T_{p}}}\right)}}\right)

\displaystyle t_{2}=\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(F_{0},y_{0},K_{0}}}\right),\ldots,\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(F_{m},y_{m},K_{m}}}\right),\operatorname{\mathcal{B}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(H_{0},L_{0}}}\right),\ldots,\operatorname{\mathcal{B}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(H_{q},L_{q}}}\right)}}\right).

We show the result by induction on
E
. Let
\operatorname{\mathcal{B}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(E,S}}\right)
be a sublevel of
t_{1}
.
If
E=\emptyset
, we consider
\sigma
the zero function. Then,

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\llbracket t_{1}}}\right\rrbracket_{\sigma}=S
, hence
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\llbracket t_{2}}}\right\rrbracket_{\sigma}=S
.
Since
S>0
, it follows that
\operatorname{\mathcal{B}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\emptyset,S}}\right)
is a sublevel of
t_{2}
.

In the induction case, we consider
\sigma
such that

\sigma(x)=1
if
x\in E
and
\sigma(x)=0
otherwise,
hence
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\llbracket t_{1}}}\right\rrbracket_{\sigma}=S
.
Then,
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\llbracket t_{2}}}\right\rrbracket_{\sigma}=S
and since
S>0
,
there exists
\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(F,x,K}}\right)
in
t_{2}
such that
F\subset E
and

\sigma(x)+K=S
or there exists
\operatorname{\mathcal{B}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(F,S}}\right)
in
t_{2}
such that
F\subset E

and
K=S
.

In the first case, we have
x\in F\subset E
, then
\sigma(x)=1
and

K=S-1
. Then, by
\cref
prop-normal_a,
\operatorname{\mathcal{A}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(F,x,S-1}}\right)\in t_{1}
which
is impossible by
\cref
def-normal since it would be comparable with

\operatorname{\mathcal{B}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(E,S}}\right)\in t_{1}
.

Then, we have
\operatorname{\mathcal{B}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(F,S}}\right)\in t_{2}
. If
F\subsetneq E
, we apply the induction
hypothesis and obtain
\operatorname{\mathcal{B}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(F,S}}\right)\in t_{1}
, impossible because it would be
comparable with
\operatorname{\mathcal{B}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(E,S}}\right)
.

Then
E=F
and
\operatorname{\mathcal{B}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(E,S}}\right)
is also an element of
t_{2}
.
∎

We immediately obtain that equivalence of minimal representations is the
syntactic equality.

Proposition 34
.

For all
t_{1},t_{2}\in\mathcal{L}_{r}
,
t_{1}=_{\mathcal{L}}t_{2}\iff t_{1}=t_{2}
.

proof
The reverse implication is trivial and the first one is a consequences
of the
\cref
prop-normal_a,prop-normal_b.

And finally, we obtain the main theorem: the existence and uniquennes of
a minimal representation for each level. Before, we show the intuitive property
that the minimal representation of a maximum of sublevels is formed with
elements of these sublevels.

Proposition 35
.

For all
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\subset\mathcal{L}_{\text{s}}
, there exists
a unique
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}}}\right\}\subseteq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}}}\right\}
such that

\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}}}\right)=\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}}}\right)
and

\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}}}\right)\in\mathcal{L}_{r}
.

proof
We apply the following procedure. We consider
E=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}}}\right\}
.
While there exists
u,v\in E
such taht
u\leqslant_{\mathcal{L}}v
, we remove

u
from
E
. The
\cref
th-uniqueness permits to obtain the uniqueness
of this minial representation.

Theorem 36

(Representation)
.

For all
t\in\mathcal{L}
, there exists a unique

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}}}\right\}\subset\mathcal{L}_{\text{s}}
such that

t=_{\mathcal{L}}\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}}}\right)
.
We say that
\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}}}\right)
is the minimal representation
of
t
and we denote it as
\operatorname{repr}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(t}}\right)
.

proof
By
\cref
th-sublevels, there exists
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\in\mathcal{L}_{\text{s}}

such that
t=_{\mathcal{L}}\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}}}\right)
, and by

\cref
prop-expand_subterms, there exists a unique minimal representation
of
\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}}}\right)
.

Having a unique minimal representation is useful to have a faithful embedding of

\mathcal{L}
in the
\lambda\Pi/\equiv
, which is done in
\cref
sec-implementation. Moreover,
this representation also gives us an easy way to compare two terms.
Indeed, a sublevel can be compared to a level using the representation.

Lemma 37
.

Let
u,v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\in\mathcal{L}_{\text{s}}
. Then
u\leqslant_{\mathcal{L}}\operatorname*{max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}}}\right)
if
and only if there exists
i
such that
u\leqslant_{\mathcal{L}}v_{i}
.

Proof.

The reverse implication is trivial. We show the direct implication by
contraposition. We suppose that
forall
i
,
u\not\leqslant_{\mathcal{L}}v_{i}
.

If
u=\operatorname{\mathcal{B}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(E,S}}\right)
, we consider
\sigma
such that
\sigma(x)=1
if
x\in E
and

0

Proof.

5
Conclusion

We introduced a new representation of the levels of the impredicative PTS where equivalent levels have the same representation. It provides us an easy procedure decision for the inequality problem in the

\operatorname{imax}
-sucessor algebra, and it permits us to get a sound encoding of these levels in the
\lambda\Pi/\equiv
, in the sense that equivalent levels have convertible translations. Moreover, this encoding corresponds to a first-order, confluent and strongly normalizing rewrite system, and in particular it permits to decide level equality.

This encoding of the levels permits to encode
\text{CC}^{\infty}
with universe polymorphism. Besides,
we still have to study how this encoding behaves well together with encodings of inductive types or cumulativity in order to get a better encoding of
Coq
. The ideas mentioned at the end of

\cref
sec-implementation are also interesting. In particular, the characterization of
the elements of
\mathcal{L}_{r}
that are actually levels would lead to a better understanding
of the
\operatorname{imax}
-successor grammar.

Finally, this idea of representation, with the linear algebra analogy, could certainly be
adapted to some sets of terms built over a maximum, a supremum or other similar operations.
In addition to providing decision procedures, it would permit to define a concept similar
to the basis of a vector space on these spaces on these sets, and then it seems to be
an interesting direction to explore.

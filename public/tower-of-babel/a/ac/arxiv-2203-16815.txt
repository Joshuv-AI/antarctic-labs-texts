Abstract
. We study the boundary behavior of solutions to the Dirichlet problems for integro-differential operators with order of differentiability s ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) s\in(0,1) and summability p > 1 p>1 . We establish a nonlocal counterpart of the Wiener criterion, which characterizes a regular boundary point in terms of the nonlocal nonlinear potential theory.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
31B25, 31B15, 35R11

1.
Introduction

Wiener, in his pioneering works
[
53
,
54
]
, provided a necessary and sufficient condition, so-called the Wiener criterion, for a boundary point to be regular in the case of the Laplacian. In the fundamental work by Littman, Stampacchia, and Weinberger
[
42
]
, the validity of the Wiener criterion was verified for second-order uniformly elliptic linear operators with bounded and measurable coefficients. After these works, the Wiener criterion has been extended to a large class of quasilinear operators of second order by using the nonlinear potential theory. See Maz’ya
[
44
]
, Gariepy and Ziemer
[
20
]
, Lindqvist and Martio
[
41
]
, and Kilpeläinen and Malý
[
31
]
. We refer the reader to the books
[
25
,
43
]
and references therein. Further results can be found in
[
37
]
for
k
-Hessian operators,
[
1
]
for
p(x)
-Laplacian, and
[
38
]
for operators with Orlicz growth.

For fractional-order operators, several sufficient conditions for a boundary point to be regular have been proposed. Under the exterior sphere condition, solutions to the Dirichlet problems are continuous across the boundary of a domain for the fractional Laplacian
[
49
]
and the fractional
p
-Laplacian
[
26
,
40
]
. See also
[
50
]
. Moreover, it is known that the measure density condition of the complement of a domain is sufficient for more general linear and nonlinear nonlocal operators with measurable coefficients
[
34
,
35
,
39
]
. We point out that these conditions serve as sufficient conditions even for a wide class of local operators. However, they are far from sharp in the sense that they cannot serve as necessary conditions even though it is easier to check whether a domain satisfies these conditions than the Wiener criterion.

There is a relatively small amount of literature on the Wiener criterion for nonlocal operators. Eilertsen
[
15
]
proved the sufficiency of the Wiener criterion for solutions to the nonhomogeneous fractional Laplace equation with zero boundary data. Recently, Björn
[
2
]
showed both sufficiency and necessity of the Wiener criterion for the fractional Laplacian by using Caffarelli–Silvestre extension
[
4
]
. Note that the Wiener criterion in
[
2
]
is obtained only for boundary data with compact support. Moreover, the methods in
[
2
,
15
]
are restricted to the case of the fractional Laplacian.

The aim of this paper is to establish a nonlocal counterpart of the Wiener criterion in full generality for a class of nonlinear nonlocal elliptic operators with measurable coefficients. Our method is based on the nonlocal nonlinear potential theory, which has been actively studied in recent years.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
s\in(0,1)
,
p>1
, and
\Lambda\geq 1
. We consider a nonlocal operator
\mathcal{L}
defined by

\mathcal{L}u(x)=2\,\mathrm{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y,

where
k:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty]
is a measurable function satisfying
k(x,y)=k(y,x)
and

(1.1)

\frac{\Lambda^{-1}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\leq k(x,y)\leq\frac{\Lambda}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}.

This operator is modeled on the fractional
p
-Laplacian,
(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}
, in which case the kernel is given by
k(x,y)=|x-y|^{-n-sp}
.

Let
\Omega
be an open and bounded subset in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We say that a boundary point
x_{0}
of
\Omega
is
regular with respect to
\mathcal{L}
if, for each function
g\in V^{s,p}(\Omega|\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap C(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the unique
\mathcal{L}
-harmonic function
u\in V^{s,p}(\Omega|\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap C(\Omega)
with
u-g\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
satisfies

\lim_{\Omega\ni x\to x_{0}}u(x)=g(x_{0}).

See
Section

2.1
for the definitions of function spaces and
\mathcal{L}
-harmonic functions, and
Section

2.2
for the solvability of the Dirichlet problem in this function space. Note that a boundary data
g
is not necessarily bounded nor compactly supported.

The following is the main theorem of this paper, which answers the question raised by
[
46
, Open Problem 7]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

A boundary point
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
is regular with respect to
\mathcal{L}
if and only if

(1.2)

\int_{0}\left(\frac{\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(\overline{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}\setminus\Omega,B_{2\rho}(x_{0}))}{\rho^{n-sp}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho}{\rho}=+\infty.

We refer to
Section

2.3
for the definition of
(s,p)
-capacity. The integral in (
1.2
) is called the
Wiener integral
. We remark that
Theorem

1.1
trivially holds when
p>n/s
. Indeed, if
p>n/s
, then it is easy to see that every boundary point is regular with respect to
\mathcal{L}
by the Sobolev embedding theorem and that (
1.2
) holds by
Lemma

2.17
.

One can easily check that if a domain satisfies the exterior cone condition, the exterior
(\delta,R)
-Reifenberg flat condition, or the exterior corkscrew condition at
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
, then the Wiener integral at
x_{0}
diverges. For the definitions of Reifenberg flat domain and corkscrew domain, we refer to
[
27
,
48
]
. As a direct consequence of
Theorem

1.1
, these geometric conditions are sufficient for
x_{0}
to be regular.

Theorem

1.1
implies that the regularity of a boundary point
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
is completely determined by the local geometry of
\partial\Omega
. This result seems somewhat surprising because we deal with nonlocal equations. Indeed, in the intermediate steps of the proof of
Theorem

1.1
, we make substantial use of various integral estimates such as local boundedness, weak Harnack inequality, and Wolff potential estimate that contain a nonlocal tail term; see
Section

3
for details. Nevertheless, at the final stage, we observe that the long-range interactions of the solution disappear in the Wiener criterion. One possible interpretation of this phenomenon is that the information concerning whether the Wiener integral diverges or not is strong enough to absorb the nonlocal effect coming from the corresponding tail terms.

The following is an immediate corollary of
Theorem

1.1
, which is expected as in the case of local operators.

Corollary 1.2
.

The regularity of a boundary point depends only on
n
,
s
, and
p
, not on the operator
\mathcal{L}
itself.

Let us outline the strategy of the proof of
Theorem

1.1
. The starting point of the sufficient part is to capture the local behaviors of weak solutions near the boundary. More precisely, we establish the local boundedness for weak subsolutions and the weak Harnack inequality for weak supersolutions up to the boundary. These are boundary variants of the interior estimates provided in
[
3
,
8
,
9
,
28
]
. To this end, we modify localization techniques developed in the aforementioned papers to obtain Caccioppoli-type estimates near the boundary and then use Moser’s iteration.

The essential challenge in the nonlocal framework arises from the nonlocal tail term, which is a quantity encoding the long-range interactions; see (
2.1
) for the precise definition. Indeed, after repeated applications of the Caccioppoli-type inequalities and the weak Harnack inequality, we control the capacity of the complement of the domain by the sum of the infimum value of supersolutions and the nonlocal tail term. The nonlocal effect turns out to be negligible from the sufficient condition that the Wiener integral diverges. In this step, we divide the domain into a countable union of annuli and then analyze the quantified tail term in an iterative way.

Let us move on to the necessary part of the proof. In this case, the nonlocal nonlinear potential theory is more involved since the main ingredient is the pointwise estimate of solutions in terms of the Wolff potential. This estimate, together with additional regularity results, was already obtained by Kuusi, Mingione, and Sire
[
36
]
for SOLA (Solutions Obtained as Limits of Approximations). Nevertheless, we could not directly adopt the estimate since they imposed the natural restriction
p>2-\frac{s}{n}
to guarantee the existence of SOLA for a given Radon measure. This problem can be overcome by using a different type of solution, namely,
\mathcal{L}
-superharmonic function, as in the local case (see
[
30
,
31
]
). In this way, we cover all ranges of
p>1
. However, we still need to prove the Wolff potential estimate for
\mathcal{L}
-superharmonic functions for
p>1
. Therefore, we present a new proof of the Wolff potential estimate, mainly following the iteration scheme given in
[
31
]
. Again, one has to take into account a suitable tail, which requires further effort to handle. The necessary part is then concluded by using the relationship between the Wolff potential generated by the
\mathcal{L}
-potential and the
\mathcal{L}
-distribution; see
Section

5
for details.

This paper is organized as follows. In
Section

2
, we introduce several definitions and collect preliminary results related to our main theorem.
Section

3
consists of local estimates up to the boundary, namely, the local boundedness and the weak Harnack inequality. The proofs for the sufficiency and the necessity of the Wiener criterion are provided in

Section

4
and
Section

5
, respectively. Some algebraic inequalities used in the previous sections can be found in
Appendix

A
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall several function spaces to define weak solutions and harmonic functions with respect to
\mathcal{L}
. By using harmonic functions, we define a regular boundary point with respect to
\mathcal{L}
. Moreover, we introduce the
\mathcal{L}
-potential, which is closely related to the
(s,p)
-capacity.

2.1.
Weak solution

Let us first collect definitions of fractional Sobolev spaces and tail spaces. For
s\in(0,1)
,
p\in[1,\infty)
, and an open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
W^{s,p}(\Omega)
be the usual
fractional Sobolev space
with the norm

\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}=\left(\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}+[u]_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}^{p}\right)^{1/p}=\left(\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/p},

see
[
10
]
. By
W^{s,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
we denote the space of functions that belong to
W^{s,p}(\Omega^{\prime})
for each open set
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega
. We recall the
tail space

L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, which is given by

L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\left\{u\in L^{p-1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(y)|^{p-1}}{(1+|y|)^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y<+\infty\right\}.

It is well known and easy to check that
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and that the
nonlocal tail
(or
tail
for short)
\mathrm{Tail}(u;x_{0},r)
, for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
r>0
, is well defined if
u\in L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where

(2.1)

\mathrm{Tail}(u;x_{0},r)=\left(r^{sp}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{r}(x_{0})}\frac{|u(y)|^{p-1}}{|y-x_{0}|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/(p-1)}.

The nonlocal tail and the tail space have been introduced and studied in a systematic way in
[
8
,
9
,
29
]
and
[
7
,
26
,
33
,
34
,
35
,
36
]
.

Let
\Lambda\geq 1
and
k
be a measurable function that is symmetric and satisfies (
1.1
). For measurable functions
u,v:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
, we define a quantity

\mathcal{E}^{k}(u,v)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x,

provided that it is finite. In particular, we denote by
\mathcal{E}^{s,p}(u,v)=\mathcal{E}^{k}(u,v)
when
k
is given by
k(x,y)=|x-y|^{-n-sp}
. We also write
\mathcal{E}^{k}(u)=\mathcal{E}^{k}(u,u)
and
\mathcal{E}^{s,p}(u)=\mathcal{E}^{s,p}(u,u)=[u]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}
. Note that, if
u\in W^{s,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\cap L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
v\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, then both
\mathcal{E}^{k}(u,v)
and
\mathcal{E}^{s,p}(u,v)
are finite. We now define a weak subsolution, supersolution, and solution with respect to
\mathcal{L}
. See also
[
33
,
34
,
35
,
40
,
46
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
f
be a measurable function in
\Omega
. A function
u\in W^{s,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
with
u_{-}\in L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is called a
weak supersolution of
\mathcal{L}u=f
in
\Omega
if
\langle f,\varphi\rangle:=\int_{\Omega}f(x)\varphi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x
is well defined and

(2.2)

\mathcal{E}^{k}(u,\varphi)\geq\langle f,\varphi\rangle

for all nonnegative functions
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. A function
u
is called a
weak subsolution
if
-u
is a weak supersolution. We call
u
a
weak solution
if it is both a weak subsolution and a weak supersolution.

Note that the summability assumption
u_{-}\in L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for weak supersolution in
Definition

2.1
can be replaced by
u\in L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. It is proved
[
35
, Lemma 1]
that these two definitions are actually equivalent.

Since test functions are not always in
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, it is desirable to deal with a larger class of test functions than
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. For this purpose, we consider the Banach spaces

(2.3)

V^{s,p}(\Omega|\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\left\{u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}:u|_{\Omega}\in L^{p}(\Omega),\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{n/p+s}}\in L^{p}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n})\right\},

equipped with the norm

\begin{split}\|u\|_{V^{s,p}(\Omega|\mathbb{R}^{n})}&=\left(\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}+[u]_{V^{s,p}(\Omega|\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\right)^{1/p}\\
&=\left(\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/p},\end{split}

and

(2.4)

W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega):=\overline{C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{V^{s,p}(\Omega|\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\overline{C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Note that
V^{s,p}(\Omega|\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset W^{s,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\cap L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. We refer the reader to
[
18
]
for more properties of these spaces.

Remark 2.2
.

(i)

The notation
V
in the definition (
2.3
) is adopted from
[
14
,
16
,
19
]
. Note that some authors denote the space in (
2.3
) by
W^{s,p}(\Omega|\mathbb{R}^{n})
or
\mathbb{W}^{s,p}(\Omega)
. See, e.g.,
[
7
,
18
]
.

(ii)

In this paper, we use
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
to denote the closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in
V^{s,p}(\Omega|\mathbb{R}^{n})
as in
[
13
,
16
,
40
]
. However, some authors often define
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
to be the closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in
W^{s,p}(\Omega)
, see
[
10
,
24
]
. Moreover, the space

W^{s,p}_{\Omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\{u\in W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):u=0\text{ a.e. on }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\},

which contains (
2.4
) by definition, is also used in
[
16
]
as a space of test functions. One should be careful since these three spaces are not equal in general especially on non-smooth domains. Indeed, if
\Omega
is an open set with continuous boundary, then
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)=W^{s,p}_{\Omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, but there exists a domain
\Omega
such that
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\subsetneq W^{s,p}_{\Omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, see
[
17
]
. Note that it is known
[
22
,
24
]
that if
\Omega
is a bounded open set with a Lipschitz boundary and
s\neq 1/p
, then all three spaces coincide, i.e.,

W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)=W^{s,p}_{\Omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\overline{C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}.

However, we emphasize that we do not assume any regularity on
\Omega
and that we will only make use of the space (
2.4
) in this paper.

If we know a priori that a weak supersolution has an additional regularity, then the space of test functions can be extended by a standard approximation argument.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
f\in(W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega))^{\ast}
. If
u\in V^{s,p}(\Omega|\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a weak supersolution of
\mathcal{L}u=f
in
\Omega
, then (
2.2
) holds for all nonnegative functions
\varphi\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
.

The next theorem shows that the Dirichlet problem for the equation
\mathcal{L}u=0
with Sobolev boundary data is solvable.

Theorem 2.4
.

Suppose that
\Omega
is bounded and that
f\in(W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega))^{\ast}
,
g\in V^{s,p}(\Omega|\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then, there exists a unique weak solution
u\in V^{s,p}(\Omega|\mathbb{R}^{n})
of
\mathcal{L}u=f
in
\Omega
with
u-g\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
.

In
Theorem

2.4
, we understand the boundary data in the weak sense, namely,
u=g
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
in the sense of
V^{s,p}(\Omega|\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if
u-g\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. We generalize this concept to inequalities in the following way. We say that
u\leq 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
in the sense of
V^{s,p}(\Omega|\mathbb{R}^{n})
if
u_{+}:=\max\{u,0\}\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. Other types of inequalities can be defined in a similar manner. For example,
u\geq 0
(
u\leq v
, respectively) in the sense of
V^{s,p}(\Omega|\mathbb{R}^{n})
if
-u\leq 0
(
u-v\leq 0
, respectively) in the same sense.

The uniqueness part of
Theorem

2.4
follows from the comparison principle.

Theorem 2.5

(Comparison principle)
.

Suppose that
\Omega
is bounded. Let
u,v\in V^{s,p}(\Omega|\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that
u\geq v
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
in the sense of
V^{s,p}(\Omega|\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

\mathcal{E}^{k}(u,\varphi)\geq\mathcal{E}^{k}(v,\varphi)

for all nonnegative functions
\varphi\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, then
u\geq v
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Theorem

2.4
and
Theorem

2.5
have been proved in slightly different settings in the literature
[
16
,
18
,
40
]
, but the same arguments work in our setting.

Let us finish the section with some functional inequalities. See
[
10
, Theorem 6.7]
,
[
47
, Corollary 2.1]
, and
[
51
, Proposition 3.2]
for the proofs.

Theorem 2.6

(Fractional Sobolev inequality)
.

Let
p\in(1,n/s)
and
R>0
. There exists a constant
C=C(n,s,p)>0
such that

\left(\int_{B_{R}}|u(x)|^{p^{\ast}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p/p^{\ast}}\leq C\left(\int_{B_{R}}\int_{B_{R}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x+R^{-sp}\int_{B_{R}}|u(x)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)

for all
u\in W^{s,p}(B_{R})
, where
p^{\ast}=np/(n-sp)
.

Theorem 2.7

(Fractional Poincaré inequality I)
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
R>0
. There exists a constant
C=C(n,s,p)>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}|u(x)-(u)_{B_{R}}|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq CR^{sp}\int_{B_{R}}\int_{B_{R}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

for all
u\in W^{s,p}(B_{R})
, where
(u)_{B_{R}}=\fint_{B_{R}}u(x)\,\mathrm{d}x
.

Theorem 2.8

(Fractional Poincaré inequality II)
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and bounded. There exists a constant
C=C(n,s,p)>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C(\mathrm{diam}(\Omega))^{sp}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

for all
u\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
.

2.2.
Harmonic function

Let us introduce a harmonic function with respect to
\mathcal{L}
and define a regular boundary point with respect to
\mathcal{L}
. We first recall that a weak solution of the equation
\mathcal{L}u=0
can be redefined in a set of measure zero so that it is continuous.

Theorem 2.9
.

[
35
, Theorem 9]

Let
u
be a weak supersolution of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
\Omega
. Then

u(x)=\mathrm{ess}\liminf_{y\to x}u(y)

for a.e.
x\in\Omega
. In particular,
u
has a representative that is lower semicontinuous in
\Omega
.

Let us next provide a definition of
\mathcal{L}
-harmonic function. See also
[
33
,
35
,
46
]
. We will define
\mathcal{L}
-superharmonic and
\mathcal{L}
-subharmonic functions later.

Definition 2.10
.

A function
u
is said to be
\mathcal{L}
-harmonic in
\Omega
if it is a weak solution of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
\Omega
that is continuous in
\Omega
.

By
Theorem

2.4
and
Theorem

2.9
, we know that there exists a unique
\mathcal{L}
-harmonic function with Sobolev boundary data.

Definition 2.11

(Regular point)
.

A boundary point
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
is said to be
regular (with respect to
\mathcal{L}
)
if, for each function
g\in V^{s,p}(\Omega|\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap C(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the unique
\mathcal{L}
-harmonic function
u\in V^{s,p}(\Omega|\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap C(\Omega)
with
u-g\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
satisfies

(2.5)

\lim_{\Omega\ni x\to x_{0}}u(x)=g(x_{0}).

Definition

2.11
corresponds to the definition of Sobolev regular boundary point
[
25
, Chapter 6]
in the case of local operators. Another definition of regular boundary point, which is based on Perron’s solutions for the fractional
p
-Laplacian, is provided in
[
40
]
. This definition corresponds to that of regular boundary point
[
25
, Chapter 9]
for local operators. We do not deal with Perron’s solutions for the operator
\mathcal{L}
within the scope of this paper, but
\mathcal{L}
-superharmonic and
\mathcal{L}
-subharmonic functions are useful for the theory.

Definition 2.12
.

A function
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(-\infty,\infty]
is said to be
\mathcal{L}
-superharmonic in
\Omega
if it satisfies the following properties:

(i)

u<+\infty
almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(ii)

u
is lower semicontinuous in
\Omega
,

(iii)

for each open set
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega
and each weak solution
v\in C(\overline{\Omega^{\prime}})
of
\mathcal{L}v=0
in
\Omega^{\prime}
with
v_{+}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
u\geq v
on
\partial\Omega^{\prime}
and a.e. on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega^{\prime}
, it holds that
u\geq v
in
\Omega^{\prime}
,

(iv)

u_{-}\in L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

A function
u
is said to be
\mathcal{L}
-subharmonic in
\Omega
if
-u
is
\mathcal{L}
-superharmonic in
\Omega
.

We collect some results on
\mathcal{L}
-superharmonic functions that we need in the sequel.

Theorem 2.13
.

[
35
, Theorem 1]

If
u
is an
\mathcal{L}
-superharmonic function in
\Omega
, then
u(x)=\liminf_{y\to x}u(y)=\mathrm{ess}\,\liminf_{y\to x}u(y)
for every
x\in\Omega
. If, in addition,
u
is locally bounded in
\Omega
or
u\in W^{s,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
, then it is a weak supersolution in
\Omega
.

Remark 2.14
.

(i)

[
35
, Corollary 7]
A function
u
is
\mathcal{L}
-harmonic in
\Omega
if and only if
u
is both
\mathcal{L}
-superharmonic and
\mathcal{L}
-subharmonic in
\Omega
.

(ii)

[
35
, Theorem 12]
If
u
is a lower semicontinuous weak supersolution of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in

satisfying

for every

, then

is
\mathcal{L}
-superharmonic in

.

2.3.
Potential theory

By using
\mathcal{L}
-harmonic functions, we define the
\mathcal{L}
-potential, which will play a crucial role in the development of the theory.

Definition 2.15
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open set and
K\subset\Omega
be a compact set. Let
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,1]
be such that
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
\psi=1
on
K
. The
\mathcal{L}
-harmonic function in
\Omega\setminus K
with
u-\psi\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega\setminus K)
is called the
\mathcal{L}
-potential of
K
in
\Omega
and denoted by
\mathfrak{R}(K,\Omega)
.

By
Theorem

2.5
it is immediate that the definition of
\mathfrak{R}(K,\Omega)
is independent of the particular choice of
\psi
.

We next recall the definition of the
(s,p)
-capacity, which is one of the fundamental quantities in the potential theory, see
[
2
,
25
,
45
]
. Suppose that
K
is a compact subset of
\Omega
and let

W(K,\Omega):=\{v\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega):v\geq 1\text{ on }K\}.

The
(s,p)
-capacity of
K
with respect to
\Omega
is defined by

(2.6)

\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(K,\Omega)=\inf_{v\in W(K,\Omega)}\mathcal{E}^{s,p}(v)=\inf_{v\in W(K,\Omega)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x.

By definition,
\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(\cdot,\cdot)
is increasing in the first argument and decreasing in the second argument with respect to the set inclusion. Moreover, by using an approximation, the set
W(K,\Omega)
in the definition (
2.6
) can be replaced by a larger set

W_{0}(K,\Omega):=\overline{W(K,\Omega)}^{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

A function in
W_{0}(K,\Omega)
is said to be
admissible for
\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(K,\Omega)
.

We now would like to propose useful properties of the
\mathcal{L}
-potential. For this purpose, we require a generalized notion of
(s,p)
-capacity, namely,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{k}(K,\Omega):=\inf_{v\in W(K,\Omega)}\mathcal{E}^{k}(v).

Note that if
k(x,y)=|x-y|^{-n-sp}
, then
\mathcal{C}^{k}(K,\Omega)=\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(K,\Omega)
.

Lemma 2.16
.

Suppose that
\Omega
is open and bounded, and
K
is a compact subset of
\Omega
.

(i)

\mathfrak{R}(K,\Omega)
is admissible for
\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(K,\Omega)
. In particular,
\mathfrak{R}(K,\Omega)\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
.

(ii)

Let

\widetilde{W}(K,\Omega):=\{v\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega):v=1\text{ in a neighborhood of }K\}.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{k}(K,\Omega)=\inf_{v\in\widetilde{W}(K,\Omega)}\mathcal{E}^{k}(v).

(iii)

\mathfrak{R}(K,\Omega)
is a minimizer of
\mathcal{E}^{k}
among all functions belonging to
W_{0}(K,\Omega)
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{k}(\mathfrak{R}(K,\Omega))=\mathcal{C}^{k}(K,\Omega).

(iv)

\mathfrak{R}(K,\Omega)
is a weak supersolution of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
\Omega
.

Proof.

(i)

Let
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
be such that
0\leq\psi\leq 1
and
\psi=1
on
K
. By the definition of
\mathcal{L}
-potential, there exists a sequence of functions
\{\varphi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega\setminus K)
such that

\displaystyle\varphi_{j}\to\mathfrak{R}(K,\Omega)-\psi\quad\text{ in }W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Since
\varphi_{j}+\psi\in W(K,\Omega)
, the desired result follows.

(ii)

Since
\widetilde{W}(K,\Omega)\subset W(K,\Omega)
, it is immediate that

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{k}(K,\Omega)\leq\inf_{v\in\widetilde{W}(K,\Omega)}\mathcal{E}^{k}(v).

For the converse inequality, given
\varepsilon>0
, let
\varphi\in W(K,\Omega)
be such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{k}(\varphi)\leq\mathcal{C}^{k}(K,\Omega)+\varepsilon.

We denote by
\{\eta_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
a sequence of functions belonging to
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that
0\leq\eta_{j}(t)\leq 1
,
\eta_{j}(t)=0
in a neighborhood of
(-\infty,0]
,
\eta_{j}(t)=1
in a neighborhood of
[1,\infty)
, and
\|\eta_{j}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq 1+1/j
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, since
\eta_{j}(\varphi(x))\in\widetilde{W}(K,\Omega)
, we have

\displaystyle\inf_{v\in\widetilde{W}(K,\Omega)}\mathcal{E}^{k}(v)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\eta_{j}(\varphi(x))-\eta_{j}(\varphi(y))|^{p}k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\|\eta_{j}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|^{p}k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq(1+1/j)^{p}\cdot(\mathcal{C}^{k}(K,\Omega)+\varepsilon).

By letting
j\to\infty
and
\varepsilon\to 0
, we finish the proof.

(iii)

Since
W_{0}(K,\Omega)
is the completion of
W(K,\Omega)
in
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{k}(K,\Omega)=\inf_{v\in W_{0}(K,\Omega)}\mathcal{E}^{k}(v).

Then, it follows from part (i) that

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{k}(K,\Omega)\leq\mathcal{E}^{k}(\mathfrak{R}(K,\Omega)).

For the reversed inequality, let
v\in\widetilde{W}(K,\Omega)
. Then, since
\mathfrak{R}(K,\Omega)-v\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega\setminus K)
and
\mathfrak{R}(K,\Omega)
is
\mathcal{L}
-harmonic in
\Omega\setminus K
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{k}(\mathfrak{R}(K,\Omega),\mathfrak{R}(K,\Omega)-v)=0.

This, together with Young’s inequality, implies that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{k}(\mathfrak{R}(K,\Omega))=\mathcal{E}^{k}(\mathfrak{R}(K,\Omega),v)\leq\frac{p-1}{p}\mathcal{E}^{k}(\mathfrak{R}(K,\Omega))+\frac{1}{p}\mathcal{E}^{k}(v),

and hence
\mathcal{E}^{k}(\mathfrak{R}(K,\Omega))\leq\mathcal{E}^{k}(v)
. By taking the infimum over
\widetilde{W}(K,\Omega)
and recalling part (ii), the conclusion follows.

(iv)

From part (i), we know that
\mathfrak{R}(K,\Omega)\in W^{s,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\cap L_{sp}^{p-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Let
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
be nonnegative in
\Omega
. Then for any
t\geq 0
,
\mathfrak{R}(K,\Omega)+t\varphi
belongs to
W_{0}(K,\Omega)
. By applying part (iii), we have

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}\mathcal{E}^{k}(\mathfrak{R}(K,\Omega)+t\varphi)\geq 0,

which leads to
\mathcal{E}^{k}(\mathfrak{R}(K,\Omega),\varphi)\geq 0
.

∎

Furthermore, we can estimate the
(s,p)
-capacity of a closed ball
\overline{B_{r}(x_{0})}
with respect to a concentric ball
B_{R}(x_{0})
as follows:

Lemma 2.17
.

Let
0<r\leq R/2
, then

\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(\overline{B_{r}(x_{0})},B_{R}(x_{0}))\simeq\begin{cases}r^{n-sp}&\text{if }n>sp,\\
R^{n-sp}&\text{if }n<sp,\\
(\log(R/r))^{1-p}&\text{if }n=sp,\end{cases}

where the comparable constants depend only on
n
,
s
, and
p
.

Proof.

We recall from
[
2
, Lemma 2.4]
that

\displaystyle\mathrm{cap}_{p,|t|^{a}}(B_{r/2}(z_{0}),B_{R}(z_{0}))\lesssim\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(\overline{B_{r}(x_{0})},B_{R}(x_{0}))\lesssim\mathrm{cap}_{p,|t|^{a}}(B_{r}(z_{0}),B_{R}(z_{0})),

where
a=p-1-sp
and
z_{0}=(x_{0},0)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. See
[
2
,
25
]
for the precise definition of the weighted variational capacity
\mathrm{cap}_{p,|t|^{a}}
. For the upper bound, we apply
[
25
, Theorem 2.19]
to conclude that

\displaystyle\mathrm{cap}_{p,|t|^{a}}(B_{r}(z_{0}),B_{R}(z_{0}))

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{B_{R}(z_{0})\setminus B_{r}(z_{0})}|z-z_{0}|^{np/(1-p)}|t|^{a/(1-p)}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1-p}

\displaystyle\simeq\left(\int_{r}^{R}\rho^{\frac{sp-n}{p-1}-1}\,\mathrm{d}\rho\right)^{1-p}

\displaystyle\simeq\begin{cases}r^{n-sp}&\text{if }n>sp,\\
R^{n-sp}&\text{if }n<sp,\\
(\log(R/r))^{1-p}&\text{if }n=sp,\end{cases}

where
z=(x,t)
. The lower bound follows from
[
25
, Theorem 2.18]
.
∎

3.
Local estimates up to the boundary

To prove the Wiener criterion, it is crucial to study the behavior of weak solutions to
\mathcal{L}u=0
near the boundary. In this section, we establish local estimates of weak solutions up to the boundary. More precisely, we study the local boundedness for weak subsolutions and the weak Harnack inequality for weak supersolutions up to the boundary by using Moser’s iteration.

3.1.
Caccioppoli-type estimates up to the boundary

The first step towards local estimates for weak solutions up to the boundary is to establish Caccioppoli-type estimates up to the boundary. For this purpose, we generalize the definition of inequality of
V^{s,p}(\Omega|\mathbb{R}^{n})
functions in the following way. Let
T
be any set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
u
be a
V^{s,p}(\Omega|\mathbb{R}^{n})
function. We say that
u\leq 0
on
T
in the sense of
V^{s,p}(\Omega|\mathbb{R}^{n})
if
u_{+}:=\max\{u,0\}
is the limit in
V^{s,p}(\Omega|\mathbb{R}^{n})
of a sequence of
C^{0,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
-functions with compact support in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus T
. In a similar manner, we say that
u\geq 0
(
u\leq v
, respectively)
on
T
in the sense of
V^{s,p}(\Omega|\mathbb{R}^{n})
if
-u\leq 0
(
u-v\leq 0
, respectively) in the same sense. In particular, if
T=\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
, this definition coincides with the earlier one given in
Section

2.1
. We also define

\wsup_{T}u=\inf\{k\in\mathbb{R}:u\leq k\text{ on }T\text{ in the sense of }V^{s,p}(\Omega|\mathbb{R}^{n})\}

and
\winf_{T}u=-\wsup_{T}(-u)
.

We prove the following three lemmas, one for weak subsolutions and the others for weak supersolutions, at the same time as in the case of local operators, see
[
21
, Theorem 8.25 and 8.26]
. In what follows, we let
\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
be such that
\gamma=\beta+p-1\neq 0
, and define quantities

(3.1)

\Phi_{\gamma;\,x_{0},R}(u)=\left(\fint_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|u(x)|^{\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/\gamma},

where
\fint_{A}
denotes
|A|^{-1}\int_{A}
. Moreover, throughout the paper, we will denote a universal constant by
C>0
, which may be different from line to line.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and assume
\gamma=\beta+p-1>p-1
. If
u\in V^{s,p}(\Omega|\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a bounded weak subsolution of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
\Omega
, then for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
0<r<R
, and
d>0
,

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\frac{|(u_{M}^{+}(x)+d)^{\gamma/p}-(u_{M}^{+}(y)+d)^{\gamma/p}|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq C\left(1+\left(\frac{|\gamma|}{|\beta|}\right)^{p}\right)R^{n-sp}\left(\frac{R}{R-r}\right)^{p}\Phi_{\gamma;\,x_{0},R}^{\gamma}(u_{M}^{+}+d)\\
&\quad+C\frac{|\gamma|^{p}}{|\beta|}R^{n-sp}\left(\frac{R}{R-r}\right)^{n+sp}\Phi_{\beta;\,x_{0},R}^{\beta}(u_{M}^{+}+d)\,\mathrm{Tail}^{p-1}(u_{M}^{+}+d;x_{0},R),\end{split}

where

(3.2)

M=\wsup_{B_{R}(x_{0})\setminus\Omega}u_{+},\quad u_{M}^{+}(x)=\max\{u(x),M\},

and
C=C(n,s,p,\Lambda)>0
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and assume
\gamma=\beta+p-1<p-1
,
\gamma\neq 0
. If
u\in V^{s,p}(\Omega|\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a weak supersolution of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
\Omega
such that
u\geq 0
in
B_{R}(x_{0})
for some ball
B_{R}(x_{0})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then for any
0<r<R
and
d>0
,

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\frac{|(u_{m}^{-}(x)+d)^{\gamma/p}-(u_{m}^{-}(y)+d)^{\gamma/p}|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq C\left(1+\frac{|\gamma|^{p}}{|\beta|}+\left(\frac{|\gamma|}{|\beta|}\right)^{p}\right)R^{n-sp}\left(\frac{R}{R-r}\right)^{p}\Phi_{\gamma;\,x_{0},R}^{\gamma}(u_{m}^{-}+d)\\
&\quad+C\frac{|\gamma|^{p}}{|\beta|}R^{n-sp}\left(\frac{R}{R-r}\right)^{n+sp}\Phi_{\beta;\,x_{0},R}^{\beta}(u_{m}^{-}+d)\,\mathrm{Tail}^{p-1}((u_{m}^{-}+d)_{-};x_{0},R),\end{split}

where

(3.3)

m=\winf_{B_{R}(x_{0})\setminus\Omega}u,\quad u_{m}^{-}(x)=\min\{u(x),m\},

and
C=C(n,s,p,\Lambda)>0
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
. If
u\in V^{s,p}(\Omega|\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a weak supersolution of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
\Omega
such that
u\geq 0
in
B_{R}(x_{0})
for some ball
B_{R}(x_{0})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then for any
0<r<R
and
d>0
,

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\frac{|\log(u_{m}^{-}(x)+d)-\log(u_{m}^{-}(y)+d)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq CR^{n-sp}\left(\left(\frac{R}{R-r}\right)^{p}+\left(\frac{R}{R-r}\right)^{n+sp}\Phi_{1-p;\,x_{0},R}^{1-p}(u_{m}^{-}+d)\,\mathrm{Tail}^{p-1}((u_{m}^{-}+d)_{-};x_{0},R)\right),\end{split}

where
u_{m}^{-}
is defined as in (
3.3
) and
C=C(n,s,p,\Lambda)>0
.

Proof of
Lemma

3.1
,
Lemma

3.2
, and
Lemma

3.3
.

Let us write
B_{R}=B_{R}(x_{0})
for simplicity. Let
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{(R+r)/2})
be a cut-off function such that
\eta\equiv 1
on
B_{r}
,
0\leq\eta\leq 1
, and
|\nabla\eta|\leq C/(R-r)
. Note that
\beta>0
when
u
is a weak subsolution and
\beta<0
when
u
is a weak supersolution. For
d>0
, we set
\bar{u}=u_{M}^{+}+d
if
u
is a weak subsolution and
\bar{u}=u_{m}^{-}+d
if
u
is a weak supersolution. Note that, if
u
is either a weak subsolution or supersolution, then so is
\bar{u}
. We define

\varphi=\begin{cases}(\bar{u}^{\beta}-\bar{M}^{\beta})\eta^{p}&\text{if }\beta>0,\\
(\bar{u}^{\beta}-\bar{m}^{\beta})\eta^{p}&\text{if }\beta<0,\end{cases}

where
\bar{M}=M+d
and
\bar{m}=m+d
. Then,
\varphi
is a nonnegative test function in
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. Thus, we have
\mathcal{E}^{k}(\bar{u},\varphi)\leq 0
if
u
is a weak subsolution and
\mathcal{E}^{k}(\bar{u},\varphi)\geq 0
if
u
is a weak supersolution. We split the double integral in
\mathcal{E}^{k}
into two parts as

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}^{k}(\bar{u},\varphi)&=\int_{B_{R}}\int_{B_{R}}|\bar{u}(x)-\bar{u}(y)|^{p-2}(\bar{u}(x)-\bar{u}(y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad+2\int_{B_{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}|\bar{u}(x)-\bar{u}(y)|^{p-2}(\bar{u}(x)-\bar{u}(y))\varphi(x)k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

If
\beta\neq 0
and
\gamma\neq 0
, then an application of
Lemma

A.1
with
a=\bar{u}(x)-d
,
b=\bar{u}(y)-d
,
\eta_{1}=\eta(x)
,
\eta_{2}=\eta(y)
, and
l=M
if
\beta>0
and
l=m
if
\beta<0
shows that

(3.4)

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{R}}\int_{B_{R}}\left|\bar{u}^{\gamma/p}(x)\eta(x)-\bar{u}^{\gamma/p}(y)\eta(y)\right|^{p}k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq C\left(1+\left(\frac{|\gamma|}{|\beta|}\right)^{p}\right)\int_{B_{R}}\int_{B_{R}}\max\{\bar{u}^{\gamma}(x),\bar{u}^{\gamma}(y)\}|\eta(x)-\eta(y)|^{p}k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad-C\frac{|\gamma|^{p}}{\beta}\int_{B_{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}|\bar{u}(x)-\bar{u}(y)|^{p-2}(\bar{u}(x)-\bar{u}(y))\varphi(x)k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=:I_{1}+I_{2},\end{split}

where
C=C(p)>0
. We obtain that, for
\beta>0
,

(3.5)

\begin{split}I_{2}&\leq C\frac{|\gamma|^{p}}{|\beta|}\int_{B_{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}(\bar{u}(y)-\bar{u}(x))^{p-1}(\bar{u}^{\beta}(x)-\bar{M}^{\beta})\eta^{p}(x){\bf 1}_{\{\bar{u}(x)\leq\bar{u}(y)\}}k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq C\frac{|\gamma|^{p}}{|\beta|}\int_{B_{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}\bar{u}^{p-1}(y)\bar{u}^{\beta}(x)\eta^{p}(x)k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq C\frac{|\gamma|^{p}}{|\beta|}\int_{B_{R}}\bar{u}^{\beta}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\left(\sup_{x\in\mathrm{supp}\,\eta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}\bar{u}^{p-1}(y)k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right),\end{split}

and for
\beta<0
,
\beta\neq-(p-1)
,

(3.6)

\begin{split}I_{2}&\leq C\frac{|\gamma|^{p}}{|\beta|}\int_{B_{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}(\bar{u}(x)-\bar{u}(y))^{p-1}(\bar{u}^{\beta}(x)-\bar{m}^{\beta})\eta^{p}(x){\bf 1}_{\{\bar{u}(x)\geq\bar{u}(y)\}}k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq C\frac{|\gamma|^{p}}{|\beta|}\int_{B_{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}(\bar{u}(x)+(\bar{u}(y))_{-})^{p-1}\bar{u}^{\beta}(x)\eta^{p}(x)k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq C\frac{|\gamma|^{p}}{|\beta|}\int_{B_{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}\bar{u}^{\gamma}(x)|\eta(x)-\eta(y)|^{p}k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad+C\frac{|\gamma|^{p}}{|\beta|}\int_{B_{R}}\bar{u}^{\beta}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\left(\sup_{x\in\mathrm{supp}\,\eta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}(\bar{u}(y))_{-}^{p-1}k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right),\end{split}

where
C=C(p)>0
. If
x\in\mathrm{supp}\,\eta\subset B_{(R+r)/2}(x_{0})
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})
, then
|x-y|\geq\frac{R-r}{2R}|y-x_{0}|
. Thus, we have, for
\beta>0
,

(3.7)

\sup_{x\in\mathrm{supp}\,\eta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}\bar{u}^{p-1}(y)k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\leq\frac{\Lambda}{R^{sp}}\left(\frac{2R}{R-r}\right)^{n+sp}\mathrm{Tail}^{p-1}(\bar{u};x_{0},R),

and for
\beta<0
,
\beta\neq-(p-1)
,

(3.8)

\sup_{x\in\mathrm{supp}\,\eta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}(\bar{u}(y))_{-}^{p-1}k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\leq\frac{\Lambda}{R^{sp}}\left(\frac{2R}{R-r}\right)^{n+sp}\mathrm{Tail}^{p-1}(\bar{u}_{-};x_{0},R).

By combining (
3.4
), (
3.5
), and (
3.7
), and using the estimate

(3.9)

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{R}}\bar{u}^{\gamma}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\eta(x)-\eta(y)|^{p}k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq\Lambda\int_{B_{R}}\bar{u}^{\gamma}(x)\left(\left(\frac{C}{R-r}\right)^{p}\int_{B_{R}(x)}\frac{|x-y|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}(x)}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq CR^{n-sp}\left(\frac{R}{R-r}\right)^{p}\Phi_{\gamma;\,x_{0},R}^{\gamma}(\bar{u}),\end{split}

If

4.
The sufficient condition for the regularity of a boundary point

We are ready to prove the sufficient condition for a regular boundary point with respect to
\mathcal{L}
. As mentioned in
Section

1
, we focus on the case
p\in(1,n/s]
. We mainly follow the ideas of
[
20
,
23
]
, but further analysis is required to treat the tail term.

Let us fix a boundary point
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
. Let
g\in V^{s,p}(\Omega|\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap C(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and let
u\in V^{s,p}(\Omega|\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap C(\Omega)
be the
\mathcal{L}
-harmonic function with
u-g\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. We will prove (
2.5
), or equivalently,

(4.1)

\lim_{\rho\to 0}\sup_{\Omega\cap B_{\rho}(x_{0})}u\leq g(x_{0})\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{\rho\to 0}\inf_{\Omega\cap B_{\rho}(x_{0})}u\geq g(x_{0}),

provided that the Wiener integral diverges. By symmetry, we only prove the first inequality of (
4.1
). We assume to the contrary that

(4.2)

L:=\lim_{\rho\to 0}\sup_{\Omega\cap B_{\rho}(x_{0})}u>g(x_{0})

and choose
l\in\mathbb{R}
so that
g(x_{0})<l<L
. By the continuity of
g
, there exists a sufficiently small radius
r_{\ast}>0
such that

l\geq\sup_{\overline{B_{r}(x_{0})}\setminus\Omega}g\quad\text{for any }r\in(0,r_{\ast}).

We set

M_{l}(r)=\wsup_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\,(u-l)_{+}\quad\text{and}\quad u_{l,r}=M_{l}(r)-(u-l)_{+}\quad\text{for }r>0.

Note that since
\lim_{r\to 0}M_{l}(r)=L-l>0
, we have
M_{l}(r)\geq L-l>0
for any
r>0
. Moreover,
u_{l,r}
is a weak supersolution of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
\Omega
. Recall the notation
u_{m}^{-}
in (
3.3
). Since
(u-l)_{+}=0
in
B_{r}(x_{0})\setminus\Omega
in the sense of
V^{s,p}(\Omega|\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have

\winf_{B_{r}(x_{0})\setminus\Omega}u_{l,r}=\wsup_{B_{r}(x_{0})\setminus\Omega}u_{l,r}=M_{l}(r),

and hence
(u_{l,r})_{m}^{-}=u_{l,r}
.

The following lemma provides a key estimate of the
(s,p)
-capacity of a compact set

(4.3)

with respect to
B_{2\rho}(x_{0})
, which will be used to find a contradiction in the end of this section.

Lemma 4.1
.

There exists a constant
C=C(n,s,p,\Lambda)>0
such that

\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(D_{\rho}(x_{0}),B_{2\rho}(x_{0}))\leq C\rho^{n-sp}\left(\frac{M_{l}(4\rho)-M_{l}(\rho)+\mathrm{Tail}((u_{l,4\rho})_{-};x_{0},4\rho)}{M_{l}(4\rho)}\right)^{p-1}

for any

, where

is given by (

Proof.

Let us write
B_{\rho}=B_{\rho}(x_{0})
and
D_{\rho}=D_{\rho}(x_{0})
for simplicity. To begin with, we define

v_{l,4\rho}:=\frac{\eta\,u_{l,4\rho}}{M_{l}(4\rho)},

where
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{3\rho/2})
is a cut-off function such that
\eta\equiv 1
on
\overline{B_{\rho}}
,
0\leq\eta\leq 1
, and
|\nabla\eta|\leq C/\rho
. It is immediately checked that
v_{l,4\rho}
is admissible for
\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(D_{\rho},B_{2\rho})
, and hence

\begin{split}\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(D_{\rho},B_{2\rho})&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|v_{l,4\rho}(x)-v_{l,4\rho}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq 2\int_{B_{3\rho/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|v_{l,4\rho}(x)-v_{l,4\rho}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x=:I.\end{split}

Since

|v_{l,4\rho}(x)-v_{l,4\rho}(y)|^{p}\leq\frac{2^{p-1}}{M_{l}^{p}(4\rho)}\left(u_{l,4\rho}^{p}(x)|\eta(x)-\eta(y)|^{p}+|u_{l,4\rho}(x)-u_{l,4\rho}(y)|^{p}\eta^{p}(y)\right)

and

(4.4)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\eta(x)-\eta(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\leq\frac{C}{\rho^{p}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|x-y|^{p}\land\rho^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\leq C\rho^{-sp},

we have

(4.5)

\begin{split}I&\leq C\frac{\rho^{n-sp}}{M_{l}^{p}(4\rho)}\fint_{B_{3\rho/2}}u_{l,4\rho}^{p}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{C}{M_{l}^{p}(4\rho)}\int_{B_{3\rho/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u_{l,4\rho}(x)-u_{l,4\rho}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\eta^{p}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq C\frac{\rho^{n-sp}}{M_{l}^{p-1}(4\rho)}\fint_{B_{2\rho}}u_{l,4\rho}^{p-1}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{C}{M_{l}^{p}(4\rho)}\int_{B_{3\rho/2}}\int_{B_{3\rho/2}}\frac{|u_{l,4\rho}(x)-u_{l,4\rho}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

For the first term on the right-hand side of (

), since

is a weak supersolution such that

in

\begin{split}\frac{\rho^{n-sp}}{M_{l}^{p-1}(4\rho)}\fint_{B_{2\rho}}u_{l,4\rho}^{p-1}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x&\leq C\frac{\rho^{n-sp}}{M_{l}^{p-1}(4\rho)}\left(\winf_{B_{\rho}}u_{l,4\rho}+\mathrm{Tail}((u_{l,4\rho})_{-};x_{0},4\rho)\right)^{p-1}\\
&=C\rho^{n-sp}\left(\frac{M_{l}(4\rho)-M_{l}(\rho)+\mathrm{Tail}((u_{l,4\rho})_{-};x_{0},4\rho)}{M_{l}(4\rho)}\right)^{p-1}.\end{split}

Lemma 4.2
.

There exists a constant
C=C(n,s,p,\Lambda)>0
such that

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{M_{l}^{p}(4\rho)}\int_{B_{3\rho/2}(x_{0})}\int_{B_{3\rho/2}(x_{0})}\frac{|u_{l,4\rho}(x)-u_{l,4\rho}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq C\rho^{n-sp}\left(\frac{M_{l}(4\rho)-M_{l}(\rho)+\mathrm{Tail}((u_{l,4\rho})_{-};x_{0},4\rho)}{M_{l}(4\rho)}\right)^{p-1}\end{split}

for any
\rho\in(0,r_{\ast}/4)
.

Proof.

We write
B_{r}=B_{r}(x_{0})
for simplicity. Let
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{13\rho/8})
be a cut-off function satisfying
\eta\equiv 1
on
\overline{B_{3\rho/2}}
,
0\leq\eta\leq 1
, and
|\nabla\eta|\leq C/\rho
. Then,
\varphi=(u-l)_{+}\eta^{p}\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. Since
u_{l,4\rho}
is a weak supersolution of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
\Omega
, we obtain

\begin{split}0&\leq\int_{B_{7\rho/4}}\int_{B_{7\rho/4}}|u_{l,4\rho}(x)-u_{l,4\rho}(y)|^{p-2}(u_{l,4\rho}(x)-u_{l,4\rho}(y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad+2\int_{B_{7\rho/4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{7\rho/4}}|u_{l,4\rho}(x)-u_{l,4\rho}(y)|^{p-2}(u_{l,4\rho}(x)-u_{l,4\rho}(y))\varphi(x)k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

By using
\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)=(u(x)-l)_{+}(\eta^{p}(x)-\eta^{p}(y))-(u_{l,4\rho}(x)-u_{l,4\rho}(y))\eta^{p}(y)
and the ellipticity assumption (
1.1
), we have

(4.6)

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{3\rho/2}}\int_{B_{3\rho/2}}\frac{|u_{l,4\rho}(x)-u_{l,4\rho}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq\Lambda\int_{B_{7\rho/4}}\int_{B_{7\rho/4}}|u_{l,4\rho}(x)-u_{l,4\rho}(y)|^{p}\eta^{p}(y)k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq\Lambda\int_{B_{7\rho/4}}\int_{B_{7\rho/4}}|u_{l,4\rho}(x)-u_{l,4\rho}(y)|^{p-1}(u(x)-l)_{+}|\eta^{p}(x)-\eta^{p}(y)|k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad+2\Lambda\int_{B_{7\rho/4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{7\rho/4}}|u_{l,4\rho}(x)-u_{l,4\rho}(y)|^{p-2}(u_{l,4\rho}(x)-u_{l,4\rho}(y))\varphi(x)k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=:I_{1}+I_{2}.\end{split}

Let us first estimate
I_{1}
. Since
(u(x)-l)_{+}\leq M_{l}(4\rho)
and
|\eta^{p}(x)-\eta^{p}(y)|\leq C|x-y|/\rho
, we estimate
I_{1}
as

I_{1}\leq CM_{l}(4\rho)\int_{B_{7\rho/4}}\int_{B_{7\rho/4}}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{p-1}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\frac{|x-y|}{\rho}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x,

where
v:=u_{l,4\rho}+d
for some
d>0
, which will be chosen later. Let
\gamma>0
be sufficiently close to
p-1
so that
1<p-\gamma<n/(n-sp)
. By Hölder’s inequality and
Lemma

A.6
, we have

\begin{split}I_{1}&\leq CM_{l}(4\rho)\left(\int_{B_{7\rho/4}}\int_{B_{7\rho/4}}\max\{v(x),v(y)\}^{(p-\gamma)(p-1)}\left(\frac{|x-y|}{\rho}\right)^{p}\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
&\quad\times\left(\int_{B_{7\rho/4}}\int_{B_{7\rho/4}}\max\{v(x),v(y)\}^{\gamma-p}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\\
&\leq C\frac{M_{l}(4\rho)}{\rho^{s}}\left(\int_{B_{2\rho}}v^{(p-\gamma)(p-1)}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{B_{7\rho/4}}\int_{B_{7\rho/4}}\frac{|v^{\gamma/p}(x)-v^{\gamma/p}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}.\end{split}

Since
0<(p-\gamma)(p-1)<(p-1)n/(n-sp)
and
v_{m}^{-}=v
,
Theorem

3.7
applied to a weak supersolution
v
shows that

\int_{B_{2\rho}}v^{(p-\gamma)(p-1)}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C\rho^{n}\left(\winf_{B_{\rho}}v+\mathrm{Tail}(v_{-};x_{0},4\rho)\right)^{(p-\gamma)(p-1)}.

Moreover, since
\gamma<p-1
, it follows from
Lemma

3.2
that

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{7\rho/4}}\int_{B_{7\rho/4}}\frac{|v^{\gamma/p}(x)-v^{\gamma/p}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq C\rho^{n-sp}\left(\fint_{B_{2\rho}}v^{\gamma}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x+\left(\fint_{B_{2\rho}}v^{\beta}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\,\mathrm{Tail}^{p-1}(v_{-};x_{0},2\rho)\right),\end{split}

where
\beta=\gamma-(p-1)
. We set
d=\mathrm{Tail}((u_{l,4\rho})_{-};x_{0},4\rho)
, then since

\mathrm{Tail}(v_{-};x_{0},2\rho)\leq\mathrm{Tail}((u_{l,4\rho})_{-};x_{0},2\rho)\leq d,

we have

\begin{split}\left(\fint_{B_{2\rho}}v^{\beta}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\,\mathrm{Tail}^{p-1}(v_{-};x_{0},2\rho)&\leq\left(\fint_{B_{2\rho}}v^{\gamma}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\,\frac{\mathrm{Tail}^{p-1}(v_{-};x_{0},2\rho)}{d^{p-1}}\leq\fint_{B_{2\rho}}v^{\gamma}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

Applying
Theorem

3.7
yields

\fint_{B_{2\rho}}v^{\gamma}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C\left(\winf_{B_{\rho}}v+\mathrm{Tail}(v_{-};x_{0},4\rho)\right)^{\gamma}.

Therefore, we obtain

(4.7)

\begin{split}I_{1}&\leq CM_{l}(4\rho)\rho^{n-sp}\left(\winf_{B_{\rho}}v+\mathrm{Tail}(v_{-};x_{0},4\rho)\right)^{p-1}\\
&\leq CM_{l}(4\rho)\rho^{n-sp}(M_{l}(4\rho)-M_{l}(\rho)+\mathrm{Tail}((u_{l,4\rho})_{-};x_{0},4\rho))^{p-1},\end{split}

where
C=C(n,s,p,\Lambda)>0
.

Let us next estimate
I_{2}
. Since
\varphi(x)\leq M_{l}(4\rho)\eta^{p}(x)
and

|u_{l,4\rho}(x)-u_{l,4\rho}(y)|^{p-2}(u_{l,4\rho}(x)-u_{l,4\rho}(y))\leq C\left(u_{l,4\rho}^{p-1}(x)+(u_{l,4\rho})_{-}^{p-1}(y)\right),

we have

\begin{split}I_{2}&\leq CM_{l}(4\rho)\int_{B_{7\rho/4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{7\rho/4}}\left(u_{l,4\rho}^{p-1}(x)+(u_{l,4\rho})_{-}^{p-1}(y)\right)\eta^{p}(x)k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq CM_{l}(4\rho)\int_{B_{7\rho/4}}u_{l,4\rho}^{p-1}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{7\rho/4}}\frac{|\eta(x)-\eta(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad+CM_{l}(4\rho)\int_{B_{7\rho/4}}\eta^{p}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{7\rho/4}}\frac{(u_{l,4\rho})_{-}^{p-1}(y)}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

By using similar arguments as in (
4.4
) and (
3.8
), and observing that
u_{l,4\rho}\geq 0
in
B_{4\rho}
, we arrive at

I_{2}\leq CM_{l}(4\rho)\rho^{n-sp}\left(\fint_{B_{2\rho}}u_{l,4\rho}^{p-1}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x+\mathrm{Tail}^{p-1}((u_{l,4\rho})_{-};x_{0},4\rho)\right).

By applying
Theorem

3.7
, we conclude that

(4.8)

I_{2}\leq CM_{l}(4\rho)\rho^{n-sp}(M_{l}(4\rho)-M_{l}(\rho)+\mathrm{Tail}((u_{l,4\rho})_{-};x_{0},4\rho))^{p-1}.

The desired result follows by combining (
4.6
), (
4.7
), and (
4.8
).
∎

We are now ready to prove the sufficiency of the Wiener criterion.

Proof of the sufficient part of
Theorem

1.1
.

As explained in the beginning of this section, we assume to the contrary that (
4.2
) holds and find a contradiction. By
Lemma

4.1
, we have

\frac{\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(D_{\rho}(x_{0}),B_{2\rho}(x_{0}))}{\rho^{n-sp}}\leq C\left(\frac{M_{l}(4\rho)-M_{l}(\rho)+\mathrm{Tail}((u_{l,4\rho})_{-};x_{0},4\rho)}{M_{l}(4\rho)}\right)^{p-1}

for any
\rho\in(0,r_{\ast}/4)
. Using this inequality and
M_{l}(4\rho)\geq L-l
, we derive an estimate for the Wiener integral:

(4.9)

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{r_{\ast}/4}\left(\frac{\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(D_{\rho}(x_{0}),B_{2\rho}(x_{0}))}{\rho^{n-sp}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho}{\rho}\\
&\leq C\int_{0}^{r_{\ast}/4}\frac{M_{l}(4\rho)-M_{l}(\rho)+\mathrm{Tail}((u_{l,4\rho})_{-};x_{0},4\rho)}{\rho\,M_{l}(4\rho)}\,\mathrm{d}\rho\\
&\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{r_{\ast}/4}\frac{M_{l}(4\rho)-M_{l}(\rho)}{\rho}\,\mathrm{d}\rho+\int_{0}^{r_{\ast}/4}\frac{\mathrm{Tail}((u_{l,4\rho})_{-};x_{0},4\rho)}{\rho}\,\mathrm{d}\rho\right)=:C(I_{1}+I_{2}).\end{split}

By a change of variables and
Theorem

3.5
, we have

(4.10)

I_{1}=\int_{r_{\ast}/4}^{r_{\ast}}\frac{M_{l}(\rho)}{\rho}\,\mathrm{d}\rho\leq(\log 4)\left(\wsup_{B_{r_{\ast}}(x_{0})}u+|l|\right)<+\infty.

Thus, the rest part of the proof is devoted to the estimate of
I_{2}
.

Let
N=N(\rho)\in\mathbb{N}
be the unique integer satisfying
4^{-N-1}r_{\ast}\leq\rho<4^{-N}r_{\ast}
. We decompose the tail term by

\mathrm{Tail}^{p-1}((u_{l,4\rho})_{-};x_{0},4\rho)\leq(4\rho)^{sp}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{r_{\ast}}(x_{0})}\frac{(u_{l,4\rho})_{-}^{p-1}}{|y-x_{0}|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{A_{j}(x_{0})}\frac{(u_{l,4\rho})_{-}^{p-1}}{|y-x_{0}|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right),

where
A_{j}(x_{0})
denotes the annulus
B_{4^{j+1}\rho}(x_{0})\setminus B_{4^{j}\rho}(x_{0})
. First of all, since
(u_{l,4\rho})_{-}\leq|u|+|l|
, we have

(4.11)

\rho^{sp}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{r_{\ast}}(x_{0})}\frac{(u_{l,4\rho})_{-}^{p-1}}{|y-x_{0}|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\leq C\left(\frac{\rho}{r_{\ast}}\right)^{sp}\left(\mathrm{Tail}^{p-1}(u;x_{0},r_{\ast})+|l|^{p-1}\right).

We next observe that
(u_{l,4\rho})_{-}\leq M_{l}(4^{j+1}\rho)-M_{l}(4\rho)
in
A_{j}(x_{0})
. Thus, we obtain

(4.12)

\rho^{sp}\int_{A_{j}(x_{0})}\frac{(u_{l,4\rho})_{-}^{p-1}}{|y-x_{0}|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\leq C4^{-spj}\left(M_{l}(4^{j+1}\rho)-M_{l}(4\rho)\right)^{p-1}.

By combining two estimates (
4.11
) and (
4.12
), we have

\begin{split}\mathrm{Tail}^{p-1}((u_{l,4\rho})_{-};x_{0},4\rho)&\leq C\left(\frac{\rho}{r_{\ast}}\right)^{sp}\left(\mathrm{Tail}^{p-1}(u;x_{0},r_{\ast})+|l|^{p-1}\right)\\
&\quad+C\sum_{j=1}^{N}4^{-spj}\left(M_{l}(4^{j+1}\rho)-M_{l}(4\rho)\right)^{p-1}.\end{split}

To proceed, we use
[
12
, Lemma 3 (b)]
: for every
t>0
and
\beta>1
there exists a constant
C>0
such that for any sequence
\{a_{j}\}
of nonnegative numbers, we have

(4.13)

\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a_{j}\right)^{t}\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\beta^{j}a_{j}^{t}.

An application of (
4.13
) with
t=1/(p-1)
and
\beta=2^{\frac{sp}{p-1}}>1
yields

\mathrm{Tail}((u_{l,4\rho})_{-};x_{0},4\rho)\leq C\left(\frac{\rho}{r_{\ast}}\right)^{\frac{sp}{p-1}}(\mathrm{Tail}(u;x_{0},r_{\ast})+|l|)+C\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{M_{l}(4^{j+1}\rho)-M_{l}(4\rho)}{2^{\frac{sp}{p-1}j}},

and hence

I_{2}\leq C(\mathrm{Tail}(u;x_{0},r_{\ast})+|l|)+C\int_{0}^{r_{\ast}/4}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(2^{-\frac{sp}{p-1}j}\frac{M_{l}(4^{j+1}\rho)-M_{l}(4\rho)}{\rho}{\bf 1}_{\{4^{j}\rho<r_{\ast}\}}\right)\mathrm{d}\rho.

By using Fubini’s theorem and a change of variables, we have

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{r_{\ast}/4}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(2^{-\frac{sp}{p-1}j}\frac{M_{l}(4^{j+1}\rho)-M_{l}(4\rho)}{\rho}{\bf 1}_{\{4^{j}\rho<r_{\ast}\}}\right)\mathrm{d}\rho\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(2^{-\frac{sp}{p-1}j}\int_{0}^{r_{\ast}/4^{j}}\frac{M_{l}(4^{j+1}\rho)-M_{l}(4\rho)}{\rho}\,\mathrm{d}\rho\right)\\
&\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(2^{-\frac{sp}{p-1}j}\int_{r_{\ast}/4^{j}}^{r_{\ast}}\frac{M_{l}(4\rho)}{\rho}\,\mathrm{d}\rho\right)\\
&\leq(\log 4)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j2^{-\frac{sp}{p-1}j}\right)\left(\wsup_{B_{4r_{\ast}}(x_{0})}u+|l|\right)\\
&\leq C\left(\wsup_{B_{4r_{\ast}}(x_{0})}u+|l|\right),\end{split}

and consequently,

(4.14)

I_{2}\leq C\left(\mathrm{Tail}(u;x_{0},r_{\ast})+\wsup_{B_{4r_{\ast}}(x_{0})}u+|l|\right)<+\infty.

Note that the left-hand side of (
4.9
) diverges by the assumption of the sufficient condition, while the right-hand side is finite by (
4.10
) and (
4.14
). This leads us to a contradiction, and hence the first inequality in (
4.1
) holds. The second inequality in (
4.1
) can be proved in the same way, so we conclude (
2.5
).
∎

5.
The necessary condition for the regularity of a boundary point

In this section, the necessary condition is established for a boundary point to be regular with respect to
\mathcal{L}
. We develop a dual approach to
(s,p)
-capacity as a nonlocal analog of the approach in
[
31
]
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open set and
K\subset\Omega
a compact set. Let
u=\mathfrak{R}(K,\Omega)
be the
\mathcal{L}
-potential of
K
in
\Omega
. Then, we recall that, by
Lemma

2.16
(iv),
u
is a weak supersolution of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
\Omega
, meaning that
\mathcal{E}^{k}(u,\varphi)\geq 0
for every nonnegative function
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Thus, there exists a unique nonnegative Radon measure
\mu_{u}
in
\Omega
such that
\mathcal{L}u=\mu_{u}
in
\Omega
in the sense of distribution, that is,

\langle\mu_{u},\varphi\rangle:=\int_{\Omega}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{u}=\mathcal{E}^{k}(u,\varphi)

for every
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. We call this measure
\mu_{u}
the
\mathcal{L}
-distribution of
K
with respect to
\Omega
. Again, by
Lemma

2.16
(i), we have
u\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
, which implies that
\mu_{u}\in(W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega))^{\ast}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open set and
K\subset\Omega
be a compact set. If
u=\mathfrak{R}(K,\Omega)
is the
\mathcal{L}
-potential of
K
in
\Omega
with the
\mathcal{L}
-distribution
\mu_{u}
, then

\displaystyle\mu_{u}(E)\leq\Lambda\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(K\cap E,\Omega)

for every compact set
E\subset\Omega
.

Proof.

Let
\nu
be the measure obtained by restricting the
\mathcal{L}
-distribution
\mu_{u}
to the compact set
E
. We know that
\mu_{u},\nu\in(W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega))^{\ast}
. Let
v\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
be the unique weak solution of
\mathcal{L}v=\nu
in
\Omega
guaranteed by
Theorem

2.4
. Then,
0\leq v\leq u\leq 1
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by
Theorem

2.5
.

Let
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
be such that
\varphi\equiv 1
on the compact set
K\cap E
. Since
\mu_{u}
and
\nu
are supported on
K
and
K\cap E
, respectively, we obtain

(5.1)

\mu_{u}(E)=\nu(E)=\int_{\Omega}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\nu=\mathcal{E}^{k}(v,\varphi)\leq\frac{p-1}{p}\mathcal{E}^{k}(v,v)+\frac{1}{p}\mathcal{E}^{k}(\varphi,\varphi),

where we used Young’s inequality in the last inequality. To estimate
\mathcal{E}^{k}(v,v)
, we consider a test function

\overline{\varphi}(x):=\max\{\varphi(x),v(x)\}\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega).

Note that
\overline{\varphi}\equiv\varphi\equiv 1
on
K\cap E
and
\overline{\varphi}-\varphi\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega\setminus(K\cap E))
. Moreover, by definition of
\overline{\varphi}
, it is immediate that
\overline{\varphi}-v\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
and that
\overline{\varphi}-v
is nonnegative in
\Omega
.

Recalling that
v
is a weak supersolution of
\mathcal{L}v=0
in
\Omega
and a weak solution of
\mathcal{L}v=0
in
\Omega\setminus(K\cap E)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{k}(v,\varphi-v)=\mathcal{E}^{k}(v,\varphi-\overline{\varphi})+\mathcal{E}^{k}(v,\overline{\varphi}-v)\geq 0.

Hence, we obtain

(5.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{k}(v,v)\leq\mathcal{E}^{k}(v,\varphi).

By combining two estimates (
5.1
) and (
5.2
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\mu_{u}(E)\leq\mathcal{E}^{k}(\varphi,\varphi)\leq\Lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

for any
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
with
\varphi\equiv 1
on
K\cap E
. We complete the proof by taking the infimum over all such
\varphi
.
∎

The necessary part of the proof of
Theorem

1.1
is based on pointwise estimates of
\mathcal{L}
-superharmonic functions in terms of Wolff potentials. We recall from
[
36
]
the definition of the Wolff potential with fractional orders.

Definition 5.2

(Wolff potential)
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
and
p\in(1,\infty)
. Let
\mu
be a nonnegative Borel measure with finite total mass on

. The
Wolff potential
{\bf W}^{\mu}_{s,p}
of the measure

is defined by

\displaystyle{\bf W}^{\mu}_{s,p}(x_{0},r):=\int_{0}^{r}\left(\frac{\mu(B_{\rho}(x_{0}))}{\rho^{n-sp}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho}{\rho},

whenever
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
0<r\leq\infty
.

We now present a pointwise estimate for nonnegative
\mathcal{L}
-superharmonic functions in
\Omega
in terms of the Wolff potential. It will be stated in a slightly different context, compared to the potential estimate established in
[
36
]
. Note that estimates in
[

]
are obtained for SOLA (Solutions Obtained as Limits of Approximations), which requires

. Our alternative approach is more suitable for proving the necessity of the Wiener criterion for all ranges of

; see also
[
1
,
6
,
30
,
31
,
32
,

]
for similar results concerning local operators.

Theorem 5.3
.

Assume that
p\in(1,n/s]
. Let
u
be an
\mathcal{L}
-superharmonic function in
B_{4r}(x_{0})
which is nonnegative in
B_{4r}(x_{0})
. If
\mu=\mathcal{L}u
exists, then

u(x_{0})\leq C\left(\inf_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u+{\bf W}_{s,p}^{\mu}(x_{0},2r)+\mathrm{Tail}(u;x_{0},r)\right)

for some
C=C(n,s,p,\Lambda)>0
.

Note that the value
u(x_{0})
in
Theorem

5.3
is defined as in
Theorem

2.13
. To prove
Theorem

5.3
, we need the following auxiliary estimate.

Lemma 5.4
.

Assume that
p\in(1,n/s]
. Let
u
be an
\mathcal{L}
-superharmonic function in
B_{2r}(x_{0})
and suppose that
\mu=\mathcal{L}u
exists. Assume that

(5.3)

p-1<\gamma<\frac{n(p-1)}{n-s(p-1)},

then there exists
C=C(n,s,p,\Lambda,\gamma)>0
such that, for any

and

,

(5.4)

\begin{split}\left(d^{-\gamma}\fint_{B_{r}(x_{0})}(u(x)-a)_{+}^{\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p/q}&\leq Cd^{-\gamma}\fint_{B_{2r}(x_{0})}(u(x)-a)_{+}^{\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}x+Cd^{1-p}\frac{\mu(B_{2r}(x_{0}))}{r^{n-sp}}\\
&\quad+Cd^{1-p}\mathrm{Tail}^{p-1}((u-a)_{+};x_{0},2r),\end{split}

provided that

(5.5)

|B_{2r}(x_{0})\cap\{u>a\}|<\frac{1}{2}d^{-\gamma}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}(u(x)-a)_{+}^{\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}x,

where

.

Proof.

It is enough to prove the lemma for locally bounded function
u
. Indeed, an
\mathcal{L}
-superharmonic function

can be approximated by a sequence of bounded
\mathcal{L}
-superharmonic functions
u_{j}:=\min\{u,j\}
,

. By the argument as in the beginning of this section, there exists a unique nonnegative Radon measure
\mu_{u_{j}}
in
\Omega
such that
\mathcal{L}u_{j}=\mu_{u_{j}}
in
\Omega
in the sense of distribution. Then, the hypothesis (
5.5
) holds for
u_{j}
for sufficiently large

. Applying the result to
u_{j}
, we obtain (
5.4
) with
u
and
\mu
replaced by
u_{j}
and

\displaystyle\int_{0}\left(\frac{\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(D_{t},B_{2t})}{t^{n-sp}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}<+\infty,

where
D_{t}
is defined as (
4.3
). To prove this, we shall employ
Lemma

5.5
; i.e., we will show that there is a
\rho\in(0,1)
such that the condition (
5.13
) holds for the
\mathcal{L}
-potential
u_{\rho}(x)=\mathfrak{R}(D_{\rho},B_{8\rho})
of
D_{\rho}
in
B_{8\rho}
.

Let
\mu_{\rho}
be the
\mathcal{L}
-distribution of
D_{\rho}
with respect to
B_{8\rho}
, that is,
\mathcal{L}u_{\rho}=\mu_{\rho}
in
B_{8\rho}
. By
Theorem

2.9
and
Remark

2.14
(ii), we may assume that
u
is
\mathcal{L}
-superharmonic in
B_{8\rho}
. Then,
Theorem

5.3
yields

(5.21)

u_{\rho}(x_{0})\leq C\left(\inf_{B_{2\rho}}u_{\rho}+{\bf W}_{s,p}^{\mu_{\rho}}(x_{0},4\rho)+\mathrm{Tail}(u_{\rho};x_{0},2\rho)\right).

By
Lemma

5.1
, we have

(5.22)

\begin{split}{\bf W}^{\mu_{\rho}}_{s,p}(x_{0},4\rho)&\leq\int_{0}^{4\rho}\left(\frac{\mu_{\rho}(\overline{B_{t}})}{t^{n-sp}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\\
&\leq\Lambda^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\int_{0}^{4\rho}\left(\frac{\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(D_{t},B_{8\rho})}{t^{n-sp}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\\
&\leq\Lambda^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\int_{0}^{4\rho}\left(\frac{\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(D_{t},B_{2t})}{t^{n-sp}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}.\end{split}

Thus, it follows from (
5.20
) that
{\bf W}_{s,p}^{\mu_{\rho}}(x_{0},4\rho)\to 0
as
\rho\to 0
.

For the first term on the right-hand side of (
5.21
), we claim that

(5.23)

\inf_{B_{2\rho}}u_{\rho}\leq C\left(\frac{\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(D_{\rho},B_{2\rho})}{\rho^{n-sp}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

Let
\lambda_{\rho}=\essinf_{B_{2\rho}}u_{\rho}
and define
v_{\rho}(x)=\min\{u_{\rho}(x),\lambda_{\rho}\}
. By testing the equation
\mathcal{L}u_{\rho}=\mu_{\rho}
with
v_{\rho}
, we obtain

(5.24)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{k}(u_{\rho},v_{\rho})=\int_{B_{8\rho}}v_{\rho}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\rho}\leq\lambda_{\rho}\mu_{\rho}(\overline{B_{\rho}})

since the support of
\mu_{\rho}
is contained in
\overline{B_{\rho}}
. Moreover, we claim that

\displaystyle|u_{\rho}(x)-u_{\rho}(y)|^{p-2}(u_{\rho}(x)-u_{\rho}(y))(v_{\rho}(x)-v_{\rho}(y))\geq|v_{\rho}(x)-v_{\rho}(y)|^{p}.

Indeed, we may assume
u_{\rho}(x)\geq u_{\rho}(y)
without loss of generality. Then,

\displaystyle|u_{\rho}(x)-u_{\rho}(y)|^{p-2}(u_{\rho}(x)-u_{\rho}(y))(v_{\rho}(x)-v_{\rho}(y))

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\textrm{if $u_{\rho}(x)\geq u_{\rho}(y)\geq\lambda_{\rho}$},\\
(u_{\rho}(x)-u_{\rho}(y))^{p-1}(\lambda_{\rho}-v_{\rho}(y)),&\textrm{if $u_{\rho}(x)>\lambda_{\rho}\geq u_{\rho}(y)$},\\
|v_{\rho}(x)-v_{\rho}(y)|^{p},&\textrm{if $\lambda_{\rho}\geq u_{\rho}(x)\geq u_{\rho}(y)$}\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\geq|v_{\rho}(x)-v_{\rho}(y)|^{p}.

Therefore, we have

(5.25)

\mathcal{E}^{k}(v_{\rho},v_{\rho})\leq\mathcal{E}^{k}(u_{\rho},v_{\rho}).

We may assume without loss of generality that
\lambda_{\rho}>0
. Then, it is easily checked that
v_{\rho}\in W_{0}^{s,p}(B_{8\rho})
and
v_{\rho}\equiv\lambda_{\rho}
on
\overline{B_{2\rho}}
. Thus,
v_{\rho}/\lambda_{\rho}
is admissible for
(s,p)
-capacity of
\overline{B_{\rho}}
with respect to
B_{8\rho}
. Therefore, the ellipticity condition (
1.1
) yields

(5.26)

\displaystyle\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(\overline{B_{\rho}},B_{8\rho})\leq\mathcal{E}^{s,p}(v_{\rho}/\lambda_{\rho},v_{\rho}/\lambda_{\rho})=\lambda_{\rho}^{-p}\mathcal{E}^{s,p}(v_{\rho},v_{\rho})\leq\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda_{\rho}^{p}}\mathcal{E}^{k}(v_{\rho},v_{\rho}).

By combining (
5.24
), (
5.25
), (
5.26
), and
Lemma

2.17
, we obtain

\displaystyle\rho^{n-sp}\leq C\lambda_{\rho}^{-(p-1)}\mu_{\rho}(\overline{B_{\rho}}).

This inequality, together with
Lemma

5.1
, proves (
5.23
).

We next show that

(5.27)

\displaystyle\liminf_{\rho\to 0}\frac{\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(D_{\rho},B_{2\rho})}{\rho^{n-sp}}=0.

Assume to the contrary that there exists a constant
\alpha>0
such that

\liminf_{\rho\to 0}\frac{\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(D_{\rho},B_{2\rho})}{\rho^{n-sp}}\geq 2\alpha.

Then, there exists a constant
\rho_{0}>0
such that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(D_{\rho},B_{2\rho})}{\rho^{n-sp}}\geq\alpha\quad\textrm{for all $\rho\in(0,\rho_{0})$.}

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(D_{\rho},B_{2\rho})}{\rho^{n-sp}}\right)^{1/(p-1)}\frac{1}{\rho}\geq\frac{\alpha^{1/(p-1)}}{\rho}\quad\text{for all }\rho\in(0,\rho_{0}),

which contradicts to (
5.20
).

It only remains to estimate the tail term in (
5.21
). Since
u_{\rho}\in W^{s,p}_{0}(B_{8\rho})
, we have

\mathrm{Tail}(u_{\rho};x_{0},2\rho)=\left((2\rho)^{sp}\int_{B_{8\rho}\setminus B_{2\rho}}\frac{u_{\rho}^{p-1}(y)}{|y-x_{0}|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

We observe that, by minimizing property of the
\mathcal{L}
-potential
u_{\rho}
(
Lemma

2.16
(iii)) and the ellipticity assumption (
1.1
), we have

\mathcal{E}^{k}(u_{\rho})\leq\mathcal{E}^{k}(v)\leq\Lambda\mathcal{E}^{s,p}(v)

for all functions
v\in W_{0}(D_{\rho},B_{8\rho})
admissible for
\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(D_{\rho},B_{8\rho})
. Taking the infimum over
v\in W_{0}(D_{\rho},B_{8\rho})
, we obatin

\Lambda\,\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(D_{\rho},B_{8\rho})\geq\int_{B_{10\rho}\setminus B_{8\rho}}\int_{B_{8\rho}\setminus B_{2\rho}}|u_{\rho}(x)-u_{\rho}(y)|^{p}k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x.

For
x\in B_{10\rho}\setminus B_{8\rho}
and
y\in B_{8\rho}\setminus B_{2\rho}
, it holds that

|x-y|\leq 18\rho\leq 9|y-x_{0}|.

Thus, we obtain

\begin{split}\Lambda\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(D_{\rho},B_{8\rho})&\geq\frac{1}{\Lambda 9^{n+sp}}\int_{B_{10\rho}\setminus B_{8\rho}}\int_{B_{8\rho}\setminus B_{2\rho}}\frac{u_{\rho}^{p}(y)}{|y-x_{0}|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\geq C\rho^{n}\int_{B_{8\rho}\setminus B_{2\rho}}\frac{u_{\rho}^{p}(y)}{|y-x_{0}|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y.\end{split}

By applying Young’s inequality, for any
\varepsilon>0
we have

\begin{split}\int_{B_{8\rho}\setminus B_{2\rho}}\frac{u_{\rho}^{p-1}(y)}{|y-x_{0}|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y&\leq\frac{p-1}{p}\varepsilon^{-\frac{p}{p-1}}\int_{B_{8\rho}\setminus B_{2\rho}}\frac{u_{\rho}^{p}(y)}{|y-x_{0}|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y+\frac{1}{p}\varepsilon^{p}\int_{B_{8\rho}\setminus B_{2\rho}}\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{|y-x_{0}|^{n+sp}}\\
&\leq C\varepsilon^{-\frac{p}{p-1}}\rho^{-n}\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(D_{\rho},B_{2\rho})+C\varepsilon^{p}\rho^{-sp}.\end{split}

Therefore, we estimate

\mathrm{Tail}(u_{\rho};x_{0},2\rho)\leq C\left(\varepsilon^{-\frac{p}{p-1}}\frac{\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(D_{\rho},B_{2\rho})}{\rho^{n-sp}}+\varepsilon^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

Taking
\varepsilon
sufficiently small and then using (
5.27
), we can make
\mathrm{Tail}(u_{\rho};x_{0},2\rho)
as small as we want.

There exists a small
\rho>0
such that

\displaystyle u(x_{0})=u_{\rho}(x_{0})<1,

as required. We conclude that
x_{0}
is an irregular boundary point by
Lemma

5.5
.
∎

Appendix A
Algebraic inequalities

In this section, we provide some algebraic inequalities that are used in the Caccioppoli-type estimates. Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
be such that
\beta=\gamma-(p-1)
. Recall that, in the local case, we use the following inequalities for the proof of Caccioppoli-type estimates
[
21
, Theorem 8.25 and 8.26]
: assume
\beta\neq 0
,
\gamma\neq 0
, and let
l\geq 0
. Then, there exists constants
c,C>0
, depending only on
p
, such that

\begin{split}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla(f(u)\eta^{p})&\geq|\nabla F(u)|^{p}\eta^{p}-\frac{|\gamma|}{|\beta|}|\nabla F(u)|^{p-1}\eta^{p-1}|F(u)||\nabla\eta|\\
&\geq\frac{1}{p}\left(|\nabla F(u)|^{p}\eta^{p}-\left(\frac{|\gamma|}{|\beta|}\right)^{p}|F(u)|^{p}|\nabla\eta|^{p}\right)\\
&\geq c\,|\nabla(F(u)\eta)|^{p}-C\left(1+\left(\frac{|\gamma|}{|\beta|}\right)^{p}\right)|F(u)|^{p}|\nabla\eta|^{p},\end{split}

where
f,F:I\to[0,\infty)
are defined by

(A.1)

f(t)=\frac{1}{\beta}\left((t+d)^{\beta}-(l+d)^{\beta}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad F(t)=\frac{p}{\gamma}(t+d)^{\gamma/p}.

Here,
I=[l,\infty)
when
\beta>0
and
I=[0,l]
when
\beta<0
. As a discrete version, we prove the following algebraic inequality.

Lemma A.1
.

Assume that
\beta\neq 0
and
\gamma\neq 0
. There exist
c,C>0
, depending only on
p
, such that

\begin{split}&|a-b|^{p-2}(a-b)(f(a)\eta_{1}^{p}-f(b)\eta_{2}^{p})\\
&\geq c\left|F(a)\eta_{1}-F(b)\eta_{2}\right|^{p}-C\left(1+\left(\frac{|\gamma|}{|\beta|}\right)^{p}\right)\max\{|F(a)|,|F(b)|\}^{p}|\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}|^{p}\end{split}

for any
a,b\in I
and
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\geq 0
.

Some inequalities similar to
Lemma

A.1
are known for almost all values of
\beta
. See, for instance,
[
3
]
for
\beta\geq 1
and
[
5
]
for
\beta<-(p-1)
.
Lemma

A.1
can be proved in a similar way, but let us provide a proof to make the paper self-contained. To this end, we first prove the following two lemmas as intermediate steps.

Lemma A.2
.

If
\beta\neq 0
and
\gamma\neq 0
, then

|a-b|^{p-2}(a-b)(f(a)-f(b))\geq|F(a)-F(b)|^{p}

and

|a-b|^{p-1}\min\left\{F^{\prime}(a),F^{\prime}(b)\right\}^{p-1}\leq|F(a)-F(b)|^{p-1}

for any
a,b\in I
.

Proof.

We may assume that
a>b
. Then, we have

\left|\frac{F(a)-F(b)}{a-b}\right|^{p}=\left|\fint_{b}^{a}F^{\prime}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\right|^{p}\leq\fint_{b}^{a}f^{\prime}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{f(a)-f(b)}{a-b}

by Jensen’s inequality, and

\left|\frac{F(a)-F(b)}{a-b}\right|^{p-1}=\left|\fint_{b}^{a}F^{\prime}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\right|^{p-1}\geq\min\{F^{\prime}(a),F^{\prime}(b)\}^{p-1}

since
F^{\prime}
is positive on
[0,\infty)
.
∎

Lemma A.3
.

Let
A,B\in\mathbb{R}
and
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\geq 0
, then

\begin{split}|A-B|^{p}\min\{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\}^{p}&\geq 2^{1-p}|A\eta_{1}-B\eta_{2}|^{p}-\max\{|A|,|B|\}^{p}|\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}|^{p}\quad\text{and}\\
|A-B|^{p}\max\{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\}^{p}&\leq 2^{p-1}|A\eta_{1}-B\eta_{2}|^{p}+2^{p-1}\max\{|A|,|B|\}^{p}|\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}|^{p}.\end{split}

Proof.

We may assume that
\eta_{1}\geq\eta_{2}
. Then, the desired inequalities follow from the equalities

A\eta_{1}-B\eta_{2}=(A-B)\eta_{2}+A(\eta_{1}-\eta_{2})=(A-B)\eta_{1}+B(\eta_{1}-\eta_{2})

and the triangle inequality.
∎

We prove
Lemma

A.1
by using
Lemma

A.2
and
Lemma

A.3
.

Proof of
Lemma

A.1
.

We may assume that
a>b
. Then, we have

(A.2)

\displaystyle(a-b)^{p-1}(f(a)\eta_{1}^{p}-f(b)\eta_{2}^{p})

\displaystyle=(a-b)^{p-1}(f(a)-f(b))\eta_{1}^{p}+(a-b)^{p-1}f(b)(\eta_{1}^{p}-\eta_{2}^{p})

(A.3)

\displaystyle=(a-b)^{p-1}(f(a)-f(b))\eta_{2}^{p}+(a-b)^{p-1}f(a)(\eta_{1}^{p}-\eta_{2}^{p}).

We apply
Lemma

A.2
to (
A.2
) if
\beta\geq 1
or to (
A.3
) if
\beta\leq 1
. Then, we obtain

\begin{split}J:=&~(a-b)^{p-1}(f(a)\eta_{1}^{p}-f(b)\eta_{2}^{p})\\
\geq&~|F(a)-F(b)|^{p}\min\{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\}^{p}-|F(a)-F(b)|^{p-1}\max\left\{\frac{|f(a)|}{F^{\prime}(a)^{p-1}},\frac{|f(b)|}{F^{\prime}(b)^{p-1}}\right\}|\eta_{1}^{p}-\eta_{2}^{p}|.\end{split}

By using

\frac{|f(t)|}{F^{\prime}(t)^{p-1}}\leq\frac{|\gamma|}{p|\beta|}|F(t)|,\quad|\eta_{1}^{p}-\eta_{2}^{p}|\leq p|\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}|\max\{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\}^{p-1},

and Young’s inequality, we deduce

\begin{split}J&\geq|F(a)-F(b)|^{p}\min\{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\}^{p}\\
&\quad-\frac{1}{2^{2p-1}}|F(a)-F(b)|^{p}\max\{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\}^{p}-C\left(\frac{|\gamma|}{|\beta|}\right)^{p}\max\{|F(a)|,|F(b)|\}^{p}|\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}|^{p},\end{split}

where
C=C(p)>0
. Applying
Lemma

A.3
with
A=F(a)
and
B=F(b)
finishes the proof.
∎

The case
\gamma=0
, or equivalently
\beta=-(p-1)
, is treated in the following lemma, which is a discrete version of

|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla(f(u)\eta^{p})\geq c|\nabla\log(u+d)|^{p}\eta^{p}-C|\nabla\eta|^{p},

where
f
is given by (
A.1
) with
\beta=-(p-1)
and
c,C>0
are constants depending only on
p
.

Lemma A.4
.

Assume that
\gamma=\beta+p-1=0
. There exist
c,C>0
, depending only on
p
, such that

|a-b|^{p-2}(a-b)(f(a)\eta_{1}^{p}-f(b)\eta_{2}^{p})\geq c\,|\log(a+d)-\log(b+d)|^{p}\min\{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\}^{p}-C|\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}|^{p}

for any
a,b\in I
and
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\geq 0
.

Proof.

We may assume that
a>b
. By applying
[
9
, Lemma 3.1]
to
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
with

\varepsilon=\delta\frac{a-b}{a+d}\in(0,1),\quad\delta\in(0,1),

we have

\eta_{1}^{p}-\eta_{2}^{p}\leq C\varepsilon\eta_{2}^{p}+C\varepsilon^{1-p}|\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}|^{p},

where
C=C(p)>0
. Thus, it follows from (
A.3
) and
f(a)\geq\frac{1}{1-p}(a+d)^{1-p}
that

\begin{split}K:=&~(a-b)^{p-1}(f(a)\eta_{1}^{p}-f(b)\eta_{2}^{p})\\
\geq&~(a-b)^{p-1}(f(a)-f(b))\eta_{2}^{p}-C\delta\left(\frac{a-b}{a+d}\right)^{p}\eta_{2}^{p}-C\delta^{1-p}|\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}|^{p}.\end{split}

By using the same arguments as in the proof of
Lemma

A.2
, we obtain

\left(\frac{a-b}{a+d}\right)^{p}\leq|\log(a+d)-\log(b+d)|^{p}\leq(a-b)^{p-1}(f(a)-f(b)).

Therefore, we deduce that

K\geq(1-C\delta)|\log(a+d)-\log(b+d)|^{p}\eta_{2}^{p}-C\delta^{1-p}|\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}|^{p},

from which we conclude the lemma by taking
\delta
sufficiently small.
∎

We also provide the following algebraic inequality, which is similar to
Lemma

A.1
.

Lemma A.5
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
,
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\geq 0
, and assume that
\gamma
satisfies (
5.3
). Let
\tau=\gamma/(p-1)
and
q=p\gamma/(p-\gamma/(p-1))
, then

\begin{split}&|a-b|^{p-2}(a-b)(g(a_{+})\eta_{1}^{p}-g(b_{+})\eta_{2}^{p})\\
&\geq c\,|G(a_{+})\eta_{1}-G(b_{+})\eta_{2}|^{p}-C\max\{(1+a_{+})^{\gamma},(1+b_{+})^{\gamma}\}{\bf 1}_{\{a>0\}}{\bf 1}_{\{b>0\}}|\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}|^{p},\end{split}

where

g(t)=\frac{1}{\tau-1}(1-(1+t)^{1-\tau}),\quad G(t)=\frac{q}{\gamma}((1+t)^{\gamma/q}-1),

and
c,C>0
are constants depending only on
p
.

Proof.

We may assume that
a>b
. The same arguments as in the proof of
Lemma

A.2
show that

(A.4)

(a_{+}-b_{+})^{p-1}(g(a_{+})-g(b_{+}))\geq|G(a_{+})-G(b_{+})|^{p}

and

(a_{+}-b_{+})^{p-1}g^{\prime}(a_{+})^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\leq|G(a_{+})-G(b_{+})|^{p-1}.

The case
b\leq 0
follows from (
A.4
). Thus, we assume
b>0
. Since
a-b\geq a_{+}-b_{+}
,
(a-b)^{p-1}g(b_{+})=(a_{+}-b_{+})^{p-1}g(b_{+})
, and
g(b_{+})\leq 1
, we obtain

\begin{split}L:=&~(a-b)^{p-1}(g(a_{+})\eta_{1}^{p}-g(b_{+})\eta_{2}^{p})\\
=&~(a-b)^{p-1}((g(a_{+})-g(b_{+}))\eta_{1}^{p}+g(b_{+})(\eta_{1}^{p}-\eta_{2}^{p}))\\
\geq&~(a_{+}-b_{+})^{p-1}((g(a_{+})-g(b_{+}))\eta_{1}^{p}+g(b_{+})(\eta_{1}^{p}-\eta_{2}^{p}))\\
\geq&~|G(a_{+})-G(b_{+})|^{p}\eta_{1}^{p}-p(a_{+}-b_{+})^{p-1}|\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}|\max\{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\}^{p-1}\\
\geq&~|G(a_{+})-G(b_{+})|^{p}\eta_{1}^{p}-p|G(a_{+})-G(b_{+})|^{p-1}\max\{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\}^{p-1}g^{\prime}(a_{+})^{-\frac{p-1}{p}}|\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}|.\end{split}

By using Young’s inequality,
Lemma

A.3
, and
G(t)^{p}\leq C(1+t)^{\gamma}
, we conclude that

\begin{split}L&\geq|G(a_{+})-G(b_{+})|^{p}\min\{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\}^{p}\\
&\quad-\frac{1}{2^{2p-1}}|G(a_{+})-G(b_{+})|^{p}\max\{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\}^{p}-C\frac{1}{g^{\prime}(a_{+})^{p-1}}|\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}|^{p}\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2^{p}}|G(a_{+})\eta_{1}-G(b_{+})\eta_{2}|^{p}-C\max\{(1+a_{+})^{\gamma},(1+b_{+})^{\gamma}\}|\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}|^{p},\end{split}

where
C=C(p)>0
.
∎

Let us finish the section with one more algebraic inequality.

Lemma A.6
.

Let
a,b>0
and assume that
\gamma<p
,
\gamma\neq 0
. Then,

\max\{a,b\}^{\gamma-p}|a-b|^{p}\leq\left(\frac{p}{|\gamma|}\right)^{p}|a^{\gamma/p}-b^{\gamma/p}|^{p}.

Proof.

We may assume without loss of generality that
a>b
. Then,

|a^{\gamma/p}-b^{\gamma/p}|^{p}=\left|\int_{b}^{a}\frac{\gamma}{p}t^{\gamma/p-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\right|^{p}\geq\left|\frac{\gamma}{p}\right|^{p}a^{\gamma-p}(a-b)^{p},

where we use the monotonicity of
t\mapsto t^{\gamma/p-1}
.
∎

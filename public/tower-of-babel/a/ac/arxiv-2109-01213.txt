Zelevinsky Duality on Basic Local Shimura Varieties
Linus Hamann

Abstract




Abstract.
We give a simple proof of a general result describing the action of the Zelevinsky involution on the cohomology of certain basic local Shimura varieties, using the machinery of Fargues-Scholze
[
FS21
]
. As an application, we generalize earlier results of Fargues
[
Far06
]
and Mieda
[
Mie14
]
on the action of the Zelevinsky involution on the cohomology of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
and
\mathrm{GSp}_{4}
type basic local Shimura varieties, respectively.

Introduction

Let
\mu

G

(G,b,\mu)
defines a diamond

and moreover carries a (non-effective) descent datum from
\breve{E}
down to

\mathrm{Sht}(G,b,\mu)_{\infty}/\underline{K}=:\mathrm{Sht}(G,b,\mu)_{K}

for varying open compact

defines a basic local Shimura datum. In this case, the members of this tower are represented by rigid analytic spaces and define local Shimura varieties in the sense of Rapoport-Viehmann
[
RV14
]
. Let

be the algebraic closure of the

-sheaf

[
FS21

\mathrm{Sht}(G,b,\mu)_{K,\mathbb{C}_{p}}
denote the base-change of these spaces to

R\Gamma_{c}(G,b,\mu):=\mathop{\rm colim}\nolimits_{K\rightarrow\{1\}}R\Gamma_{c}(\mathrm{Sht}(G,b,\mu)_{K,\mathbb{C}_{p}},\mathcal{S}_{\mu})\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}

of
G(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\times J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\times W_{E}
-modules. It is an important problem to describe the cohomology of these complexes as they are known to realize instances of the local Langlands correspondences and can be related to the cohomology of global Shimura varieties via uniformization in the case that
\mu
is minuscule. More specifically, for
\pi
(resp.
\rho

(resp.

R\Gamma_{c}(G,b,\mu)[\pi]:=R\Gamma_{c}(G,b,\mu)\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{H}(G)}\pi

R\Gamma_{c}(G,b,\mu)[\rho]:=R\Gamma_{c}(G,b,\mu)\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{H}(J_{b})}\rho

where
\mathcal{H}(G):=C^{\infty}_{c}(G(\mathbb{Q}_{p}),\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
(resp.
\mathcal{H}(J_{b})
) is the usual smooth Hecke algebra of
G
(resp.
J_{b}
). It is a recent result of Fargues-Scholze that
R\Gamma_{c}(G,b,\mu)[\pi]
(resp.
R\Gamma_{c}(G,b,\mu)[\rho]
) are valued in admissible
J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
(resp.
G(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
)-representations of finite length carrying a smooth action of
W_{E}

[
FS21
, Page 317]
. Moreover, it is concentrated in degrees
-d\leq i\leq d
, where
d=\mathrm{dim}(\mathrm{Sht}(G,b,\mu)_{\infty})=\langle 2\rho_{G},\mu\rangle
.

Let

an inner form of

\psi:W_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\rightarrow W_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\xrightarrow{\phi}\phantom{}^{L}G(\mathbb{C})

Here the first map is given by swapping the

-factors and the second map is given by trivially extending

Definition 1.1
.

For
G/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
a connected reductive group, let
\mathcal{D}_{c}(G)
be the convolution algebra of compactly supported
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-valued functions on
G(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. It contains the usual smooth Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}(G)
as a sub-algebra. We let
\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{D}_{c}(G),\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
(resp.
\mathrm{D}(G(\mathbb{Q}_{p}),\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
) denote the unbounded derived category of
\mathcal{D}_{c}(G)
-modules (resp. smooth
G(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
-modules). If we let
e_{K}\in\mathcal{H}(G)\subset\mathcal{D}_{c}(G)
be the idempotent for the Hecke algebra defined by a compact open subgroup
K\subset G(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
then we can consider the derived functor

\infty_{\mathcal{D}}:\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{D}_{c}(G),\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})\rightarrow\mathrm{D}(G(\mathbb{Q}_{p}),\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})

A\mapsto\mathop{\rm colim}\nolimits_{K\rightarrow\{1\}}e_{K}A

which is in particular exact and so has no higher derived functors (See
[
Mie14
, Section 2]
and
[
Far06
, Section 2]
for details). Then we define the (derived) Zelevinsky involution to be

\mathbb{D}_{G}:\mathrm{D}(G(\mathbb{Q}_{p}),\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})^{op}\rightarrow\mathrm{D}(G(\mathbb{Q}_{p}),\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})

A\mapsto\infty_{\mathcal{D}}\circ\mathcal{R}Hom_{\mathcal{H}(G)}(A,\mathcal{H}(G))

where
\mathcal{H}(G)
is viewed as a Hecke bi-module.

On the subcategory of compact objects, this defines a contravariant equivalence and has the property that
\mathbb{D}_{G}^{2}\simeq Id
. In this note, we seek to describe the complex
\mathbb{D}_{G}(R\Gamma_{c}(G,b,\mu)[\rho])
and
\mathbb{D}_{J_{b}}(R\Gamma_{c}(G,b,\mu)[\pi])
. This is desirable since (up to contragradients and shifts)
\mathbb{D}_{G}
should exchange the
L
-packet
\Pi_{\phi}(G)
and the
A
-packet
\Pi_{\psi}(G)

[
Hir04
]
, so better understanding the structure of this complex should give insight into the complicated way these different representations appear and interact with each other in the cohomology of
R\Gamma_{c}(G,b,\mu)[\rho]
. As a first step towards this goal, we would like to prove the existence of isomorphisms

\mathbb{D}_{J_{b}}(R\Gamma_{c}(G,b,\mu)[\pi])\simeq R\Gamma_{c}(G,b,\mu)[\mathbb{D}_{G}(\pi)]

and

\mathbb{D}_{G}(R\Gamma_{c}(G,b,\mu)[\rho])\simeq R\Gamma_{c}(G,b,\mu)[\mathbb{D}_{J_{b}}(\rho)]

as complexes of
J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\times W_{E}
and
G(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\times W_{E}
-modules, respectively.

In general, this shouldn’t be true, but, under a Hodge-Newton reducibility condition, we show that it is. In particular, our main theorem is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

For a basic local Shimura datum
(G,b,\mu)
such that the set
B(G,\mu)
is Hodge-Newton reducible (Definition 2.2) and
\pi
(resp.
\rho
) a supercuspidal representation of
G(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
(resp.
J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
), we have isomorphisms

\mathbb{D}_{J_{b}}(R\Gamma_{c}(G,b,\mu)[\pi])\simeq R\Gamma_{c}(G,b,\mu)[\mathbb{D}_{G}(\pi)]

and

\mathbb{D}_{G}(R\Gamma_{c}(G,b,\mu)[\rho])\simeq R\Gamma_{c}(G,b,\mu)[\mathbb{D}_{J_{b}}(\rho)]

as complexes of
J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\times W_{E}
and
G(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\times W_{E}
-modules, respectively.

Remark 1.1
.

A similar result should be provable through our methods with integral or torsion coefficients, for
\rho
any supercuspidal
\ell
-complete or
\ell
-modular representation, respectively. We note that in the above we have assumed
\mu
is minuscule; however, we conjecture that this is true even in the non-minuscule case.

The condition that the set
B(G,\mu)
is Hodge-Newton reducible roughly says that, for every non-basic element
b^{\prime}\in B(G,\mu)
, the Newton polygon of
b^{\prime}
seen as an element of a positive Weyl chamber touches the Hodge polygon defined by
\mu
away from the endpoints. We note that there is a complete group-theoretic classification of pairs
(G,\mu)
such that
B(G,\mu)
is Hodge-Newton reducible
[
GHN19
, Theorem 2.5]
.

We explain the idea of proof for the
\pi
-isotypic part with the proof for the
\rho
-isotypic part being analogous. The key insight is to consider the geometric analogue of the Zelevinsky involution acting on sheaves on the moduli stack
\mathrm{Bun}_{G}

\mathbb{D}_{BZ}:\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{lis}}(\mathrm{Bun}_{G},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})^{\omega,op}\rightarrow\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{lis}}(\mathrm{Bun}_{G},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})^{\omega}

as defined in
[
FS21
, Section V.5,Section VII.7]
. It defines a contravariant equivalence of
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{lis}}(\mathrm{Bun}_{G},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})^{\omega}
and has the property that
\mathbb{D}_{BZ}^{2}
is naturally isomorphic to the identity. Moreover, when restricted to the subcategories
\mathrm{D}(J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}),\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})^{\omega}
coming from basic Harder-Narasimhan(=HN)-strata of
\mathrm{Bun}_{G}
, it is given by
\mathbb{D}_{J_{b}}
. Now, with this in hand, it is useful to reinterpret the complex
R\Gamma_{c}(G,b,\mu)[\pi]
as

j_{b}^{*}T_{\mu^{-1}}j_{1!}(\mathcal{F}_{\pi})\simeq R\Gamma_{c}(G,b,\mu)[\pi]

where
j_{1}
and
j_{b}
are the inclusions of the HN-strata of
\mathrm{Bun}_{G}
corresponding to the trivial element
1\in B(G)
and
b\in B(G)
, respectively,
\mathcal{F}_{\pi}\in\mathrm{D}(G(\mathbb{Q}_{p}),\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
is the sheaf on the basic HN-stratum
\mathrm{Bun}_{G}^{1}\simeq[\ast/\underline{G(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}]
defined by
\pi
, and
T_{\mu^{-1}}
is the Hecke operator associated to
\mu^{-1}
a dominant inverse of
\mu
acting on
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{lis}}(\mathrm{Bun}_{G},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
. Given this isomorphism, Theorem 1.1 would now follow from knowing that
\mathbb{D}_{BZ}
commutes with all operations applied to
\mathcal{F}_{\pi}
on the LHS. For
j_{b!}
and the Hecke operator
T_{\mu^{-1}}
, this is shown by Fargues-Scholze. The key point is to show that it commutes with the restriction functor
j_{1}^{*}
. In general, this is not true if the complex
T_{\mu^{-1}}j_{1!}(\mathcal{F}_{\pi})
is supported on non-basic HN-strata, but in the case that
B(G,\mu)
is Hodge-Newton reducible and
\pi
is supercuspidal we show that this cannot occur. The key point is that the restriction of
T_{\mu^{-1}}j_{1!}(\mathcal{F}_{\pi})
to any non-basic HN-strata can be described in terms of the
\pi
-isotypic part of
R\Gamma_{c}(G,b^{\prime},\mu)
for non-basic
b^{\prime}\in B(G,\mu)
. However, by Hodge-Newton reducibility, the space
\mathrm{Sht}(G,b^{\prime},\mu)_{\infty}
is parabolically induced as a space with
G(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
-action from a proper parabolic of
G
, realizing some form of the Harris-Viehmann conjecture
[
RV14
, Conjecture 8.5]
. Therefore, since
\pi
is supercuspidal, it cannot contribute to the cohomology of these spaces, so the restriction vanishes.

With Theorem 1.1 in hand, we can start to derive some useful cohomological consequences. Let
\chi
denote the central character of our fixed supercuspidal
\pi
. Then the complex
R\Gamma_{c}(G,b,\mu)[\pi]
has cohomology valued in
J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
-representations with central character equal to
\chi
, where we recall that
J_{b}
is an extended pure inner form of
G
and therefore we have an isomorphism:
Z(J_{b})\simeq Z(G)
. Let
I_{\chi}(J_{b})
denote the set of equivalence classes of inertial cuspidal data
\mathfrak{s}=(M,\sigma)
, where
M\subset J_{b}
is a Levi subgroup of
J_{b}
and
\sigma
is a supercuspidal representation with
\sigma|_{Z(G)(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}=\chi
. We can consider the Bernstein decomposition

R\Gamma_{c}(G,b,\mu)[\pi]\simeq\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{s}\in I_{\chi}(G)}R\Gamma_{c}(G,b,\mu)[\pi]_{\mathfrak{s}}

where the cohomology of
R\Gamma_{c}(G,b,\mu)[\pi]_{\mathfrak{s}}
takes values in representations with supercuspidal support specified by
\mathfrak{s}
. On the other hand, since
\pi
is supercuspidal,
R\Gamma_{c}(G,b,\mu)[\mathbb{D}_{J_{b}}(\pi)]
is, up to a shift, isomorphic to
R\Gamma_{c}(G,b,\mu)[\pi^{*}]
, where
\pi^{*}
is the contragradient of
\pi
. Therefore, this complex takes values in representations with central character equal to
\chi^{-1}
. Hence, we again obtain a Bernstein decomposition:

R\Gamma_{c}(G,b,\mu)[\pi^{*}]\simeq\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{s}\in I_{\chi^{-1}}(J_{b})}R\Gamma_{c}(G,b,\mu)[\pi]_{\mathfrak{s}}

Fixing now
\mathfrak{s}=(M,\sigma)\in I_{\chi}(J_{b})
, we let
\mathfrak{s}^{*}=(M,\sigma^{*})\in I_{\chi^{-1}}(J_{b})
. Using Theorem 1.1, one can show that one has (up to a shifts) an isomorphism

\mathbb{D}_{J_{b}}(R\Gamma_{c}(G,b,\mu)[\pi]_{\mathfrak{s}})\simeq R\Gamma_{c}(G,b,\mu)[\pi^{*}]_{\mathfrak{s}^{*}}

of complexes of
J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\times W_{E}
-modules.
To a given
\mathfrak{s}
, we let
i(\mathfrak{s}):=r_{G}-r_{M}
, where

(resp.

sits in a single degree determined by

. We can define

(Definition 3.1) to be the representation determined by the non-zero cohomology. As a consequence of this, we can deduce the following result from Theorem 1.1 by passing to cohomology.

Theorem 1.2
.

For a basic local Shimura datum
(G,b,\mu)
such that
B(G,\mu)
is Hodge-Newton reducible, let
\pi
be a supercuspidal representation of
G(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
with central character
\chi
,
\mathfrak{s}\in I_{\chi}(J_{b})
with associated integer
i(\mathfrak{s})
,
\rho
a smooth irreducible representation with supercuspidal support given by
\mathfrak{s}
, and
\sigma
an irreducible representation of
W_{E}

\pi\boxtimes\rho\boxtimes\sigma

H^{q}(R\Gamma_{c}(G,b,\mu))

\pi^{*}\boxtimes Zel(\rho)\boxtimes\sigma^{*}

H^{-q+i(\mathfrak{s})}(R\Gamma_{c}(G,b,\mu))
. The analogous statement is true with the roles of
\pi
and
\rho

2.
Proof of the Main Theorem

In this section, we prove Theorem 1.1. We first address the notion of Hodge-Newton reducibility and derive a key geometric consequence. We recall that the Kottwitz set
B(G)
is equipped with two maps.

The slope homomorphism

We start by recalling some facts about the Zelevinsky involution. Set

G

\chi:Z(G)\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}^{*}

G

\sigma

\sigma|_{Z(G)}\simeq\chi

for the category of finite-length smooth representations with central character equal to
\chi
. We consider the Bernstein-decomposition
[
Ren10
, Theorem VI.7.2]
:

\mathrm{Rep}_{\chi}(G)=\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{s}\in I_{\chi}(G)}\mathrm{Rep}_{\mathfrak{s}}(G)

For
\mathfrak{s}\in I_{\chi}(G)
, we let
i(\mathfrak{s})
be the integer defined in Section
1

Proposition 3.1
.

[
SS97

For
\mathfrak{s}\in I_{\chi}(G)
and
\pi\in\mathrm{Rep}_{\mathfrak{s}}(G)
viewed as an element of
\mathrm{D}(G(\mathbb{Q}_{p}),\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
sitting in degree
0
, the cohomology of the complex
\mathbb{D}_{G}(\pi)
is concentrated only in degrees
-i(\mathfrak{s})+r
. Moreover, if
\mathfrak{s}^{*}:=(M,\sigma^{*})\in I_{\chi^{-1}}(G)
then it takes values in the Bernstein component
\mathrm{Rep}_{\mathfrak{s}^{*}}(G)
of
\mathrm{Rep}_{\chi^{-1}}(G)

This gives us the following key definition.

Definition 3.1
.

For
\pi\in\mathrm{Rep}_{\mathfrak{s}}(G)
as above, we define

to be the
-i(\mathfrak{s})+r
-cohomology of
\mathbb{D}_{G}(\pi)
. When pre-composed with the contragradient map,
Zel(-)
defines an exact categorical equivalence of
\mathrm{Rep}_{\mathfrak{s}}(G)

One can verify Proposition 3.1 by computing

explicitly in terms of the following Deligne-Luzstig complex

\mathrm{DL}(\pi):=0\rightarrow\pi\rightarrow\bigoplus_{P}i_{P}^{G}r_{P}^{G}\rightarrow\ldots\rightarrow i_{B}^{G}r_{B}^{G}(\pi)\rightarrow 0

If one has a complex

, and thereby a functor
\mathrm{DL}:\mathrm{D}(G(\mathbb{Q}_{p}),\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})\rightarrow\mathrm{D}(G(\mathbb{Q}_{p}),\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})

Theorem 3.2
.

[
SS97
,
R04
,

]

For

with admissible cohomology we have a quasi-isomorphism

\mathrm{DL}(A^{*})\simeq\mathbb{D}_{G}(A)[r]

where

denotes the smooth contragradient.

For example, using this, we can immediately deduce the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3

Let
\pi
be a supercuspidal representation of

\mathbb{D}_{G}(\pi)\simeq\pi^{*}[-r]

as

Now let’s consider the isomorphism supplied by Theorem 1.1

(1)

\mathbb{D}_{J_{b}}(R\Gamma_{c}(G,b,\mu)[\pi])\simeq R\Gamma_{c}(G,b,\mu)[\mathbb{D}_{G}(\pi)]\simeq R\Gamma_{c}(G,b,\mu)[\pi^{*}][-r]

We explain the proof for
\pi
supercuspidal with the proof for

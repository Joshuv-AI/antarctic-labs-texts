A History-dependent Dynamic Biot Model
Jakob S. Stokke \orcidID 0000-0002-2046-1475 and Morten Jakobsen \orcidID 0000-0001-8861-1938 and Kundan Kumar \orcidID 0000-0002-3784-4819 and Florin A. Radu \orcidID 0000-0002-2577-5684

Abstract
In this work, we consider a fully dynamic Biot model that includes memory effects due to evolving permeability. Time integrals are used to account for the change in structure. We propose an iterative splitting scheme for this model, extending the fixed-stress split for the quasi-static Biot. We use finite elements in space and a backward Euler discretization in time. The performance of the method is demonstrated through a numerical experiment.



1
Introduction

There is a strong interest in coupling mechanics and flow in porous media due to many relevant societal applications. For example, there are several biomedical engineering, geophysical, and environmental applications, including heart perfusion, geothermal energy extraction, and
CO_{2}
storage. The mathematical models for flow in deformable porous media consist of coupled (possibly nonlinear) partial differential equations. These models, based on Biot equations, are typically impossible to solve analytically and very challenging numerically. A lot of effort was made in the last decade for developing robust and efficient splitting schemes, but mostly for the quasi-static Biot model, see e.g.
MikelicAndro2013Coic
;
both2017etal
;
Borregales2018
;
StorvikErlend2019Otoo
.

However, when the ratio between the characteristic time and the characteristic domain time scale is large, a fully dynamic Biot model must be considered. In this work we consider the Biot-Allard model as derived by homogenization in
MikelicBiotAllard
. Specific for this model is that it includes an acceleration term in the mechanics equation and memory effects expressed as convolution integrals. The system without memory effects, which consists of two linear fully coupled equations, one hyperbolic and one parabolic, was already studied in
ShowalterR.E.2000DiPM
;
BauseMarkusSpaceTime
. A splitting scheme based on the undrained-split
KimJihoon2011SAaE
was derived and analyzed in
BauseMarkus2020ICfF
following a gradient flow approach
both2019gradient
.

In this paper, we propose a fixed-stress
KimJihoon2011SAaE
type splitting scheme for the fully dynamic Biot-Allard model including memory effects (it includes a history-dependent permeability). For the discretization in time we use an implicit Euler scheme, and in space we use Galerkin finite elements. A numerical experiment is performed to study the performance of the scheme.

The paper is structured as follows. In the next section, we present the mathematical model. Section
3
concerns the discretization and the splitting scheme. In Section
4
we have two numerical examples with a manufactured solution. The paper ends with a short concluding section.

2
A History-dependent Dynamic Biot Model

We consider flow in a saturated porous medium
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
d\in\{2,3\}
being the spatial dimension, which is elastic, homogeneous, and isotropic, and has a Lipschitz continuous boundary
\partial\Omega
.
The history-dependent dynamic Biot model we consider is a simplified Biot-Allard model: Find
(\bm{u},p)
in
\Omega\times[0,T]
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M}\partial_{t}p+\alpha\partial_{t}\nabla\cdot\bm{u}+\nabla\cdot\left[\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{A}\left(\frac{(t-\zeta)\eta}{l^{2}\rho_{f}}\right)\left(-\frac{1}{\rho_{f}}\nabla p(x,\zeta)\right)\right]d\zeta

\displaystyle=f_{2},

(1a)

\displaystyle\rho\partial_{t}^{2}\bm{u}-\nabla\cdot\left[2\mu\varepsilon(\bm{u})+\lambda\nabla\cdot\bm{u}\bm{I}-\alpha p\bm{I}\right]\quad\quad

\displaystyle+\partial_{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{A}\left(\frac{(t-\zeta)\eta}{l^{2}\rho_{f}}\right)\left(-\nabla p(x,\zeta)\right)d\zeta

\displaystyle=\textbf{f}_{1},

(1b)

with initial and boundary conditions

\displaystyle\bm{u}(0)=\bm{u}_{0},\quad\partial_{t}\bm{u}(0)=\bm{u}_{1},\quad p(0)=p_{0},\quad

\displaystyle\mbox{ in }\Omega,

(1c)

\displaystyle\bm{u}=0,\quad p=0,\quad

\displaystyle\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega\times(0,T].

(1d)

Here,
\bm{u}
denotes the solid phase displacement and
p
the pressure. Furthermore,
\alpha
is the Biot coefficient,
\rho=\rho_{f}\varphi+\rho_{s}(1-\varphi)
is the mass density with
\varphi
,
\rho_{s}
and
\rho_{f}
being the porosity, solid and fluid density respectively,
\lambda
and
\mu
are the Lamé parameters,
\frac{1}{M}
is the compressibility constant,
\eta
is the pore fluid viscosity,
l
the typical pore size,
\bm{I}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is the identity matrix and
\varepsilon(\bm{u})=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\bm{u}+\nabla\bm{u}^{T})
the linearized strain tensor. The right-hand side functions of (
1
),
\bm{f}_{1}
and
f_{2}
, are an external force density and a volumetric source, respectively. The changes in permeability are included in
\mathcal{A}(\cdot)
the dynamic permeability tensor. It can be computed by solving a Stokes system at the pore-scale (see
(
MikelicBiotAllard
, p.15)
). In this work we will assume that the solution to the Stokes problem is known. We refer to the recent paper
stokke2024a
for the well-posedness of the dynamic Biot model with memory effects.

3
A Fully Discrete Numerical Scheme

We use functional analysis notations throughout our paper. The space of Lebesque measurable and square-integrable functions is denoted by
L^{2}(\Omega)
. By
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
and
\|\cdot\|
we denote the
L^{2}(\Omega)
scalar product and norm. Further
H^{1}(\Omega)
is the space with
L^{2}
-functions having weak first order derivatives in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega):=\left\{f\in H^{1}(\Omega)\,\,|f=0\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega\right\}
.

3.1
Discretization of Convolution Integrals

To discretize (
1
), we first need to deal with the convolution terms. We neglect the scaling factor
\eta/(l^{2}\rho_{f})
for the ease of the presentation. We also assume that a part of the history of the porous medium is known before the initial time, i.e.,
\mathcal{A}(-\tau)
for a
\tau>0
. The convolutions are approximated using the trapezoid rule

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\mathcal{A}\left(t_{n}-\zeta\right)\left(-\nabla p(x,\zeta)\right)d\zeta\approx-\frac{1}{2}\big(\mathcal{A}\left(0\right)\nabla p(x,t_{n})

\displaystyle+2\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\mathcal{A}\left(t_{n}-\zeta_{i}\right)\nabla p(x,\zeta_{i})+\mathcal{A}\left(t_{n}\right)\nabla p(x,0)\big)\tau:=-\sum_{i=0}^{n}\tau\omega_{i}\mathcal{A}^{i}\nabla p^{i},

where
\tau=\zeta_{i}-\zeta_{i-1}
and
\omega_{i}
is the corresponding quadrature weight.
In addition, we need to approximate the time derivative of the convolution

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{A}\left(t-\zeta\right)\nabla p(x,\zeta)d\zeta\right)\Big|_{t=t_{n}}=

\displaystyle\,\mathcal{A}(0)\nabla p(x,t_{n})

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\partial_{t}\mathcal{A}\left(t_{n}-\zeta\right)\nabla p(x,\zeta)d\zeta,

which, by use of the backward difference approximation and the trapezoid rule, can be approximated using

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\,\mathcal{A}(0)\nabla p(x,t_{n})+\sum_{i=0}^{n}\omega_{i}\left[\mathcal{A}(t_{n}-\zeta_{i})-\mathcal{A}(t_{n}-\tau-\zeta_{i})\right]\nabla p(x,\zeta_{i})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\,\mathcal{A}^{0}\nabla p^{n}+\sum_{i=0}^{n}\omega_{i}\Delta\mathcal{A}^{i}\nabla p^{i},

where
\Delta\mathcal{A}^{i}=\mathcal{A}^{i}-\mathcal{A}^{i-1}
.

3.2
Discretization in Space and Time

Let
\mathcal{T}_{h}
be a regular decomposition of the domain
\Omega
, with
h
denoting the mesh diameter. For the spatial discretization, we use the Taylor-Hood elements for the displacement and pressure

\displaystyle V_{h}=

\displaystyle\left\{\bm{v}_{h}\in\left[H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\right]^{d}\,\big|\,\forall\,K\in\mathcal{T}_{h},\,\bm{v}_{h}|_{K}\in[\mathbb{P}_{2}]^{d}\right\},

\displaystyle Q_{h}=

\displaystyle\left\{q_{h}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\,\big|\,\forall\,K\in\mathcal{T}_{h},\,q_{h}|_{K}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}\right\},

where
\mathbb{P}_{1}
and
\mathbb{P}_{2}
denote the spaces of linear and quadratic polynomials, respectively. We consider a uniform partition of the time interval
[0,T]
, i.e.
0<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{N}=T
with constant time step size
\tau=t_{n}-t_{n-1}
. The temporal derivatives in (
1
) are approximated using finite differences,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}p(t_{n})\approx\frac{p^{n}-p^{n-1}}{\tau},\quad\partial_{t}^{2}\bm{u}(t_{n})\approx\frac{\bm{u}^{n}-2\bm{u}^{n-1}+\bm{u}^{n-2}}{\tau^{2}}.

We assume that the initial data is known and that the first time step has already been determined, then we can approximate the history-dependent dynamic Biot equations by the implicit Euler scheme: For
n\geq 2
given
\bm{u}^{n-1}_{h},\bm{u}^{n-2}_{h}
and
p^{n-1}_{h}
find
(\bm{u}_{h}^{n},p_{h}^{n})\in V_{h}\times Q_{h}
which for all
(\bm{v}_{h},q_{h})\in V_{h}\times Q_{h}
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M}\left\langle p^{n}_{h}-p^{n-1}_{h},q_{h}\right\rangle+\left\langle\alpha\nabla\cdot\left(\bm{u}^{n}_{h}-\bm{u}^{n-1}_{h}\right),q_{h}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\tau^{2}\left\langle\frac{1}{\rho_{f}}\sum_{i=0}^{n}\omega_{i}\mathcal{A}^{i}\nabla p^{i}_{h},\nabla q_{h}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\tau\left\langle f_{2},q_{h}\right\rangle,

(2a)

\displaystyle\rho\left\langle\bm{u}^{n}_{h}-2\bm{u}^{n-1}_{h}+\bm{u}^{n-2}_{h},\bm{v}_{h}\right\rangle+\tau^{2}\lambda\left\langle\nabla\cdot\bm{u}_{h}^{n},\nabla\cdot\bm{v}_{h}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+2\tau^{2}\mu\left\langle\varepsilon(\bm{u}_{h}^{n}),\varepsilon(\bm{v}_{h})\right\rangle-\tau^{2}\alpha\left\langle p_{h}^{n},\nabla\cdot\bm{v}_{h}\right\rangle

\displaystyle-\tau^{2}\left\langle\mathcal{A}^{0}\nabla p^{n}_{h},\bm{v}_{h}\right\rangle-\tau^{2}\left\langle\sum_{i=0}^{n}\omega_{i}\Delta\mathcal{A}^{i}\nabla p^{i}_{h},\bm{v}_{h}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\tau^{2}\left\langle\bm{f}_{1},\bm{v}_{h}\right\rangle.

(2b)

3.3
Fixed-Stress Splitting Scheme

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
denote the iteration index and note that in the history terms of (
2
), we sum over
p^{i}_{h}
including
p^{n}_{h}
which we take to be the
k^{\rm th}
iterate
p^{n,k}_{h}
. Now based on the fixed-stress scheme for the quasi-static Biot
KimJihoon2011SAaE
, we can write a splitting scheme for the model (
1
). At any
k\geq 1
and for a free-to-be-chosen stabilization parameter
L>0
, (we fix the value later, see remark
1
) the scheme reads:

Given
\bm{u}^{n,k-1}_{h}\in V_{h}
find
p^{n,k}_{h}\in Q_{h}
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M}\left\langle p^{n,k}_{h}-p^{n-1}_{h},q_{h}\right\rangle+L\left\langle p^{n,k}_{h}-p^{n,k-1}_{h},q_{h}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\left\langle\alpha\nabla\cdot\left(\bm{u}^{n,k-1}_{h}-\bm{u}^{n-1}_{h}\right),q_{h}\right\rangle+\tau^{2}\left\langle\frac{1}{\rho_{f}}\sum_{i=0}^{n}\omega_{i}\mathcal{A}^{i}\nabla p^{i}_{h},\nabla q_{h}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\tau\left\langle f_{2},q_{h}\right\rangle.

(3a)

The second step is solving the mechanical part: Given
p^{n,k}_{h}\in Q_{h}
find
\bm{u}^{n,k}_{h}\in Q_{h}
such that

\displaystyle\rho\left\langle\bm{u}^{n,k}_{h}-2\bm{u}^{n-1}_{h}+\bm{u}^{n-2}_{h},\bm{v}_{h}\right\rangle+\tau^{2}\lambda\left\langle\nabla\cdot\bm{u}_{h}^{n,k},\nabla\cdot\bm{v}_{h}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+2\tau^{2}\mu\left\langle\varepsilon(\bm{u}_{h}^{n,k}),\varepsilon(\bm{v}_{h})\right\rangle-\tau^{2}\alpha\left\langle p_{h}^{n,k},\nabla\cdot\bm{v}_{h}\right\rangle

\displaystyle-\tau^{2}\left\langle\mathcal{A}^{0}\nabla p^{n,k}_{h},\bm{v}_{h}\right\rangle-\tau^{2}\left\langle\sum_{i=0}^{n}\omega_{i}\Delta\mathcal{A}^{i}\nabla p^{i}_{h},\bm{v}_{h}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\tau^{2}\left\langle\bm{f}_{1},\bm{v}_{h}\right\rangle.

(3b)

Remark 1

The convergence of the scheme depends on the choice of the parameter
L
. A rigorous analysis was performed for the case of the linear quasi-static Biot in e.g.
MikelicAndro2013Coic
;
both2017etal
or for nonlinear quasi-static Biot in e.g.
Borregales2018
;
Kraus2023
. Finding an optimal
L
, in the sense of minimizing the number of iterations, is not an easy task
StorvikErlend2019Otoo
, in practice
L=\frac{\alpha}{K_{dr}}
works well, where
K_{dr}=\frac{2\mu}{d}+\lambda
.

4
Numerical results

In this section, we will perform two numerical experiments to study the convergence of the proposed splitting scheme. As a stopping criterion, we have used a similar relative
L_{2}
norm as in
StorvikErlend2019Otoo
, i.e.
\|p^{n,i}_{h}-p^{n,i-1}_{h}\|\leq\varepsilon_{r}\|p^{n,i}_{h}\|
, with
\varepsilon_{r}=10^{-9}
.

The stabilization parameter
L
we have used here corresponds to the one typically used in the fixed-stress scheme for the quasi-static Biot model, i.e.
L_{C}=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{K_{dr}}
, cf., e.g.,
KimJihoon2011SAaE
. The linear systems are solved using a direct solver. All numerical experiments were implemented using the open-source software DUNE
DunePaper
. In both examples we solve the Biot problem (
1
) in the unit-square
\Omega=(0,1)^{2}
and with the paremeters
k_{0}=\alpha=M=\rho=\rho_{f}=\eta=1
, and
\mu=\lambda=10
.

5
Conclusions

We considered a fully dynamic Biot model for flow and deformation in porous media. The model includes additional memory effects, containing a history-dependent permeability. We propose a fixed-stress type splitting scheme for efficiently solving this model. We performed two numerical experiments to study the convergence of the scheme. It is not clear under which situations the memory effects become important. This will be explored under more realistic parameter choices in further work.

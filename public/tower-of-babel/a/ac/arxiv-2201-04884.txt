Abstract
Given two graphs G G and H H , the Ramsey number R ⁡ ( G , H ) R(G,H) is the minimum integer N N such that any coloring of the edges of K N K_{N} in red or blue yields a red G G or a blue H H . Let v ⁡ ( G ) v(G) be the number of vertices of G G and χ ⁡ ( G ) \chi(G) be the chromatic number of G G . Let s ⁡ ( G ) s(G) denote the chromatic surplus of G G , the cardinality of a minimum color class taken over all proper colorings of G G with χ ⁡ ( G ) \chi(G) colors. Burr [ 3 ] showed that for a connected graph G G and a graph H H with v ⁡ ( G ) ≥ s ⁡ ( H ) v(G)\geq s(H) , R ⁡ ( G , H ) ≥ ( v ⁡ ( G ) − 1 ) ​ ( χ ⁡ ( H ) − 1 ) + s ⁡ ( H ) R(G,H)\geq(v(G)-1)(\chi(H)-1)+s(H) . A connected graph G G is called H H -good if R ⁡ ( G , H ) = ( v ⁡ ( G ) − 1 ) ​ ( χ ⁡ ( H ) − 1 ) + s ⁡ ( H ) R(G,H)=(v(G)-1)(\chi(H)-1)+s(H) . Chvátal [ 5 ] showed that any tree is K m K_{m} -good for m ≥ 2 m\geq 2 , where K m K_{m} denotes a complete graph with m m vertices. By applying this result, Stahl [ 13 ] determined the Ramsey number of a forest versus K m K_{m} . Concerning whether a tree is H H -good for H H being disjoint union of complete graphs, Chvátal and Harary [ 6 ] showed that any tree is 2 ​ K 2 2K_{2} -good, where t ​ H tH denotes the union of t t disjoint copies of graph H H . Sudarsana, Adiwijaya and Musdalifah [ 15 ] proved that the n n -vertex path P n P_{n} is 2 ​ K m 2K_{m} -good for n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 and m ≥ 2 m\geq 2 , and conjectured that any tree T n T_{n} with n n vertices is 2 ​ K m 2K_{m} -good. Recently, Pokrovskiy and Sudakov [ 11 ] proved that P n P_{n} is H H -good for a graph with n ≥ 4 ​ v ​ ( H ) n\geq 4v(H) . Balla, Pokrovskiy and Sudakov [ 2 ] showed that for all Δ \Delta and k k , there exists a constant C Δ , k C_{\Delta,k} such that for any tree T T with maximum degree at most Δ \Delta and any H H with χ ⁡ ( H ) = k \chi(H)=k satisfying v ⁡ ( T ) ≥ C Δ , k ​ v ​ ( H ) ​ log 4 ⁡ v ⁡ ( H ) v(T)\geq C_{\Delta,k}v(H)\log^{4}v(H) , T T is H H -good. In this paper, we explore the Ramsey number of forest versus disjoint union of complete graphs. We first confirm the conjecture by Sudarsana, Adiwijaya and Musdalifah [ 15 ] that any tree is 2 ​ K m 2K_{m} -good for n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 and m ≥ 2 m\geq 2 . A key proposition in our proof is that a tree with n n vertices can be obtained from any tree with n n vertices by performing a series of “Stretching” and “Expanding” operations. On this foundation, we show that these two operations preserve the “ 2 ​ K m 2K_{m} -goodness” property, and confirm that any tree is 2 ​ K m 2K_{m} -good. Our result implies the exact value of R ⁡ ( F , K m ∪ K l ) R(F,K_{m}\cup K_{l}) , where F F is a forest and m , l ≥ 2 m,l\geq 2 . It’s interesting to explore what kind of graph F F can satisfy that F F is H H -good and G G -good implies that F F is H ∪ G H\cup G -good when the number of vertices in F F is sufficiently large.

Theorem 4.1

(Gould-Jacobson

It is natural to study whether

It’s interesting to explore what kind of graph

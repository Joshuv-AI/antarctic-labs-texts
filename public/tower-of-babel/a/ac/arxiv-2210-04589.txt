Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce the cohomology theory of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Leibniz triple systems. We use the cohomological approach to study linear and formal deformations of relative Rota-Baxter operators. In particular, formal deformations and extendibility of order n n deformations of a relative Rota-Baxter operators are also characterized in terms of the cohomology theory. We also consider the relationship between cohomology of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Leibniz algebras and associated Leibniz triple systems.

Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some basic definitions of Leibniz triple systems.

Definition 2.1
.

[
9
]

A Leibniz triple system is a vector space
\mathfrak{L}
endowed with a trilinear operation
\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}:\mathfrak{L}\times\mathfrak{L}\times\mathfrak{L}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{L}
satisfying

(2.1)

\displaystyle\{a,b,\{c,d,e\}\}=\{\{a,b,c\},d,e\}-\{\{a,b,d\},c,e\}-\{\{a,b,e\},c,d\}+\{\{a,b,e\},d,c\},

(2.2)

\displaystyle\{a,\{b,c,d\},e\}=\{\{a,b,c\},d,e\}-\{\{a,c,b\},d,e\}-\{\{a,d,b\},c,e\}+\{\{a,d,c\},b,e\},

for all
a,b,c,d,e\in\mathfrak{L}
.

One can obtain a Leibniz triple system from a Lie triple system with the same ternary product. A Leibniz algebra
L
with product
[\cdot,\cdot]
becomes a Leibniz triple system when
\{x,y,z\}:=[[x,y],z],
for all
x,y,z\in L.
More examples see
[
9
]
. Denote by End
(\mathfrak{L})
the set consisting of all linear maps on a Leibniz triple system
(\mathfrak{L},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
.

A morphism
f:(\mathfrak{L},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})\rightarrow(\mathfrak{L}^{\prime},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}^{\prime})
of Leibniz triple systems is a linear map satisfying

\displaystyle f(\{x,y,z\})=\{f(x),f(y),f(z)\}^{\prime},~~\forall~x,y,z\in\mathfrak{L}.

If
f
is a bijective morphism, then we call it an isomorphism.

3.
Relative Rota-Baxter operators on Leibniz triple systems

In this section, we give the definition of Rota-Baxter operators and relative Rota-Baxter operators on Leibniz triple systems. Moreover, we give some characterisation of relative Rota-Baxter operators in terms of Nijenhuis operators and graphs.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(\mathfrak{L},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
be a Leibniz triple system. A linear map
R:\mathfrak{L}\rightarrow\mathfrak{L}
is called a Rota-Baxter operator if it satisfies

\displaystyle\{R(x),R(y),R(z)\}=R\Big(\{R(x),R(y),z\}+\{R(x),y,R(z)\}+\{x,R(y),R(z)\}\Big),

for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{L}
.

Example 3.2
.

Consider a
2
-dimensional Leibniz triple system
(\mathfrak{L},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
given with respect to a basis
\{x,y\}
by

\{x,y,y\}=\{y,y,y\}=x.

Then

R=\left(\begin{matrix}a_{11}&a_{12}\\
a_{21}&a_{22}\end{matrix}\right)
is a Rota-Baxter operator on
(\mathfrak{L},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
if and only if

\{Ra,Rb,Rc\}=R\Big(\{Ra,Rb,c\}+\{Ra,b,Rc\}+\{a,Rb,Rc\}\Big),

for all
a,b,c\in\mathfrak{L}
. We have

\displaystyle\{Rx,Ry,Ry\}=

\displaystyle\{a_{11}x+a_{21}y,a_{12}x+a_{22}y,a_{12}x+a_{22}y\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a_{11}a_{22}^{2}+a_{21}a_{22}^{2})x,

and

\displaystyle R\Big(\{Rx,Ry,y\}+\{Rx,y,Ry\}+\{x,Ry,Ry\}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R\Big(\{a_{11}x+a_{21}y,a_{12}x+a_{22}y,y\}+\{a_{11}x+a_{21}y,y,a_{12}x+a_{22}y\}+\{x,a_{12}x+a_{22}y,a_{12}x+a_{22}y\}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R\Big(a_{11}a_{22}x+a_{21}a_{22}x+a_{11}a_{22}x+a_{21}a_{22}x+a_{22}^{2}x\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2a_{11}a_{22}+2a_{21}a_{22}+a_{22}^{2})(a_{11}x+a_{21}y).

Thus, we have
(2a_{11}a_{22}+2a_{21}a_{22}+a_{22}^{2})a_{11}=a_{11}a_{22}^{2}+a_{21}a_{22}^{2}
and
a_{21}(2a_{11}a_{22}+2a_{21}a_{22}+a_{22}^{2})=0
.

Similarly, we also have

\{Ry,Ry,Ry\}=R\Big(\{Ry,Ry,y\}+\{Ry,y,Ry\}+\{y,Ry,Ry\}\Big),
i.e.,

a_{11}(2a_{12}a_{22}+3a_{22}^{2})=a_{12}a_{22}^{2}+a_{22}^{3}
and
a_{21}(2a_{12}a_{22}+3a_{22}^{2})=0
.

Hence, we have
R=\left(\begin{matrix}a_{11}&a_{12}\\
0&0\end{matrix}\right)
or

R=\left(\begin{matrix}0&a_{12}\\
0&-a_{12}\end{matrix}\right)
.

The definition of relative Rota-Baxter operators (also called
\mathcal{O}
-operators or Kupershmidt operators) is a generalization of Rota-Baxter operators in any representation.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
V
be an
\mathfrak{L}
-module and
(r,m,l)
a representation of the Leibniz triple system
(\mathfrak{L},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
on

A linear operator

is called a relative Rota-Baxter operators on
(\mathfrak{L},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
with respect to

if

(3.1)

\displaystyle\{Tu,Tv,Tw\}=T\Big(l(Tu,Tv)w+m(Tu,Tw)v+r(Tv,Tw)u\Big),

for any
u,v,w\in V.

Remark 3.4
.

If
V=\mathfrak{L}
, then
T
is a Rota-Baxter operator on
(\mathfrak{L},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
with respect to the adjoint representation.

We will show that a relative Rota-Baxter operator can be lifted up to a Rota-Baxter operator, in the following proposition.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
(r,m,l)
be a representation of
(\mathfrak{L},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
on
V
and
T:V\rightarrow\mathfrak{L}
a linear map. Define
\widehat{T}\in{\rm End}(\mathfrak{L}\oplus V)
by
\widehat{T}(x+u)=Tu
. Then
T
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator if and only if
\widehat{T}
is a Rota-Baxter operator on
\mathfrak{L}\oplus V
.

Proof.

For any
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{L}
and
u,v,w\in V
, we have

\displaystyle\{\widehat{T}(x+u),\widehat{T}(y+v),\widehat{T}(z+w)\}_{\mathfrak{L}\oplus V}-\widehat{T}\Big(\{\widehat{T}(x+u),\widehat{T}(y+v),z+w\}_{\mathfrak{L}\oplus V}

\displaystyle+\{\widehat{T}(x+u),y+v,\widehat{T}(z+w)\}_{\mathfrak{L}\oplus V}+\{x+u,\widehat{T}(y+v),\widehat{T}(z+w)\}_{\mathfrak{L}\oplus V}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{Tu,Tv,Tw\}_{\mathfrak{L}\oplus V}-\widehat{T}\Big(\{Tu,Tv,z+w\}_{\mathfrak{L}\oplus V}+\{Tu,y+v,Tw\}_{\mathfrak{L}\oplus V}+\{x+u,Tv,Tw\}_{\mathfrak{L}\oplus V}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{Tu,Tv,Tw\}-T\big(l(Tu,Tv)w+m(Tu,Tw)v+r(Tv,Tw)u\big).

Then
\widehat{T}
is a Rota-Baxter operator on
\mathfrak{L}\oplus V
if and only if
T
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator on
\mathfrak{L}
.
∎

Now, we are going to characterize relative Rota-Baxter operators in terms of graphs.

Proposition 3.6
.

A linear map
T:V\rightarrow\mathfrak{L}
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator if and only if the graph

\displaystyle Gr(T)=\{Tu+u~|~u\in V\}

is a subalgebra of the semi-direct product
\mathfrak{L}\oplus V
.

Proof.

For all
u,v,w\in\mathfrak{L}
, we have

\displaystyle\{Tu+u,Tv+v,Tw+w\}_{\mathfrak{L}\oplus V}=\{Tu,Tv,Tw\}+l(Tu,Tv)w+m(Tu,Tw)v+r(Tv,Tw)u,

which implies that
Gr(T)
is a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{L}\oplus V
, if and only if
T
satisfies

\displaystyle\{Tu,Tv,Tw\}=T\Big(l(Tu,Tv)w+m(Tu,Tw)v+r(Tv,Tw)u\Big),

hence,
T
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator.
∎

\displaystyle\{Nx,Ny,Nz\}=

\displaystyle N\{Nx,Ny,z\}+N\{x,Ny,Nz\}+N\{Nx,y,Nz\}-N^{2}\{Nx,y,z\}

\displaystyle-N^{2}\{x,Ny,z\}-N^{2}\{x,y,Nz\}+N^{3}\{x,y,z\},

for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{L}.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
(\mathfrak{L},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
be a Leibniz triple system and
(r,m,l)
a representation of
\mathfrak{L}

Proof.

For all

,

\displaystyle\{\widetilde{T}(x+u),\widetilde{T}(y+v),\widetilde{T}(z+w)\}_{\mathfrak{L}\oplus V}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{x,y,z\}+\{Tu,y,z\}+\{x,Tv,z\}+\{x,y,Tw\}+\{x,Tv,Tw\}+\{Tu,y,Tw\}

\displaystyle+\{Tu,Tv,z\}+\{Tu,Tv,Tw\}.

On the other hand, since
\widetilde{T}^{2}=\widetilde{T}
, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}\Big(\{\widetilde{T}(x+u),\widetilde{T}(y+v),z+w\}_{\mathfrak{L}\oplus V}+\{\widetilde{T}(x+u),y+v,\widetilde{T}(z+w)\}_{\mathfrak{L}\oplus V}

\displaystyle+\{x+u,\widetilde{T}(y+v),\widetilde{T}(z+w)\}_{\mathfrak{L}\oplus V}\Big)-\widetilde{T}^{2}\Big(\{\widetilde{T}(x+u),y+v,z+w\}_{\mathfrak{L}\oplus V}

\displaystyle+\{x+u,y+v,\widetilde{T}(z+w)\}_{\mathfrak{L}\oplus V}+\{x+u,\widetilde{T}(y+v),z+w\}_{\mathfrak{L}\oplus V}\Big)

\displaystyle+\widetilde{T}^{3}\Big(\{x+u,y+v,z+w\}_{\mathfrak{L}\oplus V}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{x,y,z\}+\{Tu,y,z\}+\{x,Tv,z\}+\{x,y,Tw\}+\{x,Tv,Tw\}+\{Tu,y,Tw\}

\displaystyle+\{Tu,Tv,z\}+T\Big(l(Tu,Tv)w+m(Tu,Tw)v+r(Tv,Tw)u\Big),

which implies that

5.
Deformations of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Leibniz triple systems

In this section, we will deal with the linear deformations of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Leibniz triple systems and we show that the infinitesimals of two equivalent linear deformations of a relative Rota-Baxter operators on a Leibniz triple system are in the same cohomology classes of the first cohomology group.

Definition 5.1
.

For any
\mathbb{F}

If in addition,

(5.3)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{s=0}^{+\infty}\sum\limits_{i+j+k=s}l(x_{i},y_{j})v_{k}t^{s},

(5.4)

\displaystyle m(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{+\infty}x_{i}t^{i},\sum\limits_{j=0}^{+\infty}y_{j}t^{j})(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{+\infty}v_{k}t^{k})=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{s=0}^{+\infty}\sum\limits_{i+j+k=s}m(x_{i},y_{j})v_{k}t^{s},

\displaystyle r(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{+\infty}x_{i}t^{i},\sum\limits_{j=0}^{+\infty}y_{j}t^{j})(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{+\infty}v_{k}t^{k})=

for all

and

Let

Extend it to be a

Definition 5.5
.

If

Based on the relationship between relative Rota-Baxter operators and Leibniz triple systems, we have

.

for all

u,v,w\in V.

Proposition 5.7
.

Let

For

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle+T\Big(l(\mathfrak{T}_{1}u,Tv)w+m(Tu,\mathfrak{T}_{1}w)v+r(\mathfrak{T}_{1}v,Tw)u\Big)

Definition 5.8
.

Let
T
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Leibniz triple system

Definition 5.9
.

Two formal deformations
T_{t}^{\prime}=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{+\infty}\mathfrak{T}_{i}^{\prime}t^{i}
and

(\mathfrak{L},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})

x,y\in\mathfrak{L},

\phi_{i}\in{\rm End}(\mathfrak{L})
and
\psi_{i}\in{\rm End}(V),

such that for

\displaystyle\phi_{t}=~

\displaystyle{\rm id}_{\mathfrak{L}}+t(R_{(x,y)}-R_{(y,x)})+\sum\limits_{i=2}^{+\infty}\phi_{i}t^{i},

\displaystyle\psi_{t}=~

\displaystyle{\rm id}_{V}+t(r(x,y)-r(y,x))+\sum\limits_{i=2}^{+\infty}\psi_{i}t^{i},

the following conditions hold:

\displaystyle{\rm(1)}

\displaystyle~\{\phi_{t}(x),\phi_{t}(y),\phi_{t}(z)\}=\phi_{t}\{x,y,z\},{\rm~for~all}~x,y,z\in\mathfrak{L};

\displaystyle{\rm(2)}

\displaystyle~\psi_{t}l(x,y)u=l(\phi_{t}(x),\phi_{t}(y))\psi_{t}(u),

\displaystyle~\psi_{t}m(x,y)u=m(\phi_{t}(x),\phi_{t}(y))\psi_{t}(u),

\displaystyle~\psi_{t}r(x,y)u=r(\phi_{t}(x),\phi_{t}(y))\psi_{t}(u),{\rm~for~all}~x,y\in\mathfrak{L},~u\in V;

\displaystyle{\rm(3)}

\displaystyle~T_{t}\circ\psi_{t}=\phi_{t}\circ T_{t}^{\prime}~{\rm as~}\mathbb{F}[[t]]{\rm-module~maps}.

Theorem 5.10
.

If two formal deformations of a relative Rota-Baxter operator
T
on a Leibniz triple system
(\mathfrak{L},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
with respect to a representation
(r,m,l)
are equivalent, then their infinitesimals are in the same cohomology class.

Proof.

Let
(\phi_{t},\psi_{t})
be two maps defined by Definition
5.9
, which gives an equivalence between two deformations

and
T_{t}=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{+\infty}\mathfrak{T}_{i}t^{i}
of a relative Rota-Baxter operator
T.
By
\phi_{t}\circ T^{\prime}_{t}=T_{t}\circ\psi_{t},
we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}^{\prime}_{1}v-\mathfrak{T}_{1}v=T(r(a,b)-r(b,a))v-(R_{(a,b)}-R_{(b,a)})Tv=\delta(a,b)v,~\forall v\in V,

which implies that
\mathfrak{T}^{\prime}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{T}_{1}
are in the same cohomology class.
∎

5.2.
Order

We introduce a cohomology class associated to any order

{\rm Ob}_{T}=-\partial^{1}\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}.
Thus we call this cohomology class the obstruction of an order
n
deformation being extendable.

Definition 5.11
.

Let
T
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Leibniz triple system
(\mathfrak{L},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
with respect to a representation
(r,m,l).
If
T_{t}=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}\mathfrak{T}_{i}t^{i}
with
\mathfrak{T}_{0}=T,

\mathfrak{T}_{i}\in{\rm Hom}_{\mathbb{F}}(V,\mathfrak{L})
,
i=2,3,...,n,
defines on
\mathbb{F}[[t]]/(t^{n+1})
-module map from

to the Leibniz triple system
\mathfrak{L}[[t]]/(t^{n+1}),
for all

satisfying

\displaystyle\{T_{t}(u),T_{t}(v),T_{t}(w)\}=T_{t}\Big(l(T_{t}(u),T_{t}(v))w+m(T_{t}(u),T_{t}(w))v+r(T_{t}(v),T_{t}(w))u\Big),

we say that
T_{t}
is an order
n
deformation of the relative Rota-Baxter operator

Remark 5.12
.

Obviously, the left hand side of Eq. (
5.8
) holds in the Leibniz triple system
\mathfrak{L}[[t]]/(t^{n+1})
and the right hand side makes sense since
T_{t}
is an
\mathbb{F}[[t]]/(t^{n+1})
-module map.

Definition 5.13
.

Let
T_{t}=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}\mathfrak{T}_{i}t^{i}
be an order
n
deformation of a relative Rota-Baxter operator

on a Leibniz triple system
(\mathfrak{L},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})

(r,m,l).
If there exists a
1
-cochain
\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}\in{\rm Hom}_{\mathbb{F}}(V,\mathfrak{L})
such that

is an order

deformation of the relative Rota-Baxter operator

then we say that
T_{t}
is extendable.

Theorem 5.14
.

Let
T:V\rightarrow\mathfrak{L}
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Leibniz triple system
(\mathfrak{L},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
with respect to a representation
(r,m,l)
,
and
T_{t}=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}\mathfrak{T}_{i}t^{i}
be an order
n
deformation of
T
. Then
T_{t}
is extendable if and only if the cohomology class
[{\rm Ob}_{T}]\in H^{3}_{T}(V,\mathfrak{L})
is trivial, where
{\rm Ob}_{T}\in C^{3}_{T}(V,\mathfrak{L})
is defined by

\displaystyle{\rm Ob}_{T}(u,v,w)=\sum\limits_{i+j+k=n+1\atop 0\leq i,j,k\leq n}\Bigg(\{\mathfrak{T}_{i}u,\mathfrak{T}_{j}v,\mathfrak{T}_{k}w\}-\mathfrak{T}_{i}\Big(l(\mathfrak{T}_{j}u,\mathfrak{T}_{k}v)w+m(\mathfrak{T}_{k}u,\mathfrak{T}_{j}w)v+r(\mathfrak{T}_{j}v,\mathfrak{T}_{k}w)u\Big)\Bigg),

for all
u,v,w\in V
.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{T_{t}}=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n+1}\mathfrak{T}_{i}t^{i}
be the extension of
T_{t}
, then for all
u,v,w\in V
,

(5.9)

\displaystyle\{\widetilde{T_{t}}(u),\widetilde{T_{t}}(v),\widetilde{T_{t}}(w)\}=\widetilde{T_{t}}\Big(l(\widetilde{T_{t}}(u),\widetilde{T_{t}}(v))w+m(\widetilde{T_{t}}(u),\widetilde{T_{t}}(w))v+r(\widetilde{T_{t}}(v),\widetilde{T_{t}}(w))u\Big),

Expanding the Eq. (
5.9
) and comparing the coefficients of

yields that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i+j+k=n+1\atop i,j,k\geq 0}\Bigg(\{\mathfrak{T}_{i}u,\mathfrak{T}_{j}v,\mathfrak{T}_{k}w\}-\mathfrak{T}_{i}\Big(l(\mathfrak{T}_{j}u,\mathfrak{T}_{k}v)w+m(\mathfrak{T}_{k}u,\mathfrak{T}_{j}w)v+r(\mathfrak{T}_{j}v,\mathfrak{T}_{k}w)u\Big)\Bigg)=0,

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i+j+k=n+1\atop i,j,k\geq 1}\Bigg(\{\mathfrak{T}_{i}u,\mathfrak{T}_{j}v,\mathfrak{T}_{k}w\}-\mathfrak{T}_{i}\Big(l(\mathfrak{T}_{j}u,\mathfrak{T}_{k}v)w+m(\mathfrak{T}_{k}u,\mathfrak{T}_{j}w)v+r(\mathfrak{T}_{j}v,\mathfrak{T}_{k}w)u\Big)\Bigg)

\displaystyle+\{\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}u,Tv,Tw\}+\{Tu,\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}v,Tw\}+\{Tu,Tv,\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}w\}

\displaystyle-\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}\Big(l(Tu,Tv)w+m(Tu,Tw)v+r(Tv,Tw)u\Big)

\displaystyle-T\Big(l(\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}u,Tv)w+m(\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}u,Tw)v+r(\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}v,Tw)u\Big)

\displaystyle-T\Big(l(Tu,\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}v)w+m(Tu,\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}w)v+r(Tv,\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}w)u\Big)=0,

which is also equivalent to

(5.10)

\displaystyle{\rm Ob}_{T}+\partial^{1}(\mathfrak{T}_{n+1})=0.

From Eq. (
5.10
), we get

. Thus, the cohomology class
[{\rm Ob}_{T}]
is trivial.

Conversely, suppose that the cohomology class
[{\rm Ob}_{T}]
is trivial, then there exists
\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}\in C^{1}_{T}(V,\mathfrak{L})
such that
{\rm Ob}_{T}=-\partial^{1}(\mathfrak{T}_{n+1})
. Set
\widetilde{T_{t}}=T_{t}+\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}t^{n+1}
. Then for all

,
\widetilde{T_{t}}
satisfies

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i+j+k=s}\Bigg(\{\mathfrak{T}_{i}u,\mathfrak{T}_{j}v,\mathfrak{T}_{k}w\}-\mathfrak{T}_{i}\Big(l(\mathfrak{T}_{j}u,\mathfrak{T}_{k}v)w+m(\mathfrak{T}_{k}u,\mathfrak{T}_{j}w)v+r(\mathfrak{T}_{j}v,\mathfrak{T}_{k}w)u\Big)\Bigg)=0,

which implies that

is an order

.
∎

6.
From cohomology groups of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Leibniz algebras to those on Leibniz triple systems

Motivated by the construction of Leibniz triple systems from Leibniz algebras. We give some connection between relative Rota-Baxter operators on Leibniz algebras and Leibniz triple systems.

Definition 6.1
.

[
19
]

A right Leibniz algebra is a vector space
L
together with a bilinear operation
[\cdot,\cdot]_{L}:L\otimes L\rightarrow L
such that

\displaystyle[x,[y,z]_{L}]_{L}=[[x,y]_{L},z]_{L}-[[x,z]_{L},y]_{L},~\forall~~x,y,z\in L.

In this paper, we only consider right Leibniz algebras.

Definition 6.2
.

[
19
]

A representation of a Leibniz algebra
(L,[\cdot,\cdot]_{L})
is a triple
(V;\rho^{l},\rho^{r})
, where
V
is a vector space and
\rho^{l},\rho^{r}:L\rightarrow gl(V)
are linear maps such that the following equalities hold for all
x,y\in L
,

\displaystyle\rho^{l}([x,y]_{L})=

\displaystyle[\rho^{r}(y),\rho^{l}(x)],

\displaystyle\rho^{l}([x,y]_{L})=

\displaystyle\rho^{l}(x)\rho^{l}(y)+\rho^{r}(y)\rho^{l}(x),

\displaystyle\rho^{r}([x,y]_{L})=

\displaystyle\rho^{r}(y)\rho^{r}(x)-\rho^{r}(x)\rho^{r}(y).

Let
(L,[\cdot,\cdot]_{L})
be a Leibniz algebra and
(V;\rho^{l},\rho^{r})
a representation. Define a bilinear bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{V}:(L\oplus V)\otimes(L\oplus V)\rightarrow L\oplus V
by

\displaystyle[x+u,y+v]_{V}=[x,y]_{L}+\rho^{l}(x)v+\rho^{r}(y)u,

for all
x,y\in L
and
u,v\in V
.

Recall the cohomology theory of Leibniz algebras, see
[
2
]
. For a Leibniz algebra
L
and a representation
(V;\rho^{l},\rho^{r})
, the cochains spaces are defined by

\displaystyle C^{0}_{L}(L,V)=V,~~~C^{n}_{L}(L,V)={\rm Hom}(L^{\otimes n},V),~~n>0.

Let
d^{n}:C^{n}_{L}(L,V)\rightarrow C^{n+1}_{L}(L,V)
be defined by

\displaystyle(d^{n}f)(x_{1},...x_{n+1})=

\displaystyle\rho^{l}(x_{1})f(x_{2},...x_{n+1})+\sum\limits_{i=2}^{n+1}(-1)^{i}\rho^{r}(x_{i})f(x_{1},...,\widehat{x_{i}},...x_{n+1})

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n+1}(-1)^{j+1}f(x_{1},...x_{i-1},[x_{i},x_{j}]_{L},x_{i+1},...,\widehat{x_{j}},...,x_{n+1}),

where
f\in C^{n}_{L}(L,V)
,
x_{i}\in L
and the sign
~\widehat{}~
indicates that the element below must be omitted.
The
n
-th cohomology group is defined by

\displaystyle H^{n}_{L}(L,V)=Z^{n}_{L}(L,V)/B^{n}_{L}(L,V),

where the elements
Z^{n}_{L}(L,V)=kerd^{n}
and
B^{n}_{L}(L,V)=Imd^{n-1}
are
n
-cocycles and
n
-coboundaries, respectively.

The elements
f\in Z^{1}_{L}(L,V)
and
g\in Z^{2}_{L}(L,V)
are defined as follows

\displaystyle\rho^{l}(x)f(y)+\rho^{r}(y)f(x)-f([x,y]_{L})=0,

and,

\displaystyle\rho^{l}(x)g(y,z)-\rho^{r}(z)g(x,y)+\rho^{r}(y)g(x,z)+g(x,[y,z]_{L})-g([x,y]_{L},z)+g([x,z]_{L},y)=0.

Definition 6.3
.

[
25
]

A linear map
T:V\rightarrow L
is called a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Leibniz algebra
(L,[\cdot,\cdot]_{L})
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho^{l},\rho^{r})
if
T
satisfies

\displaystyle[Tu,Tv]_{L}=T\big(\rho^{l}(Tu)v+\rho^{r}(Tv)u\big),

for all
u,v\in V
.

Now, we recall some results from
[
25
]
. Let
T:V\rightarrow L
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Leibniz algebra
(L,[\cdot,\cdot]_{L})
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho^{l},\rho^{r})
. There is a Leibniz algebra structure on
(V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{T})
, where the bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{T}:V\times V\rightarrow V
is given by

\displaystyle[u,v]_{T}=\rho^{l}(Tu)v+\rho^{r}(Tv)u,

for all
u,v\in V
. There is a representation
(L;\rho^{l}_{T},\rho^{r}_{T})
of
(V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{T})
, where
\rho^{l}_{T},\rho^{r}_{T}:V\rightarrow gl(L)
, is defined by

\displaystyle\rho^{l}_{T}(u)x=

\displaystyle[Tu,x]_{L}-T\rho^{r}(x)u,

\displaystyle\rho^{r}_{T}(u)x=

\displaystyle[x,Tu]_{L}-T\rho^{l}(x)u,

for all
u\in V
and
x\in L
.

Let
T
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Leibniz algebra
(L,[\cdot,\cdot]_{L})
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho^{l},\rho^{r})
. Consider the set of
n
-cochains
C^{n}_{T}(V,L)={\rm Hom}(\wedge^{n}V,L)
. Let
d_{T}^{n}:C^{n}_{T}(V,L)\rightarrow C^{n+1}_{T}(V,L)
be the corresponding operator of the Leibniz algebra
(V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{T})
with coefficients in the representation
(L;\rho^{l}_{T},\rho^{r}_{T})
, define for all
f\in C^{n}_{T}(V,L)
and
u_{1},...u_{n+1}\in V
by

\displaystyle(d_{T}^{n}f)(u_{1},...u_{n+1})=

\displaystyle\rho^{l}_{T}(u_{1})f(u_{2},...u_{n+1})+\sum\limits_{i=2}^{n+1}(-1)^{i}\rho^{r}_{T}(u_{i})f(u_{1},...,\widehat{u_{i}},...u_{n+1})

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n+1}(-1)^{j+1}f(u_{1},...u_{i-1},[u_{i},u_{j}]_{T},u_{i+1},...,\widehat{u_{j}},...,u_{n+1}).

Then the cochain complex
(C^{n}_{T}(V,L),d_{T})
is called a cochain complex of the relative Rota-Baxter operator
T
on a Leibniz algebra
(V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{T})
with coefficients in the representation
(L;\rho^{l}_{T},\rho^{r}_{T})
. We denote the corresponding
n
-th cohomology group by
H^{n}_{T}(V,L)
.

In the sequel, we study the relationship between Leibniz algebras and the corresponding Leibniz triple systems.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
(L,[\cdot,\cdot]_{L})
be a Leibniz algebra with a representation
(V;\rho^{l},\rho^{r})
. Define
R,M,L:\otimes^{2}L\rightarrow{\rm End}(V)
by

\displaystyle R(x,y)u=

\displaystyle\rho^{r}(y)\rho^{r}(x)(u),

\displaystyle M(x,y)u=

\displaystyle\rho^{r}(y)\rho^{l}(x)(u),

\displaystyle L(x,y)u=

\displaystyle\rho^{l}([x,y]_{L})(u),

then
(L,\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}=[[\cdot,\cdot]_{L},\cdot]_{L})
is a Leibniz triple system with a representation
(R,M,L)
.

Proof.

Let
(L,[\cdot,\cdot]_{L})
be a Leibniz algebra with a representation
(V;\rho^{l},\rho^{r})
. We know that there is a Leibniz triple system structure on
L
given by
\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}=[[\cdot,\cdot]_{L},\cdot]_{L}
. Now for all
x,y,z\in L
and
u,v,w\in V
, we have

\displaystyle\{x+u,y+v,z+w\}_{L\oplus V}=

\displaystyle[[x+u,y+v]_{L\oplus V},z+w]_{L\oplus V}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[[x,y]_{L}+\rho^{l}(x)v+\rho^{r}(y)u,z+w]_{L\oplus V}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[[x,y]_{L},z]_{L}+\rho^{l}([x,y]_{L})w+\rho^{r}(z)\big(\rho^{l}(x)v+\rho^{r}(y)u\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[[x,y]_{L},z]_{L}+L(x,y)w+M(x,z)v+R(y,z)u.

Then by Eq. (
2.3
),
(R,M,L)
is a representation of Leibniz triple system
(L,\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}=[[\cdot,\cdot]_{L},\cdot]_{L})
.
∎

Proposition 6.5
.

Let

be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Leibniz algebra
(L,[\cdot,\cdot]_{L})
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho^{l},\rho^{r})

(L,\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}=[[\cdot,\cdot]_{L},\cdot]_{L})

(R,M,L)

Proof.

For any

\displaystyle\{Tu,Tv,Tw\}=

\displaystyle[[Tu,Tv]_{L},Tw]_{L}=[T(\rho^{l}(Tu)v+\rho^{r}(Tv)u),Tw]_{L}

\displaystyle[T\rho^{l}(Tu)v,Tw]_{L}+[T\rho^{r}(Tv)u,Tw]_{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\Big(\rho^{l}\big(T\rho^{l}(Tu)v\big)w+\rho^{r}(Tw)\rho^{l}(Tu)v\Big)+T\Big(\rho^{l}\big(T\rho^{r}(Tv)u\big)w+\rho^{r}(Tw)\rho^{r}(Tv)u\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\Big(\rho^{l}([Tu,Tv]_{L})w+\rho^{r}(Tw)\rho^{l}(Tu)v+\rho^{r}(Tw)\rho^{r}(Tv)u\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\Big(L(Tu,Tv)w+M(Tu,Tw)v+R(Tv,Tw)u\Big).

This implies that

is a relative Rota-Baxter operator.
∎

Now there are two methods of constructing a Leibniz triple system structure on

from a Leibniz algebra
(L,[\cdot,\cdot]_{L})
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho^{l},\rho^{r})

(V,\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}_{T})
of the relative Rota-Baxter operator

(L,\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}=[[\cdot,\cdot]_{L},\cdot]_{L})
with respect to a representation
(R,M,L)

\displaystyle\{u,v,w\}_{T}=L(Tu,Tv)w+M(Tu,Tw)v+R(Tv,Tw)u.

On the other hand, we firstly induce a Leibniz algebra
(V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{T})
of

(L,[\cdot,\cdot]_{L})

(V;\rho^{l},\rho^{r})

(V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{T})

For convince, we consider the representation related to the relative Rota-Baxter operator

Proof.

Let
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in Z^{2}_{T}(V,L)
be two cocycles in the same cohomology class, that is

\displaystyle\phi_{2}-\phi_{1}=d(\alpha),~~\alpha\in C^{1}_{T}(V,L).

According to Lemma
6.9
, we have

\displaystyle\omega_{2}(u,v,w)-\omega_{1}(u,v,w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\phi_{2}-\phi_{1})([u,v]_{T},w)+\rho^{r}_{T}(w)(\phi_{2}-\phi_{1})(u,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d(\alpha)([u,v]_{T},w)+\rho^{r}_{T}(w)d(\alpha)(u,v)=\delta^{1}(\alpha)(u,v,w),

\alpha\in C^{1}_{T}(V,L)
, which means that
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
are in the same cohomology class.
∎

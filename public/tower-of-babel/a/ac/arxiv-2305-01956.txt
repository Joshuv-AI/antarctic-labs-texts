Lower bounds for the number of number fields with Galois group GL 2 ⁡ ( 𝔽 ℓ ) \operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
Anwesh Ray

Abstract
. Let ℓ ≥ 5 \ell\geq 5 be a prime number and 𝔽 ℓ \mathbb{F}_{\ell} denote the finite field with ℓ \ell elements. 1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a transitive subgroup of
S_{n}
. Given a number field
K
with
[K\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Q}]=n
, let
\tilde{K}
denote its Galois closure over
\mathbb{Q}
. We view
\operatorname{Gal}(\tilde{K}/\mathbb{Q})
as a transitive permutation subgroup of
S_{n}
, where the permutation action is defined by its action on the
n
embeddings of
K
into
\mathbb{C}
. For any real number
X>0
, let
N(G;X)
be the number of number fields
K
of degree
n
, with absolute discriminant at most
X
, such that
\operatorname{Gal}(\tilde{K}/\mathbb{Q})
is isomorphic to
G
as a permutation subgroup of
S_{n}
. In
[
Mal02
]
, Malle made a precise conjecture about the asymptotic growth of
N(G;X)
as a function of
X
. The conjecture posits that

N(G;X)\sim X^{\frac{1}{a(G)}}(\log X)^{b(G)-1},

where
a(G)
and
b(G)
are numbers which depend on the permutation representation of
G
. Malle’s conjecture has been proven for the abelian groups
[
Mäk85
]
,
S_{n}
for
n\leq 5

[
DH71
,
Bha05
,
BW08
,
Bha10
,
BF10
]
, the dihedral group
D_{4}

[
CDO02
]
, and various groups of the form
A\times S_{n}
, where
A
is a finite abelian group and
n\leq 5
, cf.
[
Wan21
]
. This list is not exhaustive. Motivated by such developments, various asymptotic lower bounds for the groups
S_{n}
and
A_{n}
have been proven, cf.
[
EV06
,
PTBW20
]
.

1.1.
Main result

A large class of finite groups of interest are the groups of the form
G(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, where
G
is an algebraic group over the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. These are groups that arise naturally in the study of Galois representations associated with abelian varieties, motives and automorphic forms. Let
\ell\geq 5
be an odd prime number and let
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
be the finite field with
\ell
elements. One of the simplest examples to consider are the groups
G=\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
. We view
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
as a subgroup of
S_{\#\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})}
, taken with respect to the regular representation, and prove asymptotic lower bounds for the number of Galois number fields
L/\mathbb{Q}
with Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})
isomorphic to
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
, and bounded absolute discriminant.

Given a number field
L
, set
\Delta_{L}
to denote the discriminant of
L/\mathbb{Q}
. Set
\mathcal{M}(\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell}))
to denote the set of all pairs
(L,\rho)
, where

•

L/\mathbb{Q}
is a Galois extension with Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})
isomorphic to
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
;

•

\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
is an isomorphism which arises from the
\ell
-torsion of an elliptic curve
E
.

We take
N_{\rho}
to denote the prime to
\ell
part of the Artin conductor of
\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
. This quantity is also known as the
optimal level
, with reference to Serre’s conjecture, cf.
[
RS99
, section 1.3]
. Note that if two isomorphisms
\rho,\rho^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
differ by an inner automorphism of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
, then,
N_{\rho}=N_{\rho^{\prime}}
. Taking
X\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, one is interested in obtaining asymptotic lower bounds for the cardinalities of the following sets

\begin{split}&\mathcal{M}(\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell});X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{(L,\rho)\in\mathcal{M}(\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell}))\mid N_{\rho}\leq X\},\\
&\mathcal{F}(\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell});X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{L\mid L/\mathbb{Q}\text{ is Galois with }\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})\simeq\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})\text{, and }|\Delta_{L}|\leq X\}.\end{split}

We note that
\#\mathcal{F}(\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell});X)
is simply
N(\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell});X)
with respect to the natural inclusion of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
into
S_{\#\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})}
. Given a set
S
, let
\#S\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\}
denote its cardinality. Given functions
f(X)
and
g(X)
taking on non-negative values, we set
f(X)\gg g(X)
(resp.
f(X)=O(g(X))
) to mean that there is a constant
C>0
, independent of
X
, such that
f(X)\geq Cg(X)
(resp.
f(X)\leq Cg(X)
). The main result is stated below.

Theorem A
.

Let
\ell\geq 5
be a prime number. Then,

\begin{split}&\#\mathcal{M}(\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell});X)\gg\frac{X^{\frac{1}{12}}}{\log X};\\
&N(\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell});X)=\#\mathcal{F}(\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell});X)\gg\frac{X^{\frac{\ell}{12(\ell-1)\#\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})}}}{\log X},\end{split}

where the implied constant depends only on
\ell
.

Lipnowski
[
Lip21
]
proposed a heuristic for counting
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
-extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
, that satisfy the same properties as the extensions that arise from modular Galois representations. In
loc. cit.
, a homomorphism
\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
is said to be
locally modular
, if it satisfies the same local conditions as that of a Galois representation associated with a Hecke eigencuspform of weight
2
. This conjecture is based on Bhargava’s local-global heuristics for counting
G
-number fields
[
Bha07
]
, and predicts that if
G(X)
is the number of locally modular Galois representations with squarefree conductor
N\in[X,2X]
, then,

G(X)\sim X\log X\prod_{v}\left(1-3v^{-2}+2v^{-3}\right),

where
v
runs over all prime numbers. The representations constructed in this article arise from elliptic curves, and thus are locally modular.

For any finite group
G
with
n
elements and inclusion
G\hookrightarrow S_{n}
with respect to the regular representation, the value of
a(G)
is
\left(\frac{p-1}{p}\right)\#G
, where
p
is the smallest prime divisor of
G
(cf.
[
Mal02
, p. 318]
). Therefore, the power of
X
in the predicted asymptotic for
N(\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell});X)
is
\frac{2}{\#\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})}
. In our main result for
N(\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell});X)
, the power of
X
obtained is thus
\frac{1}{24}\left(\frac{\ell}{\ell-1}\right)
times the conjectured value. In particular the conjecture predicts that
\frac{1}{a(\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell}))}\sim\frac{2}{\ell^{4}}
(as
\ell\rightarrow\infty
), and the power of
X
in our bound is
\sim\frac{1}{12\ell^{4}}
. Thus, the correct power of
\ell
has been achieved, and we are off by a factor of
24
.

1.2.
Method of proof

Let us briefly describe the methodology behind the proof of Theorem
A
. Given an elliptic curve
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
and a prime number
\ell
, one considers the associated Galois representation

\bar{\rho}_{E,\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})

on the
\ell
-torsion subgroup of
E(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})
. The
\ell
division field
L_{\ell}(E)
is the field extension of
\mathbb{Q}
, which is fixed by the kernel of
\bar{\rho}_{E,\ell}
. The number field
L_{\ell}(E)
is Galois over
\mathbb{Q}
, and the Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(L_{\ell}(E)/\mathbb{Q})
is isomorphic to
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
precisely when
\bar{\rho}_{E,\ell}
is surjective. We consider the family of all elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
, ordered according to
height
, and relate it to the family of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
-extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
, ordered by discriminant (or Artin conductor). It follows from a theorem of Duke
[
Duk97
]
that for almost all elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
, the residual representation
\bar{\rho}_{E,\ell}
is surjective. Given a Galois extension
L/\mathbb{Q}
with Galois group isomorphic to
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
, we obtain an asymptotic upper bound for the number of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
with bounded height, for which
L_{\ell}(E)=L
, cf. Proposition
3.3
. This involves an application of a result of the author and Weston
[
RW23
, Proposition 3.2]
. In fact, the argument can be traced back to a result of Duke
[
Duk97
, Theorem 2]
. Duke’s argument involves a variant of Gallagher’s large sieve. We then prove Theorem
A
via a counting argument.

1.3.
Outlook

Galois representations arise from motives and automorphic forms. The Galois representations that arise from elliptic curves are the most natural examples to consider. We expect that this methodology has the potential to lead to interesting generalizations.

1.4.
Related work

The author was informed upon the public release of an earlier version of this manuscript that Vittoria Cristante is investigating related questions in her thesis. Notably, she has concurrently obtained intriguing results connecting Malle’s conjecture to the analysis of Galois representations associated with elliptic curves.

2.
Elliptic curves and division fields

2.1.
Parametrizing
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
-extensions of
\mathbb{Q}

Let
\ell
be an odd prime number. In this section, we recall some well known results about the division field associated with the
\ell
-torsion of an elliptic curve
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
. Throughout,
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
will be a fixed algebraic closure of
\mathbb{Q}
, the absolute Galois group of
\mathbb{Q}
is denoted
\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
. The field with
\ell
elements is denoted by
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
. Given a finite group
G
and a homomorphism
\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow G
, the field
\mathbb{Q}(\rho)
denotes
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}^{\operatorname{ker}\rho}
, and is referred to as the field
cut out by
\rho
. We note that
\mathbb{Q}(\rho)/\mathbb{Q}
is a Galois extension, and that the Galois group
G_{\rho}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\rho)/\mathbb{Q})
injects into
G
via
\rho
, and we may identify
G_{\rho}
with the image of
\rho
. In particular, if
\rho
is surjective, then,
\mathbb{Q}(\rho)
is a Galois extension of
\mathbb{Q}
with Galois group
G_{\rho}
isomorphic to
G
. We shall refer to such an extension as a
G
-extension of
\mathbb{Q}
.

Let
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
be an elliptic curve and
\ell
be a prime number. Denote by
E[\ell]
the
\ell
-torsion subgroup of
E(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})
, and note that
E[\ell]
is a
2
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
-vector space. Choosing an
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
-basis for
E[\ell]
, we identify the automorphism group of
E[\ell]
with
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
. Set
\bar{\rho}_{E,\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
to be the Galois representation on
E[\ell]
and denote by
L_{\ell}(E)
the subfield of
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
fixed by the kernel of
\bar{\rho}_{E,\ell}
. In other words,
L_{\ell}(E)
is the field cut out by
\bar{\rho}_{E,\ell}
. Set
G_{\ell}(E)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Gal}(L_{\ell}(E)/\mathbb{Q})
; note that if
\bar{\rho}_{E,\ell}
is surjective, then,
G_{\ell}(E)
is isomorphic to
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
.

2.2.
Elliptic curves ordered by height

An elliptic curve
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
admits a unique Weierstrass equation
y^{2}=x^{3}+Ax+B
, where
(A,B)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
is such that
p^{4}\nmid A
and
p^{6}\nmid B
for all primes
p
(cf.
[
Duk97
]
). The Weierstrass equation
y^{2}=x^{3}+Ax+B
is minimal at every prime, and the naive height of
E
is defined as follows

H(E)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{max}\{|A|^{3},B^{2}\}.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be the set of isomorphism classes of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
and let
\mathcal{C}(X)
be the set of elliptic curves with
H(E)\leq X^{6}
. We identify
\mathcal{C}(X)
with the set of tuples
(A,B)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
such that
p^{4}\nmid A
and
p^{6}\nmid B
for all primes
p
, and
|A|\leq X^{2}
and
|B|\leq X^{3}
. It is easy to see that

(2.1)

\#\mathcal{C}(X)=\frac{4}{\zeta(10)}X^{5}+O(X^{3}),

cf.
[
Bru92
, Lemma 4.3]
. Let
\mathcal{S}
be a set of isomorphism classes of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
. For
X>0
, set
\mathcal{S}(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{C}(X)
, and let
\delta(\mathcal{S})
to denote the limit

\delta(\mathcal{S})=\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{\#\mathcal{S}(X)}{\#\mathcal{C}(X)}\right)=\frac{\zeta(10)}{4}\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{\#\mathcal{S}(X)}{X^{5}}\right),

provided it exists. Denote by
\Delta(E)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=-16(4A^{3}+27B^{2})
, and note that if
H(E)\leq X^{6}
, then,
|\Delta(E)|\leq 496X^{6}
. Let
N_{E}
denote the conductor of
E
. It follows from Ogg’s formula that
N_{E}
divides
\Delta_{E}
, cf.
[
Sil94
, p.389]
. The elliptic curve
E
is modular, and the associated modular form
f
has level
N_{E}
. Set
N_{E}^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=N_{\bar{\rho}_{E,\ell}}
; results of Carayol (cf.
[
RS99
]
) imply that
N_{E}^{\prime}
divides
N_{E}
, and hence,
N_{E}^{\prime}\leq 496X^{6}
.

Definition 2.1
.

We shall denote by
\mathcal{S}_{\ell}(X)
the subset of
\mathcal{C}(X)
for which
E

(1)

has semistable reduction at all primes
p
, except possibly at
p=2,3
,

(2)

and
\bar{\rho}_{E,\ell}
is surjective.

For
E\in\mathcal{S}_{\ell}(X)
, let
\gamma_{\ell}(E)
be the pair
(L_{\ell}(E),\bar{\rho}_{E,\ell})
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Set
Y_{1}(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left(\frac{X}{496}\right)^{\frac{1}{6}}
. For
E\in\mathcal{S}_{\ell}\left(Y_{1}(X)\right)
, the pair
\gamma_{\ell}(E)
belongs to
\mathcal{M}(\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell});X)
.

Proof.

By the discussion above,
N_{E}^{\prime}\leq|\Delta_{E}|\leq 496Y_{1}(X)^{6}=X
, and thus, the result follows.
∎

Set
\Delta_{E}^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Delta_{L_{\ell}(E)}
; we study the relationship between
\Delta(E)
and
\Delta_{E}^{\prime}
. Write
|\Delta_{E}^{\prime}|
to be a product of prime powers
\prod_{p}p^{m_{\ell}(p)}
. At each prime number
p
, let
v_{p}
be the valuation normalized by setting
v_{p}(p)=1
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
be an elliptic curve and
\ell
be a prime number. The following assertions hold.

(1)

For a prime number
p\neq\ell
at which
E
has semistable reduction, let
m_{\ell}(p)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{ord}_{p}(\Delta_{E}^{\prime})
defined above. Then, we have that
m_{\ell}(p)\leq\left(\frac{\ell-1}{\ell}\right)\#\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
.

(2)

Suppose on the other hand that
E
has additive reduction at
p
, and
p\in\{2,3\}
, then,
m_{\ell}(p)\leq(3^{2}-1)(3^{2}-3)\times 68=3264
.

(3)

We have that
m_{\ell}(\ell)\leq C_{\ell}
, where
C_{\ell}>0
is a constant which depends only on
\ell
.

Proof.

The
[
CK02
, Theoreme 1, part (a)]
implies that
m_{\ell}(p)=dD/e
, where
d=[\mathbb{Q}_{p}(E[\ell])\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}]\leq\#\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
and
e
is the index of ramification in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(E[\ell])/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. The number

D=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }\ell\mid v_{p}(j(E));\\
(\ell-1)&\text{ if }\ell\nmid v_{p}(j(E)).\end{cases}

When
\ell\nmid v_{p}(j(E))
, it follows from the theory of the Tate curve that
e=\ell
(cf.
[
CK02
, Corollarie 1, (1) p.7]
). This proves part (
1
).

For part (
2
), we note that by results in
[
CK02
]
,
D\leq 68
, and
d\leq\#\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{3})=(3^{2}-3)(3^{2}-3)
, from which the result follows.

When
p=\ell
, the result follows from the recipe in
[
Kra99
]
.
∎

Proposition 2.4
.

Set
Y_{2}(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left(\frac{X}{6^{3264}\ell^{C_{\ell}}}\right)^{\frac{\ell}{6(\ell-1)\#\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})}}
. For
E\in\mathcal{S}_{\ell}\left(Y_{2}(X)\right)
, the pair
L_{\ell}(E)
belongs to
\mathcal{F}(\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell});X)
.

Proof.

From Proposition
2.3
, we arrive at the following estimates

\begin{split}|\Delta_{E}^{\prime}|=&2^{m_{\ell}(2)}3^{m_{\ell}(3)}\ell^{m_{\ell}(\ell)}\left(\prod_{p\notin\{2,3,\ell\}}p^{m_{\ell}(p)}\right)\\
\leq&6^{3264}\ell^{C_{\ell}}\left(\prod_{p\mid\Delta_{E}}p\right)^{\left(\frac{\ell-1}{\ell}\right)\#\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})}\\
\leq&6^{3264}\ell^{C_{\ell}}|\Delta_{E}|^{\left(\frac{\ell-1}{\ell}\right)\#\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})}\\
\leq&6^{3264}\ell^{C_{\ell}}Y_{2}(X)^{6\left(\frac{\ell-1}{\ell}\right)\#\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})}\leq X.\end{split}

This proves the result.
∎

3.
Density results

In this section, we prove density results. We give a proof of Theorem
A
at the end of the section.

3.1.
A lower bound for the lower density of
\mathcal{S}_{\ell}

Let
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
be an elliptic curve. A prime number
\ell
is said to be
exceptional
if
\bar{\rho}_{E,\ell}
is not surjective.

Theorem 3.1

(Duke
[
Duk97
]
)
.

The set of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
with no exceptional primes has density
1
.

Recall from the previous section that
\mathcal{S}_{\ell}(X)
consists of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
with
H(E)\leq X^{6}
, such that

(1)

has semistable reduction at all primes
p
, except possibly at
p=2,3
,

(2)

and
\bar{\rho}_{E,\ell}
is surjective.

We thus write
\mathcal{S}_{\ell}(X)
as the intersection of sets
\mathcal{D}_{\ell}(X)
and
\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(X)
, where

\begin{split}&\mathcal{D}_{\ell}(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{E\in\mathcal{C}(X)\mid E\text{ has semistable reduction at all primes }p\neq 2,3\};\\
&\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{E\in\mathcal{C}(X)\mid\bar{\rho}_{E,\ell}\text{ is surjective}\}.\\
\end{split}

It follows from Theorem
3.1
that
\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(X)
has density
1
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
C\in\left(0,\frac{4}{\zeta(2)}\right)
, then, for all large enough values of
X
,

\#\mathcal{S}_{\ell}(X)>CX^{5}.

In particular, there is a constant
C_{1}>0
, such that for all
X>0
,

\#\mathcal{S}_{\ell}(X)>C_{1}X^{5}.

Proof.

We make a few reductions, and prove the result as a consequence of theorems of Duke
[
Duk97
]
and Sadek
[
Sad17
]
. It suffices to show that

\liminf_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{S}_{\ell}(X)}{X^{5}}\geq\frac{4}{\zeta(2)}.

Since
\mathcal{E}_{\ell}
has density
1
and
\mathcal{S}_{\ell}=\mathcal{D}_{\ell}\cap\mathcal{E}_{\ell}
, the above inequality is equivalent to showing that

\liminf_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{D}_{\ell}(X)}{\#\mathcal{C}(X)}\geq\frac{\zeta(10)}{\zeta(2)}.

This result follows from
[
Sad17
, Theorem 9.3]
.
∎

3.2.
Counting elliptic curves with prescribed division field

Throughout the rest of this section, we fix a Galois extension
L/\mathbb{Q}
such that
\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})
is isomorphic to
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
. Let
\mathcal{D}_{L}
be the set of elliptic curves
E\in\mathcal{C}
such that
L_{\ell}(E)=L
. The following is the main result of this section.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
L/\mathbb{Q}
be as above. Then, there exists a positive constant
C_{2}
, which only depends on
\ell
, and is independent of the extension
L/\mathbb{Q}
, such that for all
X>0
,

\#\mathcal{D}_{L}(X)\leq C_{2}X^{9/2}\log X.

We provide the proof at the end of this section. Let
X>0
and let us assume without loss of generality that there exists an elliptic curve
A\in\mathcal{D}_{L}
. Note that since
L_{\ell}(A)=L
, it follows that
\bar{\rho}_{A,\ell}
is surjective. We make a choice of
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
-basis
\mathcal{B}_{A}
for
A[\ell]
. Suppose that
E\in\mathcal{D}_{L}(X)
is another elliptic curve, and
\mathcal{B}_{E}
be a choice of
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
-basis for
E[\ell]
. Then, the residual representations give rise to isomorphisms, taken with respect to
\mathcal{B}_{A}
and
\mathcal{B}_{E}
respectively

\begin{split}&\bar{\rho}_{E,\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell}),\\
&\bar{\rho}_{A,\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell}).\end{split}

Therefore, there exists
\eta\in\operatorname{Aut}\left(\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})\right)
, such that
\bar{\rho}_{A,\ell}=\eta\circ\bar{\rho}_{E,\ell}
. We set
\operatorname{Inn}\left(\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})\right)
to denote the inner automorphisms of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
, and set

\operatorname{Out}\left(\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})\right)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Aut}\left(\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})\right)/\operatorname{Inn}\left(\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})\right).

The class

\bar{\eta}_{E}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\eta\operatorname{Inn}\left(\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})\right)\in\operatorname{Out}\left(\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})\right)

associated to
E\in\mathcal{D}_{L}(X)
depends only on
E
(and is independent of the choice of basis
\mathcal{B}_{E}
).

Fixing
(A,\mathcal{B}_{A})
for the entire family
\mathcal{D}_{L}
, we decompose
\mathcal{D}_{L}
into a disjoint union

\mathcal{D}_{L}=\bigsqcup_{\zeta\in\operatorname{Out}\left(\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})\right)}\mathcal{D}_{L,\zeta},

where

\mathcal{D}_{L,\zeta}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{E\in\mathcal{D}_{L}\mid\eta_{E}=\zeta\}.

Let
E_{1},E_{2}\in\mathcal{D}_{L,\zeta}
, and let
\mathcal{B}_{E_{i}}
be a choice of
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
-basis of
E_{i}[\ell]
. We find that

\bar{\rho}_{A,\ell}=\eta_{i}\circ\bar{\rho}_{E_{i},\ell},

where
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in\operatorname{Aut}\left(\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})\right)
. Since
\eta_{E_{1}}=\eta_{E_{2}}
, there is an inner automorphism
\tau
of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
such that
\eta_{1}=\eta_{2}\circ\tau
. This implies that
\bar{\rho}_{E_{2},\ell}=\tau\circ\bar{\rho}_{E_{1},\ell}
, i.e.,

\bar{\rho}_{E_{1},\ell}\simeq\bar{\rho}_{E_{2},\ell}.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
be an elliptic curve, and let
\mathcal{D}_{E}
to be the set of elliptic curves
E^{\prime}_{/\mathbb{Q}}
for which
\bar{\rho}_{E^{\prime},\ell}\simeq\bar{\rho}_{E,\ell}
.

Proposition 3.5
.

With respect to notation above, there is a set of elliptic curves
E_{1},\dots,E_{t}
with
t\leq\#\operatorname{Out}\left(\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})\right)
, such that
\mathcal{D}_{L}
is contained in the union
\bigcup_{i=1}^{t}\mathcal{D}_{E_{i}}
.

Proof.

For each
\zeta\in\operatorname{Out}\left(\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})\right)
, such that
\mathcal{D}_{L,\zeta}
is nonempty, pick an elliptic curve
E_{\zeta}\in\mathcal{D}_{L,\zeta}
. Then, from the discussion above, we find that
\mathcal{D}_{L,\zeta}
is contained in
\mathcal{D}_{E_{\zeta}}
, and the result follows.
∎

Suppose that
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
are elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
such that
\bar{\rho}_{E_{1},\ell}\simeq\bar{\rho}_{E_{2},\ell}
. Let
N_{i}
denote the conductor of
E_{i}
. For any prime number
p\nmid N_{1}N_{2}\ell
, note that
\bar{\rho}_{E_{i},\ell}
is unramified at
p
. Let
\sigma_{p}
be a choice of Frobenius at
p
, and set
t_{p}(E_{i})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{trace}\left(\bar{\rho}_{E_{i},\ell}(\sigma_{p})\right)
. We find that
t_{p}(E_{1})=t_{p}(E_{2})
for all prime numbers
p\nmid N_{1}N_{2}\ell
.

Let
(t_{1},t_{2},d)\in\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{3}
be such that
d\neq 0
. Let
(E_{1},E_{2})
be a pair of elliptic curves defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, and let
\pi_{E_{1},E_{2}}(X,t_{1},t_{2},d,\ell)
to be the number of prime numbers
p\leq X
such that

(1)

p\nmid N_{1}N_{2}
,

(2)

p\equiv d\pmod{\ell}
,

(3)

t_{p}(E_{i})=t_{i}
for
i=1,2
.

Let
\pi(X,d,\ell)
be the number of prime numbers
p\leq X
such that
p\equiv d\pmod{\ell}
. Note that if
\bar{\rho}_{E_{1},\ell}\simeq\bar{\rho}_{E_{2},\ell}
, then, the relation
t_{p}(E_{1})=t_{p}(E_{2})
implies that

\pi_{E_{1},E_{2}}(X,t_{1},t_{2},d,\ell)=0,

unless
t_{1}=t_{2}
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
(t_{1},t_{2},d)\in\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{3}
be such that
d\neq 0
, and
(E_{1},E_{2})
be any pair of elliptic curves defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then, there are constants
\delta,C>0
, such that for all
X>0
,

\frac{1}{\#\mathcal{C}(X)^{2}}\sum_{(E_{1},E_{2})\in\mathcal{C}(X)^{2}}\left(\pi_{E_{1},E_{2}}(X,t_{1},t_{2},d,\ell)-\delta\pi(X,d,\ell)\right)^{2}\leq CX.

Proof.

This result follows from a variant of Gallagher’s large sieve, and is similar argument to that of
[
Duk97
, Theorem 2]
. For a proof of the result, we refer to
[
RW23
, Proposition 3.2]
.
∎

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
be an elliptic curve. Then, there exists a constant
C_{3}>0
, which are independent of
E
, such that for all
X>0
,

\#\mathcal{D}_{E}(X)\leq C_{3}X^{9/2}\log X.

Proof.

Let
E_{1},E_{2}
be elliptic curves in
\mathcal{D}_{E}
; notice that
\bar{\rho}_{E_{1},\ell}\simeq\bar{\rho}_{E_{2},\ell}
. Taking
(t_{1},t_{2},d)\in\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{3}
such that
d\neq 0
and
t_{1}\neq t_{2}
, we find that

\pi_{E_{1},E_{2}}(X,t_{1},t_{2},d,\ell)=0.

Proposition
3.6
asserts that

\frac{1}{\#\mathcal{C}(X)^{2}}\sum_{(E_{1},E_{2})\in\mathcal{C}(X)^{2}}\left(\pi_{E_{1},E_{2}}(X,t_{1},t_{2},d,\ell)-\delta\pi(X,d,\ell)\right)^{2}\leq CX.

Restricting the sum to
\mathcal{D}_{E}(X)^{2}
, we find that

\frac{1}{\#\mathcal{C}(X)^{2}}\sum_{(E_{1},E_{2})\in\mathcal{D}_{E}(X)^{2}}\left(\pi_{E_{1},E_{2}}(X,t_{1},t_{2},d,\ell)-\delta\pi(X,d,\ell)\right)^{2}\leq CX.

Since

\pi_{E_{1},E_{2}}(X,t_{1},t_{2},d,\ell)=0,

this simplifies to give us

\left(\frac{\#\mathcal{D}_{E}(X)}{\#\mathcal{C}(X)}\right)^{2}\leq\frac{C}{\delta^{2}}\frac{X}{\pi(X,d,\ell)^{2}},

and therefore,

\left(\frac{\#\mathcal{D}_{E}(X)}{\#\mathcal{C}(X)}\right)\leq\frac{\sqrt{C}}{\delta}\frac{X^{1/2}}{\pi(X,d,\ell)}.

According to Dirichlet’s theorem,
\pi(X,d,\ell)\sim\frac{1}{\varphi(\ell)}\frac{X}{\log X}
, and therefore,

\left(\frac{\#\mathcal{D}_{E}(X)}{\#\mathcal{C}(X)}\right)=O\left(\frac{\log X}{\sqrt{X}}\right).

Since
\#\mathcal{C}(X)\sim\frac{4}{\zeta(10)}X^{5}
, we deduce that

\#\mathcal{D}_{E}(X)=O\left(X^{9/2}\log X\right),

which proves the result.
∎

Proof of Proposition
3.3
.

Proposition
3.5
asserts that there is a set of elliptic curves
E_{1},\dots,E_{t}
with
t\leq\#\operatorname{Out}\left(\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})\right)
, such that
\mathcal{D}_{L}

Therefore, setting

∎

3.3.
Proof of the main result

In this section, we prove the main result. The main ingredients will be Propositions
2.2
,
2.4
,
3.2
and
3.3
.

Proof of Theorem
A
.

Setting
Y_{1}(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left(\frac{X}{496}\right)^{\frac{1}{6}}
, we recall that Proposition
2.2
asserts that for any elliptic curve
E\in\mathcal{S}_{\ell}\left(Y_{1}(X)\right)
, the pair
\gamma_{\ell}(E)
belongs to
\mathcal{M}(\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell});X)
.
This implies that

\begin{split}\#\mathcal{S}_{\ell}(Y_{1}(X))&\leq\sum_{(L,\rho)\in\mathcal{M}(\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell});X)}\#\{E\in\mathcal{C}(Y_{1}(X))\mid\gamma_{\ell}(E)=(L,\rho)\};\\
&\leq\sum_{(L,\rho)\in\mathcal{M}(\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell});X)}\#\{E\in\mathcal{C}(Y_{1}(X))\mid L_{\ell}(E)=L\};\\
&=\sum_{(L,\rho)\in\mathcal{M}(\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell});X)}\#\mathcal{D}_{L}(Y_{1}(X))\\
&\leq C_{2}Y_{1}(X)^{9/2}\log Y_{1}(X)\#\mathcal{M}(\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell});X);\end{split}

where in the final inequality, we invoke Proposition
3.3
. We reiterate here that
C_{2}
depends only on
\ell
, and is independent of the extension
L/\mathbb{Q}
. It follows from Proposition
3.2
that there is a constant
C_{1}>0
, such that for all
X>0
, we have that
\#\mathcal{S}_{\ell}(X)>C_{1}X^{5}
. Thus, we find that

\begin{split}\#\mathcal{M}(\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell});X)&\geq\frac{\#\mathcal{S}_{\ell}(Y_{1}(X))}{C_{2}Y_{1}(X)^{9/2}\log Y_{1}(X)};\\
&\geq\frac{C_{1}(Y_{1}(X))^{5}}{C_{2}Y_{1}(X)^{9/2}\log Y_{1}(X)}\geq C\frac{X^{\frac{1}{12}}}{\log X},\end{split}

for some constant
C>0
.

Setting
Y_{2}(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left(\frac{X}{6^{3264}\ell^{C_{\ell}}}\right)^{\frac{\ell}{6(\ell-1)\#\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})}}
, we recall that Proposition
2.4
asserts that for any elliptic curve
E\in\mathcal{S}_{\ell}\left(Y_{2}(X)\right)
, the division field
L_{\ell}(E)
belongs to
\mathcal{F}(\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell});X)
. By the same argument as above, we find that

\begin{split}\#\mathcal{F}(\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell});X)&\geq\frac{\#\mathcal{S}_{\ell}(Y_{2}(X))}{C_{2}Y_{2}(X)^{9/2}\log Y_{2}(X)};\\
&\geq\frac{C_{1}(Y_{2}(X))^{5}}{C_{2}Y_{2}(X)^{9/2}\log Y_{2}(X)}\geq C\frac{X^{\frac{\ell}{12(\ell-1)\#\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})}}}{\log X},\end{split}

for some constant
C>0
.
∎

Introduction

In this paper we consider the initial value problem (IVP) for the inhomogeneous nonlinear Schrödinger (INLS) equation

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u+k(x)|x|^{-b}|u|^{\frac{4-2b}{N}}u=0,\,\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\,t>0,\\
u(0)=u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
N\geq 1
and
0<b<\min\{2,N\}
. The specific assumptions on the function
k(x)\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
will be detailed later. This model is relevant to the investigation of the nonlinear propagation of laser beams influenced by spatially varying interactions, see for instance Belmonte-Pérez-Vekslerchik-Torres
[
2
]
. For
b=0
, the IVP (
1.1
) simplifies to the model studied in the classical papers by Merle
[
20
]
and Raphaël-Szeftel
[
22
]
.

When
k(x)\equiv k>0
is a constant function, the scaling symmetry preserves the
L^{2}
norm and the INLS equation is referred to as mass critical for any
0<b<\min\{2,N\}
. In this case, the local well-posedness question for the IVP (
1.1
) has been has been extensively investigated in recent literature. We refer the reader to Genoud-Stuart
[
13
, Appendix K]
for the proof in the general case
N\geq 1
and
0<b<\min\{2,N\}
, using the abstract theory developed by Cazenave
[
8
, Theorem 4.3.1]
. Also Guzmán
[
15
, Corollary 1.6]
, Dinh
[
10
, Theorem 1.2]
, Aloui-Tayachi
[
1
, Theorem 1.2]
and Campos-Correia-Farah
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
obtained similar result, by utilizing Strichartz estimates and dividing the spatial domain into distinct regions (near and far from the origin), reaching the case
1

1

As pointed out in Dinh
[
10
, Remark 3.2]
this approach can not be applied to study the case
N=1
.

N\geq 2
and
0<b<\min\{2,N/2\}
. Genoud
[
12
]
identified sufficient conditions for global well-posedness using a sharp Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality and we review his result in Section
2
. Blow-up solutions are also known to exist for this equation. Indeed, if
u
is a solution of

i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u+k|x|^{-b}|u|^{\frac{4-2b}{N}}u=0,

(1.2)

then, for all
T,\lambda>0
, from the pseudo-conformal invariance (see Combet-Genoud
[
9
, Lemma 9]
) and the scaling symmetry, we deduce that

Q_{T,\lambda}\left(x,t\right)=\left(\frac{\lambda}{T-t}\right)^{N/2}e^{i\lambda^{2}/(T-t){-i|x|^{2}/4(T-t)}}Q_{k}\left(\frac{\lambda x}{T-t}\right),

(1.3)

is also a solution of (
1.2
), where
Q_{k}
is the unique positive and radial solution of

-\Delta Q+Q-k|x|^{-b}\left|Q\right|^{\frac{4-2b}{N}}Q=0,

(1.4)

(we refer the reader to Genoud-Stuart
[
13
]
and Genoud
[
12
]
for the existence and uniqueness theory).
Note that
\|Q_{T,\lambda}(t)\|_{L^{2}}=\|Q_{k}\|_{L^{2}}
for all existence time. Combet-Genoud
[
9
]
establish the classification of such blow-up solutions, following the ideas introduced in the seminal work of Merle
[
19
]
(see also Campos-Cardoso
[
4
]
for an alternative proof, based on a compactness theorem in the spirit of Hmidi-Keraani
[
16
, Theorem 1.1]
).

The conserved quantities for the flow of (
1.1
) are the mass and energy given, respectively, by

M[u]=\int|u|^{2}\,dx

(1.5)

and

E[u]=\frac{1}{2}\int|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx-\frac{1}{\frac{4-2b}{N}+2}\int k(x)|x|^{-b}|u|^{\frac{4-2b}{N}+2}\,dx.

(1.6)

Throughout this paper, we assume the following conditions on the function
k(x)\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

(
H1
)

k,\,\,\nabla k\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
k(0)>0
;

(
H2
)

x\cdot\nabla k\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

As a consequence of (
H1
), the local well-posedness of (
1.1
) can be obtained exactly as in
[
13
,
15
,
5
]
. In particular, from the Strichartz estimate approach, the maximal existence time of the solution
T(u_{0})>0
depends only on the
H^{1}
norm of the initial data and satisfies
T(u_{0})\gtrsim\|u_{0}\|^{-\alpha}_{H^{1}}
for some
\alpha>0
. This, in turn, together with mass conservation imply the blow-up alternative

\text{if}\quad T(u_{0})<+\infty,\quad\text{then}\quad\lim_{t\uparrow T}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}=+\infty.

(1.7)

We say that a solution blows up in finite time if that maximal existence time is finite (and therefore the above limit holds).

Our main goal in the present paper is to established the threshold for global existence and blow-up and then study the existence (or not) of blow-up solutions at the threshold level. Unlike the results obtained by Merle
[
20
]
in the case
b=0
, we prove that the behavior of the function
k(x)
at the origin is crucial to our analysis and, in particular, our results remain valid even when this function changes sign. Indeed, set
Q_{k(0)}
to be the unique positive and radial solution of equation (
1.4
) with
k=k(0)
, then as a consequence of a concentration result (see Theorem
3.1
below), we first obtain the following global result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume (
H1
). Let
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}<\|Q_{k(0)}\|_{L^{2}}
, then the corresponding solution to the IVP (
1.1
) is global in
H^{1}
.

Let
k_{1}=\|k\|_{L^{\infty}}>0
. If
k(0)<k_{1}
, then, in view of (
2.5
) below, we have that
\|Q_{k_{1}}\|_{L^{2}}<\|Q_{k(0)}\|_{L^{2}}
. The previous result says that it is possible to find a global solution with mass slightly above the level
\|Q_{k_{1}}\|_{L^{2}}
only assuming (
H1
). This is quite different from the case
b=0
studied by Merle
[
20
]
, where the threshold between global existence and blow-up is proved, under an additional condition on
k(x)
, to be
\|Q_{k_{1}}\|_{L^{2}}
.

The next result asserts blow-up in finite time for negative energy solutions.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume (
H1
), (
H2
) and

x\cdot\nabla k(x)\leq 0,\quad\mbox{for all}\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

(1.8)

If
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is such that
E[u_{0}]<0
, then the corresponding solution to the IVP (
1.1
) blows up in finite time.

In particular, the assumption (
1.8
) implies that the origin is a global maximum, that is,
k(0)=\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}k(x)
. Note that, under this assumption, if
\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}<\|Q_{k(0)}\|_{L^{2}}
, then
E[u_{0}]\geq 0
(see relations (
2.4
) and (
2.7
) below). Additionally, no radial assumption is needed in the previous theorem and the method of the proof follows the strategy introduced in Cardoso-Farah
[
6
]
.
It should be noted that we can not translate the assumption
x\cdot\nabla k(x)\leq 0
to another point
x_{0}\neq 0
as in Merle
[
20
]
. Indeed, consider the simplest case where the initial data belongs to the virial space
\Sigma=H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap\{f:|x|f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\}
. For any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, the viral identity associated to the solution of (
1.1
) reads as follows (see, for instance Farah
[
11
, Proposition 4.1]
)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\int\left|x-x_{0}\right|^{2}|u|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=16E\left[u_{0}\right]+\frac{4}{\frac{2-b}{N}+1}\int\left(x-x_{0}\right)\cdot\nabla k(x)|x|^{-b}|u|^{\frac{4-2b}{N}+2}\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{4b}{\frac{2-b}{N}+1}\int\frac{x\cdot x_{0}}{|x|^{b+2}}k(x)|u|^{\frac{4-2b}{N}+2}\,dx.

(1.9)

The first integral in the right hand side of (
1.9
) is non-positive assuming

\displaystyle\left(x-x_{0}\right)\cdot\nabla k(x)\leq 0,\quad\text{for all}\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

(1.10)

However, the last integral does not have a definite sign.

The next result shows that the level
\|Q_{k(0)}\|_{L^{2}}
is the correct threshold for global existence and blow-up. Indeed, we can find blow-up solution slightly above this level assuming a global or local condition on the function
k(x)
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume (
H1
), (
H2
) and

(i)

x\cdot\nabla k(x)\leq 0,\quad\text{for all}\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

or

(ii)

x\cdot\nabla k(x)<0,\quad\text{for all}\quad 0<|x|<\ell_{0}
and some
\ell_{0}>0
.

Then, there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
, there exists
u_{0,\varepsilon}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\left\|u_{0,\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}}=\|Q_{k(0)}\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon
and the corresponding solution
u_{\varepsilon}(t)
of (
1.1
) blows up in finite time.

Under assumption
(i)
of the previous theorem we in fact have
\varepsilon_{0}=\infty
. Moreover, assumption
(ii)
implies that the origin is a local strict maximum, that is,
k(0)>k(x)
for
0<|x|<\rho_{0}
.

Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
naturally lead to the definition of a minimal mass blow-up solution, which is a solution of the IVP (
1.1
) with initial data satisfying
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}=\|Q_{k(0)}\|_{L^{2}}
, that blows up in finite time
T>0
. The next result provides a characterization of this type of solution.

Theorem 1.4
.

Assume (
H1
). Suppose that
u(t)
is a minimal mass blow-up solution for the IVP (
1.1
) blowing up in finite time
T>0
, then

|u(t)|^{2}\rightharpoonup\|Q_{k(0)}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\delta_{0},\quad\mbox{as}\quad t\uparrow T,

(1.11)

where
\delta_{0}
stands for the delta function at zero and
T>0
is the blow-up time.

Finally, we investigate the problem to find a blow-up solution with minimal mass. For the non-existence we prove

Theorem 1.5
.

Assume (H1), (H2) and that there exist
\ell_{0},c_{0}>0
such that

x\cdot\nabla k(x)<-c_{0}|x|^{1+\alpha_{0}},\quad\text{for}\quad|x|<\ell_{0},

(1.12)

with
\alpha_{0}\in(0,1)
. Then there is no minimal mass blow-up solution for the IVP (
1.1
).

Note that the assumption (
1.12
) implies, for some
c>0
, that

k(0)-k(x)\geq c|x|^{1+\alpha_{0}}\quad\text{ for }\quad|x|<\ell_{0}.

(1.13)

Moreover, it also implies that
k(x)
is not
C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
near the origin.

On the other hand, assuming a flatness condition on
k(x)
around the origin, we have the following existence result.

Theorem 1.6
.

Assume (H1)-(H2) and that there exists
\ell_{0}>0
such that

\quad k(x)=k(0),\quad\text{for}\quad|x|<\ell_{0}.

(1.14)

Then, for any
T>0
, there exists a minimal mass blow-up solution for the IVP (
1.1
) that blows up at time
T
.

It is important to highlight that, in the last two theorems, the analysis primarily requires the behavior of the function
k(x)
near the origin. The validity of the results persists even if the global maximum of
k(x)
occurs elsewhere or this function changes sign.

To prove Theorem
1.6
, inspired by Merle
[
18
]
, we will show that
Q_{T,\lambda}
defined in (
1.3
), solution of equation (
1.2
), is close to a sequence of solutions to (
1.1
) and applying compactness arguments we construct the desired solution. To deal with the potential
|x|^{-b}
we need a new dispersive estimate in weighted Sobolev spaces (see Lemma
5.1
below).

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Sect.
2
, we introduce some notations and preliminary estimates. In Sect.
3

1.1
and
1.4
, respectively. Sect.
4

1.2
-
1.3
. Finally, in Sect.
5

2
Notations and preliminary results

The letter
c
will be used to denote a positive constant that may vary from line to line. If necessary, we use subscript to indicate different parameters the constant may depends on. Given any positive quantities
a
and
b
, the notation
a\lesssim b
means that
a\leq cb
, with
c
uniform with respect to the set where
a
and
b
vary. We write
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}}
,
p\in[1,+\infty]
, to denote the usual
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
norm in the space variable. If the integration is restricted to a subset
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
we use
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(A)}
. To simplify the notation throughout the manuscript we set

\sigma_{b}=\frac{4-2b}{N}+2.

Moreover, the weighted Lebesgue space
L_{b}^{\sigma_{b}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is defined to be the normed linear space equipped with the norm

\|f\|_{L_{b}^{\sigma_{b}}}=\left(\int|x|^{-b}|f(x)|^{\sigma_{b}}\,dx\right)^{1/\sigma_{b}}.

Next, we recall some well known facts about the mass critical INLS equation (
1.2
). In this case the conserved quantities mass and energy are given, respectively, by

M_{k}[u]=\int|u|^{2}\,dx

(2.1)

and

E_{k}[u]=\frac{1}{2}\int|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx-\frac{k}{\sigma_{b}}\int|x|^{-b}|u|^{\sigma_{b}}\,dx.

(2.2)

The standing wave solution
u(x,t)=e^{it}Q_{k}
, where
Q_{k}
is the unique positive and radial solution of (
1.4
), plays an important role in the study of the global behavior of the solutions. Indeed, this solution is connected with the best constant for the Gagliardo-Nirenberg type inequality

\int|x|^{-b}|f|^{\sigma_{b}}dx\leq\left(\frac{\sigma_{b}}{2k\left\|Q_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{4-2b}{N}}}\right)\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{4-2b}{N}},

(2.3)

see Genoud
[
12
, Corollary 2.3]
and also Farah
[
11
, Theorem 1.2]
for a more general inequality.
Thus, we deduce for all
v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
that

\displaystyle E_{k}[v]

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}\int|\nabla v|^{2}dx\right)\left(1-\left(\frac{\|v\|_{L^{2}}}{\left\|Q_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}}}\right)^{\frac{4-2b}{N}}\right),

(2.4)

which implies that solutions for the mass critical INLS equation (
1.2
) are global if the initial data satisfies
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}<\left\|Q_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}}
.

Moreover, for all
k>0
the solutions to (
1.4
) are related according to

Q_{k}=k^{-\frac{N}{4-2b}}Q_{1}.

(2.5)

The Pohozaev identities are given by

\left\|\nabla Q_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\frac{N}{2-b}\left\|Q_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

and

k\|Q_{k}\|^{\sigma_{b}}_{L_{b}^{\sigma_{b}}}=\left(1+\frac{N}{2-b}\right)\|Q_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

which implies

E_{k}[Q_{k}]=0.

(2.6)

Also note that, under the condition
k(0)=\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}k(x)
, definitions (
1.6
) and (
2.2
) imply for all
v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

E[v]\geq E_{k}[v].

(2.7)

Decay properties of the unique positive and radial solution of equation (
1.4
) were obtained by Genoud-Stuart
[
13
, Theorem 2.2]
, in particular, there exists
{\theta}>0
(independent of
k>0
by relation (
2.5
)) such that

\left|Q_{k}(x)\right|\leq ce^{-{\theta}|x|},\quad\text{for all}\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

(2.8)

Next, we recall the following variational characterization of the ground state

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

\|v\|_{L^{2}}=\left\|Q_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad E_{k}[v]=0,

(2.9)

then there exist
\lambda_{0}>0
and
\gamma_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
v(x)=e^{i\gamma_{0}}\lambda_{0}^{N/2}Q_{k}\left(\lambda_{0}x\right)

Proof.

In the particular case where
k=1
, this was proved by Combet-Genoud
[
9
, Proposition 2]
. For
k>0
, define
v_{k}=k^{\frac{N}{4-2b}}v
, then from assumption (
2.9
) and relation (
2.5
), we have

\left\|v_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}}=\left\|Q_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad E_{1}\left[v_{k}\right]=0.

Therefore, since the conclusion holds for
k=1
, we deduce

v_{k}(x)=e^{i\gamma_{0}}\lambda_{0}^{N/2}Q_{1}\left(\lambda_{0}x\right)

thus, the definition of
v_{k}
and relation (
2.5
) yield

v(x)=e^{i\gamma_{0}}\lambda_{0}^{N/2}{Q_{k}\left(\lambda_{0}x\right)}.

∎

A more general statement is the following

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\{v_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

\left\|v_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}}\rightarrow\left\|Q_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}},\quad\left\|\nabla v_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}}\rightarrow\left\|\nabla Q_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\displaystyle\limsup_{n}E_{k}\left[v_{n}\right]\leq 0.

(2.10)

Then, there exists a subsequence of
\{v_{n}\}
and
\gamma_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\lim_{n}\|v_{n}-e^{i\gamma_{0}}Q_{k}\|_{H^{1}}=0.

Proof.

This is essentially Proposition 5 in Combet-Genoud
[
9
]
. Here we give a simplified proof based on the compact embedding
H^{1}\hookrightarrow L_{b}^{\sigma_{b}}
(see, for instance, Genoud
[
12
, Lemma 2.1]
). Since
\left\{v_{n}\right\}
is a bounded sequence in
{H}^{1}
, up to a subsequence, there exists
V\in H^{1}
, such that
v_{n}\rightharpoonup V
in
H^{1}
and the compact embedding mentioned above yields

v_{n}\rightarrow V\quad\text{in}\quad L^{\sigma_{b}}_{b}.

(2.11)

Now, since

E_{k}\left[v_{n}\right]=\frac{1}{2}\int\left|\nabla v_{n}\right|^{2}\,dx-\frac{k}{\sigma_{b}}\int|x|^{-b}\left|v_{n}\right|^{\sigma_{b}}\,dx

we deduce, from (
2.10
), that
V\neq 0
. Moreover, we apply (
2.11
) to obtain

\displaystyle 0\geq\limsup_{n}\frac{1}{2}\int\left|\nabla v_{n}\right|^{2}\,dx-\frac{k}{\sigma_{b}}\lim_{n}\int|x|^{-b}\left|v_{n}\right|^{\sigma_{b}}\,dx

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\int|\nabla V|^{2}\,dx-\frac{k}{\sigma_{b}}\int|x|^{-b}|V|^{\sigma_{b}}dx=E_{k}[V]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\int|\nabla V|^{2}\,dx\left(1-\left(\frac{\|V\|_{L^{2}}}{\left\|Q_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}}}\right)^{\frac{4-2b}{N}}\right),

where in the last inequality we have used the Sharp Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
2.3
). Thus,
\|V\|_{L^{2}}\geq\left\|Q_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}}
. On the other hand, since
v_{n}\rightharpoonup V
in
H^{1}
, assumption (
2.10
) implies

\|V\|_{L^{2}}\leq\liminf_{n}\|v_{n}\|_{L^{2}}=\|Q_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\,\,\,\text{ and }\,\,\,\|\nabla V\|_{L^{2}}\leq\liminf_{n}\left\|\nabla v_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}}=\left\|\nabla Q_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}}.

Consequently
\|V\|_{L^{2}}=\left\|Q_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}}
and
E_{k}[V]=0
. From Proposition
2.1
, there exist
\lambda_{0}>0
and
\gamma_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

V(x)=e^{i\gamma_{0}}\lambda_{0}^{N/2}Q_{k}\left(\lambda_{0}x\right).

Now, again by (
2.10
)-(
2.11
), we have

\displaystyle 0=E_{k}[V]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int|\nabla V|^{2}\,dx-\frac{k}{\sigma_{b}}\int|x|^{-b}|V|^{\sigma_{b}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int\left|\nabla Q_{k}\right|^{2}\,dx-\frac{k}{\sigma_{b}}\int|x|^{-b}|V|^{\sigma_{b}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n}E_{k}\left[v_{n}\right]\leq 0,

then
\|\nabla V\|_{L^{2}}=\left\|\nabla Q_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}}
, which implies
\lambda_{0}=1
.

Finally, since
v_{n}\rightharpoonup V
in
H^{1}
and
\left\|v_{n}\right\|_{H^{1}}\rightarrow\|V\|_{H^{1}}
, we must have
v_{n}\rightarrow V=e^{i\gamma_{0}}Q_{k}
in
H^{1}
as desired.
∎

3
Concentration and global well-posedness

From the variational characterization of the ground state given in Proposition
2.2
we are able to deduce the next concentration result, it shows that any blow up solution must in fact concentrate at least the amount
\|Q_{k(0)}\|_{L^{2}}
around the origin due to the potential
|x|^{-b}
. It is worth mentioning that Campos-Cardoso
[
4
, Theorem 1.1]
proved an analogous result for the mass critical INLS equation (
1.2
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume (
H1
). Let
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that the corresponding solution to the IVP (
1.1
) blows up in finite
T>0
. Then, if
\rho(t)\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\rightarrow+\infty
as
t\uparrow T
we have

\liminf_{t\uparrow T}\int_{|x|\leq\rho(t)}\left|u(t)\right|^{2}\,dx\geq\|Q_{k(0)}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Proof.

Our proof again relies on the compact embedding
H^{1}\hookrightarrow L_{b}^{\sigma_{b}}
. Indeed, let
\{t_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{R}
such that
t_{n}\uparrow T
. For
\lambda_{n}=\|\nabla Q_{k(0)}\|_{L^{2}}/\|\nabla u(t_{n})\|_{L^{2}}
define

v_{n}(x)=\lambda_{n}^{\frac{N}{2}}u(\lambda_{n}x,t_{n}).

Then,

\|v_{n}\|_{L^{2}}=\|u(t_{n})\|_{L^{2}}=\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}},\quad\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{L^{2}}=\lambda_{n}\|\nabla u(t_{n})\|_{L^{2}}=\|\nabla Q_{k(0)}\|_{L^{2}}.

Moreover, since
\lambda_{n}\rightarrow 0
we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n}\lambda_{n}^{2}E\left[u_{0}\right]=\limsup_{n}\lambda_{n}^{2}E[u(t_{n})]

(3.1)

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n}\left(\frac{1}{2}\int\left|\nabla v_{n}\right|^{2}\,dx-\frac{k(0)}{\sigma_{b}}\int|x|^{-b}\left|v_{n}\right|^{\sigma_{b}}\,dx\right)

(3.2)

\displaystyle\quad+\lim_{n}\left(\frac{\lambda_{n}^{2}}{\sigma_{b}}\int(k(0)-k(x))|x|^{-b}|u(t_{n})|^{\sigma_{b}}\,dx\right).

(3.3)

We show that the last limit is zero. Indeed, for any
R>0
, we deduce

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{n}^{2}}{\sigma_{b}}\int|k(0)-k(x)||x|^{-b}|u(t_{n})|^{\sigma_{b}}\,dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\int|k(0)-k(\lambda_{n}x)||x|^{-b}|v_{n}|^{\sigma_{b}}\,dx

(3.4)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\|k\|_{L^{\infty}}}{R^{b}}\int_{|x|>R}|v_{n}|^{\sigma_{b}}\,dx+\lambda_{n}R\|\nabla k\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{|x|<R}|x|^{-b}|v_{n}|^{\sigma_{b}}\,dx

(3.5)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{1}{R^{b}}\|k\|_{L^{\infty}}+\lambda_{n}R\|\nabla k\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)\left\|v_{n}\right\|_{H^{1}}^{\sigma_{b}},

(3.6)

by Sobolev embedding and Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
2.3
). Since
\{v_{n}\}
is a bounded sequence in
H^{1}
and
\lambda_{n}\to 0
we deduce the desired limit.

Thus,
\limsup_{n}E_{k(0)}\left[v_{n}\right]\leq 0
and arguing as in the proof of Proposition
2.2
we deduce the existence of
V\in H^{1}
, such that, up to a subsequence,
v_{n}\rightharpoonup V
in
H^{1}
,
v_{n}\rightarrow V
in
L^{\sigma_{b}}_{b}
and
\|V\|_{L^{2}}\geq\|Q_{k(0)}\|_{L^{2}}
. The rest of the proof follows from classical argument (see, for instance, Hmidi-Keraani
[
16
, Theorem 2.1]
and Cardoso-Farah-Guzman
[
7
, Theorem 1.5]
).
∎

The above theorem immediately yields our global result.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

If the solution
u(t)\in H^{1}
for (
1.1
) with
u(0)=u_{0}
blows up in finite time
T>0
, then, for every
R>0
, mass conservation and the previous theorem imply

\left\|u_{0}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\geq\liminf_{t\uparrow T}\int_{|x|<R}\left|u(t)\right|^{2}\,dx\geq\|Q_{k(0)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}>\left\|u_{0}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}},

reaching a contradiction.
∎

Moreover, Theorem
3.1
also implies a characterization of the minimal mass blow-up solution.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

First observe that, for any
R>0
, mass conservation and Theorem
3.1
imply

\displaystyle\|Q_{k(0)}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\geq\limsup_{t\uparrow T}\int_{|x|\leq R}|u(t)|^{2}\,dx\geq\liminf_{t\uparrow T}\int_{|x|\leq R}|u(t)|^{2}\,dx\geq\|Q_{k(0)}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(3.7)

and therefore

\displaystyle\lim_{t\uparrow T}\int_{|x|\leq R}|u(t)|^{2}\,dx=\|Q_{k(0)}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{t\uparrow T}\int_{|x|>R}|u(x,t)|^{2}\,dx=0.

(3.8)

As a consequence, we have for all
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}
that

\displaystyle\int|u(t)|^{2}\varphi(x)\,dx=\|Q_{k(0)}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\varphi(0)+\int|u(t)|^{2}(\varphi(x)-\varphi(0))\,dx.

(3.9)

From the continuity of
\varphi
, given any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that

\displaystyle\int|u(t)|^{2}|\varphi(x)-\varphi(0)|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{|x|<\delta}|u(t)|^{2}\,dx+2\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{|x|>\delta}|u(t)|^{2}\,dx

(3.10)

so, from (
3.7
), we have

\lim_{t\uparrow T}\int|u(t)|^{2}|\varphi(x)-\varphi(0)|\,dx=0

and (
1.11
) holds.
∎

We end this section with two useful corollaries. The first one is similar to Proposition 3.2 in Campos-Cardoso
[
4

.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
u_{n}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}}\rightarrow\|Q_{k(0)}\|_{L^{2}}
,
\left\|\nabla u_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}}\rightarrow\infty
as
n\rightarrow+\infty
and
E\left[u_{n}\right]\leq c
. Thus, setting
\lambda_{n}=\|\nabla Q_{k(0)}\|_{L^{2}}/\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}
, there exists
\gamma_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\lambda_{n}^{N/2}e^{i\gamma_{n}}u_{n}\left(\lambda_{n}x\right)\rightarrow Q_{k(0)}(x)\quad\text{ in }\quad H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

(3.11)

and

\left|u_{n}(x)\right|^{2}\rightharpoonup\|Q_{k(0)}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\delta_{0},\quad\text{ as }\quad n\rightarrow+\infty.

Proof.

Defining
v_{n}(x)=\lambda_{n}^{N/2}u_{n}\left(\lambda_{n}x\right)
, we have

\displaystyle\|v_{n}\|_{L^{2}}=\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}\to\|Q_{k(0)}\|_{L^{2}},\quad\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{L^{2}}=\|\nabla Q_{k(0)}\|_{L^{2}}

(3.12)

and, from the proof of Theorem
3.1
,

\limsup_{n}E_{k(0)}[v_{n}]\leq\limsup_{n}\lambda^{2}_{n}E[u_{n}]\leq\limsup_{n}\lambda^{2}_{n}c=0.

Thus, Proposition
2.2
yields the limit (
3.11
). Moreover, for all
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}
we have

\displaystyle\left|\int\left[\left|u_{n}(x)\right|^{2}\varphi(x)-|Q_{k(0)}(x)|^{2}\varphi(0)\right]\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int\left|\left|u_{n}(x)\right|^{2}-\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}^{N}}\left|Q_{k(0)}\left(\frac{x}{\lambda_{n}}\right)\right|^{2}\right||\varphi(x)|\,dx

(3.13)

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\int\left[\frac{1}{\lambda^{N}_{n}}\left|Q_{k(0)}\left(\frac{x}{\lambda_{n}}\right)\right|^{2}\varphi(x)-\left|Q_{k(0)}(x)\right|^{2}\varphi(0)\right]\,dx\right|.

(3.14)

Since
\varphi\in L^{\infty}
and
\left\{u_{n}\right\}
is a bounded sequence in
L^{2}
, the first term in the right hand side of the last inequality goes to zero as
n\to\infty
by (
3.11
). On the other hand,

\displaystyle\int\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}^{N}}\left|Q_{k(0)}\left(\frac{x}{\lambda_{n}}\right)\right|^{2}\varphi(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int\left|Q_{k(0)}(x)\right|^{2}\varphi\left(\lambda_{n}x\right)\,dx

(3.15)

\displaystyle=\int\left|Q_{k(0)}(x)\right|^{2}\left(\varphi(\lambda_{n}x)-\varphi(0)\right)dx+\int\left|Q_{k(0)}(x)\right|^{2}\varphi(0)\,dx

(3.16)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\int\left|Q_{k(0)}(x)\right|^{2}\varphi(0)\,dx,\quad\text{as}\quad n\rightarrow+\infty,

(3.17)

by the Dominated Convergence Theorem, completing the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.3

Let

Then, for all

\displaystyle\liminf_{n}\int_{|x|<R}\left|u_{n}\right|^{2}\,dx\geq\|Q_{k(0)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

(3.18)

where

Next, we turn our attention to existence result stated in Theorem

Lemma 4.1
.

For all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, there exists a function

such that

\left\|u_{0,\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}}=\|Q_{k(0)}\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon\quad\mbox{and}\quad E\left[u_{0,\varepsilon}\right]\leq-1/\varepsilon.

Proof.

For
\varepsilon,\lambda>0
define

f_{\lambda,\varepsilon}(x)=(1+\varepsilon)\lambda^{N/2}Q_{k(0)}(\lambda x).

It is clear that
\|f_{\lambda,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}=(1+\varepsilon)\|Q_{k(0)}\|_{L^{2}}
. Moreover

\displaystyle E\left[f_{\lambda,\varepsilon}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int\left|\nabla f_{\lambda,\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\,dx-\frac{1}{\sigma_{b}}\int k(x)|x|^{-b}\left|f_{\lambda,\varepsilon}\right|^{\sigma_{b}}\,dx

(4.12)

\displaystyle=E_{k(0)}[f_{\lambda,\varepsilon}]+\frac{1}{\sigma_{b}}\int(k(0)-k(x))|x|^{-b}|f_{\lambda,\varepsilon}|^{\sigma_{b}}\,dx

(4.13)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}(1+\varepsilon)^{2}E_{k(0)}[Q_{k(0)}]

(4.14)

\displaystyle\quad+\lambda^{2}((1+\varepsilon)^{2}-(1+\varepsilon)^{\sigma_{b}})k(0)\int|x|^{-b}|Q_{k(0)}(x)|^{\sigma_{b}}\,dx

(4.15)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{\sigma_{b}}\int(k(0)-k(x))|x|^{-b}\left|f_{\lambda,\varepsilon}\right|^{\sigma_{b}}\,dx.

(4.16)

The first term in the right hand side of the last inequality is zero by (
2.6
). On the other hand, in view of the exponential decay of
Q_{k(0)}
(
2.8
), we have

\displaystyle\int(k(0)-k(x))|x|^{-b}\left|f_{\lambda,\varepsilon}\right|^{\sigma_{b}}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim c\|\nabla k\|_{L^{\infty}}\int|x|^{1-b}\left|f_{\lambda,\varepsilon}\right|^{\sigma_{b}}\,dx

(4.17)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda\|\nabla k\|_{L^{\infty}}\int|x|^{1-b}|Q_{k(0)}|^{\sigma_{b}}\,dx

(4.18)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda,

(4.19)

and

\lambda^{2}((1+\varepsilon)^{2}-(1+\varepsilon)^{\sigma_{b}})k(0)\int|x|^{-b}|Q_{k(0)}(x)|^{\sigma_{b}}\,dx=c\lambda^{2}((1+\varepsilon)^{2}-(1+\varepsilon)^{\sigma_{b}}).

Collecting the previous estimates, we obtain

E\left[f_{\lambda,\varepsilon}\right]\leq c\lambda^{2}((1+\varepsilon)^{2}-(1+\varepsilon)^{\sigma_{b}})+c\lambda.

Now, since
(1+\varepsilon)^{2}<(1+\varepsilon)^{\sigma_{b}}
, we take
\lambda=\lambda(\varepsilon)
sufficiently large and set

to deduce the desired result.
∎

\displaystyle\leq 16E[u_{0,\varepsilon}]-z_{0}^{\prime\prime}(t)+c-c\int_{|x|\leq\ell_{0}}x\cdot\nabla k(x)|x|^{-b}|u_{\varepsilon}(t)|^{\sigma_{b}}\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+c(\varepsilon)\int_{|x|\geq\ell_{0}/2}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(t)|^{2}\,dx.

(4.43)

From (

), we have that
c(\varepsilon)\rightarrow 0
, as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
(uniformly on
t>0
) and taking
\varepsilon>0
sufficient small we can absorb the last term in the left hand side of the previous inequality.

Thus, in view of (

), we finally have

\displaystyle z_{R}^{\prime\prime}(t)

\displaystyle\leq cE\left[u_{0,\varepsilon}\right]+c(\varepsilon)-c(\varepsilon)z_{0}^{\prime\prime}(t)

(4.44)

\displaystyle\quad+(c-c(\varepsilon))\int_{|x|\leq\ell_{0}}x\cdot\nabla k(x)|x|^{-b}\left|u_{\varepsilon}(t)\right|^{\sigma_{b}}\,dx

(4.45)

\displaystyle\leq cE\left[u_{0,\varepsilon}\right]+c(\varepsilon)-c(\varepsilon)z_{0}^{\prime\prime}(t)

(4.46)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{c}{2}\int_{|x|\leq\ell_{0}}x\cdot\nabla k(x)|x|^{-b}\left|u_{\varepsilon}(t)\right|^{\sigma_{b}}\,dx,

(4.47)

since we can take
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small such that

Therefore, from the local assumption (

sufficiently small, that

and since

∎

We start this section with the proof of our non-existence result.

Proof of Theorem

.

\displaystyle E[u_{0}]

(5.1)

(5.5)

by (

Next, we turn to the existence of minimal mass blow-up solution under a flatness condition on

. To this end we need the following new dispersive estimate in weighted Sobolev spaces.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
0<b<N
and
\sigma_{b}=\frac{4-2b}{N}+2
. Then, for all
t\neq 0
we have the dispersive estimate

\left\|S(t)\left[|\cdot|^{-b}|f|^{\sigma_{b}-1}\right]\right\|_{L_{b}^{\sigma_{b}}}\lesssim|t|^{-2/\sigma_{b}}\left\|f\right\|_{L_{b}^{\sigma_{b}}}^{\sigma_{b}-1}.

Proof.

We present a proof based on Lorentz spaces and we briefly review some theory on this topic. For
1<p<\infty
and
1\leq q\leq\infty
define

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p,q}}=\begin{cases}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[t^{\frac{1}{p}}f^{*}(t)\right]^{q}\,\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}&\text{ if }\quad 1\leq q<\infty,\\
\sup_{t>0}t^{\frac{1}{p}}f^{*}(t)&\text{ if }\quad q=\infty,\end{cases}

where the
f^{*}
is the decreasing
rearrangement of
f
defined for
t>0
by

f^{*}(t)=\inf\left\{s>0;m\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:|f(x)|>s\right\}\leq t\right\}.

L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
denotes the set of all functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{C}
with finite
\|f\|_{L^{p,q}}
. It is a quasi-Banach spaces (see Grafakos
[
14
, Theorem 1.4.11]
). Moreover, considering

f^{**}(t)=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}f^{*}(s)\,ds,\quad\textrm{for}\quad t>0,

these spaces become Banach spaces with the norm given by

\displaystyle|||f|||_{L^{p,q}}=\begin{cases}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[t^{\frac{1}{p}}f^{**}(t)\right]^{q}\,\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}&,\text{ if }\quad 1\leq q<\infty,\\
\sup_{t>0}t^{\frac{1}{p}}f^{**}(t)&,\text{ if }\quad q=\infty,\end{cases}

(see O’Neil
[
21
, Definition 2.1., page 136]
). The quasinorm
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p,q}}
and the norm
|||\cdot|||_{L^{p,q}}
are in fact equivalent and satisfy

\|f\|_{L^{p,q}}\leq|||f|||_{L^{p,q}}\leq\frac{p-1}{p}\|f\|_{L^{p,q}}.

Indeed, the first inequality is a direct consequence of
f^{\ast}(t)\leq f^{\ast\ast}(t)
and the second follows from a Hardy inequality (see Hunt
[
17
, Theorem (Hardy), page 256]
).

Some important fact about the Lorentz spaces are

•

L^{p}=L^{p,p}
for
1<p<\infty
and
L^{p,q}\subset L^{p,r}
, for
1\leq q<r\leq\infty
(see Grafakos
[
14
, Propositions 1.4.5 and 1.4.10]
);

•

\left\||f|^{a}\right\|_{L^{p,q}}=\|f\|_{L^{ap,aq}}
(see Grafakos
[
14
, Remark 1.4.7]
);

•

If
1/p=1/p_{1}+1/p_{2},1/q\leq 1/q_{1}+1/q_{2},1<p,p_{1},p_{2}<\infty,1\leq q_{1},q_{2}\leq\infty
, then

\left\|fg\right\|_{L^{p,q}}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p_{1},q_{1}}}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{p_{2},q_{2}}}.

(5.10)

(see O’Neil
[
21
, Theorem 3.4]
)

In Braz e Silva-Ferreira-Villamizar-Roa
[
3
, Lemma 2.1]
the authors proved the following dispersive estimate in Lorentz spaces: if
1\leq q\leq+\infty
,
2<p<\infty
and
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
, then

\|S(t)f\|_{L^{p,q}}\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{N}{2}(\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{p})}\|f\|_{L^{p^{\prime},q}},\quad\textrm{for}\quad t\neq 0.

(5.11)

Simple computations imply that
|x|^{-b}\in L^{N/b,\infty}
, with
0<b<N
, which implies, from the inequality (
5.10
), that the multiplication operator
|\cdot|^{-b}:L^{p,q}\to L^{p_{1},q_{1}}
is bounded for

,

. Now, since
\sigma^{\prime}_{b}<2<\sigma_{b}
and setting
1/q=1/\sigma_{b}-b/N\sigma_{b}<1/2
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\||\cdot|^{-b/\sigma_{b}}S(t)\left[|\cdot|^{-b}|f|^{\sigma_{b}-1}\right]\right\|_{L^{\sigma_{b},\sigma_{b}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|S(t)[|\cdot|^{-b}|f|^{\sigma_{b}-1}]\|_{L^{q,\sigma_{b}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{N}{2}(\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{q})}\||\cdot|^{-b/\sigma_{b}}[|\cdot|^{-b+b/\sigma_{b}}|f|^{\sigma_{b}-1}]\|_{L^{q^{\prime},\sigma_{b}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{N}{2}(\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{q})}\left\||\cdot|^{-b+b/\sigma_{b}}|f|^{\sigma_{b}-1}\right\|_{L^{\sigma_{b}^{\prime},\sigma_{b}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{N}{2}(\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{q})}\left\||\cdot|^{-b/\sigma_{b}}f\right\|_{L^{\sigma_{b}^{\prime}(\sigma_{b}-1),\sigma_{b}^{\prime}(\sigma_{b}-1)}}^{\sigma_{b}-1}

\displaystyle\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{N}{2}(\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{q})}\left\||\cdot|^{-b/\sigma_{b}}f\right\|_{L^{\sigma_{b}}}^{\sigma_{b}-1}.

(5.12)

Finally

\displaystyle\frac{N}{2}\left(\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{q}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{2}\left(1-\frac{2}{q}\right)=\frac{N}{2}\left(1-\frac{2(N-b)}{N\sigma_{b}}\right)=\frac{2}{\sigma_{b}},

(5.13)

which concludes the proof.
∎

and

Proof.

Holder’s inequality yields

\displaystyle\left\||\cdot|^{-b+b/\sigma_{b}}|u|^{\sigma_{b}-2}v\right\|_{L^{\sigma_{b}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\left\||\cdot|^{-\frac{b}{\sigma_{b}}(\sigma_{b}-2)}|u|^{\sigma_{b}-2}|\cdot|^{-{b}/{\sigma_{b}}}v\right\|_{L^{\sigma_{b}^{\prime}}}

∎

Lemma 5.4

First, setting

\int_{0}^{T-t}|z-(T-t)|^{-\alpha}\left|\frac{1}{z}\right|^{2(1-\alpha)}e^{-{\ell}/{z}}\,dz\leq c_{1}e^{-{\ell}/{|T-t|}}.

Since

The rest of the proof follows the steps as in the proof of Lemma 6 in Merle

\int_{0}^{1}(1-w)^{-\alpha}w^{-2(1-\alpha)}e^{-\frac{\lambda(1-w)}{tw}}\,dw\leq c_{1}t^{1-\alpha}.

.

On the other hand, for

\displaystyle\int_{1/2}^{1}(1-w)^{-\alpha}w^{-2(1-\alpha)}e^{-\frac{\ell(1-w)}{tw}}\,dw

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{1/2}^{1}(1-w)^{-\alpha}e^{-{\ell(1-w)}/{t}}\,dw

(5.38)

\displaystyle=c\int_{0}^{1/2}s^{-\alpha}e^{-{\ell s}/{t}}\,ds

(5.40)

\displaystyle\leq ct^{1-\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{-\alpha}e^{-{\ell r}}\,dr.

(5.41)

Since
\alpha\in(0,1)
the integral in the last line goes to zero as
\ell\to\infty
and the proof is completed.
∎

Now, we are able to prove the following result

Proposition 5.5
.

For
\ell_{0}>0
large enough, there exist
\varepsilon_{0},K,c,\theta>0
such that for
\varepsilon\in\left(0,\varepsilon_{0}\right]
we have

(i)

\|v_{\varepsilon}(T-\varepsilon_{0})\|_{L_{b}^{\sigma_{b}}}\leq K
;

(ii)

\|v_{\varepsilon}(t)-Q_{T}(t)\|_{L_{b}^{\sigma_{b}}}\leq ce^{-\theta\ell_{0}/(T-t)},\quad\mbox{for all}\quad t\in\left[T-\varepsilon_{0},T-\varepsilon\right]
.

Proof.

Let
c,\theta>0
be as in estimate (
5.36
). From Lemma
5.4
(with
\alpha=2/\sigma_{b}
and
c_{1}=1/4
), there exist
\ell_{0}\gg 1
and
\varepsilon_{0}\ll T/{2}
such that

(c+1)^{\sigma_{b}-1}\|k\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{t}^{T}|t-s|^{-2/\sigma_{b}}\left|\frac{1}{T-s}\right|^{\frac{2(\sigma_{b}-2)}{\sigma_{b}}}e^{-{\theta\ell_{0}}/2|T-s|}\,ds\leq\frac{1}{4}e^{-{\theta\ell_{0}}/2|T-t|},

(5.42)

for all
t\in[T-\varepsilon_{0},T)
.

On the other hand, reducing
\varepsilon_{0}>0
if necessary, for any
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
and for all
t\in[T-\varepsilon_{0},T-\varepsilon]
, simple computations imply that

e^{-{\theta\ell_{0}}/|T-t|}\leq\frac{1}{2}e^{-{\theta\ell_{0}}/2|T-t|}

(5.43)

and

e^{-{\theta\ell_{0}}/2|T-t|}\leq 1/|T-t|^{2/\sigma_{b}}.

(5.44)

Now, let
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
, recalling that
v_{\varepsilon}(T-\varepsilon)=Q_{T}(T-\varepsilon)
, by the continuity of the flow, there exists
\varepsilon_{1}\in\left(\varepsilon,\varepsilon_{0}\right)
such that for all
t\in\left[T-\varepsilon_{1},T-\varepsilon\right]
we have

\|v_{\varepsilon}(t)-Q_{T}(t)\|_{L_{b}^{\sigma_{b}}}<e^{-{\theta\ell_{0}}/2(T-t)}.

(5.45)

In view of relation (
5.30
) and inequalities (
5.44
) and (
5.45
), we deduce for all
t\in\left[T-\varepsilon_{1},T-\varepsilon\right]
that

\displaystyle\|v_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L_{b}^{\sigma_{b}}}

\displaystyle\leq\|v_{\varepsilon}(t)-Q_{T}(t)\|_{L_{b}^{\sigma_{b}}}+\|Q_{T}(t)\|_{L_{b}^{\sigma_{b}}}

(5.46)

\displaystyle<e^{-{\theta\ell_{0}}/2(T-t)}+c/|T-t|^{2/\sigma_{b}}<(c+1)/|T-t|^{2/\sigma_{b}}.

(5.47)

Let
\bar{\varepsilon}\in(\varepsilon_{1},T/2)
such that
T-\bar{\varepsilon}
is the first time where

\|v_{\varepsilon}(T-\bar{\varepsilon})-Q_{T}(T-\bar{\varepsilon})\|_{L_{b}^{\sigma_{b}}}=e^{-\theta\ell_{0}/2\bar{\varepsilon}}.

(5.48)

If there is no such
\bar{\varepsilon}>0
, then (
5.45
) holds for all
t\in[T-\varepsilon_{0},T-\varepsilon]
and estimate
(ii)
holds. Let us prove the same conclusion in the other case. Indeed, we claim that
\bar{\varepsilon}\geq\varepsilon_{0}
. If
\bar{\varepsilon}<\varepsilon_{0}
, by definition of
\bar{\varepsilon}
the inequality (
5.45
) holds for all
t\in(T-\bar{\varepsilon},T-\varepsilon]
and therefore estimate (
5.47
) holds in the same time interval. Thus, increasing
\ell_{0}>0
if necessary such that
c\|k\|_{L^{\infty}}\ell_{0}^{-2}<1
, we can use (
5.36
) to deduce

\displaystyle\|v_{\varepsilon}(T-\bar{\varepsilon})-Q_{T}(T-\bar{\varepsilon})\|_{L_{b}^{\sigma_{b}}}

\displaystyle\leq(c+1)^{\sigma_{b}-1}\|k\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{T-\bar{\varepsilon}}^{T-\varepsilon}|(T-\bar{\varepsilon})-s|^{-2/\sigma_{b}}\left|\frac{1}{T-s}\right|^{\frac{2\left(\sigma_{b}-2\right)}{\sigma_{b}}}

(5.49)

\displaystyle\quad\times\left\|v_{\varepsilon}(s)-Q_{T}(s)\right\|_{L_{b}^{\sigma_{b}}}\,ds+e^{-{\theta\ell_{0}}/{\bar{\varepsilon}}}

(5.50)

\displaystyle\leq(c+1)^{\sigma_{b}-1}\|k\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{T-\bar{\varepsilon}}^{T-\varepsilon}|(T-\bar{\varepsilon})-s|^{-2/\sigma_{b}}\left|\frac{1}{T-s}\right|^{\frac{2\left(\sigma_{b}-2\right)}{\sigma_{b}}}

(5.51)

\displaystyle\quad\times e^{-{\theta\ell_{0}}/2(T-s)}ds+e^{-{\theta\ell_{0}}/{\bar{\varepsilon}}}

(5.52)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}e^{-{\theta\ell_{0}}/2\bar{\varepsilon}}+\frac{1}{2}e^{-{\theta\ell_{0}}/2\bar{\varepsilon}}<e^{-{\theta\ell_{0}}/2\bar{\varepsilon}},

(5.53)

where in the last line we have used estimates (
5.42
) and (
5.43
), obtaining a contradiction with (
5.48
). This completes the proof of
(ii)
, which also implies, in view of (
5.47
), that

\|v_{\varepsilon}(T-\varepsilon_{0})\|_{L_{b}^{\sigma_{b}}}\leq(c+1)/\varepsilon_{0}^{2/\sigma_{b}},\quad\mbox{for all}\quad\varepsilon\in\left(0,\varepsilon_{0}\right],

finishing the proof.
∎

Next, we obtain the initial datum for the minimal mass blow-up solution we want to construct.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\left\{v_{1/n}\left(T-\varepsilon_{0}\right)\right\}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
given by Proposition
5.5
. Then there exists
u_{0}\in H^{1}
such that
v_{1/n}(T-\varepsilon_{0})\rightarrow u_{0}
, as
n\rightarrow\infty
, strong in
L_{b}^{\sigma_{b}}
.

Proof.

Since
v_{\varepsilon}(T-\varepsilon)=Q_{T}(T-\varepsilon)
, by mass conservation, we first have

\|v_{\varepsilon}(T-\varepsilon_{0})\|_{L^{2}}=\|v_{\varepsilon}(T-\varepsilon)\|_{L^{2}}=\|Q_{T}(T-\varepsilon)\|_{L^{2}}=\|Q_{k(0)}\|_{L^{2}}.

On the other hand, by energy conservation, we also have

\displaystyle\|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}(T-\varepsilon_{0})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=2E[v_{\varepsilon}(T-\varepsilon_{0})]+\frac{2}{\sigma_{b}}\int k(x)|x|^{-b}\left|v_{\varepsilon}(T-\varepsilon_{0})\right|^{\sigma_{b}}\,dx

(5.54)

\displaystyle=2E\left[Q_{T}(T-\varepsilon)\right]++\frac{2}{\sigma_{b}}\int k(x)|x|^{-b}\left|v_{\varepsilon}(T-\varepsilon_{0})\right|^{\sigma_{b}}\,dx

(5.55)

\displaystyle=2E_{k(0)}\left[Q_{T}(T-\varepsilon)\right]+\frac{2}{\sigma_{b}}\int(k(0)-k(x))|x|^{-b}\left|Q_{T}(T-\varepsilon)\right|^{\sigma_{b}}\,dx

(5.56)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2}{\sigma_{b}}\int k(x)|x|^{-b}\left|v_{\varepsilon}(T-\varepsilon_{0})\right|^{\sigma_{b}}\,dx.

(5.57)

In the last two lines, the first term is independent of
\varepsilon>0
, by energy conservation (
2.2
), the second term goes to zero as
\varepsilon\to 0
by Lemma
5.3
-
(ii)
and the last term is bounded by Proposition
5.5
-
(i)
, since
k\in L^{\infty}
. Therefore the set
\left\{v_{1/n}\left(T-\varepsilon_{0}\right)\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
H^{1}
and there exists
u_{0}\in H^{1}
such that
v_{1/n}(T-\varepsilon_{0})\rightharpoonup u_{0}
weakly in
H^{1}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. The result follows from the compact embedding
H^{1}\hookrightarrow L_{b}^{\sigma_{b}}
.
∎

We have all the tools to prove the existence of a minimal mass blow-up solution under the flatness assumption (
1.14
).

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Let
u(t)
be the solution of (
1.1
) with initial datum
u_{0}
obtained in Lemma
5.6
. For any
t\in[T-\varepsilon_{0},T)
,
v_{1/n}(\cdot)
is defined on the time interval
[T-\varepsilon_{0},t]
for large enough
n\in\mathbb{N}
and the
H^{1}
norm is uniformly bound on this time interval. Moreover, since
v_{1/n}(T-\varepsilon_{0})\rightarrow u_{0}
strong in
L_{b}^{\sigma_{b}}
, as
n\rightarrow\infty
, from the well-posedness theory and Proposition
5.5
-
(ii)

\|u(t)-Q_{T}(t)\|_{L_{b}^{\sigma_{b}}}\leq ce^{-\theta\ell_{0}/(T-t)},\quad\mbox{for all}\quad t\in[T-\varepsilon_{0},T).

This implies, for all
R>0
, that

\|u(t)\|_{L_{b}^{\sigma_{b}}(|x|<R)}\to\infty\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|u(t)\|_{L_{b}^{\sigma_{b}}(|x|\geq R)}\to 0,\quad\mbox{as}\quad t\to T,

(5.58)

since
Q_{T}
has the same behavior by direct computation (see the proof of Lemma
5.3
). We claim that

\int k(x)|x|^{-b}|u(t)|^{\sigma_{b}}\,dx\to\infty,\quad\mbox{as}\quad t\to T.

(5.59)

Indeed, in view of assumption (
1.14
), we have

\int k(x)|x|^{-b}|u(t)|^{\sigma_{b}}\,dx=k(0)\|u(t)\|_{L_{b}^{\sigma_{b}}(|x|<\ell_{0})}+\int_{|x|\geq\ell_{0}}k(x)|x|^{-b}|u(t)|^{\sigma_{b}}\,dx

and the limits (
5.58
) imply (
5.59
), since
k\in L^{\infty}
. Now, applying the energy conservation (
1.6
), we deduce

\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\to\infty,\quad\mbox{as}\quad t\to T,

and
u(t)
is a blow-up solution. Moreover since
v_{1/n}(T-\varepsilon_{0})\rightharpoonup u_{0}
weakly in
H^{1}
we have, by mass conservation, that

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n}\|v_{1/n}(T-\varepsilon_{0})\|_{L^{2}}=\|Q_{k(0)}\|_{L^{2}}.

(5.60)

Therefore, we must have
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}=\|Q_{k(0)}\|_{L^{2}}
, otherwise
u(t)
is a global solution in
H^{1}
by Theorem
1.1
, concluding the proof when
\ell_{0}>0
is large enough.

For general
\ell_{0}>0
, we proceed as follows. Given
\lambda>0
define

k_{\lambda}(x)=k(x/\lambda),

then
k_{\lambda}(x)=k(0)
, for all
|x|<\lambda\ell_{0}
and taking
\lambda>0
sufficiently large, from the previous case for a fixed
T>0
, there exists a minimal mass blow-up solution
u^{\lambda}(t)
defined on the time interval
[0,\lambda^{2}T)
, solution of the equation

i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u+k_{\lambda}(x)|u|^{\frac{4-2b}{N}}u=0

Let

u(x,t)=\lambda^{N/2}u^{\lambda}(\lambda x,\lambda^{2}t).

One can easily check that it is a minimal mass solution of the (
1.1
) equation that blows-up in finite time
T>0
.
∎

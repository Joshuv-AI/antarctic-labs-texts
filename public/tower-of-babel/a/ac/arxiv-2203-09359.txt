Borel factors and embeddings of systems in subshifts
Nishant Chandgotia and Spencer Unger

Abstract
. In this paper we study the combinatorics of free Borel actions of the group ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} on Polish spaces. Building upon recent work by Chandgotia and Meyerovitch, we introduce property F F on ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} -shift spaces X X under which there is an equivariant map from any free Borel action to the free part of X X . Under further entropic assumptions, we prove that any subshift Y Y (modulo the periodic points) can be Borel embedded into X X . Several examples satisfy property F F including, but not limited to, the space of proper 3 3 -colourings, tilings by rectangles (under a natural arithmetic condition), proper 2 ​ d 2d -edge colourings of ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} and the space of bi-infinite Hamiltonian paths. This answers questions raised by Seward, and Gao-Jackson, and recovers a result by Weilacher and some results announced by Gao-Jackson-Krohne-Seward.

1.
Introduction

In this paper we focus on the following question: Under what hypothesis can
subshifts model all free
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
dynamical systems? While there are numerous
contexts in which this question can be placed, we will work with free Borel

\mathbb{Z}^{d}
systems on Polish spaces. Our inspiration comes from two recent groundbreaking results by Hochman:
In his first paper
[
Hoc13
]
, Hochman showed that barring a

universally null set
, that is, sets which have measure zero for all
invariant probability measures, any Borel
\mathbb{Z}
system (with small enough
entropy) can be equivariantly embedded into mixing shifts of finite type. It
remained open to extend the embedding to “dark matter”, a term coined by Mike
Boyle, referring to part of the space which does not have any invariant
probability measures. For many interesting examples “dark matter” can
constitute the entire space. This was addressed in a later paper by Hochman

[
Hoc19
]
where he showed that that such an embedding exists even without
leaving out a universally null set. Recently, in a private communication, Seward
and Hochman mentioned that they can construct an embedding of this “dark matter” into the full shift for
countable group actions.

Fix
d\geq 2
. Our study begins with a recent paper
[
CM21
]

where the authors defined a property called
flexibility
: If a dynamical
system is flexible, the authors proved that free
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
systems can be
embedded in it barring a universally null set under natural entropic
restrictions. Numerous dynamical systems like the space of proper
3
-colourings
and domino tilings (and many non symbolic examples) possess this property. In
this paper we consider a similar condition on subshifts called property
F
(Definition
7.1
) which helps us prove similar results in the
context of Borel dynamics. For systems with property
F
we showed the following
results.

(1)

All free Borel
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
systems admit equivariant Borel maps to the free
part of a system with property
F
. (Theorem
7.2
)

(2)

All subshifts (under suitable restrictions on the
entropy) can be Borel embedded in a subshift with property
F
. (Theorem

9.1
)

In this paper, we prove that many subshifts have property
F
:

(1)

Hom-shifts: The space of graph homomorphisms from
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
to a fixed undirected graph with an odd cycle. This covers many interesting examples like the space of proper
3
-colourings. (Section
10
).

Gao and Jackson raised the following question in

[
GJ15
]
: What is the Borel chromatic number of the free part of
the full shift? Recently it has been announced in
[
GJKS18
]
that
Borel chromatic number is less than equal to
3
. Our results confirm this
announcement. In contrast it has been shown in
[
GJKS18
]
that the
continuous chromatic number of the free part of the full shift is
4
.

(2)

Rectangular tiling shifts: Tilings of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
by boxes which
satisfy a natural necessary and sufficient coprimality condition. This covers
examples like that of domino tilings (also known as perfect matchings). (Section

11
)

It was a question by Gao and Jackson in
[
GJ15
]
and
through personal communication by Brandon Seward whether this condition was
sufficient. The resolution of this question in the case of domino tilings for

\mathbb{Z}^{2}
actions was mentioned to us over personal communication by Brandon Seward
and is going to be part of their upcoming publication
[
GJKS
]
. In
contrast it has been shown that the free part of the full shift does not have
any continuous perfect matching
[
GJKS18
]
.

(3)

The space of
directed bi-infinite Hamiltonian paths on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. (Section
12
)

This is an important example which does not precisely fall in the category
of subshifts (since it is not compact). Yet our techniques extend to this with
small modifications. It recovers, partially, a weak version of Dye’s theorem

[
Dye59
]
which states that any free
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
action is orbit
equivalent to a
\mathbb{Z}
-action (barring a null set of an invariant ergodic
probability measure). Our result proves the existence of such an orbit
equivalence where a null set need not be removed. In addition the orbit
equivalence maps the generators of the
\mathbb{Z}
action to generators of the
\mathbb{Z}^{d}

action. This result for
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
actions was also mentioned to us by Brandon
Seward over personal communication and that it will be part of their upcoming
publication
[
GJKS
]
. A similar orbit equivalence was obtained in

[
DOWZ21
]
for
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
dynamical systems barring a set of
measure zero for a given free ergodic probability measure.

(4)

The space of colourings of edges of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for
d\geq 2
by
k

colours for
k\geq 2d
. (Section
13
)

It was asked in
[
GJKS18
]
to determine the Borel edge-chromatic
number of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
actions. Our theorem shows that the answer is
2d
for
\mathbb{Z}^{d}

actions. In contrast
[
GJKS18
]
shows that the continuous
chromatic number of the free part of the full shift on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
is
5
. This
recovers results by Weilacher in
[
Wei21
, Theorem 2]
. Our
result on edge colorings is also a consequence of results in

[
BHT21
]
.

1.1.
Selected literature and general context

To put our results in
context, we describe several closely related areas.

The ‘original’ context for such questions comes from Krieger’s generator theorem

[
Kri75
]
. It states that under natural assumptions on the entropy any
free ergodic probability preserving
\mathbb{Z}
system
(X,\mu,T)
can be embedded
into mixing shifts of finite type. In other words mixing shifts of finite type
are ‘universal’. Naturally the question arose, which other systems are
‘universal’? It has been found time and again that at the heart of the matter
are certain topological mixing properties: Quas and Soo proved universality under ‘specification’ and some technical
conditions
[
QS16
]
. These technical conditions were loosened by Weiss
and were finally gotten rid of by Burguet
[
Bur20
]
.
‘Universality’ under similar mixing conditions can also be found for
\mathbb{Z}^{d}

symbolic systems where
d>2
, see Şahin and Robinson

[
RŞ01
]
and Pavlov
[
Pav14
]
. In

[
CM21
]
, Chandgotia and Meyerovitch considered very general
conditions which imply “almost Borel universality” for various systems which
don’t satisfy these mixing conditions but still have sufficiently many orbit
segments which can be ‘glued’ to one another. Further the embeddings there are
modulo a universally null set, a set with measure zero with respect to all
invariant probability measures, as opposed to modulo a set of measure zero with
respect to a specific one. One is immediately led to the question: When can we
embed a space which has no invariant probability measure

[
Wei89
]
? In
[
Wei89
]
, Weiss had shown that
such spaces have countable generators (later generalised to countable groups in

[
JKL02
]
). Tserunyan showed that all such countable groups
actions have a
32
set generator under the assumption of local compactness

[
Tse15
]
and for
\mathbb{Z}
actions Hochman showed that these spaces can be embedded
into any mixing shift of finite type
[
Hoc19
]
. This question is rather
delicate and sets the context for our paper: In our results we do not remove
sets which admit no invariant probability measures. While we do not prove the
full universality of these systems we make considerable progress towards it.

An important inspiration for our work also comes from the systematic study of
Borel graphs begun by Kechris, Solecki and Todorčević
[
KST99
]
.
Identify
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with its Cayley graph with standard generators. A free
\mathbb{Z}^{d}

action on a Polish space
X
induces a natural graph structure on
X
which can
be thought as a measurable collection of copies of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. To understand what
this graph is like, we can now try to understand its Borel chromatic number, how can
it be tiled and other similar properties. We refer the reader to some
comprehensive surveys by Kechris and Kechris-Marks for more references and
details
[
Kec
,
KM
]
. Kechris, Solecki and
Todorčević
[
KST99
, Sections 4.8, 4.9]
proved that the Borel chromatic number and the Borel edge chromatic number of the

\mathbb{Z}^{d}
full shift is
2d+1
and
4d-1
respectively and asked what is the
Borel chromatic number is if periodic points were removed.

This was taken up by Gao and Jackson in
[
GJ15
]
where they
proved many results about the free part of the full shift on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
in both the
Borel and the continuous context. Modulo the periodic points, they proved that
the continuous chromatic number is less than and equal to
4
and that it can be
continuously tiled by rectangles of the type
n\times n
,
n\times(n+1)
,

(n+1)\times n
and
(n+1)\times(n+1)
. Moreover they showed that these
results are sharp in the sense that if one were to remove one of the rectangles
or reduce the number of colours, then such continuous maps no longer exist.
Naturally we are lead to the question of the existence of a Borel colouring or
tiling. In
[
GJKS18
]
, Gao, Jackson, Krohne and Seward studied
these problems very deeply in the continuous context and announced that the free
part of the full shift has Borel chromatic number
3
, can be perfectly matched
and asked about the edge chromatic number. Modulo a null set for a given ergodic
measure some of these questions were answered by Şahin
[
Şah09
]
,
Prikhod’ko
[
Pri99
]
, by Şahin and Robinson
[
Rc04
]
and by
Chandgotia and Meyerovitch
[
CM21
]
. Our results
characterise when a given free Borel action of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
can be Borel tiled by a
given set of rectangles, prove that the Borel edge chromatic number is
2d
and
recovers that the Borel chromatic number is at most
3
. The result regarding
the edge chromatic number can be found in
[
Wei21
, Theorem 2]

and is also a consequence of
[
BHT21
]
. Proof sketches for some of
these results can be found in
[
GR21
]
.

The motivation for Gao and Jackson came from a well-known question by Weiss

[
Wei84
]
about hyperfiniteness for actions of countable
amenable groups. A Borel action of a countable group on a Polish space gives
rise to the orbit equivalence relation which is a countable Borel equivalence
relation. Such a relation is called
hyperfinite
if it is an increasing
union of finite Borel equivalence relations. By versions of Rokhlin’s lemma

[
Roh48
,
Kak43
,
KW72
,
OW87
]
, it can be
shown that the equivalence relations arising from the action of amenable groups
on Polish spaces are hyperfinite modulo a universally null set. Weiss asked
whether removing the universally null set is necessary. This was answered
positively for

\mathbb{Z}^{d}
actions by Weiss, for finitely-generated groups of polynomial growth by
Jackson, Kechris, and Louveau
[
JKL02
]
, for abelian groups by
Gao and Jackson
[
GJ15
]
and for locally Nilpotent groups by
Schneider and Seward
[
SS13
]
. Recently this has been
generalised to a large class of groups by Conley, Jackson, Marks, Seward and
Tucker-Drob
[
CJM+20
]
. Given that an action is hyperfinite, one can ask
whether the witness to hyperfiniteness, that is, the finite Borel equivalence
relations approximating them, are ‘nice’ and whether they ‘respect’ the geometry
of the acting group. This can be seen as a generalisation of a marker lemma in
the Borel context and can be found in
[
GJKS15
]
(look at

[
MU17
, Theorem 5.5]
for a proof). The questions about the
Borel chromatic numbers of actions and Borel tilings makes use of these
‘nice’ witnesses to hyperfiniteness.

We remark that the continuous version of our results are especially subtle and
lot remains to be explored. For instance we know that modulo the periodic points
the
2
-full shift and the proper
3
colourings are Borel isomorphic to one
another
[
Hoc19
,
Hoc13
]
but these maps can’t be made continuous

[
Sal21
]
. Continuous embeddings for
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
subshifts is known
under strong mixing conditions
[
Lig03
,
Lig04
]
and there are
indications that it would fail otherwise
[
CM21
, Section
12]
. Results by Gao and Jackson
[
GJ15
]

and Gao-Jackson-Krohne-Seward
[
GJKS18
]
further illustrate the
discrepancy between the Borel and the continuous results.

Finally, in a recent paper Anton Bernshteyn in

[
Ber20
]
has pointed out interesting relations between
distributed computing, local algorithms and questions in descriptive
combinatorics. See also
[
BCG+21
,
GR21
]
.

1.2.
Organisation of the paper and a few words about the proofs

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we state the main results of the paper. In Section
3
, we
give some definitions that will be used throughout. In Section
4
, we give a proof of a theorem of Gao, Jackson,
Krohne and Seward using the Baire category theorem and use this to show that typical constructions done in the context of invariant probability measures
(some consequences of the Rokhlin’s lemma) do not work in the Borel context. In
Section
5
, we modify a different theorem of Gao, Jackson,
Krohne and Seward to suit our combinatorial constructions. This can be thought
of as a weak version of Rokhlin’s lemma where we prove some additional
geometrical properties of the towers/hyperfiniteness witness. In Section

6
, we prove several combinatorial results about
tilings of the hyperfiniteness witness from Section
5
by nice
rectangular boxes. In Section
7
, we introduce an
extension property (Property F) for patterns in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
as well as a version of
this property with markers and apply them to produce Borel maps. In particular,
we show that if a system
(Y,T)
has property
F
, then there is an equivariant
Borel map from any free Borel
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
system to
(Y,T)
.

In Section
8
, we introduce a marker lemma for subshifts with property
F
. This is then used to show in Section
9
that if a shift space
(Y,T)
satisfies the version of Property
F
with markers, then every subshift with low enough entropy admits a Borel embedding into
(Y,T)
. In the remaining sections we prove that the spaces from Theorem
2.1
satisfy Property
F
and give entropy bounds for the spaces in Theorem
2.2
: Hom-shifts in Section
10
, rectangular tiling shifts in Section
11
, directed bi-infinite Hamiltonian paths in Section
12
and proper edge colourings in Section
13
.

2.
Main results

2.1.
Notation

A
Borel
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
system
is a pair
(X,T)
where
X
is a standard
Borel space and
T
is an action of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
by Borel automorphisms. Given a Borel

\mathbb{Z}^{d}
system
(X,T)
we let
\textit{Free}(X)=\{x\in X~:~\mbox{ for all }\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},T^{\gamma}(x)\neq x\}.
The action is called
free
if
\textit{Free}(X)=X
. If
X
is compact and
T
is a
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
action on
X
by homeomorphisms, then the pair
(X,T)
is called a
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
topological dynamical system.

An important action that we will focus on will be
the full-shift

(\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}},\sigma)
where
\mathcal{A}
is a finite set,
\sigma
is the
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
action
on
\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
given by
(\sigma^{\gamma}(x))_{\delta}=x_{\gamma+\delta}.

We give the set
\mathcal{A}
the discrete topology making
\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
compact under the
product topology and the action
\sigma
is by homeomorphisms. In this paper we
will work with subsets
X\subseteq\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
which are invariant under the
shift-action; we will call them
symbolic systems
. If in addition they are compact as well they will be called
subshifts
. Given a set
B\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
we denote the
language
of
X
on the set
B
by
\mathcal{L}(X,B)=\{x|_{B}~:~x\in X\}

and the
language
of
X
by
\mathcal{L}(X)=\cup_{B}\mathcal{L}(X,B)
where
the union is over all finite sets
B
. A
pattern
is an element of

\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}})
while a
configuration
is an element of

\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
.

Elements of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
will typically be written as
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta,\ldots
where for example
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\ldots,\gamma_{d})
. The
standard generators of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
will be denoted by
\epsilon^{i}
, that is,

\epsilon^{i}_{j}=1
if
i=j
and
0
otherwise. The other unit vectors are
denoted by
\epsilon^{i};d<i\leq 2d
where
\epsilon^{i}=-\epsilon^{i-d}
.

2.2.
Examples

Throughout the paper we will work with the undirected Cayley graph of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}

given by the standard generators, that is, the graph with vertex set
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
edges of the form
\{\gamma,\gamma\pm\epsilon^{i}\}
for
i\leq d
.

2.2.1.
Hom-shifts

Let
H
be a finite undirected graph without multiple
edges. We denote by
\textit{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},H)
the collection of graph homomorphisms
(adjacency preserving maps) from
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
to
H
. These are called

hom-shifts
. An important special case is proper
q
-colorings of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
which can be written as
\textit{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},K_{q})
where
K_{q}
is the complete graph.

2.2.2.
Rectangular tiling shifts

A
box
in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is a
product of intervals. Given a finite set of boxes
\mathcal{T}
, a

tiling
of a set
B\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
by
\mathcal{T}
is a function
a
from

B
to
\mathcal{T}
such that for all all
R\in\mathcal{T}
,
a^{-1}(R)
is a
disjoint union of translates of
R
. The space of tilings
X(\mathcal{T})
is
the set of tilings of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
by
\mathcal{T}
. We can assume that
\mathcal{T}

consists of boxes
R
of the form
\prod_{i\leq d}[0,n_{i}^{R})
.

Our results will pertain to the so-called
coprime
rectangular shifts

X(\mathcal{T})
where for all

1\leq i\leq d
,
\gcd(n_{i}^{R};R\in\mathcal{T})=1
. An important rectangular
shift is that
domino tilings
,
X(\mathcal{T})
where
\mathcal{T}=\{\{\bar{0},\epsilon^{i}\}\mid i\leq d\}
where we recall that
\epsilon^{i}
is
the
i^{th}
standard generator of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Domino tilings are sometimes also referred
to as perfect matchings and dimer tilings.

2.2.3.
Directed bi-infinite Hamiltonian paths in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
:

[
DOWZ21
]

Let
X_{bi}
be the set of all functions
x:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\{\epsilon^{1},\dots,\epsilon^{2d}\}
such that the graph on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with directed edges of the form
(\gamma,x_{\gamma}+\gamma)
is a directed bi-infinite path.

2.2.4.
Proper edge colourings in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
:

For
t\in\mathbb{N}
let
E_{t}
be the set of injective functions from
\{\epsilon^{1},\dots,\epsilon^{2d}\}
to the set of colours
\{1,2,\ldots,t\}
. We define
the set of proper
t
-edge colourings of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
as

X^{(t)}=\{x\in(E_{t})^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}~:~\text{ if $\epsilon$ is a unit vector and $\delta\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}$ then $x_{\delta}(\epsilon)=x_{\delta+\epsilon}(-\epsilon)$}\}.

Equivalently we can think of them as colouring on edges such that edges sharing a vertex are mapped to distinct colours. It is obvious that
X^{(t)}
is non-empty if and only if
t\geq 2d
.

2.3.
Main results

Our main results are the following:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
(X,S)
be any of the following systems.

(1)

\textit{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},H)
where
H
is a graph which is not bipartite.

(2)

A coprime rectangular tiling shift.

(3)

The space of bi-infinite Hamiltonian paths.

(4)

The set of proper
k
-edge colourings of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
where
k\geq 2d
and
d>1
.

For all free Borel
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
actions
(Y,T)
there exists an equivariant
Borel map
\phi:(Y,T)\to(\textit{Free}(X),S)
.

For a
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
topological dynamical system
(Y,T)
let
h_{top}(Y,T)
denote the topological entropy of
(Y,T)
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
(X,S)
be any of the following systems.

(1)

\textit{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},H)
where
H
is a graph which is not bipartite.

(2)

The space of domino tilings

(3)

The set of proper
t
-edge colourings of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
where
t\geq 2d
and
d>1

For all shift spaces
(Y,T)
, if
h_{top}(Y,T)<h_{top}(X,S)

.

Remark 1
.

We note that for
d=1
, the space of proper
t
-edge colourings is equivalent to the space of proper
t
-vertex colourings (which is covered by the space of graph homomorphisms). Thus the same results for proper
t
-edge colourings holds for

when
d=1
.

3.
Preliminaries

In this section, we give some definitions which connect the dynamics of our
systems to the finite combinatorics of their Cayley graphs. Let
(X,T)
be a
system. We denote by
G_{T}
its natural Cayley graph structure, meaning, that
the vertex set of
G_{T}
is the space
X
and there is an edge between
x
and

y
in
X
if there is a standard generator
\gamma
of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
such that

T^{\gamma}(x)=y
. We note that the connected components of
G_{T}
are copies
of the usual Cayley graph of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
given by the standard generators. On the
other hand, we note that Borel subsets
B
of
X
often meet every connected
component, so (for example) to define a Borel function on
B
we must work
simultaneously in every connected component.

For a Borel set
B
, we will refer to the connected components of the induced
subgraph of
G_{T}
with vertex set
B
as the connected components of
B
.
Suppose that
P
is a property of finite subsets of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
which is invariant
under translation. Let
C\subseteq X
be contained in the orbit of a point
x\in X
. We will say that
C
has property
P
if
\{\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\mid T^{\gamma}(x)\in C\}
has property
P
. For instance if
T
is a free
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
action on
X
then
T^{[-m,m]^{d}}(x)
is a cube for all
x\in X
.

For a Borel set
B\subseteq X
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
F\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we write

T^{F}(B)=\cup_{\gamma\in F}T^{\gamma}(B)\text{ and }\partial^{m}B=T^{[-m,m]^{d}}(B)\setminus B;

where the latter is the
m
-external boundary of
B
. We write

\partial B
for
\partial^{1}B
. For
i\leq d
, we define
\partial_{i}B
to be
the inner boundary in the direction
i
, that is, the set of
x\in B
such that either
T^{\epsilon^{i}}(x)\notin B
or
T^{-\epsilon^{i}}(x)\notin B
. Similarly

\partial_{i}^{+}(B)
is the set of
x\in B
such that
T^{\epsilon^{i}}(x)\notin B
while
\partial_{i}^{-}(B)
is the set of
x\in B
such that
T^{-\epsilon^{i}}(x)\notin B
. All these sets
are Borel. We will use similar definitions for
B\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

Definition 3.1
.

A set
B\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is a called a

k
-grid union
if there exists
C\subseteq k\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
such that

4.
Is there a simple counterpart to Rokhlin’s lemma in Borel dynamics?

In this section, we give simple topological proofs of some theorems by Gao,
Jackson, Krohne and Seward from
[
GJKS15
]
and use them to explain
why the techniques of ergodic theory are not enough for the Borel context of our
paper.

We recall Rokhlin’s lemma which is the cornerstone of many constructions in
ergodic theory.

Theorem 4.1

(Rokhlin’s lemma
[
Roh48
,
Kak43
]
for
\mathbb{Z}

actions and
[
KW72
]
for
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
actions)
.

Consider a free measure preserving action of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
on a
probability space
(X,\mu)
. For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\epsilon>0
, there
exists a Borel set
A\subseteq X
such that
\mu(A)>1-\epsilon
and the connected
components of
A
are boxes with side length
n
.

Often in ergodic theory (for instance in many proofs of the Krieger’s generator
theorem
[
Kri75
]
), given a topological space
Y
, a function
f:X\to Y
is constructed in the following fashion. A sequence of constants
\epsilon_{n}

are chosen such that
\epsilon_{n}\to 0
and sets
A_{n}
are chosen by Rokhlin’s
Lemma (the connected components of
A_{n}
are boxes with side length
k_{n}
large
enough). Now for each
n
, a function
f_{n}:A_{n}\to Y
is constructed and the
hope is that the sequence
f_{n}
converges to a function
f
. To achieve this,

f_{n+1}
is constructed using
f_{n}
by modifying it on a small set
B_{n}
and
giving an appropriate definition to
f_{n+1}
on
A_{n+1}\setminus A_{n}
. The
connected components of
B_{n}
contain boxes of larger and larger sizes but they satisfy a summability condition:
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mu(B_{n})<\infty
. This allows an application of the Borel-Cantelli lemma and ensures that
\mu
-almost every
x\in X
lies in at most finitely many
B_{n}
. In particular, there are major modifications only finitely many times in the definition of the value of function at
\mu
-almost every point in
X
. This implies the required convergence.

Gao, Jackson, Krohne and Seward
[
GJKS15
]
have shown that under
very mild conditions this is not possible in the Borel setting. We will give a
simple proof for this result below.

Let
T
be an action of a countable group
\Gamma
on a compact space
X
by
homeomorphisms. We say that
(X,T)
is
minimal
if each orbit of the
action is dense. Equivalently, for all open sets
U
there is a finite set

F\subseteq\Gamma
such that
T^{F}(U)=X
. A
complete section
is a
measurable set which meets all the orbits of the action.

Theorem 4.2
.

[
GJKS15
, Theorem 3.2]
Let
(X,T)
be a free minimal action of a countable group
\Gamma
.
Let
B_{n}\subseteq X
be a sequence of Borel sets such that for all finite
F\subseteq\Gamma
and for all sufficiently large
n
, the set
\{x\in X\mid T^{\gamma}(x)\in B_{n}
for all
\gamma\in F\}
is a complete section of a
comeager set. Then the set
\{x\in X~:~x\mbox{ belongs to }B_{n}\mbox{ for
infinitely many }n\}
is comeager.

Remark 2
.

We make a few remarks:

(1)

Gao, Jackson, Krohne and Seward remark that such points can be found in
the free part of the full shift
(\mbox{Free}(\{0,1\}^{\Gamma}),\sigma)
. This
uses an addional theorem by Gao, Jackson and Seward

[
GJS09
]
that there are minimal subsystems contained in

(\mbox{Free}(\{0,1\}^{\Gamma},\sigma)

[

for an elementary proof of this fact using the

(2)

In
[
Pri99
]
Prikhod​
´

[

]

is an infinite sequence of rectangles which are coprime then for any ergodic measure preserving

there is a Borel partition of a set of measure one:

such that the shape of the connected components of the set

are

are complete sections for a set of measure

if the minimum side length of

A key fact that we will use in the proof is that if
B\subseteq X
is a complete
section of a comeager set, then it cannot be meager. If it were meager it would
contradict Baire’s theorem since it is a section of the set
C=\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}T^{\gamma}(B)
and the set
X\setminus C
is meager. In
addition, we will use a couple of standard facts: Borel sets
A
have the Baire
property, that is, there is an open set such that its symmetric difference with

A
is meager. Secondly if
A
is a set with the Baire property and for all open sets

is not meager then

is comeager.

Proof.

Let
A=\{x\in X~:~x\mbox{ belongs to }B_{n}\mbox{ for infinitely
many }n\}
. Given a finite set
F\subseteq\Gamma
we write

B_{n,F}=\{x\in X~:~T^{\gamma^{-1}}(x)\in B_{n}\mbox{ for all }\gamma\in F\}.

By assumption, we have that the set
B_{n,F}
is a complete section of a
comeager set for large enough

and hence cannot be meager.

Let

be an open set. Choose a finite set
F\subseteq\Gamma

such that
\bigcup_{\gamma\in F}T^{\gamma}(U)=X
. This implies that

\bigcup_{\gamma\in F}T^{\gamma}(U)\cap B_{n,F}=B_{n,F}.

Hence there is
\gamma\in F
such that
T^{\gamma}(U)\cap B_{n,F}
is
not a meager set. But if
x\in T^{\gamma}(U)\cap B_{n,F}
then
T^{\gamma^{-1}}(x)\in U\cap B_{n}
.

Thereby we have that

T^{\gamma^{-1}}\left(T^{\gamma}(U)\cap B_{n,F}\right)\subseteq U\cap B_{n}

is not meager in
U
for all sufficiently large

.

It follows that for all

is not meager. Since this is true for an arbitrary open set

and the sets

have the Baire property we have that the set

\bigcup_{n\geq N}B_{n}
is comeager. Finally we have that the set

is comeager. This completes the proof.
∎

To apply these results in Section

we will need some special properties of the tilings. To state these properties we need some more definitions. The
faces
of a box

are the sets
\partial_{i}^{+}(B)
and

\partial_{i}^{-}(B)
for

. We say that two disjoint boxes

and

if are vectors
\alpha\in A
and
\beta\in B
such that their
difference is a standard generator of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Given two disjoint boxes we say
that they meet

if one has a face which is a translate of a face of the other by a standard generator. A tiling

of a set

is called

if all boxes in

which meet, meet face to face.

Given two disjoint boxes

and

we say that they meet

if they have faces

and

such that there is a standard generator

\epsilon
for which

has cardinality at least

fraction of the minimum of the volume of

and
B^{\prime}
. Given a tiling, we
regard the boxes in the tiling as vertices of a graph which are connected by an
edge if they meet almost face to face. We say that a tiling is
almost face
to face
if the associated graph is connected. We call this graph the
almost face to face graph
.

and

For this proof we will trace the proof of Proposition

7.
Property F

In this section, we isolate a mixing condition called property

that any free Borel
\mathbb{Z}^{d}

family of patterns
is a

(P_{C}\subseteq\mathcal{L}(X,C);\text{$C$ is a $k$-grid union})
such
that

Definition 7.1
.

A family of patterns
(P_{C};\text{$C$ is a $k$-grid union})

(1)

-grid unions

,

(2)

-grid unions

-grid union

8.
Markers

The entropy of the collection
\mathcal{C}=(C_{B};B\text{ is a
$k$-grid union})
is defined by

h(\mathcal{C})=\liminf\frac{1}{(nk)^{d}}\log{|C_{[1,nk]^{d}}|}.

The limit exists under the assumption of property
F
(but we will not need this fact) and is
non-zero (except in the trivial case of each
C_{B}
being a singleton). The proof
follows from the proof of
[
CM21
, Lemma 5.3]
.

We say that patterns
a,b\in\mathcal{L}(X)
clash if there is a site

\gamma
such that
a_{\gamma}\neq b_{\gamma}
. A collection
\mathcal{M}=(M_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{L}(X,[1,nk]^{d});n\in\mathbb{N})
is said to have
the marker property
with tolerance
r
if for all
a,b\in M_{n}
and
|\gamma|<nk-r
, we have that
\sigma^{\gamma}(a)
clashes with
b
unless
\gamma=\bar{0}
and
a=b
.

We say that the collection
\mathcal{M}
has
the marker property
F
if it
has the marker property witnessed by
M_{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and there
exists a collection
\mathcal{C}=(C_{B};B\text{ is a $k$-grid union})
with
property
F
such that
M_{n}\subseteq C_{[1,nk]^{d}}\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Theorem 8.1
.

If
X
is a shift space which has a collection
\mathcal{C}
with
property
F
then it is has a collection
\mathcal{M}
with marker property
F

and
h(\mathcal{C})=h(\mathcal{M})
.

We will follow the strategy as in
[
CM21
]
. The situation is much simpler here because we are dealing with shift spaces, property
F
is much stronger than flexibility (being used in that context) and the dimension
d\geq 2
. Marker constructions have a long history in ergodic theory and similar ideas can be found throughout the literature improvised according to context.

We will need the following lemma.

Lemma 8.2

(No small periods)
.

Let
X
be a shift space,
\mathcal{C}=(C_{n};n\in\mathbb{N})
where
C_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{L}(X,[1,nk]^{d})
be a collection with entropy

. Let

. There exists a collection
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=(C_{n}^{\prime};n\in\mathbb{N})

and for all

and

.

This lemma is very similar to

essentially the same proof. However, we cannot use the statement directly, so we
repeat the argument.

Let

The elements of

are periodic with period

agree on the set

.

Hence there is an injection from

to

Thus the collection

12.4.
The compactness criterion

Suppose that
x
is a configuration such that for an increasing union of

100k
-grid unions
B_{i}
we have that
x|_{B_{i}}\in P_{B_{i}}
for all
i
. To complete the proof of Property
F
we need to show that
x
is bi-infinite directed Hamiltonian path. For this it is enough to prove that for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
there is a directed path along
x
which goes from
\alpha
to
\beta
or vice versa and that every
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
has a successor and the predecessor. For this, take
i
large enough such that
\alpha,\beta\in B_{i}
. Then immediately we have that there is a directed path along
x
of length less than or equal to
|B_{i}|
connecting the two. Finally take
i^{\prime}
large enough such that
\gamma
and its neighbours are in
B_{i^{\prime}}
. Then we have by construction that
\gamma
must have both a predecessor and a successor in
B_{i^{\prime}}
.

This completes the proof.

13.
Proper edge colourings have property
F

Recall that
E_{t}
denotes the set of injective maps from the set of unit vectors to the set of colours
\{1,2,\ldots,t\}
and
X^{(t)}
denotes the space of proper
t
-edge colourings of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. We can think of the set of proper
t
-colourings both as maps from edges of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
to the set of colours and as maps from
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
to
E_{t}
. We will use both these (equivalent) perspectives interchangeably.

Theorem 13.1
.

Let
t\geq 2d
. The space of proper
t
-colourings
X^{(t)}

has property
F
for a sequence of patterns with entropy equal to the
topological entropy of the
X^{(t)}
.

Remark 4
.

This shows that Borel edge chromatic number of any free Borel
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
action is
2d
.

Proof.

Recall that given a set
C\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
we defined
\partial_{i}^{+}(C)
as the set of
\alpha\in C
such that
\alpha+\epsilon^{i}\notin C
while
\partial_{i}^{-}(C)
was defined as the set of
\alpha\in C
such that
\alpha-\epsilon^{i}\notin C
.
Fix
t\geq 2d
. Let
C\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be connected. For
d<i\leq 2d
let
\epsilon^{i}=-\epsilon^{i-d}
. Let

\displaystyle P_{C}=\left\{a\in\mathcal{L}(X^{(t)},C)~:~\text{ the outward edges normal to the faces $\partial_{i}^{+}(C)$ and $\partial_{i}^{-}(C)$ are coloured $i$ in $a$}\right\}.

This boundary condition is amenable to certain natural reflections which we will describe next.

Let
a
be a proper
t
-edge colouring of a box
B
and let
F=\partial_{i}^{+}(B)
. Then we can reflect
a
along
F+1/2\epsilon^{i}
to get a proper
t
-edge colouring of the union of the box
B
and its reflection along
F
. This symmetry is remarkably useful and very similar to the symmetry available to us from graph homomorphisms. In particular it allows, as in
[
CM21
, Section 9]
, application of “reflection positivity” which gives us that the entropy of these patterns is equal to the entropy of the subshift. Since it is essentially the same argument, we do not repeat it here. We are left to prove property
F
for these patterns. For this we will use Proposition
6.4
in a crucial manner.

We fix
N,k=10
. Let
C,C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{r}
be
Nk
-grid unions with offsets
\bar{0},\alpha^{1},\alpha^{2},\ldots,\alpha^{r}\in[1,nk]^{d}
such that
C_{1}+[-100,100]^{d}
,
C_{2}+[-100,100]^{d}
, …,
C_{r}+[-100,100]^{d}
are disjoint and contained in
C
. By Proposition
6.4
, we know that

C\setminus\cup_{i=1}^{r}C_{i}

can be tiled by almost
100
-boxes, that is, boxes all of whose side lengths are at least
100
and at most one of them is different from
100
. Thus to complete the proof we need to show that
P_{B}
is non-empty for all almost
100
-boxes
B
. If all the sides of
B
are even then the construction is easy: The edges parallel to the direction
\epsilon^{i}
are coloured
i
and
i+d
alternatively. This would ensure that the outward edges normal to
\partial_{i}^{+}(B)
and
\partial_{i}^{-}(B)
are coloured
i
.

Now suppose (without the loss of generality) that the side in the direction
\epsilon^{1}
is odd and the rest are even. The edges in the directions
\epsilon^{i}
are coloured
i
and
i+d
alternately for
i\geq 3
. The edges in the direction
\epsilon^{1}
are coloured (from smaller to larger first coordinate value)

1
,
2+d
,
1+d
followed by
1
and
1+d
alternatively. Order the lines in the
\epsilon^{2}
direction according the value of the first coordinate on the line.
The ones with the smallest value of the first coordinate are coloured
2
and

1+d
alternatively. The lines with the next highest value of the first
coordinate are coloured
2
and
1
alternatively. The rest of the lines are
labelled
2
and
2+d
alternatively. It completes the required colouring and hence the
proof. ∎

14.
Discussions and Open questions

14.1.
From results in ergodic theory to the Borel setting

We expand here on a comment and question by Anton Bernsthyn. It is a natural question to ask if results regarding embeddings and factorings that have been proved in the presence of an ergodic measure can be automatically upgraded to the Borel setting. In other words, in ergodic theory the following situation often arises. Given the action of the group
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
on a standard probability space
(X,\mu)
, there is a map from
X
to a finite set
\{1,2,\ldots,l\}
such that the image of the equivariant induced map to
\{1,2,\ldots,l\}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
satisfies certain local constraints
\mu
almost everywhere. The question is whether a probability measure is really required in this context. If we take the alphabet to be infinite then the answer is no due to Theorem
4.2
(look at Remark
2
).

14.2.
From the Borel to the continuous setting

It will be exciting to know more about the continuous analogue of our results. We mentioned many related results in the introduction (Section
1
) and direct the reader there for the references. Here we will focus on the main questions and subtleties in the subject.

It is clear that our techniques do not automatically carry forward to the continuous settings. Theorem 2.10 of
[
GJKS18
]
showed that a continuous (clopen) version of Theorem
5.2
fails for the free part of the full shift
2^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
.

Since Theorem
5.2
is the mechanism for constructing the Borel
maps in our paper, Theorem 2.10 of
[
GJKS18
]
shows that there is
no straightforward modification of our methods to give continuous maps. In the
case of colorings, Corollary 4.4 of
[
GJKS18
]
is equivalent to
the statement that there is no continuous factor map from the free part of

2^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
to the space of proper
3
-colorings. So our result that there is a
Borel such map cannot be improved to a continuous one. Further interesting
conditions were found in
[
GJKS18
]
(given by the twelve tiles
theorem) which give equivalent formulations of whether the free part of

2^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
can be mapped continuously and equivariantly to a given shift of
finite type. These equivalent formulations may be difficult to check. For
instance, it would be nice to have an answer to the following question: When can
the free part of the full shift be tiled continuously by a given set of
rectangles? From results by Gao-Jackson-Krohne-Seward we know that there is no
continuous perfect matching (domino tiling) of the free part of the full shift.
This and the fact that there is no continuous
3
-colouring are related to the
fact that perfect matchings have a non-trivial cohomology while the full shift
doesn’t
[
Sch95
]
) On the other hand, for
k>0
there are
continuous tilings by rectangles of the form
n\times n
,
n\times(n+1)
,

(n+1)\times n
and
(n+1)\times(n+1)
.

Problems about continuous embeddings appear to be subtle. Results by Lightwood

[
Lig03
,
Lig04
]
say that the strong mixing condition are enough when
we talk about embeddings of subshifts without periodic points. On the other hand
results by Salo
[
Sal21
]
indicate that embeddings of the free
part of subshifts can be naturally extended to an embedding of the subshifts in
certain cases. This is very problematic because little is known about continuous embeddings between
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-subshifts for
d\geq 2
. For
d=1
, a necessary and sufficient condition for finding embeddings into a shift of finite type is given by Krieger’s embedding theorem
[
Kri82
]
.

14.3.
Full universality

One of our main results says that if
Y
is a subshift with a collection
\mathcal{C}
which has property
F
and entropy
h(\mathcal{C})
then the free part of any subshift with entropy lower than
h(\mathcal{C})
can be Borel embedded in it (Theorem
9.1
). On the other hand, results in
[
CM21
]
give Borel embeddings of any system modulo a universally null set into systems with a property similar to property
F
called flexibility.

The Gurevic entropy of a Borel
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
system
(X,T)
is given by the supremum of the Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy of the invariant probability measures on the space.

We conjecture that if
(Y,S)
is a subshift which has a collection
\mathcal{C}
with property
F
and entropy
h(\mathcal{C})
and
(X,T)
is any free Borel
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
system whose Gurevic entropy is strictly smaller that
h(\mathcal{C})
then
(X,T)
can be embedded into
(Y,S)
. For mixing shifts of finite type in one dimension (which are automatically strongly irreducible subshifts) this is known due to Hochman
[
Hoc13
,
Hoc19
]
. Along with some arguments by Tom Meyerovitch, this conjecture follows from an upcoming result by Mike Hochman and Brandon Seward.

14.4.
Property
F
for the non-symbolic setting

In
[
CM21
]
a property called flexibility was defined for
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
topological dynamical systems and it was shown (modulo a universally null set) that under appropriate entropy constraints any Borel
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
system can be embedded into it. We could not come with an analogue of flexibility for general
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
topological dynamical systems. In particular we would like to know whether the following is true. Let
(X,T)
be a
\mathbb{Z}
topological dynamical systems with a strong mixing property like non-uniform specification (look at
[
CM21
]
for the definition). Does there exist an equivariant map from the free part of any Borel
\mathbb{Z}
system to the free part of
(X,T)
? By
[
CM21
]
the answer is yes modulo sets of universal measure zero.

One-shot Machine Teaching: Cost Very Few Examples to Converge Faster Journal: Artificial Intelligence
Chen Zhang, Xiaofeng Cao, Yi Chang, Ivor W Tsang

Abstract
Artificial intelligence is to teach machines to take actions like humans. To achieve intelligent teaching, the machine learning community becomes to think about a promising topic named machine teaching where the teacher is to design the optimal (usually minimal) teaching set given a target model and a specific learner. However, previous works usually require numerous teaching examples along with large iterations to guide learners to converge, which is costly. In this paper, we consider a more intelligent teaching paradigm named one-shot machine teaching which costs fewer examples to converge faster. Different from typical teaching, this advanced paradigm establishes a tractable mapping from the teaching set to the model parameter. Theoretically, we prove that this mapping is surjective, which serves to an existence guarantee of the optimal teaching set. Then, relying on the surjective mapping from the teaching set to the parameter, we develop a design strategy of the optimal teaching set under appropriate settings, of which two popular efficiency metrics, teaching dimension and iterative teaching dimension are one. Extensive experiments verify the efficiency of our strategy and further demonstrate the intelligence of this new teaching paradigm.

3.2
Machine teaching

Machine teaching considers the selection of teaching set for learners such that the model parameter
\theta^{*}
can be learnt with fewer teaching examples, and teachers are only allowed to teach via examples rather than parameter clone directly

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{D}^{*}=\underset{\mathcal{D}\in\mathbb{D}}{\arg\min}\quad\mathcal{B}(\hat{\theta},\theta^{*})+\tau\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{D})\\
&\text{s.t.}\quad\hat{\theta}=\textbf{ML}(\mathcal{D})\end{aligned},

where

within one iteration) is still under exploration even tradition and iterative paradigms have made great progress towards it.

4
One-shot Teaching

In this section, we firstly establish a tractable mapping from teaching sets to model parameters, which is proved to be surjective and corroborates the existence of the optimal teaching set. Since the teaching set is closely related to the knowledge domain of teachers, we, meanwhile, present further discussion on different types of teachers. Then, we give the highly-efficient one-shot teaching strategy (OSTS) for different teachers, which solves for the optimal teaching set analytically and derives a canonical singleton. Finally, we provide the case study about typical learner loss functions, which verifies generalizability of OSTS.

4.1
One-shot paradigm

The one-shot paradigm aims to design a singleton as the optimal teaching set to steer learners to converge after one iteration. It attempts to achieve one-shot iteration by establishing a tractable mapping from teaching sets to model parameters, and only requires concrete
\theta^{*}
as well. First of all, we present an expansion insight of the gradient across teaching sets and parameters in model training algorithm Eq.(
4
) based on chain rule.

Proposition 1
.

Given a specific teaching example
(x,y)
, the gradient of loss function
\ell
w.r.t. the model
f(\theta,x)=\langle\theta,x\rangle
can be expended as a constant times the example vector based on chain rule:

\mathcal{G}(\theta;(x,y)|\ell;f)=\nabla_{f}\ell(f(\theta,x),y)\cdot x\coloneqq\zeta\cdot x,

(5)

where it omits the set symbol
\{\cdot\}
out of
\{(x,y)\}
for convenience.

The teacher is an agent who knows the target model and attempts to search examples for feeding learners. The Learner is an agent who update its parameter based on received teaching set via the model training algorithm Eq.(
4
). Fundamentally, Eq.(
4
) tells the relation between the teaching set (as the input) and the model parameter (as the output), thus we make use of this relation to establish a tractable mapping from teaching sets to model parameters: let
\eta
and input
\theta
be fixed, each input
\mathcal{D}
yields a corresponding output
\theta^{\prime}_{\mathcal{D}}
, and the assignment is characterized by Eq.(
4
) whose domain is
\mathbb{D}
and codomain is
\Theta
. Similarly, let
\eta
and input teaching set
\mathcal{D}
be fixed, each input
\theta
yields a corresponding output
\theta^{\prime}_{\theta}
, and Eq.(
4
) defines the assignment manner, of which domain and codomain are
\Theta
in this case. These derive Lemma
2
.

Lemma 2
.

Given a specific learner (
\ell
and
\eta
are fixed), the mini-batch gradient descent algorithm (Eq.(
4
)) is a tractable mapping
\mathcal{M}(\theta,\mathcal{D}):\Theta\times\mathbb{D}\mapsto\Theta
satisfying

(a)

\mathcal{M}
is surjective when
\mathcal{D}
is fixed:
\forall\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta,\exists\theta\in\Theta
such that
\mathcal{M}(\theta|\mathcal{D})=\theta^{\prime}

(b)

\mathcal{M}
is surjective when input
\theta
is fixed:
\forall\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta,\exists\mathcal{D}\in\mathbb{D}
such that
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{D}|\theta)=\theta^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we are concerned with
\left\|\mathcal{D}\right\|_{0}=1
case for simplicity, i.e.,
\mathcal{D}
has one element
(x,y)
. The proof when
\left\|\mathcal{D}\right\|_{0}>1
can be derived analogously.

(a): For proving
\mathcal{M}
is surjective in its first arguments, we need to find
\theta_{\theta^{\prime}}
such that
\theta^{\prime}=\mathcal{M}(\theta_{\theta^{\prime}}|\mathcal{D})
. Since
\Theta
and
\mathcal{X}
fundamentally are the subset of a vector space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the operations of vector addition and scalar multiplication is well defined. With fixed
(x,y)
, specific
\ell
,
f
and
\eta
,
\mathcal{M}
can be specified as

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\theta|(x,y))=\theta-\eta\zeta x

(6)

based on Proposition
1
. Therefore, we can derive that
\forall\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta
, there always exists
\theta_{\theta^{\prime}}=\theta^{\prime}+\eta\zeta x
such that
\mathcal{M}(\theta_{\theta^{\prime}}|(x,y))=\theta_{\theta^{\prime}}-\eta\zeta x=\theta^{\prime}
, which concludes the proof of (a).

(b): Similarly, with fixed
\theta
,
\forall\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta
, there always exists
x_{\theta^{\prime}}=(\theta-\theta^{\prime})/(\eta\zeta)
(since
\eta
and
\zeta
are non-zero scalars) with its label
y_{\theta^{\prime}}
such that
\mathcal{M}((x_{\theta^{\prime}},y_{\theta^{\prime}})|\theta)=\theta-\eta\zeta x_{\theta^{\prime}}=\theta^{\prime}
.
∎

The Lemma
2
corroborates that this mapping is surjective in both arguments, which is important as it provides a existence guarantee of the optimal teaching set. Intuitively, the Lemma
2
tells that

(a)

For any model
\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta
and fixed teaching set
\mathcal{D}
, there always exists a learner whose initial model is
\theta_{\theta^{\prime}}
such that this learner can learn
\theta^{\prime}
after one-iteration update with
\mathcal{D}
following Eq.(
4
).

(b)

For any model
\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta
and a learner with fixed model
\theta
, there always exists a teaching set
\mathcal{D}_{\theta^{\prime}}
such that this learner can converge to
\theta^{\prime}
after one-iteration evolution with
\mathcal{D}_{\theta^{\prime}}
via Eq.(
4
).

The established mapping brings convenience to study the relation among input
\theta
,
\mathcal{D}
and output
\theta^{\prime}
. With the perspective of surjective mapping based on (b) of Lemma

(existence guarantee), the problem has been changed to design the optimal teaching set

\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{D}^{*}|\theta^{0})=\theta^{*}

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{D}|\theta^{0},\theta^{*})\coloneqq\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{D}|\theta^{0})-\theta^{*},

Definition 4

(Pool-based teacher)
.

A teacher is called a pool-based teacher, if the knowledge domain
\mathbb{D}
of this teacher is derived from a pool
\mathcal{P}_{N}=\{(x_{i},y_{i})|x_{i}\in\mathcal{X}_{N},y_{i}\in\mathcal{Y}_{N},i\leq N<\infty\}\subsetneq\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}

Let
\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{P}}\times\mathcal{Y}_{\mathcal{P}}\subset\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
be the completion of
\mathcal{P}_{N}
w.r.t. equipped operations. Further, we define three types of pool-based teachers based on such operations.

Definition 5

(Combinable teacher)
.

A pool-based teacher is a combinable teacher, if
\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{P}}
is closed under addition and scalar multiplication, i.e.,
\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{P}}={\rm span}(\mathcal{X}_{N})
: it can provide linear combination of existing examples as new examples.

Definition 6

(Scalable teacher)
.

A pool-based teacher is a scalable teacher, if
\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{P}}
is not closed under addition but closed under scalar multiplication, i.e.,
\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{P}}=\alpha\cdot\mathcal{X}_{N},\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
: it can provide scaled examples as new examples.

Definition 7

(Naive teacher)
.

A pool-based teacher is a naive teacher, if
\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{P}}
is not closed under addition or scalar multiplication, i.e.,
\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{P}}=\mathcal{X}_{N}
: it can only provide existing examples.

4.2
One-shot teaching strategy

For different teachers, with established surjective mapping from teaching sets and parameters we design corresponding one-shot teaching strategies (OSTS), which attempts to analytically solve for a singleton root
\mathcal{D}^{*}=\{(x^{*},y^{*})\}
where
(x^{*},y^{*})
is the optimal teaching example. This means to find tighter bound of teaching dimension and iterative teaching dimension.

Theorem 8

(One-shot teachability)
.

For a target
\theta^{*}
, there exists a singleton, on which a learner with learning rate
\eta\neq 0
, initial
\theta^{0}
and loss function
\ell(f,y)
can converge to
\theta^{*}
after one-iteration training (it can achieve one-shot iteration), if learner
\ell
is differentiable w.r.t.
f
.

Proof.

To prove the existence of the singleton that can help the learner converge after one iteration, we need to solve for associated
(x^{*},y^{*})
in terms of
\theta^{*}
in closed form.

With the mapping view in Lemma
2
, it fixes
\theta^{0}
and
\theta^{*}
to find a teaching example
(x,y)
such that the learner touch
\theta^{*}
after mapping once:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}((x,y)|\theta^{0})=\theta^{*}.

(8)

It follows from Eq.(
7
) that it is equivalent to find the singleton root:

\displaystyle(x^{*},y^{*})=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\mathcal{D}|\theta^{0},\theta^{*})\qquad\text{s.t.}\,\|\mathcal{D}\|_{0}=1,

(9)

where we omit the set symbol
\{\cdot\}
for convenience. Recall Proposition
1
, when the first-order partial derivatives of
\ell(f,y)
w.r.t.
f
exists, it follows from the chain rule that the gradient at one example can be expanded as

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(\theta;(x,y)|\ell;f)=\nabla_{\theta}\ell(\left\langle\theta,x\right\rangle,y)=\nabla_{\left<\theta,x\right>}\ell(\left\langle\theta,x\right\rangle,y)x.

(10)

Therefore, analytically solving for
(x^{*},y^{*})
equals to find the root of following equation

\theta^{0}-\eta\nabla_{\left<\theta,x\right>}\ell\left(\left\langle\theta^{0},x\right\rangle,y\right)x-\theta^{*}=0.

(11)

Since
\theta^{0}\neq\theta^{*}
, we deduce that each term of the product
\eta\cdot\nabla_{\left<\theta,x\right>}\ell(\left\langle\theta^{0},x\right\rangle,y)\cdot x
is a non-zero scalar or vector. Thus, from Eq.(
11
) we can divide through by
-\eta\nabla_{\left<\theta,x\right>}\ell(\left\langle\theta^{0},x\right\rangle,y)
and rearrange, then obtain

\displaystyle x^{*}=\xi_{\eta;\theta^{0}}(\theta^{*}-\theta^{0}),

(12)

where constant
\xi_{\eta;\theta^{0}}\coloneqq-1/\left(\eta\nabla_{\left<\theta,x\right>}\ell(\left\langle\theta^{0},x^{*}\right\rangle,y^{*})\right)
.
y^{*}
is a free variable and usually can be derived by
f^{*}(x^{*})
.
Thereby, we have analytically find the singleton root expressed by
\theta^{*}

(x^{*},y^{*})=\left(\xi_{\eta;\theta^{0}}(\theta^{*}-\theta^{0}),f^{*}(\xi_{\eta;\theta^{0}}(\theta^{*}-\theta^{0}))\right),

(13)

namely the universal optimal teaching example. This completes the proof.
∎

One-shot teachability is a weaker condition compared with Lipschitz smoothness and strongly convexity since differentiable is weaker than these regularities. For instance, absolute loss is one-shot teachable but not Lipschitz smooth and strongly convex. Moreover, as a side effect, it derives that the teaching dimension and iterative teaching dimension of those strategies who provide concrete construction methods for existent singleton in Theorem
8
are one, which is tighter than that of exponential teachability
[
19
,
58
,
57
]
.

Since
\mathbb{D}
of the complete teacher is derived from the entire teaching example space, it is straightforward to derive OSTS for this type of teacher:

Proposition 9

(OSTS for the complete teacher)
.

For a learner with
\eta\neq 0
and
\theta^{0}
, the complete teacher should provide the universal optimal teaching example

\displaystyle(x^{*},y^{*})=\left(\xi_{\eta;\theta^{0}}(\theta^{*}-\theta^{0}),f^{*}(\xi_{\eta;\theta^{0}}(\theta^{*}-\theta^{0}))\right),

(14)

with learner-specific constant
\xi_{\eta;\theta^{0}}
to the learner.

Proposition
9
tells that teachers need to teach learners in accordance with their aptitude, and the optimal teaching example closely depends on learners themselves. Specifically,
(x^{*},y^{*})
is picked based on
\theta^{0}
and
\eta
of learners. If a learner is well-equipped with
primary knowledge
on
\theta^{*}
, i.e., its
\theta^{0}
is very close to
\theta^{*}
, then
x^{*}
of sampled
(x^{*},y^{*})
should be very close to
\vec{0}
, that is, an indistinguishable one. But for a learner with poor
primary knowledge
, a simple and distinguishable
x^{*}
should be provided. This is consistent with teaching strategy in curriculum learning
[
54
,
38
,
55
]
, where easy examples for learners at first, and difficult ones for learners as they approaches
\theta^{*}
. Moreover, for learners with different
\eta
, the magnitude of
x^{*}
will be scaled relatively. For instance, an unresponsive learner with a small
\eta
should be given a distinguishable
x^{*}
in relatively large scale.

For pool-based teachers, the accessibility of the universal optimal teaching example in Theorem
8
should be considered additionally. We following present teacher-specific OSTS based on teacher-equipped operations.

.

For a learner with

and
\theta^{0}
, the optimal teaching example

\mathcal{P}_{N}=\mathcal{X}_{N}\times\mathcal{Y}_{N}

\displaystyle x^{*}=\kappa x^{\dagger}

(16)

(17)

For the scalable teacher, the fundamental difference among examples is their normalized vector derived by operator
\frac{\cdot}{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{2}}
since the magnitude can be scaled correspondingly. Besides, the operator
\min\left(\left\|\cdot-\cdot\right\|_{2}^{2},\left\|\cdot+\cdot\right\|_{2}^{2}\right)
is to avoid the influence of opposite direction between
x_{i}

. Therefore, the scalable teacher should pick the example whose

For a learner with

(x^{*},y^{*})
for the naive teacher with

\displaystyle x^{*}=\underset{x_{i}\in\mathcal{X}_{N}}{\arg\min}\quad\left\|x_{i}-\xi_{\eta;\theta^{0}}(\theta^{*}-\theta^{0})\right\|_{2}^{2},

(18)

and

Limited knowledge knowledge leads to constrained capability of teachers. Therefore, the condition of learners is discussed as follows, satisfying which they can converge after one iteration with the help of a pool-based teacher. In another word, pool-based teachers can construct the universal optimal teaching example for the learner who satisfies such condition.

Theorem 13
.

For a learner with
\eta\neq 0
and
\theta^{0}
, the pool-based teachers are able to construct the universal optimal teaching example, if
\theta^{0}
satisfies following teacher-specific necessary and sufficient condition.

(a)

\theta^{0}-\theta^{*}\in\text{\rm span}(\mathcal{X}_{N})
for the combinable teacher;

(b)

\theta^{0}-\theta^{*}\in\alpha\cdot\mathcal{X}_{N},\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
for the scalable teacher;

(c)

\xi_{\eta;\theta^{0}}(\theta^{0}-\theta^{*})\in\mathcal{X}_{N}
for the naive teacher.

Proof.

The proof of the naive teacher (c) is trivial so we omit it.

"
\Rightarrow
":

(a)

Since
\theta^{0}-\theta^{*}\in\text{\rm span}(\mathcal{X}_{N})
, we can derive
\exists\,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{N}\in\mathbb{R}
not all zero, such that

\displaystyle\theta^{0}-\theta^{*}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda_{i}x_{i},\qquad x_{i}\in\mathcal{X}_{N}.

(19)

Hence, we can obtain
\xi_{\eta;\theta^{0}}(\theta^{*}-\theta^{0})=-\xi_{\eta;\theta^{0}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda_{i}x_{i}
. Let
\beta_{i}=-\xi_{\eta;\theta^{0}}\lambda_{i}
, then the combinable teacher can derive the universal optimal teaching example.

(b)

Similarly,
\because\theta^{0}-\theta^{*}\in\alpha\cdot\mathcal{X}_{N},\,\therefore\exists\alpha\neq 0\in\mathbb{R},x_{i}\in\mathcal{X}_{N}
such that
\theta^{0}-\theta^{*}=\alpha x_{i}
. Then the scalable teacher can obtain the universal optimal teaching example by setting
\beta=-\xi_{\eta;\theta^{0}}\alpha\cdot e_{i}
.

"
\Leftarrow
":

(a)

Since the combinable teacher can derive the universal optimal teaching example, we can derive
\exists\,\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, such that

\displaystyle\xi_{\eta;\theta^{0}}(\theta^{*}-\theta^{0})=\langle\beta,(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})^{T}\rangle,\qquad x_{1},\dots,x_{N}\in\mathcal{X}_{N}.

(20)

Since the constant
\xi_{\eta;\theta^{0}}
is non-zero, we have

\displaystyle\theta^{0}-\theta^{*}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}-\frac{\beta_{i}}{\xi_{\eta;\theta^{0}}}x_{i},\qquad x_{i}\in\mathcal{X}_{N},

(21)

from which we can conclude
\theta^{0}-\theta^{*}\in\text{\rm span}(\mathcal{X}_{N})
.

(b)

Analogously, we can derive
\exists\,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, such that

\displaystyle\xi_{\eta;\theta^{0}}(\theta^{*}-\theta^{0})=\beta\cdot x_{i},\qquad x_{i}\in\mathcal{X}_{N}.

(22)

For a non-zero constant
\xi_{\eta;\theta^{0}}
, we have

\displaystyle\theta^{0}-\theta^{*}=-\frac{\beta}{\xi_{\eta;\theta^{0}}}x_{i},

(23)

i.e.,
\theta^{0}-\theta^{*}\in\alpha\cdot\mathcal{X}_{N},\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
.

∎

Theorem
13
tells that the teaching ability of pool-based teachers depends on equipped operation. Specifically, the combinable teacher is better qualified than other pool-based teachers, who can construct the universal optimal teaching example if the difference between
\theta^{0}
of these learners and
\theta^{*}
is expressible in terms of
\mathcal{X}_{N}
. For the scalable teacher, the capability is worse. They are only able to generate the universal optimal teaching example for those learners whose
\theta^{0}
satisfy
\theta^{0}-\theta^{*}\in\alpha\mathcal{X}_{N}
. Moreover, for the naive teacher, they only can do that when the universal optimal teaching example is in their pools.

Remark 14
.

The specific task for model
\theta^{*}
can be not only regression but also classification. Besides above analysis of OSTS is mainly on binary classification, based on which K-class cases can be extend straightforward since a K-class classification task usually can be separated into K binary classification tasks.

4.3
Learner loss functions

We mainly discuss three typical loss functions (square loss, hinge loss and logistic loss) under the complete teacher, which can be extended to the pool-based teacher following Proposition
10
,
11
,
12
. The learners with these loss functions are called least square regression (LSR), support vector machine (SVM) and logistic regression (LR) learners, respectively. Without loss of generality, we consider the worse case where the learner has no primary knowledge, i.e.,
\theta^{0}=0
.

Proposition 15
.

Square loss,
\ell(f,y)=\left(f-y\right)^{2}
is one-shot teachable for the complete teacher.

Proof.

It follows from Theorem
8
that the first-order differentiable learner loss is one-shot teachable, and it is evident that square loss
\left(f-y\right)^{2}
has first-order partial derivatives w.r.t.
f
, which justifies the existence of the optimal teaching example. Hence, to prove square loss is one-shot teachable, it needs to solve for
\xi_{\eta;\theta^{0}}
.

Following Proposition
1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}\left(\theta^{0};(x,y)|\left(f-y\right)^{2};\left\langle\theta,x\right\rangle\right)=2\left(\left\langle\theta^{0},x\right\rangle-y\right)x.

Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\xi_{\eta;\theta^{0}}=1/(2\eta y).

Therefore, the optimal teaching example
(x^{*},y^{*})
is

(x^{*},y^{*})=\left((\theta^{*}-\theta^{0})/(2\eta y^{*}),y^{*}\right)

where
y^{*}
is a free variable, which can be arbitrarily chosen in
\mathcal{Y}
at first.
∎

We see that for the dynamic learner equipped with variable learning rate
\eta^{t},t=0,1,\dots,T
, the teacher merely requires the initial
\eta^{0}
to construct the optimal teaching example. For hinge loss, we mainly consider homogeneous
\theta
(without a bias term), and the inhomogeneous result can be derived similarly.

Proposition 16
.

Hinge loss,
\ell(f,y)=\max\left(1-y\cdot f,0\right)
is one-shot teachable for the complete teacher.

Proof.

The hinge loss,
\text{max}\left(1-y\cdot f,0\right)
, is also first-order differentiable w.r.t.
f
. Besides,
(x^{*},y^{*})
should be one of the teaching examples
(x,y)
who satisfy
1-y\cdot f(\theta^{0},x)>0
, otherwise the gradient at
(x^{*},y^{*})
is zero and
\theta^{0}
will not be updated. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}\left(\theta^{0};(x,y)|1-y\cdot f;\left\langle\theta,x\right\rangle\right)=-yx.

Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\xi_{\eta;\theta^{0}}=1/(\eta y),

which yields the optimal teaching example

(x^{*},y^{*})=\left((\theta^{*}-\theta^{0})/(\eta y^{*}),y^{*}\right)

where
y^{*}
is a free variable as well. This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 17
.

Logistic loss,
\ell(f,y)=\log\left(1+\exp\left(-y\cdot f\right)\right)
is one-shot teachable for the complete teacher.

Proof.

The logistic loss,
\text{log}\left(1+\text{exp}\left(-y\cdot f\right)\right)
, is first-order differentiable w.r.t.
f
as well.
It follows from Proposition
1
that

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}\left(\theta^{0};(x,y)|\text{log}\left(1+\text{exp}\left(-y\cdot f\right)\right);\left\langle\theta,x\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-y\text{exp}\left(-y\left\langle\theta^{0},x\right\rangle\right)}{1+\text{exp}\left(-y\left\langle\theta^{0},x\right\rangle\right)}x=\frac{-y}{1+\text{exp}\left(y\left\langle\theta^{0},x\right\rangle\right)}x.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\xi_{\eta;\theta^{0}}=2/(\eta y).

It derives the optimal teaching example

(x^{*},y^{*})=\left(2(\theta^{*}-\theta^{0})/(\eta y^{*}),y^{*}\right)

with free variable
y^{*}
, which is the desired result.
∎

As expected, these first-order differentiable loss functions are one-shot teachable, which justifies that one-shot teachability is not a strict condition and demonstrates the general applicability of OSTS.

Definition 18

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we propose a more intelligent machine teaching paradigm named one-shot machine teaching to achieve one-shot iteration, which is to search a singleton that can steer learners to converge after one iteration. Establishing a tractable mapping proved to be surjective from teaching sets to model parameters, the one-shot paradigm corroborates the existence of the optimal teaching set by which the optimal model can be learnt after one iteration. Relying on the surjective mapping, we develop a canonical design strategy of the optimal teaching set, namely one-shot teaching strategy whose teaching dimension and iterative teaching dimension are one, for both complete and pool-based (combinable, scalable and naive) teachers. We discuss the generalizability of our strategy w.r.t. the learner loss functions (e.g., square, hinge and logistic loss). The high efficiency of our strategy has been empirically verified, which also demonstrates the intelligence of this new teaching paradigm.

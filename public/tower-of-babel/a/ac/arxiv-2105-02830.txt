Probing Robust Majorana Signatures by Crossed Andreev Reflection with a Quantum Dot Preprint: APS/123-QED
Guan-Hao Feng, Hong-Hao Zhang

Abstract
We propose a three-terminal structure to probe robust signatures of Majorana zero modes. This structure consists of a quantum dot coupled to the normal metal, s-wave superconducting and Majorana Y-junction leads. The zero-bias differential conductance at zero temperature of the normal-metal lead peaks at 2 ​ e 2 / h 2e^{2}/h , which will be deflected after Majorana braiding. This quantized conductance can entirely arise from the Majorana-induced crossed Andreev reflection, protected by the energy gap of the superconducting lead. We find that the effect of thermal broadening is significantly suppressed when the dot is on resonance. In the case that the energy level of the quantum dot is much larger than the superconducting gap, tunneling processes are dominated by Majorana-induced crossed Andreev reflection. Particularly, a novel kind of crossed Andreev reflection equivalent to the splitting of charge quanta 3 ​ e 3e occurs after Majorana braiding.



I
INTRODUCTION

Majorana zero modes (MZMs) are zero-energy quasiparticle excitations
originating from coherent superpositions of electrons and holes. Following
theoretical suggestions, MZMs are supported in 1D systems, such as
InAs or InSb wires with strong spin-orbit coupling and proximity-induced
superconductivity
(
Lutchyn et al. 2010
;
Oreg et al. 2010
)
,
and they show great potential in decoherence-free quantum computation

(
Sarma et al. 2015
;
Fu and Kane 2008
;
Potter and Lee 2010
;
Kitaev 2001
)
.
Verifying the existence of MZMs and their non-Abelian braiding has
been attracting much attention in recent years
(
Bolech and Demler 2007
;
Pientka et al. 2012
;
Liu and Baranger 2011
;
Akhmerov et al. 2009
;
Fu and Kane 2009a
;
Danon et al. 2020
;
Haim et al. 2015
;
Fidkowski et al. 2012
;
Wu and Cao 2012
;
Jonckheere et al. 2019
)
.

Due to the property that an MZM can act as both an electron lead and
a hole lead in tunneling processes, one of the most exciting theoretical
predictions is a quantized zero-bias conductance peak (ZBCP) of
\frac{2e^{2}}{h}

at zero temperature
(
Flensberg 2010
;
Law et al. 2009
;
He et al. 2014
)
.
However, it is quite difficult to observe this quantization from a
direct junction between a normal-metal lead and MZMs in a single-subband
wire because of thermal broadening, overlap of Majorana wave functions,
disorder, and localized Andreev bound states
(
Bagrets and Altland 2012
;
Pientka et al. 2012
;
Peng et al. 2015
;
Liu et al. 2012
;
Awoga et al. 2019
;
Sharma and Tewari 2016
;
Cayao et al. 2015
)
.
Although the observation of ZBCP has been reported in many experiments
in recent years
(
Nadj-Perge et al. 2014
;
Mourik et al. 2012
;
Das et al. 2012
;
Zhu et al. 2020
;
Sun et al. 2016
)
,
the observation of MZMs has not been fully confirmed. Importantly,
very recently it has been recognized that one needs to be cautious
about the interpretation of non-quantized ZBCP as the signature of
MZMs in local tunneling experiments since such experiments only measure
one end of the one-dimensional setup
(
Frolov 2021
)
, while
the most important characteristics of MZMs are their nonlocal correlations.
To advance the pursuit of MZMs, new theoretical proposals and new
signatures which can reflect the nonlocal correlations of MZMs are
hence highly demanded. For example, shot noise and Fano factor in
Majorana setups can carry interesting information to identify MZMs

(
Jonckheere et al. 2019
;
Bolech and Demler 2007
;
Nilsson et al. 2008
;
Tripathi et al. 2016
;
Liu et al. 2015
;
Golub and Horovitz 2011
;
Wu and Cao 2012
;
Giuliano et al. 2018
;
Smirnov 2019
;
Smirnov 2017
)
.

Here we propose a T-shaped hybrid structure to detect MZMs, as illustrated
in Fig.
1
. The central quantum dot
(QD) acts as a transfer station of electrons and holes. Hence tunning
the energy level of the QD is equivalent to tunning the transmission
coefficients. The key to probe MZMs is the Majorana-induced crossed
Andreev reflection
(
Beckmann et al. 2004
;
Law et al. 2009
;
Nilsson et al. 2008
;
Zhang and Trauzettel 2019
)
.
The ZBCP arising from the crossed Andreev reflection in this T-shaped
structure is strongly protected by the energy gap of the superconducting
lead because quasiparticle excitations are exponentially suppressed

\sim\exp(-\Delta/T)
. Such kind of multiterminal structures with
a QD shows excellent maneuverability in the studies of spin-dependent
transport in strong Coulomb-correlated systems
(
Cottet et al. 2004
;
Zhu et al. 2002
;
Cao et al. 2004
;
Zhu et al. 2001
;
Sun et al. 1999
;
Sun et al. 2000
;
Souza et al. 2008
)
.

At zero temperature, we find that the ZBCP of the normal-metal lead
is quantized to
2e^{2}/h
before braiding, which can be completely
induced by the crossed Andreev reflection. This quantized ZBCP is
found to be considerably robust against the temperature when the QD
is on resonance (
\epsilon_{\mathrm{d}}=0
). We show that the crossed
Andreev reflection dominates over the conventional Andreev reflection
when
\epsilon_{\mathrm{d}}\gg\Delta
. Importantly, we find that
the Majorana braiding shifts the ZBCPs and arouses a novel kind of
crossed Andreev reflection equivalent to the splitting of
3e
charge
quanta, as shown in Fig.
4
. Because
of the high controllability of QD and the robustness of the predicted
signatures, our findings suggest a promising new way to identify MZMs.

It is worth noting that while the Kondo correlations
are important in a strong coupling and low-temperature regime, the
Kondo resonances are usually either unstable or unquantized
(
Koerting et al. 2010
;
Cheng et al. 2014
;
Kiršanskas et al. 2015
)
.
In sharp contrast, the Majorana-induced resonance in this paper is
always singly situated at zero bias and leads to quantized conductance.
In order to isolate and investigate observable consequences of the
Majorana-induced subgap resonances, we neglect the Kondo correlations
and focus on the Majorana-induced crossed Andreev reflection.

This paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
,
we introduce the T-shaped hybrid model and explicitly write down their
Hamiltonians. In Sec.
III
, we discuss
the electronic transport of the system and provide the corresponding
current and conductance formulas, including the analytical expressions
for the ZBCPs. In Sec.
IV
, we present the
formula for the shot noise and the Fano factor in terms of appropriate
Green’s functions. The detailed derivations of the self-energy, the
local density of states, and the shot noise are given in Appendix

A
,
B
and

C
, respectively.

II
MODEL AND FORMULATION

We introduce the three-terminal setup shown in Fig.
1
.
The three leads are coupled with a central QD and the superconducting
lead ensures that the occurrence of crossed Andreev reflection, which
protects the ZBCP from quasiparticle excitations. The tunnel-coupled
structure can be described by an effective low-energy Hamiltonian:

H=H_{\mathrm{L}}+H_{\mathrm{R}}+H_{\mathrm{QD}}+H_{\mathrm{Y}}+H_{\mathrm{T}}.

(1)

The first term in Eq. (
1
) is the Hamiltonian
of the normal-metal lead (N) in Fig.
1
,
which is characterized by

H_{\mathrm{L}}=\sum_{k\sigma}\epsilon_{\mathrm{L},k\sigma}a_{\mathrm{L},k\sigma}^{\dagger}a_{\mathrm{L},k\sigma},

(2)

where
a_{\mathrm{L},k\sigma}^{\dagger}(a_{\mathrm{L},k\sigma})

are creation (annihilation) operators with wave vector
k
and spin

\sigma=\uparrow,\downarrow
, and
\epsilon_{\mathrm{L},k\sigma}

is the corresponding electron energy. The second term, the Hamiltonian
of the superconducting lead (S) in Fig.
1
,
is given by the BCS theory

H_{\mathrm{R}}=\sum_{k\sigma}\epsilon_{\mathrm{R},k\sigma}a_{\mathrm{R},k\sigma}^{\dagger}a_{\mathrm{R},k\sigma}+\sum_{k}(\Delta a_{\mathrm{R},k\uparrow}^{\dagger}a_{\mathrm{R},-k\downarrow}^{\dagger}+\mathrm{H.c.}).

(3)

The superconducting energy gap
\Delta
is real here since a unitary
transformation has been performed on this Hamiltonian
(
Sun et al. 1999
;
Cuevas et al. 1996
)
.
In this work, we set the applied voltage of the superconducting lead

V_{\mathrm{R}}=0
. For simplicity, we use the noninteracting Hamiltonian
of the QD

H_{\mathrm{QD}}=\sum_{\sigma}\epsilon_{\mathrm{d}}d_{\sigma}^{\dagger}d_{\sigma},

(4)

where the QD level
\epsilon_{\mathrm{d}}=\epsilon_{0}-eV_{\mathrm{g}}/2

is controlled by a gate voltage
V_{\mathrm{g}}

(
Souza et al. 2008
;
Koerting et al. 2010
;
Liu and Baranger 2011
)
.
The Hamiltonian of the Majorana Y junction (Y) in Fig.
1

is given by

H_{\mathrm{Y}}=i\sum_{k=2}^{4}t_{1k}\gamma_{1}\gamma_{k},

(5)

where the Coulomb coupling constants are
t_{12}=t_{13}=t_{\mathrm{\min}}

and
t_{14}=t_{\mathrm{\max}}
with
t_{\mathrm{\min}}\ll t_{\max}

(
Van Heck et al. 2012
)
. Using two fermionic operators
c_{1}=(\gamma_{1}-i\gamma_{4})/2

and
c_{2}=(\gamma_{2}-i\gamma_{3})/2
, the Hamiltonian
H_{\mathrm{Y}}

can be represented in the four-dimensional Nambu-spinor space spanned
by
c_{\mathrm{Y}}^{\dagger}=(c_{1}^{\dagger},c_{1},c_{2}^{\dagger},c_{2})
.

The tunneling Hamiltonian consists of

H_{\mathrm{T}}=H_{\mathrm{T,L}}+H_{\mathrm{T,R}}+H_{\mathrm{T,Y}},

(6)

where

H_{\mathrm{T,L(R)}}=\sum_{k\sigma}v_{\mathrm{L}(\mathrm{R}),k}d_{\sigma}^{\dagger}a_{\mathrm{L(R)},k\sigma}+\mathrm{H.c.},

(7)

with
v_{\mathrm{L},k}
and
v_{\mathrm{R},k}
denoting the complex
tunneling amplitudes of the normal-metal and superconducting leads,
respectively. The coupling between the QD and the Majorana lead is
spin-conserving,
i.e.
, the MZM is always tunnel-coupled to
electrons in the QD with the same spin orientation
(
Zazunov et al. 2016
)
.
Since we have set the spin orientation of the Rashba spin-orbit coupling
along the z-direction in Fig.
1
, the
spin of each MZM (except
\gamma_{1}
) is parallel to the axial direction
of the corresponding nanowire
(
Oreg et al. 2010
;
Xu et al. 2017
)
.
Defining that the spin-
\uparrow
direction is along the y-direction,
the coupling between the QD and the Majorana lead is given by

H_{\mathrm{T,Y}}=\lambda d_{\uparrow}^{\dagger}\gamma_{2}+\mathrm{H.c.},

(8)

where
\lambda
is the coupling amplitude. For simplicity, we assume

\lambda
is real.

III
CURRENT AND CONDUCTANCE

The ZBCP arising from the crossed Andreev reflection in this T-shaped
structure is a remarkable signature of MZMs. In this section, we first
calculate the time-average current by using the nonequilibrium Green’s
function method
(
Meir and Wingreen 1992
;
Wu and Cao 2012
;
Haug et al. 2008
;
Cuevas et al. 1996
;
Zeng et al. 2003
;
Keldysh et al. 1965
)
,
and then derive the analytic expression of the ZBCP of each lead.

The time-average current of the normal-metal lead is given by

\displaystyle I_{\mathrm{L}}

\displaystyle=-e\bigl\langle\dot{N}_{\mathrm{L}}(t)\bigr\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{e}{h}\int\mathrm{d}\omega\thinspace\mathrm{Re}\thinspace\mathrm{Tr}\thinspace\{[G_{\mathrm{QD}}^{R}(\omega)\Sigma_{\mathrm{L}}^{<}(\omega)+G_{\mathrm{QD}}^{<}(\omega)\Sigma_{\mathrm{L}}^{A}(\omega)]\widetilde{\sigma}_{z}\},

(9)

where
N_{L}(t)=\sum_{k\sigma}a_{\mathrm{L},k\sigma}^{\dagger}(t)a_{\mathrm{L},k\sigma}(t)

is the total number operator of the electrons in the normal-metal
lead . The
4\times 4
Green’s functions
G_{\mathrm{QD}}^{<}(t,t^{\prime})\equiv-i\left\langle d(t^{\prime})d^{\dagger}(t)\right\rangle

and
G_{\mathrm{QD}}^{R}(t,t^{\prime})\equiv-i\theta(t-t^{\prime})\left\langle\left\{d(t),d^{\dagger}(t^{\prime})\right\}\right\rangle

is defined with the Nambu spinors
d^{\dagger}=(d_{\uparrow}^{\dagger},d_{\downarrow},d_{\downarrow}^{\dagger},d_{\uparrow})
.
The retarded self-energy

f_{\mathrm{L}}=f(\omega-eV_{\mathrm{L}})

When
\lambda=0
, the Majorana Y junction is disconnected with the
QD, and the remaining part is reduced to an N-QD-S structure. The
maximal ZBCP in Eq. (
24
)
is equal to
4e^{2}/h
when the QD is symmetrically coupled
(\Gamma_{\mathrm{L}}=\Gamma_{\mathrm{R}})

and on resonance
(\epsilon_{\mathrm{d}}=0)
, in accord with the
previous results of Ref.
(
Cao et al. 2004
)
. When
\lambda\neq 0
,
the ZBCP of the normal-metal lead in this three-terminal structure
equals a quantized value
2e^{2}/h
, which is consistent with the
famous conductance peak for the N-TS tunneling. As depicted in Figs.
2a
-
2c
,
the ZBCP is obviously broad for
\epsilon_{\mathrm{d}}=0
and becomes
sharp for large
\epsilon_{\mathrm{d}}
. The QD acts as a transfer
station of electrons and holes, which means that the energy level
of the QD is the tunnel barrier of the system. Hence the broadening
of the ZBCP arises from the junction transparency effect and the height
of the ZBCP is not affected. This quantized ZBCP is caused by the
perfect Majorana-induced Andreev reflection. In the next section,
we will show that the local Andreev reflection can be completely suppressed
by increasing
\epsilon_{\mathrm{d}}
, and only the crossed Andreev
reflection remains. We emphasize again that this ZBCP of
2e^{2}/h

can completely arise from the crossed Andreev reflection, which is
strongly protected by the superconducting gap
\Delta

(
Peng et al. 2015
)
.
Moreover, the results of Eqs.
25

and
26
show that the ZBCPs of
both the superconducting and the Majorana leads are insensitive to
the nonzero coupling amplitude
\lambda
, but only dependent on
\Gamma_{\mathrm{L}}
,

\Gamma_{R}
and
\epsilon_{\mathrm{d}}
.

The conductance peaks of the normal-metal lead are
closely related to the local density of states (LDOS) of the superconducting
lead in this T-shaped structure (the details of the analytical derivation
of the LDOS are provided in Appendix
B
).
As shown in Figs.
2d
-
2f
, there are
three subgap resonances in the superconducting lead two are the spin-induced
resonances situating near the gap edge
(
Koerting et al. 2010
)
,
one is the Majorana-induced resonance situating at
\omega=0
. The
conductance peaks are all situated at the subgap resonance energy.
As
\epsilon_{\mathrm{d}}
increases, all the resonances become sharper,
and the two near the gap edge merge with the dips eventually, whereas
the one at
\omega=0
remains. For large
\epsilon_{\mathrm{d}}
,
the Majorana-induced resonance situating at
\omega=0
sharpens to
form a localized bound state,
i.e.
,
a Yu-Shiba-Rusinov state (YSR state). The occurrence of the Majorana-induced
YSR state will lead to the domination of crossed Andreev reflection,
which will be discussed in the next section. The Majorana-induced
resonance in the superconducting lead is not quantized but parameters
dependent, consistent with the result in Eq.
25
.
The Majorana-induced resonance leads to a quantized ZBCP of the normal-metal
lead since the electrons and holes are transported through perfect
Andreev reflection
(
Pan et al. 2021
)
. The spin-induced
conductance peaks are unstable and unquantized,
i.e
.,
they are not robust as functions of parameters. When
\lambda
increases,
the Majorana induced resonance is enhanced and the spin-induced conductance
peaks become inconspicuous in Figs.
2b

and
2c
due to the competition
between the spin-induced and the Majorana-induced resonances in the
tunneling processes.

IV
SHOT NOISE AND FANO FACTOR

In addition to the time-average current, the shot noise can reveal
the fluctuation of the current and provide useful information about
MZMs
(
Bolech and Demler 2007
;
Nilsson et al. 2008
;
Golub and Horovitz 2011
;
Fu and Kane 2009b
)
.
The shot noise, defined as the correlation function of the current
fluctuations between leads
\eta
and
\eta^{\prime}
, takes the form
S_{\eta\eta^{\prime}}(t,t^{\prime})=\left\langle\{\delta I_{\eta}(t),\delta I_{\eta^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\}\right\rangle
,
where
\delta I_{\eta}(t)=\hat{I}_{\eta}(t)-I_{\eta}
, and
\hat{I}_{\eta}(t)=-e\dot{N}_{\eta}(t)
.
The time-average current
I_{\eta}
has been obtained by Eq. (
9
).
With the use of the Wick’s theorem and the S-matrix expansion
(
Souza et al. 2008
;
Wu and Cao 2012
)
,
we can reduce the expression of the shot noise in terms of Green’s
functions. After Fourier transform, we obtain the expression of shot
noise in the frequency space
S_{\eta\eta^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})
. The calculation
of the shot noise is shown explicitly in Appendix
C
.

Following Ref.
(
Blanter and Büttiker 2000
)
, in multi-terminal systems,
the shot noise
S_{\eta\eta^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})
with
\eta=\eta^{\prime}
must be
positive; conversely, that with
\eta\neq\eta^{\prime}
must be negative.
This property can be verified by the numerical calculation of
S_{\eta\eta}(0)

in the following. The zero-frequency Fano factor, defined by the ratio

F_{\eta}=S_{\eta\eta}(0)/2eI_{\eta}
, can gain insight into the
nature of charge quanta transferred to lead
\eta

(
Golub and Horovitz 2011
;
Cron et al. 2001
;
Cuevas et al. 1999
)
.

Beyond the linear regime in this paper, the Majorana-induced
nonlinear effective charge has been studied in detail
(
Smirnov 2017
)
,
which gives rise to fractional effective charge quanta.

e
as well as the Majorana lead, while the

2e

reflection. A hole from the left lead is reflected as an electron
into the Majorana lead, while a Cooper pair from the superconducting

into the normal-metal lead
(
Nilsson et al. 2008

, as shown
in Fig.

crossed Andreev reflection act as facilitators to propel the splitting
of Cooper pairs, and do not contribute to the transferred charge.
In this regime, local Andreev reflection is fully suppressed, and
the first kind of crossed Andreev reflection dominates.

The quantized Fano factors result from the occurrence
of the YSR state below the gap. As shown in Fig.

a very sharp Majorana-induced mid-gap resonance at
\omega=0

is regarded as a YSR bound state.
The occurrence of the YSR state means that the Cooper pairing is “softened”
so that the free electrons from the superconducting lead screen the
spins of the QD
(
Heinrich et al. 2018
;
Maurand and Schönenberger 2013
)
. The state-screened
process leads to the splitting of Cooper pairs. In this way, crossed
Andreev reflection dominates the tunneling processes, which gives
rise to the quantized Fano factor.

VI
CONCLUSION

We have studied the ZBCPs and the Fano factors of the T-shaped structure.
We have shown that the ZBCP of the normal-metal lead is always quantized
to
2e^{2}/h
at zero temperature before braiding, which is quite
robust at finite temperature when the QD is on-resonance. This quantized
conductance can entirely arise from the Majorana-induced crossed Andreev
reflection, which is protected by the energy gap of the superconducting
lead. After Majorana braiding, the quantized ZBCP shifts and becomes
dependent on the line widths
\Gamma_{\mathrm{L}}
,
\Gamma_{\mathrm{R}}

and the QD level
\epsilon_{\mathrm{d}}
. This variation is owing
to the introduction of spin-flip tunneling between the Majorana lead
and the QD after braiding. By analyzing the quantized Fano factors,
we have found that the crossed Andreev reflection dominates over the
conventional Andreev reflection when
\epsilon_{\mathrm{d}}\gg\Delta
.
We have also found a novel kind of crossed Andreev reflection equivalent
to the splitting of the
3e
charge quanta. The quantized ZBCPs and
Fano factors induced by the nonlocal crossed Andreev reflection provide
strong fingerprint for MZMs.

CALCULATION OF
\Sigma_{\mathrm{Y}}^{<}

In this section, we present the details of the analytical calculation
of the terms containing
\Sigma_{\mathrm{Y}}^{<}
in Eq.
9

. The lesser self-energy from the Majorana lead is given by

\Sigma_{\mathrm{Y}}^{<}=F_{\mathrm{Y}}(\Sigma_{\mathrm{Y}}^{A}-\Sigma_{\mathrm{Y}}^{R})=-i2F_{\mathrm{Y}}\mathrm{Im}\Sigma_{\mathrm{Y}}^{R},

(31)

where
\Sigma_{\mathrm{Y}}^{R}
is determined by

\displaystyle\kappa

\displaystyle\thickapprox\frac{-8\lambda^{2}t_{\max}^{2}}{-4t_{\mathrm{\max}}^{2}\omega^{+}+(\omega^{+})^{3}}+\frac{2\lambda^{2}(\omega^{+})^{2}}{-4t_{\mathrm{\max}}^{2}\omega^{+}+(\omega^{+})^{3}},

(32)

with
\omega^{+}=\omega+i0^{+}
, as we mentioned in the main text
(
\kappa
here is same to that appeared in Eq. (
III
)).
We use the notation
\kappa_{1}
and
\kappa_{2}
to denote the first
term and the second term of
\kappa
, respectively. Neglecting the
high-order terms of
(i0^{+})^{n\geq 2}
, we can obtain the following
expressions:

\displaystyle\kappa_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{-8\lambda^{2}t_{\max}^{2}}{(-4t_{\mathrm{\max}}^{2}\omega+\omega^{3})-(4t_{\mathrm{\max}}^{2}-3\omega^{2})(i0^{+})},

(33)

\displaystyle\kappa_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\lambda^{2}\omega^{2}}{\omega(-4t_{\mathrm{\max}}^{2}+\omega^{2})+(4t_{\mathrm{\max}}^{2}+\omega^{2})(i0^{+})}.

(34)

Using the formula

\lim_{\eta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{1}{x\pm i\eta}=\mathcal{P}\frac{1}{x}\mp i\pi\delta(x),

(35)

we can obtain

\displaystyle i\mathrm{Im(\kappa_{1})}

\displaystyle=i\pi\frac{-8\lambda^{2}t_{\max}^{2}}{4t_{\max}^{2}-3\omega^{2}}\delta\left(\frac{-4t_{\max}^{2}\omega+\omega^{3}}{4t_{\max}^{2}-3\omega^{2}}\right),

(36)

\displaystyle i\mathrm{Im}(\kappa_{2})

\displaystyle=-i\pi\frac{2\lambda^{2}\omega^{2}}{4t_{\max}^{2}+\omega^{2}}\delta\left(\frac{-4t_{\max}^{2}\omega+\omega^{3}}{4t_{\max}^{2}+\omega^{2}}\right).

(37)

Using the relationship

\delta(\phi(x))=\sum_{j}\frac{1}{|\phi^{\prime}(x)|}\delta(x-x_{j}),

(38)

with
\phi(x_{j})=0
, the imaginary part of
\kappa_{1}
and
\kappa_{2}

reduce to

\displaystyle i\mathrm{Im(\kappa_{1})}

\displaystyle=Q_{1}(\omega)[\delta(\omega)+\delta(\omega-2t_{\max})+\delta(\omega+2t_{\max})],

(39)

\displaystyle i\mathrm{Im(\kappa_{2})}

\displaystyle=Q_{2}(\omega)[\delta(\omega)+\delta(\omega-2t_{\max})+\delta(\omega+2t_{\max})],

(40)

where

\displaystyle Q_{1}(\omega)

\displaystyle=i\pi\frac{-8\lambda^{2}t_{\max}^{2}}{4t_{\max}^{2}-3\omega^{2}}\left|\frac{(4t_{\max}^{2}-3\omega^{2})^{2}}{-(4t_{\max}^{2}-3\omega^{2})^{2}-6\omega^{2}(4t_{\max}^{2}-\omega^{2})}\right|,

(41)

\displaystyle Q_{2}(\omega)

\displaystyle=-i\pi\frac{2\lambda^{2}\omega^{2}}{4t_{\max}^{2}+\omega^{2}}\left|\frac{(4t_{\max}^{2}+\omega^{2})^{2}}{(-4t_{\max}^{2}+3\omega^{2})(4t_{\max}^{2}+\omega^{2})-2\omega^{2}(-4t_{\max}^{2}+\omega^{2})}\right|.

,
Q_{1}(\pm 2t_{\max})=i\pi\lambda^{2}
,

and
Q_{2}(\pm 2t_{\max})=-i\pi\lambda^{2}

\displaystyle\int\mathrm{d}\omega G_{\mathrm{QD}}^{R}(\omega)\Sigma_{\mathrm{Y}}^{<}(\omega)

Appendix B
CALCULATION OF THE LDOS OF THE SUPERCONDUCTING LEAD

For convenience, the whole system is divided into two subsystems,
one is "quantum dot + Majorana Y-junction+Normal-metal
lead", the other is the superconducting lead. The Hamiltonian
of the superconducting lead is given by Eq. (3) in the main text,

H_{\mathrm{R}}=\sum_{k\sigma}\epsilon_{\mathrm{R},k\sigma}a_{\mathrm{R},k\sigma}^{\dagger}a_{\mathrm{R},k\sigma}+\sum_{k}(\Delta a_{\mathrm{R},k\uparrow}^{\dagger}a_{\mathrm{R},-k\downarrow}^{\dagger}+\mathrm{H.c.}).

In the Nambu space
(a_{\mathrm{R},k\uparrow}^{\dagger},a_{\mathrm{R},-k\downarrow},a_{\mathrm{R},-k\downarrow}^{\dagger},a_{\mathrm{R},k\uparrow})
,
the unperturbed Green’s function of the BCS superconductor evaluated
at the origin (
r=0
) is represented as

\displaystyle g_{\mathrm{R}}^{R}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\int\mathrm{d}^{3}k\frac{e^{ik\cdot r}}{\omega_{+}^{2}-\xi_{k}^{2}-\Delta^{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\omega_{+}+\xi_{k}&\Delta\\
\Delta&\omega_{+}-\xi_{k}\\
&&\omega_{+}+\xi_{k}&\Delta\\
&&\Delta&\omega_{+}-\xi_{k}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=-\rho_{R}\int\mathrm{d}\xi_{k}\frac{1}{\left(\xi_{k}-\sqrt{\omega_{+}^{2}-\Delta^{2}}\right)\left(\xi_{k}+\sqrt{\omega_{+}^{2}-\Delta^{2}}\right)}\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\omega_{+}+\xi_{k}&\Delta\\
\Delta&\omega_{+}-\xi_{k}\\
&&\omega_{+}+\xi_{k}&-\Delta\\
&&-\Delta&\omega_{+}-\xi_{k}\end{array}\right),

where
\rho_{R}
is the density of states and
\omega_{+}=\omega+i\eta

with
\eta=0^{+}
. We reduce the expression above by taking

\sqrt{\omega_{+}^{2}-\Delta^{2}}=\sqrt{\omega^{2}+i\omega\eta-\Delta^{2}}\approx\sqrt{\omega^{2}-\Delta^{2}}+i\mathrm{sgn}(\omega)\eta.

When
|\Delta|>|\omega|
, the pole (imaginary part >0) is
\xi_{k}=
i\sqrt{\Delta^{2}-\omega{}^{2}}
;
when
|\Delta|<|\omega|
, the pole (imag part >0) is
\xi_{k}=i\eta
+
sgn(\omega)\sqrt{\omega{}^{2}-\Delta^{2}}
.
Using the theorem of residues, we obtain the unperturbed Green’s function
of the BCS superconductor as

\displaystyle g_{\mathrm{R}}^{R}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\left[-\theta(|\Delta|-|\omega|)\pi\rho_{R}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Delta^{2}-\omega{}^{2}}}-i\theta(|\omega|-|\Delta|)\pi\rho_{R}\frac{sgn(\omega)}{\sqrt{\omega{}^{2}-\Delta^{2}}}\right]\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\omega&\Delta\\
\Delta&\omega\\
&&\omega&-\Delta\\
&&-\Delta&\omega\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=-i\pi\rho_{R}\beta\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}1&\frac{\Delta}{\omega}\\
\frac{\Delta}{\omega}&1\\
&&1&-\frac{\Delta}{\omega}\\
&&-\frac{\Delta}{\omega}&1\end{array}\right),

where
\beta(\omega)=\theta(|\Delta|-|\omega|)\frac{\omega}{i\sqrt{\Delta^{2}-\omega{}^{2}}}+\theta(|\omega|-|\Delta|)\frac{|\omega|}{\sqrt{\omega{}^{2}-\Delta^{2}}}
.
The spectral function of the superconducting lead is given by

A_{\mathrm{R}}(\omega)=i(G_{\mathrm{R}}^{R}(\omega)-G_{\mathrm{R}}^{A}(\omega))=-2\mathrm{Im}G_{\mathrm{R}}^{R}(\omega)

with

G_{\mathrm{R}}^{R}(\omega)=\left(\left(g_{\mathrm{R}}^{R}(\omega)\right)^{-1}-\Sigma_{\mathrm{QD}}^{R}(\omega)\right)^{-1}.

The self-energy at the origin (
r=0
) can be calculated as

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\mathrm{QD}}^{R}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{T,R}}^{\dagger}G_{\mathrm{QD}}^{R}(\omega)\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{T,R}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}|\nu_{\mathrm{R,k}}|^{2}G_{\mathrm{QD}}^{R}(\omega)

\displaystyle=2\pi\rho_{\mathrm{R}}|\nu_{\mathrm{R,r=0}}|^{2}G_{\mathrm{QD}}^{R}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{\mathrm{R}}G_{\mathrm{QD}}^{R}(\omega),

where we have used Fourier transform
|\nu_{\mathrm{R,r}}|^{2}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{k}e^{ikr}|\nu_{\mathrm{R,k}}|^{2}
.
The density of states
\rho_{\mathrm{R}}
around the Fermi-surface
is approximately regarded as a constant. The effective Green’s function
of the QD in the subsystem "quantum dot + Majorana Y-junction+Normal-metal
lead" is given by

G_{\mathrm{QD}}^{R}(\omega)=\left(\left(g_{\mathrm{QD}}^{R}(\omega)\right)^{-1}-\Sigma_{\mathrm{L}}^{R}(\omega)-\Sigma_{\mathrm{Y}}^{R}(\omega)\right)^{-1}.

Hence the LDOS of the superconducting lead is

\rho(\omega)=\mathrm{Tr}\left[A_{\mathrm{R}}(\omega)\right]/2\pi.

We consider the Hamiltonian of a multi-terminal systems with a noninteracting

(45)

where
H_{\mathrm{\eta}}=\sum_{k\sigma}\epsilon_{\mathrm{\eta},k\sigma}a_{\mathrm{\mathrm{\eta}},k\sigma}^{\dagger}a_{\mathrm{\mathrm{\eta}},k\sigma}

and
H_{\mathrm{QD}}=\sum_{\sigma}\epsilon_{\mathrm{d}}d_{\sigma}^{\dagger}d_{\sigma}
.

The tunneling Hamiltonian is given by

H_{T}=\sum_{\eta k\sigma}(t_{k\sigma}a_{\mathrm{\mathrm{\eta}},k\sigma}^{\dagger}d_{\sigma}+t^{*}d_{\sigma}^{\dagger}a_{\mathrm{\mathrm{\eta}},k\sigma}),

(46)

where
t_{k\sigma}
is the tunneling amplitude between the leads

\eta
and the QD. The definition of the shot noise is given by

\displaystyle S_{\eta\eta^{\prime}}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\hbar\left\langle\{\delta\hat{I}_{\eta}(t),\delta\hat{I}_{\eta^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\hbar\Biggl\langle\{\hat{I}_{\eta}(t),\hat{I}_{\eta^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\}\Biggr\rangle-2\hbar\Biggl\langle I_{\eta}(t)\Biggr\rangle\Biggl\langle I_{\eta^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\Biggr\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{e^{2}}{\hbar}\sum_{kk^{\prime}\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\Biggl\{t_{k\sigma}t_{k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}\Biggl\langle a_{\eta k\sigma}^{\dagger}(t)d_{\sigma}(t)a_{{}^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}k^{\prime}\sigma}^{\dagger}(t^{\prime})d_{\sigma^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\Biggr\rangle

\displaystyle\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace-t_{k\sigma}t_{k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}^{*}\Biggl\langle a_{\eta k\sigma}^{\dagger}(t)d_{\sigma}(t)d_{\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}(t^{\prime})a_{\eta^{\prime}k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\Biggr\rangle

\displaystyle\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace-t_{k\sigma}^{*}t_{k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}\Biggl\langle d_{\sigma}^{\dagger}(t)a_{\eta k\sigma}(t)a_{\eta^{\prime}k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}(t^{\prime})d_{\sigma^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\Biggr\rangle

\displaystyle\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace+t_{k\sigma}^{*}t_{k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}^{*}\Biggl\langle d_{\sigma}^{\dagger}(t)a_{\eta k\sigma}(t)d_{\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}(t^{\prime})a_{\eta^{\prime}k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\Biggr\rangle\Biggr\}+\mathrm{H.C.}-2\hbar\Biggl\langle I_{\eta}(t)\Biggr\rangle\Biggl\langle I_{\eta^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\Biggr\rangle.

(47)

Using the notation for four-type of the two-particle Green’s functions

\displaystyle G_{1}^{(2)}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=i^{2}\Biggl\langle T_{C}a_{\eta k\sigma}^{\dagger}(\tau)d_{\sigma}(\tau)a_{\eta^{\prime}k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}(\tau^{\prime})d_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau^{\prime})\Biggr\rangle,

(48)

\displaystyle G_{2}^{(2)}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=i^{2}\Biggl\langle T_{C}a_{\eta k\sigma}^{\dagger}(\tau)d_{\sigma}(\tau)d_{\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}(\tau^{\prime})a_{\eta^{\prime}k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau^{\prime})\Biggr\rangle,

(49)

\displaystyle G_{3}^{(2)}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=i^{2}\Biggl\langle T_{C}d_{\sigma}^{\dagger}(\tau)a_{\eta k\sigma}(\tau)a_{\eta^{\prime}k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}(\tau^{\prime})d_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau^{\prime})\Biggr\rangle,

(50)

\displaystyle G_{4}^{(2)}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=i^{2}\Biggl\langle T_{C}d_{\sigma}^{\dagger}(\tau)a_{\eta k\sigma}(\tau)d_{\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}(\tau^{\prime})a_{\eta^{\prime}k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau^{\prime})\Biggr\rangle,

(51)

the shot noise can be expressed as

\displaystyle S_{\eta\eta^{\prime}}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{2}}{\hbar}\sum_{kk^{\prime},\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\Biggl\{t_{k\sigma}t_{k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}G_{1}^{(2)>}(t,t^{\prime})-t_{k\sigma}t_{k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}^{*}G_{2}^{(2)>}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace-t_{k\sigma}^{*}t_{k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}G_{3}^{(2)>}(t,t^{\prime})+t_{k\sigma}^{*}t_{k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}^{*}G_{4}^{(2)>}(t,t^{\prime})\Biggr\}+\mathrm{H.C.}-2\hbar\Biggl\langle I_{\eta}(t)\Biggr\rangle\Biggl\langle I_{\eta^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\Biggr\rangle,

(52)

where
G_{i}^{(2)>}(t,t^{\prime})
can be obtained from
G_{i}^{(2)}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

via analtyical continuation. The expression of
G_{i}^{(2)}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

can be reduced by using the S-matrix expansion and Wick’s theorem,
and more details can be found in Ref.
(
Souza et al. 2008
)
.
After the reduction of
G_{i}^{(2)}
, we can obtain,

\displaystyle G_{1}^{(2)}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=t_{k\sigma}^{*}t_{k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}^{*}\int\int\mathrm{d}\tau_{1}\mathrm{d}\tau_{2}G_{\eta,k\sigma\sigma}(\tau_{1},\tau)G_{\eta^{\prime},k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau_{2},\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\times\left[G_{\mathrm{QD},\sigma\sigma}(\tau,\tau_{1})G_{\mathrm{QD},\sigma^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau^{\prime},\tau_{2})-G_{\mathrm{QD},\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau,\tau_{2})G_{\mathrm{QD},\sigma^{\prime}\sigma}(\tau^{\prime},\tau_{1})\right],

(53)

\displaystyle G_{2}^{(2)}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\delta_{kk^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}\eta\eta^{\prime}}G_{\eta,k\sigma^{\prime}\sigma}(\tau^{\prime},\tau)G_{\mathrm{QD},\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace+t_{k\sigma}^{*}t_{k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}\int\int\mathrm{d}\tau_{1}\mathrm{d}\tau_{2}G_{\eta,k\sigma\sigma}(\tau_{1},\tau)G_{\eta^{\prime},k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau^{\prime},\tau_{2})

\displaystyle\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\times\left[G_{\mathrm{QD},\sigma\sigma}(\tau,\tau_{1})G_{\mathrm{QD},\sigma^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau_{2},\tau^{\prime})-G_{\mathrm{QD},\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})G_{\mathrm{QD},\sigma^{\prime}\sigma}(\tau_{2},\tau_{1})\right],

(54)

\displaystyle G_{3}^{(2)}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\left[G_{2}^{(2)}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\right]^{*},

(55)

\displaystyle G_{4}^{(2)}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\left[G_{1}^{(2)}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\right]^{*},

(56)

where
G_{\eta,k\sigma\sigma}(\tau_{1},\tau)=-i\Biggl\langle T_{C}a_{\eta k\sigma}(\tau_{1})a_{\eta k\sigma}^{\dagger}(\tau)\Biggr\rangle

and
G_{\mathrm{QD},\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau,\tau_{2})=-i\Biggl\langle T_{C}d_{\sigma}(\tau)d_{\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}(\tau_{2})\Biggr\rangle
.
The two-particle Green’s functions above can be decomposed into the
connected and disconnected terms, i.e.,
G_{i}^{(2)}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=G_{i,\mathrm{disc}}^{(2)}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})+G_{i,\mathrm{conn}}^{(2)}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})
.
The disconnected terms are given by

\displaystyle G_{1,\mathrm{disc}}^{(2)}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=t_{k\sigma}^{*}t_{k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}^{*}\int\mathrm{d}\tau_{1}G_{\mathrm{QD},\sigma\sigma}(\tau,\tau_{1})G_{\eta,k\sigma\sigma}(\tau_{1},\tau^{+})\int\mathrm{d}\tau_{2}G_{\mathrm{QD},\sigma^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau^{\prime},\tau_{2})G_{\eta^{\prime},k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau_{2},\tau^{\prime+}),

(57)

\displaystyle G_{2,\mathrm{disc}}^{(2)}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=t_{k\sigma}^{*}t_{k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}\int\mathrm{d}\tau_{1}G_{\mathrm{QD},\sigma\sigma}(\tau,\tau_{1})G_{\eta,k\sigma\sigma}(\tau_{1},\tau^{+})\int\mathrm{d}\tau_{2}G_{\eta^{\prime},k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau^{\prime},\tau_{2})G_{\mathrm{QD},\sigma^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau_{2},\tau^{\prime+}),

(58)

\displaystyle G_{3,\mathrm{disc}}^{(2)}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\left[G_{2,\mathrm{disc}}^{(2)}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\right]^{*},

(59)

\displaystyle G_{4,\mathrm{disc}}^{(2)}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\left[G_{1,\mathrm{disc}}^{(2)}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\right].^{*}

(60)

The analytic continuation rules give

\displaystyle G_{1,\mathrm{disc}}^{(2)>}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=t_{k\sigma}^{*}t_{k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}^{*}F_{\eta,k\sigma}(t,t)F_{\eta^{\prime},k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(t^{\prime},t^{\prime}),

(61)

\displaystyle G_{2,\mathrm{disc}}^{(2)>}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-t_{k\sigma}^{*}t_{k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}F_{\eta,k\sigma}(t,t)F_{\eta^{\prime},k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}^{*}(t^{\prime},t^{\prime}),

(62)

\displaystyle G_{3,\mathrm{disc}}^{(2)>}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-t_{k\sigma}t_{k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}^{*}F_{\eta,k\sigma}^{*}(t,t)F_{\eta^{\prime},k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(t^{\prime},t^{\prime}),

(63)

\displaystyle G_{4,\mathrm{disc}}^{(2)>}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=t_{k\sigma}t_{k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}F_{\eta,k\sigma}^{*}(t,t)F_{\eta^{\prime},k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}^{*}(t^{\prime},t^{\prime}),

(64)

where

F_{\eta,k\sigma}(t,t)=\int\mathrm{d}t_{1}G_{\mathrm{QD},\sigma\sigma}^{R}(t,t_{1})G_{\eta,k\sigma\sigma}^{<}(t_{1},t)+G_{\mathrm{QD},\sigma\sigma}^{<}(t,t_{1})G_{\eta,k\sigma\sigma}^{A}(t_{1},t).

(65)

The total contribution of the disconnected terms is

\displaystyle\Biggl\langle\{\hat{I}_{\eta}(t),\hat{I}_{\eta^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\}\Biggr\rangle_{\mathrm{disc}}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{2}}{\hbar^{2}}\sum_{kk^{\prime}\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\Biggl\{t_{k\sigma}t_{k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}G_{1,\mathrm{disc}}^{(2)>}(t,t^{\prime})-t_{k\sigma}t_{k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}^{*}G_{2,\mathrm{disc}}^{(2)>}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace-t_{k\sigma}^{*}t_{k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}G_{3,\mathrm{disc}}^{(2)>}(t,t^{\prime})+t_{k\sigma}^{*}t_{k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}^{*}G_{4,\mathrm{disc}}^{(2)>}(t,t^{\prime})\Biggr\}+\mathrm{H.C.}

\displaystyle=2\frac{e^{2}}{\hbar^{2}}\sum_{kk^{\prime}\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}|t_{k\sigma}|^{2}|t_{k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}|^{2}\left[F_{\eta,k\sigma}(t,t)+F_{\eta,k\sigma}^{*}(t,t)\right]\left[F_{\eta^{\prime},k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(t^{\prime},t^{\prime})+F_{\eta^{\prime},k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}^{*}(t^{\prime},t^{\prime})\right].

(66)

Note that
\Sigma_{\eta,k\sigma\sigma}^{<}=|t_{k\sigma}|^{2}G_{\eta,k\sigma\sigma}^{<}(t_{1},t)
,
the time-average current can be written as

\Biggl\langle\hat{I}_{\eta}(t)\Biggr\rangle=\frac{e}{\hbar}\sum_{k\sigma}\int\mathrm{d}t_{1}\left[G_{\mathrm{QD},\sigma\sigma}^{R}(t,t_{1})\Sigma_{\eta,k\sigma\sigma}^{<}+G_{\mathrm{QD},\sigma\sigma}^{<}(t,t_{1})\Sigma_{\eta,k\sigma\sigma}^{A}\right]+\mathrm{H.C.},

and hence,

\Biggl\langle\hat{I}_{\eta}(t)\Biggr\rangle\Biggl\langle\hat{I}_{\eta^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\Biggr\rangle=2\frac{e^{2}}{\hbar^{2}}\sum_{kk^{\prime}\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}|t_{k\sigma}|^{2}|t_{k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}|^{2}\left[F_{\eta,k\sigma}(t,t)+F_{\eta,k\sigma}^{*}(t,t)\right]\left[F_{\eta^{\prime},k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(t^{\prime},t^{\prime})+F_{\eta^{\prime},k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}^{*}(t^{\prime},t^{\prime})\right].

(67)

Therefore, the disconnected part of the shot noise (C22) and (C23)
are canceled out:

\Biggl\langle\{\hat{I}_{\eta}(t),\hat{I}_{\eta^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\}\Biggr\rangle_{\mathrm{disc}}-2\Biggl\langle\hat{I}_{\eta}(t)\Biggr\rangle\Biggl\langle\hat{I}_{\eta^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\Biggr\rangle=0.

(68)

As the result, the remained part in the shot noise is only expressed
by the connected part by

\displaystyle S_{\eta\eta^{\prime}}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\hbar\Biggl\langle\{\hat{I}_{\eta}(t),\hat{I}_{\eta^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\}\Biggr\rangle_{\mathrm{conn}}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{2}}{\hbar}\sum_{kk^{\prime},\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\Biggl\{t_{k\sigma}t_{k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}G_{1,\mathrm{\mathrm{conn}}}^{(2)>}(t,t^{\prime})-t_{k\sigma}t_{k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}^{*}G_{2,\mathrm{\mathrm{conn}}}^{(2)>}(t,t^{\prime})-t_{k\sigma}^{*}t_{k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}G_{3,\mathrm{\mathrm{conn}}}^{(2)>}(t,t^{\prime})+t_{k\sigma}^{*}t_{k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}^{*}G_{4,\mathrm{conn}}^{(2)>}(t,t^{\prime})\Biggr\}+\mathrm{H.C.}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{2}}{\hbar}\sum_{k,\sigma}|t_{k\sigma}|^{2}\delta_{\eta,\eta^{\prime}}\left[G_{\eta,k\sigma\sigma}(t^{\prime},t)G_{\mathrm{QD},\sigma\sigma}(t,t^{\prime})+G_{\eta,k\sigma\sigma}(t,t^{\prime})G_{\mathrm{QD},\sigma\sigma}(t^{\prime},t)\right]^{>}

\displaystyle\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace-\frac{e^{2}}{\hbar}\sum_{kk^{\prime},\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}|t_{k\sigma}|^{2}|t_{k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}|^{2}\Biggl\{\left[\int\mathrm{d}t_{1}G_{\mathrm{QD},\sigma^{\prime}\sigma}(t^{\prime},t_{1})G_{\eta,k\sigma\sigma}(t_{1},t)\int\mathrm{d}t_{2}G_{\mathrm{QD},\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(t,t_{2})G_{\eta^{\prime},k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(t_{2},t^{\prime})\right]^{>}

\displaystyle\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace-\left[G_{\mathrm{QD},\sigma^{\prime}\sigma}(t,t^{\prime})\int\int\mathrm{d}t_{1}\mathrm{d}t_{2}G_{\eta,k\sigma\sigma}(t_{1},t)G_{\mathrm{QD},\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(t_{2},t_{1})G_{\eta^{\prime},k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(t^{\prime},t_{2})\right]^{>}

\displaystyle\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace-\left[G_{\mathrm{QD},\sigma^{\prime}\sigma}(t^{\prime},t)\int\int\mathrm{d}t_{1}\mathrm{d}t_{2}G_{\eta,k\sigma\sigma}(t,t_{1})G_{\mathrm{QD},\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(t_{1},t_{2},)G_{\eta^{\prime},k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(t_{2},t^{\prime})\right]^{>}

\displaystyle\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace+\left[\int\mathrm{d}t_{1}G_{\mathrm{QD},\sigma^{\prime}\sigma}(t_{1},t^{\prime})G_{\eta,k\sigma\sigma}(t,t_{1})\int\mathrm{d}\tau_{2}G_{\mathrm{QD},\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(t_{2},t)G_{\eta^{\prime},k^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(t^{\prime},t_{2})\right]^{>}\Biggr\}+\mathrm{H.C.}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{2}}{\hbar}\textrm{Tr}\Biggl\{\delta_{\eta,\eta^{\prime}}(\Sigma_{\eta}^{>}(t^{\prime},t)\widetilde{\sigma}_{z}G_{\mathrm{QD}}^{<}(t,t^{\prime})\widetilde{\sigma}_{z}+G_{\mathrm{QD}}^{>}(t^{\prime},t)\widetilde{\sigma}_{z}\Sigma_{\eta}^{<}(t,t^{\prime})\widetilde{\sigma}_{z})

\displaystyle\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace-[\int\mathrm{d}t_{1}G_{\mathrm{QD}}(t^{\prime},t_{1})\Sigma_{\eta}(t_{1},t)]^{>}\widetilde{\sigma}_{z}[\int\mathrm{d}t_{2}G_{\mathrm{QD}}(t,t_{2})\Sigma_{\eta^{\prime}}(t_{2},t^{\prime})]^{<}\widetilde{\sigma}_{z}

\displaystyle\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace+[G_{\mathrm{QD}}(t,t^{\prime})]^{>}\widetilde{\sigma}_{z}[\int\int\mathrm{d}t_{1}\mathrm{d}t_{2}\Sigma_{\eta}(t_{1},t)G_{\mathrm{QD}}(t_{2},t_{1},)\Sigma_{\eta^{\prime}}(t^{\prime},t_{2})]^{<}\widetilde{\sigma}_{z}

\displaystyle\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace+[\int\int\mathrm{d}t_{1}\mathrm{d}t_{2}\Sigma_{\eta}(t,t_{1})G_{\mathrm{QD}}(t_{1},t_{2},)\Sigma_{\eta^{\prime}}(t_{2},t^{\prime})]^{>}\widetilde{\sigma}_{z}[G_{\mathrm{QD}}(t^{\prime},t)]^{<}\widetilde{\sigma}_{z}

\displaystyle\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace-[\int\mathrm{d}t_{1}G_{\mathrm{QD}}(t_{1},t^{\prime})\Sigma_{\eta}(t,t_{1})]^{>}\widetilde{\sigma}_{z}[\int\mathrm{d}t_{2}G_{\mathrm{QD}}(t_{2},t)\Sigma_{\eta^{\prime}}(t^{\prime},t_{2})]^{<}\widetilde{\sigma}_{z},

(69)

where
G_{\mathrm{QD}}
is the
4\times
4 matrix form of the elements

G_{\mathrm{QD},\sigma^{\prime}\sigma}
and
\Sigma_{\eta}
is the
4\times
4
matrix form of the elements
\Sigma_{\eta,\sigma\sigma}=\sum_{k}|t_{k\sigma}|^{2}G_{\eta,k\sigma\sigma}
.
The matrix
\widetilde{\sigma}_{z}=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,1,-1)
describes
the different charge of electrons and holes. Finally, we apply the
convolution property of Fourier transform
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}(t-t^{\prime})e^{i\omega^{\prime}(t-t^{\prime})}x(t-t^{\prime})y(t^{\prime}-t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\omega F[x](\omega)F[y](\omega+\omega^{\prime})

to the above shot noise and obtain

\displaystyle S_{\eta\eta^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}(t-t^{\prime})e^{i\omega^{\prime}(t-t^{\prime})}S_{\eta\eta^{\prime}}(t-t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{2}}{h}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\omega\textrm{Tr}\{\delta_{\eta,\eta^{\prime}}(\Sigma_{\eta}^{>}(\omega)\widetilde{\sigma}_{z}G_{\mathrm{QD}}^{<}(\omega+\omega^{\prime})\widetilde{\sigma}_{z}+G_{\mathrm{QD}}^{>}(\omega)\widetilde{\sigma}_{z}\Sigma_{\eta}^{<}(\omega+\omega^{\prime})\widetilde{\sigma}_{z})

\displaystyle\thinspace\thinspace-[G_{\mathrm{QD}}(\omega)\Sigma_{\eta^{\prime}}(\omega)]^{>}\widetilde{\sigma}_{z}[G_{\mathrm{QD}}(\omega+\omega^{\prime})\Sigma_{\eta}(\omega+\omega^{\prime})]^{<}\widetilde{\sigma}_{z}-[\Sigma_{\eta}(\omega)G_{\mathrm{QD}}(\omega)]^{>}\widetilde{\sigma}_{z}[\Sigma_{\eta^{\prime}}(\omega+\omega^{\prime})G_{\mathrm{QD}}(\omega+\omega^{\prime})]^{<}\widetilde{\sigma}_{z}

\displaystyle\thinspace\thinspace+G_{\mathrm{QD}}^{>}(\omega)\widetilde{\sigma}_{z}[\Sigma_{\eta^{\prime}}(\omega+\omega^{\prime})G_{\mathrm{QD}}(\omega+\omega^{\prime})\Sigma_{\eta}(\omega+\omega^{\prime})]^{<}\widetilde{\sigma}_{z}+[\Sigma_{\eta}(\omega)G_{\mathrm{QD}}(\omega)\Sigma_{\eta^{\prime}}(\omega)]^{>}\widetilde{\sigma}_{z}G_{\mathrm{QD}}^{<}(\omega+\omega^{\prime})\widetilde{\sigma}_{z}\}.

(70)

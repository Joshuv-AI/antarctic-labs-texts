On the relation of the frame-related operators of fusion frame systems
Lukas Köhldorfer, Peter Balazs

Abstract
Frames have been investigated frequently over the last few decades due to their valuable properties, which are desirable for various applications as well as interesting for theory. Some applications additionally require distributed processing techniques, which naturally leads to the concept of fusion frames and fusion frame systems. The latter consists of a system of subspaces, equipped with local frames on each of them, and a global frame. In this paper, we investigate the relations of the associated frame-related operators on all those three levels. For that we provide a detailed investigation on bounded block diagonal operators between Hilbert direct sums. We give the relation of the frame-related operators of the fusion frame and the corresponding frame systems in terms of operator identities. By applying these identities we prove further properties of fusion frame systems.

On all three layers - subspace, local and global frames - of a fusion frame system, the typical frame-related operators, like the analysis, synthesis and frame operator, can be defined. In this paper we study the relation between these operators associated to a fusion frame system. For that purpose we provide details for bounded block diagonal operators between Hilbert direct sums. In particular, we show that for those operators we get canonical combinations, like combining the fusion synthesis with the block diagonal operator of the local synthesis operators resulting in the global synthesis operator. We show that a global Riesz basis results in a fusion Riesz basis with local Riesz bases, a result where only the opposite direction is known so far. We can extend the answer to the question, when centralized and distributed reconstruction coincide, to the case of a fusion Riesz basis. Finally, we give a collection of results on properties, which are preserved in fusion frame systems.

f=(f_{i})_{i\in I}
,
g=(g_{i})_{i\in I}\in(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus V_{i})_{\ell^{2}}
, the operation

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle_{(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus V_{i})_{\ell^{2}}}:=\sum_{i\in I}\langle f_{i},g_{i}\rangle_{V_{i}}

(9)

is easily seen to be well-defined and inherits the defining properties of an inner product from the inner products
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{V_{i}}
, see also
conw1
. Its induced norm is therefore given by

\|f\|_{(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus V_{i})_{\ell^{2}}}=\Big(\sum_{i\in I}\|f_{i}\|_{V_{i}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

(10)

and adapting any standard completeness proof for
\ell^{2}(I)
yields that
(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus V_{i})_{\ell^{2}}
is also complete with respect to this norm.

Of course, definition (
8
) extends to the case where the spaces
V_{i}
are only pre-Hilbert spaces, resulting in
(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus V_{i})_{\ell^{2}}
equipped with the inner product (
9
) being only a pre-Hilbert space. If and only if all
V_{i}
are Hilbert spaces, the space
(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus V_{i})_{\ell^{2}}
is a Hilbert space, in which case we call it a
Hilbert direct sum
. In particular, since subspaces of Hilbert spaces are closed if and only if they are complete, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 3.1
.

Consider the Hilbert direct sum
\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus V_{i}\big)_{\ell^{2}}
and for each
i\in I
, let
U_{i}
be a subspace of
V_{i}
. Then
\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus U_{i}\big)_{\ell^{2}}
is a closed subspace of
\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus V_{i}\big)_{\ell^{2}}
if and only if
U_{i}
is a closed subspace of
V_{i}
for every
i\in I
.

The next result is also easy to show, but still worthwhile to be mentioned.

Lemma 3.2
.

Consider the Hilbert direct sum
\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus V_{i}\big)_{\ell^{2}}
and the (not necessarily closed) subspaces
U_{i}
of
V_{i}
(
i\in I
) and
\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus U_{i}\big)_{\ell^{2}}
of
\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus V_{i}\big)_{\ell^{2}}
. Then

\displaystyle\bigg(\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus U_{i}\big)_{\ell^{2}}\bigg)^{\perp}=\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus U_{i}^{\perp}\big)_{\ell^{2}}.

Proof.

The ”
\supseteq
”-part is trivial. To prove the ”
\subseteq
”-part, we have to show that for any
h=(h_{i})_{i\in I}\in\left((\sum_{i\in I}\oplus U_{i})_{\ell^{2}}\right)^{\perp}
we have
h_{i}\in U_{i}^{\perp}
(for all
i\in I
). To this end, observe that since
\langle f,h\rangle_{(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus V_{i})_{\ell^{2}}}=0
for all
f\in\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus U_{i}\big)_{\ell^{2}}
, we particularly may choose
f=(...,0,0,f_{i},0,0,...)\in\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus U_{i}\big)_{\ell^{2}}
(where
f_{i}\in U_{i}
is in the
i
-th entry) and see that in this case
0=\langle f,h\rangle_{(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus V_{i})_{\ell^{2}}}=\langle f_{i},h_{i}\rangle_{V_{i}}
, which implies
h_{i}\in U_{i}^{\perp}
. Since this holds for all
i\in I
, the proof is finished.
∎

Next we consider two Hilbert direct sums
\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus V_{i}\big)_{\ell^{2}}
and
\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus W_{i}\big)_{\ell^{2}}
indexed by the same index set. Let, for every
i\in I
,
\mathcal{O}_{i}\in\mathcal{B}(V_{i},W_{i})
be given. We call the family
(\mathcal{O}_{i})_{i\in I}

completely bounded

paulsen_2003
, if there exists a constant
C>0
, such that
\|\mathcal{O}_{i}\|\leq C
for all
i\in I
. It is easy to show the next lemma, see also
conw1
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Consider the family
(\mathcal{O}_{i})_{i\in I}
of operators
\mathcal{O}_{i}\in\mathcal{B}(V_{i},W_{i})
. Then

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}(f_{i})_{i\in I}:=(\mathcal{O}_{i}f_{i})_{i\in I}

(11)

defines a well-defined and bounded operator from
\left(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus V_{i}\right)_{\ell^{2}}
into
\left(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus W_{i}\right)_{\ell^{2}}
if and only if
(\mathcal{O}_{i})_{i\in I}
is completely bounded. In that case
\|\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}\|=\sup_{i\in I}\|\mathcal{O}_{i}\|
.

Bounded operators
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}
, defined as in (
11
), naturally appear in the definition of a
dual fusion frame

kutpatphi17
;
Gav07
;
heimoanza14
;
hemo14
;
heimo18
. In accordance with these references we call such operators
block diagonal
. We also note that in those references, block diagonal operators
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}
with the additional property, that each operator
\mathcal{O}_{i}
is surjective, are called
component preserving
. In the remainder of this section, a detailed investigation of bounded block diagonal operators between Hilbert direct sums is presented, whereas component preserving operators only implicitly appear on some occasions as a special case, see e.g. Proposition
3.8
.

We omit the obvious proof of the next result. For more details, see
kohl21
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus U_{i})_{\ell^{2}}
,
(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus V_{i})_{\ell^{2}}
and
(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus W_{i})_{\ell^{2}}
be Hilbert direct sums, let
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}\in\mathcal{B}\big((\sum_{i\in I}\oplus V_{i})_{\ell^{2}},(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus W_{i})_{\ell^{2}}\big)
and
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{P}_{i}\in\mathcal{B}\big((\sum_{i\in I}\oplus U_{i})_{\ell^{2}},(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus V_{i})_{\ell^{2}}\big)
. Then the following hold.

(a)

\displaystyle\big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}\big)^{*}=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}^{*}_{i}

(b)

If
(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus V_{i})_{\ell^{2}}=(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus W_{i})_{\ell^{2}}
, then
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}
is self-adjoint if and only if
\mathcal{O}_{i}
is self-adjoint for every
i\in I
.

(c)

\displaystyle\big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}\big)\big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{P}_{i}\big)=\bigoplus_{i\in I}(\mathcal{O}_{i}\mathcal{P}_{i}).

(d)

\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}
is bounded from below by
m
if and only if
\mathcal{O}_{i}
is bounded from below by
m
for all
i\in I
.

Next, we consider the kernel and range of bounded block diagonal operators, where we notice a difference in the quality of the results.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}\in\mathcal{B}\big((\sum_{i\in I}\oplus V_{i})_{\ell^{2}},(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus W_{i})_{\ell^{2}}\big)
. Then

(a)

\mathcal{N}\big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}\big)=\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{O}_{i})\big)_{\ell^{2}}

(b)

\mathcal{R}\big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}\big)\subseteq\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{O}_{i})\big)_{\ell^{2}}

Proof.

By component-wise definition (
11
) we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}\big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}\big)

\displaystyle=\Big\{(f_{i})_{i\in I}\in\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus V_{i}\big)_{\ell^{2}}:\,\mathcal{O}_{i}f_{i}=0\,(\forall i\in I)\Big\}

\displaystyle=\Big\{(f_{i})_{i\in I}:f_{i}\in\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{O}_{i})\,(\forall i\in I),\,\sum_{i\in I}\|f_{i}\|_{V_{i}}^{2}<\infty\Big\}

\displaystyle=\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus\mathcal{N}(O_{i})\big)_{\ell^{2}},

as well as

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}\big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}\big)

\displaystyle=\Big\{(g_{i})_{i\in I}\in\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus W_{i}\big)_{\ell^{2}}:\exists(f_{i})_{i\in I}\in\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus V_{i}\big)_{\ell^{2}}:\mathcal{O}_{i}f_{i}=g_{i}\,(\forall i\in I)\Big\}

\displaystyle\subseteq\Big\{(g_{i})_{i\in I}\in\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus W_{i}\big)_{\ell^{2}}:\exists(f_{i})_{i\in I}\in(V_{i})_{i\in I}:\mathcal{O}_{i}f_{i}=g_{i}\,(\forall i\in I)\Big\}

(12)

\displaystyle=\Big\{(g_{i})_{i\in I}\in\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus W_{i}\big)_{\ell^{2}}:\,g_{i}\in\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{O}_{i})\,(\forall i\in I)\Big\}

\displaystyle=\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{O}_{i})\big)_{\ell^{2}}.

∎

In general, we cannot achieve equality in (
3
) without further assumptions, except in the trivial case
|I|<\infty
(compare to Example
3.9
). However, if
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}
has closed range, then equality is always achieved, as the next result shows.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}\in\mathcal{B}\big((\sum_{i\in I}\oplus V_{i})_{\ell^{2}},(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus W_{i})_{\ell^{2}}\big)
and assume that
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}
has closed range. Then

(a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}\big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}\big)=\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{O}_{i})\big)_{\ell^{2}}.

(b)

\mathcal{O}_{i}
has closed range for every
i\in I
.

Proof.

(a) We have

\displaystyle\overline{\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{O}_{i})\big)_{\ell^{2}}}

\displaystyle=\left(\left(\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{O}_{i})\big)_{\ell^{2}}\right)^{\perp}\right)^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\left(\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{O}_{i})^{\perp}\big)_{\ell^{2}}\right)^{\perp}

(by Lemma
3.2
)

\displaystyle=\left(\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{O}_{i}^{*})\big)_{\ell^{2}}\right)^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{N}\Big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{*}\Big)^{\perp}

(by Lemma
3.5
(a))

\displaystyle=\mathcal{N}\bigg(\Big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}\Big)^{*}\bigg)^{\perp}

(by Lemma
3.4
(a))

\displaystyle=\left(\mathcal{R}\Big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}\Big)^{\perp}\right)^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\overline{\mathcal{R}\big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}\big)}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}\big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}\big)

\displaystyle\subseteq\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{O}_{i})\big)_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\hbox to0.0pt{\hss(by Lemma \ref{Op3} (b))},

which implies (a) and also shows that
\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{O}_{i})\big)_{\ell^{2}}
is a closed subspace of
\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus W_{i}\big)_{\ell^{2}}
. By Corollary
3.1
, this implies (b).
∎

Proposition
3.6
shows, that if
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}
has closed range, so do the operators
\mathcal{O}_{i}
for all
i\in I
. Therefore their corresponding pseudo inverses
\big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}\big)^{\dagger}
and
\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\dagger}
(
i\in I
) are well-defined. In the next result, we give a relation between them.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}\in\mathcal{B}\Big(\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus V_{i}\big)_{\ell^{2}},\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus W_{i}\big)_{\ell^{2}}\Big)
and assume that
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}
has closed range. If, in addition, the family
(\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\dagger})_{i\in I}
is completely bounded and if
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\dagger}
has closed range, then

\displaystyle\Big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}\Big)^{\dagger}=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\dagger}.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.3
, the operators

\Big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}\Big)^{\dagger}:\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus W_{i}\big)_{\ell^{2}}\longrightarrow\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus V_{i}\big)_{\ell^{2}}

and

\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\dagger}:\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus W_{i}\big)_{\ell^{2}}\longrightarrow\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus V_{i}\big)_{\ell^{2}}

both are well-defined and bounded. Moreover, it holds

\big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}\big)\big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\dagger}\big)\big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}\big)=\bigoplus_{i\in I}(\mathcal{O}_{i}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\dagger}\mathcal{O}_{i})=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i},

as well as

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}\big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\dagger}\big)

\displaystyle=\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\dagger})\big)_{\ell^{2}}

(by Lemma
3.5
(a))

\displaystyle=\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus(\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{O}_{i})^{\perp}\big)_{\ell^{2}}

(by (
1
))

\displaystyle=\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{O}_{i})\big)_{\ell^{2}}^{\perp}

(by Lemma
3.2
)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}\big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}\big)^{\perp}

\displaystyle\hbox to0.0pt{\hss(by Proposition \ref{Op4} (a))}.

Finally, the assumption, that
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\dagger}
has closed range, guarantees via Proposition
3.6
(a) that

\mathcal{R}\big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\dagger}\big)=\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\dagger})\big)_{\ell^{2}},

hence

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}\big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\dagger}\big)=\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\dagger})\big)_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle=\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus(\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{O}_{i})^{\perp}\big)_{\ell^{2}}

(by (
1
))

\displaystyle=\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{O}_{i})\big)_{\ell^{2}}^{\perp}

(by Lemma
3.2
)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{N}\big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}\big)^{\perp}

\displaystyle\hbox to0.0pt{\hss(by Lemma \ref{Op3} (a))}.

∎

Next we discuss the properties injectivity, surjectivity and invertibility of block diagonal operators between Hilbert direct sums.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}\in\mathcal{B}\Big(\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus V_{i}\big)_{\ell^{2}},\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus W_{i}\big)_{\ell^{2}}\Big)
. Then

(a)

\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}
is injective if and only if
\mathcal{O}_{i}
is injective for all
i\in I
.

(b)

If
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}
is surjective, then
\mathcal{O}_{i}
is surjective for all
i\in I
.

(c)

If
\mathcal{O}_{i}
is surjective for all
i\in I
and if the family
(\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\dagger})_{i\in I}
is completely bounded, then
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}
is surjective.

(d)

If
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}
is invertible, then
\mathcal{O}_{i}
is invertible for all
i\in I
and

\Big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}\Big)^{-1}=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{-1}.

(13)

(e)

If
\mathcal{O}_{i}
is invertible for all
i\in I
and if
(\mathcal{O}_{i}^{-1})_{i\in I}
is completely bounded, then also
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}
is invertible and (
13
) holds.

Proof.

(a) follows immediately from Lemma
3.5
(a).

(b) Assume that
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}
is surjective and choose
i\in I
arbitrary. Then for any
g_{i}\in W_{i}
,
(...,0,0,g_{i},0,0,...)\in\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus W_{i}\big)_{\ell^{2}}
and by assumption there exists some
(f_{i})_{i\in I}\in\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus V_{i}\big)_{\ell^{2}}
such that
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}(f_{i})_{i\in I}=(...,0,0,g_{i},0,0,...)
. This implies
\mathcal{O}_{i}f_{i}=g_{i}
, hence
\mathcal{O}_{i}
is surjective.

(c) We have
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\dagger}\in\mathcal{B}\big((\sum_{i\in I}\oplus W_{i})_{\ell^{2}},(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus V_{i})_{\ell^{2}}\big)
by Lemma
3.3
. Moreover, by properties of pseudo-inverses,
\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\dagger}
is a right-inverse of
\mathcal{O}_{i}
on
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{O}_{i})=W_{i}
(
i\in I
). Therefore, by component-wise definition and Lemma
3.4
(c),
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\dagger}
is a right-inverse of
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}
on
\big(\sum_{i\in I}\bigoplus W_{i}\big)_{\ell^{2}}
. In particular, for any
(g_{i})_{i\in I}\in\big(\sum_{i\in I}\bigoplus W_{i}\big)_{\ell^{2}}
, we can find
(f_{i})_{i\in I}:=(\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\dagger}g_{i})_{i\in I}\in\big(\sum_{i\in I}\bigoplus V_{i}\big)_{\ell^{2}}
, such that
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}(f_{i})_{i\in I}=(g_{i})_{i\in I}
, i.e.
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}
is surjective.

(d) By (a) and (b),
\mathcal{O}_{i}
is bijective for every
i\in I
. Moreover, since
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}
is invertible, it is bounded from below by some constant
m>0
, which implies that
\mathcal{O}_{i}
is bounded from below by
m
for all
i\in I
. Consequently,
(\mathcal{O}_{i}^{-1})_{i\in I}
is completely bounded by
m^{-1}
, hence
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{-1}
is well-defined and bounded and obviously a left- and right-inverse of
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}
.

(e) Since
(\mathcal{O}_{i}^{-1})_{i\in I}=(\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\dagger})_{i\in I}
is completely bounded, we may apply (a) and (c) to see that
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}
is bijective and thus invertible. Moreover
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{-1}
is well-defined and bounded and (
13
) clearly holds.
∎

Example 3.9
.

Let
I=\mathbb{N}
and let
V_{i}=W_{i}\neq\{0\}
for all
i\in I
. For every
i\in I
, we define

\mathcal{O}_{i}:V_{i}\longrightarrow V_{i},\,\,\,\mathcal{O}_{i}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{i}}\mathcal{I}_{V_{i}}.

For each
i
we have
\|\mathcal{O}_{i}\|\leq 1
, i.e. the family
(\mathcal{O}_{i})_{i\in I}
is completely bounded by
1
. Thus, by Lemma
3.3
,
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}:\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus V_{i}\big)_{\ell^{2}}\longrightarrow\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus V_{i}\big)_{\ell^{2}}
is a well-defined and bounded operator.

(a)

Let us reconsider Lemma
3.5
(b). We give an example of an element
g\in\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus V_{i}\big)_{\ell^{2}}
, which is contained in
\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{O}_{i})\big)_{\ell^{2}}
but not in
\mathcal{R}\big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}\big)
: For every
i\in I
, choose some normalized vector
h_{i}\in V_{i}
and set
f_{i}:=\frac{h_{i}}{\sqrt{i}}\in V_{i}
and
g_{i}:=\frac{h_{i}}{i}\in V_{i}
. Clearly we have
\mathcal{O}_{i}f_{i}=g_{i}
for each
i\in I
. Observe that
g=(g_{i})_{i\in I}\in\left(\sum_{i\in I}\bigoplus V_{i}\right)_{\ell^{2}}
, since
\|g\|^{2}_{(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus V_{i})_{\ell^{2}}}=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{i^{2}}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}
. In particular,
g\in\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{O}_{i})\big)_{\ell^{2}}
. On the other hand,
f=(f_{i})_{i\in I}\not\in(\sum_{i\in I}\bigoplus V_{i})_{\ell^{2}}
, since
\|f\|^{2}_{(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus V_{i})_{\ell^{2}}}=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{i}=\infty
. However,
g\not\in\mathcal{R}\big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}\big)
, since, by component-wise definition,
f
is the only possible candidate to be mapped onto
g
by
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}
, while
f\not\in\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus V_{i}\big)_{\ell^{2}}
.

(b)

We also reconsider Proposition
3.8
(c). At first glance, one might guess that if
(\mathcal{O}_{i})_{i\in I}
is a completely bounded family of surjective operators, then
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}
has to be surjective as well. However, the above example demonstrates the importance of the (in this case missing) condition that
(\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\dagger})_{i\in I}
is completely bounded: The operators
O_{i}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{i}}\mathcal{I}_{V_{i}}
are not only surjective and completely bounded, but also injective. However,
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}
is not surjective as shown above. Observe that
\|\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\dagger}\|=\|\mathcal{O}_{i}^{-1}\|=\sqrt{i}
, i.e. the family
(\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\dagger})_{i\in I}
fails to be completely bounded.

4
Fusion frame systems and related operators

We are now prepared to prove relations between the (fusion) frames associated to a fusion
frame system in terms of their associated synthesis, analysis and frame operators.

4.1
Operator identities for fusion frame systems

Let
(V_{i},v_{i},\varphi^{(i)})_{i\in I}
be a fusion frame system with corresponding global frame
v\varphi=(v_{i}\varphi_{ij})_{i\in I,j\in J_{i}}
. The representation space of
v\varphi
is the space

\ell^{2}\big(\biguplus_{i\in I}J_{i}\big):=\Big\{(c_{ij})_{i\in I,j\in J_{i}}:c_{ij}\in\mathbb{C},\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{j\in J_{i}}|c_{ij}|^{2}<\infty\Big\},

which clearly is a Hilbert space with respect to the inner product
\langle(c_{ij})_{i\in I,j\in J_{i}},(d_{ij})_{i\in I,j\in J_{i}}\rangle=\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{j\in J_{i}}c_{ij}\overline{d_{ij}}
. We observe that norm of
(c_{ij})_{i\in I,j\in J_{i}}\in\ell^{2}(\biguplus_{i\in I}J_{i})
, which is given by

\|(c_{ij})_{i\in I,j\in J_{i}}\|=\big(\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{j\in J_{i}}|c_{ij}|^{2}\big)^{1/2},

equals the norm of
(c_{i})_{i\in I}\in\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus\ell^{2}(J_{i})\big)_{\ell^{2}}
, where
c_{i}=(c_{ij})_{j\in J_{i}}\in\ell^{2}(J_{i})
. In particular, we obtain the following result, see
kohl21
for more details.

Proposition 4.1
.

The Hilbert spaces
\ell^{2}\big(\biguplus_{i\in I}J_{i}\big)
and
\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus\ell^{2}(J_{i})\big)_{\ell^{2}}
are isometrically isomorphic.

Recall, that the definition of a fusion frame system implies that the family
(D_{\varphi^{(i)}})_{i\in I}
is completely bounded by
\sqrt{B}
. This observation begs for an application of our results from Section
3
. Indeed, the link between the global frame

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
(V_{i},v_{i},\varphi^{(i)})_{i\in I}
be a fusion frame system and
v\varphi
be the corresponding global frame. Then the following hold:

\displaystyle D_{v\varphi}

\displaystyle=D_{V}\bigoplus_{i\in I}D_{\varphi^{(i)}}

(14)

\displaystyle C_{v\varphi}

\displaystyle=\big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}C_{\varphi^{(i)}}\big)C_{V}

(15)

\displaystyle S_{v\varphi}

\displaystyle=D_{V}\big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}S_{\varphi^{(i)}}\big)C_{V}.

(16)

Proof.

If
c=(c_{ij})_{i\in I,j\in J_{i}}\in\ell^{2}(\biguplus_{i\in I}J_{i})\cong\left(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus\ell^{2}(J_{i})\right)_{l^{2}}
, then
c_{i}=(c_{ij})_{j\in J_{i}}\in\ell^{2}(J_{i})
for every
i\in I
. Since the family
(D_{\varphi^{(i)}})_{i\in I}
is completely bounded by
\sqrt{B}
, Lemma
3.3
guarantees that
\bigoplus_{i\in I}D_{\varphi^{(i)}}
is well-defined and bounded, i.e. that

(D_{\varphi^{(i)}}c_{i})_{i\in I}\in\big(\sum_{i\in I}\oplus V_{i}\big)_{\ell^{2}}.

Therefore we may write

D_{v\varphi}c=\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{j\in J_{i}}c_{ij}v_{i}\varphi_{ij}=\sum_{i\in I}v_{i}D_{\varphi^{(i)}}c_{i}=D_{V}\bigoplus_{i\in I}D_{\varphi^{(i)}}c,

which shows (
14
). Applying Lemma
3.4
yields

\displaystyle C_{v\varphi}=D^{*}_{v\varphi}

\displaystyle=\Big(D_{V}\bigoplus_{i\in I}D_{\varphi^{(i)}}\Big)^{*}

\displaystyle=\Big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}D_{\varphi^{(i)}}\Big)^{*}D_{V}^{*}

\displaystyle=\Big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}D_{\varphi^{(i)}}^{*}\Big)D_{V}^{*}=\Big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}C_{\varphi^{(i)}}\Big)C_{V}

and

\displaystyle S_{v\varphi}=D_{v\varphi}C_{v\varphi}

\displaystyle=D_{V}\Big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}D_{\varphi^{(i)}}\Big)\Big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}C_{\varphi^{(i)}}\Big)C_{V}

\displaystyle=D_{V}\Big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}D_{\varphi^{(i)}}C_{\varphi^{(i)}}\Big)C_{V}=D_{V}\Big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}S_{\varphi^{(i)}}\Big)C_{V}.

∎

4.2
Properties preserved in fusion frame systems

Our results from Section
4.1
enable us to examine fusion frame systems in terms of their associated frame-related operators. This is particularly interesting, since the properties of these operators are directly linked to the properties of their respective frames. In this spirit, we prove the following characterization by using operator theoretic arguments. We remark that the implication (ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) has been independently proved in

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
(V_{i},v_{i},\varphi^{(i)})_{i\in I}
be a fusion frame system and
v\varphi
be the corresponding global frame. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

v\varphi
is a Riesz basis.

(ii)

(V_{i},v_{i})_{i\in I}
is a fusion Riesz basis and
\varphi^{(i)}
is a Riesz basis for every
i\in I

In particular, if
V=(V_{i},v_{i})_{i\in I}
is a fusion Riesz basis with fusion Riesz bounds
\alpha_{V}
and
\beta_{V}
, then
\alpha_{v\varphi}=\alpha_{V}\cdot A
and
\beta_{v\varphi}=\beta_{V}\cdot B
are Riesz bounds for
v\varphi
. Conversely, if
v\varphi
is a Riesz basis with Riesz bounds
\alpha_{v\varphi}
and
\beta_{v\varphi}
, then

and
\beta_{V}=B_{v\varphi}/A
are fusion Riesz bounds for
V
.

Proof.

Hereinafter, we implicitly apply Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
several times.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii): If
v\varphi
is a Riesz basis, then
D_{v\varphi}=D_{V}\bigoplus_{i\in I}D_{\varphi^{(i)}}
is bounded and bijective. In particular,
\bigoplus_{i\in I}D_{\varphi^{(i)}}
is injective, which implies by Proposition
3.8
that

is bounded and bijective for every

, we now have that

(ii)
\Rightarrow

.

Consider a fusion frame system

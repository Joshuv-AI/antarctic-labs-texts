Introduction and Main result

KPZ equation is an SPDE formally given by

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}h(t,x)=\frac{1}{2}\Delta h(t,x)+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla h(t,x)|^{2}+\beta\dot{\xi}(t,x),

(1.1)

where
\xi
is a time-space white noise on
[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. This SPDE is ill-posed due to the non-linear term
\nabla h
which should be a generalized function.

For
d=1
, Bertini and Giacomin formulated the solution of (
1.1
) via Cole-Hopf solution
h=\log u

[
6
]
, where
u
is the solution of stochastic heat equation

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u(t,x)=\frac{1}{2}\Delta u(t,x)+\beta u(t,x)\dot{\xi}(t,x).

(1.2)

In dimension
d=2
, we consider a space-regularized multiplicative stochastic heat equation but we need to scale the disorder strength:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon}}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{2}\Delta u_{\varepsilon}+\beta_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\dot{\xi}_{\varepsilon},\hskip 8.50012ptu_{\varepsilon}(0,x)=u_{0}(x)

(1.3)

where
\beta_{\varepsilon}=\hat{\beta}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{-\log\varepsilon}}
with
\hat{\beta}\geq 0
, and
\xi_{\varepsilon}
is a mollification in space of
\xi
such that
\xi_{\varepsilon}\Rightarrow\xi
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, i.e.,

\dot{\xi}_{\varepsilon}(t,x)=(\dot{\xi}(t,\cdot)\star\phi_{\varepsilon})(x)=\int\phi_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\dot{\xi}(t,y)\text{\rm d}y,

with
\phi_{\varepsilon}({x})=\varepsilon^{-2}\phi(\varepsilon^{-1}x)
and
\phi
being a smooth, non-negative, compactly supported, symmetric function on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, so that
\int\phi(x)\text{\rm d}x=1

. Let

satisfies the SPDE

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}h_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{2}\Delta h_{\varepsilon}+\bigg[\frac{1}{2}|\nabla h_{\varepsilon}|^{2}-C_{\varepsilon}\bigg]+\beta_{\varepsilon}\dot{\xi}_{\varepsilon}\;,\hskip 8.50012pt\,\,h_{\varepsilon}(0,x)=h_{0}(x),

(1.4)

where

is a diverging parameter:

(1.5)

Caravenna, Sun, and Zygouras proved that if the initial condition is flat, that is

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi s}\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}V(y)e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4s}}\text{\rm d}y\leq\frac{1}{4\pi s}\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}V(y)\text{\rm d}y=\frac{1}{4\pi s}.

as
\varepsilon\to 0
, where the convergence is absolute since
\hat{\beta}\hat{\gamma}<1
.

Next, we consider the lower bound. Let

(2.9)

For fixed

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in D_{V}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\bar{r}_{s}(x,y)\text{\rm d}y

(2.11)

as
\varepsilon\to 0

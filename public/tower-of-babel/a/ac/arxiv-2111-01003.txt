Conic bundle structures on ℚ \mathbb{Q} -Fano threefolds Thanks: The paper was partially supported by the HSE University Basic Research Program.
Yuri Prokhorov

Abstract




1.
Introduction

A
conic bundle
is a proper flat morphism
\pi:X\to Z
of non-singular varieties such that it is of relative dimension 1 and the anticanonical divisor

-K_{X}
is relatively ample.
We say that a variety
X
has a conic bundle structure if there exists
a conic bundle
\pi:X^{\prime}\to Z
and a birational map
X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
.

Varieties with conic bundle structure play a very important role in the
birational classification of algebraic varieties of negative Kodaira dimension.
For example, any variety with rational curve fibration has a conic bundle structure
[
Sar82
]
.
For these varieties there are well-developed techniques to solve rationality problems
[
Sar82
]
,
[
Sho84
]
,
[
Isk87
]
,
[
Pro18
]
.

Another important class of varieties of negative Kodaira dimension is
the class of
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano varieties
. Recall that a projective variety
X
is called
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano if it has only terminal
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial singularities,
the Picard number
\uprho(X)
equals
1
, and the anticanonical class

-K_{X}
is ample.
In fact, these two classes overlap. Moreover,
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano varieties
with conic bundle structures form a large subclass in the class of all
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano varieties. It is very important for birational geometry to
investigate and describe those
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano varieties that do not have conic bundle structures. This paper is an attempt to do it in dimension three.

To formulate the main result we have to recall some definitions.
A normal
n
-dimensional variety
X
with only canonical Gorenstein singularities is called a
del Pezzo variety
if there exists an ample Cartier divisor
A
such that
-K_{X}=(n-1)A

(see
[
Fuj90
]
).
Then the intersection number
\operatorname{d}(X):=A^{n}
is called the
degree
of
X
.
We consider only those del Pezzo varieties that have at worst terminal singularities.
Del Pezzo varieties of degree
\leq 2
have special names:

(i)

a del Pezzo variety
X
of degree
1
is called
double Veronese cone
;

(ii)

a del Pezzo variety of degree
2
is called
quartic double solid
.

Recall that the Fano index
\operatorname{q_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{Q}}}}(X)
of a
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano variety is the maximal integer that divides the canonical class
K_{X}
in the torsion free group
\operatorname{Cl}(X)/{\operatorname{Tors}}

(see
2.4
).
The following fact was proved in
[
Pro19
]
:

1.1 Theorem
.

Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefold with
\operatorname{q_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{Q}}}}(X)>7
.
Then
X
is rational. In particular,
X
has a lot of conic bundle structures.

For a normal
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial projective variety
X
, denote

\operatorname{df}(X):=\max\big\{\dim|M|\,\big|\,\text{$M$ is a Weil divisor such that $-(K_{X}+M)$ is ample}\big\}.

Our main result is the following

1.2 Theorem
.

Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefold with
\operatorname{q_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{Q}}}}(X)>1
.
Assume that
X
has no conic bundle structures.
Then we have.

(i)

\operatorname{df}(X)\leq 3
.

(ii)

If
\operatorname{df}(X)=3
, then
X
is a smooth quartic double solid.

(iii)

If
\operatorname{df}(X)=2
, then there exists a birational transformation
\Psi:X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
such that one of the following holds:

•

X^{\prime}
is a smooth quartic double solid,

•

X^{\prime}
is a smooth double Veronese cone and
\Psi
is an isomorphism, or

•

X^{\prime}
is a double Veronese cone with terminal
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial singularities and

X^{\prime}
is singular.

Note that a
smooth
double Veronese cone has no conic bundle structures according to
[
Gri04
]
. Existence of conic bundle structures on smooth quartic double solids
and singular double Veronese cones is not known.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notation and terminology

In this paper we always assume that the ground field
\Bbbk
is algebraically closed and of characteristic zero.
Also, we use the standard notations and definitions of the Minimal Model Program (MMP).
When we say that a variety
X
has terminal (resp. canonical) singularities,
this means that the singularities are not worse than that, in particular,

X
can be smooth.

For a variety
X
with terminal singularities
{\mathbf{B}}(X)
denotes the basket
of its singularities (see
[
Rei87
]
).
Typically, when describing a basket we list only indices of singularities.
For example,
{\mathbf{B}}(X)=(r_{1},r_{2})
means that
{\mathbf{B}}(X)
contains two points
of types
\frac{1}{r_{1}}(1,-1,b_{1})
and
\frac{1}{r_{2}}(2,-2,b_{2})
.
For any normal variety
X
,
\operatorname{Cl}(X)
denotes the group of Weil divisors on
X
modulo linear equivalence. By
\operatorname{Cl}(X)_{\mathrm{T}}
we denote the torsion subgroup of
\operatorname{Cl}(X)
. If
{\mathscr{M}}
is a linear system, then
\operatorname{Bs}({\mathscr{M}})
denotes its base locus.

2.2.
Singularities of linear systems

2.2.1 Definition
.

Let
X
be a normal variety and let
{\mathscr{M}}
be a (not necessarily complete) linear system of Weil divisors. We assume that
\dim({\mathscr{M}})>0
and
{\mathscr{M}}
has no fixed components. Let
\mu
be non-negative rational number such that
K_{X}+\mu{\mathscr{M}}
is

\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier. For a birational morphism
f:\tilde{X}\to X
, write

K_{\tilde{X}}+\mu\tilde{\mathscr{M}}=K_{X}+\mu{\mathscr{M}}+\sum e_{i}E_{i},

where
\tilde{\mathscr{M}}:=f^{-1}_{*}{\mathscr{M}}
is the proper transform of
{\mathscr{M}}
on
\tilde{X}
,
E_{i}
are prime exceptional divisors, and
e_{i}
are rational numbers called discrepancies.
Note that in this formula discrepancies are defined with respect to
\mu M
, where
M\in{\mathscr{M}}
is
a general member
[
Kol97
, Sect. 4]
.
We say that the pair
(X,\mu{\mathscr{M}})

canonical
, resp.
terminal
if
e_{i}\geq 0
(resp.
e_{i}>0
) for all
i
and for all birational morphisms
f:\tilde{X}\to X
.

2.2.2 Lemma

(
[
Ale94
]
,
[
Kol97
]
,
[
Pro21
]
)
.

Let
(X,{\mathscr{M}})
be a pair, where
X
is a threefold with at worst terminal
singularities and
{\mathscr{M}}
is a movable linear system on
X
.

(i)

If
(X,{\mathscr{M}})
is canonical, then a general member
S\in{\mathscr{M}}
has only Du Val
singularities and in a neighborhood of each point
P\in\operatorname{Bs}({\mathscr{M}})
we have

{\mathscr{M}}\sim-K_{X}
,

(ii)

If
(X,{\mathscr{M}})
is terminal, then a general member
S\in{\mathscr{M}}
is a smooth
surface contained in the smooth locus of
X
,
and
\dim\operatorname{Bs}({\mathscr{M}})\leq 0
.

2.2.3 Theorem

(
[
Ale94
]
,
[
Pro21
]
)
.

Let
X
be a variety with terminal
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial singularities and let

{\mathscr{M}}
be a linear system on
X
without fixed components
such that the pair
(X,\mu{\mathscr{M}})
is canonical for some
\mu\geq 0
.
Let

\varphi:(X,\mu{\mathscr{M}})\dashrightarrow(X^{\prime},\mu{\mathscr{M}}^{\prime})

be a step of

K_{X}+\mu{\mathscr{M}}
-MMP, that is,
\varphi
is either a
K_{X}+\mu{\mathscr{M}}
-negative extremal divisorial contraction or a
K_{X}+\mu{\mathscr{M}}
-flip.
Then the variety
X^{\prime}
again has terminal
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial singularities
and the pair
(X^{\prime},\mu{\mathscr{M}}^{\prime})
is canonical.
If moreover
(X,\mu{\mathscr{M}})
is terminal, then so is
(X^{\prime},\mu{\mathscr{M}}^{\prime})
.

2.2.4 Corollary

(
[
Ale94
]
,
[
BCHM10
]
,
[
Pro21
]
)
.

Let
X
be a normal variety and let

{\mathscr{M}}
be a linear system on
X
without fixed components
such that
K_{X}+\mu{\mathscr{M}}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier for some
\mu\geq 0
.
Then there exists a projective birational morphism

f:(\tilde{X},\tilde{\mathscr{M}})\longrightarrow(X,{\mathscr{M}}),

where
\tilde{\mathscr{M}}:=f^{-1}_{*}{\mathscr{M}}
, such that the pair

(\tilde{X},\mu\tilde{\mathscr{M}})
is terminal,
K_{\tilde{X}}+\mu\tilde{\mathscr{M}}

is
f
-nef, and
\tilde{X}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial. Moreover,

K_{\tilde{X}}+\mu\tilde{\mathscr{M}}\sim_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{Q}}}f^{*}(K_{X}+\mu{\mathscr{M}})-\sum e_{i}E_{i},

where
E_{i}
are prime exceptional divisors and
e_{i}\geq 0
for all
i
.

Such a pair
(\tilde{X},\mu\tilde{\mathscr{M}})
is called
terminal
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial
model
of
(X,\mu{\mathscr{M}})
.

2.2.5 Corollary

(
[
Cor95
]
,
[
Ale94
]
,
[
Pro21
]
)
.

Let
X
be a variety with terminal
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial singularities and let

{\mathscr{M}}
be a linear system on
X
without fixed components
such that the pair
(X,\mu{\mathscr{M}})
is canonical for some
\mu\geq 0
.
Then there exists a projective birational morphism

f:(\tilde{X},\tilde{\mathscr{M}})\longrightarrow(X,{\mathscr{M}}),

where
\tilde{\mathscr{M}}:=f^{-1}_{*}{\mathscr{M}}
, such that
\tilde{X}
is terminal
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial, the pair

(\tilde{X},\mu\tilde{\mathscr{M}})
is canonical,

K_{\tilde{X}}+\mu\tilde{\mathscr{M}}=f^{*}(K_{X}+\mu{\mathscr{M}}),

and the exceptional locus of
f
is a prime divisor.

Such a morphism is called an
extremal log crepant blowup
of
(X,\mu{\mathscr{M}})
.

2.2.6 Corollary

(
[
BCHM10
]
)
.

Let
X
be a normal projective variety and let

{\mathscr{M}}
be a linear system on
X
without fixed components
such that
K_{X}+\mu{\mathscr{M}}
is canonical for some
\mu\geq 0
.
Assume that
K_{X}+\mu{\mathscr{M}}
is not pseudo-effective.
Then one can run
K_{X}+\mu{\mathscr{M}}
-MMP and end with a Mori fiber space.

Recall also the following well-known result.

2.2.7 Theorem

(
[
Kaw96
]
)
.

Let
(X\ni P)
be a terminal quotient singularity of type

\frac{1}{r}(1,a,r-a)
, let
f\colon\tilde{X}\to X
be a divisorial Mori contraction,
and let
E
be the exceptional divisor.
Then
f(E)=P
, the contraction
f
is a weighted blowup with weights
(1,a,r-a)
,
and the discrepancy of
E
equals
a(E,X)=1/r
.

2.3.
Del Pezzo varieties

2.3.1 Proposition

(
[
Fuj90
]
,
[
Shi89
]
)
.

Let
X
be a three-dimensional del Pezzo variety with terminal singularities.
Then

\dim\left|-\textstyle\frac{1}{2}K_{X}\right|=\operatorname{d}(X)+1

and a general member
M\in\left|-\textstyle\frac{1}{2}K_{X}\right|
is
a smooth del Pezzo surface of degree
\operatorname{d}(X)
. Moreover, the pair

\left(X,\left|-\textstyle\frac{1}{2}K_{X}\right|\right)
is terminal.

2.3.2 Theorem

(
[
Isk77
]
,
[
Fuj90
]
,
[
Shi89
]
)
.

Let
X
be a three-dimensional del Pezzo variety with terminal singularities.

(i)

If
\operatorname{d}(X)=1
(i.e.
X
is a double Veronese cone), then

X
can be realized as a hypersurface of degree
6
in the weighted projective space
\mathbb{P}(1^{3},2,3)
. In this case, the projection

\pi:X\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}(1^{3},2)

is a double cover whose branch divisor has degree
6
in
\mathbb{P}(1^{3},2)
.

(ii)

If
\operatorname{d}(X)=2
(i.e.
X
is a quartic double solid), then

X
can be realized as a hypersurface of degree
4
in the weighted projective space
\mathbb{P}(1^{4},2)
. In this case, the projection

\pi:X\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}(1^{4})=\mathbb{P}^{3}

is a double cover whose branch divisor is a quartic hypersurface.

2.4.
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano varieties

Now, let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano variety.
It follows from the definition that
\operatorname{Cl}(X)
is a finitely generated abelian group of rank 1.
The numbers

\displaystyle\operatorname{q_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{Q}}}}(X)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\max\{t\in\mathbb{Z}\mid-K_{X}\sim_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{Q}}}tA,\ \text{$A$ is a Weil divisor}\}

\displaystyle\operatorname{q_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{W}}}}(X)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\max\{t\in\mathbb{Z}\mid-K_{X}\sim tA,\ \text{$A$ is a Weil divisor}\}

are called the
Fano index
and
Fano-Weil index
, respectively.
Clearly,
\operatorname{q_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{W}}}}(X)
divides
\operatorname{q_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{Q}}}}(X)
and
\operatorname{q_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{Q}}}}(X)=\operatorname{q_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{W}}}}(X)
if
\operatorname{Cl}(X)
is torsion free.
For a
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano variety
X
throughout this paper

A
will denote a Weil divisor on
X
such that
-K_{X}\sim_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{Q}}}\operatorname{q_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{Q}}}}(X)A
. If
\operatorname{q_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{Q}}}}(X)=\operatorname{q_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{W}}}}(X)
, we take
A
so that
-K_{X}\sim\operatorname{q_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{Q}}}}(X)A
.
Since
\uprho(X)=1
and
X
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial, for any Weil divisor
M
on
X
we can write
-K_{X}\sim_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{Q}}}\lambda M
. In this situation denote

\uplambda(X,|M|)=\uplambda(X,M):=\lambda.

Thus
\operatorname{q_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{Q}}}}(X)=\uplambda(X,A)
.

2.4.1 Theorem

(
[
CF93
]
)
.

Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefold and let
M
be a smooth surface on
X

with
\varkappa(M)=-\infty
.
Assume that
X
is not rational. Then one
of the following holds:

(i)

X
is a hypersurface of degree
6
in
\mathbb{P}(1^{2},2^{2},3)
,

M
is a member of the linear system
|{\mathscr{O}}_{X}(2)|=\big|-\frac{2}{3}K_{X}\big|
,
and so
\uplambda(X,M)=3/2
,

(ii)

X
is a del Pezzo threefold of degree
\operatorname{d}(X)\leq 3
,

M
is a member of the linear system
\big|-\frac{1}{2}K_{X}\big|
, and
\uplambda(X,M)=2
.

Note that in
[
CF93
]
the surface
M
is supposed to have ample normal bundle.
This is automatically satisfied in our case because
\uprho(X)=1
by our
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano assumption.

2.4.2 Corollary
.

Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefold and let
{\mathscr{M}}
be a linear system without fixed components on
X
such that
\uplambda(X,{\mathscr{M}})>1
and the pair
(X,{\mathscr{M}})
is terminal.
Assume that
X
has no conic bundle structures.
Then one of the following holds:

(i)

X=X_{6}\subset\mathbb{P}(1^{3},2,3)
is a double Veronese cone,

(ii)

X=X_{4}\subset\mathbb{P}(1^{4},2)
is a
smooth
quartic double solid.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2.2
(ii)
a general member
M\in{\mathscr{M}}
is smooth
and is contained in the smooth locus of
X
.
By the adjunction formula
M
is a del Pezzo surface. In particular,
M
is rational.
Thus we can apply Theorem
2.4.1
.

Assume that
X
is such as in
2.4.1
(i)
.
Let
\psi(x_{1},x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2},x_{2}^{\prime},x_{3})=0
be an equation of
X=X_{6}\subset\mathbb{P}(1^{2},2^{2},3)
,
where the subscript index of the variables
x_{i}
and
x_{j}^{\prime}
is its degree.
Since the singularities of
X
are terminal,
\psi
contains the term
x_{3}^{2}
.
Thus
\psi
can be written in the form

\psi=x_{3}^{2}+\gamma_{3}(x_{2},x_{2}^{\prime})+\phi(x_{1},x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2},x_{2}^{\prime}),

where
\gamma_{3}
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
3
and
\phi
does not contain cubic terms in
x_{2},x_{2}^{\prime}
. Clearly,
\gamma_{3}\neq 0
(otherwise
X
would be singular along the line
\{x_{1}=x_{1}^{\prime}=x_{3}=0\}
). Thus after a linear coordinate change we may assume that
\gamma_{3}=x_{2}x_{2}^{\prime}(x_{2}+x_{2}^{\prime})
,
x_{2}x_{2}^{\prime 2}
, or
x_{2}^{\prime 3}
. Then the projection

X\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}(1,1,2),\qquad(x_{1},x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2},x_{2}^{\prime},x_{3})\longmapsto(x_{1},x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2}^{\prime})

is a rational curve fibration because
in the affine chart
x_{2}^{\prime}=1
the equation
\psi
becomes
quadratic in
x_{3},x_{2}
. Hence
X
has a conic bundle structure in this case.

Thus we may assume that
X
is a del Pezzo threefold of degree
\operatorname{d}(X)\leq 3

(see
2.4.1
(ii)
).
If
\operatorname{d}(X)=3
, then
X=X_{3}\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}
is a cubic with terminal singularities
and
M
is its smooth hyperplane section. In this case the projection
X\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}
from a line
l\subset X
is a rational curve fibration.

It remains to consider the case
\operatorname{d}(X)=2
, i.e. the case where

X=X_{4}\subset\mathbb{P}(1^{4},2)
is a quartic double solid.
Suppose that
X
has a singular point, say
P\in X
.
Let
\psi(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},y)=0
be an equation of
X=X_{4}\subset\mathbb{P}(1^{4},2)
, where
\deg(x_{i})=1
and
\deg(y)=2
.
Since the singularities of
X
are terminal Gorenstein,
\psi
contains the term
y^{2}
. By an obvious coordinate change we may assume that
\psi
has the form

\psi=y^{2}+\phi(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}),

where
\deg(\phi)=4
. Clearly,
P
is contained in the hyperplane
\{y=0\}
.
Hence, by a linear coordinate change we may assume that
P=(1,0,0,0,0)
and so

\psi=y^{2}+\phi_{4}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})+x_{0}\phi_{3}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})+x_{0}^{2}\phi_{2}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}),

where
\deg(\phi_{i})=i
.
As above, the projection

X\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}(1,1,1)=\mathbb{P}^{2},\qquad(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},y)\longmapsto(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})

is a rational curve fibration. Hence
X
has a conic bundle structure.
∎

Construction.

3.1 Assumptions
.

Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefold.
Consider a linear system
{\mathscr{M}}
on
X
without fixed components.
Let
c:=\operatorname{ct}(X,{\mathscr{M}})
be the canonical threshold of the pair
(X,{\mathscr{M}})

[
Cor95
]
.
Assume that
\uplambda(X,{\mathscr{M}})>c
.

3.1.1 Lemma

(see
[
Pro10
, Lemma 4.2]
)
.

Let
P\in X
be a point of index
r>1
. Assume that

\mathscr{M}\sim-tK_{X}
near
P
, where
0<t<r
. Then
\operatorname{ct}(X,{\mathscr{M}})\leq 1/t

and
\beta\geq t\alpha
.

Consider an extremal log crepant blowup
f:\tilde{X}\to X
with respect to
K_{X}+c{\mathscr{M}}

(Corollary
2.2.5
).
Let
E
be the exceptional divisor.
Recall that

\tilde{X}
has only terminal
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial singularities.
We can write

(3.1.2)

\begin{array}[]{lll}K_{\tilde{X}}&\sim_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{Q}}}&f^{*}K_{X}+\alpha E,\\[2.0pt]
\tilde{\mathscr{M}}&\sim_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{Q}}}&f^{*}{\mathscr{M}}-\beta E.\end{array}

where
\alpha,\,\beta\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
.
Then
c=\alpha/\beta
and

K_{\tilde{X}}+c\tilde{\mathscr{M}}\sim_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{Q}}}f^{*}(K_{X}+c{\mathscr{M}}).

Since
\uplambda(X,{\mathscr{M}})>c
, the divisor
-(K_{\tilde{X}}+c\tilde{\mathscr{M}})
is nef.
Take
\lambda=\uplambda(X,{\mathscr{M}})
. Then

K_{\tilde{X}}+\lambda\tilde{\mathscr{M}}\sim_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{Q}}}f^{*}(K_{X}+\lambda{\mathscr{M}})+\alpha(1-\lambda/c)E\sim_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{Q}}}\alpha(1-\lambda/c)E.

Put
\delta:=\alpha(\lambda/c-1)
. Then

(3.1.3)

K_{\tilde{X}}+\lambda\tilde{\mathscr{M}}+\delta E\sim_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{Q}}}0,\quad\text{where $\delta>0$.}

Run the MMP with respect to
K_{\tilde{X}}+c\tilde{\mathscr{M}}
.
We obtain the following diagram (Sarkisov link)

(3.1.4)

\textstyle{\tilde{X}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\scriptstyle{\chi}
\textstyle{\bar{X}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\bar{f}}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\hat{X}}

Here the varieties
\tilde{X}
and
\bar{X}

have only terminal
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial singularities,
\chi

-log flips,

\bar{f}:\bar{X}\to\hat{X}

-negative Mori contraction.
In particular,
\operatorname{rk}\operatorname{Cl}(\hat{X})=1
.

In what follows, for a divisor (or a linear system)
N
on
X

by
\tilde{N}
and
\bar{N}
we denote
proper transforms of
N

on
\tilde{X}
and
\bar{X}

\bar{f}

denotes the

N

\hat{X}
.
Apply
\chi_{*}
to (
3.1.3
):

(3.1.5)

K_{\bar{X}}+\lambda\bar{\mathscr{M}}+\delta\bar{E}\sim_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{Q}}}0.

3.1.6 Lemma

(cf.
[
Pro19
, Lemma 4.7]
)
.

5.
Applications

In this section we consider applications of Theorem
1.2
to the existence of conic bundle structures on rationally connected threefolds.

5.1 Proposition
.

Let
X
be a normal projective threefold and let
{\mathscr{M}}
be a non-empty linear system of Weil divisors on
X
such
that

(i)

{\mathscr{M}}
is not composed of a pencil,

(ii)

-(K_{X}+{\mathscr{M}})\sim_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{Q}}}\Theta
, where
\Theta
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor which is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier, and

(iii)

either
\Theta>0
or
\Theta=0
and the pair
(X,\,{\mathscr{M}})
is not canonical.

Assume that the variety
X
has no conic bundle structures.
Then
there exists a birational transformation
\Psi:X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
, where

X^{\prime}
is del Pezzo threefold as in
1.2
(iii)
.
Moreover,

\dim({\mathscr{M}})\leq\operatorname{d}(X^{\prime})+1.

Proof.

We may assume that
{\mathscr{M}}
has no fixed components.
Let

f:(\tilde{X},\tilde{\mathscr{M}})\longrightarrow(X,{\mathscr{M}})

be a terminal
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial
model of the pair
(X,{\mathscr{M}})
(see Corollary
2.2.4
). Thus
\tilde{\mathscr{M}}:=f^{-1}_{*}{\mathscr{M}}
, the pair

(\tilde{X},\tilde{\mathscr{M}})
is terminal,
K_{\tilde{X}}+\tilde{\mathscr{M}}

is
f
-nef, and the variety
\tilde{X}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial. We can write

K_{\tilde{X}}+\tilde{\mathscr{M}}\sim_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{Q}}}f^{*}(K_{X}+{\mathscr{M}})-E^{\prime},

where
E^{\prime}
is the exceptional
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor and
E^{\prime}\geq 0
.
Furthermore,

f^{*}\Theta=\tilde{\Theta}+E^{\prime\prime},

where
\tilde{\Theta}
is the proper transform of
\Theta
on
\tilde{X}

and
E^{\prime\prime}\geq 0
.
Hence,

K_{\tilde{X}}+\tilde{\mathscr{M}}+\tilde{\Xi}\sim_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{Q}}}f^{*}(K_{X}+{\mathscr{M}}+\Theta)\sim_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{Q}}}0,

where
\tilde{\Xi}:=\tilde{\Theta}+E^{\prime}+E^{\prime\prime}\geq 0
. Moreover,
\tilde{\Xi}>0
by our assumptions
5.1
(iii)
.
In particular,
K_{\tilde{X}}+\tilde{\mathscr{M}}
is not nef.
Run
(K_{\tilde{X}}+\tilde{\mathscr{M}})
-MMP. The divisor
\tilde{\Xi}
cannot be contracted, so at the end we get a Mori fiber
space
(\bar{X},\bar{\mathscr{M}})/Z
:

\textstyle{(\tilde{X},\tilde{\mathscr{M}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(\bar{X},\bar{\mathscr{M}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi}
\textstyle{Z}

where
\bar{\Xi}\sim_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{Q}}}-(K_{\bar{X}}+\bar{\mathscr{M}})
if
\varphi
-ample.
If
\dim(Z)=2
, then
\varphi
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-conic bundle and then we are done.
Assume that
\dim(Z)=1
. Then
\varphi
is a del Pezzo fibration and
Z\simeq\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
Since
\dim({\mathscr{M}})\geq 2
and a general member of
{\mathscr{M}}
is irreducible,
{\mathscr{M}}

is not
\varphi
-horizontal, i.e.
{\mathscr{M}}
is not a pull-back of a linear system
on
Z
.
For a general fiber
F
by the adjunction formula we have

K_{F}=K_{\bar{X}}|_{F}=\bar{\mathscr{M}}|_{F}+\bar{\Xi}|_{F},

where both
\bar{\mathscr{M}}|_{F}
and
\bar{\Xi}|_{F}
are ample. Since
\bar{\mathscr{M}}|_{F}
is an (integral) Cartier divisor,
the surface

is either
\mathbb{P}^{2}
or
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}

\mathbb{Q}
-Fano threefold

\bar{\mathscr{M}}
and

1.2

∎

Skin effect in Non-Hermitian systems with spin
Wenna Zhang, Yutao Hu, Hongyi Zhang, Xiang Liu, Yuecheng Shen, Georgios Veronis, Andrea Alù, Yin Huang, Wenchen Luo

Abstract
The skin effect, where bulk modes collapse into boundary modes, is a key phenomenon in topological non-Hermitian systems, has been predominantly studied in spinless systems. Recent studies illustrate the magnetic suppression of the first-order skin effect while ignoring spin. However, the physical significance of a magnetic field in non-Hermitian skin effect with spin remains elusive. Here, we systematically explore non-Hermitian spinful systems based on generalized Hatano-Nelson models with SU(2) gauge potential fields. In an open one-dimensional lattice, the spin-up and spin-down states can be uniquely separated and localized at the two boundaries without magnetic field. When an external magnetic field is applied, the skin effect exhibits a smooth transition from bidirectional to unidirectional. Remarkably, we demonstrate that the first-order skin effect can be anomalously induced by a magnetic field in a topologically trivial non-Hermitian spinful system without any skin effect at zero field. The direction of such magnetically induced skin modes can be controlled by simply changing the amplitude and polarity of the magnetic field. In addition, we demonstrate a transition between non-Bloch 𝒫 ​ 𝒯 \mathcal{PT} and anti- 𝒫 ​ 𝒯 \mathcal{PT} symmetries in the system, and uncover the spin-dependent mechanism of non-Bloch 𝒫 ​ 𝒯 \mathcal{PT} symmetry. Our results pave the way for the investigation of non-Hermitian skin effect with spin degrees of freedom.

Transition from bidirectional to unidirectional skin effect.
—A magnetic field directly couples to a spinful system and is easy to manipulate
[
39
,
31
]
.
Here, we need to constrain the magnetic field in any direction other than the
z
axis. Otherwise the NHSE does not qualitatively change due to
[\sigma_{z},H_{z}]=0
. For simplicity, the magnetic field is assumed to be along the
x

\boldsymbol{\delta}=(\delta_{x},0,0)

As shown in Fig.
2

In the presence of the magnetic field, the two Riemann surfaces corresponding to the two spins are no longer independent and collapse into one due to the interaction between the spin-up and spin-down states. When the external magnetic field is weak, even though the PBC spectra remain almost unchanged, the OBC energy spectra significantly change [Fig.
2
(a)]. Since one of the two closed loops for the PBC spectra travels around the origin clockwise while the other one travels counterclockwise, the overall winding number around any interior point in the overlapping region becomes zero. To maintain the skin effect, most of the modes originally located in the overlapping region at zero field, escape the overlapping region and enter the area enclosed with winding number
\omega\neq 0
.

FOSE induced by magnetic fields.
—In numerous systems, such as in Landau quantization, theories ignoring the spin degree of freedom work well.
However, the FOSE suppression by an external magnetic field in a spinless system does not occur in our spinful system when both
\theta
and
\phi
are real. No skin effect exists in the absence of a magnetic field. The system is essentially topologically trivial, represented by the zero winding number of the energy spectra in PBC forming two straight lines.

Here, applying a magnetic field does not induce the cyclotron-like motion in a 1D system, but the associated Zeeman coupling is able to change the topological properties of the system.
Figs.
3
(a) and
3
(b) show the winding number as a function of the gauge field
\theta
and magnetic field
\mathbf{\delta}
under different gauge fields
\phi

3
(c)]. In particular, the enclosed OBC states all aggregate to one side of the lattice due to the fact that the energy curves in PBC rotate in the same direction. This unexpected feature indicates that the magnetic field alone can induce the FOSE in topologically trivial systems without any skin effect, in stark contrast to spinless cases
[

Transition from non-Bloch
\mathcal{PT}
to anti-
\mathcal{PT}
symmetric phases.
—Interestingly, under a sufficiently strong magnetic field, our system exhibits a transition between non-Bloch
\mathcal{PT}
symmetric and anti-
\mathcal{PT}
symmetric phases, depending on the hopping phases
\theta
and
\phi
[Fig.
4
(a)].
A straightforward perturbation calculation (see SM Sec. VI. A
[
36
]
), treating the Hermitian Zeeman coupling as the unperturbed term and the hopping terms as the perturbation, allows one to explicitly classify the symmetries which determine whether the eigenenergies are real or complex. The unperturbed system is degenerate with only two eigenenergies
\pm\delta_{x}
. The phase of the system is determined as follows: if
\Delta=\cos\theta\cos\phi>0
, all OBC energies become strictly real, since the corrected energies are real-valued functions of the real variable
\sqrt{\Delta}
; if
\Delta<0
, the first-order perturbation contributes only to the imaginary part of the OBC energies, exhibiting strict non-Bloch anti-
\mathcal{PT}
symmetry. However, higher-order corrections contribute to the real part of the eigenenergies leading to non-Bloch anti-
\mathcal{PT}
symmetry with complex conjugated OBC spectra [Fig.
4
(a)]. A larger Hermitian Zeeman coupling is equivalent to smaller non-Hermiticity. The system therefore tends to be in
\mathcal{PT}
or anti-
\mathcal{PT}
symmetric phases under a sufficiently large magnetic field (see SM Sec. VI. B
[
36
]
).

\Delta=0
corresponds to the transition point between the
\mathcal{PT}
and anti-
\mathcal{PT}

Conclusion.
—In this work, we showed that by introducing appropriate SU(2) phases, and applying an external magnetic field in a spinful non-Hermitian system, states can be skinned at both sides or one side
and that magnetic fields can induce FOSE in non-Hermitian spinful systems. Such an anomalous phenomenon stems from the emergence of nonreciprocal hopping strengths induced by external magnetic fields, coupled to internal geometric asymmetries in the unit cell of our lattice. For sufficient magnetic field strength, we also demonstrated a transition between non-Bloch
\mathcal{PT}
and anti-
\mathcal{PT}
symmetric phases via the hopping phases under a strong external magnetic field. We used perturbation theory to derive the transition condition and elucidated the symmetry of the spin polarization in this system. In addition, we unveiled a unique signature of the non-Bloch
\mathcal{PT}
symmetry, which provides a new way to understand the spin-dependent origin of non-Bloch
\mathcal{PT}
symmetry in spinful non-Hermitian systems. Note that the model can be generalized to two dimensions, allowing us to control the localization of the states to any corner (see SM Sec. VII
[
36
]
).
Our results may be verified in physical platforms such as ultracold atoms and photonic systems, such as optical fibers. Artificial non-Abelian gauge potentials have been created to act on cold atoms in optical lattices
[
42
]
. Based on this platform, the SU(2) gauge potentials introduced here can be synthesized
[
43
,
44
,
45
,
46
,
47
,
48
]
, and highly tunable Zeeman couplings in cold atoms can be achieved through Raman lasers
[
49
,
50
]
. Recent experiments have also realized spin-dependent hopping using a synthetic frequency dimension platform
[
51
]
. Remarkably, spin-related detection is a potential tool to observe the features of non-Hermitian systems, especially the skin effect with
spin separation explored here.

H=\sum_{q}e^{iq}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}c_{{q,+}}^{{\dagger}}&c_{{q,-}}^{{\dagger}}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&0\\

c_{{q,-}}\end{array}\right)+e^{-i{q}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}c_{{q,+}}^{{\dagger}}&c_{{q,-}}^{{\dagger}}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\

c_{{q,-}}\end{array}\right),

we obtain the relations

\displaystyle t_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a}{2},

\displaystyle e^{2i\theta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2a,

\displaystyle t_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{b}{2},

\displaystyle e^{2i\phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{b}.

Note that
\theta
and
\phi
must be imaginary if
a
and
b
are real. In real
space, the toy model is in fact two copies of the HN model

H=\sum_{i}ac_{i,+}^{{\dagger}}c_{i+1,+}+c_{i+1,+}^{{\dagger}}c_{i,+}+\frac{1}{2}c_{i,-}^{{\dagger}}c_{i+1,-}+\frac{b}{2}c_{i+1,-}^{{\dagger}}c_{i,-}.

(S17)

\left|a\right|>1
leads the spin-up states skinning to the left, and
\left|b\right|>1
makes the spin-down states localized to the right. Conversely, when
\left|a\right|<1
and
\left|b\right|<1
, the direction of the NHSE for both spins is reversed. When
\left|a\right|=\left|b\right|\neq 1
,
a symmetric bidirectional skin effect with spin separation appears.

Although it was recently demonstrated that simultaneous presence of non-Hermitian skin modes at both boundaries can emerge due to the effective next nearest neighbor (NNN) hopping induced by the non-Abelian SU(2) gauge fields
[
48
]
, its underlying physical mechanism is fundamentally different from our case, and the spin-up and spin-down states in these studies were not separated. It was also shown that
Z_{2}
NHSE with spin separation to opposite boundaries can be realized in a stacked system with time-reversal symmetry inspired by the quantum spin Hall insulator
[
52
]
. In comparison, the NHSE with spin separation in our system does not require such symmetry protection.

III.1
Transition from bidirectional to unidirectional skin effect

When the magnetic field is turned on, the PBC spectra are

\displaystyle E_{1,2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[\left(e^{iq}\left(e^{i\theta}+e^{-i\theta}\right)+e^{-iq}\left(e^{i\phi}+e^{-i\phi}\right)\right)\pm\sqrt{4\delta^{2}+\left(e^{iq}\left(e^{i\theta}-e^{-i\theta}\right)+e^{-iq}\left(e^{i\phi}-e^{-i\phi}\right)\right)^{2}}\right],

(S39)

where
q
is a wave vector. The lattice constant is set equal to 1. When
\delta
is relatively large,
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
can be simplified using Taylor series:

\displaystyle E_{1,2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[e^{iq}\left(e^{i\theta}+e^{-i\theta}\right)+e^{-iq}(e^{i\phi}+e^{-i\phi})\right]\pm\frac{1}{8\delta}\left[e^{iq}\left(e^{i\theta}-e^{-i\theta}\right)+e^{-iq}\left(e^{i\phi}-e^{-i\phi}\right)\right]^{2}\pm\delta.

(S40)

Thus, the winding direction depends on the
first term of
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
, so that the sign of the winding number depends on the coefficients of
e^{iq}
and
e^{-iq}
. By comparing
\left|\cosh(i\theta)\right|
and
\left|\cosh(i\phi)\right|
, we can obtain the winding number.

III.2
Magnetically induced FOSE

In the presence of the magnetic field, the PBC spectra with both
\theta
and
\phi
being real are

\displaystyle E_{1,2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{iq}\cos\theta+e^{-iq}\cos\phi\pm\sqrt{\delta^{2}+\left(ie^{iq}\sin\theta+ie^{-iq}\sin\phi\right)^{2}}

(S41)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{iq}\cos\theta+e^{-iq}\cos\phi\pm\left(ie^{iq}\sin\theta+ie^{-iq}\sin\phi\right)\sqrt{1+\left(\frac{\delta}{ie^{iq}\sin\theta+ie^{-iq}\sin\phi}\right)^{2}}.

When the magnetic field
\delta
is weak, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0}{E_{1,2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{iq}\cos\theta+e^{-iq}\cos\phi\pm\left(ie^{iq}\sin\theta+ie^{-iq}\sin\phi\right)\pm\frac{1}{2}\frac{\delta^{2}}{ie^{iq}\sin\theta+ie^{-iq}\sin\phi}

(S42)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{iq}e^{\pm i\theta}+e^{-iq}e^{\pm i\phi}\mp\frac{i}{2}\frac{\delta^{2}}{e^{iq}\sin\theta+e^{-iq}\sin\phi}.

Note that
e^{iq}e^{\pm i\theta}+e^{-iq}e^{\pm i\phi}
map to two straight lines in the complex plane. When
\delta
=0, the system is explicitly topologically trivial. In contrast, when
\delta\neq 0
,
H^{\prime}=e^{iq}\sin\theta+e^{-iq}\sin\phi
represents the Hamiltonian of the standard HN model with nonreciprocal hopping strengths for
|\sin\theta|\neq|\sin\phi|
. When
|\sin\theta|>|\sin\phi|
, an extensive number of OBC states are localized at one boundary. When
|\sin\theta|<|\sin\phi|
, many OBC states are localized at the other boundary. In other words, the magnetically induced term
\delta^{2}/(e^{iq}\sin\theta+e^{-iq}\sin\phi)
forms a closed loop in the complex plane and leads to nonreciprocal effective hopping strengths which depend on phases
\theta
and
\phi
. The system under an external magnetic field is therefore nontrivial with point-gap topology.

III.3
Directional reversal of magnetically induced FOSE

As the magnetic field amplitude increases, the winding number changes sign across specific lines. This indicates that our system is nontrivial. In other words, the topological property of our system is different before and after the point-gap reopens. In the presence of the magnetic field, the PBC spectra are

\displaystyle E_{1,2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2e^{iq}\cos\theta+2e^{-iq}\cos\phi\pm\sqrt{(2\delta)^{2}+\left(2ie^{iq}\sin\theta+2ie^{-iq}\sin\phi\right)^{2}}.

(S43)

When
\phi=\theta
,
E_{1,2}=4\cos\theta\cos q\pm 4\sqrt{(\delta)^{2}-\left(\sin\theta\cos q\right)^{2}}
.
Since
\cos q
is an arc on the real axis, the image of
E_{1,2}(\cos q)
will consist of arcs on the complex plane. In other words, both spectral areas are zero and the corresponding point-gaps disappear. For
\phi=\theta-\pi
,
\phi=-\theta
, and
\phi=-\theta+\pi
, the corresponding physical scenarios are the same. The underlying physical mechanism of the NHSE is associated with the asymmetric hopping strengths in the forward and backward directions. Thus, the point-gap topology collapses when the forward and backward hopping strengths are the same. We can consequently obtain some additional transition lines when

\displaystyle\sqrt{(2\delta)^{2}+\left(2ie^{iq}\sin\theta+2ie^{-iq}\sin\phi\right)^{2}}=\pm 2ie^{iq}\sin\theta\pm 2ie^{-iq}\sin\phi,

(S44)

\displaystyle\sqrt{(2\delta)^{2}+\left(2ie^{iq}\sin\theta+2ie^{-iq}\sin\phi\right)^{2}}=\pm 2ie^{iq}\sin\theta\mp 2ie^{-iq}\sin\phi.

(S45)

The first equation requires zero magnetic field amplitude (
\delta
=0), which is inconsistent with our assumption that the system is under an external magnetic field. The second equation leads to
\delta=\pm 2\sqrt{\sin\theta\sin\phi}
, and the PBC spectra therefore collapse into arcs:

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2e^{iq}e^{\pm i\theta}+2e^{-iq}e^{\mp i\phi},

(S46)

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2e^{iq}e^{\mp i\theta}+2e^{-iq}e^{\pm i\phi}.

(S47)

Symmetry of eigenenergies and
\widetilde{\sigma}_{x,y}

The Hamiltonian in the presence of a magnetic field
\delta=\left(\delta_{x},0,0\right)
is

H=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccccc}&\delta_{x}&e^{i\theta}&&&&&\\
\delta_{x}&&&e^{-i\theta}&&&&\\

&e^{-i\phi}&\delta_{x}&&&\ldots&&\\
&&e^{i\phi}&&&&e^{i\theta}&\\
&&&\ldots&&&&e^{-i\theta}\\
&&&&e^{i\phi}&&&\delta_{x}\\
&&&&&e^{-i\phi}&\delta_{x}&\end{array}\right).

(S48)

Its
i
-th eigenvalue equation is
H\psi_{i}=E_{i}\psi_{i}
, i.e.

\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccccc}&\delta_{x}&e^{i\theta}&&&&&\\
\delta_{x}&&&e^{-i\theta}&&&&\\
e^{i\phi}&&&\delta_{x}&e^{i\theta}&&&\\
&e^{-i\phi}&\delta_{x}&&&\ldots&&\\
&&e^{i\phi}&&&&e^{i\theta}&\\
&&&\ldots&&&&e^{-i\theta}\\

&&&&&e^{-i\phi}&\delta_{x}&\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}x_{1,+}^{i}\\
x_{1,-}^{i}\\
x_{2,+}^{i}\\
x_{2,-}^{i}\\
\ldots\\
x_{N,+}^{i}\\
x_{N,-}^{i}\end{array}\right)=E_{i}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}x_{1,+}^{i}\\
x_{1,-}^{i}\\
x_{2,+}^{i}\\
x_{2,-}^{i}\\
\ldots\\
x_{N,+}^{i}\\
x_{N,-}^{i}\end{array}\right).

(S49)

We assume that states
n,l
have opposite eigenenergies:

\displaystyle H\psi_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{n}\psi_{n},

(S50)

\displaystyle H\psi_{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{l}\psi_{n}=-E_{n}\psi_{l}.

(S51)

The following equations are then satisfied:

\displaystyle\delta_{x}x_{1,-s}^{n}+e^{is\theta}x_{2,s}^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{n}x_{1,s}^{n},

(S52)

\displaystyle e^{is\phi}x_{j-1,s}^{n}+\delta_{x}x_{j,-s}^{n}+e^{is\theta}x_{j+1,s}^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{n}x_{j,s}^{n},

(S53)

\displaystyle e^{is\phi}x_{N-1,s}^{n}+\delta_{x}x_{N,-s}^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{n}x_{N,s}^{n},

(S54)

and

\displaystyle\delta_{x}x_{1,-s}^{l}+e^{is\theta}x_{2,s}^{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-E_{n}x_{1,s}^{l},

\displaystyle e^{is\phi}x_{j-1,s}^{l}+\delta_{x}x_{j,-s}^{l}+e^{is\theta}x_{j+1,s}^{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-E_{n}x_{j,s}^{l},

\displaystyle e^{is\phi}x_{N-1,s}^{l}+\delta_{x}x_{N,-s}^{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-E_{n}x_{N,s}^{l},

with
j\in\left[2,N-1\right]
and
s=\pm 1
. By summing or subtracting these, we obtain

\displaystyle\delta_{x}\left(x_{1,-}^{n}+x_{1,-}^{l}\right)+e^{i\theta}\left(x_{2,+}^{n}+x_{2,+}^{l}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{n}\left(x_{1,+}^{n}-x_{1,+}^{l}\right),

\displaystyle\delta_{x}\left(x_{1,+}^{n}-x_{1,+}^{l}\right)+e^{-i\theta}\left(x_{2,-}^{n}-x_{2,-}^{l}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{n}\left(x_{1,-}^{n}+x_{1,-}^{l}\right),

\displaystyle e^{is\phi}\left(x_{j-1,s}^{n}-s\left(-1\right)^{j-2}x_{j-1,s}^{l}\right)+\delta_{x}\left(x_{j,-s}^{n}+s\left(-1\right)^{j-1}x_{j,-s}^{l}\right)

\displaystyle+e^{is\theta}\left(x_{j+1,s}^{n}-s\left(-1\right)^{j}x_{j+1,s}^{l}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{n}\left(x_{j,s}^{n}-s\left(-1\right)^{j-1}x_{j,s}^{l}\right),

\displaystyle e^{is\phi}\left(x_{N-1,s}^{n}-s\left(-1\right)^{N-2}x_{N-1,s}^{l}\right)+\delta_{x}\left(x_{N,-s}^{n}+s\left(-1\right)^{N-1}x_{N,-s}^{l}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{n}\left(x_{N,s}^{n}-s\left(-1\right)^{N-1}x_{N,s}^{l}\right).

Once the coefficients in the two eigenvectors satisfy

x_{j,s}^{n}=s\left(-1\right)^{j-1}x_{j,s}^{l},

(S55)

all eigenvalue equations are satisfied. In fact, in the energy spectra any eigenenergy is in pair with
its opposite eigenenergy, as also verified by numerical results. This also implies that the system has the symmetry
\sigma_{z}H(\beta)\sigma_{z}=-H(\beta e^{i\pi})
in the GBZ. The
spin polarizations
\widetilde{\sigma}_{x,y}
of the pair states at any site
i
are opposite,

\widetilde{\sigma}_{n,x}^{(j)}=-\widetilde{\sigma}_{l,x}^{(j)},\quad\widetilde{\sigma}_{n,y}^{(j)}=-\widetilde{\sigma}_{l,y}^{(j)},

(S56)

since

\left(x_{i,s}^{n}\right)^{\ast}x_{i,-s}^{n}=-\left(x_{i,s}^{l}\right)^{\ast}x_{i,-s}^{l}.

(S57)

If
\theta,\phi\in\mathbf{R},
we may consider another symmetry:

\displaystyle H\psi_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{n}\psi_{n},

(S58)

\displaystyle H\psi_{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{l}\psi_{l}=E_{n}^{\ast}\psi_{l}.

(S59)

The eigenvalue equations can then be written as

\displaystyle\delta_{x}x_{1,-s}^{n}+e^{is\theta}x_{2,s}^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{n}x_{1,s}^{n},

\displaystyle e^{is\phi}x_{j-1,s}^{n}+\delta_{x}x_{j,-s}^{n}+e^{is\theta}x_{j+1,s}^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{n}x_{j,s}^{n},

\displaystyle e^{is\phi}x_{N-1,s}^{n}+\delta_{x}x_{N,-s}^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{n}x_{N,s}^{n},

\displaystyle\delta_{x}x_{1,-s}^{l}+e^{is\theta}x_{2,s}^{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{n}^{\ast}x_{1,s}^{l},

\displaystyle e^{is\phi}x_{j-1,s}^{l}+\delta_{x}x_{j,-s}^{l}+e^{is\theta}x_{j+1,s}^{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{n}^{\ast}x_{j,s}^{l},

\displaystyle e^{is\phi}x_{N-1,s}^{l}+\delta_{x}x_{N,-s}^{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{n}^{\ast}x_{N,s}^{l}.

The complex conjugate of the equations corresponding to the
l
-th state results in the condition

\left(x_{j,s}^{l}\right)^{\ast}=x_{j,-s}^{n}

(S60)

to satisfy

E_{n}^{\ast}=E_{l}.

This implies that the system respects pseudo-Hermiticity in the GBZ

\sigma_{x,y}H(\beta)\sigma_{x,y}=[H(\beta^{*})]^{\dagger}.

(S61)

We thus infer that the eigenenergies have four-fold symmetry on the complex plane, and that, if the lattice number of the chain is odd, then there are at least two real eigenenergies with opposite sign. We also verified this numerically. For the spin polarization, besides the symmetries shown in Eq. (
S56
), we have an extra symmetry

\widetilde{\sigma}_{n,z}^{(j)}=-\widetilde{\sigma}_{l,z}^{(j)}.

(S62)

VI.1
Energy Spectra

When the magnetic field is large, the Hermitian Zeeman coupling dominates the system and can be treated as the unperturbed part of the Hamiltonian

H_{0}=\sum_{i}\delta_{x}c_{i}^{{\dagger}}\sigma_{x}c_{i}.

(S63)

The other part of the Hamiltonian

H_{1}=\sum_{i}c_{i}^{{\dagger}}t_{1}e^{i\theta\mathbf{\sigma_{z}}}c_{i+1}+c_{i+1}^{{\dagger}}t_{2}e^{i\phi\mathbf{\sigma_{z}}}c_{i}

(S64)

can then be regarded as the perturbation. We now assume that
\theta,\phi\in\mathbf{R}
, and the unperturbed states
are represented by the eigenstates of
\sigma_{x},\left|\pm\right\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
\pm 1\end{array}\right),
so that

\left|i,\pm\right\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}\ldots&0&1&\pm 1&0&\ldots\end{array}\right)^{\intercal},

and the eigenenergies are degenerate:

E_{i,\pm}=\pm\delta_{x}.

In this basis, the matrix form of the perturbation is

H_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}&&e^{i\theta}&&&&\\
&&&e^{-i\theta}&&&\\
e^{i\phi}&&&&\ldots&&\\
&e^{-i\phi}&&&&e^{i\theta}&\\
&&\ldots&&&&e^{-i\theta}\\
&&&e^{i\phi}&&&\\
&&&&e^{-i\phi}&&\end{array}\right)_{2N\times 2N}.

(S65)

We use the degenerate perturbation theory. The two groups of eigenstates
\left|i,+\right\rangle
and
\left|i,-\right\rangle

form two subspaces of the Hilbert space. The first-order corrections of the degenerate energies are given by the eigenvalues of two matrices, which are in fact identical:

W_{\pm}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}&\cos\theta&&&&\\
\cos\phi&&\cos\theta&&&\\
&\cos\phi&&\ldots&&\\
&&\ldots&&&\\
&&&&&\cos\theta\\
&&&&\cos\phi&\end{array}\right)_{N\times N}.

(S66)

The first-order corrections of the eigenenergies are

E_{j,\pm}^{\left(1\right)}=2\sqrt{\cos\theta\cos\phi}\cos\left(\frac{j\pi}{N+1}\right),

(S67)

with corresponding eigenvectors

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\sqrt{\frac{\cos\theta}{\cos\phi}}\sin\frac{j\pi}{N+1}\\
\ldots\\
\left(\sqrt{\frac{\cos\theta}{\cos\phi}}\right)^{k}\sin\frac{kj\pi}{N+1}\\
\ldots\\
\left(\sqrt{\frac{\cos\theta}{\cos\phi}}\right)^{N}\sin\frac{Nj\pi}{N+1}\end{array}\right).

Given that the magnetic field is strong, the two branches of eigenenergies
with corrections are not close to each other. In each subspace, the reconstructed
eigenstates without normalization are then given by

\left|\psi_{j,s}\right\rangle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\cos\theta}{\cos\phi}}\right)^{k}\sin\frac{kj\pi}{N+1}\left|k,s\right\rangle,

(S68)

with
s=\pm 1
and the left eigenstates are

\left\langle\chi_{j,s}\right|=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\cos\phi}{\cos\theta}}\right)^{k}\sin\frac{kj\pi}{N+1}\left\langle k,s\right|.

(S69)

These eigenstates would be useful in higher-order corrections.

The energy degeneracy of the Zeeman coupling is thus lifted by the
perturbations. Consequently, we find that, if
\cos\theta\cos\phi>0
,
then the first-order corrections of the eigenenergies are real. Otherwise, if
\cos\theta\cos\phi<0
, the corrections are imaginary.

Next, we study
the second-order corrections of the eigenenergies. The inter-subspace contributions in the second-order corrections are real:

E_{j,s}^{\rm{inter}\left(2\right)}=\sum_{j^{\prime}}\frac{\left|\left\langle j^{\prime},-s\right|H_{1}\left|j,s\right\rangle\right|^{2}}{E_{j,s}-E_{j^{\prime},-s}}=\sum_{j^{\prime}}\frac{\left|\left\langle j^{\prime},-\right|H_{1}\left|j,s\right\rangle\right|^{2}}{2s\delta_{x}}\in\mathbf{R.}

(S70)

This explains why, when
\cos\theta\cos\phi<0
, the curve of the energy spectra on the complex plane is not a straight line parallel to the imaginary axis.
Higher-order corrections contribute to the real part of the eigenenergies
when
\cos\theta\cos\phi<0
.

We can then prove that, when
\cos\theta\cos\phi>0,
all order corrections
of energies are real. In an arbitrary order correction, the denominators,
which are the product of factors such as
\left(E_{j,s}-E_{j^{\prime},-s}\right)
or
\left(E_{j,s}+E_{j,s}^{\left(1\right)}-E_{j^{\prime},s}-E_{j^{\prime},s}^{\left(1\right)}\right)
, are real. In the numerators, only terms such as

\left\langle j,s\right|H_{1}\left|j_{1},s_{1}\right\rangle\left\langle j_{1},s_{1}\right|H_{1}\left|j_{2},s_{2}\right\rangle\ldots\left\langle j_{m},s_{m}\right|H_{1}\left|j,s\right\rangle

(S71)

exist. The term in Eq. (
S71
) is a product of
m+1
matrix elements. Based on the form of
H_{1}
, we have the following
properties:

\displaystyle\left\langle j_{k},s\right|H_{1}\left|j_{k^{\prime}},s\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos\theta\text{ or }\cos\phi\text{ or }0,

(S72)

\displaystyle\left\langle j_{k},+\right|H_{1}\left|j_{k^{\prime}},-\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\sin\theta\text{ or }i\sin\phi\text{ or }0.

(S73)

Since the product in Eq. (
S71
) starts from and ends to the same state with spin
s
, there is always an even number of purely imaginary matrix elements in Eq. (
S71
). Thus, the numerators which consist of terms as in Eq. (
S71
) are real. To
sum up, when
\cos\theta\cos\phi>0,
arbitrary order corrections are real, and the system exhibits
\mathcal{PT}
symmetry. When
\cos\theta\cos\phi<0
, the system is in anti-
\mathcal{PT}
symmetric phase.

VI.2
Symmetry

Even though the essential dynamics for systems in anti-
\mathcal{PT}
symmetric phases might be remarkably different from their
\mathcal{PT}
symmetric counterparts, the anti-
\mathcal{PT}
-symmetric Hamiltonian
H^{\mathcal{APT}}
can be conveniently defined in terms of a
\mathcal{PT}
-symmetric Hamiltonian
H^{\mathcal{PT}}
, such that
H^{\mathcal{APT}}=\pm iH^{\mathcal{PT}}

[
53
]
. The Hermitian Zeeman coupling can be neglected when we consider the symmetry of the Hamiltonian in our non-Hermitian systems. We assume that we have a Hamiltonian for
{\mathcal{PT}}
symmetry as follows

H^{\mathcal{PT}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}e^{i\theta}e^{iq}+e^{i\phi}e^{-i{q}}&0\\
0&e^{-i\theta}e^{i{q}}+e^{-i\phi}e^{-i{q}}\end{array}\right).

(S74)

In contrast, the system is in the anti-
{\mathcal{PT}}
symmetric phase for
\cos\theta\cos\phi<0
. In other words, a Hamiltonian for the anti-
{\mathcal{PT}}
symmetry can be obtained when the phase
\phi
shifts to
\phi+\pi
. We then have

H^{\mathcal{APT}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}e^{i\theta}e^{iq}-e^{i\phi}e^{-i{q}}&0\\
0&e^{-i\theta}e^{iq}-e^{-i\phi}e^{-i{q}}\end{array}\right).

(S75)

When the magnetic field is neglected, the PBC spectra of the Hamiltonian collapse into arcs and the wave vector
q
is within the conventional Bloch Brillouin zone instead of the non-Bloch Brillouin zone. The band structure for
H^{\mathcal{APT}}
is the same when
q\to q\pm\frac{\pi}{2}
. Thus, we have

H^{\mathcal{APT}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\pm ie^{i\theta}e^{iq}\pm ie^{i\phi}e^{-iq}&0\\
0&\pm ie^{-i\theta}e^{iq}\pm ie^{-i\phi}e^{-iq}\end{array}\right)=\pm iH^{\mathcal{PT}}.

(S76)

Note that the mathematical distinction between
{\mathcal{PT}}
and anti-
{\mathcal{PT}}
systems is semantic in the framework of the pseudo-Hermitian quantum mechanics
[
53
]
. Eq. (
S61
) demonstrates that our system indeed respects the pseudo-Hermiticity when hopping phases are real.

VI.4
Signature of the non-Bloch
\mathcal{PT}
symmetry

When
\mathcal{PT}
symmetry occurs, and all eigenenergies are real, we find a unique signature of the system,
\widetilde{\sigma}_{n,z}^{(j)}=0
for an arbitrary state
n
at any site
j
. The proof is given as follows. The eigenvalue equations in this case are given by Eqs. (
S52
)-(
S54
). Given that
E_{n}\in\mathbf{R}
, the complex conjugate of these equations gives the relation

x^{n}_{j,-}=\left(x^{n}_{j,+}\right)^{*}\cdot e^{i\gamma},

(S77)

where
\gamma
is a constant. Based on this relation, we consequently have

\widetilde{\sigma}_{n,z}^{(j)}=\left|x^{n}_{j,+}\right|^{2}-\left|x^{n}_{j,-}\right|^{2}=0.

(S78)

which can be verified numerically.

Figure S3:
Schematic of a 1-D general system. A unit cell includes spin degrees of freedom, and the range of hopping is
M
.

In fact, the signature of
\widetilde{\sigma}_{z}=0
can be generalized to an arbitrary 1-D open chain, as shown in Fig.
S3
. A unit cell is composed of spin up and spin down states, and the range of hopping is
M
, meaning that electrons hop up to the
M
-th nearest unit cells. Again, the
n
-th eigenvalue equation is

H\psi_{n}=E_{n}\psi_{n},
and the following eigenvalue equations are satisfied:

\displaystyle\delta_{x}x_{1,-s}^{n}+e^{is\theta_{1}}x_{2,s}^{n}+e^{is\theta_{2}}x_{3,s}^{n}+\ldots+e^{is\theta_{M}}x_{M+1,s}^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{n}x_{1,s}^{n},

\displaystyle e^{is\phi_{1}}x_{1,s}^{n}+\delta_{x}x_{2,-s}^{n}+e^{is\theta_{1}}x_{3,s}^{n}+e^{is\theta_{2}}x_{4,s}^{n}+\ldots+e^{is\theta_{M}}x_{M+2,s}^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{n}x_{2,s}^{n},

\displaystyle e^{is\phi_{M}}x_{j-M,s}^{n}+e^{is\phi_{M-1}}x_{j-(M-1),s}^{n}+\ldots+\delta_{x}x_{j,-s}^{n}+\ldots+e^{is\theta_{M-1}}x_{j+M-1,s}^{n}+e^{is\theta_{M}}x_{j+M,s}^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{n}x_{j,s}^{n},

\displaystyle e^{is\phi_{M}}x_{N-(M+1),s}^{n}+e^{is\phi_{M-1}}x_{N-M,s}^{n}+\ldots+\delta_{x}x_{N-1,-s}^{n}+e^{is\theta_{1}}x_{N,s}^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{n}x_{N-1,s}^{n},

\displaystyle e^{is\phi_{M}}x_{N-M,s}^{n}+e^{is\phi_{M-1}}x_{N-(M-1),s}^{n}+\ldots+\delta_{x}x_{N,-s}^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{n}x_{N,s}^{n},

with
j\in\left[M+1,N-M\right]
and
s=\pm 1
.
\theta_{k}
and
\phi_{k}
are hopping phases to the
k
-th nearest unit cell with
k\leq M
. Assuming
E_{n}\in\mathbf{R}
, the complex conjugate of these equations also leads to Eqs. (
S77
) and (
S78
).

VII
Details of eigenenergies and their skin direction in the 2D Model

We generalize the model to two dimensions, and the Hamiltonian is given by

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}c_{i,j}^{{\dagger}}e^{i\theta\sigma_{z}}c_{i+1,j}+c_{i+1,j}^{{\dagger}}e^{i\phi\sigma_{z}}c_{i,j}

(S79)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle c_{i,j}^{{\dagger}}e^{i\theta^{\prime}\sigma_{z}}c_{i,j+1}+c_{i,j+1}^{{\dagger}}e^{i\phi^{\prime}\sigma_{z}}c_{i,j}+c_{i,j}^{{\dagger}}(\boldsymbol{\delta\cdot\sigma})c_{i,j},

where
c_{i,j}
and
c_{i,j}^{{\dagger}}
are the annihilation and creation operators, respectively, at site
\left(i,j\right)
with spin, and the phases
\theta,\phi,\theta^{\prime},\phi^{\prime}
are tunable parameters. The Zeeman term
\boldsymbol{\delta\cdot\sigma}
can be induced by a magnetic background
[
54
]
, or an in-plane magnetic field where the gauge can be chosen to vanish. When a real in-plane magnetic field is applied, the gauge can be chosen as
\mathbf{A}=(0,0,\delta_{x}y-\delta_{y}x)
or
(0,-\delta_{x}z,0)
. The lattice model could be gauge free. Thus, the eigenenergies are gauge-invariant, and the skin effects remain in different gauge.

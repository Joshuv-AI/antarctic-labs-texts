Approximation of Discontinuous Functions by Kantorovich Exponential Sampling Series


Abstract
. The Kantorovich exponential sampling series I w χ ​ f I_{w}^{\chi}f at jump discontinuities of the bounded measurable signal f : ℝ + → ℝ f:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R} has been analysed. A representation lemma for the series I w χ ​ f I_{w}^{\chi}f is established and using this lemma certain approximation theorems for discontinuous signals are proved. The degree of approximation in terms of logarithmic modulus of smoothness for the series I w χ ​ f I_{w}^{\chi}f is studied. Further a linear prediction of signals based on past sample values has been obtained. Some numerical simulations are performed to validate the approximation of discontinuous signals f f by I w χ ​ f . I_{w}^{\chi}f. Keywords: Kantorovich Exponential Sampling Series, Discontinuous Signals, Logarithmic Modulus of Smoothness, Mellin Transform, Degree of Approximation. Introduction

1.1.
Preliminaries and Basic Assumptions

First we give some basic definitions and notations. Let
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
denote the set of all Lebesgue measurable essentially bounded functions on the set of all positive real numbers
\mathbb{R}^{+}
. For
c\in\mathbb{R}
, we define

X_{c}=\{f:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}:f(\cdot)(\cdot)^{c-1}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\}

and the norm on
X_{c}
is given by

\|f\|_{X_{c}}=\|f(\cdot)(\cdot)^{c-1}\|_{1}=\int_{0}^{+\infty}|f(v)|v^{c-1}dv.

The Mellin transform of a function
f\in X_{c}
is defined as

\widehat{[f]_{M}}(s):=\int_{0}^{+\infty}v^{s-1}f(v)\ dv\ ,\,\ (s=c+it,t\in\mathbb{R})

and that it is said to be Mellin band-limited on
[-\kappa,\kappa],
if
\widehat{[f]_{M}}(c+it)=0
for all
|t|>\kappa,
where
\kappa
is some positive real number.
Butzer and Jansche in
[
14
]
have initiated the study of exponential sampling. For a suitable kernel
\chi:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying certain conditions, Bardaro et.al.
[
6
]
have analysed the uniform and point-wise convergence of the following generalized sampling series for bounded continuous functions:

(S_{w}^{\chi}f)(t)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}\chi(e^{-k}t^{w})f(e^{\frac{k}{w}})

(1.1)

The above sampling series
S_{w}^{\chi}f
is well defined for
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+}).
In
[
8
]
, the exponential sampling series (
1.1
) was studied for functions in Mellin-Lebesgue spaces. The Mellin theory received a lot of attentions due to analysis by Mamedov
[
26
]
. Further it was developed by Butzer et.al. in various research articles (see
[
15
]
-
[
20
]
). Some of the work which are related to Mellin transform and Mellin approximation can be seen in
[
5
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
. Bardaro et.al.
[
4
]
analysed the analogue of Paley-Wiener theorem for Mellin transform and pointed out that it is different from that of Fourier transform. The exponential sampling series is used in certain areas like light scattering, Fraunhofer diffraction and radio astronomy, etc (see
[
12
,
22
,
24
,
27
]
).

Let
\chi:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}.
For
\nu\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\},
the algebraic and absolute moment of order
\nu
are defined by

m_{\nu}(\chi,u):=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}\chi(e^{-k}u)(k-\log u)^{\nu},\hskip 14.22636pt\forall\ u\in\mathbb{R}^{+}

and

M_{\nu}(\chi,u):=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}|\chi(e^{-k}u)||k-\log u|^{\nu},\hskip 14.22636pt\forall\ u\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.

We define
\displaystyle M_{\nu}(\chi):=\sup_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{+}}M_{\nu}(\chi,u).
We say that
\chi
is kernel if it satisfies the following conditions:

(i)

\chi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
and
\chi
is bounded on
\left[\dfrac{1}{e},e\right],

(ii)

for every
u\in\mathbb{R}{{}^{+}},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}\chi(e^{-k}u)=1,

(iii)

for some
\nu>0,

\displaystyle M_{\nu}(\chi,u)=\sup_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{+}}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}|\chi(e^{-k}u)||k-\log u|^{\nu}<+\infty.

1.2.
Kantorovich Exponential Sampling Series

The exponential sampling series
S_{w}^{\chi}f
is used to approximate a non-Mellin band limited function
f
using its values at the nodes
(e^{\frac{k}{w}}).
Measuring the exact value of the sample is difficult and measurement process depends on the aperture device used for sampling. A suitable mathematical model for such a measurement process is replacing
f(e^{\frac{k}{w}})
by the local average of signal
f
on the interval
\left[e^{\frac{k}{w}},e^{\frac{k+1}{w}}\right].
This motivates the consideration of Kantorovich version of the exponential sampling series. In 2020, Kantorovich exponential sampling series
S_{w}^{\chi}f
was introduced and their inverse and direct approximation results have been studied in
[
29
]
. For
f:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R},
the Kantorovich exponential sampling series of
f
is defined by (see
[
29
]
)

(I_{w}^{\chi}f)(x)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}\chi(e^{-k}x^{w})\ w\int_{\frac{k}{w}}^{\frac{k+1}{w}}f(e^{u})\ du.

(1.2)

Under the conditions (i) (ii) and (iii) on the kernel
\chi,
it is clear that
I_{w}^{\chi}f
is well defined for
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
at every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.

Direct convergence results and the improved order of approximation for Kantorovich exponential sampling series has been analysed in
[
31
]
. Further, the Durrmeyer type modification of exponential sampling series (
1.1
) is considered in
[
1
]
and
[
30
]
. So far the approximation of continuous functions by Kantorovich exponential sampling series
I_{w}^{\chi}f
has been studied. The Kantorovich exponential sampling series
I_{w}^{\chi}f
for discontinuous signals has not been analysed. Motivated by Butzer et.al.
[
21
]
and
[
23
]
, we study the behaviour of Kantorovich exponential sampling series
I_{w}^{\chi}f
at jump discontinuity of bounded measurable signal
f.

2.
Approximation of Discontinuous Functions by
I_{w}^{\chi}f

We analyse the behaviour of the Kantorovich exponential sampling series
I_{w}^{\chi}f
as
w\rightarrow\infty
at a jump discontinuity of
f
, i.e., at a point where the limits

f(t+0):=\lim_{y\rightarrow 0^{+}}f(t+y),

and

f(t-0):=\lim_{y\rightarrow 0^{+}}f(t-y)

exist and are different. We denote
\psi_{\chi}^{+}
and
\psi_{\chi}^{-}
two functions from
\mathbb{R}^{+}
to
\mathbb{R}
which are defined by

\psi_{\chi}^{+}(u):=\sum_{k<\log u}\chi(ue^{-k}),

and

\psi_{\chi}^{-}(u):=\sum_{k>\log u}\chi(ue^{-k}),

where
\chi
is a kernel function and one can easily verify that
\psi_{\chi}^{+}(u)
and
\psi_{\chi}^{-}(u)
are recurrent functions with fundamental interval
[1,e]
. We first prove the following representation lemma for the series
I_{w}^{\chi}f.
Throughout this section we assume that
f(t+0)
and
f(t-0)
exist and are finite.

Lemma 1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a bounded measurable signal and
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
be fixed. Let
h_{t}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be defined by

\displaystyle h_{t}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{f(x)-f(t-0),}&\mbox{if}\ x<t\\
{f(x)-f(t+0),}&\mbox{if}\ x>t\\
{0,}&\mbox{if,}\ x=t.\end{array}\right.

Then, there holds:

\displaystyle(I_{w}^{\chi}f)(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(I_{w}^{\chi}h_{t})(t)+f(t-0)+\left[\chi(e^{w\log t-\lfloor w\log t\rfloor})+\psi_{\chi}^{-}(t^{w})\right](f(t+0)-f(t-0))

\displaystyle-\chi\left(e^{w\log t-\lfloor w\log t\rfloor}\right)\left(w\log t-\lfloor w\log t\rfloor\right)(f(t+0)-f(t-0)),

if
w\log(t)\notin\mathbb{Z},
where
\lfloor.\rfloor
denotes the integer part of a given number and

\displaystyle(I_{w}^{\chi}f)(t)=(I_{w}^{\chi}h_{t})(t)+f(t-0)+\left(\chi(1)+\psi_{\chi}^{-}(t^{w})\right)[f(t+0)-f(t-0)],

if
w\log(t)\in\mathbb{Z},

w>0.

Proof.

Let
w\log(t)\notin\mathbb{Z},
and
w>0.
Then, we have

\displaystyle(I_{w}^{\chi}h_{t})(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k<\lfloor w\log t\rfloor}\chi(e^{-k}t^{w})w\int_{\frac{k}{w}}^{\frac{k+1}{w}}(f(e^{u})-f(t-0))du

\displaystyle+\sum_{k>\lfloor w\log t\rfloor}\chi(e^{-k}t^{w})w\int_{\frac{k}{w}}^{\frac{k+1}{w}}(f(e^{u})-f(t+0))du

\displaystyle+\chi\left(e^{w\log t-\lfloor w\log t\rfloor}\right)w\left(\int_{\frac{\lfloor w\log t\rfloor}{w}}^{\log t}(f(e^{u})-f(t-0))du+\int_{\log t}^{\frac{\lfloor w\log t\rfloor+1}{w}}(f(e^{u})-f(t+0))du\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k<\lfloor w\log t\rfloor}\chi(e^{-k}t^{w})w\int_{\frac{k}{w}}^{\frac{k+1}{w}}f(e^{u})du-f(t-0)\sum_{k<\lfloor w\log t\rfloor}\chi(e^{-k}t^{w})

\displaystyle+\sum_{k>\lfloor w\log t\rfloor}\chi(e^{-k}t^{w})w\int_{\frac{k}{w}}^{\frac{k+1}{w}}f(e^{u})du-f(t+0)\sum_{k>\lfloor w\log t\rfloor}\chi(e^{-k}t^{w})

\displaystyle+\chi\left(e^{w\log t-\lfloor w\log t\rfloor}\right)w\int_{\frac{\lfloor w\log t\rfloor}{w}}^{\frac{\lfloor w\log t\rfloor+1}{w}}f(e^{u})du-\chi\left(e^{w\log t-\lfloor w\log t\rfloor}\right)

\displaystyle\times w\left(f(t-0)\left(\log t-{\frac{\lfloor w\log t\rfloor}{w}}\right)+f(t+0)\left({\frac{\lfloor w\log t\rfloor+1}{w}}-\log t\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(I_{w}^{\chi}f)(t)-f(t-0)\sum_{k<\lfloor w\log t\rfloor}\chi(e^{-k}t^{w})-f(t+0)\sum_{k>\lfloor w\log t\rfloor}\chi(e^{-k}t^{w})

\displaystyle-\chi\left(e^{w\log t-\lfloor w\log t\rfloor}\right)\left[f(t-0)(w\log t-{{\lfloor w\log t\rfloor}})+f(t+0)({\lfloor w\log t\rfloor+1}-w\log t)\right].

Adding and subtracting
\displaystyle f(t-0)\sum_{k\geq\lfloor w\log(t)\rfloor}\chi(e^{-k}t^{w})
in the above equation, we obtain

\displaystyle(I_{w}^{\chi}f)(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(I_{w}^{\chi}h_{t})(t)+f(t-0)\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi(e^{-k}t^{w})-f(t-0)\sum_{k>\lfloor w\log t\rfloor}\chi(e^{-k}t^{w})

\displaystyle-f(t-0)\chi\left(e^{w\log t-\lfloor w\log t\rfloor}\right)+f(t+0)\chi\left(e^{w\log t-\lfloor w\log t\rfloor}\right)+f(t+0)\sum_{k>\lfloor w\log t\rfloor}\chi(e^{-k}t^{w})

\displaystyle+\chi\left(e^{w\log t-\lfloor w\log t\rfloor}\right)\Big[f(t-0)(w\log t-{{\lfloor w\log t\rfloor}})+f(t+0)({\lfloor w\log t\rfloor}-w\log t)\Big].

As
w\log t
is not an integer, one can easily verify that

\psi_{\chi}^{-}(t^{w})=\sum_{k>\lfloor w\log t\rfloor}\chi(e^{-k}t^{w})

and in view of condition (ii) we obtain

\displaystyle(I_{w}^{\chi}f)(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(I_{w}^{\chi}h_{t})(t)+f(t-0)+\left[\chi\left(e^{w\log t-\lfloor w\log t\rfloor}\right)+\psi_{\chi}^{-}(t^{w})\right](f(t+0)-f(t-0))

\displaystyle-\chi\left(e^{w\log t-\lfloor w\log t\rfloor}\right)\left(w\log t-\lfloor w\log t\rfloor\right)(f(t+0)-f(t-0)).

Now we analyse the case that
w\log(t)\in\mathbb{Z},
and
w>0.
We have

\displaystyle(I_{w}^{\chi}h_{t})(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k<w\log t}\chi(e^{-k}t^{w})w\int_{\frac{k}{w}}^{\frac{k+1}{w}}(f(e^{u})-f(t-0))du

\displaystyle+\sum_{k\geq w\log t}\chi(e^{-k}t^{w})w\int_{\frac{k}{w}}^{\frac{k+1}{w}}(f(e^{u})-f(t+0))du.

Adding and subtracting
\displaystyle f(t-0)\sum_{k\geq w\log(t)}\chi(e^{-k}t^{w})
and using the second condition on the kernel, we get

\displaystyle(I_{w}^{\chi}f)(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(I_{w}^{\chi}h_{t})(t)+f(t-0)+(f(t+0)-f(t-0))\sum_{k\geq w\log(t)}\chi(e^{-k}t^{w})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(I_{w}^{\chi}h_{t})(t)+f(t-0)+\left(\chi(1)+\psi_{\chi}^{-}(t^{w})\right)[f(t+0)-f(t-0)].

∎

We recall the following basic convergence result for continuous functions proved in
[
29
]
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a bounded function . Then,
(I_{w}^{\chi}{f})(t)
converges to
f(t)
at any point
t
of continuity. Further,
if
f\in C(\mathbb{R}^{+}),
then

\lim_{w\rightarrow\infty}\|f-I_{w}^{\chi}{f}\|_{\infty}=0.

Now we show that the non-removable jump discontinuity function can be approximated by Kantorovich exponential sampling series
I_{w}^{\chi}f
for
w\log(t)\in\mathbb{Z}.

Theorem 2
.

Suppose that
f
has a non-removable jump discontinuity at
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and that
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}.
Then, the following are equivalent:

(i)

\displaystyle\lim_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w\rightarrow\infty}}{{w\log(t)\in\mathbb{Z}}}}(I_{w}^{\chi}f)(t)=[\chi(1)+\alpha]f(t+0)+[1-\alpha-\chi(1)]f(t-0),\hskip 7.11317pt

(ii)

\psi_{\chi}^{-}(1):=\alpha,

(iii)

\psi_{\chi}^{+}(1):=1-\alpha-\chi(1).

Proof.

Let
w\log(t)\in\mathbb{Z}.
In view of Lemma
1
, we have

\displaystyle(I_{w}^{\chi}f)(t)=(I_{w}^{\chi}h_{t})(t)+f(t-0)+(\chi(1)+\psi_{\chi}^{-}(t^{w}))[f(t+0)-f(t-0)],

for any
w>0.
Since
h_{t}
is bounded and continuous at zero and applying Theorem
1
, we get

\displaystyle\lim_{w\rightarrow\infty}(I_{w}^{\chi}h_{t})(t)=0.

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w\rightarrow\infty}}{{w\log(t)\in\mathbb{Z}}}}(I_{w}^{\chi}f)(t)=f(t-0)+\left(\chi(1)+\displaystyle\lim_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w\rightarrow\infty}}{{w\log(t)\in\mathbb{Z}}}}\psi_{\chi}^{-}(t^{w})\right)[f(t+0)-f(t-0)].

Since
\psi_{\chi}^{-}
is recurrent function with fundamental domain
[1,e],
we get

\psi_{\chi}^{-}(t^{w})=\psi_{\chi}^{-}(1),\,\ \forall w,t\,\ \mbox{such that}\,\ w\log(t)\in\mathbb{Z}.

Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w\rightarrow\infty}}{{w\log(t)\in\mathbb{Z}}}}(I_{w}^{\chi}f)(t)=[\chi(1)+\psi_{\chi}^{-}(1)]f(t+0)+[1-\psi_{\chi}^{-}(1)-\chi(1)]f(t-0).

\mbox{Now}\,\ (i)\Longleftrightarrow[\chi(1)+\alpha]f(t+0)+[1-\alpha-\chi(1)]f(t-0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\chi(1)+\psi_{\chi}^{-}(1)]f(t+0)+[1-\psi_{\chi}^{-}(1)-\chi(1)]f(t-0)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\psi_{\chi}^{-}(1)(f(t+0)-f(t-0))=\alpha(f(t+0)-f(t-0))

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\psi_{\chi}^{-}(1)=\alpha

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle(ii)\,\ \mbox{holds}.

Since
\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}\chi(e^{-k}t^{w})=1,
we have

\psi_{\chi}^{+}(1)=1-{\chi}(1)-\psi_{\chi}^{-}(1).

This implies that
(ii)\Longleftrightarrow(iii).
Hence, the proof is completed.
∎

Next in order to obtain the convergence of Kantorovich exponential sampling series
I_{w}^{\chi}f
at a non-removable jump discontinuity at
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
when
w\log(t)\notin\mathbb{Z}
an extra condition on the kernel is required. We prove the following theorem.

Theorem 3
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a bounded measurable signal with a non-removable jump discontinuity at
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}.
Suppose that
\chi(u)=0,
for every
u\in(1,e).
Then, the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\displaystyle\lim_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w\rightarrow\infty}}{{w\log(t)\notin\mathbb{Z}}}}(I_{w}^{\chi}f)(t)=\alpha f(t+0)+(1-\alpha)f(t-0),

(ii)

\psi_{\chi}^{-}(u):=\alpha,\hskip 14.22636ptu\in(1,e)

(iii)

\psi_{\chi}^{+}(u):=1-\alpha,\hskip 14.22636ptu\in(1,e).

Proof.

Using Lemma
1
and the condition that
\chi(u)=0,
for every
u\in(1,e),
we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w\rightarrow\infty}}{{w\log(t)\notin\mathbb{Z}}}}(I_{w}^{\chi}f)(t)=f(t-0)+\left(\displaystyle\lim_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w\rightarrow\infty}}{{w\log(t)\notin\mathbb{Z}}}}\psi_{\chi}^{-}(t^{w})\right)[f(t+0)-f(t-0)].

\displaystyle(i)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\alpha f(t+0)+(1-\alpha)f(t-0)=f(t-0)+\left(\displaystyle\lim_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w\rightarrow\infty}}{{w\log(t)\notin\mathbb{Z}}}}\psi_{\chi}^{-}(t^{w})\right)[f(t+0)-f(t-0)]

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\alpha[f(t+0)-f(t-0)]=\left(\displaystyle\lim_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w\rightarrow\infty}}{{w\log(t)\notin\mathbb{Z}}}}\psi_{\chi}^{-}(t^{w})\right)[f(t+0)-f(t-0)]

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\alpha=\displaystyle\lim_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w\rightarrow\infty}}{{w\log(t)\notin\mathbb{Z}}}}\psi_{\chi}^{-}(t^{w})

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\alpha=\psi_{\chi}^{-}(u),\hskip 14.22636pt\forall u\in(1,e)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle(ii)\,\ \mbox{holds}.

Let
w\log(t)\notin\mathbb{Z}.
Since
\psi_{\chi}^{+}
and
\psi_{\chi}^{-}
are recurrent functions on
[1,e],
we have

\psi_{\chi}^{+}(t^{w})+\psi_{\chi}^{-}(t^{w})=1.

Hence, we obtain

(ii)\Longleftrightarrow\psi_{\chi}^{+}(u)=1-\alpha,\hskip 14.22636ptu\in(1,e).

∎

The above Theorem is proved by assuming that
\chi(u)=0,
for every
u\in(1,e).
In the following theorem we prove that without this condition, it is not possible to show the convergence of
I_{w}^{\chi}f
at jump discontinuities.

Theorem 4
.

Let
\chi
be a kernel such that
\psi_{\chi}^{-}(u)=\alpha
for every
u\in(1,e)
and
\chi(u)\neq 0,
for some
u\in(1,e).
Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a bounded measurable signal with a non-removable jump discontinuity
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.
Then
(I_{w}^{\chi}f)(t)
does not converge point-wise at
t
.

Proof.

Suppose on contrary that
(I_{w}^{\chi}f)(t)
converge point-wise at
t,
that is
\displaystyle\lim_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w\rightarrow\infty}}{{w\log(t)\notin\mathbb{Z}}}}(I_{w}^{\chi}f)(t)=\ell,
for some
\ell\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.
Using the existence of the limit and Lemma
1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\ell

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(t-0)+\left(\displaystyle\lim_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w\rightarrow\infty}}{{w\log(t)\notin\mathbb{Z}}}}\left[\chi(e^{w\log t-\lfloor w\log t\rfloor})+\psi_{\chi}^{-}(t^{w})\right]\right)(f(t+0)-f(t-0))

\displaystyle-[f(t+0)-f(t-0)]\left(\displaystyle\lim_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w\rightarrow\infty}}{{w\log(t)\notin\mathbb{Z}}}}\chi\left(e^{w\log t-\lfloor w\log t\rfloor}\right)\left(w\log t-\lfloor w\log t\rfloor\right)\right).

Since
\psi_{\chi}^{-}(u)=\alpha
for every
u\in(1,e),

\forall w>0,\,\ w\log(t)\notin\mathbb{Z},
we have

w\log t-\lfloor w\log t\rfloor=\xi\in(1,e),
so we obtain

\displaystyle\ell

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(t-0)+[f(t+0)-f(t-0)](\alpha+\chi(\xi))-[f(t+0)-f(t-0)]\xi\chi(\xi),\,\ \forall\,\xi\in(1,e).

Since
f
has a jump discontinuity
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+},
we obtain

\displaystyle\chi(\xi)=\left(\frac{\ell-f(t-0)}{f(t+0)-f(t-0)}-\alpha\right)\left(\frac{1}{1-\xi}\right),\,\ \forall\,\xi\in(1,e).

The above expression gives a contradiction. Indeed, if

\displaystyle\frac{\ell-f(t-0)}{f(t+0)-f(t-0)}-\alpha:=C\neq 0,

then
\chi(\xi)
is unbounded on
(1,e),
so it fails to satisfy condition (i). If
C=0,
then
\chi(\xi)=0,
for every
\xi\in(1,e),
this is again a contradiction. Hence the proof is completed.
∎

Next we prove a more general theorem on the convergence of the sampling series
I_{w}^{\chi}f
for any bounded signal
f
at jump discontinuities.

Theorem 5
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a bounded measurable signal and let
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
be a non-removable jump discontinuity point of
f.
For any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and any kernel function
\chi
satisfying the additional condition
\chi(u)=0,
for every
u\in[1,e),
the following are equivalent:

(i)

\displaystyle\lim_{w\rightarrow\infty}(I_{w}^{\chi}f)(t)=\alpha f(t+0)+(1-\alpha)f(t-0),

(ii)

\psi_{\chi}^{-}(u):=\alpha,
for every
u\in[1,e)

(iii)

\psi_{\chi}^{+}(u):=1-\alpha,
for every
u\in[1,e).

If we further assume that
\chi
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{+},
then the above statements are also equivalent to the following:

(iv)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\chi(u)u^{2k\pi i}\frac{du}{u}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\ \ \ \mbox{if}\ \ \ k\neq 0\\
\alpha,&\ \ \ \mbox{if}\ \ \ k=0\end{array}\right.

(v)

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\chi(u)u^{2k\pi i}\frac{du}{u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\ \ \ \mbox{if}\ \ \ k\neq 0\\
1-\alpha,&\ \ \ \mbox{if}\ \ \ k=0.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Proceeding along the lines proof of Theorem
2
and Theorem
3
we see that
(i),(ii)
and
(iii)
are equivalent. Now we assume that
\chi
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{+}.
Define

\displaystyle\chi_{0}(u)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\chi(u),&\ \ \ \mbox{for}\ \ \ u<1\\
0,&\ \ \ \mbox{for}\ \ \ u\geq 1.\end{array}\right.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\psi_{\chi}^{-}(u)=\sum_{k>\log u}\chi(ue^{-k})=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi_{0}(ue^{-k})

is a recurrent continuous function with the fundamental interval
[1,e].
By the Mellin Poisson’s summation formula, we get

\displaystyle\psi_{\chi}^{-}(u)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}\widehat{[\chi_{0}]_{M}}(2k\pi i)\ u^{-2k\pi i}=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\chi(u)u^{2k\pi i}\frac{du}{u}\right)u^{-2k\pi i}.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\psi_{\chi}^{-}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha,\forall u\in[1,e)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\widehat{[\chi]_{M}}(2k\pi i)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\ \ \ \mbox{if}\ \ \ k\neq 0\\
\alpha,&\ \ \ \mbox{if}\ \ \ k=0\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\chi(u)u^{2k\pi i}\frac{du}{u}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\ \ \ \mbox{if}\ \ \ k\neq 0\\
\alpha,&\ \ \ \mbox{if}\ \ \ k=0.\end{array}\right.

Hence, we have
(ii)\Longleftrightarrow(iv).
Using the condition

\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}\chi(e^{-k}u)=1\Longleftrightarrow\widehat{[\chi]_{M}}(2k\pi i)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\ \ \ \mbox{if}\ \ \ k\neq 0\\
1,&\ \ \ \mbox{if}\ \ \ k=0\end{array}\right.

the equivalence between
(iv)
and
(v)
can be established easily.
∎

Remark 1
.

If
f:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a bounded measurable signal with a removable discontinuity
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, then we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w\rightarrow\infty}}{{w\log(t)\in\mathbb{Z}}}}(I_{w}^{\chi}f)(t)=\displaystyle\lim_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w\rightarrow\infty}}{{w\log(t)\notin\mathbb{Z}}}}(I_{w}^{\chi}f)(t)=\displaystyle\lim_{w\rightarrow\infty}(I_{w}^{\chi}f)(t)=\ell.

3.
Degree of Approximation

We study the degree of approximation of
I_{w}^{\chi}f
by using the logarithmic modulus of continuity. A function
f:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is said to be
log-uniformly continuous if for every
\epsilon>0,

\exists\,\ \delta>0
such that
|f(s)-f(t)|<\epsilon
whenever
|\log s-\log t|<\delta,
for any
s,t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.
The set of all bounded log-uniformly continuous functions is
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{+}).
It is easy to see that
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
is contained in
C(\mathbb{R}^{+}),
where
C(\mathbb{R}^{+})
is the set of all bounded continuous functions on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
with usual sup norm
\|f\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{+}}|f(x)|.
For
f\in C(\mathbb{R}^{+}),
the logarithmic modulus of continuity is defined by

\omega(f,\delta):=\sup\{|f(s)-f(t)|:\ \mbox{whenever}\ |\log(s)-\log(t)|\leq\delta,\ \ \delta\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\}.

\omega(f,\delta)
has the following properties:

(a)

\omega(f,\delta)\rightarrow 0,
as
\delta\rightarrow 0.

(b)

\displaystyle|f(s)-f(t)|\leq\omega(f,\delta)\left(1+\dfrac{|\log s-\log t|}{\delta}\right).

Now we show the order of convergence for the Kantorovich exponential sampling series when
M_{\nu}(\chi)<+\infty
for
0<\nu<1.

Theorem 6
.

Let
\chi
be a kernel such that
M_{\nu}(\chi)<+\infty
for
0<\nu<1.
Then for any
f\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
and for sufficiently large
w>0,
we have

|(I_{w}^{\chi}f)(t)-f(t)|\leq\omega(f,w^{-\nu})[M_{\nu}(\chi)+2M_{0}(\chi)]+2^{\nu+1}\|f\|_{\infty}M_{\nu}(\chi)w^{-\nu},

for every
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.

Proof.

Let
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
be fixed. Then using the condition
\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}\chi(e^{-k}t^{w})=1,
we obtain

\displaystyle|(I_{w}^{\chi}f)(t)-f(t)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}\chi(e^{-k}t^{w})w\int_{\frac{k}{w}}^{\frac{k+1}{w}}(f(e^{u})-f(t))du\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{\big|k-w\log t\big|\leq\frac{w}{2}}+\sum_{\big|k-w\log t\big|>\frac{w}{2}}\right)\big|\chi(e^{-k}t^{w})\big|w\int_{\frac{k}{w}}^{\frac{k+1}{w}}|f(e^{u})-f(t)|du

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}.

Let
u\in\left[\frac{k}{w},\frac{k+1}{w}\right].
If
|w\log t-k|\leq\frac{w}{2}
, we have

|u-\log t|\leq\left|u-\frac{k}{w}\right|+\left|\frac{k}{w}-\log t\right|\leq\frac{1}{w}+\frac{1}{2}\leq 1.
Let
0<\nu<1
and for every
w>0
sufficiently large, we have

\omega\left(f,\left|u-\log t\right|\right)\leq\omega\left(f,\left|u-\log t\right|^{\nu}\right).

Therefore, using the above inequality and the property
(b),
we obtain

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\left|k-w\log t\right|\leq\frac{w}{2}}\left|\chi(e^{-k}t^{w})\right|w\int_{\frac{k}{w}}^{\frac{k+1}{w}}\omega\left(f,\left|u-\log t\right|^{\nu}\right)du

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\left|k-w\log t\right|\leq\frac{w}{2}}\left|\chi(e^{-k}t^{w})\right|w\int_{\frac{k}{w}}^{\frac{k+1}{w}}\left(1+w^{\nu}\left|u-\log t\right|^{\nu}\right)\omega(f,w^{-\nu})du

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\omega(f,w^{-\nu})\left(\sum_{\left|k-w\log t\right|\leq\frac{w}{2}}\left|\chi(e^{-k}t^{w})\right|w\int_{\frac{k}{w}}^{\frac{k+1}{w}}w^{\nu}\left|u-w\log t\right|^{\nu}du+\sum_{\left|k-w\log t\right|\leq\frac{w}{2}}\left|\chi(e^{-k}t^{w})\right|\right)

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle I_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}+I_{2}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}.

Using the condition
(ii)
we easily obtain
I_{2}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}\leq m_{0}(\chi).
Utilizing the sub-additivity of
|.|^{\nu}
for
0<\nu<1
, we have:

\displaystyle I_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\left|k-w\log t\right|\leq\frac{w}{2}}\left|\chi(e^{-k}t^{w})\right|\left(w^{\nu}\max_{u\in\left[\frac{k}{w},\frac{k+1}{w}\right]}\left|u-\log t\right|^{\nu}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\left|k-w\log t\right|\leq\frac{w}{2}}\left|\chi(e^{-k}t^{w})\right|w^{\nu}\left(\max\left(\left|\frac{k}{w}-\log t\right|^{\nu},\left|\frac{k+1}{w}-\log t\right|^{\nu}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\left|k-w\log t\right|\leq\frac{w}{2}}\left|\chi(e^{-k}t^{w})\right|\left(w^{\nu}\max\left(\left|\frac{k}{w}-\log t\right|^{\nu},\left|\frac{k}{w}-\log t\right|^{\nu}+\frac{1}{w^{\nu}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\left|k-w\log t\right|\leq\frac{w}{2}}\left|\chi(e^{-k}t^{w})\right|\left(w^{\nu}\left(\left|\frac{k}{w}-\log t\right|^{\nu}+\frac{1}{w^{\nu}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M_{\nu}(\chi)+M_{0}(\chi)<+\infty.

Finally we estimate
I_{2}
:

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\|f\|_{\infty}\sum_{\big|k-w\log t\big|>\frac{w}{2}}\big|\chi(e^{-k}t^{w})\big|\leq 2\|f\|_{\infty}\sum_{\big|k-w\log t\big|>\frac{w}{2}}\frac{\big|k-w\log t\big|^{\nu}}{\big|k-w\log t\big|^{\nu}}\big|\chi(e^{-k}t^{w})\big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{\nu+1}\|f\|_{\infty}w^{-\nu}\sum_{\big|k-w\log t\big|>\frac{w}{2}}\big|\chi(e^{-k}t^{w})\big|\big|k-w\log t\big|^{\nu}\leq 2^{\nu+1}\|f\|_{\infty}w^{-\nu}M_{\nu}(\chi)<+\infty.

On combining the estimates
I_{1}
and
I_{2},
we get the desired result.
∎

4.
Linear Prediction of Signals

The Kantorovich exponential sampling series can be used for predicting the signals at a time
t
using the sample values taken in the past. This is addressed in the following theorem.

Theorem 7
.

Let
\chi
be a kernel with compact support and
f:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a bounded measurable signals and
w>0.
If
supp\,\chi\subset(1,\infty),
then for every
w>0
and
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
with
w\log t\in\mathbb{Z},
we have

(I_{w}^{\chi}f)(t)=\sum_{k<w\log t}\chi(e^{-k}t^{w})w\int_{\frac{k}{w}}^{\frac{k+1}{w}}f(e^{u})du.

Further, if
supp\,\chi\subset(e,\infty),
then for every
w>0
and
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
with
w\log t\notin\mathbb{Z},
there holds

(I_{w}^{\chi}f)(t)=\sum_{k\leq\lfloor w\log t\rfloor-1}\chi(e^{-k}t^{w})w\int_{\frac{k}{w}}^{\frac{k+1}{w}}f(e^{u})du.

Proof.

Let
w>0.
As
supp\,\chi

\subset(1,\infty),
we obtain
\chi(e^{-k}t^{w})=0,
for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
e^{-k}t^{w}\leq 1,
this implies that
\log t\leq\frac{k}{w}.
If
w\log t\in\mathbb{Z}
, it is easy to observe that the last term of the Kantorovich exponential sampling series is

\displaystyle\chi(e)\left(w\int_{\log t-\frac{1}{w}}^{\log t}f(e^{u})du\right).

Therefore, we see that Kantorovich exponential sampling series exploit the values of the signal
f
in the past with respect to fixed time
t.

Now the second case can be estimated as follows: Let
supp\,\chi\subset(e,\infty).
If
w\log t\notin\mathbb{Z}
then
\lfloor w\log t\rfloor<w\log t<\lfloor w\log t\rfloor+1
. Finally, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k<w\log t}\chi(e^{-k}t^{w})w\int_{\frac{k}{w}}^{\frac{k+1}{w}}f(e^{u})du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\leq\lfloor w\log t\rfloor-1}\chi(e^{-k}t^{w})w\int_{\frac{k}{w}}^{\frac{k+1}{w}}f(e^{u})du

\displaystyle+\chi(e^{w\log t-\lfloor w\log t\rfloor})\left(w\int_{\frac{\lfloor w\log t\rfloor}{w}}^{\frac{\lfloor w\log t\rfloor+1}{w}}f(e^{u})du\right).

Since
\chi(e^{w\log t-\lfloor w\log t\rfloor})=0
if
0<w\log t-\lfloor w\log t\rfloor<1,
we get

(I_{w}^{\chi}f)(t)=\sum_{k\leq\lfloor w\log t\rfloor-1}\chi(e^{-k}t^{w})w\int_{\frac{k}{w}}^{\frac{k+1}{w}}f(e^{u})du.

Hence the proof is completed.
∎

Remark 2
.

It is observed that in order to approximate the signal at a time
t,
the local average values of the signals are computed before the time instance
t.
The above theorem can be used for predicting the future signal values from the past value of the signal.

5.
Examples of the Kernels and Special Cases

5.1.
Mellin-B spline kernels

The Mellin B-splines of order
n
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
are given by

\bar{B}_{n}(x):=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{j}{n\choose j}\bigg(\frac{n}{2}+\log x-j\bigg)_{+}^{n+1}.

\bar{B}_{n}(x)
is compactly supported for every
n\in\mathbb{N}.
So it is bounded and it belongs to
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+}).
Therefore condition (i) is satisfied. The Mellin transform of
\bar{B}_{n}
(see
[
6
]
) is

\displaystyle\widehat{[\bar{B}_{n}]_{M}}(c+it)=\bigg(\frac{\sin(\frac{t}{2})}{(\frac{t}{2})}\Bigg)^{n},\ \ \hskip 14.22636ptt\neq 0.

Using the Mellin’s-Poisson summation formula (see
[
6
]
), it is easy to see that
\bar{B}_{n}(x)
satisfies the condition (ii). The condition (iii) is also easily verified.

5.2.
Mellin Jackson kernels

For
y\in\mathbb{R}^{+},\beta\in\mathbb{N},\gamma\geq 1,
the Mellin Jackson kernels are defined by

J_{\gamma,\beta}^{-1}(y):=d_{\gamma,\beta}\,\ y^{-c}sinc^{2\beta}\left(\frac{\log y}{2\gamma\beta\pi}\right),

where

d_{\gamma,\beta}^{-1}:=\int_{0}^{\infty}sinc^{2\beta}\left(\frac{\log y}{2\gamma\beta\pi}\right)\frac{du}{u}.

The Mellin Jackson kernels also satisfy the conditions (i),(ii) and (iii) (see
[
6
]
). We can study the convergence of the
I_{w}^{\chi}f
with jump discontinuity associated with these kernels only for the case given in Theorem
2
and we observe that
\chi(u)\neq 0,
for every
u\in(1,e)
. Hence, Theorem
3
and Theorem
5
can not be applied for these kernels. In order to find examples of the kernels for which the exponential sampling series converge at any jump discontinuity
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
of the given bounded signal
f:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, we need to construct suitable kernels. One such construction is given below.

Theorem 8
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and let
\chi_{a},\chi_{b}
be two continuous kernels satisfying conditions (i), (ii) and (iii) such that
supp\,\chi_{a}\subseteq{[e^{-a},e^{a}]}
and
supp\,\chi_{b}\subseteq{[e^{-b},e^{b}]}.
For a fixed
\alpha\in\mathbb{R},
define
\chi:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

\chi{(u)}:=(1-\alpha)\chi_{a}(ue^{-a-1})+\alpha\chi_{b}(ue^{b}),\,\,\ u\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.

Then
\chi
also satisfies conditions (i), (ii) and (iii) and in addition
\chi(u)=0,
for every
u\in[1,e).
Further, the Kantorovich exponential sampling series
I_{w}^{\chi}{f},w>0
corresponding to
\chi
satisfying (i) of Theorem
5
at any jump discontinuity
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
of the given bounded measurable signal
f
with
\alpha
as a parameter.

Proof.

We estimate the Mellin transform of
\chi(u)
as follows:

\displaystyle\widehat{[\chi]_{M}}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-\alpha)\chi_{a}(te^{-a-1})t^{c+iv-1}dt+\int_{0}^{\infty}\alpha\chi_{b}(te^{b})t^{c+iv-1}dt:=I_{1}+I_{2}.

Making substitution
u=e^{-a-1},\,\ du=e^{-a-1}dt
in
I_{1}
and
u=te^{b},\,\ du=e^{b}dt
in
I_{2},
we easily obtain

\displaystyle\widehat{[\chi]_{M}}(s)=(1-\alpha)\widehat{[\chi_{a}]_{M}}(s)e^{(1+a)s}+\alpha\widehat{[\chi_{b}]_{M}}(s)e^{-bs},

where
s=c+iv.
It is easy to check that
\chi
satisfies conditions (i) and (iii). Now we show that kernel satisfies the condition (ii). We obtain

\displaystyle\widehat{[\chi]_{M}}(2k\pi i)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\alpha)\widehat{[\chi_{a}]_{M}}(2k\pi i)e^{(1+a)(2k\pi i)}+\alpha\widehat{[\chi_{b}]_{M}}(2k\pi i)e^{-b(2k\pi i)}.

Since
\chi_{a}
and
\chi_{b}
satisfies condition (ii), we have

\displaystyle\widehat{[\chi_{a}]_{M}}(2k\pi i)=\widehat{[\chi_{b}]_{M}}(2k\pi i)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\ \ \ \mbox{if}\ \ \ k\neq 0\\
1,&\ \ \ \mbox{if}\ \ \ k=0.\end{array}\right.

For suitable choice of
a
and
b
, we obtain

\displaystyle\widehat{[\chi]_{M}}(2k\pi i)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\ \ \ \mbox{ if}\ \ \ k\neq 0\\
1,&\ \ \ \mbox{if}\ \ \ k=0.\end{array}\right.

Hence,
\chi
satisfies condition (ii) and we easily see that
\chi
also satisfies
\chi(u)=0
for
1\leq u<e.

Now, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\chi(u)u^{2k\pi i}\frac{du}{u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\alpha)\int_{1}^{\infty}\chi_{a}(ue^{-a-1})u^{2k\pi i}\frac{du}{u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\alpha)e^{2k\pi i(1+a)}\widehat{[\chi_{a}]_{M}}(2k\pi i)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\ \ \ \mbox{if}\ \ \ k\neq 0\\
1-\alpha,&\ \ \ \mbox{if}\ \ \ k=0.\end{array}\right.

Therefore, the condition (v) of Theorem is satisfied, hence the proof is completed.
∎

As a consequence of the following theorem, condition on the kernels can be given so that
M_{\nu}(\chi)<+\infty
for some
0\leq\nu<q,
for some
q<1
and

M_{\nu}(\chi)=+\infty
for
q<\nu\leq 1.

Theorem 9
.

Let
\chi:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a function such that

\displaystyle\frac{C_{1}}{|\log u|^{p}}\leq\chi({u})\leq\frac{C_{2}}{|\log u|^{p}},\hskip 28.45274pt\forall\,\ |\log u|>M

holds for suitable constants
0<C_{1}\leq C_{2}
and for some
1<p\leq 2,
and
M>0
. Then, we have

\displaystyle M_{\nu}(\chi)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}+\infty,&\ \ p-1\leq\nu\leq 1\\
<+\infty,&\ \ \ 0\leq\nu<p-1.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Let
p-1\leq\nu\leq 1,
and
u\in\mathbb{R^{+}}
be fixed. Since
p-\nu\leq 1,
we have

\displaystyle M_{\nu}(\chi)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k=+\infty}|\chi(e^{-k}u)||\log u-k|^{\nu}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k=+\infty}|\chi(e^{-k}u)||\log u-k|^{\nu}\frac{|\log u-k|^{p}}{|\log u-k|^{p}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C_{1}\sum_{|\log u-k|>M}|\log u-k|^{\nu-p}=+\infty.

Let
0\leq\nu<p-1
and
u\in\mathbb{R^{+}}.
Since
M_{0}(\chi)<+\infty
and
\chi(u)=O(|\log u|^{-p})
, as
|u|\rightarrow+\infty
with
p>1
and using the fact that
the series

\sum_{|\log u-k|>M}|\log u-k|^{\nu-p}

with
p-\nu>1
, is uniformly convergent for every
u\in\mathbb{R^{+}},
we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{k=+\infty}|\chi(e^{-k}u)||\log u-k|^{\nu}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{|\log u-k|\leq M}+\sum_{|\log u-k|>M}\right)|\chi(e^{-k}u)||\log u-k|^{\nu}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M^{\nu}M_{0}(\chi)+C_{2}\sum_{|\log u-k|>M}|\log u-k|^{\nu-p}<+\infty.

Hence, the proof is completed.
∎

Abstract

1
Introduction

Let
(B_{t}^{H})_{t\geq 0}
be the fractional Brownian motion which is a centered Gaussian process with covariance function given by:

\mathbb{E}(B_{t}B_{s}):=\frac{1}{2}\left(t^{2H}+s^{2H}-|t-s|^{2H}\right)

The parameter
H\in\,]0,1]
is called the parameter of Hurst. When
H=1/2
we have that
B^{H}_{t}
is the classical Brownian motion. Now we consider the following stochastic differential equation perturbed by the fractional white noise
B^{H}_{t}
:

dX_{t}=-\lambda X_{t}+\epsilon dB_{t}^{H}\quad\epsilon>0,\;\lambda>0

whose unique continuous solution given in [
References
], with initial condition
x

X^{\epsilon}_{t}=e^{-\lambda t}\left(x+\epsilon\int_{0}^{t}e^{\lambda s}dB_{s}^{H}\right).

This process
X_{t}^{\epsilon}
is called the fractional Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process (fOUp). We find an upper bound for the probability of the event when the fOUp is above certain value
u>0
by the first time before the fixed time
T>0
. So we define

\tau_{u}:=\inf\{t\geq 0\,:\,X^{\epsilon}_{t}\geq u\}

then we will find an upper estimate for
\mathbb{P}(\tau_{u}<T).

But because of the following equality of events

(\tau_{u}<T)=\big(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}X^{\epsilon}_{t}>u\big)

we will work with the last one. And finally using the Borell inequality (
11
), we will get an upper bound for
\mathbb{P}(\tau_{u}<T)=\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}X^{\epsilon}_{t}>u\right)
.

In what follows, we will consider
\lambda=1
and
x=0
.

An upper bound

Here we show upper estimates for

Theorem 1
.

\forall\,u>\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}X_{t}\right)
and

H\in\;[1/2,1]

\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}X^{\epsilon}_{t}>u\right)\leq\exp\left\{-\frac{u^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}a_{H}+\frac{u}{\epsilon}b_{H}-c_{H}\right\}

Proposition 2
.

We have that,

\mathbb{E}(X^{\epsilon}_{t})^{2}=\epsilon^{2}\left[t^{2H}e^{-t}+\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}u^{2H}(e^{-u}-e^{u-2t})du\right]

\sigma_{T}^{2}=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}(X^{\epsilon}_{t})^{2}\leq\epsilon^{2}T^{2H+1}

\mathbb{E}(X^{\epsilon}_{t})^{2}=\epsilon^{2}\cdot t^{2H}-2\epsilon^{2}e^{-t}\mathbb{E}\left(B_{t}^{H}\int_{0}^{t}B_{u}^{H}e^{u}du\right)+\epsilon^{2}\cdot e^{-2t}\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}B_{u}^{H}e^{u}du\right)^{2}

,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(B_{t}^{H}\int_{0}^{t}B_{u}^{H}e^{u}du\right)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}(B_{t}^{H}B_{u}^{H})e^{u}du=\int_{0}^{t}\tfrac{1}{2}(t^{2H}+u^{2H}-|t-u|^{2H})e^{u}du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\tfrac{1}{2}t^{2H}e^{u}du+\int_{0}^{t}\tfrac{1}{2}u^{2H}e^{u}du-\int_{0}^{t}\tfrac{1}{2}(t-u)^{2H}e^{u}du

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}t^{2H}(e^{t}-1)+\int_{0}^{t}\tfrac{1}{2}u^{2H}e^{u}du-\int_{0}^{t}\tfrac{1}{2}v^{2H}e^{t-v}dv

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}B_{u}^{H}e^{u}du\int_{0}^{t}B_{s}^{H}e^{s}ds\right)

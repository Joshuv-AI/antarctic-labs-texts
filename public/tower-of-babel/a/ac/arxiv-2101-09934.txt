On relative metric mean dimension with potential and variational principles
Weisheng Wu

Abstract
. In this article, we introduce a notion of relative mean metric dimension with potential for a factor map π : ( X , d , T ) → ( Y , S ) \pi:(X,d,T)\to(Y,S) between two topological dynamical systems. To link it with ergodic theory, we establish four variational principles in terms of metric entropy of partitions, Shapira’s entropy, Katok’s entropy and Brin-Katok local entropy respectively. Some results on local entropy with respect to a fixed open cover are obtained in the relative case. We also answer an open question raised by Shi [ 23 ] partially for a very well-partitionable compact metric space, and in general we obtain a variational inequality involving box dimension of the space. Corresponding inner variational principles given an invariant measure of ( Y , S ) (Y,S) are also investigated.

Introduction

Let
(X,T)
be a topological dynamical system (TDS for short), that is,
X
is a compact metrizable space and
T:X\to X
is a homeomorphism. The topological entropy
h_{\text{top}}(T)
and metric entropy
h_{\mu}(T)
measure the complexity of the dynamical system from topological and measure-theoretic points of view respectively. The well known variational principle established by Goodwyn
[
7
]
and Goodman
[
6
]
states that

h_{\text{top}}(T)=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)}h_{\mu}(T)

where
\mathcal{M}(X)
(resp.
\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
,
\mathcal{E}_{T}(X)
) denotes the set of all (resp.
T
-invariant,
T
-ergodic) probability Borel measures on
X
.

To further quantify the complexity of dynamical systems of infinite entropy, a new invariant called topological mean dimension was introduced by Gromov
[
8
]
. Metric mean dimension was introduced later by Lindenstrauss and Weiss
[
17
]
as an upper bound for topological mean dimension. Recently, a double (minimax) variational principle is obtained for topological mean dimension by Lindenstrauss and Tsukamoto
[
16
]
, and is extended to topological mean dimension with potential by Tsukamoto
[
25
]
. Lindenstrauss and
Tsukamoto
[
15
]
also established a variational principle formulating metric mean dimension as a supremum of certain rate distortion
functions over invariant measures of the system. Alternative formulations of metric mean dimension in terms of metric entropy of partitions, Brin-Katok local entropy, Katok’s entropy and Shapira’s entropy are developed by Velozo-Velozo
[
26
]
, Gutman and Śpiewak
[
9
]
, and Shi
[
23
]
.

In this paper, we consider a factor map
\pi:(X,d,T)\to(Y,S)
, i.e.,
T:X\to X
and
S:Y\to Y
are two topological dynamical systems and
\pi:X\to Y
is a continuous, surjective map such that
\pi\circ T=S\circ\pi
. Let
C(X,\mathbb{R})
denote the set of all continuous functions
\varphi:X\to\mathbb{R}
.
The following classical relative variational principle for pressure was obtained by Ledrappier and Walters
[
14
]
and by Downarowicz and Serafin
[
4
]
: For any
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{S}(Y)
and any potential
\varphi\in C(X,\mathbb{R})
,

(1)

\displaystyle\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)}\Big\{h_{\mu}(T,X|Y)+\int_{X}\varphi(x)d\mu(x):\pi\mu=\nu\Big\}=\int_{Y}P(T,\varphi,y)d\nu(y)

and

\displaystyle\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)}\Big\{h_{\mu}(T,X|Y)+\int_{X}\varphi(x)d\mu(x)\Big\}=P(T,\varphi|Y).

This motivates us to introduce a notion of relative metric mean dimension with potential
\varphi\in C(X,\mathbb{R})
for a factor map
\pi:(X,d,T)\to(Y,S)
(See Definition
2.4
). When
(Y,S)
is a trivial system and
\varphi\equiv 0
, this notion recovers the metric mean dimension of
(X,d,T)
. We establish four variational principles in terms of metric entropy of partitions (Theorem
3.4
), Shapira’s entropy (Theorem
3.8
), Katok’s entropy (Theorem
3.9
) and Brin-Katok local entropy (Theorem
4.1
) respectively. We also investigate relative metric mean dimension with potential with respect to an invariant measure of
(Y,S)
of Ledrappier-Walters type (
1
) and establish corresponding inner variational principles. We highlight that in our variational principles “
\sup
” actually becomes “
\max
”.

A key ingredient in the proof of the variational principles, especially in terms of metric entropy of partitions, is the local versions of the variational principle with respect to a fixed open cover. In recent years, local variational principles have been extensively studied, for example in
[
1
,
21
,
5
,
10
,
12
]
, etc. Relative versions of local variational principles for entropy and also for pressure are obtained in
[
11
,
18
]
, etc. We use the relative local variational principle for pressure obtained in
[
18
]
.

To establish variational principles in terms of Shapira’s entropy, Katok’s entropy and Brin-Katok local entropy, we investigate the relations between these types of entropy at a fixed resolution
\epsilon>0
. We extend Shapira’s theorem to the relative case (Proposition
3.5
). An open question raised by Shi
[
23
]
is also considered: We obtain a variational inequality (Theorem
4.4
) involving the lower Brin-Katok local entropy when the underlying metric space has finite box dimension, and a variational principle (Theorem
4.6
) when
(X,d)
is very well-partitionable. We explore the ideas from
[
2
]
and its relative version in
[
28
]
to compare the measures of Bowen balls and the elements from refining partitions. However, the problem in general is still open.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we give a precise definition for relative metric mean dimension with potential. In Section
3
, variational principles in terms of metric entropy of partitions, Shapira’s entropy and Katok’s entropy respectively are proved. In Section
4
, the variational principles or inequality involving upper or lower Brin-Katok local entropy are obtained. In Section
5
, we discuss relative metric mean dimension with potential with respect to an invariant measure of
(Y,S)
of Ledrappier-Walters’ type (
1
) and corresponding inner variational principles. A proof of Proposition
3.5
is included in the appendix.

2.
Relative metric mean dimension with potential

Let
(X,d)
be a compact metric space and
T:X\to X
a homeomorphism. There are three equivalent ways using separated sets, spanning sets and open covers respectively, to define topological pressure and metric mean dimension with potential.
We first prepare some necessary notations and at the end of this section we give a precise definition for relative metric mean dimension with potential.

2.1.
Topological pressure

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define the
Bowen metric
as

d_{n}(x,y):=\max_{0\leq j\leq n-1}d(T^{j}(x),T^{j}(y)).

Let
K\subset X
and
\epsilon>0
. A subset
E\subset K
is called
(n,\epsilon)
separated
if
d_{n}(x,y)>\epsilon
for any
x,y\in E,x\neq y
. For any potential
\varphi\in C(X,\mathbb{R})
, define

\displaystyle P_{n}(d,T,\varphi,\epsilon,K):=\sup\Big\{

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in E}\exp((S_{n}\varphi)(y)):

\displaystyle E\text{\ is an\ }(n,\epsilon)\text{\ separated subset of\ }K\Big\}

where
(S_{n}\varphi)(y)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\varphi^{i}(y)
. Then we put

\displaystyle P(d,T,\varphi,\epsilon,K):=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log P_{n}(d,T,\varphi,\epsilon,K).

If
K
is dropped from the notation, it always means
K=X
. We note that for any
\varphi,\phi\in C(X,\mathbb{R})
(see
[
27
, Theorem 9.7(iv)]
)

(2)

\displaystyle|P(d,T,\varphi,\epsilon,K)-P(d,T,\phi,\epsilon,K)|\leq\|\varphi-\phi\|

where
\|\cdot\|
denote the
C^{0}
norm of
C(X,\mathbb{R})
.

Definition 2.1
.

We define the
topological pressure
of
T
with respect to potential
\varphi
on
K\subset X
to be

\displaystyle P(T,\varphi,K)

\displaystyle:=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}P(d,T,\varphi,\epsilon,K).

We note that
P(T,\varphi,K)
is independent of the choice of metric
d
. When
K=X
,
P(T,\varphi)
is called the
topological pressure
of
T
with respect to
\varphi
. When
\varphi\equiv 0
,
P(T,0)
reduces to the
topological entropy
of
T
.

A set
F\subset K
is called an
(n,\epsilon)
spanning set
of
K
if
K\subset\bigcup_{y\in F}B_{n}(y,\epsilon)
, where
B_{n}(y,\epsilon):=\{z\in X:d_{n}(y,z)<\epsilon\}
is the
(n,\epsilon)

Bowen ball
around
y
. Put

Q(d,T,\varphi,\epsilon,K):=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log Q_{n}(T,\varphi,\epsilon,K)

where

\displaystyle Q_{n}(d,T,\varphi,\epsilon,K):=\inf\Big\{

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in F}\exp((S_{n}\varphi)(y)):

\displaystyle F\text{\ is an\ }(n,\epsilon)\text{\ spanning subset of\ }K\Big\}.

Let
\mathcal{B}(X)
be the collection of all Borel subsets of
X
. A
cover
of
X
is a finite family of Borel subsets of

X
, whose union is
X
. A
partition
of
X
is a cover of
X
whose elements are pairwise disjoint.
Let
\mathcal{P}_{X}
denote the set of partitions of
X
,
\mathcal{C}_{X}
the set of covers of
X
and
\mathcal{C}_{X}^{o}\subset\mathcal{C}_{X}
the set of open covers of
X
. Given two covers
\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}\in\mathcal{C}_{X}
,
\mathcal{U}
is said to be finer than
\mathcal{V}
, denoted by
\mathcal{U}\succeq\mathcal{V}
or
\mathcal{V}\preceq\mathcal{U}
, if each element of

\mathcal{U}
is contained in some element of
\mathcal{V}
. Let
\mathcal{U}\vee\mathcal{V}:=\{U\cap V:U\in\mathcal{U},V\in\mathcal{V}\}
.
For
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
, denote
\mathcal{U}_{m}^{n}:=\bigvee_{i=m}^{n}T^{-i}\mathcal{U}
. For
\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}_{X}
, let
\mathrm{diam}\mathcal{U}
denote the maximal diameter of elements in
\mathcal{U}
. The Lebesgue number of
\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}^{o}_{X}
is denoted by
Leb\mathcal{U}
.

For
\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}^{o}_{X}
put

\displaystyle p_{n}(d,T,\varphi,\mathcal{U},K):=\inf\Big\{

\displaystyle\sum_{B\in\mathcal{V}}\sup_{y\in B}\exp((S_{n}\varphi)(y)):

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}\text{\ is a finite subcover of \ }\mathcal{U}_{0}^{n-1}\text{\ and}\bigcup_{V\in\mathcal{V}}\supset K\Big\}

and

p(d,T,\varphi,\mathcal{U},K):=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log p_{n}(d,T,\varphi,\mathcal{U},K).

The above “
\limsup
” becomes “
\lim
” if
K
is
T
-invariant by Theorem 11.5 in
[
19
]
.

The following lemma is straightforward but useful. For its proof, we refer to Theorem 9.2, p.209 and p.211 in
[
27
]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
K\subset X
and
\varphi\in C(X,\mathbb{R})
. We have:

(1)

If
\epsilon>0
and
\mathcal{U}
is an open cover with
\mathrm{diam}\mathcal{U}\leq\epsilon
, then

Q_{n}(d,T,\varphi,\epsilon,K)\leq P_{n}(d,T,\varphi,\epsilon,K)\leq p_{n}(d,T,\varphi,\mathcal{U},K).

(2)

If
\mathcal{U}
is an open cover with
Leb\mathcal{U}=\delta
, then

p_{n}(d,T,\varphi,\mathcal{U},K)\leq e^{n\tau_{\mathcal{U}}}Q_{n}(d,T,\varphi,\delta/2,K)\leq e^{n\tau_{\mathcal{U}}}P_{n}(d,T,\varphi,\delta/2,K)

where
\tau_{\mathcal{U}}:=\sup\{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|:d(x,y)\leq\mathrm{diam}\mathcal{U}\}
.

(3)

If
\delta=\delta(\epsilon)>0
is such that
d(x,y)<\epsilon/2
implies
|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|<\delta
, then

P_{n}(d,T,\varphi,\epsilon,K)\leq e^{n\delta(\epsilon)}Q_{n}(d,T,\varphi,\epsilon/2,K).

As an immediate corollary of Lemma
2.2
, one has the following reformulations of topological pressure.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
K\subset X
and
\varphi\in C(X,\mathbb{R})
. We have

\displaystyle P(T,\varphi,K)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}Q(d,T,\varphi,\epsilon,K)=\lim_{\mathrm{diam}\mathcal{U}\to 0}p(d,T,\varphi,\mathcal{U},K).

2.2.
Relative metric mean dimension with potential

Let
T:(X,d)\to(X,d)
and
S:Y\to Y
be two TDSs. Recall that a continuous map
\pi:X\to Y
is called a factor map between
(X,T)
and
(Y,S)
if it is onto and
\pi\circ T=S\circ\pi
. Given
\epsilon>0
and
\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}^{o}(X)
, write

\displaystyle P_{n}(d,T,\varphi,\epsilon|Y):=

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in Y}P_{n}(d,T,\varphi,\epsilon,\pi^{-1}y),

\displaystyle Q_{n}(d,T,\varphi,\epsilon|Y):=

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in Y}Q_{n}(d,T,\varphi,\epsilon,\pi^{-1}y),

\displaystyle p_{n}(d,T,\varphi,\mathcal{U}|Y):=

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in Y}p_{n}(d,T,\varphi,\mathcal{U},\pi^{-1}y).

Then we define

\displaystyle P(d,T,\varphi,\epsilon|Y):=

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log P_{n}(d,T,\varphi,\epsilon|Y),

\displaystyle Q(d,T,\varphi,\epsilon|Y):=

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log Q_{n}(d,T,\varphi,\epsilon|Y),

\displaystyle P(d,T,\varphi,\mathcal{U}|Y):=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log p_{n}(d,T,\varphi,\mathcal{U}|Y).

The limit in the last equality exists by the subadditivity of
\log p_{n}(d,T,\varphi,\mathcal{U}|Y)
, see
[
18
]
.
P(d,T,\varphi,\mathcal{U}|Y)

is called the
relative local topological pressure
of
\varphi
with respect to
\mathcal{U}
and
(Y,S)
. The
relative topological pressure
of
\varphi
with respect to
(Y,S)
is then defined as

P(T,\varphi|Y)=\sup_{\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}^{o}_{X}}P(d,T,\varphi,\mathcal{U}|Y).

By Lemma
2.2
,

P(T,\varphi|Y)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}P(d,T,\varphi,\epsilon|Y)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}Q(d,T,\varphi,\epsilon|Y)

and it is independent of the choice of metric
d
.
For given
y\in Y
, we also define

\displaystyle P(d,T,\varphi,\epsilon,y):=

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log P_{n}(d,T,\varphi,\epsilon,\pi^{-1}y),

\displaystyle Q(d,T,\varphi,\epsilon,y):=

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log Q_{n}(d,T,\varphi,\epsilon,\pi^{-1}y),

\displaystyle P(d,T,\varphi,\mathcal{U},y):=

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log p_{n}(d,T,\varphi,\mathcal{U},\pi^{-1}y).

Now we are ready to define relative metric mean dimension with potential.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\pi:(X,d,T)\to(Y,S)
be a factor map and
\varphi\in C(X,\mathbb{R}).
The
upper relative metric mean dimension with potential

\varphi
of
(X,d,T)
with respect to
(Y,S)
is defined by

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{M}(X,d,T,\varphi|Y):=\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}P(d,T,\varphi\log\frac{1}{\epsilon},\epsilon|Y).

Similarly, the
lower relative metric mean dimension with potential

\varphi
of
(X,d,T)
with respect to
(Y,S)
is defined by

\displaystyle\underline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{M}(X,d,T,\varphi|Y):=\liminf_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}P(d,T,\varphi\log\frac{1}{\epsilon},\epsilon|Y).

When
(Y,S)
is trivial, we obtain
upper/lower metric mean dimension with potential
, which is introduced and studied in
[
25
]
.

Remark 2.5
.

By definition,
\overline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{M}(X,d,T,\varphi|Y)
depends on the metric
d
on
X
, but not on the metric on
Y
. It is clear that

\overline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{M}(X,d,T,\varphi|Y)\leq\overline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{M}(X,d,T,\varphi).

Unlike the topological entropy case, it is possible that there exists some metric
d^{\prime}
on
Y
such that
\overline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{M}(X,d,T)<\overline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{M}(Y,d^{\prime},S)
.

Proposition 2.6
.

We have

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{M}(X,d,T,\varphi|Y)=

\displaystyle\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}Q(d,T,\varphi\log\frac{1}{\epsilon},\epsilon|Y),

\displaystyle\underline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{M}(X,d,T,\varphi|Y)=

\displaystyle\liminf_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}Q(d,T,\varphi\log\frac{1}{\epsilon},\epsilon|Y).

Proof.

By (1) of Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{M}(X,d,T,\varphi|Y)\geq

\displaystyle\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}Q(d,T,\varphi\log\frac{1}{\epsilon},\epsilon|Y).

By (3) of Lemma
2.2
and (
2
), we have

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{M}(X,d,T,\varphi|Y)\leq

\displaystyle\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\left(P(d,T,\varphi\log\frac{2}{\epsilon},\epsilon|Y)+\|\varphi\|\log 2\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\left(\log e^{\delta(\epsilon)\log\frac{2}{\epsilon}}+Q(d,T,\varphi\log\frac{2}{\epsilon},\epsilon/2|Y)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}Q(d,T,\varphi\log\frac{1}{\epsilon},\epsilon|Y).

where we used the facts that
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\delta(\epsilon)=0
and
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}{\log\frac{2}{\epsilon}}=1
.
The equality for
\underline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{M}(X,d,T,\varphi|Y)
in the proposition follows analogously.
∎

3.
Three variational principles

Our goal in this section is to prove three variational principles, Theorems
3.4
,
3.8
,
3.9
. To begin with, we must reacall some standard notations and classical results on measurable partitions and conditional metric entropy.

3.1.
Conditional metric entropy and local variational principle

Let
(X,\mathcal{A},\nu)
be a standard probability space. For a partition (not necessarily countable)
\xi
of
X
, let
\xi(x)
denote the element of
\xi
containing
x
.
Let
\mathcal{B}(\xi)
denote the smallest
sub-
\sigma
-algebra of
\mathcal{A}
that contains all elements of
\xi
.
A partition
\xi
of
X
is called
measurable
if there exists a countable set
\{A_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{B}(\xi)
such that
for almost every pair
C_{1},C_{2}\in\xi
, we can find some
A_{n}
which separates them
in the sense that
C_{1}\subset A_{n},C_{2}\subset X-A_{n}
.
The
canonical system
of conditional measures of
\nu
relative to
\xi

is a family of probability
measures
\{\nu_{x}^{\xi}:x\in X\}
with
\nu_{x}^{\xi}\bigl(\xi(x)\bigr)=1
,
such that for every measurable set
B\subset X
,
x\mapsto\nu_{x}^{\xi}(B)

is
\mathcal{B}(\xi)
-measurable and

\nu(B)=\int_{X}\nu_{x}^{\xi}(B)d\nu(x).

The classical result of Rokhlin (cf.
[
20
]
) says that if
\xi
is a measurable partition, then there exists a system of conditional measures relative to
\xi
. It is unique
in the sense that two such systems coincide in a set of full
\nu
-measure.
For measurable partitions
\alpha
and
\xi
, the
conditional information function
is defined as

I_{\nu}(\alpha|\xi)(x):=-\log\nu_{x}^{\xi}(\alpha(x))

and the
conditional entropy of
\alpha
given
\xi

with respect to
\nu
is defined as

H_{\nu}(\alpha|\xi):=\int_{X}I_{\nu}(\alpha|\xi)(x)d\nu(x).

Let
T:(X,\mathcal{A},\mu)\to(X,\mathcal{A},\mu)
be a measure-preserving transformation.
The
conditional entropy of
T
with respect to a measurable partition
\alpha

given
\xi
is defined as

h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|\xi):=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}H_{\mu}(\alpha_{0}^{n-1}|\xi).

A measurable partition
\xi
is called
invariant
under
T
if
T^{-1}(\xi(Tx))=\xi(x)
for
\mu\text{-a.e.}\ x.
The following is a conditional version of Shannon-McMillan-Breiman (SMB for short) theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

[
3
, Theorem B.0.1]

Let
(X,T)
be a TDS,
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
,
\xi
a
T
-invariant measurable partition and
\alpha\in\mathcal{P}_{X}
.
Then the following limit exists

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}I_{\mu}(\alpha_{0}^{n-1}|\xi)(x):=h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|\xi,x)\quad\mu\text{-a.e.}\ \text{and in }L^{1}(\mu),

and
\int_{X}h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|\xi,x)d\mu(x)=h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|\xi).
Moreover, if
\mu
is ergodic then

h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|\xi,x)=h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|\xi)

for
\mu\text{-a.e.}\ x\in X
.

Let
T:X\to X
and
S:Y\to Y
be two TDSs and
\pi:X\to Y
a factor map.
We proceed by defining the conditional metric entropy of
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
with respect to
(Y,S)
. Let
\epsilon_{Y}
denote the partition of
Y
into points and
{\mathcal{B}}(Y)
the Borel
\sigma
-algebra of
Y
. Then in the Lebesgue space
(X,\mu)
, the measurable partition
\pi^{-1}\epsilon_{Y}
generates the
\sigma
-algebra
\pi^{-1}{\mathcal{B}}(Y)
and it is
T
-invariant. We simply denote
\mu_{x}:=\mu_{x}^{\pi^{-1}\epsilon_{Y}}
and also
\mu_{y}:=\mu_{x}
if
\pi x=y
, for
\mu\text{-a.e.}\ x
. Given
\alpha\in\mathcal{P}_{X}
, define the conditional entropy of
\alpha
with respect to
(Y,S)
as

h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|Y):=h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|\pi^{-1}\epsilon_{Y})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}H_{\mu}(\alpha_{0}^{n-1}|\pi^{-1}\epsilon_{Y}).

The above limit exists since the sequence
b_{n}=H_{\mu}(\alpha_{0}^{n-1}|\pi^{-1}\epsilon_{Y})
is subadditive. Moreover, the
conditional metric entropy of
(X,T,\mu)
with respect to
(Y,S)
is defined by

h_{\mu}(T,X|Y):=\sup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{P}_{X}}h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|Y).

Applying SMB Theorem
3.1
for
\xi=\pi^{-1}\epsilon_{Y}
, we can write

h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|Y,x):=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}I_{\mu}(\alpha_{0}^{n-1}|\pi^{-1}\epsilon_{Y})(x),

which exists for
\mu\text{-a.e.}\ x\in X
and satisfies that
\int_{X}h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|Y,x)d\mu(x)=h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|Y).

We have the following classical conditional variational principle by Ledrappier and Walters.

Theorem 3.2

(
[
14
,
4
]
)
.

Let
T:(X,d)\to(X,d)
and
S:Y\to Y
be two TDSs and
\pi:X\to Y
a factor map. Then
for any
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{S}(Y)
and any potential
\varphi\in C(X,\mathbb{R})
,

\displaystyle\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)}\Big\{h_{\mu}(T,X|Y)+\int_{X}\varphi(x)d\mu(x):\pi\mu=\nu\Big\}=\int_{Y}P(T,\varphi,y)d\nu(y).

and

\displaystyle\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)}\Big\{h_{\mu}(T,X|Y)+\int_{X}\varphi(x)d\mu(x)\Big\}=P(T,\varphi|Y).

The
local conditional metric entropy with respect to
\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}_{X}^{o}
is introduced in
[
11
]
as

h_{\mu}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y):=\inf_{\alpha\in\mathcal{P}_{X},\alpha\succeq\mathcal{U}}h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|Y).

Let
\mu=\int_{\mathcal{\mathcal{E}}_{T}(X)}\nu d\tau(\nu)
be the unique ergodic decomposition
where
\tau
is a probability measure on the Borel subsets of
\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
and
\tau(\mathcal{E}_{T}(X))=1
.
Then by Theorem 5.3 in
[
11
]
,

h_{\mu}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)+\int_{X}\varphi d\mu=\int_{\mathcal{E}_{T}(X)}\Big(h_{\nu}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)+\int_{X}\varphi d\nu\Big)d\tau(\nu).

Combining it with Theorem 1.3 in
[
18
]
, we have the following local variational principle which plays a key role in our proof below.

Theorem 3.3
.

[
18
, Theorem 1.3]

Let
\pi:(X,T)\to(Y,S)
be a factor map between two TDSs and
\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}_{X}^{o}
. Then

\displaystyle P(T,\varphi,\mathcal{U}|Y)=\max_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)}\left\{h_{\mu}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)+\int\varphi d\mu\right\}.

Moreover,
\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
in the above equality can be replaced by
\mathcal{E}_{T}(X)
.

3.2.
Variational principle via entropy of partitions

Our first variational principle for relative metric mean dimension with potential is as follows.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\pi:(X,d,T)\to(Y,S)
be a factor map between two TDSs. Then
for any potential
\varphi\in C(X,\mathbb{R})
,

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{M}(X,d,T,\varphi|Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\max_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)}\inf_{\mathrm{diam}\alpha\leq\epsilon}\left(h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|Y)+\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int\varphi d\mu\right)

and

\displaystyle\underline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{M}(X,d,T,\varphi|Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\liminf_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\max_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)}\inf_{\mathrm{diam}\alpha\leq\epsilon}\left(h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|Y)+\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int\varphi d\mu\right).

Moreover, both
\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
in the above equalities can be replaced by
\mathcal{E}_{T}(X)
.

Proof.

Given
\epsilon>0
, choose
\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{U}_{\epsilon}\in\mathcal{C}^{o}_{X}
such that
\mathrm{diam}\mathcal{U}\leq\epsilon
and
Leb\mathcal{U}\geq\epsilon/4
(see Lemma 3.4 in
[
9
]
). By Theorem
3.3
, (2) of Lemma
2.2
and (
2
)

\displaystyle\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)}\inf_{\mathrm{diam}\alpha\leq\epsilon}(h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|Y)+\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int\varphi d\mu)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)}\inf_{\alpha\succeq\mathcal{U}}(h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|Y)+\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int\varphi d\mu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P(T,\varphi\log\frac{1}{\epsilon},\mathcal{U}|Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sup_{y\in Y}p_{n}(d,T,\varphi\log\frac{1}{\epsilon},\mathcal{U},\pi^{-1}y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sup_{y\in Y}(1/\epsilon)^{n\tau_{\mathcal{U}}}P_{n}(d,T,\varphi\log\frac{1}{\epsilon},Leb\mathcal{U}/2,\pi^{-1}y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sup_{y\in Y}(1/\epsilon)^{n\tau_{\mathcal{U}}}P_{n}(d,T,\varphi\log\frac{1}{\epsilon},\epsilon/8,\pi^{-1}y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P(d,T,\varphi\log\frac{8}{\epsilon},\epsilon/8|Y)+\|\varphi\|\log 8+\tau_{\mathcal{U}}\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}.

On the other hand, by (1) of Lemma
2.2
and the “moreover” part of Theorem
3.3
we have

\displaystyle P(d,T,\varphi\log\frac{1}{\epsilon},\epsilon|Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sup_{y\in Y}P_{n}(d,T,\varphi\log\frac{1}{\epsilon},\epsilon,\pi^{-1}y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sup_{y\in Y}p_{n}(d,T,\varphi\log\frac{1}{\epsilon},\mathcal{U},\pi^{-1}y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P(T,\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\varphi,\mathcal{U}|Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{\mu\in\mathcal{E}_{T}(X)}\inf_{\alpha\succeq\mathcal{U}}\left(h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|Y)+\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int\varphi d\mu\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{E}_{T}(X)}\inf_{\mathrm{diam}\alpha\leq\epsilon/8}\left(h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|Y)+\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int\varphi d\mu\right)

where in the last equality we used the fact that if
\mathrm{diam}\alpha\leq\epsilon/8\leq Leb\mathcal{U}
, then
\alpha\succeq\mathcal{U}
.
Note that as
\epsilon\to 0
,
\tau_{\mathcal{U}}\to 0
, and
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}{\log\frac{C}{\epsilon}}
for any
C>0
. Dividing both sides by
\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}
and taking the limit as
\epsilon\to 0
, we obtain the variational principle with “
\sup
”, which becomes a “
\max
” by the very last inequality above.
∎

3.3.
Variational principle via Shapira’s entropy

For
\nu\in\mathcal{M}(X)
,
\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}_{X}
and
0<\rho<1
, let
N_{\nu}(\mathcal{U},\rho)
denote the minimal number of elements of
\mathcal{U}
, needed to cover a subset of
X
whose
\nu
-measure is at least
1-\rho
.
Let
\mu\in\mathcal{E}_{T}(X)
and
\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}^{o}_{X}
. Define

\displaystyle\overline{h}^{S}_{\mu}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y):=\lim_{\rho\to 0}\overline{h}^{S}_{\mu}(T,\mathcal{U},\rho|Y):=

\displaystyle\lim_{\rho\to 0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int\log N_{\mu_{x}}(\mathcal{U}^{n-1}_{0},\rho)d\mu(x),

\displaystyle\underline{h}^{S}_{\mu}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y):=\lim_{\rho\to 0}\underline{h}^{S}_{\mu}(T,\mathcal{U},\rho|Y):=

\displaystyle\lim_{\rho\to 0}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int\log N_{\mu_{x}}(\mathcal{U}^{n-1}_{0},\rho)d\mu(x).

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\pi:(X,T)\to(Y,S)
be a factor map between two TDSs,
\mu\in\mathcal{E}_{T}(X)
and

. Then

\overline{h}^{S}_{\mu}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)=\underline{h}^{S}_{\mu}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)=h_{\mu}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y).

The proof of the proposition adapts the ideas from
[
22
]
to the relative case which is provided in the appendix.
In the following we write

Question 3.6
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{E}_{T}(X)
and
\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}_{X}^{o}
. Do we have for every
0<\rho<1
,

\overline{h}^{S}_{\mu}(T,\mathcal{U},\rho|Y)=\underline{h}^{S}_{\mu}(T,\mathcal{U},\rho|Y)?

Remark 3.7
.

The answer is positive in the classical case (i.e., when

is trivial) by Theorem 4.2 in
[
22
]
, but there are some difficulties to extend the proof (see p.234 in
[
22
]

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
T:(X,d)\to(X,d)
and
S:Y\to Y
be two TDSs and
\pi:X\to Y
a factor map. Then
for any potential
\varphi\in C(X,\mathbb{R})
,

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{M}(X,d,T,\varphi|Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\max_{\mu\in\mathcal{E}_{T}(X)}\inf_{\mathrm{diam}\mathcal{U}\leq\epsilon}\left(h^{S}_{\mu}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)+\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int\varphi d\mu\right)

and

\displaystyle\underline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{M}(X,d,T,\varphi|Y)

\displaystyle\liminf_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\max_{\mu\in\mathcal{E}_{T}(X)}\inf_{\mathrm{diam}\mathcal{U}\leq\epsilon}\left(h^{S}_{\mu}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)+\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int\varphi d\mu\right).

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{\mathrm{diam}\mathcal{U}\leq\epsilon}\inf_{\alpha\succeq\mathcal{U}}(h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|Y)+\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int\varphi d\mu)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\inf_{\mathrm{diam}\alpha\leq\epsilon}(h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|Y)+\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int\varphi d\mu).

For the other direction, we proceed as in the proof of Theorem
3.4
. Given
\epsilon>0
, choose
\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{U}_{\epsilon}\in\mathcal{C}^{o}_{X}

\mathrm{diam}\mathcal{U}\leq\epsilon

]

3.3
, (2) of Lemma
2.2

)

\displaystyle\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{E}_{T}(X)}\inf_{\mathrm{diam}\mathcal{U}\leq\epsilon}(h^{S}_{\mu}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)+\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int\varphi d\mu)

3.4.
Variational principle via Katok’s entropy

For
\nu\in\mathcal{M}(X)
, let
N_{\nu}(n,\epsilon,\rho)
denote the minimal number of
d_{n}
-balls with radius
\epsilon
which cover a subset of
X
of
\nu
-measure no less than
1-\rho
.
Let
\mu\in\mathcal{E}_{T}(X)
and then define

\displaystyle\overline{h}^{K}_{\mu}(T,\epsilon|Y):=\lim_{\rho\to 0}\overline{h}^{K}_{\mu}(T,\epsilon,\rho|Y):=

\displaystyle\lim_{\rho\to 0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int\log N_{\mu_{x}}(n,\epsilon,\rho)d\mu(x),

\displaystyle\underline{h}^{K}_{\mu}(T,\epsilon|Y):=\lim_{\rho\to 0}\underline{h}^{K}_{\mu}(T,\epsilon,\rho|Y):=

\displaystyle\lim_{\rho\to 0}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int\log N_{\mu_{x}}(n,\epsilon,\rho)d\mu(x).

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
T:(X,d)\to(X,d)
and
S:Y\to Y
be two topological dynamical systems and
\pi:X\to Y
a factor map. Then
for any potential
\varphi\in C(X,\mathbb{R})
,

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{M}(X,d,T,\varphi|Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\max_{\mu\in\mathcal{E}_{T}(X)}\left(\overline{h}^{K}_{\mu}(T,\epsilon|Y)+\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int\varphi d\mu\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\max_{\mu\in\mathcal{E}_{T}(X)}\left(\underline{h}^{K}_{\mu}(T,\epsilon|Y)+\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int\varphi d\mu\right)

and

\displaystyle\underline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{M}(X,d,T,\varphi|Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\liminf_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\max_{\mu\in\mathcal{E}_{T}(X)}\left(\overline{h}^{K}_{\mu}(T,\epsilon|Y)+\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int\varphi d\mu\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\liminf_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\max_{\mu\in\mathcal{E}_{T}(X)}\left(\underline{h}^{K}_{\mu}(T,\epsilon|Y)+\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int\varphi d\mu\right).

Proof.

Let
0<\rho<1
and
\mu\in\mathcal{E}_{T}(X)
. Let
\mathcal{U}
be any finite open cover of
X
with
\mathrm{diam}\mathcal{U}\leq\epsilon
.
As any element of
\mathcal{U}_{0}^{n-1}
is contained in an
(n,\epsilon)
-Bowen ball, we have
N_{\mu_{x}}(n,\epsilon,\rho)\leq N_{\mu_{x}}(\mathcal{U}^{n-1}_{0},\rho)
for
\mu\text{-a.e.}\ x
.
Hence
\overline{h}^{K}_{\mu}(T,\epsilon|Y)\leq h^{S}_{\mu}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y).

By Theorem
3.8
,

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{M}(X,d,T,\varphi|Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\max_{\mu\in\mathcal{E}_{T}(X)}\inf_{\mathrm{diam}\mathcal{U}\leq\epsilon}\left(h^{S}_{\mu}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)+\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int\varphi d\mu\right)

\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}_{X}^{o}
with
\mathrm{diam}\mathcal{U}\leq\epsilon
and

. As every

, we have
N_{\mu_{x}}(\mathcal{U}^{n-1}_{0},\rho)\leq N_{\mu_{x}}(n,\epsilon/4,\rho)
.
Therefore

h^{S}_{\mu}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)\leq\underline{h}^{K}_{\mu}(T,\epsilon/4|Y).

By Theorem
3.8
,

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{M}(X,d,T,\varphi|Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\max_{\mu\in\mathcal{E}_{T}(X)}\inf_{\mathrm{diam}\mathcal{U}\leq\epsilon}\left(h^{S}_{\mu}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)+\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int\varphi d\mu\right)

\displaystyle\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{E}_{T}(X)}\left(\underline{h}^{K}_{\mu}(T,\epsilon/4|Y)+\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int\varphi d\mu\right)

where in the last equality we use the trick
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}{\log\frac{4}{\epsilon}}=1
. Thus we obtain the equality with “
\sup
”, and then the latter half of the proof shows that it is in fact a “

”. The equalities for

in the theorem can be proved analogously.
∎

4.
Variational principle via Brin-Katok local entropy

In this section, we prove the variational principle involving upper Brin-Katok local entropy, and a variational inequality and principle involving lower Brin-Katok local entropy under certain conditions. Let us first introduce some terminology.

Let
T:X\to X
be a TDS. Following the idea of Brin-Katok local entropy
[
2
]
, we define for
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X)

\underline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(T,\epsilon):=\int_{X}\underline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(T,x,\epsilon)d\mu,\quad\overline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(T,\epsilon):=\int_{X}\overline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(T,x,\epsilon)d\mu

where

\displaystyle\underline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(T,x,\epsilon):=

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\mu(B_{n}(x,\epsilon)),

\displaystyle\overline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(T,x,\epsilon):=

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\mu(B_{n}(x,\epsilon)).

Furthermore, define

\displaystyle\underline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(T,x):=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\underline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(T,x,\epsilon),

\displaystyle\overline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(T,x):=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\overline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(T,x,\epsilon),

\displaystyle\underline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(T):=\int_{X}\underline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(T,x)d\mu,

\displaystyle\overline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(T):=\int_{X}\overline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(T,x)d\mu.

When
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
,
Brin-Katok’s formula

[
2
]
states that

\underline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(T,x)=\overline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(T,x)
for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in X,
and

\underline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(T)=\overline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(T)=h_{\mu}(T)

where
h_{\mu}(T)
is the metric entropy of
\mu
.
In particular, when
\mu\in\mathcal{E}_{T}(X),

\underline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(T,x)=\overline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(T,x)=h_{\mu}(T)
for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in X.

The novelty here is that the Brin-Katok local entropy can be defined for any
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X)
.

Let
T:(X,d)\to(X,d)
and
S:Y\to Y
be two TDSs and
\pi:X\to Y
a factor map. Define

\displaystyle\overline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(T,\epsilon|Y):=

\displaystyle\int\overline{h}^{BK}_{\mu_{x}}(T,\epsilon)d\mu(x),

\displaystyle\underline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(T,\epsilon|Y):=

\displaystyle\int\underline{h}^{BK}_{\mu_{x}}(T,\epsilon)d\mu(x).

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{E}_{T}(X)
. Then
\overline{h}_{\mu_{x}}(T,x,\epsilon)\geq\overline{h}_{\mu_{Tx}}(T,Tx,\epsilon)
and
\underline{h}_{\mu_{x}}(T,x,\epsilon)\geq\underline{h}_{\mu_{Tx}}(T,Tx,\epsilon)
. By Birkhoff ergodic theorem and ergodicity of
\mu
,

(3)

\displaystyle\overline{h}_{\mu_{x}}(T,x,\epsilon)=\overline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(T,\epsilon|Y),\quad\underline{h}_{\mu_{x}}(T,x,\epsilon)=\underline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(T,\epsilon|Y)

for
\mu\text{-a.e.}\ x
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\pi:(X,d,T)\to(Y,S)
be a factor map between two TDSs. Then
for any potential
\varphi\in C(X,\mathbb{R})
,

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{M}(X,d,T,\varphi|Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\max_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)}\left(\overline{h}_{\mu}^{BK}(T,\epsilon|Y)+\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int\varphi d\mu\right)

and

\displaystyle\underline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{M}(X,d,T,\varphi|Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\liminf_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\max_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)}\left(\overline{h}_{\mu}^{BK}(T,\epsilon|Y)+\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int\varphi d\mu\right).

Moreover, both
\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
in the above equalities can be replaced by
\mathcal{E}_{T}(X)
.

Proof.

Given
\epsilon>0
, let
\alpha
be any finite Borel partition of
X
with
\mathrm{diam}\alpha\leq\epsilon
. Then
\alpha_{0}^{n-1}(y)\subset B_{n}(y,\epsilon)
for any
y\in X
. For
\mu\text{-a.e.}\ x
and
\mu_{x}\text{-a.e.}\ y
, since
\mu_{x}=\mu_{y}
we have

\displaystyle\overline{h}^{BK}_{\mu_{x}}(T,y,\epsilon)=

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\mu_{x}(B_{n}(y,\epsilon))\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\mu_{x}(\alpha_{0}^{n-1}(y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\mu_{y}(\alpha_{0}^{n-1}(y))=h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|Y,y)

which implies that

\displaystyle\overline{h}_{\mu}^{BK}(T,\epsilon|Y)=

\displaystyle\int\int\overline{h}^{BK}_{\mu_{x}}(T,y,\epsilon)d\mu_{x}(y)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|Y,y)d\mu(y)=h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|Y).

It follows from Theorem
3.4
that

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{M}(X,d,T,\varphi|Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\max_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)}\inf_{\mathrm{diam}\alpha\leq\epsilon}\left(h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|Y)+\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int\varphi d\mu\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)}\left(\overline{h}_{\mu}^{BK}(T,\epsilon|Y)+\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int\varphi d\mu\right).

Let us prove the other direction. Given
\epsilon>0
, take any
\alpha\in\mathcal{P}_{X}
with
\mathrm{diam}\alpha\leq\epsilon.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{E}_{T}(X)
. By (
3
), there exist
X_{1}\in\mathcal{B}_{X}
with

\mu(X_{1})=1
and

W_{x}=\pi^{-1}\pi(x)\cap X_{1}
for any
x\in X_{1}
, such that
for each
x\in X_{1}
and
y\in W_{x},

\mu_{x}(W_{x})=1
and

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\mu_{x}(B_{n}(y,\epsilon))=\overline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(T,\epsilon|Y).

Fix
x\in X_{1}
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\delta>0,
set

I_{n}=\{y\in W_{x}:\mu_{x}(B_{n}(y,\epsilon))>\exp(-(\overline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(T,\epsilon|Y)+\delta)n)\}.

Then
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{x}(I_{n})=1.
Thus for any
0<\rho<1
, there exists sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mu_{x}(I_{n})>1-\rho.

Let
S_{n}
be a maximal
(n,2\epsilon)
separated subset of
I_{n}
, then it is also an
(n,2\epsilon)
spanning subset of
I_{n}
. It follows that

\mu_{x}(\cup_{y\in I_{n}}B_{n}(y,2\epsilon))\geq\mu_{x}(I_{n})>1-\rho

and hence
\#S_{n}\geq N_{\mu_{x}}(n,2\epsilon,\rho)
. On the other hand, since
\{B_{n}(y,\epsilon):y\in S_{n}\}
are disjoint and
S_{n}\subset I_{n}
, one has

1\geq\mu_{x}(\cup_{y\in I_{n}}B_{n}(y,\epsilon))=\sum_{y\in I_{n}}\mu(B_{n}(y,\epsilon))\geq\#S_{n}\exp(-(\overline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(T,\epsilon|Y)+\delta)n).

Then

N_{\mu_{x}}(n,2\epsilon,\rho)\leq\exp((\overline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(T,\epsilon|Y)+\delta)n).

Thus for any
\delta>0
,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int\log N_{\mu_{x}^{\xi}}(n,\epsilon,\rho)d\mu(x)\leq\overline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(T,\epsilon|Y)+\delta.

Letting
\delta\to 0
,
and then
\rho\to 0
we have

\overline{h}^{K}_{\mu}(T,\epsilon|Y)\leq\overline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(T,\epsilon|Y).

Thus by Theorem
3.9

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{M}(X,d,T,\varphi|Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\max_{\mu\in\mathcal{E}_{T}(X)}\left(\overline{h}^{K}_{\mu}(T,\epsilon|Y)+\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int\varphi d\mu\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{E}_{T}(X)}\left(\overline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(T,\epsilon|Y)+\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int\varphi d\mu\right).

Thus the equality holds with “
\sup
”, which in fact is a “
\max
” by the very last inequality above. The part for
\underline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{M}(X,d,T,\varphi|Y)
can be proved similarly.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Suppose that

5.
Relative metric mean dimension with potential after Ledrappier-Walters

Let
T:(X,d)\to(X,d)
and
S:Y\to Y
be two TDSs and
\pi:X\to Y
a factor map. In this section, we always consider
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{S}(Y)
and a potential
\varphi\in C(X,\mathbb{R})
.

Definition 5.1
.

The
upper relative metric mean dimension with potential

\varphi
of the system
(X,d,T,Y,S,\nu)
is defined by

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{mdim}}^{\nu}_{M}(X,d,T,\varphi|Y):=\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\int_{Y}P(d,T,\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\varphi,\epsilon,y)d\nu(y)

where

\displaystyle P(d,T,\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\varphi,\epsilon,y):=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log P_{n}(d,T,\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\varphi,\epsilon,\pi^{-1}y).

Similarly, the
lower relative metric mean dimension with potential

\varphi
of the system
(X,d,T,Y,\nu)
is defined by

\displaystyle\underline{\mathrm{mdim}}^{\nu}_{M}(X,d,T,\varphi|Y):=\liminf_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\int_{Y}P(d,T,\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\varphi,\epsilon,y)d\nu(y).

A local inner variational principle has been obtained in
[
18
]
.

Theorem 5.2

(Cf. Theorem 1.2 in
[
18
]
)
.

We have

\displaystyle\int_{Y}P(d,T,\varphi,\mathcal{U},y)d\nu(y)=\max_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)}\left\{h_{\mu}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)+\int\varphi d\mu:\pi\mu=\nu\right\}.

We then have the following inner variational principle involving entropy of partitions.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
T:(X,d)\to(X,d)
and
S:Y\to Y
be two TDSs and
\pi:X\to Y
a factor map. Then
for any
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{S}(Y)
and any potential
\varphi\in C(X,\mathbb{R})
,

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{mdim}}^{\nu}_{M}(X,d,T,\varphi|Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\max_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X),\pi\mu=\nu}\inf_{\mathrm{diam}\alpha\leq\epsilon}\left(h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|Y)+\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int\varphi d\mu\right)

and

\displaystyle\underline{\mathrm{mdim}}^{\nu}_{M}(X,d,T,\varphi|Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\liminf_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\max_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X),\pi\mu=\nu}\inf_{\mathrm{diam}\alpha\leq\epsilon}\left(h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|Y)+\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int\varphi d\mu\right).

Proof.

We only need modify the proof of Theorem
3.4
.
Given
\epsilon>0
, choose
\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{U}_{\epsilon}\in\mathcal{C}^{o}_{X}
such that
\mathrm{diam}\mathcal{U}\leq\epsilon
and
Leb\mathcal{U}\geq\epsilon/4
. Then

\displaystyle\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X),\pi\mu=\nu}\inf_{\mathrm{diam}\alpha\leq\epsilon}(h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|Y)+\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int\varphi d\mu)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X),\pi\mu=\nu}\inf_{\alpha\succeq\mathcal{U}}(h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|Y)+\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int\varphi d\mu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{Y}P(d,T,\varphi\log\frac{1}{\epsilon},\mathcal{U},y)d\nu(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log p_{n}(d,T,\varphi\log\frac{1}{\epsilon},\mathcal{U},\pi^{-1}y)d\nu(y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log(1/\epsilon)^{n\tau_{\mathcal{U}}}P_{n}(d,T,\varphi\log\frac{1}{\epsilon},Leb\mathcal{U}/2,\pi^{-1}y)d\nu(y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log(1/\epsilon)^{n\tau_{\mathcal{U}}}P_{n}(d,T,\varphi\log\frac{8}{\epsilon},\epsilon/8,\pi^{-1}y)d\nu(y)+\|\varphi\|\log 8

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{Y}P(d,T,\log\frac{8}{\epsilon}\varphi,\epsilon,y)d\nu(y)+\tau_{\mathcal{U}}\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}+\|\varphi\|\log 8.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\int_{Y}P(d,T,\varphi\log\frac{1}{\epsilon},\epsilon,y)d\nu(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log P_{n}(d,T,\varphi\log\frac{1}{\epsilon},\epsilon,\pi^{-1}y)d\nu(y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log p_{n}(d,T,\varphi\log\frac{1}{\epsilon},\mathcal{U},\pi^{-1}y)d\nu(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{Y}P(T,\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\varphi,\mathcal{U},y)d\nu(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X),\pi\mu=\nu}\inf_{\alpha\succeq\mathcal{U}}(h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|Y)+\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int\varphi d\mu)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X),\pi\mu=\nu}\inf_{\mathrm{diam}\alpha\leq\epsilon/8}(h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|Y)+\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int\varphi d\mu).

Dividing both sides by
\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}
and taking the limit as
\epsilon\to 0
, we obtain the equality in the theorem with
\sup
, which becomes a “
\max
” by the last inequality above. The part for
\underline{\mathrm{mdim}}^{\nu}_{M}(X,d,T,\varphi|Y)
can be proved similarly.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

From the above proof of Theorem
5.3
, we have

P(d,T,\varphi\log\frac{1}{\epsilon},\epsilon,y)\leq P(d,T,\varphi\log\frac{1}{\epsilon},\mathcal{U}_{\epsilon},y)\leq P(d,T,\varphi\log\frac{1}{\epsilon},\epsilon/8,y)

and hence

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{mdim}}^{\nu}_{M}(X,d,T,\varphi|Y)=\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\int_{Y}P(d,T,\varphi\log\frac{1}{\epsilon},\mathcal{U}_{\epsilon},y)d\nu(y).

By
[
18
, Lemma 4.7]
, for any
\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}_{X}^{o}
,

P(d,T,\varphi,\mathcal{U}|Y)=\max_{\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{S}(Y)}\int P(d,T,\varphi,\mathcal{U},y)d\nu(y).

So we can deduce the variational principle Theorem
3.4
by taking
\max_{\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{S}(Y)}
in each expression in the above proof of Theorem
5.3
.

Let
\nu\in\mathcal{E}_{T}(X)
. All the equalities in Theorems
3.8
,
3.9
,
4.1
still hold if

\overline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{M}(X,d,T,\varphi|Y),\overline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{M}(X,d,T,\varphi|Y),\max_{\mu\in\mathcal{E}_{T}(X)}

are replaced by

\overline{\mathrm{mdim}}^{\nu}_{M}(X,d,T,\varphi|Y),\overline{\mathrm{mdim}}^{\nu}_{M}(X,d,T,\varphi|Y),\max_{\mu\in\mathcal{E}_{T}(X),\pi\mu=\nu}

respectively.
Indeed, in the proof of these three theorems only the relations between entropy involving partitions, Shapira’s entropy, Katok’s entropy and Brin-Katok local entropy are involved. Thus we can modify the proof accordingly to obtain the corresponding inner variational principles.

\delta>0
,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{y}(I_{n})=1.
Thus, for sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
\mu_{y}(I_{n})>1-\rho.

Since

\displaystyle\begin{split}\#\mathcal{J}_{n}&=\#\{V\in\alpha_{0}^{n-1}:\mu_{y}(V)>\exp(-(h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|Y)+\delta)n)\\
&\leq\exp((h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|Y)+\delta)n),\end{split}

the set
I_{n}
can be covered by at most
\exp((h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|Y)+\delta)n)
elements of the partition
\alpha_{0}^{n-1}
. Then

N_{\mu_{y}}(\mathcal{U}_{0}^{n-1},\rho)\leq N_{\mu_{y}}(\alpha_{0}^{n-1},\rho)\leq\exp((h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|Y)+\delta)n).

Thus for any
\delta>0
,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int\log N_{\mu_{y}}(\mathcal{U}_{0}^{n-1},\rho)d\mu(y)\leq h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|Y)+\delta.

Letting
\delta\to 0
, we obtain

\overline{h}^{S}_{\mu}(T,\mathcal{U},\rho|Y)\leq h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|Y).

Taking infimum over
\alpha\succeq\mathcal{U}
, we have

\overline{h}^{S}_{\mu}(T,\mathcal{U},\rho|Y)\leq h_{\mu}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y).

∎

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{E}_{T}(X)
and
\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}_{X}^{o}
. Then

h_{\mu}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)\leq\underline{h}^{S}_{\mu}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y).

Proof.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{E}_{T}(X)
. If the system
(X,T)
is periodic, then
\mu
is supported on a fixed point of
T
and

\underline{h}^{S}_{\mu}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)=h_{\mu}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)=0.

Thus let us assume
(X,T)
is aperiodic.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\beta
be constructed as in the proof of Lemma
6.1
for
\mathcal{V}\succeq\mathcal{U}_{0}^{n-1}
. We also use the notation from that proof, for example,
A
is the subset of
X
such that
\mu(A)<\rho
and for any
x\notin A
,
N_{\mu_{x}}(\beta,\rho)\leq N_{\mu_{x}}(\mathcal{U}_{0}^{n-1},\rho)
. Choose
\delta>0
such that
0<\rho+\delta<1/4
. By Lemma
6.2
, we can construct a strong Rohlin tower with respect to
\beta
, with height
n
and error
<\delta
. Let
\tilde{B}
denote the base of the tower and
B=\tilde{B}\setminus A
. Clearly,
\mu(B)>(1-\rho)\mu(\tilde{B})
and
\mu(E)\geq 1-(\rho+\delta)
where
E=\cup_{i=0}^{n-1}T^{i}B
. Consider
\beta|_{\tilde{B}}
and index its elements by sequences
i_{0},\cdots,i_{n-1}
such that if
B_{i_{0},\cdots,i_{n-1}}\in\beta|_{\tilde{B}}
, then
T^{j}B_{i_{0},\cdots,i_{n-1}}\subset U_{i_{j}}
for every
0\leq j\leq n-1
. Let
\hat{\alpha}:=\{\hat{A}_{1},\cdots,\hat{A}_{M}\}
be a partition of
E
defined by

\hat{A}_{m}:=\cup\{T^{j}B_{i_{0},\cdots,i_{n-1}}:0\leq j\leq n-1,i_{j}=m\}.

Note that
\hat{A}_{m}\subset U_{m}
for every
1\leq m\leq M
. Extend
\hat{\alpha}
to a partition
\alpha
of
X
in some way such that
\alpha\succeq\mathcal{U}
and
\#\alpha=2M
.

Set
\eta^{4}=\rho+\delta
and define for every
k>n
large enough,
f_{k}(x)=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\chi_{E}(T^{i}x)
and
L_{k}:=\{x\in X:f_{k}(x)>1-\eta^{2}\}
.
Then by Birkhoff ergodic theorem
\int f_{k}>1-\eta^{4}
, and

\eta^{2}\cdot\mu(L_{k}^{c})\leq\int_{L_{k}^{c}}1-f_{k}\leq\int_{X}1-f_{k}\leq\eta^{4}

which gives
\mu(L_{k})\geq 1-\eta^{2}
.
Put
J_{k}
to be the set of
x\in X
such that for any
j\geq k
,

(4)

\mu_{x}(\alpha_{0}^{j-1}(x))<\exp(-(h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|Y)-\eta)j),

and

(5)

|\frac{1}{j}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\log N_{\mu_{T^{i}x}}(\mathcal{U}_{0}^{n-1},\rho)\chi_{B}(T^{i}x)-\int_{B}\log N_{\mu_{z}}(\mathcal{U}_{0}^{n-1},\rho)d\mu(z)|\leq\eta.

By Theorem
3.1
and the Birkhoff ergodic theorem,
\mu(J_{k})>1-\eta^{2}
for
k
large enough. Set
G_{k}=L_{k}\cap J_{k}
and then
\mu(G_{k})>1-2\eta^{2}
.
Define
\tilde{G}_{k}=\{x\in G_{k}:\mu_{x}(G_{k})\geq 1-4\eta\},

then

\tilde{G}_{k}^{c}=\{x\in G_{k}:\mu_{x}(G_{k})<1-4\eta\}\cup G_{k}^{c}=\{x\in G_{k}:\mu_{x}(G^{c}_{k})>4\eta\}\cup G_{k}^{c}.

Therefore,

\mu(\tilde{G}_{k}^{c})\cdot 4\eta\leq\int\mu_{x}(G_{k}^{c})d\mu(x)+\mu(G_{k}^{c})=2\mu(G_{k}^{c})\leq 4\eta^{2},

i.e.,
\mu(\tilde{G}_{k}^{c})\leq\eta
.

We fix an element
C_{y}
of this partition of
G_{k}\cap\pi^{-1}\pi y
and want to estimate the number of
\alpha_{0}^{n-1}
-elements needed to cover it. If
0\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m}\leq k-n
are the
times elements of
C_{y}
visit
B
, then we need at most
N_{\mu_{T^{i_{j}}y}}(\mathcal{U}_{0}^{n-1},\rho)

\alpha_{i_{j}}^{i_{j}+n-1}
-elements
to cover
C_{y}
. Because the size of
[0,k-1]\setminus\cup_{j}[i_{j},i_{j}+n-1]
is at most
\eta^{2}k+2n
,
we need at most
\prod_{j=1}^{m}N_{\mu_{T^{i_{j}}y}}(\mathcal{U}_{0}^{n-1},\rho)\cdot(2M)^{\eta^{2}k+2n}

\alpha_{0}^{k-1}
-elements to cover
C_{y}
. Finally, in view of
(
5
)
, we know that
G_{k}\cap\pi^{-1}\pi y
can be covered by no more than

(6)

e^{kH(\eta^{2}+2n/k)}\cdot(2M)^{\eta^{2}k+2n}\cdot e^{k(\int_{B}\log N_{\mu_{z}}(\mathcal{U}_{0}^{n-1},\rho)d\mu(z)+\eta)}

\alpha_{0}^{k-1}
-elements.
Since
y\in G_{k}\subset J_{k}
, any
V\in\alpha_{0}^{k-1}
intersecting nontrivially with
G_{k}\cap\pi^{-1}\pi y
has
\mu_{y}
-measure less than

\exp(-(h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|Y)-\eta)k)
by (
4
). Thus we have

(7)

\displaystyle 1-4\eta\leq\mu_{y}(G_{k}\cap\pi^{-1}\pi y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{-(h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|Y)-\eta)k}e^{kH(\eta^{2}+2n/k)}\cdot(2M)^{\eta^{2}k+2n}\cdot e^{k(\int_{B}\log N_{\mu_{z}}(\mathcal{U}_{0}^{n-1},\rho)d\mu(z)+\eta)}.

Recall that the distribution of
\beta
is the same as the distribution of the partition
\beta|_{\tilde{B}}
and
z\mapsto N_{\mu_{z}}(\mathcal{U}_{0}^{n-1},\rho)
is constant on each atom of
\beta|_{X\setminus A}
by Lemma
6.1
.
Then by (
7
) and setting
k\to\infty
, we get

\displaystyle h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|Y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\eta+H(\eta^{2})+\eta^{2}\log(2M)+\int_{B}\log N_{\mu_{z}}(\mathcal{U}_{0}^{n-1},\rho)d\mu(z)+\eta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\eta+H(\eta^{2})+\eta^{2}\log(2M)+\frac{1}{n}\int\log N_{\mu_{z}}(\mathcal{U}_{0}^{n-1},\rho)d\mu(z).

By letting
\delta\to 0
, we obtain

\displaystyle h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|Y)\leq 2\rho^{\frac{1}{4}}+H(\rho^{\frac{1}{2}})+\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\log(2M)+\frac{1}{n}\int\log N_{\mu_{z}}(\mathcal{U}_{0}^{n-1},\rho)d\mu(z).

Taking
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
and then
\lim_{\rho\to 0}
, we have

\displaystyle h_{\mu}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)\leq h_{\mu}(T,\alpha|Y)\leq\underline{h}^{S}_{\mu}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y).

∎

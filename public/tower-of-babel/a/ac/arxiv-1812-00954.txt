Trading T gates for dirty qubits in state preparation and unitary synthesis
Guang Hao Low, Vadym Kliuchnikov, Luke Schaeffer

Abstract
Efficient synthesis of arbitrary quantum states and unitaries from a universal fault-tolerant gate-set e.g. Clifford+ T is a key subroutine in quantum computation. As large quantum algorithms feature many qubits that encode coherent quantum information but remain idle for parts of the computation, these should be used if it minimizes overall gate counts, especially that of the expensive T -gates. We present a quantum algorithm for preparing any dimension- N N pure quantum state specified by a list of N N classical numbers, that realizes a trade-off between space and T -gates. Our scheme uses 𝒪 ⁡ ( log ⁡ ( N / ϵ ) ) \mathcal{O}(\log{(N/\epsilon)}) clean qubits and a tunable number of ∼ ( λ ​ log ⁡ ( log ⁡ N ϵ ) ) \sim(\lambda\log{(\frac{\log{N}}{\epsilon})}) dirty qubits, to reduce the T -gate cost to 𝒪 ⁡ ( N λ + λ ​ log ⁡ N ϵ ​ log ​ log ⁡ N ϵ ) \mathcal{O}(\frac{N}{\lambda}+\lambda\log{\frac{N}{\epsilon}}\log{\frac{\log{N}}{\epsilon}}) . This trade-off is optimal up to logarithmic factors, proven through an unconditional gate counting lower bound, and is, in the best case, a quadratic improvement in T -count over prior ancillary-free approaches. We prove similar statements for unitary synthesis by reduction to state preparation. Underlying our constructions is a T –efficient circuit implementation of a quantum oracle for arbitrary classical data.

Preparation of an arbitrary dimension

\displaystyle|\psi_{w}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in\{0,1\}^{w}}\sqrt{p_{y}}|y\rangle\underset{O_{w}}{\mapsto}\sum_{y\in\{0,1\}^{w}}\sqrt{p_{y}}|y\rangle|\theta_{y}\rangle

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{y\in\{0,1\}^{w}}\sqrt{p_{y}}|y\rangle\left(\sqrt{\frac{p_{y0}}{p_{y}}}|0\rangle+\sqrt{\frac{p_{y1}}{p_{y}}}|1\rangle\right)|\theta_{y}\rangle

4
Unitary synthesis by state preparation

The ability to prepare arbitrary quantum states enables synthesis of arbitrary unitaries
U\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times N}
. Given the matrix elements
\{|u_{k}\rangle\;|\;k\in[K]\}
for the first
K
columns of
U
, the
isometry synthesis problem is to find a quantum circuit that implements a unitary
V
that approximates
U
in the first
K
columns to error

\|U-V\|_{2,K}=\max_{x<K}\left\|(U-V)|x\rangle\right\|\leq\epsilon
.

We use the Householder reflections decomposition
[
24
]
to find a
V
that is a product of
K
reflections

\mathbb{I}-2|v_{k}\rangle\!\langle v_{k}|
and a diagonal gate
\mathrm{diag}(e^{i\phi^{\prime}_{1}},\ldots,e^{i\phi^{\prime}_{K}},1,\ldots,1)
, for some set of quantum states
|v_{k}\rangle
. Note that this representation is not unique. The diagonal gate can be eliminated by using one ancillary qubit as discussed in
[
25
]
. There, it suffices to implement the unitary

W=|0\rangle\!\langle 1|\otimes U+|1\rangle\!\langle 0|\otimes U^{\dagger}
, which is equal to the product of reflections

\mathbb{I}-2|w_{k}\rangle\!\langle w_{k}|
where
|w_{k}\rangle=(|1\rangle\otimes|k\rangle-|0\rangle\otimes|u_{k}\rangle)/\sqrt{2}
for
k\in[K]
.

Given a state preparation unitary
A_{k}|0\rangle=|u_{k}\rangle
, one can prepare state
|w_{k}\rangle
starting from
|0\rangle\otimes|e_{1}\rangle
. Apply Hadamard to the first qubit, then a sequence of CNOT gates to prepare
|1\rangle\otimes|k\rangle+|0\rangle\otimes|0\rangle
, and finally apply controlled-
A_{k}
negatively controlled on the first qubit. Note that when this method is applied to the synthesis of sparse isometries, the states being synthesized are again sparse. Moreover, the cost of converting a state into a reflection doubles the number of non-Clifford gates.
Thus the number of
T
gates used to synthesize an isometry is twice that for all the Controlled-
A_{k}
operations, and scales like
\mathcal{O}\left(K\left(\frac{N}{\lambda}+\lambda\log{(\frac{N}{\epsilon})}\log{(\frac{K\log{N}}{\epsilon})}\right)\right)
.

Let us now establish a lower bound on state preparation that holds when measurements and arbitrary number of ancillae are used.
For the purpose of the lower bound we also allow the use of post-selected measurements of multiple-qubit Pauli observables.
Every preparation of an

\Gamma

Conclusion

We have shown that arbitrary quantum states with
N
coefficients, or unitaries with
KN
values specified by classical data may be synthesized with a
T

\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{N})
reduction over prior art. As these subroutines are ubiquitous in many quantum algorithms we expect this result to be widely applicable.

We also expect our approach to be practical due to its almost exclusive usage of dirty qubits, which are typically abundant in larger quantum algorithms. Though our results are asymptotically optimal, constant factor and logarithmic improvements in costs could still be possible through careful optimization
[
30
,
31
]
. For instance, our approach can be modified to use only
\mathcal{O}(1)
additional clean qubits, but this increases the
T

T
gates and ancillary qubits are observed in other quantum circuits, such as for addition
[
22
]
or
And

[
19
]
, a major open question highly relevant to implementation in nearer-term quantum computers, is whether such a property could be generic for many other quantum circuits and algorithms.

There is an alternative construction based on implementing an

.

For

Proof.

When

Now suppose the ancillary qubits are dirty. Dirty qubits cannot store a qubit in the same way as clean qubits; since the initial state of the qubit is arbitrary, the information is encoded in the

U
of depth
O(\log^{2}N+\log b)
, using
O(bN)
dirty ancillary qubits and
O(\sqrt{N})

T
gates. Alternatively, there is a circuit for
U
using
O(\sqrt{bN})

T
gates and only
O(\lambda\sqrt{bN})
dirty ancillary qubits, but with depth
O(\sqrt{bN}/\lambda+\log^{2}N)
, for any
1\leq\lambda\leq\sqrt{bN}
.

Proof.

Divide the input into
n-k
and
k
bit pieces, where
k
is to be determined later. Let
\hat{f}\colon\{0,1\}^{n-k}\to\{0,1\}^{b\times 2^{k}}
be

\displaystyle\hat{f}(x_{hi})=\big(f(x_{hi}x_{lo})\big)_{x_{lo}\in\{0,1\}^{k}},

(32)

the function which outputs the function for all possible values of the low order bits, given the high order bits.

Suppose, for the moment, that we have as many clean ancillary qubits as we want. We can naïvely compute
\hat{f}(x_{hi})
by constructing
e^{(n-k)}(x_{hi})
, making
b\cdot 2^{k}
copies, and computing the parity of some subset of bits of
e^{(n-k)}(x_{hi})
for each output bit of
\hat{f}(x_{hi})
. Constructing
e^{(n-k)}(x_{hi})
requires
O(2^{n-k})
ancillary qubits,
O(2^{n-k})

T
gates and
O((n-k)^{2})
depth. The rest is done with
\textsc{Cnot}_{b\cdot 2^{k}}
gates to make copies and
\textsc{Cnot}_{O(2^{n-k})}
gates conjugated by Hadamards to compute parities. This uses many ancillary qubits–
O(2^{n-k})
for the original vector times
O(b2^{k})
copies is
O(bN)
qubits.

Since the layers of
Cnot
gates compute (in the output bits) a linear function of the inputs, it is not difficult to adapt for dirty ancillary qubits. Just apply the linear function, flip the dirty ancillary qubits that are set to
1
, then apply the linear function again, appealing to the identity
T(x\oplus y)\oplus T(y)=T(x)
for a linear function. Thus, whatever state
|y\rangle
was in the dirty qubits, we still manage to compute
T(x)
.

We have shown how to compute
\hat{f}(x_{hi})
and XOR it into dirty ancillary qubits. We also know how to compute
e^{k}(x_{lo})
and XOR
b
copies of it into dirty ancillary qubits. Think of
\hat{f}(x_{hi})
as a
b\times 2^{k}
matrix, then all that remains is to return the correct column of
\hat{f}(x_{hi})
. If we also think of
e^{k}(x_{lo})
as a length
2^{k}
column vector, then we are computing a matrix/vector product. The simplest way to do this is to make
b
copies of
e^{k}(x_{lo})
and execute
b
vector/vector products in parallel, at a cost of
O(2^{k})
Toffoli gates for each one.

To compute the Toffoli gates on dirty ancillary qubits, we decompose them into
Cnot
gates and single qubit gates. The layers of
Cnot
gates are linear, so it is possible to compute each such layer with dirty ancillary qubits.

Computing
\hat{f}(x_{hi})
uses
O(bN)
ancillary qubits,
O(2^{n-k})

T
gates, and has depth
O((n-k)^{2}+k+\log b)
. The rest of the circuit has
O(b2^{k})
ancillas to store
\hat{f}(x_{hi})
and/or copies of
e^{k}(x_{lo})
, and uses
O(b2^{k})

T
gates in depth
O(k^{2}+\log b)
. Since the
T
gate count is
O(2^{n-k}+b2^{k})
, we set
k
such that
2^{k}\approx\sqrt{N/b}
, and it becomes
O(\sqrt{bN})
.

It is possible to trade off depth and number of ancillary qubits. We only need
O(2^{n-k})
qubits to store
e^{(n-k)}(x_{hi})
in the computation of
\hat{f}(x_{hi})
, if we are willing to compute parities for each of the
b2^{k}
output bits of
\hat{f}(x_{hi})
one at a time, in depth
O(b2^{k})
. More generally, we can use
O(\lambda 2^{n-k})
ancillary qubits for any integer
\lambda\in[1,b2^{k}]
and use depth
O(b2^{k}/\lambda)
. For the optimal
T
count we use the same setting of
k
, giving
O(\sqrt{bN}/\lambda+\log^{2}N)
depth with
O(\lambda\sqrt{bN})
ancillary qubits and
O(\sqrt{bN})

T
gates.

∎

Appendix D
Pure-state preparation implementation details

The approach by Shende, Bullock, and Markov
[
12
]
synthesizes a unitary
A
that prepares a pure state
A|0\rangle=\sum_{x=0}^{N}\frac{a_{x}}{\|\vec{a}\|_{2}}|x\rangle=|\psi\rangle
with arbitrary coefficients in
N=2^{n}
dimensions. The underlying circuit, illustrated below for the example of
N=8
for positive coefficients,

is built from
j\in[n]
multiply-controlled arbitrary single qubit rotations,
U_{j}
where

\displaystyle U_{j}=\sum_{x=0}^{2^{j}-1}|x\rangle\!\langle x|\otimes e^{i2\pi\theta_{j,x}Z},

(33)

for some set of rotation angles
\theta_{j,x}
. Note that it suffices to consider
Z
-phase rotations as rotations about the
X,Y
Pauli operators are equivalent up to a single-qubit Clifford similarity transformation. Each multiplexor is applied twice – once to create a pure state with the right probabilities
|a_{x}|^{2}
, and once to apply the correct phase
e^{i\arg{[a_{x}]}}
. Below we describe how
U_{j}
may be implemented using the data-lookup oracle of
Eq.

5

\displaystyle O|x\rangle|0\rangle|0\rangle=|x\rangle|a_{x}\rangle|\mathrm{garbage}_{x}\rangle,

(34)

and evaluate the overall error and cost of state preparation.

D.1
Multiply-controlled phase gate from data lookup oracles

Consider a multiply-
n
-controlled arbitrary single qubit rotation

\displaystyle U=\sum_{x=0}^{2^{n}-1}|x\rangle\!\langle x|\otimes e^{i2\pi\theta_{x}Z},

(35)

where each rotation angle
\theta_{x}\in[0,1)
. Given a number state
|x\rangle
and an arbitrary single-qubit state
|z\rangle
,
this unitary performs a controlled-rotation

\displaystyle U|x\rangle|z\rangle=|x\rangle e^{i2\pi\theta_{x}Z}|z\rangle.

(36)

Each rotation angle has a binary expansion

\displaystyle\theta_{x}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{x,i}}{2^{k+1}}.

(37)

By truncating to the above to
b
-bits of precision, we obtain an integer approximation
a_{x}
of
\theta_{x}
where

\displaystyle a_{x}=2^{b}\sum_{k=0}^{b-1}\frac{a_{x,k}}{2^{k+1}},\quad\left|\frac{a_{x}}{2^{b}}-\theta_{x}\right|<\frac{1}{2^{b}}.

(38)

Let us encode these values of
a_{x}
into the data-lookup oracle, and express its
T
cost as the function
f(n,b)
. Its output is then

\displaystyle O|x\rangle|0\rangle^{\otimes b}|0\rangle=|x\rangle\left[\bigotimes_{k=0}^{b-1}|a_{x,k}\rangle\right]|\mathrm{garbage}_{x}\rangle,

(39)

where we explicitly represent the number state
|a_{x}\rangle
in terms of its component qubits.

D.1.1
Approach using arbitrary single-qubit synthesis

One possible approximation, call it
U^{\prime}
, of
U
in
Eq.

35
applies the single-qubit rotation

e^{i\pi Z/2^{k}}
to the target state
|z\rangle
, controlled by the state
|a_{x,k}\rangle
. The garbage register is then uncomputed by running
O
in reverse. Explicitly, this circuit realizes the transformation

\displaystyle|x\rangle|0\rangle^{\otimes b}|0\rangle|z\rangle

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle|x\rangle\left[\bigotimes_{k=0}^{b-1}|a_{x,k}\rangle\right]|\mathrm{garbage}_{x}\rangle|z\rangle

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle|x\rangle\left[\bigotimes_{k=0}^{b-1}|a_{x,k}\rangle\right]|\mathrm{garbage}_{x}\rangle e^{i\pi\sum^{b-1}_{k=0}a_{x,k}Z/2^{k}}|z\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|x\rangle|a_{x}\rangle|\mathrm{garbage}_{x}\rangle e^{i2\pi a_{x}/2^{b}Z}|z\rangle

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle|x\rangle|0\rangle^{\otimes b}|0\rangle e^{i2\pi a_{x}/2^{b}Z}|z\rangle.

(40)

Now, each controlled-arbitrary phase rotation decomposes into
2
arbitrary single-qubit rotations, and
CNot
gates – Note that a decomposition into
1
arbitrary single-qubit rotation is possible if we modify the above for the range
\theta_{x}\in(-1/2,1/2)
, but the explanation is slightly more complicated. As any arbitrary single-qubit rotation is approximated to error
\epsilon
using
\mathcal{O}(\log{(1/\epsilon)})

T
gates
[
43
]
, we may use a triangle inequality to bound the error to

\displaystyle\|U-U^{\prime}\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\sum_{x=0}^{2^{n}-1}|x\rangle\!\langle x|\otimes(e^{i2\pi\theta_{x}Z}-e^{i2\pi a_{x}/2^{b}Z})\right\|+2b\epsilon

\displaystyle\leq\max_{|y|<1/2^{b}}\left|(e^{i2\pi y}-1)\right|+2b\epsilon

\displaystyle<\frac{2\pi}{2^{b}}+2b\epsilon.

(41)

Thus for any target error
\|U-U^{\prime}\|=\delta
, we may solve for the
b,\epsilon
parameters and bound the number of
T
gates required to

\displaystyle f(n,b)+\mathcal{O}(\log^{2}{(1/\delta)}),\quad b=\mathcal{O}(\log{(1/\delta)}).

(42)

D.1.2
Approach using phase gradients

A more efficient approach
[
22
]
uses a Fourier state as a resource

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{b}}}\sum_{k=0}^{2^{b}-1}e^{-2\pi ik/2^{b}}|k\rangle.

(43)

combined with a reversible adder

\displaystyle\textsc{Add}|x\rangle|y\rangle

\displaystyle=|x\rangle|{y+x}\mod{2^{b}}\rangle

(44)

Observe that

\displaystyle\textsc{Add}|x\rangle|\mathcal{F}\rangle=e^{2\pi ix/2^{b}}|x\rangle|\mathcal{F}\rangle.

(45)

Thus the controlled adder, known to cost
\mathcal{O}(b)

T
gates,

\displaystyle\textsc{Cadd}=\textsc{Add}\otimes|0\rangle\langle 0|+\textsc{Add}^{\dagger}\otimes|1\rangle\langle 1|

(46)

realizes the controlled-phase rotation

\displaystyle\textsc{Cadd}|x\rangle|\mathcal{F}\rangle|z\rangle=|x\rangle|\mathcal{F}\rangle e^{2\pi ix/2^{b}Z}|z\rangle.

(47)

Thus another possible approximation, call it
U^{\prime\prime}
, uses this adder, controlled by the target state
|z\rangle
, to the registers containing the desired phase rotation
|a_{x}\rangle
, and the Fourier state. This realizes the same transformation in
Section

Assuming that the Fourier state is prepared perfectly, this approximates

with error

\displaystyle\|U-U^{\prime\prime}\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\sum_{x=0}^{2^{n}-1}|x\rangle\!\langle x|\otimes(e^{i2\pi\theta_{x}Z}-e^{i2\pi a_{x}/2^{b}Z})\right\|\leq\max_{|y|<1/2^{b}}\left|(e^{i2\pi y}-1)\right|<\frac{2\pi}{2^{b}}.

The state preparation unitary

applies

Note that the cost of approximating the Fourier state to error

(51)

The total

\displaystyle\textsc{T}\;\text{cost}=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}f(j,b)+\mathcal{O}(b)\right)+\mathcal{O}(b\log{(1/\epsilon)})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(2^{n}/\lambda+nb\lambda+nb+b\log{(1/\epsilon)})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{N}{\lambda}+b(\lambda n+\log{(1/\delta)})\right)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{N}{\lambda}+\log{\left(\frac{\log{N}}{\delta}\right)}\left(\lambda\log{N}+\log{(1/\delta)}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{N}{\lambda}+\lambda\log{\left(\frac{\log{N}}{\delta}\right)}\log{\left(\frac{N}{\delta}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{N}{\lambda}+\lambda\log^{2}{\left(\frac{N}{\delta}\right)}\right).

(52)

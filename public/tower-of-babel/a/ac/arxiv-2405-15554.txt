Slow linear shear flow past discrete particles adhered to a plane wall
Itzhak Fouxon 1,2, Alexander M. Leshansky 1

Abstract
Linear shear flow bounded by a plane wall is an idealization that occurs in microfluidic devices and many other applications. Perfect plane approximation neglects surface irregularities and discrete particles adsorbed at the surface. Here we study the disturbance to the linear shear flow due to the particle(s) rigidly attached to the surface. We first revisit the exact solution of O’Neill [ 1 ] for a spherical particle in contact with an infinite plane boundary. While the original paper contains multiple typos and provides very few details of the derivation, we present detailed solution accompanied by an alternative and simpler derivation of the viscous force and the torque exerted on the particle. We further study the universal far-field behavior of the flow due to an arbitrary particle adhered to the surface, and demonstrate that it is controlled by the stress moment of magnitude depending on particle’s volume and shape. Using the revised O’Neill solution, we compute the stress moment for a spherical particle. Using the far-field asymptotic form of the flow we estimate the net flow due to uniform and sparse layer of discrete adsorbates by superposition and demonstrate that it does not decay away from the plane.

I
Introduction

Wall-bounded shear flow is one of the most basic flows of low-Reynolds-number hydrodynamics. One of the most common examples is the pressure-driven (Poiseuille) flow near the wall of the microfluidic slit channel.
The flow is parallel to the plane wall and its magnitude grows linearly with the distance from the no-slip surface on which the flow velocity vanishes. However, realistic surfaces are typically non-smooth due to intrinsic surface roughness and/or presence of discrete micro- and nanometric particles adsorbed onto the surface from the liquid. These irregularities may distort the idealized unidirectional shear flow, possibly generating a rather complex flow pattern. The intuitive assumption would be that the flow bounded by a realistic non-smooth surface can be approximated by that above an ideally smooth plane wall at distances much further than the characteristic linear scale of the surface corrugation or size of the discrete adsorbates. In other words, one might anticipate the flow
disturbance
to be confined to the near vicinity of the bounding surface depth comparable to a typical length scale of the roughness or diameter of the adsorbates. However low-Reynolds-number flows are governed by the Stokes equations, that are rather similar to the Laplace equation of electrostatics. Considering the non-decaying electric field induced by the surface charge distributed at the flat boundary, one might anticipate the emergence of the (non-decaying) secondary flow away from the bounding wall due to surface corrugations or a layer of discrete absorbates. The well-known example of such secondary flow is the microfluidic mixer based on patterns of shallow grooves fabricated on the floor of the microchannel using photolithography
[
2
]
.

In the present paper we focus on flow disturbance due to discrete adsorbates and consider the analytically trackable case of a linear shear flow past to a spherical particle in a contact with a rigid plane. The exact solution in special“touching sphere” coordinates was first reported by O’Neill
[
1
]
, however the paper contains multiple typos that are difficult to detect, as very little details of the derivation are provided. We, therefore, revisit the solution and provide the detailed derivations correcting the typos in Ref.
[
1
]
. The expressions for the viscous force and torque exerted on the particle by the flow are obtained using an alternative and simpler approach that involves integration over the plane and then confirmed by the direct integration over the particle surface.

We then extend the analysis to adsorbates of an
arbitrary
shape, using integral representation of the wall-bounded flow. This allows to derive a universal closed-form expression for the far-field flow disturbance, being controlled by the scalar stress moment
M
of magnitude depending on the particle volume and shape. The value of
M
is then computed explicitly for a spherical particle using the revised O’Neill solution. Based on the far-field asymptotic form of the flow disturbance due to a single particle, we then estimate the
constant
net flow induced by a uniform layer of adsorbates with low surface coverage using superposition.

The present study is also relevant to the analysis of the signal (impedance) of liquid-phase Quart Crystal Microbalance (QCM) device, that relies on the fact that small matter adsorbed on the surface of the fast oscillating crystal, changes the frequency and the decay rate of the oscillations
[
3
]
. Although the frequency of the oscillations is in MHz range, for nanometric adsorbates (e.g., such as viruses or protein molecules that are much smaller than the viscous penetration depth,
\delta\approx 250
nm in water at the fundamental frequency of 5 MHz), the corresponding hydrodynamic problem in the frequency domain reduces to that studied here
[
4
]
.

Shear flow past a spherical particle attached to a plane revisited

II.1
Problem formulation

We consider the wall-bounded shear flow of the incompressible Newtonian fluid,
\bm{w}=(\dot{\gamma}z,0,0)
, where
\dot{\gamma}
is the shear-rate, impinging on the spherical particle with radius

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\nabla p=\nabla^{2}\bm{w},\ \ \nabla\cdot\bm{w}=0,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\bm{w}(z=0)=0,\ \ \bm{w}(|\bm{q}|\to\infty)=z\hat{\bm{x}},\ \ \bm{w}(|\bm{q}|=1)=\bm{0}.

(1)

The center of the sphere has coordinates
(0,0,1)

\bm{q}
is the corresponding radius-vector with the origin at the particle center,
\bm{q}\equiv(x,y,z-1)

The solution can be written as
\bm{w}=z\hat{\bm{x}}+\bm{v}
where the flow perturbation
\bm{v}

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\nabla p=\nabla^{2}\bm{v},\ \ \nabla\cdot\bm{v}=0,\ \quad\mbox{and}\quad\bm{v}(z=0)=0,\ \bm{v}(|\bm{q}|\to\infty)=\bm{0},\ \ \bm{v}(|\bm{q}|=1)=-z\hat{\bm{x}}.

(2)

In the cylindrical coordinates

2

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\frac{\partial p}{\partial\rho}=\nabla^{2}v_{\rho}-\frac{v_{\rho}}{\rho^{2}}-\frac{2}{\rho^{2}}\frac{\partial v_{\varphi}}{\partial\varphi},\ \quad\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial p}{\partial\varphi}=\nabla^{2}v_{\varphi}-\frac{v_{\varphi}}{\rho^{2}}+\frac{2}{\rho^{2}}\frac{\partial v_{\rho}}{\partial\varphi},\ \quad\frac{\partial p}{\partial z}=\nabla^{2}v_{z},

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial(\rho v_{\rho})}{\partial\rho}+\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial v_{\varphi}}{\partial\varphi}+\frac{\partial v_{z}}{\partial z}=0,\

with the boundary conditions:

\displaystyle v_{\rho}=-z\cos\varphi\,,\ \ v_{\varphi}=z\sin\varphi\,,\ v_{z}=0\quad\mbox{at}\quad|\bm{q}|=1,\ \ \bm{v}(z=0)=0,\ \ \bm{v}(|\bm{q}|\to\infty)={\bm{0}}.

The dependence on
\varphi

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!v_{\rho}=U(\rho,z)\cos\varphi,\ \ v_{\varphi}=V(\rho,z)\sin\varphi,\ \ v_{z}=W(\rho,z)\cos\varphi,\ \ p=P(\rho,z)\cos\varphi,

It can be readily shown that

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\nabla^{2}p=0,\ \ \nabla^{2}\bm{u}=0.

Then by using

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!{\hat{L}}_{1}P={\hat{L}}_{1}\phi=0,\ \ {\hat{L}}_{m}\equiv\nabla^{2}-\frac{m^{2}}{\rho^{2}}=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\rho^{2}}+\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}-\frac{m^{2}}{\rho^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}},

(5)

where the operators

are defined as in
[
1

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!u_{x}=\phi(\rho,z)\cos^{2}\varphi-V(\rho,z)\sin^{2}\varphi,\ \quad\nabla^{2}\langle u_{x}\rangle={\hat{L}}_{0}\langle u_{x}\rangle=0,

(6)

where the angular brackets stand for averages over
\varphi

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!{\hat{L}}_{0}\psi(\rho,z)=0;\ \quad\psi(\rho,z)\equiv U(\rho,z)-\frac{\rho P(\rho,z)}{2}-V(\rho,z).

(7)

Similarly, using

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!u_{y}=\chi(\rho,z)\sin\varphi\cos\varphi,\ \ {\hat{L}}_{2}\chi(\rho,z)=0;\ \ \chi(\rho,z)\equiv U(\rho,z)-\frac{\rho P(\rho,z)}{2}+V(\rho,z).

These equations could also be obtained directly by using the ansatz (
3

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\frac{\partial P}{\partial\rho}=\nabla^{2}U-\frac{2(U+V)}{\rho^{2}},\ \quad-\frac{P}{\rho}=\nabla^{2}V-\frac{2(U+V)}{\rho^{2}},\ \ \frac{\partial P}{\partial z}=\nabla^{2}W-\frac{W}{\rho^{2}},

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial(\rho U)}{\partial\rho}+\frac{V}{\rho}+\frac{\partial W}{\partial z}=0,

where we used

\displaystyle U=-z,\ V=z,\ W=0\ \quad\mbox{at}\quad|\bm{q}|=1,\ \ U=V=W=0\quad\mbox{at}\quad z=0\,.

(10)

Thus the problem reduces to solving for the zero modes of

II.2
Zero modes of the operators
{\hat{L}}_{m}

Below we derive zero modes of the operator

in “tangent sphere” coordinates defined by:

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\eta(\rho,z)=\frac{2\rho}{\rho^{2}+z^{2}},\ \ \xi(\rho,z)=\frac{2z}{\rho^{2}+z^{2}};\ \quad\ \rho(\eta,\xi)=\frac{2\eta}{\xi^{2}+\eta^{2}},\ \ z(\eta,\xi)=\frac{2\xi}{\xi^{2}+\eta^{2}}.

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\rho^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}=\frac{(\xi^{2}+\eta^{2})^{2}}{4}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\eta^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\xi^{2}}\right),

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!{\hat{L}}_{m}=\frac{(\xi^{2}+\eta^{2})^{2}}{4}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\eta^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\xi^{2}}\right)+\frac{\xi^{2}+\eta^{2}}{2\eta}\left(\frac{\xi^{2}-\eta^{2}}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta}-\eta\xi\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}\right)-\frac{m^{2}(\xi^{2}+\eta^{2})^{2}}{4\eta^{2}}.

(14)

The following operator identities

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\eta^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\xi^{2}}\right)\sqrt{\xi^{2}+\eta^{2}}=\sqrt{\xi^{2}+\eta^{2}}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\eta^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\xi^{2}}\right)+\frac{2\xi}{\sqrt{\xi^{2}+\eta^{2}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}+\frac{2\eta}{\sqrt{\xi^{2}+\eta^{2}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\xi^{2}+\eta^{2}}}

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\left(\frac{\xi^{2}-\eta^{2}}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta}-\eta\xi\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}\right)\sqrt{\xi^{2}+\eta^{2}}=\sqrt{\xi^{2}+\eta^{2}}\left(\frac{\xi^{2}-\eta^{2}}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta}-\eta\xi\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}\right)-\frac{\eta\sqrt{\xi^{2}+\eta^{2}}}{2},

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\left.\frac{\partial_{\rho}\psi}{\sqrt{\xi^{2}\!+\!\eta^{2}}}\right|_{\xi=0}=\!\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\left(\frac{D}{k}\right)^{\prime}J_{1}(k\eta)kdk\!-\!\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!J_{1}(k\eta)\left(k^{2}\frac{d^{2}}{dk^{2}}\!-\!k\frac{d}{dk}\!-\!1\right)D(k)\frac{dk}{k}\!=\!-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!J_{1}(k\eta)\left((kD)^{\prime\prime}\!-\!2D^{\prime}\right)dk,

whose comparison with Eq. (
33
) gives
x_{1}=-kD^{\prime\prime}/2
.
Similarly, it is readily seen by comparison with the previous derivation that

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\left.\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}\frac{\partial_{\rho}\psi}{\sqrt{\xi^{2}\!+\!\eta^{2}}}\right)\right|_{\xi=0}=\frac{3}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!E^{\prime}J_{1}(k\eta)kdk-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!J_{1}(k\eta)\left(k^{2}\frac{d^{2}}{dk^{2}}-k\frac{d}{dk}-1\right)E(k)dk=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!J_{1}(k\eta)k^{2}E^{\prime\prime}dk,

(34)

which implies
y_{1}=-kE^{\prime\prime}/2
.
In the last two equations we used the following relations between the Bessel functions:

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!J_{0}(k\eta)=\frac{1}{k\eta}\frac{d(kJ_{1}(k\eta))}{dk},\ \qquad J_{2}(k\eta)=\frac{J_{1}(k\eta)}{k\eta}-\frac{1}{\eta}\frac{\partial J_{1}(k\eta)}{\partial k}.

(35)

Thus conclude that

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\partial_{\rho}\psi=-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\xi^{2}\!+\!\eta^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[kD^{\prime\prime}\cosh(k\xi)+kE^{\prime\prime}\sinh(k\xi)\right]J_{1}(k\eta)dk,

(36)

Finally, we notice using
{\hat{L}}_{2}={\hat{L}}_{1}-3/\rho^{2}
that

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!0=\partial_{\rho}{\hat{L}}_{2}\chi={\hat{L}}_{2}\partial_{\rho}\chi-\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\partial_{\rho}\chi+\frac{8}{\rho^{3}}\chi,\ \ {\hat{L}}_{1}\partial_{\rho}\chi=\frac{4}{\rho^{2}}\partial_{\rho}\chi-\frac{8}{\rho^{3}}\chi,

(37)

We also notice that

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!{\hat{L}}_{1}\frac{1}{\rho}\chi=\frac{1}{\rho}{\hat{L}}_{1}\chi-\frac{2}{\rho^{2}}\frac{\partial\chi}{\partial\rho}+\frac{1}{\rho^{3}}\chi=-\frac{2}{\rho^{2}}\frac{\partial\chi}{\partial\rho}+\frac{4}{\rho^{3}}\chi.

(38)

Summation of the last two equations shows that
{\hat{L}}_{1}\left(\partial_{\rho}\chi+2\chi/\rho\right)=0
. Therefore we have

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\frac{1}{\sqrt{\xi^{2}\!+\!\eta^{2}}}\left(\partial_{\rho}\chi+\frac{2\chi}{\rho}\right)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[x_{2}(k)\cosh(k\xi)+y_{2}(k)\sinh(k\xi)\right]J_{1}(k\eta)dk,

(39)

for some functions
x_{2}(k)
and
y_{2}(k)
. Differentiation of the last of Eqs. (
23
) yields

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\frac{\partial_{\rho}\chi\!+\!2\chi/\rho}{\sqrt{\xi^{2}\!+\!\eta^{2}}}\!=\!\frac{\eta}{2}\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\left[F(k)\cosh(k\xi)\!+\!G(k)\sinh(k\xi)\right]J_{2}(k\eta)dk\!+\!\frac{\xi^{2}}{\eta}\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\left[F(k)\cosh(k\xi)\!+\!G(k)\sinh(k\xi)\right]J_{2}(k\eta)dk

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!+\frac{\xi^{2}-\eta^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\left[F(k)\cosh(k\xi)\!+\!G(k)\sinh(k\xi)\right]J_{2}^{\prime}(k\eta)kdk-\eta\xi\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\left[F(k)\sinh(k\xi)\!+\!G(k)\cosh(k\xi)\right]J_{2}(k\eta)kdk.

(40)

By setting
\xi=0
in the last equation we obtain

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\left.\frac{\partial_{\rho}\chi\!+\!2\chi/\rho}{\sqrt{\xi^{2}\!+\!\eta^{2}}}\right|_{\xi=0}=\frac{\eta}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!F(k)J_{2}(k\eta)dk-\frac{\eta^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!F(k)J_{2}^{\prime}(k\eta)kdk=\frac{\eta}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!(kF^{\prime}+2F)J_{2}(k\eta)dk

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!(F^{\prime}+2F/k)J_{1}(k\eta)dk+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!(kF^{\prime}+2F)^{\prime}J_{1}(k\eta)dk,

where we used the 2nd of Eqs. (
35
). The comparison of the last result with Eq. (
39
) readily gives
x_{2}(k)=kF^{\prime\prime}/2+2F^{\prime}+F/k
. Analogously we have

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\left.\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}\frac{\partial_{\rho}\chi\!+\!2\chi/\rho}{\sqrt{\xi^{2}\!+\!\eta^{2}}}\right)\right|_{\xi=0}\!\!=\!\frac{\eta}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!(kG^{\prime}+G)J_{2}(k\eta)kdk=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!(kG^{\prime}+G)J_{1}(k\eta)dk+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!(k^{2}G^{\prime}+kG)^{\prime}J_{1}(k\eta)dk.

(41)

which gives
{y_{2}}=kG^{\prime\prime}/2+2G^{\prime}+G/k
such that

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\partial_{\rho}\chi+\frac{2\chi}{\rho}\!=\!\frac{\sqrt{\xi^{2}\!+\!\eta^{2}}}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\left[\left(kF^{\prime\prime}\!+\!4F^{\prime}\!+\!\frac{2F}{k}\right)\cosh(k\xi)\!+\!\left(kG^{\prime\prime}\!+\!4G^{\prime}\!+\!\frac{2G}{k}\right)\sinh(k\xi)\right]J_{1}(k\eta)dk.

Finally, combining the above results the continutity equation yields the following ODE’s:

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!2\left(k\frac{d}{dk}+3\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}B\\
C\end{array}\right)-2\left(k\frac{d^{2}}{dk^{2}}+\frac{d}{dk}-\frac{1}{k}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}A\\
0\end{array}\right)-k\frac{d^{2}}{dk^{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}D\\
E\end{array}\right)+\left(k\frac{d^{2}}{dk^{2}}+4\frac{d}{dk}+\frac{2}{k}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}F\\
G\end{array}\right)=0.

Ref.
[
5
]
considered a more general case of a motion of a dumbbell made of two touching spheres in a linear shear flow. Their
Eqs. 2.9 (for
m=1
) are identical to Eqs. (
II.3
) except for a factor of
2
in the first term in the LHS.

II.4
Boundary conditions

In order to determine the seven unknown functions in the solution given by Eqs. (
23
), besides the continuity equation, we shall use the no-slip boundary conditions at the plane (
\xi=0
), and at the sphere surface (
\xi=1
). Below we follow
[
1
]
and express the six functions in terms of a single function
A(k)
. Then the system of Eqs. (
II.3
) transforms into a single ODE for
A(k)
that can be solved numerically.

We begin with the boundary condition on the vertical velocity
W
. The condition
W=0
at
\xi=0
was already applied. However, the continuity equation (
24
) together with
U=V=\partial_{\rho}U=0
at
\xi=0
provides an extra condition,
\partial_{z}W(\xi=0)=0
, which results in

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!B(k)J_{1}(k\eta)dk\!=\!-\eta^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!A(k)J_{1}(k\eta)kdk\!=\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!A(k)\left(k^{2}\frac{d^{2}}{dk^{2}}J_{1}(k\eta)+k\frac{d}{dk}J_{1}^{\prime}(k\eta)-J_{1}(k\eta)\right)\frac{dk}{k}.

(51)

where we used the equation for the Bessel function of order one
z^{2}J_{1}^{\prime\prime}+zJ_{1}^{\prime}-J_{1}=-z^{2}J_{1}
. Integrating by parts the last term in Eq. (
51
) and equating the coefficients of the Bessel function on both sides, we obtain:

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!B\!=\!(kA)^{\prime\prime}-A^{\prime}-\frac{A}{k}=kA^{\prime\prime}+A^{\prime}-\frac{A}{k}.

(52)

The condition at the particle surface
W(\xi=1)=0
gives

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!-\frac{1}{1\!+\!\eta^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\left(B(k)\cosh k\!+\!C(k)\sinh k\right)J_{1}(k\eta)dk=\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!A(k)\sinh kJ_{1}(k\eta)dk,

which can be rewritten in the form similar to that in Eq. (
51
):

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\left[B\cosh k\!+\!(C+A)\sinh k\right]J_{1}(k\eta)dk=-\eta^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!A(k)\sinh kJ_{1}(k\eta)dk,

which gives the corresponding condition

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!B\cosh k\!+\!(C\!+\!A)\sinh k\!=k\left(\frac{A\sinh k}{k}\right)^{\prime\prime}\!+\!\left(\frac{A\sinh k}{k}\right)^{\prime}\!-\!\frac{A\sinh k}{k^{2}}\!=\!

\displaystyle A^{\prime\prime}\sinh k\!-\!\frac{A^{\prime}\sinh k}{k}\!+\!2A^{\prime}\cosh k\!+\!A\sinh k-\frac{A\cosh k}{k}.

Using the Eq. (
52
) for
B
the function
C
can be readily obtained as

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!C\sinh k=A^{\prime\prime}\sinh k-k\cosh kA^{\prime\prime}-\frac{A^{\prime}\sinh k}{k}+A^{\prime}\cosh k\ \Rightarrow\ C=\left(kA^{\prime\prime}-A^{\prime}\right)K,

where following Ref.
[
1
]
we introduced:

\ K\equiv k^{-1}-\coth k\,.

The conditions of vanishing of
U
and
V
at
\xi=0
give

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!B(k)J_{1}(k\eta)dk\!=\!-\eta\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!D(k)J_{0}(k\eta)dk,\ \quad\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!B(k)J_{1}(k\eta)dk\!=\!-\eta\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!F(k)J_{2}(k\eta)dk,

where we used Eq. (
23
). Using in the above equations the relations in Eqs. (
35
) and integration by parts, we find that
B=k(D/k)^{\prime}
and
kB=-(kF)^{\prime}
. This gives expressions for
D
and
F
via
A
by using Eq. (
52
):

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dk}\frac{D}{k}=\frac{d}{dk}\left(A^{\prime}+\frac{A}{k}\right)\ \

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\ \ \ D=kA^{\prime}+A,

\displaystyle-\frac{d(kF)}{dk}=\frac{d}{dk}(k^{2}A^{\prime}-kA)\ \

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\ \ F=A-kA^{\prime},

where we made use of the fact that the coefficients vanish as
k\to\infty
. The remaining conditions are
U=-V=-z
at the particle surface
\xi=1
, see Eq. (
10
), resulting in:

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\eta\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!A(k)\sinh kJ_{1}(k\eta)dk-\frac{4}{(1+\eta^{2})^{3/2}}\!=\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\left[D(k)\cosh k\!+\!E(k)\sinh k\right]J_{0}(k\eta)dk,

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\eta\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!A(k)\sinh kJ_{1}(k\eta)dk\!=\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\left[F(k)\cosh k\!+\!G(k)\sinh k\right]J_{2}(k\eta)dk.

(53)

For the first in Eqs. (
53
) we have

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\eta\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!A(k)\sinh kJ_{1}(k\eta)dk=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!A(k)\sinh k\frac{d}{dk}J_{0}(k\eta)dk=\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!(A\sinh k)^{\prime}J_{0}(k\eta)dk,

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\frac{1}{(1+\eta^{2})^{3/2}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}ke^{-k}J_{0}(k\eta)dk,

(54)

where we assumed that
A(k)\sinh k
vanishes at
k=0
. We thus find

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!(A\sinh k)^{\prime}-4ke^{-k}=D(k)\cosh k\!+\!E(k)\sinh k,

(55)

which upon substituting

becomes

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!E=kA^{\prime}K-4k(\coth k-1).

Finally, using
k\eta J_{0}(k\eta)+k\eta J_{2}(k\eta)=2J_{1}(k\eta)
in the 2nd of Eqs. (
53
) gives

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!-\eta^{2}\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!A(k)\sinh kJ_{1}(k\eta)dk\!=\!\eta\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\left[F(k)\cosh k\!+\!G(k)\sinh k\right]J_{0}(k\eta)dk\!-\!2\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\left[F(k)\cosh k\!+\!G(k)\sinh k\right]J_{1}(k\eta)\frac{dk}{k}.

This can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!A(k)\left(k^{2}\frac{d^{2}}{dk^{2}}J_{1}(k\eta)+k\frac{d}{dk}J_{1}^{\prime}(k\eta)-J_{1}(k\eta)\right)\frac{\sinh kdk}{k^{2}}

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!=\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\left(F(k)\cosh k\!+\!G(k)\sinh k\right)\frac{d(kJ_{1}(k\eta))}{dk}\frac{dk}{k}-2\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\left(F(k)\cosh k\!+\!G(k)\sinh k\right)J_{1}(k\eta)\frac{dk}{k}.

(56)

where Eq. (
35

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!(A\sinh k)^{\prime\prime}-\left(\frac{A\sinh k}{k}\right)^{\prime}-\frac{A\sinh k}{k^{2}}=-(F\cosh k\!+\!G\sinh k)^{\prime}-\frac{F\cosh k\!+\!G\sinh k}{k}.

Substituting

, we find after some algebra

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!kKA^{\prime\prime}-(k+K)A^{\prime}+2A=-G^{\prime}-G\coth k-\frac{G(k)}{k}\ \ \Rightarrow\ \ G=\left(2A-kA^{\prime}\right)K.

(57)

Collecting all coefficients in terms of

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!B\!=\!kA^{\prime\prime}+A^{\prime}-\frac{A}{k},\ \ C=\left(kA^{\prime\prime}-A^{\prime}\right)K,\ \ D=kA^{\prime}+A,

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!E=kA^{\prime}K-4k(\coth k-1),\ \ F=A-kA^{\prime},\ \ G=\left(2A-kA^{\prime}\right)K.

Substituting the above relationship between the coefficients into Eq. (

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!k^{3}K^{\prime}A^{\prime\prime}+kA^{\prime}\left(k^{2}K^{\prime\prime}+2K+3kK^{\prime}\right)-A\left(k^{2}K^{\prime\prime}+4kK^{\prime}+2K\right)=2k^{2}(2X^{\prime}+kX^{\prime\prime})\,,

II.5
Viscous force and torque exerted on the particle

It can be readily shown that the undisturbed linear shear flow
\bm{w}\!=\!z\hat{\bm{x}}
does not contribute to the force or the torque exerted on the attached particle, therefore they can be determined from the perturbed flow in Eqs. (
2
). Below we present an alternative and simpler derivation to that of O’Neill
[
1
]
(see Appendix
A
) and correct some typos showing in his paper.

The force that the flow exerts on the particle reads

F_{x}=\oint_{r=1}\sigma_{xk}dS_{k}\,.

(71)

It is useful to rewrite Eq. (
2
) in the form
\nabla_{k}\sigma_{ik}=0
which gives upon the integration over the whole space:

F_{i}\equiv\oint_{r=1}\sigma_{ik}dS_{k}=-\int_{z=0}\sigma_{iz}dxdy\,,

(72)

where we used the fact that the integral over the infinite semi-sphere vanishes (see Sec.
III
). Therefore,

F_{x}=-\int_{z=0}\frac{\partial v_{x}}{\partial z}dxdy=-\int_{z=0}\left(\cos^{2}\theta\frac{\partial U(\rho,z)}{\partial z}-\sin^{2}\theta\frac{\partial V(\rho,z)}{\partial z}\right)\rho d\rho d\theta\,,

(73)

where we used Eqs. (
3
) and (
6
). Thus we find

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!F_{x}=-\pi\int_{z=0}\left(\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial z}+\frac{\rho}{2}\frac{\partial P}{\partial z}\right)\rho d\rho=-2\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}\left.\left(\eta\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\xi}+\frac{\partial P}{\partial\xi}\right)\right|_{\xi=0}\;\frac{d\eta}{\eta^{2}}\,,

(74)

where we used
\rho(\xi=0)=2/\eta
. It follows from Eqs. (
23
) that

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\left.\frac{\partial P}{\partial\xi}\right|_{\xi=0}\!=\!\eta\int_{0}^{\infty}C(k)J_{1}(k\eta)kdk;\ \quad\left.\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\xi}\right|_{\xi=0}\!=\!\eta\int_{0}^{\infty}E(k)J_{0}(k\eta)kdk.

(75)

We therefore conclude that

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!F_{x}=-2\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}E(k)J_{0}(k\eta)kdk+\frac{1}{\eta}\int_{0}^{\infty}C(k)J_{1}(k\eta)kdk\right)d\eta=2\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\eta}{\eta}\int_{0}^{\infty}(E^{\prime}-C)J_{1}(k\eta)kdk

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!=2\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}(E^{\prime}-C)kdk=2\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\{kA^{\prime}K^{\prime}+2KA^{\prime}-4(k(\coth k-1))^{\prime}\right\}kdk

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!=2\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\{4k(\coth k-1)-k^{2}KA^{\prime\prime}\right\}dk=2\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\{4k(\coth k-1)+(k^{2}\coth k)^{\prime\prime}A\right\}dk,

(76)

which agrees with
[
1
]
. Using the numerical solution for
A(k)
in Fig.
2
and in (
76
) yields upon integration

F_{x}=6\pi f\,,\quad f\simeq 1.70049,\,

which is close to
f\simeq 1.7009
in
[
1
]
. The extrapolated (to a vanishing separation distance from the plane) result corresponding to a freely suspended particle in a simple shear flow by in Ref.
[
7
]
gives practically the same result,
f\simeq 1.7005
. The other components of the force are zero. Clearly, the force on the particle in the the direction of the shear flow.

Next we consider the torque
\bm{L}
that the shear flow applies on the particle attached to the plane. The Stokes equations imply that
\nabla_{k}\epsilon_{lji}\left(\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{0}\right)_{j}\sigma_{ik}=0
holds for any
\bm{r}_{0}
. Integrating this identity over the flow domain gives (the integral over the infinite semi-sphere vanishes, see Sec.
III
)

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\oint_{r=1}\epsilon_{lji}\left(\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{0}\right)_{j}\sigma_{ik}d{\hat{n}}_{k}=-\int_{z=0}\epsilon_{lji}\left(\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{0}\right)_{j}\sigma_{iz}dxdy=-\int_{z=0}\epsilon_{lji}r_{j}\sigma_{iz}dxdy+\int_{z=0}\epsilon_{lzi}\sigma_{iz}dxdy,

(77)

where in the last equality we took
\bm{r}_{0}
at the center of the sphere, i.e.,
\bm{r}_{0}=(0,0,1)
and no summation is assumed over
z
. It can be readily shown that the
x
component of the torque vanishes:

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!L_{x}=-\int_{z=0}\sigma_{yz}dxdy-\int_{z=0}\epsilon_{xik}r_{i}\sigma_{kz}dxdy=-\int_{z=0}\frac{\partial v_{y}}{\partial z}dxdy-\int_{z=0}y\sigma_{zz}dxdy=\int_{z=0}ypdxdy=0\,.

(78)

The non-vanishing component is in
y-
direction. We have

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!L_{y}+F_{x}=-\int_{z=0}\epsilon_{yik}r_{i}\sigma_{kz}dxdy=\int_{z=0}x\sigma_{zz}dxdy=-\int_{z=0}xpdxdy=-\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho^{2}P(\rho,z=0)\,d\rho,

(79)

where we used
\int_{z=0}\epsilon_{yzi}\sigma_{iz}dxdy=\int_{z=0}\sigma_{xz}dxdy=-F_{x}
and the arbitrary constant in pressure is fixed by the demand of pressure vanishing at infinity. Using Eq. (
23
), where
z=0
is
\xi=0
, we find that

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!L_{y}+F_{x}=-\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{8d\eta}{\eta^{3}}\int_{0}^{\infty}B(k)J_{1}(k\eta)dk=\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{8d\eta}{\eta^{3}}\int_{0}^{\infty}(A^{\prime}+A/k)\frac{d}{dk}kJ_{1}(k\eta)dk

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!=\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{8d\eta}{\eta^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}(kA^{\prime}+A)J_{0}(k\eta)dk=\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{8d\eta}{\eta}\int_{0}^{\infty}kAJ_{1}(k\eta)dk=8\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}kAdk,

(80)

where we used
B(k)
from Eq. (
58
) re-written as
B\!=\!k(A^{\prime}+A/k)^{\prime}
. Numerical integration of the solution in Eq. (
81
) gives

L_{y}=-F_{x}+8\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}kA\,dk=4\pi g,\quad g\simeq 0.94411\,.

(81)

The vaue of
g
close to that reported in
[
1
]
,
g\simeq 0.943993
, however some typos are involved. The torque in Ref.
[
1
]
is defined as
L_{y}=-8\pi g
, which differs from our result by a factor of
2
and the negative sign. Notice that the torque should be positive,
L_{y}>0
, as the shear flow acts to rotate the particle in the clock-wise direction about the
y
-axis. Besides the value of
g
, the torque in the Eq. 4.9 of Ref.
[
1
]
is showing as
L_{y}=F_{x}+8\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}kAdk
, where the sign of the first term is wrong (c.f. Eq.
81
).

This concludes the revised analysis of the problem posed in
[
1
]
and provides a detailed and self-consistent derivation free of typos. The solution for the flow is given in special “tangent sphere” coordinates and it depends on a function
A(k)
that can be determined numerically (see Fig.
2
). Therefore it is worth to derive an asymptotic limit of the flow far from the plane where it may simplify considerably.

III
Integral representation and far-field flow

In this Section we introduce the integral representation of the solution and derive the far-field form of the flow disturbance. This analysis holds for adsorbed particles of an
arbitrary
shape
S
(it is still assumed that the origin is the point of contact with the plane) and demonstrates universal features of the far-flow. The case of surface corrugations will be considered elsewhere.

The integral representation of the flow is obtained from the Lorentz identity

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\nabla_{l}(v_{i}\sigma_{il}^{k})=\nabla_{l}(u_{i}^{k}\sigma_{il})-v_{k}\delta(\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime}),

(82)

where
\bm{u}^{k}
is the flow due to Stokeslet near an infinite (no-slip) plane,

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!-\nabla_{i}p^{k}+\nabla^{2}u^{k}_{i}=-\delta_{ik}\delta(\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime}),\ \ \nabla\cdot\bm{u}^{k}=0,\ \ \bm{u}^{k}(z=0)=\bm{u}^{k}(x=\infty)=0,

(83)

and
\sigma_{il}^{k}=-p^{k}\delta_{il}+\nabla_{i}u_{l}^{k}+\nabla_{l}u_{i}^{k}
is the stress tensor associated with this flow,
\nabla_{l}\sigma_{il}^{k}=-\delta_{ik}\delta(\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime})
. Integration of Eq. (
82
) over the whole volume of the flow
\bm{v}
gives

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\int_{S}v_{i}\sigma_{il}^{k}dS_{l}=\int_{S}u_{i}^{k}\sigma_{il}dS_{l}+v_{k}(\bm{x}^{\prime}),

(84)

where we assumed vanishing of the contributions of integrals over the surface at infinity (see below). Infinitesimal vector surface element
d\bm{S}
is assumed to point into the fluid. For a sphere
\sigma_{il}^{k}dS_{l}=\sigma_{ir}^{k}dS
, while for now we use the general surface element. Renaming
\bm{x}\to\bm{x}^{\prime}
,
i\to k
and using
u^{k}_{i}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime})=u^{i}_{k}(\bm{x}^{\prime},\bm{x})
we find the integral flow representation

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!v_{i}(\bm{x})\!=\!\int_{S^{\prime}}\!v_{k}(\bm{x}^{\prime})\sigma_{kl}^{i}(\bm{x}^{\prime},\bm{x})dS_{l}^{\prime}\!-\!\int_{S^{\prime}}\!\!u^{k}_{i}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime})\sigma_{kl}(\bm{x}^{\prime})dS_{l}^{\prime}\!=\!-\int_{S^{\prime}}\!\!z^{\prime}\sigma_{xl}^{i}(\bm{x}^{\prime},\bm{x})dS_{l}^{\prime}\!-\!\int_{S^{\prime}}\!u^{k}_{i}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime})\sigma_{kl}(\bm{x}^{\prime})dS_{l}^{\prime},

(85)

where we also used the boundary condition on the sphere (for shear flow that condition is the same for any
S
). For the first integral in the RHS of the above equation we have

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\int_{S^{\prime}}\!z^{\prime}\sigma_{xl}^{i}(\bm{x}^{\prime},\bm{x})dS_{l}^{\prime}=\int_{V^{\prime}}\nabla^{\prime}_{l}(z^{\prime}\sigma_{xl}^{i}(\bm{x}^{\prime},\bm{x}))dV^{\prime}=\int_{V^{\prime}}\sigma_{xz}^{i}(\bm{x}^{\prime},\bm{x})dV^{\prime}=\int_{V^{\prime}}\nabla^{\prime}\cdot\left(u^{i}_{x}(\bm{x}^{\prime},\bm{x}){\hat{z}}^{\prime}+u^{i}_{z}(\bm{x}^{\prime},\bm{x}){\hat{x}}^{\prime}\right)dV^{\prime}

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!=\int_{S^{\prime}}\left(u_{i}^{x}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime}){\hat{z}}^{\prime}+u_{i}^{z}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime}){\hat{x}}^{\prime}\right)\cdot d\bm{S}^{\prime},

(86)

where
\nabla^{\prime}
is the derivative with respect to
\bm{x}^{\prime}
and
V^{\prime}
stands for the particle’s interior. The above transformation allows to write the flow solely in terms of
u_{i}^{k}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime})
,

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!v_{i}(\bm{x})\!=\!-\int_{S^{\prime}}u_{i}^{k}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime})\left(\delta_{kx}\delta_{zl}+\delta_{kz}\delta_{xl}+\sigma_{kl}(\bm{x}^{\prime})\right)dS_{l}^{\prime}\equiv-\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{S^{\prime}}G_{ik}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime})\left(\delta_{kx}\delta_{zl}+\delta_{kz}\delta_{xl}+\sigma_{kl}(\bm{x}^{\prime})\right)dS_{l}^{\prime},

(87)

where
G_{ik}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime})
by
G_{ik}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime})\equiv 8\pi u_{i}^{k}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime})
is the corresponding Green’s function. Therefore, as typical in the integral flow representations, any point
\bm{x}^{\prime}
at the surface induces the flow proportional to
u_{i}^{k}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime})
at arbitrary point
\bm{x}
in the fluid. The Green’s function was first reported by Blake in Ref.
[
8
]
:

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!G_{ik}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime})=\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{R}\right)\delta_{ik}+\frac{q_{i}q_{k}}{q^{3}}-\frac{R_{i}R_{k}}{R^{3}}+2h(\delta_{k\beta}\delta_{\beta l}-\delta_{k3}\delta_{3l})\frac{\partial}{\partial R_{l}}\left(\frac{hR_{i}}{R^{3}}-\left(\frac{\delta_{i3}}{R}+\frac{R_{i}R_{3}}{R^{3}}\right)\right),

where
h\equiv z^{\prime}
is the height of the point-force singularity (source) above the plane,

\bm{q}=(x-x^{\prime},y-y^{\prime},z-h)
,
R=(x-x^{\prime},y-y^{\prime},z+h)
and
\beta
stands for either
x
or
y
(with no summation over
\beta
).
The far-field flow involves determining the asymptotic behavior of the Green’s function at large distances from the source. Its leading-order asymptotic form for
|x^{\prime}|\ll|x|
and
|y^{\prime}|\ll|y|
is found by simply setting
x^{\prime}=y^{\prime}=0
. However, for
h\ll z
we also need the higher-order term(s) in the Taylor series due to degeneracy
G_{ik}(h=0)=0
(the flow is fully hindered by the no-slip plane) such that

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!G_{ik}(\bm{x},\bm{x}^{\prime})\approx h\frac{\partial G_{ik}}{\partial h}(x^{\prime}=y^{\prime}=h=0),

(88)

where

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\frac{\partial G_{ik}}{\partial h}(x^{\prime}\!=\!y^{\prime}\!=\!h\!=\!0)\!=\!-2\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\left(\frac{\delta_{ik}}{r}\!+\!\frac{r_{i}r_{k}}{r^{3}}\right)\!-\!2(\delta_{k\beta}\delta_{\beta l}\!-\!\delta_{k3}\delta_{3l})\frac{\partial}{\partial r_{l}}\left(\frac{\delta_{i3}}{r}\!+\!\frac{r_{i}z}{r^{3}}\right)\!=\!

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!-2\left(\frac{\delta_{i3}r_{k}\!+\!\delta_{k3}r_{i}}{r^{3}}\!-\!\frac{\delta_{ik}z}{r^{3}}\!-\!\frac{3zr_{i}r_{k}}{r^{5}}\right)-2(\delta_{k\beta}\delta_{\beta l}-\delta_{k3}\delta_{3l})\left(\frac{\delta_{il}z+\delta_{3l}r_{i}}{r^{3}}-\frac{\delta_{i3}r_{l}}{r^{3}}-\frac{3r_{l}r_{i}z}{r^{5}}\right).\ \

After some algebra the above result simplifies to a compact formula:

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\frac{\partial G_{ik}}{\partial h}(x^{\prime}\!=\!y^{\prime}\!=\!h\!=\!0)=\delta_{k\beta}\frac{12zr_{\beta}r_{i}}{r^{5}}.

(89)

Substituting Eqs. (
88
) and (
89
) into Eqs. (
87
) gives the flow away from the source for
|x^{\prime}|\ll|x|
,
|y^{\prime}|\ll|y|
and
h\ll z
:

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!v_{i}(\bm{x})\approx-\frac{1}{8\pi}\frac{\partial G_{ik}}{\partial h}(x^{\prime}\!=\!y^{\prime}\!=\!h=\!0)\left(\oint_{S}z\left(\delta_{kx}\delta_{zl}+\delta_{kz}\delta_{xl}\right)dS_{l}+M_{k}\right)=-\frac{3z}{2\pi}\delta_{k\beta}\frac{r_{\beta}r_{i}}{r^{5}}\left(M_{k}+\delta_{kx}\oint_{S}zdS_{z}\right)\,,

(90)

where we introduced the stress moment

M_{k}\equiv\oint_{S}z\sigma_{kl}dS_{l}\,.

(91)

Using
\oint_{S}zdS_{z}=\int_{V}\nabla_{i}\cdot(\delta_{iz}z)=V
, where
V
is the particle volume, the Eq. (
90
) reads

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\bm{v}(\bm{x})\approx-\frac{3z\left(x(V+M_{x})+yM_{y}\right)}{2\pi|\bm{x}|^{5}}\bm{x}\,.

(92)

Adsorbed particle of an arbitrary shape induces universal far-field flow disturbance described by Eq. (
92
). The formula holds at distances from the particle that are much larger than its characteristic linear dimension.

III.1
Flow due to a sparse uniform distribution of particles

The far flow given by Eq. (
92
) decays
\propto x^{-2}
with the distance
x
from the particle (compare to
x^{-1}
-decay for the Stokeslet in unbounded fluid). This is analogous to the spatial decay of the electric field induced by a point charge in electrostatics. It is well-known that when charges are uniformly distributed over an infinite plane, the total electric field that they induce is independent of the distance from the plane. Hence, one can anticipate that a uniform distribution of adsorbed particles will induce constant flow away from the plane.

The Eq. (
92
) holds for the particle affixed to the plane at the origin. However, due to invariance of the horizontal directions, the flow originated by the particle near the arbitrary point
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
at the plane can be obtained by simply replacing
x
with
x-x^{\prime}
and
y
with
y-y^{\prime}
in Eq. (
92
). Thus, if identical particles are adsorbed uniformly over the plane with some number areal density
n
, the flow that they induce depends on the coordinate
z
only and is given by (we use
\bm{x}^{\prime}\equiv(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},0)
and
\bm{r}=(x,y,z)
):

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!v_{i}(z)=-\frac{3zn}{2\pi}\int\frac{(x-x^{\prime})(V+M_{x})+(y-y^{\prime})M_{y}}{((x-x^{\prime})^{2}+(y-y^{\prime})^{2}+z^{2})^{5/2}}(x_{i}-x^{\prime}_{i})dx^{\prime}dy^{\prime}=-\frac{3zn}{2\pi}\int\frac{r_{i}\left(x(V+M_{x})+yM_{y}\right)}{r^{5}}dxdy.

(93)

Here we assume that the particle areal number density
n
is low enough, such that the net flow is given by the superposition of the individual contributions due to each particle. The “low-surface coverage” assumption will be considered below. Eq. (
93
) also assumes that the collection of particles can be considered as a smooth surface layer rather than the collection of discrete particles. This is valid provided that the characteristic inter-particle distance is much smaller than
z
so that contributions of nearby particles are similar, i.e.,
n^{1/2}z\gg 1
.

The Eq. (
93
) indicates that, as one may anticipate from symmetry, the vertical flow vanishes,
v_{z}(z)=0
. The nontrivial horizontal components of the flow are given by

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!v_{x}(z)=-\frac{3zn(V+M_{x})}{2\pi}\int\frac{x^{2}dxdy}{(x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2})^{5/2}}=-n(V+M_{x}),\ \ v_{y}(z)=-nM_{y}.

(94)

Here and thereafter we use the identities

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\int\frac{dxdy}{r^{3}}=\frac{2\pi}{z},\ \ \ \int\frac{dxdy}{r^{5}}=\frac{2\pi}{3z^{3}},\ \ \ \int\frac{x^{2}dxdy}{r^{5}}=\int\frac{y^{2}dxdy}{r^{5}}=\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{(r^{2}-z^{2})dxdy}{r^{5}}=\frac{2\pi}{3z}.

(95)

The Eqs. (
94
) are remarkably simple and they confirm that the net flow due to a layer of adsorbed particles does not decay away from the plain, in accord with the electrostatic analogy above. However, the details are different: in electrostatics the induced field is vertical, while the far-field flow is horizontal. The
x-
component of the flow indicates flow hindrance due to a layer of adsorbed particles. (It probably can be rigorously shown that the term
V+M_{x}
is always positive, so that particles always hinder the flow). Although the flow disturbance does not decay with the distance from the plane, obviously the ratio of this constant flow and the unperturbed linear shear flow tends to zero away from the plane. On the other hand, the prospective transverse flow given by
v_{y}
, is a non-trivial leading-order contribution due to the adsorbed particles. Qualitatively, it is a result of hydrodynamic interaction of the shear flow with the asymmetric particles for which
M_{y}\neq 0
.

To the leading order, there is no vertical component in the far-field flow. The
z-
component of the flow due to a single particle in Eq. (
92
) is odd in
x
and in
y
. For instance, a spherical particle at the origin induces the vertical flow

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!v_{z}(\bm{x})\approx-\frac{3(V+M_{x})}{2\pi}\frac{xz^{2}}{r^{5}},

(96)

where
M_{y}=0
by symmetry, see the proof in the next subsection. The flow is odd in
x
, being directed upward for
x<0
and downward for
x>0
. For uniform surface distribution of spherical adsorbates, the contributions of individual particles compensate each other and the net vertical flow vanishes (the case of non-homogeneous distributions is considered in the next subsection).

The velocity field induced by a particle near the point
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
at the plane, considered as a function of
x^{\prime}
and
y^{\prime}
varies over the length scale of
\mathcal{O}(z)
. This implies the condition of uniform distribution holds given that the number of particles within an arbitrary circle with radius
r\simeq z
is about the same. For instance if the particles are distributed over a square lattice the result would hold at
z
much larger than the lattice constant (and clearly much larger than the particle size).

III.2
Far-field flow due to a spherical particle

In this subsection we consider the far-field perturbation of the shear flow due to a spherical particle affixed to a plane. We use the spherical coordinate system
(r,\theta,\varphi)
with origin at the sphere center. We shall demonstrate below that the only non-vanishing component of
M_{k}
in (
91
) is
M_{x}
, which is computed explicitly using the above solution in tangent sphere coordinates. Using the analysis of Sec.
II.1
one can show that

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!M_{k}=-\oint_{r=1}\!zP\cos\varphi\delta_{kr}dS+\oint_{r=1}\!z\tau_{kr}dS=-\pi\delta_{kx}\int_{0}^{\pi}\!zP\sin^{2}\theta d\theta+\oint_{r=1}\!z\tau_{kr}dS,\

where
\tau_{il}=\partial_{l}v_{i}+\partial_{i}v_{l}
are the components of the viscous stress tensor. Using the definitions of Sec.
II.1
we can write
\tau_{zz}
and
\tau_{zy}
in term of
U,V
and
W
in the cylindrical coordinates as

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\tau_{zz}=2\frac{\partial v_{z}}{\partial z}=2\frac{\partial W}{\partial z}\cos\varphi,\ \ \tau_{zx}=\frac{\partial(W\cos\varphi)}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial v_{x}}{\partial z}=\cos^{2}\varphi\left(\partial_{\rho}W+\partial_{z}U\right)+\sin^{2}\varphi\left(\frac{W}{\rho}-\partial_{z}V\right),

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\tau_{zy}=\frac{\partial(W\cos\varphi)}{\partial y}+\frac{\partial((U+V)\sin\varphi\cos\varphi)}{\partial z}=\sin\varphi\cos\varphi\left(\partial_{\rho}W-\frac{W}{\rho}+\frac{\partial(U+V)}{\partial z}\right).

(97)

Then, integration over
\varphi
yields

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!M_{z}=\oint_{r=1}\!z\left(\cos\theta\tau_{zz}+\sin\theta\cos\varphi\tau_{zx}+\sin\theta\sin\varphi\tau_{zy}\right)dS=0.

(98)

Similarly, the stress components
\tau_{yy}
and
\tau_{yx}
read

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\frac{\tau_{yy}}{2}\!=\!\frac{\partial v_{y}}{\partial y}\!=\!\sin\varphi\cos\varphi\frac{\partial(U\!+\!V)}{\partial y}\!+\!(U\!+\!V)\frac{\cos\varphi(\cos^{2}\varphi\!-\!\sin^{2}\varphi)}{\rho}\!=\!\sin^{2}\varphi\cos\varphi\left(\frac{\partial(U\!+\!V)}{\partial\rho}\!-\!\frac{2(U\!+\!V)}{\rho}\right)\!+\!\frac{\cos\varphi(U\!+\!V)}{\rho},

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\tau_{yx}=\sin\varphi\cos^{2}\varphi\left(\partial_{\rho}U-\frac{2(U+V)}{\rho}\right)-\sin^{3}\varphi\partial_{\rho}V+\sin\varphi\cos^{2}\varphi\partial_{\rho}(U+V)+(U+V)\frac{\sin\varphi(\sin^{2}\varphi-\cos^{2}\varphi)}{\rho},

(99)

and integration over
\varphi
gives

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!M_{y}=\oint_{r=1}\!z\left(\cos\theta\tau_{yz}+\sin\theta\cos\varphi\tau_{yx}+\sin\theta\sin\varphi\tau_{yy}\right)dS=0.

(100)

We therefore conclude that for a sphere affixed to a plane the the only nontrivial component of
M_{k}
is the
x
-component, i.e.,
M_{k}=\delta_{kx}M
.

Returning to the coordinate system with the origin at the point of contact, as used in previous subsection, it follows from Eq. (
96
) that far from an adsorbed sphere the flow disturbance is given by

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\bm{v}(\bm{x})\approx-\alpha\frac{xz\bm{r}}{r^{5}},\ \ \alpha\equiv 2+\frac{3M}{2\pi}.

(101)

The formula for pressure in the far field limit is obtained by solving the Stokes equation with the above flow,

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\partial_{i}p=\nabla^{2}v_{i}\approx-2\alpha\left(\frac{z\delta_{ix}+x\delta_{iz}}{r^{5}}-\frac{5xzr_{i}}{r^{7}}\right)\quad\Rightarrow\quad p\approx-2\alpha\frac{xz}{r^{5}}.

(102)

Note that at large distances there is a simple relation between pressure and vertical velocity,
v_{z}\approx zp/2
.

To determine
M
we compare the above asymptotic formula for the far-firld pressure with the full solution given by the first of Eqs. (
23
). Eq. (
102
) gives

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!rP\approx-2\alpha\frac{\sin\theta\cos\theta}{r^{2}}=-\frac{\alpha\eta\xi}{2}.

(103)

Using
\sqrt{\xi^{2}\!+\!\eta^{2}}=2/r
and Eqs. (
23
) we find that at
r\to\infty
or
\sqrt{\xi^{2}\!+\!\eta^{2}}\to 0

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!-\frac{\alpha\eta\xi}{2}\!\sim\!2\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\left(B(k)\cosh(k\xi)\!+\!C(k)\sinh(k\xi)\right)J_{1}(k\eta)dk\sim\eta\int_{0}^{\infty}B(k)kdk+\eta\xi\int_{0}^{\infty}C(k)k^{2}dk+\ldots,

(104)

where we used the series formula for
J_{1}
and dots stand for higher-order terms. Substituting the expression for
B(k)
from Eqs. (
58
) it can be shown that the first term in the RHS of Eq. (
104
) is zero:

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}B(k)kdk=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(k^{2}A^{\prime\prime}+kA^{\prime}-A\right)dk=0.

(105)

Using the second term in the RHS of Eq. (
104
) it follows that:

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\frac{\alpha}{2}\!=\!-\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!C(k)k^{2}dk\!=\!\int_{0}^{\infty}(kA^{\prime\prime}\!-\!A^{\prime})(k^{2}\coth k\!-\!k)dk\!=\!\int_{0}^{\infty}A\left(8k\coth k-\frac{7k^{2}}{\sinh^{2}k}-3+\frac{2k^{3}\cosh k}{\sinh^{3}k}\right)dk.

(106)

Using the above numerical solution for
A(k)
(see Fig.
2
) yields
\alpha=21.1745
. Finally, it follows from Eq. (
101
) that

M=\oint_{r=1}z\sigma_{xl}dS_{l}=\frac{4\pi}{3}\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}-1\right)\simeq 40.159\,,

(107)

This result can also be obtained using the direct integration of the tractive force over the particle surface at
r=1
(see Appendix
A
).

We now shall go back the underlying assumptions of the analysis in this subsection. The Eq. (
94
) indicates that a dilute uniform layer of spherical particles adsorbed at the plane surface induces a backflow
-n(4\pi/3+M)
with
M\simeq 40.159
and no lateral flow, as
M_{y}=0
. It can be readily seen, that the contribution proportional to the particles volume,
\propto nV
is small in comparison to that related to the dipole stress moment
\propto nM
. The disturbance to the linear shear flow created due to the layer of spherical particles obeys Eqs. (
2
) with the no-slip boundary conditions holding on each sphere. The flow given by superposition of individual contributions induced by each adsorbate is a good approximation provided that the flow disturbance in the near vicinity of a single particle due to rest of the particles (i.e., the hydrodynamic interaction) is negligible. Since the far-field flow due to each sphere holds at distances much larger than the particle radius, irrespective of
z
, then
-n(4\pi/3+M)\simeq-nM
provides an accurate approximation for the flow induced by the rest of the particles. Therefore, the low-surface coverage assumption holds if
nM\ll 1
. In the dimensional form, this condition reads
na^{2}\ll M^{-1}
.
Notice that the extra condition on the vertical distance due to the approximation of smooth layer in subsection
III.1
implies
n^{1/2}z\ll 1
.

Finally, it is important to stress that for asymmetric particles, however,
M_{y}
may not vanish and they may generate a constant flow that does not decay away from the plane, in the direction transverse to the direction of the shear flow.

The higher fixed point theorem for foliations. Applications to rigidity and integrality August 7, 2026 Thanks: Mathematical subject classification (1991). 19L47, 19M05, 19K56. Key words: C ∗ C^{*} -algebras, K-theory, Lefschetz, foliations.
Moulay Tahar Benameur, James L. Heitsch

Abstract
. We give applications of the higher Lefschetz theorems for foliations of [ BH10 ] , primarily involving Haefliger cohomology. These results show that the transverse structures of foliations carry important topological and geometric information. This is in the spirit of the passage from the Atiyah-Singer index theorem for a single compact manifold to their families index theorem, involving a compact fiber bundle over a compact base. For foliations, Haefliger cohomology plays the role that the cohomology of the base space plays in the families index theorem. We obtain highly useful numerical invariants by paring with closed holonomy invariant currents. In particular, we prove that the non-triviality of the higher A ^ \widehat{A} genus of the foliation in Haefliger cohomology can be an obstruction to the existence of non-trivial leaf-preserving compact connected group actions. We then construct a large collection of examples for which no such actions exist. Finally, we relate our results to Connes’ spectral triples, and prove useful integrality results.



1.
Introduction

In this paper we continue our program of investigating invariants for foliations which come from the fact that their transverse structures carry important topological and geometric information. These invariants arise from the extension of (generally classical cohomological) invariants to invariants which involve the Haefliger cohomology of the foliation. This is in the spirit of the passage from the Atiyah-Singer index theorem for a single compact manifold to their families index theorem, involving a compact fiber bundle over a compact base. For foliations, Haefliger cohomology plays the role that the cohomology of the base space plays in the families index theorem.

3.
Lefschetz Examples

3.1.
The de Rham complex

For the deRham complex, things are particularly simple, and we begin this section with some general results for this case. In particular, if (E,d) is the de Rham complex along the leaves, then only the zero component of the closed holonomy invariant current
C
is involved. To see this, we compute
\displaystyle\frac{i^{*}[\sigma(E,d)](h)}{\lambda_{-1}(N^{h}\otimes{{\mathbb{C}}})(h)}
in this case.
Now
\sigma(E,d)
is given by the sequence

0\longrightarrow\Lambda^{0}_{{\mathbb{C}}}T^{*}F\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\wedge\xi}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Lambda^{1}_{{\mathbb{C}}}T^{*}F\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\wedge\xi}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Lambda^{2}_{{\mathbb{C}}}T^{*}F\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\wedge\xi}}{{\longrightarrow}}\cdots

where
\xi\in T^{*}F
and
\Lambda^{k}_{{\mathbb{C}}}T^{*}F=\Lambda^{k}T^{*}F\otimes{\mathbb{C}}
is the complexified
k
-th exterior power. When we restrict this sequence to
F^{h}
, we get

\displaystyle\Lambda^{k}T^{*}F\,\,=\,\,\displaystyle\oplus_{i+j=k}\Lambda^{i}T^{*}F^{h}\otimes\Lambda^{j}N^{h,*},

and
\xi\in T^{*}F^{h}
acts only on the first factor. Thus we have that

i^{*}[\sigma(E,d)]\,\,=\,\,\sigma(E^{h},d)\lambda_{-1}(N^{h}\otimes{{\mathbb{C}}}),

where
(E^{h},d)
is the de Rham complex along
F^{h}
. So,

\frac{i^{*}[\sigma(E,d)](h)}{\lambda_{-1}(N^{h}\otimes{{\mathbb{C}}})(h)}\,\,=\,\,\sigma(E^{h},d)(h).

Now
\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\sigma(E^{h},d)(h))\operatorname{Td}(TF^{h}\otimes{\mathbb{C}})
is just
\chi(TF^{h})
, the Euler class of
TF^{h}
,
which is non-zero only in dimension equal to the dimension of
F^{h}
.
Thus
\displaystyle\int_{F^{h}}\chi(TF^{h})
is a zero dimensional Haefliger cohomology class, where
\displaystyle\int_{F^{h}}
is Haefliger’s integration over the plaques of
F^{h}
,
[
H80
]
. But,

L_{C}(h;\text{de Rham})\,\,:=\langle\left(\int_{F^{h}}\chi(TF^{h})\right),[C|_{V^{h}}]\rangle\,\,=\,\,\langle\left(\int_{F^{h}}\chi(TF^{h})\right),[C_{0}]\rangle,

where
C_{0}
is the zero component of
C|_{V^{h}}
, a holonomy invariant transverse distribution. So the higher components of
C
do not contribute to the Lefschetz formula for the de Rham complex. As an obvious corollary,
L_{C}(h;\text{de Rham})
vanishes for all holonomy invariant transverse currents without zero component. When the foliation is oriented for instance, and denoting by
[V/F]
the class of the transverse fundamental cycle, we get

L_{[V/F]}(h;\text{de Rham})=0.

In the special case where
V^{h}
is a strict transversal, the leafwise tangent bundle
TF^{h}
reduces to the zero bundle and we get:

\frac{\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathbb{C}}}([E^{i}|_{V^{h}}](h))}{\sum_{j}(-1)^{j}\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathbb{C}}}([\wedge^{j}(TF^{h}\otimes{\mathbb{C}})](h))}\,\,=\,\,\frac{\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathbb{C}}}([\wedge^{i}(TF^{h}\otimes{\mathbb{C}})](h))}{\sum_{j}(-1)^{j}\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathbb{C}}}([\wedge^{j}(TF{h}\otimes{\mathbb{C}})](h))}\,\,=\,\,1.

Hence, in the case where
C_{0}
is a positive holonomy invariant
transverse
measure, one gets

L_{C}(h;\text{de Rham})=L_{C_{0}}(h;\text{de Rham})=C_{0}(V^{h})\geq 0,

compare with
[
HL90
]
.
Second,
if the foliation is transversally oriented, the zero-th component of the transverse fundamental class is zero,
[
C86
]
. More specifically,

L_{[V/F]}(h;\text{de Rham})=0.

To see this, recall that for Haefliger forms,

\displaystyle[[V/F]|_{V^{h}}]\left(\omega\right)=0
, unless
the degree of
\omega=q
. As
q>0
,

L_{[V/F]}(h;\text{de Rham})\,\,=\,\,\left<\chi(TF^{h}),[[V/F]|_{V^{h}}]\right>\,\,=\,\,[[V/F]|_{V^{h}}]\left(\int_{F^{h}}\chi(TF^{h})\right)\,\,=\,\,0.

\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathbb{C}}}([E^{i}\,|_{V^{h}}](h)\,\,=\,\,2,

so

L_{C}(h;\text{deRham}(W_{V}))\,\,=\,\,\sum_{j=0}^{1}\left<\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1+\eta_{i}\beta_{i}),C|_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2k}_{j}}\right>\,\,=\,\,2\left<\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1+\eta_{i}\beta_{i}),C|_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2k}_{0}}\right>,

since the Haefliger current
C
is invariant under holonomy.
It is easy to see that each term in the expression
\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1+\eta_{i}\beta_{i})
has a Haefliger current which is dual to it.

3.2.2.
Signature Complex

\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathbb{C}}}([E^{i}\,|_{V^{h}}](h)\,\,=\,\,-2i,

so

L_{C}(h;\text{Signature}(W_{V}))\,\,=\,\,\frac{-2i}{2}\sum_{j=0}^{1}\left<\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1+\eta_{i}\beta_{i}),C|_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2k}_{j}}\right>\,\,=\,\,-2i\left<\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1+\eta_{i}\beta_{i}),C|_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2k}_{0}}\right>.

3.2.3.
Dolbeault Complex

Since
h
is a holomorphic map on each leaf, it induces endomorphisms of the Dolbeault leafwise complexes (for
k=0,1
)

0\longrightarrow C^{\infty}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}F\otimes_{{\mathbb{C}}}\wedge^{0}\overline{T}^{*}F)\longrightarrow C^{\infty}(\wedge^{k}T^{*}F\otimes_{{\mathbb{C}}}\wedge^{1}\overline{T}^{*}F)\longrightarrow 0,

where
T^{*}F
and
\overline{T}^{*}F
are the holomorphic and anti-holomorphic cotangent bundles respectively. We denote these endomorphisms by
\widehat{h}_{0}
and
\widehat{h}_{1}
, respectively. Then

\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathbb{C}}}([E^{i}\,|_{V^{h}}](\widehat{h}_{j})\,\,=\,\,i+1-2j,

so

L_{C}(\widehat{h}_{j};\text{Dolbeault}(W_{V}))\,\,=\,\,\frac{i+1-2j}{2}\sum_{j=0}^{1}\left<\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1+\eta_{i}\beta_{i}),C|_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2k}_{j}}\right>\,\,=\,\,(i+1-2j)\left<\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1+\eta_{i}\beta_{i}),C|_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2k}_{0}}\right>.

3.2.4.
Spin Complex

Using the two liftings, denoted
\widetilde{h}_{\pm}
, defined in
[
HL90
]
, we have

\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}\operatorname{ch}_{{\mathbb{C}}}([E^{i}\,|_{V^{h}}](\widetilde{h}_{\pm})\,\,=\,\,\pm{i}{\sqrt{2}},

so

L_{C}(\widetilde{h}_{\pm};\text{Spin}(W_{V}))\,\,=\,\,\frac{\pm{i}{\sqrt{2}}}{2}\sum_{j=0}^{1}\left<\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1+\eta_{i}\beta_{i}),C|_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2k}_{j}}\right>\,\,=\,\,\pm{i}{\sqrt{2}}\left<\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1+\eta_{i}\beta_{i}),C|_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2k}_{0}}\right>.

As
k
is as large as we please and each term in the expression
\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1+\eta_{i}\beta_{i})
has a Haefliger current which is dual to it, we thus have universal non-triviality for the higher Lefschetz numbers for all four of the classical elliptic complexes.

3.3.
Some cancellation relations

We briefly explain in this paragraph the close relation between Lefschetz formulae and some standard series relations. We concentrate here on a single example which shows how the triviality of the higher terms in our Lefschetz formula can be used already for simple foliations, to rediscover surprising cancellation identities. This approach opens up the way for other more involved relations by using more complicated foliations.. This application is thus in the spirit of the by now classical results which relate the Lefschetz theorem with number theory, see for instance
[
HZ74
]
. Recall that for any formal variable
z
,

\coth(z)=\frac{e^{z}+e^{-z}}{e^{z}-e^{-z}}=\frac{1}{z}+\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\,\frac{2^{2\ell}}{(2\ell)!}\,b_{2\ell}\;z^{2\ell-1},

where the
b_{n}
are the Bernoulli rational numbers.

Corollary 3.2
.

For any integer
n\geq 1
and any sequence of angles
0\leq\alpha_{0}\leq\cdots\leq\alpha_{n}<\pi/2
, the formal series in the commuting (formal free) variables
z_{0},\cdots,z_{n}
:

\sum_{j=0}^{n}\prod_{j\neq k=0}^{n}\coth\left[z_{k}-z_{j}+i(\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{j})\right]\,\,=\,\,\frac{1+(-1)^{n}}{2},

where
\coth\left[z_{k}-z_{j}+i(\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{j})\right]
is the power series

\displaystyle\frac{\coth(z_{k}-z_{j})+i\tan(\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{j})}{1+i\tan(\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{j})\coth(z_{k}-z_{j})}.

Said, differently, we have for any
n\geq 1
and any commuting (formal) variables
z_{0},\cdots,z_{n}

\sum_{j=0}^{n}\prod_{j\neq k=0}^{n}\frac{\coth(z_{k}-z_{j})+i\tan(\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{j})}{1+i\tan(\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{j})\coth(z_{k}-z_{j})}\,\,=\,\,\frac{1+(-1)^{n}}{2}.

Note that the corollary can be translated into an infinite number of relations corresponding to the coefficients of the series. It is also easy to see that it is equivalent to the simpler formula for the power series in the 2 by 2 distinct (for instance complex) variables
u_{0},\cdots,u_{n}
:

\sum_{j=0}^{n}\prod_{j\neq k=0}^{n}\coth(u_{k}-u_{j})\,\,=\,\,\frac{1+(-1)^{n}}{2}.

However the statement makes the proof more understandable. Also notice that these relations can be derived from a straightforward (rather astute) computation, but we prove it here as an easy application of the family Lefschetz theorem.

Proof.

We only need to give the proof of the formula under the assumption
0<\alpha_{0}<\cdots<\alpha_{n}<\pi/2
. Set
B={\mathbb{C}}P_{k_{0}}\times\cdots\times{\mathbb{C}}P_{k_{n}}
,
k_{j}\geq n
, which has cohomology the polynomial algebra
{\mathbb{R}}[x_{0},\cdots,x_{n}]
on two dimensional generators, truncated by the relations

x_{j}^{k_{j}+1}=0.

Denote by
E_{0},\cdots,E_{n}
the natural
{\mathbb{C}}
line bundles over
B
, so
c_{1}(E_{j})=x_{j}
. Denote by
P
the principal
U_{1}\times\cdots\times U_{1}\simeq{\mathbb{T}}^{n+1}
bundle associated to
\oplus_{j}E_{j}
. The torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{n+1}
acts on
{\mathbb{C}}P_{n}
by

(u_{0},\ldots,u_{n})[z_{0}:\cdots:z_{n}]\,\,:=\,\,[u_{0}z_{0}:\cdots:u_{n}z_{n}].

Set
V=P\times_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n+1}}{\mathbb{C}}P_{n}
, the quotient of
P\times{\mathbb{C}}P_{n}
by the diagonal action of
{\mathbb{T}}^{n+1}
.
Choose
a=(a_{0},\ldots,a_{n})\in T^{n+1}
with
a_{i}=\exp(i\theta_{j})
, where
0<\theta_{0}<\cdots<\theta_{n}<\pi
. Define the fiberwise action of
a
on
V
by

a[p,[z_{0}:\cdots:z_{n}]]:=[p,[a_{0}z_{0}:\cdots:a_{n}z_{n}]].

Note that the fibers of
\pi:V\to B
are oriented by their complex structures and that
a
preserves this orientation. The fixed point submanifold of
a
is the union of
n+1
connected components
(B_{j})_{0\leq j\leq n}
where
B_{j}
is given by

B_{j}=\{[p,[0:\cdots:0:1:0:\cdots:0]]\,|\,p\in P\}\text{ with }1\text{ at the }j-th\text{ position.}

Each
B_{j}
is a transversal to the fibration, and is diffeomorphic to
B
under
\pi
. The normal bundle to
B_{j}
is the pull-back under
\pi:B_{j}\to B
of the vector bundle
\oplus_{j\neq k=0}^{n}(E_{k}\otimes E_{j}^{*})
. Moreover, this decomposition into complex line bundles corresponds to the decomposition of the normal bundle of
B_{j}
into the eigenspaces of the action of the isometry
a
. We now apply the Lefschetz fixed point formula for families
[
B02
]
to
a
with respect to the signature operator
D^{+}_{vert}
along the oriented even dimensional fibers of
V
. We get

\operatorname{Sign}(a)\,\,=\,\,\operatorname{ch}(L(a;D^{+}_{vert}))\,\,=\,\,\sum_{j=0}^{n}\pi_{*}\nu_{j}\quad\in\quad\operatorname{H}^{*}(B;{\mathbb{R}}),

where
L(a;D^{+}_{vert})\in\operatorname{K}(B)\otimes{\mathbb{C}}
is the Lefschetz class of the index of
D^{+}_{vert}
evaluated at
a
. The expression
\nu_{j}\in\operatorname{H}^{*}(B_{j};{\mathbb{R}})
is the local contribution corrsponding to
B_{j}
in the fixed point formula. More precisely, denote by
{\mathcal{M}}^{\theta}
the multiplicative sequence associated with the series in the
x
variable

\frac{\tanh(i\theta/2)}{\tanh((x+i\theta)/2)}.

Then

\pi_{*}\nu_{j}\,\,=\,\,\prod_{j\neq k=0}^{n}\left(i\tan(\frac{\theta_{k}-\theta_{j}}{2})\right)^{-1}{\mathcal{M}}^{\theta_{k}-\theta_{j}}(E_{k}\otimes E_{j}^{*})\,\,=\,\,\prod_{j\neq k=0}^{n}\coth\left(\frac{x_{k}-x_{j}+i(\theta_{k}-\theta_{j})}{2}\right).

The Lefschetz class
\operatorname{Sign}(a)
can also be computed as follows. Denote the fiberwise cohomology of
V\to B
by
\operatorname{H}^{*}(V\,|\,B)
, and the
\pm
eigenspaces of fiberwise involution associated to the signature operator by
\operatorname{H}^{*}_{\pm}(V\,|\,B)
.
Since
{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
is connected, all elements act by the identity on this cohomology. Thus
\operatorname{Sign}(a)=\operatorname{Sign}(I)=\operatorname{ch}(\operatorname{H}^{n}_{+}(V\,|\,B))-\operatorname{ch}(\operatorname{H}^{n}_{-}(V\,|\,B))
. When n is odd,
\operatorname{H}^{n}(V\,|\,B))=0
, so
\operatorname{Sign}(a)=0
. When n is even,
\operatorname{H}^{n}(V\,|\,B)
is one dimensional, and

\operatorname{H}^{n}_{+}(V\,|\,B)\,\,=\,\,\operatorname{H}^{n}(V\,|\,B)\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{H}^{n}_{-}(V\,|\,B)\,\,=\,\,0.

Thus

\operatorname{Sign}(a)\,\,=\,\,\operatorname{ch}(\operatorname{H}^{n}(V\,|\,B))\,\,=\,\,1\quad\in\quad\operatorname{H}^{0}(B),

since the line bundle
\operatorname{H}^{n}(V\,|\,B)
over
B
is a trivial one dimensional bundle.
Therefore, in
\operatorname{H}^{*}(B;{\mathbb{R}})
,

\sum_{j=0}^{n}\prod_{j\neq k=0}^{n}\coth\left(\frac{x_{k}-x_{j}+i(\theta_{k}-\theta_{j})}{2}\right)\,\,=\,\,\frac{1+(-1)^{n}}{2}.

Replacing
x_{k}
by
2z_{k}
and
\theta_{k}
by
2\alpha_{k}
and noting that
the dimensions of the
{\mathbb{C}}P_{k_{j}}
are as large as we want completes the proof.
∎

4.
Obstructions to group actions

For the spin Dirac operator along the leaves of a foliation, we can generalize the Atiyah-Hirzebruch Rigidity Theorem
[
AH70
]
and prove a higher rigidity theorem which extends the measured rigidity theorem proven in
[
HL91
]
.

4.1.
The higher rigidity theorem

Suppose that
TF
is an even dimensional spin bundle, with associated leafwise Dirac operator
D
. Let
\widehat{A}(F)
be the
\widehat{A}
-characteristic class of the vector bundle
TF
.
Using our higher Lefschetz theorem, we generalize Proposition 3.2 of
[
HL91
]
by taking into account all the closed holonomy invariant currents
[
H80
]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that the tangent bundle
TF
to the foliation
(V,F)
is spin and that there exists a closed holonomy invariant current
C
such that

\left<\int_{F}\widehat{A}(TF),{[C]}\right>\neq 0.

Then no compact connected Lie group
H
can act non trivially as a group of isometries of
V
preserving the leaves of F and their spin structure.

Recall that since
H
is connected and preserves each leaf, the fixed point submanifold is transverse to F,
[
HL90
]
. Moreover, in this case all the elements of
H
act by holonomy diffeomorphisms. If
C
is given by a holonomy invariant transverse measure, we get the rigidity theorem of
[
HL91
]
. The power of Theorem
4.1
is that applies to a potentially large alternative collection of invariant currents
[
H80
]
. The proof uses the continuity of the higher Lefschetz map
h\rightarrow L_{C}(h;E,d)
and a straightforward generalization of a classical method based on Liouville’s theorem
[
AH70
]
together with our higher Lefschetz fixed point formula. A different proof given in the beautiful paper of Lawson and Yau
[
LY74
]
, with better results for effective
{\mathbb{S}}^{3}
actions, should also be extendable to foliations.

The proof of Theorem
4.1
relies on the following proposition. Notice that this proposition applies in particular to all leafwise actions of compact connected Lie groups.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
F
be an oriented foliation of the compact manifold
V
.
Assume that the compact Lie group
H
acts by holonomy diffeomorphisms on
(V,F)
. Then for any
H
-equivariant leafwise elliptic pseudodifferential complex
(E,d)
over
(V,F)
, and every closed Haefliger
2k
-current
C
on
(V,F)
, the map

h\mapsto L_{C}(h;E,d)
is a continuous map from
H
to
{\mathbb{C}}
.

Assuming Proposition
4.2
, we give the proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Proof.

We follow the proof of
[
HL91
]
, making the necessary changes and correcting some errors. We need to extend the classical proof of Atiyah-Hirzebruch
[
AH70
]
to our foliation setting. We may asume that
H={\mathbb{S}}^{1}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
. By Lemma 3.3 of
[
HL91
]
, the fixed point set
V^{H}
of
H
is a closed submanifold of
V
, which is transverse to
F
. Note that
V^{H}
is the fixed point set of any topological generator of
H
, and
V^{H}\neq V
, since
H
acts non-trivially. If
V^{H}=\emptyset
, we have immediately that
\displaystyle\left<\int_{F}\widehat{A}(TF),C\right>=0,

for all
C
, a contradiction, so in summary,
V^{H}
is a transverse submanifold such that
V\neq V^{H}\neq\emptyset
.

Denote by
V^{H}_{\alpha}
a connected component of
V^{H}
with the induced foliation
F_{\alpha}
. If
y\in V^{H}_{\alpha}\cap L
, then the normal bundle
N
to
V^{H}_{\alpha}\cap L
in
L
(=normal bundle to
V^{H}
in
V
) at
y
can be written as
\oplus N^{j}_{y}
, where
H
acts on
N^{j}_{y}
by the representation
z\mapsto z^{m_{j}}
for some positive integer
m_{j}
. It follows that the
N^{j}
are complex
H
bundles on
V^{H}_{\alpha}\cap L
.

Let
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
. The function
1/(1-ze^{-x})
can be written as
R(x,z)
, which is a formal power series in
x
whose coefficients are rational functions of
z
, having a pole only at
z=1
, and no pole at
z=\infty
. In particular, the coefficient of
x^{0}
is
1/(1-z)
, and that of
x^{n}
for
n>0
is
((-1)^{n}/n!)\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{n}z^{k}
. It is an easy exercise to prove that this is

\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}[z[\cdots[z[\frac{1}{1-z}]^{\prime}]^{\prime}\cdots]^{\prime}],

where the
′
indicates differentiation, and it is done
n
times.

Let
A^{r}(z)=(z^{r})^{1/2}\prod_{\ell=1}^{r}e^{-x_{\ell}/2}R(x_{\ell},z)
. Because of the factor
(z^{r})^{1/2}
, this is defined only up to sign. It defines a formal power series in
\sigma_{1},..,\sigma_{r}
the elementary symmetric functions in
x_{1},...,x_{r}
.

Now any
z\in H
which is a topological generator acts on
N^{j}
by multiplication by
z^{m_{j}}
. For such
z
, let
A(N^{j},z)=A^{d_{j}}(z^{m_{j}})
, where
d_{j}
is the (complex) dimension of
N^{j}
.
The Riemannian connection on
V^{H}_{\alpha}\cap L
preserves the bundles
N^{j}
and is a complex connection on each
N^{j}
. We can replace the symmetric functions
\sigma_{1},..,\sigma_{r}
by the corresponding Chern polynomials in the curvature of this connection. In this way
A(N^{j},z)
becomes a differential form on
V^{H}_{\alpha}\cap L
. Let
\widehat{A}(V^{H}_{\alpha}\cap L)
be the differential form we get by replacing the Pontrjagin classes in the expression for the
\widehat{A}
class of a real vector bundle by the Pontrjagin polynomials in the curvature of the Riemannian connection on
V^{H}_{\alpha}\cap L
, see for instance
[
AS68
]
, p. 570. Then define

A(V^{H}_{\alpha}\cap L,z)\,\,=\,\,(-1)^{(\operatorname{dim}_{{\mathbb{R}}}F)/2}\widehat{A}(V^{H}_{\alpha}\cap L)\prod_{j}A(N^{j},z).

Now
A(V^{H}_{\alpha}\cap L,z)
is a differential form on
V^{H}_{\alpha}\cap L
which has the factor
(z^{d})^{1/2}
, where
d=\sum m_{j}d_{j}
and
d_{j}=\operatorname{dim}_{{\mathbb{C}}}N^{j}
. The choice of sign is determined by adapting the argument of
[
AH70
]
, p. 21. Namely, let
y\in V^{H}_{\alpha}
and choose
(z^{d})^{1/2}
so that

2^{n_{\alpha}}(z^{d})^{1/2}\prod_{j}(1+z^{-m_{j}})^{d_{j}}\,\,=\,\,\text{trace}(z\,|\,E^{+}_{y}\oplus E^{-}_{y})),

where
L
is the leaf through
y
,
2{n_{\alpha}}=\operatorname{dim}(V^{H}_{\alpha}\cap L)
, and
E^{\pm}
are the complex vector bundles associated to the spin structure constructed from the irreducible spin representations
\Delta^{\pm}
. Then for
z
a topological generator of
H
,
\widehat{A}(V^{H}_{\alpha}\cap L,z)
is the differential form given on the right hand side of the equation in Theorem
2.8
for the spin Dirac complex
(E,d)
, so we have

L_{C}(z;E,d)\,\,=\,\,\left<\frac{ch_{{\mathbb{C}}}(i^{*}[\sigma(E,d)](z))}{ch_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\lambda_{-1}(N^{z}\otimes{{\mathbb{C}}})(z))}\,\operatorname{Td}(F^{z}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}),C\,|_{V^{z}}\right>\,\,=\,\,\sum_{\alpha}\left<\int_{F_{\alpha}}\widehat{A}(V^{H}_{\alpha}\cap L,z),C\,|\,_{V^{H}_{\alpha}}\right>.

Now consider the function
on the complex plane
given by

A(F,z)\,\,=\,\,\sum_{\alpha}\left<\int_{F_{\alpha}}\widehat{A}(V^{H}_{\alpha}\cap L,z),C\,|\,_{V^{H}_{\alpha}}\right>.

Since
\widehat{A}(V^{H}_{\alpha}\cap L,z)
is a differential form whose coefficients are rational functions having poles only at roots of unity, the same is true of
\displaystyle\int_{F_{\alpha}}\widehat{A}(V^{H}_{\alpha}\cap L,z)
, so also of
A(F,z)
. Because of the factor
(z^{d})^{1/2}
,
A(F,0)=0
, and
A(F,z)
has no pole at
z=\infty
.
If
z\in H
is not a root of unity, then it is a topological generator, and we have
L_{C}(z;E,d)=A(F,z)
. Now
L_{C}(z;E,d)
is defined for all
z\in H
, and by Proposition

4.2
it is a continuous function on
H={\mathbb{S}}^{1}
. Thus
A(F,z)
has no poles and so is analytic and bounded, and thus constant and hence zero. Therefore
L_{C}(z;E,d)=0
for all
z\in{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
. But
\displaystyle L_{C}(1;E,d)=\left<\int_{F}\widehat{A}(TF),C\right>
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

Note that any closed basic differential form yields a closed holonomy invariant current. So when the foliation is for instance Riemannian, the subring of
H^{*}(V,{\mathbb{C}})
generated by the Pontryagin classes of the normal bundle (or any basic bundle) yields interesting vanishing results.

An interesting corollary is an easy proof in the Riemannian case of the following well-known result for all spin foliations
[
LMZ01
]
. Notice that the proof given in
[
LMZ01
]
was completely different and based on the techniques of sub-Dirac operators.

Corollary 4.4
.

Assume that
F
is a Riemannian spin foliation of a compact connected oriented manifold
V
, and assume that the
\widehat{A}
-genus of
V
is non trivial, i.e.
\widehat{A}(V)[V]\not=0
. Then no compact connected Lie group can act non trivially on
V
by leaf-preserving diffeomorphisms.

Proof.

Since
V
is oriented and the leaves are spin, the transverse bundle
\nu
is also oriented. Since
F
is Riemannian, using the Levi-Civita connection on the normal bundle
\nu
(constructed out of the Bott partial connection), the Pontrjagin classes of the normal bundle
\nu
are represented in
H^{*}(V;{\mathbb{R}})
by basic closed forms. So, there is a basic closed form
\omega
with
[\omega]=\widehat{A}(\nu)
in
H^{*}(V;{\mathbb{R}})
. If a compact connected Lie group acts non trivially on
V
and preserves the leaves of the foliation
F
, then replacing this group by a two-fold cover Lie group we may assume that the action preserves the spin structure of the leaves. See Proposition 2.1 of
[
AH70
]
. By Theorem
4.1
we have,

0\;=\;\left<\int_{F}\widehat{A}(TF),C_{\omega}\right>\;=\;\left<\int_{F}\widehat{A}(TF),C_{\omega}\right>\;=\;\int_{V}\widehat{A}(TF)\widehat{A}(\nu)\;=\;\widehat{A}(V)[V]\not=0,

a contradiction. ∎

.

The pairing between

\tau_{C}
is given as usual by a foliated variation of Connes’ formula:

\tau_{C}(f_{0},\cdots,f_{2k})=\left<\int_{F}\operatorname{tr}\left([(\Psi^{E}(h)\circ f_{0})*\delta f_{1}*\cdots*\delta f_{2k}]|_{V}\right),C\right>.

Since
e
is smooth with compact support, it is clear that

(\Psi^{E}(h)\circ e)*\delta e*\cdots*\delta e,

is a uniformly bounded smoothing operator along the leaves of
{\mathcal{G}}
, with coefficents in differential forms on
V
.
Moreover, it is also transversely smooth in the sense of
[
BH11
]
[Definition 3.1]. Therefore, all (transverse) derivatives of
(\Psi^{E}(h)\circ e)*\delta e*\cdots*\delta e
yield uniformly bounded smoothing operators. This shows that the map which assigns to
h
the differential form on
V
given by

[(\Psi^{E}(h)\circ f_{0})*\delta f_{1}*\cdots*\delta f_{2k}]|_{V},

is continuous for the smooth (Fréchet) topology of the algebra of differential forms on
V
. Now, the closed holonomy invariant current
C
induces a continuous map
\displaystyle C\circ\int_{F}
for this Fréchet topology
[
H80
]
, so the proof is now complete.
∎

4.2.
Some rigidity Examples

We now give examples where Theorem
4.1
can be applied to conclude that there is no action, which are not already covered by the results of
[

]
.
A non-trivial example would be a foliation

on

, with the
\widehat{A}

being zero, (so that is not an obstruction to an action), and the
\widehat{A}
genus of each leaf is zero (so is not an obstruction to a leafwise action), but there is a closed Haefliger current

, and similarly for Corollary

Interesting examples already show up with foliations given by compact fiber bundles

,
\widehat{A}(F)[F]=0

\displaystyle\int_{F}\widehat{A}(TF)\neq 0
in

. By Poincare duality, there is an element

4.2.3.
Example 3

We now give examples using homogeneous spaces.
Recall that the complex projective space
{\mathbb{C}}P_{q}
is orientable for all
q
, and is spin if and only if
q
is odd.
In addition,

{\operatorname{H}^{*}({\mathbb{C}}P_{q};{\mathbb{Z}})=Z[\alpha]/(\alpha^{q+1}=0),\text{ where }\alpha\in\operatorname{H}^{2}({\mathbb{C}}P_{q};{\mathbb{Z}}).}

It is classical that the total Pontrjagin class of
{\mathbb{C}}P_{q}
is

p({\mathbb{C}}P_{q})\,\,=\,\,(1+\alpha^{2})^{q+1},\;\;\text{ so }\;\;\widehat{A}(T{\mathbb{C}}P_{q})\,\,=\,\,\Bigl(\frac{\alpha/2}{\sinh(\alpha/2)}\Bigr)^{q+1},

and
\displaystyle\widehat{A}({\mathbb{C}}P_{q})=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}P_{q}}\widehat{A}(T{\mathbb{C}}P_{q})
, the
\widehat{A}
-genus of
{\mathbb{C}}P_{q}
, is the coefficient of
\alpha^{q}
in this series. Thus, to get
\widehat{A}({\mathbb{C}}P_{q})
, we need to compute

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint\frac{1}{z^{q+1}}\Bigl(\frac{z/2}{\sinh(z/2)}\Bigr)^{q+1}\,dz\,\,=\,\,\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint\frac{1}{2^{q+1}}\frac{1}{(\sinh(z/2))^{q+1}}\,dz\,\,=\,\,2^{-q}\oint\frac{(2\pi i)^{-1}du}{u^{q+1}\sqrt{1+u^{2}}},

using
u=\sinh(z/2)
.
The integral gives the coefficient of
u^{q}
in the Taylor series of
1/\sqrt{1+u^{2}}
, which equals
q+2
times the coefficient of
u^{q+2}
in the Taylor series of
\sqrt{1+u^{2}}
. To see this note that the derivative of
\sqrt{1+u}
is
1/(2\sqrt{1+u})
, and then substitute
u^{2}
for
u
. We claim that this is non-zero if and only if
q
is even.

Given the claim, then
\widehat{A}({\mathbb{C}}P_{q})=\widehat{A}(U_{q+1}/(U_{1}\times U_{q})\neq 0
if and only if
q
is even. Thus we set
F={\mathbb{C}}P_{q+1}
, which is spin with
\widehat{A}
genus zero. Next, consider the universal
{\mathbb{C}}P_{q+1}
bundle,

F={\mathbb{C}}P_{q+1}\to EU_{2q+1}/(U_{1}\times U_{2q})\to BU_{2q+1}.

It satisfies
\displaystyle\int_{F}\widehat{A}(TF)
has at least one non-zero term in dimension greater than zero. So there is a compact manifold
N
and a principal bundle
F=U_{2q+1}\to V\to N
so that
\displaystyle\int_{F}\widehat{A}(TF)
has at least one non-zero term in dimension greater than zero. By Poincare duality, there is an element

so that
\displaystyle\left<\int_{F}\widehat{A}(TF),C_{\omega}\right>\neq 0
. If the
\widehat{A}
genus of

is not zero, we may take the product with
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
as above to kill it.

The proof of the claim is standard and proceeds for instance by induction.
We need to show that the Taylor series of
f=\sqrt{1+u^{2}}
is an even (which is obvious) series with non-zero coefficients for all even powers of

. Easy calculations get the induction started. So, write
f(u)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}u^{2n}
and note that
f^{2}=1+u^{2}
. Thus we have that for

2a_{n}+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}a_{j}a_{n-j}=0.

The induction hypothesis is that for

,
a_{j}=(-1)^{j+1}|a_{j}|\neq 0
. Then

2a_{n}\,\,=\,\,-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}a_{j}a_{n-j}\,\,=\,\,-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{j+1}(-1)^{n-j+1}|a_{j}|\,|a_{n-j}|\,\,=\,\,(-1)^{n+1}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}|a_{j}|\,|a_{n-j}|\neq 0.

Thus

for all

. Note that the proof also shows that the denominator of

is a power of

, as required by Theorem 25.4 of
[
BoH59
]

5.
Spectral triples and integrality

In this section we explain the construction of transverse spectral triples as proposed by Connes
[
C86
]
, and their index theory as developped by Connes and Moscovici in
[
CM95
]
, with the consequences for the higher Lefschetz formulae for foliations.

5.1.
Transverse spectral triples

We shall assume for simplicity that
F
is a Riemannian foliation, and so can employ the more precise results obtained by Kordyukov in
[
Ko97
,
Ko09
]
. For other interesting explicit results on Lefschetz formulae for foliations, see
[
Ko15
]
. We may and will assume that the metric on
V
is bundle like, so its restriction to the transverse bundle
\nu=TV/TF
is holonomy invariant.

We shall be using some elements of the basic cohomology
\operatorname{H}^{*}_{bas}(V,F)
.
In particular, let
{\widehat{E}}
be a basic Hermitian bundle on
V
, which means that there exists a connection
\nabla^{\widehat{E}}
on
{\widehat{E}}
which is locally projectable, so its curvature
\Omega^{\widehat{E}}
is basic. Examples of such a bundle are provided by bundles which are functorially constructed out of the transverse bundle
\nu=TV/TF
. Then all the characteristic classes of
\widehat{E}
can be represented by closed basic forms, i.e. the Chern classes
c_{j}({\widehat{E}})
in the complex case and the Pontrjagin classes
p_{j}({\widehat{E}})
in the real oriented case, all live in
H^{ev}_{bas}(V,F)
.
In fact,
\operatorname{H}^{*}_{bas}(V,F)
contains the ring generated by the Pontrjagin classes
p_{j}(\nu)
of
\nu
and the Chern classes
c_{j}({\widehat{E}})
of all basic Hermitian bundles.
We will be particularly interested in the Chern character

\operatorname{ch}(\widehat{E})=[\operatorname{tr}(\exp(\Omega^{\widehat{E}}/2\pi i))]
of the basic hermitian bundle
{\widehat{E}}
, as well as in the
\widehat{A}
genus
\widehat{A}(\nu)
, of the transverse bundle
\nu
. Recall that the latter is given as follows.
Denote by
R^{\nu}
the curvature of the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{\nu}
on
\nu
, which is constructed using the Bott connection and is locally the pull-back of the Levi-Civita connection on a transversal. Then
\widehat{A}(\nu)
is the basic cohomology class represented by the closed basic form
\det\left(\frac{R^{\nu}/4\pi}{\sinh(R^{\nu}/4\pi)}\right)^{1/2}
, i.e.

\widehat{A}(R^{\nu}):=\left[\det\left(\frac{R^{\nu}/4\pi}{\sinh(R^{\nu}/4\pi)}\right)^{1/2}\right].

We now recall the notion of a finite dimensional spectral triple, and the transverse spectral triples associated with Riemannian foliations as described and studied in
[
Ko97
]
. A (finite dimensional) spectral triple is composed of an (involutive) algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
which is represented in a separable Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
, so
\pi:{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{H}})
is an involutive morphism of algebras, and of a (“first order” unbounded) self-adjoint operator
D
which interacts properly with the representation. More precisely, it is assumed that:

•

D:dom(D)_{\subset{\mathcal{H}}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{H}}
is a (densely defined) self-adjoint operator on
{\mathcal{H}}
;

•

For any
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
, the operator
\pi(a)
preserves
dom(D)
and the commutator
[D,\pi(a)]
extends to a bounded operator on
{\mathcal{H}}
;

•

(finite dimensionality) There exists
\alpha\in[1,+\infty)
such that for any
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
, the operator
\pi(a)(D^{2}+I)^{-\alpha/2}
belongs to the Dixmier ideal
L^{\alpha,\infty}({\mathcal{H}})
.

The least real number
\alpha
satisfying the third item, denoted
d
, is called the dimension of the spectral triple. So for any complex number
z
such that
\Re(z)>d
, the operator
\pi(a)(D^{2}+I)^{-z/2}
is a trace class operator. In fact, there is a more accurate notion of dimension spectrum for spectral triples which was introduced in
[
CM95
]
. More precisely, we assume furthermore that there exists a discrete subset
\Gamma
of the complex plane, the dimension spectrum, whose projection to the reals is also discrete, and such that for any
a
in the algebra generated by
{\mathcal{A}}
and its commutators with
D
, the holomorphic function
z\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}\left(\pi(a)(D^{2}+I)^{-z/2}\right)
on
\{\Re(z)>d\}
extends to a meromorphic function on the complex plane whose poles are all in
\Gamma
. The dimension spectrum
\Gamma
is a simple dimension spectrum when all its elements are at most simple poles. This reflects roughly the usual notion for closed smooth manifolds with many connected components of different dimensions. In that case
\Gamma\subset\{n\in{\mathbb{N}},n\leq d\}
where
d
is the maximal dimension of the connected components, and it is a simple dimension spectrum. When the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
is moreover endowed with a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-grading, say an involution
\gamma
, which commutes with the representation
\pi
and anticommutes with
D
, such a spectral triple is called an even spectral triple. See again
[
C94
]
for more details on spectral triples and their associated Fredholm modules and index pairings.

The main example of a spectral triple we consider here is the transverse spectral triple associated with the Riemannian foliation
(V,F)
in
[
Ko97
]
. More precisely, take for
{\mathcal{A}}
the smooth convolution algebra
{\mathcal{A}}=C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathcal{G}})
associated with the holonomy groupoid
{\mathcal{G}}
. The convolution product is given by

(k_{1}k_{2})(\gamma):=\int_{{\mathcal{G}}^{r(\gamma)}}k_{1}(\alpha)k_{2}(\alpha^{-1}\gamma)\,d\eta^{r(\gamma)}(\alpha),\quad k_{1},k_{2}\in{\mathcal{A}}\text{ and }\gamma\in{\mathcal{G}},

where
d\eta=d\eta^{x}
is the
{\mathcal{G}}
-invariant Haar system on
s:{\mathcal{G}}\to V
, which is defined as the pull-back under the covering map
s
of the Lebesgue class measure on the leaves of
F
associated with the fixed metric
[
C79
]
. The involution is as usual defined as
k^{*}(\gamma):=\overline{k(\gamma^{-1})}
for
k\in{\mathcal{A}}
and
\gamma\in{\mathcal{G}}.

If
E
is any
{\mathcal{G}}
-equivariant (always assumed basic here) hermitian bundle over
V
(with basic connection
\nabla^{E}
), then the Hilbert space
L^{2}(V,E)
is endowed with the involutive average representation
\pi_{E}
given by

\pi_{E}(k)(\xi)(x):=\int_{{\mathcal{G}}^{x}}k(\gamma)h_{\gamma}(\xi(s(\gamma)))\,d\eta^{x}(\gamma),\quad\text{for }k\in{\mathcal{A}},\xi\in{\mathcal{H}}\text{ and }x\in V.

Here
h_{\gamma}:E_{s(\gamma)}\to E_{r(\gamma)}
is the holonomy action on
E
, corresponding to the element
\gamma\in{\mathcal{G}}
.

Then according to
[
Ko97
]
, any first order transversally elliptic self-adjoint (pseudo)differential operator acting on the smooth sections of
E

5.2.
The equivariant Connes bicomplex

We shall represent the equivariant Connes-Chern character of our spectral triple by the corresponding Connes-Moscovici residue cocycle in the
H
-equivariant cyclic bicomplex
(b_{H},B_{H})
. Recall that if
H
is any compact group which acts continuously on a locally convex (unital) algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
, with
h1=1
for any
h\in{\mathcal{A}}
, then the equivariant Hochschild complex
(C^{*}({\mathcal{A}},H),b_{H})
is defined as follows
[
BH10
]
. The space of cochains
C^{n}({\mathcal{A}},H)
is composed of the continuous functions

f:{\mathcal{A}}^{\otimes_{n+1}}\to C(H)
such that

f(ha^{0},\cdots,ha^{n})(hgh^{-1})=f(a^{0},\cdots,a^{n})(g),\quad\forall g,h\in H,\forall a^{j}\in{\mathcal{A}}.

We denote by
f(a^{0},\cdots,a^{n}|h)
the scalar
f(a^{0},\cdots,a^{n})(h)
for
f\in C^{n}({\mathcal{A}},H)
. The equivariant Hochschild differential
b^{H}:C^{n}({\mathcal{A}},H)\to C^{n+1}({\mathcal{A}},H)
is then defined for
f\in C^{n}({\mathcal{A}},H)
by

(b_{H}f)(a^{0},\cdots,a^{n+1}|h):=(b^{\prime}f)(a^{0},\cdots,a^{n+1}|h)+(-1)^{n+1}f(h^{-1}(a^{n+1})a^{0},a^{1},\cdots,a^{n}|h).

Here the operator
b^{\prime}
is the standard one given by

(b^{\prime}f)(a^{0},\cdots,a^{n+1}|h):=\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{j}f(a^{0},\cdots,a^{j}a^{j+1},\cdots,a^{n+1}|h).

The relations
b^{\prime 2}=0
and
(b_{H})^{2}=0
are then satisfied.

In order to define the equivariant cyclic complex as well as the equivariant cyclic bicomplex, we need to introduce an equivariant version of the Connes operator
B
. As in
[
BH10
]
, denote by
\lambda_{H}:C^{n}({\mathcal{A}},H)\to C^{n}({\mathcal{A}},H)
the equivariant cyclic permutation given by

\lambda_{H}(a^{0},\cdots,a^{n}|h):=(h^{-1}(a^{n}),a^{0},\cdots,a^{n-1}|h).

Exactly as in the non-equivariant case
[
C85
]
, one easily shows that
(\lambda_{H})^{n+1}=\operatorname{id}_{C^{n}({\mathcal{A}},H)}
. Hence, the equivariant cyclic antisymmétrisation operator
A_{H}:C^{n}({\mathcal{A}},H)\to C^{n}({\mathcal{A}},H)
can be defined by the usual formula

A_{H}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{nj}(\lambda_{H})^{j}.

Recall from
[
BH10
]
the relation
b_{H}\circ A_{H}=A_{H}\circ b^{\prime}
. Now the equivariant Connes operator, denoted here
B_{H}
, will be the operator
B_{H}:=A_{H}\circ B_{0}:C^{n}({\mathcal{A}},H)\to C^{n-1}({\mathcal{A}},H)
where

(B_{0}f)(a^{0},\cdots,a^{n-1}|h)=f(1,a^{0},\cdots,a^{n-1}|h)-(-1)^{n}f(a^{0},\cdots,a^{n-1},1|h).

Lemma 5.2
.

The following relations hold on
C^{*}({\mathcal{A}},H)
:

(1)

B_{0}\circ b_{H}+b^{\prime}\circ B_{0}=\operatorname{id}+(-1)^{n+1}\lambda_{H}
on
n
-cochains.

(2)

b_{H}\circ B_{H}+B_{H}\circ b_{H}=0
.

Proof.

The first item is a straightforward computation. If we compose this relation on the left with the operator
A_{H}
, then we get on
n
-cochains

B_{H}\circ b_{H}+A_{H}\circ b^{\prime}\circ B_{0}=A_{H}+(-1)^{n+1}A_{H}\circ\lambda_{H}.

But
A_{H}\circ b^{\prime}=b_{H}\circ A_{H}
and hence
A_{H}\circ b^{\prime}\circ B_{0}=b_{H}\circ B_{H}
. Moreover, on
C^{n}({\mathcal{A}},H)
, we have

A_{H}\circ\lambda_{H}=(-1)^{n}A_{H}\text{ therefore }(-1)^{n+1}A_{H}\circ\lambda_{H}=-A_{H}.

So, we finally get the second relation
B_{H}\circ b_{H}+b_{H}\circ B_{H}=0
on
C^{*}({\mathcal{A}},H)
.
∎

As in the non-equivariant case, the equivariant cyclic cohomology of the algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
, as defined in
[
BH10
]
using the equivariant cyclic subcomplex of the equivariant Hochschild complex, can be recovered from the second filtration of the bicomplex
(b_{H},B_{H})
. See
[
C94
]
for more precise details.

5.3.

Next we take into account the leafwise action of the group

Theorem 5.3
.

For

In addition, for

,
k

The representation

Proof.

We first point out that each

\displaystyle\phi_{0}(ha_{0}|hgh^{-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}\Bigl[z^{-1}\,\operatorname{Tr}\left(\gamma U(hgh^{-1})(ha_{0})(\operatorname{id}+(\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}^{\widehat{E}})^{2})^{-z}\right)\Bigr]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}\Bigl[z^{-1}\,\operatorname{Tr}\left(\gamma U(h)U(g)a_{0}U(h^{-1})(\operatorname{id}+(\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}^{\widehat{E}})^{2})^{-z}\right)\Bigr]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}\Bigl[z^{-1}\,\operatorname{Tr}\left(U(h)\gamma U(g)a_{0}(\operatorname{id}+(\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}^{\widehat{E}})^{2})^{-z}U(h^{-1})\right)\Bigr]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}\Bigl[z^{-1}\,\operatorname{Tr}\left(\gamma U(g)a_{0}(\operatorname{id}+(\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}^{\widehat{E}})^{2})^{-z})\right)\Bigr]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{0}(a_{0}|g).

In the same way and using again the

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}\left(\gamma\,U(hgh^{-1})\,(ha^{0})[\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}^{\widehat{E}},(ha^{1})]\cdots[\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}^{\widehat{E}},(ha^{k})]\,(\operatorname{id}+(\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}^{\widehat{E}})^{2})^{-k/2-z}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}\left(\gamma\,U(h)U(g)\,(a^{0}U(h^{-1})[\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}^{\widehat{E}},U(h)a^{1}U(h^{-1}))]\cdots[\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}^{\widehat{E}},U(h)a^{k}U(h^{-1}))]\,(\operatorname{id}+(\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}^{\widehat{E}})^{2})^{-k/2-z}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}\left(U(h)\gamma\,U(g)\,(a^{0}U(h^{-1})U(h)[\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}^{\widehat{E}},a^{1}]U(h^{-1}))\cdots U(h)[\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}^{\widehat{E}},a^{k}]U(h^{-1}))\,(\operatorname{id}+(\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}^{\widehat{E}})^{2})^{-k/2-z}\right)

Since the group

See
[

Remark 5.4

The main property of the cochains

Theorem

Remark 5.6

If for instance

Proof.

\tau(a_{0},\cdots,a_{q^{\prime}}|h)=C(q^{\prime})\operatorname{Tr}\left(\gamma U(h)a_{0}[F,a_{1}]\cdots[F,a_{q^{\prime}}]\right),

\langle[e],[\phi]\rangle(h)=\sum_{n=0}^{\operatorname{codim}(F)/2}\frac{(-1)^{n}(2n)!}{n!}(\phi_{2n}\sharp\operatorname{Tr})((\psi(h)\otimes\rho(h))(e-1/2),e,\cdots,e).

Since the operator
\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}^{\widehat{E}}
is not invertible in general (note also that the algebra
C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathcal{G}})
is rarely unital), we deform
H
-equivariantly the spectral triple as follows. Define

\widehat{{\mathcal{H}}}:={\mathcal{H}}\oplus{\mathcal{H}}\text{ with the new grading }\widehat{\gamma}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\gamma&0\\
0&-\gamma\end{array}\right)\text{ and new operator }{\widehat{\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}}}^{\widehat{E}}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}^{\widehat{E}}&Id\\
Id&-\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}^{\widehat{E}}\end{array}\right).

The action of
H
is simply extended to
\widehat{\mathcal{H}}
as the diagonal action, so
U(h)
is replaced by
{\widehat{U}}(h)
which is given by
\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}U(h)&0\\
0&U(h)\end{array}\right]
. Now with the new representation
\widehat{\pi}:k\mapsto\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\pi_{\widehat{E}}(k)&0\\
0&0\end{array}\right)
, it is easy to check that we get again an even
H
-equivariant spectral triple
({\mathcal{A}},\widehat{\mathcal{H}},{\widehat{\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}}}^{\widehat{E}})
with invertible operator. Moreover, a straightforward
H
-equivariant homotopy argument shows that the class, in the (entire)
H
-equivariant
(b^{H},B^{H})
cyclic bicomplex, of the residue cocycle
{\widehat{\phi}}
associated with
({\mathcal{A}},\widehat{\mathcal{H}},{\widehat{\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}}}^{\widehat{E}})
, say obtained as
\phi
is but replacing the representation
\pi_{\widehat{E}}
by
\widehat{\pi}
and the operator
{\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}}^{\widehat{E}}
by
{\widehat{\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}}}^{\widehat{E}}
, is unchanged if we consider the path of operators
{\widehat{\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}}}^{\widehat{E}}_{\epsilon}
for
\epsilon\in[0,1]
:

{\widehat{\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}}}^{\widehat{E}}_{\epsilon}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}^{\widehat{E}}&\epsilon Id\\
\epsilon Id&-\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}^{\widehat{E}}\end{array}\right).

Now for
\epsilon=0
we have

\operatorname{Tr}\left(\widehat{\gamma}\widehat{\psi}(h)\widehat{\pi}(a_{0})[\widehat{\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}}^{\widehat{E}}_{0},\widehat{\pi}(a_{1})]\cdots[\widehat{\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}}^{\widehat{E}}_{0},\widehat{\pi}(a_{q^{\prime}})](I+({\widehat{\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}}}^{\widehat{E}}_{0})^{2})^{-q^{\prime}/2-z}\right)\\
=\operatorname{Tr}\left(\gamma\Psi(h)a_{0}[\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}^{\widehat{E}},a_{1}]\cdots[\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}^{\widehat{E}},a^{q^{\prime}}](I+{\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}^{\widehat{E}}}^{2})^{-q^{\prime}/2-z}\right).

As a consequence we see that the pairing of the class of
\phi
with equivariant
K
-theory coincides with that of the class of the equivariant cocycle
\widehat{\phi}
. If we now consider as above the even
H
-equivariant Fredolm module
(\widehat{\mathcal{H}},\widehat{F})
where
\widehat{F}=\operatorname{sign}({\widehat{\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}}}^{\widehat{E}})
is the self-adjoint symmetry which is the sign of the invertble operator
{\widehat{\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}}}^{\widehat{E}}
, then for any even
q^{\prime}>q
, we get again an equivariant cyclic Hochschild cocycle by setting just as above

\tau(a_{0},\cdots,a_{q^{\prime}}|h):=C(q^{\prime})\operatorname{Tr}\left(\widehat{\gamma}{\widehat{U}}(h)\widehat{\pi}(a_{0})[{\widehat{F}},\widehat{\pi}(a_{1})]\cdots[{\widehat{F}},\widehat{\pi}(a_{q^{\prime}})]\right),

where
C(q^{\prime})
is some appropriate normalisation constant
[
C86
]
.
The proof is now complete.
∎

Remark 5.8

In the case of non-Riemannian foliations, in

\phi=(\phi_{k})_{k}
, see again

. However, the previous integrality theorem is still valid since again the pairing of the class of

with an equivariant

Corollary 5.9
.

The pairing, with
K^{H}(C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathcal{G}}))
, of the equivariant residue class
[\phi]
associated with the spectral triple
({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{H}},\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}^{\widehat{E}})
extends to a pairing with
K^{H}(C^{*}(V,F))
, and takes values in the representation ring
R(H)\subset C(H)
. So, for any topological generator
h
of
H
and any
x\in K^{H}(C^{*}(V,F))
, we have

<[\phi],x>\,\,\,\in\,\,\,R(H),\quad\text{ and hence }\quad<[\phi],x>(h)\,\,\,\in\,\,\,R(H)(h).

Proof.

A general method of domains of derivations of commutators with the operator
[
C86
]
allows one to define an
H
subalgebra
{\mathcal{B}}
of
C^{*}(V,F)
such that:

•

{\mathcal{B}}
contains
{\mathcal{A}}=C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathcal{G}})
;

•

{\mathcal{B}}
is closed under the holomorphic functional calculus;

•

the Connes-Moscovici residue cochains
\phi^{CM}_{k}
all extend to
{\mathcal{B}}
and define a
(b+B)
-closed cocycle on
{\mathcal{B}}
, given by the same formulae.

Therefore, the cochains
\phi^{CM}_{k}
all satisfy again the strong equivariance property of Remark
5.4
and allow us to define the equivariant
(b_{H}+B_{H})
-cocycle
\phi
on
{\mathcal{B}}
as above. The proof of Theorem
5.5
now shows that for any
H
-invariant idempotent
e
in
{\mathcal{B}}\otimes\operatorname{End}(X)
rather than in
{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\operatorname{End}(X)
, the pairing of
\phi
with the class of
e
in
K^{H}({\mathcal{B}})
belongs again to
R(H)
since it is given by an
H
-equivariant Fredholm index pairing. Since
{\mathcal{B}}
is closed under the holomorphic functional calculus and is an
H
-subalgebra, we know that the inclusion of
{\mathcal{B}}
induces an isomorphism
K^{H}({\mathcal{B}})\simeq K^{H}(C^{*}(V,F))
and every idempotent
p\in C^{*}(V,F)\otimes\operatorname{End}(X)
can be approximated by an idempotent
e\in{\mathcal{B}}\otimes\operatorname{End}(X)
which defines the same class in
K^{H}(C^{*}(V,F))
.
On the other hand, recall that the Connes
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}(V,F)
is a stable
H
-algebra
[
HS91
,
B97b
]
, and hence its (equivariant)
K
-theory can be represented by idempotents in
C^{*}(V,F)\otimes\operatorname{End}(X)
for finite dimensional representations of
H
in
X
. This shows that the pairing of
[\phi]
with
K^{H}({\mathcal{B}})
is completely determined by the pairings with
H
-invariant idempotents in
{\mathcal{B}}\otimes\operatorname{End}(X)
. The proof is thus complete.
∎

5.4.
Integrality in higher Lefschetz formulae

Before applying these results to the Lefschetz fixed point formula, we mention the following important theorem proven in
[
BH18
]
, see also
[
BH19
]
. Recall the HKR chain map constructed in
[
BH04
]
between the de Rham complex of holonomy invariant Haefliger currents and the cyclic complex of the algebra
C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathcal{G}})
. It induces:

\chi:H_{ev/odd}(V/F)\longrightarrow H_{\lambda}^{ev/odd}(C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathcal{G}})).

We are now in position to combine the previous results with the
K
-theory Lefschetz theorem of
[
B97a
]
. Recall that
H
is topologically generated by
h
and that it acts by isometries of
(V,g)
which are holonomy diffeomorphisms, and moreover, the fixed point submanifold
V^{h}=V^{H}
is assumed to be transverse to the foliation
F
, so it inherits a foliation
F^{h}
. Again when
H
is connected, all these conditions are automatically satisfied when
H
preserves the leaves. All the previous data can then be restricted to the
H
-trivial foliation
(V^{h},F^{h})
, and we have an
H
-equivariant spectral triple
({\mathcal{A}}^{h},{\mathcal{H}}^{h},\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}^{\widehat{E^{h}}})
constructed in exactly the same way, or by restricting
({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{H}},\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}^{\widehat{E}})
. More precisely,

•

{\mathcal{A}}^{h}
denotes the involutive convolution algebra associated with the holonomy groupoid of the foliation
(V^{h},F^{h})
.

•

{\widehat{E^{h}}}={\widehat{E}}|_{V^{h}}
and
{\mathcal{H}}^{h}
is the Hilbert space of
L^{2}
sections over
V^{h}
of the basic bundle
{\mathcal{S}}^{h}\otimes{\widehat{E^{h}}}
with
{\mathcal{S}}^{h}
being the spin bundle of the transverse bundle to the foliation
F^{h}
or the restriction of the ambiant spin bundle
{\mathcal{S}}
to
V^{h}
.

•

The operator
\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}^{\widehat{E^{h}}}
is the transverse Dirac operator on
(V^{h},F^{h})
with coefficients in
{\widehat{E^{h}}}
. Its principal symbol is the restriction of the principal symbol of
{\widehat{E}}
to
TV^{h}
.

The classical integrality theorems involving rational characteristic classes and proven using the Atiyah-Segal-Singer Lefschetz fixed point formulae
[
AB68
,
ASe68
,
AS68
,
HZ74
]
, can in principle be extended to smooth foliations of closed manifolds. The following theorem is only a first important step towards proving such integrality results, for two reasons. The first is that it is stated for Riemannian foliations where we could go further and deduce the integrality of the resulting rational combination of characteristic classes, in the spirit of the classical formulae. The second reason is that while this result can be stated for more general foliations using the Connes-Moscovici construction
[
CM95
]
, it is at present rather hard to deduce the integrality of corresponding characteristic numbers. These will certainly involve more exotic characteristic numbers and will be dealt with elsewhere.

Theorem 5.10
.

Denote by
\phi_{V^{h},F^{h}}^{CM}
the even Connes-Moscovici residue cocycle in the
(b,B)
-bicomplex associated with the fixed point foliation
(V^{h},F^{h})

[
CM95
]
. Denote by
\operatorname{Ind}^{CS}_{V^{h},F^{h}}:K(TF^{h})\rightarrow K(C^{*}(V^{h},F^{h}))
the Connes-Skandalis topological longitudinal index morphism for the foliation
(V^{h},F^{h})

[
CS84
]
. Then for any leafwise elliptic
H
-invariant pseudodifferential complex
(E,d)
over the ambiant foliation
(V,F)
, we have

\left\langle(\operatorname{Ind}^{CS}_{V^{h},F^{h}}\otimes{\mathbb{C}})\left(\frac{i^{*}[\sigma(E,d)](h)}{\lambda_{-1}(N^{h}\otimes{\mathbb{C}})(h)}\right)\,,\,[\phi_{V^{h},F^{h}}^{CM}]\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{C}}\right\rangle\;\in\;R(H)(h).

Some explanations are needed here. Note that the class
\frac{i^{*}[\sigma(E,d)]}{\lambda_{-1}(N^{h}\otimes{\mathbb{C}})}
belongs to the localized
R(H)_{h}
-module
K^{H}(TF^{h})_{h}\simeq K(TF^{h})\otimes R(H)_{h}
since
H
acts trivially on
TF^{h}
, hence evaluating at
h
we get an element of
K(TF^{h})\otimes{\mathbb{C}}
, and so

(\operatorname{Ind}^{CS}_{V^{h},F^{h}}\otimes{\mathbb{C}})\left(\frac{i^{*}[\sigma(E,d)](h)}{\lambda_{-1}(N^{h}\otimes{\mathbb{C}})(h)}\right)\;\in\;K(C^{*}(V^{h},F^{h}))\otimes{\mathbb{C}}.

Proof.

By the
K
-theory Lefschetz fixed point theorem
[
B97a
]
, the Lefschetz class
L(h;E,d)
of
h
with respect to
(E,d)
coincides with the image of the class

(\operatorname{Ind}^{CS}_{V^{h},F^{h}}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{R(H)_{h}})\left(\frac{i^{*}[\sigma(E,d)]}{\lambda_{-1}(N^{h}\otimes{\mathbb{C}})}\right)

under
{\mathcal{M}}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{R(H)_{h}}:K(C^{*}(V^{h},F^{h}))\otimes R(H)_{h}\rightarrow K(C^{*}(V,F))\otimes R(H)_{h}\simeq K^{H}(C^{*}(V,F))_{h}
. Here
{\mathcal{M}}
denotes the Morita extension morphism associated with the transverse submanifold
V^{h}
to the foliation
(V,F)
, see
[
B97a
]
. The important feature of the Connes-Moscovici residue cocycle in this case of a Riemannian foliation, is that it “commutes with Morita extension”. More precisely, we have

\langle{\mathcal{M}}(y),[\phi^{CM}]\rangle=\langle y,[\phi_{V^{h},F^{h}}^{CM}]\rangle,\quad\forall y\in K(C^{*}(V^{h},F^{h})).

This can be proven directly from the explicit formula for the cochains
\phi_{k}
and for the map
{\mathcal{M}}
, by adapting the proof given in
[
BH10
]
for holonomy invariant currents. Therefore, we deduce from the
K
-theory Lefschetz theorem that

\left\langle(\operatorname{Ind}^{CS}_{V^{h},F^{h}}\otimes R(H)_{h})\left(\frac{i^{*}[\sigma(E,d)]}{\lambda_{-1}(N^{h}\otimes{\mathbb{C}})}\right)\,,\,[\phi_{V^{h},F^{h}}^{CM}]\otimes R(H)_{h}\right\rangle=\langle L(h;E,d),[\phi^{CM}]\otimes R(H)_{h}\rangle.

But

\langle L(h;E,d),[\phi^{CM}]\otimes R(H)_{h}\rangle=\langle\operatorname{Ind}^{H}([\sigma(E,d)]),[\phi^{CM}]\otimes R(H)\rangle.

So

\langle L(h;E,d),[\phi^{CM}]\otimes R(H)_{h}\rangle(h)=\langle\operatorname{Ind}^{H}([\sigma(E,d)]),[\phi^{CM}]\otimes R(H)\rangle(h)\;\in\;R(H)(h).

Hence we end up with,

\left\langle(\operatorname{Ind}^{CS}_{V^{h},F^{h}}\otimes{\mathbb{C}})\left(\frac{i^{*}[\sigma(E,d)](h)}{\lambda_{-1}(N^{h}\otimes{\mathbb{C}})(h)}\right)\,,\,[\phi_{V^{h},F^{h}}^{CM}]\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{C}}\right\rangle=

\left\langle(\operatorname{Ind}^{CS}_{V^{h},F^{h}}\otimes R(H)_{h})\left(\frac{i^{*}[\sigma(E,d)]}{\lambda_{-1}(N^{h}\otimes{\mathbb{C}})}\right)\,,\,[\phi_{V^{h},F^{h}}^{CM}]\otimes R(H)_{h}\right\rangle\;(h)\;\in\;R(H)(h).

∎

In this proof, we used the explicit formula for the Connes-Moscovici residue cocycle in this simpler case of Riemannian foliations to easily deduce the natural commutation with the Morita extension map
{\mathcal{M}}
. Applying the results of
[
BH18
,
BH19
]
, that is the techniques of the Getzler rescaling argument on foliations as developped there, this commutation property can also be deduced from the Morita compatibility property proven in
[
BH10
]
for cyclic cocycles associated with closed holonomy invariant currents. More precisely, denote by
[C]
the Haefliger homology class associated with the basic cohomology class
\widehat{A}(\nu)\operatorname{ch}({\widehat{E}})
and by
[C|_{V^{h}}]
its restriction to
(V^{h},F^{h})
or the Haefliger class in
H_{ev}(V^{h}/F^{h})
associated with the basic cohomology class
\widehat{A}(\nu^{h})\operatorname{ch}({\widehat{E}}\,|\,_{V^{h}})
. Then the Connes-Moscovici cyclic cohomology class
[\phi_{V^{h},F^{h}}^{CM}]
belongs to the range under the HKR map
H_{*}(V^{h}/F^{h})\to H^{*}_{\lambda}({\mathcal{A}}^{h})
for the foliation
(V^{h},F^{h})
. Indeed, it is exactly the image of the Haefliger homology class associated with the basic cohomology class
\widehat{A}(\nu^{h})\operatorname{ch}({\widehat{E}}\,|\,_{V^{h}})
. Thus we have the following topological description of the Connes-Moscovici residue cocycle which was proven in
[
BH18
,
BH19
]
.

Theorem 5.11
.

[
BH18
,
BH19
]
For the Riemannian foliation
(V,F)
, the class
[\phi^{CM}]\in HC^{ev}(C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathcal{G}}))
of the Connes-Moscovici residue cocycle associated with the spectral triple
({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{H}},\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}^{\widehat{E}})
belongs to the range of the HKR map
\chi:H_{*}(V/F)\to H_{\lambda}^{*}(C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathcal{G}}))
. It coincides with the image under
\chi
of the Haefliger homology class associated with the basic cohomology class
\widehat{A}(\nu)\operatorname{ch}({\widehat{E}})
.

The proof relies on a long and tedious rescaling argument à la Getzler for transversely elliptic operators, see
[
BH18
,
BH19
]
. As a corollary, we get the following expected topological integrality result which opens up the way to more involved results when using the Connes-Moscovici residue cocycle for triangular structures and hence for general non-Riemannian foliations. Recall that
H
is topologically generated by
h
and that the fixed point submanifold
V^{h}=V^{H}
is transverse to the foliation.

Corollary 5.12
.

The characteristic number

\int_{V^{h}}\dfrac{\operatorname{ch}_{\mathbb{C}}(i^{*}[\sigma(E,d)](h))}{\operatorname{ch}_{\mathbb{C}}(\lambda_{-1}(N^{h}\otimes{\mathbb{C}})(h))}\operatorname{Td}(TF^{h}\otimes{\mathbb{C}})\widehat{A}(\nu^{h})\operatorname{ch}({\widehat{E}}\,|\,_{V^{h}})\text{ belongs to $R(H)(h)$}.

Proof.

By the higher Lefschetz formula of
[
BH10
]
, we have

\int_{V^{h}}\dfrac{\operatorname{ch}_{\mathbb{C}}(i^{*}[\sigma(E,d)](h))}{\operatorname{ch}_{\mathbb{C}}(\lambda_{-1}(N^{h}\otimes{\mathbb{C}})(h))}\operatorname{Td}(TF^{h}\otimes{\mathbb{C}})\widehat{A}(\nu^{h})\operatorname{ch}({\widehat{E}}\,|\,_{V^{h}})=\left\langle\left[\tau_{C_{\alpha}}\right],\operatorname{Ind}^{H}(E,d)\right\rangle(h),

with
\alpha
being the basic class
\widehat{A}(R^{\nu})\operatorname{ch}(\Omega^{\widehat{E}})
,
C_{\alpha}
its corresponding Haefliger homology class, and
\left[\tau_{C_{\alpha}}\right]
the associated
H
-equivariant cyclic cohomology class over
C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathcal{G}})
as defined in
[
BH10
]
. By Theorem
5.11
the cyclic cohomology class of
\tau_{C_{\alpha}}
coincides with the equivariant Connes-Chern character of
({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{H}},\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}^{\widehat{E}})
.
By Proposition
5.9
, the pairing of the equivariant Connes-Chern character of
({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{H}},\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}^{\widehat{E}})
with the equivariant
K
-theory takes values in
R(H)
. Hence, we deduce

\left\langle\left[\tau_{C_{\alpha}}\right],\operatorname{Ind}^{H}(E,d)\right\rangle\;\in\;R(H),

and so the conclusion.
∎

As explained above, Theorem
5.10
is expected to still hold for non-Riemannian foliations but using the more involved expression of the residue cocycle given in
[
CM95
]
. However, the characteristic expression of Corollary
5.12
is specific to Riemannian foliations. As also explained above, one expects similar integrality of characteristic numbers for non-Riemannian foliations, but involving more exotic characteristic classes associated with actions of the Connes-Moscovici Hopf algebra of vector fields.

We end this section by giving the heuristic explanation of the integrality result obatined in Corollary
5.12
in the case where
V^{h}
is a strict transversal to
F
, say with dimension equal to
\operatorname{codim}(F)
. The characteristic number of Corollary
5.12
then reduces to

\int_{V^{h}}\dfrac{\operatorname{ch}_{\mathbb{C}}(i^{*}[\sigma(E,d)](h))}{\operatorname{ch}_{\mathbb{C}}(\lambda_{-1}(TF\,|\,_{V^{h}}\otimes{\mathbb{C}})(h))}\widehat{A}(TV^{h})\operatorname{ch}({\widehat{E}}\,|\,_{V^{h}}),

since, in this case,
N^{h}=TF\,|\,_{V^{h}}
,
\nu^{h}=TV^{h}
,
\operatorname{Td}(T{\mathcal{F}}^{h}\otimes{\mathbb{C}})=1
and
\widehat{A}(\nu^{h})=\widehat{A}(TV^{h})
.
The
H
-invariant leafwise elliptic complex
(E,d)
together with the transverse Dirac operator
\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}^{\widehat{E}}
allow one to define an
H
-invariant sharp product elliptic complex
(E,d)\sharp\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}_{V}^{\widehat{E}}
over the ambiant manifold
V
, which can be achieved for instance at the level of symbols. Thanks to the Atiyah-Segal Lefschetz formula
[
AS68
]
, the above characteristic number is then equal to the Atiyah-Segal Lefschetz number of
h
with respect to
(E,d)\sharp\operatorname{\not{\hskip-2.27626pt}\partial}_{V}^{\widehat{E}}
, and hence belongs to
R(H)(h)
.

A distance formula for tuples of operators
Priyanka Grover, Sushil Singla

Abstract
. For a tuple of operators 𝑨 = ( A 1 , … , A d ) \boldsymbol{A}=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{d}) , dist ( 𝑨 , ℂ d ​ 𝑰 ) \mathop{{\rm dist}}(\boldsymbol{A},\mathbb{C}^{d}\boldsymbol{I}) is defined as min 𝒛 ∈ ℂ d ⁡ ‖ 𝑨 − 𝒛 ​ 𝑰 ‖ \min\limits_{\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}}\|\boldsymbol{A-zI}\| and var x ( 𝑨 ) \mathop{{\rm var}}\limits_{x}(\boldsymbol{A}) as ‖ 𝑨 ​ x ‖ 2 − ∑ j = 1 d | ⟨ x | A j ​ x ⟩ | 2 . \|\boldsymbol{A}x\|^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{d}{\big|}\langle x|A_{j}x\rangle{\big|}^{2}. For a tuple 𝑨 \boldsymbol{A} of commuting normal operators, it is known that dist ( 𝑨 , ℂ d ​ 𝑰 ) 2 = sup ‖ x ‖ = 1 var x ( 𝑨 ) . \mathop{{\rm dist}}(\boldsymbol{A},\mathbb{C}^{d}\boldsymbol{I})^{2}=\sup_{\|x\|=1}\mathop{{\rm var}}_{x}(\boldsymbol{A}). We give an expression for the maximal joint numerical range of a tuple of doubly commuting matrices. Consequently, we obtain that the above distance formula holds for tuples of doubly commuting matrices. We also discuss some general conditions on the tuples of operators for this formula to hold. As a result, we obtain that it holds for tuples of Toeplitz operators as well.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathscr{H}
be a Hilbert space. Let
\mathscr{B}(\mathscr{H})
be the space of bounded linear operators on
\mathscr{H}
. Björck and Thomée
[
8
]
showed that for a bounded normal operator
A
on
\mathscr{H}
,

\sup_{\|x\|=1}\left(\|Ax\|^{2}-{\big|}\langle x|Ax\rangle{\big|}^{2}\right)=R_{A}^{2},

where
R_{A}
is the radius of the smallest disc containing the spectrum of
A
. The quantity
\|Ax\|^{2}-{\big|}\langle x|Ax\rangle{\big|}^{2}
is the
variance
of
A
with respect to
x
, denoted by
\mathop{{\rm var}}\limits_{x}(A)
(see
[
4
]
). Later,
Garske
[
14
]
proved that a one side inequality is true for any operator
A
on
\mathscr{H}
:

(1)

\sup_{\|x\|=1}\mathop{{\rm var}}\limits_{x}(A)\geq R_{A}^{2}.

Let
\mathscr{H}^{d}
be the direct sum of
d
copies of
\mathscr{H}
. For
A_{1},\ldots,A_{d}\in\mathscr{H}
, let
\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}
denote the tuple
(A_{1},\ldots,A_{d}):\mathscr{H}\rightarrow\mathscr{H}^{d}
defined as
\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}x=(A_{1}x,\ldots A_{d}x)
. Then

\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}\|=\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{d}A_{j}^{*}A_{j}\right\|^{1/2}.

We define the variance of the tuple
\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}
with respect to
x\in\mathscr{H}
as
\mathop{{\rm var}}\limits_{x}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}})=\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}x\|^{2}-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}{\big|}\langle x|A_{j}x\rangle{\big|}^{2}
. Ming
[
28
]
showed that if
\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}
is a tuple of commuting operators on
\mathscr{H}
, then

(2)

\sup_{\|x\|=1}\mathop{{\rm var}}\limits_{x}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}})\geq R_{\boldsymbol{A}}^{2},

where
R_{\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}}
is the radius of the smallest disc containing the Taylor spectrum of
\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}
. Moreover, if each
A_{j}
is normal, (
2
) becomes an equality.
(See also
[
7
]
.)

Let
\mathop{{\boldsymbol{I}}}
be the tuple of identity operators
(I,\dots,I)
. For
\mathop{{\boldsymbol{z}}}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{d})\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
,
\mathop{{\boldsymbol{z}}}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{I}}}
denotes the tuple
(z_{1}I,\ldots,z_{d}I)
. Let
{\mathop{{\rm dist}}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}},\mathbb{C}^{d}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{I}}})=\min\limits_{\mathop{{\boldsymbol{z}}}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}}\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}-\mathop{{\boldsymbol{z}}}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{I}}}\|}.

For commuting normal operators,
R_{\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}}=\mathop{{\rm dist}}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}},\mathbb{C}^{d}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{I}}}),
and thus we have

(3)

\sup_{\|x\|=1}\mathop{{\rm var}}_{x}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}})=\mathop{{\rm dist}}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}},\mathbb{C}^{d}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{I}}})^{2}.

We note that for any tuple of operators
\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}
, we have

(4)

\sup_{\|x\|=1}\mathop{{\rm var}}_{x}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}})\leq\mathop{{\rm dist}}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}},\mathbb{C}^{d}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{I}}})^{2}.

To see this, let
x
be a unit vector in
\mathscr{H}
. Then for
\mathop{{\boldsymbol{z}}}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
and
1\leq j\leq d
, we have

\displaystyle\|A_{j}x\|^{2}-\big|\langle x|A_{j}x\rangle\big|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|(A_{j}-z_{j}I)x\|^{2}-\big|\langle x|(A_{j}-z_{j}I)x\rangle\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|(A_{j}-z_{j}I)x\|^{2}.

Thus for all
\mathop{{\boldsymbol{z}}}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
,

\sum_{j=1}^{d}\big(\|A_{j}x\|^{2}-\big|\langle x|A_{j}x\rangle\big|^{2}\big)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{d}\|(A_{j}-z_{j}I)x\|^{2}\leq\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}-\mathop{{\boldsymbol{z}}}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{I}}}\|^{2}.

So
\mathop{{\rm var}}\limits_{x}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}})\leq\mathop{{\rm dist}}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}},\mathbb{C}^{d}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{I}}})^{2}
. Since
x
was an arbitrary unit vector in
\mathscr{H}
, we get (
4
).

A
doubly commuting tuple
of operators is one which satisfies
A_{i}A_{j}=A_{j}A_{i}
and
A_{i}^{*}A_{j}=A_{j}A_{i}^{*}
for
1\leq i,j\leq d,i\neq j
. In particular, a tuple of commuting normal operators is doubly commuting. We show that for a finite dimensional Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}
, (
3
) holds for a doubly commuting tuple of operators.

We also give some equivalent conditions for any tuple of operators on any Hilbert space to satisfy (
3
). For a tuple of operators
\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}
on a Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}
, the
joint maximal numerical range
of
\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}
is defined as

\displaystyle W_{0}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}})=

\displaystyle\{(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{d})\in\mathbb{C}^{d}:\langle x^{(n)}|A_{j}x^{(n)}\rangle\rightarrow\lambda_{j}\text{ for all }1\leq j\leq d,

\displaystyle\text{where }\|x^{(n)}\|=1\text{ and }\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}x^{(n)}\|\rightarrow\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}\|\}.

We shall denote by
\boldsymbol{z^{0}}=(z_{1}^{0},\dots,z_{d}^{0})\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
, the unique element for which
\mathop{{\rm dist}}(\boldsymbol{A},\mathbb{C}^{d}\boldsymbol{I})=\|\boldsymbol{A-z^{0}I}\|
. Let
\boldsymbol{A^{0}}
denote
\boldsymbol{A-z^{0}I}
and for each
1\leq j\leq d
, let
A_{j}^{0}=A_{j}-z_{j}^{0}I
. It is shown that the convexity of
W_{0}(\boldsymbol{A^{0}})
is sufficient for
\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}
to satisfy (
3
). As a corollary, we obtain that (
3
) holds for tuples of Toeplitz operators as well. We give an example to show that the convexity of
W_{0}(\boldsymbol{A^{0}})
is not necessary for
\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}
to satisfy (
3
). Convexity of the
joint numerical range
and the joint maximal numerical range has been a subject of interest for many authors (see
[
2
,
3
,
5
,
13
,
19
,
25
,
26
,
29
,
33
]
).

In Section
2
, we provide results for doubly commuting matrices. In Section
3
, we give some conditions for (
3
) to hold for general tuples of operators and obtain results for Toeplitz operators. In Section
4
, we give some remarks.

2.
Doubly commuting matrices

For
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, let
x_{1}\bar{\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}}x_{2}
denote the rank one operator on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
defined as
(x_{1}\bar{\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}}x_{2})(y)=\langle x_{2}|y\rangle x_{1}
for all
y\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Let
\boldsymbol{0}
be the tuple
(0,\dots,0)\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
.

Theorem 1
.

For any tuple of matrices
\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}
,

\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}-\mathop{{\boldsymbol{z}}}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{I}}}\|\geq\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}\|\text{ for all }\mathop{{\boldsymbol{z}}}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}

if and only if

\boldsymbol{0}\in\mathop{{\rm conv}}\left\{\left(\langle x|A_{1}x\rangle,\dots,\langle x|A_{d}x\rangle\right):\|x\|=1,\textstyle\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}^{*}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}x=\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}\|^{2}x\right\}.

Proof.

Using Theorem 8.4 of
[
35
]
,
\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}-\mathop{{\boldsymbol{z}}}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{I}}}\|\geq\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}\|
for all
\mathop{{\boldsymbol{z}}}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
if and only if there exists a positive semidefinite matrix
T
with
{\mathrm{tr}}(T)=1
such that
\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}^{*}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}T=\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}\|^{2}T
and
{\mathrm{tr}}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}^{*}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{z}}}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{I}}}T)=0
for all
\mathop{{\boldsymbol{z}}}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
. Using the spectral theorem for
T
, there are positive numbers
s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{\ell}
such that
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\ell}s_{i}=1
and orthonormal vectors
x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell}
such that
T=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\ell}s_{i}x_{i}\overline{\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}}x_{i}
. We note that
\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}^{*}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}T=\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}\|^{2}T
is equivalent to
\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}^{*}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}x_{i}=\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}\|^{2}x_{i}
for each
1\leq i\leq\ell
. To see this, we observe that

\displaystyle\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}\|^{2}

\displaystyle={\mathrm{tr}}(\boldsymbol{A}^{*}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}T)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\ell}s_{i}\langle x_{i}|\boldsymbol{A}^{*}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}x_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\|\boldsymbol{A}^{*}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}\|.

So

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\ell}s_{i}\langle x_{i}|\boldsymbol{A}^{*}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}x_{i}\rangle=\|\boldsymbol{A}^{*}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}\|=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\ell}s_{i}\|\boldsymbol{A}^{*}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}\|.

By the condition of equality in the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we get
\boldsymbol{A}^{*}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}x_{i}=\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}\|^{2}x_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq\ell
.
We also have that
{\mathrm{tr}}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}^{*}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{z}}}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{I}}}T)=0
for all
\mathop{{\boldsymbol{z}}}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
is equivalent to
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\ell}s_{i}\langle x_{i}|A_{j}x_{i}\rangle=0
for all
1\leq j\leq d
. This gives the required result.
∎

Let
W(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}})=\{(\langle x|A_{1}x\rangle,\langle x|A_{2}x\rangle,\ldots,\langle x|A_{d}x\rangle):\|x\|=1\}
denote the joint numerical range of
\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}
.
Let

\mathcal{V}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}})=\{\left(\langle x|A_{1}x\rangle,\langle x|A_{2}x\rangle,\ldots,\langle x|A_{d}x\rangle\right):\|x\|=1,\boldsymbol{A}^{*}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}x=\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}\|^{2}x\}.

Note that
\mathcal{V}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}})=W(P\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}P)
, where
P
is the orthogonal projection of
\mathscr{H}
onto the subspace
\{x:\textstyle\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}^{*}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}x=\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}\|^{2}x\}
. As a consequence of Theorem
1
, we have

Corollary 2
.

Let
\boldsymbol{A}
be a tuple of matrices such that
\mathcal{V}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}})
is a convex set. Then we have

\|\boldsymbol{A}-\boldsymbol{z}\boldsymbol{I}\|\geq\|\boldsymbol{A}\|\text{ for all }\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}

if and only if there exists a unit vector
x\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
\|\boldsymbol{A}x\|=\|\boldsymbol{A}\|
and
\langle x|A_{j}x\rangle=0\text{ for all }1\leq j\leq d
.

For a tuple of matrices,
\mathcal{V}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}})=W_{0}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}})
. It was proved in
[
11
, Theorem 1]
that if
\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}
is a tuple of operators such that
W(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}})
is convex, then so is
W_{0}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}})
. Bolotnikov and Rodman
[
9
, Theorem 3.1]
showed that if
\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}
is a tuple of doubly commuting matrices, then
W(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}})
is convex.
Thus we have the following lemma.

Lemma 3
.

For a doubly commuting tuple of matrices
\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}
,
\mathcal{V}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}})
is convex.

In Theorem 1.1 of
[
9
]
, the authors gave a complete description of a doubly commuting tuple
\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}
of matrices. They showed that there is a unitary matrix
U
, positive integers
m_{1},\ldots,m_{\ell}
,
m_{k}\times m_{k}
matrices
A_{1,k},\ldots,A_{d,k}
for each
1\leq k\leq\ell
such that
n=m_{1}+m_{2}+\dots+m_{\ell}
and for
j=1,\ldots,d
,

(5)

UA_{j}U^{*}=\begin{bmatrix}A_{j,1}&0&\ldots&0\\
0&A_{j,2}&\ddots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\ldots&0&A_{j,\ell}\end{bmatrix}.

Moreover, for
1\leq k\leq\ell
,
m_{k}=p_{1,k}p_{2,k}\dots p_{d,k}
and

\displaystyle A_{1,k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X_{1,k}\otimes I_{p_{2,k}}\otimes\dots\otimes I_{p_{d,k}},

\displaystyle A_{2,k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{p_{1,k}}\otimes X_{2,k}\otimes I_{p_{3,k}}\otimes\dots\otimes I_{p_{d,k}},

\displaystyle A_{d,k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{p_{1,k}}\otimes I_{p_{2,k}}\otimes\dots\otimes I_{p_{d-1,k}}\otimes X_{d,k},

where for
1\leq j\leq d
,
X_{j,k}
are
p_{j,k}\times p_{j,k}
matrices and
I_{p_{j,k}}
denote the
p_{j,k}\times p_{j,k}
identity matrices. Using this, they proved that for doubly commuting tuples of matrices
\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}
,
W(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}})
is convex. Modifying their proof along with
[
13
, Proposition 4]
(which is also the main ingredient in
[
9
, Theorem 3.1]
), we show that

(6)

\mathcal{V}(\boldsymbol{A})=\mathop{{\rm conv}}\left\{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}\mathcal{V}(X_{j,k}):1\leq k\leq\ell,\sum_{j=1}^{d}\|X_{j,k}\|^{2}=\|\boldsymbol{A}\|^{2}\right\}.

The convexity of
\mathcal{V}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}})
(Lemma
3
) follows as a consequence. We prove (
6
) in two steps given in the subsequent lemmas.

Lemma 4
.

Let

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X_{1}\otimes I_{p_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes I_{p_{d}},

\displaystyle A_{d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{p_{1}}\otimes I_{p_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes X_{d},

where each
X_{j}
is a
p_{j}\times p_{j}
matrix and
I_{p_{j}}
is
p_{j}\times p_{j}
identity matrix. Then

\mathcal{V}(A_{1},\dots,A_{d})=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}\mathcal{V}(X_{j})
.

Proof.

We have

\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}A_{j}^{*}A_{j}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}I_{p_{1}}\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}\dots\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}I_{p_{j-1}}\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}(X_{p_{j}}^{*}X_{j})\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}I_{p_{j+1}}\dots\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}I_{p_{d}}.

So the eigenvalues of
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}A_{j}^{*}A_{j}
are precisely the sums of the eigenvalues of
X_{j}^{*}X_{j}
over
j
. This gives

(7)

\left\|\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}A_{j}^{*}A_{j}\right\|=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}\left\|X_{j}^{*}X_{j}\right\|.

Let
\left(\langle x_{1}|X_{1}x_{1}\rangle,\ldots,\langle x_{d}|X_{d}x_{d}\rangle\right)\in\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}\mathcal{V}(X_{j})
. So

X_{j}^{*}X_{j}x_{j}=\|X_{j}\|^{2}x_{j}\text{ for all }1\leq j\leq d.

Thus

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}A_{j}^{*}A_{j}\ (x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{d})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}\|X_{j}\|^{2}(x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{d})

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}A_{j}^{*}A_{j}\right\|(x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{d}).

Since

\langle x_{j}|X_{j}x_{j}\rangle=\langle x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{d}|A_{j}(x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{d})\rangle\text{ for all }1\leq j\leq d,

we obtain
\left(\langle x_{1}|X_{1}x_{1}\rangle,\ldots,\langle x_{d}|X_{d}x_{d}\rangle\right)\in\mathcal{V}(A_{1},\dots,A_{d}).

Now let
\left(\langle x|A_{1}x\rangle,\ldots,\langle x|A_{d}x\rangle\right)\in\mathcal{V}(A_{1},\dots,A_{d})
, where
\|x\|=1
and

\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}A_{j}^{*}A_{j}\right)x=\left\|\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}A_{j}^{*}A_{j}\right\|x=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}\left\|X_{j}^{*}X_{j}\right\|x.

For
1\leq j\leq d
, let
\{e_{q}^{(j)}:q=1,\ldots,p_{j}\}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{C}^{p_{j}}
.
Fix
i\in\{1,\dots,d\}
and let

S_{i}=\big\{(q_{1},\ldots,q_{i-1},q_{i+1},\ldots,q_{d}):1\leq q_{j}\leq p_{j}\text{ for all }j\in\{1,\dots,d\}\setminus\{i\}\big\}.

Then we have

x=\sum\limits_{(q_{1},\dots,q_{i-1},q_{i+1},\dots,q_{d})\in S_{i}}e_{q_{1}}^{(1)}\otimes\dots\otimes e_{q_{i-1}}^{(i-1)}\otimes f_{(q_{1},\dots,q_{i-1},q_{i+1},\dots,q_{d})}\otimes e_{q_{i+1}}^{(i+1)}\otimes\dots\otimes e_{q_{d}}^{(d)},

where
f_{(q_{1},\dots,q_{i-1},q_{i+1},\dots,q_{d})}\in\mathbb{C}^{p_{i}}
and

1=\|x\|^{2}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in S_{i}\end{subarray}}\|f_{\alpha}\|^{2}.

The condition
\left\|\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}A_{j}^{*}A_{j}\right)x\right\|=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}\|X_{j}^{*}X_{j}\|
gives

X_{i}^{*}X_{i}f_{\alpha}=\|X_{i}^{*}X_{i}\|f_{\alpha}\text{ for all }f_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{C}^{p_{i}}\setminus\{0\}.

Since

\displaystyle\langle x|A_{i}x\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in S_{i}\end{subarray}}\langle f_{\alpha}|X_{i}f_{\alpha}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in S_{i},\\
f_{\alpha}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\|f_{\alpha}\|^{2}\left\langle\dfrac{f_{\alpha}}{\|f_{\alpha}\|}\bigg|X_{i}\dfrac{f_{\alpha}}{\|f_{\alpha}\|}\right\rangle,

it lies in
\mathcal{V}(X_{i})
. Hence

\left(\langle x|A_{1}x\rangle,\ldots,\langle x|A_{d}x\rangle\right)\in\prod_{i=1}^{d}\mathcal{V}(X_{i}).

This gives
\mathcal{V}({A}_{1},\ldots,A_{d})=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{d}\mathcal{V}(X_{i})
.

∎

Lemma 5
.

For a doubly commuting tuple of matrices
\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}
,

\mathcal{V}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}})=\mathop{{\rm conv}}\left\{\mathcal{V}(A_{1,k},\dots,A_{d,k}):1\leq k\leq\ell,\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{d}A_{j,k}^{*}A_{j,k}\right\|=\|\boldsymbol{A}\|^{2}\right\},

where
A_{j,k}

(1\leq j\leq d,1\leq k\leq\ell)
are as given in (
5
).

Proof.

Let
x
be a unit vector such that
\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}^{*}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}x=\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}\|^{2}x
. Let
y=Ux=\left(y_{1},\ldots,y_{\ell}\right).

Fix
1\leq k\leq\ell
. Then

\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}A_{j,k}^{*}A_{j,k}\right)y_{k}=\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}\|^{2}y_{k}.

Note that
\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{d}A_{j,k}^{*}A_{j,k}\right\|\leq\|\boldsymbol{A^{*}A}\|
. Thus
y_{k}=0
whenever
\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{d}A_{j,k}^{*}A_{j,k}\right\|\neq\|\boldsymbol{A}\|^{2}
. Now for
1\leq k\leq\ell
such that
y_{k}\neq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}\|^{2}\|y_{k}\|

\displaystyle=\left\|U\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}A_{j}^{*}A_{j}\right)U^{*}\right\|\|y_{k}\|

\displaystyle\geq\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{d}A_{j,k}^{*}A_{j,k}\right\|\|y_{k}\|

\displaystyle\geq\left\|\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}A_{j,k}^{*}A_{j,k}\right)y_{k}\right\|.

So

\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}A_{j,k}^{*}A_{j,k}\right)y_{k}=\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{d}A_{j,k}^{*}A_{j,k}\right\|y_{k}.

Also, for each
1\leq j\leq d
,

\langle x|A_{j}x\rangle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq\ell,\\
y_{k}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\|y_{k}\|^{2}\left\langle\dfrac{y_{k}}{\|y_{k}\|}\bigg|A_{j,k}\dfrac{y_{k}}{\|y_{k}\|}\right\rangle.

Thus

\mathcal{V}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}})\subseteq\mathop{{\rm conv}}\left\{\mathcal{V}(A_{1,k},\dots,A_{d,k}):1\leq k\leq\ell,\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{d}A_{j,k}^{*}A_{j,k}\right\|=\|\boldsymbol{A}\|^{2}\right\}.

Now let

\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in\mathop{{\rm conv}}\left\{\mathcal{V}(A_{1,k},\dots,A_{d,k}):1\leq k\leq\ell,\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{d}A_{j,k}^{*}A_{j,k}\right\|=\|\boldsymbol{A}\|^{2}\right\}.

We have

\|\boldsymbol{A}\|^{2}=\max\left\{\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{d}A_{j,1}^{*}A_{j,1}\right\|,\dots,\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{d}A_{j,\ell}^{*}A_{j,\ell}\right\|\right\}.

Let
m
be the number of terms where this maximum is attained. Without loss of generality, let
1\leq k\leq m
be such that
\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{d}A_{j,k}^{*}A_{j,k}\right\|=\|\boldsymbol{A}\|^{2}
. So there are positive numbers
s_{1},\dots,s_{m}
such that
\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}s_{k}=1
and
\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}(A_{1,k},\dots,A_{d,k})
for all
1\leq k\leq m
such that

(8)

\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}s_{k}\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{k}.

Since
\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{k}\in\mathcal{V}(A_{1,k},\dots,A_{d,k})
, there exists a unit vector
y_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{m_{k}}
such that

(9)

\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{k}=\left(\langle y_{k}|A_{1,k}y_{k}\rangle,\dots,\langle y_{k}|A_{d,k}y_{k}\rangle\right),

and

Consider

. Then

Now, we have

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{A^{*}A}x

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}A_{j}^{*}A_{j}x

\displaystyle=U^{*}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}(UA_{j}U^{*})^{*}(UA_{j}U^{*})Ux\right)

\displaystyle=U^{*}\begin{bmatrix}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}A_{j,1}^{*}A_{j,1}&0&\ldots&0\\
0&\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}A_{j,2}^{*}A_{j,2}&\ddots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\ldots&0&\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}A_{j,\ell}^{*}A_{j,\ell}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\sqrt{s_{1}}y_{1}\\
\sqrt{s_{2}}y_{2}\\
\vdots\\
\sqrt{s_{m}}y_{m}\\
0\\
\vdots\\
0\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=U^{*}\left(\sqrt{s_{1}}\|\boldsymbol{A^{*}A}\|y_{1},\dots,\sqrt{s_{m}}\|\boldsymbol{A^{*}A}\|y_{m},0,\dots,0\right).

This gives

\boldsymbol{A^{*}A}x=\|\boldsymbol{A}\|^{2}x.

Further, we have

\displaystyle\langle x|A_{j}x\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle Ux|UA_{j}U^{*}(Ux)\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\left(\sqrt{s_{1}}y_{1},\dots,\sqrt{s_{m}}y_{m},0,\dots,0\right)|\left(\sqrt{s_{1}}A_{j,1}y_{1},\dots,\sqrt{s_{m}}A_{j,m}y_{m},0,\dots,0\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}s_{k}\langle y_{k}|A_{j,k}y_{k}\rangle.

So by (

), (

) and (

. Thus

\mathop{{\rm conv}}\left\{\mathcal{V}(A_{1,k},\dots,A_{d,k}):1\leq k\leq\ell,\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{d}A_{j,k}^{*}A_{j,k}\right\|=\|\boldsymbol{A}\|^{2}\right\}\subseteq\mathcal{V}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}).

Hence the result.

Theorem 6
.

For a doubly commuting tuple of matrices, we have

\mathop{{\rm dist}}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}},\mathbb{C}^{d}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{I}}})^{2}=\max_{\|x\|=1}\mathop{{\rm var}}_{x}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}).

Proof.

By (

\max\limits_{\|x\|=1}\mathop{{\rm var}}\limits_{x}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}})\leq\mathop{{\rm dist}}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}},\mathbb{C}^{d}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{I}}})^{2}

\mathop{{\boldsymbol{z}}}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}

\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}^{0}-\mathop{{\boldsymbol{z}}}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{I}}}\|\geq\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}^{0}\|
. By Corollary
2
and Lemma

, there exists a unit vector

such that

\|\textstyle\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}^{0}x\|=\|\textstyle\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}^{0}\|

and

\langle x|\textstyle A^{0}_{j}x\rangle=0\text{ for all }j=1,\ldots,d.

So

\displaystyle\mathop{{\rm dist}}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}},\mathbb{C}^{d}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{I}}})^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\textstyle\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}^{0}x\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\|A^{0}_{j}x\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\|A_{j}x-z^{0}_{j}x\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\big(\|A_{j}x\|^{2}-\big|\langle x|A_{j}x\rangle\big|^{2}\big).

Hence

Example 1
. Let
\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}=(A_{1},A_{2},A_{3})

A_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{bmatrix},A_{2}=\begin{bmatrix}0&-i\\
i&0\end{bmatrix},
and
A_{3}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{bmatrix}.
Then for any
\mathop{{\boldsymbol{z}}}=(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})\in\mathbb{C}^{3}

\displaystyle\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}-\mathop{{\boldsymbol{z}}}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{I}}}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\left(\begin{bmatrix}-z_{1}&1\\
1&-z_{1}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}-z_{2}&-i\\
i&-z_{2}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}1-z_{3}&0\\
0&-1-z_{3}\end{bmatrix}\right)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\begin{bmatrix}|z_{1}|^{2}+|z_{2}|^{2}+2+|1-z_{3}|^{2}&-(z_{1}+\overline{z_{1}})+i(z_{2}+\overline{z_{2}})\\

\displaystyle>2.

Now let
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Then

A_{1}x=(x_{2},x_{1}),A_{2}x=(-ix_{2},ix_{1})\text{ and }A_{3}x=(x_{1},-x_{2}).

and

\sum\limits_{j=1}^{3}\big|\langle x|A_{j}x\rangle\big|^{2}=4|x_{1}|^{2}|x_{2}|^{2}+(|x_{1}|^{2}-|x_{2}|^{2})^{2}=(|x_{1}|^{2}+|x_{2}|^{2})^{2}.

Hence for a unit vector
x\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
,
\mathop{{\rm var}}\limits_{x}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}})=2
. So
\mathop{{\rm dist}}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}},\mathbb{C}^{2}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{I}}})^{2}>\max\limits_{\|x\|=1}\mathop{{\rm var}}\limits_{x}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}})
.

Let

\displaystyle=\left\|\left(\begin{bmatrix}-z_{1}&1\\

-z_{1}&|z_{1}|^{2}+1+|1+z_{2}|^{2}\end{bmatrix}\right\|

\displaystyle\geq\max\{|1-z_{2}|^{2},1+|1+z_{2}|^{2}\}

\displaystyle\geq\max\{2-|1+z_{2}|^{2},1+|1+z_{2}|^{2}\}

\displaystyle\geq 3/2.

Now let

\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}x=(x_{2},0),\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{bmatrix}x=(x_{1},-x_{2}).

So
\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}x\|^{2}=|x_{2}|^{2}+|x_{1}|^{2}+|x_{2}|^{2}

Thus

\displaystyle\max_{\|x\|=1}\mathop{{\rm var}}\limits_{x}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}})

\displaystyle=\max_{\|x\|=1}\left(1+|x_{2}|^{2}-|x_{1}|^{2}|x_{2}|^{2}-(|x_{1}|^{2}-|x_{2}|^{2})^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\max_{\|x\|=1}\left(1+|x_{2}|^{2}+3|x_{1}|^{2}|x_{2}|^{2}-(|x_{1}|^{2}+|x_{2}|^{2})^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\max_{\|x\|=1}|x_{2}|^{2}(1+3|x_{1}|^{2})

\displaystyle=\max_{s\in[0,1]}(1-s)(1+3s)

\displaystyle=4/3

\displaystyle<3/2.

\mathop{{\rm dist}}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}},\mathbb{C}^{2}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{I}}})^{2}>\max\limits_{\|x\|=1}\mathop{{\rm var}}\limits_{x}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}).

Theorem 7
.

The following are equivalent.

(i)

\mathop{{\rm dist}}(\boldsymbol{A},\mathbb{C}^{d}\boldsymbol{I})^{2}=\sup\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\|x\|=1\end{subarray}}\mathop{{\rm var}}\limits_{x}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}).

(ii)

\boldsymbol{0}\in W_{0}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}^{0})
, that is, there is a sequence of unit vectors
(x^{(n)})
such that
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\boldsymbol{A^{0}}x^{(n)}\|=\|\boldsymbol{A^{0}}\|
and
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle x^{(n)}|A_{j}^{0}x^{(n)}\rangle=0
for all
1\leq j\leq d
.

(iii)

\mathop{{\rm dist}}(\boldsymbol{A},\mathbb{C}^{d}\boldsymbol{I})=\sup\left\{\big|\langle\boldsymbol{A}x|\boldsymbol{y}\rangle\big|:x\in\mathscr{H},\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathscr{H}^{d},\|x\|=\|\boldsymbol{y}\|=1,\right.\\
\langle x|y_{j}\rangle=0\text{ for all }1\leq j\leq d\big\}.

Proof.

Suppose
(i)
holds. Since
\mathop{{\rm var}}\limits_{x}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}})=\mathop{{\rm var}}\limits_{x}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}^{0})
, we have

\|\boldsymbol{A^{0}}\|^{2}=\sup_{\|x\|=1}\mathop{{\rm var}}_{x}(\textstyle\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}^{0}).

So there is a sequence of unit vectors
(x^{(n)})
such that
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathop{{\rm var}}\limits_{x^{(n)}}(\textstyle\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}^{0})=\|\boldsymbol{A^{0}}\|^{2}
. Since

\mathop{{\rm var}}\limits_{x^{(n)}}(\textstyle\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}^{0})\leq\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}^{0}x^{(n)}\|^{2}\leq\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}^{0}\|^{2},

we get
(ii)
. The proof of
(ii)
implies
(i)
is similar to the case
d=1
.

Now let us assume
(ii)
.
Then for each
1\leq j\leq d
, there is a unique
y_{j}^{(n)}\in(\mathbb{C}x^{(n)})^{\perp}
and a unique
\alpha_{j}^{(n)}\in\mathbb{C}
such that

A^{0}_{j}x^{(n)}=\alpha_{j}^{(n)}x^{(n)}+y_{j}^{(n)}.

By the assumption,
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\alpha_{j}^{(n)}=0
and
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|(y_{1}^{(n)},\dots,y_{d}^{(n)})\|=\|\boldsymbol{A^{0}}\|
.
Let
\boldsymbol{y^{(n)}}=\dfrac{(y_{1}^{(n)},\dots,y_{d}^{(n)})}{\|(y_{1}^{(n)},\dots,y_{d}^{(n)})\|}
. Then

\displaystyle\langle\boldsymbol{A}x^{(n)}|\boldsymbol{y^{(n)}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\big\langle\boldsymbol{A^{0}}x^{(n)}\big|\boldsymbol{y^{(n)}}\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\|\boldsymbol{A^{0}}x^{(n)}\|^{2}-\big\langle\boldsymbol{A^{0}}x^{(n)}\big|(\alpha_{1}^{(n)}x^{(n)},\dots,\alpha_{d}^{(n)}x^{(n)})\big\rangle}{\|(y_{1}^{(n)},\dots,y_{d}^{(n)})\|}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\|\boldsymbol{A^{0}}\|\text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty.

This gives

\mathop{{\rm dist}}(\boldsymbol{A},\mathbb{C}^{d}\boldsymbol{I})\leq\sup\left\{\big|\langle\boldsymbol{A}x|\boldsymbol{y}\rangle\big|:\|x\|=\|\boldsymbol{y}\|=1,\langle x|y_{j}\rangle=0\text{ for all }1\leq j\leq d\right\}.

If
x\in\mathcal{H}
and
\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathcal{H}^{d}
are such that
\|x\|=\|\boldsymbol{y}\|=1
and
\langle x|y_{j}\rangle=0
for all
1\leq j\leq d
, then for every
\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
, we have

\big|\langle\boldsymbol{A}x|\boldsymbol{y}\rangle\big|=\big|\langle(\boldsymbol{A-zI})x|\boldsymbol{y}\rangle\big|\leq\|\boldsymbol{A-zI}\|.

So we also have

\sup\left\{\big|\langle\boldsymbol{A}x|\boldsymbol{y}\rangle\big|:\|x\|=\|\boldsymbol{y}\|=1,\langle x|y_{j}\rangle=0\text{ for all }1\leq j\leq d\right\}\leq\mathop{{\rm dist}}(\boldsymbol{A},\mathbb{C}^{d}\boldsymbol{I}).

This gives
(iii)
.

Now assume
(iii)
holds. We prove
(ii)
along the lines of Remark 3.1 of
[
6
]
where a similar result is shown for
d=1
. By our assumption, we get sequences of unit vectors
x^{(n)}\in\mathscr{H}
and
\boldsymbol{y^{(n)}}\in\mathscr{H}^{d}
such that

\big|\langle\boldsymbol{A}^{0}x^{(n)}|\boldsymbol{y^{(n)}}\rangle\big|\rightarrow\|\boldsymbol{A}^{0}\|

and

\langle x^{(n)}|y^{(n)}_{j}\rangle=0\text{ for all }j=1,\dots,d.

Since

\big|\langle\boldsymbol{A}^{0}x^{(n)}|\boldsymbol{y^{(n)}}\rangle\big|\leq\|\boldsymbol{A}^{0}x^{(n)}\|\leq\|\boldsymbol{A}^{0}\|,

we get

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\boldsymbol{A}^{0}x^{(n)}\|=\|\boldsymbol{A}^{0}\|.

This gives
\boldsymbol{y^{(n)}}-\dfrac{\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}^{0}x^{(n)}}{\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}^{0}x^{(n)}\|}\rightarrow 0
. So

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle x^{(n)}|A_{j}^{0}x^{(n)}\rangle=\|\boldsymbol{A}^{0}\|\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle x^{(n)}|y^{(n)}_{j}\rangle=0.

Thus we get
(ii)
.

We have the following general result to Corollary

\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}

Proposition 8
.

Suppose
W_{0}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}})
is convex. Then

\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}-\mathop{{\boldsymbol{z}}}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{I}}}\|\geq\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}\|\text{ for all }\mathop{{\boldsymbol{z}}}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}

if and only if
\boldsymbol{0}\in W_{0}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}})
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of
[
32
, Theorem 2]
where this result is proved for
d=1
. We provide the details for the sake of completeness. Suppose there exists a sequence of unit vectors
x^{(n)}
such that
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}x^{(n)}\|=\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}\|
and
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle x^{(n)}|A_{j}x^{(n)}\rangle=0
for all
j=1,\ldots,d
. Then for
\mathop{{\boldsymbol{z}}}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\|(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}-\mathop{{\boldsymbol{z}}}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{I}}})x^{(n)}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}x^{(n)}\|^{2}-2\mathop{{\rm Re}}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}z_{j}\langle x^{(n)}|A_{j}x^{(n)}\rangle\right)+\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{z}}}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}\|^{2}+\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{z}}}\|^{2}\text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty.

Thus
\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}-\mathop{{\boldsymbol{z}}}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{I}}}\|^{2}\geq\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}\|^{2}+\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{z}}}\|^{2}
. In particular,
\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}-\mathop{{\boldsymbol{z}}}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{I}}}\|\geq\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}\|
.

Suppose
\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}-\mathop{{\boldsymbol{z}}}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{I}}}\|\geq\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}\|\text{ for all }\mathop{{\boldsymbol{z}}}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
and
\boldsymbol{0}\notin W_{0}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}})
. Since
W_{0}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}})
is a closed and convex set, the Hahn-Banach separation theorem gives a
\tau>0
and a unit vector
(w_{1},w_{2},\dots,w_{d})\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
such that

\mathop{{\rm Re}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}w_{i}\lambda_{i}\geq\tau>0\text{ for all }(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{d})\in W_{0}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}).

Let
\mathcal{S}=\left\{x\in\mathscr{H}:\|x\|=1\text{ and }\mathop{{\rm Re}}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}w_{i}\langle A_{i}x|x\rangle\right)\leq\tau/2\right\}
. Let
\eta=\sup\{\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}x\|:x\in\mathcal{S}\}
. Clearly
\eta<\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}\|
.
Let
\mu=\min\{\tau/2,(\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}\|-\eta)/2\}
. Let
\boldsymbol{w^{0}}=\mu(\overline{w_{1}},\overline{w_{2}},\dots,\overline{w_{d}})
. If
x\in\mathcal{S}
, then

\displaystyle\|(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}-\boldsymbol{w^{0}}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{I}}})x\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}x\|+\mu\|(w_{1},w_{2},\dots,w_{d})\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\eta+\mu

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}\|.

Suppose
x
is a unit vector such that
x\notin\mathcal{S}
. We write
\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}x
as

\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}x=\left((\alpha_{1}+i\beta_{1})x,(\alpha_{2}+i\beta_{2})x,\dots,(\alpha_{d}+i\beta_{d})x\right)+\boldsymbol{y},

where
\langle\boldsymbol{y}|(x,x,\dots,x)\rangle=0
and
\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
for all
1\leq j\leq d
. Then

\displaystyle\|(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}-\boldsymbol{w^{0}}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{I}}})x\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}(\alpha_{i}-\mu\mathop{{\rm Re}}(w_{i}))^{2}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}(\beta_{i}+\mu\mathop{{\rm Im}}(w_{i}))^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{y}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}x\|^{2}+\mu^{2}-2\mu\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}(\mathop{{\rm Re}}(w_{i})\alpha_{i}-\mathop{{\rm Im}}(w_{i})\beta_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}x\|^{2}+\left(\mu^{2}-2\mu\mathop{{\rm Re}}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}w_{i}(\alpha_{i}+i\beta_{i})\right)\right)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}\|^{2}-\mu^{2}.

The last inequality follows because
\mathop{{\rm Re}}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}w_{i}(\alpha_{i}+i\beta_{i})\right)>\tau/2\geq\mu
.
This gives
\|(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}-\boldsymbol{w^{0}}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{I}}})x\|<\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}\|
, which contradicts our assumption.∎

Example 2

A_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\

Let
\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}=(A_{1},A_{2})

\boldsymbol{z}=(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{A-zI}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\left(\begin{bmatrix}1-z_{1}&0\\

\displaystyle=\left\|\begin{bmatrix}|1-z_{1}|^{2}+|z_{2}|^{2}+1&-z_{2}\\

\displaystyle\geq\left\|\begin{bmatrix}|1-z_{1}|^{2}+|z_{2}|^{2}+1&-z_{2}\\

\displaystyle=\left((|1-z_{1}|^{2}+|z_{2}|^{2}+1)^{2}+|z_{2}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\geq 1.

Equality occurs if and only if
z_{1}=1
and
z_{2}=0
. Thus

\mathop{{\rm dist}}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}},\mathbb{C}^{2}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{I}}})^{2}=1=\|(A_{1},A_{2})-(I,0)\|^{2}.

So
\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}^{0}=\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
0&-1\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
1&0\end{bmatrix}\right)
. By definition,

W_{0}(\boldsymbol{A}^{0})=\mathcal{V}(\boldsymbol{A}^{0})=\{(\langle x|A_{1}^{0}x\rangle,\langle x|A_{2}^{0}x\rangle):\|x\|=1,(\boldsymbol{A}^{0})^{*}\boldsymbol{A}^{0}x=\|\boldsymbol{A^{0}}\|^{2}x\}.

Since
(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}^{0})^{*}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}^{0}=I
,

W_{0}(\boldsymbol{A}^{0})=W(\boldsymbol{A}^{0})=\left\{\left(-|z_{2}|^{2},z_{2}\overline{z_{1}}\right):z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{C},|z_{1}|^{2}+|z_{2}|^{2}=1\right\}.

This set is not convex (see
[
10
, p. 138]
).
Now let
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Then

\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}x\|^{2}=2|x_{1}|^{2},\langle x|A_{1}x\rangle=|x_{1}|^{2}\text{ and }\langle x|A_{2}x\rangle=x_{2}\overline{x_{1}}.

Hence

\mathop{{\rm var}}\limits_{x}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}})=2|x_{1}|^{2}-|x_{1}|^{4}-|x_{1}|^{2}|x_{2}|^{2}.

So

\max\limits_{\|x\|=1}\mathop{{\rm var}}\limits_{x}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}})=1=\mathop{{\rm dist}}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}},\mathbb{C}^{2}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{I}}})^{2}.

remarks

Remark 1
. Theorem 1.3 of
[
31
]
gives that
\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}-\mathop{{\boldsymbol{z}}}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{I}}}\|\geq\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}\|
for all
\mathop{{\boldsymbol{z}}}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
if and only if there exists an at most countable set
\mathcal{J}
, a set of positive numbers
\{s_{j}:j\in\mathcal{J}\}
and an orthonormal set
\{x_{j}:j\in\mathcal{J}\}
such that

(i)

\sum\limits_{j\in\mathcal{J}}s_{j}=1
,

(ii)

\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}^{*}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}x_{j}=\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}\|^{2}x_{j}
for each
j\in\mathcal{J}
,

(iii)

\sum\limits_{j\in\mathcal{J}}s_{j}\langle x_{j}|A_{i}x_{j}\rangle=0
for each
1\leq i\leq d
.

Another proof of Theorem
1
now follows using the following fact.
Let
X
be a convex subset of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
. Let
(\boldsymbol{z_{n}})
be a sequence of elements in
X
and let
(s_{n})
be a sequence of non-negative numbers such that
\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}s_{n}=1
. If
\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}s_{n}\boldsymbol{z_{n}}
exists, then it lies in
X
.

Remark 2
. Let
\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}
be a tuple of commuting matrices. We note that when each
A_{j}
is a

or
3\times 3
matrix,

\displaystyle\big\{\langle\boldsymbol{A}x|\boldsymbol{y}\rangle

\displaystyle=\big\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|^{2}\leq\mathop{{\rm var}}_{x}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}})\text{ for some }x\in\mathscr{H},\|x\|=1\big\}.

For a tuple of operators, we also have equivalence of
(i)
and
(iii)
in Theorem

. This raises the curiosity if (

Remark 4
. From the proof of Proposition 8, it follows that if
W_{0}(\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}})
is convex, then the following are equivalent.

(i)

\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}-\mathop{{\boldsymbol{z}}}\mathop{{\boldsymbol{I}}}\|\geq\|\mathop{{\boldsymbol{A}}}\|\text{ for all }\mathop{{\boldsymbol{z}}}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}

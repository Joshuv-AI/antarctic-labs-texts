Deformed Laurent series rings and completions of the Weyl division ring
Gang Han

Abstract
Let L ⁡ ( ( T − 1 ) ) L((T^{-1})) be the space of (inverse) Laurent series with coefficients in some field L L . It has a standard degree map and the induced topology. With its usual addition and a new product on this space which is continuous and preserves the standard degree map, it will be a complete topological division ring, and called a deformed Laurent series ring. Under mild restrictions, we give the necessary and sufficient conditions for a product on L ⁡ ( ( T − 1 ) ) L((T^{-1})) to make it a deformed Laurent series ring. Then we apply the above theory to construct the completions of the Weyl division ring D 1 D_{1} , over some field of characteristic 0, with respect to a class of discrete valuations on it. Such completions are topological division rings with nice properties. For instance, their valuation rings are non-commutative Henselian rings; the centralizer of each element not in the center is commutative.

Introduction

Let
K
be a field of Characteristic 0. Let
A_{1}(K)
be the first Weyl algebra over
K
generated by 2 generators
\textbf{p},\textbf{{q}}
satisfying the relation

[\textbf{q},\textbf{p}]=\textbf{qp}-\textbf{pq}=1.

(1.1)

We adopt the convention (
1.1

[
J1973
]

is a simple Noetherian domain of GK dimension 2. The quotient division algebra

of

is usually called the Weyl division ring or Weyl skew field.

People have been interested in
A_{1}(K)
and

for quite some time, and have conducted extensive research in this area. For example in
[
ML1983

Makar-Limanov showed that

contains a two-generator free subalgebra.

There were also some research about the valuations on
A_{1}(K)
and

. A class of discrete valuations of

compatible with the Bernstein
filtration were classified in
[

]
. All the valuations on the real Weyl algebra

whose residue field is

are classified in
[
V2024
]

A map

such that
-u:D_{1}(K)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\infty},f\mapsto-u(f)
is a rank 1 valuation will be called a degree map on

. The degree maps

on
A_{1}(K)
over
K
with the usual basis
\{\textbf{p}^{i}\textbf{q}^{j}|i,j\geq 0\}
being an evaluation basis for

are classified in Proposition
2.4
. It is interesting that they are in 1-1 correspondence with the set
\{(\rho,\sigma)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}|\rho+\sigma\geq 0\}
. Up to equivalence, those degree maps
\mathsf{v}_{\rho,\sigma}
with
(\rho,\sigma)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
,

and

are coprime, and

, correspond to discrete valuations. See Section 2.

In the influential paper
[
D1968
]
, Dixmier studied the structure of
A_{1}(K)
in great detail, and in the end he posed six problems. The first problem asks if
every endomorphism of the Weyl algebra
A_{1}(K)
is an automorphism. The conjecture that its answer is positive was later called Dixmier Conjecture. One approach to study this conjecture is to find all the solutions (up to automorphisms of
A_{1}(K)
) of the equation
[x,y]=1
for
x,y
in
A_{1}(K)
. We believe that it is better to study this equation in
D_{1}(K)
and its completions.
One purpose of this note is to construct the completions of
D_{1}(K)
with respect to such discrete valuations. In fact, certain skew
Laurent series algebras and the algebra of formal
pseudo-differential operators are completions of
D_{1}(K)
with respect to different valuations. Since there are infinitely many nonequivalent degree maps on
D_{1}(K)
, it will be interesting to find the relationship between different completions.

To construct such completions, we did the following. Let
L((T^{-1}))
be the space of (inverse) Laurent series
with coefficients in some field
L
, which has a standard degree map and the corresponding topology.
We define a new product on this space (while the addition is as usual) which is continuous and preserves the standard degree map. We call it a deformed Laurent series ring. See Def.
3.1
. Assume that it satisfies

T\cdot a=aT+\sum_{i=0}^{-\infty}\delta_{i}(a)T^{i},a\in L,

where
\delta_{i}:L\rightarrow L,i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0},
are additive maps. Then the necessary and sufficient condition for it to make
L((T^{-1}))
a deformed Laurent series ring is (See Theorem
3.10
)

\delta_{i}(ab)=a\delta_{i}(b)+\delta_{i}(a)b+\sum_{k>0}\sum_{(l,j_{1},\cdots,j_{k})}^{j_{1}+\cdots+j_{k}-k+l=i}\binom{l}{k}\delta_{l}(a)\delta_{j_{1}}\cdots\delta_{j_{k}}(b),a,b\in L,

where the summation is over all
l,j_{1},\cdots,j_{k}\leq 0
and
j_{1}+\cdots+j_{k}-k+l=i
. The proof uses a lot of combinatorial identities.

This ring, denoted by
L((T^{-1};\tilde{\delta}))
, is a complete topological skew field. It is a division ring of formal Laurent series with a deformed multiplication. Its center is the field
F=\cap_{i\leq 0}\text{Ker}(\delta_{i})
, and such
\delta_{i}
are
F
-linear differential operators on
L
.

The proof of above results is in Section 3, in which a symmetric version of such results is also given.

In Section 5, a pair of topological generators of

In

A class of degree maps on

Let
R
be a ring.

Def. 2.1
.

A map

is called a rank 1 valuation on
R
if it satisfies

1.

v(fg)=v(f)+v(g),\forall f,g\in R
;

2.

v(f+g)\geq min\{v(f),v(g)\},\forall f,g\in R
;

3.

v(f)=\infty
iff
f=0
.

If
v(R^{\times})=0
then the valuation
v
is called trivial;
If
v(R^{\times})\cong\mathbb{Z}
then the valuation
v
is called discrete.

Two rank 1 valuations
v_{1},v_{2}
on
R
are called equivalent if there exists some
c\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
,
v_{2}(f)=cv_{1}(f)
for all
f\in R^{\times}
.

All the valuations and degree maps considered in this paper will be assumed to be rank 1.

A map

u:R\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{-\infty}
such that

on

\begin{array}[]{lll}(1)\ u(fg)=u(f)+u(g),\forall f,g\in R;\\

(3)\ u(f)=-\infty\ \text{iff}\ f=0.\end{array}

(2.1)

A degree map

is called trivial (resp. discrete) if so is the valuation
-u
. Two degree maps

on

.

For a degree map

on

defined by

Given a degree map
u
on
R
, we will always equip

Proof.

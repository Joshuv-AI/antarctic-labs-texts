Introduction

A variety of universal algebras
\mathbb{V}
is called a
Mal’tsev variety

[
21
]
when its theory admits a ternary operation
p
satisfying the equations

p(x,y,y)=x\;\;\mathrm{and}\;\;p(x,x,y)=y.

Such varieties were characterised in
[
21
]
by the fact that any pair of congruences
R
and
S
on a same algebra
X
is
2-permutable
, i.e.
RS=SR
. It was shown in
[
19
]
that such varieties are also characterised by the fact that any homomorphic relation
D
from an algebra
X
to an algebra
Z
is
difunctional
:
(x_{1}Dz_{2}\wedge x_{2}Dz_{2}\wedge x_{2}Dz_{1})\Rightarrow x_{1}Dz_{1}
.

The notion of Mal’tsev variety was generalised to a categorical context in
[
8
]
(see also
[
7
,
9
,
3
]
). This was achieved by translating the above properties on homomorphic relations for a variety into similar properties on (internal) relations in a category. A regular category
[
2
]

\mathbb{C}
is called a
Mal’tsev category
when any pair of equivalence relations
R
and
S
in
\mathbb{C}
on a same object
X
is such that
RS=SR
. Mal’tsev categories can also be characterised by the difunctionality of relations:

Theorem 0.1
.

[
8
,
9
,
7
]
A finitely complete category
\mathbb{C}
is a Mal’tsev category if and only if any relation
D\to X\times Z
in
\mathbb{C}
is difunctional.

The main examples of Mal’tsev varieties are
\mathsf{Grp}
of groups,
\mathsf{Ab}
of abelian groups,
R
-
\mathsf{Mod}
of modules over a commutative ring
R
,
\mathsf{Rng}
of rings, and
\mathsf{Heyt}
of Heyting algebras. More generally, any variety whose theory contains a group operation is a Mal’tsev variety; an example of a non-Mal’tsev variety is the variety
\mathsf{Mon}
of monoids. As examples of Mal’tsev categories (that are not varietal) we have the category
\mathsf{Grp}(\mathsf{Top})
of topological groups, any abelian category or the dual of an elementary topos. Also, if
\mathbb{C}
is a Mal’tsev category, then so are the (co)slice categories
\mathbb{C}/X
and
X/\mathbb{C}
, for any object
X
of
\mathbb{C}
(see
[
3
]
, for example).

There are also several other well-known characterisations of Mal’tsev varieties through nice properties on relations, such as the fact that every reflexive relation is necessarily a congruence. All of these characteristic properties have been generalised to the Mal’tsev categorical context (see
[
8
,
9
,
7
,
3
,
6

-permutable

[
15
]

. An

-permutable variety is such that any pair of congruences

and

on a same algebra

is

and

-permutable categories in
[

[

]

]
for further properties on relations.

To what extent are W-Mal’tsev and Mal’tsev objects comparable
?

(W5)
.
∎

An interesting question is whether
\mathsf{W}(\mathbb{C})
and
\mathsf{M}(\mathbb{C})
are themselves Mal’tsev categories. From
(W3)
we know that, if
\mathbb{C}
is a well-powered regular category with coproducts, then
\mathsf{W}(\mathbb{C})
is finitely complete. Also, if every relation in
\mathsf{W}(\mathbb{C})
is also a relation in
\mathbb{C}
, then the full subcategory
\mathsf{W}(\mathbb{C})
is a Mal’tsev category; actually, it is the largest one which is closed under binary coproducts and quotients in
\mathbb{C}
(see
(W4)
). On the other hand, there are no “obvious” conditions on
\mathbb{C}
from which we could deduce finite completeness for
\mathsf{M}(\mathbb{C})
. So, we can only conclude that
\mathsf{M}(\mathbb{C})
is a Mal’tsev category when it is closed under finite limits in
\mathbb{C}
; this is
(M2)
. When this is the case, we still do not know whether
\mathsf{M}(\mathbb{C})
is the largest full subcategory of
\mathbb{C}
which is a Mal’tsev category.

In
[
25
]
is was shown that the Mal’tsev objects in
\mathsf{Mon}
are precisely the groups (Theorem 6.14). This is also the case with respect to W-Mal’tsev objects in
\mathsf{Mon}
. It follows from the above and the next proposition that
\mathsf{Grp}
is the largest full subcategory of
\mathsf{Mon}
which is a Mal’tsev category and is closed under binary coproducts and quotients in
\mathsf{Mon}
.

Proposition 1.5

A monoid

if and only if

is a group.

Proof.

If

2.
V
-categories and (W-)Mal’tsev objects

In this section we will generalise the characterisations of W-Mal’tsev objects in the duals of the category of metric spaces and of the category of topological spaces obtained in
[
27
]
. For that we will make use of the concepts of
V
-category and of
(U,V)
-category. Here, as in
[
20
]
, the notion of
V
-category will play the role of metric space, while a
(U,V)
-category (as introduced in
[
14
]
) will play the role of a topological space. We start by presenting the basic tools of this approach.

Throughout
V
is a unital and integral quantale; that is,
V
is a complete lattice equipped with a tensor product
\otimes
, with unit
k=\top\neq\bot
, that distributes over arbitrary joins. As a category,
V
is a monoidal closed category.

When more than one tensor product may be considered, we use the notation
V_{\otimes}
to indicate that we are using the tensor product
\otimes
in
V
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
\mathbf{\emph{V}}
-category
is a set
X
together with a map
X\times X\to V
, whose image of
(x,x^{\prime})
we denote by
X(x,x^{\prime})
, such that, for each
x,x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}\in X
,

(R)

k\leq X(x,x)
;

(T)

X(x,x^{\prime})\otimes X(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})\leq X(x,x^{\prime\prime})
.

A
V
-category is said to be
symmetric
if, for every
x,x^{\prime}\in X
,
X(x,x^{\prime})=X(x^{\prime},x)
.

A
V
-functor

f\colon X\to Y
is a map such that, for all
x,x^{\prime}\in X
,
X(x,x^{\prime})\leq Y(f(x),f(x^{\prime}))
.

The two axioms of a
V
-category express the existence of identities and the categorical composition law, but they may also be seen as a reflexivity and a transitivity condition, or even as two conditions usually imposed to metric structures: if in the complete half real line
[0,\infty]
we consider the order relation
\geq
and the tensor product
+
, (R) and (T) read as

(R’)

0\geq X(x,x)
;

(T’)

X(x,x^{\prime})+X(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})\geq X(x,x^{\prime\prime})
,

with (R) meaning that the distance from a point to itself is
0
, and (T) the usual triangular inequality.

We denote by
V\mbox{-}\mathsf{Cat}
the category of
V
-categories and
V
-functors, and by
V\mbox{-}\mathsf{Cat}_{\rm sym}
its full subcategory of symmetric
V
-categories.

Remark 2.2
.

When
V
is a complete lattice which is a
frame
, so that finite meets distribute over arbitrary joins, then
V_{\wedge}=(V,\leq,\wedge,\top)
is a (unital and integral) quantale. We call such quantales
cartesian
. It is well-known that, if
V_{\wedge}
is a cartesian quantale, then the category
V_{\wedge}\mbox{-}\mathsf{Cat}
has special features, like being cartesian closed
[
20
]
. Here we will show that it also has a key role in the study of W-Mal’tsev objects of
(V_{\wedge}\mbox{-}\mathsf{Cat})^{\rm op}
.

Moreover, even if the quantale
V_{\otimes}
is not a frame, the
V_{\otimes}
-categories
X
which, in addition, verify

X(x,x^{\prime})\wedge X(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})\leq X(x,x^{\prime\prime})

will be specially relevant, and we will call them also
V_{\wedge}
-categories. We note that, since
k=\top
, they also satisfy
\top\leq X(x,x)
for every
x\in X
; moreover,
u\otimes v\leq u\wedge v
always holds, so the above condition guarantees immediately condition (T) of the above definition.

Examples 2.3
.

(1)

When
V=2=(\{0<1\},\wedge,1)
, a
V
-category is a preordered set and a
V
-functor is a monotone map. Hence
V\mbox{-}\mathsf{Cat}
is the well-known category
\mathsf{Ord}
of preordered sets and monotone maps.

(2)

When
V=[0,\infty]_{+}=([0,\infty],\geq,+,0)
, that is
V
is the complete lattice
[0,\infty]
, ordered by
\geq
, with tensor product
\otimes=+
, a
V
-category is a (
generalised
)
Lawvere metric space

[
20
]
(not necessarily separated nor symmetric, with
\infty
as a possible distance), since the two conditions above mean that
X(x,x)=0
and
X(x,y)+X(y,z)\geq X(x,z)
, for
x,y,z\in X
, and a
V
-functor is a
non-expansive map
.

When
V
is the cartesian quantale

, with tensor product
\otimes={\rm max}
, then
V_{\rm max}\mbox{-}\mathsf{Cat}
is the category of (

)
ultrametric spaces
.

(3)

The complete lattice
([0,1],\leq)
can be equipped with several tensor products – usually called

– including the

, which lead to interesting instances of categories of the form

.

(4)

The set of
distribution functions

MBM classes and contraction loci on low-dimensional hyperkähler manifolds of K3
[n]

Abstract




Abstract

An MBM locus on a hyperkähler manifold is the
union of all deformations of a minimal rational
curve with negative self-intersection.
MBM loci can be equivalently defined as
centers of bimeromorphic contractions.
In
[
AV3
]
, it was shown that
the MBM loci on deformation equivalent
hyperkähler manifolds are diffeomorphic.
We determine the MBM loci on a hyperkähler
manifold of K3
[n]
-type for small
n
using
a deformation to a Hilbert scheme of a
non-algebraic K3 surface.

1
Introduction

In the last twenty years or so, a considerable effort has been made to understand the Mori cone, or, dually,
the ample (or Kähler in the non-projective case) cone of irreducible holomorphic symplectic (IHS) manifolds. For K3 surfaces it has been known for a long time
that the extremal rays correspond to
(-2)
-curves (that
is, irreducible curves of square
-2
, automatically
smooth and rational), so that the ample (or Kähler)
classes are characterized, among positive classes, as
those which are positive on all
(-2)
-curves. In higher
dimension, though, until recently the only general result,
due to Huybrechts and Boucksom, was that one could still
test the Kähler property on rational curves.
However, no boundedness statement for such curves was
available. A boundedness statement (equivalent to the
Morrison-Kawamata cone conjecture) was obtained in

[
AV2
]
.

In
[
HT1
]
Hassett and Tschinkel conjectured a
description of the Mori cone for projective IHS manifolds
which are deformation equivalent to the Hilbert square of
a K3 surface. Such manifolds are also called holomorphic
symplectic fourfolds of K3
[n]
type.
The conjecture by Hassett and Tschinkel (Conjecture 3.1 of

[
HT1
]
) stated that the extremal rays are generated
by the classes with some particular numerical
properties. For the Hilbert square of a K3 surface
containing a smooth rational curve
C
, these numerics are either
those of the rational curves contracted
by the Hilbert-Chow morphism, or of the rational
curves arising from
C
, or of the line in the projective
plane which is the symmetric square of such a
C
. Hassett
and Tschinkel have subsequently proved this conjecture in

[
HT2
]
(in the projective case).

For high-dimensional holomorphic symplectic manifolds of K3
[n]
type
the analogous initial conjecture by Hassett and Tschinkel
(Conjecture 1.2 of
[
HT3
]
turned out to be false. The description of the Mori cone was provided by Bayer and
Macri
[
BM
]
using advanced technology around Bridgeland’s stability conditions.

Another conjecture by Hassett and Tschinkel (Conjecture 3.6 of
[
HT1
]
, and still a conjecture 24 in
[
HT2
]
) was concerned with the geometry of the loci covered by the minimal rational
curves
according to the numerical properties of the corresponding
extremal rays (by Kawamata base-point-free theorem, one
can interpret those loci
as exceptional sets of birational contractions). As it is
natural, one expects that such a locus is either
a lagrangian plane or a
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
-bundle over a K3 surface, the former covered by curves with Beauville-Bogomolov square

-5/2
, the latter either
-1/2
(the type of the
exceptional divisor of the Hilbert-Chow map) or
-2
(the
type of the divisor of subschemes with some support on
a
-2
-curve
C
on the K3-surface). One can also expect a similar
description in higher dimension. Hassett and Tschinkel
have given lists of possible types of extremal rays and
corresponding contraction loci in
[
HT3
]
and then
completed a few missing types following Bayer and Macri in

[
HT4
]
.

Bayer and Macri’s technology allows, in principle, to describe contraction loci as fibrations in projective spaces
over
K3-related manifolds (up to birational equivalence). Those manifolds arise as moduli spaces of sheaves on a K3 surface in question, and the
projective spaces are projectivisations of Ext-groups between the sheaves. But this approach is not very explicit,
whereas at list in low dimension it seems possible and useful to describe the extremal rays and the subvarieties covered by the corresponding curves “by hand”.

The purpose of this note is to provide a particularly simple description of possible extremal rays and contraction loci for holomorphic symplectic manifolds of K3
[n]
type in low dimension, particularly four and six
(i.e. for manifolds which are deformation equivalent to the Hilbert square or the Hilbert cube of a K3 surface). Rather than studying extremal rays, we work with a slightly different notion of MBM classes. This notion was introduced in
[
AV1
]
to put things in the deformation-invariant context, and indeed the crucial point in our argument is the deformation to the Hilbert scheme of a non-projective K3 surface.

The MBM classes are the classes which correspond to extremal rays of the Mori cone on some deformation of
X
, or, equivalently, the classes of extremal rays up to monodromy and birational equivalence (the name derives from “monodromy birationally minimal”). From a uniruled
subvariety
Z
of an IHS
X
we obtain an MBM class as follows: let
C
be a rational curve
which is minimal among the rational curves in
Z
(in the sense that
C
does not bend-and-break). If its class is Beauville-Bogomolov negative, then it is MBM.

The parameter space
\operatorname{\sf Teich}_{\alpha}^{min}
for complex structures where an MBM class
\alpha
is represented by a minimal rational
curve coincides with the whole space where
\alpha
stays
of type
(1,1)
up to inseparability issues. Such curves
can always be contracted (
[
AV3
, Corollary 5.3]
).

In
[
AV3
]
we proved some deformation-invariance statements about the corresponding contraction
loci (i.e. exceptional sets of morphisms contracting the class
\alpha
). We have, in particular, shown that the diffeomorphism type of such a contraction locus is constant over
\operatorname{\sf Teich}_{\alpha}^{min}
. Moreover, so is the biholomorphism type of the fibers of the rational quotient fibration of the exceptional locus, with a possible exception of the points of
\operatorname{\sf Teich}_{\alpha}^{min}
corresponding to the complex structures with maximal Picard number.

The idea now is as follows. Let
\alpha
be a negative integral
(1,1)
-class on a hyperkähler fourfold
X
of K3
[n]
type. Using Markman’s description of the monodromy group, we prove that
X
can be deformed to a non-projective manifold
X^{\prime}\cong\operatorname{Hilb}^{2}(S^{\prime})
where
S^{\prime}
is a K3 surface with cyclic Picard group generated by a
negative class, and in such a way that
\alpha
stays of type
(1,1)
. Since rational curves on
X^{\prime}
are easy to describe, we obtain the classification of subvarieties which can be covered by rational curves in the class
\alpha
, by deformation-invariance.

If
X
is an IHS of K3
[n]
type of dimension six, it
turns out that
X
still deforms to the Hilbert cube of a
non-projective K3 surface
S
, but its Picard group can
now be generated by an element with negative or zero
Beauville-Bogomolov square. The geometry in this last case
is also well-understood. Namely,
S
is an elliptic
K3

surface with singular fibers which are nodal rational
curves, and there is no other curve at all. All rational
curves (up to deformation)
on the symmetric cube of
S
are obtained from linear
systems
g_{k}^{1},k\leqslant 3
on the fibers,
1
1

1

As
defined in
[
Har
, §IV.5]
,
g_{k}^{d}

denotes a linear system of dimension
d
and degree
k

on a curve. In particular,
g_{k}^{1}
correspond to

k
-sheeted maps to
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
.

so again easy to
describe. We obtain a table of five types of MBM classes,
their squares and the types of the corresponding
contraction loci, which agrees with the data by Hassett
and Tschinkel (one may notice here that the MBM class
initially overlooked in
[
HT3
]
and exhibited in

[
HT4
]
while working out Bayer and Macri’s formulae on
this example, comes in our picture from the Hilbert cube
of a surface with zero intersection form on the Picard
group).

The same calculation works in fact for eightfolds (again bringing into the picture a class originally overlooked
in
[
HT3
]
) and tenfolds, but not for higher-dimensional IHS of K3
[n]
type. It would be interesting to see
whether the present elementary method could be adapted to give a reasonable classification in all dimensions.

2
Hyperkähler manifolds

In this section we recall some definitions concerning IHS manifolds and rational curves.
For more details see

[
Bea
]
and
[
AV1
]
.

2.1
Holomorphically symplectic manifolds

Definition 2.1: A holomorphically symplectic manifold

is a complex manifold equipped with a nowhere degenerate holomorphic
two-form.

Definition 2.2:
For the rest of this paper,

a hyperkähler manifold

is a compact, Kähler, holomorphically symplectic manifold.

Definition 2.3:
A hyperkähler manifold
M
is called
simple
, or
IHS
, or
of maximal holonomy

if
\pi_{1}(M)=0
,
H^{2,0}(M)={\mathbb{C}}
.

The terminology originates from
Bogomolov’s decomposition theorem
stating that
any hyperkähler manifold admits a finite covering which
is a product of a torus and several IHS,

[
Bo1
]
. This decomposition also
corresponds the de Rham-Berger local decomposition
of a Riemannian manifold into a product of manifolds
with irreducible Riemannian holonomy. Then, the IHS manifolds correspond
to the hyperkähler manifolds which have irreducible holonomy.
Further on, all hyperkähler manifolds
are assumed to be of maximal holonomy.

Example 2.4:
The basic series of examples is that of punctual Hilbert schemes of K3 surfaces. As observed by
Beauville in
[
Bea
]
, for any K3 surface
S
, the Hilbert scheme
S^{[n]}
of ideal sheaves of colength
n

on
S
is an IHS. An IHS which is a deformation of
S^{[n]}
for a K3 surface
S
is said to be
of K3
[n]
type
. There is one more series (generalized Kummer manifolds) and two sporadic families (O’Grady’s examples)
known, and we don’t know whether there is anything else.

2.2
The Bogomolov-Beauville-Fujiki form

Theorem 2.5:
(Fujiki,
[
F
]
)
Let
\eta\in H^{2}(M)
, and
\dim M=2n
, where
M
is
hyperkähler. Then
\int_{M}\eta^{2n}=cq(\eta,\eta)^{n}
,
for some primitive integral quadratic form
q
on
H^{2}(M,{\mathbb{Z}})
,
and
c>0
a rational number.

Definition 2.6:
This form is called

Bogomolov-Beauville-Fujiki form
. It is defined
by the Fujiki’s relation uniquely, up to a sign. The sign is found
from an explicit formula due to Beauville and Bogomolov, using integration against exterior powers of the holomorphic symplectic form
\Omega
and its conjugate, see e.g.
[
Bea
]
.

Remark 2.7:
The form
q
has signature
(3,b_{2}-3)
.
It is negative definite on primitive forms, and positive
definite on
\langle\Omega,\overline{\Omega},\omega\rangle
,
where
\omega
is a Kähler form.

Remark 2.8:
The BBF form is in general not unimodular. We shall use it to embed

H_{2}(M,{\mathbb{Z}})
into
H^{2}(M,{\mathbb{Q}})
as an overlattice of
H^{2}(M,{\mathbb{Z}})
. This allows us to view the classes of curves on
M

as rational
(1,1)
-classes and compute the BBF square of a curve which is a rational number (cf.
[
HT1
]
).

Example 2.9:
If
M
is a K3 surface, the BBF form is just the intersection form. If
M=S^{[n]}
for a K3 surface
S
,
we have an orthogonal direct sum decomposition of lattices
H^{2}(M,{\mathbb{Z}})=H^{2}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}e
, where
H^{2}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})
is equipped with the intersection form and
e^{2}=-2(n-1)
. In fact
e
is one half of the class of the diagonal, that is, the divisor of non-reduced subschemes of length
n
in
S
(this divisor is also the exceptional set of the Hilbert-Chow morphism for
S^{[n]}
.
This decomposition holds for IHS of K3
[n]
type (but the class
e
may cease to be algebraic).

2.3
Extremal curves

Let
Z\subset M
be an irreducible uniruled subvariety of a compact Kähler manifold
(M,\omega)
.
We call a rational curve
C\subset Z
minimal if it is of minimal degree with respect to the Kähler form,
i.e.
\int_{C}\omega
is minimal, among the curves passing through a general point of
Z
.

Remark 2.10:
Thanks to Mori’s bend-and-break lemma, there is the following necessary condition
for minimality: for general
x,y\in Z
, the space of deformations

Z_{x,y}
of
C
passing through
x,y
is 0-dimensional. Indeed, if
\dim Z_{x,y}>0
, we can use Mori lemma to deform
C
to a reducible curve which contains
x,y
. The area
\int_{C^{\prime}}\omega
of each
component of this union is strictly smaller than that of
C
.

If
X
is IHS, minimal rational curves on
X
have the following remarkable properties (see for instance
[
AV1
]
).

Proposition 2.11:
Let
X
be IHS of dimension
2n
, then a minimal rational curve
C
(with respect to some maximal uniruled subvariety
Z
) deforms together with
X
whenever its cohomology class remains of type
(1,1)
.
The space of deformations of
C
within
X
is of dimension
2n-2
. The subvariety
Z
is coisotropic and can be of any dimension between
n
and
2n-1
. Its codimension in
X
is equal to the relative dimension of its rational quotient fibration.

Definition 2.12:
A rational curve is called
MBM
if it is minimal and
its cohomology class has negative self-intersection.

One reason why we are especially interested in negative
self-intersection rational curves is that they determine
the Kähler cone
\operatorname{Kah}(M)
(or the ample cone in the
projective case) inside the

positive cone
. The latter is defined as the connected component of the set

\{\eta\in H^{1,1}(M,{\mathbb{R}})\ \ |\ \ (\eta,\eta)>0\}

(2.1)

which contains the Kähler cone
\operatorname{Kah}(M)
. Note that the BBF form on
H^{1,1}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
has signature
(1,b_{2}-3)

and therefore the set (
2.1
)
has two connected components.

Theorem 2.13:
(Huybrechts, Boucksom, see
[
Bou
]
)
The Kähler cone of
M
is the set of all
\eta\in\operatorname{Pos}(M)

such that
(\eta,C)>0
for all rational curves
C
.

Clearly we may consider only negative rational curves, as by the Hodge index theorem the positive classes never have zero intersection with curves of positive square. If moreover the class of such a curve cannot be written as a sum
of two other such classes, it is called an
extremal
rational curve. The Kähler cone is locally polyhedral (with
some round pieces in the boundary), and its faces are
orthogonal complements to the classes of extremal curves. Note that an MBM curve as in
2.3
is extremal on a
generic deformation, as only its class, up to proporionality, survives as a
(1,1)
-class there.

2.4
MBM classes

A minimal rational curve may split, and thus cease to be
minimal, under a deformation. To put the things into a
deformation-invariant context, we have introduced the
notion of
MBM classes
in
[
AV1
]
. These are,
up to a rational multiple, monodromy transforms (see next
subsection for the definition of the monodromy) of the classes of
extremal rational curves on IHS birational models of
M
.
Note that if
M
and
M^{\prime}
are two birational IHS, or, more
generally, two birational manifolds with trivial canonical
bundle, then
H^{2}(M)
is naturally identified with

H^{2}(M^{\prime})
. The following is a reformulation of

[
AV1
, Theorem 1.17]
.

Theorem 2.14:
Let
\eta
be the cohomology class of an extremal curve on a
hyperkähler manifold, and

(M_{1},\eta)
be obtained as a deformation of
(M,\eta)

in a family such that
\eta
remains of type
(1,1)

for all fibers of this family. Then
M_{1}
is birational to
a hyperkähler manifold

M_{1}^{\prime}
such that
\gamma(\eta)
is a class of an extremal
curve on
M_{1}^{\prime}
for some
\gamma
in the monodromy group.

Remark 2.15:
Birational hyperkähler manifolds are deformation equivalent by a result of Huybrechts. In fact a more precise version of the theorem above
(see
[
AV3
]
) states that an extremal rational curve remains extremal on an
M_{1}^{\prime}
unseparable from
M_{1}
in the
Teichmüller space (see next section for preliminaries on Teichmüller space and more details).

MBM classes can be defined in several equivalent ways. The simplest one to formulate is as follows.

Definition 2.16:
A class
\eta\in H^{2}(M,{\mathbb{Z}})
which, up to a rational multiple, can be represented by an extremal curve
on a deformation on
M

is called
an MBM class
.

Remark 2.17:
Another equivalent definition (
[
AV1
]
):
An MBM class
\eta\in H^{2}(M)
is a Beauville-Bogomolov negative class such that
\lambda\eta
can be represented by a rational curve in some
(M,I)
when
(M,I)
is a non-algebraic deformation of
M
with
\operatorname{Pic}(M,I)_{\mathbb{Q}}=\langle\eta\rangle
.
In that case it can be represented by a rational curve in all such
(M,I)
.

The set of MBM classes is thus the same for a given deformation type. If a complex structure
I
is fixed, some of them are of type
(1,1)
, and among those, some correspond to extremal curves (that is, determine the Kähler cone), others correspond to extremal curves on birational models and their monodromy transforms. The following result further explains the connection between MBM classes and the Kähler cone.

Theorem 2.18:
(
[
AV1
]

Let
(M,I)
be a hyperkähler manifold and
S
the set of all
MBM classes of type (1,1) on
(M,I)
. Let
S^{\bot}
the union of all
orthogonal complement to all
s\in S
. Then
\operatorname{Kah}(M,I)
is
a connected component of
\operatorname{Pos}(M)\backslash S^{\bot}
.

Following E. Markman, we call these connected components

the Kähler-Weil chambers
.

3
Teichmüller spaces, Torelli theorem and monodromy

3.1
Teichmüller space

This subsection and the next one follow
[
V1
]
.

Definition 3.1:
Let
M
be a hyperkähler manifold, and

\operatorname{\sf Diff}_{0}(M)
a connected component of its diffeomorphism group
(the group of isotopies). Denote by
\operatorname{\sf Comp}

the space of complex structures of Kähler type on
M
, and let

\operatorname{\sf Teich}:=\operatorname{\sf Comp}/\operatorname{\sf Diff}_{0}(M)
. We call
it
the Teichmüller space.

Remark 3.2:
The space
\operatorname{\sf Teich}
is a finite-dimensional
complex manifold (
[
Cat
]
), not necessarily Hausdorff.

Definition 3.3:
Let
\operatorname{\sf Diff}(M)
be the group of
diffeomorphisms of
M
. We call
\Gamma:=\operatorname{\sf Diff}(M)/\operatorname{\sf Diff}_{0}(M)

the
mapping class group
.

The quotient
\operatorname{\sf Teich}/\Gamma

is identified with the set of equivalence classes of complex structures. However the notion of the moduli space usually does not make sense as the action of
\Gamma
on
\operatorname{\sf Teich}
is “too bad”.

By a result of Huybrechts, the quotient

\operatorname{\sf Comp}/\operatorname{\sf Diff}_{0}(M)
has only finitely many connected components. To simplify notations we denote by
\operatorname{\sf Teich}
the one which contains our given complex structure, that is, a parameter space for the deformations of
M
. The group
\Gamma
shall mean the subgroup of the mapping class group which preserves
\operatorname{\sf Teich}
.

Definition 3.4:
The
monodromy group

\operatorname{\sf Mon}
is the image of the representation of
\Gamma

on
H^{2}(M,{\mathbb{Z}})
.

Definition 3.5:
Let

\operatorname{\sf Per}:\;\operatorname{\sf Teich}{\>\longrightarrow\>}{\mathbb{P}}H^{2}(M,{\mathbb{C}})

map a complex structure
J
to the line
H^{2,0}(M,J)\in{\mathbb{P}}H^{2}(M,{\mathbb{C}})
.
The map
\operatorname{\sf Per}:\;\operatorname{\sf Teich}{\>\longrightarrow\>}{\mathbb{P}}H^{2}(M,{\mathbb{C}})
is
called
the period map
. The map
\operatorname{\sf Per}
maps
\operatorname{\sf Teich}
to an open subset of a
quadric

\operatorname{{\mathbb{P}}\sf er}:=\{l\in{\mathbb{P}}H^{2}(M,{\mathbb{C}})\ \ |\ \ q(l,l)=0,q(l,\overline{l})>0\}.

It is called
the period space
of
M
.

Remark 3.6:
The space
\operatorname{{\mathbb{P}}\sf er}
can be naturally
identified with

SO(b_{2}-3,3)/SO(2)\times SO(b_{2}-3,1)=\operatorname{Gr}_{++}(H^{2}(M,{\mathbb{R}}))

(the Grassmannian of Beauville-Bogomolov positive, oriented planes in
H^{2}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
).

Indeed, the group
SO(H^{2}(M,{\mathbb{R}}),q)=SO(b_{2}-3,3)
acts transitively on

\operatorname{{\mathbb{P}}\sf er}
, and
SO(2)\times SO(b_{2}-3,1)
is the
stabilizer of a point. More explicitly, we can associate
to each complex line a real plane generated by its real
and imaginary part.

3.2
Global Torelli theorem

Definition 3.7:
Let
M
be a topological space. We say that
x,y\in M

are
non-separable
(denoted by
x\sim y
)
if for any open sets
V\ni x,U\ni y
,
U\cap V\neq\emptyset
.

Definition 3.8:
The space
\operatorname{\sf Teich}_{b}:=\operatorname{\sf Teich}/\sim
is called
the
birational Teichmüller space
of
M
. Since
\operatorname{\sf Teich}_{b}

is obtained by gluing together all non-separable points,
it is also called
the Hausdorff reduction
of
\operatorname{\sf Teich}
.

Theorem 3.9:
(Global Torelli theorem for hyperkähler manifolds)

The period map

\operatorname{\sf Teich}_{b}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\operatorname{\sf Per}}}{{{\>\longrightarrow\>}}}\operatorname{{\mathbb{P}}\sf er}
is a diffeomorphism,
for each connected component of
\operatorname{\sf Teich}_{b}
.

Huybrechts in
[
H1
]
observed that unseparable points of
\operatorname{\sf Teich}
correspond to birational IHS,
up to the action of the mapping class group. The following precise description of unseparable points is due to Markman.

Theorem 3.10:

The points in
\operatorname{\sf Per}^{-1}(\operatorname{\sf Per}I)
correspond to the Kähler-Weil chambers (that is, to the connected components
of
\operatorname{Pos}(M)\backslash\cup z^{\bot}
where
z
runs through the MBM classes of type
(1,1)
).
Each chamber is the Kähler cone of the corresponding point in
\operatorname{\sf Per}^{-1}(\operatorname{\sf Per}I)
.

In particular, as a general hyperkähler manifold has no curves (the ones which have curves belong to a countable union of divisors in

\operatorname{\sf Teich}
),
\operatorname{\sf Teich}
is generically Hausdorff.

3.3
The space
\operatorname{\sf Teich}_{\alpha}^{{\operatorname{\sf min}}}

Let now
\alpha
be an MBM class, we are interested in the complex structures
I
from
\operatorname{\sf Teich}
such that
\alpha

is of type
(1,1)
in
I
. Denote the subspace of such
structures in
\operatorname{\sf Teich}
by
\operatorname{\sf Teich}_{\alpha}
.
Clearly,
\operatorname{\sf Teich}_{\alpha}=\operatorname{\sf Per}^{-1}(\alpha^{\bot})
.
From
3.2
it is clear that
\operatorname{\sf Teich}_{\alpha}

is not Hausdorff even generically (indeed each complex structure
I
in
\operatorname{\sf Teich}_{\alpha}
has at least two
Kähler-Weil chambers in which
\alpha^{\bot}\subset H^{1,1}(I)
divides the positive cone). On the other hand,

\operatorname{\sf Teich}_{\alpha}
is naturally divided in two halves, according to whether
{\alpha}
is negative or positive on the
Kähler classes. In the “positive” half
\operatorname{\sf Teich}_{\alpha}^{+}
take the part formed by the complex structures whose
Kähler cones are adjacent to
{\alpha}^{\bot}
.

Definition 3.11:
The space thus defined is called
\operatorname{\sf Teich}_{\alpha}^{{\operatorname{\sf min}}}
. In other words
\operatorname{\sf Teich}_{\alpha}^{{\operatorname{\sf min}}}
parameterizes the complex
structures such that

{\alpha}
is the class of a minimal rational curve, up to a positive multiple.

Remark 3.12:
It is clear from this description that
\operatorname{\sf Teich}_{\alpha}^{{\operatorname{\sf min}}}
is generically Hausdorff and that
\operatorname{\sf Per}

is an isomorphism from
\operatorname{\sf Teich}_{\alpha,b}^{{\operatorname{\sf min}}}
to
\alpha^{\bot}\subset\operatorname{{\mathbb{P}}\sf er}
.

3.4
Monodromy for manifolds of K3
[n]
type

The monodromy group is very important for understanding the geometry of IHS manifolds, but in the general (“classification-free”) case its structure remains somewhat mysterious. One knows, thanks to Markman
[
Ma2
]
, that the
reflections in the classes of uniruled divisors are in
\operatorname{\sf Mon}
, and this already has nontrivial consequences. But it is not clear how to decide whether a given MBM class
is “divisorial” or not (that is, whether the corresponding rational curves cover a divisor or a subvariety of higher codimension).

A lot of work has been done on the known examples of
IHS. For manifolds of K3
[n]
type, Markman proved the
following.

Theorem 3.13:
(
[
Ma3
, Theorem 1.2]
)
The monodromy group
\operatorname{\sf Mon}
is the subgroup of

O(H^{2}(M,{\mathbb{Z}}))
generated by the reflections in the classes of
square
-2
and and the antireflections in those of square

2
, that is, by the transformations

\rho_{u}(x)=\frac{-2x}{q(u)}+q(x,u)u
, where the

4
Birational contractions and invariance by deformation

4.1
Birational contractions

In this section we recall the Kawamata base point free theorem which implies that extremal rational curves
on projective IHS manifolds are contractible, as well as a more recent observation by Bakker and Lehn concerning
the nonprojective case.

Definition 4.1:
A line bundle
L
on a compact complex k-fold
M
is
nef
if
c_{1}(L)
lies in the closure of the
Kähler cone,
big
if
H^{0}(M,L^{\otimes N})=O(k^{N})
for
N
sufficiently big and sufficiently divisible,
and
semiample
if
L^{\otimes N}
is generated by its global sections for such
N
.

Theorem 4.2:
(Kawamata base point free theorem,
[
K1
, Theorem 4.3]
)

Let
L
be a nef line bundle on a projective
M
such that
L^{\otimes N}\otimes{\cal O}(-K_{M})
is nef and big for some
N
.
Then
L
is semiample.

For manifolds with trivial canonical bundle this just means that big and nef bundles are semiample.

It follows that the sections of a suitable power of a big
and nef bundle
L
on such manifolds define a
birational contraction (that is, a birational morphism with connected fibers)
\varphi:M{\>\longrightarrow\>}Y
.

Suppose now that
M
is a projective IHS manifold,

\alpha
is an MBM class and the given complex structure

I
on
M
is such that

I\in\operatorname{\sf Teich}_{\alpha}^{{\operatorname{\sf min}}}
, so that
\alpha
is in fact the class of an extremal curve: the hyperplane
\alpha^{\bot}
in

H^{1,1}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
contains a
wall of the Kähler cone. Since
M
is projective, the interior of this wall contains an integral
(1,1)
-class which by Lefschetz theorem
is
c_{1}(L)
for a line bundle
L
. This line bundle is nef, and also big. Indeed its BBF square is positive, and hence so is
c_{1}(L)^{2n}
.
Therefore a power of
L
defines a birational morphism contracting exactly the curves of class proportional to
\alpha
.

It turns out that extremal curves can be contracted also in the non-projective case. This is essentially due to Bakker and Lehn in
[
BL
]
. More precisely, putting together some results in
[
AV1
]
,
[
BL
]
and
[
AV3
]
gives the following.

Theorem 4.3:
(
[
BL
, Proposition 4.10]
,
[
AV3
, Corollary
5.3]
)

Let
M
be an IHS manifold and
\alpha

the class of an extremal rational curve on
M
. Then there
exists a birational contraction
\varphi:M{\>\longrightarrow\>}Y

contracting to points the curves whose class is
proportional to
\alpha
. Moreover, for every component
Z
of the
exceptional set, the fibers of
\varphi|_{Z}
are rationally
connected, and
\varphi|_{Z}
is the rational quotient
fibration of
Z
.

In particular the uniruled subvarieties of
M
covered by deformations of an extremal rational curve are irreducible components of the
exceptional sets of birational contractions. We sometimes refer to these as
MBM loci
.

4.2
Invariance by deformation

The following is the main result of

[
AV3
]
.

Theorem 4.4:
Let
\alpha
be an MBM class of type
(1,1)

on a hyperkähler manifold
M
with
b_{2}(M)>5
. Assume that
M
and its deformation
M^{\prime}
are both in
\operatorname{\sf Teich}_{\alpha}^{{\operatorname{\sf min}}}
, so that

\alpha
is a class of an extremal rational curve on both.
Then the corresponding contraction centers on
M
and
M^{\prime}
are sent one to another by a diffeomorphism
\Psi:M\to M^{\prime}
preserving the fibers of the contraction. If moreover neither
M
nor

M_{1}
has maximal Picard rank,
\Psi
can be chosen identifying the contracted extremal curves and thus induces the biholomorphism of the fibers provided that they are normal.

Remark 4.5:
Clearly,
H^{1,1}(M)
is obtained as orthogonal complement
to the 2-dimensional space
\langle\operatorname{Re}\Omega,\operatorname{Im}\Omega\rangle
,
where
\Omega
is the cohomology class of the holomorphic
symplectic form. Then
\operatorname{Pic}(M)=H^{1,1}(M)\cap H^{2}(M,{\mathbb{Z}})

has maximal rank only if the plane
\langle\operatorname{Re}\Omega,\operatorname{Im}\Omega\rangle

is rational. There is at most countable number of such
M
.

Remark 4.6:
Existence of
\Psi
follows from ergodicity of mapping class
group action, global Torelli theorem, Bakker-Lehn
contractibility result, and Thom-Mathers stratification
of proper real analytic maps.

We set
t=2(k-1)
so that
q(e)=-t
, and we eventually replace
z
by a multiple in order to write uniformly
z=tx+be
,
b\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
x
primitive.
We first remark is that one may suppose that
|b|\leqslant t/2
. Indeed
tx+be
has the same square as
tx^{\prime}+(b\pm t)e
for some

x^{\prime}\in H^{2}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})
(the equation
t^{2}q(x)-tb^{2}=t^{2}q(x^{\prime})-t(b^{2}\pm 2tb+t^{2})
on
q(x^{\prime})
has a solution since the terms
tb^{2}
cancel and the other ones are divisible by

2t^{2}
), and when
x^{\prime}
is primitive the two have the same
divisibility: indeed by
6
this divisibility is g.c.d.
(t,bt)
resp. g.c.d.
(t,(b\pm t)t)
, and the two are clearly equal. Therefore
tx+be
and
tx^{\prime}+(b\pm t)e
are in the same monodromy orbit.

We know that
q(z)
is negative. For small
t
it means that
q(z)\leqslant-tb^{2}
: indeed this is the case when
q(x)
is non-positive, and when
t\leqslant 8
(that is,
k\leqslant 5
), already for
q(x)=2
one has
2t^{2}-b^{2}t\geqslant 0
provided
|b|\leqslant t/2
. Therefore the matrix of
q
on
\langle z,e\rangle
is negative semidefinite, and one concludes in the same way as before.

Starting from
k=6
, the statement fails. Indeed, let
k=6
, so
t=10
, and
z=2x-e
where
x
is primitive of square 2, then
z^{2}=-2
but the determinant of
q
on
\langle z,e\rangle
is negative and will remail negative when we
replace
z
by a conjugate.

Remark 6.5:
In the last case mentioned, that is,
k=6
, the computation above shows that the only case when the pair
(M,z)
cannot be deformed to the Hilbert scheme of a nonprojective surface with cyclic Picard group
is exactly as described:
z^{2}=-2
,
z=2x-e
with
x
orthogonal to
e
,
x
of square
2
. On a generic deformation where

e
becomes a Hodge class,
M
becomes the Hilbert scheme of a

K3
surface
S
with Picard group generated by
x
of square
2
. Such an
S
is a double covering of
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
and
the MBM curve has class
z/2
. Up to monodromy, by
5
and the unicity statement (
3.4
), it comes from a
g_{4}^{1}
on a curve of genus 2 (the inverse image of a line under the covering map is of genus 2). Its MBM locus is birational to a
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
-bundle over a product of a K3 surface with the symmetric square of a K3 surface.

This evokes the paper
[
KLM
]
where many rational curves on Hilbert schemes of a K3 surface and on generalized Kummer varieties are constructed from special linear systems on (possibly nodal) curves via Brill-Noether theory. Notice that in this particular case no Brill-Noether theory is needed, since obviously any curve of genus 2 carries a
g_{4}^{1}
. It seems likely and could be interesting to verify that such a description can be given in general.

Two-weight rank-metric codes
Ferdinando Zullo 1 , Olga Polverino 1 , Paolo Santonastaso 1 and John Sheekey 2

Abstract
Two-weight linear codes are linear codes in which any nonzero codeword can have only two possible distinct weights. Those in the Hamming metric have proven to be very interesting for their connections with authentication codes, association schemes, strongly regular graphs, and secret sharing schemes. In this paper, we characterize two-weight codes in the rank metric, answering a recent question posed by Pratihar and Randrianarisoa.



I
Introduction

Rank-metric codes have garnered significant attention in recent decades, owing to their applications and intriguing connections with various mathematical objects, such as in linear random network coding, see
[
5
]
. Nevertheless, the origin of rank-metric codes traces its roots to Delsarte
[
14
]
in 1978, and later to Gabidulin in
[
15
]
and Roth in
[
24
]
.

Most of the focus on the study of rank-metric codes has regarded the linear case, that is
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspaces of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
, where
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
is equipped with the rank distance, defined as follows: for any
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}),y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
then

d(x,y)=w(x-y)=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\langle x_{1}-y_{1},\ldots,x_{n}-y_{n}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}),

where
w(x-y)
denotes the
rank weight
of
x-y
.

In this paper, a
rank-metric code
is any
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
.
Let
\C\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
be a rank-metric code. We will write that
\C
is an
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code if
k
is the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-dimension of
\C
and
d
is its minimum distance, that is

d=\min\{d(x,y)\colon x,y\in\C,x\neq y\}.

When the minimum distance is not known or is irrelevant, we simply write
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
.

It is possible to prove a Singleton-like bound for a rank-metric code.

Theorem I.1
.

(see
[
14
]
)
Let
\C\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
be an
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code.
Then

mk\leq\max\{m,n\}(\min\{n,m\}-d+1).

(1)

An
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code is called
Maximum Rank Distance code
(or shortly
MRD code
) if its parameters attains the bound (
1
).
A family of MRD codes has been shown in
[
14
,
15
,
24
]
.

In the classical Hamming-metric setting, well-studied codes are those such that the weight of its nonzero elements can only assume two distinct values; see the well celebrated paper by Calderbank and Kantor
[
10
]
. These codes present a wide variety of properties, and their geometry can be very different, although some classification results have been proved; see e.g.
[
16
]
.

In this paper we analyze rank-metric codes
\C
which are
two-weight
codes, that is the rank of its nonzero elements can only assume two distinct values.
An example of two-weight code in the rank metric is given by MRD codes in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
with
n\leq m
and with minimum distance
n-1
.
The study of two-weight rank-metric codes was initiated by Pratihar and Randrianarisoa in
[
22
]
, considering
antipodal
two-weight rank-metric codes
\C
. An
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code is an
antipodal
code if there exists a codeword of weight
n
. They ask whether or not all of two-weight antipodal codes
arise
from MRD codes. Using a geometric description of rank-metric codes developed in
[
23
]
and also in
[
25
]
and by a clever characterization in terms of spreads, they prove a complete classification of antipodal two-weight rank-metric codes when the minimum rank distance is equal to half of the length.
More precisely, they proved that such a code is always induced by an MRD code over an extension of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
(see
[
22
]
and
[
21
]
).
This proved that in this case, two-weight codes in the rank metric have a strong geometric structure.

In this paper we give a more general geometric characterisation of antipodal two-weight codes, proving that, when
q
is large enough, all the two-weight rank-metric codes arise from well-studied combinatorial objects known as
scattered spaces
. Indeed, in Section
IV
, we prove that this is the case for all (not necessarily antipodal) two-weight codes. Moreover, using the lower bound on the dimension of scattered subspaces and their existence results, we are able to give a lower bound on the length of a two-weight code and a range of values for its length in which we can guarantee the existence of such codes.

II
Preliminaries

In this section we briefly recall some definitions and results we will need in the paper.

II-A

Linear sets

Let
V
be a
k
-dimensional vector space over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
and let
\Lambda=\mathrm{PG}(V,{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}})=\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{m})
.
Recall that, if
U
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace of
V
of dimension
n
, then the set of points

L_{U}=\{\langle{u}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}:{u}\in U\setminus\{{0}\}\}\subseteq\Lambda

is said to be an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-
linear set of rank
n
.
Let
P=\langle v\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}
be a point in
\Lambda
. The
weight of
P
in
L_{U}
is defined as

w_{U}(P)=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U\cap\langle v\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}).

If
N_{i}
denotes the number of points of
\Lambda
having weight
i\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
in
L_{U}
, the following relations hold:

|L_{U}|\leq\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1},

(2)

|L_{U}|=N_{1}+\ldots+N_{n},

(3)

N_{1}+N_{2}(q+1)+\ldots+N_{n}(q^{n-1}+\ldots+q+1)=q^{n-1}+\ldots+q+1.

(4)

Furthermore,
L_{U}
is called
scattered
if it has the maximum number
\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}
of points, or equivalently, if all points of
L_{U}
have weight one. Moreover, for a positive integer
e\mid m
, with
e<m
, we say that an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}
-subspace
U
of
V
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}
-scattered subspace
of
V
if

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}}(U\cap\langle v\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})\leq 1,

for any
v\in V
.
Blokhuis and Lavrauw provided the following bound on the rank of a scattered liner set.

Theorem II.1
.

(see
[
7
]
)
Let
L_{U}
be a scattered
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear set of rank
n
in
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{m})
, then

n\leq\frac{mk}{2}.

A scattered
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear set of rank
km/2
in
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{m})
is said to be a
maximum scattered
and
U
is said to be a maximum scattered
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace as well.

In the next result we summarize what is known on the existence of maximum scattered linear sets/subspaces.

Theorem II.2
.

(see
[
7
,
3
,
4
,
12
]
)
If
mk
is even then there exist maximum scattered subspaces in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
.

If
L_{U}
is a scattered
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear set for which
L_{U}
is not contained in another scattered
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear set is said to be
maximally scattered
and
U
is called a
maximally scattered
subspace. Clearly, maximum scattered linear sets are maximally scattered, but the converse does not hold in general; see e.g.
[
18
]
.

Now, we recall the notion of the dual of a linear set.
Let
\sigma\colon V\times V\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
be a nondegenerate reflexive bilinear form on the
k
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-vector space
V
and consider

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\sigma^{\prime}:&V\times V&\longrightarrow&{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\\
&(x,y)&\longmapsto&\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{m}/q}(\sigma(x,y)).\end{array}

So,
\sigma^{\prime}
is a nondegenerate reflexive bilinear form on
V
seen as an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector space of dimension
km
. Then we may consider
\perp
and
\perp^{\prime}
as the orthogonal complement maps defined by
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
. For an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear set in
\mathrm{PG}(V,{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}})
of rank
n
, the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear set
L_{U^{\perp^{\prime}}}
in
\mathrm{PG}(V,{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}})
of rank
km-n
is, called the
dual linear set of
L_{U}
with respect to
\sigma
.
The definition of the dual linear set does not depend on the choice of
\sigma
; see e.g.
[
20
, Proposition 2.5]
.

Moreover, we have the following relation between the weight of a subspace with respect to a linear set and the weight of its polar space with respect to the dual linear set.

Proposition II.3
.

(see
[
20
, Property 2.6]
)
Let
L_{U}\subseteq\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{m})
be a linear set of rank
n
. Let
\Omega_{s}=\mathrm{PG}(W,{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}})
be an
s
-dimensional projective space of
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{m})
.
Then

w_{U^{\perp^{\prime}}}(\Omega_{s}^{\tau})=w_{U}(\Omega_{s})+km-n-(s+1)m,

i.e.

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U^{\perp^{\prime}}\cap W^{\perp})=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U\cap W)+\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(V)

-\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U)-\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(W).

We refer to
[
19
]
and
[
20
]
for comprehensive references on linear sets.

II-B

Rank-metric codes and
q
-systems

Now, we recall the definition of equivalence between rank-metric codes in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
. An
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-linear isometry
\phi
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-linear map of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
that preserves the rank weight, i.e.
w(x)=w(\phi(x))
, for every
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
, or equivalently
d(x,y)=d(\phi(x),\phi(y))
, for every
x,y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
.
It is known that the group of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-linear isometries of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
equipped with rank distance is generated by the (nonzero) scalar
multiplications of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
and the general linear group
\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
, see e.g.
[
6
]
. So we say that two rank-metric codes
\C,\C^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
are (linearly) equivalent if there exists an isometry
\phi
such that
\phi(\C)=\C^{\prime}
.
Clearly, when studying equivalence of
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
codes the
action of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}
is trivial. This means that two
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
codes
\C
and
\C^{\prime}
are equivalent if and only if
there exists
A\in\mathrm{GL}(n,q)
such that

\C^{\prime}=\C A=\{vA:v\in\C\}
.
We will consider codes that are
nondegenerate
.

Definition II.4
.

An
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code
\C
is said to be
nondegenerate
if the columns of any generator matrix of
\C
are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linearly independent.

The geometric counterpart of rank-metric are the systems.

Definition II.5
.

An
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}

system

U
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
of dimension
n
, such that

\langle U\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
and

d=n-\max\left\{\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U\cap H)\mid H\textnormal{ is an ${\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}$-hyperpl. of }{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\right\}.

Moreover, two
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
systems
U
and
U^{\prime}
are
equivalent
if there exists an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-isomorphism
\varphi\in\mathrm{GL}(k,{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}})
such that

\varphi(U)=U^{\prime}.

We denote the set of equivalence classes of
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
systems by
\mathfrak{U}[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
.

Theorem II.6
.

(see
[
23
]
)
Let
\C
be an
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
rank-metric code and let
G
be an its generator matrix.
Let
U\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
be the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-span of the columns of
G
.
The rank weight of an element
xG\in\C
, with
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
is

w(xG)=n-\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U\cap x^{\perp}),

(5)

where
x^{\perp}=\{y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\colon x\cdot y=0\}
and
\cdot
denotes the standard dot product in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
. In particular,

d=n-\max\left\{\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U\cap H)\colon H\mbox{ is an }{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}\mbox{-hyperpl. of }{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\right\}.

(6)

Thanks to Theorem
II.6
, it can be proved that there is a one-to-one correspondence between equivalence classes of
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
codes and equivalence classes of
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
systems; see
[
2
,
23
]
.

Finally, we recall that by using the dual of a system associated to a code, it is possible to define a new code as follows, see
[
9
]
.

Definition II.7
.

Let
\C
be an
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
and let
U
be a system associated with
\C
. Assume that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U\cap\langle v\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})<m
, for any
v\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
.
Then a
geometric dual
of
\C
(with respect to
\perp^{\prime}
) is defined as
\C^{\prime}
, where
\C^{\prime}
is any code associated with the system
U^{\perp^{\prime}}
.

Note that the geometric dual of an
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code is an
[mk-n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code.

III
Auxiliary results on linear sets

The problem of determining bounds on the size of a linear set is a hard problem.
The following is a lower bound on the number of points of a linear set admitting a point of weight one (which generalizes
[
8
, Lemma 2.2]
).

Theorem III.1
.

(see
[
13
, Theorem 1.2]
)
Let
L_{U}
be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear set in
\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{m})
of rank
n
, such that there exists
P\in L_{U}
with
w_{U}(P)=1
.
Then

|L_{U}|\geq q^{n-1}+1.

We also refer to
[
1
]
for more bounds in higher dimensional projective spaces. We also recall the following recent result, which regards the linearity of a linear set.

Theorem III.2
.

(see
[
11
, Theorem 2]
)
Let
L_{U}
be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear set of rank
n
in
\mathrm{PG}(k-1,q^{m})
with
n\leq(k-1)m
and
m\leq q
.
Let
e=\min\{w_{U}(P)\colon P\in L_{U}\}
and suppose
e>1
. Then there exists a positive integer
t\geq e
such that
t\lvert m
and
L_{U}=L_{W}
where
W=\langle U\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{t}}}
.

In the following we use the above mentioned results to prove that if an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace
U
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{2}
meets every one-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{2}
either trivially or in an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace of dimension
e>0
, then
e\mid m
and
U
is
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}
-linear, as well.

Corollary III.3
.

Assume that
q\geq m
. Let
U
be an
n
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace of a
k
-dimensional vector space
V
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
. Suppose that
n\leq(k-1)m
,
\langle U\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}=V

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U\cap\langle w\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})\in\{0,e\},

,

and

is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}
-subspace of dimension

. In other words,

is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}
-scattered subspace of

.

Proof.

The assertion is clear if

. So suppose that

. First, assume that

. Let consider the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear set
L_{U}
which, by the assumption on

\frac{q^{n}-1}{q^{e}-1}

.
By Theorem

We may also assume that there exists a point

(q^{t})^{g-1}+1\leq\frac{q^{n}-1}{q^{e}-1}\leq\frac{q^{gt}-1}{q^{t}-1}.

(7)

IV
Two-weight linear rank-metric codes

In this section we completely describe two-weight codes. A way to construct two-weight codes is via scattered subspaces.

Construction IV.1
.

Let
U
be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}
-scattered subspace in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
such that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U)=n
and
\langle U\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
. A code
\C
having
U
as an its associated system is said to be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}
-scattered code
.

Note that, when
k=2
, an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}
-scattered code
\C
is an
[n,2,n-e]_{q^{m}/q}
code with nonzero weights
n-e
and
n
. In addition, when
e=1
,
\C
is MRD for any value of
n\leq m
.
The above construction can be extended via duality as follows.

Construction IV.2
.

Let
U
be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}
-scattered subspace in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
such that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U)=n
and
\langle U\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
. Consider
U^{\perp^{\prime}}
the dual of
U
.
Then
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U^{\perp^{\prime}})=km-n
,
\langle U^{\perp^{\prime}}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
and by Proposition
II.3

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U^{\perp^{\prime}}\cap H)\in\{(k-1)m-n,(k-1)m-n+e\}.

Consider
\C
the rank-metric code associated with
U^{\perp^{\prime}}
, then
\C
is a nondegenerate
[km-n,k,m-e]_{q^{m}/q}
code with nonzero weights
m-e
and
m
. In other words, the geometric dual of an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}
-scattered
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code is an
[km-n,k,m-e]_{q^{m}/q}
with nonzero weights
m-e
and
m
.

Remark IV.3
.

For an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}
-scattered subspace
U
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
such that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U)=n
and
\langle U\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
, it results that
\langle U^{\perp^{\prime}}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
. Indeed, suppose that
U^{\perp^{\prime}}
is contained in a hyperplane of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
, i.e.
\langle U^{\perp^{\prime}}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}\subseteq H
. This means that
H^{\perp}\subseteq U
and so
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(H^{\perp}\cap U)=m>e
, a contradiction.

In Construction
IV.2
, it can happen that the obtained codes are antipodal, for instance when
m=n
and
k=2
.

Remark IV.4
.

The
[km-n,k,m-e]_{q^{m}/q}
code
\C
from Construction
IV.2
is an MRD code if and only if

|\C|=q^{\max\{m,km-n\}(\min\{m,km-n\}-d+1)},

which can be read as

km=(km-n)(e+1),

(11)

since
n\leq km/2
via the bound of Theorem
II.1
. Again, by
II.1
, (
11
) implies that

km=(km-n)(e+1)\geq\frac{km}{2}(e+1),

from which
e=1
. Hence, from (
11
) it follows that
n=\frac{km}{2}
.

We will start by characterizing the antipodal two weight codes.

Our approach now is to use the geometric correspondence between rank-metric codes and systems of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{2}
. We will start by recalling some results for the antipodal case from
[
22
]
.

Proposition IV.5
.

Let
\C
be a nondegenerate antipodal two-weight
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code. Then
n\leq m
.

Theorem IV.6
.

(see
[
22
, Theorem 2]
)
Let
\C
be a nondegenerate
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code. If
\C
is an antipodal two-weight code then
k=2
.

Lemma IV.7
.

(see
[
22
, Corollary 1]
)
Let
\C
be a nondegenerate
[n,2,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code. If
\C
is an antipodal two-weight code then
n-d\mid n
.

Now, we are able to characterize antipodal codes, extending
[
22
]
.

Theorem IV.8
.

Assume that
q\geq m.
Let
\C
be a nondegenerate antipodal two-weight
[n,2,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code with
n=\ell(n-d)
. Then
n-d\mid m
and
\C
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n-d}}
-scattered code.

Proof.

Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{2}
be a system associated with the code
\C
and

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U\cap\langle v\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}^{\perp})\in\{0,n-d\},

for any
v\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{2}
.
Note that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U)=\ell(n-d)
.
By Corollary
III.3
, it follows that
n-d
divides
m
and
U
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n-d}}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{2}
with the property that

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n-d}}}(U\cap\langle w\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})\leq 1,

for any
w\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{2}
and hence by Theorem
II.6
the assertion follows.
∎

This answers to the question posed by Pratihar and Randrianarisoa in
[
22
]
, proving that every antipodal two-weight code is induced by an MRD code over an extension field of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.

Moreover, this result allows us to completely characterize
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-linear rank-metric codes with two distinct weights.

Remark IV.9
.

Note that, since a nondegenerate
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code always has at least one codeword having rank weight equal to
\min\{m,n\}
(see
[
2
, Proposition 3.11]
), a nondegenerate two-weight
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code admits nonzero weights
d
and
\min\{m,n\}
.

Let us start with the case
k=2
.

Corollary IV.10
.

Assume that
q\geq m
. Let
\C
be a nondegenerate two-weight
[n,2,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code. Suppose that
\C
is not antipodal. Then
m-d\mid m
and
\C
is a geometric dual of an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m-d}}
-scattered
[2m-n,2,m-n+d]_{q^{m}/q}
code.

Proof.

Let
U
be the system associated with
\C
. Let
d
and
d^{\prime}
be the two nonzero weights of
\C
. By Remark
IV.9
, we know that
d^{\prime}=m
and
m<n
, since
\C
is not antipodal.
Then by Theorem
II.6
we obtain that

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U\cap\langle v\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})\in\{n-d,n-m\},

and
n-d,n-m\neq 0
.
The dual
U^{\perp^{\prime}}
of
U
has dimension
2m-n<m
and

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U^{\perp^{\prime}}\cap\langle v\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})\in\{0,m-d\}.

We can then use Corollary
III.3
, obtaining that
U^{\perp^{\prime}}
is
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m-d}}
-linear, i.e.
U^{\perp^{\prime}}
is a scattered
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m-d}}
-subspace.
∎

We can now extend the above result to the remaining dimensions.

Theorem IV.11
.

Assume that
q\geq m
.
Let
\C
be a nondegenerate two-weight
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code with
k>2
. Then
m-d\mid m
,
m-d\mid n
and a system
U
associated with
\C
is the dual of an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m-d}}
-scattered subspace in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
.
In particular,
n\geq\frac{mk}{2}
.
Moreover,

\frac{km}{2(m-d)}\leq\frac{n}{m-d}\leq\frac{mk}{m-d}-1.

Proof.

By Remark
IV.9
, we know that the nonzero weights of
\C
are
d
and
m=\min\{m,n\}
, and
m<n
, since
\C
is not antipodal (otherwise
k=2
).
Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{k}
be a system associated with the code
\C
.
Then by Theorem
II.6
we obtain that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U\cap H)\in\{n-m,n-d\}
,
for any hyperplane of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
.
By Proposition
II.3
, it follows that

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U^{\perp^{\prime}}\cap H^{\perp})\in\{0,m-d\},

or in other words,

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U^{\perp^{\prime}}\cap\langle w\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})\in\{0,m-d\},

for any
w\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
. Let
S=\langle U^{\perp^{\prime}}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
and let
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}(S)=h
. If
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U^{\perp^{\prime}})>(h-1)m
then

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U^{\perp^{\prime}}\cap\langle w\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})=m-d

for any
w\in S\setminus\{\underline{0}\}
, and hence

(q^{m-d}-1)\frac{q^{hm}-1}{q^{m}-1}=q^{km-n}-1

which gives
d=0
, a contradiction. This means that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U^{\perp^{\prime}})\leq(h-1)m
and hence by Corollary
III.3
we get,
m-d\mid m
,
m-d\mid(km-n)
and
U^{\perp^{\prime}}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m-d}}
-scattered subspace of dimension
\frac{km-n}{m-d}
of
S\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
. In particular, applying the bound of Theorem
II.1
to
U^{\perp^{\prime}}
, we get
\frac{km-n}{m-d}\leq\frac{hm}{2(m-d)}
, and hence
n\geq\frac{mk}{2}
.
Finally, since
\C
is a two-weight code,
n<km
and hence
n\leq km-(m-d)
.
∎

In the above theorem, we classified all the two-weight rank-metric codes as those codes arising from the dual of scattered subspaces over extensions of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. In other terms, such codes arise from a rank-metric code in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{\prime m^{\prime}}}^{n^{\prime}}
, where
q^{\prime}=q^{m-d}
,
m^{\prime}=m/(m-d)
and
n^{\prime}=n/(m-d)
, see
[
21
]
.

We will now show that, under certain assumptions, two-weight rank-metric codes exists for any length satisfying the above bound.

Theorem IV.12
.

Let
m,k
and
d
be positive integers such that
d<m
,
(m-d)\mid m
and
mk/(m-d)
is even, then for any
q
and for any
n
multiple of
m-d
satisfying

\frac{km}{2(m-d)}\leq\frac{n}{m-d}\leq\frac{mk}{m-d}-1,

there exists a nondegenerate two-weight
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code
\C
.

Proof.

Because of Theorem
II.2
, there exists at least one scattered
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m-d}}
-subspace
W
with dimension
t
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{(q^{m-d})^{\frac{m}{m-d}}}^{k}
for any

1\leq t\leq mk/2(m-d).

Note that, since
d<m
and
W
is a scattered
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m-d}}
-subspace,
W
does not contain any
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
. This implies that any code associated with
U=W^{\perp^{\prime}}
is a non-degenerate two-weight
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code with
n=km-t(m-d)
.
∎

Another natural question regards whether or not the
puncturing
of a two-weight rank-metric code preserves the property of having exactly two weights. As we will see in the next result, this property strongly depends on whether or not the subspace is
maximally scattered
.

Remark IV.13
.

The puncturing operation has been defined for rank-metric codes of matrices by multiplying every codeword by a fixed full-rank (not necessarily square) matrix. In order to have the analogue definition in vector terms, we need to consider a matrix
A\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n\times n^{\prime}}
, with
n^{\prime}\leq n
of rank
n^{\prime}
. The
punctured code of

\mathcal{C}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
via the matrix
A
is

\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\{(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})A\colon(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})\in\C\}.

Note that if
\mathcal{C}
is
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-linear, then
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-linear as well. Moreover, if
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
are have the same dimension, then the system associated with
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is contained in the system associated with
\mathcal{C}
.

Theorem IV.14
.

Assume that
q\geq m
. Let
\C
be a nondegenerate
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code with
d=m-1
.
Let
U
be an its associated system.
If
U^{\perp^{\prime}}
is maximally scattered then a punctured code of
\C
which has dimension
k
is either a two-weight code such that an its associated system is the dual of an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}
-scattered subspace, with
e>1
, or it has more than three weights.

Proof.

Let
W
be the system of a punctured code of
\C
. In particular, this means that
W
is contained in
U
and hence
W^{\perp^{\prime}}\supset U^{\perp^{\prime}}
.
Since
U^{\perp^{\prime}}
is maximally scattered, then
W^{\perp^{\prime}}
is not scattered over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. So, either
W^{\perp^{\prime}}
is a
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{e}}
-scattered subspace (for some positive integer
e
) or it is not scattered.
This implies that the code associated with
W
either it is a two-weight code associated with a scattered subspace over a proper extension of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
or it has more than three nonzero weights.
∎

Apart from the maximum scattered subspaces (which give MRD codes), very few examples of maximally scattered
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspaces are known, see
[
17
, Example 3.2]
.

Criticality of the O ⁡ ( N ) O(N) universality via global solutions to nonperturbative fixed-point equations
Yang-yang Tan, Chuang Huang, Yong-rui Chen, Wei-jie Fu

Abstract
Fixed-point equations in the functional renormalization group approach are integrated from large to vanishing field, where an asymptotic potential in the limit of large field is implemented as initial conditions. This approach allows us to obtain a global fixed-point potential with high numerical accuracy, that incorporates the correct asymptotic behavior in the limit of large field. Our calculated global potential is in good agreement with the Taylor expansion in the region of small field, and it also coincides with the Laurent expansion in the regime of large field. Laurent expansion of the potential in the limit of large field for general case, that the spatial dimension d d is a continuous variable in the range 2 ≤ d ≤ 4 2\leq d\leq 4 , is obtained. Eigenfunctions and eigenvalues of perturbations near the Wilson-Fisher fixed point are computed with the method of eigenperturbations. Critical exponents for different values of d d and N N of the O ⁡ ( N ) O(N) universality class are calculated.



I
Introduction

The renormalization group (RG) has been recognized as a powerful theoretical tool to study phase transitions and critical phenomena since the seminal work by Wilson
et al.

Wilson 1971a
;
Wilson 1971b
;
Wilson and Fisher 1972
;
Wilson and Kogut 1974
. A second-order phase transition is regarded to be located on the critical surface of a stable fixed point of RG flow equations. Critical behaviors of the phase transition, such as universal critical exponents and their relations to the dimension and symmetries, are closely connected to relevant eigenperturbations in the proximity of the fixed point, see, e.g.,
Ma 2000
for more details.

When the spatial dimension
d
is smaller than and close to 4, that is, the parameter
\varepsilon\equiv 4-d
being a positive and small quantity, the RG flows can be expanded in powers of
\varepsilon

Wilson and Fisher 1972
;
Wilson and Kogut 1974
. In other words, one is able to use the technique of perturbation theory to compute, e.g., critical exponents, in powers of
\varepsilon
order by order. Nevertheless, the reliability of perturbation theory is gradually loosened with the increase of the expansion parameter
\varepsilon
, e.g., in the case that the dimension is
d=3
, or even approaches toward 2. In such cases, nonperturbative RG flows are indispensable.

The functional renormalization group (fRG) provides us with a convenient framework to deal with nonperturbative RG flows
Wetterich 1993
. In the fRG approach physics of nonperturbation theory are encoded in a self-consistent flow equation for the effective action or effective potential. For more details about the method of fRG and its applications in studies of nonperturbative physics, such as the strongly correlated QCD, one is referred to, e.g.,
Berges et al. 2002
;
Pawlowski 2007
;
Braun 2012
;
Dupuis et al. 2021
;
Fu 2022
for reviews and
Braun et al. 2016
;
Mitter et al. 2015
;
Rennecke 2015
;
Cyrol et al. 2016
;
Cyrol et al. 2018a
;
Cyrol et al. 2018b
;
Fu et al. 2020
;
Braun et al. 2020
;
Fu et al. 2022
for recent progresses in first-principle fRG calculations in QCD.

Truncations for the RG flows in the fRG approach can be usually made in a systematic way, such as the derivative expansions (DE)
Balog et al. 2019
;
De Polsi et al. 2020
, which provides us with a set of closed flow equations or fixed-point equations for the effective potential and other dressing functions. In order to obtain scaling solutions of RG flows, one can evolve the flow equations into a scale-independent solution
Bohr et al. 2001
;
Papp et al. 2000
, or directly solve the fixed-point equations. To solve, e.g., the fixed-point equation for the potential, one can either use the Taylor expansion to expand the potential around vanishing or finite field if the dimension
d
is not too small
Litim 2002
, or integrate the fixed-point equation starting from the vanishing field
Codello 2012
. It is found that direct integration of the fixed-point equation starting from the vanishing field would end up in a singularity at a finite field
Codello 2012
, which implies that a proper treatment at large field is necessary. Studies of the global solution to the fixed-point equation with the correct asymptotic behavior in the limit of large field in the fRG approach have made progress in recent years. A combined technique with both small and large field expansion is used to obtain the global potential of the fixed point
Jüttner et al. 2017
. Moreover, pseudospectral methods are also employed to construct global solutions
Borchardt and Knorr 2015
or to evolve the flow equation
Chen et al. 2021
. Recently, discontinuous Galerkin methods have been developed to resolve the flow equation of a global potential
Grossi and Wink 2019
;
Grossi et al. 2021
;
Ihssen et al. 2022
.

In calculating the global potential to a fixed-point equation, the whole range of the argument of potential, i.e., the field, is usually segmented into several subranges, e.g. the region of large field and that of small field. Different methods are applied in different regimes, such as, Taylor expansion in the region of small field and numerical calculations in that of large field
Jüttner et al. 2017
, a standard Chebyshev series in small field and a rational Chebyshev series in large field
Borchardt and Knorr 2015
. A global potential is finally obtained by connecting the solutions in different regimes. In this work, we try to simplify the procedure, and would like to directly integrate the fixed-point equation starting at a sufficient large value of field, where the asymptotic potential in the limit of large field is implemented as initial conditions. As a consequence, a global fixed-point potential with high numerical accuracy is resolved after the integration is finished at vanishing field, that naturally incorporates the correct asymptotic behaviors both in the limit of vanishing and in the large field. Moreover, we would like to discuss the Laurent expansion of the potential in the limit of large field for a general case that the spatial dimension
d
is a continuous variable in the range
2\leq d\leq 4
.

This paper is organized as follows: In Sec.
II
we give a brief introduction about the fRG approach with the flow equation of the effective potential and the fixed-point equation, as well as some notations used thereafter. The Taylor expansion of the potential around vanishing or finite field and the Laurent expansion in the limit of large field are discussed in Sec.
III
. In Sec.
IV
we discuss how the fixed-point equation is integrated out with the asymptotic potential in the limit of large field implemented. Moreover, eigenperturbations near the fixed point are also discussed. In Sec.
V
we present numerical results on fixed-point potentials and critical exponents. Finally, a summary with outlook is given in Sec.
VI
.

II
Flow equation of the effective potential

We begin with a RG scale
k
-dependent effective action for the
O(N)
scalar theory, which reads

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k}[\phi]=

\displaystyle\int\mathrm{d}^{d}x\left[\frac{1}{2}Z_{\phi,k}\left(\partial_{\mu}\phi_{a}\right)^{2}+V_{k}(\rho)\right]\,,

(1)

with
\mu=1,\,2,\cdots d
and
a=0,\,1,\,2,\cdots N-1
, where
d
is the spatial dimension and
N
is the number of components for the scalar field. Summations over the subscripts
\mu
and
a
in Eq.
1
are assumed. The effective potential
V_{k}(\rho)
is
O(N)
invariant with
\rho=\phi^{2}/2
and
\phi^{2}=\phi_{a}\phi_{a}
. Note that in Eq.
1
we have employed the local potential approximation (LPA) supplemented with a
k
-dependent wave function renormalization
Z_{\phi,k}
, which is usually called as the truncation of
\mathrm{LPA}^{\prime}
.

The evolution of effective action in Eq.
1
with the RG scale is described by the Wetterich equation
Wetterich 1993
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Gamma_{k}[\phi]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}\Big\{\big(\partial_{t}R_{k}\big)G_{k}[\phi]\Big\}\,,

(2)

with the RG time
t\equiv\ln(k/\Lambda)
, where
\Lambda
is a reference scale, e.g., the UV cutoff or the initial evolution scale. The propagator in Eq.
2
reads

\displaystyle G_{k}[\phi]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma^{(2)}_{k}[\phi]+R_{k}}\,,

(3)

with

\displaystyle\Gamma^{(2)}_{k}[\phi]

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\delta^{2}\Gamma_{k}[\phi]}{\delta\phi^{2}}\,.

(4)

Here, the bilinear regulator
R_{k}
in Eq.
2
and Eq.
3
is devised to suppress quantum fluctuations of momenta smaller than the RG scale, while make others unaltered, and see, e.g.,
Pawlowski 2007
;
Fu 2022
for more details. In this work we adopt the flat regulator
Litim 2000
;
Litim 2001
, which reads

\displaystyle R_{k}(q)

\displaystyle=Z_{\phi,k}q^{2}r(q^{2}/k^{2})\,,

(5)

with

\displaystyle r(x)

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{1}{x}-1\Big)\Theta(1-x)\,,

(6)

where
\Theta(x)
is the Heaviside step function.

The flow equation of the effective potential in Eq.
1
is readily obtained from the Wetterich equation in Eq.
2
, viz.,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}V_{k}(\rho)=

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}k^{d}\left[\frac{1}{1+\bar{m}_{\sigma,k}^{2}}+\frac{N-1}{1+\bar{m}_{\pi,k}^{2}}\right]\,,

(7)

with the coefficient

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{d/2}}\frac{1}{\Gamma(d/2)}\left[(2-\eta)\frac{2}{d}+\eta\frac{2}{d+2}\right]\,,

(8)

where the masses of the longitudinal and transversal modes are denoted by

\displaystyle\bar{m}_{\sigma,k}^{2}\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Z_{\phi,k}k^{2}}\Big(V^{\prime}_{k}(\rho)+2\rho V^{(2)}_{k}(\rho)\Big)\,,

(9)

\displaystyle\bar{m}_{\pi,k}^{2}\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Z_{\phi,k}k^{2}}V^{\prime}_{k}(\rho)\,,

(10)

respectively, and the anomalous dimension reads

\displaystyle\eta=

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial_{t}Z_{\phi,k}}{Z_{\phi,k}}\,.

(11)

It is more convenient to work with renormalized and dimensionless variables, such that the explicit dependence of the RG scale
k
is absorbed. To that end, one introduces

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}=

\displaystyle k^{-(d-2)}Z_{\phi,k}\rho\,,\qquad u_{k}(\bar{\rho})=k^{-d}V_{k}(\rho)\,.

(12)

Hence, the flow equation of the effective potential in Eq.
7
turns out to a flow of
u_{k}(\bar{\rho})
with
\bar{\rho}
fixed, that is,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u(\bar{\rho})=

\displaystyle-d\,u(\bar{\rho})+(d-2+\eta)\,\bar{\rho}\,u^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})

\displaystyle+\mathscr{C}\left[\frac{1}{1+u^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})+2\bar{\rho}u^{(2)}(\bar{\rho})}+\frac{N-1}{1+u^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})}\right]\,,

(13)

where the subscript
k
for
u_{k}
is not shown explicitly. In the truncation of
\mathrm{LPA}^{\prime}
, the anomalous dimension can be extracted from the momentum dependence of the two-point correlation function of the transversal
\pi
mode or the longitudinal
\sigma
mode. Both modes give the same anomalous dimension for the Gaußian fixed point, while there is indeed a difference in the case of, e.g., the Wilson-Fisher (WF) fixed point
Wilson and Fisher 1972
. In this work we adopt the anomalous dimension of the
\pi
mode, which reads

\displaystyle\eta=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{d-2}\pi^{d/2}\Gamma(1+\frac{d}{2})}\frac{\bar{\rho}_{0}u^{(2)}(\bar{\rho}_{0})^{2}}{\left[1+2\bar{\rho}_{0}u^{(2)}(\bar{\rho}_{0})\right]^{2}}\,,

(14)

where
\bar{\rho}_{0}
stands for the location of minimum of the potential
u(\bar{\rho})
. The equation of fixed points for the effective potential is obtained by demanding
\partial_{t}u=0
, that immediately yields

\displaystyle(d-2+\eta)\,\bar{\rho}\,u^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})-d\,u(\bar{\rho})

\displaystyle+\mathscr{C}\left[\frac{1}{1+u^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})+2\bar{\rho}u^{(2)}(\bar{\rho})}+\frac{N-1}{1+u^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})}\right]=0\,.

(15)

III
Local solutions of the fixed-point equation

Prior to the discussion of global solutions to the fixed-point equation in Eq.
15
, we would like to have a brief review on two approaches that provide us with local information for the solution of effective potential, that is, the Taylor expansion at vanishing or finite field, and the Laurent expansion in terms of
1/\bar{\rho}
in the limit of
\bar{\rho}\to\infty
.

III.1
Taylor expansion at vanishing or finite field

The most straightforward method to solve the flow equation of effective potential in Eq.
13
is to expand the potential around the vanishing field
\bar{\rho}=0
, to wit,

\displaystyle u(\bar{\rho})\simeq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\mathrm{tr}}}\frac{\lambda_{n}}{n!}\bar{\rho}^{n}\,,

(16)

where the field-independent term is ignored, and
N_{\mathrm{tr}}
is the maximal order of the Taylor expansion used in a calculation. Inserting Eq.
16
into Eq.
13
, one is led to a set of flow equations for the expansion coefficients of effective potential, i.e.,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\lambda_{n}\equiv

\displaystyle\beta_{n}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\cdots,\lambda_{n+1})\,,

(17)

where the
\beta
function of order
n
is a function of couplings up to order of
n+1
. Evidently, the fixed points are determined by
\partial_{t}\lambda_{n}^{*}=0
, to wit,

\displaystyle\beta_{n}(\lambda_{1}^{*},\lambda_{2}^{*},\cdots,\lambda_{n+1}^{*})=0\,,

(18)

which constitute a set of closed equations with number
N_{\mathrm{tr}}
. Critical behaviors of flows near a fixed point is readily analyzed by linearizing the flows in Eq.
17
near the fixed point with
\lambda_{n}\simeq\lambda_{n}^{*}+\delta\lambda_{n}
, where
\delta\lambda_{n}
is a small quantity. Hence, one arrives at

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\delta\lambda_{n})=

\displaystyle\sum_{n^{\prime}=1}^{N_{\mathrm{tr}}}M_{nn^{\prime}}\delta\lambda_{n^{\prime}}\,,

(19)

with the stability matrix
M
given by

\displaystyle M_{nn^{\prime}}=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\beta_{n}}{\partial\lambda_{n^{\prime}}}\bigg|_{\lambda=\lambda^{*}}\,,

(20)

whose eigenvalues provide us with the critical exponents pertinent to the fixed point, see, e.g.,
Litim 2002
for more detailed discussions.

The Taylor expansion at vanishing field in Eq.
16
is efficient and reliable to compute, e.g., critical exponents, when the spatial dimension is
d\to 4
, or at least not far away from
d=4
. A naive power counting immediately yields the dimension of
\lambda_{n}
, i.e.,
[\lambda_{n}]=2n-(n-1)d
, if the anomalous dimension is assumed to be vanishing for the moment. Irrelevance of the expansion coefficient
\lambda_{n}
demands
[\lambda_{n}]<0
, that leaves us with

\displaystyle n>

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d-2}\,.

(21)

Therefore, as the spatial dimension is approaching
d=2
from above, the required expansion order
N_{\mathrm{tr}}
in Eq.
16
increases significantly, due to the rapidly increased number of relevant parameters. Generically, the convergence of Eq.
16
becomes more and more difficult as
d\to 2
.

A natural extension of the Taylor expansion at vanishing field is to expand the effective potential at a finite field, which is able to alleviate the aforementioned problem born by the vanishing field expansion in some degree. This finite-field expansion reads

\displaystyle u(\bar{\rho})\simeq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\mathrm{tr}}}\frac{\lambda_{n}}{n!}\big(\bar{\rho}-\kappa\big)^{n}\,,

(22)

where the expansion point
\kappa
can be chosen to be
k
-independent or dependent. Moreover, it is convenient to choose
\kappa=\bar{\rho}_{0}
being the minimum of the function
u(\bar{\rho})
. Substituting Eq.
22
into Eq.
13
one is able to obtain fixed points and their relevant critical exponents by using the similar method described above, which will not be elaborated on anymore.

III.2
Laurent expansion in the limit of large field

When the field in Eq.
22
is very large, e.g.,
\bar{\rho}/\bar{\rho}_{0}\gg 1
, with
\bar{\rho}_{0}
being the minimum of
u(\bar{\rho})
, the nonlinear terms in the square bracket in Eq.
15
can be safely neglected, and then the asymptotic behavior of
u(\bar{\rho})
in the limit of large field is readily obtained, as follows

\displaystyle u(\bar{\rho})\sim

\displaystyle\,\gamma\,{\bar{\rho}}^{d/(d-2+\eta)}\,,\qquad\bar{\rho}\to\infty\,,

(23)

with a constant
\gamma
. The missing subleading terms on the right side of Eq.
23
can be formulated with a Laurent expansion in terms of
1/\bar{\rho}
. Firstly, let us consider the case that the leading power
d/(d-2+\eta)
is an integer
Litim and Marchais 2017
;
Jüttner et al. 2017
, and a more general case is discussed in Sec.
III.2.1
. The expansion in the limit of large field reads

\displaystyle u(\bar{\rho})\simeq

\displaystyle\,\gamma\,{\bar{\rho}}^{d/(d-2+\eta)}\left[1+\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\mathrm{tr}}}\gamma_{n}\left(\frac{1}{\bar{\rho}}\right)^{n}\right]\,.

(24)

In the same way, the expansion coefficients
\gamma_{n}
can be extracted by inserting Eq.
24
into the fixed-point equation
15
. In the following, we present the first few nonvanishing coefficients for some values of
d
with
\eta=0
. When
d=3
, one is left with

\displaystyle\gamma_{5}=

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}\frac{5N-4}{75\gamma^{2}}\,,\quad\gamma_{7}=-\mathscr{C}\frac{25N-24}{1575\gamma^{3}}\,,

\displaystyle\gamma_{9}=

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}\frac{125N-124}{30375\gamma^{4}}\,,\cdots\,.

(25)

Here, the order of the first nonvanishing coefficient is
n=5
. In the case of
d=2.5
, one arrives at

\displaystyle\gamma_{9}=

\displaystyle 2\mathscr{C}\frac{9N-8}{405\gamma^{2}}\,,\quad\gamma_{13}=-2\mathscr{C}\frac{81N-80}{26325\gamma^{3}}\,,

\displaystyle\gamma_{17}=

\displaystyle 2\mathscr{C}\frac{729N-728}{1549125\gamma^{4}}\,,\cdots\,.

(26)

For
d=2.1
, one has

\displaystyle\gamma_{41}=

\displaystyle 10\mathscr{C}\frac{41N-40}{35301\gamma^{2}}\,,\quad\gamma_{61}=-10\mathscr{C}\frac{1681N-1680}{45220581\gamma^{3}}\,,

\displaystyle\gamma_{81}=

\displaystyle 10\mathscr{C}\frac{68921N-68920}{51700467861\gamma^{4}}\,,\cdots\,.

(27)

Evidently, as the spatial dimension
d
decreases towards
d=2
, the order of the first nonvanishing coefficient increases significantly.

III.2.1
Laurent expansion in the limit of large field for the case of
d/(d-2+\eta)
being a rational fraction

We have discussed above the Laurent expansion of potential in the limit of large field, cf. Eq.
24
, where the leading power
d/(d-2+\eta)
is an integer. In this subsection, we discuss a more general case with
d/(d-2+\eta)
being a rational fraction. Note that even it is an irrational number, one can always approximate it with a rational one up to any desired accuracy. Let the leading power be

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d-2+\eta}=

\displaystyle\frac{l}{m}\,,

(28)

where the right side has been fully simplified, and both
l
and
m
are integers. Then the Laurent expansion in Eq.
24
can be modified as

\displaystyle u(\bar{\rho})\simeq

\displaystyle\,\gamma\,{\bar{\rho}}^{d/(d-2+\eta)}\left[1+\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\mathrm{tr}}}\gamma_{n}\left(\frac{1}{\bar{\rho}^{\frac{1}{m}}}\right)^{n}\right]\,.

(29)

Here we show some examples with
\eta=0
and the dimension in the vicinity of
d=3
. Firstly, considering the case of
d=2.9
, one has
d/(d-2+\eta)=29/9
. In the same way, inserting Eq.
29
into the fixed-point equation
15
, one is able to obtain the first few nonvanishing coefficients, as follows

\displaystyle\gamma_{49}=

\displaystyle 90\mathscr{C}\frac{49N-40}{69629\gamma^{2}}\,,\quad\gamma_{69}=-270\mathscr{C}\frac{2401N-2320}{46442543\gamma^{3}}\,,

\displaystyle\gamma_{89}=

\displaystyle 7290\mathscr{C}\frac{117649N-116920}{255371390029\gamma^{4}}\,,\cdots\,.

(30)

Then the potential in Eq.
29
reads

\displaystyle u(\bar{\rho})\Big|_{d=2.9}\simeq

\displaystyle\,\gamma\,{\bar{\rho}}^{3.22}+\gamma\gamma_{49}{\bar{\rho}}^{-2.22}+\gamma\gamma_{69}{\bar{\rho}}^{-4.44}

\displaystyle+\gamma\gamma_{89}{\bar{\rho}}^{-6.67}+\cdots\,.

(31)

If the value of
d
is increased up to
d=2.96
, the fraction in Eq.
28
reads
l/m=37/12
. It follows that

\displaystyle\gamma_{62}=

\displaystyle 75\mathscr{C}\frac{31N-25}{35557\gamma^{2}}\,,\quad\gamma_{87}=-600\mathscr{C}\frac{961N-925}{38152661\gamma^{3}}\,,

\displaystyle\gamma_{112}=

\displaystyle 1350\mathscr{C}\frac{29791N-29575}{10563024661\gamma^{4}}\,,\cdots\,,

(32)

which yields

\displaystyle u(\bar{\rho})\Big|_{d=2.96}\simeq

\displaystyle\,\gamma\,{\bar{\rho}}^{3.08}+\gamma\gamma_{62}{\bar{\rho}}^{-2.08}+\gamma\gamma_{87}{\bar{\rho}}^{-4.17}

\displaystyle+\gamma\gamma_{112}{\bar{\rho}}^{-6.25}+\cdots\,.

(33)

From Eq.
25
, one immediately finds for the Laurent expansion of potential with
d=3

\displaystyle u(\bar{\rho})\Big|_{d=3}\simeq

\displaystyle\,\gamma\,{\bar{\rho}}^{3}+\gamma\gamma_{5}{\bar{\rho}}^{-2}+\gamma\gamma_{7}{\bar{\rho}}^{-4}+\gamma\gamma_{9}{\bar{\rho}}^{-6}+\cdots\,.

(34)

From Eqs.
31
,
33
,
34
, one immediately finds that as the dimension
d
approaches towards
d=3
, the powers of
\bar{\rho}
of different orders converges at their respective values of
d=3
.

IV
Global solutions of the fixed-point equation

In this section we would like to solve the fixed-point equation in Eq.
15
directly by using numerical method.
Equation

15
is a differential algebraic equation (DAE) of index 1
Campbell et al. 2008
, which indicates that one has to take one further derivative on the DAE to transform it into a set of ordinary differential equations (ODE), and see e.g.,
Grossi and Wink 2019
for relevant discussions. The resulting ODEs read

\displaystyle u^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})=

\displaystyle u_{1}(\bar{\rho})\,,

(35a)

\displaystyle u_{1}^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})=

\displaystyle u_{2}(\bar{\rho})\,,

(35b)

\displaystyle u_{2}^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\mathscr{C}\bar{\rho}}\Big(1+u_{1}(\bar{\rho})+2\bar{\rho}u_{2}(\bar{\rho})\Big)^{2}\bigg[(-2+\eta)u_{1}(\bar{\rho})

\displaystyle+(d-2+\eta)\bar{\rho}u_{2}(\bar{\rho})-\mathscr{C}(N-1)\frac{u_{2}(\bar{\rho})}{\big(1+u_{1}(\bar{\rho})\big)^{2}}\bigg]

\displaystyle-\frac{3}{2\bar{\rho}}u_{2}(\bar{\rho})\,.

(35c)

Here, we have defined
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
in
35a
and
35b
, which correspond to the first and second derivatives of
u(\bar{\rho})
, respectively.
Equation

35c
is obtained by differentiating Eq.
15
with respect to
\bar{\rho}
.

We integrate the differential equations in
35
, starting at a sufficient large
\bar{\rho}=\bar{\rho}_{L}
with initial values for
u(\bar{\rho}_{L})
,
u_{1}(\bar{\rho}_{L})
and
u_{2}(\bar{\rho}_{L})
obtained from the asymptotic expression of
u(\bar{\rho})
in the limit of large field as shown in Eq.
23
. The anomalous dimension
\eta
in
35
and
23
is given an initial value, say
\eta=0
. The functions are integrated out from
\bar{\rho}=\bar{\rho}_{L}
towards
\bar{\rho}\to 0
. The parameter
\gamma
in Eq.
23
is fine tuned such that the potential
u(\bar{\rho})
and its derivatives of different orders
u^{(n)}(\bar{\rho})
are finite in the limit of
\bar{\rho}\to 0
. Then, the obtained potential is inserted back into Eq.
14
to update the value of
\eta
. Iterate the process mentioned above until convergence is obtained. Using such approach, one is able to find a solution to the fixed-point equation
15
, denoted by
u^{*}(\bar{\rho})
in what follows.

We proceed with the discussion of eigenperturbations near the fixed-point potential
u_{*}(\bar{\rho})
and the relevant eigenvalues, i.e., critical exponents
Codello et al. 2015
. The effective potential near the fixed point can be written as

\displaystyle u(\bar{\rho})=

\displaystyle u_{*}(\bar{\rho})+\epsilon\,\mathrm{e}^{-\omega t}v(\bar{\rho})\,,

(36)

where
\epsilon
is a small parameter,
v(\bar{\rho})
and
\omega
are the eigenfunction and the corresponding eigenvalue. One can see that only perturbations of eigenvalue
\omega>0
are relevant. Inserting Eq.
36
into the flow equation of the effective potential in Eq.
13
and reformulating the equation by powers of the small parameter
\epsilon
, one immediately finds that the fixed-point equation in Eq.
15
is reproduced in the zeroth-order term, and the linear term in
\epsilon
provides us with a homogeneous differential equation for the eigenfunction
v(\bar{\rho})
that we are looking for, i.e.,

\displaystyle\omega v(\bar{\rho})=

\displaystyle dv(\bar{\rho})-(d-2+\eta)\bar{\rho}v^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})

\displaystyle+\mathscr{C}\Bigg[\frac{v^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})+2\bar{\rho}v^{(2)}(\bar{\rho})}{\big(1+u_{*}^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})+2\bar{\rho}u_{*}^{(2)}(\bar{\rho})\big)^{2}}+\frac{(N-1)v^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})}{\big(1+u_{*}^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})\big)^{2}}\Bigg]\,.

(37)

The asymptotic behavior of
v(\bar{\rho})
in the limit of
\bar{\rho}\to\infty
is readily obtained from Eq.
37
by ignoring the subleading terms in the square bracket, since they are suppressed by
u_{*}^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})
and
u_{*}^{(2)}(\bar{\rho})
in the denominator. Thus, one is led to

\displaystyle v(\bar{\rho})\simeq

\displaystyle\,c\,{\bar{\rho}}^{(d-\omega)/(d-2+\eta)}\,,\qquad\bar{\rho}\to\infty\,.

(38)

with a normalized constant
c
. In the same way, as
\bar{\rho}\to 0
one obtains from Eq.
37
the relation as follows

\displaystyle v^{\prime}(0)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathscr{C}N}(\omega-d)\big(1+u_{*}^{\prime}(0)\big)^{2}v(0)\,.

(39)

Similarly as solving the equations in
35
, we integrate the differential equation for the eigenfunction
v(\bar{\rho})
in Eq.
37
, starting at a sufficient large value of
\bar{\rho}
, e.g.,
\bar{\rho}=\bar{\rho}_{L}
with initial values for
v(\bar{\rho}_{L})
and
v^{\prime}(\bar{\rho}_{L})
obtained in Eq.
38
. The function
v(\bar{\rho})
is resolved as the differential equation is integrated out from
\bar{\rho}=\bar{\rho}_{L}
to
\bar{\rho}\to 0
. We fine tune the value of
\omega
, such that
v(\bar{\rho})
and
v^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})
are finite in the limit of
\bar{\rho}\to 0
, or more exactly the relation in Eq.
39
is satisfied. Then one can obtain the eigenfunction
v(\bar{\rho})
and its related eigenvalue
\omega
.

VI
Summary and outlook

In this work the fixed-point equation for the nonperturbative effective potential in the fRG approach is integrated from large to vanishing field, where the asymptotic potential in the limit of large field is implemented as initial conditions. This approach provides us with a global fixed-point potential with high numerical accuracy, that captures both the asymptotic behavior in the limit of vanishing field and more importantly that in the limit of large field. The obtained global potential is in good agreement with the results from the Taylor expansion and the Laurent expansion in the regimes where they are applicable, i.e., small field for the former and large field for the latter, respectively. Furthermore, Laurent expansion of the potential in the limit of large field for the general case, that the spatial dimension
d
is a continuous variable in the range
2\leq d\leq 4
, is obtained.

By virtue of the method of eigenperturbations, we also compute the eigenfunctions and eigenvalues of perturbations near the Wilson-Fisher fixed point with high numerical accuracy. Consequently, critical exponents for different values of the spatial dimension
d
and the number of field components
N
of the
O(N)
universality class are obtained. Our calculated critical exponents are in good agreement with the relevant results in the literatures with the same truncation, and are also comparable with the exact results for the
2d
Ising model and the spherical model with
N\to\infty
.

Furthermore, it is also desirable to apply the approach used in this work to other physical problems of interest, such as the dynamical critical exponent
Tan et al. 2022
, the Yang–Lee edge singularity
Stephanov 2006
;
Mukherjee and Skokov 2021
;
Connelly et al. 2020
;
Rennecke and Skokov 2022
;
Ihssen and Pawlowski 2022
, multi-critical fixed points
Yabunaka and Delamotte 2017
, etc. Extension of this approach to higher-order derivative expansions of effective action beyond
\mathrm{LPA}^{\prime}
is also very valuable.

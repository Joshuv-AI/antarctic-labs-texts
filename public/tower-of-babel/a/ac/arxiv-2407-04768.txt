High-Energy Neutrinos From Millicharged Dark Matter Annihilation in the Sun Preprint: FERMILAB-PUB-24-0353-T
Asher Berlin a,b, Dan Hooper a,c,d

Abstract
Millicharged dark matter particles can be efficiently captured by the Sun, where they annihilate into tau leptons, leading to the production of high-energy neutrinos. In contrast to the Earth, the high temperature of the Sun suppresses the fraction of millicharged particles that are bound to nuclei, allowing for potentially high annihilation rates. We recast existing constraints from the IceCube Neutrino Observatory and use this information to place new limits on the fraction of the dark matter that is millicharged. This analysis excludes previously unexplored parameter space for masses of m χ ∼ ( 5 − 100 ) ​ GeV m_{\chi}\sim(5-100)\ \text{GeV} , charges of q χ ∼ 10 − 3 − 10 − 2 q_{\chi}\sim 10^{-3}-10^{-2} , and fractional abundances as small as f DM ∼ 10 − 5 f_{{}_{\text{DM}}}\sim 10^{-5} .

Introduction

Although all known particles carry values of electric charge that are of order unity (or are electrically neutral),
models featuring elementary particles with much smaller quantities of charge have received a significant amount of interest in recent years

Magill et al. 2019
;
Kelly and Tsai 2019

Acciarri et al. 2020
;

;

;
Plestid et al. 2020
;
Bloch et al. 2021
;
Foroughi-Abari et al. 2021
;
Harnik et al. 2021
;
Marocco and Sarkar 2021
;
Berlin et al. 2023a
;
Chen et al. 2023
;
Budker et al. 2022
;
Argüelles Delgado et al. 2021
;
Afek et al. 2021
. Such millicharged particles can arise, for example, if an effective charge is generated through the kinetic mixing of the photon with a new light dark photon,
\mathscr{L}\supset(\epsilon/2)F^{\mu\nu}F^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}
, where
F^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}
is the dark photon field strength and
\epsilon
is a small dimensionless parameter. This mixing causes particles,
\chi^{\pm}
, charged under this new
U(1)^{\prime}
, to acquire an effective electric charge,
q_{\chi}=\epsilon\,e^{\prime}/e
, where
e^{\prime}
is the
U(1)^{\prime}
gauge coupling and
e
is the standard electromagnetic coupling
Holdom 1986
.

Within the context of effective field theory, any value of
\epsilon\ll 1
is technically natural. If the Standard Model is embedded within a Grand Unified Theory,
however, this mixing is generated only through loops of particles that carry both hypercharge and
U(1)^{\prime}
charge. At the one-loop level, the expected size of this mixing is given by

\epsilon\sim\frac{e^{\prime}e}{16\pi^{2}}\ln\bigg(\frac{M^{\prime}}{M}\bigg)\simeq 6\times 10^{-4}\times\bigg(\frac{e^{\prime}}{e}\bigg)\,\ln\bigg(\frac{M^{\prime}}{M}\bigg)\,,

(1)

where
M^{\prime}/M
is the ratio of the masses in the loop
Holdom 1986
;
Cohen et al. 2010
;
Baumgart et al. 2009
. From this perspective, particles with
q_{\chi}\sim 10^{-3}
are particularly well motivated.

In this work, we consider the possibility that a fraction of the cosmological dark matter density consists of millicharged particles. Although observations of the cosmic microwave background and measurements of the primordial element abundances have been used to place strong constraints on charged dark matter, these observations remain consistent with the possibility that a small fraction,
f_{{}_{\text{DM}}}\lesssim 10^{-2}
, of the dark matter could carry significant electric charge
Dubovsky et al. 2004
;
de Putter et al. 2019
;
Kovetz et al. 2018
;
Buen-Abad et al. 2022
;
Stebbins and Krnjaic 2019
. In particular,
a millicharged dark matter subcomponent could arise as a thermal relic of the early universe. Assuming standard thermal freeze-out, the requirement of perturbative unitarity
Griest and Kamionkowski 1990
can be used to place the following lower limit on the fractional abundance of this component:

f_{{}_{\text{DM}}}\gtrsim 10^{-10}\times(m_{\chi}/\text{GeV})^{2}~,

(2)

where
m_{\chi}
is the mass of the millicharged particle,
\chi^{\pm}
.

Even for very small fractional abundances, underground searches for dark matter scattering have been used to place extremely strong constraints on millicharged subcomponents, testing particles with charges as small as
q_{\chi}\sim 10^{-10}/f_{{}_{\text{DM}}}^{1/2}

Emken et al. 2019
. These experiments are almost completely insensitive to particles with sufficiently large values of
q_{\chi}
, however, since such particles scatter many times in the terrestrial overburden, shedding their kinetic energy and thermalizing down to room temperature,
300\ \text{K}\simeq 26\ \text{meV}
, well below the threshold of typical large-scale experiments. As a result, millicharged particles with
q_{\chi}\gtrsim 10^{-4}\times(m_{\chi}/\text{GeV})^{1/2}
reside within a blind spot of existing direct detection efforts
Emken et al. 2019
.

The efficient scattering of millicharged particles with astrophysical objects within our Solar System has a drastic effect on their local distribution and density. For
m_{\chi}\gg 1\ \text{GeV}
, the characteristic thermal velocity of these particles will be well below the gravitational escape velocity of the Sun or Earth. As a result, such particles remain gravitationally bound to these objects after scattering, accumulating as a dense hydrostatic gas over the age of the Solar System.

The possible accumulation of dark matter particles within stellar bodies has been studied extensively, dating back to the 1980s
Krauss et al. 1985
;
Press and Spergel 1985
;
Griest and Seckel 1987
;
Gould 1987a
;
Gould 1987b
;
Gould and Raffelt 1990
. It has also long been appreciated that such particles could give rise to interesting signatures, arising from their annihilation into Standard Model states. Examples include the prompt annihilation into neutrinos
Silk et al. 1985
;
Srednicki et al. 1987
;
Kamionkowski 1991
;
Barger et al. 2002
;
Flacke et al. 2009
or long-lived mediators
Schuster et al. 2010
;
Batell et al. 2010
in the Sun, annihilations in the local volume of terrestrial neutrino detectors
Pospelov and Ramani 2021
;
McKeen et al. 2023
, and annihilations in Earth’s core
Mack et al. 2007
;
Pospelov and Ray 2024
.

Signals arising from the annihilation of millicharged particles gravitationally bound to Earth was studied in Ref.
Pospelov and Ramani 2021
. However, in this case, an additional complication arises from the fact that
\chi^{-}
can bind with atomic nuclei,
N
. In turn, the resulting Coulomb barrier between
(\chi^{-}N)
and
\chi^{+}
drastically suppresses the local annihilation rate of millicharged particles. For
q_{\chi}\gg 10^{-5}
, the binding energy of
(\chi^{-}N)
greatly exceeds the temperature of Earth’s core, exponentially suppressing the rate of terrestrial-based annihilation signals.

In this paper, we instead focus on the capture and annihilation of millicharged particles in the Sun, leading to the production of high-energy neutrinos through
\chi^{+}\chi^{-}\to\tau^{+}\tau^{-}
followed by the decay of the tau leptons into neutrinos. In contrast to millicharged particles in the Earth, the high temperature of the solar interior suppresses the fraction of these particles that will be bound to nuclei, making this an ideal environment to produce detectable fluxes of annihilation products. We recast the results of existing searches for high-energy neutrinos from the Sun, as recently performed by the IceCube Collaboration
Aartsen et al. 2017
;
Abbasi et al. 2022
. Using this data, we place new limits on millicharged dark matter, excluding previously unexplored parameter space corresponding to
m_{\chi}\sim(5-100)\ \text{GeV}
and
q_{\chi}\sim 10^{-3}-10^{-2}
for fractional abundances as small as
f_{{}_{\text{DM}}}\sim 10^{-5}
.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Secs.
II
and
III

IV
addresses modifications that can arise if
\chi^{-}
can efficiently form bound states with solar nuclei. This formalism is applied in Sec.
V
to derive constraints, using existing data from the high-energy neutrino telescope, IceCube. We then compare these limits to other bounds on millicharged dark matter. In Sec.
VI

II
Capture and Annihilation of Dark Matter in the Sun

The total number millicharged particles that are gravitationally bound to the Sun,
N_{\chi}
, evolves in time according to

\frac{dN_{\chi}}{dt}\simeq\Gamma_{\text{cap}}-N_{\chi}^{2}\,K_{\text{ann}}~,

(3)

where the first and second terms on the right-hand side correspond to the capture and annihilation rates, respectively. Here, we have neglected the effects of millicharged particle evaporation, since for
m_{\chi}\gtrsim 1\ \text{GeV}
their thermal velocities are smaller than the gravitational escape velocity near the Sun’s surface,
v_{\text{esc}}=(2GM_{\odot}/R_{\odot})^{1/2}\simeq 2\times 10^{-3}
, where
M_{\odot}
and
R_{\odot}
are the solar mass and radius, respectively.

Within the parameter space that we will consider in this work, nearly every millicharged particle that passes through the Sun will scatter, become gravitationally bound, and reach kinetic equilibrium
Pospelov and Ramani 2021
;
Berlin et al. 2024
. To determine the capture rate, we therefore simply calculate the rate at which such particles enter the Sun. The gravitational pull of the Sun significantly increases the flux of dark matter particles that reach its surface. The distance of closest approach to the Sun for a dark matter particle entering the Solar System with an impact parameter,
b
, and a velocity,
v_{{}_{\text{DM}}}
, is given by

R_{\text{min}}=\frac{G\,M_{\odot}}{v^{2}_{{}_{\text{DM}}}}\,\bigg[\bigg(1+\frac{b^{2}\,v^{4}_{{}_{\text{DM}}}}{G^{2}M^{2}_{\odot}}\bigg)^{1/2}-1\bigg]\,.

(4)

Setting
R_{\text{min}}\leq R_{\odot}
, we find that such particles will pass through the Sun for all impact parameters less than

b\leq b_{\text{max}}=R_{\odot}\,\bigg(1+\frac{v_{\text{esc}}^{2}}{v^{2}_{{}_{\text{DM}}}}\bigg)^{1/2}\,.

(5)

From this, it follows that the effective cross-sectional area for capture in the Sun will be given by

\displaystyle A_{\text{cap}}=\pi\,b_{\text{max}}^{2}=\pi R^{2}_{\odot}\bigg(1+\frac{v_{\text{esc}}^{2}}{v^{2}_{{}_{\text{DM}}}}\bigg)\,.

(6)

This leads to a capture rate of

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\text{cap}}

\displaystyle\simeq(f_{{}_{\text{DM}}}\,\rho_{{}_{\text{DM}}}/m_{\chi})\,v_{{}_{\text{DM}}}\,A_{\text{cap}}\,,

(7)

where
\rho_{{}_{\text{DM}}}\simeq 0.4\ \text{GeV}/\text{cm}^{3}
is the local dark matter density. To account for the spread in dark matter velocities, we average the quantity
v_{{}_{\text{DM}}}A_{\text{cap}}
in Eq.
7
over the velocity distribution of the Standard Halo Model, consisting of a boosted-Maxwellian (with
v_{0}=220\ \text{km}/\text{s}
and
v_{\odot}=240\ \text{km}/\text{s}
) truncated at the Galactic escape velocity,
v_{\text{esc}}^{\rm Gal}=550\ \text{km}/\text{s}
. We find that this can be approximated by simply using Eqs.
6
and
7
with a representative value of
v_{{}_{\text{DM}}}\simeq 9\times 10^{-4}
.

. For larger masses, the concentration of millicharged particles near the solar core enhances the likelihood of annihilation.

For a charge-symmetric population of millicharged particles, the quantity
K_{\text{ann}}
appearing in Eq.
3
is given by

\displaystyle K_{\text{ann}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2N_{\chi}^{2}}\,\int_{V_{\odot}}\hskip-2.84544ptn_{\chi}^{2}\,\langle\sigma v\rangle\,dV\simeq\frac{\langle\sigma v\rangle}{2\,V_{\odot}}\times\frac{\langle n_{\chi}^{2}\rangle}{\langle n_{\chi}\rangle^{2}}\,,

(8)

where the integral in the first equality is performed over the volume of the Sun,
V_{\odot}
,
n_{\chi}\equiv n_{\chi^{+}}+n_{\chi^{-}}
is the number density of millicharged particles, and
\langle\sigma v\rangle
is the thermally-averaged
\chi^{+}\chi^{-}
annihilation cross section. In the second equality of this expression, we have approximated
\langle\sigma v\rangle
as independent of temperature, and the brackets for
\langle n_{\chi}\rangle
and
\langle n_{\chi}^{2}\rangle
denote an average over the solar volume.

The propagation of charged particles in the Solar System can be impacted by the turbulent solar wind and its embedded magnetic field, the effects of which are collectively known as solar modulation
Gleeson and Axford 1968
;
Strauss et al. 2012
;
Maccione 2013
;
Cholis et al. 2016
;
Cholis et al. 2022
. Solar modulation significantly suppresses the intensity of the cosmic ray spectrum at the location of Earth for kinetic energies below
\sim E_{\text{kin}}\lesssim|Q|\Phi
, where
Q
is the charge of the cosmic ray and
\Phi\sim(0.2-1)\ \text{GeV}

Potgieter 2014
;
Cholis et al. 2016
.

Analogously, it is possible that solar modulation could suppress the flux of millicharged dark matter particles that reaches the inner Solar System. We expect these effects to be significant if
m_{\chi}\,(v_{{{}_{\text{DM}}}}^{2}+v^{2}_{\text{esc}})/2\lesssim q_{\chi}\,\Phi
, or equivalently,

m_{\chi}\lesssim 100\ \text{GeV}\times\bigg(\frac{q_{\chi}}{10^{-3}}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{\Phi}{0.2\ \text{GeV}}\bigg)\,.

(9)

This condition will be satisfied in much of the parameter space that we are interested in, potentially reducing the sensitivity of any solar or terrestrial-based searches for such particles.

In the above discussion of solar modulation, we have explicitly assumed that the millicharged particles couple to electromagnetic fields in the same way as ordinary charged particles. If these interactions are instead mediated by a kinetically-mixed dark photon,
A^{\prime}
, their range will be limited to
\sim m_{A^{\prime}}^{-1}
, where
m_{A^{\prime}}
is the mass of the dark photon. In this work, we will focus on charges,
q_{\chi}=\epsilon\,e^{\prime}/e\gtrsim 10^{-4}
, corresponding to kinetic mixing parameters of
\epsilon\gtrsim 10^{-5}/e^{\prime}
. For light dark photons (
m_{A^{\prime}}\lesssim\text{MeV}
), such values of
\epsilon
are highly constrained unless
m_{A^{\prime}}\lesssim 10^{-14}\ \text{eV}\sim(10^{4}\ \text{km})^{-1}

Mirizzi et al. 2009a
;
Mirizzi et al. 2009b
;
Kunze and Vázquez-Mozo 2015
;
Caputo et al. 2020a
;
Caputo et al. 2020b
;
Garcia et al. 2020
, corresponding to distances larger than an Earth radius. Hence, within the viable region of parameter space,
\chi^{\pm}
efficiently couples to terrestrial magnetic fields.
On the other hand, since the coherence length of the solar magnetic field is of the same order as the solar radius
Longair 2011
, the effects of solar modulation will be suppressed if
m_{A^{\prime}}\gg R_{\odot}^{-1}\sim 10^{-16}\ \text{eV}
. Throughout the remainder of this work, we will implicitly assume that this is the case, allowing us to provide a meaningful comparison between the signals discussed here and other local searches for millicharged dark matter (all of which are subject to the effects of solar modulation). Alternatively, solar modulation may be implicitly incorporated into our analysis by rescaling the incoming
millicharged particle flux, and thereby the definition of
f_{{}_{\text{DM}}}
.

To evaluate the annihilation rate in Eq.
8
, we will need the radial density profile of millicharged particles in the Sun,
n_{\chi}
. From the condition of hydrostatic equilibrium, the Euler equation implies that

m_{\chi}\,n_{\chi}\,\boldsymbol{g}_{\odot}=\nabla P_{\chi}~,

(10)

where
\boldsymbol{g}_{\odot}
is the solar gravitational field and
P_{\chi}
is the pressure of the millicharged particles. Treating these particles as an ideal gas, the pressure is given by
P_{\chi}\simeq T\,n_{\chi}
, where
T
is the temperature of the Sun. In the spherically-symmetric limit, Eq.
10
can be written as

\frac{1}{n_{\chi}(r)}\,\frac{dn_{\chi}(r)}{dr}+\frac{1}{T(r)}\,\frac{dT(r)}{dr}+\frac{m_{\chi}\,g_{\odot}(r)}{T(r)}\simeq 0~.

(11)

For a given value of
m_{\chi}
, we solve Eq.
11
numerically, incorporating the solar density and temperature profiles from the standard solar model
Bahcall and Pinsonneault 2004
. These solutions are presented in Fig.
1
. In the left panel, we show the normalized density profile,
n_{\chi}(r)/\langle n_{\chi}\rangle
, for several choices of
m_{\chi}
. For
m_{\chi}\lesssim 1\ \text{GeV}
, gravitationally bound millicharged particles are distributed approximately uniformly throughout the solar interior. For heavier particles, larger pressure and density gradients are required to offset the gravitational attraction, leading to a profile that is much more highly concentrated near the solar core. This behavior is also reflected in the right panel of Fig.
1
, in which we show the normalized annihilation rate,
\langle n_{\chi}^{2}\rangle/\langle n_{\chi}\rangle^{2}
, as appears in the last factor of Eq.
8
. For
m_{\chi}\ll 1\ \text{GeV}
, the uniform density profile of millicharged particles implies that
\langle n_{\chi}^{2}\rangle/\langle n_{\chi}\rangle^{2}\simeq 1
. For larger masses, the concentration of millicharged particles near the solar core enhances the likelihood of annihilation, such that
\langle n_{\chi}^{2}\rangle/\langle n_{\chi}\rangle^{2}\gg 1
.

We are now in a position to calculate the capture and annihilation rates of millicharged particles in the Sun. Solving Eq.
3
with the initial condition
N_{\chi}=0
at
t=0
, the value of
N_{\chi}
today is given by

\displaystyle N_{\chi}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{\Gamma_{\text{cap}}}{K_{\text{ann}}}\,\tanh{\big(\sqrt{\Gamma_{\text{cap}}\,K_{\text{ann}}}~t_{\odot}\big)}

\displaystyle\simeq\begin{cases}\Gamma_{\text{cap}}\,t_{\odot}&(t_{\odot}\ll\tau)\\[3.0pt]
\sqrt{\Gamma_{\text{cap}}/K_{\text{ann}}}&(t_{\odot}\gg\tau)~,\end{cases}

(12)

where
t_{\odot}\simeq 4.5\ \text{Gyr}
is the age of the Sun and
\tau\equiv(\Gamma_{\text{cap}}\,K_{\text{ann}})^{-1/2}
. From Eqs.
II
and
3
, the annihilation rate grows as
N_{\chi}^{2}\,K_{\text{ann}}\sim\Gamma^{2}_{\text{cap}}\,K_{\text{ann}}\,t^{2}
until
t\sim\tau
, after which equilibrium is reached between the rates of capture and annihilation,
N_{\chi}^{2}\,K_{\text{ann}}\simeq\Gamma_{\text{cap}}
.

III
Millicharged Dark Matter Annihilation

Millicharged particles can annihilate directly into pairs of charged Standard Model fermions through the exchange of an intermediate photon,
\chi^{+}\chi^{-}\to\gamma^{*}\to f^{+}f^{-}
. In the non-relativistic limit, the cross section for this processes is approximately given by

\langle\sigma v\rangle_{\chi^{+}\chi^{-}\rightarrow f^{+}f^{-}}\simeq\frac{\pi\alpha^{2}q_{\chi}^{2}}{m_{\chi}^{2}}\sum_{f}\,n_{f}\,q_{f}^{2}~,

(13)

where the sum is over all fermions with mass
m_{f}\ll m_{\chi}
and charge
q_{f}
, and
n_{f}
is the number of colors of the fermion. Annihilations to electroweak bosons and neutrinos are also possible, but depend on the details of the full theory. For instance, if these interactions are generated by a kinetically-mixed dark photon,
\chi^{\pm}
will be millicharged under Standard Model hypercharge, such that it develops a small coupling to both the photon and the
Z
boson. Hence, for
m_{\chi}\gtrsim 10^{2}\ \text{GeV}
, annihilations to pairs of
W
bosons and neutrinos are unsuppressed, further contributing to the signals discussed here. Since such processes are model-dependent, we have not included their contributions in our final estimate.

In models that include a dark photon,
A^{\prime}
, the dark matter can also annihilate to a pair of such particles. The cross section for this process is given by

\langle\sigma v\rangle_{\chi^{+}\chi^{-}\rightarrow A^{\prime}A^{\prime}}\simeq\frac{\pi\alpha^{\prime\,2}}{m_{\chi}^{2}}~,

(14)

where
m_{A^{\prime}}
is the mass of the dark photon and
\alpha^{\prime}=e^{\prime\,2}/4\pi
. If
\alpha^{\prime}
is sufficiently large, annihilations to dark photons could dominate over those to Standard Model final states, resulting in a suppression of the neutrino flux from the Sun as calculated in this study. In our analysis, we neglect this process, which is equivalent to taking
\alpha^{\prime}\ll\alpha
.

IV
Bound States

Negative-millicharged particles,
\chi^{-}
, can efficiently bind to nuclei,
N
, in the Sun, with a characteristic binding energy that is given by
E_{\chi N}\simeq(q_{\chi}Z\alpha)^{2}\,\mu_{\chi N}/2
, where
Z
is the atomic number of the nucleus and
\mu_{\chi N}
is the reduced mass of the system. Hence, for
m_{\chi}\gtrsim\mu_{\chi N}\sim 10\ \text{GeV}
and
q_{\chi}\gtrsim(m_{e}/\mu_{\chi N})^{1/2}\sim 10^{-2}
, millicharged particles will be much more tightly-bound to nuclei than electrons are. Furthermore, for
Z\sim 10
and similar values of the coupling
q_{\chi}
,
E_{\chi N}
will exceed the temperature in the solar core,
T\sim 1\ \text{keV}
, in which case the formation of
(\chi^{-}N)
bound states will be energetically favored.

Since
(\chi^{-}N)
has a net positive charge of
Q_{\chi N}=Z-q_{\chi}\simeq Z
, its formation suppresses the rate at which millicharged particles annihilate. In particular, such annihilations require that a
(\chi^{-}N)
and
\chi^{+}
overcome the repulsive Coulomb barrier, which is comparable to the binding energy,
Q_{\chi N}\,q_{\chi}\,\alpha/r_{\chi N}\simeq E_{\chi N}
, where
r_{\chi N}\simeq(q_{\chi}Z\alpha\,\,\mu_{\chi N})^{-1}
is the size of the bound state. As a result, if
E_{\chi N}\gtrsim T
, then
(\chi^{-}N)
and
\chi^{+}
will not possess sufficient kinetic energy to overcome their electrical repulsion.
1
1

1

For sufficiently large
\alpha^{\prime}
, the attractive dark Coulomb potential can overcome this repulsion. However, since this also suppresses the fraction of annihilations to Standard Model states (as discussed in Sec.
III
), we will ignore this possibility.

As the rates for the formation and disruption of
(\chi^{-}N)
bound states in the Sun are both very rapid
Pospelov and Ramani 2021
, chemical equilibrium will be reached and maintained between the bound,
(\chi^{-}N)
, and unbound,
\chi^{-}
, populations. From the condition of chemical equilibrium,
\mu_{\chi^{-}}+\mu_{N}=\mu_{(\chi^{-}N)}
, we can use the Maxwell-Boltzmann distributions of these species to relate the number density of
(\chi^{-}N)
bound states to the number densities of free nuclei and millicharged particles:

\frac{n_{\chi^{-}}\,n_{N}}{n_{(\chi^{-}N)}}=\Big(\frac{\mu_{\chi N}\,T}{2\pi}\Big)^{3/2}\,e^{-E_{\chi N}/T}~.

(15)

Since annihilations between
\chi^{+}
and
(\chi^{-}N)
are exponentially suppressed by their repulsive Coulomb barrier, we incorporate the effect of bound state formation by rescaling the
\chi^{+}\chi^{-}
annihilation cross section by
\langle\sigma v\rangle\to\mathcal{R}_{\text{BS}}\,\langle\sigma v\rangle
, where

\mathcal{R}_{\text{BS}}\equiv\frac{n_{\chi^{-}}+e^{-E_{\chi N}/T}\,n_{(\chi^{-}N)}}{n_{\chi^{-}}+n_{(\chi^{-}N)}}~.

(16)

Using Eq.
15
, this becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\text{BS}}

\displaystyle=\frac{F_{N}+1}{F_{N}+\,e^{E_{\chi N}/T}}~,

(17)

where

\displaystyle F_{N}

\displaystyle\equiv\Big(\frac{\mu_{\chi N}\,T}{2\pi}\Big)^{3/2}\,\frac{1}{n_{N}}~.

(18)

From this calculation, we see that bound state formation exponentially suppresses the annihilation rate when
E_{\chi N}\gtrsim T\,\ln{F_{N}}
. We incorporate this into our analysis by considering interactions with thorium (Th) nuclei, the highest-
Z
element measured in the Sun, which constitutes
\sim 2\times 10^{-10}
of the mass density in the photosphere
Asplund et al. 2009
. We take this to be representative of the entire solar volume, with
n_{N}\simeq 7\times 10^{11}\ \text{cm}^{-3}
independent of radius. We use this in Eq.
17
along with
T=1\ \text{keV}
, which yields
\ln F_{N}\sim 50
for
m_{\chi}\gtrsim 1\ \text{GeV}
.

V
Constraints From IceCube

The IceCube Neutrino Observatory consists of several thousand optical modules distributed throughout a cubic kilometer of Antarctic ice. In the energy range of interest, this instrument is primarily sensitive to muon tracks which result from the charged-current interactions of muon neutrinos in or near the detector’s instrumented volume. Dark matter annihilating in the Sun could produce a potentially detectable flux of energetic neutrinos, allowing IceCube to place constraints on the particle nature of dark matter.

The IceCube Collaboration has placed stringent limits on the flux of high-energy neutrinos that reaches us from the direction of the Sun
Abbasi et al. 2022
;
Aartsen et al. 2017
;
Aartsen et al. 2016
;
Aartsen et al. 2013
;
Abbasi et al. 2012
;
Abbasi et al. 2009
. In this study, we recast the limits presented in Refs.
Aartsen et al. 2017
;
Abbasi et al. 2022
, which constrain the following quantity:

\Gamma_{\text{ann}}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\,N_{\chi}^{2}\,K_{\text{ann}}^{\text{SM}}=\frac{1}{4}\,\int_{V_{\odot}}\hskip-2.84544ptn_{\chi}^{2}\,\langle\sigma v\rangle_{\text{SM}}\,dV~,

(19)

where
K_{\text{ann}}^{\text{SM}}
is defined analogously to Eq.
8
, but in terms of the cross section for annihilations to a particular Standard Model final state,
\langle\sigma v\rangle_{\text{SM}}
. In this study, we focus on the case of
\tau^{+}\tau^{-}
final states, as these lead to strong bounds and do not depend on the complete model details of the theory (see the discussion in Sec.
III
).

VI
Summary and Conclusions

In this study, we have calculated the capture and annihilation rates of millicharged particles in the Sun, assuming that these particles make up a subdominant fraction of the total dark matter abundance. We have taken into account the effects of solar modulation, and the formation of
(\chi^{-}N)
bound states in the solar environment. From this annihilation rate and the resulting high-energy neutrino flux, we use previously published results from the IceCube Collaboration to constrain this class of models. We find that the IceCube data rules out significant regions of previously unexplored parameter space, featuring
m_{\chi}\sim(5-100)\ \text{GeV}
,
q_{\chi}\sim 10^{-3}-10^{-2}
, and fractional abundances as small as
f_{{}_{\text{DM}}}\sim 3\times 10^{-5}
.

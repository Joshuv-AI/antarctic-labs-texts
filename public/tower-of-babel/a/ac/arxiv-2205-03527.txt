On Tjurina Ideals of Hypersurface Singularities Thanks: The author was partially supported by FAPERJ, ARC 211.361/2019
João Hélder Olmedo Rodrigues

Abstract
. The Tjurina ideal of a germ of a holomorphic function f f is the ideal of 𝒪 ℂ n , 0 \mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0} - the ring of those germs at 0 ∈ ℂ n 0\in\mathbbm{C}^{n} - generated by f f itself and by its partial derivatives. Here it is denoted by T ⁡ ( f ) T(f) . The ideal T ⁡ ( f ) T(f) gives the structure of closed subscheme of ( ℂ n , 0 ) (\mathbbm{C}^{n},0) to the singular set of the hypersurface defined by f f , being an object of central interest in Singularity Theory. In this note we introduce T T -fullness and T T -dependence , two easily verifiable properties for arbitrary ideals of germs of holomorphic functions. These two properties allow us to give necessary and sufficient conditions on an ideal I ⊂ 𝒪 ℂ n , 0 I\subset\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0} for the equation I = T ⁡ ( f ) I=T(f) to admit a solution f f . As a result we characterize closed subschemes of ( ℂ n , 0 ) (\mathbbm{C}^{n},0) arising as singularities of germs of hypersurfaces.

1.
Introduction

Motivation for the present work comes from the classification of germs of hypersurface singularities in
(\mathbbm{C}^{n},0)
. A germ of a complex hypersurface
(X,0)\subset(\mathbbm{C}^{n},0)
at the origin
0\in\mathbbm{C}^{n}
is defined as the zero set of some - non-trivial - principal ideal
I(X)
of
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
, the ring of germs of holomorphic functions at
0\in\mathbbm{C}^{n}
. Let
{\mathfrak{m}}
denote the unique maximal ideal of
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
. A generator
f\in{\mathfrak{m}}
- which is well-defined, modulo multiplication by an invertible element in
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
- of
I(X)=\langle f\rangle
is said to be an
equation
for
(X,0)
; we often say in this case that
(X,0)\subset(\mathbbm{C}^{n},0)

is defined by

f
.

As it is customary, we want to decide when two objects - germs of hypersurfaces at
0\in\mathbbm{C}^{n}
, in the present case - are
alike
, in some prescribed sense. A quite natural notion is the
biholomorphic
equivalence of germs of hypersurfaces. We say that two germs of hypersurfaces,
(X_{1},0)\subset(\mathbbm{C}^{n},0)
and
(X_{2},0)\subset(\mathbbm{C}^{n},0)
with equations
f_{1},\,f_{2}
are
biholomorphically equivalent
if there exist small open neighbourhoods
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
of the origin
0\in\mathbbm{C}^{n}
, where
f_{1},\,f_{2}
converge and a (germ of) biholomorphism
\Phi:U_{1}\rightarrow U_{2}
- taking the origin to itself - such that
\Phi(X_{1}\cap U_{1})=X_{2}\cap U_{2}
. This is clearly an equivalence relation among germs of hypersurfaces. We refer to the totality of germs of hypersurfaces all biholomorphically equivalent to one another as a
biholomorphic class
. It is easy to see that
(X_{1},0)
and
(X_{2},0)
as above are biholomorphically equivalent if and only if there exists an automorphism
\varphi
of
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
and an invertible element
u\in\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
such that
\varphi(f_{1})=uf_{2}
. It is often said in this case that
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are
contact equivalent
. So, a guiding big problem is to look for a complete set of invariants describing completely biholomorphic classes. For instance, one of the most famous invariants of a biholomorphic class is the
multiplicity
of their elements. We recall that if a system
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
of generators of
{\mathfrak{m}}\subset\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
is chosen and if
(X,0)\subset(\mathbbm{C}^{n},0)
is a germ of hypersurface defined by
f
, its multiplicity
{\rm mult}(X,0)={\rm mult}(f)
is the smallest degree
m
of a non-zero homogeneous polynomial appearing in a series expansion
f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=f_{m}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})+f_{m+1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})+\ldots\,\,
Clearly the multiplicity of a germ doesn’t depend on the choice of
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
. So, two biholomorphically equivalent germs of hypersurfaces in
(\mathbbm{C}^{n},0)
have the same multiplicity, but not the other way around.

Given the previous terminology, the starting point for our research is a result of J. Mather and S. Yau’s study
[
MY
]
, which involves a certain
\mathbbm{C}
-algebra associated to a germ of hypersurface
(X,0)\subset(\mathbbm{C}^{n},0)
. Fix a system
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
of minimal generators of
{\mathfrak{m}}\subset\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}=\mathbbm{C}\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
. Suppose
(X,0)
is defined by
f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
. We define the
Tjurina Algebra
of
(X,0)
- or more accurately of
f
- as the quotient ring

A(X)=A(f)=\mathbbm{C}\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}/\langle f,\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}},\ldots,\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{n}}\rangle.

The ideal
\langle f,\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}},\ldots,\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{n}}\rangle
appearing as the denominator is called the
Tjurina ideal
- or extended Jacobian ideal - of
f
and we denote it as
T(f)
. If
g
is another generator of
I(X)
then
g=uf
, for some invertible element
u\in\mathbbm{C}\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
. Hence
A(f)=A(g)
, because actually
T(f)=T(g)
and this shows that
A(X)
really doesn’t depend on the chosen generator for
I(X)
.

In order to explain Mather-Yau’s result, we observe that when
(X_{1},0)
and
(X_{2},0)
are biholomorphically equivalent germs of hypersurfaces, then from a relation of type
\varphi(f_{1})=uf_{2}
as above, it is quite straightforward to check that the Tjurina algebras
A(X_{1})
and
A(X_{2})
are isomorphic as
\mathbbm{C}
-algebras. The converse holds but it is far more subtle and originally proved for the class of
isolated hypersurface singularities
. The result is generally referred to as the Mather-Yau theorem. There is also a generalization due to Gaffney and Hauser (cf.
[
GH
]
for details), which is valid for the class of
hypersurface singularities of isolated type
. This class includes (strictly) isolated hypersurfaces singularities. The aforementioned result is stated here in a simplified manner:

Theorem 1.1

(Mather, Yau;  Gaffney, Hauser)
.

Two germs of complex hypersurfaces of isolated type,
(X_{1},0)\subset(\mathbbm{C}^{n},0)
and
(X_{2},0)\subset(\mathbbm{C}^{n},0)
are biholomorphically equivalent if and only if
A(X_{1})
and
A(X_{2})
are isomorphic as
\mathbbm{C}
-algebras.

That’s why Mather and Yau originally baptised
A(X)
as the
moduli algebra
of the germ of hypersurface
(X,0)\subset(\mathbbm{C}^{n},0)
: the preceding result tells us that the problem of the classification of germs of hypersurfaces singularities of Isolated Type
(X,0)\subset(\mathbbm{C}^{n},0)
up to biholomorphic equivalence is equivalent to that of the classification of their Tjurina algebras
A(X)
up to
\mathbbm{C}
-algebra isomorphism.

In this vein we ask ourselves what seems to be a prior question - algebraic of course, as the methods we will use to attack it -, namely:

Question 1.2
.

Fixed
n\geqslant 1
, how to find necessary and sufficient conditions for an ideal
I\,\subset\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
to be the Tjurina ideal of some
f\in{\mathfrak{m}}
?

Clearly, for all
n\geqslant 1
, the zero ideal and the whole ring
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
are Tjurina ideals of
0
and
x_{1}
, say, respectively.

Example 1.3
.

If
n=1
then any ideal
I\subset\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{1},0}=\mathbbm{C}\{x_{1}\}
is of the form
I={\mathfrak{m}}^{k}=\langle x_{1}^{k}\rangle
,
k\geqslant 0
; this is the Tjurina ideal of
f(x_{1})=x_{1}^{k+1}
.

So, if
n=1
, Question
1.2
has a trivial answer: any ideal is the Tjurina ideal of some function. However, beginning at
n=2
, this is not true any more. Clearly a necessary condition for an ideal
I\,\subset\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
to be the Tjurina ideal of some
f\in\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
is that the minimal number of generators of
I
should be at most
n+1
, but this is not sufficient as we see in the next

Example 1.4
.

Let
I={\mathfrak{m}}^{2}=\langle x_{1}^{2},x_{1}x_{2},x_{2}^{2}\rangle\subset\mathbbm{C}\{x_{1},x_{2}\}=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{2},0}
- i.e., the ideal defining a planar triple point - is not of the form
T(f)
, for any
f(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{2},0}
. Indeed,
\dim_{\mathbbm{C}}\,\Big(\frac{\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{2},0}}{I}\Big)=3
and it is known (cf.
[
H1
]
, Example 14.1) that there exists - up to contact equivalence - only one
f(x_{1},x_{2})
such that
\dim_{\mathbbm{C}}\,\Big(\frac{\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{2},0}}{T(f)}\Big)=3
, namely
x_{1}^{4}+x_{2}^{2}
. Now in
\frac{\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{2},0}}{I}
any non-invertible element
\xi
satisfies
\xi^{2}=0
while in
\frac{\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{2},0}}{T(x_{1}^{4}+x_{2}^{2})}
,
\eta=\bar{x_{1}}
, the residual class of
x_{1}
, is such that
\eta^{2}\neq 0
.

Example 1.5
.

Let
n\geqslant 2
and consider
I=\langle x_{1}x_{2},\ldots,x_{n-1}x_{n}\rangle=\langle\{x_{i}x_{j}\}_{i<j}\rangle
, an ideal defining the union of
n
lines in
(\mathbbm{C}^{n},0)
meeting transversally at
0
. If
n\geqslant 4
, then
I
is not a Tjurina ideal of any
f\in\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
. Indeed,
I
is minimally generated by
\frac{n(n-1)}{2}
monomials, which exceeds
n+1
if
n\geqslant 4
. If
n=3
we see that for
f=x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}
,
T(f)=\langle x_{2}x_{3},x_{1}x_{3},x_{1}x_{2}\rangle=I
. We will show below (Proposition
3.3
) that in the case
n=2
, the principal ideal
I=\langle x_{1}x_{2}\rangle
is
not
a Tjurina ideal.

Notice that for a given
f\in{\mathfrak{m}}\subset\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
the ideal
T(f)
defines the
singular (closed) subscheme
of the germ
(X,0)\subset(\mathbbm{C}^{n},0)
defined by
f
. So our Question
1.2
above has an algebraic-geometric counterpart: which closed subchemes of
(\mathbbm{C}^{n},0)
are singular subschemes of germs of hypersurfaces in
(\mathbbm{C}^{n},0)
? With this geometric viewpoint it is of course important to emphasize that the closed subscheme of
(\mathbbm{C}^{n},0)
defined by some ideal
I
is in general non-reduced and shall not be confused with the one defined by the radical
\sqrt{I}
, which is reduced. For example, in
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{2},0}
the ideal
{\mathfrak{m}}
is the Tjurina ideal of
f=x_{1}x_{2}
but the previous Example
1.4
has shown that the ideal
{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}
is not a Tjurina ideal.

With this scheme theoretic phrasing, our question was mentioned before by Hauser in
[
Ha
]
and addressed by Aluffi in
[
A
]
using what he refers to as the
\mu
-class
of the closed subscheme, in an appropriate Chow group. Aluffi, in (loc.cit.) obtains obstructions for a closed scheme to be the singular scheme of some hypersurface and out of his considerations, several interesting
non-examples
- subschemes that are
not
singular subschemes of hypersurfaces - are presented. The
\mu
-class is defined only for singular schemes of hypersurfaces. Our approach works for
any
closed subscheme of
(\mathbbm{C}^{n},0)
(or rather any ideal of
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
) providing sufficient and necessary conditions for an ideal
I
to be a Tjurina ideal.

In the literature (see
[
E
]
,
[
IK
]
,
[
Y
]
) our question is also related to the long standing
Recognition
and
Reconstruction
problems. The Recognition problem is to decide whether a given commutative
\mathbbm{C}
-algebra is isomorphic to the Tjurina algebra of some hypersurface singularity, whereas the Reconstruction problem is to find an equation for the hypersurface singularity out of which some given Tjurina algebra arises. We remark that a corollary of our approach is a solution to the problems of Recognition and Reconstruction.

We now describe the contents of the paper. In Section
2
, along with some preliminaries on Tjurina ideals, we introduce and investigate some properties of the set
\Delta(I)
of antiderivatives of an arbitrary ideal
I
, which is a natural set of elements of
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
to look at in the search for a solution
f
for the equation
I=T(f)
. We also suggest an easily applicable method for computation of
\Delta(I)
in examples, with routines already implemented in
SINGULAR

[
DGPS
]
. In Section
3
we present, by means of the introduction of two simple and quite computable operations on arbitrary ideals of
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
, our main result, namely a characterization of the ideals
I
for which the equation
I=T(f)
admits a solution
f
. In other words, we characterize Tjurina ideals giving an explicit answer for Question
1.2
. In Section
4
, some properties of the formation of Tjurina ideals are discussed in the monomial case, in connection with recent results of Epure and Schulze
[
ES
]
.

2.
Generalities

2.1.
Tjurina Ideals

As we have indicated in the Introduction, we will be concerned with Tjurina ideals of germs of functions
f\in\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
. In what follows we fix, once and for all, a basis
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
- minimal set of generators - of the maximal ideal
{\mathfrak{m}}\subset\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
; then we identify
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}=\mathbbm{C}\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
and compute
T(f)=\langle f,\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}},\ldots,\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{n}}\rangle
with respect to this basis. Occasionally, in examples with small
n
we use
x,\,\,\{x,y\},\,\,\{x,y,z\},
etc. instead of
x_{1},\,\,\{x_{1},x_{2}\},\,\,\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\},
etc. Below we collect some elementary, straightforward properties of the formation of Tjurina ideals:

Remark 2.1
.

Let
f,\,g\in\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
. Then

T(f+g)\subseteq T(f)+T(g)
;

T(fg)\subseteq fT(g)+gT(f)\subseteq T(f)T(g)\subseteq T(f)\cap T(g)
.

If
\langle f\rangle=\langle g\rangle
then
T(f)=T(g)
;

Notice that
(iii)
in the previous Remark allows us to talk about
T

of a principal ideal
. It will be useful to be able to extend
T
to an arbitrary ideal, as follows.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
J=\langle g_{1},\ldots,g_{q}\rangle
be an ideal of
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
. We denote by
T(J)
the ideal
T(g_{1})+\ldots+T(g_{q})
.

It is an easy matter, based on general properties (cf. Remark
2.1
), to check that indeed the ideal
T(J)
doesn’t depend on the particular choice of generators for
J
. It shall be referred to as
the Tjurina ideal of
J
. The properties below are easy to verify.

Remark 2.3
.

J\subseteq T(J)
;

If
J_{1}\subseteq J_{2}
is an inclusion of ideals then
T(J_{1})\subseteq T(J_{2})
;

If
\{J_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is any family of ideals of
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
then
T(\sum_{\lambda}J_{\lambda})=\sum_{\lambda}T(J_{\lambda})
;

T(J_{1}\cap J_{2})\subseteq T(J_{1})\cap T(J_{2})
.

In addition to the preceding considerations relative to Tjurina ideals and in view of our guiding Question
1.2
in which an ideal
I
is fixed, it is natural to introduce the concept below:

Definition 2.4
.

For any given ideal
I\subseteq\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
we define the
set of antiderivatives
of
I
by means of

\Delta(I)=\{f\in\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}\,|\,T(f)\subseteq I\}.

An expression for
\Delta(I)
, free of coordinates, can be given. We recall that a
\mathbbm{C}
-derivation
\delta
of
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
is a
\mathbbm{C}
-linear map
\delta
from
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
to itself that satisfies the Leibniz’ rule: for all
f,\,g\in\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
,
\delta(fg)=f\delta(g)+g\delta(f).
The set
Der_{\mathbbm{C}}(\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0})
of
\mathbbm{C}
-derivations of
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
is naturally a
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
-module, which is freely generated by the partial derivatives
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
. It is then easy to check that
\Delta(I)
coincides with

\{f\in I\,|\,\delta(f)\in I,\,\mbox{for every}\,\,\delta\in\,Der_{\mathbbm{C}}(\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0})\,\}.

This shows in particular that
\Delta(I)
does not depend on our choice of parameters
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
used to compute Tjurina ideals.

If
I
is a non-zero ideal of
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
, we use
{\rm mult}(I)
to denote
\operatorname{min}\{{\rm mult}(g)\,|\,g\in I\}=\operatorname{max}\{k\,|\,I\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}^{k}\}
. Below we summarize some easy properties of
\Delta
.

Remark 2.5
.

\Delta(I)
is an ideal of
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
;

I^{2}\subseteq\Delta(I)\subseteq I
;

If
\{I_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is any family of ideals of
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
then
\Delta(\bigcap_{\lambda}I_{\lambda})=\bigcap_{\lambda}\Delta(I_{\lambda})
;

If
I\subseteq J
is an inclusion of ideals in
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
then
\Delta(I)\subseteq\Delta(J)
;

If
I\neq\langle 0\rangle
and
I\neq\langle 1\rangle
then
{\rm mult}(\Delta(I))\geqslant{\rm mult}(I)+1
.

Proof.

The verification of
(i)
to
(iv)
is straightforward. When it comes to
(v)
, we always have
{\rm mult}(\Delta(I))\geqslant{\rm mult}(I)
. If equality holds, take
g\in\Delta(I)
such that
{\rm mult}(g)={\rm mult}(\Delta(I))={\rm mult}(I)
. In this case all partial derivatives
\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{i}}
of
g
would belong to
I
and we would obtain
{\rm mult}(\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{i}})\geqslant{\rm mult}(I)={\rm mult}(g)
. This is only possible if
g
is an invertible element of
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
. Hence
I=\langle 1\rangle
.
∎

Example 2.6
.

Let
{\mathfrak{m}}^{k}
, with
k\geqslant 1
, denote the
k
-th power of the maximal ideal of
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
. Clearly
\Delta({\mathfrak{m}}^{k})\supset{\mathfrak{m}}^{k+1}
and according to Remark
2.5
, (v), if
f\in\Delta({\mathfrak{m}}^{k})
then
{\rm mult}(f)\geqslant{\rm mult}({\mathfrak{m}}^{k})+1=k+1
. Hence,
f\in{\mathfrak{m}}^{k+1}
. It follows that
\Delta({\mathfrak{m}}^{k})={\mathfrak{m}}^{k+1}
.

Example 2.7
.

Let
I=\langle f^{k}\rangle
be a principal ideal,
k\geqslant 1
, with
f\in\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
an irreducible element. Then
\Delta(I)=\langle f^{k+1}\rangle
: because if
g\in\Delta(I)
, we have
g=af^{k}
, for some
a\in\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
and then, for all
i=1,\ldots,n
,
\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{i}}=f^{k}.\frac{\partial a}{\partial x_{i}}+akf^{k-1}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}
is also a multiple of
f^{k}
. Hence
f
must divide
ka\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}
, for all
i=1,\ldots,n
. Now, if
f
divides
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}
for all
i=1,\ldots,n
then
\langle\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}},\ldots,\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{n}}\rangle\subseteq\langle f\rangle
and we would deduce as in
[
B
]
, Lemma 1.2.13, that
f=0\in\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
, which is not the case. So, for some
i
,
f
does not divide
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}
and, being an irreducible element,
f
must divide
a
. Hence
g\in\langle f^{k+1}\rangle
.

Remark 2.8
.

Notice that if one is interested in fields
\mathbbm{k}
of positive characteristic
p
, the preceding two Examples are false, being enough to take
I=\langle x_{1}^{p}\rangle\subset\mathbbm{k}[[x_{1}]]
as counterexamples. In this case
\Delta(I)=I\neq\langle x_{1}^{p+1}\rangle
.

Proposition 2.9
.

If
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
is a prime ideal and
I\subset\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
is a
\mathfrak{p}
-primary ideal, then its ideal of antiderivatives
\Delta(I)
is also a
\mathfrak{p}
-primary ideal.

Proof.

We must check that
\Delta(I)
is primary, that is, we will show that if
f,g\in\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
,
fg\in\Delta(I)
and
g\not\in\Delta(I)
, then
f\in\sqrt{\Delta(I)}
. We argue as follows: if
g\not\in\Delta(I)
there are two possibilities to consider. The first one is
g\not\in I
. In this case, as
I
is primary, and
fg\in I
there exists some
k\geqslant 1
such that
f^{k}\in I
. The second possibility is that
g\in I
and then it is forced that, for some
i=1,\ldots,n
,
\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{i}}\not\in I
. From
\frac{\partial(fg)}{\partial x_{i}}\in I
we deduce
f\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{i}}\in I
. Again using that
I
is primary, there exists some
k\geqslant 1
such that
f^{k}\in I
. So we see that in any of the two possibilities,
f^{k}\in I
. It is then easy to check that
f^{k+1}\in\Delta(I)
as we wanted. Now, the assertion on being
\mathfrak{p}
-primary is a consequence of Remark
2.5
, (ii).
∎

By taking primary decomposition of ideals, Remark
2.5
,(iv) and the previous result imply that when it comes to compute
\Delta(I)
it is possible to restrict attention to primary ideals. One instance where this observation can be applied directly is presented in the example below.

Example 2.10
.

Let
I=\langle f\rangle
be a non-trivial principal ideal and
f=f_{1}^{k_{1}}\ldots f_{r}^{k_{r}}
be a factorization of
f
into irreducible, non-associated, elements with positive
k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}
. Then
I=\langle f_{1}^{k_{1}}\rangle\cap\ldots\cap\langle f_{r}^{k_{r}}\rangle
. It follows from Remark
2.5
, (iii) and Example
2.7
that
\Delta(I)=\langle f_{1}^{k_{1}+1}\rangle\cap\ldots\cap\langle f_{r}^{k_{r}+1}\rangle=\langle f_{1}^{k_{1}+1}\ldots f_{r}^{k_{r}+1}\rangle
.

2.2.
Computation of
\Delta(I)

To this point we have computed explicitly the ideal of antiderivatives
\Delta(I)
only in the case where
I
is a power of the maximal ideal (cf. Example
2.6
) or
I
is a principal ideal (cf. Example
2.10
). Now we suggest a method to compute
\Delta(I)
for an arbitrary ideal
I\subset\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
. Our procedure will be illustrated with a number of examples obtained using basic routines already implemented in the software
SINGULAR
,
[
DGPS
]
.

Fix a basis
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
- minimal set of generators - of the maximal ideal
{\mathfrak{m}}\subset\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
; we identify
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}=\mathbbm{C}\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
as before. A free
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
-module of certain rank
\ell
will be denoted by
F_{\ell}
; also, for any
\underline{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{\ell})^{t}
,
\underline{c}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{\ell})^{t}\in F_{\ell}
we put
\underline{b}\cdot\underline{c}
to denote the element
\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}b_{k}c_{k}\in\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
.

We assume the ideal
I
given by generators
I=\langle f_{1},\ldots,f_{s}\rangle
. Then some element
g=\underline{a}\cdot\underline{f}=\sum_{k}a_{k}f_{k}\in I
is an element of
\Delta(I)
if and only if, for all
j=1,\ldots,n
, the partial derivative

\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{j}}=\sum_{k}a_{k}\frac{\partial f_{k}}{\partial x_{j}}+\sum_{k}\frac{\partial a_{k}}{\partial x_{j}}f_{k}=\underline{a}\cdot\frac{\partial\underline{f}}{\partial x_{j}}+\frac{\partial\underline{a}}{\partial x_{j}}\cdot\underline{f}

belongs to
I
. In other words,
g=\underline{a}\cdot\underline{f}\in\Delta(I)
if and only if
\underline{a}\cdot\frac{\partial\underline{f}}{\partial x_{j}}\in I,
for all
j=1,\ldots,n
.

For each
j=1,\ldots,n
we denote by
M^{j}
the submodule of
F_{s}
consisting of the elements
\underline{a}\in F_{s}
such that
\underline{a}\cdot\frac{\partial\underline{f}}{\partial x_{j}}\in I
. Then
M:=\bigcap_{j=1}^{n}\,M^{j}\subseteq F_{s}
is a finitely generated
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
-submodule of
F_{s}
consisting of elements
\underline{a}\in\,F_{s}
such that
g=\underline{a}\cdot\underline{f}\in\Delta(I)
. Let
\underline{m}_{\,1},\ldots,\underline{m}_{\,q}\in F_{s}
be generators of
M
:
\underline{m}_{\,i}=(m_{1i},\ldots,m_{si})^{t}
, where
m_{ki}\in\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
.

Notation being as above we have

Proposition 2.11
.

The ideal of antiderivatives
\Delta(I)
is generated by
\underline{m}_{\,i}\cdot\underline{f}
, for
i=1,\ldots,q
.

Proof.

As discussed above,
g\in\Delta(I)
if and only if
g=\underline{a}\cdot\underline{f}\in\Delta(I)
for some
\underline{a}\in F_{s}
such that
\underline{a}\cdot\frac{\partial\underline{f}}{\partial x_{j}}\in I,
for all
j=1,\ldots,n
. That is, if and only if there exists some
\underline{a}\in M
such that
g=\underline{a}\cdot\underline{f}
. Since
M
is generated by the
\underline{m}_{\,i}
, the result follows from the
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
-linearity of the
(-)\cdot\underline{f}
product.
∎

3.
Two key conditions

3.1.
T-fullness

We now introduce
T
-fullness, a natural concept which together with
T
-dependence (cf. Definition
3.7
below) is present in our characterization of Tjurina ideals of
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
(see Theorem
3.12
).

Recall the definition of the Tjurina ideal of an arbitrary ideal of
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
(cf. Definition
2.2
).

Definition 3.1
.

Let
I
be an ideal of
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
. In general we have
T(\Delta(I))\subseteq I
. We say that
I
is
T
-full
if
I=T(\Delta(I))
.

Example 3.2
.

Let
I=\langle xy,x^{4}+y^{3}\rangle\subset\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{2},0}=\mathbbm{C}\{x,y\}
. Following the routine of SINGULAR,
[
DGPS
]
, described as in the example used to illustrate Proposition
2.11
, we compute

M=\begin{pmatrix}4y^{2}&3x^{3}&xy&y^{3}&x^{4}\\
x&y&0&0&0\\
\end{pmatrix}\subset F_{2}.

Hence,
\Delta(I)=\langle 5xy^{3}+x^{5},4x^{4}y+y^{4},x^{2}y^{2},xy^{4},x^{5}y\rangle
. Then
xy\not\in T(\Delta(I))=\langle x^{2}y,xy^{2},x^{4}+y^{3},x^{5}\rangle
, so
I
is not
T
-full.

T
-fullness is a property that characterizes Tjurina ideals in the special case of principal ideals. This is discussed in detail in the result below:

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
n\geqslant 2
and
I\subset\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
be a (non-trivial) principal ideal.

(i)

If
I=\langle f^{k}\rangle
with
f\in\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
irreducible,
k\geqslant 1
. Then
I
is a Tjurina ideal if and only if
f
has multiplicity one;

(ii)

Let
I=\langle f\rangle
,
f=f_{1}^{k_{1}}\ldots f_{r}^{k_{r}}
, with non associated irreducible factors
f_{i}
and
r\geqslant 2
. Then
I
is not a Tjurina ideal;

(iii)

I
is a Tjurina ideal if and only if
I
is
T
-full.

Proof.

If
f
has multiplicity one, clearly
I=\langle f^{k}\rangle=T(f^{k+1})
. Conversely, if
I=\langle f^{k}\rangle
with
f
irreducible then as we have seen in Example
2.7
,
\Delta(I)
consists of multiples of
f^{k+1}
. Hence
T(\Delta(I))=T(f^{k+1})=f^{k}T(f)=IT(f)
. Therefore, if
I
is a Tjurina ideal it equals
T(f^{k+1})
and in this case, by Nakayama’s Lemma, we conclude that
T(f)=\langle 1\rangle=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
. Since
f\in{\mathfrak{m}}
this last condition can occur only if
f
has multiplicity one. This is part
(i)
.

Now, let
I=\langle f\rangle
be a principal ideal and
f=f_{1}^{k_{1}}\ldots f_{r}^{k_{r}}
as in
(ii)
. We have seen in Example
2.10
that
\Delta(I)
consists of multiples of
f_{1}^{k_{1}+1}\ldots f_{r}^{k_{r}+1}
. Hence
T(\Delta(I))=T(f_{1}^{k_{1}+1}\ldots f_{r}^{k_{r}+1})
. Therefore, if
I
is a Tjurina ideal it equals
T(f_{1}^{k_{1}+1}\ldots f_{r}^{k_{r}+1})
. A routine computation using Leibniz’ rule shows that for all
j=1,\ldots,n
,

\frac{\partial(f_{1}^{k_{1}+1}\ldots f_{r}^{k_{r}+1})}{\partial x_{j}}\in\langle f_{1}^{k_{1}}\ldots f_{r}^{k_{r}}\rangle\cdot\langle\{f_{1}\ldots f_{i-1}\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}f_{i+1}\ldots f_{r}\}_{i=1}^{r}\rangle.

As a result
T(f_{1}^{k_{1}+1}\ldots f_{r}^{k_{r}+1})\subseteq IK
, where
K
is the ideal generated by
f_{1}\ldots f_{r}
and

\{f_{1}\ldots f_{i-1}\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}f_{i+1}\ldots f_{r}\}_{i,j},

where
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
and
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Hence
T(\Delta(I))\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}^{r-1}I
and since
r\geqslant 2
we conclude, using Nakayama’s Lemma, that
I
is not a Tjurina ideal. This finishes part
(ii)
.

Part
(iii)
is a consequence of the proofs presented for
(i)
and
(ii)
.
∎

Example 3.4
.

The previous Proposition
3.3
,
(i)
shows that there is no
f
with
T(f)
equal to, say,
I=\langle x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{3}\rangle
. Besides,
(ii)
applies for
I=\langle x_{1}x_{2}\rangle
, (the case
n=2
of Example
1.5
) showing that
I
is not a Tjurina ideal.

The general significance (beyond principal ideals) of
T
-fullness in our investigation is apparent in the next result.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
I
be an ideal of
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
. If
I
is a Tjurina ideal then
I
is
T
-full.

Proof.

Let
\Delta(I)=\langle g_{1},\ldots,g_{q}\rangle
. If
I
is a Tjurina ideal, then there exists
f\in\Delta(I)
such that
I=T(f)
. We may write
f=\sum_{k=1}^{q}r_{k}g_{k}
, for some
r_{k}\in\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
. Using Remark
2.1
, we obtain
I=T(f)\subseteq\sum_{k=1}^{q}T(r_{k}g_{k})\subseteq\sum_{k=1}^{q}T(g_{k})=T(\Delta(I))\subseteq I
and equality holds throughout. We conclude that
I
is
T
-full.
∎

In the general case
T
-fullness is not sufficient for
I
to be a Tjurina ideal as shows the next

Example 3.6
.

Let
I=\langle x^{2},xy,y^{2}\rangle={\mathfrak{m}}^{2}\subset\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{2},0}=\mathbbm{C}\{x,y\}
. We have
\Delta({\mathfrak{m}}^{2})={\mathfrak{m}}^{3}=\langle x^{3},x^{2}y,xy^{2},y^{3}\rangle
, so
T(\Delta(I))=T(x^{3})+T(x^{2}y)+T(xy^{2})+T(y^{3})={\mathfrak{m}}^{2}=I
and
I
is
T
-full. We have seen before that
I
is not a Tjurina ideal.

3.2.
T-dependence

Here we explain the last ingredient which is, together with
T
-fullness (cf. Definition
3.1
), present in our characterization of Tjurina ideals.

We begin with an example. Consider the ideal
I={\mathfrak{m}}
and its ideal of antiderivatives
\Delta(I)={\mathfrak{m}}^{2}=\langle x^{2},xy,y^{2}\rangle
inside
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{2},0}=\mathbbm{C}\{x,y\}
. We notice that a
general

\mathbbm{C}
-linear combination of the given generators of
\Delta(I)
is a germ
f
satisfying
I=T(f)
. By contrast, for
special
linear combinations of the generators, the corresponding
T(f)
is smaller than
I
.

We aim to coin a concept which encompasses the above example and systematizes the intuition, that of a general linear combination
f
of the given generators of
\Delta(I)
has the largest possible
T(f)
and could reveal whether a given ideal
I
is a Tjurina ideal. To this end, our results and definitions here are expressed geometrically, since it seemed to us more appropriate to explain these ideas. Therefore, we use standard concepts on schemes, consistent with
[
H2
]
, Chapter II, to which we refer for unexplained terminology.

Let
J\subset\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
be any ideal. We regard
J
as an ideal sheaf on
\mbox{Spec}\,\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
. Suppose
J
is given by generators:
J=\langle g_{1},\ldots,g_{q}\rangle\subset\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
; then consider the projective
(q-1)
-space over
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
, namely,
{\mathbbm{P}}^{q-1}=\mbox{Proj}(S)
, where
S=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}[\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{q}]=\bigoplus_{d\geqslant 0}\,S_{d}
is the ring of polynomials over
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
, with variables
\alpha_{i}
, graded so that
\operatorname{deg}\alpha_{i}=1
, for all
i
.

Let
\pi:{\mathbbm{P}}^{q-1}\rightarrow\mbox{Spec}\,\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
be the natural morphism of
\mbox{Spec}\,\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
-schemes and let
\sigma
denote the global section
\sum_{i=1}^{q}\,g_{i}\alpha_{i}
of
\pi^{*}(J)\otimes\mathscr{O}_{{\mathbbm{P}}^{q-1}}(1)
. Then there is a
Tjurina ideal sheaf
of
\sigma
on
{\mathbbm{P}}^{q-1}
, namely
\mathscr{T}(\sigma)
, the sheaf associated to the homogeneous ideal
\langle\sigma,\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial x_{1}},\ldots,\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial x_{n}}\rangle
of
S
. Clearly
\mathscr{T}(\sigma)
is a subsheaf of
\pi^{*}(T(J))
, so we can consider the quotient sheaf

\mathscr{F}=\frac{\pi^{*}(T(J))}{\mathscr{T}(\sigma)}

on
{\mathbbm{P}}^{q-1}
. For later use, we recall that since
\mathscr{F}
is coherent and
{\mathbbm{P}}^{q-1}
is a noetherian scheme, the support
\mbox{Supp}\mathscr{F}
of
\mathscr{F}
is a closed subscheme of
{\mathbbm{P}}^{q-1}
given by the vanishing of
\Big(\mathscr{T}(\sigma):\pi^{*}(T(J))\Big)
.

Definition 3.7
.

We say that an ideal
J\subset\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
is
T
-dependent
if
\pi^{-1}({\mathfrak{m}})\not\subset\mbox{Supp}\mathscr{F}
. Equivalently,
J
is
T
-dependent if
\Big(\mathscr{T}(\sigma):\pi^{*}(T(J))\Big)\not\subset{\mathfrak{m}}S
.

We check that
T
-dependence for
J
is a well-defined concept, being independent on the choice of generators for
J
. For, let the ideal
J
be given by another system of generators, say
J=\langle h_{1},\ldots,h_{u}\rangle
. Let
{\mathbbm{P}}^{u-1}=\mbox{Proj}(S^{\prime})
, being
S^{\prime}=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}[\,\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{u}]
. Carry the above construction with the obvious morphism
\pi^{\prime}:{\mathbbm{P}}^{u-1}\rightarrow\mbox{Spec}\,\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
and the section
\sigma^{\prime}=\sum_{j=1}^{u}\,h_{j}\beta_{j}
instead of
\pi
and
\sigma
, obtaining a sheaf
\mathscr{F^{\prime}}
on
{\mathbbm{P}}^{u-1}
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Notation being as above,
\pi^{-1}({\mathfrak{m}})\not\subset\mbox{Supp}\mathscr{F}
if and only if
\pi^{\prime-1}({\mathfrak{m}})\not\subset\mbox{Supp}\mathscr{F^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

We will show that
\pi^{-1}({\mathfrak{m}})\not\subset\mbox{Supp}\mathscr{F}
implies
\pi^{\prime-1}({\mathfrak{m}})\not\subset\mbox{Supp}\mathscr{F^{\prime}}
. The other implication can be proved analogously.

Since the
g_{i}
’s and the
h_{j}
’s generate the same ideal
J
of
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
, we can write for all
i
,
g_{i}=\sum_{j}r_{ji}h_{j}
with some
r_{ji}\in\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
and at least one
r_{ji}\not\in{\mathfrak{m}}
. We construct an homomorphism
\Phi:S^{\prime}\rightarrow S
of graded
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
-algebras (preserving degrees) induced by
\beta_{j}\mapsto\sum_{i}r_{ji}\alpha_{i}
. This gives a natural morphism of
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
-schemes,
\varphi:U\rightarrow{\mathbbm{P}}^{u-1}
, where
U\subset{\mathbbm{P}}^{q-1}
is the complement of the indeterminacy locus of
\varphi
, given by
V(\Phi(\beta_{1}),\ldots,\Phi(\beta_{u}))\subset{\mathbbm{P}}^{q-1}
. Observe that
\pi^{-1}({\mathfrak{m}})
is not contained in the indeterminacy locus of
\varphi
because
\Phi(\beta_{j})\not\in{\mathfrak{m}}S
for at least one
j
. Notice also that the construction of
\varphi
implies both
\sigma|_{U}=\varphi^{*}(\sigma^{\prime})
and
\pi|_{U}=\pi^{\prime}\circ\varphi
. In particular,
\mathscr{T}(\sigma)|_{U}=\varphi^{*}(\mathscr{T}(\sigma^{\prime}))
and
\pi^{*}(T(J))|_{U}=\varphi^{*}\pi^{\prime*}(T(J))
. According to the definition of
\mathscr{F}
and
\mathscr{F^{\prime}}
, we deduce
\mathscr{F}|_{U}=\varphi^{*}\mathscr{F}^{\prime}
.

Assume
\pi^{-1}({\mathfrak{m}})\not\subset\mbox{Supp}\mathscr{F}
. As observed above
\mbox{Supp}\mathscr{F}
is closed in the irreducible
{\mathbbm{P}}^{q-1}
. It follows that there exists some
P\in\pi^{-1}({\mathfrak{m}})\cap U
,
P\not\in\mbox{Supp}\mathscr{F}
. Since
U\cap\mbox{Supp}\mathscr{F}=\varphi^{-1}(\mbox{Supp}\mathscr{F}^{\prime})
, we have
\varphi(P)\not\in\mbox{Supp}\mathscr{F^{\prime}}
and
{\mathfrak{m}}S^{\prime}\subset\Phi^{-1}({\mathfrak{m}}S)\subset\Phi^{-1}(P)=\varphi(P)
. Therefore
\varphi(P)\in\pi^{\prime-1}({\mathfrak{m}})
, showing that
\pi^{\prime-1}({\mathfrak{m}})\not\subset\mbox{Supp}\mathscr{F^{\prime}}
.∎

3.3.
Main Result

We are ready to state and prove our result characterizing Tjurina ideals.

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
I\subset\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
be an ideal. Then
I
is a Tjurina ideal if and only if
I
is
T
-full and
\Delta(I)
is
T
-dependent.

Proof.

Let
\Delta(I)=\langle g_{1},\ldots,g_{q}\rangle
and let
\mathscr{F}
denote the sheaf
\pi^{*}(T(\Delta(I)))/\mathscr{T}(\sigma)
on
{\mathbbm{P}}^{q-1}
, as described before. For any
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{q})\in\mathbbm{C}^{q}\setminus\{0\}
we will denote by
p_{\lambda}\subseteq\mathbbm{C}[\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{q}]
the homogeneous prime ideal generated by
\{\lambda_{k}\alpha_{\ell}-\lambda_{\ell}\alpha_{k}\}_{k,{\ell}}
. If
P\in D_{+}(\alpha_{\ell})\subset{\mathbbm{P}}^{q-1}
one checks easily that
\mathscr{T}(\sigma)_{P}=T\Big(\sigma/\alpha_{\ell}\Big)S_{(P)}
, where parenthetical notation
-\,_{(P)}
(here and in what follows) indicates the submodule of degree zero elements of the localization of a graded module at
P\in{\mathbbm{P}}^{q-1}
. Moreover, if
p_{\lambda}S\subseteq P\in D_{+}(\alpha_{\ell})
then
\lambda_{\ell}\in\mathbbm{C}\setminus 0
and we can use Remark
2.1
to obtain the following Estimate on ideals in
S_{(P)}
:

(3.1)

T\Big(\sum_{k}\Big(\lambda_{k}-\frac{\lambda_{\ell}\alpha_{k}}{\alpha_{\ell}}\Big)g_{k}\Big)S_{(P)}\subseteq\\
\subseteq\sum_{k}T\Big(\Big(\lambda_{k}-\frac{\lambda_{\ell}\alpha_{k}}{\alpha_{\ell}}\Big)g_{k}\Big)S_{(P)}\subseteq\sum_{k}\Big(\lambda_{k}-\frac{\lambda_{\ell}\alpha_{k}}{\alpha_{\ell}}\Big)T(g_{k})S_{(P)}\subseteq IP_{(P)}.

To prove the direct affirmation in the statement, we assume that
I
is a Tjurina ideal. We have already seen that
I
is
T
-full (cf. Proposition
3.5
); in particular
\mathscr{F}=\pi^{*}(I)/\mathscr{T}(\sigma)
. Now we show that
\Delta(I)
is
T
-dependent. Since this is clear if
I
is principal, particularly if
I=\langle 0\rangle
- cf. Examples
2.10
and
3.11
- we assume
I=T(f)
, non-principal, with
f=\sum_{k}r_{k}g_{k}
for certain
r_{k}\in\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
. We write
r_{k}=\lambda_{k}+s_{k}
, for
\lambda_{k}\in\mathbbm{C}
and
s_{k}\in{\mathfrak{m}}
, for all
k=1,\ldots,q
. Since
I\neq\langle 0\rangle
we can use Remark
2.5
, (v) to check that
f\not\in{\mathfrak{m}}\Delta(I)
. Hence, at least one of the
r_{k}
’s is an invertible element of
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
. Hence, using Remark
2.1
again we can (and do) assume
(r_{1},\ldots,r_{q})=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{q})\in\mathbbm{C}^{q}\setminus\{0\}
. We consider
P=p_{\lambda}S+{\mathfrak{m}}S\in{\mathbbm{P}}^{q-1}
. Then
P\in\pi^{-1}({\mathfrak{m}})
, so that
\pi^{*}(I)_{P}=I_{\pi(P)}\otimes S_{(P)}=I_{{\mathfrak{m}}}\otimes S_{(P)}=IS_{(P)}
. On the other hand (say if
P\in D_{+}(\alpha_{\ell})
), our assumption
I=T(f)
together with Estimate (
3.1
) above produces

IS_{(P)}=T\Big(\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}g_{k}\Big)S_{(P)}\subseteq\\
\subseteq T\Big(\sum_{k}\Big(\lambda_{k}-\frac{\lambda_{\ell}\alpha_{k}}{\alpha_{\ell}}\Big)g_{k}\Big)S_{(P)}+T\Big(\lambda_{\ell}\sigma/\alpha_{\ell}\Big)S_{(P)}\subseteq IP_{(P)}+\mathscr{T}(\sigma)_{P}\subseteq IS_{(P)}.

It follows that
IP_{(P)}+\mathscr{T}(\sigma)_{P}=IS_{(P)}
. Then we can use Nakayama’s Lemma to obtain
\pi^{*}(I)_{P}=IS_{(P)}=\mathscr{T}(\sigma)_{P}
. Hence
P\not\in\mbox{Supp}\mathscr{F}
, which completes the proof that
\Delta(I)
is
T
-dependent.

To prove the reverse affirmation in the statement
I
is assumed to be
T
-full and
\Delta(I)
is assumed to be
T
-dependent. Then
I=T(\Delta(I))
and there exists some
P\in\pi^{-1}({\mathfrak{m}})\setminus\mbox{Supp}\mathscr{F}
. It follows that

\pi^{*}(I)_{P}=\pi^{*}(T(\Delta(I))_{P}=\mathscr{T}(\sigma)_{P}.

Notice that out of the decomposition
S=\mathbbm{C}[\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{q}]+{\mathfrak{m}}S
we obtain
P=P\cap\mathbbm{C}[\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{q}]+P\cap{\mathfrak{m}}S=P\cap\mathbbm{C}[\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{q}]+{\mathfrak{m}}S
. Therefore, there exists some
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{q})\in\mathbbm{C}^{q}\setminus\{0\}
,
p_{\lambda}\supseteq P\cap\mathbbm{C}[\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{q}]
, such that the homogeneous prime ideal
p_{\lambda}S+{\mathfrak{m}}S
also belongs to
\pi^{-1}({\mathfrak{m}})\setminus\mbox{Supp}\mathscr{F}
.

With this simplification - assuming
P=p_{\lambda}S+{\mathfrak{m}}S\in D_{+}(\alpha_{\ell})
, as before - the displayed equality of stalks above reads
IS_{(P)}=T\Big(\lambda_{\ell}\sigma/\alpha_{\ell}\Big)S_{(P)}.
The task is to prove
I=T(f)
, for
f=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}g_{k}
. Estimate (
3.1
) here gives

IS_{(P)}\subseteq T\Big(\sum_{k}\Big(\lambda_{k}-\frac{\lambda_{\ell}\alpha_{k}}{\alpha_{\ell}}\Big)g_{k}\Big)S_{(P)}+T\Big(\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}g_{k}\Big)S_{(P)}\subseteq\\
\subseteq IP_{(P)}+T\Big(\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}g_{k}\Big)S_{(P)}\subseteq IS_{(P)}.

We obtain
IP_{(P)}+T(\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}g_{k})S_{(P)}=IS_{(P)}
and by Nakayama’s Lemma again we deduce
T(\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}g_{k})S_{(P)}=IS_{(P)}
.

It remains to verify that
I\subseteq T(\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}g_{k})
. Clearly it suffices to check that if
h\in I
and there are some
u\in T(\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}g_{k})
and
G_{1},G_{2}\in S
, homogeneous of same degree with
G_{2}\not\in P
such that
G_{2}h=G_{1}u
, then
h\in T(\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}g_{k})
. With this reduction, a simple argument involving divisibility and degree in the UFD
S
then shows
zh=uH
, for some homogeneous element
H\in S
and some degree zero factor
z
of
G_{2}
. In particular,
z\in\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}\setminus{\mathfrak{m}}
. Again by degree reasons,
H
has degree zero, i.e., it belongs to
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
. This shows that
h\in T(\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}g_{k})
. Hence
I\subseteq T(\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}g_{k})\subseteq I
and we conclude that
I
is a Tjurina ideal of
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
.∎

Notation as in the proof of Theorem
3.12
we have the Corollary below, the usefulness of which resides in the fact it says that computing the Tjurina ideal of a sufficiently general
\mathbbm{C}
-linear combination of generators of
\Delta(I)
reveals whether
I\subset\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
is a Tjurina ideal. As a consequence it gives an explicit solution to both the Recognition and the Reconstruction problems mentioned in the Introduction.

Corollary 3.13
.

Let
I\subset\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
be an ideal and let
\Delta(I)=\langle g_{1},\ldots,g_{q}\rangle
. Then
I
is a Tjurina ideal if and only if
I=T(\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}g_{k})
for
p_{\lambda}
varying in a (non empty) Zariski open subset of
{\mathbbm{P}}_{\mathbbm{C}}^{q-1}
.

Proof.

The canonical inclusion
\mathbbm{C}\rightarrow\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
induces
\mathbbm{C}[\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{q}]\rightarrow S
, a graded inclusion of
\mathbbm{C}
-algebras; hence a morphism
\rho:{\mathbbm{P}}^{q-1}\rightarrow{\mathbbm{P}}_{\mathbbm{C}}^{q-1}
, which is proper and dominant. For the non-trivial part of the statement, assume that
I
is a Tjurina ideal. From the Theorem, we know that
\Delta(I)
is
T
-dependent, so that
\pi^{-1}({\mathfrak{m}})\setminus\mbox{Supp}\mathscr{F}
is a non empty Zariski open subset of
\pi^{-1}({\mathfrak{m}})
. It follows that its image under
\rho
is a non-empty open Zariski subset
U\subset{\mathbbm{P}}_{\mathbbm{C}}^{q-1}
. Homogeneous prime ideals
p_{\lambda}
in
U
correspond bijectively, via
\rho
, to homogeneous prime ideals of type
p_{\lambda}S+{\mathfrak{m}}S\in\pi^{-1}({\mathfrak{m}})\setminus\mbox{Supp}\mathscr{F}
. Since the Theorem also guarantees that
I
is
T
-full, we can proceed as in its proof to show that
I=T(\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}g_{k})
, for all
p_{\lambda}\in U
.∎

4.
Remarks and Examples

Now that we achieved a complete characterization of Tjurina ideals by means of Theorem
3.12
, we include some observations regarding intersections of Tjurina ideals. We focus on monomial ideals, since it already contains the kind of phenomenon we aim to illustrate.

Example 4.1
.

Irreducible
monomial ideals are necessarily Tjurina ideals. Recall that irreducible monomial ideals are of the form
\langle x_{i_{1}}^{e_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{s}}^{e_{s}}\rangle
, for
\{i_{1}<\ldots<i_{s}\}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}
and all
e_{j}>0
. Hence

\langle x_{i_{1}}^{e_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{s}}^{e_{s}}\rangle=T(x_{i_{1}}^{e_{1}+1}+\ldots+x_{i_{s}}^{e_{s}+1}).

The following well-known result (see
[
HH
]
, Theorem 1.3.1, Corollary 1.3.2, for instance) motivates us to ask questions about monomial ideals which are also Tjurina ideals.

Proposition 4.2
.

Any monomial ideal
I\subset\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{n},0}
can be written uniquely as a finite and irredundant intersection
I=\cap_{k=1}^{m}\,Q_{k}
of
irreducible
monomial ideals.

Of course we regard the irreducible monomial ideals as the building blocks of more general monomial ideals. Unfortunately, the result of the previous Example does not extend to arbitrary monomial ideals:
\langle x^{2},xy,y^{2}\rangle=\langle x^{2},y\rangle\cap\langle x,y^{2}\rangle\subset\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{2},0}=\mathbbm{C}\{x,y\}
is not a Tjurina ideal (cf. Example
1.4
); even for principal monomial ideals such a result does not hold (cf. Proposition
3.3
).

This is
not
to say that reducible monomial ideals are never Tjurina ideals. For instance,
I=\langle yz,xz,xy\rangle=\langle x,y\rangle\cap\langle x,z\rangle\cap\langle y,z\rangle\subset\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{3},0}=\mathbbm{C}\{x,y,z\}
of Example
1.5
is a Tjurina ideal. Indeed, quite recently Epure and Schulze characterized in their main result of
[
ES
]
, functions with monomial Tjurina ideals as those which are right equivalent to Thom-Sebastiani polynomials, solving
Problem

2^{*}
in
[
HaS
]
, which asks for such a characterization.

In our computations of Example
4.5
, the next example will be useful.

Example 4.3
.

Let
I=\langle x_{i_{1}}^{e_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{s}}^{e_{s}}\rangle
be an irreducible monomial ideal as in Example
4.1
. From the results in Section
2
, discussion before Proposition
2.11
we deduce that
a_{1}x_{i_{1}}^{e_{1}}+\ldots+a_{s}x_{i_{s}}^{e_{s}}\in\Delta(I)
if and only if
a_{k}x_{i_{k}}^{e_{k}-1}\in I
, for all
k=1,\ldots,s
. Here we used that
I
is monomial. This, in turn, happens if and only if
a_{k}\in(I:x_{i_{k}}^{e_{k}-1})=\langle x_{i_{1}}^{e_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{k}},\ldots,x_{i_{s}}^{e_{s}}\rangle
. As a result we have
\Delta(I)=\langle x_{i_{1}}^{e_{1}+1},\ldots,x_{i_{s}}^{e_{s}+1}\rangle+I^{2}
, a monomial ideal.

Remark 4.4
.

The previous Example together with Proposition
4.2
and Remark
2.5
, (iii) show that the ideal of antiderivatives of a monomial ideal is also monomial.

It seems worth examining the Example below, in which a monomial and reducible Tjurina ideal is presented, together with some considerations regarding the dynamics of formation of Tjurina ideals. The Example also provides an example of a Tjurina ideal with an embedded component of codimension
2
, so answering negatively a Question left by Aluffi (
[
A
]
, Example 3.1).

Example 4.5
.

Let
f=xw^{2}-yz^{2}\in\mathscr{O}_{\mathbbm{C}^{4},0}=\mathbbm{C}\{x,y,z,w\}
. It has a monomial Tjurina ideal given by
T(f)=\langle yz,z^{2},xw,w^{2}\rangle=I_{1}\cap I_{2}\cap I_{3}\cap I_{4}
, where
I_{1}=\langle z,w\rangle
,
I_{2}=\langle y,z^{2},w\rangle
,
I_{3}=\langle x,z,w^{2}\rangle
and
I_{4}=\langle x,y,z^{2},w^{2}\rangle
. The ideal
T(f)
has an embedded component of codimension
2
at
\langle x,y,z^{2},w^{2}\rangle
.

We notice that
I_{1}\cap I_{2}\cap I_{3}\cap I_{4}
is a Tjurina ideal but that some “partial intersections” are not. For instance
I_{1}\cap I_{4}
or
I_{2}\cap I_{3}
are not Tjurina ideals as one can easily verify computing numbers of generators: both are minimally
6
-generated.

Harder is to check that
I_{1}\cap I_{2}\cap I_{3}=\langle yz,z^{2},xw,zw,w^{2}\rangle
is not a Tjurina ideal. We begin computing its ideal of antiderivatives
\Delta(I_{1}\cap I_{2}\cap I_{3})=\Delta(I_{1})\cap\Delta(I_{2})\cap\Delta(I_{3})
. Using the result of the previous Example we get
\Delta(I_{1})=\langle z^{2},w^{2},zw\rangle,\,\,\,

\Delta(I_{2})=\langle y^{2},z^{3},w^{2},yz^{2},yw,z^{2}w\rangle,\,\,\,
and
\Delta(I_{3})=\langle x^{2},z^{2},w^{3},xz,xw^{2},zw^{2}\rangle.
Hence

\Delta(I_{1}\cap I_{2}\cap I_{3})=\langle yz^{2},z^{3},z^{2}w,xw^{2},zw^{2},w^{3},xyzw\rangle.

It is clear now that
I_{1}\cap I_{2}\cap I_{3}
is
T
-full but we can check with SINGULAR
[
DGPS
]
that
\Delta(I_{1}\cap I_{2}\cap I_{3})
is not
T
-dependent. Then Theorem
3.12
, guarantees that
I_{1}\cap I_{2}\cap I_{3}
is not a Tjurina ideal.

Moduli of sheaves on fourfolds as derived Lagrangian intersections
Nachiketa Adhikari, Yun Shi

Abstract
. We show that any ( − 2 ) (-2) -shifted symplectic derived scheme X (of finite type over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero) is locally equivalent to the derived intersection of two Lagrangian morphisms to a ( − 1 ) (-1) -shifted symplectic derived scheme which is the ( − 1 ) (-1) -shifted cotangent stack of a smooth classical scheme. This leads to the possibility of the following viewpoint that is, at least to us, new: any n n -shifted symplectic derived scheme can be obtained, locally, by repeated derived Lagrangian intersections in a smooth classical scheme. We also give a separate proof of our main result in the case where the local Darboux atlas cdga for X has an even number of generators in degree ( − 1 ) (-1) ; in this case we strengthen the result by showing that X is in fact locally equivalent to the derived critical locus of a shifted function, which we’ve been told is a folklore result in the field. We indicate the implications of this for derived moduli stacks of sheaves on Calabi-Yau fourfolds by spelling out the case when the fourfold is ℂ 4 \mathbb{C}^{4} .



1.
Introduction

It is well-known (see, for example,
[
BBS13
, §2.2]
,
[
RS21
, §2]
) that the moduli stack
\mathcal{M}^{d}(\mathbb{C}^{3})
of coherent sheaves of length
d
on
\mathbb{C}^{3}
can be written as the critical locus of a regular function on an ambient stack i.e. let
R:=End_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{3}
, the affine space of triples of
d\times d
matrices; the function

defined by

descends to the quotient stack

of

by the conjugation action of

can be identified with the critical locus of

\mathfrak{M}

One can rephrase this as follows: the cotangent stack

(i.e. the total space of the cotangent bundle of
\mathfrak{M}
, which is naturally a symplectic stack) contains two Lagrangian substacks isomorphic to
\mathfrak{M}
, one of them obtained by embedding
\mathfrak{M}
into it via the zero section, and the other by embedding via the section
d_{dR}f
. Then
\mathcal{M}^{d}(\mathbb{C}^{3})
is the intersection of these two Lagrangians inside
\mathfrak{M}
, so we have a pullback diagram

(1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathfrak{M}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathfrak{M}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{T^{*}\mathfrak{M}}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d_{dR}f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces z}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

carries a canonical

-shifted symplectic structure by the following theorem:

(
[

, Theorem 2.9]
)
.

Let

be an

-shifted symplectic derived Artin stack and

,

two morphisms of derived Artin stacks carrying Lagrangian structures. Then the derived stack

On the other hand, we also have the following theorem.

(
[

, Theorem 0.1]
,
[

, §1]
)
.

Let

For

Motivated by these results about

Question 1

Does a picture similar to

-shifted symplectic structure by

, not a

-shifted one. More generally:

Given a

-shifted symplectic derived scheme, when can we say that it is the derived intersection of two Lagrangians inside a
(-1)
-shifted symplectic derived scheme?

We obtain the following answers:

The answer to

.

Let

.

(

1.1.
Generalizations

Here are some potential generalizations of the results in this paper, which we leave for future exploration.

•

We expect all results about
(-2)
-shifted symplectic derived schemes can be extended to
(-2)
-shifted symplectic derived stacks in a manner analogous to the generalization of the Darboux Theorem in
[
BBJ19
]
to the one in
[
Ben+15
]
. Consequently,
Theorem

3.13
can be rephrased as a statement about
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}^{d}(\mathbb{C}^{4})

itself
being a derived critical locus (instead of needing to work on an atlas).

•

Preliminary calculations show that similar adaptation techniques can be applied to the Darboux Theorems in
[
Ben+15
]
to obtain a general result along the lines of:
“given
n<0
, any
n
-shifted symplectic derived stack is locally equivalent to a Lagrangian intersection in an
(n+1)
-shifted symplectic derived stack”
. We have not included such a general result here but expect it to be true.

1.2.
Notation

We work in the derived algebraic geometry framework introduced in
[
TV05
,
TV08
]
and elsewhere. Here are some of the conventions we adopt in this paper:

•

We work over a field
k
which is algebraically closed with characteristic
0
.

•

All schemes are of finite type over
k
. We often refer to such schemes as
classical
or ordinary to
distinguish
them from derived schemes.

•

We use the abbreviation
dga
to refer to a differential graded algebra and
cdga
to refer to a graded-commutative differential algebra. All such algebras
A
are connective (i.e.
H^{i}(A)=0
for
i>0
), and the differentials
d:A^{m}\to A^{m+1}
are of degree +1.

•

We use boldface letters to denote derived schemes and stacks e.g.
X
,
\bf\mathcal{M}
. Accordingly, we use
Spec

to denote affine derived schemes, and
Spec
to denote classical affine schemes.

•

We denote by
t_{0}
the functor that assigns to any derived stack its classical truncation.

•

We denote by
QCoh({\bf F})
the differential graded category (
dg-category
) of quasicoherent sheaves on the derived stack
\bf F
, and by
L_{qcoh}({\bf F})
its homotopy category; the latter is a triangulated category and, when
\bf F
is equivalent to a classical scheme, is equivalent to its ordinary derived category of complexes of quasicoherent sheaves.

Realization as a Lagrangian intersection

In this section we prove our main results

Definition 2.1

]

Theorem 2.4

Let
\bf X

-shifted symplectic form
\omega

JS19
, Example 3.6]
, with the superpotential
\varepsilon\in\tilde{B}^{-1}
given by

\varepsilon=-\sum_{i}\left(g_{i}\theta_{i}+y_{i}\beta_{i}\right).

Note that we have rewritten the “quadratic form” in degree
-1
here as a nondegenerate pairing since we have an even number of generators in degree
-1
. The Lagrangian structure on
z
is equivalent to
-\sum_{i}d_{dR}y_{i}d_{dR}\theta_{i}
, which is equivalent to the restriction of
\nu^{0}
to
\tilde{B}
. We conclude that
\nu
is equivalent to the canonical Lagrangian structure on the section
z
.

A similar calculation shows that
\mu
is a Lagrangian structure, and is equivalent to the canonical Lagrangian structure on the section
d_{dR}\Psi
, and we’re done.
∎

This completes the proof in the even case. Observe that we have proved something stronger than
Theorem

1.4
for this case: the local description is not just as a Lagrangian intersection, but as a
derived critical locus
, which is a special type of Lagrangian intersection.

2.2.
The general case

We now proceed to prove the general case. As will be clear in the proof, this is not a generalization of the proof in the even case, and is in fact a completely independent proof even for the situation where we have an even number of generators in degree
(-1)
.

Let
A
be a cdga in the strong Darboux form in
[
BBJ19
]
with a
(-2)
-shifted symplectic structure. In particular,
A
is of the form in Example 5.10 in
[
BBJ19
]
. We first recall the setup. Let
m_{0}
,
A(0)
, be as in Section 2.1. We denote the degree
-1
generators by
y_{1},...y_{m_{1}}
, and the degree
-2
generators by
z_{1},...,z_{m_{0}}
.
Recall the classical master equation is given by
\frac{1}{4}\sum_{j=1}^{m_{1}}(\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial y_{j}})^{2}=0
. Choose a Hamiltonian
\Phi\in A^{-1}
that satisfies the classical master equation, we write

\Phi=\sum_{j=1}^{m_{1}}f_{j}y_{j},

where
f_{i}\in A(0)
and
\sum_{i}f_{i}^{2}=0
.
Let
h_{j}:=\frac{1}{2}f_{j}
, and
g_{k}^{j}:=\frac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial x_{k}}
. The differentials on
A
are given by

\begin{split}dy_{j}&=h_{j},\\
dz_{k}&=\sum_{j}g_{k}^{j}y_{j}.\end{split}

For the cdga
A
, let
A(1)
be the cdga freely generated over
A(0)
by the degree
-1
generators, with the same differential.
Consider the following fiber product diagram

(5)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\textbf{\text{Spec}\,}}A(0)\times^{h}_{T^{*}[-1]{\textbf{\text{Spec}\,}}A(0)}{\textbf{\text{Spec}\,}}A(1)}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\textbf{\text{Spec}\,}}A(1)}

by
{\textbf{\text{Spec}\,}}C_{0}

,

Proposition 2.7
.

There exists a map

Proof.

For the first claim, it is enough to show that

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{\prime}}

is a homotopy pushout diagram. For this we construct a cofibrant replacement for
A(0)
as a
C_{0}
-algebra. Recall that
C_{0}=A(0)[\alpha_{k}]
. Consider the semi-free dg
C_{0}
-module

,
with

in degree
-2

. Then, as a chain complex, we have

where

and
\tau_{k}\mapsto\alpha_{k}
.

. Then

. As a chain complex

,and

.
Then the map

where

is an isomorphism of cdgas.
∎

Consider the
(-1)
-shifted symplectic form on

:
\omega_{L}=(\omega_{L}^{0},...)
, where

\omega_{L}^{0}=\sum_{i}d_{dR}x_{i}d_{dR}\alpha_{i}.

We explore the Lagrangian structure on

and

(relative to

). We begin with the Lagrangian structure on

. Consider the following
2

\nu=-\sum_{j}d_{dR}y_{j}d_{dR}y_{j}\in\left(\bigwedge^{2}\Omega_{A(1)}^{1}\right)^{-2}.

Then we have

On the other hand, we have

\begin{split}q^{*}\omega_{L}&=q^{*}\sum_{k}d_{dR}x_{k}d_{dR}\alpha_{k}\\
&=\sum_{k}\sum_{j}g_{k}^{j}d_{dR}x_{k}d_{dR}y_{j}\end{split}

Hence

. Then by
[
JS19
, (2.4)]
,
we have
\nu\in\text{Isot}(q,\omega_{L})
. To show
\nu
is a Lagrangian structure on
q
relative to
\omega_{L}
, we need to show
\Theta_{\nu}:\mathbb{T}_{q}\rightarrow\mathbb{L}_{{\textbf{\text{Spec}\,}}A(1)}[-2]
is a quasi-isomorphism.

The tangent complex
\mathbb{T}_{{\textbf{\text{Spec}\,}}A(1)}
is, as an

\mathbb{T}_{{\textbf{\text{Spec}\,}}A(1)}=A(1)\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}},\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}}\rangle

. While
\mathbb{T}_{{\textbf{\text{Spec}\,}}C_{0}}=C_{0}\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}},\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha_{j}}\rangle
, with

in degree

The map

\begin{split}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}&\mapsto q^{*}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}+\sum_{k}\sum_{j}\frac{\partial g_{k}^{j}}{\partial x_{i}}y_{j}q^{*}\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha_{k}},\\

Then

. We write the degree

(8)

\begin{array}[]{c}A(0)\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\rangle\\

3.
The derived moduli stack of coherent sheaves

In this section, we work out an explicit example of the construction in Section 2.1, the derived moduli stack of length
d
sheaves on
\mathbb{C}^{4}
. We denote this moduli stack by
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}^{d}(\mathbb{C}^{4})
. For this section we assume that
k=\mathbb{C}
.

3.1.
Resolutions of the polynomial algebra

In this subsection we recall some properties of polynomial algebras that will be useful when constructing cofibrant replacements later. While all results in this subsection are true for general commutative Koszul Calabi-Yau algebras, we restrict ourselves to the polynomial algebra case for concreteness. We work in the setup of
[
LV12
]
.

Let
V
be an
n
-dimensional vector space over
k
with basis
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
. The symmetric algebra
A:=S(V)\simeq k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
is a quadratic algebra: it is a quotient of
T(V)
, the tensor algebra of
V
, by the quadratic ideal generated by elements of the form
x_{i}\otimes x_{j}-x_{j}\otimes x_{i}
. The exterior algebra
\wedge V
is the Koszul dual coalgebra of
A
, and we will denote it by
A^{\text{\textexclamdown}}
. We treat it as a nonpositively graded vector space i.e.
(A^{\text{\textexclamdown}})^{-k}=\wedge^{k}V
. The graded coalgebra structure is given by the shuffle coproduct:

\displaystyle\Delta:\wedge^{k}V

\displaystyle\to\bigoplus_{i=0}^{k}\wedge^{i}V\otimes\wedge^{k-i}V

\displaystyle x_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge x_{k}

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{(i+1,k-i)\text{-shuffles }\sigma}\varepsilon(\sigma)\left(x_{\sigma(0)}\wedge\ldots\wedge x_{\sigma(i)}\right)\otimes\left(x_{\sigma(p+1)}\wedge\ldots\wedge x_{\sigma(k)}\right)

where we use the notation
x_{0}=1
and
\varepsilon(\sigma)
denotes the Koszul sign of
\sigma
. The natural inclusion
k\to A^{\text{\textexclamdown}}
is a coaugmentation map for this coalgebra and induces a decomposition
A^{\text{\textexclamdown}}=k\oplus\overline{\wedge V}
, where
\overline{\wedge V}=\oplus_{k=1}^{n}\wedge^{k}V
. The coproduct
\Delta
induces a reduced coproduct
\overline{\Delta}
on
\overline{\wedge V}
, given by
\overline{\Delta}(\alpha)=\Delta(\alpha)-1\otimes\alpha-\alpha\otimes 1
.

Denote by
\Omega A^{\text{\textexclamdown}}
the free graded unital associative algebra on
\overline{\wedge V}[-1]
(i.e. the graded tensor algebra) with product denoted by
\otimes
. We have, for example, that

\left(\Omega A^{\text{\textexclamdown}}\right)^{0}\simeq k\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\rangle,

the free associative algebra on the generators of
V
, and
(\Omega A^{\text{\textexclamdown}})^{-1}
is generated as a
(\Omega A^{\text{\textexclamdown}})^{0}
-module by the generators of
\wedge^{2}V
. Note that the product
\otimes
on
\Omega A^{\text{\textexclamdown}}
should not be confused with the wedge product on
\wedge V
. The reduced coproduct
\overline{\Delta}
can be extended to a graded derivation
d:\Omega A^{\text{\textexclamdown}}\to\Omega A^{\text{\textexclamdown}}
of degree +1, making
\Omega A^{\text{\textexclamdown}}
a differential graded algebra (dga). This is precisely the cobar construction of
A^{\text{\textexclamdown}}
. The inclusion
V\to A
(i.e. mapping
x_{i}
to
x_{i}
) induces a dga map
p:\Omega A^{\text{\textexclamdown}}\to A
. By the Koszul criterion, we have

Proposition 3.1

(e.g.
[
LV12
, Theorem 3.4.6]
)
.

The dga map
p:\Omega A^{\text{\textexclamdown}}\to A
is a minimal resolution of
A
. In particular, it is a cofibrant replacement in the category of dgas.

Group Actions on Product Systems
Valentin Deaconu, Leonard Huang

Abstract
. We introduce the concept of crossed product of a product system by a locally compact group. We prove that the crossed product of a row-finite and faithful product system by an amenable group is also a row-finite and faithful product system. We illustrate with examples related to group actions on k k -graphs and to higher rank Doplicher-Roberts algebras.

Primary 46L05

1.
Introduction

Product systems over various discrete semigroups were introduced by N. Fowler in
[
8
]
, inspired by work of W. Arveson and studied by several authors (see
[
1
,
3
,
17
]
, for example). Several interesting examples of product systems already occur over the semigroup
\left(\mathbb{N}^{k},+\right)
, where
k\geq 2
.

We first recall the Toeplitz algebra and the Cuntz-Pimsner algebra of a product system. We use the covariance condition in Fowler’s sense. Next, we introduce the concept of an action of a (locally compact and Hausdorff) group on a product system and then define the associated crossed product product system. We prove that the crossed product of a row-finite and faithful product system by an amenable group is also row-finite and faithful, and, furthermore, we establish a version of the Hao-Ng Theorem for product systems in our setting.

Motivations come from two sources: (i) group actions on higher-rank graphs; (ii) the higher rank Doplicher-Roberts algebra defined from
k
representations of a compact group.

2.
C^{\ast}
-Algebras of Product Systems

Let us first recall the definition of a product system. Let
\left(P,\cdot\right)
be a discrete monoid with identity
e
, and let
A
be a
C^{\ast}
-algebra. A
P
-indexed
product system
of
C^{\ast}
-correspondences over
A
is a semigroup
\displaystyle\mathsf{Y}=\bigsqcup_{p\in P}\mathsf{Y}_{p}
with the following properties:

•

For each
p\in P
, the object
\mathsf{Y}_{p}
is a
C^{*}
-correspondence over
A
, which we call the
fiber
of
\mathsf{Y}
at
p
. Its inner product is denoted by
\left\langle\cdot\middle|\cdot\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}}
.

•

The fiber
\mathsf{Y}_{e}
of
\mathsf{Y}
at
e
is
{}_{A}A_{A}
, which is
A
viewed as an
A
-correspondence over itself.

•

For each
p,q\in P
, the semigroup multiplication on
\mathsf{Y}
maps
\mathsf{Y}_{p}\times\mathsf{Y}_{q}
to
\mathsf{Y}_{pq}
, so we have an
A
-balanced
\mathbb{C}
-bilinear map

\mathsf{M}_{p,q}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textnormal{df}}}{{=}}\left\{\begin{matrix}\mathsf{Y}_{p}\times\mathsf{Y}_{q}&\to&\mathsf{Y}_{pq}\\
\displaystyle\left(x,y\right)&\mapsto&\displaystyle x\cdot y\end{matrix}\right\}.

•

For each
p,q\in P\setminus\left\{e\right\}
, the map
\mathsf{M}_{p,q}:\mathsf{Y}_{p}\times\mathsf{Y}_{q}\to\mathsf{Y}_{pq}
induces an isomorphism
\overline{\mathsf{M}}_{p,q}:\mathsf{Y}_{p}\otimes_{A}\mathsf{Y}_{q}\to\mathsf{Y}_{pq}
.

•

For each
p\in P
, the maps
\mathsf{M}_{e,p}
and
\mathsf{M}_{p,e}
implement, respectively, the left and right actions of
A
on
\mathsf{Y}_{p}
. Consequently,
\overline{\mathsf{M}}_{p,e}:\mathsf{Y}_{p}\otimes_{A}\left({}_{A}A_{A}\right)\to\mathsf{Y}_{p}
is an isomorphism for all
p\in P
.

For each
p\in P
, let
\phi_{p}:A\to\mathcal{L}\left(\mathsf{Y}_{p}\right)
denote the left action of
A
on
\mathsf{Y}_{p}
by adjointable operators. We say that
\mathsf{Y}
is
essential
if and only if
\mathsf{Y}_{p}
is an essential
A
-correspondence, i.e.,
\operatorname{Span}\left(\phi_{p}\left[A\right]\left[\mathsf{Y}_{p}\right]\right)
is dense in
\mathsf{Y}_{p}
, for each
p\in P
, in which case
\overline{\mathsf{M}}_{e,p}:\left({}_{A}A_{A}\right)\otimes_{A}\mathsf{Y}_{p}\to\mathsf{Y}_{p}
is also an isomorphism.

If
\phi_{p}
takes values in the
C^{\ast}
-algebra
\mathcal{K}\left(\mathsf{Y}_{p}\right)
of compact operators on
\mathsf{Y}_{p}
for each
p\in P
, then
\mathsf{Y}
is said to be
row-finite
or
proper
, and if
\phi_{p}
is furthermore injective for each
p\in P
, then
\mathsf{Y}
is said to be
faithful
.

There are various
C^{\ast}
-algebras associated to a product system under certain assumptions. For our future reference, let us recall some standard facts.

Let
\mathsf{Y}
be a
P
-indexed product system over
A
, and let
B
be a
C^{\ast}
-algebra. A map
\psi:\mathsf{Y}\to B
is then called a
Toeplitz representation
of
\mathsf{Y}
if and only if, writing
\psi_{p}
for
\psi|_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}}
, the following properties hold:

•

\psi_{p}:\mathsf{Y}_{p}\to B
is
\mathbb{C}
-linear for all
p\in P
.

•

\psi_{e}:A\to B
is a
C^{\ast}
-homomorphism, and
\psi_{e}\left(\left\langle\zeta\middle|\eta\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}}\right)=\psi_{p}\left(\zeta\right)^{\ast}\psi_{p}\left(\eta\right)
for all
p\in P
and
\zeta,\eta\in\mathsf{Y}_{p}
.

•

\psi_{p}\left(\zeta\right)\psi_{q}\left(\eta\right)=\psi_{pq}\left(\zeta\eta\right)
for all
p,q\in P
,
\zeta\in\mathsf{Y}_{p}
, and
\eta\in\mathsf{Y}_{q}
.

One can construct a
C^{\ast}
-algebra
\mathcal{T}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)
— known as the
Toeplitz algebra
of
\mathsf{Y}
— and a Toeplitz representation
i_{\mathsf{Y}}:\mathsf{Y}\to\mathcal{T}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)
of
\mathsf{Y}
such that the pair
\left(\mathcal{T}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right),i_{\mathsf{Y}}\right)
is universal in the following sense:
\mathcal{T}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)
is generated by
i_{\mathsf{Y}}\left[\mathsf{Y}\right]
, and for any Toeplitz representation
\psi:\mathsf{Y}\to B
, there is a
C^{\ast}
-homomorphism
\psi_{\ast}:\mathcal{T}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)\to B
such that
\psi_{\ast}\circ i_{\mathsf{Y}}=\psi
.

For each
p\in P
, there exists a
C^{\ast}
-homomorphism
\psi^{\left(p\right)}:\mathcal{K}\left(\mathsf{Y}_{p}\right)\to B
obtained as the continuous extension of the map

\forall\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{n},\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{n}\in\mathsf{Y}_{p}:\qquad\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Theta_{\zeta_{i},\eta_{i}}\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{n}\psi_{p}\left(\zeta_{i}\right)\psi_{p}\left(\eta_{i}\right)^{\ast}.

Here
\Theta_{\zeta,\eta}(\xi)=\zeta\left\langle\eta\middle|\xi\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}}
. Note that
\psi^{\left(e\right)}=\psi_{e}
.

A Toeplitz representation
\psi:\mathsf{Y}\to B
is then called
Cuntz-Pimsner covariant
(in Fowler’s sense) if and only if

\forall p\in P,~\forall a\in\phi_{p}^{-1}\left[\mathcal{K}\left(\mathsf{Y}_{p}\right)\right]:\qquad\psi^{\left(p\right)}\left(\phi_{p}\left(a\right)\right)=\psi_{e}\left(a\right).

One can construct a
C^{\ast}
-algebra
\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)
— known as the
Cuntz-Pimsner algebra
of
\mathsf{Y}
— and a Cuntz-Pimsner covariant Toeplitz representation
j_{\mathsf{Y}}:\mathsf{Y}\to\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)
of
\mathsf{Y}
such that the pair
\left(\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right),j_{\mathsf{Y}}\right)
is universal in the following sense:
\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)
is generated by
j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left[\mathsf{Y}\right]
, and for any Cuntz-Pimsner covariant Toeplitz representation
\psi:\mathsf{Y}\to B
, there is a
C^{\ast}
-homomorphism
\psi_{\ast}:\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)\to B
such that
\psi_{\ast}\circ j_{\mathsf{Y}}=\psi
.

Example 2.1
.

For a product system
\mathsf{Y}\to P
with fibers
\mathsf{Y}_{p}
nonzero finitely dimensional Hilbert spaces, in particular
A=\mathsf{Y}_{e}=\mathbb{C}
, let us fix an orthonormal basis
\mathcal{B}_{p}
in
\mathsf{Y}_{p}
. Then a Toeplitz representation
\psi:\mathsf{Y}\to B
gives rise to a family of isometries
\{\psi(\xi):\xi\in\mathcal{B}_{p}\}_{p\in P}
with mutually orthogonal range projections. In this case
\mathcal{T}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)
is generated by a colection of Cuntz-Toeplitz algebras which interact according to the multiplication maps
\overline{\mathsf{M}}_{p,q}
in
\mathsf{Y}
.

A representation
\psi:\mathsf{Y}\to B
is Cuntz-Pimsner covariant if

\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{B}_{p}}\psi(\xi)\psi(\xi)^{*}=\psi(1)

for all
p\in P
.
The Cuntz-Pimsner algebra
\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)
is generated by a collection of Cuntz algebras, so it could be thought as a multidimensional Cuntz algebra. N. Fowler proved in
[
7
]
that if the function
p\mapsto\dim\mathsf{Y}_{p}
is injective, then the algebra
\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)
is simple and purely infinite. For other examples of multidimensional Cuntz algebras, see
[
2
]
.

Example 2.2
.

A row-finite
k
-graph with no sources
\Lambda
(see
[
13
]
) determines a product system
\mathsf{Y}\to\mathbb{N}^{k}
with
\mathsf{Y}_{0}=A=C_{0}(\Lambda^{0})
and
\mathsf{Y}_{n}=\overline{C_{c}(\Lambda^{n})}
for
n\neq 0
such that we have an isomorphism
\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)\cong C^{*}(\Lambda)
.

3.
Group Actions on Product Systems and Crossed Products

Given a locally compact group
G
and an
C^{*}
-correspondence
\mathsf{X}
over
A
, recall that an action of
G
on
\mathsf{X}
is a pair
\left(\alpha,\beta\right)
with the following properties:

•

\alpha
is a strongly continuous action of
G
on
A
by
C^{\ast}
-automorphisms.

•

\beta
is a strongly continuous action of
G
on
\mathsf{X}
by surjective
\mathbb{C}
-linear isometries.

•

For all
s\in G
,
a\in A
, and
x,y\in\mathsf{X}
,

\left\langle\beta_{s}\left(x\right)\middle|\beta_{s}\left(y\right)\right\rangle_{\mathsf{X}}=\alpha_{s}\left(\left\langle x\middle|y\right\rangle_{\mathsf{X}}\right),\qquad\beta_{s}\left(xa\right)=\beta_{s}\left(x\right)\alpha_{s}\left(a\right),\qquad\beta_{s}\left(ax\right)=\alpha_{s}\left(a\right)\beta_{s}\left(x\right).

By the universal property of Cuntz-Pimsner algebras defined using the Katsura ideal, see
[
12
]
, there is an action
\gamma
of
G
on
\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{X}\right)
.

The crossed product
\mathsf{X}\rtimes_{\beta}G
of
\mathsf{X}
by
G
is defined as the completion of the
C_{c}\left(G,A\right)
-bimodule
C_{c}\left(G,\mathsf{X}\right)
, and its
\left(A\rtimes_{\alpha}G\right)
-correspondence structure is uniquely determined by the following operations:

\displaystyle\forall f\in C_{c}\left(G,A\right),~\forall\zeta,\eta\in C_{c}\left(G,\mathsf{X}\right),~\forall s\in G:

\displaystyle\left(f\zeta\right)\left(s\right)=\int_{G}f\left(t\right)\beta_{t}\left(\zeta\left(t^{-1}s\right)\right)~\mathrm{d}{t},\quad\left(\zeta f\right)\left(s\right)=\int_{G}\zeta\left(t\right)\alpha_{t}\left(f\left(t^{-1}s\right)\right)~\mathrm{d}{t},

\displaystyle\left\langle\zeta\middle|\eta\right\rangle_{\mathsf{X}\rtimes_{\beta}G}\left(s\right)=\int_{G}\alpha_{t^{-1}}\left(\left\langle\zeta\left(t\right)\middle|\eta\left(ts\right)\right\rangle_{\mathsf{X}}\right)~\mathrm{d}{t}.

For
G
amenable, it is proven in
[
10
]
that

\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{X}\right)\rtimes_{\gamma}G\cong\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{X}\rtimes_{\beta}G\right).

Definition 3.1
.

An action
\beta
of a locally compact group
G
on a product system
\mathsf{Y}\to P
over
A
is a
P
-indexed family
\left(\beta^{p}\right)_{p\in P}
such that
\left(\beta^{e},\beta^{p}\right)
is an action of
G
on
\mathsf{Y}_{p}
for each
p\in P
, and furthermore,

\forall s\in G,~\forall\zeta\in\mathsf{Y}_{p},~\forall\eta\in\mathsf{Y}_{q}:\qquad\beta^{pq}_{s}\left(\zeta\eta\right)=\beta^{p}_{s}\left(\zeta\right)\beta^{q}_{s}\left(\eta\right).

We will usually denote
\beta^{e}
by
\alpha
.

Example 3.2
.

For an essential product system
\mathsf{Y}
indexed by
P=\left(\mathbb{N}^{k},+\right)
such that
\phi_{p}
is an injection into
\mathcal{K}\left(\mathsf{Y}_{p}\right)
for all
p\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
, universality allows us to define a strongly continuous gauge action
\gamma:\mathbb{T}^{k}\to\operatorname{Aut}\left(\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)\right)
such that

\forall z\in\mathbb{T}^{k},~\forall p\in\mathbb{N}^{k},~\forall a\in A,~\forall\zeta\in\mathsf{Y}_{p}:\qquad\gamma_{z}\left(a\right)=a\qquad\text{and}\qquad\gamma_{z}\left(j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\zeta\right)\right)=z^{p}j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\zeta\right).

Here,
\displaystyle z^{p}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textnormal{df}}}{{=}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}z_{i}^{p_{i}}
. Then the fixed-point algebra
\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)^{\gamma}
is
C^{\ast}
-isomorphic to the inductive limit

\varinjlim_{p\in\mathbb{N}^{k}}\mathcal{K}\left(\mathsf{Y}_{p}\right),

where the order relation on
\mathbb{N}^{k}
is the coordinatewise order.

Example 3.3
.

For a compact group
G
and
k
finite dimensional unitary representations
\rho_{i}
of
G
on Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}_{i}
for
i=1,...,k
, we can construct a product system
\mathsf{Y}
with fibers

\mathsf{Y}_{n}=\mathcal{H}_{1}^{\otimes n_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{H}_{k}^{\otimes n_{k}}

for
n=(n_{1},...,n_{k})\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
, see
[
4
]
. Then the group
G
acts on each fiber
\mathsf{Y}_{n}
via the representation
\rho^{n}=\rho_{1}^{\otimes n_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\rho_{k}^{\otimes n_{k}}
. This action is compatible with the multiplication maps and commutes with the gauge action of
\mathbb{T}^{k}
.

Definition 3.4
.

If
\beta
is an action of
G
on a
P
-indexed product system
\mathsf{Y}
, then the
crossed product

\mathsf{Y}\rtimes_{\beta}G
is defined as the
P
-indexed product system with fibers
\mathsf{Y}_{p}\rtimes_{\beta^{p}}G
, which are
C^{*}
-correspondences over
A\rtimes_{\alpha}G
. For
\zeta\in C_{c}\left(G,\mathsf{Y}_{p}\right)
and
\eta\in C_{c}\left(G,\mathsf{Y}_{q}\right)
, the product
\zeta\eta\in C_{c}\left(G,\mathsf{Y}_{pq}\right)
is given by

\forall s\in G:\qquad\left(\zeta\eta\right)\left(s\right)=\int_{G}\zeta\left(t\right)\beta^{q}_{t}\left(\eta\left(t^{-1}s\right)\right)~\mathrm{d}{t}.

Proposition 3.5
.

The semigroup
\displaystyle\mathsf{Y}\rtimes_{\beta}G=\bigsqcup_{p\in P}\left(\mathsf{Y}_{p}\rtimes_{\beta^{p}}G\right)
with the above multiplication law satisfies all the properties of a product system over
A\rtimes_{\alpha}G
.

Proof.

Let us first prove that the multiplication law for
\mathsf{Y}\rtimes_{\beta}G
is associative on the function-algebra level. Let
p,q,r\in P
, and let
\zeta\in C_{c}\left(G,\mathsf{Y}_{p}\right)
,
\eta\in C_{c}\left(G,\mathsf{Y}_{q}\right)
, and
\xi\in C_{c}\left(G,\mathsf{Y}_{r}\right)
. Then for all
s\in G
,

\displaystyle\left[\left(\zeta\eta\right)\xi\right]\left(s\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\left(\zeta\eta\right)\left(t\right)\beta^{r}_{t}\left(\xi\left(t^{-1}s\right)\right)~\mathrm{d}{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\left[\int_{G}\zeta\left(u\right)\beta^{q}_{u}\left(\eta\left(u^{-1}t\right)\right)~\mathrm{d}{u}\right]\beta^{r}_{t}\left(\xi\left(t^{-1}\right)s\right)~\mathrm{d}{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{G\times G}\left[\zeta\left(u\right)\beta^{q}_{u}\left(\eta\left(u^{-1}t\right)\right)\right]\beta^{r}_{t}\left(\xi\left(t^{-1}\right)s\right)~\mathrm{d}{\left(u\times t\right)}

\displaystyle=\int_{G\times G}\zeta\left(u\right)\left[\beta^{q}_{u}\left(\eta\left(u^{-1}t\right)\right)\beta^{r}_{t}\left(\xi\left(t^{-1}\right)s\right)\right]~\mathrm{d}{\left(u\times t\right)}

and

\displaystyle\left[\zeta\left(\eta\xi\right)\right]\left(r\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\zeta\left(t\right)\beta^{qr}_{t}\left(\left(\eta\xi\right)\left(t^{-1}r\right)\right)~\mathrm{d}{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\zeta\left(t\right)\beta^{qr}_{t}\left(\int_{G}\eta\left(s\right)\beta^{r}_{s}\left(\xi\left(s^{-1}t^{-1}r\right)\right)~\mathrm{d}{s}\right)~\mathrm{d}{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{G\times G}\zeta\left(t\right)\beta^{qr}_{t}\left(\eta\left(s\right)\beta^{r}_{s}\left(\xi\left(s^{-1}t^{-1}r\right)\right)\right)~\mathrm{d}{\left(s\times t\right)}

\displaystyle=\int_{G\times G}\zeta\left(s\right)\beta^{qr}_{s}\left(\eta\left(t\right)\beta^{r}_{t}\left(\xi\left(t^{-1}s^{-1}r\right)\right)\right)~\mathrm{d}{\left(s\times t\right)}

\displaystyle=\int_{G\times G}\zeta\left(s\right)\beta^{q}_{s}\left(\eta\left(t\right)\right)\beta^{r}_{st}\left(\xi\left(t^{-1}s^{-1}r\right)\right)~\mathrm{d}{\left(s\times t\right)}\qquad\left(\text{By the axioms for a group action.}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{G\times G}\zeta\left(s\right)\beta^{q}_{s}\left(\eta\left(s^{-1}t\right)\right)\beta^{r}_{t}\left(\xi\left(t^{-1}r\right)\right)~\mathrm{d}{\left(s\times t\right)}.\qquad\left(\text{By the change of variables $t\mapsto s^{-1}t$.}\right)

It follows that for all
p,q\in P

\left\{\begin{matrix}C_{c}\left(G,\mathsf{Y}_{p}\right)\times C_{c}\left(G,\mathsf{Y}_{q}\right)&\to&C_{c}\left(G,\mathsf{Y}_{pq}\right)\\
\displaystyle\left(\zeta,\eta\right)&\mapsto&\displaystyle\zeta\eta\end{matrix}\right\}

is a
C_{c}\left(G,A\right)
-balanced
\mathbb{C}
-bilinear map, which then induces a
\mathbb{C}
-linear map

\Omega_{p,q}=\left\{\begin{matrix}C_{c}\left(G,\mathsf{Y}_{p}\right)\otimes_{C_{c}\left(G,A\right)}C_{c}\left(G,\mathsf{Y}_{q}\right)&\to&C_{c}\left(G,\mathsf{Y}_{pq}\right)\\
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\zeta_{i}\odot\eta_{i}&\mapsto&\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\zeta_{i}\eta_{i}\end{matrix}\right\}.

Let us show that
\Omega_{p,q}
extends uniquely to a
\mathbb{C}
-linear isometry

\overline{\Omega}_{p,q}:\left(\mathsf{Y}_{p}\rtimes_{\beta^{p}}G\right)\otimes_{A\rtimes_{\alpha}G}\left(\mathsf{Y}_{q}\rtimes_{\beta^{q}}G\right)\to\mathsf{Y}_{pq}\rtimes_{\beta^{pq}}G.

Observe that for all
\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{n}\in C_{c}\left(G,\mathsf{Y}_{p}\right)
and
\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{n}\in C_{c}\left(G,\mathsf{Y}_{q}\right)
we have

\displaystyle~\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\zeta_{i}\otimes\eta_{i}\right\|_{\left(\mathsf{Y}_{p}\rtimes_{\beta^{p}}G\right)\otimes_{A\rtimes_{\alpha}G}\left(\mathsf{Y}_{q}\rtimes_{\beta^{q}}G\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\left\|\left\langle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\zeta_{i}\otimes\eta_{i}\middle|\sum_{j=1}^{n}\zeta_{j}\otimes\eta_{j}\right\rangle_{\left(\mathsf{Y}_{p}\rtimes_{\beta^{p}}G\right)\otimes_{A\rtimes_{\alpha}G}\left(\mathsf{Y}_{q}\rtimes_{\beta^{q}}G\right)}\right\|_{A\rtimes_{\alpha}G}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\left\|\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left\langle\zeta_{i}\otimes\eta_{i}\middle|\zeta_{j}\otimes\eta_{j}\right\rangle_{\left(\mathsf{Y}_{p}\rtimes_{\beta^{p}}G\right)\otimes_{A\rtimes_{\alpha}G}\left(\mathsf{Y}_{q}\rtimes_{\beta^{q}}G\right)}\right\|_{A\rtimes_{\alpha}G}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\left\|\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left\langle\eta_{i}\middle|\left\langle\zeta_{i}\middle|\zeta_{j}\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}\rtimes_{\beta^{p}}G}\eta_{j}\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{q}\rtimes_{\beta^{q}}G}\right\|_{A\rtimes_{\alpha}G}^{\frac{1}{2}}

and

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\zeta_{i}\eta_{i}\right\|_{\mathsf{Y}_{pq}\rtimes_{\beta^{pq}}G}

\displaystyle=\left\|\left\langle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\zeta_{i}\eta_{i}\middle|\sum_{j=1}^{n}\zeta_{j}\eta_{j}\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{pq}\rtimes_{\beta^{pq}}G}\right\|_{A\rtimes_{\alpha}G}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left\langle\zeta_{i}\eta_{i}\middle|\zeta_{j}\eta_{j}\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{pq}\rtimes_{\beta^{pq}}G}\right\|_{A\rtimes_{\alpha}G}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

To see that

\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\zeta_{i}\otimes\eta_{i}\right\|_{\left(\mathsf{Y}_{p}\rtimes_{\beta^{p}}G\right)\otimes_{A\rtimes_{\alpha}G}\left(\mathsf{Y}_{q}\rtimes_{\beta^{q}}G\right)}=\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\zeta_{i}\eta_{i}\right\|_{\mathsf{Y}_{pq}\rtimes_{\beta^{pq}}G},

it thus suffices to show that for all
i,j=1,...,n

\left\langle\eta_{i}\middle|\left\langle\zeta_{i}\middle|\zeta_{j}\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}\rtimes_{\beta^{p}}G}\eta_{j}\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{q}\rtimes_{\beta^{q}}G}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\left\langle\zeta_{i}\eta_{i}\middle|\zeta_{j}\eta_{j}\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{pq}\rtimes_{\beta^{pq}}G}

are identical elements of
C_{c}\left(G,A\right)
. Indeed, for all
r\in G
,

\displaystyle~\left\langle\eta_{i}\middle|\left\langle\zeta_{i}\middle|\zeta_{j}\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}\rtimes_{\beta^{p}}G}\eta_{j}\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{q}\rtimes_{\beta^{q}}G}\left(r\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\int_{G}\alpha_{u^{-1}}\left(\left\langle\eta_{i}\left(u\right)\middle|\left(\left\langle\zeta_{i}\middle|\zeta_{j}\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}\rtimes_{\beta^{p}}G}\eta_{j}\right)\left(ur\right)\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{q}}\right)~\mathrm{d}{u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\int_{G}\alpha_{u^{-1}}\left(\left\langle\eta_{i}\left(u\right)\middle|\int_{G}\left\langle\zeta_{i}\middle|\zeta_{j}\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}\rtimes_{\beta^{p}}G}\left(t\right)\beta^{q}_{t}\left(\eta_{j}\left(t^{-1}ur\right)\right)~\mathrm{d}{t}\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{q}}\right)~\mathrm{d}{u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\int_{G\times G}\alpha_{u^{-1}}\left(\left\langle\eta_{i}\left(u\right)\middle|\left\langle\zeta_{i}\middle|\zeta_{j}\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}\rtimes_{\beta^{p}}G}\left(t\right)\beta^{q}_{t}\left(\eta_{j}\left(t^{-1}ur\right)\right)\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{q}}\right)~\mathrm{d}{\left(t\times u\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\int_{G\times G}\alpha_{u^{-1}}\left(\left\langle\eta_{i}\left(u\right)\middle|\left[\int_{G}\alpha_{s^{-1}}\left(\left\langle\zeta_{i}\left(s\right)\middle|\zeta_{j}\left(st\right)\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}}\right)~\mathrm{d}{s}\right]\beta^{q}_{t}\left(\eta_{j}\left(t^{-1}ur\right)\right)\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{q}}\right)~\mathrm{d}{\left(t\times u\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\int_{G\times G\times G}\alpha_{u^{-1}}\left(\left\langle\eta_{i}\left(u\right)\middle|\alpha_{s^{-1}}\left(\left\langle\zeta_{i}\left(s\right)\middle|\zeta_{j}\left(st\right)\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}}\right)\beta^{q}_{t}\left(\eta_{j}\left(t^{-1}ur\right)\right)\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{q}}\right)~\mathrm{d}{\left(s\times t\times u\right)}

and

\displaystyle~\left\langle\zeta_{i}\eta_{i}\middle|\zeta_{j}\eta_{j}\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{pq}\rtimes_{\beta^{pq}}G}\left(r\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\int_{G}\alpha_{u^{-1}}\left(\left\langle\left(\zeta_{i}\eta_{i}\right)\left(u\right)\middle|\left(\zeta_{j}\eta_{j}\right)\left(ur\right)\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{pq}}\right)~\mathrm{d}{u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\int_{G}\alpha_{u^{-1}}\left(\left\langle\int_{G}\zeta_{i}\left(s\right)\beta^{q}_{s}\left(\eta_{i}\left(s^{-1}u\right)\right)~\mathrm{d}{s}\middle|\int_{G}\zeta_{j}\left(t\right)\beta^{q}_{t}\left(\eta_{j}\left(t^{-1}ur\right)\right)~\mathrm{d}{t}\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{pq}}\right)~\mathrm{d}{u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\int_{G\times G\times G}\alpha_{u^{-1}}\left(\left\langle\zeta_{i}\left(s\right)\beta^{q}_{s}\left(\eta_{i}\left(s^{-1}u\right)\right)\middle|\zeta_{j}\left(t\right)\beta^{q}_{t}\left(\eta_{j}\left(t^{-1}ur\right)\right)\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{pq}}\right)~\mathrm{d}{\left(s\times t\times u\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\int_{G\times G\times G}\alpha_{u^{-1}}\left(\left\langle\zeta_{i}\left(s\right)\otimes\beta^{q}_{s}\left(\eta_{i}\left(s^{-1}u\right)\right)\middle|\zeta_{j}\left(t\right)\otimes\beta^{q}_{t}\left(\eta_{j}\left(t^{-1}ur\right)\right)\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}\otimes_{A}\mathsf{Y}_{q}}\right)~\mathrm{d}{\left(s\times t\times u\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\int_{G\times G\times G}\alpha_{u^{-1}}\left(\left\langle\beta^{q}_{s}\left(\eta_{i}\left(s^{-1}u\right)\right)\middle|\left\langle\zeta_{i}\left(s\right)\middle|\zeta_{j}\left(t\right)\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}}\beta^{q}_{t}\left(\eta_{j}\left(t^{-1}ur\right)\right)\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{q}}\right)~\mathrm{d}{\left(s\times t\times u\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\int_{G\times G\times G}\alpha_{u^{-1}s}\left(\left\langle\eta_{i}\left(s^{-1}u\right)\middle|\alpha_{s^{-1}}\left(\left\langle\zeta_{i}\left(s\right)\middle|\zeta_{j}\left(t\right)\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}}\right)\beta^{q}_{s^{-1}t}\left(\eta_{j}\left(t^{-1}ur\right)\right)\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{q}}\right)~\mathrm{d}{\left(s\times t\times u\right)}

\displaystyle~\left(\text{By the axioms for a group action on a $C^{\ast}$-correspondence.}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\int_{G\times G\times G}\alpha_{u^{-1}s}\left(\left\langle\eta_{i}\left(s^{-1}u\right)\middle|\alpha_{s^{-1}}\left(\left\langle\zeta_{i}\left(s\right)\middle|\zeta_{j}\left(st\right)\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}}\right)\beta^{q}_{t}\left(\eta_{j}\left(t^{-1}s^{-1}ur\right)\right)\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{q}}\right)~\mathrm{d}{\left(s\times t\times u\right)}

\displaystyle~\left(\text{By the change of variables $t\mapsto st$.}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\int_{G\times G\times G}\alpha_{u^{-1}}\left(\left\langle\eta_{i}\left(u\right)\middle|\alpha_{s^{-1}}\left(\left\langle\zeta_{i}\left(s\right)\middle|\zeta_{j}\left(st\right)\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}}\right)\beta^{q}_{t}\left(\eta_{j}\left(t^{-1}ur\right)\right)\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{q}}\right)~\mathrm{d}{\left(s\times t\times u\right)}.

\displaystyle~\left(\text{By the change of variables $u\mapsto su$.}\right)

Hence,

\forall r\in G:\qquad\left\langle\eta_{i}\middle|\left\langle\zeta_{i}\middle|\zeta_{j}\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}\rtimes_{\beta^{p}}G}\eta_{j}\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{q}\rtimes_{\beta^{q}}G}\left(r\right)=\left\langle\zeta_{i}\eta_{i}\middle|\zeta_{j}\eta_{j}\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{pq}\rtimes_{\beta^{pq}}G}\left(r\right)

as claimed, so

\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\zeta_{i}\otimes\eta_{i}\right\|_{\left(\mathsf{Y}_{p}\rtimes_{\beta^{p}}G\right)\otimes_{A\rtimes_{\alpha}G}\left(\mathsf{Y}_{q}\rtimes_{\beta^{q}}G\right)}=\left\|\Omega_{p,q}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\zeta_{i}\otimes\eta_{i}\right)\right\|_{\mathsf{Y}_{pq}\rtimes_{\beta^{pq}}G}.

As
C_{c}\left(G,\mathsf{Y}_{p}\right)\otimes_{C_{c}\left(G,A\right)}C_{c}\left(G,\mathsf{Y}_{q}\right)
is dense in
\left(\mathsf{Y}_{p}\rtimes_{\beta^{p}}G\right)\otimes_{A\rtimes_{\alpha}G}\left(\mathsf{Y}_{q}\rtimes_{\beta^{q}}G\right)
, we can conclude that
\Omega_{p,q}
extends uniquely to a
\mathbb{C}
-linear isometry

\overline{\Omega}_{p,q}:\left(\mathsf{Y}_{p}\rtimes_{\beta^{p}}G\right)\otimes_{A\rtimes_{\alpha}G}\left(\mathsf{Y}_{q}\rtimes_{\beta^{q}}G\right)\to\mathsf{Y}_{pq}\rtimes_{\beta^{pq}}G.

We wish to show that
\overline{\Omega}_{p,q}
is
\left(A\rtimes_{\alpha}G\right)
-linear for all
p,q\in P
, but this will turn out to be a consequence of the following two facts about these maps, which we need to prove in any case:

•

For
p\in P
,
f\in A\rtimes_{\alpha}G
, and
\zeta\in\mathsf{Y}_{p}\rtimes_{\beta^{p}}G
,

f\zeta=\overline{\Omega}_{e,p}\left(f\otimes\zeta\right)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\zeta f=\overline{\Omega}_{p,e}\left(\zeta\otimes f\right).

•

For
p,q,r\in P
,
\zeta\in\mathsf{Y}_{p}\rtimes_{\beta^{p}}G
,
\eta\in\mathsf{Y}_{q}\rtimes_{\beta^{q}}G
, and
\xi\in Y_{r}\rtimes_{\beta^{r}}G
,

\overline{\Omega}_{pq,r}\left(\overline{\Omega}_{p,q}\left(\zeta\otimes\eta\right)\otimes\xi\right)=\overline{\Omega}_{p,qr}\left(\zeta\otimes\overline{\Omega}_{q,r}\left(\eta\otimes\xi\right)\right),

which holds because the multiplication law of the product system is associative.

To prove the first fact, let
p\in P
. Then for all
f\in C_{c}\left(G,A\right)
,
\zeta\in C_{c}\left(G,\mathsf{Y}_{p}\right)
, and
s\in G
,

\displaystyle\left[\Omega_{e,p}\left(f\otimes\zeta\right)\right]\left(s\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\overline{\mathsf{M}}_{e,p}\left(f\left(t\right)\otimes\beta^{p}_{t}\left(\zeta\left(t^{-1}s\right)\right)\right)~\mathrm{d}{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{G}f\left(r\right)\beta^{p}_{t}\left(\zeta\left(t^{-1}s\right)\right)~\mathrm{d}{t}

\displaystyle=\left(f\zeta\right)\left(s\right).

By continuity, therefore,
f\zeta=\overline{\Omega}_{e,p}\left(f\otimes\zeta\right)
for all
f\in A\rtimes_{\alpha}G
and
\zeta\in\mathsf{Y}_{p}\rtimes_{\beta^{p}}G
, and the same kind of argument establishes that
\zeta f=\overline{\Omega}_{p,e}\left(\zeta\otimes f\right)
also.

Now, to see the
\left(A\rtimes_{\alpha}G\right)
-linearity of
\overline{\Omega}_{p,q}
for all
p,q\in P
, simply observe for all
f\in A\rtimes_{\alpha}G
,
\zeta\in\mathsf{Y}_{p}\rtimes_{\beta^{p}}G
, and
\eta\in\mathsf{Y}_{q}\rtimes_{\beta^{q}}G
that

\displaystyle\overline{\Omega}_{p,q}\left(\left(\zeta\otimes\eta\right)f\right)

\displaystyle=\overline{\Omega}_{p,q}\left(\zeta\otimes\eta f\right)

\displaystyle=\overline{\Omega}_{p,q}\left(\zeta\otimes\overline{\Omega}_{q,e}\left(\eta\otimes f\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\overline{\Omega}_{pq,e}\left(\overline{\Omega}_{p,q}\left(\zeta\otimes\eta\right)\otimes f\right)

\displaystyle=\overline{\Omega}_{p,q}\left(\zeta\otimes\eta\right)f.

By linearity and continuity,
\overline{\Omega}_{p,q}
is therefore
\left(A\rtimes_{\alpha}G\right)
-linear.

Finally, we will prove that
\overline{\Omega}_{p,q}
is surjective for all
p,q\in P
. Firstly, note that for all
p\in P
and
\zeta\in C_{c}\left(G,\mathsf{Y}_{p}\right)
,

\displaystyle\left\|\zeta\right\|_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}\rtimes_{\beta^{p}}G}

\displaystyle=\left\|\left\langle\zeta\middle|\zeta\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}\rtimes_{\beta^{p}}G}\right\|_{A\rtimes_{\alpha}G}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\left\langle\zeta\middle|\zeta\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}\rtimes_{\beta^{p}}G}\right\|_{L^{1}\left(G,A\right)}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\left[\int_{G}\left\|\left\langle\zeta\middle|\zeta\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}\rtimes_{\beta^{p}}G}\left(t\right)\right\|_{A}~\mathrm{d}{t}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\left[\int_{G}\left\|\int_{G}\alpha_{s^{-1}}\left(\left\langle\zeta\left(s\right)\middle|\zeta\left(st\right)\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}}\right)~\mathrm{d}{s}\right\|_{A}~\mathrm{d}{t}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left[\int_{G\times G}\left\|\alpha_{s^{-1}}\left(\left\langle\zeta\left(s\right)\middle|\zeta\left(st\right)\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}}\right)\right\|_{A}~\mathrm{d}{\left(s\times t\right)}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\left[\int_{G\times G}\left\|\left\langle\zeta\left(s\right)\middle|\zeta\left(st\right)\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}}\right\|_{A}~\mathrm{d}{\left(s\times t\right)}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left[\int_{G\times G}\left\|\zeta\left(s\right)\right\|_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}}\left\|\zeta\left(st\right)\right\|_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}}~\mathrm{d}{\left(s\times t\right)}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\quad\left(\text{By the Cauchy-Schwarz Inequality.}\right)

\displaystyle=\left[\int_{G}\left(\left\|\zeta\left(s\right)\right\|_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}}\int_{G}\left\|\zeta\left(st\right)\right\|_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}}~\mathrm{d}{t}\right)~\mathrm{d}{s}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\left[\int_{G}\left\|\zeta\left(s\right)\right\|_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}}\left\|\zeta\right\|_{L^{1}\left(G,\mathsf{Y}_{p}\right)}~\mathrm{d}{s}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\left[\left\|\zeta\right\|_{L^{1}\left(G,\mathsf{Y}_{p}\right)}^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\zeta\right\|_{L^{1}\left(G,\mathsf{Y}_{p}\right)}.

Fix
p,q\in P
. Clearly, we can approximate a function
\zeta\in C_{c}\left(G,\mathsf{Y}_{pq}\right)
with respect to
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L^{1}\left(G,\mathsf{Y}_{pq}\right)}
— and hence with respect to
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathsf{Y}_{pq}\rtimes_{\beta^{pq}}G}
— by a linear combination of functions of the form
f\odot z
, where
f\in C_{c}\left(G\right)
and
z\in\mathsf{Y}_{pq}
. As
\overline{\mathsf{M}}_{p,q}:\mathsf{Y}_{p}\otimes_{A}\mathsf{Y}_{q}\to\mathsf{Y}_{pq}
is an isomorphism, we can approximate
z
itself by a linear combination of elements of
\mathsf{Y}_{pq}
of the form
\overline{\mathsf{M}}_{p,q}\left(x\otimes y\right)
, where
x\in\mathsf{Y}_{p}
and
y\in\mathsf{Y}_{q}
. Now, for any
\epsilon>0
, we can find an open neighborhood
U
of
e_{G}
and a non-negative function
g\in C_{c}\left(G,\mathbb{R}\right)
with
\operatorname{Supp}\left(g\right)\subseteq U
and integral
1
such that

\left\|f\odot\overline{\mathsf{M}}_{p,q}\left(x\otimes y\right)-\Omega_{p,q}\left(\left(g\odot x\right)\otimes\left(f\odot y\right)\right)\right\|_{L^{1}\left(G,\mathsf{Y}_{pq}\right)}<\epsilon.

This yields, according to the foregoing discussion,

\left\|f\odot\overline{\mathsf{M}}_{p,q}\left(x\otimes y\right)-\Omega_{p,q}\left(\left(g\odot x\right)\otimes\left(f\odot y\right)\right)\right\|_{\mathsf{Y}_{pq}\rtimes_{\beta^{pq}}G}<\epsilon.

Therefore,
\operatorname{Range}\left(\overline{\Omega}_{p,q}\right)
is dense in
\mathsf{Y}_{pq}\rtimes_{\beta^{pq}G}
, and as
\overline{\Omega}_{p,q}
is an isometry between Banach spaces, it follows that
\overline{\Omega}_{p,q}
is surjective.

As
\overline{\Omega}_{p,q}
is a surjective
\left(A\rtimes_{\alpha}G\right)
-linear isometry for all
p,q\in P
, we can apply the main result of
[
14
]
by Lance to conclude that it is a unitary operator.
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

Suppose that a group
G
acts on a row-finite and faithful
P
-indexed product system
\mathsf{Y}
over
A
via automorphisms
\beta^{p}_{g}
. Then
G
acts on
\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)
via automorphisms denoted by
\gamma_{g}
. Moreover, if
G
is amenable, then
\mathsf{Y}\rtimes_{\beta}G
is row-finite and faithful, and

\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)\rtimes_{\gamma}G\cong\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\rtimes_{\beta}G\right).

Proof.

Let
p\in P
. Recall the strongly-continuous action
\tau^{p}
of
G
on
\mathcal{K}\left(\mathsf{Y}_{p}\right)
given by

\forall x,y\in\mathsf{Y}_{p}:\qquad\tau^{p}_{g}\left(\Theta_{x,y}\right)=\Theta_{\beta^{p}_{g}\left(x\right),\beta^{p}_{g}\left(y\right)}.

The left-action
\phi_{p}:A\to\mathcal{K}\left(\mathsf{Y}_{p}\right)
is injective by assumption. To see that it is equivariant for
\alpha
and
\tau^{p}
, firstly observe that for all
g\in G
,
a\in A
, and
x\in\mathsf{Y}_{p}

\beta^{p}_{g}\left(\left[\phi_{p}\left(a\right)\right]\left(x\right)\right)=\beta^{p}_{g}\left(ax\right)=\alpha_{g}\left(a\right)\beta^{p}_{g}\left(x\right)=\left[\phi_{p}\left(\alpha_{g}\left(a\right)\right)\right]\left(\beta^{p}_{g}\left(x\right)\right),

so
\beta^{p}_{g}\circ\phi_{p}\left(a\right)=\phi_{p}\left(\alpha_{g}\left(a\right)\right)\circ\beta^{p}_{g}
; equivalently,
\beta^{p}_{g}\circ\phi_{p}\left(a\right)\circ\beta^{p}_{g^{-1}}=\phi_{p}\left(\alpha_{g}\left(a\right)\right)
.
Next, observe for all
g\in G
and
x,y,z\in\mathsf{Y}_{p}
that

\displaystyle\left(\beta^{p}_{g}\circ\Theta_{x,y}\circ\beta^{p}_{g^{-1}}\right)\left(z\right)

\displaystyle=\beta^{p}_{g}\left(x\left\langle y\middle|\beta^{p}_{g^{-1}}\left(z\right)\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}}\right)

\displaystyle=\beta^{p}_{g}\left(x\right)\alpha_{g}\left(\left\langle y\middle|\beta^{p}_{g^{-1}}\left(z\right)\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}}\right)

\displaystyle=\beta^{p}_{g}\left(x\right)\left\langle\beta^{p}_{g}\left(y\right)\middle|z\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}}

\displaystyle=\Theta_{\beta^{p}_{g}\left(x\right),\beta^{p}_{g}\left(y\right)}\left(z\right),

so
\tau^{p}_{g}\left(\Theta_{x,y}\right)=\beta^{p}_{g}\circ\Theta_{x,y}\circ\beta^{p}_{g^{-1}}
. In particular, as
\operatorname{Range}\left(\phi_{p}\right)\subseteq\mathcal{K}\left(\mathsf{Y}_{p}\right)
, we have

\forall a\in A:\qquad\tau^{p}_{g}\left(\phi_{p}\left(a\right)\right)=\beta^{p}_{g}\circ\phi_{p}\left(a\right)\circ\beta^{p}_{g^{-1}}=\phi_{p}\left(\alpha_{g}\left(a\right)\right),

which proves the equivariance of
\phi_{p}
for
\alpha
and
\tau^{p}
. According to the theory of reduced
C^{\ast}
-crossed products,
\phi_{p}
induces the injective
\ast
-homomorphism

\overline{\phi_{p}}:A\rtimes_{\alpha,\operatorname{red}}G\to\mathcal{K}\left(\mathsf{Y}_{p}\right)\rtimes_{\tau^{p},\operatorname{red}}G,

where
\overline{\phi_{p}}\left(f\right)=\phi_{p}\circ f
for all
f\in C_{c}\left(G,A\right)
. However,
G
is amenable, so

\overline{\phi_{p}}:A\rtimes_{\alpha}G\to\mathcal{K}\left(\mathsf{Y}_{p}\right)\rtimes_{\tau^{p}}G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{K}\left(\mathsf{Y}_{p}\rtimes_{\beta^{p}}G\right),

where the inverse of the
\ast
-isomorphism on the right is defined in
[
10
]
by

\forall\zeta,\eta\in C_{c}\left(G,\mathsf{Y}_{p}\right),~\forall s\in G:\qquad\left[\Lambda\left(\Theta_{\zeta,\eta}\right)\right]\left(s\right)=\int_{G}\Delta\left(s^{-1}r\right)\Theta_{\zeta\left(r\right),\beta^{p}_{s}\left(\eta\left(s^{-1}r\right)\right)}~\mathrm{d}{r},

where
\Delta
is the modular function of
G
. Therefore,
\mathsf{Y}\rtimes_{\beta}G
is also a row-finite and faithful product system, as claimed.

Next, we show that there exists a strongly-continuous action
\gamma
of
G
on
\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)
that satisfies

(1)

\forall g\in G,~\forall p\in P,~\forall y\in\mathsf{Y}_{p}:\qquad\gamma_{g}\left(j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(y\right)\right)=j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\beta^{p}_{g}\left(y\right)\right),

where
j_{\mathsf{Y}}:\mathsf{Y}\to\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)
denotes the universal Cuntz-Pimsner representation. Let
g\in G
. Then the map
\Psi_{g}:\mathsf{Y}\to\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)
defined by
\Psi_{g}\left(y\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textnormal{df}}}{{=}}j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\beta^{p}_{g}\left(y\right)\right)
for all
p\in P
and
y\in\mathsf{Y}_{p}
is a Cuntz-Pimsner representation of
\mathsf{Y}
on
\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)
:

•

For all
p,q\in P
,
x\in\mathsf{Y}_{p}
, and
y\in\mathsf{Y}_{q}
, we have

\displaystyle\Psi_{g}\left(xy\right)

\displaystyle=j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\beta^{p+q}_{g}\left(xy\right)\right)

\displaystyle=j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\beta^{p}_{g}\left(x\right)\beta^{q}_{g}\left(y\right)\right)

\displaystyle=j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\beta^{p}_{g}\left(x\right)\right)j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\beta^{q}_{g}\left(y\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\Psi_{g}\left(x\right)\Psi_{g}\left(y\right).

•

For all
p\in P
and
x,y\in\mathsf{Y}_{p}
, we have

\displaystyle\Psi_{g}\left(\left\langle x\middle|y\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}}\right)

\displaystyle=j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\alpha_{g}\left(\left\langle x\middle|y\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\left\langle\beta^{p}_{g}\left(x\right)\middle|\beta^{p}_{g}\left(y\right)\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}}\right)

\displaystyle=j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\beta^{p}_{g}\left(x\right)\right)^{\ast}j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\beta^{p}_{g}\left(y\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\Psi_{g}\left(x\right)^{\ast}\Psi_{g}\left(y\right).

•

Let
p\in P
. The foregoing argument tells us that
\Psi_{g}
is a Toeplitz representation of
\mathsf{Y}
on
\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)
, so there exists an extension
\Psi_{g}^{\left(p\right)}:\mathcal{K}\left(\mathsf{Y}_{p}\right)\to\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)
such that

\displaystyle\forall x,y\in\mathsf{Y}_{P}:\qquad\Psi_{g}^{\left(p\right)}\left(\Theta_{x,y}\right)

\displaystyle=\Psi_{g}\left(x\right)\Psi_{g}\left(y\right)^{\ast}

\displaystyle=j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\beta^{p}_{g}\left(x\right)\right)j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\beta^{p}_{g}\left(y\right)\right)^{\ast}

\displaystyle=j_{\mathsf{Y}}^{\left(p\right)}\left(\Theta_{\beta^{p}_{g}\left(x\right),\beta^{p}_{g}\left(y\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=j_{\mathsf{Y}}^{\left(p\right)}\left(\tau^{p}_{g}\left(\Theta_{x,y}\right)\right),

which implies by continuity that
\Psi_{g}^{\left(p\right)}=j_{\mathsf{Y}}^{\left(p\right)}\circ\tau^{p}_{g}
. As we have shown that
\phi_{p}
is equivariant for
\alpha
and
\tau^{p}
, we thus have

\forall a\in A:\qquad\Psi_{g}^{\left(p\right)}\left(\phi_{p}\left(a\right)\right)=j_{\mathsf{Y}}^{\left(p\right)}\left(\tau^{p}_{g}\left(\phi_{p}\left(a\right)\right)\right)=j_{\mathsf{Y}}^{\left(p\right)}\left(\phi_{p}\left(\alpha_{g}\left(a\right)\right)\right)=j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\alpha_{g}\left(a\right)\right)=\Psi_{g}\left(a\right),

proving that
\Psi_{g}
is a Cuntz-Pimsner representation of
\mathsf{Y}
.

By universality, there is thus a
C^{\ast}
-endomorphism
S
on
\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)
such that

\forall p\in P,~\forall y\in\mathsf{Y}_{p}:\qquad S\left(j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(y\right)\right)=j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\beta^{p}_{g}\left(y\right)\right).

Similarly, there is a
C^{\ast}
-endomorphism
T
on
\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)
such that

\forall p\in P,~\forall y\in\mathsf{Y}_{p}:\qquad T\left(j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(y\right)\right)=j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\beta^{p}_{g^{-1}}\left(y\right)\right).

As
ST=\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)}=TS
, we see that
S
is a
C^{\ast}
-isomorphism, and as
g
is arbitrary, there is an action
\gamma
of
G
on
\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)
that satisfies (
1
). The strong continuity of
\gamma
immediately follows from the continuity of
j_{\mathsf{Y}}
.

We now show that a Cuntz-Pimsner representation
\psi:\mathsf{Y}\rtimes_{\beta}G\to\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)\rtimes_{\gamma}G
exists and that it satisfies

\forall p\in P,~\forall\zeta\in C_{c}\left(G,\mathsf{Y}_{p}\right):\qquad\psi_{p}\left(\zeta\right)=j_{\mathsf{Y}}\circ\zeta.

As
j_{\mathsf{Y}}|_{A}:A\to\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)
is a
\ast
-homomorphism, and as
\gamma_{g}\left(j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(a\right)\right)=j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\alpha_{g}\left(a\right)\right)
for all
a\in A
, we find that
j_{\mathsf{Y}}|_{A}
is equivariant for
\alpha
and
\gamma
. Hence,
j_{\mathsf{Y}}|_{A}
induces a
\ast
-homomorphism

\psi_{e}:A\rtimes_{\alpha}G\to\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)\rtimes_{\gamma}G

such that
\psi_{e}\left(f\right)=j_{\mathsf{Y}}\circ f
for all
f\in C_{c}\left(G,A\right)
. Let
p\in P
and
\zeta,\eta\in C_{c}\left(G,\mathsf{Y}_{p}\right)
. Then for all
s\in G
,

\displaystyle\left[\left(j_{\mathsf{Y}}\circ\zeta\right)^{\ast}\left(j_{\mathsf{Y}}\circ\zeta\right)\right]\left(s\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\left(j_{\mathsf{Y}}\circ\zeta\right)^{\ast}\left(r\right)\gamma_{r}\left(\left(j_{\mathsf{Y}}\circ\zeta\right)\left(r^{-1}s\right)\right)~\mathrm{d}{r}

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\Delta\left(r^{-1}\right)\cdot\gamma_{r}\left(j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\zeta\left(r^{-1}\right)\right)^{\ast}\right)\gamma_{r}\left(j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\zeta\left(r^{-1}s\right)\right)\right)~\mathrm{d}{r}

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\gamma_{r^{-1}}\left(j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\zeta\left(r\right)\right)^{\ast}\right)\gamma_{r^{-1}}\left(j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\zeta\left(rs\right)\right)\right)~\mathrm{d}{r}

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\gamma_{r^{-1}}\left(j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\zeta\left(r\right)\right)^{\ast}j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\zeta\left(rs\right)\right)\right)~\mathrm{d}{r}

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\gamma_{r^{-1}}\left(j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\left\langle\zeta\left(r\right)\middle|\zeta\left(rs\right)\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}}\right)\right)~\mathrm{d}{r}

\displaystyle=\int_{G}j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\alpha_{r^{-1}}\left(\left\langle\zeta\left(r\right)\middle|\zeta\left(rs\right)\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}}\right)\right)~\mathrm{d}{r}

\displaystyle=j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\int_{G}\alpha_{r^{-1}}\left(\left\langle\zeta\left(r\right)\middle|\zeta\left(rs\right)\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}}\right)~\mathrm{d}{r}\right)

\displaystyle=j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\left\langle\zeta\middle|\zeta\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}\rtimes_{\beta^{p}}G}\left(s\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\left[\psi_{0}\left(\left\langle\zeta\middle|\zeta\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}\rtimes_{\beta^{p}}G}\right)\right]\left(s\right),

so

\displaystyle\left\|j_{\mathsf{Y}}\circ\zeta\right\|_{\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)\rtimes_{\gamma}G}

\displaystyle=\left\|\left(j_{\mathsf{Y}}\circ\zeta\right)^{\ast}\left(j_{\mathsf{Y}}\circ\zeta\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)\rtimes_{\gamma}G}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\psi_{0}\left(\left\langle\zeta\middle|\zeta\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}\rtimes_{\beta^{p}}G}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)\rtimes_{\gamma}G}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\left\langle\zeta\middle|\zeta\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}\rtimes_{\beta^{p}}G}\right\|_{A\rtimes_{\alpha}G}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\zeta\right\|_{\mathsf{Y}\rtimes_{\beta}G}.

In light of this norm-inequality, there exists a continuous linear map

\psi_{p}:\mathsf{Y}_{p}\rtimes_{\beta^{p}}G\to\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)\rtimes_{\gamma}G

such that
\psi_{p}\left(\zeta\right)=j_{\mathsf{Y}}\circ\zeta
for all
\zeta\in C_{c}\left(G,\mathsf{Y}_{p}\right)
. By combining the various
\psi_{p}
’s, we get a map
\psi:\mathsf{Y}\rtimes_{\beta}G\to\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)\rtimes_{\gamma}G
. The following show that
\psi
is a Toeplitz representation:

•

As seen above,
\psi_{e}\left(\left\langle\zeta\middle|\zeta\right\rangle_{\mathsf{Y}_{p}\rtimes_{\beta^{p}}G}\right)=\psi_{p}\left(\zeta\right)^{\ast}\psi_{p}\left(\zeta\right)
for all
p\in P
and
\zeta\in C_{c}\left(G,\mathsf{Y}_{p}\right)
.

•

For all
p,q\in P
,
\zeta\in\mathsf{Y}_{p}\rtimes_{\beta^{p}}G
,
\eta\in\mathsf{Y}_{q}\rtimes_{\beta^{q}}G
, and
s\in G
,

\displaystyle\left[\psi_{p}\left(\zeta\right)\psi_{q}\left(\eta\right)\right]\left(s\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\left[\psi_{p}\left(\zeta\right)\right]\left(r\right)\gamma_{r}\left(\left[\psi_{q}\left(\eta\right)\right]\left(r^{-1}s\right)\right)~\mathrm{d}{r}

\displaystyle=\int_{G}j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\zeta\left(r\right)\right)\gamma_{r}\left(j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\eta\left(r^{-1}s\right)\right)\right)~\mathrm{d}{r}

\displaystyle=\int_{G}j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\zeta\left(r\right)\right)j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\beta^{q}_{r}\left(\eta\left(r^{-1}s\right)\right)\right)~\mathrm{d}{r}

\displaystyle=j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\int_{G}\zeta\left(r\right)\beta^{q}_{r}\left(\eta\left(r^{-1}s\right)\right)~\mathrm{d}{r}\right)

\displaystyle=j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\left(\zeta\eta\right)\left(s\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\left[\psi_{p+q}\left(\zeta\eta\right)\right]\left(s\right),

so
\psi_{p}\left(\zeta\right)\psi_{q}\left(\eta\right)=\psi_{p+q}\left(\zeta\eta\right)
.

It thus remains to check Cuntz-Pimsner covariance. If

\psi^{\left(p\right)}:\mathcal{K}\left(\mathsf{Y}_{p}\rtimes_{\beta^{p}}G\right)\to\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)\rtimes_{\gamma}G

denotes the extension of
\psi_{p}
, then letting
p\in P
,
\zeta,\eta\in C_{c}\left(G,\mathsf{Y}_{p}\right)
, and
s\in G
,

\displaystyle\left[\psi^{\left(p\right)}\left(\Theta_{\zeta,\eta}\right)\right]\left(s\right)

\displaystyle=\left[\psi_{p}\left(\zeta\right)\psi_{p}\left(\eta\right)^{\ast}\right]\left(s\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\left[\psi_{p}\left(\zeta\right)\right]\left(r\right)\gamma_{r}\left(\left[\psi_{p}\left(\eta\right)^{\ast}\right]\left(r^{-1}s\right)\right)~\mathrm{d}{r}

\displaystyle=\int_{G}j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\zeta\left(r\right)\right)\gamma_{r}\left(\Delta\left(s^{-1}r\right)\cdot\gamma_{r^{-1}s}\left(j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\eta\left(s^{-1}r\right)\right)^{\ast}\right)\right)~\mathrm{d}{r}

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\Delta\left(s^{-1}r\right)\cdot j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\zeta\left(r\right)\right)\gamma_{s}\left(j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\eta\left(s^{-1}r\right)\right)^{\ast}\right)~\mathrm{d}{r}

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\Delta\left(s^{-1}r\right)\cdot j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\zeta\left(r\right)\right)\gamma_{s}\left(j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\eta\left(s^{-1}r\right)\right)\right)^{\ast}~\mathrm{d}{r}

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\Delta\left(s^{-1}r\right)\cdot j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\zeta\left(r\right)\right)j_{\mathsf{Y}}\left(\beta^{p}_{s}\left(\eta\left(s^{-1}r\right)\right)\right)^{\ast}~\mathrm{d}{r}

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\Delta\left(s^{-1}r\right)\cdot j_{\mathsf{Y}}^{\left(p\right)}\left(\Theta_{\zeta\left(r\right),\beta^{p}_{s}\left(\eta\left(s^{-1}r\right)\right)}\right)~\mathrm{d}{r}

\displaystyle=j_{\mathsf{Y}}^{\left(p\right)}\left(\int_{G}\Delta\left(s^{-1}r\right)\cdot\Theta_{\zeta\left(r\right),\beta^{p}_{s}\left(\eta\left(s^{-1}r\right)\right)}~\mathrm{d}{r}\right)

\displaystyle=j_{\mathsf{Y}}^{\left(p\right)}\left(\left[\Lambda\left(\Theta_{\zeta,\eta}\right)\right]\left(s\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\left[j_{\mathsf{Y}}^{\left(p\right)}\circ\Lambda\left(\Theta_{\zeta,\eta}\right)\right]\left(s\right).

Hence,
\psi^{\left(p\right)}\left(\Theta_{\zeta,\eta}\right)=j_{\mathsf{Y}}^{\left(p\right)}\circ\Lambda\left(\Theta_{\zeta,\eta}\right)
, which means that
\psi^{\left(p\right)}\left(T\right)=j_{\mathsf{Y}}^{\left(p\right)}\circ\Lambda\left(T\right)
for all
T\in\mathcal{K}\left(\mathsf{Y}\rtimes_{\beta^{p}}G\right)
. In particular, we have for all
f\in C_{c}\left(G,A\right)
that

\displaystyle\psi^{\left(p\right)}\left(\overline{\phi_{p}}\left(f\right)\right)

\displaystyle=j_{\mathsf{Y}}^{\left(p\right)}\circ\Lambda\left(\overline{\phi_{p}}\left(f\right)\right)

\displaystyle=j_{\mathsf{Y}}^{\left(p\right)}\circ\Lambda\left(\Lambda^{-1}\left(\phi_{p}\circ f\right)\right)

\displaystyle=j_{\mathsf{Y}}^{\left(p\right)}\circ\phi_{p}\circ f

\displaystyle=j_{\mathsf{Y}}\circ f

\displaystyle=\psi_{e}\left(f\right).

Therefore,
\psi^{\left(p\right)}\circ\overline{\phi_{p}}=\psi_{e}
for all
p\in P
, which proves that
\psi
is Cuntz-Pimsner covariant. By universality,
\psi
determines a unique
\ast
-homomorphism

\psi_{\ast}:\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\rtimes_{\beta}G\right)\to\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)\rtimes_{\gamma}G.

The image of
\psi_{\ast}
generates
\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)\rtimes_{\gamma}G
, so
\psi_{\ast}
is surjective. The injectivity of
\psi_{\ast}
follows from the injectivity of
\psi_{e}
and the existence of a gauge action of
\mathbb{T}^{k}
on
\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)\rtimes_{\gamma}G
; see Lemma 3.3.2 in
[
6
]
or Corollary 4.14 in
[
3
]
.
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

Suppose
P=\mathbb{N}^{k}
. If
A
is AF and each
C^{\ast}
-correspondence
\mathsf{Y}_{n}
is full and separable, then
\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)\rtimes_{\gamma}\mathbb{T}^{k}
is AF.

Proof.

Recall that in this case
\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)\rtimes_{\gamma}\mathbb{T}^{k}
is Morita-Rieffel equivalent with the core
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}\left(Y\right)^{\gamma}\cong\varinjlim_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{k}}\mathcal{K}\left(\mathsf{Y}_{n}\right)
and that each
\mathcal{K}\left(\mathsf{Y}_{n}\right)
is Morita-Rieffel equivalent to
A
.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

Katsoulis obtained similar results in
[
11
]
for the so-called generalized gauge action on a product system over a semigroup
P
which is the positive cone of an abelian group. Moreover, using a Fourier transform, he proves a Takai duality result and generalizes some results of Schafhauser from
[
16
]
.

Example 3.9
.

Let
G
be a compact group and let
\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{k}
be finite-dimensional representations of
G
on Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}_{1},...,\mathcal{H}_{k}
. Let
\mathsf{Y}
be the product system with fibers
\mathsf{Y}_{n}=\mathcal{H}^{n}=\mathcal{H}_{1}^{\otimes n_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{H}_{k}^{\otimes n_{k}}
for
n=(n_{1},...,n_{k})\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
, in particular,
A=\mathsf{Y}_{0}=\mathbb{C}
.

The compact group
G
acts on each fiber
\mathsf{Y}_{n}
of the product system
\mathsf{Y}
via the representation
\rho^{n}=\rho_{1}^{\otimes n_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\rho_{k}^{\otimes n_{k}}
. This action is compatible with the multiplication maps and commutes with the gauge action of
\mathbb{T}^{k}
. The crossed product
\mathsf{Y}\rtimes G
becomes a row-finite and faithful product system indexed by
\mathbb{N}^{k}
over the group
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}(G)
. Moreover,

\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)\rtimes G\cong\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\rtimes G\right).

The Doplicher-Roberts algebra
\mathcal{O}_{\rho_{1},...,\rho_{k}}
constructed in
[
4
]
from intertwiners Hom
(\rho^{n},\rho^{m})
is isomorphic to the fixed point algebra
\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)^{G}
and is Morita-Rieffel equivalent to
\mathcal{O}\left(\mathsf{Y}\right)\rtimes G
.

Example 3.10
.

If a locally compact group
G
acts on a
k
-graph
\Lambda
by automorphisms, then
G
acts on the product system
\mathsf{Y}
constructed from
\Lambda
as in Example
2.2
and the
C^{*}
-algebra of the product system
\mathsf{Y}\rtimes G
is isomorphic to
C^{*}(\Lambda)\rtimes G
. In
[
9
]
the authors consider the particular case when
G=\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}
and they construct a
(k+\ell)
-graph
\Lambda\times\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}
such that
C^{*}(\Lambda\times\mathbb{Z}^{\ell})\cong C^{*}(\Lambda)\rtimes\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}
. Our result gives a new perspective of this situation.

Invariance of plurigenera for generalized polarized pairs with abundant nef parts
Zhan Li, Zhiwei Wang

Abstract
. We show the invariance of plurigenera for generalized polarized pairs with abundant nef parts and generalized canonical singularities. This is obtained by investigating a type of newly introduced multiplier ideal sheaf which is of bimeromorphic nature.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
32Q99, 14E99

1.
Introduction

Let
X\to\Delta
be a smooth projective family of complex manifolds over a disc. A celebrated result of Siu (see
[
Siu98
,
Siu02
]
) asserts that the plurigenera
h^{0}(X_{t},\mathcal{O}_{X_{t}}(mK_{X_{t}}))
is a constant function of
t\in\Delta
for each positive integer
m
. Results of such type have been investigated for a long time (see
[
Kaw99b
]
for a survey) and have been generalized in many directions after Siu’s work (see
[
Pău07
,
Tak07
,
BP12
,
RT20
]
, etc.). Invariance of plurigenera is an important ingredient in the proof of the boundedness of moduli of general type varieties (see
[
HMX13
, Theorem 1.8]
,
[
HMX18
, §2.3]
). Moreover, Siu’s method has a profound impact on problems such as deformations of canonical singularities (see
[
Kaw99a
]
) and the existence of good minimal models (see
[
DHP13
]
).

Let
Y
be a smooth projective variety and
\mu:Y\dashrightarrow X
be the canonical model of
Y
(see
[
BCHM10
, Definition 3.6.5]
,
[
Li22
, Definition 2.1]
). It is natural to ask whether
X
(birationally) belongs to a bounded moduli space given
Y
satisfying certain restrictions (see
[
FS20
]
,
[
Li20
, Conjecture 1.2]
). When
Y
is of general type, then the answer is affirmative if
Y
has a fixed dimension and a bounded volume (see
[
HMX13
,
HMX18
]
). To study the general situation, one can adopt the same strategy as
[
HMX13
,
HMX18
]
. In this case, the base variety
X
admits a generalized polarized pair structure instead of a log pair structure (see Definition
2.1
). Roughly speaking, there is a triple
(X,B+M)
with
B,M
divisors on
X
. Here
B\geq 0
accounts for the singularities of the fibers, and
M
is the push-forward of a nef and abundant divisor which accounts for the moduli of the fibers. It is this additional
M
that ruins the straightforward applications of the results in
[
HMX13
,
HMX18
]
. For one thing,
M
is only well-defined up to linear equivalence.

More generally, if
Y\to X
is a
K_{Y}
-trivial fibration, then
X
also admits a generalized polarized pair structure. In fact, the concept of generalized polarized pairs originates from such observations (see
[
Bir21
]
). Note that in the definition of generalized polarized pairs, the nef part is just assumed to be the push-forward of a nef divisor instead of a nef and abundant divisor. However, the latter case is more meaningful in geometry, and our main motivation is to study the invariance of plurigenera in such setting:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\pi:X\to\Delta
be a projective contraction from a complex space
X
to the disc
\Delta
. Assume that
X
has canonical singularities. Let
L
be a Cartier divisor on
X
and
h
be a metric for
\mathcal{O}_{X}(L)
with non-negative curvature current. Let
X_{0}\subset X
be the fiber over
0\in\Delta
. Suppose that
X_{0}
has canonical singularities and
h|_{X_{0}}
is well-defined. Then for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, any section of

H^{0}(X_{0},\mathcal{O}_{X_{0}}(mK_{X_{0}}+L|_{X_{0}})\otimes\mathcal{G}_{m}(h|_{X_{0}}))

extends over
X
(i.e. any section has a preimage in
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(mK+L))
under the map in Lemma
5.8
).

The newly introduced ideal sheaf
\mathcal{G}_{m}(h)
is of the birational (or bimeromorphic) nature (see Section
5.3
). Comparing with previous studies, this birational point of view gives new ingredients even in the smooth case (see Example
5.6
, Remark
5.16
). For relevant notions of metrics on complex spaces which are adapted to this perspective, see Section
3
.

Theorem
1.1
involves with metrics and
L^{2}
-conditions. In order to use it in algebraic geometry, we need to obtain such analytic requirements by natural algebro-geometric conditions. The following statement is metric-free.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\pi:X\to\Delta
be a projective contraction from a complex space
X
to the disc
\Delta
. Let
X^{\prime}\xrightarrow{f}X\xrightarrow{\pi}\Delta
be a generalized polarized pair with the boundary part
B
and the abundant nef part
M
. Let
X_{0}\subset X
be the fiber over
0\in\Delta
and
(X_{0},B_{0}+M_{0})
be the generalized polarized pair obtained by restricting to
X_{0}
. Assume that
(X_{0},B_{0}+M_{0})
has g-canonical singularities (in particular,
B_{0}=0
). Then for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
mM
is Cartier, any section of

H^{0}(X_{0},\mathcal{O}_{X_{0}}(m(K_{X_{0}}+M|_{X_{0}})))

extends over
X
.

A direct consequence of Theorem
1.2
is

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
\pi:X\to\Delta
be a projective contraction from a complex space
X
to the disc
\Delta
. Let
X^{\prime}\xrightarrow{f}X\xrightarrow{\pi}\Delta
be a generalized polarized pair with the boundary part
B
and the abundant nef part
M
. Let
X_{t}\subset X
be the fiber over
t\in\Delta
and
(X_{t},B_{t}+M_{t})
be the generalized polarized pair obtained by restricting to
X_{t}
. Assume that for any
t\in\Delta
,
(X_{t},B_{t}+M_{t})
has g-canonical singularities (in particular,
B_{t}=0
). Then for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
mM
is Cartier,

h^{0}(X_{t},\mathcal{O}_{X_{t}}(m(K_{X_{t}}+M|_{X_{t}})))

is independent of
t\in\Delta
.

Both the abundant assumption and the singularity assumption are indispensable. In fact, Corollary
1.3
is false if
M
is just assumed to be nef (see Example
5.17
). An example of Kawamata shows that the extension of local sections fails if the singularities are klt (see
[
Kaw99b
, Example 4.3]
).

It turns out that even in the smooth case, Păun’s twisted version of invariance of plurigenera (see
[
Pău07
, Theorem 1]
) is not enough to obtain Theorem
1.2
(see Remark
5.16
). Instead, we need Theorem
1.1
which allows potentially more sections to be extended.

Besides,
[
FS20
, Theorem 4.1]
establishes a version of invariance of plurigenera assuming that
K_{X}+B+M
or
B+M
is big over
\Delta
and
M
is nef over
\Delta
. The argument of
[
FS20
, Theorem 4.1]
follows from
[
HMX13
, Theorem 4.2]
which relies on the minimal model program for varieties of general type.

We discuss the structure of the paper. Section
2
gives the background material on generalized polarized pairs for complex spaces. Section
3
discusses metrics of
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisors on complex spaces. Section
4
gives the construction of metrics for the abundant nef parts of generalized polarized pairs. Section
5
introduces a new type multiplier ideal sheaf and proves the aforementioned theorems and corollary.

2.
Generalized polarized pairs

2.1.
Notation and conventions

Let
\mathbb{Z}
be the set of integers and
\mathbb{N}=\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
be the set of natural numbers.

A holomorphic map
f:X\to Y
between complex spaces is called a morphism, and a morphism is called a contraction if it is surjective with connected fibers. The morphism
f
is called a proper modification if (1)
f
is proper and surjective, and (2) there exists a nowhere dense analytic subset
Z\subset Y
such that
f|_{X-f^{-1}(Z)}:X-f^{-1}(Z)\to Y-Z
is an isomorphism. A bimeromorphic map
f:X\dashrightarrow Y
between complex spaces is a meromorphic map such that the graph
\Gamma_{f}
is an irreducible analytic subset in
X\times Y
, and the natural maps
\Gamma_{f}\to X,\Gamma_{f}\to Y
are proper modifications (see
[
Uen75
, Definitions 2.1, 2.2, 2.7]
). A proper modification
\mu:W\to X
is called a resolution if
W
is smooth. A proper morphism
f:X\to Y
between complex spaces is called projective if for any relatively compact open set
U\subset Y
, there is an embedding
j:f^{-1}(U)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}_{U}^{n}\coloneqq\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\times U
such that
f|_{f^{-1}(U)}=p_{2}\circ j
, where
p_{2}:\mathbb{P}_{U}^{n}\to U
is the natural projection map (see
[
Pet94b
, Chapter V]
).

A divisor means a Weil divisor. A finite
\mathbb{Q}
-linear combination of divisors gives
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors. A
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor is called
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier if it is a finite
\mathbb{Q}
-linear combination of Cartier divisors.

Let
D
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
X
. Then
(X,D)
is called a log pair. A resolution
\mu:W\to X
is called a log resolution of
(X,D)
if
W
is smooth and
\operatorname{Supp}\mu_{*}^{-1}D\cup\operatorname{Supp}\operatorname{Exc}(\mu)
is a simple normal crossing divisor, where
\mu_{*}^{-1}D
is the strict transform of
D
and
\operatorname{Exc}(\mu)
is the exceptional locus of
\mu
. When
X
is a reduced complex space,
(X,D)
always admits a projective log resolution (see
[
Wło09
, Theorem 2.0.2]
).

Let
X
be a normal complex space. Let
X\to S
be a morphism to a complex space
S
. We write the relative property over
S
by
/S
. Let
k=\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Q}
. For two
k
-divisors
B
and
D
, we use
B\sim_{k}D/S
to denote that
B
and
D
are
k
-linearly equivalent over
S
.

Let
D
be a Weil divisor on a normal complex space
X
, then
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
denotes the sheaf associated with
D
. To be precise, let
\mathscr{M}_{X}
be the sheaf of germs of meromorphic functions on
X
(see
[
Dem12
, Chapter II, §6.2]
), then
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)\subset\mathscr{M}_{X}
is the sheaf such that for any open set
U\subset X
,

\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)(U)=\{f\in\mathscr{M}_{X}(U)\mid\operatorname{div}(f)+D|_{U}\geq 0\}.

The sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
is coherent and the subscript of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
will be omitted if it is clear from the context.

Recall that for an
n
-dimensional normal complex space
X
, the canonical divisor
K_{X}
is any Weil divisor such that
j_{*}\Omega^{n}_{X_{\rm reg}}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X})
for the open embedding
j:X_{\rm reg}\hookrightarrow X
, where
X_{\rm reg}
is the smooth locus of
X
and
\Omega^{n}_{X_{\rm reg}}
is the sheaf of holomorphic
n
-forms on
X_{\rm reg}
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a morphism and
B
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor on
Y
. We write
f^{*}B
for the
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor which is the pull-back of
B
. If
D
is a Cartier divisor on
X
, then we write
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
for the corresponding line bundle, and
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
for the push-forward of the sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a proper modification between normal complex spaces and
D
be a prime divisor on
X
. We define the push-forward divisor

f_{*}D\coloneqq\begin{cases}f(D),&\text{ if~ }D\text{~is not~}f\text{-exceptional},\\
0,&\text{ if~ }D\text{~is~}f\text{-exceptional}.\end{cases}

This construction can be extended to
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors by linearity. Moreover, for
k=\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Q}
, if
B\sim_{k}D/S
, then
f_{*}B\sim_{k}f_{*}D/S
because the push-forward of a principal divisor is still a principal divisor.

Finally, for a projective morphism
X\to S
, a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor
D
on
X
is nef
/S
if there is an ample
/S
divisor
H
on
X
such that
D+\epsilon H
is ample
/S
for any
\epsilon\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
, and
D
is big
/S
if
D|_{X_{t}}
is big for a general
t\in S
.

2.2.
Generalized polarized pairs with abundant nef part

Generalized polarized pairs originate from the canonical bundle formula (see
[
Kaw98
]
). It was observed by
[
BZ16
]
that the generalized polarized pair structure deserves to be studied separately and it behaves like the usual log pair structure in many ways. We state the definition of generalized polarized pairs for complex spaces (see
[
BZ16
, Definition 1.4]
for the original definition in the algebraic category).

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X^{\prime},X
and
S
be normal complex spaces. A generalized polarized pair (g-pair) consists of projective morphisms
X^{\prime}\xrightarrow{f}X\to S
where
f
is a modification, a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
B\geq 0
on
X
, and a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor
M^{\prime}
on
X^{\prime}
which is nef
/S
such that
K_{X}+{B}+{M}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier, where
M\coloneqq f_{*}M^{\prime}
. We call
B
the boundary part and
M
the nef part.

Remark 2.2
.

In the definition of g-pairs, we can replace
X^{\prime}
by any projective modification of
X^{\prime}
and
M^{\prime}
by its pull-back. Therefore, we can assume that
f
is a projective log resolution of
(X,B)
.

Definition 2.3
.

Under the notation of Definition
2.1
, if there exists a proper contraction
g:X^{\prime}\to Z/S
such that
M^{\prime}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}g^{*}H/S
for some nef and big
/S

\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor
H
on
Z
, then we call that such g-pair has the abundant nef part.

Replacing
X^{\prime}
by a projective modification and
M^{\prime}
by its pull-back (see Remark
2.2
), the new g-pair still has the abundant nef part.

Remark 2.4
.

A g-pair with the abundant nef part naturally appears in the canonical bundle formula where the concept of g-pairs originates (see
[
Amb05
]
). The word “abundant” comes from the fact that divisors satisfying the property in Definition
2.3
is related to the abundance conjecture (see
[
Kaw85
, Proposition 2.1]
).

2.3.
Singularities and adjunctions for g-pairs

Definition 2.5
.

A normal complex space is called
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein if
K_{X}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor.

Definition 2.6
.

A normal complex space
X
has canonical singularities if (1)
X
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein, and (2) for any resolution
f:W\to X
, if
K_{W}=f^{*}K_{X}+E
with
E
an
f
-exceptional divisor, then
E\geq 0
.

Definition 2.7
.

Under the notation of Definition
2.1
, for a prime divisor
P
over
X
, its discrepancy with respect to (X, B+M) is defined to be

{\rm discp}(P;X,B+M)\coloneqq\operatorname{mult}_{P}\left(K_{Y}+g^{*}M^{\prime}-(f\circ g)^{*}(K_{X}+B+M)\right),

where
g:Y\to X^{\prime}
is a proper modification such that
P\subset Y
.

Definition 2.8
.

A g-pair
(X,B+M)
has g-canonical (resp. g-terminal, g-klt, g-lc) singularities if
{\rm discp}(P;X,B+M)\geq 0
(resp.
>0
,
>-1
,
\geq-1
) for each prime divisor
P
over
X
.

The adjunction formula for g-pairs is given by
[
BZ16
, Definition 4.7]
in the algebraic setting. It can be naturally extended to the analytic setting.

Definition 2.9

(Adjunction formula for g-pairs)
.

Let
(X,B+M)
be a g-pair with data
X^{\prime}\xrightarrow{f}X\to S
and
M^{\prime}
. Let
P
be a normal irreducible component of
\lfloor B\rfloor
and
P^{\prime}
be its strict transform on
X^{\prime}
. We may assume that
f
is a projective log resolution of
(X,B+M)
. Write

K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{\prime}+M^{\prime}=f^{*}(K_{X}+B+M),

then

K_{P^{\prime}}+B_{P^{\prime}}+M_{P^{\prime}}\coloneqq(K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{\prime}+M^{\prime})|_{P^{\prime}},

where
B_{P^{\prime}}=(B^{\prime}-P^{\prime})|_{P^{\prime}}
and
M_{P^{\prime}}=M^{\prime}|_{P^{\prime}}
. Let
g
be the induced morphism
P^{\prime}\to P
. Set
B_{P}=g_{*}B_{P^{\prime}}
and
M_{P}=g_{*}M_{P^{\prime}}
. Then we get the equality

K_{P}+B_{P}+M_{P}=(K_{X}+B+M)|_{P},

which is referred as (generalized) adjunction formula.

In general,
M|_{P}\neq M_{P}
, and even if
(X,B+M)
has the abundant nef part,
(P,B_{P}+M_{P})
may not have the abundant nef part. On the other hand, in the category of algebraic varieties, if
(X,B+M)
is g-lc, then
(P,B_{P}+M_{P})
is still g-lc with data
P^{\prime}\xrightarrow{f}P\to S
and
M_{P^{\prime}}
by
[
BZ16
, Remark 4.8]
.

Let
X\to\Delta
be a projective contraction to the disc
\Delta
. Let
X_{0}
be the fiber over
0\in\Delta
. Suppose that
X_{0}
is normal, then we define

(2.3.1)

(X_{0},B_{0}+M_{0})

to be the g-pair by the adjunction of
(X,X_{0}+B+M)
on
X_{0}
. In the sequel,
(X_{0},B_{0}+M_{0})
will be assumed to have g-canonical singularities. Under this assumption,
B_{0}=0
and
M_{0}=M|_{X_{0}}
.

3.
Metrics on complex spaces

3.1.
Metrics and
\mathbb{Q}
-metrics

Let
X
be a normal complex space,
\mathcal{L}
be a holomorphic line bundle on
X
. We define the notion of singular metrics in this setting.

Let
x\in X
be a point and
\mathcal{L}_{x}
be the stalk at
x
. Assume that there is a function

\|-\|_{h}:\mathcal{L}_{x}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cup\{+\infty\},

such that if
\theta:\mathcal{L}|_{U}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{U}
is a trivialization on an open set
U\subset X
, then for any
s\in\mathcal{L}_{x}
, we have

\|s\|_{h}=|\theta(s)|e^{-\varphi}

for a function
\varphi:U\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
. This
\varphi
is called a local weight and it depends on
\theta
. Let
\mu:W\to X
be a resolution. Then
\mu^{*}\theta:\mu^{*}\mathcal{L}|_{\mu^{-1}(U)}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mu^{-1}(U)}
is a trivialization for
\mu^{*}\mathcal{L}
. For any
\tilde{s}\in(\mu^{*}\mathcal{L})_{w}
with
w\in\mu^{-1}(U)
, set

(3.1.1)

\|\tilde{s}\|_{\mu^{*}h}\coloneqq|\mu^{*}\theta(\tilde{s})|e^{-\mu^{*}\varphi},

where
\mu^{*}\varphi(w)=\varphi(\mu(w))
. Note that
\|-\|_{\mu^{*}h}
is independent of the choice of trivializations. In fact, for local trivializations
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}
, there is a nowhere vanishing holomorphic function
u
such that
\theta_{1}=u\theta_{2}
. Thus
|u|e^{-\varphi_{1}}=e^{-\varphi_{2}}
and

(3.1.2)

|\mu^{*}u|e^{-\mu^{*}\varphi_{1}}=e^{-\mu^{*}\varphi_{2}}.

Let
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(V)
be the set of locally integrable functions on a smooth open set
V
.

Definition 3.1

(Singular metrics on normal complex spaces)
.

Under the above notation,
h
is a (singular) metric of
\mathcal{L}
if for any resolution
\mu:W\to X
, we have
\mu^{*}\varphi\in L_{\rm loc}^{1}(\mu^{-1}(U))
for any open set
U\subset X
.

The property of
\varphi
in Definition
3.1
will be referred as the
L^{1}_{\rm loc}
-property. By abuse of terminology, if
D
is a Cartier divisor, then we also call a metric of the corresponding line bundle
\mathcal{O}(D)
a metric of
D
. This is particularly convenient to treat
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisors.

Definition 3.2

(
[
Pet94b
, Definition 1.4]
)
.

Let
X
be a complex space. A plurisubharmonic (psh for short) function on
X
is a function
\varphi:X\rightarrow[-\infty,\infty)
having the following property. For every
x\in X
there is an open neighborhood
U
with a biholomorphic map
h:U\rightarrow V
onto a closed complex subspace
V
of some domain
G\subset\mathbb{C}^{m}
and a plurisubharmonic function
\tilde{\varphi}:G\rightarrow[-\infty,\infty)
such that
\varphi|_{U}=\tilde{\varphi}\circ h
.

If
f:X\rightarrow Y
is a holomorphic map between two complex spaces, and
\varphi:Y\rightarrow[-\infty,\infty)
is a psh function on
Y
, then
\varphi\circ f
is a psh function on
X
(see
[
Nar61
, Page 356]
).

Definition 3.3
.

Under the notation of Definition
3.1
,
(\mathcal{L},h)
is said to have non-negative curvature current if any local weight
\varphi
on an open subset of
X
is a psh function.

Remark 3.4
.

In Definition
3.1
, the local
L_{\rm loc}^{1}
-property is needed to hold for all resolutions. If the
L_{\rm loc}^{1}
-property holds for one resolution
\mu:W\to X
, then it is not true that this property holds for another resolution
\nu:V\to X
even if
\nu
factors through
\mu
. In fact, by the change-of-variables formula,

\int_{W}|\tilde{\varphi}|~dV_{W}=\int_{V}|\nu^{*}\tilde{\varphi}|\cdot|s|^{2}~dV_{V},

where
s
is the local equation for the effective exceptional divisor
K_{V}-f^{*}K_{W}
(hence
s
is holomorphic). Hence
\tilde{\varphi}\in L_{\rm loc}^{1}
does not imply
\nu^{*}\tilde{\varphi}\in L_{\rm loc}^{1}
.

However, if the curvature current is non-negative (see Definition
3.3
), then it is enough to just consider one resolution (see Proposition
3.5
).

The notion of psh functions is of bimeromorphic nature:

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\mu:W\to X
be a resolution of a normal complex space
X
. Then a function
\varphi:X\to[-\infty,\infty]
is a psh function iff
\varphi\circ\mu:Y\to[-\infty,\infty]
is a psh function.

Proof.

One only needs to show the sufficient part. As
\mu
is a proper modification,
Z\coloneqq\mu(\operatorname{Exc}(\mu))
is an analytic set of codimension
\geq 2
. Then
\varphi
is a psh function on
X-Z
. By
[
Pet94b
, Theorem 1.5]
, the definition of psh functions in
[
GR56
]
is the same as Definition
3.2
. By
[
GR56
, Page 181, Satz 4]
,
\varphi
extends to a psh function
\hat{\varphi}
on
X
. As
\hat{\varphi}\circ\mu
is a psh function on
W
which coincides with
\varphi\circ\mu
on
W-\operatorname{Exc}(\mu)
, we have
\hat{\varphi}\circ\mu=\varphi\circ\mu
on
W
. Hence
\varphi=\hat{\varphi}
is a psh function.
∎

For simplicity, we can formally extend the notion of metrics for
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisors (or
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundles).

Definition 3.6
.

Let
D
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor on a complex space
X
. Then a
\mathbb{Q}
-metric
h
for
D
is a triple
(m,D,h_{mD})
such that
m\in\mathbb{N}
with
mD
a Cartier divisor and
h_{mD}
is a metric for
mD
. We say that two
\mathbb{Q}
-metrics
(m,D,h_{mD})
and
(n,D,h_{nD})
are equal if
h_{mD}^{n}=h_{nD}^{m}
as metrics for the Cartier divisor
mnD
. For simplicity,
h
is also called a metric.

Remark 3.7
.

Given a metric
(m,D,h_{mD})
, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
nD
is Cartier, there always exists a metric
(n,D,h_{nD})
which is equal to
(m,D,h_{mD})
. In fact, we can set
h_{nD}=h_{mD}^{\frac{n}{m}}
.

If
D=\sum D_{i}
is a finite sum of
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisors such that each
D_{i}
admits a metric
h_{i}
, then we define a metric
h\coloneqq\prod_{i}h_{i}
for
D
as follows. Take
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that each
mD_{i}
is Cartier, then

h=(m,D,h_{mD})

with
h_{mD}=\prod_{i}h_{mD_{i}}
where
h_{i}=(m,D_{i},h_{mD_{i}})
(see Remark
3.7
). It is straightforward to check that this definition is independent of the choice of
m
. Let
D
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor with a metric
h
, then for
r\in\mathbb{Q}
, we can similarly define a metric
h^{r}
for
rD
.

Let
f:W\to X
be a proper modification between normal complex spaces. Let
D
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor on
X
with a metric
h
. We can define the pull-back metric
f^{*}h
for the
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor
f^{*}D
. In fact, it is enough to assume that
D
is a Cartier divisor by the definition of metrics for
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisors. If
\theta:\mathcal{O}(D)|_{U}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{U}
is a trivialization, then
f^{*}\theta:\mathcal{O}(f^{*}D)|_{f^{-1}(U)}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{f^{-1}(U)}
. If
\|s\|_{h}=|\theta(s)|e^{-\varphi}
for
x\in U
and
s\in\mathcal{O}(D)_{x}
, then

\|\tilde{s}\|_{f^{*}h}\coloneqq|f^{*}\theta(\tilde{s})|e^{-f^{*}\varphi}

for
w\in f^{-1}(U)
and
\tilde{s}\in\mathcal{O}(f^{*}D)_{w}
. This definition is independent of the choice of
\theta
.

Remark 3.8
.

It is more convenient to think of a metric in terms of a local weight: if
\varphi
is the local weight for
h_{mD}
(under a given trivialization), then
\frac{\varphi}{m}
is defined to be the local weight of
(m,D,h_{mD})
. The summation (resp. rational multiple, pull-back) of local weights corresponds to the product (resp. rational exponent, pull-back) of metrics.

Remark 3.9
.

Suppose that
B,D
are
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisors such that
B\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}D
, then a metric of
B
is also a metric of
D
. In fact, local weights are identified under an isomorphism
\mathcal{O}(mB)\simeq\mathcal{O}(mD)
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

On a complex manifold, a function
\varphi
is called quasi-plurisubharmonic (quasi-psh) if it is a summation of a psh function with a smooth function.

Definition 3.10

(
[
DPS00
, Definition 1.4]
)
.

For two quasi-psh functions
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
, we say that
\varphi_{1}
is less singular than
\varphi_{2}
(and write
\varphi_{1}\preceq\varphi_{2}
) if
\varphi_{2}\leq\varphi_{1}+c
for a constant
c
. We write
\varphi_{1}\approx\varphi_{2}
if
\varphi_{1}\preceq\varphi_{2}
and
\varphi_{2}\preceq\varphi_{1}
.

For two functions
\varphi_{i}:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\},i=1,2
on a complex space
X
, we use the same notation as above if the corresponding relations hold after pulling back
\varphi_{i}
to all the resolutions.

3.2.
Metrics defined by global sections

Let
X
be a normal complex space and
L
be a Cartier divisor on
X
. Suppose that
\sigma_{i}\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(L)),1\leq i\leq k
are global sections (may not necessarily be distinct). They can be used to define a metric
h
for
L
just as in the smooth case.

Let
U\subset X
be an open set such that
\theta:\mathcal{O}(L)|_{U}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{U}
is a trivialization. Then for any
s\in L_{x}
, set

(3.2.1)

\|s\|^{2}_{h}=\frac{|\theta(s)|^{2}}{\sum_{1\leq i\leq k}|\theta(\sigma_{i})|^{2}}=|\theta(s)|^{2}e^{-2\varphi}.

The local weight of
h
(under the trivialization
\theta
) is

\varphi=\frac{1}{2}\log(\sum_{1\leq i\leq k}|\theta(\sigma_{i})|^{2}).

It satisfies the
L_{\rm loc}^{1}
-property for any resolution
\mu:W\to X
because

\mu^{*}\varphi=\frac{1}{2}\log(\sum_{1\leq i\leq k}|\mu^{*}\theta(\mu^{*}\sigma_{i})|^{2})

is a psh function and thus Proposition
3.5
applies to this situation.

More generally, for a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor
D
on
X
, suppose that
mD
is Cartier and
\sigma_{i}\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(mD)),1\leq i\leq k
are global sections. If
\theta:\mathcal{O}(mD)|_{U}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{U}
is a trivialization, then set

\varphi=\frac{1}{2m}\log(\sum_{1\leq i\leq k}|\theta(\sigma_{i})|^{2})

as the local weight of
h
(see Remark
3.8
).

We call the metrics defined above the metrics defined by global sections.

For an effective Cartier divisor
D
, choose
\sigma=1\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))
, then the metric as (
3.2.1
) is denoted by
\hbar_{D}
.

In general, let
D
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor. If
D=\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}r_{i}D_{i}-\sum_{1\leq j\leq m}s_{j}B_{j}
is a decomposition such that
r_{i},s_{j}\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
and
D_{i},B_{j}
are effective Cartier divisors, then set

(3.2.2)

\hbar_{D}\coloneqq\left(\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}\hbar_{D_{i}}^{r_{i}}\right)\cdot\left(\prod_{1\leq j\leq m}(\hbar^{-1}_{B_{j}})^{s_{j}}\right),

where
\hbar^{-1}_{B_{j}}
is the dual metric for
\mathcal{O}(-B_{j})
. Note that
\hbar_{D}
is a metric as the corresponding weight still satisfies the
L^{1}_{\rm loc}
-property.

The above
\hbar_{D}
is independent of the choice of decompositions of
D
. Indeed, suppose that
mD
is Cartier for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
\theta:\mathcal{O}(mD)|_{U}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{U}
is a trivialization. Then for any
x\in U
and
s\in\mathcal{O}(mD)_{x}
, we always have
\|s\|^{2}_{\hbar_{D}^{m}}=|\theta(s)|^{2}/|\theta(1)|^{2}
.

Let
Z\subset Y
be a normal complex subspace such that
Z\not\subset\operatorname{Supp}D
, then
D|_{Z}
is still a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor. Let
\hbar_{D}|_{Z}
be the pull-back of
\hbar_{D}
to
Z
, then

(3.2.3)

\hbar_{D}|_{Z}=\hbar_{D|_{Z}}.

Proposition 3.11
.

Suppose that
h_{B},h_{D}
are metrics defined by global sections for
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisors
B,D
respectively. Then
h_{B}h_{D}
is a metric for
B+D
which is also defined by global sections.

Proof.

Suppose that
h_{B}
(resp.
h_{D}
) is defined by global sections of
\mathcal{O}(rB)
(resp.
\mathcal{O}(sD)
), and
\theta_{rB}:\mathcal{O}(rB)|_{U}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{U}
(resp.
\theta_{sD}:\mathcal{O}(sD)|_{U}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{U}
) is a trivialization. Then the local weights are

\varphi_{B}=\frac{1}{2r}(\log\sum_{i=1}^{m_{B}}|\theta_{rB}(\sigma_{i})|^{2}),\quad\varphi_{D}=\frac{1}{2s}(\log\sum_{j=1}^{m_{D}}|\theta_{sD}(\tau_{j})|^{2}),

where
\sigma_{i}\in H^{0}(U,\mathcal{O}(rB)),\tau_{j}\in H^{0}(U,\mathcal{O}(sD))
. The local weight for
B+D
is

\begin{split}\varphi_{B}+\varphi_{D}&=\frac{1}{2rs}\log\left((\sum_{i=1}^{m_{B}}|\theta_{rB}(\sigma_{i})|^{2})^{s}\cdot(\sum_{j=1}^{m_{D}}|\theta_{sD}(\tau_{j})|^{2})^{r}\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{2rs}\log\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},\cdots,i_{s}\\
j_{1}\cdots j_{r}\end{subarray}}|\theta_{rB}(\sigma_{i_{1}})\cdots\theta_{rB}(\sigma_{i_{s}})\cdot\theta_{sD}(\tau_{j_{1}})\cdots\theta_{sD}(\tau_{j_{r}})|^{2}\right).\end{split}

By
\sigma_{i_{1}}\cdots\sigma_{i_{s}}\cdot\tau_{j_{1}}\cdots\tau_{j_{r}}\in H^{0}(U,\mathcal{O}(rs(B+D)))
, the claim follows.
∎

3.3.
Push-forward metrics defined by global sections

Let
f:X^{\prime}\to X
be a proper modification between normal complex spaces and
D
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor on
X^{\prime}
. Suppose that
B=f_{*}D
is still a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor on
X^{\prime}
. Suppose that
D
admits a metric
h^{\prime}
. In general, there is no push-forward metric
f_{*}h^{\prime}
of
h^{\prime}
. However, if
h^{\prime}
is given by global sections, then we can naturally define
f_{*}h^{\prime}
as follows.

Recall that for a
\sigma\in\mathscr{M}_{X^{\prime}}(X^{\prime})
,
f_{*}\sigma
is defined as the meromorphic extension of
\sigma|_{X-f(\operatorname{Exc}(f))}
on
X
(see
[
Dem12
, Chapter II (10.2)]
). This is possible as
\operatorname{codim}_{X}f(\operatorname{Exc}(f))\geq 2
. Hence
f_{*}\sigma\in\mathscr{M}_{X}(X)
.

Suppose that
mD
and
mB
are Cartier divisors. If
\sigma\in H^{0}(X^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}(mD))
, that is,
\sigma\in\mathscr{M}_{X^{\prime}}(X^{\prime})
such that
\operatorname{div}(\sigma)+mD\geq 0
, then
f_{*}\sigma\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(mB))
because

\operatorname{div}(f_{*}\sigma)+mB=f_{*}\operatorname{div}(\sigma)+f_{*}(mD)\geq 0.

Therefore, if
h^{\prime}
is the metric of
D
defined by global sections as (
3.2.1
), then the push-forwards of these sections also define a metric
h\coloneqq f_{*}h^{\prime}
for
B
. We call this metric the push-forward of
h^{\prime}
.

Lemma 3.12
.

Under the above notation and assumptions, let
D+F=f^{*}B
with
F
an
f
-exceptional divisor (
F
may not be effective). Then
f^{*}h
is a metric for
D+F
such that

(3.3.1)

h^{\prime}\cdot\hbar_{F}=f^{*}h,

where
\hbar_{F}
is the metric defined as (
3.2.2
).

Proof.

Suppose that
h^{\prime}
is defined by sections
\sigma_{i}\in H^{0}(X^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}(mD)),i=1,\cdots,k
, then
\sigma_{i}=f^{*}(f_{*}\sigma_{i})\in H^{0}(X^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}(mD+mF)),i=1,\cdots,k
.

Let
\theta:\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}(mD)|_{U}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{U},\theta_{F}:\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}(mF)|_{U}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{U}
be trivializations on
U\subset X^{\prime}
, and
\theta_{B}:\mathcal{O}_{X}(mB)|_{V}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{V}
be a trivialization on
V\subset X
such that
U\subset f^{-1}(V)
and
f^{*}\theta_{B}=\theta\theta_{F}
. Then
h^{\prime}\cdot\hbar_{F}
has the local weight

\frac{1}{2m}(\log\sum_{i}|\theta(\sigma_{i})|^{2}+\log|\theta_{F}(1)|^{2})=\frac{1}{2m}\log(\sum_{i}|\theta\theta_{F}(\sigma_{i})|^{2}),

where
\sigma_{i}
in
\theta(\sigma_{i})
is a section with respect to the divisor
mD
while
\sigma_{i}
in
\theta\theta_{F}(\sigma_{i})
is a section with respect to the divisor
mD+mF
.

The local weight for
h
is

\frac{1}{2m}\log(\sum_{i}|\theta_{B}(f_{*}\sigma_{i})|^{2}).

Thus
f^{*}h
has the local weight

f^{*}\left(\frac{1}{2m}\log(\sum_{i}|\theta_{B}(f_{*}\sigma_{i})|^{2})\right)

under the trivialization
f^{*}\theta_{B}
(see (
3.1.1
)). The claim follows from

f^{*}(\theta_{B}(f_{*}\sigma_{i}))=(f^{*}\theta_{B})(f^{*}(f_{*}\sigma_{i}))=(\theta\theta_{F})(\sigma_{i}).

∎

4.1.

Let

f:X^{\prime}\to X/\Delta

is a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on

is a nef and big
\mathbb{Q}

\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\textstyle{X^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

As the Picard group

on

The following lemma is a direct consequence of the negativity lemma (see

Lemma 4.1
.

Let

f^{*}M=M^{\prime}+\Upxi,

Lemma 4.2
.

Under the above notation and assumptions. For a g-pair

be the fiber over

on

\mathbb{Q}

(2)

(3)

for each

, there exists an ample
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor

on

such that

M^{\prime}+\frac{1}{k}E\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}g^{*}H_{k}+\frac{1}{k}F,

(4)

\tilde{X}_{0}
is not a component of

.

Proof.

There is a nef and big
/\Delta
divisor
H^{\prime}
on a smooth complex space
Z
such that

. Then for any

where

is ample and
B\geq 0
(see
[

). Let

, then

(4.1.2)

g^{*}B-c(\pi\circ f)^{*}(0)=g^{*}B-c(\tilde{X}_{0}+E_{f})

does not contain
\tilde{X}_{0}
in its support, where

is some

is viewed as a divisor and

, where
\varphi_{k},\varphi_{E},\varphi_{F},\varphi_{\Upxi}
are local weights for
h_{k},\hbar_{E},\hbar_{F},\hbar_{\Upxi}
respectively, and

(4)

f_{0}^{*}\varphi_{k,0}+\frac{1}{k}\varphi_{E_{0}}\approx\frac{1}{k}\varphi_{F_{0}}+\varphi_{\Upxi_{0}}
, where
\varphi_{k,0},\varphi_{E_{0}},\varphi_{F_{0}},\varphi_{\Upxi_{0}}
are local weights for
h_{k}|_{X_{0}},\hbar_{E_{0}},\hbar_{F_{0}},\hbar_{\Upxi_{0}}
respectively.

Proof.

As the metric defined by global sections always has non-negative curvature current, we have (1).

For (2), by Lemma
4.3
,
h^{\prime}_{k}|_{\tilde{X}_{0}}\not\equiv\infty
. By

f^{*}M=f^{*}(f_{*}(M^{\prime}+\frac{1}{k}E))=(M^{\prime}+\frac{1}{k}E)+\Upxi-\frac{1}{k}E,

Lemma
3.12
implies
f^{*}h_{k}=h_{k}^{\prime}\cdot\hbar_{\Upxi-\frac{1}{k}E}
, which is the same as

(4.2.1)

f^{*}h_{k}\cdot\hbar_{E}^{\frac{1}{k}}=h_{k}^{\prime}\cdot\hbar_{\Upxi}.

As
E,\Upxi
are
f
-exceptional divisors,
h_{E}|_{\tilde{X}_{0}}\not\equiv\infty,h_{\Upxi}|_{\tilde{X}_{0}}\not\equiv\infty
. Hence
f^{*}h_{k}|_{\tilde{X}_{0}}\not\equiv\infty
. This implies
h_{k}|_{\tilde{X}_{0}}\not\equiv\infty
as
f_{0}
is a proper modification.

(3) follows from (
4.2.1
) and Lemma
4.3
(2).

For (4), restricting (
4.2.1
) to
\tilde{X}_{0}
, we have

f^{*}h_{k}|_{\tilde{X}_{0}}\cdot\hbar_{E}^{\frac{1}{k}}|_{\tilde{X}_{0}}=h_{k}^{\prime}|_{\tilde{X}_{0}}\cdot\hbar_{\Upxi}|_{\tilde{X}_{0}}.

Moreover,
\hbar_{E}|_{\tilde{X}_{0}}=\hbar_{E_{0}},\hbar_{\Upxi}|_{\tilde{X}_{0}}=\hbar_{\Upxi_{0}}
. By
f^{*}h_{k}|_{\tilde{X}_{0}}=f_{0}^{*}(h_{k}|_{X_{0}})
, we have

f_{0}^{*}(h_{k}|_{X_{0}})\cdot\hbar_{E_{0}}^{\frac{1}{k}}=h_{k}^{\prime}|_{\tilde{X}_{0}}\cdot\hbar_{\Upxi_{0}}.

The result follows from Lemma
4.3
(3).
∎

Remark 4.5
.

In application, it is possible to work with
h_{\min}
, the metric with minimal singularities for
M
(which also makes sense on complex spaces under suitable modifications). Comparing with
h_{k}
, we have
\varphi_{\min}\preceq\varphi_{k}
where
\varphi_{\min}
is the local weight for
h_{\min}
. In the following, we adopt the more direct approach to work with

5.1.
Multiplier ideal sheaves

Recall that for a complex manifold
X
and a Cartier divisor
L
with a metric
h
. If the local weight of
h
is
\varphi
, then the multiplier ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}(h)
is the sheaf of germs of holomorphic functions
\alpha
such that
|\alpha|^{2}e^{-2\varphi}
is locally integrable. Therefore,

\begin{split}H^{0}(U,\mathcal{I}(h)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(L))=\{&s\in H^{0}(U,\mathcal{O}_{X}(L))\mid|\theta(s)|^{2}e^{-2\varphi}\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(U),\\
&\text{where~}\theta\text{~is a trivialization of~}\mathcal{O}_{X}(L)\}.\end{split}

We have the following observation.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
X
be a complex manifold. Let
L
be a Cartier divisor with a metric
h
and
E
be a Cartier divisor with a metric
\hbar_{E}
. If
s\in\mathscr{M}_{X}(X)
is a global section of both
L
and
L+E
(i.e.
\operatorname{div}(s)+L\geq 0,\operatorname{div}(s)+L+E\geq 0
), then
s\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(L)\otimes\mathcal{I}(h))
iff
s\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(L+E)\otimes\mathcal{I}(h\hbar_{E}))
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\theta_{L}:\mathcal{O}(L)|_{U}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{U}
is a trivialization of
\mathcal{O}(L)
on
U\subset X
. Shrinking
U
, let
\sigma_{E}\in\mathscr{M}_{X}(U)
be a local equation of
E
. There is a trivialization
\theta_{E}:\mathcal{O}(E)|_{U}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{U}
by multiplying
\sigma_{E}
. Therefore, locally at a point
x\in U
,

\int|\theta_{L}(s)|^{2}e^{-2\varphi_{h}}~dV_{X}=\int|\theta_{L}\theta_{E}(s)|^{2}e^{-2\varphi_{h}}e^{-2\log|\sigma_{E}|}~dV_{X},

where
\varphi_{h}
is the local weight of
h
corresponding to
\theta_{L}
.
∎

5.2.
Extension theorem for twisted pairs

We need a modification of
[
Tak07
, Theorem 3.1]
(see Lemma
5.2
). In fact, we only work in the setting of
[
Tak07
, Theorem 3.1]
instead of generalizing this result (see Remark
5.3
).

Let
\mu:W\to X
be a log resolution and
\tau:W\to X\to\Delta
be the corresponding morphism. Let
N
be a Cartier divisor on
W
and
\tilde{h}_{N}
be a metric for
N
. Suppose that
X_{0}
is a normal complex subspace and
Y_{0}\subset\tau^{-1}(0)
is the strict transform of
X_{0}
. Taking a higher log resolution, we can assume that
Y_{0}
is a smooth divisor. Viewing
X_{0}
as an effective Cartier divisor, we have
\mu^{*}X_{0}=Y_{0}+\Theta
with
\Theta\geq 0
a
\mu
-exceptional divisor. In particular,
\Theta_{0}\coloneqq\Theta|_{Y_{0}}\geq 0
. Restricting

K_{W}+Y_{0}+\Theta\sim K_{W}

to
Y_{0}
and by the adjunction formula, we have

(5.2.1)

K_{Y_{0}}+\Theta_{0}\sim K_{W}|_{Y_{0}}.

Therefore, a section of
\mathcal{O}(mK_{W}|_{Y_{0}})
corresponds to a section of
\mathcal{O}(mK_{Y_{0}}+m\Theta_{0})
, and its integrability with respect to the metric
\hbar_{m\Theta_{0}}
makes sense (see Remark
3.9
).

We explain the natural isomorphism

\tilde{\nu}:\mathcal{O}(K_{Y_{0}}+\Theta_{0})\simeq\mathcal{O}(K_{W}|_{Y_{0}})

following
[
Tak07
, Page 5-6]
. The pull-back of the coordinate function
t
on
\Delta
is regarded as a holomorphic function on
W
and it is still denoted by
t
. Suppose that
w_{1},\ldots,w_{n-1},y_{0}
are local coordinates with
Y_{0}=\{y_{0}=0\}
. Then
t=\xi y_{0}
with
\Theta=\{\xi=0\}
. For a sufficiently small open set
U\subset Y_{0}
, let

\tau\omega_{Y_{0}}\coloneqq\tau dw_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dw_{n-1}\in H^{0}(U,\mathcal{O}(K_{Y_{0}}+\Theta_{0})),

where
\tau\in H^{0}(U,\mathcal{O}(\Theta_{0}))
is a section. Let
\tilde{\tau}
be a local meromorphic extension of
\tau
near
U
such that
\operatorname{div}(\tilde{\tau})+\Theta\geq 0
. Then
\tilde{\tau}dw_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dw_{n-1}
is a local extension of
\tau\omega_{Y_{0}}
. By abuse of notation,
\omega_{Y_{0}}
is still used to denote a local extension of
\omega_{Y_{0}}
to
W
. In fact, in what follows, we always restrict the above forms to
Y_{0}
, hence the results are independent of the choice of extensions.

Then

(5.2.2)

\tilde{\nu}:\tau\omega_{Y_{0}}\mapsto(\tilde{\tau}\omega_{Y_{0}}\wedge dt)|_{Y_{0}}.

Note that this is well-defined because
dt
is independent of the choices of the open set
U
. In order to write
(\omega_{Y_{0}}\wedge dt)|_{Y_{0}}
in terms of
(dw_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dw_{n-1}\wedge dy_{0})|_{Y_{0}}
, note that

dt=\xi dy_{0}+y_{0}d\xi

and
dw_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dw_{n-1}\wedge d\xi=0
. Thus

(5.2.3)

\tilde{\nu}:\tau\omega_{Y_{0}}\mapsto(\tilde{\tau}\xi\omega_{Y_{0}}\wedge dy_{0})|_{Y_{0}}.

Because
\Theta=\{\xi=0\}
and
\operatorname{div}(\tilde{\tau})+\Theta\geq 0
,
\tilde{\tau}\xi
is holomorphic. Hence
(\tilde{\tau}\xi\omega_{Y_{0}}\wedge dy_{0})|_{Y_{0}}\in H^{0}(U,\mathcal{O}(K_{W}|_{Y_{0}}))
. From such local expressions,
\tilde{\nu}
is seen to be an isomorphism. Moreover, the above discussion also gives a natural isomorphism

(5.2.4)

\mathcal{O}(mK_{Y_{0}}+m\Theta_{0})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{O}(mK_{W}|_{Y_{0}}).

Lemma 5.2
.

Under the above notation and assumptions. If
\tilde{h}_{N}|_{Y_{0}}
is well-defined, then for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, as long as
\tilde{h}_{N}\hbar_{m\Theta}
has non-negative curvature current, any section of

H^{0}(Y_{0},\mathcal{O}(mK_{W}|_{Y_{0}}+N|_{Y_{0}})\otimes\mathcal{I}(\tilde{h}_{N}|_{Y_{0}}\hbar_{m\Theta_{0}}))

extends over
W
.

Proof.

The argument is identically as that for
[
Pău07
, Theorem 1]
(a simplified and twisted version of
[
Siu02
]
). Therefore, we just sketch the argument.

We show that the naturally defined morphisms
p,\nu,q
(defined below) give the following commutative diagram

(5.2.5)

\textstyle{\mathcal{O}_{W}(mK_{W}+N)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{q}
\scriptstyle{p}
\textstyle{\mathcal{O}_{Y_{0}}(mK_{Y_{0}}+m\Theta_{0}+N|_{Y_{0}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\nu}
\textstyle{\mathcal{O}_{Y_{0}}(mK_{W}|_{Y_{0}}+N|_{Y_{0}}).}

It suffices to define the morphism when
N=0
. First,
p
is defined through

\mathcal{O}_{W}(mK_{W})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{O}_{W}(mK_{W}+mY_{0}+m\Theta_{0})\to\mathcal{O}_{Y_{0}}(mK_{Y_{0}}+m\Theta_{0}).

Suppose that locally
\mathcal{O}_{Y_{0}}(K_{Y_{0}})|_{U}=\omega_{Y_{0}}\mathcal{O}_{U}
, then the above is given in local expressions by

\alpha(\omega_{Y_{0}}\wedge dy_{0})^{\otimes m}\mapsto\frac{\alpha}{t^{m}}(\omega_{Y_{0}}\wedge dy_{0})^{\otimes m}\mapsto\frac{\alpha_{0}y_{0}^{m}}{t^{m}}\omega_{Y_{0}}^{\otimes m},

where
\alpha
is a local section of
\mathcal{O}_{W}
and
\alpha_{0}=\alpha|_{Y_{0}}
.

The
\nu
is given as (
5.2.4
) and the
q
is the natural restriction map. To show
q=\nu\circ p
, set
\xi_{0}\coloneqq\xi|_{Y_{0}}
. As
t=\xi y_{0}
, we have

\frac{\alpha_{0}y_{0}^{m}}{t^{m}}\omega_{Y_{0}}^{\otimes m}=\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\xi_{0}^{m}}\omega_{Y_{0}}^{\otimes m}.

Thus

p:\alpha(\omega_{Y_{0}}\wedge dy_{0})^{\otimes m}\mapsto\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\xi_{0}^{m}}\omega_{Y_{0}}^{\otimes m}.

By
dt=\xi dy_{0}+y_{0}d\xi
, we have

(5.2.6)

\omega_{Y_{0}}\wedge dy_{0}=\frac{1}{\xi}\omega_{Y_{0}}\wedge dt.

Thus

q:\alpha(\omega_{Y_{0}}\wedge dy_{0})^{\otimes m}\mapsto\alpha_{0}(\frac{1}{\xi_{0}}(\omega_{Y_{0}}\wedge dt)|_{Y_{0}})^{\otimes m}.

By (
5.2.2
),
\nu:\mathcal{O}(mK_{Y_{0}}+m\Theta_{0})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{O}(mK_{W}|_{Y_{0}})
is given by

\nu:\tau_{0}\omega_{Y_{0}}^{\otimes m}\mapsto\tilde{\tau}_{0}(\omega_{Y_{0}}\wedge dt)^{\otimes m}|_{Y_{0}},

where
\tau_{0}\in H^{0}(U,\mathcal{O}(m\Theta_{0}))
and
\tilde{\tau}_{0}
is a meromorphic extension of
\tau_{0}
. Take
\tau_{0}=\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\xi_{0}^{m}}
, we have
q=\nu\circ p
.

We claim that any section of

H^{0}(Y_{0},\mathcal{O}(mK_{Y_{0}}+(N|_{Y_{0}}+m\Theta_{0}))\otimes\mathcal{I}(\tilde{h}_{N}|_{Y_{0}}h_{m\Theta_{0}}))

extends over
W
(i.e. such section has preimage in
H^{0}(W,\mathcal{O}(mK_{W}+N))
). To show this, the argument of
[
Pău07
, Theorem 1]
goes through with the version of the Ohsawa-Takegoshi extension theorem in
[
Pău07
, Theorem 2.1]
replaced by
[
Tak07
, Lemma 3.6]
.

Now, as
\nu
induces the isomorphism

\begin{split}&H^{0}(Y_{0},\mathcal{O}(mK_{Y_{0}}+(N|_{Y_{0}}+m\Theta_{0}))\otimes\mathcal{I}(\tilde{h}_{N}|_{Y_{0}}\hbar_{m\Theta_{0}}))\\
\xrightarrow{\sim}&H^{0}(Y_{0},\mathcal{O}(mK_{W}|_{Y_{0}}+(N|_{Y_{0}}))\otimes\mathcal{I}(\tilde{h}_{N}|_{Y_{0}}\hbar_{m\Theta_{0}})),\end{split}

the commutativity of the diagram (
5.2.5
) gives the desired result.
∎

5.3.
The construction of
\mathcal{G}_{m}(h)

Let
X
be a complex space with canonical singularities. Note that the canonical divisor
K_{X}
is a Weil divisor which may not be Cartier. There exists
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\ell K_{X}
is Cartier. Let
\mu:W\to X
be a resolution. Let
w_{i},i=1\ldots,n
be local coordinates on
W
and
z_{i},i=1\ldots,n
be local coordinates on
X_{\rm reg}
. There is a multiple-valued meromorphic function
J(\mu)
such that

(d\mu^{*}z_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge d\mu^{*}z_{n})^{\otimes\ell}=J(\mu)^{\ell}(dw_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dw_{n})^{\otimes\ell},

where
J(\mu)^{\ell}
is a (single-valued) meromorphic function. In what follows, over possibly singular locus, we are only concerned with
|J(\mu)|
, hence the multi-valuedness of
J(\mu)
will not cause any problem.

If we write
E=\mu^{*}K_{X}-K_{W}
with
E
the
\mu
-exceptional
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor, then
J(\mu)
is the local equation of
-E
(up to multiply a nowhere vanishing function). As
X
has canonical singularities,
E\leq 0
and
J(\mu)
is a multiple-valued holomorphic function.

Let
L
be a Cartier divisor. As
X
has canonical singularities, for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
(
mK_{X}
may not be Cartier), there is a natural pull-back

\mu^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(mK_{X})\to\mathcal{O}_{W}(mK_{W})

which induces the natural map

\mu^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(mK_{X}+L)\to\mathcal{O}_{W}(mK_{W}+\mu^{*}L).

To be precise, suppose that on an open set
U\subset X_{\operatorname{reg}}
, we have

s\in H^{0}(U,\mathcal{O}_{X}(mK_{X}+L))

such that

s=\alpha(dz_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dz_{n})^{\otimes m}

with
\alpha\in H^{0}(U,\mathcal{O}(L))
. Let

d\mu^{*}z_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge d\mu^{*}z_{n}=J(\mu)dw_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dw_{n}.

As
U
is smooth,
J(\mu)
is a single-valued holomorphic function on
U
. Then

(5.3.1)

\mu^{*}s=\mu^{*}\alpha\cdot J(\mu)^{m}(dw_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge w_{n})^{\otimes m},

where
\mu^{*}\alpha\cdot J(\mu)^{m}\in H^{0}(\mu^{-1}(U),\mathcal{O}_{W}(\mu^{*}L))
. As
E\leq 0
,

\mu^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(mK_{X}+L)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{W}(mK_{W}+\mu^{*}L),

and thus
\mu^{*}\alpha\cdot J(\mu)^{m}(dw_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge w_{n})^{\otimes m}
extends over
W
.

Let
X
be a normal complex space and
L
be a Cartier divisor with a metric
h
. Assume that
\varphi
is a local weight of
h
under some trivialization. The following type of ideal sheaves generalizes multiplier ideal sheaves.

Definition 5.4

(Definition of
\mathcal{G}_{m}(h)
)
.

Under the above notation and assumptions. Let
\mathcal{G}_{m}(h)
be the sheaf of germs of holomorphic functions such that for any
x\in X
,

\begin{split}\mathcal{G}_{m}(h)_{x}=\{&\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{X,x}\mid|\mu^{*}\alpha|^{2}|J(\mu)|^{2m}e^{-2\mu^{*}\varphi}\in L_{\rm loc}^{1}(\mu^{-1}(U)),\\
&\text{where~}\mu:W\to X\text{~is a resolution and~}U\text{~is a neighborhood of~}x\}.\end{split}

Remark 5.5
.

(1) Because of (
3.1.2
), the above definition is independent of the choice of trivializations. However,
\alpha
may depend on
\mu
. (2) For technical reasons (see Lemma
5.12
), we require
\mu
to be a (global) resolution of
X
instead of a neighborhood of
x
.

When
X
is smooth, we certainly have
\mathcal{G}_{m}(h)\supset\mathcal{I}(h)
. Furthermore, if
m=1
, then
\mathcal{G}_{1}(h)=\mathcal{I}(h)
by the change-of-variables formula (see (
5.4.2
)). The following example shows that the inclusion
\mathcal{G}_{m}(h)\supset\mathcal{I}(h)
may be strict.

Example 5.6
.

Let
x,y
be the coordinates of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Let
h=e^{-\varphi}
with
\varphi\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\log(|x|^{4}+|y|^{4})
be the metric for the trivial divisor. Then
1\not\in\mathcal{I}(h)
. We claim that
1\in\mathcal{G}_{2}(h)
. In fact, let
\mu:W\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
be the blow-up of the origin. Then
W=\{(x,y)\times[u:v]\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\mid xv=yu\}
. It is covered by two pieces
U_{1}=\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\mid xz=y)\}
and
U_{2}=\{(x,y,w)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\mid x=yw)\}
. By the symmetry of
\varphi
, it suffices to consider the
L_{\rm loc}^{1}
-property on one piece, say
U_{1}
. Choose
x,z
as local coordinates on
U_{1}
. By

, we have

xdx\wedge dz=d\mu^{*}x\wedge d\mu^{*}y.

Thus
J(\mu)=x
. Then locally on
U_{1}
,

\begin{split}&\int 1\cdot|J(\mu)|^{4}e^{-2\mu^{*}\varphi}~dV_{W}\\
=&\int\frac{|x|^{4}}{|x|^{4}+|y|^{4}}~dV_{W}=\int\frac{1}{1+|z|^{4}}~dV_{W}<\infty.\end{split}

It is natural to ask the following question:

Question 5.7

To study the above question, it seems that the

Let
\mathcal{F}\coloneqq\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(mK_{X}+(m-1)X_{t}+L)
and
\mathcal{G}\coloneqq\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(mK_{X}+mX_{t}+L)
, then

\begin{split}&\operatorname{Ker}(\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(mK_{X}+mX_{t}+L)\otimes\mathbb{C}(t)\to H^{0}(X_{t},\mathcal{O}_{X_{t}}(mK_{X_{t}}+L_{t})))\\
\simeq&(\mathcal{F}_{t}+m_{t}\mathcal{G}_{t})/m\mathcal{G}_{t}.\end{split}

We claim that
(\mathcal{F}_{t}+m_{t}\mathcal{G}_{t})/m_{t}\mathcal{G}_{t}=0
. In fact, as
m_{t}=(z-t)\mathcal{O}_{\Delta,t}
,
\pi^{*}(z-t)
is the defining equation of
X_{t}
, we have
\mathcal{F}_{t}\subset m_{t}\mathcal{G}_{t}
. Because
X_{t}\sim 0
, we have
\mathcal{O}_{X}(mK_{X}+(m-1)X_{t}+L)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X}(mK_{X}+L)
. Thus (
5.4.8
) is an isomorphism.

Note that
\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(mK_{X}+L)
is a coherent sheaf. By the upper semi-continuity of

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(mK_{X}+L)\otimes\mathbb{C}(t))

and the isomorphism (
5.4.8
),
h^{0}(X_{t},\mathcal{O}_{X_{t}}(mK_{X_{t}}+L_{t}))
is upper semi-continuous. Fix a
t_{0}\in\Delta
, any section of
H^{0}(X_{t_{0}},\mathcal{O}_{X_{t_{0}}}(mK_{X_{t_{0}}}+L_{t_{0}}))
extends over
X
by Theorem
1.2
. Hence

h^{0}(X_{t_{0}},\mathcal{O}_{X_{t_{0}}}(mK_{X_{t_{0}}}+L_{t_{0}}))\leq h^{0}(X_{t},\mathcal{O}_{X_{t}}(mK_{X_{t}}+L_{t}))

for a general
t\in\Delta
. Thus,
h^{0}(X_{t},\mathcal{O}_{X_{t}}(mK_{X_{t}}+L_{t}))
must be a constant for all
t\in\Delta
.
∎

5.5.
Further discussions

The following example shows that nefness of
M
along does not guarantee the invariance of plurigenera.

Example 5.17
.

Let
A/\mathbb{C}
be an abelian variety and
A^{\vee}
be its dual abelian variety. If
\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(A)
is the identity component of the Picard variety of
A
, then
\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(A)=A^{\vee}
. Let
\mathcal{P}
be the Poincaré bundle on
A\times A^{\vee}
. Suppose that
0\in A^{\vee}
corresponds to
\mathcal{P}_{0}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{A}
. Let
0\in\Delta\subset A^{\vee}
be a disc containing
0
. Let
X=A\times\Delta
and
\mathcal{P}_{\Delta}\coloneqq\mathcal{P}|_{X}
be a line bundle on
X
.
\mathcal{P}_{\Delta}
is nef over
\Delta
as
\mathcal{P}_{t}
is numerically trivial for each
t\in\Delta
. Moreover,
\mathcal{O}(K_{X})=\mathcal{O}_{X}
. Note that for an abelian variety, a line bundle
\mathcal{L}\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(A)
has global sections if and only if
\mathcal{L}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{A}
(see
[
Mum70
, Page 76, (vii)]
). Therefore, for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

h^{0}(X_{X_{t}},\mathcal{O}_{X_{t}}(mK_{X_{t}})\otimes\mathcal{P}_{\Delta}^{\otimes m}|_{X_{t}})=\begin{cases}1,&\text{ if~ }t=0,\\
0,&\text{ if~ }t\in\Delta-\{0\}.\end{cases}

Next, recall that for a non-smooth family of varieties, we have

Theorem 5.18

(
[
Tak07
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

Let
\pi:X\to C
be a proper surjective algebraic morphism with connected fibers from a complex variety
X
to a smooth curve
C
. Assume that every fiber
X_{t}=\pi^{-1}(t)
has only canonical singularities. Then
h^{0}(X_{t},\mathcal{O}_{X_{t}}(mK_{X_{t}}))
is independent of
t\in C
for any positive integer
m
.

Such result has been established by
[
Kaw99a
, Theorem 6]
under the additional assumption that each fiber is of general type. On the other hand, for klt singularities, local sections of fibers may not lift to global sections (see
[
Kaw99b
, Example 4.3]
). For g-pairs, the additional nef part introduces the singularities even if each
X_{t}
is smooth.

The above discussions show that both assumptions on the abundant nef parts and the g-canonical singularities are indispensable for Theorem
1.2

and Corollary
1.3
.

Some aspects of anisotropic curvature flow of planar partitions
Giovanni Bellettini, Shokhrukh Yu. Kholmatov

Abstract
. We consider the geometric evolution of a network in the plane, flowing by anisotropic curvature. We discuss local existence of a classical solution in the presence of several smooth anisotropies. Next, we discuss some aspects of the polycrystalline case.

2.1.

We denote by

We let

The dual of

\phi^{o}(\xi)=\max\limits_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\,\,\phi(\eta)=1}\,\,\xi\cdot\eta,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

\mathbb{R}^{2}
. We do not use different symbols for the domain of
\phi
and
\phi^{o}
.
Notice that
\phi^{oo}=\phi.

B_{\phi}
is sometimes called Wulff shape, and
B_{\phi^{o}}
Frank
diagram.

We say that
\phi

if
\phi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\})

\nabla^{2}\phi(\nu)\tau\cdot\tau\geq\bar{c}>0

with
\tau\cdot\nu=0

2.3.
Tangential divergence of a vector field

The
tangential divergence
of a vector field
g\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2})

over an embedded Lipschitz curve
\Sigma
is defined as

\mathrm{div}_{\Sigma}g(q)=\nabla g(q)\tau_{\Sigma}(q)\cdot\tau_{\Sigma}(q),\quad\text{$\mathcal{H}^{1}$-a.e. $q\in\Sigma$}.

When the curve is
C^{1}
, this equality holds at every point of
\Sigma.

Remark 2.2
.

When we will define Cahn-Hoffman vector fields, we consider the tangential divergence of a Lipschitz vector field
N_{\phi}
defined only along a Lipschitz curve
\Sigma.
In this case, we extend
N_{\phi}
to a tubular neighborhood of
\Sigma
constant along the vector
N_{\phi}(q)
for
q\in\Sigma,
i.e., if
z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
z=q+\lambda N_{\phi}(q)
for a unique
q\in\Sigma
and sufficiently small
|\lambda|,
then we set
N_{\phi}(z):=N_{\phi}(q).

The tangential divergence can also be introduced using parametrizations. More precisely, if
\sigma\in{\rm Lip}([0,1];\mathbb{R}^{2})
is a regular parametrization of
\Sigma
and
g:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a Lipschitz vector field along
\Sigma
, i.e.,
g\circ\sigma\in{\rm Lip}([0,1];\mathbb{R}^{2})
, then

(2.2)

\mathrm{div}_{\Sigma}\,g(q)=\frac{[g\circ\sigma]^{\prime}(x)\cdot\sigma^{\prime}(x)}{|\sigma^{\prime}(x)|^{2}},\qquad q=\sigma(x)

at points of differentiability.
One can readily check that the tangential divergence is independent of the parametrization.

2.5.
Anisotropic energy of a network

Let
\{E_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be a Lipschitz partition of
\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Let
\Phi:=\{\phi_{ij}\}
be a collection of anisotropies in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that each
\phi_{ij}
is associated to
\Sigma_{ij}.
Notice that
\phi_{ij}=\phi_{ji}
and
\Sigma_{ij}=\Sigma_{ji}.
The
\Phi
-length
of
\Sigma:=\bigcup_{ij}\Sigma_{ij}
in an open set
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
is defined as

(2.3)

\ell_{\Phi}(\Sigma;U):=\sum\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\,\int_{U\cap\Sigma_{ij}}\phi_{ij}^{o}(\nu_{\Sigma_{ij}})d\mathcal{H}^{1}.

By assumption, each
\Sigma_{ij}
is either empty, or a finite set of points or a Lipschitz curve with boundary and therefore,
\ell(\Sigma;U)<+\infty
for any bounded open set
U.
We also set

\ell_{\Phi}(\Sigma):=\ell_{\Phi}(\Sigma;\mathbb{R}^{2})

provided that
\Sigma
is bounded.

Remark 2.3
.

We assume

(2.4)

\phi_{ij}^{o}+\phi_{jk}^{o}\geq\phi_{ik}^{o},

which is important since the invalidity of (
2.4
) yields local instabilities. Indeed, in this situation, a creation of a very thin new phase along the interfaces with large surface tensions would decrease the length.
In the proof of Theorem
3.9
we do not use (
2.4
) because of our assumptions on the shape of admissible networks (we do not allow creation of new phases).

)), to prove the short-time existence of a smooth geometric flow we only need to establish short-time existence of a special geometric flow starting from
\Sigma^{0}.

Let us consider a special geometric flow
u(t)
and the evolution of some triple junction

q(t):=u^{j_{1}}(t,y_{1})=u^{j_{2}}(t,y_{2})=u^{j_{3}}(t,y_{3})

for some
(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})\in\{0,1\}^{3}
and for all
t\in(0,1)
. Since all
u^{j}\in C^{1,2}((0,T)\times[0,1];\mathbb{R}^{2}),
we have

(3.7)

q_{t}(t)=u_{t}^{j_{1}}(t,y_{1})=u_{t}^{j_{2}}(t,y_{2})=u_{t}^{j_{3}}(t,y_{3}).

Thus, inserting (
3.4
) in (
3.6
) at
q(t)
we get

(3.8)

\displaystyle\phi_{i}^{o}(\nu^{i})\big(\nabla^{2}\phi_{i}^{o}(\nu^{i})\tau^{i}\cdot\tau^{i}\big)\,\frac{u_{xx}^{i}(t,y)}{|u_{x}^{i}(t,y)|^{3}}=\phi_{j}^{o}(\nu^{j})\big(\nabla^{2}\phi_{j}^{o}(\nu^{j})\tau^{j}\cdot\tau^{j}\big)\,\frac{u_{xx}^{j}(t,y)}{|u_{x}^{j}(t,y)|^{3}}

for all
i,j\in\{j_{1},j_{2},j_{3}\}.
If
\Sigma(\cdot)
is smooth up to
t=0,
the second order compatibility condition (
3.8
) should be satisfied by the initial network
\Sigma^{0}.

Later in this section we show that if
\Sigma^{0}
satisfies conditions
(
3.3
)
and (
3.8
), then there exist
T>0
and a special geometric anisotropic curvature flow
\{\Sigma(t)\}_{t\in[0,T]}
starting from
\Sigma^{0}.

3.4.
Role of the Herring condition

Let
\Sigma(t)
be a smooth geometric flow starting from a bounded network
\Sigma^{0}.
We claim that condition (
3.3
)
implies that the anisotropic length
\ell_{\Phi}(\Sigma(t))
is non-increasing in
t>0.
Indeed, without loss of generality, we may assume that
\Sigma(t)
is special (see Remark
3.6
). Hence, using the definition of
\ell_{\Phi},
integration by parts, (
3.7
) and (
3.5
) we get

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\,\ell_{\Phi}(\Sigma(t))=-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\phi_{j}^{o}(u_{x}^{\perp})}{|u_{x}^{j}|^{3}}\,\Big|\nabla^{2}\phi_{j}^{o}(u_{x}^{\perp})u_{xx}^{j}\Big|^{2}\,dx+\sum\limits_{q(t)\in J(t)}q_{t}(t)\cdot\sum\limits_{i=1}^{3}\nabla\phi_{j_{i}}^{o}(\nu^{j_{i}}(q(t))),

where
J(t)
is the set of all triple junctions,

q(t):=u^{j_{1}}(t,y_{1})=u^{j_{2}}(t,y_{2})=u^{j_{3}}(t,y_{3})

(
j_{i}
and
y_{i}
are different for different
q
).
Hence, condition (
3.3
)
implies that

\frac{d}{dt}\,\ell_{\Phi}(\Sigma(t))\leq 0,

i.e. the map
t\mapsto\ell_{\Phi}(\Sigma(t))
is non-increasing.

3.5.
Existence and uniqueness of special flows

In this section we prove the short-time existence and uniqueness of a smooth anisotropic curvature flow starting from a given network
\Sigma^{0}
satisfying
conditions (
3.3
) and (
3.8
) at triple junctions. For simplicity, we consider only
theta-shaped
networks, i.e. bounded
C^{2}
-networks consisting of only three embedded curves meeting at two triple junctions.

The main result of the section reads as follows.

Theorem 3.7

(Local existence and uniqueness)
.

Let
\Sigma^{0}
be a
C^{2+\alpha}
theta-shaped network satisfying at both triple junctions

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{3}\nabla\phi_{i}^{o}(\nu_{\Sigma^{0}}^{i})=0

and admitting a parametrization satisfying the second order compatibility condition (
3.8
). Then there exists a unique smooth geometric flow starting from
\Sigma^{0}.

As observed in Remark
3.6
, concerning existence we just need to prove the existence of a special flow. Then uniqueness follows from uniqueness of the special flow.

We postpone the proof after several auxiliary results. Before going further, we recall some notions related to parabolic Hölder spaces.
For a function
v:[0,T]\times[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
we let

[v]_{\alpha,x}:=\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T],\,x,y\in[0,1],\,x\neq y}\,\,\frac{|v(t,x)-v(t,y)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}},

[v]_{\alpha,t}:=\sup\limits_{s,t\in[0,T],\,s\neq t,\,x\in[0,1]}\,\,\frac{|v(s,x)-v(t,x)|}{|s-t|^{\alpha}}.

For
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
we denote by
C^{\frac{k+\alpha}{2},k+\alpha}([0,T]\times[0,1])
the space of all functions
v:[0,T]\times[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
whose continuous derivatives
\partial_{t}^{i}\partial_{x}^{j}v
exist for all
i,j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
2i+j\leq k
and satisfy

\|v\|_{C^{\frac{k+\alpha}{2},k+\alpha}([0,T]\times[0,1])}:=\sum\limits_{2i+j=0}^{k}\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T],\,x\in[0,1]}\,\,|\partial_{t}^{i}\partial_{x}^{j}v(t,x)|\\
+\sum\limits_{2i+j=k}[\partial_{t}^{i}\partial_{x}^{j}v]_{\alpha,x}+\sum\limits_{k+\alpha-2i-j<2}\,\,[\partial_{t}^{i}\partial_{x}^{j}v]_{\frac{k+\alpha-2i-j}{2},t}<+\infty.

The
C^{\frac{k+\alpha}{2},k+\alpha}
-norm of a
vector valued map
is the sum of the norms of its components.
We also adopt the following conventions:

•

whenever it is clear from the context, we set

C_{T}^{\frac{k+\alpha}{2},k+\alpha}:=C^{\frac{k+\alpha}{2},k+\alpha}([0,T]\times[0,1];\mathbb{R}^{2})

and

\|v\|_{C_{T}^{\frac{k+\alpha}{2},k+\alpha}}:=\|v\|_{C^{\frac{k+\alpha}{2},k+\alpha}([0,T]\times[0,1];\mathbb{R}^{2})};

•

for functions
v
depending only on one variable (space or time), we set

C^{k+\alpha}:=C^{k+\alpha}([0,1];\mathbb{R}^{2})\quad\text{and}\quad C_{T}^{\frac{k+\alpha}{2}}:=C^{\frac{k+\alpha}{2}}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{2})

and

\|v\|_{k+\alpha}:=\|v\|_{C^{k+\alpha}([0,1];\mathbb{R}^{2})}\quad\text{and}\quad\|v\|_{\frac{k+\alpha}{2},T}:=\|v\|_{C^{\frac{k+\alpha}{2}}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

By

\mathcal{X}_{n}^{k,\alpha}

we denote the set of
n
-tuples
\sigma:=(\sigma^{1},\ldots,\sigma^{n})\in[C^{k+\alpha}]^{n}
such that
\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\sigma^{i}([0,1])
is a network with only triple junctions.
Similarly, we denote by

\mathcal{X}_{n}^{k,\alpha,T}

the set of all
n
-tuples
\sigma:=(\sigma^{1},\ldots,\sigma^{n})\in\big[C_{T}^{\frac{k+\alpha}{2},k+\alpha}\big]^{n}
such that
\sigma(t)=(\sigma^{1}(t,\cdot),\ldots,\sigma^{n}(t,\cdot))\in\mathcal{X}_{n}^{k,\alpha}
for any
t\in[0,T]
.

We start with a general result related to the existence of special smooth geometric flows.

Assumption 3.8
.

(A)

\Theta:=(\theta_{i},\theta_{2},\theta_{3})
are positively one-homogeneous
C^{3+\alpha}
-functions in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
for some
\alpha\in(0,1],

(B)

\mathcal{B}:=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3})
are even
C^{2+\alpha}
-functions defined in a tubular neighborhood of the unit circle
\mathbb{S}^{1}
such that

0<m\leq\min_{\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{1}}\beta_{i}(\nu)\leq\max_{\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{1}}\beta_{i}(\nu)\leq\frac{1}{m},\quad i=1,2,3,

for some
m\in(0,1],

(C)

\mathcal{F}:=(f^{1},f^{2},f^{3})\in(C_{T}^{\alpha})^{3}.

The first variation formula (
3.1
) for length shows that for the anisotropic curvature flow we need to choose

\theta_{i}(\tau)=\phi_{i}^{o}(\tau^{\perp})\quad\text{and}\quad\beta_{i}(\tau)=\phi_{i}^{o}(\tau^{\perp})\nabla^{2}\phi_{i}^{o}(\tau^{\perp})\tau\cdot\tau\quad\text{and}\quad f_{i}=0.

Notice that in Assumption
3.8
(A) we are not assuming
\theta_{i}
to be even.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
k\geq 2,

\alpha\in(0,1]
and let
\sigma\in\mathcal{X}_{3}^{k,\alpha}
satisfy the compatibility conditions

(3.9)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\sigma^{1}(y)=\sigma^{2}(y)=\sigma^{3}(y),\\
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{3}\nabla\theta_{i}\Big(\tfrac{\sigma_{x}^{i}(y)}{|\sigma_{x}^{i}(y)|}\Big)=0,\\
\beta_{i}\Big(\tfrac{\sigma_{x}^{i}(y)}{|\sigma_{x}^{i}(y)|}\Big)\,\tfrac{\sigma_{xx}^{i}(y)}{|\sigma_{x}^{i}(y)|^{2}}=\beta_{j}\Big(\tfrac{\sigma_{x}^{j}(y)}{|\sigma_{x}^{j}(y)|}\Big)\,\tfrac{\sigma_{xx}^{j}(y)}{|\sigma_{x}^{j}(y)|^{2}},\quad i,j=1,2,3,\end{cases}

whenever
y=0
or
y=1
.
Let
\mathcal{F}
be such that

f^{i}(t,y)=f^{j}(t,y)\qquad\text{whenever $(t,y)\in[0,T]\times\{0,1\}.$}

Then there exist
T>0
and a unique flow
u(t)=(u^{1}(t,\cdot),u^{2}(t,\cdot),u^{3}(t,\cdot))\in\mathcal{X}_{3}^{k+2,\alpha,T}
of networks such that

(3.10)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u(0,x)=\sigma(x)&\text{for $x\in[0,1],$}\\
u^{1}(t,y)=u^{2}(t,y)=u^{3}(t,y)&\text{for $t\in[0,T]$ and $y\in\{0,1\},$}\\
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{3}\nabla\theta_{i}\Big(\tfrac{u_{x}^{i}(t,y)}{|u_{x}^{i}(t,y)|}\Big)=0,&\text{for $t\in[0,T]$ and $y\in\{0,1\},$}\\
u_{t}^{i}=\beta_{i}\Big(\tfrac{u_{x}^{i}}{|u_{x}^{i}|}\Big)\,\tfrac{u_{xx}^{i}}{|u_{x}^{i}|^{2}}+f^{i}&\text{in $[0,T]\times[0,1]$ and $i=1,2,3.$}\end{cases}

To prove the theorem we follow the arguments of
[
35
, Section 3]
. Namely, we linearize the problem near the initial and boundary data, then using the results of Solonnikov
[
44
]
we solve the linear problem, and finally, using careful Hölder estimates we reduce the problem to a Banach fixed point argument. Note that in
[
35
]
the authors consider just one anisotropy and networks having a single triple junction together with a Dirichlet boundary condition.

We divide the proof into several steps.

3.5.1.
Main functional spaces

For
T>0,

k\geq 2,

\alpha\in(0,1]
and
M>0
let

X_{M,T}^{j}:=\Big\{v\in C_{T}^{\frac{k+\alpha}{2},k+\alpha}:\,\,\|v\|_{C_{T}^{\frac{k+\alpha}{2},k+\alpha}}\leq M,\,\,v(0,\cdot)=\sigma^{j}(\cdot)\Big\}

for
j=1,2,3.
Note that if
M
is large, then
X_{M,T}^{j}
is non-empty.

Lemma 3.10
.

For
\sigma\in\mathcal{X}_{3}^{k,\alpha}
assume that

\min\limits_{z\in[0,1]}|\sigma_{x}^{j}(z)|=\delta>0,\quad j=1,2,3.

Then for any
v\in X_{M,T}^{j}
one has

\min\limits_{t\in[0,T],\,z\in[0,1]}|v_{x}(t,z)|\geq\delta/2

provided that

(3.11)

T\leq\frac{\delta}{2M}.

Proof.

Since

v_{x}(t,z)=v_{x}(0,z)+\int_{0}^{t}v_{tx}(s,z)ds,

by the Hölder estimate of
v\in X_{M,T}^{j}
we have

|v_{x}(t,z)|\geq|v_{x}(0,z)|-\int_{0}^{t}|v_{tx}(s,z)|ds\geq\delta-Mt\geq\frac{\delta}{2},

whenever
t\leq T\leq\frac{\delta}{2M}.

∎

From now on we assume (
3.11
) and we set

(3.12)

\mathcal{R}_{M,T}:=\prod_{j=1}^{3}X_{M,T}^{j}.

This will be our main functional space.

3.5.2.
Linearized problem

Given
\bar{u}=(\bar{u}_{1},\bar{u}_{2},\bar{u}_{3})\in\mathcal{R}_{M,T},
we look for
u=(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})\in\big[C_{T}^{\frac{k+\alpha}{2},k+\alpha}\big]^{3}
solving the linear system of PDE’s

(3.13)

\begin{cases}&u_{t}^{j}-\alpha_{j}u_{xx}^{j}=F_{\bar{u}}^{j},\\
&u^{j}(0,x)=\sigma^{j}(x),\end{cases}

where
j=1,2,3,

\alpha_{j}:=\frac{\beta_{j}(\frac{\sigma_{x}^{j}}{|\sigma_{x}^{j}|})}{|\sigma_{x}^{j}|^{2}},\qquad F_{\bar{u}}^{j}:=f^{j}+\Bigg(\frac{\beta_{j}(\frac{\bar{u}_{x}^{j}}{|\bar{u}_{x}^{j}|})}{|\bar{u}_{x}^{j}|^{2}}-\alpha_{j}\Bigg)\bar{u}_{xx}^{j},

with the linearized boundary conditions

(3.14)

\displaystyle u^{1}(t,y)=u^{2}(t,y)=u^{3}(t,y),

(3.15)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{3}\Bigg(\theta_{j}\Big(\tfrac{\sigma_{x}^{j}(y)}{|\sigma_{x}^{j}(y)|}\Big)\,\tfrac{u_{x}^{j}(t,y)}{|\sigma_{x}^{j}(y)|}+\bigg[\nabla\theta_{j}\Big(\tfrac{\sigma_{x}^{j}(y)}{|\sigma_{x}^{j}(y)|}\Big)\cdot\tfrac{[\sigma_{x}^{j}(y)]^{\perp}}{|\sigma_{x}^{j}(y)|}\bigg]\tfrac{[u_{x}^{j}(t,y)]^{\perp}}{|\sigma_{x}^{j}(y)|}\Bigg)=B_{\bar{u}}(t,y)

for
(t,y)\in[0,T]\times\{0,1\},
where

B_{\bar{u}}(t,y):=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{3}\Bigg\{\Big[\theta_{j}\Big(\tfrac{\sigma_{x}^{j}(y)}{|\sigma_{x}^{j}(y)|}\Big)\,\tfrac{1}{|\sigma_{x}^{j}(y)|}-\theta_{j}\Big(\tfrac{\bar{u}_{x}^{j}(t,y)}{|\bar{u}_{x}^{j}(t,y)|}\Big)\,\tfrac{1}{|\bar{u}_{x}^{j}(t,y)|}\Big]\,\bar{u}_{x}^{j}(t,y)\\
+\bigg(\Big[\nabla\theta_{j}\Big(\tfrac{\sigma_{x}^{j}(y)}{|\sigma_{x}^{j}(y)|}\Big)\cdot\tfrac{[\sigma_{x}^{j}(y)]^{\perp}}{|\sigma_{x}^{j}(y)|}\Big]\tfrac{1}{|\sigma_{x}^{j}(y)|}-\Big[\nabla\theta_{j}\Big(\tfrac{\bar{u}_{x}^{j}(t,y)}{|\bar{u}_{x}^{j}(t,y)|}\Big)\cdot\tfrac{[\bar{u}_{x}^{j}(t,y)]^{\perp}}{|\bar{u}_{x}^{j}(t,y)|}\Big]\tfrac{1}{|\bar{u}_{x}^{j}(t,y)|}\bigg)[\bar{u}_{x}^{j}(t,y)]^{\perp}\Bigg\}.

In the linearization (
3.15
) of
condition (
3.3
)
we used the positive one-homogeneity and continuous differentiability of
\theta_{j},
hence,

\theta_{j}(a)=\nabla\theta_{j}(a)\cdot a,\quad a\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\},

which implies

\nabla\theta_{j}(a)=(\nabla\theta_{j}(a)\cdot a)\,a+(\nabla\theta_{j}(a)\cdot a^{\perp})\,a^{\perp}=\theta_{j}(a)\,a+(\nabla\theta_{j}(a)\cdot a^{\perp})\,a^{\perp},\quad a\in\mathbb{S}^{1}.

3.5.3.
Solvability of the linear problem

To check solvability we need to check that the linear system is compatible with boundary and initial data
[
44
]
.

We use the Fourier symbols
p=\partial_{t}
and
\xi=\partial_{x}.

The linear operator corresponding to the linear system (
3.13
) has the
6\times 6
-matrix

\mathcal{L}(x,p,\xi)=\begin{pmatrix}(p-\alpha_{1}\xi^{2})\,\mathbf{I}&\mathbf{O}&\mathbf{O}\\
\mathbf{O}&(p-\alpha_{2}\xi^{2})\,\mathbf{I}&\mathbf{O}\\
\mathbf{O}&\mathbf{O}&(p-\alpha_{3}\xi^{2})\,\mathbf{I}\end{pmatrix},

where
\alpha_{i}:=\alpha_{i}(x),

\xi,p\in\mathbb{C}
and

\mathbf{I}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad\mathbf{O}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

In particular, for
{\rm i}=\sqrt{-1}
and
\xi,p\in\mathbb{C}

L(x,p,{\rm i}\xi):=\det\mathcal{L}(x,p,{\rm i}\xi)=\prod_{j=1}^{3}(p+\alpha_{j}\xi^{2})^{2},

and the matrix

\widehat{\mathcal{L}}(x,p,{\rm i}\xi):=L(x,p,{\rm i}\xi)\,\mathcal{L}(x,p,{\rm i}\xi)^{-1}

reads as

\widehat{\mathcal{L}}(x,p,{\rm i}\xi)=\\
\begin{pmatrix}(p+\alpha_{2}\xi^{2})(p+\alpha_{3}\xi^{2})\,\mathbf{I}&\mathbf{O}&\mathbf{O}\\
\mathbf{O}&(p+\alpha_{1}\xi^{2})(p+\alpha_{3}\xi^{2})\,\mathbf{I}&\mathbf{O}\\
\mathbf{O}&\mathbf{O}&(p+\alpha_{1}\xi^{2})(p+\alpha_{2}\xi^{2})\,\mathbf{I}\end{pmatrix}.

Since

\alpha_{j}=\frac{\beta_{j}(\sigma_{x}^{j}/|\sigma_{x}^{j}|)}{|\sigma_{x}^{j}|^{2}}\geq m\,\min\Big\{\tfrac{1}{|\sigma_{x}^{j}(x)|}:\,\,j=1,2,3,\,\,x\in[0,1]\Big\}>0,

the system (
3.13
) is parabolic, here
m
is given by Assumption
3.8
(B).

Following
[
35
]
,
fix
p\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
with
\Re(p)\geq 0
. Then the polynomial
L(x,p,{\rm i}\tau),

\tau\in\mathbb{C},
has six roots with positive imaginary part and six roots with negative imaginary part. More precisely, setting
p=|p|e^{{\rm i}\zeta_{p}}
with
|\zeta_{p}|\leq\pi/2
and

(3.16)

\displaystyle\tau_{j}^{+}:=\tau_{j}^{+}(x,p)=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{|p|}{\alpha_{j}}}\,e^{{\rm i}\big(\tfrac{\pi}{2}+\tfrac{\zeta_{p}}{2}\big)},

\displaystyle\tau_{j}^{-}:=\tau_{j}^{-}(x,p)=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{|p|}{\alpha_{j}}}\,e^{{\rm i}\big(\tfrac{3\pi}{2}+\tfrac{\zeta_{p}}{2}\big)},

we may write

L(x,p,{\rm i}\tau)=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{3}\alpha_{j}^{2}(\tau-\tau_{j}^{+})^{2}(\tau-\tau_{j}^{-})^{2}.

Let

P^{+}:=P^{+}(x,p,\tau)=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{3}(\tau-\tau_{j}^{+})^{2}.

Now we turn to define the matrix associated to the boundary conditions (
3.14
)-(
3.15
). It is given as

\mathcal{B}(y,\xi)=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{I}&-\mathbf{I}&\mathbf{O}\\
\mathbf{O}&\mathbf{I}&-\mathbf{I}\\
\mathbf{Q}_{1}&\mathbf{Q}_{2}&\mathbf{Q}_{3}\end{pmatrix},\qquad y=0,1,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{C},

where

\mathbf{Q}_{j}:=b_{j1}\,\begin{pmatrix}\xi&0\\
0&\xi\end{pmatrix}+b_{j2}\,\begin{pmatrix}0&-\xi\\
\xi&0\end{pmatrix},\quad j=1,2,3,

with all the coefficients evaluated at
y=0
and
y=1,
and

(3.17)

b_{j1}:=\tfrac{1}{|\sigma_{x}^{j}(y)|}\,\theta_{j}\Big(\tfrac{\sigma_{x}^{j}(y)}{|\sigma_{x}^{j}(y)|}\Big),\qquad b_{j2}:=\nabla\theta_{j}\Big(\tfrac{\sigma_{x}^{j}(y)}{|\sigma_{x}^{j}(y)|}\Big)\cdot\tfrac{[\sigma_{x}^{j}(y)]^{\perp}}{|\sigma_{x}^{j}(y)|^{2}}.

Consider the matrix

\mathcal{A}(y,p,{\rm i}\tau):=\mathcal{B}(y,{\rm i}\tau)\widehat{\mathcal{L}}(y,p,{\rm i}\tau)=\\
\begin{pmatrix}(p+\alpha_{2}\tau^{2})(p+\alpha_{3}\tau^{2})\,\mathbf{I}&-(p+\alpha_{1}\tau^{2})(p+\alpha_{3}\tau^{2})\,\mathbf{I}&\mathbf{O}\\
\mathbf{O}&(p+\alpha_{1}\tau^{2})(p+\alpha_{3}\tau^{2})\,\mathbf{I}&-(p+\alpha_{1}\tau^{2})(p+\alpha_{2}\tau^{2})\,\mathbf{I}\\
(p+\alpha_{2}\tau^{2})(p+\alpha_{3}\tau^{2})\,\mathbf{Q}_{1}&(p+\alpha_{1}\tau^{2})(p+\alpha_{3}\tau^{2})\,\mathbf{Q}_{2}&(p+\alpha_{1}\tau^{2})(p+\alpha_{2}\tau^{2})\,\mathbf{Q}_{3}\end{pmatrix}.

By definition, the complementary condition holds
[
44
]
if the rows of this matrix are linearly independent modulo
P^{+}
whenever
p\neq 0
with
\Re(p)\geq 0.

Thus, we need to check if
w\in\mathbb{R}^{6}

(considered as a
1\times 6
-matrix) is such that

w\cdot\mathcal{A}(y,p,{\rm i}\tau)=(0,0,0,0,0,0)\quad\mod\,P^{+},

then
w=0.
This equation yields six linear equations. For instance, for the first column of
\mathcal{A}
we have

(p+\alpha_{2}\tau^{2})(p+\alpha_{3}\tau^{2})\,(w_{1}+{\rm i}b_{11}w_{5}\tau+{\rm i}b_{12}w_{6}\tau)=0\quad\mod P^{+}.

Then by the definition of
P^{+}
we have

w_{1}+{\rm i}b_{11}w_{5}\tau+{\rm i}b_{12}w_{6}\tau=0\quad\mod\,(\tau-\tau_{1}^{+}),

or equivalently,

w_{1}+{\rm i}b_{11}w_{5}\tau_{1}^{+}+{\rm i}b_{12}w_{6}\tau_{1}^{+}=0.

Treating similarly the remaining columns we get

\begin{cases}w_{1}+{\rm i}b_{11}\tau^{+}_{1}\,w_{5}+{\rm i}b_{12}\tau^{+}_{1}\,w_{6}=0,\\
w_{2}-{\rm i}b_{12}\tau^{+}_{1}\,w_{5}+{\rm i}b_{11}\tau^{+}_{1}\,w_{6}=0,\\
-w_{1}+w_{3}+{\rm i}b_{21}\tau^{+}_{2}\,w_{5}+{\rm i}b_{22}\tau^{+}_{2}\,w_{6}=0,\\
-w_{2}+w_{4}-{\rm i}b_{22}\tau^{+}_{2}\,w_{5}+{\rm i}b_{21}\tau^{+}_{2}\,w_{6}=0,\\
-w_{3}+{\rm i}b_{31}\tau^{+}_{3}\,w_{5}+{\rm i}b_{32}\tau^{+}_{3}\,w_{6}=0,\\
-w_{4}-{\rm i}b_{32}\tau^{+}_{3}\,w_{5}+{\rm i}b_{31}\tau^{+}_{3}\,w_{6}=0.\end{cases}

The determinant of this system is computed as

\displaystyle\Delta:=

\displaystyle\det\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0&0&{\rm i}b_{11}\tau_{1}^{+}&{\rm i}b_{12}\tau_{1}^{+}\\
0&1&0&0&-{\rm i}b_{12}\tau_{1}^{+}&{\rm i}b_{11}\tau_{1}^{+}\\
-1&0&1&0&{\rm i}b_{21}\tau_{2}^{+}&{\rm i}b_{22}\tau_{2}^{+}\\
0&-1&0&1&-{\rm i}b_{22}\tau_{2}^{+}&{\rm i}b_{21}\tau_{2}^{+}\\
0&0&-1&0&{\rm i}b_{31}\tau_{3}^{+}&{\rm i}b_{32}\tau_{3}^{+}\\
0&0&0&-1&-{\rm i}b_{32}\tau_{3}^{+}&{\rm i}b_{31}\tau_{3}^{+}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(b_{11}\tau_{1}^{+}+b_{21}\tau_{2}^{+}+b_{31}\tau_{3}^{+})^{2}-(b_{12}\tau_{1}^{+}+b_{22}\tau_{2}^{+}+b_{32}\tau_{3}^{+})^{2}.

Now recalling the definitions of
b_{ij}
in (
3.17
) and of
\tau_{j}^{+}
in (
3.16
) we get

\Delta=p\bigg[\sum\limits_{j=1}^{3}\theta_{j}\Big(\tfrac{\sigma_{x}^{j}}{|\sigma_{x}^{j}|^{2}\alpha_{j}}\Big)\bigg]^{2}+p\bigg[\sum\limits_{j=1}^{3}\nabla\theta_{j}\Big(\tfrac{\sigma_{x}^{j}}{|\sigma_{x}^{j}|}\Big)\cdot\tfrac{[\sigma_{x}^{j}]^{\perp}}{|\sigma_{x}^{j}|^{2}\alpha_{j}}\bigg]^{2}\neq 0.

Thus,
w=0.

Now we check the complementary conditions for the initial datum. Let
\mathcal{C}
be the
6\times 6
-identity matrix. Note that at
t=0
we have
\mathcal{C}u=\sigma.
We need to check that the rows of the matrix

\mathcal{D}(x,p)=\mathcal{C}\cdot\widehat{\mathcal{L}}(x,p,0)=\begin{pmatrix}p^{2}\mathbf{I}&\mathbf{O}&\mathbf{O}\\
\mathbf{O}&p^{2}\mathbf{I}&\mathbf{O}\\
\mathbf{O}&\mathbf{O}&p^{2}\mathbf{I}\end{pmatrix}

are linearly independent modulo
L(x,p,0)=p^{6},
which is obvious.

In view of (
3.9
) the linear problem satisfies the compatibility condition of order
0,
and therefore, by the theory of linear parabolic systems
[

3.6.
Evolution of networks in the Euclidean setting

Starting from the work
[

]

(see e.g.
[

38
,
39

40

and references therein). In this section we shortly describe known results related to evolution of networks in the Euclidean setting; we refer to the recent survey

for more details.

In the Euclidean setting, the condition

) at the triple junctions reduces to a

-condition between normals. The existence and regularity of a flow with Dirichlet boundary conditions has been established, for instance, in

-condition at all triple junctions of the initial network.

The behaviour of such a smooth flow near the maximal time, as in the curvature evolution of closed curves, is obtained using integral estimates for the curvature. Namely,

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
\{\Sigma_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T)}
be the smooth geometric flow in the maximal time interval
[0,T),
starting from an (admissible) network
\Sigma_{0}
in a bounded convex open set
\Omega

\partial\Omega
. Then:

•

either the lower limit of the length of at least one curve in
\Sigma_{t}
converges to
0
as
t\nearrow T,

•

or

\limsup\limits_{t\nearrow T}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\kappa^{2}\,dx=+\infty.

Moreover, if the lengths of all curves in
\Sigma_{t}
are uniformly bounded away from zero as
t\nearrow T,
then there exists
C>0
such that

(3.19)

\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\kappa^{2}\,dx\geq\frac{C}{\sqrt{T-t}}\qquad\text{for all $t<T.$}

Note that under the uniform lower bound on the length, (

) implies

(3.20)

\max\limits_{\Sigma_{t}}\,\kappa^{2}\,\geq\,\frac{C^{\prime}}{\sqrt{T-t}}\qquad\text{for all $t<T,$}

where

depends also on the lengths of the curves, which is slightly weaker than in the blow up case of closed curves
\{\gamma_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T)}
, which reads as

\max\limits_{\gamma_{t}}\,\kappa^{2}\,\geq\,\frac{C}{T-t}\qquad\text{for all $t<T.$}

However, the latter estimate for networks is not known even for simple triods (three smooth curves with a single triple junction).

The next question is the blow-up behaviour of the rescaled networks near the maximal time.
As in the closed curves setting, one can establish Huisken’s monotonicity formula for networks
[
33
,
40
]
and then, using parabolic rescaling, one approaches some limiting network as

. A complete classification of these limiting networks is a hard problem, because they could be not regular; for example, we may loose the
120^{\circ}
condition (collapse of two triple junctions), two curves of the network may collapse (higher-multiplicity), or even some phase may collapse to a point or segment. Of course, one of the possibilities are self-shrinking networks; for their classification we refer to
[
5
,
6
,
22
,
40
]
.

3.7.
Long time behaviour of the anisotropic flow

We recall from
[
35
]
that, as in the Euclidean curvature flow of networks, at the maximal time in a triod with Dirichlet boundary conditions either some of the curves disappear or the curvature of some curve blows up and also near the maximal time the

provided that the length of the curves is uniformly bounded away from zero.

However, to our knowledge, not much is known on theta-shaped networks near the maximal existence time even in the case of a single noneuclidean anisotropy: in this case we cannot straightforwardly repeat/adapt the arguments of

because (as in the isotropic case) we could have not only singularities related to the blow-up of the curvature or disappearance of a curve, but also collapse of triple points or region disappearance.
Moreover, the problem of existence of homothetically shrinking theta-shaped networks seems open in the anisotropic case. Recall that in the isotropic case such a homothetic network does not exist

4

+\frac{z_{1}^{2}}{\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{11})}+\frac{y_{2}^{2}}{\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{15})}+\frac{y_{1}^{2}}{\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{31})}+\frac{z_{2}^{2}}{\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{35})}+\frac{(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}}{\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{21})}.

Recalling that
y_{i}=x_{i}
and
z_{i}=1-x_{i},
we rewrite the last equality as

\frac{1}{\phi_{o}}\int_{\Sigma}[\mathrm{div}_{\Sigma}N]^{2}\phi^{o}(\nu_{\Sigma})\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}=\alpha_{11}x_{1}^{2}+2\alpha_{12}x_{1}x_{2}+\alpha_{22}x_{2}^{2}+2\alpha_{1}x_{1}+2\alpha_{2}x_{2}+\alpha_{0},

where

\displaystyle\alpha_{11}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{11})}+\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{21})}+\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{31})},

\displaystyle\alpha_{22}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{15})}+\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{21})}+\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{35})},

\displaystyle\alpha_{12}=-\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{21})},\qquad\alpha_{1}=-\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{11})},\qquad\alpha_{2}=-\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{35})},

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}=\sum\limits_{i\in\{1,3\},\,j\in\{1,2,3,4,5\}}\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{ij})}.

Thus the minimum problem (
4.5
) reduces to find

(4.20)

\min\limits_{(x_{1},x_{2})\in[0,1]^{2}}\,\Big[\alpha_{11}x_{1}^{2}+2\alpha_{12}x_{1}x_{2}+\alpha_{22}x_{2}^{2}+2\alpha_{1}x_{1}+2\alpha_{2}x_{2}+\alpha_{0}\Big],

whose unique solution is

x_{1}^{\min}:=\frac{\alpha_{12}\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{22}\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{11}\alpha_{22}-\alpha_{12}^{2}},\qquad x_{2}^{\min}:=\frac{\alpha_{12}\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{11}\alpha_{2}}{\alpha_{11}\alpha_{22}-\alpha_{12}^{2}},

which satisfies
(x_{1}^{\min},x_{2}^{\min})\in(0,1)^{2},
i.e., the values of
N_{\min}
at both triple junctions belong to the interior of the admissible regions for triplets (dark regions in Figure
12
).

Remark 4.15
.

The minimum problems (
4.16
) and (
4.20
) show that, in the case of a single even crystalline anisotropy, if
\Sigma
is an admissible network with
n
-triple junctions, then the minimum problem (
4.5
) is reduced to the minimum problem

\min\limits_{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in[a,b]}P(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}),

where

P(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\sum\limits_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}\alpha_{ij}x_{i}x_{j}+\sum\limits_{1\leq i\leq n}\alpha_{i}x_{i}+\alpha_{0}

is a quadratic polynomial of
n
-variables and coefficients
\{\alpha_{ij},\alpha_{i}\}
depending only on
1/\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{k}),
where
S_{k}
are segments of
\Sigma
which end at a triple junction and
\alpha_{ii}>0.
In other words, the crystalline curvature of a network must “see” all triple junctions. This non-locality of crystalline curvature flow makes the problem hard, but at the same time remarkable.
Note that if the minimum
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
of
P
belongs to
(a,b)^{n}
, then the same holds for all sufficiently small admissible perturbations of
\Sigma.
Moreover, the minimum is uniquely determined only by the numbers
1/\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{k}).

4.6.
Stable admissible networks

and
[
10

encourage the following definition.

Definition 4.16
.

Given crystalline anisotropies
\Phi:=(\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{n}),
a polygonal admissible network
\Sigma=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\Sigma_{i}
is called
stable
provided:

•

if
Q
is a triple junction of curves
\Sigma_{i_{1}},

\Sigma_{i_{2}}
and
\Sigma_{i_{3}},
then there exists
\epsilon>0
for which for any
X\in\partial B_{\phi_{i_{1}}}
with
|X-N_{\min}|_{\Sigma_{i_{1}}}(Q)|<\epsilon
there exist
Y\in\partial B_{\phi_{i_{2}}}
and
Z\in\partial B_{\phi_{i_{3}}}
such that

|Y-N_{\min}|_{\Sigma_{i_{2}}}(Q)|+|Z-N_{\min}|_{\Sigma_{i_{3}}}(Q)|<\epsilon\quad\text{and}\quad X+Y+Z=0;

•

N_{\min}|_{\Sigma_{i_{j}}}(Q)
is not a vertex of
B_{\phi_{i_{j}}}.

In other words, a network is stable if and only if the minimal Cahn-Hoffman vector field at every triple junction lies in the interior of the corresponding admissible regions of triplets. For instance, for the Wulff shapes depicted in Figure
6
any network satisfying
N_{\min}=X
at a triple junction is not stable.
As we have seen in the case of a single anisotropy
\phi,
whose Wulff shape is a regular polygon, any admissible
\Sigma
is stable if and only if at each triple junction
N_{\min}
is not a vertex of
B_{\phi}
(see Figure
10
). In particular, the theta-shaped network in Figure
12
and the triod in Figure
11
(provided (
4.18
) holds) are stable.

Appearance of a
\Phi
-zero curvature curve in an unstable network. Notice that in this situation
S_{2}
has zero crystalline curvature, but
S_{1}
and
S_{3}
have non-zero crystalline curvature, therefore, in the evolution they move, whereas

Remark 4.17
.

For any admissible
\Sigma:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\Sigma_{i}
let

(4.21)

\delta_{\Sigma}:=\min\,|{N_{\min}^{\Sigma}}_{\lower 2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle|{\Sigma_{i}}$}}(Q)-{N}_{\lower 2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle|{\Sigma_{i}}$}}(Q)|,

where the minimum is taken over all triple junctions
Q
and all Cahn-Hoffman vector fields
N\in\mathcal{N}^{\Sigma}
such that at least one
{N}_{\lower 2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle|{\Sigma_{i}}$}}(Q)
belongs to the boundary of the admissible region or to a vertex of
B_{\phi_{i}}.
Then
\Sigma
is stable if and only if
\delta_{\Sigma}>0.
Therefore, in view of Lemma
4.8
, as we have observed above in the octagon and hexagon examples, a slight (still admissible) perturbation of a stable network is again stable. An interesting phenomena may occur in the unstable (i.e. in the not stable) case: in this case a slight perturbation of the network either becomes stable or a new zero
\Phi
-curvature curve/segment may start to grow from the triple junction. A discussion on such phenomena can be found in
[
10
]
for a single anisotropy.

Theorem 4.18

(Local existence and uniqueness)
.

Let
\Phi:=\{\phi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{3}
be crystalline anisotropies and
\Sigma^{0}:=\cup_{i=1}^{3}\Sigma_{i}^{0}
be a stable polygonal network having a single triple junction, where each
\Sigma_{i}^{0}
consists of a single segment
S_{i}
and a half-line
L_{i}
, oriented starting from the triple junction. Then there exist
T>0
and a unique polycrystalline curvature flow
\{\Sigma(t)\}_{t\in[0,T]}
of admissible stable networks starting from
\Sigma^{0}.

Proof.

Let
S_{i}
(resp.
L_{i}
),
i=1,2,3,
be the segments (resp. half-lines) forming a triple junction, oriented from the triple junction, and let
\theta_{i}
be the angle between
S_{j}
and
S_{k},

i\neq j\neq k\neq i,
so that

(4.22)

\cos\theta_{i}=\tau_{S_{j}}\cdot\tau_{S_{k}},\quad\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}+\theta_{3}=2\pi.

Let us denote by
\gamma_{i}\in(0,\pi)
the angle between
S_{i}
and
L_{i}
at their junction.

Step 1.
Given
\rho>0,
let
\mathcal{G}_{\rho}
be the collection of all admissible networks
\Sigma
parallel to
\Sigma^{0}
such that
d(\Sigma,\Sigma^{0})\leq\rho.
Let us show that there exists
\rho_{0}>0
depending only on
\Sigma^{0}
such that any network
\Sigma\in\mathcal{G}_{\rho_{0}}
is stable.

For
\rho>0
and
\Sigma\in\mathcal{G}_{\rho},
let
\delta_{\Sigma}\geq 0
be given by (
4.21
). Since
\Sigma^{0}
is stable, we have
\delta_{\Sigma^{0}}>0.
Assume that there exists a sequence
\{\Sigma(k)\}
of unstable networks parallel to
\Sigma^{0}
such that
d(\Sigma(k),\Sigma^{0})<1/k
for any
k\geq 1.
Then for
i=1,2,3,
by (
4.8
)

{N_{\min}^{\Sigma(k)}}_{\lower 2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle|{S_{i}(k)}$}}\big(\zeta(S_{i}(k),S_{i},x)\big)\to{N_{\min}^{\Sigma^{0}}}_{\lower 2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle|{S_{i}}$}}(x)

uniformly in
x\in S_{i}
, where

\zeta(S_{i}(k),S_{i},\cdot):S_{i}\to S_{i}(k)
is a linear bijection of
S_{i}
to the corresponding parallel segment
S_{i}(k)\subset\Sigma(k).
Thus,
0=\delta_{\Sigma(k)}\geq\delta_{\Sigma^{0}}/2>0
for all large
k,
a contradiction.

Step 2.
Let
T_{1},T_{2},T_{3}
(resp.
\bar{T}_{1},\bar{T}_{2},\bar{T}_{3}
) be segments, forming a triple junction and oriented from the triple junction, such that
T_{i}||\bar{T}_{i}
for
i=1,2,3.
If

h_{i}:=H(\bar{T}_{i},T_{i})\cdot\nu_{T_{i}},\quad i=1,2,3,

then

(4.23)

h_{1}\sin\omega_{1}+h_{2}\sin\omega_{2}+h_{3}\sin\omega_{3}=0,

where
\omega_{i}
is the angle between
T_{j}
and
T_{k},

i\neq j\neq k\neq i.

Assume that we are as in the situation of Figure
14
, i.e.,
h_{3}\geq 0\geq h_{1},h_{2}
and let
x_{i}=|h_{i}|
for
i=1,2,3
.

Figure 14.
Expressing
x_{3}
with
x_{1}
and
x_{2}.

Then

\mathcal{H}^{1}([AC])=\frac{x_{3}}{\sin(\pi-\omega_{1})}=\frac{x_{3}}{\sin\omega_{1}}.

On the other hand, since

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{1}([AC])=

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{1}([AB])+\mathcal{H}^{1}([DE])+\mathcal{H}^{1}([EF])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{x_{2}}{\tan\omega_{1}}+\frac{x_{1}}{\sin\omega_{3}}-\frac{x_{2}}{\tan\omega_{3}}=\frac{x_{1}}{\sin\omega_{3}}-\frac{x_{2}\sin(\omega_{1}+\omega_{3})}{\sin\omega_{1}\sin\omega_{3}}

and
\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}+\omega_{3}=2\pi,
we have

\frac{x_{3}}{\sin\omega_{1}}=\frac{x_{1}}{\sin\omega_{3}}+\frac{x_{2}\sin\omega_{2}}{\sin\omega_{1}\sin\omega_{3}}

and (
4.23
) follows. The proof in the other cases is similar.

Step 3.
Let
h_{1},h_{2},h_{3}
be real numbers such that
|h_{i}|\leq\rho_{0}
and

(4.24)

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{3}h_{i}\sin\theta_{i}=0.

Then there exists a unique
\Sigma\in\mathcal{G}_{\rho_{0}}
such that

h_{i}=H_{i}(S_{i},S_{i}^{0})\cdot\nu_{S_{i}^{0}},\quad i=1,2,3.

Indeed, using
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
we can construct a unique network
\Sigma
parallel to
\Sigma^{0}
and
h_{i}=H_{i}(S_{i},S_{i}^{0})\cdot\nu_{S_{i}^{0}}
for
i=1,2.
Let
h_{3}^{\prime}:=H(S_{i},S_{i}^{0})\cdot\nu_{S_{i}^{0}}.

By Step 2 we know

h_{1}\sin\theta_{1}+h_{2}\sin\theta_{2}+h_{3}^{\prime}\sin\theta_{3}=0.

Then (
4.24
) implies
h_{3}=h_{3}^{\prime}
and hence,
\Sigma\in\mathcal{G}_{\rho_{0}}.

Step 4.
Let us study some properties of the minimizing Cahn-Hoffman vector field
N_{\star}:=N_{\min}^{\Sigma}
of
\Sigma\in\mathcal{G}_{\rho_{0}}.

By Remark
4.7

N_{\star}
is uniquely defined at the endpoints of each
L_{i},
and coincide with
N_{\star}^{0}:=N_{\min}^{\Sigma^{0}}.
Thus, we only need to care at the triple junction of
S_{1},S_{2},S_{3}
. Writing
S_{i}:=[A_{0}A_{i}]
and using Lemma
4.8
(b) we find that
N_{\star}
minimizes the functional

\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma}(N):=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{3}\frac{\phi_{i}^{o}(\nu_{S_{i}})}{\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{i})}\,\Big|[{N}_{\lower 2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle|{S_{i}}$}}(A_{i})-{N}_{\lower 2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle|{S_{i}}$}}(A_{0})]\cdot\tau_{S_{i}}\Big|^{2},\quad N\in\mathcal{N}^{\Sigma},

where
\phi_{i}
is the anisotropy, corresponding to
\Sigma_{i}.
Since
A_{i}
is the endpoint of the half-line
L_{i},
as we observed above
N(A_{i})=N_{\star}(A_{i})=N_{\star}^{0}(A_{i}^{0}),
where we set
S_{i}^{0}:=[A_{0}^{0}A_{i}^{0}].
Thus, recalling
\nu_{S_{i}}=\nu_{S_{i}^{0}}
and
\tau_{S_{i}}=\tau_{S_{i}^{0}},
we get

(4.25)

\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma}(N):=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{3}\frac{\phi_{i}^{o}(\nu_{S_{i}^{0}})}{\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{i})}\,\Big|a_{i}-x_{i}\Big|^{2},

where

a_{i}:=[{N_{\star}^{0}}_{\lower 2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle|{S_{i}^{0}}$}}(A_{i}^{0})-{N_{\star}^{0}}_{\lower 2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle|{S_{i}^{0}}$}}(A_{0})]\cdot\tau_{S_{i}^{0}}=\kappa^{\Phi}(S_{i}^{0})\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{i}^{0})

and

x_{i}:=[{N}_{\lower 2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle|{S_{i}}$}}(A_{0})-{N_{\star}^{0}}_{\lower 2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle|{S_{i}^{0}}$}}(A_{0}^{0})]\cdot\tau_{S_{i}^{0}}.

By the choice of
\rho_{0}
and the stability of
\Sigma,
we may consider only those
N\in\mathcal{N}^{\Sigma}
such that
{N}_{\lower 2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle|{S_{i}}$}}(A_{0})
and
{N_{\star}^{0}}_{\lower 2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle|{S_{i}^{0}}$}}(A_{0}^{0})
lie in the same side of the Wulff shape
B_{\phi_{k_{i}}}
, parallel to
\tau_{S_{i}^{0}},
and therefore

{N}_{\lower 2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle|{S_{i}}$}}(A_{0})={N_{\star}^{0}}_{\lower 2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle|{S_{i}^{0}}$}}(A_{0}^{0})+x_{i}\tau_{S_{i}^{0}}.

Now by the balance condition at
A_{0}
we get

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{3}x_{i}\tau_{S_{i}^{0}}=0,

or equivalently, by (
4.22
)

x_{i}=-x_{j}\cos\theta_{k}-x_{k}\cos\theta_{j},\quad i\neq j\neq k\neq i.

These equalities immediately imply

\frac{x_{1}}{\sin\theta_{1}}=\frac{x_{2}}{\sin\theta_{2}}=\frac{x_{3}}{\sin\theta_{3}}.

Therefore, we have only one independent variable, i.e., as in the case of a single crystalline anisotropy, knowing only one value of
{N}_{\lower 2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle|{S_{i}}$}}
we uniquely determine the admissible triplet. Letting

(4.26)

x_{2}=x_{1}\,\frac{\sin\theta_{2}}{\sin\theta_{1}}\quad\text{and}\quad x_{3}=x_{1}\,\frac{\sin\theta_{3}}{\sin\theta_{1}},

we can rewrite
\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma}(N)
in (
4.25
) as a quadratic function of
x_{1}:

\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma}(N)=\alpha_{2}x_{1}^{2}+\alpha_{1}x_{1}+\alpha_{0}:=f(x_{1}),

where

\displaystyle\alpha_{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sin^{2}\theta_{1}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{3}\frac{\phi_{i}^{o}(\nu_{S_{i}^{0}})\sin^{2}\theta_{i}}{\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{i})},

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}=

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{\sin\theta_{1}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{3}\phi_{i}^{o}(\nu_{S_{i}^{0}})\kappa^{\Phi}(S_{i}^{0})\,\frac{\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{i}^{0})\sin\theta_{i}}{\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{i})},

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{3}\frac{\phi_{i}^{o}(\nu_{S_{i}^{0}})}{\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{i})}\,[\kappa^{\Phi}(S_{i}^{0})\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{i}^{0})]^{2}.

Since
\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma}
has a unique minimizer
N=N_{\star}
and
\Sigma
is stable,
f
has a unique minimizer
x_{1}^{\star}:=-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2\alpha_{0}}.
Thus, uniquely defining
x_{2}^{\star}
and
x_{3}^{\star}
using the relations (
4.26
), we get

{N_{\star}}_{\lower 2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle|{S_{i}}$}}(A_{0})={N_{\star}^{0}}_{\lower 2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle|{S_{i}^{0}}$}}(A_{0}^{0})+x_{i}^{\star}\tau_{S_{i}^{0}}.

Therefore, by the definition of the crystalline curvature,

(4.27)

\kappa^{\Phi}(S_{i})=\frac{[{N_{\star}}_{\lower 2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle|{S_{i}}$}}(A_{i})-{N_{\star}}_{\lower 2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle|{S_{i}}$}}(A_{0})]\cdot\tau_{S_{i}^{0}}}{\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{i})}=\frac{\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{i}^{0})\kappa^{\Phi}(S_{i}^{0})-x_{i}^{\star}}{\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{i})}.

By uniqueness,
N_{\star}=N_{\star}^{0}
if
\Sigma=\Sigma^{0},
and thus, in this case we should have
x_{i}^{\star}=0.
Then by the explicit expression of
x_{1}^{\star}
we get

(4.28)

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{3}\phi_{i}^{o}(\nu_{S_{i}^{0}})\kappa^{\Phi}(S_{i}^{0})\sin\theta_{i}=0.

Note that this condition on curvatures is analogous to the smooth case (
3.8
).

Similarly, slightly perturbing
\Sigma
and repeating the same arguments above we find that (
4.28
) holds also with
\Sigma
in place of
\Sigma^{0},
i.e., any admissible stable network
\Sigma,
parallel to
\Sigma^{0},
satisfies this curvature-balance condition. This condition will be important in the sequel.

Step 5.
By Proposition
4.3
we can compute

\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{i})=\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{i}^{0})+g_{i}(h_{1},h_{2},h_{3})

for some positively one-homogeneous Lipschitz function
g_{i}.
Inserting this in (
4.27
) we get

\kappa^{\Phi}(S_{i})=\kappa^{\Phi}(S_{i}^{0})+G_{i}(h_{1},h_{2},h_{3}),

where
G_{i}
is a Lipschitz function, satisfying
G_{i}(0,0,0)=0.

Step 6.
Given
T>0,
let

\mathcal{S}_{T}
be the space of all triplets
h:=(h_{1},h_{2},h_{3})
of functions
h_{i}\in C([0,T])
satisfying

(4.29)

h_{1}(t)\sin\theta_{1}+h_{2}(t)\sin\theta_{2}+h_{3}(t)\sin\theta_{3}=0,\quad i\in[0,T],

and

(4.30)

h_{i}(0)=0,\quad\max_{i}\|h_{i}\|_{\infty}\leq\rho_{0}.

Consider the map
F_{h}=(F_{h}^{1},F_{h}^{2},F_{h}^{3}),
where

F_{h}^{i}(t):=\phi_{i}^{o}(\nu_{S_{i}^{0}})\,\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\kappa^{\Phi}(S_{i}^{0})+G_{i}(h_{1}(s),h_{2}(s),h_{3}(s))\Big)ds,\quad t\in[0,T].

We claim that
F_{h}
has a fixed point in
\mathcal{S}_{T}
provided that

T\leq\frac{\rho_{0}}{1+\max_{i}\Big(\|\phi_{i}^{o}\|_{\infty}[|\kappa^{\Phi}(S_{i})|+3\ell_{i}\rho_{0}]\Big)},

where
\ell_{i}>0
is the Lipschitz constant of
G_{i}.

Indeed, note that
\mathcal{S}_{T}
is convex and closed. Let us show that
F_{h}\in\mathcal{S}_{T}
for any
h\in\mathcal{S}_{T}.
Clearly,
F_{h}(0)=0.
By the definition of
G_{i},

|G_{i}(h_{1},h_{2},h_{3})|\leq\ell_{i}(|h_{1}|+|h_{2}|+|h_{3}|)\leq 3\ell_{i}\rho_{0}.

Therefore, by the choice of
T,

\|F_{h}^{i}\|_{\infty}\leq\|\phi_{i}^{o}\|_{\infty}(|\kappa^{\Phi}(S_{i})|+3\ell_{i}\rho_{0})T<\rho_{0}.

Next we show

(4.31)

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{3}F_{h}^{i}(t)\sin\theta_{i}=0\quad\text{for any $t\in[0,T]$}.

By assumptions (
4.29
)-(
4.30
) and Step 3, for each
s\in[0,T]
there exists a unique network
\Sigma(s)\in\mathcal{G}_{\rho_{0}}
satisfying
h_{i}(s)=H_{i}(S_{i}(s),S_{i}^{0})\cdot\nu_{S_{i}^{0}}
.
Then by Step 5 its curvature is given by

(4.32)

\kappa^{\Phi}(S_{i}(s))=\kappa^{\Phi}(S_{i}^{0})+G_{i}(h_{1}(s),h_{2}(s),h_{3}(s))

and by Step 4, it satisfies the curvature-balance condition

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{3}\phi_{i}^{o}(\nu_{S_{i}^{0}})\kappa^{\Phi}(S_{i}(s))\sin\theta_{i}=0.

From this and (
4.32
) we deduce

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{3}\phi_{i}^{o}(S_{i}^{0})[\kappa^{\Phi}(S_{i}^{0})++G_{i}(h_{1}(s),h_{2}(s),h_{3}(s))]\sin\theta_{i}=0,\quad s\in[0,T],

and hence integrating this equality we obtain (
4.31
).

Next let us prove that the set
\{F_{h}\}_{h\in\mathcal{S}_{T}}
is compact in
(C([0,T]))^{3}
. Indeed, by the choice of
T,
this set is equibounded in
(C([0,T]))^{3}.
Moreover, as we have seen above

\|[F_{h}^{i}]^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq|\kappa^{\Phi}(S_{i}^{0})|+3\ell_{i}\rho_{0}.

Thus,
\{F_{h}\}_{h\in\mathcal{S}_{T}}
is also equicontinuous in
(C[0,T])^{3}.
Therefore, by the Arzela-Ascoli theorem, it is compact. Now the Schauder fixed point theorem implies the existence of
\bar{h}\in\mathcal{S}_{T}
such that

F_{\bar{h}}=\bar{h}.
A bootstrap argument shows that
\bar{h}\in C^{1}([0,T])
and hence,
\bar{h}^{\prime}=F_{\bar{h}}^{\prime},
i.e.,

\bar{h}_{i}^{\prime}(t)=\phi_{i}^{o}(S_{i}^{0})\Big(\kappa^{\Phi}(S_{i}^{0})+G_{i}(\bar{h}_{1}(t),\bar{h}_{2}(t),\bar{h}_{3}(t)\Big),\quad i=1,2,3.

As we observed earlier, the right-hand side of this equality is the (multiple of the) crystalline curvature of the unique network
\bar{\Sigma}(t)\in\mathcal{G}_{\rho_{0}}
such that

\bar{h}_{i}(t)=H_{i}(\bar{S}_{i}(t),S_{i}^{0})\cdot\nu_{S_{i}^{0}},\quad t\in[0,T].

In particular, by Step 1 each
\bar{\Sigma}(t)
is stable. Since
h_{i}(0)=0,
we have
\bar{\Sigma}(0)=\Sigma^{0}
, and therefore
\{\bar{\Sigma}(\cdot)\}
is the crystalline curvature flow of stable networks starting from
\Sigma^{0}
(in the sense of Definition
4.11
).

Finally, let us show the uniqueness of the flow. Let
\{\bar{\Sigma}(\cdot)\}
and
\{\widehat{\Sigma}(\cdot)\}
be two different flows in
[0,T]
starting from
\Sigma^{0},
and let
\bar{h}:=(\bar{h}_{1},\bar{h}_{2},\bar{h}_{3})
and
\widehat{h}:=(\widehat{h}_{1},\widehat{h}_{2},\widehat{h}_{3})
be the corresponding signed distances. Since both solve the same ODE of Definition
4.11
, we have

\bar{h}=F_{\bar{h}}\quad\text{and}\quad\widehat{h}=F_{\widehat{h}}.

Thus, by the Lipschitzianity of
G_{i}
we get

|\bar{h}(t)-\widehat{h}(t)|\leq\max_{i}\ell_{i}\|\phi_{i}^{o}\|_{\infty}\|\bar{h}-\widehat{h}\|_{\infty}T\quad\text{for any $t\in[0,T]$}.

Thus, by the choice of
T,
we get

0<\|\bar{h}-\widehat{h}\|_{\infty}\leq\max_{i}\ell_{i}\|\phi_{i}^{o}\|_{\infty}\|\bar{h}-\widehat{h}\|_{\infty}T<\|\bar{h}-\widehat{h}\|_{\infty},

a contradiction.
∎

5.
Appendix

In this appendix we prove two lemmas used in the proof of Theorem
3.9
.

Lemma 5.1

(
Hölder continuity of the composition
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a bounded open set,
\rho\in(0,1]
and let
G\in C^{1+\rho}(\overline{\Omega}).
Then

(5.1)

\displaystyle[G(b_{1})-G(b_{2})]_{\rho^{\prime}}\leq\|\nabla G\|_{\infty}[b_{1}-b_{2}]_{\rho^{\prime}}+[\nabla G]_{\rho}\max\{[b_{1}]_{\rho},[b_{2}]_{\rho^{\prime}}\}\|b_{1}-b_{2}\|_{\infty}^{\rho}

for any
b_{1},b_{2}\in C^{\rho^{\prime}}([0,1];\overline{\Omega})
with
\rho^{\prime}\in(0,\rho],
where
[\cdot]_{\rho}
is the Hölder seminorm.

Proof.

First we assume that
n=1
and
\Omega
is a bounded interval of
{\mathbb{R}}.
Then for any
b_{1},b_{2}\in C^{\rho^{\prime}}([0,1];\overline{\Omega})
and
x,y\in[0,1],

x\neq y,
we have

(5.2)

\displaystyle G(b_{1}(x))-G(b_{2}(x))-G(b_{1}(y))+G(b_{2}(y))=\int_{b_{1}(y)}^{b_{1}(x)}G^{\prime}(t)dt-\int_{b_{2}(y)}^{b_{2}(x)}G^{\prime}(t)dt.

Since

\int_{a}^{b}G^{\prime}(t)dt=(b-a)\int_{0}^{1}G^{\prime}(a+t(b-a))dt,

we can rewrite (
5.2
) as

\displaystyle G(b_{1}(x))-G(b_{2}(x))-G(b_{1}(y))+G(b_{2}(y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(b_{1}(x)-b_{1}(y))\int_{0}^{1}\Big[G^{\prime}(b_{1}(y)+t[b_{1}(x)-b_{1}(y)])-G^{\prime}(b_{2}(y)+t[b_{2}(x)-b_{2}(y)])\Big]dt

\displaystyle+(b_{1}(x)-b_{1}(y)-b_{2}(x)+b_{2}(y))\int_{0}^{1}G^{\prime}(b_{1}(y)+t[b_{1}(x)-b_{1}(y)])dt.

Hence, using
G^{\prime}\in C^{\rho}(\overline{\Omega})
we get

\displaystyle\frac{|G(b_{1}(x))-G(b_{2}(x))-G(b_{1}(y))+G(b_{2}(y))|}{|x-y|^{\rho^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle[b_{1}]_{\rho^{\prime}}[G^{\prime}]_{\rho}\int_{0}^{1}\Big|(1-t)|b_{1}(y)-b_{2}(y)|+t|b_{1}(x)-b_{1}(y)|\Big|^{\rho}dt+[b_{1}-b_{2}]_{\rho^{\prime}}\|G^{\prime}\|_{\infty}.

Therefore,

(5.3)

[G(b_{1})-G(b_{2})]_{\rho^{\prime}}\leq\|G^{\prime}\|_{\infty}[b_{1}-b_{2}]_{\rho^{\prime}}+[G^{\prime}]_{\rho}\max\{[b_{1}]_{\rho^{\prime}},[b_{2}]_{\rho^{\prime}}\}\,\|b_{1}-b_{2}\|_{\infty}^{\rho}.

Now consider the case
n>1
and let
b_{i}=(b_{i}^{1},\ldots,b_{i}^{n}).
Then by the triangle inequality we have

\displaystyle[G(b_{1})-G(b_{2})]_{\rho^{\prime}}\leq

\displaystyle[G(b_{1}^{1},b_{2}^{1}\ldots,b_{1}^{n})-G(b_{2}^{1},b_{1}^{1}\ldots,b_{1}^{n})]_{\rho^{\prime}}+\ldots

\displaystyle+[G(b_{2}^{1},\ldots,b_{2}^{n-1},b_{1}^{n})-G(b_{2}^{1},\ldots,b_{2}^{n-1},b_{2}^{n})]_{\rho^{\prime}}.

By (
5.3
)

[G(\cdots,b_{1}^{i},\cdots)-G(\cdots,b_{2}^{i},\cdots)]_{\rho^{\prime}}\\
\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|\nabla_{i}G\|_{\infty}[b_{1}^{i}-b_{2}^{i}]_{\rho^{\prime}}+[\nabla_{i}G]_{\rho}\max\{[b_{1}^{i}]_{\rho^{\prime}},[b_{2}^{i}]_{\rho^{\prime}}\}\|b_{1}^{i}-b_{2}^{i}\|_{\infty}^{\rho},

where the corresponding variables in place of
\cdots
in
G(\cdots,b_{1}^{i},\cdots)
and
G(\cdots,b_{2}^{i},\cdots)
are the same and
\nabla_{i}f:=\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}.

Therefore,

[G(b_{1})-G(b_{2})]_{\rho^{\prime}}\leq\|\nabla G\|_{\infty}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}[b_{1}^{i}-b_{2}^{i}]_{\rho^{\prime}}+[\nabla G]_{\rho}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\max\{[b_{1}^{i}]_{\rho^{\prime}},[b_{2}^{i}]_{\rho^{\prime}}\}\|b_{1}^{i}-b_{2}^{i}\|_{\infty}^{\rho}

and (
5.1
) follows.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\psi
be a
C^{2+\alpha}
-function defined in a tubular neighborhood of the unit circle
\mathbb{S}^{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that

0<m\leq\min\limits_{\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{1}}\,\psi(\nu)\leq\max\limits_{\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{1}}\,\psi(\nu)\leq\frac{1}{m}

for some
m\in(0,1].
For
T>0
and
m_{1},m_{2}>0
, let
X_{T,m_{1},m_{2}}
be the subset of all
v\in C_{T}^{\frac{\alpha}{2},\alpha}
such that

\min\limits_{t\in[0,T],x\in[0,1]}|v(t,x)|\geq m_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad\|v\|_{C_{T}^{\frac{\alpha}{2},\alpha}}\leq m_{2}.

For
v\in X_{T,m_{1},m_{2}}
define

a(v):=|v|^{-2}\psi\Big(\frac{v}{|v|}\Big).

Then for any
v^{\prime},v^{\prime\prime}\in X_{T,m_{1},m_{2}}

\displaystyle\|a(v^{\prime})-a(v^{\prime\prime})\|_{\infty}\leq L\,\|v^{\prime}-v^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty},

(5.4)

\displaystyle[a(v^{\prime})-a(v^{\prime\prime})]_{\alpha,x}\leq L\Big[\|v^{\prime}-v^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}+[v^{\prime}-v^{\prime\prime}]_{\alpha,x}\Big],

(5.5)

\displaystyle[a(v^{\prime})-a_{1}(v^{\prime\prime})]_{\alpha/2,t}\leq L\Big[\|v^{\prime}-v^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}+[v^{\prime}-v^{\prime\prime}]_{\alpha/2,t}\Big],

and

(5.6)

\displaystyle\|a(v^{\prime})\|_{C_{T}^{\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\leq L,

where
L
depends (continuously) only on
m_{1},

m_{2},

\|\psi\|_{\infty},

\|\nabla\psi\|_{\infty}
and
\|\nabla^{2}\psi\|_{\infty}.

In the proof of Theorem
3.9
we apply this lemma with functions whose space-derivative
v_{x}
belongs to
X_{T,\delta/2,M}
and
\psi=\beta_{j}.

Proof.

Note that
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\mapsto|\xi|^{-n},

n\in\mathbb{N},
is Lipschitz continuous in
\{|\xi|\geq m_{1}\}.
Indeed,

\displaystyle\big||\xi_{1}|^{-n}-|\xi_{2}|^{-n}\big|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|}{|\xi_{1}|^{n}|\xi_{2}|^{n}}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}|\xi_{1}|^{i}|\xi_{2}|^{n-1-i}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{n|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|}{|\xi_{1}|^{n}|\xi_{2}|^{n}}\,\max\{|\xi_{1}|^{n-1},|\xi_{2}|^{n-1}\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{n|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|}{\min\{|\xi_{1}|^{n},|\xi_{2}|^{n}\}\max\{|\xi_{1}|,|\xi_{2}|\}}

so that

\big||\xi_{1}|^{-n}-|\xi_{2}|^{-n}\big|\leq\frac{n}{m_{1}^{n+1}}\,|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|.

Similarly, the map
\xi\mapsto\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}
is Lipschitz in
\{|\xi|\geq m_{1}\}:

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{\xi_{1}}{|\xi_{1}|}-\frac{\xi_{2}}{|\xi_{2}|}\Big|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|}{|\xi_{1}|}+\frac{\big||\xi_{1}|-|\xi_{2}|\big|}{|\xi_{1}|}\leq\frac{2|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|}{|\xi_{1}|}

so that

\Big|\frac{\xi_{1}}{|\xi_{1}|}-\frac{\xi_{2}}{|\xi_{2}|}\Big|\leq\frac{2}{m_{1}}\,|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|.

Let

E:=\{m_{1}\leq|\xi|\leq m_{2}\}

and let us estimate
\|a\|_{\infty}
and
[a]_{\alpha}
in
E.
Obviously,

\|a\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\|\psi\|_{\infty}}{m_{1}}

and

\displaystyle|a(\xi^{\prime})-a(\xi^{\prime\prime})|\leq

\displaystyle\big||\xi^{\prime}|^{-2}-|\xi^{\prime\prime}|^{-2}\big|\,\psi\big(\tfrac{\xi^{\prime}}{|\xi^{\prime}|}\big)+|\xi^{\prime\prime}|^{-2}\Big[\psi\big(\tfrac{\xi^{\prime}}{|\xi^{\prime}|}\big)-\psi\big(\tfrac{\xi^{\prime\prime}}{|\xi^{\prime\prime}|}\big)\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2\|\psi\|_{\infty}}{m_{1}^{3}}\,|\xi^{\prime}-\xi^{\prime\prime}|+\frac{2\|\nabla\psi\|_{\infty}}{m_{1}^{3}}\,|\xi^{\prime}-\xi^{\prime\prime}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\|\psi\|_{\infty}+2\|\nabla\psi\|_{\infty}}{m_{1}^{3}}\,|\xi^{\prime}-\xi^{\prime\prime}|

so that

\|a(v^{\prime})-a(v^{\prime\prime})\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{2\|\psi\|_{\infty}+2\|\nabla\psi\|_{\infty}}{m_{1}}\,\|v^{\prime}-v^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}.

Moreover, since
\psi
is
C^{2+\alpha}
in a tubular neighborhood of
\mathbb{S}^{1},
the function

\nabla a(\xi)=|\xi|^{-5}\big[\nabla\psi(\xi/|\xi|)\cdot\xi^{c}\big]\xi^{c}-4|\xi|^{-4}\psi(\xi/|\xi|)\xi,

is Lipschitz, where
\xi^{c}=(\xi_{2},\xi_{1})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}.
Its Lipschitz constant does not exceed

L_{1}:=\frac{5\|\nabla\psi\|_{\infty}+2\|\nabla^{2}\psi\|_{\infty}}{m_{1}^{6}}\,m_{2}^{2}+\frac{16\|\psi\|_{\infty}+10\|\nabla\psi\|_{\infty}}{m_{1}^{5}}\,m_{2}+\frac{4\|\psi\|_{\infty}}{m_{1}^{4}}.

Now by Lemma
5.1
applied with
a,

\rho=1
and
\rho^{\prime}=\alpha
and
\rho^{\prime}=\alpha/2
we get

\displaystyle[a(v^{\prime})-a(v^{\prime\prime})]_{\alpha,x}\leq\|a\|_{\infty}[v^{\prime}-v^{\prime\prime}]_{\alpha,x}+\|\nabla a\|_{\infty}\max\{[v^{\prime}]_{\alpha,x},[v^{\prime\prime}]_{\alpha,x}\}\|v^{\prime}-v^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}

and

\displaystyle[a(v^{\prime})-a(v^{\prime\prime})]_{\alpha/2,t}\leq\|a\|_{\infty}[v^{\prime}-v^{\prime\prime}]_{\alpha/2,t}

\displaystyle+\|\nabla a\|_{\infty}\max\{[v^{\prime}]_{\alpha/2,t},[v^{\prime\prime}]_{\alpha/2,t}\}\|v^{\prime}-v^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty},

and hence, estimates (
5.4
)-(
5.5
) follow with

L:=\max\big\{\|\psi\|_{\infty}/m_{1},\,L_{1}\big\}.

The proof of (
5.6
) is similar.
∎

Maximal Polynomial Modulations of Singular Radon Transforms
Lars Becker

Abstract
. We prove L 2 → L p L^{2}\to L^{p} estimates on the torus for maximal polynomial modulations of Calderón-Zygmund operators with anisotropic scaling. We obtain improved constants in these estimates. As a corollary, maximal polynomial modulations of a mollified version of the Hilbert transform along the parabola are bounded with only logarithmic dependence of the estimate on the Lipschitz constant of the mollifier.

42B20

1.
Introduction

Given a singular integral operator
T:L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}})
and a set
\mathcal{Q}
of polynomial functions
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}\to\mathbb{R}
, define the maximal modulation operator
T^{\mathcal{Q}}
by

T^{\mathcal{Q}}f(x)=\sup_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}|T(M_{Q}f)(x)|\,,

where
M_{Q}f(t)=e^{iQ(t)}f(t)
.

The study of maximal modulation operators grew out of the work of Carleson
[
Car66
]
, who proved that the Fourier series of an
L^{2}
function
f
converges pointwise almost everywhere to
f
.
Carleson’s theorem is equivalent to the boundedness from
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
into
L^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
of the Carleson operator
H^{\mathcal{Q}_{1}}f
, where
\mathcal{Q}_{1}
is the set of linear polynomials and
H
denotes the Hilbert transform

Hf(x)=p.v.\,\frac{1}{\pi}\int f(x-t)\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\,.

Later, other proofs of Carleson’s theorem were given by Fefferman
[
Fef73
]
and Lacey and Thiele
[
LT00
]
.
Sjölin
[
Sjö71
]
replaced the Hilbert transform
H
with a general Calderón-Zygmund operator
T
and proved boundedness on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}})
of the maximal modulation operators
T^{\mathcal{Q}_{1}}
, see also
[
PT03
]
for an alternative proof. A multilinear analogue of the Carleson operator was studied in
[
LM07
]
.

The investigation of more general maximal polynomial modulation operators originates in the work of Stein
[
Ste95
]
. He proved an
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
estimate for the operator
H^{\mathcal{Q}}
, where
\mathcal{Q}=\{\alpha t^{2}\,:\,\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\}
.
Stein and Wainger
[
SW01
]
showed
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}})
bounds for
T^{\mathcal{Q}}
for Calderón-Zygmund operators
T
and
\mathcal{Q}
the set of all polynomials of degree at most
d
with no linear term. Finally, the restriction on the linear term of the polynomials was removed by Lie
[
Lie09
]
,
[
Lie20
]
for the Hilbert transform and by Zorin-Kranich
[
Zor21
]
for Hölder continuous Calderón-Zygmund kernels in arbitrary dimension.

This article deals with a problem introduced by Pierce and Young
[
PY19
]
. They considered singular integrals on a paraboloid

Sf(x,y)=\int f(x-z,y-|z|^{2})K(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\,,

where
K
is some Calderón-Zygmund kernel on
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}
, and proved
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}+1})
estimates for the operator
S^{\mathcal{Q}}
when
1<p<\infty
,
\mathbf{d}\geq 2
and
\mathcal{Q}
is a subspace of polynomials satisfying certain restrictions. In particular, the polynomials in
\mathcal{Q}
are not allowed to have linear terms and the quadratic term cannot be a multiple of
|x|^{2}
. This motivates the following question:

Open Problem 1
.

Define the Hilbert transform along the parabola by

H_{P}f(x,y)=p.v.\,\int f(x-t,y-t^{2})\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\,.

Is the maximally modulated Hilbert transform
H_{P}^{\mathcal{Q}_{1}}
bounded from
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
into
L^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
?

A natural line of attack towards
1
is to approximate the Hilbert transform along the parabola by Calderón-Zygmund operators
T
with anisotropic scaling which have smooth kernels away from
0
. If one could prove uniform bounds for maximal modulations of such operators
T
, one could answer
1
positively by a limiting argument. In the present paper, we prove bounds for maximal polynomial modulations of such operators
T
, extending the results from
[
Zor21
]
to the anisotropic setting, and improve the constant in the estimates compared to
[
Zor21
]
.

Fix
\mathbf{d}\in\mathbb{N}
and a vector of exponents
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dotsc,\alpha_{\mathbf{d}})\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbf{d}}
with
\alpha_{1}\leq\dotsc\leq\alpha_{\mathbf{d}}
. Denote its length by
|\alpha|=\sum_{i=1}^{\mathbf{d}}\alpha_{i}
. The anisotropic dilations with exponent
\alpha
are

\delta_{r}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{\mathbf{d}})=(r^{\alpha_{1}}x_{1},\dotsc,r^{\alpha_{\mathbf{d}}}x_{\mathbf{d}})\,

and the anisotropic distance function is defined as

\rho(x)=\inf\{r>0\,:\,|\delta_{r^{-1}}(x)|\leq 1\}\,.

A
Calderón-Zygmund kernel with anisotropic scaling
is a tempered distribution
K
agreeing on
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}\setminus\{0\}
with a function
K:\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{C}
such that for some
A>0

(1)

\displaystyle|K(x)|

\displaystyle\leq A\,\rho(x)^{-|\alpha|}\,,

(2)

\displaystyle|K(x)-K(x^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq A\,\frac{\rho(x-x^{\prime})}{\rho(x)^{|\alpha|+1}}\ \text{if}\ 2\rho(x-x^{\prime})\leq\rho(x)\,,

(3)

\displaystyle|\hat{K}(\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq A\,.

The Calderón-Zygmund operator
T
associated to
K
is the operator defined on Schwartz functions
f
by
Tf=K*f
. Note that (
3
) implies
\|Tf\|_{2}\leq A\|f\|_{2}
for
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}})
.

An
admissible decomposition
of a Calderón-Zygmund kernel
K
with anisotropic scaling is a decomposition
K=\sum_{s\in\mathbb{Z}}K_{s}
, where for all
s\in\mathbb{Z}
, the function
K_{s}
is supported in
\{x\,:\,2^{s-1}/4\leq\rho(x)\leq 2^{s}/2\}
and is Lipschitz continuous with constant
A2^{-(|\alpha|+1)s}
.
Given an admissible decomposition, we define for
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}})
the maximally truncated singular integral

(4)

\displaystyle R^{K}f(x)=\sup_{\underline{\sigma}\leq\overline{\sigma}\leq 0}\left|\sum_{s=\underline{\sigma}}^{\overline{\sigma}}\int_{y\in x+\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}}}K_{s}(x-y)f(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right|

and the maximal average

(5)

\displaystyle M^{K}f(x)=\sup_{s}\int_{y\in x+\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}}}|K_{s}(x-y)||f(y)|\,\mathrm{d}y\,.

It is not hard to explicitly construct admissible decompositions, however for proving estimates for
M^{K}
and
R^{K}
it is often helpful to have flexibility in the choice of
K_{s}
.

Fix
d\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\mathcal{Q}
be the vector space of all real polynomials on
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}
of degree at most
d
without constant term. We define for
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}})

(6)

\displaystyle T^{\mathcal{Q}}f(x)=\sup_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\sup_{0<\underline{R}<\overline{R}}\left|\int_{y\in x+\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}}:\underline{R}<\rho(x-y)<\overline{R}}K(x-y)e^{iQ(y)}f(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right|\,.

Here and in all similar integrals we identify
\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}}
with
[-1/2,1/2)^{\mathbf{d}}
.
Then we have the following new theorem, which extends the main result from
[
Zor21
]
to Calderón-Zygmund operators with anisotropic scaling and improves the constants.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
1\leq p<2
and let
K
be a Calderón-Zygmund kernel with anisotropic scaling, as defined in (
1
) - (
3
) with constant
A
, with admissible decomposition
K=\sum_{s\in\mathbb{Z}}K_{s}
.
Then the operator
T^{\mathcal{Q}}
defined by (
6
) is bounded from
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}})
into
L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}})
with

(7)

\|T^{\mathcal{Q}}\|_{2\to p}\lesssim_{\alpha,d,p}(\|R^{K}\|_{2\to 2}+\|M^{K}\|_{2\to 2})\log^{2}(e+\frac{A}{\|M^{K}\|_{2\to 2}+\|R^{K}\|_{2\to 2}})\,.

Remark 1
.

The conclusion of
Theorem

1.1
still holds if
K
is merely Hölder continuous. In fact, the same proof works for Hölder continuous kernels, one merely has to change some exponents.

We explain how this estimate
“improves the constant”
. It is possible to adapt the proof in
[
Zor21
]
to the anisotropic setting and to make all constants in the proof explicit, this yields the estimate
\|T^{\mathcal{Q}}\|_{2\to 2}\lesssim A
, and thus
\|T^{\mathcal{Q}}\|_{2\to p}\lesssim A
.
To compare this to estimate (
7
), note that the function
x\log^{2}(e+A/x)
is increasing in
x>0
for all
A>0
, and that it holds in general that
\|M^{K}\|_{2\to 2}+\|R^{K}\|_{2\to 2}\lesssim A
. Thus
Theorem

1.1
also implies the estimate
\|T^{\mathcal{Q}}\|_{2\to p}\lesssim A
for
p<2
, and improves it if better estimates for
M^{K}
and
R^{K}
are available.

We now give some examples where this is the case.
Studying
1
, the situation we are mostly interested in is when
K
approximates a Hilbert transform along a homogeneous curve. In this case,
R^{K}
and
M^{K}
are bounded uniformly, because the maximal functions associated to the Hilbert transform along a homogeneous curve are bounded, see
[
SW78
]
. Hence (
7
) implies that in this case, the dependence of
\|T^{\mathcal{Q}}\|_{2\to p}
on the Lipschitz constant
A
improves from linear to squared logarithmic.
The same applies for singular integrals supported on paraboloids, as discussed in
[
PY19
]
.
We refer to
[
Chr+99
]
for a general criterion for boundedness of the maximal functions associated to singular integrals on submanifolds. For all singular integrals supported on submanifolds for which the associated maximal average and maximally truncated operator are bounded, (
7
) implies the same improvement of the constant from linear in the Lipschitz constant
A
to squared logarithmic in
A
.

To answer
1
positively, one would have to show uniform estimates for operators
T
approximating a Hilbert transform along the parabola. Our improvement of the constant can thus be viewed as partial progress towards
1
. In the Appendix we have worked out how this progress manifests itself in the roughness of the singular integrals for which one can show boundedness of maximal modulations.
We deduce from
Theorem

1.1
that a version of
1
can be answered positively where
H^{P}
is replaced by a slightly less singular operator. While
H_{P}
is supported on the parabola and thus looks like
\delta(y-x^{2})
on a line
x=const.
, our operator will have a singularity
(|y-x^{2}|\log^{3+\varepsilon}(1/|y-x^{2}|))^{-1}
. In this context one should think of
\delta
as having a singularity of order
-1
, so this misses the required singularity only by a logarithmic factor. As another application we show that maximal polynomial modulations of homogeneous, odd Calderón-Zygmund kernels with anisotropic scaling are bounded, under a very weak assumption on the modulus of continuity. All of these results are new.

We note that in all of our results, the only information used about
H_{P}
is the boundedness of the maximal average and the maximally truncated operator associated to
H_{P}
. This is in contrast to the results in
[
PY19
]
, which use algebraic properties of the paraboloid. It would be interesting to find ways to exploit the algebraic structure of the parabola to make further progress on
1
.
One consequence of the algebraic structure is the following simple observation: For every polynomial
Q
of two variables, there exists a polynomial
Q^{\prime}
of one variable whose coefficients depend only on
x
,
y
and
Q
, such that
Q(x-t,y-t^{2})=Q^{\prime}(x-t)
. Thus, to answer
1
positively, it would suffice to show a version of
Theorem

1.1
with uniform constants under the assumption that the polynomials only depend on
x
. However, this assumption does not seem to allow for improvements in the conclusion of
Theorem

1.1
or simplifications in the proof.

1
has been investigated by other authors, we list some relevant references. Since
H_{P}
is a convolution operator, there exists a Fourier multiplier
m_{P}
such that
H_{P}f=(m_{P}\hat{f}){\check{}}
. The anisotropic dilation symmetry of
H_{P}
implies that
m_{P}
is homogeneous of degree zero with respect to an anisotropic scaling.
Roos
[
Roo19
]
made some progress on
1
by showing that, if
m
is homogeneous of degree zero with respect to an anisotropic scaling and sufficiently smooth, and
Tf=(m\hat{f}){\check{}}
, then
T^{\mathcal{Q}_{1}}
is bounded from
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}})
into
L^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}})
. Unfortunately, the multiplier
m_{P}
is not smooth enough to directly apply Roos’s result to it. We note that our
Theorem

1.1
generalizes Roos’s result to polynomial modulations, by the Hörmander-Mikhlin theorem. Another related paper is
[
Ram21
]
, where the Fourier multiplier
m_{P}
is restricted to lines and uniform bounds for maximal modulations of the resulting Fourier multipliers on
\mathbb{R}
are shown. In
[
Guo+17
]
the authors obtain
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
bounds for partial suprema of
H_{P}M_{Q}f
. Finally we refer to
[
Mna22
]
, where convergence of analogues of Fourier series for certain perturbations of the trigonometric system is proved, using an abstraction of the methods in
[
Zor21
]
in a similar spirit as in the present paper.

1.1.
Outline of the Proof

We now give an overview of the proof of
Theorem

1.1
. To show the
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}})
estimate, we fix
\lambda
and estimate the measure of the set
\{x\,:\,|T^{\mathcal{Q}}f(x)|>\lambda\}
.
The first step of the argument, given in Section 2, is a discretization of
T^{\mathcal{Q}}
: The continuous truncation is replaced by a discrete truncation and the suprema are eliminated using stopping time functions.
The next step is a decomposition of the operator.
First, we decompose the operator according to so called tiles, which are localized on some
D
-adic cube and on which the stopping time functions are localized in a small subset of the space of polynomials
\mathcal{Q}
.
Next, the set of tiles is organized into certain collections of tiles. Estimates for the parts of the operator corresponding to these collections are shown in Section 3 and Section 4. We give short sketches of the corresponding arguments in the beginning of these sections. All of this is combined in Section 5, where the main theorem
is derived from the results of Sections 2 to 4.
The basic structure of the proof is due to Charles Fefferman
[
Fef73
]
, it was adapted to polynomial modulations by Lie
[
Lie09
]
,
[
Lie20
]
. Our argument is based on the paper
[
Zor21
]
.

In large parts of the proof we follow
[
Zor21
]
, with some straightforward modifications to translate the proof to the anisotropic setting. The whole proof in
[
Zor21
]
could be modified in this manner, yielding
L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}})
boundedness of
T^{\mathcal{Q}}
for
1<p<\infty
with
\|T^{\mathcal{Q}}\|_{p\to p}\lesssim A
. However, we were not able to obtain the improved constants of
Theorem

1.1
in all steps of the proof. Because of that, our proof is organized differently in some parts:

•

we use an exceptional set instead of the
“stopping generations”
in
[
Zor21
]
, Lemma 3.3. This simplifies the arguments in Section 3 compared to those in
[
Zor21
]
, and in particular makes it possible to obtain the improved constants there.

•

we estimate boundary parts of trees (see Section 3.2) using an exceptional set argument.

This is done similarly in
[
Lie09
]
,
[
Fef73
]
, essentially we revert the changes in the argument that were introduced by Lie
[
Lie20
]
to show strong
L^{2}
estimates without interpolation.

The new contributions in the present paper are that we adapt the proof to the anisotropic setting, and that we keep track of the constants in the proof of
Theorem

1.1
and improve them by combining the estimates from
[
Zor21
]
with simpler estimates using the maximal functions
M^{K}
and
R^{K}
.
The basic idea of this argument is as follows: In the proof of
Theorem

1.1
, the operator is decomposed according to some parameter
n
. The different parts are then estimated with exponential decay in
n
, so that the proof can be completed by summing a geometric series. The constant in these exponentially decaying estimates is proportional to
A
. We show in addition different estimates without decay in
n
, using the maximal functions
M^{K}
and
R^{K}
. The constants in these estimates are of size
\|M^{K}\|_{2\to 2}+\|R^{K}\|_{2\to 2}
. Using the better one of these estimates one obtains the logarithmic upper bound. The square of the logarithm arises because the first estimate actually has slightly slower than exponential decay in
n
.

1.2.
Notation

We use the notation
X\lesssim Y
if there exists a constant
C
such that
X\leq CY
, we write
X\gtrsim Y
if
Y\lesssim X
and
X\sim Y
means that
X\lesssim Y
and
Y\lesssim X
. If not stated otherwise, the implicit constant
C
depends only on the exponents
\alpha
, the dimension
\mathbf{d}
and the degree
d
of the polynomials. Sometimes we use
C
to explicitly denote a constant which only depends on
\alpha
,
\mathbf{d}
and
d
, similarly
\varepsilon
is a small positive number depending only on
\alpha
,
\mathbf{d}
and
d
. The letter
A
is used throughout to denote the constant of the Calderón-Zygmund kernel
K
, as defined in (
1
), (
2
) and (
3
).

B_{\rho}(x,r)=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}\,:\,\rho(x-y)\leq r\}
denotes a ball with respect to the anisotropic distance function
\rho
, while
B(x,r)
denotes standard euclidean balls on
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}
. Similarly, the diameter of a set with respect to the anisotropic distance function is abbreviated as
\diam_{\rho}E=\sup_{x,y\in E}\rho(x-y)
.

The letter
M
always stands for the anisotropic version of the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function. The
q
-maximal function is defined as
M^{q}f=(M|f|^{q})^{1/q}
. By the
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}})
boundedness of
M
for
p>1
, the
q
-maximal function is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}})
for
p>q
.

We denote by
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}})
the space of Schwartz functions on
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}
and use the notation
e(x)=\exp(ix)
.

2.
Discretization

In this section, we carry out some basic reductions and decompose the operator
T^{\mathcal{Q}}
. We first discretize the truncation in the definition of
T^{\mathcal{Q}}
and replace it by a smooth truncation. Then we construct the collection of all tiles, and use it to decompose the operator into pieces
T_{\mathfrak{p}}
, where
\mathfrak{p}
runs through the set of tiles. Finally, we organize the set of all tiles into antichains and forests.
We mostly follow Section 2 of
[
Zor21
]
and adapt it to the anisotropic setting. The main difference is that we do not use stopping generations. Instead we use an exceptional set, see
Lemma

2.6
. This also simplifies the decomposition we obtain.

We note that there is a small inconsistency in the notation: In the definition of an admissible decomposition the kernels
K_{s}
have support in
\{\rho(x)\sim 2^{s}\}
, while in (
10
) below the support is in
\{\rho(x)\sim D^{s}\}
. Given an admissible decomposition
K_{s}
one can build kernels satisfying (
10
) with
D=2^{l}
by taking sums of
l
consecutive
K_{s}
. The maximal functions
M^{K}
and
R^{K}
for the latter decomposition are clearly bounded by those for the former, hence this change does not cause any problems. We have chosen to accept this inconsistency, because it allows us to state
Theorem

1.1
without reference to the constant
D
, which is fixed only later.

2.1.
Discretization by Scale

Fix a large
D\in\mathbb{N}
such that
D
is a power of two.
D
will be specified in the proof of
Corollary

2.2
.
Let
K
be a kernel with admissible decomposition
K=\sum_{k}\tilde{K}_{k}
. Let
D=2^{l}
and define

K_{s}=\sum_{k=l(s-1)}^{ls-1}\tilde{K}_{k}\,.

Then
K_{s}
satisfies

(8)

\displaystyle|K_{s}(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim AD^{-s|\alpha|}\,,

(9)

\displaystyle|K_{s}(x)-K_{s}(x^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\lesssim A\frac{\rho(x-x^{\prime})}{D^{s(1+|\alpha|)}}\,,\ \text{and}

(10)

\displaystyle\spt K_{s}\subset\{x\,

\displaystyle:\,D^{s-1}/8\leq\rho(x)\leq D^{s}/4\}\,.

It further holds that

\displaystyle T^{\mathcal{Q}}f(x)\lesssim\sup_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\sup_{\underline{\sigma}\leq\overline{\sigma}\leq 0}\left|\sum_{s=\underline{\sigma}}^{\overline{\sigma}}\int_{x+\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}}}K_{s}(x-y)e^{iQ(y)}f(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right|+M^{K}f(x)\,.

Thus, it suffices to estimate

(11)

\sup_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\sup_{\underline{\sigma}\leq\overline{\sigma}\leq 0}\left|\sum_{s=\underline{\sigma}}^{\overline{\sigma}}\int_{x+\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}}}K_{s}(x-y)e^{iQ(y)}f(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right|

where
K_{s}
satisfies the properties (
8
) to (
10
).

By continuity in
Q
of the integral in (
11
), we can restrict the supremum in
Q
to a countable dense subset of
\mathcal{Q}
. By the monotone convergence theorem, we can further restrict the suprema to a finite set of polynomials
Q
and scales
\underline{\sigma}\leq\overline{\sigma}
. Then the suprema become maxima, hence there exist measurable stopping time functions
Q:\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}\to\mathcal{Q}
,
\underline{\sigma}:\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}\to\mathbb{Z}
and
\overline{\sigma}:\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}\to\mathbb{Z}
with
\overline{\sigma}\geq\underline{\sigma}
, taking only finitely many values, such that the restricted supremum equals the absolute value of

T^{Q,\sigma}f(x)=\sum_{\underline{\sigma}(x)\leq s\leq\overline{\sigma}(x)}\int_{x+\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}}}K_{s}(x-y)e(Q_{x}(x)-Q_{x}(y))f(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,.

We conclude that it is enough to show bounds for the operator
T^{Q,\sigma}
which do not depend on
Q
or
\sigma
. In the following, we fix
Q
and
\sigma
and set
s_{\min}=\min\underline{\sigma}
and
s_{\max}=\max\overline{\sigma}
.

2.2.
D
-adic Cubes

The anisotropic
D
-adic grid
\mathcal{D}
is defined as the union

\mathcal{D}=\bigcup_{s\leq 0}\mathcal{D}_{s}

where

\mathcal{D}_{s}=\{x+\delta_{D^{s}}([0,1)^{\mathbf{d}})\,:\,x\in\delta_{D^{s}}(\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbf{d}})\cap[0,1)^{\mathbf{d}}\}\,.

The elements of
\mathcal{D}
are called
D
-adic cubes. If
I\in\mathcal{D}_{s}
, then we say that
I
has scale
s
and write
s(I)=s
. Two
D
-adic cubes are either disjoint or one is contained in the other. Every
D
-adic cube
I
of scale
s
is contained in a unique
D
-adic cube
\hat{I}
of scale
s+1
. Two
D
-adic cubes
I
and
J
are neighbours if they have the same scale and their closures intersect. We denote the union of
I
and all its neighbours by
I^{*}
. A box
L
is a cartesian product of intervals. Its center is denoted by
c(L)
, and we write

rL=c(L)+\delta_{r}(L-c(L))

for the box
L
anisotropically dilated by a factor of
r
.

2.3.
Tiles

Recall that
\mathcal{Q}
is the vector space of all polynomials of degree at most
d
on
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}
with vanishing constant term. We will decompose
\mathcal{Q}
into
uncertainty regions
. They play the role of the dyadic frequency intervals in the proofs
[
Fef73
]
,
[
LT00
]
of Carleson’s theorem. In the setting of polynomial modulations, one has to choose the decomposition of the space of polynomials differently depending on the spatial location.

For a bounded subset
I\subset\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}
with nonempty interior, define a norm
\|\cdot\|_{I}
on
\mathcal{Q}
by

\|Q\|_{I}=\sup_{x,x^{\prime}\in I}|Q(x)-Q(x^{\prime})|\,.

We will write
B_{I}(Q,r)=\{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Q}\,:\,\|Q^{\prime}-Q\|_{I}\leq r\}
for the closed balls with respect to this norm.
The importance of the norms
\|\cdot\|_{I}
lies in the fact that one can obtain upper bounds for oscillatory integrals with phase
Q
over boxes
I
with power decay in
\|Q\|_{I}
, see
Lemma

3.2
.

The following lemma allows us to compare the norms
\|\cdot\|_{B}
for nested balls
B
:

Lemma 2.1
.

If
Q\in\mathcal{Q}
and
B(x,r)\subset B(x,R)\subset\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}
are euclidean balls then

\|Q\|_{B(x,R)}\lesssim(R/r)^{d}\|Q\|_{B(x,r)}

and

\|Q\|_{B(x,r)}\lesssim r/R\|Q\|_{B(x,R)}\,.

If
B_{\rho}(x,r)\subset B_{\rho}(x,R)\subset\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}
are anisotropic balls then

\|Q\|_{B_{\rho}(x,R)}\lesssim(R/r)^{d\alpha_{\mathbf{d}}}\|Q\|_{B_{\rho}(x,r)}

and

\|Q\|_{B_{\rho}(x,r)}\lesssim(r/R)^{\alpha_{1}}\|Q\|_{B_{\rho}(x,R)}\,.

Proof.

We start with the first inequality. Applying a translation and dilation, we can assume
x=0
,
r=1
and
\|Q\|_{B(x,1)}=1
. Then we have to show that

\sup_{B(0,R)}|Q(x)|\lesssim R^{d}\,.

The coefficients of
Q
can be expressed as linear combinations of finitely many values of
Q
in the unit ball, see e.g.
[
Nic72
]
. Hence the coefficients are
\lesssim_{d,\mathbf{d}}1
, which implies the estimate. For the second inequality we assume similarly
x=0
,
R=1
and
\|Q\|_{B(x,1)}=1
. Then we have to show

\sup_{B(0,r)}|Q(x)|\lesssim r\,,

which is true by the same argument. The estimates in the anisotropic setting follows from those in the isotropic setting and the inclusions
B_{\rho}(0,R)\subset B(0,R^{\alpha_{\mathbf{d}}})
for
R\geq 1
and
B_{\rho}(0,r)\subset B(0,r^{\alpha_{1}})
for
r\leq 1
.
∎

Corollary 2.2

(
[
Zor21
]
, Cor. 2.9)
.

If
D
is chosen sufficiently large then for every
I\in\mathcal{D}
and
Q\in\mathcal{Q}
it holds that

(12)

\displaystyle\|Q\|_{\hat{I}}\geq 10^{4}\|Q\|_{I}\,.

Proof.

By precomposing
Q
with an anisotropic dilation and a translation, we can assume that
\hat{I}=[0,1)^{\mathbf{d}}
. Then by
Lemma

2.1

\displaystyle\|Q\|_{I}

\displaystyle\leq\|Q\|_{B(c(I),\mathbf{d}^{1/2}D^{-1})}\lesssim D^{-1}\|Q\|_{B(c(I),\mathbf{d}^{1/2})}

\displaystyle\leq D^{-1}\|Q\|_{B(c([0,1)^{\mathbf{d}}),2\mathbf{d}^{1/2})}\lesssim D^{-1}\|Q\|_{B(c([0,1)^{\mathbf{d}}),1/2)}\leq D^{-1}\|Q\|_{[0,1)^{\mathbf{d}}}\,.

Hence (
12
) holds for sufficiently large
D
depending only on
d
,
\mathbf{d}
.
∎

Definition 2.3

(
[
Zor21
]
, Def. 2.11)

A

\mathfrak{p}
consists of a

of

We are now ready to construct the collection of all tiles.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
Zor21
]
, Lem. 2.12)
.

There exist collections of pairs
\mathfrak{P}_{I}
, indexed by
I\in\mathcal{D}
with
s_{\min}\leq s(I)\leq s_{\max}
, such that the following holds:

(1)

For every pair
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{P}_{I}
there is a central polynomial
Q_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mathcal{Q}(\mathfrak{p})
with

B_{I}(Q_{\mathfrak{p}},0.2)\subset\mathcal{Q}(\mathfrak{p})\subset B_{I}(Q_{\mathfrak{p}},1)\,

(2)

for each
I\in\mathcal{D}
the uncertainty regions
\mathcal{Q}(\mathfrak{p})
,
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{P}_{I}
form a disjoint cover of
\mathcal{Q}

(3)

if
I\subset I^{\prime}
,
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{P}_{I}
and
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{P}_{I^{\prime}}
, then either
\mathcal{Q}(\mathfrak{p})\cap\mathcal{Q}(\mathfrak{p}^{\prime})=\emptyset
or
\mathcal{Q}(\mathfrak{p}^{\prime})\subset\mathcal{Q}(\mathfrak{p})

Proof.

For each
D
-adic cube
I\in\mathcal{D}
choose a maximal set of polynomials
\mathcal{Q}_{I}\subset\mathcal{Q}
with the property that
\|Q-Q^{\prime}\|_{I}\geq 0.7
for all

Then the balls
B_{I}(Q,0.3)
,
Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{I}
are disjoint and the balls

,

cover
\mathcal{Q}
by maximality.
Pick an enumeration
\mathcal{Q}_{I}=\{Q_{j}\,:\,j\in\mathbb{N}\}
and set

\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}(I,Q_{j})=B_{I}(Q_{j},0.7)\setminus\bigcup_{k<j}\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}(I,Q_{k})\setminus\bigcup_{k\neq j}B(Q_{k},0.3)\,.

This yields a partition

\mathcal{Q}=\bigcup_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{I}}\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}(I,Q)

of
\mathcal{Q}
with
B_{I}(Q,0.3)\subset\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}(I,Q)\subset B_{I}(Q,0.7)
for all
Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{I}
.

The partitions
\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}(I,Q)
satisfy
(1)
and
(2)
. We now modify them in order of decreasing scale of
I
to obtain condition
(3)
. For
s=s_{\max}
, we set
\mathcal{Q}(I,Q)=\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}(I,Q)
. Now assume that we have constructed partitions
\mathcal{Q}(I,Q)
,
Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{I}
of
\mathcal{Q}
satisfying condition
(3)
for all
I\in\mathcal{D}
of scale larger than
s
. Let
I\in\mathcal{D}_{s}
. Define for

\mathcal{Q}(I,Q)=\bigcup_{\hat{Q}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\hat{I}}\cap\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}(I,Q)}\mathcal{Q}(\hat{I},\hat{Q})\,.

Then the sets
\mathcal{Q}(I,Q)
,
Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{I}
form a partition of
\mathcal{Q}
and condition
(3)
holds for this
I
. It remains to check that
(1)
still holds, i.e. that
B_{I}(Q,0.2)\subset\mathcal{Q}(I,Q)\subset B_{I}(Q,1)
.
Pick
\tilde{Q}\in\mathcal{Q}(I,Q)
. There is some
\hat{Q}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\hat{I}}\cap\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}(I,Q)
with

. Hence

\displaystyle\|\tilde{Q}-Q\|_{I}

\displaystyle\leq\|\hat{Q}-Q\|_{I}+\|\tilde{Q}-\hat{Q}\|_{I}

\displaystyle\leq\|\hat{Q}-Q\|_{I}+10^{-4}\|\tilde{Q}-\hat{Q}\|_{\hat{I}}\leq 0.7+10^{-4}\leq 1\,.

On the other hand, if
\tilde{Q}\notin\mathcal{Q}(I,Q)
, then there is some
\hat{Q}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\hat{I}}\setminus\tilde{\mathcal{Q}}(I,Q)
with
\tilde{Q}\in\mathcal{Q}(\hat{I},\hat{Q})
. Therefore

\displaystyle\|\tilde{Q}-Q\|_{I}\geq\|\hat{Q}-Q\|_{I}-\|\tilde{Q}-\hat{Q}\|_{I}\geq\|\hat{Q}-Q\|_{I}-10^{-4}\|\tilde{Q}-\hat{Q}\|_{\hat{I}}\geq 0.2\,.

Thus, the partitions
\mathcal{Q}(I,Q)
satisfy conditions
(1)
to
(3)
.
∎

From now on, we fix collections of pairs
\mathfrak{P}_{I}
as in
Lemma

2.4
and define the set of all tiles

\mathfrak{P}=\bigcup_{I\in\mathcal{D},s_{\min}\leq s(I)\leq s_{\max}}\mathfrak{P}_{I}\,.

For each
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{P}
, we further fix a choice of central polynomial
Q_{\mathfrak{p}}
such that condition
(1)
of
Lemma

2.4
holds.

Given a pair
\mathfrak{p}
, we define

E(\mathfrak{p})=\{x\in I_{\mathfrak{p}}\,:\,Q_{x}\in\mathcal{Q}(\mathfrak{p})\,\text{and}\,\underline{\sigma}(x)\leq s(\mathfrak{p})\leq\overline{\sigma}(x)\}

and

\overline{E}(\mathfrak{p})=\{x\in I_{\mathfrak{p}}\,:\,Q_{x}\in\mathcal{Q}(\mathfrak{p})\}\,.

The larger sets
\overline{E}(\mathfrak{p})
will be used in arguments exploiting the smallness of the density of tiles in the proof of
Lemma

3.4
and the proofs of
Lemma

4.5
. There it will be necessary to control the measure of the set of all

with

, regardless of

.

The operator associated to the tile
\mathfrak{p}
is defined as

T_{\mathfrak{p}}f(x)=\mathbf{1}_{E(\mathfrak{p})}(x)\int_{x+\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}}}K_{s(\mathfrak{p})}(x-y)e(Q_{x}(x)-Q_{x}(y))f(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,,

its adjoint is

T_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}g(y)=\int_{y+\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}}}e(-Q_{x}(x)+Q_{x}(y))\overline{K_{s(\mathfrak{p})}(x-y)}(\mathbf{1}_{E(\mathfrak{p})}g)(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,.

Clearly

vanishes outside of

, and for all functions

it holds that

\spt T_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}g\subset I_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\,.

Given any subset
\mathfrak{S}\subset\mathfrak{P}
we define

T_{\mathfrak{S}}=\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{S}}T_{\mathfrak{p}}\,.

Then we have in particular that
T_{\mathbb{T}}^{Q,\sigma}=T_{\mathfrak{P}}
.

2.4.
Organizing the Set of Tiles

Now we further organize the set of all tiles into forests and antichains, following
[
Zor21
]
.

We define a partial order
\leq
on the set of all tiles, similarly to the order introduced in
[
Fef73
]
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
,
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
be pairs. We say that

•

\mathfrak{p}<\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
if
I_{\mathfrak{p}}\subsetneq I_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}
and
\mathcal{Q}(\mathfrak{p}^{\prime})\subset\mathcal{Q}(\mathfrak{p})

•

\mathfrak{p}\leq\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
if
I_{\mathfrak{p}}\subset I_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}
and
\mathcal{Q}(\mathfrak{p}^{\prime})\subset\mathcal{Q}(\mathfrak{p})
.

Corollary

A.1
and
Theorem

1.1
are proven by estimating the distribution function
\lvert\{\lvert T_{\mathfrak{P}}f(x)\rvert>\lambda\}\rvert
of
T_{\mathfrak{P}}f
, see Section 5. From now on we will therefore fix the parameter
\lambda>10e
.

Lemma 2.6
.

There exists an exceptional set
E_{1}\subset\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}}
which is a disjoint union of
D
-adic cubes, such that the following holds:

(1)

|E_{1}|\lesssim\lambda^{-2}

(2)

Let

\mathfrak{P}_{good}=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{P}\,:\,I_{\mathfrak{p}}\not\subset E_{1}\}\,.

For every
n\geq 1
the set of tiles

\mathfrak{M}_{n}=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{P}_{good}\,\text{maximal w.r.t. $\leq$ s.t.}\,|\overline{E}(\mathfrak{p})|/|I_{\mathfrak{p}}|\geq 2^{-n}\}

satisfies

(13)

\bigg\|\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{M}_{n}}\mathbf{1}_{I_{\mathfrak{p}}}\bigg\|_{\infty}\lesssim 2^{n}\log(n+1)\log(\lambda)\,.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{\mathfrak{M}}_{n}
be the collection of maximal tiles
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{P}
with respect to the ordering
\leq
satisfying
|\overline{E}(\mathfrak{p})|/|I_{\mathfrak{p}}|\geq 2^{-n}
.
Then the sets
\overline{E}(\mathfrak{p})
,
\mathfrak{p}\in\tilde{\mathfrak{M}}_{n}
are pairwise disjoint: If
x\in\overline{E}(\mathfrak{p})\cap\overline{E}(\mathfrak{p}^{\prime})
then
x\in I_{\mathfrak{p}}\cap I_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}
, thus without loss of generality
I_{\mathfrak{p}}\subset I_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}
. But also
Q_{x}\in\mathcal{Q}(\mathfrak{p})\cap\mathcal{Q}(\mathfrak{p}^{\prime})
, which implies
\mathcal{Q}(\mathfrak{p}^{\prime})\subset\mathcal{Q}(\mathfrak{p})
. Therefore
\mathfrak{p}\leq\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
and by maximality
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
. The disjointness implies the Carleson packing condition

\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in\tilde{\mathfrak{M}}_{n}\,:\,I_{\mathfrak{p}}\subset J}|I_{\mathfrak{p}}|\leq 2^{n}\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in\tilde{\mathfrak{M}}_{n}\,:\,I_{\mathfrak{p}}\subset J}|\overline{E}(\mathfrak{p})|\leq 2^{n}|J|

for all
J\in\mathcal{D}
.
Let
C
be a large constant to be fixed later. Consider

E_{1}=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\{x\in[0,1)^{\mathbf{d}}\,:\,\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in\tilde{\mathfrak{M}}_{n}}\mathbf{1}_{I_{\mathfrak{p}}}(x)\geq C2^{n}\log(n+1)\}\,.

By the John-Nirenberg inequality it holds that

|E_{1}|\leq e^{2}\sum_{n\geq 1}\exp(-\frac{1}{2e}\frac{C2^{n}\log(n+1)}{2^{n}})=e^{2}\sum_{n\geq 1}(n+1)^{-C/(2e)}\lesssim 2^{-C/(2e)}\,.

Choosing
C=4e\log(\lambda)/\log(2)
, we obtain
|E_{1}|\lesssim\lambda^{-2}
. Furthermore, the set
E_{1}
satisfies condition
(2)
. Indeed, with the definition of
\mathfrak{M}_{n}
there, it holds that
\mathfrak{M}_{n}\subset\tilde{\mathfrak{M}}_{n}
. Assume that there was some point
x
with

\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{M}_{n}}\mathbf{1}_{I_{\mathfrak{p}}}(x)>C2^{n}\log(n+1)\,.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{M}_{n}
be a tile with minimal
I_{\mathfrak{p}}
such that
x\in I_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Then the above estimate holds on
I_{\mathfrak{p}}
, and since
\mathfrak{M}_{n}\subset\tilde{\mathfrak{M}}_{n}
it follows that
I_{\mathfrak{p}}\subset E_{1}
, a contradiction to
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{P}_{good}
.
∎

Definition 2.8

(
[

Lemma 2.12

(
[

]
, Prop. 3.22)

For every

Proof.

We first want to restrict to the simpler set

\mathfrak{p}_{0}<\dotsb<\mathfrak{p}_{n}
in
\mathfrak{h}_{n}\setminus\mathfrak{C}_{n}

\mathfrak{A}_{i}

Assume that there was a chain
\mathfrak{p}_{0}<\dotsb<\mathfrak{p}_{n}

\mathfrak{h}_{n}
, there exists a tile
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{P}_{good}

. Therefore, one needs at most

which implies by (

satisfies

.

.

a\leq Ca^{\dim\mathcal{Q}}\leq 2^{n}|\overline{E}(a\mathfrak{p}^{\prime})|/|I_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}|\leq 2^{n}\,.

Hence we have for all
Q\in\mathcal{Q}(100\mathfrak{m})
that

\displaystyle\|Q_{\mathfrak{p}_{0}}-Q\|_{I_{\mathfrak{p}_{0}}}

\displaystyle\leq\|Q_{\mathfrak{p}_{0}}-Q_{\mathfrak{p}_{n}}\|_{I_{\mathfrak{p}_{0}}}+\|Q_{\mathfrak{p}_{n}}-Q_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}\|_{I_{\mathfrak{p}_{0}}}+\|Q_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}-Q_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime\prime}}\|_{I_{\mathfrak{p}_{0}}}

3.

In this section, we deal with the parts of the operators

and

corresponding to antichains and boundary parts of trees.

3.1.
Antichains

We first prove estimates for the operators
T_{\mathfrak{A}}
associated to antichains
\mathfrak{A}
. We briefly explain the strategy. For an antichain
\mathfrak{A}
, the sets
E(\mathfrak{p}),\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{A}
are disjoint. Therefore the pointwise estimate
|T_{\mathfrak{A}}f|\lesssim M^{K}f
holds. With this estimate, one can control one operator
T_{\mathfrak{A}}
, it is however not good enough to control the countable collection of operators
T_{\mathfrak{A}_{n,j}}
. Since there are
O(n^{2}\log\log(\lambda)^{2})
antichains of each density parameter
n
, one can control all antichains by showing exponential decay of the operator norm of
T_{\mathfrak{A}_{n,j}}
in
n
. In
[
Zor21
]
, this is done as follows: One derives a bound for
T_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}T^{*}_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}
which decays like a power of the Fourier separation
\Delta(\mathfrak{p}_{1},Q_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}})
. If this separation is of size
\gtrsim 2^{\varepsilon n}
, this bound is good enough.
On the other hand, the measure of the union of all
E(\mathfrak{p}_{2})
with smaller separation is exponentially small since
\dens(\mathfrak{A}_{n,j})\lesssim 2^{-n}
.
This strategy carries over to the anisotropic setting without problems, and yields the estimate
\|T_{\mathfrak{A}}\|_{2\to 2}\lesssim A2^{-\varepsilon n}
.
To obtain the improved constants in
Theorem

1.1
, we will use this estimate for
n>n_{0}
and the maximal function argument described above at density
n_{0}
, where we choose
n_{0}\sim\log(A/(\|M^{K}\|_{2\to 2}+\|R^{K}\|_{2\to 2})
. The square of the logarithm in
Theorem

1.1
arises then because there are
O(n_{0}^{2})
antichains of density
n_{0}
.

We now carry out the details of this argument. We start with the maximal function estimate:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be an antichain. Then it holds that

\|T_{\mathfrak{A}}\|_{2\to 2}\lesssim\|M^{K}\|_{2\to 2}\,.

Proof.

Since
\mathfrak{A}
is an antichain the sets
E(\mathfrak{p})
,
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{A}
are pairwise disjoint. Therefore

\displaystyle|T_{\mathfrak{A}}f(x)|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{A}}\mathbf{1}_{E(\mathfrak{p})}(x)\int K_{s(\mathfrak{p})}(x-y)e(Q_{x}(x)-Q_{x}(y))f(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{A}}\mathbf{1}_{E(\mathfrak{p})}(x)\int|K_{s(\mathfrak{p})}(x-y)||f(y)|\,\mathrm{d}y\lesssim M^{K}f(x)\,.\qed

To show estimates with decay in the density parameter
n
, we need the following van der Corput type estimate for oscillatory integrals with polynomial phase:

Lemma 3.2

(
[
Zor21
]
, Lem. A1)
.

Let
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}\to\mathbb{C}
be a measurable function supported in
\delta_{r}(J)
for an isotropic cube

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}}e^{iQ(x)}\psi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\lesssim\sup_{|y|<\Delta^{-1/d}l(J)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}}|\psi(x)-\psi(x-\delta_{r}(y))|\,\mathrm{d}x\,,

where
l(J)
is the side length of
J
and
\Delta=\|Q\|_{\delta_{r}(J)}+1
.

Proof.

The lemma follows from
[
Zor21
]
, Lemma A1, after precomposing everything with
\delta_{r}
.
∎

We have the following estimate for separated tiles:

Lemma 3.3

(
[
Zor21
]
, Lem. 4.1)

Let
\mathfrak{p}_{1},\mathfrak{p}_{2}\in\mathfrak{P}
with

Then

(20)

\left|\int T_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}^{*}g_{1}\overline{T_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}^{*}g_{2}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right|\lesssim A^{2}\,\frac{\Delta(\mathfrak{p}_{1},Q_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}})^{-1/(\alpha_{\mathbf{d}}d)}}{|I_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}|}\int_{E(\mathfrak{p}_{1})}|g_{1}|\,\mathrm{d}x_{1}\int_{E(\mathfrak{p}_{2})}|g_{2}|\,\mathrm{d}x_{2}\,.

Proof.

Writing out the left hand side and pulling absolute values inside yields

(21)

\displaystyle\int_{E(\mathfrak{p}_{1})}\int_{E(\mathfrak{p}_{2})}g_{1}(x_{1})g_{2}(x_{2})\left|\int e((Q_{x_{1}}-Q_{x_{2}})(y))\overline{K_{s(\mathfrak{p}_{1})}(x_{1}-y)}K_{s(\mathfrak{p}_{2})}(x_{2}-y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right|\mathrm{d}x_{1}\mathrm{d}x_{2}\,.

We want to estimate the inner integral using
Lemma

3.2
. By the assumptions (
8
) and (
9
), the product
\psi(y)=\overline{K_{s(\mathfrak{p}_{1})}(x_{1}-y)}K_{s(\mathfrak{p}_{2})}(x_{2}-y)

Furthermore, since

\displaystyle\lesssim D^{|\alpha|s(\mathfrak{p}_{1})}\,A^{2}\,D^{s(\mathfrak{p}_{1})}\,\frac{\sup_{|y|<3\Delta^{-1/d}}\rho(y)}{D^{s(\mathfrak{p}_{1})(1+|\alpha|)+s(\mathfrak{p}_{2})|\alpha|}}

\displaystyle\lesssim A^{2}\,\frac{\Delta^{-1/(\alpha_{\mathbf{d}}d)}}{|I_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}|}\,,

The following lemma will allow us to exploit the small density of the antichains

Lemma 3.4

(
[

]

There exists

Note that the statement holds for

Fix

to be chosen later. Since the sets

are disjoint and contained in

-adic cubes

for some

\displaystyle\|\tilde{Q}-Q_{\mathfrak{p}}\|_{I_{\mathfrak{p}}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\tilde{Q}-Q_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}\|_{I_{\mathfrak{p}}}+\|Q_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}-Q\|_{I_{\mathfrak{p}}}+\|Q-Q_{\mathfrak{p}}\|_{I_{\mathfrak{p}}}

\displaystyle\leq 10^{-4}(\|\tilde{Q}-Q_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}\|_{I_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}}+\|Q_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}-Q\|_{I_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}})+\|Q-Q_{\mathfrak{p}}\|_{I_{\mathfrak{p}}}

3.2.
Boundary Parts of Trees

Let
\mathfrak{T}
be a tree. Define its boundary part

\bd(\mathfrak{T})=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{T}\,:\,I_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\not\subset I_{\mathfrak{T}}\}\,.

We deal with the boundary parts of trees separately because we will later need that

is supported in

, which only holds after removing the boundary part from

Our treatment of the boundary parts follows the argument in
[

]
and
[
Lie09
]
, and differs from the arguments in
[
Lie20
]
,
[
Zor21
]
.
The strategy is as follows: The boundary tiles in a tree that are

, so we need to estimate only

layers and can pack the rest into an exceptional set.

We have the following estimates for the operator associated to the set of all boundary tiles in a forests

:

Lemma 3.6
.

Let

and

be given. Consider the collection of tiles

. There exists an exceptional set

\|\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}}\setminus E_{2}}T_{\mathfrak{S}}\|_{2\to 2}\lesssim\begin{cases}n\log(\lambda)A2^{-n\varepsilon}&\text{if $n>n_{0}$}\\
n\log(\lambda)\|M^{K}\|_{2\to 2}&\text{if $n=n_{0}$}\end{cases}\,.

For
k\geq 1
and a tree
\mathfrak{T}
, let

be the set of tiles

with

Tiles

in separated trees are also not comparable.
Indeed, else we had without loss of generality that

Then, by separation and the fact that

Since

We conclude that the set

4.
Trees and Forests

In this section, we prove estimates for operators
T_{\mathfrak{T}}
and
T_{\mathfrak{F}}
corresponding to trees
\mathfrak{T}
or forests
\mathfrak{F}
.
The basic idea is that, given a tree
\mathfrak{T}
and
x\in E(\mathfrak{p})
for some tile
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{T}
, the phase
Q_{x}
is
“close”
to
Q_{\mathfrak{T}}
.
If one replaces
Q_{x}
by
Q_{\mathfrak{T}}
, the resulting operator is bounded by
R^{K}(M_{-Q_{\mathfrak{T}}}f)
. On the other hand, the definition of a tree is chosen exactly so that the error in this replacement is bounded by the maximal average
M^{K}
. Thus the operator associated to a single tree is bounded on
L^{2}
, with norm bounded by
\|M^{K}\|_{2\to 2}+\|R^{K}\|_{2\to 2}
. Using boundedness of the nontangential version of the maximally truncated singular integral, we then show estimates for single trees with exponential decay in the density parameter
n
when
n>n_{0}
, however the constant in these estimates is of order
A
.

Next, we bound the operators associated to forests. Using an orthogonality argument, we can control collections of trees with spatially disjoint tops, so called rows. After that we show an estimate for
T_{\mathfrak{T}_{1}}T_{\mathfrak{T}_{2}}^{*}
for separated trees
\mathfrak{T}_{1}
,
\mathfrak{T}_{2}
with power decay in the separation, and use it to show a similar bound for
T_{\mathfrak{R}_{1}}T_{\mathfrak{R}_{2}}^{*}
for separated rows.
With these two ingredients, we can then control a whole
L^{\infty}
-forest by splitting it into rows and using that these rows are
2^{\gamma n}
-separated.
In this part of the proof the argument for separated rows,
Lemma

4.9
, has constants proportional to
A
. This is problematic in the case
n=n_{0}
, since there we want to obtain an estimate by
\|M^{K}\|_{2\to 2}+\|R^{K}\|_{2\to 2}
. However,
n_{0}
will be chosen sufficiently large so that the
2^{\gamma n_{0}}
separation of the trees cancels the constant
A
.

4.1.
Basic Estimates for Trees

As described above, the operators
T_{\mathfrak{T}}
are bounded on

with norms depending only on the norms of
M^{K}
and
R^{K}
:

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{T}\subset\mathfrak{P}
be a tree and assume that
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}})
. Then it holds that

\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}}}gT_{\mathfrak{T}}f\right|\lesssim(\|M^{K}\|_{2\to 2}+\|R^{K}\|_{2\to 2})\|f\|_{2}\|g\|_{2}\,.

Proof.

We fix
x
. Define

\sigma=\sigma(\mathfrak{T},x)=\{s\,:\,\exists\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{T},\,s(\mathfrak{p})=s,\,x\in E(\mathfrak{p})\}\,.

This is the set of all scales
s
for which there exists a tile contributing to
T_{\mathfrak{T}}f(x)
. It is a convex subset of
\mathbb{Z}
since
\mathfrak{T}
is convex and if
\mathfrak{p}\leq\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}\leq\mathfrak{p}^{\prime\prime}
then

We write

\displaystyle|T_{\mathfrak{T}}f(x)|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{s\in\sigma}\int e(-Q_{x}(y))K_{s}(x-y)f(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\sum_{s\in\sigma}\int e(-Q_{\mathfrak{T}}(y)+Q_{\mathfrak{T}}(x))K_{s}(x-y)f(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{s\in\sigma}\int|e(-Q_{\mathfrak{T}}(y)+Q_{\mathfrak{T}}(x)+Q_{x}(y)-Q_{x}(x))-1||K_{s}(x-y)||f(y)|\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\eqqcolon A(x)+B(x)\,.

The first term

is bounded by

. To treat

, note that if
K_{s}(x-y)\neq 0
, then

. Therefore, for all such

, it holds that

\displaystyle|e(-Q_{\mathfrak{T}}(y)+Q_{\mathfrak{T}}(x)+Q_{x}(y)-Q_{x}(x))-1|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|Q_{x}-Q_{\mathfrak{T}}\|_{B_{\rho}(x,D^{s}/4)}

\displaystyle\lesssim D^{\alpha_{1}(s-\max\sigma)}\|Q_{x}-Q_{\mathfrak{T}}\|_{B_{\rho}(x,D^{\max\sigma}/4)}\,,

where we applied

2.1
. There exists a tile
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{T}
of scale

. Using

once more, we can estimate

\|Q_{x}-Q_{\mathfrak{T}}\|_{B_{\rho}(x,D^{\max\sigma}/4)}\leq\|Q_{x}-Q_{\mathfrak{T}}\|_{I_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}}\lesssim\|Q_{x}-Q_{\mathfrak{T}}\|_{I_{\mathfrak{p}}}\leq 5\,.

Therefore we have

\displaystyle B(x)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s\in\sigma}D^{\alpha_{1}(s-\max\sigma)}\int|K_{s}(x-y)||f(y)|\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{s\leq\max\sigma}D^{\alpha_{1}(s-\max\sigma)}M^{K}f(x)\lesssim M^{K}f(x)\,.

This completes the proof.

Now we want to show estimates with decay in

. We will need the following well known result:

Lemma 4.2
.

The nontangential maximal function

R_{\mathcal{N}}^{K}f(x)=\sup_{\underline{\sigma}\leq\overline{\sigma}}\sup_{\rho(x-z)\leq CD^{\underline{\sigma}}}\left|\sum_{s=\underline{\sigma}}^{\overline{\sigma}}\int K_{s}(z-y)f(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right|

See
[

]

[

]

, where

\displaystyle+\sum_{s\in\sigma}\int|e(-Q_{\mathfrak{T}}(y)+Q_{\mathfrak{T}}(x)+Q_{x}(y)-Q_{x}(x))-1||K_{s}(x-y)||f(y)|\,\mathrm{d}y

with
K_{s}(x-y)\neq 0
, it holds that

\displaystyle|e(-Q_{\mathfrak{T}}(y)+Q_{\mathfrak{T}}(x)+Q_{x}(y)-Q_{x}(x))-1|\lesssim D^{\alpha_{1}(s-\max\sigma)}\,.

Combining this, the upper bound (
8
) for
K_{s}
, and the fact that
\mathcal{J}
is a partition of
\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}}
we obtain

\displaystyle C(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim A\sum_{s\in\sigma}D^{\alpha_{1}(s-\max\sigma)}D^{-s|\alpha|}\sum_{J\in\mathcal{J}\,:\,J\cap B_{\rho}(x,D^{s}/4)\neq\emptyset}\int_{J}|f(y)|\,\mathrm{d}y\,.

Note that this expression does not change upon replacing
|f|
with
P_{\mathcal{J}}|f|
.
If
J
intersects
B_{\rho}(x,D^{s}/4)
and
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{T}
is a tile of scale
s
with
x\in E(\mathfrak{p})
, then
J
also intersects
I_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}
. If
s(J)\geq s(\mathfrak{p})
then this implies
I_{\mathfrak{p}}\subset 3J
, which contradicts the definition of
\mathcal{J}
. Thus
J\subset I_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\subset B_{\rho}(x,CD^{s})
. As observed above, it holds that
\rho(x-z)\lesssim D^{s}
for all
z\in L
. We conclude that, for all
z\in L
and all
J\in\mathcal{J}
with
J\cap B_{\rho}(x,D^{s}/4)\neq\emptyset
, it holds that
J\subset B_{\rho}(z,CD^{s})
. Hence

\displaystyle C(x)\lesssim A\sum_{s\in\sigma}D^{\alpha_{1}(s-\max\sigma)}\inf_{z\in L}MP_{\mathcal{J}}|f|(z)\lesssim A\inf_{z\in L}MP_{\mathcal{J}}|f|(z)\,.

It remains to take care of the second term
B
. Denoting
h(y)=e(-Q_{\mathfrak{T}}(y))f(y)
, we have that

\displaystyle B(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{T}}\mathbf{1}_{E(\mathfrak{p})}\sum_{J\in\mathcal{J},J\subset 3I_{\mathfrak{p}}}\int_{J}K_{s(\mathfrak{p})}(x-y)(h(y)-\frac{1}{|J|}\int_{J}h(z)\,\mathrm{d}z)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{T}}\mathbf{1}_{E(\mathfrak{p})}\sum_{J\in\mathcal{J},J\subset 3I_{\mathfrak{p}}}\int_{J}(K_{s(\mathfrak{p})}(x-y)-\frac{1}{|J|}\int_{J}K_{s(\mathfrak{p})}(x-z)\,\mathrm{d}z)h(y)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq A\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{T}}\mathbf{1}_{E(\mathfrak{p})}\sum_{J\in\mathcal{J},J\subset 3I_{\mathfrak{p}}}\frac{\diam_{\rho}(J)}{D^{s(\mathfrak{p})|\alpha|+1}}\int_{J}|f(y)|\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq A\sum_{I\in\mathcal{H}}\mathbf{1}_{I}\sum_{J\in\mathcal{J},J\subset 3I}\frac{\diam_{\rho}(J)}{D^{s(I)|\alpha|+1}}\int_{J}P_{\mathcal{J}}|f|\,\mathrm{d}y\,,

where
\mathcal{H}=\{I_{\mathfrak{p}}\,:\,\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{T}\}
. Define
S
by

Sf(x)=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{H}}\mathbf{1}_{I}\sum_{J\in\mathcal{J},J\subset 3I}D^{s(J)-s(I)}\frac{1}{|I|}\int_{J}f\,\mathrm{d}y\,.

Using that
\diam_{\rho}(J)\lesssim D^{s(J)}
, we can then estimate

B(x)\lesssim ASP_{\mathcal{J}}|f|(x)\,.

Note that
SP_{\mathcal{J}}|f|
is constant on all cubes
L\in\mathcal{L}
, and therefore we have that

SP_{\mathcal{J}}|f|(x)=\inf_{z\in L}SP_{\mathcal{J}}|f|(z)
. Finally, we show that
\|S\|_{2\to 2}\lesssim 1
. It holds that

\displaystyle\left|\int gSf\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{J\in\mathcal{J}}\int_{J}|f(y)|\,\mathrm{d}y\sum_{I\in\mathcal{H},J\subset 3I}D^{s(J)-s(I)}\frac{1}{|I|}\int_{I}|g(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{J\in\mathcal{J}}\int_{J}|f(y)|Mg(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\sum_{I\in\mathcal{H},J\subset 3I}D^{s(J)-s(I)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int|f(y)|Mg(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\lesssim\|f\|_{2}\|g\|_{2}\,.

This completes the proof.
∎

Finally, we use
Lemma

4.4
to obtain an estimate for trees with decay in the density
\dens(\mathfrak{T})
.

Lemma 4.5

(
[
Zor21
]
, Cor. 5.10)
.

Let
\mathfrak{T}\subset\mathfrak{P}
be a tree. Then it holds that

\|T_{\mathfrak{T}}\|_{2\to 2}\lesssim A\dens(\mathfrak{T})^{1/2}\,.

Proof.

Let
L\in\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{T})
.
By definition of
\mathcal{L}
, there exists some tile
\mathfrak{p}_{L}\in\mathfrak{T}
with
I_{\mathfrak{p}_{L}}\subset\hat{L}
.
Let
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
be the unique tile with
\mathfrak{p}_{L}\leq\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
,
I_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}=\hat{L}
and
Q_{\mathfrak{T}}\in\mathcal{Q}(\mathfrak{p}^{\prime})
. Then it holds that
10\mathfrak{p}_{L}\leq 10\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
: This is obvious if
\mathfrak{p}_{L}=\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
. If
\mathfrak{p}_{L}\neq\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
then
I_{\mathfrak{p}_{L}}\subsetneq I_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}
, which implies by
Corollary

2.2
that for all
Q\in\mathcal{Q}(10\mathfrak{p}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\|Q-Q_{\mathfrak{p}_{L}}\|_{I_{\mathfrak{p}_{L}}}

\displaystyle\leq\|Q-Q_{\mathfrak{T}}\|_{I_{\mathfrak{p}_{L}}}+\|Q_{\mathfrak{T}}-Q_{\mathfrak{p}_{L}}\|_{I_{\mathfrak{p}_{L}}}

\displaystyle\leq 10^{-4}\|Q-Q_{\mathfrak{T}}\|_{I_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}}+\|Q_{\mathfrak{T}}-Q_{\mathfrak{p}_{L}}\|_{I_{\mathfrak{p}_{L}}}

\displaystyle\leq 10^{-4}(\|Q-Q_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}\|_{I_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}}+\|Q_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}-Q_{\mathfrak{T}}\|_{I_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}})+\|Q_{\mathfrak{T}}-Q_{\mathfrak{p}_{L}}\|_{I_{\mathfrak{p}_{L}}}

\displaystyle\leq 10^{-4}(10+1)+1\leq 10\,.

Furthermore, every tile
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{T}
with
L\cap I_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq\emptyset
satisfies
10\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}\leq\mathfrak{p}
. Indeed, if
I_{\mathfrak{p}}\cap L\neq\emptyset
, then
I_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}=\hat{L}\subset I_{\mathfrak{p}}
and for all
Q\in\mathcal{Q}(\mathfrak{p})
it holds that

\displaystyle\|Q-Q_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}\|_{I_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq\|Q-Q_{\mathfrak{p}}\|_{I_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}}+\|Q_{\mathfrak{p}}-Q_{\mathfrak{T}}\|_{I_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}}+\|Q_{\mathfrak{T}}-Q_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}\|_{I_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq\|Q-Q_{\mathfrak{p}}\|_{I_{\mathfrak{p}}}+\|Q_{\mathfrak{p}}-Q_{\mathfrak{T}}\|_{I_{\mathfrak{p}}}+4\leq 1+4+4\leq 10\,.

Let
E(L)=\cup_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{T}}E(\mathfrak{p})\cap L
. Since
10\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}\leq\mathfrak{p}
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{T}
with
E(\mathfrak{p})\cap L\neq\emptyset
, it holds that
E(L)\subset\overline{E}(10\mathfrak{p}^{\prime})
.
Thus

\displaystyle|E(L)|\leq|\overline{E}(10\mathfrak{p}^{\prime})|\leq 10^{\dim\mathcal{Q}}\dens(\mathfrak{p}_{L})|I_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}|\lesssim\dens(\mathfrak{T})|L|\,.

We write
E=\cup_{L}E(L)
. Then
Lemma

4.4
implies that

\displaystyle\left|\int gT_{\mathfrak{T}}f\right|=\left|\int g\mathbf{1}_{E}T_{\mathfrak{T}}f\right|\lesssim A\|f\|_{2}\|P_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{1}_{E}g)\|_{2}\,.

The estimates for the size of
E(L)
allow us to obtain an improved bound for
P_{\mathcal{L}}\mathbf{1}_{E}
:

\displaystyle\|P_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{1}_{E}g)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{L}|L|\left(\frac{1}{|L|}\int_{L}\mathbf{1}_{E}|g|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{L}|L|\left(\frac{1}{|L|}\int_{L}\mathbf{1}_{E}^{2}\right)\left(\frac{1}{|L|}\int_{L}|g|^{2}\right)\leq\dens(\mathfrak{T})\|g\|_{2}^{2}\,.

This completes the proof.
∎

4.2.
Separated Trees

Definition 4.6
.

A tree
\mathfrak{T}
is called
normal
if for every
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{T}
it holds that
I_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\subset I_{\mathfrak{T}}
.

If
\mathfrak{T}
is normal then
T_{\mathfrak{T}}^{*}f
is supported in
I_{\mathfrak{T}}
for all
f
, and if
\mathfrak{T}
is any tree then
\mathfrak{T}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{T}\setminus\bd(\mathfrak{T})
is a normal tree.

We have the following improved estimate for
T_{\mathfrak{T}_{1}}T_{\mathfrak{T}_{2}}^{*}
for separated normal trees
\mathfrak{T}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{T}_{2}
:

Lemma 4.7

(
[
Zor21
]
, Lem. 5.16)
.

There exists
\varepsilon>0
such that for any two
\Delta
-separated normal trees
\mathfrak{T}_{1}
,
\mathfrak{T}_{2}
, and all
g_{1},g_{2}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}})
it holds that

(22)

\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}T_{\mathfrak{T}_{1}}^{*}g_{1}\overline{T_{\mathfrak{T}_{2}}^{*}g_{2}}\right|\lesssim\Delta^{-\varepsilon}\prod_{j=1,2}\||T^{*}_{\mathfrak{T}_{j}}g_{j}|+AMg_{j}\|_{L^{2}(I_{\mathfrak{T}_{1}}\cap I_{\mathfrak{T}_{2}})}\,.

Proof.

The estimate holds without the factor
\Delta^{-\varepsilon}
, so we can assume that
\Delta\gg 1
. If
I_{\mathfrak{T}_{1}}
and
I_{\mathfrak{T}_{2}}
are disjoint the inequality is trivial, so we can further assume that
I_{0}\coloneqq I_{\mathfrak{T}_{1}}\subset I_{\mathfrak{T}_{2}}
and
\mathfrak{T}_{1}\neq\emptyset
. We denote
Q=Q_{\mathfrak{T}_{1}}-Q_{\mathfrak{T}_{2}}
and fix some
0<\eta<1
which will be specified later. Let

\mathfrak{S}=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{T}_{1}\cup\mathfrak{T}_{2}\,:\,\|Q\|_{I_{\mathfrak{p}}}\geq\Delta^{1-\eta}\}\,.

The
\Delta
-separation of
\mathfrak{T}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{T}_{2}
implies that every
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{T}_{1}
satisfies

\|Q\|_{I_{\mathfrak{p}}}\geq\|Q_{\mathfrak{p}}-Q_{\mathfrak{T}_{2}}\|_{I_{\mathfrak{p}}}-\|Q_{\mathfrak{p}}-Q_{\mathfrak{T}_{1}}\|_{I_{\mathfrak{p}}}\geq\Delta-1-4\,.

The same holds if
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{T}_{2}
with
I_{\mathfrak{p}}\subset I_{0}
, by the same argument. If
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{T}_{2}\cup\mathfrak{T}_{1}
with
I_{0}\subset I_{\mathfrak{p}}
, then in particular
I_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}\subset I_{\mathfrak{p}}
for each
\mathfrak{p}_{1}\in\mathfrak{T}_{1}
. Since
\mathfrak{T}_{1}
is not empty, we can pick one such
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
, and since
\|Q\|_{I}
is increasing in
I
, we conclude that
\|Q\|_{I_{\mathfrak{p}}}\geq\|Q\|_{I_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}}\geq\Delta-5
also for such
\mathfrak{p}
. Hence if
\Delta
is sufficiently large, then

I_{\mathfrak{p}}\cap I_{0}=\emptyset\ \text{for all $\mathfrak{p}\in(\mathfrak{T}_{1}\cup\mathfrak{T}_{2})\setminus\mathfrak{S}$.}

Define
\mathcal{J}=\{J\in\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{S})\,:\,J\subset I_{0}\}
. Since
\mathfrak{T}_{1}\subset\mathfrak{S}
, there is no
J\in\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{S})
with
I_{0}\subset J
. Hence
\mathcal{J}
is a partition of
I_{0}
. We claim that there exists a partition of unity
\chi_{J}
,
J\in\mathcal{J}
such that:

(1)

Each
\chi_{J}
is smooth on
I_{0}
,

(2)

\mathbf{1}_{I_{0}}=\sum_{J\in\mathcal{J}}\chi_{J}
,

(3)

|\chi_{J}(x)-\chi_{J}(y)|\lesssim D^{-s(J)}\rho(x-y)
for all
J\in\mathcal{J}
and
x,y\in I_{0}
,

(4)

\chi_{J}
is supported in
N(J)
, where
N(J)
is the union of all
D
-adic cubes
J^{\prime}
of scale
s(J)-1
whose closure intersects the closure of
J
.

The functions
\chi_{J}
will not be continuous outside of
I_{0}
but this will not cause any problems.

To show the existence of the partition of unity, note that two cubes
J,J^{\prime}\in\mathcal{J}
with
\overline{J}\cap\overline{J}^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
have scales differing by at most one. Indeed, assume that
s(J^{\prime})\geq s(J)+2
. There is a cube
I_{\mathfrak{p}}
,
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{S}
with
I_{\mathfrak{p}}\subset 100D\hat{J}
, by the definition of
\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{S})
. Since
s(J^{\prime})>s(\hat{J})
and
\overline{J}\cap\overline{J}^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
, it follows that
I_{\mathfrak{p}}\subset 100DJ^{\prime}
, a contradiction.
Using this fact, the construction of such a partition is standard, see for example
[
Gra14
]
, Appendix J.

Set
\Delta_{J}=\|Q\|_{J}
for
J\in\mathcal{J}
. For every
J\in\mathcal{J}
, there exists some
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{S}
with
100D\hat{J}\supset I_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Thus

(23)

\Delta_{J}=\|Q\|_{J}\gtrsim\|Q\|_{100D\hat{J}}\gtrsim\|Q\|_{I_{\mathfrak{p}}}\geq\Delta^{1-\eta}

for all
J\in\mathcal{J}
.

We now consider the contribution of
\mathfrak{S}
. The strategy of the argument is to prove Hölder continuity of
e(-Q_{\mathfrak{T}})T_{\mathfrak{T}}^{*}f
for trees
\mathfrak{T}
and then use
Lemma

3.2
.
For every tile
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{T}
and all
y,y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}}
, it holds that

\displaystyle|e(-Q_{\mathfrak{T}}(y))T_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}f(y)-e(-Q_{\mathfrak{T}}(y^{\prime}))T_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}f(y^{\prime})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg|\int e(-Q_{x}(x)+Q_{x}(y)-Q_{\mathfrak{T}}(y))\overline{K_{s(\mathfrak{p})}(x-y)}(\mathbf{1}_{E(\mathfrak{p})}f)(x)

\displaystyle-e(-Q_{x}(x)+Q_{x}(y^{\prime})-Q_{\mathfrak{T}}(y^{\prime}))\overline{K_{s(\mathfrak{p})}(x-y^{\prime})}(\mathbf{1}_{E(\mathfrak{p})}f)(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{E(\mathfrak{p})}|f(x)||e(Q_{x}(y)-Q_{x}(y^{\prime})-Q_{\mathfrak{T}}(y)+Q_{\mathfrak{T}}(y^{\prime}))\overline{K_{s(\mathfrak{p})}(x-y)}-\overline{K_{s(\mathfrak{p})}(x-y^{\prime})}|\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{E(\mathfrak{p})}|f(x)||e(Q_{x}(y)-Q_{x}(y^{\prime})-Q_{\mathfrak{T}}(y)+Q_{\mathfrak{T}}(y^{\prime}))-1||\overline{K_{s(\mathfrak{p})}(x-y)}|\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{E(\mathfrak{p})}|f(x)||\overline{K_{s(\mathfrak{p})}(x-y)}-\overline{K_{s(\mathfrak{p})}(x-y^{\prime})}|\,\mathrm{d}x

Let
y,y^{\prime}\in I_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}
. Then we can estimate using
Lemma

2.1

\displaystyle|Q_{x}(y)-Q_{x}(y^{\prime})-Q_{\mathfrak{T}}(y)+Q_{\mathfrak{T}}(y^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq\|Q_{x}-Q_{\mathfrak{T}}\|_{B_{\rho}(y,\rho(y-y^{\prime}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{\rho(y-y^{\prime})}{D^{s(\mathfrak{p})}}\right)^{\alpha_{1}}\|Q_{x}-Q_{\mathfrak{T}}\|_{B_{\rho}(y,D^{s(\mathfrak{p})})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{\rho(y-y^{\prime})}{D^{s(\mathfrak{p})}}\right)^{\alpha_{1}}\|Q_{x}-Q_{\mathfrak{T}}\|_{I_{\mathfrak{p}}}\lesssim\left(\frac{\rho(y-y^{\prime})}{D^{s(\mathfrak{p})}}\right)^{\alpha_{1}}\,.

Applying this and (
9
), we obtain for
y,y^{\prime}\in I_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}

\displaystyle|e(-Q_{\mathfrak{T}}(y))T_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}f(y)-e(-Q_{\mathfrak{T}}(y^{\prime}))T_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}f(y^{\prime})|\lesssim A\frac{\rho(y-y^{\prime})}{D^{s(\mathfrak{p})}}D^{-s(\mathfrak{p})|\alpha|}\int_{E(\mathfrak{p})}|f(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x\,.

Since
T_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}f
is supported in
I_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}
and vanishes on the boundary this estimate holds for all
y,y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}}
.

Next, suppose that
J\in\mathcal{D}
has the property that

(24)

\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{T},I_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\cap N(J)\neq\emptyset\implies s(\mathfrak{p})\geq s(J)\,.

Then, for
y,y^{\prime}\in N(J)
, we have that:

\displaystyle\quad|e(-Q_{\mathfrak{T}}(y))T_{\mathfrak{T}}^{*}f(y)-e(-Q_{\mathfrak{T}}(y^{\prime}))T_{\mathfrak{T}}^{*}f(y^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{T},I_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\cap N(J)\neq\emptyset}|e(-Q_{\mathfrak{T}}(y))T_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}f(y)-e(-Q_{\mathfrak{T}}(y^{\prime}))T_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}f(y^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\lesssim A\rho(y-y^{\prime})\sum_{s\geq s(J)}D^{-s(|\alpha|+1)}\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{T},s(\mathfrak{p})=s,I_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\cap N(J)\neq\emptyset}\int_{E(\mathfrak{p})}|f(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim A\rho(y-y^{\prime})\sum_{s\geq s(J)}D^{-s}\inf_{J}Mf

(25)

\displaystyle\lesssim A\frac{\rho(y-y^{\prime})}{D^{s(J)}}\inf_{J}Mf\,.

It follows that

(26)

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in N(J)}|T_{\mathfrak{T}}^{*}f(y)|\leq\inf_{y\in\frac{1}{2}J}|T^{*}_{\mathfrak{T}}f(y)|+CA\inf_{y\in J}Mf(y)\,.

We claim that there exists an absolute constant
s_{0}
such that:

(27)

\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{T}_{2}\setminus\mathfrak{S},J\in\mathcal{J},I_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\cap J\neq\emptyset\implies s(\mathfrak{p})\leq s(J)+s_{0}\,.

Suppose that
s(\mathfrak{p})>s(J)+s_{0}
. By the definition of
\mathcal{J}
, there exists some
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{S}
with
I_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}\subset 100D\hat{J}
. We further note that since
|D^{s_{0}-1}\hat{J}|<|I_{\mathfrak{p}}|
and
I_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\cap J\neq\emptyset
, it follows that
D^{s_{0}-1}\hat{J}\subset 10I_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
Hence

\displaystyle\Delta^{1-\eta}

\displaystyle>\|Q\|_{I_{\mathfrak{p}}}\gtrsim\|Q\|_{10I_{\mathfrak{p}}}\geq\|Q\|_{D^{s_{0}-1}\hat{J}}\gtrsim D^{\alpha_{1}s_{0}}\|Q\|_{100D\hat{J}}

\displaystyle\geq D^{\alpha_{1}s_{0}}\|Q\|_{I_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}}\geq D^{\alpha_{1}s_{0}}\Delta^{1-\eta}\,.

Choosing
s_{0}
large enough, we arrive at a contradiction and the claim follows.

Recall that if
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{T}_{2}\setminus\mathfrak{S}
, then
I_{\mathfrak{p}}\cap I_{0}=\emptyset
. Thus if
I_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\cap\frac{1}{2}J\neq\emptyset
for some
J\in\mathcal{J}
, then
s(\mathfrak{p})\geq s(J)
. Using this and the claim, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in\frac{1}{2}J}|T_{\mathfrak{T}_{2}\setminus\mathfrak{S}}^{*}g(y)|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{y\in\frac{1}{2}J}\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{T}_{2}\setminus\mathfrak{S},I_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\cap J\neq\emptyset}|T_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}g(y)|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{y\in\frac{1}{2}J}\sum_{s=s(J)}^{s(J)+s_{0}}\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{P},s(\mathfrak{p})=s}|T_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}g(y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim A\sum_{s=s(J)}^{s(J)+s_{0}}\sup_{y\in\frac{1}{2}J}D^{-s|\alpha|}\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{P},s(\mathfrak{p})=s}\int_{B_{\rho}(y,D^{s}/2)}|g\mathbf{1}_{E(\mathfrak{p})}|

\displaystyle\lesssim A(s_{0}+1)\inf_{y\in J}Mg(y)\,.

Note now that the set
\mathfrak{T}_{2}\cap\mathfrak{S}
is a tree with the property (
24
): If
I_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\cap N(J)\neq\emptyset
and
s(\mathfrak{p})<s(J)
, then
I_{\mathfrak{p}}\subset 100DJ
, contradicting the definition of
\mathcal{J}
. Thus we can apply (
26
) and obtain for all
J\in\mathcal{J}
:

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in N(J)}|T^{*}_{\mathfrak{T}_{2}\cap\mathfrak{S}}g(y)|

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{y\in\frac{1}{2}J}|T_{\mathfrak{T}_{2}\cap\mathfrak{S}}^{*}g(y)|+CA\inf_{y\in J}Mg(y)

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{y\in\frac{1}{2}J}|T^{*}_{\mathfrak{T}_{2}}g(y)|+\sup_{y\in\frac{1}{2}J}|T^{*}_{\mathfrak{T}_{2}\setminus\mathfrak{S}}g(y)|+CA\inf_{y\in J}Mg(y)

(28)

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{y\in\frac{1}{2}J}|T^{*}_{\mathfrak{T}_{2}}g(y)|+CA\inf_{y\in J}Mg(y)\,.

Set

h_{J}(y)=\chi_{J}(y)(e(-Q_{\mathfrak{T_{1}}}(y))T_{\mathfrak{T}_{1}}^{*}g_{1}(y))\cdot\overline{(e(-Q_{\mathfrak{T_{2}}}(y))T_{\mathfrak{T}_{2}\cap\mathfrak{S}}^{*}g_{2}(y))}\,.

Since
\mathfrak{T}_{1}
is contained in
\mathfrak{S}
it satisfies (
24
), hence we can also apply (
25
) and(
26
) to
\mathfrak{T}=\mathfrak{T}_{1}
. Combining this with (
25
) for
\mathfrak{T}=\mathfrak{S}\cap\mathfrak{T}_{2}
and (
28
) as well as the Lipschitz estimate for
\chi_{J}
yields for
y,y^{\prime}\in N(J)

(29)

|h_{J}(y)-h_{J}(y^{\prime})|\lesssim\frac{\rho(y-y^{\prime})}{D^{s(J)}}\prod_{j=1,2}(\inf_{\frac{1}{2}J}|T_{\mathfrak{T}_{j}}^{*}g_{j}|+A\inf_{J}Mg_{j})(\mathbf{1}_{N(J)}(y)+\mathbf{1}_{N(J)}(y^{\prime}))\,.

Since
T_{\mathfrak{T}_{i}}^{*}g_{i}
is continuous on
\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}}
for
i=1,2
,
T_{\mathfrak{T}_{1}}^{*}g_{1}
vanishes outside of
I_{0}
and
\chi_{J}
is continuous (and bounded) on
I_{0}
and vanishes outside of
N(J)
, the functions
h_{J}
are continuous on
\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}}
and supported in
N(J)
. Thus the Lipschitz estimate (
29
) holds in fact for all
y,y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}}
.

We can finally apply
Lemma

3.2
(viewing the sets
N(J)
as subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}
):

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}}}T_{\mathfrak{T}_{1}}^{*}g_{1}\overline{T_{\mathfrak{T}_{2}\cap\mathfrak{S}}^{*}g_{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{J\in\mathcal{J}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}}e(Q(y))h_{J}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{J\in\mathcal{J}}\Delta_{J}^{-1/(d\alpha_{\mathbf{d}})}|J|\prod_{j=1,2}(\inf_{\frac{1}{2}J}|T_{\mathfrak{T}_{j}}^{*}g_{j}|+A\inf_{J}Mg_{j})

\displaystyle\leq\Delta^{-(1-\eta)/(d\alpha_{\mathbf{d}})}\prod_{j=1,2}\||T_{\mathfrak{T}_{j}}^{*}g_{j}|+AMg_{j}\|_{L^{2}(I_{0})}\,.

The last step uses (
23
).

It remains to estimate the contribution of
\mathfrak{T}_{2}\setminus\mathfrak{S}
. Define
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}=\{J\in\mathcal{J}(\mathfrak{T}_{1})\,:\,J\subset I_{0}\}
. We claim that for some
s_{\Delta}
with
D^{s_{\Delta}}\sim\Delta^{\eta/(d\alpha_{\mathbf{d}})}
it holds that

(30)

\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{T}_{2}\setminus\mathfrak{S},J\in\mathcal{J}^{\prime},I_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\cap J\neq\emptyset\implies s(\mathfrak{p})\leq s(J)-s_{\Delta}\,.

Indeed, assume that
s(\mathfrak{p})>s(J)-s_{\Delta}
. The cube
D^{s_{\Delta}}I_{\mathfrak{p}}
is larger than
J
and intersects it. Thus, if
C
is choosen large enough, then
100D\hat{J}\subset CD^{s_{\Delta}}I_{\mathfrak{p}}
. On the other hand, there exists by the definition of
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}
some
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{T}_{1}
with
I_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}\subset 100D\hat{J}
. This gives, by the definition of
\mathfrak{S}
and
Lemma

2.1
:

\displaystyle\Delta^{1-\eta}>\|Q\|_{I_{\mathfrak{p}}}\gtrsim D^{-\alpha_{\mathbf{d}}ds_{\Delta}}\|Q\|_{CD^{s_{\Delta}}I_{\mathfrak{p}}}\gtrsim D^{-\alpha_{\mathbf{d}}ds_{\Delta}}\|Q\|_{I_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}}\geq D^{-\alpha_{\mathbf{d}}ds_{\Delta}}(\Delta-2)\,.

If the constant
c
in
D^{s_{\Delta}}\leq c\Delta^{\eta/(d\alpha_{\mathbf{d}})}
is chosen sufficiently small, this is a contradiction.

Note that
\mathfrak{T}_{2}\setminus\mathfrak{S}
is still a tree, since
\mathfrak{S}
is an up set.
Thus, by
Lemma

4.4
it holds that

\displaystyle\quad\left|\int T_{\mathfrak{T}_{1}}^{*}g_{1}\overline{T_{\mathfrak{T}_{2}\setminus\mathfrak{S}}^{*}g_{2}}\right|\lesssim A\|g_{1}\mathbf{1}_{I_{0}}\|_{2}\|P_{\mathcal{J}^{\prime}}|T_{\mathfrak{T}_{2}\setminus\mathfrak{S}}^{*}g_{2}|\|_{2}\,.

Using (
30
) we have

\displaystyle\|P_{\mathcal{J}^{\prime}}|T_{\mathfrak{T}_{2}\setminus\mathfrak{S}}^{*}g_{2}|\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s\geq s_{\Delta}}\left(\sum_{J\in\mathcal{J}^{\prime}}|J|^{-1}\left|\int_{J}\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathfrak{T}_{2}\setminus\mathfrak{S}:s(\mathfrak{p})=s(J)-s,I_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\cap J\neq\emptyset}T_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}g_{2}\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq A\sum_{s\geq s_{\Delta}}\left(\sum_{J\in\mathcal{J}^{\prime}}|J|^{-1}\left|\int_{J}Mg_{2}\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}_{s(J)-s},I\cap I_{0}=\emptyset,I^{*}\cap J\neq\emptyset}\mathbf{1}_{I^{*}}\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq A\sum_{s\geq s_{\Delta}}\left(\sum_{J\in\mathcal{J}^{\prime}}\int_{J}(Mg_{2})^{2}|J|^{-1}\int_{J}\left(\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}_{s(J)-s},I\cap I_{0}=\emptyset,I^{*}\cap J\neq\emptyset}\mathbf{1}_{I^{*}}\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}\,.

The cubes

\{I^{*}\,:\,I\in\mathcal{D}_{s(J)-s},I\cap I_{0}=\emptyset,I^{*}\cap J\neq\emptyset\}

have bounded overlap and cover a set of size
\lesssim|J|D^{-\alpha_{1}s}
. Thus we can further estimate the
s
-sum by

\displaystyle\sum_{s\geq s_{\Delta}}D^{-\alpha_{1}s/2}\|\mathbf{1}_{I_{0}}Mg_{2}\|_{2}\lesssim D^{-\alpha_{1}s_{\Delta}/2}\|\mathbf{1}_{I_{0}}Mg_{2}\|_{2}\lesssim\Delta^{-\alpha_{1}\eta/(2d\alpha_{\mathbf{d}})}\|\mathbf{1}_{I_{0}}Mg_{2}\|_{2}\,.

This completes the estimate of the contribution of
\mathfrak{T}_{2}\setminus\mathfrak{S}
. Finally, set
\eta=2/(2+\alpha_{1})
to obtain (
22
) with
\varepsilon=\alpha_{1}/((2+\alpha_{1})\alpha_{\mathbf{d}}d)
.
∎

4.3.
Rows of Trees

Definition 4.8
.

A
row
is a union of normal trees with pairwise disjoint spatial cubes.

Lemma

4.7
implies an estimate for
T_{\mathfrak{R}_{1}}T_{\mathfrak{R}_{2}}^{*}
for separated rows
\mathfrak{R}_{1}
,
\mathfrak{R}_{2}
with decay in the separation:

Lemma 4.9

(
[
Zor21
]
, Lem. 5.26)
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}_{1}
,
\mathfrak{R}_{2}
be rows such that the trees in
\mathfrak{R}_{1}
are
\Delta
-separated from the trees in
\mathfrak{R}_{2}
. Then for any
g_{1}
,
g_{2}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}})
, it holds that

\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}}}T_{\mathfrak{R}_{1}}^{*}g_{1}\overline{T_{\mathfrak{R}_{2}}^{*}g_{2}}\right|\lesssim A^{2}\Delta^{-\varepsilon}\|g_{1}\|_{2}\|g_{2}\|_{2}\,.

Proof.

Denote by
S_{\mathfrak{T}}
the operator
|T_{\mathfrak{T}}^{*}|+AM
. These operators are bounded on
L^{2}
with norm
\lesssim A
for all trees
\mathfrak{T}
, by
Lemma

4.4
. Using this and
Lemma

4.7
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\left|\int T^{*}_{\mathfrak{R}_{1}}g_{1}\overline{T_{\mathfrak{R}_{2}}^{*}g_{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\mathfrak{T}_{1}\in\mathfrak{R}_{1},\,\mathfrak{T}_{2}\in\mathfrak{R}_{2}}\left|\int T^{*}_{\mathfrak{T}_{1}}g_{1}\overline{T_{\mathfrak{T}_{2}}^{*}g_{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\Delta^{-\varepsilon}\sum_{\mathfrak{T}_{1}\in\mathfrak{R}_{1},\,\mathfrak{T}_{2}\in\mathfrak{R}_{2}}\|S_{\mathfrak{T}_{1}}\mathbf{1}_{I_{\mathfrak{T}_{1}}}g_{1}\|_{L^{2}(I_{\mathfrak{T}_{1}}\cap I_{\mathfrak{T}_{2}})}\|S_{\mathfrak{T}_{2}}\mathbf{1}_{I_{\mathfrak{T}_{2}}}g_{2}\|_{L^{2}(I_{\mathfrak{T}_{1}}\cap I_{\mathfrak{T}_{2}})}

\displaystyle\leq\Delta^{-\varepsilon}\prod_{j=1,2}\left(\sum_{\mathfrak{T}_{1}\in\mathfrak{R}_{1},\,\mathfrak{T}_{2}\in\mathfrak{R}_{2}}\|S_{\mathfrak{T}_{j}}\mathbf{1}_{I_{\mathfrak{T}_{j}}}g_{j}\|_{L^{2}(I_{\mathfrak{T}_{1}}\cap I_{\mathfrak{T}_{2}})}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\Delta^{-\varepsilon}\prod_{j=1,2}\left(\sum_{\mathfrak{T}_{j}\in\mathfrak{R}_{j}}\|S_{\mathfrak{T}_{j}}\mathbf{1}_{I_{\mathfrak{T}_{j}}}g_{j}\|_{L^{2}(I_{\mathfrak{T}_{j}})}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim A^{2}\Delta^{-\varepsilon}\left(\sum_{\mathfrak{T}_{1}\in\mathfrak{R}_{1}}\|\mathbf{1}_{I_{\mathfrak{T}_{1}}}g_{1}\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{\mathfrak{T}_{2}\in\mathfrak{R}_{2}}\|\mathbf{1}_{I_{\mathfrak{T}_{2}}}g_{1}\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq A^{2}\Delta^{-\varepsilon}\|g_{1}\|_{2}\|g_{2}\|_{2}\,.

Here the third step follows from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the fourth and last step follow from the disjointness of the spatial cubes
I_{\mathfrak{T}}
for
\mathfrak{T}\in\mathfrak{R}_{j}
.
∎

4.4.
Forests

Finally, we estimate the contribution of forests of normal trees using an orthogonality argument. All trees can be assumed to be normal, since we already estimated the contribution of boundary parts of trees in
Section

3.2
. We fix
n
and drop it from the notation.

Lemma 4.10

(
[
Zor21
]
, Prop. 5.27)
.

Let
\mathfrak{N}_{j,l}=\mathfrak{T}_{j,l}\setminus\bd(\mathfrak{T}_{j,l})
and set
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}_{j}=\cup_{l}\mathfrak{N}_{j,l}
. If
n>n_{0}
, then it holds that

\|T_{\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}_{j}}\|_{2\to 2}\lesssim A2^{-n/2}

and if
n=n_{0}\geq\frac{2}{\log(2)}\log(A/(\|R^{K}\|_{2\to 2}+\|M^{K}\|_{2\to 2}))
, then it holds that

\|T_{\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}_{j}}\|_{2\to 2}\lesssim\|R^{K}\|_{2\to 2}+\|M^{K}\|_{2\to 2}\,.

Proof.

We subdivide the forest
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}_{j}
into rows
\mathfrak{R}_{m}
using the following procedure: If
\mathfrak{R}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{R}_{i}
have been selected,
\mathfrak{R}_{i+1}
is a maximal union of trees
\mathfrak{N}_{j,l}
such that the cubes
I_{\mathfrak{N}_{j,l}}
are maximal among the spatial cubes of not yet selected trees. Since the cubes
I_{\mathfrak{N}_{j,l}}
have overlap bounded by
2^{n}\log(n+1)\log(\lambda)
we obtain
\lesssim 2^{2n}\log(\lambda)
rows. By
Lemma

4.1
and
Lemma

4.5
, it holds that

\|T_{\mathfrak{N}_{j,l}}\|_{2\to 2}\lesssim\begin{cases}A2^{-n/2}&\text{if $n>n_{0}$}\\
\|R^{K}\|_{2\to 2}+\|M^{K}\|_{2\to 2}&\text{if $n=n_{0}$}\end{cases}\eqqcolon C(n)\,.

We have that
T_{\mathfrak{N}_{j,l}}^{*}f=T_{\mathfrak{N}_{j,l}}^{*}(\mathbf{1}_{I_{\mathfrak{N}_{j,l}}}f)
. Hence, by the disjointness of the spatial cubes in a row:

\displaystyle\|T_{\mathfrak{R}_{m}}^{*}f\|^{2}_{2}=\sum_{l}\|T_{\mathfrak{N}_{j,l}}^{*}(\mathbf{1}_{I_{\mathfrak{N}_{j,l}}}f)\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim C(n)^{2}\sum_{l}\|\mathbf{1}_{I_{\mathfrak{N}_{j,l}}}f\|_{2}^{2}\leq C(n)^{2}\|f\|_{2}^{2}\,.

It follows that for all
m
we have
\|T_{\mathfrak{R}_{m}}\|_{2\to 2}\lesssim C(n)
.
Note that the sets
E(\mathfrak{p})
for
\mathfrak{p}
in different rows are disjoint, since the rows are separated.
This implies that
T^{*}_{\mathfrak{R}_{m}}T_{\mathfrak{R}_{m^{\prime}}}=0
for
m\neq m^{\prime}
. Thus the subspaces
\overline{\im T_{\mathfrak{R}_{m}}}
for distinct
m
are orthogonal. Denote by
\pi_{m}
the orthogonal projection onto
\overline{\im T_{\mathfrak{R}_{m}}}
, so that
T^{*}_{\mathfrak{R}_{m}}=T^{*}_{\mathfrak{R}_{m}}\circ\pi_{m}
for each
m
. Then

\displaystyle\|\sum_{m}T^{*}_{\mathfrak{R}_{m}}f\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\sum_{m}T^{*}_{\mathfrak{R}_{m}}\pi_{m}f\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m}\|T^{*}_{\mathfrak{R}_{m}}\pi_{m}f\|_{2}^{2}+\sum_{m\neq m^{\prime}}\int\pi_{m}(f)T_{\mathfrak{R}_{m}}T^{*}_{\mathfrak{R}_{m^{\prime}}}\pi_{m^{\prime}}(f)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\lesssim C(n)^{2}\sum_{m}\|\pi_{m}f\|_{2}^{2}+A^{2}2^{-\gamma n\varepsilon}\sum_{m\neq m^{\prime}}\|\pi_{m}f\|_{2}\|\pi_{m^{\prime}}f\|_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim C(n)^{2}\|f\|_{2}^{2}+A^{2}2^{2n}\log(\lambda)2^{-\gamma n\varepsilon}\|f\|_{2}^{2}\,.

Here we used the
2^{\gamma n}
-separation of the rows
\mathfrak{R}_{m}
and
Lemma

4.9
to estimate the
m\neq m^{\prime}
terms. The last inequality holds since there are
\lesssim 2^{2n}\log(\lambda)
rows. Choosing
\gamma=\log\log(\lambda)/(\varepsilon\log 2)+3/\varepsilon
, this is smaller than

(C(n)^{2}+A^{2}2^{-n})\|f\|_{2}^{2}\,.

In both cases
n=n_{0}
and
n>n_{0}
, this implies the claimed estimates.
∎

5.
Proof of the Main Theorem

We now prove
Theorem

1.1
using the results from Sections 2 to 4.

Proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

Fix
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}})
with
\|f\|_{2}\leq 1
and
\lambda>10e
.
By the reductions in Section 2, it suffices to estimate
T_{\mathfrak{P}}f
. We do this by showing bounds for the distribution function
|\{|T_{\mathfrak{P}}f(x)|>\lambda\}|
.

By
Lemma

2.6
, there exists an exceptional set
E_{1}
with
|E_{1}|\lesssim\lambda^{-2}
and

(31)

\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}}\setminus E_{1}}T_{\mathfrak{P}}=\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}}\setminus E_{1}}\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{C(\gamma n^{2}+\gamma n\log\log\lambda)}T_{\mathfrak{A}_{n,j}}+\sum_{j=1}^{C(n+\log\log\lambda)}T_{\mathfrak{F}_{n,j}}\right)\,.

Lemma

3.1
yields that
\|T_{\mathfrak{A}_{n,j}}\|_{2\to 2}\lesssim\|M^{K}\|
for
n=n_{0}
and
Lemma

3.5
implies the estimate
\|T_{\mathfrak{A}_{n,j}}\|_{2\to 2}\lesssim A2^{-n\varepsilon}
for
n>n_{0}
. We choose
n_{0}
as the closest integer to

100\varepsilon^{-1}\log(e+\frac{A}{\|M^{K}\|+\|R^{K}\|})\,.

It holds that
\|R^{K}\|+\|M^{K}\|\lesssim A
, by
Lemma

4.2
and since
M^{K}\leq AM
.
Using this and
\gamma\sim\log\log\lambda
, we find

\left\|\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}\sum_{j=1}^{C(\gamma n^{2}+\gamma n\log\log\lambda)}T_{\mathfrak{A}_{n,j}}\right\|_{2\to 2}\lesssim(\|M^{K}\|+\|R^{K}\|)\log^{2}(e+\frac{A}{\|M^{K}\|+\|R^{K}\|})(\log\log\lambda)^{2}\,.

Next, we split the second summand in (
31
) into forests of normal trees and boundary parts of trees:

\sum_{j=1}^{C(n+\log\log\lambda)}T_{\mathfrak{F}_{n,j}}=\sum_{j=1}^{C(n+\log\log\lambda)}T_{\mathfrak{F}_{n,j}^{\prime}}+\sum_{j=1}^{C(n+\log\log\lambda)}T_{\cup_{l}\bd\mathfrak{T}_{n,j,l}}\,.

For the boundary parts of trees we apply
Lemma

3.6
. We obtain exceptional sets
E_{2}(n,j)
such that
|E_{2}(n,j)|\lesssim 2^{-n}\lambda^{-2}
and

\|\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}}\setminus E_{2}(n,j)}T_{\cup_{l}\bd\mathfrak{T}_{n,j,l}}\|_{2\to 2}\lesssim\begin{cases}n\log(\lambda)A2^{-n\varepsilon}&\text{if $n>n_{0}$}\\
n\log(\lambda)\|M^{K}\|&\text{if $n=n_{0}$}\end{cases}\,.

Then the set
E_{2}\coloneqq\cup_{n,j}E_{2}(n,j)
satisfies
|E_{2}|\lesssim\lambda^{-2}\log\log\lambda
and

\left\|\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}}\setminus E_{2}}\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}\sum_{j=1}^{C(n+\log\log\lambda)}T_{\cup_{l}\bd\mathfrak{T}_{n,j,l}}\right\|_{2\to 2}\lesssim(\|M^{K}\|+\|R^{K}\|)\log^{2}(e+\frac{A}{\|M^{K}\|+\|R^{K}\|})\log^{2}\lambda\,.

By
Lemma

4.10
it holds that

\|T_{\mathfrak{F}_{n,j}^{\prime}}\|_{2\to 2}\lesssim\begin{cases}A2^{-n/2}&\text{if $n>n_{0}$}\\
\|R^{K}\|+\|M^{K}\|&\text{if $n=n_{0}$}\end{cases}\,.

Summing these estimates yields

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}\sum_{j=1}^{C(n+\log\log\lambda)}T_{\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}_{n,j}}\right\|_{2\to 2}

\displaystyle\lesssim(n_{0}+\log\log\lambda)(\|R^{K}\|+\|M^{K}\|+A2^{-n_{0}/2})

\displaystyle\lesssim(\log\log\lambda+\log(e+\frac{A}{\|M^{K}\|+\|R^{K}\|}))(\|M^{K}\|+\|R^{K}\|)\,.

Putting everything together, we obtain with Tschebyscheff’s inequality

\displaystyle|\{x\,:\,|T_{\mathfrak{P}}f(x)|>\lambda\}|\lesssim\frac{\log^{4}\lambda}{\lambda^{2}}(1+(\|M^{K}\|+\|R^{K}\|)^{2}\log^{4}(e+\frac{A}{\|M^{K}\|+\|R^{K}\|}))\,.

By multiplying the kernel
K
with a constant, we can assume without loss of generality that
(\|R^{K}\|_{2\to 2}+\|M^{K}\|_{2\to 2})\log^{2}(e+\frac{A}{\|M^{K}\|+\|R^{K}\|})=1
. Integrating the above estimate then yields
\|T^{\mathfrak{P}}f\|_{p}\lesssim_{p}1
for
p<2
, as required.
∎

Appendix A
Appendix: Applications of
Theorem

1.1

A.1.
Weak type
L^{2}
estimates

We define for
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}})
the maximal modulation operator on
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}}

(32)

\displaystyle T_{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathcal{Q}}f(x)=\sup_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\sup_{0<\underline{R}<\overline{R}}\left|\int_{\underline{R}<\rho(x-y)<\overline{R}}K(x-y)e^{iQ(y)}f(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right|\,.

Corollary A.1
.

Let
K
be a Calderón-Zygmund with anisotropic scaling, with constant
A
.
Then the operator
T^{\mathcal{Q}}
is bounded from
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}})
into
L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}})
for
1\leq p<2
with norm

\|T^{\mathcal{Q}}\|_{2\to p}\lesssim_{\alpha,d,p}A\,.

The operator
T^{\mathcal{Q}}
is bounded from
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}})
into
L^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}})
and the operator
T_{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathcal{Q}}
is bounded from
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}})
into
L^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}})
.

Remark 2
.

It is also possible to prove strong estimates on
L^{p}
, for
1<p<\infty
, by adapting the arguments in
[
Zor21
]
to the anisotropic setting.
With this approach one obtains
\|T^{\mathcal{Q}}\|_{p\to p}\lesssim A
and
\|T_{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathcal{Q}}\|_{p\to p}\lesssim A
.

Proof of
Corollary

A.1
.

Let
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}((1/8,1/2))
with
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\psi(2^{k}t)=1
for all
t>0
. For every Calderón-Zygmund kernel
K
, the kernels
K_{s}(x)=\psi(2^{-s}\rho(x))K(x)
form an admissible decomposition of
K
. It holds that
M^{K}\lesssim AM
, where
M
is the Hardy-Littlewood maximal functions, and
\|R^{K}\|_{2\to 2}\lesssim A
, see
Lemma

4.2
. Thus
Theorem

1.1
implies that for
1\leq p<2

\|T^{\mathcal{Q}}\|_{2\to p}\lesssim(\|M^{K}\|_{2\to 2}+\|R^{K}\|_{2\to 2})\log^{2}(e+\frac{A}{\|M^{K}\|_{2\to 2}+\|R^{K}\|_{2\to 2}})\lesssim A\,,

where the last inequality holds because
x\log^{2}(e+A/x)
is increasing in
x>0
for all
A>0
.

The integral in the definition of
T^{\mathcal{Q}}
is continuous in the polynomial
Q
and in the truncation parameters
\underline{R}
and
\overline{R}
, thus the operator
T^{\mathcal{Q}}
does not change when the supremum is restricted to a countable dense set of polynomials and truncations. For fixed
Q
,
\underline{R}
and
\overline{R}
, the operator without suprema is a convolution operator with bounded kernel, thus it is translation invariant, linear, and bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}})
. Hence the Stein maximum principle (see
[
Ste61
]
, Cor. 1) implies that
there exists a constant
C
such that

\|T^{\mathcal{Q}}f\|_{L^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}})}

for every
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}})
.

It remains to show that
\|T_{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathcal{Q}}f\|_{L^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}})}\leq C^{\prime}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}})}
for all
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}})
. By density, we may assume that
f
is compactly supported.
Since the operator
T_{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathcal{Q}}
is translation invariant and the conditions of
Corollary

A.1
are invariant under conjugation by anisotropic dilations, we may further assume that
f
is supported in
[-1/4,1/4]^{\mathbf{d}}
.
Write

\displaystyle T_{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathcal{Q}}f(x)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\sup_{0<\underline{R}<\overline{R}<1/8}\left|\int_{\underline{R}<\rho(x-y)<\overline{R}}K(x-y)e^{iQ(y)}f(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right|

\displaystyle+\int_{1/8<\rho(x-y)}|K(x-y)||f(y)|\,\mathrm{d}y\,.

The first term is only nonzero if
\rho(x-y)\leq 1/8
for some
y\in\spt f\subset[-1/4,1/4]^{\mathbf{d}}
. If
\rho(x-y)\leq 1/8
then
|x-y|\leq 1/8
. Hence the first term is supported in
[-1/2,1/2)^{\mathbf{d}}
and there it is dominated by
T^{\mathcal{Q}}f(x)
. By the support assumption on
f
, there exists a constant
C_{\mathbf{d}}
and some
r_{0}>0
such that the integrand in the second term is supported in
\{y\,:\,r_{0}\leq\rho(x-y)\leq C_{\mathbf{d}}r_{0}\}
.
Hence the second term is bounded by
A\,Mf
, where
M
is the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function. We conclude that
T^{\mathcal{Q}}_{\mathbb{R}}
is bounded from
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}})
into
L^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbf{d}})
.
∎

A.2.
Singular Integrals Concentrated Near a Parabola

1.1

\phi

Thus
K^{j}
is a Calderón-Zygmund kernel with anisotropic scaling with constant
A(j)\lesssim 2^{-2j}|j-1|^{-3-\varepsilon}
for all
j
. We further set

K^{j}_{s}(x,y)=\phi_{j}(\frac{y}{x^{2}}-1)\psi(2^{-s}x)\frac{1}{x^{3}}\,.

For
\delta>0
sufficiently small, this defines an admissible decomposition of
K^{j}
.
Next, we deduce from (
33
) that
\|\phi_{j}(x)\|_{1}\lesssim|j-1|^{-3-\varepsilon}
. From the boundedness of the maximal functions associated to the Hilbert transform along the parabola it follows that

\displaystyle\|M^{K^{j}}\|_{2\to 2}\sim\|\phi_{j}\|_{1}\lesssim|j-1|^{-3-\varepsilon}\,,\quad\|R^{K^{j}}\|_{2\to 2}\lesssim\|\phi_{j}\|_{1}\lesssim|j-1|^{-3-\varepsilon}\,.

Now we apply
Theorem

1.1
.
Denoting by
(T^{j})^{\mathcal{Q}}
the maximal modulation operator on the torus associated to
K^{j}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|(T^{j})^{\mathcal{Q}}\|_{2\to p}\lesssim|j-1|^{-3-\varepsilon}\log^{2}(e+2^{-2j})\lesssim|j-1|^{-1-\varepsilon}\,.

This is summable in
j
, hence
\|T^{\mathcal{Q}}\|_{2\to p}<\infty
.
The weak type
L^{2}
estimates on the torus and on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
follows from this estimate, using similar arguments as in the proof of
Corollary

A.1
.
∎

A.3.
Singular Integrals with Weak Continuity Assumptions

In a different direction,
Theorem

1.1
can be used to weaken the continuity requirements of the kernel
K
in
Corollary

A.1
, as long as it is homogeneous and odd. Define a modulus of continuity to be an increasing function
\omega:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
such that
\lim_{x\to 0}\omega(x)=\omega(0)=0
and
\omega(x+y)\leq\omega(x)+\omega(y)
for all
x,y
. In addition, we will assume that it satisfies

(35)

\|\omega\|=\int_{0}^{1}\omega(t)\log^{2}(\frac{1}{t})\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}<\infty\,.

Note that this condition is slightly stronger than the Dini condition
\int_{0}^{1}\omega(t)\,\mathrm{d}t/t<\infty
. The Dini condition occurs in the theory of singular integrals as a sufficient condition on the modulus of continuity of an
L^{p_{0}}
bounded Calderón-Zygmund kernel to ensure
L^{p}
boundedness for
1<p\leq p_{0}
, see
[
Ste93
]
I.6.3..

Let
\phi:S^{1}\to\mathbb{C}
be an odd function such that for all
x,x^{\prime}\in S^{1}

|\phi(x)-\phi(x^{\prime})|\leq\omega(|x-x^{\prime}|)\,.

Let
\alpha=(1,2)
. Define a kernel
K
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
by

K(x)=\frac{1}{\rho(x)^{|\alpha|}}\phi(\delta_{\rho(x)^{-1}}(x))\,.

Since
\phi
is odd, the kernel
K
has integral
0
over all sets
\{\underline{R}\leq\rho(x)\leq\overline{R}\}
. This implies that for all Schwartz functions
f
the limit

Kf=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\rho(x)>\varepsilon}f(x)K(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

exists and defines a tempered distribution.
Let
T
be the operator defined on Schwartz functions by
Tf=K*f
. Then
T
extends to a bounded operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
since it is a superposition of Hilbert transforms along the curves
(t,c\sgn(t)t^{2})
. Since
K
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
, we can define the operators
T^{\mathcal{Q}}
and
T_{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathcal{Q}}
for
L^{2}
functions using (
6
) and (
32
).
Theorem

1.1
implies the following new result:

Corollary A.3
.

Suppose that
\phi
and
K
are as above.
Then the operator
T^{\mathcal{Q}}
defined by (
6
) is bounded from
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
into
L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
for
1\leq p<2
with norm

\|T^{\mathcal{Q}}\|_{2\to p}\lesssim_{d,p}\|\omega\|\,.

The operator
T^{\mathcal{Q}}
is bounded from
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
into
L^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
and the operator
T_{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathcal{Q}}
defined by (
32
) is bounded from
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
into
L^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

Remark 4
.

Corollary

A.3
should be compared to
Corollary

A.1
, which has stronger continuity requirements, but does not require the kernel to be homogeneous and odd.
The corollary is formulated on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
\alpha=(1,2)
only to simplify the notation, it is still true in higher dimension and for all
\alpha
, with a similar proof.
Note also that in the isotropic case this theorem holds for all integrable
\phi:\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{d}-1}\to\mathbb{C}
, without any continuity assumptions. This follows from the polynomial Carleson theorem, since
K
can then be written as a superposition of Hilbert transforms along lines.

Remark 5
.

We briefly explain where the oddness and homogeneity assumptions are used. In the proof of
Corollary

A.3
, we will decompose the kernel
K
into smoother pieces
K^{\prime}
. To then apply
Theorem

1.1
, we need good estimates for the maximal functions
M^{K^{\prime}}
and
R^{K^{\prime}}
. Since
K
is odd and homogeneous, we are able to choose the pieces
K^{\prime}
to be odd and homogeneous. Then we can write them as superpositions of Hilbert transforms along the curves
(t,c\sgn(t)t^{2})
to obtain bounds for the maximal functions. The oddness assumption can be replaced by the condition
K(x,y)=-K(-x,y)
, in that case the same argument works with Hilbert transforms along parabolas.
For general kernels
K
we are not aware of methods to decompose them while keeping good control of the maximal functions.

Proof of
Corollary

A.3
.

Fix some function
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}((-1,1))
with
\int\eta=1
and set
\eta_{j}(x)=2^{-j}\eta(2^{-j}x)
for
j\leq 0
, and
\eta_{1}(x)=0
.
Then we have
\phi=\sum_{j\leq 0}\phi_{j}
with
\phi_{j}=\phi*(\eta_{j}-\eta_{j+1})
.
By the Dini continuity of
\phi
it holds for all
j
that

|\phi_{j}(x)|\leq\int|\phi(x-y)-\phi(x)|(|\eta_{j}(y)|+|\eta_{j+1}(y)|)\,\mathrm{d}y\lesssim\omega(2^{j})\,.

The functions
\phi_{j}
are differentiable and
|\phi_{j}^{\prime}(x)|\lesssim\omega(1)2^{-2j}
.
We decompose the kernel
K
: Let

K^{j}(x)=\frac{1}{\rho(x)^{|\alpha|}}\phi_{j}(\delta_{\rho(x)^{-1}}(x))\,.

The uniform convergence of
\phi=\sum_{j\leq 0}\phi_{j}
implies that
K=\sum_{j\leq 0}K^{j}
with uniform convergence on compact subsets not containing
0
. Denoting by
(T^{j})^{\mathcal{Q}}
the maximal modulation operator on the torus associated to
K^{j}
, we thus have for all
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{\mathbf{d}})
that

T^{\mathcal{Q}}f\leq\sum_{j\leq 0}(T^{j})^{\mathcal{Q}}f

The estimates for
\phi_{j}
and
\phi_{j}^{\prime}
imply that
K^{j}
is a Calderón-Zygmund kernel with constant
A(j)\lesssim 2^{-2j}
. Using boundedness of the maximal functions associated to the curves
(t,c\sgn(t)t^{2})
, one obtains

\displaystyle\|M^{K^{j}}\|_{2\to 2}\sim\|\phi_{j}\|_{1}\lesssim\omega(2^{j})\,,\quad\|R^{K^{j}}\|_{2\to 2}\lesssim\|\phi_{j}\|_{1}\lesssim\omega(2^{j})\,.

Applying
Theorem

1.1
, it follows that

\|(T^{j})^{\mathcal{Q}}\|_{2\to p}\lesssim\omega(2^{j})\log^{2}(e+2^{-2j}\frac{\omega(1)}{\omega(2^{j})})\lesssim j^{2}\omega(2^{j})\,.

We conclude that

\|T^{\mathcal{Q}}\|_{2\to p}\leq\sum_{j\leq 0}\|(T^{j})^{\mathcal{Q}}\|_{2\to p}\lesssim\sum_{j\leq 0}j^{2}\omega(2^{j})\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}\omega(t)\log^{2}(\frac{1}{t})\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\lesssim\|\omega\|\,.

This completes the proof of the
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
estimate for
T^{\mathcal{Q}}
. The weak type estimates on the torus and on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
follow now exactly as in the proof of
Corollary

A.1
.
∎

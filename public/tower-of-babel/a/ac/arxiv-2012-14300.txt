Abstract
We determine the structure of automorphism groups of finite graphs of bounded Hadwiger number. This in particular settles three of Babai’s conjectures from the 1980s. The first one states that the order of non-alternating, non-abelian composition factors for automorphism groups of graphs of bounded Hadwiger number is bounded. The second one, the subcontraction conjecture, states that a non-trivial minor-closed graph class represents only finitely many non-abelian simple groups. And the third one states that if the order of such a group does not have small prime factors, then the group is obtained by iterated wreath and direct products from abelian groups. Our proof includes a structural analysis of finite edge-transitive graphs.

Introduction

Frucht’s classic theorem shows that every abstract group is the automorphism group of a finite graph, and in fact the graph can even be required to be connected and 3-regular
[
Fru49
]
. We say that the class of finite connected 3-regular graphs is
universal
.
In the years before 1990, various classes of graphs were proven to not be universal, typically by providing a structure theorem for automorphism groups of graphs in the class.
Along these lines, we know that automorphism groups of trees are iterated wreath products of symmetric groups
[
Pól37
]
. More generally,
Babai gave a classification of automorphism groups of finite planar graphs
[
Bab75
]
. An early survey of known results, for example including lattices, designs, and strongly regular graphs, is given by Babai and Goodman
[
BG93
]
.

In this paper, we are interested in classes of finite graphs closed under taking minors (sometimes called subcontractions), that is, classes closed under edge contractions as well as vertex and edge deletions. We investigate the structure of the automorphism groups of such graphs. These classes in particular include the class of graphs of bounded Hadwiger number. Other special cases are classes of graphs of bounded treewidth and classes of graphs of bounded genus, which include the class of planar graphs.

In 1974, Babai showed that a non-trivial minor-closed graph class is not universal by proving that large alternating groups cannot appear as automorphism groups of graphs of bounded Hadwiger number
[
Bab74a
]
. In fact, he also showed that for a sufficiently large prime
p
the group
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{3}
does not appear as a subgroup of a simple group represented by these graphs
[
Bab74b
]
.

However, no structural description of groups represented by graphs of bounded Hadwiger number has been available.
Progress towards this was made independently by Babai
[
Bab91
]
and Thomassen
[
Tho91
]
. They investigated
vertex-transitive graphs of bounded Hadwiger number and in particular showed that for
g\geq 3
there are only finitely many vertex-transitive graphs of genus
g
.

Regarding graphs of bounded Hadwiger number, one of Babai’s central theorems shows that there is a function
f
so that almost all finite vertex-transitive graphs of Hadwiger number at most
h
can be embedded on the torus or are
(f(h),f(h))
-ring-like. The latter means that there is a system of blocks of imprimitivity each of size at most
f(h)
which has a circular ordering and edges only connect blocks of distance
f(h)
in this circular ordering.
This theorem is mentioned as early as 1993. While a formal proof has not appeared, the theorem has been mentioned in various publications (
[
BG93
,
Bab95
,
Cam04
,
DM11
]

In this paper, we determine the structure of the automorphism group of finite graphs of bounded Hadwiger number. We do so by first proving that for connected,
K_{h+1}
-minor-free, edge-transitive, twin-free, finite graphs the non-abelian composition factors of the automorphism group have bounded order (Theorem
18
).
This allows us to show that automorphism groups of finite graphs of bounded Hadwiger number are obtained by repeated group extensions using abelian groups, symmetric groups and groups of bounded order (Theorem
34
).

Our structure theorem resolves three of Babai’s long-standing conjectures stated already in Babai’s 1981 survey on the abstract group of automorphisms
[
Bab81
]
.

First, regarding composition factors, Babai conjectured that there is a function
f
such that a composition factor of the automorphism group of a graph of Hadwiger number
h
is cyclic, alternating, or has order at most
f(h)
.

Second, regarding representability of simple groups, Babai’s
subcontraction conjecture
states that only finitely many non-cyclic simple groups are represented by graphs of bounded Hadwiger number. In fact, he anticipated that alternating composition factors can only appear within their corresponding symmetric factors. This is indeed the case.

Finally third, Babai conjectured that if the order of the automorphism group of a graph of bounded Hadwiger number does not have small prime factors, then the automorphism group is obtained by forming repeated direct products and wreath products of abelian groups.

All the conjectures follow fairly directly in the affirmative from our structural theorem.

Regarding our proof, we combine techniques from various
areas of graph and group theory, as well as geometry. In particular, we transfer Babai’s
theorem mentioned above from vertex-transitive graphs
to edge-transitive graphs, following his approach involving sphere
packings, infinite rooted limit graphs and Archimedian tilings. We
also exploit submodularity arguments for separations and graph
covering maps.

We should highlight that previously results of this sort were neither known for graphs of bounded Hadwiger number nor for prominent special cases such as graphs of bounded treewidth and graphs of bounded genus.

On

is denoted

, the

,

.

.

.
In this case, the bijection

to

.
The

of a graph, denoted

, is the

A graph

is

For vertex-colored graphs, an isomorphism has to preserve the vertex coloring in addition to the edge relation, i.e.,

.

.
The equivalence classes of this relation are called the

.
It is known (see

]
)
that infinite, connected, locally finite and

Groups

We use capital Greek letters to denote groups except for the

, which we denote by

Restricted Group Classes

Let
\Gamma_{d}
be the smallest class of groups satisfying the following properties:

1.

the trivial group is in
\Gamma_{d}
,

2.

\Gamma_{d}
is closed under taking extensions by subgroups of

(i.e., if
\Psi\trianglelefteq\Delta

\Delta/\Psi\in\Gamma_{d}

),

3.

\Gamma_{d}
is closed under taking extensions by a cyclic groups (i.e., if
\Psi\trianglelefteq\Delta
with
\Psi\in\Gamma_{d}
and

is cyclic, then

).

For convenience, in this paper we explicitly allow extensions by abelian infinite groups. This simplifies some of our arguments that deal with infinite graphs.
Another way of describing finite groups in
\Gamma_{d}
is that they are groups whose non-abelian composition factors are subgroups of
S_{d}
.

Let
\Theta_{d}
be the smallest class of groups satisfying the following properties:

1.

the trivial group is in
\Theta_{d}
,

2.

\Theta_{d}
is closed under taking direct products (i.e., if

3.

\Theta_{d}
is closed under taking wreath products with

then
\Delta\wr S_{t}\in\Theta_{d}
),

4.

\Theta_{d}
is closed under taking extensions by groups in
\Gamma_{d}
(i.e., if
\Psi\trianglelefteq\Delta
with
\Psi\in\Theta_{d}
and
\Delta/\Psi\in\Gamma_{d}
, then
\Delta\in\Theta_{d}
).

Thus, the non-abelian composition factors of groups in
\Theta_{d}
are subgroups of
S_{d}
or alternating groups. However, there is a restriction on how alternating groups may appear. Intuitively, alternating groups that are not subgroups of
S_{d}
can only arise if the respective symmetric group is present.

Rooted pointwise limits

In this section, we recapitulate the limit constructions for rooted graphs described in
[
Bab91
]
. This also gives us the chance to verify that they apply not only to limits of vertex-transitive graphs but also limits of edge-transitive graphs.

Let
U
be a set. A sequence
X=(X_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of subsets of

converges

there is an

so that

if and only if

for all

. The limit
of

is the set of those

for all but
finitely many

.

We will consider connected ordered graphs

, that is, graphs whose vertex set is precisely the set
\{1,\ldots,n\}
for some integer

. The vertex

can also be thought of as the

of the graph. Following
[
Bab91
]
, we require that the vertices are ordered according to a breath-first search traversal (
BFS-labeled
), which means that for vertices

we have
\operatorname{dist}_{G}(1,i)\leq\operatorname{dist}_{G}(1,j)
.

Let
(G_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence of finite BFS-labeled graphs. The graph

is the pointwise limit of the sequence if
V(G)
and
E(G)
are the pointwise limits of
(V(G_{i}))_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
and

, respectively.

It follows from the
compactness principle (or directly from Tychonoff’s Theorem) that
every sequence of graphs whose vertex set is a subset of the
natural numbers has a convergent subsequence.

Lemma 1
.

Let
(G_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a convergent sequence of connected finite, BFS-labeled, edge-transitive graphs of maximum degree at most
d
. Let
\overline{G}
be the limit graph. Then

1.

for every
t\in{\mathbb{N}}
there is an

so that for
n\geq n_{t}
the following holds: for every vertex
v\in V(G_{n})
there is an isomorphism from
G_{n}[B_{t,G_{n}}(v)]
to
\overline{G}[B_{t,\overline{G}}(x)]
mapping

to
x
for some

.

2.

\overline{G}
is connected and edge-transitive

Proof.

We can assume without loss of generality that no graph

is

\{1,2\}
is an edge of each graph

,
and thus of

.

We prove Part

.
It follows from the limit construction and the fact that the graphs

the subgraph graph of

induced by ball

converges to the subgraph of
\overline{G}
induced by ball

.
The statement now follows from edge-transitivity of the graphs

.

We prove Part
2
. Since the graphs

are BFS-labeled, the limit graph
\overline{G}
is connected. Let
e
be
an edge of
\overline{G}
. We show that there is an automorphism
mapping
\{1,2\}
to

. For some

we have that for

the edge

also appears in

. For each

choose an

\varphi_{n}
of

mapping

to

. Since the maximum

is bounded and the graphs are BFS-labeled, we can for

give a bound

independent of

so

The limit

of this subsequence is an

mapping

to

∎

Infinite edge-transitive graphs with two ends

Following Babai’s approach to analyze vertex-transitive graphs
[
Bab91
]
, we investigate the structure of large edge-transitive graphs by considering infinite limit graphs. We then draw conclusions about finite graphs from the properties of the infinite graphs.
Being almost vertex-transitive, the limit graph has one, two or infinitely many ends. These cases can be studied separately.
In
[
Bab91
]
, the most interesting case is that of one end and most results transfer fairly directly to the edge-transitive case. However, for edge-transitivity, the two ended case turns out to be significantly more interesting and involved, so we study this case separately and first.
Throughout the section, we suppose

is a connected, locally finite, edge-transitive graph with two ends.
The
connectivity between the two ends
is the minimum number of vertices in a separator separating the two ends.

Lemma 2

(Halin
[

]
)
.

If an infinite locally finite graph with two ends has connectivity

then there are

A connected graph

is called a

and an automorphism
\psi\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)

such that

is non-empty and finite,

\psi(C\cup S)\subseteq C
,
and

is finite.
In
[

]
, it is shown
that connected, locally finite, vertex-transitive graphs with two ends
are strips.
This also holds for edge-transitive instead of vertex-transitive graphs.

Lemma 3
.

Let
G
be a connected, locally finite and edge-transitive graph with two ends.
Then,
G
is a strip.

Proof.

We follow an idea similar to the proof of
[
Jun81

for vertex-transitive graphs.
Let
S
be a minimum separator separating the two ends.
Let
S^{L}
and
S^{R}
be the two infinite connected components of

(containing the ends).

Let
D\in{\mathbb{N}}
be the diameter of

, i.e., the maximal distance of vertices

measured in

.
Since

is edge-transitive and locally finite, we can pick a vertex

and an automorphism

\psi\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)
such that

and
\operatorname{dist}(v,v^{\psi})>2D
.
Then, it holds that

.
Similarly, we can pick a second automorphism

\psi^{\prime}
such that

and
\operatorname{dist}(v,v^{\psi^{\prime}})>2D

Claim 1
.

There is an automorphism

Proof of Claim 1.

If

does not interchange the two ends, then we are done since

Thus, assume that both

(S^{L}\cup S)^{\psi^{\prime}}\subseteq S^{R}

In this case, it holds that
(S^{R}\cup S)^{\psi\psi^{\prime}}\subseteq(S^{L})^{\psi^{\prime}}\subseteq(S^{L}\cup S)^{\psi^{\prime}}\subseteq S^{R}
.
∎

Note that

is finite
since

is a minimum finite separator,
and note that

is finite since

has two ends.
Thus, the claim completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

For two finite separators
S
and
S^{\prime}
separating the two ends, we let
[S,S^{\prime}]
be the set of vertices of
G
that do not lie in an infinite connected component of
G-(S\cup S^{\prime})
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
G
be a connected, locally finite and edge-transitive graph with two ends.
There is a constant
D
that bounds the diameter of every minimum separator
S
separating the two ends (i.e.,
\operatorname{dist}_{G}(v,v^{\prime})\leq D
for all
v,v^{\prime}\in S
).

Proof.

Fix some minimum cardinality separator
S_{0}
separating
the two ends.
By
3
, there is
an automorphism
\psi\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)
of infinite order.
By possibly replacing
\psi
with a suitable power, we can assume that

[S_{0},S_{0}^{\psi}]
is a subset that is connected (using only paths inside
[S_{0},S_{0}^{\psi}]
).
Now, consider the intervals
\ldots,I^{-1},I^{0},I^{1},I^{2},\ldots

where
I^{\ell}\coloneqq[S_{0}^{\psi^{\ell}},S_{0}^{\psi^{\ell+1}}]
for
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
By
2
, there are
k\coloneqq|S|
vertex-disjoint paths connecting
the two ends. Note that each minimum separator
S
contains exactly one vertex from each path.
Thus, since
[S_{0},S_{0}^{\psi}]
is connected, for each minimum separator
S
the set of intervals

\{I^{\ell}\mid I^{\ell}\cap S\neq\emptyset\}
that non-trivially intersect
S
must be a contiguous sequence of intervals.
Therefore,
each separator
S
is contained in the interval
[S_{0}^{\psi^{k_{0}}},S_{0}^{\psi^{k_{0}+2k}}]

for some
k_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
where
k=|S_{0}|=|S|
is the connectivity between the two ends.
Note that the diameter of
[S_{0}^{\psi^{k_{0}}},S_{0}^{\psi^{k_{0}+2k}}]
in
G

is equal to diameter
D\in{\mathbb{N}}
between vertices in
[S_{0},S_{0}^{\psi^{2k}}]
since
\psi^{k_{0}}

is an automorphism of
G
.
Thus, the diameter of
S
in
G
is bounded by the constant
D

(which only depends on
S_{0}
and
\psi
but not on
S
).
∎

Level Sets

Since
G
is edge-transitive, it has at most two vertex orbits. Let
V_{\mathop{\mathrm{sep}}}\subseteq V(G)
be the union of all minimum separators of
G
separating the two ends.
If some vertex is in
V_{\mathop{\mathrm{sep}}}
,
then its entire vertex orbit is in
V_{\mathop{\mathrm{sep}}}
.
Thus, the set
V_{\mathop{\mathrm{sep}}}
contains an entire orbit
O_{1}
,
i.e.,
O_{1}\subseteq V_{\mathop{\mathrm{sep}}}
.

Fix the two ends of the graph
G
, call them left and right. For every finite separator
S
that separates the two ends let
S^{L}
and
S^{R}
be the connected components of
G-S
containing the left and right end, respectively.
Note that
N_{G}(S^{L})=S=N_{G}(S^{R})
.

Lemma 5
.

For every vertex
v\in O_{1}
, there is a unique leftmost minimum separator
S_{v}
in the following sense:
for every minimum separator
S
separating the two ends and containing
v
we have
(S_{v})^{L}\subseteq S^{L}
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}=\{S\mid S
is a minimum separator separating the ends that contains
v\}

and note that
{\mathcal{S}}
is finite
since each
S\in{\mathcal{S}}
is contained in a ball centered at
v
with radius
D
(
4
).
For
S,S^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}
,
the intersection
S^{L}\cap{S^{\prime}}^{L}
is infinite since it contains the left infinite component of
G-(S\cup S^{\prime})
.
We now use a submodularity argument. Define
S^{\cap}\coloneqq(S\cap S^{\prime})\cup(S\cap{S^{\prime}}^{L})\cup(S^{L}\cap S^{\prime})
.
We claim that
S^{\cap}\in\mathcal{S}
and
{S^{\cap}}^{L}\subseteq S^{L}\cap{S^{\prime}}^{L}
. Indeed,
S^{\cap}
is a separator separating the ends since every path from the left end to the right end has to enter
S
and
S^{\prime}
at the same time or
S
first or
S^{\prime}
first. The separator
S^{\cap}
is of minimum cardinality since otherwise
S^{\cup}\coloneqq(S\cap S^{\prime})\cup(S\cap{S^{\prime}}^{R})\cup(S^{R}\cap S^{\prime})
would be a separator of cardinality smaller than
|S|=|S^{\prime}|
(since
|S^{\cup}|+|S^{\cap}|=|S|+|S^{\prime}|
).

Therefore, there is a leftmost minimum separator
S_{v}\in{\mathcal{S}}
where

S_{v}^{L}=\bigcap_{S\in{\mathcal{S}}}S^{L}
is
the intersection of finitely many left components
and where
S_{v}=N_{G}(S_{v}^{L})
.
∎

For vertices
v,v^{\prime}\in O_{1}
define
D(v,v^{\prime})\coloneqq|S^{L}_{v}\setminus S^{L}_{v^{\prime}}|-|S^{L}_{v^{\prime}}\setminus S^{L}_{v}|
. Note that
D(v,v^{\prime})
is finite since
S^{L}_{v}\setminus S^{L}_{v^{\prime}}
and
S^{L}_{v^{\prime}}\setminus S^{L}_{v}
are finite.
Fix some arbitrary vertex
v_{0}\in O_{1}
.
We define a linearly ordered partition of
O_{1}
into so-called
(primary) level sets

by partitioning the vertices
v\in O_{1}
according to the value
D(v,v_{0})\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
More precisely, the ordered partition
(L_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
of
O_{1}
into non-empty sets is defined
such that
D(v,v_{0})<D(w,v_{0})
if and only if
v\in L_{i},w\in L_{j}
for
i,j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
with
i<j
.
Without loss of generality, we can assume that the indices
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}
are chosen such that
L_{0}=\{v\in O_{1}\mid D(v,v_{0})=0\}
(which is non-empty since
v_{0}\in L_{0}
).
We also set the
level
of a vertex
v\in L_{i}
as
\mathop{\mathrm{Lev}}(v)\coloneqq\mathop{\mathrm{Lev}}(v)_{v_{0}}\coloneqq i\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
and thus
L_{i}=\{v\in O_{1}\mid\mathop{\mathrm{Lev}}(v)=i\}
.
In case that
G
has exactly two orbits
O_{1},O_{2}
, we assign

secondary level sets
by defining

J_{i+\frac{1}{2}}\coloneq\{v\in O_{2}\mid N(v)\subseteq L_{i}\cup L_{i+1}\}
.
We also set the
level
of
v\in J_{i+\frac{1}{2}}
as

\mathop{\mathrm{Lev}}(v)\coloneqq i+\frac{1}{2}
.
The next lemma in particular shows (Part
5
) that every vertex of
G
has a well-defined
level.

Lemma 6
.

Let
G
be a connected, locally finite and edge-transitive graph with two ends.
Suppose
u,v,w,v_{0},v_{0}^{\prime}\in O_{1}
.

1.

D(u,v)+D(v,w)=D(u,w)
.

2.

There is a constant
c\coloneqq c(v_{0},v_{0}^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{Z}}

such that for all vertices
x\in O_{1}
we have
\mathop{\mathrm{Lev}}(x)_{v_{0}^{\prime}}=\mathop{\mathrm{Lev}}(x)_{v_{0}}+c
.

3.

The cardinality of the (primary) level sets
L_{i},i\in{\mathbb{Z}}
is finite.

4.

The partition
{\mathcal{P}}\coloneqq\{L_{i}\mid i\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
of
O_{1}
into (primary) level sets is
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
-invariant.

5.

If
G
has exactly two orbits, then
{\mathcal{Q}}\coloneqq\{J_{i+\frac{1}{2}}\mid i\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
is
a partition of
O_{2}
and this partition is
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
-invariant.

6.

If
G
is vertex-transitive, all edges
e\in E(G)
have endpoints in
L_{i}
and
L_{i+1}

for some
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

7.

If
G
has exactly two orbits, then
all edges
e\in E(G)
have endpoints in
L_{i}
and
J_{i^{\prime}}
for some
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
i^{\prime}\in\{i-\frac{1}{2},i+\frac{1}{2}\}
.

8.

If
G
is vertex-transitive, then the
graph
G[L_{i},L_{i+1}]

is regular for all
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

9.

If
G
has exactly two orbits, then the graphs
G[L_{i},J_{i+\frac{1}{2}}],G[J_{i+\frac{1}{2}},L_{i+1}]
are biregular for all
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Proof.

We prove Part
1
.
Let
S_{u},S_{v},S_{w}
be leftmost minimum separators.
Since
G
is connected and locally finite, it follows that for each finite separator
S
separating the two ends
there are finitely many connected components in
G-S
,
and exactly two of them are infinite.
Let
S_{u}^{L},S_{v}^{L},S_{w}^{L}
be the respective connected components of
G-S_{u},G-S_{v},G-S_{w}
containing the left end.
From what we just observed the graph
G-(S_{u}\cup S_{v}\cup S_{w})
has exactly two infinite
connected components (containing the left and right end),
and the one containing the left end is in turn contained in the intersection
I\coloneqq S_{u}^{L}\cap S_{v}^{L}\cap S_{w}^{L}
.
Therefore, the sets
S_{u}^{L}\setminus I,S_{v}^{L}\setminus I,S_{w}^{L}\setminus I
are finite.
This implies that
D(u,v)=|S_{u}^{L}\setminus I|-|S_{v}^{L}\setminus I|
.
Similarly, it holds that
D(v,w)=|S_{v}^{L}\setminus I|-|S_{w}^{L}\setminus I|

and
D(u,w)=|S_{u}^{L}\setminus I|-|S_{w}^{L}\setminus I|
.
Therefore, it holds that
D(u,v)+D(v,w)=|S_{u}^{L}\setminus I|-|S_{v}^{L}\setminus I|+|S_{v}^{L}\setminus I|-|S_{w}^{L}\setminus I|=D(u,v)
.
This proves Part
1
.

We prove Part
2
.
By Part
1
, there is a constant
d\coloneqq D(v_{0},v_{0}^{\prime})

such that for all
x\in O_{1}
it holds that

D(x,v_{0}^{\prime})=D(x,v_{0})+d
.
This implies Part
2
.

We prove Part
3
.
We consider a leftmost minimum separator
S_{v}
for some vertex
v\in L_{i}
.
Since the distance of vertices within a minimum separator is bounded by a constant
D
(
4
),
there are vertices
u,w
and leftmost minimum separators
S_{u},S_{w}

such that
S_{u}^{L}\subsetneq S_{v}^{L}\subsetneq S_{w}^{L}

where
S_{w}^{L}\setminus S_{u}^{L}
is finite.
Moreover, it holds that
L_{i}\subseteq S_{w}^{L}\setminus S_{u}^{L}
, and thus
L_{i}
is finite.

We prove Part
4
.
By Part
2
, the partition
{\mathcal{P}}
into level sets does not depend on
v_{0}
.
We need to be careful that the partition
{\mathcal{P}}
does not change when we swap the roles of the left and the right end,
i.e., if we were to define the level sets with respect to the right rather than the left end
by considering the
rightmost minimum separator

\widetilde{S}_{v}
containing
v
with an inclusion minimal component
\widetilde{S}_{v}^{R}
.
Since
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
acts transitively on
O_{1}
,
the size of
V(G)\setminus(S_{v}^{L}\cup\widetilde{S}_{v}^{R})
is an
invariant for all vertices
v\in O_{1}
(and is finite since
G
has two ends). It follows that two vertices
v,v^{\prime}\in O_{1}
are in the same (primary) level set if and only if they are
in the same (primary) level set if we define level sets with respect to the right rather than the left end. This shows that the property
of being in the same level set is preserved under automorphisms.

We prove Part
5
.
Let
v
be a vertex in
O_{2}
. We argue that
v
has neighbors in exactly two (primary) levels sets.

If
v
has neighbors in only one (primary) level set, then this is the case for all vertices in
O_{2}
,
and since
G
is edge-transitive, there is no path in
G
connecting two distinct (primary) level sets,
contradicting that
G
is connected.

Next, we rule out the case that
v
has neighbors in more than two (primary) level sets.
We say that an edge
e=vw_{j}
with
v\in O_{2},w_{j}\in L_{j}

lies between other edges
if there are edges
vw_{i},vw_{k}\in E(G)
such that

w_{i}\in L_{i},w_{k}\in L_{k}
and
i<j<k
.
If
v
has neighbors in more than two level sets, then there are edges lying between others.
However, there are also edges that do not lie in between other edges since
G
is locally finite.
This contradicts the fact that
G
is edge-transitive.
Thus, every vertex
v\in O_{2}
has neighbors in exactly two (primary) level sets.

Finally, we show that each vertex
v
has neighbors in two consecutive (primary) level sets.
Let
v_{i},v_{j}\in N(v)
such that
v_{i}\in L_{i},v_{j}\in L_{j},i\neq j
.
Since
O_{2}
is an orbit, we can conclude that the difference
|i-j|

is an invariant across all vertices from
O_{2}
.
If this invariant were different from
1
, then the graph would not be connected.
More precisely, if the invariant would be
c>1
, then the
set
O_{2}\cup\bigcup_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}L_{c\cdot i}\subsetneq V(G)
would contain a non-trivial connected component of
G
, contradicting that
G
is connected.
This means that
{\mathcal{Q}}
forms a partition of
O_{2}
.

We prove Part
6
.
Since
G
is edge-transitive and level sets are blocks under automorphisms, for every edge
e=vw\in E(G)
the value of
|\mathop{\mathrm{Lev}}(v)-\mathop{\mathrm{Lev}}(w)|
is an invariant among all edges.
(The sign of
\mathop{\mathrm{Lev}}(v)-\mathop{\mathrm{Lev}}(w)
depends on the choice of the left end.)
Again, if this invariant would be
c>1
, then
the set
\bigcup_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}L_{c\cdot i}\subsetneq V(G)
would contain a non-trivial connected component of
G
, contradicting that
G
is connected.

Part
7
follows directly from Part
5

since each vertex
v\in J_{i+\frac{1}{2}}
has only neighbors in

L_{i}
and
L_{i+1}
for all
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

We prove Part
8
.
Suppose for the sake of contradiction that two vertices in
L_{i}
have a different number of neighbors in
L_{i+1}
.
(In principle, we might think this could happen because
G
has reflections swapping the two ends.)
Then, every level set
L_{i}
can be partitioned into two non-empty sets
L_{i}^{>}
and
L_{i}^{<}
consisting of those vertices that have more neighbors in
L_{i+1}
than in
L_{i-1}
and vice versa.
Let
L^{>>}\coloneqq\bigcup_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}(L_{i}^{>}),L^{<<}\coloneqq\bigcup_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}(L_{i}^{<})
and
let
L^{><}\coloneqq\bigcup_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}(L_{2i}^{>}\cup L_{2i+1}^{<}),L^{<>}\coloneqq\bigcup_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}(L_{2i}^{<}\cup L_{2i+1}^{>})
.
Note that
{\mathcal{B}}_{1}\coloneqq\{L^{>>},L^{<<}\}
and

{\mathcal{B}}_{2}\coloneqq\{L^{><},L^{<>}\}
are both block systems.
Let
e\in E(G)
be an edge. Then,
e
is contained in some block of one of those block systems, i.e.,

e\subseteq B
for some
B\in{\mathcal{B}}_{i^{*}}
and some
i^{*}\in\{1,2\}
.
By edge-transitivity, all edges are contained in blocks of
{\mathcal{B}}_{i^{*}}
,
i.e., for all edges
e\in E(G)
there is a block
B\in{\mathcal{B}}_{i^{*}}

such that
e\subseteq B
.
But then, there are no edges connecting the two blocks in
{\mathcal{B}}_{i^{*}}
,
contradicting that
G
is connected.
Therefore, each graph
G[L_{i},L_{i+1}]
is biregular. Since
|L_{i}|=|L_{i+1}|
, the graph is even regular.

We prove Part
9
.
We want to rule out that vertices in
L_{i}
have a different number of neighbors in
J_{i+\frac{1}{2}}
.
If there is a vertex in
L_{i}
that does not have a neighbor in
both
J_{i-\frac{1}{2}}
and
J_{i+\frac{1}{2}}
, then this would hold
for all vertices in
L_{i}
since
L_{i}\subseteq O_{1}
.
In this case,
J_{i-\frac{1}{2}}
and
J_{i+\frac{1}{2}}
would not be in the same connected
component, contradicting that
G
is connected.
Thus, let
v_{1},v_{2}\in L_{i}
and
let
e_{k}
be an edge connecting
v_{k}\in L_{i}
with
J_{i+\frac{1}{2}}
for
k=1,2
.
Since
G
is edge-transitive, there is an automorphism
that maps
e_{1}
to
e_{2}
. Since
L_{i}\subseteq O_{1}
and
J_{i+\frac{1}{2}}\subseteq O_{2}
,
this automorphism also maps
v_{1}
to
v_{2}
and stabilizes
J_{i+\frac{1}{2}}
setwise.
Therefore, the number
d_{k}^{+}\coloneqq|N_{G}(v_{k})\cap J_{i+\frac{1}{2}}|
of neighbors
is the same for both
k=1,2
.
Since
v_{1},v_{2}\in L_{i}
have the same degree
d_{k}(v_{k})\coloneqq\deg_{G}(v_{k})
, also the number

d_{k}^{-}\coloneqq|N_{G}(v_{k})\cap J_{i-\frac{1}{2}}|

of neighbors coincide.
By swapping the role of the primary and secondary level sets,
the same arguments can be applied to vertices
w_{1},w_{2}
in a secondary level set
J_{i+\frac{1}{2}}
.
Thus, the bipartite graph induced on two level sets is biregular.
∎

Now, we consider biregular graphs.

Lemma 7
.

Let
G
be graph and
A,B,C\subseteq V(G)
disjoint subsets of the vertices such that
G[A,B]
and
G[B,C]
induce biregular graphs that are not edgeless. Suppose
m\coloneqq|A|=|C|\leq|B|
.
Then, there are
m
vertex-disjoints paths from
A
to
C
.

Proof.

Let
S
be a minimum separator between
A
and
C
.
By Menger’s theorem, it suffices to show that
|S|\geq m
.
Let
S_{A}\coloneqq S\cap A,S_{B}\coloneqq S\cap B
and
S_{C}\coloneqq S\cap C
.
Let
R\subseteq V(G)
be the set of vertices that can be reached by a path in
G-S
starting in
A\setminus S
.
Let
R_{A}\coloneqq R\cap A,R_{B}\coloneqq R\cap B
and
R_{C}\coloneqq R\cap C
.
Clearly, it holds that
|R_{A}|=|A|-|S_{A}|
.
Define
c\coloneqq\frac{|B|}{m}\geq 1
.
Since
G[A,B]
is regular, the neighborhood of
R_{A}\subseteq A
in
B
is at least
c\cdot|R_{A}|
.
Note that
N(R_{A})\cap B\subseteq R_{B}\cup S_{B}
.
This leads to
|R_{B}|\geq|N(R_{A})\cap B|-|S_{B}|\geq c\cdot|R_{A}|-|S_{B}|
.
With the same argument
|R_{C}|\geq|N(R_{B})\cap C|-|S_{C}|\geq c^{-1}\cdot|R_{B}|-|S_{C}|
.
In total, we have that
|R_{C}|\geq|A|-|S_{A}|-c^{-1}\cdot|S_{B}|-|S_{C}|\geq|A|-|S|
.
On the other hand,
|R_{C}|=0
since
S
separates
A
and
C
.
This means that
|S|\geq|A|=m
.
∎

The following lemma shows that the size of the (primary) level sets is the connectivity between the two ends.

Lemma 8
.

Let
G
be a connected, locally finite and edge-transitive graph with two ends
and let
m\coloneqq|L_{0}|
be the size of a (primary) level set. There are
m
vertex-disjoint paths
connecting the two ends, and thus
m
is the connectivity between the two ends.
Furthermore, if
G
has exactly two orbits, then
|L_{0}|\leq|J_{0}|
.

Proof.

Case 1.
G
is vertex-transitive:

By Part
6
and
8
of
6
, each edge of
G
lies within a regular graph
G[L_{i},L_{i+1}]
for some
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
It follows from Hall’s marriage theorem
that
G[L_{i},L_{i+1}]
has a matching of size
m\coloneqq|L_{i}|=|L_{i+1}|
.
This leads to
m
vertex-disjoint paths
P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}
connecting the ends.

Case 2.
G
is not vertex-transitive:
In that case
G
has a second orbit
O_{2}\neq O_{1}
.
By Parts
7
and
9
of
6
,
each edge of
G
belongs to one of the biregular graphs

G[L_{i},J_{i+\frac{1}{2}}],G[J_{i+\frac{1}{2}},L_{i+1}]
for some
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Assume first that
m\leq|J_{i+\frac{1}{2}}|
.
By
7
, there are
m
vertex-disjoint paths from
L_{i}
to
L_{i+1}
(via
J_{i+\frac{1}{2}}
).
This leads to
m
vertex-disjoint paths connecting the two ends.

Now, assume for the sake of contradiction that
|J_{i+\frac{1}{2}}|<m
.
Then, with the same argument, we obtain vertex-disjoint paths from
J_{i-\frac{1}{2}}
to
J_{i+\frac{1}{2}}
.
But then, there are vertices in
O_{1}
that are not contained in these paths
(and thus not contained in any minimum separator separating the two ends),
contradicting the fact that each vertex in
O_{1}\subseteq V_{\mathop{\mathrm{sep}}}
is in such a separator.
∎

Suppose that the connectivity between the two ends is
m
. For bidirectionally infinite paths
P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}

and a subset
S\subseteq V(G)
,
we let
H^{S}_{P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}}

be the graph with vertex set
\{1,\ldots,m\}
in which two vertices
i,j\in[m]
are adjacent if there is a path in
G

from some vertex in
P_{i}
to some vertex in
P_{j}

whose vertices are in
S
and
whose internal vertices do not lie on any of the
m
paths
P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}
.
We also write
H_{P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}}
for
H^{V(G)}_{P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}}
.

Lemma 9
.

Let
G
be a connected, locally finite and edge-transitive graph with two ends
and let
m
be the connectivity between the two ends.
For vertex-disjoint paths
P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}
connecting the two ends there are vertex-disjoint paths
P^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,P^{\prime}_{m}

connecting the two ends and an automorphism
\psi\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)
of infinite order
which maps each path
P^{\prime}_{i}
to itself so that
H_{P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}}=H_{P^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,P^{\prime}_{m}}
.

Proof.

Let
S
be a minimum separator separating the two ends.
Then, the separator
S
contains exactly one vertex from every path
P_{i}
.
By
3
,
there is an automorphism
\psi\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)
of infinite order
such that
S\cap S^{(\psi^{t})}=\emptyset

for all
t\in{\mathbb{Z}},t\neq 0
.
Let
P_{i}[S,S^{\psi}]
be the restriction of
P_{i}
to
[S,S^{\psi}]
. Note that
P_{i}[S,S^{\psi}]
is a path with one endpoint in
S
and one endpoint in
S^{\psi}
.
By possibly replacing
\psi
with a suitable power of itself, we can ensure that the connections between the different paths that are responsible for edges in
H_{P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}}
also occur in the interval
[S,S^{\psi}]
, i.e., that

H_{P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}}=H^{[S,S^{\psi}]}_{P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}}
.
Again, by possibly replacing
\psi
with a suitable power of itself, we can ensure that for all
i\in[m]
and all vertices
v\in V(G)
it holds that
v\in P_{i}\cap S
if and only if
v^{\psi}\in P_{i}\cap S^{\psi}
.
Define
P^{\prime}_{i}
to be
\bigcup_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}P_{i}[S,S^{\psi}]^{(\psi^{i})}
for each
i\in[m]
. Then, the collection of paths
P^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,P^{\prime}_{m}
together with
\psi
satisfies the requirements of the lemma.
∎

We write
P_{\infty}
to denote be the bidirectionally infinite path on vertex set
\mathbb{Z}
.

Corollary 10
.

Let
G
be a connected, locally finite and edge-transitive graph with two ends. Let
m
be the connectivity between the two ends.
Let
P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}
be vertex-disjoint paths connecting the two ends
and let
H\coloneqq H_{P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}}
.
Then, the Cartesian product
H\square P_{\infty}
is a minor of
G
.

Proof.

By
9
, we can assume that there is
an automorphism
\psi
of infinite order leaving the paths
P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}
invariant,
and thus each every edge

\{i,j\}
in
H_{P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}}
is realized by
infinitely many disjoint connections between the corresponding
paths
P_{i}
and
P_{j}
in

.
∎

The next lemma shows that we can find paths such that
H_{P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}}

is vertex-transitive.

Lemma 11
.

Let
G
be a connected, locally finite and edge-transitive graph with two ends
and let
m\coloneqq|L_{0}|
be the size of a (primary) level set.
There are vertex-disjoint paths
P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}
connecting the two ends such that
H_{P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}}
is vertex-transitive.

Proof.

By
8
, there are vertex-disjoint paths
P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}
connecting the two ends,
and by Lemma
9
, we can assume that there is an automorphism
\psi

of infinite order mapping each path to itself.
We argue that if
H\coloneqq H_{P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}}
is not transitive, we can choose different paths
P^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,P^{\prime}_{m}
so that
H^{\prime}\coloneqq H_{P^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,P^{\prime}_{m}}
has more edges than
H
. Since the
m
-vertex graph
H
has at most
\binom{m}{2}
edges, this eventually proves the theorem.
Suppose that
H
is not vertex-transitive, and thus there is no automorphism from
i
to
j
for some
i,j\in[m]
.
Let
L_{0}
be a (primary) level set.
Since
L_{0}\subseteq O_{1}
, there is an automorphism
\varphi\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)
that
maps the vertex of
L_{0}
belonging to
P_{i}
to the vertex of
L_{0}
belonging to
P_{j}
.
Since the partition of
O_{1}
into (primary) level sets is invariant under automorphisms (
6
Part
4
),
the automorphism
\varphi
stabilizes
L_{0}
setwise.
Therefore,
\varphi
induces a permutation
\widetilde{\varphi}
of
[m]
(the indices of the paths) that maps
i
to
j
.
Note that
P_{1}^{\varphi},\ldots,P_{m}^{\varphi}
are
m
vertex-disjoint paths
that are invariant under the automorphism
\varphi^{-1}\psi\varphi
of infinite order,
and also note that
H_{P_{1}^{\varphi},\ldots,P_{m}^{\varphi}}=H^{\widetilde{\varphi}}
.

There are exactly two infinite connected components
L,R
of
G-L_{0}
.
Let
P^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,P^{\prime}_{m}
be the paths that agree with
P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}
on
L\cup L_{0}

and that agree with
P_{1}^{\varphi},\ldots,P_{m}^{\varphi}
on
L_{0}\cup R

and define
H^{\prime}\coloneqq H_{P_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,P_{m}^{\prime}}
.
It holds that
H^{\prime}=H^{L\cup L_{0}}_{P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}}\cup H^{L_{0}\cup R}_{P_{1}^{\varphi},\ldots,P_{m}^{\varphi}}
.

Since
P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}
are invariant under some automorphism of infinite order,
every edge of
H
is supported infinitely many times inside of
L
, and thus
H\subseteq H^{\prime}
.
Similarly (with
R
in the role of
L
), the paths
P_{1}^{\varphi},\ldots,P_{m}^{\varphi}
are invariant under some automorphism of infinite order,
and thus
H\subseteq H^{\widetilde{\varphi}}
.
However, the graphs
H
and
H^{\widetilde{\varphi}}
are not identical,
otherwise the permutation
\widetilde{\varphi}
of
[m]
would
be an automorphism of
H
mapping
i
to
j
.
Therefore, the graph
H^{\prime}
is a proper supergraph of
H
.
∎

Thus, we can find paths such that
H_{P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}}
is vertex-transitive.
Note that connected vertex-transitive graphs with at least three vertices are 2-connected.
We make a case distinction of
H
being a cycle or not,
and each case is handled separately in one of the following two lemmas.

Lemma 12
.

Let
H
be a finite 2-connected graph with
n\geq 3
vertices that is not a cycle. Then, the graph
H\square P_{\infty}
has a
K_{n-1}
-minor.

Proof.

We construct a minor with
n-1
branch sets
B_{1},\ldots,B_{n-1}\subseteq V(H\square P_{\infty})
as follows.
Each slice
S_{i}\coloneqq V(H)\times\{i\}\subseteq V(H\square P_{\infty})
will contain at least one vertex from each branch set, i.e,

S_{i}\cap B_{j}\neq\emptyset
.
Exactly one branch set will intersect the slice in two vertices, and these two vertices form an edge, i.e., for each
i
there
is exactly one
B_{j}
such that
e_{i}\coloneqq S_{i}\cap B_{j}
is a 2-element subset, and for this set it holds that
e_{i}\in E(H\square P_{\infty})
.
In the next slice
S_{i+1}
the same vertices of
H
will intersect the same
branch sets except that for one vertex
v\in V(H)
the branch set will be different in
S_{i}
compared
to
S_{i+1}
, i.e.,
(v,i)
and
(v,i+1)
are in distinct branch sets for exactly one
v\in V(H)
.
In each of the slices this vertex has to be the vertex which appears in the branch set that contains two vertices,
i.e.,
(v,i)\in e_{i},(v,i+1)\in e_{i+1}
.
It suffices now to construct the branch sets so that for each pair of branch sets
B,B^{\prime}

there is a slice
S_{i},i\in{\mathbb{Z}}
so that
B\cap S_{i}
and
B^{\prime}\cap S_{i}
are adjacent.

Overall, this translates into the following sliding puzzle. Consider the graph
H

and suppose there are
n-1
pebbles (corresponding to the branch sets) placed on
n-1
different vertices of the graph.
A legal move is to move one pebble across an edge to the previously unoccupied spot, called
gap
in the following.
The edge across which a pebble slides in step
i
corresponds to the edge
e_{i}
.
To solve the puzzle, the task is to perform a sequence of legal moves so that over time each pair of
pebbles was situated on adjacent vertices at some point.

We now argue that if
H
is 2-connected and not a cycle, then the puzzle is solvable. Let
p
and
p^{\prime}
be non-adjacent pebbles on
H
.
We first observe that we can perform a sequence of moves so that
p
,
p^{\prime}
and the gap lie on a common cycle. Indeed,
p
and
p^{\prime}
lie on a common cycle
C
since
H
is 2-connected. Due to 2-connectivity, there are two shortest paths
P
and
P^{\prime}
from the gap to
C

which are vertex-disjoint (except on the gap).
If one of these paths does not end in
p
or
p^{\prime}
,
we can directly move the gap onto the cycle.
Otherwise, the paths
P
and
P^{\prime}
end in
p
and
p^{\prime}
, respectively.
In that case, the paths
P
and
P^{\prime}
together with a path in
C
joining
p
and
p^{\prime}
form the desired cycle.

Let
\widetilde{C}
be a cycle containing
p
,
p^{\prime}
and the gap.
Since
H
is not a cycle and due to 2-connectivity, there is a path
\widetilde{P}
whose endpoints
v,v^{\prime}
are distinct vertices on
\widetilde{C}
and whose internal vertices are not on
\widetilde{C}
. We rotate the cycle
\widetilde{C}
(by moving the gap on the cycle) so that
p
is located on
v
, and we then move the gap along
\widetilde{C}
without moving
p
so that the gap is located on
v^{\prime}
.
On
\widetilde{C}
there are two paths
\widetilde{P}_{1},\widetilde{P}_{2}
from
v
to
v^{\prime}
and with each of them the path
\widetilde{P}
forms a cycle.
One of these cycles
\widetilde{P}_{1}\cup\widetilde{P},\widetilde{P}_{2}\cup\widetilde{P}
does not contain
p^{\prime}
.
We may assume that
\widetilde{P}_{1}
does not contain
p^{\prime}
.
We argue that we may assume that
\widetilde{P}
has an internal vertex. Indeed, if
\widetilde{P}
does not have an internal vertex, then
\widetilde{P}_{1}
must have an internal vertex.
In that case, we interchange the names of
\widetilde{P}
and
\widetilde{P}_{1}
, thereby replacing
\widetilde{C}
with
(\widetilde{C}\setminus\widetilde{P}_{1})\cup\widetilde{P}
.

We rotate the cycle
\widetilde{P}_{1}\cup\widetilde{P}
by one so that the
gap remains on
\widetilde{C}
but the pebble
p
that was on
v

is now on the internal vertex of
\widetilde{P}
adjacent to
v
.
Finally, we rotate
\widetilde{C}
to move
p^{\prime}
to
v
, making
p
and
p^{\prime}
adjacent.
∎

Corollary 13
.

Let
G
be a connected, locally finite,
K_{h+1}
-minor-free and edge-transitive graph with two ends.
Let

be the size of a (primary) level set

,
the Cartesian product

is a minor of

, and

that

.

Lemma 14
.

Let
G
be a connected, locally finite,
K_{h+1}
-minor-free, twin-free and edge-transitive graph with two ends.
Let
m\coloneqq|L_{0}|
and let
P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}
be
vertex-disjoint paths connecting the two ends.
If
H\coloneqq H_{P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}}
is a cycle (with at least three vertices), then
m\leq h+1
or
G
is a subdivision of the twisted cylindrical grid.

Proof.

By possibly applying
9
,
we can assume that the paths
P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}
are invariant under some
automorphism of infinite order.
By possibly renaming the indices, we can assume that
H
is the cycle
1,\ldots,m,1
.
Furthermore, we can assume that
m\geq 4
, otherwise if
m=3
, then
h\geq 3
and thus
m=3\leq h+1
.

Case 1.
G
is vertex-transitive:

Fix some
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
let
v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}
be the vertices of
P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}
in
L_{i}

and
w_{1},\ldots,w_{m}
be the vertices of
P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}
in
L_{i+1}
.
Also, define
v_{m+1}\coloneqq v_{1},w_{0}\coloneqq w_{m},w_{m+1}\coloneqq w_{1}
.
We say that a set of edges
X\subseteq E(G)
is
crossing
if
there is some
\ell\in[m]
such that

X\coloneqq\{v_{\ell}w_{\ell},v_{\ell}w_{\ell+1},v_{\ell+1}w_{\ell},v_{\ell+1}w_{\ell+1}\}
.

Claim 1
.

The graph
G
is the twisted cylindrical grid,
or
G[L_{i},L_{i+1}]
has a crossing edge set
X
.

Proof of Claim 1.

Since
H
is a cycle, the degrees in
G[L_{i},L_{i+1}]
are at least two
and at most three.
Consider the subgraph
H^{\prime}\coloneqq H^{[L_{i},L_{i+1}]}_{P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}}\subseteq H

and the directed graph
H_{\rightarrow}^{\prime}
where
there is a directed edge
(\ell,\ell^{\prime})
for each edge
\{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\}\in E(H^{\prime})

for which
v_{\ell}w_{\ell^{\prime}}\in E(G)
.
(Note that an unordered pair
\{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\}\in E(H^{\prime})
has a directed edge in both directions
if and only if
X\coloneqq\{v_{\ell}w_{\ell},v_{\ell}w_{\ell^{\prime}},v_{\ell^{\prime}}w_{\ell},v_{\ell^{\prime}}w_{\ell^{\prime}}\}

is a crossing edge set).
If
G[L_{i},L_{i+1}]
is
(2,2)
-biregular, then
the directed graph
H_{\rightarrow}^{\prime}
has only vertices with an indegree and outdegree of 1,
and thus it is a disjoint union of directed cycles.
If
H_{\rightarrow}^{\prime}
is a single directed cycle, then

E(G[L_{i},L_{i+1}])=\{v_{\ell}w_{\ell}\mid\ell\in[m]\}\cup\{v_{\ell}w_{\ell^{\prime}}\mid\ell\in[m]\}

where
\ell^{\prime}\in\{\ell+1,\ell-1\}
,
and in particular,
G[L_{i},L_{i+1}]
and
G[L_{i+1},L_{i}]
are isomorphic.
By using
6
, we conclude that the graphs
G[L_{k},L_{k+1}],k\in{\mathbb{Z}}

are all pairwise isomorphic and capture all edges of
G
,
and thus
G
is the twisted cylindrical grid.
If
H_{\rightarrow}^{\prime}
is a disjoint union of more than
one directed cycle, then these directed cycles must be of length 2 (two vertices with two directed edges)
since
H^{\prime}
is a subgraph of
H
.
But if
(\ell,\ell^{\prime}),(\ell^{\prime},\ell)
both are directed edges in
H_{\rightarrow}^{\prime}
, then

X\coloneqq\{v_{\ell}w_{\ell},v_{\ell}w_{\ell^{\prime}},v_{\ell^{\prime}}w_{\ell},v_{\ell^{\prime}}w_{\ell^{\prime}}\}\subseteq E(G)

is a crossing edge set.

In the remaining case,
we assume that
G[L_{i},L_{i+1}]
is
(3,3)
-regular,
and we show that there is a crossing edge set
X
.
Indeed, in that case for each
\ell\in[m]
the neighborhood of
v_{\ell}

is precisely
\{w_{\ell-1},w_{\ell},w_{\ell+1}\}
.
Thus, the set
X\coloneqq\{v_{\ell}w_{\ell},v_{\ell}w_{\ell+1},v_{\ell+1}w_{\ell},v_{\ell+1}w_{\ell+1}\}\subseteq E(G)

is a crossing edge set.
This proves the claim.
∎

In case
G[L_{i},L_{i+1}]
has a crossing edge set
X
for some
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we use
the crossing edge set
X
to define new paths as follows. Formally, we
define paths
P_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,P_{m}^{\prime}
as the symmetric difference
of the edge sets of
P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}
and the set of edges
X
.
These are basically the same paths expect
that some end of two paths is swapped.
We consider the graph
H^{\prime}\coloneqq H_{P_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,P_{m}^{\prime}}
.
Since the paths
P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}
are invariant under
some automorphism of infinite order, each edge in
H
is supported infinitely many times.
Thus, if
L
and
R
are the two infinite connected components of
G-(L_{i}\cup L_{i+1})
,
then
H^{L}_{P_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,P_{m}^{\prime}}
and
H^{R}_{P_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,P_{m}^{\prime}}
are both cycles.
However, these two cycles are not identical due to the swap of two paths (and since
m\geq 4
).
Therefore, the graph
H^{\prime}
is a proper supergraph of a cycle,
and in particular 2-connected.
By
10
,
the graph
H^{\prime}\square P_{\infty}
is a minor of
G
,
and it follows from
12
that
m\leq h+1
.

Case 2.
G
is not vertex-transitive:

In that case
G
has a second orbit
O_{2}\neq O_{1}
.

If
|J_{i-\frac{1}{2}}|=|L_{i}|
, then we can use the same arguments as in the vertex-transitive case
and conclude that
m\leq h+1
or
G
is a twisted cylindrical grid.
Otherwise, we have that
|J_{i-\frac{1}{2}}|>m
. If the vertices in
J_{i-\frac{1}{2}}
have degree 2,
then we can dissolve them by deleting each such vertex and adding an edge between its two neighbors.
This gives us a vertex- and edge-transitive (topological) minor of
G
.
Therefore, also in this case, we conclude that
m\leq h+1
or
G

is a subdivision of the twisted cylindrical grid.

In the remaining case, we have that
|J_{i-\frac{1}{2}}|>m
and the degree of vertices in
J_{i-\frac{1}{2}}
within the graph
G[J_{i-\frac{1}{2}},L_{i}]

is at least 2 (and at least 4 in
G
). In the following, we argue that the degrees are exactly 2.
Let
v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}
be the vertices of
P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}
in
J_{i-\frac{1}{2}}

and
w_{1},\ldots,w_{m}
be the vertices of
P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}
in
L_{i}
.
Also, define
v_{m+1}\coloneqq v_{1},w_{m+1}\coloneqq w_{1}
.
Since
|J_{i-\frac{1}{2}}|>m
there is a vertex
v\in J_{i-\frac{1}{2}}

that is not contained in the paths
P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}
.
Since
H
is a cycle, the neighborhood of the vertex
v
in
L_{i}
can only consist of two vertices
w_{\ell},w_{\ell+1}

for some
\ell\in[m]
.
Then, since
G[J_{i-\frac{1}{2}},L_{i}]
is biregular (
6
),
all vertices in
J_{i-\frac{1}{2}}
have degree 2 in
G[J_{i-\frac{1}{2}},L_{i}]
(and 4 in
G
).

In the following, we will use that

G
is twin-free in order to find a crossing edge set (that we will define similarly to the vertex-transitive case).
Consider the 2-element sets
N(v)\cap L_{i}
for all vertices
v\in J_{i-\frac{1}{2}}

(including the vertices that are contained in the fixed paths
P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}
).
Since
H
is a cycle, it holds that
N(v)\cap L_{i}=\{w_{\ell}w_{\ell+1}\}
for some
\ell\in[m]
.
Since
|J_{i-\frac{1}{2}}|>|L_{i}|
, there are two vertices
v,v^{\prime}
having the same neighborhood in
L_{i}
,
i.e., there is an
\ell^{*}\in[m]
such that
N(v)\cap L_{i}=\{w_{\ell^{*}},w_{\ell^{*}+1}\}=N(v^{\prime})\cap L_{i}
.

Consider the case that none of the vertices
v,v^{\prime}

is contained in the paths
P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}
.
Let
u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}
be the vertices of
P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}
in
L_{i-1}
, and set
u_{m+1}\coloneqq u_{1}
.
Then, it holds that
N(v)\cap L_{i-1}=\{u_{\ell^{*}},u_{\ell^{*}+1}\}=N(v^{\prime})\cap L_{i-1}

(for the same
\ell^{*}\in[m]
as above)
since if
u_{k}\in N(v)\cap L_{i-1}
for
k\notin\{\ell^{*},\ell^{*}+1\}
, then there would be
three paths
u_{k},v,w_{\ell^{*}}
and
u_{k},v,w_{\ell^{*}+1}
and
w_{\ell^{*}},v,w_{\ell^{*}+1}
,
contradicting that
H
is a cycle.
Thus,
v
and
v^{\prime}
are twins, contradicting that
G
is twin-free.

Consider the case that both vertices
v,v^{\prime}

are contained in the paths
P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}
.
Since
v,v^{\prime}
have the same neighborhood in
L_{i}
,
there is some
\ell\in[m]
such that
\{v,v^{\prime}\}=\{v_{\ell},v_{\ell+1}\}
.
Without loss of generality, we can assume that
v=v_{\ell},v^{\prime}=v_{\ell+1}
.
Then, we can find a crossing edge set
and swap the paths
P_{\ell}
and
P_{\ell+1}
as follows.
We define new paths
P_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,P_{m}^{\prime}

by deleting the two edges
v_{\ell}w_{\ell},v_{\ell+1}w_{\ell+1}

and adding the two edges
v_{\ell}w_{\ell+1},v_{\ell+1}w_{\ell}
.
With the same argument as in the vertex-transitive case,
the new paths
P_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,P_{m}^{\prime}
lead to a new graph
H^{\prime}
that is 2-connected and not a cycle
such that
H^{\prime}\square P_{\infty}
is a minor of
G
.
We conclude from Lemma
12
that
m\leq h+1
.

Finally, consider the case that the vertex
v
, but not
v^{\prime}
, is contained in
the paths
P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}
.
Without loss of generality assume that
v=v_{\ell}

and that
N(v)\cap L_{i}=\{w_{\ell},w_{\ell+1}\}=N(v^{\prime})\cap L_{i}
.
It holds that
N(v^{\prime})\cap L_{i-1}=\{u_{\ell},u_{\ell+1}\}

(since
H
is a cycle and
v^{\prime}
is not contained in the paths).
Clearly, it holds that
u_{\ell}\in N(v_{\ell})\cap L_{i-1}
.
Again, we can swap the two paths.
We delete all edges in the two paths
u_{\ell},v_{\ell},w_{\ell}
and
u_{\ell+1},v_{\ell+1},w_{\ell+1}

and add the edges in the two paths
u_{\ell},v_{\ell},w_{\ell+1}

and
u_{\ell+1},v^{\prime},w_{\ell}
.
∎

Lemma 15
.

Let
G
be a connected, locally finite,
K_{h+1}
-minor-free, twin-free and edge-transitive graph with two ends.
Then, the automorphism group of
G
is a
\Gamma_{h+1}
-group.

Proof.

By
11
, there are vertex-disjoint paths

P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}
connecting the two ends
such that
H\coloneqq H_{P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}}

is transitive.
As a finite transitive graph, the graph
H
is 2-connected or has at most 2 vertices.
In the latter case, it holds that
|L_{i}|=1<h
or
|L_{i}|=2<h
for all (primary) levels
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
In the former case, we apply

13
and
14
to conclude that

G
is a subdivision of the twisted cylindrical grid or

|L_{i}|\leq h+1
for all
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

In either case, the automorphism group
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
has a normal subgroup
\Delta\trianglelefteq\operatorname{Aut}(G)
that leaves the level sets fixed. The quotient
\operatorname{Aut}(G)/\Delta
is a cyclic or an infinite dihedral group.
If an automorphism in
\Delta
fixes all points in the sets
L_{i}
, it must fix all points since
G
is twin-free.
Since the action on the (primary) level sets is faithful, it thus suffices to consider the action of
\Delta
on the sets

.
If
|L_{i}|\leq h+1
the normal subgroup

is a subgroup of a direct product of symmetric groups

.
If

is a subdivision of the twisted cylindrical grid,
the normal subgroup
\Delta
is a subgroup of a direct product of dihedral groups since

is a cycle.
∎

4
Finite edge-transitive graphs

We now turn to finite edge-transitive graphs. We will first investigate the possible degrees that may occur in
K_{h+1}
-minor-free graphs.

Theorem 16

(Kostochka
[
Kos84
]
)
.

There is a constant
a\geq 1
such that for every
h\geq 1
the average
degree of a
K_{h+1}
-minor-free finite graph is at most
a\cdot h\cdot\sqrt{\log h}
.

In the following, we use
\alpha_{h}\coloneqq\lceil{a\cdot h\cdot\sqrt{\log h}\rceil}

where
a
is the constant of the theorem.
We say that a bipartite graph
G
with bipartition
V_{1},V_{2}
is
left-twin-free

if there are no distinct vertices in
V_{1}
that are twins.

Lemma 17
.

Let
G
be a
(c_{1},c_{2})
-biregular, left-twin-free,

K_{h+1}
-minor-free, bipartite finite graph with bipartition
V_{1},V_{2}
such that
c_{1}\leq c_{2}
. Then, it holds that

c_{2}\leq\alpha_{h}\cdot\left(\binom{\alpha_{h}}{\lceil\alpha_{h}/2\rceil}+1\right)
.

Proof.

The assertion is trivial for
h=1

and for
c_{1}\leq 1
.
Also, note that
c_{1}\leq\alpha\coloneqq\alpha_{h}
by
16
.
Let us
assume that
h\geq 2
and
c_{1}\geq 2
.
Suppose for the sake of contradiction that

c_{2}>\alpha\cdot\left(\binom{\alpha}{\lceil\alpha/2\rceil}+1\right).

(1)

We argue that there is a set of edges
E^{\prime}\subseteq E(G)
such that
each vertex in
V_{1}
is incident with at most one edge of
E^{\prime}
and
each vertex of
V_{2}
is incident with at
least
\binom{\alpha}{\lceil\alpha/2\rceil}
edges
of
E^{\prime}
.
Indeed, create
\binom{\alpha}{\lceil\alpha/2\rceil}
copies of
each vertex of
V_{2}
giving us a new set
V_{2}^{\prime}
in which each vertex
is in a twin class of size
\binom{\alpha}{\lceil\alpha/2\rceil}+1
.
Since
|V_{2}^{\prime}|=\left(\binom{\alpha}{\lceil\alpha/2\rceil}+1\right)\cdot|V_{2}|<\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}\cdot|V_{2}|=|V_{1}|
,
by Hall’s marriage
theorem there is a matching that matches each vertex in
V_{2}^{\prime}
with
a vertex in
V_{1}
. Identifying the twins again yields the desired
set of edges
E^{\prime}
.

Let
M
be the minor of
G
with vertex set
V(M)=V_{2}
obtained from

G
by contracting the edges in
E^{\prime}
.
We show that
M
has
average degree greater than
\alpha
.

Observe that for every
v_{2}\in V_{2}
it holds that

N_{M}(v_{2})\supseteq\bigcup_{v_{1}v_{2}\in E^{\prime}}(N_{G}(v_{1})\setminus\{v_{2}\}).

In the following, we argue that
N_{M}(v_{2})
is large.
First, note that
|\{v_{1}v_{2}\in E^{\prime}\}|>\binom{\alpha}{\lceil\alpha/2\rceil}\geq\binom{\alpha}{c_{1}-1}
.
Since
G
is left-twin-free and all vertices in
V_{1}
have degree
c_{1}
,
the sets
N_{G}(v_{1})\setminus\{v_{2}\}
are mutually distinct sets of size

c_{1}-1\leq\alpha
.
Therefore, we have that
\deg_{M}(v_{2})=|N_{M}(v_{2})|>\alpha
.
Thus, the average degree of
M
is greater than
\alpha
.
By Theorem
16
, the graph

has a
K_{h+1}
-minor, contradicting that

is

K_{h+1}
-minor-free.
∎

Let us briefly record that it is not possible to have a subexponential bound in the previous lemma. Indeed,
for each triple
(t,h,r)
of positive integers with

there is an edge-transitive graph of order
t\binom{h}{r}^{2}+th
that is
connected,
twin-free,

\left(2r,2\binom{h-1}{r-1}\binom{h}{r}\right)
-biregular, and

K_{{\mathcal{O}}(h)}
-minor-free.
For this, let
V
be the set
\mathbb{Z}_{t}\times\{1,\ldots,h\}
.
Let
U
be the set
\mathbb{Z}_{t}\times\binom{\{1,\ldots,h\}}{r}\times\binom{\{1,\ldots,h\}}{r}
.
Connect
u=(i,A_{1},A_{2})\in U
with

if

and also connect
u=(i,A_{1},A_{2})
with

if
j\in A_{2}
.
For all expressions, the first indices are taken modulo

.

Our goal in the rest of this section is to characterize the composition factors of edge-transitive graphs as follows.

Theorem 18
.

There is a function
f
such that every automorphism group of a connected
K_{h+1}
-minor-free, edge-transitive, twin-free and finite graph is contained in
\Gamma_{f(h)}
.

Towards the theorem,
assume for the sake of contradiction that there is an

and an infinite sequence
H_{1},H_{2},H_{3},\ldots
of connected
K_{h+1}
-minor-free, edge-transitive, twin-free finite graphs for which there is no

such that
\operatorname{Aut}(H_{j})\in\Gamma_{d}
for all

.
We can assume that if
\operatorname{Aut}(H_{j+1})\in\Gamma_{d}
, then
\operatorname{Aut}(H_{j})\in\Gamma_{d}
for all
d\geq 0,j\geq 1
.
By
16
and
17
, there is a constant bounding
the degree of each graph in the sequence.
As argued in the preliminaries,
this sequence has a convergent subsequence
G_{1},G_{2},G_{3},\ldots
and a corresponding connected edge-transitive infinite limit graph
\overline{G}
.
Since the balls of
\overline{G}
correspond to balls of graphs

(see

),
the limit graph
\overline{G}
is also twin-free,
K_{h+1}
-minor-free and locally finite.

We perform a case distinction depending on the number of ends of
\overline{G}
and each possibility will give us a contradiction.
As mentioned in the preliminaries,
the number of ends of an infinite, connected, almost vertex-transitive and locally finite graph is one, two or infinite.

One end

Suppose
\overline{G}
has one end. In this case,
we can apply various techniques that have been previously developed for vertex-transitive graphs.
However, we need to ensure that they apply to the edge-transitive case.
We first collect some information on the connectivity of
\overline{G}
.

Lemma 19

(Mader
[
Mad70
]
)
.

A finite, connected and edge-transitive graph of minimum degree
d
has
connectivity at least
d
.

A graph
G
is
almost 4-connected
if it is 3-connected and for every 3-separator
S
the graph
G-S
has exactly two connected components, one of which consists only of one vertex.

Lemma 20
.

In case the limit graph
\overline{G}
has only one end, it is almost-4-connected or
\overline{G}
has two orbits and the vertices in one of the orbits have degree
2
. In the latter case,
\overline{G}
is a subdivision of a vertex-transitive and edge-transitive graph that has the same automorphism group as
\overline{G}
.

Proof.

Let
S
be a minimum separator.
Since
\overline{G}
is locally finite, the separator
S
is finite.
Note that exactly one of the connected components of
\overline{G}-S
is infinite (since
\overline{G}
has one end),
and thus at least one of the connected components of
\overline{G}-S
is finite.
Therefore, for sufficiently large
j
each minimum separator in the graph
G_{j}

has size
|S|
, and thus the connectivity of
G_{j}
and
\overline{G}
coincides.
This implies that
G_{j}
and thus
\overline{G}
has minimum degree
|S|
by Mader’s Theorem (Theorem
19
).

Thus, if
|S|=1
, then the minimum degree of
\overline{G}
is 1, contradicting that
\overline{G}
is edge-transitive,
infinite and twin-free.
If
|S|=2
, then the minimum degree of
\overline{G}
is 2, and therefore

\overline{G}
must have two orbits and the vertices in one of the orbits, say
O
, have degree 2.
In this case, we can replace every path of length 2 that has an internal vertex from
O
by an edge and
obtain a graph
\overline{G}^{\prime}
that is vertex-transitive and has the same automorphism group as
\overline{G}
.

Suppose now that
|S|=3
.
If a finite connected component of
\overline{G}-S
were to contain more than one vertex,
then for sufficiently large
j

there is a separator in
G_{j}
separating more than one vertex.
However, finite 3-connected edge-transitive graphs are known to be almost-4-connected (see for example here
[
ZM08
]
).
Therefore, the finite connected component of
\overline{G}-S
consists of only one vertex,
and thus the limit graph
\overline{G}
is almost-4-connected.
∎

Recall that a graph is almost vertex-transitive
if it has only finitely many vertex orbits under its automorphism group. Note that edge-transitive graphs are almost vertex-transitive.
An end of a graph is
thick
if it contains an infinite collection of
pairwise disjoint one-way infinite paths. We will only use the concept in the following two theorems.

Theorem 21

(
[
Tho92
, Theorem 5.6]
)
.

If
G
is an infinite, locally finite, connected and almost vertex-transitive graph
with only one end, then that end is thick.

Theorem 22

(
[
Tho92
, Theorem 4.1]
)
.

Let
G
be a connected, locally finite, non-planar, almost vertex-transitive,
3-connected and almost-4-connected graph with at least one thick end. Then,
G
is
contractible into an infinite complete graph.

Overall, we conclude that our limit graph
\overline{G}
is planar. We will use a theorem of Babai relating vertex-transitive planar graphs to Archimedean tilings.

Theorem 23

(Babai
[
Bab97
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

Let
G
be a locally finite, connected, vertex-transitive planar graph with at most one end. Then,
G
has an embedding on a natural geometry as an Archimedean tiling. All automorphisms of
G
extend to automorphisms of the tiling and are induced by isometries of the geometry.

Here, the
natural geometries
are the spheres, the Euclidean
plane and hyperbolic geometries. Their
Archimedean tilings
are
tilings by regular polygons such that the group of isometries of the
tiling acts transitively on the vertices of the tiling.
The spherical geometries arise precisely when the graph is finite, which we can rule out since
\overline{G}
is infinite.

We need to deal with the fact that
\overline{G}
might not be vertex-transitive in our case, say having two vertex orbits
O_{1}
and
O_{2}
. However, in that case, we can consider the graph
\widehat{G}
obtained from
\overline{G}
by removing the vertices from
O_{2}
and joining two vertices
v_{1},v_{2}
in
O_{1}
if they have a common neighbor in
O_{2}
and lie on a common face in the (up to reflection locally unique) planar embedding of a sufficiently large neighborhood of
v_{1}
. The graph
\widehat{G}
is also planar but vertex-transitive. (It may have a larger Hadwiger number than
\overline{G}
.) If
\overline{G}
only has one orbit, we simply define
\widehat{G}\coloneqq\overline{G}
.

We will use the following fact about hyperbolic spaces.

Fact 24
.

The circumference of balls in a hyperbolic space grows exponentially with the radius of the ball.

We need some observations that, in the hyperbolic case, allow us to relate distances in the metric space to distances in the graph
\overline{G}
. The next lemma essentially says that distances measured in
\overline{G}
,
\widehat{G}
, and in the natural geometry agree up to a constant factor and that there can be no dead ends of unbounded depth (see
[
Leh09
]
for more information on dead ends in groups).

Lemma 25
.

Assume the Archimedean geometry of the tiling of the graph
\widehat{G}
is hyperbolic. Consider the graphs
\overline{G}
,
\widehat{G}
and the hyperbolic metric space
X
into which
\widehat{G}
has an embedding. Then, it holds that
V(\widehat{G})\subseteq X
,
V(\widehat{G})\subseteq V(\overline{G})
and there are positive constants
c_{1},c_{2}
and
c_{3}
(depending on
\widehat{G}
) so that

1.

for all vertices
v,v^{\prime}\in V(\widehat{G})
we have
\frac{1}{c_{1}}\cdot d_{\widehat{G}}(v,v^{\prime})\leq d_{\overline{G}}(v,v^{\prime})\leq c_{1}\cdot d_{\widehat{G}}(v,v^{\prime})
,

2.

for all vertices
v,v^{\prime}\in V(\widehat{G})
we have
\frac{1}{c_{2}}\cdot d_{\overline{G}}(v,v^{\prime})\leq d_{X}(v,v^{\prime})\leq c_{2}\cdot d_{\overline{G}}(v,v^{\prime})
, and

3.

for every pair of vertices
v,v^{\prime}\in V(\overline{G})

there is a vertex
w\in V(\overline{G})
with
d_{\overline{G}}(v^{\prime},w)\leq c_{3}
and
d_{\overline{G}}(v,w)>d_{\overline{G}}(v,v^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Part
1
follows from the fact that
\overline{G}
is a 3-connected, edge-transitive, planar graph, and thus the neighbors of vertices in
V(\overline{G})\setminus V(\widehat{G})

have bounded distance in
V(\widehat{G})
.

Part
2
follows from the fact that there is an absolute bound on the diameter of the tiles in the Archimedean tiling.

Finally, Part
3
follows from the fact that the statement
is true in the space
X
and that every point in
X
is of bounded
distance from
V(\overline{G})
.
∎

We can now use Babai’s sphere packing argument to rule out that the Archimedean geometry of the vertex-transitive graph
\widehat{G}
is hyperbolic (i.e., has negative curvature).

Lemma 26
.

If the Archimedean geometry of the tiling of the graph
\widehat{G}
is hyperbolic, then we have
\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\Had(G_{j})=\infty
.

Proof.

By the previous lemma, distances in
\overline{G}
agree with distances
in
\widehat{G}
and with distances in the hyperbolic metric space

up to a constant factor.
We can therefore use Fact
24
to conclude the following. There are superlinear functions
f_{1},f_{2}\in\omega(t)
so that for

we can,
on the boundary of the ball of radius
t
, that is in
\delta B_{t,\overline{G}}(\overline{v})\coloneqq B_{t,\overline{G}}(\overline{v})\setminus B_{t-1,\overline{G}}(\overline{v})=N(B_{t-1,\overline{G}}(\overline{v}))
, find
f_{1}(t)
distinct vertices with a pairwise distance of at least
f_{2}(t)
outside of

(i.e., the distance is measured in
\overline{G}-B_{t-c_{3},\overline{G}}(\overline{v})

Let

be some integer. Choose
j_{0}
sufficiently
large so that for

the balls of radius
(3+c_{3})t
in
G_{j}
are
isomorphic to balls of radius
(3+c_{3})t
in
\overline{G}
. Let
S
be an
inclusion-wise maximal set in
G_{j}
of vertices of pairwise distance at
least
2t

G_{j}
to a vertex of

of
closest distance, ties broken arbitrarily.
This gives us a minor
H
of
G_{j}
. We claim that the minimum

tends to infinity as

tends to infinity. This will
show the statement by Theorem
16
.

Consider the ball
B_{t,G_{j}}(v)
around a vertex

of

of
f_{1}(t)
vertices in
G_{j}
at distance

from

that have pairwise distance at least
\min\{f_{2}(t),2(2+c_{3})t+1\}
outside of

(3+c_{3})t

\overline{G}
are isomorphic to balls of

(3+c_{3})t

and vertices of

2(2+c_{3})t+1

For each

in

that is relatively close to

as follows.
We start a walk in

. We take at most
c_{3}

than

. We repeat the process.
Overall we obtain a walk
y_{0},y_{1},y_{2},\ldots

with a subsequence

so that

and

Let

This means that

Note that for the minor

It suffices now to argue that the

This can be seen as follows. The distance between

Lemma 27
.

If the limit graph
\overline{G}
is planar with one end then, for sufficiently large
j
, the graph
G_{j}
has Euler characteristic 0.

Proof.

By
26
, we know that the graph
\widehat{G}
can be
interpreted as Archimedean tiling of the Euclidean plane.

Suppose
\widehat{G}
has degree
d
.
Pick an arbitrary vertex
v
and let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{d}
be the number of edges for each of the
t
faces incident with
v
.
Since
\widehat{G}
is vertex-transitive, the numbers
f_{1},\ldots,f_{d}
do not depend on
v
.
(We actually know that there can be at most two different face sizes, i.e.,
|\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{d}\}|\leq 2
, but we do not use this fact.)
The arguments in
[
Bab97
]
in fact tell us that the curvature of the space on which the tiling acts can be described in terms of the face sizes around a vertex. In particular, we know that
\sum_{i=1}^{d}(1/2-1/f_{i})=1
, since otherwise the Archimedean tiling will not be on the Euclidean plane.

The arguments in
[
Bab91
, Section 6.2]
show
that, for sufficiently large
j
, the graph
G_{j}
has locally a unique
planar embedding. Babai further argues that these local embeddings are locally consistent and
overall give us an embedding of
G_{j}
onto some surface. In analogy to
our previous operation, we can construct the graph
\widehat{G_{j}}
by
removing vertices from orbit
O_{2}
and joining vertices of
O_{1}
if
they are at distance 2 and share a face. As explained in
[
Bab91
, Section 6.2]
, the
graph
\widehat{G_{j}}
satisfies
\sum_{i=1}^{d}(1/2-1/f_{i})=1
. This
implies that the embedding of
\widehat{G_{j}}
and thus the embedding
of
G_{j}
has Euler characteristic 0. (By double counting, a vertex of
degree
d
contributes 1 vertex,
d/2
edges and
1/f_{i}
faces
to each adjacent face, so in total
\sum_{i\in[d]}1/f_{i}
to the
faces. Thus, the contribution to the Euler characteristic is
1-d/2+\sum_{i\in[d]}1/f_{i}=0
for each vertex).
∎

Overall, we have proven that for
j
sufficiently large the graph
G_{j}
admits an embedding on the torus or the Klein bottle. Now, we can use Babai’s classification or Thomassen’s classification for such graphs.
In particular, the automorphism group
\Aut(G_{j})
has an abelian normal subgroup
of index at most 12 that is generated by at most two elements. Moreover, the automorphism group
\Aut(G_{j})
is solvable.

Infinitely many ends

For the vertex-transitive case with infinitely many ends, Babai proved the following theorem.

Theorem 28

(
[
Bab91
, Theorem 5.4]
)
.

Suppose
G_{1},G_{2},G_{3},\ldots
is a convergent sequence of finite
connected vertex-transitive graphs of bounded degree. If the limit graph
\overline{G}
of the sequence has more than two ends, then
\lim_{j\to\infty}\Had(G_{j})=\infty
.

An inspection of the proof of the theorem shows that it can be extended to the edge-transitive case.

Theorem 29
.

Suppose that
G_{1},G_{2},G_{3},\ldots
is a convergent sequence of finite
connected edge-transitive graphs of bounded degree. If the limit
\overline{G}
of the sequence has more than two ends, we have
\lim_{j\to\infty}\Had(G_{j})=\infty
.

Proof.

We can essentially apply Babai’s proof
[
Bab91
, Theorem 5.4]
with obvious adaptations accounting for the possibility of two orbits as follows.
Choose
t
such that for every vertex
\overline{v}\in V(\overline{G})
and ball
B_{t,\overline{G}}(\overline{v})
the graph
\overline{G}-B_{t,\overline{G}}(\overline{v})
has at least
m
infinite components (this is possible since there can only be two isomorphism types of balls).
Choose
j_{0}
so that for
j\geq j_{0}
balls of radius
4t
in
G_{j}
are isomorphic to balls of radius
4t
in
\overline{G}
.
Let
S
be a maximal set of points in
G_{j}
of pairwise distance at least
2t
.

Assign every vertex
x\in V(G_{j})
to a vertex of
s_{x}\in S
of closest distance, ties broken arbitrarily. This gives us a minor
H
of
G_{j}
, where two vertices are in the same branch set if they are assigned to the same vertex of
S
.
We claim that the minimum degree of
H
is at least
m
.
This will show the statement by Theorem
16
.

Pick a vertex
v\in S
.
In the limit graph
\overline{G}
the graph
\overline{G}-B_{t,\overline{G}}(\overline{v})
has at least
m
infinite components, so in each component we can choose vertices
\overline{v}_{1},\ldots,\overline{v}_{m}
at distance
2t
from
\overline{v}\in V(\overline{G})
(the vertex corresponding to
v
).

Let
v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}\in V(G_{j})
be the vertices corresponding to
\overline{v}_{1},\ldots,\overline{v}_{m}\in V(\overline{G})
in
G_{j}
obtained by the isomorphism of the balls from
\overline{G}
to
G_{j}
.
Note that the distance
\operatorname{dist}_{G_{j}}(v_{i},s_{v_{i}})
is at most

(otherwise, we could add

to the set

),
in particular,
v\neq s_{v_{i}}
.
Then, the distance between
v
and a vertex
s_{v_{i}}
is at least
2t
(since

) and at most

.
(A vertex-colored
H_{\chi}=(H,\chi)
minor is invariant
if additionally
\chi^{\varphi}
is invariant under automorphisms of
G
.)

Lemma 31
.

If
H_{\chi}=(H,\chi)
is an
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{\chi})
-invariant vertex-colored minor of a vertex-colored graph
G_{\chi}=(G,\chi)
, then there is a homomorphism from
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{\chi})
to
\operatorname{Aut}(H_{\chi})
whose kernel is a subgroup of the direct product of
automorphism groups of the vertex-colored graphs induced by the branch sets.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\colon V(H)\to{\mathcal{B}}\subseteq 2^{V(G)}
be an
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{\chi})
-invariant minor model of
H
in
G
.
Since
V(H)^{\varphi},E(H)^{\varphi}
and
\chi^{\varphi}
are invariant under
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{\chi})
, the group
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{\chi})
acts on
V(H)
via
v^{\gamma}\coloneqq v^{\varphi\gamma\varphi^{-1}}
for
v\in V(H)
,
preserving edges and colors.
This leads to a homomorphism
g\colon\operatorname{Aut}(G_{\chi})\to\operatorname{Aut}(H_{\chi})

where the kernel of
g
is a subgroup of the direct product
\bigtimes_{v\in V(H)}\operatorname{Aut}(G_{\chi}[\varphi(v)])
.
∎

Lemma 32
.

There is a function
f
such that if
G_{\chi}=(G,\chi)
is a connected,
K_{h+1}
-minor-free
vertex-colored graph,
then for every vertex orbit
O
of minimum cardinality the subgroup induced by
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{\chi})
on
O
is a
\Gamma_{f(h)}
-group.

Proof.

Case 1.
G_{\chi}
is edge-transitive:

To apply Theorem
18
, we need to get rid of twins.
We call the set of twins of a vertex the
twin class
of this vertex.
Let
O_{1}
be a minimum cardinality orbit.

Claim 1
.

The size
c
of the twin class of a vertex in
O_{1}
is at most
h
.

Proof of Claim 1.

If
G_{\chi}

is vertex-transitive and has twin classes of size
c
,
then
c=|V(G)|\leq h
or
G
has the complete bipartite graph
K_{c,c}
as a minor.
In this case, the complete graph
K_{c}
is a minor (a matching is a minor model for
K_{c}
in

K_{c,c}
), and thus
c\leq h
. Assume that
G_{\chi}
has exactly two orbits
O_{1},O_{2}
where

|O_{1}|\leq|O_{2}|
. Let
G^{\prime}
be the graph obtained from
G
by
removing all but one twin in each twin class in
O_{1}
and let

O_{1}^{\prime},O_{2}
be its orbits.
Note that
|O_{1}^{\prime}|=\frac{1}{c}|O_{1}|\leq\frac{1}{c}|O_{2}|
.
Since
G^{\prime}
is biregular and every vertex in
O_{2}
has a neighbor in
O_{1}
, the degree of each vertex in
O_{1}^{\prime}
is at least

c
.
Therefore, the graph
G
has a
K_{c,c}
-minor implying that
c\leq h
.
∎

Let
G^{\prime\prime}
be the (uncolored) graph that is obtained from
G
by removing all but one twin in each twin class (of
O_{1}
as well as
O_{2}
).
Note that
G^{\prime\prime}
is connected, twin-free, edge-transitive and uncolored.
This allows us to
apply
18
to
G^{\prime\prime}
. Thus, it holds that
\Aut(G^{\prime\prime})\in\Gamma_{f(h)}
.
Since the class
\Gamma_{f(h)}
is closed under subgroups
and wreath products with symmetric (base) groups
S_{c}\in\Gamma_{f(h)}
where
c\leq h\leq f(h)
, the result also follows for the vertex-colored graph
G_{\chi}

with twin classes in
O_{1}
of size at most
c
. (Note that
h\leq f(h)
due to the graph
K_{h}
.)

Case 2.
G_{\chi}
is not edge-transitive but vertex-transitive:

Let
\widehat{E}\subseteq E(G)
be an edge
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{\chi})
-orbit and consider the graph
\widehat{G}\coloneqq(V(G),\widehat{E})
induced by
\widehat{E}
.
Let
G^{*}_{\chi}\coloneqq(G^{*},\chi^{*})
be the vertex-colored minor of
G_{\chi}
obtained by contracting the edges
\widehat{E}

where
\chi^{*}
is the vertex-coloring induced by
\chi
(the connected components of
\widehat{G}

get assigned a fresh color,
and the remaining vertices keep their old color according to
\chi
).

Note that
G^{*}_{\chi}
is an
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{\chi})
-invariant vertex-colored minor of
G_{\chi}
.
By induction, it holds that
\operatorname{Aut}(G^{*}_{\chi})\in\Gamma_{f(h)}

and
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{\chi}[Z])\in\Gamma_{f(h)}
for each connected component
Z
of
\widehat{G}
.
By Lemma
31
, it follows that
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{\chi})
is in
\Gamma_{f(h)}
.

Case 3.
G_{\chi}
is not edge-transitive and not vertex-transitive:

Let
O_{1}
be a vertex
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{\chi})
-orbit of minimum size, and
let
O_{2}
be a vertex
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{\chi})
-orbit adjacent to
O_{1}
. Let
\widehat{E}
be an edge
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{\chi})
-orbit whose edges have end points in both
O_{1}
and
O_{2}
. Consider the graph
\widehat{G}\coloneqq(V(G),\widehat{E})
induced by
\widehat{E}
.
Consider the set
{\mathcal{Z}}
of connected components of
\widehat{G}
.
For each connected component
Z\in{\mathcal{Z}}
of
\widehat{G}
,
we define the vertex-colored graph
G_{Z,\chi}\coloneqq(G[Z],\chi^{\prime})

where
\chi^{\prime}(v)\coloneqq(\chi(v),i)
for
v\in Z
and
i\in\{1,2\}
is defined such that
v\in O_{i}
.
Then, for each connected component
Z\in{\mathcal{Z}}
of
\widehat{G}
the vertex-colored graph
G_{Z,\chi}
has exactly two
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{Z,\chi})
-orbits
O_{1}\cap Z\neq O_{2}\cap Z
,
and for these two orbits it holds that
|O_{1}\cap Z|\leq|O_{2}\cap Z|
.
Now, consider the
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{\chi})
-invariant minor
G^{*}_{\chi}\coloneqq(G^{*},\chi^{*})
that is obtained from
G
by contracting the connected components of
\widehat{G}

where
\chi^{*}
is the vertex-coloring induced by
\chi
(as defined in the previous case).
Note that
O^{*}\coloneqq{\mathcal{Z}}
is the unique smallest
\operatorname{Aut}(G^{*}_{\chi})
-orbit of
G^{*}
.
By induction, for each branch set
Z\in{\mathcal{Z}}
it holds that
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{Z,\chi})[O_{1}\cap Z]\in\Gamma_{f(h)}

and also for
G^{*}_{\chi}
it holds that
\operatorname{Aut}(G^{*}_{\chi})[O^{*}]\in\Gamma_{f(h)}
.
By Lemma
31
, it follows that
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{\chi})[O_{1}]
is in
\Gamma_{f(h)}
.
∎

Lemma 33
.

If
G_{\chi}=(G,\chi)
is a vertex-colored disconnected graph, then
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{\chi})
is a direct product of wreath products with the symmetric group as a top group of the automorphism groups induced on the connected components of
G
.

Proof.

If the graphs consists of
t
isomorphic connected graphs, we obtain precisely the wreath product with the symmetric group
S_{t}
. Partitioning the components by isomorphism type, we obtain the direct product of the corresponding wreath products.
∎

Theorem 34
.

If
G
is a
K_{h+1}
-minor-free graph, then
\operatorname{Aut}(G)\in\Theta_{f(h)}
.

Proof.

We show the more general statement for vertex-colored graphs
G_{\chi}=(G,\chi)
by induction on the size of
G

and the number of color classes.

If
G
is disconnected, then by induction and by Lemma
33
, the automorphism group is a direct product of wreath products with symmetric (top) groups and (base) groups in
\Theta_{f(h)}
.

If there is an
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{\chi})
-orbit
O
of size exactly one, we can get rid of this orbit
by removing this vertex in
O
from the graph and by coloring the neighbors accordingly.
More precisely, we define a vertex-colored graph
G_{\chi}^{\prime}\coloneqq(G-O,\chi^{\prime})
where

\chi^{\prime}(v)\coloneqq(\chi(v),N_{G}(v)\cap O)
for
v\in V(G)\setminus O
.
Now, the group
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{\chi})
is isomorphic to
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{\chi}^{\prime})
, and thus the theorem follows by induction.

We can thus assume that the smallest
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{\chi})
-orbit has size at least 2 and that
G
is connected.
Let
O
be an orbit of minimum size. The group
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{\chi})
acts naturally on
O
via the induced action.
By Lemma
32
, we know that the induced group on the orbit is in
\Gamma_{f(h)}
. Consider the kernel of this homomorphism. It suffices to show that this kernel is in
\Theta_{f(h)}
since
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{\chi})
is an extension of this kernel by the
\Gamma_{f(h)}
-action on
O
.
The kernel consists of the automorphisms that fix all points in
O
. We individualize all vertices in that orbit by refining the coloring.
More precisely, we define a vertex-colored graph
G_{\chi}^{\prime}\coloneqq(G,\chi^{\prime})

where
\chi^{\prime}(v)=(\chi(v),0)
for all
v\in V(G)\setminus O
and
\chi^{\prime}(v)=(v,1)
for all
v\in O
.
Then, the kernel is equal to
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{\chi}^{\prime})
, and is in
\Theta_{f(h)}
by induction.
∎

6
Babai’s conjectures

We now state three of Babai’s conjectures that can for example be found in
[
Bab81
]
, and argue that our structural analysis shows that indeed each conjecture holds.

Theorem 35
.

There is a function
f
such that a composition factor of the automorphism group of a graph of Hadwiger number at most
h
is cyclic, alternating or has order at most
f(h)
.

Proof.

This follows directly from Theorem
34

since groups in
\Theta_{f(h)}
have the desired composition factors.
∎

Theorem 36
.

Only finitely many non-cyclic simple groups are represented by graphs of bounded Hadwiger number.

Proof.

Again this follows directly from Theorem
34
since
non-cyclic simple groups in
\Theta_{f(h)}
have bounded order.
∎

The third conjecture states that if the order of the automorphism group of a graph of bounded Hadwiger number does not have small prime divisors, then the group is a repeated direct and wreath product of abelian groups.

Recall the parameter
\alpha_{h}\in{\mathcal{O}}(h\cdot\sqrt{\log h})
from

16
bounding the average degree in
K_{h+1}
-minor-free graphs.

Lemma 37
.

Let
G
be a connected,
K_{h+1}
-minor-free graph.
Let
\Delta\leq\operatorname{Aut}(G)
be a subgroup such that all
prime factors of
|\Delta|

are greater than
\alpha_{h}
,
and let
O
be a minimum cardinality
\Delta
-orbit.
Then, the induced group
\Delta[O]
is regular and abelian.
Furthermore, if
\Delta
is fixed-point free, then there is a fixed-point-free element
\delta\in\Delta
.

Proof.

We can assume that
|V(G)|>1
, otherwise we are done.

Case 1.
\Delta
is transitive:

Note that in this case the graph
G
is vertex-transitive since
\Delta\leq\operatorname{Aut}(G)
.

We argue first that
\Delta
is regular, i.e., that all point
stabilizers are trivial (in addition to transitivity). Indeed, if this
were not the case, then since
G
is connected there would be a vertex
v
so that in the
point stabilizer
\Delta_{v}\leq\Delta
there is an automorphism that
moves neighbors of
v
.
However, Theorem
16
implies that the number of neighbors of
v
is at
most
\alpha_{h}
(since
G
is regular),
which would lead to an
automorphism whose order has a prime factor of size at most
\alpha_{h}
.

The regularity also implies that there is a fixed-point-free element
\delta\in\Delta
, in fact all non-trivial elements in
\Delta
are fixed-point free.

Claim 1
.

Each edge orbit under
\Delta\leq\operatorname{Aut}(G)
is a disjoint union of cycles
of length at least
\alpha_{h}
.

Proof of Claim 1.

Consider now an edge
e=x_{1}x_{2}\in E(G)
with a direction
(x_{1},x_{2})
say. Let
E_{\rightarrow}
be the
\Delta
-orbit of
the directed edge
(x_{1},x_{2})
under
\Delta
.
Since
\Delta
is transitive, for every vertex
v
there is at least one directed edge
(v,w)
for some
w\in V(G)
in the
\Delta
-orbit of
(x_{1},x_{2})
. Since
\Delta
is regular, there is at most one such edge.
Thus, the (directed) graph
(V(G),E_{\rightarrow})
is a disjoint union of (directed) cycles.
These cycles all have length at least
\alpha_{h}
,
due to the absence of small factors in
|\Delta|
. Since
e
was arbitrary, the statement holds for all edge
\Delta
-orbits.
∎

Let
\widehat{E}\subsetneq E(G)
be a maximal union of edge orbits under
\Delta
so
that
\widehat{G}\coloneqq(V(G),\widehat{E})

is not connected (possibly
\widehat{E}
is empty).
If
\widehat{G}
is edgeless, then by
1
, the graph
G
contains an edge
\Delta
-orbit that is
one single cycle, and thus
\Delta\leq\operatorname{Aut}(G)
is a cyclic group.
In the following, we thus assume that
\widehat{G}
has at least one edge.
Note that all components of
\widehat{G}
induce isomorphic graphs.
Let
{\mathcal{Z}}
be the set of connected components of
\widehat{G}
,
and let
G^{*}\coloneqq G/\widehat{E}
be the minor that is obtained by contracting all edges in
\widehat{E}

(or equivalently all connected components
Z\in{\mathcal{Z}}
).
Let
e_{0}\in E(G)
be an edge not in
\widehat{E}
(so that
e_{0}
has endpoints in two distinct connected components
Z,Z^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{Z}}
) and let
E_{0}\subseteq E(G)
be its
\Delta
-orbit.

Claim 2
.

There is a cyclic order
Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n}\in{\mathcal{Z}}
of the connected
components such that all edges in
E_{0}
lie between two consecutive connected
components
Z_{i},Z_{i+1}
(where
Z_{n+1}\coloneqq Z_{1}
). Moreover, the edge set
E_{0}

induces a perfect matching between each pair of consecutive connected
components
Z_{i},Z_{i+1}
.

Proof of Claim 2.

Consider the natural homomorphism
g\colon\operatorname{Aut}(G)\to\operatorname{Aut}(G^{*})
with image

\Delta^{*}\coloneqq g(\Delta)\leq\operatorname{Aut}(G^{*})
. Note that

\Delta^{*}\leq\operatorname{Aut}(G^{*})
is transitive. Since
\Delta^{*}
is a
homomorphic image of
\Delta
, all prime factors of
|\Delta^{*}|
are
greater than
\alpha_{h}
. Moreover, the factor graph
G^{*}

is a minor of
G
, and thus it is
K_{h+1}
-minor free as well.
Thus,

1
also holds for
G^{*}
and
\Delta^{*}
. For this
reason, the image of
E_{0}
in
G^{*}
is a disjoint union of
cycles. However, by the definition of
\widehat{E}
, it must be one
single cycle (otherwise
E_{0}
would have been added to
\widehat{E}
). This shows the
desired cyclic ordering of the connected components in
{\mathcal{Z}}
.

In order to show that
E_{0}
induces a matching, we
consider the bipartite graph that is induced by
E_{0}
on two
consecutive connected components
Z_{i},Z_{i+1}
. On the one hand,
all vertices in this bipartite graph have degree at most 1 (by

1
). On the other hand, there are no isolated
vertices since each vertex in
Z_{i}\cup Z_{i+1}
is adjacent to some edge in

E_{0}
(by transitivity of
\Delta
).
∎

By
2
, the factor graph
G^{*}
contains an edge

\Delta^{*}
-orbit that is a cycle.
We say that a connected component
Z

has a spanning cycle

if there is an edge
\Delta
-orbit that induces a cycle on
Z
.

Case 1.a. There is a spanning cycle for some (and thus for all)
Z\in{\mathcal{Z}}
:

Let
E_{c}
be an edge
\Delta
-orbit inducing a cycle on
Z
, let
e_{c}=x_{1}x_{2}\in E_{c}
be an edge
with an orientation
(x_{1},x_{2})
say, and let
E_{\rightarrow}
be the
\Delta
-orbit of
(x_{1},x_{2})
.
Clearly, the (directed) edge set
E_{\rightarrow}
induces a directed cycle on each
Z\in{\mathcal{Z}}
.

Suppose that
E_{\rightarrow}\cup E_{0}
is
locally a grid
,
i.e., there are vertices
v,w\in Z,v^{\prime},w^{\prime}\in Z^{\prime}
such that

(v,w),(v^{\prime},w^{\prime})\in E_{\rightarrow}
and
vv^{\prime},ww^{\prime}\in E_{0}
. Take an automorphism

\delta\in\Delta
that maps
v
to
w
(and thus
v^{\prime}
to
w^{\prime}
since
\delta
maps each
Z\in{\mathcal{Z}}
to itself), and
take an automorphism
\delta^{\prime}\in\Delta
that maps
v
to
v^{\prime}
(and
thus
w
to
w^{\prime}
). Then, it holds that

v^{\delta\delta^{\prime}}=w^{\prime}=v^{\delta^{\prime}\delta}
. By regularity of
\Delta
,
we have that
\delta\delta^{\prime}=\delta^{\prime}\delta
, and thus the automorphisms
commute.
Note that
\delta
and
\delta^{\prime}
generate
\Delta

since the generated group is transitive on
V(G)
and
\Delta
is regular.
Thus, the group

\Delta
is abelian.

Now, suppose
E_{\rightarrow}\cup E_{0}
is not locally a grid. We construct a minor as follows.
Recall that
n=|V(G^{*})|>\alpha_{h}
and that
m\coloneqq|Z|>\alpha_{h}
for
Z\in{\mathcal{Z}}
(by
1
).
Let
Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n}\in{\mathcal{Z}}
be the connected components in their cyclic order, i.e.,

Z_{i},Z_{i+1}
are matched via
E_{0}
(where
Z_{n+1}=Z_{1}
).
First, we delete all edges between
Z_{n}
and
Z_{1}
.
This leads to
m
vertex-disjoint
E_{0}
-paths
P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}
with
n
vertices (and
n-1
edges) each.
We define
H
as the minor with
m
vertices
that is obtained by contracting each path
P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}

to a single vertex.

Claim 3
.

The minor
H
has average degree greater than
\alpha_{h}
.

Proof of Claim 3.

Let
C_{1}\coloneqq v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}
be the (directed)
E_{\rightarrow}
-cycle in

Z_{1}
. By possibly renaming the indices of the paths, we can
assume that
v_{i}
belongs to
P_{i}
for all
i\in[m]
. For all

i\in[n]
let
C_{i}
be the (directed)
E_{\rightarrow}
-cycle in
Z_{i}
. The existence of the
matching between
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
implies that if an automorphism

\delta\in\Delta
rotates the cycle
C_{1}
by one, it rotates the
cycle
C_{2}
by some positive integer
k\geq 1
(and in fact

k\geq 2
since we do not have a local grid). By the regularity of

\Delta
, the integer
k
is co-prime to
m
. The fact that

\Delta
acts cyclic on
{\mathcal{Z}}
implies that consecutive cycles

Z_{i},Z_{i+1}
are matched isomorphically,
and thus if
C_{i}
is rotated by one, then
C_{i+1}
is rotated by

k
(for the same
k
as above). In general, if an automorphism rotates
C_{1}
by
one, then for all
i\in[n]
the cycle
C_{i}
is rotated by
k^{i-1}

modulo
m
. Now, let
n_{0}
be the largest integer such that

k^{0},k^{1},k^{2},\ldots,k^{n_{0}-1}
are pairwise distinct modulo
m
.
Since
k
is co-prime to
m
and since
k^{n}\equiv k^{0}\mod m
,
it follows that
n_{0}
is a divisor of
n
, and
thus it holds that
n_{0}>\alpha_{h}
. Finally,
H
can be described as
a graph with vertex set
v_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,v_{m}^{\prime}
and an edge set
E(H)
that
is a union of
n_{0}
cycles
C_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,C_{n_{0}}^{\prime}
being pairwise
edge-disjoint (when viewed as directed cycles). Then, each vertex in
H
has degree
2n_{0}>\alpha_{h}

(in particular,
(v_{1}^{\prime},v_{j}^{\prime})
is a directed edge in
C_{i}^{\prime}
if and
only if
j-1\equiv k^{i-1}\mod m
).
∎

Combining
3
with
16
implies
that
G
has a
K_{h+1}
minor.

Case 1.b. There is no spanning cycle for
Z\in{\mathcal{Z}}
:

In the remaining case, the graph
\widehat{G}
has connected components

Z\in{\mathcal{Z}}
that do not contain spanning cycles.
We show that also in
this case
G
has a
K_{h+1}
minor. By
2
, there are
(consecutive) connected components
Z,Z^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{Z}}
and an edge orbit

E_{0}
that induces a matching between
Z
and
Z^{\prime}
. Our strategy is to find a collection of disjoint cycles in
Z
and a collection of disjoint cycles in
Z^{\prime}
so that each cycle in
Z
is adjacent to many cycles on
Z^{\prime}
via the matching and vice versa. Contracting the cycles will give a minor of large average degree.

For a set
E
and
Z\in{\mathcal{Z}}
,
let
{\mathcal{Z}}_{E,Z}
be the set of
connected components of
(V(G),E)[Z]
.
Pick an edge

e^{\prime}\subseteq Z^{\prime}
such that the number of connected components of the edge
\Delta
-orbit
E^{\prime}
is as
small as possible, i.e.,
|{\mathcal{Z}}_{E^{\prime},Z^{\prime}}|
is minimal. Assume that
e^{\prime}=v^{\prime}w^{\prime}
and let
v,w\in Z
be the
vertices that are matched via
E_{0}
with
v^{\prime}
and
w^{\prime}
, respectively. Let

F
be the
\Delta
-orbit of
vw
restricted to
Z
(and note that the set
F
might consist of non-edges).
Note that
|{\mathcal{Z}}_{F,Z}|=|{\mathcal{Z}}_{E^{\prime},Z^{\prime}}|>1
since
Z^{\prime}
has no spanning cycle.
Therefore, we can find an edge
orbit
E\subseteq E(G)
such that each connected component
in
{\mathcal{Z}}_{E,Z}
intersects at least two connected components in
{\mathcal{Z}}_{F,Z}
.
On the other hand, also each connected component of
{\mathcal{Z}}_{F,Z}
intersects
at least two connected components in
{\mathcal{Z}}_{E,Z}

since
|{\mathcal{Z}}_{F,Z}|=|{\mathcal{Z}}_{E^{\prime},Z^{\prime}}|\leq|{\mathcal{Z}}_{E,Z}|
by the minimal choice of
E^{\prime}
.
We define the minor
H
of
G
by
restricting the graph to
Z\cup Z^{\prime}
and contract all edges in
E
and
all edges in
E^{\prime}
.

Claim 4
.

The minor
H
has average degree greater than
\alpha_{h}
.

Proof of Claim 4.

It suffices to show that each connected component in
{\mathcal{Z}}_{E,Z}
intersects more than
\alpha_{h}

connected components in
{\mathcal{Z}}_{F,Z}
,
and each connected component in
{\mathcal{Z}}_{F,Z}
intersects more than
\alpha_{h}
connected components in
{\mathcal{Z}}_{E,Z}
. If
Z\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{F,Z}
intersects the connected components
Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{t}\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{E,Z},t\geq 2
,
then there is a permutation
\delta\in\Delta
that rotates the
F
-cycle in
Z
(stabilizing

Claim 5
.

For all connected components

Proof of Claim 5.

Assume for the sake of contradiction that there is a vertex

Then, since all edges of

Regarding the parameter

Theorem 38
.

Let
G
be a
K_{h+1}
-minor-free graph
such that all
prime factors of
|\operatorname{Aut}(G)|
are greater than

\max\{\alpha_{h},2\}
.
Then, the automorphism group
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
is a repeated direct and wreath product of abelian groups.

Proof.

To facilitate induction over
|V(G)|
, we prove the statement for vertex-colored
graphs
G_{\chi}=(G,\chi)
.
In the base case when
|V(G)|=1
, there is nothing to show.

If
G
had isomorphic connected components, then
\Aut(G_{\chi})
would have
an automorphism of order 2. Thus, the automorphism group
\Aut(G_{\chi})
is the direct product of
the automorphism groups of the connected components.
We can thus assume that
G
is connected.

By applying
37
to the (uncolored) graph
G
with
\Delta\coloneqq\operatorname{Aut}(G_{\chi})
,
we conclude that there is a (minimal)
\Aut(G_{\chi})
-orbit
O
such that

\operatorname{Aut}(G_{\chi})[O]

is abelian. Let
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{\chi})_{(O)}
be the pointwise stabilizer of
O
.
Consider the fixed points
F\supseteq O
of
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{\chi})_{(O)}
.
By definition of

, the induced group
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{\chi})[F]
is isomorphic to

\operatorname{Aut}(G_{\chi})[O]
,
and thus abelian.
Let
{\mathcal{Z}}
be the connected components of

G-F
.

Claim 1
.

|N_{G}(Z)|\leq h
for each
Z\in{\mathcal{Z}}
.

Proof of Claim 1.

Assume for the sake of contraction that
|N_{G}(Z)|>h
.
We construct a
K_{h+1}
minor as follows.
Since the automorphism group order
|\operatorname{Aut}(G_{\chi})|
does not have 2 as a prime factor,
the group
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{\chi})_{(O)}\leq\operatorname{Aut}(G_{\chi})
fixes all connected components
setwise (otherwise there is a permutation in
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{\chi})_{(O)}
that swaps two components in
{\mathcal{Z}}
which must have even order).
The group
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{\chi})_{(O)}
induces on

a group
\Delta_{Z}\coloneqq\operatorname{Aut}(G_{\chi})_{(O)}[Z]
.
Note that
\Delta_{Z}\leq\operatorname{Aut}(G_{\chi}[Z])
and, the group being a restriction of
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{\chi})_{(O)}
, the order of
\Delta_{Z}
does only have prime factors
greater than
\alpha_{h}
.
Furthermore, the subgroup
\Delta_{Z}
does not have fixed points since all fixed points of
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{\chi})_{(O)}

are in
F
, which is disjoint from
Z
.
By
37
,
there is a fixed-point-free permutation
\delta_{Z}\in\Delta_{Z}
.
In the following, we construct a tree in
G[Z\cup N(Z)]
that contains at most
one vertex from each
\Delta_{Z}
-orbit and whose set of leaves is
exactly the set
N(Z)
.
This can be done greedily: initially, we pick an arbitrary vertex
v\in N(Z)

and add this vertex to the tree
T
, i.e., define
V(T)\coloneqq\{v\}
.
While there is a vertex
w\in N(Z)

that is not yet contained in
V(T)
, we extend the tree
T

by adding a shortest path in
G[Z\cup\{w\}]
from
V(T)
to
w
.
Note that if
v,v^{\prime}\in O
are in the same
\Delta_{Z}
-orbit
O
,
then
v
and

have the same distance to

.
For this reason, the set

does not contain two vertices in the same

-orbit
throughout the construction.

Having defined
T
, we apply the fixed-point-free permutation
\delta_{Z}

to

and define

Since
\delta_{Z}
has no fixed points on

has only prime factors greater than

,
all
\delta_{Z}

. Thus, the trees

-minor

∎

Let
G_{Z,\chi}
be the vertex-colored graph based on

-orbit

Claim 2
.

Each automorphism

Proof of Claim 2.

Suppose

.

.
Let

Since

By

7
Conclusion

We characterized the automorphism groups of
graphs of bounded Hadwiger number. The characterization lends itself
to proving various properties as witnessed by the resolution of Babai’s three conjectures. A central part of the characterization
analyzes edge-transitive graphs, and this is done via limit
constructions.

However, this approach does not lead to explicit bounds and it remains
as interesting future work to analyze how large the graphs have to be
for the structural requirements to kick in. For example, it might be
interesting to determine reasonable bounds for the function
f(h)
in
the classification theorems. In particular, it remains open what
quantitative results can further be concluded for vertex- or
edge-transitive graphs.

In our proofs we focused on the possible groups that can arise as
automorphism groups from bounded Hadwiger number graphs. However, our
proofs actually show that the structure of the graphs is also very
restricted when the automorphism groups are sufficiently rich. It is
an interesting question whether we can make further use of the
structure that must necessarily emerge in the graphs.

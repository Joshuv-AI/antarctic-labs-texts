An ∞ \infty -Categorical Localisation Functor for Diagrams of Simplicial Sets


Abstract
Associated to each small category 𝒞 \mathscr{C} , there is a category of 𝒞 \mathscr{C} -shaped diagrams of simplicial sets and an ∞ \infty -category of N ​ 𝒞 N\mathscr{C} -shaped homotopy coherent diagrams of spaces. We present a functor which exhibits the latter as the ∞ \infty -categorical localisation of the former at the objectwise weak homotopy equivalences. This builds on a Quillen equivalence between the projective and covariant model structures associated to 𝒞 \mathscr{C} due to Heuts-Moerdijk [ 11 ] , as well as Cisinski’s theory of ∞ \infty -categorical localisations [ 2 ] . We use the localisation functor to give simplified proofs that the left (resp. right) homotopy Kan extension of diagrams of simplicial sets presents the ∞ \infty -categorical left (resp. right) Kan extension of coherent diagrams of spaces.



Severin Bunk

Introduction

The use of homotopy coherent mathematics, and in particular of
\infty
-categories, is becoming increasingly common.
Where available, an often powerful approach is to combine
\infty
-categorical theory with stricter classical constructions, such as in the setting of simplicial or model categories.
Presenting
\infty
-categorical constructions by stricter models can have the upshot of combining the larger scope of coherent mathematics with the easier and more explicit computability of classical constructions.
To exploit this, it is necessary that one can pass easily between both frameworks.
The main goal of this paper is to provide a short and explicit transition from strict diagrams of simplicial sets, indexed by a small category
\mathscr{C}
, to coherent diagrams of spaces, indexed by the nerve
N\mathscr{C}
.

More concretely, let
{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}}
denote the category of simplicial sets, and let
{\mathscr{K}\hskip-0.56917pt\mathrm{an}}\subset{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}}
denote the full subcategory on the Kan complexes.
We combine the Quillen equivalences, treated in
[
11
]
, between the projective and covariant model structures associated to a small category
\mathscr{C}
with the model for the
\infty
-category
\mathscr{S}
of spaces and the
\infty
-categorical localisation theory from
[
2
]
to write down a functor

\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}\colon N{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{C},{\mathscr{K}\hskip-0.56917pt\mathrm{an}})\longrightarrow{\mathscr{F}\hskip-0.85355pt\mathrm{un}}(N\mathscr{C},\mathscr{S})

(1.1)

from the nerve of the category of
\mathscr{C}
-shaped diagrams of Kan complexes to the
\infty
-category of
N\mathscr{C}
-shaped diagrams of spaces.
We then prove:

Theorem 1.2

The functor
\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}
exhibits
{\mathscr{F}\hskip-0.85355pt\mathrm{un}}(N\mathscr{C},\mathscr{S})
as the
\infty
-categorical localisation of
{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{C},{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}})
at the objectwise weak homotopy equivalences.
Furthermore, the functor
\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}
allows us to compute mapping spaces, internal homs and Kan extensions in
{\mathscr{F}\hskip-0.85355pt\mathrm{un}}(N\mathscr{C},\mathscr{S})
via the classical derived constructions in the projective model structure on
{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{C},{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}})
.

The mere fact that
{\mathscr{F}\hskip-0.85355pt\mathrm{un}}(N\mathscr{C},\mathscr{S})
is the
\infty
-categorical localisation of
{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{C},{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}})
at the objectwise weak equivalences is known:
one way to see this is through a combination of Lurie’s
[
14
, Prop. 4.2.4.4]
, Hinich’s
[
9
, Prop. 1.2.1]
and classical results by Dwyer and Kan
[
6
,
4
,
5
]
.
However, this chain of arguments is rather indirect.
It also does not lend itself easily to being used in calculations because it passes through various models for
\infty
-categories (quasicategories, fibrant simplicial categories and marked simplicial sets).
More recently, Cisinski provided an alternative proof of the fact
[
2
, Thm. 7.8.9, Cor. 7.9.9]
—entirely in the world of quasicategories—in the course of developing his theory of
\infty
-categorical localisations.
While this proof is conceptually clearer and includes the provision of a localisation functor (combining
[
2
, Par. 7.3.14, 7.8.5, 7.8.7]
), the latter is still hard to compute explicitly (for instance, it still involves an
\infty
-categorical left Kan extension).

The contribution of this paper lies in providing an explicit and computable localisation functor as well as a much simplified proof of the fact that
{\mathscr{F}\hskip-0.85355pt\mathrm{un}}(N\mathscr{C},\mathscr{S})
is the
\infty
-categorical localisation of
{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{C},{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}})
.
One way of approaching the localisation is through Lurie’s relative nerve functor
[
14
, Def. 3.2.5.2]
, which is part of a Quillen equivalence between the projective model structure on
{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{C},{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}})
and the covariant model structure on
{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}}_{/N\mathscr{C}}
; this was shown in
[
14
, Prop. 3.2.5.18]
and a simplified proof was given in
[
11
]
.
One could then invoke abstract arguments or further constructions which show that either of the above model categories present the
\infty
-category
{\mathscr{F}\hskip-0.85355pt\mathrm{un}}(N\mathscr{C},\mathscr{S})
(this is proved, for instance, in the aforementioned
[
14
, Prop. 4.2.4.4]
and
[
2
, Thm. 7.8.9, Cor. 7.9.9]
).

Here, we circumvent this second step:
instead, we enhance the relative nerve functor to write down the functor
\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}
, which takes its values directly in the
\infty
-category
{\mathscr{F}\hskip-0.85355pt\mathrm{un}}(N\mathscr{C},\mathscr{S})
, and prove that this already exhibits the
\infty
-categorical localisation (like
[
11
]
, we avoid the homotopy coherent nerve
[
14
,
16
]
in doing so).
At the same time, we obtain a framework which unifies three perspectives on
\mathscr{S}
-valued functors:
they can be presented as strict
{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}}
-valued diagrams, or as left fibrations over

{\mathscr{F}\hskip-0.85355pt\mathrm{un}}(N\mathscr{C},\mathscr{S})

Furthermore, we make manifest the full homotopical meaning of the rectification of simplicial diagrams
F\colon\mathscr{C}\to{\mathscr{K}\hskip-0.56917pt\mathrm{an}}
from
[
11
]
:
it is really the left fibration presented by the coherent diagram of spaces
\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}F\colon N\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{S}
described by

.

In the case where
\mathscr{C}=*
is the final category, we obtain an
\infty
-categorical localisation functor
\gamma\colon N{\mathscr{K}\hskip-0.56917pt\mathrm{an}}\to\mathscr{S}

We show an explicit instance of the fact that localisation commutes with taking functor categories; that is, we show that there is a commutative diagram of

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}

(1.3)

Finally, we remark that shortly before the completion of the present paper, the preprint
[
15
]
appeared on the ArXiv.
There, the Quillen equivalences from
[
11

are lifted from the projective and covariant model structures based on the Kan-Quillen model structure to those based on the Joyal model structure on
{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}}

Employing Sharma’s results it should be possible to also prove versions of the results in the present paper for diagrams of
\infty

4

In this section we prove our main theorem:
the functor

exhibits

at the objectwise weak homotopy equivalences.
To achieve this, we first show that

Proposition 4.1

is a morphism in

Proof.

, the restriction

of

to

is an equivalence in
\mathscr{S}

[
2
, Cor. 3.5.12]
.

Suppose first that

is a weak equivalence.
In particular, for each

, the morphism

in the notation of Proposition
2.51
.
By Corollary

the morphism

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta^{1}\times N\mathscr{C}}

(4.3)

That is, the restriction

, this is even a Kan fibration.
Thus, the functor

factors through the maximal subgroupoid

, by
[
2
, Prop. 5.2.13]
.

Now suppose that

is such that
\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}(X_{f})
is an equivalence in
{\operatorname{Fun}}(N\mathscr{C},\mathscr{S})
.
By Corollary
3.43
, the above pullback diagram and
[
2
, Prop. 5.2.13]
, it follows that

(4.4)

is a Kan fibration, for each

.
We need to show that

is local in

, for each

.
That will follow if

is isomorphic in

to a diagram

which sends the non-identity morphism in

to a weak equivalence.
Theorem
2.49
shows that

, and Theorem

implies that there is an isomorphism in

∎

to consist of the honest equivalences (i.e. the objectwise weakly invertible morphisms) in
{\mathscr{F}\hskip-0.85355pt\mathrm{un}}(N\mathscr{C},\mathscr{S})
.
This satisfies the axioms in Definitions
A.1
and
A.3
because
\mathscr{S}
has finite limits.

\triangleleft

The following is a well-known result, but we state it here nevertheless; the construction of

allows us to see this result very explicitly.

Lemma 4.9

Let
\mathscr{I}
be a
{\mathrm{U}}
-small category, and let
X\colon\mathscr{I}\to{\mathscr{K}\hskip-0.56917pt\mathrm{an}}
be a functor.
Then, there is a canonical equivalence of spaces

\gamma(\operatorname{holim}_{\mathscr{I}}X)\simeq\operatorname{lim}^{\mathscr{S}}_{\mathscr{I}}(\gamma_{\mathscr{I}}X)\,.

(4.10)

Here,
\gamma
is as in Theorem
3.19
and
\gamma_{\mathscr{I}}
is as in Theorem
3.31
.

Proof.

The functor
\gamma_{\mathscr{I}}X
classifies the left fibration
r_{\mathscr{I}}^{*}X\to N\mathscr{I}
(by Corollary
3.43
).
The
\infty
-categorical limit
\operatorname{lim}^{\mathscr{S}}_{\mathscr{I}}(\gamma_{\mathscr{I}}X)
is computed by the space of sections of this map by
[
2
, Cor. 5.4.7]
under the equivalence
\mathrm{h}\mathscr{S}\simeq{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}})
(see, for instance,
[
2
, Cor. 5.3.21, Thm. 5.4.5]
for
A=*
; note that this does not rely on the statement that
\mathscr{S}
is the
\infty
-categorical localisation of
{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}}
).
One checks that the identity map
1_{N\mathscr{I}}
coincides with
r_{\mathscr{I}}^{*}(*)
as objects of
{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}}_{/N\mathscr{I}}
; here,
r_{\mathscr{I}}^{*}(*)
is the functor
r_{\mathscr{I}}^{*}\colon{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{I},{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}})\longrightarrow{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}}_{/N\mathscr{I}}
applied to the constant diagram with value
\Delta^{0}
.
Furthermore, by Theorem
2.46
and since each object in
{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}}_{/N\mathscr{I}}
is cofibrant, the (non-derived) counit
r_{\mathscr{I}!}r_{\mathscr{I}}^{*}(*)\to*
is a cofibrant replacement of
*
in the projective model structure on
{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{I},{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}})
.
Consequently, we have the following canonical isomorphisms in
{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}})
:

\displaystyle\underline{{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}}_{/N\mathscr{I}}}(N\mathscr{I},r_{\mathscr{I}}^{*}X)

\displaystyle\cong\underline{{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}}_{/N\mathscr{I}}}(r_{\mathscr{I}}^{*}(*),X)

(4.11)

\displaystyle\cong\underline{{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{I},{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}})}(r_{\mathscr{I}!}r_{\mathscr{I}}^{*}(*),X)

(4.12)

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{holim}_{\mathscr{I}}(X)\,.

(4.13)

That completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 4.14

The functor
\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}\colon N{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{C},{\mathscr{K}\hskip-0.56917pt\mathrm{an}})\longrightarrow{\mathscr{F}\hskip-0.85355pt\mathrm{un}}(N\mathscr{C},\mathscr{S})
is left exact as a functor between
\infty
-categories with weak equivalences and fibrations (see Definition
A.10
).

Proof.

We need to check the axioms of Definition
A.10
.
Axiom (1) is satisfied because
\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}(*)
is the functor
N\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{S}
classifies the left fibration
1_{N\mathscr{C}}\colon N\mathscr{C}\to N\mathscr{C}
, which corresponds to the final object in
{\operatorname{Fun}}(N\mathscr{C},\mathscr{S})
.
(Alternatively, the final object of
{\mathscr{F}\hskip-0.85355pt\mathrm{un}}(N\mathscr{C},\mathscr{S})
is the essentially unique functor whose value on each
c\in\mathscr{C}
is a final object
e\in\mathscr{S}
, and we have
(\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}(*))(c)\simeq\gamma_{[0]}(*(c))\simeq e
.)
Axiom (2) holds true because each morphism in
\mathscr{S}
is a fibration (see Remark
4.8
) and the functor
\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}
preserves weak equivalences (by Proposition
4.1
).
For axiom (3), consider morphisms
v\colon Y^{\prime}\to Y
and
f\colon X\to Y
in
{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{C},{\mathscr{K}\hskip-0.56917pt\mathrm{an}})
, where
f
is a projective fibration.
Let
\mathrm{CSp}=\{0\to 1\leftarrow 2\}
denote the cospan category, and let
D\colon\mathrm{CSp}\times\mathscr{C}\to{\mathscr{K}\hskip-0.56917pt\mathrm{an}}
denote the diagram corresponding to the cospan
Y^{\prime}\to Y\leftarrow X
in
{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{C},{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}})
.
We have to show that the square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}\big(Y^{\prime}\times_{Y}X\big)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}(Y^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}(Y)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}(f)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}(v)}

(4.15)

is cartesian in
{\mathscr{F}\hskip-0.85355pt\mathrm{un}}(N\mathscr{C},\mathscr{S})
.
The cospan obtained by omitting the top-left corner of this diagram is equivalently described by a digram
\tilde{D}\colon\Lambda^{2}_{2}\times N\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{S}
(since
N\mathrm{CSp}=\Lambda^{2}_{2}
).
Explicitly, this diagram is given as
\tilde{D}=\gamma_{\mathrm{CSp}\times\mathscr{C}}D
.

There is a canonical morphism
\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}(Y^{\prime}\times_{Y}X)\to\operatorname{lim}_{\Lambda^{2}_{2}}D
, and it suffices to show that this morphism is an equivalence in
{\operatorname{Fun}}(N\mathscr{C},\mathscr{S})
.
However, since limits in
{\mathscr{F}\hskip-0.85355pt\mathrm{un}}(N\mathscr{C},\mathscr{S})
are computed objectwise, we may restrict ourselves to the case where
\mathscr{C}=*
is the final category.
That is, we may replace the
\infty
-category
{\mathscr{F}\hskip-0.85355pt\mathrm{un}}(N\mathscr{C},\mathscr{S})
by
\mathscr{S}
.
In that case, we need to show that the canonical morphism

\gamma(Y^{\prime}\times_{Y}X)\longrightarrow\operatorname{lim}^{\mathscr{S}}_{\Lambda^{2}_{2}}(\gamma_{\mathrm{CSp}}D)

(4.16)

is an equivalence (with
\gamma
as in Theorem
3.19
).
We have shown in Lemma
4.9
that there is a canonical equivalence

\gamma\big(\operatorname{holim}_{\mathrm{CSp}}D\big)\simeq\operatorname{lim}^{\mathscr{S}}_{\Lambda^{2}_{2}}(\gamma_{\mathrm{CSp}}D)\,.

(4.17)

However, by our assumptions on the cospan
Y^{\prime}\to Y\leftarrow X
, the pullback
Y^{\prime}\times_{Y}X
already computes the homotopy limit of
D
, showing that
Y^{\prime}\times_{Y}X\to\operatorname{lim}^{\mathscr{S}}(\gamma_{\mathrm{CSp}}D)
is indeed an equivalence.
∎

Theorem 4.18

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a
{\mathrm{U}}
-small category.
The functor

as the
\infty
-categorical localisation of

Proof.

Using the notation of Remarks
4.6
and
4.8
, we have a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{C},{\mathscr{K}\hskip-0.56917pt\mathrm{an}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathscr{F}\hskip-0.85355pt\mathrm{un}}(N\mathscr{C},\mathscr{S})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{\mathsf{W}}N{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{C},{\mathscr{K}\hskip-0.56917pt\mathrm{an}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{\mathsf{W}^{\prime}}{\mathscr{F}\hskip-0.85355pt\mathrm{un}}(N\mathscr{C},\mathscr{S})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ell_{\mathsf{W}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{R}\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}}

The vertical functors are localisation functors; the equality on the right-hand side holds true because we have chosen

to consist precisely of the honest equivalences in

Our goal is to show that the right derived functor

4.14

By
[
2
, Rmk. 7.1.6]
there is a canonical equivalence of categories

\mathrm{h}\big(L_{\mathsf{W}}N{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{C},{\mathscr{K}\hskip-0.56917pt\mathrm{an}})\big)\simeq{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{C},{\mathscr{K}\hskip-0.56917pt\mathrm{an}})[\mathsf{W}^{-1}]\,,

where on the right-hand side we have the ordinary 1-categorical localisation.

{\operatorname{Ho}}({\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{C},{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}}))

We claim that there is a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{C},{\mathscr{K}\hskip-0.56917pt\mathrm{an}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{h}{\mathscr{F}\hskip-0.85355pt\mathrm{un}}(N\mathscr{C},\mathscr{S})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Ho}}\big({\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{C},{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}})\big)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}}_{/N\mathscr{C}})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{h}\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{RepFib}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{R}r_{\mathscr{C}}^{*}}

of (ordinary) categories and functors.
The functor
\operatorname{RepFib}
sends an
\infty
-functor

to the left fibration it classifies; we refer to
[
2
, Par. 5.3.14]
and the proof of
[
2
, Thm. 5.4.5]

The functor
\mathbb{R}r_{\mathscr{C}}^{*}
is an equivalence by Theorem
2.46
, as is the functor
\operatorname{RepFib}

[
2
, Thm. 5.4.5]
(note that Cisinski writes

for
{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}}_{/N\mathscr{C}})
).
Granted the claim that diagram (
4.23
) commutes, it follows that the functor
\mathrm{h}\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}
exhibits
\mathrm{h}{\mathscr{F}\hskip-0.85355pt\mathrm{un}}(N\mathscr{C},\mathscr{S})
as the localisation of
{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{C},{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}})

.
We thus infer that

is an equivalence as well.

It remains to show that diagram (
4.23
) commutes.
The commutativity on objects follows directly from Corollary
3.43
; we can write

\operatorname{RepFib}\circ\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}(X)=(r_{\mathscr{C}}^{*}X\to N\mathscr{C})\,.

(4.25)

To see the commutativity on morphisms, let

be a morphism in
{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{C},{\mathscr{K}\hskip-0.56917pt\mathrm{an}})
.
Equivalently, this is encoded in a functor

(4.26)

The morphism in
{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}}_{/N\mathscr{C}})
obtained from this by applying the functor
\operatorname{RepFib}
is described as follows (see
[
2
, Par. 5.3.14]
):
first, note that because both objects are fibrant and cofibrant the set of morphisms
{\operatorname{Ho}}({\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}}_{/N\mathscr{C}})(r_{\mathscr{C}}^{*}X_{0},r_{\mathscr{C}}^{*}X_{1})
coincides with the set
{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}}_{/N\mathscr{C}}(r_{\mathscr{C}}^{*}X_{0},r_{\mathscr{C}}^{*}X_{1})/{\sim_{l}}
of left homotopy equivalence classes of morphisms in

(in the sense of, for instance,
[
12
, Def. 1.2.4]
).
Consider the following diagram in
{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}}
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta^{\{0\}}\times r_{\mathscr{C}}^{*}X_{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces r_{[1]\times\mathscr{C}}^{*}X_{f}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta^{1}\times r_{\mathscr{C}}^{*}X_{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta^{1}\times N\mathscr{C}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}

(4.27)

It admits a lift
\varphi
because the left-hand vertical morphism is a cofinal inclusion and hence left anodyne
[
2
, Cor. 4.1.9]
, and the right-hand vertical morphism is a left fibration.
Taking the fibre of

over

(and using Lemma

), we obtain a morphism

.

is the image of

under

(where

[
2
, Par. 5.3.14]
and the proof of
[
2
, Thm.5.4.5]
.

by
[
2
, Prop. 5.3.15]
.

We have to show that there exists a choice for
\varphi
such that
\varphi_{1}\sim_{l}r_{\mathscr{C}}^{*}(f)
.
This is greatly simplified by the description of

from Lemma
2.18
:
each

-simplex of

arises from a unique pair

of functors

(4.28)

by applying the Yoneda embedding of

to

and the nerve functor to

(recall that we denote the map

arising from

u
).
We may describe each
n
-simplex in

over an

-simplex

, where

) and surrounding text).

\displaystyle x_{ij}\in X_{0}(\alpha_{j})_{i}\,,

\displaystyle u(j)=0\,,

(4.30)

\displaystyle y_{ij}\in X_{1}(\alpha_{j})_{i}\,,

-categorical localisation of
{\mathscr{K}\hskip-0.56917pt\mathrm{an}}
at the weak equivalences in the Kan-Quillen model structure.
In light of this, we can now interpret diagram (
4.39
) as an explicit manifestation of
[
2
, Prop. 7.9.2]
, i.e. the fact that localisation commutes with forming functor
\infty
-categories.

5
Computational properties of the localisation functor

In this section we exhibit some of the pleasant computational properties of the localisation functor
\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}
:
we show that, for
F,G\in{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{C},{\mathscr{K}\hskip-0.56917pt\mathrm{an}})
, the
\infty
-categorical mapping space between
\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}F
and
\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}G
in
{\mathscr{F}\hskip-0.85355pt\mathrm{un}}(N\mathscr{C},\mathscr{S})
can be computed as the model-categorical mapping space.
The same holds true for the internal hom (or exponential) in
{\mathscr{F}\hskip-0.85355pt\mathrm{un}}(N\mathscr{C},\mathscr{S})
.
Moreover, we show that the left and right (
\infty
-categorical) Kan extensions of
\mathscr{S}
-valued functors can be computed by the left and right homotopy Kan extensions of
{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}}
-valued functors.
These insights themselves are not new (see, for instance,
[
14
, Thm. 4.2.4.1]
), but here we are able to derive them in a particularly straightforward fashion, building on
[
2
, Cor. 5.4.7]
, the results of this paper so far and manipulations of model-categorical mapping spaces.
Throughout this section, let
\mathscr{C}
be a
{\mathrm{U}}
-small category.
Recall that we denote the simplicial hom sets in a simplicially enriched category
{\mathscr{M}}
by
\underline{{\mathscr{M}}}(-,-)
.

Proposition 5.1

Let
F,G\colon\mathscr{C}\to{\mathscr{K}\hskip-0.56917pt\mathrm{an}}
.
There exist canonical equivalences in
\mathscr{S}
,

{\mathscr{F}\hskip-0.85355pt\mathrm{un}}(N\mathscr{C},\mathscr{S})(\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}F,\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}G)\simeq\underline{{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}}_{/N\mathscr{C}}}(r_{\mathscr{C}}^{*}F,r_{\mathscr{C}}^{*}G)\simeq\underline{{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{C},{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}})}(r_{\mathscr{C}!}r_{\mathscr{C}}^{*}F,G)\,,

(5.2)

and a further equivalence

\underline{{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{C},{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}})}(r_{\mathscr{C}!}r_{\mathscr{C}}^{*}F,G)\simeq\underline{{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{C},{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}})}(QF,G)

(5.3)

where
QF
is any functorial cofibrant replacement of
F
in the projective model structure on
{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{C},{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}})
.
These equivalences are natural in
F
and
G
.

This can be derived from general arguments about localisations along the lines of
[
2
, Sec. 7.10]
, but we give a more direct proof:

Proof.

The first equivalence follows directly from Corollary
3.43
and
[
2
, Cor. 5.4.7]
.
The second equivalence can be obtained, for instance, by using that
r_{\mathscr{C}!}\dashv r_{\mathscr{C}}^{*}
is a simplicial Quillen equivalence:
since
F
is fibrant and
r_{\mathscr{C}}^{*}F
is cofibrant, the derived counit of this adjunction provides an equivalence
r_{\mathscr{C}!}r_{\mathscr{C}}^{*}F\to F
.
It thus exhibits
r_{\mathscr{C}!}r_{\mathscr{C}}^{*}F
as a cofibrant replacement of
F
in the projective model structure on
{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{C},{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}})
.
For any other functorial cofibrant replacement
QF
, there is a natural zig-zag of weak equivalences

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces r_{\mathscr{C}!}r_{\mathscr{C}}^{*}F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q(r_{\mathscr{C}!}r_{\mathscr{C}}^{*}F)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces QF}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

(5.4)

between cofibrant objects, which the functor
\underline{{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{C},{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}})}(-,G)

sends to an equivalence in
\mathscr{S}
.
∎

A particular choice of cofibrant replacement functor for the projective model structure on
{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{C},{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}})
is given by Dugger’s cofibrant replacement functor
[
7
, Sec. 2.6]
; we denote this functor by
Q^{p}
(note that since here we use simplicial pre
co
sheaves on
\mathscr{C}
instead of simplicial presheaves, we need to introduce an additional

in Dugger’s conventions).
Explicitly, we have

(Q^{p}F)_{n}(c)=\coprod_{c_{0}\to\cdots\to c_{n}\to c}F_{n}(c_{0})\,,

(5.5)

where the coproduct is taken over chains of morphisms in
\mathscr{C}
.
The functor
Q^{p}
comes with a natural weak equivalence to the identity functor on
{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{C},{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}})
, which we denote by

.

Corollary 5.6

For each
F,G\in{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{C},{\mathscr{K}\hskip-0.56917pt\mathrm{an}})
, there is an equivalence, natural in
F
and
G
,

(\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}G)^{\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}F}\simeq\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}(G^{Q^{p}F})\,.

(5.7)

Proof.

By the Yoneda Lemma, it suffices to show that there is a natural equivalence

{\mathscr{F}\hskip-0.85355pt\mathrm{un}}(N\mathscr{C},\mathscr{S})\big(-,(\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}G)^{\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}F}\big)\simeq{\mathscr{F}\hskip-0.85355pt\mathrm{un}}(N\mathscr{C},\mathscr{S})\big(-,\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}(G^{Q^{p}F})\big)

(5.8)

of functors
{\mathscr{F}\hskip-0.85355pt\mathrm{un}}(N\mathscr{C},\mathscr{S})^{\mathrm{op}}\longrightarrow\widehat{\mathscr{S}}
(where
\widehat{\mathscr{S}}
is the
\infty
-category of
{\mathrm{V}}
-small spaces).
By Theorem
4.18

\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}
is a localisation functor.
It thus induces an equivalence
[
2
, Def. 7.1.2]

\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}^{*}\colon{\mathscr{F}\hskip-0.85355pt\mathrm{un}}\big({\mathscr{F}\hskip-0.85355pt\mathrm{un}}(N\mathscr{C},\mathscr{S})^{\mathrm{op}},\widehat{\mathscr{S}}\big)\longrightarrow{\mathscr{F}\hskip-0.85355pt\mathrm{un}}_{\mathsf{W}}\big(N{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{C},{\mathscr{K}\hskip-0.56917pt\mathrm{an}})^{\mathrm{op}},\widehat{\mathscr{S}}\big)\,,

(5.9)

where on the right-hand side

{\mathscr{F}\hskip-0.85355pt\mathrm{un}}_{\mathsf{W}}\big(N{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{C},{\mathscr{K}\hskip-0.56917pt\mathrm{an}})^{\mathrm{op}},\widehat{\mathscr{S}}\big)\subset{\mathscr{F}\hskip-0.85355pt\mathrm{un}}\big(N{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{C},{\mathscr{K}\hskip-0.56917pt\mathrm{an}})^{\mathrm{op}},\widehat{\mathscr{S}}\big)

(5.10)

denotes the full
\infty
-subcategory on those functors which send weak equivalences to equivalences.
Therefore, it suffices to show that there is an equivalence of mapping spaces

{\mathscr{F}\hskip-0.85355pt\mathrm{un}}(N\mathscr{C},\mathscr{S})\big(\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}A,(\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}G)^{\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}F}\big)\simeq{\mathscr{F}\hskip-0.85355pt\mathrm{un}}(N\mathscr{C},\mathscr{S})\big(\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}A,\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}(G^{Q^{p}F})\big)\,,

(5.11)

natural in
A\in{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{C},{\mathscr{K}\hskip-0.56917pt\mathrm{an}})
.

Since

is left exact (Proposition
4.14
) and both
A
and
F
are fibrant objects in
N{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{C},{\mathscr{K}\hskip-0.56917pt\mathrm{an}})
(seen as an
\infty
-category of fibrant objects—see Remark

), we have an equivalence

{\mathscr{F}\hskip-0.85355pt\mathrm{un}}(N\mathscr{C},\mathscr{S})(\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}A,(\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}G)^{\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}F})\simeq{\mathscr{F}\hskip-0.85355pt\mathrm{un}}(N\mathscr{C},\mathscr{S})(\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}(A\times F),\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}G)\,.

(5.12)

By Proposition
5.1
there is a further equivalence

{\mathscr{F}\hskip-0.85355pt\mathrm{un}}(N\mathscr{C},\mathscr{S})(\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}(A\times F),\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}G)\simeq\underline{{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{C},{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}})}\big(Q^{p}(A\times F),G\big)\,.

(5.13)

For this choice of cofibrant replacement functor there exists a canonical commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q^{p}(A\times F)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q^{p}A\times Q^{p}F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A\times F}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q^{p}_{A\times F}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q^{p}_{A}\times q^{p}_{F}}

(5.14)

whose horizontal arrow stems from the canonical functors of slice categories

\mathscr{C}_{/A_{n}\times F_{n}}\longrightarrow\mathscr{C}_{/A_{n}}\times\mathscr{C}_{/F_{n}}

(5.15)

for each

.
The right-hand arrow is a weak equivalence since

and

are so and the injective model structure on

is cartesian monoidal

(it has the same weak equivalences as the projective model structure).

Thus, we have natural equivalences

\displaystyle{\mathscr{F}\hskip-0.85355pt\mathrm{un}}(N\mathscr{C},\mathscr{S})(\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}A,(\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}G)^{\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}F})

\displaystyle\simeq\underline{{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{C},{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}})}\big(Q^{p}(A\times F),G\big)

(5.16)

\displaystyle\simeq\underline{{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{C},{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}})}(Q^{p}A,G^{Q^{p}F})

where the last step is again Proposition

.

Proposition 5.19

Let
\pi\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{D}
be a functor, and let

The

r_{\mathscr{D}}^{*}\operatorname{hoRan}_{\pi}(X)\longrightarrow N\mathscr{D}
, where

along
\pi
.
Equivalently, there is a canonical equivalence

(N\pi)_{*}\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}(X)\simeq\gamma_{\mathscr{D}}(\operatorname{hoRan}_{\pi}X)\,.

Proof.

We show that
\gamma_{\mathscr{D}}(\operatorname{hoRan}_{\pi}X)
represents the functor

{\mathscr{F}\hskip-0.85355pt\mathrm{un}}(N\mathscr{C},\mathscr{S})(N\pi^{*}(-),\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}X)\colon{\mathscr{F}\hskip-0.85355pt\mathrm{un}}(N\mathscr{D},\mathscr{S})\longrightarrow\widehat{\mathscr{S}}\,.

(5.21)

To that end, let
F\colon N\mathscr{D}\to\mathscr{S}
be a functor.
By the same logic as in the proof of Corollary
5.6
, we may precompose the left-hand argument of
{\mathscr{F}\hskip-0.85355pt\mathrm{un}}(N\mathscr{C},\mathscr{S})(-,-)
with the localisation functor
\gamma_{\mathscr{C}}
.
That is, we may assume that
F
is of the form
\gamma_{\mathscr{D}}Y
for some functor

.
Furthermore, we may assume that

\operatorname{hoRan}_{\pi}
and

(see Proposition
4.1
).
In particular, we then have that
\operatorname{hoRan}_{\pi}X\simeq\operatorname{Ran}_{\pi}X

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{*}:{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{D},{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{C},{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}}):\operatorname{Ran}_{\pi}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\perp}

(5.22)

is a Quillen adjunction with respect to the injective model structures.

There are canonical natural equivalences of spaces

\displaystyle{\mathscr{F}\hskip-0.85355pt\mathrm{un}}(N\mathscr{D},\mathscr{S})\big(\gamma_{\mathscr{D}}Y,\gamma_{\mathscr{D}}(\operatorname{hoRan}_{\pi}X)\big)

\displaystyle\overset{(1)}{\simeq}\underline{{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}}_{/N\mathscr{D}}}\big(r_{\mathscr{D}}^{*}Y,r_{\mathscr{D}}^{*}(\operatorname{Ran}_{\pi}X)\big)

\displaystyle\overset{(2)}{\cong}\underline{{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{D},{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}})}(r_{\mathscr{D}!}r_{\mathscr{D}}^{*}Y,\operatorname{Ran}_{\pi}X)

(5.24)

\displaystyle\overset{(3)}{\simeq}\underline{{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{D},{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}})}(Y,\operatorname{Ran}_{\pi}X)

(5.25)

(5.26)

\displaystyle\overset{(5)}{\simeq}\underline{{\operatorname{Fun}}(\mathscr{C},{\mathscr{S}\mathrm{et}_{\hskip-0.85355pt{\mathbbe{\Delta}}}})}(r_{\mathscr{C}!}r_{\mathscr{C}}^{*}\pi^{*}Y,X)

(5.27)

(5.28)

(5.29)

Equivalence (1) stems from Proposition
5.1
.
For step (2), we have used that the adjunction

is simplicial

Step (3) uses that

is projectively fibrant, that

is a Quillen equivalence between the projective and the covariant model structure

, and the final step (8) is an application of Corollary

(5.32)

Note that
\gamma_{\mathscr{D}}(R^{p}\circ{\operatorname{hoLan}}_{\pi}X)

.

and Proposition

, since

Appendix A
Background on
\infty
-categorical localisation

For the reader’s convenience, we recall some definitions and results on localisation of
\infty
-categories from
[
2
, Ch. 7]

Definition A.1

[
2
, Def. 7.4.6]
,
[
3
]

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be an
\infty
-category with a fixed final object
e
.
A
class of fibrations in
{\mathscr{A}}
is a subobject
{\mathsf{F}}\subset{\mathscr{A}}
with the following properties:

(1)

{\mathsf{F}}
contains all identity morphisms (i.e. all degenerate edges).

(2)

{\mathsf{F}}
is closed under composition.

(3)

For any morphisms
g\colon y^{\prime}\to y
and
f\colon x\to y
in
{\mathscr{A}}
such that
y^{\prime}\to e
,
y\to e
and
x\to e
are in
{\mathsf{F}}
, the pullback
y^{\prime}\times_{y}x
in
{\mathscr{A}}
exists, and, for any cartesian square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces x^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces x}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}

(A.2)

in
{\mathscr{A}}
, the morphism
f^{\prime}
is in
{\mathsf{F}}
whenever
f
is so.

Morphisms in
{\mathsf{F}}
are called
fibrations
.
Given an
\infty
-category with a class of fibrations
({\mathscr{A}},{\mathsf{F}})
, we call an object
y\in{\mathscr{A}}

fibrant
if the morphism
y\to e
is in
{\mathsf{F}}
.

In particular, since
{\mathsf{F}}
contains all identity morphisms, the final object
e\in{\mathscr{A}}
is necessarily fibrant.

Definition A.3

[
2
, Def. 7.4.12]

An
\infty

Theorem A.14

[
2
, Cor. 7.6.11]

Let
({\mathscr{A}},{\mathsf{F}}_{\mathscr{A}},\mathsf{W}_{\mathscr{A}})
and
(\mathscr{B},{\mathsf{F}}_{\mathscr{B}},\mathsf{W}_{\mathscr{B}})
be
\infty
-categories with fibrations and weak equivalences, and let

be a left exact functor.
The right derived functor
\mathbb{R}F\colon L_{\mathsf{W}_{\mathscr{A}}}{\mathscr{A}}\to L_{\mathsf{W}_{\mathscr{B}}}\mathscr{B}
is an equivalence if and only if it is so on the level of homotopy categories; that is,
\mathrm{h}\mathbb{R}F\colon\mathrm{h}L_{\mathsf{W}_{\mathscr{A}}}{\mathscr{A}}\to\mathrm{h}L_{\mathsf{W}_{\mathscr{B}}}\mathscr{B}
is an equivalence of

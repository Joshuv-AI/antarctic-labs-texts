Quantitative Estimates in Passive Scalar Transport: A Unified Approach in W 1 , p W^{1,p} via Christ-Journé Commutator Estimates Date : August 7, 2026. MSC : 35F05, 35Q35, 76B03, 76F25, 76R10 Keywords : Passive scalar transport, quantitative estimates, Sobolev vector field, harmonic estimates, mixing estimates, DiPerna-Lions commutator.
Lucas Huysmans, Ayman Rimah Said

Abstract
This paper establishes new applications of the Christ-Journé singular integral estimate to the transport equation for divergence-free vector fields in the Sobolev class W 1 , p W^{1,p} with p > 1 p>1 . Our main result is a stability estimate for the Cauchy problem, which quantifies the continuous dependence of solutions on initial data in the weak topology by giving bounds on the transfer of mass from high frequencies to low frequencies. Among other applications to stability and vanishing diffusion, this implies a mixing bound valid for all initial data in the DiPerna-Lions well-posedness class, with the mixing rate expressed in terms of a dimensionless ratio of the passive scalar and vector field. We also demonstrate that the passive scalar propagates a full "logarithm of a derivative" and finally discuss connections between the commutator estimates developed in this paper and uniform decay rates for the standard DiPerna-Lions commutator.

Introduction

In this paper, we give new applications of the Christ-Journé singular integral estimate in
[
21
]
to the transport equation, giving a new commutator estimate that controls the transport of specific frequencies of the passive scalar. We show that this provides a unified framework to tackle various well-studied problems related to well-posedness, mixing, stability, diffusion, and regularity of a passive scalar transported by a divergence-free vector field belonging to the Sobolev class
W^{1,p}
with
p>1
.

The main result of the paper, Theorem
2.4
, is a stability estimate for the Cauchy problem, which quantifies the continuous dependence of solutions on the initial data in a weak topology by bounding the transfer of mass from high frequencies to low frequencies. This estimate improves existing estimates on mixing and extends results in
[
23
,
24
]
on quantitative convergence rates for the vanishing diffusion limit or variation of the vector field.

Theorem
2.4
is closely related to the problem of how quickly a Sobolev vector field mixes a passive scalar. In particular, we give a bound on such mixing rates for all initial data in the DiPerna-Lions well-posedness class
[
13
]
in terms of a dimensionless ratio of the passive scalar and vector field. This mixing bound improves on similar results in
[
22
,
17
,
18
]
and is further related to an open conjecture by Bressan
[
6
,
11
]
.

Applying the Christ-Journé commutator estimate to study the differentiability of the passive scalar, we show that the passive scalar propagates a full ‘logarithm of a derivative’, extending results on similar ‘fractional logarithms’ in
[
18
,
8
]
. From this, we further conjecture the sharp regularity of the passive scalar to be like a logarithm to the power
p
.

Finally, we discuss connections between the commutator estimates developed in this paper and quantitative decay rates for the standard DiPerna-Lions commutator,
[
13
]
. Although no uniform decay occurs, we show that the harmonic analysis in Theorem
2.4
is equivalent to the statement that the DiPerna-Lions commutator decays
on average
at a uniform rate. We give two such estimates, one for pointwise decay and one for decay of the norm of the commutator. The latter requires a different argument quantifying weak compactness in
W^{1,p}
. In a follow-up paper
[
16
]
, we extend these ideas to
BV
vector fields, obtaining quantitative estimates when
p=1
.

In this paper, we assume throughout that
p>1
, and we remark that this assumption was also necessary for previous work, see for instance
[
11
,
22
,
18
,
15
]
, since these quantitative estimates also rely on harmonic techniques valid only when
p>1
. It is a significant open problem to extend any of these results to the case
p=1
(for which well-posedness also holds, see
[
13
,
3
]
), and this is related to a mixing conjecture by Bressan
[
6
]
.

To be more precise, let
\rho(x,t):\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
be a weak solution to the transport equation

\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}(x,t)+\nabla\cdot(u(x,t)\rho(x,t))=0,

along a divergence-free vector field
u(x,t):\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
u(x,t)\in L_{t}^{1}W_{x}^{1,p}
. Suppose
\rho(x,t)\in L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}
, and denote by
\rho_{0}(x)\in L^{q}
the initial datum, and also by
\rho_{T}(x)\in L^{q}
the weak trace of
\rho(x,t)
at time
t=T
. See, for instance; the lecture notes
[
2
]
. We will denote by
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}
and assume throughout that
1<p,q\leq\infty
,
1\leq r<\infty
.

The key idea of this paper, inspired by work on mixing in
[
18
,
15
]
, is that if
p,q>1
, the Christ-Journé singular integral estimate in
[
21
]
controls the transport of frequencies
(\rho*K)(x,t)
for any convolution kernel
K(x)
. Consider the error in the transport of
(\rho*K)(x,t)
:

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+u(x,t)\cdot\nabla\right)(\rho*K)(x,t)=R(x,t),

then we have the following bound on the commutator
R(x,t)
:

Lemma
2.1
.

For the commutator

R(x,t)=u(x,t)\cdot(\rho*\nabla K)(x,t)-\nabla\cdot((u\rho)*K)(x,t),

we have the bound

\left\|R(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}\leq C_{p,q}\left\|\rho(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}\left\|\nabla u(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}}\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\left\|\frac{\partial(x_{j}K)}{\partial x_{i}}\right\|_{CZ}\right),

(1)

where
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}
with
1\leq r<\infty
, and where
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{CZ}
refers to the Calderon-Zygmund norm, i.e. the minimum constant such that

\begin{gathered}\left|K(x)\right|\leq|x|^{-d}\left\|K\right\|_{CZ},\\
\left|\nabla K(x)\right|\leq|x|^{-d-1}\left\|K\right\|_{CZ},\\
|\hat{K}(\xi)|\leq\left\|K\right\|_{CZ}.\end{gathered}

The main focus of this paper is to show how, by making appropriate choices of the kernel
K(x)
, this harmonic approach provides a new, stronger, unified framework to address the following well-studied topics:

1.1
Well-posedness and stability of the Cauchy problem

Our main result is a quantitative estimate of the stability of the Cauchy problem. We show the continuous dependence of the solution on the initial datum in the weak topology, using frequency cutoffs to quantify the dependence of lower frequencies on higher frequencies.

We remark that continuous dependence on the initial datum in the strong topology is a standard result of the theory of renormalised solutions,
[
13
]
. In contrast, by constructing a kernel in (
1
) to control the transfer of mass between two specific length scales/frequencies, we extend this to continuous dependence in a weak topology. One could measure continuous dependence in negative Sobolev norms (such as
W^{-1,r}
). However, we prefer to give the estimate in terms of a frequency cutoff, from which one can deduce the corresponding bounds in any desired negative Sobolev space, as it allows us to prove more general results.

We say a mollifier
\varphi(x)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
(a Schwartz function with integral
\int\varphi(x)dx=1
) is a frequency-cutoff if its Fourier transform
\hat{\varphi}(\xi)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
satisfies the following:

\begin{gathered}\hat{\varphi}(\xi)=1\text{ for }|\xi|\leq 1,\\
\hat{\varphi}(\xi)=0\text{ for }|\xi|\geq 2.\end{gathered}

(2)

Recall also the notation for the rescaled mollifier
\varphi_{\delta}(x)=\delta^{-d}\varphi\left(\frac{x}{\delta}\right)
for any
\delta>0
.

Theorem
2.4
.

Consider a weak solution
\rho(x,t):\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
, with
\rho(x,t)\in L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}
, and initial datum
\rho_{0}(x)\in L_{x}^{q}
, to the forced transport equation

\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}(x,t)+\nabla\cdot(u(x,t)\rho(x,t))=f(x,t),

along a divergence-free vector field
u(x,t):\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
\nabla u(x,t)\in L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}
, for
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{r}
, with
1<p,q\leq\infty
,
1\leq r<\infty
, and distributional force
f(x,t)\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
a Schwartz distribution supported on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T]
.

Let
\varphi(x)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
be a standard mollifier satisfying the frequency-cutoff (
2
).

Then for any
\delta>0
and
\kappa>0
we have the following quantitative bound:

If

\delta_{0}\leq\frac{1}{4}\delta\exp\left(-\frac{C_{p,q}}{\kappa}\frac{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}}{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\right),

(3)

then

\left\|\rho*\varphi_{\delta}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{r}}\leq\kappa+A\left\|\rho_{0}*\varphi_{\delta_{0}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}+A\left\|f*\varphi_{\delta_{0}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{r}},

(4)

where the constant
C_{p,q}>0
depends only on the parameters
1<p,q\leq\infty
, the choice of mollifier
\varphi(x)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
, and the dimension
d\geq 1
, while
A>0
depends only on the choice of mollifier
\varphi(x)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
and the dimension
d\geq 1
.

This theorem quantifies the continuous dependence of the solution on specific frequencies of the initial datum. As an immediate corollary, we recover the well-posedness of the transport equation when
p>1
, initially due to DiPerna-Lions
[
13
]
.

More specifically, we bound the transfer of mass between two specific length scales/frequencies. Fixing a pair of length scales
0<\delta_{0}<\frac{1}{4}\delta
, this estimate says that at most
\kappa>0
mass can transfer from below the threshold
\delta_{0}
to above the threshold
\delta
, with
\kappa>0
determined by the expression (
3
). Alternatively, one can fix some suitably small
\kappa>0
, and then the estimate says that essentially no mass can transfer from length scales below
\delta_{0}
to length scales above
\delta
, with
\delta>0
now given by an exponential in the dimensionless quantity

\frac{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}}{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}.

By examples of self-similar mixing, see
[
1
]
, this rate is sharp in the regularity of the vector field
\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}
. The key features of this result, and also our main improvement over the mixing estimates
[
11
,
17
,
22
,
18
,
15
]
which we discuss more in Section
1.2
, is the dependence of the exponential rate on the ratio
\frac{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}}{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}}
. This ratio is conserved by the flow. Therefore, the mixing rate doesn’t depend on any rearrangement of the initial data, say by transport. Some dependence on the initial datum is necessary, however, by a scaling argument since
\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}
has units of length to the power
\frac{d}{p}
and so cannot bound the exponential mixing rate on its own. In contrast, the conserved ratio
\frac{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}}{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}}
has units of length to the power
-\frac{d}{p}
and so precisely ensures that the exponential rate is dimensionless. Indeed, in the case
p=\infty
this fraction cancels, and we recover the classical exponential rate in
\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{\infty}}
in the Cauchy-Lipschitz theory
[
2
]
.

Including a distributional force
f(x,t)
in Theorem
2.4
also allows us to bound the stability of the transport equation under modification of the equation. For example, for transport along a different vector field or with diffusion.

For transport along a different vector field
\bar{u}(x,t)\in L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}
, if
\left\|u-\bar{u}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\leq\delta\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}
, then

\left\|(\rho-\bar{\rho})*\varphi_{\delta}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{r}}\leq C_{p,q}\left\|\bar{\rho}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\left(\log\left(\frac{\delta\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}}{\left\|u-\bar{u}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}}\right)\right)^{-1},

see Proposition
3.4
and Corollary
3.5
for details. This improves a stability result in
[
23
]
on the logarithmic convergence of the passive scalar as
\left\|u-\bar{u}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\to 0
.

For transport with diffusion, for suitably small diffusion
\nu>0
, then

\left\|(\rho-\rho_{\nu})*\varphi_{\delta}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{r}}\leq C_{p,q}\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\left(\log\left(\frac{\delta^{2}}{\nu T}\left(\frac{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}}{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\right)\right)\right)^{-1},

see Proposition
3.6
and Corollary
3.7
for details. This result extends a quantitative result in
[
24
]
on similar metrized convergence of the passive scalar in the weak topology. We also mention that, for regular initial data, and
p>2
, in
[
7
]
, the authors show that one has similar logarithmic convergence of the vanishing diffusion limit in the strong topology
L_{x}^{2}
. Their argument requires a-priori ‘logarithmic’ regularity of the passive scalar, which is related to the topics discussed in Section
1.3
. Moreover, the authors obtain a rate
(-\log\nu)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}
which is faster than the rate above when
p>4
.

1.2
Mixing

We now address the connection between Theorem
2.4
and the rate at which a Sobolev vector field can mix a passive scalar. One approach to quantify mixing is through the minimum length scale
\delta>0
such that an object of the form
\left\|\rho(\cdot,t)*\varphi_{\delta}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}
is small. In
[
1
]
, one refers to such a length scale as the geometric mixing scale; see also
[
6
]
. Indeed, by fixing
\kappa>0
, and reversing time, the contrapositive of Theorem
2.4
says that if
\left\|\rho_{T}*\varphi_{\delta_{0}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}
is small then
\delta_{0}>0
is large, thus giving an exponential lower bound on the geometric mixing scale in the (dimensionless) parameter

\frac{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}}{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}}\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\nabla u(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}}.

(5)

Such mixing estimates have a long history of study; see
[
22
,
17
,
18
,
15
]
for a collection of mixing bounds in different settings,
[
6
]
for a conjecture on the maximal mixing rate by Bressan, and
[
12
,
1
]
for examples of self-similar mixing.

An alternative approach to measuring mixing is through negative Sobolev norms such as
H_{x}^{-1}
, which measure the average length scale in the passive scalar. One then expects a bound of the form

\left\|\rho_{T}\right\|_{H_{x}^{-1}}\geq A(\rho_{0})\exp\left(-C(\rho_{0})\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\nabla u(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}}\;dt\right),

(6)

where the constants
A,C>0
depend also on the initial datum
\rho_{0}(x)
, see
[
22
,
17
,
18
]
for different special cases.

Comparing with (
5
), we will show that Theorem
2.4
quantifies the dependence of
C(\rho_{0})
on the conserved ratio of the passive scalar,
\frac{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}}{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}}
, and in particular gives the first mixing bound valid for all passive scalar in the DiPerna-Lions class
\rho_{0}(x)\in L_{x}^{q}\cap L_{x}^{r}
.

Theorem
3.2
.

Fix a length scale
0<L\leq\infty
and for
\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{r}=1
define the negative Sobolev norm
W_{x}^{-1,r}
as the Banach space dual to

\left\|\phi\right\|_{W_{x}^{1,r^{\prime}}}=\frac{1}{L}\left\|\phi\right\|_{L_{x}^{r^{\prime}}}+\left\|\nabla\phi\right\|_{L_{x}^{r^{\prime}}}.

Let
\varphi(x)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
be any positive mollifier,
\varphi(x)\geq 0
. If
0<\delta\leq L
, and

\left\|\rho_{0}*\varphi_{\delta}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}},

i.e.
\rho_{0}(x)
is not already mixed at scale
\delta>0
, then

\left\|\rho_{T}\right\|_{W_{x}^{-1,r}}\geq\delta A\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}\exp\left(-C_{p,q}\frac{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}}{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}}\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\nabla u(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}}\;dt\right),

where the constants
A,C_{p,q}>0
also depend on the choice of positive mollifier
\varphi(x)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
and the dimension
d\geq 1
.

This result is a bound of the form (
6
) with
A(\rho_{0})=\delta A\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}
, and
C(\rho_{0})=C_{p,q}\frac{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}}{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}}
, for the negative Sobolev
W_{x}^{-1,r}
, which in compact domains controls
H_{x}^{-1}
when
r\leq 2
. This unifies the bounds given in
[
22
,
17
,
18
]
by removing any additional assumptions on the passive scalar, and indeed is this mixing bound is valid for all initial data
\rho_{0}(x)\in L_{x}^{q}\cap L_{x}^{r}
(or in compact domains just
\rho_{0}(x)\in L_{x}^{q}
, the DiPerna-Lions class
[
13
]
). In contrast, in
[
22
]
, the mixing bound is valid when
\rho_{0}(x)
is a bounded-variation, zero-average indicator function, meanwhile in
[
17
]
one requires
\rho_{0}(x)\in L_{x}^{\infty}
and the expressions for
A(\rho_{0})
and
C(\rho_{0})
are more involved, and finally in
[
22
]
the initial datum must possess a ‘logarithm’ of a derivative (such regularity is discussed more in Subsection
1.3
). On the other hand, in Theorem
3.2
the dependence of
C(\rho_{0})
on the conserved ratio
\frac{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}}{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}}
is optimal from a scaling point of view, while
A(\rho_{0})
is precisely the so-called "geometric mixing scale" of the initial data, see
[
1
]
.

1.3
Regularity of the passive scalar

We now give a different application of the commutator estimate (
1
). It was shown in
[
18
,
8
]
that the passive scalar has some regularity
\|\log(1-\Delta)^{\frac{p}{2}}\rho\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}
, provided the initial datum is also suitably regular. Moreover, the authors of
[
8
]
showed this to be sharp. However, they did not consider other norms than
L_{x}^{2}
. We show that we can improve the regularity to a full logarithm
\log(1-\Delta)
at the expense of integrability in
L_{x}^{r}
. By taking the kernel
K(x)=\log(1-\Delta)
in (
1
), we have

\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\log(1-\Delta)\rho(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}\leq C_{p,q}\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}\left\|\nabla u(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}},

(7)

see Proposition
3.8
for details.

When
p<2
, we improve the regularity
\log(1-\Delta)^{\frac{p}{2}}\rho(\cdot,t)
in
[
8
]
at the expense of the integrability. On the other hand, when
p>2
, we improve the integrability at the expense of the regularity. It is now natural to ask if one can unify these results, and to this end, we conjecture that there exists a stronger bound of the form

\left\|\log(1-\Delta)^{\frac{p}{r}}\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{r}}\leq\left\|\log(1-\Delta)^{\frac{p}{r}}\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}+C_{p}\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}^{\frac{p}{r}}.

In particular, when
\rho_{0}(x)\in L_{x}^{\infty}
, we conjecture finiteness of

\left\|\log(1-\Delta)^{p}\rho(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L_{x}^{1}},

in contrast to the exponent
\frac{p}{2}
in
[
8
]
.

1.4
Connection with the standard commutator

Finally, we discuss how certain harmonic estimates in this paper are equivalent to quantitative estimates on the decay of the DiPerna-Lions commutator in the standard proof of well-posedness
[
13
]
. Th DiPerna-Lions commutator arises as a special case of the commutator in Lemma
2.1
by taking the kernel to be the mollifier
K(x)=\varphi_{\delta}(x)
. The resulting commutator
R_{\delta}(x,t)
controls the error in the transport of
(\rho*\varphi_{\delta})(x,t)
and in
[
13
]
its norm
\left\|R_{\delta}(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}
is shown to vanish as
\delta\to 0
, from which well-posedness of the transport equation follows (for all
p\geq 1
). Quantitative estimates on the decay of the DiPerna-Lions commutator should, therefore, give quantitative estimates for the well-posedness of the Cauchy problem, akin to Theorem
2.4
, and this was the original motivation for this work. Indeed, the key harmonic estimate in the proof of Theorem
2.4
is equivalent to an integral estimate on the decay of the DiPerna-Lions commutator. In particular,

\left\|\int_{0}^{\infty}R_{\delta}(x,t)\;\frac{d\delta}{\delta}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}\leq C_{p,q}\left\|\rho(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}\left\|\nabla u(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}},

(8)

see Proposition
4.1
for details. When sharing a version of this manuscript, we were made aware of an unpublished result similar to (
8
) by Brué, Colombo and De Philippis
[
9
]
. Since the integral against
\frac{d\delta}{\delta}
is singular, this implies some quantitative control on the pointwise decay of
R_{\delta}(x,t)\to 0
as
\delta\to 0
. While this is not a quantitative decay rate for the norm
\left\|R_{\delta}(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}\to 0
, it still implies the stability estimate Theorem
2.4
. We now ask if we can improve this estimate since stronger estimates on the DiPerna-Lions commutator should lead to stronger quantitative estimates for transport.

Directly applying Lemma
2.1
to give a quantitative decay rate for
\left\|R_{\delta}(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}
fails, as the Calderon-Zygmund norm of
\left\|\frac{\partial(x_{j}\varphi_{\delta})}{\partial x_{i}}\right\|_{CZ}
in the bound (
1
) does not vanish as
\delta\to 0
. Indeed, we later show that such a quantitative decay rate does not exist; see point 1 of Proposition
4.7
. Despite this, one may still improve upon the estimate (
8
) by different methods. Using the Lion-Peetre interpolation method to quantify the compactness argument in Proposition
4.3
, we show that

\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\|R_{\delta}(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}^{\max(p,q)}\;\frac{d\delta}{\delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\max(p,q)}}\leq C_{p,q}\left\|\rho(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}\left\|\nabla u(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}},

see Theorem
4.4
, and Corollary
4.6
for details. Rather than a quantitative decay rate, this gives an
integral
decay rate for the norm
\left\|R_{\delta}(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}\to 0
. However, this comes at the expense of the weaker exponent
\max(p,q)
, which is sharp when
p,q=2
, see point 2 of Proposition
4.7
. While this leads to suboptimal quantitative estimates compared to (
8
), the methods are interesting as an alternative approach to quantitative estimates in general. In particular, in the follow-up work
[
16
]
, we extend these ideas to give quantitative estimates for passive scalar transport when
p=1
, and even when the vector field is in
BV
.

2
Quantitative well-posedness estimates

For any convolution kernel
K(x):\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, consider the commutator
R(x,t)
which gives the error in the transport of
(\rho*K)(x,t)
:

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+u(x,t)\cdot\nabla\right)(\rho*K)(x,t)=R(x,t),

i.e.

R(x,t)=u(x,t)\cdot(\rho*\nabla K)(x,t)-\nabla\cdot((u\rho)*K)(x,t).

By the Chris-Journé singular integral estimate in
[
21
]
, this commutator admits the following bound:

Lemma 2.1
.

Consider a scalar
\rho(x):\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
with
\rho(x)\in L_{x}^{q}
, and a divergence-free vector field
u(x):\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
\nabla u(x)\in L_{x}^{p}
. Let
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{r}
, with
1<p,q\leq\infty
,
1\leq r<\infty
.

For any convolution kernel
K(x):\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, define the commutator

R(x)=u(x)\cdot(\rho*\nabla K)(x)-\nabla\cdot((u\rho)*K)(x),

then

\left\|R\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}\leq C_{p,q}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}}\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\left\|\frac{\partial(x_{j}K)}{\partial x_{i}}\right\|_{CZ}\right),

where the constant
C_{p,q}>0
depends only on the parameters
1<p,q\leq\infty
and the dimension
d\geq 1
, and where
\left\|K\right\|_{CZ}
refers to the Calderon-Zygmund norm, defined by

\displaystyle\left|K(x)\right|\leq|x|^{-d}\left\|K\right\|_{CZ},

\displaystyle\left|\nabla K(x)\right|\leq|x|^{-d-1}\left\|K\right\|_{CZ},

\displaystyle|\hat{K}(\xi)|\leq\left\|K\right\|_{CZ},

for all
x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
\hat{K}(\xi):\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
denotes the Fourier transform.

Proof.

We have, as for the DiPerna-Lions commutator
[
13
]
,

\displaystyle R(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(y)(u(x)-u(y))\cdot\nabla K(x-y)\;dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(x-h)\sum^{d}_{i,j=1}\left(\int_{0}^{1}h_{j}\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}(x-th)\;dt\right)\frac{\partial K}{\partial h_{i}}(h)\;dh

\displaystyle=\begin{aligned} &\sum^{d}_{i,j=1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(x-h)\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}(x-th)\;dt\right)\frac{\partial(h_{j}K)}{\partial h_{i}}(h)\;dh\\
&-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(x-h)\left(\int_{0}^{1}(\nabla\cdot u)(x-th)\;dt\right)K(h)\;dh.\end{aligned}

hence since
\nabla\cdot u(x)=0
, we have

\left\|R\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}\leq\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(x-h)\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}(x-th)\;dt\right)\frac{\partial(h_{j}K)}{\partial h_{i}}(h)\;dh\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}},

and so we conclude by the key harmonic analysis estimate
[
21
]
*Theorem 1.1.
∎

From this, we deduce the main result of this paper, which unifies various stability, mixing, and well-posedness estimates:

Definition 2.2

(Mollifier)
.

We say
\varphi(x)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
is a standard mollifier if

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(x)\;dx=1\text{ and define }\varphi_{\delta}(x)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\ \varphi_{\delta}(x)=\delta^{-d}\varphi\left(\frac{x}{\delta}\right),

for all
\delta>0
. Note that one often asks for additional smoothness of
\varphi(x)
such as
\varphi(x)\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We will make this explicit when it is required.

Definition 2.3

(Frequency cutoff)
.

We say a mollifier
\varphi(x)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
(a Schwartz function) is a frequency-cutoff if its Fourier transform satisfies the following:

\begin{gathered}\hat{\varphi}(\xi)=1\text{ for }|\xi|\leq 1,\\
\hat{\varphi}(\xi)=0\text{ for }|\xi|\geq 2.\end{gathered}

(9)

We now give the main result of this paper, a quantitative well-posedness result for passive scalar transport, which quantifies the propagation of the smallness of the initial data. Continuous dependence on the initial data in the strong topology is standard by the theory of renormalised solutions,
[
13
]
. In contrast, using Lemma
2.1
, we show continuous dependence on the initial data in the weak topology, quantified by frequency cutoffs:

Theorem 2.4
.

Consider a weak solution
\rho(x,t):\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
, with
\rho(x,t)\in L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}
, and initial datum
\rho_{0}(x)\in L_{x}^{q}
, to the forced transport equation

\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}(x,t)+\nabla\cdot(u(x,t)\rho(x,t))=f(x,t),

along a divergence-free vector field
u(x,t):\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
\nabla u(x,t)\in L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}
, for
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{r}
, with
1<p,q\leq\infty
,
1\leq r<\infty
, and distributional force
f(x,t)\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
a Schwartz distribution supported on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T]
.

Fix a mollifier/frequency cutoff
\varphi(x)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
as in Definition
2.3
.

Then for any
\delta>0
and
\kappa>0
we have the following quantitative bound:

If

\delta_{0}\leq\frac{1}{4}\delta\exp\left(-\frac{C_{p,q}}{\kappa}\frac{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}}{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\right),

then

\left\|\rho*\varphi_{\delta}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{r}}\leq\kappa+A\left\|\rho_{0}*\varphi_{\delta_{0}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}+A\left\|f*\varphi_{\delta_{0}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{r}},

where the constant
C_{p,q}>0
depends only on the parameters
1<p,q\leq\infty
, the choice of mollifier
\varphi(x)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
, and the dimension
d\geq 1
, while
A>0
depends only on the choice of mollifier
\varphi(x)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
and the dimension
d\geq 1
.

Proof.

Throughout,
\lesssim
will denote less than or equal to up to a constant depending only on the choice of mollifier
\varphi(x)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
, and the dimension
d\geq 1
.

We consider the following convolution kernel in Lemma
2.1
:

K(x)=\int_{\delta_{1}}^{\delta_{2}}\varphi_{\delta}(x)\;\frac{d\delta}{\delta}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}),

(10)

for some
0<\delta_{1}<\delta_{2}
to be chosen later.

Let
\psi(x,t)\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T];\mathbb{R})
be any test function. Let
\phi(x,t)\in L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{p}
be a weak solution to the
backwards
transport equation with force
\psi(x,t)
:

\begin{gathered}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}(x,t)+\nabla\cdot(u(x,t)\phi(x,t))=\psi(x,t),\\
\phi(x,T)=0.\end{gathered}

(11)

satisfying also the standard energy estimate

\left\|\phi\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{r^{\prime}}}\leq\left\|\psi\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{r^{\prime}}},

(12)

for
\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{r}=1
, see for instance the lecture notes
[
2
]
.

Convolving this equation with
\bar{K}(x)=K(-x)
gives

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\phi*\bar{K})(x,t)+\nabla\cdot(u(x,t)(\phi*\bar{K})(x,t))=R(x,t)+(\psi*\bar{K})(x,t),

(13)

in terms of the commutator

\displaystyle R(x,t)

\displaystyle=u(x,t)\cdot\nabla(\phi*\bar{K})(x,t)-(\nabla\cdot(u\phi)*\bar{K})(x,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(y)(u(x)-u(y))\cdot\nabla\bar{K}(x-y)\;dy,

Applying Lemma
3.8
, for
\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{q}=1
,
\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{r}=1
, we have the bound

\displaystyle\left\|R(x,t)\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{q^{\prime}}}\leq C_{p,q}\left\|\phi\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{r^{\prime}}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\left\|\frac{\partial(x_{j}\bar{K})}{\partial x_{i}}\right\|_{CZ}\right),

(14)

for some constant
C_{p,q}>0
depending only on the parameters
1<p,q\leq\infty
and the dimension
d\geq 1
.

To estimate this Calderon-Zygmund norm, we note that

\left\|\frac{\partial(x_{j}\bar{K})}{\partial x_{i}}\right\|_{CZ}=\left\|\frac{\partial(x_{j}K)}{\partial x_{i}}\right\|_{CZ},

and so define

\displaystyle K_{i,j}^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial(x_{j}K)}{\partial x_{i}}(x)=\int_{\delta_{1}}^{\delta_{2}}\frac{\partial(x_{j}\varphi)}{\partial x_{i}}\left(\frac{x}{\delta}\right)\;\frac{d\delta}{\delta^{d+1}}.

We then have the following Calderon-Zygmund estimates:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |K_{i,j}^{\prime}(x)|&\leq\left\|(1+|x|^{d+1})\frac{\partial(x_{j}\varphi)}{\partial x_{i}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\left(\int_{|x|}^{\infty}\delta^{-d-1}d\delta+\int_{0}^{|x|}|x|^{-d-1}d\delta\right)\\
&\lesssim|x|^{-d},\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left|\frac{\partial K_{i,j}^{\prime}}{\partial x_{k}}(x)\right|&=\left|\int_{\delta_{1}}^{\delta_{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}(x_{j}\varphi)}{\partial x_{k}\partial x_{i}}\left(\frac{x}{\delta}\right)\;\frac{d\delta}{\delta^{d+2}}\right|\\
&\leq\left\|(1+|x|^{d+2})\frac{\partial^{2}(x_{j}\varphi)}{\partial x_{k}\partial x_{i}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\left(\int_{|x|}^{\infty}\delta^{-d-2}d\delta+\int_{0}^{|x|}|x|^{-d-2}d\delta\right)\\
&\lesssim|x|^{-d-1},\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |\hat{K}_{i,j}^{\prime}(\xi)|&=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{i\xi\cdot x}\int_{\delta_{1}}^{\delta_{2}}\frac{\partial(x_{j}\varphi)}{\partial x_{i}}\left(\frac{x}{\delta}\right)\;\frac{d\delta}{\delta^{d+1}}\;dx\right|\\
&=\left|\int_{\delta_{1}}^{\delta_{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{i\delta\xi\cdot h}\frac{\partial(x_{j}\varphi)}{\partial x_{i}}(h)\;dh\right)\frac{d\delta}{\delta}\right|\\
&=\left|\int_{\delta_{1}}^{\delta_{2}}-i\delta\xi_{i}\frac{\partial\hat{\varphi}}{\partial\xi_{j}}(\delta\xi)\;\frac{d\delta}{\delta}\right|\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}|\xi_{i}|\left|\frac{\partial\hat{\varphi}}{\partial\xi_{j}}\right|(\delta\xi)\;d\delta\\
&\leq\left\|(1+|\xi_{i}|^{2})\frac{\partial\hat{\varphi}}{\partial\xi_{j}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\left(\int_{0}^{|\xi_{i}|^{-1}}|\xi_{i}|\;d\delta+\int_{|\xi_{i}|^{-1}}^{\infty}|\xi_{i}|^{-1}\delta^{-2}\;d\delta\right)\\
&\lesssim 1.\end{aligned}

where, in particular, none of the bounds depends on
0<\delta_{1}<\delta_{2}
. That is

\left\|K_{i,j}^{\prime}\right\|_{CZ}\lesssim 1.

Therefore, by Lemma
2.1
and the energy estimate
\left\|\phi\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{r^{\prime}}}\leq\left\|\psi\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{r^{\prime}}}
in (
12
), then (
14
) becomes

\left\|R(x,t)\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{q^{\prime}}}\lesssim C_{p,q}\left\|\psi\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{r^{\prime}}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}.

(15)

By the expression for the kernel
K(x)
in (
10
), we estimate

\displaystyle\left\|\phi*\bar{K}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{r^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|K\right\|_{L_{x}^{1}}\left\|\phi\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{r^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\log\left(\frac{\delta_{2}}{\delta_{1}}\right)\left\|\psi\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{r^{\prime}}}

(16)

Recall that
\rho(x,t)
is a weak solution to the following forced transport equation

\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}(x,t)+\nabla\cdot(u(x,t)\rho(x,t))=f(x,t),

(17)

with distributional force
\psi(x,t)\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
supported on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T]
.

Assume that the distribution
(f*\varphi_{2^{-1}\delta_{1}})(x,t)
has finite
L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{r}
-norm, else the statement of the theorem is trivial. Then also
(f*\varphi_{2^{-1}\delta_{1}}*K)(x,t)\in L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{r}
.

Since the mollifier
\varphi(x)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
is a frequency-cutoff (
9
), we see that for all
\delta\geq 2\delta_{2}
, and
\delta_{1}\leq\delta^{\prime}\leq\delta_{2}

\begin{gathered}\varphi_{\delta}=\varphi_{\delta^{\prime}}*\varphi_{\delta},\\
\varphi_{\delta^{\prime}}=\varphi_{2^{-1}\delta_{1}}*\varphi_{\delta^{\prime}}.\end{gathered}

(18)

In particular, by integrating over
\delta^{\prime}
,

\begin{gathered}\log\left(\frac{\delta_{2}}{\delta_{1}}\right)\varphi_{\delta}(x)=(K*\varphi_{\delta})(x),\\
K(x)=(\varphi_{2^{-1}\delta_{1}}*K)(x).\end{gathered}

(19)

Since
(f*\varphi_{2^{-1}\delta_{1}}*K)(x,t)\in L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{r}
, then
(f*K)(x,t)\in L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{r}
.

Therefore, we may take
(\phi*\bar{K})(x,t)
as a test function to (
17
):

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T]}\rho(x,t)\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\phi*\bar{K})(x,t)+u(x,t)\cdot\nabla(\phi*\bar{K})(x,t)\right)\;dxdt

\displaystyle\qquad=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{0}(x)(\phi*\bar{K})(x,0)\;dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}}(f*K)(x,t)\phi(x,t)\;dxdt.

and so, using (
13
),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T]}\rho(x,t)(\psi*\bar{K})(x,t)\;dxdt+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T]}\rho(x,t)R(x,t)\;dxdt

\displaystyle\qquad=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{0}(x)(\phi*\bar{K})(x,0)\;dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}}(f*K)(x,t)\phi(x,t)\;dxdt.

By (
19
), we also have
\bar{K}(x)=(\bar{K}*\varphi_{2^{-1}\delta_{1}})(x)
, and so

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T]}(\rho*K)(x,t)\psi(x,t)\;dxdt+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T]}\rho(x,t)R(x,t)\;dxdt

\displaystyle\qquad=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\rho_{0}*\varphi_{2^{-1}\delta_{1}})(x)(\phi*\bar{K})(x,0)\;dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}}(f*\varphi_{2^{-1}\delta_{1}})(x,t)(\phi*\bar{K})(x,t)\;dxdt.

Therefore, by (
15
), (
16
) we have the bound

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T]}(\rho*K)(x,t)\psi(x,t)\;dxdt

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\begin{aligned} &\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}}\left\|R\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{q^{\prime}}}+\left\|\rho_{0}*\varphi_{2^{-1}\delta_{1}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}\left\|K\right\|_{L_{x}^{1}}\left\|\phi\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{r^{\prime}}}\\
&+\left\|f*\varphi_{2^{-1}\delta_{1}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{r}}\left\|\phi*\bar{K}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{r^{\prime}}}\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\begin{aligned} &C_{p,q}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}}\left\|\psi\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{r^{\prime}}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}+\log\left(\frac{\delta_{2}}{\delta_{1}}\right)\left\|\rho_{0}*\varphi_{2^{-1}\delta_{1}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}\left\|\psi\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{r^{\prime}}}\\
&+\log\left(\frac{\delta_{2}}{\delta_{1}}\right)\left\|f*\varphi_{2^{-1}\delta_{1}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{r}}\left\|\psi\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{r^{\prime}}},\end{aligned}

and since
\psi(x,t)\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T];\mathbb{R})
is arbitrary, this becomes

\left\|\rho*K\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{r}}\lesssim C_{p,q}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}+\log\left(\frac{\delta_{2}}{\delta_{1}}\right)\left\|\rho_{0}*\varphi_{2^{-1}\delta_{1}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}+\log\left(\frac{\delta_{2}}{\delta_{1}}\right)\left\|f*\varphi_{2^{-1}\delta_{1}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{r}}.

Then by (
18
), for any
\delta\geq 2\delta_{2}
,

\displaystyle\left\|\rho*\varphi_{\delta}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{r}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\log\left(\frac{\delta_{2}}{\delta_{1}}\right)}\left\|\rho*K\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{C_{p,q}}{\log\left(\frac{\delta_{2}}{\delta_{1}}\right)}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}+\left\|\rho_{0}*\varphi_{2^{-1}\delta_{1}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}+\left\|f*\varphi_{2^{-1}\delta_{1}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{r}}.

We now pick
0<\delta_{1}<\delta_{2}
. For any
\kappa>0
, let

\displaystyle\delta_{2}=\delta_{1}\exp\left(\frac{C_{p,q}}{\kappa}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\right).

Then for any

\delta\geq 4(2^{-1}\delta_{1})\exp\left(\frac{C_{p,q}}{\kappa}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\right),

we have the following bounds:

\left\|\rho*\varphi_{\delta}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{r}}\lesssim\kappa+\left\|\rho_{0}*\varphi_{2^{-1}\delta_{1}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}+\left\|f*\varphi_{2^{-1}\delta_{1}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{r}},

and so the result follows by letting
\delta_{1}=2\delta_{0}
, and redefining
\kappa>0
.
∎

3
Mixing, regularity, and other applications

We now apply Lemma
2.1
and Theorem
2.4
to four well-studied problems in the theory of passive scalar transport: mixing, approximation of the vector field, the vanishing diffusion limit, and regularity of the passive scalar.

Definition 3.1

(
W_{x}^{-1,r}
)
.

For scaling reasons, in the definition of the weak norm
W_{x}^{-1,r}
, we first fix a length scale
0<L\leq\infty
. For
\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{r}=1
, we define the norm
W_{x}^{-1,r}
as the Banach space dual to

\left\|\phi\right\|_{W_{x}^{1,r^{\prime}}}=\frac{1}{L}\left\|\phi\right\|_{L_{x}^{r^{\prime}}}+\left\|\nabla\phi\right\|_{L_{x}^{r^{\prime}}}.

We now apply Theorem
2.4
to give an exponential lower bound on the decay of the negative Sobolev norm
\left\|\rho(\cdot,t)\right\|_{W_{x}^{-1,r}}
, also studied in
[
22
,
17
,
18
]
. The norm
W_{x}^{-1,r}
measures the average length scale of the passive scalar at time
t>0
, and so quantifies how mixed is the passive scalar:

Theorem 3.2

(Mixing bound)
.

Consider a weak solution
\rho(x,t):\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
, with
\rho(x,t)\in L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}
, and initial datum
\rho_{0}(x)\in L_{x}^{q}
, to the transport equation

\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}(x,t)+\nabla\cdot(u(x,t)\rho(x,t))=0,

along a divergence-free
u(x,t):\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
\nabla u(x,t)\in L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}
, for
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{r}
, with
1<p,q\leq\infty
,
1\leq r<\infty
.

Let
\varphi(x)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
be any positive mollifier.

For some length scale
0<L\leq\infty
define the norm
W_{x}^{-1,r}
as in Definition
3.1
.

For any
0<\delta\leq L
such that

\left\|\rho_{0}*\varphi_{\delta}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}},

then

\left\|\rho(\cdot,T)\right\|_{W_{x}^{-1,r}}\geq\delta A\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}\exp\left(-C_{p,q}\frac{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}}{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\right),

where the constant
C_{p,q}>0
depends only on the parameters
1<p,q\leq\infty
and the dimension
d\geq 1
, while
A>0
depends on the choice of positive mollifier
\varphi(x)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
and the dimension
d\geq 1
.

Proof.

For such a weak solution
\rho(x,t)
, define the time reverse
\tilde{\rho}(x,t)=\rho(x,T-t)
, and similar
\tilde{u}(x,t)=-u(x,T-t)
. Then
\tilde{\rho}(x,t)
is a weak solution to the transport equation along
\tilde{u}(x,t)
with initial datum
\tilde{\rho}_{0}(x)=\rho(x,T)
satisfying
\left\|\tilde{\rho}_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}=\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}
,
\left\|\tilde{\rho}_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}=\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}

[
13
]
.

Therefore, by Theorem
2.4
, for all
\delta_{0}>0
and any
\kappa>0
, we have the following quantitative bound on the propagation of length scales. For

\displaystyle\delta^{\prime}

\displaystyle=4\delta_{0}\exp\left(\frac{C_{p,q}}{\kappa}\frac{\left\|\tilde{\rho}_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}}{\left\|\tilde{\rho}_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}}\left\|\nabla\tilde{u}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\right)

\displaystyle=4\delta_{0}\exp\left(\frac{C_{p,q}}{\kappa}\frac{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}}{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\right)

then fixing a mollifier
\tilde{\varphi}(x)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
satisfying the frequency cutoff (
9
) as in the statement of Theorem
2.4
, one has

\displaystyle\left\|\rho_{0}*\tilde{\varphi}_{\delta^{\prime}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\tilde{\rho}*\tilde{\varphi}_{\delta^{\prime}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{r}}

\displaystyle\leq\kappa\left\|\tilde{\rho}_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}+A\left\|\tilde{\rho}_{0}*\tilde{\varphi}_{\delta_{0}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}

\displaystyle\leq\kappa\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}+A\left\|\rho(\cdot,T)*\tilde{\varphi}_{\delta_{0}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}},

with constants
C_{p,q}>0
depending only on the parameters
1<p,q\leq\infty
and the dimension
d\geq 1
, and
A>0
depending only on the dimension
d\geq 1
.

Meanwhile,

\displaystyle\left\|\rho(\cdot,T)*\tilde{\varphi}_{\delta_{0}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}

\displaystyle\qquad=\sup_{\left\|\phi\right\|_{L_{x}^{r^{\prime}}}\leq 1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(y)\tilde{\varphi}_{\delta_{0}}(y-x)\rho(x,T)\;dy\;dx

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\left\|\rho(\cdot,T)\right\|_{W_{x}^{-1,r}}\sup_{\left\|\phi\right\|_{L_{x}^{r^{\prime}}}\leq 1}\left(\frac{1}{L}\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(y)\tilde{\varphi}_{\delta_{0}}(y-x)\;dy\right\|_{L_{x}^{r^{\prime}}}+\left\|\nabla\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(y)\tilde{\varphi}_{\delta_{0}}(y-x)\;dy\right\|_{L_{x}^{r^{\prime}}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\left\|\rho(\cdot,T)\right\|_{W_{x}^{-1,r}}\left(\frac{1}{L}\left\|\tilde{\varphi}_{\delta_{0}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{1}}+\left\|\nabla\tilde{\varphi}_{\delta_{0}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{1}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq A^{\prime}\left(\frac{1}{L}+\frac{1}{\delta}\right)\left\|\rho(\cdot,T)\right\|_{W_{x}^{-1,r}},

for a constant
A^{\prime}>0
depending only on the choice of mollifier
\tilde{\varphi}(x)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
and the dimension
d\geq 1
.

Combining the above, we see that

\displaystyle\left\|\rho(\cdot,T)\right\|_{W_{x}^{-1,r}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{AA^{\prime}}\left(\frac{1}{L}+\frac{1}{\delta}\right)^{-1}\left(\left\|\rho_{0}*\tilde{\varphi}_{\delta^{\prime}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}-\kappa\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{AA^{\prime}}\left(\frac{1}{L}+\frac{4}{\delta^{\prime}}\exp\left(\frac{C_{p,q}}{\kappa}\frac{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}}{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\right)\right)^{-1}\left(\left\|\rho_{0}*\tilde{\varphi}_{\delta^{\prime}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}-\kappa\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}\right),

for any choice of
\delta^{\prime}>0
. In particular, if
\delta^{\prime}\leq L
is such that

\left\|\rho_{0}*\tilde{\varphi}_{\delta^{\prime}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}\geq\frac{1}{4}\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}},

then taking
\kappa=\frac{1}{8}
we have the bound

\left\|\rho(\cdot,T)\right\|_{W_{x}^{-1,r}}\geq\frac{\delta^{\prime}\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}}{40AA^{\prime}}\exp\left(-8C_{p,q}\frac{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}}{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\right).

Now consider a positive mollifier
\varphi(x)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
, so that in particular,
\left\|\varphi\right\|_{L_{x}^{1}}=1
. Let
0<\delta\leq L
be small enough that

\left\|\rho_{0}*\varphi_{\delta}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}.

Since
(\varphi*\tilde{\varphi}_{\alpha})(x)\xrightarrow{\alpha\to 0}\varphi(x)
in
L_{x}^{1}
, there exists some
0<\alpha\leq 1
such that
\left\|\varphi*\tilde{\varphi}_{\alpha}-\varphi\right\|_{L_{x}^{1}}\leq\frac{1}{4}
, and so for all
\delta>0
, also

\left\|\varphi_{\delta}*\tilde{\varphi}_{\alpha\delta}-\varphi_{\delta}\right\|_{L_{x}^{1}}\leq\frac{1}{4}.

Let
\delta^{\prime}=\alpha\delta\leq L
. Then since
\left\|\varphi\right\|_{L_{x}^{1}}=1
,

\displaystyle\left\|\rho_{0}*\tilde{\varphi}_{\delta^{\prime}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}

\displaystyle\geq\left\|\rho_{0}*\varphi_{\delta}*\tilde{\varphi}_{\delta^{\prime}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}

\displaystyle\geq\left\|\rho_{0}*\varphi_{\delta}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}-\left\|\rho_{0}*\varphi_{\delta}*\tilde{\varphi}_{\delta^{\prime}}-\rho_{0}*\varphi_{\delta}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}-\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}\left\|\varphi_{\delta}*\tilde{\varphi}_{\alpha\delta}-\varphi_{\delta}\right\|_{L_{x}^{1}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}},

and so

\left\|\rho(\cdot,T)\right\|_{W_{x}^{-1,r}}\geq\frac{\alpha\delta\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}}{40AA^{\prime}}\exp\left(-8C_{p,q}\frac{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}}{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\right),

where
\alpha>0
depends only on the choice of positive mollifier
\varphi(x)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

We remark that the dependence of the decay rate on the conserved ratio
\frac{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}}{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}}
is necessary by dimensional analysis. This ratio captures the relevant length scale of the passive scalar, and ensures that the full rate

\frac{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}}{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}},

is dimensionless. In the case
p=\infty
this reduces to the classical decay rate
\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{\infty}}
, however for
p<\infty
it is important to observe that
\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}
has units of length to the power
\frac{d}{p}
and so cannot bound the mixing rate by its self.

We next apply Theorem
2.4
to give a quantitative convergence rate for the passive scalar under any approximation of the vector field, also studied in
[
23
,
24
]
:

Proposition 3.4

(Approximation of the vector field)
.

Consider a weak solution
\rho(x,t):\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
, with
\rho(x,t)\in L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}
, and initial datum
\rho_{0}(x)\in L_{x}^{q}
, to the transport equation

\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}(x,t)+\nabla\cdot(u(x,t)\rho(x,t))=0,

along a divergence-free vector field
u(x,t):\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
\nabla u(x,t)\in L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}
, for
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{r}
, with
1<p,q\leq\infty
,
1\leq r<\infty
.

Let
\bar{\rho}(x,t)\in L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}
be a weak solution with the same initial datum
\rho_{0}(x)\in L_{x}^{q}
along a different (low-regularity) divergence-free vector field
\bar{u}(x,t):\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Fix a mollifier/frequency cutoff
\varphi(x)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
as in Definition
2.3
.

We have the following weak convergence of
\bar{\rho}(x,t)\xrightharpoonup{}\rho(x,t)
as
\left\|u-\bar{u}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\to 0
:

For any
\delta>0
, if
\left\|u-\bar{u}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\leq\delta\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}
, then

\left\|(\rho-\bar{\rho})*\varphi_{\delta}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{r}}\leq C_{p,q}\left\|\bar{\rho}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\left(\log\left(\frac{\delta\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}}{\left\|u-\bar{u}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}}\right)\right)^{-1},

where the constant
C_{p,q}>0
depends only on the parameters
1<p,q\leq\infty
, the choice of mollifier
\varphi(x)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
, and the dimension
d\geq 1
.

Corollary 3.5
.

For some length scale
0<L\leq\infty
define the norm
W_{x}^{-1,r}
as in Definition
3.1
.

If

\left\|u-\bar{u}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\leq L\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\min\bigg(1,\bigg(\frac{\left\|\bar{\rho}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}}}{\left\|\bar{\rho}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{r}}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\bigg)^{2}\bigg),

then

\left\|\rho-\bar{\rho}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}W_{x}^{-1,r}}\leq C_{p,q}L\left\|\bar{\rho}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\left(\log\left(\frac{L\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}}{\left\|u-\bar{u}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}}\right)\right)^{-1},

where the constant
C_{p,q}>0
depends only on the parameters
1<p,q\leq\infty
and the dimension
d\geq 1
.

Proof.

We begin by writing
(\rho-\bar{\rho})(x,t)\in L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}
as a weak solution with zero initial datum to the following forced transport equation along
u(x,t)
:

\frac{\partial(\rho-\bar{\rho})}{\partial t}(x,t)+\nabla\cdot(u(x,t)(\rho-\bar{\rho})(x,t))=f(x,t),

with the distributional force given by

f(x,t)=-\nabla\cdot((u\bar{\rho}-\bar{u}\bar{\rho})(x,t)).

By Theorem
2.4
, for any
\kappa>0
, we have the following quantitative bound:

For

\delta^{\prime}=\frac{\delta}{4}\exp\left(-\frac{C_{p,q}}{\kappa}\left\|\rho-\bar{\rho}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\right),

then

\displaystyle\left\|(\rho-\bar{\rho})*\varphi_{\delta}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{r}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\kappa+A\left\|f*\varphi_{\delta^{\prime}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{r}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\kappa+A\left\|\nabla\varphi_{\delta^{\prime}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}\left\|u\bar{\rho}-\bar{u}\bar{\rho}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{1}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\kappa+A\delta^{\prime-1}\left\|\nabla\varphi\right\|_{L_{x}^{1}}\left\|u-\bar{u}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\left\|\bar{\rho}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\kappa+\frac{4A}{\delta}\exp\left(\frac{C_{p,q}}{\kappa}\left\|\rho-\bar{\rho}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\right)\left\|u-\bar{u}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\left\|\bar{\rho}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\kappa+A^{\prime}\delta^{-1}\left\|u-\bar{u}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\left\|\bar{\rho}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}}\exp\left(\frac{C_{p,q}^{\prime}}{\kappa}\left\|\bar{\rho}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\right),

(20)

where the constants
C_{p,q},C_{p,q}^{\prime}>0
depends only on the parameters
1<p,q\leq\infty
, the choice of mollifier
\varphi(x)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
, and the dimension
d\geq 1
, while
A,A^{\prime}>0
depend only on the choice of mollifier
\varphi(x)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
and the dimension
d\geq 1
.

Let

\displaystyle\epsilon=\delta\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}},

\displaystyle\gamma=2\left\|\bar{\rho}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}},

and rewrite (
20
) as, for any
\kappa>0
,

\left\|(\rho-\bar{\rho})*\varphi_{\delta}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{r}}\leq\kappa+A^{\prime}\frac{\left\|u-\bar{u}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}}{2\epsilon}\gamma\exp\left(C_{p,q}^{\prime}{\frac{\gamma}{\kappa}}\right).

We now crudely optimise over
\kappa>0
:

For
\left\|u-\bar{u}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\leq\epsilon
, let

\kappa=\frac{2C_{p,q}^{\prime}\gamma}{-\log\left\|u-\bar{u}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}+\log\epsilon},

then, using
x^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\frac{2e^{-1}}{-\log x}
for
x\in(0,1]
,

\displaystyle\kappa+A^{\prime}\frac{\left\|u-\bar{u}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}}{2\epsilon}\gamma\exp\left(C_{p,q}^{\prime}\frac{\gamma}{\kappa}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{2C_{p,q}^{\prime}\gamma}{-\log\left\|u-\bar{u}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}+\log\epsilon}+A^{\prime}\frac{\left\|u-\bar{u}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}}{2\epsilon}\gamma\left(\frac{\epsilon}{\left\|u-\bar{u}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{2C_{p,q}^{\prime}\gamma}{-\log\left\|u-\bar{u}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}+\log\epsilon}+\frac{A^{\prime}e^{-1}\gamma}{-\log\left\|u-\bar{u}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}+\log\epsilon}.

This bound concludes the proof of Theorem
3.4
. We now turn to the proof of Corollary
3.5
.

For some length scale
0<L\leq\infty
define the norm
W_{x}^{-1,r}
as in Definition
3.1
. In general, we may bound the weak norm
W_{x}^{-1,r}
by

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{W_{x}^{-1,r}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\frac{1}{L}\left\|\phi\right\|_{L_{x}^{r^{\prime}}}+\left\|\nabla\phi\right\|_{L_{x}^{r^{\prime}}}\leq 1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)\phi(x)\;dx

\displaystyle=\sup_{\frac{1}{L}\left\|\phi\right\|_{L_{x}^{r^{\prime}}}+\left\|\nabla\phi\right\|_{L_{x}^{r^{\prime}}}\leq 1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(f*\varphi_{\delta})(x)\phi(x)\;dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(f-f*\varphi_{\delta})(x)\phi(x)\;dx\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\frac{1}{L}\left\|\phi\right\|_{L_{x}^{r^{\prime}}}+\left\|\nabla\phi\right\|_{L_{x}^{r^{\prime}}}\leq 1}\left(\left\|f*\varphi_{\delta}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}\left\|\phi\right\|_{L_{x}^{r^{\prime}}}+\delta\left\|f\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}\left\|\nabla\phi\right\|_{L_{x}^{r^{\prime}}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim L\left\|f*\varphi_{\delta}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}+\delta\left\|f\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}.

Applying this to
(\rho-\bar{\rho})(x,t)
, and the previous bounds, gives

\displaystyle\left\|\rho-\bar{\rho}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}W_{x}^{-1,r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim L\left\|\bar{\rho}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\left(\log\left(\frac{\epsilon}{\left\|u-\bar{u}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}}\right)\right)^{-1}+\delta\left\|\rho-\bar{\rho}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim L\left\|\bar{\rho}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\left(\log\left(\frac{\epsilon}{\left\|u-\bar{u}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}}\right)\right)^{-1}+\delta\left\|\bar{\rho}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{r}},

(21)

where
\epsilon>0
depends on
\delta>0
.

We now crudely optimise over
\delta>0
:

For
\left\|u-\bar{u}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\leq
let

\delta=\frac{L\left\|\bar{\rho}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}}{\left\|\bar{\rho}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{r}}}\left(\log\left(\frac{L\left\|\bar{\rho}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}^{2}}{\left\|u-\bar{u}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\left\|\bar{\rho}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{r}}}\right)\right)^{-1},

and so, using that
-\log\log x\geq-e^{-1}\log x
for
x\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\log\left(\frac{\epsilon}{\left\|u-\bar{u}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\log\left(\frac{L\left\|\bar{\rho}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}^{2}}{\left\|u-\bar{u}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\left\|\bar{\rho}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{r}}}\right)-\log\log\left(\frac{L\left\|\bar{\rho}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}^{2}}{\left\|u-\bar{u}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\left\|\bar{\rho}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{r}}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq(1-e^{-1})\log\left(\frac{L\left\|\bar{\rho}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}^{2}}{\left\|u-\bar{u}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\left\|\bar{\rho}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{r}}}\right),

Therefore, (
21
) becomes

\left\|\rho-\bar{\rho}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}W_{x}^{-1,r}}\lesssim L\left\|\bar{\rho}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\left(\log\left(\frac{L\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}}{\left\|u-\bar{u}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}}\right)+\log\left(\frac{\left\|\bar{\rho}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}}}{\left\|\bar{\rho}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{r}}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\right)\right)^{-1}.

Using now that for positive reals
a,b>0
, if
a\leq\min(1,b^{2})
, then
\left(\log\frac{b}{a}\right)^{-1}\leq 2\left(\log\frac{1}{a}\right)^{-1}
, we see that if in addition

\frac{\left\|u-\bar{u}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}}{L\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}}\leq\min\left(1,\left(\frac{\left\|\bar{\rho}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}}}{\left\|\bar{\rho}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{r}}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\right)^{2}\right),

then

\left(\log\left(\frac{L\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}}{\left\|u-\bar{u}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}}\right)+\log\left(\frac{\left\|\bar{\rho}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}}}{\left\|\bar{\rho}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{r}}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\right)\right)^{-1}\leq 2\left(\log\left(\frac{L\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}}{\left\|u-\bar{u}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}}\right)\right)^{-1},

which gives the result.
∎

Proposition 3.6

(Convergence of the vanishing diffusion limit)
.

Consider a weak solution
\rho(x,t):\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
, with
\rho(x,t)\in L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}
, and initial datum
\rho_{0}(x)\in L_{x}^{q}
, to the transport equation

\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}(x,t)+\nabla\cdot(u(x,t)\rho(x,t))=0,

along a divergence-free vector field
u(x,t):\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
\nabla u(x,t)\in L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}
, for
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{r}
, with
1<p,q\leq\infty
,
1\leq r<\infty
.

For any
\nu>0
let
\rho_{\nu}(x,t)\in L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}
be a weak solution to the transport-diffusion equation

\frac{\partial\rho_{\nu}}{\partial t}(x,t)+\nabla\cdot(u(x,t)\rho_{\nu}(x,t))=\nu\Delta\rho_{\nu},

with the same initial datum
\rho_{0}(x)\in L_{x}^{q}
.

Fix a mollifier/frequency cutoff
\varphi(x)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
as in Definition
2.3
.

We have the following weak convergence of
\rho_{\nu}(x,t)\xrightharpoonup{}\rho(x,t)
as
\nu\to 0
:

For any
\delta>0
, if
\nu T\leq\delta^{2}\frac{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}}{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}
, then

\left\|(\rho-\rho_{\nu})*\varphi_{\delta}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{r}}\leq C_{p,q}\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\left(\log\left(\frac{\delta^{2}}{\nu T}\left(\frac{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}}{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\right)\right)\right)^{-1},

where the constant
C_{p,q}>0
depends only on the parameters
1<p,q\leq\infty
, the choice of mollifier
\varphi(x)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
, and the dimension
d\geq 1
.

Corollary 3.7
.

For some length scale
0<L\leq\infty
define the norm
W_{x}^{-1,r}
as in Definition
3.1
.

If

\nu T\leq L^{2}\min\left(1,\bigg(\frac{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}}{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\bigg)^{6}\right),

then

\left\|\rho-\rho_{\nu}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}W_{x}^{-1,r}}\leq C_{p,q}L\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\left(\log\left(\frac{L^{2}}{\nu T}\right)\right)^{-1},

where the constant
C_{p,q}>0
depends only on the parameters
1<p,q\leq\infty
and the dimension
d\geq 1
.

Proof.

Fix a mollifier
\varphi(x)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
satisfying the frequency-cutoff (
9
).

We begin by writing
(\rho-\rho_{\nu})(x,t)\in L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}
as a weak solution with zero initial datum to the following distributional forced transport equation along
u(x,t)
:

\frac{\partial(\rho-\rho_{\nu})}{\partial t}(x,t)+\nabla\cdot(u(x,t)(\rho-\rho_{\nu})(x,t))=f(x,t),

with the distributional force given by

f(x,t)=-\nu\Delta\rho_{\nu}(x,t).

By Theorem
2.4
, for any
\kappa>0
, we have the following quantitative bound:

For

\delta^{\prime}=\frac{\delta}{4}\exp\left(-\frac{C_{p,q}}{\kappa}\left\|\rho-\rho_{\nu}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\right),

then

\displaystyle\left\|(\rho-\rho_{\nu})*\varphi_{\delta}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{r}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\kappa+A\left\|f*\varphi_{\delta^{\prime}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{r}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\kappa+\nu A\left\|\Delta\varphi_{\delta^{\prime}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{1}}\left\|\rho_{\nu}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{r}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\kappa+\nu A\delta^{\prime-2}\left\|\nabla\varphi\right\|_{L_{x}^{1}}T\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\kappa+\nu\frac{16A}{\delta^{2}}\exp\left(2\frac{C_{p,q}}{\kappa}\left\|\rho-\rho_{\nu}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\right)\left\|\nabla\varphi\right\|_{L_{x}^{1}}T\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\kappa+\nu A^{\prime}\delta^{-2}T\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}\exp\left(\frac{C_{p,q}^{\prime}}{\kappa}\left\|\rho-\rho_{\nu}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\kappa+\nu A^{\prime}\delta^{-2}T\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}\exp\left(\frac{C_{p,q}^{\prime}}{\kappa}\left(\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}}+\left\|\rho_{\nu}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}}\right)\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\kappa+\nu A^{\prime}\delta^{-2}T\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}\exp\left(\frac{C_{p,q}^{\prime}}{\kappa}2\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\right),

(22)

where the constants
C_{p,q},C_{p,q}^{\prime}>0
depends only on the parameters
1<p,q\leq\infty
, the choice of mollifier
\varphi(x)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
, and the dimension
d\geq 1
, while
A,A^{\prime}>0
depend only on the choice of mollifier
\varphi(x)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
and the dimension
d\geq 1
.

Let

\displaystyle\epsilon=\delta^{2}\frac{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}}{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}},

\displaystyle\gamma=2\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}},

and rewrite (
22
) as, for any
\kappa>0
,

\left\|(\rho-\rho_{\nu})*\varphi_{\delta}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{r}}\leq\kappa+\nu TA^{\prime}\frac{\gamma}{2\epsilon}\exp\left(C_{p,q}^{\prime}{\frac{\gamma}{\kappa}}\right).

We now crudely optimise over
\kappa>0
:

For
\nu T\leq\epsilon
, let

\kappa=\frac{2C_{p,q}^{\prime}\gamma}{-\log\nu T+\log\epsilon},

then, using
x^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\frac{2e^{-1}}{-\log x}
for
x\in(0,1]
,

\displaystyle\kappa+\nu TA^{\prime}\frac{\gamma}{2\epsilon}\exp\left(C_{p,q}^{\prime}\frac{\gamma}{\kappa}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{2C_{p,q}^{\prime}\gamma}{-\log\nu T+\log\epsilon}+\nu TA^{\prime}\frac{\gamma}{2\epsilon}\left(\frac{\epsilon}{\nu T}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{2C_{p,q}^{\prime}\gamma}{-\log\nu T+\log\epsilon}+\frac{A^{\prime}e^{-1}\gamma}{-\log\nu T+\log\epsilon}.

This bound concludes the proof of Theorem
3.6
. We now turn to the proof of Corollary
3.7
.

For some length scale
0<L\leq\infty
define the norm
W_{x}^{-1,r}
as in Definition
3.1
. As in the proof of Corollary
3.5
, we may bound the weak norm by

\left\|f\right\|_{W_{x}^{-1,r}}\lesssim L\left\|f*\varphi_{\delta}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}+\delta\left\|f\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}.

Applying this to
(\rho-\rho_{\nu})(x,t)
, and the previous bounds, gives

\displaystyle\left\|\rho-\rho_{\nu}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}W_{x}^{-1,r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim L\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\left(\log\left(\frac{\epsilon}{\nu T}\right)\right)^{-1}+\delta\left\|\rho-\rho_{\nu}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim L\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\left(\log\left(\frac{\epsilon}{\nu T}\right)\right)^{-1}+\delta\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}},

(23)

where
\epsilon>0
depends on
\delta>0
.

We now crudely optimise over
\delta>0
:

For
\nu T\leq L^{2}\left(\frac{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}}{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\right)^{3}
let

\delta=L\frac{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}}{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\left(\log\left(\frac{L^{2}}{\nu T}\left(\frac{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}}{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\right)^{3}\right)\right)^{-1},

and so, using that
-\log\log x\geq-e^{-1}\log x
for
x\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\log\left(\frac{\epsilon}{\nu T}\right)

\displaystyle=\log\left(\frac{L^{2}}{\nu T}\left(\frac{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}}{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\right)^{3}\right)-2\log\log\left(\frac{L^{2}}{\nu T}\left(\frac{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}}{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\right)^{3}\right)

\displaystyle\geq(1-2e^{-1})\log\left(\frac{L^{2}}{\nu T}\left(\frac{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}}{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\right)^{3}\right),

Therefore (
23
) becomes

\left\|\rho-\rho_{\nu}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}W_{x}^{-1,r}}\lesssim L\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\left(\log\left(\frac{L^{2}}{\nu T}\right)+\log\left(\frac{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}}{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\right)^{3}\right)^{-1}.

Using now that for positive reals
a,b>0
, if
a\leq\min(1,b^{2})
, then
\left(\log\frac{b}{a}\right)^{-1}\leq 2\left(\log\frac{1}{a}\right)^{-1}
, then if in addition

\frac{\nu T}{L^{2}}\leq\min\left(1,\left(\frac{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}}{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\right)^{6}\right),

then

\left(\log\left(\frac{L^{2}}{\nu T}\right)+\log\left(\frac{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}}{\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}}\right)^{3}\right)^{-1}\leq 2\left(\log\left(\frac{L^{2}}{\nu T}\right)\right)^{-1},

which gives the result.
∎

Lastly, we apply Lemma
2.1
to show that the passive scalar propagates regularity corresponding to a logarithm of a derivative, also studied in
[
18
,
8
,
19
]
:

Proposition 3.8

(Propagation of regularity)
.

Consider a weak solution
\rho(x,t):\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
, with
\rho(x,t)\in L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}
, and initial datum
\rho_{0}(x)\in L_{x}^{q}
, to the transport equation

\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}(x,t)+\nabla\cdot(u(x,t)\rho(x,t))=0,

along a divergence-free vector field
u(x,t):\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
\nabla u(x,t)\in L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{p}
, for
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{r}
, with
1<p,q\leq\infty
,
1\leq r<\infty
.

Then for the convolution kernel
K(x)=\log(1-\Delta)
, given by its Fourier transform

\hat{K}(\xi)=\log(1+\left|\xi\right|^{2}),

one has the propagation of

\frac{d}{dt}\left\|(\rho*K)(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}\leq C_{p,q}\left\|\rho(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}\left\|\nabla u(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}},

where the constant
C_{p,q}>0
depends only on the parameters
1<p,q\leq\infty
and the dimension
d\geq 1
, and where
\left\|K\right\|_{CZ}
refers to the Calderon-Zygmund norm, defined by

\displaystyle\left|K(x)\right|\leq|x|^{-d}\left\|K\right\|_{CZ},

\displaystyle\left|\nabla K(x)\right|\leq|x|^{-d-1}\left\|K\right\|_{CZ},

\displaystyle|\hat{K}(\xi)|\leq\left\|K\right\|_{CZ},

for all
x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
\hat{K}(\xi):\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
denotes the Fourier transform.

Proof.

We apply Lemma
2.1
with the kernel
K(x)=\log(1-\Delta)
, whose Fourier transform
\hat{K}(\xi):\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is given by
\hat{K}(\xi)=\log\left(1+|\xi|^{2}\right)
. It remains to show that the kernel

K_{i,j}(x)=\frac{\partial(x_{j}K)}{\partial x_{i}}(x),

,
whose Fourier transform is given by

\displaystyle\hat{K}_{i,j}(\xi)

\displaystyle=-\xi_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi_{j}}\hat{K}(\xi)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\xi_{i}\xi_{j}}{1+|\xi|^{2}}

has finite Calderon-Zygmund norm for all
i,j=1,\dots,d
.

To this end, we apply the following version of the Mikhlin-multiplier theorem:
[
20
]
*Theorem 8.2. We must show that

|\xi|^{n}|\nabla^{n}\hat{K}_{i,j}(\xi)|

is bounded for all
n=0,\dots,d+2
. Indeed, we have, by direct calculation, the bound

\displaystyle|\nabla^{n}\hat{K}_{i,j}(\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq C_{n}\frac{|\xi|^{n+2}}{(1+|\xi|^{2})^{n+1}},

for all
n\geq 0
, and some
C_{n}>0
.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

We remark that the results of this section do not extend beyond the DiPerna-Lions regime
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}\leq 1
. Outside this class of weak solutions
\rho(x,t)\in L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{q}
there are counterexamples to uniqueness (and thus any quantitative estimates), see
[
10
]
. We remark, however, that there is still a unique regular Lagrangian flow in this setting
[
2
]
, and whether solutions selected by this flow satisfy quantitative estimates analogous to these results is an open question.

4
Estimates on the DiPerna-Lions commutator

Finally, we discuss how some of the results in this paper are related to decay rates for the standard DiPerna-Lions commutator in
[
13
]
.

The DiPerna-Lions commutator controls the transport error of the mollification
(\rho*\varphi_{\delta})(x)
:

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\rho*\varphi_{\delta})(x,t)+u(x,t)\cdot\nabla(\rho*\varphi_{\delta})(x,t)=R_{\delta}(u,\rho)(x,t),

where we have kept the dependence of the commutator
R_{\delta}(u,\rho)(x,t)
on the passive scalar
\rho(x,t)
and the vector field
u(x,t)
. The DiPerna-Lions commutator is a special case of the commutator
R(x,t)
in Lemma
2.1
by taking the kernel
K(x)=\varphi_{\delta}(x)
. Applying Lemma
2.1
as in the proof of Theorem
2.4
, the harmonic estimate in the proof of Theorem
2.4
can be rephrased as the following integral estimate on the decay of the DiPerna-Lions commutator. Suppressing the time-dependence for readability:

Proposition 4.1

(Integral estimate on the DiPerna-Lions commutator)
.

Consider a passive scalar
\rho(x):\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, with
\rho(x)\in L_{x}^{q}
, and a divergence-free vector field
u(x):\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
\nabla u(x)\in L_{x}^{p}
, for
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{r}
, with
1<p,q\leq\infty
,
1\leq r<\infty
.

Let
\varphi(x)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
(a Schwartz function) be a standard mollifier and denote by

R_{\delta}(u,\rho)(x)=u(x)\cdot\nabla(\rho*\varphi_{\delta})(x)-\nabla\cdot((u\rho)*\varphi_{\delta})(x),

the DiPerna-Lions commutator, as in
[
13
]
.

Then for any
0<\delta_{1}<{\delta_{2}}
,

\left\|\int_{\delta_{1}}^{\delta_{2}}R_{\delta}(u,\rho)(x)\;\frac{d\delta}{\delta}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}\leq C_{p,q}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}},

where the constant
C_{p,q}>0
depends only on the parameters
1<p,q\leq\infty
, the choice of mollifier
\varphi(x)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
, and the dimension
d\geq 1
.

Proof.

Throughout,
\lesssim
will denote less than or equal to up to a constant depending only on
p,p^{\prime}
, the dimension
d
, and the mollifier
\varphi(x)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
(and in particular not on
\delta_{1},{\delta_{2}}
).

We first rewrite

\displaystyle\int_{\delta_{1}}^{\delta_{2}}R_{\delta}(u,\rho)(x)\;\frac{d\delta}{\delta}

\displaystyle=\int_{\delta_{1}}^{\delta_{2}}u(x)\cdot(\rho*\nabla\varphi_{\delta})(x)-\nabla\cdot((u\rho)*\varphi_{\delta})(x)\;\frac{d\delta}{\delta}

\displaystyle=u(x)\cdot(\rho*K)(x)-\nabla\cdot((u\rho)*K)(x),

in terms of the kernel

K(x)=\int_{\delta_{1}}^{\delta_{2}}\varphi_{\delta}(x)\frac{d\delta}{\delta}.

We now apply Lemma
2.1
as in the proof of Theorem
2.4
to obtain the estimate

\left\|\int_{\delta_{1}}^{\delta_{2}}R_{\delta}(u,\rho)(x)\;\frac{d\delta}{\delta}\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}\leq C_{p,q}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}}\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\left\|\frac{\partial(x_{j}K)}{\partial x_{i}}\right\|_{CZ}\right),

(24)

where the constant
C_{p,q}>0
depends only on the parameters
1<p,q\leq\infty
and the dimension
d\geq 1
, and where
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{CZ}
refers to the Calderon-Zygmund norm, as defined in Lemma
2.1
.

Now let

\displaystyle K_{i,j}^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial(x_{j}K)}{\partial x_{i}}(x)=\int_{\delta_{1}}^{\delta_{2}}\frac{\partial(x_{j}\varphi)}{\partial x_{i}}\left(\frac{x}{\delta}\right)\;\frac{d\delta}{\delta^{d+1}}.

As in the proof of Theorem
2.4
, we have the following Calderon-Zygmund estimates on
K_{i,j}^{\prime}(x)
:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |K_{i,j}^{\prime}(x)|&\leq\left\|(1+|x|^{d+1})\frac{\partial(x_{j}\varphi)}{\partial x_{i}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\left(\int_{|x|}^{\infty}\delta^{-d-1}d\delta+\int_{0}^{|x|}|x|^{-d-1}d\delta\right)\\
&\lesssim|x|^{-d},\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left|\frac{\partial K_{i,j}^{\prime}}{\partial x_{k}}(x)\right|&=\left|\int_{\delta_{1}}^{\delta_{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}(x_{j}\varphi)}{\partial x_{k}\partial x_{i}}\left(\frac{x}{\delta}\right)\;\frac{d\delta}{\delta^{d+2}}\right|\\
&\leq\left\|(1+|x|^{d+2})\frac{\partial^{2}(x_{j}\varphi)}{\partial x_{k}\partial x_{i}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\left(\int_{|x|}^{\infty}\delta^{-d-2}d\delta+\int_{0}^{|x|}|x|^{-d-2}d\delta\right)\\
&\lesssim|x|^{-d-1},\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |\hat{K}_{i,j}^{\prime}(\xi)|&=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{i\xi\cdot x}\int_{\delta_{1}}^{\delta_{2}}\frac{\partial(x_{j}\varphi)}{\partial x_{i}}\left(\frac{x}{\delta}\right)\;\frac{d\delta}{\delta^{d+1}}\;dx\right|\\
&=\left|\int_{\delta_{1}}^{\delta_{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{i\delta\xi\cdot h}\frac{\partial(x_{j}\varphi)}{\partial x_{i}}(h)\;dh\right)\frac{d\delta}{\delta}\right|\\
&=\left|\int_{\delta_{1}}^{\delta_{2}}-i\delta\xi_{i}\frac{\partial\hat{\varphi}}{\partial\xi_{j}}(\delta\xi)\;\frac{d\delta}{\delta}\right|\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}|\xi_{i}|\left|\frac{\partial\hat{\varphi}}{\partial\xi_{j}}\right|(\delta\xi)\;d\delta\\
&\leq\left\|(1+|\xi_{i}|^{2})\frac{\partial\hat{\varphi}}{\partial\xi_{j}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\left(\int_{0}^{|\xi_{i}|^{-1}}|\xi_{i}|\;d\delta+\int_{|\xi_{i}|^{-1}}^{\infty}|\xi_{i}|^{-1}\delta^{-2}\;d\delta\right)\\
&\lesssim 1.\end{aligned}

where, in particular, none of the bounds depends on
0<\delta_{1}<\delta_{2}
. That is

\left\|K_{i,j}^{\prime}\right\|_{CZ}\lesssim 1,

which, when combined with (
24
), is the statement of the proposition.
∎

This is the same harmonic analysis used in the proof of the main result of this paper, Theorem
2.4
. Compared with the analysis of DiPerna-Lions, in
[
13
]
, the authors show that

Lemma 4.2

(Decay of the DiPerna-Lions commutator
[
13
]
)
.

Let
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
with
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{r}
with
1\leq r<\infty
. Let
u(x)\in W_{x}^{1,p}
be divergence-free, and
\rho(x)\in L_{x}^{q}
then

R_{\delta}(u,\rho)(x)\xrightarrow{\delta\to 0}0\text{ in }L_{x}^{r},

Proof.

See
[
13
]
.
∎

Quantitative estimates for passive scalar transport then follow from quantifying this decay. However, in the original proof
[
13
]
, this decay is not uniform in the norms of
\nabla u(x)\in L_{x}^{p}
or
\rho(x)\in L_{x}^{q}
. Proposition
4.1
partly addresses this problem by giving a uniform integral estimate on the decay of the commutator, and the main result of this paper, Theorem
2.4
, is proved similarly. We now ask if we can improve this to quantitative estimates on the decay of the
norm
of the commutator,
\left\|R_{\delta}(u,\rho)\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}\to 0
. We start by observing, by a compactness argument, that there must exist some uniform decay of the norm:

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
with
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
. Let
u(x)\in W_{x}^{1,p}
be divergence-free, and
\rho(x)\in L_{x}^{q}
.

If
p,q>1
, then for any
\epsilon>0
, for any
\bar{\delta}>0
, there exists
\delta_{\epsilon}>0
independent of
u(x)
and
\rho(x)
such that

\inf_{\delta\in[\delta_{\epsilon},\bar{\delta}]}\left\|R_{\delta}(u,\rho)\right\|_{L_{x}^{1}}\leq\epsilon\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}.

Proof.

Assume for the sake of contradiction that this is not the case. Then there exists a sequence
\nabla u_{n}(x)\in L_{x}^{p}
,
\rho_{n}(x)\in L_{x}^{q}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n>\bar{\delta}^{-1}
, so that

\inf_{\delta\in[\frac{1}{n},\bar{\delta}]}\left\|R_{\delta}(u_{n},\rho_{n})\right\|_{L_{x}^{1}}>\epsilon\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}.

By scaling we may take
\left\|\nabla u_{n}\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}},\left\|\rho_{n}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}}=1
in the above. Since
p,q>1
, we may take a weakly-* converging subsequence. Without loss of generality, we take this to be the original sequence,

\displaystyle\nabla u_{n}(x)\xrightharpoonup{n\to\infty}\nabla u(x)\in L_{x}^{p},

\displaystyle\rho_{n}(x)\xrightharpoonup{n\to\infty}\rho(x)\in L_{x}^{q},

and by the Rellich-Kondrachov theorem
[
14
]
*Section 5.7, we have the strong convergence

u_{n}(x)\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}u(x)\in L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{p},

and so strong convergence of

R_{\delta}(u_{n},\rho_{n})(x)\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}R_{\delta}(u,\rho)(x)\in L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}.

(25)

Since we assumed that

\inf_{\delta\in[\frac{1}{n},\bar{\delta}]}\left\|R_{\delta}(u_{n},\rho_{n})\right\|_{L_{x}^{1}}>\epsilon,

then by (
25
) also

\inf_{\delta\in[\frac{1}{n},\bar{\delta}]}\left\|R_{\delta}(u,\rho)\right\|_{L_{x}^{1}}>\epsilon,

but this contradicts the decay of Theorem
4.2
.
∎

Our goal is to quantify this compactness argument. To this end we introduce the homogeneous Besov space
\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}})
as the Schwartz distributions
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}})
for which the following homogeneous Besov norm is finite,

\left\|f\right\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}})}=\left(\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}2^{ns}\left\|\psi_{n}*f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}})}^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

where
\psi_{n}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
is a Littlewood-Paley decomposition, given by the Fourier transform

\hat{\psi}_{n}(\xi)=\chi(2^{-n}\xi),

for a fixed choice of
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
satisfying
\mathrm{supp}\;\chi=\{\xi:\frac{1}{2}\leq|\xi|\leq 2\}
,
\chi(\xi)>0
if
\frac{1}{2}<|\xi|<2
, and
\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\chi(2^{-n}\xi)=1
for
\xi\neq 0
, as in
[
5
]
*Chapter 6.

Recall the continuous embedding
L_{x}^{p}\hookrightarrow\dot{B}_{p,\mathrm{max}({p,2})}^{0}
for all
1<p\leq\infty
, see
[
4
]
*Theorem 2.40, 2.41.

We prove the following integral estimate on the decay of the norm of the DiPerna-Lions commutator, improving on the integral estimate in Proposition
4.1
at the expense of the decay rate. We omit the time dependence for readability:

Theorem 4.4

(Integral estimate on the norm of the DiPerna-Lions commutator)
.

Let
p,p^{\prime},q,r\in[1,\infty]
with
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{r}
.

Let
\rho(x):\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
with
\rho(x)\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})\cap\dot{B}^{0}_{p^{\prime},q}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
, and
u(x):\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be divergence-free with
\nabla u(x)\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap\dot{B}^{0}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Let
\varphi(x)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
(a Schwartz function) be a standard mollifier. Denote the commutator by

R_{\delta}(u,\rho)(x)=u(x)\cdot\nabla(\rho*\varphi_{\delta})(x)-\nabla\cdot((u\rho)*\varphi_{\delta})(x).

Then

\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\|r_{\delta}(u,\rho)\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}^{q}\;\frac{d\delta}{\delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C\left(\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{p,q}}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{x}^{p^{\prime}}}+\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}}\left\|\rho\right\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{p^{\prime},q}}\right),

where the constant
C>0
depends only on the dimension
d\geq 1
, and the following norm of the mollifier
\varphi(x)
:
\left\|(1+|x|^{2})\nabla\varphi\right\|_{L_{x}^{1}}+\left\||x|\nabla^{2}\varphi\right\|_{L_{x}^{1}}
. In particular, not on the parameters
1\leq p,p^{\prime},q,r\leq\infty
.

Proof.

Throughout
\lesssim
will denote less than or equal to up to a constant depending only on the dimension
d
, and the norm of the mollifier:
\left\|(1+|x|^{2})\nabla\varphi\right\|_{L_{x}^{1}}+\left\||x|\nabla^{2}\varphi\right\|_{L_{x}^{1}}
, and in particular not on
\delta>0
.

We first rewrite

\displaystyle R_{\delta}(u,\rho)(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(y)(u(x)-u(y))\cdot\nabla\varphi_{\delta}(x-y)\;dy,

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(x-\delta h)\left(\frac{u(x)-u(x-\delta h)}{\delta}\right)\cdot\nabla\varphi(h)\;dh,

and so by testing against
\phi(x)\in L_{x}^{r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
for
\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}=1
we see immediately that

\left\|R_{\delta}(u,\rho)\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\delta}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{x}^{p^{\prime}}}\left\|u\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}}.

(26)

Next, we consider the following approximation

\displaystyle R_{\delta}(u,\rho)(x)-\sum^{d}_{i,j=1}\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(x-\delta h)h_{j}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial h_{i}}\;dh

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(x-\delta h)\sum^{d}_{i=1}\left(\frac{u_{i}(x)-u_{i}(x-\delta h)}{\delta}-\sum^{d}_{j=1}h_{j}\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}(x)\right)\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial h_{i}}\;dh

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(x-\delta h)\sum^{d}_{i,j=1}\left(h_{j}\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}(x-t\delta h)-h_{j}\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}(x)\right)\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial h_{i}}\;dh\;dt

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(x-\delta h)\sum^{d}_{i,j,k=1}\left(-t\delta h_{j}h_{k}\frac{\partial^{2}u_{i}}{\partial x_{j}\partial x_{k}}(x-st\delta h)\right)\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial h_{i}}\;dh\;dt\;ds,

and so by testing against
\phi(x)\in L_{x}^{r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
we have

\left\|R_{\delta}(u,\rho)-\sum^{d}_{i,j=1}\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(x-\delta h)h_{j}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial h_{i}}\;dh\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}\lesssim\delta\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{x}^{p^{\prime}}}\left\|\nabla^{2}u\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}}.

(27)

Now notice, since
\nabla\cdot u(x)=0
,

\displaystyle\sum^{d}_{i,j=1}\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(x-\delta h)h_{j}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial h_{i}}\;dh

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum^{d}_{i,j=1}\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(x-\delta h)\frac{\partial(h_{j}\varphi)}{\partial h_{i}}\;dh

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum^{d}_{i,j=1}\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\delta\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial x_{i}}(x-\delta h)h_{j}\varphi\;dh,

and so by testing against
\phi(x)\in L_{x}^{r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
we have the bound

\left\|\sum^{d}_{i,j=1}\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(x-\delta h)h_{j}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial h_{i}}\;dh\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}\lesssim\delta\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}}\left\|\nabla\rho\right\|_{L_{x}^{p^{\prime}}}.

(28)

Finally, assume
\rho(x)=-\Delta g(x)
for some
g(x)\in W_{x}^{1,p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
, then

\displaystyle\sum^{d}_{i,j=1}\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(x-\delta h)\frac{\partial(h_{j}\varphi)}{\partial h_{i}}\;dh

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum^{d}_{i,j,k=1}\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\partial^{2}g}{\partial x_{k}\partial x_{k}}(x-\delta h)\frac{\partial(h_{j}\varphi)}{\partial h_{i}}\;dh

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum^{d}_{i,j,k=1}\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}-\frac{1}{\delta}\frac{\partial}{\partial h_{k}}\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{k}}(x-\delta h)\frac{\partial(h_{j}\varphi)}{\partial h_{i}}\;dh

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum^{d}_{i,j,k=1}\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{\delta}\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{k}}(x-\delta h)\frac{\partial^{2}(h_{j}\varphi)}{\partial h_{k}\partial h_{i}}\;dh,

and so

\left\|\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(x-\delta h)h_{j}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial h_{i}}\;dh\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\delta}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}}\left\|\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\rho\right\|_{L_{x}^{p^{\prime}}}.

(29)

Putting equations (
26
) to (
29

\bar{u}(x)\in W_{x}^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\bar{\rho}(x)\in W_{x}^{1,p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
such that

,

\displaystyle\left\|R_{\delta}(u,\rho)\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\begin{aligned} &\left\|R_{\delta}(u-\bar{u},\rho)\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}+\left\|R_{\delta}(\bar{u},\rho)-\sum^{d}_{i,j=1}\frac{\partial\bar{u}_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(x-\delta h)h_{j}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial h_{i}}\;dh\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}\\
&+\left\|\sum^{d}_{i,j=1}\frac{\partial\bar{u}_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\rho-\bar{\rho})(x-\delta h)h_{j}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial h_{i}}\;dh\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}\\
&+\left\|\sum^{d}_{i,j=1}\frac{\partial\bar{u}_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bar{\rho}(x-\delta h)h_{j}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial h_{i}}\;dh\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\begin{aligned} &\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{x}^{p^{\prime}}}\left(\frac{1}{\delta}\left\|u-\bar{u}\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}}+\delta\left\|\nabla^{2}\bar{u}\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}}\right)\\
&+\left\|\nabla\bar{u}\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}}\left(\frac{1}{\delta}\left\|\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}(\rho-\bar{\rho})\right\|_{L_{x}^{p^{\prime}}}+\delta\left\|\nabla\bar{\rho}\right\|_{L_{x}^{p^{\prime}}}\right).\end{aligned}

(30)

We now need some standard real interpolation inequalities. For this we recall the definition of

as the Schwartz distributions
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}

By

(\dot{B}^{0}_{p,1},\dot{B}^{2}_{p,1})_{\frac{1}{2},q}\text{ and }(\dot{B}^{-1}_{p^{\prime},1},\dot{B}^{1}_{p^{\prime},1})_{\frac{1}{2},q},

\displaystyle\left|\rho\right|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{p^{\prime},q}}=\bigg(\int_{0}^{+\infty}\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bar{\rho}\in\dot{B}^{1}_{p^{\prime},1}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})\\

there exists a universal constant

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\|r_{\delta}(u,\rho)\right\|_{L_{x}^{r}}^{q}\;\frac{d\delta}{\delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\begin{aligned} &\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{x}^{p^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{\delta}\left\|u-\bar{u}_{\delta}\right\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{p,1}}+\delta\left\|\bar{u}_{\delta}\right\|_{\dot{B}^{2}_{p,1}}\right)^{q}\;\frac{d\delta}{\delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\\

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{x}^{p^{\prime}}}\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{B}^{1}_{p,q}}+\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}}\left\|\rho\right\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{p^{\prime},q}},

where we have used

Corollary 4.6
.

Let

\rho(x,t)\in L_{t}^{p^{\prime}}L_{x}^{q^{\prime}}

R_{\delta}(u,\rho)(x,t)=u(x,t)\cdot\nabla(\rho*\varphi_{\delta})(x,t)-\nabla\cdot((u\rho)*\varphi_{\delta})(x,t).

If

\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\|r_{\delta}(u,\rho)\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{r}}^{\mathrm{max}(q,q^{\prime})}\;\frac{d\delta}{\delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{{\mathrm{max}(q,q^{\prime})}}}\leq C_{q,q^{\prime}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L_{t}^{p}L_{x}^{q}}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L_{t}^{p^{\prime}}L_{x}^{q^{\prime}}},

where the constant
C_{q,q^{\prime}}>0
depends only on the parameters
1<q,q^{\prime}<\infty
, the dimension

, and the following norm of the mollifier in
\varphi(x)

\left\|(1+|x|^{2})\nabla\varphi\right\|_{L_{x}^{1}}+\left\||x|\nabla^{2}\varphi\right\|_{L_{x}^{1}}
.

Finally, we show that the integral estimates given in this section are optimal, in the sense that a non-integral estimate on the decay of the commutator does not exist (see point 1 below) and that the rate given in Proposition
4.4

Proposition 4.7

(Sharpness of decay of the DiPerna-Lions commutator)
.

Let
u(x)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2})
be the time-independent linear shear

u(x)=(x_{2},0)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

For any compactly supported mollifier
\varphi(x)\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R})
, denote the commutator by

R_{\delta}(u,\rho)(x)=u(x)\cdot\nabla(\rho*\varphi_{\delta})(x)-\nabla\cdot((u\rho)*\varphi_{\delta})(x).

Then

1.

for any cutoff
\chi(x)\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R})
with
\chi(x)=1
whenever
|x|\leq 1
,

\liminf_{\delta\to 0}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho(x)\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R})\\
|\rho(x)|\leq\chi(x)\end{subarray}}\left\|R_{\delta}(u,\rho)\right\|_{L_{x}^{1}}>0.

2.

for some particular choice of
\bar{\rho}(x):\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
which is in
L_{x}^{p}
for
p<\infty
, and supported on
|x|\leq 1
, then for any
1\leq q<2

\left(\int_{0}^{1}\left\|R_{\delta}(u,\bar{\rho})\right\|_{L_{x}^{1}}^{q}\frac{d\delta}{\delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}=\infty,

Proof.

For any
\rho(x)\in L_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R})
,

\displaystyle R_{\delta}(u,\rho)(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\rho(y)(x_{2}-y_{2})\frac{\partial\varphi_{\delta}}{\partial x_{1}}(x-y)\;dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\rho(x-h)\frac{\partial(h_{2}\varphi_{\delta})}{\partial h_{1}}(h)\;dh,

\displaystyle=(\rho*K_{\delta})(x),

where
K_{\delta}(x)=\delta^{-2}K(\delta^{-1}x)
and
K(x)=\frac{\partial(x_{2}\varphi)}{\partial x_{1}}(x)
.

In particular, the Fourier transforms of
K_{\delta}(x)
and
K(x)
are given by

\displaystyle\hat{K}_{\delta}(\xi)=\hat{K}(\delta\xi),

\displaystyle\hat{K}(\xi)=-\xi_{1}\frac{\partial\hat{\varphi}}{\partial\xi_{2}}.

For now consider any
\rho(x)\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R})
a Schwartz distribution. Then
(\rho*K_{\delta})(x)
will coincide with
R_{\delta}(u,\rho)(x)
if in addition
\rho(x)\in L_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R})
.

We now prove the first statement of the theorem:

Consider the harmonic
\rho(x)=e^{i\delta^{-1}\bar{\xi}\cdot x}
for some
\bar{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
|\hat{K}(\bar{\xi})|>0
, which exists since
\varphi(x)\in L_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R})
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\varphi(x)\;dx=1
. Then
(\rho*K_{\delta})(x)=e^{i\delta^{-1}\bar{\xi}\cdot x}\hat{K}(\bar{\xi})
.

We now cutoff
\rho(x)
so that it lies in
L_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
. Let the mollifier
\varphi(x)
be supported on
|x|\leq R
, then
\varphi_{\delta}(x)
is supported on
|x|\leq\delta R
. Taking a compactly supported cutoff
\chi(x)
with
\chi(x)=1
on
|x|\leq 1
, then
R_{\delta}(u,\chi\rho)(x)=(\rho*K_{\delta})(x)
whenever
|x|\leq 1-\delta R
. Therefore, for all
\delta\leq\frac{1}{2R}
,
\left\|R_{\delta}(u,\chi\rho)\right\|_{L_{x}^{1}}\geq|\hat{K}(\bar{\xi})|
as required.

Note that
(\chi\rho)(x)
is currently complex-valued, but by taking either the real or imaginary part, a similar non-zero bound must hold.

We now prove the second statement of the theorem:

Take some
\bar{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
|\bar{\xi}|=1
, and in addition some
0<c_{1}<c_{2}<2c_{1}
, such that
\inf_{\delta\in[c_{1},c_{2}]}|\hat{K}(\delta\bar{\xi})|=\epsilon>0
, noting that
\hat{K}(\xi)
is continuous if we assume in addition

Let
\rho(x)\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R})
be of the form
\rho(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}e^{i\delta_{n}^{-1}\bar{\xi}\cdot x}
for some
a_{n}\in[0,1]
and
\delta_{n}\in(0,\infty)
.

Now,

\displaystyle\left|\left(e^{i\delta_{n}^{-1}\bar{\xi}\cdot x}*K_{\delta}\right)(x)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\hat{K}\left(\frac{\delta}{\delta_{n}}\bar{\xi}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\min\left\{\frac{\delta}{\delta_{n}}\left\|\frac{\partial\hat{\varphi}}{\partial\xi_{2}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}},\frac{\delta_{n}}{\delta}\left\||\xi|^{2}\frac{\partial\hat{\varphi}}{\partial\xi_{2}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq C\min\left\{\frac{\delta}{\delta_{n}},\frac{\delta_{n}}{\delta}\right\}.

Now let
\delta_{n}=2^{-n^{2}}
. Then for
\frac{\delta}{\delta_{n}}\in[c_{1},c_{2}]\subset[c_{1},2c_{1}]

\displaystyle|(\rho*K_{\delta})(x)|

\displaystyle\geq a_{n}\left|\hat{K}\left(\frac{\delta}{\delta_{n}}\bar{\xi}\right)\right|-C\sum_{1\leq m\leq n-1}|a_{m}|\frac{c_{1}2^{-n^{2}}}{2^{-m^{2}}}-C\sum_{m=n+1}^{\infty}|a_{m}|\frac{2^{-m^{2}}}{c_{2}2^{-n^{2}}}

\displaystyle\geq a_{n}\left|\hat{K}\left(\frac{\delta}{\delta_{n}}\bar{\xi}\right)\right|-c_{1}C2^{2-2n}-c_{2}^{-1}C2^{-2n}

\displaystyle\geq a_{n}\epsilon-C(c_{1}+c_{2}^{-1})4^{1-n}.

Therefore, for any
q\in[1,2)
,

\displaystyle\int_{\delta_{n}c_{1}}^{2\delta_{n}c_{1}}\left(\int_{\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]^{2}}|(\rho*K_{\delta})(x)|\;dx\right)^{q}\frac{d\delta}{\delta}

and so, for
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]^{2}}|(\rho*K_{\delta})(x)|\;dx\right)^{q}\frac{d\delta}{\delta}\geq\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}\right)\epsilon^{q}\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}a_{n}^{q}-\frac{C^{q}(c_{1}+c_{2}^{-1})^{q}}{1-4^{-q}}\log\left(\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}\right).

Now, choose
\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset(0,\infty)
so that
\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}a_{n}^{q}=\infty
but
\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}a_{n}^{2}<\infty
.
Then
\rho(x)\in\dot{B}^{0}_{p,2}\left([-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]^{2};\mathbb{R}\right)
for all

(noting that
\rho(x)
is periodic with period 1, and each

lies in a different Littlewood-Paley block). By the embedding

whenever

[
4

*Theorem 2.40, we have that

We now cutoff

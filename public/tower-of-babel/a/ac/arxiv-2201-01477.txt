Introduction

Chemotaxis
, an extremely universal biological phenomenon, the biased movement of biological individuals (e.g., body cells, bacteria, single cells and multicellular organisms) in response to certain chemical gradients in the surrounding environment. Chemotaxis performs a pivotal role throughout the life cycle of biological individuals. For example, cancer cells secrete some enzymes for more cancerous cells to invade
[
6
]
, the egg sends some chemical substances to attract sperm cells
[
11
]
, and immune cells move to sites of inflammation
[
23
]
. This attracts many researchers to conduct extensive experiments into chemotactic mechanism of bacteria, such as
Escherichia coli
[
2
]
and
Dictyostelium discoideum

[
7
]
. The mathematical modeling of chemotactic processes can be traced back to the pioneering works of Patlak in
[
17
]
and Keller and Segel in
[
13
,
14
]
. Nowadays, Keller-Segel system is widely known as a chemotaxis model and its most prototypical version coupling two parabolic equations is given by

(1.1)

\left.\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}n-\Delta n=-\chi\nabla\cdot(n\nabla c)\\
&\tau\partial_{t}c-\Delta c=-c+n\\
\end{aligned}\right\},

where
n
and
c
respectively denote the density of cells and the concentration of chemical substances.
\chi>0
, chemotactic coefficient, measures the strength of attractive chemotaxis.
\tau>0
represents a relaxation time scale.
There exists a vast mathematical literature on system (
1.1
), mainly focusing on existence, boundedness, large time behaviour of bounded solutions and blow-up mechanisms. One striking feature of equations (
1.1
) consists in its ability to chemotactic collapse under certain circumstances, shown
in many experimental frameworks and in rigorous mathematical framework of singularity formation (the
L^{\infty}
norm of the cell density
n(x,t)
becomes unbounded when
t
reaches a blow-up time). Full of biological significance and mathematical challenges, many literatures are devoted to this popular issue on whether global-in-time bounded solutions exist, and if not, when blow-up phenomenon occurs. Some well-known results corresponding to this problem for full parabolic-parabolic system (
1.1
) are listed as follows: In 1-D, all solutions of equations (
1.1
) are global and bounded, which implies chemotactic aggregation is entirely ruled out
[
10
,
19
]
. In 2-D, one can find a threshold such that if initial mass is lower than the threshold, the solution will be global and bounded in a bounded domain with homogeneous Neumann boundary condition
[
9
,
16
]
and global exist in
\mathbb{R}^{2}

[
3
]
. And there exist initial data such that the corresponding solution blows up either in finite or infinite time, provided initial mass (
L^{1}
norm of bacteria) is larger than a threshold under homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions in a smooth bounded domain
[
12
,
21
]
. In higher dimension
d\geq 3
, smallness of initial mass cannot efficiently prevent chemotactic collapse, this conclusion has been proved by constructing blow-up solutions in finite time if domain is a ball
[
24
,
26
]
and in entire space
\mathbb{R}^{d}

[
28
]
. Instead, smallness condition of initial data
(n_{0},c_{0})
in
L^{\frac{d}{2}}\times\dot{W}^{1,d}
under Neumann boundary conditions in a bounded domain
[
4
]
and in
L^{a}\times\dot{W}^{1,d}
for
\frac{d}{2}<a\leq d
in the whole space
[
5
]
guarantee global existence of solutions.
In many biological phenomena, the cell growth and death play an indelible role in chemotactic movement on small timescales, and the aggregating pattern-dynamics involved in growth of the population was first studied in
[
15
]
. A prototypical choice to reveal this cell kinetics is to add a logistic source

\lambda n-\mu n^{2}
in the evolution equation for
n
in (
1.1
). Here
\lambda\geq 0,\mu>0
. Hence, the fully parabolic version (
\tau>0
) of classical Keller-Segel system with logistic source is given by

(1.2)

\left.\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}n-\Delta n=-\chi\nabla\cdot(n\nabla c)+\lambda n-\mu n^{2}\\
&\tau\partial_{t}c-\Delta c=-c+n\end{aligned}\right\}

As several literatures have revealed, logistic source can suppress chemotactic aggregation in some cases. For instance, solutions of equations (
1.2
) are global-in-time (and bounded for Neumann initial-boundary value problem) for any
\mu>0
in the setting of dimension
d\leq 2

[
18
,
19
,
20
,
29
]
.
Unfortunately, in higher-dimension
d\geq 3
, aggregation vs. logistic damping effect becomes complicated. And results when
d\geq 3
mostly cover cases that
\mu
is greater than some positive constant under the Neumann boundary condition in a
bounded
domain. More precisely, Winkler
[
25
]
firstly rigorously proved that system (
1.2
) possesses a unique global-in-time bounded classical solution if
\mu
is large enough and initial data is sufficiently smooth. Subsequently, a large body of works are devoted to study qualitatively and quantitatively on
\mu
when logistic damping suppresses bacteria aggregation. So far, it is only known that solutions are global and bounded when
\mu>\theta_{0}\chi
for some positive constant
\theta_{0}
in Neumann problems for system (
1.2
)
(\forall\tau>0)
in a
d\,(d\geq 3)
dimensional bounded domain
[
22
,
30
,
31
,
32
]
or in Cauchy problem for system (
1.2
)
(\tau=1)
in
[
25
]
. However, for
\mu\leq\theta_{0}\chi
or even for small
\mu>0
, whether or not blow-up occurs in system (
1.2
) is still an open problem.
As mentioned above, in high dimension (
d\geq 3
), previous results of global boundedness for solutions of system (
1.2
) are corresponding to Neumann problems in a
bounded
domain. And only in the special case
\tau=1
, one so far can derive a similar result for Cauchy problem. Indeed, the function
z:=\frac{\tau}{2}|\nabla c|^{2}+\frac{1}{\chi}n
, as is shown in
[
25
,
27
]
, satisfies a scalar parabolic inequality which leads to the upper bound of both
n
and
\nabla c
. More precisely, from system (
1.2
) we formally infer that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{\,d}}{\mathrm{\,d}t}\left(\frac{\tau}{2}|\nabla c|^{2}+\frac{1}{\chi}n\right)=\nabla c\cdot\nabla\Delta c-|\nabla c|^{2}+\frac{1}{\chi}\Delta n-n\Delta c+\frac{\lambda}{\chi}n-\frac{\mu}{\chi}n^{2}.

Noting the fact that
\nabla c\cdot\nabla\Delta c=\Delta(\frac{1}{2}|\nabla c|^{2})-|D^{2}c|^{2}
and
|\Delta c|^{2}\leq d|D^{2}c|^{2}
, it yields by Young’s inequality that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{\,d}}{\mathrm{\,d}t}\left(\frac{\tau}{2}|\nabla c|^{2}+\frac{1}{\chi}n\right)\leq\Delta\left(\frac{1}{2}|\nabla c|^{2}+\frac{1}{\chi}n\right)-|\nabla c|^{2}-\left(\frac{\mu}{\chi}-\frac{d}{4}\right)n^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{\chi}n.

Here we see that if
\tau=1
and
\mu>\frac{d}{4}\chi
, the function
z
satisfies that

(1.3)

z_{t}-\Delta z+z\leq\frac{(\lambda+1)^{2}}{4\mu\chi-d\chi^{2}}.

Hence, in the special case
\tau=1
, one can take advantage of the above nice inequality and the maximum principle to show global boundedness of solutions when
u_{0}
and
\nabla v_{0}
are bounded for Cauchy problem. Unfortunately, in the general case
\tau\neq 1
, the nice structure (
1.3
) is broken. For a convex bounded domain, Winkler
[
25
]
cleverly made use of the coupling structure of system (
1.2
) and exploited a Moser-type iteration to derive the global boundedness solutions for any
\tau>0
. In problems posed in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
the approaches in
[
25
]
and other relevant literatures focused on bounded domain cease to directly apply. Hence Winkler
[
25
]
raised an interesting question
whether a similar statement remains true when the physical domain is the entire space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, which is still an open problem.
The purpose of this paper is to study the above interesting problem that global boundedness for solutions of (
1.2
) in the whole space. To be more precise, we consider Cauchy problem of the following parabolic-parabolic Keller-Segel system with logistic source:

(1.4)

\left.\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}n-\Delta n=&-\chi\nabla\cdot(n\nabla c)+\lambda n-\mu n^{2}\\
\tau\partial_{t}c-\Delta c=&-c+n\end{aligned}\right\}\quad\text{in}\,\,\,\,\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},

(1.5)

n(0,x)=n_{0}(x),\quad c(0,x)=c_{0}(x)\quad\text{in}\,\,\,\,\mathbb{R}^{d}.

And initial data
(n_{0},c_{0})
is equipped with the conditions that

(1.6)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &n_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,\text{ is nonnegative},\\
&c_{0}\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,\text{ is nonnegative},\end{aligned}\right.

Our main result read as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\chi,\,\tau,\,\mu>0
,
\lambda\geq 0
and
d\geq 1
. Assume the initial data
(n_{0},c_{0})
satisfy condition (
1.6
). Then there exists
\mu_{0}>0
such that for all
\mu\geq\mu_{0}
, problem (
1.4
)-(
1.5
) possesses a unique nonnegative classical global-in-time solution

(n,c)\in C_{\textnormal{w}}([0,\infty);L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\times C_{\textnormal{w}}([0,\infty);W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))

which enjoys global-in-time boundedness, that is, there exits a positive constant
C
only depending on
\chi,\,\tau,\,\lambda,\,\mu,\,d
,
\|n_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
and
\|c_{0}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
such that for all
t>0,

\|n(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|c(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C.

Remark 1.2
.

Compared with Winkler’s result
[
25
]
, we get the same result in Theorem
1.1
for Cauchy problem, but the method is very different.
To overcome difficulty caused by the unbounded domain
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we develop localization in space of solution in order to exploit damping effect of logistic source, which helps in turn to establish global-in-time boundedness of solution. And we believe that our method here can be used the other related model in the unbounded domain with logistic source.

Remark 1.3
.

For
d=1,2
, we can remove the additional condition
\mu\geq\mu_{0}
as in
[
25
]
for the initial boundary value problem.

Let us now sketch the main idea in our paper briefly.
Relevant literatures (e.g.
[
25
,
30
,
31
]
) reveal that the upper bounds on
n
and
\nabla c
for system (
1.2
) in a smooth bounded domain
\Omega
depend on
|\Omega|
. This intuitively shows us that the methods that deal with bounded domain cases fail to directly apply in Cauchy problem. Roughly speaking, in a smooth bounded domain
\Omega
, from E.q.
\eqref{KSL}_{1}
we can infer by the damping effect of the logistic source that

\frac{\mathrm{\,d}}{\mathrm{\,d}t}\int_{\Omega}n\,\mathrm{\,d}x+\int_{\Omega}n\,\mathrm{\,d}x=\int_{\Omega}(\lambda+1)n-\mu n^{2}\,\mathrm{\,d}x\leq\frac{(\lambda+1)^{2}|\Omega|}{4\mu},

where
n\geq 0
, moreover, we get the global-in-time upper bound

\|n(t)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\max\left\{\|n_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)},\,\,\frac{(\lambda+1)^{2}|\Omega|}{4\mu}\right\}.

In the same way, one can obtain the global-in-time upper bound for
\|\nabla c\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
. Furthermore, both uniformly bounds can act as a starter for improving regularity to
L^{p}\times\dot{W}^{1,2p}
by Moser-type iteration, within which for some large
p>1
one enables to infer the uniform boundedness (
L^{\infty}
-norm) by standard
L^{p}
-
L^{q}
estimates of heat semigroup.
Unfortunately, these global-in-time upper bounds strongly depend on the measure of domain
\Omega
. So it does not hold for the unbound domain such as the entire space
\mathbb{R}^{d}.
The main reason is that logistic term can not provide the damping effect in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Without damping effect, we only get the following upper bounds which depend on time
t
(cf. Proposition
2.2
), that is,

\|n(t)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq e^{\lambda t}\|n_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\,\text{ and }\,\,\|\nabla c(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\|\nabla c_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\frac{e^{\lambda t}}{\tau}\|n_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

According to this, we can show the
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-norm of
(n,\nabla c)
enjoys upper bound dependent of
t
by repeating the iteration procedure in
[
25
]
. And then we get the global-in-time well-posedness for Cauchy problem via the standard argument. However the problem concerning global-in-time boundedness of solution is not solved. To do this, we have a new observation that
L^{\infty}
-norm characterizes a “local property” to some extend, that is

L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow L^{p}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for each
1\leq p<\infty
.
This property inspires us to perform the same procedure in
[
25
]
in the uniformly local space framework. Of course, the implementation of the process is not effortless. It takes some new techniques to cope with new difficulties (compared to bounded domain cases) to preform this process. For example, the space localization equation of
n
takes

(\phi n)_{t}-\Delta(\phi n)=-\nabla\cdot\big((\phi n)\nabla c\big)+\lambda(\phi n)-\mu\phi n^{2}-\Delta\phi n-2\nabla n\cdot\nabla\phi+n\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla c,

where
\phi
is a smooth cut-off function. Moreover, it is achieved by integrating in space variable that

\frac{\mathrm{\,d}}{\mathrm{\,d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi n\mathrm{\,d}x=\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi n\mathrm{\,d}x-\mu\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi n^{2}\mathrm{\,d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Delta\phi n\mathrm{\,d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla c\mathrm{\,d}x.

Since lack of regularity of
\nabla c
, it seems impossible to establish the natural estimate of
\|n\|_{L^{1}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
independently, which is cornerstone of “a priori” estimates in previous results. To accomplish this task, by taking advantage of coupling structure, a new combination of
L^{1}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-norm of
n
and
L^{2}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-norm of
\nabla c
is applied, similar to (
1.3
). As a result, it reduces redundant items and close the estimates by choosing a appropriate test function.

The rest of paper is organized as follows. Section 2 is devoted to proving the local-in-time solution. In section 3, we study the global-in-time well-posedness and boundedness of solution to Cauchy problem.

Notation.

\mathbb{R}^{+}=(0,\infty)
and
C
stands for a ”generic” constant which may changes from line to line. For
p,q\in[1,\infty]

and
\|\cdot\|_{L_{t}^{q}L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

For

\displaystyle\|f\|_{p,\lambda}:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\|f\|_{L^{p}\left(B_{\lambda}(x)\right)}\right)=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{|x-y|<\lambda}|f(y)|^{p}\mathrm{~d}y\right)^{1/p},

\displaystyle L_{\textnormal{uloc}}^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right):=\left\{f\in L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right);\|f\|_{p,1}<\infty\right\},\quad\|f\|_{L_{\textnormal{uloc}}^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}:=\|f\|_{p,1}.

Finally,
\mathcal{D}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
is a space of smooth compactly supported functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and space

is the general Sobolev spaces with
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
s\geq 0
.

2.
Local-in-time well-posedness for Cauchy problem

In this section, we are going to show local-in-time existence and uniqueness of the classical solution to system (
1.4
)-(
1.5
). Let us firstly give the statement concerning local-in-time well-posedness.

Theorem 2.1

(Local-in-time well-posedness)
.

Let
\chi,\,\tau,\,\mu>0
,
\lambda\geq 0,

d\geq 1
and the initial data
(n_{0},c_{0})
satisfy

(2.7)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &n_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,\text{ is nonnegative},\\
&c_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,\text{ is nonnegative}.\end{aligned}\right.

Then there exist a maximal
T_{\max}\in(0,\infty]
and a uniquely nonnegative classical solution
(n,c)
to system (
1.4
)-(
1.5
) satisfying

\displaystyle n\in C^{0}([0,T_{\max});L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\cap C^{2,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T_{\max})),

\displaystyle c\in C^{0}([0,T_{\max});H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\cap C^{2,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T_{\max})).

Moreover, if
T_{\max}<\infty
, then

(2.8)

\lim_{T\to T_{\textnormal{max}}-}\left(\|n(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|c(\cdot,t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)=\infty.

We will proceed to prove it step by step by using the standard argument as used in
[
28
]
.

Step 1: Existence
.   According to hypotheses (
2.7
), there exists a constant
M>0
such that the initial data satisfy

\|n_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|n_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq M

and

\|c_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|c_{0}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq M.

For small
T\in(0,1)
, we choose the following space
X
as the solution space:

X_{T}:=C^{0}\big([0,T];\,\left(L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\times(H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\right),

and the closed subset
S
is defined by

\begin{split}S:=\Big\{(n,c)\in X_{T}\Big|\,\,&\|n\|_{L^{\infty}\big([0,T];L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\big)}+\|n\|_{L^{\infty}\big([0,T];L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\big)}\leq 2M,\\
&\|c\|_{L^{\infty}\big([0,T];H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\big)}+\|c\|_{L^{\infty}\big([0,T];W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\big)}\leq 2M\Big\}.\end{split}

Next, we consider a mapping
\Phi=(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2})
on
S
defined by

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}(n,c)=

\displaystyle e^{t\Delta}n_{0}-\chi\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta}\nabla\cdot(n\nabla c)\mathrm{\,d}s+\lambda\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta}n\mathrm{\,d}s-\mu\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta}n^{2}\mathrm{\,d}s

and

\displaystyle\Phi_{2}(n,c)=e^{\frac{t}{\tau}(-1+\Delta)}c_{0}+\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\frac{(t-s)}{\tau}(-1+\Delta)}n\mathrm{\,d}s

for each
t\in[0,T].

It is easy to check that
t\mapsto e^{t\Delta}n_{0}
belongs to
C^{0}([0,\infty);L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
and
t\mapsto e^{t(-1+\Delta)}c_{0}
belongs to
C^{0}([0,\infty);H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
. Collecting with some basic regularity properties concerning semigroup
e^{t\Delta}\,(e^{t(-1+\Delta)})
, for example established in
[
28
, Lemma 2.1]
, we can deduce that
\Phi
maps
S
into
X_{T}
.
Taking advantage of standard
L^{p}
-
L^{q}
estimate for heat semigroup, we can show that for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{1}(n,c)(t)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq

\displaystyle\|n_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+C_{1}\chi\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|n(s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\nabla c(s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\mathrm{\,d}s

\displaystyle+\lambda\int_{0}^{t}\|n(s)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\mathrm{\,d}s+\mu\int_{0}^{t}\|n(s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\mathrm{\,d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|n_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+C_{1}\chi T^{\frac{1}{2}}\|n\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\|n\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\|\nabla c\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}

\displaystyle+\lambda T\|n\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}+\mu T\|n\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\|n\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}.

Similarly, by Hölder’s and Young’s inequalities, it yields that for
0\leq t\leq T
,

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{1}(n,c)(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq

\displaystyle\|n_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+C_{2}\chi\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|n(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\nabla c(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\mathrm{\,d}s

\displaystyle+\lambda\int_{0}^{t}\|n(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\mathrm{\,d}s+\mu\int_{0}^{t}\|n(s)\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\mathrm{\,d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|n_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+C_{2}\chi T^{\frac{1}{2}}\|n\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\|\nabla c\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}

\displaystyle+\lambda T\|n\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}+\mu T\|n\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}.

Therefore, adding the above two inequality leads to

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{1}(n,c)(t)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|\Phi_{1}(n,c)(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|n_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|n_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+C_{1}\chi T^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\|n\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}+\|n\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\right)\|\nabla c\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}

\displaystyle+C_{2}\chi T^{\frac{1}{2}}\|n\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\|\nabla c\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}+\lambda T\left(\|n\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}+\|n\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\right)

\displaystyle+\mu T\|n\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\left(\|n\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}+\|n\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M+4C_{1}\chi T^{\frac{1}{2}}M^{2}+4C_{2}\chi T^{\frac{1}{2}}M^{2}+2\lambda TM+4\mu TM^{2}.

If we choose

T_{1}=\min\left\{1,\,\frac{1}{4\lambda},\,\big(8M(C_{1}\chi+C_{2}\chi+\mu)\big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right\},

it is easy to check that for
0<t\leq T_{1}
,

\|\Phi_{1}(n,c)(t)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|\Phi_{1}(n,c)(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq 2M.

In the similar fashion as above, we can show

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{2}(n,c)(t)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|\Phi_{2}(n,c)(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}}\left(\|c_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|c_{0}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)+\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\frac{t-s}{\tau}}\left(\|n(s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|n(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)\mathrm{\,d}s

\displaystyle+\frac{C_{1}}{{{\tau}}}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{t-s}{\tau}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{t-s}{\tau}}\left(\|n(s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|n(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)\mathrm{\,d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\|c_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|c_{0}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)+2\left(\frac{T}{\tau}+C_{1}\left(\frac{T}{\tau}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\left(\|n\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}+\|n\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M+4M\bigg(\frac{T}{\tau}+C_{1}\left(\frac{T}{\tau}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg).

Taking

T_{2}=\min\left\{1,\,\left(\frac{\tau}{4(1+C_{1}\sqrt{\tau})}\right)^{2}\right\},

we obtain that

\|\Phi_{2}(n,c)(t)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|\Phi_{2}(n,c)(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq 2M.

Then
\Phi(t)
maps
S
into itself for
t\leq T_{0}:=\min\{T_{1},T_{2}\}
.
Next, we show
\Phi
is a contraction mapping in a short time. For any couple
(n_{1},c_{1})\in S
and couple
(n_{2},c_{2})\in S
, an easy computation yields that

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{1}(n_{1},c_{1})(t)-\Phi_{1}(n_{2},c_{2})(t)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\chi\int_{0}^{t}\left\|e^{(t-s)\Delta}\nabla\cdot\left((n_{1}(s)\nabla c_{1}(s))-(n_{2}(s)\nabla c_{2}(s))\right)\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\mathrm{\,d}s

\displaystyle+\lambda\int_{0}^{t}\left\|e^{(t-s)\Delta}\left(n_{1}(s)-n_{2}(s)\right)\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\mathrm{\,d}s+\mu\int_{0}^{t}\left\|e^{(t-s)\Delta}\left(n^{2}_{1}(s)-n^{2}_{2}(s)\right)\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\mathrm{\,d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\chi\int_{0}^{t}\left\|e^{(t-s)\Delta}\nabla\cdot((n_{1}(s)-n_{2}(s))\nabla c_{1}(s))\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\mathrm{\,d}s

\displaystyle+\chi\int_{0}^{t}\left\|e^{(t-s)\Delta}\nabla\cdot(n_{2}(s)\nabla(c_{1}(s)-c_{2}(s))\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\mathrm{\,d}s

\displaystyle+\lambda T\|n_{1}-n_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}+\mu T\|n_{1}+n_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\|n_{1}-n_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}\chi T^{\frac{1}{2}}\|n_{1}-n_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\|c_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}+C_{1}\chi T^{\frac{1}{2}}\|n_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\|c_{1}-c_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}

\displaystyle+\lambda T\|n_{1}-n_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}+\mu T\left(\|n_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}+\|n_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\right)\|n_{1}-n_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(4C_{1}M\chi T^{\frac{1}{2}}+\lambda T+4M\mu T\right)\|(n_{1},c_{1})-(n_{2},c_{2})\|_{X_{T}}.

Similarly, we estimate

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{1}(n_{1},c_{1})(t)-\Phi_{1}(n_{2},c_{2})(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{4}\chi T^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{d}{2q}}\|n_{1}-n_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\|\nabla c_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}

\displaystyle+C_{4}\chi T^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{d}{2q}}\|n_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\|\nabla(c_{1}-c_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}+\lambda T\|n_{1}-n_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}

\displaystyle+\mu T\left(\|n_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}+\|n_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\right)\|n_{1}-n_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(4C_{4}M\chi T^{\frac{1}{2}}+\lambda T+4M\mu T\right)\|(n_{1},c_{1})-(n_{2},c_{2})\|_{X_{T}},

and

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{2}(n_{1},c_{1})(t)-\Phi_{2}(n_{2},c_{2})(t)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|\Phi_{2}(n_{1},c_{1})(t)-\Phi_{2}(n_{2},c_{2})(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C_{1}}{\tau}\int_{0}^{t}\bigg(1+\left(\frac{t-s}{\tau}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\bigg)\left(\|n_{1}(s)-n_{2}(s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|n_{1}(s)-n_{2}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)\mathrm{\,d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{5}\left(\frac{T}{\tau}+\left(\frac{T}{\tau}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\|n_{1}-n_{2}\|_{X_{T}}.

Taking

\widetilde{T}=\min\left\{T_{0},\,{\left(16MC_{4}\chi+4\lambda+16M\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{d}{2q}}},\,\frac{\tau^{2}}{4C_{5}^{2}(1+\sqrt{\tau})^{2}}\right\},

then
\Phi
is a contractive mapping on
S
. We apply the Banach fixed point theorem to obtain that there exists
(n,c)\in S
such that
\Phi(n,c)=(n,c)
. Moreover, by standard arguments involving semigroup estimates (cf. Lemma 3.3 in
[
8
]
), it can be checked that
(n,c)
belongs to
C^{2,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\widetilde{T}))^{2}
and solves problem (
1.4
)-(
1.5
) classically in
(0,\widetilde{T})\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Step 2: Nonnegativity
We are going to prove that
n,c\geq 0
if initial data
n_{0},c_{0}\geq 0
.
We firstly choose is a smooth cut-off function
\phi
satisfying

(2.9)

\phi(x)=\begin{cases}1,\quad x\in B_{R}(0),\\
0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash B_{2R}(0),\end{cases}

where
R>0
is a real number.

According to the local property
n\in C^{2,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\widetilde{T})),
the global property

\partial_{t}(\phi n),\,\,\,\phi\nabla n\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

can be achieved by introducing the cut-off function
\phi
.
Let us denote

n^{-}=\max\{0,-n\}.

Multiplying E.q.
\eqref{che}_{1}
by
\phi^{2}n^{-}
and integrating, it yields that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{\,d}}{\mathrm{\,d}t}\|\phi n^{-}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|\phi\nabla n^{-}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\nabla c\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\phi\nabla n^{-}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\phi n^{-}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|n\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\nabla c\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle+2\|n\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\phi\nabla n^{-}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\lambda\|\phi n^{-}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\|\nabla c\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\phi n^{-}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\frac{C}{R}\|n\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\nabla c\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\frac{C}{R}\|n\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\frac{1}{2}\|\phi\nabla n^{-}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle+\lambda\|\phi n^{-}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Since
(n,c)\in X_{\widetilde{T}}
, we obtain by Grönwall’s inequality that for any
t<\widetilde{T}
,

\displaystyle\|\phi n^{-}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}2\|\nabla c(s)\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\mathrm{\,d}s+\lambda t\right)\|n^{-}_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\frac{C}{R}\widetilde{T}.

Taking
R
goes to infinite, we immediately get by the continuous property that
n(x,t)\geq 0
in the domain
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\widetilde{T})
.

Letting
\tilde{c}=e^{t}c,
it follows from
\eqref{che}_{2}
and the nonnegativity of
n
that

\partial_{t}\tilde{c}-\Delta\tilde{c}=e^{t}n\geq 0,

which means that
\tilde{c}
is the super-solution of the linear heat equation. Since
\tilde{c}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})),
we get by weak maximum principle for Cauchy problem that

\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,\widetilde{T})}\tilde{c}(x,t)=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{c}(x,0)=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}{c}_{0}(x).

Hence

c(x,t)=e^{-t}\tilde{c}(x,t)\geq e^{-t}\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}{c}_{0}(x)\geq 0\quad\text{for all}\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\big(0,\widetilde{T}\big),

because
c_{0}\geq 0
.

Step 3: Uniqueness
First of all, we denote the maximal lifespan by
T_{\max}
. Now we begin to prove the uniqueness of the solution of (
1.4
). Since

(n,c)\in C^{0}\big([0,T_{\max});(L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\times H^{1}\cap W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\big),

we see that the couple
(n,c)
vanishes to
0
as
|x|\to\infty
. Therefore, the following estimates have no boundary term involving integrating by parts.
Let
(n,c)
and
(\widetilde{n},\widetilde{c})
be the two solutions to system (
1.4
) in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T_{\max})
with the same initial data. Set
\delta n=n-\widetilde{n}
and
\delta c=c-\widetilde{c}
, we easily find that the couple
(\delta n,\delta c)
satisfies

(2.10)

\left.\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\delta n-\Delta\delta n=&-\chi\nabla\cdot(\delta n\nabla c)-\chi\nabla\cdot(\widetilde{n}\nabla\delta c)+\lambda\delta n-\mu(n+\widetilde{n})\delta n\\
\tau\partial_{t}\delta c-\Delta\delta c=&-\delta c+\delta n\end{aligned}\right\}\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T_{\max}),

which is supplemented with the homogeneous initial data

(\delta n,\delta c)(x,0)=(0,0)\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Then we can show by using energy method and introducing the cut-off function
\phi
defined in (
2.9
) that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{\,d}}{\mathrm{\,d}t}\|\phi\delta n(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|\phi\nabla\delta n(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\chi\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi^{2}\delta n\nabla c\cdot\nabla\delta n\mathrm{\,d}x+2\chi\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi\delta n\nabla c\cdot\nabla\phi\delta n\mathrm{\,d}x+\chi\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi^{2}\widetilde{n}\nabla\delta c\cdot\nabla\delta n\mathrm{\,d}x

\displaystyle+2\chi\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi\widetilde{n}\nabla\delta c\cdot\nabla\phi\delta n\mathrm{\,d}x-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi\nabla\delta n\cdot\nabla\phi\delta n\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\|\phi\delta n\|^{2}_{L^{2}}(\lambda+\mu\|n\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\mu\|\widetilde{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\chi}\|\nabla c\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\phi\delta n\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\phi\nabla\delta n\|_{L^{2}}+\chi\|\widetilde{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\phi\nabla\delta c\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\phi\nabla\delta n\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle+2\chi\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\delta n\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left(\|\nabla c\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\phi\delta n\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|\widetilde{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\phi\nabla\delta c\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)

\displaystyle+\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\phi\nabla\delta n\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\delta n\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|\phi\delta n\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left(\lambda+\mu\|n\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\mu\|\widetilde{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\phi\nabla\delta n\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+C\chi^{2}\|\nabla c\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\phi\delta n\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+C\chi^{2}\|\widetilde{n}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\phi\nabla\delta c\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle+\|\phi\delta n\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left(\lambda+\mu\|n\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\mu\|\widetilde{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)+\frac{C}{R},

in the last line, we have used the fact

(n,c),\,(\widetilde{n},\widetilde{c})\in C^{0}\big([0,T_{\max});(L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\times H^{1}\cap W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\big).

Similarly, with aid of Hölder’s and Young’s inequalities, one gets that

\displaystyle\frac{\tau}{2}\frac{\mathrm{\,d}}{\mathrm{\,d}t}\|\phi\nabla\delta c(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|\phi\Delta\delta c(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|\phi\nabla\delta c(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi^{2}\nabla\delta n\cdot\nabla\delta c\mathrm{\,d}x-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi\Delta\delta c\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\delta c\mathrm{\,d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\phi\delta n\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\frac{1}{2}\|\phi\Delta\delta c\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+8\|\nabla\phi\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\nabla\delta c\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Therefore, combined the above two estimates shows that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{\,d}}{\mathrm{\,d}t}\left(\frac{1}{2}\|\phi\delta n(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\frac{\tau}{2}\|\phi\nabla\delta c(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\phi\delta n\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left(C\chi^{2}\|\nabla c\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\lambda+\mu\left(\|n\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|\widetilde{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)+1\right)

\displaystyle+C\chi^{2}\|\widetilde{n}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\phi\nabla\delta c\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\frac{C}{R}+\frac{C}{R^{2}}.

Integrating the above inequality with respect to
t
and then taking
R
goes to infinite yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\delta n(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\frac{\tau}{2}\|\nabla\delta c(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|\delta n\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left(C\chi^{2}\|\nabla c\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\lambda+\mu\left(\|n\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|\widetilde{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)+1\right)\mathrm{\,d}x

\displaystyle+C\chi^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|\widetilde{n}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\nabla\delta c\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\mathrm{\,d}s.

Noting the fact that
n,\,\widetilde{n}\in L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}([0,T_{\max}),\,L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
and
c,\,\widetilde{c}\in L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}([0,T_{\max}),\,W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, by Grönwall inequality we obtain that
(n,c)\equiv(\widetilde{n},\widetilde{c})
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T_{\max})
.

From our definition of
T
it becomes clear through another standard reasoning that
(n,c)
can be extended up to a maximal
T_{\max}\in(0,\infty]
fulfilling

(2.11)

\|n(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|n(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|c(\cdot,t)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|c(\cdot,t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\to\infty\,\,\,\,\text{as}\,\,t\nearrow T_{\max}.

Now we adopt proof by contradiction to verify conclusion (
2.11
). Suppose (
2.11
) does not hold, there exists a constant
R>0
such that

\|n(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|n(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|c(\cdot,t)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|c(\cdot,t)\|_{W^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq R.

According to selection method of
\widetilde{T}
, we choose

\displaystyle\varepsilon=\min\bigg\{1,\,\frac{1}{4\lambda},\,

\displaystyle\left(8R(C_{1}\chi+C_{2}\chi+\mu)\right)^{2},\,\left(\frac{\tau}{4(1+C_{1}\sqrt{\tau})}\right)^{2},

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\left(16RC_{4}\chi+4\lambda+16M\mu\right)^{2},\,\frac{\tau^{2}}{4C_{5}^{2}(1+\sqrt{\tau})^{2}}\bigg\}.

From the above existence proof, we conclude that there exists a unique solution
(\bar{n},\bar{c})
with initial data
\big(n(T_{\max}-{\varepsilon}/{2}),c(T_{\max}-{\varepsilon}/{2})\big)
on
[0,\varepsilon]
, moreover, we have by uniqueness that

\bar{n}(t)=n\left(t+T_{\max}-{\varepsilon}/{2}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\bar{c}(t)=c\left(t+T_{\max}-{\varepsilon}/{2}\right)

on
[0,{\varepsilon}/{2})
, which implies that
(\bar{n},\bar{c})
extends solution
(n,c)
beyond
T_{\max}
. This contradicts the definition of
T_{\max}
. Hence (
2.11
) is valid.

Step 4: Continuation
. Our task is now to show (
2.8
). Thanks to (
2.11
), it suffice to prove that two quantities
\|n(t)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
and
\|c(t)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
cannot blow up at finite time.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
(n,c)
be the solution of system (
1.4
)-(
1.5
). Then, for all
t\in(0,T_{\max})
, the couple
(n,c)
satisfies the following estimates independent of

:

(2.12)

\|n(t)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\int_{0}^{t}\|n(s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\mathrm{\,d}s\leq e^{\lambda t}\|n_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

(2.13)

\begin{split}\tau\|c(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\int_{0}^{t}\|c(s)\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\mathrm{\,d}s\leq\tau\|c_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+e^{\lambda t}\|n_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\end{split}

and

Proof of Proposition
2.2
.

Owning to
n\geq 0
, multiplying E.q.
\eqref{che}_{1}
by
\phi
and integrating with respect to spatial variable yields that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{\,d}}{\mathrm{\,d}t}\|\phi n(t)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\mu\big\|\sqrt{\phi}n(t)\big\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lambda\|\phi n(t)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\frac{C}{R^{2}}\|n\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\frac{C}{R}\|\nabla c\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|n\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Performing Grönwall’s inequality and taking
R
goes to infinite, the above equality implies estimate (
2.12
).

Multiplying E.q.
\eqref{che}_{2}
by
\phi^{2}c
and integrating in terms of spatial variable, we obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{\tau}{2}\frac{\mathrm{\,d}}{\mathrm{\,d}t}\|\phi c(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\frac{1}{2}\|\phi\nabla c(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\frac{1}{2}\|\phi c(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\phi n\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\frac{C}{R^{2}}\|c\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Integrating the above inequality with respect to time variable from
0
to
t
and taking
R
to infinite lead to

\displaystyle\tau\|c(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|\nabla c(s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|c(s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)\mathrm{\,d}s\leq\tau\|c_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\int_{0}^{t}\|n(s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\mathrm{\,d}s,

combining with estimate (

) yields (
2.13
).

Similarly, we can show

\displaystyle\frac{\tau}{2}\frac{\mathrm{\,d}}{\mathrm{\,d}t}\|\phi\nabla c(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|\phi\nabla^{2}c(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|\phi\nabla c(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n\Delta c\mathrm{\,d}x-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi\nabla c\cdot\big(\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\nabla c\big)\mathrm{\,d}x-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi n\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla c\mathrm{\,d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\|\phi n\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\frac{1}{2}\|\phi\nabla^{2}c\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\frac{C}{R^{2}}\|\nabla c\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Thanks to inequality (
2.12
), it follows from the above inequality that (
3.1
).

3.
Proof of main result

This section is devoted to show Theorem
1.1
. We will adopt the following approximate schemes for Cauchy problem (
1.4
)-(
1.5
) to do this:

(3.15)

\left.\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}n_{M}-\Delta n_{M}=&-\chi\nabla\cdot(n_{M}\nabla c_{M})+\lambda n_{M}-\mu n_{M}^{2}\\
\tau\partial_{t}c_{M}-\Delta c_{M}=&-c_{M}+n_{M}\end{aligned}\right\}\quad\text{in}\,\,\,\,\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},

(3.16)

n_{M}(0,x)=\psi(x/M)n_{0}(x),\quad c_{M}(0,x)=\psi(x/M)c_{0}(x)\quad\text{in}\,\,\,\,\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Here
\psi
is a smooth function satisfying

\psi(x)=\begin{cases}1,\quad&x\in B_{1}(0)\\
0,&x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash B_{2}(0)\end{cases}.

Since
n_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
c_{0}\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, it is easy to check that the initial data in (
3.16
) satisfy

n_{M}(0,x)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

and

c_{M}(0,x)\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Moreover, we have from Theorem
2.1
that problem (
3.15
)-(
3.16
) admits a unique nonnegative solution
(n_{M},c_{M})\in\big(C^{2,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T_{\max}))\big)^{2}
such that

\displaystyle n^{M}\in C^{0}\left([0,T_{\max});L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\cap C^{2,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T_{\max})\right),

\displaystyle c^{M}\in C^{0}\left([0,T_{\max});H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\cap C^{2,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T_{\max})\right),

if
T_{\max}<\infty
, then

(3.17)

\lim_{T\to T_{\textnormal{max}}-}\left(\left\|n^{M}(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\left\|c^{M}(\cdot,t)\right\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)=\infty.

This implies the solutions to the regularized problem (
3.15
)-(
3.16
) are smooth and
decay sufficiently fast at infinity, so there are no boundary terms when we integrate
by parts in our calculations below. In addition, we define uniformly local space
L^{p,R}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
consists of the local integral function
f
satisfying

\|f\|_{p,R}<+\infty,

where
R
is undermined now and fixed later. Let us point out that
L^{p,R}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
coincides with space
L^{p}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
defined in Notation for arbitrary
R>0
. Indeed, it is obvious that for
R\geq 1,

\|f\|_{p,1}\leq\|f\|_{p,R}.

On the other hand, we have by the covering theorem that for
R\geq 1,

\|f\|_{p,R}\leq\left(CR^{d}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{p,1}.

To simplify the notation, we agree that
(n,c):=(n_{M},c_{M})
and
L^{p}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=L^{p,R}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
in the following part of this section.
Next we are going to establish some useful
a priori estimates
for
(n_{M},c_{M})
by introducing the uniformly local space
L^{p,R}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume
\tau,\mu,\chi>0
and
\lambda\geq 0
. Let the couple
(n,c)
be the solution of system (
3.15
)-(
3.16
), if
\mu>\frac{d\chi}{4}
, then there exists a
R_{0}>1
such that for all
R\geq R_{0}
and
T\in(0,T_{\textnormal{max}})
,

\displaystyle\|n\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{1}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\frac{\chi\tau}{4}\|\nabla c\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4\|n_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+2{\chi\tau}\|\nabla c_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+C(\lambda,\tau,\mu,\chi,d,R).

Proof.

We introduce a function
\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by

(3.18)

\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\exp\left(\frac{4}{3}+\frac{4R^{2}}{|x-x_{0}|^{2}-4R^{2}}\right),&|x-x_{0}|<2R,\\
&0,&|x-x_{0}|\geq 2R.\end{aligned}\right.

From the definition of
\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}
, it is easy to check that

(i)

\displaystyle 1\leq\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}(x)<2
,
\forall\,x\in B_{R}(x_{0})
.

(ii)

\displaystyle\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\big|_{\partial B_{2R}(x_{0})}=\frac{\partial\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}}{\partial\nu}\bigg|_{\partial B_{2R}(x_{0})}=0
.

(iii)

\displaystyle|\nabla\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}|\leq\frac{C}{R}
,
\displaystyle|D^{2}\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}|\leq\frac{C}{R^{2}}
, where
C
is an absolute constant independent of
R
.

To begin with, using

\Delta\nabla c\cdot\nabla c=\frac{1}{2}\Delta|\nabla c|^{2}-|D^{2}c|^{2},

we can infer from system (
1.4
) that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{\,d}}{\mathrm{\,d}t}\left(n+\frac{\chi\tau}{2}|\nabla c|^{2}\right)-\Delta\left(n+\frac{\chi}{2}|\nabla c|^{2}\right)+\chi\left(|D^{2}c|^{2}+|\nabla c|^{2}\right)=-\chi n\Delta c+\lambda n-\mu n^{2}.

Multiplying the above equality by
\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}
and integrating in terms of spatial variable, it yields that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{\,d}}{\mathrm{\,d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(n+\frac{\chi\tau}{2}|\nabla c|^{2}\right)\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Delta(n+\frac{\chi}{2}|\nabla c|^{2})\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x+\chi\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(|D^{2}c|^{2}+|\nabla c|^{2})\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\chi\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n\Delta c\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x+\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x-\mu\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{2}\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x.

Performing integration by parts, noting the definition of
\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Delta\left(n+\frac{\chi}{2}|\nabla c|^{2}\right)\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{2R}(x_{0})}\left(n+\frac{\chi}{2}|\nabla c|^{2}\right){\Delta\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}}\mathrm{\,d}x.

Because there exists a family of balls
\displaystyle\{B_{R}(y_{i})\}_{i=1}^{3^{d}}
such that

B_{2R}(x_{0})\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{3^{d}}B_{R}(y_{i}),

we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2R}(x_{0})}\left(n+\frac{\chi}{2}|\nabla c|^{2}\right){\Delta\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}}\mathrm{\,d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{R^{2}}\int_{B_{2R}(x_{0})}\left(n+\frac{\chi}{2}|\nabla c|^{2}\right)\mathrm{\,d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{R^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{3^{d}}\int_{B_{R}(y_{i})}\left(n+\frac{\chi}{2}|\nabla c|^{2}\right)\mathrm{\,d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C3^{d}}{R^{2}}\left(\|n\|_{L^{1}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\frac{\chi}{2}\|\nabla c\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right).

Owning to
|\Delta c|^{2}\leq d|D^{2}c|^{2}
, we have by Hölder’s and Young’s inequalities that

\displaystyle-\chi\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n\Delta c\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\chi}{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\Delta c|^{2}\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x+\frac{d\chi}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{2}\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\chi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|D^{2}c|^{2}\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x+\frac{d\chi}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{2}\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x.

Therefore, the above estimates imply that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{\,d}}{\mathrm{\,d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(n+\frac{\chi\tau}{2}|\nabla c|^{2}\right)\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x+\frac{2}{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(n+\frac{\chi\tau}{2}|\nabla c|^{2}\right)\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C3^{d}}{R^{2}}\left(\|n\|_{L^{1}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\frac{\chi}{2}\|\nabla c\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)+\left(\lambda+\frac{2}{\tau}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}+\left(\frac{d\chi}{4}-\mu\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{2}\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x.

Since
n^{2}\geq\varepsilon n-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4}
for any
\varepsilon\geq 0
and for
\mu>\frac{d\chi}{4}
, we have that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{\,d}}{\mathrm{\,d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(n+\frac{\chi\tau}{2}|\nabla c|^{2}\right)\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x+\frac{2}{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(n+\frac{\chi\tau}{2}|\nabla c|^{2}\right)\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C3^{d}}{R^{2}}\left(\|n\|_{L^{1}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\frac{\chi}{2}\|\nabla c\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)+\left(\lambda+\frac{2}{\tau}-\varepsilon\left(\mu-\frac{d\chi}{4}\right)\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x

\displaystyle+\left(\mu-\frac{d\chi}{4}\right)\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C3^{d}}{R^{2}}(\|n\|_{L^{1}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\frac{\chi}{2}\|\nabla c\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})})+\left(\lambda+\frac{2}{\tau}-\varepsilon\left(\mu-\frac{d\chi}{4}\right)\right)\|n\|_{L^{1}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle+C\left(\mu-\frac{d\chi}{4}\right)\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4}R^{d}.

For any
t\in[0,T]
, the above estimate shows that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{\,d}}{\mathrm{\,d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(n+\frac{\chi\tau}{2}|\nabla c|^{2}\right)\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x+\frac{2}{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(n+\frac{\chi\tau}{2}|\nabla c|^{2}\right)\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C3^{d}}{R^{2}}\left(\|n\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{1}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\frac{\chi}{2}\|\nabla c\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)+\left(\lambda+\frac{2}{\tau}-\varepsilon\left(\mu-\frac{d\chi}{4}\right)\right)\|n\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{1}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle+C\left(\mu-\frac{d\chi}{4}\right)\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4}R^{d}.

Using the fact that
y^{\prime}(t)+cy(t)\leq C
in
(0,T]
implies

y\leq\max\left\{y(0),\,\frac{C}{c}\right\}\,\,\text{ for all }t\in(0,T],

from the above inequality we obtain that, for any
t\in(0,T]
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n(t)\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x+\frac{\chi\tau}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla c(t)|^{2}\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max\Bigg\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n_{0}\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x+\frac{\chi\tau}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla c_{0}|^{2}\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x,\,\,\frac{C3^{d}\tau}{2R^{2}}\left(\|n\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{1}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\frac{\chi}{2}\|\nabla c\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\frac{\tau}{2}\left(\lambda+\frac{2}{\tau}-\varepsilon\left(\mu-\frac{d\chi}{4}\right)\right)\|n\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{1}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\frac{C\tau}{2}\left(\mu-\frac{d\chi}{4}\right)\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4}R^{d}\Bigg\}.

Noting the definition of
L^{p}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and the support property of
\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}
, we infer from the above inequality that for any
0<t\leq T
,

\displaystyle\|n(t)\|_{L^{1}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\frac{\chi\tau}{2}\|\nabla c(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max\Bigg\{2\|n_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+{\chi\tau}\|\nabla c_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\,\,\frac{C3^{d}\tau}{R^{2}}\left(\|n\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{1}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\frac{\chi}{2}\|\nabla c\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+{\tau}\left(\lambda+\frac{2}{\tau}-\varepsilon\left(\mu-\frac{d\chi}{4}\right)\right)\|n\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{1}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+{C\tau}\left(\mu-\frac{d\chi}{4}\right)\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4}R^{d}\Bigg\}.

Taking sup with respect to time variable
t
, we get that

\displaystyle\|n\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{1}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\frac{\chi\tau}{2}\|\nabla c\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\max\Bigg\{2\|n_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+{\chi\tau}\|\nabla c_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\,\,\frac{C3^{d}\tau}{R^{2}}\left(\|n\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{1}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\frac{\chi}{2}\|\nabla c\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\,\,+{\tau}\left(\lambda+\frac{2}{\tau}-\varepsilon\left(\mu-\frac{d\chi}{4}\right)\right)\|n\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{1}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+{C\tau}\left(\mu-\frac{d\chi}{4}\right)\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4}R^{d}\Bigg\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\Bigg\{4\|n_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+2{\chi\tau}\|\nabla c_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\,\,\frac{C3^{d}\chi\tau}{R^{2}}\|\nabla c\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+{C\tau}\left(\mu-\frac{d\chi}{4}\right)\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}R^{d}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\,\,\,\,+2{\tau}\left(\frac{C3^{d}}{R^{2}}+\lambda+\frac{2}{\tau}-\varepsilon\left(\mu-\frac{d\chi}{4}\right)\right)\|n\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{1}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\Bigg\}.

We choosing some
R_{0}>1
, such that for all
R\geq R_{0}
,

\frac{C3^{d}}{R^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{4}.

As long as
\mu>\frac{d\chi}{4}
, we can choose some
\varepsilon
such that

\frac{C3^{d}}{R^{2}}+\lambda+\frac{2}{\tau}-\varepsilon\left(\mu-\frac{d\chi}{4}\right)=0.

Then we infer that

\displaystyle\|n\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{1}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\frac{\chi\tau}{2}\|\nabla c\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max\left\{4\|n_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+2{\chi\tau}\|\nabla c_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\,\frac{\chi\tau}{4}\|\nabla c\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+C(\lambda,\tau,\mu,\chi,d,R)\right\}.

Hence we can conclude that

\displaystyle\|n\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{1}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\frac{\chi\tau}{4}\|\nabla c\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4\|n_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+2{\chi\tau}\|\nabla c_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+C(\lambda,\tau,\mu,\chi,d,R).

We complete the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\lambda,\,\chi>0
,
R\geq 1
and
\mu\geq 0
. Then for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
k\geq 2
, one can find some absolute constant
C
and
C_{k}
depending on
k,\lambda,\chi
such that the solution
(n,c)
of equations (
3.15
)-(
3.16
) satisfies

(3.19)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{\,d}}{\mathrm{\,d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{k}\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x+\frac{k(k-1)}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla n|^{2}n^{k-2}\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C3^{d}k}{2(k-1)R^{2}}\|n\|^{k}_{L^{k}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\frac{C3^{d}k}{R^{2k}}\|\nabla c\|^{2k}_{L^{2k}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+k\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{2}|\nabla c|^{2k-2}\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x

\displaystyle+(C_{k}-\mu k)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{k+1}\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x+C(\lambda+1)R^{d}k.

Proof.

Multiplying E.q.
\eqref{che}_{1}
by
n^{k-1}\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}
and then integrating with respect to spatial variable, we obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}\frac{\mathrm{\,d}}{\mathrm{\,d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{k}\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Delta nn^{k-1}\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\chi\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla\cdot(n\nabla c)n^{k-1}\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x+\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{k}\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x-\mu\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{k+1}\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x.

Integration by parts yields

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Delta nn^{k-1}\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x=(k-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla n|^{2}n^{k-2}\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{k-1}\nabla n\cdot\nabla\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x,

and

\displaystyle-\chi\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla\cdot(n\nabla c)n^{k-1}\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x=\chi\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{k}\nabla c\cdot\nabla\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x+\chi(k-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{k-1}\nabla c\cdot\nabla n\,\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x.

Therefore, from the above two equalities, we obtain that

(3.20)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}\frac{\mathrm{\,d}}{\mathrm{\,d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{k}\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x+(k-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla n|^{2}n^{k-2}\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{k-1}\nabla n\cdot\nabla\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x+\chi\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{k}\nabla c\cdot\nabla\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x+\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{k}\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x

\displaystyle-\mu\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{k+1}\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x+\chi(k-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{k-1}\nabla c\cdot\nabla n\,\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x.

By virtue of Hölder’s inequality and Young’s inequality, we achieve that

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{k-1}\nabla n\cdot\nabla\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x\leq\frac{k-1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla n|^{2}n^{k-2}\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x+\frac{1}{2(k-1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{k}\frac{|\nabla\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}|^{2}}{\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}}\mathrm{\,d}x.

According to the expression of
\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}
, an easy computation yields that for
x\in B_{2R}(x_{0})
,

\displaystyle\phi^{-1}_{x_{0},R}|\nabla\phi_{x_{0},R}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{|x-x_{0}|}{4R^{2}}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{4R^{2}}{|x-x_{0}|^{2}-4R^{2}}\right)^{4}\exp\left(\frac{4}{3}+{\frac{4R^{2}}{|x-x_{0}|^{2}-4R^{2}}}\right)\leq\frac{C}{R^{2}}.

Hence, it easy to check that

(3.21)

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{k-1}\nabla n\cdot\nabla\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{k-1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla n|^{2}n^{k-2}\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x+\frac{C}{2(k-1)R^{2}}\int_{B_{2R}(x_{0})}n^{k}\mathrm{\,d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{k-1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla n|^{2}n^{k-2}\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x+\frac{C3^{d}}{2(k-1)R^{2}}\|n\|^{k}_{L^{k}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Similarly, by Hölder’s inequality and Young’s inequality, using the definition of
\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}
, we infer that

(3.22)

\displaystyle\chi\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{k}\nabla c\cdot\nabla\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\chi^{\frac{k+1}{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{k+1}\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla c|^{2k}(\phi^{R}_{x_{0}})^{-\frac{k}{k+1}\cdot 2k}|\nabla\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}|^{2k}\mathrm{\,d}x+\Big(\int_{B_{2R}(x_{0})}1\mathrm{\,d}x\Big)^{\frac{k-1}{2k(k+1)}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\chi^{\frac{k+1}{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{k+1}\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x+\frac{C}{R^{2k}}\int_{B_{2R}(x_{0})}|\nabla c|^{2k}\mathrm{\,d}x+CR^{\frac{k-1}{2k(k+1)}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\chi^{\frac{k+1}{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{k+1}\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x+\frac{C3^{d}}{R^{2k}}\|\nabla c\|^{2k}_{L^{2k}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+CR^{\frac{k-1}{2k(k+1)}}.

Also, the third term on the right-hand side of equality (
3.20
) can be bounded as follows

(3.23)

\displaystyle\chi(k-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{k-1}\nabla c\cdot\nabla n\,\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{k-1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla n|^{2}n^{k-2}\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x+\chi^{2}(k-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n^{k}|\nabla c|^{2}\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x

\displaystyle\leq

Lemma 3.7

Lemma 3.10

Let

\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}+\|\dot{S}_{j}f\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}\leq C2^{\frac{d}{p}j}\|f\|_{p,1}.

[
1

Letting

we rewrite

\displaystyle(\varphi*f)(x_{0})=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(x_{0}-y)f(y)\mathrm{\,d}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}(x_{0})}\varphi(x_{0}-y)f(y)\mathrm{\,d}y+\sum_{k\geq 0}\int_{\mathcal{C}(k,k+1,x_{0})}\varphi(x_{0}-y)f(y)\mathrm{\,d}y.

\displaystyle|(\varphi*f)(x_{0})|\leq

\displaystyle C\|f\|_{1,1}+\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big||x|^{2d}\varphi(x)\big|\sum_{k\geq 0}\int_{\mathcal{C}(k,k+1,x_{0})}|x_{0}-y|^{-{2d}}|f|(y)\mathrm{\,d}y

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|f\|_{1,1}+C\sum_{k\geq 0}2^{-2dk}\int_{\mathcal{C}(k,k+1,x_{0})}|f|(y)\mathrm{\,d}y

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|f\|_{1,1}+C\|f\|_{1,1}\sum_{k\geq 0}2^{-2dk}2^{kd},

taking
\sup
in terms of
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, which implies the desired estimate (
3.40
).

Next, we can show the second estimate by the scaling analysis. Let
f_{j}(x):=f\left(x/2^{j}\right)
, we see that

\big|\dot{\Delta}_{j}f(x)\big|=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(y)f_{j}\left(2^{j}x-y\right)\mathrm{d}y\right|=\left|\dot{\Delta}_{0}f_{j}\left(2^{j}x\right)\right|.

Moreover, we get by (
3.40
) that for each
p\geq 1,

\big\|\dot{\Delta}_{0}f_{j}\big\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}\leq C\left\|f_{j}\right\|_{1,1}=C\left\|f\left(\cdot/2^{j}\right)\right\|_{1,1}\leq C\left\|f\left(\cdot/2^{j}\right)\right\|_{p,1},

namely,

\big\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}f\big\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}\leq C\left\|f\left(\cdot/2^{j}\right)\right\|_{p,1}\leq C2^{dj/p}\|f\|_{p,2^{-j}}.

where we have used the following fact

\left(\int_{|x-y|\leq 1}\left|f\left(\frac{y}{2^{j}}\right)\right|^{p}\mathrm{~d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\left(2^{dj}\int_{\left|2^{-j}x-y\right|\leq 2^{-j}}|f(y)|^{p}\mathrm{~d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq C2^{dj/p}\|f\|_{p,2^{-j}}.

In the same way, we can show estimate

\big\|\dot{S}_{j}f\big\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}\leq C2^{dj/p}\|f\|_{p,2^{-j}},

and we complete the proof of the lemma.
∎

With Lemma
3.10
in hand, we begin to show Proposition
3.9
which is the key estimate in our paper.

Proof of Proposition
3.9
.

From system (
3.15
), we can write in term of semigroup that

\displaystyle\nabla c(t)=

\displaystyle e^{\frac{t}{\tau}(-1+\Delta)}\nabla\big(\psi(x/M)c_{0}\big)+\int_{0}^{t}e^{\frac{(t-s)}{\tau}(-1+\Delta)}\nabla n\mathrm{\,d}s.

According to low-high frequency decomposition, we split
\nabla c
into two parts as follow

\nabla c=\dot{S}_{0}\nabla c+\sum_{j\geq 0}\dot{\Delta}_{j}\nabla c:=\nabla c^{L}+\nabla c^{H}.

For low frequency regime, by the generalized Young inequality (
3.41
), we have

\|\nabla c^{L}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|\nabla c_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

As for high frequency regime, we can bound it by using
[
1
, Lemma 2.4]
and (
3.41
) that for
k>d,

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla c^{H}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq

\displaystyle C\|c_{0}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\sum_{j\geq 0}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}e^{\frac{(t-s)}{\tau}(-1+\Delta)}\nabla n\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\mathrm{\,d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|c_{0}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+C\sum_{j\geq 0}C2^{j(1+\frac{d}{k})}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-c(t-s)2^{2j}}\left\|n(s)\right\|_{L^{k}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\mathrm{\,d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|c_{0}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+C\|n\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{k}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\sum_{j\geq 0}C2^{j(-1+\frac{d}{k})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|c_{0}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+C\|n\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{k}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Combining the above both estimates yields

\displaystyle\|\nabla c\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|\nabla c_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+C\|c_{0}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+C\|n\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{k}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Similarly, we can show that
k>d,

\displaystyle\|n(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq

\displaystyle C\|n\|_{L^{1}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+C\|c_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\sum_{j\geq 0}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}e^{(t-s)\Delta}\nabla(n\nabla c)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\mathrm{\,d}s

\displaystyle+\mu\sum_{j\geq 0}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}e^{(t-s)\Delta}n^{2}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\mathrm{\,d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|n\|_{L^{1}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+C\|c_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+C\mu\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{j\frac{2d}{k}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-c(t-s)2^{2j}}\|n\|^{2}_{L^{k}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\mathrm{\,d}s

\displaystyle+C\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{j(1+\frac{d}{k})}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-c(t-s)2^{2j}}\|n\|_{L^{k}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\nabla c\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\mathrm{\,d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|n\|_{L^{1}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|c_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\mu\|n\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{k}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|\nabla c\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right).

It remains to show the uniformly local
L^{2}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-estimate for
c
. In the same fashion as above for
\nabla c
, one has

\displaystyle\frac{\tau}{2}\frac{\textnormal{d}}{\textnormal{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}c\right)^{2}\mathrm{\,d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}c\right)^{2}\mathrm{\,d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Delta cc\left(\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\right)^{2}\mathrm{\,d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}nc\left(\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\right)^{2}\mathrm{\,d}x.

Integrating by parts

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Delta cc\left(\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\right)^{2}\mathrm{\,d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\nabla c\right)^{2}\mathrm{\,d}x+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}c\nabla c\cdot\nabla\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x.

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we see that

2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}c\nabla c\cdot\nabla\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\mathrm{\,d}x\leq 2C\left\|\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}c\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left\|\nabla c\right\|_{L^{2}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

On the other hand, we get by the Hölder inequality that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}nc\left(\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\right)^{2}\mathrm{\,d}x\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|n\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left\|\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}c\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CR^{\frac{d}{2}}\|n\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left\|\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}c\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Collecting the above estimates yields

\displaystyle\tau\frac{\textnormal{d}}{\textnormal{d}t}\left\|\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}c\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\left\|\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}c\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\left\|\nabla c\right\|_{L^{2}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+CR^{\frac{d}{2}}\|n\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Integrating the above inequality with respect to time
t
, we readily obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}c(t)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}}\left\|\phi^{R}_{x_{0}}\psi(x/M)c_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle+\left(C\left\|\nabla c\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+CR^{\frac{d}{2}}\|n\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\tau}e^{-\frac{1}{\tau}(t-s)}\mathrm{\,d}s.

Since

\left\|\nabla c\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|n\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C(\lambda,\tau,\mu,\chi,d,R),

we immediately have

\|c\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L^{2}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\|c_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+C(\lambda,\tau,\mu,\chi,d,R).

By the sharp interpolation inequality, we have

\|c\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|c\|^{\frac{2}{d+2}}_{L^{2}_{\textnormal{uloc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\nabla c\|^{\frac{d}{d+2}}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Collecting the above estimate, we eventually get that for
0<t<T_{\max},

\|n(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|c(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\left(\tau,\chi,k,\lambda,d,\mu,\|n_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\|c_{0}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right),

and then we finish proof of the proposition.
∎

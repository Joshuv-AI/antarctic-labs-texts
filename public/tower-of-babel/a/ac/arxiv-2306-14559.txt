Optimal control of a parabolic equation with a nonlocal nonlinearity
Cyrille Kenne, Landry Djomegne, Gisèle Mophou

Abstract
. This paper proposes an optimal control problem for a parabolic equation with a nonlocal nonlinearity. The system is described by a parabolic equation involving a nonlinear term that depends on the solution and its integral over the domain. We prove the existence and uniqueness of the solution to the system and the boundedness of the solution. Regularity results for the control-to-state operator, the cost functional and the adjoint state are also proved. We show the existence of optimal solutions and derive first-order necessary optimality conditions. In addition, second-order necessary and sufficient conditions for optimality are established.

Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
n\geq 1
) be an open bounded set, with regular boundary
\partial\Omega
. Let
\omega
is a nonempty subset of
\Omega
. We set
Q:=\Omega\times(0,T)
,
\Sigma=\partial\Omega\times(0,T)
and
\omega_{T}=\omega\times(0,T)
, where
T>0
.

In this paper, we investigate the following control problem:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\min_{v\in{\mathcal{U}_{ad}}}J(y,v),

(1.1)

with

\displaystyle\displaystyle J(y,v):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{Q}\left\lvert y-y_{d}\right\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\frac{\mu}{2}\int_{\omega_{T}}\left\lvert v\right\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t,

(1.2)

where
y_{d}\in L^{\infty}(Q),

\mu>0
and the set of admissible controls is given by

\mathcal{U}_{ad}:=\left\{w\in L^{\infty}(\omega_{T}):\alpha\leq w\leq\beta,\,\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R},\,\beta>\alpha\right\}.

(1.3)

The state
y:=y(v)
satisfies the following parabolic equation with a nonlinear term, nonlocal in space:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rllll}\displaystyle y_{t}-\Delta y+a\left(l(y(t))\right)y&=&v\chi_{\omega}&\mbox{in}&Q,\\
\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}y&=&0&\mbox{in}&\Sigma,\\
\displaystyle y(0,\cdot)&=&y^{0}&\mbox{in}&\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(1.4)

In the above system,
\partial_{\nu}
is the usual normal derivative,
v\in L^{2}(\omega_{T})
is the control function ,
\chi_{\omega}
denotes the characteristic function of the control set
\omega
and
y^{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
is the initial data. The function
l
is defined by

l(y(t))=\int_{\Omega}y(t,x^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}.

(1.5)

Recently, nonlocal problems have become increasingly considered by researchers. They have been
used for many application, for instance, in population dynamics
[

,

,

,

, biology
[

,

[

,

]
, so they are of great importance and interest. In the case of population dynamics, the most straightforward way of introducing non-local effects is by considering the crowding effect
[
24
]
. In this case, the state
y
of (
1.4
) can be interpreted as a density of population and the system (
1.4
) describes the evolution of this population according to the fact that
v
is a supply and
a\left(l(y(t))\right)
proportional to the density of population by a factor
a
represents the density of death or extinction of the population at stake
[
20
]
. The term

defined in (
1.5
) represents the total population at any time

. As an example, we mention the population of bacteria in which the price of life in crowded regions could drop dramatically.

We make the following assumptions on the real function

:

Assumption 1.1
.

•

The mortality rate of species increases with density. That is

is a non-decreasing function.

•

The function

\begin{array}[]{llll}\displaystyle a\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(\mathbb{R})\;\;\text{and}\;\;\exists\ a_{0},a_{1},M>0\;\;\text{such that}\,\,0<a_{0}\leq a(r)\leq a_{1}\;\;\text{and}\\
|a^{\prime}(r)|+|a^{\prime\prime}(r)|\leq M\;\;\text{for all}\;r\in\mathbb{R}.\end{array}

(1.6)

Remark 1.2
.

We observe that the assumption

for all

is not too restrictive. Indeed, one can replace this assumption by: there exists

such that

|a(r)|\leq\alpha\;\;\text{for all}\;\;r\in\mathbb{R},

and then make the classical change of variables

in (

to obtain:

\displaystyle z_{t}-\Delta z+\left[k+a\left(\int_{\Omega}e^{kt}z(\cdot,x)\,dx\right)\right]z=e^{-kt}v\chi_{\omega}.

Consequently, by choosing

Optimal control problems have been widely studied in various fields of science

due to their practical applications in designing optimal control strategies for complex systems. Nonlinear optimal control problems are particular challenging, since the associated objective function can be non convex, leading to multiple local minima. In addition, the first order necessary optimality conditions should be complemented by a second order analysis.

Second-order analysis for nonlinear partial differential equations has been studied by several authors, see e.g.
[

,

,

,

,

,

and the references therein. This type of analysis is used to ensure the stability of optimal solutions with respect to perturbations of problems such as finite element discretizations
[

]
. Regarding the second-order analysis for optimal control problems such as (
1.1
)-(
1.4
), we refer to
[

]
where the authors studied the measure control of a semilinear equation with a nonlocal time delay, and to the very recent paper
[

where the authors investigated the control of a parabolic equation with memory.

In this paper, we consider an optimal control problem for a parabolic equation with a nonlocal nonlinearity. The system is described by a partial differential equation involving a nonlinear term that depends on the solution and its integral over the domain. The nonlocal nonlinearity in the system arises due to the presence of the spatial integral operator, which makes the system more complex and challenging to analyze. Let us point some of them:

•

In general, the maximum principle may not hold for nonlocal equations such as (
1.4
). The reason for this is that nonlocal equations involve integral operators that account for the effect of the entire domain, rather than just the local behavior at a given point. Hence, we carefully establish the boundedness of the solution to (
1.4
) in theorems
3.3
and
3.5
.

•

Unlike in other types of problems, the adjoint system (
5.11
) is not of the same type as (
1.4
). Consequently, the well-posedness of the adjoint problem and the boundedness of its solution cannot be deduced from the well-posedness of (
1.4
) and the boundedness of its solution. Therefore, it is necessary to establish these results separately, as done in Proposition
5.3
and theorems
3.2
and
3.4
.

Our objective is to minimize the cost functional (
1.2
), including the difference between the solution and a desired target, as well as the
L^{2}

1.4

)-(
1.4
).

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2

5.3.
First order necessary optimality conditions

The aim of this section is to derive the first-order necessary optimality conditions and to characterize the optimal control. We have the following result.

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
u\in\mathcal{U}_{ad}
be an
L^{\infty}
-local minimum for (
1.1
). Assume that Assumptions
1.1
hold. Then,

\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(u)(v-u)\geq 0\;\;\;\text{for every}\;\;\;v\in\mathcal{U}_{ad},

(5.29)

equivalently

\int_{\omega_{T}}(q+\mu u)(v-u)\,\mathrm{d}x\geq 0\;\;\;\text{for every}\;\;\;v\in\mathcal{U}_{ad},

(5.30)

where

is the unique weak solution to (

).

Proof.

Let
v\in\mathcal{U}_{ad}
be arbitrary. Since
\mathcal{U}_{ad}
is convex, we have that

for all
\lambda\in(0,1]
. But
u
is an
L^{\infty}
-local minimum so,
\mathcal{J}(u+\lambda(v-u))\geq\mathcal{J}(u)
. Hence,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{J}(u+\lambda(v-u))-\mathcal{J}(u)}{\lambda}\geq 0
for all

Letting

in the latter inequality, we obtain (
5.29
). Thanks to (
5.23
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\omega_{T}}(q+\mu u)(v-u)\,\mathrm{d}x\geq 0\;\;\;\text{for every}\;\;\;v\in\mathcal{U}_{ad}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.9

∎

7.
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we studied the optimal control of a nonlinear parabolic system. In contrast to many studies, the nonlinearity of the system (
1.4
) involves an nonlocal term and so the problem is more challenging. We proved rigorously some regularity results of systems considered and established the existence as well as the characterization of the optimal solutions. Because of the nonlinearity, the functional defined in (
1.2
) was non convex and the first order optimality conditions was necessary but not sufficient for the optimality. We then derived the second optimality conditions and obtained by the way, the local uniqueness of the optimal solutions. As a perspective, one could consider nonlocal problems with a control appearing nonlinearly in the state equation. More precisely, it means to consider, the right hand side of (
1.4
) on the form
F(x,y,v)
. This has been done recently in
[
17
]
for an elliptic equation and in
[
29
]
for class of semilinear fractional elliptic equations. One of the main novelty relies on the existence proof of optimal controls which differ from the standard one that is based on minimizing sequences of controls and their weak convergence. Here, the proof uses the measurable selection theorem
[
5
]
. Another important task will be to derive the first- and second- order conditions for optimality.

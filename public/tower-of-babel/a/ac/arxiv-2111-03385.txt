A monotonicity result for the first Steklov-Dirichlet Laplacian eigenvalue
Nunzia Gavitone, Gianpaolo Piscitelli

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider the first Steklov-Dirichlet eigenvalue of the Laplace operator in annular domains with a spherical hole. We prove a monotoni- city result with respect to the hole, when the outer region is centrally symmetric. MSC 2020: Primary: 35J25, 35P15; Secondary: 28A75, 49Q10. Keywords : Laplacian eigenvalue, Steklov-Dirichlet boundary conditions, Shape derivative.



1.
Introduction and main result

In recent years, the study of eigenvalue problems in holed domains has been the object of much interest. These kind of problems are usually defined in annular domains with the outer region and the hole satisfying suitable assumptions. Specifically, different boundary conditions can be imposed on the outer and inner boundary and hence several optimization problems can be studied (e.g. Robin-Neumann
[
PPT
]
, Neumann-Robin
[
DP
]
, Dirichlet-Neumann
[
AA
,
AAK
]
, Steklov-Dirichlet
[
PPS
,
GPPS
,
HLS
]
, Steklov-Robin
[
GS
]
). At the mean time, the optimal placement of an obstacle has been studied, so as to maximize or minimize a prescribed functional (e.g. the Dirichlet heat content
[
L
]
, the first Steklov eigenvalue
[
F
]
).

In this paper, we consider an eigenvalue problem for the Laplace operator in a suitable annular domain with outer Steklov and inner Dirichlet boundary conditions.

More precisely, let
\Omega_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, be an open bounded set with Lipschitz boundary and
B_{r}(y)
be the ball of radius
r>0
, centered at
y
, such that
B_{r}(y)\subset\abxring{\Omega}_{0}
. We study the following Steklov-Dirichlet eigenvalue problem

(1.1)

\sigma(\Omega)=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in H^{1}_{\partial B_{r}(y)}(\Omega)\\
v\not\equiv 0\end{subarray}}\left\{\int_{\Omega}|Dv|^{2}\;dx,\,\|v\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{0})}=1\right\},

where
H^{1}_{\partial B_{r}(y)}(\Omega)
denotes the set of Sobolev functions which vanish on the boundary of
B_{r}(y)
(see Section 2 for the precise definition). If
u\in H^{1}_{\partial B_{r}(y)}(\Omega)
is a minimizer of (
1.1
), then it satisfies:

(1.2)

\begin{cases}\Delta u=0&\mbox{in}\ \Omega\\
\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=\sigma\,u&\mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega_{0}\\
u=0&\mbox{on}\ \partial B_{r}(y),\end{cases}

where
\Omega
is the annular domain
\Omega=\Omega_{0}\setminus\overline{B_{r}(y)}
,
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
and
\nu
is the outer unit normal to
\partial\Omega_{0}
.

In
[
PPS
]
(see also
[
D
,
HLS
]
), the authors prove that the minimum in (
1.1
) is achieved by a function
u\in H^{1}_{\partial B_{r}(y)}(\Omega)
, which is a weak solution to problem (
1.2
) with constant sign in
\Omega
, and that
\sigma(\Omega)
is simple.
Furthermore, the authors prove that, keeping the measure of
\Omega
and the radius of the inner ball
r
fixed,
\sigma(\Omega)
is maximized, among quasi-spherical sets, when
\Omega
is a spherical shell, that is when
\Omega_{0}
is a ball with the same center of the hole. On the other hand, in
[
GPPS
]
the authors extend this result to a class of annular sets with a suitable convex outer domain
\Omega_{0}
.

More properties are known when
\Omega
is an eccentric spherical shell, that is, when the outer domain
\Omega_{0}
is a ball not necessarly centered at the same point of the spherical hole.
In
[
VS
]
, the authors study the optimal placement of the hole in eccentric spherical shell
\Omega
so that
\sigma(\Omega)
is maximized when the outer ball and the inner radius are fixed. If
n\geq 3
, they prove that
\sigma(\Omega)
achieves the maximum when the two balls are concentric. Subsequently, this result has been also proved for any dimension
n\geq 2
in
[
F
]
, by using different proofs (see also
[
S
]
for an analogous result in two-points homogeneous spaces).

Moreover, by performing numerical experiments, the authors in
[
HLS
]
exhibit that
\sigma(\Omega)
is monotone decreasing with respect to the distance between the centers of the two disks. Our aim is to prove that this monotonicity property holds in any dimension and in a more general setting.

Through this paper, we assume that the outer domain
\Omega_{0}
verifies the following hypotheses.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\Omega_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, be an open, bounded set with Lipschitz boundary and centrally symmetric with respect to
x_{0}\in\abxring{\Omega}_{0}
, that is there exists
x^{\prime}\in\overline{\Omega_{0}}
such that
\frac{1}{2}\left(x+x^{\prime}\right)=x_{0}
for any
x\in\overline{\Omega_{0}}
.

Let
r>0
be fixed, our first question is

(\mathcal{Q}_{1})

Where we have to place the center of the spherical hole with fixed radius
r
in order to maximize
\sigma(\Omega)
?

To give an answer, let
w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a unit vector and let us consider the holes
B_{r}(t)
with the centers on the
w
-direction:

(1.3)

B_{r}(t)=\left\{x(t)=x+tw,\,x\in B_{r}(x_{0})\right\}\hskip 10.00002pt0\leq t<\rho_{w}(x_{0},r)-r,

where

(1.4)

\rho_{w}=\rho_{w}(x_{0},r)=\sup\{t>0\colon B_{r}(x_{0}+tw)\subset\abxring{\Omega}\}.

We stress that
\rho_{w}
is the distance between
x_{0}
and the center of the farthest ball from
x_{0}
, well contained in
\Omega_{0}
, in the direction
w
passing at
x_{0}
.
Then we consider the following type of annular domains

(1.5)

\Omega(t)=\Omega_{0}\setminus\overline{B_{r}(t)},

where
\Omega_{0}
verifies the assumption of Definition
1.1
. We stress that when
\Omega_{0}
is a ball, then the sets
\Omega(t)
are eccentric spherical shells. Furthermore, for any
0\leq t<\rho_{w}(x_{0},r)-r
, we denote by
\sigma(t)
the first Steklov-Dirichlet eigenvalue of the Laplacian in
\Omega(t)
, that is

(1.6)

\sigma(t)=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in H^{1}_{\partial B_{r}(t)}(\Omega(t))\\
v\not\equiv 0\end{subarray}}\left\{\int_{\Omega(t)}|Dv|^{2}\;dx,\ \,\|v\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{0})}=1\right\}.

Our second questions is

(\mathcal{Q}_{2})

Is
\sigma(t)
decreasing with respect to
t
?

Our main result gives an answer to both questions
(\mathcal{Q}_{1})
and
(\mathcal{Q}_{2})
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega_{0}
be as in Definition
1.1
,
w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a unit vector,
B_{r}(t)
,
\rho_{w}
,
\Omega(t)
and
\sigma(t)
be defined as in (
1.3
), (
1.4
), (
1.5
) and (
1.6
), respectively. Then,
\sigma(t)
is strictly monotone decreasing with respect to
t\in[0,\rho_{w}-r)
.

As an immediate consequence of our main result, we obtain that, in order to maximize
\sigma(t)
, the hole has to be centered at the symmetry point of
\Omega_{0}
, when the inner radius is fixed.

To prove Theorem
1.2
, we use a shape derivative approach. In particular we compute the first and the second domain derivatives of the first Steklov-Dirichlet eigenvalue. We emphasize that our result implies that the monotonicity property holds also for eccentric spherical shell in any dimension and in particular in two dimensions, as suggested by the numerical computation contained in
[
HLS
]
.

Finally, we describe the outline of the paper. In Section
2
, we summarize some results about the first Steklov-Dirichlet eigenvalue problems. In Section
3
, we compute the first shape derivative of
\sigma(t)
, observing that a stationary set occurs when the center of the hole coincides with the center of symmetry of the outer region. Finally, in Section
4
, we compute the second shape derivative of
\sigma(t)
and prove the main result.

2.
The Steklov-Dirichlet Laplacian eigenvalue problem

In this Section, we study the Steklov-Dirichlet Laplacian eigenvalue problem both on fixed domain
\Omega
and on a parameter-dependent domain
\Omega(t)

2.1.
Foundation of the problem

Let
\Omega_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open, bounded set with Lipschitz boundary and such that
B_{r}(y)\subset\abxring{\Omega}_{0}
, where
B_{r}(y)
is the ball of radius
r>0
centered at
y
. Let us consider the annular domain
\Omega:=\Omega_{0}\setminus\overline{B_{r}(y)}
. In what follows we denote the set of Sobolev functions on
\Omega
vanishing on
\partial B_{r}(y)
by
H^{1}_{\partial B_{r}(y)}(\Omega)
, that is (see
[

]
) the closure in
H^{1}(\Omega)
of the following set

C^{\infty}_{\partial B_{r}(y)}(\Omega):=\{u|_{\Omega}\ |\ u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\ \spt(u)\cap\partial B_{r}(y)=\emptyset\}.

It is known (see for instance
[
A
,
D
,
P
]
) that the spectrum of the Steklov-Dirichlet eigenvalue problem (
1.2
) for the Laplace operator in
\Omega
is discrete and that the sequence of eigenvalues can be ordered as follows

0<\sigma(\Omega)\leq\sigma_{2}(\Omega)\leq\sigma_{3}(\Omega)\leq\ldots\nearrow+\infty.

In particular, the first eigenvalue
\sigma(\Omega)
has the variational characterization (
1.1
), see for instance
[
D
,
PPS
,
HLS
]
.
Moreover in
[
PPS
]
(see also
[
HLS
]
), the authors prove the following result.

Proposition 2.1
.

There exists a function
u\in H^{1}_{\partial B_{r}(y)}(\Omega)
which achieves the minimum in (
1.1
) and is a weak solution to the problem (
1.2
). Moreover
\sigma(\Omega)
is simple and the first eigenfunctions have constant sign in
\Omega
.

In order to prove our main result, we need to compute the first and the second shape derivative of
\sigma(\Omega)
and, to study these derivatives, we need to consider a family of domains approaching to
\Omega
. In our case, we get the desired monotonicity result by studying the behavior of the involved quantities on the family of domains obtained by moving the hole in a fixed direction.

Differentiability of

Proposition 2.2

\Omega_{0}

Remark 2.3
.

We observe that being
u^{t}
harmonic for any
t\in[0,\rho_{w}-r)
, then
u^{t}\in C^{\infty}\left(\overline{\Omega(t)}\right)

u^{t}

Since, to reach our aims, we need to consider the total and partial derivative of
u^{t}
with respect to the parameter

, we observe that, by (

), we have

(2.5)

\frac{d}{dt}[u^{t}(t,x(t))]=(u^{t})^{\prime}+\langle\nabla u^{t},w\rangle,

where
(u^{t})^{\prime}=\displaystyle\frac{\partial u^{t}}{\partial t}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the usual scalar product in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Recalling that the perturbed hole

is the zero-level set of the function

, then (

(2.6)

(u^{t})^{\prime}=-\frac{\partial u^{t}}{\partial\nu}\,\langle\nu,w\rangle,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ on }\partial B_{r}(t),

for any
t\in[0,\rho_{w}-r)
. Moreover, we observe that it holds

(2.7)

\nu=-\frac{\nabla u^{t}}{|\nabla u^{t}|}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ on }\partial B_{r}(t)

and

(n-1)H=\text{div}\left(\frac{\nabla u^{t}}{|\nabla u^{t}|}\right)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ on }\partial B_{r}(t).

Therefore, we have

(2.8)

\begin{split}\frac{(n-1)}{r}=(n-1)H&=-\frac{1}{|\nabla u^{t}|^{2}}\langle\nabla\left(|\nabla u^{t}|\right),\nabla u^{t}\rangle\\
&=-\frac{1}{|\nabla u^{t}|^{3}}\langle\nabla u^{t}\cdot D^{2}(u^{t}),\nabla u^{t}\rangle\quad\text{ on }\partial B_{r}(t),\end{split}

where

denotes the Hessian matrix of

.
Furthermore, by using (

is a solution to (

\begin{cases}\Delta(u^{t})^{\prime}=0&\mbox{in}\ \Omega(t)\\

\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}(u^{t})^{\prime}\,u^{t}\,\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=0.

3.
The first order Shape Derivative of
\sigma(t)

In this Section, we compute the first oder derivative of the eigenvalue
\sigma(t)
on
\Omega(t)
.
Before doing this, we recall an Hadamard’s formula in the framework of the domain derivative (see for instance
[
SZ
,
B
,
HP
]

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open bounded set with Lipschitz boundary and let
V(x)
a vector field such that
V\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})
. For any
t>0
, let
E(t)=\{x(t)=x+tV(x),\,x\in E\}
, and
f(t,x(t))
be such that
f(t,\cdot)\in W^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and differentiable at
t
.
Then it holds:

(3.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{E(t)}f(t,x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=&\int_{E(t)}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}f(t,x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\\
&+\int_{+\partial E(t)}f(t,x)\langle\nu,V(x)\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1},\end{split}

where
\nu
is the outer unit normal to the boundary of
E(t)
and
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
denotes the Hausdorff measure (see for instance the Chapter 5 of
[
HP
]
). We use formula (
3.1
) to prove the following.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\Omega_{0}
be as in Definition
1.1
,
w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a unit vector,
B_{r}(t)
,
\rho_{w}
,
\Omega(t)
and
\sigma(t)
be defined as in (
1.3
), (
1.4
), (
1.5
) and (
1.6
), respectively. Then, for any
t\in[0,\rho_{w}-r)
, it holds

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\sigma(t)=-\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{r}(t)}|\nabla u^{t}|^{2}\langle w,\nu\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1},

where
u^{t}
the is the normalized, positive eigenfunction corresponding to
\sigma(t)

Proof.

By using the Hadamard’s formula (
3.1
), by observing that the unit outer normals
\nu(x)
to
\partial B_{r}
and
\nu(x(t))
to
\partial B_{r}(t)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\sigma(t)=&\frac{d}{dt}\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega(t)}|\nabla u^{t}|^{2}\,dx\right)\\
=&2\displaystyle\int_{\Omega(t)}\langle\nabla u^{t},\nabla(u^{t})^{\prime}\rangle\,dx+\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{r}(t)}|\nabla u^{t}|^{2}\langle w,\nu\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
=&2\left(\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}(u^{t})^{\prime}\,\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\int_{\partial B_{r}(t)}(u^{t})^{\prime}\,\frac{\partial u^{t}}{\partial\nu}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right)+\\
&+\int_{\partial B_{r}(t)}|\nabla u^{t}|^{2}\langle w,\nu\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
=&2\left(\sigma(t)\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}(u^{t})^{\prime}\ \,u\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-\int_{\partial B_{r}(t)}\left(\frac{\partial u^{t}}{\partial\nu}\right)^{2}\,\langle w,\nu\rangle d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right)+\\
&+\int_{\partial B_{r}(t)}|\nabla u^{t}|^{2}\langle w,\nu\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.\end{split}

By taking into account the relation (
2.10
), we get the conclusion.
∎

In order to obtain our main result, we study the behavior of the first order derivative of
\sigma(t)
for
t=0
. We stress that the symmetry of
\Omega_{0}
has a key role in the proofs of these results.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\Omega_{0}
be as in Definition
1.1
,
w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a unit vector,
B_{r}(t)
,
\rho_{w}
,
\Omega(t)
and
\sigma(t)
be defined as in (
1.3
), (
1.4
), (
1.5
) and (
1.6
), respectively. Then it holds

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}[\sigma(t)]_{t=0}=0.

Proof.

Let
u^{t}
be the normalized, positive eigenfunctions corresponding to
\sigma(0)=\sigma(\Omega)
. We observe that, since
\Omega_{0}
is centrally symmetric with respect the origin (see Definition
1.1
), then

(3.2)

u^{t}(x)=u^{t}(-x)\hskip 10.00002ptx\in\partial B_{r}.

If we denote by
x^{\prime}
the symmetric point of
x
, then (
3.2
) immediately follows by taking
v(x)=u^{t}(x^{\prime})
as test function in (
1.1
).

Finally, by Theorem
3.1
and being
\nu=-\displaystyle\frac{x}{r}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}[\sigma(t)]_{t=0}=-\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{r}}|\nabla u^{t}|^{2}\langle\nu,w\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=0.

∎

4.
The second order Shape Derivative of
\sigma(t)

To prove the main result (Theorem
1.2
), we need a stationary property of the first order derivative (analyzed in the previous Section) and a sign of the second order derivative. We compute the second order domain derivative of
\sigma(t)
, by using the same notations of the previous Section and by recalling some useful definitions from
[
BW
]
.

For any bounded domain
E\in C^{2,\alpha}
and for any
f\in C^{1}(\partial E)
, the tangential derivative of
v
is given by

(4.1)

\nabla^{\tau}f=\nabla f-\langle\nabla f,\nu\rangle\nu\hskip 10.00002pt\text{on }\partial E,

where
\nu
is the unit outer normal. Furthermore, for any smooth vector field
\Psi:\overline{E}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
, the tangential divergence is defined as

(4.2)

\dive_{\partial E}(\Psi)=\dive(\Psi)-\langle\nu,D(\Psi)\nu\rangle\hskip 10.00002pt\text{on }\partial E,

where
D(\Psi)
is the Jacobian matrix of
\Psi
. Then, the Gauss theorem on surfaces holds:

(4.3)

\int_{\partial E}f\dive_{\partial E}\Psi d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=-\int_{\partial E}\langle\Psi,\nabla^{\tau}f\rangle\ d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+(n-1)\int_{\partial E}f\,H\,\langle\Psi,\nu\rangle\ d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

The following Theorem gives the expression of the second order domain derivative of
\sigma(t)
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\Omega_{0}
be as in Definition
1.1
,
w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a unit vector,
B_{r}(t)
,
\rho_{w}
,
\Omega(t)
and
\sigma(t)
be defined as in (
1.3
), (
1.4
), (
1.5
) and (
1.6
), respectively.
Then, for any
t\in[0,\rho_{w}-r)
, it holds

\begin{split}\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\sigma(t)&-\frac{2(n-1)}{r}\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\sigma(t)=-2\int_{B_{r}(t)}|\nabla H\left(|\nabla u^{t}|\langle w,\nu\rangle\right)|^{2}dx\\
&-\dfrac{1}{r}\int_{\partial B_{r}(t)}|\nabla u^{t}|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-\frac{n-2}{r}\int_{\partial B_{r}(t)}|\nabla u^{t}|^{2}\left(\langle w,\nu\rangle\right)^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1},\end{split}

where
u^{t}
the is the normalized positive eigenfunction corresponding to
\sigma(t)
and
H(|\nabla u^{t}|\langle w,\nu\rangle)
is the harmonic extension of
|\nabla u^{t}|\langle w,\nu\rangle
in
\overline{B_{r}(t)}
, defined in (
2.11
).

Proof.

In order to compute the second order derivative,
we first observe that

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{r}(t)}|\nabla u^{t}|^{2}\langle w,\nu\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\int_{\partial\Omega(t)}|\nabla u^{t}|^{2}\langle V,\nu\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1},

where
V
is defined in (
2.1
). Therefore, by using the divergence Theorem, we have

\sigma^{\prime}(t)=-\int_{\Omega(t)}\dive(|\nabla u^{t}|^{2}V)\,dx.

By using the Hadamard’s formula (
3.1
) in the right-hand side, we compute the second order derivative of
\sigma(t)
:

(4.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sigma^{\prime\prime}(t)=-\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega(t)}\dive(|\nabla u^{t}|^{2}V)\,dx=&-\int_{\Omega(t)}\dive\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|\nabla u^{t}|^{2}V\right)\,dx\\
&-\int_{\partial\Omega(t)}\dive(|\nabla u^{t}|^{2}V)\ \langle V,\nu\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.\end{split}

Let us consider the second term in (
4.4
), by using the tangential divergence (
4.2
), we have

(4.5)

\begin{split}&-\int_{\partial\Omega(t)}\dive(|\nabla u^{t}|^{2}V)\langle V,\nu\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=-\int_{\partial B_{r}(t)}\dive(|\nabla u^{t}|^{2}w)\langle w,\nu\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&\quad=-\int_{\partial B_{r}(t)}\dive_{\partial B_{r}(t)}(|\nabla u^{t}|^{2}w)\langle w,\nu\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-\int_{\partial B_{r}(t)}\langle\nu,D(|\nabla u^{t}|^{2}w)\nu\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.\end{split}

Hence, by inserting (
4.5
) in (
4.4
), we have

(4.6)

\begin{split}\sigma^{\prime\prime}(t)=&-\int_{\Omega(t)}\dive\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|\nabla u^{t}|^{2}V\right)\,dx-\int_{\partial B_{r}(t)}\dive_{\partial B_{r}(t)}(|\nabla u^{t}|^{2}w)\langle w,\nu\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&-\int_{\partial B_{r}(t)}\langle\nu,D(|\nabla u^{t}|^{2}w)\nu\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}:=I+II+III.\end{split}

For reader’s convenience, we separately study the terms
I
,
II
and
III
in (
4.6
). Let us focus on
I
; by using the divergence Theorem, we have

(4.7)

\begin{split}I=-\int_{\Omega(t)}\dive\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|\nabla u^{t}|^{2}V\right)\,dx&=-2\int_{\Omega(t)}\dive\left(\langle\nabla u^{t},\nabla(u^{t})^{\prime}\rangle V\right)\,dx\\
&=-2\int_{\partial\Omega(t)}\langle\nabla u^{t},\nabla(u^{t})^{\prime}\rangle\ \langle V,\nu\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&=-2\int_{\partial B_{r}(t)}\langle\nabla u^{t},\nabla(u^{t})^{\prime}\rangle\ \langle w,\nu\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&=-2\int_{\partial B_{r}(t)}\displaystyle\frac{\partial(u^{t})^{\prime}}{\partial\nu}(u^{t})^{\prime}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&=-2\int_{B_{r}(t)}|\nabla H\left(|\nabla u^{t}|\langle w,\nu\rangle\right)|^{2}dx.\end{split}

Let us remark that
\nu
is also the inner unit normal to
\partial B_{r}(t)
.

Now let us consider
II
; by using the Gauss Theorem (
4.3
), we have

(4.8)

\begin{split}II&=-\int_{\partial B_{r}(t)}\dive_{\partial B_{r}(t)}(|\nabla u^{t}|^{2}w)\langle w,\nu\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&=\int_{\partial B_{r}(t)}\langle|\nabla u^{t}|^{2}w,\nabla^{\tau}(\langle w,\nu\rangle)\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-\frac{n-1}{r}\int_{\partial B_{r}(t)}|\nabla u^{t}|^{2}(\langle w,\nu\rangle)^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&=\int_{\partial B_{r}(t)}\langle|\nabla u^{t}|^{2}w,\nabla(\langle w,\nu\rangle)-\langle\nabla(\langle w,\nu\rangle),\nu\rangle\nu\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\ -\frac{n-1}{r}\int_{\partial B_{r}(t)}|\nabla u^{t}|^{2}(\langle w,\nu\rangle)^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&=\int_{\partial B_{r}(t)}\left\langle|\nabla u^{t}|^{2}w,-\frac{w}{r}+\left\langle\frac{w}{r},\nu\right\rangle\nu\right\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-\frac{n-1}{r}\int_{\partial B_{r}(t)}|\nabla u^{t}|^{2}(\langle w,\nu\rangle)^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&=-\frac{1}{r}\int_{\partial B_{r}(t)}|\nabla u^{t}|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\frac{1}{r}\int_{\partial B_{r}(t)}|\nabla u^{t}|^{2}(\langle w,\nu\rangle)^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\ -\frac{n-1}{r}\int_{\partial B_{r}(t)}|\nabla u^{t}|^{2}(\langle w,\nu\rangle)^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&=-\frac{1}{r}\int_{\partial B_{r}(t)}|\nabla u^{t}|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-\frac{n-2}{r}\int_{\partial B_{r}(t)}|\nabla u^{t}|^{2}(\langle w,\nu\rangle)^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1},\end{split}

where in the third line we have used the tangential gradient (
4.1
).

Let us focus on
III
; we have

(4.9)

\begin{split}III&=-\int_{\partial B_{r}(t)}\langle\nu,D(|\nabla u^{t}|^{2}w)\nu\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=-2\int_{\partial B_{r}(t)}\langle\nu,D^{2}(u^{t})\nabla u^{t}\rangle\ \langle w,\nu\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&=2\int_{\partial B_{r}(t)}\dfrac{1}{|\nabla u^{t}|^{3}}\langle\nabla u^{t},D^{2}(u^{t})\nabla u^{t}\rangle|\nabla u^{t}|^{2}\langle w,\nu\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&=-\dfrac{2(n-1)}{r}\int_{\partial B_{r}(t)}|\nabla u^{t}|^{2}\langle w,\nu\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1},\end{split}

where we have used the relations (
2.7
) and (
2.8
).
The conclusion follows by using (
4.7
), (
4.8
) and (
4.9
) in (
4.6
).
∎

Finally, the proof of the main Theorem is a direct consequence of the computed expressions of the first and second order shape derivative of the first Steklov-Dirichlet eigenvalue.

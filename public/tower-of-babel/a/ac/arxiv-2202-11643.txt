Abstract
. This work deals with the a posteriori error estimates for the Darcy-Forchheimer problem. We introduce the corresponding variational formulation and discretize it by using the finite-element method. A posteriori error estimate with two types of computable error indicators is showed. The first one is linked to the linearization and the second one to the discretization. Finally, numerical computations are performed to show the effectiveness of the error indicators.



1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open domain of
{\rm I\hskip-2.15277ptR}^{d}
(
d=2,3
) with Lipschitz-continuous boundary
\Gamma=\partial\Omega
. We consider the Darcy-Forchheimer equation

(1.1)

\displaystyle\frac{\mu}{\rho}K^{-1}{\bf u}+\frac{\beta}{\rho}|{\bf u}|{\bf u}+\nabla p={\bf f}\;\;\;\;\;\mbox{in}\;\;\;\Omega,

with the divergence constraint

(1.2)

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}{\bf u}=b\;\;\;\;\;\mbox{in}\;\;\;\Omega{\color[rgb]{0,0,1},}

and the boundary condition

(1.3)

\displaystyle{\bf u}\cdot{\bf n}=g\;\;\;\;\;\mbox{on}\;\;\partial\Omega,

where
{\bf u}
represents the velocity,
p
represents the pressure,
{\bf n}
is the unit exterior normal vector to
\Gamma
,
|.|
denotes the Euclidean norm and
|{\bf u}|^{2}={\bf u}\cdot{\bf u}
. The parameters
\rho
,
\mu
and
\beta
represent the density of the fluid, its viscosity and its dynamic viscosity, respectively.
\beta
is also referred as Forchheimer number which is a scalar positive constant.
K
is the permeability tensor, assumed to be uniformly positive definite and bounded such that there exists two positive real numbers
K_{m}
and
K_{M}
verifying

(1.4)

0<K_{m}\,{\bf{x}}\cdot{\bf{x}}\leq(K^{-1}({\bf{x}}){\bf{x}})\cdot{\bf{x}}\leq K_{M}\,{\bf{x}}\cdot{\bf{x}}.

For the compatibility, we suppose that
b
and
g
verify the following compatibility condition:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}b({\bf{x}})d{\bf{x}}=\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\mbox{g}(s)\,ds.

We denote by Problem
(P)
the system of equations ((
1.1
), (
1.2
), (
1.3
)).

Darcy’s law describes the creeping flow of Newtonian fluids in porous media. It is represented by Equation (
1.1
) without the non-linear term
\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{\rho}|{\bf u}|{\bf u}
and is valid by experiment under the condition that the creeping velocity is low and the porosity and permeability are small enough by Darcy in
1856

[
3
]
. Forchheimer
[
25
]
conducted flow experiments in sandpacks and recognized that when the velocity is higher and the porosity is nonuniform, Darcy’s equation can be replaced by Equation (
1.1
). A theoretical derivation of Forchheimer’s law can be found in
[
32
]
.

Multiple works approximated the Darcy-Forchheimer equation by using finite element methods. Girault and Wheeler
[
17
]
approximated the velocity by piecewise constants and the pressure by Crouzeix-Raviart element. They also proposed and studied an alternating directions iterative method to solve the system of nonlinear equations obtained by finite element discretizaton. Lopez and al.
[
28
]
carried out numerical tests of the methods studied in
[
17
]
in order to corroborate the results presented there. In
[
31
]
, the authors proposed and studied a mixed element approximation: the Raviart-Thomas mixed element, Brezzi-Douglas-Marini mixed element. Salas J. et al.
[
33
]
presented a theoretical study of the mixed finite element space, as proposed in
[
28
]
. In
[
34
]
, we considered the Darcy-Forchheimer problem coupled with the convection-diffusion-reaction problem. We established existence of solutions by using a Galerkin method and we proved uniqueness. Then, we introduced and analyzed a numerical scheme based on the finite element method and we derived an optimal
a priori
error estimates for the proposed numerical scheme.
In this work, we introduce a numerical iterative scheme, show the corresponding convergence, establish the corresponding
a posteriori
error estimates and show corresponding numerical investigations.

The present work investigates
a posteriori
error estimates for the finite element discretization of Darcy-Forchheimer problem.These estimates can be used to evaluate the solution errors of the discrete problem without requiring any a priori information on the exact solution. Indeed, the
a posteriori
analysis controls the overall discretization error of a problem by providing error indicators that are easy to compute. Once these error indicators are constructed, their efficiency can be proven by bounding each indicator by the local error.
A posteriori
analysis was first introduced by I. Babu
\check{s}
ka
[
5
]
, developed by R. Verfürth
[
37
]
, and has been the object of a large number of publications.
A posteriori
error estimations have been studied for several types of partial differential equations such that the Stokes or Navier-Stokes equation (see for instance
[
37
,
10
,
4
,
1
,
12
,
13
,
19
]
), the Maxwell and Lamé equations
[
30
,
8
]
. Many works have been established for the Darcy flow, see for instance
[
2
,
11
,
14
,
29
]
. In
[
15
]
, Chen and Wang establish optimal
a poteriori
error estimates for the
H({\rm div},\Omega)
conforming mixed finite element method applied to the coupled Darcy-Stokes system in two dimensions. For the Darcy equations with pressure dependent viscosity, we refer to
[
26
]
and the references therein. In
[
20
]
, we establish
a posteriori
error estimates for Darcy’s problem coupled with the heat equation. In this paper, we study the
posteriori
error estimates corresponding to Problem
(P)
and show corresponding numerical results.

—

—

a posteriori

—

2.
Variational formulation

In this section, we begin by introducing several definitions and notations in order to write the weak formulation corresponding to Problem
(P)
.

Let
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots\alpha_{d})
be a
d
-uplet of non negative
integers, set
|\alpha|=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}\alpha_{i}
, and define the partial
derivative
\partial^{\alpha}
by

\partial^{\alpha}=\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{|\alpha|}}{\partial x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial x_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\dots\partial x_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}}.

Then, for any positive integer
m
and any number
q\geq 1
, we recall
the classical Sobolev space

(2.1)

W^{m,q}(\Omega)=\{v\in L^{q}(\Omega);\,\forall\,|\alpha|\leq m,\;\partial^{\alpha}v\in L^{q}(\Omega)\},

equipped with the seminorm

(2.2)

|v|_{W^{m,q}(\Omega)}=\big\{\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\int_{\Omega}|\partial^{\alpha}v|^{q}\,d{\bf{x}}\,\big\}^{\frac{1}{q}}

and the norm

(2.3)

\|v\|_{W^{m,q}(\Omega)}=\big\{\sum_{0\leq k\leq m}|v|_{W^{k,q}(\Omega)}^{q}\big\}^{\frac{1}{q}}.

When
q=2
, this space is the Hilbert space
H^{m}(\Omega)
.
In
particular, the scalar product of
L^{2}(\Omega)
is denoted by

(.,.)
. Furthermore, we recall the following standard space for Darcy’s equations:

L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)=\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega);\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}v\,d{\bf{x}}=0\}.

The definitions of these spaces are extended straightforwardly to
vectors, with the same notation, but with the following
modification for the norms in the non-Hilbert case. Let
{\bf v}
be a
vector valued function; we set

(2.4)

\|{\bf v}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}=\big(\int_{\Omega}|{\bf v}|^{q}\,d{\bf{x}}\,\big)^{\frac{1}{q}},

where
|.|
denotes the Euclidean vector norm.

We introduce the spaces:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0ptX=L^{3}(\Omega)^{d},\\
M=W^{1,\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega)\cap L^{2}_{0}(\Omega),\\
{H=\left\{{\bf v}\in X;\operatorname{div}{\bf v}\in L^{3d/(d+3)}(\Omega)\right\},}\\
{V=\{{\bf v}\in X;\forall q\in M,\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla q\cdot{\bf v}\,d{\bf{x}}=0\}.}\end{array}

The space
H
endowed with the graph norm is complete. Moreover

D(\overline{\Omega})
is dense in
H
, and
{\bf v}\cdot{\bf n}
belongs to
W^{1,3/2}(\Gamma)
for all
v\in H

[
35
]
.
We have the following inf-sup condition (see
[
17
]
for the proof)

(2.5)

\underset{q\in M}{\inf}\;\;\underset{{\bf v}\in X}{\sup}\;\;\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla q\cdot{\bf v}\,d{\bf{x}}}{||\nabla q||_{L^{3/2}(\Omega)}||{\bf v}||_{L^{3}(\Omega)}}=1.

The velocity
{\bf u}
and pressure
p
of Problem
(P)
are considered respectively in
H
and
M
, while
b
and
g
are assumed to be respectively in
L^{3d/(d+3)}(\Omega)
and
L^{3(d-1)/d}(\Gamma)
(see
[
17
]
for details).

In order to write the variational formulation associated to Problem
(P)
, we introduce the mapping
{\bf v}\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}({\bf v})
defined by:

\begin{array}[]{rccl}\mathcal{A}:&L^{3}(\Omega)^{d}&\mapsto&L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega)^{d}\\
&{\bf v}&\mapsto&\mathcal{A}({\bf v})=\displaystyle\frac{\mu}{\rho}K^{-1}{\bf v}+\frac{\beta}{\rho}|{\bf v}|{\bf v}.\end{array}

We refer to
[
17
,
24
]
for the following properties of
\mathcal{A}
.

Property 2.1
.

We have the following monotonicity properties:

(1)

for all
{\bf v}\in L^{3}(\Omega)^{d}
,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\frac{\mu}{\rho}\int_{\Omega}K^{-1}{\bf v}\cdot{\bf v}\,d{\bf{x}}\geq\displaystyle\frac{\mu K_{m}}{\rho}\left\|{\bf v}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

(2)

for all
{\bf v},{\bf w}\in L^{3}(\Omega)^{d}
,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{\rho}\int_{\Omega}(|{\bf v}|{\bf v}-|{\bf w}|{\bf w})({\bf v}-{\bf w})\,d{\bf{x}}\geq c_{m}\left\|{\bf v}-{\bf w}\right\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}^{3}.

where
c_{m}
is a strictly positive constant.

Recall the following Green’s formula
[
35
]

(2.8)

\forall q\in M,\;\forall v\in H,\quad\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla q\cdot{\bf v}\,d{\bf{x}}=\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}q\operatorname{div}vd{\bf{x}}+\int_{\Gamma}q{\bf v}\cdot{\bf n}ds.

Using the Green formula (
2.8
) one can show that Problem
(P)
is equivalent to the following variational formulation: Find
({\bf u},p)\in X\times M
such that

(2.9)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\forall{\bf v}\in X,\quad\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{A}({\bf u})\cdot{\bf v}\,d{\bf{x}}+\int_{\Omega}\nabla p\cdot{\bf v}\,d{\bf{x}}=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}{\bf f}\cdot{\bf v}\,d{\bf{x}},\\
\forall q\in M,\quad\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla q\cdot{\bf u}\,d{\bf{x}}=-\int_{\Omega}b\,q\,d{\bf{x}}+\int_{\Gamma}g\,q\,ds.\end{array}\right.

It can be demonstrated that, for all
b\in L^{3d/(d+3)}(\Omega)
and
g\in L^{3(d-1)/d}(\Gamma)
, there is only one
{\bf u}_{l}\in{L^{3}(\Omega)/V}
such that

(2.10)

\forall q\in M,\,\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla q\cdot{\bf u}_{l}\,d{\bf{x}}=-\int_{\Omega}b\,q\,d{\bf{x}}+\int_{\Gamma}g\,q\,ds,

and

\displaystyle||{\bf u}_{l}||_{L^{3}(\Omega)^{d}/V}\leq C\big(||b||_{L^{3d/(d+3)}(\Omega)}+||g||_{L^{3(d-1)/d}(\Gamma)}\big),

where
C>0
is a constant depending only on
\Omega
and
d
.

Using The Banach-Necas-Babu
\check{s}
ka Theorem (see for instance
[
6
]
, Theorem A.1 in

[
36
]
) one can show that (
2.9
), and equivalently the problem
(P)
admits a unique solution
({\bf u},p)\in X\times M
satisfying the relations

(2.11)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt||{\bf u}||_{L^{3}(\Omega)}\leq C\Big(||{\bf u}_{l}||_{L^{3}(\Omega)}^{3}+||{\bf u}_{l}||_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+||{\bf f}||_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\Big)^{1/3},\\
||\nabla p||_{L^{3/2}(\Omega)}\leq C\Big(||{\bf u}||_{L^{3/2}(\Omega)}+||{\bf u}||_{L^{3}(\Omega)}^{2}+||{\bf f}||_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Big).\end{array}

For further details of the weak formulation corresponding to Problem
(P)
presented in this section, We refer to
[
17
]
.

3.
Discretization and
a posteriori
estimation

In this section, we introduce an iterative numerical scheme to solve the weak variational formulation (
2.9
) of the Problem
(P)
, and study its convergence. We establish the corresponding
a posteriori
error estimate in a second step.

From now on, we assume that
\Omega
is a polygon when
d=2
or
polyhedron when
d=3
, so it can be completely meshed. For
the space discretization, we consider a
regular (see Ciarlet
[
16
]
) family of triangulations

(\mathcal{T}_{h})_{h}
of
\Omega
which is a set of closed non degenerate
triangles for
d=2
or tetrahedra for
d=3
, called elements,
satisfying,

—

for each
h
,
\overline{\Omega}
is the union of all elements of

\mathcal{T}_{h}
;

—

the intersection of two distinct elements of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
is
either empty, a common vertex, or an entire common edge (or face
when
d=3
);

—

the ratio of the diameter
h_{\kappa}
of an element
\kappa
in
\mathcal{T}_{h}
to
the diameter
\rho_{\kappa}
of its inscribed circle when
d=2
or ball when
d=3

is bounded by a constant independent of
h
: there exists a positive constant
\sigma
independent of
h
such that,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\max_{\kappa\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\frac{h_{\kappa}}{\rho_{\kappa}}\leq\sigma.

As usual,
h
denotes the maximal diameter
of all elements of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
. To define the finite element functions, let
r
be a non negative integer. For each
\kappa
in

\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we denote by
{\rm I\hskip-2.15277ptP}_{r}(\kappa)
the space of restrictions to
\kappa
of polynomials in
d
variables and total
degree at most
r
, with a similar notation on the faces or edges of
\kappa
. For every edge (when
d=2
) or face (when
d=3
)
e
of the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we denote by
h_{e}
the diameter of
e
.

We shall use the following inverse inequality: for any numbers
p,q\geq 2
, for any dimension
d
, and for any non negative integer
r
, there exist constants
C_{I}(p)
and
C_{J}(q)
such that for any polynomial function
v_{h}
of degree
r
on an element
\kappa
or an edge (when
d=2
) or face (when
d=3
)
e
of the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
,

(3.2)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\|v_{h}\|_{L^{p}(\kappa)}\leq C_{I}(p)h_{\kappa}^{\frac{d}{p}-\frac{d}{2}}\|v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\kappa)}\\
\mbox{and}\\
\|v_{h}\|_{L^{q}(e)}\leq C_{J}(q)h_{e}^{\frac{d-1}{q}-\frac{d-1}{2}}\|v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(e)}.\end{array}

C_{I}
and
C_{J}
depend on the regularity parameter
\sigma
of (
3.1
). In all the rest of the paper and for the simplicity, we will omit the dependency of
C_{I}
and
C_{J}
with
p
and
q
.

Let
X_{h}\subset X
and
M_{h}\subset M
be the discrete spaces corresponding to the velocity and the pressure given by:

(3.3)

\begin{split}Z_{h}=&\{q_{h}\in C^{0}(\bar{\Omega});\,\forall\,\kappa\in\mathcal{T}_{h},\;q_{h}|_{\kappa}\in{\rm I\hskip-2.15277ptP}_{1}\},\\
X_{h}=&\{{\bf v}_{h}\in L^{2}(\bar{\Omega})^{d};\,\forall\,\kappa\in\mathcal{T}_{h},\;{\bf v}_{h}|_{\kappa}\in{\rm I\hskip-2.15277ptP}_{0}^{d}\},\\
M_{h}=&Z_{h}\cap L^{2}_{0}(\Omega).\end{split}

They satisfy the following inf-sup condition (see
[
33
]
):

(3.4)

\forall\,q_{h}\in M_{h},\;\sup_{{\bf v}_{h}\in X_{h}}\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla q_{h}\cdot{\bf v}_{h}\,d{\bf{x}}\,}{\|{\bf v}_{h}\|_{X}}\geq\beta_{1}\|q_{h}\|_{M_{h}},

where
\beta_{1}
is a positive constant independent of
h
.

Problem (
2.9
) can be discretized as following:

(3.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\forall{\bf v}_{h}\in X_{h},\quad\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{A}({\bf u}_{h})\cdot{\bf v}_{h}\,d{\bf{x}}+\int_{\Omega}\nabla p_{h}\cdot{\bf v}_{h}\,d{\bf{x}}=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}{\bf f}\cdot{\bf v}_{h}\,d{\bf{x}},\\
\forall q_{h}\in M_{h},\quad\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla q_{h}\cdot{\bf u}_{h}\,d{\bf{x}}=-\int_{\Omega}bq_{h}\,d{\bf{x}}+\int_{\Gamma}gq_{h}\,ds.\end{array}\right.

It is shown in
[
33
]
that there exists a unique
{\bf u}_{h,l}\in X_{h}
such that

(3.6)

\forall q_{h}\in M_{h},\quad\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla q_{h}\cdot{\bf u}_{h,l}\,d{\bf{x}}=\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}b\,q_{h}d{\bf{x}}+\int_{\Gamma}g\,q_{h}ds,

and
{\bf u}_{h,l}
satisfies the following bound,

(3.7)

||{\bf u}_{h,l}||_{L^{3}(\Omega)^{d}}\leq C_{l}\big(||b||_{L^{\frac{3d}{3+d}}(\Omega)}+||g||_{L^{\frac{3(d-1)}{d}}(\Gamma)}\big).

It is also shown in
[
33
]
that Problem (
3.5
) admits a unique solution
({\bf u}_{h},p_{h})\in X_{h}\times M_{h}
satisfying exactly similar bounds as (
2.11
). We refer to
[
33
]
for the proof of the following
a priori
error estimates:

Theorem 3.1
.

The solutions
({\bf u},p)
of (
2.9
) and
({\bf u}_{h},p_{h})
of
(
3.5
)
verify the following a priori error:

If
({\bf u},p)\in W^{1,4}(\Omega)^{d}\times W^{2,3/2}(\Omega)
, then there exists strictly positive constants
C_{u},C_{p}
independent of
h
such that

(3.8)

\begin{array}[]{ll}||{\bf u}-{\bf u}_{h}||_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{u}h,\\
||\nabla(p-p_{h})||_{3/2,\Omega}\leq C_{p}h.\end{array}

3.1.
Iterative algorithm:

To compute the solution of the non-linear problem (
3.5
), we introduce the following iterative algorithm:
for a given initial guess
{\bf u}_{h}^{0}\in X_{h}
and having
{\bf u}_{h}^{i}
at each iteration
i
, we compute
({\bf u}_{h}^{i+1},p^{i+1}_{h})
solution of

(3.9)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\forall{\bf v}_{h}\in X_{h},\quad\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\alpha({\bf u}_{h}^{i+1}-{\bf u}_{h}^{i})\cdot{\bf v}_{h}\,d{\bf{x}}+\frac{\mu}{\rho}\int_{\Omega}K^{-1}{\bf u}^{i+1}_{h}\cdot{\bf v}_{h}\,d{\bf{x}}+\frac{\beta}{\rho}\int_{\Omega}|{\bf u}^{i}_{h}|{\bf u}^{i+1}_{h}\cdot{\bf v}_{h}\,d{\bf{x}}\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\hskip 142.26378pt+\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla p^{i+1}_{h}\cdot{\bf v}_{h}\,d{\bf{x}}=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}{\bf f}\cdot{\bf v}_{h}\,d{\bf{x}},\\
\forall q_{h}\in M_{h},\quad\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla q_{h}\cdot{\bf u}^{i+1}_{h}\,d{\bf{x}}=-\int_{\Omega}bq_{h}\,d{\bf{x}}+\int_{\Gamma}gq_{h}\,ds,\end{array}\right.

We next address the convergence of Scheme (
3.9
). The analysis of convergence has two principal steps. The first is to derive an uniform bound to the numerical iterative solution
with respect to the iteration index. Then the obtained
bound is used to study the convergence of a linear explicit scheme.

Theorem 3.2
.

Problem
(\ref{V1hi})
admits a unique solution
({\bf u}_{h}^{i+1},p_{h}^{i+1})\in X_{h}\times M_{h}
. Furthermore, if the initial value
{\bf u}_{h}^{0}
satisfies the condition

(3.10)

{||{\bf u}_{h}^{0}||_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\displaystyle L_{1}({\bf f},{\bf u}_{h,l},\alpha),}

where

(3.11)

\displaystyle L_{1}({\bf f},{\bf u}_{h,l},\alpha)=\displaystyle\left(\ell_{0}+\ell_{1}\alpha\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

with

(3.12)

\displaystyle\ell_{0}={\left(\frac{2\rho}{\mu K_{m}}\right)^{2}}\Big(\displaystyle(\frac{3\rho}{2\mu K_{m}}+\frac{1}{2})||{\bf f}||^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3\mu K_{M}^{2}}{2\rho K_{m}})||{\bf u}_{h,l}||^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{4\beta}{3\rho}||{\bf u}_{h,l}||^{3}_{L^{3}(\Omega)}\Big),

(3.13)

\displaystyle\ell_{1}={\left(\frac{2\rho}{\mu K_{m}}\right)^{2}}||{\bf u}_{h,l}||^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

and if
\alpha\geq\alpha^{\star}
with
\alpha^{\star}=4(\gamma_{1}+\sqrt{\gamma_{1}^{2}+\gamma_{2}})^{2}
where

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\gamma_{1}&=&\displaystyle\frac{8\beta K_{M}}{3\rho K_{m}}C_{I}^{3}h^{-\frac{d}{2}}\sqrt{\ell_{1}},\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\gamma_{2}&=&\displaystyle\frac{3\beta^{2}}{2\rho\mu K_{m}}{C_{I}^{4}}h^{-\frac{2d}{3}}||{\bf u}_{h,l}||^{2}_{L^{3}(\Omega)}+\frac{8\beta}{3\mu K_{m}}{C_{I}^{3}}h^{-\frac{d}{2}}\|{\bf f}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{2}}\\
&&\displaystyle+\frac{8\beta K_{M}}{3\rho K_{m}}{C_{I}^{3}}h^{-\frac{d}{2}}\sqrt{\ell_{0}}+\frac{8\beta^{2}}{3\rho^{2}}{C_{I}^{6}}h^{-d}\max(\frac{2\rho\ell_{0}}{\mu K_{m}},\ell_{1}),\end{array}

then the solution of Problem
(\ref{V1hi})
satisfies the estimates

(3.14)

{||{\bf u}_{h}^{i+1}||_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\displaystyle L_{1}({\bf f},{\bf u}_{h,l},\alpha),}

and

(3.15)

{||{\bf u}_{h}^{i+1}||^{3}_{L^{3}(\Omega)}\leq\displaystyle\frac{3\mu K_{m}}{2\beta}L_{1}^{2}({\bf f},{\bf u}_{h,l},\alpha).}

Proof.
To prove the existence and uniqueness of the solution of Problem
(\ref{V1hi})
which is a square linear system in finite dimension, it suffices to show the uniqueness. For a given
{\bf u}_{h}^{i}
, let
({\bf u}_{h1}^{i+1},p_{h1}^{i+1})
and
({\bf u}_{h2}^{i+1},p_{h2}^{i+1})
two different solutions of Problem
(\ref{V1hi})
and let
{\bf w}_{h}={\bf u}_{h1}^{i+1}-{\bf u}_{h2}^{i+1}
and
\xi_{h}=p_{h1}^{i+1}-p_{h2}^{i+1}
, then
({\bf w}_{h},\xi_{h})
is the solution of the following problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\forall{\bf v}_{h}\in X_{h},\quad\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\alpha{\bf w}_{h}\cdot{\bf v}_{h}\,d{\bf{x}}+\frac{\mu}{\rho}\int_{\Omega}K^{-1}{\bf w}_{h}\cdot{\bf v}_{h}\,d{\bf{x}}+\frac{\beta}{\rho}\int_{\Omega}|{\bf u}^{i}_{h}|{\bf w}_{h}\cdot{\bf v}_{h}\,d{\bf{x}}+\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla\xi_{h}\cdot{\bf v}_{h}\,d{\bf{x}}=0,\\

({\bf u}_{h}^{i+1},p_{h}^{i+1})
the solutions of (
2.9
) and of
(
3.9
)
. We have the following bounds of the indicators: for each element
\kappa\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,

(3.60)

\eta^{(L)}_{\kappa,i}\leq||{\bf u}-{\bf u}_{h}^{i}||_{L^{2}(\kappa)}+||{\bf u}-{\bf u}_{h}^{i+1}||_{L^{2}(\kappa)},

and

(3.61)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\eta^{(D_{2})}_{\kappa,i}&\leq&C\displaystyle\Big(\|{\bf v}_{r}\|_{L^{3}(w_{\kappa})}+h_{k}\|b-b_{h}\|_{L^{3}(w_{\kappa})}+\sum_{e\in\partial\kappa}h_{e}^{\frac{1}{3}}||g_{h}-g||_{L^{3}(e)}\Big),\end{array}

where
C
is a constant independent of the mesh step but depends on the exact solution
({\bf u},p)
.

Proof.

The bound (
3.60
) is a simple consequence of the definition of
\eta_{\kappa,i}^{(L)}
and the triangle inequality.

In order to prove (
3.61
), we consider first Equation (
3.35
) with
q_{h}=0
and

q=q_{\kappa}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}b_{h}\psi_{\kappa}&\mbox{on }\kappa,\\
0&\mbox{on }\Omega\backslash\kappa,\end{array}\right.

where
\psi_{\kappa}
is the bubble functions on a given element
\kappa\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
. We obtain by using Relation (
3.47
) the following equation:

(3.62)

\displaystyle\int_{\kappa}b_{h}^{2}\psi_{\kappa}\,d{\bf{x}}=-\displaystyle\int_{\kappa}\nabla(b_{h}\psi_{\kappa})\cdot{\bf v}_{r}\,d{\bf{x}}-\int_{\kappa}(b_{h}\psi_{\kappa})(b-b_{h})\,d{\bf{x}}.\,

Then we use Property
3.17
, the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality and the relation
||{\bf v}||_{L^{2}(\kappa)}\leq|\kappa|^{1/6}||{\bf v}||_{L^{3}(\kappa)}
, and by multiplying by
h_{\kappa}
to get:

(3.63)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pth_{\kappa}||b_{h}||_{L^{2}(\kappa)}&\leq&ch_{\kappa}(h_{\kappa}^{-1}||{\bf v}_{r}||_{L^{2}(\kappa)}+||b-b_{h}||_{L^{2}(\kappa)})\\
&\leq&c_{1}(h_{\kappa}^{\frac{1}{2}}||{\bf v}_{r}||_{L^{3}(\kappa)}+h^{\frac{3}{2}}_{\kappa}||b-b_{h}||_{L^{3}(\kappa)}).\end{array}

Then we get by using the inverse inequality (
3.2
) with
p=3
,

(3.64)

h_{\kappa}||b_{h}||_{L^{3}(\kappa)}\leq c_{2}(||{\bf v}_{r}||_{L^{3}(\kappa)}+h_{\kappa}||b-b_{h}||_{L^{3}(\kappa)}),

which is the part of the indicator
\eta_{\kappa,i}^{(D_{2})}
corresponding to
b_{h}
.

Again, we consider Equation (
3.35
) with
q_{h}=0
and

q=q_{e}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{L}_{e,\kappa}\big(\phi_{h,1}^{e}\psi_{e}\big)&\hskip-14.22636pt\mbox{on }\{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}\},\\
0&\hskip-8.5359pt\mbox{ on }\Omega\backslash(\kappa\cup\kappa^{\prime}),\\
\end{array}\right.

where
\psi_{e}
is the bubble function of
e
and
\kappa^{\prime}
denotes the other element of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
that share
e
with
\kappa
. We get the following equation:

\displaystyle\int_{e}\phi_{h,1}^{e}q\,ds=\displaystyle-\int_{\kappa\cup\kappa^{\prime}}q(b-b_{h})\,d{\bf{x}}\,-\int_{\kappa\cup\kappa^{\prime}}b_{h}q\,d{\bf{x}}-\int_{e}(g_{h}-g)q\,ds-\int_{\kappa\cup\kappa^{\prime}}\nabla q\cdot{\bf v}_{r}\,d{\bf{x}}.

Properties
3.17
and
3.18
allow us to get the following bound:

||\phi_{h,1}^{e}||_{L^{2}(e)}\leq C\big(h_{e}^{\frac{1}{2}}||b-b_{h}||_{L^{2}(\kappa\cup\kappa^{\prime})}+h_{e}^{\frac{1}{2}}||b_{h}||_{L^{2}(\kappa\cup\kappa^{\prime})}+||g-g_{h}||_{L^{2}(e)}+h_{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}}||{\bf v}_{r}||_{L^{2}(\kappa\cup\kappa^{\prime})}\big).

By using again the inverse inequality
3.2
and the relation
\|{\bf v}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\kappa)}\leq|\kappa|^{1/6}\|{\bf v}_{h}\|_{L^{3}(\kappa)}
, we obtain the bound:

(3.65)

h_{e}^{\frac{1}{3}}||\phi_{h,1}^{e}||_{L^{3}(e)}\leq C_{1}\big(h_{e}||b-b_{h}||_{L^{3}(\kappa\cup\kappa^{\prime})}+h_{e}||b_{h}||_{L^{3}(\kappa\cup\kappa^{\prime})}+h_{e}^{\frac{1}{3}}||g-g_{h}||_{L^{3}(e)}+||{\bf v}_{r}||_{L^{3}(\kappa\cup\kappa^{\prime})}\big).

Hence, we bound the part of
\eta_{\kappa,i}^{(D_{2})}
corresponding to
\phi_{h,1}^{e}
. Relations (
3.64
) and (
3.65
) give Relation (
3.61
).
∎

5.
Conclusion

In this article, we discretize a steady Darcy-Forchheimer problem. We introduce error indicators and establish optimal a posteriori error estimates. We perform several numerical simulations where the indicators are used for mesh adaptation, and we show the efficiency of these adaptive methods.

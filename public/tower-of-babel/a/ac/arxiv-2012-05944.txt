On a radical extension of the field of rational functions in several variables
Xiang-dong Hou, Christopher Sze

Abstract
. Let F F be a field and let F ⁡ ( X 1 , … , X n ) F(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}) be the field of rational functions in n n variables X 1 , … , X n X_{1},\dots,X_{n} over F F . Let T = X 1 + ⋯ + X n ∈ F ⁡ ( X 1 , … , X n ) T=X_{1}+\cdots+X_{n}\in F(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}) and let m m be a positive integer such that char ​ F ∤ m \text{char}\,F\nmid m . Is it possible to express each X i X_{i} as a rational function in X 1 m ​ … , X n m X_{1}^{m}\dots,X_{n}^{m} and T T over F F ? It is not difficult to prove that this can be done but it is another matter to show how this is done. We answer the above question affirmatively with a nonconstructive proof and a constructive proof.

1.
Introduction

While studying permutation rational functions over finite fields, the authors of
[
1
]
found it necessary to express each
X_{i}
in the field of rational functions
\mathbb{F}_{q}(X_{1},\dots,X_{4})
as rational function in
X_{1}^{2},\dots,X_{4}^{2}
and
X_{1}+\cdots+X_{4}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, where
q
is odd. More generally, consider the following question. Let
F
be a field and
F(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
be the field of rational functions in
n
variables
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
over
F
. Let
T=X_{1}+\cdots+X_{n}\in F(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
and let
m
be a positive integer such that
\text{char}\,F\nmid m
. Is it possible to express each
X_{i}
as a rational function in
X_{1}^{m},\dots,X_{n}^{m},T
over
F
? The answer is positive; a quick nonconstructive proof is given in Section 2. Now that we know that it can be done, the next question how it is done. This becomes more interesting as we are looking for a rational function
f\in F(X_{1},\dots,X_{n},X_{n+1})
such that

X_{1}=f(X_{1}^{m},\dots,X_{n}^{m},T).

(A transposition of
X_{1}
and
X_{i}
in
f(X_{1}^{m},\dots,X_{n}^{m},T)
gives
X_{i}
.) In Section 3, we find such a rational function
f
, and in fact,
f\in F(X_{1}^{m},\dots,X_{n}^{m})[T]
.

Remark 1.1
.

(i)

Each
X_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq n
, can be expressed as a rational function in
X_{1}^{m},\dots,X_{n}^{m},T
over
F
if and only if

(1.1)

F(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})=F(X_{1}^{m},\dots,X_{n}^{m},T).

By symmetry, (
1.1
) holds if and only if
X_{1}\in F(X_{1}^{m},\dots,X_{n}^{m},T)
.

(ii)

If
n>1
and
\text{\rm char}\,F=p\mid m
, then (
1.1
) does not hold. In fact,

[F(X_{1}^{p},\dots,X_{n}^{p},T):F(X_{1}^{p},\dots,X_{n}^{p})]\leq p

since
T^{p}=X_{1}^{p}+\cdots+X_{n}^{p}
. On the other hand,

[F(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}):F(X_{1}^{p},\dots,X_{n}^{p})]=p^{n}>p.

Hence

F(X_{1}^{m},\dots,X_{n}^{m},T)\subset F(X_{1}^{p},\dots,X_{n}^{p},T)\subsetneq F(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}).

In our notation,
\text{char}\,F
denotes the characteristic of a field
F
and
\overline{F}
denotes the algebraic closure of
F
.
\mathbb{F}_{q}
denotes the finite field with
q
elements. For
a=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
and
b=(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
,
\langle a,b\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}b_{i}
. The symbol
[a_{ij}]_{i\in I,\,j\in J}
represents a matrix whose rows are labeled by a set
I
and columns by a set
J
and whose
(i,j)
entry is
a_{ij}
.
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker delta.
s_{i}(\ )
is the
i
th elementary symmetric function.

2.
A Nonconstructive Proof

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
F
be a field and let
T=X_{1}+\cdots+X_{n}\in F(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
. Let
m
be a positive integer such that
\text{\rm char}\,F\nmid m
. Then

(2.1)

F(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})=F(X_{1}^{m},\dots,X_{n}^{m},T).

Proof.

Since
\text{char}\,F\nmid m
,
F(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
is a finite separable extension over
F(X_{1}^{m},\dots,X_{n}^{m})
. Let
K
be the Galois closure of
F(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
over
F(X_{1}^{m},\dots,X_{n}^{m})
. (
K=F(X_{1},\dots,X_{n},\epsilon)
, where
\epsilon
is a primitive
m
th root of unity in
\overline{F}
.) To prove (
2.1
), it suffices to show that in the Galois correspondence of
K/F(X_{1}^{m},\dots,X_{n}^{m})
, the corresponding groups of
F(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
and
F(X_{1}^{m},\dots,X_{n}^{m},T)
are the same, i.e.,

(2.2)

\text{Aut}(K/F(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}))=\text{Aut}(K/F(X_{1}^{m},\dots,X_{n}^{m},T)).

Let
\sigma\in\text{Aut}(K/F(X_{1}^{m},\dots,X_{n}^{m},T))
. For each
1\leq i\leq n
, we have
\sigma(X_{i})=\epsilon^{a_{i}}X_{i}
for some
a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then

X_{1}+\cdots+X_{n}=T=\sigma(T)=\epsilon^{a_{1}}X_{1}+\cdots+\epsilon^{a_{n}}X_{n}.

It follows that for all
1\leq i\leq n
,
\epsilon^{a_{i}}=1
, that is,
\sigma(X_{i})=X_{i}
. Therefore
\sigma\in\text{Aut}(K/F(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}))
and (
2.2
) is proved.
∎

Assume that
\text{char}\,F\nmid m
and let
\epsilon\in\overline{F}
be a primitive
m
th root of unity. By Theorem
2.1
,

[F(X_{1}^{m},\dots,X_{n}^{m},T):F(X_{1}^{m},\dots,X_{n}^{m})]=[F(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}):F(X_{1}^{m},\dots,X_{n}^{m})]=m^{n},

that is,
T
is of degree
m^{n}
over
F(X_{1}^{m},\dots,X_{n}^{m})
. On the other hand, every conjugate of
T
over
F(X_{1}^{m},\dots,X_{n}^{m})
is of the form
\epsilon^{a_{1}}X_{1}+\cdots+\epsilon^{a_{n}}X_{n}
, where
(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in\{0,1,\dots,m-1\}^{n}
. Hence the minimal polynomial of
T
over
F(X_{1}^{m},\dots,X_{n}^{m})
is

\prod_{(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in\{0,1,\dots,m-1\}^{n}}\Bigl(X-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\epsilon^{a_{i}}X_{i}\Bigr).

Remark 2.2
.

In Theorem
2.1
,
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
can be replaced by elements
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
in an extension of
F
. As long as
x_{1}+\cdots+x_{n}=\epsilon^{a_{1}}x_{1}+\cdots+\epsilon^{a_{n}}x_{n}
implies
\epsilon^{a_{i}}=1
for all
i
, Theorem
2.1
holds. The result in this form frequently appears as exercises in graduate algebra textbooks; see, for example,
[
3
, Exercise 18.14]
.

3.
A Constructive Proof

Assume that
\text{char}\,F\nmid m
. Our objective is to express
X_{1}
as a polynomial in
T=X_{1}+\cdots+X_{n}
with coefficients in
F(X_{1}^{m},\dots,X_{n}^{m})
.

3.1.
A naive approach

Let’s begin with an example to show what a naive approach might look like.

Example 3.1
.

Let
m=2
. We try to express
X_{1}
as a rational function in
X_{1}^{2},\dots,X_{n}^{2},T
.

When
n=1
,
X_{1}=T
.

When
n=2
,

T^{2}=X_{1}^{2}+X_{2}^{2}+2X_{1}X_{2}=X_{1}^{2}+X_{2}^{2}+2X_{1}(T-X_{1})=-X_{1}^{2}+X_{2}^{2}+2TX_{1},

whence

X_{1}=\frac{T^{2}+X_{1}^{2}-X_{2}^{2}}{2T}.

When
n=3
, from
(T-X_{1})^{2}=(X_{2}+X_{3})^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle T^{2}+X_{1}^{2}-2TX_{1}=X_{2}^{2}+X_{3}^{2}+2X_{2}X_{3},

\displaystyle T^{2}+X_{1}^{2}-X_{2}^{2}-X_{3}^{2}-2TX_{1}=2X_{2}X_{3},

\displaystyle(T^{2}+X_{1}^{2}-X_{2}^{2}-X_{3}^{2}-2TX_{1})^{2}=4X_{2}^{2}X_{3}^{2},

\displaystyle(T^{2}+X_{1}^{2}-X_{2}^{2}-X_{3}^{2})^{2}+4T^{2}X_{1}^{2}-4T(T^{2}+X_{1}^{2}-X_{2}^{2}-X_{3}^{2})X_{1}=4X_{2}^{2}X_{3}^{2},

\displaystyle X_{1}=\frac{(T^{2}+X_{1}^{2}-X_{2}^{2}-X_{3}^{2})^{2}+4T^{2}X_{1}^{2}-4X_{2}^{2}X_{3}^{2}}{4T(T^{2}+X_{1}^{2}-X_{2}^{2}-X_{3}^{2})}.

When
n=4
, from
(T-X_{1}-X_{2})^{2}=(X_{3}+X_{4})^{2}
, we have

(T-X_{1})^{2}+X_{2}^{2}-2(T-X_{1})X_{2}=X_{3}^{2}+X_{4}^{2}+2X_{3}X_{4}.

In what follows,
f_{1},\dots,f_{5}
are polynomials in
X_{1}^{2},\dots,X_{n}^{2},T
over
F
. We have

\displaystyle-2TX_{1}+f_{1}=2(T-X_{1})X_{2}+2X_{3}X_{4},

\displaystyle(-TX_{1}+f_{1}/2)^{2}=((T-X_{1})X_{2}+X_{3}X_{4})^{2},

\displaystyle-Tf_{1}X_{1}+f_{2}=(T-X_{1})^{2}X_{2}^{2}+2(T-X_{1})X_{2}X_{3}X_{4},

\displaystyle(2TX_{2}^{2}-Tf_{1})X_{1}+f_{3}=2(T-X_{1})X_{2}X_{3}X_{4},

\displaystyle(T(2X_{2}^{2}-f_{1})X_{1}+f_{3})^{2}=(2(T-X_{1})X_{2}X_{3}X_{4})^{2},

\displaystyle 2T(2X_{2}^{2}-f_{1})f_{3}X_{1}+f_{4}=4(T-X_{1})^{2}X_{2}^{2}X_{3}^{2}X_{4}^{2},

\displaystyle(2T(2X_{2}^{2}-f_{1})f_{3}+8TX_{2}^{2}X_{3}^{2}X_{4}^{2})X_{1}=f_{5},

\displaystyle X_{1}=\frac{f_{5}}{2T((2X_{2}^{2}-f_{1})f_{3}+4X_{2}^{2}X_{3}^{2}X_{4}^{2})}.

Unfortunately, when
n\geq 5
, it appears that this method no longer works.

3.2.
The case
\text{char}\,\boldsymbol{F=p>0}

When
\text{char}\,F=p>0
, we can take advantage of the fact that
T^{p}=X_{1}^{p}+\cdots+X_{n}^{p}
. Since
p\nmid m
, there exists a positive integer
e
such that
p^{e}\equiv 1\pmod{m}
. Let
q=p^{e}
. The matrix

M=\left[\begin{matrix}X_{1}^{q^{0}}&\cdots&X_{n}^{q^{0}}\cr\vdots&&\vdots\cr X_{1}^{q^{n-1}}&\cdots&X_{n}^{q^{n-1}}\end{matrix}\right]

is called the
Moore Matrix
. Its determinant, the
Moore determinant
, denoted by
\Delta(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
, is given in
[
6
]
by

\Delta(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})=\det M=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\,\prod_{a_{1},\dots,a_{i-1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\Bigl(X_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}a_{j}X_{j}\Bigr);

also see
[
2
, Exercise 2.15]
or
[
4
, Lemma 3.51]
. (When
i=1
, the inner product in the above is understood to be
X_{1}
.) We have

M\left[\begin{matrix}X_{1}^{-1}\cr&\ddots\cr&&X_{n}^{-1}\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}X_{1}\cr\vdots\cr X_{n}\end{matrix}\right]=\left[\begin{matrix}T^{q^{0}}\cr\vdots\cr T^{q^{n-1}}\end{matrix}\right],

where the entries of

M\left[\begin{matrix}X_{1}^{-1}\cr&\ddots\cr&&X_{n}^{-1}\end{matrix}\right]

belong to
F[X_{1}^{q-1},\dots,X_{n}^{q-1}]\subset F[X_{1}^{m},\dots,X_{n}^{m}]
. Thus

\left[\begin{matrix}X_{1}\cr\vdots\cr X_{n}\end{matrix}\right]=\left[\begin{matrix}X_{1}\cr&\ddots\cr&&X_{n}\end{matrix}\right]M^{-1}\left[\begin{matrix}T^{q^{0}}\cr\vdots\cr T^{q^{n-1}}\end{matrix}\right],

where the entries of

\left[\begin{matrix}X_{1}\cr&\ddots\cr&&X_{n}\end{matrix}\right]M^{-1}

belong to
F(X_{1}^{q-1},\dots,X_{n}^{q-1})
. We have

M^{-1}=\frac{1}{\Delta(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})}\left[\begin{matrix}\Delta_{0}&-\Delta_{1}&\cdots&(-1)^{n-1}\Delta_{n-1}\cr\cr&&*\cr\cr\end{matrix}\right],

where

(3.1)

\Delta_{i}=\left|\begin{matrix}X_{2}^{q^{0}}&\cdots&X_{n}^{q^{0}}\cr\vdots&&\vdots\cr X_{2}^{q^{i-1}}&\cdots&X_{n}^{q^{i-1}}\cr X_{2}^{q^{i+1}}&\cdots&X_{n}^{q^{i+1}}\cr\vdots&&\vdots\cr X_{2}^{q^{n-1}}&\cdots&X_{n}^{q^{n-1}}\end{matrix}\right|,\qquad 0\leq i\leq n-1.

Hence

(3.2)

X_{1}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{(-1)^{i}X_{1}\Delta_{i}}{\Delta(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})}T^{q^{i}},

where

\frac{X_{1}\Delta_{i}}{\Delta(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})}\in F(X_{1}^{q-1},\dots,X_{n}^{q-1}).

The determinant
\Delta_{i}
can be computed by using a normal basis of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
or by using the roots of any irreducible polynomial of degree
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
; see the appendix for the details.

3.3.
The general case

Let
\Lambda=\{0,1,\dots,m-1\}^{n}
. For
t\geq 0
,

T^{t}=\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}}\binom{t}{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}}X_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots X_{n}^{i_{n}}=\sum_{\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})\in\Lambda}a(t,\lambda)X_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}\cdots X_{n}^{\lambda_{n}},

where

\displaystyle a(t,\lambda)\,

\displaystyle=X_{1}^{-\lambda_{1}}\cdots X_{n}^{-\lambda_{n}}\sum_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\equiv\lambda\,({\rm mod}\,m)}\binom{t}{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}}X_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots X_{n}^{i_{n}}

\displaystyle\in F(X_{1}^{m},\dots,X_{n}^{m}).

So, we have a linear system in
X_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}\cdots X_{n}^{\lambda_{n}}
,
\lambda\in\Lambda
:

\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}a(t,\lambda)X_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}\cdots X_{n}^{\lambda_{n}}=T^{t},\quad 0\leq t\leq m^{n}-1,

whose coefficient matrix is

A=[a(t,\lambda)]_{0\leq t\leq m^{n}-1,\,\lambda\in\Lambda}.

Let
\epsilon\in\overline{F}
be a primitive
m
th root of unity. Since

\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\epsilon^{ij}=\begin{cases}0&\text{if}\ j\not\equiv 0\pmod{m},\cr m&\text{if}\ j\equiv 0\pmod{m},\end{cases}

we have

\displaystyle a(t,\lambda)

\displaystyle=X_{1}^{-\lambda_{1}}\cdots X_{n}^{-\lambda_{n}}\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}}\binom{t}{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}}X_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots X_{n}^{i_{n}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\Bigl(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j_{k}=0}^{m-1}\epsilon^{(i_{k}-\lambda_{k})j_{k}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=m^{-n}X_{1}^{-\lambda_{1}}\cdots X_{n}^{-\lambda_{n}}\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}}\binom{t}{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}}X_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots X_{n}^{i_{n}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\Bigl(\sum_{j_{k}=0}^{m-1}\epsilon^{(i_{k}-\lambda_{k})j_{k}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=m^{-n}X_{1}^{-\lambda_{1}}\cdots X_{n}^{-\lambda_{n}}\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}}\binom{t}{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}}X_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots X_{n}^{i_{n}}\sum_{(j_{1},\dots,j_{n})\in\Lambda}\epsilon^{\langle i-\lambda,j\rangle}

\displaystyle\kern 175.00026pt(i=(i_{1},\dots,i_{n}),\ j=(j_{1},\dots,j_{n}))

\displaystyle=m^{-n}X_{1}^{-\lambda_{1}}\cdots X_{n}^{-\lambda_{n}}\sum_{j\in\Lambda}\epsilon^{-\langle\lambda,j\rangle}\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}}\binom{t}{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}}X_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots X_{n}^{i_{n}}\epsilon^{\langle i,j\rangle}

\displaystyle=m^{-n}X_{1}^{-\lambda_{1}}\cdots X_{n}^{-\lambda_{n}}\sum_{j\in\Lambda}\epsilon^{-\langle\lambda,j\rangle}\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}}\binom{t}{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}}(\epsilon^{j_{1}}X_{1})^{i_{1}}\cdots(\epsilon^{j_{n}}X_{n})^{i_{n}}

\displaystyle=m^{-n}X_{1}^{-\lambda_{1}}\cdots X_{n}^{-\lambda_{n}}\sum_{j\in\Lambda}\epsilon^{-\langle\lambda,j\rangle}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\epsilon^{j_{k}}X_{k}\Bigr)^{t}.

Therefore

A=m^{-n}BCD,

where

B=\Bigl[\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\epsilon^{j_{k}}X_{k}\Bigr)^{t}\Bigr]_{0\leq t\leq m^{n}-1,\,j\in\Lambda}

is a nonsingular Vandermonde matrix,

C=\bigl[\epsilon^{-\langle j,\lambda\rangle}\bigr]_{j\in\Lambda,\,\lambda\in\Lambda},

and
D
is the
m^{n}\times m^{n}
diagonal matrix labeled by
\Lambda\times\Lambda
whose
(\lambda,\lambda)
entry is
X_{1}^{-\lambda_{1}}\cdots X_{n}^{-\lambda_{n}}
. From the orthogonality of the characters of
(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{n}
, we have

C^{-1}=m^{-n}\bigl[\epsilon^{\langle\lambda,j\rangle}\bigr]_{\lambda\in\Lambda\,j\in\lambda}.

Hence

(3.3)

A^{-1}=m^{n}D^{-1}C^{-1}B^{-1}=D^{-1}\bigl[\epsilon^{\langle\lambda,j\rangle}\bigr]_{\lambda\in\Lambda\,j\in\lambda}B^{-1}.

Let

b_{j}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\epsilon^{j_{k}}X_{k},\qquad j=(j_{1},\dots,j_{n})\in\Lambda.

Then

B=\bigl[b_{j}^{t}\bigr]_{0\leq t\leq m^{n}-1,\,j\in\Lambda}.

Although the inverse of a nonsingular Vandermonde matrix is well known
[
5
]
, we include a brief derivation of
B^{-1}
for the reader’s conevenience. Let

(3.4)

B^{-1}=[a_{jt}]_{j\in\Lambda,\,0\leq t\leq m^{n}-1}.

Then

\sum_{t=0}^{m^{n}-1}a_{jt}b^{t}_{i}=\delta_{ji},\qquad i\in\Lambda.

Hence

\sum_{t=0}^{m^{n}-1}a_{jt}X^{t}=\frac{\prod_{i\neq j}(X-b_{i})}{\prod_{i\neq j}(b_{j}-b_{i})}.

So

(3.5)

a_{jt}=(-1)^{m-1-t}\,\frac{s_{m^{n}-1-t}((b_{i})_{i\neq j})}{\prod_{i\neq j}(b_{j}-b_{i})},

where
s_{m^{n}-1-t}(\ )
is the
(m^{n}-1-t)
-th elementary symmetric function. Now by (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), we have

X_{1}=X_{1}\sum_{j\in\Lambda}\sum_{t=0}^{m^{n}-1}\epsilon^{j_{1}}a_{jt}T^{t}=\sum_{t=0}^{m^{n}-1}\Bigl(X_{1}\sum_{j\in\Lambda}\epsilon^{j_{1}}a_{jt}\Bigr)T^{t},

where
X_{1}\sum_{j\in\Lambda}\epsilon^{j_{1}}a_{jt}\in F(X_{1}^{m},\dots,X_{n}^{m})
.

Appendix. Computation of
\Delta_{i}

Let
\Delta_{i}
be the determinant in (
3.1
). We show that it can be computed in two ways: by using a normal basis of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
or by using the roots of an irreducible polynomial of degree
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

A1. Computation of
\boldsymbol{\Delta_{i}}
using a normal basis of
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}
over
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{F}_{q}}

For
z\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, define

M(z)=\left[\begin{matrix}z&z^{q}&\cdots&z^{q^{n-1}}\cr z^{q}&z^{q^{2}}&\cdots&z\cr\vdots&&\vdots\cr z^{q^{n-1}}&z&\cdots&z^{q^{n-2}}\end{matrix}\right].

Then
z,z^{q},\dots,z^{q^{n-1}}
form a normal basis of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
if and only if
\det M(z)=\Delta(z,z^{q},\dots,z^{q^{n-1}})\neq 0
. Choose
z\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
such that
\det M(z)\neq 0
. Then
M(z)^{-1}=M(w)
for some
\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
; see
[
1
, §2]
. Since

M(z)^{-1}=\frac{1}{\det M(z)}\left[\begin{matrix}a_{11}&\cdots\cr\vdots\end{matrix}\right],

where

\displaystyle a_{11}\,

\displaystyle=\left|\begin{matrix}z^{q^{2}}&z^{q^{3}}&\cdots&z\cr z^{q^{3}}&z^{q^{4}}&\cdots&z^{q}\cr\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\cr z&z^{q}&\cdots&z^{q^{n-2}}\end{matrix}\right|=\left|\begin{matrix}z&z^{q}&\cdots&z^{q^{n-2}}\cr z^{q}&z^{q^{2}}&\cdots&z^{q^{n-1}}\cr\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\cr z^{q^{n-2}}&z^{q^{n-1}}&\cdots&z^{q^{n-4}}\end{matrix}\right|^{q^{2}}

\displaystyle=\Delta(z,z^{q},\dots,z^{q^{n-2}})^{q^{2}},

We have

w=\frac{a_{11}}{\det M(z)}=\frac{\Delta(z,z^{q},\dots,z^{q^{n-2}})^{q^{2}}}{\Delta(z,z^{q},\dots,z^{q^{n-1}})}.

From
M(w)M(z)=I
, we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(w^{q^{i}})^{q^{k}}(z^{q^{k}})^{q^{j}}=\delta_{ij}.

Hence

\displaystyle\Delta_{i}\,

\displaystyle=(-1)^{i}\left|\begin{matrix}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(w^{q^{i}})^{q^{k}}(z^{q^{k}})^{q^{0}}&X_{2}^{q^{0}}&\cdots&X_{n}^{q^{0}}\cr\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\cr\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(w^{q^{i}})^{q^{k}}(z^{q^{k}})^{q^{n-1}}&X_{2}^{q^{n-1}}&\cdots&X_{n}^{q^{n-1}}\end{matrix}\right|

\displaystyle=(-1)^{i}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(w^{q^{i}})^{q^{k}}\left|\begin{matrix}(z^{q^{k}})^{q^{0}}&X_{2}^{q^{0}}&\cdots&X_{n}^{q^{0}}\cr\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\cr(z^{q^{k}})^{q^{n-1}}&X_{2}^{q^{n-1}}&\cdots&X_{n}^{q^{n-1}}\end{matrix}\right|

\displaystyle=(-1)^{i}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}w^{q^{i+k}}\Delta(z^{q^{k}},X_{2},\dots,X_{n})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{i}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{\Delta(z,z^{q},\dots,z^{q^{n-2}})^{q^{i+k+2}}}{\Delta(z,z^{q},\dots,z^{q^{n-1}})^{q^{i+k}}}\Delta(z^{q^{k}},X_{2},\dots,X_{n}).

A2. Computation of
\boldsymbol{\Delta_{i}}
using the roots of an irreducible polynomial of degree
\boldsymbol{n}
over
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{F}_{q}}

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
be of degree
n
over

(A1)

g(X)=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(X-\alpha_{i})}{\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(\alpha_{0}-\alpha_{i})}=a_{0}+\cdots+a_{n-1}X^{n-1},

It follows that

\left[\begin{matrix}\alpha^{0}&\alpha^{1}&\cdots&\alpha^{n-1}\cr\alpha^{0q}&\alpha^{1q}&\cdots&\alpha^{(n-1)q}\cr\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\cr\alpha^{0q^{n-1}}&\alpha^{1q^{n-1}}&\cdots&\alpha^{(n-1)q^{n-1}}\end{matrix}\right]^{-1}=\left[\begin{matrix}a_{0}&a_{0}^{q}&\cdots&a_{0}^{q^{n-1}}\cr\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\cr a_{n-1}&a_{n-1}^{q}&\cdots&a_{n-1}^{q^{n-1}}\end{matrix}\right],

and hence

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\alpha^{kq^{i}}a_{k}^{q^{j}}=\delta_{ij}.

Therefore

(A2)

\displaystyle\Delta_{i}\,

\displaystyle=(-1)^{i}\left|\begin{matrix}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\alpha^{kq^{i}}a_{k}^{q^{0}}&X_{2}^{q^{0}}&\cdots&X_{n}^{q^{0}}\cr\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\cr\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\alpha^{kq^{i}}a_{k}^{q^{n-1}}&X_{2}^{q^{n-1}}&\cdots&X_{n}^{q^{n-1}}\end{matrix}\right|

(3.6)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{i}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\alpha^{kq^{i}}\left|\begin{matrix}a_{k}^{q^{0}}&X_{2}^{q^{0}}&\cdots&X_{n}^{q^{0}}\cr\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\cr a_{k}^{q^{n-1}}&X_{2}^{q^{n-1}}&\cdots&X_{n}^{q^{n-1}}\end{matrix}\right|

\displaystyle=(-1)^{i}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\alpha^{kq^{i}}\Delta(a_{k},X_{2},\dots,X_{n}).

a_{k}=\frac{(-1)^{n-1-k}s_{n-i-k}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n-1})}{\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(\alpha_{0}-\alpha_{i})}.

However, it is more effective to compute
a_{k}

f(X)

\alpha
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}

f(X)=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}(X-\alpha_{i})=X^{n}+b_{n-1}X^{n-1}+\cdots+b_{0}.

Then

f^{\prime}(\alpha)=\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(\alpha-\alpha_{i})

and

f^{\prime}(\alpha)(X-\alpha)(a_{0}+\cdots+a_{n-1}X^{n-1})=b_{0}+\cdots+b_{n-1}X^{n-1}+X^{n},

that is,

f^{\prime}(\alpha)\left[\begin{matrix}-\alpha\cr 1&-\alpha\cr&\cdot&\cdot\cr&&\cdot&\;\cdot\cr&&&\;\cdot&\;\cdot\cr&&&&1&-\alpha\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}a_{0}\cr\vdots\cr a_{n-1}\end{matrix}\right]=\left[\begin{matrix}b_{0}\cr\vdots\cr b_{n-1}\end{matrix}\right].

In the above, the inverse of the
n\times n
matrix is

-\left[\begin{matrix}\alpha^{-1}\cr\alpha^{-2}&\alpha^{-1}\cr\cdot&\cdot&\cdot\cr\cdot&&\cdot&\ \cdot\cr\cdot&&&\ \cdot&\cdot\cr\alpha^{-n}&\cdot&\cdot&\ \cdot&\alpha^{-2}&\alpha^{-1}\end{matrix}\right].

Hence

(A3)

a_{k}=\frac{-1}{f^{\prime}(\alpha)}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\alpha^{-k-1+j}b_{j}.

Making substitution (
A3
) in (
A2
) gives

\displaystyle\Delta_{i}\,

\displaystyle=(-1)^{i}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\alpha^{kq^{i}}\Delta\Bigl(\frac{-1}{f^{\prime}(\alpha)}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\alpha^{-k-1+j}b_{j},X_{2},\dots,X_{n}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n+i}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\alpha^{kq^{i}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}b_{j}\Delta\Bigl(\frac{\alpha^{-k-1+j}}{f^{\prime}(\alpha)},X_{2},\dots,X_{n}\Bigr).

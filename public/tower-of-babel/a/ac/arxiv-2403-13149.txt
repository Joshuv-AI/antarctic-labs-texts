1.
Introduction

1.1.
Notation and Preliminaries

Let us first define the
L_{p}
- and
\ell_{p}
-quasinorms

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}\|f\|_{p}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{1/p},&\|f\|_{p}^{*}:=\left(\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|f(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{1/p},&p\in(0,{\infty}];\\
\|a\|_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{Z})}:=\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|a_{k}\right|^{p}\right)^{1/p},&\|{\lambda}\|_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{Z}_{+})}:=\left(\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\left|{\lambda}_{d}\right|^{p}\right)^{1/p},&p\in(0,{\infty}].\end{array}

The Fourier transform of an integrable function
\phi
and its inverse Fourier transform are defined by
the standard formulae

\displaystyle\widehat{\phi}(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(x)e^{-ix\xi}dx,\qquad\check{\phi}(\xi):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(x)e^{ix\xi}dx.

Next, let
\mathcal{T}_{n}
be the space of all trigonometric polynomials

T_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=-n}^{n}c_{k}e^{ikx}
of degree at most
n
with complex coefficients.
We also use the Dirichlet kernel
D_{N}(x):=1+2\sum_{k=1}^{N}\cos kx
of order
N\in\mathbb{N}

on several occasions.

In addition, let
E_{{\sigma},p}
be the Paley–Wiener space of all complex-valued entire functions

f
of exponential type

{\sigma}>0
with
\|f\|_{p}<{\infty}

(recall that an entire complex-valued function
f
is of exponential type
{\sigma}
if for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
a constant
C_{\varepsilon,f}
such that
|f(z)|\leqslant C_{\varepsilon,f}e^{({\sigma}+\varepsilon)|z|}
for all complex
z
).
It is well known that
E_{{\sigma},p}\subset E_{{\sigma},q}
, if
0<p<q\leqslant{\infty}
,
and
E_{{\sigma},p}

is a Banach space for
p\in[1,{\infty}]
and a quasi-Banach space for
p\in(0,1)
.
Throughout the paper, we use the notation
E_{p}:=E_{1,p}
.

In the paper, we often use without referencing
the following fundamental result by Paley and Wiener:

f\in E_{2}
if and only if there exists
\psi
with

\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{2}<{\infty}
such that
\psi
is supported in
[-1,1]

and
f(\xi)=\widehat{\psi}(\xi)
.

The sinc function
\sinc x:=\sin x/x
,
the floor function
\lfloor x\rfloor
,
the ceiling function
\lceil x\rceil
,
the gamma function
\Gamma(z)
,
and the beta function

(1.2)

B(z_{1},z_{2}):=\int_{0}^{1}t^{z_{1}-1}(1-t)^{z_{2}-1}dt=\frac{\Gamma(z_{1})\Gamma(z_{2})}{\Gamma(z_{1}+z_{2})},\qquad z_{1}>0,\quad z_{2}>0,

are used as well.
The following asymptotic formula for the quotient of the gamma functions is well known
(see
[
11
, Sect. 1.18, Eqn. (5)]
):

(1.3)

\lim_{z\to{\infty}}z^{b-a}\frac{\Gamma(z+a)}{\Gamma(z+b)}=1,\qquad z>0,\quad a,b\in\mathbb{R}.

We also use the notations

\lesssim,\,\gtrsim
, and
\asymp
in the following sense:

{\varphi}(\tau,\gamma,\ldots)\lesssim\delta(\tau,\gamma,\ldots)
,

{\varphi}(\tau,\gamma,\ldots)\gtrsim\delta(\tau,\gamma,\ldots),

and

{\varphi}(\tau,\gamma,\ldots)\asymp\delta(\tau,\gamma,\ldots)

mean that there exist constants
C_{1}>0
and/or
C_{2}>0
independent of the essential parameters

\tau,\,\gamma,\ldots
such that

{\varphi}(\tau,\gamma,\ldots)\leqslant C_{1}\delta(\tau,\gamma,\ldots),\,{\varphi}(\tau,\gamma,\ldots)\geqslant C_{2}\delta(\tau,\gamma,\ldots)
,
and
C_{2}\delta(\tau,\gamma,\ldots)\leqslant{\varphi}(\tau,\gamma,\ldots)\leqslant C_{1}\delta(\tau,\gamma,\ldots),
respectively, for the relevant ranges of
\tau,\gamma,\ldots
.
The dependence of the constants on certain parameters is indicated
by using subscripts, e.g.,
\lesssim_{p,q}
or
\asymp_{p,q}
. The absence of the subscripts means that

C_{1}
and/or
C_{2}
are absolute constants.

1.2.
Bernstein–Nikolskii Inequalities

The following
Bernstein–Nikolskii inequalities:

(1.4)

\displaystyle\left\|T_{n}^{(s)}\right\|^{*}_{q}\lesssim_{s,p,q}n^{s+1/p-1/q}\left\|T_{n}\right\|^{*}_{p},\qquad T_{n}\in\mathcal{T}_{n};

(1.5)

\displaystyle\left\|f^{(s)}\right\|_{q}\lesssim_{s,p,q}{\sigma}^{s+1/p-1/q}\left\|f\right\|_{p},\qquad f\in E_{{\sigma},p};

for
0<p\leqslant q\leqslant{\infty}
and
s=0,\,1,\ldots
,
have been known for more than sixty years.

Both (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) are fundamental results in pure and applied harmonic analysis.
The applications include various topics in approximation theory, function analysis, and PDE’s.

Various estimates and asymptotics for the constants in (
1.4
) and (
1.5
)
were discussed in numerous publications (see, e.g.,
[
13
,
7
,
20
,
16
,
12
,
6
,
3
,
14
,
4
]

and references therein).
In particular, the sharp constants in (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) are known for

q={\infty}
and
p=2
(see
[
12
, Eqns. (1.5) and (1.13)]
).
The first and third authors
[
12
]
discussed limit relations between the sharp constants
in (
1.4
) and (
1.5
).
Bang and Huy
[
3
, Theorem 2.1]
studied the influence of a large
s
on the constant in (
1.5
).
Simonov and Glazyrina
[
20
, Theorem 1]
studied the sharp constant in the closely related to (
1.4
) Markov-Nikolskii inequality for
q={\infty}
and
p=1
.
In the case
s=0
and
p<q=\infty
, estimate (
1.5
) was recently investigated in
[
16
,
6
,
4
]
.

Importantly, the problem of finding the asymptotic behavior of the constant in (
1.4
) as
n\to{\infty}
remains open.

In this paper, we obtain new estimates of the constants in (
1.4
) and (
1.5
)
that cannot be improved on the spaces
\mathcal{T}_{n}
and
E_{{\sigma},p}

with respect to the parameter
s\in\mathbb{N}
.

1.3.
Main Results

Theorem 1.1
.

For every
n,s\in\mathbb{N}
and
0<p<q\leqslant\infty
, the following relation holds:

(1.6)

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{n}\in\mathcal{T}_{n},\\
\left\lVert T_{n}\right\rVert_{p}^{*}=1\end{subarray}}\left\lVert T_{n}^{(s)}\right\rVert_{q}^{*}\asymp_{p,q}n^{s}\left(1+\left({n}/{s}\right)^{{1}/{p}-{1}/{q}}\right).

Theorem 1.2
.

For every
s\in\mathbb{N}
and
0<p<q\leqslant\infty
, the following relation holds:

(1.7)

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in E_{{\sigma},p},\\
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{p}=1\end{subarray}}\left\lVert f^{(s)}\right\rVert_{q}\asymp_{p,q}{\sigma}^{s}({\sigma}/s)^{{1}/{p}-{1}/{q}}.

For a certain subset of
\mathcal{T}_{n}
, relation (
1.6
)
for
q={\infty}
and
p=1
can be improved.

Definition 1.3
.

We say that a finite sequence of numbers
c=(c_{0},\dots,c_{n})
is concave if
c_{0}\geqslant c_{1}\geqslant\dots\geqslant c_{n}\geqslant c_{n+1}:=0
and

c_{n}-c_{n+1}=:\Delta c_{n}\geqslant\Delta c_{n-1}\geqslant\dots\geqslant\Delta c_{0}=c_{0}-c_{1}
.

The next theorem shows that by restricting ourselves just to concave sequences of coefficients, we come to the better behavior in
s
and
n
on the right-hand side of (
1.6
).
Let
\mathcal{T}_{n,\mbox{c}}\subset\mathcal{T}_{n}
be the set of all trigonometric polynomials
T_{n}(x)=c_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}2c_{k}\cos{kx}

with a concave sequence
c
.

Theorem 1.4
.

For every
n,s\in\mathbb{N}
, the following relation holds:

(1.8)

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{n}\in\mathcal{T}_{n,\mbox{c}},\\
\left\lVert T_{n}\right\rVert_{1}^{*}=1\end{subarray}}\left\lVert T_{n}^{(s)}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{*}\asymp\left(n^{s}+\frac{n^{s+1}}{s+1}\right)\left(\frac{1}{\log{(s+2)}}+\frac{1}{\log(n+2)}\right).

Remark 1.5
.

A weighted version of the
\lesssim
part of Theorem
1.2
for

with power weights was established
by Bang and Huy in
[

.

The proofs of the lower and upper estimates in (

2.
Lower Bounds in Theorems
1.1
and
1.2

In this section, we prove the
\gtrsim
parts of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For every
s\in\mathbb{N}
and
0<p<q\leqslant\infty
, the following relation holds:

(2.1)

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in E_{p},\\
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{p}=1\end{subarray}}\left\lVert f^{(s)}\right\rVert_{q}\gtrsim_{p,q}{s}^{{1}/{q}-{1}/{p}}.

Proof.

Let
\phi
be a non-negative bump function, that is, a smooth function supported in
[-1,1]
such that
\phi(x)=1
for
|x|\leqslant{1}/{2}
. Let us set

\displaystyle\phi_{s}(x):=s\phi\left(sx-s+1\right),\qquad s\geqslant 2.

Since
\phi_{s}
is a smooth function supported in
[1-{2}/{s},1]
, its Fourier transform
\widehat{\phi}_{s}
is also smooth and it belongs to
E_{p}
for any
p\in(0,{\infty}]
.
Moreover,

\left|\widehat{\phi}_{s}(\xi)\right|=\left|\widehat{\phi}\left(s^{-1}\xi\right)\right|.

Therefore,

(2.2)

\left\lVert\widehat{\phi}_{s}\right\rVert_{p}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\widehat{\phi}\left(s^{-1}\xi\right)\right|^{p}d\xi\right)^{1/p}=\left\lVert\widehat{\phi}\right\rVert_{p}s^{1/p}.

Furthermore, if
|\xi|\leqslant s
, then

\displaystyle\left|\left(\widehat{\phi}_{s}\right)^{(s)}(\xi)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{1-2/s}^{1}s\phi\left(sx-s+1\right)e^{-ix\xi}\,x^{s}dx\right|=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{-1/s}^{1/s}s\phi\left(sx\right)e^{-ix\xi}\,\left(x+1-{1}/{s}\right)^{s}dx\right|

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle(1-1/s)^{s}\int_{0}^{1/s}s\phi\left(sx\right)\cos x\xi dx\gtrsim\int_{0}^{1/s}s\phi\left(sx\right)dx>1/2.

Hence,

(2.3)

\left\lVert\left(\widehat{\phi}_{s}\right)^{(s)}\right\rVert_{q}\geqslant\left(\int_{0}^{s}\left|\left(\widehat{\phi}_{s}\right)^{(s)}(\xi)\right|^{q}d\xi\right)^{1/q}\gtrsim s^{1/q}.

Finally, (
2.1
) follows from (
2.2
) and (
2.3
).
∎

For trigonometric polynomials, we use a variation of the previous argument, with the Jackson kernel taking the place of the bump function.

Lemma 2.2
.

For every
n,s\in\mathbb{N}
and
0<p<q\leqslant\infty
, the following relation holds:

(2.4)

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{n}\in\mathcal{T}_{n},\\
\left\lVert T_{n}\right\rVert_{p}^{*}=1\end{subarray}}\left\lVert T_{n}^{(s)}\right\rVert_{q}^{*}\gtrsim_{p,q}n^{s}\left(1+\left({n}/{s}\right)^{{1}/{p}-{1}/{q}}\right).

Proof.

Note first that

(2.5)

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{n}\in\mathcal{T}_{n},\\
\left\lVert T_{n}\right\rVert_{p}^{*}=1\end{subarray}}\left\lVert T_{n}^{(s)}\right\rVert_{q}^{*}\geqslant\left\lVert\left(e^{in\cdot}\right)^{(s)}\right\rVert_{q}^{*}/\left\lVert e^{in\cdot}\right\rVert_{p}^{*}\gtrsim_{p,q}n^{s}.

Next, let
r=r(p)\in\mathbb{N}
be the smallest number such that
pr>1
.
Then (
2.5
) shows that if
n\leqslant 4rs
, (
2.4
) holds.

Furthermore, assuming that
n>4rs
for
s\geqslant 2
and setting

N:=\lfloor{n/rs}\rfloor\geqslant 4
, we consider the Jackson kernel

\displaystyle J_{r,N}(x):=\left(\frac{\sin{(N+1/2)x}}{\sin{x/2}}\right)^{r}

with

(2.6)

\left\lVert J_{r,N}\right\rVert_{p}^{*}=\left\lVert D_{N}\right\rVert_{rp}^{*r}\asymp_{p}N^{r-1/p}.

Note that (
2.6
) easily follows from the Hardy–Littlewood equivalence for monotone Fourier coefficients; namely, for
f(x)\sim\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}\cos kx
with a non-increasing
\{a_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
we have
\|f\|_{p}^{p}\asymp_{p}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}^{p}(1+k)^{p-2}
(see
[
22
, Ch. XII, Lemma 6.6]
).

The Fourier coefficients
\hat{J}_{r,N}(\cdot)

of
J_{r,N}
are the convolution of the coefficients of
J_{1,N}
with themselves
r
times. Since
\hat{J}_{1,N}(m)=1
for
|m|\leqslant N
and
0
otherwise, we deduce that
\hat{J}_{2,N}(m)\asymp N
for
|m|\leqslant N
. Likewise, we obtain that
\hat{J}_{3,N}(m)\asymp N^{2}
for
|m|\leqslant N/2
. By induction on
r
, we further obtain that the coefficients
\hat{J}_{r,N}(\cdot)
are non-negative and there is a
\lambda=\lambda(r)\in(0,1)
such that for
N
large enough,

(2.7)

\widehat{J}_{r,N}(m)\asymp_{r}N^{r-1},\qquad|m|\leqslant\lambda N.

Note that (
2.7
) for an even
r
can be also proved by the following asymptotic relation
(see, e.g.,
[
8
, Ch. 2, Eqn. (3.38)]
):

1-\pi\widehat{J}_{r,N}(m)/\left\lVert J_{r,N}\right\rVert_{1}^{*}\asymp_{r}(m/N)^{2}
.

Next, setting

\displaystyle T(x):=J_{r,N}(x)e^{i(n-rN)x},

we see that
T\in\mathcal{T}_{n}
and by (
2.6
),

(2.8)

\left\lVert T\right\rVert_{p}^{*}\asymp_{p}N^{r-1/p}\asymp_{p}\left({n}/{s}\right)^{r-1/p}.

Then it follows from (
2.7
) that for
|x|\leqslant{\pi}/{(4rN)}\asymp_{p}s/n
we have

\displaystyle|T^{(s)}(x)|

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{m=-rN}^{rN}\widehat{J}_{r,N}(m)(n-rN+m)^{s}\cos mx\right|

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle\sum_{|m|\leqslant\lambda N}\widehat{J}_{r,N}(m)(n-rN+m)^{s}

\displaystyle\gtrsim_{p}

\displaystyle(n-rN)^{s}N^{r}\geqslant(1-1/s)^{s}n^{s}N^{r}\gtrsim_{p}n^{s}\left({n}/{s}\right)^{r}.

Hence,

(2.9)

\left\lVert T^{(s)}\right\rVert_{q}^{*}\geqslant\left(\int_{|x|\leqslant\pi/(4rN)}\left|T^{(s)}(x)\right|^{q}dx\right)^{1/q}\gtrsim_{p,q}n^{s}\left({n}/{s}\right)^{r-1/q},

and combining (
2.8
) with (
2.9
), we obtain

(2.10)

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{n}\in\mathcal{T}_{n},\\
\left\lVert T_{n}\right\rVert_{p}^{*}=1\end{subarray}}\left\lVert T_{n}^{(s)}\right\rVert_{q}^{*}\gtrsim_{p,q}n^{s}\Big({n}/{s}\Big)^{{1}/{p}-{1}/{q}}.

Finally, (
2.4
) for
n>4rs
and
s\geqslant 2

follows from (
2.10
) and (
2.5
).
This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

3.
Upper Bounds in Theorem
1.1

In this section, we prove the
\lesssim
part of Theorem
1.1
.

3.1.
The case
p\in[1,{\infty})

Lemma 3.1
.

If
n,s\in\mathbb{N}
and
r\in(1,\infty],
then

(3.1)

\left\lVert D_{n}^{(s)}\right\rVert_{r}^{*}\lesssim_{r}n^{s}\left(1+\left({n}/{s}\right)^{1-{1}/{r}}\right).

Proof.

The inequality is trivial for
r=\infty
. If
r\in(1,{\infty})
,
then using again the Hardy–Littlewood equivalence for monotone Fourier coefficients

[
22
, Ch. XII, Lemma 6.6]
, we obtain

(3.2)

\left\lVert D_{n}^{(s)}\right\rVert_{r}^{*}\lesssim\left\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{s}e^{ik\cdot}\right\rVert_{r}^{*}=\left\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{n}(n-k+1)^{s}e^{ik\cdot}\right\rVert_{r}^{*}\lesssim_{r}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{r-2}(n-k+1)^{rs}\right)^{{1}/{r}}.

Next, we have by elementary estimates and by (
1.2
) and (
1.3
),

(3.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=3}^{n}\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)^{r-2}\left(1-\frac{k-1}{n}\right)^{rs}\lesssim_{r}n\sum_{k=3}^{n}\int_{(k-2)/n}^{(k-1)/n}t^{r-2}(1-t)^{rs}dt

\displaystyle<n\int_{0}^{1}t^{r-2}(1-t)^{rs}dt\lesssim_{r}n{\Gamma(rs+1)}/{\Gamma(rs+r)}\lesssim_{r}ns^{1-r}.

Combining now (
3.2
) with (
3.3
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\left\lVert D_{n}^{(s)}\right\rVert_{r}^{*}\lesssim_{r}\left(n^{rs}+2^{r-2}(n-1)^{rs}+n^{r-1+rs}s^{1-r}\right)^{1/r}\lesssim_{r}n^{s}+n^{s+1-1/r}s^{1/r-1}.

Thus (
3.1
) is established.
∎

As a corollary of Lemma
3.1
, we obtain the
\lesssim
part of Theorem
1.1
for
p\in[1,{\infty})
.

Corollary 3.2
.

For
n,s\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leqslant p<q\leqslant\infty
,

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{n}\in\mathcal{T}_{n},\\
\left\lVert T_{n}\right\rVert_{p}^{*}=1\end{subarray}}\left\lVert T_{n}^{(s)}\right\rVert_{q}^{*}\lesssim_{p,q}n^{s}\left(1+\left({n}/{s}\right)^{{1}/{p}-{1}/{q}}\right).

Proof.

Using Young’s inequality for periodic convolutions and Lemma
3.1
, we obtain
for any
T_{n}\in\mathcal{T}_{n}

\displaystyle\left\lVert T_{n}^{(s)}\right\rVert_{q}^{*}\lesssim\left\lVert D_{n}^{(s)}\right\rVert_{r}^{*}\left\lVert T_{n}\right\rVert_{p}^{*}\lesssim_{p,q}n^{s}\left(1+\left({n}/{s}\right)^{1-{1}/{r}}\right)\left\lVert T_{n}\right\rVert_{p}^{*},

where
0\leqslant 1/r:=1/q-1/p+1<1
.
∎

3.2.
The case
p\in(0,1]

Here, we prove the
\lesssim
part of Theorem
1.1
for
p\in(0,1]
.
Note that the case
p=1
is also discussed in Corollary
3.2
.
Our main result is the following:

Lemma 3.3
.

If
s\in\mathbb{N}
and
p\in(0,1]
, then for any
f\in E_{p}
,

(3.4)

\left\lVert f^{(s)}\right\rVert_{\infty}\lesssim\int_{-1}^{1}|\xi|^{s}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|\,d\xi\lesssim_{p}s^{-1/p}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{p}.

Remark 3.4
.

Note that for
p=1
, the right relation of (
3.4
) can be easily obtained as follows:

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}|\xi|^{s}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|\,d\xi\leqslant\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{1}\int_{-1}^{1}|\xi|^{s}\,d\xi\lesssim s^{-1}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{1}.

However, the proof for
p\in(0,1)
is more complicated,
and it requires certain properties of the discrete Hardy spaces.

The counterpart of Lemma
3.3
for trigonometric polynomials is given in the next result.

Lemma 3.5
.

If
n,s\in\mathbb{N}
and
p\in(0,1]
, then for any

T_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=-n}^{n}c_{k}e^{ikx}\in\mathcal{T}_{n}
,

(3.5)

\left\lVert T_{n}^{(s)}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{*}\leqslant\sum_{k=-n}^{n}|c_{k}||k|^{s}\lesssim_{p}n^{s}\left(1+\left({n}/{s}\right)^{1/p}\right)\left\lVert T_{n}\right\rVert_{p}^{*}.

Combining together Lemmas
3.3
and
3.5
with Bernstein’s inequalities for entire functions of exponential type
1
and trigonometric polynomials, we obtain Theorems
1.2
and
1.1

for
0<p<q\leqslant{\infty}
and
p\in(0,1]
.

Corollary 3.6
.

If
s\in\mathbb{N}
and
0<p<q\leqslant{\infty},\,p\in(0,1]
,
then, for any
f\in E_{p}
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert f^{(s)}\right\rVert_{q}\lesssim_{p}s^{{1}/{p}-{1}/{q}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{p}.

Proof.

Using Bernstein’s inequality (see (1.2) for
p=q
) and relation
(
3.4
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left\lVert f^{(s)}\right\rVert_{q}\leqslant\left\lVert f^{(s)}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{1-p/q}\left\lVert f^{(s)}\right\rVert_{p}^{p/q}\lesssim_{p}s^{{1}/{q}-{1}/{p}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{p}.

∎

Corollary 3.7
.

If
n,s\in\mathbb{N}
and
0<p<q\leqslant\infty,\,p\in(0,1]
,
then for any
T_{n}\in\mathcal{T}_{n}
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert T_{n}^{(s)}\right\rVert_{q}^{*}\lesssim_{p}n^{s}\left(1+\left({n}/{s}\right)^{{1}/{p}-{1}/{q}}\right)\left\lVert T_{n}\right\rVert_{p}.

The proof is similar to that of Corollary
3.6
.

We first prove Lemma
3.3
for special functions from
E_{p}
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{N}
and
p\in(0,1]
. In addition, let
a
be an
H_{p}
-atom as defined in
Definition
7.3
, and let
f_{a}
be defined by (
7.1
). Then

(3.6)

\int_{-1}^{1}|\xi|^{s}|\widehat{f_{a}}(\xi)|\,d\xi\lesssim_{p}s^{-1/p}\left\lVert f_{a}\right\rVert_{p}.

Proof.

Let the interval
I:=[-N+M,N+M],\,N\in\mathbb{N},\,M\in\mathbb{Z}
, contain the support of
a
. It follows from the properties of the
H_{p}
-atoms
(see Definition
7.3
)
that
\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\infty}\lesssim N^{-1/p}

and
\sum_{k=-N}^{N}a_{k+M}(k+M)^{j}=0
for any integer
j
such that
0\leqslant j\leqslant p^{-1}-1
.
It is easy to show by induction that

\sum_{k=-N}^{N}a_{k+M}k^{j}=0
for
0\leqslant j\leqslant p^{-1}-1
.

In addition,
a\in H_{p}(\mathbb{Z})
by Theorem
7.4
.
Therefore,

f_{a}(x)=\sum_{k=-N+M}^{N+M}(-1)^{k}a_{k}\sinc({x-k\pi})
belongs to
E_{p}

by Theorem
7.2

and
\left\|f_{a}\right\|_{1}<{\infty}
by (
1.5
). Then we have

\displaystyle\widehat{f_{a}}(\xi)=(-1)^{M}e^{-iM\pi\xi}\sum_{k=-N}^{N}(-1)^{k}a_{k+M}e^{-ik\pi\xi}\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\pi,&|\xi|<1,\\
\pi/2,&|\xi|=1,\\
0,&|\xi|>1.\end{array}\right.

Furthermore, setting

\displaystyle g(\xi):=\pi\sum_{k=-N}^{N}(-1)^{k}a_{k+M}e^{-ik\pi\xi},\qquad j_{0}:=\lfloor p^{-1}-1\rfloor,

we observe that the following properties hold:

1)

|g(\xi)|=|\widehat{f}_{a}(\xi)|,\,\xi\in(-1,1);

2)

|g(\xi)|\lesssim N^{1-1/p},\,\xi\in\mathbb{R};

3)

g^{(j)}(1)=(-i)^{j}\pi^{j+1}\sum_{k=-N}^{N}a_{k+M}k^{j}=0

for
0\leqslant j\leqslant j_{0}
;

4)

\left|g^{(j)}(\xi)\right|\lesssim N^{j+1-1/p}

for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
and
j\geqslant 0
;

5)

|g(\xi)|\lesssim(1-\xi)^{j_{0}+1}N^{j_{0}+2-1/p},\,\xi\in[-1,1].

Note that the last property follows from property 4) and from
Taylor’s theorem for
g
at
\xi=1
with the Lagrange form of the remainder
since the first
j_{0}
derivatives of
g
vanish at
\xi=1

by property 3).
To conclude the proof of the lemma, we show that

(3.8)

\int_{0}^{1}\xi^{s}|{g}(\xi)|d\xi\lesssim_{p}s^{-1/p}.

The estimate

(3.9)

\int_{-1}^{0}|\xi|^{s}|{g}(\xi)|d\xi\lesssim_{p}s^{-1/p}.

can be established similarly.

If
N>s
, then using property 2), we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\xi^{s}|{g}(\xi)|d\xi\lesssim_{p}N^{1-1/p}s^{-1}\leqslant s^{-1/p}.

If
N\leqslant s
, then property 5) and relations (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) yield

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\xi^{s}|{g}(\xi)|d\xi\lesssim N^{j_{0}+2-1/p}\int_{0}^{1}\xi^{s}(1-\xi)^{j_{0}+1}d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}

\displaystyle N^{j_{0}+2-1/p}\Gamma(s+1)/\Gamma\left(s+j_{0}+3\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}

\displaystyle N^{j_{0}+2-1/p}s^{-2-j_{0}}\leqslant s^{-1/p}.

Hence, (
3.8
) and analogously (
3.9
) follow. Finally, combining (
3.8
) and (
3.9
) with property 1), we arrive at (
3.6
).
∎

Having (
3.4
) for functions
f_{a}
in hand,
we use the atomic structure of the discrete Hardy spaces given in Theorem
7.4
to extend Lemma
3.8
to any function
f
.

Proof of Lemma
3.3
.

By Theorem
7.2
, for
f\in E_{p}
there exists a unique
a\in H_{p}(\mathbb{Z})
such that

f=f_{a}
with
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{p}\asymp_{p}\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{H_{p}(\mathbb{Z})}
. Furthermore, by Theorem
7.4
,
a
has an atomic decomposition
a=\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{d}a(d)
, where
a(0),\,a(1),\ldots,
are
H_{p}
-atoms,
with
\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{H_{p}(\mathbb{Z})}\asymp_{p}\left\lVert\lambda\right\rVert_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{Z}_{+})}
.

Then,
f_{a}=\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{d}f_{a(d)}
and since
\left\|f_{a}\right\|_{1}<{\infty}
by (
1.5
),
we also have

\widehat{f_{a}}=\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{d}\widehat{f}_{a(d)}
.
Therefore, using Lemma
3.8
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}|\xi|^{s}|\widehat{f}_{a}(\xi)|d\xi

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}|\xi|^{s}\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}|\lambda_{d}||\widehat{f}_{a(d)}(\xi)|d\xi=\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}|\lambda_{d}|\int_{-1}^{1}|\xi|^{s}|\widehat{f}_{a(d)}(\xi)|d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}

\displaystyle s^{-1/p}\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}|\lambda_{d}|\leqslant s^{-1/p}\left\lVert\lambda\right\rVert_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{Z}_{+})}\lesssim_{p}s^{-1/p}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{p}.

∎

We now use Lemma
3.3
to obtain Lemma
3.5
by a discretization argument.

Proof of Lemma
3.5
.

Let
T_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=-n}^{n}c_{k}e^{ikx}
and let
\phi
be a non-negative bump function supported in
[-1,1]
such that
\phi(\tau)=1
for
|\tau|\leqslant{1}/{2}
.

If
2n+1\geqslant s
, let us define a function
f
by the following formula for its Fourier transform:

(3.10)

\widehat{f}(\xi):=(2n+1)\sum_{k=-n}^{n}c_{k}\phi\left((2n+1)\xi-2k\right).

Then
\phi\left((2n+1)\xi-2k\right)

is supported in
I_{k}:=\left[\frac{2k-1}{2n+1},\frac{2k+1}{2n+1}\right],\,-n\leqslant k\leqslant n
,

\widehat{f}
is supported in
[-1,1]
, and moreover,

(3.11)

\widehat{f}(\xi)=(2n+1)c_{k}\phi\left((2n+1)\xi-2k\right),\qquad\xi\in I_{k},\quad-n\leqslant k\leqslant n.

Next, applying the inverse Fourier transform to both sides of
(
3.10
), we observe that the function

\displaystyle f(x)=\check{\phi}\left(\frac{x}{2n+1}\right)\sum_{k=-n}^{n}c_{k}e^{\frac{2ikx}{2n+1}}=\check{\phi}\left(\frac{x}{2n+1}\right)T_{n}\left(\frac{2x}{2n+1}\right)

belongs to
E_{p}
and

(3.12)

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{p}\lesssim_{p}n^{{1}/{p}}\left\lVert T_{n}\right\rVert_{p}^{*}.

Indeed,

\displaystyle\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{p}=(n+1/2)^{1/p}\left\lVert\Phi_{p}T_{n}\right\rVert_{p}^{*}\leqslant(n+1/2)^{1/p}\left\lVert\Phi_{p}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{*}\left\lVert T_{n}\right\rVert_{p}^{*},

where

\Phi_{p}(y):=\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\check{\phi}(y/2+k\pi)\right|^{p}\right)^{1/p},\,y\in[-\pi,\pi)
.

Furthermore, using (
3.11
) and
taking into account that
(n+1/2)^{s}\lesssim n^{s}
for
2n+1\geqslant s
, we obtain

(3.13)

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}|\xi|^{s}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|\,d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2n+1)\int_{-1}^{1}|\xi|^{s}\left|\sum_{k=-n}^{n}c_{k}\phi\left((2n+1)\xi-2k\right)\right|d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2n+1)\sum_{k=-n}^{n}|c_{k}|\int_{I_{k}}|\xi|^{s}\phi\left((2n+1)\xi-2k\right)d\xi

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle(2n+1)\sum_{k=-n}^{n}|c_{k}|\frac{\left|(k+1/4)^{s+1}-(k-1/4)^{s+1}\right|}{(s+1)(n+1/2)^{s+1}}

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle n^{-s}\sum_{k=-n}^{n}|c_{k}||k|^{s}.

Therefore, using Lemma
3.3
and relations (
3.12
) and (
3.13
), we conclude that

(3.14)

\sum_{k=-n}^{n}|c_{k}||k|^{s}\lesssim_{p}n^{s+1/p}s^{-1/p}\left\lVert T_{n}\right\rVert_{p}^{*},\qquad 2n+1\geqslant s.

If
2n+1<s
and
s\geqslant 2
, we proceed by using a modification of the previous argument.
Let

\displaystyle\widehat{f}(\xi):=s\sum_{k=-n}^{n}c_{k}\phi\left(s\xi-(s-1){k}/{n}\right).

Then

\phi\left(s\xi-(s-1){k}/{n}\right)
is supported
in
I_{k}:=\left[-\frac{1}{s}+\frac{(s-1)k}{ns},\frac{1}{s}+\frac{(s-1)k}{ns}\right]
,
-n\leqslant k\leqslant n
,

\widehat{f}
is supported in
[-1,1]
, and moreover,

(3.15)

\widehat{f}(\xi)=sc_{k}\phi\left(s\xi-(s-1){k}/{n}\right),\qquad\xi\in I_{k},\quad-n\leqslant k\leqslant n,

since the family
\left\{I_{k}\right\}_{k=-n}^{n}
is pairwise disjoint
by the assumption
2n+1<s
.
Next, the function

\displaystyle f(x)=\check{\phi}(x/s)\sum_{k=-n}^{n}c_{k}e^{(1-s^{-1})\frac{ikx}{n}}=\check{\phi}\left(\frac{x}{s}\right)T_{n}\left(\frac{\left(1-s^{-1}\right)x}{n}\right)

belongs to
E_{p}
and

(3.16)

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{p}\lesssim_{p}s^{{1}/{p}}\left\lVert T_{n}\right\rVert_{p}^{*}.

Indeed, note that the polynomial
T_{n}\left((2\pi/\alpha)x\right)
is
\alpha
-periodic with
\alpha:={2\pi n}/{(1-s^{-1})}
, and since
\check{\phi}
is rapidly decaying, it satisfies the estimate

\left|\check{\phi}(x/s)\right|\lesssim_{p}{(1+|x/s|)^{-2/p}}
.
Then we have

(3.17)

\displaystyle\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+|x/s|)^{-2}\left|T_{n}((2\pi/\alpha)x)\right|^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1+j)^{2}}\int_{js}^{(j+1)s}\left(\left|T_{n}((2\pi/\alpha)x)\right|^{p}+\left|T_{n}(-(2\pi/\alpha)x)\right|^{p}\right)dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1+j)^{2}}\int_{\alpha\lfloor js/\alpha\rfloor}^{\alpha\lceil(j+1)s/\alpha\rceil}\left(\left|T_{n}((2\pi/\alpha)x)\right|^{p}+\left|T_{n}(-(2\pi/\alpha)x)\right|^{p}\right)dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha}{(1+j)^{2}}\int_{\lfloor js/\alpha\rfloor}^{\lceil(j+1)s/\alpha\rceil}\left(\left|T_{n}(2\pi x)\right|^{p}+\left|T_{n}(-2\pi x)\right|^{p}\right)dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1+j)^{2}}\right)^{1/p}\left((2\alpha+s)\int_{0}^{1}\left|T_{n}(2\pi x)\right|^{p}dx\right)^{1/p}.

Since
\alpha<\pi s
, we see that (
3.17
) implies (
3.16
).
Finally, using (
3.15
), we obtain

(3.18)

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}|\xi|^{s}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|\,d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s\int_{-1}^{1}|\xi|^{s}\left|\sum_{k=-n}^{n}c_{k}\phi\left(s(\xi-(1-s^{-1}){k}/{n})\right)\right|d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s\sum_{k=-n}^{n}|c_{k}|\int_{I_{k}}|\xi|^{s}\phi\left(s(\xi-(1-s^{-1}){k}/{n})\right)d\xi

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\frac{s}{s+1}\sum_{k=-n}^{n}|c_{k}|\left|\left(\frac{(s-1)k}{sn}+\frac{1}{2s}\right)^{s+1}-\left(\frac{(s-1)k}{sn}-\frac{1}{2s}\right)^{s+1}\right|

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\sum_{k=-n}^{n}|c_{k}|\left((1-s^{-1}){|k|}/{n}\right)^{s}\gtrsim n^{-s}\sum_{k=-n}^{n}|c_{k}||k|^{s}.

Therefore, using Lemma
3.3
and relations (
3.16
) and (
3.18
), we conclude that

(3.19)

\sum_{k=-n}^{n}|c_{k}||k|^{s}\lesssim_{p}n^{s}\left\lVert T_{n}\right\rVert_{p}^{*},\qquad 2n+1<s.

Thus, the right relation of
(
3.5
) follows from (
3.14
) and (
3.19
).
Lemma
3.5
and Corollary
3.7
are established.
∎

4.
Properties of Concave Sequences and Polynomials from
\mathcal{T}_{n,\mbox{c}}

Throughout this section,
c=(c_{0},\ldots,c_{n})
is a concave sequence (see Definition
1.3
).
The proof of Theorem
1.4
is based on several technical lemmas and on Proposition
4.5
, all of which are of independent interest.

Let us define the following sequences:

(4.1)

\displaystyle v_{l}(k):=\min\left\{1-\frac{k-1}{n},1-\frac{l-1}{n}\right\},\qquad l=0,\ldots,n,\quad k=0,\ldots,n;

\displaystyle V_{l}:=(v_{l}(0),\ldots,v_{l}(n)),\qquad l=0,\ldots,n.

The sequences
V_{l},\,l=0,\ldots,n
, are concave. Moreover,
it turns out that every concave sequence can be represented as a linear combination of

V_{l},\,l=0,\ldots,n
, with nonnegative coefficients.

Lemma 4.1
.

If
c
is concave, then there exist numbers
h_{l}\geqslant 0,\,l=0,\ldots,n
, such that

c=\sum_{l=0}^{n}h_{l}V_{l}
.

Proof.

Since

c_{k}=\sum_{j=k}^{n}\Delta c_{j}

with
c_{n+1}:=0
and

\Delta c_{j}=\Delta c_{0}+\sum_{l=1}^{j}\left(-\Delta^{2}c_{l-1}\right)
,
we have

c_{k}=\sum_{j=k}^{n}\left(\Delta c_{0}+\sum_{l=1}^{j}\left(-\Delta^{2}c_{l-1}\right)\right),\,0\leqslant k,j\leqslant n
. Hence for
0\leqslant k\leqslant n
,

\displaystyle c_{k}=\Delta c_{0}\,(n-k+1)+\sum_{l=1}^{n}\left(-\Delta^{2}c_{l-1}\right)\min\{n-k+1,n-l+1\}:=n\sum_{l=0}^{n}h_{l}v_{l}(k),

since
n-k+1=\min\{n-k+1,n+1\}=nv_{0}(k)
.
∎

The next three lemmas contain technical estimates for concave sequences.

Lemma 4.2
.

The following relations are valid:

(4.2)

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{v_{l}(k)}{n-k+1}\asymp\left(1-\frac{l-1}{n}\right)\left(\left|\log\left(1-\frac{l-1}{n}\right)\right|+1\right),\qquad 0\leqslant l\leqslant n;

(4.3)

\min_{0\leqslant l\leqslant n-1}\left(1-\frac{l}{n}\right)\left(\left|\log\left(1-\frac{l}{n}\right)\right|+1\right)=\frac{\log n+1}{n}.

Proof.

First, we note that
the function
y(\log(1/y)+1)
is increasing on
(0,1)
, which yields (
4.3
).
Next, by the definition of
v_{l}(k)
,

(4.4)

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{v_{l}(k)}{n-k+1}=\left(1-\frac{l-1}{n}\right)\left(\sum_{m=n-l+1}^{n+1}\frac{1}{m}+1\right)-\frac{1}{n},\qquad 0\leqslant l\leqslant n.

Hence, (
4.2
) is valid for
l=0
.
In addition,

(4.5)

\sum_{m=n-l+1}^{n+1}\frac{1}{m}+1\asymp\left|\log\left(1-\frac{l-1}{n}\right)\right|+1,\qquad 1\leqslant l\leqslant n.

Thus, (
4.2
) follows from relations (
4.3
)–(
4.5
).
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

For
n,s\in\mathbb{N}
we have

(4.6)

\sup_{\tau\in[0,1]}H_{n,s}(\tau):=\sup_{\tau\in[0,1]}\frac{\tau^{s+1}}{1-\log\left(1+n^{-1}-\tau\right)}\lesssim\frac{1}{\log(s+2)}+\frac{1}{\log(n+2)}.

Proof.

Let us set
{\gamma}(s):=(s+2)^{-1}\log\log(s+2)
.
First, we have

(4.7)

H_{n,s}(\tau)\lesssim\tau^{s+1}\leqslant\frac{1}{\log(s+2)},\qquad\tau\in\left[0,1-{\gamma}(s)\right.\left.\right).

Next, for
\tau\in\left[1-{\gamma}(s),1\right]
we obtain

\displaystyle 1-\log(1+n^{-1}-\tau)\geqslant 1-\log\left(s^{-1}\log\log(s+2)+n^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle 1-\log\left((s+2)^{-1/2}+n^{-1/2}\right)

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle\log(\min\{n+2,s+2\}).

Hence

(4.8)

H_{n,s}(\tau)\lesssim\frac{1}{1-\log(1+n^{-1}-\tau)}\lesssim\frac{1}{\log(s+2)}+\frac{1}{\log(n+2)},\qquad\tau\in\left[1-{\gamma}(s),1\right].

Thus, the upper estimate in (
4.6
) follows from (
4.7
) and (
4.8
).
∎

The next lemma discusses a relation between concave sequences.

Lemma 4.4
.

If
c
is concave, then

(4.9)

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}c_{k}+\int_{n^{-1}}^{\pi}x^{-1}c_{n-{\lfloor x^{-1}+1\rfloor}}dx\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{c_{k}}{n-k+1}.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.1
, it suffices to establish (
4.9
) for all
c_{k}=v_{l}(k),\,0\leqslant k\leqslant n
, defined
by (
4.1
),
with a fixed
l,\,0\leqslant l\leqslant n
.
In addition, taking into account (
4.2
), we see that inequality (
4.9
)
is a consequence of the following
inequality:

(4.10)

\frac{1}{n}{\sum_{k=0}^{n}v_{l}(k)}+\int_{n^{-1}}^{\pi}x^{-1}v_{l}({n-{\lfloor x^{-1}+1\rfloor})}dx\lesssim\left(1-\frac{l-1}{n}\right)\left(\left|\log\left(1-\frac{l-1}{n}\right)\right|+1\right).

To prove (
4.10
), we first note that the estimate

(4.11)

\frac{1}{n}{\sum_{k=0}^{n}v_{l}(k)}\leqslant 2\left(1-\frac{l-1}{n}\right),\qquad 0\leqslant l\leqslant n,

immediately follows from (
4.1
).
Next, we show that for
0\leqslant l\leqslant n
,

(4.12)

I_{n}(l):=\int_{n^{-1}}^{\pi}x^{-1}v_{l}({n-{\lfloor x^{-1}+1\rfloor})}dx\lesssim\left(1-\frac{l-1}{n}\right)\left(\left|\log\left(1-\frac{l-1}{n}\right)\right|+1\right).

To prove it, we need the following simple estimates for
x\in(0,\pi]
:

(4.13)

v_{l}({n-{\lfloor x^{-1}+1\rfloor}})\leqslant\min\left\{\frac{2}{n}+\frac{1}{nx},1-\frac{l-1}{n}\right\}\leqslant\min\left\{\frac{2\pi+1}{nx},1-\frac{l-1}{n}\right\}.

If
l=0
, then using (
4.13
), we see that
I_{n}(0)\lesssim 1
, that is, (
4.12
) holds.
Furthermore, using again (
4.13
) for
1\leqslant l\leqslant n
, we have

\displaystyle I_{n}(l)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{n^{-1}}^{n^{-1}\left(1-\frac{l-1}{n}\right)^{-1}}x^{-1}v_{l}({n-{\lfloor x^{-1}+1\rfloor}})dx

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{{n^{-1}\left(1-\frac{l-1}{n}\right)^{-1}}}^{\pi}x^{-1}v_{l}({n-{\lfloor x^{-1}+1\rfloor}})dx

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{l-1}{n}\right)\int_{n^{-1}}^{n^{-1}\left(1-\frac{l-1}{n}\right)^{-1}}x^{-1}dx+\frac{1}{n}\int_{{n^{-1}\left(1-\frac{l-1}{n}\right)^{-1}}}^{\pi}x^{-2}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{l-1}{n}\right)\left(\left|\log(1-\frac{l-1}{n})\right|+1\right).

Therefore, (
4.12
) holds for
0\leqslant l\leqslant n
, and (
4.10
) follows from (
4.11
) and (
4.12
),
completing the proof.
∎

Our next result describes the integral norm of a trigonometric polynomial with concave coefficients.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
c
be concave and
T_{n}(x)=c_{0}+2\sum_{k=1}^{n}c_{k}\cos{kx}
. Then we have

(4.14)

\left\lVert T_{n}\right\rVert_{1}^{*}\asymp\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{c_{k}}{n-k+1}.

The proof is based on two lemmas. The first one is a Hardy-type estimate (cf., e.g,
[
22

Lemma 4.6
.

For any
T_{n}(x)=c_{0}+2\sum_{k=1}^{n}c_{k}\cos{kx}
, we have

(4.15)

\left\lVert T_{n}\right\rVert_{1}^{*}\gtrsim\left|\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{c_{k}}{n-k+1}\right|.

Proof.

For

and positive coefficients a weaker version of inequality (

) was proved by Nikolskii
[

, Theorem 1]
. The proof of (
4.15
) is similar to that one.
Since the polynomial

\displaystyle Q_{2n+1}(x)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2(n+1)}+2\sin\,(n+1)x\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\sin kx}{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2(n+1)}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\cos kx}{n-k+1}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\cos\,(k+n+1)x}{k}

is uniformly bounded on

∎

Lemma 4.7
.

If
c
is concave and
T_{n}(x)=c_{0}+2\sum_{k=1}^{n}c_{k}\cos{kx}

(4.16)

\displaystyle|T_{n}(x)|\lesssim x^{-1}c_{n-{\lfloor x^{-1}+1\rfloor}}\qquad x\in(1/n,\pi),

(4.17)

\displaystyle\left\lVert T_{n}\right\rVert_{1}^{*}\lesssim\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}c_{k}+\int_{n^{-1}}^{\pi}x^{-1}c_{n-{\lfloor x^{-1}+1\rfloor}}dx.

Proof.

We prove the lemma by the standard argument (see, e.g.,
[
22
, Ch. V, Sect. 1]
).
Since
T_{n}
is a polynomial with non-increasing coefficients, the Abel transformation shows that
for

(4.18)

\displaystyle|T_{n}(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle x^{-1}\left|\sum_{k=0}^{n}\Delta c_{k}\sin{(k+1/2)x}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle x^{-1}\left|\sum_{k=0}^{n}\Delta c_{k}e^{ikx}\right|+x^{-1}\left|\sum_{k=0}^{n}\Delta c_{k}e^{-ikx}\right|=:I_{1}(x)+I_{2}(x),

where

Hence, setting

we obtain

\displaystyle I_{1}(x)=x^{-1}\left|\sum_{k=0}^{n}b_{k}e^{-ikx}\right|\lesssim x^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor x^{-1}+1\rfloor}b_{k}+x^{-1}\left|\sum_{k=\lfloor x^{-1}+1\rfloor}^{n}b_{k}e^{-ikx}\right|.

Now, applying again the Abel transformation to the

\sum_{k=\lfloor x^{-1}+1\rfloor}^{n}
above
and using the fact that

is non-increasing with

, we arrive at

(4.19)

I_{1}(x)\lesssim x^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor x^{-1}+1\rfloor}b_{k}+x^{-2}b_{\lfloor x^{-1}+1\rfloor}\lesssim x^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor x^{-1}+1\rfloor}b_{k}=x^{-1}c_{n-{\lfloor x^{-1}+1\rfloor}}.

The similar estimate holds for

. Thus, (
4.16
) follows from (
4.18
), (
4.19
), and the corresponding estimate for

.
To prove (

), we split the integral

\int_{0}^{\pi}|T_{n}(x)|dx\leqslant(1/n)\|T_{n}\|_{\infty}^{*}+\int_{1/n}^{\pi}|T_{n}(x)|dx

and use (
4.16
).
∎

Proof of Proposition

.

The
\lesssim
part of relation (

Remark 4.8

A weaker version of the

Remarks on Extremal Polynomials

Below we discuss several facts about the extremal trigonometric polynomials
P_{n}\in\mathcal{T}_{n}
for
Bernstein–Nikolskii inequality (
1.4
), with
p=1,\,q=\infty
, and
s=0,\,1,\ldots
.
In the special case of
s=0
, the similar results are known either for trigonometric polynomials or entire functions
of exponential type (cf.
[
15
,
21
,
2
]
).

The polynomials
P_{n}
are defined by the relations

(6.1)

P_{n}^{(s)}(0)=1,\qquad\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{n}\in\mathcal{T}_{n},\\
T_{n}^{(s)}(0)=1\end{subarray}}\left\lVert T_{n}\right\rVert_{1}^{*}=\left\|P_{n}\right\|_{1}^{*},

and their existence is well known.
In addition, it is clear that by considering

\left(\overline{P_{n}(x)}+P_{n}(x)\right)/2
and

\left(P_{n}(x)+(-1)^{s}P_{n}(-x)\right)/2
,
we may assume that first,
P_{n}
is real-valued, and second,
it is even for even
s
and odd for odd
s
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
P_{n}
satisfy (
6.1
). Then for any
Q_{n}\in\mathcal{T}_{n}
, we have

(6.2)

\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\sign\left(P_{n}(x)\right)\,Q_{n}(x)dx=\left\lVert P_{n}\right\rVert_{1}^{*}\,Q_{n}^{(s)}(0).

Proof.

For any
Q_{n}\in\mathcal{T}_{n}
, the function

\displaystyle{\varphi}({\lambda}):=\left\lVert R_{n,{\lambda}}\right\rVert_{1}^{*}:=\left\lVert P_{n}+\lambda\left(Q_{n}-Q_{n}^{(s)}(0)P_{n}\right)\right\rVert_{1}^{*},\qquad{\lambda}\in\mathbb{R},

has a minimum at
\lambda=0
since
R_{n,{\lambda}}\in\mathcal{T}_{n}
and

R_{n,{\lambda}}^{(s)}(0)=1
. Hence, calculating
{\varphi}^{\prime}({\lambda})
, we arrive at (
6.2
).
∎

For
s=0
and entire functions of exponential type,
the similar fact was proved in
[
15
, Eqn. (2.10)]
.
As a simple corollary of Lemma
6.1
, we obtain the following result (cf.
[
21
, p. 211]
for
s=0
):

Corollary 6.2
.

Let
P_{n}
satisfy (
6.1
).
Then the best constant
\left(\left\lVert P_{n}\right\rVert^{*}_{1}\right)^{-1}

in Bernstein–Nikolskii inequality (
1.4
), with
p=1,\,q=\infty
, and
s=0,\,1,\ldots
,
is the distance in the uniform norm from the function
h_{n,s}:=(1/(2\pi))D_{n}^{(s)}
to the space
F_{n}
of all functions with Fourier coefficients
c_{k}=0
for
\left|k\right|\leqslant n
.

Proof.

Let us set

\psi_{n}:=\sign(P_{n})\left(\left\lVert P_{n}\right\rVert^{*}_{1}\right)^{-1}
.
It follows from Lemma
6.1
that the function

h_{n,s}(x)-\psi_{n}(-x)
belongs to
F_{n}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\dist_{\infty}(h_{n,s},F_{n})\leqslant\left\lVert\psi_{n}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{*}=\left(\left\lVert P_{n}\right\rVert^{*}_{1}\right)^{-1}.

Conversely,
for any
f\in F_{n}
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert h_{n,s}-f\right\rVert_{\infty}^{*}\geqslant\left(\left\lVert P_{n}\right\rVert_{1}^{*}\right)^{-1}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left(h_{n,s}(-x)-f(-x)\right)\,P_{n}(x)dx=P_{n}^{(s)}(0)\left(\left\lVert P_{n}\right\rVert_{1}^{*}\right)^{-1},

and the result follows.
∎

Finally, we discuss certain properties of zeros of extremal polynomials.

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
P_{n}
be a polynomial, satisfying (
6.1
) for
s=0
. Then
the following properties are valid:

(a)

P_{n}
has
2n
simple zeros

-\pi<\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}<\cdots<\alpha_{2n}<\pi
;

(b)

for any polynomial
Q_{n}\in\mathcal{T}_{n}
, the following equality holds:

(6.3)

2\sign(P_{n}(\pi))\sum_{k=1}^{2n}(-1)^{k+1}Q_{n}(\alpha_{k})=\left\lVert P_{n}\right\rVert_{1}^{*}Q_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}(0).

Proof.

First we recall that
P_{n}
is an even polynomial, so
P_{n}
does not change its sign at
0
and
\pi
. Then the number
m
of zeros from
(-\pi,\pi]
, where
P_{n}
changes its sign, is even. Denote the corresponding zeros by

{\beta}_{k},\,1\leqslant k\leqslant m
, where

\displaystyle-\pi={\beta}_{0}<{\beta}_{1}<\ldots<{\beta}_{m/2}<0<{\beta}_{m/2+1}<\ldots<{\beta}_{m}<{\beta}_{m+1}=\pi.

Next, by Lemma
6.1
, the following equalities are valid for any
Q_{n}\in\mathcal{T}_{n}
:

(6.4)

\displaystyle\left\lVert P_{n}\right\rVert_{1}^{*}Q_{n}^{\prime}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\sign(P_{n}(x))\,Q^{\prime}_{n}(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sign(P_{n}(\pi))\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{k}\int_{\beta_{k}}^{\beta_{k+1}}Q_{n}^{\prime}(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sign(P_{n}(\pi))\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{k}\left(Q_{n}(\beta_{k+1})-Q_{n}(\beta_{k})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sign(P_{n}(\pi))\sum_{k=1}^{m}(-1)^{k+1}Q_{n}(\beta_{k}).

Furthermore, we show that
m=2n
. Indeed, let us assume that
m/2<n
. Then the polynomial

\displaystyle Q_{n,0}(x):=\sin x\prod_{k=1}^{m/2}(\cos x-\cos{\beta}_{k})

belongs to
\mathcal{T}_{n}
and
Q_{n,0}^{\prime}(0)=\prod_{k=1}^{m/2}(1-\cos{\beta}_{k})\neq 0
. On the other hand,

Q_{n,0}(\beta_{k})=0
for
1\leqslant k\leqslant m
; therefore,
Q^{\prime}_{n,0}(0)=0
by (
6.4
). This contradiction shows that

m=2n
, i.e., all zeros
\alpha_{k}={\beta}_{k},\,1\leqslant k\leqslant 2n
, of

are real and simple.
Finally, (
6.3
) follows from (
6.4
).
∎

Note that for
s=0
and a special class of even trigonometric polynomials, property (a) was proved in

[
2
, Lemma 6]
.

First, we recall certain facts from
[
10
]
about discrete Hardy spaces.

Definition 7.1
.

(Eoff
[
10
, p. 509]
)
For a complex-valued sequence
a=(a_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell_{p}(\mathbb{Z}),\,p\in(0,{\infty})
, let us define

\displaystyle H(a)_{m}:=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z},k\neq m}\frac{a_{k}}{m-k},\quad m\in\mathbb{Z};\qquad H(a):=(H(a)_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}.

For
p\in(0,{\infty})
, the discrete Hardy space
H_{p}(\mathbb{Z})
is the space of all
a\in\ell_{p}(\mathbb{Z})
for which
H(a)\in\ell_{p}(\mathbb{Z})
with the quasi-norm

\displaystyle\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{H_{p}(\mathbb{Z})}:=\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{Z})}+\left\lVert H(a)\right\rVert_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{Z})}.

In addition, for a complex-valued sequence
a=(a_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
, let us define

(7.1)

f_{a}(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{k}a_{k}\sinc(x-\pi k).

Then the following result holds:

Theorem 7.2
.

The following statements are valid:

(a)

for
p\in(1,{\infty})
, the map
a\mapsto f_{a}
is an isomorphism between
\ell_{p}(\mathbb{Z})
and
E_{p}
;

(b)

for
p\in(0,1]
, the map
a\mapsto f_{a}
is an isomorphism between
H_{p}(\mathbb{Z})
and
E_{p}
.

Statement (a) of Theorem
7.2
was proved by Plancherel and Pólya
[
18
]

(see also
[
10
, Theorem 5]
), while statement (b)
was established by Eoff
[
10
, Theorem 6]
.

Second, we state a result about the structure of the discrete Hardy spaces.

Definition 7.3
.

(Boza and Carro
[
5
, Definition 3.9]
)
Let
p\in(0,1]
. We say that a complex-valued sequence
a=(a_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is an
H_{p}
-atom if
it satisfies the following properties:

1)

\supp a
is contained in an interval
I
with the length
|I|\geqslant 1
;

2)

\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}\leqslant|I|^{-\frac{1}{p}}
;

3)

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{k}k^{j}=0
for any integer
j
such that
0\leqslant j\leqslant p^{-1}-1
.

In particular, it immediately follows from properties 1) and 2) that for an
H_{p}
-atom
a
,

(7.2)

\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{Z})}\lesssim_{p}1,\qquad p\in(0,1].

Then the following atomic description for
H_{p}(\mathbb{Z})
holds:

Theorem 7.4
.

(Boza and Carro
[
5
, Theorems 3.10 and 3.14]
)
Let
p\in(0,1]
. Then for any sequence
a\in H_{p}(\mathbb{Z})
we have

\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{H_{p}(\mathbb{Z})}\asymp_{p}\inf\left\lVert\lambda\right\rVert_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{Z}_{+})}
,
where
\lambda=\left(\lambda_{d}\right)_{d\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\in\ell_{p}(\mathbb{Z}_{+})
and
the infimum is taken over all possible representations
a=\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{d}a(d)
as a linear combination
of
H_{p}
-atoms
\{a(d)\}_{d=0}^{\infty}
.

Note that Definitions
7.1
,
7.3
and Theorems
7.2
,
7.4
are used in Lemma
3.8
and in the proof of Lemma
3.3
.

Third, we define

\displaystyle H_{c}(a)_{m}:=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{a_{k}}{m-k+1/2},\quad m\in\mathbb{Z};\qquad H_{c}(a):=(H_{c}(a)_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{Z}};

\displaystyle\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{H_{p}(\mathbb{Z})}^{*}:=\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{Z})}+\left\lVert H_{c}(a)\right\rVert_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{Z})},

and show that
the operator
H_{c}(a)
is equivalent to
H(a)
in the definition of the Hardy space for
p\in(0,1]
.
This statement is not trivial but
Eoff
[
10
, p. 509]
states this fact without a proof, while using
H_{c}(a)
in the proof of statement (b)
of Theorem
7.2
as well as in the equivalent definition of
H_{p}(\mathbb{Z})
.
That is why we need to prove the following result.

Proposition 7.5
.

For any
a\in H_{p}(\mathbb{Z}),\,p\in(0,1]
, the following relation holds:

(7.3)

\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{H_{p}(\mathbb{Z})}^{*}\asymp_{p}\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{H_{p}(\mathbb{Z})}.

Proof.

Note first that the proof of the
\gtrsim
part of the proposition is based on
the following discretization inequalities of Plancherel and Pólya

[
18
, p. 110]
(see also

[
10
, Theorems 2 (ii) and 4 (i)]
):

(7.4)

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|f(\pi n)|^{p}\right)^{{1}/{p}}\lesssim_{p}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{p},\qquad f\in E_{p},\quad p>0;

(7.5)

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|f(\pi n)|^{p}\right)^{{1}/{p}}\gtrsim_{p,\tau}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{p},\qquad\left(f(\pi n)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell_{p}(\mathbb{Z}),\quad p>0;

where
f
in (
7.5
)
is an entire function of exponential type
\tau\in(0,1)
.

Assume that
\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{H_{p}(\mathbb{Z})}^{*}<{\infty}
.
Then
f_{a}
, defined by (
7.1
), belongs to
E_{p}
and

(7.6)

\left\|f_{a}(\cdot/2)\right\|_{p}\lesssim_{p}\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{H_{p}(\mathbb{Z})}^{*}.

Indeed, since
a\in\ell_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, then
\left\|f_{a}\right\|_{2}<{\infty}
and
\mbox{supp}\,\widehat{f_{a}}\in[-1,1]
. Therefore,
f_{a}(\cdot/2)\in E_{1/2,2}
.
In addition,

\displaystyle f_{a}(\pi n/2)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(-1)^{m}a_{m},&n=2m,\\
(-1)^{m}\pi^{-1}H_{c}(a)_{m},&n=2m+1,\end{array}\right.,\qquad m\in\mathbb{Z}.

Hence

\left(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|f_{a}(\pi n/2)\right|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\lesssim_{p}\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{H_{p}(\mathbb{Z})}^{*}
.
Thus (
7.6
) follows from (
7.5
)
for
\tau=1/2
and
f=f_{a}(\cdot/2)
and, in addition,
f_{a}\in E_{p}

(cf.
[
10
, Proof of Theorem 6]
).

Next, using Bernstein’s inequality (see (
1.5
) for
s=1
and
p=q
) and, in addition,
applying inequalities (
7.4
) and (
7.6
) to
f_{a}^{\prime}
and
f_{a}
, respectively, we have

(7.8)

\left\lVert H(a)\right\rVert_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{Z})}=\pi\left(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}|f^{\prime}_{a}(\pi m)|^{p}\right)^{{1}/{p}}\lesssim_{p}\left\lVert f_{a}^{\prime}\right\rVert_{p}\lesssim_{p}\left\lVert f_{a}\right\rVert_{p}\lesssim_{p}\left\lVert a\right\rVert^{*}_{H_{p}(\mathbb{Z})}.

To prove the
\lesssim
part of the proposition, we first note that the following estimate for the norm of
H_{c}(a)
established in
[
18
, Lemme 8, p. 136]
:

(7.9)

\left\lVert H_{c}(a)\right\rVert_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{Z})}\lesssim_{p}\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{Z})},\qquad p>1,

is not valid for
p\in(0,1]
. That is why
we first estimate
\left\lVert H(a)\right\rVert_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{Z})},\,p\in(0,1],
for an
H_{p}
-atom
a
(see Definition
7.3
) and then discuss any sequence from
H_{p}(\mathbb{Z})
.
Without loss of generality we can assume that
\mbox{supp}\,a\subseteq[-N,N]\cap\mathbb{Z},\,N\in\mathbb{N}
,
with

(7.10)

\displaystyle\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}\lesssim_{p}N^{-1/p},

(7.11)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=-N}^{N}a_{k}k^{j}=0,\qquad 0\leqslant j\leqslant j_{0},\quad j_{0}:=\lfloor 1/p-1\rfloor.

Then using Hölder’s inequality, estimate (
7.9
) for
p=2
,
and (
7.10
), we obtain

(7.12)

\sum_{m=-2N}^{2N}|H_{c}(a)_{m}|^{p}\lesssim_{p}N^{1-{p}/{2}}\left\lVert H_{c}(a)\right\rVert_{\ell_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}^{p}\lesssim N^{1-{p}/{2}}\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}^{p}\lesssim_{p}1.

Next, we show that

(7.13)

\sum_{|m|=2N+1}^{\infty}|H_{c}(a)_{m}|^{p}\lesssim_{p}1.

Indeed, using (
7.11
), we obtain for
m\geqslant 2N+1
,

\displaystyle H_{c}(a)_{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=-N}^{N}\frac{a_{k}}{m-(k-1/2)}=m^{-1}\sum_{k=-N}^{N}a_{k}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{k-1/2}{m}\right)^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=-N}^{N}a_{k}\left(\frac{k-1/2}{m}\right)^{j}=m^{-1}\sum_{j=j_{0}+1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=-N}^{N}a_{k}\left(\frac{k-1/2}{m}\right)^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m^{-1}\sum_{k=-N}^{N}a_{k}\sum_{j=j_{0}+1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{k-1/2}{m}\right)^{j}=m^{-j_{0}-1}\sum_{k=-N}^{N}\frac{a_{k}}{m-(k-1/2)}(k-1/2)^{j_{0}+1},

where the series above are convergent.
Hence, taking account of (
7.10
), we have

(7.14)

\left|H_{c}(a)_{m}\right|\lesssim_{p}m^{-j_{0}-2}N^{j_{0}+2-1/p},\qquad m\geqslant 2N+1,\quad N\in\mathbb{N}.

It follows from (
7.14
) that

\sum_{m=2N+1}^{\infty}|H_{c}(a)_{m}|^{p}\lesssim_{p}1

since
p(j_{0}+2)=p(\lfloor 1/p\rfloor+1)>1
. The estimate

\sum_{m=-2N-1}^{-\infty}|H_{c}(a)_{m}|^{p}\lesssim_{p}1

can be proved similarly.
Thus, (
7.13
) is established.
Combining together (
7.12
) with (
7.13
), we see that for an
H_{p}
-atom
a
,

(7.15)

\left\lVert H_{c}(a)\right\rVert_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{Z})}\lesssim_{p}1.

Furthermore, by Theorem
7.4
, any sequence
a\in H_{p}(\mathbb{Z})
has an atomic decomposition
a=\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{d}a(d)
, where
a(0),\,a(1),\ldots,
are
H_{p}
-atoms,
with
\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{H_{p}(\mathbb{Z})}\asymp_{p}\left\lVert\lambda\right\rVert_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{Z}_{+})}
.
In addition, we have by (
7.2
) that

\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|\lambda_{d}||a_{k}(d)|\lesssim_{p}\left\lVert\lambda\right\rVert_{\ell_{1}(\mathbb{Z}_{+})}\leqslant\left\lVert\lambda\right\rVert_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{Z}_{+})}<\infty
.
Therefore,

(7.16)

H_{c}\left(\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{d}a(d)\right)_{m}=\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{d}H_{c}\left(a(d)\right)_{m}.

Thus, using (
7.15
), (
7.16
), and Theorem
7.4
we arrive at the relations

(7.17)

\left\lVert H_{c}(a)\right\rVert^{p}_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{Z})}\leqslant\inf_{\lambda}\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{d}^{p}\left\lVert H_{c}(a(d))\right\rVert_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{Z})}^{p}\lesssim_{p}\inf_{\lambda}\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{d}^{p}\lesssim_{p}\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{H_{p}(\mathbb{Z})}^{p},

where the infima in (
7.17
) are taken over all possible representations

a=\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{d}a(d)
.
Finally, (
7.3
) follows from (
7.8
) and (
7.17
).
∎

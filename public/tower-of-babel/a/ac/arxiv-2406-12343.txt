A note on improvement by iteration for the approximate solutions of second kind Fredholm integral equations with Green’s kernels
Gobinda Rakshit, Shashank K. Shukla, Akshay S. Rane

Abstract
Consider a linear operator equation x − K ​ x = f x-Kx=f , where f f is given and K K is a Fredholm integral operator with a Green’s function type kernel defined on C ⁡ [ 0 , 1 ] C[0,1] . For r ≥ 0 r\geq 0 , we employ the interpolatory projection at 2 ​ r + 1 2r+1 collocation points (not necessarily Gauss points) onto a space of piecewise polynomials of degree ≤ 2 ​ r \leq 2r with respect to a uniform partition of [ 0 , 1 ] [0,1] . Previous researchers have established that, in the case of smooth kernels with piecewise polynomials of even degree, iteration in the collocation method and its variants improves the order of convergence by projection methods. In this article, we demonstrate the improvement in order of convergence by modified collocation method when the kernel is of Green’s function type.

Introduction

Let
X=C[0,1]
, and
\Omega=[0,1]\times[0,1]
. Let
\kappa:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a real-valued continuous function. Consider the following Fredholm integral operator

(Kx)(s)=\int_{0}^{1}\kappa(s,t)x(t)~dt,\quad x\in X,\quad s\in[0,1].

(1.1)

Then
K:C[0,1]\rightarrow C[0,1]
is a compact linear operator.
Consider the following Fredholm integral equation

x-Kx=f,

(1.2)

where
f\in X
is given. Assume that
1
is not an eigenvalue of
K
, that is
I-K
is continuously invertible. So, the equation (
1.2
) possesses a unique solution (say)
\varphi
.

As it is challenging to find an exact solution of (
1.2
), our focus turns towards finding approximate solution of this equation. One approach involves collocation methods in which we choose a sequence of continuous finite rank interpolatory projections that point-wise converges to the identity operator. These projections are used to define operators that approximate the operator
K
.

Let
X_{n}
be a sequence of finite dimensional approximating subspaces of
X
and let
P_{n}
be a sequence of interpolatory projections from
X
to
X_{n}
. There are various techniques for obtaining approximate solutions of (
1.2
) based on interpolation discussed in
[
1
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
12
]
. In the collocation method, the equation (
1.2
) is approximated by

\phi_{n}^{C}-P_{n}K\phi_{n}^{C}=P_{n}f.

and the iterated collocation solution is defined by

\phi_{n}^{S}=K\phi_{n}^{C}+f.

The above iterated collocation method was introduced by Sloan
[
11
]
.

In Kulkarni
[
8
]
, the following modified collocation method of approximation has been introduced:

\phi_{n}^{M}-K_{n}^{M}\phi_{n}^{M}=f,

where

K_{n}^{M}=P_{n}K+KP_{n}-P_{n}KP_{n}.

The iterated modified collocation solution is defined as

\tilde{\phi}_{n}^{M}=K\phi_{n}^{M}+f.

Let a uniform partition of
[0,1]
be defined as
\{0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{n}=1\}
, where
t_{j}=\frac{j}{n}
for
j=1,2,\ldots,n
. Let
h=\frac{1}{n}
. For
r\geq 0
, let
X_{n}
be the space of piecewise polynomials of degree
\leq 2r
associated with the above partition.
Choose
2r+1
distinct points in each subinterval as collocation points, and let
P_{n}:X\rightarrow X_{n}
be the interpolatory projection at the collocation points. Then,
P_{n}x|_{[t_{j-1},t_{j}]}
is a polynomial of degree
\leq 2r
for each
j=1,2,\ldots,n
.
If
f\in C^{2r+1}([0,1])
, then
\phi\in C^{2r+1}([0,1])
. (See
[
3
]
). If
\kappa\in C^{2r+1}(\Omega)
, then from
(
8
, Theorem 3.1)
, we have

\left\lVert\phi-\phi_{n}^{C}\right\rVert_{\infty}=O\left(h^{2r+1}\right),\quad\left\lVert\phi-\phi_{n}^{M}\right\rVert_{\infty}=\left\lVert\phi-\tilde{\phi}_{n}^{M}\right\rVert_{\infty}=O\left(h^{4r+2}\right).

Here, one step of iteration does not improve the order of convergence. Now, divide each subinterval
[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
by
2r
parts and choose the nodes as the
2r+1
collocation points. Then we have the following results in the case of sufficiently smooth kernel,

\left\lVert\phi-\phi_{n}^{C}\right\rVert_{\infty}=O\left(h^{2r+1}\right),\quad\left\lVert\phi-\phi_{n}^{S}\right\rVert_{\infty}=O\left(h^{2r+2}\right).

(See Atkinson
(
1
, Section 3.4.3)
.)
In this case, the modified and iterated modified projection has been considered in Rane
[
10
]
, which gives the following orders of convergence. If
\kappa\in C^{4r+2}(\Omega)
and
\phi\in C^{4r}([0,1])
, then

\left\lVert\phi-\phi_{n}^{M}\right\rVert_{\infty}=O(h^{4r+3}),\quad\left\lVert\phi-\tilde{\phi}_{n}^{M}\right\rVert_{\infty}=O(h^{4r+4}).

However, in the above cases, the iterated solutions (i.e.
\phi_{n}^{S}
and
\tilde{\phi}_{n}^{M}
) converge to
\phi
faster than
\phi_{n}^{C}
and
\phi_{n}^{M}
, respectively.

In this paper, our aim is to obtain the orders of convergence for the iterated collocation solution (
\phi_{n}^{S}
), modified collocation solution (
\phi_{n}^{M}
) and its iterated version
\tilde{\phi}_{n}^{M}
in the case of the Fredholm integral operator with the Green’s function type kernel using the same collocation points as mentioned above.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we establish the framework for our analysis by defining the approximating space, interpolatory projection, and Green’s function type kernels. We also introduce some important notations in this section. Towards the end of this section, we prove two crucial lemmas based on the divided difference of
Kx
. Section 3 is dedicated to establishing some crucial estimates which will be used to obtain our main results. Finally, the conclusions from our study are drawn in section 4.

2
Preliminaries

Let
X=C[0,1]
be the space of all real valued continuous functions on
[0,1]
. The Fredholm integral operator
K:X\to X
is given as

(Kx)(s)=\int_{0}^{1}\kappa(s,t)x(t)~dt,\quad x\in X,\quad s\in[0,1],

where the kernel
\kappa(s,t)
is a real-valued continuous function of Green’s function type.
Note that, for
r\geq 1
, it is assumed that
\alpha>r
, and the kernel
\kappa
has the following properties:

Let

\Omega_{1}=\{(s,t):0\leq t\leq s\leq 1\},\quad\Omega_{2}=\{(s,t):0\leq s\leq t\leq 1\}.

There exist functions
\kappa_{i}\in C^{\alpha}(\Omega_{i}),\;i=1,2
, with

\kappa(s,t)~=~\begin{cases}\kappa_{1}(s,t)~~~&(s,t)\in\Omega_{1},\\
\kappa_{2}(s,t)~~~&(s,t)\in\Omega_{2},\\
\end{cases}

and
\kappa_{1}(s,s)=\kappa_{2}(s,s).
Such a kernel
\kappa
is referred as Green’s function type of kernel. Clearly,
\kappa
is a non-smooth kernel. We use the following notations throughout the article:

Let
j,k
be non-negative integer and we set

D^{(j,k)}\kappa_{i}(s,t)=\frac{\partial^{j+k}\kappa_{i}}{\partial s^{j}\partial t^{k}}(s,t),\quad\text{for}\;i=1,2.

Define

C_{1}=\max_{j,k=0,1,2}\bigg\{\sup_{(s,t)\in\Omega_{i}}\left|D^{(j,k)}\kappa_{i}(s,t)\right|:\;\text{for}\;i=1,2\bigg\},

For
l=1,2,
we define

M_{l}=\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\bigg\{\sup_{t_{k-1}\leq t\leq s\leq t_{k}}\left|D^{(l,0)}\kappa_{1}(s,t)\right|,\sup_{t_{k-1}\leq s\leq t\leq t_{k}}\left|D^{(l,0)}\kappa_{2}(s,t)\right|\bigg\}.

For a positive integer
\alpha
, if
x\in C^{2r+\alpha}([0,1])
, we define

\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{2r+\alpha,\infty}=\max_{0\leq i\leq 2r+\alpha}\left\lVert x^{(i)}\right\rVert_{\infty}=\max_{0\leq i\leq 2r+\alpha}\left\{\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\left|x^{(i)}(t)\right|\right\}.

where
x^{(i)}
is the
i^{th}
derivative of the function
x
, and

\left\lVert x^{(i)}\right\rVert_{\infty}=\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\left|x^{(i)}(t)\right|.

In particular,

\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty}=\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\left|x(t)\right|.

Now, for
x\in X
and
s\in[0,1]
we see that

(Kx)(s)=\int_{0}^{1}\kappa(s,t)x(t)~dt=\int_{0}^{s}\kappa_{1}(s,t)x(t)~dt+\int_{s}^{1}\kappa_{2}(s,t)x(t)~dt.

Using Leibnitz rule

\displaystyle(Kx)^{\prime}(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{s}\frac{\partial\kappa_{1}}{\partial s}(s,t)x(t)~dt+\int_{s}^{1}\frac{\partial\kappa_{2}}{\partial s}(s,t)x(t)~dt,

which implies

|(Kx)^{\prime}(s)|\leq\left(\sup_{0\leq t\leq s\leq 1}\left|D^{(1,0)}\kappa_{1}(s,t)\right|\right)s\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty}+\left(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t\leq 1}\left|D^{(1,0)}\kappa_{2}(s,t)\right|\right)(1-s)\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty}.

Therefore,

\left\lVert(Kx)^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C_{1}\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty}.

(2.3)

2.1
Interpolatory Projection

Denote the uniform partition of
[0,1]
as

\Delta^{(n)}:=\{0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{n}=1\},

where
t_{j}=\displaystyle{\frac{j}{n}},\quad j=1,2,\ldots,n
. Denote
\Delta_{j}=[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
. Let
r
be a non-negative integer, and define

X_{n}=\left\{x\in L^{\infty}[0,1]:x|_{\Delta_{j}}\text{ is a polynomial of degree }\leq 2r\right\}.

In each sub-interval
[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
, consider
2r+1
interpolation points as

\tau_{j}^{k}=t_{j-1}+\frac{kh}{2r},\quad k=0,1,2,\ldots,2r.

That is, the distance between any two consecutive interpolation points is
\frac{h}{2r}
. Observe that the dimension of approximating space
X_{n}
is
n(2r+1)
.

The interpolatory operator
P_{n}:X\to X_{n}
is defined as:

P_{n}x(\tau_{j}^{k})=x(\tau_{j}^{k}),\quad k=0,1,2,\ldots,2r;\quad j=1,2,\ldots,n,

(2.4)

Note that

P_{n}x\to x\quad\text{for all}\;x\in C[0,1].

Using the Hahn-Banach extension theorem,
P_{n}
can be extended to
L^{\infty}[0,1]
, and then
P_{n}:L^{\infty}[0,1]\to X_{n}
is a projection.

Let
x\in C[0,1]
and
\left\{\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r}\right\}
be distinct points in
\Delta_{j}
. Denote the first order divided difference of
x
at
\tau_{j}^{0}
as
\left[\tau_{j}^{0}\right]x=x(\tau_{j}^{0})
and
the
(2r+1)^{\text{th}}
divided difference at
\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r}
as
\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r}\right]{x}
. Then

\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r}\right]x=\frac{\left[\tau_{j}^{1},\tau_{j}^{2},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r}\right]x-\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r-1}\right]x}{\tau_{j}^{2r}-\tau_{j}^{0}},

and so on. Let

\Psi_{j}(t)=\left(t-\tau_{j}^{0}\right)\left(t-\tau_{j}^{1}\right)\cdots\left(t-\tau_{j}^{2r}\right),\quad j=1,2,\ldots,n.

Since the polynomial
P_{n,j}x
interpolates
x
at the points
\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r}
,

x(t)-P_{n,j}x(t)=\Psi_{j}(t)\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r},t\right]x,\quad t\in\Delta_{j}.

If
x\in C^{2r+1}(\Delta_{j})
, then we have

\left\lVert(I-P_{n,j})x\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C_{2}\left\lVert x^{(2r+1)}\right\rVert_{\infty}h^{2r+1},

(2.5)

where
x^{(2r+1)}
denotes the
(2r+1)^{\text{th}}
derivative of
x
and
C_{2}
is a constant independent of
h
.

2.2
Even degree polynomial interpolation

The case of interpolatory projection at equidistant collocation points (not the Gauss points) for Green’s type kernel onto a space of piecewise polynomials of degree
\leq 2r
with respect to a uniform partition of
[0,1]
has not been considered in the research literature. We study the case here.

We first prove two important lemmas based on the divided difference of
Kx
at
\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r}
and
s
, denoted by
\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r},s\right]Kx
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
r\geq 0
and
\left\{\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r}\right\}
be the set of collocation points in
[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
for
j=1,2,\ldots,n
. Then

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}]}\left|\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r},s\right]Kx\right|\leq C_{4}\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty}h^{-2r},

for some constant
C_{4}
.

Proof.

Recall that

\tau_{j}^{i}=t_{j-1}+\frac{ih}{2r}=t_{j-1}+h\zeta_{i},~\text{ for }i=0,1,2,\ldots,2r;~j=1,2,\ldots,n,

where
\zeta_{i}=\frac{i}{2r}\in[0,1]
.
Clearly
\tau_{j}^{i}\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
, we have

(Kx)(s)=\int_{0}^{1}\kappa(s,t)x(t)~dt,~\text{ with }~\kappa(s,t)=\begin{cases}\kappa_{1}(s,t)~~~&0\leq t\leq s\leq 1,\\
\kappa_{2}(s,t)~~~&0\leq s\leq t\leq 1.\\
\end{cases}

Let
s\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
, then

\displaystyle[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},s]Kx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},s]\kappa(.,t)x(t)~dt

\displaystyle=\underset{k\neq j}{\sum_{k=1}^{n}}~\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},s]\kappa(.,t)x(t)~dt+\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},s]\kappa(.,t)x(t)~dt.

(2.6)

Since
\kappa
is sufficiently differentiable in the interval
[t_{k-1},t_{k}]
for
k\neq j
,

\left|~\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},s]\kappa(.,t)x(t)~dt\right|\leq M_{2}\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty}h.

(2.7)

For
\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},s\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
, we need to find bound for
[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},s]\kappa(.,t)
. Fix
s\in[t_{j-1},{t_{j}}].

Case (I)

Whenever
t_{j-1}\leq s<\tau_{j}^{0}
. Note that

[\tau_{j}^{0},s]\kappa(.,t)=\dfrac{\kappa(s,t)-k(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}~=~\begin{cases}\dfrac{\kappa_{1}(s,t)-\kappa_{1}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\quad&\text{if}\;t_{j-1}\leq t\leq s,\\
\dfrac{\kappa_{2}(s,t)-\kappa_{1}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\quad&\text{if}\;s<t<\tau_{j}^{0},\\
\dfrac{\kappa_{2}(s,t)-\kappa_{2}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\quad&\text{if}\;\tau_{j}^{0}\leq t\leq t_{j}.\end{cases}

Thus, for a fixed
s\in[t_{j-1},\tau_{j}^{0}]
, the function
[\tau_{j}^{0},s]\kappa(.,t)
is continuous on
[t_{j-1},t_{j}].

Since
\kappa_{1}(.,t)
and
\kappa_{2}(.,t)
are continuous on
[s,\tau_{j}^{0}]
and differentiable on
(s,\tau_{j}^{0})
, by mean value theorem, we have

\frac{\kappa_{1}(s,t)-\kappa_{1}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}=D^{(1,0)}\kappa_{1}(\eta_{1},t),\;\;\text{and}\;\;\frac{\kappa_{2}(s,t)-\kappa_{2}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}=D^{(1,0)}\kappa_{2}(\eta_{2},t),

for some
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in(s,\tau_{j}^{0}).

Now, for
t_{j-1}\leq t\leq s
,

\bigg|\frac{\kappa_{1}(s,t)-\kappa_{1}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\bigg|=\left|\ D^{(1,0)}\kappa_{1}(\eta_{1},t)\right|\leq\bigg(\sup_{t_{j-1}\leq t\leq s\leq t_{j}}\left|D^{(1,0)}\kappa_{1}(s,t)\right|\bigg)=M_{1}.

Similarly, for
\tau_{j}^{0}\leq t\leq t_{j}
,

\bigg|\frac{\kappa_{2}(s,t)-\kappa_{2}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\bigg|=\left|\ D^{(1,0)}\kappa_{2}(\eta_{2},t)\right|\leq\bigg(\sup_{t_{j-1}\leq s\leq t\leq t_{j}}\left|D^{(1,0)}\kappa_{2}(s,t)\right|\bigg)=M_{1}.

Hence,

\left|[\tau_{j}^{0},s]\kappa(.,t)\right|\leq M_{1},~\;\;\;\text{if}\;t_{j-1}\leq t\leq s\;\text{and}\;\tau_{j}^{0}\leq t\leq t_{j}.

(2.8)

Now, for
s<t<\tau_{j}^{0}
, first we find

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa_{2}(s,t)-\kappa_{1}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa_{2}(s,t)-\kappa_{2}(t,t)+\kappa_{1}(t,t)-\kappa_{1}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}

\displaystyle=\frac{D^{(1,0)}\kappa_{2}(\eta_{3},t)(s-t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}+\frac{D^{(1,0)}\kappa_{1}(\eta_{4},t)(t-\tau_{j}^{0})}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}},

for some
\eta_{3}\in(s,t)
and
\eta_{4}\in(t,\tau_{j}^{0}).

Hence,

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{\kappa_{2}(s,t)-\kappa_{1}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\bigg|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\frac{D^{(1,0)}\kappa_{2}(\eta_{3},t)(s-t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}+\frac{D^{(1,0)}\kappa_{1}(\eta_{4},t)(t-\tau_{j}^{0})}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\bigg|\leq 2M_{1}.

Therefore
\displaystyle{\left|[\tau_{j}^{0},s]\kappa(.,t)\right|\leq 2M_{1}}
if
s<t<\tau_{j}^{0}
.
Thus, by (
2.8
) and the above inequality, we obtain

\sup_{t\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}]}\left|[\tau_{j}^{0},s]\kappa(.,t)\right|\leq 2M_{1}.

(2.9)

Case (II)

Whenever
s=\tau_{j}^{0}
. In this case,

\displaystyle[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{0}]\kappa(.,t)=\begin{cases}D^{(1,0)}\kappa_{1}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)\quad&\text{if}\;t_{j-1}\leq t<\tau_{j}^{0}<1,\\
D^{(1,0)}\kappa_{2}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)\quad&\text{if}\;t_{j-1}\leq\tau_{j}^{0}<t<1.\\
\end{cases}

Hence,

\sup_{t\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}]}\left|[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{0}]\kappa(.,t)\right|\leq M_{1}.

(2.10)

Case (III)

Whenever
\tau_{j}^{0}<s\leq t_{j}
. Proceeding the same way as in Case (I), we have

\sup_{t\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}]}\left|[\tau_{j}^{0},s]\kappa(.,t)\right|\leq 2M_{1}.

Hence, by (
2.9
), (
2.10
) and the above inequality, we obtain

\sup_{s,t\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}]}\left|[\tau_{j}^{0},s]\kappa(.,t)\right|\leq 2M_{1}.

(2.11)

Note that

[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},s]\kappa(.,t)=\dfrac{[\tau_{j}^{0},s]\kappa(.,t)-[\tau_{j}^{1},s]\kappa(.,t)}{\tau_{j}^{0}-\tau_{j}^{1}}=\dfrac{1}{h(\zeta_{0}-\zeta_{1})}\bigg[[\tau_{j}^{0},s]\kappa(.,t)-[\tau_{j}^{1},s]\kappa(.,t)\bigg],

which implies

\bigg|[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},s]\kappa(.,t)\bigg|\leq\dfrac{1}{h|\zeta_{0}-\zeta_{1}|}\bigg[\left|[\tau_{j}^{0},s]\kappa(.,t)\right|+\left|[\tau_{j}^{1},s]\kappa(.,t)\right|\bigg].

From (
2.11
), it follows that

\sup_{s,t\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}]}\left|[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},s]\kappa(.,t)\right|\leq\dfrac{4M_{1}}{h|\zeta_{0}-\zeta_{1}|}.

Therefore,

\bigg|\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},s]\kappa(.,t)x(t)~dt\bigg|\leq\dfrac{4M_{1}}{h|\zeta_{0}-\zeta_{1}|}\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty}=\dfrac{C_{3}}{h}\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty},

where
C_{3}=\dfrac{4M_{1}}{|\zeta_{0}-\zeta_{1}|}
is some constant.
Hence, using (
2.7
) and above inequality in (
2.6
), we obtain

\left|[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},s]Kx\right|\leq\bigg(M_{2}h+\dfrac{C_{3}}{h}\bigg)\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\dfrac{C_{4}}{h}\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty},

where
C_{4}
is some constant. This gives the required estimate

\left|[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r},s]Kx\right|\leq C_{5}\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty}\dfrac{1}{h^{2r}},

for some constant
C_{5}
. Since
s\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
is arbitrary, the proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
r\geq 0
and
\left\{\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r}\right\}
be the set of collocation points in
[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
for
j=1,2,\ldots,n
. Then

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}]}\left|\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r},s,s\right]Kx\right|\leq C_{8}\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty}h^{-2r},

for some constant
C_{8}
.

Proof.

Let
s\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
, then

\displaystyle[\tau_{j}^{0},s,s]Kx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}[\tau_{j}^{0},s,s]\kappa(.,t)x(t)~dt

\displaystyle=\underset{k\neq j}{\sum_{k=1}^{n}}~\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}[\tau_{j}^{0},s,s]\kappa(.,t)x(t)~dt+\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}[\tau_{j}^{0},s,s]\kappa(.,t)x(t)~dt.

(2.12)

Since
\kappa
is sufficiently differentiable in the interval
[t_{k-1},t_{k}]
for
k\neq j
,

\left|~\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}[\tau_{j}^{0},s,s]\kappa(.,t)x(t)dt\right|\leq M_{2}\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty}h.

(2.13)

For
\tau_{j}^{0},s\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
, we need to find bound for
[\tau_{j}^{0},s,s]\kappa(.,t)
.
Fix
s\in[t_{j-1},{t_{j}}].

Case (I)

Whenever
t_{j-1}\leq s<\tau_{j}^{0}
. Note that

[\tau_{j}^{0},s,s]\kappa(.,t)=\dfrac{[s,s]\kappa(.,t)-[\tau_{j}^{0},s]\kappa(.,t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}.

Thus,

[\tau_{j}^{0},s,s]\kappa(.,t)~=~\begin{cases}\dfrac{\frac{\partial\kappa_{1}}{\partial s}(s,t)-\left(\frac{\kappa_{1}(s,t)-\kappa_{1}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\right)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\quad&\text{if}\;t_{j-1}\leq t\leq s,\\
\dfrac{\frac{\partial\kappa_{2}}{\partial s}(s,t)-\left(\frac{\kappa_{2}(s,t)-\kappa_{1}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\right)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\quad&\text{if}\;s<t<\tau_{j}^{0},\\
\dfrac{\frac{\partial\kappa_{2}}{\partial s}(s,t)-\left(\frac{\kappa_{2}(s,t)-\kappa_{2}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\right)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\quad&\text{if}\;\tau_{j}^{0}\leq t\leq t_{j}.\end{cases}

This function
[\tau_{j}^{0},s,s]\kappa(.,t)
is possibly discontinuous at
t=s
.
Since
\kappa_{1}(.,t)
and
\kappa_{2}(.,t)
are continuous on
[s,\tau_{j}^{0}]
and differentiable on
(s,\tau_{j}^{0})
, by mean value theorem, we obtain

\frac{\kappa_{1}(s,t)-\kappa_{1}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}=D^{(1,0)}\kappa_{1}(\nu_{1},t),\;\;\text{and}\;\;\frac{\kappa_{2}(s,t)-\kappa_{2}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}=D^{(1,0)}\kappa_{2}(\nu_{2},t),

for some
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\in(s,\tau_{j}^{0})
. Now, for
t_{j-1}\leq t\leq s
,

\displaystyle\Bigg|\dfrac{\frac{\partial\kappa_{1}}{\partial s}(s,t)-\left(\frac{\kappa_{1}(s,t)-\kappa_{1}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\right)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\Bigg|

\displaystyle=\Bigg|\dfrac{D^{(1,0)}\kappa_{1}(s,t)-D^{(1,0)}\kappa_{1}(\nu_{1},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\Bigg|

\displaystyle=\Bigg|\dfrac{D^{(2,0)}\kappa_{1}(\nu_{3},t)(s-\nu_{1})}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\Bigg|,~\;\text{for some}\;\nu_{3}\in(s,\nu_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\sup_{t_{j-1}\leq t\leq s\leq t_{j}}\left|D^{(2,0)}\kappa_{1}(s,t)\right|\bigg)=M_{2}.

Similarly, it can be shown that if
\tau_{j}^{0}\leq t\leq t_{j}
, then

\Bigg|\dfrac{\frac{\partial\kappa_{2}}{\partial s}(s,t)-\left(\frac{\kappa_{2}(s,t)-\kappa_{2}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\right)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\Bigg|\>\leq\>\bigg(\sup_{t_{j-1}\leq s\leq t\leq t_{j}}\left|D^{(2,0)}\kappa_{2}(s,t)\right|\bigg)=M_{2}.

Hence,

\left|[\tau_{j}^{0},s,s]\kappa(.,t)\right|\leq M_{2},~\;\;\;\text{if}\;t_{j-1}\leq t\leq s\;\text{and}\;\tau_{j}^{0}\leq t\leq t_{j}.

(2.14)

Now, for
s<t<\tau_{j}^{0}
, first we find

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa_{2}(s,t)-\kappa_{1}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa_{2}(s,t)-\kappa_{2}(t,t)+\kappa_{1}(t,t)-\kappa_{1}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}

\displaystyle=\frac{D^{(1,0)}\kappa_{2}(\nu_{5},t)(s-t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}+\frac{D^{(1,0)}\kappa_{1}(\nu_{6},t)(t-\tau_{j}^{0})}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}},

for some
\nu_{5}\in(s,t)
and
\nu_{6}\in(t,\tau_{j}^{0})
.
Then,

\Bigg|\frac{\partial\kappa_{2}}{\partial s}(s,t)-\left(\frac{\kappa_{2}(s,t)-\kappa_{1}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\right)\Bigg|\\
=\Bigg|D^{(1,0)}\kappa_{2}(s,t)-\frac{D^{(1,0)}\kappa_{2}(\nu_{5},t)(s-t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}-\frac{D^{(1,0)}\kappa_{1}(\nu_{6},t)(t-\tau_{j}^{0})}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\Bigg|,

which gives

\Bigg|\frac{\partial\kappa_{2}}{\partial s}(s,t)-\left(\frac{\kappa_{2}(s,t)-\kappa_{1}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\right)\Bigg|\leq M_{1}+M_{1}+M_{1}=3M_{1}.

Hence,

\mathop{\text{\Large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.63885pts$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\tau_{j}^{0}$}}$}}\nolimits\Bigg|\dfrac{\frac{\partial\kappa_{2}}{\partial s}(s,t)-\left(\frac{\kappa_{2}(s,t)-\kappa_{1}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\right)}{s-\tau_{j}^{0}}\Bigg|~dt\leq 3M_{1}.

Using (
2.14
) and the above inequality, we obtain

\bigg|\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}[\tau_{j}^{0},s,s]\kappa(.,t)x(t)~dt\bigg|\leq\left(M_{2}+3M_{1}+M_{2}\right)\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty}=\left(3M_{1}+2M_{2}\right)\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty}.

Therefore, using (
2.13
) and the above inequality in (
2.12
) we have

\left|[\tau_{j}^{0},s,s]Kx\right|\leq\left(M_{2}h+3M_{1}+2M_{2}\right)\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C_{5}\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty},

(2.15)

where
C_{5}
is some constant.

Case (II)

Whenever
s=\tau_{j}^{0}
.

\displaystyle[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{0}]Kx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(Kx)^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\tau_{j}^{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial\kappa_{1}}{\partial s}(\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{0})-\frac{\partial\kappa_{2}}{\partial s}(\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{0})\right)x(\tau_{j}^{0})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left[~\int_{t_{j}-1}^{\tau_{j}^{0}}\frac{\partial^{2}\kappa_{1}}{\partial^{2}s}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)x(t)~dt+\int_{\tau_{j}^{0}}^{t_{j}}\frac{\partial^{2}\kappa_{2}}{\partial^{2}s}(\tau_{j}^{0},t)x(t)~dt\right].

Hence,

\left|[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{0}]Kx\right|\leq\left(M_{1}+M_{2}\right)\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty}.

(2.16)

Case (III)

Whenever
\tau_{j}^{0}<s\leq t_{j}
. Similar in Case (I), we have

\left|[\tau_{j}^{0},s,s]Kx\right|\leq\left(M_{2}h+3M_{1}+2M_{2}\right)\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C_{6}\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty},

where
C_{6}
is some constant. From (
2.15
), (
2.16
) and above inequality, it follows that

\sup_{s\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}]}\left|[\tau_{j}^{0},s,s]Kx\right|\leq C_{6}\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty},

(2.17)

which proves the required estimate for the case
r=0
. Now, for
r=1
,

[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},s,s]Kx=\dfrac{[\tau_{j}^{0},s,s]Kx-[\tau_{j}^{1},s,s]Kx}{\tau_{j}^{0}-\tau_{j}^{1}}=\frac{1}{h(\zeta_{0}-\zeta_{1})}\bigg[[\tau_{j}^{0},s,s]Kx-[\tau_{j}^{1},s,s]Kx\bigg],

which implies

\left|[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},s,s]Kx\right|\leq\frac{1}{h|\zeta_{0}-\zeta_{1}|}\bigg[\left|[\tau_{j}^{0},s,s]Kx\right|+\left|[\tau_{j}^{1},s,s]Kx\right|\bigg].

Hence, from (
2.17
), we obtain

\sup_{s\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}]}\left|[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},s,s]Kx\right|\leq\frac{2C_{6}}{h|\zeta_{0}-\zeta_{1}|}\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty}=C_{7}\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty}\frac{1}{h},

where
C_{7}=\frac{2C_{6}}{|\zeta_{0}-\zeta_{1}|}
is some constant.
By mathematical induction, it follows that

\sup_{s\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}]}\left|[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r},s,s]Kx\right|\leq C_{8}\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty}\frac{1}{h^{2r}}\;,

where
C_{8}
is some constant.
∎

3
Iteration methods of approximation

Recall that
K
is a Fredholm integral operator with Green’s function type kernel and
P_{n}
is the interpolatory operator at
2r+1
interpolating points defined by (
2.4
). In the collocation method, the main equation (
1.2
) is approximated by

\phi_{n}^{C}-P_{n}K\phi_{n}^{C}=P_{n}f.

(3.18)

Sloan
[
11
]
introduced iterated collocation solution by taking one step iteration as

\phi_{n}^{S}=K\phi_{n}^{C}+f.

(3.19)

The following modified collocation method has been introduced by Kulkarni
[
8
]
:

\phi_{n}^{M}-K_{n}^{M}\phi_{n}^{M}=f,

(3.20)

where

K_{n}^{M}=P_{n}K+KP_{n}-P_{n}KP_{n}.

The one step iteration is defined as

\tilde{\phi}_{n}^{M}=K\phi_{n}^{M}+f,

(3.21)

which gives the solution for iterated modified collocation method.

Now, we establish preliminary results below using the standard technique along with the aforementioned lemmas, and these outcomes will be useful for the main result.

Proposition 1
.

If
x\in C^{2r+2}([0,1])
, then

\displaystyle\left\lVert K(I-P_{n})x\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq 2C_{1}\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{2r+2,\infty}h^{2r+2},

where
C_{1}
is a constant independent of
h
.

Proof.

Let
s\in[0,1]
. Then we have

\displaystyle K(I-P_{n})x(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\kappa(s,t)(I-P_{n})x(t)~dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\kappa(s,t)(I-P_{n,j})x(t)~dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n~}\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\kappa(s,t)\Psi_{j}(t)\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r},t\right]x~dt,

where

\Psi_{j}(t)=\prod_{i=0}^{2r}(t-\tau_{j}^{i}),\quad\text{for}~j=1,2,\ldots,n.

Let
s\in[t_{i-1},t_{i}]\subset[0,1]
, for some
1\leq i\leq n
. Then we write

\displaystyle K(I-P_{n})x(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}~\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\kappa(s,t)\Psi_{j}(t)\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r},t\right]x~dt

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}\kappa(s,t)\Psi_{i}(t)\left[\tau_{i}^{0},\tau_{i}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{i}^{2r},t\right]x~dt

(3.22)

For fix
s
, let

\displaystyle f_{j}^{s}(t)=\kappa(s,t)\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r},t\right]x,\quad t\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}],~\text{for}~j=1,2,\ldots,n.

That is

f_{j}^{s}(t)~=~\begin{cases}\kappa_{1}(s,t)\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r},t\right]x~~~&j\leq i,\\
\kappa_{2}(s,t)\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r},t\right]x~~~&j\geq i.\\
\end{cases}

Note that the function
f_{j}^{s}
is continuous on
[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
and differentiable on
(t_{j-1},t_{j})
for
j\neq i
. Therefore, by Taylor series expansion of
f_{j}^{s}
about the point
\frac{t_{j-1}+t_{j}}{2}
, there exist a point
\zeta_{j1}\in(t_{j-1},t_{j})
such that

(f_{j}^{s})(t)=f_{j}^{s}\left(\frac{t_{j-1}+t_{j}}{2}\right)+\left(t-\frac{t_{j-1}+t_{j}}{2}\right)(f_{j}^{s})^{\prime}(\zeta_{j1}),

where

(f_{j}^{s})^{\prime}(\zeta_{j1})~=~\begin{cases}\kappa_{1}(s,\zeta_{j1})\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r},\zeta_{j1},\zeta_{j1}\right]x\\
+D^{(0,1)}\kappa_{1}(s,\zeta_{j1})\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r},\zeta_{j1}\right]x~~~&j\leq i,\\
\kappa_{2}(s,\zeta_{j1})\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r},\zeta_{j1},\zeta_{j1}\right]x\\
+D^{(0,1)}\kappa_{2}(s,\zeta_{j1})\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r},\zeta_{j1}\right]x~~~&j\geq i.\\
\end{cases}

Since
x\in C^{2r+2}([0,1])
, then recall that

\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{2r+2,\infty}=\max_{0\leq i\leq 2r+2}\left\lVert x^{(i)}\right\rVert_{\infty}=\max_{0\leq i\leq 2r+2}\left\{\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\left|x^{(i)}(t)\right|\right\}.

Thus

\left|(f_{j}^{s})^{\prime}(\zeta_{j1})\right|\leq 2C_{1}\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{2r+2,\infty}.

Now, we have

\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\kappa(s,t)\Psi_{j}(t)\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r},t\right]x~dt=\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\left(t-\frac{t_{j-1}+t_{j}}{2}\right)(f_{j}^{s})^{\prime}(\zeta_{j1})\Psi_{j}(t)~dt.

It follows that

\left|\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\kappa(s,t)\Psi_{j}(t)\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r},t\right]x~dt\right|\leq 2C_{1}\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{2r+2,\infty}h^{2r+3}.

Since
\displaystyle{\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}\Psi_{i}(t)~dt=0}
,

\displaystyle\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}\kappa(s,t)\Psi_{i}(t)\left[\tau_{i}^{0},\tau_{i}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{i}^{2r},t\right]x~dt

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}(f_{i}^{s})(t)\Psi_{i}(t)~dt,\quad s\in[t_{i-1},t_{i}]

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}[(f_{i}^{s})(t)-(f_{i}^{s})(s)]\Psi_{i}(t)~dt.

Since the function
f_{i}^{s}
is continuous on
[t_{i-1},t_{i}]
and differentiable on
(t_{i-1},t_{i})
, by mean value theorem we have

(f_{i}^{s})(t)-(f_{i}^{s})(s)=(t-s)(f_{j}^{s})^{\prime}(\zeta_{j2}),

for some
\zeta_{j2}\in(t_{i-1},t_{i})
. Then

\left|~\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}\kappa(s,t)\Psi_{i}(t)\left[\tau_{i}^{0},\tau_{i}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{i}^{2r},t\right]x~dt\right|\leq 2C_{1}\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{2r+2,\infty}h^{2r+3}.

By (
3.22
),

\displaystyle\left|K(I-P_{n})x(s)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}~\left|~\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\kappa(s,t)\left([\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r},t]x\right)\Psi_{j}(t)~dt\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\left|~\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}\kappa(s,t)\left([\tau_{i}^{0},\tau_{i}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{i}^{2r},t]x\right)\Psi_{i}(t)~dt\right|.

Hence

\left\lVert K(I-P_{n})x\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq 2C_{1}\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{2r+2,\infty}h^{2r+2},

which proves the result.
∎

Proposition 2
.

If
x\in C^{2r+2}([0,1])
, then

\left\lVert(I-P_{n})K(I-P_{n})x\right\rVert_{\infty}=\begin{cases}O(h^{2r+2})~~~&r\geq 1,\\
O(h^{3})~~~&r=0.\\
\end{cases}

Proof.

For
r\geq 1
, whenever
x\in C^{2r+2}([0,1])
, note that

\left\lVert(I-P_{n})K(I-P_{n})x\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\left\lVert I-P_{n}\right\rVert\left\lVert K(I-P_{n})x\right\rVert_{\infty}.

Since
\left\lVert P_{n}\right\rVert
is uniformly bounded that means
\left\lVert P_{n}\right\rVert\leq p
, for
p>0
and by Proposition (
1
), we have

\left\lVert(I-P_{n})K(I-P_{n})x\right\rVert_{\infty}=O(h^{2r+2}).

If
r=0
, using (
2.5
) we write

\left\lVert(I-P_{n})K(I-P_{n})x\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C_{2}\left\lVert(K(I-P_{n})x)^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\infty}h.

From Chatelin-Lebbar
(
4
, Theorem 15)
, we have

\left\lVert(K(I-P_{n})x)^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\infty}=O(h^{2}).

Combining the above two estimates, we obtain

\left\lVert(I-P_{n})K(I-P_{n})x\right\rVert_{\infty}=O(h^{3}).

∎

Proposition 3
.

If
x\in C^{2r+1}([0,1]),
then

\left\lVert K(I-P_{n})K(I-P_{n})x\right\rVert_{\infty}=\begin{cases}O(h^{2r+3})~~~&r\geq 1,\\
O(h^{4})~~~&r=0.\\
\end{cases}

Proof.

For any
s\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle K(I-P_{n})K(I-P_{n})x(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\kappa(s,t)(I-P_{n})K(I-P_{n})x(t)~dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\kappa(s,t)(I-P_{n,j})K(I-P_{n})x(t)~dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}~\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\kappa(s,t)\left(\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r},t\right]K(I-P_{n})x\right)\Psi_{j}(t)~dt.

(3.23)

Let
s\in[t_{i-1},t_{i}]\subset[0,1]
, for some
1\leq i\leq n
. Let

\displaystyle g_{j}^{s}(t)=\kappa(s,t)\left(\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r},t\right]K(I-P_{n})x\right),\quad t\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}],~\text{for}~j=1,2,\ldots,n.

Note that

g_{j}^{s}(t)~=~\begin{cases}\kappa_{1}(s,t)\left(\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r},t\right]K(I-P_{n})x\right)~~~&j\leq i,\\
\kappa_{2}(s,t)\left(\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r},t\right]K(I-P_{n})x\right)~~~&j\geq i.\\
\end{cases}

Denote

\left(\delta_{j}^{2r}K(I-P_{n})x\right)[t]=\left(\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r},t\right]K(I-P_{n})x\right),

and

\left(\delta_{j}^{2r}K(I-P_{n})x\right)[t,t]=\left(\left[\tau_{j}^{0},\tau_{j}^{1},\ldots,\tau_{j}^{2r},t,t\right]K(I-P_{n})x\right).

Note that the function
g_{j}^{s}
is continuous on
[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
and differentiable on
(t_{j-1},t_{j})
for
j\neq i
. Then, it follows that

(g_{j}^{s})^{\prime}(t)~=~\begin{cases}\kappa_{1}(s,t)\left(\delta_{j}^{2r}K(I-P_{n})x\right)[t,t]+\left(\delta_{j}^{2r}K(I-P_{n})x\right)[t]~D^{(0,1)}\kappa_{1}(s,t)~&j\leq i,\\
\kappa_{2}(s,t)\left(\delta_{j}^{2r}K(I-P_{n})x\right)[t,t]+\left(\delta_{j}^{2r}K(I-P_{n})x\right)[t]~D^{(0,1)}\kappa_{2}(s,t)~&j\geq i.\\
\end{cases}

Then by Lemma
2.1
, Lemma
2.2
and the estimate (
2.5
), we have

\left\lVert(g_{j}^{s})^{\prime}(t)\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C_{9}C_{1}\left\lVert(I-P_{n})x\right\rVert_{\infty}h^{-2r}\leq C_{9}C_{2}C_{1}\left\lVert x^{(2r+1)}\right\rVert_{\infty}h,

where
C_{9}
is some constant. Replacing
g_{j}^{s}
in ((
3.23
)), we write

\displaystyle K(I-P_{n})K(I-P_{n})x(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}~\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}g_{j}^{s}(t)\Psi_{j}(t)~dt+\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}g_{i}^{s}(t)\Psi_{i}(t)~dt.

By employing similar argument as presented in the proof of Proposition
1
, we acquire

\left|K(I-P_{n})K(I-P_{n})x(s)\right|\leq 2C_{9}C_{2}C_{1}\left\lVert x^{(2r+1)}\right\rVert_{\infty}h^{2r+3}.

This proves the proposition for case
r\geq 1
.

If
r=0
and let
x\in C^{2}([0,1])
, then from Kulkarni-Nidhin
(
9
, Proposition 3.7)
it follows that

\left\lVert K(I-P_{n})K(I-P_{n})x\right\rVert_{\infty}=O(h^{4}).

That is our desired estimate.
∎

One of our main results which determine the order of convergence for both the collocation solution
\phi_{n}^{C}
and the iterated collocation solution
\phi_{n}^{S}
is proved as follows.

Theorem 4
.

Let
K
be the Fredholm integral operator defined by (
1.1
) with a Green’s function type kernel. Let
\phi\in C^{2r+2}([0,1])
be the unique solution of the equation (
1.2
) and
P_{n}
be the interpolatory operator onto the approximating space
X_{n}
defined by (
2.4
). Let
\phi_{n}^{C}
be the unique solution of (
3.18
) and the iterated collocation solution
\phi_{n}^{S}
is defined by (
3.19
). Then

\displaystyle\left\lVert\phi-\phi_{n}^{C}\right\rVert_{\infty}=O\left(h^{2r+1}\right),\quad\left\lVert\phi-\phi_{n}^{S}\right\rVert_{\infty}=O\left(h^{2r+2}\right).

Proof.

We quote the following error estimates from Atkinson
(
1
, Section 3.4)
:

\left\lVert\phi-\phi_{n}^{C}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C_{10}\left\lVert(I-P_{n})\phi\right\rVert_{\infty}

and

\left\lVert\phi-\phi_{n}^{S}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C_{10}\left\lVert K(I-P_{n})\phi\right\rVert_{\infty},

where
C_{10}
is a constant independent of
n
.
Therefore, by inequality (
2.5
) and Proposition
1
, we have

\left\lVert\phi-\phi_{n}^{C}\right\rVert_{\infty}=O\left(h^{2r+1}\right),\quad\left\lVert\phi-\phi_{n}^{S}\right\rVert_{\infty}=O\left(h^{2r+2}\right).

∎

We now prove our main result by the following theorem which determine the orders of convergence for both the modified collocation solution
\phi_{n}^{M}
and the iterated modified collocation solution
\tilde{\phi}_{n}^{M}
.

Theorem 5
.

Let
K
be the Fredholm integral operator defined by (
1.1
) with a Green’s function type kernel. Let
\phi\in C^{2r+2}([0,1])
be the unique solution of the integral equation (
1.2
). Let
P_{n}
be the interpolatory operator onto the approximating space
X_{n}
defined by (
2.4
). Let
\phi_{n}^{M}
be the unique solution of (
3.20
) and the iterated modified collocation solution
\tilde{\phi}_{n}^{M}
is defined by (
3.21
). Then

\left\lVert\phi-\phi_{n}^{M}\right\rVert_{\infty}=\begin{cases}O\left(h^{2r+2}\right)~~~&r\geq 1,\\
O\left(h^{3}\right)~~~&r=0,\\
\end{cases}

and

\left\lVert\phi-\tilde{\phi}_{n}^{M}\right\rVert_{\infty}=\begin{cases}O\left(h^{2r+3}\right)~~~&r\geq 1,\\
O\left(h^{4}\right)~~~&r=0.\\
\end{cases}

Proof.

Since
K
is a bounded linear operator and using (
2.5
), we get

\left\lVert K(I-P_{n})K(I-P_{n})x\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C_{2}\left\lVert K\right\rVert\left\lVert(K(I-P_{n})x)^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\infty}h.

It follows from inequality (
2.4
) that

\displaystyle\left\lVert K(I-P_{n})K(I-P_{n})x\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}C_{1}\left\lVert K\right\rVert\left\lVert(I-P_{n})x\right\rVert_{\infty}h

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}C_{2}\left\lVert K\right\rVert\left\lVert I-P_{n}\right\rVert\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty}h

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}C_{2}(1+p)\left\lVert K\right\rVert\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty}h.

Then,

\left\lVert K(I-P_{n})K(I-P_{n})\right\rVert=O(h).

(3.24)

We quote the following inequality from Kulkarni
[
8
]
:

For all large
n
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\phi-\phi_{n}^{M}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C_{11}\left\lVert(I-P_{n})K(I-P_{n})\phi\right\rVert_{\infty},

and

\displaystyle\left\lVert\phi-\tilde{\phi}_{n}^{M}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\left\lVert(I-K)^{-1}\right\rVert(\left\lVert K(I-P_{n})K(I-P_{n})\phi\right\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle+\left\lVert K(I-P_{n})K(I-P_{n})\right\rVert\left\lVert\phi-\phi_{n}^{M}\right\rVert_{\infty}),

where

From Proposition

\left\lVert\phi-\phi_{n}^{M}\right\rVert_{\infty}=\begin{cases}O\left(h^{2r+2}\right)~~~&r\geq 1,\\

4
Conclusion

We consider collocation method and its variants for approximate solutions of a Fredholm integral equation. The kernel of the integral operator is of the type of Green’s function, and the projection is chosen to be an interpolatory projection at
2r+1
collocation points. We have seen that if we choose collocation points as Gauss points (i.e. zeros of the Legendre polynomials), then the iterative methods improve the orders of convergence. It has been observed that if the collocation points are not the Gauss points, then the collocation solution
\phi_{n}^{C}
and iterated collocation solution
\phi_{n}^{S}
converge at the same rate.

For example, consider
X=C[0,1]
and
X_{n}
be the space of piecewise constant functions with respect to the uniform partition of
[0,1]
, defined as
\{0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{n}=1\}
, where
t_{j}=\frac{j}{n}
for
j=0,1,\ldots,n
. Let
P_{n}:C[0,1]\to X_{n}
be the interpolatory map at the points
\tau_{j}=\frac{3j-2}{3n}
, for
j=1,\ldots,n
. Consider the Fredholm integral equation as

x-Kx=2s,\quad x\in X,\;s\in[0,1],

and let
K
be the following linear operator defined on
C[0,1]
as

(Kx)(s)=\int_{0}^{1}s\>x(t)\>dt,\quad s\in[0,1].

Note that
K
is a compact operator and
1
is not an eigenvalue of
K
. Also, we see that the above integral equation has a unique solution in
C[0,1]
as
\phi(s)=4s
for all
s\in[0,1]
.
Observe that

(I-P_{n})\phi(s)=4s-\frac{4(3j-2)}{3n},\quad s\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}],

and

\int_{0}^{1}(I-P_{n})\phi(t)~dt=\sum_{j=1}^{n}~\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\left(4t-\frac{4(3j-2)}{3n}\right)~dt=\frac{2}{3n}.

Therefore

\left\lVert K(I-P_{n})\phi\right\rVert_{\infty}=\max_{s\in[0,1]}\left|\left(\int_{0}^{1}(I-P_{n})\phi(t)~dt\right)s\right|=\frac{2}{3n},

\left\lVert(I-P_{n})\phi\right\rVert_{\infty}=\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\left\{\max_{s\in[t_{j-1},t_{j}]}|(I-P_{n})\phi(s)|\right\}\leq\frac{8}{3n}.

We know that the iterated collocation solution is superconvergent if and only if

\frac{\left\lVert K(I-P_{n})\phi\right\rVert_{\infty}}{\left\lVert(I-P_{n})\phi\right\rVert_{\infty}}\to 0\;\text{as}\;n\to\infty.

In this case, the above condition fails as we see that

\frac{\left\lVert K(I-P_{n})\phi\right\rVert_{\infty}}{\left\lVert(I-P_{n})\phi\right\rVert_{\infty}}\geq\frac{1}{4}.

However, in our article we consider the sequence of projections as the interpolation at
2r+1
points (which are not Gauss points) onto a space of piecewise polynomials of degree
\leq 2r
with respect to a uniform partition of
[0,1]
. Then we apply the iterated collocation and iterated modified collocation methods based on these projections. The results obtained in the Theorem
4
and Theorem
5
, in which the orders of convergence of the iterated solutions are obtained are the main contribution of this paper. These results show the improvement in the orders of convergence for both the iterated collocation solution and iterated modified collocation solution, respectively.

Birational geometry of Calabi–Yau pairs and 3 3 -dimensional Cremona transformations
Carolina Araujo, Alessio Corti, Alex Massarenti

Abstract
. In this paper we develop a framework that allows one to describe the birational geometry of Calabi–Yau pairs ( X , D ) (X,D) . After establishing some general results for Calabi–Yau pairs ( X , D ) (X,D) with mild singularities, we focus on the special case when X = ℙ 3 X=\mathbb{P}^{3} and D ⊂ ℙ 3 D\subset\mathbb{P}^{3} is a quartic surface. We investigate how the appearance of increasingly worse singularities in D D enriches the birational geometry of the pair ( ℙ 3 , D ) (\mathbb{P}^{3},D) , and lead to interesting subgroups of the Cremona group of ℙ 3 \mathbb{P}^{3} .

1.2.
Calabi-Yau pairs

Definition 1.1
.

A

Calabi–Yau (CY) pair
is a pair
(X,D)
consisting of a
normal projective variety
X
and an effective integral Weil divisor

D
on
X
such that
K_{X}+D
is linearly equivalent to
0
.
(In particular,
K_{X}+D
is Cartier.)
There
exists a top degree rational differential form
\omega=\omega_{X,D}

on
X
, unique up to multiplication by a nonzero constant, such that

D+\divisor_{X}\omega=0
. With a slight abuse of language, we call

\omega
the
volume form
of the CY pair
(X,D)
.

Let
(X,D_{X})
and
(Y,D_{Y})
be CY pairs, with associated volume forms
\omega_{X,D_{X}}
and
\omega_{Y,D_{Y}}
.
A birational map
\varphi\colon X\dasharrow Y
is
volume preserving
if there exists a
nonzero constant
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
such that

\varphi^{*}(\omega_{Y,D_{Y}})=\lambda\omega_{X,D_{X}}.

We abuse language and say that
\varphi
is a
birational map
of CY pairs
to mean that it is a volume preserving map.

We denote by
\Bir(X,D)
the group of volume preserving birational
self-maps of the CY pair
(X,D)
, and by
\Bir\big((X,D_{X}),(Y,D_{Y})\big)
the set of volume preserving birational maps between CY pairs
(X,D_{X})
and
(Y,D_{Y})
.

Volume preserving birational maps are called
crepant birational
in
[
Kol13
]
.

Next we introduce several natural classes of singularities of pairs.
Given a CY pair
(X,D)
and a divisorial valuation
E
of

, we denote by

the discrepancy of

(X,D)

[

.
We denote by
\centre_{E}X

X

Depending on the context, we view it as a scheme-theoretic point or a subvariety of
X

When
\codim_{(\centre_{E}X)}X\geq 2
, we say that

has
small centre
on
X
.

Definition 1.2
.

A pair
(X,D)
has
terminal singularities
if for all

(a)

if
\codim_{z}X\leq 2
, then both
X
and

(b)

\codim_{z}X>2

A pair
(X,D)

X

a(E,K_{X}+D)\geq 0
.
Following common usage, we say that a pair “is” terminal (respectively canonical) if it has terminal (respectively canonical) singularities.

.

(1)

Suppose that

If the pair

We say that a pair

Remark 1.10

Let

Given an integral Weil divisor

Theorem A
.

Let

(1)

4.
Extremal contractions

In this section we classify extremal contractions of different types
in various contexts. These results are the toolkit that we need to prove
Theorems B and C. Indeed, the proofs of these results centre on the classification of
all possible volume preserving Sarkisov links

(X,D)/S\dasharrow(X^{\dagger},D^{\dagger})/S^{\dagger}

that start from a known and given Mf CY pair
(X,D)/S
. The links
are themselves constituted of elementary modifications that are
naturally associated to contractions of extremal rays: volume
preserving extremal divisorial contractions; flips, flops,
antiflips; and Mori fiber spaces. These elementary constituents are the
topic of this section. The results are technical and we advise the
reader to skip this section on first reading and use it as
reference, coming back to it as needed.

4.2.
Extremal neighbourhoods

Definition 4.2
.

An

is the analytic germ of a
3-fold

around a projective curve

\Gamma\subset X

We say that the extremal neighbourhood is

We say that a nonsingular extremal neighbourhood is

\Gamma

In our situation, the curve

Convention 4.3
.

An extremal neighbourhood is an analytic germ, that is, an equivalence class of analytic neighbourhoods
(\Gamma\subset X)
, called

(\Gamma\subset X_{1})\quad\text{and}\quad(\Gamma\subset X_{2})

are equivalent if there is a third representative contained in both:

\textstyle{(\Gamma\subset X_{1})}
\textstyle{(\Gamma\subset X_{3})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(\Gamma\subset X_{2}).}

When we speak of an extremal neighbourhood

we are often (and without saying so explicitly) abusing language and actually taking
(\Gamma\subset X)
to be a representative, which we may allow ourselves subsequently to shrink at our convenience.

We often (and sometimes implicitly) assume that the extremal neighbourhood is
contractible

where

(i)

\pi

(ii)

All of this is common practice when writing about germs, and we trust that it will be clear from the context what is meant.

Then

has two irreducible components, given by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\alpha=-\frac{B}{A}\gamma+\delta,\\
\beta=-\frac{C}{A}\gamma\end{array}\right.\ \ \ and\ \ \ \ \left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\alpha=-\delta,\\
\beta=\frac{C}{A}\gamma-\frac{B}{A}\delta.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

We have that

\phi=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&\delta\end{array}\right)\in G_{Q}
if and only if

\rank\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}A&B&C\\
\alpha^{2}A+\alpha\gamma B+\gamma^{2}C&2\alpha\beta A+\alpha\delta B+\beta\gamma B+2\gamma\delta C&\beta^{2}A+\beta\delta B+\delta^{2}C\end{array}\right)\leq 1,

or, equivalently, if and only if

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\alpha^{2}AC+\alpha\gamma BC+\gamma^{2}C^{2}-\beta^{2}A^{2}-\beta\delta AB-\delta^{2}AC=0,\\

),

\beta=\frac{C}{A}\gamma-\frac{B}{A}\delta,\end{array}\right.\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\alpha=-\frac{B}{A}\gamma+\delta,\\
\beta=-\frac{C}{A}\gamma,\end{array}\right.\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\alpha=\frac{-B+\varepsilon}{2A}\gamma,\\

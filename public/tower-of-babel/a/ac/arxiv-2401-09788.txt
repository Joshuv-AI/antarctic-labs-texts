Geometric inequalities and their stabilities for modified quermassintegrals in hyperbolic space
Chaoqun Gao, Rong Zhou

Abstract
. In this paper, we first consider the curve case of Hu-Li-Wei’s flow for shifted principal curvatures of h-convex hypersurfaces in ℍ n + 1 \mathbb{H}^{n+1} proposed in [ 10 ] . We prove that if the initial closed curve is smooth and strictly h-convex, then the solution exists for all time and preserves strict h-convexity along the flow. Moreover, the evolving curve converges smoothly and exponentially to a geodesic circle centered at the origin. The key ingredient in our proof is the Heintze-Karcher type inequality for h-convex curves proved recently in [ 14 ] . As an application, we then provide a new proof of geometric inequalities involving weighted curvature integrals and modified quermassintegrals for h-convex curves in ℍ 2 \mathbb{H}^{2} . We finally discuss the stability of these inequalities as well as Alexandrov-Fenchel type inequalities for modified quermassintegrals for strictly h-convex domains in ℍ n + 1 \mathbb{H}^{n+1} .

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we discuss some locally constrained curvature flows and their applications to proving inequalities involving weighted curvature integrals and modified quermassintegrals for smooth h-convex domains in hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
.

We consider hyperbolic space as a warped product space
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}=[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{S}^{n}
equipped with metric
\overline{g}=\mathrm{d}r^{2}+\phi^{2}(r)g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}
, where
\phi(r)=\sinh r,\ r\in[0,\infty)
. Let
\Phi(r)=\cosh r-1
. It’s well known that
V=\overline{\nabla}\Phi=\phi(r)\partial_{r}
is a conformal killing field.

A smooth bounded domain
\Omega
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
is said to be h-convex (resp. strictly h-convex) if the principal curvatures of the boundary
\partial\Omega
satisfy
\kappa_{i}\geqslant 1
(resp.
\kappa_{i}>1
) for all
i=1,2,\cdots,n
. Let
M
be a smooth closed hypersurface, denote
\nu
as its unit outward normal vector, and its support function is defined by
u=\langle V,\nu\rangle
. We say that
M
is star-shaped if its support function
u
is positive everywhere on
M
.

1.1.
Curvature flows

Locally constrained curvature flows have been studied widely in recent years. Brendle, Guan and Li
[
3
]
(see also
[
9
]
) introduced the flow
X:M^{n}\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{N}^{n+1}(K)

(1.1)

\partial_{t}X(x,t)=\left(\dfrac{\phi^{\prime}(r)E_{k-1}(\kappa)}{E_{k}(\kappa)}-u\right)\nu(x,t),\ \ k=1,2,\cdots,n

for a family of smooth closed hypersurfaces in
n+1
dimensional space forms with constant sectional curvature
K=1,-1
and discuss some convergence results under several assumptions when
n\geqslant 2
. Here
E_{k}(\kappa)
denotes the
k
th normalized elementary symmetric function of principal curvatures
\kappa
of
M_{t}=X(M,t)
.

For
n\geqslant 2
, Hu, Li and Wei
[
10
]
used the tensor maximum principle to verify that the h-convexity preserves along the flow (
1.1
) and thus proved the long time existence and exponential convergence to a geodesic sphere if the initial hypersurface is h-convex in hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
.

Kwong, Wei, Wheeler and Wheeler
[
13
]
considered the curve case of (
1.1
) in space forms and proved that if the initial curve is strictly convex (i.e. the curvature of curve
\kappa>0
), then the solution curve exists for all time and preserves strict convexity. Moreover, the evolving curve smoothly and exponentially converges to a geodesic circle centered at the origin.

In
[
10
]
, Hu, Li and Wei introduced a new locally constrained curvature flow
X:M^{n}\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
,

(1.2)

\partial_{t}X(x,t)=\left((\phi^{\prime}(r)-u)\dfrac{E_{k-1}(\tilde{\kappa})}{E_{k}(\tilde{\kappa})}-u\right)\nu(x,t),\ \ k=1,2,\cdots,n,

where
\tilde{\kappa}_{i}=\kappa_{i}-1
are shifted principal curvatures of
M_{t}=X(M,t)
,
E_{k}(\tilde{\kappa})
is the normalized
k
th elementary symmetric function of
\tilde{\kappa}
. They proved the following long time existence and convergence results of the flow (
1.2
) for strictly h-convex hypersurfaces in higher dimensions (
n\geqslant 2
).

Theorem 1.1

(
[
10
, Theorem 1.6]
)
.

Let
X_{0}:M^{n}\to\mathbb{H}^{n+1}\ (n\geqslant 2)
be a smooth embedding such that
M_{0}=X_{0}(M)
is a smooth, strictly h-convex hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. Then the flow (
1.2
) has a smooth solution for all time
t\in[0,\infty)
, and
M_{t}=X_{t}(M)
is strictly h-convex for each
t>0
and converges smoothly and exponentially to a geodesic sphere centered at the origin.

The convergence results for
n=1
has not been obtained yet. In the first part of this paper, we aim to make up this gap. When
n=1
, the flow (
1.2
) becomes

(1.3)

\partial_{t}X(x,t)=\left(\dfrac{\phi^{\prime}(r)-u}{\kappa-1}-u\right)\nu(x,t).

Let’s consider a family of immersed closed strictly h-convex curves
X:\mathbb{S}^{1}\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{H}^{2}
satisfying the above flow equation and we will prove the long time existence and convergence results for the flow (
1.3
).

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X_{0}:\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{H}^{2}
be a smooth strictly h-convex closed curve containing the origin. There exists a unique solution
X:\mathbb{S}^{1}\times[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{H}^{2}
to the flow (
1.3
) such that
X(\cdot,0)=X_{0}
. For each
t\in[0,\infty)
,
\gamma_{t}=X(\mathbb{S}^{1},t)
is strictly h-convex and converges smoothly and exponentially to a geodesic circle
\gamma_{\infty}=X_{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
centered at the origin.

We remark that for a strictly h-convex initial curve, the short time existence of the flow (
1.3
) is guaranteed by standard theory of parabolic equations. The long time existence follows by the standard procedure after a priori estimates. To show the convergence, the key ingredient is the Heintze-Karcher type inequality for h-convex curves proved in
[
14
, Proposition 8.2]
, from which we know that the area of domains enclosed by h-convex curves is monotonically non-decreasing along the flow (
1.3
). It also helps us to find the limit curve be a geodesic circle and to prove smooth convergence. In the proof of exponential convergence, inspired by
[
13
, Section 4.3]
and
[
21
, Section 9]
, we consider the linearization of the flow (
3.13
) at infinity and used the spherical harmonics to expand functions in
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
to obtain asymptotic behavior around the infinite time.

1.2.
Applications to geometric inequalities

In the second part of this paper, we will use the flow (
1.3
) to provide a new proof of some geometric inequalities involving weighted curvature integrals and modified quermassintegrals for h-convex curves in
\mathbb{H}^{2}
. (See Section
2.2
for precise definitions.)

Many scholars studied the proof of Alexandrov-Fenchel type inequalities in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
by using curvature flow methods. In 2014, Wang and Xia
[
20
]
proved the quermassintegral inequalities for a h-convex domain
\Omega
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}\ (n\geqslant 2)
,

(1.4)

W_{k}(\Omega)\geqslant f_{k}\circ f_{\ell}^{-1}(W_{\ell}(\Omega)),\ \ 0\leqslant\ell<k\leqslant n

with equality holds if and only if
\Omega
is a geodesic ball. Here
f_{k}:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is a monotone function defined by
f_{k}(r)=W_{k}(\overline{B}_{r})
, the
k
th quermassintegral for the geodesic ball of radius
r
, and
f_{\ell}^{-1}
is the inverse function of
f_{\ell}
. In 2021, Andrews, Chen and Wei
[
1
]
first introduced modified quermassintegrals for h-convex domains and proved inequalities for a bounded, smooth strictly h-convex domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}^{n+1}\ (n\geqslant 2)
,

(1.5)

\tilde{W}_{k}(\Omega)\geqslant\tilde{f}_{k}\circ\tilde{f}_{\ell}^{-1}(\tilde{W}_{\ell}(\Omega)),\ \ 0\leqslant\ell<k\leqslant n.

Equality holds in (
1.5
) if and only if
\Omega
is a geodesic ball. Here
\tilde{f}_{k}:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is a monotone function defined by
\tilde{f}_{k}(r)=\tilde{W}_{k}(\overline{B}_{r})
, the
k
th modified quermassintegral for the geodesic ball of radius
r
, and
\tilde{f}_{\ell}^{-1}
is the inverse function of
\tilde{f}_{\ell}
.

Later, Hu-Li-Wei
[
10
]
applied the flow (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) for h-convex hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
to provide a new proof of inequalities (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) respectively. They also used the flow (
1.2
) to derive inequalities involving weighted curvature integrals and modified quermassintegrals for a strictly h-convex domain
\Omega
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}\ (n\geqslant 2)
(see
[
10
, Theorem 1.8]
): For each
k=1,2,\cdots,n
, there holds

(1.6)

\int_{\partial\Omega}\left(\phi^{\prime}(r)-u\right)E_{k}(\tilde{\kappa})\mathrm{d}\mu\geqslant\tilde{g}_{k}\circ\tilde{f}_{k}^{-1}(\tilde{W}_{k}(\Omega)),

where
\tilde{g}_{k}(r)=\int_{\partial\overline{B}_{r}}\left(\phi^{\prime}(r)-u\right)E_{k}(\tilde{\kappa})\mathrm{d}\mu
,
\tilde{f}_{k}(r)=\tilde{W}_{k}(\overline{B}_{r})
. Equality holds in (
1.6
) if and only if
\Omega
is a geodesic ball centered at the origin.

Recently, Kwong and Wei
[
12
]
investigated a family of three-term geometric inequalities involving weighted curvature integrals and quermassintegrals for hypersurfaces in space forms. As a special case of
[
12
, Theorem 5.4]
, they proved that for a smooth bounded h-convex domain
\Omega
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}\ (n\geqslant 2)
, there holds

(1.7)

\int_{\partial\Omega}\Phi E_{k}(\kappa)\mathrm{d}\mu+kW_{k-1}(\Omega)\geqslant(\xi_{k}+kf_{k-1})\circ f_{\ell}^{-1}(W_{\ell}(\Omega))

for all
k=1,2,\cdots,n
and
l=0,1,\cdots,k
. Equality holds in (
1.7
) if and only if
\Omega
is a geodesic ball centered at the origin.
\xi_{k}(r)=\int_{\partial\overline{B}_{r}}\Phi E_{k}(\kappa)\mathrm{d}\mu
,
f_{k}(r)=W_{k}(\overline{B}_{r})
.

Li and Xu proposed a conjecture of horospherical
p
-Minkowski inequalities in
[
14
, Conjecture 10.5]
. Especially, they proved some inequalities which are special cases of the conjecture from a horospherically convex geometric point of view. They used the flow (
1.2
) and the convergence result (Theorem
1.1
) to prove the following three-term inequality involving weighted curvature integrals and modified quermassintegrals for strictly h-convex hypersurfaces. We state their result in our setting.

Theorem 1.3

(
[
14
, Theorem 10.9]
)
.

Let
M
be a smooth strictly h-convex hypersurface enclosing a domain
\Omega
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}\ (n\geqslant 2)
. For any
k=1,2,\cdots,n
, there holds

(1.8)

\int_{M}(\Phi-u)E_{k}(\tilde{\kappa})\mathrm{d}\mu+(n-2k)\tilde{W}_{k}(\Omega)\geqslant\tilde{h}_{k}\circ\tilde{f}_{k}^{-1}(\tilde{W}_{k}(\Omega)),

where
\tilde{h}_{k}(r)=\int_{\partial\overline{B}_{r}}(\Phi-u)E_{k}(\tilde{\kappa})\mathrm{d}\mu+(n-2k)\tilde{W}_{k}(\overline{B}_{r})
,
\tilde{f}_{k}(r)=\tilde{W}_{k}(\overline{B}_{r})
,
\overline{B}_{r}
is a geodesic ball centered at the origin,
\tilde{f}_{k}^{-1}
is the inverse function of
\tilde{f}_{k}(r)
. Equality holds if and only if
M
is a geodesic sphere centered at the origin.

Note that when
n=1
, we have
\tilde{W}_{1}(\Omega)=L[\gamma]-A[\gamma]
by (
2.10
), where
L[\gamma]
denotes the length of the curve
\gamma
,
A[\gamma]
denotes the area of the domain
\Omega
enclosed by
\gamma
. It has been proved in
[
14
, Theorem 10.13]
that (
1.8
) holds for a smooth strictly h-convex curve in
\mathbb{H}^{2}
by using horospherical Gauss map.

Motivated by the work
[
12
,
14
]
, we provide a new proof of inequality (
1.8
) for h-convex curves in
\mathbb{H}^{2}
by using the flow (
1.3
) and the convergence result (Theorem
1.2
).

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\gamma
be a smooth h-convex closed curve enclosing a domain
\Omega
in
\mathbb{H}^{2}
. There holds

(1.9)

\int_{\gamma}(\Phi-u)(\kappa-1)\mathrm{d}s+(L[\gamma]-A[\gamma])\geqslant\dfrac{1}{2\pi}(L[\gamma]-A[\gamma])^{2}

with equality if and only if
\gamma
is a geodesic circle centered at the origin. Here
\Phi(r)=\cosh r-1
,
u=\langle V,\nu\rangle
is the support function of
\gamma
,
L[\gamma]
denotes the length of
\gamma
,
A[\gamma]
denotes the area of the domain
\Omega
enclosed by
\gamma
.

We first use the flow (
1.3
) to prove inequality (
1.9
) for strictly h-convex curves in
\mathbb{H}^{2}
. Then inequality (
1.9
) for h-convex curves follows by an approximation argument following
[
7
]
and
[
13
]
.

1.3.
Stability results

We finally consider the stability of inequalities (
1.9
) for curves, (
1.5
) and (
1.8
) for hypersurfaces characterized by the Hausdorff distance between the hypersurface and an appropriate geodesic sphere. Recall that

(1.10)

\mathrm{dist}(K,L)=\inf\{\delta>0:K\subset B_{\delta}(L)\ \mbox{and}\ L\subset B_{\delta}(K)\}

is the Hausdorff distance between two compact sets
K
and
L
.

For
n=1
, we will use Theorem
1.2
to prove the stability of inequality (
1.9
) for strictly h-convex curves.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\gamma
be a smooth strictly h-convex closed curve in
\mathbb{H}^{2}
enclosing a domain
\Omega
. Regard
\gamma=\{(\rho(\theta),\theta):\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{1}\}
as a graph on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
. There exists a constant
C(\rho,\max\limits_{\gamma}|\rho_{\theta}|,\min\limits_{\gamma}\kappa)
and a geodesic circle
\gamma_{\mathbb{H}}
, such that

(1.11)

\mathrm{dist}(\gamma,\gamma_{\mathbb{H}})\leqslant Cf\left[\int_{\gamma}(\Phi-u)(\kappa-1)\mathrm{d}s+(L[\gamma]-A[\gamma])-\dfrac{1}{2\pi}(L[\gamma]-A[\gamma])^{2}\right],

where
f(s)=s^{\frac{1}{2}}+s^{\frac{1}{6}}\ (s\geqslant 0)
, and
\mathrm{dist}(\gamma,\gamma_{\mathbb{H}})
denotes the Hausdorff distance between
\gamma
and
\gamma_{\mathbb{H}}
.

The proof relies on the Poincaré inequality, which is used to characterize the distance between the curve and a geodesic circle.

In
[
17
]
, Sahjwani and Scheuer used flow (
1.1
) to prove the stability of quermassintegral inequalities (
1.4
) when
l=k-1
for h-convex domains in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}\ (n\geqslant 2)
. Their proof relied on the following nearly umbilical theorem.

Theorem 1.6

(
[
16
, Theorem 1.2]
)
.

Let
n\geqslant 2
,
p\in(1,\infty)
. There exists
\varepsilon(n,p)>0
, if
\Sigma
is a closed convex hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
satisfying

(1.12)

|\Sigma|=|\mathbb{S}^{n}|,\ \ \int_{\Sigma}|\mathring{A}|^{p}\mathrm{d}\mu\leqslant\varepsilon,

then there exist a smooth parametrization
\varphi:\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and a point
\mathcal{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
such that the following estimate holds:

(1.13)

\|\varphi-\mathrm{id}-\mathcal{P}\|_{W^{2,p}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}\leqslant C(n,p)\|\mathring{A}\|_{L^{p}(\Sigma)},

where
\mathrm{id}:\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{S}^{n}
is the identity map,
\mathring{A}
is the trace free Weingarten matrix of
\Sigma
.

For
n\geqslant 2
, inspired by the work
[
17
]
, we will use the flow (
1.2
) with the convergence result (Theorem
1.1
) and the nearly umbilical result (Theorem
1.6
) to discuss the stability of inequalities (
1.5
) and (
1.8
) for strictly h-convex hypersurfaces.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
M
be a smooth strictly h-convex closed hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}\ (n\geqslant 2)
enclosing a domain
\Omega
. For any
1\leqslant k\leqslant n-1
, there exist a constant
C=C(\rho_{-}(\Omega),\mathrm{dist}(\tilde{\kappa},\partial\Gamma_{k}^{+}),M,n,k)
and a geodesic sphere
S_{\mathbb{H}}
, such that

(1.14)

\mathrm{dist}(\partial\Omega,S_{\mathbb{H}})\leqslant Cf\left[\tilde{W}_{k+1}(\Omega)-\tilde{f}_{k+1}\circ\tilde{f}_{k}^{-1}\left(\tilde{W}_{k}(\Omega)\right)\right]

and

(1.15)

\mathrm{dist}(\partial\Omega,S_{\mathbb{H}})\leqslant Cf\left[\int_{M}(\Phi-u)E_{k}(\tilde{\kappa})\mathrm{d}\mu+(n-2k)\tilde{W}_{k}(\Omega)-\tilde{h}_{k}\circ\tilde{f}_{k}^{-1}\left(\tilde{W}_{k}(\Omega)\right)\right]

hold, where
f(s)=s^{\frac{1}{2}}+s^{\frac{1}{4}}\ (s\geqslant 0)
,
\mathrm{dist}(\partial\Omega,S_{\mathbb{H}})
is the Hausdorff distance between
\partial\Omega
and
S_{\mathbb{H}}
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we will make some preparations for our proof, including properties of elementary symmetric functions, definitions for quermassintegrals and modified quermassintegrals, some formulas for hypersurfaces in hyperbolic space and general evolution equations for curvature flows.

2.1.
Elementary symmetric functions

Here we review some properties of elementary symmetric functions. For integer
k=1,2,\cdots,n
, for any point
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\cdots,\kappa_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the normalized
k
th elementary symmetric polynomial
E_{k}(\kappa)
is defined by

(2.1)

E_{k}(\kappa)={n\choose k}^{-1}\sum\limits_{1\leqslant i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{k}\leqslant n}\kappa_{i_{1}}\kappa_{i_{2}}\cdots\kappa_{i_{k}}

and has the convention that
E_{0}=1
and
E_{k}(\kappa)=0
for
k>n
. The definition can be extended to symmetric matrices. Let
A=(a_{ij})
be a
n\times n
symmetric matrix, denote
\kappa=\kappa(A)
as the eigenvalue vector of
A
. Define
E_{k}(A)=E_{k}(\kappa(A))
, and we have

(2.2)

E_{k}(A)=\dfrac{(n-m)!}{n!}\delta_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{k}}^{j_{1}j_{2}\cdots j_{k}}a_{i_{1}j_{1}}a_{i_{2}j_{2}}\cdots a_{i_{k}j_{k}},\ \ k=1,2,\cdots,n.

We include some useful properties here but omit their proofs. For more details, we may refer to
[
6
, Section 2]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Denote
\dot{E}^{ij}_{k}=\dfrac{\partial E_{k}(A)}{\partial a_{ij}}
, then we have the following identities.

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i,j}\dot{E}^{ij}_{k}\delta^{j}_{i}=kE_{k-1}(A),

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i,j}\dot{E}^{ij}_{k}a_{ij}=kE_{k}(A),

(2.3)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i,j}\dot{E}^{ij}_{k}(A^{2})_{ij}=nE_{1}(A)E_{k}(A)-(n-k)E_{k+1}(A).

For each
k=1,2,\cdots,n
, let

\Gamma_{k}^{+}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:E_{i}(x)>0,\ i=1,2,\cdots,k\}

be the Garding cone.

Lemma 2.2

(Newton-MacLaurin inequality)
.

If
\kappa\in\Gamma_{k}^{+}
, we have the following inequality

(2.4)

E_{k+1}(\kappa)E_{l-1}(\kappa)\leqslant E_{l}(\kappa)E_{k}(\kappa),\ \ 1\leqslant l\leqslant k.

Equality holds if and only if
\kappa_{1}=\kappa_{2}=\cdots=\kappa_{n}
.

The following divergence free property will be used frequently in integration by parts.

Lemma 2.3
.

If
A=(a_{ij})
is a Codazzi tensor on Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
, let
\{e_{1},e_{2},\cdots,e_{n}\}
be a local frame, then

(2.5)

\sum\limits_{j}\nabla_{j}\dot{E}_{k}^{ij}(A)=0.

2.2.
Quermassintegrals and modified quermassintegrals

Recall that for a convex domain
\Omega
in hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
, the quermassintegrals of
\Omega
are defined as follows (see
[
19
, Definition 2.1]
):

(2.6)

W_{k}(\Omega)=\dfrac{\omega_{k-1}\cdots\omega_{0}}{\omega_{n-1}\cdots\omega_{n-k}}\int_{\mathcal{L}_{k}}\chi(L_{k}\cap\Omega)\mathrm{d}L_{k},\ k=1,2,\cdots,n,

where
\omega_{k}=|\mathbb{S}^{k}|
denotes the area of
k
-dimensional sphere,
\mathcal{L}_{k}
is the space of
k
-dimensional totally geodesic subspaces
L_{k}
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. The function
\chi
is defined to be 1 if
L_{k}\cap\Omega\neq\varnothing
and to be 0 otherwise. Furthermore, we set

(2.7)

W_{0}(\Omega)=|\Omega|,\ \ W_{n+1}(\Omega)=|\mathbb{B}^{n+1}|=\dfrac{\omega_{n}}{n+1}.

If the boundary of
\Omega
is smooth, the quermassintegrals and the curvature integrals of a smooth domain
\Omega
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
are related by the following equations:

\displaystyle W_{1}(\Omega)=\dfrac{|\partial\Omega|}{n},

(2.8)

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}E_{k}(\kappa)\mathrm{d}\mu=(n-k)W_{k+1}(\Omega)+kW_{k-1}(\Omega),\ k=1,2,\cdots,n-1,

(2.9)

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}E_{n}(\kappa)\mathrm{d}\mu=\omega_{n}+nW_{n-1}(\Omega).

Recently, Andrews-Chen-Wei
[
1
]
introduced modified quermassintegrals for h-convex domains in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
:

(2.10)

\tilde{W}_{k}(\Omega):=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-i}{k\choose i}W_{i}(\Omega),\ \ k=0,1,\cdots,n.

We know that
\tilde{W}_{k}(\Omega)
is a linear combination of the quermassintegrals of
\Omega
. It can be verified that (see
[
14
, Lemma 10.1]
)

(2.11)

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}E_{k}(\tilde{\kappa})\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n-k)\tilde{W}_{k+1}(\Omega)+(n-2k)\tilde{W}_{k}(\Omega),\ k=1,2,\cdots,n-1,

(2.12)

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}E_{n}(\tilde{\kappa})\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{n}-n\tilde{W}_{n}(\Omega),

where
\tilde{\kappa}_{i}=\kappa_{i}-1
are the shifted principal curvatures of the hypersurface
\partial\Omega
. These new quermassintegrals satisfy the following nice variational formula (see
[
1
, Lemma 2.4]
)

(2.13)

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\tilde{W}_{k}(\Omega_{t})=\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}}\eta E_{k}(\tilde{\kappa})\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}

along any variation in the direction of outward normal with speed function
\eta
.

2.3.
Hypersurfaces in hyperbolic space

In this paper, we consider
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}=\mathbb{S}^{n}\times[0,\infty)
as a warped product manifold equipped with metric

(2.14)

\overline{g}=\mathrm{d}r^{2}+\phi^{2}(r)g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}},

where
\phi(r)=\sinh r
,
r\in[0,\infty)
,
g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}
denotes the standard metric on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Let

(2.15)

\Phi(r)=\int_{0}^{r}\phi(s)\mathrm{d}s=\cosh r-1.

It’s well known that
V=\overline{\nabla}\Phi=\phi(r)\partial_{r}
is a conformal Killing field on
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
, that is

(2.16)

\overline{\nabla}V=\phi^{\prime}(r)\overline{g}.

Let
n=1
. Let
\gamma=X(\theta)
be a smooth closed curve with its position vector
X:\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{H}^{2}
. Denote
v=|X_{\theta}|=\sqrt{\phi^{2}(r)+r_{\theta}^{2}}
, and define the arc-length parameter
s
by

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial s}=\dfrac{1}{v}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\theta},

then
T=\dfrac{\partial X}{\partial s}=\dfrac{1}{v}X_{\theta}
is the unit tangent vector. Denote
\nu
as the unit outward normal vector and
\kappa
as the curvature of
\gamma
, we have the Frenet formula

(2.17)

\overline{\nabla}_{T}T=-\kappa\nu,\ \ \overline{\nabla}_{T}\nu=\kappa T.

It has been verified in
[
13
, Proposition 3.2]
that the following formulas hold for smooth curves, which can be regarded as the special case for higher dimensions.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\gamma
be a smooth closed curve in
\mathbb{H}^{2}
with
s
be its arc-length parameter. Let
u=\langle V,\nu\rangle
be its support function,
\kappa
be its curvature, then

(2.18)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial\phi^{\prime}}{\partial s}=\langle V,T\rangle,\ \ \dfrac{\partial^{2}\phi^{\prime}}{\partial s^{2}}=\phi^{\prime}-\kappa u,

(2.19)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial s}=\kappa\langle V,T\rangle,\ \,\dfrac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial s^{2}}=\dfrac{\partial\kappa}{\partial s}\langle V,T\rangle+\kappa(\phi^{\prime}-\kappa u).

Moreover,

(2.20)

\int_{\gamma}\left(\phi^{\prime}(r)-\kappa u\right)\mathrm{d}s=0.

Let
n\geqslant 2
. Let
M
be a smooth closed hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
with induced metric
g
and unit outward normal vector
\nu
. Let
\{x^{1},x^{2},\cdots,x^{n}\}
be a local coordinate system on
M
, we denote
g_{ij}=g(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})
and its second fundamental form
h_{ij}=\langle\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{i}}\nu,\partial_{j}\rangle
. The Weingarten matrix is denoted by
A=(h^{i}_{j})
, where
h^{i}_{j}=g^{ik}h_{kj}
, and
(g^{ij})
is the inverse matrix of
(g_{ij})
. The principal curvatures
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\cdots,\kappa_{n})
are eigenvalues of the Weingarten matrix
A
. The connection on
M
is denoted by
\nabla
.

The following formulas hold for smooth hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
, see
[
8
, Lemmas 2.2 and 2.6]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
(M,g)
be a smooth hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
with local frame
\{\partial_{1},\cdots,\partial_{n}\}
, then
\Phi|_{M}
satisfies

(2.21)

\nabla_{i}\Phi=\langle V,e_{i}\rangle,\ \ \nabla_{j}\nabla_{i}\Phi=\phi^{\prime}g_{ij}-uh_{ij},

and the support function
u=\langle V,\nu\rangle
satisfies

(2.22)

\nabla_{i}u=\langle V,e_{l}\rangle h_{i}^{l},\ \ \nabla_{j}\nabla_{i}u=\langle V,\nabla h_{ij}\rangle+\phi^{\prime}h_{ij}-u(h^{2})_{ij},

where
(h^{2})_{ij}=h_{ik}h^{k}_{j}
.

We also have the following Minkowski formulas.

Lemma 2.6

(
[
8
, Proposition 2.5]
)
.

Let
M
be a smooth closed hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. Then

(2.23)

\int_{M}\phi^{\prime}E_{k}(\kappa)\mathrm{d}\mu=\int_{M}uE_{k+1}(\kappa)\mathrm{d}\mu,\ \ k=0,1,\cdots,n-1,

where
E_{k}(\kappa)
is the
k
th mean curvature of
M
.

For shifted
k
th mean curvature, the Minkowski type formulas also hold.

Lemma 2.7

(
[
10
, Lemma 2.6]
)
.

Let
M
be a smooth closed hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. Let
\tilde{\kappa}_{i}=\kappa_{i}-1
be the shifted principal curvatures of
M
, then

(2.24)

\int_{M}(\phi^{\prime}-u)E_{k}(\tilde{\kappa})\mathrm{d}\mu=\int_{M}uE_{k+1}(\tilde{\kappa})\mathrm{d}\mu,\ \ k=0,1,\cdots,n-1.

2.4.
General evolution equations

Let
X:M^{n}\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
be a family of immersed hypersurfaces satisfying the outward normal variation with speed
\eta

(2.25)

\partial_{t}X(x,t)=\eta(x,t)\nu(x,t).

Denote
M_{t}=X_{t}(M)
and let
\{\partial_{1},\partial_{2},\cdots,\partial_{n}\}
be local frame on
M_{t}
. Then the induced metric
g_{ij}
, the unit outward normal vector
\nu
, the second fundamental form
h_{ij}
, the weingarten tensor
h^{i}_{j}
, the induced area element
\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}
evolve as follows (see
[
8
, Lemma 3.1]
):

(2.26)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}g_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\eta h_{ij},

(2.27)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\nu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\nabla\eta,

(2.28)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}h_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\nabla_{j}\nabla_{i}\eta+\eta(h^{2})_{ij}+\eta g_{ij},

(2.29)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}h^{i}_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\nabla_{j}\nabla^{i}\eta-\eta(h^{2})^{i}_{j}+\eta\delta^{i}_{j},

(2.30)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta H\mathrm{d}\mu_{t},

where
H=nE_{1}(\kappa)
is the mean curvature of
M_{t}
,
\nabla
denotes the Levi-Civita connection with respect to the induced metric
g_{ij}
on
M_{t}
.

Moreover, along the flow (
2.25
),
\Phi(r)=\cosh r-1
and the support function
u
evolve as follows:

(2.31)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\Phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\overline{\nabla}\Phi,\partial_{t}X\right\rangle=\langle V,\eta\nu\rangle=\eta u,

(2.32)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{t}X}V,\nu\rangle+\langle V,\partial_{t}\nu\rangle=\eta\phi^{\prime}(r)-\langle V,\nabla\eta\rangle.

Let
\Omega_{t}
be the domain enclosed by
M_{t}
, then the volume of
\Omega_{t}
denoted by
\mathrm{Vol}(\Omega_{t})
evolves by

(2.33)

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\mathrm{Vol}(\Omega_{t})=\int_{M_{t}}\eta\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}.

Now we would like to derive the evolution equation for the shifted
k
th mean curvature
E_{k}(\tilde{\kappa})
(see also
[
10
, Section 9.1]
). Set
S^{i}_{j}=h^{i}_{j}-\delta^{i}_{j}
, then the shifted weingarten tensor satisfies

(2.34)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}S^{i}_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\nabla_{j}\nabla^{i}\eta-\eta\left((S^{2})^{i}_{j}+2S^{i}_{j}\right).

Denote
\dot{E}_{k}^{ij}(\tilde{\kappa})=\dfrac{\partial E_{k}(\tilde{\kappa})}{\partial S^{i}_{j}}
, then by Lemma
2.1
, we have

(2.35)

\displaystyle\dot{E}_{k}^{ij}(\tilde{\kappa})\delta^{i}_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle kE_{k-1}(\tilde{\kappa}),

(2.36)

\displaystyle\dot{E}_{k}^{ij}(\tilde{\kappa})S^{i}_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle kE_{k}(\tilde{\kappa}),

(2.37)

\displaystyle\dot{E}_{k}^{ij}(\tilde{\kappa})\left(S^{2}\right)^{i}_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle nE_{1}(\tilde{\kappa})E_{k}(\tilde{\kappa})-(n-k)E_{k+1}(\tilde{\kappa}).

From Lemma
2.5
, we get

(2.38)

\displaystyle\nabla_{j}\nabla^{i}\Phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\phi^{\prime}-u)\delta^{i}_{j}-uS^{i}_{j},

(2.39)

\displaystyle\nabla_{j}\nabla^{i}u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle V,\nabla S^{i}_{j}\rangle-u\left((S^{2})^{i}_{j}+2S^{i}_{j}+\delta^{i}_{j}\right)+\phi^{\prime}(S^{i}_{j}+\delta^{i}_{j}),

(2.40)

\displaystyle\nabla_{j}\nabla^{i}(\Phi-u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\phi^{\prime}S^{i}_{j}-\langle V,\nabla S^{i}_{j}\rangle+u\left((S^{2})^{i}_{j}+S^{i}_{j}\right).

Combining the above identities, we obtain

(2.41)

\dot{E}_{k}^{ij}(\tilde{\kappa})\nabla_{j}\nabla^{i}\Phi=k\left((\phi^{\prime}-u)E_{k-1}(\tilde{\kappa})-uE_{k}(\tilde{\kappa})\right),

and the shifted
k
th mean curvature evolves as follows:

(2.42)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}E_{k}(\tilde{\kappa})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\dot{E}_{k}^{ij}(\tilde{\kappa})\nabla_{j}\nabla^{i}\eta-\left(nE_{1}(\tilde{\kappa})E_{k}(\tilde{\kappa})-(n-k)E_{k+1}(\tilde{\kappa})\right)\eta-2kE_{k}(\tilde{\kappa})\eta.

3.
Flow for strictly h-convex curves in hyperbolic space

In this section, we will prove Theorem
1.2
, the long time existence and convergence results for the flow (
1.3
).

Recall that Heintze-Karcher type inequality for h-convex curves is obtained in
[
14
, Proposition 8.2]
. Let
\gamma
be a smooth strictly h-convex curve in
\mathbb{H}^{2}
enclosing a domain
\Omega
, then

(3.1)

\int_{\partial\Omega}\dfrac{\phi^{\prime}(r)-u}{\kappa-1}-u\mathrm{d}s\geqslant 0.

Equality holds in (
3.1
) if and only if
\Omega
is a geodesic ball. This is an important ingredient in our proof.

We will first verify that the difference between the length of curve and the area of the domain enclosed by the curve
L[\gamma_{t}]-A[\gamma_{t}]
preserves along the flow (
1.3
).

Lemma 3.1
.

Along the flow (
1.3
), the smooth closed curve
\gamma_{t}
satisfies

(3.2)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(L[\gamma_{t}]-A[\gamma_{t}]\right)=0,

where
L[\gamma_{t}]
is the length of
\gamma_{t}
,
A[\gamma_{t}]
denotes the area of the domain enclosed by
\gamma_{t}
.

Proof.

Set
\eta=\dfrac{\phi^{\prime}(r)-u}{\kappa-1}-u
. By the evolution equation of length (
2.30
) and area (
2.33
), and Minkowski formula (
2.20
), we know

(3.3)

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(L[\gamma_{t}]-A[\gamma_{t}]\right)=\int_{\gamma_{t}}\eta(\kappa-1)\mathrm{d}s=\int_{\gamma_{t}}\left(\phi^{\prime}(r)-\kappa u\right)\mathrm{d}s=0,

as desired.
∎

3.1.
A priori estimates

We derive some a priori estimates for the flow (
1.3
).

Proposition 3.2

(Curvature Estimate)
.

Let
X_{0}
be a strictly h-convex curve, then
\gamma_{t}
is strictly h-convex along the flow (
1.3
). Furthermore, we have the uniform curvature estimate

(3.4)

\min\kappa_{0}\leqslant\kappa(\cdot,t)\leqslant\max\kappa_{0},

where
\kappa_{0}
is the curvature of
X_{0}
.

Proof.

We calculate the evolution equation of curvature
\kappa(\cdot,t)
for
\gamma_{t}
. Let
s
be the arc-length parameter. After a direct computation, we get

(3.5)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial\kappa}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\kappa-1}\left(-\dfrac{\partial^{2}\phi^{\prime}}{\partial s^{2}}+\dfrac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial s^{2}}\right)+\dfrac{2}{(\kappa-1)^{2}}\left(\dfrac{\partial\phi^{\prime}}{\partial s}-\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial s}\right)\dfrac{\partial\kappa}{\partial s}+\dfrac{\phi^{\prime}-u}{(\kappa-1)^{2}}\dfrac{\partial^{2}\kappa}{\partial s^{2}}

\displaystyle-\dfrac{2(\phi^{\prime}-u)}{(\kappa-1)^{3}}\left(\dfrac{\partial\kappa}{\partial s}\right)^{2}+\dfrac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial s^{2}}-(\kappa^{2}-1)\left(\dfrac{\phi^{\prime}-u}{\kappa-1}-u\right).

By using

(3.6)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial\phi^{\prime}}{\partial s}=\langle V,T\rangle,\ \ \dfrac{\partial^{2}\phi^{\prime}}{\partial s^{2}}=\phi^{\prime}-\kappa u,\ \ \dfrac{\partial u}{\partial s}=\kappa\langle V,T\rangle,\ \ \dfrac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial s^{2}}=\dfrac{\partial\kappa}{\partial s}\langle V,T\rangle+\kappa(\phi^{\prime}-\kappa u),

we get

(3.7)

\dfrac{\partial\kappa}{\partial t}=\dfrac{\phi^{\prime}-u}{(\kappa-1)^{2}}\dfrac{\partial^{2}\kappa}{\partial s^{2}}+\dfrac{\partial\kappa}{\partial s}\left[\dfrac{\partial}{\partial s}\left(\dfrac{\phi^{\prime}-u}{(\kappa-1)^{2}}\right)+\langle V,T\rangle\right].

Note that
\phi^{\prime}-u>0
, by the maximum principle, we have

(3.8)

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\max\limits_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{1}}\kappa(x,t)\leqslant 0,\ \dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\min\limits_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{1}}\kappa(x,t)\geqslant 0.

Then (
3.4
) follows and
\gamma_{t}
remains strictly h-convex.
∎

Note that h-convexity implies star-shapedness with respect to some point inside the domain. In the following, we will regard the solution curve of the flow (
1.3
) as the graph over
\mathbb{S}^{1}
,
\gamma_{t}=\{(\rho(\theta),\theta):\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{1}\}
, where
\rho:\mathbb{S}^{1}\to(0,\infty)
is the radial distance. The unit outward normal vector is

(3.9)

\nu=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1+\rho_{\theta}^{2}\phi(\rho)^{-2}}}\left(\partial\rho-\rho_{\theta}\phi(\rho)^{-2}\partial_{\theta}\right),

the curvature is

(3.10)

\kappa=\dfrac{\phi(\rho)^{2}\phi^{\prime}(\rho)+2\rho_{\theta}^{2}\phi^{\prime}(\rho)-\rho_{\theta\theta}\phi(\rho)}{\left(\phi(\rho)^{2}+\rho_{\theta}^{2}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}},

the support function is

(3.11)

u=\langle V,\nu\rangle=\dfrac{\phi(\rho)}{\sqrt{1+\rho_{\theta}^{2}\phi(\rho)^{-2}}}.

For a family of curves which can be regarded as the graph over
\mathbb{S}^{1}
, we can write

X(x,t)=(\rho(\psi(x,t),t),\psi(x,t)),

where
\psi(\cdot,t):\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
is a family of diffeomorphism and
\rho(\cdot,t):\mathbb{S}^{1}\to(0,\infty)
is the radial distance. Then

\dfrac{\partial X}{\partial t}=\left(\dfrac{\partial\rho}{\partial\theta}\dfrac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}+\dfrac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}\right)\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\rho}+\dfrac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\theta}.

By a direct computation,

(3.12)

\left\langle\dfrac{\partial X}{\partial t},\nu\right\rangle=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1+\rho_{\theta}^{2}\phi(\rho)^{-2}}}\dfrac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}.

Therefore, we express the flow (
1.3
) equivalently as the initial value problem:

(3.13)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}=\dfrac{\phi^{\prime}(\rho)\sqrt{1+\rho_{\theta}^{2}\phi(\rho)^{-2}}-\phi(\rho)}{\kappa-1}-\phi(\rho),&(\theta,t)\in\mathbb{S}^{1}\times[0,T),\\
\rho(\theta,0)=\rho_{0}(\theta).\end{array}\right.

In the following, we will use (
3.13
) to obtain some estimates.

Proposition 3.3

(
C^{0}
Estimate)
.

Let
\rho(\cdot,t)\in\mathbb{S}^{1}\times[0,T)
be the solution to (
3.13
), then

(3.14)

\min\limits_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\rho(\cdot,0)\leqslant\rho(\cdot,t)\leqslant\max\limits_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\rho(\cdot,0).

Proof.

At the spatial extremal point of
\rho
, we have
\rho_{\theta}=0
. The equation (
3.13
) then becomes

(3.15)

\dfrac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}=\dfrac{\phi^{\prime}(\rho)-\phi(\rho)}{\kappa-1}-\phi(\rho).

At the maximum point of
\rho
, i.e.
\rho(\theta,t)=\max\limits_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\rho(\cdot,t)
, we have
\kappa\geqslant\dfrac{\phi^{\prime}(\rho)}{\phi(\rho)}
. Thus

(3.16)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho_{\max}(t)\leqslant\dfrac{\phi^{\prime}-\phi}{\frac{\phi^{\prime}}{\phi}-1}-\phi=0.

At the minimum point of
\rho
, i.e.
\rho(\theta,t)=\min\limits_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\rho(\cdot,t)
, we have
\kappa\leqslant\dfrac{\phi^{\prime}(\rho)}{\phi(\rho)}
. Thus

(3.17)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho_{\min}(t)\geqslant\dfrac{\phi^{\prime}-\phi}{\frac{\phi^{\prime}}{\phi}-1}-\phi=0.

The conclusion follows by the standard maximum principle.
∎

Proposition 3.4

(
C^{1}
Estimate)
.

Along the flow (
3.13
), there exists a constant
C>0
depending only on
\rho_{0}
, such that

(3.18)

\|\rho_{\theta}\|_{C^{0}(\mathbb{S}^{1}\times[0,T))}\leqslant C.

Proof.

1. Firstly, we prove that star-shapedness preserves along the flow (
1.3
). For any
t\in[0,T)
, at the spatial minimum point
P
of
u
, we have

(3.19)

\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial s}=\kappa\langle V,T\rangle=0.

Since
\gamma_{t}
is strictly h-convex, that is
\kappa>1
, then
\langle V,T\rangle=0
. This implies that
\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\rho}
is parallel to the unit outward normal vector
\nu
at
P
. Hence

(3.20)

u(P)=\langle V,\nu\rangle(P)=\phi(\rho)|_{P}.

By Proposition
3.3
, we have

(3.21)

u\geqslant\min_{\gamma_{t}}\phi(\rho)>0.

2. Combining (
3.11
) and (
3.21
), we get

(3.22)

\dfrac{|\rho_{\theta}|}{\phi(\rho)}\leqslant\sqrt{1+\rho_{\theta}^{2}\phi(\rho)^{-2}}\leqslant\dfrac{\max\limits_{\gamma_{t}}\phi(\rho)}{\min\limits_{\gamma_{t}}\phi(\rho)}\leqslant\dfrac{\max\limits_{\gamma_{0}}\phi}{\min\limits_{\gamma_{0}}\phi},

where we have used that
\phi(\rho)=\sinh\rho
is monotonically increasing in the last inequality. Therefore,

(3.23)

|\rho_{\theta}|\leqslant\dfrac{\max\limits_{\gamma_{0}}\phi^{2}}{\min\limits_{\gamma_{0}}\phi}:=C,

where the constant
C
depends only on
\rho_{0}
.
∎

3.2.
Long time existence

By the above a priori estimates, we know that if
\rho
is a positive admissible solution to the flow (
3.13
) in
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times[0,T)
, then by the expression (
3.10
) of
\kappa
, we have

(3.24)

\|\rho\|_{C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1}\times[0,T))}\leqslant C,

where
C
depends only on
\rho_{0}
. By Krylov and Safanov’s theorem
[
11
, Section 5.5]
, we get
\|\rho\|_{C^{2,\alpha}}\leqslant C
, where
C
depends only on
\rho_{0}
. By Schauder’s estimate, we get the uniform
C^{k}
estimate of
\rho
(
k\geqslant 2
). Therefore, the flow (
1.3
) exists in
t\in(0,\infty)
.

3.3.
Smooth convergence

We first obtain subsequential convergence.

Lemma 3.5
.

There exists a sequence
\{t_{j}\}
such that when
t_{j}\to\infty
,
Q(t_{j})
monotonically decreasing converges to 0, where

(3.25)

Q(t)=\int_{\gamma_{t}}\left(\dfrac{\phi^{\prime}(r)-u}{\kappa-1}-u\right)\mathrm{d}s,

and
\gamma_{t_{j}}
converges smoothly to a geodesic circle centered at the origin.

Proof.

1. Existence of
\gamma_{\infty}
. Note that
A^{\prime}(t)=Q(t)
, then

(3.26)

\int_{0}^{t}Q(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau=A(t)-A(0).

By Heintze-Karcher type inequality (
3.1
), we know
Q(t)\geqslant 0
. By Proposition
3.3
,
A(t)\leqslant 2\pi\Phi(\max\limits_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\rho_{0})
. Thus
Q(t)
is a nonnegative
L^{1}(0,\infty)
function. Then by Arzelà-Ascoli theorem and uniform estimates, there exists
\{t_{j}\}
such that
\gamma_{t_{j}}
converges smoothly to
\gamma_{\infty}
, and the following holds:

(3.27)

\int_{\gamma_{\infty}}\left(\dfrac{\phi^{\prime}-u}{\kappa-1}-u\right)\mathrm{d}s=0.

By the rigidity of Heintze-Karcher type inequality proved in
[
14
, Proposition 8.2]
, we know that
\gamma_{\infty}
is a geodesic circle.

2. We prove that
\gamma_{\infty}
is a geodesic circle centered at the origin. Let
\rho_{\infty}
be the radius of
\gamma_{\infty}
, and its curvature is
\dfrac{\phi^{\prime}(\rho_{\infty})}{\phi(\rho_{\infty})}
. We argue by contradiction. Suppose
\gamma_{\infty}
is not centered at the origin, then at the spatial point where
\rho
attains its maximum of
\theta
,

(3.28)

\dfrac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}=\dfrac{\phi^{\prime}(\rho)-\phi(\rho)}{\dfrac{\phi^{\prime}(\rho_{\infty})}{\phi(\rho_{\infty})}-1}-\phi(\rho)=\dfrac{\phi(\rho_{\infty}-\rho)}{\phi^{\prime}(\rho_{\infty})-\phi(\rho_{\infty})}<0,

which contradicts that
\gamma_{\infty}
is the stationary solution to the flow (
1.3
).
∎

Then we get smooth convergence.

Proposition 3.6
.

There exists
\rho_{\infty}>0
such that along the flow (
3.13
), when
t\to\infty
, there holds
\|\rho(\cdot,t)-\rho_{\infty}\|_{C^{k}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\to 0
,
\forall\ k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

We have proved that there exist
t_{j}\to\infty
and
\rho_{\infty}
such that
\|\rho(\cdot,t_{j})-\rho_{\infty}\|_{C^{k}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\to 0,\ \forall\ k\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
\rho_{\max}(t)=\max\limits_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\rho(\theta,t)
is monotonically non-increasing, and
\rho_{\min}(t)=\min\limits_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\rho(\theta,t)
is monotonically non-decreasing, we have
\|\rho(\cdot,t)-\rho_{\infty}\|_{C^{0}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\to 0\ (t\to\infty)
.

We recall the special case of Gagliardo-Nirenberg interpolation inequality
[
15
]
: For any positive integer
j
and
k
, where
j<k
, and let
\delta=\frac{j}{k}
, there exists a constant
C(j,k)
, such that for any
f\in W^{k,2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
,

(3.29)

\left\|\dfrac{\partial^{j}}{\partial\theta^{j}}f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\leqslant\left\|\dfrac{\partial^{k}}{\partial\theta^{k}}f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}^{\delta}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}^{1-\delta}.

Choose
f=\rho(\theta,t)-\rho_{\infty}
, we have

(3.30)

\left\|\dfrac{\partial^{j}}{\partial\theta^{j}}\rho(\theta,t)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\leqslant\left\|\dfrac{\partial^{k}}{\partial\theta^{k}}\rho(\theta,t)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}^{\delta}\|\rho(\theta,t)-\rho_{\infty}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}^{1-\delta}.

For any positive integer
j
, and any
\varepsilon>0
, for a large enough time
t
, by the boundedness of
\|\rho\|_{C^{k}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}
and
\|\rho(\cdot,t)-\rho_{\infty}\|_{C^{0}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}<\varepsilon
, we know that
\left\|\dfrac{\partial^{j}}{\partial\theta^{j}}\rho(\theta,t)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}<C\varepsilon
. Then Sobolev embedding theorem implies that
\|\rho(\cdot,t)-\rho_{\infty}\|_{C^{j-1}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}<C\varepsilon
. This shows that
\rho(\cdot,t)
converges smoothly to
\rho_{\infty}
when
t\to\infty
.
∎

3.4.
Exponential convergence

We have shown that when
t\to\infty
,
\gamma_{t}
converges smoothly to a geodesic circle centered at the origin with radius
\rho_{\infty}
. Therefore, for each large enough time
t
,
\gamma_{t}
lies in the neighborhood of
\gamma_{\infty}
. We consider the linearization of flow (
3.13
) at
\rho=\rho_{\infty}
(See
[
13
, Lemma 4.3]
). Let

(3.31)

\sigma(\theta,t)=\rho(\theta,t)-\rho_{\infty},

then as
t\to\infty
,
\sigma(\theta,t)\to 0
. Denote
G[\rho(\theta,t)]
as the right hand side of (
3.13
), then

(3.32)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\sigma(\theta,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}(\rho(\theta,t)-\rho_{\infty})=G[\rho(\theta,t)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G[\rho_{\infty}]+DG|_{\rho_{\infty}}(\sigma(\theta,t))+O\left(\|\sigma(\theta,t)\|_{C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\phi^{\prime}(\rho_{\infty})-\phi(\rho_{\infty})}\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\theta^{2}}\sigma(\theta,t)+O\left(\|\sigma(\theta,t)\|_{C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}^{2}\right).

Consider the eigenfunction of laplacian on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
(see also
[
21
, Section 9]
), i.e.

(3.33)

\dfrac{\partial^{2}\phi_{k}(\theta)}{\partial\theta^{2}}=-\lambda_{k}\phi_{k}(\theta),

where
0=\lambda_{0}<\lambda_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant\lambda_{k}\to\infty
,
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
, and
\{\phi_{k}(\theta)\}
constitutes a set of orthonormal basis in
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
. Let

(3.34)

\sigma(\theta,t)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\varphi_{k}(\theta,t),

where
\varphi_{k}(\theta,t)=f_{k}(t)\phi_{k}(\theta)
,
f_{k}(t)\in C^{\infty}(I)
, then

(3.35)

\varphi_{0}=\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\sigma(\theta,t)\mathrm{d}\theta.

Therefore,

(3.36)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\sigma(\theta,t)^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}2\sigma(\theta,t)\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\sigma(\theta,t)\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\dfrac{2}{\phi^{\prime}(\rho_{\infty})-\phi(\rho_{\infty})}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\sigma(\theta,t)\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\theta^{2}}\sigma(\theta,t)\mathrm{d}\theta+C\|\sigma(\theta,t)\|_{C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\sigma(\theta,t)|\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{2}{\phi^{\prime}(\rho_{\infty})-\phi(\rho_{\infty})}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}-\sum\limits_{k\geqslant 1}\lambda_{k}\varphi_{k}^{2}(\theta,t)\mathrm{d}\theta+C\|\sigma(\theta,t)\|^{2}_{C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\sigma(\theta,t)|\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\dfrac{2}{\phi^{\prime}(\rho_{\infty})-\phi(\rho_{\infty})}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\sum\limits_{k\geqslant 1}\lambda_{1}\varphi_{k}^{2}(\theta,t)\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle-\dfrac{2}{\phi^{\prime}(\rho_{\infty})-\phi(\rho_{\infty})}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\sum\limits_{k\geqslant 1}(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{1})\varphi_{k}^{2}(\theta,t)\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle+C\|\sigma(\theta,t)\|^{2}_{C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\sigma(\theta,t)|\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle-\dfrac{2\lambda_{1}}{\phi^{\prime}(\rho_{\infty})-\phi(\rho_{\infty})}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\sum\limits_{k\geqslant 0}\varphi_{k}^{2}(\theta,t)\mathrm{d}\theta+\dfrac{2\lambda_{1}}{\phi^{\prime}(\rho_{\infty})-\phi(\rho_{\infty})}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\varphi_{0}^{2}(\theta,t)\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle+C\|\sigma(\theta,t)\|^{2}_{C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\sigma(\theta,t)|\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\dfrac{2\lambda_{1}}{\phi^{\prime}(\rho_{\infty})-\phi(\rho_{\infty})}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\sigma(\theta,t)^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta+\underbrace{\dfrac{\lambda_{1}}{\pi\left(\phi^{\prime}(\rho_{\infty})-\phi(\rho_{\infty})\right)}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\sigma(\theta,t)\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{2}}_{I}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{C\|\sigma(\theta,t)\|^{2}_{C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\sigma(\theta,t)|\mathrm{d}\theta}_{II}.

By Lemma
3.1
, we know that
[L-A](\rho(\theta,t))=[L-A](\rho_{\infty})
. We claim that: There exists a constant
C
such that

(3.37)

\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\sigma(\theta,t)\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{2}\leqslant C\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\|\sigma(\theta,t)\|^{2}_{C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{2}\leqslant C\|\sigma(\theta,t)\|_{C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}^{4}.

Indeed, we use the Taylor expansion of
[L-A](\rho(\theta,t))=[L-A](\sigma(\theta,t)+\rho_{\infty})
at
\rho_{\infty}
to obtain

(3.38)

[L-A](\rho(\theta,t))=[L-A](\rho_{\infty})+\left(\phi^{\prime}(\rho_{\infty})-\phi(\rho_{\infty})\right)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\sigma(\theta,t)\mathrm{d}\theta+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}O\left(\|\sigma(\theta,t)\|_{C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}\theta.

This implies

(3.39)

\left(\phi^{\prime}(\rho_{\infty})-\phi(\rho_{\infty})\right)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\sigma(\theta,t)\mathrm{d}\theta+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}O\left(\|\sigma(\theta,t)\|_{C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}\theta=0,

and thus there exists a constant
C
such that

\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\sigma(\theta,t)\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{2}\leqslant C\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\|\sigma(\theta,t)\|^{2}_{C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{2}\leqslant C\|\sigma(\theta,t)\|_{C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}^{4}.

In the following, we will choose
f=\sigma(\theta,t)
in Gagliardo-Nirenberg interpolation inequality (
3.29
) to estimate
\|\sigma(\theta,t)\|_{C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}^{2}
. For any positive integer
j,\ k
satisfying
j<k
, let
\delta=\frac{j}{k}
. There exists a constant
C(j,k)
such that

(3.40)

\left\|\dfrac{\partial^{j}}{\partial\theta^{j}}\sigma(\theta,t)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\leqslant\left\|\dfrac{\partial^{k}}{\partial\theta^{k}}\sigma(\theta,t)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}^{\delta}\|\sigma(\theta,t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}^{1-\delta}.

By Sobolev embedding theorem, we have

(3.41)

\|\sigma(\theta,t)\|_{C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\leqslant C\|\sigma(\theta,t)\|_{W^{j,2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})},\ \ j\geqslant 3.

Combining (
3.40
) and (
3.41
), taking
k
large enough, we obtain that
\forall\ 0<\varepsilon<1
, there exists a positive constant
C(\varepsilon)
such that

(3.42)

\|\sigma(\theta,t)\|_{C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}^{2}\leqslant C(\varepsilon)\|\sigma(\theta,t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}^{1+\varepsilon}.

Choose
\varepsilon=\dfrac{1}{2}
, we get

(3.43)

\|\sigma(\theta,t)\|_{C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}^{2}\leqslant C\|\sigma(\theta,t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}^{\frac{3}{2}}.

Now from (
3.37
) and (
3.43
), we know

(3.44)

\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\sigma(\theta,t)\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{2}\leqslant C\|\sigma(\theta,t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}^{3}=C\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\sigma(\theta,t)^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1+\frac{1}{2}}.

By (
3.43
) and Hölder inequality, we obtain

(3.45)

\displaystyle\|\sigma(\theta,t)\|_{C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\sigma(\theta,t)|\mathrm{d}\theta\leqslant C\|\sigma(\theta,t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}^{\frac{5}{2}}=C\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\sigma(\theta,t)^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1+\frac{1}{4}}.

Substituting (
3.44
) and (
3.45
) into (
3.36
), we get

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\sigma(\theta,t)^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\dfrac{2\lambda_{1}}{\phi^{\prime}(\rho_{\infty})-\phi(\rho_{\infty})}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\sigma(\theta,t)^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta+C\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\sigma(\theta,t)^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1+\frac{1}{2}}+C\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\sigma(\theta,t)^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1+\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\dfrac{2\lambda_{1}}{\phi^{\prime}(\rho_{\infty})-\phi(\rho_{\infty})}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\sigma(\theta,t)^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta\left(1-C^{\prime}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\sigma(\theta,t)^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}-C^{\prime\prime}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\sigma(\theta,t)^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\right).

Since
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\sigma(\theta,t)^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta\to 0\ (t\to\infty)
, for any
\delta>0
, there exists
t_{\delta}>0
such that for any
t>t_{\delta}
,

(3.46)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\sigma(\theta,t)^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta\leqslant-\dfrac{2\lambda_{1}}{\phi^{\prime}(\rho_{\infty})-\phi(\rho_{\infty})}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\sigma(\theta,t)^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta\left(1-\delta\right).

Choose
\delta=\frac{1}{2}
, we obtain

(3.47)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\sigma(\theta,t)^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta\leqslant C\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\phi^{\prime}(\rho_{\infty})-\phi(\rho_{\infty})}t}.

By (
3.40
) and Sobolev embedding theorem:

(3.48)

\|\sigma(\theta,t)\|_{C^{j-1}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\leqslant C\|\sigma(\theta,t)\|_{W^{j,2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})},\ \ j\geqslant 1,

we obtain that

(3.49)

\|\sigma(\theta,t)\|_{C^{k}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\leqslant C_{1}\mathrm{e}^{-C_{2}t},\ \ \forall\ k\geqslant 0,

where
C_{1},\ C_{2}
depends only on
\rho_{0},\ k
.

Above all, we complete the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

4.
Geometric inequalities

In this section, we will prove geometric inequalities involving weighted curvature integrals and modified quermassintegrals for h-convex domains.

4.1.
Inequalities for curves

We will prove Theorem
1.4
in this subsection.

Lemma 4.1
.

Along the flow (
1.3
), if the initial curve is strictly h-convex, then there holds

(4.1)

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{\gamma_{t}}\left(\Phi-u\right)(\kappa-1)\mathrm{d}s\leqslant 0

with equality if and only if
\gamma_{t}
is a geodesic circle centered at the origin.

Proof.

Along the flow
\partial_{t}X=\eta\nu
, we calculate that

(4.2)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{\gamma_{t}}(\Phi-u)(\kappa-1)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma_{t}}\left(\partial_{t}\Phi-\partial_{t}u\right)(\kappa-1)\mathrm{d}s+\int_{\gamma_{t}}(\Phi-u)\partial_{t}\kappa\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{\gamma_{t}}(\Phi-u)(\kappa-1)\partial_{t}\mathrm{d}s.

Note that

(4.3)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\overline{\nabla}\Phi,\partial_{t}X\rangle=\langle V,\eta\nu\rangle=\eta u,

(4.4)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{t}X}V,\nu\rangle+\langle V,\partial_{t}\nu\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta\langle\overline{\nabla}_{\nu}V,\nu\rangle-\langle V,\dfrac{\partial\eta}{\partial s}T\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta\phi^{\prime}-\dfrac{\partial\eta}{\partial s}\dfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial s},

we get the evolution equation

(4.5)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\int_{\gamma_{t}}(\Phi-u)(\kappa-1)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma_{t}}\left[(u-\phi^{\prime})\eta+\dfrac{\partial\eta}{\partial s}\dfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial s}\right](\kappa-1)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle-\int_{\gamma_{t}}(\Phi-u)\left[\dfrac{\partial^{2}\eta}{\partial s^{2}}+(\kappa^{2}-1)\eta\right]\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{\gamma_{t}}(\Phi-u)(\kappa-1)\kappa\eta\mathrm{d}s.

Using integration by parts, we have

(4.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma_{t}}(\Phi-u)\dfrac{\partial^{2}\eta}{\partial s^{2}}\mathrm{d}s=-\int_{\gamma_{t}}\left(\dfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial s}-\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial s}\right)\dfrac{\partial\eta}{\partial s}\mathrm{d}s=\int_{\gamma_{t}}(\kappa-1)\dfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial s}\dfrac{\partial\eta}{\partial s}\mathrm{d}s.

We obtain that

(4.7)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\int_{\gamma_{t}}(\Phi-u)(\kappa-1)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma_{t}}(u-\phi^{\prime})(\kappa-1)\eta\mathrm{d}s-\int_{\gamma_{t}}(\Phi-u)(\kappa-1)\eta\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma_{t}}(2u-2\Phi-1)(\kappa-1)\eta\mathrm{d}s,

where we used
\phi^{\prime}=\Phi+1
. Substituting

\eta=\dfrac{\phi^{\prime}-u}{\kappa-1}-u=\dfrac{\phi^{\prime}-\kappa u}{\kappa-1}

into (
4.7
), using (
2.18
) and integration by parts, we obtain that

(4.8)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\int_{\gamma_{t}}(\Phi-u)(\kappa-1)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma_{t}}(2u-2\Phi-1)(\phi^{\prime}-\kappa u)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma_{t}}(2u-2\Phi-1)\dfrac{\partial^{2}\Phi}{\partial s^{2}}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\int_{\gamma_{t}}\left(\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial s}-\dfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial s}\right)\dfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial s}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\int_{\gamma_{t}}(\kappa-1)\left|\dfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial s}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}s\leqslant 0,

where we used that the curvature of
\gamma_{t}
is
\kappa>1
by Proposition
3.2
. Equality holds in (
4.8
) if and only if
\dfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial s}\equiv 0
holds along
\gamma_{t}
, which is equivalent to that
r\equiv r_{0}
. Thus
\gamma_{t}
is a geodesic circle centered at the origin.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

We divide the proof into two steps.

Step 1.
Suppose
\gamma
is a smooth strictly h-convex closed curve in
\mathbb{H}^{2}
. Let
\gamma
be the initial value of the flow (
1.3
) and we obtain the solution
\{\gamma_{t}\}_{t\in[0,\infty)}
by Theorem
1.2
. Moreover, when
t\to\infty
,
\gamma_{t}
converges smoothly to a geodesic circle
\gamma_{\infty}
centered at the origin with radius
\rho_{\infty}
. By (
3.2
) and (
4.1
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma}(\Phi-u)(\kappa-1)\mathrm{d}s+(L[\gamma]-A[\gamma])

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma_{\infty}}(\Phi-u)(\kappa-1)\mathrm{d}s+(L[\gamma_{\infty}]-A[\gamma_{\infty}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\pi\left(\Phi(\rho_{\infty})-\phi(\rho_{\infty})\right)\left(\phi^{\prime}(\rho_{\infty})-\phi(\rho_{\infty})\right)

\displaystyle+2\pi\left(\phi(\rho_{\infty})-\Phi(\rho_{\infty})\right)

(4.9)

\displaystyle(\mbox{by}\ \phi^{\prime}=\Phi+1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\pi\left(\phi(\rho_{\infty})-\Phi(\rho_{\infty})\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\left(L[\gamma_{\infty}]-A[\gamma_{\infty}]\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\left(L[\gamma]-A[\gamma]\right)^{2}.

By Lemma
4.1
, equality holds in (
4.9
) if and only if
\gamma
is a geodesic circle centered at the origin.

Step 2.
Now we consider
\gamma
as a smooth h-convex closed curve in
\mathbb{H}^{2}
. We evolve
\gamma
along curve shortening flow

(4.10)

\partial_{t}X=-\kappa\nu,

and obtain solution curve
\gamma_{t}
with curvature
\kappa(x,t)
satisfying

(4.11)

\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}(\kappa-1)=\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial s^{2}}(\kappa-1)+(\kappa-1)\kappa(\kappa+1).

Note that for a compact h-convex curve, there exists at least one point on
\gamma
with
\kappa>1
. By strong maximum principle,
\gamma_{t}\ (\forall\ t>0)
is strictly h-convex. Note that for
\gamma_{t}\ (t>0)
, inequality (
1.9
) holds by
Step 1
. Now let
t\to 0
, we obtain that inequality (
1.9
) holds for the h-convex curve
\gamma
.

If equality in (
1.9
) holds for a h-convex curve
\gamma
, we are going to show that
\gamma
is strictly h-convex. Let

(4.12)

\tilde{\gamma}=\{p\in\gamma:\kappa(p)>1\}.

Since there exists at least one point on
\gamma
satisfying
\kappa>1
for a compact h-convex curve
\gamma
, we know that
\tilde{\gamma}
is a nonempty open set in
\gamma
. We next show that
\tilde{\gamma}
is also closed in
\gamma
. Following
[
7
]
, we choose a smooth function
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\tilde{\gamma})
with compact support in
\tilde{\gamma}
, and consider a normal variation of
\gamma

(4.13)

\partial_{t}X=-\varphi\nu,

where
t\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
, and
\varepsilon>0
is small enough such that
\gamma_{t}=X(\mathbb{S}^{1},t)
is h-convex. Let

(4.14)

Q(t)=\int_{\gamma_{t}}(\Phi-u)(\kappa-1)\mathrm{d}s+(L[\gamma_{t}]-A[\gamma_{t}])-\dfrac{1}{2\pi}(L[\gamma_{t}]-A[\gamma_{t}])^{2},

we have

(4.15)

Q(t)\geqslant 0,\ t\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon),\ \ Q(0)=0,

which implies that
Q(t)\ (t\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon))
attains its minimum at
t=0
. Thus

(4.16)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left.\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}Q(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\gamma}(2u-2\Phi-1)(\kappa-1)\varphi\mathrm{d}s-\int_{\gamma}(\kappa-1)\varphi\mathrm{d}s+\dfrac{L[\gamma]-A[\gamma]}{\pi}\int_{\gamma}(\kappa-1)\varphi\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma}\left(\dfrac{L[\gamma]-A[\gamma]}{\pi}-(2u-2\Phi)\right)(\kappa-1)\varphi\mathrm{d}s,\ \ \forall\ \varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\tilde{\gamma}).

In particular, the curve
\tilde{\gamma}
satisfies

(4.17)

2u-2\Phi\equiv\dfrac{L[\gamma]-A[\gamma]}{\pi},

and hence

(4.18)

\tilde{\gamma}=\left\{p\in\gamma:(u-\Phi)(p)=\dfrac{L[\gamma]-A[\gamma]}{2\pi}\right\}.

This implies that
\tilde{\gamma}
is closed in
\gamma
. By the connectness of
\gamma
,
\gamma\equiv\tilde{\gamma}
, thus
\gamma
is strictly h-convex. By the equality case in
Step 1
, we conclude that
\gamma
is a geodesic circle centered at the origin.
∎

4.2.
Inequalities for hypersurfaces

We include the complete proof of Theorem
1.3
in this subsection though it has been proved in
[
14
]
. Some details in this proof will be used in proving stability results later. Let’s first derive the general evolution equation.

Lemma 4.2
.

Along the flow
\partial_{t}X=\eta\nu
, hypersurface
M_{t}=\partial\Omega_{t}\subset\mathbb{H}^{n+1}

(n\geqslant 2)
satisfies

(4.19)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\left[\int_{M_{t}}(\Phi-u)E_{k}(\tilde{\kappa})\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}+(n-2k)\tilde{W}_{k}(\Omega_{t})\right]

Proof.

Denote
S^{i}_{j}=h^{i}_{j}-\delta^{i}_{j}
, we will compute

(4.20)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\int_{M_{t}}(\Phi-u)E_{k}(\tilde{\kappa})\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}+(n-2k)\tilde{W}_{k}(\Omega_{t})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M_{t}}(\partial_{t}\Phi-\partial_{t}u)E_{k}(\tilde{\kappa})\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}+\int_{M_{t}}(\Phi-u)\partial_{t}E_{k}(\tilde{\kappa})\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}

\displaystyle+\int_{M_{t}}(\Phi-u)E_{k}(\tilde{\kappa})\eta H\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}+(n-2k)\int_{M_{t}}\eta E_{k}(\tilde{\kappa})\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}.

Substituting (
2.13
), (
2.31
), (
2.32
) and (
2.42
), and combining
nE_{1}(\tilde{\kappa})=H-n
and
\phi^{\prime}=\Phi+1
, we obtain

(4.21)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\int_{M_{t}}(\Phi-u)E_{k}(\tilde{\kappa})\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}+(n-2k)\tilde{W}_{k}(\Omega_{t})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M_{t}}\left[(u-\phi^{\prime})\eta+\langle V,\nabla\eta\rangle\right]E_{k}(\tilde{\kappa})\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}-\int_{M_{t}}(\Phi-u)\dot{E}_{k}^{ij}(\tilde{\kappa})\nabla_{j}\nabla^{i}\eta\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}

\displaystyle+(n-2k)\int_{M_{t}}(\phi^{\prime}-u)E_{k}(\tilde{\kappa})\eta\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}+(n-k)\int_{M_{t}}(\Phi-u)E_{k+1}(\tilde{\kappa})\eta\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}.

By divergence free property of
E_{k}(\tilde{\kappa})
(Lemma
2.3
) and using integration by parts, we have

(4.22)

\displaystyle-\int_{M_{t}}(\Phi-u)\dot{E}_{k}^{ij}(\tilde{\kappa})\nabla_{j}\nabla^{i}\eta\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{M_{t}}\dot{E}_{k}^{ij}(\tilde{\kappa})\nabla_{j}\nabla^{i}(\Phi-u)\eta\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{M_{t}}\dot{E}_{k}^{ij}(\tilde{\kappa})\left[-\phi^{\prime}S^{i}_{j}-\langle V,\nabla S^{i}_{j}\rangle+u\left((S^{2})^{i}_{j}+S^{i}_{j}\right)\right]\eta\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M_{t}}\left[\phi^{\prime}kE_{k}(\tilde{\kappa})+\langle V,\nabla E_{k}(\tilde{\kappa})\rangle\right]\eta\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}

\displaystyle-\int_{M_{t}}u\left(nE_{1}(\tilde{\kappa})E_{k}(\tilde{\kappa})-(n-k)E_{k+1}(\tilde{\kappa})+kE_{k}(\tilde{\kappa})\right)\eta\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}.

Note that by using integration by parts and
\Delta\Phi=n\phi^{\prime}-Hu=n\phi^{\prime}-(nE_{1}(\tilde{\kappa})+n)u
, we obtain

(4.23)

\displaystyle\int_{M_{t}}\langle V,\nabla\eta\rangle E_{k}(\tilde{\kappa})\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}+\int_{M_{t}}\langle V,\nabla E_{k}(\tilde{\kappa})\rangle\eta\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M_{t}}\langle V,\nabla(\eta E_{k}(\tilde{\kappa}))\rangle\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{M_{t}}\Delta\Phi E_{k}(\tilde{\kappa})\eta\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{M_{t}}\left[n\phi^{\prime}-\left(nE_{1}(\tilde{\kappa})+n\right)u\right]E_{k}(\tilde{\kappa})\eta\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}.

Combining (
4.21
), (
4.22
) and (
4.23
), and after a direct computation we will obtain (
4.19
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

For
k=1,2,\cdots,n
, we evolve
M
along the flow (
1.2
) and get a family of strictly h-convex hypersurfaces
\{M_{t}\}_{t\in[0,\infty)}
by Theorem
1.1
. Moreover, as
t\to\infty
,
M_{t}
converges smoothly to a geodesic sphere
M_{\infty}=\partial\Omega_{\infty}
centered at the origin with radius
\rho_{\infty}
.

Substituting
\eta=\dfrac{(\phi^{\prime}-u)E_{k-1}(\tilde{\kappa})}{E_{k}(\tilde{\kappa})}-u
in (
4.19
), by Newton-MacLaurin inequality (
2.4
) and (
2.41
), we have

(4.24)

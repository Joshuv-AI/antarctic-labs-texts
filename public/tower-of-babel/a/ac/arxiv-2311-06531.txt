Large deviation principles for graphon sampling
Jan Grebík, Oleg Pikhurko

Abstract
We investigate possible large deviation principles (LDPs) for the n n -vertex sampling from a given graphon with various speeds s ⁡ ( n ) s(n) and resolve all the cases except when the speed s ⁡ ( n ) s(n) is of order n 2 n^{2} . For quadratic speed s = ( c + o ⁡ ( 1 ) ) ​ n 2 s=(c+o(1))n^{2} , we establish an LDP for an arbitrary k k -step graphon, which extends a result of Chatterjee and Varadhan [ Europ. J. Combin. , 32 (2011) 1000–1017] who did this for k = 1 k=1 (that is, for the homogeneous binomial random graphs). This is done by reducing the problem to the LDP for stochastic k k -block models established by Borgs, Chayes, Gaudio, Petti and Sen [ “A large deviation principle for block models” , arxiv:2007.14508, 2020]. We also improve some results from this paper.

Overview of the new results

We study large deviations for the

Binomial random graph and graphons

A basic but central model

, where the vertex set is
[n]

[

As it turns out, the “right” setting is to consider

. (See Section

where

The space

[
33

This way,

when viewed as a graphon up to weak isomorphism, belongs to the set

.

Also, recall that, for

, the

is defined by

h_{p}(r):=p\ \ell\!\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)+(1-p)\ \ell\!\left(\frac{1-r}{p}\right),\quad r\in[0,1],

(3)

where

is defined by

\ell\!\left(x\right):=x\log(x),\quad\mbox{for $x\in[0,\infty]$,}

(4)

with the agreement that

Theorem 1.2

(Chatterjee and Varadhan
[
9
]
)
.

Let

gives a well-defined function

1.4
Sampling from a graphon

There are various important inhomogeneous random graph models that generalise the binomial random graph
{\mathbbm{G}}(n,p)
. The main objective of this paper is to initiate a systematic study of large deviations for the following model.

For a graphon
W\in{\mathcal{W}}
and an integer
n\in{\mathbbm{N}}
, the
n
-vertex
W
-sample

{\mathbbm{G}}(n,W)
is the random graph on
[n]
generated by first sampling
uniform elements
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in[0,1]
and then making each pair
\{i,j\}
an edge with probability
W(x_{i},x_{j})
, where all choices are mutually independent. As it easily follows from standard results (e.g.
[
33
, Lemma 10.23]
), the distribution of
{\mathbbm{G}}(n,W)
will remain the same if we replace
W
by any weakly isomorphic graphon, so we could write
{\mathbbm{G}}(n,\widetilde{W})
. However, here and in many similar situations, we prefer to talk about graphons rather than equivalence classes of graphons, just for brevity and notational convenience.

Let
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}_{n,W}
be the corresponding (discrete) measure on
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
where we take the equivalence class
\widetilde{f^{G}}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
of the sampled graph
G\sim{\mathbbm{G}}(n,W)
. When
W
is the constant function

, we get exactly the binomial random graph
{\mathbbm{G}}(n,p)
and
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}_{n,W}=\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{n,p}
.

Let us give some definitions that are needed to state the first batch of our LDP results, namely that, informally speaking, the only “interesting” speeds for the sequence
(\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}_{n,W})_{n\in{\mathbbm{N}}}
of measures are
\Theta(n)
and
\Theta(n^{2})
, as well as a general LDP for speed
\Theta(n)
. Recall that we work on the graphon space
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
endowed with the cut-distance
\delta_{\Box}
.

Let
W\in{\mathcal{W}}
be a graphon.
We assign to
W
two
\delta_{\Box}
-closed subsets
\mathbf{RN}(W)
and
\mathbf{FORB}(W)
of
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
that satisfy
W\in\mathbf{RN}(W)\subseteq\mathbf{FORB}(W)
as follows.

The set
\mathbf{FORB}(W)
consists by definition of those graphons
U
such that
t_{\mathrm{ind}}(F,W)=0
implies
t_{\mathrm{ind}}(F,U)=0
for every graph
F
, where after relabelling
V(F)
to be
[n]
we define

t_{\mathrm{ind}}(F,U):=\int_{[0,1]^{{n}}}\prod_{\{i,j\}\in E(F)}U(x_{i},x_{j})\prod_{\{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}\}\in E(\overline{F})}\left(1-U(x_{i^{\prime}},x_{j^{\prime}})\right)\,\mathrm{d}x_{1}\dots\,\mathrm{d}x_{n}

(6)

to be the
induced (homomorphism) density
of
F
in
U
. (Equivalently for
V(F)=[n]
, we can define
t_{\mathrm{ind}}(F,U)
as the probability that
{\mathbbm{G}}(n,U)
is equal to
F
.)
In other words,
\mathbf{FORB}(W)
consists of those graphons that do not contain induced subgraphs not present in
W
. Since each function

is well-defined and continuous on
(\widetilde{\mathcal{W}},\delta_{\Box})
(see e.g.
[
33

), the set
\mathbf{FORB}(W)
is closed and depends only on the weak isomorphism class
\widetilde{W}
.

The set
\mathbf{RN}(W)
consists, roughly speaking, of graphons that can be formed from

by changing the measure on the interval
[0,1]
.
Formally, suppose that
\mu
is a Borel probability measure on
[0,1]
with
\mu\ll\lambda
, that is,
\mu

is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure
\lambda
. Fix any
measure-preserving map
\phi:([0,1],\lambda)\to([0,1],\mu)
, which exists by the Isomorphism Theorem for standard probability atomless spaces, and define
W_{\mu}\in\mathcal{W}
by

W_{\mu}(x,y):=W(\phi(x),\phi(y)),\quad x,y\in[0,1].

(7)

While this definition depends on the choice of

and the other properties of

that we use in this paper
do not. For example,

is given by the formula in (

The reader familiar with the theory of generalised graphons (in the sense of

as the generalised graphon obtained from

to
([0,1],\mu)
. We define
\mathbf{RN}(W)
to be the

-closure of the set of graphons

is weakly isomorphic to

.

For

we denote as

the function that is

In the case when

Note that

-closed.

Let us illustrate the above notions on a simple example. Namely, suppose that

is a
k
-step graphon

into measurable sets such that

is a constant

on each product

.
Let us assume additionally that each

It is easy to see that

consists exactly of weak equivalence classes of
k
-step graphons that can be produced from

by stretching or shrinking

.
In other words, every element of
\mathbf{RN}(W)
comes from a
k
-step graphon

with parts

such that the (constant) value of

on

is the same as the value of

for every

. The structure of the set

is more complicated. For example, if

, then every graph has positive induced density in

can be shown to consist of

such that

(meaning that there is

is 0 a.e. on all diagonal products

). Suppose that we consider an LDP for

for some speed

. Informally, the price in probability to change substantially the number of sampled

Thus at speed

. For speed

. Likewise, for speed

, every possible sampled graph must admit a vertex partition

such that for each

are non-edges (resp. edges, apart the diagonal pairs if

; the corresponding set of graphons in the limit as

.

Staying with the above

that

which is equal to

The natural (and correct) guess of the rate function for general

where

Our first result settles LDP for

Theorem 1.3
.

Take any

and any function

. We consider the existence of an LDP for the sequence

Here (in Theorem

We need some preparation to define the rate function for the given matrix

\mathbold{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k})\in[0,\infty)^{k}
, let
(I_{1}^{(\mathbold{\alpha})},\dots,I_{k}^{(\mathbold{\alpha})})
denote the partition of
[0,1]
into consecutive intervals such that each interval
I_{i}^{(\mathbold{\alpha})}
has length
\alpha_{i}/\|\mathbold{\alpha}\|_{1}
. Also, define the function
J_{\mathbold{\alpha},\mathbold{p}}:\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}\to[0,\infty)
by

J_{\mathbold{\alpha},\mathbold{p}}(\widetilde{U}):=\inf_{V\in\widetilde{U}}\,\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j\in[k]}\int_{I_{i}^{(\mathbold{\alpha})}\times I_{j}^{(\mathbold{\alpha})}}h_{p_{i,j}}(V(x,y))\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y,\quad U\in{{\mathcal{W}}}.

(9)

Note that the function
J_{\mathbold{\alpha},\mathbold{p}}
will not change if we multiply the vector
\mathbold{\alpha}
by any positive scalar. Finally, define

R_{\mathbold{p}}(\widetilde{U}):=\inf_{\mathbold{\alpha}\in[0,1]^{k}\atop\alpha_{1}+\ldots+\alpha_{k}=1}J_{\mathbold{\alpha},\mathbold{p}}(\widetilde{U}),\quad U\in{\mathcal{W}}.

(10)

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
W
be a
k
-step graphon with
k
non-null parts whose values are encoded by a symmetric
k\times k
matrix
\mathbold{p}\in[0,1]^{k\times k}
. Let

be any function such that, for some

Then the function

is lower semi-continuous with respect to the metric
\delta_{\Box}
. Moreover, the sequence of measures
(\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}_{n,W})_{n\in{\mathbbm{N}}}
on

satisfies an LDP with speed

and rate function

.

For

, this gives the LDP result of Chatterjee and Varadhan

]
(that is, Theorem

1.6
Stochastic block models

Sampling from a step graphon is closely related to a
stochastic block model
, which provides another important generalisation of the binomial random graph
{\mathbbm{G}}(n,p)
and which we define now. Here we are given
a symmetric
k\times k
matrix
\mathbold{p}=(p_{i,j})_{i,j\in[k]}\in[0,1]^{k\times k}
with entries in
[0,1]
and an integer vector
\mathbold{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})\in{\mathbbm{N}}_{\geqslant 0}^{k}
, where

{\mathbbm{N}}_{\geqslant 0}:=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}

denotes the set of non-negative integers. The corresponding probability distribution on
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
is defined as follows. Let
n
be equal to
\|\mathbold{a}\|_{1}=a_{1}+\dots+a_{k}
and let
(A_{1},\dots,A_{k})
be the partition of
[n]
into
k
consecutive intervals with
A_{i}
having
a_{i}
elements. The random graph
{\mathbbm{G}}(\mathbold{a},\mathbold{p})
on
[n]
is produced by making each pair
\{x,y\}
of
[n]
an edge with probability
p_{i,j}
where
i,j\in[k]
are the indices with
x\in A_{i}
and
y\in A_{j}
, with all choices made independently of each other. Output the weak isomorphism class
\widetilde{f^{G}}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
of the graphon
f^{G}
of the generated graph
G\sim{\mathbbm{G}}(\mathbold{a},\mathbold{p})
on
[n]
. Let
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbold{a},\mathbold{p}}
be the correponding (discrete) probability measure on
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
.

Let us point out the difference between the random graphs
{\mathbbm{G}}((a_{1},\dots,a_{k}),(p_{i,j})_{i,j\in[k]})
and
{\mathbbm{G}}(a_{1}+\dots+a_{k},W)
where
W
is a
k
-step graphon whose values are encoded by a matrix
(p_{i,j})_{i,j\in[k]}
.
In the former model, we have exactly
a_{i}
vertices in the
i
-th block for each
i\in[k]
. In the latter model, each vertex is put into one of the
k
blocks with the probabilities given by the measures of
A_{1},\dots,A_{k}
, independently of the other vertices; thus the number
of vertices in each block is binomially distributed. Of course, if
k=1
, then both models coincide with the binomial random graph
{\mathbbm{G}}(a_{1},p_{1,1})
.

Borgs, Chayes, Gaudio, Petti and Sen
[
5
]
extended Theorem
1.2
to
k
-block stochastic models as follows. For a real-valued function
I
on a topological space
X
, let its
lower semi-continuous
regularization
be

I_{\mathrm{lsc}}(x):=\sup\left\{\inf_{y\in G}I(y):\mbox{$G\subseteq X$ is open and }G\ni x\right\},\quad x\in X.

(11)

As it is well-known (see e.g.
[
39
, Lemma 2.8]
),

I_{\mathrm{lsc}}
is lower semi-continuous and, in fact,
I_{\mathrm{lsc}}
is the largest lsc function with
I_{\mathrm{lsc}}\leqslant I
.

Theorem 1.5

(Borgs, Chayes, Gaudio, Petti and Sen
[
5
]
)
.

Let
\mathbold{\alpha}\in{\mathbbm{N}}_{\geqslant 0}^{k}
be a non-zero integer
k
-vector and let
\mathbold{p}\in[0,1]^{k\times k}
be a symmetric
k\times k
matrix. Then the sequence of measures
(\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{n\mathbold{\alpha},\mathbold{p}})_{n\in{\mathbbm{N}}}
on
(\widetilde{\mathcal{W}},\delta_{\Box})
satisfies an LDP with speed
(n\,\|\mathbold{\alpha}\|_{1})^{2}
and rate function
(J_{\mathbold{\alpha},\mathbold{p}})_{\mathrm{lsc}}
.

Our paper uses Theorem
1.5
to derive Theorem

, even though we initially proved Theorem

independently of the work by Borgs et al

2.1
Graphons

A
graphon

U
is a function
U:[0,1]^{2}\to[0,1]
which is
symmetric
(that is,

U(x,y)=U(y,x)
for all
x,y\in[0,1]
) and
measurable
(that is, for every
a\in{\mathbbm{R}}
, the level set
\{W\leqslant a\}
is a Lebesgue measurable subset of
[0,1]^{2}
).
Recall that we denote the set of all graphons by
\mathcal{W}
. We call a graphon
W

constant
if there is
c\in[0,1]
such that the measure of
[0,1]^{2}\setminus W^{-1}(c)
is 0.

We define the pseudo-metric
d_{\Box}:{\mathcal{W}}^{2}\to[0,1]
by

d_{\Box}(U,V):=\sup_{A,B\subseteq[0,1]}\left|\int_{A\times B}\left(U-V\right)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda^{\oplus 2}\right|,\quad U,V\in\mathcal{W},

(12)

where the supremum is taken over all pairs of measurable subsets of
[0,1]
.
For a function
\phi:[0,1]\to[0,1]
and a graphon
U
,
the
pull-back

U^{\phi}
of
U
along
\phi
is defined
by

U^{\phi}(x,y):=U(\phi(x),\phi(y)),\quad x,y\in[0,1].

The
cut-distance

\delta_{\Box}:{\mathcal{W}}^{2}\to[0,1]
can be defined as

\delta_{\Box}(U,V):=\inf_{\phi,\psi}d_{\Box}(U^{\phi},V^{\psi}),

(13)

where the infimum is taken over all measure-preserving maps
\phi,\psi:[0,1]\to[0,1]
.
We refer the reader to
[
33
, Section 8.2]
for more details and, in particular, to
[
33
, Theorem 8.13]
for some alternative definitions that give the same distance. It can be easily verified that
\delta_{\Box}
is a pseudo-metric on
{\mathcal{W}}
.
Recall that we denote the metric quotient by
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
and the equivalence class of
U\in\mathcal{W}
by
\widetilde{U}
. For brevity, we may write
W
instead of
\widetilde{W}
when the stated property is independent of the choice of a representative in
\widetilde{W}
.

Define the
homomorphism density
of a finite graph
F
on
[n]
in a graphon
W
by

t(F,W):=\int_{[0,1]^{n}}\prod_{\{i,j\}\in E(F)}W(x_{i},x_{j})\,\mathrm{d}\lambda^{\oplus n}.

This can be easily related to the induced homomorphism density defined in (
6
): namely,
t(F,W)
is the sum of
t_{\mathrm{ind}}(F^{\prime},W)
over all graphs
F^{\prime}
on
[n]
with
E(F^{\prime})\supseteq E(F)
.
Also, the random
n
-vertex sample
{\mathbbm{G}}(n,W)
(and the corresponding measure
{\mathbbm{R}}_{n,W}
on
\mathcal{W}
) can be equivalently defined as the random graph on
[n]
taking each possible value
F
with probability
t_{\mathrm{ind}}(F,W)
.

We will need the following auxiliary results. The first one, due to Lovász and Szegedy
[
32
]
*Theorem 5.1 (see also

), states that the metric space

is compact.
This result is also the special case

of our more general Theorem

whose proof closely follows that of

and is sketched here.

The next result is a consequence of
the Counting Lemma and the Inverse Counting Lemma in Borgs et al
[

(see also

Theorem 2.2
.

A sequence of graphons
(W_{n})_{n\in{\mathbbm{N}}}
converges to

for every graph

.

We will also need the following result.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
U
be a graphon and
\phi:[0,1]\to[0,1]
be a measurable function such that the
push-forward measure

\phi_{*}\lambda
(defined by
(\phi_{*}\lambda)(X):=\lambda(\phi^{-1}(X))
for measurable
X\subseteq[0,1]
) satisfies
\phi_{*}\lambda\ll\lambda
, that is,
\phi_{*}\lambda

is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure
\lambda
. Then
U^{\phi}
is a graphon. Moreover,
if the Radon-Nikodym derivative
D:=\frac{\,\mathrm{d}(\phi_{*}\lambda)}{\,\mathrm{d}\lambda}
satisfies
D(x)\leqslant 1+\epsilon
for
\lambda
-a.e.
x\in[0,1]
then

\delta_{\Box}(U,U^{\phi})\leqslant 2\epsilon.

Proof.

The function
U^{\phi}:[0,1]^{2}\to[0,1]
is clearly symmetric so we have to show that it is measurable.
Since the pre-image under
\phi
of any
\lambda
-null set is again
\lambda
-null by our assumption
\phi_{*}\lambda\ll\lambda
, there is a Borel map
\psi:[0,1]\to[0,1]
such that the set

X:=\{x\in[0,1]:\psi(x)\not=\phi(x)\}

is
\lambda
-null. (For a proof, see e.g.
[
12
, Proposition 2.2.5]
.)
Take any
r\in{\mathbbm{R}}
. The set

A:=\{(x,y)\in[0,1]^{2}:U(x,y)\leqslant r\}

is measurable so by e.g.
[
12
, Proposition 1.5.2]
there are
B,N\subseteq[0,1]^{2}
such that
B
is Borel,
N
is
\lambda^{\oplus 2}
-null and
A\bigtriangleup B\subseteq N
, where
A\bigtriangleup B:=(A\setminus B)\cup(B\setminus A)
denotes the
symmetric difference
of the sets
A
and
B
. The pre-image of
N
under the Borel map
\psi^{\oplus 2}(x,y):=(\psi(x),\psi(y))
is also
\lambda^{\oplus 2}
-null for otherwise
this would contradict the absolute continuity
(\phi_{*}\lambda)^{\oplus 2}\ll\lambda^{\oplus 2}
(which follows from

\phi_{*}\lambda\ll\lambda
by the Fubini-Tonelli Theorem for Complete Measures).
Thus the level set

\{U^{\phi}\leqslant r\}

is Lebesgue measurable since its symmetric difference with the Borel set
(\psi^{\oplus 2})^{-1}(B)
is a subset of the null set
(\psi^{\oplus 2})^{-1}(N)\cup(X\times[0,1])\cup([0,1]\times X)
. As
r\in{\mathbbm{R}}
was arbitrary,
U^{\phi}
is a measurable function and thus a graphon.

For the second part, it will be convenient to use the following generalisation of a graphon.
Namely, by
a
generalised graphon
we mean a triple
(V,\Omega,\mu)
where
(\Omega,\mu)
is an atomless standard probability space and
V:(\Omega^{2},\mu^{\oplus 2})\to[0,1]
is a symmetric measurable function. In the special case
(\Omega,\mu)=([0,1],\lambda)
we get the (usual) notion of a graphon. Most definitions
and results extend with obvious modifications from graphons to generalised graphons (see
[
33
, Chapter 13.1]
for details).
For example, the
n
-vertex random sample from
(V,\Omega,\mu)
is obtained
by taking
\mu

and making
\{i,j\}
an edge with probability
V(x_{i},x_{j})
, with all choices being independent.
In particular, we will need the facts that if
(V,\Omega,\mu)
is a generalised graphon and

\phi:(\Omega^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})\to(\Omega,\mu)
is a measure-preserving map between standard probability spaces,
then the function
V^{\phi}
is measurable (which can be proved by adapting the proof of the first part of the lemma) and

\delta_{\Box}((V,\Omega,\mu),(V^{\phi},\Omega^{\prime},\mu^{\prime}))=0,

(14)

where we define
V^{\phi}(x,y):=V(\phi(x),\phi(y))
for
x,y\in\Omega^{\prime}
and
\delta_{\Box}
is the extension of the cut-distance to generalised graphons via the obvious analogues of (
12
) and (

).

Let us return to the proof of the second part of the lemma.
We can assume that the set
\{x\in[0,1]:D(x)\not=1\}
has positive measure for otherwise
\phi
is a measure-preserving map and
\delta_{\Box}(U,U^{\phi})=0
by (
14
), as required.
Let
(V,[0,1]^{2},\lambda^{\oplus 2})
be the generalised graphon defined by
V((x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})):=U(x,x^{\prime})
, for
x,y,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in[0,1]
. Thus
V=U^{\pi}
, where

\pi:[0,1]^{2}\to[0,1]
is the (measure-preserving) projection on the first coordinate.
By (
14
), it holds that

\delta_{\Box}((V,[0,1]^{2},\lambda^{\oplus 2}),U)=0.

By changing
D
on a
\lambda
-null set, we can make it a Borel function with
D(x)\leqslant 1+\epsilon
for every
x\in[0,1]
. Then

\Omega:=\{(x,y)\in[0,1]\times{\mathbbm{R}}:0\leqslant y\leqslant D(x)\}

is a Borel subset of
[0,1]\times{\mathbbm{R}}
(see e.g.
[
12
, Example 5.3.1]
) and thus induces a standard measurable space. Let
\mu
be the restriction of the Lebesgue measure on
{\mathbbm{R}}^{2}
to
\Omega
. Define
W:\Omega^{2}\to[0,1]
by
W((x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})):=U(x,x^{\prime})
for
(x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in\Omega
. Thus
U^{\phi}
and
(W,\Omega,\mu)
are measure-preserving pull-backs of the generalised graphon
(U,[0,1],\phi_{*}\mu)

along respectively the map
\phi
and the projection
\Omega\to[0,1]
on the first coordinate.
Therefore we have by (
14
) and the Triangle Inequality for
\delta_{\Box}
that

\delta_{\Box}(U^{\phi},(W,\Omega,\mu))\leqslant\delta_{\Box}(U^{\phi},(U,[0,1],\phi_{*}\mu))+\delta_{\Box}((U,[0,1],\phi_{*}\mu),(W,\Omega,\mu))=0.

Thus it suffices to show that the cut-distance
\delta_{\Box}
between
(V,[0,1]^{2},\lambda^{\oplus 2})
and
(W,\Omega,\mu)
is at most

.
The functions
V
and

and the measures
\lambda^{\oplus 2}
and
\mu

coincide on

, where

.
Since

on a set of positive measure, it holds that
\mu(X)<1
.
The Borel subsets

and

of

have the same positive Lebesgue measure and thus,

, there is a Borel measure-preserving bijection

between them. By letting

Since

, we have that

. The function
W
and the pull-back

, as maps

, coincide on the set

of measure at least

. (Indeed, when we compute it via the analogue of (

Informally speaking, the following result states that if we delete a small subset of
[0,1]
and stretch the rest of a graphon uniformly then the new graphon is close to the original one.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
U\in{\mathcal{W}}
be a graphon,
s\in(0,1]
be a non-zero real and
\phi:[0,1]\to[0,1]
be the map that sends
x
to
sx
.
Then
U^{\phi}
is a graphon and
\delta_{\Box}(U,U^{\phi})\leqslant 2(\frac{1}{s}-1)
.

Proof.

Clearly, the push-forward
\phi_{*}\lambda
is the uniform probability measure on
[0,s]
so the Radon-Nikodym derivative
\frac{\,\mathrm{d}(\phi_{*}\lambda)}{\,\mathrm{d}\lambda}
is a.e.
1/s
on
[0,s]
and
0
on
[s,1]
. The result now follows from Lemma
2.3
.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

Without affecting
(\widetilde{\mathcal{W}},\delta_{\Box})

and required that the measure-preserving maps in the definition of

are Borel. Then some parts could be simplified (for example, the first claim of Lemma

would not be necessary as the function

would be Borel for every Borel

). However,
we prefer to use the (now standard) conventions from Lovász’ book

2.2
Topology

The (pseudo-)metrics on the graphon space
\mathcal{W}
that we use are
d_{\Box}
,
d_{1}
and
d_{\infty}
with the last two coming from the
L^{1}
and
L^{\infty}
norms respectively, where we view
\mathcal{W}
as a subset of the vector space of bounded measurable functions on
[0,1]^{2}
. When we consider
[0,1]^{n}
, we use the
L^{\infty}
-metric, denoting it also by
d_{\infty}
:
d_{\infty}(x,y)=\max\{|x_{i}-y_{i}|:i\in[n]\}
for
x,y\in[0,1]^{n}
.
Let

B_{d}(x,r):=\{y:d(x,y)<r\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\overline{B}_{d}(x,r):=\{y:d(x,y)\leqslant r\}

denote respectively the open and closed balls of radius
r
around a point
x
in a metric
d
.
If it is clear from the context we write, e.g.,
B_{\infty}(x,r)
instead of
B_{d_{\infty}}(x,r)
, etc.

Let
X
be a topological space and
\mu
be a Borel probability measure on
X
. Recall that
L^{1}(X,\mu)
is the space of all integrable functions,
L^{\infty}(X,\mu)
is the space of all essentially bounded functions,
C(X)
is the space of all continuous functions and
\mathcal{P}(X)
is the space of all Borel probability measures on
X
.
Some of the topologies that will consider on these spaces are as follows:

•

the
weak
∗
topology on
\mathcal{P}(X)
, where
\nu_{n}\xrightarrow{weak^{*}}\nu
if and only if
\int_{X}f\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{n}\to\int_{X}f\,\mathrm{d}\nu
for every
f\in C(X)
,

•

the
weak
∗
topology on
L^{1}(X,\mu)
, where
f_{n}\xrightarrow{weak^{*}}f
if and only if
\int_{X}f_{n}g\,\mathrm{d}\mu\to\int_{X}fg\,\mathrm{d}\mu
for every
g\in C(X)
,

•

the
\tau^{k}
-topology on
\mathcal{P}(X)
for given
k\in{\mathbbm{N}}
, which is the coarsest topology such that, for every bounded Borel function
a:X^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
, the map
\mu\mapsto\int_{X^{k}}a\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\oplus k}
is continuous,

•

the
weak topology on
L^{1}(X,\mu)
, where
f_{n}\xrightarrow{weak}f
if and only if
\int_{X}f_{n}g\,\mathrm{d}\mu\to\int_{X}fg\,\mathrm{d}\mu
for every
g\in L^{\infty}(X,\mu)
.

By the
weak
topology on
\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}([0,1]):\mu\ll\lambda\}
, we will mean the topology inherited from the weak topology on
L^{1}([0,1],\lambda)
via the natural embedding
\mu\mapsto\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mu}{\,\mathrm{d}\lambda}
.
On this set, the weak topology coincides with the
\tau^{1}
-topology, since every measurable function can be made Borel by changing it on a null-set.
Also, note that if we view each element
f
of
L^{1}(X,\mu)
as a Borel measure
f\,\mathrm{d}\mu
defined by
(f\,\mathrm{d}\mu)(A):=\int_{A}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)
on
X
, then on the intersection with
\mathcal{P}(X)
, the corresponding weak
∗
topologies coincide.

Some classical results that we will need are collected in Appendix
A
.

We start with some basic observations concerning the function

Proposition 3.1
.

Let

.
Then we have the following:

1.

2.

4.

if

\mathbf{RN}(W)
contains at least two (not weakly isomorphic) graphons and there is

Proof.

Recall that
\ell\!\left(x\right):=x\log(x)
for

.
Its restriction to

is a convex continuous function.

Proof of
1
.
Since
\ell
is a convex function on
[0,\infty)

and
\int\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mu}{\,\mathrm{d}\lambda}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda=1
for every probability measure

, we have by Jensen’s inequality that
\int\ell\!\left(\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mu}{\,\mathrm{d}\lambda}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\geqslant\ell\!\left(\int\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mu}{\,\mathrm{d}\lambda}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\right)=\ell\!\left(1\right)=0
, giving the required inequality

for every

.

Before we proceed with the remaining parts of the proposition, we formulate an auxiliary claim.

Claim 3.2
.

Let a measure
\mu
and a sequence
(\mu_{n})_{n\in{\mathbbm{N}}}
of measures in
\{\nu\in\mathcal{P}([0,1]):\nu\ll\lambda\}
satisfy
\mu_{n}\xrightarrow{weak}\mu
(that is,

\frac{d\mu_{n}}{d\lambda}\xrightarrow{weak}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mu}{\,\mathrm{d}\lambda}

Then we have

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{[0,1]}\ell\!\left(\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}}{\,\mathrm{d}\lambda}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\geqslant\int_{[0,1]}\ell\!\left(\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mu}{\,\mathrm{d}\lambda}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda,\quad\mbox{as $n\to\infty$}.

Proof.

For
a\in[-1/{\mathrm{e}},\infty)
define

Y_{a}:=\left\{f\in L^{1}([0,1],\lambda):f\geqslant 0\mbox{ $\lambda$-a.e.},\ \int_{[0,1]}f\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\leqslant 1,\ \int_{[0,1]}\ell\!\left(f\right)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\leqslant a\right\}.

Let us show that for every
a\in[-1/{\mathrm{e}},\infty)
, the subset
Y_{a}\subseteq L^{1}([0,1],\lambda)
is closed in the
L^{1}
-topology. Take any functions
f_{n}
in
Y_{a}
that converge in the
L^{1}
-norm to some
f\in L^{1}([0,1],\lambda)
as

. Of course,
f\geqslant 0

\lambda
-a.e. and

\int_{[0,1]}f\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\leqslant 1
.
By passing to a subsequence, we can assume that
f_{n}(x)\to f(x)
for
\lambda
-a.e.

. By the continuity of the function
\ell
, we have that
\ell\!\left(f_{n}(x)\right)\to\ell\!\left(f(x)\right)
for
\lambda
-a.e.
x\in[0,1]
. Since
\ell
is bounded from below by
-1/{\mathrm{e}}
, it holds by Fatou’s lemma that

a\geqslant\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{[0,1]}\ell\!\left(f_{n}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\geqslant\int_{[0,1]}\left(\liminf_{n\to\infty}\ell\!\left(f_{n}\right)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda=\int_{[0,1]}\ell\!\left(f\right)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda.

Thus

and we conclude that the set
Y_{a}
is closed as required.

Since
\ell
is a convex function, the set
Y_{a}
is also convex for every
a
. Thus, by Mazur’s lemma (Theorem
A.4
), the set
Y_{a}
is also weakly closed.

Let
a:=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{[0,1]}\ell\!\left(\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}}{\,\mathrm{d}\lambda}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda
. We have
a\geqslant-1/{\mathrm{e}}
. Suppose that
a<\infty
as otherwise the claim holds trivially. For any
\epsilon>0
there are infinitely many
n
with
\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}}{\,\mathrm{d}\lambda}\in Y_{a+\epsilon}
; thus the weak limit
\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mu}{\,\mathrm{d}\lambda}
also belongs to the weakly closed set
Y_{a+\epsilon}
. Since
\epsilon>0
was arbitrary, it holds that
\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mu}{\,\mathrm{d}\lambda}\in Y_{a}
, as desired.∎

Proof of
2
.
If
\delta_{\Box}(U,W)=0
then
K_{W}(U)\leqslant 0
(since we can take
\mu:=\lambda
in the definition of

For the forward implication, suppose that
K_{W}(U)=0
.
By the definition of
K_{W}
, there is a sequence
(\mu_{n})_{n\in{\mathbbm{N}}}

\mathcal{P}([0,1])

\mu_{n}\ll\lambda

\delta_{\Box}(W_{\mu_{n}},U)=0

.

Let

.
It is easy to see that

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}^{\oplus k}}{\,\mathrm{d}\lambda^{\oplus k}}(x)=\prod_{i\in[k]}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}}{\,\mathrm{d}\lambda}(x_{i})

holds for

A direct use of Fubini-Tonelli’s theorem gives

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]^{k}}\ell\!\left(\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}^{\oplus k}}{\,\mathrm{d}\lambda^{\oplus k}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda^{\oplus k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]^{k}}\left(\prod_{i\in[k]}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}}{\,\mathrm{d}\lambda}(x_{i})\right)\left(\sum_{i\in[k]}\log\left(\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}}{\,\mathrm{d}\lambda}(x_{i})\right)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda^{\oplus k}(x)

\displaystyle k\int_{[0,1]}\ell\!\left(\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}}{\,\mathrm{d}\lambda}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda,\quad\mbox{for every $n\in{\mathbbm{N}}$.}

This tends to

\ell\geqslant-1/{\mathrm{e}}

By iteratively repeating the above step for each

and doing diagonalisation, we can assume that, for every

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}^{\oplus k}}{\,\mathrm{d}\lambda^{\oplus k}}\xrightarrow{weak}\frac{d\nu_{k}}{\lambda^{\oplus k}}
as

.

Set
\mu:=\nu_{1}
. Let us show that, for each

\nu_{k}

of Borel sets since such products are closed under intersection and generate the Borel

\int{\mathbbm{1}}_{A_{1}\times\dots\times A_{k}}d\nu_{k}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int{\mathbbm{1}}_{A_{1}\times\dots\times A_{k}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}^{\oplus k}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\int{\mathbbm{1}}_{A_{i}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\int{\mathbbm{1}}_{A_{i}}d\mu,

as desired.

Next we exploit the fact that

For every graph

\begin{split}t(F,U)=t(F,W_{\mu_{n}})=&\int_{[0,1]^{{k}}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}^{\oplus k}}{\,\mathrm{d}\lambda^{\oplus k}}(x)\prod_{\{i,j\}\in E(F)}W(x_{i},x_{j})\,\mathrm{d}\lambda^{\oplus k}(x)\\

Thus
\delta_{\Box}(U,W_{\mu})=0

It remains to show that
\mu=\lambda

.
Claim
3.2
gives that

\varphi:[0,1]\to[0,1]
be an arbitrary measure preserving bijection.
We can assume that at least half measure of the pre-image
\varphi^{-1}(\,(0,\frac{1}{2})\,)
lies in the first half of
[0,1]
by the symmetry between
X_{A}
and
Y_{A}
. Thus
C:=\varphi^{-1}(\,(0,\frac{1}{2})\,)\cap(0,\frac{1}{2})
has measure at least
1/4
. By the measure preservation of
\varphi
, we have that the measure of
D:=\varphi^{-1}(\,(\frac{1}{2},1)\,)\cap(\frac{1}{2},1)
is

\textstyle\lambda(D)=\frac{1}{2}-\lambda\left(\varphi^{-1}(\,(\frac{1}{2},1)\,)\cap(0,\frac{1}{2})\right)=\frac{1}{2}-\lambda\left((0,\frac{1}{2})\setminus C\right)=\lambda(C).

Then we have

\begin{split}\int_{C\times D}V^{\varphi}(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda^{\oplus 2}\ &=\ \int_{\varphi(C)\times\varphi(D)}V(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda^{\oplus 2}\ \geqslant\ b\lambda^{\oplus 2}(C\times D)-\frac{\epsilon}{4}\\
\int_{C\times D}U(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda^{\oplus 2}\ &\leqslant\ a\lambda^{\oplus 2}(C\times D)+\frac{\epsilon}{4}.\end{split}

and thus

d_{\Box}(U,V^{\varphi})\geqslant(b-a)\lambda(C_{i}\times D_{i})-\frac{\epsilon}{2}\geqslant\frac{(b-a)-8\epsilon}{16}>\frac{(b-a)}{144}.

Since
\varphi
was an arbitrary measure preserving function, we have
d_{\Box}(U,V)>0
, as desired.

Finally, take any
\widetilde{V}\in\mathbf{RN}(W)
not weakly isomorphic to
W
, which exists by above. By Part
3
, there is
\widetilde{U}\in\mathbf{RN}(W)
such that
K_{W}(U)<\infty
and
\delta_{\Box}(U,V)<\delta_{\Box}(W,V)
. Then
\delta_{\Box}(U,W)>0
and, by Parts
1
and
2
, we have
K_{W}(U)>0
. Thus
K_{W}(U)\in(0,\infty)
, finishing the proof.∎

(\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}_{n,W})_{n\in{\mathbbm{N}}}

In this section, we completely resolve the LDP problem for the measures
\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}_{n,W}
, which come from sampling uniform
x\in[0,1]^{n}
and taking the graphon of the edge-weighted graph
H
on
[n]
with

.

Theorem 4.1
.

Take any graphon
W\in{\mathcal{W}}
and any function
s:{\mathbbm{N}}\to(0,\infty)
with
s(n)\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. We consider the existence of an LDP for the sequence
(\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}_{n,W})_{n\in{\mathbbm{N}}}
of probability measures on the metric space
(\widetilde{\mathcal{W}},\delta_{\Box})
with speed
s
.

1.

If
s(n)=o(n)
then there is an LDP with rate function
\chi_{W}
.

2.

If
s(n)=(c+o(1))n
for some
c\in(0,\infty)
then there is an LDP with rate function
\frac{1}{c}\,K_{W}
.

3.

If
s(n)=\omega(n)
, then there is an LDP with rate function
\chi_{\mathbf{RN}(W)}
.

4.

If
W
is a constant graphon, then LDP holds (regardless of the speed
s
) with rate function
\chi_{W}=K_{W}=\chi_{\mathbf{RN}(W)}
.

5.

If
W
is not a constant graphon and
s(n)
does not satisfy any of the assumptions
1
–
3
, then there is no LDP (that is, no function
I:\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}\to[0,\infty]
satisfies Definition
1.1
).

Before we prove Theorem
4.1
, let us show that the measures
(\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}_{n,W})_{n\in{\mathbbm{N}}}
are supported around
\mathbf{RN}(W)
in the metric
\delta_{\Box}
(which implies that any rate function for any speed must be
\infty
outside of the closed set
\mathbf{RN}(W)
).

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
W\in{\mathcal{W}}
and
n\in{\mathbbm{N}}
.
Then

\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}_{n,W}\left(\left\{\widetilde{U}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}:\delta_{\Box}(\widetilde{U},\mathbf{RN}(W))\leqslant\frac{1}{n}\right\}\right)=1.

Proof.

The proof consists of two steps.
In the first step we show that the claim holds whenever
W
attains only finitely many values.
In the second step we use the fact that such graphons are dense in
\mathcal{W}
in the
L^{\infty}
-norm to extend the claim to any
W
. It will be convenient in the proof to use also the measures
(\mathbb{H}_{n,W})_{n\in{\mathbbm{N}}}
on the space
\mathcal{W}
. Recall that this is the version of
(\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}_{n,W})_{n\in{\mathbbm{N}}}
, where we do not take the equivalence class
\widetilde{f^{H}}
of the sampled weighted graph
H
but simply output
f^{H}
.

Step (I).

Suppose first that
W
attains only finitely many values, i.e., the set
W\!\left([0,1]^{2}\right)
is finite.
Then the measures
\mathbb{H}_{n,W}
are atomic for every
n\in{\mathbbm{N}}
.
Let
U\in\mathcal{W}
be such that
\mathbb{H}_{n,W}(\{U\})>0
.
Note that by the definition of
\mathbb{H}_{n,W}
there is
H\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
such that
U=f^{H}
and if we put

X_{H}:=\left\{x\in[0,1]^{{n}}:\mbox{for all }i<j\mbox{ in }[n]\ \ W(x_{i},x_{j})=w_{H}(i,j)\right\},

then we have
\lambda^{\oplus n}\left(X_{H}\right)>0
.
By
d_{1}\geqslant d_{\Box}
, it is enough to find a sequence
(V_{k})_{k\in{\mathbbm{N}}}
in
\mathcal{W}
such that

\lim_{k\to\infty}d_{1}\left(V_{k},U\right)\leqslant\frac{1}{n}
and
\widetilde{V}_{k}\in\mathbf{RN}(W)
for each
k
.

Recall that
B_{\infty}(x,r)
denotes the (open) radius-
r
ball around
x\in[0,1]^{{n}}
in the
L^{\infty}
-metric.
By the Lebesgue Density Theorem (for the
L^{\infty}
-metric on
[0,1]^{{n}}
), the set of density points of
X_{H}
has measure
\lambda^{\oplus n}(X_{H})>0
so it contains a point
{{x}}\in(0,1)^{{n}}
such that
{{x}}_{i}\not={{x}}_{j}
for every
i<j
in
[n]
. Recall that
{x}
being a density point of
X_{H}
means that

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{\lambda^{\oplus n}(X_{H}\cap B_{\infty}\left({{x}},\epsilon\right))}{\lambda^{\oplus n}(B_{\infty}\left({{x}},\epsilon\right))}=1.

Let
k\in{\mathbbm{N}}
be large enough. Then the intervals
J_{i,k}:=\left[{{x}}_{i}-\frac{1}{k},{{x}}_{i}+\frac{1}{k}\right]
, for
i\in[n]
, are pairwise disjoint.
Similarly to Definition
3.3
, let

V_{k}:=W_{(J_{1,k},\dots,J_{n,k})}

be obtained from
W
by putting mass
1/n
uniformly into each of the intervals
J_{1,k},\dots,J_{n,k}
.
By definition,
V_{k}\in\mathbf{RN}(W)
.
Let
i<j
in
[n]
and define

A_{i,j,k}:=\left\{(a,b)\in I^{n}_{i}\times I^{n}_{j}:V_{k}(a,b)\not=w_{H}(i,j)\right\}.

Since the intervals
I_{1}^{n},\ldots,I_{n}^{n}
in
V_{k}
correspond to the invervals
J_{1,k},\ldots,J_{n,k}
in
W
, we have by Fubini-Tonelli’s Theorem that

\begin{split}\lambda^{\oplus 2}\left(A_{i,j,k}\right)\,=\,&\textstyle\frac{(2k)^{2}}{n^{2}}\,\lambda^{\oplus 2}\left(\left\{(a,b)\in B_{\infty}\!\left(({{x}}_{i},{{x}}_{j}),\frac{1}{k}\right):W(a,b)\not=w_{H}(i,j)\right\}\right)\\
\,=\,&\textstyle\frac{(2k)^{n}}{n^{2}}\,\lambda^{\oplus n}\left(\left\{{{z}}\in B_{\infty}\left({{x}},\frac{1}{k}\right):W({{z}}_{i},{{z}}_{j})\not=w_{H}(i,j)\right\}\right)\\
\,\leqslant\,&\textstyle\frac{(2k)^{n}}{n^{2}}\,\lambda^{\oplus n}(B_{\infty}\!\left({{x}},\frac{1}{k}\right)\setminus X_{H})\,=\,\frac{\lambda^{\oplus n}(B_{\infty}\left({{x}},\frac{1}{k}\right)\setminus X_{H})}{n^{2}\,\lambda^{\oplus n}(B_{\infty}\!\left({{x}},\frac{1}{k}\right))},\end{split}

which tends to
0
as

since
{x}
is a density point for
X_{H}
.

Noticing that, for
1\leqslant i<j\leqslant k
,
A_{i,j,k}
is exactly the subset of
I^{n}_{i}\times I^{n}_{j}
where
U
and
V_{k}
differ we have

\begin{split}d_{1}\left(V_{k},U\right)\ =\ &\int_{[0,1]^{2}}\left|V_{k}-U\right|\,\mathrm{d}\lambda^{\oplus 2}\\
\ \leqslant\ &\sum_{i\in[n]}\lambda^{\oplus 2}\left(I^{n}_{i}\times I^{n}_{i}\right)+2\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}\lambda^{\oplus 2}\left(A_{i,j,k}\right)\ \xrightarrow{k\to\infty}\ \frac{1}{n}.\end{split}

This finishes the first step of the proof.

Step (II).
Let
W
be an arbitrary graphon and let
n\in{\mathbbm{N}}
.
For
k\in{\mathbbm{N}}
, let
W_{k}\in\mathcal{W}
be such that

and
W_{k}
attains only finitely many values. Note that
for every Borel probability measure

that is absolutely continuous with respect to
\lambda
, we have

\delta_{\Box}\left(W_{\mu},(W_{k})_{\mu}\right)\leqslant\frac{1}{k}.

Indeed, we can assume that we use the same measure-preserving map

when defining

and

as in (

\delta_{\Box}\left(W_{\mu},(W_{k})_{\mu}\right)\leqslant\sup_{A,B}|\int_{A\times B}(W-W_{k})\mu^{\oplus 2}|
while the function

is at most

except on a set which is null in

and thus also in
\mu^{\oplus 2}\ll\lambda^{\oplus 2}
.

It follows that

\begin{split}\left\{\widetilde{U}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}:\delta_{\Box}\left(\widetilde{U},\mathbf{RN}(W_{k})\right)\leqslant\frac{1}{n}\right\}\subseteq\left\{\widetilde{V}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}:\delta_{\Box}\left(\widetilde{V},\mathbf{RN}(W)\right)\leqslant\frac{1}{n}+\frac{1}{k}\right\}.\end{split}

(15)

In the natural coupling between

and

and thus the

with probability 1. Since

and the left-hand side of (

-measure

by Step (I), we conclude that the

-measure of the

in

is also 1. Now the proposition follows
by the

-additivity of
\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}_{n,W}
since

was arbitrary.
∎

Now we can now resolve the LDP problem for
\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}_{n,W}
for every speed.

Let

Claim 4.3
.

The map

Proof of Claim.

It is enough to show that, for every

,

, and every Borel function

. The pre-image is exactly the set

Let

\infty,&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

(17)

Theorem 4.4

(Eichelsbacher

)

Let

and let

5
Exponential equivalence

The following notion of
exponential equivalence
is useful for transferring an LDP proved for one sequence of measures to another (see e.g.
[
16
]
*Theorem 4.2.13)
We use the standard definition (as in
[
16
, Definition 4.2.10]
),
which simplifies slightly when restricted to the case of separable metric spaces.

Definition 5.1

(Exponential equivalence)
.

Let
(\mu_{n})_{n\in{\mathbbm{N}}}
and
(\nu_{n})_{n\in{\mathbbm{N}}}
be sequences of Borel probability measures on a separable metric space
(X,d)
. Let
s:{\mathbbm{N}}\to(0,\infty)
satisfy
s(n)\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
.
We say that
(\mu_{n})_{n\in{\mathbbm{N}}}
and
(\nu_{n})_{n\in{\mathbbm{N}}}
are
exponentially equivalent for speed
s
if there is a sequence of Borel probability measure
(\Theta_{n})_{n\in{\mathbbm{N}}}
on
X\times X
such that for every
n\in{\mathbbm{N}}
the marginals of
\Theta_{n}
are
\mu_{n}
and
\nu_{n}
, and for every
\alpha>0
it holds that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{s(n)}\log\left(\Theta_{n}\left(\{(x,y)\in X\times X:d(x,y)>\alpha\}\right)\right)=-\infty.

Informally speaking, the definition states that there is a coupling such that the probability of seeing a pair of outcomes at distance bounded from 0 decays faster than
{\mathrm{e}}^{-O(s)}
.

Let us show that, for any speed
o(n^{2})
, the
n
-vertex samples from a graphon, one with edge rounding and the other without edge rounding, are exponentially equivalent.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
W\in{\mathcal{W}}
. Take any function
s:{\mathbbm{N}}\to(0,\infty)
such that
s(n)\to\infty
and
s(n)/n^{2}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.
Then the sequences
(\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}_{n,W})_{n\in{\mathbbm{N}}}
and
(\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}_{n,W})_{n\in{\mathbbm{N}}}
are exponentially equivalent for speed
s
.

Proof.

Let
n\in{\mathbbm{N}}
and
H\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
.
Recall that
w_{H}
denotes the edge-weight function of
H
. Let
\Lambda_{n,H}
be the atomic probability measure on
\mathcal{W}
which, for every graph
G
on
[n]
, gives mass

\Lambda_{n,H}(\{f^{G}\}):=\prod_{\{i,j\}\in E(G)}w_{H}(i,j)\prod_{\{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}\}\in E(\overline{G})}\left(1-w_{H}(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\right)

(19)

to
f^{G}
. This is exactly the distribution after edge-rounding the weighted graph
H
(and taking the corresponding graphon in
\mathcal{W}
).
It is easy to see that, for every
n\in{\mathbbm{N}}
, the assignment
(H,G)\mapsto\Lambda_{n,H}\left(\left\{f^{G}\right\}\right)
is Borel measurable.
This allows us to define a Borel probability measure
\Lambda_{n,W}
on
\mathcal{W}\times\mathcal{W}
by

\Lambda_{n,W}(A):=\int\Lambda_{n,H}\left(\left\{f^{G}:G\in\mathcal{G}_{n},\ \left(f^{H},f^{G}\right)\in A\right\}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{H}_{n,W}(f^{H}),

where
A\subseteq\mathcal{W}\times\mathcal{W}
is a Borel set.
Informally speaking, the first component is the weighted
n
-vertex sample
H
from
W
while the second component is an edge rounding of
H
(or, more precisely, we take the graphons corresponding to these graphs).
Define the probability measure
\Theta_{n,W}
as the push-forward of
\Lambda_{n,W}
via
\mathcal{W}\times\mathcal{W}\twoheadrightarrow\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}\times\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
, the product of the quotient maps.
One can easily verify that the marginals of
\Theta_{n,W}
are
\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}_{n,W}
and
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}_{n,W}
.

Let us show that the coupling given by
\Theta_{n,W}
satisfies Definition
5.1
.
Take any
\alpha>0
.
We set

\mathcal{O}_{\alpha}:=\{(\widetilde{U},\widetilde{V})\in\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}\times\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}:\delta_{\Box}(\widetilde{U},\widetilde{V})>\alpha\}

and

\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}:=\{(U,V)\in\mathcal{W}\times\mathcal{W}:\delta_{\Box}(U,V)>\alpha\}.

Clearly, we have
\Theta_{n,W}(\mathcal{O}_{\alpha})=\Lambda_{n,W}(\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha})
. Our aim is to show that this is
{\mathrm{e}}^{-\omega(s(n))}
as
n\to\infty
.

We need the following result. Recall that the measure
\Lambda_{n,H}
was defined in (
19
).

Claim 5.3
.

For every
k\in{\mathbbm{N}}
there is
C>0
such that for every
F\in\mathcal{G}_{k}
,
\beta>0
,
n\in{\mathbbm{N}}
and
H\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
it holds that

\Lambda_{n,H}\left(\left\{f^{G}:G\in\mathcal{G}_{n},\ \left|t(F,f^{G})-t(F,f^{H})\right|>\beta\right\}\right)\leqslant{\mathrm{e}}^{-C\beta^{2}n^{2}}.

(20)

Proof.

Let
F\in\mathcal{G}_{k}
,
n\in{\mathbbm{N}}
and
H\in\mathcal{H}_{n}
.
Let
\hom(F,G)
denote the number of
homomorphisms
from
F
to
G
, that is, functions
V(F)\to V(G)
that send edges to edges.
Note that
\hom(F,G)=t(F,f^{G})\,n^{k}
.
With this notation we have that the left-hand side of (
20
) is equal to

\Lambda_{n,H}\left(\left\{f^{G}:G\in\mathcal{G}_{n},\ \left|\hom(F,G)-t(F,f^{H})n^{k}\right|>\beta n^{k}\right\}\right).

Enumerate the set
\binom{[n]}{2}
as
\left\{e_{1},\dots,e_{m}\right\}
,
m:={n\choose 2}
.
Consider the corresponding edge-exposure martingale for
t(F,\_)
in the edge rounding process for
H
. Formally, for
s\in\{0,\dots,m\}
we define a random variable
X_{s}:\mathcal{G}_{n}\to\mathbb{R}
as

X_{s}(G):=\sum_{f\in[n]^{[k]}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{i,j\}\in E(F)\\
a(f(i),f(j))>s\end{subarray}}w_{H}(f(i),f(j))\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{i,j\}\in E(F)\\
a(f(i),f(j))\leqslant s\end{subarray}}{\mathbbm{1}}_{\{f(i),f(j)\}\in E(G)},\quad G\in\mathcal{G}_{n},

where
a(i,j)
for
ij\in{[n]\choose 2}
is the unique index in
\left[m\right]
with
e_{a(i,j)}=\{i,j\}
.
Consider the
(
\sigma
-)algebra
filtration
\left(\mathcal{B}_{s}\right)_{s=0}^{m}
on
\mathcal{G}_{n}
that is generated by the functions
\left({\mathbbm{1}}_{e_{i}\in E(G)}\right)_{i=1}^{s}
, i.e., revealing edges one-by-one according to the enumeration.
It is easy to see that the sequence
\left(X_{s}\right)_{s=0}^{m}
forms a martingale with respect to the filtration
\left(\mathcal{B}_{s}\right)_{s=0}^{m}
.
Moreover
\mathbb{E}(X_{0})=t(F,f^{H})n^{k}
and
X_{m}(G)=\hom(F,G)
for every
G\in\mathcal{G}_{n}
.
Let
s\in[m]
.
Then we have

\begin{split}\left|X_{s}-X_{s-1}\right|\leqslant&\ \left|\left\{f:\in[n]^{[k]}:e_{s}\subseteq f(\,[k]\,)\right\}\right|\leqslant k^{2}n^{k-2}.\end{split}

By Azuma’s inequality (see e.g.
[
1
, Theorem 7.2.1]
), we have that, for some
C=C(k)>0
,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{n,H}\left(\left\{f^{G}:G\in{\mathcal{G}}_{n},\ \left|\hom(F,G)-t(F,f^{H})n^{k}\right|>\beta n^{k}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Lambda_{n,H}\left(\left\{f^{G}:G\in{\mathcal{G}}_{n},\ \left|X_{m}-\mathbb{E}\left(X_{0}\right)\right|>\beta n^{k}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\ 2{\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{\beta^{2}n^{2k}}{2mk^{4}n^{2(k-2)}}}\leqslant{\mathrm{e}}^{-C\beta^{2}n^{2}}.

This finishes the proof of the claim.
∎

We are now ready to verify Definition
5.1

for any given
\alpha>0
. The Inverse Counting Lemma of Borgs et al
[
6
, Theorem 2.7(b)]
(see also
[
33
, Lemma 10.32]
)
implies that (depending on
\alpha>0
) there are
k\in{\mathbbm{N}}
and
\beta>0
such that

\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}\subseteq\{(U,V)\in\mathcal{W}\times\mathcal{W}:\exists F\in{\mathcal{G}}_{k}\ \left|t(F,U)-t(F,V)\right|>\beta\}.

We have

\begin{split}\Lambda_{n,W}\left(\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}\right)=&\int\Lambda_{n,H}\left(\left\{f^{G}:G\in\mathcal{G}_{n},\ \left(f^{H},f^{G}\right)\in\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}\right\}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{H}_{n,W}(f^{H})\\
\leqslant&\int\Lambda_{n,H}\left(\left\{f^{G}:G\in\mathcal{G}_{n},\ \exists F\in{\mathcal{G}}_{k}\ \left|t(F,f^{H})-t(F,f^{G})\right|>\beta\right\}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{H}_{n,W}(f^{H})\\
\leqslant&\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{G}}_{k}}\int\Lambda_{n,H}\left(\left\{f^{G}:G\in\mathcal{G}_{n},\ \left|t(F,f^{H})-t(F,f^{G})\right|>\beta\}\right\}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{H}_{n,W}(f^{H})\\
\leqslant&\ 2^{{k\choose 2}}{\mathrm{e}}^{-C\beta^{2}n^{2}}\end{split}

where
C>0
depends only on
k\in{\mathbbm{N}}
and the last inequality follows from Claim
5.3
.
Putting all together, we obtain

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{s(n)}\log\left(\Theta_{n,W}\left(\mathcal{O}_{\alpha}\right)\right)\leqslant\limsup_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{s(n)}C\beta^{2}n^{2}=-\infty

by the assumption that
s(n)=o(n^{2})
.

This verifies all requirements of Definition
5.1
and finishes the proof.
∎

6
LDPs for the measures
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}_{n,W}

Now we are ready to establish Theorem
1.3
that deals with LDPs for the random (edge rounded) graph
{\mathbbm{G}}(n,W)
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

It is well-known (see e.g.
[
16
]
*Theorem 4.2.13) that, for a given speed, if two sequences of probability measures on the same space are exponentially equivalent then one sequence satisfies an LDP if and only if the other satisfies the same LDP.
Thus the combination of Lemma
5.2
and Theorem
4.1
easily implies all claims of Theorem
1.3
, apart from Part
4
.

Let us prove Part
4
. Here the speed is
s(n)=\omega(n^{2})
and the stated rate function is
\chi_{\mathbf{FORB}(W)}
. There are two steps in the proof.

(I)

If
\widetilde{U}\not\in\mathbf{FORB}(W)
, then there are
\alpha>0
and
n_{0}\in{\mathbbm{N}}
such that
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}_{n,W}\left(B_{\delta_{\Box}}(\widetilde{U},\alpha)\right)=0
for every
n\geqslant n_{0}
.

(II)

If
\alpha>0
and
G\in\mathcal{G}_{k}
are such that
t_{\mathrm{ind}}(G,W)\not=0
, then for every
k\in{\mathbbm{N}}
we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{s(n)}\log\left(\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}_{n,W}\left(B_{\delta_{\Box}}\left(\widetilde{f^{G}},\frac{1}{k}+\alpha\right)\right)\right)=0.

(21)

First we show how
(I)
and
(II)
imply Part
4
of the theorem.

Let
F\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
be a
\delta_{\Box}
-closed set.
If
F\cap\mathbf{FORB}(W)\not=\emptyset
, then the LDP upper bound (
2
) is vacuously satisfied.
Otherwise for some
\alpha>0
we have
\delta_{\Box}(F,\mathbf{FORB}(W))\geqslant\alpha
because we take the distance between two disjoint closed sets in a compact metric space.
For each
\widetilde{U}\in F
we pick
\alpha_{U}\in(0,\alpha)
and
n_{U}\in{\mathbbm{N}}
that satisfy
(I)
. Of course, the open balls centred at
\widetilde{U}
of radius
\alpha_{U}
over all
\widetilde{U}\in F
cover
F
.
By the compactness of
F
there is a finite subcover; let it be given by
\widetilde{U}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{U}_{l}\in F
.
Then, for every
n\geqslant\max_{i\in[l]}n_{U_{i}}
, we have

\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}_{n,W}(F)\leqslant\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}_{n,W}\left(\bigcup_{i\in[l]}B_{\delta_{\Box}}\left(\widetilde{U}_{i},\alpha_{U_{i}}\right)\right)=0.

Thus the LDP upper bound (
2
) vacuously holds.

Let
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
be a
\delta_{\Box}
-open set.
If
\mathcal{O}\cap\mathbf{FORB}(W)=\emptyset
, then the LDP lower bound (
1
) is vacuously satisfied.
Otherwise there are
\widetilde{U}\in\mathbf{FORB}(W)
and
\alpha>0
such that

B_{\delta_{\Box}}(\widetilde{U},3\alpha)\subseteq\mathcal{O}
.
It is known that the cut-distance between any graphon
U
and its typical
n
-vertex sample tends to 0 as
n\to\infty
(see e.g.
[
33
, Lemma 10.16]
). Thus there is a graph
G\in\mathcal{G}_{k}
, where we can additionally assume that
k>1/\alpha
, such that
t_{\mathrm{ind}}(G,U)>0
and
\delta_{\Box}(f^{G},U)<\alpha
. It follows that

B_{\delta_{\Box}}\left(\widetilde{f^{G}},\frac{1}{k}+\alpha\right)\subseteq B_{\delta_{\Box}}\left(\widetilde{U},\frac{1}{k}+\alpha+\delta_{\Box}(f^{G},U)\right)\subseteq B_{\delta_{\Box}}(\widetilde{U},3\alpha)\subseteq\mathcal{O}.

Since
\widetilde{U}\in\mathbf{FORB}(W)
, we must have
t_{\mathrm{ind}}(G,W)>0
. Thus we have by
(II)
that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{s(n)}\log\left(\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}_{n,W}\left(\mathcal{O}\right)\right)\geqslant\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{s(n)}\log\left(\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}_{n,W}\left(B_{\delta_{\Box}}\left(\widetilde{f^{G}},\frac{1}{k}+\alpha\right)\right)\right)=0,

and the LDP lower bound (
1
) vacuously holds.

It remains to establish each of
Steps (I)
and
(II)
.

Step (I).

Let
\widetilde{U}\not\in\mathbf{FORB}(W)
.
By definition there are
k\in{\mathbbm{N}}
and
H\in\mathcal{G}_{k}
such that
t_{\mathrm{ind}}(H,W)=0
and
t_{\mathrm{ind}}(H,U)>0
.
Since the map
t_{\mathrm{ind}}(H,\_)
is
\delta_{\Box}
-continuous by Theorem
2.2
, we can find
\alpha>0
such that

B_{\delta_{\Box}}(\widetilde{U},\alpha)\subseteq\left\{\widetilde{V}\in\mathcal{W}:t_{\mathrm{ind}}(H,V)>\frac{1}{2}\,t_{\mathrm{ind}}(H,U)\right\}.

We show that this
\alpha
works as required with
n_{0}:=\lceil{k^{2}}/t_{\mathrm{ind}}(H,U)\rceil
.

Let
n\geqslant n_{0}
and
G\in\mathcal{G}_{n}
be any graph such that
\delta_{\Box}(f^{G},U)<\alpha
.
In particular, we have

t_{\mathrm{ind}}(H,f^{G})-\binom{k}{2}\frac{1}{n}>\frac{1}{2}\left(t_{\mathrm{ind}}(H,U)-\frac{k^{2}}{n}\right)>0

(22)

Note that

\lambda^{\oplus k}\left(\left\{x\in[0,1]^{k}:\exists\,l\in[n]\ \exists\,i\not=j\in[k]\ x_{i},x_{j}\in I^{n}_{l}\right\}\right)\leqslant\binom{k}{2}\frac{1}{n}.

Thus, by
(
22
) we can find
an injective embedding of
H
into
G
. It follows by Fubini-Tonelli’s theorem that

t_{\mathrm{ind}}(G,W)\leqslant t_{\mathrm{ind}}(H,W)
.
Since
t_{\mathrm{ind}}(H,W)=0
, we have
t_{\mathrm{ind}}(G,W)=0
. This implies that

\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}_{n,W}(\{\widetilde{f^{G}}\})\leqslant n!\,t_{\mathrm{ind}}(G,W)
is zero. Since the discrete measure
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}_{n,W}
is supported on
\{\widetilde{f^{G}}:G\in{\mathcal{G}}_{n}\}
, we conclude that
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}_{n,W}\left(B_{\delta_{\Box}}\left(\widetilde{U},\alpha\right)\right)=0
, as desired.

Step (II)
.
Let
\alpha>0
and let
G\in\mathcal{G}_{k}
be such that

. Let
H\sim{\mathbbm{G}}(n,W)
be the random

-vertex sample from

.
For
\gamma\in(0,1)
define

X_{\gamma}:=\{x\in[0,1]^{{k}}:\forall\{i,j\}\in E(G)\ W(x_{i},x_{j})\geqslant\gamma,\ \forall\{i,j\}\in E(\overline{G})\ W(x_{i},x_{j})\leqslant 1-\gamma\}.

It follows from
t_{\mathrm{ind}}(G,W)>0
and the

\lambda^{\oplus k}
that there is

By the Lebesgue Density Theorem, we can find
{{x}}\in X_{\gamma}
such that
{{x}}_{i}\not={{x}}_{j}

[k]

\lim_{l\to\infty}\frac{\lambda^{\oplus k}\left(X_{\gamma}\cap B_{\infty}\left({{x}},\frac{1}{l}\right)\right)}{\lambda^{\oplus k}\left(B_{\infty}\left({{x}},\frac{1}{l}\right)\right)}=1.

Fix

large enough so that

(23)

and the intervals

, are pairwise disjoint.

First, we prove (

) in the case when

is taken only over integers divisible by
k
. Informally, the main idea is that if an

\delta_{\Box}(f^{G},f^{H})=0
; furthermore, by adding any edges inside the

we increase the cut-distance by at most

and thus we can afford any further
\alpha{n\choose 2}
rounding errors without the cut-distance going over
1/k+\alpha
,

Let

be such that
m:=n/k
is an integer. For

, let

denote the (unique) integer in

congruent to

modulo
k
.
Define

\mathcal{X}:=\left\{x\in[0,1]^{{n}}:\forall i\in[n]\ x_{i}\in J_{i\mathrm{\,mod\,}k}\right\}

and note that
\lambda^{\oplus k}\left(\mathcal{X}\right)=\left(\frac{2}{l}\right)^{n}
.

Let
x
be a random
n
-vector, chosen uniformly from
\mathcal{X}
. Thus we put exactly
m
of the coordinates
x_{i}
into each interval
J_{j}
. Call a pair
ij\in{[n]\choose 2}

crossing
if
i\mathrm{\,mod\,}k\not=j\mathrm{\,mod\,}k
. Clearly, there are
m^{2}{k\choose 2}
crossing pairs. Let

be the set of crossing pairs

such that either
\{i\mathrm{\,mod\,}k,j\mathrm{\,mod\,}k\}\in E(G)
and
W(x_{i},x_{j})<\gamma
or
\{i\mathrm{\,mod\,}k,j\mathrm{\,mod\,}k\}\in E(\overline{G})
and
W(x_{i},x_{j})>1-\gamma
. We call the pairs in

x
-bad
and call all other crossing pairs
x
-good
.

Take any
i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\in[n]

to respectively

. Then
(x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{k}})

and by Fubini-Tonelli’s theorem, the probability of seeing at least one
x
-bad pair in
{\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\}\choose 2}
is the probability that a uniform element of

. This is at most

by (

). Thus the expected fraction (for uniform

) of

\alpha/2
.
Since every crossing pair participates in the same number of

-tuples in

with distinct residues modulo

x

. Thus the expected

By Markov’s inequality, the probability that

is at least

.

Note that if

Thus the probability that

•

Altogether we have

Consider the case of general

If

7
Coloured graphons

The definitions and results of this section are needed in order to establish the lower semi-continuity of the functions
J_{\mathbold{\alpha},\mathbold{p}}
and
R_{\mathbold{p}}
that were defined in Section
1.5
.

Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
By a
k
-coloured graphon
we mean a pair
(W,\mathcal{A})
where
W\in\mathcal{W}
and
\mathcal{A}\in{\bf A}^{(k)}
, that is,
\mathcal{A}
is a partition of
[0,1]
into
k
measurable parts. One can view the partition
\mathcal{A}
as a
k
-colouring of
[0,1]
.
Write
\mathcal{W}^{(k)}:={\mathcal{W}}\times{\bf A}^{(k)}
for the space of all
k
-coloured graphons.
We define the pseudo-metric
d^{(k)}_{\Box}
(the analogue of
d_{\Box}
) on
\mathcal{W}^{(k)}
as

d^{(k)}_{\Box}((U,\mathcal{A}),(V,\mathcal{B})):=\sup_{C,D\subseteq[0,1]}\sum_{i,j\in[k]}\left|\int_{C\times D}({{\mathbbm{1}}}_{A_{i}\times A_{j}}U-{{\mathbbm{1}}}_{B_{i}\times B_{j}}V)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda^{\oplus 2}\right|+\sum_{i\in[k]}\lambda(A_{i}\triangle B_{i}),

for
(U,\mathcal{A}),(V,\mathcal{B})\in\mathcal{W}^{(k)}
, where
{\mathcal{A}}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{k})
and
{\mathcal{B}}=(B_{1},\dots,B_{k})
.
Informally speaking, two
k
-coloured graphons are close to each other in
d^{(k)}
if
they have similar distributions of coloured edges across cuts, where an edge is coloured by the colours of its endpoints. The second term is added so that e.g. we can distinguish two constant-0 graphons with different part measures.

The
cut-distance
for coloured graphons is then defined as

\delta^{(k)}_{\Box}((U,\mathcal{A}),(V,\mathcal{B})):=\inf_{\phi,\psi}d^{(k)}_{\Box}((U,\mathcal{A})^{\phi},(V,\mathcal{B})^{\psi}),

(25)

where the infimum is taken over measure-preserving maps
\phi,\psi:[0,1]\to[0,1]
and we denote

(U,\mathcal{A})^{\phi}:=(U^{\phi},{\mathcal{A}}^{\phi})
and
{\mathcal{A}}^{\phi}:=(\phi^{-1}(A_{1}),\dots,\phi^{-1}(A_{k}))

with
(A_{1},\dots,A_{k})
being the parts of
{\mathcal{A}}
.
As in the graphon case (compare with e.g.
[
33
, Theorem 8.13]
), some other definitions give the same distance (e.g. it is enough to take the identity function for
\psi
). We chose this definition as it is immediately clear from it that the function
\delta^{(k)}_{\Box}
is symmetric and defines a pseudo-metric.
We write
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}^{(k)}
for the corresponding quotient, where we identify two
k
-coloured graphons
at
\delta^{(k)}_{\Box}
-distance 0.

Theorem 7.1
.

The metric space
(\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}^{(k)},\delta^{(k)}_{\Box})
is compact.

Proof.

The proof is obtained by the obvious adaptation of the proof of Lovász and Szegedy
[
32
, Theorem 5.1]
(see also
[
33
, Theorem 9.23]
) that the space
(\widetilde{\mathcal{W}},\delta_{\Box})
is compact.

Let
(W_{n},\mathcal{A}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an arbitrary sequence of elements of
\mathcal{W}^{(k)}
. We have to find a subsequence
that converges to some element of
\mathcal{W}^{(k)}
with respect to
\delta_{\Box}^{(k)}
.

When dealing with the elements of
\mathcal{W}^{(k)}
,
we can ignore null subsets of
[0,1]
; thus all relevant statements, e.g. that one partition refines another, are meant to hold almost everywhere.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let the parts of
\mathcal{A}_{n}
be
(A_{n,1},\dots,A_{n,k})
and, by applying a measure-preserving bijection to
(W_{n},\mathcal{A}_{n})
, assume by Theorem
2.1
that the colour classes
A_{n,1},\dots,A_{n,k}\subseteq[0,1]
are all intervals,
coming in this order. By passing to a subsequence, assume that, for each
i\in[k]
, the length of
A_{n,i}
converges to some
\alpha_{i}\in[0,1]
as
n\to\infty
. With
\mathbold{\alpha}:=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k})
, this gives rise to the “limiting” partition

\mathcal{A}:=(I_{1}^{(\mathbold{\alpha})},\dots,I_{k}^{(\mathbold{\alpha})})\in{\bf A}^{(\mathbold{\alpha})}

of
[0,1]
into intervals.

Let
m_{1}:=k
and inductively for
q=2,3,\ldots
let
m_{q}
be sufficiently large such that for every graphon
W
and a measurable partition
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
of
[0,1]
with
|\mathcal{A}^{\prime}|\leqslant m_{q-1}
there is a measurable partition
\mathcal{P}=(P_{1},\dots,P_{m})
of
[0,1]
refining
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
such that
m\leqslant m_{q}
and
d_{\Box}(W,W_{\mathcal{P}})\leqslant 1/q
. Here
W_{\mathcal{P}}
denotes the projection of
W
to the space of
{\mathcal{P}}
-step graphons; namely, for every
i,j\in[m]
with
P_{i}\times P_{j}
non-null in
\lambda^{\oplus 2}
,
W_{{\mathcal{P}}}
assumes the constant value
\frac{1}{\lambda(P_{i})\lambda(P_{j})}\int_{P_{i}\times P_{j}}W\,\mathrm{d}\lambda^{\oplus 2}
on
P_{i}\times P_{j}
(and, say,
W_{{\mathcal{P}}}
is defined to be 0 on all
\lambda^{\oplus 2}
-null products
P_{i}\times P_{j}
).
Such a number
m_{q}

exists by
[
33
, Lemma 9.15]
, a version of the Weak Regularity Lemma for graphons.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we do the following. Let
\mathcal{P}_{n,1}:=\mathcal{A}_{n}
and, inductively on
q=2,3,\dots
, let
\mathcal{P}_{n,q}
be the partition with at most
m_{q}
parts obtained by applying the above Weak Regularity Lemma to
(W_{n},\mathcal{P}_{n,q-1})
. By adding empty parts to
\mathcal{P}_{n,q}
, for each
q\geqslant 1
, we can assume that it has the same number of parts (namely,
m_{q}
) of each colour, that is, we can denote its parts as
(P_{n,q,i,j})_{i\in[k],j\in[m_{q}]}
, so that
P_{n,q,i,j}\subseteq A_{n,i}
for all
(i,j)\in[k]\times[m_{q}]
.
Also, define
W_{n,q}:=(W_{n})_{\mathcal{P}_{n,q}}
to be the projection of the graphon
W_{n}
on the space of
\mathcal{P}_{n,q}
-step graphons.

Then, iteratively for
q=2,3,\dots
, repeat the following. Find a measure-preserving bijection
\phi:[0,1]\to[0,1]
such that
(\mathcal{P}_{n,q})^{\phi}
is a partition into intervals and
\phi
preserves the previous partitions

\mathcal{P}_{n,1},\dots,\mathcal{P}_{n,q-1}
(each of which is a partition into intervals by induction on
q
). Then, for each
m\geqslant q
, replace
(W_{n,m},\mathcal{P}_{n,m})
by
(W_{n,m},\mathcal{P}_{n,m})^{\phi}
. When we are done with this step, the following properties hold for each integer
q\geqslant 2
:

•

\delta_{\Box}^{(k)}((W_{n,q},\mathcal{A}_{n}),(W_{n},\mathcal{A}_{n}))\leqslant k^{2}/q
;

•

the partition
\mathcal{P}_{n,q}
refines
\mathcal{P}_{n,q-1}
(and, inductively, also refines
\mathcal{A}_{n}=\mathcal{P}_{n,1}
);

•

|\mathcal{P}_{n,q}|=km_{q}
with exactly
m_{q}
parts assigned to each colour class of
\mathcal{A}_{n}
.

Next, iteratively for
q=1,2,\dots
, we pass to a subsequence of
n
so that for every
(i,j)\in[k]\times[m_{q}]
, the length of the interval
P_{n,q,i,j}
converges, and for every pair
(i,j),(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\in[k]\times[m_{q}]
, the common value of the step-graphon
W_{n,q}
on
P_{n,q,i,j}\times P_{n,q,i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}
converges.
It follows that the sequence
W_{n,q}
converges pointwise to some graphon
U_{q}
which is itself a step-function with
km_{q}
parts that are intervals. We use diagonalisation to find a subsequence of
n
so that, for each
q\in\mathbb{N}
,
W_{n,q}
converges to some step-graphon
U_{q}
a.e. as
n\to\infty
, with the step partition
\mathcal{P}_{q}
of
U_{q}
consisting of
km_{q}
intervals and refining the partition
{\mathcal{A}}
.

It follows that, for all
s<t
in
{\mathbbm{N}}
, the partition
\mathcal{P}_{t}
is a refinement of
\mathcal{P}_{s}
and, moreover,
U_{s}
is the conditional expectation
\mathbb{E}[U_{t}|\mathcal{P}_{s}]
a.e.
As observed in the proof of Lovász and Szegedy
[
32
, Theorem 5.1]
,
this (and
0\leqslant U_{t}\leqslant 1
a.e.) implies by the Martingale Convergence Theorem that
U_{q}
converge a.e. to some graphon
U
as
q\to\infty
. By the Dominated Convergence Theorem,
U_{q}\to U
also in the
L^{1}
-distance.

We claim that
\delta_{\Box}((W_{n},\mathcal{A}_{n}),(U,\mathcal{A}))\to 0
as
n\to\infty
(after we passed to the subsequence defined as above). Take any
\epsilon>0
. Fix an integer
q>4/\epsilon
such that
\|U-U_{q}\|_{1}\leqslant\epsilon/(4k^{2})
. Given
q
, fix
n_{0}
such that for all
n\geqslant n_{0}
we have
\|U_{q}-W_{n,q}\|_{1}\leqslant\epsilon/(4k^{2})
and
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda(I_{i}^{(\mathbold{\alpha})}\bigtriangleup A_{n,i})\leqslant\epsilon/4
. Then, for every
n\geqslant n_{0}
we have

\displaystyle\delta_{\Box}^{(k)}((U,\mathcal{A}),(W_{n},\mathcal{A}_{n}))

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle d_{\Box}^{(k)}((U,\mathcal{A}),(U_{q},\mathcal{A}))+d_{\Box}^{(k)}((U_{q},\mathcal{A}),(W_{n,q},\mathcal{A}_{n}))

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\delta_{\Box}^{(k)}((W_{n,q},\mathcal{A}_{n}),(W_{n},\mathcal{A}_{n}))

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle k^{2}\|U-U_{q}\|_{1}+k^{2}\|U_{q}-W_{n,q}\|_{1}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda(I_{i}^{(\mathbold{\alpha})}\bigtriangleup A_{n,i})+1/q

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\epsilon/4+\epsilon/4+\epsilon/4+\epsilon/4\ =\ \epsilon.

Since
\epsilon>0
was arbitrary, the claim is proved. Thus the metric space
(\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}^{(k)},\delta^{(k)}_{\Box})
is indeed compact.
∎

\displaystyle d_{\Box}(\Gamma(U,\mathcal{A}),\Gamma(V,\mathcal{B}))

.

,

\displaystyle\delta_{\Box}\left(F(U,{\mathcal{A}}),F(V,{\mathcal{B}})\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle d_{\Box}\left((F(U,{\mathcal{A}}))^{\psi},(F(V,{\mathcal{B}}))^{\phi}\right)\ =\ d_{\Box}\left(F(U^{\psi},{\mathcal{A}}^{\psi}),F(V^{\phi},{\mathcal{B}}^{\phi})\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle d_{\Box}^{(k)}((U^{\psi},{\mathcal{A}}^{\psi}),(V^{\phi},{\mathcal{B}}^{\phi}))\ <\ \delta_{\Box}^{(k)}\left((U,{\mathcal{A}}),(V,{\mathcal{B}})\right)+\epsilon.

This implies both claims about

For

(27)

In the special case of (

Lemma 8.3
.

The function
I^{(k)}_{\mathbold{p}}
gives a well-defined function
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}^{(k)}\to[0,\infty]
which, moreover, is lower semi-continuous as a function on
(\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}^{(k)},\delta^{(k)}_{\Box})
.

Proof.

Note that we can write

I^{(k)}_{\mathbold{p}}(U,\mathcal{A})=\sum_{1\leqslant i\leqslant j\leqslant k}I_{p_{i,j}}(\Gamma_{i,j}(U,\mathcal{A})),\quad(U,{\mathcal{A}})\in\mathcal{W}^{(k)},

(28)

because
\Gamma_{i,j}(U,{\mathcal{A}})
assumes value
p_{i,j}
outside of
(A_{i}\times A_{j})\cup(A_{j}\times A_{i})
while
I_{p}(p)=0
for any
p\in[0,1]
. Recall that, by Theorem
1.2
,

I_{p}
gives a well-defined function
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}\to[0,\infty]
for every
p\in[0,1]
.
Thus, by Lemma
8.2
, the right-hand side of (
28
) does not change if we replace
(U,{\mathcal{A}})
by any other element of
\mathcal{W}^{(k)}
at
\delta_{\Box}^{(k)}
-distance 0. We conclude that
I_{\mathbold{p}}^{(k)}
gives a well-defined function on
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}^{(k)}
.

Each composition
I_{p}\circ\Gamma_{i,j}

is lsc as a function
(\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}^{(k)},\delta_{\Box}^{(k)})\to[0,\infty]
because, for every

, the level set
\{I_{p}\circ\Gamma_{ij}\leqslant r\}
is closed as the pre-image under the continuous function
\Gamma_{i,j}:\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}^{(k)}\to\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
of the closed

\{I_{p}\leqslant r\}
.
(Recall that the function
I_{p}:\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}\to[0,\infty]
is lsc by Theorem

.)
Thus
I^{(k)}_{\mathbold{p}}:\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}^{(k)}\to[0,\infty]
is lsc by (

), as a finite sum of lsc functions.
∎

Now we are ready to show that the functions
J_{\mathbold{\alpha},\mathbold{p}}
and

are lsc (thus proving Theorem
1.6
and the first part of Theorem

). The argument showing the lower semi-continuity of these functions is motivated by the proof of the Contraction Principle.

Theorem 8.4
.

For every symmetric matrix
\mathbold{p}\in[0,1]^{k\times k}
and every non-zero real vector
\mathbold{\alpha}\in[0,\infty)^{k}
, the functions
J_{\mathbold{\alpha},\mathbold{p}}
and
R_{\mathbold{p}}
are lower semi-continuous on
(\widetilde{\mathcal{W}},\delta_{\Box})
.

Proof.

Note that
J_{\mathbold{\alpha},\mathbold{p}}(\widetilde{U})
for any

is equal to the infimum of

over all

\Gamma(V,{\mathcal{A}})=V
belongs to
\widetilde{U}
. Indeed, for any partition
{\mathcal{A}}\in{\bf A}^{(\mathbold{\alpha})}
of
[0,1]
, one can find
a measure-preserving Borel bijection
\phi
of

such that
{\mathcal{A}}^{\phi}
is equal a.e. to
(I_{1}^{(\mathbold{\alpha})},\dots,I_{k}^{(\mathbold{\alpha})})
.

In the rest of the proof, let us view
\Gamma
and
I^{(k)}_{\mathbold{p}}
as functions on
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}^{(k)}
(by Lemmas
8.2
and
8.3
). Thus we have

J_{\mathbold{\alpha},\mathbold{p}}(\widetilde{U})=\inf_{\Gamma^{-1}(\widetilde{U})\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}^{(\mathbold{\alpha})}}\,I_{\mathbold{p}}^{(k)},\quad\mbox{for each $U\in{\mathcal{W}}$},

(29)

where
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}^{(\mathbold{\alpha})}\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}^{(k)}
denotes the set of all

Take any graphon

is a closed subset of
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}^{(k)}
by the continuity of
\Gamma
(Lemma

). Also,
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}^{(\mathbold{\alpha})}

is a closed subset of

is compact by Theorem

I^{(k)}_{\mathbold{p}}:\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}^{(k)}\to[0,\infty]

,
there is

.

under

Proof of Theorem
1.7

Before we can prove Theorem
1.7
, we need to give two auxiliary lemmas. The first one states, informally speaking, that the measure
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbold{a},\mathbold{p}}
is “uniformly continuous” in
\mathbold{a}
.

Lemma 9.1
.

For every symmetric matrix
\mathbold{p}\in[0,1]^{k\times k}
, real
\epsilon\in(0,1)
and non-zero integer vectors
\mathbold{a},\mathbold{b}\in{\mathbbm{N}}^{k}_{\geqslant 0}
, if

\|\mathbold{b}-\mathbold{a}\|_{1}\leqslant\epsilon\,\min\left\{\,\|\mathbold{a}\|_{1},\|\mathbold{b}\|_{1}\,\right\}

then there is a (discrete) measure
\widetilde{{\mathbbm{C}}}
on
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}\times\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
which gives a coupling between
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbold{a},\mathbold{p}}
and
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbold{b},\mathbold{p}}
such that for every
(\widetilde{U},\widetilde{V})
in the support of
\widetilde{{\mathbbm{C}}}
we have
\delta_{\Box}(U,V)\leqslant 4\epsilon/(1-\epsilon)
.

Proof.

Let
m:=\|\mathbold{a}\|_{1}
and
n:=\|\mathbold{b}\|_{1}
.
Let
[m]=A_{1}\cup\dots\cup A_{k}
(resp.
[n]=B_{1}\cup\dots\cup B_{k}
) be the partition into consecutive intervals with
a_{1},\dots,a_{k}
(resp.
b_{1},\dots,b_{k}
) elements. For each
i\in[k]
fix some subsets
A_{i}^{\prime}\subseteq A_{i}
and
B_{i}^{\prime}\subseteq B_{i}
of size
\min(a_{i},b_{i})
. Define
A^{\prime}:=\cup_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}^{\prime}
and

B^{\prime}:=\cup_{i=1}^{k}B_{i}^{\prime}
. Fix any bijection
h:A^{\prime}\to B^{\prime}
that sends each
A_{i}^{\prime}
to
B_{i}^{\prime}
. We have

\left|\,[m]\setminus A^{\prime}\,\right|\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{k}\max(0,a_{i}-b_{i})\leqslant\|\mathbold{a}-\mathbold{b}\|_{1}\leqslant\epsilon m

(30)

and similarly
\left|\,[n]\setminus B^{\prime}\,\right|\leqslant\epsilon n
.

We can couple random graphs
G\sim{\mathbbm{G}}(\mathbold{a},\mathbold{p})
and
H\sim{\mathbbm{G}}(\mathbold{b},\mathbold{p})
so that every pair
\{x,y\}
in
A^{\prime}
is an edge in
G
if and only if
\{h(x),h(y)\}
is an edge in
H
.
This is possible because, with
i,j\in[k]
satisfying
(x,y)\in A_{i}^{\prime}\times A_{j}^{\prime}
, the probability of
\{x,y\}\in E(G)
is
p_{i,j}
, the same as the probability with which
h(x)\in B_{i}
and
h(y)\in B_{j}
are made adjacent in
H
(so we can just use the same coin flip for both pairs). By making all edge choices to be mutually independent otherwise, we get a probability measure
{\mathbbm{C}}
on pairs of graphs which is a coupling between
{\mathbbm{G}}(\mathbold{a},\mathbold{p})
and
{\mathbbm{G}}(\mathbold{b},\mathbold{p})
.
The corresponding measure
\widetilde{{\mathbbm{C}}}
on
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}\times\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
gives a coupling between
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbold{a},\mathbold{p}}
and
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbold{b},\mathbold{p}}
.

Let us show that this coupling
\widetilde{{\mathbbm{C}}}
satisfies the distance requirement of the lemma. Take any pair of graphs
(G,H)
in the support of
{\mathbbm{C}}
. Let
G^{\prime}:=G[A^{\prime}]
be obtained from
G
by removing all vertices in
[m]\setminus A^{\prime}
. We remove at most
\epsilon
fraction of vertices by (
30
). By relabelling the vertices of
G
, we can assume that all removed vertices come at the very end. Then the union of the intervals in the graphon
f^{G}
corresponding to the vertices of
G^{\prime}
is an initial segment of
[0,1]
of length
s\geqslant 1-\epsilon
and the graphon of
G^{\prime}
is the pull-back of
f^{G}
under the map
x\mapsto sx
.
By Lemma
2.4
, we have
\delta_{\Box}(f^{G},f^{G^{\prime}})\leqslant 2(\frac{1}{1-\epsilon}-1)=\frac{2\epsilon}{1-\epsilon}
. By symmetry, the same estimate applies to the pair
(H,H^{\prime})
, where
H^{\prime}
is obtained from
H
by deleting all vertices from
[n]\setminus B^{\prime}
. By the definition of our coupling, the function
h:V(G^{\prime})\to V(H^{\prime})
is (deterministically) an isomorphism between
G^{\prime}
and
H^{\prime}
. Thus the graphons of
G^{\prime}
and
H^{\prime}
are weakly isomorphic.
This gives by the Triangle Inequality the required upper bound on the cut-distance between
\widetilde{f^{G}}
and
\widetilde{f^{H}}
.
∎

The function
J_{\mathbold{\alpha},\mathbold{p}}(\widetilde{V})
is not in general continuous in
\mathbold{\alpha}
even when
\mathbold{p}
and
\widetilde{V}
are fixed. (For example, if
k=2
,
V=1-f^{K_{2}}
is the limit of
K_{n}\sqcup K_{n}
, the disjoint union of two cliques of order
n
each, and
\mathbold{p}
is the identity
2\times 2
matrix then
J_{\mathbold{\alpha},\mathbold{p}}(\widetilde{V})
is
0
for
\mathbold{\alpha}=(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
and
\infty
otherwise.) However, the following version of “uniform semi-continuity” in
\mathbold{\alpha}
will suffice for our purposes.

Lemma 9.2
.

Fix any symmetric
\mathbold{p}\in[0,1]^{k\times k}
.
Then for every
\eta>0
there is
\epsilon=\epsilon(\eta,\mathbold{p})>0
such that if vectors
{\mathbold{\gamma}},\mathbold{\kappa}\in[0,\infty)^{k}
with
\|\mathbold{\gamma}\|_{1}=\|\mathbold{\kappa}\|_{1}=1
satisfy

\kappa_{i}\leqslant(1+\epsilon)\gamma_{i},\quad\mbox{for every $i\in[k]$},

(31)

then
for every
U\in{\mathcal{W}}
there is
{V}\in{{\mathcal{W}}}
with
\delta_{\Box}(U,{V})\leqslant\eta
and

J_{\mathbold{\kappa},\mathbold{p}}(\widetilde{V})\leqslant J_{{\mathbold{\gamma}},\mathbold{p}}(\widetilde{U})+\eta.

Proof.

For every fixed
p\in(0,1)
, the relative entropy function
h_{p}:[0,1]\to[0,\infty]
is bounded (in fact, by
\max\{h_{p}(0),h_{p}(1)\}<\infty
). Thus we can find a constant
C\in(0,\infty)
that depends on
\mathbold{p}
only such that for every
x\in[0,1]
and
i,j\in[k]
,
h_{p_{i,j}}(x)
is either
\infty
or at most
C
.

Let us show that any positive
\epsilon\leqslant\min\{\,\frac{\eta}{3(2C+\eta)},\,\frac{\eta}{2}\,\}
works. Let
{\mathbold{\gamma}}
,
\mathbold{\kappa}
, and
U
be as in the lemma. Assume that
J_{{\mathbold{\gamma}},\mathbold{p}}(\widetilde{U})<\infty
for otherwise we can trivially take
{V}:=U
. By the choice of
C
, we have that
J_{{\mathbold{\gamma}},\mathbold{p}}(\widetilde{U})\leqslant C
.

By replacing
U\in{\mathcal{W}}
with a weakly isomorphic graphon, assume that, for the partition
(I_{i}^{({\mathbold{\gamma}})})_{i\in[k]}
of
[0,1]
into intervals,
we have

\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j\in[k]}\int_{I_{i}^{({\mathbold{\gamma}})}\times I_{j}^{({\mathbold{\gamma}})}}I_{p_{i,j}}(U)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda^{\oplus 2}<J_{{\mathbold{\gamma}},\mathbold{p}}(\widetilde{U})+\frac{\eta}{2}.

Let
\phi:[0,1]\to[0,1]
be the a.e. defined function which on each interval
I_{i}^{({\mathbold{\kappa}})}
of positive length is the increasing linear function that bijectively maps this interval onto
I_{i}^{({\mathbold{\gamma}})}
.
The
Radon-Nikodym derivative
D:=\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mu}{\,\mathrm{d}\lambda}
of the push-forward
\mu:=\phi_{*}\lambda
of the Lebesgue measure
\lambda
along
\phi
assumes value

\kappa_{i}/\gamma_{i}
a.e. on
I_{i}^{({\mathbold{\gamma}})}
. (The union of
I_{i}^{(\mathbold{\gamma})}
with
\gamma_{i}=0

is a countable and thus null set,
and can be ignored from the point of view of
D
.)
Let
V:=U^{\phi}
. It is a graphon by the first part of Lemma
2.3
.
We have by the definition of
J_{\mathbold{\kappa},\mathbold{p}}
that

J_{\mathbold{\kappa},\mathbold{p}}(\widetilde{V})\leqslant\ \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j\in[k]}\int_{I_{i}^{({\mathbold{\kappa}})}\times I_{j}^{({\mathbold{\kappa}})}}I_{p_{i,j}}(U^{\phi})\,\mathrm{d}\lambda^{\oplus 2}=\ \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j\in[k]}\frac{\kappa_{i}\kappa_{j}}{\gamma_{i}\gamma_{j}}\int_{I_{i}^{({\mathbold{\gamma}})}\times I_{j}^{({\mathbold{\gamma}})}}I_{p_{i,j}}(U)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda^{\oplus 2}.

By
\frac{\kappa_{i}\kappa_{j}}{\gamma_{i}\gamma_{j}}\leqslant(1+\epsilon)^{2}\leqslant 1+3\epsilon
, this is at most

(1+3\epsilon)\,\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j\in[k]}\int_{I_{i}^{({\mathbold{\gamma}})}\times I_{j}^{({\mathbold{\gamma}})}}I_{p_{i,j}}(U)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda^{\oplus 2}\leqslant(1+3\epsilon)\left(J_{{\mathbold{\gamma}},\mathbold{p}}(\widetilde{U})+\frac{\eta}{2}\right).

By
J_{{\mathbold{\gamma}},\mathbold{p}}(\widetilde{U})\leqslant C
and our choice of
\epsilon
, this in turn is at most

J_{{\mathbold{\gamma}},\mathbold{p}}(\widetilde{U})+3\epsilon C+(1+3\epsilon)\frac{\eta}{2}\leqslant\ J_{{\mathbold{\gamma}},\mathbold{p}}(\widetilde{U})+\eta.

Thus it remains to estimate the cut-distance between
U
and
{V}=U^{\phi}
. Since the Radon-Nikodym derivative
D=\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\phi_{*}\lambda}{\,\mathrm{d}\lambda}
satisfies
D(x)\leqslant 1+\epsilon
for a.e.
x\in[0,1]
by (
31
), we have by Lemma
2.3
that
\delta_{\Box}(U,{V})\leqslant 2\epsilon\leqslant\eta
. Thus
V
is as desired.∎

Lemma 9.3
.

Let
(X,d)
be a compact metric space,

, and

\displaystyle-I(x),\quad\mbox{for every $x\in X$,}

\displaystyle\lim_{\eta\to 0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{s(n)}\,{\log\Big(\mu_{n}\big(\overline{B}_{d}(x,\eta)\big)\Big)}

\displaystyle-I(x),\quad\mbox{for every $x\in X$}.

(33)

) and (

\mathbold{a}_{n}/\|\mathbold{a}_{n}\|_{1}
converges to
\mathbold{\alpha}/\|\mathbold{\alpha}\|_{1}
as
n\to\infty
and we chose

to be sufficiently large,
we can assume that

if and only if
\alpha_{i}=0
and that
Lemma

applies for
\mathbold{\gamma}:=\mathbold{b}/\|\mathbold{b}\|_{1}
and
\mathbold{\kappa}:=\mathbold{\alpha}/\|\mathbold{\alpha}\|_{1}
(and our
\eta
).
The lemma gives that
there is
\widetilde{W}\in{\overline{B}}_{\delta_{\Box}}(\widetilde{V},\eta)
such that
J_{\mathbold{\alpha},\mathbold{p}}(\widetilde{W})-\eta\leqslant J_{\mathbold{b},\mathbold{p}}(\widetilde{V})
. Thus we get the following upper bound on the right-hand side of (

-\inf_{{\overline{B}}_{\delta_{\Box}}(\widetilde{U},\eta)}J_{\mathbold{b},\mathbold{p}}\leqslant-J_{\mathbold{b},\mathbold{p}}(\widetilde{V})+\eta\leqslant-J_{\mathbold{\alpha},\mathbold{p}}(\widetilde{W})+2\eta\leqslant-\inf_{{\overline{B}}_{\delta_{\Box}}(\widetilde{U},2\eta)}J_{\mathbold{\alpha},\mathbold{p}}+2\eta.

(39)

By putting (

), (

) and (

) together we get that, for every
\eta\in(0,1/9)
,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1+\eta}{(\|\mathbold{a}_{n}\|_{1})^{2}}\log\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbold{a}_{n},\mathbold{p}}({\overline{B}}_{\delta_{\Box}}(\widetilde{U},\eta/2))\leqslant-\inf_{{\overline{B}}_{\delta_{\Box}}(\widetilde{U},2\eta)}J_{\mathbold{\alpha},\mathbold{p}}+2\eta.

If we take here the limit as

then the infimum in the right-hand side converges to

by the lower semi-continuity of

(established in Theorem

Let us turn to the lower bound, i.e. we prove (

) for
\widetilde{U}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
. As before, take any sufficiently small
\eta>0
, then sufficiently large

and define
\mathbold{b}
by (

and

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{(n\,\|\mathbold{b}\|_{1})^{2}}\log\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{n\mathbold{b},\mathbold{p}}(B_{\delta_{\Box}}(\widetilde{U},2\eta))\geqslant-\inf_{{\overline{B}}_{\delta_{\Box}}(\widetilde{U},\eta)}J_{\mathbold{b},\mathbold{p}}.

(40)

Similarly as for the upper bound, the left-hand side can be upper bounded via Lemma

by, for example,

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1-\eta}{(\|\mathbold{a}_{n}\|_{1})^{2}}\log\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbold{a}_{n},\mathbold{p}}({\overline{B}}_{\delta_{\Box}}(\widetilde{U},3\eta)).

(41)

Since

is sufficiently large,
we can apply Lemma

\mathbold{\kappa}:={\mathbold b}/\|\mathbold{b}\|_{1}

\mathbold{\gamma}:=\mathbold{\alpha}/\|\mathbold{\alpha}\|_{1}

Proof of Theorem
1.4

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Recall that
W
is a
k
-step graphon with non-null parts whose values are encoded by a symmetric
k\times k
matrix
\mathbold{p}\in[0,1]^{k\times k}
. We consider the
W
-random graph
{\mathbbm{G}}(n,W)
where we first sample
n
independent uniform points
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in[0,1]
and then make each pair
\{i,j\}\subseteq[n]
an edge with probability
W(x_{i},x_{j})
. We have to prove an LDP for the corresponding sequence
(\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}_{n,W})_{n\in{\mathbbm{N}}}
of measures on the metric space
(\widetilde{\mathcal{W}},\delta_{\Box})
with speed
s(n)=(c+o(1))n^{2}
. Of course, it is enough to do the case
s(n)=n^{2}
as this affects only the scaling factors. Then the claimed rate function is
R_{\mathbold{p}}
, which was defined in (
10
). Recall that the lower semi-continuity of
R_{\mathbold{p}}
was established in Theorem
8.4
.

Let us show the LDP lower bound. Since the underlying space
(\widetilde{\mathcal{W}},\delta_{\Box})
is compact, it is enough to prove the bound in (
32
) of Lemma
9.3
for any given
\widetilde{U}\in\widetilde{{\mathcal{W}}}
, that is, that

\lim_{\eta\to 0}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\log\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}_{n,W}({\overline{B}}_{\delta_{\Box}}(\widetilde{U},\eta))\geqslant-R_{\mathbold{p}}(\widetilde{U}).

(42)

Take any
\epsilon>0
. By the definition of
R_{\mathbold{p}}
, we can fix a vector
\mathbold{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k})\in[0,1]^{k}

such that
\|\mathbold{\alpha}\|_{1}=1
and

R_{\mathbold{p}}(\widetilde{U})\geqslant J_{\mathbold{\alpha},\mathbold{p}}(\widetilde{U})-\epsilon.

Let
m
be the number of non-zero entries of
\mathbold{\alpha}
. As
\mathbold{\alpha}
is non-zero, we have
m\geqslant 1
. We can assume by symmetry that
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m}
are the non-zero entries. Define
\xi:=\frac{1}{2}\min_{i\in[m]}\alpha_{i}>0
.

For each
n\in{\mathbbm{N}}
, take any integer vector
\mathbold{a}_{n}=(a_{n,1},\dots,a_{n,k})\in{\mathbbm{N}}_{\geqslant 0}^{k}
such that
\|\mathbold{a}_{n}\|_{1}=n
and
\|\mathbold{a}_{n}-n\,\mathbold{\alpha}\|_{\infty}<1
(in particular, we have
a_{n,i}=0
if
\alpha_{i}=0
).

Let
n
be sufficiently large. In particular, for every
i\in[m]
we have that
a_{n,i}/n\geqslant\xi
.
When we generate
G\sim{\mathbbm{G}}(n,W)
by choosing first random
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in[0,1]
,
it holds with probability at least, very roughly,
\xi^{-n}
that for each
i\in[k]
the number of
x_{j}
’s that belong to the
i
-th part of the step graphon
W
is exactly
a_{n,i}
. Conditioned on this event of positive measure, the resulting graphon

\widetilde{f^{G}}
is distributed according to
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbold{a}_{n},\mathbold{p}}
. Thus

\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}_{n,W}(S)\geqslant\xi^{-n}\,\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbold{a}_{n},\mathbold{p}}(S),\quad\mbox{for every $S\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}$}.

This and Theorem
1.7
give that, for every
\eta>0
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\log\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}_{n,W}({\overline{B}}_{\delta_{\Box}}(\widetilde{U},\eta))

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\log\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbold{a}_{n},\mathbold{p}}({\overline{B}}_{\delta_{\Box}}(\widetilde{U},\eta))

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle-\inf_{{\overline{B}}_{\delta_{\Box}}(\widetilde{U},\eta/2)}J_{\mathbold{\alpha},\mathbold{p}}\ \geqslant\ -J_{\mathbold{\alpha},\mathbold{p}}(\widetilde{U})\ \geqslant\ -R_{\mathbold{p}}(\widetilde{U})-\epsilon.

Taking the limit as
\eta\to 0
, we conclude that the LDP lower bound (
42
) holds within additive error
\epsilon
. As
\epsilon>0
was arbitrary, the lower bound
holds.

Let us show the upper bound (
2
) of Definition
1.1
for any closed set
F\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
, that is, that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\log\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}_{n,W}(F)\leqslant-\inf_{F}R_{\mathbold{p}}.

(43)

For each
n\in{\mathbbm{N}}
, we can write
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}_{n,W}(F)
as the sum over all
\mathbold{a}\in{\mathbbm{N}}_{\geqslant 0}^{k}
with
\|\mathbold{a}\|_{1}=n
of the probability that the distribution of random independent
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in[0,1]
per
k
steps of
W
is given by the vector
\mathbold{a}
times the probability conditioned on
\mathbold{a}
to hit the set
F
. This conditional probability is exactly
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbold{a},\mathbold{p}}(F)
. Thus
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}_{n,W}(F)
is a convex combination of the reals
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbold{a},\mathbold{p}}(F)
and there is a vector
\mathbold{a}_{n}\in{\mathbbm{N}}_{\geqslant 0}^{k}
with
\|\mathbold{a}_{n}\|_{1}=n
such that

\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}_{n,W}(F)\leqslant\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbold{a}_{n},\mathbold{p}}(F).

(44)

Fix one such vector
\mathbold{a}_{n}
for each
n\in{\mathbbm{N}}
.

Since the set of all real vectors
\mathbold{\alpha}\in[0,1]^{k}
with
\|\mathbold{\alpha}\|_{1}=1
is compact, we can find an increasing sequence
(n_{i})_{i\in{\mathbbm{N}}}
of integers such that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\log\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}_{n,W}(F)=\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{1}{n_{i}^{2}}\log\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}_{n_{i},W}(F),

(45)

and the scaled vectors
\frac{1}{n_{i}}\,\mathbold{a}_{n_{i}}
converge to some real vector
\mathbold{\alpha}\in[0,1]^{k}
.

Let
(\mathbold{b}_{n})_{n\in{\mathbbm{N}}}
be obtained by filling the gaps in
(\mathbold{a}_{n_{i}})_{n\in{\mathbbm{N}}}
, meaning that if
n=n_{i}
for some
i\in{\mathbbm{N}}
then we let
\mathbold{b}_{n}:=\mathbold{a}_{n_{i}}
; otherwise we pick any
\mathbold{b}_{n}\in{\mathbbm{N}}_{\geqslant 0}^{k}
that satisfies
\|\mathbold{b}_{n}\|_{1}=n
and
\|\mathbold{b}_{n}-n\,\mathbold{\alpha}\|_{\infty}<1
. Since the normalised vectors
\mathbold{b}_{n}/\|\mathbold{b}_{n}\|_{1}
converge to the same limiting vector
\mathbold{\alpha}
, we have by Theorem
1.7
that

\limsup_{i\to\infty}\frac{1}{n_{i}^{2}}\log\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbold{a}_{n_{i}},\mathbold{p}}(F)\leqslant\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\log\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbold{b}_{n},\mathbold{p}}(F)\leqslant-\inf_{F}J_{\mathbold{\alpha},\mathbold{p}}.

(46)

Putting (
44
), (
45
) and (
46
) together with the trivial consequence
J_{\mathbold{\alpha},\mathbold{p}}\geqslant R_{\mathbold{p}}
of the definition of
R_{\mathbold{p}}
, we get the desired upper bound (
43
). This finishes the proof of Theorem
1.4
.∎

11
Concluding remarks

The existence of an LDP for
(\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}_{n,W})_{n\in{\mathbbm{N}}}
in the case case of a general graphon
W
and speed
\Theta(n^{2})
is the key remaining open problem.
We remark that it is not hard to show that any potential rate function for speed
s(n)=\Theta(n^{2})
has to be zero on
\mathbf{RN}(W)
. On the other hand, there are graphons
W
such that
\mathbf{RN}(W)=\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
: for example, take a countable set of graphons
\{W_{i}:i\in{\mathbbm{N}}\}
dense in
(\widetilde{\mathcal{W}},\delta_{\Box})
and let
W
be zero except it has a scaled copy of
W_{i}
on each square
[2^{-i},2^{-i+1}]^{2}
. It is easy to see that the LDP for
(\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}_{n,W})_{n\in{\mathbbm{N}}}
and speed
n^{2}
must have the rate function identically 0. It is plausible that by taking different sets of the graphons
W_{i}
in the above example, one may get rate functions with rather complicated zero sets.

Our results fully resolve the LDP problem for
(\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}_{n,W})_{n\in{\mathbbm{N}}}
for every graphon
W
with
\mathbf{RN}(W)=\mathbf{FORB}(W)
,
with the statements precisely mirrowing those of Theorem
4.1
. Indeed, all claimed results on the existence of an LDP follow from Theorem
1.3
: for example, if
s(n)=\omega(n)
then the rate function is
\chi_{\mathbf{RN}(W)}=\chi_{\mathbf{FORB}(W)}
with the LDP lower (resp. upper) bound following from Part
3
(resp. Part
4
) of Theorem
1.3
. Also, the proof of Part
5
of Theorem
4.1
(the non-existence of an LDP) relies only on the already established LDPs and the fact that the corresponding rate functions are distinct from each other, and thus the same proof works in this case as well.

However, it is unclear for which graphons
W
it holds that
\mathbf{RN}(W)=\mathbf{FORB}(W)
. By the uniqueness of the rate function, Theorems
1.3
and
4.1
imply that
\mathbf{RN}(W)=\mathbf{FORB}(W)
for any
\{0,1\}
-valued graphon. On the other hand, the constant-
p
graphon
W
for
p\in(0,1)
is extreme in the sense that
\mathbf{RN}(W)
is the singleton
\{\widetilde{W}\}
while
\mathbf{FORB}(W)=\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
is the whole space. The earlier example of a graphon
W
with countably many “blocks” such that
\mathbf{RN}(W)=\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
(when
\mathbf{FORB}(W)\supseteq\mathbf{RN}(W)
is also the whole of
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
) shows that we can have equality for graphons assuming values in
(0,1)
on a set of positive measure. We do not see any good characterisation of
W
with
\mathbf{RN}(W)=\mathbf{FORB}(W)
.

We did not include any non-existence statements in Theorem
1.3
, since we are rather far from understanding the full picture. Of course, if
s(n)=o(n^{2})
then, by the exponential equivalence, the non-existence statements of Theorem
4.1
translate verbatim into those for
(\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}_{n,W})_{n\in{\mathbbm{N}}}
. Also, further non-existence results can be obtained in those cases when there are two subsequences of
n
for which we have LDPs (e.g. by Theorems
1.3
) with different rate functions. However, the lack of general positive results for
s(n)=\Theta(n^{2})
prevents us from completing the picture.

Appendix A
Functional analysis

In this appendix we collect some results
that we need in this paper.

Let
\mu
be a probability measure on a measurable space
X
. Recall that a collection
\mathcal{F}\subseteq L^{1}(X,\mu)
is
uniformly integrable
if

\lim_{C\to\infty}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\int_{\{|f|>C\}}|f|\,\,\mathrm{d}\mu=0.

(Since
\mu(X)<\infty
, some other equivalent definitions are possible, see e.g.,
[
4
, Proposition 4.5.3]
.)
We use the following results whose (more general) statements can be found in e.g.
[
4
]
(as
Theorems 4.7.18, 4.5.9 and 4.7.10 respectively).

Theorem A.1

(Dunford–Pettis)
.

A collection
\mathcal{F}\subseteq L^{1}(X,\mu)
is uniformly integrable if and only if its
weak
closure is compact in the
weak
topology.

Theorem A.2

(De la Vallée-Poussin)
.

A collection
\mathcal{F}\subseteq L^{1}(X,\mu)
is uniformly integrable if and only if there is non-negative non-decreasing convex function
G:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{G(t)}{t}=\infty
and

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\int_{X}G(|f|)\ d\mu<\infty.

Theorem A.3

(Eberlein–Šmulian)
.

Let
A\subseteq L^{1}(X,\mu)
be closed in the
weak
topology.
Then
A
is compact in the
weak
topology if and only if every sequence contains a subsequence that converges in the
weak
topology.

The following result is an easy consequence of the Hahn-Banach Theorem, for a proof see e.g.
[
23
, Page 6]

Theorem A.4

(Mazur’s lemma)
.

A convex subset of a normed space is weakly closed if and only if it is closed.

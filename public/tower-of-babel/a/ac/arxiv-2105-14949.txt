Abstract
. By following the paradigm of the global quantisation, instead of the analysis under changes of coordinates, in this work we establish a global analysis for the explicit computation of the Dixmier trace and the Wodzicki residue of (elliptic and subelliptic) pseudo-differential operators on compact Lie groups. The regularised determinant for the Dixmier trace is also computed. We obtain these formulae in terms of the global symbol of the corresponding operators. In particular, our approach links the Dixmier trace and Wodzicki residue to the representation theory of the group. Although we start by analysing the case of compact Lie groups, we also compute the Dixmier trace and its regularised determinant on arbitrary closed manifolds M M , for the class of invariant pseudo-differential operators in terms of their matrix-valued symbols. This analysis includes e.g. the family of positive and elliptic pseudo-differential operators on M M .

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Outline

In this work we compute the Wodzicki-Guillemin residue and the Dixmier trace of pseudo-differential operators on compact Lie groups in terms of their global symbols, or equivalently, in terms of the representation theory of these groups. We prove our geometric/spectral formulae in the context of pseudo-differential operators associated to Riemannian and sub-Riemannian structures on compact Lie groups
[
RT10
,
CR20
]
. The validity of such results can be conjectured from the techniques developed in
[
LNP16
,
PS07
]
where the Wodzicki residue was studied by using a twisted version of the global-symbolic calculus on the torus developed by the third author with Turunen
[
RT10
]
. See also
[
CDC20
]
. Indeed, the theory in
[
RT10
]
, can be understood as a discretisation of the algebraic formalism by Connes and Moscovici
[
CM95
]
in the setting of Lie groups, making that theory suitable for the global analysis of residues, determinants and traces.

As it was pointed out by Connes (cf.
[
Con94
]
), in algebraic quantum field theory, in order to write down an action in operator language, a functional that replaces integration is required. For the Yang-Mills theory, that is the Dixmier trace
\textnormal{\bf Tr}_{\omega}
, which in contrast to the usual trace, vanishes on the ideal of trace class operators, and is defined in the ideal
\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}
of the compact operators
T
such that the partial sums of their singular values diverge logarithmically. On the other hand, the Wodzicki (or non-commutative) residue, is the only extension of the Dixmier trace on the algebra of classical pseudo-differential operators, in view of a fundamental result due to Connes
[
Con94
]
. These two traces, are of relevant interests in several branches of mathematics such as differential geometry, spectral theory, mathematical physics, and non-commutative geometry (c.f.
[
Con94
]
). A reason for this is that they allow to study specific topological/geometric invariants, where very specific elliptic operators –such as the Dirac operator– have been shown to contain very important information relevant for classification problems, see Lesch
[
Les99
]
, Paycha
[
Pay12
]
, Sukochev and Usachev
[
SU16
]
, Scott
[
Sco10
]
, Grubb and Schrohe
[
GS04
]
, Fedosov, Golse, Leichtnam, and Schrohe
[
FGLS96
]
and references therein, just to mention a few.

According to the theory developed by Hörmander
[
Hör85
]
, to any pseudo-differential operator
A
acting on
C^{\infty}(M),
with
M
a closed manifold
M
(i.e. compact and without boundary) one can associate a symbol, defined by local coordinate systems, which is a function on the cotangent bundle
T^{*}M.
However, some information about the operator (such as its lower terms) can be lost from the geometric point of view. On the other hand, it was described by the third author and Turunen in
[
RT10
]
, that if
M=G
is a compact Lie group, one can give a global definition of symbol for the Hörmander classes, and that these symbols can be realised as matrix-valued functions of the phase space
G\times\widehat{G},
where
\widehat{G}
is the unitary dual of
G
, that is the set of equivalence classes of continuous irreducible unitary representations on the group.

In this work we address the following problems:
(1) to compute the Dixmier trace and the regularised determinant associated with it, for pseudo-differential operators when
M=G
is a compact Lie group. The reason of considering the case of a compact Lie group is to exploit the symmetries of the group as in
[
RT10
]
. Also, by following
[
CR20
]
, we also consider the problem of computing the Dixmier trace of subelliptic pseudo-differential operators.
(2) To compute the Wodzicki residue of (elliptic and subelliptic) pseudo-differential operators on a compact Lie group
G
in terms of their matrix-valued symbols or equivalently, in terms of the representation theory of the group, and (3) to compute the regularised determinant for the Dixmier trace in the case of invariant pseudo-differential operators on a compact manifold
M
in terms of their matrix-valued symbols, by following the formalism developed in
[
DR18
]
, where the global symbols are defined in terms of a global Fourier analysis on
M
and covers the case of elliptic and positive pseudo-differential operators on
M.

1.2.
Main results

For simplicity, we will discuss briefly our main results in the setting of positive (elliptic or subelliptic) pseudo-differential operators. However, we present the general situation without positivity assumptions in Section
3
.

By following Hörmander
[
Hör85
]
, a classical pseudo-differential operator
A
on a closed manifold
M
of dimension
n,
has a symbol

\sigma^{A}(x,\xi)\sim\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sigma^{A}_{m-j}(x,\xi),\,\,(x,\xi)\in T^{*}M,

defined by localisations, and
the Wodzicki residue, which measures the locality of the operator, is given by

\textnormal{res}\,(A)=\frac{1}{n(2\pi)^{n}}\int\limits_{M}\int\limits_{|\xi|=1}\sigma^{A}_{-n}(x,\xi)\,d\xi\,dx.

(1.1)

By a fundamental theorem of Connes
[
Con94
]
, if
A
has order
-n,
then
\textnormal{res}\,(A)
coincides with the Dixmier trace of
A
, that is:

\boxed{{\textnormal{ Spectral (global) information }=\textnormal{\bf Tr}_{\omega}(A)=\textnormal{res}\,(A)=\textnormal{ Geometric (local) information}}}

Now, if
M
has symmetries, for instance if
M
is the torus,
M=\textnormal{SU}(2),
or
M=G
is an arbitrary compact Lie group, the work of the third author and Turunen
[
RT10
]
shown that for describing the Hörmander classes in
[
Hör85
]
, is enough to consider the representation theory of
G.
Then, a natural question is if the Wodzicki residue can be computed from this algebraic formalism, or equivalently:

\boxed{{\textnormal{is }\textnormal{res}\,(A)\,\,\textnormal{ information encoded by the representation theory of }G\textnormal{?}}}

(1.2)

In this work we answer (
1.2
) in the affirmative, by proving the validity of the formula

\textnormal{res}(A)=\int\limits_{G}\|\sigma_{-n}(x,[\xi])\|_{\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(\widehat{G})}dx,

(1.3)

for a positive pseudo-differential operator
A
of order
-n,
where the full symbol
\sigma_{-n}(x,[\xi]),

(x,[\xi])\in G\times\widehat{G},
is the component of order
-n,
of the matrix-valued symbol
\sigma_{A}(x,[\xi])
of
A,
which is a global mapping on the non-commutative phase space
G\times\widehat{G}
. Here, the
\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}
-norm of the symbol is given by

\|\sigma_{-n}(x,[\xi])\|_{\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(\widehat{G})}:=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log N}\sum_{[\xi]:\langle\xi\rangle\leq N}d_{\xi}\textnormal{{Tr}}(|\sigma_{-n}(x,[\xi])|),

where the sequence
\langle\xi\rangle,

[\xi]\in\widehat{G},
is formed by the eigenvalues of the operator
(1+\mathcal{L}_{G})^{\frac{1}{2}},
with
\mathcal{L}_{G}=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{j}^{2}
being the positive Laplacian on
G,
see Remark
2.2
for details.
Moreover, we compute the Wodzicki residue for classical pseudo-differential operators in Corollary
3.11
without the assumption of the positivity of the operator.

The residue formula will be obtained for classical operators as a consequence of a more general formula (see Corollary
3.5
) that we prove in the setting of subelliptic operators on compact Lie groups
[
CR20
]
. Indeed, in Corollary
3.6
, for a positive subelliptic operator
A,
we prove the formula

\textnormal{\bf{Tr}}_{\omega}(A)=\int\limits_{G}\|\sigma_{-Q}(x,[\xi])\|_{\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(\widehat{G})}dx=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log(N)}\sum_{j=1}^{N}s_{j}(A),

(1.4)

where
s_{j}(A)
denotes the sequence of singular values of
A
with multiplicities taken into account. We refer the reader to Theorem
3.9
for the Dixmier trace formula of
A
without the positivity assumption. In (
1.4
),
Q
denotes the Hausdorff dimension associated to a positive sub-Laplacian
\mathcal{L}=-\sum_{j=1}^{k}X_{j}^{2},
associated to a Hörmander system of vector-fields
\{X_{j}\}_{j=1}^{k}
(see Subsection
2.2
for details), and
A
is a positive operator in the Hörmander classes associated to
\mathcal{L},
see Subsection
2.2
or
[
CR20
]
for details. Let us remark that (
1.4
) is a Lidskii type formula, that relates the Dixmier trace with the eigenvalues of the operator. An analogous of the Lidskii formula can be obtained in the setting of Dixmier traces following the recent work of Sukochev, Zanin et al
[
GG17
]
,
[
SSZ15
]
,
[
SZ14b
]
,
[
GS07
]
,
[
SZ14a
]
.

We conclude the introduction by focusing our attention on two aspects of the analysis on Lie groups that are regarded as quite far apart, and that are related with the validity of (
1.3
). One comes from the theories in
[
RT10
,
CR20
]

which show that the analysis of pseudo-differential operators using global symbols can be effectively treated when working on compact Lie groups. Indeed, different techniques regarding the representation theory of a compact Lie group (where the unitary dual is a discrete set) allow to use generalisations of the Fourier analysis on the torus, or on
\textnormal{SU(2)}.
In this framework Lidskii type formulae are used to localise the spectrum of an operator and to establish trace formulae in terms of the matrix-valued symbol of an operator (c.f.
[
Del10a
,
Del10b
,
DR14a
,
DR14b
,
DR17
,
DR18
]
).
Another aspect, from the point of view of the twisted versions of the periodic analysis in
[
RT10
]
, or equivalently, from the twisted structures arising in the non-commutative geometry, e.g. by deformation of the torus, or by deformation of
\textnormal{SU}(2),
shows that one can generalise Wodzicki type results as it was firstly observed in
[
LNP16
]
using the notion of a global symbol (with predecessor works on the subject as in
[
FK13
]
). Extension of
the Dixmier trace in a more general setting of Banach spaces is available from the works of Pietsch
[
Pie12
]
. For a recent survey on the theory of traces and their history we refer the reader to Pietsch
[
Pie14
]
.

The manuscript is organised as follows: In Section
2
we first recall basic notions of pseudodifferential calculus on compact Lie groups, subelliptic classes, determinants on Hilbert spaces and the Dixmier ideal. In Section
3
we study Dixmier traces and regularised determinants. We present there the main results of this work. For instance, we prove the validity of (
1.3
) in its more general form for subelliptic operators in Theorem
3.9
, and consequently for classical elliptic pseudo-differential operators in Corollary
3.10
. In Corollary
3.11
we compute a formula for the Wodzicki residue of elliptic and classical pseudo-differential operators in terms of their global symbols. Finally, in Section
4
we give some final remarks about the regularised determinant for the Dixmier trace on arbitrary compact manifolds.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we recall some basics of the pseudodifferential calculus on compact Lie groups, the subelliptic Hörmander classes recently introduced in
[
CR20
]
, determinants on Hilbert spaces and the Dixmier ideal.

2.1.
Global Hörmander classes of pseudo-differential operators

First, let us record the notion of the unitary dual
\widehat{G}
of a compact Lie group
G.
So,
let us assume that
\xi
is a continuous, unitary and irreducible representation of
G,
that is

•

\xi\in\textnormal{Hom}(G,\textnormal{U}(H_{\xi})),
for some finite-dimensional vector space
H_{\xi}\cong\mathbb{C}^{d_{\xi}},
i.e.
\xi(xy)=\xi(x)\xi(y)
and for the adjoint of
\xi(x),

\xi(x)^{*}=\xi(x^{-1}),
for every
x,y\in G.

•

The map
(x,v)\mapsto\xi(x)v,
from
G\times H_{\xi}
into
H_{\xi}
is continuous.

•

For every
x\in G,
and
W_{\xi}\subset H_{\xi},
if
\xi(x)W_{\xi}\subset W_{\xi},
then
W_{\xi}=H_{\xi}
or
W_{\xi}=\emptyset.

Let
\textnormal{Rep}(G)
be the set of unitary, continuous and irreducible representations of
G.
The relation,

\xi_{1}\sim\xi_{2}\textnormal{ if and only if, there exists }\varkappa\in\textnormal{End}(H_{\xi_{1}},H_{\xi_{2}}),\textnormal{ such that }\varkappa\xi_{1}(x)\varkappa^{-1}=\xi_{2}(x),

for every
x\in G,
is an equivalence relation and the unitary dual of
G,
denoted by
\widehat{G}
is defined via

\widehat{G}:={\textnormal{Rep}(G)}/{\sim}.

The compactness of
G
implies that
\widehat{G}
is a discrete set. By a suitable changes of basis, we always can assume that every
\xi
is matrix-valued and that
H_{\xi}=\mathbb{C}^{d_{\xi}}.
If a representation
\xi
is unitary, then

\xi(G):=\{\xi(x):x\in G\}

is a subgroup (of the group of matrices
\mathbb{C}^{d_{\xi}\times d_{\xi}}
) which is isomorphic to the original group
G
. Thus the homomorphism
\xi
allows us to represent the compact Lie group
G
as a group of matrices. This is the motivation for the term ‘representation’.

The prototype of differential operators of the first order are vector-fields. One can associate first differential operators to vector fields using the exponential mapping as follows. We denote by
\mathfrak{g}
the Lie algebra of
G
.

Remark 2.1
.

Every
X\in\mathfrak{g},
can be identified with the differential operator
X:C^{\infty}(G)\rightarrow C^{\infty}(G)
defined by

(X_{x}f)(x):=\frac{d}{dt}f(x\exp(tX))|_{t=0},\quad x\in G.

The Laplacian on a compact Lie group is the model of a differential operator of second order. Its eigenvalues will be used to define the Hörmander classes on
G
(see Remark
2.7
below). It can be defined as follows.

Remark 2.2

(The Laplacian on
G
)
.

Let
G
be a compact Lie group of dimension
n

and let
\textnormal{ad}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\textnormal{End}(\mathfrak{g})
be the adjoint representation of its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.
With respect to the the Killing form

B(Y_{1},Y_{2}):=\textnormal{Tr}(\textnormal{ad}(Y_{1})\circ\textnormal{ad}(Y_{2})),

on
\mathfrak{g},
let us choose an orthonormal basis
X_{1},\cdots,X_{n},
of
\mathfrak{g}.
The positive Laplacian on
G,
(Casimir element) is defined via

\mathcal{L}_{G}=-\sum_{j=1}^{n}X_{j}^{2}.

(2.1)

The operator
\mathcal{L}_{G}
has
L^{2}(G)
-discrete spectrum
\{\lambda_{[\xi]}\}_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}},
and the eigenvalues of the (Bessel potential) operator
(1+\mathcal{L}_{G})^{\frac{1}{2}}
are given by

\langle\xi\rangle:=(1+\lambda_{[\xi]})^{\frac{1}{2}},

(2.2)

in view of the spectral mapping theorem. The weight function (
2.2
) will be crucial in defining the Hörmander classes of symbols.

Remark 2.3
.

Let
[\xi]\in\widehat{G},
and
\xi\in[\xi].
The entry-functions
\xi_{ij}
of any unitary representation
\xi=(\xi_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{d_{\xi}}
span an eigenspace
\mathcal{H}^{\xi}\subset L^{2}(G)
of the Laplacian and the restriction
\mathcal{L}_{G}|_{\mathcal{H}^{\xi}}=\lambda_{[\xi]}I,
is a multiple of the identity operator on
\mathcal{H}^{\xi},
see Theorem 10.3.13 of
[
RT10
]
. The number
\lambda_{[\xi]}
is independent of the choice of
\xi\in[\xi].
Consequently, for any
\xi,

the entry-functions
\xi_{ij}
are of
C^{\infty}
-class. Indeed, they are eigenfunctions of the positive Laplace operator
\mathcal{L}_{G}
, that is
\mathcal{L}_{G}\xi_{ij}=\lambda_{[\xi]}\xi_{ij}.

We now follow
[
RT10
, Chapter 10]
to introduce the analysis of operators on the phase space
G\times\widehat{G}.
Indeed, if
A
is a continuous linear operator on
C^{\infty}(G),
there exists a function

a:G\times\widehat{G}\rightarrow\cup_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{C}^{\ell\times\ell},

(2.3)

such that for every equivalence class
[\xi]\in\widehat{G},

a(x,\xi):=a(x,[\xi])\in\mathbb{C}^{d_{\xi}\times d_{\xi}},
(where
d_{\xi}
is the dimension of the continuous, unitary and irreducible representation
\xi:G\rightarrow\textnormal{U}(\mathbb{C}^{d_{\xi}})
) and satisfying

Af(x)=\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}d_{\xi}\textnormal{{Tr}}[\xi(x)a(x,\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)],\,\,f\in C^{\infty}(G).

(2.4)

Here

\widehat{f}(\xi)\equiv(\mathscr{F}f)(\xi):=\int\limits_{G}f(x)\xi(x)^{*}dx\in\mathbb{C}^{d_{\xi}\times d_{\xi}},\,\,\,[\xi]\in\widehat{G},

is the matrix-valued Fourier transform of
f
at
\xi=(\xi_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{d_{\xi}},
and
the function
a
in (
2.4
) satisfies the identity,

a(x,\xi)\equiv\widehat{A}(x,\xi)\equiv\sigma_{A}(x,\xi)=\xi(x)^{*}(A\xi)(x),\,\,A\xi:=(A\xi_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{d_{\xi}},\,\,\,\,[\xi]\in\widehat{G}.

(2.5)

In general, we refer to the function
a
as the (global or matrix)
symbol
of the operator
A.

Remark 2.4
.

We denote by
\mathscr{S}(\widehat{G}):=\mathscr{F}(C^{\infty}(G))
the Schwartz space on the unitary dual. Then the Fourier transform on the group
\mathscr{F}
is a bijective mapping from
C^{\infty}(G)
into
\mathscr{S}(\widehat{G})
(see
[
RT10
, Page 541]
), and in terms of the Fourier transform we have

Af(x)=\mathscr{F}^{-1}[a(x,\cdot)(\mathscr{F}{f})\,](x),

for every
f\in C^{\infty}(G).
In particular, if
a(x,\xi)=I_{d_{\xi}}
is the identity matrix in every representation space,
A\equiv I
is the identity operator on
C^{\infty}(G),
and we recover the Fourier inversion formula

f(x)=\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}d_{\xi}\textnormal{Tr}[\xi(x)\widehat{f}(\xi)],\,\,f\in C^{\infty}(G).

To classify symbols in the Hörmander classes developed in
[
RT10
]
, the notion of
difference operators
on the unitary dual, by endowing
\widehat{G}
with a difference structure, is an instrumental tool. By following
[
RW15
]
, a difference operator
Q_{\xi}
of order
k,
is defined by

Q_{\xi}\widehat{f}(\xi)=\widehat{qf}(\xi),\,[\xi]\in\widehat{G},

(2.6)

for all
f\in C^{\infty}(G),
for some function
q
vanishing of order
k
at the identity
e=e_{G}.
We will denote by
\textnormal{diff}^{k}(\widehat{G})
the set of all difference operators of order
k.
For a fixed smooth function
q,
the associated difference operator will be denoted by
\Delta_{q}:=Q_{\xi}.
We will choose an admissible collection of difference operators (see e.g.
[
RW15
]
),

\Delta_{\xi}^{\alpha}:=\Delta_{q_{(1)}}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\Delta_{q_{(i)}}^{\alpha_{i}},\,\,\alpha=(\alpha_{j})_{1\leqslant j\leqslant i},

where

\textnormal{rank}\{\nabla q_{(j)}(e):1\leqslant j\leqslant i\}=\textnormal{dim}(G),\textnormal{ and }\Delta_{q_{(j)}}\in\textnormal{diff}^{1}(\widehat{G}).

We say that this admissible collection is strongly admissible if

\bigcap_{j=1}^{i}\{x\in G:q_{(j)}(x)=0\}=\{e_{G}\}.

Remark 2.5
.

A special type of difference operators can be defined by using the unitary representations of
G.
Indeed, if
\xi_{0}
is a fixed irreducible and unitary representation of
G
, consider the matrix

\xi_{0}(g)-I_{d_{\xi_{0}}}=[\xi_{0}(g)_{ij}-\delta_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{d_{\xi}},\,\quad g\in G.

(2.7)

Then, we associate to the function

q_{ij}(g):=\xi_{0}(g)_{ij}-\delta_{ij},\quad g\in G,
a difference operator via

\mathbb{D}_{\xi_{0},i,j}:=\mathscr{F}(\xi_{0}(g)_{ij}-\delta_{ij})\mathscr{F}^{-1}.

(2.8)

If the representation is fixed we omit the index
\xi_{0}
so that, from a sequence
\mathbb{D}_{1}=\mathbb{D}_{\xi_{0},j_{1},i_{1}},\cdots,\mathbb{D}_{n}=\mathbb{D}_{\xi_{0},j_{n},i_{n}}
of operators of this type we define
\mathbb{D}^{\alpha}=\mathbb{D}_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\mathbb{D}^{\alpha_{n}}_{n}
, where
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
.

Remark 2.6

(Leibniz rule for difference operators)
.

The difference structure on the unitary dual
\widehat{G},
induced by the difference operators acting on the momentum variable
[\xi]\in\widehat{G},
implies the following Leibniz rule

\displaystyle\Delta_{\xi}^{\alpha}(a_{1}a_{2})(x_{0},\xi)=\sum_{|\gamma|,|\varepsilon|\leqslant|\alpha|\leqslant|\gamma|+|\varepsilon|}C_{\varepsilon,\gamma}(\Delta_{\xi}^{\gamma}a_{1})(x_{0},\xi)(\Delta_{\xi}^{\varepsilon}a_{2})(x_{0},\xi),\quad(x_{0},[\xi])\in G\times\widehat{G},

for
a_{1},a_{2}\in C^{\infty}(G,{S}^{\prime}(\widehat{G})).
For more details we refer the reader to
[
RT10
]
.

Remark 2.7

(Hörmander classes on Lie groups)
.

If
A\in\Psi^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(G,\textnormal{loc}),

\rho\geqslant 1-\delta,
the matrix-valued symbol
\sigma_{A}
of
A
satisfies (see
[
RT10
,
RTW14
]
),

\|{X}_{x}^{\beta}\Delta_{\xi}^{\gamma}\sigma_{A}(x,\xi)\|_{\textnormal{op}}\leqslant C_{\alpha,\beta}\langle\xi\rangle^{m-\rho|\gamma|+\delta|\beta|}

(2.9)

for all
\beta
and
\gamma
multi-indices and all
(x,[\xi])\in G\times\widehat{G}
. Now, if
0\leqslant\delta,\rho\leqslant 1,

we say that
\sigma_{A}\in{S}^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G}),
if the global symbol inequalities (
2.9
) hold true. So, for
\sigma_{A}\in{S}^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G})
we write
A\in\Psi^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(G)\equiv\textnormal{Op}({S}^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G})).
Observe that

\textnormal{Op}({S}^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G}))=\Psi^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(G,\textnormal{loc}),\,\,\,0\leqslant\delta<\rho\leqslant 1,\,\rho\geqslant 1-\delta.

An important type of pseudo-differential operators are the elliptic operators. They are invertible modulo smoothing operators as we discuss in the following remark.

Remark 2.8
.

For
m\in\mathbb{R},
and
0\leqslant\delta<\rho\leqslant 1,
a symbol
a=a(x,\xi)\in{S}^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G})
is elliptic, if
a(x,\xi)
is invertible for every
(x,[\xi])\in G\times\widehat{G},
(with the exception of finitely many equivalence classes
[\xi]
) and if it satisfies

\|a(x,\xi)^{-1}\|_{\textnormal{op}}\leq C\langle\xi\rangle^{-m},\,\,\,\,\langle\xi\rangle\geq N,

(2.10)

for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
large enough. In this case we say that the pseudo-differential operator
A
is elliptic of order
m.
The crucial property of elliptic operators is that they are of Fredholm type on
L^{2}(G).
Indeed, by following
[
RT10
]
, if
0\leq\delta<\rho\leq 1,
there exists
B\in{S}^{-m}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G}),
such that

R_{1}:=AB-I,R_{2}:=BA-I\in{S}^{-\infty}(G\times\widehat{G}):=\bigcap_{m\in\mathbb{R}}{S}^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G}).

Indeed, the operators
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
are compact on
L^{2}(G).
This holds in general for closed manifolds
but the additional condition
\rho\geq 1-\delta
is required.

2.2.
Hörmander classes in the presence of a sub-Riemannian structure

The Hörmander classes in
[
RT10
]
are defined using the spectrum of the Laplacian. In
[
CR20
]
, these classes were extended by using as measure of decay the spectrum of the sub-Laplacian. This generalisation is a useful tool when studying subelliptic (analytic and spectral) problems as it was extensively developed in
[
CR20
]
. To introduce this calculus let us fix the necessary notations.

Let
G
be a compact Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}.
Under the identification
\mathfrak{g}\simeq T_{e_{G}}G,
where
e_{G}

A central notion in the analysis of the sub-Laplacian is that of the Hausdorff dimension, in this case, associated to

(2.11)

2.3.
Determinants on Hilbert spaces

Since we are interested in the study of the Dixmier trace and its regularised determinant we will require some preliminary basic notions. First, we recall the definition of Schatten-von Neumann ideals on Hilbert spaces. A reason for this is that we will use operators
T
of Dixmier type, where
T^{p}
is of class trace.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a complex separable Hilbert space endowed with an inner product denoted
by
{\left({\cdot,\cdot}\right)}
, and let
T:{\mathcal{H}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{H}}
be a linear compact operator. Let
T^{*}:{\mathcal{H}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{H}}
be the adjoint of
T
. Then the linear operator
(T^{*}T)^{\frac{1}{2}}:{\mathcal{H}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{H}}
is positive and compact. Let
(\psi_{k})_{k}
be an orthonormal basis for
{\mathcal{H}}
consisting of eigenvectors of
|T|=(T^{*}T)^{\frac{1}{2}}
, and let
s_{k}(T)
be the eigenvalue corresponding to the eigenvector

\psi_{k}
,
k=1,2,\dots
. The non-negative numbers
s_{k}(T)
,
k=1,2,\dots
, are called the
singular values
of
T:{\mathcal{H}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{H}}
.
If

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}s_{k}(T)<\infty,

then the linear operator
T:{\mathcal{H}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{H}}
is said to be in the
trace class

S_{1}(\mathcal{H})
. It can be shown that
S_{1}({\mathcal{H}})
is a Banach space in which the norm
\|\cdot\|_{S_{1}}
is given by

\|T\|_{S_{1}}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}s_{k}(T),\,T\in S_{1}(\mathcal{H}),

with multiplicities counted.
Let
T:{\mathcal{H}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{H}}
be an operator in
S_{1}({\mathcal{H}})
and let
(\phi_{k})_{k}
be any orthonormal basis for
{\mathcal{H}}
. Then, the series
\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}{\left({T\phi_{k},\phi_{k}}\right)}
is absolutely convergent and the sum is independent of the choice of the orthonormal basis
(\phi_{k})_{k}
. Thus, we can define the trace
\Tr(T)
of any linear operator

T:{\mathcal{H}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{H}}
in
S_{1}(\mathcal{H})
by

\Tr(T):=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}{\left({T\phi_{k},\phi_{k}}\right)},

where
\{\phi_{k}:k=1,2,\dots\}
is any orthonormal basis for
{\mathcal{H}}
. If the singular values
are square-summable
T
is called a
Hilbert-Schmidt
operator. It is clear that every trace class operator is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator.

More generally, if
1\leq p<\infty
and the sequence of singular values is
p
-summable, then
T

is said to belong to the Schatten-von Neumann class
{S}_{p}({\mathcal{H}})
. Indeed, it is well known that each
{S}_{p}({\mathcal{H}})
is an ideal in
\mathcal{L}({\mathcal{H}})
. If
1\leq p<\infty
, a norm is associated to
{S}_{p}({\mathcal{H}})
by

\|T\|_{S_{p}}=\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}(s_{k}(T))^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

If
1\leq p<\infty

the class
S_{p}({\mathcal{H}})
becomes a Banach space endowed by the norm
\|T\|_{S_{p}}
. If
p=\infty
we define
S_{\infty}({\mathcal{H}})
as the class of bounded linear operators on
H
, with

\|T\|_{S_{\infty}}:=\|T\|_{\textnormal{op}}
, the operator norm.

Remark 2.14
.

We note that for a positive compact operator
A
on
\mathcal{H}
and
1\leq p<\infty,

A^{p}\in S_{1}(\mathcal{H}),
if and only
A\in S_{p}(\mathcal{H}),

For operators in the class
{S}_{1}(\mathcal{H})
we have the following determinant formula, see
[
GGK00
, Page 19]
.

Theorem 2.15

(Plemelj-Smithies formula)
.

If
T\in S_{1}({\mathcal{H}})
one has

\det(I+\lambda T)=\exp\left(\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m+1}}{m}\Tr(T^{m})\lambda^{m}\right),

for
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\lambda|
small enough.

2.5.
The Dixmier ideal on Hilbert spaces

By following Connes
[
Con94
]
, if

is a Hilbert space (we are interested in

where
M
is a closed manifold of dimension

\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(\mathcal{H})
consists of those compact linear operators
A
on
\mathcal{H}
satisfying

\sum_{1\leq n\leq N}s_{n}(A)=O(\log(N)),\,\,\,N\rightarrow\infty,

(2.22)

where
\{s_{n}(A)\}
denotes the sequence of singular values of
A
, i.e. the square roots of the eigenvalues of the positive-definite self-adjoint operator
A^{\ast}A.

So,
\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(\mathcal{H})
is endowed with the norm

\|A\|_{\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(\mathcal{H})}=\sup_{N\geq 2}\frac{1}{\log(N)}\sum_{1\leq n\leq N}s_{n}(A).

(2.23)

For an operator
A\in\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(\mathcal{H})
the sequence

\alpha_{N}(A):=\frac{1}{\log(N)}\sum_{j=1}^{N}s_{j}(A),\quad N\in\mathbb{N},

(2.24)

is thus bounded. If the limit of the sequence
\{\alpha_{N}(A)\}
when
N\rightarrow\infty,
does exist we will define

\displaystyle\textnormal{\bf Tr}_{\textnormal{Dix}}(A):=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log(N)}\sum_{j=1}^{N}s_{j}(A).

We recall the main result in Comptes Rendus’s note of Dixmier
[
Dix66
]
regarding the existence of a trace functional different from
the spectral trace defined on the ideal
\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(\mathcal{H})
which contains the ideal of trace class operators
S_{1}(\mathcal{H})
.

Theorem 2.19

(Dixmier
[
Dix66
]
)
.

Let
\omega\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\})^{*}
be a bounded functional satisfying the following conditions:

(D1):

\omega
is a positive linear functional that satisfies
\omega(1,1,\cdots,1,\cdots)=1.

(D2):

\omega((a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}})=0,
for a sequence
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}}\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\})
if

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}a_{n}=0.

(D3):

For any
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}}\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}),

\omega(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{n},\cdots)=\omega(a_{1},a_{1},a_{2},a_{2},\cdots,a_{n},a_{n},\cdots).

For a compact positive operator
A
in
\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(\mathcal{H})
we set

\textnormal{\bf Tr}_{\omega}(A):=\omega\left(\left\{\frac{1}{\log(N)}\sum_{j=1}^{N}s_{j}(A)\right\}_{N\geq 1}\right).

(2.25)

Then,
\textnormal{\bf Tr}_{\omega}
extends by linearity to a trace on
\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(\mathcal{H}),
and

\textnormal{\bf Tr}_{\omega}(A)=\textnormal{\bf Tr}_{\textnormal{Dix}}(A):=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log(N)}\sum_{j=1}^{N}s_{j}(A),

(2.26)

Moreover,
\textnormal{\bf Tr}_{\omega}(A)=0,

Remark 2.20
.

Let us describe the extension of
\textnormal{\bf Tr}_{\omega}
from the positive elements in
\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(\mathcal{H})
to general compact operators in this ideal, see

We use the decomposition of

\textnormal{Re}(A):=\frac{A+A^{*}}{2},\,\,\textnormal{Im}(A):=\frac{A-A^{*}}{2i},

and the decomposition of

into their positive and negative parts,

\displaystyle\textnormal{Re}(A)^{+}:=\frac{\textnormal{Re}(A)+|\textnormal{Re}(A)|}{2},\,\,\textnormal{Re}(A)^{-}:=\frac{|\textnormal{Re}(A)|-\textnormal{Re}(A)}{2},

and

\displaystyle\textnormal{Im}(A)^{+}:=\frac{\textnormal{Im}(A)+|\textnormal{Im}(A)|}{2},\,\,\textnormal{Im}(A)^{-}:=\frac{|\textnormal{Im}(A)|-\textnormal{Im}(A)}{2}.

Now, the operator
A
can be written as

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\textnormal{Re}(A)+i\textnormal{Im}(A)

\displaystyle=\left(\textnormal{Re}(A)^{+}-\textnormal{Re}(A)^{-}\right)+i\left(\textnormal{Im}(A)^{+}-\textnormal{Im}(A)^{-}\right).

So, by the linearity of the Dixmier trace
\textnormal{Tr}_{\omega}
we have

\displaystyle\textnormal{Tr}_{\omega}(A)

\displaystyle=\textnormal{Tr}_{\omega}(\textnormal{Re}(A))+i\textnormal{Tr}_{\omega}(\textnormal{Im}(A))

\displaystyle=\left(\textnormal{Tr}_{\omega}(\textnormal{Re}(A)^{+})-\textnormal{Tr}_{\omega}(\textnormal{Re}(A)^{-})\right)+i\left(\textnormal{Tr}_{\omega}(\textnormal{Im}(A)^{+})-\textnormal{Tr}_{\omega}(\textnormal{Im}(A)^{-})\right).

Definition 2.21
.

We denote by

\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}_{\omega}(\mathcal{H}):=\{A\in\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(\mathcal{H}):\textnormal{\bf{Tr}}_{\textnormal{Dix}}(A)<\infty\}.

(2.27)

the sub-class of operators in

such that the limit in the right-hand side of (

2.6.
Connes equivalence theorem

A remarkable result due to Connes computes the Dixmier class for the classical pseudo-differential operators on a closed manifold.

We record that for a compact orientable manifold without boundary
M
of dimension
n,
a pseudo-differential operator
A
on
M
can be defined by using the notion of a local symbol, this means that for any local chart
U
, the operator
A
has the form

Au(x)=\int\limits_{T^{*}_{x}U}e^{2\pi ix\cdot\xi}\sigma^{A}(x,\xi)\widehat{u}(\xi)\,d\xi.

The pseudo-differential operator
A
is called classical, if
\sigma^{A}
admits an asymptotic expansion

\sigma^{A}(x,\xi)\sim\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sigma^{A}_{m-j}(x,\xi)
in such a way that each function
\sigma_{m-j}(x,\xi)
is homogeneous in
\xi
of order
m-j
for
\xi\neq 0
. The set of classical pseudo-differential operators of order
m
is denoted by
\Psi^{m}_{cl}(M)
, and we denote by
\Psi^{m}_{+e}(M)
the class of positive elliptic classical pseudo-differential operators of order
m\in\mathbb{R}.

For
A\in\Psi_{cl}^{m}(M)
, and for
x\in M
,
\int_{|\xi|=1}\sigma_{-n}(x,\xi)\,d\xi
defines a local density which can be glued over
M
. In this case, the non-commutative residue of
A
is defined by the expression

\textnormal{res}\,(A)=\frac{1}{n(2\pi)^{n}}\int\limits_{M}\int\limits_{|\xi|=1}\sigma^{A}_{-n}(x,\xi)\,d\xi\,dx.

(2.34)

Classical pseudo-differential operators with order
-n,
where
n=\dim(M)
are in the Dixmier class
\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(L^{2}(M)),
and moreover:

Theorem 2.26

(Connes,
[
Con94
]
)
.

If
A\in\Psi^{-n}_{cl}(M)
is a classical pseudo-differential operator, then

\textnormal{res}(A)=\textnormal{\bf{Tr}}_{\omega}(A),

(2.35)

for any functional
\omega
satisfying the conditions in Dixmier’s Theorem
2.19
.

3.

In this section we investigate the Dixmier traceability of pseudo-differential operators on compact Lie groups. We will apply our analysis to the case of subelliptic pseudo-differential classes
\Psi^{-Q,\mathcal{L}}_{\rho,0}(G\times\widehat{G}).
In particular, if we replace the sub-Laplacian

Lemma 3.1

For

Lemma 3.3

For any

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\|f\|_{L^{2}}=1}\|\sup_{z\in G}|\textnormal{Op}[[\xi]\mapsto\sigma_{R_{p}}(z,[\xi])\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{(Q+\varepsilon)p}(\xi)]f(x)|\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\|f\|_{L^{2}}=1}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq{[n/2]+1}}\|\textnormal{Op}[[\xi]\mapsto X_{z}^{\alpha}\sigma_{R_{p}}(z,[\xi])\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{(Q+\varepsilon)p}(\xi)]f(x)\|_{L^{2}(G_{x}\times G_{z})}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\|f\|_{L^{2}}=1}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq{[n/2]+1}}\sup_{z\in G}\|\textnormal{Op}[[\xi]\mapsto X_{z}^{\alpha}\sigma_{R_{p}}(z,[\xi])\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{(Q+\varepsilon)p}(\xi)]\|_{\mathscr{B}(L^{2})}\|f\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq{[n/2]+1}}\sup_{z\in G}\sup_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}\|X_{z}^{\alpha}\sigma_{R_{p}}(z,[\xi])\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{(Q+\varepsilon)p}(\xi)\|_{\textnormal{op}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{n}\sup_{|\alpha|\leq{[n/2]+1}}\sup_{(z,[\xi])\in G\times\widehat{G}}\|X_{z}^{\alpha}\sigma_{R_{p}}(z,[\xi])\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{(Q+\varepsilon)p}(\xi)\widehat{\mathcal{M}}^{\rho-\varepsilon p}(\xi)\|_{\textnormal{op}}\|\widehat{\mathcal{M}}^{-\rho+\varepsilon p}(\xi)\|_{\textnormal{op}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 1,

where we have used that

\sup_{(z,[\xi])\in G\times\widehat{G}}\|X_{z}^{\alpha}\sigma_{R_{p}}(z,[\xi])\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{(Q+\varepsilon)p}(\xi)\widehat{\mathcal{M}}^{\rho-\varepsilon p}(\xi)\|_{\textnormal{op}}=O(1),\textnormal{ }\|\widehat{\mathcal{M}}^{-\rho+\varepsilon p}(\xi)\|_{\textnormal{op}}\leq 1,

in view of (
3.13
) and the fact that
-\rho+\varepsilon p<0
. The analysis above, and the positivity of the operator
{\mathcal{M}}_{-(Q+\varepsilon)p},
imply that

\displaystyle|\lim_{p\rightarrow 1^{+}}(p-1)\textnormal{\bf{Tr}}(R_{p})|

\displaystyle=\lim_{p\rightarrow 1^{+}}(p-1)|\textnormal{\bf{Tr}}(R_{p})|

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{p\rightarrow 1^{+}}(p-1)C\|R_{p}{\mathcal{M}}_{(Q+\varepsilon)p}\|_{\mathscr{B}(L^{2}(G))}|\textnormal{\bf{Tr}}({\mathcal{M}}_{-(Q+\varepsilon)p})|

\displaystyle\lesssim\lim_{p\rightarrow 1^{+}}(p-1)|\textnormal{\bf{Tr}}({\mathcal{M}}_{-(Q+\varepsilon)p})|

\displaystyle=\lim_{p\rightarrow 1^{+}}(p-1)\textnormal{\bf{Tr}}({\mathcal{M}}^{-(Q+\varepsilon)p})

\displaystyle=\textnormal{\bf{Tr}}_{\omega}({\mathcal{M}}^{-(Q+\varepsilon)}).

Because of Lemma
3.1
, we have that
\textnormal{\bf{Tr}}_{\omega}({\mathcal{M}}^{-(Q+\varepsilon)})=0,
and then

\lim_{p\rightarrow 1^{+}}(p-1)\textnormal{\bf{Tr}}(R_{p})=0.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\textnormal{\bf{Tr}}_{\omega}(A)=\lim_{p\rightarrow 1^{+}}(p-1)\left(\textnormal{\bf{Tr}}[\textnormal{Op}(\sigma_{-Q}^{p})]+\textnormal{\bf{Tr}}[R_{p}]\right)=\int\limits_{G}\|\sigma_{-Q}(x,\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(\widehat{G})}dx.

Thus, we end the proof by applying Lemma
2.23
.
∎

Now, we will use the functional calculus to remove the positivity assumptions in Lemma
3.3
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
A\in\Psi^{-Q,\mathcal{L}}_{\rho,0}(G\times\widehat{G})
be an
\mathcal{L}
-elliptic pseudo-differential operator and let
0<\rho\leq 1
. Assume that the symbol of
A
admits an asymptotic expansion

\sigma_{A}(x,[\xi])\sim\sum_{k=-\infty}^{-Q}\sigma_{k}(x,[\xi]),\,

in components with decreasing order, which means that, for any
N\in\mathbb{N},

\sigma_{A}(x,[\xi])-\sum_{k=-N-Q}^{-Q}\sigma_{k}(x,[\xi])\in S^{-(N+1)\rho-Q}_{\rho,0}(G\times\widehat{G}).

Then
|A|\in\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(L^{2}(G)),

\textnormal{\bf{Tr}}_{\omega}(|A|)=\int\limits_{G}\|\sigma_{-Q}(x,[\xi])\|_{\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(\widehat{G})}dx,

and we have

\textnormal{Det}_{\omega,|A|}(\lambda)=\exp\left(\textnormal{\bf{Tr}}_{\omega}(|A|)\lambda\right)=\exp\left(\int\limits_{G}\|\sigma_{-Q}(x,[\xi])\|_{\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(\widehat{G})}dx\cdot\lambda\right),

(3.14)

for
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\lambda|
small enough,
and

\textnormal{Det}_{\omega,|A|}^{\prime}(0):=\frac{d}{d\lambda}\textnormal{Det}_{\omega,|A|}(\lambda)|_{\lambda=0}=\textnormal{\bf{Tr}}_{\omega}(|A|).

Proof.

From the subelliptic calculus (see (i) in Theorem
2.11
), we have that the matrix-valued symbol
\sigma_{A^{*}A}
of
A^{*}A,
admits the asymptotic expansion

\sigma_{A^{*}A}(x,[\xi])\sim\sum_{k=-\infty}^{-2Q}\tilde{\sigma}_{k}(x,[\xi]),\,

in components with decreasing order, which means that, for any
N\in\mathbb{N},

\sigma_{A}(x,[\xi])-\sum_{k=-N-2Q}^{-2Q}{\tilde{\sigma}}_{k}(x,[\xi])\in S^{-(N+1)\rho-2Q}_{\rho,0}(G\times\widehat{G}),

with the principal term given by

{\tilde{\sigma}}_{-2Q}(x,[\xi])={\sigma}_{-Q}(x,[\xi])^{*}{\sigma}_{-Q}(x,[\xi])=|{\sigma}_{-Q}(x,[\xi])|^{2},

and consequently positive for any
(x,[\xi]).
The functional calculus in
[
CR20
]
, implies that the symbol of
|A|:=\sqrt{A^{*}A},
admits an asymptotic expansion of the type

\sigma_{|A|}(x,[\xi])\sim\sum_{k=-\infty}^{-Q}{\sigma^{\prime}}_{k}(x,[\xi]),\,

that is, for any
N\in\mathbb{N},

\sigma_{|A|}(x,[\xi])-\sum_{k=-N-Q}^{-Q}{\sigma^{\prime}}_{k}(x,[\xi])\in S^{-(N+1)\rho-Q}_{\rho,0}(G\times\widehat{G}),

with the principal term given by

{\sigma^{\prime}}_{-Q}(x,[\xi])=\sqrt{{\sigma}_{-Q}(x,[\xi])^{*}{\sigma}_{-Q}(x,[\xi])}=|{\sigma}_{-Q}(x,[\xi])|,

which is then positive for any
(x,[\xi]).
Applying Lemma
3.3
to
|A|,
and observing that

\|\sigma^{\prime}_{-Q}(x,[\xi])\|_{\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(\widehat{G})}=\|\sigma_{-Q}(x,[\xi])\|_{\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(\widehat{G})},

we conclude that

\textnormal{\bf{Tr}}_{\omega}(|A|)=\int\limits_{G}\|\sigma_{-Q}(x,[\xi])\|_{\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(\widehat{G})}dx.

We end the proof by applying Lemma
2.23
.
∎

.

Proof.

Since
A
is positive, we can apply Theorem
3.4
to

and the proof can be concluded.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

Under the hypothesis of Lemma
3.3
, we have

\textnormal{\bf{Tr}}_{\omega}(A)=\int\limits_{G}\|\sigma_{-Q}(x,[\xi])\|_{\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(\widehat{G})}dx=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log(N)}\sum_{j=1}^{N}s_{j}(A),

where

denotes the sequence of singular values of

with multiplicities taken into account.

Proof.

It follows from the Dixmier Theorem

\textnormal{\bf Tr}_{\omega}(A)=\textnormal{\bf Tr}_{\textnormal{Dix}}(A),
provided that the limit
\textnormal{\bf Tr}_{\textnormal{Dix}}(A)

is equivalent to the existence of
\textnormal{\bf Tr}_{\textnormal{Dix}}(A),

[

and in the case of their existence they have the same value, that is
\textnormal{\bf Tr}_{\omega}(A)
. The identity in (
3.18
) now follows from the proof of Lemma
3.3
, where we have shown that
\textnormal{\bf Tr}_{\omega}(A)=\lim_{p\rightarrow 1^{+}}(p-1)\textnormal{\bf Tr}(A^{p}).

∎

Now, we present the main theorem of this section.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
A\in\Psi^{-Q,\mathcal{L}}_{\rho,0}(G\times\widehat{G})
be an
\mathcal{L}
-elliptic pseudo-differential operator and let
0<\rho\leq 1
. Assume that the symbol of
A
admits an asymptotic expansion

\sigma_{A}(x,[\xi])\sim\sum_{k=-\infty}^{-Q}\sigma_{k}(x,[\xi]),\,

in components with decreasing order, which means that, for any
N\in\mathbb{N},

\sigma_{A}(x,[\xi])-\sum_{k=-N-Q}^{-Q}\sigma_{k}(x,[\xi])\in S^{-(N+1)\rho-Q}_{\rho,0}(G\times\widehat{G}).

Then
A\in\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(L^{2}(G)),
and its Dixmier trace is given by

\displaystyle\textnormal{\bf{Tr}}_{\omega}(A)=\int\limits_{G}\left(\|\textnormal{Re}(\sigma_{-Q}(x,[\xi]))^{+}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(\widehat{G})}-\|\textnormal{Re}(\sigma_{-Q}(x,[\xi]))^{-}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(\widehat{G})}\right)dx

\displaystyle+i\int\limits_{G}\left(\|\textnormal{Im}(\sigma_{-Q}(x,[\xi]))^{+}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(\widehat{G})}-\|\textnormal{Im}(\sigma_{-Q}(x,[\xi]))^{-}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(\widehat{G})}\right)dx.

Proof.

In view of the polar decomposition
A=|A|U
of
A,
where
U
is a unitary operator on
L^{2}(G)
(see e.g.
[
GGK00
]
), that
\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(L^{2}(G))
is an ideal of operators in the
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathscr{B}(L^{2}(G)),
that
|A|\in\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(L^{2}(G))
in view of Theorem
3.4
, we deduce that
A\in\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(L^{2}(G)).
Now we will use the linearity properties of the Dixmier trace in order to compute the Dixmier trace of
A.

We use the decomposition of
A
into its real and imaginary part as in Remark
2.20
,

\textnormal{Re}(A):=\frac{A+A^{*}}{2},\,\,\textnormal{Im}(A):=\frac{A-A^{*}}{2i},

and the decomposition of
\textnormal{Re}(A)
and
\textnormal{Im}(A)
into their positive and negative parts,

\displaystyle\textnormal{Re}(A)^{+}:=\frac{\textnormal{Re}(A)+|\textnormal{Re}(A)|}{2},\,\,\textnormal{Re}(A)^{-}:=\frac{|\textnormal{Re}(A)|-\textnormal{Re}(A)}{2},

and

\displaystyle\textnormal{Im}(A)^{+}:=\frac{\textnormal{Im}(A)+|\textnormal{Im}(A)|}{2},\,\,\textnormal{Im}(A)^{-}:=\frac{|\textnormal{Im}(A)|-\textnormal{Im}(A)}{2}.

Now, the operator
A
can be written as

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\textnormal{Re}(A)+i\textnormal{Im}(A)

\displaystyle=\left(\textnormal{Re}(A)^{+}-\textnormal{Re}(A)^{-}\right)+i\left(\textnormal{Im}(A)^{+}-\textnormal{Im}(A)^{-}\right).

So, by the linearity of the Dixmier trace
\textnormal{Tr}_{\omega}
we have

\displaystyle\textnormal{Tr}_{\omega}(A)

\displaystyle=\textnormal{Tr}_{\omega}(\textnormal{Re}(A))+i\textnormal{Tr}_{\omega}(\textnormal{Im}(A))

\displaystyle=\left(\textnormal{Tr}_{\omega}(\textnormal{Re}(A)^{+})-\textnormal{Tr}_{\omega}(\textnormal{Re}(A)^{-})\right)+i\left(\textnormal{Tr}_{\omega}(\textnormal{Im}(A)^{+})-\textnormal{Tr}_{\omega}(\textnormal{Im}(A)^{-})\right).

Now, we will exploit the subelliptic functional calculus in
[
CR20
]
in order to compute the symbols of the positive operators
\textnormal{Re}(A)^{+},\textnormal{Re}(A)^{-},\textnormal{Im}(A)^{+},\textnormal{Im}(A)^{-}.
Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\sigma_{\textnormal{Re}(A)}(x,[\xi])=\sigma_{\frac{A+A^{*}}{2}}(x,[\xi])=\textnormal{Re}(\sigma_{-Q}(x,[\xi]))+\textnormal{lower order terms},

\displaystyle\sigma_{\textnormal{Im}(A)}(x,[\xi])=\sigma_{\frac{A-A^{*}}{2i}}(x,[\xi])=\textnormal{Im}(\sigma_{-Q}(x,[\xi]))+\textnormal{lower order terms},

\displaystyle\sigma_{\textnormal{Re}(A)^{+}}(x,[\xi])=\sigma_{\frac{\textnormal{Re}(A)+|\textnormal{Re}(A)|}{2}}(x,[\xi])=\textnormal{Re}(\sigma_{-Q}(x,[\xi]))^{+}+\textnormal{lower order terms},

\displaystyle\sigma_{\textnormal{Re}(A)^{-}}(x,[\xi])=\sigma_{\frac{|\textnormal{Re}(A)|-\textnormal{Re}(A)}{2}}(x,[\xi])=\textnormal{Re}(\sigma_{-Q}(x,[\xi]))^{-}+\textnormal{lower order terms},

and

\displaystyle\sigma_{\textnormal{Im}(A)^{+}}(x,[\xi])=\sigma_{\frac{\textnormal{Im}(A)+|\textnormal{Im}(A)|}{2}}(x,[\xi])=\textnormal{Im}(\sigma_{-Q}(x,[\xi]))^{+}+\textnormal{lower order terms},

\displaystyle\sigma_{\textnormal{Im}(A)^{-}}(x,[\xi])=\sigma_{\frac{|\textnormal{Im}(A)|-\textnormal{Im}(A)}{2}}(x,[\xi])=\textnormal{Im}(\sigma_{-Q}(x,[\xi]))^{-}+\textnormal{lower order terms}.

Now, by applying Corollary
3.5
, we can eliminate the lower terms when computing the Dixmier trace, and we have the following formulae

\textnormal{\bf{Tr}}_{\omega}(\textnormal{Re}(A)^{+})=\int\limits_{G}\|\textnormal{Re}(\sigma_{-Q}(x,[\xi]))^{+}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(\widehat{G})}dx

\textnormal{\bf{Tr}}_{\omega}(\textnormal{Re}(A)^{-})=\int\limits_{G}\|\textnormal{Re}(\sigma_{-Q}(x,[\xi]))^{-}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(\widehat{G})}dx

\textnormal{\bf{Tr}}_{\omega}(\textnormal{Im}(A)^{+})=\int\limits_{G}\|\textnormal{Im}(\sigma_{-Q}(x,[\xi]))^{+}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(\widehat{G})}dx

\textnormal{\bf{Tr}}_{\omega}(\textnormal{Im}(A)^{-})=\int\limits_{G}\|\textnormal{Im}(\sigma_{-Q}(x,[\xi]))^{-}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(\widehat{G})}dx.

In view of the linearity of the Dixmier trace
\textnormal{\bf{Tr}}_{\omega}
and the previous trace formula we end the proof.
∎

4.
Final remarks on compact manifolds

We now illustrate some connections between the determinant of operators in the setting of the invariance notion recalled in Section
2.4
and introduced in
[
DR18
]
, and the problem of computing the Dixmier trace of elliptic operators. We denote by
\Psi^{m}_{+e}(M)
the class of positive elliptic classical pseudo-differential operators of order
m\in\mathbb{R}.

If
A\in\Psi^{-{n}}_{+e}(M)
, then
A
is not necessarily an operator of trace class. Indeed, the best information that we know about the Schwartz kernel
K_{A}
of
A,
is its logarithmic behaviour over the diagonal (see Hörmander
[
Hör85
]
). So, the determinant function
\textnormal{Det}(I+\lambda\cdot)
is not a well defined functional on the class of operators
\Psi^{-{n}}_{+e}(M)
. However, when studying the regularised version

\textnormal{Det}_{\omega,A}(\lambda):=\lim_{p\rightarrow 1^{+}}\textnormal{Det}(I+\lambda A^{p})^{p-1},

(4.1)

of
`\textnormal{Det}(I+\lambda A)
’, we can observe that it can be computed in terms of the global symbol
\sigma_{A,E}\equiv(\sigma_{A,E}(\ell))_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
of
A,
and of its Dixmier trace (and also, in view of the Connes equivalence in Theorem
2.26
, of its non-commutative residue).

Remark 4.1
.

Let
M
be a compact manifold of dimension
n
without boundary endowed with a volume form
dx
, and let
E
be a positive elliptic classical pseudo-differential operator of order
\nu>0.
Let us denote the eigenvalues of
E
by
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j\geq 0}.

If
A:C^{\infty}(M)\rightarrow C^{\infty}(M)
is invariant with respect to the subspaces
H_{j}=\textnormal{Ker}(E-\lambda_{j}I),
in terms of the symbol
\sigma_{A,E}(\ell)
of
A,
let us define the functional

\|\sigma_{A,E}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(\mathbb{N},E)}:=\frac{1}{n}\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log N}\sum_{l:(1+\lambda_{l})^{\frac{1}{\nu}}\leq N}\textnormal{\bf{Tr}}(|\sigma_{A,E}(l)|).

(4.2)

It was proved in
[
CDC20
]
, that
A\in\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(L^{2}(M)),
if and only if,

\|\sigma_{A,E}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(\mathbb{N},E)}<\infty.

In particular, if
A
is positive (with respect to the inner product on
L^{2}(M)
),

\textnormal{\bf{Tr}}_{\omega}(A)=\|\sigma_{A,E}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(\mathbb{N},E)}.

In view of Lemma
2.23
and Remark
4.1
we have the following theorem.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
A\in\Psi^{-n}_{+e}(M)
be a positive and elliptic operator. Then
A\in\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(L^{2}(M)),
and

\textnormal{Det}_{\omega,A}(\lambda)=\exp\left(\textnormal{\bf{Tr}}_{\omega}(A)\lambda\right)=\exp\left(\lambda\|\sigma_{A,E}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(\mathbb{N},E)}\right),

(4.3)

for
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\lambda|
small enough and any
E\in\Psi^{-n}_{+e}(M)
commuting with
A
.
In particular,

\textnormal{Det}_{\omega,A}^{\prime}(0):=\frac{d}{d\lambda}\textnormal{Det}_{\omega,A}(\lambda)|_{\lambda=0}=\textnormal{\bf{Tr}}_{\omega}(A)=\|\sigma_{A,E}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(\mathbb{N},E)},

and

\textnormal{\bf{Det}}_{\omega}(I+A)=\exp(\|\sigma_{A,E}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(\mathbb{N},E)}).

(4.4)

Proof.

Let us prove that
A
satisfies the hypothesis in Lemma
2.23
. Indeed, the fact that
A\in\Psi^{-n}_{+}(M),
implies that
A\in\mathcal{L}^{(1,\infty)}(L^{2}(M))
(see
[
Con94
]
). The functional calculus for elliptic operators implies that
A^{p}\in\Psi^{-np}_{+}(M),
and consequently, for
1<p<\infty,

\textnormal{\bf{Tr}}_{\omega}(A^{p})=0.
Indeed, the condition
A^{p}\in\Psi^{-np}_{+}(M),
implies that
A^{p}\in S_{1}(L^{2}(M))
which implies that its Dixmier functional vanishes (see
[
Con94
]
). In view of Lemma
2.23
we have proved the left hand side of (
4.3
). The right-hand side of (
4.3
) is a consequence of Remark
4.1
.
∎

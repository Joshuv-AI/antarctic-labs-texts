Functional Limit Theorems for Hawkes Processes
Ulrich Horst, and, Wei Xu

Abstract
We prove that the long-run behavior of Hawkes processes is fully determined by the average number and the dispersion of child events. For subcritical processes we provide FLLNs and FCLTs under minimal conditions on the kernel of the process with the precise form of the limit theorems depending strongly on the dispersion of child events. For a critical Hawkes process with weakly dispersed child events, functional central limit theorems do not hold. Instead, we prove that the rescaled intensity processes and rescaled Hawkes processes behave like CIR-processes without mean-reversion, respectively integrated CIR-processes. We provide the rate of convergence by establishing an upper bound on the Wasserstein distance between the distributions of rescaled Hawkes process and the corresponding limit process. By contrast, critical Hawkes process with heavily dispersed child events share many properties of subcritical ones. In particular, functional limit theorems hold. However, unlike subcritical processes critical ones with heavily dispersed child events display long-range dependencies. MSC 2020 subject classifications: Primary 60F17, 60G55; secondary 60J80 Keywords and phrases: Hawkes process, functional limit theorem, regular variation, convergence rate.

Introduction

A Hawkes process
N:=\{N(t):t\geq 0\}
is a random point process that models self-exciting arrivals of random events. Its
intensity

\Lambda:=\{\Lambda(t):t\geq 0\}
is usually of the form

\displaystyle\Lambda(t):=\mu(t)+\sum_{0<\tau_{i}<t}\phi(t-\tau_{i})=\mu(t)+\int_{(0,t)}\phi(t-s)N(ds),

(1.1)

for some
immigration density

\mu\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{R}_{+})
that captures the immigration of exogenous events, and some
kernel

\phi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{R}_{+})
that captures the self-exciting impact of past events on the arrivals of future events.
The random variable
\tau_{i}
denotes the arrival time of the
i
-th event, for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

A Hawkes process can be reconstructed as a Poisson cluster process associated to an age-dependent branching process with an average offspring number
m:=\|\phi\|_{L^{1}}
. The criticality of branching processes gives rise to three regimes.
Depending on the average number of offsprings, a Hawkes process is called subcritical/stationary
(m<1)
, critical/quasi-stationary
(m=1)
or super-critical/non-stationary
(m>1)
.

In this paper we analyse the asymptotic behavior of subcritical and critical Hawkes processes by establishing FLLNs and FCLTs (classical and non-clasical) for such processes.

1.2
Our contribution

This paper establishes functional and scaling limit theorems for Hawkes processes under minimal conditions on their kernels. The following key questions will be addressed. First, in
[
7
]
the moment condition (
1.2
) plays an important role in establishing the classical FCLT for subcritical Hawkes processes. Our first key question is hence:

(Q1)

Is the condition (
1.2
) sharp or can FCLTs still be established for subcritical Hawkes processes if this condition fails to hold?

The FCLT for subcritical processes uncovers that despite the cross-dependencies on the levels of event arrivals, subcritical Hawkes processes not only do
not
display long-range dependencies, but actually enjoy similar asymptotic properties as Poisson point processes where the event arrivals are entirely uncorrelated. This motivates our second key question:

(Q2)

Which classes of Hawkes processes display long-range dependencies?

It is well known that subcritical Hawkes processes with constant immigration rate converge to a stationary regime in the long run.
To the best of our knowledge, no general result on the asymptotic stationarity or non-stationary of critical Hawkes processes has so far been established in the literature.
3
3

3

The existence of non-trivial stationary Hawkes processes on
\mathbb{R}
with zero exogenous arrival intensity was proved in
[
16
]
under regular variation conditions on the kernel
\phi
.
Our third key question is thus:

(Q3)

Are all critical Hawkes processes with constant immigration asymptotically non-stationary? Do FLLNs and FCLTs hold for non-stationary processes?

We provide detailed answers to all three questions, thereby providing a comprehensive analysis of the long-run behavior of both subcritical and critical Hawkes processes.
To answer questions
(Q1)
-
(Q3)
we first extend the duality method developed in
[
1
,
2
,
72
]
and establish an explicit exponential-affine representation of the Fourier-Laplace functional of the process of
(N,\Lambda)
in term of the unique solution to a certain nonlinear Volterra equation.
The functional captures most microscopic properties of
(N,\Lambda)
including the finite-dimensional distributions, event cascades, and the distribution and genealogy of all child events triggered by a mother event.
The representation of the Lapalce functional is the first main contribution of this paper.

1.2.1
Subcritical Hawkes processes

Our second main contribution is twofold and fully answers question
(Q1)
. First, we show that the moment condition (
1.2
) is not sharp;
second we show that FCLTs for subcritical Hawkes processes with constant immigration rate can be established under much weaker conditions with the precise form depending on the dispersion of child events.

More precisely, by exploring the release of excitation of each event on future events, we prove that the long-term behavior of a subcritical Hawkes process is fully determined by the function

\displaystyle{\it\Psi}_{1}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}s\phi(s)ds,\quad t\geq 0

that describes the dispersion of child events. We prove that a classical FCLT holds if and only if
\frac{{\it\Psi}_{1}(t)}{\sqrt{t}}\to 0
as
t\to\infty
, which is much weaker than the condition (
1.2
). If
\frac{{\it\Psi}_{1}(t)}{\sqrt{t}}\to{\it\Psi}_{*}\in(0,\infty)
, a non-classical FCLT holds. In this case, rescaled Hawkes process behaves asymtotically like a Brownian motion with linear drift plus an additional square root function, i.e.,

\displaystyle\frac{N(nt)}{n}\sim C_{0}\cdot t+\frac{C_{2}}{\sqrt{n}}\cdot B(t)+\frac{C_{1}}{\sqrt{n}}\cdot\sqrt{t}.

Finally, if
\frac{{\it\Psi}_{1}(t)}{\sqrt{t}}\to\infty
and the integrated tail-function

\displaystyle{\it\Phi}(t):=\int_{t}^{\infty}\phi(s)ds,\quad t\geq 0,

of the kernel
\phi
is regularly varying with index
-\alpha\in[-1/2,0]
, a degenerate FCLT holds. In this case,

\displaystyle\frac{N(nt)}{n}\sim C_{0}\cdot t+C_{3}\cdot{\it\Phi}(n)\cdot t^{1-\alpha}.

1.2.2
Critical Hawkes processes

Whereas subcritical Hawkes processes do not display long-range dependencies we prove that critical Hawkes processes do. This answers question
(Q2)
.
Furthermore,
we prove that the long run behavior of critical Hawkes processes and hence the answer to question
(Q3)
critically depends on the
dispersion coefficient

\sigma:={\it\Psi}_{1}(\infty).

We call a Hawkes process
weakly critical
if
\sigma<\infty
and
strongly critical
if
\sigma=\infty
. It turns out that weakly critical processes are not asymptotically stationary so that neither a classical FLLN nor a classical FCLT can be established for such processes. By contrast, classical FLLNs and FCLTs can be established for strongly critical processes. This answers question
(Q3)
.

Our limit results for weakly critical processes complement the work of Jaisson and Rosenbaum
[
49
]
. They considered scaling limits for sequences of weakly critical processes with light-tailed kernels when the number of average offsprings increases to one. In this case, the limiting model can be described by a CIR model with mean-reversion. By contrast, we consider limit theorems for critical Hawkes processes (with a fixed number of average offsprings). It turns out that in the weakly critical case our limit model can also be described by a CIR model, but
without
mean-reversion. The long run dynamics of our model is hence very different from the one in
[
49
]
.

Our results for strongly critical processes complement the scaling limits for nearly unstable processes with heavy-tailed kernels established in
[
50
]
and refined in Horst et al
[
45
]
. Again, the limiting results are very different. In particular, it does not seem to be possible to obtain the rough fractional diffusion without mean-reversion obtained in
[
45
,
50
]
by scaling a single critical Hawkes processes.

In what follows we summarize our main results for critical Hawkes processes in greater detail.

1.2.3
Weakly critical Hawkes processes

Motivated by scaling limits established in
[
42
]
, we prove that after a suitable scaling in time and space, the intensity of a weakly critical Hawkes process can be approximated in law by a CIR model without mean-reversion
\Lambda^{*}
, and that the Hawkes process
N
behaves asymptotically as the integrated process of
\Lambda^{*}
. That is, we prove that

\displaystyle\Lambda^{(n)}(t):=\frac{\Lambda(nt)}{n}\sim\Lambda^{*}(t)\quad\mbox{and}\quad N^{(n)}(t):=\frac{N(nt)}{n^{2}}\sim\int_{0}^{t}\Lambda^{*}(s)ds=:\mathcal{I}_{\Lambda^{*}}(t),\quad t\geq 0.

The CIR model without mean-reversion satisfies
\Lambda^{*}(t)\to\infty
a.s. as
t\to\infty
and

{\rm Corr}(\Lambda^{*}(t),\Lambda^{*}(t+T))\sim C/T\quad\mbox{as}\quad T\to\infty.

The latter suggests that the correlation between increments of the point process
N
decreases at a rate
\frac{1}{T}
, i.e. that

\displaystyle{\rm Corr}\big(N(t,t+1),N(t+T,t+T+1)\big)\sim{\rm Corr}\big(\Lambda(t),\Lambda(t+T)\big)\sim\frac{C}{T}.

As a result, we cannot expect weakly stationary processes to be asymptotically stationary but we do expect them to display long-range dependencies.

We also investigate the rate of convergence by establishing two upper bounds on the distance between the path-distributions of
(\Lambda^{(n)},N^{(n)})
and
(\Lambda^{*},\mathcal{I}_{\Lambda^{*}})
on compact time intervals with respect to the Fourier-Laplace functional and the Wasserstein distance.
It turns out that the rate of convergence is fully determined by the decay rate of
\|\mathcal{I}_{R^{(n)}}-\mathcal{I}_{R^{*}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}
to
0
, where
\mathcal{I}_{R^{(n)}}
is the integral function of the time-scaled resolvent of
\phi
and
\mathcal{I}_{R^{*}}
is the corresponding limit function. Under a regular variation condition on the integrated tail-function
{\it\Phi}
, an upper estimate in term of
{\it\Phi}
on
\|\mathcal{I}_{R^{(n)}}-\mathcal{I}_{R^{*}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}
is established.

The lack of Markovianity, martingale and regularity properties of kernel
\phi
renders the analysis of the convergence rate challenging as many standard methods such as Stein’s method, Lyapunov function and Markov coupling developed to study convergence rates and ergodicity of Markov processes and martingales cannot be applied in our setting.

To overcome these difficulties, we use the explicit exponential-affine representations of the Fourier-Laplace functionals of
(\Lambda^{(n)},N^{(n)})
and
(\Lambda^{*},\mathcal{I}_{\Lambda^{*}})
to bound the distance of their distribution with respect to the Fourier-Laplace functional through the difference of their Fourier-Laplace exponents. Bounds for the latter difference are successfully established by considering the corresponding nonlinear Volterra equations.
For the distributional distance with respect to the Wasserstein distance, we first use the Plancherel theorem to rewrite the distance in terms of Fourier-Laplace functionals, for which upper estimates can be established through a series of tail estimates for the Laplace-Stieltes transforms of the integrated kernel function and integrals of functions that describe the dispersion of child events.

1.2.4
Strongly critical Hakes processes

While FLLNs and FCLTs do not hold for weakly critical processes the situation is very different for strongly critical processes.
If the arrivals of child events are highly dispersed, then it is almost impossible to trace a child event back to its mother event and we expect the distribution of the Hawkes process to display some form of asymptotic loss of memory.

While some form of stationarity is expected in the long run and hence FLLNs and FCLTs are expected to hold, we will see that strongly critical Hawkes processes display long-range dependencies.
More precisely, we prove that when the integrated tail-function
{\it\Phi}
is regularly varying with index
-\alpha\in[-1,0]
, strongly critical Hawkes processes asymptotically behave like Gaussian processes with long-range dependencies when suitably normalized and hence display long-range dependencies.
Specifically, we prove that

\displaystyle\frac{N(nt)-\mathbf{E}[N(nt)]}{\mathcal{I}^{2}(n)}\sim C_{4}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(n)}}{n}\cdot\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha}s^{\alpha/2}dB(s)

where the function
\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}
denotes the repeated integral of order two of the resolvent of
\phi
.
The function
\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}
is regularly varying with index
1+\alpha\in[1,2]
and
\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(n)=o(n^{2})
as
n\to\infty
. Furthermore, if
\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}
is second-order regularly varying with auxiliary function
A
that is regularly varying with index
\rho\leq 0
, then we prove that

\displaystyle\frac{N(nt)}{\mathcal{I}^{2}(n)}\sim C_{5}\cdot t^{\alpha+1}+C_{6}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(n)}}{n}\cdot\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha}s^{\alpha/2}dB(s)+C_{7}\cdot t+C_{8}\cdot t^{\alpha+1}\cdot\int_{1}^{t}u^{\rho-1}du.

2.1
Hawkes processes and their Fourier-Laplace transforms

Associated to the kernel
\phi
is the
resolvent

R
that is defined by the unique solution to the linear Volterra equation

\displaystyle R=\phi+R*\phi.

(2.1)

This equaiton is also known as
resolvent equation
or
renewal equation
. The resolvent describes the combined direct and indirect impact of an individual event on the arrival of future events. It admits the Neumann series expansion

\displaystyle R(t)=\phi(t)+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\phi^{(*k)}(t),\quad t\geq 0,

where
\phi^{(*k)}
denotes the
k
-th convolution of
\phi
. The first term
\phi
represents the direct impact of an immigrant event, and the term
\phi^{(*k)}
can be interpreted as describing the indirect impact on future arrivals through the direct impact of the descendant events in the
(k-1)
-th generation. We shall also need the following three important functions:

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\Lambda}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}\Lambda(s)ds,\quad\mathcal{I}_{R}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}R(s)ds\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{I}_{R}^{2}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{I}_{R}(s)ds,\quad t\geq 0.

The first function describes the
cumulative event rate
.
The second and third function specify the
cumulative impact
and
integrated cumulative impact
of each immigrant event and its descendants on the arrivals of future events, respectively. Let

m:=\|\phi\|_{L^{1}}

denote the average number of descendants of each event. The
total impact

\mathcal{I}_{R}(\infty)
of an immigrant event is finite if and only if the Hawkes process is subcritical
(m<1)
, in which case

\mathcal{I}_{R}(\infty)=\frac{m}{1-m}.

Remark 2.1

For supercritical processes
(m>1)
both
\mathcal{I}_{R}
and
\mathcal{I}_{\Lambda}
grow exponentially in time and each event has an infinite number descendants with positive probability. In the critical case
(m=1)
the long-term behavior of
\mathcal{I}_{R}
strongly depends on the choice of
\phi
. The function often grows to infinity at a power rate, but sometimes it increases only slowly; see Proposition
2.6
for details.

The Hawkes process
N
has the compensator
\mathcal{I}_{\Lambda}
and the compensated point process

\widetilde{N}:=N-\mathcal{I}_{\Lambda}

(2.2)

is an
(\mathscr{F}_{t})
-martingale. By using the variation of constants formula for linear Volterra integral equations as given in
[
33
, p.36, Equation (1.2)]
, Bacry et al.
[
7
]
established the following martingale representation theorem for the intensity process in terms of the compensated point process.

Lemma 2.2
(Martingale representation)

The intensity process
\Lambda
admits the representation

\displaystyle\Lambda(t)\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\mu(t)+\mu*R(t)+\int_{0}^{t}R(t-s)\widetilde{N}(ds),\quad t\geq 0.

(2.3)

Moreover, the expected intensity and the expected number of events are given by, respectively,

\mathbf{E}[\Lambda(t)]=H_{\mu}(t)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathbf{E}[N(t)]=\mathbf{E}[\mathcal{I}_{\Lambda}(t)]=\mathcal{I}_{\mu}(t)+\mu*\mathcal{I}_{R}(t),

where

H_{\mu}(t):=\mu(t)+\mu*R(t)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{I}_{\mu}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}\mu(s)ds.

The first two terms on the right side of (
2.3
) specify the average direct and indirect impact of external events on the arrivals of future events.
The third term specifies the random variations in the number of child events on the arrivals of future events. Integrating both sides of (
2.3
) and then using the stochastic Fubini theorem we see that the integrated intensity can be represented as

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\Lambda}(t)=\mathcal{I}_{\mu}(t)+\mu*\mathcal{I}_{R}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}R(t-s)\widetilde{N}(s)ds,\quad t\geq 0.

2.2
Asymptotic results for resolvents

We proceed to establish various asymptotic results for integrated resolvents that will be key to our analysis of the long-run behavior of Hawkes processes. As we will see the long-run behavior is fully determined by the following quantities:

Contrary to the weakly critical case, strongly dispersed child events render Hawkes processes more stable. In contrast to weakly critical Hawkes processes where the excitation of each event on the future events will be fully released in a short time, for strongly critical processes the release of excitation is much slow and may last much longer. We refer to this phenomena as the loss of self-excitation.

As a result, it becomes increasingly difficult to trace a descendant event back to its mother event. This suggests that the distribution of child events and hence of all events should enjoy some mixing property in the long run in which case both a FLLN and a FCLT should prevail. The following result confirms our intuition; the proof is given in Section 6.

In this section we prove Theorem

,

) under the assumption of the existence of non-continuable solutions to (

Proposition 3.1

For
\nu\in M(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{C}_{-})
and
f\in L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{C}_{-})
, if
(V,T_{\infty})\in L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}([0,T_{\infty});\mathbb{C})\times(0,\infty]
is a non-continuable solution of (
2.5
), then (
2.6
) holds for any
T\in[0,T_{\infty})
.

Proof.
Recall the function
W=e^{V+f}-1-V
.
For each
T\in[0,T_{\infty})
, let
\Lambda_{T}:=\Lambda*d\nu(T)+W*\Lambda(T)
. Taking expectations on both sides of (
2.3
), we see that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}[\Lambda(t)]=H_{\mu}(t)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathbf{E}[\Lambda_{T}]=H_{\mu}*d\nu(T)+W*H_{\mu}(T).

To prove that the Fourier-Laplace functional equals
e^{\mathbf{E}[\Lambda_{T}]}
as claimed, we show that this quantity can be expressed as the initial value of an exponential martingale
\{e^{Z_{T}(t)}:t\in[0,T]\}
. To this end, we first introduce the Doob martingale

\{\Lambda_{T}(t):=\mathbf{E}[\Lambda_{T}|\mathscr{F}_{t}]:t\in[0,T]\}.

Using the martingale representation of the intensity process
\Lambda
established in Lemma
2.2
along with the stochastic Fubini theorem and - in the second step - the fact that

V=R*d\nu+R*W
we obtain that

\displaystyle\Lambda_{T}(t)\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}[\Lambda_{T}]+\int_{0}^{T}W(T-r)\int_{0}^{t\wedge r}R(r-s)\widetilde{N}(ds)dr

(3.2)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\nu(d(T-r))\int_{0}^{t\wedge r}R(r-s)\widetilde{N}(ds)

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}[\Lambda_{T}]+\int_{0}^{t}V(T-s)\widetilde{N}(ds).

(3.3)

Next, we introduce a semi-martingale
\{Z_{T}(t):t\in[0,T]\}
by

\displaystyle Z_{T}(t)\!\!\!

\displaystyle:=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\Lambda_{T}(t)-\int_{0}^{t}W(T-s)\Lambda(s)ds+\int_{0}^{t}f(T-s)N(ds)

(3.4)

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}[\Lambda_{T}]+\int_{0}^{t}(f-W)(T-s)\Lambda(s)ds+\int_{0}^{t}(V+f)(T-s)\widetilde{N}(ds).

(3.5)

Applying Itô’s formula to
\exp\{Z_{T}(t)\}
and then using the fact that
W=e^{V+f}-1-V
, we obtain that

\displaystyle e^{Z_{T}(t)}=e^{\mathbf{E}[\Lambda_{T}]}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{Z_{T}(s-)}\big[e^{(V+f)(T-s)}-1\big]\widetilde{N}(ds),\quad t\in[0,T],

(3.6)

which is a
(\mathscr{F}_{t})
-local martingale. If the local martingale were a true martingale, then the desired result follows from

\displaystyle Z_{T}(T)=\Lambda*d\nu(T)+f*N(T)

by taking expectations on both sides of (
3.6
) with
t=T
. In fact, in this case,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[\exp\big\{\Lambda*d\nu(T)+f*N(T)\big\}\big]=\mathbf{E}\big[\exp\{Z_{T}(T)\}\big]=\exp\big\{\mathbf{E}[\Lambda_{T}]\big\}=\exp\big\{H_{\mu}*d\nu(T)+W*H_{\mu}(T)\big\}.

It hence remains to prove that
\{\exp\{Z_{T}(t)\}:t\in[0,T]\}
is a true
(\mathscr{F}_{t})
-martingale. The proof is similar to those of Lemma 6.8 in
[
72
]
and Lemma 6.3 in
[
1
]
. We first define the process

\displaystyle U_{T}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}\big[e^{(V+f)(T-s)}-1\big]\widetilde{N}(ds),\quad t\geq 0.

In view the local integrability of
H_{\mu}
and the local boundedness of
(V,f)
, this is an
(\mathscr{F}_{t})
-local martingale with quadratic variation

\displaystyle\big[U_{T}\big]_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\big|e^{(V+f)(T-s)}-1\big|^{2}N(ds),\quad t\geq 0.

It follows from the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality (e.g. see Theorem 26.12 in
[
53
, p.524]
) that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|U_{T}(t)|^{2}\Big]\leq C\cdot\mathbf{E}\big[\big[U_{T}\big]_{T}\big]\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle C\cdot\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{E}\big[\Lambda(s)\big]\big|e^{(V+f)(T-s)}-1\big|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle C\cdot\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\big|e^{(V+f)(t)}-1\big|^{2}\cdot\mathcal{I}_{H_{\mu}}(T)<\infty

and hence that
\{U_{T}(t):t\in[0,T]\}
is a locally uniformly square integrable
(\mathscr{F}_{t})
-martingale. Let

\mathcal{E}_{U_{T}}:=\{\mathcal{E}_{U_{T}}(t):t\geq 0\}

be the Doléan-Dade exponential of
U_{T}
. By Itô’s formula,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{U_{T}}(t)=\exp\Big\{\int_{0}^{t}\big(f-W\big)(T-s)\Lambda(s)ds+\int_{0}^{t}(V+f)(T-s)\widetilde{N}(ds)\Big\},\quad t\geq 0.

From this and (
3.4
), we see that

e^{Z_{T}(t)}=e^{\mathbf{E}[\Lambda_{T}]}\mathcal{E}_{U_{T}}(t),\quad t\in[0,T].

Since
\mathcal{E}_{U_{T}}
is a non-negative local martingale it is a super-martingale and so
\mathbf{E}[\mathcal{E}_{U_{T}}(t)]\leq 1
. It hence suffices to prove that

\mathbf{E}[\mathcal{E}_{U_{T}}(t)]=1,\quad t\geq 0.

To this end, we introduce, for each
t_{0}\geq 0
and
n\geq 1
the quantities

\displaystyle\tau_{n}:=\inf\{s\geq 0:\mathcal{I}_{\Lambda}(s)\geq n\}\wedge t_{0}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{E}_{U_{T}}^{(n)}(t):=\mathcal{E}_{U_{T}}(\tau_{n}\wedge t),\quad t\geq 0.

Since
(V,f)
is locally bounded, there exists a constant
C>0
such that uniformly in
t\in[0,T]
the following holds:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{1}_{\{s\leq\tau_{n}\}}\Lambda(s)\big|1-\big(1-(V+f)(T-s)\big)\exp\big\{(V+f)(T-s)\big\}\big|ds\leq C\cdot\int_{0}^{\tau_{n}}\Lambda(s)ds\leq C\cdot n.

Hence, the process
\mathcal{E}_{U_{T}}^{(n)}
is a martingale for each
n\geq 1
, due to Theorem IV.3 in
[
56
]
.
Thus,

\displaystyle\begin{split}1=\mathbf{E}\big[\mathcal{E}_{U_{T}}^{(n)}(t_{0})\big]&=\mathbf{E}\big[\mathcal{E}_{U_{T}}^{(n)}(t_{0});\tau_{n}=t_{0}\big]+\mathbf{E}\big[\mathcal{E}_{U_{T}}^{(n)}(t_{0});\tau_{n}<t_{0}\big]\\
&=\mathbf{E}\big[\mathcal{E}_{U_{T}}(t_{0});\tau_{n}=t_{0}\big]+\mathbf{E}\big[\mathcal{E}_{U_{T}}^{(n)}(\tau_{n});\tau_{n}<t_{0}\big].\end{split}

By the monotone convergence theorem and the fact that
\tau_{n}\to t_{0}
a.s. as
n\to\infty
, we have that

\mathbf{E}\big[\mathcal{E}_{U_{T}}(t_{0});\tau_{n}=t_{0}\big]\to\mathbf{E}\big[\mathcal{E}_{U_{T}}(t_{0})\big]

and so it suffices to prove that
\mathbf{E}[\mathcal{E}_{U_{T}}^{(n)}(\tau_{n});\tau_{n}<t_{0}]\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

To this end, we define a probability law
\mathbf{Q}_{T}^{(n)}
on
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathscr{F}_{t})
by

\frac{d\mathbf{Q}_{T}^{(n)}}{d\mathbf{P}}=\mathcal{E}_{U_{T}}^{(n)}(\tau_{n}).

By the definition of
\tau_{n}
and Chebyshev’s inequality it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[\mathcal{E}_{U_{T}}^{(n)}(\tau_{n});\tau_{n}<t_{0}\big]=\mathbf{Q}_{T}^{(n)}\big(\tau_{n}<t_{0}\big)=\mathbf{Q}_{T}^{(n)}\big(\mathcal{I}_{\Lambda}(t_{0})\geq n\big)\leq\frac{1}{n}\mathbf{E}^{\mathbf{Q}_{T}^{(n)}}\big[\mathcal{I}_{\Lambda}(t_{0})\big]

and hence the desired result holds if we can establish a uniform upper bound on
\mathbf{E}^{\mathbf{Q}_{T}^{(n)}}\big[\mathcal{I}_{\Lambda}(t_{0})\big]
. In what follows we prove that there exists
\beta>0
such that

\sup_{n\geq 1}\mathbf{E}^{\mathbf{Q}_{T}^{(n)}}\big[\mathcal{I}_{\Lambda}(t_{0})\big]\leq 2e^{\beta t_{0}}\mathcal{I}_{H_{\mu}}(t_{0}).

As in
[
41
, Section 2]
, on an extension of the original probability space we can define a time-homogeneous Poisson random measure (PRM)
N_{0}(ds,dz)
on
(0,\infty)^{2}
with intensity
dsdz
such that

\displaystyle N(t)\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\Lambda(s-)}N_{0}(ds,dz)\quad{and}\quad\Lambda(t)=H_{\mu}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\Lambda(s-)}R(t-s)\widetilde{N}_{0}(ds,dz),

(3.7)

for any
t\geq 0
, where
\widetilde{N}_{0}(ds,dz):=N_{0}(ds,dz)-dsdz
denotes the compensated random measure. By Girsanov’s theorem for random measures as stated in, e.g. Theorem 3.17 in
[
47
, p.170]
, the PRM
N_{0}(ds,dz)
is a random point measure under
\mathbf{Q}_{T}^{(n)}
with intensity

\mathbf{1}_{\{s\leq\tau_{n}\}}\cdot\exp\{(V+f)(T-s)\}dsdz.

Moreover, the second equation in (
3.7
) - which holds a.s. under the measure
\mathbf{P}
- induces the following equality in distribution under the measure
\mathbf{Q}_{T}^{(n)}
:

\displaystyle\Lambda(t)\!\!\!

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\!\!\!

\displaystyle H_{\mu}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{1}_{\{s\leq\tau_{n}\}}\Lambda(s)R(t-s)\big(e^{(V+f)(T-s)}-1\big)ds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\Lambda(s-)}R(t-s)\widetilde{N}_{0}(ds,dz),\quad t\geq 0.

Taking expectations on both sides of the above equation and then integrating them over
[0,t]
yields that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{E}^{\mathbf{Q}^{(n)}_{T}}\big[\Lambda(s)\big]ds\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{H_{\mu}}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}dr\int_{0}^{r}\mathbf{E}^{\mathbf{Q}^{(n)}_{T}}\big[\mathbf{1}_{\{s\leq\tau_{n}\}}\Lambda(s)\big]R(r-s)\big(e^{(V+f)(T-s)}-1\big)ds

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{H_{\mu}}(t)+C_{0}\int_{0}^{t}dr\int_{0}^{r}\mathbf{E}^{\mathbf{Q}^{(n)}_{T}}\big[\Lambda(s)\big]R(t-s)ds,

for some constant
C_{0}>0
that is independent of
t
and
n
.
Applying Fubini’s theorem to the term on the left-hand side of the equality and to the double integral on the right-hand side of the inequality and using the fact that
\mathcal{I}_{\Lambda}
is non-decreasing we see that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{\mathbf{Q}^{(n)}_{T}}\big[\mathcal{I}_{\Lambda}(t)\big]\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{H_{\mu}}(t)+C_{0}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{E}^{\mathbf{Q}^{(n)}_{T}}\Big[\int_{0}^{s}\Lambda(r)dr\Big]R(t-s)ds

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{H_{\mu}}(t)+C_{0}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{E}^{\mathbf{Q}^{(n)}_{T}}\big[\mathcal{I}_{\Lambda}(s)\big]R(t-s)ds.

From (
2.1
), for any
\beta>0
such that
\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\beta t}\phi(t)dt\leq\frac{1/2}{1+C_{0}}
we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\beta t}R(t)dt=\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\beta t}\phi(t)dt}{1-\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\beta t}\phi(t)dt}\leq\frac{1}{1+2C_{0}},

and hence

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,t_{0}]}e^{-\beta t}\mathbf{E}^{\mathbf{Q}^{(n)}_{T}}\big[\mathcal{I}_{\Lambda}(t)\big]\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle e^{-\beta t}\mathcal{I}_{H_{\mu}}(t)+C_{0}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\beta s}\mathbf{E}^{\mathbf{Q}^{(n)}_{T}}\big[\mathcal{I}_{\Lambda}(s)\big]e^{-\beta(t-s)}R(t-s)ds

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,t_{0}]}e^{-\beta t}\mathcal{I}_{H_{\mu}}(t)+\sup_{t\in[0,t_{0}]}e^{-\beta t}\mathbf{E}^{\mathbf{Q}^{(n)}_{T}}\big[\mathcal{I}_{\Lambda}(t)\big]\cdot C_{0}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\beta s}R(s)ds

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,t_{0}]}e^{-\beta t}\mathcal{I}_{H_{\mu}}(t)+\frac{1}{2}\cdot\sup_{t\in[0,t_{0}]}e^{-\beta t}\mathbf{E}^{\mathbf{Q}^{(n)}_{T}}\big[\mathcal{I}_{\Lambda}(t)\big],

which yields that

.
Consequently,

\displaystyle\sup_{n\geq 1}\mathbf{E}^{\mathbf{Q}^{(n)}_{T}}\big[\mathcal{I}_{\Lambda}(t_{0})\big]\leq e^{\beta t_{0}}\sup_{n\geq 1}\sup_{t\in[0,t_{0}]}e^{-\beta t}\mathbf{E}^{\mathbf{Q}^{(n)}_{T}}\big[\mathcal{I}_{\Lambda}(t)\big]\leq 2e^{\beta t_{0}}\mathcal{I}_{H_{\mu}}(t_{0}).

\leq&\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\big|\mathbf{E}[F_{n}(X^{(n)}_{f}(t))]-\mathbf{E}[F_{n}(X^{*}_{f}(t))]\big|+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbf{P}(|X^{(n)}_{f}(t)|\geq|\theta_{n}|^{\varrho})+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbf{P}(|X^{*}_{f}(t)|\geq|\theta_{n}|^{\varrho}).\end{split}

(5.60)

By Chebyshev’s inequality and the power inequality, there exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbf{P}(|X^{(n)}_{f}(t)|\geq|\theta_{n}|^{\varrho})\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\theta_{n}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbf{E}[|X^{(n)}_{f}(t)|^{1/\varrho}]

(5.61)

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\theta_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbf{E}\big[|f^{i}*X^{(n)}(t)|^{1/\varrho}\big]

(5.62)

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\theta_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbf{E}\big[|(f_{1}^{i}+\mathcal{I}_{f_{2}^{i}}+f_{3}^{i})*\Lambda^{(n)}(t)|^{1/\varrho}\big]

(5.64)

\displaystyle+\frac{C}{\theta_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbf{E}\big[|(f_{3}^{i}/n+f_{4}^{i})*d\widetilde{N}^{(n)}(t)|^{1/\varrho}\big].

The boundedness of
f^{i}
yields a constant
C>0
that is independent of
n\geq 1
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbf{E}\big[|(f_{1}^{i}+\mathcal{I}_{f_{2}^{i}}+f_{3}^{i})*\Lambda^{(n)}(t)|^{1/\varrho}\big]\leq C\mathbf{E}\big[|\mathcal{I}_{\Lambda^{(n)}}(T)|^{1/\varrho}\big].

Moreover, by Theorem D.1 in
[
72
]
and the fact that
\|f^{i}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}\leq K
,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbf{E}\big[|(f_{3}^{i}/n+f_{4}^{i})*d\widetilde{N}^{(n)}(t)|^{\frac{1}{\varrho}}\big]\leq C\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbf{E}\Big[\Big|\int_{0}^{t}(f_{3}^{i}/n+f_{4}^{i})^{2}(t-s)N^{(n)}(ds)\Big|^{\frac{1}{2\varrho}}\big]\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle C\mathbf{E}\big[|N^{(n)}(T)|^{\frac{1}{2\varrho}}\big],

for some constant
C>0
independent of
n\geq 1
.
Taking these estimates back into (
5.61
) and the using Proposition
5.5
, we have as
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbf{P}(|X^{(n)}_{f}(t)|\geq|\theta_{n}|^{\varrho})\leq C/\theta_{n}\to 0.

Similarly, one also can prove that
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbf{P}(|X^{*}_{f}(t)|\geq|\theta_{n}|^{\varrho})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.
To obtain (
5.58
) it thus remains to prove that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\big|\mathbf{E}[F_{n}(X^{(n)}_{f}(t))]-\mathbf{E}[F_{n}(X^{*}_{f}(t))]\big|\leq C\cdot|\theta_{n}|^{-\kappa},

(5.65)

uniformly in
n\geq 1
,
F\in\mathcal{H}_{\rm Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R})
and
f^{1},f^{2}\in\mathcal{A}_{K}^{\circ}
. This can be achieved by employing Fourier analysis.
Using the Fourier inversion theorem and then Fubini’s theorem, we see that
F_{n}=\mathcal{F}\overline{\mathcal{F}F_{n}}
and that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}[F_{n}(X_{f}^{(n)}(t))]\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{\mathtt{i}\langle z,X_{f}^{(n)}(t)\rangle}\overline{\mathcal{F}F_{n}}(z)dz\Big]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbf{E}[e^{\mathtt{i}\langle z,X_{f}^{(n)}(t)\rangle}]\cdot\overline{\mathcal{F}F_{n}}(z)dz,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}[F_{n}(X_{f}^{*}(t))]\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{\mathtt{i}\langle z,X_{f}^{*}(t)\rangle}\overline{\mathcal{F}F_{n}}(z)dz\Big]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbf{E}[e^{\mathtt{i}\langle z,X_{f}^{*}(t)\rangle}]\cdot\overline{\mathcal{F}F_{n}}(z)dz.

For a constant
\delta\in(0,1)
to be specified later, it is straightforward to verify that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\big|\mathbf{E}[F_{n}(X^{(n)}_{f}(t))]-\mathbf{E}[F_{n}(X^{*}_{f}(t))]\big|\leq\|\varepsilon^{(n)}_{f,1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}+\|\varepsilon^{(n)}_{f,2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}},

(5.66)

where

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{(n)}_{f,1}(t)\!\!\!

\displaystyle:=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\int_{|z|\leq\delta\theta_{n}}\Big(\mathbf{E}[e^{\mathtt{i}\langle z,X_{f}^{(n)}(t)\rangle}]-\mathbf{E}[e^{\mathtt{i}\langle z,X_{f}^{*}(t)\rangle}]\Big)\overline{\mathcal{F}F_{n}}(z)dz,

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{(n)}_{f,2}(t)\!\!\!

\displaystyle:=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\int_{|z|>\delta\theta_{n}}\Big(\mathbf{E}[e^{\mathtt{i}\langle z,X_{f}^{(n)}(t)\rangle}]-\mathbf{E}[e^{\mathtt{i}\langle z,X_{f}^{*}(t)\rangle}]\Big)\overline{\mathcal{F}F_{n}}(z)dz.

We estimate the above terms separately, starting with the second one. Applying the estimate (
5.59
) with
z_{0}=\theta_{n}
to
\varepsilon^{(n)}_{f,2}(t)
, yields a constant
C>0
such that for any
f\in\mathcal{A}_{K}
and
F\in\mathcal{H}_{\rm Lip}
,

\displaystyle\|\varepsilon^{(n)}_{f,2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}\leq 2\int_{|z|>\delta\theta_{n}}\big|\overline{\mathcal{F}F_{n}}(z)\big|dz\leq C\cdot\frac{1+|\theta_{n}|^{\varrho}}{(1+\theta_{n})^{1/2}}\leq C\cdot(1+\theta_{n})^{\varrho-1/2}\leq C\cdot\theta_{n}^{-\kappa}.

(5.67)

Estimating the first term is more involved. We start with the following representation of the exponents.
For any
z:=(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, let
f_{z}:=\mathtt{i}z_{1}f^{1}+\mathtt{i}z_{2}f^{2}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathtt{i}\mathbb{R}^{4})
.
It follows from (
5.57
) that

\displaystyle\mathtt{i}\langle z,X_{f}^{(n)}\rangle=f_{z}*X^{(n)}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathtt{i}\langle z,X_{f}^{*}\rangle=f_{z}*X^{*}.

Moreover, it is easy to verify that uniformly in
f^{1},f^{2}\in\mathcal{A}^{\circ}_{K}
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

\displaystyle\|f_{z}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}+\|f^{\prime}_{z}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}\leq CK\cdot|z|.

(5.68)

Next, let
V^{(n)}_{z}\in L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{C}_{-})
, respectively,
V^{*}_{z}\in L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{C}_{-})
be the unique solution to the Volterra equation (
5.11
), respectively, and (
2.17
) associated with the measure

w(dt)=(f_{z,1}+f_{z,3}+\mathcal{I}_{f_{z,2}})(t)dt

and the respectively functions

g=f_{z,3}/n+f_{z,4}\quad\mbox{and}\quad g=f_{z,4}.

From (
5.54
) and (
5.10
), respectively, (
5.55
) and (
2.16
), it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[e^{\mathtt{i}\langle z,X_{f}^{(n)}(t)\rangle}\big]\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\exp\Big\{\frac{\mu_{0}}{n}\big(\mathcal{I}_{f_{z,1}+\mathcal{I}_{f_{z,2}}+f_{z,3}}(t)+\mathcal{I}_{W^{(n)}_{z}}(t)\big)+\mu_{0}\mathcal{I}_{V^{(n)}_{z}}(t)\Big\},\quad t\geq 0.

where

and that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[e^{\mathtt{i}\langle z,X_{f}^{*}(t)\rangle}\big]=\exp\big\{\mu_{0}\cdot\mathcal{I}_{V^{*}_{z}}(t)\big\},\quad t\geq 0.

Taking these two representations back into
\varepsilon^{(n)}_{f,1}(t)
, we see that
\varepsilon^{(n)}_{f,1}(t)=\varepsilon^{(n)}_{f,1,1}(t)+\varepsilon^{(n)}_{f,1,2}(t)
with

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{(n)}_{f,1,1}(t)\!\!\!

\displaystyle:=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\int_{|z|\leq\delta\theta_{n}}\Big(\exp\Big\{\frac{\mu_{0}}{n}\big(\mathcal{I}_{f_{z,1}+f_{z,3}+\mathcal{I}_{f_{z,2}}}(t)+\mathcal{I}_{W^{(n)}_{z}}(t)\big)\Big\}-1\Big)\cdot\exp\big\{\mu_{0}\mathcal{I}_{V^{(n)}_{z}}(t)\big\}\overline{\mathcal{F}F_{n}}(z)dz,

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{(n)}_{f,1,2}(t)\!\!\!

\displaystyle:=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\int_{|z|\leq\delta\theta_{n}}\Big(\exp\big\{\mu_{0}\mathcal{I}_{V^{(n)}_{z}}(t)\big\}-\exp\big\{\mu_{0}\cdot\mathcal{I}_{V^{*}_{z}}(t)\big\}\Big)\overline{\mathcal{F}F_{n}}(z)dz.

It remains to prove that the above terms of both of order
o(1/\theta_{n})
as
n\to\infty
.
Since
{\tt Re}f_{z}=0
and
{\tt Re}V^{(n)}_{z},{\tt Re}W^{(n)}_{z}\leq 0
, and since
|e^{x}-1|\leq|x|
for any
x\in\mathbb{C}_{-}
the first terms admits the pointwise estimate

\displaystyle|\varepsilon^{(n)}_{f,1,1}(t)|\leq\frac{\mu_{0}}{n}\int_{|z|\leq\delta\theta_{n}}\big|\mathcal{I}_{f_{z,1}+f_{z,3}+\mathcal{I}_{f_{z,2}}}(t)+\mathcal{I}_{W^{(n)}_{z}}(t)\big|\cdot\big|\overline{\mathcal{F}F_{n}}(z)\big|dz.

Moreover, by Lemma
5.2
(2) and (
5.68
), there exists a constant
C>0
such that
\|W^{(n)}_{z}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}\leq CK\cdot|z|
for any
n\geq 1
,
z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
f^{1},f^{2}\in\mathcal{A}_{K}
.
Since
\|\mathcal{F}F_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|F_{n}\|_{L^{1}}\leq 4(|\theta_{n}|^{\varrho}+4)^{2}
, we conclude that as

,

\displaystyle\|\varepsilon^{(n)}_{f,1,1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}\leq\frac{C}{n}\int_{|z|\leq\delta\theta_{n}}(1+|z|)\cdot\big|\mathcal{F}F_{n}(z)\big|dz\leq C\cdot\frac{|\theta_{n}|^{2+2\varrho}+1}{n}=o(1/\theta_{n}).

(5.69)

We proceed with the second term. Since
\mathcal{I}_{V^{(n)}_{z}}(t),\mathcal{I}_{V^{*}_{z}}(t)\in\mathbb{C}_{-}
and
|e^{x}-e^{y}|\leq|x-y|
uniformly in

,

\displaystyle\|\varepsilon^{(n)}_{f,1,2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\mu_{0}\int_{|z|\leq\delta\theta_{n}}\big\|\mathcal{I}_{V^{(n)}_{z}}-\mathcal{I}_{V^{*}_{z}}\big\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}\cdot\big|\overline{\mathcal{F}F_{n}}(z)\big|dz\leq\mu_{0}\cdot T\cdot\int_{|z|\leq\delta\theta_{n}}\big\|V^{(n)}_{z}-V^{*}_{z}\big\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}\cdot\big|\overline{\mathcal{F}F_{n}}(z)\big|dz.

By Lemma
5.7
(1) and (
5.68
), there exist a constant
C_{0}>0
independent of
\delta
such that for any
n\geq 1
,
z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

and

,

\displaystyle\|V^{(n)}_{z}-V^{*}_{z}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}\leq C_{0}e^{C_{0}K\cdot|z|}e^{-\theta_{n}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|\varepsilon^{(n)}_{f,1,2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}\leq C_{0}e^{-\theta_{n}}\cdot\int_{|z|\leq\delta\theta_{n}}e^{C_{0}K\cdot|z|}\cdot\big|\mathcal{F}F_{n}(z)\big|dz.

It is not difficult to show that

\displaystyle\|\varepsilon^{(n)}_{f,1,2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}\leq C_{0}e^{(C_{0}K\delta-1)\theta_{n}}\cdot 4|\delta\theta_{n}|^{2}\cdot\|\mathcal{F}F_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Since the support of

belongs to
\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|z|\leq|\theta_{n}|^{\varrho}+4\}
, we also have that

\|\mathcal{F}F_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|F_{n}\|_{L^{1}}\leq 4(|\theta_{n}|^{\varrho}+4)^{2}.

Combining all estimates and then choosing

, we see that as

\displaystyle\|\varepsilon^{(n)}_{f,1,2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}\leq C_{0}\cdot 4|\delta\theta_{n}|^{2}\cdot 4(|\theta_{n}|^{\varrho}+4)^{2}\cdot e^{(C_{0}K\delta-1)\theta_{n}}=o(1/\theta_{n}).

Taking this together with (
5.67
) and (
5.69
) backing into (
5.66
), the desired inequality (
5.65
) follows.

Proposition 5.9

h_{\beta}(t)=\mathcal{F}\overline{\mathcal{F}h_{\beta}}(t)

Lemma 6.1

For any

It has been shown in Remark

Lemma 6.2

, we may assume
\widetilde{N}^{(n)}_{R}\to G
a.s. and uniformly on compact sets. As argued in the proof of Lemma
6.2
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\Lambda}(nt)-\mathbf{E}[\mathcal{I}_{\Lambda}(nt)]=\int_{0}^{t}R(n(t-s))\tilde{N}(ns)ds

and

\displaystyle n|\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(n)|^{-3/2}\cdot\big(\mathcal{I}_{\Lambda}(nt)-\mathbf{E}[\mathcal{I}_{\Lambda}(nt)]\big)\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{n^{2}}{\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(n)}\int_{0}^{t}R(n(t-s))\widetilde{N}^{(n)}_{R}(s)ds

(6.3)

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{n^{2}}{\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(n)}\int_{0}^{t}R(n(t-s))\big(\widetilde{N}^{(n)}_{R}(s)-G(s)\big)ds

(6.5)

\displaystyle+\frac{n^{2}}{\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(n)}\int_{0}^{t}R(ns)G(t-s)ds.

In view of (
2.12
), the first term on the right-hand side of the second equality can be bounded uniformly in
t\in[0,T]
by

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{N}^{(n)}_{R}-G\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}\cdot n\mathcal{I}_{R}(nT)/\mathcal{I}_{R}^{2}(n)\leq C\cdot\|\widetilde{N}^{(n)}_{R}-G\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}

that tends to
0
as
n\to\infty
. Let us then turn to the last term in (
6.3
), which we denote by
M^{(n)}
for convenience. It remains to prove the weak convergence of
M^{(n)}
to the right-hand side of (
6.2
).

To prove the tightness of the
\{M^{(n)}\}_{n\geq 1}
we fix an
(\mathscr{F}_{nt})
-stopping time
\tau\in[0,T]
and some
\delta\in(0,1)
. By (
2.12
) we have that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[|M^{(n)}(\tau+\delta)-M^{(n)}(\tau)|\big]\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{n^{2}}{\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(n)}\int_{0}^{T}R(ns)\mathbf{E}\big[|G(\tau+\delta-s)-G(\tau-s)|\big]ds

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{n}{\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(n)}\int_{0}^{nT}R(s)ds\cdot\mathbf{E}[\zeta(\delta)]

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{n\mathcal{I}_{R}(nT)}{\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(n)}\cdot\mathbf{E}[\zeta(\delta)]

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle C\cdot\mathbf{E}[\zeta(\delta)],

for some constant
C>0
that is independent of
(n,\tau)
, where

\zeta(\delta):=\sup_{s,t\in[0,T+1],|t-s|\leq\delta}|G(t)-G(s)|.

Notice that
\sup_{\delta\in(0,1)}\zeta(\delta)\leq 2\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|G(t)|
and that the continuous martingale
G
has predictable quadratic variation
\langle G\rangle_{t}=\mu_{0}(\alpha+1)\int_{0}^{t}s^{\alpha}ds
.
By the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality; see Theorem 17.7 in
[
53
, p.333]
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\Big[\sup_{\delta\in(0,1)}\zeta(\delta)\Big]\leq 2\cdot\mathbf{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|G(t)|\Big]\leq C\int_{0}^{T}s^{\alpha}ds=\frac{C}{1+\alpha}\cdot T^{\alpha+1}.

By the dominated convergence theorem and the locally uniform continuity of
G
it follows that

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0+}\mathbf{E}[\zeta(\delta)]=\mathbf{E}\Big[\lim_{\delta\to 0+}\zeta(\delta)\Big]=0.

The desired tightness of
\{M^{(n)}\}_{n\geq 1}
thus follows from Aldous’s criterion; see
[
4
]
.

To characterize the limit processes, we introduce the following two
\sigma
-finite measures on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
:

\displaystyle\nu_{n}(ds):=n^{2}/\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(n)\cdot R(ns)ds\quad\mbox{and}\quad\nu_{*}(ds):=\alpha s^{\alpha-1}ds.

Then,

M^{(n)}=G*\nu_{n}=G*\nu_{*}+G*(\nu_{n}-\nu_{*}).

From Proposition
2.6
(3) and the locally uniform continuity of
G
, for any
t\geq 0
, we know that
\nu_{n}\to\nu_{*}
weakly on
(0,t]
and hence
G*(\nu_{n}-\nu_{*})(t)\to 0
a.s. as
n\to\infty
.
As a result,

M^{(n)}(t)\to G*\nu_{*}(t)\quad\mbox{a.s. for any $t\geq 0$}

and by the stochastic Fubini lemma,

\displaystyle G*\nu_{*}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha}dG(s)=\sqrt{\mu_{0}(\alpha+1)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha}s^{\alpha/2}dB(s).

\Box

Proof of Theorem
2.14
.

From the martingale representation theorem (Lemma
2.2
), we deduce that

\displaystyle n|\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(n)|^{-3/2}\cdot\big(N(nt)-\mathbf{E}[N(nt)]\big)\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{n\cdot\tilde{N}^{(n)}_{R}(t)}{\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(n)}+\frac{n}{|\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(n)|^{3/2}}\cdot\big(\mathcal{I}_{\Lambda}(nt)-\mathbf{E}[\mathcal{I}_{\Lambda}(nt)]\big).

The second statement hence follows from Lemma
6.3
and
6.4
.
Moreover, by Lemma
6.1
,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\big|N(nt)/\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(n)-\mu_{0}\cdot t^{\alpha+1}\big|\sim\frac{|\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(n)|^{1/2}}{n}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big|\frac{n}{|\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(n)|^{3/2}}\cdot\big(N(nt)-\mathbf{E}[N(nt)]\big)\Big|,

as
n\to\infty
and so the first statement follows directly from the second along with (
2.12
).

\Box

Proof of Theorem
2.16
.

By the martingale representation theorem (Lemma
2.2
), we rewrite our normalized process as follows:

\displaystyle\gamma(n)\Big(\frac{N(nt)}{\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(n)}-\mu_{0}\cdot t^{\alpha+1}\Big)\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma(n)}{\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(n)}\big(N(nt)-\mathbf{E}[N(nt)]\big)+\gamma(n)\Big(\frac{\mathbf{E}[N(nt)]}{\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(n)}-\mu_{0}\cdot t^{\alpha+1}\Big)

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\gamma(n)\frac{n}{\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(n)}\cdot\mu_{0}t+\mu_{0}\cdot\gamma(n)A(n)\cdot\frac{1}{A(n)}\Big(\frac{\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(nt)}{\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(n)}-t^{\alpha+1}\Big)

\displaystyle+\gamma(n)\frac{|\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(n)|^{1/2}}{n}\cdot\frac{n}{|\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(n)|^{3/2}}\cdot\big(N(nt)-\mathbf{E}[N(nt)]\big).

By assumption and Theorem
2.14
(2), the first and third terms on the right side of the last equality converge to the corresponding limits uniformly on compact sets.

As for the second term, the assumptions
\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(n)\in{\rm 2RV}_{\alpha+1,\rho}^{\infty}(A)
and
\gamma(n)A(n)\to\gamma_{2}^{*}
imply that for any
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\Big|\mu_{0}\cdot\gamma(n)A(n)\cdot\frac{1}{A(n)}\Big(\frac{\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(nt)}{\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(n)}-t^{\alpha+1}\Big)-\mu_{0}\cdot\gamma_{2}^{*}\cdot t^{\alpha+1}\int_{1}^{t}u^{\rho-1}du\Big|\to 0,

(6.6)

as
n\to\infty
.
It remains to prove that this convergence also holds uniformly in
t\in[0,T]
for any
T>0
.

Case I.
If
-\alpha-1<\rho\leq 0
, then a simple calculation shows that
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}t^{\alpha+1}\int_{1}^{t}u^{\rho-1}du<\infty
and there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
n
and
t
such that the left-hand side of (
6.6
) can be bounded by

\displaystyle C\cdot\big|\gamma(n)A(n)-\gamma_{2}^{*}\big|+C\cdot\Big|\frac{1}{A(n)}\Big(\frac{\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(nt)}{\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(n)}-t^{\alpha+1}\Big)-t^{\alpha+1}\int_{1}^{t}u^{\rho-1}du\Big|.

By assumption (
2.24
) the first term vanishes as
n\to\infty
. The second term can bounded as follows.
By Corollary 16.3.3 in
[
58
]
, for
\epsilon\in(0,1)
and
\delta\in(0,\alpha+\rho+1)
,
there exists a constant
x_{0}>0
such that for any
n\geq 1
and
t\in[x_{0}/n,T]
,

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{1}{A(n)}\Big(\frac{\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(nt)}{\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(n)}-t^{\alpha+1}\Big)-t^{\alpha+1}\int_{1}^{t}u^{\rho-1}du\Big|\leq C\cdot t^{\alpha+1}\cdot\big(t^{\rho-\delta}\vee t^{\rho+\delta}\big)\cdot\epsilon,

(6.7)

which is bounded by
C(T^{\alpha+\rho+\delta+1}\vee 1)\cdot\epsilon
uniformly in
t\in[x_{0}/n,T]
.
Since

\displaystyle A(n)\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(n)\to\infty\quad\mbox{and}\quad n^{\alpha+1}A(n)\to\infty,

we also have as
n\to\infty
that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,x_{0}/n]}\Big|\frac{1}{A(n)}\Big(\frac{\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(nt)}{\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(n)}-t^{\alpha+1}\Big)-t^{\alpha+1}\int_{1}^{t}u^{\rho-1}du\Big|\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{C}{A(n)\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(n)}+\frac{C}{n^{2-\alpha}A(n)}+\frac{C\log n}{n^{2-\alpha}A(n)}\to 0.

Putting these two results together and then letting
\epsilon\to 0+
, we deduce that (
6.6
) holds uniformly on any compact set.

Case II.
If
\rho\leq-\alpha-1
, then using that
n|\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(n)|^{-1/2}\in{\rm RV}_{(1-\alpha)/2}^{\infty}
and
\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(n)/n\in{\rm RV}_{\alpha}^{\infty}
, we have as
n\to\infty
that

\displaystyle n|\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(n)|^{-1/2}+\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(n)/n=o(1/A(n))\quad\mbox{and}\quad\gamma(n)\in{\rm RV}_{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}\wedge\alpha}^{\infty},

from which we deduce that
\gamma^{*}_{2}=0
.
Additionally, by (
6.7
), there exits a constant
C>0
such for any
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[x_{0}/n,T]}\frac{1}{A(n)}\Big|\frac{\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(nt)}{\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(n)}-t^{\alpha+1}\Big|\leq\frac{C}{n^{\alpha+\rho-\delta+1}}.

Since
\gamma(n)A(n)/n^{\alpha+\rho-\delta+1}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, we see that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{t\in[x_{0}/n,T]}\gamma(n)\Big|\frac{\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(nt)}{\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(n)}-t^{\alpha+1}\Big|=0.

On the other hand, there exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,x_{0}/n]}\gamma(n)\Big|\frac{\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(nt)}{\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(n)}-t^{\alpha+1}\Big|\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle C\Big(\frac{\gamma(n)}{\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R}(n)}+\frac{\gamma(n)}{n^{\alpha+1}}\Big),

which also goes to
0
as
n\to\infty
.
Putting these two estimates together, one can see that (
6.6
) holds uniformly on any compact set.

\Box

APPENDIX

Appendix A
Distance between finite measures

This section recalls several well-known distances on space of finite measures.
A collection of Borel-measurable
\mathbb{C}
-valued functions on a topological space
(\mathbb{E},\mathscr{E})
, denoted by
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{E})
, is said to be
separating
if any two finite measures
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}
on
\mathbb{E}
are equal if
\nu_{1}(f)=\nu_{2}(f)
for any
f\in\mathcal{S}
. Examples of separating classes include the sets
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{E})=\{f:f\mbox{ is a bounded and measureable function on }\mathbb{E}\}
,
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{E})=\{f:f\in C_{b}(\mathbb{E},\mathbb{R})\}
and
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\{e^{\mathtt{i}\langle\lambda,\cdot\rangle}:\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
if
\mathbb{E}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Definition A.1

For a separating class
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{E})
and two finite measures
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}
on
\mathbb{E}
satisfying that
|\nu_{1}(f)|+|\nu_{2}(f)|<\infty
for any
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{E})
, the
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{E})
-distance
between
\nu_{1}
and
\nu_{2}
is given by

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{E})}(\nu_{1},\nu_{2}):=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{E})}\big|\nu_{1}(f)-\nu_{2}(f)\big|.

(A.1)

Due to the one-to-one corresponding between measures on
\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{C})
-distance between two measures on
\mathbb{C}
is equivalent to the
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
-distance between the two corresponding measures on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Specially, for some
n\geq 1
, if finite the measures
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
have density functions
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}
, then we also write
d_{\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})
for
d_{\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})
for convention.
We now recall several well-known distances on the space of finite measures on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

\bullet

d_{\rm Kol}(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})
: the
Kolmogorov distance
of two finite measures
\nu_{1}
and
\nu_{2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is obtained from (
A.1
) by taking
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to be the class of all functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
of the type
f(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{1}_{(-,\infty,x_{i}]}(z_{i})
with
(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle d_{\rm Kol}(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})\!\!\!

\displaystyle:=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\sup_{(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big|\nu_{1}\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{n}(-\infty,x_{i}]\Big)-\nu_{2}\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{n}(-\infty,x_{i}]\Big)\Big|.

(A.2)

\bullet

d_{\rm W}(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})
: the
Wasserstein distance
of two finite measures
\nu_{1}
and
\nu_{2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is obtained from (
A.1
) by taking
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\mathcal{H}_{\rm Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})
that is the class of Lipschitz continuous functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
with Lipschitz constant
\|f\|_{\rm Lip}\leq 1
, where

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\rm Lip}:=\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},x\neq y}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|z-y|}.

\bullet

d_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})
: the
k
-smooth Wasserstein distance
of two finite measures
\nu_{1}
and
\nu_{2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
is obtained from (
A.1
) by taking
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\mathcal{H}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})
that is the class of functions
f\in C^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})
with
\|\frac{\partial^{i}}{\partial x^{i}}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1
for
i=0,1,\cdots,k
,

As a direct consequence of Proposition 1.2(2) in
[
66
]
and Proposition 2.1 in
[
31
]
, the next proposition deduces the Kolmogorov distance between two finite measures on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in terms of their Wasserstein distance and their
k
-smooth Wasserstein distance.

Proposition A.2

Let
\nu_{1}
be a finite measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with bounded density function.
For any
K\geq 1
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that for any finite measure
\nu_{2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\nu_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\leq K
,

\displaystyle d_{\rm Kol}(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})\leq C\cdot\min\Big\{\big(d_{\rm W}(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})\big)^{1/2},\big(d_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})\big)^{\frac{1}{k+1}}\Big\}.

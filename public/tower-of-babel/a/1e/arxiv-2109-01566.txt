Scalar Gaussian Wiretap Channel: Properties of the Support Size of the Secrecy-Capacity-Achieving Distribution
Luca Barletta ∗ , Alex Dytso ∗∗

Abstract
This work studies the secrecy-capacity of a scalar-Gaussian wiretap channel with an amplitude constraint on the input. It is known that for this channel, the secrecy-capacity-achieving distribution is discrete with finitely many points. This work improves such result by showing an upper bound of the order 𝖠 σ 1 2 \frac{\mathsf{A}}{\sigma_{1}^{2}} where 𝖠 \mathsf{A} is the amplitude constraint and σ 1 2 \sigma_{1}^{2} is the variance of the Gaussian noise over the legitimate channel.

I
Introduction

Consider the Gaussian wiretap channel with outputs

\displaystyle Y_{1}

\displaystyle=X+N_{1},

(1)

\displaystyle Y_{2}

\displaystyle=X+N_{2},

(2)

where
N_{1}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{1}^{2})
and
N_{2}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{2}^{2})
, and with
(X,N_{1},N_{2})
independent of each other. The output
Y_{1}
is observed by the legitimate receiver whereas the output
Y_{2}
is observed by the malicious receiver. In this work, we assume that the input
X
is limited by a peak-power constraint or amplitude constraint given by
|X|\leq\mathsf{A}
.
For this setting, the secrecy-capacity is given by

\displaystyle C_{s}(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\mathsf{A})

\displaystyle=\max_{P_{X}:|X|\leq\mathsf{A}}I(X;Y_{1})-I(X;Y_{2})

(3)

\displaystyle=\max_{P_{X}:|X|\leq\mathsf{A}}I(X;Y_{1}|Y_{2}).

(4)

We are interested in studying the input distribution
P_{X^{\star}}
that maximizes (
4
). It can be shown that for
\sigma_{1}^{2}\geq\sigma^{2}_{2}
the secrecy-capacity is equal to zero. Therefore, in the remaining, we assume that
\sigma_{1}^{2}<\sigma^{2}_{2}
.

Literature Review

The wiretap channel was introduced by Wyner in
[
1
]
, who also established the secrecy-capacity of the degraded wiretap channel. The wiretap channel plays a central role in network information theory; the interested reader is referred to
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
and reference therein for an in-detail treatment of the topic.

The secrecy-capacity of a Gaussian wiretap channel with an average power constraint was shown by Leung and Hellman in
[
6
]
where the secrecy-capacity-achieving input distribution was shown to be Gaussian. The secrecy-capacity of Gaussian wiretap channel with an amplitude and power constraint was considered by Ozel et al. in
[
7
]
where the author showed that the secrecy-capacity-achieving input distribution is discrete with finitely many points. The work of
[
7
]
was extended to noise-dependent channels by Soltani and Rezki in
[
8
]
. For further studies of the properties of secrecy-capacity-achieving input distribution for a class of degraded wiretap channels, the interested reader is referred to
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
.

The classical approach for demonstrating that the secrecy-capacity-achieving distributions are discrete relies on an analytic argument introduced to information theory by Smith in
[
12
]
. The drawback of this technique is that it does not provide any bounds on the support size of the secrecy-capacity-achieving distribution and only asserts that the support is countable. In this work, instead of following the approach of
[
12
]
, we follow the approach introduced in
[
13
]
, which relies on the
variation diminishing
property
[
14
]
.

This work has two goals.
The first goal is to sharpen the results of
[
7
]
by establishing a firm upper bound on the number of points in the support of the secrecy-capacity-achieving distribution. The second goal is to study the necessary techniques required to extend the method introduced in
[
13
]
to network information theory problems. The wiretap channel serves as an ideal first test candidate in this research program.

\displaystyle{\mathsf{supp}}(P_{X})

\displaystyle=\{x:\text{ for every open set $\mathcal{D}\ni x$ }

\displaystyle\quad\qquad\text{
we have that $P_{X}(\mathcal{D})>0$}\}.

(5)

The relative entropy between distributions
P

Main Result

In this section, we state our main results. We first present the following ancillary lemma the first part of which was shown in

Lemma 1
.

P_{X^{\star}}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{D}(f_{Y_{1}|X}(\cdot|x)\|f_{Y_{1}^{\star}})-\mathsf{D}(f_{Y_{2}|X}(\cdot|x)\|f_{Y_{2}^{\star}})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[g(Y_{1})|X=x\right]+\log\left(\frac{\sigma_{2}}{\sigma_{1}}\right),

(9)

The first part of Lemma

\displaystyle\mathsf{D}(f_{Y_{1}|X}(\cdot|x)\|f_{Y_{1}^{\star}})-\mathsf{D}(f_{Y_{2}|X}(\cdot|x)\|f_{Y_{2}^{\star}})-\log\left(\frac{\sigma_{2}}{\sigma_{1}}\right)

(11)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\log\frac{1}{f_{Y_{1}^{\star}}(y)}\phi_{\sigma_{1}}(y-x){\rm d}y

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\log\frac{1}{f_{Y_{2}^{\star}}(y)}\mathbb{E}[\phi_{\sigma_{1}}(y-x-N)]{\rm d}y

(12)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\log\frac{1}{f_{Y_{1}^{\star}}(y)}\phi_{\sigma_{1}}(y-x){\rm d}y

(13)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\log\frac{f_{Y_{2}^{\star}}(y+N)}{f_{Y_{1}^{\star}}(y)}\right]\phi_{\sigma_{1}}(y-x){\rm d}y

The main result of this paper is summarized in the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

For
\mathsf{A}>0

\displaystyle|{\mathsf{supp}}(P_{X^{\star}})|\leq\mathrm{N}\left([-R,R],g(\cdot)+\kappa_{1}\right)<\infty

(16)

where

\displaystyle\kappa_{1}

\displaystyle=\log\left(\frac{\sigma_{2}}{\sigma_{1}}\right)-C_{s},

(17)

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle=\mathsf{A}\frac{\sigma_{2}+\sigma_{1}}{\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1}}+\sqrt{\frac{\frac{\sigma_{2}^{2}-\sigma_{1}^{2}}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}+2C_{s}}{\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}-\frac{1}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}}}.

where

Proof of the bound in (

The function

Lemma 2
.

Let

\bar{C}_{s}=\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{1+\frac{\mathsf{A}^{2}}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}}{1+\frac{\mathsf{A}^{2}}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}}\right).

Then,

C_{s}\leq\bar{C}_{s}.

(21)

Moreover, there exists some

\displaystyle\mathrm{N}\left(\mathbb{R},g(\cdot)+\log\left(\frac{\sigma_{2}}{\sigma_{1}}\right)-C_{s}\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\mathrm{N}\left([-R,R],g(\cdot)+\log\left(\frac{\sigma_{2}}{\sigma_{1}}\right)-C_{s}\right)<\infty.

(22)

Furthermore,
R
can be upper-bounded as follows:

R\leq\mathsf{A}d_{1}+d_{2}

(23)

where

\displaystyle d_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma_{2}+\sigma_{1}}{\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1}},

(24)

\displaystyle d_{2}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{\frac{\sigma_{2}^{2}-\sigma_{1}^{2}}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}+2C_{s}}{\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}-\frac{1}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\frac{\sigma_{2}^{2}-\sigma_{1}^{2}}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}+2\bar{C}_{s}}{\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}-\frac{1}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}}}.

(25)

Proof.

First, note that

\displaystyle C_{s}

\displaystyle=\max_{P_{X}:\>|X|\leq\mathsf{A}}I(X;Y_{1}|Y_{2})

(26)

\displaystyle=\max_{P_{X}:\>|X|\leq\mathsf{A},\,\mathbb{E}[X^{2}]\leq\mathsf{A}^{2}}I(X;Y_{1}|Y_{2})

\displaystyle\leq\max_{P_{X}:\>\mathbb{E}[X^{2}]\leq\mathsf{A}^{2}}I(X;Y_{1}|Y_{2}).

(28)

The last expression is the secrecy-capacity of a Gaussian wiretap channel with an average power constraint, which is given in (
20
).

Second, for
|y|\geq\mathsf{A}
, we can lower-bound the function
g
as follows:

\displaystyle g(y)=\mathbb{E}\left[\log f_{Y_{2}^{\star}}(y+N)\right]-\log f_{Y_{1}^{\star}}(y)

(29)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\log\mathbb{E}[\phi_{\sigma_{2}}(y+N-X^{\star})|N]\right]-\log\mathbb{E}[\phi_{\sigma_{1}}(y-X^{\star})]

(30)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}\left[\log\phi_{\sigma_{2}}(y+N-X^{\star})\right]-\log\mathbb{E}[\phi_{\sigma_{1}}(y-X^{\star})]

(31)

\displaystyle\geq\log\frac{\sigma_{1}}{\sigma_{2}}-\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{(y+N-X^{\star})^{2}}{2\sigma_{2}^{2}}\right]+\frac{(|y|-\mathsf{A})^{2}}{2\sigma_{1}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\log\frac{\sigma_{1}}{\sigma_{2}}-\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{(y-X^{\star})^{2}}{2\sigma_{2}^{2}}\right]-\frac{\sigma_{2}^{2}-\sigma_{1}^{2}}{2\sigma_{2}^{2}}+\frac{(|y|-\mathsf{A})^{2}}{2\sigma_{1}^{2}}

(33)

\displaystyle\geq\log\frac{\sigma_{1}}{\sigma_{2}}-\frac{(|y|+\mathsf{A})^{2}}{2\sigma_{2}^{2}}-\frac{\sigma_{2}^{2}-\sigma_{1}^{2}}{2\sigma_{2}^{2}}+\frac{(|y|-\mathsf{A})^{2}}{2\sigma_{1}^{2}},

where (

\mathbb{E}[\phi_{\sigma_{1}}(y-X^{\star})]\leq\phi_{\sigma_{1}}(|y|-\mathsf{A}),\qquad|y|\geq\mathsf{A};

. The function

\displaystyle g(y)+\log\left(\frac{\sigma_{2}}{\sigma_{1}}\right)-C_{s}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{(|y|+\mathsf{A})^{2}}{2\sigma_{2}^{2}}-\frac{\sigma_{2}^{2}-\sigma_{1}^{2}}{2\sigma_{2}^{2}}+\frac{(|y|-\mathsf{A})^{2}}{2\sigma_{1}^{2}}-C_{s}

\displaystyle\geq\mathsf{A}\frac{\sigma_{2}+\sigma_{1}}{\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1}}+\sqrt{\frac{\frac{\sigma_{2}^{2}-\sigma_{1}^{2}}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}+2C_{s}}{\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}-\frac{1}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}}}.

This concludes the proof for the bound on

.

To show the bound on the number of points, we need to first present a number of ancillary results.
We start with the following definition.

Definition 1

(Sign Changes of a Function)
.

The number of sign changes of a function
\xi:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is given by

\mathscr{S}(\xi)=\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\left\{\sup_{y_{1}<\cdots<y_{m}\subseteq\Omega}\mathscr{N}\{\xi(y_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m}\right\}\text{,}

(39)

where
\mathscr{N}\{\xi(y_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m}
is the number of changes of sign of the sequence
\{\xi(y_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m}
.

The following theorem, shown in
[
14
]
, will be a key step in the proof of the upper bound on the number of mass points.

Theorem 2

(Oscillation Theorem)
.

Given domains
\mathbb{I}_{1}
and
\mathbb{I}_{2}
, let
p\colon\mathbb{I}_{1}\times\mathbb{I}_{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be a strictly totally positive
kernel.
1
1

1

A function
f:\mathbb{I}_{1}\times\mathbb{I}_{2}\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be a totally positive kernel of order
n
if
\det\left([f(x_{i},y_{j})]_{i,j=1}^{m}\right)>0
for all
1\leq m\leq n
, and for all
x_{1}<\cdots<x_{m}\in\mathbb{I}_{1}
, and
y_{1}<\cdots<y_{m}\in\mathbb{I}_{2}
. If
f
is totally positive kernel of order
n
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then
f
is a strictly totally positive kernel.
For an arbitrary
y
, suppose

is an
n
-times differentiable function. Assume that
\mu
is a measure on
\mathbb{I}_{2}
, and let
\xi\colon\mathbb{I}_{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be a function with
\mathscr{S}(\xi)=n
. For

, define

\Xi(x)=\int\xi(y)p(x,y){\rm d}\mu(y)\text{.}

(40)

If
\Xi\colon\mathbb{I}_{1}\to\mathbb{R}
is an
n
-times differentiable function, then either
\mathrm{N}(\mathbb{I}_{1},\Xi)\leq n
, or
\Xi\equiv 0
.

The above theorem says that the number of zeros of a function

, which is the output of integral transformation, is less than the number of sign changes of the function

, which is the input to the integral transformation. The fact that the Gaussian pdf is a strictly totally positive kernel was show in

\displaystyle<\infty,

(46)

where (

(

III-B

Counting the number of zeros: Proof of the bound in (
19
)

The key to finding an explicit upper bound will be the following complex-analytic result.

Lemma 3

(Tijdeman’s Number of Zeros Lemma
[
15
]
)
.

Let
R,s,t
be positive numbers such that
s>1
. For the complex valued function
f\neq 0
which is analytic on
|z|<(st+s+t)R
, its number of zeros
\mathrm{N}(\mathcal{D}_{R},f)
within the disk
\mathcal{D}_{R}=\{z\colon|z|\leq R\}
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathrm{N}(\mathcal{D}_{R},f)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\log s}\left(\log\max_{|z|\leq(st+s+t)R}|f(z)|-\log\max_{|z|\leq tR}|f(z)|\right)\text{.}

(48)

Furthermore, the following loosened version of the bound in (
16
) will be useful.

Lemma 4
.

\displaystyle|{\mathsf{supp}}(P_{X^{\star}})|

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{N}\left([-R,R],h(\cdot)\right)+1

(49)

where

\displaystyle\frac{h(y)}{\sigma_{1}^{2}f_{Y_{1}}(y)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{E}_{N}\left[\mathbb{E}[X^{\star}|Y_{2}=y+N]\right]-y}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}-\frac{\mathbb{E}[X^{\star}|Y_{1}=y]-y}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}

(50)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[N\log f_{Y_{2}}(y+N)\right]}{\sigma^{2}_{2}-\sigma^{2}_{1}}-\frac{\mathbb{E}[X^{\star}|Y_{1}=y]-y}{\sigma_{1}^{2}},

(51)

and where
N\sim{\cal N}(0,\sigma_{2}^{2}-\sigma_{1}^{2})
.

Proof:

Starting from (
45
), we can write

\displaystyle|{\mathsf{supp}}(P_{X^{\star}})|

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{N}\left([-R,R],g(\cdot)+\log\left(\frac{\sigma_{2}}{\sigma_{1}}\right)-C_{s}\right)

(52)

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{N}\left([-R,R],g^{\prime}(\cdot)\right)+1

(53)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{N}\left([-R,R],\sigma_{1}^{2}f_{Y_{1}}(\cdot)g^{\prime}(\cdot)\right)+1

(54)

where in step (
53
) we have applied Rolle’s theorem, and in step (
54
) we used the fact that multiplying by a strictly positive function (i.e.,
\sigma_{1}^{2}f_{Y_{1}}
) does not change the number of zeros. The first derivative of
g
can be computed as follows:

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(y)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}y}\log f_{Y_{2}}(y+N)\right]-\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}y}\log f_{Y_{1}}(y)

(55)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{E}_{N}\left[\mathbb{E}[X^{\star}|Y_{2}=y+N]\right]-y}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}-\frac{\mathbb{E}[X^{\star}|Y_{1}=y]-y}{\sigma_{1}^{2}},

(56)

where in the last step we have used the well-known Tweedy’s formula (see for example
[
16
,
17
]
):

\mathbb{E}[X^{\star}|Y_{i}=y]=y+\sigma^{2}_{i}\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}y}\log f_{Y_{i}}(y).

(57)

An alternative expression for the first term in the RHS of (
55
) is as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}y}\log f_{Y_{2}}(y+N)\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f_{N}(n)\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}y}\log f_{Y_{2}}(y+n){\rm d}n

(58)

\displaystyle=-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}n}f_{N}(n)\right)\cdot\log f_{Y_{2}}(y+n){\rm d}n

(59)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{n}{\sigma^{2}_{2}-\sigma^{2}_{1}}f_{N}(n)\cdot\log f_{Y_{2}}(y+n){\rm d}n

(60)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}_{2}-\sigma^{2}_{1}}\mathbb{E}\left[N\log f_{Y_{2}}(y+N)\right],

(61)

The proof is concluded by letting

\displaystyle h(y)\triangleq\sigma_{1}^{2}f_{Y_{1}}(y)g^{\prime}(y).

(62)

\displaystyle a_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{3\sigma_{1}^{2}}{\sigma_{2}^{2}\sqrt{\sigma_{2}^{2}-\sigma_{1}^{2}}},

(64)

\displaystyle a_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{2}\sigma_{1}^{2}}{\sqrt{\sigma_{2}^{2}}\sqrt{\sigma_{2}^{2}-\sigma_{1}^{2}}}+2,

(65)

\displaystyle a_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma_{1}^{2}}{\sqrt{\sigma_{2}^{2}-\sigma_{1}^{2}}}\left(\sqrt{|\log(2\pi\sigma^{2}_{2})|^{2}+\frac{24(\sigma_{2}^{2}-\sigma_{1}^{2})^{2}}{\sigma^{4}_{2}}+\pi^{2}}\right).

(66)

Lemma 6
.

Let
\breve{h}:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
denote the complex extension of the function

\displaystyle\mathsf{B}\geq\mathsf{A}\frac{\sigma_{2}^{2}+\sigma_{1}^{2}}{\sigma_{2}^{2}-\sigma_{1}^{2}}

\displaystyle\max_{|z|\leq\mathsf{B}}|\breve{h}(z)|\geq\left(c_{1}\mathsf{B}-c_{2}\mathsf{A}\right)\frac{\exp\left(-\frac{(\mathsf{B}+\mathsf{A})^{2}}{2\sigma_{1}^{2}}\hskip-2.84544pt\right)}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma_{1}^{2}}}>0,

Proof.

First, note that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{E}_{N}\left[\mathbb{E}[X^{\star}|Y_{2}=\mathsf{B}+N]\right]}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}-\frac{\mathbb{E}[X^{\star}|Y_{1}=\mathsf{B}]}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}\geq-\frac{\mathsf{A}}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}-\frac{\mathsf{A}}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}.

Second, note that the condition in (

\displaystyle 0\leq\mathsf{B}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}-\frac{1}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}\right)-\frac{\mathsf{A}}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}-\frac{\mathsf{A}}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}.

Therefore, by using (

IV
Conclusion

This works has focused on deriving upper bounds on the number of mass points of secrecy-capacity-achieving distribution.

The upper bounds in Theorem
1
are generalizations of the upper bounds on the number of points presented in
[
13
]
in the context of a point-to-point additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) channel with an amplitude constraint. Indeed, if we let
\sigma_{2}\to\infty
, while keeping
\sigma_{1}
and
\mathsf{A}
fixed, then the wiretap channel reduces to the AWGN point-to-point channel.

An interesting future direction would be to find a matching implicit lower bound in (
16
). In
[
13
]
such a matching lower bound was found and shown to be tight with a multiplicative factor of two from the upper bound.
These results effectively show that the oscillation theorem (see Theorem
2
) is a strong enough tool for producing upper bounds on the cardinality of secrecy-capacity-achieving distributions for point-to-point channels. A matching lower bound in the case of the wiretap channel would demonstrate that oscillation theorem can also play an important role in network information theory problems. In
[
13
]
, the key tool to finding the lower bound was the observation that a linear combination of
n+1
distinct Gaussians with distinct variances can have at most
2n
zeros. In the wiretap channel, due to a more complicated structure of the function
g
in (
10
), it is not immediately clear how such an argument can be applied.

It will also be interesting to augment an explicit upper bound on the number of points in (
19
) with a lower bound on the number of points. A possible line of attack consists of the following steps:

\displaystyle C_{s}(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\mathsf{A})

\displaystyle=I(X^{\star};Y_{1})-I(X^{\star};Y_{2})

(100)

\displaystyle\leq H(X^{\star})-I(X^{\star};Y_{2})

(101)

\displaystyle\leq\log(|{\mathsf{supp}}(P_{X^{\star}})|)-I(X^{\star};Y_{2}),

(102)

where the above uses the non-negativity of entropy and the fact that entropy is maximized by a uniform distribution. Furthermore, by using a suboptimal uniform (continuous) distribution on
[-\mathsf{A},\mathsf{A}]
as an input and the entropy power inequality, the secrecy-capacity can be lower-bounded by

C_{s}(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\mathsf{A})\geq\frac{1}{2}\log\left(1+\frac{\frac{2\mathsf{A}^{2}}{\pi\mathrm{e}\sigma_{1}^{2}}}{1+\frac{\mathsf{A}^{2}}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}}\right).

(103)

Combing bounds in (
102
) and (
103
) we arrive at the following lower bound on the number of points:

\displaystyle|{\mathsf{supp}}(P_{X^{\star}})|\geq\sqrt{1+\frac{\frac{2\mathsf{A}^{2}}{\pi\mathrm{e}\sigma_{1}^{2}}}{1+\frac{\mathsf{A}^{2}}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}}}\mathrm{e}^{I(X^{\star};Y_{2})}.

(104)

At this point one needs to determine the behavior of
I(X^{\star};Y_{2})
. A trivial lower bound on
|{\mathsf{supp}}(P_{X^{\star}})|
can be found by lower bounding
I(X^{\star};Y_{2})
by zero. However, this lower bound on
|{\mathsf{supp}}(P_{X^{\star}})|
does not grow with
\mathsf{A}
while the upper bound increases with
\mathsf{A}
.
A possible way of establishing a lower bound that is increasing in
\mathsf{A}
is by showing that
I(X^{\star};Y_{2})\approx\frac{1}{2}\log\left(1+\frac{\mathsf{A}^{2}}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}\right)
. However, because not much is known about the structure of the optimal input distribution
P_{X^{\star}}
, it is not immediately evident how one can establish such an approximation or whether it is valid.

Reducibility and nonlinear stability for a quasi-periodically forced NLS
E. Haus B. Langella, A. Maspero, M. Procesi

Abstract
Motivated by the problem of long time stability vs. instability of KAM tori of the Nonlinear cubic Schrödinger equation (NLS) on the two dimensional torus 𝕋 2 := ( ℝ / 2 ​ π ​ ℤ ) 2 {\mathbb{T}}^{2}:=({\mathbb{R}}/2\pi{\mathbb{Z}})^{2} , we consider a quasi-periodically forced NLS equation on 𝕋 2 {\mathbb{T}}^{2} arising from the linearization of the NLS at a KAM torus. We prove a reducibility result as well as long time stability of the origin. The main novelty is to obtain the precise asymptotic expansion of the frequencies which allows us to impose Melnikov conditions at arbitrary order.



1
Introduction and main results

The nonlinear cubic Schrödinger equation on the two dimensional torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}:=({\mathbb{R}}/2\pi{\mathbb{Z}})^{2}

{\rm i}\partial_{t}v=-\Delta v+|v|^{2}v,\quad x\in{\mathbb{T}}^{2}\

(1.1)

is one of the fundamental equation in mathematical physics.
The pioneering work by Bourgain

[
11
]
ensures that the equation is locally well-posed for any data in
H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})
for all
s>0
, where

\|u\|_{H^{s}}:=\left(\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}(1+|n|)^{2s}|\widehat{u}_{n}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Much less is known on the long time behaviour of the solutions, and in the last ten years a rich and diverse dynamics has been discovered for (
1.1
).
Such dynamics comprise both “regular” dynamics, such as periodic
[
25
]
, quasiperiodic
[
16
,
26
,
37
]
, almost-periodic
[
13
,
10
]
trajectories contained in invariant tori, and more “irregular” one, such as
weakly turbulent trajectories undergoing energy cascade phenomenons
[
14
,
24
,
22
,
27
,
21
]
.

A particularly interesting long term goal is to understand the interplay of these two behaviours, in particular whether there are weakly turbulent trajectories connecting distinct invariant tori.

An important intermediate step is to understand the local dynamics close to these invariant sets, namely if one takes an initial datum close to an invariant torus, for how long does its trajectory stay close to the torus? The next step is to show that, beyond the stability times, unstable phenomena occur, and one may explicitly construct orbits which start
\delta
-close to an invariant torus and then slowly drift away by a finite but arbitrarily large factor. A convenient way of ensuring that trajectories do not stay close to a given torus is to show that they exhibit a Sobolev norm explosion, namely that after some (very long) time their Sobolev norm, in some fixed
H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})
with
s\neq 0,1
, becomes larger than an arbitrary number
K
(recall that on a given invariant torus the Sobolev norms are essentially constant).

The simplest invariant object is clearly the fixed point
v=0
.
In this case stability results were proved for example in

[
5
,
17
,
9
,
4
,
6
]
, while instability results in
[
12
,
31
,
32
,
33
]
, the landmark result in
[
14
]
and subsequent generalizations
[
24
,
28
,
22
]
.
For these problems a fundamental role is played by the linearized frequencies at
v=0
of (
1.1
), namely

\Omega_{j}=|j|^{2},\quad j\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2},

(1.2)

and the interest is either to avoid resonances between them to prove stability results or to exploit them to create instability phenomenon.

A much more challenging problem is to study stability/instability of more complicated invariant objects.
The simplest nontrivial invariant objects are the invariant tori of dimension 1 filled with plane wave solutions of the form
u(t,x)=\rho e^{{\rm i}jx-\omega t}
,
\omega=|j|^{2}+\rho^{2}
. Their long time stability was proved in

[
18
]
, whereas their instability in
H^{s}
,
s\in(0,1)
, in
[
27
]
.
Again a fundamental role is played by the resonance properties of the linearized frequencies at plane waves solutions, which are easily computed to be

\Omega_{j}=\sqrt{|j|^{4}+2|j|^{2}\rho^{2}}=|j|^{2}+\frac{c_{j}(\rho)}{|j|^{2}},\quad j\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}\ .

(1.3)

Here it is important to notice how the correction to the unperturbed frequencies (
1.2
) decays in
|j|
, namely whatever is the direction of the vector
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
.

The next studied invariant objects have been the so-called finite gap solutions, which are solutions for the integrable 1d cubic NLS.
In particular, they are solutions of (
1.1
) depending only on 1 variable, say
x_{1}
, but they are quasi-periodic in time and fill invariant tori of finite dimension
{\mathtt{d}}\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Their long time stability has been studied in
[
34
]
whereas their instability, again in
H^{s}
with
s\in(0,1)
in
[
21
]
.
As the reader might guess, again a fundamental role is played by the linearized frequencies at finite gap solutions.
In this case it is quite involved to compute their expression, and it requires partial Birkhoff normal form, a sort of “1d pseudodifferential normal form” and KAM reducibility techniques.
The final result is that they expand as

\Omega_{j}=|j|^{2}+\mu(j)+\frac{{\mathfrak{a}}(m)}{\langle m\rangle^{2}}+\frac{R_{j}}{\langle j\rangle^{2}},\qquad j=(m,n)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus\{0\}\ ,

(1.4)

where
\mu(j)
has a finite number of possible values while
{\mathfrak{a}},R
are of size
{\varepsilon}
.
Note the first difference with (
1.2
) and (
1.3
):
the correction to the unperturbed frequencies depends on the direction of the vector
j=(m,n)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
.

Towards stability/instability of KAM tori.

The next natural step is to study the long time stability vs instability of more general (truly bidimensional) quasiperiodic tori of
Procesi-Procesi
[
37
]
.
There are a number of very serious difficulties to overcome in order to deal with this more general case.
Again the main challenge is to obtain the correct asymptotic expansion of the linearized frequencies at KAM tori, namely the analogous of formulas (
1.3
), (
1.4
).
This is a very hard problem which requires new ideas with respect to the finite gap case.

The goal of this paper is to settle the machinery to obtain such asymptotics and to apply them to study a simplified NLS-like equation which,
despite not having a particular physical interest, contains the main difficulties to be overcome in order to tackle the full problem of stability/instability of KAM tori.

In order to justify the model and the simplifications that we will introduce, let us first give a brief description of how the KAM-solutions appear.
The first step is to choose an appropriate approximate solution, typically a solution of the linearized equation at
v=0
, which is the starting point of a quadratic algorithm which converges to a true solution. The idea in
[
26
,
36
,
42
]
is to fix a finite set
{\mathcal{S}}=\{{\bf k}^{(1)},\dots,{\bf k}^{(d)}\}\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
(of maximal rank), look for solutions of (
1.1
) close to

v_{\rm lin}(t,x)=\sum_{j\in{\mathcal{S}}}\sqrt{\xi_{j}}\,e^{{\rm i}j\cdot x+{\rm i}|j|^{2}t}\,,

and show that for
most
choices of
{\mathcal{S}}
and
many
choices of
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{d}
(in some small ball of radius
{\varepsilon}
) there exists a true solution
q^{\rm qp}
of (
1.1
) essentially supported on the Fourier modes in
{\mathcal{S}}
which is quasi-periodic in time with frequency

\omega=(\omega_{i})_{i=1}^{d},\qquad\omega_{i}=|{\bf k}^{(i)}|^{2}+{\mathcal{O}}({\varepsilon})\,.

Since the NLS equation is Gauge and translation invariant it turns out that the solutions in
[
37
]
have the form of
covariant quasi-periodic traveling waves
, according to the following definition:

Definition 1.1

(Quasi-periodic traveling waves)
.

Let

{\bf K}\in
Mat
{}_{d\times 2}({\mathbb{Z}})
(of maximal rank) with

{\bf K}^{T}=\begin{pmatrix}{\bf k}^{(1)}_{1}&\dots&{\bf k}^{(d)}_{1}\\
{\bf k}^{(1)}_{2}&\dots&{\bf k}^{(d)}_{2}\end{pmatrix}\ .

(1.5)

A function
q(\varphi,x)
is called a quasi-periodic traveling wave if it has the form

q(\varphi,x):=Q(\varphi+{\bf K}x)

where
Q\colon{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{C}}
is a real analytic function.

We say that a traveling wave is Gauge covariant if
Q
is a function with Fourier coefficients supported in the set
\{\ell\colon\ell\cdot\vec{1}=1\}
, i.e.

Q(\varphi+t\vec{1})=e^{{\rm i}t}Q(\varphi),\qquad\vec{1}=(1,\dots,1)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\ .

(1.6)

We say it is Gauge invariant if supported in the set
\{\ell\colon\ell\cdot\vec{1}=0\}
.
Finally we set

\|Q\|_{{\mathtt{a}},{\mathtt{p}}}^{2}:={\sum_{\ell}e^{2{\mathtt{a}}|\ell|}|Q(\ell)|^{2}\langle\ell\rangle^{2{\mathtt{p}}}}<\infty\,,\quad\mbox{for}\quad{\mathtt{a}}>0,\;{\mathtt{p}}>\frac{d}{2}\ .

(1.7)

Note that Gauge covariant, quasi-periodic traveling waves satisfy

\displaystyle q(\varphi+{\bf K}\zeta,\cdot)=\tau_{\zeta}q(\varphi,\cdot)\ ,\quad\forall\zeta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

(1.8)

\displaystyle q(\varphi+t\vec{1},\cdot)=e^{{\rm i}t}q(\varphi,\cdot)\ ,\quad\forall t\in{\mathbb{R}}\,,

(1.9)

In this sense, the solutions in
[
37
]
are covariant quasiperioric traveling waves of the form

q^{\rm qp}(\omega t,x)=q^{\rm qp}(\varphi,x)|_{\varphi=\omega t}=Q(\omega t+{\bf K}x)

(1.10)

where
\omega\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
belongs to a Cantor-like set of positive
measure and
{\bf K}
as in (
1.5
).

Linearizing (
1.1
) at
q^{\rm qp}
, one gets the equation

{\rm i}\partial_{t}u=-\Delta u+A(\omega t)u+{\cal N}(\omega t,u)

(1.11)

with

\displaystyle A(\varphi)u:=2|q^{\rm qp}(\varphi,\cdot)|^{2}u+q^{\rm qp}(\varphi,\cdot)^{2}\bar{u}\ ,

(1.12)

\displaystyle{\cal N}(\varphi,u):=2q^{\rm qp}(\varphi,\cdot)|u|^{2}+\bar{q}^{\rm qp}(\varphi,\cdot)u^{2}+|u|^{2}u\ .

As the original equation (
1.1
) is gauge and translation covariant and
q^{\rm qp}(\varphi,x)
is a gauge covariant quasi-periodic traveling wave, the linear operator
A(\varphi)
and the nonlinearity
{\mathcal{N}}(\varphi,u)
fulfill the following covariant properties, as we show in Appendix
C
: for any

(\varphi,\zeta,t)\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle A(\varphi+t\vec{1})\circ e^{{\rm i}t}=e^{{\rm i}t}\circ A(\varphi),\qquad\qquad A(\varphi+{\bf K}\zeta)\circ\tau_{\zeta}=\tau_{\zeta}\circ A(\varphi),

(1.13)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}(\varphi+t\vec{1},e^{{\rm i}t}u)=e^{{\rm i}t}\circ{\mathcal{N}}(\varphi,u),\qquad{\mathcal{N}}(\varphi+{\bf K}\zeta,\tau_{\zeta}u)=\tau_{\zeta}\circ{\mathcal{N}}(\varphi,u)\,,

(1.14)

where
\tau_{\zeta}
is the translation operator
(\tau_{\zeta}u)(x):=u(x+\zeta)
.

The fact that one may reduce to constant coefficients the operator
A(\varphi)
was already discussed in
[
36
,
37
]
(and is actually simpler to prove than in the case of finite gap solutions
[
34
]
).
On the other hand the methods of
[
36
,
37
]
do not give any information on the asymptotics of the frequencies, and actually one can prove that an expansion as the one in (
1.4
) cannot hold.
Then one is not able to prove that the non-resonance conditions hold and one may not prove neither the stability result nor the successive instability one.

The results.

As we already mentioned, the goal is this paper is to initiate the analysis of stability/instability of the general case by studying the nonlinear stability problem for a simplified
non-resonant
model, which contains only the difficulties related to the Melnikov conditions and avoids the further algebraic complications (which one expects to deal with exactly as in
[
36
]
and
[
34
]
).
To this purpose we study the following simplified model:

{\rm i}u_{t}-\Delta u+{{\mathscr{V}}}(\omega t,x)u+\partial_{\bar{u}}{{\mathscr{P}}}(t,x,u,\bar{u})=0\,,\qquad x\in{\mathbb{T}}^{2}\ .

(1.15)

We make the following assumptions on
{{\mathscr{V}}}
and
{{\mathscr{P}}}
:

(H
{{\mathscr{V}}}
)

{{\mathscr{V}}}(\omega t,x)
is the multiplication operator by an
analytic gauge invariant traveling wave (see Definition
1.1
), namely

{\mathscr{V}(\omega t,x)}=V(\omega t+{\bf K}x)\,,\quad V(\varphi)=\sum_{\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}V(\ell)e^{{\rm i}\ell\cdot\varphi}\,,

(1.16)

with the constraint that

V(\ell)=0\quad\forall\ell:\;\pi(\ell)=0\,,\quad\mbox{or}\quad\sum_{i=1}^{d}\ell_{i}\neq 0\,.

(1.17)

(H
{{\mathscr{P}}}
)

The non linear term

{{\mathscr{P}}}(t,x,y_{1},y_{2})=P(\omega t+{\bf K}x,y_{1},y_{2})
with
P
an analytic function on
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\times B_{\mathtt{r}_{0}}(0)
,

P(\varphi,y_{1},y_{2})=\sum_{\ell,d_{1},d_{2}}P_{\ell,d_{1},d_{2}}e^{{\rm i}\ell\cdot\varphi}y_{1}^{d_{1}}y_{2}^{d_{2}}\,,\quad\|P\|_{{\mathtt{a}},\mathtt{r}_{0}}=\sum_{\ell,d_{1},d_{2}}|P_{\ell,d_{1},d_{2}}|e^{{\mathtt{a}}|\ell|}\mathtt{r}_{0}^{d_{1}+d_{2}}<\infty\ ,

with a zero of order at least two
and satisfying the invariance property

P(\varphi+t\vec{1},e^{{\rm i}t}y_{1},e^{-{\rm i}t}y_{2})=P(\varphi,y_{1},y_{2})\,.

Remark 1.2
.

We model
{{\mathscr{V}}}
on the first term of the operator
A(\varphi)
in (
1.12
), namely the multiplication operator by
|q^{\rm qp}(\omega t,x)|^{2}
, which is a gauge invariant traveling wave with

\int_{\mathbb{T}}|q^{\rm qp}(\omega t,x)|^{2}dx
constant in time. So we assume that the same applies to
{{\mathscr{V}}}
.

Remark 1.3
.

The operator
{{\mathscr{V}}}(\varphi,\cdot)
and the nonlinearity
{{\mathscr{P}}}(\varphi,\cdot)
fulfill (
1.13
), (
1.14
).

For any
p\geq 0
we introduce the space
\mathfrak{H}^{p}=\mathfrak{H}^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{2}):=\ell^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{2})\cap h^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{2})
endowed with the norm

\|u\|_{p}:=\|u\|_{\ell^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{2})}+\|u\|_{h^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{2})}.

Note that, by Young inequality, the space
\mathfrak{H}^{p}
is an algebra with respect to the convolution product, i.e.

\|u*v\|_{p}\leq 2^{2p+1}\|u\|_{p}\,\|v\|_{p}

(1.18)

We are now ready to state our stability result.

Theorem 1.4
.

Fix
p>0
and consider the equation (
1.15
). Assume (H
{{\mathscr{V}}}
) and (H
{{\mathscr{P}}}
). Let
{\mathtt{R}}:=[-1,1]^{d}
.
There exist
\gamma_{*},\epsilon_{*}>0
and for any
\gamma\in(0,\gamma_{*})
, and any potential
V
satisfying

\epsilon:=\gamma^{-1}\,\|V\|_{{\mathtt{a}},{\mathtt{p}}}<\epsilon_{*}\ ,

(1.19)

there exist a Cantor-like set

{\mathcal{C}}
with measure

{\rm meas}({\mathtt{R}}\setminus{\mathcal{C}})\leq C\gamma\,,

and

r_{*},T_{*}>0
such that for any
\omega\in{\mathcal{C}}
,
r\in(0,r_{*})
the following holds true.
For any
u\in\mathfrak{H}^{p}
, with
\|u(0)\|_{p}\leq r
, the solution
u(t)\in\mathfrak{H}^{p}
of (
1.15
) fulfills

\|u(t)\|_{p}\leq 2r,\qquad\forall|t|\leq\frac{T_{*}}{r^{2}}\ .

(1.20)

As already mentioned, the proof of Theorem
1.4
follows the same general scheme of
[
34
]
based on the three steps: 1. Reducibility, 2. Non-resonance conditions, 3. Order three Birkhoff Normal Form.
Since the last step is standard (it is briefly discussed in Section
6
) we concentrate on the first two.

We start by studying the reducibility for the Schrödinger operator

A={\rm i}{\mathfrak{L}}\,,\quad{\mathfrak{L}}=-\Delta+{{\mathscr{V}}}\,,

(1.21)

in the space
\mathfrak{H}^{p}:=\ell^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{2})\cap h^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{2})
, where
p>0
is given, which amounts to prove that the following property holds:

Definition 1.5

(Reducibility)
.

We say that a time dependent Schrödinger operator
{\mathfrak{L}}
is reducible on
\mathfrak{H}^{p}
if there exists a quasi-periodic in time bounded and invertible linear operator

G
such that

G_{*}{\mathfrak{L}}:=-{\rm i}G^{-1}\dot{G}+G^{-1}{\mathfrak{L}}G={\rm diag}(\Omega_{j})\,,\quad\Omega_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}\,.

(1.22)

In
[
15
]
, Eliasson and Kuksin have proven in the analytic setting the existence of an invertible and time quasi-periodic linear operator
G
such that
G_{*}{\mathfrak{L}}
as in (
1.22
) is reduced to a time independent, block diagonal operator
Q
, under smallness assumptions on
\epsilon
as in (
1.19
) and for a Borel set of frequencies
\omega
of asymptotically full measure in
\epsilon
, without the constraint that
{{\mathscr{V}}}
is a quasi-periodic traveling wave. Furthermore, it is well known (see for instance
[
40
]
) that if
{\mathfrak{L}}
is covariant in the sense of (
1.13
), then
Q
is diagonal. However, the reducibility result of
[
15
]
does not provide an asymptotic expansion of the frequencies
\{\Omega_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}
. Thus here
we proceed to prove a more refined result, from which we obtain an asymptotic expansion for
\{\Omega_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}
that generalizes the one found in
[
21
]
in the finite gap case (see (
1.4
)).
To state our result we first need two definitions.

Definition 1.6

(Generators)
.

A vector
v=(v_{1},v_{2})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
shall be called a
generator
if

{\rm gcd}(v_{1},v_{2})=1\mbox{ and }v_{1}>0\quad\mbox{ or }\quad v=(0,1)\ .

(1.23)

We shall denote by
{\mathcal{V}}\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
the set of generators.

From now on, we fix

0<\delta\ll 1,\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\mu:=1-2\delta\ .

(1.24)

Definition 1.7
.

Given
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
, we define
v(j)\in{\mathcal{V}}
as the first vector w.r.t. the lexicographic ordering that attains the minimum in

\min_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}:|v|\leq|j|^{\delta}}|v\cdot j|

(1.25)

and set

b(j):=j\cdot v(j)
. This means that
\,|b(j)|=\min\,\{|v\cdot j|\ |\ v\in{\mathcal{V}}:|v|\leq|j|^{\delta}\}
.

With these notations, we prove the following:

Theorem 1.8
.

There exist
\gamma_{*},\epsilon_{*}>0
and for any
\gamma\in(0,\gamma_{*})
, any potential
V
satisfying (
1.19
),
there exist a Cantor-like set

{\mathcal{C}}
with measure

{\rm meas}({\mathtt{R}}\setminus{\mathcal{C}})\leq C\gamma\,,

a sequence of Lipschitz functions
\mathtt{R}\ni\omega\mapsto\Omega_{j}(\omega)
and a bounded linear operator
G
, close to the identity and with quasi-periodic dependence on time, such that for any
\omega\in{\mathcal{C}}

G_{*}{\mathfrak{L}}={\rm diag}(\Omega_{j})\,,

(1.26)

with

\begin{gathered}\Omega_{j}(\omega):=\Omega_{j}=|j|^{2}+\frac{\varpi(v(j),b(j),\omega)}{\langle b(j)\rangle^{2}}{e^{-\kappa|v(j)|}}+\frac{\Theta^{(1)}(j,\omega)}{\langle b(j)\rangle\langle j\rangle^{\mu}}+\frac{\Theta^{(2)}(j,\omega)}{\langle j\rangle^{2\mu}}\quad\forall j\,,\\
\sup_{\omega\in{\mathtt{R}}}\,\sup_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}\left(|\varpi(b(j),v(j),\omega)|+|\Theta^{(1)}(j,\omega)|+|\Theta^{(2)}(j,\omega)|\right)<2\|V\|_{{\mathtt{a}},{\mathtt{p}}}\,,\end{gathered}

(1.27)

with
\kappa>0
depending on
{\mathtt{a}}
and on the matrix
{\bf K}
only. Moreover, for any integer
N\geq 3
, there exist
\tau_{N}>0,c^{*}_{N}>0
and a Cantor set
{\mathcal{C}}_{N}\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}
, such that

{\rm meas}({\mathtt{R}}\backslash{\mathcal{C}}_{N})\leq c^{*}_{N}\gamma\,,

and
\forall\omega\in{\mathcal{C}}_{N}
,
\forall(\ell,L)\in{\mathcal{G}}_{N}
,
where

{\mathcal{G}}_{N}:=\Big\{(\ell,L)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{Z}}^{{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}:\;\mbox{if}\;\ell=0\,,\quad\mbox{then}\quad\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}|j|^{2}L_{j}\neq 0\\
|L|:=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}|L_{j}|\leq N\,,\quad\;\sum_{i=1}^{d}\ell_{i}+\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}L_{j}=0\,,\quad\sum_{i=1}^{d}\ell_{i}{\bf k}^{(i)}+\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}jL_{j}=0\Big\}\,,

(1.28)

one has

|\omega\cdot\ell+\Omega\cdot L|\geq\gamma\langle\ell\rangle^{-\tau_{N}}\,.

(1.29)

The major step in proving non-resonance conditions (
1.29
) is to show that the final frequencies
\{\Omega_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}
satisfy the asymptotic expansions (
1.27
). Actually the problem of exhibiting asymptotic expansions for the eigenvalues of a linear Schrödinger operator

\widetilde{{\mathfrak{L}}}=-\Delta+W

on
{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
,
n\geq 1,
has been widely investigated already in the case of a time independent
W
(see for instance
[
19
,
20
,
41
,
29
,
30
,
38
,
39
,
7
,
8
]
). Roughly speaking, in such works one partitions the spectrum of
{\mathfrak{L}}
into two subsets: the stable spectrum, which is composed by the eigenvalues such that

\Omega_{j}=|j|^{2}+O\left(\frac{1}{\langle j\rangle^{2\mu}}\right)\,,

(1.30)

and the unstable one, namely the set of eigenvalues for which (
1.30
) does not hold. We point out that with our notations, stable eigenvalues correspond to those such that

\langle b(j)\rangle\geq\langle j\rangle^{\mu}\,.

(1.31)

Refined asymptotic expansions were available in the time independent case also for eigenvalues in the unstable spectrum, and in
[
8
]
it was proven that all the eigenvalues of
\widetilde{{\mathfrak{L}}}
on
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
are of the form

\begin{gathered}\Omega_{j}=|j|^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{{\varpi}^{\ast}_{n}(j)}{\langle b(j)\rangle^{2n}}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{{\Theta}^{\ast}_{n}(j)}{\langle j\rangle^{2n\mu}}+O\left(\frac{1}{\langle b(j)\rangle^{2N}}\right)\,,\end{gathered}

(1.32)

using techniques based on pseudo-differential calculus and a geometric decomposition of the space of indexes
j
à la Nekhoroshev (see
[
35
,
23
]
). However, an expansion of the form (
1.32
) would not suffice in order to show non-resonance conditions (
1.29
) at any order
N
: this is the reason why we use a slightly different approach, based on a structure which we refer to as quasi-Töplitz (see Definition
2.16
below), and prove expansions (
1.27
). Remark that the major difference with respect to the expansions (
1.32
) is represented by the functions
\varpi(b(j),v(j))
, namely by the fact that the terms which decay with
\langle b(j)\rangle
depend on the vector
j
through
v(j)
and
b(j)
only and decay exponentially in
v(j)
. This is the key to proving the non-resonance conditions (
1.29
).

Indeed, rewriting

\omega\cdot\ell+\Omega\cdot L=\omega\cdot\ell\pm\Omega_{j_{1}}\pm\Omega_{j_{2}}\pm\dots\pm\Omega_{j_{k}}

the asymptotic expansion guarantees that if all the
|j_{i}|\gg|\ell|^{a_{1}}
, and either all the
|b(j_{i})|\gg|\ell|^{a_{2}}
or
|v(j_{i})|\gg|\ell|,
then (
1.29
) follows by imposing Diophantine conditions on
\omega
of the form

|\omega\cdot\ell+K|\gtrsim|\ell|^{-\tau}\quad\forall\ell\neq 0\,,\quad\forall K\in{\mathbb{Z}}\,.

(and it is well known that such conditions hold for a positive measure set of
\omega
provided that
\tau
is small enough).

Following this line of reasoning in (
1.29
) we can ignore all the terms
\frac{\Theta^{(1)}(j_{i})}{\langle b(j_{i})\rangle\langle j_{i}\rangle^{\mu}}
,
\frac{\Theta^{(2)}(j_{i})}{\langle j_{i}\rangle^{2\mu}}
where the
|j_{i}|
are sufficiently large. Similarly we can ignore all the terms
\frac{{\varpi}(v(j_{i}),b(j_{i})}{\langle b(j_{i})\rangle^{2}}e^{-\kappa|v(j_{i})|}
where either
v(j_{i})
or
b(j_{i})
is large. This means that, for
|L|\leq N
, the expression
\omega\cdot\ell+\Omega\cdot L
assumes (up to a negligible error) only a finite number of distinct values (of cardinality bounded only by
\ell
) which are small.

In turn this means that one can surely impose conditions of the type (
1.29

2
Functional setting

2.1
Time-dependent momentum preserving operators.

We shall consider quasi-periodic time dependent operators
t\mapsto M(\omega t)\in{\mathcal{L}}(\mathfrak{H}^{p})
with frequency
\omega\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, where

M(\varphi)=\sum_{\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}M(\ell)e^{{\rm i}\varphi\cdot\ell}\,,\quad M(\ell)\in{\mathcal{L}}(\mathfrak{H}^{p}),

(2.1)

that are analytic and momentum preserving according to the following definition:

Definition 2.1

(Momentum preserving operators)
.

Here and in the following we shall define the linear map
\pi:{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}

\pi(\ell):=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\ell_{i}{\bf k}^{(i)}\,,\quad{\mathtt{c}}^{-1}:=\sup_{\ell\neq 0}\frac{|\pi(\ell)|}{|\ell|}\,.

(2.2)

We say that a time dependent linear operator
M(\omega t)
is momentum preserving if

j-j^{\prime}\neq\pi(\ell)\ \ \Rightarrow\ \ M_{j}^{j^{\prime}}(\ell):=\langle M(\ell)e^{{\rm i}j^{\prime}\cdot x},e^{{\rm i}j\cdot x}\rangle=0\ .

(2.3)

For any
a>0
we define the norm

|M|_{a}:=\sup_{\|u\|_{p}\leq 1}\|\underline{M}_{a}u\|_{p}\,,\quad(\underline{M}_{a})_{j}^{j^{\prime}}:=\sum_{\ell:\pi(\ell)=j-j^{\prime}}e^{a|\ell|}|M_{j}^{j^{\prime}}(\ell)|.

We denote the space of time dependent momentum preserving operators with finite norm as
{\mathcal{L}}_{a}(\mathfrak{H}^{p})
.

Definition 2.2

(Gauge covariant operators)
.

We say that
M
as above is Gauge covariant if
\sum_{i=1}^{d}\ell_{i}\neq 0
implies
M_{j}^{j^{\prime}}(\ell)=0\,.

A convienent way of envisioning such operators is as
normally analytic
maps from a thickened torus

{\mathbb{T}}^{d}_{a}:=\{\varphi\in{\mathbb{C}}^{d}:\quad{\mbox{Re}}(\varphi)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}/(2\pi{\mathbb{Z}})^{d}\,,\quad|{\mbox{Im}}(\varphi)|\leq a\}\quad\to{\mathcal{L}}(\mathfrak{H}^{p})\,.

(2.4)

Remark 2.3
.

If
M\in{\mathcal{L}}_{a}(\mathfrak{H}^{p})
, then
M(\varphi)\in{\mathcal{L}}(\mathfrak{H}^{p})
for all
\varphi\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}_{a}
and the operator norm satisfies
{\|M(\varphi)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(\mathfrak{H}^{p})}\leq|M|_{a}.}

One may easily verify (see Lemma
2.14
) that
{\mathcal{L}}_{a}(\mathfrak{H}^{p})
is in fact an algebra with respect to composition. Then it is standard to define the commutator and the adjoint action as follows:

Definition 2.4
.

For
M,S\in{\mathcal{L}}_{a}(\mathfrak{H}^{p})
, we set

{\rm ad}S[M](\varphi):=[M(\varphi),S(\varphi)]:=M(\varphi)S(\varphi)-S(\varphi)M(\varphi)
, which in matrix form reads

([M,S])_{j}^{j-\pi(\ell)}(\ell)=\sum_{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}=\ell}M_{j}^{j-\pi(\ell_{1})}(\ell_{1})S_{j-\pi(\ell_{1})}^{j-\pi(\ell)}(\ell_{2})-S_{j}^{j-\pi(\ell_{1})}(\ell_{1})M_{j-\pi(\ell_{1})}^{j-\pi(\ell)}(\ell_{2})\,.

Definition 2.5
.

We say that
M\in{\mathcal{L}}_{a}(\mathfrak{H}^{p})
is self-adjoint if it satisfies

M_{j}^{j-\pi(\ell)}(\ell)=\overline{M_{j-\pi(\ell)}^{j}(-\ell)}

(2.5)

so that
M(\varphi)
is self-adjoint for all
\varphi\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
.
As is standard, we say that a bounded operator
G\in{\mathcal{L}}_{a}(\mathfrak{H}^{p})
is symplectic if

G=e^{{\rm i}A}
with
A\in{\mathcal{L}}_{a}(\mathfrak{H}^{p})
self-adjoint.

Remark 2.6
.

If
A,B
are self-adjoint, then so is
{\rm i}[A,B]
.
Consequently, anti self-adjoint operators form a Lie algebra. Moreover, if
M
is self-adjoint and
G=e^{S}
is symplectic (i.e.,
{\rm i}S
is self-adjoint) then

G^{-1}MG=\exp({\rm ad}(S))M
is self-adjoint.
Similarly Gauge covariant operators form a Lie algebra, so if
S,M
are Gauge covariant, so is
G
and
G^{-1}MG
.

Time-dependent changes of variables

Given the linear PDE
u_{t}={\rm i}{\mathfrak{L}}u
,
where
{\mathfrak{L}}

The Lie exponentiation formula gives a nice representation of

(2.6)

Remark 2.7
.

Note that, by Remark

is self-adjoint and

The same holds for the Gauge covariance.

Symplectic structure

As we have already explained in the introduction, time dependent Schrödinger operators have a natural Hamiltonian structure on the phase space

, equipped with the symplectic form

To this purpose we associate to

as in (

), with

with Hamilton equations

\dot{\varphi}=\omega,\ \dot{u}={\rm i}{\mathfrak{L}}u\,,\dot{\mathcal{Y}}=-(u,P_{\varphi}u)_{\ell_{2}({\mathbb{C}})}

Note that (by the Lie exponentiation formula)

Finally if

\displaystyle|\Pi_{|\ell|>K}M|^{\mathtt{T},{\mathcal{O}}}_{a^{\prime},-m}\leq e^{-(a-a^{\prime})K}|M|^{\mathtt{T},{\mathcal{O}}}_{a,-m},\qquad|\Pi_{|\ell|>K}M|^{\mathtt{qT},{\mathcal{O}}}_{a^{\prime},-m}\leq e^{-\frac{a-a^{\prime}}{2}K}|M|^{\mathtt{qT},{\mathcal{O}}}_{a,-m}.

Lemma 2.20
.

Given a
time independent
(and hence diagonal) operator
A={\rm diag}{(A_{j})}\in{\mathcal{L}}^{\mathtt{qT},{\mathcal{O}}}_{a,-m}
,
there exists a decomposition of the eigenvalues

A_{j}=\mathfrak{a}(v(j),b(j))+\sum_{k=1}^{m}r^{(k)}_{j}

so that

\sup_{v\in{\mathcal{V}},b\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|{\mathfrak{a}}(v,b)|^{{\mathcal{O}}}e^{a{{\mathtt{c}}}|v|}{\langle b\rangle^{m}}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sup_{j}|r^{(k)}_{j}|^{{\mathcal{O}}}\langle j\rangle^{k\mu}\langle b(j)\rangle^{m-k}\leq 2|A|^{\mathtt{qT},{\mathcal{O}}}_{a,-m}

(2.18)

Moreover, if
A_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, then also
{\mathfrak{a}},r^{(k)}_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}
for
k=1,\ldots m
.

Proof.

Since
A\in{\mathcal{L}}_{a,-m}^{\tt qT,{\mathcal{O}}}
there exists a decomposition

A=A^{\mathtt{T}}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}A^{(k)}\,,\quad\mbox{ such that}\quad|A^{\mathtt{T}}|^{\mathtt{T},{\mathcal{O}}}_{a,-m}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}|A^{(i)}|^{\mathcal{O}}_{\frac{a}{2};-(i,m-i)}\leq 2|A|^{\mathtt{qT},{\mathcal{O}}}_{a,-m}.

Recalling that
A
is time-independent, we have

A=\langle A\rangle_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}=\langle A^{\mathtt{T}}\rangle_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}\langle A^{(k)}\rangle_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}.

Now, the operators
\langle A^{\mathtt{T}}\rangle_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}},\langle A^{(k)}\rangle_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}
are all time-independent and hence diagonal; moreover,
\langle A^{\mathtt{T}}\rangle_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}
is still line Töplitz. The bounds follow from Remark
2.8
. The reality condition follows by taking the real part in both sides of the equality above.
∎

We now discuss the algebra properties of
{\mathcal{L}}^{\mathtt{qT},{\mathcal{O}}}_{a,-m}
. Our purpose is to get a result analogous to the one in Lemma
2.14
. With this in mind, we give two propositions on the product of operators in
{\mathcal{L}}^{\mathtt{qT},{\mathcal{O}}}_{a,-m}
.

Proposition 2.21
.

Let
M_{1}\in{\mathcal{L}}^{\mathtt{qT},{\mathcal{O}}}_{a,-m_{1}}
and
M_{2}\in{\mathcal{L}}^{\mathtt{qT},{\mathcal{O}}}_{a,-m_{2}}
, and set

m=\min\{m_{1},m_{2}\}
.
Then we have
M_{1}M_{2}\in{\mathcal{L}}^{\mathtt{qT},{\mathcal{O}}}_{a,-m}
, with the bound

|M_{1}M_{2}|^{\mathtt{qT},{\mathcal{O}}}_{a,-m}\leq Ca^{-q_{0}}|M_{1}|^{\mathtt{qT},{\mathcal{O}}}_{a,-m_{1}}\,|M_{2}|^{\mathtt{qT},{\mathcal{O}}}_{a,-m_{2}}\,.

(2.19)

Moreover, setting
m_{1}=m_{2}=1
, one also has
\forall 0<a^{\prime}<a
that
M_{1}M_{2}\in{\mathcal{L}}^{\mathtt{qT},{\mathcal{O}}}_{a^{\prime},-2}
, with

|M_{1}M_{2}|^{\mathtt{qT},{\mathcal{O}}}_{a^{\prime},-2}\leq{C(a-a^{\prime})^{-q_{1}}}|M_{1}|^{\mathtt{qT},{\mathcal{O}}}_{a,-1}\,|M_{2}|^{\mathtt{qT},{\mathcal{O}}}_{a,-1}\,.

(2.20)

Here
C,q_{0},q_{1}
are positive constants depending only on
m_{1},m_{2},\mu,\delta,{\mathtt{c}},p
.

The proof, being quite technical, is postponed to Appendix
B
.

Corollary 2.22

(Exponentials)
.

Given
a,m>0
and
A\in{\mathcal{L}}^{\mathtt{qT},{\mathcal{O}}}_{a,-m}
such that (the constants
C,\gamma
are defined in Proposition
2.21
)

|A|^{\mathtt{qT},{\mathcal{O}}}_{a,-m}\leq\delta:=\frac{a^{q_{0}}}{4C}

(2.21)

one has for any sequence

, and for all

|\sum_{k=d}^{\infty}c_{k}{\rm ad}(A)^{k}M|^{\mathtt{qT},{\mathcal{O}}}_{a,-m}\leq 2|c|_{\infty}\Big({\frac{|A|^{\mathtt{qT},{\mathcal{O}}}_{a,-m}}{2\delta}}\Big)^{d}

(2.22)

Proof.

It follows directly by iterating the bounds in Proposition
2.21
.
∎

Remark 2.23
.

Estimate (
2.19
) of Proposition
2.21
and Corollary
2.22
ensure that the well known fact that smoothing operators generate bounded changes of variables holds also in the quasi-Töplitz context. On the other hand, estimate (
2.20
) shows that the product of two operators of order
-1
is actually of order
-2
, up to a small loss of analyticity.

Lemma 3.2

6
Nonlinear stability results

In order to prove stability we apply a classical Birkhoff normal form procedure, see for instance Theorem 8.1 of
[
34
]
.
We start by recalling some standard notions on time dependent Hamiltonians.

One works in the
extended phase space

(\varphi,{\mathcal{Y}},u)\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times\mathfrak{H}^{p}
with the symplectic form

d{\mathcal{Y}}\wedge d\varphi+{\rm i}du\wedge d\bar{u}
.

In this context we shall consider
real on real
analytic functions on the complex domain

\displaystyle D(a,r):={\mathbb{T}}^{\mathtt{d}}_{a}\times D(r)\,,\quad

\displaystyle D(r):=\left\{{\mathcal{Y}}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\mathtt{d}}:\;|{\mathcal{Y}}|_{1}:=\sum_{i=1}^{\mathtt{d}}|{\mathcal{Y}}_{i}|<r^{2}\,,\quad u\in\mathfrak{H}^{p}:\;\|u\|_{p}<r\right\}\,,

with
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}_{a}
defined in (
2.4
).
Given a real analytic function
F(\varphi,{\mathcal{Y}},u)
we consider its Taylor-Fourier series

F(\varphi,{\mathcal{Y}},u)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\alpha},{\beta}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}\\
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\mathtt{d}},l\in{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathtt{d}}\end{subarray}}F_{{\alpha},{\beta},l,\ell}\,e^{{\rm i}\ell\cdot\varphi}\,{\mathcal{Y}}^{l}\,u^{\alpha}\,\bar{u}^{\beta}\,,\quad F_{{\alpha},{\beta},l,\ell}=\bar{F}_{{\beta},{\alpha},l,-\ell}\,,\quad u^{\alpha}\bar{u}^{\beta}=\Pi_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}u_{j}^{\alpha_{j}}\bar{u}_{j}^{{\beta}_{j}}

Correspondingly we expand vector fields in Taylor Fourier series (again well defined and pointwise absolutely convergent):

X^{(w)}(\varphi,{\mathcal{Y}},u)=\sum_{{\alpha},{\beta}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}},\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\mathtt{d}},l\in{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathtt{d}}}X_{{\alpha},{\beta},l,\ell}^{(w)}\,e^{{\rm i}\ell\cdot\varphi}\,{\mathcal{Y}}^{l}\,u^{\alpha}\,\bar{u}^{\beta}\,,

where
w
denotes the components
\varphi_{i},{\mathcal{Y}}_{i}
or
u_{j},\bar{u}_{j}
.
To a vector field we associate its
majorant

\underline{X}_{a}^{(w)}[{\mathcal{Y}},u]:=\sum_{\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\mathtt{d}},l\in{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathtt{d}},{\alpha},{\beta}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}}|X^{(w)}_{{\alpha},{\beta},l,\ell}|\,e^{a\,|\ell|}\,{\mathcal{Y}}^{l}\,u^{\alpha}\,\bar{u}^{\beta}\ .

(6.1)

Then we have the following

Definition 6.1
.

A vector field
X:D(a,r)\to{\mathbb{C}}^{{\mathtt{d}}}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{\mathtt{d}}\times\mathfrak{H}^{p}
will be said to be majorant analytic in
D(a,r)
if
\underline{X}_{a}
defines an analytic vector field
D(r)\to{\mathbb{C}}^{\mathtt{d}}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{\mathtt{d}}\times\mathfrak{H}^{p}
.

Since Hamiltonian functions are defined modulo constants,
we give the following definition of
regular
Hamiltonian and its norm:

Definition 6.2
.

A real valued Hamiltonian
H
will be said to be regular in
D(a,r)
if its Hamiltonian vector field
X_{H}
is majorant analytic in
D(a,r)
. We define its norm by

|H|_{a,r}:=\sup_{({\mathcal{Y}},u)\in D(r)}{|\mkern-6.0mu|\mkern-6.0mu|\,\underline{(X_{H})}_{a}({\mathcal{Y}},u)\,|\mkern-6.0mu|\mkern-6.0mu|}_{r}\,,\quad{|\mkern-6.0mu|\mkern-6.0mu|\,X\,|\mkern-6.0mu|\mkern-6.0mu|}_{r}:=|X^{(\varphi)}|_{\infty}+\frac{|X^{({\mathcal{Y}})}|_{1}}{r^{2}}+\frac{\|X^{(u)}\|}{r}+\frac{\|X^{(\bar{u})}\|}{r}\ .

(6.2)

Note that the norm
|F|_{a,r}
controls the norm
{|\mkern-6.0mu|\mkern-6.0mu|\,X_{F}\,|\mkern-6.0mu|\mkern-6.0mu|}_{r}
in
D(a,r)
.

If
F(\omega;\varphi,{\mathcal{Y}},u)\equiv F(\omega)
depends on the frequency
\omega\in{\mathcal{O}}
, where
{\mathcal{O}}
is some compact set, we define the
weighted Lipschitz
norm:

|F|_{a,r}^{\mathcal{O}}:=\sup_{\omega\in{\mathcal{O}}}|F(\omega,\cdot)|_{a,r}+\gamma\sup_{\omega_{1}\neq\omega_{2}\in{\mathcal{O}}}\frac{|F(\omega_{1},\cdot)-F(\omega_{2},\cdot)|_{a,r}}{|\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}|}\ .

(6.3)

Definition 6.3
.

We say that a regular Hamiltonian
H
is Gauge and Momentum preserving if

\pi(\ell)+\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}j({\alpha}_{j}-{\beta}_{j})\neq 0\quad\mbox{or}\quad\sum_{i=1}^{d}\ell_{i}+\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}({\alpha}_{j}-{\beta}_{j})\neq 0\quad\Rightarrow\quad H_{{\alpha},{\beta},l,\ell}=0

By convention we define the scaling degree of a monomial
e^{{\rm i}\ell\cdot\varphi}\,{\mathcal{Y}}^{l}\,u^{\alpha}\,\bar{u}^{\beta}
as
\deg(l,\alpha,\beta):=2|l|+|\alpha|+|\beta|-2\,,

and define the projection on the homogeneous components of scaling degree
d
as

H^{(d)}:=\sum_{\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\mathtt{d}},l\in{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathtt{d}},{\alpha},{\beta}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}\atop 2|l|+|{\alpha}|+|{\beta}|=d+2}\,H_{{\alpha},{\beta},l,\ell}\,e^{{\rm i}\ell\cdot\varphi}{\mathcal{Y}}^{l}u^{\alpha}\,\bar{u}^{\beta}\ ,

similarly for
H^{(\leq d)}
and
H^{(\geq d)}
. One easily verifies that
\{H^{(d_{1})},H^{(d_{2})}\}
has scaling degree

.

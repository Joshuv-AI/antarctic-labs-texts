Sketching Approximability of (Weak) Monarchy Predicates
Chi-Ning Chou, Alexander Golovnev, Amirbehshad Shahrasbi, Madhu Sudan, Santhoshini Velusamy

Abstract
We analyze the sketching approximability of constraint satisfaction problems on Boolean domains, where the constraints are balanced linear threshold functions applied to literals. In particular, we explore the approximability of monarchy-like functions where the value of the function is determined by a weighted combination of the vote of the first variable (the president) and the sum of the votes of all remaining variables. The pure version of this function is when the president can only be overruled by when all remaining variables agree. For every k ≥ 5 k\geq 5 , we show that CSPs where the underlying predicate is a pure monarchy function on k k variables have no non-trivial sketching approximation algorithm in o ⁡ ( n ) o(\sqrt{n}) space. We also show infinitely many weaker monarchy functions for which CSPs using such constraints are non-trivially approximable by O ⁡ ( log ⁡ ( n ) ) O(\log(n)) space sketching algorithms. Moreover, we give the first example of sketching approximable asymmetric Boolean CSPs. Our results work within the framework of Chou, Golovnev, Sudan, and Velusamy (FOCS 2021) that characterizes the sketching approximability of all CSPs. Their framework can be applied naturally to get a computer-aided analysis of the approximability of any specific constraint satisfaction problem. The novelty of our work is in using their work to get an analysis that applies to infinitely many problems simultaneously.



1
Introduction

In this paper we consider the sketching complexity of solving constraint satisfaction problems (CSPs) approximately where the constraints are given by linear threshold functions over a collection of Boolean literals. We introduce these terms below.

CSPs:

Given a Boolean function
f:\{-1,1\}^{k}\to\{0,1\}
, the Boolean CSP associated with
f
, denoted
\textsf{Max-CSP}(f)
is the following optimization problem. Given
m
constraints
C_{1},\ldots,C_{m}
on
n
Boolean variables
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
, where each constraint applies
f
to a sequence of
k
distinct literals from the set
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n},-X_{1},\ldots,-X_{n}\}
, find the maximum fraction of constraints that can be satisfied by an assignment to the
n
variables. For an instance
\Psi
of
\textsf{Max-CSP}(f)
we use
\textsf{val}_{\Psi}
to denote this maximum value.
We are interested in approximating
\textsf{val}_{\Psi}
and this task is known to be equivalent to solving a gapped decision version of
\textsf{Max-CSP}(f)
. For
0\leq\beta<\gamma\leq 1
we define the
(\gamma,\beta)
-gapped version of
\textsf{Max-CSP}(f)
, abbreviated to
(\gamma,\beta)
-
\textsf{Max-CSP}(f)
, to be the following promise decision problem: Given an instance
\Psi
satisfying
\textsf{val}_{\Psi}\geq\gamma
or
\textsf{val}_{\Psi}<\beta
decide which one of the two conditions holds.

Sketching algorithms:

The class of algorithms we consider (and rule out) are randomized sketching algorithms. Inputs to these algorithms arrive as a stream of elements, in our case a stream of constraints. We consider algorithms that use some bounded amount of space, denoted
s(n)
, to process the stream and maintain a sketch of their output. When the stream ends the algorithm outputs it verdict based on the current sketch. A key restriction of a sketching algorithm is that its sketch should satisfy the following composability property. Given two streams
\sigma
and
\tau
and a fixing of the randomness, the sketch of their concatenation
S(\sigma\circ\tau)
should be determined by their sketches
S(\sigma)
and
S(\tau)
alone.
1
1

1

In contrast, a general streaming algorithm maintains a state
S(\sigma\circ\tau)
that may depend on
S(\sigma)
and all of
\tau
.
Most existing algorithms for streaming CSPs are sketching algorithms. We say a sketching algorithm solves a (gapped) decision problem if on every input its answer is correct with probability at least
2/3
.

Approximability and approximation resistance:

For
\alpha\in[0,1]
, we say an algorithm is an
\alpha
-approximation algorithm for
\textsf{Max-CSP}(f)
if the following holds: on every input instance
\Psi
, the algorithm outputs
v
such that
\alpha\cdot\textsf{val}_{\Psi}\leq v\leq\textsf{val}_{\Psi}
with probability at least
2/3
. Note that the existence of an
\alpha
-approximation algorithm is equivalent to the existence of an algorithm for solving
(\gamma,\beta)
-
\textsf{Max-CSP}(f)
for every
\gamma,\beta\in[0,1]
with
\beta\leq\alpha\cdot\gamma
.

For a function
f:\{-1,1\}^{k}\to\{0,1\}
, define
\rho(f)=2^{-k}\cdot|\{x\in\{-1,1\}^{k}|f(x)=1\}|
.
For every
f
and every instance
\Psi
of
\textsf{Max-CSP}(f)
, a random assignment satisfies
\rho(f)
fraction of the constraints in expectation and so every
\Psi
satisfies
\textsf{val}_{\Psi}\geq\rho(f)
. Thus the
(1,\rho(f))
-
\textsf{Max-CSP}(f)
problem is trivially solvable by the algorithm that always outputs
\textsf{val}_{\Psi}\geq 1
(since the set
\{\Psi|\textsf{val}_{\Psi}<\rho(f)\}
is empty). We say
\textsf{Max-CSP}(f)
is sketching approximable within space
s(n)
if there is an
\varepsilon>0
and a sketching algorithm using at most
s(n)
space that solves
(1-\varepsilon,\rho(f)+\varepsilon)
-
\textsf{Max-CSP}(f)
. We say that
\textsf{Max-CSP}(f)
is approximation resistant to space
s(n)
if for every
\varepsilon>0
, every sketching algorithm for
(1,\rho(f)+\varepsilon)
-
\textsf{Max-CSP}(f)
requires
\Omega(s(n))
space.

1.2
Main results

In this paper we study sketching approximability of CSPs on linear threshold functions. Below we define the classes of linear threshold functions and balanced linear threshold functions.

1.2
Linear threshold function.

A linear threshold function, or LTF, is a Boolean function
f:\{-1,1\}^{k}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
of the form

f(x)=\textsf{sign}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}w_{i}x_{i}+\theta\right),

where
w_{1},\dots,w_{k},\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. The function
\textsf{sign}(z)
has value
1
if
z>0
and
0
if
z\leq 0
;
w_{1},\dots,w_{k}
are called the weights of
f
and
\theta
is the threshold.

1.3
Balanced linear threshold function.

A balanced linear threshold function, or balanced LTF, is an LTF with threshold
0
and the additional restriction that for every
x\in\{-1,1\}^{k}
, we have
\sum_{i=1}^{k}w_{i}x_{i}\neq 0
. Specifically, a balanced LTF
f
satisfies
f(-x)=1-f(x)
for every
x
.

Note that for a balanced LTF
f
,
\rho(f)=1/2
, and the goal of approximability is to beat this factor. Balanced LTFs form a technically important class of functions to study visavis CSP approximability. For instance Potechin
[
Pot19
]
studies them in the polynomial time regime giving a (somewhat complex) approximation-resistant function in this class.
In the sketching setting, interest in this class of functions comes from

[
CGSV22
, Theorem 1.3]
which shows that if a function
f
supports one-wise independence (i.e.,
f^{-1}
supports a distribution on
\{-1,1\}^{k}
that is uniform on each of the
k
marginals) then
\textsf{Max-CSP}(f)
is approximation resistant to
o(\sqrt{n})
space streaming algorithms. Balanced LTFs are the most basic class of functions that
do not
support one-wise independence and hence are not covered by this theorem. Studying this class thus offers the possibility of finding new classes of CSPs that are approximation resistant to
o(\sqrt{n})
-space streaming algorithms.

Our first result shows that every balanced LTF on up to
4
variables is sketching approximable. (So to search for new approximation resistant functions we need to look at functions on more variables!) We note that there are only finitely many such LTFs, but already this theorem gives the first example of an asymmetric Boolean CSP which is approximable by sketching algorithms.

2.4
Fourier analysis of Boolean functions

We will need the following basic notions from Fourier analysis over the Boolean hypercube (see, for instance,
[
O’D14
]
).

2.8
Characteristic functions.

For every
S\subseteq[k]
such that
|S|\geq 1
, the characteristic function
\chi_{S}:\{-1,1\}^{k}\rightarrow\{-1,1\}
is defined as
\chi_{S}(x)=\prod_{i\in S}x_{i}
. The characteristic function corresponding to the empty set is defined as the constant function
\chi_{\emptyset}(x)=1
for all
x\in\{-1,1\}^{k}
.

2.9
Fourier expansions.

The Fourier expansion of a Boolean function
f:\{-1,1\}^{k}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
is given by

f=\sum_{S\subseteq[k]}\widehat{f}(S)\cdot\chi_{S}\,,

where
\widehat{f}(S)=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathsf{Unif}\{-1,1\}^{k}}[f(x)\cdot\chi_{S}(x)]
and
\mathsf{Unif}(\{-1,1\}^{k})
denotes the uniform distribution on
\{-1,1\}^{k}
.

2.10
Chow parameters.

The Chow parameters of a Boolean function
f:\{-1,1\}^{k}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
are the degree-
0
Fourier coefficient and the
k
degree-
1
Fourier coefficients of
f
, i.e.,
\widehat{f}(\emptyset),\widehat{f}(\{1\}),\dots,\widehat{f}(\{k\})
.

2.11
.

For every Boolean function
f:\{-1,1\}^{k}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
,

1.

\rho(f)=\widehat{f}(\emptyset)
,

2.

for every
S\subseteq[k]
,
|\widehat{f}(S)|\leq\widehat{f}(\emptyset)
, and

3.

for every
x\in\{-1,1\}^{k}
,
-\widehat{f}(\emptyset)\cdot k\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\widehat{f}(\{i\})\cdot x_{i}\leq\widehat{f}(\emptyset)\cdot k
.

Proof.

The first statement of the proposition follows directly from the definition of
\rho(f)
:
\rho(f)=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathsf{Unif}(\{-1,1\}^{k})}[f(x)]=\widehat{f}(\emptyset)
. For the second statement, observe that for all
S\subseteq[k]
,

\displaystyle|\widehat{f}(S)|

\displaystyle=|\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathsf{Unif}(\{-1,1\}^{k})}[f(x)\cdot\chi_{S}(x)]|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathsf{Unif}(\{-1,1\}^{k})}[|f(x)\cdot\chi_{S}(x)|]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathsf{Unif}(\{-1,1\}^{k})}[f(x)]

\displaystyle=\widehat{f}(\emptyset)\,.

It immediately follows that for all
x\in\{-1,1\}^{k}
,

\left|\sum_{i=1}^{k}\widehat{f}(\{i\})\cdot x_{i}\right|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}|\widehat{f}(\{i\})\cdot x_{i}|\leq\widehat{f}(\emptyset)\cdot k\,.

\displaystyle=\Pr\{x_{1}=-1\text{ and exactly $i$ of the rest of $x_{i}$s are 1}\}\;.

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}(1/2+\delta)^{m-i+1}(1/2-\delta)^{i}\left(\binom{m}{i}-\binom{m}{i-1}\right)

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=\lceil m/2\rceil+1}^{m+1}(1/2-\delta)^{i}(1/2+\delta)^{m-i+1}\left(\binom{m}{i-1}-\binom{m}{i}\right)\,.

Using

, we update the first summation’s limits:

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\lceil m/2\rceil}(1/2+\delta)^{m-i+1}(1/2-\delta)^{i}\left(\binom{m}{i}-\binom{m}{i-1}\right)

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=\lceil m/2\rceil+1}^{m+1}(1/2-\delta)^{i}(1/2+\delta)^{m-i+1}\left(\binom{m}{i-1}-\binom{m}{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{m}(1/2-\delta)^{i}(1/2+\delta)^{m-i+1}\left(\binom{m}{i}-\binom{m}{i-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{m+1}{\mathsf{E}}[x_{1}\cdot\mathds{1}_{i}]={\mathsf{E}}[x_{1}]=2\delta\,,

which concludes the proof.

(ii)
\bm{v\geq\left(\rho(f)+\varepsilon^{*}(f)-\varepsilon\right)\cdot\textsf{val}_{\Psi}}
.

We have

v=\rho(f)+\frac{\tilde{B}\tilde{\delta}}{(1+\varepsilon^{\prime})^{2}}\geq\rho(f)+B_{\bm{\lambda}}(\Psi)\delta(\Psi)\left(\frac{1-\varepsilon^{\prime}}{1+\varepsilon^{\prime}}\right)^{2}\geq\rho(f)+B_{\bm{\lambda}}(\Psi)\delta(\Psi)(1-\varepsilon)\,,

(5.9)

where the last inequality follows from the choice of
\varepsilon^{\prime}
. Let us first consider the case when
B_{\bm{\lambda}}(\Psi)\geq\varepsilon_{0}(f)
. We have

B_{\bm{\lambda}}(\Psi)\delta(\Psi)\geq\varepsilon_{0}(f)\cdot\min\left\{\frac{1}{3k},\frac{2\varepsilon_{0}(f)}{9\rho(f)k^{2}}\right\}\geq\varepsilon^{*}\,,

(5.10)

where the last equality follows from the definition of
\varepsilon^{*}(f)
in
Definition

5.2
.

Combining
Eq.

5.9
and
Eq.

5.10
, we get

v\geq\rho(f)+\varepsilon^{*}(f)(1-\varepsilon)\geq(\rho(f)+\varepsilon^{*}(f)-\varepsilon)\textsf{val}_{\Psi}\,,

where the last inequality follows from
\textsf{val}_{\Psi}\leq 1
.

Now, let us consider the case when
B_{\bm{\lambda}}(\Psi)<\varepsilon_{0}(f)
. It follows from
Proposition

2.11
that
\varepsilon_{0}(f)\leq\rho(f)k
. Therefore,

\frac{2B_{\bm{\lambda}}(\Psi)}{9\rho(f)k^{2}}\leq\frac{2\varepsilon_{0}(f)}{9\rho(f)k^{2}}\leq\frac{2}{9k}<\frac{1}{3k}\,,

and so
\delta(\Psi)=\frac{2B_{\bm{\lambda}}(\Psi)}{9\rho(f)k^{2}}
. Combining
Eq.

5.9
and
Lemma

5.8
, we have

\frac{v}{\textsf{val}_{\Psi}}\geq(1-\varepsilon)\left(\frac{\rho(f)+\frac{2B_{\bm{\lambda}}(\Psi)^{2}}{9\rho(f)k^{2}}}{\rho(f)+\frac{B_{\bm{\lambda}}(\Psi)}{k}}\right)\left(\rho(f)+\frac{\varepsilon_{0}(f)}{k}\right)\,.

We show that for
0\leq B_{\bm{\lambda}}(\Psi)\leq\varepsilon_{0}(f)
,

\frac{\rho(f)+\frac{2B_{\bm{\lambda}}(\Psi)^{2}}{9\rho(f)k^{2}}}{\rho(f)+\frac{B_{\bm{\lambda}}(\Psi)}{k}}\geq\frac{\rho(f)+\frac{2\varepsilon_{0}(f)^{2}}{9\rho(f)k^{2}}}{\rho(f)+\frac{\varepsilon_{0}(f)}{k}}\,.

(5.11)

This immediately implies that

\frac{v}{\textsf{val}_{\Psi}}\geq(1-\varepsilon)\left(\rho(f)+\frac{2\varepsilon_{0}(f)^{2}}{9\rho(f)k^{2}}\right)\geq(1-\varepsilon)(\rho(f)+\varepsilon^{*}(f))>\rho(f)+\varepsilon^{*}(f)-\varepsilon\,.

Consider the function
g(p)=\frac{\rho(f)+\frac{2p^{2}}{9\rho(f)}}{\rho(f)+p}
. In order to show
Eq.

5.11
, it suffices to show that in the range
p\in[0,\frac{\varepsilon_{0}(f)}{k}]
,
g(p)
attains the minimum value at
p=\frac{\varepsilon_{0}(f)}{k}
, i.e,
g^{\prime}(p)<0
in this range. We have

g^{\prime}(p)=\frac{\left(\frac{2(p+\rho(f))^{2}}{9\rho(f)}-\frac{11\rho(f)}{9}\right)}{(\rho(f)+p)^{2}}
and for
p\in[0,\frac{\varepsilon_{0}(f)}{k}]
, we have

\left(\frac{2(p+\rho(f))^{2}}{9\rho(f)}-\frac{11\rho(f)}{9}\right)\leq\left(\frac{2(\varepsilon_{0}(f)/k+\rho(f))^{2}}{9\rho(f)}-\frac{11\rho(f)}{9}\right)\leq\frac{8\rho(f)}{9}-\frac{11\rho(f)}{9}=-\frac{\rho(f)}{3}<0\,.

This completes the proof of
Theorem

5.3
.
∎

Proof of
Lemma

5.7
.

Let
\mathsf{Bern}(p)\in\Delta(\{-1,1\})
denote the Bernoulli distribution where
1
is sampled with probability
p
. Given an instance
\Psi=(C_{1},\ldots,C_{m};w_{1},\ldots,w_{m})
of
\textsf{Max-CSP}(f)
where
C_{i}=(\mathbf{j}(i),\mathbf{b}(i))
and
w_{i}\geq 0
, let
\gamma=3\cdot\delta(\Psi)=\min\{\frac{1}{k},\frac{2B_{\bm{\lambda}}(\psi)}{3\rho(f)k^{2}}\}
. Let
\bm{\sigma}=\arg\max_{\mathbf{a}\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}\langle\mathbf{a},\textsf{bias}_{\bm{\lambda}}(\Psi)\rangle
. It follows from
Lemma

5.5
that
B_{\bm{\lambda}}(\Psi)=\langle\bm{\sigma},\textsf{bias}_{\bm{\lambda}}(\Psi)\rangle
. In order to prove the lemma, we will show that

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{a}\sim\left(\mathsf{Bern}(\frac{1+\gamma}{2})\right)^{n}}[\textsf{val}_{\Psi}(\mathbf{a}\odot\bm{\sigma})]\geq\rho(f)+B_{\bm{\lambda}}(\Psi)\delta(\Psi)\,.

The lemma then directly follows from the fact that
\textsf{val}_{\Psi}\geq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{a}\sim\left(\mathsf{Bern}(\frac{1+\gamma}{2})\right)^{n}}[\textsf{val}_{\Psi}(\mathbf{a}\odot\bm{\sigma})]
.

We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{a}\sim\left(\mathsf{Bern}(\frac{1+\gamma}{2})\right)^{n}}[\textsf{val}_{\Psi}(\mathbf{a}\odot\bm{\sigma})]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{a}\sim\left(\mathsf{Bern}(\frac{1+\gamma}{2})\right)^{n}}\left[\frac{1}{W}\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_{i}\cdot f(\mathbf{a}|_{\mathbf{j}(i)}\odot\bm{\sigma}|_{\mathbf{j}(i)}\odot\mathbf{b}(i))\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{a}\sim\left(\mathsf{Bern}(\frac{1+\gamma}{2})\right)^{n}}\left[\frac{1}{W}\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_{i}\cdot\sum_{S\subseteq[k]}\widehat{f}(S)\cdot\chi_{S}(\mathbf{a}|_{\mathbf{j}(i)}\odot\bm{\sigma}|_{\mathbf{j}(i)}\odot\mathbf{b}(i))\right]

(Fourier expansion of
f
)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{W}\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_{i}\cdot\sum_{S\subseteq[k]}\widehat{f}(S)\cdot\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{a}\sim\left(\mathsf{Bern}(\frac{1+\gamma}{2})\right)^{n}}\left[\chi_{S}(\mathbf{a}|_{\mathbf{j}(i)}\odot\bm{\sigma}|_{\mathbf{j}(i)}\odot\mathbf{b}(i))\right]

(Linearity of expectation)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{W}\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_{i}\cdot\sum_{S\subseteq[k]}\widehat{f}(S)\cdot\chi_{S}(\mathbf{b}(i)\odot\bm{\sigma}|_{\mathbf{j}(i)})\cdot\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{a}\sim\left(\mathsf{Bern}(\frac{1+\gamma}{2})\right)^{n}}\left[\chi_{S}(\mathbf{a}|_{\mathbf{j}(i)})\right]

(Since
\chi_{S}(a\odot b)=\chi_{S}(a)\cdot\chi_{S}(b)
)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{W}\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_{i}\cdot\sum_{S\subseteq[k]}\widehat{f}(S)\cdot\chi_{S}(\mathbf{b}(i)\odot\bm{\sigma}|_{\mathbf{j}(i)})\cdot\gamma^{|S|}

(Since
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{a}\sim\left(\mathsf{Bern}(\frac{1+\gamma}{2})\right)^{n}}[a_{\ell}]=\gamma
for all
\ell\in[n]
)

\displaystyle=\widehat{f}(\emptyset)+\frac{1}{W}\sum_{i\in[m]}w_{i}\sum_{t\in[k]}\widehat{f}(\{t\})\cdot b(i)_{t}\cdot\sigma_{j(i)_{t}}\cdot\gamma

\displaystyle~~~+\frac{1}{W}\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_{i}\cdot\sum_{S\subseteq[k]:|S|\geq 2}\widehat{f}(S)\cdot\chi_{S}(\mathbf{b}(i)\odot\bm{\sigma}|_{\mathbf{j}(i)})\cdot\gamma^{|S|}

\displaystyle=\widehat{f}(\emptyset)+\sum_{\ell\in[n]}\left(\frac{1}{W}\sum_{i\in[m],t\in[k]:j(i)_{t}=\ell}\widehat{f}(\{t\})\cdot w_{i}\cdot b(i)_{t}\right)\sigma_{\ell}\cdot\gamma

\displaystyle~~~+\frac{1}{W}\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_{i}\cdot\sum_{S\subseteq[k]:|S|\geq 2}\widehat{f}(S)\cdot\chi_{S}(\mathbf{b}(i)\odot\bm{\sigma}|_{\mathbf{j}(i)})\cdot\gamma^{|S|}

(Rearranging the summations)

\displaystyle\geq\widehat{f}(\emptyset)+\gamma\langle\textsf{bias}_{\bm{\lambda}}(\Psi),\bm{\sigma}\rangle-\sum_{S\subseteq[k]:|S|\geq 2}|\widehat{f}(S)|\cdot\gamma^{|S|}

(By the definition of
\bm{\lambda}
,
\textsf{bias}_{\bm{\lambda}}(\Psi)
), and
|\chi_{S}(\cdot)|\leq 1
)

\displaystyle\geq\rho(f)+\gamma\cdot B_{\bm{\lambda}}(\Psi)-\rho(f)\sum_{S\subseteq[k]:|S|\geq 2}\gamma^{|S|}

(By the definition of
\bm{\sigma}
and
Proposition

2.11
)

\displaystyle=\rho(f)+\gamma\cdot B_{\bm{\lambda}}(\Psi)-\rho(f)\sum_{r=2}^{k}\binom{k}{r}\cdot\gamma^{r}\,.

We now prove that
\rho(f)\sum_{r=2}^{k}\binom{k}{r}\cdot\gamma^{r}\leq\frac{2\gamma}{3}\cdot B_{\bm{\lambda}}(\Psi)
. Consider the combinatorial identity
\binom{k}{r}=\frac{k\cdot\binom{k-1}{r-1}}{r}
. Since
\gamma\leq\frac{1}{k}
and
r\geq 2
, we have

\binom{k}{r}\gamma^{r}=\frac{k\cdot\binom{k-1}{r-1}}{r}\cdot\gamma^{r}\leq\frac{1}{2}\cdot\binom{k-1}{r-1}\cdot\gamma^{r-1}<\frac{1}{2}\cdot\binom{k}{r-1}\cdot\gamma^{r-1}\,.

Hence
\sum_{r=2}^{k}\binom{k}{r}\cdot\gamma^{r}\leq 2\cdot\binom{k}{2}\cdot\gamma^{2}
. Since
\gamma\leq\frac{2B_{\bm{\lambda}}(\Psi)}{3\rho(f)k^{2}}
, we have

\rho(f)\cdot\sum_{r=2}^{k}\binom{k}{r}\cdot\gamma^{r}\leq\rho(f)\cdot 2\cdot\binom{k}{2}\cdot\gamma^{2}\leq\frac{2B_{\bm{\lambda}}(\Psi)\cdot\gamma}{3}\,.

Recall that
\gamma=3\delta(\Psi)
. Finally, we conclude that

\textsf{val}_{\Psi}\geq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{a}\sim\left(\mathsf{Bern}(\frac{1+\gamma}{2})\right)^{n}}[\textsf{val}_{\Psi}(\mathbf{a}\odot\bm{\sigma})]\geq\rho(f)+\frac{\gamma}{3}\cdot B_{\bm{\lambda}}(\Psi)=\rho(f)+B_{\bm{\lambda}}(\Psi)\delta(\Psi)\,.

∎

Proof of
Lemma

5.8
.

Let
\Psi=(C_{1},\ldots,C_{m};w_{1},\ldots,w_{m})
be an instance of
\textsf{Max-CSP}(f)
where
C_{i}=(\mathbf{j}(i),\mathbf{b}(i))
and
w_{i}\geq 0
. Let
\mathbf{a}^{*}\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
denote the assignment that satisfies the maximum weight of constraints in
\Psi
, i.e.,
\mathbf{a}^{*}=\arg\max_{\mathbf{a}\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}\textsf{val}_{\Psi}(\mathbf{a})
. It follows from
Lemma

5.5
that
B_{\bm{\lambda}}(\Psi)\geq\langle\mathbf{a}^{*},\textsf{bias}_{\bm{\lambda}}(\Psi)\rangle
. Let
S
be the set of indices corresponding to constraints of
\Psi
satisfied by
\mathbf{a}^{*}
, i.e.,
S=\{i\in[m]:f(\mathbf{a}^{*}|_{\mathbf{j}(i)}\odot\mathbf{b}(i))=1\}
. We have

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{a}^{*},\textsf{bias}_{\bm{\lambda}}(\Psi)\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\in[n]}a^{*}_{\ell}\cdot\frac{1}{W}\cdot\sum_{i\in[m],t\in[k]:j(i)_{t}=\ell}\lambda_{t}w_{i}b(i)_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{W}\sum_{i\in[m]}w_{i}\sum_{t\in[k]}\lambda_{t}\cdot b(i)_{t}\cdot a^{*}_{j(i)_{t}}

(Exchanging the summations)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{W}\sum_{i\in[m]}w_{i}\sum_{t\in[k]}\widehat{f}(\{t\})\cdot b(i)_{t}\cdot a^{*}_{j(i)_{t}}

(
\bm{\lambda}=(\widehat{f}(\{1\}),\dots,\widehat{f}(\{k\}))
)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{W}\sum_{i\in S}w_{i}\sum_{t\in[k]}\widehat{f}(\{t\})\cdot b(i)_{t}\cdot a^{*}_{j(i)_{t}}+\frac{1}{W}\sum_{i\notin S}w_{i}\sum_{t\in[k]}\widehat{f}(\{t\})\cdot b(i)_{t}\cdot a^{*}_{j(i)_{t}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{W}\sum_{i\in S}w_{i}\cdot\varepsilon_{0}(f)-\frac{1}{W}\sum_{i\notin S}w_{i}\cdot\rho(f)\cdot k

(By the definition of
S
and
\varepsilon_{0}(f)
, and
Proposition

2.11
)

\displaystyle=\textsf{val}_{\Psi}\cdot\varepsilon_{0}(f)-(1-\textsf{val}_{\Psi})\rho(f)\cdot k

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\mbox{(By the definition of $\mathbf{a}^{*}$)}\,.

Therefore, we get

B_{\bm{\lambda}}(\Psi)\geq\textsf{val}_{\Psi}\cdot\varepsilon_{0}(f)-(1-\textsf{val}_{\Psi})\rho(f)\cdot k\,.

Rearranging the terms, we get

\textsf{val}_{\Psi}\leq\frac{B_{\bm{\lambda}}(\Psi)+\rho(f)\cdot k}{\varepsilon_{0}(f)+\rho(f)\cdot k}\,.

5.3
Approximability of weak monarchy functions

In this section, we analyze the streaming approximability of
\textsf{Max-CSP}(f)
where
f
is a weak monarchy function. Note that in order for
\textsf{WMON}_{k,j}
to be a balanced LTF, the total number of votes, i.e.,
j+k-1
, needs to be odd. Therefore, we make such assumption throughout the rest of this section.

5.12
.

For all integers
j\geq 2
and
k\geq 7j^{3}
such that
k+j
is even,

\textsf{WMON}_{k,j}(x)=\textsf{sign}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\widehat{\textsf{WMON}_{k,j}}(\{i\})x_{i}\right).

Note that
Lemma

5.12
along with
Theorem

5.1
directly conclude
Theorem

1.6
restated below.

See
1.6

Proof of
Lemma

5.12
.

We start by finding the Chow parameters of
\textsf{WMON}_{k,j}
. As mentioned earlier, we only consider the case where
k+j
is even. For the president,

\displaystyle\widehat{\textsf{WMON}_{k,j}}(\{1\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Pr\{x_{1}=1,\textsf{WMON}_{k,j}(x)=1\}\times 1

\displaystyle\Pr\{x_{1}=-1,\textsf{WMON}_{k,j}(x)=1\}\times(-1)

\displaystyle\Pr\{x_{1}=1,\textsf{WMON}_{k,j}(x)=0\}\times 0

\displaystyle\Pr\{x_{1}=-1,\textsf{WMON}_{k,j}(x)=0\}\times 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{k}}\left(\binom{k-1}{\geq\frac{k+j}{2}-j}-\binom{k-1}{\geq\frac{k+j}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{k}}\left(\binom{k-1}{\geq\frac{k-j}{2}}-\binom{k-1}{\geq\frac{k+j}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{k}}\left(2^{k-1}-2\binom{k-1}{<\frac{k-j}{2}}\right)\,.

For citizen
x_{i}
(
i>1
),

\displaystyle\widehat{\textsf{WMON}_{k,j}}(\{i\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Pr\{x_{1}=1,x_{i}=1,\textsf{WMON}_{k,j}(x)=1\}\times 1

\displaystyle\Pr\{x_{1}=1,x_{i}=-1,\textsf{WMON}_{k,j}(x)=1\}\times(-1)

\displaystyle\Pr\{x_{1}=-1,x_{i}=1,\textsf{WMON}_{k,j}(x)=1\}\times 1

\displaystyle\Pr\{x_{1}=-1,x_{i}=-1,\textsf{WMON}_{k,j}(x)=1\}\times(-1)

\displaystyle\Pr\{x_{1}=1,x_{i}=1,\textsf{WMON}_{k,j}(x)=0\}\times 0

\displaystyle\Pr\{x_{1}=1,x_{i}=-1,\textsf{WMON}_{k,j}(x)=0\}\times 0

\displaystyle\Pr\{x_{1}=-1,x_{i}=1,\textsf{WMON}_{k,j}(x)=0\}\times 0

\displaystyle\Pr\{x_{1}=-1,x_{i}=-1,\textsf{WMON}_{k,j}(x)=0\}\times 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{k}}\left(\binom{k-2}{\geq\frac{k+j}{2}-j-1}-\binom{k-2}{\geq\frac{k+j}{2}-j}+\binom{k-2}{\geq\frac{k+j}{2}-1}-\binom{k-2}{\geq\frac{k+j}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{k}}\left(\binom{k-2}{\frac{k+j}{2}-j-1}+\binom{k-2}{\frac{k+j}{2}-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\binom{k-2}{\frac{k-j}{2}-1}}{2^{k-1}}\,.

Note that in order for functions
\textsf{WMON}_{k,j}(x)
and
\textsf{sign}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\widehat{\textsf{WMON}_{k,j}}(\{i\})x_{i}\right)
to be the same, it suffices to have

j-1<\frac{\widehat{\textsf{WMON}_{k,j}}(\{1\})}{\widehat{\textsf{WMON}_{k,j}}(\{i\})}<j+1\,.

Thus, in the rest of the proof, we find values for
k
that guarantee the bounds above. We start with the upper-bound:

\frac{\widehat{\textsf{WMON}_{k,j}}(\{1\})}{\widehat{\textsf{WMON}_{k,j}}(\{i\})}=\frac{2^{k-2}-\binom{k-1}{<\frac{k-j}{2}}}{\binom{k-2}{\frac{k-j}{2}-1}}\leq\frac{\frac{j}{2}\cdot\binom{k-1}{\lfloor\frac{k-1}{2}\rfloor}}{\binom{k-2}{\frac{k-j}{2}-1}}\,.

(5.13)

The last inequality holds as below:

•

If
k-1
is odd:
2^{k-2}=\sum_{i=0}^{\frac{k-2}{2}}\binom{k-1}{i}\Rightarrow 2^{k-2}-\binom{k-1}{<\frac{k-j}{2}}=\sum_{i=\frac{k-j}{2}}^{\frac{k-2}{2}}\binom{k-1}{i}\leq\frac{j}{2}\cdot\binom{k-1}{\frac{k-2}{2}}

•

If
k-1
is even:
2^{k-2}=\frac{1}{2}\binom{k-1}{\frac{k-1}{2}}+\sum_{i=0}^{\frac{k-3}{2}}\binom{k-1}{i}\Rightarrow 2^{k-2}-\binom{k-1}{<\frac{k-j}{2}}\leq\frac{j}{2}\cdot\binom{k-1}{\frac{k-1}{2}}

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{\widehat{\textsf{WMON}_{k,j}}(\{1\})}{\widehat{\textsf{WMON}_{k,j}}(\{i\})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{j}{2}\cdot\frac{\frac{(k-1)!}{(\lfloor\frac{k-1}{2}\rfloor)!(\lceil\frac{k-1}{2}\rceil)!}}{\frac{(k-2)!}{(\frac{k-j}{2}-1)!(\frac{k+j}{2}-1)!}}=\frac{j}{2}\cdot\frac{k-1}{\lfloor\frac{k-1}{2}\rfloor}\cdot\frac{(\frac{k+j}{2}-1)\cdots(\lceil\frac{k-1}{2}\rceil+1)}{(\lfloor\frac{k-1}{2}-1\rfloor)\cdots(\frac{k-j}{2})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{j}{2}\cdot 2\left(1+\frac{1}{k-2}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{\lceil\frac{k-1}{2}\rceil+1}{\frac{k-j}{2}}\right)^{\lfloor\frac{j-1}{2}\rfloor}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle j\cdot\left(1+\frac{1}{k-2}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{k+2}{k-j}\right)^{\frac{j-1}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle j\cdot\left(1+\frac{1}{k-2}\right)\cdot\left(1+\frac{j+2}{k-j}\right)^{\frac{j-1}{2}}\leq j\cdot\left(1+\frac{j+2}{k-j}\right)^{j}\,.

For any given
j
,
\left(1+\frac{j+2}{k-j}\right)^{j}
tends to 1 as
k
goes to
\infty
. Therefore, there exits some
K_{0}
such that for all
k\geq K_{0}
,

\frac{\widehat{\textsf{WMON}_{k,j}}(\{1\})}{\widehat{\textsf{WMON}_{k,j}}(\{i\})}<j+1.
More precisely, we take
k
to be at least
K_{0}=2j^{3}+4j^{2}+j\leq 7j^{3}
. This way,

\displaystyle\frac{\widehat{\textsf{WMON}_{k,j}}(\{1\})}{\widehat{\textsf{WMON}_{k,j}}(\{i\})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle j\cdot\left(1+\frac{1}{2j^{2}}\right)^{j}\leq j\cdot\left(1+j\cdot\frac{1}{2j^{2}}+j^{2}\cdot\frac{1}{(2j^{2})^{2}}+j^{3}\cdot\frac{1}{(2j^{2})^{3}}+\cdots\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle j\cdot\left(1+\frac{1}{2j}+\frac{1}{(2j)^{2}}+\frac{1}{(2j)^{3}}+\cdots\right)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle j\cdot\left(1+\frac{1}{j}\right).

We now proceed to the lower bound.

\frac{\widehat{\textsf{WMON}_{k,j}}(\{1\})}{\widehat{\textsf{WMON}_{k,j}}(\{i\})}=\frac{2^{k-2}-\binom{k-1}{<\frac{k-j}{2}}}{\binom{k-2}{\frac{k-j}{2}-1}}\geq\frac{\frac{j}{2}\cdot\binom{k-1}{\frac{k-j}{2}}}{\binom{k-2}{\frac{k-j}{2}-1}}\,.

(5.14)

Similar to the upper-bound case, the last inequality can be observed as follows:

•

If
k-1
is odd:
2^{k-2}=\sum_{i=0}^{\frac{k-2}{2}}\binom{k-1}{i}\Rightarrow 2^{k-2}-\binom{k-1}{<\frac{k-j}{2}}=\sum_{i=\frac{k-j}{2}}^{\frac{k-2}{2}}\binom{k-1}{i}\geq\frac{j}{2}\cdot\binom{k-1}{\frac{k-j}{2}}

•

If
k-1
is even:
2^{k-2}=\frac{1}{2}\binom{k-1}{\frac{k-1}{2}}+\sum_{i=0}^{\frac{k-3}{2}}\binom{k-1}{i}\Rightarrow 2^{k-2}-\binom{k-1}{<\frac{k-j}{2}}\geq\frac{j}{2}\cdot\binom{k-1}{\frac{k-j}{2}}

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{\widehat{\textsf{WMON}_{k,j}}(\{1\})}{\widehat{\textsf{WMON}_{k,j}}(\{i\})}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{j}{2}\cdot\frac{\binom{k-1}{\frac{k-j}{2}}}{\binom{k-2}{\frac{k-j}{2}-1}}=\frac{j}{2}\cdot\frac{\binom{k-2}{\frac{k-j}{2}}+\binom{k-2}{\frac{k-j}{2}-1}}{\binom{k-2}{\frac{k-j}{2}-1}}=\frac{j}{2}\cdot\left(1+\frac{\binom{k-2}{\frac{k-j}{2}}}{\binom{k-2}{\frac{k-j}{2}-1}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{j}{2}\cdot\left(1+\frac{\frac{k+j}{2}-1}{\frac{k-j}{2}}\right)=\frac{j}{2}\cdot\left(1+\frac{k+j-2}{k-j}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle j\cdot\left(1+\frac{j-1}{k-j}\right)\,.

This lower bound is larger than
j
for every
k>j
. Thus, for every
k\geq 2j^{3}+4j^{2}+j
,

j\leq\frac{\widehat{\textsf{WMON}_{k,j}}(\{1\})}{\widehat{\textsf{WMON}_{k,j}}(\{i\})}<j+1
which implies that

\textsf{WMON}_{k,j}(x)=\textsf{sign}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\widehat{\textsf{WMON}_{k,j}}(\{i\})x_{i}\right)
, and concludes the proof.
∎

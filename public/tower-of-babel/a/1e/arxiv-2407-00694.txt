Enumeration of minimal transversals of hypergraphs of bounded VC-dimension
A. Mary

Abstract
We consider the problem of enumerating all minimal transversals (also called minimal hitting sets) of a hypergraph ℋ \mathcal{H} . An equivalent formulation of this problem known as the transversal hypergraph problem (or hypergraph dualization problem) is to decide, given two hypergraphs, whether one corresponds to the set of minimal transversals of the other. The existence of a polynomial time algorithm to solve this problem is a long standing open question. In [ 15 ] , the authors present the first sub-exponential algorithm to solve the transversal hypergraph problem which runs in quasi-polynomial time, making it unlikely that the problem is (co)NP-complete. In this paper, we show that when one of the two hypergraphs is of bounded VC-dimension, the transversal hypergraph problem can be solved in polynomial time, or equivalently that if ℋ \mathcal{H} is a hypergraph of bounded VC-dimension, then there exists an incremental polynomial time algorithm to enumerate its minimal transversals. This result generalizes most of the previously known polynomial cases in the literature since they almost all consider classes of hypergraphs of bounded VC-dimension. As a consequence, the hypergraph transversal problem is solvable in polynomial time for any class of hypergraphs closed under partial subhypergraphs. We also show that the proposed algorithm runs in quasi-polynomial time in general hypergraphs and runs in polynomial time if the conformality of the hypergraph is bounded, which is one of the few known polynomial cases where the VC-dimension is unbounded.

A hypergraph

Preliminaries

A trace on
V:=V(\mathcal{H})
is a pair
(T,S)
with
S\subseteq V
and
T\subseteq S
. The size of the trace
(T,S)
is defined as
|S|
, and it is called a
k
-trace if
|S|=k
. We say that a subset
F\subseteq V

realizes
a trace
(T,S)
if
F\cap S=T
, and we denote by

traces_{k}(F,V):=\{(F\cap S,S)\mid S\subseteq V,\ |S|=k\}

the set of
k
-traces realized by
F
on
V
.
When the set
V
is clear from the context, we will simply use
traces_{k}(F)
instead of
traces_{k}(F,V)
.

For a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
we denote by

traces_{k}(\mathcal{H}):=\bigcup\limits_{F\in\mathcal{H}}traces_{k}(F,V(\mathcal{H}))

the set of traces realized by its hyperedges. In other words, a
k
-trace
(T,S)
belongs to
traces_{k}(\mathcal{H})
if there exists a hyperedge
F\in\mathcal{H}
such that
F\cap S=T
.
A subset
E\subseteq V(\mathcal{H})
is
k
-compatible
with
\mathcal{H}
if
traces_{k}(E)\subseteq traces_{k}(\mathcal{H})
, i.e., for each
k
-subset
S\subseteq V(\mathcal{H})
there exists
F\in\mathcal{H}
such that
E\cap S=F\cap S
. Given a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
and
k\leq|V(\mathcal{H})|
, the
k
-extension of
\mathcal{H}
,
ext_{k}(\mathcal{H})
, is the hypergraph on
V(\mathcal{H})
whose hyperedges are all subsets of
V
that are
k
-compatible with
\mathcal{H}
, i.e.
ext_{k}(\mathcal{H}):=(V(\mathcal{H}),\{E\mid E\subseteq V,traces_{k}(E)\subseteq traces_{k}(\mathcal{H})\})
. Since in particular every hyperedge of
\mathcal{H}
is
k
-compatible with
\mathcal{H}
,
\mathcal{H}\subseteq ext_{k}(\mathcal{H})
.

A subset of vertices
U\subseteq V(\mathcal{H})
is
shattered
in
\mathcal{H}
if for all
U^{\prime}\subseteq U
, there exists
F\in\mathcal{H}
such that
F\cap U=U^{\prime}
i.e. if
(U^{\prime},U)\in traces_{|U|}(\mathcal{H})
for all
U^{\prime}\subseteq U
. The
VC-dimension
of
\mathcal{H}
,
\textsc{VC-dim}(\mathcal{H})
, is the size of its largest shattered set. Using the trace definition,
\textsc{VC-dim}(\mathcal{H})<k
if for all
k
-subsets
S
of
V
there exists
T\subseteq S
such that
(T,S)\notin traces_{k}(\mathcal{H})
.
One of the most important properties of hypergraphs of bounded dimension is given by the Sauer–Shelah Lemma.

Lemma 2

(Sauer–Shelah Lemma)
.

If
\mathcal{H}
is of VC-dimension strictly less than
k
, then
|\mathcal{H}|=O(|V|^{k})
.

Corollary 3
.

If
\mathcal{H}
is of VC-dimension strictly less than
k
, then
|ext_{k}(\mathcal{H})|=O(|V|^{k})
.

Proof.

Since by definition,
ext_{k}(\mathcal{H})
has exactly the same
k
-traces as
\mathcal{H}
, a subset of vertices of size
k
is shattered in
ext_{k}(\mathcal{H})
if and only if it is shattered in
\mathcal{H}
. Since
\mathcal{H}
shatters no subset of size
k
,
\textsc{VC-dim}(ext_{k}(\mathcal{H}))<k
. The result follows from Lemma
2
.
∎

Given a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
and a subset of hyperedges
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
, the hypergraph
(\bigcup\limits_{F\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}F,\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
is called the

of
\mathcal{H}
induced by
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
. Given a subset of vertices
V^{\prime}\subseteq V(\mathcal{H})
the hypergraph
(V^{\prime},\{F\cap V^{\prime}\mid F\in\mathcal{H},\;F\cap V^{\prime}\neq\emptyset\})
is the
subhypergraph
induced by
V^{\prime}
. Given
V^{\prime}\subseteq V
and
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
the
partial subhypergraph
induced by
V^{\prime}
and

is the hypergraph
(V^{\prime},\{F\cap V^{\prime}\mid F\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime},\;F\cap V^{\prime}\neq\emptyset\})
. A partial subhypergraph of
\mathcal{H}
that does not contain isolated vertices (i.e. vertices that do not belong to any hyperedge) is a partial hypergraph of a subhypergraph of
\mathcal{H}
or equivalently a subhypergraph of a partial hypergraph of

.

Main algorithm

In this section we assume that we are given a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
with
\textsc{VC-dim}(\mathcal{H})<k
and a hypergraph
\mathcal{G}\subseteq Tr(\mathcal{H})
. We want to decide whether
Tr(\mathcal{H})=\mathcal{G}
, or equivalently whether
\mathcal{G}=Tr(\mathcal{H})
, and find a new minimal transversal in
Tr(\mathcal{H})\setminus\mathcal{G}
otherwise. We present Algorithm
1
to solve this problem whose running time is
O(2^{k}(n|\mathcal{G}|)^{k+1}+n^{2k+1}|\mathcal{G}|)
as stated by Theorem
1
.

Notice that we can assume that
\mathcal{H}\subseteq Tr(\mathcal{G})
since otherwise the answer is no, and we can easily find a new minimal transversal in
Tr(\mathcal{H})\setminus\mathcal{G}
. Indeed assume that there exists
E\in\mathcal{F}
such that

is not a minimal transversal of
\mathcal{G}

T
of
\mathcal{H}

belongs to

.

The algorithm will actually either answer that

or find a new minimal transversal

We are trying to find

(i.e.

The strategy is summarized in Algorithm

The first loop of Algorithm

.

For any

Let us show first that if a set

Assume first that

3.1
Finding a minimal transversal satisfying a
k
-trace

In this section, we show that the condition in line 3 of Algorithm
1
can be checked in polynomial time. More precisely, given a
k
-trace
(T,S)
, we show that we can check in time
O(n|\mathcal{G}|^{|T|+1})
whether there exists a minimal transversal of
\mathcal{G}
realizing
(T,S)
. When
k
is a constant,
T
is of constant size and the above-mentioned complexity is polynomial. To do so, we reduce the problem to deciding whether
T
is a sub-transversal of a subhypergraph of
\mathcal{G}
. A set of vertices
T
is a

of
\mathcal{H}
if there exists a minimal transversal
E\in Tr(\mathcal{H})
such that
T\subseteq E
. In

5
]
, the authors proved that one can check in polynomial time whether a subset of vertices

of bounded size is a sub-transversal.

Lemma 5

(
[
5
]
)
.

Given a subset of vertices
T
, one can check in time
O(n|\mathcal{H}|^{|T|+1})
whether
T
is a sub-transversal of
\mathcal{H}
.

Lemma 6
.

Let
(T,S)
be a
k
-trace on
V
and let
V^{\prime}:=V\setminus(S\setminus T)
. Then, there exists a minimal transversal of
\mathcal{G}
realizing
(T,S)
if and only if
T
is a sub-transversal of the subhypergraph
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}=(V^{\prime},\{F\cap V^{\prime}\mid F\in\mathcal{G}\})

Proof.

Observe first that
E\in Tr(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})
if and only if
E
is a minimal transversal of
\mathcal{G}
such that
E\subseteq V^{\prime}
. Assume first that
T
is a sub-transversal of
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
. There exists
E\in Tr(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})
such that

. Since
E
is a minimal transversal of
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
, it is also a minimal transversal of
\mathcal{G}
. Now, since

and

, we have

, and thus

.
Assume now that there exists

such that

(T,S)
. We have

, and hence

. Therefore,

is a minimal transversal of

.
∎

Note that in the lemma above,

may contain empty hyperedges, namely when some hyperedges of

are entirely contained in

(and thus become empty after restriction to

). In that case, no minimal transversal of
\mathcal{G}
can realize

has no minimal transversals (a hypergraph with an empty hyperedge has no transversals).

Combining the two previous lemmas, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 7
.

Given a
k
-trace
(T,S)
, one can check in time
O(n|\mathcal{G}|^{|T|+1})
whether there exists a minimal transversal

of
\mathcal{G}

(T,S)
.

For

The first immediate consequence of Theorem

Corollary 11
.

Trans-Hyp

Even if several quasi-polynomial time algorithms are already known for general hypergraphs, we observe Algorithm

(n|\mathcal{G}|)^{O(\log(\mathcal{H}))}

Another important property of Algorithm

]
, a hypergraph

As already mentioned in the introduction, a consequence of Theorem

4.1

Lemma 15
.

4.2
Other subhypergraphs types

Several types of subhypergraphs have been defined in the literature namely,
partial hypergraphs
,
subhypergraphs
,
partial subhypergraphs
,
edge-induced subhypergraphs
and
restrictions
.
Even if the concepts of partial hypergraphs, subhypergraphs and partial subhypergraphs have been already defined, we recall them for comparison to the other notions. For a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}=(V,\mathcal{F})
,

•

a
partial hypergraph
of
\mathcal{H}
is a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}=(V,\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
, for
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
;

•

a
subhypergraph
of
\mathcal{H}
is a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}=(V^{\prime},\{F\cap V^{\prime}\mid F\in\mathcal{F},F\cap V^{\prime}\neq\emptyset\})
, for
V^{\prime}\subseteq V
;

•

a
partial subhypergraph
of
\mathcal{H}
is a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}=(V^{\prime},\{F\cap V^{\prime}\mid F\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\})
, for
V^{\prime}\subseteq V
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
;

•

an
edge-induced subhypergraph
of
\mathcal{H}
is a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}=(\bigcup\limits_{F\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}F,\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
, for
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
;

•

a
restriction
of
\mathcal{H}
is a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}=(V^{\prime},\{F\in\mathcal{F}\mid F\subseteq V^{\prime}\})
, for
V^{\prime}\subseteq V
.

We show that a result similar to the one obtained in Corollary
1
for the other notions of subhypergraphs would imply a polynomial time algorithm for the general problem.
More precisely we focus on the class of hypergraphs that forbids a specific hypergraph
\mathcal{F}
as subhypergraph. Given a hypergraph
\mathcal{F}
, we denote by
\mathscr{H}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\textsc{S}}
,
\mathscr{H}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\textsc{Es}}
,
\mathscr{H}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\textsc{R}}
, the set of hypergraphs that forbid
\mathcal{F}
respectively as subgraph, edge-induced subgraph and restriction.
For the case of edge-induced subhypergraphs, for any hypergraph
\mathcal{F}
, we prove that the
Trans-Hyp
problem in the class
\mathscr{H}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\textsc{Es}}
is as hard as in the general case.

For the case of restrictions and induced subhypergraphs, the difficulty of
Trans-Hyp
in the classes
\mathscr{H}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\textsc{R}}
and
\mathscr{H}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\textsc{S}}
depends on the hypergraph
\mathcal{F}
. For example, if
\mathcal{F}
is the complete hypergraph on
k
vertices (the hypergraph that contains all subsets as hyperedges), then the class
\mathscr{H}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\textsc{S}}
is precisely the class of hypergraphs of VC-dimension strictly smaller than
k
and by Theorem
1
the problem is polynomial. However, if the hypergraph
\mathcal{F}
is sparse enough, we prove that the
Trans-Hyp
problem restricted to the class
\mathscr{H}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\textsc{S}}
is as hard as in general hypergraphs.

For the case of restrictions, if
\mathcal{F}
is the empty hypergraph on
k
vertices (the hypergraph without any hyperedge), then the class
\mathscr{H}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\textsc{R}}
is the class of hypergraph for which the maximum independent set is strictly smaller than
k
. Since maximal independent sets are the complements of minimal transversals, one can enumerate all subsets of size smaller than
k
and check whether the complement is a minimal transversal. Therefore, if
\mathcal{F}
is the empty hypergraph, then
Trans-Hyp
problem in
\mathscr{H}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\textsc{R}}
is polynomial. In any other case, we prove that
Trans-Hyp
problem restricted to
\mathscr{H}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\textsc{R}}
is as hard as the general problem.

In order to prove the results of this section, we will use two different reductions. Given a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
and an integer
k
, we define
\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{k}
to be the hypergraph obtained from
\mathcal{H}
by adding
k
new vertices to every hyperedges, and
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
to be the hypergraph obtained from
\mathcal{H}
by adding
\binom{n}{k}
new hyperedges of size
k+1
corresponding to all
k
-subsets of vertices of
\mathcal{H}
plus a new vertex

.

•

\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{k}=(V(\mathcal{H})\cup\{x_{1},...,x_{k}\},\{E\cup\{x_{1},...,x_{k}\}\mid E\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{H})\})

•

\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{k}=(V(\mathcal{H})\cup\{x\},\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{H})\cup\{X\cup\{x\}\mid X\subseteq V(\mathcal{H}),|X|\leq k\})

We now show how the enumeration of minimal transversal of
\mathcal{H}
can be reduced to the enumeration of minimal transversal of
\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{k}
and
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
.

. Assume now that
x\in U
. Since we assumed that
\mathcal{H}
has at least
k
vertices,
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\ell}
has at least
k+1
vertices and there exists a vertex
y\notin U
. Now for any subset
E
of
U\setminus\{x\}
, of size
\ell-1
, there exists a hyperedge
E\cup\{x,y\}
in
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\ell}
and so the subhypergraph induced by
U
has
E\cup\{x\}
as hyperedge. So the subhypergraph induced by
U
contains at least
\binom{k-1}{\ell-1}
hyperedges of size
\ell
and cannot be isomorphic to
\mathcal{F}
.
∎

Theorem 20
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a hypergraph, then the restriction of the
Trans-Hyp
problem to the following classes of hypergraph is as hard as the
Trans-Hyp
problem in general hypergraphs:

•

\mathscr{H}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\textsc{Es}}

•

\mathscr{H}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\textsc{R}}
if
\mathcal{F}
is different from the empty hypergraph.

•

\mathscr{H}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\textsc{S}}
if there exists
\ell\leq|V(\mathcal{F})|
such that
\mathcal{F}
has less than
\binom{|V(\mathcal{F})|-1}{\ell-1}
hyperedges of size
\ell
.

Proof.

By lemma
19
it is sufficient to prove that for a given integer
k
, one can reduce in polynomial time the enumeration of minimal transversal of
\mathcal{H}
to the enumeration of minimal transversal of
\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{k}
and
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
.

Assume that one can enumerate the minimal transversal of
\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{k}
in
O((|\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{k}|+|Tr(\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{k})|)^{\ell})
. By lemma
17
,
Tr(\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{k})=Tr(\mathcal{H})\cup\{\{x\}\mid x\in V(\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{k})\setminus V(\mathcal{H})\}
. So one can simply check for each
T\in Tr(\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{k})
whether
T
is a minimal transversal of
\mathcal{H}
. Since
|\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{k}|=|\mathcal{H}|
and
|Tr(\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{k})|=|Tr(\mathcal{H})|+k
, the total running time is
O((|\mathcal{H}|+|Tr(\mathcal{H})|)^{\ell})
.

Assume now that one can enumerate the minimal transversal of
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
in
O((|\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{k}|+|Tr(\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{k})|)^{\ell})
. By lemma
18
,
Tr(\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{k})=\{T\cup\{x\}\mid T\in Tr(\mathcal{H}),|T|<n-k+1\}\cup\mathcal{J}
where
\mathcal{J}
only contains subsets of size larger than
n-k+1
. One can start by enumerating all minimal transversal of
\mathcal{H}
of size larger than
n-k+1
in a brute-force way by checking all the
O(n^{k-1})
such subsets. Then for every
T\in Tr(\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{k})
with
|T|\leq n-k+1
that contains
x
, output
T\setminus\{x\}
(where
x
is the additional vertex of
V(\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{k})\setminus V(\mathcal{H})
). Since
|\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{k}|=O(|\mathcal{H}|+n^{k})
and
|Tr(\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{k})|=O(|Tr(\mathcal{H})|+n^{k})
, the total running time is
O(|\mathcal{H}|+|Tr(\mathcal{H})|)^{\ell k}
.

∎

5
Discussions

Although the existence of a polynomial-time algorithm for
Trans-Hyp
remains a widely open problem, we show that
Trans-Hyp
can be solved in polynomial time whenever one of the two input hypergraphs has bounded VC-dimension. This result generalizes many existing tractable cases and yields a new quasi-polynomial-time algorithm for general hypergraphs.

A natural question is whether
Trans-Hyp
, parameterized by the minimum VC-dimension of the two input hypergraphs, is fixed-parameter tractable (FPT). From the viewpoint of parameterized complexity, our result shows that the problem is in XP (i.e., solvable in time
N^{f(k)}
for some function
f
, where
N
is the total input size), but the existence of an FPT algorithm remains open.

Open problem 1:
Given
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{G}
with
\textsc{VC-dim}(\mathcal{H})<k
, can we decide in time
f(k)\cdot p(|\mathcal{H}|+|\mathcal{G}|)
whether
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{G}
are dual, where
f
is a computable function and
p
is a polynomial?

As a weaker question, can one remove the exponential dependence on
k
from the contribution of
|\mathcal{G}|
in the running time? This would make sense from the perspective of the enumeration problem (
Trans-Enum
), since the exponential dependence on
k
would then be carried only by the input size. Since, by Lemma
2
, the number of hyperedges of
\mathcal{H}
is polynomially bounded in
n
, this amounts to asking whether the exponential dependence on
k
can be confined to the number of vertices.

Open problem 2:
Given
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{G}
with
\textsc{VC-dim}(\mathcal{H})<k
, can we decide in time
n^{f(k)}\cdot p(|\mathcal{H}|+|\mathcal{G}|)
whether
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{G}
are dual, where
f
is a computable function,
p
is a polynomial, and
n
is the number of vertices of
\mathcal{H}
(equivalently, of
\mathcal{G}
)?

Finally, for which hypergraphs
\mathcal{F}
can
Trans-Enum
, restricted to the class
\mathscr{H}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\textsc{S}}
of hypergraphs forbidding
\mathcal{F}
as an induced subhypergraph, be solved in polynomial time? By Theorem
20
, a positive answer for sparse hypergraphs
\mathcal{F}
would imply a polynomial-time algorithm in the general case, but the question remains open for dense hypergraphs.

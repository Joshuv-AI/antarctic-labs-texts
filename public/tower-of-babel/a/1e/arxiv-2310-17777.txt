A Unified Treatment of Tractability for Approximation Problems Defined on Hilbert Spaces
Onyekachi Emenike, Fred J. Hickernell, Peter Kritzer

Abstract
A large literature specifies conditions under which the information complexity for a sequence of numerical problems defined for dimensions 1 , 2 , … 1,2,\ldots grows at a moderate rate, i.e., the sequence of problems is tractable . Here, we focus on the situation where the space of available information consists of all linear functionals, and the problems are defined as linear operator mappings between Hilbert spaces. We unify the proofs of known tractability results and generalize a number of existing results. These generalizations are expressed as five theorems that provide equivalent conditions for (strong) tractability in terms of sums of functions of the singular values of the solution operators.

Introduction

The information complexity of a problem is the number of function data required to solve the problem within the desired error tolerance. A problem is tractable if the information complexity does not grow too large as the dimension of the problem increases or the error threshold decreases. There are a number of tractability results for numerical problems, beginning in the 1990s with
[
17
,
18

[

The authors have identified common themes in the proofs of necessary and sufficient conditions for tractability that appear in the literature. By abstracting these themes, this article aims to unify and generalize many existing tractability results for approximation problems where all possible linear functional data are available. This is done in a series of five theorems, which provide necessary and sufficient conditions for (strong) tractability.

\displaystyle K_{\boldsymbol{p},\tau}\,T(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},1,\tau\boldsymbol{p})\geq[T(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},1,\boldsymbol{p})]^{\tau}\geq\left[\frac{(i-1)}{C_{\boldsymbol{p}}}\right]^{\tau}\qquad\forall i\geq i(\infty,\boldsymbol{p}),\ d\in\mathbb{N},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},1,\tau\boldsymbol{p})}\leq\frac{K_{\boldsymbol{p},\tau}\,C_{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\tau}}{(i-1)^{\tau}}\qquad\forall i\geq\max\{i(\infty,\boldsymbol{p}),2\},\ d\in\mathbb{N}.

Summing both sides of this last inequality over
i
from
i(\infty,\boldsymbol{p})+1\geq 2
to
\infty
yields

\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{i=i(\infty,\boldsymbol{p})+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{T(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},1,\tau\boldsymbol{p})}\leq K_{\boldsymbol{p},\tau}\,C_{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\tau}\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(i-1)^{\tau}}\\
\leq K_{\boldsymbol{p},\tau}\,C_{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\tau}\zeta(\tau)<\infty,

where
\zeta
denotes the Riemann zeta function.
This yields (
11
) with
\boldsymbol{p}
replaced by
\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}=\tau\boldsymbol{p}
and
L_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}=i(\infty,\boldsymbol{p})+1
, and so we see the necessity of
(
11
)
.

Optimality:

To complete the proof, we must show that
\mathcal{P}_{\textup{strct}}=\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{strct}}
. Then, the expression for
\mathcal{P}_{\textup{sopt}}
automatically follows from its definition in (
10
).

First we show that if
\boldsymbol{p}^{*}\in\mathcal{P}_{\textup{strct}}
, then
\boldsymbol{p}^{*}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{strct}}
. If
\boldsymbol{p}^{*}\in\mathcal{P}_{\textup{strct}}
, then (
6
) must hold for all
\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}\in(\boldsymbol{p}^{*},\boldsymbol{\infty})
. For any
\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}\in(\boldsymbol{p}^{*},\boldsymbol{\infty})
, we may choose
\boldsymbol{p}
and
\tau
such that
\boldsymbol{p}^{*}<\boldsymbol{p}<\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}=\tau\boldsymbol{p}
. Since
\boldsymbol{p}>\boldsymbol{p}^{*}
, (
6
) also holds for
\boldsymbol{p}
, and it follows that (
11
) must hold for
\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}
by the proof of necessity above. Thus,
\boldsymbol{p}^{*}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{strct}}
.

Next we show that if
\boldsymbol{p}^{*}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{strct}}
, then
\boldsymbol{p}^{*}\in\mathcal{P}_{\textup{strct}}
. If
\boldsymbol{p}^{*}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{strct}}
, then (
11
) holds for all
\boldsymbol{p}\in(\boldsymbol{p}^{*},\boldsymbol{\infty})
. By the argument to prove the sufficient condition above, it follows that (
6
) must also hold for all
\boldsymbol{p}\in(\boldsymbol{p}^{*},\boldsymbol{\infty})
. Thus,
\boldsymbol{p}^{*}\in\mathcal{P}_{\textup{strct}}
.

This concludes the proof of Theorem
1
.

2.3
Tractability

The argument proving equivalent conditions for tractability is similar to, but somewhat more involved than the proof of Theorem
1
. The lower limit on the sum is somewhat more complicated as well.

Theorem 2
.

Let
T
be a tractability function as specified in (
3
) and satisfying (
7
) and (
8
). A problem is
T
-tractable iff there exists
\boldsymbol{p}\geq\boldsymbol{0}
and a positive constant
L_{\boldsymbol{p}}
such that

S_{\boldsymbol{p}}:=\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{i=\lceil L_{\boldsymbol{p}}\,T(0,d,\boldsymbol{p})\rceil}^{\infty}\frac{1}{T(\lambda^{-1}_{i,d},d,\boldsymbol{p})}<\infty.

(16)

If (
16
) holds for some
\boldsymbol{p}
, let
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{trct}}:=\{\boldsymbol{p}^{*}:\eqref{eq:tractiff4}\text{ holds }\forall\boldsymbol{p}\in(\boldsymbol{p}^{*},\boldsymbol{\infty})\}
.
Then
\mathcal{P}_{\textup{trct}}=\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{trct}}
, and the set of optimal tractability parameters is

\mathcal{P}_{\textup{opt}}=\{\boldsymbol{p}^{*}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{trct}}:\boldsymbol{p}^{*}\notin[\widetilde{\boldsymbol{p}},\boldsymbol{\infty})\ \forall\widetilde{\boldsymbol{p}}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{trct}}\setminus\{\boldsymbol{p}^{*}\}\}.

Comparing the equivalent conditions for strong tractability in (
11
) and tractability in (
16
), they are seen to be nearly the same. The difference lies in the lower summation index, which is allowed to depend on
d
for tractability, and the second argument of
T
in the sum, which is
1
for strong tractability, and
d
in general. Both of these can allow
S_{\boldsymbol{p}}
to be finite in Theorem
2
, when it may be infinite in Theorem
1
.

Proof.

Sufficient condition:

Suppose that (
16
) holds for some
\boldsymbol{p}\geq\boldsymbol{0}
.
By the argument in the proof of the sufficient condition for Theorem
1
, it follows from (
13
) with
\mathscr{D}=d
and
N=\lceil L_{\boldsymbol{p}}\,T(0,d,\boldsymbol{p})\rceil
that

\displaystyle\comp(\varepsilon,d)

\displaystyle\leq T(\varepsilon^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p})\Biggl[\frac{\lceil L_{\boldsymbol{p}}\,T(0,d,\boldsymbol{p})\rceil}{T(\varepsilon^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p})}+\underbrace{\sum_{i=\lceil L_{\boldsymbol{p}}\,T(0,d,\boldsymbol{p})\rceil}^{\infty}\frac{1}{T(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p})}}_{\leq S_{\boldsymbol{p}}\text{ by \eqref{eq:tractiff4}}}\Biggr]\quad\forall\varepsilon>0,\ d\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\leq T(\varepsilon^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p})\left[\frac{L_{\boldsymbol{p}}T(0,d,\boldsymbol{p})+1}{T(\varepsilon^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p})}+S_{\boldsymbol{p}}\right]\qquad\forall\varepsilon>0,\ d\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\leq T(\varepsilon^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p})\left[\frac{L_{\boldsymbol{p}}T(0,d,\boldsymbol{p})}{T(\varepsilon^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p})}+\frac{1}{T(0,1,\boldsymbol{p})}+S_{\boldsymbol{p}}\right]\qquad\text{by \eqref{eq:Tnondecreasing}}

\displaystyle\leq T(\varepsilon^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p})\underbrace{\left[L_{\boldsymbol{p}}+\frac{1}{T(0,1,\boldsymbol{p})}+S_{\boldsymbol{p}}\right]}_{=:C_{\boldsymbol{p}}}\qquad\text{by \eqref{eq:Tnondecreasing}}.

It follows that we have
T
-tractability, which shows sufficiency of (
16
).

Necessary condition:

Suppose that we have

T
-tractability. That is, for some
\boldsymbol{p}\geq\boldsymbol{0}
, there exists a positive constant
C_{\boldsymbol{p}}
such that

\comp(\varepsilon,d)\leq C_{\boldsymbol{p}}\,T(\varepsilon^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p})\qquad\forall\varepsilon>0,\ d\in\mathbb{N}.

Since the sequence of singular values
\lambda_{1,d},\lambda_{2,d},\ldots
is non-increasing, we have

\lambda_{\lfloor C_{\boldsymbol{p}}\,T(\varepsilon^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p})\rfloor+1,d}\leq\varepsilon\qquad\forall\varepsilon>0,\ d\in\mathbb{N}.

(17)

Define the integers

i(\varepsilon,d,\boldsymbol{p}):=\lfloor C_{\boldsymbol{p}}\,T(\varepsilon^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p})\rfloor+1,\qquad i(\infty,d,\boldsymbol{p}):=\lfloor C_{\boldsymbol{p}}\,T(0,d,\boldsymbol{p})\rfloor+1\geq 1.

Thus, it follows by (
17
) that
\lambda_{i(\varepsilon,d,\boldsymbol{p}),d}\leq\varepsilon
.
Note furthermore that we always have

i(\varepsilon,d,\boldsymbol{p})\leq C_{\boldsymbol{p}}\,T(\varepsilon^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p})+1\leq C_{\boldsymbol{p}}T(\lambda_{i(\varepsilon,d,\boldsymbol{p}),d}^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p})+1\qquad\forall\varepsilon>0,\ d\in\mathbb{N},

since

T(\cdot,d,\boldsymbol{p})
is non-decreasing.

For
\varepsilon
taking on all positive values,
i(\varepsilon,d,\boldsymbol{p})
takes on, by (
7b
), all values greater than or equal to
i(\infty,d,\boldsymbol{p})
, so

i\leq C_{\boldsymbol{p}}\,T(\varepsilon^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p})+1\leq C_{\boldsymbol{p}}T(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p})+1\qquad\forall i\geq i(\infty,d,\boldsymbol{p}),\ d\in\mathbb{N}.

This implies via our technical assumption (
8
) that for any
\tau>1
,

\displaystyle K_{\boldsymbol{p},\tau}\,T(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},d,\tau\boldsymbol{p})\geq[T(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p})]^{\tau}\geq\left[\frac{(i-1)}{C_{\boldsymbol{p}}}\right]^{\tau}\qquad\forall i\geq i(\infty,d,\boldsymbol{p}),\ d\in\mathbb{N},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},d,\tau\boldsymbol{p})}\leq\frac{K_{\boldsymbol{p},\tau}\,C_{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\tau}}{(i-1)^{\tau}}\qquad\forall i\geq\max\{i(\infty,d,\boldsymbol{p}),2\},\ d\in\mathbb{N}.

Let
\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}=\tau\boldsymbol{p}
. Note that
i(\infty,d,\cdot)
is non-decreasing. Summing both sides of the latter inequality over
i
from
i(\infty,d,\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime})+1\geq i(\infty,d,\boldsymbol{p})+1\geq 2
to
\infty
it follows that

\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\,\,\sum_{i=i(\infty,d,\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime})+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{T(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime})}\leq K_{\boldsymbol{p},\tau}\,C_{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\tau}\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(i-1)^{\tau}}\\
\leq K_{\boldsymbol{p},\tau}\,C_{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\tau}\zeta(\tau)<\infty,

where
\zeta
denotes the Riemann zeta function.

Note now that, for any
d\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle i(\infty,d,\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime})+1

\displaystyle=\lfloor C_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}\,T(0,d,\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime})\rfloor+2\leq\left[C_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}+\frac{2}{T(0,d,\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime})}\right]\,T(0,d,\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{\left[C_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}+\frac{2}{T(0,1,\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime})}\right]}_{=:L_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}}\,T(0,d,\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime})\leq\lceil L_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}T(0,d,\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime})\rceil.

For this choice of
L_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}
we have

\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\,\,\sum_{i=\lceil L_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}T(0,d,\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime})\rceil}^{\infty}\frac{1}{T(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime})}\leq\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\,\,\sum_{i=i(\infty,d,\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime})+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{T(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime})}<\infty.

This yields (
16
) with
\boldsymbol{p}
replaced by
\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}
, so we see the necessity of
(
16
)
.

Optimality:

This proof is similar to the proof for the optimality condition for strong tractability in Theorem
1
. We must show that
\mathcal{P}_{\textup{trct}}=\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{trct}}
. Then, the expression for
\mathcal{P}_{\textup{opt}}
in this theorem automatically follows from its definition in (
9
).

First we show that if
\boldsymbol{p}^{*}\in\mathcal{P}_{\textup{trct}}
, then
\boldsymbol{p}^{*}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{trct}}
. If
\boldsymbol{p}^{*}\in\mathcal{P}_{\textup{trct}}
, then (
5
) must hold for all
\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}\in(\boldsymbol{p}^{*},\boldsymbol{\infty})
. For any
\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}\in(\boldsymbol{p}^{*},\boldsymbol{\infty})
, we may choose
\boldsymbol{p}
and
\tau
such that
\boldsymbol{p}^{*}<\boldsymbol{p}<\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}=\tau\boldsymbol{p}
. Since
\boldsymbol{p}>\boldsymbol{p}^{*}
, (
5
) also holds for
\boldsymbol{p}
, and it follows that (
16
) must hold for
\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}
by the proof of necessity above. Thus,
\boldsymbol{p}^{*}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{trct}}
.

Next we show that if
\boldsymbol{p}^{*}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{trct}}
, then
\boldsymbol{p}^{*}\in\mathcal{P}_{\textup{trct}}
. If
\boldsymbol{p}^{*}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{trct}}
, then (
16
) holds for all
\boldsymbol{p}\in(\boldsymbol{p}^{*},\boldsymbol{\infty})
. By the argument to prove the sufficient condition above, it follows that (
5
) must also hold for all
\boldsymbol{p}\in(\boldsymbol{p}^{*},\boldsymbol{\infty})

3
Examples

In this section, we present six examples relating to Theorem
1
and Theorem
2
, both of which were shown in Section
2
, for the various notions of tractability that are listed in Table
2
. In the subsequent sections, we will continue to present various examples.

The concept of (strong) polynomial tractability for both the algebraic case and the exponential case will be first considered as follows:

Example 1

(Algebraic polynomial tractability)
.

Let the tractability function,
T
,
be defined by

T(\varepsilon^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p})=\max\{1,\varepsilon^{-p}\}\,d^{q}\qquad\forall\varepsilon>0,\,d\in\mathbb{N},\,\boldsymbol{p}\in[0,\infty)^{2},

where in this case
\boldsymbol{p}=(p,q)
.
Then Theorem
2
yields the equivalent condition for algebraic polynomial tractability:

\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\,d^{-q}\sum_{i=\lceil L_{(p,q)}d^{q}\rceil}^{\infty}\min\{1,\lambda_{i,d}^{p}\}<\infty\text{ for some }p,q\geq 0,L_{(p,q)}>0.

Furthermore, for optimality, let
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{ALG-trct}}:=\{(p^{*},q^{*}):\text{the above condition holds }\forall(p,q)\in(p^{*},\infty)\times(q^{*},\infty)\}
. Therefore, the set of optimal
(p,q)
is the set

\{(p^{*},q^{*})\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{ALG-trct}}:(p^{*},q^{*})\notin[\widetilde{p},\infty)\times[\widetilde{q},\infty)\,\,\forall(\widetilde{p},\widetilde{q})\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{ALG-trct}}\setminus\{p^{*},q^{*}\}\}.

This essentially recovers the result on polynomial tractability in
[
12
, Theorem 5.1]
.

Having defined the tractability function as above, we will now examine a specific case where we always set
d=1
, i.e.,

T(\varepsilon^{-1},1,\boldsymbol{p})=\max\{1,\varepsilon^{-\boldsymbol{p}}\}\qquad\forall\varepsilon>0,\,d\in\mathbb{N},\,\boldsymbol{p}\in[0,\infty),

where in this case
\boldsymbol{p}=p
is a scalar.

Then Theorem
1
yields the equivalent condition for algebraic strong polynomial tractability:

\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{i=L_{p}}^{\infty}\min\{1,\lambda_{i,d}^{p}\}<\infty\text{ for some }p\geq 0,L_{p}\in\mathbb{N}.

Furthermore, the optimal
p
is the infimum of all
p
for which the above condition holds. This essentially recovers the result in
[
12
, Theorem 5.1]
.

Next, we study the exponential case where we replace
\varepsilon^{-1}
by
\log(1+\varepsilon^{-1})
and consider the same notions of tractability as for the algebraic case.

Example 2

(Exponential polynomial tractability)
.

Let the tractability function,
T
,
be defined by

T(\varepsilon^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p})=\left[\max\{1,\log(1+\varepsilon^{-1})\}\right]^{p}\,d^{q}\qquad\forall\varepsilon>0,\,d\in\mathbb{N},\,\boldsymbol{p}\in[0,\infty)^{2},

where
\boldsymbol{p}=(p,q)
. Then Theorem
2
yields the equivalent condition for exponential polynomial tractability:

\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\,d^{-q}\sum_{i=\lceil L_{(p,q)}d^{q}\rceil}^{\infty}\frac{1}{[\max\{1,\log(1+\lambda_{i,d}^{-1})\}]^{p}}<\infty\text{ for some }p,q\geq 0,L_{(p,q)}>0.

(18)

Furthermore, for optimality, let
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{EXP-trct}}:=\{(p^{*},q^{*}):\text{the above condition holds }\forall(p,q)\in(p^{*},\infty)\times(q^{*},\infty)\}
. Therefore, the set of optimal
(p,q)
is the set

\{(p^{*},q^{*})\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{EXP-trct}}:(p^{*},q^{*})\notin[\widetilde{p},\infty)\times[\widetilde{q},\infty)\,\,\forall(\widetilde{p},\widetilde{q})\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{EXP-trct}}\setminus\{p^{*},q^{*}\}\}.

Again, we will look at the special case in which we always set
d=1
. In this case, we will define
T
as follows.

T(\varepsilon^{-1},1,p)=[\max\{1,\log(1+\varepsilon^{-1})\}]^{\boldsymbol{p}}\quad\forall\varepsilon>0,\,d\in\mathbb{N},\,p\in[0,\infty),

where
\boldsymbol{p}=p
is a scalar. Then Theorem
1
yields the equivalent condition for exponential strong polynomial tractability:

\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{i=L_{p}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{[\max\{1,\log(1+\lambda_{i,d}^{-1})\}]^{p}}<\infty,\quad\text{ for some }p\geq 0,L_{p}\in\mathbb{N}.

(19)

In this case, the optimal
p
is the infimum of all
p
for which the above condition holds.

In the paper
[
11
]
, the authors derived conditions equivalent to exponential strong polynomial tractability and exponential polynomial tractability, respectively. These conditions read (with notation slightly adapted to our present paper) as follows:

•

We have exponential strong polynomial tractability iff there exists a
\tau>0
and
a
C\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{i=C}^{\infty}\lambda_{i,d}^{i^{-\tau}}<\infty.

(20)

•

We have exponential polynomial tractability iff there exist
\tau_{1},\tau_{3}\geq 0
and
\tau_{2},C>0
such that

\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N}}d^{-\tau_{1}}\sum_{i=\lceil Cd^{\tau_{3}}\rceil}^{\infty}\lambda_{i,d}^{i^{-\tau_{2}}}<\infty.

(21)

Firstly, we remark that the role of
L_{p}
in (
19
) corresponds to that of
C
in (
20
).
Moreover,
q
in (
18
) has a similar role as
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{3}
in (
21
) (i.e., (
21
) is slightly more precise than (
18
) regarding the exponents of
d
, but this is just a minor difference); furthermore
L_{(p,q)}
in (
18
) corresponds to
C
in (
21
).

Secondly, it should be noted that our condition (
19
) is easier to check in practice than condition (
20
). Since in (
20
) the summation indices
i
show up in the exponents of the summands, we need the exact ordering of the singular values
\lambda_{i,d}
, whereas in (
19
) we only have a summation of the values of
1/T(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p})
, and the exact order of the singular values can be neglected. A similar
observation holds for (
18
) and (
21
).

The following proposition states that the conditions (
19
) and (
20
), as well as the conditions (
18
) and (
21
), are indeed equivalent.
The proof of this result is deferred to Appendix 2 where we will prove this statement for the latter two conditions, i.e., for the conditions on exponential polynomial tractability. The case of exponential strong polynomial tractability is not explicitly included, as it is actually simpler and can be treated analogously. We give a short example of how we can apply the condition (
19
) to a concrete problem in Appendix 1.

Proposition 1
.

The conditions (
19
) and (
20
) are equivalent. Furthermore, the conditions (
18
) and (
21
) are equivalent.

Next, we provide a generalization of the above examples of tractability. We would like to emphasize that this example was previously known (see, e.g.,
[
4
]
) where in this case the roles of
\varepsilon^{-1}
and
d
are separated. In order to accomplish this, we will first define two functions
\phi
and
\psi
with positive values that are increasing in both of their arguments. The equivalent conditions for this example take the same form as those in Theorems
1
and
2
.

Example 3

((Strong) Separable tractability)
.

Suppose that the tractability function is defined by

T(\varepsilon^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p})=\phi(\varepsilon^{-1},p)\,\psi(d,q)\qquad\forall\varepsilon>0,\,d\in\mathbb{N},\,\boldsymbol{p}\in[0,\infty)^{2},

with non-decreasing functions
\phi:(0,\infty)\times(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
and
\psi:\mathbb{N}\times(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
and where
\boldsymbol{p}=(p,q)
, hence
T
is non-decreasing in all variables.

We say that a problem is
T
-separably tractable with parameter
\boldsymbol{p}
iff there exists a positive constant
C_{\boldsymbol{p}}
which does not depend on
\varepsilon
and
d
such that

\comp(\varepsilon,d)\leq C_{\boldsymbol{p}}\,\phi(\varepsilon^{-1},p)\,\psi(d,q)\qquad\forall\varepsilon>0,\,d\in\mathbb{N}.

A problem is strongly
T
-separably tractable with parameter
\boldsymbol{p}
iff the information complexity is independent of
d
, that is, there exists a positive constant
C_{\boldsymbol{p}}
which again does not depend on
\varepsilon
and
d
such that

\comp(\varepsilon,d)\leq C_{\boldsymbol{p}}\,\phi(\varepsilon^{-1},p)\qquad\forall\varepsilon>0,\,d\in\mathbb{N},

where in this case
\boldsymbol{p}=p
is a scalar.
Furthermore, we assume the following, which corresponds to the conditions in (
7b
), (
7c
), and (
8
). We assume that

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\phi(\varepsilon^{-1},p)=\infty\qquad\forall d\in\mathbb{N},p>0,

since there are an infinite number of positive singular values. Furthermore, we have that
\phi(\cdot,p)
is non-decreasing, hence we define the following limit

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow\infty}\phi(\varepsilon^{-1},p)>0.

Additionally, we have the following technical assumptions. Suppose that, for any real
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}>0
and any
p,q>0
, we have that

\displaystyle[\phi(\varepsilon^{-1},p)]^{\tau_{1}}\leq\hat{K}_{p,\tau_{1}}\phi(\varepsilon^{-1},\tau_{1}p)\quad\mbox{and}\quad[\psi(d,q)]^{\tau_{2}}\leq\hat{K}_{q,\tau_{2}}\psi(d,\tau_{2}q)\qquad\forall\,\varepsilon>0,\,d\in\mathbb{N},

where
\hat{K}_{p,\tau_{1}}>0
is a constant that may depend on
p
and
\tau_{1}
, but is independent of
\varepsilon
, and where
\hat{K}_{q,\tau_{2}}>0
is a constant that may depend on
q
and
\tau_{2}
, but is independent of
d
.

Theorem
1
yields the following equivalent condition for strong
T
-separable tractability:

\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{i=L_{p}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\phi(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},p)}<\infty,

for some
p>0
and
L_{p}\in\mathbb{N}
. Furthermore, the optimal
p
is also the infimum of all
p
satisfying the latter condition. Also,
Theorem
2
yields the following equivalent condition for
T
-separable tractability:

\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{\psi(d,q)}\sum_{i=\lceil L_{(p,q)}\psi(d,q)\rceil}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\phi(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},p)}<\infty,

for some
p,q>0
and a positive constant
L_{(p,q)}
.

Next, we introduce another notion of tractability called
non-separable tractability
. See
[
5
]
, which is probably where the term “non-separable tractability” was first introduced.
In the following, we will define two functions
\hat{\phi}
and
\hat{\psi}
that take the roles of
\varepsilon^{-1}
and
d
. As will be seen below, this kind of tractability function includes the special case of the so-called
quasi-polynomial tractability
.

Example 4

(Non-separable tractability)
.

Suppose we define the tractability function
T
by

T(\varepsilon^{-1},d,p)=\exp(p\,\hat{\phi}(\varepsilon^{-1})\,\hat{\psi}(d))=[\exp(\hat{\phi}(\varepsilon^{-1}))]^{p\,\hat{\psi}(d)}\qquad\forall\varepsilon>0,\,d\in\mathbb{N},\,p\in[0,\infty),

with non-decreasing functions
\hat{\phi}:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
and
\hat{\psi}:\mathbb{N}\to(0,\infty)
.
We say that a problem is non-separably tractable with scalar parameter
p
iff there exists a positive
C_{p}
such that

\comp(\varepsilon,d)\leq C_{p}\exp(p(\hat{\phi}(\varepsilon^{-1})\hat{\psi}(d))).

Hence we can apply Theorem
2
as follows. We have the following equivalent condition for non-separable tractability:

\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{i=\lceil L_{p}\,\hat{\phi}(0)^{p\,\hat{\psi}(d)}\rceil}[\exp(\hat{\phi}(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1}))]^{-p\,\hat{\psi}(d)}\quad\text{for some }p\geq 0,L_{p}>0.

For
\hat{\phi}
and
\hat{\psi}
of special form, the condition above can be simplified further as in the following two special cases of non-separable tractability.

Example 5

(Algebraic quasi-polynomial tractability)
.

Let the tractability function,
T
,
be defined by

T(\varepsilon^{-1},d,p)=\exp\{p(1+\log(\max\{1,\varepsilon^{-1}\}))(1+\log(d))\}\qquad\forall\varepsilon>0,\,d\in\mathbb{N},\,p\in[0,\infty).

We note that the choice of
T
is equivalent to the definition in
[
7
]
. This can be rewritten as

T(\varepsilon^{-1},d,p)=e^{p}(\max\{1,\varepsilon^{-1}\})^{p(1+\log(d))}d^{p}.

Then Theorem
2
yields the following equivalent condition for quasi-polynomial tractability in the algebraic case:

\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\,d^{-p}\sum_{i=\lceil L_{p}\,d^{p}\rceil}\frac{1}{\left(\max\{1,\lambda_{i,d}\}\right)^{p(1+\log(d))}}<\infty\qquad\text{for some }p\geq 0,L_{p}>0.

This essentially recovers
[
14
, Theorem 23.1]
.

Next, we will consider the exponential case as follows.

Example 6

(Exponential quasi-polynomial tractability)
.

Suppose that the tractability function,
T
, is defined by

T(\varepsilon^{-1},d,p)=\exp\{p(1+\log(\max\{1,\log(1+\varepsilon^{-1})\}))(1+\log(d))\}\qquad\forall\varepsilon>0,\,d\in\mathbb{N},\,p\in[0,\infty).

This can be rewritten as

T(\varepsilon^{-1},d,p)=e^{p}(\max\{1,\log(1+\varepsilon^{-1})\})^{p(1+\log(d))}d^{p}.

Hence we can apply Theorem
2
as follows. We have the following equivalent condition for quasi-polynomial tractability in the exponential case:

\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\,d^{-p}\sum_{i=\lceil L_{p}\,d^{p}\rceil}\frac{1}{\left(\max\{1,\log(1+\lambda_{i,d}^{-1})\}\right)^{p(1+\log(d))}}<\infty\quad\text{for some }p\geq 0,L_{p}>0.

This essentially recovers
[
11
, Theorem 2]
.

4
Sub-
h
Tractability

In this section we generalize the concept of weak tractability (see again
[
12
]
–
[
14
]
). Let
h:[0,\infty)\to[1,\infty)
satisfy

h\text{ is strictly increasing},\qquad h(0)=1,\qquad h(x+y)\geq h(x)\cdot h(y)\quad\forall x,y\geq 0.

(22)

This means that
\log(h)
is a superadditive function. An example of the function
h
is the exponential function, from which we can recover weak tractability.

Furthermore, let us denote the inverse function of
h
by
h^{-1}
.
Then, for arbitrary
z,w\in[1,\infty)
we choose
x=h^{-1}(z)
and
y=h^{-1}(w)
, to obtain from (
22
)

zw\leq h(h^{-1}(z)+h^{-1}(w)),

which yields by the monotonicity of
h^{-1}
that

h^{-1}(zw)\leq h^{-1}(z)+h^{-1}(w).

(23)

Now we define a generalized notion of weak tractability.

Definition 3
.

A problem is sub-
h
-
T
tractable for parameter
\boldsymbol{p}>\boldsymbol{0}
if

\lim_{\varepsilon^{-1}+d\to\infty}\frac{h^{-1}(\max(1,\comp(\varepsilon,d)))}{T(\varepsilon^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p})}=0.

Note that this definition of sub-
h
-
T
tractability implies that

\lim_{\varepsilon^{-1}+d\to\infty}\frac{\comp(\varepsilon,d)}{h(cT(\varepsilon^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p}))}=0\qquad\forall c>0,

(24)

which means that for
\varepsilon\to 0
or
d\to\infty

\comp(\varepsilon,d)
must increase slower than
h(cT(\varepsilon^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p}))
, no matter how small
c
is.

Theorem 3
.

Let
T
be a tractability function as specified in (
3
) and satisfying (
7
). Moreover, assume that

\lim_{\varepsilon^{-1}+d\to\infty}T(\varepsilon^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p})=\infty

for all
\boldsymbol{p}>\boldsymbol{0}
. The problem is sub-
h
-
T
tractable if and only if

S_{c}=S_{c,\boldsymbol{p}}:=\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\,\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{h(c\,T(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p}))}<\infty\qquad\forall c>0.

Proof.

The idea of this proof follows
[
16
]
.
We suppress the
\boldsymbol{p}
dependence in the proof, because it is insignificant.

Sufficient condition:

Suppose that
S_{c}<\infty
for all
c>0
. Since the terms of the series in the definition of
S_{c}
are non-increasing, we have

\frac{n}{h(cT(\lambda_{n,d}^{-1},d))}\leq S_{c}\qquad\forall n,d\in\mathbb{N},

or equivalently

n\leq S_{c}\,h(cT(\lambda_{n,d}^{-1},d))\qquad\forall n,d\in\mathbb{N}.

(25)

This implies that for the special choice

n=\left\lceil S_{c}\,h(cT(\varepsilon^{-1},d))\right\rceil\geq 1

we have

T(\lambda_{n,d}^{-1},d)\geq T(\varepsilon^{-1},d)\qquad\forall\varepsilon>0,\,d\in\mathbb{N}.

Since
T(\cdot,d)
is non-decreasing, it follows that
\lambda_{n,d}\leq\varepsilon
, and thus

\comp(\varepsilon,d)\leq\left\lceil S_{c}\,h(cT(\varepsilon^{-1},d))\right\rceil\leq\lceil S_{c}\,h(cT(\lambda_{1,1}^{-1},1))\,h(cT(\varepsilon^{-1},d))\rceil\qquad\forall c,\varepsilon>0,\,d\in\mathbb{N}

because
h
is no smaller than
1
.

As
S_{c}\geq 1/h(cT(\lambda_{1,1}^{-1},1))
by (
25
), it follows that
S_{c}\,h(cT(0,1))\,h(cT(\varepsilon^{-1},d))\geq 1
. Define
\widetilde{S_{c}}:=2S_{c}\,h(cT(\lambda_{1,1}^{-1},1))
, and note that
\widetilde{S}_{c}
must also be finite for all
c>0
. It follows that

\max(1,\comp(\varepsilon,d))\leq\lceil(\widetilde{S}_{c}/2)\,h(cT(\varepsilon^{-1},d))\rceil\leq\widetilde{S}_{c}\,h(cT(\varepsilon^{-1},d))\qquad\forall c,\varepsilon>0,\,d\in\mathbb{N}.

Since
h
is strictly increasing, so is
h^{-1}
, and thus by (
23
),

\displaystyle h^{-1}(\max(1,\comp(\varepsilon,d)))

\displaystyle\leq h^{-1}\left(\widetilde{S}_{c}\,h(cT(\varepsilon^{-1},d))\right)

\displaystyle\leq h^{-1}(\widetilde{S}_{c})+cT(\varepsilon^{-1},d)\quad\forall c,\varepsilon>0,\,d\in\mathbb{N}.

By the hypothesis of this theorem,
\lim_{\varepsilon^{-1}+d\to\infty}T(\varepsilon^{-1},d)=\infty
. Therefore

\lim_{\varepsilon^{-1}+d\to\infty}\frac{h^{-1}(\max(1,\comp(\varepsilon,d)))}{T(\varepsilon^{-1},d)}\leq\lim_{\varepsilon^{-1}+d\to\infty}\frac{h^{-1}(2S_{c})}{T(\varepsilon^{-1},d)}+c=c\qquad\forall c>0.

Since this limit is bounded above by all positive
c
, it must be zero, and the problem is sub-
h
-
T
tractable.

Necessary condition:

Suppose that the problem is sub-
h
-
T
tractable. Then by (
24
), for any
c>0
there exists a positive integer
V_{c}
such that

\comp(\varepsilon,d)\leq\left\lfloor h(cT(\varepsilon^{-1},d))\right\rfloor\qquad\forall\varepsilon^{-1}+d\geq V_{c}.

By the definition of the information complexity in (
2
), it follows that

\lambda_{n(\varepsilon,d,c),d}\leq\varepsilon\quad\forall\varepsilon^{-1}+d\geq V_{c},\qquad\text{where }n(\varepsilon,d,c):=\left\lfloor h(cT(\varepsilon^{-1},d))\right\rfloor+1.

Since
h(cT(\cdot,d))
is non-decreasing, it follows that

n(\varepsilon,d,c)\leq h(cT(\varepsilon^{-1},d))+1\leq h(cT(\lambda^{-1}_{n(\varepsilon,d,c),d},d))+1\qquad\quad\forall\varepsilon^{-1}+d\geq V_{c}.

(26)

For a fixed
c
and
d
, define

\varepsilon^{-1}_{\max}(d,c):=\max(V_{c}-d,\lambda_{1,d}^{-1}),\qquad n^{*}(d,c):=n(\varepsilon^{-1}_{\max}(d,c),d,c)\leq h(cT(\varepsilon^{-1}_{\max}(d,c),d))+1.

(27)

By varying
\varepsilon^{-1}
in the interval
[\varepsilon^{-1}_{\max}(d,c),\infty)
, the integer
n(\varepsilon,d,c)
attains, by Assumption (
7b
), all values greater than or equal to
n^{*}(d,c)
. Thus, by (
26
),

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle\leq h(cT(\lambda^{-1}_{n,d},d))\qquad\forall n\geq n^{*}(d,c),

(28)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h(2cT(\lambda^{-1}_{n,d},d))}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{[h(cT(\lambda^{-1}_{n,d},d))]^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}\qquad\forall n\geq n^{*}(d,c)\qquad\text{by \eqref{eq:h_cond}}.

Combining this inequality with the upper bound on
n^{*}(d,c)
yields

\displaystyle S_{2c}

\displaystyle=\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\,\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{h(2cT(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},d))}

\displaystyle=\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n^{*}(d,c)-1}\frac{1}{h(2cT(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},d))}+\sum_{i=n^{*}(d,c)}^{\infty}\frac{1}{h(2cT(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},d))}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\left[\frac{n^{*}(d,c)-1}{h(2cT(\lambda_{1,d}^{-1},d))}+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{i^{2}}\right]\qquad\text{by \eqref{eq:subhTneq}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\left[\frac{h(cT(\varepsilon^{-1}_{\max}(d,c),d))}{h(2cT(\lambda_{1,d}^{-1},d))}\right]+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\qquad\text{by \eqref{eq:nstardef}.}

(29)

Note that

\varepsilon^{-1}_{\max}(d,c)=\max(V_{c}-d,\lambda_{1,d}^{-1})=\begin{cases}V_{c}-d\leq V_{c},&d\in\{1,\ldots,d^{*}_{c}:=\lfloor V_{c}-\lambda_{1,d}^{-1}\rfloor\},\\
\lambda_{1,d}^{-1},&d\in\{d^{*}_{c}+1,\ldots\},\end{cases}

and furthermore, it follows from above that we always have
d^{*}_{c}\leq\lfloor V_{c}\rfloor
.
Recalling that
T
is non-decreasing in its arguments, this implies that

\displaystyle{\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\,\frac{h(cT(\varepsilon^{-1}_{\max}(d,c),d))}{h(2cT(\lambda_{1,d}^{-1},d))}}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\sup_{d\in\{1,\ldots,d^{*}_{c}\}}\,\frac{h(cT(\varepsilon^{-1}_{\max}(d,c),d))}{h(2cT(\lambda_{1,d}^{-1},d))},\sup_{d\in\{d^{*}_{c}+1,\ldots\}}\,\frac{h(cT(\varepsilon^{-1}_{\max}(d,c),d))}{h(2cT(\lambda_{1,d}^{-1},d))}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\sup_{d\in\{1,\ldots,d^{*}_{c}\}}\,\frac{h(cT(V_{c},d))}{h(2cT(\lambda_{1,d}^{-1},d))},\sup_{d\in\{d^{*}_{c}+1,\ldots\}}\,\frac{h(cT(\lambda_{1,d}^{-1},d))}{h(2cT(\lambda_{1,d}^{-1},d))}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\frac{h(cT(V_{c},d^{*}_{c}))}{1},\sup_{d\in\{d^{*}_{c}+1,\ldots\}}\,\frac{h(cT(\lambda_{1,d}^{-1},d))}{[h(cT(\lambda_{1,d}^{-1},d))]^{2}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\frac{h(cT(V_{c},\lfloor d^{*}_{c}\rfloor))}{1},1\right\}

\displaystyle=h(cT(V_{c},\lfloor V_{c}\rfloor))

\displaystyle<\infty,

where we used that
h(\cdot)
is always at least 1. Combining this bound with the upper bound on
S_{c}
in (
29
) establishes the finiteness of
S_{2c}
and completes the proof of the necessary condition.
This concludes the proof of Theorem
3
.

∎

Example 7

(Weak Tractability)
.

Suppose that

T(\varepsilon^{-1},d,s,t)=\max(1,\varepsilon^{-1})^{s}+d^{t}\quad\forall\varepsilon,s,t>0,\,d\in\mathbb{N},\qquad h(x)=\exp(x)\quad\forall x\geq 0.

Then Theorem
3
implies that

\lim_{\varepsilon^{-1}+d\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log(\comp(\varepsilon,d))}{\varepsilon^{-s}+d^{t}}=0\iff\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\,\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\exp(-c\,(\min\{1,\lambda_{i,d}\}^{s}+d^{t}))<\infty\qquad\forall c>0.

The expression on the left is the notion of
(s,t)
-weak tractability introduced in
[
15
]
, and the condition on the right was derived in
[
16
, Theorem 3.1]
. Weak tractability corresponds to
(s,t)=(1,1)
, and uniform weak tractability corresponds to the case when a problem is weakly tractable for all positive
s
and
t
.

5
(Strong) Tractability on a Restricted Domain

Suppose that the domain of interest
(\varepsilon^{-1},d)
is not all of
(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{N}
but some subset

\Omega:=\{(\varepsilon^{-1},d):\varepsilon\in(\mathscr{E}(d),\infty),\ d\in\mathbb{N}\},\qquad\text{where }\mathscr{E}:\mathbb{N}\to[0,\infty).

(30)

We then expect that the equivalent conditions for (strong) tractability will be similar in form, but weaker than the conditions in Theorems
1
and
2
.

Definition 4
.

A problem is strongly

T
-tractable with parameter
\boldsymbol{p}

on the restricted domain
\Omega
iff the information complexity is independent of the dimension of the problem, that is, there exists a positive constant
C_{\boldsymbol{p}}
, again independent of
\varepsilon
and
d
, such that

\comp(\varepsilon,d)\leq C_{\boldsymbol{p}}\,T(\varepsilon^{-1},1,\boldsymbol{p})\qquad\forall(\varepsilon^{-1},d)\in\Omega.

(31)

A problem is

T
-tractable with parameter
\boldsymbol{p}

on the restricted domain
\Omega
iff there exists a positive constant
C_{\boldsymbol{p}}
, again independent of
\varepsilon
and
d
, such that

\comp(\varepsilon,d)\leq C_{\boldsymbol{p}}\,T(\varepsilon^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p})\qquad\forall(\varepsilon^{-1},d)\in\Omega.

(32)

Define the closures of the sets of parameters for which our (strong) tractability conditions hold:

\mathcal{P}_{\textup{rtrct}}:=\{\boldsymbol{p}^{*}:\eqref{eq:rest_tract_def}\text{ holds }\forall\boldsymbol{p}\in(\boldsymbol{p}^{*},\boldsymbol{\infty})\},\qquad\mathcal{P}_{\textup{rstrct}}:=\{\boldsymbol{p}^{*}:\eqref{eq:rest_strong_tract_def}\text{ holds }\forall\boldsymbol{p}\in(\boldsymbol{p}^{*},\boldsymbol{\infty})\}.

The set of optimal parameters is defined as all of those parameters satisfying the (strong) tractability conditions that are not greater than or equal to others:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\textup{ropt}}:=\{\boldsymbol{p}^{*}\in\mathcal{P}_{\textup{rtrct}}:\boldsymbol{p}^{*}\notin[\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{\infty})\ \forall\boldsymbol{p}\in\mathcal{P}_{\textup{rtrct}}\setminus\{\boldsymbol{p}^{*}\}\},

(33)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\textup{rsopt}}:=\{\boldsymbol{p}^{*}\in\mathcal{P}_{\textup{rstrct}}:\boldsymbol{p}^{*}\notin[\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{\infty})\ \forall\boldsymbol{p}\in\mathcal{P}_{\textup{rstrct}}\setminus\{\boldsymbol{p}^{*}\}\}.

(34)

Define
\mathscr{U}:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{\infty\}

\mathscr{U}(d):=\begin{cases}\min\{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:\lambda_{n+1,d}\leq\mathscr{E}(d)\},&\mathscr{E}(d)>0,\\
\infty,&\mathscr{E}(d)=0.\end{cases}

(35)

Also, for each
d\in\mathbb{N}
define the set

\mathcal{I}_{d}:=\begin{cases}\emptyset,&\mathscr{U}(d)=0,\\
\{1,\ldots,\mathscr{U}(d)\},&0<\mathscr{U}(d)<\infty,\\
\mathbb{N},&\mathscr{U}(d)=\infty.\end{cases}

(36)

From the definition of
\mathscr{U}(d)
it follows that
(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},d)\in\Omega\iff i\in\mathcal{I}_{d}
, and

\comp(\varepsilon,d)\begin{cases}=0,&\mathscr{U}(d)=0,\\
\in\mathcal{I}_{d},&\mathscr{U}(d)>0,\end{cases}\qquad\forall(\varepsilon^{-1},d)\in\Omega.

(37)

The equivalent conditions for (strong)
T
-tractability on a restricted domain in Theorems
4
and
5
mimic the conditions given in Theorems
1
and
2
, except that the upper limits on the sums now correspond to
\mathscr{U}(d)
rather than
\infty
. The proofs are a bit more delicate, but similar arguments are used.

We note that it may be possible to define
\Omega
such that
\lambda_{1,d}\leq\mathscr{E}(d)

for all
d\in\mathbb{N}
. In this case,
\mathscr{U}(d)=0
for all
d\in\mathbb{N}
, the equivalent conditions for (strong) tractability are trivially satisfied, and the zero algorithm satisfies the error tolerance.

5.1
Strong Tractability on a Restricted Domain

Theorem 4
.

Let
T
be a tractability function as specified in (
3
) and satisfying (
7
) and (
8
). A problem is strongly
T
-tractable
on the restricted domain
\Omega
iff there exists
\boldsymbol{p}\in[\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{\infty})
and an integer
L_{\boldsymbol{p}}>0
such that

S_{\boldsymbol{p}}:=\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{i=L_{\boldsymbol{p}}}^{\mathscr{U}(d)}\frac{1}{T(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},1,\boldsymbol{p})}<\infty.

(38)

By convention, if
\mathscr{U}(d)<L_{\boldsymbol{p}}
, the sum is zero.

If (
38
) holds for some
\boldsymbol{p}
, let
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{rstrct}}:=\{\boldsymbol{p}^{*}:\eqref{eq:strong_rest_tractiff}\text{ holds }\forall\boldsymbol{p}\in(\boldsymbol{p}^{*},\boldsymbol{\infty})\}
. Then
\mathcal{P}_{\textup{rstrct}}=\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{rstrct}}
, and the set of optimal strong tractability parameters on the restricted domain is

\mathcal{P}_{\textup{rsopt}}=\{\boldsymbol{p}^{*}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{rstrct}}:\boldsymbol{p}^{*}\notin[\widetilde{\boldsymbol{p}},\boldsymbol{\infty})\ \forall\widetilde{\boldsymbol{p}}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{rstrct}}\setminus\{\boldsymbol{p}^{*}\}\}.

Proof.

Sufficient condition:

Fix
d
. If
\mathscr{U}(d)=0
, then
\comp(\varepsilon,d)=0\leq C_{\boldsymbol{p}}\,T(\varepsilon^{-1},1,\boldsymbol{p})
automatically for all
\varepsilon\in[\mathscr{E}(d),\infty)
.

For the case
\mathscr{U}(d)>0
, following the argument for the sufficient condition in Theorem
1
it follows that for all
\mathscr{D}\in\mathbb{N}
and integers
n,N\in\mathcal{I}_{d}
with
N\leq n
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{n+1,d}\leq\lambda_{n,d}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{N,d}

\displaystyle\implies\frac{1}{T(\lambda_{n+1,d}^{-1},\mathscr{D},\boldsymbol{p})}\leq\frac{1}{T(\lambda_{n,d}^{-1},\mathscr{D},\boldsymbol{p})}\leq\cdots\leq\frac{1}{T(\lambda_{N,d}^{-1},\mathscr{D},\boldsymbol{p})}

\displaystyle\implies\frac{1}{T(\lambda_{n+1,d}^{-1},\mathscr{D},\boldsymbol{p})}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n-N+1}\sum_{i=N}^{n}\frac{1}{T(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},\mathscr{D},\boldsymbol{p})}\leq\frac{1}{n-N+1}\sum_{i=N}^{\mathscr{U}(d)}\frac{1}{T(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},\mathscr{D},\boldsymbol{p})}.

Thus, we can conclude from the previous line that

n-N+1\geq T(\varepsilon^{-1},\mathscr{D},\boldsymbol{p})\sum_{i=N}^{\mathscr{U}(d)}\frac{1}{T(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},\mathscr{D},\boldsymbol{p})}\\
\implies\frac{1}{T(\lambda_{n+1,d}^{-1},\mathscr{D},\boldsymbol{p})}\leq\frac{1}{n-N+1}\sum_{i=N}^{\mathscr{U}(d)}\frac{1}{T(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},\mathscr{D},\boldsymbol{p})}\leq\frac{1}{T(\varepsilon^{-1},\mathscr{D},\boldsymbol{p})}\\
\forall\mathscr{D}\in\mathbb{N},\ N\in\mathcal{I}_{d},\ \mbox{and}\ n\geq N

(39)

Moreover, given that
T
is increasing in its arguments, we have the following equivalent expression for the information complexity via
(
2
):

\displaystyle\comp(\varepsilon,d)

\displaystyle=\min\{n\in\mathcal{I}_{d}:\lambda_{n+1,d}\leq\varepsilon\}\qquad\text{by \eqref{eq:comprestr}}

\displaystyle=\min\biggl\{n\in\mathcal{I}_{d}:\frac{1}{T(\lambda_{n+1,d}^{-1},\mathscr{D},\boldsymbol{p})}\leq\frac{1}{T(\varepsilon^{-1},\mathscr{D},\boldsymbol{p})}\biggr\}\qquad\forall\mathscr{D}\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\leq\min\biggl\{n\in\mathcal{I}_{d}:n\geq N-1+T(\varepsilon^{-1},\mathscr{D},\boldsymbol{p})\sum_{i=N}^{\mathscr{U}(d)}\frac{1}{T(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},\mathscr{D},\boldsymbol{p})}\biggr\}\;\forall\mathscr{D},\ 1\leq N\leq\mathscr{U}(d)

by (
39
).

This last statement holds for all
N\leq\mathscr{U}(d)
because
n\geq N
automatically in the expression above. Thus,

\displaystyle\comp(\varepsilon,d)

\displaystyle\leq T(\varepsilon^{-1},\mathscr{D},\boldsymbol{p})\Biggl[\frac{N}{T(\varepsilon^{-1},\mathscr{D},\boldsymbol{p})}+\sum_{i=N}^{\mathscr{U}(d)}\frac{1}{T(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},\mathscr{D},\boldsymbol{p})}\Biggr]\quad\forall\mathscr{D},\ 1\leq N\leq\mathscr{U}(d).

(40)

Now we take this upper bound further, specializing to the case of
\mathscr{D}=1
and
N=\min\{L_{\boldsymbol{p}},\mathscr{U}(d)\}
:

\displaystyle\comp(\varepsilon,d)

\displaystyle\leq T(\varepsilon^{-1},1,\boldsymbol{p})\Biggl[\frac{\min\{L_{\boldsymbol{p}},\mathscr{U}(d)\}}{T(\varepsilon^{-1},1,\boldsymbol{p})}+\underbrace{\sum_{i=\min\{L_{\boldsymbol{p}},\mathscr{U}(d)\}}^{\mathscr{U}(d)}\frac{1}{T(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},1,\boldsymbol{p})}}_{\leq S_{\boldsymbol{p}}+1/T(\lambda_{\mathscr{U}(d),d}^{-1},1,\boldsymbol{p})\text{ by \eqref{eq:strong_rest_tractiff}}}\Biggr]

\displaystyle\leq T(\varepsilon^{-1},1,\boldsymbol{p})\underbrace{\left[\frac{L_{\boldsymbol{p}}+1}{T(0,1,\boldsymbol{p})}+S_{\boldsymbol{p}}\right]}_{=:C_{\boldsymbol{p}}}\qquad\text{by \eqref{eq:Tnondecreasing}}

\displaystyle=C_{\boldsymbol{p}}T(\varepsilon^{-1},1,\boldsymbol{p}).

This means that we have strong
T
-tractability on the restricted domain
\Omega
via (
31
), and verifies the sufficiency of (
38
).

Necessary condition:

Suppose that we have strong

T
-tractability on the restricted domain
\Omega
as defined in (
31
). That is, for some
\boldsymbol{p}\geq\boldsymbol{0}
there exists a positive constant
C_{\boldsymbol{p}}
such that

\comp(\varepsilon,d)\leq C_{\boldsymbol{p}}\,T(\varepsilon^{-1},1,\boldsymbol{p})\qquad\forall(\varepsilon^{-1},d)\in\Omega.

Since the sequence of singular values
\lambda_{1,d},\lambda_{2,d},\ldots
is non-increasing, we have

\lambda_{\lfloor C_{\boldsymbol{p}}\,T(\varepsilon^{-1},1,\boldsymbol{p})\rfloor+1,d}\leq\varepsilon\qquad\forall(\varepsilon^{-1},d)\in\Omega.

(41)

For all
\varepsilon>0
, define the positive integers

i(\varepsilon,\boldsymbol{p}):=\lfloor C_{\boldsymbol{p}}\,T(\varepsilon^{-1},1,\boldsymbol{p})\rfloor+1,\quad i(\infty,\boldsymbol{p})=\lfloor C_{\boldsymbol{p}}\,T(0,1,\boldsymbol{p})\rfloor+1\geq 1,

just as in (
15
).
Thus, it follows by (
41
) that

\lambda_{i(\varepsilon,\boldsymbol{p}),d}\leq\varepsilon\qquad\forall(\varepsilon^{-1},d)\in\Omega.

(42)

Note furthermore that we always have

i(\varepsilon,\boldsymbol{p})\leq C_{\boldsymbol{p}}\,T(\varepsilon^{-1},1,\boldsymbol{p})+1\leq C_{\boldsymbol{p}}T(\lambda_{i(\varepsilon,\boldsymbol{p}),d}^{-1},1,\boldsymbol{p})+1\qquad\forall(\varepsilon^{-1},d)\in\Omega,

since

T(\cdot,1,\boldsymbol{p})
is non-decreasing.

For
\varepsilon
taking on all values in
(\mathscr{E}(d),\infty)
,
i(\varepsilon,\boldsymbol{p})
takes on (at least) all values in

\mathscr{I}_{d,\boldsymbol{p}}:=\begin{cases}\emptyset,&i(\mathscr{E}(d),\boldsymbol{p})=i(\infty,\boldsymbol{p}),\\
\{i(\infty,\boldsymbol{p}),\ldots,i(\mathscr{E}(d),\boldsymbol{p})-1\},&\mathscr{E}(d)>0,\\
\{i(\infty,\boldsymbol{p}),i(\infty,\boldsymbol{p})+1,\ldots\},&\mathscr{E}(d)=0.\end{cases}

So,

i\leq C_{\boldsymbol{p}}T(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},1,\boldsymbol{p})+1\qquad\forall i\in\mathscr{I}_{d,\boldsymbol{p}},\ d\in\mathbb{N}.

This implies via our technical assumption (
8
) that, for
\tau>1
,

\displaystyle K_{\boldsymbol{p},\tau}\,T(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},1,\tau\boldsymbol{p})\geq[T(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},1,\boldsymbol{p})]^{\tau}\geq\left[\frac{(i-1)}{C_{\boldsymbol{p}}}\right]^{\tau}\qquad\forall i\in\mathscr{I}_{d,\boldsymbol{p}},\ d\in\mathbb{N},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},1,\tau\boldsymbol{p})}\leq\frac{K_{\boldsymbol{p},\tau}\,C_{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\tau}}{(i-1)^{\tau}}\qquad\forall i\in\mathscr{I}_{d,\boldsymbol{p}}\setminus\{1\},\ d\in\mathbb{N}.

(43)

To complete the proof, we will sum both sides of (
43
) over the range of
i
appearing in (
38
), to establish that
S_{\tau\boldsymbol{p}}
is finite. To do this we need to show that (
43
) holds for that range. Choose
L_{\tau\boldsymbol{p}}=i(\infty,\boldsymbol{p})+1\geq 2
. There are three cases.

i)

If
\mathscr{U}(d)<L_{\tau\boldsymbol{p}}
, then

\sum_{i=L_{\tau\boldsymbol{p}}}^{\mathscr{U}(d)}\frac{1}{T(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},1,\tau\boldsymbol{p})}=0,

which does not affect the finiteness of
S_{\tau\boldsymbol{p}}
.

ii)

If
\mathscr{U}(d)\geq L_{\tau\boldsymbol{p}}
and
\mathscr{E}(d)=0
, then
\{L_{\tau\boldsymbol{p}},L_{\tau\boldsymbol{p}}+1,\ldots\}\subseteq\mathscr{I}_{d,\boldsymbol{p}}\setminus\{1\}
.

iii)

If
\mathscr{U}(d)\geq L_{\tau\boldsymbol{p}}
and
\mathscr{E}(d)>0
, then (
35
) implies that
\lambda_{\mathscr{U}(d)+1,d}\leq\mathscr{E}(d)<\lambda_{\mathscr{U}(d),d}
. Moreover, there must be some
\varepsilon^{*}
satisfying
\mathscr{E}(d)<\varepsilon^{*}<\lambda_{\mathscr{U}(d),d}
, for which
\lambda_{i(\varepsilon^{*},\boldsymbol{p})}\leq\varepsilon^{*}<\lambda_{\mathscr{U}(d),d}
by (
42
). Because of the ordering of the singular values, this implies that
i(\varepsilon^{*},\boldsymbol{p})\geq\mathscr{U}(d)+1
. Since
i(\cdot,\boldsymbol{p})
is nonincreasing,
i(\mathscr{E}(d),\boldsymbol{p})\geq\mathscr{U}(d)+1
.
This means that
\mathscr{U}(d)\leq i(\mathscr{E}(d),\boldsymbol{p})-1
. So,
\{L_{\tau\boldsymbol{p}},\ldots,\mathscr{U}(d)\}\subseteq\mathscr{I}_{d,\boldsymbol{p}}\setminus\{1\}
.

In both the second and third cases,

\displaystyle S_{\tau\boldsymbol{p}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{i=L_{\tau\boldsymbol{p}}}^{\mathscr{U}(d)}\frac{1}{T(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},1,\tau\boldsymbol{p})}\leq\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{i\in\mathscr{I}_{d,\boldsymbol{p}}\setminus\{1\}}\frac{1}{T(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},1,\tau\boldsymbol{p})}

\displaystyle\leq K_{\boldsymbol{p},\tau}\,C_{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\tau}\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(i-1)^{\tau}}\leq K_{\boldsymbol{p},\tau}\,C_{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\tau}\zeta(\tau)<\infty\qquad\text{by \eqref{eq:rest_strong_ineq},}

where
\zeta
denotes the Riemann zeta function.

Thus, in all three cases, the assumption of strong tractability on the restricted domain implies (
38
) with
\boldsymbol{p}
replaced by
\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}=\tau\boldsymbol{p}
, and so we see the necessity of
(
38
)
.

Optimality:

The proof of optimality is analogous to that for Theorem
1
and is omitted.

This concludes the proof of Theorem
4
.
∎

5.2
Tractability on a Restricted Domain

The equivalent condition for tractability on a restricted domain is analogous to the conditions for tractability on an unrestricted domain and for strong tractability on a restricted domain. The proof is analogous as well.

Theorem 5
.

Let
T
be a tractability function as specified in (
3
) and satisfying (
7
) and (
8
). A problem is
T
-tractable on the restricted domain
\Omega
iff there exists
\boldsymbol{p}\geq\boldsymbol{0}
and a positive constant
L_{\boldsymbol{p}}
such that

S_{\boldsymbol{p}}:=\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{i=\lceil L_{\boldsymbol{p}}\,T(0,d,\boldsymbol{p})\rceil}^{\mathscr{U}(d)}\frac{1}{T(\lambda^{-1}_{i,d},d,\boldsymbol{p})}<\infty.

(44)

If (
44
) holds for some
\boldsymbol{p}
, let
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{rtrct}}:=\{\boldsymbol{p}^{*}:\eqref{eq:rest_tractiff}\text{ holds }\forall\boldsymbol{p}\in(\boldsymbol{p}^{*},\boldsymbol{\infty})\}
.
Then
\mathcal{P}_{\textup{rtrct}}=\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{rtrct}}
, and the set of optimal tractability parameters is

\mathcal{P}_{\textup{ropt}}=\{\boldsymbol{p}^{*}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{rtrct}}:\boldsymbol{p}^{*}\notin[\widetilde{\boldsymbol{p}},\boldsymbol{\infty})\ \forall\widetilde{\boldsymbol{p}}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{\textup{rtrct}}\setminus\{\boldsymbol{p}^{*}\}\}.

Proof.

Sufficient condition:

Fix
d
. If
\mathscr{U}(d)=0
, then
\comp(\varepsilon,d)=0\leq C_{\boldsymbol{p}}\,T(\varepsilon^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p})
automatically for all
\varepsilon\in[\mathscr{E}(d),\infty)
.

For the case
\mathscr{U}(d)>0
, we utilize the upper bound (
40
), specializing to the case of
\mathscr{D}=d
and
N=\min\left\{\lceil L_{\boldsymbol{p}}\,T(0,d,\boldsymbol{p})\rceil,\mathscr{U}(d)\right\}
:

\displaystyle\comp(\varepsilon,d)

\displaystyle\leq T(\varepsilon^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p})\Biggl[\frac{\min\left\{\lceil L_{\boldsymbol{p}}\,T(0,d,\boldsymbol{p})\rceil,\mathscr{U}(d)\right\}}{T(\varepsilon^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p})}+\underbrace{\sum_{i=\min\left\{\lceil L_{\boldsymbol{p}}\,T(0,d,\boldsymbol{p})\rceil,\mathscr{U}(d)\right\}}^{\mathscr{U}(d)}\frac{1}{T(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p})}}_{\leq S_{\boldsymbol{p}}+1/T(\lambda_{\mathscr{U}(d),d}^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p})\text{ by \eqref{eq:rest_tractiff}}}\Biggr]

\displaystyle\leq T(\varepsilon^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p})\left[\frac{L_{\boldsymbol{p}}\,T(0,d,\boldsymbol{p})+1}{T(0,d,\boldsymbol{p})}+S_{\boldsymbol{p}}+\frac{1}{T(\lambda_{\mathscr{U}(d),d}^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p})}\right]\qquad\text{by \eqref{eq:Tnondecreasing}}

\displaystyle\leq T(\varepsilon^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p})\underbrace{\left[L_{\boldsymbol{p}}+\frac{2}{T(0,1,\boldsymbol{p})}+S_{\boldsymbol{p}}\right]}_{=:C_{\boldsymbol{p}}}\qquad\text{by \eqref{eq:Tnondecreasing}}

\displaystyle=C_{\boldsymbol{p}}T(\varepsilon^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p}).

This means that we have strong
T
-tractability on the restricted domain
\Omega
via (
32
), and verifies the sufficiency of (
44
).

Necessary condition:

Suppose that we have

T
-tractability on the restricted domain. That is, for some
\boldsymbol{p}\geq\boldsymbol{0}
, there exists a positive constant
C_{\boldsymbol{p}}
such that

\comp(\varepsilon,d)\leq C_{\boldsymbol{p}}\,T(\varepsilon^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p})\qquad\forall(\varepsilon^{-1},d)\in\Omega.

Since the sequence of singular values
\lambda_{1,d},\lambda_{2,d},\ldots
is non-increasing, we have

\lambda_{\lfloor C_{\boldsymbol{p}}\,T(\varepsilon^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p})\rfloor+1,d}\leq\varepsilon\qquad\forall(\varepsilon^{-1},d)\in\Omega.

(45)

For
(\varepsilon^{-1},d)\in\Omega
, define the positive integers

i(\varepsilon,d,\boldsymbol{p}):=\lfloor C_{\boldsymbol{p}}\,T(\varepsilon^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p})\rfloor+1,\qquad i(\infty,d,\boldsymbol{p}):=\lfloor C_{\boldsymbol{p}}\,T(0,d,\boldsymbol{p})\rfloor+1\geq 1.

It follows by (
45
) that
\lambda_{i(\varepsilon,d,\boldsymbol{p}),d}\leq\varepsilon

for any
(\varepsilon^{-1},d)\in\Omega
.
Note furthermore that since
T(\cdot,d,\boldsymbol{p})
is non-decreasing, we have

i(\varepsilon,d,\boldsymbol{p})\leq C_{\boldsymbol{p}}\,T(\varepsilon^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p})+1\leq C_{\boldsymbol{p}}T(\lambda_{i(\varepsilon,d,\boldsymbol{p}),d}^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p})+1\qquad\forall(\varepsilon^{-1},d)\in\Omega.

For
\varepsilon
taking on all values in
(\mathscr{E}(d),\infty)
,
i(\varepsilon,d,\boldsymbol{p})
takes on (at least) all values in

\mathscr{I}_{d,\boldsymbol{p}}:=\begin{cases}\emptyset,&i(\mathscr{E}(d),d,\boldsymbol{p})=i(\infty,d,\boldsymbol{p}),\\
\{i(\infty,d,\boldsymbol{p}),\ldots,i(\mathscr{E}(d),d,\boldsymbol{p})-1\},&\mathscr{E}(d)>0,\\
\{i(\infty,d,\boldsymbol{p}),i(\infty,d,\boldsymbol{p})+1,\ldots\},&\mathscr{E}(d)=0.\end{cases}

So,

i\leq C_{\boldsymbol{p}}T(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p})+1,\qquad\forall i\in\mathscr{I}_{d},\ d\in\mathbb{N}.

This implies via our technical assumption (
8
) that, for
\tau>1
,

\displaystyle K_{\boldsymbol{p},\tau}\,T(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},d,\tau\boldsymbol{p})\geq[T(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p})]^{\tau}\geq\left[\frac{(i-1)}{C_{\boldsymbol{p}}}\right]^{\tau}\qquad\forall i\in\mathscr{I}_{d,\boldsymbol{p}},\ d\in\mathbb{N},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},d,\tau\boldsymbol{p})}\leq\frac{K_{\boldsymbol{p},\tau}\,C_{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\tau}}{(i-1)^{\tau}}\qquad\forall i\in\mathscr{I}_{d,\boldsymbol{p}}\setminus\{1\},\ d\in\mathbb{N}.

(46)

To complete the proof, we will sum both sides of (
46
) over the range of
i
appearing in (
44
), to establish that
S_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}
is finite, where
\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}=\tau\boldsymbol{p}
. To do this we need to show that (
46
) holds for that range.

For any
d\in\mathbb{N}
, let

\displaystyle L_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle:=C_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}+\frac{2}{T(0,1,\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime})},

and note that

\displaystyle\max\{i(\infty,d,\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}),2\}

\displaystyle\leq i(\infty,d,\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime})+1=\lfloor C_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}\,T(0,d,\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime})\rfloor+2\leq\left[C_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}+\frac{2}{T(0,d,\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime})}\right]\,T(0,d,\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=L_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}\,T(0,d,\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime})\leq\lceil L_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}T(0,d,\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime})\rceil.

This is the inequality needed for the lower limit of the sum.

There are three cases.

i)

If
\mathscr{U}(d)<\lceil L_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}T(0,d,\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime})\rceil
, then

\sum_{i=\lceil L_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}T(0,d,\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime})\rceil}^{\mathscr{U}(d)}\frac{1}{T(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},d,\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime})}=0,

which does not affect the finiteness of
S_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}
.

ii)

If
\mathscr{U}(d)\geq\lceil L_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}T(0,d,\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime})\rceil
and
\mathscr{E}(d)=0
, then
\{\lceil L_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}T(0,d,\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime})\rceil,\lceil L_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}T(0,d,\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime})\rceil+1,\ldots\}\subseteq\mathscr{I}_{d,\boldsymbol{p}}\setminus\{1\}
.

iii)

If
\mathscr{U}(d)\geq\lceil L_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}T(0,d,\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime})\rceil
and
\mathscr{E}(d)>0
, then (
35
) and (
45
) imply that

\lambda_{i(\mathscr{E}(d),d,\boldsymbol{p}),d}\leq\lambda_{\mathscr{U}(d)+1,d}\leq\mathscr{E}(d)<\lambda_{\mathscr{U}(d),d}.

By the same argument as in the proof of strong tractability,
\mathscr{U}(d)\leq i(\mathscr{E}(d),d,\boldsymbol{p})-1
. So,
\{\lceil L_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}T(0,d,\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime})\rceil,\ldots,\mathscr{U}(d)\}\subseteq\mathscr{I}_{d,\boldsymbol{p}}\setminus\{1\}
.

In both the second and third cases,

\displaystyle S_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{i=\lceil L_{\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}}T(0,d,\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime})\rceil}^{\mathscr{U}(d)}\frac{1}{T(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},d,\tau\boldsymbol{p})}\leq\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{i\in\mathscr{I}_{d,\boldsymbol{p}}\setminus\{1\}}\frac{1}{T(\lambda_{i,d}^{-1},d,\tau\boldsymbol{p})}

\displaystyle\leq K_{\boldsymbol{p},\tau}\,C_{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\tau}\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(i-1)^{\tau}}\leq K_{\boldsymbol{p},\tau}\,C_{\boldsymbol{p}}^{\tau}\zeta(\tau)<\infty\qquad\text{by \eqref{eq:rest_ineq}},

where
\zeta
denotes the Riemann zeta function.

Thus, in all three cases, the assumption of strong tractability on the restricted domain implies (
44
) with
\boldsymbol{p}
replaced by
\boldsymbol{p}^{\prime}=\tau\boldsymbol{p}
, and so we see the necessity of
(
44
)
.

Optimality:

The proof of optimality is analogous to that for Theorem
2
and is omitted.

This concludes the proof of Theorem
5
.
∎

6
Discussion and Further Work

We have shown that many proofs of equivalent conditions for (strong) tractability of various types can be unified into a handful of proofs. Even the arguments underlying these handful are quite similar and could be consolidated even further. This unification simplifies our understanding of these equivalent conditions and spotlights the key ideas about what makes a class of problems hard.

However, some questions remain:

•

What interesting cases of
T
do not correspond to known tractability measures?

•

What interesting cases are there where there is no (strong) tractability on an unrestricted domain, but there is (strong) tractability on a nontrivial restricted domain, such as
(0,\infty)\times\{1,\ldots,d_{\max}]\cup(\varepsilon_{\min}^{-1},\infty)\times\mathbb{N}
?

•

For Hilbert spaces and solution operators,
\{\SOL_{d}:\mathcal{F}_{d}\to\mathcal{G}_{d}\}_{d\in\mathbb{N}}
, of tensor product form, the singular values take the form
\lambda_{i,d}=\lambda_{i,d,1}\cdots\lambda_{i,d,d}
. What more can be said about the equivalent conditions for (strong) tractability in this case?

•

For information complexity defined over cones of functions rather than balls (see e.g.,
[
2
,
3
]
), what can be said about equivalent conditions for (strong) tractability?

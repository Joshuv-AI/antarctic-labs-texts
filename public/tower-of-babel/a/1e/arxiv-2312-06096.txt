On quotients of numerical semigroups for almost arithmetic progressions
Feihu Liu 1

Abstract
. Let ⟨ A ⟩ \langle A\rangle be the numerical semigroup generated by relatively prime positive integers { a 1 , a 2 , … , a n } \{a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{n}\} . The quotient of ⟨ A ⟩ \langle A\rangle with respect to a positive integer p p is defined by ⟨ A ⟩ p = { x ∈ ℕ ∣ p ​ x ∈ ⟨ A ⟩ } \frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}=\{x\in\mathbb{N}\mid px\in\langle A\rangle\} . The quotient ⟨ A ⟩ p \frac{\langle A\rangle}{p} is known to be a semigroup but is hard to study. When p p is a positive divisor of a 1 a_{1} , we reduce the computation of the Apéry set of a 1 p \frac{a_{1}}{p} in ⟨ A ⟩ p \frac{\langle A\rangle}{p} to a simple minimization problem. This allow us to obtain closed formulas of the Frobenius number of the quotient for some special numerical semigroups. These includes the cases when ⟨ A ⟩ \langle A\rangle is the almost arithmetic progressions, the almost arithmetic progressions with initial gaps, etc. In particular, we partially solve an open problem proposed by A. Adeniran et al.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbb{P}
be the set of all integers, non-negative integers and positive integers, respectively. A
numerical semigroup
is a nonempty subset
S
of
\mathbb{N}
that is closed under the addition,
0\in S
, and
\mathbb{N}\setminus S
is finite.

For a positive integers sequence
A=(a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{n})
,
n\geq 2
, if
\gcd(A)=1
, then the set

\langle A\rangle=\left\{a_{1}x_{1}+a_{2}x_{2}+\cdots+a_{n}x_{n}\ \mid x_{i}\in\mathbb{N}\right\}

is a numerical semigroup (see
[
16
]
). We say that
A
is a
system of generators
of
\langle A\rangle
. This naturally attracts many scholars to study the following two invariants: 1).
The Frobenius number
is denoted by
F(A)
that is the greatest integer not belonging to
\langle A\rangle
. 2).
The genus
(or
Sylvester number
) is denoted by
g(A)
that is the cardinality of
\mathbb{N}\backslash\langle A\rangle
.

Frobenius proposed the problem of giving a formula for
F(A)
. Sylvester
[
19
,
20
]
obtained

F(a_{1},a_{2})=a_{1}a_{2}-a_{1}-a_{2}\ \ \text{and}\ \ g(a_{1},a_{2})=\frac{1}{2}(a_{1}-1)(a_{2}-1).

(1)

For
n\geq 3
, F. Curtis
[
5
]
proved that the
F(A)
can not be given by closed formulas of a certain type. But many special cases have been considered, see
[
10
]
.

In
[
15
]
, a numerical semigroup
S(a,b,c)
is called
proportional modular
if

S(a,b,c)=\{x\in\mathbb{N}\mid ax\mod b\leq cx\}
and
a,b,c\in\mathbb{P}
. Suppose
\langle A\rangle
is a numerical semigroup and
p\in\mathbb{P}
. A numerical semigroup
\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}
is called the
the quotient of
\langle A\rangle
by
p
if
\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}=\{x\in\mathbb{N}\mid px\in\langle A\rangle\}
. It is easy to verify that
S(a,b,c)
and
\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}
are numerical semigroups (also see
[
16
]
). We can also get
\langle A\rangle\subseteq\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}
and
\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}=\mathbb{N}
if and only if
p\in\langle A\rangle
. In
[
12
]
, it is shown that a numerical semigroup is proportional modular if and only if it is the quotient of
\langle A\rangle
, where
A=(a,a+1),a\in\mathbb{P}
.

Suppose
A=(a_{1},a_{2})
,
\gcd(A)=1
and
p\in\mathbb{P}
. A natural problem is to obtain the formulas for
F\left(\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right)
and
g\left(\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right)
. In particular, it is an open problem to get the formulas of these two invariants (see
[
7
]
). Recently, some related developments can be found in
[
18
,
1
,
4
]
.

We are concerned in this paper with the case
p\mid a_{1}
, i.e., the
p
is a positive divisor of
a
. For
A=(a_{1},a_{2})
and
\gcd(A)=1
, we can get
\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}=\langle\frac{a_{1}}{p},a_{2}\rangle
(see
[
14
]
). By Equation (
1
), we have

\displaystyle F\left(\frac{\langle a_{1},a_{2}\rangle}{p}\right)=\frac{a_{1}a_{2}}{p}-\frac{a_{1}}{p}-a_{2}\ \ \text{and}\ \ g\left(\frac{\langle a_{1},a_{2}\rangle}{p}\right)=\frac{1}{2p}(a_{1}-p)(a_{2}-1).

The motivation of this paper comes from the paper
[
1
]
that A. Adeniran et al. obtained the formulas of Frobenius number
F\left(\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right)
and the genus
F\left(\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right)
for
A=(a,a+d,a+2d)
,
A=(a,a+d,...,a+(a-1)d)
under
\gcd(a,d)=1
and
p\mid a
. They also proposed the following open problem:

Open problem 1.1

(
[
1
]
)
.

Let
A=(a,a+d,a+2d,...,a+kd)
, where
\gcd(a,d)=1
,
a,d\in\mathbb{P}
and
3\leq k\leq a-2
. Find
F\left(\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right)
and
g\left(\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right)
, as well as relationships between these quantities, for positive integers
p
.

In this paper, we will partially solve the open problem, i.e, the case
p
is a positive divisor of
a
. Furthermore, we will study the Frobenius formulas
F\left(\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right)
and
g\left(\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right)
for the
almost arithmetic progressions

A=(a,ha+d,ha+2d,...,ha+kd)
, the
almost arithmetic progressions with initial gaps

A=(a,ha+(K+1)d,ha+(K+2)d,...,ha+kd)
, the
almost arithmetic progressions with odd terms

A=(a,ha+d,ha+3d,ha+5d,...,ha+(2k+1)d)
and
A=(a,ha-d,ha+d)
.

In this work we will modify the reduction method proposed in
[
9
]
by authors. As a well-known result of Lemma
2.1
, once a Apéry set (see Section 2) of numerical semigroup is obtained, the Frobenius number and genus are easy to calculate. We reduce the computation of the elements in the Apéry set of the quotient of a numerical semigroup by the positive integer
p
to a simple minimization problem
O_{B}(M)
. By calculating
O_{B}(M)
, we can obtain an explicit formulas for the elements in the Apéry set. The first main result obtained by this method is as follows:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
A=(a,dB)=(a,db_{1},db_{2},...,db_{k})
,
d,p\in\mathbb{P}
,
p\mid a
and
\gcd(A)=1
. Then:

\displaystyle F\left(\frac{\langle a,db_{1},db_{2},...,db_{k}\rangle}{p}\right)=d\cdot F\left(\frac{\langle a,b_{1},b_{2},...,b_{k}\rangle}{p}\right)+\frac{a(d-1)}{p},

\displaystyle g\left(\frac{\langle a,db_{1},db_{2},...,db_{k}\rangle}{p}\right)=d\cdot g\left(\frac{\langle a,b_{1},b_{2},...,b_{k}\rangle}{p}\right)+\frac{(a-p)(d-1)}{2p}.

Next, we obtain the formulas
F\left(\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right),g\left(\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right)
of the almost arithmetic progressions
A
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
A=(a,ha+d,ha+2d,...,ha+kd)
,
\gcd(A)=1
,
a,h,d,k,p\in\mathbb{P}
and
1\leq k\leq a-1
. If
p\mid a
, then

\displaystyle F\left(\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\lceil\frac{a-p}{k}\right\rceil\cdot\frac{ha}{p}+\frac{a(d-1)}{p}-d,

\displaystyle g\left(\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right)

\displaystyle=h\cdot\sum_{r=1}^{\frac{a}{p}-1}\left\lceil\frac{rp}{k}\right\rceil+\frac{(a-p)(d-1)}{2p}.

When
h=1
, this is a partial answer to Open Problem
1.1
. The other main results of this paper are as follows:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
A=(a,ha+(K+1)d,ha+(K+2)d,...,ha+kd)
, where
\gcd(A)=1
,
a,h,d,K,k,p\in\mathbb{P}
,
K\leq\frac{k-1}{2}
and
a\geq 2
. If
p\mid a
, then

\displaystyle F\left(\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\left\lceil\frac{a-p}{k}\right\rceil\cdot\frac{ha}{p}+\frac{a(d-1)}{p}-d\ &\text{if}&\ \ p>K,\\
&\left\lceil\frac{a+\left\lfloor\frac{K}{p}\right\rfloor\cdot p}{k}\right\rceil\cdot\frac{ha}{p}+\frac{a(d-1)}{p}+\left\lfloor\frac{K}{p}\right\rfloor\cdot d\ &\text{if}&\ \ p\leq K.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

\displaystyle g\left(\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{aligned} &h\cdot\sum_{r=1}^{\frac{a}{p}-1}\left\lceil\frac{rp}{k}\right\rceil+\frac{(a-p)(d-1)}{2p}\ &\text{if}&\ \ p>K,\\
&h\cdot\left(\sum_{r=1}^{\left\lfloor\frac{K}{p}\right\rfloor}\left\lceil\frac{a+rp}{k}\right\rceil+\sum_{r=\left\lfloor\frac{K}{p}\right\rfloor+1}^{\frac{a}{p}-1}\left\lceil\frac{rp}{k}\right\rceil\right)+\frac{(a-p)(d-1)}{2p}+\left\lfloor\frac{K}{p}\right\rfloor\cdot d\ &\text{if}&\ \ p\leq K.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
A=(a,ha-d,ha+d)
,
d,h,p\in\mathbb{P}
,
\gcd(a,d)=1
,
\gcd(A)=1
,
ha-d>1
and
s=\left\lfloor\frac{ha-d}{2hp}\right\rfloor
. If
p\mid a
, then

\displaystyle F\left(\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\max\left\{\left\lfloor\frac{ha-d}{2hp}\right\rfloor(ha+d)-\frac{a}{p},\left(\frac{a}{p}-\left\lceil\frac{ha-d}{2hp}\right\rceil\right)(ha-d)-\frac{a}{p}\right\},

\displaystyle g\left(\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(ha+d)ps(s+1)}{2a}+\frac{(ha-d)(a-sp)(a-sp-p)}{2pa}-\frac{a-p}{2p}.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
A=(a,ha+d,ha+3d,ha+5d,...,ha+(2k+1)d)
, where
\gcd(A)=1
,
a,h,d,k,p\in\mathbb{P}
,
a>2
,
3\leq 2k+1\leq a-1
. And let
a-p=(2k+1)s^{\prime}+t^{\prime}
, where
1\leq t^{\prime}\leq 2k+1
.
If
p\mid a
and

F\left(\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right)=\frac{1}{p}\left(ha\left(\left\lfloor\frac{a-p-1}{2k+1}\right\rfloor+2\right)+(a-p)d-a\right).

When

2.
A Reduction of Apéry Set

Suppose
A=(a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{n})
,
a_{i}\in\mathbb{P},1\leq i\leq n
and
\gcd(A)=1
. We know that the set

\langle A\rangle=\left\{a_{1}x_{1}+a_{2}x_{2}+\cdots+a_{n}x_{n}\ \mid x_{i}\in\mathbb{N}\right\}

is a numerical semigroup. Let
a\in\langle A\rangle\backslash\{0\}
. The
Apéry set
of
a
in
\langle A\rangle
is
Ap(A,a)=\{w\in\langle A\rangle\mid w-a\notin\langle A\rangle\}
. In
[
16
]
, we can obtain

Ap(A,a)=\{N_{0},N_{1},N_{2},...,N_{a-1}\},

where
N_{r}=\min\{a_{0}\mid a_{0}\equiv r\mod a,\ a_{0}\in\langle A\rangle\}
,
0\leq r\leq a-1
. We usually take
a:=a_{1}
.

The following results were obtained by A. Brauer and J. E. Shockley
[
3
]
and E. S. Selmer
[
17
]
respectively. Then the Frobenius and genus are easy to compute.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
3
]
,
[
17
]
)
.

Suppose
A=(a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{n})
and
\gcd(A)=1
. The
Apéry set
of
a
in
\langle A\rangle
is
Ap(A,a)=\{N_{0},N_{1},N_{2},...,N_{a-1}\}
. Then the Frobenius number and genus of
A
are respectively:

\displaystyle F(A)=\max_{r\in\{0,1,...,a-1\}}\{N_{r}\}-a,\ \ \ \ \ \ g(A)=\frac{1}{a}\sum_{r=1}^{a-1}N_{r}-\frac{a-1}{2}.

Note that
N_{0}=0
is always 0. Suppose
d\in\mathbb{P}
and
\gcd(a,d)=1
. If
r
takes one of the remaining classes of
a
, then
dr
is also. Therefore we have

\{N_{0},N_{1},N_{2},...,N_{a-1}\}=\{N_{d\cdot 0},N_{d\cdot 1},N_{d\cdot 2},...,N_{d\cdot(a-1)}\}.

Suppose
A=(a,ha+dB)=(a,ha+db_{1},...,ha+db_{k})
,
\gcd(A)=1
. We have
\gcd(a,d)=1
. Next we study the Apéry set of the quotient of a numerical semigroup by a positive integer
p
, where
p
is a positive divisor of
a
. The Apéry set can be reduced to a simple minimization problem. This reduction will facilitate the calculation of two invariants: The Frobenius number
F\left(\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right)
and the genus
g\left(\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right)
. When
p
is a divisor of
a
, some results about the Apéry set also see
[Chapter 5,
[
16
]
].

Proposition 2.2
.

Suppose
A=(a,ha+db_{1},...,ha+db_{k})
,
k,h,d\in\mathbb{P}
and
\gcd(A)=1
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
p
be a positive divisor of
a
, i.e.,
p\mid a
. The elements of Apéry set of
\frac{a}{p}
in the numerical semigroup
\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}
are

N_{dr,p}=\min\left\{O_{B}(ma+rp)\cdot\frac{ha}{p}+\left(\frac{ma}{p}+r\right)d\mid m\in\mathbb{N}\right\},

(2)

where
0\leq r\leq\frac{a}{p}-1
and

O_{B}(M)=\min\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\mid\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}x_{i}=M,\ M,x_{i}\in\mathbb{N},1\leq i\leq k\right\}.

Proof.

By
\gcd(A)=1
, we have
\gcd(a,d)=1
. By the definition of the Apéry set, for a given
0\leq r\leq\frac{a}{p}-1
, we have

\displaystyle N_{dr,p}

\displaystyle=\min\left\{a_{0}\mid a_{0}\equiv dr\mod\frac{a}{p};\ a_{0}\in\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right\}

\displaystyle=\min\left\{a_{0}\mid a_{0}\equiv dr\mod\frac{a}{p};\ pa_{0}\in\langle A\rangle\right\}

\displaystyle=\min\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{(ha+db_{i})}{p}x_{i}\mid\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{(ha+db_{i})}{p}x_{i}\equiv dr\mod\frac{a}{p},\ x_{i}\in\mathbb{N},1\leq i\leq k\right\}

\displaystyle=\min\left\{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\right)\frac{ha}{p}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{x_{i}b_{i}d}{p}\mid\sum_{i=1}^{k}(ha+db_{i})x_{i}\equiv drp\mod a,\ x_{i}\in\mathbb{N},1\leq i\leq k\right\}

\displaystyle=\min\left\{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\right)\frac{ha}{p}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{x_{i}b_{i}d}{p}\mid\sum_{i=1}^{k}db_{i}x_{i}\equiv drp\mod a,\ x_{i}\in\mathbb{N},1\leq i\leq k\right\}

\displaystyle=\min\left\{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\right)\frac{ha}{p}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{x_{i}b_{i}d}{p}\mid\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}x_{i}\equiv rp\mod a,\ x_{i}\in\mathbb{N},1\leq i\leq k\right\}

\displaystyle=\min\left\{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\right)\frac{ha}{p}+\left(\frac{ma}{p}+r\right)d\mid\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}x_{i}=ma+rp,\ m,x_{i}\in\mathbb{N},1\leq i\leq k\right\}.

Now for fixed
m
, and hence fixed
M=ma+rp
,

\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}
is minimized to
O_{B}(ma+rp)
. This completes the proof.
∎

From the proof in Proposition
2.2
for
h=0
, we can obtain the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

For a given
0\leq r\leq\frac{a}{p}-1
, we denote
N_{dr,p}(a,dB)
and
N_{r,p}(a,B)
as the element of the Apéry set of
\frac{\langle a,db_{1},db_{2},...,db_{k}\rangle}{p}
and
\frac{\langle a,b_{1},b_{2},...,b_{k}\rangle}{p}
respectively. By the proof in Proposition
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle N_{dr,p}(a,dB)

\displaystyle=\min\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{x_{i}b_{i}d}{p}\mid\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}b_{i}d\equiv drp\mod a,x_{i}\in\mathbb{N},1\leq i\leq k\right\}

\displaystyle=d\cdot\min\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{x_{i}b_{i}}{p}\mid\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}b_{i}\equiv rp\mod a,x_{i}\in\mathbb{N},1\leq i\leq k\right\}

\displaystyle=d\cdot N_{r,p}(a,B).

The second “=” in the above equation is because
\gcd(A)=1
,
\gcd(a,d)=1
. By Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle F\left(\frac{\langle a,db_{1},db_{2},...,db_{k}\rangle}{p}\right)+\frac{a}{p}

\displaystyle=\max_{0\leq r\leq\frac{a}{p}-1}N_{dr,p}(a,dB)=d\cdot\max_{0\leq r\leq\frac{a}{p}-1}N_{r,p}(a,B)

\displaystyle=d\cdot\left(F\left(\frac{\langle a,b_{1},b_{2},...,b_{k}\rangle}{p}\right)+\frac{a}{p}\right).

The first part theorem then follows. For the genus, by Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle g\left(\frac{\langle a,db_{1},db_{2},...,db_{k}\rangle}{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{a}\sum_{r=1}^{\frac{a}{p}-1}N_{dr,p}(a,dB)-\frac{a-p}{2p}=\frac{dp}{a}\sum_{r=1}^{\frac{a}{p}-1}N_{r,p}(a,B)-\frac{a-p}{2p}

\displaystyle=d\left(g\left(\frac{\langle a,b_{1},b_{2},...,b_{k}\rangle}{p}\right)+\frac{a-p}{2p}\right)-\frac{a-p}{2p}.

This completes the proof.
∎

If
p=1
in Theorem
1.2
, then we can clearly get the following corollary.

Corollary 2.3

(
[

]
,
[

]
)
.

Let
A=(a,dB)=(a,db_{1},db_{2},...,db_{k})
,

. Then we have

\displaystyle F\left(a,db_{1},db_{2},...,db_{k}\right)=d\cdot F\left(a,b_{1},b_{2},...,b_{k}\right)+a(d-1),

\displaystyle g\left(a,db_{1},db_{2},...,db_{k}\right)=d\cdot g\left(a,b_{1},b_{2},...,b_{k}\right)+\frac{(a-1)(d-1)}{2}.

We need to mention that the proof of Theorem

is quite simple. The proof of the Corollary

was also collected in

]
. But this proof process is relatively cumbersome. Moreover, we do not require

, we can define an intermediate function with respect to

, namely:

N_{dr,p}(m):=O_{B}(ma+rp)\cdot\frac{ha}{p}+\left(\frac{ma}{p}+r\right)d,\quad\text{where}\ A=(a,ha+dB)\ \text{and}\ \ p\mid a.

(3)

Lemma
2.2
suggests the following strategy for

where

: we first try to solve

for general

. If we have a formula that is nice enough, then we can analyze
N_{dr,p}(m)
. In fact, if
N_{dr,p}(m)
increases with

, then we have a formula for

. Hence we can further obtain the formula of

and

,
1.4
,

And

In this section, we will first give the proof of the formulas

A
.
The central problem is to find an explicit formula of

.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

By the Equation (
3
), we have

N_{dr,p}(m)=O_{B}(ma+rp)\cdot\frac{ha}{p}+\left(\frac{ma}{p}+r\right)d,

where
O_{B}(ma+rp)=\min\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\mid\sum_{i=1}^{k}i\cdot x_{i}=ma+rp,x_{i}\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
.

If
ma+rp=s\cdot k+t
with
1\leq t\leq k
, then
O_{B}(ma+rp)=s+1
, which
minimizes at
x_{k}=s,x_{t}=1,x_{i}=0,(i\neq k,t)
when
t\neq k
,
and minimizes at
x_{k}=s+1,x_{i}=0,(i\neq k)
when
t=k
. Therefore

N_{dr,p}(m)=\frac{(s+1)ha}{p}+\left(\frac{ma}{p}+r\right)d=\frac{ha}{p}\left\lceil\frac{ma+rp}{k}\right\rceil+\left(\frac{ma}{p}+r\right)d

is increasing with respect to
m
for a given
r
. It follows that

N_{dr,p}=N_{dr,p}(0)=\frac{ha}{p}\left\lceil\frac{rp}{k}\right\rceil+rd.

This ia a explicit formula respect to
r
. Moreover the
N_{dr}
is increasing with respect to
r
. By Lemma
2.1
, we have the Frobenius number

\displaystyle F\left(\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right)=\mathop{\max}\limits_{r\in\{0,1,...,\frac{a}{p}-1\}}\{N_{dr,p}\}-\frac{a}{p}=N_{d(\frac{a}{p}-1),p}-\frac{a}{p}=\frac{ha}{p}\left\lceil\frac{a-p}{k}\right\rceil+\left(\frac{a}{p}-1\right)d-\frac{a}{p}.

Similarly, we have the genus

\displaystyle g\left(\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right)=\frac{p}{a}\cdot\sum_{r=1}^{\frac{a}{p}-1}N_{dr,p}-\frac{a-p}{2p}=h\cdot\sum_{r=1}^{\frac{a}{p}-1}\left\lceil\frac{rp}{k}\right\rceil+\frac{(a-p)(d-1)}{2p}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

By the Equation (
3
), we have

N_{dr,p}(m)=O_{B}(ma+rp)\cdot\frac{ha}{p}+\left(\frac{ma}{p}+r\right)d,

where
O_{B}(ma+rp)=\min\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k-K}x_{i}\mid\sum_{i=1}^{k-K}(K+i)\cdot x_{i}=ma+rp,x_{i}\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
.

Let
ma+rp=k\cdot s+t
, where
s\geq 0,1\leq t\leq k
.
When
K+1\leq t\leq k
, we have
O_{B}(ma+rp)=s+1
, which minimizes at
x_{k-K}=s,x_{t-K}=1,x_{i}=0,(i\neq k-K,t-K),t\neq k
and
x_{k-K}=s+1,x_{i}=0,(i\neq k-K),t=k
.
When
1\leq t\leq K
, there is no solution for
s=0
. For
s\geq 1
, we have
ma+rp=(s-1)k+k+t
and
O_{B}(M)=s+1
, which minimizes at
x_{k-K}=s-1,x_{k+t-2K-1}=1,x_{1}=1,x_{i}=0,(i\neq 1,k+t-2K-1,k-K)
; This is because
K\leq\frac{k-1}{2}
.

Therefore, we have

N_{dr,p}(m)=\frac{(s+1)ha}{p}+d\left(\frac{ma}{p}+r\right)=\left\lceil\frac{ma+rp}{k}\right\rceil\cdot\frac{ha}{p}+d\left(\frac{ma}{p}+r\right),

which is increasing with respect to
m
. Therefore
N_{dr,p}=N_{dr,p}(0)
for
s\neq 0
or
K+1\leq t\leq k
and
N_{dr,p}=N_{dr,p}(1)
for
s=0,1\leq t\leq K

(note that
N_{dr,p}(0)
does not exit in this case).

Thus we have

\displaystyle N_{dr,p}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{ha}{p}\left\lceil\frac{a+rp}{k}\right\rceil+d\left(\frac{a}{p}+r\right)\ &\text{if}&\ \ s=0,1\leq t\leq K,\\
&\frac{ha}{p}\left\lceil\frac{rp}{k}\right\rceil+dr\ &\text{if}&\ \ \text{otherwise}.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

For a given
r
, if
p>K
, then we have
N_{dr,p}=N_{dr,p}(0)=\frac{ha}{p}\lceil\frac{rp}{k}\rceil+rd
. This is increasing with respect to
r
. By Lemma
2.1
, we have the Frobenius number

F\left(\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right)=N_{d\left(\frac{a}{p}-1\right),p}-\frac{a}{p}=\left\lceil\frac{a-p}{k}\right\rceil\cdot\frac{ha}{p}+\left(\frac{a}{p}-1\right)d-\frac{a}{p},

and the genus

g\left(\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right)=\frac{p}{a}\sum_{r=1}^{\frac{a}{p}-1}N_{dr,p}-\frac{a-p}{2p}=h\cdot\sum_{r=1}^{\frac{a}{p}-1}\left\lceil\frac{rp}{k}\right\rceil+\frac{(a-p)(d-1)}{2p}.

If
p\leq K
, we assume
K=cp+c^{\prime}
,
0\leq c^{\prime}\leq p-1
. So we have
c=\left\lfloor\frac{K}{p}\right\rfloor\geq 1
. Now, the
N_{dr,p}
is reduced to

\displaystyle N_{dr,p}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{ha}{p}\left\lceil\frac{a+rp}{k}\right\rceil+d\left(\frac{a}{p}+r\right)\ &\text{if}&\ \ 1\leq r\leq c,\\
&\frac{ha}{p}\left\lceil\frac{rp}{k}\right\rceil+dr\ &\text{if}&\ \ c+1\leq r\leq\frac{a}{p}-1.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

We can obviously get

\mathop{\max}\limits_{r\in\{0,1,...,\frac{a}{p}-1\}}\{N_{dr,p}\}=\max\{N_{dc,p},N_{d(\frac{a}{p}-1),p}\}=N_{dc,p}.

By Lemma
2.1
, we have the Frobenius number

F\left(\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right)=N_{dc,p}-\frac{a}{p}=\left\lceil\frac{a+cp}{k}\right\rceil\cdot\frac{ha}{p}+\left(\frac{a}{p}+c\right)d-\frac{a}{p},

and the genus

g\left(\frac{\langle A\rangle}{p}\right)=\frac{p}{a}\sum_{r=1}^{\frac{a}{p}-1}N_{dr,p}-\frac{a-p}{2p}=h\cdot\left(\sum_{r=1}^{c}\left\lceil\frac{a+rp}{k}\right\rceil+\sum_{r=c+1}^{\frac{a}{p}-1}\left\lceil\frac{rp}{k}\right\rceil\right)+\frac{(a-p)(d-1)}{2p}+dc.

This completes the proof by
c=\left\lfloor\frac{K}{p}\right\rfloor
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

2.2
, we consider

at

\max_{1\leq r\leq\frac{a}{p}-1}\{N_{dr,p}\}=\max\left\{\left\lfloor\frac{ha-d}{2hp}\right\rfloor(ha+d),\left(\frac{a}{p}-\left\lceil\frac{ha-d}{2hp}\right\rceil\right)(ha-d)\right\}.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

We first consider

O_{B}(ma+rp)=\min\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{k}x_{i}\mid x_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}(2i+1)\cdot x_{i}=ma+rp\right\}.

, where
1\leq t\leq 2k+1
.

If
t
is even, we have
O_{B}(ma+rp)=s+2
and

N_{dr,p}(m)=\frac{ha(s+2)}{p}+d\left(\frac{ma}{p}+r\right)=\frac{ha}{p}\left(\left\lfloor\frac{ma+rp-1}{2k+1}\right\rfloor+2\right)+d\left(\frac{ma}{p}+r\right).

If
t
is odd, we have
O_{B}(M)=s+1
and

&\frac{ha}{p}\left(\left\lfloor\frac{rp-1}{2k+1}\right\rfloor+1\right)+dr\ \ &\text{if}\ \ &t\ \text{is odd}.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

2.1

This completes the proof.

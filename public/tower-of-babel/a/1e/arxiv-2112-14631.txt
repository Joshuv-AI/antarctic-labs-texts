Quantum Toroidal Comodule Algebra of Type 𝑨 𝒏 − 𝟏 \boldsymbol{A_{n-1}} and Integrals of Motion
Boris FEIGIN ab, Michio JIMBO c and Evgeny MUKHIN d

Abstract
We introduce an algebra 𝒦 n \mathcal{K}_{n} which has a structure of a left comodule over the quantum toroidal algebra of type A n − 1 A_{n-1} . Algebra 𝒦 n \mathcal{K}_{n} is a higher rank generalization of 𝒦 1 \mathcal{K}_{1} , which provides a uniform description of deformed W W algebras associated with Lie (super)algebras of types BCD. We show that 𝒦 n \mathcal{K}_{n} possesses a family of commutative subalgebras.

Although the expressions of

We do not completely understand the nature of algebras
\mathcal{K}_{n}
.
The
\mathcal{K}_{n}
algebras we discuss here are of A type.
Similar algebras can be defined in the same way for arbitrary simply laced types,
though the construction of integrals of motion is more challenging.
At first glance, algebra
\mathcal{K}_{n}
bears resemblance to
\imath

[
13

[

.
However, the details seem to be very different.
For example, the relations in
\mathcal{K}_{n}
are invariant under scaling
z\to cz
but
such homogeneity does not exist in those of
[
17
]
.
The current appearing in the residues in the right-hand side of (5.5) in
[
17
]

is commutative, whereas our current
K_{i}^{-}(z)K_{i}^{+}\big(C^{2}z\big)
in (

The
\imath

we are not aware of inclusion of
\mathcal{K}_{n}
to
\mathcal{E}_{n}
.
We do not know if
\imath

Natural candidates which could be compared to
\mathcal{K}_{n}
are the reflection algebras and, in particular,
quantum twisted Yangians, see
[
18

The quantum twisted Yangians are coideal subalgebras behind integrable systems with reflections.
However, the exact relation to
\mathcal{K}_{n}
algebras is unclear. The quantum twisted Yangians are given in terms of the

R

\mathcal{K}_{n}

and the formulas for the generators of the coideal are quadratic in generators of quantum group.

In quantum toroidal algebras
\mathcal{E}_{n}
, the integrals of motion originate from the standard construction of transfer matrices
and depend on
n
arbitrary parameters.
They commute for a simple reason following from the Yang–Baxter equation.
Then a computation provides the expression for the integrals of motion in the form of integrals.
The integrals of motion in the
\mathcal{K}_{n}
algebra are given virtually by the same formula as in
\mathcal{E}_{n}
,
except that one of the parameters (the elliptic nome) is fixed by the central element
C
.
But we do not have any general reason for their commutativity; we simply check it directly.

It is known that in type A there is a natural duality of such models of
(\mathfrak{gl}_{m},\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
type, see
[
6
]
.
Under that duality, the algebras
\mathcal{E}_{m}
and
\mathcal{E}_{n}
act on the same space in such a way that the screening operators
for one algebra are given in terms of generating currents of the other.
Furthermore the integrals of motion of both algebras commute with each other.
For
\mathcal{K}_{1}
acting in a representation, we have a system of screening operators, see
[
7
]
.
We expect that the algebras generated by the corresponding screening currents also possess a commutative family
which should be called “non-local” integrals of motion, and that “local” and “non-local” integrals of motion commute.

Similarly, we expect that algebras
\mathcal{K}_{n}
acting in representations commute with a family of screening operators.
Moreover the algebra generated by the screening currents does not depend on
n
.
For the case of the tensor product of
\ell
Fock
\mathcal{E}_{n}
modules with a boundary

\mathcal{K}_{n}
module, we anticipate
\ell+1
screening currents each given as a sums of
n
vertex operators.
The corresponding “non-local” integrals of motion should commute with the “local”
integrals of motion
\{\mathbf{I}_{N}\}
constructed in this paper.
We plan to address this issue in future publications.

2.1
Quantum toroidal algebra
\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{E}}_{n}}

Fix a positive integer
n\geq 3
.
We define
g_{i,j}(z,w)
,
G_{i,j}(x)
for
i,j\in{\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}}
as follows

\displaystyle g_{i,i}(z,w)=z-q^{2}w,\qquad G_{i,i}(x)=q^{2}\frac{1-q^{-2}x}{1-q^{2}x},

\displaystyle g_{i,i\pm 1}(z,w)=z-q^{-1}d^{\pm 1}w,\qquad G_{i,i\pm 1}(x)=q^{-1}\frac{1-qd^{\pm 1}x}{1-q^{-1}d^{\pm 1}x},

\displaystyle g_{i,j}(z,w)=z-w,\qquad G_{i,j}(x)=1\qquad\text{if}\quad j\not\equiv i,i\pm 1.

Here
G_{i,j}(x)
should be understood as power series expansion in
x
.
As rational function we have

\displaystyle G_{i,j}(w/z)=-\frac{g_{j,i}(w,z)}{d_{i,j}g_{i,j}(z,w)}=G_{j,i}(z/w)^{-1},

where
d_{i,j}=d^{\pm 1}
,
i\equiv j\pm 1
, and
d_{i,j}=1
otherwise.

Let
P
be a free
{\mathbb{Z}}
module with basis
\{\varepsilon_{i}\mid i\in{\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}}\}
, equipped with a symmetric bilinear form
(~,~)\colon P\times P\to{\mathbb{Z}}

such that
\varepsilon_{i}
are orthonormal. We write
\bar{\alpha}_{i}=\varepsilon_{i-1}-\varepsilon_{i}
.

The quantum toroidal algebra
{\mathcal{E}}_{n}
of type
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
is a unital associative algebra generated by

\displaystyle e_{i,k},\quad f_{i,k},\quad h_{i,r},\quad i\in{\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}},\ k\in{\mathbb{Z}},\ r\in{\mathbb{Z}}\backslash\{0\},\qquad q^{h},\quad h\in P,\qquad C^{\pm 1}.

In terms of the generating series

\displaystyle e_{i}(z)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}e_{i,k}z^{-k},\qquad f_{i}(z)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}f_{i,k}z^{-k},

\displaystyle\psi^{\pm}_{i}(z)=\psi_{i,0}^{\pm}\exp\bigg({\pm}\big(q-q^{-1}\big)\sum_{r>0}h_{i,\pm r}z^{\mp r}\bigg),\qquad\psi_{i,0}^{\pm}=q^{\pm\bar{\alpha}_{i}},

the defining relations read as follows

\displaystyle q^{h}q^{h^{\prime}}=q^{h+h^{\prime}},\quad h,h^{\prime}\in P,\qquad q^{0}=1,\qquad\text{$C$ is central},

\displaystyle q^{h}e_{i}(z)q^{-h}=q^{(h,\bar{\alpha}_{i})}e_{i}(z),\quad q^{h}f_{i}(z)q^{-h}=q^{-(h,\bar{\alpha}_{i})}f_{i}(z),\quad q^{h}\psi^{\pm}_{i}(z)q^{-h}=\psi_{i}^{\pm}(z),\quad h\in P,

\displaystyle\psi^{\pm}_{i}(z)\psi^{\pm}_{j}(w)=\psi^{\pm}_{j}(w)\psi^{\pm}_{i}(z),

\displaystyle\psi^{+}_{i}(z)\psi^{-}_{j}(w)=\psi^{-}_{j}(w)\psi^{+}_{i}(z)G_{i,j}(Cw/z)^{-1}G_{i,j}\big(C^{-1}w/z\big),

\displaystyle\psi_{i}^{+}(z)e_{j}(w)=e_{j}(w)\psi_{i}^{+}(z)G_{i,j}\big(C^{-1}w/z\big),\qquad e_{j}(w)\psi_{i}^{-}(z)=\psi_{i}^{-}(z)e_{j}(w)G_{j,i}(z/w),

\displaystyle\psi_{i}^{+}(z)f_{j}(w)=f_{j}(w)\psi_{i}^{+}(z)G_{i,j}(w/z)^{-1},\qquad f_{j}(w)\psi_{i}^{-}(z)=\psi_{i}^{-}(z)f_{j}(w)G_{j,i}(Cz/w)^{-1},

\displaystyle[e_{i}(z),f_{j}(w)]=\frac{\delta_{i,j}}{q-q^{-1}}(\delta\bigl(C{w}/{z}\bigr)\psi_{i}^{+}(w)-\delta\bigl(C{z}/{w}\bigr)\psi_{i}^{-}(z)),

\displaystyle[e_{i}(z),e_{j}(w)]=0,\qquad[f_{i}(z),f_{j}(w)]=0,\qquad i\not\equiv j,j\pm 1,

\displaystyle d_{i,j}g_{i,j}(z,w)e_{i}(z)e_{j}(w)+g_{j,i}(w,z)e_{j}(w)e_{i}(z)=0,

\displaystyle d_{j,i}g_{j,i}(w,z)f_{i}(z)f_{j}(w)+g_{i,j}(z,w)f_{j}(w)f_{i}(z)=0,

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}_{{z_{1},z_{2}}}[e_{i}(z_{1}),[e_{i}(z_{2}),e_{i\pm 1}(w)]_{q}]_{q^{-1}}=0,

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}_{{z_{1},z_{2}}}[f_{i}(z_{1}),[f_{i}(z_{2}),f_{i\pm 1}(w)]_{q}]_{q^{-1}}=0.

Here
[A,B]_{p}=AB-pBA
, and the symbol
\mathrm{Sym}
stands for symmetrization:

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}_{x_{1},\dots,x_{N}}\ f(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})=\frac{1}{N!}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{N}}f(x_{\sigma(1)},\dots,x_{\sigma{(N)}}).

The relations above imply in particular

\displaystyle[h_{i,r},h_{j,s}]=\delta_{r+s,0}\cdot a_{i,j}(r)\frac{C^{r}-C^{-r}}{q-q^{-1}},\qquad r,s\neq 0,

\displaystyle a_{i,j}(r)=\frac{1}{r}\frac{q^{r}-q^{-r}}{q-q^{-1}}\big((q^{r}+q^{-r})\delta^{(n)}_{i,j}-d^{r}\delta^{(n)}_{i,j-1}-d^{-r}\delta^{(n)}_{i,j+1}\big).

3.1
Algebra
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}_{n}}

We introduce an algebra
\mathcal{K}_{n}
through generating series

\displaystyle E_{i}(z)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}E_{i,k}z^{-k},\qquad K^{\pm}_{i}(z)=K^{\pm}_{i,0}\exp\biggl(\pm\big(q-q^{-1}\big)\sum_{\pm r>0}H_{i,r}z^{-r}\biggr),\qquad i\in{\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}},

and an invertible central element
C
.
The defining relations read as follows

\displaystyle K_{i}^{\pm}(z)K_{j}^{\pm}(w)=K_{j}^{\pm}(w)K_{i}^{\pm}(z),

(3.1)

\displaystyle K_{i}^{+}(z)K^{-}_{j}(w)=K^{-}_{j}(w)K_{i}^{+}(z)G_{i,j}(w/z)G_{i,j}\big(C^{2}w/z\big),

(3.2)

\displaystyle K_{i}^{+}(z)E_{j}(w)=E_{j}(w)K_{i}^{+}(z)G_{i,j}(w/z),\quad E_{j}(w)K_{i}^{-}(z)=K_{i}^{-}(z)E_{j}(w)G_{j,i}(z/w),

(3.3)

\displaystyle d_{i,j}g_{i,j}(z,w)E_{i}(z)E_{j}(w)+g_{j,i}(w,z)E_{j}(w)E_{i}(z)

(3.4)

\displaystyle\quad{}=\!\frac{\delta_{i,j}}{q\!-\!q^{-1}}\big(g_{i,i}(z,w)\delta\bigl(C^{2}z/w\bigr)K^{-}_{i}(z)K^{+}_{i}\big(C^{2}z\big)\!+\!g_{i,i}(w,z)\delta\bigl(C^{2}w/z\bigr)K^{-}_{i}(w)K^{+}_{i}\big(C^{2}w\big)\big),

\displaystyle E_{i}(z)E_{j}(w)=E_{j}(w)E_{i}(z),\qquad i\not\equiv j,\,j\pm 1.

(3.5)

In addition, we impose the Serre relations

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}}_{z_{1},z_{2}}\,[E_{i}(z_{1}),[E_{i}(z_{2}),E_{i\pm 1}(w)]_{q}]_{q^{-1}}=-\mathop{\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}}_{z_{1},z_{2}}

\displaystyle\bigg\{\bigg(\frac{1}{1-q^{-1}d^{\mp 1}z_{1}/w}+\frac{q^{-1}d^{\pm 1}w/z_{2}}{1-q^{-1}d^{\pm 1}w/z_{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\times\delta\big(C^{2}z_{1}/z_{2}\big)K_{i}^{-}(z_{1})E_{i\pm 1}(w)K_{i}^{+}(z_{2})\bigg\}.

(3.6)

In the right-hand side, the rational functions of the form
1/(1-az_{1}/w)
or
1/(1-bw/z_{2})

stand for their expansions in non-negative powers of
z_{1}/w
or
w/z_{2}
, respectively.

In the right-hand sides of (
3.4
) and (
3.6
), the Fourier coefficients of
K_{i}^{-}(z)K_{i}^{+}\big(C^{2}z\big)
are infinite series.
Therefore the relations require some justification. We proceed as follows.
We define a
{\mathbb{Z}}
grading of generators by

\displaystyle\deg E_{i,k}=k,\qquad\deg H_{i,\pm r}=\pm r,\qquad\deg K_{i,0}^{\pm}=\deg C=0.

We call it the homogeneous grading.
Consider the free algebra
\mathcal{A}
generated by
E_{i,k}
,
H_{i,\pm r}
,
K^{\pm}_{i,0}
,
C^{\pm 1}
.
Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
be the completion of
\mathcal{A}
with respect to the grading in the positive direction.
Then we define
\mathcal{K}_{n}
to be the graded quotient algebra of
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
under the relations (
3.1
)–(
3.6
).

The quadratic relations (
3.4
) imply

\displaystyle d_{i,j}\hat{g}_{i,j}(z,w)E_{i}(z)E_{j}(w)+\hat{g}_{j,i}(w,z)E_{j}(w)E_{i}(z)=0,

(3.7)

where

\displaystyle\hat{g}_{i,j}(z,w)=\begin{cases}\big(z-C^{2}w\big)\big(z-C^{-2}w\big)\big(z-q^{2}w\big),&i=j,\\
g_{i,j}(z,w),&i\neq j.\end{cases}

The factor
\big(z-C^{2}w\big)\big(z-C^{-2}w\big)
for
i=j
is chosen to kill the delta functions in the right-hand side of (
3.4
).

Remark 3.1
.

Algebra
\mathcal{K}_{n}
has a filtration defined by

\mathop{\rm pdeg}E_{i}(z)=1
,
\mathop{\rm pdeg}K_{i}^{\pm}(z)=0
for all
i
, which we call
the principal filtration.
In the associated graded algebra, the subalgebra generated by
\{E_{i}(z)\}
is isomorphic to
the subalgebra of the quantum toroidal algebra
{\mathcal{E}}_{n}
generated by
\{e_{i}(z)\}
.

The relations for
K^{\pm}_{i}(z)
are slightly different from those of
\psi^{\pm}_{i}(z)
in
{\mathcal{E}}_{n}
,
since the right-hand side of (
3.2
) contains a product of
G_{i,j}
’s as opposed to the ratio.
In particular, while the elements
\psi^{\pm}_{i,0}\in{\mathcal{E}}_{n}
are inverse to each other,
the elements
K_{i,0}^{\pm}\in\mathcal{K}_{n}
are not mutually commutative,

\displaystyle K_{i,0}^{+}K_{j,0}^{-}=q^{2(\bar{\alpha}_{i},\bar{\alpha}_{j})}K_{j,0}^{-}K_{i,0}^{+}.

We have the relations

\displaystyle[H_{i,r},H_{i,s}]=-\delta_{r+s,0}\cdot a_{i,j}(r)\frac{1+C^{2r}}{q-q^{-1}},\qquad r>0,

which are to be compared with (
2.1
).
In the limit
q\to 1
with
C=q^{c}
, algebra
{\mathcal{E}}_{n}
reduces to the enveloping algebra of a Lie algebra
of matrix valued difference operators, see
[
3
, Section 3.7]
.
In contrast, algebra
\mathcal{K}_{n}
does not seem to have a reasonable limit due to the presence of the sum
1+C^{2r}
.

All representations of

4
Integrals of motion

We continue to work with admissible representations of
\mathcal{K}_{n}
on which
C
acts as a scalar.
Our goal in this section is to introduce a family of commuting operators acting on each such representation
(with mild restrictions on parameters),
which we call integrals of motion (IMs). Loosely speaking, we may think of them as generators of
a commutative subalgebra of
\mathcal{K}_{n}
.

The IMs will depend on
q
,
d
,
C
as well as additional arbitrary parameters

\displaystyle\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n}\in{\mathbb{C}},\qquad\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{i}=0.

(4.1)

In addition, they depend also on an “elliptic” parameter
p
. However,
our experience with the algebra
\mathcal{K}_{1}

tells that the IMs exist only when
p
and
C
are related in a special way.
Guided by the result in
[
7
]
, we shall impose the relation

\displaystyle C^{2}=pq^{2}

and make the following assumption:

\displaystyle|p|<1,\qquad\big|qd^{\pm 1}\big|<1,\qquad\big|pq^{-2}\big|<1,\qquad\big|pq^{-1}d^{\pm 1}\big|<1.

(4.2)

In particular
|q^{2}|<1
. We use parameters
\tau\in{\mathbb{C}}
with
\mathop{\rm Im}\tau>0
and
\beta,\gamma\in{\mathbb{C}}
given by

\displaystyle p={\rm e}^{-2\pi{\rm i}/\tau},\qquad q^{2}=p^{\gamma},\qquad d=p^{\beta}.

(4.3)

We use also
\theta(u)=p^{u^{2}/2-u/2}\Theta_{p}(p^{u})
, which satisfies

\displaystyle\theta(u+1)=-\theta(u),\qquad\theta(u+\tau)=-{\rm e}^{-2\pi{\rm i}u-\pi{\rm i}\tau}\theta(u).

(4.4)

4.1
Dressed currents

A basic constituent of IMs is the dressed currents, obtained by modifying the currents

of
\mathcal{K}_{n}
.

In the following we define operators
H_{i,0}
by

(K_{i,0}^{-})^{-1}K_{i,0}^{+}=q^{2H_{i,0}}

The dressed currents of

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{i}(z)=\mathbf{K}_{i}^{-}(z)^{-1}E_{i}(z)z^{-\gamma H_{i,0}-\gamma+\mu_{i}},

\displaystyle\mathbf{K}_{i}^{-}(z)=\exp\bigg({-}\big(q-q^{-1}\big)\sum_{r>0}\frac{H_{i,-r}}{1-p^{r}}z^{r}\bigg),

The dressed currents have well-defined matrix coefficients on admissible representations.

\displaystyle\lambda_{i,j}(z_{2}/z_{1})\mathbf{E}_{i}(z_{1})\mathbf{E}_{j}(z_{2})=\lambda_{j,i}(z_{1}/z_{2})\mathbf{E}_{j}(z_{2})\mathbf{E}_{i}(z_{1}),

Here and after, we set

Proof.

This follows from the relations

\displaystyle\lambda_{i,i\pm 1}\big(p^{\mp u}\big)\theta(u\pm\gamma/2-\beta)=p^{(\gamma/2\mp\beta)^{2}/2-(\gamma/2-\beta)/2}\,\eta(u).

∎

4.3

\vartheta_{1},\vartheta_{2}\in{X}_{n}
. We set
\Phi_{n,M,N}=0
if
M=0
or
N=0
.

Lemma A.4
.

For
n\geq 2
,
\Phi_{n,M,N}
has the quasi-periodicity in each variable
u_{i,a}
,

\displaystyle\Phi_{n,M,N}(\dots,u_{i,a}+1,\dots)=\Phi_{n,M,N}(\dots,u_{i,a},\dots),

\displaystyle\Phi_{n,M,N}(\dots,u_{i,a}+\tau,\dots)={\rm e}^{-2\pi{\rm i}(2\bar{u}_{i}-\bar{u}_{i-1}-\bar{u}_{i+1}-\mu_{i}+\tau)}\Phi_{n,M,N}(\dots,u_{i,a},\dots).

For
n=1
,
\Phi_{1,M,N}
has periods
1
and
\tau
.

Modulo
{\mathbb{Z}}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}\tau
, the only poles of
\Phi_{n,M,N}
are
u_{i,a}=u_{i,b}+\gamma
with some
a
,
b
,
i
.

Proof.

The quasi-periodicity follows from (
4.4
), (
4.6
) and (
4.7
).
Since
\Phi_{n,M,N}
is symmetric in each group of variables
\{u_{i,a}\}_{a=1,\dots,M+N}
, it cannot have a simple pole at

u_{i,a}=u_{i,b}
.
∎

Lemma A.5
.

We have

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm res}_{u_{1,1}=u_{1,2}+\gamma}\cdots\mathop{\rm res}_{u_{n,1}=u_{n,2}+\gamma}\,\Phi_{n,M,N}(\mathbf{u}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{u}_{n}|\vartheta_{1},\vartheta_{2})

\displaystyle\qquad{}=A^{n}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{a=2}^{M+N}\theta(u_{i,a}-u_{i+1,2}-\alpha)\theta(u_{i,2}-u_{i+1,a}-\alpha+\gamma)}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{a=3}^{M+N}\theta(u_{i,a}-u_{i,2})\theta(u_{i,a}-u_{i,2}-\gamma)}

\displaystyle\qquad\hphantom{=}{}\times\Phi_{n,M-1,N-1}(\mathbf{u}^{\prime}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{u}^{\prime}_{n}|\vartheta^{\prime}_{1},\vartheta^{\prime}_{2}),

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}^{\prime}_{i}=(u_{i,3},\dots,u_{i,M+N}),

\displaystyle\vartheta^{\prime}_{s}(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})=\vartheta_{s}(v_{1}+u_{1,2},\dots,v_{n}+u_{n,2}),\qquad s=1,2,

and
A=\theta(\gamma)^{-1}\mathop{\rm res}_{u=0}\theta(u)^{-1}
.

Proof.

In the sum
{\rm LHS}_{n,M,N}
, the iterated residue is non-zero only for terms such that

1\in I_{i}
and
2\in J_{i}
for all
i=1,\dots,n
.
Write
I_{i}=\{1\}\sqcup\bar{I}_{i}
and
J_{i}=\{2\}\sqcup\bar{J}_{i}
.
Similarly, for
{\rm RHS}_{n,M,N}
one must have

2\in I_{i}
and
1\in J_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,n
. Write
I_{i}=\{2\}\sqcup\bar{I}_{i}
and
J_{i}=\{1\}\sqcup\bar{J}_{i}
in that case.

In either case, the functions
\vartheta_{s}
become

\displaystyle\vartheta_{1}(u_{1,2}+\bar{u}_{1,\bar{I}_{1}},\dots,u_{n,2}+\bar{u}_{n,\bar{I}_{n}})\vartheta_{2}(u_{1,2}+\bar{u}_{1,\bar{J}_{1}},\dots,u_{n,2}+\bar{u}_{n,\bar{J}_{n}})

due to Lemma
A.2
.

The rest is a simple computation.
∎

Lemma A.7
.

Fix
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. Then the following hold:

\displaystyle\Phi_{n,M,N}(\mathbf{u}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{u}_{n}|\vartheta_{1},\vartheta_{2})\bigl|_{\begin{subarray}{c}u_{i+1,a}=u_{i,a}-\alpha\\
1\leq a\leq M+N\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle\qquad{}=(-1)^{MN}\Phi_{n-1,M,N}(\mathbf{u}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{u}_{i},\mathbf{u}_{i+2},\dots,\mathbf{u}_{n}|\vartheta^{\prime\prime}_{1},\vartheta^{\prime\prime}_{2}),

\displaystyle\Phi_{n,M,N}(\mathbf{u}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{u}_{n}|\vartheta_{1},\vartheta_{2})\bigl|_{\begin{subarray}{c}u_{i+1,a}=u_{i,a}-\alpha+\gamma\\
1\leq a\leq M+N\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle\qquad{}=(-1)^{MN}\Phi_{n-1,M,N}(\mathbf{u}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{u}_{i},\mathbf{u}_{i+2},\dots,\mathbf{u}_{n}|\vartheta^{\prime\prime\prime}_{1},\vartheta^{\prime\prime\prime}_{2}),

where

\displaystyle\vartheta^{\prime\prime}_{1}(v_{1},\dots,v_{n-1})=\vartheta_{1}(v_{1},\dots,v_{i},v_{i}-M\alpha,v_{i+1},\dots,v_{n-1}),

\displaystyle\vartheta^{\prime\prime}_{2}(v_{1},\dots,v_{n-1})=\vartheta_{2}(v_{1},\dots,v_{i},v_{i}-N\alpha,v_{i+1},\dots,v_{n-1}),

and
\vartheta^{\prime\prime\prime}_{s}
is given by replacing
\alpha
by
\alpha-\gamma
in
\vartheta^{\prime\prime}_{s}
.

Proof.

Since the calculations are similar, we consider the first case.
Under this specialization, terms in
{\rm LHS}_{n,M,N}
survive only when

I_{i}\cap J_{i+1}=\varnothing
, which implies
I_{i}=I_{i+1}
and
J_{i}=J_{i+1}
.
Then the factor

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in I_{i}\\
b\in J_{i+1}\end{subarray}}\theta(u_{i,a}-u_{i+1,b}-\alpha)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in I_{i+1}\\
b\in J_{i}\end{subarray}}\theta(u_{i,b}-u_{i+1,a}-\alpha+\gamma)

from the numerator cancels

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in I_{i+1}\\
b\in J_{i+1}\end{subarray}}\theta(u_{i+1,a}-u_{i+1,b})\theta(u_{i+1,a}-u_{i+1,b}-\gamma)

from the denominator, thereby reducing
n
to
n-1
.
For
{\rm RHS}_{n,M,N}
the situation is entirely similar.
∎

Lemma A.8

(
[
19
]
)
.

The identity (
A.1
) holds for
n=1
.

Proof.

For
n=1
we may assume
\vartheta_{1}=\vartheta_{2}=1
, so the identity takes the form

\displaystyle\sum_{I,J}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in I\\
b\in J\end{subarray}}\frac{\theta(u_{a}-u_{b}-\alpha)\theta(u_{b}-u_{a}-\alpha+\gamma)}{\theta(u_{a}-u_{b})\theta(u_{a}-u_{b}-\gamma)}=\sum_{I,J}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in I\\
b\in J\end{subarray}}\frac{\theta(u_{b}-u_{a}-\alpha)\theta(u_{a}-u_{b}-\alpha+\gamma)}{\theta(u_{b}-u_{a})\theta(u_{b}-u_{a}-\gamma)}.

Lemma
A.5
together with the symmetry shows that

\Phi_{1,M,N}
has no pole at
u_{a}=u_{b}+\gamma
. Hence
\Phi_{1,M,N}
is an elliptic function without poles, therefore it is a constant.
Upon specializing
u_{a}=a\alpha
,
a=1,\dots,M+N
, we obtain

\displaystyle{\rm LHS}_{1,M,N}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{MN}\frac{\prod_{a=1}^{M}\theta((a-1)\alpha+\gamma)/\theta(a\alpha)\prod_{a=1}^{N}\theta((a-1)\alpha+\gamma)/\theta(a\alpha)}{\prod_{a=1}^{M+N}\theta((a-1)\alpha+\gamma)/\theta(a\alpha)}

\displaystyle={\rm RHS}_{1,M,N},

thereby proving
\Phi_{1,M,N}=0
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
A.1
.

We use induction on
n
. The base of induction is proved in Lemma
A.8
.
Assume that theorem is true for
n-1
.

For
i=1,\dots,n
we set

\displaystyle Z_{i}=\mathop{\rm res}_{u_{i,2}=u_{i,1}-\gamma}\mathop{\rm res}_{u_{i+1,2}=u_{i+1,1}-\gamma}\cdots\mathop{\rm res}_{u_{n,2}=u_{n,1}-\gamma}\,\Phi_{n,M,N}(\mathbf{u}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{u}_{n},\alpha,\gamma|\vartheta_{1},\vartheta_{2}).

We show
Z_{i}=0
by induction on
i
.
By Lemma
A.5
and the induction hypothesis
\Phi_{n-1,M,N}=0
, we have
Z_{1}=0
.
Suppose
Z_{i}=\mathop{\rm res}_{u_{i,2}=u_{i,1}-\gamma}Z_{i+1}=0
. Then
Z_{i+1}
has no poles in
\mathbf{u}_{i}
.

Since
u_{i+1,2}=u_{i+1,1}-\gamma
, non-zero terms in
Z_{i+1}
are such that

1\in I_{i+1}
,
2\in J_{i+1}
in LHS. Then in the numerator we have factors

\theta(u_{i,a}-u_{i+1,2}-\alpha)
,
a\in I_{i}
, and

\theta(u_{i,b}-u_{i+1,1}-\alpha+\gamma)
,
b\in J_{i}
. Therefore each term has a factor

\displaystyle\prod_{a=1}^{M+N}\theta(u_{i,a}-u_{i+1,2}-\alpha).

By the same argument, RHS, and hence
Z_{i+1}
, is shown to have the same factor.
If
Z_{i+1}\neq 0
, this is a contradiction because the quasi-periodicity property of
Z_{i+1}
implies that
it is a function of
\bar{u}_{i}=\sum_{a=1}^{M+N}u_{i,a}
, see Lemma
A.3
.
Hence we have
Z_{i+1}=0
.

It follows that
\Phi_{n,M,N}
has no poles at
u_{n,a}=u_{n,b}-\gamma
.
Therefore, if non-zero, it is a function of the sum
\bar{u}_{n}=\sum_{a=1}^{M+N}u_{n,a}
.
On the other hand, by Lemma
A.7
and the induction hypothesis,

\Phi_{n,M,N}
has the following zeros with respect to
\bar{u}_{n}
:

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{n}=\bar{u}_{1}+(M+N)\alpha,\qquad\bar{u}_{n}=\bar{u}_{1}+(M+N)(\alpha-\gamma),

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{n}=\bar{u}_{n-1}-(M+N)\alpha,\qquad\bar{u}_{n}=\bar{u}_{n-1}-(M+N)(\alpha-\gamma).

From the quasi-periodicity, if
\Phi_{n,M,N}\neq 0
, then it can have only two zeros modulo
{\mathbb{Z}}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}\tau
,
which contradicts to the above zeros.

This completes the proof of Theorem
A.1
.
∎

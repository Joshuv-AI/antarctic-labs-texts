L 2 L^{2} -type invariants and cohomology jump loci for complex smooth quasi-projective varieties
Fenglin Li, Yongqiang Liu

Abstract
. Let X X be a complex smooth quasi-projective variety with a fixed epimorphism ν : π 1 ​ ( X ) ↠ ℤ \nu\colon\pi_{1}(X)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z} . We consider the asymptotic behaviour of invariants such as Betti numbers with any field coefficients and the order of the torsion part of singular integral homology associated to ν \nu , known as the L 2 L^{2} -type invariants. At homological degree one, we give concrete formulas for these limits by the geometric information of X X when ν \nu is orbifold effective. The proof relies on a study about cohomological degree one jump loci of X X . We extend part of Arapura’s result for cohomological degree one jump loci of X X with complex field coefficients to the one with positive characteristic field coefficients. As an application, when X X is a hyperplane arrangement complement, a combinatoric upper bound is given for the number of parallel positive dimensional components in cohomological degree one jump loci with complex coefficients. Another application is that we give a positive answer to a question posed by Denham and Suciu for hyperplane arrangement.

1.
Introduction

There is a general principal to consider a classical invariant of a finite CW complex and define its analog for its covering space. This leads to the
L^{2}
-type invariants. Atiyah
[
Ati76
]
introduced the notion of
L^{2}
-Betti numbers in the context of a regular covering of a closed Riemannian manifold. After that, there has been vast literature for the
L^{2}
-invariant theory, see
[
Luc02
]
. The
L^{2}
-invariant theory has attracted considerable interests and has relations with many other fields, such as operator theory and Algebraic
K
-theory. A particular important result is Lück’s approximation theorem
[
Luc94
]
, which states that the
L^{2}
-Betti numbers of the universal cover of a finite polyhedron can be found as limits of normalised Betti numbers of finitely sheeted normal coverings.

1.1.
L^{2}
-type invariants

Assume that
X
is a connected finite CW-complex with a fixed group epimorphism
\nu\colon\pi_{1}(X)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}
.
Let
X^{\nu}
denote the corresponding infinite cyclic covering space of
X
.
For any positive integer
N
, let
X^{\nu,N}
denote the covering space of
X
associated to the following composition of maps

\pi_{1}(X)\overset{\nu}{\twoheadrightarrow}\mathbb{Z}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}.

One can study
X^{\nu}
by its approximation
X^{\nu,N}
. Consider the following two limits

(1)

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\dim H_{i}(X^{\nu,N},\mathbb{K})}{N}

for any field coefficient
\mathbb{K}
and

(2)

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log|H_{i}(X^{\nu,N},\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{tor}}|}{N},

where
|H_{i}(X^{\nu,N},\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{tor}}|
denotes the cardinality of the torsion part of
H_{i}(X^{\nu,N},\mathbb{Z})
.

It is known that the first limit always exists for any field coefficients and does not depend on the choice of chains, see e.g.
[
LLS11
, Theorem 0.2]
.
When
\mathrm{char}(\mathbb{K})=0
, the existence of limit follows also from the well known Lück’s approximation theorem
[
Luc94
]
, which shows that the first limit coincides with the
L^{2}
-Betti number defined by the Von Neumann algebra. (Lück’s approximation theorem is proved with much more generality.)
Set

\alpha_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})\coloneqq\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\dim H_{i}(X^{\nu,N},\mathbb{K})}{N}

and one can compute it by homology jump loci as follows, see e.g.
[
DS14
, Theorem 2.5]
.

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be an algebraically closed field.
With the assumptions and notations as above, for any
i\geq 0
we have

\alpha_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})=\dim H_{i}(X,L_{\rho})
for
\rho\in\mathbb{K}^{*}
being general.

Here
L_{\rho}
is the rank one
\mathbb{K}
-local system on
X
corresponding to
\rho
pulling back by
\nu
.
In particular,
\alpha_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})
is always an integer.

We will give a self-contained proof for the above proposition, see Proposition
2.5
. When
X
is a complex smooth projective variety and
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
, similar formulas for Betti numbers and Hodge numbers can be found in
[
DL24
]
. When
X
is relaxed to be a complex smooth quasi-projective variety, there are a lot of works to study the polynomial periodicity for the Betti numbers and Hodge numbers of congruence covers, see
[
Suc01
, Section 5]
and
[
Bud09
]
. However we do not pursue this direction in this paper.

On the other hand, the limit in (
2
) also exists, e.g. see
[
Le14
, Theorem 5]
, and it can be computed by the Mahler measure (see Definition
2.7
) of the
i
-th integral Alexander polynomial
\Delta_{i}(X^{\nu})\in\mathbb{Z}[t,t^{-1}]
(see Definition
2.3
).
Set

\mathbf{M}_{i}(X^{\nu})\coloneqq\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log|H_{i}(X^{\nu,N},\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{tor}}|}{N}.

The above discussion shows that these two limits are determined by jump loci and integral Alexander polynomials. When
X
is a smooth complex quasi-projective variety, under certain assumptions for
\nu
we give concrete formulas to compute these limits at homological degree one (namely
i=1
) by geometric information of
X
. The formulas rely on a detailed study about cohomological degree one jump loci of
X
.

1.2.
Main results

1.2.1.
Cohomology jump loci and orbifold maps

Let
X
be a smooth complex quasi-projective variety with
\pi_{1}(X)=G
and
\mathbb{K}
be an algebraically closed field.
The group of
\mathbb{K}
-valued characters,
\mathrm{Hom}(G,\mathbb{K}^{*})
, is a commutative affine algebraic group. Each character
\rho\in\mathrm{Hom}(G,\mathbb{K}^{*})
defines a rank one
\mathbb{K}
-local system on
X
, denoted by
L_{\rho}
.

Definition 1.2
.

The cohomology jump loci of
X
are defined as

\mathcal{V}^{i}_{k}(X,\mathbb{K})\coloneqq\{\rho\in\mathrm{Hom}(G,\mathbb{K}^{*})\mid\dim_{\mathbb{K}}H^{i}(X,L_{\rho})\geq k\}.

When
k=1
, we simply write
\mathcal{V}^{i}(X,\mathbb{K})
.

When
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
, the cohomology jump loci of complex smooth quasi-projective variety have been intensively studied. In particular, the well-known structure theorem (see e.g. Theorem
3.3
) for
\mathcal{V}^{i}_{k}(X,\mathbb{C})
put strong constraints for the homotopy type of complex smooth quasi-projective variety. If we focus on cohomological degree one jump loci, a celebrity result due to Beauville
[
Bea92
]
, Arapura
[
Ara97
]
and Artal Bartolo, Cogolludo-Agustín and Matei
[
ACM13
]
puts even stronger constraints for its fundamental group.

To explain their results, we recall the definition of orbifold maps. Let
\Sigma_{g,r}
be a complex smooth algebraic curve of genus
g\geq 0
with
r\geq 0
points removed. We always assume that
b_{1}(\Sigma_{g,r})=2g+r-1>0
, i.e.,
\Sigma_{g,r}\neq\mathbb{CP}^{1},\mathbb{C}
.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
X
be a complex smooth quasi-projective variety.
An algebraic map
f:X\to\Sigma_{g,r}
is called an orbifold map, if
f
is surjective and has connected generic fiber. There exists a maximal Zariski open subset
U\subset\Sigma_{g,r}
such that
f
is a fibration over
U
. Say
B=\Sigma_{g,r}-U
(could be empty) has
s
points, denoted by
\{q_{1},\ldots,q_{s}\}
. We assign
the multiplicity
\mu_{j}\geq 1
of the fiber
f^{*}q_{j}
(the
\gcd
of the coefficients of the divisor
f^{*}q_{j}
) to the point
q_{j}
. Then
f^{-1}(q_{j})
is called a multiple fiber of
f
if
\mu_{j}>1
.
Such orbifold map
f
is called of type
(g,r,{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}})
with
{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}}=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{s})
.

We say
f
has no multiple fiber if
\prod_{j=1}^{s}\mu_{j}=1
.

f
is called null if
2g+r-2=0
and
\prod_{j=1}^{s}\mu_{j}=1
, otherwise
f
is called hyperbolic.

The orbifold group associated to these data is defined as

\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(\Sigma_{g,r},{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}})\coloneqq\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g,r}\backslash\{q_{1},\ldots,q_{s}\})/\langle\gamma_{j}^{\mu_{j}}=1\text{ for all }1\leq j\leq s\rangle,

where
\gamma_{j}
is a meridian of
q_{j}
.
When
\Sigma_{g,r}
is clear in the context, we simply write
\Sigma
.
An orbifold map
f\colon X\to\Sigma
of type
(g,r,{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}})
induces a surjective map on the fundamental groups (see e.g.
[
CKO03
, Lemma 3]
)

f_{*}\colon\pi_{1}(X)\twoheadrightarrow\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(\Sigma_{g,r},{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}}),

which induces an embedding

\mathcal{V}^{1}(\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(\Sigma_{g,r},{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}}),\mathbb{K})\to\mathcal{V}^{1}(X,\mathbb{K}),

see e.g.
[
Suc14B
, Proposition A.1]
.
The following theorem shows that every positive dimensional component of
\mathcal{V}^{1}(X,\mathbb{C})
can be realized by the orbifold map in this way. This idea first appeared in Beauville’s work
[
Bea92
]
for the projective case and was extended to the
quasi-projective case by Arapura
[
Ara97
]
. Further properties were found by Dimca
[
Dim07
,
Dim09
]
, Dimca, Papadima and Suciu
[
DPS08
,
DPS09
]
, Artal Bartolo, Cogolludo-Agustín and Matei
[
ACM13
]
, etc.
We recall the theorem here and adjust it for our needs.

Theorem 1.4
.

[
Ara97
,
ACM13
]

Let
X
be a complex smooth quasi-projective variety.

(a)

We have

\mathcal{V}^{1}(X,\mathbb{C})=\bigcup_{f}{f^{*}}\mathcal{V}^{1}(\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(\Sigma_{g,r},{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}})),\mathbb{C})\cup Z,

where
Z
is a finite set of torsion points and the union runs over a finite set of hyperbolic orbifold maps
f\colon X\rightarrow\Sigma
of of type
(g,r,{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}})
.

(b)

Given an orbifold map
f\colon X\to\Sigma
of type
(g,r,{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}})
and any
\rho\in\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(\Sigma_{g,r},{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}}),\mathbb{C}^{*})
, we have

\dim H^{1}(X,f^{*}L_{\rho})\geq\dim H^{1}(\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(\Sigma_{g,r},{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}}),L_{\rho})

and equality holds with finitely many (torsion) exceptions.

In this paper, we consider the above theorem with arbitrary algebraically closed field coefficients. Theorem
1.4
(a)
is generalized by Delzant as follows using Bieri-Neumann-Strebel invariants when
X
is smooth projective.

Theorem 1.5
.

[
Delz08
]
Let
X
be a complex smooth projective variety and
\mathbb{K}
be an algebraically closed field. Then we have

\mathcal{V}^{1}(X,\mathbb{K})=\bigcup_{f}{f^{*}}\mathcal{V}^{1}(\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(\Sigma_{g,r},{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}})),\mathbb{K})\cup Z^{\prime},

where
Z^{\prime}
is a finite set of points (depends on
\mathbb{K}
) and the union runs over a finite set of hyperbolic orbifold maps
f\colon X\rightarrow\Sigma
of type
(g,r,{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}})
.

We prove the following generalization of Theorem
1.4
(b).

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
X
be a complex smooth quasi-projective variety and
\mathbb{K}
be an algebraically closed field. Given an orbifold map
f\colon X\to\Sigma
of type
(g,r,{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}})
and any
\rho\in\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(\Sigma_{g,r},{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}}),\mathbb{K}^{*})
,
we have

\dim H^{1}(X,f^{*}L_{\rho})\geq\dim H^{1}(\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(\Sigma_{g,r},{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}}),L_{\rho})

and equality holds with finitely many exceptions.

For any
\rho\in\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(\Sigma_{g,r},{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}}),\mathbb{K}^{*})
,
\dim H^{1}(\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(\Sigma_{g,r},{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}}),L_{\rho})
is computed in subsection
3.2
. Theorem
1.6
together with Theorem
1.5
indeed gives a complete description of all positive dimensional component of
\mathcal{V}^{1}_{k}(X,\mathbb{K})
for all
k
when
X
is smooth projective.

Remark 1.7
.

Theorem
1.4
(see e.g.
[
Suc14A
, Theorem 9.3]
) and Theorem
1.5
(
[
Del74
]
) also work for compact Kähler manifold. Correspondingly, one can also prove Theorem
1.6
for compact Kähler manifold by the same proof presented in this paper.

1.2.2.
Formulas for
L^{2}
-type invariants

Next we connect the epimorphism
\nu
to the orbifold maps.

Definition 1.8
.

Let
X
be a complex smooth quasi-projective variety with an epimorphism
\nu\colon\pi_{1}(X)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}
. We say that
\nu
is orbifold effective if there is an orbifold map
f\colon X\rightarrow\Sigma
of type
(g,r,{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}})
such that
\nu
factors through
f_{*}
as follows:

\textstyle{\pi_{1}(X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\nu}
\scriptstyle{f_{*}}
\textstyle{\mathbb{Z},}
\textstyle{\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(\Sigma_{g,r},{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

We say that
\nu
is orbifold effective by
f
and call
\nu
being of type
(g,r,{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}})
.

Remark 1.9
.

This definition is well-defined, see Remark
4.3
.
Note that there are examples where
\nu
is not orbifold effective, see Example
4.5
. On the other hand, if
H^{1}(X,\mathbb{Q})
is a pure Hodge structure of type
(1,1)
, (i.e.
X
has a smooth compactification
\overline{X}
such that
H^{1}(\overline{X},\mathbb{Q})=0
), then any epimorphism
\nu\colon\pi_{1}(X)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}
is orbifold effective. This is why we include null orbifold maps in Definition
1.3
.
Typical examples are hyperplane arrangement complement, see Remark
4.4
.

Theorem 1.10
.

Let
X
be a complex smooth quasi-projective variety and
\mathbb{K}
be a field with
\mathrm{char}(\mathbb{K})=p\geq 0
.
Consider an epimorphism
\nu\colon\pi_{1}(X)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}
. Then we have the following.

(a)

If
\nu
is orbifold effective of type
(g,r,{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}})
, we have

\alpha_{1}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})=2g+r-2+\#\{j\mid p\text{ divides }\mu_{j}\}

(here we use the convention that
0
does not divide any nonzero integer) and

\mathbf{M}_{1}(X^{\nu})=\sum_{j=1}^{s}\log\mu_{j}.

In particular,
\Delta_{1}(X^{\nu})
is a product of
\prod_{j=1}^{s}\mu_{j}
with some cyclotomic polynomials.

(b)

Assume that
X
is either smooth projective or
H^{1}(X,\mathbb{Q})
is a pure Hodge structure of type
(1,1)
.
If
\alpha_{1}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})>0
for some field
\mathbb{K}
or
\mathbf{M}_{1}(X^{\nu})>0
,
\nu
is orbifold effective by a hyperbolic orbifold map.

The above formula for
\alpha_{1}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})
follows from Theorem
1.6
directly and the one for
\mathbf{M}_{1}(X^{\nu})
follows by a similar proof to Theorem
1.6
. Theorem
1.10
(b) follows from Theorem
1.5
and Remark
1.9
.

Definition 1.11
.

We define
the
i
-th Aomoto Betti number with
\mathbb{K}
-coefficients
as

\beta_{i}(X,\nu_{\mathbb{K}})\coloneqq\dim_{\mathbb{K}}H^{i}(H^{\ast}(X,\mathbb{K}),\cdot\nu_{\mathbb{K}}).

and
the
i
-th Aomoto torsion number
as

\tau_{i}(X,\nu_{\mathbb{Z}})\coloneqq|H^{i+1}(H^{*}(X,\mathbb{Z}),\cdot\nu_{\mathbb{Z}})_{\mathrm{tor}}|.

Here the shift by 1 is due to the Universal Coefficient Theorem.

Since it is well-known that the cohomology ring
H^{*}(X,\mathbb{Z})
are combinatorially determined, so are
\beta_{*}(X,\nu_{\mathbb{K}})
and
\tau_{*}(X,\nu_{\mathbb{Z}})
once
\nu
, considered as an element in
H^{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})
, is fixed.
For any field
\mathbb{K}
, Papadima and Suciu
[
PS10
]
showed that

\alpha_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})\leq\beta_{i}(X,\nu_{\mathbb{K}}).

On the other hand, we give the following combinatorial upper bound for
\mathbf{M}_{*}(X^{\nu})
.

Proposition 1.12
.

Let
X
be the complement of a hyperplane arrangement.
For any epimorphism
\nu\colon\pi_{1}(X)\to\mathbb{Z}
and any
i\geq 0
, we have

\exp(\mathbf{M}_{i}(X^{\nu}))\mid\tau_{i}(X,\nu_{\mathbb{Z}}).

In particular, if
\nu
is orbifold effective of type
(g,r,{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}})
, then

(3)

\prod_{j=1}^{s}\mu_{j}\mid\tau_{1}(X,\nu_{\mathbb{Z}}).

Based on Theorem
1.4
(a) and the computations given in subsection
3.2
later, we give a different interpretation of (
3
) as follows.

Corollary 1.13
.

Let
X
be a hyperplane arrangement complement. Let
V
be a positive dimensional irreducible component of
\mathcal{V}^{1}(X,\mathbb{C})
and say it has
m
many parallel components (including itself) in
\mathcal{V}^{1}(X,\mathbb{C})
.
An epimorphism
\nu\colon\pi_{1}(X)\to\mathbb{Z}
induces an embedding

2.1.
Algebraic Preliminaries

We start with some basic commutative algebra facts.
Let
R
be a Noetherian UFD and
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module. The rank of
M
, denoted by
\mathrm{rank}M
, is defined to be
\dim_{Q}Q\otimes_{R}M
, where
Q
is the fraction field of
R
.
Consider a finite presentation of
M

\textstyle{R^{p}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\partial}
\textstyle{R^{q}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0,}

where
\partial
is a
(p\times q)
matrix with entries in
R
. Assume that the matrix
\partial
has rank
r
. Let

I(M)
denote the ideal generated by all possible
(r\times r)
-minors of
\partial
.
Two finite presentations of
M
can be related by a sequence of elementary operations, so this ideal does not depend on the choice of presentation. Since
R
is a UFD, we can define the greatest common divisor of an ideal and set

\Delta(M)\coloneqq\mathrm{gcd}(I(M)).

Then
\Delta(M)
is defined uniquely up to a multiplication with a unit of
R
.
When
\partial=0
,
\Delta(M)=1
by convention.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
Tur01
, Lemma 4.9]

With the assumptions and notations as above, we have

\Delta(M)=\Delta(M_{\mathrm{tor}}),

where
M_{\mathrm{tor}}
denote the torsion sub-module of
M
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
0\to M^{\prime}\to M\to M^{\prime\prime}\to 0
be a short exact sequence of finitely generated
R
-modules. Then we have

\Delta(M)\mid\Delta(M^{\prime})\cdot\Delta(M^{\prime\prime}).

Moreover, if
\mathrm{rank}M=\mathrm{rank}M^{\prime\prime}
(i.e.
\mathrm{rank}M^{\prime}=0
), we have

\Delta(M)=\Delta(M^{\prime})\cdot\Delta(M^{\prime\prime}).

Proof.

The first claim follows from Horseshoe Lemma
[
Wei94
, Horseshoe Lemma 2.2.8]
. In fact, for a finite presentation of
M^{\prime}
with matrix
\partial^{\prime}
and one for
M^{\prime\prime}
with matrix
\partial^{\prime\prime}
, Horseshoe Lemma gives a way to construct a finite presentation for
M
with matrix

\begin{pmatrix}\partial^{\prime}&*\\
0&\partial^{\prime\prime}\end{pmatrix}.

Since localization is an exact functor, we get
\mathrm{rank}M=\mathrm{rank}M^{\prime}+\mathrm{rank}M^{\prime\prime}
, which implies

\mathrm{rank}\begin{pmatrix}\partial^{\prime}&*\\
0&\partial^{\prime\prime}\end{pmatrix}=\mathrm{rank}\partial^{\prime}+\mathrm{rank}\partial^{\prime\prime}.

Then it is easy to see
\Delta(M)\mid\Delta(M^{\prime})\cdot\Delta(M^{\prime\prime}).

For the second claim, we have the following commutative diagram:

\textstyle{M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{M^{\prime\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{M\otimes_{R}Q\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{M^{\prime\prime}\otimes_{R}Q}

where
Q
is the fraction field of
R
. The additional assumption
\mathrm{rank}M=\mathrm{rank}M^{\prime\prime}
implies that the bottom horizontal map is an isomorphism. We claim that the induced map
M_{\mathrm{tor}}\to M^{\prime\prime}_{\mathrm{tor}}
is surjective. For any
x\in M^{\prime\prime}_{\mathrm{tor}}
, there exists
y\in M
maps to
x
. Note that
x=0
in
M^{\prime\prime}\otimes_{R}Q
, hence so is
y
, which implies
y\in M_{\mathrm{tor}}
. Note that taking torsion part is a left exact functor. Putting all together we get a short exact sequence of finitely generated
R
-modules:

0\to M^{\prime}_{\mathrm{tor}}\to M_{\mathrm{tor}}\to M^{\prime\prime}_{\mathrm{tor}}\to 0.

It implies
\Delta(M_{\mathrm{tor}})=\Delta(M^{\prime}_{\mathrm{tor}})\cdot\Delta(M^{\prime\prime}_{\mathrm{tor}})
, see e.g.
[
Lev67
, Lemma 5]
. Then the claim follows from Lemma
2.1
directly.
∎

2.2.
Alexander modules and Alexander polynomials

Recall that
X
is a connected finite CW-complex with an epimorphism

.
The group of covering transformations of

is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
and acts on it. By choosing lifts of the cells of

to

-coefficients) of

. So the cellular chain complex of

(4)

\cdots\to C_{i+1}(X^{\nu},R)\overset{\partial_{i}^{R}}{\to}C_{i}(X^{\nu},R)\overset{\partial_{i-1}^{R}}{\to}C_{i-1}(X^{\nu},R)\overset{\partial_{i-2}^{R}}{\to}\cdots\overset{\partial_{0}^{R}}{\to}C_{0}(X^{\nu},R)\to 0.

With the above free basis for

Definition 2.3
.

The

.

is called

-th Alexander polynomial of
(X,\nu)
with
R
-coefficients
, denoted by

.

Consider the case

.
Note that

is only defined up to multiplication by a unit in

. But there is a unique representative of the associate class with no negative powers of

non-zero constant term and positive coefficient for the leading term. We simply use the notation

to denote this representative.

Note that the homology Alexander modules and Alexander polynomials are homotopy invariants for the pair

.
In particular,

One can also define

Proposition 2.4
.

Suppose that the

in (

) has rank

. Then

Note that we have the following short exact sequence of

2.3.1.
L^{2}
-Betti number

As mentioned in Section
1.1
, for any field
\mathbb{K}
the following limit

\alpha_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\dim H_{i}(X^{\nu,N},\mathbb{K})}{N}

exists. Note that Betti number with
\mathbb{K}
-coefficients only depend on
\mathrm{char}(\mathbb{K})
, not on the specific choice of the field
\mathbb{K}
. So without loss of generality, we only need to consider the case where
\mathbb{K}
is algebraically closed.
In this subsection, we give a self-contained proof for the existence of the limit and relate these limits with Alexander modules.

Proposition 2.5
.

For any
i\geq 0
the limit
\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\dim H_{i}(X^{\nu,N},\mathbb{K})}{N}
exists. Moreover, we have

\alpha_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})=\mathrm{rank}H_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})=\dim H_{i}(X,L_{\rho})

where
\rho\in\mathbb{K}^{*}
is general and
L_{\rho}
is the corresponding rank one
\mathbb{K}
-local system on
X
pulling back by
\nu
.

For the proof of this proposition, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 2.6
.

Given a nonzero polynomial
h(t)=t^{d}+\cdots+a_{1}t+a_{0}\in\mathbb{K}[t]
, we denote by
J_{N}
the following
N\times N
(
N\geq d
) matrix

J_{N}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0&\cdots&0\\
0&0&1&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&1\\
1&0&0&\cdots&0\\
\end{pmatrix}.

Then there exists a positive integer
c
such that for
N
large enough we have

N-c\leq\mathrm{rank}h(J_{N})\leq N,

where
c
does not depend on
N
.

Proof.

Set
g(t)=\gcd(t^{N}-1,h(t))
. Then there exist
u_{1}(t),u_{2}(t)\in\mathbb{K}[t]
such that

g(t)=u_{1}(t)(t^{N}-1)+u_{2}(t)h(t).

Note that
J_{N}^{N}=\mathrm{Id}
, where
\mathrm{Id}
is the
(N\times N)
identity matrix.
It gives that

g(J_{N})=u_{1}(J_{N})(J_{N}^{N}-\mathrm{Id})+u_{2}(J_{N})h(J_{N})=u_{2}(J_{N})h(J_{N}),

hence
\mathrm{rank}g(J_{N})\leq\mathrm{rank}h(J_{N})
.
On the other hand, since
g(t)\mid h(t)
, we have
\mathrm{rank}g(J_{N})\geq\mathrm{rank}h(J_{N})
. Hence

\mathrm{rank}g(J_{N})=\mathrm{rank}h(J_{N}).

There exists a positive integer
c
, which does not depend on
N
, such that
g(t)\mid t^{c}-1.

In fact, since
\mathbb{K}
is algebraically closed, we can write down
h(t)=\prod_{j=1}^{d}(t-\alpha_{j}).
Collect all
\alpha_{j}
such that there exists some positive integer
n_{j}
with
\alpha_{j}^{n_{j}}=1
. When
\mathrm{char}(\mathbb{K})=0
,
c
can be taken as the product of such
n_{j}
; while when
\mathrm{char}(\mathbb{K})=p>0
,
c
can be taken as the product of such
n_{j}
with some power of
p
.

Note that for any field
\mathbb{K}
the dimension of
\mathrm{ker}(J^{c}_{N}-\mathrm{Id})
is
\gcd(c,N)
.
Hence for
N
large enough we have

\mathrm{rank}h(J_{N})=\mathrm{rank}g(J_{N})\geq\mathrm{rank}(J^{c}_{N}-\mathrm{Id})\geq N-c

The other part of the inequality is obvious.
∎

Proof of Proposition
2.5
.

Recall that
C_{*}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})
is the cellular chain complex of
X^{\nu}
with the field coefficient
\mathbb{K}
. Each
C_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})
is a finitely generated free
\mathbb{K}[t^{\pm 1}]
-module, since
X
is a finite CW complex.
Note that
\mathbb{K}[t^{\pm 1}]
is a PID. By choosing suitable basis we can assume that
\partial_{i}^{\mathbb{K}}
has the following form

\begin{pmatrix}h_{1}&&&&\\
&\ddots&&&\\
&&h_{m_{i}}&&\\
&&&0&\end{pmatrix}

with
m_{i}=\mathrm{rank}\partial_{i}^{\mathbb{K}}
. In particular,

\mathrm{rank}H_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})=\mathrm{rank}C_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})-m_{i}-m_{i-1}.

By tensoring with
\mathbb{K}[t^{\pm 1}]/(t^{N}-1)
, we get the cellular chain complex for
X^{\nu,N}
with
\mathbb{K}
-coefficients. We use
\partial_{i,N}^{\mathbb{K}}
to denote the corresponding differential map. Then

\dim H_{i}(X^{\nu,N},\mathbb{K})=\mathrm{rank}C_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})\cdot N-\mathrm{rank}\partial_{i,N}^{\mathbb{K}}-\mathrm{rank}\partial_{i-1,N}^{\mathbb{K}}.

Note that each polynomial
h_{j}(t)
(
1\leq j\leq m_{i})
) becomes a
N\times N
matrix
h_{j}(J_{N})
in
\partial_{i,N}^{\mathbb{K}}
. By Lemma
2.6
, there exist constant numbers
c_{i}
and
c_{i-1}
such that

\mathrm{rank}H_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})\leq\frac{\dim H_{i}(X^{\nu,N},\mathbb{K})}{N}\leq\mathrm{rank}H_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})+\frac{c_{i}+c_{i-1}}{N}.

Taking
N\rightarrow\infty
, we get

\alpha_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\dim H_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})}{N}=\mathrm{rank}H_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K}).

Let
\pi_{N}\colon X^{\nu,N}\rightarrow X
denote the covering map. Then

H_{i}(X^{\nu,N},\mathbb{K})=H_{i}(X,(\pi_{N})_{*}\mathbb{K}),

where
(\pi_{N})_{*}\mathbb{K}
is the push forward of the
\mathbb{K}
-constant sheaf on
X^{\nu,N}
, hence a rank
N

\mathbb{K}
-local system. Since we have already known that
\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\dim H_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})}{N}
exists, one can compute this limit with the subsequence

. In this case

is a direct sum of

\mathbb{K}^{*}\to\mathbb{Z}
by sending

to

is semi-continuous,

\mathbb{K}
-local systems with larger homology dimension than the general one. In particular, these finitely many local systems only depends on the pair
(X,\nu)
, not on
N
. Hence we have

\dim H_{i}(X,L_{\rho})\leq\frac{b_{i}(X^{\nu,N},\mathbb{K})}{N}\leq\dim H_{i}(X,L_{\rho})+\frac{c^{\prime}}{N},

for some constant number

and

being general.

, we are done.

2.3.2.
Limits of torsion

One can also study the following limit associated to the order of the torsion part of
H_{i}(X^{\nu,N},\mathbb{Z})

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log|H_{i}(X^{\nu,N},\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{tor}}|}{N}.

This limit can be computed by the Mahler measure of the Alexander polynomial
\Delta_{i}(X^{\nu})
.
Let us recall the definition of Mahler measure based on Jensen’s formula
[
Ahl66
, p. 208]
.

Definition 2.7
.

[
Mah60
,
Mah62
]

For a non-zero polynomial
h(t)\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
, assume that

h(t)=a_{d}t^{d}+a_{d-1}t^{d-1}+\cdots+a_{0}=a_{d}\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{d}(t-\alpha_{j})
with
a_{d}\neq 0
and
\alpha_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
.

One can define the
Mahler measure
of
h
to be

\mathbf{M}(h)=\log(|a_{d}|\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{d}\max\{1,|\alpha_{j}|\}).

We say that
h
is of
cyclotomic type
, if all its roots
\alpha_{j}
are roots of unity. In particular, in this case
\mathbf{M}(h)=\log|a_{d}|
.

Theorem 2.8
.

[
Le14
, Theorem 5]

With the above assumptions and notations, for any
i\geq 0
the limit
\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log|H_{i}(X^{\nu,N},\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{tor}}|}{N}
exists and we have that

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log|H_{i}(X^{\nu,N},\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{tor}}|}{N}=\mathbf{M}(\Delta_{i}(X^{\nu})).

Based on the above theorem, we set

\mathbf{M}_{i}(X^{\nu})\coloneqq\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log|H_{i}(X^{\nu,N},\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{tor}}|}{N}=\mathbf{M}(\Delta_{i}(X^{\nu})).

2.3.3.
Universal Coefficients Theorem

By the Universal Coefficients Theorem,

\dim H_{i}(X,\mathbb{C})\leq\dim H_{i}(X,\mathbb{K})

for any field coefficient
\mathbb{K}
. Moreover, if
\mathrm{char}(\mathbb{K})=p>0
and the inequality is strict, then either
H_{i}(X,\mathbb{Z})
or
H_{i-1}(X,\mathbb{Z})
has non-trivial
p
-torsion. On the other hand, if
H_{i}(X,\mathbb{Z})
has non-trivial
p
-torsion, then

\dim H_{i}(X,\mathbb{C})<\dim H_{i}(X,\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
and
\dim H_{i+1}(X,\mathbb{C})<\dim H_{i+1}(X,\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
X
be a connected finite CW-complex with a fixed group epimorphism
\nu\colon\pi_{1}(X)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}
. Then we have
\alpha_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{C})\leq\alpha_{i}(X^{\nu},\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
. Moreover,
if
\alpha_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{C})<\alpha_{i}(X^{\nu},\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
, then we have the following

(1)

For
N
large enough either
H_{i}(X^{\nu,N},\mathbb{Z})
or
H_{i-1}(X^{\nu,N},\mathbb{Z})
has non-trivial
p
-torsion.

(2)

\Delta_{i}(X^{\nu})=0
or
\Delta_{i-1}(X^{\nu})=0
in
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[t,t^{-1}]
, hence
\mathbf{M}_{i}(X^{\nu})>0
or
\mathbf{M}_{i-1}(X^{\nu})>0
, respectively.

Proof.

(1) Set
\epsilon=|\alpha_{i}(X,\mathbb{C})-\alpha_{i}(X,\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p})|/2
. For
N
large enough, we have

\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}H_{i}(X^{\nu,N},\mathbb{C})}{N}<\alpha_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{C})+\epsilon=\alpha_{i}(X^{\nu},\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p})-\epsilon<\frac{\dim_{\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}H_{i}(X^{\nu,N},\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p})}{N}.

Hence
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}H_{i}(X^{\nu,N},\mathbb{C})<\dim_{\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}H_{i}(X^{\nu,N},\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
and the conclusion follows.

(2)
\alpha_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{C})<\alpha_{i}(X^{\nu},\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
is equivalent to

\mathrm{rank}C_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{C})-\mathrm{rank}\partial_{i}^{\mathbb{C}}-\mathrm{rank}\partial_{i-1}^{\mathbb{C}}<\mathrm{rank}C_{i}(X^{\nu},\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p})-\mathrm{rank}\partial_{i}^{\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}-\mathrm{rank}\partial_{i-1}^{\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}},

which implies either
\mathrm{rank}\partial_{i}^{\mathbb{Z}}=\mathrm{rank}\partial_{i}^{\mathbb{C}}>\mathrm{rank}\partial_{i}^{\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}

or the same inequality for degree
i-1
.
Note that
\mathrm{rank}\partial_{i}^{\mathbb{Z}}>\mathrm{rank}\partial_{i}^{\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}
if and only if
p\mid\Delta_{i}(X^{\nu})
(i.e.,
\Delta_{i}(X^{\nu})=0
in
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[t,t^{-1}]
).
In particular,
\mathbf{M}_{i}(X^{\nu})\geq\log p
. Then the claim follows.
∎

Remark 2.10
.

If
\Delta_{i}(X^{\nu})
is of cyclotomic type and
\mathbf{M}_{i}(X^{\nu})\neq 0
, then there exists a prime number
p
such that
\Delta_{i}(X^{\nu})=0
in
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[t,t^{-1}]
, hence

\alpha_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{C})<\alpha_{i}(X^{\nu},\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
and
\alpha_{i+1}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{C})<\alpha_{i+1}(X^{\nu},\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
.

2.4.
Aomoto complex

Given an epimorphism
\nu\colon\pi_{1}(X)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
, we denote
\nu_{\mathbb{Z}}
the corresponding element in
H^{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})
. By obstruction theory, there is a map
g\colon X\rightarrow S^{1}
and a class
\omega\in H^{1}(S^{1},\mathbb{Z})
such that
\nu_{\mathbb{Z}}=g^{\ast}(\omega)
. Hence we have

\nu_{\mathbb{Z}}\cup\nu_{\mathbb{Z}}=f^{\ast}(\omega\cup\omega)=0.

For any field
\mathbb{K}
, there is a corresponding class
\nu_{\mathbb{K}}\in H^{1}(X,\mathbb{K})
and we have
\nu_{\mathbb{K}}\cup\nu_{\mathbb{K}}=0
.
Then one gets the following two Aomoto complexes by cup product

\textstyle{(H^{\ast}(X,\mathbb{Z}),\cdot\nu_{\mathbb{Z}})\colon}
\textstyle{H^{0}(X,\mathbb{Z})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\nu_{\mathbb{Z}}}
\textstyle{H^{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\nu_{\mathbb{Z}}}
\textstyle{H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\cdots}

and

\textstyle{(H^{\ast}(X,\mathbb{K}),\cdot\nu_{\mathbb{K}})\colon}
\textstyle{H^{0}(X,\mathbb{K})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\nu_{\mathbb{K}}}
\textstyle{H^{1}(X,\mathbb{K})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\nu_{\mathbb{K}}}
\textstyle{H^{2}(X,\mathbb{K})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\cdots}

Definition 2.11
.

With the above assumptions and notations, we define
the
i
-th Aomoto Betti number with
\mathbb{K}
-coefficients
as

\beta_{i}(X,\nu_{\mathbb{K}})\coloneqq\dim_{\mathbb{K}}H^{i}(H^{\ast}(X,\mathbb{K}),\cdot\nu_{\mathbb{K}}).

and
the
i
-th Aomoto torsion number
as

\tau_{i}(X,\nu_{\mathbb{Z}})\coloneqq|H^{i+1}(H^{*}(X,\mathbb{Z}),\cdot\nu_{\mathbb{Z}})_{\mathrm{tor}}|.

Here the shift by 1 is due to the Universal Coefficient Theorem.

The following nice theorem due to Papadima-Suciu
[
PS10
]
gives the relation between
\alpha_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})
and
\beta_{i}(X,\nu_{\mathbb{K}})
.
In fact, they constructed a spectral sequence converges to
H_{*}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})
(
[
PS10
, Proposition 8.1]
) with the first page being the Aomoto complex
[
PS10
, Corollary 8.3]
.

Theorem 2.12
.

Given the pair
(X,\nu)
and any field
\mathbb{K}
, we have that

\alpha_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})\leq\beta_{i}(X,\nu_{\mathbb{K}}).

Next we recall the following theorem due to Papadima and Suciu
[
PS10
, Theorem 12.6]
specialised to our case.

Theorem 2.13
.

Let
X
be a connected finite minimal CW-complex with a fixed epimorphism
\nu\colon\pi_{1}(X)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}
. Then the linearization of the equivariant cochain complex of the cover
X^{\nu}
, with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}
or a field
\mathbb{K}
, coincides with the Aomoto complex
(H^{\ast}(X,\mathbb{Z}),\cdot\nu_{\mathbb{Z}})
or
(H^{\ast}(X,\mathbb{K}),\cdot\nu_{\mathbb{K}})
, respectively.

As an application of this theorem, we have the following result.

Proposition 2.14
.

If
X
is a connected finite minimal CW complex with a fixed epimorphism
\nu\colon\pi_{1}(X)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}
such that
\alpha_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{C})=\beta_{i}(X,\nu_{\mathbb{C}})
, then we have

\Delta^{\prime}_{i}(X^{\nu})(1)\neq 0
and
\Delta^{\prime}_{i}(X^{\nu})(1)\mid\tau_{i}(X,\nu_{\mathbb{Z}}),

where
\Delta^{\prime}_{i}(X^{\nu})
is obtained from
\Delta_{i}(X^{\nu})
by taking out
t-1
factors. The same conclusion also holds at homological degree
i-1
.

Proof.

When
X
is a minimal CW complex, up to homotopy and under suitable basis we can assume
\partial_{i}^{\mathbb{Z}}(1)=0
and
write
\partial_{i}^{\mathbb{Z}}
in matrix form
(t-1)A_{i}
. Under the same basis, the above theorem shows that the map
H^{i}(X,\mathbb{Z})\overset{\nu_{\mathbb{Z}}}{\to}H^{i+1}(X,\mathbb{Z})
can be identified with
A_{i}(1)
(up to dual operation).
Note that

\alpha_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{C})=b_{i}(X)-\mathrm{rank}\partial_{i}^{\mathbb{Z}}-\mathrm{rank}\partial_{i-1}^{\mathbb{Z}}

and

\beta_{i}(X,\nu_{\mathbb{C}})=b_{i}(X)-\mathrm{rank}A_{i}(1)-\mathrm{rank}A_{i-1}(1).

Since
\mathrm{rank}\partial_{i}^{\mathbb{Z}}=\mathrm{rank}(t-1)A_{i}=\mathrm{rank}A_{i}
,
\alpha_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{C})=\beta_{i}(X,\nu_{\mathbb{C}})

implies

\mathrm{rank}A_{i}=\mathrm{rank}A_{i}(1)
and
\mathrm{rank}A_{i-1}=\mathrm{rank}A_{i-1}(1)
.

Let
r_{i}
denote the rank of
A_{i}
.
For a
(r_{i}\times r_{i})
-minor of
A_{i}
, say
g
, it may happen
g(1)=0
. Hence
\Delta(A_{i})
taking values at 1 divides the greatest common divisor of all possible
(r_{i}\times r_{i})
-minors of
A_{i}(1)
, which coincides with
\tau_{i}(X,\nu_{\mathbb{Z}}).
In particular,
\Delta(A_{i})
taking value at 1 is not 0. Then the conclusion follows by noticing that
\Delta(A_{i})=\Delta^{\prime}_{i}(X^{\nu})
. The claim for homological degree
i-1
follows by the same argument.
∎

Hyperplane arrangement complement satisfies the assumptions in the above proposition. For more details, see Section
5
.

3.
Cohomology jump loci and orbifold groups

3.1.
Cohomology jump loci

Let
X
be a connected finite CW-complex with
\pi_{1}(X)=G
and
\mathbb{K}
be an algebraically closed field, e.g.
\mathbb{C}
,
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. The group of
\mathbb{K}
-valued characters,
\mathrm{Hom}(G,\mathbb{K}^{*})
, is the moduli space of rank one
\mathbb{K}
-local system on
X
.
The cohomology jump loci
\mathcal{V}^{i}_{k}(X,\mathbb{K})
of
X
are defined as in Definition
1.2
.
Cohomology jump loci are closed sub-varieties of
\mathrm{Hom}(G,\mathbb{K}^{*})
and homotopy invariants of
X
.
For cohomological degree one,
\mathcal{V}^{1}_{k}(X,\mathbb{K})
depends only on
\pi_{1}(X)
(e.g. see
[
Suc11
, Section 2.2]
). So for any finitely presented group
G
,
\mathcal{V}^{1}_{k}(G,\mathbb{K})
is well defined.

Remark 3.1
.

One can also define the homology jump loci of
X
as follows

\mathcal{W}_{i}^{k}(X,\mathbb{K})\coloneqq\{\rho\in\mathrm{Hom}(G,\mathbb{K}^{*})\mid\dim_{\mathbb{K}}H_{i}(X,L_{\rho})\geq k\}.

Let
\rho^{-1}
denote the inverse of
\rho
in
\mathrm{Hom}(G,\mathbb{K}^{*})
. Then we have the following isomorphism between
\mathbb{K}
-vector spaces
[
Dim04
, p. 50]

H^{i}(X,L_{\rho^{-1}})\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{K}}(H_{i}(X,L_{\rho}),\mathbb{K}),

which gives

\mathcal{V}^{i}_{k}(X,\mathbb{K})=\{\rho\in\mathrm{Hom}(G,\mathbb{K}^{*})\mid\rho^{-1}\in\mathcal{W}_{i}^{k}(X,\mathbb{K})\}.

So
\mathcal{V}^{i}_{k}(X,\mathbb{K})
and
\mathcal{W}^{k}_{i}(X,\mathbb{K})
share the same information.

Remark 3.2
.

If
X
is a finite connected CW complex, then
\mathcal{V}_{1}^{0}(X,\mathbb{K})=\{\mathbb{K}_{X}\}
consists of just one point, the trivial rank-one local system, and
\mathcal{V}^{0}_{k}(X,\mathbb{K})=\emptyset
for
k>1
.

Chomology jump loci can be viewed as generalizations of
\alpha_{*}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})
. In fact, consider an epimorphism
\nu\colon\pi_{1}(X)\to\mathbb{Z}
. It induces an embedding

\nu^{*}\colon\mathbb{K}^{*}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(G,\mathbb{K}^{*}).

By Remark
3.1
and Proposition
2.5
, it is easy to see that
\alpha_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})=k
if and only if

\mathrm{im}\nu^{*}\subseteq\mathcal{V}^{i}_{k}(X,\mathbb{K})
and
\mathrm{im}\nu^{*}\nsubseteq\mathcal{V}^{i}_{k+1}(X,\mathbb{K})
.

The corresponding generalizations of
\beta_{*}(X,\nu_{\mathbb{K}})
are the resonance varieties. For its definition and properties, see e.g.
[
Suc11
]
.

The following structure theorem for cohomology jump loci of complex smooth quasi-projective varieties are contributed by many people and we name a few here: Green and Lazarsfeld
[
GL91
]
, Simpson
[
Sim93
]
, Arapura
[
Ara97
]
, Dimca and Papadima
[
DP14
]
, etc. It is finalized by Budur and Wang in
[
BW15
,
BW20
]
.

Theorem 3.3
.

[
BW15
,
BW20
]
If
X
is a complex smooth quasi-projective variety, then
\mathcal{V}^{i}_{k}(X,\mathbb{C})
is a finite union of torsion translated sub-tori of
\mathrm{Hom}(G,\mathbb{C}^{*})
.

The following question abut the cohomology jump loci for arbitrary field coefficients are still open
[
Suc01
, Section 3.5]
:

Question 3.4
.

Does the structure theorem hold for
\mathcal{V}^{i}_{k}(X,\mathbb{K})
of a complex smooth quasi-projective variety
X
with
\mathrm{char}(\mathbb{K})>0
?

Theorem
3.3
implies the following property for the Alexander polynomial associated to the pair
(X,\nu)
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
X
be a complex smooth quasi-projective variety. For any epimorphism
\nu\colon\pi_{1}(X)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}
,
\Delta_{i}(X,\nu)
is of cyclotomic type for any
i\geq 0
. Hence
\exp(\mathbf{M}_{i}(X^{\nu}))
is a positive integer.

Proof.

By
[
BLW18
, Proposition 1.4]
, the structure theorem for cohomology jump loci implies that all the roots of
\Delta_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{C})
are roots of unity. Since
\Delta_{i}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{C})
and
\Delta_{i}(X^{\nu})
only differ by multiplication with a non-zero constant integer, the claim follows.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

Note that
\exp(\mathbf{M}_{*}(X^{\nu}))
is not always an integer for general
X
. For example, the integral
Alexander polynomial of the complement
X
of the knot
4_{1}
in
S^{3}

[
SW02
, p. 981]
is given by

\Delta_{1}(X^{\nu})=t^{2}-3t+1,

hence
\exp(\mathbf{M}_{1}(X^{\nu}))=\frac{3+\sqrt{5}}{2}
.

3.2.
Orbifold Groups

In this subsection, we compute the degree 1 cohomology jump loci and
L^{2}
-type invariants for orbifold groups.

Let
\Sigma_{g,r}
be a Riemann surface of genus
g\geq 0
and with
r\geq 0
points removed. To have
b_{1}(X)>0
, we always assume
X\neq\mathbb{CP}^{1},\mathbb{C}
.
Consider
\Sigma_{g,r}
with
s
marked points
\{q_{1},\ldots,q_{s}\}
and a weight vector
{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}}=(\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{s})
, where
\mu_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, as in
[
Dim07
,
ACM13
,
Suc14A
]
. The orbifold group
\Gamma=\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(\Sigma_{g,r},{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}})
associated to these data is defined as

\Gamma=\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(\Sigma_{g,r},{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}})\coloneqq\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g,r}\backslash\{q_{1},\ldots,q_{s}\})/\langle\gamma_{j}^{\mu_{j}}=1\text{ for all }1\leq j\leq s\rangle,

where
\gamma_{j}
is a meridian of
q_{j}
.
If
\mu_{j}=1
for some
j
, we get the same orbifold group by omitting
q_{j}
and
\mu_{j}
. So without loss of generality, we assume
\mu_{j}>1
for all
1\leq j\leq s
.

3.2.1.
Non-compact case

If
\Sigma_{g,r}
is not compact (i.e.,
r>0
), then
\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g,r})
is a free group with rank
n=2g+r-1
, hence

\Gamma\cong F_{n}\ast\mathbb{Z}_{\mu_{1}}\cdots\ast\mathbb{Z}_{\mu_{s}}.

Then we have

\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,\mathbb{K}^{*})\coloneqq\{(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n},\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{s})\in(\mathbb{K}^{*})^{n+s}|\lambda_{j}^{\mu_{j}}=1\ \text{ for all }\ 1\leq j\leq s\}.

When
\mathrm{char}(\mathbb{K})=0
, we have the following short exact sequence as in
[
ACM13
, Proposition 2.7]
:

1\to(\mathbb{K}^{*})^{n}\to\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,\mathbb{K}^{*})\to\oplus_{j=1}^{s}C_{\mu_{j}}\to 1,

where
C_{\mu_{j}}
is the cyclic multiplicative subgroup of
\mathbb{K}^{*}
with order
\mu_{j}
. Hence
\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,\mathbb{K}^{*})
has
\prod_{j=1}^{s}\mu_{j}
connected components, with every connected component isomorphic to
(\mathbb{K}^{*})^{n}
.

On the other hand, when
\mathrm{char}(\mathbb{K})=p>0
, let
\mu^{\prime}_{j}
be the largest positive integer such that
\mu^{\prime}_{j}\mid\mu_{j}
(
\mu^{\prime}_{j}
could be 1) and
\gcd(p,\mu^{\prime}_{j})=1
.
Then we have the following short exact sequence:

1\to(\mathbb{K}^{*})^{n}\to\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,\mathbb{K}^{*})\to\oplus_{j=1}^{s}C_{\mu^{\prime}_{j}}\to 1,

where
C_{\mu^{\prime}_{j}}
is the cyclic multiplicative subgroup of
\mathbb{K}^{*}
with order
\mu^{\prime}_{j}
. Hence
\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,\mathbb{K}^{*})
has
\prod_{j=1}^{s}\mu^{\prime}_{j}
connected components, with every connected component isomorphic to
(\mathbb{K}^{*})^{n}
. In particular, if
\mu_{j}
is a power of
p
for all
1\leq j\leq s
(i.e.,
\prod_{j=1}^{s}\mu^{\prime}_{j}=1
),
\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,\mathbb{K}^{*})\cong(\mathbb{K}^{*})^{n}.

In both cases, for any
\rho=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n},\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{s})\in\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,\mathbb{K}^{*})
, we set
\ell_{\mathbb{K}}(\rho,{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}})
as the number of trivial coordinates
\lambda_{j}=1
such that
\mathrm{char}(\mathbb{K})=p\nmid\mu_{j}
. When
\rho
is the trivial character, we simply write
\ell_{\mathbb{K}}({\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}}).

Proposition 3.7
.

With the above notations, we have

\dim H^{1}(\Gamma,L_{\rho})=\begin{cases}n+s-\ell_{\mathbb{K}}(\rho,{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}})-1,&\mathrm{if}\ L_{\rho}\neq\mathbb{K}_{\Gamma},\\
n+s-\ell_{\mathbb{K}}({\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}}),&\mathrm{if}\ L_{\rho}=\mathbb{K}_{\Gamma}.\end{cases}

Here
\mathbb{K}_{\Gamma}
denotes the trivial character on
\Gamma
. Let
\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,\mathbb{K}^{*})^{0}
denote the connected component of
\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,\mathbb{K}^{*})
, which contains
\mathbb{K}_{\Gamma}
. Then we have

\mathcal{V}^{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{K})=\begin{cases}\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,\mathbb{K}^{*}),&\text{if }n>1,\text{ or }n=1\text{ and }p\\
&\text{divides some }\mu_{j},\\[5.0pt]
(\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,\mathbb{K}^{*})\setminus\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,\mathbb{K}^{*})^{0})\cup\{\mathbb{K}_{\Gamma}\},&\text{if }n=1,\prod_{j=1}^{s}\mu^{\prime}_{j}>1,\\
&\text{and }p\text{ does not divide any }\mu_{j},\\[5.0pt]
\{\mathbb{K}_{\Gamma}\},&\text{if }n=1,\prod_{j=1}^{s}\mu^{\prime}_{j}=1,\\
&\text{and }p\text{ does not divide any }\mu_{j}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Consider the Eilenberg–MacLane space
K(\pi_{1},1)
of
\Gamma
, say
Y
. The homology group
H_{1}(\Gamma,L_{\rho})
can be computed by the chain complex
C_{*}(Y,L_{\rho})
, see
[
Dim04
, p. 50]
. By Fox calculus
\partial_{1}^{\rho}
has the form of a
(n+s)\times s
matrix as follows

(5)

\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\lambda_{1}^{\mu_{1}}-1}{\lambda_{1}-1}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&\frac{\lambda_{2}^{\mu_{2}}-1}{\lambda_{2}-1}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&\frac{\lambda_{s}^{\mu_{s}}-1}{\lambda_{s}-1}\\
0&0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&0\\
\end{pmatrix}.

Note that for any
1\leq j\leq s
,
\frac{\lambda_{j}^{\mu_{j}}-1}{\lambda_{j}-1}\neq 0
in
\mathbb{K}
if and only if
\lambda_{j}=1
and
p\nmid\mu_{j}
.
Then we see that the above matrix has rank
\ell_{\mathbb{K}}(\rho,{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}})
. If
L_{\rho}
is not the constant sheaf, then the rank of
\partial_{1}^{\rho}
is 1, hence

\dim H_{1}(Y,L_{\rho})=n+s-\ell_{\mathbb{K}}(\rho,{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}})-1.

On the other hand, if
L_{\rho}
is the constant sheaf, then the rank of
\partial_{1}^{\rho}
is 0, hence

\dim H_{1}(Y,\mathbb{K})=n+s-\ell_{\mathbb{K}}({\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}}).

Then the claim follows from Remark
3.1
.
∎

3.2.2.
Compact case

If
\Sigma_{g,r}
is compact (i.e.,
r=0
), then

\Gamma\cong\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{g},y_{1},\ldots,y_{g},\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{s}|\prod_{i=1}^{g}[x_{i},y_{i}]\prod_{j=1}^{s}\gamma_{j}=1,\gamma_{j}^{\mu_{j}}=1\text{ for all }1\leq j\leq s\rangle,

hence

\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,\mathbb{K}^{*})=\{(t_{1},\ldots,t_{2g},\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{s})\in(\mathbb{K}^{*})^{2g+s}|\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{s}=1,\lambda_{j}^{\mu_{j}}=1\ \mathrm{for}\ 1\leq j\leq s\}.

As in the non-compact case, when
\mathrm{char}(\mathbb{K})=p>0
, we set
\mu^{\prime}_{j}
to be the largest positive integer such that
\mu^{\prime}_{j}\mid\mu_{j}
and
\gcd(p,\mu^{\prime}_{j})=1
. To unify the notations, we set
\mu^{\prime}_{j}=\mu_{j}
when
\mathrm{char}(\mathbb{K})=0
.
Then in both cases, we have the following short exact sequence:

1\to(\mathbb{K}^{*})^{2g}\to\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,\mathbb{K}^{*})\to\big(\oplus_{j=1}^{s}C_{\mu^{\prime}_{j}}\big)/C_{\mu^{\prime}}\to 1,

where
\mu^{\prime}\coloneqq\mathrm{lcm}(\mu^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\mu^{\prime}_{s})
and the last term is the cokernel of the natural mapping
C_{\mu^{\prime}}\to\oplus_{j=1}^{s}C_{\mu^{\prime}_{j}}
. Hence
\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,\mathbb{K}^{*})
has
\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{s}\mu^{\prime}_{j}}{\mu^{\prime}}
connected components, with every connected component being isomorphic to
(\mathbb{K}^{*})^{2g}
.

For any
\rho=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{2g},\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{s})\in\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,\mathbb{K}^{*})
, we set
\ell_{\mathbb{K}}(\rho,{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}})
as the number of trivial coordinates
\lambda_{j}=1
such that
\mathrm{char}(\mathbb{K})=p\nmid\mu_{j}
. When
\rho
is the trivial character, we simply write
\ell_{\mathbb{K}}({\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}}).

Proposition 3.8
.

With the notations above, we have

\dim H^{1}(\Gamma,L_{\rho})=\begin{cases}2g+s-2-\ell_{\mathbb{K}}(\rho,{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}}),&\mathrm{if}\ L_{\rho}\neq\mathbb{K}_{\Gamma},\\
2g+s-1-\ell_{\mathbb{K}}({\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}}),&\mathrm{if}\ L_{\rho}=\mathbb{K}_{\Gamma},\ell_{\mathbb{K}}({\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}})<s\\
2g,&\mathrm{if}\ L_{\rho}=\mathbb{K}_{\Gamma},\ell_{\mathbb{K}}({\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}})=s.\end{cases}

Let
\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,\mathbb{K}^{*})^{0}
denote the connected component of
\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,\mathbb{K}^{*})
, which contains the constant sheaf
\mathbb{K}_{\Gamma}
. Then we have

\mathcal{V}^{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{K})=\begin{cases}\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,\mathbb{K}^{*}),&\text{if }g>1,\text{ or }g=1\text{ and }p\\
&\text{divides some }\mu_{j},\\[5.0pt]
(\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,\mathbb{K}^{*})\setminus\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,\mathbb{K}^{*})^{0})\cup\{\mathbb{K}_{\Gamma}\},&\text{if }g=1,\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{s}\mu^{\prime}_{j}}{\mu^{\prime}}>1,\\
&\text{and }p\text{ does not divide any }\mu_{j},\\[5.0pt]
\{\mathbb{K}_{\Gamma}\},&\text{if }g=1,\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{s}\mu^{\prime}_{j}}{\mu^{\prime}}=1,\\
&\text{and }p\text{ does not divide any }\mu_{j}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Consider the Eilenberg–MacLane space
K(\pi_{1},1)
of
\Gamma
, say
Y
. By Fox calculus
\partial_{1}^{\rho}
has the form of a
(2g+s)\times(s+1)
matrix as follows

(6)

\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\lambda_{1}^{\mu_{1}}-1}{\lambda_{1}-1}&0&\cdots&0&1\\
0&\frac{\lambda_{2}^{\mu_{2}}-1}{\lambda_{2}-1}&\cdots&0&\lambda_{1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&\frac{\lambda_{s}^{\mu_{s}}-1}{\lambda_{s}-1}&\prod_{j=1}^{s-1}\lambda_{j}\\
0&0&\cdots&0&y_{1}-1\\
0&0&\cdots&0&1-x_{1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&0&y_{g}-1\\
0&0&\cdots&0&1-x_{g}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

If
L_{\rho}
is not the constant sheaf, this matrix has rank
\ell_{\mathbb{K}}(\rho,{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}})+1
and
\partial_{0}^{\rho}
has rank 1, hence

\dim H_{1}(Y,L_{\rho})=2g+s-2-\ell_{\mathbb{K}}(\rho,{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}}).

While for the constant sheaf case, if
\ell_{\mathbb{K}}({\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}})=s
, the above matrix has rank
s
; and if
\ell_{\mathbb{K}}({\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}})<s
(i.e., there exists some
\mu_{j}
such that
\mathrm{char}(\mathbb{K})=p\mid\mu_{j}
), the above matrix has rank
\ell_{\mathbb{K}}({\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}})+1
. Then we have

\dim H_{1}(Y,\mathbb{K})=\begin{cases}2g+s-\ell_{\mathbb{K}}({\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}})-1,&\mathrm{if}\ \ell_{\mathbb{K}}({\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}})<s\\
2g,&\mathrm{if}\ \ell_{\mathbb{K}}({\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}})=s.\end{cases}

Then the claim follows from Remark
3.1
.
∎

.

\mathrm{char}(\mathbb{K})=0

and Proposition

. Proposition

4.
Proof of the main results

The following well known result will play a crucial role in the proofs of Theorem
1.6
and Theorem

, see e.g.

Theorem 4.1
.

Consider an orbifold map
f\colon X\to\Sigma
of type
(g,r,{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}})
.
Let
F
denote the generic fiber of
f
. Then we have a short exact sequence of groups

\pi_{1}(F)\to\pi_{1}(X)\overset{f_{*}}{\to}\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(\Sigma_{g,r},{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}})\to 1,

where the first map is induced by the inclusion from
F
to

.
In particular the kernel of

is finitely generated.

4.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.6

By Remark
3.1
, we only need to prove the theorem for the homology version. By Theorem
4.1
, the orbifold map
f
gives the following group extension

1\rightarrow K\rightarrow G\overset{f_{*}}{\to}\Gamma\rightarrow 1,

where
G=\pi_{1}(X)
,
\Gamma=\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(\Sigma_{g,r},{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}})
and the kernel
K
of
f_{*}
is a finitely generated normal subgroup of
G
.

Given a character
\rho\in\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,\mathbb{K}^{*})
, it gives a rank one
\mathbb{K}
-local system
L_{\rho}
of
\Gamma
. Let
f^{*}L_{\rho}
denote the rank one local system pulling back to
G
.
Then we have the Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence:

E^{2}_{s,t}=H_{s}(\Gamma,H_{t}(K,f^{*}L_{\rho}))\Rightarrow H_{s+t}(G,f^{*}L_{\rho}).

Next we explain how to review
H_{t}(K,f^{*}L_{\rho})
as a
\Gamma
-module.
Let
P_{\bullet}
be a free
G
-resolution of
\mathbb{Z}
, then the
\Gamma
action on
H_{*}(K,f^{*}L_{\rho})=H_{*}(P_{\bullet}\otimes_{K}f^{*}L_{\rho})
is induced by the tensor product of the
G
-action on
f^{*}L_{\rho}
and
G
-action on
P_{\bullet}
. Since
K
is the kernel of
f_{*}
,
f^{*}L_{\rho}
taking restriction over
K
is the constant sheaf. Then we have

H_{*}(K,f^{*}L_{\rho})=H_{*}(P_{\bullet}\otimes_{K}f^{*}L_{\rho})\cong H_{*}(K,\mathbb{Z})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{K}

as
\Gamma
-modules.
The
\Gamma
-action on
H_{*}(K,\mathbb{Z})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{K}
is the tensor product of the
\Gamma
-conjugation on
H_{*}(K,\mathbb{Z})
and the
\Gamma
-action on
\mathbb{K}
induced by
\rho
. To emphasize the second action, we denote the
\Gamma
-module
H_{*}(K,\mathbb{Z})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{K}
by
H_{1}(K,\mathbb{Z})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}L_{\rho}
.
In particular, we have
E^{2}_{1,0}=H_{1}(\Gamma,L_{\rho})
and
E^{2}_{0,1}=H_{0}(\Gamma,H_{1}(K,\mathbb{Z})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}L_{\rho})
. Then the spectral sequence gives us the following short exact sequence

0\rightarrow E^{2}_{0,1}/\mathrm{im}d_{2}\rightarrow H_{1}(G,f^{*}L_{\rho})\rightarrow H_{1}(\Gamma,L_{\rho})\rightarrow 0,

where
d_{2}\colon E^{2}_{2,0}\to E^{2}_{0,1}
is the differential map on
E^{2}
-page.
Hence

\mathrm{dim}H_{1}(G,f^{*}L_{\rho})\geq\mathrm{dim}H_{1}(\Gamma,L_{\rho})

and the equality holds if
E^{2}_{0,1}=0
.

Since
K
is a finitely generated group,
H_{1}(K,\mathbb{Z})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}L_{\rho}
is a finite dimensional
\mathbb{K}
-vector space, say denoted by
W
. For any
\gamma\in\Gamma
, let
c(\gamma)
denote the corresponding linear transformation on
W
induced by the
\Gamma
-conjugation on
H_{1}(K,\mathbb{Z})
. Note that the linear transformation
c(\gamma)
is independent with the character
\rho
. Therefore

E^{2}_{0,1}=H_{0}(\Gamma,H_{1}(K,\mathbb{Z})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}L_{\rho})\cong W/\langle\big(c(\gamma)\otimes\rho(\gamma)-\mathrm{Id}\big)(w)|w\in W,\gamma\in\Gamma\rangle.

Recall the moduli space
\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,\mathbb{K}^{*})
as in section
3.2
. In the non-compact case, for any
\rho=(t_{1},\cdots,t_{n},\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{s})\in\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,\mathbb{K}^{*})
,
E^{2}_{0,1}=0
if we choose
t_{i}
not to be the inverse of the eigenvalues of
c(x_{i})
for some
1\leq i\leq n
. Here
x_{i}
is the
i
-th generator of the group
\Gamma=F_{n}\ast\mathbb{Z}_{\mu_{1}}\cdots\ast\mathbb{Z}_{\mu_{s}}
. Then the claim follows since
c(x_{i})
has only finitely many eigenvalues and there are only finitely many choices for
\lambda_{j}
.
The compact case follows by a similar proof.

4.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.10

The formula for
\alpha_{1}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{K})
follows from Theorem
1.6
and the computations in subsection
3.2
directly.
The rest part is devoted to the proof for
\mathbf{M}_{1}(X^{\nu})
.

By Definition
1.8
, one indeed gets the following sequence of epimorphisms:

G\overset{f_{*}}{\to}\Gamma\overset{\nu^{\prime}}{\twoheadrightarrow}\mathbb{Z}.

The map
\nu^{\prime}\colon\Gamma\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}
gives a rank one local system
\mathscr{L}
of
\Gamma
with stalk
\mathbb{Z}[t^{\pm}]
. Let
f^{*}\mathscr{L}
denote the rank one local system pulling back to
G
.
In particular,

H_{1}(\Gamma,\mathscr{L})\cong H_{1}(\Gamma^{\nu^{\prime}},\mathbb{Z})
and
H_{1}(G,f^{*}\mathscr{L})\cong H_{1}(X^{\nu},\mathbb{Z})
.

Applying the Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence, we get:

E^{2}_{s,t}=H_{s}(\Gamma,H_{t}(K,f^{*}\mathscr{L}))\Rightarrow H_{s+t}(G,f^{*}\mathscr{L}),

where
K
is the kernel of
f_{*}
.
Similarly to the proof of Theorem
1.6
, we have
E^{2}_{1,0}=H_{1}(\Gamma,\mathscr{L})
,
E^{2}_{0,1}=H_{0}(\Gamma,H_{1}(K,f^{*}\mathscr{L}))
and
a short exact sequence of finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}[t^{\pm}]
-modules

0\rightarrow E^{2}_{0,1}/\mathrm{im}d_{2}\rightarrow H_{1}(G,f^{*}\mathscr{L})\rightarrow H_{1}(\Gamma,\mathscr{L})\rightarrow 0.

Note that
H_{0}(\Gamma,H_{1}(K,f^{*}\mathscr{L}))
is isomorphic to

H_{1}(K,\mathbb{Z})\otimes\mathbb{Z}[t^{\pm}]/\langle\big(c(\gamma)\otimes\nu^{\prime}(\gamma)-\mathrm{Id}\big)(x\otimes t^{i})|x\otimes t^{i}\in H_{1}(K,\mathbb{Z})\otimes\mathbb{Z}[t^{\pm}],\gamma\in\Gamma\rangle

as
\mathbb{Z}[t^{\pm}]
-modules,
where
c(\gamma)
is the automorphism on
H_{1}(K,\mathbb{Z})
induced by the conjugation of
\gamma
on
K
. Since
\nu^{\prime}\colon\Gamma\to\mathbb{Z}
is surjective,
H_{0}(\Gamma,H_{1}(K,f^{*}\mathscr{L}))
is a quotient abelian group of
H_{1}(K,\mathbb{Z})
, hence
E^{2}_{0,1}
is a finitely generated abelian group. In fact, for any
x\in H_{1}(K,\mathbb{Z})
and
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, there exists some
\gamma\in\Gamma
such that

\nu^{\prime}(\gamma)=i
, hence
x\otimes t^{i}
is equivalent to
c(\gamma)^{-1}(x)\otimes 1
in
H_{0}(\Gamma,H_{1}(K,f^{*}\mathscr{L}))
.

E^{2}_{0,1}
being a finitely generated abelian group implies that the rank of
E^{2}_{0,1}
as a
\mathbb{Z}[t^{\pm}]
-module is 0, hence so is
E^{2}_{0,1}/\mathrm{im}d_{2}
. By Lemma
2.2
we have

\Delta_{1}(G^{\nu})=\Delta_{1}(\Gamma^{\nu})\cdot\Delta(E^{2}_{0,1}/\mathrm{im}d_{2}).

By Proposition
3.5
,
\Delta_{1}(G^{\nu})
is of cyclotomic type, hence so is
\Delta(E^{2}_{0,1}/\mathrm{im}d_{2})
and

\Delta_{1}(\Gamma^{\nu})
.
Then
\mathbf{M}(\Delta(E^{2}_{0,1}/\mathrm{im}d_{2}))
only depends on the leading coefficient of
\Delta(E^{2}_{0,1}/\mathrm{im}d_{2})
.
This leading coefficient is indeed 1.
Otherwise, there exists a prime number
p
which divides the leading coefficient. Since
\Delta(E^{2}_{0,1}/\mathrm{im}d_{2})
is cyclotomic type,
p
divides
\Delta(E^{2}_{0,1}/\mathrm{im}d_{2})
. By the definition of Alexander polynomial, this implies that rank of

as a

-module is at least one. In particular,

is not a finitely generated abelian group. This contradicts with the fact that
E^{2}_{0,1}
is a finite generated abelian group.

Putting all together, we have that

\mathbf{M}_{1}(X^{\nu})=\mathbf{M}_{1}(\Gamma^{\nu})=\sum_{j=1}^{s}\log\mu_{j},

where the last equality follows from Proposition
3.10
.

\scriptstyle{f_{1}}
\scriptstyle{f_{2}}

For an orbifold map

, but the proof works as long as

Remark 4.3
.

Assume that

is orbifold effective. Note that for the last claim in Definition
1.8
it is possible that
\nu
factors through two different orbifold maps.

If the associated orbifold map

in Definition

\nu
and

On the other hand, when

Remark 4.4
.

We show that under certain Hodge structure assumptions

is always orbifold effective.

(1)

Example 4.5
.

In general
\nu
is not necessarily always orbifold effective. Consider

with

being an Elliptic curve. Then we have

.

5.

In this section, we specialize to the case of hyperplane arrangements.
An arrangement of hyperplanes is a finite collection of hyperplanes in a complex affine or projective space.
Let

\alpha_{1}(M(\mathcal{A})^{\nu},\mathbb{K})=\begin{cases}0,&\mathrm{if}\ \sum_{\ell=1}^{d}n_{\ell}\neq 0,\\
d-2,&\mathrm{if}\ \sum_{\ell=1}^{d}n_{\ell}=0,\end{cases}

hence
\mathbf{M}_{1}(M(\mathcal{A})^{\nu})=0
.

We have a basis
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}\}
for
H^{1}(M(\mathcal{A}),\mathbb{Z})
, where
a_{\ell}
corresponds to the
\ell
-th hyperplane for
1\leq\ell\leq d
, hence
\nu_{\mathbb{Z}}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}n_{\ell}a_{\ell}
.
H^{2}(M(\mathcal{A}),\mathbb{Z})
is generated by
\{a_{ij}\coloneqq a_{i}\cup a_{j}\}_{1\leq i<j\leq d}
with the relation
a_{ij}=a_{1j}-a_{1i}
. Hence
H^{2}(M(\mathcal{A}),\mathbb{Z})
has a basis
\{a_{1\ell}\}_{2\leq\ell\leq d}
. Let us write down the matrix of the map
H^{1}(M(\mathcal{A}),Z)\overset{\nu_{\mathbb{Z}}}{\to}H^{2}(M(\mathcal{A}),\mathbb{Z})
under these basis:

\begin{pmatrix}n_{2}&n_{3}&n_{4}&\cdots&n_{d}\\
-\sum_{i\neq 2}n_{i}&n_{3}&n_{4}&\cdots&n_{d}\\
n_{2}&-\sum_{i\neq 3}n_{i}&n_{4}&\cdots&n_{d}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
n_{2}&n_{3}&n_{4}&\cdots&-\sum_{i\neq d}n_{i}\end{pmatrix}

When
\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}n_{\ell}\neq 0
, this matrix has rank
d-1
and
\tau_{1}(M(\mathcal{A}),\nu_{\mathbb{Z}})=(\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}n_{\ell})^{d-2}
(since
\gcd\{n_{\ell}\colon 1\leq\ell\leq d\}=1
). On the other hand, when
\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}n_{\ell}=0
, it has rank
1
and the matrix can be changed to
\begin{pmatrix}1&0&\ldots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&0\\
1&0&\ldots&0\end{pmatrix}
by elementary column operation since
\gcd\{n_{\ell}\colon 1\leq\ell\leq d\}=1
, hence
\tau_{1}(M(\mathcal{A}),\nu_{\mathbb{Z}})=1
. Then we have

\beta_{1}(M(\mathcal{A}),\nu_{\mathbb{K}})=\begin{cases}d-2,&\mathrm{if}\ \sum_{\ell=1}^{d}n_{\ell}=0,\\
d-2,&\mathrm{if}\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}n_{\ell}\neq 0\text{ and }\mathrm{char}(\mathbb{K})=p\mid\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}n_{\ell},\\
0,&\mathrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Hence if
\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}n_{\ell}\neq 0
and
p
divides
\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}n_{\ell}
, we have
\alpha_{1}(M(\mathcal{A})^{\nu},\mathbb{C})<\beta_{1}(M(\mathcal{A}),\nu_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}})
and

\exp(\mathbf{M}_{1}(M(\mathcal{A})^{\nu}))=1<(\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}n_{\ell})^{d-2}=\tau_{1}(M(\mathcal{A}),\nu_{\mathbb{Z}}).

Under suitable basis
\partial_{1}^{\mathbb{Z}}
can be written as form of matrix of size
d\times(d-1)

\begin{pmatrix}1-t^{n_{1}}&1-t^{n_{2}}&\ldots&1-t^{n_{d-1}}\\
t^{\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}n_{\ell}}-1&0&\ldots&0\\
0&t^{\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}n_{\ell}}-1&\ldots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&t^{\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}n_{\ell}}-1\end{pmatrix}

One can get

\Delta_{1}(M(\mathcal{A})^{\nu})=\begin{cases}(t-1)(t^{\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}n_{\ell}}-1)^{d-2},&\mathrm{if}\ \sum_{\ell=1}^{d}n_{\ell}\neq 0,\\
t-1,&\mathrm{if}\ \sum_{\ell=1}^{d}n_{\ell}=0.\end{cases}

In particular, if
\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}n_{\ell}\neq 0
,
(1+t+\ldots+t^{(\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}n_{\ell})-1})^{d-2}|_{t=1}=(\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}n_{\ell})^{d-2}
, which coincides with
\tau_{1}(M(\mathcal{A}),\nu_{\mathbb{Z}})
.

5.2.
Multinet

We first recall the definition of multinet.

Definition 5.5
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an essential hyperplane arrangement in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. A multinet on
\mathcal{A}
is a partition of
\mathcal{A}
into
k\geq 3
subsets
\mathcal{A}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{A}_{k}
, together with an assignment of multiplicities,
n\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, and a subset
\mathfrak{X}
of intersection points in
\mathcal{A}
satisfying the following conditions:

(a)

\sum_{H\in\mathcal{A}_{i}}n_{H}=\kappa
, independent of
i
;

(b)

for each
H\in\mathcal{A}_{i}
and
H^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{j}
with
i\neq j
,
H\cap H^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{X}
;

(c)

for each
x\in\mathfrak{X}
, the sum
\sum_{H\in\mathcal{A}_{i},x\in H}n_{H}
is independent of
i
;

(d)

for every
1\leq i\leq k
and
H,H^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{i}
, there is a sequence
H=H_{0},H_{1},\cdots,H_{s}=H^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{A}_{i}
such that
H_{j-1}\cap H_{j}\notin\mathfrak{X}
for
1\leq j\leq s
.

(e)

\gcd(n_{H}\colon H\in\mathcal{A})
=1.

Such multinet is called a
(k,\kappa)
-multinet.

By results of Pereira-Yuzvinsky
[
PY08
]
and Yuzvinsky
[
Yuz09
]
,
k=3
or
4
if
|\mathfrak{X}|>1
. It was conjectured by Yuzvinksy that
k=4
only happens for Hessian arrangement.
As shown by Falk, Pereira and Yuzvinsky
[
FY07
,
PY08
]
, every
(k,\kappa)
-multinet determines a hyperbolic orbifold map
f\colon X(\mathcal{A})\to\Sigma_{0,k}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}
by

f(x)\coloneqq[\prod_{H\in\mathcal{A}_{1}}f_{H}^{n_{H}}(x),\prod_{H\in\mathcal{A}_{2}}f_{H}^{n_{H}}(x)].

Moreover, the positive dimension components of
\mathcal{V}^{1}(X,\mathbb{C})
passing through the origin are combinatorially determined
as follows, see e.g.
[
Suc14A
, Section 3]
.

Theorem 5.6
.

[
FY07
]

\mathcal{A}

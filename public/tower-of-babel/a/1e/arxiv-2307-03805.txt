A geometric computation of cohomotopy groups in co-degree one
Michael Jung, Thomas O. Rot

Abstract
. Using geometric arguments, we compute the group of homotopy classes of maps from a closed ( n + 1 ) (n+1) -dimensional manifold to the n n -sphere for n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 . Our work extends results from Kirby, Melvin and Teichner for closed oriented 4-manifolds and from Konstantis for closed ( n + 1 ) (n+1) -dimensional spin manifolds, considering possibly non-orientable and non-spinnable manifolds. In the process, we introduce two types of manifolds that generalize the notion of odd and even 4-manifolds. Furthermore, for the case that n ≥ 4 n\geq 4 , we discuss applications for rank n n spin vector bundles and obtain a refinement of the Euler class in the cohomotopy group that fully obstructs the existence of a non-vanishing section.

2.
Preliminaries

In what follows we assume that
X
is a closed, connected
(n+1)
-dimensional smooth manifold, where
n\geq 3
.
If
\iota\colon M\looparrowright X
is an immersion, we denote by
\nu_{\iota}
the associated normal bundle.
Provided that
\iota\colon M\hookrightarrow X
is an embedding, and if the context allows, we may suppress the map
\iota
and denote the normal bundle simply by
\nu_{M}
.
A trivialization of a vector bundle up to a homotopy through trivializations is referred to as a
framing
.

Pin-structures

A
\mathrm{Pin}
-structure generalizes the notion of a
\mathrm{Spin}
-structure to non-orientable vector bundles.
While
\mathrm{SO}(r)
is double covered by
\mathrm{Spin}(r)
, the group
\mathrm{O}(r)
has two possible central
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
extensions, denoted by
\mathrm{Pin}^{\pm}(r)
.
Suppose
E\to X
is a vector bundle over
X
of rank
r
.
A
\mathrm{Pin}^{\pm}
-principal bundle over
X
that lifts the frame bundle of
E
is called a
\mathrm{Pin}^{\pm}
-structure over
E
.
In our article, we focus entirely on
\mathrm{Pin}^{-}
-structures.
The obstruction to putting a
\mathrm{Pin}^{-}
-structure on
E
is given by
w_{1}^{2}(E)+w_{2}(E)
.
If
E
admits a
\mathrm{Pin}^{-}
-structure, the set of all equivalent
\mathrm{Pin}^{-}
-structures is a
H^{1}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
-torsor.
In other words, the group
H^{1}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
acts simply transitive on the set of equivalent
\mathrm{Pin}^{-}
-structures.
We denote the (stable) bordism group of
r
-dimensional manifolds with an
\mathrm{Pin}^{-}
-structure on the
tangent bundle
by
\Omega^{\mathrm{Pin}^{-}}_{r}
.
An in-depth analysis of both
\mathrm{Pin}^{-}
and
\mathrm{Pin}^{+}
-structures and their low-dimensional bordism groups is done by Kirby and Taylor
[
8
]
.

Homology with twisted coefficients

Since we are interested in non-orientable manifolds as well, we need to consider
homology with local coefficients
.
An exposition of their definition and properties, also for cohomology, can be found in Spanier
[
12
]
as well as Davis and Kirk
[
3
]
.
Recall that the
orientation sheaf
is defined as the local coefficient system

\displaystyle o_{X}=\bigsqcup_{x\in X}H_{n+1}(X,X-\{x\};\mathbb{Z}),

sometimes referred to as
twisted coefficients
.
Our object of interest is the associated homology
H_{*}(X;o_{X})
.

We are particularly interested in the following functorial property.
If
\iota\colon M\looparrowright X
is an immersion of an
(k+1)
-dimensional manifold, we get an isomorphism of orientation bundles

\iota^{*}\left(\bigwedge\nolimits^{n+1}TX\right)\cong\bigwedge\nolimits^{k+1}TM\otimes\bigwedge\nolimits^{n-k}\nu_{\iota}.

An orientation of
\nu_{\iota}
induces an isomorphism
\bigwedge\nolimits^{k+1}TM\cong\iota^{*}(\bigwedge\nolimits^{n+1}TX)
, and hence an isomorphism of local coefficients
o_{M}\cong\iota^{*}o_{X}
.
This then defines a pushforward
\iota_{*}\colon H_{*}(M;o_{M})\to H_{*}(X;o_{X})
.

Next, we discuss a universal coefficient theorem for local coefficients.
*
*

*

Spanier mentions the possibility of deducing such a theorem in
[
12
, p. 283]
, but leaves the statement entirely open as an exercise.

Suppose
\Gamma
is a local coefficient system over
X
, and
G
is an abelian group.
We obtain a new local coefficient system

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,G):=\bigsqcup_{x\in X}\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma(x),G).

A possible formulation of a universal coefficient theorem for local coefficents in cohomology is then as follows:

Let
\Gamma
be a local system of

whose fibers are free abelian groups.
Then we have a natural short exact sequence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Hom}(H_{k}(X;\Gamma),G)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}

This sequence splits, but not naturally.

Observe that there is a canonical chain isomorphism

\displaystyle C^{*}(X;\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,G))\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathrm{Hom}(C_{*}(X;\Gamma),G),

where the boundary maps on the right are given by precomposition with
\partial

As the chain complex

Analogous to the (untwisted) oriented case, we have
twisted Poincaré duality
for any manifold:

\displaystyle\mathrm{PD}_{\mathrm{tw}}\colon H^{k}(X;\mathbb{Z})\xrightarrow{\cong}H_{n+1-k}(X;o_{X}).

To prove this, replace the ordinary cap product

\displaystyle\cap\colon C_{p}(X;\mathbb{Z})\times C^{q}(X;\mathbb{Z})\to C_{p-q}(X;\mathbb{Z})

with the twisted cap product

\displaystyle\cap\colon C_{p}(X;o_{X})\times C^{q}(X;\mathbb{Z})\to C_{p-q}(X;o_{X}).

Every manifold has a preferred
twisted fundamental class
because

H_{n+1}(X,X-\{x\};o_{X})\cong H_{n+1}(X,X-\{x\};\mathbb{Z})\otimes H_{n+1}(X,X-\{x\};\mathbb{Z})

has a preferred generator.
The isomorphism can be understood through a twisted version of the universal coefficient theorem in homology, but we omit the details.
The Poincaré duality isomorphism is then given by the twisted cap product with the twisted fundamental class.

It follows from Thom’s seminal work

16
]
that every homology class in degree

The twisted case is analogous and is explained by Atiyah

The idea is to consider the commutative diagram

Here, we denote by

the set of closed

-dimensional submanifolds of

with an orientation of the normal bundle up to bordism in

with an orientation of the normal bundle.
The top horizontal arrow is given by the Pontryagin-Thom construction.
The left vertical arrow is induced by the Thom class

denotes the Thom space of the universal bundle for

The right vertical arrow is given by the pushforward of the twisted fundamental class.

has shown that

That is if we assume

Two types of manifolds

Throughout this article, we consider two types of manifolds.
Denote by
(w^{2}_{1}+w_{2})\colon H_{2}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{2})\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
the functional associated with
w_{1}^{2}(X)+w_{2}(X)
and by
r\colon H_{2}(X;o_{X})\to H_{2}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
the
\mathrm{mod}\>2
reduction.
We say that

\displaystyle X~\text{is of type I}\quad

\displaystyle:\Longleftrightarrow\quad(w_{1}^{2}+w_{2})\circ r\not\equiv 0,

\displaystyle X~\text{is of type II}\quad

\displaystyle:\Longleftrightarrow\quad(w_{1}^{2}+w_{2})\circ r\equiv 0.

It is clear from the definition that
\mathrm{Pin}^{-}
manifolds are automatically of type II.
But not all manifolds of type II are
\mathrm{Pin}^{-}
.
We say that
X
is of
type IIa
if it is
\mathrm{Pin}^{-}
and of
type IIb
if it is of type II but not
\mathrm{Pin}^{-}
.

We now give a geometric interpretation of this condition in terms of normal bundles of surfaces.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\iota\colon\Sigma\looparrowright X
be an immersed closed surface.
Then

{(w_{1}^{2}+w_{2})(\iota_{*}[\Sigma]_{2})=\left\langle w_{2}(\nu_{\iota}),[\Sigma]_{2}\right\rangle}.

Proof.

A simple computation reveals

\displaystyle(w_{1}^{2}+w_{2})(\iota_{*}[\Sigma]_{2})

\displaystyle=\left\langle w_{1}^{2}(X)+w_{2}(X),\iota_{*}[\Sigma]_{2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\iota^{*}\!\left(w_{1}^{2}(X)+w_{2}(X)\right),[\Sigma]_{2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle w_{1}^{2}(\Sigma)+w_{2}(\Sigma)+w_{2}(\nu_{\iota})+w_{1}(\Sigma)w_{1}(\nu_{\iota})+w^{2}_{1}(\nu_{\iota}),[\Sigma]_{2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle w_{2}(\nu_{\iota})+w_{1}(\Sigma)w_{1}(\nu_{\iota})+w^{2}_{1}(\nu_{\iota}),[\Sigma]_{2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle w_{2}(\nu_{\iota}),[\Sigma]_{2}\right\rangle.

Here we used the fact that
\iota^{*}(TX)\cong T\Sigma\oplus\nu_{\iota}
, and that all surfaces are
\mathrm{Pin}^{-}
, which means
w_{1}^{2}(\Sigma)+w_{2}(\Sigma)=0
.
The last step follows from Wu’s formula which implies
w_{1}(\Sigma)w_{1}(\nu_{\iota})=\mathrm{Sq}^{1}(w_{1}(\nu_{\iota}))
and hence
w_{1}(\Sigma)w_{1}(\nu_{\iota})=w^{2}_{1}(\nu_{\iota})
.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

If there exist an immersed closed surface
\iota\colon\Sigma\looparrowright X
with
w_{1}(\nu_{\iota})=0
and
w_{2}(\nu_{\iota})\neq 0
, then
X
is of type I.
Conversely, if
X
is of type I, then there exists an embedded closed surface
\iota\colon\Sigma\hookrightarrow X
such that
w_{1}(\nu_{\iota})=0
and
w_{2}(\nu_{\iota})\neq 0
.

Proof.

Suppose
\iota\colon\Sigma\looparrowright X
is an immersed closed surface with
w_{1}(\nu_{\iota})=0
.
We have seen in
Lemma

2.2
that the functional
w_{1}^{2}+w_{2}
measures the triviality of the second Stiefel-Whitney class of the normal bundle
w_{2}(\nu_{\iota})
.
Together with functoriality in twisted coefficients, this shows the first implication.
The converse follows from the fact that
H_{2}(X;o_{X})
is generated by embedded closed surfaces with an orientation of the normal bundle as discussed in the previous paragraph.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

It is worth mentioning that
2.3
implies that if there exists an immersed closed surface
\iota\colon\Sigma\looparrowright X
with
w_{1}(\nu_{\iota})=0
and
w_{2}(\nu_{\iota})\neq 0
, then there automatically exists an embedded one with that property.

Equivalently, we obtain the geometric statement for manifolds of type II.

Corollary 2.5
.

If all embedded surfaces
\iota\colon\Sigma\hookrightarrow X
with
w_{1}(\nu_{\iota})=0
have
w_{2}(\nu_{\iota})=0
, then
X
is of type II.
Conversely, if
X
is of type II, then all immersed surfaces
\iota\colon\Sigma\looparrowright X
with
w_{1}(\nu_{\iota})=0
must have
w_{2}(\nu_{\iota})=0
.

When
X
is a 4-manifold, the class
w_{2}(\nu_{\Sigma})
reports
\mathrm{mod}\>2
self-intersection for normally oriented surfaces
\Sigma\subset X
.
If
X
is in addition oriented, type I is equivalent to
X
being
odd
, and type II is equivalent to
X
being
even
.
For higher dimensions, that is if
n\geq 4
, we can give another geometric description.
The key ingredient is the following remark.

Remark 2.6
.

Recall that any vector bundle
E
of rank
n\geq 3
over a 2-dimensional cell complex
\Sigma
is trivializable if and only if
w_{1}(E)
and
w_{2}(E)
vanish.
This can be seen as follows.
Since
n\geq 3
, the second Stiefel Whitney class
w_{2}(E)
provides a full obstruction of finding an
(n-1)
-frame over
\Sigma
, see Milnor and Stasheff
[
10
, §13]
.
Then the
(n-1)
-frame can be completed to an
n
-frame over
\Sigma
if and only if the bundle is orientable, or equivalently if and only if
w_{1}(E)
vanishes.

With that remark, we deduce the following corollary:

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
n\geq 4
.
Then
X
is of type I if and only if there is a closed surface
\Sigma\subset X
such that
\nu_{\Sigma}
is orientable but not trivializable.
Equivalently,
X
is of type II if and only if all closed surfaces
\Sigma\subset X
for which
\nu_{\Sigma}
is orientable have that
\nu_{\Sigma}
is trivializable.

The forgetful map

For any
k
, there is an obvious map
{\mathbb{F}_{k}(X)\to\mathbb{L}_{k}(X;o_{X})}
that forgets the framing but remembers the orientation of the normal bundle.
For
k=1
, we can identify
H_{1}(X;o_{X})\cong\mathbb{L}_{1}(X;o_{X})
, and hence we obtain a group homomorphism

h\colon\mathbb{F}_{1}(X)\to H_{1}(X;o_{X}).

Since any orientable vector bundle over a 1-dimensional cell complex is trivializable, this map is surjective.
Therefore,
\mathbb{F}_{1}(X)
always fits into the short exact sequence (
1.2
).
In the following sections, we determine
\ker(h)
and classify the extension with geometric methods.

3.
The kernel of the forgetful map

To gain insights into the kernel of
h\colon\mathbb{F}_{1}(X)\to H_{1}(X;o_{X})
, we need to familiarize ourselves with certain properties of framings over surfaces with boundary.
For what follows, we call a framing
positive
if it is compatible with a given orientation.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
Y
be a 2-dimensional cell complex and
E\to Y
be a trivializable vector bundle of rank
n\geq 3
with a given orientation.
Then distinct positive framings of
E
are in one-to-one correspondence with elements of
H^{1}(Y;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
.

Proof.

All distinct positive framings of
E
are in one-to-one correspondence with homotopy classes in
[Y,\mathrm{SO}(n)]
via composition with a fixed positive framing.
We represent the generator of
H^{1}(\mathrm{SO}(n),\mathbb{Z}_{2})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}
by a map

Lemma 3.5
.

The subgroup

is generated by

.

Proof.

For any element

, there is a normally oriented bordism

from

to the empty set.

Then we can cut off an embedded disk

Since two copies of

Proposition 3.6

The forgetful map

is either an isomorphism or a 2-to-1 epimorphism.

X
.
Let
\kappa\colon\mathbb{F}_{1}(X)\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
be the corresponding induced splitting map.
Suppose
\gamma\in\nicefrac{{H^{1}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{2})}}{{\left<w_{1}(X)\right>}}
, and let
\bar{\gamma}\in H^{1}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
be a lift of
\gamma
.
Moreover assume
(C,\varphi)
is a normally framed circle in
X
.
When we identify
H^{1}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{2})\cong\mathrm{Hom}(H_{1}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{2}),\mathbb{Z}_{2})
, the action of
\bar{\gamma}
on the given
\mathrm{Pin}^{-}
-structure can be described as follows:

(6.1)

\displaystyle\bar{\gamma}([C]_{2})=\begin{cases}0&\text{iff $\bar{\gamma}$ does \emph{not} change the $\mathrm{Pin}^{-}$-structure on $\left.TX\right|_{C}$},\\
1&\text{iff $\bar{\gamma}$ changes the $\mathrm{Pin}^{-}$-structure on $\left.TX\right|_{C}$}.\end{cases}

Because
\left.TX\right|_{C}
is orientable, we have
w_{1}([C]_{2})=0
.
This implies that
\bar{\gamma}([C]_{2})
does not depend on the choice of lift.
With the identification in
Lemma

6.3
, we conclude

\displaystyle(\kappa\cdot\gamma)([C,\varphi])

\displaystyle=\kappa([C,\varphi])+(\gamma\circ h)([C,\varphi])

(6.2)

\displaystyle=\kappa([C,\varphi])+\bar{\gamma}([C]_{2}).

The statement follows from substituting (
6.1
) into (
6.2
).
∎

7.
Application to vector bundles

By obstruction theory, a choice of
\mathrm{Spin}
-structure over an oriented vector bundle of rank 3 or higher is equivalent to a choice of framing over the 1-skeleton that extends over the 2-skeleton.
This leads to the following lemma:

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
B
be a 1-dimensional cell complex and
E\to B
be an oriented spin vector bundle of rank
n\geq 3
.
Then
E
is isomorphic to the trivial bundle and a
\mathrm{Spin}
-structure uniquely determines a framing of
E
.

A geometric proof of the above statement can be found in
[
9
, Lemma 3.1]
.
For the rest of this section, we assume that
n\geq 4
.
Suppose
E\to X
is an oriented spin vector bundle of rank
n
, and we fix a
\mathrm{Spin}
-structure over
E
.
Let
s\colon X\to E
be a global section, and let
0_{E}\colon X\hookrightarrow E
be the zero section.
We may assume that
s
intersects
0_{E}
transversally, and we denote
s^{-1}(0_{E}):=L
.
As the intersection is transverse, the differential
\mathrm{d}s\colon TX\to TE
defines a canonical isomorphism of bundles
\nu_{L}\cong\left.E\right|_{L}
.
The bundle
\left.E\right|_{L}
inherits a
\mathrm{Spin}
-structure from
E
.
By
Lemma

7.1
this induces a unique framing of
\left.E\right|_{L}
and therefore a normal framing
\varphi
of
\nu_{L}
.
This defines a framed bordism class
[L,\varphi]\in\mathbb{F}_{1}(X)
.
The class
h([L,\varphi])\in H_{1}(X;o_{X})
is the (twisted) Poincaré dual to the Euler class
e(E)\in H^{n}(X;\mathbb{Z})
.
In other words, the class
[L,\varphi]
is a refinement of the Euler class
e(E)
.
We will show that this class is a
full
obstruction to finding a non-vanishing section on
E
.

Lemma 7.2
.

The class
[L,\varphi]
does not depend on the choice of section
s
up to homotopy.

Proof.

Suppose that
s^{\prime}\colon X\to E
is a section transverse to
0_{E}
that is homotopic (through sections) to
s
.
That is, there is a homotopy
\bar{s}\colon X\times[0,1]\to E
with
\bar{s}(\,\cdot\,,0)=s
and
\bar{s}(\,\cdot\,,1)=s^{\prime}
.
Consider the projection
\mathrm{pr}_{1}\colon X\times[0,1]\to X
and denote
\bar{E}=\mathrm{pr}_{1}^{*}E
.
Then
\bar{s}
is a section of
\bar{E}
and without loss of generality, we may assume that
\bar{s}
is transverse to
0_{\bar{E}}
.
Denote the zero locus of
s^{\prime}
by
L^{\prime}:=(s^{\prime})^{-1}(0_{E})
and the induced normal framing by
\varphi^{\prime}
.
Without loss of generality, we assume that the submanifold
\Sigma:=\bar{s}^{-1}(0_{\bar{E}})\subset X\times[0,1]
has no connected components without boundary.
Then the boundary of
\Sigma
is precisely
L
and
L^{\prime}
and
\Sigma\simeq\text{1-dim.\ cell complex}
.
The restricted bundle
{\bar{E}}|_{\Sigma}
inherits a
\mathrm{Spin}
-structure from
E
, and by
Lemma

7.1
, this gives a unique framing of
{\bar{E}}|_{\Sigma}
.
This framing induces a normal framing over
\Sigma
so that
(L,\varphi)
is normally framed to
(L^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})
.
∎

Lemma 7.3
.

The class
[L,\varphi]
does not depend on the choice of the
\mathrm{Spin}
-structure over
E
if
w_{n}(E)=0
.

Proof.

To begin, let us observe that
\mathrm{PD}_{2}([L]_{2})=w_{n}(E)
.
Consequently, we can deduce that
w_{n}(E)=0
if and only if
[L]_{2}=0
.
A change of
\mathrm{Spin}
-structure corresponds to a homomorphism
\pi_{1}(X)\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, i.e. an element in
H^{1}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
.
That homomorphism takes the homotopy class of a circle
C
and “twists” the framing over
C
by an element in
\pi_{1}(\mathrm{SO}(n))\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.
However, given that
[L]_{2}=0
, any such change does not impact the framing over
L
.
∎

Let
W
be a connected closed
(n+k)
-dimensional manifold with connected boundary
\partial W
, where
k\geq 1
.
Consider a smooth map
f\colon W\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
for which zero is a regular value and
f^{-1}(0)\cap\partial W=\varnothing
.
Then
M:=f^{-1}(0)
is a closed submanifold of
\mathrm{int}(W)
.
The manifold
M
comes equipped with an induced normal framing
\varphi
.
Note that
(M,\varphi)
defines an element
[M,\varphi]
in the

Here,
\mathbb{F}_{k}(W,\partial W)

Now, we set

\displaystyle g\colon\partial W\to S^{n-1}\quad\text{where}\quad g(x):=\frac{f(x)}{|f(x)|}.

The map

Proof.

To begin the proof, we observe that the map

The Pontryagin-Thom construction gives an isomorphism

\mathbb{F}_{k}(W,\partial W)\cong[(W,\partial W),(S^{n},\infty)].

This isomorphism associates the bordism class

with the homotopy class

We are ready to prove the main theorem of this section that generalizes the invariant introduced by Konstantis
[
9
]
.

Theorem 7.5
.

Let
E\to X
be an oriented vector bundle of rank
n\geq 4
with a
\mathrm{Spin}
-structure.
Moreover, denote by
(L,\varphi)
the zero locus of a section transverse to the zero section together with the normal framing induced by the
\mathrm{Spin}
-structure.
Then
E
admits a non-vanishing section if and only if
[L,\varphi]=0
in
\mathbb{F}_{1}(X)
.

Proof.

Suppose
E
admits a non-vanishing section
s^{\prime}
.
Then the zero locus of
s^{\prime}
is the empty set, and because of
Lemma

7.2
, we get
[L,\varphi]=0
.

For the converse, assume that
[L,\varphi]=0
in
\mathbb{F}_{1}(X)
.
Let
\Sigma\subset X\times[0,1]
be the associated framed null-bordism.
Notice that
\Sigma
may not be connected, i.e.
\Sigma=\Sigma_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup\Sigma_{m}
.
Without loss of generality, we assume that each connected component
\Sigma_{k}
has a non-empty boundary.
We isotope each
\Sigma_{k}
relative to
\partial\Sigma_{k}
to a submanifold
\iota_{k}\colon\Sigma_{k}\hookrightarrow X
.
There, we attach a closed collar, i.e. we extend
\iota_{k}
to an embedding

\displaystyle\Sigma_{k}\cup_{\partial\Sigma_{k}}\big(\partial\Sigma_{k}\times[0,1]\big)\hookrightarrow X,

and consider the tubular neighborhood of this embedding.
The corresponding disk bundle gives us a compact manifold with corners.
We can smoothen the corners to produce a compact manifold
W_{k}
with connected and smooth boundary
\partial W_{k}
.
By construction, we have
[\partial\Sigma_{k},\varphi_{k}]=0
in
\mathbb{F}_{1}(W_{k},\partial W_{k})
, where
\varphi_{k}
is the restriction of
\varphi
to
\partial\Sigma_{k}
.
As
W_{k}
is homotopy equivalent to
\Sigma_{k}
, we have
\left.E\right|_{W_{k}}\cong W_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
If we restrict
s
to
W_{k}
, we get an induced map
f_{k}\colon W_{k}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
whose zero locus is precisely
\partial\Sigma_{k}
.
We can now apply
Lemma

7.4
to deform
s
into a section that has no zeroes in each
W_{k}
.
∎

Remark 7.6
.

The proof above can be interpreted as a version of the Whitney trick.
To see this, we apply the same trick if the rank of
E
is equal to the dimension of
X
, namely
n+1
.
In that case, the null-bordism is given by a bunch of arcs that isotope to arcs in
X
.
We concatenate these arcs with their corresponding lifts along the section
s
and obtain closed curves in the total space
E
that bound contractible disks.
These disks can now be used to “push”
s
from
0_{E}
in a neighborhood of each arc.

Now, we would like to relate

7.5
to type I and type II manifolds.
If

1.3
is an immediate consequence of

7.5
.
To obtain a well-defined invariant in the case of type IIa manifolds, we require the next lemma.

.

Suppose that

is of type IIa, and choose a
\mathrm{Pin}^{-}
-structure over

.
We denote by

the induced splitting map.
Moreover, let

as in
Theorem

7.5
.
If

, then

The proof is similar to the proof of

and follows from

.
If

, we obtain from (

) that

remains unchanged for any choice of

-structure.
∎

Together with

, this allows us to define

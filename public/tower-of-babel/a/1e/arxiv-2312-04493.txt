Incoherent diffractive dijet production and gluon Bose enhancement in the nuclear wave function
Tiyasa Kar, Alexander Kovner, Ming Li, Vladimir V. Skokov

Abstract
We investigate the effect of gluon Bose enhancement in the nuclear wave function on the dijet production in incoherent diffractive processes in DIS and ultraperipheral collisions. We demonstrate that Bose enhancement leads to an enhancement of diffractive dijet production cross section when the transverse momenta of the two jets are aligned at zero relative angle. This enhancement is maximal when the magnitude of the transverse momenta of the two jets are equal, and disappears rather quickly as a function of the ratio of the two momenta. We study both the dilute limit and fully nonlinear dense regime where the nuclear wave function is evolved with the leading order JIMWLK equation. In both cases we observe a visible effect, with it being enhanced by the evolution due to the dynamical generation of the color neutralization scale.

The reason we expect dijet production to be sensitive to the Bose correlation is rather transparent.
At high energy, in the infinite momentum frame,
the virtual photon fluctuates into a quark-antiquark pair (dipole) with transverse momenta

In the final state this leads to two (quark-anti-quark) jets with the

In the hadronic wave function prior to the scattering gluons are correlated via Bose enhancement. That is, the probability of finding two gluons is enhanced if they have the same color and transverse momentum (
{\underline{k}}_{1}\approx{\underline{k}}_{2}

The two jets may be away from the back-to-back regime and may even have almost the same transverse momenta (while longitudinal momentum can be very different), if the exchange with the target is due to hard gluons.

The dijet observable is much simpler and better experimentally accessible than the trijet we have considered before. In this paper we study numerically the diffractive dijet production cross section as a function of the relative angle between the transverse momenta of the two jets in the CGC approach.
We start with the dilute regime where the calculation can be done to lowest order in the density of the target. We then study the full nonlinear case where the target is evolved by the leading order JIMWLK evolution.

This enhancement is most significant when the transverse momenta of the two jets are equal in magnitude, and disappears when the ratio of the two momenta is of order 1.5-2. The effect is enhanced when the nonvanishing color neutralization scale, and is more pronounced for the JIMWLK evolved results.

III
Bose enhancement

III.1
Soft gluon density matrix and averaging over target configurations

CGC is an effective field theory based on the separation of the degrees of freedom into valence and soft sectors as quantified by the fraction of the longitudinal momentum of the hadron they carry. The valence charges are treated as static sources of faster soft degrees of freedom.

The conventional approach in CGC is to first average over the soft sector and then over the valence target configurations – equations in the previous section were obtained in this precise way. Although this approach is most convenient for practical calculations it is not very illuminating as far as the quantum correlations in the target are concerned. To analyze the latter, one, first, averages over valence sector and only then considers expectations values of operators of interest in the soft sector.

The CGC wave function
[
62
,
63
]
is given by

|\psi\rangle=|s_{v}\rangle\otimes|v\rangle\,,

(9)

where
|v\rangle
is the state vector characterizing the valence degrees of freedom and
|s_{v}\rangle
is the vacuum of the soft fields in the presence of the valence source.

In the leading perturbative order

|s_{v}\rangle=\mathcal{C}|0\rangle,\quad{\cal C}=\exp\left\{2i{\rm tr}\int_{{\underline{k}}}b^{i}({\underline{k}})\phi^{a}_{i}({\underline{k}})\right\}\,

(10)

where

\phi_{i}({\underline{k}})\equiv a_{i}^{+}({\underline{k}})+a_{i}(-{\underline{k}})\,

(11)

is the soft gluon field operator with
a^{+}_{i}
,
a_{i}
being the gluon creation and annihilation operators.
The background field
b^{i}_{a}
is determined by the valence color charge density
\rho
via:

b^{i}_{a}({\underline{k}})=g\rho_{a}({\underline{k}})\frac{i{\underline{k}}_{i}}{k^{2}}+{\cal O}(\rho^{2})\,.

(12)

In this and the next section, for the sake of simplicity of presentation, we consider the dilute approximation in the target, that is
the leading contribution of the color charge density. At the leading order in
\rho({\underline{k}})
, only gluons with the longitudinal polarization contribute to
{\cal C}
and
|s_{v}\rangle
.

The valence wave function
|v\rangle
is customarily modeled in the so-called McLerran-Venugopalan (MV) model as
[
57
,
56
]
. The MV model can be formulated in terms of diagonal matrix elements of the valence density matrix in the color charge density basis:

\langle\rho|v\rangle\langle v|\rho\rangle=Ne^{-\int_{{\underline{k}}}\frac{1}{2\mu^{2}}\rho_{a}({\underline{k}})\rho^{*}_{a}({\underline{k}})}\,,

(13)

where
N
is the normalization factor and the parameter
\mu^{2}
determines the average color charge density in the valence wave function.

The full hadron density matrix reads

\hat{\rho}=|v\rangle\otimes|s_{v}\rangle\langle s_{v}|\otimes\langle v|\,.

(14)

Integrating out the valence modes yields the reduced density matrix for the soft sector

\hat{\rho}_{r}={\rm Tr}_{\rho}\hat{\rho}\equiv\int D\rho\,\langle\rho|\hat{\rho}|\rho\rangle=\int D\rho\,\langle\rho|v\rangle\,|s_{v}\rangle\langle s_{v}|\,\langle v|\rho\rangle\,.

(15)

In the particle number representation it was derived in Ref.
[
63
]
. The nonvanishing matrix elements of
\hat{\rho}_{r}
between states in the momentum space Fock basis are

\displaystyle\rho_{n,m,\alpha,\beta}\equiv\langle n_{c}({\underline{q}}),m_{c}(-{\underline{q}})|\hat{\rho}_{r}({\underline{q}})|\alpha_{c}({\underline{q}}),\beta_{c}(-{\underline{q}})\rangle=(1-R)\frac{\left(n+\beta\right)!}{\sqrt{n!m!\alpha!\beta!}}\left(\frac{R}{2}\right)^{n+\beta}\delta_{\left(n+\beta\right),\left(m+\alpha\right)}\,,

(16)

\displaystyle R=\left(1+\frac{{\underline{q}}^{2}}{2g^{2}\mu^{2}}\right)^{-1}

(17)

with other elements being zero. The matrix elements are explicitly diagonal in color and momentum. The state
|n_{c}({\underline{q}}),m_{c}(-{\underline{q}})\rangle
denotes
n
gluons with momentum
{\underline{q}}
and
m
gluons with momentum
-{\underline{q}}
.

III.2
Bose enhancement in the soft density matrix

Consider the correlator (only longitudinal polarization of gluons is included)

\displaystyle D({\underline{k}},{\underline{p}})={\rm Tr}\left(\hat{\rho}_{r}a_{b}^{+}({\underline{k}})a_{c}^{+}({\underline{p}})a_{b}({\underline{k}})a_{c}({\underline{p}})\right).

(18)

This measures the correlation between two gluons drawn from the target.

This can be easily calculated in the MV model.
Consider first

\displaystyle\langle a_{a}^{+}({\underline{k}}_{1})a_{b}({\underline{k}}_{2})\rangle={\rm Tr}\left(\hat{\rho}_{r}a_{a}^{+}({\underline{k}}_{1})a_{b}({\underline{k}}_{2})\right)=(2\pi)^{2}\delta^{(2)}({\underline{k}}_{1}-{\underline{k}}_{2})\delta_{ab}\sum_{n,m}n\rho_{n,m,n,m}=(2\pi)^{2}\delta^{(2)}({\underline{k}}_{1}-{\underline{k}}_{2})\delta_{ab}\frac{g^{2}\bar{\mu}^{2}}{k_{1}^{2}}\,.

(19)

The sum is computed in Appendix
A
.

Additionally

\displaystyle\langle a_{a}^{+}({\underline{k}}_{1})a^{+}_{b}({\underline{k}}_{2})\rangle=(2\pi)^{2}\delta^{(2)}({\underline{k}}_{1}-{\underline{k}}_{2})\delta_{ab}\sum_{n,m}\sqrt{n+1}\sqrt{m+1}\rho_{n,m,n+1,m+1}=(2\pi)^{2}\delta^{(2)}({\underline{k}}_{1}+{\underline{k}}_{2})\delta_{ab}\frac{g^{2}\bar{\mu}^{2}}{k_{1}^{2}}\,

(20)

with the same result for
\langle a_{a}({\underline{k}}_{1})a_{b}({\underline{k}}_{2})\rangle
.

In Ref.
[
64
]
, it was shown that the density matrix
\rho_{r}
is Gaussian, that is
\hat{\rho}_{r}=\exp\left[-\int d^{2}q\,\beta\omega({\underline{q}})c^{+}({\underline{q}})c({\underline{q}})\right]
where
c({\underline{q}})
is a linear combination of
a^{+}({\underline{q}})

and
a(-{\underline{q}})
.
Therefore we can use Wick theorem to find the expectation value of
D({\underline{k}},{\underline{p}})
. We should keep in mind that in addition to the standard Wick contraction
\langle a^{\dagger}a\rangle
, also the contractions
\langle a^{\dagger}a^{\dagger}\rangle
and
\langle aa\rangle
are nonvanishing and are given by (
20
). We get

\displaystyle D({\underline{k}},{\underline{p}})

\displaystyle=\left\langle a_{b}^{+}({\underline{k}})a_{c}^{+}({\underline{p}})a_{b}({\underline{k}})a_{c}({\underline{p}})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle a_{b}^{+}({\underline{k}})a_{b}({\underline{k}})\right\rangle\left\langle a_{c}^{+}({\underline{p}})a_{c}({\underline{p}})\right\rangle+\left\langle a_{b}^{+}({\underline{k}})a_{c}^{+}({\underline{p}})\right\rangle\left\langle a_{b}({\underline{k}})a_{c}({\underline{p}})\right\rangle+\left\langle a_{b}^{+}({\underline{k}})a_{c}({\underline{p}})\right\rangle\left\langle a_{c}^{+}({\underline{p}})a_{b}({\underline{k}})\right\rangle\,.

(21)

Here, as we are interested in density-enhanced terms, we can ignore the order of the operators, that is we can treat the creation annihilation operators as commuting. We thus obtain

\displaystyle D({\underline{k}},{\underline{p}})

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\left(S_{\perp}(N_{c}^{2}-1)\frac{g^{2}\bar{\mu}^{2}}{{\underline{k}}^{2}}\right)}_{n({\underline{k}})}\underbrace{\left(S_{\perp}(N_{c}^{2}-1)\frac{g^{2}\bar{\mu}^{2}}{{\underline{p}}^{2}}\right)}_{n({\underline{p}})}

\displaystyle+(2\pi)^{2}(N_{c}^{2}-1)S_{\perp}\left(\frac{g^{2}\bar{\mu}^{2}}{{\underline{k}}^{2}}\right)^{2}\left[\delta^{(2)}({\underline{k}}+{\underline{p}})+\delta^{(2)}({\underline{k}}-{\underline{p}})\right]\,.

(22)

The first term is the “classical” independent and factorizing contribution, while the last term represents the non-trivial effect of Bose correlations.
Note that the correlation contains the sum of two delta functions. The first one leads to the enhancement of gluon correlations for back-to-back kinematics. Although this term is important, in physical processes, it is often overpowered by the stronger effect of the transverse momentum conservation. The later term leads to the enhancement of gluons at the same momentum and this is the effect we are interested in. Note that the sensitivity to the Bose enhancement starts from the observables quartic in creation/annihilation operators or, in other words, from the order of
\bar{\mu}^{4}
.

IV
Dilute approximation

We now discuss averaging over the target in dilute approximation. The basic objects we have to average are products of Wilson lines.
The Wilson line in the fundamental representation is

\displaystyle V({\underline{x}})={\cal P}\exp\left(ig\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dx^{-}t^{a}A_{a}^{+}(x^{-},x^{+}=0,{\underline{x}})\right)\,.

(23)

In the covariant gauge

\displaystyle\partial^{2}A_{a}^{+}(x^{-},{\underline{x}})=g\rho_{a}(x^{-},{\underline{x}})\,.

(24)

which yields

\displaystyle A_{a}^{+}(x^{-},{\underline{x}})=g\frac{1}{\partial^{2}}\rho(x^{-},{\underline{x}})=-\frac{g}{2\pi}\int d^{2}{\underline{y}}\ \ln(|{\underline{x}}-{\underline{y}}|\Lambda)\ \rho(x^{-},{\underline{y}})=\frac{g}{2\pi}\int\frac{d^{2}{\underline{k}}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{1}{k^{2}}\rho(x^{-},{\underline{k}})\,.

In the MV model, the correlators of

\displaystyle\langle\rho^{a}(x^{-},{\underline{x}})\rho^{b}(y^{-},{\underline{y}})\rangle=\delta^{ab}\mu^{2}(x^{-})\delta(x^{-}-y^{-})\delta^{(2)}({\underline{x}}-{\underline{y}})\,.

(26)

For the field correlators this translates into

\displaystyle\langle A^{a}(x^{-},{\underline{x}})A^{b}(y^{-},{\underline{y}})\rangle=\delta^{ab}g^{2}\mu^{2}(x^{-})\delta(x^{-}-y^{-})L({\underline{x}}-{\underline{y}})

\displaystyle\left.\quad\quad\quad+A_{b}^{+}(x_{1}^{-},{\underline{x}})\langle A_{a}^{+}(x_{0}^{-},{\underline{x}})A_{c}^{+}(x_{2}^{-},{\underline{x}})\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{f}L({\underline{0}})}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dx_{0}^{-}\int_{-\infty}^{x_{0}^{-}}dx_{1}^{-}t^{a}A_{a}^{+}(x_{0}^{-},{\underline{x}})\mu^{2}(x_{1}^{-})+\frac{C_{f}L({\underline{0}})}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dx_{0}^{-}\int_{-\infty}^{x_{0}^{-}}dx_{2}^{-}t^{a}A_{a}^{+}(x_{2}^{-},{\underline{x}})\mu^{2}(x_{0}^{-})

V
Beyond dilute approximation and small x evolution

The analysis performed in the previous section relied upon the dilute approximation. In this section, we present the results obtained by numerical simulations of the full MV model with subsequent small-x evolution computed using leading-order fixed coupling JIMWLK equation.

JIMWLK is the renormalization group evolution towards smaller x, as conveniently parametrized by
\alpha_{s}Y=\alpha_{s}\ln\frac{1}{x}
. We initialize the evolution with the MV model, generalized to include the color neutralization scale
m_{0}=1/4Q^{0}_{s}
in (
40
), where
Q_{s}^{0}
is the saturation momentum at zero rapidity. We follow the widely accepted definition of the saturation scale in the numerical calculation through the fundamental dipole scattering matrix
S(r_{s}=\sqrt{2}/Q_{s})=\exp(-1/2)
.

As discussed above, the conventional MV model does not incorporate color neutralization. However the JIMWLK evolution generates a color neutralization scale dynamically even if it is not present in the initial condition. This scale turns out to be proportional to the saturation momentum with the proportionality coefficient of order unity, see Ref.
[
68
]
. With this in mind we include such a scale in the initial condition as well albeit with a smaller proportionality factor.

Numerical procedures for MV and JIMWLK are identical to those described in Ref.
[
69
]
and Ref.
[
70
]
correspondingly.
As in the previous section, we fixed the value of
z
(although it can be chosen arbitrary), in order to compute the correlations functions defined in Eq. (
39
). The details of the numerical calculation of the diffractive observable are presented in Appendix
B
.

VI
Conclusions

Let us discuss our numerical results.

First consider Fig.
2
. The correlator plotted exhibits the features expected from the Bose enhancement. Both, for
m=0
and
m=Q_{s}
the correlator has a maximum at zero angle as long as the momenta of the two gluons are close to each other. The maximum is most pronounced when
k_{1}=k_{2}
and disappears when the ratio of the two momenta becomes roughly 1.5. Introduction of the color neutralization scale makes the maximum more robust, both in terms of it being more pronounced and surviving for wider range of momenta. This feature was observed also in our earlier work on trijet production
[
45
,
46
]
. Qualitatively, the origin of this behavior is that finite color neutralization scale suppresses gluons with small transverse momenta. These gluons mostly contribute to the correlator at
\Delta\phi=\pi
, and suppressing them makes the peak at
\Delta\phi=0
more pronounced. Thus the presence of a large saturation (or color neutralization) scale is a welcome feature from the perspective of observing the Bose enhancement effects.

Moving on to Fig.
3
we see the confirmation of the same trend. Evolution in energy generates color neutralization scale
[
68
]
. This scale quickly grows beyond
Q_{s}^{0}
present in the initial condition. As a result evolving from
\alpha_{s}Y=0
to
\alpha_{s}Y=.4
significantly increases the Bose enhancement signal. This is clearly seen moving from Fig.
3(a)
to Fig.
3(b)
.
Interestingly, further evolution to
\alpha_{s}Y=.8
and beyond changes the picture very little. Although there is marginal enhancement of the signal, it is much less noticeable than the initial change starting with the MV model.
This suggests that there is importance not only to the value of the saturation scale, but also to the shape of the gluon distribution in the hadron.

To summarize, we have shown that one can probe Bose enhancement with a simpler observable than discussed earlier - the diffractive dijet production. To do that one needs to go away from the correlation limit into regime where the total momentum and the momentum imbalance of the two jets are close.

Appendix B
Numerical evaluation of the incoherent diffractive cross-section

The main difficulty in computing the expectation value for both diffractive and inclusive cross section is in computing the integrals of the term involving four Wilson lines

\displaystyle T=

\displaystyle\int\frac{\text{d}^{2}{\underline{x}}_{1}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{\text{d}^{2}{\underline{x}}_{1}^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{\text{d}^{2}{\underline{x}}_{2}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{\text{d}^{2}{\underline{x}}_{2}^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{-i{\underline{k}}_{1}\cdot({\underline{x}}_{1}-{\underline{x}}_{1}^{\prime})}e^{-i{\underline{k}}_{2}\cdot({\underline{x}}_{2}-{\underline{x}}_{2}^{\prime})}K_{0}(\epsilon_{f}|{\underline{x}}_{1}-{\underline{x}}_{2}|)K_{0}(\epsilon_{f}|{\underline{x}}_{1}^{\prime}-{\underline{x}}_{2}^{\prime}|)

\displaystyle\times\left[V^{\dagger}({\underline{x}}_{2})V({\underline{x}}_{1})\right]_{ij}\left[V^{\dagger}({\underline{x}}^{\prime}_{1})V({\underline{x}}^{\prime}_{2})\right]_{kl}\,.

(47)

To proceed define the amplitude

\displaystyle M_{ij}({\underline{k}}_{1},{\underline{k}}_{2})=

\displaystyle\int\frac{\text{d}^{2}{\underline{x}}_{1}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{\text{d}^{2}{\underline{x}}_{2}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{-i{\underline{k}}_{1}\cdot{\underline{x}}_{1}-i{\underline{k}}_{2}{\underline{x}}_{2}}K_{0}(\epsilon_{f}|{\underline{x}}_{1}-{\underline{x}}_{2}|)\left[V^{\dagger}({\underline{x}}_{2})V({\underline{x}}_{1})\right]_{ij}

(48)

which can be efficiently evaluated using the fast Fourier transform.

Once the amplitude is known, we can find the compination

\displaystyle T=M_{ij}({\underline{k}}_{1},{\underline{k}}_{2})M_{kl}(-{\underline{k}}_{2},-{\underline{k}}_{1})

(49)

and thus for the diffractive we simply

\displaystyle T={\rm tr}M({\underline{k}}_{1},{\underline{k}}_{2})\,{\rm tr}M(-{\underline{k}}_{2},-{\underline{k}}_{1})

(50)

which requires storing a four dimensional matrix
{\rm tr}M({\underline{k}}_{1},{\underline{k}}_{2})
as enumerates by the momentum components. For inclusive one has to store full
M_{ij}({\underline{k}}_{1},{\underline{k}}_{2})
. That is computing the inclusive cross section requires a factor of
N_{c}^{2}-1
more operating memory and thus is more challenging.

Finally, the diffractive cross section boils down to evaluating the averages over the target proportional to

\displaystyle\langle{\rm tr}M({\underline{k}}_{1},{\underline{k}}_{2})\,{\rm tr}M(-{\underline{k}}_{2},-{\underline{k}}_{1})\rangle-\langle{\rm tr}M({\underline{k}}_{1},{\underline{k}}_{2})\,\rangle\langle{\rm tr}M(-{\underline{k}}_{2},-{\underline{k}}_{1})\rangle\,.

(51)

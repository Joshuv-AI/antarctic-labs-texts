Sharp L 1 L^{1} -Approximation of the log-Heston SDE by Euler-type methods
Annalena Mickel, Andreas Neuenkirch

Abstract
We study the L 1 L^{1} -approximation of the log-Heston SDE at equidistant time points by Euler-type methods. We establish the convergence order 1 / 2 − ϵ 1/2-\epsilon for ϵ > 0 \epsilon>0 arbitrarily small, if the Feller index ν \nu of the underlying CIR process satisfies ν > 1 \nu>1 . Thus, we recover the standard convergence order of the Euler scheme for SDEs with globally Lipschitz coefficients. Moreover, we discuss the case ν ≤ 1 \nu\leq 1 and illustrate our findings by several numerical examples. Key words: CIR process, Heston model, Euler-type methods, L 1 L^{1} -error 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification: 65C30; 60H35; 91G60

Introduction and Main Results

The CIR process originates from the works of Feller in the 1950s, see e.g.
[
16
]
, and was used by Cox, Ingersoll and Ross
[
10
]
to model short term interest rates. It is the solution to the following stochastic differential equation (SDE)

dV_{t}=\kappa(\theta-V_{t})dt+\sigma\sqrt{V_{t}}dW_{t},\qquad t\in[0,T],

(1)

where
V_{0}=v_{0}>0
and
W=(W_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
is a Brownian motion. The parameters can be interpreted as follows:
\theta>0
is the long run mean of the process,
\kappa>0
is its speed of mean reversion and
\sigma>0
is its volatility. We assume the initial value
v_{0}
to be deterministic. We denote the Feller index of the CIR process by

\nu:=\frac{2\kappa\theta}{\sigma^{2}}.

Note that the CIR process takes positive values only. Additionally, if
\nu\geq 1
almost all sample paths are strictly positive.
The CIR process is used in particular to model the volatility of the asset price in the Heston model
[
22
]
. Here, the SDE for the price process and its volatility are given by

\begin{aligned} dS_{t}&=\mu S_{t}dt+\sqrt{V_{t}}S_{t}\left(\rho dW_{t}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}dB_{t}\right),\\
dV_{t}&=\kappa(\theta-V_{t})dt+\sigma\sqrt{V_{t}}dW_{t},\end{aligned}\qquad t\in[0,T],

(2)

where
S_{0}=s_{0}>0
is deterministic,
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
is the risk-free interest rate,
\rho\in[-1,1]
determines the correlation between the two processes and
W=(W_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
,
B=(B_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
are independent Brownian motions. Usually, the log-Heston model instead of the Heston model is considered in numerical practice. This yields the SDE

\begin{aligned} dX_{t}&=\left(\mu-\frac{1}{2}V_{t}\right)dt+\sqrt{V_{t}}\left(\rho dW_{t}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}dB_{t}\right),\\
dV_{t}&=\kappa(\theta-V_{t})dt+\sigma\sqrt{V_{t}}dW_{t},\end{aligned}\qquad t\in[0,T],

(3)

where
X_{t}=\log(S_{t})
and
x_{0}=\log(s_{0})\in\mathbb{R}
. Since the right hand side of the SDE for the log-asset price does not depend on
X_{t}
, its approximation reduces to the approximation of a Riemann integral of
V
and an Itō integral of
\sqrt{V}
. So, the main difficulty is the approximation of the CIR process
V=(V_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
. Since the CIR process takes positive values only and the diffusion coefficient is a square root and thus not globally Lipschitz continuous, much effort has been devoted to this problem in the last 25 years, see Subsection
1.1
.

In this article, we are looking at Euler discretization schemes for the CIR process and the log-Heston model. We will work with an equidistant discretization

t_{k}=k\Delta t,\quad k=0,\ldots,N,

with
\Delta t=T/N
. A naive Euler discretization will give negative values and is not well defined
due to the square root coefficient. Therefore a ”fix” is required. A summary of the existing Euler schemes for the CIR process and a numerical comparison can be found in
[
28
]
, where a general framework for Euler schemes for the CIR process is proposed as

\displaystyle\bar{v}_{t_{k+1}}

\displaystyle=f_{1}(\bar{v}_{t_{k}})+\kappa\left(\theta-f_{2}(\bar{v}_{t_{k}})\right)(t_{k+1}-t_{k})+\sigma\sqrt{f_{3}(\bar{v}_{t_{k}})}\left(W_{t_{k+1}}-W_{t_{k}}\right)

(4)

\displaystyle\hat{v}_{t_{k+1}}

\displaystyle=f_{3}(\bar{v}_{t_{k+1}})

for

with

\displaystyle{\tt id}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle\tt{\displaystyle id}(x)=x,

\displaystyle{\tt abs}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,\infty),

Here we will study the Euler schemes with

\displaystyle f_{1}={\tt id},\quad f_{2}\in\{{\tt id,abs,sym}\},\quad f_{3}\in\{{\tt abs,sym}\}

(5)

or

The first set of conditions modifies the coefficients of the CIR process to deal with negative values, which may arise in the computation. For example,

1.1
Previous results for the CIR process

The strong approximation of the CIR process has been intensively studied in the last years.
The first works on this topic are
[
14
,
1
,
23
]
, which prove strong convergence (without a polynomial rate) of various explicit and implicit schemes using the Yamada-Watanabe approach.

One of the schemes of
[
1
]
is the drift-implicit square root Euler scheme which is well defined and positivity preserving for
\nu\geq\frac{1}{2}
. This scheme turned out to be accessible to a more detailed error analysis, see
[
15
,
2
,
32
,
25
]
. In particular, for the
L^{1}
-approximation at the final time point
[
2
]
establishes convergence order 1 for
\nu>2
, while
[
15
]
gives convergence order
\frac{1}{2}
for
\nu>1
,
[
25
]
yields order
\nu-\frac{1}{2}-\epsilon
for
\nu>\frac{1}{2}
and
[
19
]
establishes order
\frac{1}{2}
for the particular case
\nu=\frac{1}{2}
.

A breakthrough for the (very challenging) case
\nu\leq 1
was provided by
[
19
]
and
[
18
]
. In particular, the truncated Milstein scheme of
[
18
]
attains
L^{1}
-convergence order
\min\{\frac{1}{2},\nu\}-\epsilon
in this regime.

So, which rates are best possible for the (non-adaptive)
L^{1}
-approximation of the CIR process at the final time point?
This question has been answered by the works
[
20
]
and
[
21
]
, which
yield

\liminf_{N\rightarrow\infty}\,N^{\min\{\nu,1\}}\,\inf_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\big|u(W_{t_{1}},W_{t_{2}},\ldots,W_{t_{N}})-V_{T}\big|\right]>0,

where
\mathcal{U}
is the set of measurable functions
u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.
Thus, the convergence rate of the truncated Milstein scheme for
\nu\leq\frac{1}{2}
and the rate of the drift-implicit square root Euler for
\nu>2
are optimal.

In contrast to this, convergence rate results for the explicit Euler schemes in Table
1
have been rare. In
[
6
]
, the authors prove
L^{p}
-convergence order
\frac{1}{2}
for the Symmetrized Euler but with a strong restriction on the Feller index. For FTE the
L^{p}
-convergence order
\frac{1}{2}
for
2\leq p<\nu-1
and
\nu>3
is shown in
[
12
]
. As mentioned,
[
28
]
provides a survey and numerical comparison of Euler-type schemes. Further contributions on the strong approximation of the CIR process can be found in
[
17
,
9
,
31
,
8
]
, where
[
31
]
provides in particular a method for the uniform approximation of the CIR process.

Clearly, the truncated Milstein scheme and the drift-implicit square root Euler scheme have similar or even
better convergence properties than the Euler schemes studied in this article. However, Euler schemes for the approximation of the CIR process are nevertheless popular and our analysis addresses a problem that has been open for a long time, namely to establish (sharp) convergence rates for these schemes under mild assumptions on the Feller index. Moreover, for the log-Heston SDE the obtained convergence rate is optimal for
\nu>1
, see the next subsection.

1.2
Previous results for the log-Heston SDE

We are not aware of any results concerning the strong approximation of the log-Heston model except
[
27
,
3
]
. In
[
3
]
the drift-implicit square root Euler for the CIR process is combined with an Euler discretization of the log-Heston process and
L^{2}
-convergence order
\frac{1}{2}
is obtained for
\nu>2
, while
[
27
]
uses a drift-implicit Milstein discretization of the CIR process instead and obtains
L^{2}
-convergence for

\nu>1
.

In view of these results, Theorem
1.1
and Proposition
1.2
are a major improvement, in particular with respect to the conditions on the Feller index. Moreover, for
\nu>1
the obtained convergence rate is best possible. In
[
30
]
we study the optimal
L^{1}
-approximation of the log-Heston model by equidistant discretizations of
W
and
B
and show in particular that

\liminf_{N\rightarrow\infty}\,N^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\inf_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\big|u(W_{t_{1}},W_{t_{2}},\ldots,W_{t_{N}},B_{t_{1}},B_{t_{2}},\ldots,B_{t_{N}})-X_{T}\big|\right]>0,

for
\nu>1
, where
\mathcal{U}
is the set of measurable functions from
\mathbb{R}^{2N}
to
\mathbb{R}
.

The strong approximation of the full Heston model, i.e. of
S_{T}
instead of
X_{T}
carries an additional burden, since the SDE for the asset price has superlinear coefficients and admits moment explosions, i.e.
{\mathbb{E}}(S_{T}^{p})=\infty
for certain parameter constellations and
p>1
, see e.g.
[
4
]
. The article
[
11
]
, where
exponential integrability results for several Euler-type methods for the CIR process have been established, is dedicated to this problem.

1.3
Further results

Our analysis is taylor-made for the
L^{1}
-approximation and based on the Tanaka-Meyer formula combined with a clever control of the arising local time of the error process. We found this approach in
[
13
]
, where the approximation of SDEs with irregular drift and additive noise has been studied.
For the
L^{p}
-approximation with
p\geq 1
we could deduce the upper bound
\min\{1,\nu\}/(2p)
for the convergence order by
a standard application of the Hölder inequality. However, this bound is unlikely to be sharp, compare e.g.
[
6
]
and
[
12
]
, so we do not spell out this result in detail.

More importantly, our results can in particular be helpful for the Monte-Carlo pricing of (path-dependent) European options, since they allow to control the bias:

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
\nu>1
,
\epsilon>0
and
(\hat{x}_{t_{k}})_{k\in\{0,\ldots,N\}}
as in Theorem
1.1
. Moreover, let

be a measurable mapping for which

such that

for all measurable

. Finally, set

with

.
Then we have

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\,\,N^{1/2-\epsilon}\,\big|\mathbb{E}\left[G(X)\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[G\left(\hat{x}^{pc,N}\right)\right]\big|=0.

Examples for

Lemma 2.1

The next auxiliary result on the smoothness of the CIR process and the log-Heston-SDE is also well known:

Lemma 2.2
.

Let

\sup_{0\leq s<t\leq T}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{|V_{t}-V_{s}|^{p}}{|t-s|^{p/2}}\right]+\sup_{0\leq s<t\leq T}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{|X_{t}-X_{s}|^{p}}{|t-s|^{p/2}}\right]<\infty.

Let
p\geq 2

\left|x+y\right|^{p}\leq 2^{p-1}\left(|x|^{p}+|y|^{p}\right)

\left|x+y+z\right|^{p}\leq 3^{p-1}\left(|x|^{p}+|y|^{p}+|z|^{p}\right)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq s<t\leq T}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{|V_{t}-V_{s}|^{p}}{|t-s|^{p/2}}\right]

\displaystyle=\sup_{0\leq s<t\leq T}\frac{1}{|t-s|^{p/2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{s}^{t}\kappa(\theta-V_{u})du+\sigma\int_{s}^{t}\sqrt{V_{u}}dW_{u}\right|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left|v_{0}+\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\kappa(\theta-f_{2}(\bar{v}_{\eta(s)}))ds+\sigma\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\sqrt{f_{3}(\bar{v}_{\eta(s)})}dW_{s}\right|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\left(v_{0}^{p}+\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\kappa(\theta-f_{2}(\bar{v}_{\eta(s)}))ds\right|^{p}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\sigma\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\sqrt{f_{3}(\bar{v}_{\eta(s)})}dW_{s}\right|^{p}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\left(1+\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\kappa(\theta-f_{2}(\bar{v}_{\eta(s)}))ds\right|^{p}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\sigma^{2}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}(1+|\bar{v}_{\eta(s)}|)^{2}ds\right|^{p/2}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\left(1+\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}|\bar{v}_{\eta(s)}|^{p}ds\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}|\bar{v}_{\eta(s)}|^{2}ds\right|^{p/2}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\left(1+\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}|\bar{v}_{\eta(s)}|^{p}ds\right]\right)

by applications of the Hölder and the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality. Therefore, we have shown that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|\bar{v}_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}|^{p}\right]\leq C_{p}\left(1+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[|\bar{v}_{\eta(s)\wedge\tau_{n}}|^{p}\right]ds\right)

and we consequently obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\mathbb{E}\left[|\bar{v}_{s\wedge\tau_{n}}|^{p}\right]\leq C_{p}\left(1+\int_{0}^{t}\sup_{u\in[0,s]}\mathbb{E}\left[|\bar{v}_{u\wedge\tau_{n}}|^{p}\right]ds\right).

The Gronwall inequality now yields

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\left[|\bar{v}_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}|^{p}\right]\leq C_{p},

where the constant
C_{p}>0
does in particular not depend on
n
. Taking the limit
n\rightarrow\infty
, we obtain (
11
). Due to

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\bar{v}_{t}|^{p}\leq C_{p}\left(1+\int_{0}^{T}\left|\bar{v}_{\eta(s)}\right|^{p}ds+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\sigma\sqrt{f_{3}(\bar{v}_{\eta(s)})}dW_{s}\right|^{p}\right),

the assertion now follows from the properties of
f_{3}
, an application of the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality and Equation (
11
).
∎

\sup_{u\in[0,t]}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|e_{u}\right|\right]\leq C_{\alpha}(\Delta t)^{\alpha-1/2}+2\kappa\int_{0}^{t}\sup_{v\in[0,u]}\mathbb{E}\left[|e_{v}|\right]du

and the assertion follows by choosing
\alpha=1-\varepsilon
and an application of Gronwall’s lemma.

∎

3.2
Error analysis - Case II and
\mathbf{\nu>1}

In this section, we will analyze both schemes from (
12
) under the condition
\nu>1
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\hat{v}
be given by (
12
) and
\nu>1
.
Then, for all
\varepsilon>0
there exists a constant
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|V_{t}-\hat{v}_{t}\right|\right]\leq C_{\varepsilon}\left(\Delta t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}.

Proof.

The proof is very similar to the proof of Theorem
3.1
. Differences are only due to the additional terms in the expansion of the schemes and we will give the required additional steps in the following. For
e_{t}=V_{t}-\hat{v}_{t}
we have

\displaystyle e_{t}=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\kappa\left(V_{s}-\hat{v}_{\eta(s)}^{\star}\right)ds+\sigma\int_{0}^{t}\left(\sqrt{V_{s}}-\sqrt{\hat{v}_{\eta(s)}^{\star}}\right)dW_{s}

(23)

\displaystyle+2c^{\star}\sigma\int_{0}^{t}\mathbbm{1}_{\{z_{s}^{\star}\leq 0\}}\sqrt{\hat{v}_{\eta(s)}^{\star}}dW_{s}+2c^{\star}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbbm{1}_{\{z_{s}^{\star}\leq 0\}}\kappa\left(\theta-\hat{v}_{\eta(s)}^{\star}\right)ds

\displaystyle-c^{\star}L_{t}^{0}(z^{\star}).

(i) The Tanaka-Meyer formula yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|e_{t}\right|\right]=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\operatorname{sign}(e_{u})de_{u}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[L^{0}_{t}(e)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\kappa\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\operatorname{sign}(e_{u})(V_{u}-\hat{v}^{\star}_{\eta(u)})du\right]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\left[\sigma\int_{0}^{t}\operatorname{sign}(e_{u})\left(\sqrt{V_{u}}-\sqrt{\hat{v}^{\star}_{\eta(u))}}\right)dW_{u}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[L^{0}_{t}(e)\right]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\left[2c^{\star}\sigma\int_{0}^{t}\operatorname{sign}(e_{u})\mathbbm{1}_{\{z_{u}^{\star}\leq 0\}}\sqrt{\hat{v}_{\eta(u)}^{\star}}dW_{u}\right]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\left[2c^{\star}\int_{0}^{t}\operatorname{sign}(e_{u})\mathbbm{1}_{\{z_{u}^{\star}\leq 0\}}\kappa\left(\theta-\hat{v}_{\eta(u)}^{\star}\right)du-c^{\star}\int_{0}^{t}\operatorname{sign}(e_{u})dL^{0}_{u}(z^{\star})\right].

However, Lemma
2.8
and the martingale property of Itō integrals imply

\mathbb{E}\left[2c^{\star}\sigma\int_{0}^{t}\operatorname{sign}(e_{u})\mathbbm{1}_{\{z_{u}^{\star}\leq 0\}}\sqrt{\hat{v}_{\eta(u)}^{\star}}dW_{u}\right]=0

and Lemma
2.11
gives

\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\operatorname{sign}(e_{u})\left(\mathbbm{1}_{\{z_{u}^{\star}\leq 0\}}2\kappa(\theta-\hat{v}_{\eta(u)}^{\star})du-dL^{0}_{u}(z^{\star})\right)\right]\right|\leq C_{\varepsilon}\left(\Delta t\right)^{\nu\frac{1-\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}}

by choosing
\beta=\varepsilon
.
Thus, we have

\sup_{u\in[0,t]}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|e_{u}\right|\right]\leq C(\Delta t)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\kappa\int_{0}^{t}\sup_{v\in[0,u]}\mathbb{E}\left[|e_{v}|\right]du+\mathbb{E}\left[L^{0}_{t}(e)\right]

(24)

as in the first step of the previous proof by choosing
\varepsilon
appropriately and exploiting that
\nu>1
.

(ii) In the same way Lemma
2.11
and Lemma
2.8
also yield

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}Y_{s}de_{s}\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq C(\Delta t)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\kappa\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[|e_{u}|\right]du.

Finally, we have

\displaystyle\langle e\rangle_{t}=\sigma^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\sqrt{V_{s}}-\sqrt{\hat{v}^{\star}_{\eta(s)}}+2c^{\star}\mathbbm{1}_{\{z_{s}^{\star}\leq 0\}}\sqrt{\hat{v}_{\eta(s)}^{\star}}\right)^{2}ds.

But again Lemma
2.11
with
\beta=\varepsilon
gives that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\delta}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\mathbbm{1}_{\{e_{s}>\delta\}}e^{1-\frac{e_{s}}{\delta}}d\langle e\rangle_{s}\right]\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\delta}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\mathbbm{1}_{\{e_{s}>\delta\}}e^{1-\frac{e_{s}}{\delta}}\left(\sqrt{V}_{s}-\sqrt{\hat{v}_{\eta(s)}}\right)^{2}\right]ds+C_{\varepsilon}\frac{(\Delta t)^{\nu\frac{1-\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}}}{\delta}

and proceeding as in the previous proof we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[L^{0}_{t}(e)\right]\leq 4\delta+C(\Delta t)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+\kappa\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[|e_{u}|\right]du+C_{\alpha}\delta^{2\alpha-1}+C\frac{\Delta t}{\delta}+C_{\varepsilon}\frac{(\Delta t)^{\nu\frac{1-\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}}}{\delta}.

(iii) Setting
\delta=(\Delta t)^{1/2}
and using
\nu>1
now yield

\mathbb{E}\left[L^{0}_{t}(e)\right]\leq\kappa\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[|e_{u}|\right]du+C_{\alpha}(\Delta t)^{\alpha-1/2}+C_{\varepsilon}(\Delta t)^{\frac{1-\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}-1/2}.

Combining this with (
24
) gives

\sup_{u\in[0,t]}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|e_{u}\right|\right]\leq C_{\alpha}(\Delta t)^{\alpha-1/2}+C_{\varepsilon}(\Delta t)^{\frac{1-\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}-1/2}+2\kappa\int_{0}^{t}\sup_{v\in[0,u]}\mathbb{E}\left[|e_{v}|\right]du.

Choosing
\alpha=1-\varepsilon
and
observing that
\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon\geq\frac{1-\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{2}
for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)

an application of Gronwall’s lemma yields then

\sup_{u\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|e_{u}\right|\right]\leq C_{\varepsilon}(\Delta t)^{\frac{1-\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}-1/2}

and the assertion follows by choosing
\varepsilon
appropriately.

∎

3.3
Error analysis - Case I and
\mathbf{\nu\leq 1}

Now we will study again the discretization from (
8
) but under the condition
\nu\leq 1
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\bar{v}
be given by (
8
) and
\nu\leq 1
.
Then, for all
\varepsilon>0
there exists a constant
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|V_{t}-\bar{v}_{t}\right|\right]\leq C_{\varepsilon}\left(\Delta t\right)^{\frac{\nu}{2}-\varepsilon}.

Proof.

Define again
e=(e_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
by
e_{t}=V_{t}-\bar{v}_{t}
.

(i) Proceeding as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
we obtain

\sup_{u\in[0,t]}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|e_{u}\right|\right]\leq C(\Delta t)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\kappa\int_{0}^{t}\sup_{v\in[0,u]}\mathbb{E}\left[|e_{v}|\right]du+\mathbb{E}\left[L^{0}_{t}(e)\right]

(25)

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[L^{0}_{t}(e)\right]\leq 4\delta

\displaystyle+C(\Delta t)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\kappa\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[|e_{u}|\right]du+\frac{1}{\delta}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\mathbbm{1}_{\{e_{s}>\delta\}}e^{1-\frac{e_{s}}{\delta}}d\langle e\rangle_{s}\right]

(26)

with

\langle e\rangle_{t}=\sigma^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\sqrt{V_{s}}-\sqrt{f_{3}(\bar{v}_{\eta(s)})}\right)^{2}ds.

(ii) For the remaining term in (
26
) we apply Lemma
2.4
with
\lambda=1-\nu(1-\zeta)
for
\zeta\in(0,1)
and Lemma
2.1
, Lemma
2.7
, Hölder’s and Minkowski’s inequality as well as the properties of
f_{3}
to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\delta}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\mathbbm{1}_{\{e_{s}>\delta\}}e^{1-\frac{e_{s}}{\delta}}d\langle e\rangle_{s}\right]

(27)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{\zeta}}{\delta}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\mathbbm{1}_{\{e_{s}>\delta\}}e^{1-\frac{e_{s}}{\delta}}\ \frac{\left|V_{s}-\bar{v}_{s}\right|^{1+\nu(1-\zeta)}}{{V_{s}}^{\nu(1-\zeta)}}ds\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{C_{\zeta}}{\delta}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\mathbbm{1}_{\{e_{s}>\delta\}}e^{1-\frac{e_{s}}{\delta}}\frac{\left|\bar{v}_{s}-\bar{v}_{\eta(s)}\right|^{1+\nu(1-\zeta)}}{V_{s}^{\nu(1-\zeta)}}ds\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{\zeta}}{\delta}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\mathbbm{1}_{\{e_{s}>\delta\}}e^{1-\frac{e_{s}}{\delta}}\ \frac{\left|e_{s}\right|^{1+\nu(1-\zeta)}}{V_{s}^{\nu(1-\zeta)}}ds\right]

\displaystyle+C_{\zeta}\frac{\left(\Delta t\right)^{(1+\nu(1-\zeta))/2}}{\delta}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{V_{s}^{\nu(1-\zeta^{2})}}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{1+\zeta}}ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{\zeta}}{\delta}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\mathbbm{1}_{\{e_{s}>\delta\}}e^{1-\frac{e_{s}}{\delta}}\frac{\left|e_{s}\right|^{1+\nu(1-\zeta)}}{V_{s}^{\nu(1-\zeta)}}ds\right]

\displaystyle+C_{\zeta}\left(\frac{\left(\Delta t\right)^{(1+\nu(1-\zeta))/2}}{\delta}\right).

Now let again
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Since

\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\left[|e_{s}|^{p}\right]\leq C_{p}

for all
p\geq 1
due to Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
2.7
, we have that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\delta}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\mathbbm{1}_{\{e_{s}>\delta\}}e^{1-\frac{e_{s}}{\delta}}\frac{\left|e_{s}\right|^{1+\nu(1-\zeta)}}{V_{s}^{\nu(1-\zeta)}}ds\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\delta}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\mathbbm{1}_{\{e_{s}\in(\delta,\delta^{\alpha})\}}e^{1-\frac{e_{s}}{\delta}}\frac{\left|e_{s}\right|^{1+\nu(1-\zeta)}}{V_{s}^{\nu(1-\zeta)}}ds\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\delta}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\mathbbm{1}_{\{e_{s}\geq\delta^{\alpha}\}}e^{1-\frac{e_{s}}{\delta}}\frac{\left|e_{s}\right|^{{1+\nu(1-\zeta)}}}{V_{s}^{\nu(1-\zeta)}}ds\right]

\displaystyle\leq\delta^{({1+\nu(1-\zeta)})\alpha-1}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{V_{s}^{\nu(1-\zeta)}}\right]ds

\displaystyle+C\frac{e^{-\delta^{\alpha-1}}}{\delta}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{V_{s}^{\nu(1-\zeta^{2})}}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{1+\zeta}}ds

\displaystyle\leq C_{\zeta,\alpha}\delta^{(1+\nu(1-\zeta))\alpha-1}

by another application of Hölder’s inequality, Lemma
2.1
and
\limsup_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\frac{e^{-\delta^{\alpha-1}}}{\delta^{2\alpha}}=0
.

Summarizing the previous steps we have shown that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[L^{0}_{t}(e)\right]\leq 4\delta

\displaystyle+C_{\zeta}\frac{\left(\Delta t\right)^{(1+\nu(1-\zeta))/2}}{\delta}+C_{\zeta,\alpha}\delta^{(1+\nu(1-\zeta))\alpha-1}

\displaystyle+\kappa\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[|e_{u}|\right]du+C(\Delta t)^{1/2}.

Setting
\delta=(\Delta t)^{1/2}
and
\alpha=1-\zeta
gives

\mathbb{E}\left[L^{0}_{t}(e)\right]\leq\kappa\int_{0}^{t}\sup_{v\in[0,u]}\mathbb{E}\left[|e_{v}|\right]du+C_{\zeta}\left(\Delta t\right)^{\nu(1-\zeta)/2}+C_{\zeta}(\Delta t)^{(\nu(1-\zeta)^{2}-\zeta)/2}.

Combining this with (
25
) yields

\sup_{u\in[0,t]}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|e_{u}\right|\right]\leq C_{\zeta}(\Delta t)^{(\nu(1-\zeta)^{2}-\zeta)/2}+2\kappa\int_{0}^{t}\sup_{v\in[0,u]}\mathbb{E}\left[|e_{v}|\right]du

and the assertion follows by choosing
\zeta
sufficiently small and an application of Gronwall’s lemma.

∎

4
Log-Heston model: convergence rates

In this section, we will in particular show that the results from Theorem
3.1
, Theorem
3.2
and Proposition
3.3
carry over to a discretization of the log-Heston model, where the log-price process is discretized with the Euler scheme (
14
), that is

\displaystyle\hat{x}_{t}=\hat{x}_{\eta(t)}

\displaystyle+\left(\mu-\frac{1}{2}\hat{v}_{\eta(t)}\right)(t-\eta(t))+\sqrt{\hat{v}_{\eta(t)}}\left(U_{t}-U_{\eta(t)}\right)

(28)

with

U_{t}=\rho W_{t}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}B_{t},\qquad t\in[0,T].

Recall that our first set of Euler schemes is given by

\displaystyle\bar{v}_{t}

\displaystyle=\bar{v}_{\eta(t)}+\int_{\eta(t)}^{t}\kappa(\theta-f_{2}(\bar{v}_{\eta(s)}))ds+\sigma\int_{\eta(t)}^{t}\sqrt{f_{3}(\bar{v}_{\eta(s)})}dW_{s},

(29)

\displaystyle\hat{v}_{t}

\displaystyle=f_{3}\left(\bar{v}_{t}\right),

where

\displaystyle f_{2}\in\{{\tt id,abs,sym}\},\qquad f_{3}\in\{{\tt abs,sym}\},

(30)

while SE and AE can be expressed as

\displaystyle\hat{v}^{\star}_{t}=

\displaystyle\hat{v}_{\eta(t)}^{\star}+\int_{\eta(t)}^{t}\kappa\left(\theta-\hat{v}_{\eta(s)}^{\star}\right)ds+\sigma\int_{\eta(t)}^{t}\sqrt{\hat{v}_{\eta(s)}^{\star}}dW_{s}

(31)

\displaystyle-2c^{\star}\sigma\int_{\eta(t)}^{t}\mathbbm{1}_{\{z_{s}^{\star}\leq 0\}}\sqrt{\hat{v}_{\eta(s)}^{\star}}dW_{s}-2c^{\star}\int_{\eta(t)}^{t}\mathbbm{1}_{\{z_{s}^{\star}\leq 0\}}\kappa\left(\theta-\hat{v}_{\eta(s)}^{\star}\right)ds

\displaystyle+c^{\star}\left(L_{t}^{0}(z^{\star})-L_{\eta(t)}^{0}(z^{\star})\right)

where
c^{sym}=1
and
c^{abs}=\frac{1}{2}
.

4.1
Error analysis - Case I

The key ingredient here and also for the second case is the observation that two continuous martingales
M=(M_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}

and
\tilde{M}=(\tilde{M}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
, whose quadratic variation coincides, have
equivalent moments for their supremum norm.
This directly follows from Proposition

2.5
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(\hat{x},\hat{v})
be given by (
28
), (
29
) and (
30
). Then, for all
\epsilon>0
there exists a constant
C_{\epsilon}>0
such that

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\left|X_{t}-\hat{x}_{t}\right|\right]\leq C_{\epsilon}\left(\Delta t\right)^{\frac{\min\{1,\nu\}}{2}-\epsilon}.

Proof.

(i) Without loss of generality, we can assume
\mu=0
. We have to analyze

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\left|X_{t}-\hat{x}_{t}\right|\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\hat{v}_{\eta(s)}-V_{s}\right)ds+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\sqrt{V_{s}}-\sqrt{\hat{v}_{\eta(s)}}\right)dU_{s}\right|\right].

Using Theorem
3.1
, Proposition
3.3
and Lemma
2.7
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\left|X_{t}-\hat{x}_{t}\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|V_{s}-\hat{v}_{s}\right|\right]ds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\hat{v}_{s}-\hat{v}_{\eta(s)}\right|\right]ds+\mathbb{E}\left[\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\left|M_{t}\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varepsilon}\left(\Delta t\right)^{\frac{\min\{1,\nu\}}{2}-\varepsilon}+\mathbb{E}\left[\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\left|M_{t}\right|\right],

(32)

where

M_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\left(\sqrt{V_{s}}-\sqrt{\hat{v}_{\eta(s)}}\right)dU_{s}.

(ii) Let

\tilde{M}_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\left(\sqrt{V_{s}}-\sqrt{\hat{v}_{\eta(s)}}\right)dW_{s},\qquad t\in[0,T].

Clearly, we have

\langle M\rangle_{t}=\langle\tilde{M}\rangle_{t},\qquad t\in[0,T],

and so Proposition
2.5
yields

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\left|M_{t}\right|\right]\leq K_{1/2}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\langle M\rangle_{T}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]=K_{1/2}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\langle\tilde{M}\rangle_{T}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]\leq\frac{K_{1/2}}{k_{1/2}}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}|\tilde{M}_{t}|\right].

(33)

Now, the Lyapunov inequality and an application of Doob’s maximal inequality, i.e. Proposition
2.6
, give

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\left|M_{t}\right|\right]\leq\frac{K_{1/2}(1+\beta)}{k_{1/2}\beta}\,\left(\mathbb{E}\left[|\tilde{M}_{T}|^{1+\beta}\right]\right)^{1/(1+\beta)}

(34)

for
\beta>0
.

Using (
29
) and the SDE for the CIR process we have

\tilde{M}_{T}=\frac{1}{\sigma}\left(V_{T}-\overline{v}_{T}+\kappa\int_{0}^{T}\left(V_{s}-f_{2}(\overline{v}_{\eta(s)})\right)ds\right).

Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|\tilde{M}_{T}|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sigma}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left|V_{T}-\overline{v}_{T}\right|\right]+\kappa\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|V_{s}-\overline{v}_{s}\right|\right]ds+\kappa\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\overline{v}_{s}-\overline{v}_{\eta(s)}\right|\right]ds\right)

(35)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varepsilon}\left(\Delta t\right)^{\frac{\min\{1,\nu\}}{2}-\varepsilon},

where we used Theorem
3.1
, Proposition
3.3
, Lemma
2.7
and the properties of
f_{2}
.
Moreover, for all
p\geq 1
there exists a constant
C_{p}>0
such that

\mathbb{E}\left[|\tilde{M}_{T}|^{p}\right]\leq C_{p}

due to Lemma
2.7
and Lemma
2.1
.
Thus, a standard application of Hölder’s inequality yields

\mathbb{E}\left[|\tilde{M}_{T}|^{1+\beta}\right]\leq C_{\beta}\,\left(\mathbb{E}\left[|\tilde{M}_{T}|\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{1+\beta}},

which in turn together with (
34
) and (
35
) gives

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\left|M_{t}\right|\right]\leq C_{\beta,\varepsilon}\left(\Delta t\right)^{\left(\frac{\min\{1,\nu\}}{2}-\varepsilon\right)\frac{1}{(1+\beta)^{2}}}.

(36)

(iii) The assertion follows now from (
32
) and (
36
)
by choosing
\varepsilon
and
\beta
sufficiently small.
∎

4.4
Proof of Proposition 1.3

Recall that
\Delta t=\frac{T}{N}
. The Lipschitz continuity of
G
implies that

\left|\mathbb{E}\left[G\left(X^{pc,N}\right)\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[G\left(\hat{x}^{pc,N}\right)\right]\right|\leq L_{G}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X_{t}-\hat{x}_{t}|\right],

where we have set
X^{pc,N}_{t}=X_{\eta(t)}
with
\eta(t)=\max\{k\in\{0,...,N\}:t_{k}\leq t\}\Delta t
.
Now Theorem
4.1
and Theorem
4.2
, respectively, yield that

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\,\,N^{1/2-\epsilon}\,\big|\mathbb{E}\left[G\left(X^{pc,N}\right)\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[G\left(\hat{x}^{pc,N}\right)\right]\big|=0

for
\nu>1
.
Moreover, the Lipschitz continuity of
G
also implies that

\big|\mathbb{E}\left[G(X)\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[G\left(X^{pc,N}\right)\right]\big|\leq L_{G}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X_{t}-X_{\eta(t)}|\right].

Finally, recall that due to Lemma
2.2
we have that

\sup_{0\leq s<t\leq T}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{|X_{t}-X_{s}|^{p}}{|t-s|^{p/2}}\right]<\infty

for all
p\geq 1
. Thus, the classical Garsia-Rodemich-Rumsey Lemma, see e.g. Equation A.10 with
m=\gamma(\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon)
and
\gamma>\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
in Appendix A.3 in
[
33
]
, implies that

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leq s<t\leq T}\frac{|X_{t}-X_{s}|}{\,|t-s|^{\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}}\right]<\infty

for all
\varepsilon>0
.
Therefore, we obtain

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\,\,N^{1/2-\epsilon}\,\big|\mathbb{E}\left[G(X)\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[G\left(X^{pc,N}\right)\right]\big|=0,

which concludes the proof of Proposition 1.3.

. First, we will describe the design of the numerical experiments. We would like to estimate the rate of the decay of the errors

\displaystyle e_{v}(N)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left|V_{T}-\hat{v}_{t_{N}}^{(N)}\right|\right],\qquad e_{x}(N)=\mathbb{E}\left[\left|X_{T}-\hat{x}_{t_{N}}^{(N)}\right|\right],

for the numerical scheme
\hat{v}^{(N)},\hat{x}^{(N)}
with step size
\Delta t=\frac{T}{N}
. Since we cannot compute these quantities exactly, we approximate their decay, see e.g.
[
1
]
, by calculating

\displaystyle{\tt err}_{v}(N)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\left|\left(\hat{v}_{t_{N}}^{(N)}-\hat{v}_{t_{2N}}^{(2N)}\right)^{(i)}\right|,\qquad{\tt err}_{x}(N)=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\left|\left(\hat{x}_{t_{N}}^{(N)}-\hat{x}_{t_{2N}}^{(2N)}\right)^{(i)}\right|,

where
M
is the number of Monte Carlo repetitions and
(\hat{v}_{t_{N}}^{(N)}-\hat{v}_{t_{2N}}^{(2N)})^{(i)},i=1,...,M
, are iid copies of
\hat{v}_{t_{N}}^{(N)}-\hat{v}_{t_{2N}}^{(2N)}
. The same holds for
(\hat{x}_{t_{N}}^{(N)}-\hat{x}_{t_{2N}}^{(2N)})^{(i)},i=1,...,M
. In our simulations, we chose
M=10^{5}
and
N\in\{2^{1},...,2^{15}\}
. To cover a wide range of different Feller indices, we will perform numerical simulations with five different parameter sets. We always choose
T=1
and
s_{0}=100
. The other parameters can be found in Table

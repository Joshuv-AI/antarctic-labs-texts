Abstract
An initial-boundary value problem of subdiffusion type is considered; the temporal component of the differential operator has the form ∑ i = 1 ℓ q i ​ ( t ) ​ D t α i ​ u ​ ( x , t ) \sum_{i=1}^{\ell}q_{i}(t)\,D_{t}^{\alpha_{i}}u(x,t) , where the q i q_{i} are continuous functions, each D t α i D_{t}^{\alpha_{i}} is a Caputo derivative, and the α i \alpha_{i} lie in ( 0 , 1 ] (0,1] . Maximum/comparison principles for this problem are proved under weak hypotheses. A new positivity result for the multinomial Mittag-Leffler function is derived. A posteriori error bounds are obtained in L 2 ​ ( Ω ) L_{2}(\Omega) and L ∞ ​ ( Ω ) L_{\infty}(\Omega) , where the spatial domain Ω \Omega lies in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} with d ∈ { 1 , 2 , 3 } d\in\{1,2,3\} . An adaptive algorithm based on this theory is tested extensively and shown to yield accurate numerical solutions on the meshes generated by the algorithm.

Introduction

The numerical solution of
fractional
differential equations (FDEs) is currently the subject of much research (see for example
[
12
,
23
]
), since such equations model many physical processes but their exact solution is generally impossible. Of course this is also true for classical integer-order differential equations, where mesh-adaptive numerical methods based on a posteriori error analyses have played a significant role for many years. Methods of this type have very general usefulness since they require no knowledge of the properties of the unknown solution to the problem. But for FDEs, there has been little progress in theory-based adaptive numerical methods; their development has been impeded by the absence of a satisfactory a posteriori theory for their error analysis.

As it is often difficult to analyse the regularity and other fundamental properties of the unknown solutions to FDEs, it can be impossible to give any rigorous a priori analysis error analysis of numerical methods for their solution. This makes it even more desirable to devise an a posteriori error analysis that does not require any information about the unknown solution.

Recently a new and very promising a posteriori error estimation methodology for FDEs appeared in
[
15
]
, which considered initial-value and initial-boundary value time-fractional subdiffusion problems whose differential equations contained a single temporal derivative of fractional order. It is clearly desirable to extend this theory to time-fractional FDEs containing several fractional derivatives, as these offer more powerful modelling capabilities. Our primary aim in the current paper is to develop the a posteriori theory for this extension and to show experimentally that an adaptive algorithm based on our theory is able to compute accurate numerical solutions to problems whose solutions have singularities (as is usually the case with FDEs). It should be noted that these accurate solutions are computed on nonuniform meshes that are constructed automatically by the algorithm — the user does not have to provide any special mesh, nor input any attributes of the unknown solution.

The relationship between our paper, which studies a multiterm fractional derivative operator, and
[
15
]
, where only a single fractional derivative appears, is the following.
Section
3
below points out similarities between Lemma
3.1
, Theorem
3.2
and Corollary
3.3
and results from [15]; but while Corollary
3.4
is analogous to the second bound in
[

, Corollary 2.4]
, the proof of Corollary

is much deeper since it involves hypergeometric functions whereas
[

Outside Section

— see Theorem

The paper is structured as follows. Section

1.1
The multiterm time-fractional subdiffusion problem

We shall study the multiterm time-fractional subdiffusion problem

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\bigl[q_{i}(t)\,D_{t}^{\alpha_{i}}u(x,t)\bigr]+{\mathcal{L}}u(x,t)

\displaystyle=f(x,t)\quad\text{for}\;\;(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T],

(1.1a)

with initial and boundary conditions

\displaystyle u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\quad\text{for}\;\;x\in\Omega,\quad u(x,t)

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{for}\;\;x\in\partial\Omega\;\;\text{and}\;\;0<t\leq T.

(1.1b)

Here

\ell
be a positive integer, the constants
\alpha_{i}
(for
i=1,2,\dots,\ell
) satisfy

0<\alpha_{\ell}<...<\alpha_{2}<\alpha_{1}\leq 1,

(1.2)

while each
q_{i}\in C[0,T]
with

\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}q_{i}(t)>0\quad\text{and}\quad q_{i}(t)\geq 0,\;\;i=1,\ldots,\ell,\quad\text{for}\;\;t\in[0,T].

(1.3)

This problem is posed in a bounded Lipschitz domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
(where
d\in\{1,2,3\}
), and involves
a spatial linear second-order elliptic operator
{\mathcal{L}}
.
Each Caputo temporal fractional derivative
D_{t}^{\alpha_{i}}
is defined
[
7
]
for
0<\alpha_{i}<1
and
t>0
by

D_{t}^{\alpha_{i}}u:=J_{t}^{1-\alpha_{i}}(\partial_{t}u),\qquad J_{t}^{1-\alpha_{i}}y(\cdot,t):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha_{i})}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha_{i}}\,y(\cdot,s)\,ds,

(1.4)

where
\Gamma(\cdot)
is the Gamma function, and
\partial_{s}
denotes the partial derivative in
s
.
From
[
7
, Theorem 2.20 and Lemma 3.4]
it follows that
\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow 1^{-}}D_{t}^{\alpha}u(x,t)=\partial_{t}u(x,t)
for each
(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T]
when
u(x,\cdot)\in C^{1}[0,T]
, so for
\alpha_{1}=1
we take
D_{t}^{\alpha_{1}}u=D_{t}^{1}u:=\partial_{t}u
.

.

In the case where each

Nonnegative solutions of certain initial-value problems

Our a posteriori analysis will rely on the property that the solutions of certain multiterm fractional initial-value problems are nonnegative; we derive this result in this section after presenting a reformulation of the definition (
1.4
) of the fractional derivative
D_{t}^{\alpha_{i}}y(\cdot,t)
that can be applied to a more general class of functions.

For simplicity, in this section we write
y(t)
instead of
y(x,t)
since the dependence on
x
is irrelevant here.

2.1
Function regularity and reformulated Caputo derivative

In (
1.4
) one can integrate by parts to reformulate the definition of
D_{t}^{\alpha_{i}}y(t)
for
\alpha_{i}<1
as

\Gamma({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}1-\alpha_{i}})\,D_{t}^{\alpha_{i}}y(t)=t^{-\alpha_{i}}\left[y(t)-y(0)\right]+\int_{0}^{t}\!\alpha_{i}(t-s)^{-\alpha_{i}-1}\,\left[y(t)-y(s)\right]\,ds

(2.1)

for
0<t\leq T
.
This reformulation appeared already in
[
15
, eq. (2.4)]
, and in, e.g.,
[
4
, Lemma 3.1]
,
[
11
, Lemma 2.10]
,
[
18
, Proof of Theorem 1]
, and
[
25
, Theorem 5.2]
. We will show that it has the advantage that it permits the use of less smooth functions

than (
1.4

3.1
below.

Recall that (
2.1
) was obtained from (
1.4
) by integration by parts. From the proof of the integration by parts formula, one sees that this calculation is valid if for each
t^{\prime}\in[0,t)
the function
\psi(t;\cdot)
defined by
\psi(t;s):=(t-s)^{-\alpha_{i}}[y(s)-y(t)]
is absolutely continuous on
[0,t^{\prime}]
and satisfies
\lim_{t^{\prime}\to t^{-}}\psi(t;t^{\prime})=0
, because one can integrate by parts
\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}(t-s)^{-\alpha_{i}}\,y^{\prime}(s)\,ds
, then take
\lim_{t^{\prime}\to t^{-}}
.

For example, if
y
lies in the standard Hölder space
C^{\beta}[0,T]
for some
\beta>\alpha_{i}
, then this derivation of (
2.1
) from (
1.4
) is valid; see
[
4
, Lemma 3.1]
.

As in
[
15
]
, we consider now a more general class of functions for which the definition (
1.4
) is unsuitable but (
2.1
) can be used.

Let us assume that

y\in C[0,T]\cap W^{1,\infty}(\epsilon,t)\ \text{ for all }\epsilon,t\ \text{ satisfying }0<\epsilon<t\leq T.

(2.2)

The hypothesis that
y\in W^{1,\infty}(\epsilon,t)
is equivalent to assuming that
y
is Lipschitz continuous on each interval
[\epsilon,t]
; see
[
10
, p.154]
.

If
\alpha_{1}=1
, then we strengthen (
2.2
) by assuming that
y\in C[0,T]
and

is a left-continuous function on
(0,T]
that may have jump discontinuities; see Section

.

Fix

. The integral
\int_{0}^{t/2}\!\alpha_{i}(t-s)^{-\alpha_{i}-1}\,\left[y(t)-y(s)\right]\,ds
is defined and finite as its integrand lies in
C\left[0,\frac{1}{2}t\right]
. For
\int_{t/2}^{t}\!\alpha_{i}(t-s)^{-\alpha_{i}-1}\,\left[y(t)-y(s)\right]\,ds
, since

one has

with a constant

that depends on

but is independent of

, which implies that the integral exists and is finite. Thus for all

satisfying (
2.2
), we can define

by (
2.1
).

The next two remarks describe weakenings of the hypothesis (

) on the function

by (

).

Remark 2.1
.

One could replace

in (

) by

Remark 2.2

(initial discontinuity in

)
.

Note that

In (

2.2
The initial-value problem

Consider the initial-value problem

D_{t}^{\bar{\alpha}}w(t)+\lambda w(t)=v(t)\ \text{ for }0<t\leq T,\quad w(0)=w_{0},

(2.3)

where we assume that the parameter
\lambda\geq 0
. (We shall use the notation
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}u(x,t)}
for the solution of (
1.1
) and
w(t)
for the solution of (
2.3
).)

In the next lemma we specify hypotheses allowing, for example, the possibility that
w
is a piecewise polynomial.
Given a function
g
that has a jump discontinuity at a finite number of points in
(0,T)
but is continuous otherwise on
(0,T]
, at any point of discontinuity
\tau
we take
g(\tau)=\lim_{t\to\tau^{-}}g(t)
. That is, we regard
g
as left-continuous on
(0,T]
.

Lemma 2.3

(Comparison principle for the initial-value problem)
.

Consider (
2.3
) where
v\geq 0
may have a finite number of jump discontinuities in
(0,T)
and is left-continuous on
(0,T]
.
Suppose that
w
satisfies the regularity hypothesis (
2.2
). Define
D_{t}^{\alpha_{i}}w
by (
2.1
) if
\alpha_{i}<1
. If
\alpha_{1}=1
, suppose also that
w^{\prime}
may have jump discontinuities but is a left-continuous function on
(0,T]
. Assume that
w_{0}\geq 0
.
Then
w(t)\geq 0
for
t\in[0,T]
.

Proof.

Suppose that the result is false. Then since
w\in C[0,T]
and
w(0)\geq 0
, there exists a point
t_{0}\in(0,T]
such that
w(t_{0})<0\leq w(0)
and
w(t_{0})\leq w(t)
for all
t\in[0,T]
. From (

) one sees immediately that each
D_{t}^{\alpha_{i}}w(t_{0})<0

\alpha_{i}<1
, while if
\alpha_{i}=1
then

(consider the interval

and use the left-continuous property of

). Hence
D_{t}^{\bar{\alpha}}w(t_{0})+\lambda w(t_{0})<0\leq v(t_{0})
, so

(The case where
q_{i}(t_{0})=0
for

is exceptional, as we then get only

\tilde{w}(t):=e^{-\mu t}w(t)

∎

The following extension of Lemma

Corollary 2.4

First, recall the well-known two-parameter Mittag-Leffler function

Theorem 2.5
.

Consider (

Suppose that
w
satisfies the regularity hypothesis (

Then

w(t)\geq w_{0}\max_{j=1,\dots,\ell}E_{\alpha_{j},1}(-\lambda t^{\alpha_{j}}/\underline{q}_{j})\quad\text{for }t\in[0,T],

(2.4)

where one sets
E_{\alpha_{j},1}(-\lambda t^{\alpha_{j}}/\underline{q}_{j})\equiv 0
if
\underline{q}_{j}=0
.

Proof.

Fix

. Define the barrier function

\underline{q}_{j}D_{t}^{\alpha_{j}}B_{j}(t)+\lambda B_{j}(t)=0

B_{j}(t)=w_{0}E_{\alpha_{j},1}(-\lambda t^{\alpha_{j}}/\underline{q}_{j})

Consequently

\left(D_{t}^{\bar{\alpha}}+\lambda\right)(w-B_{j})(t)=v(t)-\left[q_{j}(t)-\underline{q}_{j}\right]D_{t}^{\alpha_{j}}B_{j}(t)-\sum_{i\neq j}q_{i}D_{t}^{\alpha_{i}}B_{j}(t)\geq 0\ \text{ for }t>0.

Remark 2.6
.

Assume the hypotheses of Theorem
2.5

Assume also that
q_{1}(0)\neq 0

q_{1}\in C^{1}[0,T]

q_{1}(0)=1

Set
\phi(t):=t^{\alpha_{1}}/\Gamma(1+\alpha_{1})
. Then

\left(D_{t}^{\bar{\alpha}}+\lambda\right)\phi(t)=q_{1}(t)+\sum_{i=2}^{\ell}\frac{q_{i}(t)t^{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{i}}}{\Gamma(1+\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{i})}+\frac{\lambda t^{\alpha_{1}}}{\Gamma(1+\alpha_{1})}=1+O(t^{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}}),

\left(D_{t}^{\bar{\alpha}}+\lambda\right)[1-\lambda\phi(t)]=O(t^{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}})

Let
\delta

\widetilde{w}_{\delta}(t):=w_{0}\,[1-\lambda\phi(t)]+[v(0)+\delta]\,\phi(t)

\widetilde{w}_{\delta}(0)=w_{0}

(D_{t}^{\bar{\alpha}}+\lambda)\,\widetilde{w}_{\delta}(t)=O(t^{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}})+[v(0)+\delta]\left[1+O(t^{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}})\right]=v(t)+\delta+O(t^{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}})

v(t)=v(0)+O(t)
. Now choose
\delta
to be a small positive constant. Then choose
\epsilon>0
such that for
t\in(0,\epsilon)
one has
|O(t^{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}})|\leq\delta
in the previous equation. Now the comparison principle (Lemma

\widetilde{w}_{-\delta}(t)\leq w(t)\leq\widetilde{w}_{\delta}(t)
for
0\leq t\leq\epsilon
.
That is,

[v(0)-\lambda w_{0}-\delta]\,\phi(t)\leq w(t)-w_{0}\leq[v(0)-\lambda w_{0}+\delta]\,\phi(t)\ \text{ for }0\leq t\leq\epsilon.

In this subsection we consider the special case where

Lemma 2.11
.

Assume that

for all

, and this solution is unique.

Proof.

Lemma

implies that any solution of (
2.3
) is unique.
To show existence of a solution we assume without loss of generality that
q_{1}(t)\equiv 1
for
t\in[0,T]
, since one can divide (
2.3
) by
q_{1}(t)
. Set
\phi(t)=w^{\prime}(t)
. Using the definition (
1.4
), write (
2.3
) as

\phi(t)+\sum_{i=2}^{\ell}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha_{i}}q_{i}(t)\phi(s)\,ds+\lambda\int_{0}^{t}\phi(s)\,ds=v(t)-\lambda w_{0}\ \text{ for }t\in[0,T].

This is a Volterra integral equation of the second kind in the unknown function

.
Observe first that any solution of (

) in

must be unique, because two distinct solutions

would yield two distinct solutions

(

) of (

).
It is well known (see, e.g.,
[
3
, Appendix A.2.2]
) that each of the operators

is a compact operator from the Banach space

to itself, and a finite sum of compact operators is also a compact operator, and we saw already that any solution of (
2.8
) is unique;
thus we can apply the Fredholm Alternative Theorem
[
3
, Theorem A.2.17]
to conclude that (

) has a solution

. Hence (
2.3

, and this solution clearly lies in

.
∎

Appendix A
A variant of Lemma
2.3

In this appendix we shall prove a result (Lemma
A.3
) that complements Lemma
2.3
. This is done by using an explicit complex contour integral formula to derive a positivity property of the multinomial Mittag-Leffler function (Lemma
A.2
) that appears to be new.

Our argument starts with the following elementary result.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
m
and
n
be nonnegative integers with
m<n
. Set
S(s)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}k_{j}s^{\gamma_{j}}
for
s\in[0,\infty)
, where
0=\gamma_{0}<\gamma_{1}<\dots<\gamma_{n}\leq 1
and
k_{j}>0
for
0\leq j\leq m
,
k_{j}<0
for
m<j\leq n
. Then the equation
S(s)=0
has a unique solution
s_{0}\in(0,\infty)
, with
S(s)>0
for
0\leq s<s_{0}
and
S(s)<0
for
s_{0}<s<\infty
.

Proof.

If
t\in(0,\infty)
, then

\displaystyle S^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}k_{j}\gamma_{j}t^{\gamma_{j}-1}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}k_{j}\gamma_{j}t^{\gamma_{j}-1}-\sum_{j=m+1}^{n}|k_{j}|\gamma_{j}t^{\gamma_{j}-1}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma_{m+1}t^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{m}k_{j}t^{\gamma_{j}}-\gamma_{m+1}t^{-1}\sum_{j=m+1}^{n}|k_{j}|t^{\gamma_{j}}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{m+1}t^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}k_{j}t^{\gamma_{j}}

\displaystyle<\gamma_{m+1}t^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{n}k_{j}t^{\gamma_{j}}=\gamma_{m+1}t^{-1}S(t).

Hence

S(t)\leq 0\ \text{ implies }S^{\prime}(t)<0.

(A.1)

One has
S(0)=k_{0}>0
. As
s\to\infty
, the term
k_{n}s^{\gamma_{n}}
in
S(s)
will dominate; it follows that
S(s)<0
for all sufficiently large
s
. Hence
S(s)=0
has at least one solution
s_{0}
in
(0,\infty)
.

From (
A.1
) it follows that
S^{\prime}(s_{0})<0
, so
S(s)<0
on some interval
(s_{0},s_{0}+\delta)
. Now (
A.1
) ensures that
S
can never reach a minimum on
(s_{0},\infty)
, which implies that
S(s)<0
for
s\in(s_{0},\infty)
. Thus
s_{0}
is the unique solution of
S(s)=0
.
∎

We now prove a new positivity property of the multinomial Mittag-Leffler function that
is related to
Lemma
2.9
; this proof is in the spirit of classical analyses of Mittag-Leffler functions. The argument used is partly based on
[
21
, pp.215–216]
, where a similar result was obtained for the simpler case of the two-parameter Mittag-Leffler function
E_{\alpha,\beta}(t)
.

Lemma A.2
.

Assume that
1<\beta<1+\alpha_{1}
and
\lambda>0
. Then

\tilde{E}(t):=E_{(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{\ell},\dots,\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{3},\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}),\beta}(-\lambda t^{\alpha}_{1},-q_{\ell}t^{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{\ell}},\dots,-q_{3}t^{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{3}},-q_{2}t^{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}})>0

for all
t>0
.

Proof.

For each
t>0
, by Remark
2.8
and
[
17
, eq.(47)]
we have

\displaystyle\tilde{E}(t)

\displaystyle=E_{(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2},\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{3},\dots,\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{\ell},\alpha_{1}),\beta}(-q_{2}t^{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}},-q_{3}t^{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{3}},\dots,-q_{\ell}t^{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{\ell}},-\lambda t^{\alpha_{1}})

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{1-\beta}}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma(R_{m},-\pi,\pi)}\frac{e^{\zeta t}\zeta^{\alpha_{1}-\beta}}{\zeta^{\alpha_{1}}+\sum_{j=2}^{\ell}q_{j}\zeta^{\alpha_{j}}+\lambda}\,d\zeta,

where
r:=\max\left\{1,\left(\lambda+\sum q_{j}\right)^{1/(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})}\right\}
, and
{\gamma(R,\theta_{1},\theta_{2})}
(for
R\geq 0
and
-\pi\leq\theta_{1}\leq\theta_{2}\leq\pi
) denotes the complex-plane Hankel contour that comprises the ray
\arg\zeta=\theta_{1}
with
|\zeta|\geq R
, the arc
|\zeta|=R
with
\theta_{1}\leq\arg\zeta\leq\theta_{2}
, and the ray
\arg\zeta=\theta_{2}
with
|\zeta|\geq R
, and the contour is traversed in the direction of increasing
\arg\zeta
.

The substitution
w=\zeta^{\alpha_{1}}
gives

\tilde{E}(t)=\frac{t^{1-\beta}}{2\alpha_{1}\pi i}\int_{\gamma(r^{\alpha_{1}},-\alpha_{1}\pi,\alpha_{1}\pi)}\frac{w^{(1-\beta)/\alpha_{1}}\exp\left(tw^{1/\alpha_{1}}\right)}{w+\sum_{j=2}^{\ell}q_{j}w^{\alpha_{j}/\alpha_{1}}+\lambda}\,dw

(A.2)

Observe that if
w\in\gamma(r^{\alpha_{1}},-\alpha_{1}\pi,\alpha_{1}\pi)
, then
|\arg w|\leq\alpha_{1}\pi
independently of
r
; hence

\left|\arg\left(w+\sum_{j=2}^{\ell}q_{j}w^{\alpha_{j}/\alpha_{1}}\right)\right|\leq\max\{\alpha_{1}\pi,\alpha_{2}\pi,\dots,\alpha_{\ell}\pi\}=\alpha_{1}\pi<\pi,

so (recall that
\lambda>0
) the denominator of the integrand will not vanish if we change the value of
r
in the contour, and consequently the value of the integral will not change (by Cauchy’s integral theorem). Furthermore, we can permit
r\to 0
because
\beta<1+\alpha_{1}
ensures that the integral remains finite. Thus we can replace the contour
\gamma(r^{\alpha_{1}},-\alpha_{1}\pi,\alpha_{1}\pi)
in (
A.2
) by
\gamma(0,-\alpha_{1}\pi,\alpha_{1}\pi)
.

Next, set
w=se^{\pm i\alpha_{1}\pi}
along the ray
\arg w=\pm\alpha_{1}\pi
(choose same sign). This yields

\displaystyle\tilde{E}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{1-\beta}}{2\alpha_{1}\pi i}\left[\int_{\infty}^{0}\frac{s^{(1-\beta)/\alpha_{1}}e^{-i(1-\beta)\pi}\exp\left(ts^{1/\alpha_{1}}e^{-i\pi}\right)e^{-i\alpha_{1}\pi}}{se^{-i\alpha_{1}\pi}+\sum_{j=2}^{\ell}q_{j}s^{\alpha_{j}/\alpha_{1}}e^{-i\alpha_{j}\pi}+\lambda}\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\left.+\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{s^{(1-\beta)/\alpha_{1}}e^{i(1-\beta)\pi}\exp\left(ts^{1/\alpha_{1}}e^{i\pi}\right)e^{i\alpha_{1}\pi}}{se^{i\alpha_{1}\pi}+\sum_{j=2}^{\ell}q_{j}s^{\alpha_{j}/\alpha_{1}}e^{i\alpha_{j}\pi}+\lambda}\right]\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{1-\beta}}{2\alpha_{1}\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{(1-\beta)/\alpha_{1}}\exp\left(-ts^{1/\alpha_{1}}\right)\left[\frac{e^{i\beta\pi}}{s+\xi}-\frac{e^{-i\beta\pi}}{s+\bar{\xi}}\right]\,ds,

(A.3)

where
\xi:=\sum_{j=2}^{\ell}q_{j}s^{\alpha_{j}/\alpha_{1}}e^{i(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{j})\pi}+\lambda e^{i\alpha_{1}\pi}
and
\bar{\xi}
is its complex conjugate. Now

\displaystyle\frac{e^{i\beta\pi}}{s+\xi}-\frac{e^{-i\beta\pi}}{s+\bar{\xi}}

\displaystyle=\frac{s(e^{i\beta\pi}-e^{-i\beta\pi})+\bar{\xi}e^{i\beta\pi}-\xi e^{-i\beta\pi}}{s^{2}+\xi^{2}}=\frac{2is\sin\beta\pi+2i\Im(\bar{\xi}e^{i\beta\pi})}{s^{2}+\xi^{2}}=\frac{2iv(s)}{s^{2}+\xi^{2}}\,,

where
v(s):=s\sin\beta\pi+\sum_{j=2}^{\ell}q_{j}s^{\alpha_{j}/\alpha_{1}}\sin(\beta-\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{j})\pi+\lambda\sin(\beta-\alpha_{1})\pi
. Hence (
A.3
) becomes

\tilde{E}(t)=\frac{t^{1-\beta}}{\alpha_{1}\pi}I(t),\quad\text{where }I(t):=\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{(1-\beta)/\alpha_{1}}\exp\left(-ts^{1/\alpha_{1}}\right)\frac{v(s)}{s^{2}+\xi^{2}}\,ds.

(A.4)

Note that
v(s)
has exactly the same structure as
S(s)
in Lemma
A.1
, since
0<\alpha_{j}/\alpha_{1}<1
,
1<\beta<1+\alpha_{1}
and
\lambda>0
. Thus there exists
s_{0}>0
such that
v(s)>0
for
0<s<s_{0}
and
v(s)<0
for
s>s_{0}
. From Definition
2.7
we get
\tilde{E}(0)=1/\Gamma(\beta)>0
. By continuity we can choose
t_{0}>0
such that
\tilde{E}(t)>0
on
(0,t_{0}]
, which implies
I(t_{0})>0
. That is, recalling the properties of
s_{0}
,

\int_{0}^{s_{0}}s^{(1-\beta)/\alpha_{1}}\exp\left(-t_{0}s^{1/\alpha_{1}}\right)\frac{v(s)}{s^{2}+\xi^{2}}\,ds>\int_{s_{0}}^{\infty}s^{(1-\beta)/\alpha_{1}}\exp\left(-t_{0}s^{1/\alpha_{1}}\right)\frac{|v(s)|}{s^{2}+\xi^{2}}\,ds.

(A.5)

Then for any
t>t_{0}
, using (
A.5
) we get

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\int_{0}^{s_{0}}s^{(1-\beta)/\alpha_{1}}\exp\left(-ts^{1/\alpha_{1}}\right)\frac{v(s)}{s^{2}+\xi^{2}}\,ds

\displaystyle\geq\exp\left(-(t-t_{0})s_{0}^{1/\alpha_{1}}\right)\int_{0}^{s_{0}}s^{(1-\beta)/\alpha_{1}}\exp\left(-t_{0}s^{1/\alpha_{1}}\right)\frac{v(s)}{s^{2}+\xi^{2}}\,ds

\displaystyle>\exp\left(-(t-t_{0})s_{0}^{1/\alpha_{1}}\right)\int_{s_{0}}^{\infty}s^{(1-\beta)/\alpha_{1}}\exp\left(-t_{0}s^{1/\alpha_{1}}\right)\frac{|v(s)|}{s^{2}+\xi^{2}}\,ds

\displaystyle\geq\int_{s_{0}}^{\infty}s^{(1-\beta)/\alpha_{1}}\exp\left(-ts^{1/\alpha_{1}}\right)\frac{|v(s)|}{s^{2}+\xi^{2}}\,ds.

Now move the integral
\int_{s_{0}}^{\infty}\dots
to the left-hand side; this gives
I(t)>0
. Hence
\tilde{E}(t)>0
for
t>t_{0}
and we are done.
∎

We can now prove our variant of Lemma
2.3
.

Lemma A.3
.

Consider the homogeneous version of the initial-value problem (
2.3
):

D_{t}^{\bar{\alpha}}y(t)+\lambda y(t)=0\ \text{ for }0<t\leq T,\quad y(0)=1,

(A.6)

where the
q_{i}
are constants and
\lambda\geq 0
.
Then this problem has a solution
y
, with
y(t)\geq 0
for
t\in[0,T]
.

Proof.

If
\lambda=0
then
y(t)\equiv 1
is the unique solution of (
A.6
) by
[
17
, Theorem 4.1]
. Thus we can assume that
\lambda>0
.
From
[
19
, Theorem 6]
the solution of (
A.6
) is

y(t)=1-\lambda t^{\alpha_{1}}E_{(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2},\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{3},\dots,\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{\ell}),1+\alpha_{1}}(-\lambda t^{\alpha_{1}},-q_{2}t^{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}},-q_{3}t^{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{3}},\dots,-q_{\ell}t^{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{\ell}}).

But
[
16
, Lemma 3.1]
states that for
m\geq 1
one has

\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta_{0})}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}z_{j}E_{(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m}),\beta_{0}+\beta_{j}}(z_{1},\dots,z_{m})=E_{(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m}),\beta_{0}}(z_{1},\dots,z_{m})

for
0<\beta_{0}<2
and
0<\beta_{j}<1
(
j=1,\dots,m
) and any
z_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
. In particular this implies that

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle-\lambda t^{\alpha_{1}}E_{(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2},\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{3},\dots,\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{\ell}),1+\alpha_{1}}(-\lambda t^{\alpha_{1}},-q_{2}t^{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}},-q_{3}t^{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{3}},\dots,-q_{\ell}t^{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{\ell}})

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=2}^{\ell}q_{j}t^{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{j}}E_{(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2},\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{3},\dots,\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{\ell}),1+\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{j}}(-\lambda t^{\alpha_{1}},-q_{2}t^{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}},-q_{3}t^{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{3}},\dots,-q_{\ell}t^{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{\ell}})

\displaystyle\qquad=E_{(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2},\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{3},\dots,\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{\ell}),1}(-\lambda t^{\alpha_{1}},-q_{2}t^{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}},-q_{3}t^{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{3}},\dots,-q_{\ell}t^{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{\ell}})

Hence, using Remark
2.8
, we get

\displaystyle y(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=2}^{\ell}q_{j}t^{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{j}}E_{(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2},\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{3},\dots,\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{\ell}),1+\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{j}}(-\lambda t^{\alpha_{1}},-q_{2}t^{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}},-q_{3}t^{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{3}},\dots,-q_{\ell}t^{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{\ell}})

\displaystyle\qquad+E_{(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2},\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{3},\dots,\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{\ell}),1}(-\lambda t^{\alpha_{1}},-q_{2}t^{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}},-q_{3}t^{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{3}},\dots,-q_{\ell}t^{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{\ell}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=2}^{\ell}q_{j}t^{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{j}}E_{(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{\ell},\dots,\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{3},\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}),1+\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{j}}(-\lambda t^{\alpha_{1}},-q_{\ell}t^{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{\ell}},\dots,-q_{3}t^{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{3}},-q_{2}t^{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}})

\displaystyle\qquad+E_{(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{\ell},\dots,\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{3},\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}),1}(-\lambda t^{\alpha_{1}},-q_{\ell}t^{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{\ell}},\dots,-q_{3}t^{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{3}},-q_{2}t^{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=2}^{\ell}q_{j}{\mathcal{F}}_{(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{\ell},\dots,\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{3},\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}),1+\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{j}}(t;\lambda,q_{\ell},\dots,q_{3},q_{2})

\displaystyle\qquad+{\mathcal{F}}_{(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{\ell},\dots,\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{3},\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}),1}(t;\lambda,q_{\ell},\dots,q_{3},q_{2}).

The result now follows by applying Lemma
2.9
to the term
{\mathcal{F}}_{(\dots),1}
and Lemma
A.2
to each term
{\mathcal{F}}_{(\dots),1+\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{j}}
.
∎

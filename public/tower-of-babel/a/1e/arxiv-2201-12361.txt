Electric-magnetic duality and ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} symmetry enriched Abelian lattice gauge theory
Zhian Jia, Dagomir Kaszlikowski, and Sheng Tan

Abstract
Kitaev’s quantum double model is a lattice realization of Dijkgraaf-Witten topological quantum field theory (TQFT). Its topologically protected ground state space has broad applications for topological quantum computation and topological quantum memory. We investigate the ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} symmetry enriched generalization of the model for the Abelian group in a categorical framework and present an explicit Hamiltonian construction. This model provides a lattice realization of the ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} symmetry of the topological phase. We discuss in detail the categorical symmetry of the phase, for which the electric-magnetic (EM) duality symmetry is a special case. The aspects of symmetry defects are investigated using the G G -crossed unitary braided fusion category (UBFC). By determining the corresponding anyon condensation, the gapped boundaries and boundary-bulk duality are also investigated. In the last part, an explicit lattice realization of EM duality is discussed.



1
Introduction

Duality is one of the central topics in modern physics. It weaves together different theories such that these theories can be understood from each other’s perspective; typical examples include AdS/CFT duality
Maldacena 1999
;
Witten 1998
, Electric-magnetic duality
Montonen and Olive 1977
;
Kapustin and Witten 2007
, T-duality
Sathiapalan 1987
, and so on.
For Kitaev’s quantum double model
D(G)
based on a finite group
G

Kitaev 2003
, which can be regarded as a lattice gauge theoretic realization of Dijkgraaf-Witten topological quantum field theory (TQFT)
Dijkgraaf and Witten 1990
, there is a famous duality known as electric-magnetic (EM) duality
Buerschaper and Aguado 2009
;
Buerschaper et al. 2013a
;
Hu et al. 2018
;
Wang et al. 2020
;
Hu and Wan 2020
;
Delcamp 2021
.
When the group
G
is Abelian, the theory has EM duality.
The dual gauge group is
\hat{G}=\mathrm{Irr}(G)
whose elements are unitary irreducible representations of
G
.
However, when
G
is non-Abelian, there is no such EM duality. To remedy this problem, the models are generalized to quantum double models based on finite-dimensional semisimple Hopf algebras, for which EM duality can be realized
Buerschaper et al. 2013b
;
Buerschaper et al. 2013a
.

From a topologically ordered phase perspective, a quantum double model is a lattice realization of the
2d
non-chiral topological order
Kitaev 2003
;
Levin and Wen 2005
;
Kitaev 2006
;
Jia et al. 2023a
;
Jia et al. 2023b
.
The
2d
topologically ordered phase is mathematically characterized by a unitary modular tensor category (UMTC)
\mathcal{D}=(\mathcal{D},\oplus,\otimes,\mathds{1},\alpha,\beta,\theta)

Bakalov and Kirillov 2001
;
Turaev 2016
;
Etingof et al. 2016
, where
\alpha,\beta,\theta
are associator, braiding, and twist.
The anyons are labeled with simple objects
a\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathcal{D})
.
\operatorname{Irr}(\mathcal{D})
is a finite set, and the vacuum charge will be labeled as
\mathds{1}
(also denoted
0
when appearing in the index).
Each simple object (anyon) in
\mathcal{D}
has an antiparticle,
viz.
, there is an involution map
*:\operatorname{Irr}(\mathcal{D})\to\operatorname{Irr}(\mathcal{D})
such that
a^{**}=a
and
\mathds{1}^{*}=\mathds{1}
.
Anyons in
\mathcal{D}
can fuse:
a\times b=\sum_{c}N_{ab}^{c}c
, and the fusion is associative in the sense that
\sum_{e}N_{ab}^{e}N_{ec}^{d}=\sum_{f}N_{bc}^{f}N_{af}^{d}
. The numbers
\{N_{ab}^{c}\}
are called the fusion multiplicities and satisfy the symmetric properties:
N_{ab}^{c}=N_{ba}^{c}=N_{a^{*}b^{*}}^{c^{*}}=N_{ac^{*}}^{b^{*}}
; and
N_{ab^{*}}^{0}=\delta_{ab}
.
Anyon’s braiding is characterized by
\beta:a\otimes b\to b\otimes a
and each anyon has its topological spin
\theta_{a}
.
Each anyon
a\in\mathcal{D}
has its quantum dimension
d_{a}
for which
d_{a}=d_{a^{*}}
. From fusion process we have
d_{a}d_{b}=\sum_{c}N_{ab}^{c}d_{c}
(it is evident that
d_{a}
is the eigenvalue of the matrix
[N_{a}]_{bc}=N^{c}_{ab}
). The anyons with quantum dimension
1
are called Abelian anyons, otherwise they will be called non-Abelian anyons.
If all anyons of a phase are Abelian, the phase is called an Abelian topological phase.
The typical Abelian topological phases are Kitaev’s
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
quantum double
D(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
phase, whose topological order is given by the representation category
\mathsf{Rep}(D(\mathbb{Z}_{N}))
.
These Abelian phases can also be realized using Levin-Wen’s string-net model by choosing input unitary fusion category
\mathcal{C}
as the representation category
\textsf{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
or the category of
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector spaces
\textsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{Z}_{N}}

Levin and Wen 2005
. These different realizations are related with each other by Morita equivalence
\mathsf{Rep}(D(\mathbb{Z}_{N}))\simeq\mathcal{Z}(\textsf{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{N}))\simeq\mathcal{Z}(\textsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{Z}_{N})})

Etingof et al. 2016
, where “
\simeq
” represents braided equivalence and
\mathcal{Z}
represents taking the Drinfeld center.

In this work, we consider another kind of generalization, that is, the topological phase enriched with a symmetry group. This kind of phase will be called the symmetry enriched topological (SET) phase.
By introducing symmetry, the corresponding phase will exhibit more complicated quasi-particle excitations and mutual statistics.
The SET phases have been explored using modular extension of a unitary braided fusion category (UBFC)
Lan et al. 2017
and using
G
-crossed UBFC
Cui et al. 2016
;
Barkeshli et al. 2019
;
Bischoff et al. 2019
.
Besides their fundamental importance, the study of these SET phases and topological defects is also very crucial for topological quantum computation.
Two typical examples are:
(i) A topologically protected degeneracy appears when we cut boundaries of
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
toric code on a sphere. This topological degeneracy generates an error correcting code
Dennis et al. 2002
;
Terhal 2015
; (ii) Twist defects fix the problem of Abelian anyons of
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
toric code, converting it into a universal topological quantum computer
Nayak et al. 2008
;
Cong et al. 2017a
.

In this paper, we study the simplest SET phase: the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry enriched Abelian topological phase.
In Sec.
2
, we review the Hamiltonian of Kitaev quantum double model for abelian group and discuss its topological properties.
Then in Sec.
3
, we review the SET phase in
G
-crossed UBFC framework. The EM duality symmetry is a special case of the more general categorical symmetry. We will discuss the EM duality in detail within the framework of categorical symmetry.
One of our main contributions is given in Sec.
4
, where we study the gapped boundary of the SET phase from the perspective of anyon condensation.
The gapped boundary phase is obtained from anyon condensation of the SET phase, and the bulk phase is recovered from the boundary phase by taking relative center, and different boundary phases are Morita equivalent.
We review the existing framework of SET boundary theory, which is based on condensable algebra description, and propose a new formalism grounded on Frobenius algebra.
With the above preparation, in Sec.
5
, we present a lattice realization of the EM duality symmetry via the ribbon operator and discuss its physical properties.

[g]

\pi

The vacuum charge corresponds to

The antiparticle is given by

Z_{N}X_{N}=\omega_{N}X_{N}Z_{N}.

(6)

(7)

Z_{N}

Y^{g,h}Y^{k,l}=\omega_{N}^{hk-gl}Y^{k,l}Y^{g,h}.

(8)

The eigenstates of

|h\rangle
with corresponding eigenvalues
\omega_{N}^{h}

X_{N}

|\psi_{g}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{h\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}}\omega_{N}^{gh}|h\rangle

\omega_{N}^{-g}

Using the notations in Ref.

L_{+}^{g}|x\rangle=X_{N}^{g}|x\rangle,\quad L_{-}^{g}|x\rangle=(X^{\dagger}_{N})^{g}|x\rangle.

(10)

Z_{N}^{g}|x\rangle=\omega_{N}^{gx}|x\rangle

(Z^{\dagger}_{N})^{g}|x\rangle=\omega_{N}^{-gx}|x\rangle

Hereinafter, to avoid cluttering of equations, we will denote
X_{N}
and
Z_{N}

X
and
Z

(1/N)\sum_{h\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}}\omega_{N}^{gh}=\delta_{g,0}
,
\forall\,g\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}
.

Notice that for
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
case (toric code), one usually shifts the local terms by a constant, namely,

\displaystyle A_{v}=I+A(v)=I+X\otimes X\otimes X\otimes X,\quad B_{f}=I+B(f)=I+Z\otimes Z\otimes Z\otimes Z.

(14)

But we will take
A(v)=X\otimes X\otimes X\otimes X
and
B(f)=Z\otimes Z\otimes Z\otimes Z
as local stabilizers.
This point of view can also be generalized to the
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
case.

Proposition 1
.

The ground state space of the
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
quantum double model is given by

\mathcal{V}_{\rm GS}=\{\,|\psi\rangle\,|\,A(v)|\psi\rangle=|\psi\rangle,B(f)|\psi\rangle=|\psi\rangle,\forall\,v,f\}.

(15)

Proof.

For vertex stabilizers, it is clear that
A(v)|\psi\rangle=|\psi\rangle
implies
A_{v}|\psi\rangle=|\psi\rangle
.
The reverse direction can be obtained by taking the spectral decomposition
A(v)=\sum_{\lambda}e^{i\theta_{\lambda}}\Pi_{\lambda}
, where
\theta_{\lambda}=2\pi\lambda/N
(
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}
), and
\Pi_{\lambda}
is the projection to the corresponding eigenspace of
A(v)
.
This implies that

A_{v}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{g\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}}A(v)^{g}=\sum_{\lambda}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{g\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}}e^{ig\theta_{\lambda}}\right)\Pi_{\lambda}=\Pi_{0}.

(16)

From this we see that
A_{v}|\psi\rangle=|\psi\rangle
implies that
A(v)|\psi\rangle=|\psi\rangle
.
For face stabilizers, we can similarly show that conditions
B(f)|\psi\rangle=|\psi\rangle
and
B_{f}|\psi\rangle=|\psi\rangle
are equivalent.
∎

Ribbon operator and excitations.
—
Notice that each group element of
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
is a conjugacy class
[g]=\{g\}
and all their centralizers are the Abelian group
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
, thus
N
irreps
\pi
are all one-dimensional.
There are
N^{2}
different anyons, from Eq. (
4
), all of these anyons have quantum dimension 1, thus the resulting topological phase
\mathcal{A}(N)=\textsf{Rep}(D(\mathbb{Z}_{N}))
is an Abelian phase:

\mathcal{A}(N)=\{\mathds{1},e^{g},m^{h},\varepsilon^{g,h}=e^{g}\otimes m^{h}\}.

(17)

We will also use the notion
\varepsilon^{g,0}=e^{g}
,
\varepsilon^{0,h}=m^{h}
and
\varepsilon^{0,0}=\mathds{1}
.
The electric charge
e^{g}
is associated with a vertex
v
such that
A(v)|\psi\rangle=\omega_{N}^{g}|\psi\rangle
; the magnetic charge
m^{h}
is associated with a face
f
such that
B(f)|\psi\rangle=\omega_{N}^{h}|\psi\rangle
; and dyon
\varepsilon^{g,h}
is associated with a site
s=(v,f)
such that both of the above conditions hold.

Since
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
is Abelian, the ribbon operator
F_{\rho}^{g,h}
over a ribbon
\rho
becomes two string operators,
F^{g,h}_{\rho}=(\otimes_{i\in\partial\rho}Z(i)^{g})\otimes(\otimes_{j\in\bar{\partial}\rho}X(j)^{h})
,
where
\partial\rho
and
\bar{\partial}\rho
respectively denote the direct boundary and dual boundary of
\rho
.
Notice that here the string operators are constructed in a way such that they are consistent with the definition of vertex operators and face operators; for example, a
Z^{g}
-string operator along a direct path
\partial\rho=e_{1}e_{2}\cdots e_{n}
is defined as

Z^{g}(\partial\rho)=\otimes_{i\in\partial\rho}Z(i)^{g},

(18)

where we set
Z(i)=Z
when the direction of the edge
e_{i}
is the same as the direction of the path, otherwise we set
Z(i)=Z^{\dagger}
. Similarly for
X^{h}
-strings (the direction of the dual edge is defined as the direction obtained by rotating
\pi/2
of the direct edge counterclockwise).
A
Z^{g}
-string creates
e^{g}
and
e^{-g}
particles over two ends of the string. Similarly, an
X^{h}
-string creates
m^{h}
and
m^{-h}
particles over two ends of the string.

The fusion rule is given by

(e^{g}\otimes m^{h})\otimes(e^{k}\otimes m^{l})=e^{g+k}\otimes m^{h+l}.

(19)

The fusion rule is invariant under an exchanging of electric and magnetic charges
e\leftrightarrow m
.
Different anyons can also braid with each other, and both fusion and braiding can be realized in the lattice using string operators.

Although we will mainly study the quantum double lattice realization of Abelian phase
\mathcal{A}(N)
, it is also helpful to investigate the phase from the string-net perspective in some situations (especially when in the category-theoretic framework).
In this case, the input data is a UFC
\mathcal{C}=\textsf{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
, and the topological excitation is characterized by its Drinfeld center
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
.
In
Kitaev and Kong 2012
, it is pointed out that the topological excitations can also be regarded as point defects in the bulk.
A point defect is a zero-dimensional defect between two bulk trivial domain walls.
It is mathematically described by a bimodule functor between two
\mathcal{C}|\mathcal{C}
-bimodules, thus
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})\simeq\textsf{Fun}_{\mathcal{C}|\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C})
where
\textsf{Fun}_{\mathcal{C}|\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C})

is the category of all bimodule functors.
To summarize, the cyclic Abelian topological phase is characterized by UMTCs

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(N)

\displaystyle=\textsf{Rep}(D(\mathbb{Z}_{N}))\simeq\mathcal{Z}(\textsf{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{N}))\simeq\mathcal{Z}(\textsf{Vect}(\mathbb{Z}_{N}))

(20)

\displaystyle\simeq\textsf{Fun}_{\textsf{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})|\textsf{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})}(\textsf{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{N}),\textsf{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{N}))

\displaystyle\simeq\textsf{Fun}_{\textsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{Z}_{N}}|\textsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{Z}_{N}}}(\textsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{Z}_{N}},\textsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{Z}_{N}}).

These different realizations of the same topological phase are Morita equivalent.

2.2
General Abelian group model

Let us now extend the
2d
cyclic group model to the Abelian group case.
According to the fundamental theorem of Abelian groups, a finite Abelian group
G
can be decomposed as

G\cong\mathbb{Z}_{N_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{N_{n}},

(21)

for which
|G|=p_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots p_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
and
N_{i}=p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}
(
p_{i}
’s are prime numbers).
We denote
g\in G
as an
n
-tuple
g=(g_{1},\cdots,g_{n})
, where
g_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{N_{i}}
.
All irreducible representations of
G
are one-dimensional; we introduce their corresponding characters as

\chi_{g}(k)=\exp\left(2\pi i\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{g_{j}k_{j}}{N_{j}}\right).

(22)

Notice that
\chi_{g}(a+b)=\chi_{g}(a)\chi_{g}(b)
and
\chi_{g}\chi_{h}=\chi_{g+h}
.
The set of all characters forms a dual group
G^{\vee}
which is isomorphic to
G
.

To each edge of the lattice, we attach a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{e}=\mathbb{C}[G]\cong\mathbb{C}^{N_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{C}^{N_{n}}
, where for
g\in G
,
|g\rangle=|g_{1}\rangle\otimes\cdots\otimes|g_{n}\rangle
.
We introduce the following operators

X_{G}^{g}=\sum_{h\in G}|h+g\rangle\langle h|,\quad Z_{G}^{g}=\sum_{h\in G}\chi_{g}(h)|h\rangle\langle h|.

(23)

If we denote
X_{N_{i}}
and
Z_{N_{i}}
the operators introduced in Eq. (
5
) for cyclic group
\mathbb{Z}_{N_{i}}
, it is clear that

X_{G}=\otimes_{i=1}^{n}X_{N_{i}},\quad Z_{G}=\otimes_{i=1}^{n}Z_{N_{i}}.

(24)

and
X_{G}^{g}=(X_{G})^{g}=\otimes_{i=1}^{n}X_{N_{i}}^{g_{i}}
,
Z_{G}^{g}=(Z_{G})^{g}=\otimes_{i=1}^{n}Z_{N_{i}}^{g_{i}}
.

The vertex operator is of the form

A(v)=\prod_{j\in\partial v}X_{G}(j),\quad A_{v}=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}A(v)^{g},

(25)

and the face operator is of the form

B(f)=\prod_{j\in\partial f}Z_{G}(j),\quad B_{f}=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}B(f)^{g}.

(26)

Notice that
\chi_{g}(h)=\chi_{h}(g)
and thus that
\sum_{g\in G}\chi_{g}(h_{1})\cdots\chi_{g}(h_{n})=\sum_{g\in G}\chi_{h_{1}+\cdots+h_{n}}(g)=\delta_{h_{1}+\cdots+h_{n},e}
(here
e=0
).
Operators
A(v)
and
B(f)
are unitary but not Hermitian; their eigenvalues are
\omega_{G}^{g}=\exp(2\pi i\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{g_{j}}{N_{j}})
.
The ground state manifold is the invariant state space of
A(v)
and
B(f)
.

Proposition 2
.

The ground state space of the Abelian group quantum double model is given by

\mathcal{V}_{\rm GS}=\{\,|\psi\rangle\,|\,A(v)|\psi\rangle=|\psi\rangle,\,B(f)|\psi\rangle=|\psi\rangle,\forall\,v,f\}.

(27)

This can be proved in a similar way as the cyclic group case.

3
Algebraic theory of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry enriched Abelian topological order

Before discussing the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
enriched Abelian topological order, let us first review the general algebraic theory of SET.
The main object we are going to discuss is symmetry defect, which is a point-like object but not intrinsic topological excitation of the topological order.
It plays an important role in our understanding of symmetries of the topological order.

3.1
Categorical symmetry of topological phase

We first review the definition of categorical symmetry of the topological phase
Cui et al. 2016
;
Barkeshli et al. 2019
;
Bischoff et al. 2019
, which is universal, namely, it is independent of the specific Hamiltonian realization of the phase.

To motivate the definition, let us recall that for the Ising phase, which is an equivalence class of Hamiltonians
\mathbf{Ising}=\{(H_{\mathrm{Ising}},\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{Ising}})\}
.
For a
k
-site Hamiltonian
H_{k}
, the full symmetry of the phase is
\mathcal{U}_{k}
, which is a set of unitary (or antiunitary) operators
U_{k}\in B(\mathcal{H}_{k}),\mathcal{H}_{k}\in\mathbf{Ising}
such that
U_{k}H_{k}=H_{k}U_{k}
.
By saying that the phase has
a
G
symmetry, we mean that for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, there is a homomorphism
\rho_{k}:G\to\mathcal{U}_{k}
.
The above discussion of symmetry is based on the Hamiltonian realization of the phase. To introduce the universal symmetry of the phase, which does not depend on the Hamiltonian realization, we need to introduce the concept of categorification of a group
G
(also known as
2
-group).

The categorification of
G
is denoted as
\underline{G}
, whose objects are elements of
G
and the
\mathrm{Hom}(g,h)=\delta_{g,h}
, i.e., there is no morphism between different elements and for
g
there is only one morphism to itself (identity morphism), namely,
G
is regarded as a discrete category. We can define the tensor product for the category
\underline{G}
as
g\otimes h:=gh
,
the tensor unit is thus the identity element
e=1\in G
.
Equipped with this tensor product,
\underline{G}
becomes a strict monoidal category.
Here we also introduce some other crucial algebraic notions that will be used later in our discussion:

•

We denote
\operatorname{Aut}_{\otimes}^{br}(\mathcal{D})
the group of braided monoidal autoequivalences of a UMTC
\mathcal{D}
up to natural isomorphisms, and
\operatorname{Aut}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{C})
the group of monoidal autoequivalences of a UFC
\mathcal{C}
up to natural isomorphisms.

•

Let
\operatorname{Inv}(\mathcal{C})
be the group of invertible objects in a UFC
\mathcal{C}
with group operation given by tensor product and the unit given by tensor unit.

•

The Brauer-Picard group
\operatorname{BrPic}(\mathcal{C})
of a UFC
\mathcal{C}
is the group of equivalence classes of invertible
\mathcal{C}
-bimodule categories with respect to the
\mathcal{C}
-module tensor product for the UFC
\mathcal{C}
,
and we have the group isomorphism
\operatorname{BrPic}(\mathcal{C})\simeq\operatorname{Aut}_{\otimes}^{br}(\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C}))
and monoidal equivalence

\underline{\operatorname{BrPic}(\mathcal{C})}\simeq\underline{\operatorname{Aut}_{\otimes}^{br}(\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C}))}
.

•

The Picard group
\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal{D})
of UMTC
\mathcal{D}
is the group of invertible
\mathcal{D}
-modules (which can be regarded as
\mathcal{D}
-bimodules using the braiding of
\mathcal{D}
). There are a group isomorphism
{\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal{D})}\simeq{\operatorname{Aut}_{\otimes}^{br}(\mathcal{D})}
and a monoidal equivalence
\underline{\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal{D})}\simeq\underline{\operatorname{Aut}_{\otimes}^{br}(\mathcal{D})}
.

For topological phase UMTC
\mathcal{D}
, the full symmetry is characterized by
\operatorname{Aut}_{\otimes}^{br}(\mathcal{D})
.
When the phase is non-chiral,
\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
for a UFC
\mathcal{C}
, then the full symmetry is equivalently characterized by the Brauer-Picard group of
\mathcal{C}
, Picard group of
\mathcal{D}
, and braided monoidal equivalence group of
\mathcal{D}
,
viz
.,

\operatorname{Aut}_{\otimes}^{br}(\mathcal{D})\simeq\operatorname{BrPic}(\mathcal{C})\simeq\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal{D}).

(28)

A functor
\varphi\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\otimes}^{br}(\mathcal{D})
can be regarded as a symmetry of the topological phase, it may permute the topological charge of the phase
\varphi(a)=a^{\prime}
but the vacuum charge must be left invariant
\varphi(\mathds{1})=\mathds{1}
.
The gauge invariant information of the phase must be left invariant under the symmetry transformation, thus we have

\displaystyle\text{fusion multiplicity:}\quad N_{ab}^{c}=N_{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}}^{c^{\prime}};

(29)

\displaystyle\text{quantum dimension:}\quad\operatorname{FPdim}a=\operatorname{FPdim}a^{\prime};

(30)

\displaystyle F\text{-matrix}:\quad F_{a}^{bcd}=F_{a^{\prime}}^{b^{\prime}c^{\prime}d^{\prime}};

(31)

\displaystyle\text{braiding:}\quad R_{ab}^{c}=R_{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}}^{c^{\prime}};

(32)

\displaystyle\text{topological spin:}\quad\theta_{a}=\theta_{a^{\prime}};

(33)

\displaystyle\text{modular matrix:}\quad S_{ab}=S_{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}}.

(34)

A
G
symmetry of
\mathcal{D}
is a group homomorphism
\rho:G\to\operatorname{Aut}_{\otimes}^{br}(\mathcal{D})
.
If
\rho
can be promoted into a monoidal functor
\underline{\rho}:\underline{G}\to\underline{\operatorname{Aut}_{\otimes}^{br}(\mathcal{D})}
, it is referred to as a categorical global symmetry of
\mathcal{D}
.
Technically, it consists of the following data:

(a)

unitary braided monoidal functors
(\underline{\rho}_{g},s_{g})_{g\in G}
where
\underline{\rho}_{g}:=\underline{\rho}(g)
and
s_{g}^{X,Y}:\underline{\rho}_{g}(X\otimes Y)\overset{\simeq}{\to}\underline{\rho}_{g}(X)\otimes\underline{\rho}_{g}(Y)
and
s_{g}^{\mathds{1}}:\underline{\rho}_{g}(\mathds{1})\overset{\simeq}{\to}\mathds{1}
are isomorphisms. Here “braided monoidal functor” means that the functor preserves the tensor product and braiding;

(b)

natural isomorphisms
\gamma_{g,h}:\underline{\rho}_{gh}\overset{\simeq}{\to}\underline{\rho}_{g}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\underline{\rho}_{h}
and
\gamma_{e}:\underline{\rho}_{e}\overset{\simeq}{\to}\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{D}}
. For an anyon
a\in\mathcal{D}
, we will denote the
g
action simply by
g(a):=\underline{\rho}_{g}(a)
when there is no ambiguity. Then, in terms of objects, we have
gh(a)\overset{\simeq}{\to}g(h(a))
and
e(a)\overset{\simeq}{\to}a
.

The action of global symmetry on simple objects of
\mathcal{D}
is just like a permutation, namely, for
a\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathcal{D})
,
g(a)\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathcal{D})
. In particular,
g(\mathds{1})=\mathds{1}
for all
g\in G
.
The topological phase
\mathcal{D}
is left invariant under the categorical global symmetry.

3.2
Symmetry defects and SET phase

For a topological phase
\mathcal{D}
with a categorical symmetry
G
, we can introduce the point-like defects carrying flux associated with symmetry
g\in G
, such kind of defects are called symmetry defects.

Since symmetry defects are extrinsic objects of a topological phase
\mathcal{D}
, to describe it, extra data are needed. For a given symmetry group
G
of the topological phase
\mathcal{D}
, it turns out that a symmetry defect carrying
g
-flux can be modeled with simple objects in a
\mathcal{D}
-bimodule category
\mathcal{D}_{g}
.
We refer to the collection
\mathcal{D}_{g}
of symmetric defects with
g
-flux as
g
-defect sector and denote objects in
\mathcal{D}_{g}
as
a_{g}
with a
g
-subscript, and
\mathcal{D}_{0}=\mathcal{D}
(here we use
0
to denote identity of
G
instead of
1_{G}
) is called the trivial sector.
Since symmetric defect behaves like a point-particle, anyons in
\mathcal{D}
can fuse with
defects, and defects in different sectors can also fuse, this means that
\oplus_{g\in G}\mathcal{D}_{g}
forms a multi-fusion category. We can also consider the braiding of defects and anyons, these data give a
G
-crossed UBFC structure.

In summary, the bulk phase with
G
-symmetry defects are characterized by a
G
-crossed UBFC
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{D}_{G}^{\times}
which is a
G
-crossed unitary braided extension of
\mathcal{D}
with respect to the categorical symmetry
\underline{\rho}:\underline{G}\to\underline{\operatorname{Aut}_{\otimes}^{\mathrm{br}}(\mathcal{D})}
; it consists of the following data:

(a) a (not necessarily faithful
1
1

1

A faithful
G
-grading is a grading
\oplus_{g\in G}\mathcal{D}_{g}
for which each sector
\mathcal{D}_{g}\neq 0
.
)
G
-grading
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{D}_{G}^{\times}=\oplus_{g\in G}\mathcal{D}_{g}
, and the fusion is
G
-crossed fusion, namely, for
a_{g}\in\mathcal{D}_{g},b_{h}\in\mathcal{D}_{h}
,
a_{g}\otimes b_{h}\in\mathcal{D}_{gh}
for all
g,h\in G
;

(b) a
G
-action
\underline{\rho}:\underline{G}\to\underline{\operatorname{Aut}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{P})}
extending the action on
\mathcal{D}_{0}=\mathcal{D}
, which satisfies
g(\mathcal{D}_{h})=\mathcal{D}_{ghg^{-1}}
for all
g,h\in G
. The associator for
\underline{\rho}_{g}
is denoted as
s_{g}
and the associator between
\underline{\rho}_{gh}
and
\underline{\rho}_{g}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\underline{\rho}_{h}
is denoted as
\gamma_{g,h}
;

(c) a
G
-crossed braiding which extends the braiding on
\mathcal{D}_{0}=\mathcal{D}
. It is a set of natural isomorphisms
c_{a_{g},b}:a_{g}\otimes b\to g(b)\otimes a_{g}
with

and

, graphically

The phase

It is natural to ask that for a given topological phase
\mathcal{D}
and global

;
Barkeshli et al. 2019
.
To introduce the symmetric defect and give a well-defined fusion rule, the first obstruction is the cohomology class

where
\operatorname{Inv}(\mathcal{D})
is the group of invertible objects in

. Now when we have the consistent fusion rule, which is given by a pair

, the fusion operation may not be associative, this is the obstruction

. When the obstruction vanishes, the resulting fusion category is parameterized by

. See
Etingof et al. 2010
;
Cui et al. 2016
;
Barkeshli et al. 2019
for details.

There are two useful properties about the bulk phase with symmetry defects
\mathcal{D}_{G}^{\times}=\oplus_{g\in G}\mathcal{D}_{g}
that we will use later:

•

The number of simple objects in
\mathcal{D}_{g}
(called rank of
\mathcal{D}_{g}
) is equal to the number of fixed points of the action
\rho_{g}
on
\operatorname{Irr}(\mathcal{D})
(see
Barkeshli et al. 2019
).

•

The Frobenius-Perron dimensions of different sectors of
\mathcal{D}_{G}^{\times}
are equal, that is
\operatorname{FPdim}(\mathcal{D}_{g})=\operatorname{FPdim}(\mathcal{D}_{h})
for all
g,h\in G
(see
(
Müger 2004
, Proposition 3.23)
).

For
G
-enriched SET phase
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{D}_{G}^{\times}
, it is called non-chiral if there exist a UFC
\mathcal{C}
and
G
-graded UFC
\mathcal{C}_{G}^{\times}
such that
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{C}_{G}^{\times})\simeq\oplus_{g\in G}\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{C}_{g})
and
\mathcal{D}_{g}=\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{C}_{g})
, otherwise it is called chiral.
Here
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{C}}
is a relative center concerning
\mathcal{C}
.
The SET phases we will discuss later are all assumed to be non-chiral.

3.3
Symmetry-gauged SET phase

The symmetry defects with flux
g\in G
are extrinsic defects,
realized microscopically by deforming the uniform Hamiltonian
H_{0}
. When we regard the symmetry defects as dynamical objects, we can discuss their confinement and deconfinement. If the energy cost to separate two symmetry defects grows with the distance between them, they are confined, meanwhile, if the energy cost to separate them is finite and independent of their distance, they are deconfined.
When the
G
-symmetry defects are deconfined, the corresponding global
G
-symmetry of the phase effectively becomes an emergent local gauge invariance at the long-wavelength limit; this process is generally known as symmetry-gauging
Cui et al. 2016
;
Barkeshli et al. 2019
.
For a SET phase
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{D}_{G}^{\times}
, after gauging the symmetry group
G
, the symmetry defects become deconfined quasiparticles of the gauged phase and the resultant phase is described by a UMTC
\mathcal{P}^{G}=(\mathcal{D}_{G}^{\times})^{G}
.

Mathematically, the symmetry gauging of the SET phase is characterized by the

of the
G
-crossed UBFC
\mathcal{D}_{G}^{\times}

3.4
EM duality symmetry enriched Abelian SET phase

We are now in a position to discuss the EM duality enriched SET phase for Abelian quantum double phase
\mathcal{A}(N)
.
As shown in
Teo et al. 2015
;
Barkeshli et al. 2019
, these SET phases are characterized by the relative center of Tambara-Yamagami categories
Tambara and Yamagami 1998
, thus, they are non-chiral SET phases. In this part, we will present a detailed discussion of these phases and their lattice realization. In the next section, we will show that these Tambara-Yamagami categories are the boundary phases.

For Abelian phase
\mathcal{A}(N)
, consider the EM duality
\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\{0,1\}
symmetry

\underline{\rho}:\underline{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\to\underline{\operatorname{Aut}_{\otimes}^{br}(\mathcal{A}(N))},

(39)

for which
\underline{\rho}_{0}=\mathrm{id}
and
\underline{\rho}_{1}
permutes electric and magnetic charges
e\leftrightarrow m
(this
\underline{\rho}_{1}
is the EM duality symmetry).
The anyon sector is just the
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
anyons
\mathcal{A}(N)_{0}=\textsf{Rep}(D(\mathbb{Z}_{N}))
.
For
1
-flux symmetry defect sector
\mathcal{A}(N)_{1}
,
notice that there are
N
topological charges that is invariant under the action of
\underline{\rho}_{1}
; they are
\varepsilon^{g,g}
for all
g\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}
. As we have pointed at the end of Subsection
3.2
,
\mathcal{A}(N)_{1}
has
N
simple objects, which we denote as
\sigma_{0},\cdots,\sigma_{N-1}
. The corresponding SET phase thus is

\mathcal{A}(N)_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}^{\times}=\mathcal{A}(N)_{0}\oplus\mathcal{A}(N)_{1}=\{\mathds{1},e^{g},m^{h},\varepsilon^{g,h}\}\oplus\{\sigma_{0},\cdots,\sigma_{N-1}\}.

(40)

This is the relative center of the Tambara-Yamagami category for cyclic group
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
.

Tambara and Yamagami completely classified the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded fusion categories in which all but one element are invertible, and the only non-invertible simple object carries a nontrivial symmetry flux. For
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
case,

\mathsf{TY}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})=\mathsf{TY}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})_{0}\oplus\mathsf{TY}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})_{1}=\{a|a\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}\}\oplus\{\sigma\}.

(41)

The fusion rule is given by

a\otimes b=a\oplus b,\quad a\otimes\sigma=\sigma\otimes a=\sigma,\quad\sigma\otimes\sigma=\oplus_{a\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}}a.

(42)

The associator
\alpha
is parameterized by a non-degenerate bicharacter
\chi:\mathbb{Z}_{N}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and a square root
\tau
of
|\mathbb{Z}_{N}|^{-1}=1/N
, and the unit morphisms are the identity map.

\mathsf{TY}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
is rigid, with
a^{*}=-a
and
\sigma^{*}=\sigma
.
The quantum dimensions are
\operatorname{FPdim}a=1
for all
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}
and
\dim\sigma=\sqrt{N}
. See Appendix
A
for a detailed discussion.

From the relative center construction
Gelaki et al. 2009
, the fusion rules of the SET phase
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}^{\times}
can be calculated. The fusion between anyons remains unchanged; for fusion involving symmetry defects, we have

\varepsilon^{g,h}\otimes\sigma_{k}=\sigma_{k}\otimes\varepsilon^{g,h}=\sigma_{k+g-h},\quad\sigma_{k}\otimes\sigma_{l}=\oplus_{g}\varepsilon^{g+k+l,g}.

(43)

The braiding structure can also be calculated.
The EM duality symmetry operation
\underline{\rho}_{1}
exchanges the electric and magnetic charges; however, the symmetry defects are invariant under EM duality symmetry.

4
Two algebraic theories of gapped boundary

A crucial feature of topologically ordered phases is the boundary-bulk duality
Kong et al. 2017
. In this duality, the boundary phase is obtained from bulk by anyon condensation, and the bulk phase is recovered from the boundary by taking Drinfeld center.
Here we discuss a generalization of the above picture to the case of the SET phase.
We will discuss the boundary theory of SET phase via anyon condensation.
We first review the existing anyon condensation theory based on Lagrangian algebra. Then we propose a new anyon condensation theory based on Frobenius algebra and prove the equivalence of two approaches in describing the gapped boundaries.

4.1
Bulk-to-boundary condensation I

Consider a
2d
(not necessarily non-chiral) phase
\mathcal{D}
enriched by a symmetry group
G
, and the corresponding SET phase is described by a
G
-crossed UBFC
\mathcal{P}_{1}=\mathcal{D}_{G}^{\times}
.
Suppose that an anyon condensation happens in a region inside the phase
\mathcal{P}_{1}
.
During the condensation process, some anyons are confined in the domain wall and the others become deconfined and can cross the domain wall to enter the condensed phase, and similarly for twist defects.
From this picture,
if the symmetry group
G
of the SET phase is broken into a subgroup
K\leq G
,
the condensed phase is described by another

K
-crossed UBFC
\mathcal{P}_{2}=\mathcal{F}_{K}^{\times}
, and the domain wall between them is a
1d
phase described by a
G
-crossed UFC
\mathcal{P}_{1|2}=\mathcal{W}_{G}^{\times}
.
When the symmetry group
G
is completely broken and
\mathcal{P}_{2}
becomes a trivial phase, the domain wall becomes a gapped boundary.
In this part, we will discuss the relations between these phases using natural physical requirements.

From the anyon condensation theory between two topological phases
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{F}
without symmetry, the anyon condensation is controlled by a
condensable algebra

A\in\mathcal{D}

Kong 2014
.

Definition 1
.

A condensable algebra
A
in a UMTC
\mathcal{D}
is an algebra with multiplication
\mu_{A}:A\otimes A\to A
and unit map
\eta_{A}:\mathds{1}\to A
such that:

(a)

A
is commutative, i.e.,
\mu_{A}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}c_{A,A}=\mu_{A}
where
c_{A,A}
is the braiding map in
\mathcal{D}
;

(b)

A
is separable, i.e.,
\mu_{A}:A\otimes A\to A
splits as a morphism of
A
-bimodules. Namely, there is an
A
-bimodule map
e_{A}:A\rightarrow A\otimes A
such that
\mu_{A}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}e_{A}=\mathrm{id}_{A}
;

(c)

A
is connected, viz.,
\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathds{1},A)=1
. In other words, the vacuum charge
\mathds{1}
only appears once in the decomposition of
A=\mathds{1}\oplus a\oplus b\oplus\cdots
.

If an algebra only satisfies (a) and (b), it is called an
étale algebra

Davydov et al. 2013
. If a condensable algebra
A
satisfies
(\operatorname{FPdim}A)^{2}=\operatorname{FPdim}\mathcal{D}:=\sum_{a\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{D})}(\operatorname{FPdim}a)^{2}
, then
A
is called a Lagrangian algebra

The condensable algebra determines the anyon condensation from one phase
\mathcal{D}
to another phase
\mathcal{F}
with domain wall
\mathcal{W}
. When the phase
\mathcal{F}=\mathsf{Hilb}
is a trivial topological phase, the gapped domain wall becomes a gapped boundary and the corresponding condensable algebra must be Lagrangian.
In
Fröhlich et al. 2006
, it is proven that an algebra
A
is commutative if and only if, when
A
decomposes into direct sum of simple objects in
\mathcal{D}
as
A=\oplus_{a\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{D})}N_{A}^{a}a
, all
a
’s in the decomposition are bosonic (topological spin
\theta_{a}=1
) if
N_{A}^{a}\neq 0
. This means that
A
is a coherent superposition of bosons. In
Cong et al. 2017b
, it is shown that
A
is separable if and only if for any
a,b\in\mathcal{D}
, there is a partial isometry
\mathrm{Hom}(a,A)\otimes\mathrm{Hom}(b,A)\to\mathrm{Hom}(a\otimes b,A)
; and
(\operatorname{FPdim}A)^{2}=\operatorname{FPdim}\mathcal{D}
if and only if, for all
a,b\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathcal{D})
, we have
N_{A}^{a}N_{A}^{b}\leq\sum_{c\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathcal{D})}N_{ab}^{c}N_{\mathcal{A}}^{c}
where
N_{ab}^{c}
are fusion coefficients of
a,b
.
These results can help us to determine the Lagrangian algebras of a given bulk phase
\mathcal{D}
.
It is worth mentioning that the algebra object
A
cannot uniquely determine the Lagrangian algebra. There exist examples of Lagrangian algebras
(A,\mu_{A},\eta_{A})
and
(A,\mu^{\prime}_{A},\eta^{\prime}_{A})
with the same algebra object but non-isomorphic Lagrangian algebra structures
Davydov 2014
.

Let us now recall what happens for anyons during the condensation from UMTC
\mathcal{D}
to UMTC
\mathcal{F}
with a gapped domain wall
\mathcal{W}
.
The condensation is controlled by a condensable algebra
A\in\mathcal{D}
, and
A
is the vacuum (tensor unit) of the condensed phase
\mathcal{F}
.
When an anyon
a
of
\mathcal{D}
moves into the vicinity of domain wall between
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{F}
, it will fuse with
A
.
The resulting domain wall phase
\mathcal{W}
is thus given by the monoidal functor
\bullet\otimes A
. These fused anyons form a right
A
-module category:
\mathcal{W}=\mathsf{Mod}_{A}(\mathcal{D})
. Some of the domain wall anyons are confined in the domain wall, while some others are deconfined that they can cross the wall and become anyons of the condensed phase
\mathcal{F}
. The deconfined anyons are local
A
-modules in
\mathcal{W}
, thus
\mathcal{F}=\mathsf{Mod}_{A}^{{loc}}(\mathcal{D})
.

In order to generalize the above picture for anyon condensation to the case of SET phase, the first thing we need to consider is the condition for a categorical symmetry
\underline{K}\hookrightarrow\underline{G}\to\underline{\operatorname{Aut}_{\otimes}^{br}(\mathcal{D})}
to be preserved after anyon condensation.
This has been extensively studied in
Bischoff et al. 2019
, and it is shown that this is equivalent to two conditions:

(a)

For each
k\in K
,
k(A)
is isomorphic to
A
as algebras;

(b)

There is a
K
-equivariant algebra structure
u_{K}=\{u_{k}:k(A)\to A\}_{k\in K}
on
A
.

This means that
A
must be a
K
-equivariant condensable algebra.
Assume that the symmetry group
G
is preserved during the anyon condensation,
viz.
, the above two conditions of
A
hold for group
G
.
The anyon condensation of
\mathcal{D}_{0}=\mathcal{D}
sector remains unchanged. For
g
-flux symmetry defect sector
\mathcal{D}_{g}

g
-flux defects fuse with
A

A
-module category
\mathcal{W}_{g}=\mathsf{Mod}_{A}(\mathcal{D}_{g})
.
Some
g
-flux symmetry defects are confined in the domain wall and others are deconfined, thus they can cross the domain wall to become
g
-flux defects in the condensed phase. The deconfined defects in
A
-module framework are thus
g
-local modules
Bischoff et al. 2019
: a module

is called

. Notice that here the
g
-local module condition is a natural result of the
g
-crossed braiding.
To summarize, the condensed phase is given by

\mathcal{F}^{\times}_{G}=\oplus_{g\in G}(\mathcal{W}_{g})_{dc}\simeq\oplus_{g\in G}\mathsf{Mod}_{A}^{g\text{-loc}}(\mathcal{D}_{g}).

(44)

Here
\mathsf{Mod}_{A}^{g\text{-loc}}(\mathcal{D}_{g})
denotes the category of
g
-local modules in
\mathcal{D}_{g}
.

For unitary
G
-crossed braided extension
\mathcal{D}_{G}^{\times}
of anyon model
\mathcal{D}
, the domain wall phase determined by the condensable algebra
A
is
\mathcal{W}_{G}^{\times}\simeq\mathsf{Mod}_{A}(\mathcal{D}_{G}^{\times})\simeq\oplus_{g\in G}\mathsf{Mod}_{A}(\mathcal{D}_{g})
, and the condensation is described as

\mathcal{D}_{G}^{\times}\overset{\bullet\otimes A}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{W}_{G}^{\times}\simeq\mathsf{Mod}_{A}(\mathcal{D}_{G}^{\times})\simeq\oplus_{g\in G}\mathsf{Mod}_{A}(\mathcal{D}_{g})\to\mathcal{F}_{G}^{\times}=\oplus_{g\in G}\mathsf{Mod}^{g\text{-loc}}(\mathcal{D}_{g}).

(45)

When
A
is a Lagrangian algebra, and all symmetries are broken,
\mathcal{F}_{g}=\emptyset
for all
g\neq 1_{G}
; so the condensed phase is the trivial phase
\mathsf{Hilb}
.
In this case, we obtain a gapped boundary
\mathcal{W}_{G}^{\times}\simeq\mathsf{Mod}_{A}(\mathcal{D}_{G}^{\times})\simeq\oplus_{g\in G}\mathsf{Mod}_{A}(\mathcal{D}_{g})
, which is a
G
-crossed UFC.

4.2
Bulk-to-boundary condensation II

Now we give another way to describe the anyon condensation of the SET phase using the quotient category construction, which generalizes the anyon condensation process presented in
Cong et al. 2017b
. This description is also very powerful for us to investigate the gapped boundaries.
To this end, we first introduce the notion of special Frobenius algebra.

Definition 2
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a tensor category. A Frobenius algebra in
\mathcal{D}
is a quintuple
(F,\mu,\eta,\Delta,\varepsilon)
with
F\in\mathcal{D}
,
\mu:F\otimes F\to F
,
\eta:\mathds{1}\to F
,
\Delta:F\to F\otimes F
and
\varepsilon:F\to\mathds{1}
, such that
(F,\mu,\eta)
forms an algebra,
(F,\Delta,\varepsilon)
forms a coalgebra, and

(\mathrm{id}_{F}\otimes\mu)\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}(\Delta\otimes\mathrm{id}_{F})=\Delta\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\mu=(\mu\otimes\mathrm{id}_{F})\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}(\mathrm{id}_{F}\otimes\Delta).

(46)

A Frobenius algebra is called special if there exist
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
such that

\mu\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\Delta=\alpha\mathrm{id}_{F},\quad\varepsilon\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\eta=\beta\mathrm{id}_{\mathds{1}}.

(47)

When
\alpha=1
and
\beta=\operatorname{FPdim}F
, it is called normalized-special.

A condensable algebra
(A,\mu_{A},\eta_{A})
in UMTC
\mathcal{D}
is a normalized-special Frobenius algebra, see
Kong 2014
for a proof.

Definition 3

(Pre-quotient category
\mathcal{D}/F
)
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a BFC and
F
a special Frobenius algebra in
\mathcal{D}
. The pre-quotient category
\mathcal{D}/F
consists of the following data:

(a) Objects
\mathrm{Obj}(\mathcal{D}/F)=\mathrm{Obj}(\mathcal{D})
;

(b) Morphisms are defined as
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}/F}(X,Y):=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(F\otimes X,Y)
and the composition between
f\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}/F}(X,Y)
and
g\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}/F}(Y,Z)
is defined as
g\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}f:=g\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}(\mathrm{id}_{F}\otimes f)\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}(\Delta\otimes\mathrm{id}_{X})
;

(c) Tensor product is defined as
X\otimes_{\mathcal{D}/F}Y:=X\otimes_{\mathcal{D}}Y
and for morphisms
f\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}/F}(X,Y)
,
g\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}/F}(Z,W)
,
f\otimes_{\mathcal{D}/F}g:=(f\otimes g)\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}(\mathrm{id}_{F}\otimes c_{F,X}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{Z})\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}(\Delta\otimes\mathrm{id}_{X}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{Z})
.

The functor
Q:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{D}/F
, which maps object
X
to
X
and morphism
f\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(X,Y)
to
\varepsilon\otimes f\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}/F}(X,Y)
, is a faithful monoidal functor.
Since we would like to describe the anyon condensation using the pre-quotient category, the problem is that
\mathcal{D}/F
may not be semisimple. Thus we need the following definition.

Definition 4

(Idempotent completion or Karoubi envelope)
.

For the pre-quotient category
\mathcal{D}/F
, we can define its canonical idempotent completion as the category
\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{D},F)
which consists of the following data:

(a) The objects in
\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{D},F)
are
\mathrm{Obj}(\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{D},F))=\{(X,p)\,|\,X\in\mathrm{Obj}(\mathcal{D}/F),p=p^{2}\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{D}/F}(X)\}
. Here
p:F\otimes X\to X
is known as an idempotent of
X
;

(b) The morphisms of
\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{D},F)
are given by

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{D},F)}((X,p),(Y,q)):=\{f\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}/F}(X,Y)|f\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}p=q\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}f\}.

All other structures of
\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{D},F)
are inherited from
\mathcal{D}/F
.

The anyon condensation is controlled by the normalized-special Frobenius algebra structure of a condensable algebra
A
in
\mathcal{D}
. The vacuum of the gapped domain wall turns out to be
A
and the wall excitation
\mathcal{W}
is the idempotent completion
\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{D},A)
of the pre-quotient category
\mathcal{D}/A
.
The condensation from phase
\mathcal{D}
to domain wall phase
\mathcal{W}
is given by the functor

\mathcal{D}\overset{Q}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{D}/A\overset{I.C.}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{D},A),

(48)

where
Q
and
I.C.
represents the pre-quotient functor and idempotent complete functor respectively.
Notice that gapped wall phase is a
1d
phase, thus is described by the UFC
\mathcal{W}\simeq\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{D},A)
.
The quantum dimension of the domain wall phase
\mathcal{W}
is
Müger 2004

\operatorname{FPdim}\mathcal{W}=\operatorname{FPdim}\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{D},A)=\frac{\operatorname{FPdim}\mathcal{D}}{\operatorname{FPdim}A}.

(49)

It is proven that the category
\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{D},A)
is equivalent to the
A
-module category
\mathsf{Mod}_{A}(\mathcal{D})
in
\mathcal{D}

Müger 2004
, thus the domain wall phase can also be described by the category
\mathsf{Mod}_{A}(\mathcal{D})
and the condensation is given by the functor

F:\mathcal{D}\to\mathsf{Mod}_{A}(\mathcal{D}),\;X\mapsto A\otimes X,

(50)

where
A\otimes X
automatically has an
A
-module structure originating from the algebra structure of
A
. The adjoint functor of
F
is the forgetful functor
I:\mathsf{Mod}_{A}(\mathcal{D})\to\mathcal{D},(M,\mu_{M})\mapsto M
. This matches well with the anyon condensation description we have discussed before.

Let us now see what happens for symmetry defect sector
\mathcal{D}_{g}
. In this case, to ensure that the symmetry
K\leq G
is not broken,
A
must have a
K
-equivariant Frobenius structure.
For simplicity, we assume that the entire symmetry group
G
is not broken.
Since
X_{g}\in\mathcal{D}_{g}
behaves like a point particle, when it is dragged near the domain wall, it undergoes the same physical process as the anyons. Therefore, the domain wall defect sector is the idempotent completion
\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{D}_{g},A)
of the pre-quotient category
\mathcal{D}_{g}/A
. For
G
-crossed UBFC
\mathcal{D}_{G}^{\times}
, the condensable algebra
A
gives the domain wall phase as
\mathcal{W}_{G}^{\times}\simeq\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{D}_{G}^{\times},A)\simeq\oplus_{g\in G}\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{D}_{g},A)
, and the condensation is described as

\mathcal{D}_{G}^{\times}\overset{Q}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{D}_{G}^{\times}/A\simeq\oplus_{g\in G}(\mathcal{D}_{g}/A)\overset{I.C.}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{W}^{\times}_{G}=\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{D}_{G}^{\times},A)\simeq\oplus_{g\in G}\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{D}_{g},A).

(51)

For each domain wall
g
-sector
\mathcal{W}_{g}
, we have the decomposition

\mathcal{W}_{g}=(\mathcal{W}_{g})_{\rm c}\oplus(\mathcal{W}_{g})_{\rm dc},

(52)

where “c” and “dc” represent confined and deconfined respectively.
The deconfined
g
-flux defect
X_{g}
must have
g
-trivial double braiding with the vacuum
(A,u_{G}=\{u_{g}:g(A)\to A\})
of the condensed phase,

(u_{g}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{X_{g}})\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}c_{X_{g},A}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}c_{A,X_{g}}=\mathrm{id}_{A\otimes X_{g}}.

4.3
Example: Gapped boundaries of cyclic Abelian SET phase

Now let us consider the gapped boundaries of the Abelian SET phase
\mathcal{A}(N)^{\times}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}
. To simplify the discussion, we assume that
N
is a prime number
p
.
Recall that
\mathcal{A}(p)
is the quantum double phase
\mathcal{A}(p)=\textsf{Rep}(D(\mathbb{Z}_{p}))
.
In Ref.
Bravyi and Kitaev 1998
, it has been argued via relative homology group method that there are two types of boundaries of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-toric code: smooth and rough ones. A more general analysis is given for general
D(G)
-quantum double model for finite group
G
in Refs.
Bombin and Martin-Delgado 2008
;
Beigi et al. 2011
;
Cong et al. 2017b
, which indicates that the boundary is determined by a subgroup
K\subseteq G
up to conjugation and a 2-cocycle
\omega\in H^{2}(K,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
. Since there are two subgroups
K_{1}=\mathbb{Z}_{1}
and
K_{2}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}
(recall that we assume the order
p
of the cyclic group
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is prime) and the 2nd cohomology group for both cases are trivial, there are exactly two boundaries of the model. The triviality of the 2-cocycle can be seen as follows:
the case that
H^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{1},\mathbb{C}^{\times})=0
is trivial; for
H^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mathbb{C}^{\times})
, we use the relation
H^{n}(G,\mathbb{Z})\simeq H^{n-1}(G,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})\simeq H^{n-1}(G,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
for all
n\geq 2
and the fact
H^{3}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mathbb{Z})=0
.

Figure 1:
The lattice realizations of smooth and rough boundaries. When a magnetic charge moves into the smooth boundary, it condenses into the boundary vacuum; when an electric charge moves into the boundary, it condenses into the boundary vacuum.

For the rough boundary characterized by
K=\mathbb{Z}_{1}
, the corresponding Lagrangian algebra is
A_{e}=\mathds{1}\oplus e^{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus e^{p-1}
. When the electric
e^{g}
particles move from the bulk to the boundary, they condense into the vacuum of the boundary phase.
The boundary excitations are given by
\mathcal{R}=\textsf{Mod}_{A_{e}}(\mathcal{A}(p))\simeq\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{A}(p),A_{e})
.
From string-net perspective, the rough boundary is characterized by a
\textsf{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
-module category
\mathcal{M}=\mathsf{Hilb}
, and the boundary excitations are given by
\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\simeq\mathsf{Fun}_{\mathsf{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})}(\mathsf{Hilb},\mathsf{Hilb})
(the category of all
\textsf{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
-module functors between
\mathsf{Hilb}
to itself). To summarize,

\mathcal{R}=\mathsf{Mod}_{A_{e}}(\mathcal{A}(p))\simeq\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{A}(p),A_{e})\simeq\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\simeq\mathsf{Fun}_{\mathsf{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})}(\mathsf{Hilb},\mathsf{Hilb}).

(56)

There are
p
simple objects in
\mathcal{R}
, which we denote as
I,M_{1},\cdots,M_{p-1}
. Their fusion satisfies
M_{a}\otimes M_{b}=M_{a+b}
.

Similarly, for the smooth boundary
K=\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, the corresponding Lagrangian algebra is
A_{m}=\mathds{1}\oplus m^{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus m^{p-1}
.
When the magnetic
m^{g}
particles move from the bulk to the boundary, they condense into the vacuum of the boundary phase.
The boundary excitations are given by
\mathcal{S}=\mathsf{Mod}_{A_{m}}(\mathcal{A}(p))\simeq\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{A}(p),A_{m})
.
From string-net perspective, the rough boundary is characterized by a
\textsf{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
-module
\textsf{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, and
the corresponding topological excitations are given by
\mathsf{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\simeq\mathsf{Fun}_{\mathsf{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})}(\mathsf{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}),\mathsf{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}))
.
To summarize,

\mathcal{S}=\mathsf{Mod}_{A_{m}}(\mathcal{A}(p))\simeq\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{A}(p),A_{m})\simeq\mathsf{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\simeq\mathsf{Fun}_{\mathsf{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})}(\mathsf{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}),\mathsf{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})).

(57)

There are
p
simple objects in
\mathcal{S}
, which we denote as
I,E_{1},\cdots,E_{p-1}
. Their fusion satisfies
E_{a}\otimes E_{b}=E_{a+b}
.

Now consider the EM duality symmetry enriched phase
\mathcal{A}(p)^{\times}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}
. Under the EM duality symmetry,

\underline{\rho}_{1}(A_{e})=A_{m},\quad\underline{\rho}_{1}(A_{m})=A_{e}.

(58)

This implies that there is no
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
equivariant algebra structure on
A_{e}
and
A_{m}
, thus the symmetry is broken during the anyon condensation.
From fusion rules we presented in Eq. (
43
), we see that

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}\otimes A_{e}=\oplus_{g\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\sigma_{g},\forall\,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{p},

(59)

\displaystyle\sigma_{l}\otimes A_{m}=\oplus_{g\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\sigma_{g},\forall\,l\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}.

(60)

We denote
\sigma=\oplus_{g\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\sigma_{g}
.

Let us first consider the rough boundary phase. The bulk-to-boundary condensation gives

\mathcal{A}(p)^{\times}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\to\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}^{\times}=\mathcal{R}_{0}\oplus\mathcal{R}_{1}=\{M_{g}|g\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\}\oplus\{\sigma\}

(61)

The fusion rule satisfies

M_{a}\otimes M_{b}=M_{a+b},\quad M_{a}\otimes\sigma=\sigma\otimes M_{a}=\sigma,\quad\sigma\otimes\sigma=\sigma

(62)

for
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.
This means that the rough boundary phase is a Tambara-Yamagami category.
For the smooth boundary, the analysis is completely the same and we have

\mathcal{A}(p)^{\times}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\to\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}^{\times}=\mathcal{S}_{0}\oplus\mathcal{S}_{1}=\{E_{g}|g\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\}\oplus\{\sigma\}.

(63)

The fusion rule satisfies

E_{a}\otimes E_{b}=E_{a+b},\quad E_{a}\otimes\sigma=\sigma\otimes E_{a}=\sigma,\quad\sigma\otimes\sigma=\sigma

(64)

for
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}
. Thus the smooth boundary phase is also a Tambara-Yamagami category.

5
Lattice realization of EM duality for Abelian phase with dislocation

Now let us consider the quantum double lattice realization of these EM duality symmetry defects for the Abelian topological phases.
The EM duality symmetry defect can be realized by dislocation. An explicit example of the construction for the toric code model is given by Kitaev and Kong in
Kitaev and Kong 2012
.
Here we will give a generalization to the cyclic Abelian group and discuss the corresponding ribbon operator and topological excitations. The generalization to arbitrary finite Abelian groups is straightforward.

Let us now consider how to construct the dislocation model of
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
quantum double phase and how to use the ribbon operator to realize the EM duality symmetry.
Recall that the grading group now is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, with
1\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
corresponding to the EM dual symmetry.

Lattice model of dislocation.
—
The process works as follows: for a given
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
lattice gauge theory
H_{0}
, a pair of defects carrying symmetry fluxes
g
and
g^{-1}
can be created and localized at two distant faces by modifying the original Hamiltonian with dislocation.
We can introduce a dislocation line into the original lattice such that the symmetry defects are located at two endpoints of the dislocation line.
The face operators and vertex operators along the dislocation line need to be modified and two new face operators
Q^{g}
and
Q^{g^{-1}}
at the endpoints of the dislocation line will be introduced, see Fig.
2
for an illustration.

EM-exchange ribbon operator.
—
Let us now consider how to construct the ribbon operator across the defect line
that realizes the EM duality symmetry.
Under the EM duality, the electric and magnetic charges will be exchanged:

e\leftrightarrow m,\quad\varepsilon^{a,b}\leftrightarrow\varepsilon^{b,a}.

(71)

We will see that when the bulk topological excitations pass through the dislocation line in either way, their electric charge and magnetic charge will be exchanged.

Let us first recall that a ribbon
\rho
consists of direct and dual triangles. The EM-exchange ribbon is the one crossing the dislocation line, see Fig.
3
. In this case, the EM-ribbon operator is defined by

F_{\rho}^{g,h}=(Z_{1}^{\dagger})^{g}(X_{2})^{h}(Z_{3}^{\dagger})^{g}(Z_{4}^{\dagger})^{h}(X_{5})^{g}.

(72)

At one end of the ribbon, an anyon
\varepsilon^{g,h}=e^{g}\otimes m^{h}
is created, when this anyon is dragged across the dislocation line, an EM duality operation will act on this anyon and it becomes
\varepsilon^{h,g}=e^{h}\otimes m^{g}
.

Figure 3:
An EM-ribbon
\rho
crossing the dislocation line. When passing the dislocation line, an
e
-particle is changed to an
m
-particle, and an
m
-particle is changed to an
e
-particle.

Proposition 4
.

The EM-exchange ribbon operator satisfies
[F_{\rho},A(v)]=0
,
[F_{\rho},B(f)]=0
, and
[F_{\rho},\tilde{B}(f)]=0
for all vertices, faces that are not the ends of the ribbon.

Proof.

One only needs to check that
[F_{\rho},\tilde{B}(f)]=0
for
f\in l
. Consider the configuration in Fig.
3
, where
\tilde{B}(f)=Z_{3}^{\dagger}X_{4}^{\dagger}Z_{6}Z_{2}^{\dagger}
. As
(X_{2})^{h}Z_{2}^{\dagger}=\omega_{N}^{-h}Z_{2}^{\dagger}(X_{2})^{h}
and
(Z_{4}^{\dagger})^{h}X_{4}^{\dagger}=\omega_{N}^{h}X_{4}^{\dagger}(Z_{4}^{\dagger})^{h}
, one has
F_{\rho}^{g,h}\tilde{B}(f)=\omega_{N}^{-h}\omega_{N}^{h}\tilde{B}(f)F^{g,h}_{\rho}=\tilde{B}(f)F_{\rho}^{g,h}
.
∎

The above proposition guarantees that no other topological excitations are created during the EM exchange process.
If we regard the end of the dislocation line as a symmetry defect, then the EM exchange ribbon realizes the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-crossed braiding given in Eq. (
35
).
The EM exchange ribbon can drag an anyon
a=\varepsilon^{g,h}
around the symmetry defect. When crossing the dislocation line, the
Z^{g}
-string becomes
X^{g}
string, thus the
e^{g}
particle becomes
m^{g}
particle. Similarly, the
X^{h}
-string becomes
Z^{h}
string, thus the
m^{h}
particle becomes
e^{h}
particle. This is exactly the crossed braiding
\sigma_{k}\otimes a=\underline{\rho}_{1}(a)\otimes\sigma_{k}
given by the EM duality symmetry.

Another result derived from the EM exchange ribbon construction is that we can only construct a double-braiding closed ribbon across the dislocation line. See Fig.
4
. If a ribbon crosses the dislocation line by an odd number of times, the anyon’s electric and magnetic charges are exchanged, and we can never construct such a closed ribbon. On the other hand, if a ribbon crosses the dislocation line by an even number of times, the anyon remains unchanged, and thus such a closed ribbon can be constructed.

Absorbing anyons via dislocation.
—
A crucial property of the two ends of the dislocation line is that they behave like a non-Abelian anyon.
Each end can be regarded as a zero-dimensional defect that connects a smooth domain wall and a rough domain wall.
We can thus consider the fusion and braiding of bulk anyons with these defects and also the fusion and braiding between these defects.
An interesting phenomenon is that bulk anyons
\varepsilon^{g,g}
will be absorbed by the defect. This has been proven in Eq. (
43
) via the algebraic analysis; here we show that in the lattice realization, this can be realized via the ribbon operators that terminate at the defect site.

From the fusion rule in Eq. (
43
), we see that anyons that are invariant under EM duality symmetry will be absorbed when fuse with symmetry defects:

\varepsilon^{g,g}\otimes\sigma_{k}=\sigma_{k},\quad\forall g\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}.

(73)

Thus the symmetry defects can be regarded as sinks of these anyons.
These fusion processes can be realized by the blue ribbon operator
F^{g,g}_{\xi}
drawn in Fig.
2
. Moving the anyon
\varepsilon^{g,g}
is realized by a
Z^{g}
-string and an
X^{g}
string along the direct and dual boundaries of the ribbon
\xi
. Since
F^{g,g}_{\xi}
commutes with all vertex and face operators near the defects, the anyon
\varepsilon^{g,g}
is absorbed by the defect.

EM duality.
—
We have presented a detailed discussion of the EM duality symmetry. In this part, let us construct a lattice realization of the EM duality for the SET phase.

Figure 5:
The direct and dual lattice of the symmetry enriched quantum double model.

Mathematically speaking, the EM duality of the quantum double model stems from the Poincaré duality of the underlying space manifold.
For a given lattice
\Sigma=V\cup E\cup F
, we introduce its dual lattice
\Sigma^{*}
for which the vertices are the faces of
\Sigma
and the faces are the vertices of
\Sigma
. Since each edge of
\Sigma
has a corresponding direction, the direction of the corresponding dual edge
e^{*}
is obtained by rotating by
\pi/2
of the direct edge counterclockwise.

We can introduce a local unitary map
U_{e}
between
\mathcal{H}_{e}=\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}_{N}]
and
\mathcal{H}_{e^{*}}=F(\mathbb{Z}_{N})=\mathbb{C}[\hat{\mathbb{Z}}_{N}]
with
\hat{\mathbb{Z}}_{N}\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{N}
the dual group of
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
.
The local unitary operator is the generalized Hadamard operator

U_{e}=\sum_{g\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}}|\psi_{g}\rangle\langle g|

(76)

where
|\psi_{g}\rangle
is the eigenstate of
X_{N}
as in Eq. (
9
). Notice that

\displaystyle U_{e}ZU_{e}^{\dagger}=Z^{*},\quad U_{e}XU_{e}^{\dagger}=X^{*},

(77)

where
Z^{*}=\sum_{h\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}}w^{h}|\psi_{h}\rangle\langle\psi_{h}|
and
X^{*}=\sum_{h\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}}|\psi_{h+1}\rangle\langle\psi_{h}|
are the Weyl operators in the dual space basis.
Then the global unitary operator
U_{\Sigma}=\otimes_{e\in E}U_{e}
gives a duality between quantum double models
D(\mathbb{Z}_{N})_{\Sigma}
on
\Sigma
and
D(\hat{\mathbb{Z}}_{N})_{\Sigma^{*}}
on
\Sigma^{*}
:

U_{\Sigma}A^{\Sigma}_{v}U_{\Sigma}^{\dagger}=B_{v}^{\Sigma^{*}},\quad U_{\Sigma}B^{\Sigma}_{f}U_{\Sigma}^{\dagger}=A_{f}^{\Sigma^{*}}.

(78)

Now for the SET phase
\mathcal{A}(N)^{\times}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}
with boundaries, all of the bulk symmetry defect operators, face and vertex operators along the dislocation line, and the boundary operators are transformed in a similar way.
See Fig.
5
for an illustration of the direct and dual lattices of SET.
From this EM duality, the electric charge on the vertex of
\Sigma
becomes a magnetic charge on the face. When the model has a gapped smooth boundary, the EM duality maps it into a rough boundary, and similarly, a rough boundary is mapped to a smooth boundary.
Thus EM duality allows us to understand the smooth boundary and rough boundary from each other’s perspective.

For non-Abelian group
G
, there is no EM duality, but the partial EM duality for some Abelian subgroups can be constructed
Hu and Wan 2020
.
The more general setting is for the Hopf algebraic model, in which the EM duality can be established using the Tannaka-Krein duality
Buerschaper et al. 2013b
.
Our construction here provides a simple example for further investigation of the extended Tannaka-Krein duality, which is applied for the
G
-crossed UBFC together with a corresponding Lagrangian algebra
A
. This general case will be discussed in our other work.

The EM duality provides a way to understand the direct lattice model
D(\mathbb{Z}_{N})_{\Sigma}
and dual lattice model
D(\mathbb{Z}_{N})_{\Sigma^{*}}
from each other’s perspective.
For example, if we obtain the ground state
|\Omega\rangle_{\Sigma}
of the direct lattice, our explicit construction of EM duality allows us to obtain the ground state of the dual lattice directly from the unitary transformation
|\Omega\rangle_{\Sigma^{*}}=U_{\Sigma}|\Omega\rangle_{\Sigma}
. For excited states, using the duality for ribbon operators, we also have this correspondence.

6
Conclusion

In this work, we systematically investigated the simplest class of SET phases, EM duality symmetry enriched cyclic Abelian topological phase
\mathcal{A}(N)^{\times}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}
.
The phase is studied via both algebraic analysis and lattice Hamiltonian realizations.
The
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
quantum double phase has EM duality symmetry, a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry, which is a special case of the more general categorical symmetry. Using this symmetry, the
\mathcal{A}(N)
phase can be enriched into a SET phase.
By introducing the anyon condensation process for the SET phase, the gapped boundaries are discussed in detail.
From the boundary-bulk duality discussion, we show that the SET phase
\mathcal{A}(N)^{\times}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}
is characterized by the relative center of Morita equivalent Tambara-Yamagami categories, which correspond to different gapped boundaries.
We also present an explicit lattice relation of this SET phase and its gapped boundaries and the explicit lattice realization of the EM duality is constructed.

The general lattice Hamiltonian constructions of SET phase are still largely unexploited. There are some constructions for special examples of SET phases
Barkeshli et al. 2020
;
Barkeshli and Cheng 2020
;
Wang and Cheng 2021
.
The symmetry-enriched string-net model is discussed in
Heinrich et al. 2016
, which is conjectured to realize a large class of SET phases. There are also some trials for introducing new tools for studying the SET phase, including tensor network representation and neural network representation of topological quantum states of the SET phases
Williamson et al. 2017
;
Bridgeman et al. 2017
;
Jia et al. 2019a
;
Jia et al. 2019b
;
Zhang et al. 2018
.
However, a systematic construction based on Kitaev quantum double model for arbitrary group
G
(or more generally, Hopf algebra
H
) is still an open problem; our result is an example in this direction. We conjecture that all Kitaev models based on semisimple Hopf algebras can be enriched by EM duality symmetry, and we left the explicit construction of these models for future study.

Besides the fundamental importance, investigating the SET phase is also crucial for quantum information processing, especially for topological quantum computation and topological quantum memory.
We know that the real samples of quantum materials always have boundaries and defects, hence a better understanding of these defects can help us to design more robust topological quantum memory materials.
Another crucial point is that by introducing the defects into the Abelian phase, the resulting SET phase can be used to do the universal topological quantum computation, which cannot be done by the original Abelian phase
Cong et al. 2017a
;
Fowler et al. 2012
;
Brown et al. 2017
.
We leave the study of these possible applications for our future work.

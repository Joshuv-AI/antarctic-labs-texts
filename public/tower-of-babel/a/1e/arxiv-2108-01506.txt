Moduli spaces of quasi-trivial sheaves
Douglas Guimarães, Marcos Jardim

Abstract
. A torsion-free sheaf E E on a projective variety X X is called quasi-trivial if E ∨ ⁣ ∨ = 𝒪 X ⊕ r E^{\vee\vee}=\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus r} . While such sheaves are always μ \mu -semistable, they may not be semistable. We study the Gieseker–Maruyama moduli space 𝒩 X ​ ( r , n ) \mathcal{N}_{X}(r,n) of rank r r semistable quasi-trivial sheaves on X X with E ∨ ⁣ ∨ / E E^{\vee\vee}/E being a 0-dimensional sheaf of length n n via the Quot scheme of points Quot ⁡ ( 𝒪 X ⊕ r , n ) \quot(\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus r},n) . We show that, when ( X , A ) (X,A) is a good projective variety, then 𝒩 X ​ ( r , n ) \mathcal{N}_{X}(r,n) is empty when r > n r>n , while 𝒩 X ​ ( n , n ) \mathcal{N}_{X}(n,n) has no stable points and is isomorphic to the symmetric product Sym n ⁡ ( X ) \Sym^{n}(X) . Our main result is the construction of an irreducible component of 𝒩 X ​ ( r , n ) \mathcal{N}_{X}(r,n) of dimension n ⁡ ( d + r − 1 ) − r 2 + 1 n(d+r-1)-r^{2}+1 when r < n r<n . Furthermore, if we restrict to X = ℙ 3 X=\mathbb{P}^{3} this is the only irreducible component when n ≤ 10 n\leq 10 .



1.
Introduction

Let
(X,A)
be a polarized projective variety, let
P
be a polynomial in
\mathbb{Q}[t]
, and denote by
\mathcal{M}_{X}(P)
the Gieseker–Maruyama moduli space of semistable sheaves on
X
with Hilbert polynomial
P
. Maruyama proved in
[
18
]
that the space
\mathcal{M}_{X}(P)
is a projective scheme. However, especially when
\dim X\geq 3
, the geometry of such a scheme remains largely unknown, despite the efforts of many authors in the past four decades, and questions about connectedness, irreducibility, the number of irreducible components, and so on, remain open even when we restrict to work over projective spaces taking, for example,
X=\mathbb{P}^{3}
.

For instance, let
X=\mathbb{P}^{3}
and denote
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(P)=\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(r,c_{1},c_{2},c_{3})
, where
c_{1}
,
c_{2}
, and
c_{3}
are the first, second, and third Chern classes of
E
, respectively. When
r=1
and
c_{1}=0
(which can always be achieved after twisting by an appropriate line bundle), one gets that
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(1,0,c_{2},c_{3})
is isomorphic to the Hilbert scheme
\Hilb^{d,g}(\mathbb{P}^{3})
of 1-dimensional schemes of degree
d=-c_{2}
and genus
g=c_{3}-2c_{2}

[
15
, Lemma B.5.6]
, which is known to always be connected
[
7
]
. Not much is known in general when
r\geq 2
, though:

(1)

\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(2,c_{1},c_{2},c_{3})
is irreducible for
c_{3}=c_{2}^{2}-c_{2}+2
when
c_{1}=0
, or
c_{3}=c_{2}^{2}
when
c_{1}=-1
, see
[
20
, Theorem 1.1]
and the references therein;

(2)

\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(2,0,2,c_{3})
has 2 irreducible components when
c_{3}=2
and it has 3 irreducible components when
c_{3}=0

[
14
, Section 6]
.

(3)

\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(2,-1,2,c_{3})
has 2 irreducible components when
c_{3}=2
and it has 4 irreducible components when
c_{3}=0

[
1
, Main Theorem 3]
.

Moreover, the moduli spaces in items (2) and (3) are connected. For higher values of
c_{2}
, one can check that the number of irreducible components of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(2,c_{1},c_{2},0)
grows with
c_{2}
, see
[
5
, Proposition 3.6]
; it is not known whether
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(2,c_{1},c_{2},c_{3})
is always connected.

The goal of this paper is to explore a somewhat exotic case, namely

\mathcal{N}_{X}(r,n):=\mathcal{M}_{X}(r\cdot P_{X}(t)-n)\text{ with }r,n\geq 1,

whose points correspond to
quasi-trivial
rank
r
sheaves, that is, sheaves
E
on
X
such that
E^{\vee\vee}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}^{\oplus r}
; this nomenclature is borrowed from Artamkin
[
2
]
. The motivation comes from its close relationship, described in the body of the paper, between
\mathcal{N}_{X}(r,n)
and the Hilbert and Quot schemes of points in
X
. Moreover, even though the main focus of this paper is the moduli space of semistable quasi-trivial sheaves, we also provide some results regarding
\mu
-semistable quasi-trivial sheaves.

First, we study
\mu
-semistable sheaves
E
on
(X,A)
with
\rk(E)\geq 1
and vanishing Chern classes, and show that they are always extensions of ideal sheaves of subschemes of
X
of codimension at least 3, see Lemma
2.3
below. In addition, when
(X,A)
is a
good
projective variety (cf. Definition
2.4
), we prove that the moduli space of such sheaves is a GIT quotient of a Quot scheme
\quot(\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus r},u)
, where
u
is a polynomial of degree less than or equal to
d-3
, see Theorem
3.6
.

Here is the main result of this paper.

Main Theorem
.

(1)

\mathcal{N}_{X}(r,n)
is empty whenever
r>n
or
n<0
.

(2)

\mathcal{N}_{X}(n,n)
is isomorphic to
\Sym^{n}(X)
.

(3)

If
r<n
, then
\mathcal{N}_{X}(r,n)
has an irreducible component of dimension
n(d+r-1)-r^{2}+1
. Moreover, if
X=\mathbb{P}^{3}
and
n\leq 10
, then
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(r,n)
is irreducible.

The bound on
n
comes from the fact that the variety
\mathcal{C}(n)
of triples of
n\times n
commuting matrices is known to be irreducible precisely for
n\leq 10
; in fact, our conclusion is that
\mathcal{N}(r,n)
is irreducible whenever
\mathcal{C}(n)
is.

The paper is organized as follows. We start by studying reflexive sheaves with vanishing Chern classes in Section
2
; we prove a key technical result about the triviality of these sheaves, which is subsequently used in the following sections. In Section
3
we give a criterion that tells when a torsion-free sheaf coming arising as the kernel of an element of
\quot(\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus r},u)
is (semi)stability, and explain the relation between sheaf (semi)stability and the GIT-stability with respect to the natural action of
\GL_{r}
on
\quot(\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus r},u)
.

Section
4
is where we establish items (1) and (2) of the Main Theorem, and we prove many technical results that will be used in sections
5
and
6
. In Section
5
we construct an irreducible component of
\mathcal{N}_{X}(2,n)
that we will use this as an induction step to construct an irreducible component for
\mathcal{N}_{X}(r,n)
. In the last section, we restrict to
X=\mathbb{P}^{3}
and prove that
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(r,n)
is irreducible for
n<10
.

We work over the complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
. For a torsion-free sheaf
E
, (semi)stable always means Gieseker (semi)stability, while
\mu
-(semi)stability refers to stability in the sense of Mumford–Takemoto. As usual, we denote by the lower capital letters the dimension of the respective cohomology or Ext group:
h^{i}(F):=\dim H^{i}(F)
and
\ext^{i}(F,G):=\dim\Ext^{i}(F,G)
.

2.
Semistable reflexive sheaves with vanishing Chern classes

Recall that a torsion-free sheaf
E
on a smooth polarized projective variety
(X,A)
of dimension
d
is said to be
\mu
-(semi)stable
with respect to an ample divisor
A
if
E
is torsion-free and

\dfrac{c_{1}(F)\cdot A^{d-1}}{\rk(F)}<~(\leq)~\dfrac{c_{1}(E)\cdot A^{d-1}}{\rk(E)}

for every nontrivial subsheaf
F\subset E
. Furthermore,
E
is
Gieseker (semi)stable
if every nontrivial subsheaf
F\subset E
satisfies

\dfrac{P_{F}(t)}{\rk(F)}<~(\leq)~\dfrac{P_{E}(t)}{\rk(E)}

where

P_{E}(t):=\chi\big(E\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(t\cdot A)\big)=\sum_{k=0}^{d}\alpha_{k}(E)t^{k}

denotes the Hilbert polynomial of the sheaf
E
with respect to the divisor
A
.

For the remainder of this paper, unless otherwise specified, (semi)stability will always refer to Gieseker (semi)stability. Remark that

\mu\text{-stability}~~\Longrightarrow~~~~\text{Gieseker stability}~~\Longrightarrow~~\text{Gieseker semistability}~~\Longrightarrow~~\mu\text{-semistability},

see
[
13
, Lemma 1.2.13]
. Moreover,
E
is
\mu
-(semi)stable if and only if the dual sheaf
E^{\vee}
is
\mu
-(semi)stable.

The main characters of the present paper are presented in the following definition, borrowed from Artamkin
[
2
, p. 452]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A torsion-free sheaf
E
on a projective variety
X
is said to be
quasi-trivial
if
E^{\vee\vee}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus r}
.

Quasi-trivial sheaves of rank 1 are just ideal sheaves of subschemes of codimension at least 2. Clearly, quasi-trivial sheaves are properly
\mu
-semistable (ie.
\mu
-semistable but not
\mu
-stable) when
\rk(E)>1
.

Notice that if
E
is quasi-trivial and
\codim Q_{E}\geq 3
where
Q_{E}:=E^{\vee\vee}/E
, then
c_{1}(E)=c_{2}(E)=0
. We will now investigate to what extent the converse is true; since we will make frequent use of the following condition, it is worth fixing it in a definition.

Definition 2.2
.

A torsion-free sheaf
F
on a polarized projective variety
(X,A)
is said to have
vanishing Chern classes
if
c_{1}(F)\cdot A^{d-1}=c_{2}(F)\cdot A^{d-2}=0
.

By Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch,
F
has vanishing Chern classes if and only if

P_{F,2}(t)=r\cdot P_{X,2}(t),

where
r=\rk(F)
and
P_{X}(t)=\chi\big(\mathcal{O}_{X}(t\cdot A)\big)
is the Hilbert polynomial of the structural sheaf.

A particular case of a result due to Simpson, see
[
21
, Theorem 2]
, establishes that if
F
is a
\mu
-semistable reflexive sheaf with vanishing Chern classes on
(X,A)
, then
F
is an extension of
\mu
-stable locally free sheaves with vanishing Chern classes. This has the following interesting consequence.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(X,A)
be a smooth polarized projective variety such that every
\mu
-stable reflexive sheaf with vanishing Chern classes is a line bundle. If
E
is a semistable reflexive sheaf with vanishing Chern classes, then its Jordan–Hölder filtration has factors in
\Pic^{0}(X)
.

By the Corlette–Simpson correspondence
[
21
, Corollary 1.3]
, the hypothesis of the lemma holds whenever
\pi_{1}(X)
is abelian. Examples of such varieties are Fano varieties, rational surfaces, abelian varieties, K3 surfaces, products of the previous ones, and quotients of simply connected varieties by finite abelian groups (e.g., Enriques surfaces). One can also check that the conclusion of the lemma holds when
(X,A)
is a smooth projective surface with
K_{X}\cdot A\leq 0
and satisfying
\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})=1
.

Proof.

We argue by induction on
\rk(E)=r
. If
E
is a rank
2
semistable reflexive sheaf with
c_{1}=c_{2}=0
, then by Simpson’s result,
E
must be an extension of
\mu
-stable locally free sheaves with vanishing Chern classes, that is, we can write
E
in the following short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow L^{\prime}\longrightarrow 0,

where
L
and
L^{\prime}
are in
\Pic^{0}(X)
. Thus we can take

0\subset L\subset E

as the desired Jordan-Hölder filtration.

Now suppose the result is valid for a rank less than
r
and let
E
be a semistable reflexive sheaf with
c_{1}=c_{2}=0
. Again, by Simpson’s result, there exists a filtration of
E

0=G_{0}\subset G_{1}\subset\cdots\subset G_{k}\subset E,

such that each quotient is a
\mu
-stable locally free sheaf with vanishing Chern classes. Let
F:=G_{k}
and
F^{\prime}:=E/G_{k}
and consider the following exact sequence

0\longrightarrow F\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow F^{\prime}\longrightarrow 0.

Since
F^{\prime}
is a
\mu
-stable locally free with vanishing Chern classes, the hypothesis implies that
F^{\prime}\in\Pic^{0}(X)
. Now we can apply the induction hypothesis to
F
which has rank
r-1
, and we get a Jordan–Hölder filtration of
F

0=F_{0}\subset F_{1}\subset\cdots\subset F_{l}\subset F

whose factors are in
\Pic^{0}(X)
. Finally, we take

0=F_{0}\subset F_{1}\subset\cdots\subset F_{l}\subset F\subset E,

and this is a Jordan–Hölder filtration for
E
which satisfies our requirements.
∎

Since varieties satisfying the hypotheses of Lemma
2.3
will play an important role in the present paper, we introduce the following definition.

Definition 2.4
.

A smooth polarized projective variety
(X,A)
is said to be
good
if
h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X})=0
,
\Pic(X)=\mathbb{Z}
, and every
\mu
-stable reflexive sheaf
F
with vanishing Chern classes is a line bundle.

Fano manifolds with Picard rank 1 are perhaps the most relevant examples of good projective varieties.

We complete this section with the following application of Simpson’s result.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
E
be a
\mu
-semistable sheaf of rank
r
on a good polarized projective variety
(X,A)
of dimension
d\geq 3
. If
c_{1}(E)\cdot A^{d-1}=c_{2}(E)\cdot A^{d-2}=0
, then
E
is quasi-trivial and
\codim Q_{E}\geq 3
.

Proof.

Since
E
is torsion-free, we have
\dim Q_{E}\leq d-2
by
[
22
, II, Corollary of Lemma 1.1.8]
. It follows that we can write the Hilbert polynomial of
Q_{E}
as
P_{Q_{E}}(t)=at^{d-2}+q(t)
for some
a\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
and
q\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
with
\deg(u)\leq d-3
. Since
c_{1}(E)\cdot A^{d-1}=c_{2}(E)\cdot A^{d-2}=0
, it follows that the Hilbert polynomial of
E
is of the form
P_{E}(t)=r\cdot P_{X}(t)+v(t)
where
v\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
with
\deg(v)\leq d-3
and
r:=\rk(E)
.
Hence, we can use the standard exact sequence

(1)

0\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow E^{\vee\vee}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi_{E}}}{{\longrightarrow}}Q_{E}\longrightarrow 0

to note that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}P_{E^{\vee\vee}}(t)&=&P_{E}(t)+P_{Q_{E}}(t)\\
&=&r\cdot P_{X}(t)+at^{d-2}+q(t)+v(t).\end{array}

A simple calculation with Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch yields

a=-\dfrac{1}{(d-2)!}c_{2}(E^{\vee\vee})\cdot A^{d-2}

since
c_{1}(E^{\vee\vee})\cdot A^{d-1}=c_{1}(E)\cdot A^{d-1}=0
. Since
E^{\vee\vee}
is
\mu
-semistable, we can apply the Bogomolov inequality
[
13
, Theorem 7.1]
, obtaining the following inequality

\Delta(E^{\vee\vee})=2rc_{2}(E^{\vee\vee})\cdot A^{d-2}\geq 0

thus
a\leq 0
. It follows that
a=0
, thus
E^{\vee\vee}
is a
\mu
-semistable reflexive sheaf of rank
r
on
X
with
c_{1}(E^{\vee\vee})\cdot A^{d-1}=c_{2}(E^{\vee\vee})\cdot A^{d-2}=0
. According to Simpson’s result,
E^{\vee\vee}
must be a successive extension of copies of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
, since
\Pic^{0}(X)=\{\mathcal{O}_{X}\}
. But the hypothesis
h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X})=0
implies that extensions of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
by itself are trivial, we conclude that
E^{\vee\vee}=\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus r}
. Moreover,
P_{Q_{E}}(t)=q(t)
so
\deg(P_{Q_{E}})\leq d-3
and
\codim Q_{E}\geq 3
.
∎

3.
Moduli spaces of semistable quasi-trivial sheaves

Let
(X,A)
be a smooth polarized variety of dimension
d
, and let
V_{r}
denote a complex vector space of dimension
r
.

If
E
is a quasi-trivial sheaf of rank
r
on
X
, then the standard exact sequence (
1
) provides an element
(\varphi_{E},Q_{E})
of the
\quot
scheme
\quot(V_{r}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X},u)
, where
u(t)=r\cdot P_{X}(t)-P_{E}(t)
is a rational polynomial with rational coefficients of degree
\leq d-2
. Applying the functor
\Hom(\cdot,\mathcal{O}_{X})
to the exact sequence in display (
1
), we obtain

(2)

V_{r}\simeq\Hom(E,\mathcal{O}_{X})

since
\Hom(Q_{E},\mathcal{O}_{X})=0
(because
Q
is a torsion sheaf) and

\Ext^{1}(Q_{E},\mathcal{O}_{X})\simeq H^{n-1}(Q\otimes\omega_{X})^{*}=0

since
\dim Q_{E}\leq n-2
.

Conversely, consider an element
(\varphi,Q)
of the
\quot
scheme
\quot(V_{r}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X},u)
with
u\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
, that is,
V_{r}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}\twoheadrightarrow Q
is an epimorphism onto a sheaf
Q
with Hilbert polynomial
P_{Q}(t)=u(t)
. Set
E:=\ker\varphi
; we have a short exact sequence

(3)

0\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow V_{r}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}\longrightarrow Q\longrightarrow 0.

Clearly,
E
is a torsion-free sheaf of rank
r
; note that

P_{E}(t)=P_{V_{r}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}}(t)-P_{Q}(t)=r\cdot P_{X}(t)-u(t).

Proposition 3.1
.

Given
(\varphi,Q)\in\quot(V_{r}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X},u)
with
\deg(u)\leq d-2
, the sheaf
E:=\ker\varphi
is a quasi-trivial sheaf of rank
r
.

Proof.

Applying
\mathcal{H}om(-,\mathcal{O}_{X})
to sequence (
3
) we have

0\longrightarrow Q^{\vee}\longrightarrow(V_{r}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X})^{\vee}\longrightarrow E^{\vee}\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}xt^{1}(Q,\mathcal{O}_{X}).

Since
\codim(Q)\geq 2
, we have that
Q^{\vee}=\mathcal{E}xt^{1}(Q,\mathcal{O}_{X})=0
by
[
13
, Proposition 1.1.6]
, so that
E^{\vee}\cong V_{r}^{*}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}
. Since
E^{\vee}
is properly
\mu
-semistable, then so is
E
. ∎

However, different points in
\quot(V_{r}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X},u)
may lead to isomorphic quasi-trivial sheaves. Indeed, consider the following action of
\GL(V_{r})\simeq\Aut(V_{r}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X})
on
\quot(V_{r}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X},u)
:

(4)

g\cdot(\varphi,Q):=(\varphi\circ g,Q).

Note that
g\cdot(\varphi,Q)
is clearly in
\quot(V_{r}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X},u)
again. Previous considerations lead to the following theorem characterizing the set of isomorphism classes of quasi-trivial sheaves. Note that scalar multiples of the identity
\mathbf{1}\in\GL(V_{r})
act trivially on the Quot scheme.

Theorem 3.2
.

There is a bijection between the set of isomorphism classes of rank
r
quasi-trivial sheaves with Hilbert polynomial
P(t)
on
X
, and the set of orbits

\quot(V_{r}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X},u)\big/\PGL_{r}~~{\rm where}~~u(t):=r\cdot P_{X}(t)-P(t).

Proof.

Let
(\varphi,Q)
,
(\varphi^{\prime},Q^{\prime})
be points in
\quot(V_{r}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X},u)
lying in the same
\GL_{r}
-orbit, that is, there is some
g\in\GL_{r}
such that
(\varphi,Q)=g\cdot(\varphi^{\prime},Q^{\prime})=(\varphi^{\prime}\circ g,Q^{\prime})
. Hence, there is an isomorphism
f:Q\to Q^{\prime}
such that
f\circ\varphi=\varphi^{\prime}\circ g
. Let
E=\ker\varphi
and
E^{\prime}=\ker\varphi^{\prime}
be the corresponding
\mu
-semistable sheaves given by Proposition
3.1
. Thus we can consider the following diagram

(5)

\dim H^{\prime}=\dim U+\ext^{1}(\mathcal{I}_{q},\mathcal{I}_{Z})-1=d+d(n-1)+n-3=n(d+1)-3.

Moreover, Proposition
4.8
yields
\ext^{1}(E,E)=n(d+1)-3
.

Theorem 5.3
.

For every
n\geq 3
,
\mathcal{N}_{X}(2,n)
contains an irreducible component of dimension
n(d+1)-3
.

Proof.

Now
\mathcal{N}_{X}(2,n)
is a coarse moduli space, so our family
\mathcal{H}
on
X\times H^{\prime}
gives us a modular morphism
\Psi:H^{\prime}\to\mathcal{N}_{X}(2,n)
whose image is precisely the subset of stable unbalanced sheaves. However, as we have seen in Proposition
4.6
, the representation of an unbalanced sheaf as an extension of the ideal sheaf of a point by the ideal sheaf of a 0-dimensional scheme is not unique, meaning that the morphism
\Psi
is not injective. Nonetheless, we argue that it is a finite map.

Indeed, note that the Lemma
4.7
shows that an unbalanced sheaf can be represented as an extension of an ideal sheaf of a point by an ideal sheaf of a reduced 0-dimensional scheme in at most
n
different ways. In other words, if
E\in\im\Phi\subset\mathcal{N}_{X}(2,n)
, then
\Phi^{-1}(E)
consists of at most
n
different points.

This means that the dimension of the image of
\Psi
in
\mathcal{N}_{X}(2,n)
is equal to the dimension of
H
. Since every
E\in\im\Psi
satisfies

\dim T_{E}\mathcal{N}_{X}(2,n)=\ext^{1}(E,E)=n(d+1)-3=\dim\im\Psi

we conclude that the closure of
\im\Psi
within
\mathcal{N}_{X}(2,n)
is an irreducible component of
\mathcal{N}_{X}(2,n)
, as desired.
∎

6.
An irreducible component of
\mathcal{N}_{X}(r,n)

In this section, we will construct an irreducible component of
\mathcal{N}_{X}^{\rm st}(r,n)
whenever

. We will use the results from the first sections, and the previous section as an induction step.

We will argue by induction. The case
r=2
is already done, so suppose we have a family
\mathcal{H}_{r,n}
that gives us an irreducible component
H_{r,n}
of
\mathcal{N}_{X}(r,n)
with dimension
n(d+r-1)-r^{2}+1
, that is,
\mathcal{H}_{r,n}
is sheaf on
X\times H_{r,n}
such that for every
h\in H_{r,n}
,
\mathcal{H}_{r,n}|_{h}\in\mathcal{N}_{X}(r,n)^{st}
.

Consider the following diagram

\textstyle{\Hilb^{1}(X)\times H_{r-1,n-1}}
\textstyle{X\times\Hilb^{1}(X)\times H_{r-1,n-1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{p_{1}}
\scriptstyle{p_{2}}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{X\times\Hilb^{1}(X)}

where
H_{r-1,n-1}
denotes the irreducible component given by the induction hypothesis on
\mathcal{N}_{X}(r-1,n-1)

Define

U:=\{(y,F)\in\Hilb^{1}\times H_{r-1,n-1}\mid y\notin\supp Q_{F},~~\supp Q_{F}=(\supp Q_{F})_{\rm red}\}

as an open subset of
\Hilb^{1}\times H_{r-1,n-1}

and let

Let
\mathcal{E}^{i}:=\mathcal{E}xt^{i}_{\pi}(p_{1}^{*}\mathcal{H}^{1},p_{2}^{*}\mathcal{H}_{r-1,n-1})
. By Proposition
4.4
and Lemma
5.2
,
\mathcal{E}^{1}
is a locally free on

sheaf whose fibres over a point

.

Note that
\Hom_{X}(\mathcal{I}_{p},F)=0
for every

. So, by Theorem
5.1
,
\mathcal{E}^{0}=0
and
[

implies that there is an universal extension
\mathcal{H}
on

with
H=\mathbb{P}((\mathcal{E}^{1})^{*})
such that for every

, the restriction
\mathcal{H}|_{h}
is a nonsplit extension the following form

(13)

0\to F\to E\to\mathcal{I}_{y}\to 0.

In other words, every member
E_{h}

4.7
guarantees that there is an open subset

Theorem 6.1

Let

7.
Projective spaces

In this section we prove that
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(r,n)
is irreducible for
n\leq 10
. To do this we begin by establishing how the results proved in the previous sections translate to this particular case.

Every sheaf
E\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(r,n)
is also
\mu
-semistable, therefore Lemma
2.5
implies that
E
is
\ker\varphi
for some
(\varphi,Q)\in\quot(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}^{\oplus r},n)
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. For the converse, Proposition
3.1
says that every
\ker\varphi
is
\mu
-semistable for
(\varphi,Q)\in\quot(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}^{\oplus r},n)
. Lemma
3.5
allows us to express
E
as an extension

0\to F\to E\to G\to 0,

with
F\in\quot(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}^{\oplus s},k)
semistable and
G\in\quot(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}^{\oplus r-s},n-k)
stable, for some
k\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
and
s\in\{1,\dots,r-1\}
. Moreover, when
r=2
this implies that
E
is an extension of a sheaf of ideals

0\to I_{Z}\to E\to I_{Z^{\prime}}\to 0

where
Z
and
Z^{\prime}
are
0
-dimensional subschemes of
\mathbb{P}^{3}
with
h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{Z})+h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{Z^{\prime}})=n
.

Theorem
3.6
applied to our case translates into the following:
E=\ker\varphi
for
(\varphi,Q)\in\quot(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}^{\oplus r},n)
is not (semi)stable if, and only if, there is a torsion-free sheaf
F\hookrightarrow E
with
F=\ker\psi
for some
(\psi,Q_{F})
in
\quot(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}^{\oplus s},k)
satisfying
0<s<r
and

k<~~(\leq)~~\frac{s\cdot n}{r}.

For the rank
2
case, this criterion reduces to check whether there is a sheaf of ideals
I_{Z}\hookrightarrow E
satisfying

k<~~(\leq)~~\frac{n}{2}

where
k=h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{Z})
. Moreover
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(r,n)
is the GIT–quotient of
\quot(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}^{\oplus r},n)
by
\GL_{r}
.

Our starting point is the fact that the affine Quot scheme
\quot(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{A}^{3}}}^{\oplus r},n)
is irreducible for
n\leq 10
, see
[
8
]
. In order to see that the same holds for the projective Quot scheme
\quot(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}^{\oplus r},n)
, we will use the following technical lemma.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
X
be the union of two irreducible subschemes
A
and
B
, and let
C
be the intersection of
A
and
B
. Assume
C
is open in
A
and
B
. Then
X
is irreducible

Proof.

The closure of
A
in
X
is the same as the closure of
C
in
X
. Indeed, a function vanishing on
C
vanishes on
A
because
C
is open in
A
. Also, the closure of
B
in
X
is the same as the closure of
C
in
X
. Since
X=A\cup B
, we conclude that
X
is the closure of
C
in
X
. But
C
is an open subset of an irreducible scheme
A
, thus
X
is irreducible.
∎

As a simple consequence, we have:

Lemma 7.2
.

If
\quot(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{d}}^{\oplus r},n)
is irreducible, then
\quot(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{d}}^{\oplus r},n)
is irreducible.

Proof.

Fix coordinates
[x_{0}:x_{1}:\ldots:x_{m}]
for
\mathbb{P}^{d}
and let
H_{i}=\{x_{i}=0\}
so
A_{i}:=\mathbb{P}^{d}\setminus H_{i}\cong\mathbb{A}^{d}
. For
(\varphi,Q)\in\quot(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{d}}^{\oplus r},n)
, if
\supp(Q)\cap H_{i}=\emptyset
, then we can restrict
\varphi
to
A_{i}
and we obtain an element of
\quot(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{d}}^{\oplus r},n)
. Since we are assuming
\quot(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{d}}^{\oplus r},n)
to be irreducible, we can write
\quot(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{d}}^{\oplus r},n)
as the union of irreducible subschemes such that the two-by-two intersection is open inside those two. Therefore, by induction and the previous lemma, we get the statement.
∎

In particular, we conclude that
\quot(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}^{\oplus r},n)
is irreducible for
n\leq 10
. Theorem
3.6
implies that
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(r,n)
is the GIT-quotient of
\quot(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}^{\oplus r},n)
by
\GL_{r}
. Thus, whenever
\quot(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}^{\oplus r},n)
is irreducible,
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(r,n)
is also irreducible. We have therefore established the following claim.

Corollary 7.3
.

\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(r,n)
is irreducible for
n\leq 10
.

Note that when
n\leq 10
, Corollary
7.3
tells us that the component constructed in Theorem
5.3
and Theorem

is the only one, and it provides an explicit description of
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(r,n)
for
n\leq 10
.

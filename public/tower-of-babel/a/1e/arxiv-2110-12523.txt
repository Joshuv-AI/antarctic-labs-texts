Influence of N,N,N-trimethyl-1-adamantyl ammonium (TMAda + ) Structure Directing Agent on Al Pair Distributions and Features in Chabazite Zeolite
Xiaoyu Wang, Yujia Wang, Ahmad Moini, Rajamani Gounder, Edward J. Maginn, William F. Schneider

Abstract
While organic structure directing agents (OSDAs) are well known to have a directional influence on the topology of a crystallizing zeolite, the relationship between OSDA charge and siting of aliovalent ions on a primarily siliceous framework is unclear. Here, we explore the relationship between OSDA orientation, Al 3+ \text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{3+}} siting, and lattice energy, taking as a model system CHA zeolite occluded with N,N,N-trimethyl-1-adamantyl ammonium (TMAda + ) at an Si/Al ratio of 11/1. We use density functional theory calculations to parametrize a fixed-charge classical model describing van der Waals and electrostatic interactions between framework and OSDA. We enumerate and explore all possible combinations of OSDA orientation and Al location (attending to Löwenstein’s rule) within a 36 T-site supercell. We find that interaction energies vary over 60 kJ/double-six-ring-unit (d6r). Further, analysis of configurations reveals that energies are sensitive to Al − Al \text{Al}{-}\text{Al} proximity, such that low energies are associated with Al 3+ \text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{3+}} pairs in 8-membered rings and higher energies associated with Al 3+ \text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{3+}} pairs in smaller 6- and 4-membered rings. Comparisons with Al siting inferred from CHA zeolite crystallized with TMAda + suggests that these computed interaction energies are useful reporters of observed Al siting in CHA synthesized with TMAda + .

Introduction

Zeolites comprise a large class of microporous and crystalline aluminosilicates constructed primarily of silicon-centered and corner-sharing oxygen tetrahedra.
Corma 2003

Specific zeolite topologies are often accessed synthetically through co-crystallization of amorphous phases and gels with organic and/or inorganic structure directing agents (SDAs).
Moliner et al. 2013

Organic structure directing agents (OSDAs) are believed to guide zeolites toward particular crystal structures through favorable interactions between the OSDA and the forming framework
Gómez-Hortigüela and Camblor 2017
.
The computed interaction energy between a preformed framework and an occluded OSDA has been successfully used as a reporter of the potential for an OSDA to crystallize a particular framework.
Pophale et al. 2013
;
Schmidt et al. 2014
;
Davis et al. 2016

This relationship has been exploited to create new zeolites
Deem et al. 2009
and to crystallize zeolites with cages tailored to accommodate the transition state of a target reaction
Boal et al. 2016
;
Gallego et al. 2017
.

While purely siliceous zeolites are known, the large majority of zeolites contain some amount of aliovalent
\text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{3+}}
substitution onto the
\text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{4+}}
lattice, introducing a net charge onto the zeolite framework. Compensation of that charge by protons generates Brønsted acid sites useful for various hydrocarbon transformations.
Haag et al. 1984

Further, the relative proximity of those Brønsted sites within a framework can influence chemical and catalytic properties.
Kester et al. 2021
;
Chen et al. 2020
;
Nystrom et al. 2018
;
Bhan and Iglesia 2008
;
Román-Leshkov et al. 2011

The
\text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{3+}}
centers can also serve as coordination sites for extra lattice metal ions.
Dedecek and Wichterlova 1994
;
Dedecek et al. 1995

Here too the proximity of centers can have an influence on metal ion speciation, nuclearity, and reactivity.
Walspurger and Louis 2008
;
Giordanino et al. 2013
;
Paolucci et al. 2016
;
Li et al. 2018
;
Li et al. 2019
;
Devos et al. 2019

Experimental evidence indicates that synthesis conditions can influence the
\text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{3+}}
siting preferences in zeolites.
Knott et al. 2018
In zeolites that possess more than one type of symmetry-distinct tetrahedral (T-)site, those conditions can bias
\text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{3+}}
away from or towards particular T-site types, for instance in MFI
Yokoi et al. 2015
;
Pashkova et al. 2015
;
Biligetu et al. 2017
;
Nimlos et al. 2020
and FER
Román-Leshkov et al. 2011
;
Pinar et al. 2009
. These effects can be rationalized based on the relative access of charge compensating ions during synthesis to T-sites of distinct environment. On frameworks constructed from a single symmetry-distinct T-site, such as CHA, these influences are manifested in differences in the proximity of
\text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{3+}}
sites.
Di Iorio and Gounder 2016
;
Di Iorio et al. 2017
;
Di Iorio et al. 2020
;
zhang_importance_2020

CHA is formed from ABC stackings of double-six-ring (d6r) secondary building units and can be crystallized with N,N,N-trimethyl-1-adamantylammonium (
\text{TMAda}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
) OSDA
Zones 1985
. CHA zeolites crystallized solely with
\text{TMAda}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
are observed to exhibit no
\text{Co}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{2+}}
uptake capacity and to exchange
\text{Cu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{2+}}
only in its monovalent,
\text{CuOH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
form
Di Iorio and Gounder 2016
, both indicating that the framework contains no six-membered-rings (6MRs) containing two
\text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{3+}}
. In contrast, synthesis with
\text{Na}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
as a secondary, inorganic SDA results in an enrichment in these
\text{Al}{-}\text{Al}
pair features in the 6MRs
Di Iorio and Gounder 2016
. Vibrational spectroscopy provides independent verification of these differences
Kester et al. 2021
. Synthesis with the larger
\text{K}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
cation as the secondary SDA again results in CHA zeolites that lack the 6MR
\text{Al}{-}\text{Al}
pair feature
Di Iorio et al. 2020
.

Charge compensation thus has a determining effect on
\text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{3+}}
siting in CHA. Density functional theory (DFT) calculations show that the proximity-dependent energy of an
\text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{3+}}
pair depends sensitively on the identities of the charge-compensating ions
Fletcher et al. 2017
;
Li et al. 2018
. Energy is a decreasing function of
\text{Al}{-}\text{Al}
separation in the Brønsted form and exhibits minima at other separations in the presence of mono- (
\text{Na}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
)
Fletcher et al. 2017
or divalent (
\text{Cu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{2+}}
)
Li et al. 2018
cations. The oblong
\text{TMAda}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
OSDA is found to occupy the CHA cage in alignment with the long axis; further, the energy of an
\text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{3+}}
charge-compensated by
\text{TMAda}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
is a strong function of the separation between
\text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{3+}}
and the charged end of the OSDA, reflecting underlying electrostatic interactions
Li et al. 2019
. Similar calculations of
\text{TMAda}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
and
\text{Na}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
or
\text{K}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
co-occlusion within the CHA cage are consistent with the former pair resulting in an enrichment in 6MR
\text{Al}{-}\text{Al}
pairs while the latter promotes
\text{Al}{-}\text{Al}
pairs at greater separation
Di Iorio et al. 2020
.

These results highlight the potential to explore the relationship between OSDA and Al siting and proximity more generally. While DFT calculations in principle can provide reliable energy predictions, they are in general too expensive to be used to explore over a wide configuration space of
\text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{3+}}
and OSDA locations. Classical forcefields, however, can be evaluated rapidly and are well suited to capturing the non-bonded and electrostatic interactions most important to the relative energies of
\text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{3+}}
distributions in a field of OSDAs. To explore this approach, we focus here on the CHA/
\text{TMAda}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
system. As shown in the schematic representation in
Figure

1
, this system has the advantages of a single, symmetry-distinct T-site and an OSDA that can orient in only one of two equivalent directions within the zeolite cage
Li et al. 2019
. We start with the Dreiding force field successfully applied to neutral analogs of OSDAs
Mayo et al. 1990
and augment with charges derived from DFT calculations. We enumerate and explore all possible combinations of OSDA orientation and Al location (attending to Löwenstein’s rule) within a 36 T-site supercell. We find that interaction energies vary over
60\text{\,}\mathrm{k}\mathrm{J}\mathrm{/}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{6}\mathrm{r}
. Further, analysis of configurations reveals that energies are sensitive to
\text{Al}{-}\text{Al}
proximity, such that low energies are associated with
\text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{3+}}
pairs in 8-membered rings and higher energies associated with
\text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{3+}}
in smaller 6- and 4-membered rings. Comparisons with Al siting inferred from CHA zeolite crystallized with
\text{TMAda}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
suggests that these computed interaction energies are useful reporters of Al siting.

Simulation Details

2.1
DFT and Ab Initio Molecular Dynamics Simulations

DFT simulations were performed on 36 T-site supercells of varying Al configurations and
\text{TMAda}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
orientations using the Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package (VASP), version 5.4.1
Kresse and Furthmüller 1996
. Lattice constants were obtained from the Database of Zeolite Structures
Baerlocher and McCusker
. Core-valence interactions were treated using the projector augmented wave (PAW)
Blöchl 1994
;
Kresse and Joubert 1999
, exchange and correlation treated within the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) generalized gradient approximation (GGA)
Perdew and Wang 1992
, and the DFT model augmented with the D3 method to describe van der Waals interactions
Grimme et al. 2010
. Plane waves were included to a
400\text{\,}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}
cutoff and the first Brillouin zone sampled at the Gamma point only. Energies and forces for structures used to parameterize the charge model were converged to
1\text{\times}{10}^{-6}\text{\,}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}
and
0.03\text{\,}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}\mathrm{/}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolAngstrom}
, respectively (CONTCARs included in the Supporting Information). Single-point calculations were performed on the relaxed structures to generate AECCAR0, AECCAR2, and CHGCAR files required for performing subsequent atomic population analysis
Manz
.

Ab initio molecular dynamics (AIMD) simulations were performed at
633\text{\,}\mathrm{K}
in the canonical (NVT) ensemble, using a Nosé-Hoover thermostat with Nosé mass-parameter (SMASS) of
0.01
. A
1\text{\,}\mathrm{f}\mathrm{s}
time step was used, and hydrogen atoms were replaced by deuterium to accommodate a longer time step. Zeolite framework atoms (Si, Al
,
and O) were fixed at positions used in the classical simulations described below during the AIMD simulations to facilitate comparisons with the classical models. At each step, self-consistent-field (SCF) electronic energies were converged to
1\text{\times}{10}^{-5}\text{\,}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}
. Dynamics simulations were run for
10\text{\,}\mathrm{p}\mathrm{s}
for each configuration. The first
2.5\text{\,}\mathrm{p}\mathrm{s}
of the trajectory was discarded and the remaining
7.5\text{\,}\mathrm{p}\mathrm{s}
was used to calculate the average potential energy.

2.2
Classical Force Field Parameterization

We used the Dreiding force field
Mayo et al. 1990
, previously shown to provide good predictions for interactions between OSDAs and siliceous zeolite frameworks
Pophale et al. 2013
;
Davis et al. 2016
, to describe
\text{TMAda}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
and its van der Waals interactions with the silica-alumina CHA frameworks considered here.
We treated
\text{TMAda}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
as flexible and the CHA framework as rigid. Lattice constants and framework atom positions were fixed at those from the Database of Zeolite Structures
Baerlocher and McCusker
.

We augmented the Dreiding model with fixed partial charges on
\text{TMAda}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
and framework atoms to capture electrostatic interactions.
To derive the partial charges, we choose three arbitrary initial 36 T-site structures and three occluded
\text{TMAda}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
, relaxed the structures, and used the Density Derived Electrostatic and Chemical (DDEC) approach
Manz and Sholl 2010
;
Manz and Sholl 2012
;
Manz and Limas 2016
to extract atomic net charges. XYZ files containing raw partial charges obtained from the different minimizations are provided as a zipped file in Supporting Information. To reduce the number of distinct atom types, and based on analysis of the DDEC-derived charges, we characterized atoms based on distinct chemical environments (
Tables

1
and
2
). We reserved one atom type for Al and two atom types for O, including
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{b}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{b}}\text{}}
ions that connect Al and Si and
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{z}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{z}}\text{}}
ions that connect two Si. The Si charge is sensitive to the number of neighboring
\text{AlO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417pt}}
tetrahedra, leading to four distinct Si atom types.
\text{TMAda}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
C and H atoms are categorized based on their positions relative to the quaternary ammonium group.
Atomic charges were derived by averaging over raw charges from the three configurations and imposing overall electroneutrality. The procedure was repeated on ten additional relaxed DFT structures and charges found to vary by less than 5%. The relative energies are also insensitive to the partial charge variations, as two different partial charge sets only lead to
1.0\text{\,}\mathrm{k}\mathrm{J}\mathrm{/}\mathrm{m}\mathrm{o}\mathrm{l}_{d6r}
energy difference across tested configurations.

2.3
Classical Molecular Dynamics Simulations

The LAMMPS
Plimpton 1995
package was used to carry out all classical molecular dynamics (CMD) simulations. The cell parameters and locations of atoms, which were obtained from the Database of Zeolite Structures
Baerlocher and McCusker
, are the same as used in AIMD simulations.
Each simulation was equilibrated for
500\text{\,}\mathrm{p}\mathrm{s}
followed by a production run of
1500\text{\,}\mathrm{p}\mathrm{s}
, all using a time step of
0.2\text{\,}\mathrm{f}\mathrm{s}
.
The production runs were divided into three sections, from which three block-averaged potential energies and their standard deviations were calculated to reflect the fluctuation of the energies.
The NVT ensemble with the Nosé-Hoover
Nosé 1984
;
Hoover 1985
thermostat at
433\text{\,}\mathrm{K}
was applied.
A cutoff of
10\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolAngstrom}
was used for non-bonded and electrostatic interactions.
A standard long-range van der Waals tail correction was added to the energy and pressure, while a particle-particle particle-mesh solver
Hockney and Eastwood 2021
was used to describe the long-range electrostatics.

3
Results and Discussion

3.1
Al Configurations and OSDA Orientations

We seek to explore the relationship between Al siting,
\text{TMAda}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
orientation, and system energy. To that end, we systematically enumerated all combinations of Al location and
\text{TMAda}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
orientations possible within a 36 T-site supercell containing three unique cages, shown in
Figure

3
. Three Al were distributed over all possible T-sites, excluding those that contain Al-O-Al linkages that violate Löwenstein’s rule
loewenstein1954distribution
and are not properly described by the classical force field.
By enumerating all possibilities of Al residing on different T-site locations, we make sure that all possible Al configurations are adequately sampled, which brings us 4908 different Al combinations.
Three
\text{TMAda}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
were then introduced to these 4908 configurations either all in the same orientation (“AAA”) or with two
\text{TMAda}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
cations oriented in one direction and the third pointing in the opposite one (“AAB”), as illustrated in
Figure

3
, resulting in 9816 separate initial configurations that span all possibilities within the 36 T-site supercell.
Since the CHA zeolite is a high symmetry framework and the 36 T-site framework is symmetric from three directions, by only considering “AAA” and “AAB” we can still ensure that all different Al with
\text{TMAda}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
configurations have been investigated. All “AAA” and “AAB” structures have been provided in SI in the “xyz” format.

Figure 3
:
36 T-site CHA supercell. Periodic cell boundaries shown in blue, T-sites in yellow, oxygen in red. Left and right images illustrate two unique (“AAA” and “AAB”) occlusions of
\text{TMAda}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
, orientations highlighted with blue or green shading of cha cages and with arrows.

Molecular dynamics simulations were performed at
433\text{\,}\mathrm{K}
on each of these 9816 configurations. Consistent with prior work
Li et al. 2019
,
\text{TMAda}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
maintain their orientation throughout the simulation. Potential energy fluctuations, which were calculated from three block averages, during the MD simulations are less than
5\text{\,}\mathrm{k}\mathrm{J}\mathrm{/}\mathrm{m}\mathrm{o}\mathrm{l}_{d6r}
after initial equilibrations of
500\text{\,}\mathrm{p}\mathrm{s}
. The average potential energy
\langle U\rangle_{i}
of each configuration
i
was computed every
1000\text{\,}\mathrm{t}\mathrm{i}\mathrm{m}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{s}\mathrm{t}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{p}\mathrm{s}
and the relative energy of each configuration
\Delta U_{i}
was computed as

\Delta U_{i}=(\langle U\rangle_{i}-U_{\text{ref}})/N_{d6r}

(1)

where the reference potential energy
U_{\text{ref}}
is taken as the lowest energy configuration

U_{\text{ref}}=\min\limits_{i}\{\langle U\rangle_{i}\}

(2)

and the energy is normalized by the number of d6r units. The left panels of
Figure

4
report
\Delta U_{i}
, sorted from lowest to highest energy, for the Al configurations in a field of AAA- and AAB-oriented TMAda
+
, respectively. Several observations are immediately evident. First, energies span nearly
40
and
60\text{\,}\mathrm{k}\mathrm{J}\mathrm{/}\mathrm{m}\mathrm{o}\mathrm{l}_{d6r}
in the AAA and AAB data sets, respectively, reflecting a substantial sensitivity to Al configuration within a given field of
\text{TMAda}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
and different sensitivities to different fields. Second, the lowest energy Al configurations in the AAA and AAB sets are of similar energy. And third, a small handful of configurations dominate the low energy (and high energy) regimes.

:

Before analyzing the relationship between configurations and energy, we tested classical model predictions against AIMD results. We chose the ten lowest- and ten highest-energy configurations from the AAA and AAB sets and augmented with a number of intermediate energy configurations to create a basket of 72 configurations. 4
Conclusions

While the ability to guide zeolite structure through OSDA selection, and approaches to simulate this influence, are well established, the relationship between OSDA choice and the distribution of Al on a zeolite framework is less clear. CHA is an ideal template for exploring these effects, as it has only one symmetry-distinct T-site; all T-sites thus see identical void environments within the framework, and non-random Al distributions must reflect either kinetic or thermodynamic factors at play during crystallization. Here we explore the thermodynamics of Al distributions in the field of TMAda
+
structure directing agents, using a combination of
ab initio
and classical dynamics models of TMAda
+
occluded within the three cages of a CHA unit cell. System energies are observed to be sensitive both to Al proximity and to the field of TMAda
+
, and energy variations are consistent with a substantial contribution from OSDA-Al electrostatics, related to the ability of quaternary nitrogen to approach Al T-sites. Configurations the place Al pairs in 8MRs maximize favorable electrostatic contacts with
\text{TMAda}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
and are low in energy. Further, the energies of those configurations are less sensitive to
\text{TMAda}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
orientation than are Al pairs in smaller rings. This robustness to OSDA order (or disorder) may thus be a relevant factor determining Al distributions.
Al configurations that place two Al within the same 6MR are high in energy, consistent with experimental observations that these features are rare on CHA zeolite prepared with TMAda
+
as the sole structure-directing agent
Di Iorio and Gounder 2016
;
Di Iorio et al. 2017
. This correspondence suggests that lattice energies are, at least in this system, a useful predictor of Al siting preferences, as has been observed in similar simulations exploring the influence of
\text{Na}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
and
\text{TMAda}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
co-occlusion on Al siting
Di Iorio et al. 2020
.

The results highlight the potential to apply similar strategies to other OSDAs and frameworks. The CHA-TMAda
+
system is simplified by the fact that
\text{TMAda}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
can adopt only one of two primary orientations within the cha cage, and models here are limited to Al as the sole charge-carrying site on the framework. Further extensions will benefit from improvements in forcefield parameterization, in configurational sampling, and in model generalizations to framework compositions away from 1:1 OSDA to Al.

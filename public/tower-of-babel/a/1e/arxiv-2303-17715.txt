Elliptic Sklyanin Algebra, Baxter Equation and Discrete Liouville Equation.
Sergey M. Sergeev

Abstract
In this paper we discuss some properties of Baxter’s TQ equation for the eight-vertex elliptic Sklyanin algebra it its compact representation based on the elliptic Gamma-functions. As the main result, we establish the structure of the spectrum of the corresponding quantum integrable model.

In this paper we consider the Baxter’s TQ equation

[

(1)

\eta

is defined in specific representation related to the Baxter’s vectors.

The representation structure of

\Gamma
-function

-operators is based on Spiridonov integral identity
[

[

, and we will refer to such Ising-type weights as to the Master solution of the Yang-Baxter equation. However, the definition of
\mathcal{Q}

The TQ equation in our case reads

t(x|\tau)\mathcal{Q}(x)\;=\;\theta_{1}(x+y|\tau)^{N}\mathcal{Q}(x+\eta)+\theta_{1}(x-y|\tau)^{N}\mathcal{Q}(x-\eta)\;,

(2)

where

is the “auxiliary” transfer-matrix, and
y

\eta

\tau

The main subject of this paper is equation (

\mathcal{Q}(x+\eta+\tau)\mathcal{Q}(x)\;-\;\textrm{{\large e}}^{2\pi\mathsf{i}yN}\,\mathcal{Q}(x+\eta)\mathcal{Q}(x+\tau)\;=\;R(x,y)\;,

(3)

\Gamma
-function. We study these equations in several regimes where solutions
\mathcal{Q}(x)
have different structures. This will be the subject of Section
3

However, before this, in Section
2
we give an exposition of the method of Baxter’s vectors, eight-vertex “Solid-on-Solid” (SOS) model, representation of Sklyanin algebra, derivation of Ising-type weights and derivation of TQ equations.

Baxter equation

3.1
General considerations for
v<1
.

Now it is more convenient to proceed to variables
u,v,q,p
. Let

\mathcal{Q}(x)\;=\;\boldsymbol{\chi}(u)\;,\quad t(x|\tau)\;=\;\left(\frac{\mathsf{i}q^{1/8}(uv)^{-1/2}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\right)^{N}\boldsymbol{t}_{q}(u)\;.

(44)

Baxter’ TQ equations can be rewritten as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\boldsymbol{t}_{q}(u)\boldsymbol{\chi}(u)\;=\;h_{q}(uv)^{N}\boldsymbol{\chi}(pu)\;+\;v^{N}h_{q}(u/v)^{N}\boldsymbol{\chi}(u/p)\;,\\

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{t}_{p}(u)\boldsymbol{\chi}(u)\;=\;h_{p}(uv)^{N}\boldsymbol{\chi}(qu)\;+\;v^{N}h_{p}(u/v)^{N}\boldsymbol{\chi}(u/q)\;.\end{array}\right.

Note e.g. the symmetries of the transfer-matrix,

\boldsymbol{t}_{q}(qu)\;=\;\boldsymbol{t}_{q}(1/u)\;=\;(-u)^{-N}\boldsymbol{t}_{q}(u)\;,

so that the transfer-matrix allows for instance the following decomposition:

\boldsymbol{t}_{q}(u)\;=\;\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}t_{q,k}h_{q}(\omega^{k}u)^{N}\;,\quad\omega\;=\;\textrm{{\large e}}^{\frac{2\pi\mathsf{i}}{N}}\;,\quad t_{q,N-k}\;=\;t_{q,k}\;,

(48)

This is the discrete Liouville equation since the right hand side satisfies the discrete Laplace equation

|\digamma(u)|<1
, so that
\delta f_{k}\;\to\;0
exponentially, and therefore
\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(g)}(u)
gives the expression for the free energy:

\frac{1}{N}\log\boldsymbol{Q}(x)\;=\;\sum_{k\neq 0}\frac{p^{k}q^{k}}{k(1-p^{k})(1-q^{k})(1+p^{k}q^{k})}\left((uv)^{-k}+(v/u)^{-k}\right)\;,

(74)

explicitly in accordance with the results from
[
5
]
.

3.2
Special set for the case
v>1
.

Perturbation expansion from the previous subsection shows an interesting feature. Function
\boldsymbol{\chi}
(or
H
) in general position has denominators of the type
(1-p^{m}q^{n}v^{2})^{-1}
.

It gives a special set of values of parameter
y
,

y\;=\;-\lambda\;,\quad\lambda\;=\;\frac{m}{2}\eta+\frac{n}{2}\tau\;.

(75)

There is a temptation to call these set as
\displaystyle(\frac{m}{2},\frac{n}{2})
-spin set.
In this case
R(u)
has no denominator. Moreover, solution to (
45
) is given by

\mathcal{Q}(x)\;=\;A(x|\tau)A^{\prime}(x|\eta)+B(x|\tau)B^{\prime}(x|\eta)\;,

(76)

where

A(x|\tau)\;=\;\prod_{j=1}^{mN}\theta_{4}(\frac{x-x_{j}}{2}|\frac{\tau}{2})\;,\quad A^{\prime}(x|\eta)\;=\;\prod_{j=1}^{nN}\theta_{4}(\frac{x-x_{j}^{\prime}}{2}|\frac{\eta}{2})\;,

(77)

and
B(x|\tau)\;=\;A(x+1|\tau)
,
B^{\prime}(x|\eta)=A^{\prime}(x+1|\eta)
. Due to the requirement
A(-x)=A(x)
, the sets
\{x_{j}\}
and
\{x_{j}^{\prime}\}
are symmetric. These sets are subject of the Bethe-Ansatz-type equations,

\left(\frac{\displaystyle\theta_{\sigma(n)}(\frac{m}{2}\eta-x_{k}|\tau)}{\displaystyle\theta_{\sigma(n)}(\frac{m}{2}\eta+x_{k}|\tau)}\right)^{N}\prod_{j=1}^{mN}\frac{\displaystyle\theta_{1}(\frac{x_{k}-x_{j}+\eta}{2}|\frac{\tau}{2})}{\displaystyle\theta_{1}(\frac{x_{k}-x_{j}-\eta}{2}|\frac{\tau}{2})}\;=\;-1\;,

(78)

where

\sigma(\textrm{even})\;=\;4\;,\quad\sigma(\textrm{odd})\;=\;1\;,

(79)

and symmetric equations with
\eta\leftrightarrow\tau
,
n\leftrightarrow m
,
x_{j}\leftrightarrow x_{j}^{\prime}
. Note that in the Baxter’s classical case
n=0
and
m=1
,

\mathcal{Q}(x)\;=\;A(x|\tau)+B(x|\tau)\;\sim\;\prod_{j=1}^{N/2}\theta_{1}(x-\xi_{j}|\tau)\;,

(80)

so that the alternative Bethe variables
x_{j}
and
\xi_{j}
are non-trivially related.

It is interesting to note that in these exceptional cases we do not have the classification structure exposed in the previous subsection for
v<1
.

Abstract
. The current paper is an addition to the previous paper by author ( Overgroups of subsystem subgroups in exceptional groups : a 2 ​ A 1 2{A}_{1} -proof , (2020)), where the overgroup lattice of the elementary subsystem subgroup E ⁡ ( Δ , R ) E(\Delta,R) of the Chevalley group G ⁡ ( Φ , R ) G(\Phi,R) for a large enough root subsystem Δ \Delta was studied. Now we study the connection between the elementary subgroup E ^ ​ ( σ ) \widehat{E}(\sigma) given by the net of ideals σ \sigma of the ring R R , and the stabilizer S ⁡ ( σ ) S(\sigma) of the corresponding Lie subalgebra of the Chevalley algebra. In particular, we prove that under a certain condition the subgroup E ^ ​ ( σ ) \widehat{E}(\sigma) is normal in S ⁡ ( σ ) S(\sigma) , and we also study some properties of the corresponding quotient group.



1.
Introduction

In the paper
[
7
]
its authors suggested the following problem: to describe overgroups of the elementary subsystem subgroup
E(\Delta,R)
in the Chevalley group
G(\Phi,R)
for a large enough subsystem
\Delta
; the corresponding hypotheses were formulated in
[
8
]
. According to these hypotheses the description must be given in terms of nets of ideals of the ground ring
R
(see definition in Subsection
2.4
). Namely, for any overgroup
E(\Delta,R)\leqslant H\leqslant G(\Phi,R)
the ground ring
R
has a unique net of ideals
\sigma
called a
level
of this overgroup such that
E(\sigma)\leqslant H\leqslant N_{G(\Phi,R)}(E(\sigma))
, where the group
E(\sigma)
is defined as in Subsection
2.6
. Such answer is called a
standard description
or a
sandwich-classification
. Here by sandwich we mean the set of overgroups of some fixed level.

Most of that hypotheses with certain rectification were proved in the author’s paper
[
10
]
assuming that the ring
R
does not have a residue field of two elements. In that paper the known results for classical groups are not just generalised to exceptional groups but also are rectified in such a way to take into account irreducible components of type
A_{1}
in the subsystem
\Delta
. The rectification was that the normaliser of the group
E(\sigma)
was replaced by the stabiliser
S(\sigma)
of certain subalgebra
L(\sigma)
in Chevalley algebra
L(\Phi,R)
(see definitions in Subsections
2.5
and
2.6
). Such form of the answer was called a
pseudostandard description
.

It is easy to see that under certain assumption the inclusion
N_{G(\Phi,R)}(E(\sigma))\leqslant S(\sigma)
holds true (see Corollary 2 in
[
10
]
). Therefore, the question on normality of
E(\sigma)
in
S(\sigma)
arises; this question is, in fact, independent to results of
[
10
]
.

There are many interesting cases where the subsystem
\Delta
has irreducible components of type
A_{1}
; in these cases we do not have normality in general because the elementary group
E(2,R)
is not normal in
\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}\nolimits(2,R)
in general. In order to rectify that, we define the groups
\widehat{E}(\Delta,R)
and
\widehat{E}(\sigma)
by adding certain unipotents to the set of generators. If for a given subsystem and a given ring the pseudostandard description in sence of
[
10
]
holds true, then for overgroups of the subgroup
\widehat{E}(\Delta,R)
the similar description with
E(\sigma)
replaced by
\widehat{E}(\sigma)
holds true. In addition, in case where the subsystem
\Delta
has no irreducible components of type
A_{1}
, we have
\widehat{E}(\Delta,R)=E(\Delta,R)
and
\widehat{E}(\sigma)=E(\sigma)
.

In the present paper we prove under certain assumptions (see condition
(*)
in §
3
) that the subgroup
\widehat{E}(\sigma)
is normal in
S(\sigma)
(Theorem
3
). Our proof uses the same methods as the proof of normality of the elementary subgroup in Chevalley group given by Taddei in
[
23
]
. Before we prove Theorem
3
we will need to study the intersection of the group
S(\sigma)
with the congruence subgroup whose level is the Jacobson radical (Theorem
1
); also we will need to write down the generators of the group
S(\sigma)
in case where the ground ring is local (Theorem
2
).

Further we define the group
G(\sigma)
, which is something like the ”connected component” of the group
S(\sigma)
. Theorem
4
of the present paper shows that this subgroup is normal in
S(\sigma)
and the corresponding quotient group can be embedded in a product of certain subquotients of the Weyl group
W(\Phi)
. In case where the ground ring is Noetherian the number of factors is finite; hence the quotient group
S(\sigma)/G(\sigma)
is also finite. For the classical groups these results are known from the papers by Borevich, Vavilov and Shchegolev
[
2
,
5
,
4
,
20
,
13
,
14
]
.

Another result of the present paper is similar to the theorem proved in
[
18
]
about the
K_{1}
group being nilpotent. Namely, assuming, in addition, that the subsystem
\Delta
has no irreducible components if type
A_{1}
, we prove that the group
G(\sigma)/E(\sigma)
is nilpotent-by-abelian (Theorem
5
). Note that the special case of this Theorem was proved in the author’s paper
[
9
]
, where the problem on overgroups for subsystems
A_{l}\leqslant D_{l}
,
D_{5}\leqslant E_{6}
and
E_{6}\leqslant E_{7}
was studied.

We introduce all the necessary notation in §
2
; after that in §
3
we give precise statements for all the theorems mentioned above. The remaining Sections (§§4–10) contain proofs.

2.1.
Root systems and Chevalley groups

Let

We denote by

Reduction homomorphisms and congruence subgroups

If
\sigma
if a net of ideals
,

.

Given two nets of ideals

We denote by

We denote by

For a net of ideals

2.7.
Images of
\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}\nolimits_{2}

For a root
\alpha\in\Phi
we denote by
\varphi_{\alpha}
the homomorphism of algebraic groups

\varphi_{\alpha}\colon\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}\nolimits(2,R)\to G(\Phi,R)

such that

\varphi\left(\begin{pmatrix}1&\xi\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\right)=x_{\alpha}(\xi)\text{ and }\varphi\left(\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
\xi&1\end{pmatrix}\right)=x_{-\alpha}(\xi)\text{.}

For
\varepsilon\in R^{*}
we set

h_{\alpha}(\varepsilon)=\varphi_{\alpha}\left(\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon&0\\
0&\varepsilon^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\right)\text{.}

For
\xi
,
\zeta
,
\eta\in R
set

t_{\alpha}^{\zeta,\eta}(\xi)=\varphi_{\alpha}\left(\begin{pmatrix}1-\xi\zeta\eta&\xi\zeta^{2}\\
-\xi\eta^{2}&1+\xi\zeta\eta\end{pmatrix}\right)\text{.}

The matrix in the last notation is the transvection

e+\begin{pmatrix}\zeta\\
\eta\end{pmatrix}\xi\begin{pmatrix}-\eta&\zeta\end{pmatrix}\text{.}

Let us make the following observation.

Lemma 3
.

Let
\alpha\in\Phi,

g\in\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}\nolimits(2,R),

\xi,\zeta,\eta\in R,
and let

g\cdot\begin{pmatrix}\zeta\\
\eta\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\zeta_{1}\\
\eta_{1}\end{pmatrix}\text{.}

Then
\phantom{,}{}^{\varphi_{\alpha}(g)}t_{\alpha}^{\zeta,\eta}(\xi)=t_{\alpha}^{\zeta_{1},\eta_{1}}(\xi)
.

For every net of ideals
\sigma
we set

\displaystyle\widehat{E}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\widehat{E}(\Phi,\Delta,R,\sigma)

\displaystyle=\big\langle\{x_{\alpha}(\xi)\,:\,\alpha\in\Phi,\,\xi\in\sigma_{\alpha}\}\cup\{t_{\alpha}^{\zeta,\eta}(\xi)\,:\,\alpha\in\Delta,\;\xi,\zeta,\eta\in R\}\big\rangle\text{.}

Remarks
.

(1)

If a root
\alpha
belongs to a component of the subsystem
\Delta
with rank at least 2, then the generators
t_{\alpha}^{\zeta,\eta}(\xi)
are superfluous because they can be expressed by elementary root elements, see Lemma
14
. However, it was shown by Cohn
[
17
]
that this does not hold for the group
\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}\nolimits(2,R)
over the polynomial ring of two variables with coefficients in a field.

(2)

If we set

\quad\qquad\widehat{E}(\Delta,R)=\big\langle\big\{x_{\alpha}(\xi)\,:\,\alpha\in\Delta,\,\xi\in R\big\}\cup\{t_{\alpha}^{\zeta,\eta}(\xi)\,:\,\alpha\in\Delta,\;\xi,\zeta,\eta\in R\}\big\rangle,

then we have
\widehat{E}(\sigma)=\langle E(\sigma)\cup\widehat{E}(\Delta,R)\rangle
. Therefore, if for a given subsystem and a given ring the pseudostandard description of overgroups in sense of
[
10
]
holds, then for overgroups of the subgroup
\widehat{E}(\Delta,R)
the similar description holds with
E(\sigma)
being replaced by
\widehat{E}(\sigma)
.

(3)

The definition of the group
\widehat{E}(\sigma)
is motivated by the fact that we can prove its normality the group
S(\sigma)
. The subgroup
E(\sigma)
is not normal in
S(\sigma)
in general because the elementary group is not normal in
\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}\nolimits(2,R)
in general.

N. A. Vavilov suggested that the results of the present paper remain true if instead of using all the elements
t_{\alpha}^{\zeta,\eta}(\xi)
one use only the images of elements from the normal closure of elementary subgroup in
\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}\nolimits(2,R)
. However such set of generators is not so good when interacting with localisation; hence our proof would not work for it; the problem arises in the first item of Lemma
15
.

(4)

Elements
t_{\alpha}^{\zeta,\eta}(\xi)
are images of symplectic transvections (recall that
\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}\nolimits(2,R)=\mathop{\mathrm{Sp}}\nolimits(2,R)
). N. A. Vavilov also suggested that the subgroup of
\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}\nolimits(2,R)
generated by them coincides with the subgroup generated by all transvections; in other words, that Eichler group for
\mathop{\mathrm{Sp}}\nolimits(2,R)
coincides with Eichler group for
\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}\nolimits(2,R)
. However, we do not know any such results.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\sigma
be a net of ideals. Then
\widehat{E}(\sigma)\leqslant S(\sigma)
.

Proof.

Direct calculation.

2.8.
Localisation

Let
s\in R
be a non nilpotent element. Let
R_{s}=R[s^{-1}]
be the principal localisation. We denote by
F_{s}
the natural localisation homomorphism
F_{s}\colon R\to R_{s}
, and also the corresponding group homomorphism
F_{s}\colon G(R)\to G(R_{s})
.

Similarly, if
\mathfrak{P}
be a prime ideal, then we denote by
R_{\mathfrak{P}}
the localisation of the ring at this ideal; by
F_{\mathfrak{P}}
we denote the corresponding homomorphism.

We need the following lemma.

Lemma 5
.

Let
\sigma
be a net of ideals in
R
. Then

S(\sigma)=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{M}\in\mathop{\mathrm{Max}}\nolimits(R)}F_{\mathfrak{M}}^{-1}\big(S(F_{\mathfrak{M}}(\sigma))\big),

where
\mathop{\mathrm{Max}}\nolimits(R)
is the set of all maximal ideals in
R
.

Proof.

Inclusion of the left-hand side to the right-hand side is obvious. Further it is easy to see that

F_{\mathfrak{M}}^{-1}\big(S(F_{\mathfrak{M}}(\sigma))\big)\leqslant S\big(F_{\mathfrak{M}}^{-1}(F_{\mathfrak{M}}(\sigma))\big)

for any
\mathfrak{M}\in\mathop{\mathrm{Max}}\nolimits(R)
; and also that

L(\sigma)=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{M}\in\mathop{\mathrm{Max}}\nolimits(R)}L\big(F_{\mathfrak{M}}^{-1}(F_{\mathfrak{M}}(\sigma))\big)\text{,}

which implies the converse inclusion.
∎

2.9.
Affine schemes and generic elements

The functor
G(\Phi,-)
from the category of rings to the category of groups is an affine group scheme (a Chevalley–Demazure scheme). This means that its composition with the forgetful functor to the category of sets is representable, i.e.,

G(\Phi,R)=\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits(\mathbb{Z}[G],R).

The ring
\mathbb{Z}[G]
here is called the
ring of regular functions
on the scheme
G(\Phi,-)
.

An element
g_{\gen}\in G(\Phi,\mathbb{Z}[G])
that corresponds to the identity ring homomorphism is called the
generic element
of the scheme
G(\Phi,-)
. This element has a universal property: for any ring
R
and for any
g\in G(\Phi,R)
, there exists a unique ring homomorphism

f\colon\mathbb{Z}[G]\to R,

such that
f_{*}(g_{\gen})=g
. For details about application of the method of generic elements
to the problems similar to that of ours, see the paper of A. V. Stepanov
[
21
]
.

Further for a commutative ring
R
we set

R[G]=R\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}[G].

The image of the element
g_{\gen}
under the group homomorphism induced by the natural ring homomorphism
\mathbb{Z}[G]\to R[G]
we denote by

g_{\gen,R}\in G(R[G]).

This element also has a universal property: for any
R
-algebra
A
and any
g\in G(\Phi,A)
there exists a unique
R
-algebra homomorphism

f\colon R[G]\to A

such that
f_{*}(g_{\gen,R})=g
.

In particular,
augmentation homomorphism
is a unique
R
-algebra homomorphism

i\colon R[G]\to R

such that
i_{*}(g_{\gen,R})=e
. It’s kernel

I_{\aug,R}=\mathop{\mathrm{Ker}}\nolimits i

is called the
augmentation
ideal.

If
\xi\in R[G]
and
g\in G(\Phi,A)
for some
R
-algebra
A
, then we set
\xi(g)=f(\xi)
, where
f
is the
R
-algebra homomorphism as above. Therefore, elements of the ring
R[G]
can be considered as functions.

Note that if
\{\sigma_{i}\}
is a family of ideals in some ring, then we have

\bigcap_{i}S(\sigma_{i})\leqslant S\Big(\bigcap_{i}\sigma_{i}\Big)\text{.}

This allows us to introduce the following notation. Let
\sigma
be a net of ideals in the ring
R
. Denote by
\dot{\sigma}
the smallest net of ideals in
R[G]
such that
g_{\gen,R}\in S(\dot{\sigma})
and for any
\alpha\in\Phi
we have
\sigma_{\alpha}\otimes 1\subseteq\dot{\sigma}_{\alpha}
. It can be obtained as intersection of all nets with such properties.

We need the following lemma.

Lemma 6
.

Let
\sigma
be a net of ideals in the ring
R
; and let
g\in S(\sigma)
. Let
f\colon R[G]\to R
be such
R
-algebra homomorphism that
f_{*}(g_{\gen,R})=g
. Then
f(\dot{\sigma})\leqslant\sigma
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that
f^{-1}(\sigma)
is a net of ideals in
R[G]
such that
g_{\gen,R}\in S(f^{-1}(\sigma))
and for any
\alpha\in\Phi
we have

\sigma_{\alpha}\otimes 1\subseteq f^{-1}(\sigma)_{\alpha}.

Hence
\dot{\sigma}\leqslant f^{-1}(\sigma)
, which means exactly that
f(\dot{\sigma})\leqslant\sigma
.
∎

3.
Statements of the main results

In the rest of the paper, in particular in all the theorems below, we assume that the following condition holds true.

(
*
)

\begin{split}&\text{For any }\gamma\in\Phi\setminus\Delta\text{ there exists }\alpha\in\Delta\text{ such that }\alpha+\gamma\in\Phi\text{,}\\
&\text{and the corresponding }\text{structural constant }c_{\alpha,\gamma}\text{ of the Chevalley algebra}\\
&\text{is invertible in the ring }R\text{.}\end{split}

If
\sigma
is a net of ideals in
R
and
I\unlhd R
, then we set
(\sigma\cap I)_{\alpha}=\sigma_{\alpha}\cap I
. The collection of ideals
\sigma\cap I
satisfies only the first condition in the definition of a net; nevertheless, we consider the subgroup

E(\sigma\cap I)=\langle x_{\alpha}(\xi)\colon\alpha\in\Phi,\,\xi\in\sigma_{\alpha}\cap I\rangle\leqslant G(\Phi,R)\text{.}

Theorem 1
.

Let
\sigma
be a net of ideals. Let
J
be the Jacobson radical of the ring
R
. Then

S(\sigma)\cap G(\Phi,R,J)\leqslant T(\Phi,R,J)E(\sigma\cap J)\text{.}

We denote by
N(\Phi,-)\leqslant G(\Phi,-)
the scheme normaliser of the group subscheme
T(\Phi,-)
. There is a natural homomorphism from the scheme
N(\Phi,-)
to the Weyl group
W(\Phi)
viewed as a constant scheme. This homomorphism is surjective on points; and its kernel is the subscheme
T(\Phi,R)
.

We denote by
\overline{W}(\Phi)
the image of the group
N(\Phi,\mathbb{Z})
under homomorphism
G(\Phi,\mathbb{Z})\to G(\Phi,R)
induced by the unique ring homomorphism
\mathbb{Z}\to R
. We have dropped the letter
R
in this notation because this group almost independent of the ring: the group
\overline{W}(\Phi)
is isomorphic to the group
N(\Phi,\mathbb{Z})
if
2\neq 0
on
R
; otherwise, it is isomorphic to the group
W(\Phi)
.

Further let
\overline{W}(\Phi,\sigma)\leqslant\overline{W}(\Phi)
be the preimage of the group

W(\Phi,\sigma)=\{w\colon\forall\alpha\in\Phi\;\sigma_{w\alpha}=\sigma_{\alpha}\}\leqslant W(\Phi)\text{.}

It is easy to see that
\overline{W}(\Phi,\sigma)=\overline{W}(\Phi)\cap S(\sigma)
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
R
be a local ring; and let
\sigma
be a net of ideals in it. Then

S(\sigma)=T(\Phi,R)E(\sigma)\overline{W}(\Phi,\sigma)\text{.}

Theorem 3
.

Let
\sigma
be the net of ideals in the ring
R
. Then
\widehat{E}(\sigma)
is a normal subgroup of
S(\sigma)
.

By
\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\nolimits(R)
we denote the set of prime ideals of the ring
R
. Consider the following subgroup in
S(\sigma)

\displaystyle G(\sigma)\!=\!\big\{g\!\in\!G(\Phi,R)\,:\,\forall\mathfrak{P}\!\in\!\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\nolimits\,(R)\;\;F_{\mathfrak{P}}(g)\!\in\!T(\Phi,R_{\mathfrak{P}})E(F_{\mathfrak{P}}(\sigma))\!\leqslant\!G(\Phi,R_{\mathfrak{P}})\big\}\text{.}

It is clear that
\widehat{E}(\sigma)\leqslant G(\sigma)\leqslant S(\sigma)
; the second inclusion here follows from Lemma
5
.

Remark
.

It can be shown that the group
G(\sigma)
coincides with the net subgroup defined in
[
6
]
. However, the definition above is more convenient for our purposes.

Let
{\mathfrak{P}}\in\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\nolimits(R)
. Set
\Delta^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{P}}=\{\alpha\in\Phi\colon\sigma_{\alpha}\not\subseteq\mathfrak{P}\}
. It is easy to see that this is a closed subset of roots (i.e., if
\alpha
,
\beta\in\Delta^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{P}}
and
\alpha+\beta\in\Phi
, then
\alpha+\beta\in\Delta^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{P}}
). Then
\Delta^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{P}}\cap-\Delta^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{P}}
is a subsystem and it is invariant under the action of the group

Lemma 7
.

Let
\sigma
be a net of ideals. Let
g\in T(\Phi,R,J)E(\sigma\cap J)
. Then the element

can be written as

g=h\prod_{\gamma\in\Phi}x_{\gamma}(\xi_{\gamma}),

where
\xi_{\gamma}\in\sigma_{\gamma}\cap J,

h\in T(\Phi,R,J),
and the order of factors in the product coincides with the order
(\,\leqslant\,)
.

Proof.

The following elements will be called the generators of the group
T(\Phi,R,J)E(\sigma\cap J)
: the elements
x_{\gamma}(\xi)
, where
\xi\in\sigma_{\gamma}\cap J
, and the elements of the group
T(\Phi,R,J)
. The element

can be expressed by a word in these generators. Since
\sigma
is a net of ideals, it follows that after applying the following transformations of the word (which do not change the element expressed by this word) we again obtain a word in these generators.

(1)

For

and
\xi\in(\sigma_{\gamma}\cap J)
replace
hx_{\gamma}(\xi)
by
x_{\gamma}(\xi^{\prime})h
or vice versa, where
\xi^{\prime}
is
\xi
multiplied by suitable invertible element of the ring.

(2)

Replace two adjacented elements of
T(\Phi,R,J)
by their product.

(3)

Replace
x_{\gamma}(\xi_{1})x_{\gamma}(\xi_{2})
by
x_{\gamma}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})
.

(4)

For
\gamma_{1}\neq\pm\gamma_{2}
swap
x_{\gamma_{1}}(\xi_{1})
and
x_{\gamma_{2}}(\xi_{2})
adding factors from the Chevalley commutator formula.

(5)

Replace
x_{\gamma}(\xi_{1})x_{-\gamma}(\xi_{2})
by
x_{-\gamma}(\xi_{2}(1+\xi_{1}\xi_{2})^{-1})x_{\gamma}(\xi_{1}(1+\xi_{1}\xi_{2}))h_{\gamma}(1+\xi_{1}\xi_{2})

(1+\xi_{1}\xi_{2})
is invertible because

. The corresponding relation in the group

follows from the relation in

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}1&\xi_{1}\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
\xi_{2}&1\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
\xi_{2}(1+\xi_{1}\xi_{2})^{-1}&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&\xi_{1}(1+\xi_{1}\xi_{2})\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}(1+\xi_{1}\xi_{2})&0\\
0&(1+\xi_{1}\xi_{2})^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\text{.}

It is also easy to see that using these five types of transformations, any word can be transformed to the required shape.

R
. Then the following inclusion holds true

[S(\sigma)\cap\widetilde{F}_{s}^{-1}(E(\widetilde{F}_{s}(\sigma))),F_{s}^{-1}(E(F_{s}(\sigma)))]\leqslant E(\sigma)\text{.}

Proof.

Let
R_{i}
be the inductive system of finitely generated subrings of the ring
R
that contain the element
s
. Then it is easy to see that the following equalities holds true

\displaystyle S(\sigma)\cap\widetilde{F}_{s}^{-1}(E(\widetilde{F}_{s}(\sigma)))

\displaystyle=\varinjlim(S(\sigma\cap R_{i})\cap\widetilde{F}_{s}^{-1}(E(\widetilde{F}_{s}(\sigma\cap R_{i})))),

\displaystyle F_{s}^{-1}(E(F_{s}(\sigma)))

\displaystyle=\varinjlim(F_{s}^{-1}(E(F_{s}(\sigma\cap R_{i}))))\text{.}

Therefore, the proof is reduced to the case where
R
is finitely generates; hence
\widetilde{R}_{s}=\widehat{R}_{s}
.

Let
g\in S(\sigma)\cap\widetilde{F}_{s}^{-1}(E(\widetilde{F}_{s}(\sigma)))
and
h\in F_{s}^{-1}(E(F_{s}(\sigma)))
. Since the ring
R
is finitely generated and, in particular, Noetherian, we can apply Lemma
22
and take such a number
N
that
[S(\sigma,(s^{N})),h]\subseteq E(\sigma)
.

Since the projection onto quotient ring
\pr\colon R\to R/(s^{N})
factors through homomorphism
\widetilde{F}_{s}
, it follows that
\pr_{*}(g)\in E(\pr(\sigma))
. Since the group
E(\sigma)
maps onto the group
E(\pr(\sigma))
surjectively, it follows that
g=g_{1}g_{2}
, where
g_{1}\in E(\sigma)
and
g_{2}\in S(\sigma,(s^{N}))
. Using the definition of the number
N
and Theorem
3
, we obtain that

[g,h]=[g_{1}g_{2},h]=\phantom{,}^{g_{1}}[g_{2},h][g_{1},h]\in E(\sigma)\text{.}\qed

Lemma
20
, and Theorem
3
allow us to give the following definition:

G_{d}(\sigma)=\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\colon R\to A\\
\delta(A)\leqslant d\end{subarray}}\mathop{\mathrm{Ker}}\nolimits(G(\sigma)\to G(f(\sigma))/\widehat{E}(f(\sigma)))\text{.}

Lemma 24
.

In the previous notation the subgroup
G_{0}(\sigma)
is normal in the group
G(\sigma),
and the corresponding quotient group is abelian.

Proof.

It is enough to prove that
[G(\sigma),G(\sigma)]\leqslant G_{0}(\sigma)
, i.e. that for any homomorphism
f\colon R\to A
to a semilocal ring we have
f_{*}([G(\sigma),G(\sigma)])\leqslant E(f(\sigma))
. Applying Lemma
20
, we may assume that
R=A
and
f=\id
. In this case the statement follows from Lemma
21
.
∎

Lemma 25
.

In the previous notation assume that the subsystem
\Delta
has no irreducible components of type
A_{1}
. Then
[G_{d}(\sigma),G_{0}(\sigma)]\leqslant G_{d+1}(\sigma)
for any
d\geqslant 0
.

Proof.

We must prove that for any homomorphism

f\colon R\to A,\ \text{ where }\ \delta(A)\leqslant d+1,

we have
f_{*}([G_{d}(\sigma),G_{0}(\sigma)])\leqslant E(f(\sigma))
. Applying Lemma
20
, we may assume that
R=A
and
f=\id
. Then there exists a finite collection of maximal ideals
\mathfrak{M}_{1}
,
\dots
,
\mathfrak{M}_{k}
of the ring
R
such that for any
s\in R\setminus\left(\bigcup_{k}\mathfrak{M}_{k}\right)
we have
\delta(\widetilde{R}_{s})\leqslant d
.

Let
g\in G_{d}(\sigma)
and
h\in G_{0}(\sigma)
. Consider the multiplicative set
S=R\setminus\left(\bigcup_{k}\mathfrak{M}_{k}\right)
. The ring
R[S^{-1}]
is semilocal; hence by definition of the group
G_{0}(\sigma)
there exists an element
s\in S
such that
F_{s}(h)\in E(F_{s}(\sigma))

\mathfrak{M}_{1}
,
\dots
,
\mathfrak{M}_{k}
and by definition of the group
G_{d}(\sigma)
, we have
\widetilde{F}_{s}(g)\in E(\widetilde{F}_{s}(\sigma))
. The statement, therefore, follows from Lemma
23
.
∎

Intuitionism with Truth Tables: A Decision Procedure for IPC Based on RNMatrix
–, Renato Leme, Marcelo Coniglio, Bruno Lopes --

Abstract
This paper provides a new decision procedure for intuitionistic propositional logic ( IPC ) based on restricted non-deterministic matrix and proves its soundness and completeness.



1
Introduction

A famous result, proved first by K. Gödel in
1932
, says that it is impossible to characterize intuitionistic propositional logic (
IPC
) via a finite-valued (deterministic) matrix
[
1
]
. In other words, Gödel proved that there is no effective decision procedure based on a finite-valued deterministic matrix for
IPC
.

Later, in
1940
, a maybe less famous but equally important result was proved by Dugundji, who adapted Gödel’s argument to establish that the same is also true about the family of modal logics between
S1
and
S5

[
1
]
.

To overcome Dugundji’s result, J. Kearns
[
5
]
introduced a
4
-valued nmatrix (non-deterministic matrix) for modal logics
KT
,
S4
, and
S5
and restricted the set of valuations to a subset called “level valuations.” He proved that this restricted nmatrix (hereafter called RNmatrix
[
3
]
) constitutes sound and complete semantics for these modal logics.

Unfortunately, in principle, Kearns’s level valuations fail to provide an effective decision procedure for any of the modal logics. More than that, until recently, it was unknown whether obtaining such a decision procedure from level valuations would be possible.

(…) the interesting problem of determining whether or not a decision procedure can be obtained by means of level valuations remains open.
[
2
]

L. Grätz recently solved this problem for some modal systems
[
4
]
. He refined Kearn’s original RNmatrix to obtain a decidable
3
-valued RNmatrix for modal logics
KT
and
S4
. He noticed that, with an appropriate notion of
partial valuation
for level semantics (i.e., preserving analyticity and co-analyticity), the truth tables could be obtained by removing any spurious row eventually produced in the valuation process.

In this paper, we will try to overcome the original problem posed by Gödel on the characterizability of
IPC
via finite-valued matrix. We will propose a decidable nmatrix for
IPC
combining Grätz’s RNmatrix for
S4
and Gödel-McKinsey-Tarski’s box translation.

3.1
Grätz’s decision procedure for S4

While the soundness and completeness of level valuations for
S4
(with the rule of necessitation) were already known, it was only recently that an effective decision procedure was discovered.

Before that, J. V. Ivlev seems to have suggested the following approach
[
4
]
: remove every partial valuation
v_{p}
whenever (1)
v_{p}(\square A)\notin D
and (2)
w_{p}(A)\in D
for every partial valuation
w_{p}
. The reason for this method is very intuitive if we consider the definition of level valuations, and indeed, it works for two-valued semantics, as well as for some examples in
\mathcal{M}_{S4}
, such as
\square\alpha
where
\alpha
is any propositional tautology. However, this criterion is not sufficient for
\mathcal{M}_{S4}
because it leaves behind invalid partial level valuations (consider the valid formula
\square p\rightarrow\square\neg\neg p
in
\mathcal{M}_{S4}
).

In
[
4
]
, Grätz identified the need to find a suitable method by refining the concept of
partial valuation
. To address this, he suggests a new definition for partial level valuations:

Definition 3.6

(Grätz’s partial level valuation)
.

Let
\Delta\subseteq For(\Sigma)
be a set closed under subformulas and
v_{p}:\Delta\rightarrow\{0,1,2\}
be a partial valuation in
\mathcal{M}_{S4}
.
v_{p}
is a partial
level
valuation (PLV) in
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{M}_{S4})
iff

1.

\forallAlt\alpha\in\Delta
such that
v_{p}(\alpha)=1
, there is a PLV
w_{p}
such that
w_{p}(\alpha)=0
and,
\forallAlt\beta\in\Delta
,
w_{p}(\beta)=2
whenever
v_{p}(\beta)=2
.

Theorem 3.7
.

\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{M}_{S4})
is analytic and co-analytic with PLV.

and proved that this notion is analytic in
\mathcal{M}_{S4}
(i.e, every partial level valuation in
\mathcal{M}_{S4}
can be extended to a level valuation in
\mathcal{M}_{S4}
) and co-analytic (i.e, for any level valuation
v
in
\mathcal{M}_{S4}
, a restriction of
v
to a partial level valuation
v_{p}
closed to subformulas is a partial level valuation). He also provides definitions and proofs for the modal logic
KT
.

Definition 3.8
.

Let
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{M}_{S4})=\langle\mathcal{M}_{S4},\mathcal{L}_{S4}\rangle
as in Definition
3.5
.

\Vdash^{\mathcal{L}}_{S4}\alpha\text{ iff for every }v\in\mathcal{L}_{S4},v(\alpha)\in\mathcal{D}

Using this, one can easily prove that the truth table method is sound and complete, that is,

Theorem 3.9
.

\Vdash^{\mathcal{L}}_{S4}\alpha
if, and only if, for every
v_{p}
, if
v_{p}
is
PLV
, then
v_{p}(\alpha)\in\mathcal{D}
.

Therefore, to construct a truth table, compute every partial valuation and remove any non-PLV.

Decision procedure for IPC

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\Omega=\{\{\neg\},\{\land,\lor,\rightarrow\}\}
be a signature. We will denote the set of every intuitionistic wf as
For(\Omega)
.

4.1
Gödel-McKinsey-Tarski Box Translation

Definition 4.2
.

(Box translation function)
Let
f_{\square}:For(\Omega)\rightarrow For(\Sigma)
be a function such that:

i)

f_{\square}(p_{n})=\square p_{m}
, where
n=m

ii)

f_{\square}(\neg\alpha)=\square\neg f_{\square}(\alpha)

iii)

f_{\square}(\alpha\#\beta)=\square(f_{\square}(\alpha)\#f_{\square}(\beta))

where
\#\in\{\rightarrow,\lor,\land\}
.

It is widely known that this translation is faithful
[
6
]
.

4.2
Truth tables for intuitionistic logic

We introduce the truth tables for intuitionistic logic based on the presented translation.

4.2.1
Intuitionistic non-deterministic valuations

Remark 4.3
.

If
v
is a valuation in
\mathcal{M}_{S4}
, then, for every
\alpha\in For(\Sigma)
,
v(\square\alpha)=\square^{\mathcal{A}_{S4}}(v(\alpha))
. This follows from the fact that the multioperation
\square^{\mathcal{A}_{S4}}
is deterministic.

Definition 4.4
.

We define the function
h:For(\Omega)\rightarrow For(\Sigma)
as follows:

i)

h(p_{n})=p_{m}
, where
n=m

ii)

h(\neg\alpha)=\neg f_{\square}(\alpha)

iii)

h(\alpha\#\beta)=f_{\square}(\alpha)\#f_{\square}(\beta)

where
\#\in\{\rightarrow,\lor,\land\}
. We call
h
as the semi-translation function.

Lemma 4.5
.

For every
\alpha\in For(\Omega)
,
f_{\square}(\alpha)=\square(h(\alpha))
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha
be an intuitionistic formula. We proceed by case analysis.

Case
\alpha=p_{n}

In this case,
f_{\square}(\alpha)=f_{\square}(p_{n})=\square p_{m}
. Also,
\square(h(\alpha))=\square(h(p_{n}))=\square p_{m}
. Note that
n=m
in both cases. Hence,
f_{\square}(p_{n})=\square(h(p_{n}))
.

Case
\alpha=\neg\beta

By Definition
4.4
,
\square(h(\neg\beta))=\square(\neg f_{\square}(\beta))
. Moreover, by Definition
4.2
,
f_{\square}(\neg\beta)=\square(\neg f_{\square}(\beta))
. Hence,
f_{\square}(\neg\beta)=\square(h(\neg\beta))
.

Case
\alpha=\beta\#\gamma
where
\#\in\{\rightarrow,\land,\lor\}

By Definition
4.4
, we have that for each
\#
,
\square(h(\beta\#\gamma))=\square(f_{\square}(\beta)\#f_{\square}(\gamma))
. Moreover, by Definition
4.2
,
f_{\square}(\beta\#\gamma)=\square(f_{\square}(\beta)\#f_{\square}(\gamma))
. Therefore,
f_{\square}(\beta\#\gamma)=\square(h(\beta\#\gamma))
.

∎

4.3
Soundness and completeness

Grätz proved the analyticity and co-analyticity of level semantics with PLV.

Theorem 4.19
.

The level semantics
\mathcal{L}
(
\mathcal{M}_{S4}
) is co-analytic and analytic with PLV.

That is,

Analyticity

Every PLV can be extended to a valuation
v\in\mathcal{L}_{S4}
.

Co-analyticity

Let
v\in\mathcal{L}_{S4}
. If
\Delta\subseteq For(\Sigma)
is closed under subformulas, then
v\arrowvert_{\Delta}
is a PLV.

Definition 4.20

(Valid intuitionistic formula)
.

Let
\alpha\in For(\Omega)
. We say that
\alpha
is a valid intuitionistic formula (denoted as
\Vdash_{{IPC}}\alpha
) iff for every
\tilde{v}_{p}
, if
\tilde{v}_{p}
is iPLV, then
\tilde{v}_{p}(\alpha)=\textbf{T}
.

Theorem 4.21

(Soundness)
.

Let
\alpha\in For(\Omega)
. If
\Vdash_{IPC}\alpha
, then
\Vdash^{\mathcal{L}}_{S4}f_{\square}(\alpha)
.

Proof.

Assume that
\Vdash_{IPC}\alpha
for some
\alpha\in For(\Omega)
.

Let
v_{p}
be an arbitrary PLV and
\tilde{v}_{p}
be the partial valuation induced by
v_{p}
. By iPLV Lemma [
4.16
],
\tilde{v}_{p}
is an iPLV. Hence, by the definition of validity [
4.20
],
\tilde{v}_{p}(\alpha)=\textbf{T}=\langle 2;2\rangle
. Therefore, by the definition of intuitionistic valuation function [
4.7
],
v_{p}(f_{\square}(\alpha))=2
. Hence,
v_{p}(f_{\square}(\alpha))\in D
. By truth table completeness [
3.9
],
\Vdash^{\mathcal{L}}_{S4}f_{\square}(\alpha)
.
∎

Theorem 4.22

(Completeness)
.

Let
\alpha\in For(\Omega)
. If
\Vdash^{\mathcal{L}}_{S4}f_{\square}(\alpha)
, then
\Vdash_{IPC}\alpha
.

Proof.

Assume that
\Vdash^{\mathcal{L}}_{S4}f_{\square}(\alpha)
. Let
v_{p}
be some PLV. By hypothesis and
f_{\square}
Lemma [
4.8
],
v_{p}(f_{\square}(\alpha))=2
. Let
\tilde{v}_{p}
be the partial valuation induced by
v_{p}
. By iPLV Lemma [
4.16
],
\tilde{v}_{p}
is iPLV. Therefore,

\tilde{v}_{p}(\alpha)=\langle v_{p}(h(\alpha)),v_{p}(f_{\square}(\alpha))\rangle=\langle v_{p}(h(\alpha)),2\rangle

Using Truth Lemma [
4.9
], we conclude that
v_{p}(h(\alpha))=2
. Therefore,
\tilde{v}_{p}(\alpha)=\langle 2;2\rangle=\textbf{T}
. Hence,
\Vdash_{IPC}\alpha
.

∎

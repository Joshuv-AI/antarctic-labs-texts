Pavel Gvozdevsky*

Abstract
. We prove that the width of any word in a simply connected Chevalley group of rank at least 2 over the ring that is a localisation of the ring of integers in a number field is bounded by a constant that depends only on the root system and on the degree of the number field.

1.
Introduction

In the remarkable paper
[
1
]
, Nir Avni and Chen Meiri studied the width of words in
\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}\nolimits(n,\mathbb{Z})
. In particular, they proved
[
1
, Theorem 1.2]
that the width of any word in
\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}\nolimits(n,\mathbb{Z})
,
n\geqslant 3
is bounded by some constant that depend only on
n
. In the present paper, being inspired by this result, we extend it in the following ways. Firstly, we generalise it to all Chevalley groups. Secondly, we generalize it to all arithmetic rings. Thirdly, we simplify some parts of the proof. Now we explain the setting of the problem in details.

For a group
G

a word map
is a map

G^{n}\to G\text{,}\qquad(g_{1},\ldots,g_{m})\mapsto w(g_{1},\ldots,g_{m})\text{,}

where
w
is a word of
m
variables (i.e an element of the free group on
m
generators), and where
w(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n})
is what we get if we substitute the elements
g_{1}
,
\ldots
,
g_{n}
into this word.

In recent studies of word maps, the case of
G=\mathcal{G}(K)
, where
\mathcal{G}
is a simple or semisimple algebraic group defined over a field
K
, is of particular interest, see references in
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
. Almost all the results in this direction have been proved for the cases where
K
is an algebraically closed field, or where
\mathcal{G}
is either split or anisotropic over
K
.

Given a word
w=w(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})\in F_{d}
and a group
G
, the set of
w
-values is the union of the word map image with its inverse.

w(G)=\{w(g_{1},\ldots,g_{k}),w(g_{1},\ldots,g_{k})^{-1}\mid g_{i}\in G\}\text{.}

The width of the word
w
in the group
G
is a minimal constant
C
such that
\langle w(G)\rangle=w(G)^{C}
, where
\langle w(G)\rangle
is the group generated by
w(G)
and
w(G)^{C}
is the set of elements that can be writen as a product of
C
elements from
w(G)
.

To put the results of the present paper in a context, we now recall some results that are known at the moment.

\bullet
If
G
is a connected simple algebraic group over an algebraically closed fied, and
w
is a non-trivial word, then
w(G)
contains an open dense subset, see
[
5
]
; hence the width of
w
is at most 2.

\bullet
The set of word values in the unitary group
U_{n}
can have arbitrarily small radii, see
[
6
]
. Hence the width of
w
can be arbitrarily large (but it is finite for every
w
).

\bullet
For any word
w
, if a finite simple group
G
is large enough (the meaning of "large enough" depends on
w
), then the width of
w
in
G
is at most 2, see
[
7
]
.

\bullet
For any word
w
, if a classical connected real compact Lie group

has large enough rank (the meaning of "large enough" depends on
w
), then the width of
w
in
G
is at most 2, see
[
8
]
.

\bullet
If
G
is a Chevalley group over an infinite field, then the width of any word in
G
is at most 5, also
[
8
]
.

\bullet
If
D
is a skew-field that has finite dimension over its center, then the width of
w=[x,y]
in
\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}\nolimits(n,D)
is at most [the width of
w=[x,y]
in
D^{*}
divided by

(upper integer part)]+1 and at least
(
[the width of
w=[x,y]
in
D^{*}
]
+2n^{2}-3n+1):(8n^{2}-13n+8)
, see
[

]
, also see
[

]
,
[

]
,
[

]
, and
[

.

\bullet
If

is large enough, then the width of
w=[x,y]
in

is at most 6, see
[

. The question of whether it is equal to one is wide open.

\bullet
If
G(R)
is a Chevalley group of rank at least 2 over a commutative ring

has finite width if and only if

[

\bullet
The width of any word in

[

in

In the present paper we study the width of words in Chevalley groups over a localisation of the ring of integers in a number field. Our main result is the following theorem, which is a generalisation of
[

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Phi
be an irreducible root system with
\mathop{\mathrm{rk}}\nolimits\Phi\geqslant 2
. Let
d
be a positive integer. Then there exists a constant
C=C(\Phi,d)
such that for any ring
R
satisfying:

(1)

R
is a localisation of the ring of integers in a number field
K
, with
[K\colon\mathbb{Q}]=d
;

(2)

if
\Phi=C_{2}
of
G_{2}
, then
R
has no residue field of two elements;

the width of any word in
G_{\mathrm{sc}}(\Phi,R)
does not exceed
C(\Phi,d)
.

The paper is organised as follows. In Section
2

2.
Basic notation

2.1.
Words and verbal width

A word is an element in a free group. Given a word
w=w(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})\in F_{d}
and a group
G
, we have the word map
w\colon G^{k}\to G
defined by substitution. The set of
w
-values is the set

w(G)=\{w(g_{1},\ldots,g_{k}),w(g_{1},\ldots,g_{k})^{-1}\mid g_{i}\in G\}\text{.}

The width of a word
w
in a group
G
is a minimal constant
C
such that
\langle w(G)\rangle=w(G)^{C}
. The verbal width of a group
G
is the supremum of the widths of all words.

2.2.
Chevalley groups

Let
\Phi
be an irreducible root system in the sense of
[
16
]
,
R
a commutative associative ring with unity,
G(\Phi,R)=G_{\mathrm{sc}}(\Phi,R)
a simply connected Chevalley group of type
\Phi
over
R
,
T(\Phi,R)
a split maximal torus of
G(\Phi,R)
. For every root
\alpha\in\Phi
we denote by
X_{\alpha}=\{x_{\alpha}(\xi),\colon\xi\in R\}
the corresponding unipotent root subgroup with respect to
T
. We denote by
E(\Phi,R)
the elementary subgroup generated by all
X_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in\Phi
.

For an ideal
I\unlhd R
we denote by
\rho_{I}
the reduction homomorphism

\rho_{I}\colon G(\Phi,R)\to G(\Phi,R/I)\text{,}

induced by projection
R\to R/I
.

The kernel of this homomorphism
G(\Phi,R,I)=\mathop{\mathrm{Ker}}\nolimits(\rho_{I})\leqslant G(\Phi,R)
is called the principal congruence subgroup. Set
E(\Phi,I)=\langle x_{\alpha}(\xi)\text{,}\,\alpha\in\Phi\text{,}\,\xi\in I\rangle
. Then, by definition, the relative elementary subgroup
E(\Phi,R,I)
of level
I
is the normal closure of
E(\Phi,I)
in
E(\Phi,R)
, actually it is automatically normal in
G(\Phi,R)
.

2.3.
Affine schemes and generic elements

The functor
G(\Phi,-)
from the category of rings to the category of groups is an affine group scheme (a Chevalley–Demazure scheme). This means that its composition with the forgetful functor to the category of sets is representable, i.e.,

G(\Phi,R)=\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits(\mathbb{Z}[G],R).

The ring
\mathbb{Z}[G]
here is called the ring of regular functions on the scheme
G(\Phi,-)
.

An element
g_{\mathrm{gen}}\in G(\Phi,\mathbb{Z}[G])
that corresponds to the identity ring homomorphism is called the generic element of the scheme
G(\Phi,-)
. This element has a universal property: for any ring
R
and for any
g\in G(\Phi,R)
, there exists a unique ring homomorphism

f\colon\mathbb{Z}[G]\to R,

such that
f_{*}(g_{\mathrm{gen}})=g
. For details about application of the method of generic elements
to the problems similar to that of ours, see the paper of A. V. Stepanov
[
17
]
.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We need several lemmas. The next lemma is a simple technical group-theoretic result.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be a group, and let
X\subseteq G
be a symmetric set containing identity element such that there are
k
translates of
X
that cover
G
. Then we have
X^{3k-2}=\langle X\rangle
.

Proof.

Assume that
G=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}g_{i}X
, without loss of generality we may assume that
g_{1}
is the identity element. Let
g\in\langle X\rangle
, and let
h_{0}
,
\ldots
,
h_{n}
be the shortest sequence such that
h_{0}
is the identity element,
h_{j+1}\in h_{j}X
for all
0\leqslant j\leqslant n-1
, and
h_{n}=g
. We must prove that
n\leqslant 3k-2
.

For all
0\leqslant j\leqslant n
let
h_{j}\in g_{i_{j}}X
with
i_{0}=1
. If for some
j
and
j^{\prime}
we have
j^{\prime}-j>2
and
i_{j}=i_{j^{\prime}}
, then our sequence can be shortened, because
h_{j^{\prime}}\in h_{j}X^{2}
. In particular this means that for any
i
we have
|\{j\mid i_{j}=i\}|\leqslant 3
. Similarly, if for some
j>1
we have
i_{j}=1
, then we can shorten our sequence by removing everything between
h_{0}
and
h_{j}
; hence
|\{j\mid i_{j}=1\}|\leqslant 2
.

Therefore, we have
n+1\leqslant 3(k-1)+2
, or
n\leqslant 3k-2
.
∎

Let
G=G(\Phi,-)
be a simply connected Chevalley–Demazure group scheme. Let
\mathbb{Z}[G]
be the corresponding ring of regular functions. The center of
G(\Phi,-)
is a closed subscheme, so it corresponds to some ideal in
\mathbb{Z}[G]
. Let us fix some generators
\{\eta_{i}\}_{i=1}^{i_{\max}}
of that ideal. For example we can take
\eta_{i}
to be the differences between entries of the adjoint representation with the corresponding entries of the identity matrix.

Further we need an effective version of the well known result by Abe about classification of normal subgroups in Chevalley groups (see
[
18
]
).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\Phi
be an irreducible root system with
\mathop{\mathrm{rk}}\nolimits\Phi\geqslant 2
. Set
e=1
if
\Phi
is of type ADE,
e=2
if
\Phi
is of type BCF, and
e=3
if
\Phi=G_{2}
. Let
\eta_{i}
be as above. Then the following holds.

(1)

Let
\Phi
be distinct from
C_{2}
and
G_{2}
. Then there exists a constant
C=C(\Phi)
such that for any commutative ring
R
and for any
g\in G(\Phi,R)
each of the following elements can be decomposed as a product of at most
C
elements conjugate to
g
or
g^{-1}
:

•

elements
x_{\alpha}(\xi\eta_{i}(g))
,
\xi\in R
,
\alpha\in\Phi
if
\Phi
is of type ADE;

•

elements
x_{\alpha}(\xi\eta_{i}(g))
,
\xi\in R
,
\alpha
is a short root if
\Phi
is not of type ADE;

•

elements
x_{\alpha}(\xi\eta_{i}(g)^{e})
and
x_{\alpha}(e\xi\eta_{i}(g))
,
\xi\in R
,
\alpha
is a long root if
\Phi
is not of type ADE.

(2)

Let
\Phi=C_{2}
,
G_{2}
, and let
l
be a positive integer. Then there exists a constant
C=C(\Phi,l)
such that for any commutative ring
R
containing elements
t_{1}
,
\ldots
,
t_{l}
such that the set
\{t_{i}^{2}+t_{i}\colon 1\leqslant i\leqslant l\}
generates a unit ideal and for any
g\in G(\Phi,R)
each of the elements as above can be decomposed as a product of at most
C
elements conjugate to
g
or
g^{-1}
.

Proof.

First let us prove that the elements in question must belong to the normal subgroup generated by
g
.

Let
H\unlhd G(\Phi,R)
be the normal subgroup generated by
g
. By
[
18
, Theorem 3]
there exists a so called admissible pair
(A,B)
such that

E(R,A,B)\leqslant H\leqslant E^{*}(R,A,B)\text{,}

where
E(R,A,B)
is a normal subgroup of
E(\Phi,R)
generated by
x_{\alpha}(\xi)
,
\xi\in A
,
\alpha
is a short root, and
x_{\beta}(\zeta)
,
\zeta\in B
,
\beta
is a long root (in case where
\Phi
is of type ADE, we have
A=B
); and

E^{*}(R,A,B)=\{h\in G(\Phi,R)\mid[h,E(\Phi,R)]\leqslant E(R,A,B)\}\text{.}

In case
\Phi=C_{2}
,
G_{2}
this theorem requires that
R
has no residue field of two elements, which we ensure by requiring that the set
\{t_{i}^{2}+t_{i}\colon 1\leqslant i\leqslant l\}
generate a unit ideal.

By definition of an admissible pair
A
is an ideal, and
B\subseteq A
; hence
E(R,A,B)
is trivial modulo
A
. Therefore, modulo
A
, the element
g
commutes with the elementary subgroup, and hence must be central (see, for example
[
19
, Main Theorem]
). Therefore, we have
\eta_{i}(g)\in A
for all
g
, which proves that the elements of the first and the second type are in
H
.

Also by definition of an admissible pair, the ideal generated by
e\xi
and
\xi^{e}
for
\xi\in A
is contained in
B
, which proves that the elements of the third type are in
H
.

Now let us prove that the number of conjugates of
g
and
g^{-1}
required to express these elements can be bounded by a constant that depends only on
\Phi
or, if
\Phi=C_{2}
,
G_{2}
, on
\Phi
and
l
.

We start with the case
\Phi\neq C_{2}
,
G_{2}
. Consider the ring
\mathbb{Z}[G][\xi]
, i.e the ring of polynomials over the ring of regular functions. Let
g=g_{\mathrm{gen}}
be the generic element. By what we proved previously, the elements
x_{\alpha}(\xi\eta_{i})
(or
x_{\alpha}(\xi\eta_{i}^{e})
and
x_{\alpha}(e\xi\eta_{i})
in case of long roots in multiply laced systems) are products of conjugates of
g
and
g^{-1}
. Take
C
to be the maximum of the corresponding numbers of conjugates. Since
g
can be specialised to any element of the group over any ring and simultaneously the variable
\xi
can be specialised to any element of this ring, it follows that the bound
C
works in all the cases.

In case
\Phi=C_{2}
,
G_{2}
the proof is similar. The only difference is that we must consider the ring
\mathbb{Z}[G][\xi,\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{k},t_{1},\ldots,t_{l}]/(\zeta_{1}(t_{1}^{2}+t_{1})+\ldots+\zeta_{k}(t_{k}^{2}+t_{l})-1)
.
∎

The next lemma helps to use the previous one in the cases
\Phi=C_{2}
,
G_{2}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
R
be a localisation of the ring of integers in a number field, and suppose that
R
has no residue field of two elements. Then there are
t_{1}
,
t_{2}\in R
such that
t_{1}^{2}+t_{1}
and
t_{2}^{2}+t_{2}
generate the unit ideal.

Proof.

Take
t_{1}
to be any element such that
t_{1}^{2}+t_{1}\neq 0
. Let
J
be the Jacobson radical of the ideal generated by
t_{1}^{2}+t_{1}
. Then
R/J
is a product of fields, none of which is
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Hence we can take
t_{2}
to be the element such that the images of
t_{2}^{2}+t_{2}
in all these fields are non zero. Therefore,
t_{1}^{2}+t_{1}
and
t_{2}^{2}+t_{2}
generate the unit ideal.
∎

For a ring
R
and an ideal
I\unlhd R
the group
E(\Phi,R,I)
is generated by elements
x_{\alpha}(\xi)^{g}
, where
\alpha\in\Phi
,
\xi\in I
and
g\in G(\Phi,R)
. We denote by
E(\Phi,R,I)^{\leqslant C}
the subset of
E(\Phi,R,I)
that consist of elements that can be decomposed as a product of at most
C
such generators.

The next two lemmas allow one to establish the equality
E(\Phi,R,I)=E(\Phi,R,I)^{\leqslant C}
for arithmetic rings. See also
[
20
]
and
[
21
]
for some different variations of it.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\Phi
be an irreducible root system with
\mathop{\mathrm{rk}}\nolimits\Phi\geqslant 2
and
d
be a positive integer. Then there exists a first-order statement
\varphi=\varphi_{\Phi,d}
in the language of rings with the following properties:

(1)

If
R
is a localisation of the ring of integers in a number field
K
, with
[K\colon\mathbb{Q}]=d
, then
\varphi
holds in
R
.

(2)

If the statement
\varphi
holds in a ring
R
and
I\unlhd R
is a 2-generated ideal, then
[G(\Phi,R,I):E(\Phi,R,I)]\leqslant 4(8d)!
.

Proof.

Pick a long root
\alpha\in\Phi
. By
[
22
, Corollary 4.5]
, if
R
is a localisation of the ring of integers in a number field, then we have
G(\Phi,R,I)=G(\{\alpha,-\alpha\},R,I)E(\Phi,R,I)
. The proof in
[
22
]
is constructive, so it is possible to extract from this proof a bound
C=C(\Phi)
such that
G(\Phi,R,I)=G(\{\alpha,-\alpha\},R,I)E(\Phi,R,I)^{\leqslant C}
.

Note that for fixed
\Phi
and
C
the statement "for any 2-generated ideal
I
, we have
G(\Phi,R,I)=G(\{\alpha,-\alpha\},R,I)E(\Phi,R,I)^{\leqslant C}
" is a first-order statement in the language of rings. Denote this statement by
\mathrm{Red}(\Phi,C)
.

Now we construct our statement
\varphi
as a conjunction of
\mathrm{Red}(\Phi,C)
and the statements
\mathrm{SR}_{1\tfrac{1}{2}}
,
\mathrm{Gen}(2,1)
,
\mathrm{Gen}(2(8d)!,1)
,
\mathrm{Exp}(2(8d)!,2)
(see
[
23
, Definition 2.10, 3.2, 3.6]
).

If
R
is a localisation of the ring of integers in a number field
K
, with
[K\colon\mathbb{Q}]=d
, then it satisfies
\mathrm{Red}(\Phi,C)
as explained above, and it satisfies the other statements by
[
23
, Lemma 2.13, Corrollary 3.5, Theorem 3.9]
.

Now let
R
be a ring that satisfies
\varphi
. Since
\mathop{\mathrm{rk}}\nolimits\Phi>1
, we have one of the following.

Case 1
The root
\alpha
is contained in a subsystem of type
A_{2}
. Then it follows from
\mathrm{Red}(\Phi,C)
that the quotient
G(\Phi,R,I)/E(\Phi,R,I)
is generated by relative Mennicke symbols; and by
[
23
, Theorem 3.11]
the order of the universal Mennicke group is bounded by
2(8d)!
.

Case 2
The root
\alpha
is contained in a subsystem of type
C_{2}
. Then it follows from
\mathrm{Red}(\Phi,C)
that we have a surjection
G(C_{2},R,I)/E(C_{2},R,I)\to G(\Phi,R,I)/E(\Phi,R,I)
(in other words
\mathrm{Red}(\Phi,C)
implies the surjective stability of the
K_{1}
-functor); and by
[
24
, Theorem 3.16]
the order of the group
G(C_{2},R,I)/E(C_{2},R,I)
is bounded by
4(8d)!
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\Phi
be an irreducible root system with
\mathop{\mathrm{rk}}\nolimits\Phi\geqslant 2
and
d
be a positive integer. Then there exists a constant
C=C(\Phi,d)
such that for any ring
R
that is a localisation of the ring of integers in a number field
K
, with
[K\colon\mathbb{Q}]=d
, and any ideal
I\unlhd R
we have
E(\Phi,R,I)=E(\Phi,R,I)^{\leqslant C}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
it is enough to show that there exists a constant
C(\Phi,d)
such that for any ring
R
as above and any ideal
I\unlhd R
there exist
g_{1}
,
\ldots
,
g_{4(8d)!}
such that
G(\Phi,R,I)=\bigcup_{i}g_{i}E(\Phi,R,I)^{\leqslant C}
.

Assume that the above statement is false. Then for every
C\in\mathbb{N}
there exist a ring
R_{C}
as above, an ideal
I_{C}\unlhd R_{C}
and elements
g_{C,1}
,
\ldots
,
g_{C,4(8d)!+1}\in G(\Phi,R_{C},I_{C})
such that
(g_{C,i})^{-1}g_{C,j}\notin E(\Phi,R_{C},I_{C})^{\leqslant C}
if
i\neq j
. Here the elements
g_{C,i}
are constructed as follows:
g_{C,1}
is picked arbitrary and
g_{C,j+1}
is picked from
G(\Phi,R_{C},I_{C})\setminus\bigcup_{i\leqslant j}g_{i}E(\Phi,R,I)^{\leqslant C}
that guarantees that
(g_{C,i})^{-1}g_{C,j}\notin E(\Phi,R_{C},I_{C})^{\leqslant C}
for
i<j
, and since
E(\Phi,R_{C},I_{C})^{\leqslant C}
coincides with its inverse, we have the same for
i>j
.

It is well known fact from model theory that the first order statements are inherited by ultraproducts. Choose a non-principal ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
on
\mathbb{N}
, and let
R_{\infty}
be the ultraproduct of
R_{C}
over
\mathcal{U}
. Then the statement
\varphi
from Lemma
3.4
holds in
R_{\infty}
and
G(\Phi,R_{\infty})
is isomorphic to the ultraproduct of
G(\Phi,R_{C})
over
\mathcal{U}
. Let
I_{\infty}
be the ideal of
R_{\infty}
represented by
\prod_{C}I_{C}
; this ideal is 2-generated because all the
I_{C}
are 2-generated in
R_{C}
. For every
1\leqslant i\leqslant 4(8d!)+1
, let
g_{i}\in G(\Phi,R_{\infty},I_{\infty})
be the element represented by
(g_{C,i})_{C}
. Then
g_{1}
,
\ldots
,
g_{(4(8d!)+1}
belong to different cosets of
E(\Phi,R_{\infty},I_{\infty})
contradicting Lemma
3.4
.
∎

Now we combine the previous results to establish the following lemma.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\Phi
be an irreducible root system with
\mathop{\mathrm{rk}}\nolimits\Phi\geqslant 2
. Let
d
be a positive integer. Then there exists a constant
C=C(\Phi,d)
such that for any ring
R
satisfying assumptions (1)–(2) of Theorem
1.1
and any symmetric conjugation-invariant subset
X\subseteq G_{\mathrm{sc}}(\Phi,R)
that contains the identity element, and that is not contained in the center there is a non-trivial ideal
I\unlhd R
such that
E(\Phi,R,I)\leqslant X^{C}
.

Proof.

Let
C_{1}
be the constant from Lemma
3.2
(in case
\Phi=C_{2}
,
G_{2}
we take
l=2
), and let
C_{2}
be the constant from Lemma
3.5
. Then we can take
C=C_{1}C_{2}
.

Indeed, let
X
be as above. Since
X
is not contained in the center, it follows that there are
x\in X
and
i
such that
\eta_{i}(x)\neq 0
. Take
I=e\eta_{i}(x)R
, then by Lemmas
3.2
and
3.3
all the elements
x_{\alpha}(\xi)
,
\alpha\in\Phi
,
\xi\in I
are in
X^{C_{1}}
. Therefore,
E(\Phi,R,I)\leqslant X^{C_{1}C_{2}}
.

∎

For a ring
R
and a positive integer
n
we denote by
\mu_{n}(R)
the group of
n
-th roots of unity in the ring
R
.

For a ring
R
and an ideal
\mathfrak{p}\unlhd R
we denote by
R_{(\mathfrak{p})}
the corresponding completion of
R
. Note that
R_{(\mathfrak{p})}
is a DVR.

Now we prove some technical lemmas about the groups over such completions.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
R
be a localisation of the ring of integers in a number field
K
, with
[K\colon\mathbb{Q}]=d
, and let
n
be a positive integer. Then for any prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\unlhd R
and any positive integer
m
we have
|\mu_{n}(R/\mathfrak{p}^{m})|\leqslant n^{2d}
.

Proof.

Let
p
be the characteristic of
R/\mathfrak{p}
, let
q=|R/\mathfrak{p}|
. Let
n=p^{k}z
, where
z
is coprime to
p
. Note that if
k=0
, then by Hensel’s lemma every
n
-th root of unity in
\mu_{n}(R/\mathfrak{p}^{m})
can be lifted to
R_{(\mathfrak{p})}
and hence
|\mu_{n}(R/\mathfrak{p}^{m})|\leqslant n
; thus we may assume that
k\geqslant 1
. Let
\pi
be the uniformizing element in
R_{(\mathfrak{p})}
, and let
p=\xi\pi^{r}
, where
\xi\in R_{(\mathfrak{p})}^{*}
.

We claim that if
m>2kr
, then we have

\mathop{\mathrm{Ker}}\nolimits\left((R/\mathfrak{p}^{m+1})^{*}\to(R/\mathfrak{p}^{m})^{*}\right)\leqslant((R/\mathfrak{p}^{m+1})^{*})^{n}\text{.}

Indeed, elements from this kernel are of the from
1+s\pi^{m}
. We have

\left(1+\frac{s}{z\xi^{k}}\pi^{m-kr}\right)^{n}\equiv 1+s\pi^{m}\mod\mathfrak{p}^{2(m-kr)}\text{,}

and if
m>2kr
, then
2(m-kr)>m
.

Therefore, for
m>2kr
, we have

(R/\mathfrak{p}^{m+1})^{*}/((R/\mathfrak{p}^{m+1})^{*})^{n}\simeq(R/\mathfrak{p}^{m})^{*}/((R/\mathfrak{p}^{m})^{*})^{n}\text{.}

Since
(R/\mathfrak{p}^{m})^{*}
is a finite abelian group, we have

|\mu_{n}(R/\mathfrak{p}^{m})|=|(R/\mathfrak{p}^{m})^{*}/((R/\mathfrak{p}^{m})^{*})^{n}|\text{.}

Therefore, for
m>2kr
, we have

|\mu_{n}(R/\mathfrak{p}^{m+1})|=|\mu_{n}(R/\mathfrak{p}^{m})|\text{.}

Since
(R/\mathfrak{p})^{*}
is a cyclic group of order coprime to
p
, we have
|\mu_{n}(R/\mathfrak{p})|\leqslant z
. For the kernel of the reduction map we have:

|\mathop{\mathrm{Ker}}\nolimits(\mu_{n}(R/\mathfrak{p}^{m})\to\mu_{n}(R/\mathfrak{p}))|\leqslant|\mathop{\mathrm{Ker}}\nolimits(R/\mathfrak{p}^{m}\to R/\mathfrak{p})|=q^{m-1}\text{.}

Hence we have
|\mu_{n}(R/\mathfrak{p}^{m})|\leqslant zq^{m-1}
. Therefore, for every
m
we have

|\mu_{n}(R/\mathfrak{p}^{m})|\leqslant zq^{2kr}\leqslant zp^{2kd}\leqslant(zp^{k})^{2d}=n^{2d}\text{.}

Here we need to explain the equality
q^{r}\leqslant p^{d}
. Let
L
be the fraction field of
R_{(\mathfrak{p})}
. Let
F
be the maximal unramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
in
L
. Then
[L:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]=[L:F][F:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]=r\cdot[\mathbb{F}_{q}:\mathbb{F}_{p}]
. Therefore,
q^{r}=p^{[L:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]}
. On the other hand
d
is a rank of
R
as a
\mathbb{Z}
-module, hence it is a rank of
R\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
as a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module. Now
R\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{p}=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}R_{(\mathfrak{p}_{i})}
where
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
are all primes of
R
lying over
p
. Therefore, we have
d=\sum_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}\mathop{\mathrm{rk}}\nolimits_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}R_{(\mathfrak{p}_{i})}=\sum_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}[\mathrm{Frac}(R_{(\mathfrak{p}_{i})}):\mathbb{Q}_{p}]
, and
[L:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]
here is one of the summands.
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
\Phi
be an irreducible root system with
\mathop{\mathrm{rk}}\nolimits\Phi\geqslant 2
. Let
d
be a positive integer. Then there exists a constant
C=C(\Phi,d)
such that for any ring
R
satisfying assumptions (1)–(2) of Theorem
1.1
, any prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\unlhd R
and any symmetric conjugation-invariant subset
X\subseteq G_{\mathrm{sc}}(\Phi,R_{(\mathfrak{p})})
that contains the identity element we have
\langle X\rangle=X^{C}
.

Proof.

Let
C_{1}
be the constant from Lemma
3.2
(in case
\Phi=C_{2}
,
G_{2}
take
l=1
; note that in a local ring with the residue field distinct from
\mathbb{F}_{2}
there exists an element
t
such that
t^{2}+t
is invertible). Let
n
be a number such that the center of the group scheme
G(\Phi,-)
is the group
\mu_{n}
of the
n
-th roots of unity (in case
\Phi=D_{2l}
, where the center is
\mu_{2}\times\mu_{2}
we take
n=4
), so
n
depends only on
\Phi
.

We claim that we can take

C=C_{1}\cdot\left(\frac{3}{2}|\Phi|+2\mathop{\mathrm{rk}}\nolimits\Phi+1\right)\cdot\left(3^{d(|\Phi|+\mathop{\mathrm{rk}}\nolimits\Phi)+1}\cdot n^{2d}-2\right)\text{.}

Indeed, let
X
be as above. We may assume that
X
is non-central, because otherwise, we have
|\langle X\rangle|\leqslant n
and then, clearly,
\langle X\rangle=X^{n}
. Let
k
be the biggest number such that
X\subseteq CG(\Phi,R_{(\mathfrak{p})},\mathfrak{p}^{k})
, where
CG(\Phi,R_{(\mathfrak{p})},\mathfrak{p}^{k})
is the full congruence subgroup, i.e. the preimage of the center under the reduction map
G(\Phi,R_{(\mathfrak{p})})\to G(\Phi,R_{(\mathfrak{p})}/\mathfrak{p}^{k})
.

Recall that in the notation of Lemma
3.2
we set
e=1
if
\Phi
is of type ADE,
e=2
if
\Phi
is of type BCF, and
e=3
if
\Phi=G_{2}
.

Case 1

k=0
.

Recall that by
\eta_{i}
we denote some fixed generator of the ideal in
\mathbb{Z}[G]
that correspond the scheme center of
G
. In this case there exists
x\in X
and an index
i
such that
\eta_{i}(x)\notin\mathfrak{p}
. Thus
\eta_{i}(g)^{e}
is invertible; hence
x_{\alpha}(\xi)\in X^{C_{1}}
for all
\alpha\in\Phi
,
\xi\in R_{(\mathfrak{p})}
, and by
[
25
, Lemma 5.3]
(take
I
to be the unit ideal) we have

G(\Phi,R_{(\mathfrak{p})})=X^{C_{1}\left(\frac{3}{2}|\Phi|+2\mathop{\mathrm{rk}}\nolimits\Phi+1\right)}\text{.}

Case 2

k\geqslant 1
.

Let
\pi
be the uniformizing element,
p
be the characteristic of
R/\mathfrak{p}
, let
q=|R/\mathfrak{p}|
, and let
p=\xi\pi^{r}
, where
\xi\in R_{(\mathfrak{p})}^{*}
.

There is an element
x\in X
and an index
i
such that
\eta_{i}(x)\notin\mathfrak{p}^{k+1}
. Hence
x_{\alpha}(\xi e\pi^{k})\in X^{C_{1}}
for all
\alpha\in\Phi
,
\xi\in R_{(\mathfrak{p})}
, and by
[
25
, Lemma 5.3]
(take
I=e\mathfrak{p}^{k}
) we have

G(\Phi,R_{(\mathfrak{p})},e\mathfrak{p}^{k})\leqslant X^{C_{1}\left(\frac{3}{2}|\Phi|+2\mathop{\mathrm{rk}}\nolimits\Phi+1\right)}\text{.}

Therefore, since
X\subseteq CG(\Phi,R_{(\mathfrak{p})},\mathfrak{p}^{k})
, it follows by Lemma
3.1
that it is enough to show that
[CG(\Phi,R_{(\mathfrak{p})},\mathfrak{p}^{k}):G(\Phi,R_{(\mathfrak{p})},e\mathfrak{p}^{k})]\leqslant 3^{d(|\Phi|+\mathop{\mathrm{rk}}\nolimits\Phi)}\cdot n^{2d}
.

It follows by Lemma
3.7
that
[CG(\Phi,R_{(\mathfrak{p})},\mathfrak{p}^{k}):G(\Phi,R_{(\mathfrak{p})},\mathfrak{p}^{k})]\leqslant n^{2d}
. Therefore, it remains to estimate
[G(\Phi,R_{(\mathfrak{p})},\mathfrak{p}^{k}):G(\Phi,R_{(\mathfrak{p})},e\mathfrak{p}^{k})]
. From now we may assume that
p=e\in\{2,3\}
because otherwise this index is equal to one.

All elements from
G(\Phi,R_{(\mathfrak{p})},\mathfrak{p}^{k})
belong to the main Gauss cell, which is an open subscheme of
G(\Phi,-)
isomorphic to
\mathbb{A}_{1}^{|\Phi|}\times\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\mathop{\mathrm{rk}}\nolimits\Phi}
. Modulo
e\mathfrak{p}^{k}=\mathfrak{p}^{k+r}
every component of this product can take one of the
q^{r}
values; hence

[G(\Phi,R_{(\mathfrak{p})},\mathfrak{p}^{k}):G(\Phi,R_{(\mathfrak{p})},e\mathfrak{p}^{k})]\leqslant q^{r(|\Phi|+\mathop{\mathrm{rk}}\nolimits\Phi)}\leqslant p^{d(|\Phi|+\mathop{\mathrm{rk}}\nolimits\Phi)}\leqslant 3^{d(|\Phi|+\mathop{\mathrm{rk}}\nolimits\Phi)}\text{.}

∎

We need one more technical group-theoretic lemma.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
G_{i}
be a collection of groups, and let
G=\prod_{i}G_{i}
. Let
w
be a word. Assume that for all
i
the width of
w
in
G_{i}
is at most
C
. Then the width of
w
in
G
is at most
2C
.

Proof.

Let
g\in\langle w(G)\rangle
, and let
g_{i}
be the components of
g
. Set

w^{+}(G)=\{w(h_{1},\ldots,h_{k})\mid h_{i}\in G\}\text{,}\qquad w^{-}(G)=\{w(h_{1},\ldots,h_{k})^{-1}\mid h_{i}\in G\}\text{.}

By assumption each of the
g_{i}
is a product of at most
C
elements from
w(G_{i})
. Since
w^{+}(G_{i})
is conjugation-invariant, we can reorder this product so that the elements from
w^{+}(G_{i})
comes before the elements of
w^{-}(G_{i})
. Therefore,
g_{i}\in w^{+}(G_{i})^{C}w^{-}(G_{i})^{C}
; hence
g\in w^{+}(G)^{C}w^{-}(G)^{C}\subseteq w(G)^{2C}
.
∎

Now let us prove Theorem
1.1
.

Let
X=w(G(\Phi,R))
. Clearly, the set
X
is not contained in the center (because
G(\Phi,R)
contains a copy of
\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}\nolimits(2,\mathbb{Z})
, and hence a copy of a free subgroup). Let
R
be the ring that satisfy (1)–(2). Let
C_{1}
be the constant from Lemma
3.6
,
C_{2}
be the constant from Lemma
3.8
, and
m
be the number of roots of unity in
K
. We prove that
\langle X\rangle=X^{(C_{1}+2C_{2})(3m-2)}
. Clearly
m
is bounded by some constant that depens on
d
(we have
\varphi(m)\leqslant d
, where
\varphi
is the Euler function; hence
m\leqslant 2d^{2}
); therefore, that would be enough.

By Lemma
3.6
, there is a non-trivial ideal
I\unlhd R
such that
E(\Phi,R,I)\leqslant X^{C_{1}}
. By
[
26
, Corollary 4.3]
and
[
22
, Corollary 4.5]
we have
|G(\Phi,R,I)/E(\Phi,R,I)|\leqslant m
. Therefore, by Lemma
3.1
, it is enough to prove that
\langle X\rangle\leqslant G(\Phi,R,I)X^{2C_{2}}
.

Let
I=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{k_{i}}
, and let
g\in\langle X\rangle
. It follows by Lemmas
3.8
and
3.9
the image of
g
in
G(\Phi,\prod_{i=1}^{n}R_{(\mathfrak{p}_{i})})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}G(\Phi,R_{(\mathfrak{p}_{i})})
belong to
w(G(\Phi,\prod_{i=1}^{n}R_{(\mathfrak{p}_{i})}))^{2C_{2}}
. Since
R/I\simeq(\prod_{i=1}^{n}R_{(\mathfrak{p}_{i})})/\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{k_{i}}
, and the reduction map
G(\Phi,R)\to G(\Phi,R/I)
is surjective, it follows that
g
is congruent to an element from
X^{2C_{2}}
modulo
G(\Phi,R,I)
as required.

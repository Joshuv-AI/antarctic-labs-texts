then the result follows from Lemma
2.3
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
G
be an elementary abelian
p
-group of rank
n\geq 2
. Then
K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G]/I)
is an elementary abelian
p
-group of rank
(n-1)p^{n}+1+\tau(p)
, set
T\backslash T_{1}
together with
\langle-p,-p\rangle
(if
p
=2) forms a basis for
K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G]/I)
.

Proof
.

Let
G=\langle\sigma_{i}\mid\sigma_{i}^{p}=1,\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}=\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i},1\leq i,j\leq n\rangle
, and
G_{i}=\langle\sigma_{i}\mid\sigma_{i}^{p}=1\rangle
, and let

\Gamma_{i}
be the maximal
\mathbb{Z}
-order of
\mathbb{Z}[G_{i}]
,
1\leq i\leq n
.
According to
[
4
, page 1587]
,
the canonical map of rings

f:\mathbb{Q}[G]\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}[G_{i}]
gives rise to
f(|G|\Gamma)=|G|\Gamma_{i}
, and induces a surjective map of rings
\mathbb{Z}[G]/|G|\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[G_{i}]/|G|\Gamma_{i}
, so, the map
g:K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G]/I)\rightarrow K_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p}[G])
is surjective by Lemma
2.1
.
\mbox{Ker}(g)
contains
\langle\sigma_{i}-1,(\sigma_{i}-1)^{p-1}\rangle
(
1\leq i\leq n
) and
\langle-p,-p\rangle
(if
p=2
), these generators have exponent
p
by Lemma
3.1
. Since
p^{n+1}\mathbb{Z}[G]\subseteq pJ\subseteq I\subseteq p\mathbb{Z}[G]
, we conclude from the proof of Theorem
2.6
that

\mbox{Ker}(K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G]/(p^{n+1}))\rightarrow K_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]))\cong\mbox{Ker}(K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G]/I)\rightarrow K_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p}[G])),

then
K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G]/I)
is isomorphic to
K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G]/(p^{n+1}))
, the result follows.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
G
be an elementary abelian
p
-group of rank
n\geq 2
. Then

K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G]/p^{n}\Gamma)
is an elementary abelian
p
-group of rank
(n-1)(p^{n}-1)+\tau(p)
, the set
T\backslash(T_{1}\cup T_{3})
together with
\langle-p,-p\rangle
(if
p
=2) forms a basis for
K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G]/p^{n}\Gamma)
.
For any
k\geq n+1
,
K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G]/p^{k}\Gamma)
is an elementary abelian
p
-group of rank
(n-1)p^{n}+1+\tau(p)
, the set
T\backslash T_{1}
together with
\langle-p,-p\rangle
(if
p
=2) forms a basis for
K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G]/p^{k}\Gamma)
.

Proof
.

The Cartesian square (
3.1
) gives rise to a natural commutative diagram with exact rows:

\begin{CD}K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G]/I,J/I)@>{f_{1}}>{}>K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G]/I)@>{f_{2}}>{}>K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G]/J)@>{}>{}>1\\
@V{g_{1}}V{}V@V{g_{2}}V{}V@V{g_{3}}V{}V\\
1@>{}>{}>K_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p}[G],(\widetilde{G}))@>{}>{}>K_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p}[G])@>{}>{}>K_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]/(\widetilde{G}))@>{}>{}>1\end{CD}

where the bottom row is given by (
3.2
). By Lemma
2.1
,
f_{2}
,
g_{2}
and
g_{3}
are surjective. By Lemma 4.2 in
[
4
]
,
g_{1}
is an isomorphism, then
K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G]/I,J/I)
is an elementary abelian
p
-group and
f_{1}
is injective.
A corollary of Lemma
2.1
and Theorem
2.6
shows that both
K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G]/I)
and
K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G]/J)
are
elementary abelian
p
-group, it follows that the top exact sequence splits. By Snake Lemma,
\mbox{Ker}(g_{2})\cong\mbox{Ker}(g_{3})
, also, note that both
g_{2}
and
g_{3}
are split surjections, then

K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G]/J)\cong K_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]/(\widetilde{G}))\oplus\mbox{Ker}\big(K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G]/I)\rightarrow K_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p}[G])\big).

By Lemma
2.3
,
K_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p}[G])
has
p
-rank
(n-1)(p^{n}-1)
, by Lemma
3.2
,
K_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]/(\widetilde{G}))
has
p
-rank
(n-1)(p^{n}-2)-1
, by Lemma
3.3
,
K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G]/I)
has
p
-rank
(n-1)p^{n}+1+\tau(p)
. Then
K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G]/p^{n}\Gamma)
is an elementary abelian
p
-group of rank
(n-1)(p^{n}-1)+\tau(p)
. Since
pJ\subseteq I
, for any
k\geq n+1
,

K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G]/p^{k}\Gamma)
maps onto
K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G]/I)
, note that
K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G]/(p^{k}))
maps onto
K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G]/p^{k}\Gamma)
, it suffices to prove both
K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G]/(p^{k}))
and
K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G]/I)
have the same
p
-rank, this follows from Theorem
2.6
and Lemma
3.3
, which completes our proof.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

From the proof, we conclude that for any
k\geq n+1
,
K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G]/(p^{k}))
is isomorphic to
K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G]/p^{k}\Gamma)
.

4
Applications.

In
[
5
]
, the following exact sequence associated to the pair
(\mathbb{Z}[G],p\mathbb{Z}[G])
:

K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G])\rightarrow K_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p}[G])\rightarrow SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[G],p\mathbb{Z}[G])\rightarrow SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[G])\rightarrow 1,

was used to give lower bounds of the
p
-rank of
K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G])
and
Wh_{2}(G)
, where
G
is an elementary abelian
p
-group of rank
n
. However, we find the estimate is inaccurate when
p
is an odd prime since the explicit structure of
SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[G],p\mathbb{Z}[G])
is unknown. In this section, we will give the explicit structure of
SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[G],(p^{k}))
for
k\geq 1
, and new estimates of the lower bounds of the
p
-rank of
K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G])
and
Wh_{2}(G)
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian
p
-group,
\Gamma
the maximal
\mathbb{Z}
-order in
\mathbb{Q}[G]
, and

\chi:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{*}
an irreducible character of
G
.
\chi(G)
is a finite cyclic group of roots of unity whose order dividing
|G|
, and
\chi
induces a surjective homomorphism from
\mathbb{Q}[G]
to
\mathbb{Q}[\chi]
, where
\mathbb{Q}[\chi]
denotes the cyclotomic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
generated by
\chi(G)
. Recall that a cluster of characters
S
for
G
is any set of irreducible characters which contains exactly one character dividing each irreducible
\mathbb{Q}[G]
-module (see
[
2
, Definition 2.1]
), and there is a natural isomorphism
\mathbb{Q}[G]\cong\prod_{\chi\in S}\mathbb{Q}[\chi],
furthermore, by
[
2
, Proposition 2.2]
,
\Gamma\cong\prod_{\chi\in S}\mathbb{Z}[\chi]
, and
|G|\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb{Z}[G]\subseteq\Gamma
. An imaginary cluster of characters for
G
is defined to be

S_{0}=\{\chi\in S|\mathbb{Z}[\chi]\neq\mathbb{Z}\}
, obviously,

S_{0}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}\{\chi\in S\mid|\mbox{Im}(\chi)|>2\}&&{\mbox{if $p=2$},}\\
S\backslash\{\mbox{trivial character}\}&&{\mbox{if $p\neq 2$}.}\\
\end{array}\right.

Lemma 4.1
.

[
7
, Lemma 13.37]

If
R
is a commutative ring with ideals
J^{{}^{\prime}}\subseteq J
of finite index, then inclusion on
SL
induces a surjective homomorphism
SK_{1}(R,J^{{}^{\prime}})\rightarrow SK_{1}(R,J)
.

We first give the structure of the inverse limit of
SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[G],(p^{k}))
:

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian
p
-group,
S_{0}
an imaginary
cluster of characters for
G
. Then

\lim\limits_{\overset{\longleftarrow}{k}}SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[G],(p^{k}))\cong\prod_{\chi\in S_{0}}\mbox{Im}(\chi).

Proof
.

Set
|G|=p^{k_{0}}
, then for any
k\geq k_{0}
,

p^{k}\Gamma\subseteq p^{k-k_{0}}\mathbb{Z}[G]\subseteq p^{k-k_{0}}\Gamma
, therefore

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\overset{\longleftarrow}{k}}SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[G],(p^{k}))

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{\overset{\longleftarrow}{k\geq k_{0}}}SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[G],(p^{k}))\cong\lim\limits_{\overset{\longleftarrow}{k\geq k_{0}}}SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[G],p^{k}\Gamma)

(by Lemma
4.1
)

\displaystyle\cong\lim\limits_{\overset{\longleftarrow}{k\geq k_{0}}}SK_{1}(\Gamma,p^{k}\Gamma)

(by
[
2
, Theoerm 1.3(f)]
)

\displaystyle\cong\lim\limits_{\overset{\longleftarrow}{k\geq k_{0}}}\prod_{\chi\in S}SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[\chi],p^{k}\mathbb{Z}[\chi])

\displaystyle\cong\lim\limits_{\overset{\longleftarrow}{k\geq k_{0}}}\prod_{\chi\in S}SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[\chi],p^{k}\mathbb{Z}[\chi])\cong\prod_{\chi\in S_{0}}\mbox{Im}(\chi).

(by Corollary 4.3 in
[
3
]
)

∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
p
be an odd prime,
G
an elementary abelian
p
-group of rank
n
. Then

SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[G],p\mathbb{Z}[G])
is an elementary abelian
p
-group of rank

\dfrac{p^{n}-1}{p-1}-n
, for any
k\geq 2
,

SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[G],p^{k}\mathbb{Z}[G])
is an elementary abelian
p
-group of rank
\dfrac{p^{n}-1}{p-1}
.

Proof
.

Since
SK_{1}
of finite rings are trivial, we have the following natural commutative diagram with exact rows of
K
-groups:

\begin{CD}K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G])@>{}>{}>K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G]/(p^{k+1}))@>{}>{}>SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[G],(p^{k+1}))@>{}>{}>SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[G])@>{}>{}>1\\
\Big\|@V{}V{\cong}V@V{}V{}V\Big\|\Big\|\\
K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G])@>{}>{}>K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G]/(p^{k}))@>{}>{}>SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[G],(p^{k}))@>{}>{}>SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[G])@>{}>{}>1\\
\end{CD}

By Theorem
2.6
,
K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G]/(p^{k}))
is stable for
k\geq 2
, then a Snake Lemma argument shows that
SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[G]/(p^{k}))
is also stable for
k\geq 2
, and hence coincides with its inverse limit, thus
SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[G],(p^{k}))
is an elementary abelian
p
-group of rank

\dfrac{p^{n}-1}{p-1}
by Lemma
4.2
. To complete the proof, it suffices to compute
SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[G],(p))
.
Write
G=\prod_{i=1}^{n}G_{i}
, where
G_{i}
denotes a cyclic group of order
p
, and let
\Gamma_{i}
be the maximal
\mathbb{Z}
-order of
\mathbb{Z}[G_{i}]
, by the proof of Proposition
4.2
, we have

SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[G_{i}],p^{k}\Gamma_{i})\cong SK_{1}(\Gamma_{i},p^{k}\Gamma_{i})\cong SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z},p^{k}\mathbb{Z})\oplus SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{p}],p^{k}\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{p}])=C_{p}.

Note that there is a natural
commutative diagram of
K
-groups:

\begin{CD}K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G]/(p^{k}))@>{\partial}>{}>SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[G],(p^{k}))\\
@V{p_{i*}}V{}V@V{p_{i*}}V{}V\\
K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G_{i}]/(p^{k}))@>{\partial}>{}>SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[G_{i}],(p^{k}))\end{CD}

(4.1)

in which
\partial
denotes the connecting homomorphism in the algebraic
K
-theory sequence,
p_{i*}
denotes the map induced by the projection
p_{i}:G\rightarrow G_{i}
.
Write
L=\mbox{Ker}(K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G]/(p^{k}))\rightarrow K_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]))
, then from the proof of Theorem
2.6
,
L
is an elementary abelian
p
-group of rank
n
, and

L\cong\prod_{i=1}^{n}K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G_{i}]/(p^{k})),

since
SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[G_{i}])
is trivial, applying the algebraic
K
-theory sequence associated to the pair
(\mathbb{Z}[G_{i}],(p^{k}))
,
K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G_{i}]/(p^{k}))
maps onto
SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[G_{i}],(p^{k}))
, while by Lemma

4.1
,
SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[G_{i}],(p^{k}))
maps onto
SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[G_{i}],p^{k}\Gamma_{i})
, which is a cyclic group of order
p
, while by Lemma
2.4
,
K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G_{i}]/(p^{k}))
is a cyclic group of order
p
, hence

K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G_{i}]/(p^{k}))\cong SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[G_{i}],(p^{k}))=C_{p}.

Since the square (
4.1
) commutes and
SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[G],(p^{k}))
has exponent
p
,
SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[G_{i}],(p^{k}))
is isomorphic to a direct summand of
SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[G],(p^{k}))
. Let
L^{{}^{\prime}}
be the image of
L
in
SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[G],(p^{k}))
, then
L^{{}^{\prime}}
is an elementary abelian
p
-group of rank
n
, and

L^{{}^{\prime}}\cong\prod_{i=1}^{n}SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[G_{i}],(p^{k})),

therefore, for any
k\geq 2
, we have another commutative diagram with exact rows:

\begin{CD}K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G])@>{}>{}>K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G]/(p^{k}))/L@>{}>{}>SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[G],(p^{k}))/L^{{}^{\prime}}@>{}>{}>SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[G])@>{}>{}>1\\
\Big\|@V{}V{\cong}V@V{}V{}V\Big\|\Big\|\\
K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G])@>{}>{}>K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G]/(p))@>{}>{}>SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[G],(p))@>{}>{}>SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[G])@>{}>{}>1\\
\end{CD}

by definition, the second vertical arrow is an isomorphism, then by Snake Lemma, the third vertical arrow is also an isomorphism, hence
SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[G],(p))
is an elementary abelian
p
-group of rank
\dfrac{p^{n}-1}{p-1}-n
.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

For
k\geq 2
, from the proof of Proposition
4.3
, we have
SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[G],(p^{k}))\cong SK_{1}(\Gamma,(p^{k}))
, while according to Theorem A.2 and Theorem A.3 in the appendix of
[
2
]

SK_{1}(\Gamma,(p^{k}))\cong K_{2}(\Gamma/(p^{k}))

[

, the image of

in
SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[G],(p^{k}))
is an elementary abelian
p
-group of rank
\dbinom{p+n-1}{p}
, for
k\geq 1
, the image has
p
-rank
\dbinom{p+n-1}{p}-n
. Hence we rediscover the classical result that
SK_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[G])
is an elementary abelian
p
-group of rank
\dfrac{p^{n}-1}{p-1}-\dbinom{p+n-1}{p}
.

With minor modifications of the proofs of Theorem 8 and Corollary 9 in
[
5
]
, one can give more precise estimations of the lower bounds of the

-rank of
K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G])
and
Wh_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G])

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
p
be an odd prime,
G
an elementary abelian
p
-group of rank
n
. Then

K_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[G])
has
p

1+(n-1)p^{n}-\dbinom{p+n-1}{p},

and

1+(n-1)p^{n}-\dbinom{p+n-1}{p}-\dfrac{n(n-1)}{2}.

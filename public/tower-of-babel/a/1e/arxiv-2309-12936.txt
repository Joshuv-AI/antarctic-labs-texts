Constructing smoothings of stable maps
Fatemeh Rezaee, Mohan Swaminathan

Abstract
. Let X X be a smooth projective variety. Define a stable map f : 𝒞 → X f:\mathscr{C}\to X to be eventually smoothable if there is an embedding X ↪ ℙ N X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N} such that ( 𝒞 , f ) (\mathscr{C},f) occurs as the limit of a 1 1 -parameter family of stable maps to ℙ N \mathbb{P}^{N} with smooth domain curves. Via an explicit deformation-theoretic construction, we produce a large class of stable maps (called stable maps with model ghosts ), and show that they are eventually smoothable.



1.
Introduction

We work over a fixed algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic
0
. Given a projective variety
X
, we will consider stable maps to it in the sense of Gromov and Kontsevich (see Definition
1.1
). The motivating problem for this paper is the following.

Problem
.

Given a stable map
f:\mathscr{C}\to X
, determine necessary and sufficient conditions under which we can find an embedding
X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N}
, so that
(\mathscr{C},f)
arises as a limit of stable maps from nonsingular curves to
\mathbb{P}^{N}
.

In the present paper, we derive an explicit sufficient condition and give several examples, in arbitrary genus, which satisfy this condition. In a follow up paper
[
33
]
, we will investigate necessary conditions.

This problem can be naturally interpreted in terms of the moduli stacks
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
of
n
-pointed stable maps to
X
of genus
g
and class
\beta
. These moduli stacks are important in enumerative geometry and physics, because when
X
is non-singular, we can use virtual intersection theory on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
to define the Gromov–Witten invariants of
X
.

The stack
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
is proper over
k
and contains an open substack
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
parametrizing maps from non-singular curves to
X
. It is known (see §
1.3
) that when
g
is positive,
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
is usually
not
dense in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
, even when
X=\mathbb{P}^{N}
and the degree
c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(1))\cap\beta
is much bigger than the genus
g
. This stems from the fact that a stable map may contract an entire irreducible component of the domain curve to a point in the target variety. Following
[
38
]
, it is therefore natural to ask if we can explicitly describe a
sharper
compactification
\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{\circ}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
with the following properties.

(i)

\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{\circ}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
is (strictly) contained in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
as a closed substack and contains
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
as an open substack.

(ii)

For any embedding
X\hookrightarrow X^{\prime}
of projective varieties, with the class
\beta^{\prime}
being the image of
\beta
, the stack
\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{\circ}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
is the inverse image of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{\circ}_{g,n}(X^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})
under the morphism

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}).

(iii)

Given
(\mathscr{C},p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})\to X
lying in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{\circ}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
, we can find
X\hookrightarrow X^{\prime}
as above such that
(\mathscr{C},p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})\to X\hookrightarrow X^{\prime}
lies in the closure of
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(X^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})
in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})
.

Property (
i
) says that
\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{\circ}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
is a compactification of
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
within
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
. Property (
ii
) says that this compactification is
functorial under embeddings
, while property (
iii
) says that it is
optimal
(or
sharp
) in a precise sense.

Our ultimate goal is to obtain an explicit description of a compactification
\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{\circ}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
satisfying (
i
)–(
iii
) above by characterizing the stable maps which belong to it. An important application of the sharp compactification was the resolution of the genus one Bershadsky–Cecotti–Ooguri–Vafa (BCOV) conjecture in
[
39
]
(see §
1.3
for more information).

1.1.
Main results

We start by briefly recalling some notions that are required for the discussion. As above,
X
denotes a projective variety.

Definition 1.1

(Prestable curve, stable map)
.

A
prestable curve

\mathscr{C}
is a projective connected reduced curve with at worst ordinary nodes
1
1

1

By this, we mean a singularity which is étale locally given by
\Spec k[x,y]/(xy)
.
as singularities. A
stable map
to
X
is a morphism
f:\mathscr{C}\to X
from a prestable curve such that the group of automorphisms of
\mathscr{C}
which preserve
f
is finite. It is also possible to consider a variant of this definition where one allows
\mathscr{C}
to have a finite ordered collection
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\in\mathscr{C}
of non-singular marked points.

Denote by
\overline{\mathcal{M}}(X)=\bigsqcup_{g,\beta}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,0}(X,\beta)
, the moduli stack of stable maps to
X
(without marked points) and with no restriction on their genus or degree. Denote by
\mathcal{M}(X)
the open substack parametrizing maps defined on non-singular domain curves. We are interested in the question of when a stable map is
smoothable
(Definition
3.2
), i.e., understanding when a stable map lies in the closure of
\mathcal{M}(X)
within
\overline{\mathcal{M}}(X)
. For a general target variety
X
, smoothability of a stable map is rather subtle and depends delicately on the geometry of
X
. We instead study the following more flexible notion, as in our motivating problem.

Definition 1.2

(Eventual smoothability)
.

A stable map
f:\mathscr{C}\to X
is said to be
eventually smoothable
if there exists an embedding
X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N}
such that the point of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbb{P}^{N})
defined by the stable map
\mathscr{C}\to X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N}
lies in the closure of the open substack
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{P}^{N})
.

For a stable map
f:\mathscr{C}\to X
, a
ghost component
(Definition
3.20
) is a maximal connected union of irreducible components of
\mathscr{C}
on which
f
is constant. Eventual smoothability of a stable map is expected to depend only on its local behavior near each of its ghost components. Our first main result is a local-to-global principle in this vein.

Theorem A

(see Theorem
3.23
, Local criterion for eventual smoothability)
.

If a non-constant stable map is locally formally smoothable, then it is eventually smoothable.

Our second main result concerns the eventual smoothability of a large class of stable maps, which we call
stable maps with model ghosts
.

Theorem B

(see Theorem
4.29
)
.

Stable maps with model ghosts are eventually smoothable.

Let us now explain the meaning of the terms appearing in Theorems
A
and
B
.

In the statement of Theorem
A
,
local formal smoothability
(Definition
3.21
) is a condition on the behavior of a stable map near its ghost components. This condition is related to the smoothability of the singularities of a certain non-prestable curve, of the same arithmetic genus as the domain of the stable map, obtained by collapsing the ghost components.

In the statement of Theorem
B
,
stable maps with model ghosts
(Definition
4.24
) are a class of stable maps whose behavior near any ghost component is governed by an explicit model which we now describe. Given any non-singular projective curve
C
of positive genus with
n\geq 1
marked points
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\in C
(and relatively prime integers
d_{1},\ldots,d_{n}\geq 1
), we construct a corresponding
model

1
-parameter family
\{f_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{A}^{1}}
of stable maps, to a projective space, with the following properties (see §
4.1
for details). When
t\neq 0
,
f_{t}
is a non-constant stable map with domain
C
, while
f_{0}
is a stable map with exactly one ghost component
C
and
n
non-constant
\mathbb{P}^{1}
components attached to
C
at the points
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}
. The number
1/d_{i}
encodes the
rate of smoothing
2
2

2

This means that if the local equation of the central fibre (near the node
p_{i}
) is
z_{i}w_{i}=0
, then the local equation of the smoothing has the form
z_{i}w_{i}=t^{d_{i}}
. The value
d_{i}

rate of node formation, i.e., degeneration

rate of smoothing
.
at the node
p_{i}
in the central fibre. Additionally, the restriction of

\mathbb{P}^{1}

, of the
n

\mathbb{P}^{1}

p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}
and regular elsewhere (see §

(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})\in\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
is a general point or whether it lies on some locus of special curves (e.g. hyperelliptic curves). With this in mind, we note that a stable map has
model ghosts
if, near each ghost component, it satisfies the same conditions on its Taylor expansions as in the central fibre of a corresponding model family (Remark
4.27
). We also allow ghost components of genus
0

stable maps with model ghosts
, without requiring any special behavior near these (Remark
4.26
).

Once we verify that the models described above satisfy the local formal smoothability hypothesis, Theorem
B

A

B

To illustrate the scope of Theorem
B

, provide pictures where possible and explicitly work out the Taylor expansion conditions in each case. For instance, we have the following example which is of genus
>2

Example 1.3

(see §

, specifically Example

.

Let
(C,p_{1},p_{2})

2

\mathbb{P}^{1}

The notion of eventual smoothability has been studied – although not using this terminology – in the algebraic setting as well as in the setting of closed symplectic manifolds equipped with compatible almost complex structures. We emphasize that these prior works give

1.2.
Strategy of the proof

The proof of Theorem
A
is based on formal deformation theory. Given a stable map
f:\mathscr{C}\to X
, we use the local formal smoothability hypothesis on it to construct a formal deformation of a neighborhood of each ghost component in
\mathscr{C}
. These local deformations are then patched together into a global deformation of the stable map
f:\mathscr{C}\to X
, yielding the desired smoothing. For this local-to-global step to work, we require a positivity assumption and it is here that we make essential use of the freedom to replace the original target variety
X
by a projective space
\mathbb{P}^{N}
.

As explained in §
1.1
, the proof of Theorem
B
involves the construction of explicit model
1
-parameter families of stable maps to which Theorem
A
can be applied. To explain the construction of the model families, in a special case, let us consider a non-singular projective curve
C
of genus
\geq 1
and a marked point
p\in C
. Let
h_{1},\ldots,h_{r}
be a set of non-constant elements of the coordinate ring of the affine curve
C\setminus\{p\}
. In particular, for
1\leq i\leq r
, the rational function
h_{i}
has a pole of order
m_{i}\geq 1
at
p
and is regular elsewhere on
C
. By re-ordering, we may assume that
m_{r}=\max_{1\leq i\leq r}m_{i}
. Now, consider the rational map

\displaystyle f:C\times\mathbb{A}^{1}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{r}

\displaystyle f(z,t)=[1:t^{m_{1}}h_{1}(z):\cdots:t^{m_{r}}h_{r}(z)].

Then,
f
determines a well-defined morphism on
C\times\mathbb{A}^{1}\setminus\{(p,0)\}
. The indeterminacy of
f
at
(p,0)
is resolved by blowing up this point and this defines a family of stable maps over
\mathbb{A}^{1}
(see Figure
1
). The stable map
f_{0}
, which is the central fibre of this family, has domain curve given by the union of
C
with a copy of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
attached at
p\in C
. From the expression for
f(z,0)
, it is clear that
f_{0}
maps the whole of
C
to the point
q=[1:0:\cdots:0]\in\mathbb{P}^{r}
. An explicit computation, using the local equation
zv=tu
for the blow-up of
C\times\mathbb{A}^{1}
at
(p,0)
, shows that the restriction of
f_{0}
to the exceptional curve
\mathbb{P}^{1}
is given by

\displaystyle f_{0}([u:v])=[u^{m_{r}}:c_{1}v^{m_{1}}u^{m_{r}-m_{1}}:\cdots:c_{r}v^{m_{r}}],

where
c_{1},\ldots,c_{r}
are some nonzero elements of the field
k
. The model family is obtained when we specialize the discussion above to the case when
m_{1},\ldots,m_{r}
generate the
Weierstrass semigroup
of
C
at
p
(see §
4.3.1
for more information).

Conventions

Unless explicitly stated otherwise, the schemes we consider will be of finite type (usually, quasi-projective) over a fixed algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic
0
. Similarly,
\mathbb{A}^{n}
(resp.
\mathbb{P}^{n}
) without subscripts is understood to be the
n

k

k

k

\Sym V^{\vee}
. When tensor products and vector space dimensions are written without subscripts, they are understood to be over
k

The word

is reserved for a scheme which is irreducible and reduced.
Morphisms
(resp.
rational maps
) of schemes are defined everywhere (resp. on a dense open subset) and indicated by
\to
(resp.
\dashrightarrow
). In particular, a

is defined everywhere (though its inverse is typically a birational map which is defined only on a dense open subset). Following established terminology, we make an exception when the domain is a prestable curve and we say
stable map
instead of stable morphism. A morphism of varieties is

if its image is dense. For affine varieties, a dominant morphism corresponds to an injective map of coordinate rings. When
X
is a scheme over a ring

, its base change along a map

. We sometimes refer to a morphism of schemes

as the
S
-scheme
X
(especially when the morphism is clear from context). A mophism

between two
S

S
.

For sheaves of
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-modules
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}
on a scheme

\mathcal{H}om_{S}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G})
denote the sheaf of

\mathcal{H}om_{S}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G})

\Hom_{S}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G})
. For a point

The notation

2.1.
Basic notions

To state the key results we need about abstract (resp. embedded) deformations of schemes, we review the definitions of the Lichtenbaum–Schlessinger
T^{i}
functors (resp. normal sheaves). Additionally, we also recall some standard notions of deformation theory (such as functors of Artin rings).

Notation 2.1
.

Let
A\to B
be a map of commutative rings with identity (not necessarily of finite presentation or over a base field). Let
\Mod_{B}
denote the category of
B
-modules.

We briefly describe the construction of the Lichtenbaum–Schlessinger functors
T^{i}(B/A,-)
, for
0\leq i\leq 2
. The reader is referred to
[
22
, Construction 3.1]
for more details.

To begin, choose a presentation
B=R/I
of the
A
-algebra
B
with
R=A[x]
being a free polynomial algebra (with
x
denoting the possibly infinite collection of variables) and
I\subset R
being an ideal. Next, choose a free
R
-module
F
fitting into a short exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to Q\to F\xrightarrow{\alpha}I\to 0

of
R
-modules. Let
F_{0}\subset Q
be the
R
-sub-module generated by the elements
\alpha(f)f^{\prime}-\alpha(f^{\prime})f
(called Koszul relations) ranging over
f,f^{\prime}\in F
. Having made these choices, we can define the (truncated) cotangent complex of
A\to B
.

Definition 2.2

(Cotangent complex)
.

With notation as above, define the
B
-modules
L_{i}
for
0\leq i\leq 2
as follows.

\displaystyle L_{0}

\displaystyle:=\Omega_{R/A}\otimes_{R}B

\displaystyle L_{1}

\displaystyle:=F\otimes_{R}B=F/IF

\displaystyle L_{2}

\displaystyle:=Q/F_{0}

Here,
\Omega_{R/A}
is the module of relative Kähler differentials of the
A
-algebra
R
. Next, define the
B
-module maps
d_{i}:L_{i}\to L_{i-1}
for
i=1,2
as follows. The map
d_{1}
is defined to be the composition of the maps
L_{1}=F/IF\to I/I^{2}
induced by
\alpha
and
I/I^{2}\to\Omega_{R/A}\otimes_{R}R/I=L_{0}
induced by the derivation
R\to\Omega_{R/A}
. The map
d_{2}
is induced by the inclusion
Q\subset F
.
These yield a complex

\displaystyle L_{2}\xrightarrow{d_{2}}L_{1}\xrightarrow{d_{1}}L_{0}

of
B
-modules, called the
cotangent complex
of
A\to B
, denoted by
L_{\bullet}
.

Using the cotangent complex
L_{\bullet}
, we can define
T^{i}(B/A,M)
for
0\leq i\leq 2
.

Definition 2.3

(
T^{i}
modules)
.

With notation as above, for any
B
-module
M
, define

\displaystyle T^{i}(B/A,M):=h^{i}(\Hom_{B}(L_{\bullet},M))

for
0\leq i\leq 2
.

Remark 2.4
.

Using
[
22
, Lemma 3.2 and Lemma 3.3]
, we see that
T^{i}(B/A,M)
are well-defined
B
-modules, i.e., they are independent of the choices of the presentations
B=R/I
and
I=F/Q
. Moreover, by
[
22
, Remark 3.3.1]
, the complex
L_{\bullet}
gives a well-defined element of the derived category of
\Mod_{B}
.

Remark 2.5
.

Using Definition
2.3
and Remark
2.4
, we obtain functors

\displaystyle T^{i}(B/A,-):\Mod_{B}\to\Mod_{B}

for
0\leq i\leq 2
. One can show that
T^{i}
functors are compatible with localization and this can be used to extend the definition to the non-affine case.

When
B
is a
k
-algebra of finite type and
M
is a
B
-module, the following statement allows us to explicitly compute
T^{i}(B/k,M)
. The case
M=B
is relevant to the study of abstract deformations of the affine scheme
\Spec B
.

Proposition 2.6

(
[
22
, Proposition 3.10]
)
.

Let
A=k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, and let
B=A/I
be a
k
-algebra obtained as a quotient of
A
. Then, for any
B
-module
M
, we have a natural exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to T^{0}(B/k,M)\to\mathrm{Hom}(\Omega_{A/k},M)\to\mathrm{Hom}(I/I^{2},M)\to T^{1}(B/k,M)\to 0

of
B
-modules. Furthermore, we have a natural isomorphism

\displaystyle T^{2}(B/A,M)=T^{2}(B/k,M)

of
B
-modules.

For the study of embedded deformations, the following notion is useful.

Definition 2.7

(Normal sheaf)
.

Let
X
be a scheme, and let
Y\subset X
be a closed subscheme with ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}:=\mathcal{I}_{Y/X}
. The
conormal sheaf
of
Y
in
X
is defined to be
\mathcal{I}/\mathcal{I}^{2}
. The
normal sheaf
of
Y
in
X
is defined to be

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{Y/X}:=\mathcal{H}om_{X}(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{O}_{Y})=\mathcal{H}om_{Y}(\mathcal{I}/\mathcal{I}^{2},\mathcal{O}_{Y}).

If
X
is non-singular and

is a local complete intersection in

, then the
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
-module
\mathcal{I}/\mathcal{I}^{2}
and its dual
\mathcal{N}_{Y/X}
are locally free. In this case,
\mathcal{N}_{Y/X}
is called the
normal bundle
of

in
X
.

Finally, we recall some standard notions which are required to state the key results on abstract and embedded deformations.

Definition 2.8

(Torsor and pseudotorsor)
.

Let
\mathfrak{S}
be a set equipped with the action of a group
G
. We call
\mathfrak{S}
a
pseudotorsor
under the action of
G
, if
G
acts freely and transitively on
\mathfrak{S}
. If
\mathfrak{S}
is also non-empty, then it is said to be a
torsor
under the action of
G
.

Notation 2.9
.

For a local
k
-algebra
R
, we denote its maximal ideal by
m_{R}
. Let
\Art_{k}
denote the category of local Artinian
k
-algebras with residue field
k
. Similarly, let
\Art_{k}^{\wedge}
denote the category of complete local
k
-algebras
R
such that for all
n\geq 1
,
R/\mathfrak{m}_{R}^{n}\in\Art_{k}
. Morphisms in
\Art_{k}
and
\Art_{k}^{\wedge}
are local
k
-algebra maps. Also, we introduce the notation
D:=k[t]/(t^{2})
, for the ring of dual numbers.

Definition 2.10

(Extension of Artin rings)
.

An
extension of Artin rings
is a surjection
R^{\prime}\twoheadrightarrow R
in
\Art_{k}
. Defining
J:=\ker(R^{\prime}\twoheadrightarrow R)
, we get an associated short exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to J\to R^{\prime}\to R\to 0.

We say the extension
R^{\prime}\twoheadrightarrow R
is
small
if we have
\mathfrak{m}_{R^{\prime}}J=0
.

Remark 2.11
.

Our definition of small extensions is a slight generalization of the one given in
[
22
]
where it is also required that
J
be
1
-dimensional over
k
.

Definition 2.12

(Functors of Artin rings)
.

A
functor of Artin rings
is a functor

F:\Art_{k}\to\Sets
. A natural transformation
F\to G
between functors of Artin rings is said to be
strongly surjective
if, for any extension
R^{\prime}\twoheadrightarrow R
in
\Art_{k}
, the induced map
F(R^{\prime})\to F(R)\times_{G(R)}G(R^{\prime})
is surjective.

Definition 2.13

(Pro-representable functor)
.

For
S\in\Art_{k}^{\wedge}
, we define
h_{S}
to be the functor of Artin rings which maps
R\in\Art_{k}
to the set of local
k
-algebra maps
S\to R
. A functor
F
of Artin rings is said to be
pro-representable
if it is isomorphic to
h_{S}
for some
S\in\Art_{k}^{\wedge}
. In this, case we also say
S

pro-represents

F
.

Definition 2.14

(Versality)
.

Let
F
be a functor of Artin rings,
S\in\Art_{k}^{\wedge}
and consider a natural transformation
\eta:h_{S}\to F
. We say that
\eta
is a

(i)

(formally) versal family
if
\eta
is strongly surjective and
\eta(D)
is a bijection,

(ii)

(formally) universal family
if
\eta
is an isomorphism.

Remark 2.15

(Formal deformations)
.

When
F
is the functor corresponding to deformations of a geometric object (as in Definitions
2.17
and
2.26
below) and
S\in\Art_{k}^{\wedge}
, we will refer to a natural transformation
\eta:h_{S}\to F
as a
formal deformation
parametrized by
S
. This is because, by the Yoneda lemma,
\eta
is equivalent to the data of a compatible collection of elements
\eta_{n}\in F(S/\mathfrak{m}_{S}^{n})
for
n\geq 1
.

2.2.
Deformations of schemes

We review (abstract) deformations of schemes and then specialize to the affine case. As usual, the schemes under consideration are assumed to be of finite type over
k
.

Definition 2.16

(Deformations of a scheme)
.

Let
X_{0}
be a scheme and
R\in\Art_{k}
. A
deformation of
X
over
R
is a pair
(X_{R},i_{R})
, where
X_{R}
is a scheme which is flat over
R
and
i_{R}:X_{0}\hookrightarrow X_{R}
is a closed immersion inducing an isomorphism
X_{0}\simeq X_{R}\otimes_{R}k
of schemes over
k
.

For fixed
X
and
R
, two deformations
(X_{R},i_{R})
and
(X^{\prime}_{R},i^{\prime}_{R})
of
X_{0}
over
R
are considered
equivalent
, if there is an isomorphism
f:X_{R}\xrightarrow{\simeq}X^{\prime}_{R}
of schemes over
R
such that
f\circ i_{R}=i^{\prime}_{R}
.

Definition 2.17

(Deformation functor of a scheme)
.

Let
X_{0}
be a scheme. The functor of Artin rings

\displaystyle\Def_{X_{0}}:\Art_{k}\to\Sets

which maps any
R\in\Art_{k}
to the set of equivalence classes of deformations of
X_{0}
over
R
is called the
deformation functor of
X_{0}
.

Definition 2.18

(Extensions of deformations of schemes)
.

Let
X_{0}
be a scheme and let
R^{\prime}\twoheadrightarrow R
be a surjection in
\Art_{k}
with kernel
J
as in Definition
2.10
. Let
(X_{R},i_{R})
be a deformation of
X_{0}
over
R
. An extension of
(X_{R},i_{R})
over
R^{\prime}
is a scheme
X_{R^{\prime}}
which is flat over
R^{\prime}
along with a closed immersion
X_{R}\hookrightarrow X_{R^{\prime}}
inducing an isomorphism
X_{R}\simeq X_{R^{\prime}}\otimes_{R^{\prime}}R
of schemes over
R
.

Two such extensions are equivalent if there is an
R^{\prime}
-isomorphism between them which is compatible with the closed immersion of
X_{R}
.

Remark 2.19
.

In the situation of Definitions
2.16
and
2.18
, we usually omit the closed immersion
i_{R}
from the notation when it is clear from the context.

Remark 2.20

(Restriction of deformation functors)
.

Since the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{R}
of a ring
R\in\Art_{k}
is nilpotent, any deformation of a scheme
X_{0}
over
R
has the same underlying topological space as
X_{0}
. In particular, if
U_{0}\subset X_{0}
is an open subscheme, then it makes sense to restrict a deformation of
X_{0}
to
U_{0}
. This gives a restriction map
\Def_{X_{0}}\to\Def_{U_{0}}
between the deformation functors of
X_{0}
and
U_{0}
.

Remark 2.21

(Deformations of affine schemes are affine)
.

If
X_{0}=\Spec B_{0}
is an affine scheme, then any deformation of
X_{0}
over
R\in\Art_{k}
is necessarily affine
[
2
, Remark 1.1]
. In this case, Definitions
2.16
,
2.17
and
2.18
have obvious reformulations with rings in place of affine schemes.

The deformations of an affine scheme over the ring
D
of dual numbers can be characterized as follows.

Proposition 2.22

(
[
22
, Corollary 5.2]
)
.

Let
X_{0}=\Spec B_{0}
be an affine scheme. Then, there is a natural bijection

\displaystyle\Def_{X_{0}}(D)=T^{1}(B_{0}/k,B_{0}).

To study higher order deformations of affine schemes, we have the following statement characterizing when extensions of deformations exist.

Proposition 2.23

(
[
22
, Theorem 10.1]
)
.

Let
B_{0}
be a
k
-algebra,
B_{R}\twoheadrightarrow B_{0}
be a deformation of
B_{0}
over
R\in\Art_{k}
and let
R^{\prime}\twoheadrightarrow R
be a small extension in
\Art_{k}
with kernel
J
as in Definition
2.10
. Then, there is an obstruction in
T^{2}(B_{0}/k,B_{0}\otimes J)
whose vanishing is equivalent to the existence of an extension
B_{R^{\prime}}\twoheadrightarrow B_{R}
of
B_{R}\twoheadrightarrow B_{0}
to
R^{\prime}
. Moreover, if extensions do exist, then the set of their equivalence classes is a torsor under the action of
T^{1}(B_{0}/k,B_{0}\otimes J)
.

Example 2.24

(Versal deformation of a node
[
22
, Theorem 14.1]
)
.

Consider the algebra corresponding to a node, i.e.,
B_{0}=k[x,y]/(xy)
. Then, a formally versal family for
\Def_{B_{0}}
is given by the restrictions of
k[x,y,t]/(xy-t)
to
k[t]/(t^{n})\in\Art_{k}
, for
n\geq 1
. We call

the
smoothing parameter
for the node.

2.3.
Deformations of closed subschemes

We review (embedded) deformations of closed subschemes.

Definition 2.25

(Deformations of closed subschemes)
.

Fix a closed subscheme
Y_{0}\subset X_{0}
and
R\in\Art_{k}
. An
embedded deformation of
Y_{0}
in
X_{0}
over
R
is a closed subscheme
Y_{R}\subset X_{0}\otimes_{k}R
such that
Y_{R}
is flat over
R
, and
Y_{R}\otimes_{R}k=Y_{0}\subset X_{0}
.

Definition 2.26

(Deformation functor of a closed subscheme)
.

Let
Y_{0}\subset X_{0}
be a closed subscheme. The functor of Artin rings

\displaystyle\Hilb_{(Y_{0},X_{0})}:\Art_{k}\to\Sets

which maps any
R\in\Art_{k}
to the set of embedded deformations of
Y_{0}
in
X_{0}
over
R
is called the
local Hilbert functor of
Y_{0}\subset X_{0}
.

Remark 2.27

(Restriction of local Hilbert functors)
.

If
U_{0}\subset X_{0}
is an open subscheme and
W_{0}:=Y_{0}\cap U_{0}\subset U_{0}
, then any embedded deformation of
Y_{0}
in
X_{0}
defines an embedded deformation of
W_{0}
in
U_{0}
, yielding a map
\Hilb_{(Y_{0},X_{0})}\to\Hilb_{(W_{0},U_{0})}
between local Hilbert functors.

Remark 2.28

(Forgetting the embedding)
.

An embedded deformation of
Y_{0}
in
X_{0}
also yields a deformation of just
Y_{0}
by forgetting the data of the embedding. This gives a forgetful map
\Hilb_{(Y_{0},X_{0})}\to\Def_{Y_{0}}
.

The embedded deformations of
Y_{0}
in
X_{0}
over the ring
D
of dual numbers can be characterized as follows.

Proposition 2.29

(
[
22
, Theorem 2.4]
)
.

For a closed subscheme
Y_{0}\subset X_{0}
, we have a natural bijection

\displaystyle\Hilb_{(Y_{0},X_{0})}(D)=H^{0}(Y_{0},\mathcal{N}_{Y_{0}/X_{0}}).

Example 2.30

(
[
22
, Corollary 2.5]
)
.

Let
Y_{0}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
be a closed subscheme with Hilbert polynomial
P(t)
. Let
H=\Hilb_{P(t)}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
be the Hilbert scheme which parametrizes closed subschemes of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
with Hilbert polynomial equal to
P(t)
. Then, the Zariski tangent space of
H
at the point
Y_{0}
can be computed as

\displaystyle T_{H,Y_{0}}=H^{0}(Y_{0},\mathcal{N}_{Y_{0}/\mathbb{P}^{n}})

using Proposition
2.29
.

To study higher order embedded deformations, we have the following statement characterizing when extensions of embedded deformations exist.

Proposition 2.31

(Special case of
[
22
, Theorem 6.2]
)
.

Let
Y_{0}\subset X_{0}
be a closed subscheme and let
R^{\prime}\twoheadrightarrow R
be a small extension in
\Art_{k}
with kernel
J
as in Definition
2.10
. Let
Y_{R}\subset X_{0}\otimes_{k}R
be a given element of
\Hilb_{(Y_{0},X_{0})}(R)
and consider lifts of this element to the set
\Hilb_{(Y_{0},X_{0})}(R^{\prime})
.

(i)

The set of lifts is a pseudotorsor under the action of
H^{0}(Y_{0},\mathcal{N}_{Y_{0}/X_{0}}\otimes_{k}J)
.

(ii)

If there exist local lifts of
Y_{R}\subset X\otimes_{k}R
to

, then there is an obstruction in
H^{1}(Y_{0},\mathcal{N}_{Y_{0}/X_{0}}\otimes_{k}J)
whose vanishing is equivalent to the global existence of a lift. Moreover, if there is one global lift, then the set of all global lifts is a torsor under the action of

.

2.4.
Local-to-global principle for deformations

We record a useful result which allows one, under suitable assumptions, to lift local abstract deformations into global embedded deformations.

Notation 2.32
.

For an affine scheme
U_{0}=\Spec B_{0}
, write
T^{1}_{U_{0}}:=T^{1}(B_{0}/k,B_{0})
.

Theorem 2.33

(
[
22
, Theorem 29.7]
)
.

Let
Z
be a non-singular projective scheme and let
X_{0}\subset Z
be a closed subscheme with isolated singularities. Let
U_{0}=\bigsqcup_{i}U_{i}
, where each
U_{i}\subset X_{0}
is an affine open subscheme containing one and only one singularity of
X_{0}
. The morphism
U_{0}\to X_{0}
induces a map

(2.1)

\displaystyle\Hilb_{(X_{0},Z)}\to\Def_{U_{0}}

of functors of Artin rings.
Suppose that
H^{1}(X_{0},\mathcal{N}_{X_{0}/Z})=0
and that the map

(2.2)

\displaystyle H^{0}(X_{0},\mathcal{N}_{X_{0}/Z})\to T^{1}_{U_{0}}

induced by (
2.1
), combined with Propositions
2.29
and
2.22
, is surjective. Then, the map (
2.1
) is strongly surjective in the sense of Definition
2.12
.

We only require the following special case of Theorem
2.33
.

Corollary 2.34
.

Using the notation of Theorem
2.33
, consider the special case in which
X_{0}
is a reduced curve and
Z=\mathbb{P}^{r}\times\mathbb{P}^{s}
. If we have

(2.3)

\displaystyle H^{1}(X_{0},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{r}}(1)|_{X_{0}})=H^{1}(X_{0},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{s}}(1)|_{X_{0}})=0,

then the hypothesis of Theorem
2.33
is satisfied, and as a result, the map (
2.1
) is strongly surjective.

Proof.

Up to replacing
\mathbb{P}^{r}\times\mathbb{P}^{s}
by
\mathbb{P}^{r}
and replacing the condition (
2.3
) by the condition
H^{0}(X_{0},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{r}}(1)|_{X_{0}})=0
, this statement is shown in the proof of
[
22
, Proposition 29.9]
. To get the result for
Z=\mathbb{P}^{r}\times\mathbb{P}^{s}
, we replace the Euler exact sequence for
\mathbb{P}^{r}
by its analogue for
\mathbb{P}^{r}\times\mathbb{P}^{s}
, namely

\displaystyle 0\to\mathcal{O}_{X_{0}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X_{0}}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{r}}(1)^{\oplus(r+1)}|_{X_{0}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{s}}(1)^{\oplus(s+1)}|_{X_{0}}\to T_{\mathbb{P}^{r}\times\mathbb{P}^{s}}|_{X_{0}}\to 0.

and use the condition (
2.3
) in place of
H^{0}(X_{0},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{r}}(1)|_{X_{0}})=0
. The argument from
[
22
, Proposition 29.9]
then applies word for word.
∎

3.

3.4.

Let

The non-constant stable map

Remark 3.22
.

Note that the condition of local formal smoothability of a stable map is vacuously satisfied when there are no ghosts.

Proposition 4.28

(Local smoothability of genus
0
pinching)
.

Let
\nu:\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{S}
be a pinching. Let
s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}\in\mathscr{S}
be the points over which
\nu
fails to be an isomorphism. For
1\leq i\leq r
, let

be the set-theoretic inverse image of

under
\nu
.

Fix

. Denote the closure of
\mathscr{C}\setminus C_{i}
by
\Sigma_{i}\subset\mathscr{C}
. Suppose that no irreducible component of
\Sigma_{i}
meets

more than once and that

. Then, the pinching

is smoothable near
s_{i}
, in the sense of Definition
3.16
.

Proof.

Let
\Sigma_{i,1},\ldots,\Sigma_{i,n}
be an enumeration of the irreducible components of

which meet
C_{i}
. By assumption, for
1\leq j\leq n
, the intersection

consists of a single point, which we denote by

. Since we are investigating

, we may replace
\mathscr{C}
by the sub-curve

. It is enough to exhibit

as an étale pullback (near

) of another locally smoothable pinching. By choosing a local coordinate in
\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma_{i,j},p_{j}}
, for

, this means we may replace each

by a copy of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

Let us summarize the reductions so far. We now have the pinching of a genus
0
prestable curve at connected sub-curve. Moreover, the complement of this sub-curve consists of
n
pairwise disjoint copies of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
. We are left to show that this special kind of pinching is locally smoothable. The last assertion is exactly the content of Proposition

.
∎

in §

.

Theorem 4.29

(Stable maps with model ghosts are eventually smoothable)
.

Let

be a stable map with model ghosts in the sense of Definition
4.24
. Then, it is eventually smoothable in the sense of Definition

We have a pinching

4.24

4.3.4.
Suspension

We give a procedure to create new model singularities out of old ones.

Start with
(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
and
d_{1},\ldots,d_{n}\geq 1
as before and let
\mathfrak{A}
be the coordinate ring of the model singularity (with
n
branches) associated to this data. Using Corollary
4.18
, define
M\geq 1
to be the smallest integer for which we have
\mathfrak{A}_{m}=Q_{m}
for all
m\geq M
. Now, choose an integer

and
r
distinct points

and integers

be the coordinate ring of the model singularity (with
n+r

(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n+r})
and
d_{1},\ldots,d_{n+r}
. Let

be the coordinate ring of its seminormalization (as in Definition
4.16
) with
\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}\subset Q^{\prime}
being the induced inclusion of graded algebras. We can completely describe
\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}
in terms of
\mathfrak{A}
.

Since we chose
d_{n+1},\ldots,d_{n+r}\geq M
, we have
\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}_{m}=\mathfrak{A}_{m}
and

for

. Corollary
4.18
shows that

and

are both

\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}_{m}=Q^{\prime}_{m}
for

. We refer to the model singularity
\Spec\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}
as a

of the model singularity
\Spec\mathfrak{A}
.

Remark 4.37

(Explicit geometric description of a suspension)
.

Given a closed embedding

with

with

while the second is the product of
\{0\}\subset\mathbb{A}^{N}
with the union of the

coordinate axes in
\mathbb{A}^{r}
.

Remark 4.40

(Model ghost condition for suspension)
.

We can generalize the observation of Examples
4.38
and
4.39
as follows. The model ghost condition for the suspension
\Spec\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}
amounts to the same condition on
f|_{\mathscr{E}}
near
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}
as for
\Spec\mathfrak{A}
and
no additional condition
on
f|_{\mathscr{E}}
near
p_{n+1},\ldots,p_{n+r}
.

This can be informally explained as follows. A ghost component modeled on
\Spec\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}
may be smoothed in two steps as follows. To begin, we smooth the nodes at
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}
, using the model
\Spec\mathfrak{A}
. Now that the ghost is gone, we are free to smooth the remaining nodes

without any additional conditions. To carry out this
2

1

postpone
smoothing the nodes
p_{n+1},\ldots,p_{n+r}
until the smoothing of the nodes
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}
is done, i.e., we take the rates of smoothing

to be small in comparison to
1/d_{1},\ldots,1/d_{n}
.

(Contraction property)

\displaystyle\mathscr{X}\setminus C_{0}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathscr{Y}\setminus\{\overline{c}\}

Let

for a regular function

\displaystyle A_{g}=\Gamma(\mathscr{X},\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}})_{g}=\Gamma(\mathscr{X}_{g},\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}_{g}}).

Lemma A.14

(Inverse of contraction)

Define the subvariety

\displaystyle\Gamma\subset\mathscr{Y}\times C

The natural morphism

\displaystyle\mathscr{X}\to\Gamma\subset\mathscr{Y}\times C,

on the first factor and

on the second factor, is bijective on closed points (and is even an isomorphism away from

When

\displaystyle A\otimes R_{i}\to\frac{R_{i}[t,w_{i}]}{(z_{i}w_{i}-t^{d_{i}})}

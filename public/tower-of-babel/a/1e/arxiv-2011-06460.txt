A non-uniform corner-cutting subdivision scheme with an improved accuracy
Byeongseon Jeong § , Hyoseon Yang † , and Jungho Yoon ‡

Abstract
The aim of this paper is to construct a new non-uniform corner-cutting (NUCC) subdivision scheme that improves the accuracy of the classical (stationary and nonstationary) methods. The refinement rules are formulated via the reproducing property of exponential polynomials. An exponential polynomial has a shape parameter so that it may be adapted to the characteristic of the given data. In this study, we propose a method of selecting the shape parameter, so that it enables the associated scheme to achieve an improved approximation order (that is, three ), while the classical methods attain the second order accuracy. An analysis of convergence and smoothness of the proposed scheme is conducted. The proposed scheme is shown to have the same smoothness as the classical Chaikin’s corner-cutting algorithm, that is, C 1 C^{1} . Finally, some numerical examples are presented to demonstrate the advantages of the new corner-cutting algorithm.

Introduction

Subdivision schemes are recursive processes of generating smooth curves
or surfaces from a given sequence of control points.
By applying a set of refinement rules to an initial
sequence of points iteratively, a subdivision scheme computes denser
sequences of points under a suitable convergence criteria.
Due to the algorithmic efficiency in geometric modeling and design,
subdivision schemes are extensively studied and utilized in many research fields
such as computer-aided geometric design, computer graphics, image processing, scientific computing etc.
General discussions on subdivision schemes may be found in the references

[
3
,
17
,
21
]
.

The smoothness and approximation order of limit functions obtained
by the recursive refinement process are two key features
of a convergent subdivision scheme.
They are strongly related to the generation or reproduction properties
of a class of functions such as
a set of algebraic polynomials

[
3
,
4
,
6
,

,

,

,

,

]

and exponential polynomials
[
19
,
22
,
31
]
.
Mostly well-known subdivision schemes based on algebraic
polynomials are the B-spline
[
4
,
8
]

and Delauriers-Dubuc interpolatory schemes
[
14
]
,
which respectively provide optimal smoothness and approximation order.
Recent studies have reported that exponential polynomials
are useful and effective in designing
and approximating various geometric shapes.
The refinement rules of the associated subdivision scheme are level-dependent,
in which case we call the scheme
nonstationary
.
Since the earlier works as
[
19
,

,
31
]
on
exponential B-splines,
important theoretical progress has been achieved during the last two decades,
especially in regards to
the reproducing and generation properties of exponential polynomials

and the construction of wavelets

[

,

,

]

Also, important practical subdivision
algorithms have been reported. The readers are referred to

[

,

,

]
) and the references therein.

The quadratic B-spline,
known as Chaikin’s corner-cutting algorithm
[

]
,
is one of the most popular
and fundamental scheme with various extensions and applications

[

,

,

,

,

,
33

.
The exponential B-spline scheme of degree
2
is its
natural nonstationary extension. It has advantages because it can reproduce
geometrically important shapes in CAGD.
Such merits are attributed to the ability to reproduce
exponential polynomials.
At this point, one should notice that
an exponential polynomial has a shape parameter that can be
tuned to the characteristic of the given data.
Thus, the final approximation may be more faithful to the data
initially given.
Motivated by this observation,
this study aims to introduce a new non-uniform corner-cutting
algorithm that generalizes the exponential B-spline of degree 2.
In particular, we propose an optimal method of selecting
the shape parameter in the exponential polynomial, so that
the proposed scheme achieves the improved approximation
order (that is,

while the classical corner-cutting (stationary and nonstationary) methods
have the second order accuracy.

Further, we show that the proposed scheme provides
the same smoothness (that is,

as the classical Chaikin’s corner-cutting algorithm.
Finally, some numerical examples are presented to demonstrate the
advantages of the new scheme.

2
Preliminaries

In this section we present some necessary notation
and background material.
Let
\mathbb{Z}
be the set of all integers, and denote by
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
the set
of nonnegative integers.
For a given sequence
{\mathbf{f}}^{k}:=\{{\mathbf{f}}^{k}_{j}:j\in\mathbb{Z}\}
,
the refinement rule of a non-uniform subdivision scheme

\{S_{k}:k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\}
is defined by

\displaystyle{\mathbf{f}}^{k+1}_{2j+\nu}:=S_{k}{\mathbf{f}}^{k}_{2j+\nu}:=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbf{a}}^{j,k}_{2i+\nu}{\mathbf{f}}^{k}_{j-i},\quad\nu=0,1.

(1)

This can be written formally as
{\mathbf{f}}^{k+1}:=S_{k}{\mathbf{f}}^{k}
.
The sequence
{\mathbf{a}}^{j,k}:=\{{\mathbf{a}}^{j,k}_{n}:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
is called
the subdivision
mask
and is assumed to be finitely supported.
In fact, some fundamental
characteristics of a subdivision are embedded in its symbol
associated with the mask
{\mathbf{a}}^{j,k}
:

\displaystyle a^{j,k}(z):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbf{a}}^{j,k}_{n}z^{n}.

Since the mask
{\mathbf{a}}^{j,k}
is finitely supported, the symbol

a^{j,k}(z)
becomes a Laurent polynomial.
The norm of a subdivision operator
S_{k}
associated with the refinement
masks
\{{\mathbf{a}}^{j,k}:j\in\mathbb{Z}\}
is defined by

\displaystyle\|S_{k}\|_{\infty}=\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big\{\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}|{\mathbf{a}}^{j,k}_{2i+\nu}|:\nu=0,1\Big\}.

It is of necessity to recall the concept of parametrization for
a subdivision scheme.
For each level
k
, the value
{\mathbf{f}}^{k}_{j}
can be attached to a grid point
value
t^{k}_{j}:=2^{-k}j
or
2^{-k}(j-1/2)
, which are respectively called
primal
and
dual
parametrization.
The value
k
indicates the scale parameter while
j-\frac{1}{2}

(or
j
) stands
for the location parameter.
For a given parametrization
\{t^{k}_{j}\}
,
a subdivision scheme
\{S_{k}\}
is
convergent
if for any initial data
{\mathbf{f}}^{0}
,
there exists a function
f\in C(\mathbb{R})
such that for any compact subset
\Omega
of
\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}\cap 2^{k}\Omega}|{\mathbf{f}}^{k}_{j}-f(t^{k}_{j})|=0

and
f\not\equiv 0
for some
{\mathbf{f}}^{0}
.
Also, a subdivision scheme
\{S_{k}\}
is said to be
stable
if
there exists a constant
C>0
such that
for any
{\mathbf{f}}\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
,

\displaystyle\sup_{k,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\|S_{k+n}\cdots S_{k}{\mathbf{f}}\|_{\infty}\leq C\|{\mathbf{f}}\|_{\infty}.

In this study, the term ‘reproduction’ is used to indicate the capability
of a subdivision scheme of reproducing the functions from which
the initial sequence of data values are sampled.
Below, we specify the definition of exponential polynomial reproduction.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathbb{E}
be a space of exponential polynomials.
We say that a subdivision scheme
\{S_{k}\}

reproduces
the
functions in
\mathbb{E}
if
for the initial data
{\mathbf{f}}^{0}:=\{f(t^{0}_{j}):j\in\mathbb{Z}\}

with
f\in\mathbb{E}
, each sequence
{\mathbf{f}}^{k}:=S_{k-1}\cdots S_{0}{\mathbf{f}}^{0}

with
k\geq 1

satisfies the relation
{\mathbf{f}}^{k}_{j}=f(t^{k}_{j})
for any
j\in\mathbb{Z}
.

It is well-known that the exponential B-splines can reproduce
at most two exponential polynomials if a suitable normalization
factor is taken. Hence, this study is focused on the
following type of space

\mathbb{E}_{2}:={\rm span}\{\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1}\},

where
\varphi_{0}
and
\varphi_{1}
are linearly independent
exponential polynomials.
A basic requirement for
\{\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1}\}
is that its Wronskian
matrix at
\vartheta=0
is invertible, that is,

{\rm det}\big(\varphi^{(\beta)}_{\alpha}(0):\alpha,\beta=0,1\big)\not=0.

It is clear that if this condition is satisfied,
then the functions
\varphi_{0}
and
\varphi_{1}
are linearly independent.

In addition, for later use, we introduce some notations.
For a given sequence
{\mathbf{f}}^{k}:=\{{\mathbf{f}}^{k}_{j}:j\in\mathbb{Z}\}

with density
2^{-k}
,

\Delta{\mathbf{f}}^{k}
indicates its second-order difference defined by

\Delta{\mathbf{f}}^{k}_{j}:=2^{2k}({\mathbf{f}}^{k}_{j-1}-2{\mathbf{f}}^{k}_{j}+{\mathbf{f}}^{k}_{j+1}).

Also, denote
\nabla{\mathbf{f}}^{k}_{j+1}=2^{k}({\mathbf{f}}^{k}_{j+1}-{\mathbf{f}}^{k}_{j})
.
In addition, letting
{\mathbf{f}}^{0}
be the initial sequence of data,
put
{\mathbf{d}}^{0}=\Delta{\mathbf{f}}^{0}
, and then, for each refinement level
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

{\mathbf{d}}^{k}
is defined as

\displaystyle{\mathbf{d}}^{k}:=S^{k}_{a}{\mathbf{d}}^{0}=S^{k}_{a}\Delta{\mathbf{f}}^{0},

(2)

where
S_{a}
is the subdivision of the B-spline of degree
2
,
i.e., the classical corner-cutting method.

3
Nonuniform corner-cutting subdivision scheme

The objective of this section is to construct a novel non-uniform corner-cutting
(referred to as ‘NUCC’) subdivision scheme.
The new subdivision masks are defined via the property of
reproducing two exponential polynomials
\{\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1}\}

based on the dual parametrization.
The exponential polynomials
\{\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1}\}

and the internal shape parameter are adjusted
by reflecting the local data feature to improve the accuracy.
To this end, for a given data
{\mathbf{f}}^{k}_{i}
at level
k
, we assume
without great loss that
{\mathbf{f}}^{k}_{i}
and
\nabla{\mathbf{f}}^{k}_{i+1}

do not vanish simultaneously. If this is not the case,
one may put the internal shape parameter as zero, then the scheme becomes
the classical corner-cutting method.

\bullet

Non-uniform Subdivision Mask.

For a given sequence of data
{\mathbf{f}}^{k}
at level
k
,
we first assume that
{\mathbf{f}}^{k}_{j}

are nonzero (practically, away from zero).
The subdivision mask
{\mathbf{a}}^{j,k}:=\{{\mathbf{a}}^{j,k}_{n}:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}

is formulated via the reproducing property of two
exponential polynomials
\{\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1}\}

at the evaluation point, say

\overline{t}:=t^{k+1}_{\ell}
(where
\ell=2j,2j+1
).
According to the parity of location
\ell
,
we determine the two sets of nonzero coefficients: even mask

\{{\mathbf{a}}^{j,k}_{-2},\ {\mathbf{a}}^{j,k}_{0}\}

and odd mask
\{{\mathbf{a}}^{j,k}_{-1},\ {\mathbf{a}}^{j,k}_{1}\}
.
To derive the even mask, consider the following local reproduction property
of the two exponential functions
\{\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1}\}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{0}(\overline{t})\varphi_{n}(t^{k}_{j})+\mathcal{L}_{1}(\overline{t})\varphi_{n}(t^{k}_{j+1})=\varphi_{n}(\overline{t}),\quad n=0,1.

(3)

where
\mathcal{L}_{0}
and
\mathcal{L}_{1}
are the Lagrange functions
in the space
\mathbb{E}_{2}={\rm span}\{\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1}\}
.
Representing this linear system explicitly yields the matrix form

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\varphi_{0}(t^{k}_{j})&\varphi_{0}(t^{k}_{j+1})\\
\varphi_{1}(t^{k}_{j})&\varphi_{1}(t^{k}_{j+1})\\
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\mathcal{L}_{0}(\overline{t})\\
\mathcal{L}_{1}(\overline{t})\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\varphi_{0}(\overline{t})\\
\varphi_{1}(\overline{t})\end{bmatrix}.

The even mask of the proposed subdivision scheme
(associated to the location
\overline{t}
) is defined as
the solution of this linear system.
To be more precise,
letting
\overline{t}:=t^{k+1}_{2j}
be an evaluation point at level
k+1
,
we select

\{\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1}\}:=\{\exp\big(\gamma_{2j}(\cdot-\overline{t})\big),\exp\big(-\gamma_{2j}(\cdot-\overline{t})\big)\},

where
\gamma_{2j}
is real or pure imaginary.
Then the solution of the above linear system
can be formulated specifically as

\displaystyle{\mathbf{a}}^{j,k}_{0}:=\mathcal{L}_{0}(\overline{t})=\frac{\sinh(\frac{3}{4}\gamma_{2j}2^{-k})}{\sinh(\gamma_{2j}2^{-k})},\quad{\mathbf{a}}^{j,k}_{-2}:=\mathcal{L}_{1}(\overline{t})=\frac{\sinh(\frac{1}{4}\gamma_{2j}2^{-k})}{\sinh(\gamma_{2j}2^{-k})}.

(4)

The odd mask is defined in a similar way.
Letting
\overline{t}=t^{k+1}_{2j+1}
,
we consider the reproduction of two exponential functions

\{\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1}\}=\{\exp\big(\gamma_{2j+1}(\cdot-\overline{t})\big),\exp\big(-\gamma_{2j+1}(\cdot-\overline{t})\big)\}
.
Under this setting,
the odd mask is also formulated as the solution of
the linear system (
3
):

\displaystyle{\mathbf{a}}^{j,k}_{1}=\frac{\sinh(\frac{1}{4}\gamma_{2j+1}2^{-k})}{\sinh(\gamma_{2j+1}2^{-k})},\quad{\mathbf{a}}^{j,k}_{-1}=\frac{\sinh(\frac{3}{4}\gamma_{2j+1}2^{-k})}{\sinh(\gamma_{2j+1}2^{-k})}.

(5)

As a conclusion, the new subdivision mask is of the form

\displaystyle{\mathbf{a}}^{j,k}=\Big\{\frac{\sinh(\frac{1}{4}\gamma_{2j}2^{-k})}{\sinh(\gamma_{2j}2^{-k})},\ \frac{\sinh(\frac{3}{4}\gamma_{2j+1}2^{-k})}{\sinh(\gamma_{2j+1}2^{-k})},\ \frac{\sinh(\frac{3}{4}\gamma_{2j}2^{-k})}{\sinh(\gamma_{2j}2^{-k})},\ \frac{\sinh(\frac{1}{4}\gamma_{2j+1}2^{-k})}{\sinh(\gamma_{2j+1}2^{-k})}\Big\}.

(6)

In this study, we especially suggest to choose the shape parameter

\gamma_{2j+\nu}
for
\nu\in\{0,1\}
as

\displaystyle\gamma_{2j+\nu}:=\gamma_{2j+\nu,k}:=\sqrt{\frac{{\mathbf{d}}^{k}_{j+\nu}}{{\mathbf{f}}^{k}_{j+\nu}+\epsilon}},\qquad{\rm sign}({\mathbf{f}}^{k}_{j})={\rm sign}(\epsilon)

(7)

where a nonzero number
\epsilon
is employed
to prevent the denominator becoming too small or zero.

Remark 3.1
.

Suppose that the initial data values are sampled from a smooth function

f
. Let
\overline{t}=t^{k+1}_{2j+\nu}
be an evaluation point at level
k+1
.
It can be easily deduced from (
7
) that

\gamma^{2}_{2j+\nu}\approx{\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(\overline{t})}{f(\overline{t})}}.

We will see later that this choice of the shape parameter enables
the proposed scheme to provide improved order of accuracy.
For better readability of this paper, the specific discussion
on the motivation for this choice
\gamma_{2j+\nu}
is postponed
to Section
5
.

Remark 3.2
.

We suggest to choose the non-zero value
\epsilon
depending on
the density of the given initial data.
For instance, one may put
\epsilon
as
|\epsilon|=2^{-2k_{0}}
.
Accordingly, the shape parameter
\gamma_{j}
can
be uniformly bounded independent of
j
.

This study is mainly focused on the mask in (
6
). However,
the denominator in (
7
) is zero or very small,
the refinement rule may be amended as below.

\bullet

Alternative Approach.

If
{\mathbf{f}}^{k}_{j+\nu}
(
\nu=0,1
) is zero or very close to zero,
an alternative way to construct
the subdivision masks is given as follows.
Let
\overline{t}=t^{k+1}_{2j+\nu}
be the grid point associated to

{\mathbf{f}}^{k+1}_{2j+\nu}
.
Then we consider the space of exponential polynomials

\mathbb{E}_{2}={\rm span}\{1,\exp(\gamma_{2j+\nu}(\cdot-\overline{t}))\}

and then select the shape parameter as

\displaystyle\gamma_{2j+\nu}:=\gamma_{2j+\nu,k}:={\frac{{\mathbf{d}}^{k}_{j+\nu}}{\nabla{\mathbf{f}}^{k}_{j+1}+\epsilon}},\qquad\nabla{\mathbf{f}}^{k}_{j+1}=2^{k}({\mathbf{f}}^{k}_{j+1}-{\mathbf{f}}^{k}_{j}).

(8)

Accordingly, by following the same technique as above,
the new subdivision mask can be derived as the form

\displaystyle{\mathbf{a}}^{j,k}_{-2}=\frac{e^{\gamma_{2j}\frac{1}{4}2^{-k}}-1}{e^{\gamma_{2j}2^{-k}}-1},\quad{\mathbf{a}}^{j,k}_{-1}=\frac{e^{\gamma_{2j+1}\frac{3}{4}2^{-k}}-1}{e^{\gamma_{2j+1}2^{-k}}-1},\quad{\mathbf{a}}^{j,k}_{0}=1-{\mathbf{a}}^{j,k}_{-2},\quad{\mathbf{a}}^{j,k}_{1}=1-{\mathbf{a}}^{j,k}_{-1}.

(9)

One should note that the above even and odd rules
satisfy the partition of unity, respectively. Hence,
for later use, it is necessary to remark that
its Laurent polynomial
a^{j,k}(z)

satisfies the equations

a^{j,k}(1)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbf{a}}^{j,k}_{n}=2,\qquad a^{j,k}(-1)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbf{a}}^{j,k}_{n}(-1)^{n}=0.

(10)

Remark 3.3
.

It is obvious that the mask
{\mathbf{a}}^{j,k}
converges to

{\bf a}=\{\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4},\frac{3}{4},\frac{1}{4}\}
,
as
k\to\infty
, which is the mask of B-spline of degree
2

(known as Chaikin’s corner cutting method).
If
{\mathbf{d}}^{k}_{j}
is zero, then
the NUCC scheme turns out to be the corner-cutting method.
So, the NUCC method is nonlinear and it can be understood
as a perturbation of the Chaikin’s corner-cutting algorithm.
In this regard, without great loss,
we assume throughout this paper that

-\frac{1}{4}+{\bf a}_{n}<{\mathbf{a}}^{j,k}_{n}<{\bf a}_{n}+\frac{1}{4}

for the mask
{\mathbf{a}}_{n}
of the Chaikin’s algorithm.

4
Analysis of convergence and smoothness

In this section, we discuss the convergence and the smoothness of
the NUCC scheme
\{S_{k}\}
with the masks defined in (
6
).
Our specific goal is to prove that the scheme
\{S_{k}\}

generates
C^{1}
limit curves. Our analysis basically relies on
the concept of the ‘asymptotic equivalence’ relation
between two subdivision schemes.

Definition 4.1
.

A non-uniform subdivision scheme
\{S_{k}\}
with the mask

\{{\mathbf{a}}^{j,k}_{n}\}
is said to be

asymptotically equivalent
to a uniform stationary scheme
S
with
the mask
\{{\mathbf{a}}_{n}\}
if

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|{\mathbf{a}}^{j,k}_{n}-{\mathbf{a}}_{n}|<\infty.

(11)

Based on this concept,
we first show that the NUCC scheme is stable and convergent.
To do this, we cite a result
[
23
, Proposition 3.2]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

[
23
]

Suppose that a non-uniform subdivision scheme
\{S_{k}\}
is asymptotically
equivalent to a uniform stationary scheme
S
.
If
S
is stable and convergent,
then so is the scheme
\{S_{k}\}
.

Theorem 4.3
.

The NUCC scheme
\{S_{k}\}

is stable and convergent, that is
C^{0}
.

Proof.

Using the Maclaurin series expansion of

\sinh(\gamma x)

which appears in the explicit form of the mask

{\mathbf{a}}^{j,k}_{n}
in (
6
),
it is clear that

\displaystyle|{\mathbf{a}}^{j,k}_{n}-{\mathbf{a}}_{n}|\leq c\gamma_{2j+\nu}^{2}2^{-2k},\quad\forall j\in\mathbb{Z},\quad\nu=0,1

(12)

for the mask
{\mathbf{a}}_{n}
of the quadratic B-spline scheme.
In view of the discussion in Remark
3.2
,

\gamma_{2j+\nu}
is uniformly bounded. Hence,
the relation (
11
) is readily satisfied.
Similarly, we can prove

|{\mathbf{a}}^{j,k}_{n}-{\mathbf{a}}_{n}|\leq c\gamma_{2j+\nu}2^{-k}
for the mask in (
9
).
It ensures that
the NUCC scheme
\{S_{k}\}
is asymptotically
equivalent to
the quadratic B-spline scheme
S_{\mathbf{a}}
.
Since
S_{\mathbf{a}}
is stable and convergent, by Lemma
4.2
,
the scheme
\{S_{k}\}
is also stable and convergent.
∎

To analyze the smoothness of a scheme
\{S_{k}\}
,
we use the so-called ‘Property A’ introduced in
[
23
]
,
which is described in terms of the Laurent polynomial

\displaystyle D^{j,k}_{m}(z):=\sum_{\ell=0}^{m}(-1)^{\ell}{m\choose\ell}z^{\ell}a^{j-\ell,k}(z),

(13)

where
a^{j,k}(z)
is the symbol associated to the mask

\{{\mathbf{a}}^{j,k}_{n}:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
.

Definition 4.4
.

A non-uniform subdivision scheme
\{S_{k}\}
is said to satisfy
the
Property A of order m
if

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{k(m-\alpha)}\Big|\frac{d^{\alpha}D^{j,k}_{m}}{dz^{\alpha}}(\pm 1)\Big|<\infty,\quad 0\leq\alpha<m.

A sufficient condition for the smoothness of a non-uniform subdivision scheme
is discussed in
[
23
]
.

Theorem 4.5
.

[
23
]

Suppose that a non-uniform binary scheme
\{S_{k}\}

is asymptotically equivalent to a stationary
scheme
S_{a}
.
If
\{S_{k}\}
satisfies Property A of orders
1\leq\ell\leq m
and

S_{a}
is a
C^{m}
scheme with a stable basic limit function, then

\{S_{k}\}
is also
C^{m}
.

Theorem 4.6
.

The NUCC scheme
\{S_{k}\}
is
C^{1}
.

Proof.

We first prove that
the NUCC satisfies Property A of order
1
. To this end,
we need to estimate the Laurent
polynomial

D^{j,k}_{1}(z)=a^{j,k}(z)-za^{j-1,k}(z)
at
z=-1,1
.
Let
a(z)
be the symbol of the quadratic B-spline.
It follows that

\displaystyle|D^{j,k}_{1}(\pm 1)|\leq|a^{j,k}(\pm 1)-a(\pm 1)|+|a(\pm 1)-(\pm 1)a^{j-1,k}(\pm 1)|.

where
a(1)=2
and
a(-1)=0
.
Here, to estimate the above terms, we need to consider the Laurent
polynomial
a^{j,k}(z)
associated to both of the cases (
6
)
and (
9
) respectively.
It is easy to see that

a^{j,k}(z)
of (
6
) satisfies

a^{j,k}(\pm 1)=a(\pm 1)+O(2^{-2k}).

Also, as observed in (
10
),
the mask of the NUCC of (
9
) satisfies
the partition of unity
such that
a^{j,k}(1)=2
and
a^{j,k}(-1)=0
.
So, clearly,
a^{j,k}(\pm 1)=a(\pm 1)
.
Consequently, it is immediate that

|D^{j,k}_{1}(\pm 1)|\leq c2^{-2k}

for a constant
c>0
.
It verifies that the NUCC scheme fulfills the Property A.
Moreover, as shown in the proof of Theorem
4.3
,
the NUCC scheme
\{S_{k}\}
is asymptotically equivalent to
the quadratic B-spline subdivision
S_{a}
.
Since
S_{a}
is
C^{1}
and its basic limit functions is stable,
by Theorem
4.5
,

\{S_{k}\}
is also
C^{1}
. It completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

A bivariate subdivision scheme for modeling surfaces can be easily
constructed via the tensor product of the univariate schemes.
Hence, it clearly follows from
[
9
, Corollary 1]

that the tensor product
of univariate NUCC schemes is
C^{1}
.

5
Approximation order

This section aims to show that the NUCC scheme

\{S_{k}\}
achieves the approximation order
3
,
while the classical methods provide the second order accuracy.
Suppose that the initial data is of the form

{\mathbf{f}}^{0}=\{f(2^{-k_{0}}(n-\frac{1}{2})):n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
with
k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.
We shall show that

\|{\mathbf{f}}^{\infty}-f\|_{\infty}\leq c_{f}2^{-3k_{0}}

where

{\mathbf{f}}^{\infty}=\lim_{k\to\infty}S_{k}\cdots S_{0}{\mathbf{f}}^{0}
.
This work especially focuses on approximating functions
in the Sobolev space

W^{r}_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}):=\big\{f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}:\|f\|_{r,\infty}:=\sum_{n=0}^{r}\|f^{(n)}\|_{\infty}<\infty\big\}

for
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

Since the proposed scheme is data-dependent,
the techniques commonly used for the proof in the uniform case
are not applicable to our case. Eventually, the technique for this proof is
more involved.
In order to facilitate our proof, throughout this section,
we use the following notation.
For a given
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
denote by

\displaystyle{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k}:=\big\{f(2^{-k_{0}}t^{k}_{j}):j\in\mathbb{Z}\big\}

(14)

the sequence sampled from a smooth function
f(2^{-k_{0}}\cdot)

at the
k
th level grid points
t^{k}_{j}
.
In the following lemma,
we discuss the approximation properties of the subdivision operator
S_{k}

to the sequence of data
{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

For a given function
f\in W^{3}_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, let
{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k}

be the sequence of data of the form (
14
).
If
\{S_{k}\}
is the NUCC scheme
acting on the initial data of the form

{\mathbf{f}}^{0}=\{f(2^{-k_{0}}(n-\frac{1}{2})):n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
, then we have

|S_{k}{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k}_{j}-{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k+1}_{j}|\leq c_{f}2^{-2k_{0}-k},\quad j\in\mathbb{Z},

(15)

with a constant
c_{f}>0
depending on
f
.

Proof.

Letting
S_{\mathbf{a}}
be the subdivision operator of the quadratic B-spline scheme
with the mask
{\mathbf{a}}
,
we can express

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} S_{k}{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k}_{j}-{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k+1}_{j}=\big(S_{k}{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k}_{j}-S_{{\mathbf{a}}}{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k}_{j}\big)+\big(S_{{\mathbf{a}}}{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k}_{j}-{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k+1}_{j}\big)\end{aligned}

(16)

Since the quadratic B-spline
S_{\mathbf{a}}

provides the second-order accuracy for smooth functions and
the data
{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k}
has the density
2^{-k_{0}-k}
,
it holds clearly that

|S_{{\mathbf{a}}}{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k}_{j}-{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k+1}_{j}|=O(2^{-2(k_{0}+k)})
.
Thus, it remains to estimate the term

|S_{k}{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k}_{j}-S_{{\mathbf{a}}}{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k}_{j}|
.
From the proof of Theorem
4.3
and Remark
3.2
,
we have

\displaystyle|S_{k}{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k}_{j}-S_{{\mathbf{a}}}{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k}_{j}|\leq\|{\mathbf{a}}^{j,k}-{\mathbf{a}}\|_{\infty}\|{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k}\|_{\infty}\leq c2^{-k}\|{\mathbf{d}}^{k}\|_{\infty}\|{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k}\|_{\infty}

with a constant
c>0
.
By construction,
{\mathbf{d}}^{k}=S^{k}_{\mathbf{a}}\Delta{\mathbf{f}}^{0}
.
Since the initial data
{\mathbf{f}}^{0}
is sampled from the dilated
function
f(2^{-k_{0}}\cdot)
,
it is clear that

|\Delta{\mathbf{f}}^{0}_{n}|\leq|2^{-2k_{0}}f^{\prime\prime}(2^{-k_{0}}(n-\frac{1}{2}))|+c^{\prime}2^{-3k_{0}}\|f^{\prime\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}

for some constant
c^{\prime}>0
.
It concludes

|S_{k}{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k}_{j}-S_{{\mathbf{a}}}{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k}_{j}|\leq c_{f}2^{-2k_{0}-k}

with
c_{f}>0
independent of
k
.
Hence, we obtain the lemma’s claim.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Suppose that the initial data

{\mathbf{f}}^{0}:=\{f(2^{-k_{0}}(n-\frac{1}{2})):n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
is sampled
from a function
f\in W^{3}_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, and let
{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k}
be the sequence of data of the form (
14
).
If
{\mathbf{f}}^{k}=S_{k-1}\cdots S_{0}{\mathbf{f}}^{0}

with the NUCC scheme
\{S_{k}\}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|{\mathbf{f}}^{k}-{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k}\|_{\infty}\leq c_{f}2^{-2k_{0}},

where
c_{f}>0
is a positive constant depending on
f
but independent of

k_{0}
.

Proof.

We can represent
{\mathbf{f}}^{k}
as the following telescoping sum:

\displaystyle{\mathbf{f}}^{k}=S_{k-1}{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k-1}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k-1}S_{k-1}\cdots S_{k-\ell}(S_{k-\ell-1}{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k-\ell-1}-{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k-\ell}).

According to Theorem
4.3
, the NUCC scheme
\{S_{k}\}
is stable
such that

\|S_{k-1}\cdots S_{k-\ell}\|_{\infty}\leq c
for any
k
and
\ell

with
k\geq\ell
.
It induces the relation

\displaystyle|{\mathbf{f}}^{k}_{j}-{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k}_{j}|

\displaystyle\leq\|S_{k-1}{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k-1}-{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k}\|_{\infty}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k-1}\|S_{k-1}\cdots S_{k-\ell}\|_{\infty}\|S_{k-\ell-1}{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k-\ell-1}-{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k-\ell}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq c\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}\|S_{\ell}{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{\ell}-{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{\ell+1}\|_{\infty}.

Further, by Lemma
5.1
,

\|S_{\ell}{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{\ell}-{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{\ell+1}\|_{\infty}\leq c_{f}2^{-2k_{0}-\ell}
. Hence, it leads to the bound

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}\|S_{\ell}{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{\ell}-{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{\ell+1}\|_{\infty}\leq c_{f}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}2^{-2k_{0}-\ell}\leq c_{f}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}2^{-2k_{0}-\ell}\leq c^{\prime}_{f}2^{-2k_{0}},

which finishes the proof.
∎

Based on the above results, we shall investigate the approximation order
of the NUCC scheme. For better readability of this paper,
our proof will be done by focusing on the mask in (
6
);
the other case in (
9
) can be done similarly.
In advance to proceed further, we introduce some notation.
For each
j\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\nu=0,1
,
we use the abbreviation

j_{\nu}:=2j+\nu.

Denote by

\displaystyle\widehat{{\mathbf{f}}^{\prime\prime}}{}^{k}:=\big\{f^{\prime\prime}(2^{-k_{0}}t^{k}_{j}):j\in\mathbb{Z}\big\}

(17)

the sequence sampled from a smooth function
f^{\prime\prime}(2^{-k_{0}}\cdot)

at the
k
th level grid points
t^{k}_{j}
.
Let
\overline{t}=2^{-k_{0}}t^{k+1}_{j_{\nu}}
with
\nu=0,1

be the evaluation points
at level
k+1
between
2^{-k_{0}}t^{k}_{j}
and
2^{-k_{0}}t^{k}_{j+1}
.
Then, for a given smooth function
f
,
consider an auxiliary function
Q_{\nu}f

defined by

\displaystyle Q_{\nu}f(t)=\mathcal{L}_{0,\nu}(t)f(2^{-k_{0}}t^{k}_{j})+\mathcal{L}_{1,\nu}(t)f(2^{-k_{0}}t^{k}_{j+1})

(18)

where
\mathcal{L}_{0,\nu}
and
\mathcal{L}_{1,\nu}
are the
Lagrange functions as given in (
3
)
in the exponential space

\mathbb{E}_{2}:={\rm span}\{\exp({\hat{\gamma}}_{j_{\nu}}(\cdot-\overline{t})),\ \exp(-{\hat{\gamma}}_{j_{\nu}}(\cdot-\overline{t}))\}

(19)

satisfying

\mathcal{L}_{n,\nu}(t^{k}_{j+\ell})=\delta_{\ell,n}
for
\ell,n=0,1
.
Here, we suppose that the shape parameter is chosen as

\displaystyle{\hat{\gamma}}_{j_{\nu}}:={\hat{\gamma}}_{j_{\nu},k}:=\sqrt{\frac{\widehat{{\mathbf{f}}^{\prime\prime}}{}_{j+\nu}^{k}}{{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k}_{j+\nu}}}.

(20)

where
\widehat{{\mathbf{f}}^{\prime\prime}}{}^{k}_{j+\nu}:=f^{\prime\prime}(2^{-k_{0}}t^{k}_{j+\nu})
.
It is obvious that

\widehat{{\mathbf{f}}^{\prime\prime}}{}^{k}_{j+\nu}=f^{\prime\prime}(\overline{t})+O(2^{-k_{0}-k}).

(21)

Note that the shape parameter
\gamma_{j_{\nu}}
in the mask of
the NUCC scheme (see (
7
))
is an approximation to
{\hat{\gamma}}_{j_{\nu}}
.
The only difference is that the shape parameter (
20
)
is given in terms of the original function values.
Further, in view of Section
3
, it is obvious that
the operator
Q_{\nu}
locally reproduces the exponential polynomials
in the space
\mathbb{E}_{2}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

For each
\nu=0,1
, let
Q_{\nu}f
be defined as in (
18
)
with
f\in W^{3}_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
.
Let
\overline{t}:=2^{-k_{0}}t^{k+1}_{j_{\nu}}
and
assume that

|f(t)|\geq\tau
for
|t-\overline{t}|\leq 2^{-k_{0}-k}
with a constant
\tau>0
.
Then, we have the estimate

\displaystyle|Q_{\nu}f(2^{-k_{0}}t^{k+1}_{j_{\nu}})-f(2^{-k_{0}}t^{k+1}_{j_{\nu}})|\leq c_{f,\tau}2^{-3(k_{0}+k)}

(22)

for some constant
c_{f,\tau}>0
depending on
f
and
\tau
.

Proof.

Noting that the space
\mathbb{E}_{2}
in (
19
)
can be written as

\mathbb{E}_{2}={\rm span}\{\cosh({\hat{\gamma}}_{j_{\nu}}(\cdot-\overline{t})),\ \sinh({\hat{\gamma}}_{j_{\nu}}(\cdot-\overline{t}))\}
,
we define a function
G_{f}
by

\displaystyle G_{f}=f(\overline{t})\cosh({\hat{\gamma}}_{j_{\nu}}(\cdot-\overline{t}))+f^{\prime}(\overline{t})\frac{\sinh({\hat{\gamma}}_{j_{\nu}}(\cdot-\overline{t}))}{{\hat{\gamma}}_{j_{\nu}}}.

(23)

Clearly,
G_{f}
is in the space
\mathbb{E}_{2}
and
approximates
f
in the sense that

G_{f}(\overline{t})=f(\overline{t})
and
G_{f}^{\prime}(\overline{t})=f^{\prime}(\overline{t})
.
Moreover,

G_{f}^{\prime\prime}(\overline{t})={\hat{\gamma}}^{2}_{j_{\nu}}f(\overline{t})=\frac{\widehat{{\mathbf{f}}^{\prime\prime}}{}^{k}_{j+\nu}}{{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k}_{j+\nu}}f(\overline{t}).

Then, taking the Taylor expansion of
f-G_{f}
around
\overline{t}
,
it follows that

\displaystyle f(t)-G_{f}(t)=\Big(f^{\prime\prime}(\overline{t})-\frac{\widehat{{\mathbf{f}}^{\prime\prime}}{}^{k}_{j+\nu}}{{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k}_{j+\nu}}f(\overline{t})\Big)\frac{(t-\overline{t})^{2}}{2}+(f-G_{f})^{(3)}(\xi)\frac{(t-\overline{t})^{3}}{6}.

(24)

By (
21
),

\widehat{{\mathbf{f}}^{\prime\prime}}{}^{k}_{j+\nu}=f^{\prime\prime}(\overline{t})+O(2^{-k_{0}-k})
.
Also,

{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k}_{j+\nu}=f(\overline{t})+O(2^{-k_{0}-k})
.
Based on the definition of
{\hat{\gamma}}_{j_{\nu}}
,
some elementary calculation reveals that

|G_{f}^{(3)}(\xi)|\leq c_{f}
for some
c_{f}>0
.
Since
|f(t)|\geq\tau
for
|t-\overline{t}|\leq 2^{-k_{0}-k}
,
the identity (
24
) leads to the following bound:

\displaystyle|f(t)-G_{f}(t)|\leq c_{f,\tau}2^{-3(k+k_{0})}

(25)

with a constant
c_{f,\tau}>0
depending on
f
and
\tau
.
Now, since
G_{f}
is in the space
\mathbb{E}_{2}
,
due to the exponential reproducing property of
\mathcal{L}_{0,\nu}

and
\mathcal{L}_{0,\nu}
,
G_{f}
is represented as

G_{f}(\overline{t})=\mathcal{L}_{0,\nu}(\overline{t})G_{f}(2^{-k_{0}}t^{k}_{j})+\mathcal{L}_{1,\nu}(\overline{t})G_{f}(2^{-k_{0}}t^{k}_{j+1}).

It leads to the expression

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} Q_{\nu}f(\overline{t})-f(\overline{t})&=Q_{\nu}f(\overline{t})-G_{f}(\overline{t})\\
&=\mathcal{L}_{0,\nu}(\overline{t})(f(2^{-k_{0}}t^{k}_{j})-G_{f}(2^{-k_{0}}t^{k}_{j}))+\mathcal{L}_{1,\nu}(\overline{t})(f(2^{-k_{0}}t^{k}_{j+1})-G_{f}(2^{-k_{0}}t^{k}_{j+1})).\end{aligned}

(26)

Applying the estimate (
25
) to (
26
)
induces the required result (
22
).
∎

Using the above results,
we will show that the NUCC scheme
\{S_{k}\}
can provide
an improved order of accuracy better than the classical methods.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\{S_{k}\}
be the NUCC scheme
acting on the initial data

{\mathbf{f}}^{0}=\{f(2^{-k_{0}}(n-\frac{1}{2}):n\in\mathbb{Z}\}

with a function
f\in W^{3}_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
.
Let
{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k}=\{f(2^{-k_{0}}t^{k}_{j}):j\in\mathbb{Z}\}
as given in (
14
) and

\overline{t}:=2^{-k_{0}}t^{k+1}_{j_{\nu}}
for
\nu=0,1
.
If
|f(t)|\geq\tau
for
|t-\overline{t}|\leq 2^{-k_{0}-k}

with a constant
\tau>0
,
then we get

\displaystyle|S_{k}{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k}_{j_{\nu}}-{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k+1}_{j_{\nu}}|\leq c_{f,\tau}2^{-(3k_{0}+2k)},

(27)

where
c_{f,\tau}>0
is a constant dependent upon
f
and
\tau
.

Proof.

Using the auxiliary function
Q_{\nu}f
in (
18
),
let us write

\displaystyle|S_{k}{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k}_{j_{\nu}}-{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k+1}_{j_{\nu}}|

\displaystyle\leq|S_{k}{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k}_{j_{\nu}}-Q_{\nu}f(\overline{t})|+|Q_{\nu}f(\overline{t})-{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k+1}_{j_{\nu}}|.

(28)

Then we first consider the case
j_{\nu}=2j
, i.e.,
\nu=0
.
To investigate the first term in the right-hand side of this inequality,
we see that

\displaystyle S_{k}{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k}_{2j}-Q_{0}f(\overline{t})=\big({\mathbf{a}}^{i,k}_{0}-\mathcal{L}_{0,0}(\overline{t})\big){\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k}_{j}+\big({\mathbf{a}}^{i,k}_{-2}-\mathcal{L}_{1,0}(\overline{t})\big){\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k}_{j+1}

(29)

with the Lagrange functions
\mathcal{L}_{n,0}
in (
18
).
Recall that the mask
{\mathbf{a}}^{i,k}
of the NUCC scheme and

\mathcal{L}_{0,0}(\overline{t})
are
written in terms of the function

\frac{\sinh(\frac{3}{4}\gamma_{t})}{\sinh\gamma t}

with a suitable
\gamma
respectively.
By using the Maclaurin series expansion argument,
we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\big|\big({\mathbf{a}}^{j,k}_{0}-\mathcal{L}_{0,0}(\overline{t})\big){\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k}_{j}\big|&\leq c_{1}\Big|\Big(\frac{2^{-2k}{\mathbf{d}}^{k}_{j}}{{\mathbf{f}}^{k}_{j}}-\frac{2^{-2(k_{0}+k)}\widehat{{\mathbf{f}}^{\prime\prime}}{}^{k}_{j}}{{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k}_{j}}\Big){\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k}_{j}\Big|+c_{2}2^{-4(k_{0}+k)}\\
&=c_{1}2^{-2k}\Big|\frac{{\mathbf{d}}^{k}_{j}}{{\mathbf{f}}^{k}_{j}}{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k}_{j}-2^{-2k_{0}}\widehat{{\mathbf{f}}^{\prime\prime}}{}^{k}_{j}\Big|+c_{2}2^{-4(k_{0}+k)},\end{split}

(30)

for some constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
.
Here,
{\mathbf{d}}^{k}=S^{k}_{{\mathbf{a}}}\Delta{\mathbf{f}}^{0}
and
the data
{\mathbf{f}}^{0}
has the density
2^{-k_{0}}
. So,
obviously,

S^{k}_{{\mathbf{a}}}\Delta{\mathbf{f}}^{0}_{j}=2^{-2k_{0}}f^{\prime\prime}(2^{-k_{0}}t^{k+1}_{2j})+O(2^{-3k_{0}}).

Also, by (
21
),

\widehat{{\mathbf{f}}^{\prime\prime}}{}^{k}_{j}=f^{\prime\prime}(2^{-k_{0}}t^{k+1}_{2j})+O(2^{-k_{0}})
.
Moreover, by Lemma
5.2
,

{\mathbf{f}}^{k}_{j}={\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k}_{j}+O(2^{-2k_{0}})
.
Thus
a direct computation from (
30
) yields

\displaystyle\big|\big({\mathbf{a}}^{i,k}_{0}-\mathcal{L}_{0,0}(\overline{t})\big){\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k}_{j}\big|\leq c_{f,\tau}2^{-3k_{0}-2k}

for a constant
c_{f,\tau}>0
depending on
f
and
\tau
.
In a similar way, we can get

\big|\big({\mathbf{a}}^{i,k}_{-2}-\mathcal{L}_{1,0}(\overline{t})\big){\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k}_{j}\big|\leq c_{f,\tau}2^{-3k_{0}-2k}

which estimates
the second term in the right-hand side of (
29
).
It concludes that

\displaystyle|S_{k}{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k}_{j}-Q_{0}f(\overline{t})|\leq c2^{-3k_{0}-2k}.

(31)

Due to Lemma
5.3
, the last term in (
28
)
satisfies

|Q_{0}f(\overline{t})-{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k+1}_{2j}|\leq c_{f,\tau}2^{-3(k_{0}+k)}
.
This together with the bound (
31
) implies the estimate (
27
).
The proof for the case
j_{\nu}=2j+1
can be done similarly.
So, we finish the proof.
∎

We are now ready to prove our main result.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
\{S_{k}\}
be the NUCC scheme
acting on the initial data of the form

{\mathbf{f}}^{0}=\{f(2^{-k_{0}}(n-\frac{1}{2}):n\in\mathbb{Z}\}

with a function
f\in W^{3}_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
.
Put

\overline{t}=2^{-k_{0}}t^{k+1}_{j}
be a dyadic point.
Then if
|f(t)|\geq\tau
with
\tau>0
for
|t-\overline{t}|\leq 2^{-k_{0}-k}
,
then the limit function

{\mathbf{f}}^{\infty}=\lim_{k\to\infty}S_{k}\cdots S_{0}{\mathbf{f}}^{0}

satisfies the estimate

\displaystyle|{\mathbf{f}}^{\infty}(\overline{t})-f(\overline{t})|\leq c_{f,\tau}2^{-3k_{0}}

(32)

for a constant
c_{f,\tau}>0
dependent upon
f
and

\tau
but independent of
k_{0}
.

Proof.

Using the telescoping sum (as in the proof of Lemma
5.2
)
and applying Lemma
5.4
,
we can estimate the error between
{\mathbf{f}}^{k+1}
and
{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k+1}
as

\displaystyle\|{\mathbf{f}}^{k+1}-{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{k+1}\|_{\infty}\leq c\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\|S_{\ell}{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{\ell}-{\hat{\mathbf{f}}}^{\ell+1}\|_{\infty}\leq c_{f,\tau}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}2^{-(3k_{0}+2\ell)}\leq c_{f,\tau}2^{-3k_{0}}

for a constant
c_{f,\tau}>0
depending on
f
and

\tau
.
Since this relation holds for any dyadic point
t^{k+1}_{j}
,
we can obtain the desired result.
∎

Corollary 5.6
.

Let the initial data be of the form
{\mathbf{f}}^{0}=\{f(2^{-k_{0}}t^{0}_{n}):n\in\mathbb{Z}\}

with
f\in W^{3}_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
.
Assume that

\|{\mathbf{f}}^{0}\|_{\infty}\geq\tau
for some
\tau>0
.
Under the same conditions of Theorem
5.5
, we have

\displaystyle\|{\mathbf{f}}^{\infty}-f\|_{\infty}\leq c_{f,\tau}2^{-3k_{0}}.

The following theorem treats the local approximation order of the
NUCC scheme for the (alternative) case that is suggested in (
9
).
It can be proved in a similar fashion to the case of Theorem
5.5
.

.

Let
f\in W^{3}_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and put
\overline{t}=2^{-k_{0}}t^{k+1}_{j}

be a dyadic point. Assume that
|f^{\prime}(t)|\geq\tau
with
\tau>0

for
|t-\overline{t}|\leq 2^{-k_{0}-k}
. Then, under the same conditions
and assumptions of Theorem
5.5
, we have

\displaystyle|{\mathbf{f}}^{\infty}(\overline{t})-f(\overline{t})|\leq c_{f,\tau}2^{-3k_{0}}

(33)

for a constant
c_{f,\tau}>0
.

Proof.

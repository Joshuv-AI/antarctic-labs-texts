Families over the integral Bernstein Center and Tate cohomology of local Base change lifts for GL n ​ ( F ) {\rm GL}_{n}(F)
Sabyasachi Dhar and Santosh Nadimpalli

Abstract
. Let p p and l l be distinct odd primes, and let F F be a p p -adic field. Let π \pi be a generic smooth integral representation of GL n ​ ( F ) {\rm GL}_{n}(F) over an ℚ ¯ l \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{l} -vector space. Let E E be a finite Galois extension of F F with [ E : F ] = l [E:F]=l . Let Π \Pi be the base change lift of π \pi to the group GL n ​ ( E ) {\rm GL}_{n}(E) . Let 𝕎 0 ​ ( Π , ψ E ) \mathbb{W}^{0}(\Pi,\psi_{E}) be the lattice of ℤ ¯ l \overline{\mathbb{Z}}_{l} -valued functions in the Whittaker model of Π \Pi , with respect to a standard Gal ⁡ ( E / F ) {\rm Gal}(E/F) -equivaraint additive character ψ E : E → ℚ ¯ l × \psi_{E}:E\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}^{\times} . We show that the unique generic sub-quotient of the zero-th Tate cohomology group of 𝕎 0 ​ ( Π , ψ E ) \mathbb{W}^{0}(\Pi,\psi_{E}) is isomorphic to the Frobenius twist of the unique generic sub-quotient of the mod- l l reduction of π \pi . We first prove a version of this result for a family of smooth generic representations of GL n ​ ( E ) {\rm GL}_{n}(E) over the integral Bernstein center of GL n ​ ( F ) {\rm GL}_{n}(F) . Our methods use the theory of Rankin-selberg convolutions and simple identities of local γ \gamma -factors. The results of this article remove the hypothesis that l l does not divide the pro-order of GL n − 1 ​ ( F ) {\rm GL}_{n-1}(F) in our previous work [ DN22 ] .

Introduction

p

\langle\sigma\rangle

lifting. We use local Rankin-Selberg zeta functions and their functional equations to study the Tate cohomology and linkage
principle. Our approach is in the

[
DN22
]

Integral Bernstein Center and Base change

{\rm GL}_{n}

[

]

]

2.1.

be a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}

2.3.

Helm in his foundational work
[

described integral
Bernstein center in terms of local Galois deformation rings. He

cuspidal support maps of base change lifting. For the cyclic extension

An

(M,\sigma)\leftrightsquigarrow\overline{\rho}\ (\text{mod}-l\ \text{LLC})

B_{E/F}:R_{\nu}^{E}\rightarrow R_{\nu}^{F}.

3.
Rankin–Selberg convolution in families

In this section, we review the theory of Rankin-Selberg convolution over arbitrary
Noetherian
\Lambda
-algebras. We start by recalling the notion of
co-Whittaker modules. For a precise reference, see

[
HM18
]
,
[
Mos16a
]
.

3.1.
Co-Whittaker modules

Fix a non-trivial additive character

\psi_{K}:K\rightarrow\Lambda^{\times}
. For any Noetherian

\Lambda
-algebra
R
, we denote by
\psi_{K,R}
the composition

K\xrightarrow{\psi_{K}}\Lambda^{\times}\rightarrow R^{\times}.

Let
N_{n}(K)
be the group of all unipotent upper triangular matrices
in
G_{n}(K)
. The character
\psi_{K,R}:K\rightarrow R^{\times}
induces
a character of
N_{n}(K)
, defined as

(x_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}\longmapsto\psi_{K,R}(x_{12}+x_{23}+\cdots x_{n-1,n}),

which is denoted by
\Psi_{K,R}
. Let
(\pi,V)
be a smooth

R[G_{n}(K)]
-module. We denote by
V^{(n)}
the space of

\Psi_{K,R}
-coinvariants, which is defined as the quotient of
V

modulo the
R
-submodule generated by the set

\Big\{\pi(x)v-(\Psi_{K,R})(x)v:x\in N_{n}(K),v\in V\Big\}
. The

R
-module
V^{(n)}
is the
n
-th Bernstein-Zelevinski derivative of

V
.

3.1.1.

A smooth admissible
R[G_{n}(K)]
-module
(\pi,V)
is called
co-Whittaker
if

(1)

the
n
-th derivative
V^{(n)}
is a free
R
-module
of rank one,

(2)

for any quotient
W
of
V
with
W^{(n)}=0
, we have
W=0
.

A co-Whittaker module
(\pi,V)
admits a central character denoted
by
\varpi_{\pi}:F^{\times}\rightarrow R^{\times}
. By definition, the
representation
(\pi,V)
admits an
R
-module isomorphism

V^{(n)}\simeq R
. By Frobenius reciprocity, this isomorphism
induces a
G_{n}(K)
-equivariant homomorphism

V\longrightarrow{\rm Ind}_{N_{n}(K)}^{G_{n}(K)}(\Psi_{K,R}).

The image of
V
under the above map is called the
Whittaker

space of
\pi
. It is denoted by
\mathbb{W}(\pi,\psi_{K,R})
and is
independent of the choice of isomorphism
V^{(n)}\simeq R
. The
identification
V^{(n)}\simeq R
, also induces a
P_{n}(K)
-equivariant
map

V\longrightarrow{\rm Ind}_{N_{n}(K)}^{P_{n}(K)}(\Psi_{K,R}),

the image of the above map is called
Kirillov
space of
\pi
,
and it is denoted by
\mathbb{K}(\pi,\psi_{K,R})
. The map

W\mapsto{\rm res}_{P_{n}(K)}(W)
gives an isomorphism

\mathbb{W}(\pi,\psi_{K,R})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{K}(\pi,\psi_{K,R})

(
[
MM22
, Section 4]
). Moreover, the
space
\mathbb{K}(\pi,\psi_{K,R})
contains

{\rm ind}_{N_{n}(K)}^{P_{n}(K)}(\Psi_{K,R})
as
R[P_{n}(K)]
-submodule.

3.1.2.

For a co-Whittaker
R[G_{n}(K)]
-module
(\pi,V)
, the endomorphism ring

{\rm End}_{R[G_{n}(K)]}(V)
is equal to
R
, and the action of the
integral Bernstein center
\mathcal{Z}_{n}^{K}
on
V
induces a map

f_{\pi}:\mathcal{Z}_{n}^{K}\rightarrow R
. The map
f_{\pi}
is called the

supercuspidal support
of
\pi
. The supercuspidal supports of
two co-Whittaker
R[G_{n}(K)]
-modules are the same if and only if they
have the same Whittaker spaces (see
[
LM20
, Lemma
2.4]
). Moreover, if we have two co-Whittaker

R[G_{n}(K)]
-modules
(\pi_{1},V_{1})
and
(\pi_{2},V_{2})
with a

G_{n}(K)
-equivariant surjection
V_{1}\rightarrow V_{2}
, then

f_{\pi_{1}}=f_{\pi_{2}}
.

3.1.3.

Let
W_{n}^{K}
be the smooth
\Lambda[G_{n}(K)]
-module

{\rm ind}_{N_{n}(K)}^{G_{n}(K)}(\Psi_{K}^{-1})
. For a primitive idempotent

e
in the integral Bernstein center
\mathcal{Z}_{n}^{K}
, the space

eW_{n}^{K}
is a co-Whittaker
e\mathcal{Z}_{n}^{K}[G_{n}(K)]
-module. The
following result is due to
[
Hel16b
, Theorem 6.3]
,
which specify the universal property of
eW_{n}^{K}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian
\Lambda
-algebra, equipped with a

e\mathcal{Z}_{n}^{K}
-algebra structure. Then

eW_{n}^{K}\otimes_{e\mathcal{Z}_{n}^{K}}R
is a co-Whittaker

R[G_{n}(K)]
-module. Conversely, if
(\pi,V)
is a co-Whittaker

R[G_{n}(K)]
-module in the category
\mathcal{M}_{e}(G_{n}(K))
, then
there exists a surjection

\beta:eW_{n}^{K}\otimes_{e\mathcal{Z}_{n}^{K}}R\rightarrow V
such that
the induced map

\beta^{(n)}:(eW_{n}^{K}\otimes_{e\mathcal{Z}_{n}^{K}}R)^{(n)}\rightarrow V^{(n)}
is an isomorphism.

We end this subsection with a version of key vanishing result, usually
known as completeness of Whittaker models, provided by G.Moss
(
[
Mos21
, Corollary 4.3]
). This vanishing
result will be crucially used in proving the main results of this
article.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
R
be a
\Lambda
-algebra, and let

be an element of

{\rm ind}_{N_{n}(K)}^{G_{n}(K)}(\Psi_{K,R})

for all

idempotents

In this section, we study the compatibility of Tate cohomology with

4.1.

Let

be a finite Galois extension of a
p
-adic field

with

, where

and
p
are distinct primes. We fix a generator

\gamma
of the cyclic group
{\rm Gal}(E/F)

\Lambda
-algebra

and any smooth
R[G_{E}]
-module

the smooth

-module, where the underlying set is

but the
G_{E}
-action on

is twisted by

the functor

is exact and covariant.

4.2.
Galois invariance

Let
e_{\mathfrak{s}}
be a primitive idempotent of
\mathcal{Z}_{n}^{F}
,
and let
\nu
be the
l
-inertial type corresponding to

e_{\mathfrak{s}}
. Then there exists a primitive idempotent

of
\mathcal{Z}_{n}^{E}

z_{E/F}:e_{\mathfrak{r}}\mathcal{Z}_{n}^{E}\rightarrow e_{\mathfrak{s}}\mathcal{Z}_{n}^{F}
and

B_{E/F}:R_{\nu}^{E}\rightarrow R_{\nu}^{F}
, as defined in the subsection

\mathbb{L}_{\nu}^{F}\circ z_{E/F}=B_{E/F}\circ\mathbb{L}_{\nu}^{E}.

(4.1)

We denote by

and
A_{F}
the Noetherian
\Lambda
-algebras

e_{\mathfrak{r}}\mathcal{Z}_{n}^{E}
and

e_{\mathfrak{s}}\mathcal{Z}_{n}^{E}
respectively. Note that the ring

is considered as an
A_{E}
-module via the map

.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be the co-Whittaker
A_{F}[G_{n}(E)]
-module

e_{\mathfrak{r}}W_{n}^{E}\otimes_{A_{E}}A_{F}
. Then we have

\mathbb{W}(\mathcal{V},\psi_{E,A_{F}})=\mathbb{W}(\mathcal{V}^{\gamma},\psi_{E,A_{F}}).

Proof.

We use the local converse therorem (
[
LM20
, Theorem
1.1]
) for co-Whittaker modules.
It is enough to show that

\gamma(X,\mathcal{V},\tau,\psi_{E})=\gamma(X,\mathcal{V}^{\gamma},\tau,\psi_{E}),

for
\tau
varying over all irreducible generic integral

\overline{\mathcal{K}}
-representation of
G_{t}(E)
, for

1\leq t\leq[\frac{n}{2}]
. For a specialization

, the

(e_{\mathfrak{r}}W_{n}^{E}\otimes_{A_{E}}A_{F})\otimes_{x}\overline{\mathcal{K}}
, denoted by

, is the base change lift
of a smooth representation
\pi_{x}
of
G_{n}(F)

\Pi_{x}^{\gamma}\simeq\Pi_{x}
, for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Since

x(\gamma(X,\mathcal{V},\tau,\psi_{E}))=\gamma(X,\Pi_{x},\tau,\psi_{E}),

and

x(\gamma(X,\mathcal{V}^{\gamma},\tau,\psi_{E}))=\gamma(X,\Pi_{x}^{\gamma},\tau,\psi_{E}),

we have

x(\gamma(X,\mathcal{V},\tau,\psi_{E}))=x(\gamma(X,\mathcal{V}^{\gamma},\tau,\psi_{E})),

for all

\overline{\mathcal{K}}
-points of
A_{F}
. The lemma now follows since

A_{F}
is reduced and flat over
\Lambda
.
∎

From these, we deduce:

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be the co-Whittaker module

e_{\mathfrak{r}}W_{n}^{E}\otimes_{A_{E}}A_{F}
. Then the Whittaker space

\mathbb{W}(\mathcal{V},\psi_{E,A_{F}})
is invariant under the
action of
{\rm Gal}(E/F)
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{V}
is co-Whittaker, we have the
A_{F}
-module
isomorphism
\mathcal{V}^{(n)}\simeq A_{F}
. Precomposing this
isomorphism with the quotient map

\mathcal{V}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}^{(n)}
induces the
A_{F}
-linear
map
\mathcal{W}:\mathcal{V}\rightarrow A_{F}
with

\mathcal{W}(n.v)=\Psi_{E,A_{F}}(n)\mathcal{W}(v),

for all

and

. Moreover, for any

\gamma\in{\rm Gal}(E/F)
, we have

\mathcal{W}(\gamma(n).v)=\Psi_{E,A_{F}}(\gamma(n))\mathcal{W}(v)=\Psi_{E,A_{F}}(n)\mathcal{W}(v).

Therefore, the Whittaker spaces of
\mathcal{V}
and

\mathcal{V}^{\gamma}
with respect to the character
\psi_{E}
are
induced by the same linear map
\mathcal{W}
. Let
W_{v}
be an element
of
\mathbb{W}(\mathcal{V},\psi_{E,A_{F}})

(\gamma^{-1}.W_{v})(g)=\mathcal{W}(\gamma(g).v).

This shows that

\gamma^{-1}.W_{v}\in\mathbb{W}(\mathcal{V}^{\gamma},\psi_{E,A_{F}})
. But

\mathbb{W}(\mathcal{V}^{\gamma},\psi_{E,A_{F}})=\mathbb{W}(\mathcal{V},\psi_{E,A_{F}})
, by Lemma
4.1
. This
completes the proof.
∎

We now prove the following theorem.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be the co-Whittaker

-module

e_{\mathfrak{r}}W_{n}^{E}\otimes_{A_{E}}A_{F}

\mathcal{V}_{F}

\mathbb{F}

\mathcal{M}\longrightarrow\mathbb{W}(\mathcal{V}_{F}^{(l)},\psi_{F,R}^{l}).

Proof.

We prove the above theorem using induction on the integer

did in Theorem
4.3
. First, note that

\gamma(X,\pi_{E},\mathcal{V}^{\prime},\psi_{E})=(f_{\pi_{E}}\otimes z_{E/F}^{\prime})(\gamma(X,e_{\mathfrak{r}}W_{n}^{E},e_{\mathfrak{r}^{\prime}}W_{n-1}^{E},\psi_{E})).

Using the relations (
4.1
) and (
4.3
)
and the fact that
f_{\pi_{E}}=f_{\pi_{F}}\circ z_{E/F}
, we get the
following equality:

\gamma(X,\pi_{E},\mathcal{V}^{\prime},\psi_{E})=\prod_{\eta}\gamma(X,\pi_{F},e_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}W_{n-1}^{F}\otimes\eta,\psi_{F}),

where
\eta
runs over the characters of
{\rm Gal}(E/F)
. Applying
the morphism
(r_{\ell}\otimes\theta_{\ell}^{\prime})
to the above identity, we
get

(r_{\ell}\otimes\theta_{\ell}^{\prime})(\gamma(X,\pi_{E},\mathcal{V}^{\prime},\psi_{E}))=\gamma(X,J_{\ell}(\pi_{F}),\mathcal{V}_{F}^{\prime},\psi_{F})^{l}.

Finally, the identity (
3.3
)
gives

(r_{\ell}\otimes\theta_{\ell}^{\prime})(\gamma(X,\pi_{E},\mathcal{V}^{\prime},\psi_{E}))=\gamma(X^{l},J_{\ell}(\pi_{F})^{(l)},\mathcal{V}_{F}^{\prime(l)},\psi_{F}^{l}).

Thus, the space
\mathcal{N}(\psi_{F})
is stable under the action of

G_{n}(F)
and the map
\Phi_{n}
is surjective. Now, using

[
DN22
, Proposition 6.3]
, we get that there is a unique
generic subquotient of

\widehat{H}^{0}(\mathbb{W}(\pi_{E},\psi_{E,\overline{\mathcal{K}}})
,
and this is necessarily equal to
J_{\ell}(\pi_{F})^{(l)}
. This
completes the proof.

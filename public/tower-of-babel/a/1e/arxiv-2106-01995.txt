Abstract
. The Lagrange problem is established in the discrete field theory subject to constraints with values in a Lie group. For the admissible sections that satisfy a certain regularity condition, we prove that the critical sections of such problems are the solutions of a canonically unconstrained variational problem associated with the Lagrange problem (discrete Lagrange multiplier rule). This variational problem has a discrete Cartan 1-form, from which a Noether theory of symmetries and a multisymplectic form formula are established. The whole theory is applied to the Euler-Poincaré reduction in the discrete field theory, concluding as an illustration with the remarkable example of the harmonic maps of the discrete plane in the Lie group S ​ O ​ ( n ) SO(n) .

2.
Discrete Lagrange problems with constraints valued in a Lie group

The data that define this type of problems are: a cellular complex, a fiber bundle over the set of vertices, and the jet extensions, a Lagrangian density, and a constraint map. Following the notation of
[
5
]
(sections 2 and 3), we describe briefly these objects next.

•

For an
n
-dimensional abstract cellular complex
V
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
V_{k}=\{\alpha\in V|\dim\alpha=k\}
the set of the
k
-dimensional cells,
k\in\mathbb{N}
. In particular, the elements of
V_{0}
are called vertices and the elements of
V_{n}
are called faces.

We said that a cell
\alpha\in V_{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, is
adherent
to another cell
\beta\in V_{k+l}
,
l\in\mathbb{N}
, denoted by
\alpha\prec\beta
, if
\alpha=\beta
or there exists a sequence of cells
\alpha=\gamma_{k},\gamma_{k+1},\dots,\gamma_{k+l}=\beta
, where
\gamma_{k+i}\in V_{k+i},i=0,\dots,l
, such that each one is incident to the following.

The notion of proximity is given by the
spherical neighborhood
of a vertex
v\in V_{0}
,
S_{v}=\{\alpha\in V_{n}|v\prec\alpha\}
, that is, the set of the different faces that have
v
as adherent vertex.

Given a vertex
v\in V_{0}
and a subset of faces
\mathcal{V}\subset V_{n}
, we say that
v
is
interior
to
\mathcal{V}
if
S_{v}\subset\mathcal{V}
, is exterior to
\mathcal{V}
if
S_{v}\subset V_{n}\setminus\mathcal{V}
or is
frontier
of
\mathcal{V}
otherwise. We denote by
\operatorname{int}\mathcal{V}
and
\operatorname{fr}\mathcal{V}
the set of interior and frontier vertices of
\mathcal{V}
, respectively.

•

Consider a fiber bundle
\pi:Y\to V_{0}
over the set of vertices of an
n
-dimensional cellular complex
V
. That is,
Y
is a differential manifold, and for any
v\in V_{0}
the fiber
Y_{v}=\pi^{-1}(v)
is a differentiable manifold of dimension
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

•

The role of the
bundle of 1-jets
in the continuous case is given in the discrete theory of fields by the bundle
j^{1}\pi:J^{1}Y\to V_{n}
over the set of faces of the cellular complex
V
. For that, given
\alpha\in V_{n}
consider

(J^{1}Y)_{\alpha}=(j^{1}\pi)^{-1}(\alpha)=\prod_{v\prec\alpha}Y_{v}\quad\textrm{ and }\quad J^{1}Y=\bigsqcup_{\alpha\in V_{n}}(J^{1}Y)_{\alpha}.

For a section
y\in\Gamma(V_{0},Y)
, the 1-jet extension
j^{1}y\in\Gamma(V_{n},J^{1}Y)
is defined at
\alpha\in V_{n}
by
(j^{1}y)(\alpha)=(y(v))
, where

v\prec\alpha
.

In this context, the
2-jet bundle
extension of
\pi:Y\to V_{0}
is the bundle
j^{2}\pi:J^{2}Y\to V_{0}
over the vertices of
V
such that the fiber over a
v\in V_{0}
is:

(J^{2}Y)_{v}=(j^{2}\pi)^{-1}(v)=\prod_{v^{\prime}\prec\alpha\in S_{v}}Y_{v^{\prime}},

where
S_{v}\subset V_{n}
is the spherical neighborhood of
v
. That is, the fiber over the vertex
v
is the product of the fibers of
\pi:Y\to V_{0}
over each vertex of each face that has
v
as adherent vertex. If
y\in\Gamma(V_{0},Y)
is a section, the 2-jet extension of
y
is
j^{2}y\in\Gamma(V_{0},J^{2}Y)
defined by
(j^{2}y)(v)=(y(v^{\prime}))
,
v^{\prime}\prec\alpha\in S_{v}
for each vertex
v\in V_{0}
.

Note that there exists a canonical projection between these jet extensions. More precisely, for any vertex
v\in V_{0}
and any face
\alpha\in S_{v}
, given
(y_{v^{\prime}})\in(J^{2}Y)_{v}
, where
v^{\prime}\prec\beta\in S_{v}
, we can collect the values over the vertices that are adherent precisely to the face
\alpha
(and ignore the others). So we can define
\pi_{v\alpha}:(J^{2}Y)_{v}\to(J^{1}Y)_{\alpha}
given by

\pi_{v\alpha}\big(\underbrace{(y_{v^{\prime}})}_{v^{\prime}\prec\beta\in S_{v}}\big)=\big(\underbrace{(y_{v^{\prime}})}_{v^{\prime}\prec\alpha}\big).

•

A
discrete Lagrangian density

\mathcal{L}:J^{1}Y\to\mathbb{R}
over the bundle
j^{1}\pi:J^{1}Y\to V_{n}
is a family of smooth functions
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}:(J^{1}Y)_{\alpha}\to\mathbb{R}
where
\alpha\in V_{n}
.

•

Finally, a
constraint with values in a Lie group

G
is a map
\Phi:J^{1}Y\to G
such that, for all
\alpha\in V_{n}
,
\Phi_{\alpha}=\Phi|_{(J^{1}Y)_{\alpha}}
is a differential map being
e\in G
(the identity element of the group) a regular value. Thus,
S_{\alpha}=\Phi_{\alpha}^{-1}(e)
is a submanifold of
(J^{1}Y)_{\alpha}
, and we can express
S=\Phi^{-1}(e)
as the disjoint union
S=\sqcup_{\alpha\in V_{n}}S_{\alpha}
. We denote the tangent map of
\Phi_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in V_{n}
, at
j^{1}_{\alpha}y\in(J^{1}Y)_{\alpha}
by
(d\Phi_{\alpha})_{j^{1}_{\alpha}y}
.

Now, let
\mathcal{V}
be a finite set of faces and
\mathcal{V}_{0}=\{v\in V_{0}\,|\,v\prec\alpha\in\mathcal{V}\}
the (finite) set of its adherent vertices. Then, similar to those set out in
[
5
]
, we may give the following definitions.

Definition 2.1
.

A section
y\in\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)
is said to be admissible if
\textrm{img}(j^{1}y)\subset S
, that is,
(j^{1}y)(\alpha)\in S_{\alpha}\subset(J^{1}Y)_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha\in\mathcal{V}
.

We shall denote this subset of section by
\Gamma_{S}(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)
.

Definition 2.2
.

Given an admissible section
y\in\Gamma_{S}(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)
, an admissible infinitesimal variation of
y
is a tangent vector
\delta y\in T_{y}\big(\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\big)
whose 1-jet extension
j^{1}\delta y
is tangent to
S\subset J^{1}Y
along
\textrm{img}(j^{1}y)\subset S
, that is,
(d\Phi_{\alpha})_{(j^{1}y)(\alpha)}(j^{1}\delta y)(\alpha)=0
for all
\alpha\in\mathcal{V}
.

We shall denote by
T_{y}\big(\Gamma_{S}(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\big)
this subspace of infinitesimal variations, and by
T_{y}^{c}\big(\Gamma_{S}(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\big)
the subspace of those that vanish at the frontier of
\mathcal{V}
.

Definition 2.3
.

An admissible section
y\in\Gamma_{S}(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)
is critical with fixed boundary for the discrete Lagrange problem with Lagrangian density
\mathcal{L}:J^{1}Y\to\mathbb{R}
and constraint submanifold
S=\Phi^{-1}(e)\subset J^{1}Y
if
\big(d\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{L})\big)_{y}=0
on the subspace
T_{y}^{c}\big(\Gamma_{S}(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\big)
, where
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{L})
is the action functional

\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{L}):y\in\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\mapsto\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{V}}\mathcal{L}\big((j^{1}y)(\alpha)\big).

As in
[
5
]
, under a certain condition of regularity such critical sections can be characterized as the solutions of an Euler-Lagrange operator by proceeding as follows.

From an initial section
y_{0}\in\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)
, let
\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\big(y_{0}(\operatorname{fr}\mathcal{V})\big)
be the submanifold of
\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)
defined by the sections with fixed boundary
y_{0}(\operatorname{fr}\mathcal{V})\subset Y
and
\Gamma_{S}(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\big(y_{0}(\operatorname{fr}\mathcal{V})\big)
the subset of the admissible ones.

We define the mapping
\Psi:\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\to\textrm{Map}(\mathcal{V},G)
by the rule:

(1)

\Psi(y):\alpha\in\mathcal{V}\mapsto\Phi_{\alpha}\big((j^{1}y)(\alpha)\big),\quad y\in\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y).

Since
\Phi_{\alpha}:(J^{1}Y)_{\alpha}\to G
is differentiable so is
\Psi
, expressing its differential
(d\Psi)_{y}:T_{y}\big(\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\big)\to T_{\Psi(y)}\textrm{Map}(\mathcal{V},G)
at
y\in\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)
as:

(2)

(d\Psi)_{y}(\delta y):\alpha\in\mathcal{V}\mapsto(d\Phi_{\alpha})_{(j^{1}y)(\alpha)}\big((j^{1}\delta y)(\alpha)\big),\quad\delta y\in T_{y}\big(\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\big).

Otherwise, taking into account the isomorphism
T_{\Psi(y)}\textrm{Map}(\mathcal{V},G)\approx\textrm{Map}(\mathcal{V},\mathfrak{g})
induced by the left translations of the elements
g\in G
,
D_{g}\in T_{g}G\mapsto L_{g^{-1}}D_{g}\in T_{e}G=\mathfrak{g}
, the map
(d\Psi)_{y}
can also be expressed as:

(3)

(d\Psi)_{y}(\delta y):\alpha\in\mathcal{V}\mapsto\big(\theta\circ(d\Phi_{\alpha})_{(j^{1}y)(\alpha)}\big)\big((j^{1}\delta y)(\alpha)\big),\quad\delta y\in T_{y}\big(\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\big),

where
\theta
is the Maurer-Cartan 1-form of the group
\theta:g\in G\mapsto\theta_{g}
(
\theta_{g}:D_{g}\in T_{g}G\mapsto L_{g^{-1}}D_{g}\in T_{e}G=\mathfrak{g}
) and where
\circ
is the composition of maps.

In particular, for the admissible sections
y\in\Gamma_{S}(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)
both expressions (
2
) and (
3
) of
(d\Psi)_{y}
coincide because in this case
\Phi_{\alpha}(j^{1}y)=e
,
\alpha\in\mathcal{V}
.

By restricting
\Psi
to the submanifold
\Gamma(V_{0},Y)\big(y_{0}(\operatorname{fr}\mathcal{V})\big)\subset\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)
and
(d\Psi)_{y}
to the subspace
T_{y}\big(\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\big(y_{0}(\operatorname{fr}\mathcal{V})\big)\big)\subset T_{y}\big(\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\big)
we get:

Proposition 2.4
.

\Gamma_{S}(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\big(y_{0}(\operatorname{fr}\mathcal{V})\big)=\Psi^{-1}(e),\quad T_{y}^{c}\big(\Gamma_{S}(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\big)=\ker(d\Psi)_{y}.

Proof.

It is enough to apply definitions
2.1
and
2.2
, and formulas (
1
) and (
2
)
\equiv
(
3
) because
y
is admissible.
∎

Now, a key point of our approach is the following condition of regularity for admissible sections:

Definition 2.5
.

An admissible section
y\in\Gamma_{S}(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\big(y_{0}(\operatorname{fr}\mathcal{V})\big)
is said to be regular if
(d\Psi)_{y}:T_{y}\big(\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\big(y_{0}(\operatorname{fr}\mathcal{V})\big)\big)\to\textrm{Map}(\mathcal{V},\mathfrak{g})
is onto.

Under this hypothesis, the Inverse Function Theorem proves the existence in the manifold
\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\big(y_{0}(\operatorname{fr}\mathcal{V})\big)
of an open neighborhood
\mathcal{U}(y)
of
y
such that
\Gamma_{S}(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\big(y_{0}(\operatorname{fr}\mathcal{V})\big)\cap\mathcal{U}(y)
is a submanifold of
\mathcal{U}(y)
whose tangent space at
y
is
\ker(d\Psi)_{y}=T_{y}^{c}\big(\Gamma_{S}(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\big)
. Therefore, Definition
2.3
of stationarity is equivalent to stating that
y
is a critical point of the restriction of the action functional
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{L})
to the submanifold
\Gamma_{S}(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\big(y_{0}(\operatorname{fr}\mathcal{V})\big)\cap\mathcal{U}(y)
. On the other hand, we have the exact sequence:

(4)

0\to T_{y}^{c}\big(\Gamma_{S}(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\big)\to T_{y}\Big(\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\big(y_{0}(\operatorname{fr}\mathcal{V})\big)\Big)\xrightarrow{(d\Psi)_{y}}\textrm{Map}(\mathcal{V},\mathfrak{g})\to 0,

which, finally, will allow us to characterize critical regular sections as solutions of an Euler-Poincaré operator.

Indeed, defining the notion of Cartan 1-form associated to the constraint morphism
\Phi:J^{1}Y\to G
as the family of 1-forms
\Theta_{\alpha}^{v}(\Phi)
on
J^{1}Y
with values in Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
,
\alpha\in\mathcal{V}
,
v\in\mathcal{V}_{0}
,
v\prec\alpha
, such that

(5)

\theta\circ(d\Phi_{\alpha})_{j^{1}_{\alpha}y}=\sum_{v\prec\alpha}\big(\Theta^{v}_{\alpha}(\Phi)\big)_{j^{1}_{\alpha}y},\quad j^{1}_{\alpha}y\in(J^{1}Y)_{\alpha},

the mapping
(d\Psi)_{y}
of (
4
) can be expressed as:

(6)

(d\Psi)_{y}(\delta y):\alpha\in\mathcal{V}\mapsto\sum_{v\prec\alpha}\big(\Theta_{\alpha}^{v}(\Phi)\big)_{(j^{1}y)(\alpha)}(j^{1}\delta y)(\alpha),\quad\delta y\in T_{y}\Big(\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\big(y_{0}(\operatorname{fr}\mathcal{V})\big)\Big),

from where we can obtain the following characterization of the critical sections.

Theorem 2.6
.

A regular admissible section
y\in\Gamma_{S}(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\big(y_{0}(\operatorname{fr}\mathcal{V})\big)
is critical with fixed boundary for the Lagrange problem with Lagrangian density
\mathcal{L}:J^{1}Y\to\mathbb{R}
and constraint submanifold
S=\Phi^{-1}(e)\subset J^{1}Y
if and only if there exists a mapping
\lambda\in\textrm{Map}(\mathcal{V},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
such that for any
v\in\operatorname{int}\mathcal{V}
:

(7)

\Big(\mathcal{E}_{v}(\mathcal{L})+\sum_{\alpha\in S_{v}}\pi^{*}_{v\alpha}\big(\lambda(\alpha)\circ\Theta_{\alpha}^{v}(\Phi)\big)\Big)_{(j^{2}y)(v)}=0,

where
\mathcal{E}_{v}(\mathcal{L})
is the Euler-Lagrange 1-form of
\mathcal{L}
at
v
as unconstrained variational problem,
S_{v}
is the spherical neighborhood of
v
,
\circ
is the bilinear product of the duality between
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, and
\pi_{v\alpha}
is the canonical projection from
(J^{2}Y)_{v}
to
(J^{1}Y)_{\alpha}
. This mapping
\lambda
is unique and we shall call the
multiplier associated
to the critical regular section
y
.

Proof.

It is enough to repeat mutatis mutandis the proof of the Theorem 4.6 of Section 4 in
[
5
]
. Indeed, from the exact dual sequence of (
4
):

0\to\textrm{Map}(\mathcal{V},\mathfrak{g})^{*}\xrightarrow{(d\Psi)_{y}^{*}}T_{y}\Big(\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\big(y_{0}(\operatorname{fr}\mathcal{V})\big)\Big)^{*}\to T_{y}^{c}\big(\Gamma_{S}(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\big)^{*}\to 0,

follows the identification of
\textrm{img}(d\Psi)_{y}^{*}
with the elements of
T_{y}\Big(\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\big(y_{0}(\operatorname{fr}\mathcal{V})\big)\Big)^{*}
incidents with
T_{y}^{c}\big(\Gamma_{S}(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\big)
.

So, if the section
y
is critical (Definition
2.3
), then there exists a map
\lambda\in\textrm{Map}(\mathcal{V},\mathfrak{g})^{*}

=\textrm{Map}(\mathcal{V},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
, that is unique by the injectivity of
(d\Psi)_{y}^{*}
, such that:

(8)

-\big(d\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{L})\big)_{y}(\delta y)=\big((d\Psi)_{y}^{*}\lambda\big)(\delta y),\quad\delta y\in T_{y}\Big(\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\big(y_{0}(\operatorname{fr}\mathcal{V})\big)\Big).

By the variation formula (10) of
[
5
]
, Section 3:

\big(d\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{L})\big)_{y}(\delta y)=\sum_{v\in\operatorname{int}\mathcal{V}}\big(\mathcal{E}_{v}(\mathcal{L})\big)_{(j^{2}y)(v)}(j^{2}\delta y)(v),

since
(\delta y)_{v}=0
at the vertices
v\in\operatorname{fr}\mathcal{V}
.

On the other hand, by (

\displaystyle\big((d\Psi)_{y}^{*}\lambda\big)(\delta y)=

\displaystyle\lambda\circ(d\Psi)_{y}(\delta y)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{V}}\lambda(\alpha)\circ\bigg(\sum_{v\prec\alpha}\big(\Theta_{\alpha}^{v}(\Phi)\big)_{(j^{1}y)(\alpha)}(j^{1}\delta y)(\alpha)\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in\operatorname{int}\mathcal{V}}\sum_{\alpha\in S_{v}}\lambda(\alpha)\circ\big(\Theta_{\alpha}^{v}(\Phi)\big)_{(j^{1}y)(\alpha)}(j^{1}\delta y)(\alpha),

since by swapping the sums, as
(\delta y)_{v}=0
at the vertices

From here the result follows because

is an arbitrary variation at the vertices

.

∎

3.
Lagrange multiplier rule. Noether’s theorem. Multisymplectic form formula

The aim of section is to formulate the Lagrange problems that we have seen in the previous section as free variational problems via a Lagrange multipliers rule. In this sense the equations (
7
) that characterize the critical sections of the Lagrange problem will be interpreted as the Euler-Lagrange equations of an unconstrained variational problem extended to the multipliers
\lambda\in\textrm{Map}(\mathcal{V},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
as follows.

Let
\alpha\in V_{n}
and let us denote by
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}
and
\Phi_{\alpha}
the restriction of
\mathcal{L}
and
\Phi
to the fibers
(J^{1}Y)_{\alpha}
as before. Then, for every
\lambda_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
we can define the 1-form

d\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}+\lambda_{\alpha}\circ(\theta\circ d\Phi_{\alpha})

on
(J^{1}Y)_{\alpha}
; where the first
\circ
from the left denotes the duality pairing between
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. That is, for every
\lambda\in\textrm{Map}(V_{n},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
we have the 1-form

(9)

d\mathcal{L}+\lambda\circ(\theta\circ d\Phi),

on
J^{1}Y\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}\equiv J^{1}Y\times_{V_{n}}\mathfrak{G}^{*}
, where
\mathfrak{G}^{*}=\mathfrak{g}^{*}\times V_{n}\to V_{n}
is the trivial bundle on
V_{n}
with fiber
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. We will use
\lambda_{\alpha}
or
\lambda(\alpha)
to denote the same element of
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.

This 1-form defines an unconstrained variational problem on the manifold
\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0}\times Y)\times\textrm{Map}(\mathcal{V},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
with action 1-form given by:

(10)

\big(\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{L},\Phi)\big)_{(y,\lambda)}(\delta y,\delta\lambda)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{V}}(d\mathcal{L}_{\alpha})_{(j^{1}y)(\alpha)}(j^{1}\delta y)(\alpha)+\lambda_{\alpha}\circ(\theta\circ d\Phi_{\alpha})_{(j^{1}y)(\alpha)}(j^{1}\delta y)(\alpha),

(y,\lambda)\in\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\times\textrm{Map}(\mathcal{V},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
,
(\delta y,\delta\lambda)\in T_{y}\big(\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\big)\oplus T_{\lambda}\big(\textrm{Map}(\mathcal{V},\mathfrak{g}^{*})\big)
, from where the following notion of stationarity is given.

Definition 3.1
.

We say that a section-mapping
(y,\lambda)\in\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\times\textrm{Map}(\mathcal{V},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
is critical for the action 1-form
\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{L},\Phi)
if the section
y\hskip-0.9pt\in\hskip-1.0pt\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)
is admissible and
\hskip-1.0pt\big(\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{L},\Phi)\big)_{(y,\lambda)}
vanishes on the subspace
T_{y}^{c}\big(\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\big)\oplus T_{\lambda}\big(\textrm{Map}(\mathcal{V},\mathfrak{g}^{*})\big)
.

From (
10
) we get that the subspace
T_{\lambda}\big(\textrm{Map}(\mathcal{V},\mathfrak{g}^{*})\big)
is incident with the 1-form
\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{L},\Phi)
, i.e.:

(11)

\big(\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{L},\Phi)\big)_{(y,\lambda)}(0,\delta\lambda)=0\quad\textrm{ for all }\quad\delta\lambda\in T_{\lambda}\big(\textrm{Map}(\mathcal{V},\mathfrak{g}^{*})\big).

Remark 3.2
.

The unconstrained variational problem defined by the 1-form (
9
) differs substantially from that introduced in Section 5 of
[
5
]
for Lagrange problems with constraints
\Phi:J^{1}Y\to E
valued in vector spaces. As noted in the Introduction, these problems are defined by an unconstrained Lagrangian density
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}=\mathcal{L}+\lambda\circ\Phi
(
\circ
the bilinear product of the duality between
E
and
E^{*}
), which is not generalizable to constraints
\Phi:J^{1}Y\to G
valued in Lie groups. However, differentiating
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}
we obtain:

d\widehat{\mathcal{L}}=d\mathcal{L}+\lambda\circ d\Phi+d\lambda\barwedge\Phi,

where
\barwedge
denotes the wedge product of valued forms with respect to the bilinear form
\circ
induced by the duality pairing. The first two terms of this formula are generalizable via the expression (
9
) in which the Maurer-Cartan 1-form of
G
is inserted. In this sense, it is important to observe that by dispensing with the term
d\lambda\barwedge\Phi
in this formulation (which is also not generalizable), condition (
11
) no longer allows to obtain the admissibility of the critical section-mappings as part of the Euler-Lagrange equations of the extended problem. This condition must be imposed separately, as contemplated in Definition
3.1
that we have given of stationarity.

Following the route of Section 5 of
[
5
]
with the obvious changes we will have:

Proposition 3.3

(Variational formula)
.

For any section-mapping
(y,\lambda)\in\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\times

\textrm{Map}(\mathcal{V},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
and for any tangent vector
(\delta y,\delta\lambda)\in T_{(y,\lambda)}\big(\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\times\textrm{Map}(\mathcal{V},\mathfrak{g}^{*})\big)
, we have now:

(12)

\displaystyle\big(\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{L},\Phi)\big)_{(y,\lambda)}(\delta y,\delta\lambda)=

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in\operatorname{int}\mathcal{V}}\big(\mathcal{E}_{v}(\mathcal{L})+\sum_{\alpha\in S_{v}}\lambda(\alpha)\circ\pi^{*}_{v\alpha}\Theta_{\alpha}^{v}(\Phi)\big)_{(j^{2}y)(v)}(j^{2}\delta y)(v)

\displaystyle+\sum_{(v\in\operatorname{fr}\mathcal{V})\prec\alpha\in\mathcal{V}}\big(\Theta_{\alpha}^{v}(\mathcal{L})+\lambda(\alpha)\circ\Theta_{\alpha}^{v}(\Phi)\big)_{(j^{1}y)(\alpha)}(j^{1}\delta y)(\alpha).

Corollary 3.4

(Euler-Lagrange equations)
.

A section-mapping
(y,\lambda)\in\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\times

\textrm{Map}(\mathcal{V},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
is critical for the action 1-form
\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{L},\Phi)
if and only if the section
y\in\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)
is admissible and for any
v\in\operatorname{int}\mathcal{V}
:

(13)

\big(\mathcal{E}_{v}(\mathcal{L})+\sum_{\alpha\in S_{v}}\lambda(\alpha)\circ\pi^{*}_{v\alpha}\Theta_{\alpha}^{v}(\Phi)\big)_{(j^{2}y)(v)}=0.

Theorem 3.5

(Lagrange Multiplier Rule)
.

A section-mapping
(y,\lambda)\in\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\times

\textrm{Map}(\mathcal{V},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
, being
y
an admissible regular section, is critical for the action 1-form
\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{L},\Phi)
if and only if its component
y\in\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)
is an admissible regular critical section for the Lagrange problem with Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}:J^{1}Y\to\mathbb{R}
and constraint submanifold
S=\Phi^{-1}(e)\subset J^{1}Y
, being its component
\lambda
the multiplier associated to
y
.

On the other hand, the expression of the boundary term of the variation formula (
12
) suggests to take as Cartan 1-form associated to the action 1-form
\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{L},\phi)
the family of 1-forms:

\Theta_{\alpha}^{v}(\mathcal{L})+\lambda_{\alpha}\circ\Theta_{\alpha}^{v}(\Phi),\quad v\in\mathcal{V}_{0},\quad\alpha\in\mathcal{V}_{n},\quad v\prec\alpha.

This concept will allow us to formulate a Noether’s theory of symmetries for the Lagrange problems with constraints valued in Lie groups, as well as to establish a corresponding formula of the multisymplectic form.

Definition 3.6
.

An infinitesimal symmetry of a Lagrange problem with Lagrangian density
\mathcal{L}:J^{1}Y\to\mathbb{R}
and constraint submanifold
S=\Phi^{-1}(e)
, with
\Phi:J^{1}Y\to G
, is a vector field
D\in\mathfrak{X}(Y)
such that
(j^{1}D)\mathcal{L}=0
and
(j^{1}D)(\Phi)=(d\Phi)(j^{1}D)=0
where:

(d\Phi)(j^{1}D):j^{1}_{\alpha}y\in(J^{1}Y)_{\alpha}\mapsto(d\Phi)_{j^{1}_{\alpha}y}(j^{1}D)_{j^{1}_{\alpha}y}\in T_{\Phi(j^{1}_{\alpha}y)}G.

Theorem 3.7

(Noether)
.

If
y\in\Gamma_{S}(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)
is a regular critical section of the Lagrange problem given by
(\mathcal{L},\Phi)
and
D\in\mathfrak{X}(Y)
is an infinitesimal symmetry, then:

\sum_{(v\prec\operatorname{fr}\mathcal{V})\prec\alpha}\left[\big(\Theta^{v}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{L})+\lambda(\alpha)\circ\Theta^{v}_{\alpha}(\Phi)\big)(j^{1}D)_{\alpha}\right](j^{1}y)(\alpha)=0,

where
\lambda\in\textrm{Map}(\mathcal{V},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
is the multiplier associated to the regular critical section.

Proof.

If we call
(\delta y)(v)=(D_{v})_{y(v)}
,
v\in\mathcal{V}_{0}
, then
(j^{1}\delta y)(\alpha)=\big((j^{1}D)_{\alpha}\big)_{(j^{1}y)(\alpha)}
,
\alpha\in\mathcal{V}
, and we obtain
\big(\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{L},\Phi)\big)_{(y,\lambda)}(\delta y,0)=0
by (
10
) and for being
D
an infinitesimal symmetry of the Lagrange problem. On the other hand, if
y\in\Gamma_{S}(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)
is a regular critical section of the Lagrange problem with associated multiplier
\lambda\in\textrm{Map}(\mathcal{V},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
, then for any
v\in\operatorname{int}\mathcal{V}
Theorem
2.6
provides:

\left(\mathcal{E}_{v}(\mathcal{L})+\sum_{\alpha\in S_{v}}\lambda(\alpha)\circ\pi^{*}_{v\alpha}\Theta^{v}_{\alpha}(\Phi)\right)_{(j^{2}y)(v)}=0.

Now the result follows if we substitute these both zero terms in the variation formula (
12
).
∎

As for the establishment of a multisymplectic form formula for this kind of Lagrange problems we will proceed as follows.

The variation formula (
12
) can be expressed on the manifold
\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\times\textrm{Map}(\mathcal{V},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
as:

(14)

\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{L},\Phi)=\sum_{v\in\operatorname{int}\mathcal{V}}\big(\mathcal{E}_{v}(\mathcal{L})+\sum_{\alpha\in S_{v}}\lambda_{\alpha}\circ\Theta_{\alpha}^{v}(\Phi)\big)+\sum_{(v\in\operatorname{fr}\mathcal{V})\prec\alpha\in\mathcal{V}}\big(\Theta_{\alpha}^{v}(\mathcal{L})+\lambda_{\alpha}\circ\Theta_{\alpha}^{v}(\Phi)\big),

where the terms of this sum are considered 1-forms on
\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\times\textrm{Map}(\mathcal{V},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
through the canonical projections from
\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\times\textrm{Map}(\mathcal{V},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
to
(J^{1}Y\times_{\mathcal{V}}\mathfrak{G}^{*})_{\alpha}
and
(J^{2}Y\times_{\mathcal{V}_{0}}j^{1}\mathfrak{G}^{*})_{v}
respectively,
(y,\lambda)\mapsto\big((j^{1}y)(\alpha),e^{*}(\alpha)=(\alpha,\lambda(\alpha))\big)
and
(y,\lambda)\mapsto\big((j^{2}y)(v),j^{1}e^{*}(v)\big)
, being
j^{1}\mathfrak{G}^{*}\to\mathcal{V}_{0}
the 1-jet bundle of
\mathfrak{G}^{*}\to\mathcal{V}
defined by
(j^{1}\mathfrak{G}^{*})_{v}=\prod_{\alpha\in S_{v}}\mathfrak{G}^{*}_{\alpha}
,
v\in\mathcal{V}_{0}
, and where
j^{1}e^{*}:v\in\mathcal{V}_{0}\mapsto\big(e^{*}(\alpha)\big)
,
\alpha\in S_{v}
, denotes the 1-jet extension of
e^{*}\in\Gamma(\mathcal{V},\mathfrak{G}^{*})
.

If
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{L},\Phi)
is the 1-form on
\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\times\textrm{Map}(\mathcal{V},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
:

(15)

\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{L},\Phi)=\sum_{v\in\operatorname{int}\mathcal{V}}\big(\mathcal{E}_{v}(\mathcal{L})+\sum_{\alpha\in S_{v}}(\lambda_{\alpha}\circ\Theta_{\alpha}^{v})(\Phi)\big)

then the critical section-mappings
(y,\lambda)\in\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\times\textrm{Map}(\mathcal{V},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
are characterized through Corollary
3.4
by the conditions:

y\quad\textrm{ admisible and }\quad\big(\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{L},\Phi)\big)_{(y,\lambda)}=0.

Local expression.
Let
\{y^{i}_{v}|1\leq i\leq m\}
be a local coordinate system in a neighborhood of each
y_{v}\in Y_{v}
and
\{y^{i}_{v}|1\leq i\leq m,v\prec\alpha\}
, the local coordinate system induced in
(J^{1}Y)_{\alpha}
. On the other hand, let
\{g^{I}|1\leq I\leq l\}
,
l=\dim G
, be a local system of coordinates of
G
in a neighborhood of
e\in G
and
\big\{\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial g^{I}}\right)_{e}|1\leq I\leq l\big\}
and
\big\{(dg^{I})_{e}|1\leq I\leq l\big\}
the induced basis into the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and its dual
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
respectively. If
L_{g}:G\to G
is the left translation by
g\in G
,
L_{g}(\overline{g})=g\cdot\overline{g}
,
L_{g}^{I}:G\to\mathbb{R}
is the function
L_{g}^{I}=g^{I}\circ L_{g}
and
\Phi_{\alpha}^{I}=g^{I}\circ\Phi_{\alpha}
,
1\leq I\leq l
, then for any
\alpha\in\mathcal{V}_{n}
,
\theta\circ d\Phi_{\alpha}
is the 1-form over
(J^{1}Y)_{\alpha}
with values in the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
given by:

(16)

(\theta\circ d\Phi_{\alpha})_{j^{1}_{\alpha}y}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\prec\alpha\\
1\leq I\leq l\\
1\leq i\leq m\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{J=1}^{l}\bigg(\frac{\partial L^{I}_{g^{-1}}}{\partial g^{J}}\bigg)_{g}\bigg(\frac{\partial\Phi^{J}_{\alpha}}{\partial y^{i}_{v}}\bigg)_{j^{1}_{\alpha}y}\right)(dy^{i}_{v})_{j^{1}_{\alpha}y}\otimes\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial g^{I}}\Big)_{e}

for
j^{1}_{\alpha}y\in(J^{1}Y)_{\alpha}
and
g=\Phi_{\alpha}(j^{1}_{\alpha}y)\in G
.

From here it follows that:

(17)

\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{L},\Phi)=\sum_{v\in\operatorname{int}\mathcal{V}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{E}^{i}_{v}(\mathcal{L},\Phi)dy^{i}_{v},

where, for
\lambda(\alpha)=\sum_{1\leq I\leq l}\lambda(\alpha)^{I}(dg^{I})_{e}
:

\mathcal{E}^{i}_{v}(\mathcal{L},\Phi)=\sum_{\alpha\in S_{v}}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}}{\partial y^{i}_{v}}+\sum_{I,J=1}^{l}\lambda(\alpha)^{I}\bigg(\frac{\partial L^{I}_{g^{-1}}}{\partial g^{J}}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{\partial\Phi^{J}_{\alpha}}{\partial y^{i}_{v}}\bigg).

Definition 3.8
.

A Jacobi field along a critical section-mapping
(y,\lambda)\in\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\times

\textrm{Map}(\mathcal{V},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
of the action 1-form
\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{L},\Phi)
is a tangent vector
(\delta y,\delta\lambda)\in T_{(y,\lambda)}\big(\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\times\textrm{Map}(\mathcal{V},\mathfrak{g}^{*})\big)
that verifies:
\delta y
is admissible and
(\delta y,\delta\lambda)
has an extension
D\in\mathfrak{X}\big(\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\times\textrm{Map}(\mathcal{V},\mathfrak{g}^{*})\big)
such that:

(18)

\big(L_{D}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{L},\Phi)\big)_{(y,\lambda)}=0.

The definition is correct since from (
17
) we have:

L_{D}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{L},\Phi)=\sum_{v\in\operatorname{int}\mathcal{V}}(D\mathcal{E}^{i}_{v})dy^{i}_{v}+\mathcal{E}^{i}_{v}(\mathcal{L},\Phi)L_{D}dy^{i}_{v},

and therefore, evaluating this expression at
(y,\lambda)
, the second term vanishes because
(y,\lambda)
is critical and the first one depends only on
(\delta y,\delta\lambda)
.

Continuing with this approach, a key point for obtaining a multisymplectic form formula for this new class of Lagrange problems is the following property.

Proposition 3.9
.

If
(y,\lambda)\in\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\times\textrm{Map}(\mathcal{V},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
is a section-mapping with
y
admissible and
\big((\delta y)^{i},(\delta\lambda)^{i}\big)\in T_{(y,\lambda)}\big(\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\times\textrm{Map}(\mathcal{V},\mathfrak{g}^{*})\big)
are two tangent vectors at
(y,\lambda)
with
(\delta y)^{i}
admissible,
i=1,2
, we have:

\big(d\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{L},\Phi)\Big)_{(y,\lambda)}\big(\big((\delta y)^{1},(\delta\lambda)^{1}\big),\big((\delta y)^{2},(\delta\lambda)^{2}\big)\big)=0.

Proof.

From (
10
):

\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{L},\Phi)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{V}}d\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}+\lambda_{\alpha}\circ(\theta\circ d\Phi_{\alpha})

where the terms of this sum are considered 1-forms over
\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\times\textrm{Map}(\mathcal{V},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
via the canonical projection
(y,\lambda)\mapsto\big((j^{1}y)(\alpha),e^{*}(\alpha)=(\alpha,\lambda(\alpha))\big)
from
\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\times\textrm{Map}(\mathcal{V},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
to
(J^{1}Y\times_{\mathcal{V}}\mathfrak{G}^{*})_{\alpha}
.

From here we get:

d\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{L},\Phi)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{V}}\underbrace{d^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}}_{=0}+d\lambda_{\alpha}\barwedge(\theta\circ d\Phi_{\alpha})+\lambda_{\alpha}\circ d(\theta\circ d\Phi_{\alpha}),

where, as before,
\barwedge
is the wedge product of valued forms in
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
with forms valued in
\mathfrak{g}
, applying the duality pairing. This gives:

\displaystyle\big(d\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{L},\Phi)\big)_{(y,\lambda)}

\displaystyle\big(\big((\delta y)^{1},(\delta\lambda)^{1}\big),\big((\delta y)^{2},(\delta\lambda)^{2}\big)\big)=

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{V}}\big(d\lambda_{\alpha}\barwedge(\theta\circ d\Phi_{\alpha})\big)_{(y,\lambda)}\big(\big((\delta y)^{1},(\delta\lambda)^{1}\big),\big((\delta y)^{2},(\delta\lambda)^{2}\big)\big)

\displaystyle+\big(\lambda_{\alpha}\circ d(\theta\circ d\Phi_{\alpha})\big)_{(y,\lambda)}\big(\big((\delta y)^{1},(\delta\lambda)^{1}\big),\big((\delta y)^{2},(\delta\lambda)^{2}\big)\big).

The first term vanishes because
(\delta y)^{i}
,
i=1,2
are admissible. Indeed:

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{V}}

\displaystyle\big(d\lambda_{\alpha}\barwedge(\theta\circ d\Phi_{\alpha})\big)_{(y,\lambda)}\big(\big((\delta y)^{1},(\delta\lambda)^{1}\big),\big((\delta y)^{2},(\delta\lambda)^{2}\big)\big)=

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{V}}

\displaystyle\Big((d\lambda_{\alpha})_{(y,\lambda)}\big((\delta y)^{1},(\delta\lambda)^{1}\big)\cdot(\theta\circ d\Phi_{\alpha}))_{(y,\lambda)}\big((\delta y)^{2},(\delta\lambda)^{2}\big)

\displaystyle-(d\lambda_{\alpha})_{(y,\lambda)}\big((\delta y)^{2},(\delta\lambda)^{2}\big)\cdot(\theta\circ d\Phi_{\alpha}))_{(y,\lambda)}\big((\delta y)^{1},(\delta\lambda)^{1}\big)\Big)=0

since we know from the admissibility of
(\delta y)^{i}
, by Definition 2.2, that

(d\Phi_{\alpha})_{(j^{1}y)(\alpha)}(j^{1}\delta y^{i})(\alpha)\big)=0,

for
i=1,2
, and so:

(\theta\circ d\Phi_{\alpha}))_{(y,\lambda)}\big((\delta y)^{i},(\delta\lambda)^{i}\big)=\theta_{\Phi_{\alpha}((j^{1}y)(\alpha))}\big((d\Phi_{\alpha})_{(j^{1}y)(\alpha)}(j^{1}\delta y^{i})(\alpha)\big)=0\quad i=1,2.

Regarding the second term, from the local expression (
16
) we obtain:

\displaystyle d(\theta\circ d\Phi_{\alpha})=

\displaystyle\left(d\left(\sum_{v\prec\alpha,I,i}\left(\sum_{J}\bigg(\frac{\partial L^{I}_{g^{-1}}}{\partial g^{J}}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{\partial\Phi^{J}_{\alpha}}{\partial y^{i}_{v}}\bigg)\right)\right)\wedge dy^{i}_{v}\right)\otimes\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial g^{I}}\Big)_{e}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{I,J}\left(d\bigg(\frac{\partial L^{I}_{g^{-1}}}{\partial g^{J}}\bigg)\wedge d\Phi^{J}_{\alpha}+\bigg(\frac{\partial L^{I}_{g^{-1}}}{\partial g^{J}}\bigg)\underbrace{d^{2}\Phi^{J}_{\alpha}}_{=0}\right)\otimes\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial g^{I}}\Big)_{e}

from which, if
\lambda(\alpha)=\sum_{I}\lambda(\alpha)^{I}(dg^{I})_{e}
as before, we get:

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{V}}

\displaystyle\big(\lambda_{\alpha}\circ d(\theta\circ d\Phi_{\alpha})\big)_{(y,\lambda)}\big(\big((\delta y)^{1},(\delta\lambda)^{1}\big),\big((\delta y)^{2},(\delta\lambda)^{2}\big)\big)=

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{V},I,J}

\displaystyle\lambda(\alpha)^{I}\left(d\bigg(\frac{\partial L^{I}_{g^{-1}}}{\partial g^{J}}\bigg)\wedge d\Phi^{J}_{\alpha}\right)_{(j^{1}y)(\alpha)}\big(j^{1}(\delta y)^{1}(\alpha),j^{1}(\delta y)^{2}(\alpha)\big)=0

because of, again, the admissibility of
(\delta y)^{i}
,
i=1,2
.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

As noted in Remark
3.2
, the action 1-form
\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{L},\Phi)
generalizes to discrete Lagrange problems valued in a Lie group the differential,
d\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{V}}(\widehat{\mathcal{L}})
, of the action function
\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{V}}(\widehat{\mathcal{L}})
of the extended Lagrangian
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}=\mathcal{L}+\lambda\circ\Phi
of discrete Lagrange problems valued in a vector space. In this case, Proposition
3.9
is trivial because:

d\big(d\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{V}}(\widehat{\mathcal{L}})\big)=d^{2}\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{V}}(\widehat{\mathcal{L}})=0.

At this point we finally have:

Theorem 3.11

(Multisymplectic form formula)
.

If
(y,\lambda)\in\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\times\textrm{Map}(\mathcal{V},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
is a critical section-mapping of the action 1-form
\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{L},\Phi)
and
\big((\delta y)^{i},(\delta\lambda)^{i}\big)\in T_{(y,\lambda)}\big(\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\times\textrm{Map}(\mathcal{V},\mathfrak{g}^{*})\big)
,
i=1,2
, are two Jacobi fields along
(y,\lambda)
, then we have:

\sum_{(v\prec\operatorname{fr}\mathcal{V})\prec\alpha}\Big(d\big(\Theta^{v}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{L})+\lambda_{\alpha}\circ\Theta^{v}_{\alpha}(\Phi)\big)\Big)_{(y,\lambda)}\big(\big((\delta y)^{1},(\delta\lambda)^{1}\big),\big((\delta y)^{2},(\delta\lambda)^{2}\big)\big)=0.

Proof.

Taking the differential of (
14
) and having in mind (
15
), we get:

d\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{L},\Phi)=d\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{L},\Phi)+\sum_{(v\in\operatorname{fr}\mathcal{V})\prec\alpha}d\big(\Theta^{v}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{L})+\lambda_{\alpha}\circ\Theta^{v}_{\alpha}(\Phi)\big),

from which, if
D^{i}\in\mathfrak{X}\big(\Gamma(\mathcal{V}_{0},Y)\times\textrm{Map}(\mathcal{V},\mathfrak{g}^{*})\big)
,
i=1,2
, are extensions of
\big((\delta y)^{i},(\delta\lambda)^{i}\big)
verifying the Jacobi equation (
18
), then by Proposition
3.9
we have:

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\Big(\big(d\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{L},\Phi)\big)(D^{1},D^{2})\Big)(y,\lambda)=\Big(\big(d\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{L},\Phi)\big)(D^{1},D^{2})\Big)(y,\lambda)

\displaystyle+\sum_{(v\in\operatorname{fr}\mathcal{V})\prec\alpha}\Big(d\big(\Theta^{v}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{L})+\lambda_{\alpha}\circ\Theta^{v}_{\alpha}(\Phi)\big)\Big)_{(y,\lambda)}\big(\big((\delta y)^{1},(\delta\lambda)^{1}\big),\big((\delta y)^{2},(\delta\lambda)^{2}\big)\big).

Now, applying the Cartan formula to the first term of the previous sum, and taking into account the Jacobi equation (
18
) and Euler-Lagrange equations
\big(\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{L},\Phi)\big)_{(y,\lambda)}=0
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\Big(\big(d\mathcal{E}

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L},\Phi)\big)(D^{1},D^{2})\Big)(y,\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(D^{1}\big(\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{L},\Phi)(D^{2})\big)\Big)(y,\lambda)-\Big(D^{2}\big(\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{L},\Phi)(D^{1})\big)\Big)(y,\lambda)-\big(\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{L},\Phi)([D^{1},D^{2}])\big)(y,\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(L_{D^{1}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{L},\Phi)\big)_{(y,\lambda)}(D^{2}_{(y,\lambda)})-\big(L_{D^{2}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{L},\Phi)\big)_{(y,\lambda)}(D^{1}_{(y,\lambda)})+\big(\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{L},\Phi)([D^{1},D^{2}])\big)(y,\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

So, the result is concluded.
∎

4.
Application to Euler-Poincaré reduction in discrete field theory

In its simplest original version, this discrete reduction problem arises when trying to solve an unconstrained discrete problem for a principal bundle over the standard simplicial complex of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with Lagrangian density invariant by the action of the structural group of the bundle (see for example
[
7
]
and the references cited therein).

More precisely, following
[
7
]
, let
V
be the simplicial complex with vertices
V_{0}=\big\{(i,j)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}\big\}
, oriented edges
V_{1}=\big\{[(i,j),(i+1,j)],[(i,j),(i,j+1)],\,i,j\in\mathbb{Z}\big\}
, and oriented faces
V_{2}=\big\{\Delta_{ij}=[(i,j),(i+1,j),(i,j+1)],\,i,j\in\mathbb{Z}\big\}
. In this case,
V_{2}
is identified with
V_{0}
by the bijection
\Delta_{ij}\mapsto(i,j)
which we will assume in what follows. Given a Lie group
G
, let
P=G\times V_{0}\to V_{0}
be the (left) trivial principal bundle, and we consider a Lagrangian density
\mathcal{L}:(J^{1}P=G\times G\times G\times V_{0}\to V_{0})\to\mathbb{R}
invariant by the diagonal action of
G
on
J^{1}P:g\big((g_{ij},g_{i+1j},g_{ij+1}),(i,j)\big)=\big((gg_{ij},gg_{i+1j},gg_{ij+1}),(i,j)\big)
. Identifying
J^{1}P/G
with the fiber bundle
Y=G\times G\times V_{0}\to V_{0}
by the rule
G\big((g_{ij},g_{i+1j},g_{ij+1}),(i,j)\big)=\big((g_{ij}^{-1}g_{i+1j},g_{ij}^{-1}g_{ij+1}),(i,j)\big)
, the Lagrangian density
\mathcal{L}
is projected onto a Lagrangian density
\mathit{l}:Y=J^{1}P/G\to\mathbb{R}
by the projection (reduction map)

\displaystyle\pi:J^{1}P\hskip 10.00002pt\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002ptY=J^{1}P/G

\displaystyle\big((g_{ij},g_{i+1j},g_{ij+1}),(i,j)\big)\mapsto

\displaystyle\big((u_{ij}=g^{-1}_{ij}g_{i+1j},v_{ij}=g^{-1}_{ij}g_{ij+1}),(i,j)\big)

On the other hand, the sections
g:(i,j)\in V_{0}\mapsto\big(g_{ij},(i,j)\big)
of
P
are projected by
\pi\circ j^{1}
in the sections
y:(i,j)\in V_{0}\mapsto\big((u_{ij}=g^{-1}_{ij}g_{i+1j},v_{ij}=g^{-1}_{ij}g_{ij+1}),(i,j)\big)
of

\Phi(j^{1}y)=e

(19)

\Phi_{ij}\big((u_{ij},v_{ij},u_{i+1j},v_{i+1j},u_{ij+1},v_{ij+1}),(i,j)\big)=u_{ij}v_{i+1j}u_{ij+1}^{-1}v_{ij}^{-1}.

Similarly, the infinitesimal variations
\delta g:(i,j)\in V_{0}\mapsto\big(\delta g_{ij},(i,j)\big)
of each section

are projected by
\pi\circ j^{1}

\delta y:(i,j)\in V_{0}\mapsto\big((\delta u_{ij},\delta v_{ij}),(i,j)\big)
of the section

(20)

\begin{split}\delta u_{ij}=&(L_{u_{ij}})_{*}(\theta_{i+1j})-(R_{u_{ij}})_{*}(\theta_{ij})\in T_{u_{ij}}G,\\
\delta v_{ij}=&(L_{v_{ij}})_{*}(\theta_{ij+1})-(R_{v_{ij}})_{*}(\theta_{ij})\in T_{v_{ij}}G,\end{split}

being
\theta_{ij}=(L_{g_{ij}^{-1}})_{*}(\delta g_{ij})\in\mathfrak{g}
.

Thus we have a constrained variational problem on

(reduced problem) whose action functional with respect to a finite set of faces
\mathcal{V}\subset V_{2}
(

\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{U}}(\mathit{l}):y\in\Gamma(\mathcal{U}_{0},Y)\mapsto\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{U}}\mathit{l}\big((u_{ij},v_{ij}),(i,j)\big),

where
\mathcal{U}_{0}
is the (finite) set of adherent vertices to
\mathcal{V}
.

Under these conditions, we will say that a section
y\in\Gamma(\mathcal{U}_{0},Y)
is critical for the reduced problem if
(d\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{U}}(\mathit{l}))_{y}
is zero over the infinitesimal variations (
20
) such that
\theta_{ij}
vanishes at the frontier of
\mathcal{U}
.

Under this approach, the main result of

Theorem 4.2

(Reduction)
.

Let
\mathcal{L}

in the interior points of
\mathcal{U}
.

b)

g
is critical for arbitrary variations
\delta g
that vanishes in the frontier of
\mathcal{U}
.

c)

(

) where

.

From Theorem

.

Reciprocally
[

]
:

Theorem 4.3

(Reconstruction)
.

Let
y\in\Gamma(\mathcal{U}_{0},Y)
be a solution of the discrete Euler-Poincaré equations (
21
) verifying the constraint
\Phi(j^{1}y)=e
. Then there exists a solution
g\in\Gamma(\mathcal{U}_{0},P)
of the unreduced Euler-Lagrange equations that projects over

. In this case,

is uniquely determined up to right translation by an element of

.

The Euler-Poincaré reduction just summarized provides an interesting example of a discrete Lagrangian problem valued in a Lie group taking as problem data the reduced Lagrangian density
\mathit{l}:Y=J^{1}P/G\to\mathbb{R}
and the constraint morphism
\Phi:J^{1}Y\to G
given by formula (
19
).

In particular, the solutions of this problem will be solutions of the discrete Euler-Poincaré equations (
21
), which has the nice consequence of being able to apply to the discrete Euler-Poincaré reduction the formalism that we have developed in this paper for Lagrange problems.

The procedure we are going to follow is to eliminate the Lagrange multiplier from equations (
13
) and observe that the result of such elimination satisfies the Euler-Poincaré equations (
21
).

Indeed. Equations (
13
) are in this case:

(22)

(d\mathit{l})_{y_{ij}}+\lambda_{\Delta_{ij}}\circ\big(\Theta_{\Delta_{ij}}^{ij}(\Phi)\big)_{j^{1}y(\Delta_{ij})}+\lambda_{\Delta_{i-1j}}\circ\big(\Theta_{\Delta_{i-1j}}^{ij}(\Phi)\big)_{j^{1}y(\Delta_{ij})}\\
+\lambda_{\Delta_{ij-1}}\circ\big(\Theta_{\Delta_{ij-1}}^{ij}(\Phi)\big)_{j^{1}y(\Delta_{ij})}=0

where, because
y
is admissible, the 1-forms
\Theta_{\Delta_{ij}}^{ij}(\Phi)
are calculated along
j^{1}y
by the formula:

(23)

\big(d\Phi_{\Delta_{ij}}\big)_{j^{1}y(\Delta_{ij})}=\big(\Theta_{\Delta_{ij}}^{ij}(\Phi)\big)_{j^{1}y(\Delta_{ij})}+\big(\Theta_{\Delta_{ij}}^{i+1j}(\Phi)\big)_{j^{1}y(\Delta_{ij})}+\big(\Theta_{\Delta_{ij}}^{ij+1}(\Phi)\big)_{j^{1}y(\Delta_{ij})}.

Applying (
22
) to
\delta y_{ij}=(\delta u_{ij},\delta v_{ij})
these equations are equivalent to the system:

(24)

\left\{\begin{aligned} d\mathit{l}(\cdot,v_{ij})(\delta u_{ij})+&\lambda_{\Delta_{ij}}\circ\big(\Theta_{\Delta_{ij}}^{ij}(\Phi)\big)_{j^{1}y(\Delta_{ij})}(\delta u_{ij})+\lambda_{\Delta_{i-1j}}\circ\big(\Theta_{\Delta_{i-1j}}^{ij}(\Phi)\big)_{j^{1}y(\Delta_{ij})}(\delta u_{ij})\\
+&\lambda_{\Delta_{ij-1}}\circ\big(\Theta_{\Delta_{ij-1}}^{ij}(\Phi)\big)_{j^{1}y(\Delta_{ij})}(\delta u_{ij})=0\\
d\mathit{l}(u_{ij},\cdot)(\delta v_{ij})+&\lambda_{\Delta_{ij}}\circ\big(\Theta_{\Delta_{ij}}^{ij}(\Phi)\big)_{j^{1}y(\Delta_{ij})}(\delta v_{ij})+\lambda_{\Delta_{i-1j}}\circ\big(\Theta_{\Delta_{i-1j}}^{ij}(\Phi)\big)_{j^{1}y(\Delta_{ij})}(\delta v_{ij})\\
+&\lambda_{\Delta_{ij-1}}\circ\big(\Theta_{\Delta_{ij-1}}^{ij}(\Phi)\big)_{j^{1}y(\Delta_{ij})}(\delta v_{ij})=0\end{aligned}\right.

On the other hand, by the expression (
19
) of the constraint morphism, the only nonzero components of
(d\Phi_{\Delta_{ij}})_{j^{1}y(\Delta_{ij})}
are those resulting from applying this 1-form to
\delta u_{ij}
,
\delta v_{ij}
,
\delta u_{ij+1}
,
\delta v_{i+1j}
which, taking into account the constraint condition of
y_{ij}=(u_{ij},v_{ij})
, are computed as follows:

\displaystyle(d\Phi_{\Delta_{ij}})_{j^{1}y(\Delta_{ij})}

\displaystyle(\delta u_{ij})=\delta u_{ij}v_{i+1j}u^{-1}_{ij+1}v^{-1}_{ij}=\delta u_{ij}u^{-1}_{ij}=(R_{u^{-1}_{ij}})_{*}(\delta u_{ij}),

\displaystyle(d\Phi_{\Delta_{ij}})_{j^{1}y(\Delta_{ij})}

\displaystyle(\delta v_{ij})=u_{ij}v_{i+1j}u^{-1}_{ij+1}(-v^{-1}_{ij}\delta v_{ij}v^{-1}_{ij})=-(R_{v^{-1}_{ij}})_{*}(\delta v_{ij}),

\displaystyle(d\Phi_{\Delta_{ij}})_{j^{1}y(\Delta_{ij})}

\displaystyle(\delta u_{ij+1})=u_{ij}v_{i+1j}(-u^{-1}_{ij+1}\delta u_{ij+1}u^{-1}_{ij+1})v^{-1}_{ij}=-Ad_{v_{ij}}(\delta u_{ij+1}u^{-1}_{ij+1}),

\displaystyle(d\Phi_{\Delta_{ij}})_{j^{1}y(\Delta_{ij})}

\displaystyle(\delta v_{i+1j})=u_{ij}\delta v_{i+1j}u^{-1}_{ij+1}v^{-1}_{ij}=u_{ij}\delta v_{i+1j}v^{-1}_{i+1j}u^{-1}_{ij}=Ad_{u_{ij}}(\delta v_{i+1j}v^{-1}_{i+1j}).

From here, formula (
23
) allows to calculate all the terms of the system of equations (
24
) obtaining:

\left\{\begin{aligned} d\mathit{l}(\cdot,v_{ij})(\delta u_{ij})+\lambda_{\Delta_{ij}}\circ(R_{u^{-1}_{ij}})_{*}(\delta u_{ij})-\lambda_{\Delta_{ij-1}}\circ Ad_{v_{ij-1}}(R_{u^{-1}_{ij}})_{*}(\delta u_{ij})&=0\\
d\mathit{l}(u_{ij},\cdot)(\delta v_{ij})-\lambda_{\Delta_{ij}}\circ(R_{v^{-1}_{ij}})_{*}(\delta v_{ij})+\lambda_{\Delta_{i-1j}}\circ Ad_{u_{i-1j}}(R_{v^{-1}_{ij}})_{*}(\delta v_{ij})&=0\end{aligned}\right.

By transposition and translating to the identity element
e\in G
by
R_{u_{ij}}
and
R_{v_{ij}}
respectively we obtain the system of equations:

(25)

\left\{\begin{aligned} (R_{u_{ij}})^{*}\big(d\mathit{l}(\cdot,v_{ij})\big)+\lambda_{\Delta_{ij}}-Ad^{*}_{v_{ij-1}}\lambda_{\Delta_{ij-1}}&=0\\
(R_{v_{ij}})^{*}\big(d\mathit{l}(u_{ij},\cdot)\big)-\lambda_{\Delta_{ij}}+Ad^{*}_{u_{i-1j}}\lambda_{\Delta_{i-1j}}&=0\end{aligned}\right.

Under these conditions we have the following:

Theorem 4.4
.

If
(y,\lambda)\in\Gamma(\mathcal{U}_{0},Y)\times\textrm{Map}(\mathcal{U},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
is a section-mapping, with
y
admissible, solution of equations (
25
) of the Lagrange problem with Lagrangian density
\mathit{l}:Y=J^{1}P/G\to\mathbb{R}
and constraint morphism
\Phi:J^{1}Y\to G
given by formula (
19
), then its component
y
is a solution of the Euler-Poincaré equations (
21
).

Proof.

Passing the multipliers to the second member in (
25
) we have:

(26)

\left\{\begin{aligned} (R_{u_{ij}})^{*}\big(d\mathit{l}(\cdot,v_{ij})\big)=&-\lambda_{\Delta_{ij}}+Ad^{*}_{v_{ij-1}}\lambda_{\Delta_{ij-1}}\\
(R_{v_{ij}})^{*}\big(d\mathit{l}(u_{ij},\cdot)\big)=&\lambda_{\Delta_{ij}}-Ad^{*}_{u_{i-1j}}\lambda_{\Delta_{i-1j}}\end{aligned}\right.

Subtracting
Ad^{*}_{u_{i-1j}}(R_{u_{i-1j}})^{*}\big(d\mathit{l}(\cdot,v_{i-1j})\big)
in each member of the first equation and
Ad^{*}_{v_{ij-1}}(R_{v_{ij-1}})^{*}\big(d\mathit{l}(u_{ij-1},\cdot)\big)
in the second one, and then adding both expressions then the left-hand side of the new equality will be:

\displaystyle(R_{u_{ij}})^{*}\big(d\mathit{l}(\cdot,v_{ij})\big)-

\displaystyle Ad^{*}_{u_{i-1j}}(R_{u_{i-1j}})^{*}\big(d\mathit{l}(\cdot,v_{i-1j})\big)+(R_{v_{ij}})^{*}\big(d\mathit{l}(u_{ij},\cdot)\big)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle Ad^{*}_{v_{ij-1}}(R_{v_{ij-1}})^{*}\big(d\mathit{l}(u_{ij-1},\cdot)\big)

which, taking into account the identities
Ad^{*}_{u_{i-1j}}R^{*}_{u_{i-1j}}=L^{*}_{u_{i-1j}}
and
Ad^{*}_{v_{ij-1}}R^{*}_{v_{ij-1}}=L^{*}_{v_{ij-1}}
, is precisely the first member of the Euler-Poincaré equations (
21
).

As for the right-hand side of this new equation, applying again (
26
) we get:

\displaystyle\Big(

\displaystyle-\lambda_{\Delta_{ij}}+Ad^{*}_{v_{ij-1}}\lambda_{\Delta_{ij-1}}-Ad^{*}_{u_{i-1j}}\big(-\lambda_{\Delta_{i-1j}}+Ad^{*}_{v_{i-1j-1}}\lambda_{\Delta_{i-1j-1}}\big)\Big)

\displaystyle+\Big(\lambda_{\Delta_{ij}}-Ad^{*}_{u_{i-1j}}\lambda_{\Delta_{i-1j}}-Ad^{*}_{v_{ij-1}}\big(\lambda_{\Delta_{ij-1}}-Ad^{*}_{u_{i-1j-1}}\lambda_{\Delta_{i-1j-1}}\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(Ad^{*}_{v_{ij-1}}\circ Ad^{*}_{u_{i-1j-1}}-Ad^{*}_{u_{i-1j}}\circ Ad^{*}_{v_{i-1j-1}})\lambda_{\Delta_{i-1j-1}}

However, since the section
y
is admissible, it satisfies the constraint

u_{i-1j-1}v_{ij-1}=v_{i-1j-1}u_{i-1j},

and from here we obtain:

\displaystyle Ad^{*}_{v_{ij-1}}\circ Ad^{*}_{u_{i-1j-1}}=

\displaystyle(Ad_{u_{i-1j-1}}\circ Ad_{v_{ij-1}})^{*}=Ad^{*}_{u_{i-1j-1}v_{ij-1}}=Ad^{*}_{v_{i-1j-1}u_{i-1j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Ad^{*}_{u_{i-1j}}\circ Ad^{*}_{v_{i-1j-1}}.

Thus, this right-hand side vanishes, and the result can be concluded.
∎

Reciprocally, in an analogous way to the continuous case
[
3
]
, the following result is verified.

Theorem 4.5
.

If
y\in\Gamma(\mathcal{U}_{0},Y)
is an admissible section solution of the Euler-Poincaré equations (
21
), then locally there exists a non-unique mapping
\lambda\in\textrm{Map}(\mathcal{U},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
such that
(y,\lambda)
is a solution of equations (
25
) of the Lagrange problem.

Proof.

For each vertex
(i,j)\in\operatorname{int}\mathcal{U}
, choosing arbitrarily
\lambda_{\Delta_{ij}}
, you can solve uniquely
\lambda_{\Delta_{i-1j}}
and
\lambda_{\Delta_{ij-1}}
in the system of equations (
25
) over the spherical neighborhood
S_{(i,j)}=\{\Delta_{ij},\Delta_{i-1j},\Delta_{ij-1}\}
of
(i,j)
, due to the isomorphisms
Ad^{*}_{u_{i-1j}}
and
Ad^{*}_{v_{ij-1}}
respectively.
∎

5.
Example: discrete harmonic mappings from the discrete plane into the Lie group
SO(n)

For this example,
P=SO(n)\times V_{0}\to V_{0}
and the reduced Lagrangian density
\mathit{l}:(Y=J^{1}P/SO(n)=SO(n)\times SO(n)\times V_{0}\to V_{0})\to\mathbb{R}
is given by the formula

(27)

\mathit{l}\big((u_{ij},v_{ij}),(i,j)\big)=\operatorname{tr}u_{ij}+\operatorname{tr}v_{ij}

where
SO(n)
is considered immersed in the standard way in the space of the square matrices
M_{n}(\mathbb{R})

[
7
]
.

If
(a_{kl})
and
(b_{kl})
are two copies of the usual local coordinates of
M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
coordinating, respectively, the components
u_{ij}\in SO(n)\subset M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
and
v_{ij}\in SO(n)\subset M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
, then the Lagrangian density (
27
) is expressed as:

(28)

\mathit{l}=\sum_{i^{\prime}=1}^{n}a_{i^{\prime}i^{\prime}}(u_{ij})+b_{i^{\prime}i^{\prime}}(v_{ij}).

On the other hand, the Lie algebra of
SO(n)
has as basis the tangent vectors
E_{kl}=\big(\frac{\partial}{\partial a_{kl}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial a_{lk}}\big)_{e}
,
k<l
, for the coordinates
(a_{kl})
and
E_{kl}=\big(\frac{\partial}{\partial b_{kl}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial b_{lk}}\big)_{e}
,
k<l
, for the coordinates
(b_{kl})
.

Under these conditions, the four terms of the Euler-Poincaré equations (
21
) can be calculated as follows.

Given
u_{ij}\in SO(n)
, the equations of the translation
R_{u_{ij}}:a\in SO(n)\mapsto au_{ij}
in the coordinates
(a_{kl})
are:
a_{st}\circ R_{u_{ij}}=\sum_{v}a_{vt}(u_{ij})a_{sv}
from where:

\displaystyle\big((R_{u_{ij}})_{*,e}E_{kl}\big)a_{st}=

\displaystyle E_{kl}(a_{st}\circ R_{u_{ij}})=\big(\frac{\partial}{\partial a_{kl}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial a_{lk}}\big)_{e}\big(\sum_{v}a_{vt}(u_{ij})a_{sv}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{kj}a_{lt}(u_{ij})-\delta_{ls}a_{kt}(u_{ij}).

From this it follows:

(29)

\big((R_{u_{ij}})_{*,e}E_{kl}\big)=\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{n}a_{lj^{\prime}}(u_{ij})\frac{\partial}{\partial a_{kj^{\prime}}}-a_{kj^{\prime}}(u_{ij})\frac{\partial}{\partial a_{lj^{\prime}}}.

Then, taking into account (
28
) and (
29
) we have:

\displaystyle\big(R^{*}_{u_{ij}}d\mathit{l}(\cdot,v_{ij})\big)_{e}E_{kl}=

\displaystyle\big(d\mathit{l}(\cdot,v_{ij})\big)_{u_{ij}}\big((R_{u_{ij}})_{*,e}E_{kl}\big)=\big((R_{u_{ij}})_{*,e}E_{kl}\big)\mathit{l}(\cdot,v_{ij})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{n}a_{lj^{\prime}}(u_{ij})\frac{\partial}{\partial a_{kj^{\prime}}}-a_{kj^{\prime}}(u_{ij})\frac{\partial}{\partial a_{lj^{\prime}}}\Big)\big(\sum_{i^{\prime}=1}^{n}a_{i^{\prime}i^{\prime}}+b_{i^{\prime}i^{\prime}}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{lk}(u_{ij})-a_{kl}(u_{ij}).

Proceeding analogously with the other terms of equations (
21
) we obtain:

\displaystyle\big(

\displaystyle L^{*}_{u_{i-1j}}d\mathit{l}(\cdot,v_{i-1j})\big)_{e}E_{kl}=a_{lk}(u_{i-1j})-a_{kl}(u_{i-1j}),

\displaystyle\big(

\displaystyle R^{*}_{v_{ij}}d\mathit{l}(u_{ij},\cdot)\big)_{e}E_{kl}=b_{lk}(v_{ij})-b_{kl}(v_{ij}),

\displaystyle\big(

\displaystyle L^{*}_{v_{ij-1}}d\mathit{l}(u_{ij-1},\cdot)\big)_{e}E_{kl}=b_{lk}(v_{ij-1})-b_{kl}(v_{ij-1}).

Then, equations (
21
) applied to the elements
E_{kl}
,
k<l
of the Lie algebra of
SO(n)
are:

\displaystyle\big(a_{lk}(u_{ij})-a_{kl}(u_{ij})\big)-

\displaystyle\big(a_{lk}(u_{i-1j})-a_{kl}(u_{i-1j})\big)+\big(b_{lk}(v_{ij})-b_{kl}(v_{ij})\big)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\big(b_{lk}(v_{ij-1})-b_{kl}(v_{ij-1})\big)=0

or equivalently, with the notation of
a^{t}
for the transposition of
a
,

a_{kl}(u^{t}_{ij}-u_{ij}+u_{i-1j}-u^{t}_{i-1j})+b_{kl}(v^{t}_{ij}-v_{ij}+v_{ij-1}-v^{t}_{ij-1})=0.

In other words, equations (23) are equivalent to the fact that, for all
k<l
, the
(k,l)
element of the matrix
u^{t}_{ij}-u_{ij}+u_{i-1j}-u^{t}_{i-1j}
coincides with the
(k,l)
element of the matrix
-(v^{t}_{ij}-v_{ij}+v_{ij-1}-v^{t}_{ij-1})
. This, by virtue of the skew-symmetry of both matrices, is equivalent to this equality being verified for every
(k,l)
element. Thus, the Euler-Poincaré equations (
21
) of our example are:

u_{ij}+v_{ij}-u_{i-1j}-v_{ij-1}=u^{t}_{ij}+v^{t}_{ij}-u^{t}_{i-1j}-v^{t}_{ij-1},

which were obtained in
[
7
]
by another procedure.

Applying again the above calculations to equations (
25
) of the corresponding problem of Lagrange, they can be expressed as follows:

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\lambda_{\Delta_{ij}}+Ad^{*}_{v_{ij-1}}\lambda_{\Delta_{ij-1}}&=\sum_{k<l}\big(a_{lk}(u_{ij})-a_{kl}(u_{ij})\big)E^{*}_{kl}\\
\lambda_{\Delta_{ij}}-Ad^{*}_{u_{i-1j}}\lambda_{\Delta_{i-1j}}&=\sum_{k<l}\big(b_{lk}(v_{ij})-b_{kl}(v_{ij})\big)E^{*}_{kl}\end{aligned}\right.

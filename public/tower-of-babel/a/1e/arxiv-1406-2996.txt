Stochastic Mappings and Random Distribution Fields. A Correlation Approach
Păstorel Gaşpar, Lorena Popa

Abstract
. This paper contains a study of multivariate second order stochastic mappings indexed by an abstract set Λ \Lambda in close connection to their operator covariance functions. The characterizations of the normal Hilbert module or of Hilbert spaces associated to such a multivariate second order stochastic mapping in terms of reproducing kernel structures are given, aiming not only to gather into a unified way some concepts from the field, but also to indicate an instrument for extending the very well elaborated theory of multivariate second order stochastic processes (or random fields) to the case of multivariate second order random distribution fields, including multivariate second order stochastic measures. In particular a general Wold type decomposition is extended and discussed in our framework.

1.
Introduction

The study of stochastic or random processes (i.e. a family of random variables) is nowadays among the important topics in mathematical research.
The family of random variables composing a stochastic process was, naturally, first indexed by the set of integers
{\mathbb{Z}}
and then by the set of real numbers
{\mathbb{R}}
. Further the necessity of simultaneous study of more stochastic processes or to describe more complicated phenomena from technical, natural or social sciences, led to consider the process as consisting of multivariate (finite or infinite dimensional) random variables or even generalized random variables (i.e. continuous linear operators sending the complex random variables into some Hilbert space) and, on the other side, to index the process by
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
and
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
(with
d
a positive integer) or, even by an abstract semigroup or other algebraic-topological structures.

In an attempt to gather the existing frameworks for studying random fields we introduce
multivariate stochastic mappings
as families of
H
-valued random variables indexed by an arbitrary set
\Lambda
(often taken with a topological structure), with
H
an infinite dimensional separable complex Hilbert space.

The plan of the paper runs as follows. In Section 2 the basic concepts regarding the organization of the set of second order
H
-valued random variables into a normal Hilbert
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
-module and some other basic notations are introduced, the outline of the chosen framework and it’s connections to the developments from the specialized literature being mentioned.

Section 3 presents basic results regarding the correlation theory (including a Kolmogorov-type factorization), formulated in the general setting of multivariate second order stochastic mappings, where reproducing kernel techniques are essentially used.

In Section 4 multivariate second order random distribution fields are defined and using the space
{\mathcal{D}}_{d}
of test functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
from distribution theory as index set, the results from Section 3 are applied to obtain a correlation theory for such type of stochastic mappings. A general Wold decomposition in this extended framework ends this Section.

Since, in further developments of the theory of multivariate random distribution fields their subclass of regular multivariate stochastic not necessarily bounded measures on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
play an important role, Section 5 is devoted to transpose the results of Sections 3 and 4 to such measures as stochastic mappings indexed by some
\delta
-rings of Borel sets in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

Finally in Section 6 considerations about next steps in developing the theory in this framework are made.

3.
Correlation theory of multivariate second order stochastic mappings

In this section we consider
(continuous) m.s.o. stochastic mappings
, indexed over an abstract set (topological space)
\Lambda
, which in what follows will be denoted (in both cases) by
\boldsymbol{M}(\Lambda,{\mathscr{H}})
. In analogy to
[
14
, Section 4.1]
we associate to such stochastic mappings
the vector domain, the modular domain, the measurements space and the operator and scalar (cross) covariance functions
. The corresponding
modular and vector domains are then characterized as reproducing kernel structures (a Hilbert module and a Hilbert space) considered in
[
14
, Section 2.4]
, reproduced by a positive definite
{\mathscr{C}}_{1}(H)
-valued kernel on
\Lambda
, which is just the operator covariance function of the stochastic mapping. Analogously the measurements space will be identified as a Hilbert space reproduced by the scalar covariance function of the stochastic mapping.
Also for a general positive definite
{\mathscr{C}}_{1}(H)
-valued kernel, as considered in

[
14
, Section 2.4]
, a Kolmogorov type factorization in terms of a m.s.o. stochastic mapping is obtained.
Thus, this Section can be regarded as being complementary to Section 2.4. from the book
[
14
]
.

For a given m.s.o.s.m.
\Phi\in\boldsymbol{M}(\Lambda,{\mathscr{H}})
we denote by
{\mathscr{H}}_{(\Phi)}
the closed linear subspace of
L_{s,0}^{2}(\wp,H)
generated by the values
\{\Phi(\mu),\mu\in\Lambda\}
and call it
the vector domain
of
\Phi
, while the
modular domain
of
\Phi
is the closure
{\mathscr{H}}_{\Phi}
in
L_{s,0}^{2}(\wp,H)
of the submodule

(3.1)

{\mathscr{H}}^{0}_{\Phi}:\ =\left\{\sum\limits_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{m}}a_{i}\Phi(\lambda_{i})\,,a_{i}\in{\mathcal{B}}(H),\lambda_{i}\in\Lambda,i\in\mathbb{N}_{m},m\in\mathbb{N}\right\},

where
{\mathbb{N}}_{m}=\{1,2,\dots,m\}
. Obviously,
{\mathscr{H}}_{(\Phi)}\subset{\mathscr{H}}_{\Phi}\subset{\mathscr{H}}
. These terms can serve also to introduce in
\boldsymbol{M}(\Lambda,{\mathscr{H}})
the relation of subordination, which will be useful in the general Wold decomposition. Namely, we shall say that
\Phi
is
subordinate (operator subordinate)
to
\Psi
if
{\mathscr{H}}_{(\Phi)}\subset{\mathscr{H}}_{(\Psi)}
(
{\mathscr{H}}_{\Phi}\subset{\mathscr{H}}_{\Psi}
, respectively).

Now, using the operator model of the normal Hilbert
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
-module
{\mathscr{H}}_{\Phi}
(see Corr. 7 pp. 30 of
[
14
]
) and denoting
G_{\Phi}:=G_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\Phi}}

\Phi

(3.2)

{\mathscr{H}}_{\Phi}\cong\mathscr{C}_{2}(G_{\Phi},H)=H\widehat{\otimes}_{2}\overline{G}_{\Phi}\cong H\widehat{\otimes}G_{\Phi},

where

is the hilbertian tensor product of the Hilbert spaces
H
and
G
as in
[
14
]
pp.20.

Also for two elements
\Phi,\Psi\in\boldsymbol{M}(\Lambda,{\mathscr{H}})
the

operator cross covariance function

\Gamma_{\Phi,\Psi}
will be defined as

(3.3)

\Gamma_{\Phi,\Psi}(\lambda,\mu):=\left[\Phi(\lambda),\Psi(\mu)\right]_{{\mathscr{H}}};\quad\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda,

while the
scalar cross covariance function

is defined by

\gamma_{\Phi,\Psi}(\lambda,\mu):=\mathrm{tr}\Gamma_{\Phi,\Psi}(\lambda,\mu);\quad\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda.

When
\Phi=\Psi

, respectively the

Now, the operator covariance function
\Gamma_{\Phi}
(the scalar one
\gamma_{\Phi}
, respectively) of

is a

-valued (

-valued)

\sum\limits_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}_{m}}a_{i}\Gamma_{\Phi}(\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j})a_{j}^{\ast}\geq 0,

for any

and any finite systems

\sum\limits_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}_{m}}\alpha_{i}\overline{\alpha}_{j}\gamma_{\Phi}(\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j})\geq 0

for any

and any finite systems
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}\in\mathbb{C},\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m}\in\Lambda)

Indeed the relation (

\sum\limits_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}_{m}}a_{i}\Gamma_{\Phi}(\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j})a_{j}^{\ast}=\sum\limits_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}_{m}}a_{i}\left[\Phi(\lambda_{i}),\Phi(\lambda_{j})\right]_{{\mathscr{H}}}a_{j}^{\ast}=\\
\sum\limits_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}_{m}}\left[a_{i}\Phi(\lambda_{i}),a_{j}\Phi(\lambda_{j})\right]_{{\mathscr{H}}}=\left[\sum\limits_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{m}}a_{i}\Phi(\lambda_{i}),\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{m}}a_{j}\Phi(\lambda_{j})\right]_{{\mathscr{H}}}\geqslant 0,

\Gamma\in\mathbf{\Gamma}\left(\Lambda,\mathscr{C}_{1}(H)\right)
.

Theorem 3.3
.

(i)

If
\Lambda
is an arbitrary set (a topological space), then for any positive definite
\mathscr{C}_{1}(H)
- valued kernel
\Gamma
on
\Lambda
(continuous on
\Lambda\times\Lambda
), there exists a (continuous) multivariate second order stochastic mapping
\Phi:\Lambda\rightarrow{\mathscr{H}}
, which assures for
\Gamma
a Kolmogorov factorization:

(3.11)

\Gamma(\lambda,\mu)=[\Phi(\lambda),\Phi(\mu)]_{{\mathscr{H}}}\ ;\ \lambda,\mu\in\Lambda,

i.e.
\Gamma
coincides with the operator covariance function
\Gamma_{\Phi}
of
\Phi
.

(ii)

If
\Phi_{1}
and
\Phi_{2}
are two (continuous) m.s.o. stochastic mappings on
\Lambda
for which
\Gamma_{\Phi_{1}}=\Gamma_{\Phi_{2}}
, then
\Phi_{1}
and
\Phi_{2}
are gramian unitary equivalent, i.e. there exists a gramian unitary operator
\mathrm{W}:{\mathscr{H}}_{\Phi_{2}}\rightarrow{\mathscr{H}}_{\Phi_{1}}
such that

(3.12)

\Phi_{1}(\lambda)=\mathrm{W}\Phi_{2}(\lambda)\,,\quad\lambda\in\Lambda.

Proof.

(i) Let
\Gamma
be a positive definite
{\mathscr{C}}_{1}(H)
-valued kernel on
\Lambda
and
{\mathscr{H}}_{\Gamma}
(
G_{\Gamma}
respectively) the normal Hilbert
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
-module (Hilbert space respectively) reproduced by
\Gamma
, generated as in
[
14
]
Sec.2.4,Thm.13,pp.37 or Prop.23,pp.44, with the gramian (respectively the scalar product) defined there. Since, by Thm. 2.4., pp. 45 of
[
14
]
,
{\mathscr{C}}_{2}(G_{\Gamma},H)
is an operatorial model for
{\mathscr{H}}_{\Gamma}
, embedding
G_{\Gamma}
into a space of the form
L^{2}_{0}(\wp)
(see
[
18
]
)
{\mathscr{H}}_{\Gamma}
embeds into a normal Hilbert
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
-module of the form
\mathscr{C}_{2}\left(L^{2}_{0}(\wp),H\right)
.

In this way the
{\mathscr{H}}_{\Gamma}
-valued function defined by

\Phi(\lambda):=\Gamma(\lambda,\cdot),\ (\lambda\in\Lambda)

gives us exactly the m.s.o. stochastic mapping we seek, since we get for any
\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda

\Gamma_{\Phi}(\lambda,\mu)=\left[\Phi(\lambda),\Phi(\mu)\right]_{{\mathscr{H}}}=\left[\Gamma(\lambda,\cdot),\Gamma(\mu,\cdot)\right]_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\Gamma}}=\Gamma(\lambda,\mu).

(ii) From the hypothesis on
\Phi_{1}
and
\Phi_{2}
we have

\left[\Phi_{1}(\lambda),\Phi_{1}(\mu)\right]_{{\mathscr{H}}}=\left[\Phi_{2}(\lambda),\Phi_{2}(\mu)\right]_{{\mathscr{H}}},\quad\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda,

which means that the mapping
\mathrm{W}
defined on
\left\{\Phi_{2}(\mu),\mu\in\Lambda\right\}
by

\mathrm{W}\Phi_{2}(\mu)=\Phi_{1}(\mu)\,,\quad\mu\in\Lambda

preserves the gramian, is easily extended by
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
-linearity, still conserving the gramian and is clearly surjective between the
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
-modules
{\mathscr{H}}_{\Phi_{2}}^{0}
and
{\mathscr{H}}_{\Phi_{1}}^{0}
(see (
3.1
)). This allows the extension of
{\mathrm{W}}
by continuity to a gramian unitary operator from
{\mathscr{H}}_{\Phi_{2}}
to
{\mathscr{H}}_{\Phi_{1}}
, satisfying (
3.12
).
∎

Remark 3.1
.

Analogous results, formulated in a Hilbert space setting, hold for the scalar correlation mapping

(3.13)

\boldsymbol{M}(\Lambda,{\mathscr{H}})\ni\Phi\mapsto\gamma_{\Phi}\in\boldsymbol{\gamma}(\Lambda),

where
\boldsymbol{\gamma}(\Lambda)
is the set of complex valued positive definite kernels on
\Lambda
.

4.
Multivariate second order random distribution fields and their covariance distributions

In this Section we illustrate how the results from the previous Section can be applied to the case of multivariate second order (m.s.o.) random distribution fields.

For completeness we shall work with the
m
-order
(0\leqslant m\leqslant\infty)
m.s.o. random distribution field

U
, which is a continuous linear m.s.o stochastic mapping having the index set
\Lambda={\mathcal{D}}_{d}^{m}
, the space of complex valued compactly supported continuously derivable functions up to the order
m\,(m=0,1,2,\ldots,\infty)
. In other words
U\in\big({\mathcal{D}}^{m}_{d}\big)^{\prime}({\mathscr{H}}):={\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{D}}_{d}^{m},{\mathscr{H}})
, i.e. it is a
{\mathscr{H}}
-valued
m
-order distribution on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(see also
[
21
]
). For
m=0
,
U
will be a (
{\mathscr{H}}
-valued) m.s.o. random Radon measure and for
m=\infty
, the index
m
and the “
m
-order” will be omitted, otherwise
U
will be called of
finite
m
-order
.

Now for
U,V\in\big({\mathcal{D}}^{m}_{d}\big)^{\prime}({\mathscr{H}})
the scalar cross covariance function
\gamma_{U,V}
and the operator cross covariance function
\Gamma_{U,V}
are defined by (
3.4
) and (
3.3
).
We shall comment only the properties of
\Gamma_{U,V}
, which – from the linearity and continuity of
U
and
V
– is sesquilinear and continuous on
{\mathcal{D}}_{d}^{m}\times{\mathcal{D}}_{d}^{m}
. Moreover
\Gamma_{U}
is obviously positive definite, i.e. it satisfies a relation of the form (
3.5
).

It is possible as in Theorems
3.1
and
3.3
to describe and to characterize the m.s.o. random distribution fields, their modular and vector domains and measurements space in terms of such kernels. We prefer but to do that by using everywhere a distributional framework, where instead of
{\mathscr{C}}_{1}(H)
-valued sesquilinear continuous kernels on
{\mathcal{D}}_{d}^{m}\times{\mathcal{D}}_{d}^{m}
, we use the so-called distribution kernels on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
in the sense of L. Schwartz (see
[
20
, I, pp. 138]
), which are
m
- order distributions on
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
.
Therefore we need the following Lemma, where we identify
{\mathcal{D}}_{2d}^{m}
with the inductive tensor product
{\mathcal{D}}_{d}^{m}\otimes_{i}{\mathcal{D}}_{d}^{m}
(see for example
[
10
, pp. 84]
).

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a
{\mathscr{C}}_{1}(H)
-valued sesquilinear kernel on
{\mathcal{D}}_{d}^{m}
, which is continuous on the diagonal of
{\mathcal{D}}_{d}^{m}\times{\mathcal{D}}_{d}^{m}
. Then there is an
m
-order distribution kernel
C_{\Gamma}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that

(4.1)

C_{\Gamma}(\varphi\otimes\psi)=\Gamma(\varphi,\bar{\psi}),\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\mathcal{D}}_{d}^{m}.

Moreover, when
\Gamma
is positive on the diagonal, then
C_{\Gamma}
is a positive definite
m
-order distribution kernel.

Proof.

First, from the hypothesis, it is easily seen that the kernel
\Gamma
is continuous on
{\mathcal{D}}_{d}^{m}\times{\mathcal{D}}_{d}^{m}
and when
\Gamma(\varphi,\varphi)\geq 0,\ \varphi\in{\mathcal{D}}_{d}^{m}
, then
\Gamma
is also positive definite. We shall attach an
m
-order
\mathscr{C}_{1}(H)
- valued distribution
C_{\Gamma}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
as follows. Define first
C_{\Gamma}
on the elementary tensors
\varphi\otimes\psi
from
{\mathcal{D}}^{m}_{d}\otimes{\mathcal{D}}^{m}_{d}
by

C_{\Gamma}(\varphi\otimes\psi):=\Gamma(\varphi,\overline{\psi})
,
extend that by linearity, and then
by continuity to the whole
{\mathcal{D}}^{m}_{d}\widehat{\otimes}_{i}{\mathcal{D}}^{m}_{d}={\mathcal{D}}^{m}_{2d}
preserving the notation
C_{\Gamma}
.

So
C_{\Gamma}\in\big({\mathcal{D}}^{m}_{2d}\big)^{\prime}(\mathscr{C}_{1}(H))
, which, when
\Gamma
is positive on the diagonal, will be a positive definite distribution kernel on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. We shall refer to this as
C_{\Gamma}\in pd({\mathcal{D}}^{m}_{2d})^{\prime}\big({\mathscr{C}}_{1}(H)\big)
.
∎

Since, for each
U,V\in\big({\mathcal{D}}^{m}_{d}\big)^{\prime}({\mathscr{H}})
the operator cross covariance functions
\Gamma_{U,V}
,
\Gamma_{U}
satisfy the hypotheses from Lemma
4.1
, the existence of the distribution kernels
C_{\Gamma_{U,V}}
and
C_{\Gamma_{U}}
is assured, the last one being even positive definite. These will be called the
operator cross covariance distribution
of
U
and
V
, respectively the
operator covariance distribution of
the m.s.o. random distribution field
U
and denoted by
C_{U,V}
, respectively
C_{U}
. Correspondingly
c_{U,V}
defined by
c_{U,V}(\chi):=trC_{U,V}(\chi),\,\chi\in{\mathcal{D}}^{m}_{2d}
, will be called the
scalar cross covariance distribution
of
U
and
V
, respectively
c_{U}=c_{U,U}
the
scalar covariance distribution
of
U
.

By applying Theorem
3.1
, the following description of the domains associated to a m.s.o.r.d.f. in terms of its covariance distribution holds.

Corollary 4.2
.

For a given m.s.o. random distribution field
U\in\big({\mathcal{D}}^{m}_{d}\big)^{\prime}({\mathscr{H}})
, the modular domain
{\mathscr{H}}_{U}
, respectively the measurements space
G_{U}
will be isomorph to the
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
-module
{\mathscr{H}}_{\Gamma^{C_{U}}}
, respectively the Hilbert space
G_{\Gamma^{C_{U}}}
both reproduced by the
\mathscr{C}_{1}(H)
-valued kernel
\Gamma^{C_{U}}
, while the vector domain
{\mathscr{H}}_{(U)}
of
U
is isomorphic to the Hilbert space reproduced by the complex valued kernel
\gamma^{C_{U}}
.

The operator cross covariance distribution together with the operator covariance distributions of two m.s.o.r.d.f. will be used to obtain from Theorem
3.2
the following characterization of their subordination.

Corollary 4.3
.

Given two m.s.o.r.d.f.
U
and
V
then
U
is subordinate to
V
, iff for each fixed
\varphi\in{\mathcal{D}}_{d}
, the function
K_{\varphi}(\cdot)
given by

{\mathcal{D}}_{d}\ni\psi\mapsto C_{U,V}(\varphi\otimes\bar{\psi})\in{\mathscr{C}}_{1}(H)

lies in the
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
-module
{\mathscr{H}}_{\Gamma^{C_{V}}}
and
\bigl[K_{\varphi},K_{\psi}\bigr]_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\Gamma^{C_{V}}}}=C_{U}(\varphi\otimes\bar{\psi})
.

The operator correlation mapping (
3.10
) becomes

(4.2)

({\mathcal{D}}_{d}^{m})^{\prime}({\mathscr{H}})\ni U\mapsto C_{U}\in pd({\mathcal{D}}_{2d}^{m})^{\prime}({\mathscr{C}}_{1}(H)),

which we shall call the
operator covariance distribution mapping
.
Its properties are contained in the following Corollaries.

Corollary 4.4
.

For an
m
-order distribution kernel on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
C\in\big({\mathcal{D}}^{m}_{2d}\big)^{\prime}(\mathscr{C}_{1}(H))
the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

C\in pd\big({\mathcal{D}}^{m}_{2d}\big)^{\prime}(\mathscr{C}_{1}(H))
; i.e. the operatorial kernel
\Gamma^{C}
on
{\mathcal{D}}^{m}_{d}
defined by

\Gamma^{C}(\varphi,\psi):=C(\varphi\otimes\overline{\psi})\ (\varphi,\psi\in{\mathcal{D}}^{m}_{d})

is positive definite.

(ii)

The operatorial kernel
\Gamma^{C}
is sesquilinear and positive (in the sense of positivity from
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
) on the diagonal of
{\mathcal{D}}^{m}_{d}\times{\mathcal{D}}^{m}_{d}
.

(iii)

There is a m.s.o. random distribution field
U\in\big({\mathcal{D}}^{m}_{d}\big)^{\prime}(\mathscr{H})
such that
C=C_{U}
(i.e.
C
has a Kolmogorov type factorization).

The uniqueness (up to a gramian unitary equivalence) of
U
in (iii) of the previous Corollary results now from Theorem
3.3
(ii). More precisely it holds

Corollary 4.5
.

If the m.s.o. random distribution fields
U^{1},\,U^{2}\in\big({\mathcal{D}}^{m}_{d}\big)^{\prime}({\mathscr{H}})
have the same operator covariance distribution, i.e.
C_{U^{1}}=C_{U^{2}}
, then there exists a gramian unitary operator
W:{\mathscr{H}}_{U^{1}}\rightarrow{\mathscr{H}}_{U^{2}}
such that

(4.3)

W\,U^{1}_{\varphi}=U^{2}_{\varphi},\quad\varphi\in{\mathcal{D}}^{m}_{d}.

It would be of course interesting to know
if each
{\mathscr{C}}_{1}(H)
-valued distribution kernel on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is the operator cross covariance distribution of some pair
U,V\in({\mathcal{D}}_{d}^{m})^{\prime}({\mathscr{H}})
, but this won’t be our goal here.

Now we shall introduce in our general frame the concepts of determinism and nondeterminism and we give a general decomposition of Wold type of a m.s.o.r.d.f.,
U
, into deterministic and purely nondeterministic parts, both summands being operator subordinate to
U
.

In
[
19
]
(see also
[
1
]
), the observable space up to the moment
t_{0}
for a random distribution for the case
d=1
and
H=\mathbb{C}
was defined. By using in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
the ordering relation
s=(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})\leq t=(t_{1},\dots,t_{d})
if
s_{j}\leq t_{j},\ j=1,\dots,d
, we shall define by analogy these observable structures for m.s.o.r.d.f. up to the moment
t_{0}
.

To this purpose we introduce first the subspace
{\mathcal{D}}_{d}^{t_{0}}
in
{\mathcal{D}}_{d}
by

(4.4)

\varphi\in{\mathcal{D}}_{d}^{t_{0}}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\textrm{supp}\varphi\subset\left\{t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,:\,t\leqslant t_{0}\right\}.

Thus, for a m.s.o.r.d.f.
U=\{U_{\varphi}\}_{\varphi\in{\mathcal{D}}_{d}^{m}}
we call
the observable module

{\mathscr{H}}_{U}^{t_{0}}
(
the observable space

{\mathscr{H}}_{(U)}^{t_{0}}
)
up to the moment
t_{0}
, the closed
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
-module (space) in
{\mathscr{H}}
, generated by the set

\left\{U_{\varphi}\quad,\,\varphi\in{\mathcal{D}}_{d}^{t_{0}}\right\}
.

We notice that the Hilbert
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
-module (Hilbert space) generated by
\bigcup\limits_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\mathscr{H}}_{U}^{t}
(
\bigcup\limits_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\mathscr{H}}_{(U)}^{t}
respectively) is just the modular domain
{\mathscr{H}}_{U}
(vector domain respectively) of the m.s.o.r.d.f.
U
, while
\bigcap\limits_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\mathscr{H}}_{U}^{t}
(
\bigcap\limits_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\mathscr{H}}_{(U)}^{t}
respectively) will be called the
remote past
module (space) of
U
and denoted by
{\mathscr{H}}_{U}^{-\infty}
(
{\mathscr{H}}_{(U)}^{-\infty}
respectively). We shall say that
\varphi\mapsto U_{\varphi}
is
operator deterministic
(or, simply
deterministic
), if
{\mathscr{H}}_{U}={\mathscr{H}}_{U}^{-\infty}
(
{\mathscr{H}}_{(U)}={\mathscr{H}}_{(U)}^{-\infty}
respectively). If
{\mathscr{H}}_{U}^{-\infty}\subsetneqq{\mathscr{H}}_{U}
(
{\mathscr{H}}_{(U)}^{-\infty}\subsetneqq{\mathscr{H}}_{(U)}
respectively) we say that
U
is
operator nondeterministic
(
nondeterministic
).

For extreme situations, where
{\mathscr{H}}_{U}^{-\infty}=\{0\}
, (
{\mathscr{H}}_{(U)}^{-\infty}=\{0\}
respectively), the term
non-deterministic
becomes
purely non-deterministic
(see also
[
32
]
,
[
14
, Sec. 5.1]
for the first, respectively the classical result). In this context we have the following general Wold decomposition for m.s.o.r.d.f.

Theorem 4.6
.

For a m.s.o.r.d.f.
\{U_{\varphi}\}_{\varphi\in{\mathcal{D}}_{d}}
taking values in
{\mathscr{H}}
, there exists a unique decomposition

(4.5)

U_{\varphi}=U_{\varphi}^{det}+U_{\varphi}^{p}\,,\quad\varphi\in{\mathcal{D}}_{d}

of
U
such that

(i)

\{U_{\varphi}^{det}\}_{\varphi\in{\mathcal{D}}_{d}}
is operator deterministic and
\{U_{\varphi}^{p}\}_{\varphi\in{\mathcal{D}}_{d}}
is operator purely nondeterministic,

(ii)

U^{det}
and
U^{p}
are operator subordinate to
U
,

(iii)

U^{det}
and
U^{p}
are operator uncorrelated, i.e. the operator cross covariance distribution
C_{U^{det},U^{p}}
vanishes on
{\mathcal{D}}_{2d}
.

Moreover the gramian orthogonal decomposition

{\mathscr{H}}_{U}={\mathscr{H}}_{U^{det}}\oplus{\mathscr{H}}_{U^{p}}

holds.

Proof.

Since in normal Hilbert
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
-modules, any closed submodule has a gramian projection (Lemma 2, Section 2.2, pp.22
[
14
]
), let
P
be the gramian projection associated to the submodule
{\mathscr{H}}_{U}^{-\infty}
and let

(4.6)

U_{\varphi}^{det}=PU_{\varphi}\quad,\quad U_{\varphi}^{p}=(I-P)U_{\varphi}\,,\quad\varphi\in{\mathcal{D}}_{d}.

One can verify by using standard arguments (as in
[
14
, Section 5.1]
) that (
4.6
) gives us the very m.s.o.r.d.f. we seek, the uniqueness of decomposition (
4.5
) being obtained by direct reasoning.
∎

5.
Multivariate second order stochastic measures and their associated bimeasures

In this Section we shall see how from the theory of m.s.o.r.d.f. we can indeed infer the classical theory of m.s.o. random fields, as well as how to fit in this general theory the special class of m.s.o.r.d.f., namely that of (not necessarily bounded) measures, which is very useful for the spectral representations of random (distribution) fields.

Each very well known continuous m.s.o. random field
F
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(i.e.
F\in\mathcal{E}^{0}_{d}({\mathscr{H}})
) will be identified with the m.s.o. random Radon measure
U^{F}
(i.e. m.s.o. random distribution fields of zero order having as index set
{\mathcal{D}}_{d}^{0}
), defined by

(5.1)

U_{\varphi}^{F}=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(t)F(t)dt,\quad\,\varphi\in{\mathcal{D}}^{0}_{d}.

Moreover, since
F
is bounded on each bounded Borel subset
A
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
(i.e. on each
A\in\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}or({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
), it can be even regarded as a not necessarily bounded regular measure
\xi^{F}
through

(5.2)

\xi^{F}(A)=\int\limits_{A}F(t)dt,\quad A\in\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}or(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

In such a way the locally convex
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
-module
{\mathcal{E}}_{d}^{0}({\mathscr{H}})
is embedded into the class of
multivariate second order stochastic, not necessarily bounded, regular measures
, for which the index set
\Lambda
is the
\delta
-ring
\widetilde{{\mathcal{B}}}or(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Playing an important role in the definitions of various kinds of harmonizability of m.s.o. random distribution fields, they are often supposed to have finite operator semivariation i.e.

(5.3)

\|\xi\|_{o}(A)<\infty,\quad A\in\widetilde{{\mathcal{B}}}or(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where
\|\cdot\|_{o}
is defined as in
[
14
, pp. 56 Def.4 (1)]
, or finite semivariation i.e.

(5.4)

\|\xi\|(A)<\infty,\quad A\in\widetilde{{\mathcal{B}}}or(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where
\|\cdot\|
is defined as in
[
14
, pp.54 Def.1]
, the corresponding classes of m.s.o. stochastic regular measures being denoted by
fosvr\mathcal{M}_{d}({\mathscr{H}})
, or by
fsvr\mathcal{M}_{d}({\mathscr{H}})
, respectively. They are locally convex
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
-modules with the seminorms given by (
5.3
) and (
5.4
) respectively.
However, since
\|\xi\|(A)\leqslant\|\xi\|_{0}(A),\,A\in\widetilde{{\mathcal{B}}}or(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the inclusion
fosvr\mathcal{M}_{d}({\mathscr{H}})\subset\linebreak fsvr\mathcal{M}_{d}({\mathscr{H}})
holds with continuous embedding.

Now an element
\xi\in fsvr\,{\mathcal{M}}_{d}({\mathscr{H}})
is to be regarded as a
{\mathscr{H}}
-valued distribution
U^{\xi}
, which will be even a
{\mathscr{H}}
- valued Radon measure, by putting

(5.5)

U_{\varphi}^{\xi}=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(t)d\xi(t),\quad\varphi\in{\mathcal{D}}_{d}^{0}.

Thus, we have obtained

Proposition 5.1
.

For the above mentioned classes of m.s.o. random distribution fields the following inclusions hold with continuous embedding

(5.6)

{\mathcal{E}}^{0}_{d}({\mathscr{H}})\subset fosvr\,{\mathcal{M}}_{d}({\mathscr{H}})\subset fsvr\,{\mathcal{M}}_{d}({\mathscr{H}})\subset\\
\subset({\mathcal{D}}_{d}^{0})^{\prime}({\mathscr{H}})\subset({\mathcal{D}}_{d}^{m})^{\prime}({\mathscr{H}})\subset{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}_{d}({\mathscr{H}}).

Let’s observe that a m.s.o. stochastic mapping from one of the mentioned classes can have more than one index set. For example, looking to the above identifications:

\xi=\{\xi(A)\}_{A\in\widetilde{\mathscr{B}}or({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\ \text{ with }\ U^{\xi}=\{U^{\xi}_{\varphi}\}_{\varphi\in{\mathcal{D}}_{d}^{0}},

respectively

F=\{F(t)\}_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\ \text{ with }\ \xi^{F}=\{\xi^{F}(A)\}_{A\in\widetilde{\mathscr{B}}or({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\ \text{ and }\ U^{F}=U^{\xi^{F}}

it is not difficult to see that

{\mathscr{H}}_{\xi}={\mathscr{H}}_{U^{\xi}},\ \text{ respectively }\ {\mathscr{H}}_{F}={\mathscr{H}}_{\xi^{F}}={\mathscr{H}}_{U^{F}},

analogous relations being true for the corresponding vector domains and measurements spaces.
It is of interest how those classes of positive definite kernels which are co-domains of the “restrictions” of the operator covariance distribution mapping (
4.2
) to the submodules from (
5.6
) can be suitable described.
Let’s mention that for the operator covariance distribution of an element
\xi\in fsvr{\mathcal{M}}_{d}({\mathscr{H}})
regarded as a m.s.o. random distribution field we naturally use the notation
\Gamma_{U^{\xi}}
, while if
\xi
is regarded as a m.s.o. stochastic mapping on

\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}or({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, then its operator covariance function
\Gamma_{\xi}
represents a positive definite regular bimeasure on
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}or({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\times\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}or({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, for which we shall use also the notation
\tau_{\xi}
. It will be also called the
operator covariance bimeasure
associated to the m.s.o stochastic measure
\xi
.

For the bimeasures on
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}or({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\times\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}or({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and their semivariation or operator semivariation we adopt analogue definitions as in
[
14
]
(Definition 9 (1) and (3) pp.62 and Definition 16 pp.65). For the spaces of such (
{\mathscr{C}}_{1}(H)
-valued) regular bimeasures with finite (operator) semivariation we shall use the notation
fsvr\,\mathfrak{M}_{2d}\big({\mathscr{C}}_{1}(H)\big)
(
fosvr\,\mathfrak{M}_{2d}\big({\mathscr{C}}_{1}(H)\big)
, respectively).

However, since in our case, the measure and the bimeasure are defined on a
\delta
-ring, some properties from
[
14
]
do not automatically hold.

The corresponding classes of positive definite bimeasures will be denoted by
fsvr\,\mathfrak{M}_{2d}^{pd}\big({\mathscr{C}}_{1}(H)\big)
and
fosvr\,\mathfrak{M}_{2d}^{pd}\big({\mathscr{C}}_{1}(H)\big)
. So in the above notation
\tau_{\xi}\in f(o)svr{\mathfrak{M}}_{2d}^{pd}({\mathscr{C}}_{1}(H))
.

Let’s also mention that it is not hard to see that a Morse-Transue (MT)
strict integral
as in
[
14
, Section 1.2 pp.5]
can be defined for bimeasures
\tau
on
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}or({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\times\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}or({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. With such a strict MT-integral to each
\tau\in fsvr{\mathfrak{M}}_{2d}({\mathscr{C}}_{1}(H))
we can attach a distribution
C^{\tau}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}
(a distribution kernel on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, in the sense of L. Schwartz), first defined on elementary tensors trough

(5.7)

C^{\tau}(\varphi\otimes\psi)=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(s)\psi(t)d\tau(s,t),\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\mathcal{D}}_{d}^{m},

and then, by the usual extension, to the whole
{\mathcal{D}}^{m}_{2d}
.

On the other hand, each
K\in{\mathcal{E}}^{0}_{2d}\big({\mathscr{C}}_{1}(H)\big)
can be regarded as a regular bimeasure

On geometric invariants of singular plane curves
J. W. Bruce, M. A. C. Fernandes, F. Tari

Abstract
Given a germ of a smooth plane curve ( { f ( x , y ) = 0 } , 0 ) ⊂ ( 𝕂 2 , 0 ) , 𝕂 = ℝ , ℂ (\{f(x,y)=0\},0)\subset(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{K}$}^{2},0),\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{K}$}=\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{R}$},\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$} , with an isolated singularity, we define two invariants I f I_{f} and V f ∈ ℕ ∪ { ∞ } V_{f}\in\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{N}$}\cup\{\infty\} which count the number of inflections and vertices (suitably interpreted in the complex case) concentrated at the singular point; the first is an affine invariant and the second is invariant under similarities of ℝ 2 \mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{R}$}^{2} , and their analogue for ℂ 2 \mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$}^{2} . We show that for almost all representations of f f , in the sense that their complement is of infinite codimension, these invariants are finite. Indeed when the curve has no smooth components they are always finite and bounded and we can be much more explicit about the values they can attain; the set of possible values is of course an analytic invariant of f f . We illustrate our results by computing these invariants for Arnold’s 𝒦 {\cal K} -simple singularities as well as singularities that have 𝒜 {\cal A} -simple parametrisations. We also obtain a relationship between these invariants, the Milnor number of f f and the contact of the curve germ with its ‘osculating circle’.

Introduction

Suppose given a germ of a plane curve with an isolated singularity. The plane can be endowed with an affine, Euclidean (real plane) or Hermitian (complex plane) structure, and it is natural to ask what geometry emerges from the singularity when the curve is deformed. In this paper we count the number of inflections and vertices ‘concentrated’ at the singular point.

Inflections and vertices of plane curves capture key aspects of their geometry.
Inflections are classical objects of study in plane projective geometry: the inflections of a nonsingular algebraic plane curve
F=0
are the points of intersection of the curve with its Hessian
H(F)=0
so that generically a projective curve of degree
d
has
3d(d-2)
inflections. Plucker’s equations then indicate amongst other things how this number may change as the curve acquires singular points.
Viro
[
16
]
extended Plucker’s formula to include the number sextatic points; these are smooth points where the osculating conic intersects the curve with intersection multiplicity
6
or higher.
An upper bound for the number of vertices of a real algebraic curve is also given in
[
16
]
using the complexification of the curve. Vertices here can be defined in terms of the contact of the curve with complex circles: these are the conics which, when projectivised, pass through the ‘circular points at infinity’
[1:\pm i:0]
(
[
16
]
).

While primarily motivated by the real case it is most efficient to start with germs of singular holomorphic curves
f(x,y)=0
in
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$}^{2}
; we denote this germ by
(C_{f},0)
. We define the curvature of a regular curve in
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$}^{2}
endowed with the Hermitian inner product. A vertex of a plane curve turns out to be a point where the curvature has an extremum, and inflections are points where the curvature vanishes, all as in the real case. We define numbers
I_{f}
and
V_{f}
in
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{N}$}\cup\{\infty\}
which count the number of inflections and vertices concentrated at the singular point.
We establish relations between the above numbers and those associated to the irreducible branches of
f
. Given a parametrisation
\gamma
of an irreducible germ
f
, we
establish relations between the number of inflections and vertices of
f
, those of
\gamma
defined in
[
4
]
, and the Milnor number
\mu(f)
of
f
.

We show that any germ
f
with an isolated singularity is
\mathcal{K}
-equivalent to a germ
\tilde{f}

with
I_{\tilde{f}}
and
V_{\tilde{f}}
finite in the real or complex case. In fact the set of representations of
f
for which this is not true is of infinite codimension in the sense of Tougeron, see
[
17
]
, p. 513.
We prove that
f
admits deformations with only ordinary inflections and vertices. In the complex case, when
C_{f}
has no smooth components, we show that
I_{f}
and
V_{f}
are bounded and provide detailed information for the range of values they can attain. As an illustration we determine the values
I_{f}
and
V_{f}
can take when
f
is one of Arnold’s simple singularities as well as the
{\cal A}
-simple singularities
(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$},0)\to(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$}^{2},0)
in
[
2
]
.

One of our results (Theorem
3.9
part (1)) concerning inflections is contained in the work of Wall in
[
20
]
. Just as the computations of
I_{f}
lead to Plucker type formulae and information on the dual as in
[
18
]
, so the calculations on
V_{f}
give results on the singularities of the evolute.

This paper is organized as follows. In §
2
we give some preliminaries and results about vertices of regular complex analytic curves.
In §
3
, we define
I_{f}
and
V_{f}
and give some of their properties.
We calculate in §
4
the range of the values of
I_{f}
and
V_{f}
for curves with simple singularities.
We return to the real case in §
5
, deducing our results from the complex case using finite determinacy results. In what follows all our defining functions
f:(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$}^{2},0)\to(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$},0)
are
{\cal K}
-finite, that is
f
has no repeated factors.

2.1
Vertices and inflections of regular curves

Recall that two regular curves in
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$}^{2}
, one parametrised by
\gamma:U\to\mathbb{\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$}}^{2}
and the other given by the equation
g=0
, with
g:V\to\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$}
,
U\subset\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$}
and
V\subset\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$}^{2}
open, have
k
-point contact
at
t_{0}
,
k\geq 1
, if
\gamma(t_{0})\in V
,
(g\circ\gamma)(t_{0})=0
,
(g\circ\gamma)^{(i)}(t_{0})=0
for
i=1,..,k-1
and
(g\circ\gamma)^{(k)}(t_{0})\neq 0
. They have
at least
k
-point contact
if we drop the last condition.

Inflections of regular holomorphic curves in
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$}^{2}
(or algebraic curves in
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$}\mathbb{P}^{2}
) are points where the tangent line has at least 3-point contact with the curve. An extension of the traditional concept of a vertex to the complex case can also be defined using the contact of the curve with circles, see
[
16
]
. We now give some details about such contact and define the curvature of a regular holomorphic curve.

We suppose that the complex plane
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$}^{2}
is endowed with the Hermitian inner product

\langle{\bf x}_{1},{\bf x}_{2}\rangle=x_{1}\overline{x_{2}}+y_{1}\overline{y_{2}}

for

{\bf x}_{1}=(x_{1},y_{1})
and
{\bf x}_{2}=(x_{2},y_{2})
in
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$}^{2}
.

Let
\gamma(t)=(x(t),y(t))
,
t\in U\subset\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$}
, be a parametrisation of a regular holomorphic curve in
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$}^{2}
, where
U

is an open set in
\mathbb{C}
.
The tangent vector to
\gamma
at
\gamma(t)
is
\gamma^{\prime}(t)=(x^{\prime}(t),y^{\prime}(t))
and a normal vector (with respect to the Hermitian inner product) is one parallel to
\gamma^{\prime}(t)^{\perp}=(-\overline{y^{\prime}(t)},\overline{x^{\prime}(t)})
.

The tangent line of
\gamma
at
t_{0}
is
-y^{\prime}(t_{0})(x-x(t_{0}))+x^{\prime}(t_{0})(y-y(t_{0}))=0
, so
t_{0}
is an inflection point of
\gamma
if and only if

(x^{\prime}y^{\prime\prime}-y^{\prime}x^{\prime\prime})(t_{0})=0.

A complex circle in
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$}^{2}
is a conic with equation

(x-a)^{2}+(y-b)^{2}=c^{2},

where
a,b,c\in\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$}
(see
[
11
]
for more on complex circles); projectivised these are the conics passing through the circular points at infinity
(1:\pm i:0)
. We shall refer to
{p}=(a,b)
as the centre of the circle and
c
as its radius. The equation of a circle can also be written as

\langle{\bf x}-p,\overline{{\bf x}-p}\rangle=c^{2},

where
\overline{\bf x}=(\overline{x},\overline{y})
.

The contact of the curve
\gamma
at
t_{0}
with circles of centre
{p}=(a,b)
passing through
\gamma(t_{0})
is measured by the singularities of the (contact) function

d_{p}(t)=(x(t)-a)^{2}+(y(t)-b)^{2},

that is, by the order of the function
d_{p}
at
t_{0}
. We have

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\frac{1}{2}d^{\prime}_{p}(t_{0})&=&x^{\prime}(t_{0})(x(t_{0})-a)+y^{\prime}(t_{0})(y(t_{0})-b),\\
\frac{1}{2}d^{\prime\prime}_{p}(t_{0})&=&x^{\prime\prime}(t_{0})(x(t_{0})-a)+y^{\prime\prime}(t_{0})(y(t_{0})-b)+x^{\prime}(t_{0})^{2}+y^{\prime}(t_{0})^{2},\\
\frac{1}{2}d^{\prime\prime\prime}_{p}(t_{0})&=&x^{\prime\prime\prime}(t_{0})(x(t_{0})-a)+y^{\prime\prime\prime}(t_{0})(y(t_{0})-b)+3x^{\prime}(t_{0})x^{\prime\prime}(t_{0})+3y^{\prime}(t_{0})y^{\prime\prime}(t_{0}).\end{array}

It follows that
d^{\prime}_{p}(t_{0})=0
if and only if

x(t_{0})-a=-\lambda y^{\prime}(t_{0})
and
y(t_{0})-b=\lambda x^{\prime}(t_{0})
for some
\lambda\in\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$}
.
This means that the circle is tangent to the curve at
t_{0}
if and only if
{p}=(a,b)

belongs to the line parallel to
\overline{\gamma^{\prime}(t)^{\perp}}
and passing through
\gamma(t_{0})
.
We call such line the
conjugate normal line
to
\gamma
.

Suppose that
t_{0}
is not an inflection point of
\gamma
. Then
d^{\prime}_{p}(t_{0})=d^{\prime\prime}_{p}(t_{0})=0
if and only if

x(t_{0})-a=-\lambda y^{\prime}(t_{0})
and
y(t_{0})-b=\lambda x^{\prime}(t_{0})
with

\lambda=\frac{x^{\prime}(t_{0})^{2}+y^{\prime}(t_{0})^{2}}{(x^{\prime}y^{\prime\prime}-y^{\prime}x^{\prime\prime})(t_{0})}
, equivalently,

{p}=(a,b)=(x(t_{0}),y(t_{0}))+\frac{x^{\prime}(t_{0})^{2}+y^{\prime}(t_{0})^{2}}{(x^{\prime}y^{\prime\prime}-y^{\prime}x^{\prime\prime})(t_{0})}(-y^{\prime}(t_{0}),x^{\prime}(t_{0})).

(1)

We call the circle of centre
{p}
in (
1
) the
osculating complex circle
of
\gamma
at
t_{0}
.

Suppose that
t_{0}
is not an inflection point and
d^{\prime}_{p}(t_{0})=d^{\prime\prime}_{p}(t_{0})=0
. Then
d^{\prime\prime\prime}_{p}(t_{0})=0

if and only if

(x^{\prime\prime\prime}y^{\prime}-y^{\prime\prime\prime}x^{\prime})(x^{\prime 2}+y^{\prime 2})+3(x^{\prime}x^{\prime\prime}+y^{\prime}y^{\prime\prime})(x^{\prime}y^{\prime\prime}-x^{\prime\prime}y^{\prime})=0

(2)

at
t_{0}
.

Let
\gamma(t)=(x(t),y(t))
be a regular holomorphic curve in
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$}^{2}

We say that
\gamma(t_{0})

An inflection
(

(

-point

We can rewrite (
1

\begin{array}[]{rcl}n(t_{0})&=&\frac{1}{\langle\gamma^{\prime}(t_{0})^{\perp},\overline{\gamma^{\prime}(t_{0})^{\perp}}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}}\overline{\gamma^{\prime}(t_{0})^{\perp}}\\
&=&\frac{1}{\langle\gamma^{\prime}(t_{0}),\overline{\gamma^{\prime}(t_{0})}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}}\overline{\gamma^{\prime}(t_{0})^{\perp}}\\
&=&\frac{1}{(x^{\prime 2}(t_{0})+y^{\prime 2}(t_{0}))^{\frac{1}{2}}}(-y^{\prime}(t_{0}),x^{\prime}(t_{0})).\end{array}

Clearly

where

(3)

Definition 2.2

Let
\gamma(t)=(x(t),y(t))
be a regular holomorphic curve in
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$}^{2}
. We call

\kappa(t)
in
(\ref{eq:curvature})
the curvature of
\gamma
at
t

and the curve parametrised by

e(t)=\gamma(t)+\frac{1}{\kappa(t)}n(t)

.

Proposition 2.3

Let
\gamma(t)=(x(t),y(t))
be a regular holomorphic curve in
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$}^{2}
.

(1)
The curve
\gamma
has an inflection at
t_{0}
if and only if

\kappa(t_{0})=0
. It has a vertex at
t_{0}
if and only if
\kappa^{\prime}(t_{0})=0
.

(2)

\gamma
is a part of a line if and only if
\kappa\equiv 0
.

(3)

\gamma
is a part of a complex circle if and only if
\kappa\equiv const\neq 0
.

(4)
The evolute of
\gamma
is the envelope of its conjugate normal lines. It is singular at
t_{0}
if and only if
t_{0}
is
a vertex of
\gamma
.

(5)
The dual of
\gamma
has an ordinary cusp
(A_{2})
at a point corresponding to a simple inflection.

(6)
The evolute of
\gamma
has an ordinary cusp
(A_{2})
at a point corresponding to a simple vertex which is not an inflection.

The results are elementary and proved in the usual way.

Remarks 2.4

1. Viro
[
16
]
considered unit speed parametrizations, that is those with
\langle{\bf x}^{\prime},\overline{{\bf x}^{\prime}}\rangle=1
. Any regular curve can be re-parametrised in this way and of course then
\kappa=x^{\prime}y^{\prime\prime}-x^{\prime\prime}y^{\prime}
.

2. Ness
[
13
,
14
]
endowed a holomorphic curve in
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$}^{2}
with the metric induced by the Fubini-Study
and studied its Gaussian curvature away from singular points.

3. When
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{K}$}=\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{R}$}
the group preserving the circles is the similarity group. In the complex case, if we projectivise, it is the group preserving the circular points at infinity. We can think of the subgroup fixing those points as the analogue of the orientation preserving transformations, and the coset exchanging them corresponding to orientation reversing transformations. One can check that the group fixing the circular points at infinity and the origin consists of the linear maps
(x,y)\mapsto(ax+by,-bx+ay)
, with
a,b\in\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$}
and
a^{2}+b^{2}\neq 0
.

2.2
Multiplicity and intersection number

Two germs
f_{i}:(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{K}$}^{n},0)\to(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{K}$},0)
, with
i=1,2
and
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{K}$}=\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$}
or
\mathbb{R}
, are said to be
\mathcal{R}
-
equivalent
if there is a germ of a diffeomorphism
h:(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{K}$}^{n},0)\to(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{K}$}^{n},0)
such that
f_{2}=f_{1}\circ h^{-1}
. They are
\mathcal{K}
-
equivalent
if
there exists an invertible germ
A:(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{K}$}^{n},0)\to\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{K}$}
(i.e.,
A(0)\neq 0
) and a germ of a diffeomorphism
h
such that
f_{2}(x)=A(x)(f_{1}\circ h^{-1})(x)
.
Two germs
f_{i}:(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{K}$},0)\to(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{K}$}^{n},0)
, with
i=1,2
, are
\mathcal{A}
-
equivalent
if there are germs of diffeomorphisms
h:(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{K}$},0)\to(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{K}$},0)
and
k:(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{K}$}^{n},0)\to(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{K}$}^{n},0)
such that

f_{2}=k\circ f_{1}\circ h^{-1}
.

If
G
is one of the above groups (
\mathcal{R}
,
\mathcal{K}
,
\mathcal{A}
) a germ
f
is
k
-
G
-
determined
if any germ
g
with
j^{k}g=j^{k}f
is
G
-equivalent to
f
(where
j^{k}f
is the
k
-jet of
f
at the origin, that is its Taylor polynomial of degree
k
without the constant term). A germ
f
is said to be
G
-
finite
if it is
k
-
G
-determined for some
k
. There are algebraic conditions determining if a germ is
G
-finite, discussed in
[
17
]
for example. A germ
f:(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$}^{2},0)\to(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$},0)
is
{\cal K}
-finite if and only if
f
has an isolated singularity at
0
, which is equivalent to
f
having no repeated factors.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{n}
denote the ring of germs of holomorphic functions
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$}^{n},0\to\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$}
. The order of
\rho=\sum a_{k}t^{k}\in\mathcal{O}_{1}
, denoted by
\textrm{ord}(\rho)
, is the smallest positive integer
k>0
for which
a_{k}\neq 0
, so

\textrm{ord}(\rho)=\dim_{\mathbb{\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$}}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{1}}{\mathcal{O}_{1}\langle\rho\rangle},

where
\mathcal{O}_{1}\langle\rho\rangle
is the ideal in
\mathcal{O}_{1}
generated by
\rho
. Clearly,
\textrm{ord}(\rho\sigma)=\textrm{ord}(\rho)+\textrm{ord}(\sigma)
for any
\rho,\sigma\in\mathcal{O}_{1}
. Furthermore, if
\rho(0)=0
then
\textrm{ord}(\rho^{\prime})=\textrm{ord}(\rho)-1
.

As above, a germ of a holomorphic function
f:(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$}^{2},0)\to(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$},0)
with an isolated singularity defines a germ of a holomorphic curve
(C_{f},0)\subset(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$}^{2},0)
. Decomposing
f
into irreducible factors in
\mathcal{O}_{2}
,
f=f_{1}\cdot f_{2}\cdots f_{n}
clearly gives
C_{f}=C_{f_{1}}\cup\cdots\cup C_{f_{n}}
. The curves
C_{f_{i}}
are called
branches
or
components
of
C_{f}
. Furthermore, if we write

f=F_{r}+F_{r+1}+\cdots,

where each
F_{i}
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
i
in the variables
x
and
y
and
F_{r}\neq 0
, then
r
is the
multiplicity
of
f
, denoted by
mult(f)
, and
C_{F_{r}}
is the
tangent cone
of
C_{f}
. We shall also consider parametrisations of curves
\gamma:(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$},0)\to(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$}^{2},0)
; we will ask that these are
{\cal A}
-finite, which reduces to them being smooth injections. Any such map can be written in the form
(t^{m},y(t))
for some positive integer
m
with
\textrm{ord}(y)>m
. If we consider the powers appearing in
y(t)
, the lowest not divisible by
m
is called the
first Puiseux exponent
of
\gamma
. If
\gamma
is singular (that is
\gamma^{\prime}(0)=0
) and
{\cal A}
-finite then the first Puiseux exponent is finite, since
m>1
and otherwise
y(t)=y_{1}(t^{m})
for some
y_{1}
and
\gamma
is not injective. This is clearly an analytic invariant of
\gamma
and of the corresponding defining equation
f
.

Given
f,g\in\mathcal{O}_{2}
the
intersection number
of
f
and
g
, denoted by
m(f,g)
, is the codimension of the ideal
\mathcal{O}_{2}\langle f,g\rangle
in
\mathcal{O}_{2}
, that is,

m(f,g)=\dim_{\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\mathcal{O}_{2}\langle f,g\rangle}.

More details about the intersection multiplicity/index can be found in
[
9
]
. We will use the following properties:

1.
m(f,g)=m(g,f)
;

4.
m(f,g+fh)=m(f,g)
;

2.
m(uf,vg)=m(f,g)
;

5.
m(f\circ\Phi,g\circ\Phi)=m(f,g)
;

3.
m(f,gh)=m(f,g)+m(f,h)
;

where
f,g,h\in\mathcal{O}_{2}
,
u,v\in\mathcal{O}_{2}
are invertible in
\mathcal{O}_{2}
and
\Phi=(\phi_{1},\phi_{2}):(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$}^{2},0)\to(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$}^{2},0)
is a germ of a diffeomorphism. Note that if
f,g
are irreducible then
m(f,g)
is non-finite if and only if
f
and
g
define the same curve. These are all simple consequences of the definition. In fact,
m(f,g)
is the local number of inverses images of a regular value of
(f,g):(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$}^{2},0)\to(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$}^{2},0)
; see
[
1
]
, Chapter 5. The following results are well-known, the proofs can be found in
[
9
]
.

Proposition 2.5

(1)
Suppose that
C_{f}
is an irreducible curve with parametrisation
\gamma:(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$},0)\to(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathbb{C}$}^{2},0)
. Then

. If

is reducible with

.

(2)
Suppose that
C_{f}
is an irreducible curve with parametrisation

with

. Then one can write the equation of the curve in the form

, where

.

(3)
If
f,g
are irreducible and their tangent cones are transverse, then

.

(4)
If
f=0
is smooth and
g
is irreducible then
m(f,g)\leq\beta(g)
where
\beta(g)
is the first Puiseux exponent of
g
.

2.3
Geometric invariants of parametrized plane curves

The fundamental question is: when a germ of a curve is perturbed how many inflections and vertices emerge? It is important to distinguish if we are defining the curve by a parametrisation or an equation since the set of deformations are even topologically different. The parametrised case is dealt with in
[
4
]
.
Given a germ of a parametrised singular curve
\gamma
, one has the expression for its curvature function
\kappa
and its derivative
\kappa^{\prime}
away from the singular point.
In
[
4
]
, the number of inflections
I_{\gamma}
(resp. vertices
V_{\gamma}
) concentrated at the singular points is defined as the multiplicity of the numerator
i_{\gamma}
(resp.
v_{\gamma}
) of
\kappa
(resp.
\kappa^{\prime}
).

We rewrite here some of the results from
[
4
]
.
An inflection at a regular point is where the order of contact of the curve and the tangent line is

at all the pre-images of a regular value near

Energy equality for the isentropic compressible Navier-Stokes equations without upper bound of the density
Yulin Ye, Yanqing Wang, Huan Yu

Abstract
In this paper, we are concerned with the minimal regularity of both the density and the velocity for the weak solutions keeping energy equality in the isentropic compressible Navier-Stokes equations. The energy equality criteria without upper bound of the density are established. Introduction

The classical isentropic compressible Navier-Stokes equations

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\varrho_{t}+\nabla\cdot(\varrho v)=0,\\
&(\varrho v)_{t}+\mathrm{div}\,(\varrho v\otimes v)+\nabla P(\varrho)-\text{div\,}(\mu\mathfrak{D}v)-\nabla(\lambda\text{div\,}v)=0,\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
\varrho
stands for the density of the flow,
v

reprents the flow velocity field and
P(\varrho)=\varrho^{\gamma}
is the scalar pressure;
The viscosity coefficients
\mu
and
\lambda
satisfy
\mu\geq 0
and
2\mu+d\lambda>0
.

\mathfrak{D}v=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla v\otimes\nabla v^{T})
is the stain tensor;
We complement equations (
1.1
) with initial data

\varrho(0,x)=\varrho_{0}(x),\ (\varrho v)(0,x)=(\varrho_{0}v_{0})(x),\ x\in\Omega,

(1.2)

where we define
v_{0}=0
on the sets
\{x\in\Omega:\ \varrho_{0}=0\}.
In the present paper, we consider the periodic case, that is
\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{d}
with dimension
d\geq 2
.

One of the celebrated results of the isentropic compressible Navier-Stokes equations (
1.1
) is the global existence of
the finite energy weak solutions due to Lions
[
18
]
with
\gamma\geq\frac{3d}{d+2}
for
d=2
or
3
, where
d
is the spatial dimension. Subsequently, in
[
10
]
, Feireisl-Novotny-Petzeltová further extended the Lions’ work to
\gamma>\frac{3}{2}
for
d=3
. In
[
13
]
, Jiang and Zhang considered the global existence of weak solutions for the case
\gamma>1

with the spherical symmetric initial data. For the convenience of the reader, we recall the definition of the finite energy weak solutions:

Definition 1.1
.

A pair (
\varrho,v
) is called a weak solution to (
1.1
) with initial data (
1.2
) if (
\varrho,v
) satisfies

(i)

equation (
1.1
) holds in
D^{\prime}(0,T;\Omega)
and

P(\varrho),\varrho|v|^{2}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\Omega)),\ \ \ \nabla v\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)),

(1.3)

(ii)

the density
\varrho
is a renormalized solution of (
1.1
) in the sense of
[
9
]
.

(iii)

the energy inequality holds

\displaystyle E(t)+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(\mu|\nabla v|^{2}+(\mu+\lambda)|\mathrm{div}\,v|^{2}\right)dxdt\leq E(0),

(1.4)

where
E(t)=\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|v|^{2}+\frac{\varrho^{\gamma}}{\gamma-1}\right)dx
.

Note that the finite energy weak solutions constructed in
[
18
,
10
]
satisfy
the energy inequality (
1.4
). A natural question is
how much regularity of weak solutions in
these system are required to ensure energy equality.
In
[
32
]
,
Yu opened the door of research of the Lions-Shinbrot type energy equality criterion
to the weak solutions of the compressible Navier-Stokes equations with both non vacuum and vacuum cases.
The so-called Lions-Shinbrot type criterion is that if
the Leray-Hopf weak solutions
v
of the 3D incompressible Navier-Stokes equations satisfy

v\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{d})),~\text{with}~\frac{2}{p}+\frac{2}{q}=1~\text{and}~q\geq 4,

then the following corresponding energy equality holds

\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}ds=\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Yu’s results in
[
32
]
without vacuum can be formulated as: if a weak solution

(\varrho,v)
of the compressible Navier-Stokes equations (
1.1
) with
\mu=2\varrho
and
\lambda=0

satisfies

\displaystyle\sqrt{\varrho}v\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)),\sqrt{\varrho}\nabla v\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)),

(1.5)

\displaystyle 0<c_{1}\leq\varrho\leq c_{2}<\infty,\ \ \nabla\sqrt{\varrho}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle v\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\Omega))~~\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}\leq\frac{5}{12}\ \text{ and}\ q\geq 6,\sqrt{\varrho_{0}}v_{0}\in L^{4}(\Omega),

then such a weak solution
(\varrho,v)
for any
t\in[0,T]
satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|v|^{2}+\frac{\varrho^{\gamma}}{\gamma-1}\right)dx+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}

\displaystyle\varrho|\mathfrak{D}v|^{2}dxdt=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{0}|v_{0}|^{2}+\frac{\varrho_{0}^{\gamma}}{\gamma-1}\right)dx.

(1.6)

Subsequently, Nguye-Nguye-Tang
[

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\sup_{|h|<\varepsilon}|h|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\varrho(\cdot+h,t)-\varrho(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}<\infty,

One goal of this paper is to analysis the role of the integrability of density
in the energy equality for the weak solutions of the compressible Navier-Stokes equations.
In the case of no vacuum, we formulate our first result as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(\varrho,v)
be a weak solutions in the sense of definition (
1.1

\displaystyle 0<c\leq\varrho\in L^{k}(0,T;L^{l}(\mathbb{T}^{d})),

(1.9)

\displaystyle v\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{d})),\nabla v\in L^{\frac{kp}{k(p-2)-4p}}(0,T;L^{\frac{lq}{l(q-2)-4q}}(\mathbb{T}^{d})),

with
k>\max\{\frac{4p}{p-2},\frac{(\gamma-2)p}{2}\}
,
l>\max\{\frac{4q}{q-2},\frac{(\gamma-2)q}{2}\}
and
k(4-p)+7p\geq 0,\ l(4-q)+7q\geq 0

the energy equality below is valid, for any
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|v|^{2}+\frac{\varrho^{\gamma}}{\gamma-1}\right)dx+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}

\displaystyle\left[\mu|\nabla v|^{2}+(\mu+\lambda)|\mathrm{div}\,v|^{2}\right]dxdt

(1.10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{0}|v_{0}|^{2}+\frac{\varrho_{0}^{\gamma}}{\gamma-1}\right)dx.

.

This theorem is a generalization of recent wroks
[
27
,
21

Remark 1.2

A special case of (

v\in L^{4}(0,T;L^{4}(\mathbb{T}^{d})),

Notations and some auxiliary lemmas

First, we introduce some notations used in this paper.
For
p\in[1,\,\infty]
, the notation
L^{p}(0,\,T;X)
stands for the set of measurable functions on the interval
(0,\,T)
with values in
X
and
\|f(t,\cdot)\|_{X}
belonging to
L^{p}(0,\,T)
. The classical Sobolev space
W^{k,p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
is equipped with the norm
\|f\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}=\sum\limits_{|\alpha|=0}^{k}\|D^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}
. For simplicity, we denote by

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}f(t,x)dxdt=\int_{0}^{T}\int f\ ~~\text{and}~~\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,T;X)}=\|f\|_{L^{p}(X)}.

On the one hand, if we let
\eta
be non-negative smooth function only supported in the space ball of radius 1 and its integral equals to 1, we define the rescaled space mollifier
\eta_{\varepsilon}(x)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\eta(\frac{x}{\varepsilon})

then we have the following standard properties of the mollifier kernel:

.

Let

Proof.

By direct calculation and using the lower bound of

\displaystyle\Big\|\nabla(\frac{f^{\varepsilon}}{g^{\varepsilon}})\Big\|_{L^{\frac{ql}{l+q}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\Big\|\frac{g^{\varepsilon}}{(g^{\varepsilon})^{2}}\nabla f^{\varepsilon}\Big\|_{L^{\frac{ql}{l+q}}}+\Big\|f^{\varepsilon}\frac{\nabla g^{\varepsilon}}{(g^{\varepsilon})^{2}}\Big\|_{L^{\frac{ql}{l+q}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}g(y)\eta(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon})dy\int\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}f(y)\nabla\eta(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon})\frac{1}{\varepsilon}dy

3

When we consider the compressible Navier-Stokes equations with the density containing non-vacuum, due to the moumentum eqaution
\eqref{INS}_{2}
, just spatially regularized velocity
v^{\varepsilon}
can be used as a test function to generate the global energy equality. However, to avoid a commutator estimate involing time
t

\frac{(\varrho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\varrho^{\varepsilon}}
instead of
v^{\varepsilon}
as the test function, which was introduced in
[
21
,
15
]

\eta
be non-negative smooth function only supported in the space ball of radius 1 and its integral equals to 1. We define the rescaled space mollifier
\eta_{\varepsilon}(x)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\eta(\frac{x}{\varepsilon})

f^{\varepsilon}(x)=\int_{\Omega}f(y)\eta_{\varepsilon}(x-y)dyds.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Multiplying
\eqref{INS}_{2}
by
\left(\frac{(\varrho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\varrho^{\varepsilon}}\right)^{\varepsilon}

\varepsilon

yields that

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{s}^{t}\int\nabla\Big(\frac{(\varrho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\varrho^{\varepsilon}}\Big)[(\varrho v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}-(\varrho v)^{\varepsilon}\otimes v^{\varepsilon}]-\int_{s}^{t}\int\nabla\Big(\frac{(\varrho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\varrho^{\varepsilon}}\Big)(\varrho v)^{\varepsilon}\otimes v^{\varepsilon}.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{t}\int\mathrm{div}\,(\varrho^{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon})\frac{|(\varrho v)^{\varepsilon}|^{2}}{(\varrho^{\varepsilon})^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{s}^{t}\int\nabla\Big(\frac{(\varrho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\varrho^{\varepsilon}}\Big)[(\varrho v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}-(\varrho v)^{\varepsilon}\otimes v^{\varepsilon}]

2.2
,

\|(\varrho v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}-(\varrho v)^{\varepsilon}\otimes v^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{kp}{k(p-1)-3p}}(L^{\frac{lq}{l(q-1)-3q}})}\leq C\varepsilon\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\frac{kp}{k(p-2)-4p}}(L^{\frac{lq}{l(q-2)-4q}})}\|v\|_{L^{p}(L^{q})}\|\varrho\|_{L^{k}(L^{l})}

(3.23)

Thanks to the Hölder’s inequality and (
3.21
)-(
3.23
), we find

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{s}^{t}\int\nabla\Big(\frac{(\varrho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\varrho^{\varepsilon}}\Big)[(\varrho v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}-(\varrho v)^{\varepsilon}\otimes v^{\varepsilon}]\Big|

(3.24)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big\|\nabla\Big(\frac{(\varrho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\varrho^{\varepsilon}}\Big)\Big\|_{L^{\frac{kp}{k+2p}}(L^{\frac{lk}{l+2q}})}\|(\varrho v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}-(\varrho v)^{\varepsilon}\otimes v^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{kp}{k(p-1)-3p}}(L^{\frac{lq}{l(q-1)-3q}})}\|1\|_{L^{k}(L^{l})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon\Big\|\nabla\Big(\frac{(\varrho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\varrho^{\varepsilon}}\Big)\Big\|_{L^{\frac{kp}{k+2p}}(L^{\frac{lk}{l+2q}})}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\frac{kp}{k(p-2)-4p}}(L^{\frac{lq}{l(q-2)-4q}})}\|v\|_{L^{p}(L^{q})}\|\varrho\|_{L^{k}(L^{l})}.

which in turn implies

\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\Big|\int_{s}^{t}\int\nabla\Big(\frac{(\varrho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\varrho^{\varepsilon}}\Big)[(\varrho v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}-(\varrho v)^{\varepsilon}\otimes v^{\varepsilon}]\Big|=0.

We turn our attentions to the term
\int_{s}^{t}\int\Big[\varrho^{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon}-(\varrho v)^{\varepsilon}\Big]\frac{(\varrho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\varrho^{\varepsilon}}\nabla\frac{(\varrho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\varrho^{\varepsilon}}
.
We conclude from Lemma
2.2
that

\|\varrho^{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon}-(\varrho v)^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{kp}{k(p-2)-3p}}(L^{\frac{lq}{l(q-2)-3q}})}\leq C\varepsilon\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\frac{kp}{k(p-2)-4p}}(L^{\frac{lq}{l(q-2)-4q}})}\|\varrho\|_{L^{k}(L^{l})}.

(3.25)

Using the Hölder’s inequality and (
3.25
) we find,

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{s}^{t}\int\Big[\varrho^{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon}-(\varrho v)^{\varepsilon}\Big]\frac{(\varrho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\varrho^{\varepsilon}}\nabla\frac{(\varrho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\varrho^{\varepsilon}}\Big|

(3.26)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\varrho^{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon}-(\varrho v)^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{kp}{k(p-2)-3p}}(L^{\frac{lq}{l(q-2)-3q}})}\Big\|\frac{(\varrho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\varrho^{\varepsilon}}\Big\|_{L^{\frac{kp}{k+p}}(L^{\frac{lq}{l+q}})}\Big\|\nabla\frac{(\varrho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\varrho^{\varepsilon}}\Big\|_{L^{\frac{kp}{k+2p}}(L^{\frac{lk}{l+2q}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\frac{kp}{k(p-2)-4p}}(L^{\frac{lq}{l(q-2)-4q}})}\|\varrho\|_{L^{k}(L^{l})}^{2}\|v\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}\Big\|\nabla\frac{(\varrho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\varrho^{\varepsilon}}\Big\|_{L^{\frac{kp}{k+2p}}(L^{\frac{lk}{l+2q}})}

Together this with (
3.21
) and (
3.22
) yield that

\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\Big|\int_{s}^{t}\int\Big[\varrho^{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon}-(\varrho v)^{\varepsilon}\Big]\frac{(\varrho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\varrho^{\varepsilon}}\nabla\frac{(\varrho v)^{\varepsilon}}{\varrho^{\varepsilon}}\Big|=0.

Collecting all the above estimates, using the weak continuity of
\varrho
and
\varrho v
, we complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

4
Energy equality in compressible Navier-Stokes equations allowing vacuum

Unlike the case with non-vacuum, when we consider the compressible Navier-Stokes equations with the density containing vacuum, only spatially regularized velocity
v^{\varepsilon}
fails to possess enough temporal regularity to qualify for a test function. To over this difficulity, we need to mollify the velocity both in space and time. Hence, in this section, we let
\eta
be non-negative smooth function supported in the space-time ball of radius 1 and its integral equals to 1. We define the rescaled space mollifier
\eta_{\varepsilon}(t,x)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d+1}}\eta(\frac{t}{\varepsilon},\frac{x}{\varepsilon})
and

f^{\varepsilon}(t,x)=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}f(\tau,y)\eta_{\varepsilon}(t-\tau,x-y)dyd\tau.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
\phi(t)
be a smooth function compactly supported in
(0,+\infty)
.
Multiplying
\eqref{INS}_{2}
by
(\phi v^{\varepsilon})^{\varepsilon}
, then integrating over
(0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d}
, we infer that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)v^{\varepsilon}\Big[\partial_{t}(\varrho v)^{\varepsilon}+\mathrm{div}\,(\varrho v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}+\nabla P(\varrho)^{\varepsilon}-\mu\Delta v^{\varepsilon}-(\mu+\lambda)\nabla(\mathrm{div}\,v)^{\varepsilon}\Big]=0.

(4.1)

To pass the limit of
\varepsilon
, we
reformulate every term of the last equation.
A straightforward computation leads to

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)v^{\varepsilon}\partial_{t}(\varrho v)^{\varepsilon}=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)v^{\varepsilon}\Big[\partial_{t}(\varrho v)^{\varepsilon}-\partial_{t}(\varrho v^{\varepsilon})\Big]+\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)v^{\varepsilon}\partial_{t}(\varrho v^{\varepsilon})

(4.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)v^{\varepsilon}\Big[\partial_{t}(\varrho v)^{\varepsilon}-\partial_{t}(\varrho v^{\varepsilon})\Big]+\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)\varrho\partial_{t}{\frac{|v^{\varepsilon}|^{2}}{2}}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)\varrho_{t}|v^{\varepsilon}|^{2}.

It follows from integration by parts and the mass equation
\eqref{INS}_{1}
that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)v^{\varepsilon}\mathrm{div}\,(\varrho v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)v^{\varepsilon}\mathrm{div}\,[(\varrho v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}-(\varrho v)\otimes v^{\varepsilon}]+\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)v^{\varepsilon}\mathrm{div}\,(\varrho v\otimes v^{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)\nabla v^{\varepsilon}[(\varrho v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}-(\varrho v)\otimes v^{\varepsilon}]+\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)\left(\mathrm{div}\,(\varrho v)|v^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\varrho v\nabla|v^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)\nabla v^{\varepsilon}[(\varrho v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}-(\varrho v)\otimes v^{\varepsilon}]+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)\mathrm{div}\,(\varrho v)|v^{\varepsilon}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)\nabla v^{\varepsilon}[(\varrho v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}-(\varrho v)\otimes v^{\varepsilon}]-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)\partial_{t}\varrho|v^{\varepsilon}|^{2}.

(4.3)

We rewrite the pressure term as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)v^{\varepsilon}\nabla(\varrho^{\gamma})^{\varepsilon}=\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)[v^{\varepsilon}\nabla(\varrho^{\gamma})^{\varepsilon}-v\nabla(\varrho^{\gamma})]+\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)v\nabla(\varrho^{\gamma}).

(4.4)

Then using the integration by parts and mass equation
\eqref{INS}_{1}
again, we find

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)v\cdot\nabla(\varrho^{\gamma})=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)\varrho^{\gamma-1}\varrho\text{div\,}v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)\varrho^{\gamma-1}(\partial_{t}\varrho+v\cdot\nabla\varrho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\gamma}\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)\partial_{t}\varrho^{\gamma}+\frac{1}{\gamma}\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)v\cdot\nabla\varrho^{\gamma},

which in turn means that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)v\cdot\nabla(\varrho^{\gamma})=\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)\partial_{t}\varrho^{\gamma}.

(4.5)

Thanks to integration by parts, we arrive at

\displaystyle-\mu\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)v^{\varepsilon}\Delta v^{\varepsilon}=\mu\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)|\nabla v^{\varepsilon}|^{2},

(4.6)

\displaystyle-(\mu+\lambda)\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)v^{\varepsilon}\nabla\text{div\,}v^{\varepsilon}=(\mu+\lambda)\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)|\mathrm{div}\,{v^{\varepsilon}}|^{2}.

Plugging (
4.2
)-(
4.6
) into (
4.1
) and using the integration by parts, we conclude that

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)_{t}\left(\varrho{\frac{|v^{\varepsilon}|^{2}}{2}}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\varrho^{\gamma}\right)+\int_{0}^{T}\int\left(\mu|\nabla v^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+(\mu+\lambda)|\mathrm{div}\,v^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right)

(4.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)v^{\varepsilon}\Big[\partial_{t}(\varrho v)^{\varepsilon}-\partial_{t}(\varrho v^{\varepsilon})\Big]+\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)\nabla v^{\varepsilon}[(\varrho v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}-(\varrho v)\otimes v^{\varepsilon}]

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)[v^{\varepsilon}\nabla(\varrho^{\gamma})^{\varepsilon}-v\nabla(\varrho^{\gamma})].

It is enough to prove that the terms on the right hand-side of (
4.7
) tend to zero as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.

Under the hypothesis

\displaystyle 0\leq\varrho\in L^{k}(0,T;L^{l}(\mathbb{T}^{d})),\nabla\sqrt{\varrho}\in L^{\frac{2pk}{2k(p-3)-p}}(0,T;L^{\frac{2lq}{2l(q-3)-q}}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))

(4.8)

\displaystyle v\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{d})),\nabla v\in L^{\frac{pk}{k(p-2)-p}}(0,T;L^{\frac{ql}{l(q-2)-q}}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))\ and\ v_{0}\in L^{\max\{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma-1},\frac{q}{2}\}}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),

with
k>\max\{\frac{p}{2(p-3)},\frac{p}{p-2},\frac{(\gamma-1)p}{2},\frac{(\gamma-1)(d+q)p}{2q-d(p-3)}\}
,
l>\max\{\frac{q}{2(q-3)},\frac{q}{q-2},\frac{(\gamma-1)q}{2}\}
,
p>3
and
q>\max\{3,\frac{d(p-3)}{2}\}
.

In view of Hölder’s inequality and Lemma (
2.5
), we know that

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\int\phi(t)v^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\Big[\partial_{t}(\varrho v)^{\varepsilon}-\partial_{t}(\varrho v^{\varepsilon})\Big]\leq C\|v^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(L^{q})}\|\partial_{t}(\varrho v)^{\varepsilon}-\partial_{t}(\varrho v^{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{\frac{p}{p-1}}(L^{\frac{q}{q-1}})}

(4.9)

\displaystyle\leq C\|v\|^{2}_{L^{p}(L^{q})}\left(\|\varrho_{t}\|_{L^{\frac{p}{p-2}}(L^{\frac{q}{q-2}})}+\|\nabla\varrho\|_{L^{\frac{p}{p-2}}(L^{\frac{q}{q-2}})}\right).

To bound
\varrho_{t}
and
\nabla\varrho
, we
employ mass equation
\eqref{INS}_{1}
to obtain

\varrho_{t}=-2\sqrt{\varrho}v\cdot\nabla\sqrt{\varrho}-{\varrho}\text{div}v,\ and\ \nabla\varrho=2\sqrt{\varrho}\nabla\sqrt{\varrho}.

As a consequence, the triangle inequality and Hölder’s inequality guarantee that

\displaystyle\|\varrho_{t}\|_{L^{\frac{p}{p-2}}(L^{\frac{q}{q-2}})}

(4.10)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|-2\sqrt{\varrho}v\cdot\nabla\sqrt{\varrho}\|_{L^{\frac{p}{p-2}}(L^{\frac{q}{q-2}})}+\|{\varrho}\text{div}v\|_{L^{\frac{p}{p-2}}(L^{\frac{q}{q-2}})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|v\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}\|\nabla\sqrt{\varrho}\|_{L^{\frac{2pk}{2k(p-3)-p}}(L^{\frac{2lq}{2l(q-3)-q}})}\|\varrho\|_{L^{k}(L^{l})}^{1/2}+\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\frac{pk}{k(p-2)-p}}(L^{\frac{ql}{l(q-2)-q}})}\|\varrho\|_{L^{k}(L^{l})}\right),

and

\displaystyle\|\nabla\varrho\|_{L^{\frac{p}{p-2}}(L^{\frac{q}{q-2}})}\leq

\displaystyle C\|\sqrt{\varrho}\nabla\sqrt{\varrho}\|_{L^{\frac{p}{p-2}}(L^{\frac{q}{q-2}})}

(4.11)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\nabla\sqrt{\varrho}\|_{L^{\frac{2pk}{2k(p-2)-p}}(L^{\frac{2lq}{2l(q-2)-q}})}\|\varrho\|_{L^{k}(L^{l})}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\nabla\sqrt{\varrho}\|_{L^{\frac{2pk}{2k(p-3)-p}}(L^{\frac{2lq}{2l(q-3)-q}})}\|\varrho\|_{L^{k}(L^{l})}^{1/2}.

Plugging (
4.10
) and (
4.11
) into (
4.9
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)v^{\varepsilon}\Big[\partial_{t}(\varrho v)^{\varepsilon}-\partial_{t}(\varrho v^{\varepsilon})\Big]

(4.12)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|v\|^{2}_{L^{p}(L^{q})}

\displaystyle\left[\Big(\|v\|_{L^{p}(L^{q})}+1\Big)\|\nabla\sqrt{\varrho}\|_{L^{\frac{2pk}{2k(p-3)-p}}(L^{\frac{2lq}{2l(q-3)-q}})}\|\varrho\|_{L^{k}(L^{l})}^{1/2}+\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\frac{pk}{k(p-2)-p}}(L^{\frac{ql}{l(q-2)-q}})}\|\varrho\|_{L^{k}(L^{l})}\right],.

From Lemma (
2.5
), we end up with, as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
,

\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)v^{\varepsilon}\Big[\partial_{t}(\varrho v)^{\varepsilon}-\partial_{t}(\varrho v^{\varepsilon})\Big]\rightarrow 0.

In the light of the Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\varrho v\otimes v\|_{L^{\frac{pk}{2k+p}}(L^{\frac{ql}{2l+q}})}\leq\|v\|^{2}_{L^{p}(L^{q})}\|\varrho\|_{L^{k}(L^{l})}

(4.13)

Using the integration by parts, we observe that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)\nabla v^{\varepsilon}[(\varrho v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}-(\varrho v)\otimes v^{\varepsilon}]\right|

(4.14)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\nabla v^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{pk}{k(p-2)-p}}(L^{\frac{ql}{l(q-2)-q}})}\|(\varrho v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}-(\varrho v)\otimes v^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{pk}{2k+p}}(L^{\frac{ql}{2l+q}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\frac{pk}{k(p-2)-p}}(L^{\frac{ql}{l(q-2)-q}})}\left(\|(\varrho v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}-\varrho v\otimes v\|_{L^{\frac{pk}{2k+p}}(L^{\frac{ql}{2l+q}})}+\|\varrho v\otimes v-\varrho v\otimes v^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{pk}{2k+p}}(L^{\frac{ql}{2l+q}})}\right).

Hence, by the standard properties of the mollification, we have

\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)\nabla v^{\varepsilon}[(\varrho v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}-(\varrho v)\otimes v^{\varepsilon}]\rightarrow 0\text{ as }\varepsilon\rightarrow 0.

According to Hölder’s inequality on bound domain, we observe that

\|\nabla(\varrho^{\gamma})\|_{L^{\frac{p}{p-1}}(L^{\frac{q}{q-1}})}\leq C\|\varrho\|^{\frac{2\gamma-1}{2}}_{L^{\frac{pk(2\gamma-1)}{4k+p}}(L^{\frac{ql(2\gamma-1)}{4l+q}})}\|\nabla\sqrt{\varrho}\|_{L^{\frac{2pk}{2k(p-3)-p}}(L^{\frac{2lq}{2l(q-3)-q}})},

which in turn implies that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)[v^{\varepsilon}\nabla(\varrho^{\gamma})^{\varepsilon}-v\nabla(\varrho^{\gamma})]\rightarrow 0,

(4.15)

where we have used lemma
2.1
and the condition
k\geq\frac{(\gamma-1)p}{2}
,
l\geq\frac{(\gamma-1)q}{2}
.

Then together with (
4.12
)-(
4.15
), passing to the limits as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, we know that

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi_{t}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|v|^{2}+\frac{\varrho^{\gamma}}{\gamma-1}\right)+\int_{0}^{T}\int\phi(t)\left(\mu|\nabla v|^{2}+(\mu+\lambda)|\mathrm{div}\,v|^{2}\right)=0.

(4.16)

The next objective is to get the energy equality up to the initial time
t=0
by the similar method in
[
6
]
and
[
32
]
, for the convenience of the reader and the integrity of the paper, we give the details. First we prove the continuity of
\sqrt{\varrho}v(t)
in the strong topology as
t\to 0^{+}
. To do this, we define the function
f
on
[0,T]
as

f(t)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(\varrho v)(t,x)\cdot\phi(x)dx,\ for\ any\ \phi(x)\in\mathfrak{D}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),

which is a continuous function with respect to
t\in[0,T]
. Moreover, since

\varrho\in L^{\infty}{0,T;L^{\gamma}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\ and\ \sqrt{\varrho}v\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})),

we can obtain
\varrho v\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}(\mathbb{T}^{d})).

From the moument equation, we have

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(\varrho v)(t,x)\cdot\phi(x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho v\otimes v:\nabla\phi(x)-P\mathrm{div}\,\phi(x)-\mu\nabla v\nabla\phi(x)-(\mu+\lambda)\mathrm{div}\,v\mathrm{div}\,\phi(x)dx,

which is bounded for any function
\phi\in\mathfrak{D}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
. Then it follows from the Corollary 2.1 in
[
11
]
that

\varrho v\in C([0,T];L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}_{\text{weak}}(\mathbb{T}^{d})).

(4.17)

On the other hand, we derive from the mass equation
\eqref{INS}_{1}
that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\varrho^{\gamma})=-\gamma\varrho^{\gamma}\mathrm{div}\,v-2\gamma\varrho^{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}v\cdot\nabla\sqrt{\varrho},

(4.18)

and

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\sqrt{\varrho})=\frac{\sqrt{\varrho}}{2}\mathrm{div}\,v+v\cdot\nabla\sqrt{\varrho},

(4.19)

which together with (
1.12
) gives

\partial_{t}\varrho^{\gamma}\in L^{\frac{kp}{k(p-2)+(\gamma-1)p}}(L^{\frac{lq}{l(q-2)+(\gamma-1)q}}),\ \ \ \nabla\varrho^{\gamma}\in L^{\frac{kp}{k(p-3)+(\gamma-1)p}}(L^{\frac{lq}{l(q-3)+(\gamma-1)q}}),

and

\partial_{t}\sqrt{\varrho}\in L^{\frac{2kp}{2k(p-2)-p}}(L^{\frac{2lq}{2l(q-2)-q}}),\ \ \ \nabla\sqrt{\varrho}\in L^{\frac{2kp}{2k(p-3)-p}}(L^{\frac{2lq}{2l(q-3)-q}}),

Hence, using the Aubin-Lions Lemma
2.6
, we can obtain

\varrho^{\gamma}\in C([0,T];L^{\frac{lq}{l(q-3)+(\gamma-1)q}}((\mathbb{T}^{d}))\,and\,\ \sqrt{\varrho}\in C([0,T];L^{\frac{2lq}{2l(q-3)-q}}(\mathbb{T}^{d})),\

(4.20)

for
k\geq\frac{(\gamma-1)(d+q)p}{2q-d(p-3)}
,
p>3
and
q>\max\{3,\frac{d(p-3)}{2}\}
.

Meanwhile, using the natural energy (
1.4
), (
4.17
) and (
4.20
), we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\overline{\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}}\int|\sqrt{\varrho}v-\sqrt{\varrho_{0}}v_{0}|^{2}dx

(4.21)

\displaystyle=2\overline{\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}}\left(\int\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|v|^{2}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\varrho^{\gamma}\right)dx-\int\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{0}|v_{0}|^{2}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\varrho_{0}^{\gamma}\right)dx\right)

\displaystyle+2\overline{\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}}\left(\int\sqrt{\varrho_{0}}v_{0}\left(\sqrt{\varrho_{0}}v_{0}-\sqrt{\varrho}v\right)dx+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\int\left(\varrho_{0}^{\gamma}-\varrho^{\gamma}\right)dx\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\overline{\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}}\int\sqrt{\varrho_{0}}v_{0}\left(\sqrt{\varrho_{0}}v_{0}-\sqrt{\varrho}v\right)dx

\displaystyle=2\overline{\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}}\int v_{0}\left(\varrho_{0}v_{0}-\varrho v\right)dx+2\overline{\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}}\int v_{0}\sqrt{\varrho}v\left(\sqrt{\varrho}-\sqrt{\varrho_{0}}\right)dx=0,

from
which it follows

\sqrt{\varrho}v(t)\rightarrow\sqrt{\varrho}v(0)\ \ strongly\ in\ L^{2}(\Omega)\ as\ t\rightarrow 0^{+}.

(4.22)

Similarly, one has the right temporal continuity of
\sqrt{\varrho}v
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, hence, for any
t_{0}\geq 0
, we infer that

\sqrt{\varrho}v(t)\rightarrow\sqrt{\varrho}v(t_{0})\ \ strongly\ in\ L^{2}(\Omega)\ as\ t\rightarrow t_{0}^{+}.

(4.23)

Before we go any further, it should be noted that (
4.16
) remains valid for function
\phi
belonging to
W^{1,\infty}
rather than
C^{1}
, then for any
t_{0}>0
, we redefine the test function
\phi
as
\phi_{\tau}
for some positive
\tau
and
\alpha
such that
\tau+\alpha<t_{0}
, that is

\phi_{\tau}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}0,&0\leq t\leq\tau,\\
\frac{t-\tau}{\alpha},&\tau\leq t\leq\tau+\alpha,\\
1,&\tau+\alpha\leq t\leq t_{0},\\
\frac{t_{0}-t}{\alpha},&t_{0}\leq t\leq t_{0}+\alpha,\\
0,&t_{0}+\alpha\leq t.\end{array}\right.

(4.24)

Then substituting this test function into (
4.16
), we arrive at

\displaystyle-\int_{\tau}^{\tau+\alpha}\int

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho v^{2}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\varrho^{\gamma}\right)+\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+\alpha}\int\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho v^{2}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\varrho^{\gamma}\right)

(4.25)

\displaystyle+\int_{\tau}^{t_{0}+\alpha}\int\phi_{\tau}\left(\mu|\nabla v|^{2}+\left(\mu+\lambda\right)|\mathrm{div}\,v|^{2}\right)=0.

Taking
\alpha\rightarrow 0
and using the fact that
\int_{0}^{t}\int\left(\mu|\nabla v|^{2}+\left(\mu+\lambda\right)|\mathrm{div}\,v|^{2}\right)
is continuous with respect to
t
and the Lebesgue point Theorem, we deduce that

\displaystyle-\int

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho v^{2}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\varrho^{\gamma}\right)(\tau)dx+\int\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho v^{2}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\varrho^{\gamma}\right)(t_{0})dx

(4.26)

\displaystyle+\int_{\tau}^{t_{0}}\int\left(\mu|\nabla v|^{2}+\left(\mu+\lambda\right)|\mathrm{div}\,v|^{2}\right)=0.

Finally, letting
\tau\rightarrow 0
, using the continuity of
\int_{0}^{t}\int\left(\mu|\nabla v|^{2}+\left(\mu+\lambda\right)|\mathrm{div}\,v|^{2}\right)
, (
4.17
) and (
4.22
), we can obtain

\displaystyle\int\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho v^{2}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\varrho^{\gamma}\right)(t_{0})dx+\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\int

\displaystyle\left(\mu|\nabla v|^{2}+\left(\mu+\lambda\right)|\mathrm{div}\,v|^{2}\right)dxds

(4.27)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{0}v_{0}^{2}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\varrho_{0}^{\gamma}\right)dx.

Then we complete the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
∎

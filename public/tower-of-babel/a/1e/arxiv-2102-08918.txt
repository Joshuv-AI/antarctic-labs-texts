Compact Dual-Polarization Silicon Integrated Couplers for Multicore Fibers Journal: ol
Julian L. Pita Ruiz, Lucas G. Rocha, Jun Yang, Şükrü Ekin Kocabaş, Ming-Jun Li, Ivan Aldaya, Paulo Dainese, Lucas H. Gabrielli

Abstract
Compact fiber-to-chip couplers play an important role in optical interconnections, especially in data centers. However, the development of couplers has been mostly limited to standard single-mode fibers, with few devices compatible with multicore and multimode fibers. Through the use of state-of-the-art optimization algorithms, we designed a compact dual-polarization coupler to interface chips and dense multicore fibers, demonstrating, for the first time, coupling to both polarizations of all the cores, with measured coupling efficiency of − 4.3 dB -4.3\text{\,}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{B} and with a 3 dB 3\text{\,}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{B} bandwidth of 48 nm 48\text{\,}\mathrm{n}\mathrm{m} . The dual-polarization coupler has footprint of 200 µ ​ m 200\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro m} per core, which makes it the smallest fiber-to-chip coupler experimentally demonstrated on a standard silicon-on-insulator platform.

Spatial division multiplexing (
SDM
)
based on multicore fibers has emerged as one of the potential candidates to overcome the capacity crunch of traditional optical communication systems
[
1
,
2
]
.
Nonetheless, the adoption of this kind of fiber will be possible only if light can be efficiently coupled to all the cores.
So far, the widely adopted solution relies on fiber fan-outs that connect several single-core fibers to a single multicore fiber
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
.
When connecting to an integrated photonics transceiver, however, grating couplers offer a more straightforward and compact alternative, directly coupling the multicore fiber to the integrated waveguides.
Focused grating couplers can efficiently couple a single polarization from a single-mode fiber into the chip
[
6
,
7
]
, and can also be applied to multicore fibers with large core-to-core separation if only one polarization is employed.
Losing polarization diversity, however, is not acceptable for high-end communication systems, and therefore it is paramount to address both polarization states of each core.
This open problem has motivated an increasing effort to the development of couplers with ultra-compact footprints, without impacts on coupling efficiency, wavelength bandwidth, and
crosstalk (
XT
)
.

Significant progress has been made recently in couplers for fibers with large inter-core separation.
For example, Dwivedi
et al.
reported a single-polarization compact focused grating employing silicon nitride instead of silicon.
The lower index contrast between the silicon-nitride core and silica cladding resulted in weaker modal confinement, thus reducing the footprint of the grating down to
$16\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro m}$\times$18\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro m}$
, achieving a
3
-
\mathrm{d}\mathrm{B}
bandwidth of
60\text{\,}\mathrm{n}\mathrm{m}
, a
XT
of
-35\text{\,}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{B}
, and a coupling efficiency of
-5.4\text{\,}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{B}

[
8
,
9
,
10
]
.
Ding
et al.
demonstrated another single-polarization coupler with
-3.8\text{\,}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{B}
efficiency,
48\text{\,}\mathrm{n}\mathrm{m}
bandwidth, and
-32\text{\,}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{B}

XT

[
11
]
.
In this case, the improved efficiency is achieved by placing a metal layer below the grating, which is not be desirable due to fabrication complexity and incompatibility with conventional foundry processes
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
.
Meanwhile, Tong
et al.
proposed a single-polarization coupler with an efficiency of
-2.8\text{\,}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{B}
and a bandwidth of
58.6\text{\,}\mathrm{n}\mathrm{m}
through the use of an extra poly-silicon layer above the silicon layer
[
15
,
16
]
, requiring, however, a relatively large inter-core spacing of
45\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro m}
.
No measurement of
XT
is available for that coupler, because the experimental characterization was performed using a single-core fiber.
Regarding dual-polarization coupling to fibers with more than four cores, Hayashi
et al.

[
17
]
reported coupling to half of the cores of a
2\times 4
multicore fiber with
45\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro m}
core separation, limited primarily by the coupler footprint.
Because the footprint limits the integration density of the individual couplers, the other half of the fiber cores can only be coupled through single polarization structures.
To the best of our knowledge, no dual-polarization coupler proposal has so far been able to address all core of a multicore fiber, which is required for transmission at full capacity.

In this work, we present a compact dual-polarization silicon coupler capable of addressing all seven-cores on a hexagonal arrangement with center-to-center distance of
32\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro m}
, illustrated in
fig.

1
a.
The device shows coupling efficiency of
-4.3\text{\,}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{B}
with a
3\text{\,}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{B}
bandwidth of
48\text{\,}\mathrm{n}\mathrm{m}
and
XT
below
-42.7\text{\,}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{B}
between adjacent cores.

The coupler is composed of seven two-dimensional gratings with area
$10\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro m}$\times$10\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro m}$
connected via ultra-compact tapers to single-mode waveguides.

Figure

1

SEM

[
18
,
19
]
, designed and simulated by the
finite difference time domain (
FDTD
)
method.
Different devices were fabricated and characterized, revealing that the best found performance corresponded to a pitch of
623\text{\,}\mathrm{n}\mathrm{m}
and a fill factor of 0.7.
The tapers connecting each grating output to a
450\text{\,}\mathrm{n}\mathrm{m}
-width silicon waveguide were designed using
SPINS

[
20
]
on an area of only
$10\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro m}$\times$5\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro m}$
.
Minimal feature size was set to
100\text{\,}\mathrm{n}\mathrm{m}
in compliance with the fabrication process design kit to ensure high fabrication yield
[
21
]
.
Simulations indicate an insertion loss of
0.5\text{\,}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{B}
for the taper.
Such a low insertion loss is an excellent result considering the compact footprint is key to allow the network of 14 single-mode waveguides to be routed around all fiber core positions without introducing bend losses, as
fig.

1
d shows.
Because the gratings are designed for a
10\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolDegree}
coupling angle, the 2-dimensional gratings and tapers are not exactly orthogonal, but at an angle of
83.1\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolDegree}
, which was optimized through 3D simulations.
The complete testing system for the dual-polarization coupler is illustrated in
fig.

1
a, where each waveguide is coupled to a single-mode fiber through a conventional focusing grating.
The experimental characterization was performed in fan-out configuration, that is, the light is injected using single-core fibers and collected at the multi-core fiber. It is worth mentioning that the proposed coupler can be equally employed in fan-in configuration. In this case, depending on the particular application, the light coupled to the waveguides corresponding to the two polarizations can be combined in the optical domain or individually detected for
multiple-input multiple-output (
MIMO
)
processing.
The 3D
FDTD
simulated coupling efficiency for each mode is
-3.9\text{\,}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{B}
with
3\text{\,}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{B}
bandwidth of
50\text{\,}\mathrm{n}\mathrm{m}
, as can be seen in the plot of

The device was fabricated in a dedicated run at
Applied Nanotools (
ANT
)

silicon-on-insulator (
SOI
)
wafer from
SOITEC
with a buried oxide layer of
3\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro m}
, and a silicon layer of
250\text{\,}\mathrm{n}\mathrm{m}
.
Patterning was done via electron-beam lithography with minimal feature sizes for both positive and negative layers of
100\text{\,}\mathrm{n}\mathrm{m}
.
Two lithography steps (
250\text{\,}\mathrm{n}\mathrm{m}
full etch and
120\text{\,}\mathrm{n}\mathrm{m}
partial etch) in conjunction with reactive ion etching was used to transfer patterns to the silicon layer.
As a final step,
1\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro m}
of silicon dioxide cladding was deposited on the samples by
plasma-enhanced chemical vapor deposition (
PECVD
)
.

The multicore fiber used has
200\text{\,}\mathrm{m}
in length and seven identical cores (shown in
fig.

1
b) with modal diameters of
8\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro m}
at
1550\text{\,}\mathrm{n}\mathrm{m}
.
The center-to-center separation between adjacent cores is
32\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro m}
and their

10\text{\,}\mathrm{k}\mathrm{m}

-43\text{\,}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{B}
.
This fiber was aligned with the dual-polarization coupler on one side of the chip, while an array of 14 focused grating couplers was used on the other side to allow coupling to single-mode fibers.
The best coupling efficiency was achieved when the multi-core fiber had an angle of approximately
13\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolDegree}
with respect to the vertical.
Light from a tunable continuous laser source was coupled to the single-mode fiber and collected at the end of the multicore fiber.
For coupling efficiency measurements, the total power at the output of the multicore fiber was measured employing a power meter equipped with an integrating sphere, whereas to measure the
XT
, a second single-mode fiber was used.
This second fiber was aligned to each one of the adjacent cores with the help of two piezo-controlled stages and delivered the power to an
optical spectrum analyzer (
OSA
)
.
The efficiency of the focused grating couplers for single-core fibers and the waveguide propagation losses were measured through linear regression of 8 calibration waveguides (2 waveguides of
0.5\text{\,}\mathrm{c}\mathrm{m}
, 2 waveguides of
1\text{\,}\mathrm{c}\mathrm{m}
, 2 waveguides of
3\text{\,}\mathrm{c}\mathrm{m}
, and 2 waveguides of
5\text{\,}\mathrm{c}\mathrm{m}
).
The obtained values were
-3.65\text{\,}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{B}
for the coupling efficiency and
1.4\text{\,}\mathrm{dB}
for the propagation loss.

The plot also presents the largest measured

The dual-polarization coupler shows a
3\text{\,}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{B}

48\text{\,}\mathrm{n}\mathrm{m}

1505\text{\,}\mathrm{n}\mathrm{m}

Compared to the numerical results presented in fig.

Its highest coupling efficiency is

We have presented the design, fabrication, and characterization of compact couplers compatible with multicore fibers with a core separation distance of
32\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro m}
.
The coupler was designed to operate in dual-polarization, addressing the 14 modes of the fiber simultaneously.
The dual-polarization coupler has a footprint of
200\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro m}
per core, maximum coupling efficiency of
-4.3\text{\,}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{B}
, operation bandwidth of
48\text{\,}\mathrm{n}\mathrm{m}
, and
XT
below
-42.7\text{\,}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{B}
, representing the first experimental demonstration of dual-polarization coupling from a chip to all seven cores of a multicore fiber with an intercore distance of
32\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro m}
. The most compact hexagonal arrangement of cores that could be addressed by the proposed couplers (including room for the feed waveguides) would be for an intercore distance of
25\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro m}
.
These figures make the device particularly attractive for advanced communication systems where wavelength division multiplexing can be combined with
SDM
and polarization diversity for high data rates.
It is also worth mentioning that besides their use for multicore fibers, the designed couplers are also interesting solutions for space-constrained situations, for example in high-density fiber arrays.

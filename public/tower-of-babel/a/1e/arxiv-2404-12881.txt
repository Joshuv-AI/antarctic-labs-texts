On the field independent additive constant in Wilson actions
Carlo Pagani, Hidenori Sonoda

Abstract
We discuss the field independent additive constant in Wilson actions carefully within the exact renormalization group formalism. The additive constant does not affect the correlation functions of fields normalized by the partition function, and for that reason it is often ignored. But it is an essential part of the partition function, and in the limit where the UV cutoff goes to zero, the constant gives a renormalized vacuum energy density. We discuss two concrete examples: the Gaussian theory and the linear sigma model in the large N N limit.



I
Introduction

The Wilsonian renormalization group allows one to compute correlation
functions by performing piecemeal functional integration: rather than
integrating over all degrees of freedom at once, one lowers the
momentum cutoff gradually. This procedure allows one to implement
several approximation schemes, which can even be of non-perturbative
nature. If one were able to make the calculation exactly, one would
recover the correlation functions of the original microscopic model.
This holds true also for the partition function.

A particularly powerful implementation of these ideas has been
developed by Wilson
[
1
]
, who put forward an exact
differential equation for the action functional associated with the
degrees of freedom that have not been integrated out yet. Since then,
several exact renormalization group (ERG) equations have been derived
and studied

[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
.

In this paper we focus on the Wilson action and study carefully how
one can derive the partition function or equivalently the vacuum
energy density from it.
For the partition function to be preserved, the ERG must take the form
of a continuity equation, i.e., the change of the exponentiated Wilson
action under the change of the momentum cutoff
\Lambda
must be a
total differential with respect to the fluctuating field:

\displaystyle-\Lambda\partial_{\Lambda}e^{S_{\Lambda}\left[\phi\right]}=\int d^{D}x\,\frac{\delta}{\delta\phi(x)}\Bigr[\cdots\Bigr]e^{S_{\Lambda}\left[\phi\right]}\,.

(1)

Despite its being conceptually clear from the very beginning

[
1
,
2
,
7
]
, in practice almost all
the works employing the Wilson action neglect the vacuum energy
density and the relevant terms in the associated ERG equation.

In the present work several aspects of the vacuum energy density are
studied and discussed in detail. In particular, following Wilson

[
1
]
, we impose that an ERG equation have a
diffusion-inspired form mentioned above. In so doing we shall
introduce two cutoff functions.
We highlight the role of different variables and their relation to the
limit
\Lambda\rightarrow 0
as well as their relation to other
functionals, such as the associated effective average action.
Furthermore, we relate equivalent Wilson actions, i.e., Wilson actions
constructed via different cutoff functions whose partition functions
and correlation functions are identical
[
8
]
, by
means of a functional integral formula.

The paper is organized as follows. In section
II
we
introduce our formalism for the case of a scalar field theory and
discuss the introduction of different variables and the relation
between equivalent Wilson actions. In section
III
we
generalize our discussion to include fermions. In section

IV
we relate the Wilson action to the
generating functional of connected correlation functions and to the
effective average action. We show how the vacuum energy density
appears in each of these functionals. In section
V
and

VI
we consider two examples: the Gaussian theory for scalars
and fermions and the large
N
limit of the
\mathrm{O}\left(N\right)

linear sigma model. We summarize our findings in section

VII
. Some technical discussions are relegated to
Appendices
A
and
B
.

Throughout the paper we work on
D
-dimensional Euclidean space, and
employ the following short-hand notations:

\int_{p}=\int\frac{d^{D}p}{(2\pi)^{D}},\quad\delta(p)=(2\pi)^{D}\delta^{(D)}(p)\,.

(2)

II
Real scalar theory

We introduce an ultraviolet momentum cutoff
\Lambda
in terms of a
cutoff function
R_{\Lambda}(p)
that has the following properties:

1.

It is a decreasing but positive function of
p^{2}
.

2.

It is an increasing function of
\Lambda
.

3.

It can be written as

R_{\Lambda}(p)=\Lambda^{2}R(p/\Lambda)\,,

(3)

where

\displaystyle R(0)

\displaystyle=1\,,

(4a)

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+\infty}R(x)

\displaystyle=0\,.

(4b)

R_{\Lambda}(p)
gives a degree of functional integration over the
fluctuating fields of momentum
p
. The smaller it is, the amount of
remaining integration is smaller. Since
R_{\Lambda}(p)
is a
decreasing function of
p
, the fluctuating fields of higher momenta
are integrated more than those of lower momenta.

We start with a Wilson action
S_{\Lambda}[\phi]
whose modified
correlation functions
[
8
]

\left\langle\kern-3.00003pt\left\langle\phi(p_{1})\cdots\phi(p_{n})\right\rangle\kern-3.00003pt\right\rangle\equiv\prod_{1}^{n}\frac{1}{K(p_{i}/\Lambda)}\cdot\left\langle\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\frac{k(p/\Lambda)}{p^{2}}\frac{\delta^{2}}{\delta\phi(p)\delta\phi(-p)}\right)\,\phi(p_{1})\cdots\phi(p_{n})\right\rangle_{S_{\Lambda}}

(5)

are independent of the cutoff
\Lambda
. The two cutoff functions

K(p/\Lambda)
and
k(p/\Lambda)
are related to
R_{\Lambda}(p)
by

R_{\Lambda}(p)=\frac{p^{2}}{k(p/\Lambda)}K(p/\Lambda)^{2}\,.

(6)

For example, in
[
1
]
the following choice is made:

K(p/\Lambda)=\exp\left(-\frac{p^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\right),\quad k(p/\Lambda)=\frac{p^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}},\quad R_{\Lambda}(p)=\Lambda^{2}\exp\left(-2\frac{p^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\right)\,.

(7)

The same
R_{\Lambda}(p/\Lambda)
is also obtained from

K(p/\Lambda)=\frac{\Lambda^{2}e^{-2\frac{p^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}}}{p^{2}+\Lambda^{2}e^{-2\frac{p^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}}},\quad k(p/\Lambda)=K(p/\Lambda)\left(1-K(p/\Lambda)\right)=p^{2}\frac{\Lambda^{2}e^{-2\frac{p^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}}}{\left(p^{2}+\Lambda^{2}e^{-2\frac{p^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}}\right)^{2}}\,.

(8)

Please note that the modified correlation functions
(
5
) are not normalized by the partition function

Z_{\Lambda}\equiv\int[d\phi]\exp\left(S_{\Lambda}[\phi]\right)\,.

(9)

Hence, for
n=0
, the
\Lambda
independence of
(
5
) amounts to the
\Lambda
independence of the
partition function

\frac{d}{d\Lambda}Z_{\Lambda}=0\,.

(10)

This implies that
\partial_{\Lambda}e^{S_{\Lambda}[\phi]}
must be a
total differential with respect to the field variables as we explained
in Sec.
I
. Indeed we find the ERG differential
equation as
[
8
]
1
1

1

We have added a constant to
rewrite (26) of
[
8
]
as a total differential.

-\Lambda\partial_{\Lambda}e^{S_{\Lambda}[\phi]}=\int_{p}\frac{\delta}{\delta\phi(p)}\left[\Lambda\frac{\partial\ln K(p/\Lambda)}{\partial\Lambda}\,\phi(p)+\frac{1}{2}\Lambda\frac{\partial R_{\Lambda}(p)}{\partial\Lambda}\cdot\frac{K(p/\Lambda)^{2}}{R_{\Lambda}(p)^{2}}\frac{\delta}{\delta\phi(-p)}\right]e^{S_{\Lambda}[\phi]}\,.

(11)

We would like to replace the field variables
\phi(p)
by alternative
field variables
\sigma(p)
that simplify the Wilson action in the
limit
\Lambda\to 0+
. We define

\sigma(p)\equiv\frac{\sqrt{R_{\Lambda}(p)}}{K(p/\Lambda)}\,\phi(p)=\sqrt{\frac{p^{2}}{k(p/\Lambda)}}\,\phi(p)\,.

(12)

We assume that this is an analytic change of variables with respect to
the momentum
p
. (That is the case with the two examples
(
7
,
8
).) The Jacobian of this
change of variables is a field independent constant, and the Wilson
action for the
\sigma
field is given by

e^{S^{\prime}_{\Lambda}[\sigma]}=\frac{\int[d\phi]\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\frac{p^{2}}{k(p/\Lambda)}\phi(p)\phi(-p)\right)}{\int[d\sigma]\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\sigma(p)\sigma(-p)\right)}\,e^{S_{\Lambda}[\phi]}\,.

(13)

By definition, the partition function does not change:

\int[d\sigma]e^{S^{\prime}_{\Lambda}[\sigma]}=\int[d\phi]e^{S_{\Lambda}[\phi]}\,.

(14)

In the following we adopt a particular convention of the Gaussian
functional integral:

\int[d\sigma]\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\sigma(p)\sigma(-p)\right)=1\,.

(15)

This implies

\int[d\sigma]\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}A(p)\sigma(p)\sigma(-p)\right)=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\delta\left(0\right)\int_{p}\ln A(p)\right)\,,

(16)

where

\delta(0)=\int d^{D}x\,e^{ip\cdot 0}=VT

(17)

is the volume of spacetime.

In terms of the
\sigma
variables, the same correlation functions are
given by

\left\langle\kern-3.00003pt\left\langle\phi(p_{1})\cdots\phi(p_{n})\right\rangle\kern-3.00003pt\right\rangle\equiv\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\sqrt{R_{\Lambda}(p_{i})}}\cdot\left\langle\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\frac{\delta^{2}}{\delta\sigma(p)\delta\sigma(-p)}\right)\,\sigma(p_{1})\cdots\sigma(p_{n})\right\rangle_{S^{\prime}_{\Lambda}}\,,

(18)

The ERG equation (
11
) that guarantees the
\Lambda

independence of the correlation functions is now given by

-\Lambda\frac{\partial}{\partial\Lambda}e^{S^{\prime}_{\Lambda}[\sigma]}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\Lambda\frac{\partial\ln R_{\Lambda}(p)}{\partial\Lambda}\frac{\delta}{\delta\sigma(p)}\left[\left(\sigma(p)+\frac{\delta}{\delta\sigma(-p)}\right)e^{S^{\prime}_{\Lambda}[\sigma]}\right]\,.

(19)

The Wilson action
S^{\prime}_{\Lambda}[\sigma]
can be interpreted so that the
field modes with
p<\Lambda
are still to be integrated. As we
decrease
\Lambda
toward
0
, less and less number of degrees of
freedom are to be integrated, and eventually we obtain

\lim_{\Lambda\to 0+}S^{\prime}_{\Lambda}[\sigma]=-\varepsilon_{\textrm{vac}}\,\delta(0)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\sigma(p)\sigma(-p)\,.

(20)

We will derive this limit in Appendix
A
. Let
us note that this was already observed also by Wilson

[
1
]
in its zero dimensional version of the ERG before
discussing the full fledged ERG in terms of “spin variables”.
The reader may be puzzled by such a simple form of

S^{\prime}_{\Lambda}[\sigma]
at
\Lambda=0
for any microscopic model; it
seems that the physics is lost. The crucial observation here is that
the choice of variables is very important. As we shall see in section

IV
,
S^{\prime}_{\Lambda}[\sigma]
is related to the
generating functional of connected correlation functions. Thus, the
physics is not lost, but in order to obtain desired results one must
adopt suitable variables before taking the limit

\Lambda\rightarrow 0
.

Using (
15
), we obtain the cutoff independent
partition function as

\int[d\sigma]e^{S^{\prime}_{\Lambda}[\sigma]}=\exp\left(-\varepsilon_{\textrm{vac}}\,\delta(0)\right)\,,

(21)

where
\varepsilon_{\textrm{vac}}
is the density of the vacuum energy. Taking
\delta(0)

literally, it diverges, and the partition function is either infinite
(if
\varepsilon_{\textrm{vac}}<0
) or zero (if
\varepsilon_{\textrm{vac}}>0
), but
\varepsilon_{\textrm{vac}}
is a finite physical
quantity. We will drop the prime from the Wilson action

S^{\prime}_{\Lambda}[\sigma]
from now on.

Let us conclude this section by considering two Wilson actions
associated with the same microscopic action and built via two
different sets of cutoff functions, say
K_{1},R_{1}
and
K_{2},R_{2}
.
Augmenting the result from
[
8
]
by a constant
multiple, one obtains

\displaystyle e^{S_{2}\left[\phi\right]}=\frac{1}{\int\left[d\phi^{\prime\prime}\right]\exp\Biggr[-\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\frac{1}{\frac{1}{R_{2}(p)}-\frac{1}{R_{1}(p)}}\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}\left(p\right)}{K_{2}\left(p\right)}\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}\left(-p\right)}{K_{2}\left(p\right)}\Biggr]}

\displaystyle\times\int\left[d\phi^{\prime}\right]\exp\Biggr[S_{1}\left[\phi^{\prime}\right]-\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\frac{1}{\frac{1}{R_{2}(p)}-\frac{1}{R_{1}(p)}}\left(\frac{\phi^{\prime}\left(p\right)}{K_{1}\left(p\right)}-\frac{\phi\left(p\right)}{K_{2}\left(p\right)}\right)\left(\frac{\phi^{\prime}\left(-p\right)}{K_{1}\left(p\right)}-\frac{\phi\left(-p\right)}{K_{2}\left(p\right)}\right)\Biggr]\,.

(22)

This is consistent with

\displaystyle\int\left[d\phi\right]e^{S_{1}\left[\phi\right]}=\int\left[d\phi\right]e^{S_{2}\left[\phi\right]}\,.

(23)

III
Dirac fermion theory

It is straightforward to generalize what we have introduced for the
scalar theory to the Dirac fermion theory. We introduce Dirac spinor
fields
\sigma(p)
and
\bar{\sigma}(-p)
so that the modified
correlation functions, defined by

\displaystyle\left\langle\kern-3.00003pt\left\langle\psi(p_{1})\cdots\psi(p_{n})\bar{\psi}(-q_{1})\cdots\bar{\psi}(-q_{n})\right\rangle\kern-3.00003pt\right\rangle

\displaystyle\equiv\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\sqrt{R_{\Lambda}(p_{i})R_{\Lambda}(q_{i})}}\,\cdot\left\langle\sigma(p_{1})\cdots\sigma(p_{n})\exp\left(-\int_{p}\frac{\overleftarrow{\delta}}{\delta\sigma(p)}\frac{\overrightarrow{\delta}}{\delta\bar{\sigma}(-p)}\right)\bar{\sigma}(-q_{1})\cdots\bar{\sigma}(-q_{n})\right\rangle_{S_{\Lambda}}\,,

(24)

are independent of
\Lambda
. This implies the ERG equation

\displaystyle-\Lambda\frac{\partial}{\partial\Lambda}e^{S_{\Lambda}[\sigma,\bar{\sigma}]}=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\Lambda\partial_{\Lambda}\log R_{\Lambda}(p)

(25)

\displaystyle\times\mathrm{Tr}\,\left[\frac{\overrightarrow{\delta}}{\delta\bar{\sigma}(-p)}\left\{e^{S_{\Lambda}}\left(\bar{\sigma}(-p)+\frac{\overleftarrow{\delta}}{\delta\sigma(p)}\right)\right\}+\left\{\left(\sigma(p)+\frac{\overrightarrow{\delta}}{\delta\bar{\sigma}(-p)}\right)e^{S_{\Lambda}}\right\}\frac{\overleftarrow{\delta}}{\delta\sigma(p)}\right]\,,

(26)

where the right-hand side is again a total differential. As opposed
to (
3
) for scalar fields, we use

R_{\Lambda}(p)=\Lambda R(p/\Lambda)

(27)

for the Dirac fermions.

In this case we find the asymptotic behavior

\lim_{\Lambda\to 0+}S_{\Lambda}[\sigma,\bar{\sigma}]=-\varepsilon_{\textrm{vac}}\,\delta(0)-\int_{p}\bar{\sigma}(-p)\sigma(p)\,.

(28)

Using the convention

\int[d\sigma d\bar{\sigma}]\,\exp\left[-\int_{p}\bar{\sigma}(-p)\sigma(p)\right]=1\,,

(29)

we obtain

\int[d\sigma d\bar{\sigma}]e^{S_{\Lambda}[\sigma,\bar{\sigma}]}=\exp\left(-\varepsilon_{\textrm{vac}}\,\delta(0)\right)\,.

(30)

IV
Effective potential

So far we have discussed how to obtain the vacuum energy density as
the limit of a Wilson action as
\Lambda\to 0+
. In literature it is
more standard to calculate it as the minimum of the effective
potential. Let us briefly explain the equivalence of the two
approaches.

We define

W_{\Lambda}[J]\equiv S_{\Lambda}[\sigma]+\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\sigma(p)\sigma(-p)\,,

(31)

where

J(p)\equiv\sqrt{R_{\Lambda}(p)}\,\sigma(p)\,.

(32)

The ERG equation for
W_{\Lambda}[J]
is obtained from that for

S_{\Lambda}[\sigma]
as

-\Lambda\frac{\partial}{\partial\Lambda}e^{W_{\Lambda}[J]}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\Lambda\frac{\partial R_{\Lambda}(p)}{\partial\Lambda}\frac{\delta^{2}}{\delta J(p)\delta J(-p)}e^{W_{\Lambda}[J]}\,.

(33)

As is explained in Appendix
A
,
W_{\Lambda}[J]
becomes the generating
functional of the connected correlation functions in the limit

\Lambda\to 0+
. Expanding
W_{\Lambda}[J]
in powers of
J
, we
obtain

W_{\Lambda}[J]=c_{\Lambda}\delta(0)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}J(p)C_{2\Lambda}(p)J(-p)+\cdots\,,

(34)

where the constant part is the same as that in the expansion of

S_{\Lambda}[\sigma]
. The ERG equation (
33
) gives

-\Lambda\frac{\partial c_{\Lambda}}{\partial\Lambda}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\Lambda\frac{\partial R_{\Lambda}(p)}{\partial\Lambda}\,C_{2\Lambda}(p)\,.

(35)

If we know
C_{2\Lambda}(p)
, we can solve this to determine

c_{\Lambda}
. That is what we will do in Sec.
VI
.

What is usually studied in the context of the exact renormalization
group is the one-particle-irreducible (1PI) Wilson action

\Gamma_{\Lambda}
, which is defined as the Legendre transform of

W_{\Lambda}[J]
:

\Gamma_{\Lambda}[\Phi]-\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}R_{\Lambda}(p)\Phi(p)\Phi(-p)\equiv W_{\Lambda}[J]-\int_{p}J(-p)\Phi(p)\,,

(36)

where

\Phi(p)\equiv\frac{\delta W_{\Lambda}[J]}{\delta J(-p)}\,.

(37)

In the limit
\Lambda\to 0+
, we obtain the effective action

\lim_{\Lambda\to 0+}\Gamma_{\Lambda}[\Phi]=\Gamma_{\textrm{eff}}[\Phi]\,.

(38)

For constant fields
J(p)=j\,\delta(p)
and
\Phi(p)=\varphi\,\delta(p)
, we obtain

W_{\Lambda}[J]=w_{\Lambda}(j)\,\delta(0),\quad\Gamma_{\Lambda}[\Phi]=G_{\Lambda}(\varphi)\,\delta(0)\,.

(39)

Eq. (
36
) reduces to the Legendre transformation

G_{\Lambda}(\varphi)-\frac{1}{2}\Lambda^{2}\varphi^{2}=w_{\Lambda}(j)-j\varphi\,,

(40)

where

\varphi=w^{\prime}_{\Lambda}(j)\,.

(41)

The inverse Legendre transformation gives

j=-G^{\prime}_{\Lambda}(\varphi)\,.

(42)

We assume that
j=0
corresponds to the minimum, not the maximum, of

-G_{\Lambda}(\varphi)
. The effective potential is the limit

V_{\textrm{eff}}(\varphi)=-\lim_{\Lambda\to 0+}G_{\Lambda}(\varphi)\,.

(43)

Let
v_{\Lambda}
be the value of
\varphi
at the minimum of

-G_{\Lambda}(\varphi)
, satisfying

G^{\prime}_{\Lambda}(v_{\Lambda})=0\,.

(44)

We then obtain

c_{\Lambda}=w_{\Lambda}(0)=G_{\Lambda}(v_{\Lambda})-\frac{1}{2}\Lambda^{2}v_{\Lambda}^{2}\,.

(45)

Hence, we obtain

\varepsilon_{\textrm{vac}}=-\lim_{\Lambda\to 0+}c_{\Lambda}=-\lim_{\Lambda\to 0+}G_{\Lambda}(v_{\Lambda})=V_{\textrm{eff}}(v)\,,

(46)

where
v
is at the minimum of the effective potential satisfying

V_{\textrm{eff}}^{\prime}(v)=0\,.

(47)

V
The Gaussian theory

In this section we compute
\varepsilon_{\textrm{vac}}
for the Gaussian theory, both bosonic
and fermionic.

V.1
Scalar theory

We assume a quadratic form

S_{\Lambda}[\sigma]=c_{\Lambda}\,\delta(0)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\sigma(p)C_{\Lambda}(p)\sigma(-p)\,.

(48)

Substituting this into (
19
), we obtain

\displaystyle-\Lambda\frac{\partial}{\partial\Lambda}c_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\Lambda\frac{\partial\ln R_{\Lambda}(p)}{\partial\Lambda}\,\left(1-C_{\Lambda}(p)\right)\,,

(49a)

\displaystyle\Lambda\frac{\partial}{\partial\Lambda}C_{\Lambda}(p)

\displaystyle=-\Lambda\frac{\partial\ln R_{\Lambda}(p)}{\partial\Lambda}\left(1-C_{\Lambda}(p)\right)C_{\Lambda}(p)\,.

(49b)

The second equation can be solved as

C_{\Lambda}(p)=\frac{F(p)}{F(p)+R_{\Lambda}(p)}\,,

(50)

where
F(p)
is independent of
\Lambda
. This corresponds to the
correlation function

\left\langle\kern-3.00003pt\left\langle\phi(p)\phi(q)\right\rangle\kern-3.00003pt\right\rangle=\delta(p+q)\,\frac{1}{F(p)}\times e^{-\varepsilon_{\textrm{vac}}\,\delta(0)}\,.

(51)

The Gaussian theory is given by

F(p)=p^{2}+m^{2}\,,

(52)

where
m^{2}
is a constant squared mass.

Eq. (
49a
) now gives

-\Lambda\frac{\partial}{\partial\Lambda}c_{\Lambda}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\Lambda\frac{\partial R_{\Lambda}(p)}{\partial\Lambda}\,\frac{1}{p^{2}+m^{2}+R_{\Lambda}(p)}\,.

(53)

We will solve this first for
2<D<4
, and then for
D=4
.

For the scalar theory,
R_{\Lambda}(p)=\Lambda^{2}R(p/\Lambda)
, and

\Lambda\frac{\partial R_{\Lambda}(p)}{\partial\Lambda}=\left(2-p\cdot\partial_{p}\right)R_{\Lambda}(p)\,.

(54)

For
2<D<4
, we can rewrite (
53
) as

\displaystyle-\Lambda\frac{\partial c_{\Lambda}}{\partial\Lambda}

\displaystyle=\Lambda^{D}\int_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}p\cdot\partial_{p}\right)R(p)\cdot\frac{1}{p^{2}+\frac{m^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}+R(p)}

\displaystyle=\Lambda^{D}\int_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}p\cdot\partial_{p}\right)R(p)\cdot\left[\frac{1}{p^{2}+R(p)}-\frac{m^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\frac{1}{\left(p^{2}+R(p)\right)^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\Lambda\frac{\partial R_{\Lambda}(p)}{\partial\Lambda}\left[\frac{1}{p^{2}+m^{2}+R_{\Lambda}(p)}-\frac{1}{p^{2}+R_{\Lambda}(p)}+\frac{m^{2}}{\left(p^{2}+R_{\Lambda}(p)\right)^{2}}\right]\,.

(55)

Integrating this, we obtain

\displaystyle c_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=c-\frac{1}{D}\Lambda^{D}\int_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}p\cdot\partial_{p}\right)R(p)\cdot\frac{1}{p^{2}+R(p)}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{D-2}m^{2}\Lambda^{D-2}\int_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}p\cdot\partial_{p}\right)R(p)\cdot\frac{1}{\left(p^{2}+R(p)\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\left[\ln\frac{p^{2}+m^{2}+R_{\Lambda}(p)}{p^{2}+R_{\Lambda}(p)}-\frac{m^{2}}{p^{2}+R_{\Lambda}(p)}\right]\,,

(56)

where
c
is a constant of integration with mass dimension
D
, and
the last integral is UV finite. Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\textrm{vac}}

\displaystyle=-\lim_{\Lambda\to 0+}c_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=-c+\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\left[\ln\frac{p^{2}+m^{2}}{p^{2}}-\frac{m^{2}}{p^{2}}\right]\,,

(57)

where the integral is UV finite, and we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\left[\ln\frac{p^{2}+m^{2}}{p^{2}}-\frac{m^{2}}{p^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{m^{2}}{D}\int_{p}\left[\frac{1}{p^{2}+m^{2}}-\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{\frac{D}{2}}}\Gamma\left(-\frac{D}{2}\right)\,(m^{2})^{\frac{D}{2}}\,,

(58)

which is negative for
2<D<4
.
\varepsilon_{\textrm{vac}}
is not analytic with respect
to
m^{2}
. This is equal to the zero-point energy calculated with
dimensional regularization:

\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{d^{D-1}p}{(2\pi)^{D-1}}\sqrt{\vec{p}\,^{2}+m^{2}}=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{\frac{D}{2}}}\Gamma\left(-\frac{D}{2}\right)\,(m^{2})^{\frac{D}{2}}\,.

(59)

For
D=4
, we can rewrite (
53
) as

\displaystyle-\Lambda\frac{\partial c_{\Lambda}}{\partial\Lambda}=\int_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}p\cdot\partial_{p}\right)R(p)\cdot\left[\Lambda^{4}\frac{1}{p^{2}+R(p)}-m^{2}\Lambda^{2}\frac{1}{\left(p^{2}+R(p)\right)^{2}}+m^{4}\frac{1}{\left(p^{2}+R(p)\right)^{3}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\Lambda\frac{\partial R_{\Lambda}(p)}{\partial\Lambda}\left[\frac{1}{p^{2}+m^{2}+R_{\Lambda}(p)}-\frac{1}{p^{2}+R_{\Lambda}(p)}+\frac{m^{2}}{\left(p^{2}+R_{\Lambda}(p)\right)^{2}}-\frac{m^{4}}{\left(p^{2}+R_{\Lambda}(p)\right)^{3}}\right]\,,

(60)

where the integral multiplied by
m^{4}
is obtained as

\displaystyle\int_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}p\cdot\partial_{p}\right)R(p)\cdot\frac{1}{\left(p^{2}+R(p)\right)^{3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\int_{p}\left(p\cdot\partial_{p}+4\right)\frac{1}{\left(p^{2}+R(p)\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\frac{2}{(4\pi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dp^{2}\frac{d}{dp^{2}}\frac{p^{4}}{\left(p^{2}+R(p)\right)^{2}}=\frac{1}{2(4\pi)^{2}}\,.

(61)

Integrating (
60
) over
\Lambda
, we obtain

\displaystyle c_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=c-\frac{\Lambda^{4}}{4}\int_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}p\cdot\partial_{p}\right)R(p)\cdot\frac{1}{p^{2}+R(p)}+\frac{m^{2}\Lambda^{2}}{2}\int_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}p\cdot\partial_{p}\right)R(p)\cdot\frac{1}{\left(p^{2}+R(p)\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2(4\pi)^{2}}m^{4}\ln\frac{\Lambda}{\mu}+\Lambda^{4}F\left(\frac{m^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\right)\,,

(62)

where
c
is a constant of integration,
\mu
is an arbitrary mass
parameter, and

\displaystyle\Lambda^{4}F\left(\frac{m^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Lambda}^{\infty}d\Lambda^{\prime}\frac{\partial}{\partial\Lambda^{\prime}}\int_{p}\left[\ln\frac{p^{2}+R_{\Lambda^{\prime}}(p)+m^{2}}{p^{2}+R_{\Lambda^{\prime}}(p)}-\frac{m^{2}}{p^{2}+R_{\Lambda^{\prime}}(p)}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{m^{4}}{\left(p^{2}+R_{\Lambda^{\prime}}(p)\right)^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\left[\ln\frac{p^{2}+R_{\Lambda}(p)+m^{2}}{p^{2}+R_{\Lambda}(p)}-\frac{m^{2}}{p^{2}+R_{\Lambda}(p)}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{m^{4}}{\left(p^{2}+R_{\Lambda}(p)\right)^{2}}\right]\,.

(63)

By definition we find

\Lambda^{4}F\left(\frac{m^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\right)\overset{\Lambda\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0\,.

(64)

Since
c_{\Lambda}
remains finite as
\Lambda\to 0+
, we obtain

\Lambda^{4}F\left(\frac{m^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\right)\overset{\Lambda\to 0+}{\longrightarrow}-\frac{1}{4(4\pi)^{2}}m^{4}\ln\frac{m^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}+\mathrm{const}\times m^{4}\,.

(65)

Hence, we obtain

\varepsilon_{\textrm{vac}}=-\lim_{\Lambda\to 0+}c_{\Lambda}=-c+\frac{1}{4(4\pi)^{2}}m^{4}\ln\frac{m^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\,,

(66)

where we have redefined
c
by absorbing a constant multiple of
m^{4}
.
The change of
\mu
can be compensated by a change of
c
. This
result is consistent with the zero-point energy obtained by
dimensional regularization with a minimal subtraction (
D=4-\epsilon
):

\mu^{\epsilon}\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{d^{D-1}p}{(2\pi)^{D-1}}\sqrt{\vec{p}\,^{2}+m^{2}}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\frac{m^{4}}{2(4\pi)^{2}}\overset{\epsilon\to 0}{\longrightarrow}\frac{m^{4}}{4(4\pi)^{2}}\ln\frac{m^{2}e^{\gamma-\frac{3}{2}}}{4\pi\mu^{2}}\,.

(67)

Let us conclude this section by commenting on the massless case which
would be the Gaussian fixed point in the dimensionless framework.
From Eqs. (
57
) and
(
66
), we expect that the vacuum
energy density vanishes for the fixed point theory. We find it
instructive to see how this happens in some details. In the
dimensionless framework, where all the physical quantities are
rendered dimensionless by using appropriate powers of the cutoff

\Lambda
, the Gaussian theory is given by

\bar{S}_{t}\left[\bar{\sigma}\right]=\bar{c}_{t}\,\delta(0)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\bar{\sigma}(-p)\frac{p^{2}+\bar{m}_{t}^{2}}{p^{2}+\bar{m}_{t}^{2}+R(p)}\bar{\sigma}(p)\,,

(68)

where the logarithmic scale
t
is introduced by
\Lambda=\mu e^{-t}
, and

\bar{c}_{t}\equiv\frac{c_{\Lambda}}{\Lambda^{D}},\quad\bar{m}_{t}^{2}\equiv\frac{m^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}=\frac{m^{2}}{\mu^{2}}e^{2t}\,.

(69)

Eq. (
53
) gives

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d}{dt}-D\right)\bar{c}_{t}=\int_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}p\partial_{p}\right)R\left(p\right)\frac{1}{p^{2}+\bar{m}^{2}_{t}+R\left(p\right)}\,.

(70)

At the fixed-point
\bar{m}_{t}^{2}=0
, we obtain

\bar{S}^{*}\left[\bar{\sigma}\right]=\bar{c}^{*}\delta\left(0\right)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\bar{\sigma}\left(-p\right)\frac{p^{2}}{p^{2}+R\left(p\right)}\bar{\sigma}\left(p\right)\,,

(71)

where the fixed point value
\bar{c}^{*}
is given by
2
2

2

This
result is reminiscent of the fixed point value for the cosmological
constant. See, for example,

[
9
,
10
,
11
]
.

\displaystyle\bar{c}^{*}=-\frac{1}{D}\int_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}p\partial_{p}\right)R\left(p\right)\frac{1}{p^{2}+R\left(p\right)}\,.

(72)

By integrating over the fluctuating field
\bar{\sigma}
in
(
71
), we obtain the vanishing vacuum energy density

\bar{\varepsilon}^{*}=-\bar{c}^{*}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\log\frac{p^{2}}{p^{2}+R\left(p\right)}=0\,.

(73)

Though this result is expected, it is still nice to get it by
integrating out the fluctuating field.

V.2
Dirac theory

We assume a quadratic form

S_{\Lambda}[\sigma,\bar{\sigma}]=c_{F,\Lambda}\delta(0)-\int_{p}\bar{\sigma}(-p)C_{F,\Lambda}(p)\sigma(p)\,.

(74)

We obtain

C_{F,\Lambda}(p)=\frac{i\left(\hbox{$p$\kern-5.0pt\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{/}}+im\right)}{R_{\Lambda}(p)+i\left(\hbox{$p$\kern-5.0pt\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{/}}+im\right)}\,,

(75)

where

R_{\Lambda}(p)=\Lambda R(p/\Lambda)\,.

(76)

This corresponds to

\left\langle\kern-3.00003pt\left\langle\psi(p)\bar{\psi}(-q)\right\rangle\kern-3.00003pt\right\rangle=\delta(p-q)\cdot\frac{1}{i\left(\hbox{$p$\kern-5.0pt\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{/}}+im\right)}\,e^{-\varepsilon_{\textrm{vac}}\,\delta(0)}\,.

(77)

The ERG equation for
c_{F,\Lambda}
is given by

\displaystyle\Lambda\frac{\partial c_{F,\Lambda}}{\partial\Lambda}

\displaystyle=\int_{p}\Lambda\partial_{\Lambda}R_{\Lambda}(p)\cdot\mathrm{Tr}\,\frac{1}{R_{\Lambda}(p)+i\left(\hbox{$p$\kern-5.0pt\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{/}}+im\right)}

(78)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\,1\cdot\int_{p}\Lambda\partial_{\Lambda}R_{\Lambda}(p)\cdot\frac{R_{\Lambda}(p)-m}{\left(R_{\Lambda}(p)-m\right)^{2}+p^{2}}\,.

(79)

The solution to this equation is analogous to the scalar case. We
give it only for
3<D<4
:

\displaystyle c_{F,\Lambda}

\displaystyle=c_{F}+\mathrm{Tr}\,1\cdot\Bigg[\frac{\Lambda^{D}}{D}\int_{p}(1-p\cdot\partial_{p})R(p)\cdot\frac{R(p)}{p^{2}+R(p)^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{m\Lambda^{D-1}}{D-1}\int_{p}(1-p\cdot\partial_{p})R(p)\cdot\frac{R(p)^{2}-p^{2}}{\left(p^{2}+R(p)^{2}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+m^{2}\frac{\Lambda^{D-2}}{D-2}\int_{p}(1-p\cdot\partial_{p})R(p)\cdot\frac{R(p)^{3}-3p^{2}R(p)}{\left(p^{2}+R(p)^{2}\right)^{3}}

\displaystyle\quad+m^{3}\frac{\Lambda^{D-3}}{D-3}\int_{p}(1-p\cdot\partial_{p})R(p)\cdot\frac{p^{4}-6p^{2}R(p)^{2}+R(p)^{4}}{(p^{2}+R(p)^{2})^{4}}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{p}\left\{\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{\left(R_{\Lambda}(p)-m\right)^{2}+p^{2}}{p^{2}+R_{\Lambda}(p)^{2}}+m\frac{R_{\Lambda}(p)}{p^{2}+R_{\Lambda}(p)^{2}}+m^{2}\frac{-p^{2}+R_{\Lambda}(p)^{2}}{2\left(p^{2}+R_{\Lambda}(p)^{2}\right)^{2}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\qquad+m^{3}\frac{R_{\Lambda}(p)\left(R_{\Lambda}(p)^{2}-3p^{2}\right)}{3\left(p^{2}+R_{\Lambda}(p)^{2}\right)^{3}}\right\}\Bigg]\,,

(80)

where
c_{F}
is a constant of integration. Taking the limit

\Lambda\to 0+
, we obtain

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\textrm{vac}}

\displaystyle=-c_{F}-\mathrm{Tr}\,1\cdot\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\left(\ln\frac{p^{2}+m^{2}}{p^{2}}-\frac{m^{2}}{p^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=-c_{F}+\mathrm{Tr}\,1\cdot\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{\frac{D}{2}}}\Gamma\left(-\frac{D}{2}\right)(m^{2})^{\frac{D}{2}}\,.

(81)

This is consistent with the energy density of the Dirac vacuum:

-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}\,1\cdot\int\frac{d^{D-1}p}{(2\pi)^{D-1}}\sqrt{\vec{p}\,^{2}+m^{2}}\,.

(82)

The factor
\frac{1}{2}
is there because only the negative energy
states contribute to the vacuum.

VI
The large
N
limit

We consider the
\mathrm{O}(N)
linear sigma model in
D
dimensions (
2<D<4
). Let

S_{\Lambda}[\sigma]
be the Wilson action in terms of the spin
variables
\sigma^{I}(p)\,(I=1,\cdots,N)
. Expanding
S_{\Lambda}[\sigma]
in powers of fields, we obtain

S_{\Lambda}[\sigma]=c_{\Lambda}\,\delta(0)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\sigma^{I}(p)\sigma^{I}(-p)\,c_{2\Lambda}(p)+\cdots\,,

(83)

where the repeated indices are summed. The ERG equation

-\Lambda\frac{\partial}{\partial\Lambda}e^{S_{\Lambda}[\sigma]}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\Lambda\frac{\partial\log R_{\Lambda}(p)}{\partial\Lambda}\frac{\delta}{\delta\sigma^{I}(p)}\left[\left(\sigma^{I}(p)+\frac{\delta}{\delta\sigma^{I}(-p)}\right)e^{S_{\Lambda}[\sigma]}\right]

(84)

gives

-\Lambda\frac{\partial}{\partial\Lambda}c_{\Lambda}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\Lambda\frac{\partial\ln R_{\Lambda}(p)}{\partial\Lambda}N\left(1+c_{2\Lambda}(p)\right)\,.

(85)

In the large
N
limit, we find

1+c_{2\Lambda}(p)=\frac{R_{\Lambda}(p)}{p^{2}+R_{\Lambda}(p)+m_{\Lambda}^{2}}\,,

(86)

where the squared mass
m_{\Lambda}^{2}
satisfies

-\Lambda\frac{\partial m_{\Lambda}^{2}}{\partial\Lambda}=\frac{\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\Lambda\partial_{\Lambda}R_{\Lambda}(p)\frac{1}{\left(p^{2}+R_{\Lambda}(p)+m_{\Lambda}^{2}\right)^{2}}}{\frac{1}{\lambda}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\frac{1}{\left(p^{2}+R_{\Lambda}(p)+m_{\Lambda}^{2}\right)^{2}}}\,.

(87)

The positive constant
\lambda
is a
\phi^{4}
self-coupling.
Eq. (
85
), combined with (
86
), is the
same as Eq. (
53
) for the Gaussian theory, except for
the cutoff dependence of the squared mass. We refer the reader to
Appendix
B
for a derivation of
Eqs. (
85
-
87
).

The solution to (
85
) is given by

\frac{1}{N}c_{\Lambda}=c-\frac{1}{2\lambda}m_{\Lambda}^{4}+m_{\Lambda}^{2}\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\left(\frac{1}{p^{2}+R_{\Lambda}(p)+m_{\Lambda}^{2}}-\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\left(\ln\frac{p^{2}+R_{\Lambda}(p)+m_{\Lambda}^{2}}{p^{2}}-\frac{m_{\Lambda}^{2}}{p^{2}}\right)\,,

(88)

where
c
is a constant of integration. Denoting the physical squared
mass by

m_{\mathrm{ph}}^{2}=\lim_{\Lambda\to 0+}m_{\Lambda}^{2}\,,

(89)

we obtain the vacuum energy density as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\varepsilon_{\textrm{vac}}

\displaystyle=-\lim_{\Lambda\to 0+}\frac{1}{N}c_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=-c+\frac{1}{2\lambda}m_{\mathrm{ph}}^{4}-m_{\mathrm{ph}}^{2}\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\left(\frac{1}{p^{2}+m_{\mathrm{ph}}^{2}}-\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\left(\ln\frac{p^{2}+m_{\mathrm{ph}}^{2}}{p^{2}}-\frac{m_{\mathrm{ph}}^{2}}{p^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=-c+\frac{1}{2\lambda}m_{\mathrm{ph}}^{4}+\frac{D-2}{4}\frac{\Gamma\left(-\frac{D}{2}\right)}{(4\pi)^{\frac{D}{2}}}(m_{\mathrm{ph}}^{2})^{\frac{D}{2}}\,,

(90)

where
2<D<4
. With
c
and
m_{\mathrm{ph}}^{2}
fixed, this has a well defined
strong coupling limit

\frac{1}{N}\varepsilon_{\textrm{vac}}\overset{\lambda\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}c+\frac{D-2}{4}\frac{\Gamma\left(-\frac{D}{2}\right)}{(4\pi)^{\frac{D}{2}}}(m_{\mathrm{ph}}^{2})^{\frac{D}{2}}\,.

(91)

For (
90
) to have a good weak coupling limit, we must
introduce appropriate
\lambda
dependence to
c
. We note

k\equiv-\frac{m_{\Lambda}^{2}}{\lambda}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\left(\frac{1}{p^{2}+R_{\Lambda}(p)+m_{\Lambda}^{2}}-\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right)

(92)

is independent of
\Lambda
. Writing

c=c^{\prime}+\frac{\lambda}{2}k^{2}\,,

(93)

we obtain an alternative expression for

c_{\Lambda}
:

\frac{1}{N}c_{\Lambda}=c^{\prime}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\left\{\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\left(\frac{1}{p^{2}+R_{\Lambda}(p)+m_{\Lambda}^{2}}-\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right)\right\}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\left(\ln\frac{p^{2}+R_{\Lambda}(p)+m_{\Lambda}^{2}}{p^{2}}-\frac{m_{\Lambda}^{2}}{p^{2}}\right)\,.

(94)

This gives

\frac{1}{N}\varepsilon_{\textrm{vac}}=-c^{\prime}-\frac{\lambda}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\left(4\pi\right)^{D/2}}\frac{D}{4}\Gamma\left(-\frac{D}{2}\right)(m_{\mathrm{ph}}^{2})^{\frac{D-2}{2}}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Gamma\left(-\frac{D}{2}\right)}{(4\pi)^{\frac{D}{2}}}(m_{\mathrm{ph}}^{2})^{\frac{D}{2}}\,.

(95)

In the weak coupling limit, where
c^{\prime}
is fixed instead of
c
, we
reproduce the Gaussian result (
58
):

\frac{1}{N}\varepsilon_{\textrm{vac}}\overset{\lambda\to 0}{\longrightarrow}-c^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Gamma\left(-\frac{D}{2}\right)}{(4\pi)^{\frac{D}{2}}}(m_{\mathrm{ph}}^{2})^{\frac{D}{2}}\,.

(96)

VII
Conclusions

In this work we have studied the field independent part of the Wilson
action that is fundamental in the calculation of the partition
function.
Let us summarize our results.

We have considered a general ERG equation for the Wilson action
involving two cutoff functions. This equation has a diffusion-like
form for both scalar fields and fermions.
We have introduced the field variables
\sigma
that simplify the form
of the Wilson action in the limit
\Lambda\rightarrow 0
, as given by
(
20
) and (
28
). Moreover,
in Eq. (
22
), we have formally related two
equivalent Wilson actions, constructed by two different sets of cutoff
functions, that give rise to the same correlation functions and
partition function. We have also related our formalism to the
generating functional for connected correlation functions and to the
1PI generating functional in Sec.
IV
.
Finally, we have made our discussion concrete by considering two
examples: the Gaussian models for scalar and fermionic fields
(Sec.
V
) and the large
N
limit of the

\mathrm{O}\left(N\right)
linear sigma model (Sec.
VI
).
We hope this work lays a ground for further generalizations. For
instance, it may be natural to go beyond the flat space and work on
more general background spacetimes.

Appendix A
The limit of
S_{\Lambda}[\sigma]
as
\Lambda\to 0+

In the limit
\Lambda\to 0+
we expect
S_{\Lambda}[\sigma]
to become
“trivial” since below the UV cutoff
\Lambda=0
there is no mode left
to be integrated. To make this statement precise let us compute

\lim_{\Lambda\to 0+}S_{\Lambda}[\sigma]

explicitly, where
\sigma
is a special choice of field variables
defined by (
12
).

For this goal, we take a rather roundabout path. We first consider
the generating functional
\mathcal{W}[\mathcal{J}]
of the connected
correlation functions defined by
[
12
]

\displaystyle e^{\mathcal{W}[\mathcal{J}]}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\int_{p_{1},\cdots,p_{n}}\mathcal{J}(-p_{1})\cdots\mathcal{J}(-p_{n})\,\left\langle\kern-3.00003pt\left\langle\phi(p_{1})\cdots\phi(p_{n})\right\rangle\kern-3.00003pt\right\rangle

(97)

\displaystyle=\sum_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\int_{p_{1},\cdots,p_{n}}\prod_{1}^{n}\frac{\mathcal{J}(-p_{i})}{\sqrt{R_{\Lambda}(p_{i})}}\cdot\left\langle\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\frac{\delta^{2}}{\delta\sigma(p)\delta\sigma(-p)}\right)\sigma(p_{1})\cdots\sigma(p_{n})\right\rangle_{S_{\Lambda}}

\displaystyle=\int[d\sigma]e^{S_{\Lambda}[\sigma]}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\frac{\delta^{2}}{\delta\sigma(p)\delta\sigma(-p)}\right)\exp\left(\int_{p}\frac{\mathcal{J}(-p)}{\sqrt{R_{\Lambda}(p)}}\sigma(p)\right)

\displaystyle=\int[d\sigma]\exp\left[S_{\Lambda}[\sigma]-\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\frac{\mathcal{J}(p)\mathcal{J}(-p)}{R_{\Lambda}(p)}+\int_{p}\frac{\mathcal{J}(-p)}{\sqrt{R_{\Lambda}(p)}}\sigma(p)\right]\,.

(98)

We now introduce new field variables by

J(p)\equiv\sqrt{R_{\Lambda}(p)}\,\sigma(p)\,.

(99)

Taking the Jacobian into account, we obtain

\displaystyle e^{\mathcal{W}[\mathcal{J}]}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\int[dJ^{\prime}]\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\frac{J^{\prime}(p)J^{\prime}(-p)}{R_{\Lambda}(p)}\right)}\int[dJ]\exp\Bigg[W_{\Lambda}[J]

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\frac{1}{R_{\Lambda}(p)}\left(\mathcal{J}(p)-J(p)\right)\left(\mathcal{J}(-p)-J(-p)\right)\Bigg]\,,

(100)

where

W_{\Lambda}[J]\equiv S_{\Lambda}[\sigma]+\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\sigma(p)\sigma(-p)=S_{\Lambda}\left[\frac{J}{\sqrt{R_{\Lambda}}}\right]+\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\frac{J(p)J(-p)}{R_{\Lambda}(p)}\,,

(101)

and we have used the triviality of the Gaussian functional integral

\int[d\sigma]\,\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\sigma(p)\sigma(-p)\right)=1\,.

(102)

The right-hand side of (
100
) is independent of
\Lambda
. In
the limit
\Lambda\to 0+
the functional integral becomes one over
the delta functional

\displaystyle\lim_{\Lambda\to 0+}\frac{1}{\int[dJ^{\prime}]\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\frac{J^{\prime}(p)J^{\prime}(-p)}{R_{\Lambda}(p)}\right)}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\frac{1}{R_{\Lambda}(p)}\left(\mathcal{J}(p)-J(p)\right)\left(\mathcal{J}(-p)-J(-p)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p}\delta\left(\mathcal{J}(p)-J(p)\right)\,,

(103)

and we obtain

\lim_{\Lambda\to 0+}W_{\Lambda}[J]=\mathcal{W}[J]\,.

(104)

Fixing
\sigma(p)
, we obtain

\lim_{\Lambda\to 0+}J(p)=0\,.

(105)

Hence, we obtain

\lim_{\Lambda\to 0+}W_{\Lambda}\left[\sqrt{R_{\Lambda}}\,\sigma\right]=\mathcal{W}[0]=-\varepsilon_{\textrm{vac}}\,\delta(0)\,.

(106)

Thus, Eq. (
101
) gives the desired limit

\lim_{\Lambda\to 0+}S_{\Lambda}[\sigma]=-\varepsilon_{\textrm{vac}}\,\delta(0)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\sigma(p)\sigma(-p)\,.

(107)

In this appendix we briefly review the large

approximation in the

[
13
]
, which is
based on the method introduced in
[
14
]
for the

N
limit of the ERG has been

[
15
,
14
,
16
,
17
]

and references therein for a complete list of references that

.

The main aim of this appendix is to derive
Eqs. (
85
-
87
) in the main text. We work in
D

dimensions with
2<D<4
. To obtain the large
N
limit it is
convenient to adopt the 1PI formalism. Let us consider the ansatz

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\phi^{i}(-p)p^{2}\phi^{i}(p)+N\Gamma_{I\Lambda}\left[\frac{\phi^{i}\phi^{i}}{2N}\right]\,.

(108)

This is consistent at leading order in large
N
. We next introduce
the variable
\varphi

\varphi\equiv\frac{\phi^{i}\phi^{i}}{2N}
. In the large
N
limit,
the ERG equation for
\Gamma_{I\Lambda}

\displaystyle-\Lambda\partial_{\Lambda}\Gamma_{I\Lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\Lambda\partial_{\Lambda}R_{\Lambda}\left(p\right){\cal G}_{\Lambda;p,-p}\left[\varphi\right]\,,

\displaystyle F_{\Lambda}\left[\sigma\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma_{I\Lambda}\left[\varphi\right]-\int_{p}\sigma\left(p\right)\varphi\left(-p\right)\,,

(111)

\sigma=\frac{\delta\Gamma_{I\Lambda}}{\delta\varphi}\left[\varphi\right]

\displaystyle\varphi\left(p\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\delta F_{\Lambda}}{\delta\sigma\left(-p\right)}\,.

(112)

The ERG equation for

\displaystyle-\Lambda\partial_{\Lambda}F_{\Lambda}\left[\sigma\right]

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{p}\Lambda\partial_{\Lambda}R_{\Lambda}\left(p\right){\cal G}_{\Lambda;p,-p}\left[\sigma\right]\,,

\int_{q}{\cal G}_{\Lambda;p,-q}\left[\sigma\right]\Biggr\{\left(q^{2}+R_{\Lambda}\left(q\right)\right)\delta\left(q-r\right)-\sigma(r-q)\Biggr\}=\delta\left(p-r\right)\,,

(114)

and can be expressed by a geometric series of
\sigma

113

\displaystyle I_{\Lambda}\left[\sigma\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{\Lambda}\delta\left(0\right)+c_{1\Lambda}\sigma\left(0\right)

(115)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2n}\int_{p_{1}\cdots p_{n}}\sigma\left(p_{1}\right)\cdots\sigma\left(p_{n}\right)\delta\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}\right)I_{n\Lambda}\left(p_{1},\cdots,p_{n}\right)\,,

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{q}\log\left(\frac{q^{2}+R_{\Lambda}\left(q\right)}{q^{2}}\right)\,,

\displaystyle c_{1\Lambda}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{q}\left(\frac{1}{q^{2}+R_{\Lambda}\left(q\right)}-\frac{1}{q^{2}}\right)\,,

(116b)

\displaystyle I_{n\Lambda}

\displaystyle=\int_{q}h_{\Lambda}\left(q\right)h_{\Lambda}\left(q+p_{1}\right)\cdots h_{\Lambda}\left(q+p_{1}+\cdots+p_{n-1}\right)\,,

with

\displaystyle F_{\Lambda}\left[\sigma\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{F}\left[\sigma\right]+I_{\Lambda}\left[\sigma\right]\,,

(117)

where
\tilde{F}\left[\sigma\right]
is an arbitrary functional independent
of
\Lambda
. In the present case we consider

\displaystyle\tilde{F}\left[\sigma\right]

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle f_{0}\delta\left(0\right)+f_{1}\sigma\left(0\right)+\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{p}\frac{1}{2}\sigma\left(p\right)\sigma\left(-p\right)\,,

(118)

where
\lambda
is a positive constant reminiscent of the

\phi^{4}
-interaction coupling. Finally, let us also write down in a
compact form the solution for the case of constant field
\sigma
. We
find

\displaystyle F_{\Lambda}\left(\sigma\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{F}\left(\sigma\right)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{q}\Biggr[\log\frac{q^{2}-\sigma+R_{\Lambda}}{q^{2}}+\frac{\sigma}{q^{2}}\Biggr]\,,

(119)

where

F_{\Lambda}[\sigma]=F_{\Lambda}(\sigma)\,\delta(0)\,,\quad\tilde{F}[\sigma]=\tilde{F}(\sigma)\,\delta(0)\,.

(120)

The main aim of this appendix has been to derive
Eqs. (
85
-
87
) in the main
text. Let us work in the symmetric phase, where

c_{\Lambda}=G_{\Lambda}\left(0\right)
(see
Eq. (
45
)). According to the relations
among the various functionals detailed in section

IV
, we can write the RHS of
(
85
) by employing the following equation:

\displaystyle 1+c_{2\Lambda}\left(p\right)=R_{\Lambda}\left(p\right)\left(p^{2}-\frac{\delta\Gamma_{I\Lambda}}{\delta\varphi}+R_{\Lambda}\left(p\right)\right)^{-1}\Bigr|_{\varphi=0}\,,

(121)

which implies Eq. (
86
) after we identify
m_{\Lambda}^{2}

with

-\sigma_{{\rm os}}=-\frac{\delta\Gamma_{I\Lambda}}{\delta\varphi}\left[\varphi=0\right]=m_{\Lambda}^{2}\,.

(122)

The ERG equation associated with
\sigma_{\rm{os}}
reads

-\Lambda\frac{d}{d\Lambda}\sigma_{{\rm os}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(-\partial_{\sigma}^{2}F_{\Lambda}\right)^{-1}\int_{q}\frac{\Lambda\partial_{\Lambda}R_{\Lambda}}{\left(q^{2}-\sigma_{{\rm os}}+R_{\Lambda}\right)^{2}}\,,

(123)

which reproduces Eq. (
87
) once expressed in
terms of
m_{\Lambda}^{2}
.

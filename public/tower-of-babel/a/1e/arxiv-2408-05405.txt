Locally noetherian quiver representations
Henning Krause

Abstract
. It is shown that a quiver is left noetherian if and only if the category of quiver representations in any locally noetherian abelian category is again locally noetherian. Here, locally noetherian means that any object is the directed union of its noetherian subobjects. For a quiver to be left noetherian means that the left ideals of paths starting at any fixed vertex satisfy the ascending chain condition. The proof generalises to representations of any small category that admits a Gröbner enrichment.

1.
Introduction

Hilbert’s basis theorem asserts that a polynomial ring
A[X]
in one
indeterminate is left noetherian if the coefficient ring
A
is left
noetherian
[
7
]
. The category of
A[X]
-modules identifies
with the category of
A
-linear representations of the
Jordan
quiver
consisting of one vertex and a single loop. Thus an
equivalent formulation of Hilbert’s theorem is that the category of

A
-linear representations of the Jordan quiver is locally noetherian
if the category of
A
-modules is locally noetherian.

We generalise Hilbert’s theorem as follows and write

\operatorname{Rep}(Q,\mathcal{A})
for the category of representation of a quiver
Q
in an
abelian category
\mathcal{A}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For a quiver
Q
and a left noetherian ring
A
the following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

The quiver
Q
is left noetherian.

(2)

The abelian category
\operatorname{Rep}(Q,\mathcal{A})
is locally noetherian for every
locally noetherian abelian category
\mathcal{A}
.

(3)

The abelian category
\operatorname{Rep}(Q,\operatorname{Mod}A)
is locally noetherian.

For a quiver to be
left noetherian
simply means that the left
ideals of paths starting at any fixed vertex satisfy the ascending
chain condition, and the quiver is
right noetherian
if the
analogous condition for paths terminating at any fixed vertex is
satisfied; see Definition
2.2
.

When the quiver
Q
has only finitely many vertices the category

\operatorname{Rep}(Q,\operatorname{Mod}A)
identifies with the module category of the
path
algebra

A[Q]
. Thus we obtain for
A[Q]
a simple characterisation to be
left noetherian; see Corollary
5.1
.

There is an analogue of the above theorem for
locally finite
categories
of representations. This requires both chain conditions
for ideals in a quiver; see Theorem
6.3
. Moreover, we
provide a generalisation of the above theorem which covers
representations of any
small category
. This involves the notion
of a
Gröbner enrichment
and the proof remains basically
unchanged; see Theorem
7.1
.

Theorem
1.1
has some predecessors, but in this form it
seems to be new, because we give a simple definition/description of a
noetherian quiver and we consider representations in any locally
noetherian abelian category. In fact, our definition of a locally
noetherian abelian category is more general than the usual one

[
6
, II.4]
; it simply requires that any object is the
directed union of its noetherian subobjects. For example, the category
of finite dimensional vector spaces over a field is locally
noetherian. We consider representations in abelian categories that are
not necessarily module categories; this requires some new techniques
since elements cannot be used.

Let us provide the relevant references that contain predecessors of
the above theorem. The first is
[
8
]
where it is assumed that
the quiver has a finite set of vertices, while
[
9
]
considers
representations of arbitrary quivers and even more general
categories. The description of noetherian quivers in

[
8
,
9
]
is somewhat complicated. So the results from

[
9
]
are reproved in
[
5
]
, using a more transparent
(though incorrect, as pointed out in
[
1
]
) description of
noetherian quivers. All mentioned papers restrict to representations
in module categories. For a survey on more recent generalisations
of Hilbert’s basis theorem, including their applications, see
[
4
]
.

2.
Noetherian quivers

Let
Q=(Q_{0},Q_{1},s,t)
be a
quiver
, given by a set of vertices
Q_{0}

and a set of arrows
Q_{1}
such that each arrow
\alpha
starts at
s(\alpha)

and terminates at
t(\alpha)
. A
path

\phi\colon x\to y
of

length

n
in
Q
is a sequence
\alpha_{n}\ldots\alpha_{2}\alpha_{1}
of arrows

\alpha_{i}
such that
s(\alpha_{i+1})=t(\alpha_{i})
for all
i
, where

s(\phi):=x=s(\alpha_{1})
and
t(\phi):=y=t(\alpha_{n})
. For each
x\in Q_{0}
there is
the
trivial path

x\to x
of length zero. For any pair of
vertices
x,y
in
Q
let
Q(x,y)
denote the set of paths
x\to y
. We write
\mathcal{Q}
for the
path category
with set of objects

Q_{0}
and set of morphism
x\to y
given by
Q(x,y)
for all

x,y\in Q_{0}
. The concatenation of paths yields the composition in

\mathcal{Q}
.

For a vertex
x
in
Q
let
Q(x)
denote the set of paths starting at

x
. This set is partially ordered via

\phi\leq\psi\quad:\iff\quad\phi=\chi\psi\;\text{ for some path }\;\chi\colon t(\psi)\to t(\phi).

Let
P=(P,\leq)
be a poset. An
ideal
of
P
is a non-empty
subset
I\subseteq P
such that
x\leq y
and
y\in I
implies

x\in I
. The poset
P
is
noetherian
if the ascending chain
condition holds for the ideals in
P
. We refer to

[
2
, VIII.1]
for basic facts about noetherian
posets.

Sometimes a poset is defined to be
noetherian

if the ascending chain condition holds for chains of

elements
. Clearly, one condition implies the other (by
passing from an element to the ideal generated by that element),
but the conditions are not equivalent.
For instance, it is easily checked
that a poset is noetherian if and only if every ideal is generated by
finitely many elements. For later use we record another well known
characterisation.

Lemma 2.1
.

A poset is noetherian if and only if for every infinite sequence

(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of elements there is a map
\nu\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}

such that
i<j
implies
\nu(i)<\nu(j)
and
x_{\nu(j)}\leq x_{\nu(i)}
.

Proof.

We prove the direction which is used later on and leave the other
one to the interested reader. So assume the poset is
noetherian. First oberserve that for every infinite sequence

(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of elements there exists
i\in\mathbb{N}
such that

x_{j}\leq x_{i}
for infinitely many
j\in\mathbb{N}
. This follows by looking
at the ascending chain of ideals generated by the elements

x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}
, which must stabilise.
Now define
\nu\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
recursively by
taking for
\nu(0)
the smallest
i\in\mathbb{N}
such that
x_{j}\leq x_{i}

for infinitely many
j\in\mathbb{N}
. For
n>0
set

\nu(n)=\operatorname{min}\{i>\nu(n-1)\mid x_{j}\leq x_{i}\leq x_{\nu(n-1)}\text{ for infinitely many
}j\in\mathbb{N}\}.\qed

The multiplication of paths in
Q
yields the following notion of an
ideal. A non-empty set of paths
I
in
Q
is a
left ideal
if
for any pair
\phi,\psi
of paths with
s(\psi)=t(\phi)
the condition

\phi\in I
implies
\psi\phi\in I
. A
right ideal
of paths is
defined analogously. Clearly, a subset
I\subseteq Q(x)
is a poset
ideal if and only if it is a left ideal of paths.

Definition 2.2
.

A quiver
Q
is called
left noetherian
if for each vertex
x
the poset

Q(x)
is noetherian. This means the left ideals of paths starting at
any fixed vertex satisfy the ascending chain condition. The quiver is

right noetherian
if the right ideals of paths terminating at
any fixed vertex satisfy the ascending chain condition.

This local condition can be checked by counting the number of

maximal paths
starting at a fixed vertex. By definition,
this is either a path
\alpha_{n}\ldots\alpha_{2}\alpha_{1}
such that there is no arrow
starting at
t(\alpha_{n})
, or it is a path
\ldots\alpha_{2}\alpha_{1}
of
infinite length.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
Q
be a quiver. For a vertex
x
the following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

The poset
Q(x)
is noetherian.

(2)

For all
\phi\in Q(x)
there are only finitely many
arrows starting at
t(\phi)
, and for almost all
\phi\in Q(x)
there is at most one
arrow starting at
t(\phi)
.

(3)

There are only finitely many maximal paths
starting at
x
.

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2): Let
\phi\in Q(x)
. The ideal of
Q(x)
that is generated by all
paths
\alpha\phi
with
\alpha
an arrow starting at
t(\phi)
is finitely generated. It follows that there
are only finitely many arrows starting at
t(\phi)
. Suppose

\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\ldots
is an infinite sequence of paths such that there
is more than one arrow starting at each
t(\phi_{i})
. Because
Q(x)

is noetherian, we may assume
\phi_{i+1}\leq\phi_{i}
for all
i
. For each

i
we may choose
\alpha_{i}
such that
\phi_{i+1}\not\leq\alpha_{i}\phi_{i}
. Then
the ideal of
Q(x)
generated by
\{\alpha_{i}\phi_{i}\mid i\in\mathbb{N}\}
cannot
be generated by finitely many elements. This is a contradiction.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(3): Let
n
be the number of paths
\phi\in Q(x)
such
that there is more than one arrow starting at
t(\phi)
, and let
d
be
the maximal number of such arrows. Then there are at most
d^{n}

maximal paths starting at
x
.

(3)
\Rightarrow
(1): Let
I\subseteq Q(x)
be an
ideal. For each maximal path starting at
x
and given by a (finite or infinite)
sequence of arrows

\ldots\alpha_{2}\alpha_{1}
we check if there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\alpha_{n}\ldots\alpha_{2}\alpha_{1}

belongs to
I
. In that case we choose
n
minimal, and it is easily
checked that
I
is generated by all paths of this form.
∎

Let us add another criterion for
Q(x)
to be noetherian, following

[
8
,
9
]
. We consider the
path component

Q_{x}
, so the
full subquiver of
Q
with set of vertices
y\in Q_{0}
that admit a
path
x\to y
. An
oriented cycle
is a path
y\to y
of length
at least one.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
Q
be a quiver and
x
a vertex. Then the poset
Q(x)
is
noetherian if and only if there are arrows
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}

such that there is a decomposition

(2.4.1)

Q_{x}\smallsetminus\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\}=Q^{0}\amalg Q^{1}\amalg\cdots\amalg Q^{n}

into full and pairwise disjoint subquivers satisfying the following:

(1)

The quiver
Q^{0}
has only finitely many vertices and arrows, and
there is precisely one arrow starting at each vertex lying on an oriented cycle.

(2)

For
i\neq 0
each quiver
Q^{i}
is isomorphic
to the infinite quiver

\circ\longrightarrow\circ\longrightarrow\circ\longrightarrow\cdots.

(3)

Each arrow
\alpha_{i}

starts at a vertex of
Q^{0}
not lying on an oriented cycle and terminates at the source of
Q^{i}
.

Proof.

It is straightforward to check that the conditions on
Q_{x}
are
equivalent to the condition on
Q(x)
to have only finitely many
maximal paths starting at
x
. So the assertion follows from Proposition
2.3
.
∎

An immediate consequence is the following simple criterion for quivers with finitely many vertices.

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
Q
be quiver having only a finite number of vertices. Then
Q
is
left noetherian if and only if there are only finitely many arrows
and there is precisely one arrow starting at each vertex lying on an
oriented cycle.

Proof.

We apply Proposition
2.4
and note that in the
decomposition (
2.4.1
) we have
Q_{x}=Q^{0}
, since for

i\neq 0
the quiver
Q^{i}
has infinitely many vertices.
∎

3.
Locally noetherian categories

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an abelian category. We assume that the subobjects of any
object form a complete lattice and that for every directed set of
subobjects
(X_{i})_{i\in I}
of an object
X
and
Y\subseteq X
one
has

\left(\sum_{i\in I}X_{i}\right)\cap Y=\sum_{i\in I}(X_{i}\cap Y).

A collection of objects
(G_{i})_{i\in I}

generates

\mathcal{A}
if for each non-zero morphism
\phi\colon X\to Y

in
\mathcal{A}
we have
\phi\pi\neq 0
for some morphism
\pi\colon G_{i}\to X
.
An object of an abelian category is called
noetherian
if
the ascending chain condition holds for its subobjects. We write

\operatorname{Noeth}\mathcal{A}
for the full subcategory of noetherian objects in
\mathcal{A}
.
An abelian category is
cocomplete
if for any set of objects its coproduct exist.

Definition 3.1
.

The abelian category
\mathcal{A}
is
noetherian
if every object is
noetherian, and
\mathcal{A}
is
locally noetherian
if every object is
the directed union of its noetherian subobjects.

The following shows that our definition agrees with the usual one for
Grothendieck categories
[
6
, II.4]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

The category
\mathcal{A}
is locally noetherian if and only if there is
a collection of noetherian generators.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathcal{A}
is locally noetherian. Then we may take the noetherian
objects as the collection of generators, because for a morphism

\phi\colon X\to Y
with
X=\sum_{i}X_{i}
written as the directed union of
its noetherian subobjects, we have

\operatorname{Hom}(X,Y)\cong\lim_{i}\operatorname{Hom}(X_{i},Y).

If
\phi\neq 0
, then one of the composites
X_{i}\hookrightarrow X\to Y

needs to be non-zero.

Now suppose there is a collection of noetherian generators

(G_{i})_{i\in I}
. Let
X
be an object and
X^{\prime}\subseteq X
the
directed union of its noetherian subobjects. It is clear that every composite

G_{i}\to X\twoheadrightarrow X/X^{\prime}
is zero. Thus
X^{\prime}=X
.
∎

Let
Y\in\mathcal{A}
be an object, written as the directed union
Y=\sum_{j}Y_{j}
of a set of subobjects. Then it is easily checked that for each
noetherian object
X\in\mathcal{A}
the canonical map

(3.2.1)

\operatorname*{colim}_{j}\operatorname{Hom}(X,Y_{j})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(X,\sum_{j}Y_{j})

is a bijection. Clearly, the map is injective, and because
X
is
noetherian any morphism
X\to Y
factors through the inclusion

Y_{j_{0}}\to Y
for some index
j_{0}
. Here one uses the
condition (AB5). Thus the map is also surjective.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
X=\sum_{i}X_{i}
and
Y=\sum_{j}Y_{j}
be objects in a locally
noetherian category, written as directed union of their noetherian
subobjects. Then there is a natural isomorphism of abelian groups

(3.3.1)

\lim_{i}\operatorname*{colim}_{j}\operatorname{Hom}(X_{i},Y_{j})\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }\operatorname{Hom}(X,Y).

Proof.

Combine (
3.2.1
) with the fact that
\operatorname{Hom}(-,-)
preserves
colimits in the first argument.
∎

Now assume that
\operatorname{Noeth}\mathcal{A}
is essentially small. We write

\operatorname{Lex}((\operatorname{Noeth}\mathcal{A})^{\mathrm{op}},\mathrm{Ab})
for the category of additive contravariant functors

\operatorname{Noeth}\mathcal{A}\to\mathrm{Ab}
into the category of abelian groups that are left
exact and consider the
restricted Yoneda functor

\mathcal{A}\longrightarrow\bar{\mathcal{A}}:=\operatorname{Lex}((\operatorname{Noeth}\mathcal{A})^{\mathrm{op}},\mathrm{Ab}),\quad X\mapsto h_{X}:=\operatorname{Hom}(-,X)|_{\operatorname{Noeth}\mathcal{A}}.

Then the following is a slight generalisation of a theorem of Gabriel
[
6
]
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a locally noetherian abelian category. Then the category
\operatorname{Lex}((\operatorname{Noeth}\mathcal{A})^{\mathrm{op}},\mathrm{Ab})
is a locally noetherian and
cocomplete abelian category. The restricted Yoneda functor

\mathcal{A}\to\bar{\mathcal{A}}
is fully faithful and exact. It restricts to an
equivalence
\operatorname{Noeth}\mathcal{A}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\operatorname{Noeth}\bar{\mathcal{A}}
, and its essential image is
closed under subobjects.

Proof.

Let
X=\sum_{i}X_{i}
and
Y=\sum_{j}Y_{j}
be objects in
\mathcal{A}
, written as
directed union of their noetherian subobjects. Using
(
3.2.1
) we have

h_{X}\cong\operatorname*{colim}_{i}\operatorname{Hom}(-,X_{i})

and compute

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}(h_{X},h_{Y})

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname*{colim}_{i}\operatorname{Hom}(-,X_{i}),\operatorname*{colim}_{j}\operatorname{Hom}(-,Y_{j}))

\displaystyle\cong\lim_{i}\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Hom}(-,X_{i}),\operatorname*{colim}_{j}\operatorname{Hom}(-,Y_{j}))

\displaystyle\cong\lim_{i}\operatorname*{colim}_{j}\operatorname{Hom}(X_{i},Y_{j})

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}(X,Y).

The second isomorphism uses that
\operatorname{Hom}(-,-)
preserves colimits
in the first argument, the third follows from Yoneda’s lemma, and the
last is (
3.3.1
). Thus
\mathcal{A}\to\bar{\mathcal{A}}
is fully faithful. For
the rest of the assertion, see
[
6
, II.4]
.
∎

Example 3.5
.

Let
A
be a ring. Then the
regular module
A
is a finitely generated generator for the category
\operatorname{Mod}A
of left
A
-modules.
Thus
\operatorname{Mod}A
is locally noetherian if and only if

is left

4.
Representations of noetherian quivers

Let
Q
be a quiver and
\mathcal{Q}
its path category. A
representation
of

Q
in a category
\mathcal{A}
is by definition a functor
\mathcal{Q}\to\mathcal{A}
. Morphisms between representations are the natural
transformations, and we write
\operatorname{Rep}(Q,\mathcal{A})
for the category of representations.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a cocomplete abelian category. We assume that the
subobjects of any object form a complete lattice and that the
condition (AB5) holds. Given an object
M\in\mathcal{A}
, the functor

\operatorname{Hom}(M,-)\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathrm{Set}
admits a left adjoint, which takes a set
X

to the coproduct
M[X]
of copies of
M

indexed by the elements of
X
. Thus for each object
N\in\mathcal{A}
there is
a natural isomorphism

\operatorname{Hom}(M[X],N)\cong\operatorname{Hom}(X,\operatorname{Hom}(M,N)).

Given a vertex
x\in Q_{0}
, we write
\mathds{1}_{x}
for the subquiver of
Q

consisting of the single vertex
x
and no arrows.. Restriction along the inclusion
\mathds{1}_{x}\hookrightarrow Q

yields the evaluation functor

\operatorname{Rep}(Q,\mathcal{A})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Rep}(\mathds{1}_{x},\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{A},\quad M\mapsto M(x).

The left Kan extension provides a left adjoint, which is given by

\mathcal{A}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Rep}(Q,\mathcal{A}),\quad N\mapsto N[Q(x,-)].

Thus we have for objects
N\in\mathcal{A}
and
M\in\operatorname{Rep}(Q,\mathcal{A})
a natural
isomorphism

(4.0.1)

\operatorname{Hom}(N[Q(x,-)],M)\cong\operatorname{Hom}(N,M(x)).

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(M_{i})_{i\in I}
be a collection of generators of
\mathcal{A}
. Then the representations

M_{i}[Q(x,-)]
with
i\in I
and
x\in Q_{0}
generate
\operatorname{Rep}(Q,\mathcal{A})
.

Proof.

This follows from the adjunction isomorphism (
4.0.1
).
∎

An object
X
is
finitely generated
if for any directed set
of subobjects
(X_{i})_{i\in I}
with
X=\sum_{i\in I}X_{i}
there is

i_{0}\in I
such that
X=X_{i_{0}}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

If
M\in\mathcal{A}
is finitely generated, then
M[Q(x,-)]
is finitely
generated in
\operatorname{Rep}(Q,\mathcal{A})
for each
x\in Q_{0}
.

Proof.

Let
M[Q(x,-)]=\sum_{i\in I}N_{i}
be a directed union
of subobjects
N_{i}
. Then the adjunction isomorphism
(
4.0.1
) yields a morphism
M\to\sum_{i\in I}N_{i}(x)
, which
factors through
N_{i_{0}}(x)\to\sum_{i\in I}N_{i}(x)
for some
i_{0}\in I
since
M
is
finitely generated. Thus the identity
M[Q(x,-)]\to\sum_{i\in I}N_{i}

factors through
N_{i_{0}}\to\sum_{i\in I}N_{i}
, so
M[Q(x,-)]=N_{i_{0}}
.
∎

Let
Q
be a left noetherian quiver and fix
x\in Q_{0}
. We choose a
total order
\preceq
on the countable set of arrows in
Q
that lie on
a path in
Q(x)
. This order extends lexicographically to a total
order
\preceq
on
Q(x)
such that
\phi<\psi
implies

\phi\prec\psi
. Note that
(Q(x),\preceq)
is noetherian, because any
ideal with respect to
\preceq
is also one with respect to
\leq
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
Q
be a left noetherian quiver and
x
a vertex. If
M\in\mathcal{A}
is a
noetherian object, then the representation
M[Q(x,-)]
is
noetherian.

Proof.

Let
U\subseteq M[Q(x,-)]
be a subrepresentation and
\phi\in Q(x,y)
a
path. We set

Q(x,y)_{\phi}:=\{\psi\in Q(x,y)\mid\phi\preceq\psi\}

and write

U_{\phi}
for the image of the composite

U(y)\cap M[Q(x,y)_{\phi}]\hookrightarrow M[Q(x,y)_{\phi}]\twoheadrightarrow M,

where
the second map denotes the projection corresponding to
\phi
. For

\psi\in Q(x,y)
, we have

(4.3.1)

\phi\leq\psi\quad\implies\quad U_{\psi}\subseteq U_{\phi}.

More precisely, if there is a
path
\chi
such that
\phi=\chi\psi
, then composition with
\chi

induces a map

Q(x,y)_{\phi}\longrightarrow Q(x,y)_{\psi}

such that
\phi
is the only element that is mapped to
\psi
.
Thus
U(\chi)
maps
U_{\psi}
into
U_{\phi}
.

Given subrepresentations
U\subseteq V\subseteq M[Q(x,-)]
, we have

U_{\phi}\subseteq V_{\phi}
for all
\phi\in Q(x)
. If
U\neq V
, we claim
there exists
\phi\in Q(x)
such that
U_{\phi}\neq V_{\phi}
. To see this,
choose
y\in Q_{0}
such that
U(y)\neq V(y)
. Observe that the union

(4.3.2)

Q(x,y)=\bigcup_{\phi\in Q(x,y)}Q(x,y)_{\phi}

is directed, and therefore condition (AB5) implies

U(y)=\sum_{\phi\in Q(x,y)}U(y)\cap M[Q(x,y)_{\phi}].

Thus there exists

\phi\in Q(x,y)
with

U(y)\cap M[Q(x,y)_{\phi}]\neq V(y)\cap M[Q(x,y)_{\phi}].

Using that
Q(x)

is noetherian we may choose
\phi
maximal with respect to

\preceq
. Next observe that

(4.3.3)

Q(x,y)_{\phi}\smallsetminus\{\phi\}=\bigcup_{\phi\prec\psi}Q(x,y)_{\psi}

is a directed union. This yields the following commutative diagram
with exact rows, using again the condition (AB5):

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sum_{\phi\prec\psi}U(y)\cap M[Q(x,y)_{\psi}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U(y)\cap M[Q(x,y)_{\phi}]}

5.
Modules over path algebras

Let

be a quiver with a finite set of vertices and
A

is by definition the

A

A[Q]:=\coprod_{x,y\in Q_{0}}A[Q(x,y)]

with multiplication
induced by the composition of paths in
Q
. From the isomorphism
(

) it follows that
A[Q]
identifies with the endomorphism
ring of the representation
\coprod_{x\in Q_{0}}A[Q(x,-)]
, and that the
assignment
M\mapsto\coprod_{x\in Q_{0}}M(x)
induces an equivalence

(5.0.1)

\operatorname{Rep}(Q,\operatorname{Mod}A)\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }\operatorname{Mod}A[Q].

The following is well known, at least for path algebras over a field
[
3
, §1]
.

Corollary 5.1
.

Let
Q
be a quiver with a finite set of vertices and
A
a left
noetherian ring. Then the path algebra
A[Q]
is left noetherian if
and only if
Q
has only finitely many arrows and there is
precisely one arrow starting at each vertex lying on an oriented

Proof.

The assertion follows from Theorem
1.1
and

, because of the

).
∎

6.
Locally finite representations

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an abelian category. We assume that the
subobjects of any object form a complete lattice and that the
condition (AB5) holds. Recall that an object is of
finite
length
it it admits a finite composition series. An equivalent
condition is that both chain conditions hold for subobjects.

Definition 6.1
.

The abelian category
\mathcal{A}
is a
length category
if every object is
of finite length, and
\mathcal{A}
is
locally finite
if every object is
the directed union of its finite length subobjects.

Definition 6.2
.

A quiver is called
left finite
if for each vertex
x
the set
of paths starting at
x
is finite. A quiver is
right finite

if for each vertex
x
the set of paths terminating at
x
is
finite.

Using Proposition
2.3
it is easily checked that
Q(x)
is
left finite if and only if both chain conditions hold for ideals of

Q(x)
. This observation suggests the following analogue of
Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 6.3
.

For a quiver
Q
and a left artinian ring
A
the following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

The quiver
Q
is left finite.

(2)

The abelian category
\operatorname{Rep}(Q,\mathcal{A})
is locally finite for every
locally finite abelian category
\mathcal{A}
.

(3)

The abelian category
\operatorname{Rep}(Q,\operatorname{Mod}A)
is locally finite.

Proof.

Recall that a ring is left artinian if and only if its category of
left modules is locally finite
[
11
, VIII.1]
. On the other
hand, the quiver
Q
is left finite if and only if both chain conditions
hold for the ideals of
Q(x)
for all vertices
x
. Thus we can
adapt the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

7.
Gröbner enrichments

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a
small category
. Thus the objects in
\mathcal{C}
form a
set. For a pair of objects
x,y
let

\mathcal{C}(x,y)
denote the set of morphisms
x\to y
and let
\mathcal{C}(x)
denote
the set of all morphisms with domain
x
. A partial order
\preceq
on

\mathcal{C}(x)
is called a
Gröbner enrichment
if the following holds
for each
y\in\mathcal{C}
:

(G1)

\phi\prec\psi
implies
\omega\phi\prec\omega\psi
for all
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{C}(x,y)
and
\omega\in\mathcal{C}(y)
.

(G2)

The poset
\mathcal{C}(x,y)
is totally ordered and every
non-empty subset has a maximal element.

For any abelian category
\mathcal{A}
let
\operatorname{Rep}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{A})
denote the category of
functors
\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{A}
. The following result generalises Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a small category and
A
a left noetherian ring. Suppose
there exists a Gröbner enrichment for each object in
\mathcal{C}
. Then the
following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

The left ideals of morphisms starting at any fixed object satisfy the ascending chain condition.

(2)

The abelian category
\operatorname{Rep}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{A})
is locally noetherian for every
locally noetherian abelian category
\mathcal{A}
.

(3)

The abelian category
\operatorname{Rep}(\mathcal{C},\operatorname{Mod}A)
is locally noetherian.

Proof.

The special case that
\mathcal{C}
is the path category of a quiver is
Theorem
1.1
. In fact, the Gröbner enrichment for a
path category is specified right before Lemma
4.3
. The
proof of the general case is essentially the same. The crucial
ingredient is Lemma
4.3
where the Gröbner enrichment is
used; its definition is extracted from the proof of that lemma. For
instance, condition (G1) is needed for (
4.3.1
), and the
condition (G2) ensures that in (
4.3.2
) and (
4.3.3
)
the union is directed.
∎

Remark 7.2
.

The above theorem is a variation of the main result of

[
9
]
. This was rediscovered by Sam and Snowden in

[
10
]
and they provide many interesting examples. Their
notion of a
Gröbner category
and our notion of a Gröbner
enrichment basically agree, except that they require each poset

\mathcal{C}(x,y)
to be
well-ordered
. Passing from
\preceq
to its
opposite shows that both concepts are equivalent. The work in

[
9
,
10
]
restricts to representations in module
categories; so these proofs are different from the one given here.

Remark 7.3
.

The above definition of a Gröbner enrichment for every object in

\mathcal{C}
means that the category
\mathcal{C}
is enriched in the category of
posets satisfying condition (G2), with morphisms the strictly
increasing maps.

Remark 7.4
.

We write
\phi\leq\psi
for
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{C}(x)
if
\phi=\chi\psi
for
some morphism
\chi
. In many examples the partial order
\preceq

is a
refinement
of
\leq
, which means

\phi\leq\psi\quad\implies\quad\phi\preceq\psi.

In that case the condition in (G2) that every non-empty subset has a
maximal element is automatic when (1) in
Theorem
7.1
holds. So it seems natural to impose
this condition in (G2).

Remark 7.5
.

The above condition (G2) can be relaxed. We need that for every
collection
\mathcal{X}
of subsets of
\mathcal{C}(x,y)
that is closed under taking subsets and
directed unions the following holds: if
\mathcal{C}(x,y)\not\in\mathcal{X}
, there exists

\phi\in\mathcal{C}(x,y)
such that

\{\psi\in\mathcal{C}(x,y)\mid\phi\prec\psi\}\in\mathcal{X}\quad\text{and}\quad\{\psi\in\mathcal{C}(x,y)\mid\phi\preceq\psi\}\not\in\mathcal{X}.

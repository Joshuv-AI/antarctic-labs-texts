On the Dotsenko-Fateev complex twin
of the Selberg integral and its extensions

Abstract




The Selberg integral has a twin (‘the Dotsenko–Fateev integral’)
of the following form. We replace real variables
x_{k}
in the integrand

\prod|x_{k}|^{\sigma-1}\,|1-x_{k}|^{\tau-1}\prod|x_{k}-x_{l}|^{2\theta}
of the Selberg
integral by complex variables
z_{k}
,
integration over a cube we replace by an integration over the whole
complex space
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. According to Dotsenko, Fateev, and Aomoto, such integral is a product of Gamma functions.
We define and evaluate a family of beta integrals over spaces
\mathbb{C}^{m}\times\mathbb{C}^{m+1}\times\dots\times\mathbb{C}^{n}
, which for
m=n
gives the complex twin of
the Selberg integral mentioned above (with
three additional integer parameters).

1.
Results of the paper

Recall that the Selberg integral (Selberg, 1944) is given by

(1.1)

S_{n}(\sigma,\tau;\theta):=\int_{0}^{1}\dots\int_{0}^{1}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|x_{k}|^{\sigma-1}|1-x_{k}|^{\tau-1}\prod_{1\leqslant k<l\leqslant n}|x_{k}-x_{l}|^{2\theta}\prod_{j=1}^{n}dx_{j}=\\
=n!\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\Gamma(\sigma+(k-1)\theta)\,\Gamma(\tau+(k-1)\theta)\,\Gamma(k\theta)}{\Gamma(\sigma+\tau+(n+k-2)\theta)\,\Gamma(\theta)},

see a detailed discussion in Andrews, Askey, Roy
[
2
]
, Chapter 8, for original Selberg’s proof, see Lique, Thibon
[
25
]
.
Dotsenko and Fateev (1985,
[
12
]
, formula (B.9)) published (without a proof) the following identity, which looks like a twin of the Selberg integral,

(1.2)

S_{n}^{\mathbb C}(\sigma,\tau;\theta):=\\
:=\int_{{\mathbb C}^{n}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}|z_{k}|^{2\sigma-2}|1-z_{k}|^{2\tau-2}\prod_{1\leqslant k<l\leqslant n}|z_{k}-z_{l}|^{4\theta}\prod_{k}d\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits z_{k}\,d\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits z_{k}=\\
=\frac{1}{n!}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\sin\pi(\sigma+(j-1)\gamma)\,\sin\pi(\tau+(j-1)\theta)\,\sin\pi(j\theta)}{\sin\pi(\sigma+\tau+(n+j-2)\theta)\,\sin\pi(\theta)}\cdot S(\sigma,\tau;\theta)^{2}.

This integral was rediscovered by
Aomoto
[
3
]
(who also presented a proof), see also Mimachi, Yoshida
[
28
]
and a review of integrals of the Selberg type integrals by Forrester, Warnaar
[
13
]
.

The domain of convergence of this integral is narrow, see
[
13
]
,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\sigma>0,\quad\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\tau>0,\quad\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\theta>-\tfrac{1}{n},

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits(\sigma+(n-1)\theta)>0,\quad\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits(\tau+(n-1)\theta)>0;

(1.5)

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits(\sigma+\tau+(n-1)\theta)<1,\quad\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits(\sigma+\tau+2(n-1)\theta)<1.

1.1. Some notation.

For a complex variable
z
we denote

\,d\,{\overline{\overline{z}}}=d\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits z\,\,d\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits z=\tfrac{1}{2i}dz\,d\overline{z}.

By

\mathbf{a}=a\bigl|a^{\prime}

we denote a pair of complex numbers such that
a-a^{\prime}
is integer.
Denote by
\Lambda
the set of such pairs.
It splits into a countable family of complex lines
a-a^{\prime}=k
, where
k
ranges in
{\mathbb Z}
.
Denote

{\boldsymbol{1}}:=1\bigl|1,\qquad{\boldsymbol{2}}:=2\bigl|2,\qquad\lfloor\mathbf{a}\rfloor=\tfrac{1}{2}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits(a+a^{\prime}).

For a complex
z

denote

z^{\mathbf{a}}=z^{a}\,\overline{z}^{a^{\prime}}=|z|^{a+a^{\prime}}e^{i(\arg z)(a-a^{\prime})}.

In particular,

z^{\boldsymbol{1}}=|z|^{2},\qquad(-1)^{\mathbf{a}}=(-1)^{a-a^{\prime}}.

1.2. Gamma-function of the complex field.

Following Gelfand, Graev, and Retakh
[
14
]
, we define the Gamma function of the complex field by

(1.6)

\Gamma^{\mathbb C}(\mathbf{a})=\Gamma^{\mathbb C}(a|a^{\prime}):=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb C}z^{\mathbf{a}-{\boldsymbol{1}}}e^{2i\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits z}\,d\,{\overline{\overline{z}}}:=\\
=i^{a-a^{\prime}}\frac{\Gamma(a)}{\Gamma(1-a^{\prime})}=i^{a^{\prime}-a}\frac{\Gamma(a^{\prime})}{\Gamma(1-a)}=\frac{i^{a^{\prime}-a}}{\pi}\Gamma(a)\,\Gamma(a^{\prime})\sin\pi a^{\prime}.

The integral conditionally converges if
0<\lfloor\mathbf{a}\rfloor<1
, it admits a meromorphic continuation
to each complex line
a-a^{\prime}=k
.

The
beta function of the complex field
is defined by

(1.7)

\mathrm{B}^{\mathbb C}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}):=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb C}z^{\mathbf{a}-{\boldsymbol{1}}}(1-z)^{\mathbf{b}-{\boldsymbol{1}}}\,\,d\,{\overline{\overline{z}}}=\frac{\Gamma^{\mathbb C}(\mathbf{a})\,\Gamma^{\mathbb C}(\mathbf{b})}{\Gamma^{\mathbb C}(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b})}.

The domain of (absolute) convergence is

\lfloor\mathbf{a}\rfloor>0,\quad\lfloor\mathbf{b}\rfloor>0,\quad\lfloor\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}\rfloor<1.

\,d\,{\overline{\overline{z}}}_{n1},\dots,\,d\,{\overline{\overline{z}}}_{nn}
, two factors

\prod_{j=m}^{n-1}
disappear. The integrand transforms to

\prod_{p=1}^{n}z_{np}^{{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{1}}}(1-z_{np})^{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{1}}}\cdot\prod_{1\leqslant\alpha,\beta\leqslant n;\,\alpha\neq\beta}(z_{n\beta}-z_{n\alpha})^{{\boldsymbol{\nu}}-\frac{{\boldsymbol{1}}}{{\boldsymbol{2}}}}\cdot\prod_{1\leqslant p<q\leqslant n}(z_{np}-z_{nq})^{\boldsymbol{1}}.

We unite the second and third factors and come to the desired form (
1.13
).

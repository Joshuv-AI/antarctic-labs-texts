1.1.
Backgrounds

For
\mathcal{H}
a complex separable Hilbert space,
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})

The spectrum and essential spectrum of an operator

An operator

Such an

containing

and reducing

itself. Any two mnes of

are unitarily equivalent in a manner that fixes

We say

does not contain a non-trivial direct normal summand and

is irreducible if

does not have a non-trivial reducing invariant subspace.
The spectrum

is the union of

and some collection of bounded components of

The book
[
6

For a compact subset

For

Since the support of

The multiplication operator

5.
Spectral Mapping Theorems for Irreducible Subnormal Operators

Notice that the construction of
S
in the proof of Corollary
2.3
indicates that for
S
and
f
in the corollary,
S=\oplus_{n=1}^{\infty}S_{n},
where
S_{n}
is irreducible, and (
1.7
) holds. Our next result shows this phenomenon disappears when
S
is irreducible. As a result, we show that the second open question in
[
9
, page 386]
has an affirmative answer for irreducible subnormal operators.
In fact, Theorem
5.1
also holds for
S=\oplus_{n=1}^{m}S_{n},
where
m<\infty
and
S_{n}
is irreducible.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
S\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
be an irreducible subnormal operator and let
\mu
be the scalar-valued spectral measure for
N=mne(S).
Then for
f\in R^{\infty}(\sigma(S),\mu),
the following properties hold:

(1)
M_{S,f}
is invertible if and only if
f
is invertible in
R^{\infty}(\sigma(S),\mu).

(2)
M_{S,f}
is Fredholm if and only if
f=pf_{0}
, where
p
is a polynomial with only zeros
z_{1},z_{2},...,z_{m}\in\sigma(S)\setminus\sigma_{e}(S)
, multiplicity of
z_{i}
is
n_{i}
, and
f_{0}
is invertible in
R^{\infty}(\sigma(S),\mu).

In this case,

\ \text{ind}(M_{S,f})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}n_{i}\text{ind}(S-z_{i}).

Consequently, we have the following spectral mapping theorem for irreducible subnormal operators.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
S\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
be an irreducible subnormal operator and let
\mu
be the scalar-valued spectral measure for
N=mne(S).
Then for
f\in R^{\infty}(\sigma(S),\mu),

\ \sigma(M_{S,f})=cl(\rho_{S}(f),E_{S}^{\infty})

and

\ \sigma_{e}(M_{S,f})=cl_{e}(\rho_{S}(f),E_{S}^{\infty}).

The following is an important property for an irreducible subnormal operator. Recall that the removable set
\mathcal{R}_{S}
is defined as in (
3.11
). Set
\mathcal{F}_{S}=\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathcal{R}_{S}.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
S\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
be an irreducible subnormal operator and let
\mu
be the scalar-valued spectral measure for
N=mne(S).
Then for
\lambda\in\sigma(S),

\ \underset{\delta\rightarrow 0}{\underline{\lim}}\dfrac{\gamma(\mathbb{D}(\lambda,\delta)\setminus\mathcal{F}_{S})}{\delta}=\underset{\delta\rightarrow 0}{\underline{\lim}}\dfrac{\gamma(\mathbb{D}(\lambda,\delta)\cap\mathcal{R}_{S})}{\delta}>0.

To prove Theorem
5.3
, we need the following lemma, which combines Thomson’s coloring scheme (see
[

and
[

[

.

Lemma 5.4
.

For
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\epsilon_{0}>0
(depending on
\epsilon

,
then there is a sequence of simply connected regions
\{G_{n}\}_{n\geq m}
for some integer

satisfying:

\ \mathbb{D}\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)\subset G_{n}\subset G_{n+1}\subset\mathbb{D}

and for
\lambda\in\partial G_{n},
we have

\ \mathbb{D}(\lambda,n^{2}2^{-n})\subset G_{n+1}\text{ and }\gamma(\mathbb{D}(\lambda,2^{-n})\setminus E)<\epsilon 2^{-n}.

Let
f
be an analytic function outside the disc

satisfying the condition
f(\infty)=0.
We consider the Laurent expansion of

centered at

Lemma 5.5

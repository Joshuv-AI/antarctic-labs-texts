Shifts of semi-invariants and complete commutative subalgebras in polynomial Poisson algebras
I. K. Kozlov

Abstract
We study commutative subalgebras in the symmetric algebra S ⁡ ( 𝔤 ) S(\mathfrak{g}) of a finite-dimensional Lie algebra 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} . A. M. Izosimov introduced extended Mischenko-Fomenko subalgebras ℱ ~ a \tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{a} and gave a completeness criterion for them. We generalize his construction and extend Mischenko-Fomenko subalgebras with the shifts of all semi-invariants of 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} . We prove that the new commutative subalgebras have the same transcendence degree as ℱ ~ a \tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{a} .

Introduction

In this paper we discuss a slight generalization of the results of A. M. Izosimov from
[
7

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional Lie algebra. For simplicity sake we assume that underlying field is
\mathbb{C}
, although all statements remain true for an arbitrary algebraically closed field
\mathbb{K}
with
\operatorname{char}\mathbb{K}=0
. The symmetric algebra
S(\mathfrak{g})
can be naturally identified with the algebra of polynomials on the dual space
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. The algebra
S(\mathfrak{g})
has a natural Poisson bracket on it (called the
Lie-Poisson bracket
). The bracket is defined on linear functions by

\left\{\xi,\eta\right\}=[\xi,\eta],\qquad\forall\xi,\eta\in\mathfrak{g},

and is extended to all polynomials by the Leibnitz identity.

We study the commutative subalgebras in
S(g)
. If
C\subset S(\mathfrak{g})
is a commutative subalgebra, then

\operatorname{tr.deg.}C\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\dim\mathfrak{g}+\operatorname{ind}\mathfrak{g}\right).

(1)

If the equality holds in (
1
), then
C
is called a
complete commutative subalgebra
. Each complete commutative subalgebra in
S(\mathfrak{g})
can be regarded as an integrable system on the Poisson manifold
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. There are several well-known methods for constructing commutative subalgebras in
S(\mathfrak{g})
:

1.

The
argument shift method
was introduced by A. S. Mishchenko and A. T. Fomenko in
[
9
]
. It is a generalization of the S. V. Manakov’s construction
[
8
]
for the Lie algebra
\operatorname{so}(n)
. We describe a slight modification of the argument shift method, which is due to A. V. Brailov (see A. V. Bolsinov
[
3
]
).

Let
a\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
be an arbitrary regular element. Denote the stabilizer of
x\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
w.r.t. the coadjoint representation by

\mathfrak{g}_{x}=\left\{\xi\in\mathfrak{g}\,\,\bigr|\,\,\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{\xi}(x)=0\right\}.

There exist
m=\operatorname{ind}\mathfrak{g}
local analytic invariants
f_{1},\dots,f_{m}
of the coadjoint representation defined in a neighbourhood of
a
such that their differentials
df_{i}(a)
form a basis of

\mathfrak{g}_{a}
. Take the Taylor expansions of
f_{i}
at
a
:

f_{i}(a+\lambda x)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}f_{ij}(x)\lambda^{j},

where
f_{ij}(x)
is a homogeneous polynomial in
x
of degree
k
and
\lambda
is a formal parameter.

Definition 1.1
.

The algebra
\mathcal{F}_{a}\subset S(\mathfrak{g})
generated by polynomials

f_{ij},\qquad i=1,\dots,\operatorname{ind}\mathfrak{g},\,j>0,

is called the
algebra of (polynomial) shifts
.

It was proved in
[
9
]
that
\mathcal{F}_{a}
is a commutative subalgebra in
S(\mathfrak{g})
. Moreover, if
\mathfrak{g}
is semisimple, then
\mathcal{F}_{a}
is complete.

Completeness criterion for
\mathcal{F}_{a}
was proved by A. V. Bolsinov:

In general, the algebra of shifts
\mathcal{F}_{a}
is not complete. It was conjectured by A. S. Mischenko and A. T. Fomenko that
for any finite-dimensional Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, there exists a complete commutative subalgebra
C\subset S(\mathfrak{g})
. This conjecture was proved by S. T. Sadetov
[

(see also Bolsinov
[
2
]
). However, Sadetov’s family
\mathcal{F}\subset S(\mathfrak{g})
is essentially different from the algebra
\mathcal{F}_{a}
of shifts. In particular, it is not commutative w.r.t. the following Poisson bracket. For any
a\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
there is a natural constant Poisson bracket
\left\{\cdot,\cdot\right\}_{a}
on
S(\mathfrak{g})
(the so-called
frozen argument bracket
). On linear functions it is given by

\left\{\xi,\eta\right\}_{a}=\langle a,[\xi,\eta]\rangle,\qquad\xi,\eta\in\mathfrak{g},

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the pairing between
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Then the bracket
\left\{\cdot,\cdot\right\}_{a}
is extended to all polynomials by the Leibnitz identity.

•

The algebra of shifts
\mathcal{F}_{a}
is commutative w.r.t. the both brackets
\left\{\cdot,\cdot\right\}
and
\left\{\cdot,\cdot\right\}_{a}
.

•

The Sadetov’s algebra
\mathcal{F}
is commutative w.r.t.
\left\{\cdot,\cdot\right\}
, but (in general) is not commutative w.r.t.
\left\{\cdot,\cdot\right\}_{a}
.

The following conjecture was stated by A. V. Bolsinov and P. Zhang in
[
4

.

Generalised argument shift conjecture.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be an arbitrary finite-dimensional Lie algebra. Then for every regular element
a\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
there exists a complete family
\mathcal{G}_{a}\subset S(\mathfrak{g})
of polynomials in bi-involution, i.e. in involution w.r.t. the two brackets
\left\{\cdot,\cdot\right\}
and
\left\{\cdot,\cdot\right\}_{a}
.

3.

In
[
7
]
A. M. Izosimov introduced extended Mischenko-Fomenko subalgebras
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{a}
and gave a completeness criterion for them. This subalgebras are defined as follows. Let
t=\dim\mathfrak{g}-\operatorname{ind}\mathfrak{g}
. Fix a basis in
\mathfrak{g}
, and let
c_{ij}^{k}
be the structure constants in this basis. Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{x}=\left(\sum_{k}c_{ij}^{k}x_{k}\right)
be the structure matrix of
\mathfrak{g}
. Consider the Pfaffians
\operatorname{Pf}C_{i_{1}\dots i_{t}}(x)
of the
t\times t
minors of the structure matrix
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{x}
. The
fundamental semi-invariant

p_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)
is the the greatest common divisor of the Pfaffians
\operatorname{Pf}C_{i_{1}\dots i_{t}}(x)
. Next, consider the polynomial
p_{\mathfrak{g}}(a+\lambda x)
and expand it in powers of
\lambda
:

p_{\mathfrak{g}}(a+\lambda x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}(x)\lambda^{i},

(2)

where
n=\deg p_{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Definition 1.2
.

The
extended Mischenko-Fomenko subalgebra

\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{a}
is a subalgebra in
S(\mathfrak{g})
generated by all elements of the algebra of shifts
\mathcal{F}_{a}
and the polynomials
p_{1},\dots,p_{n}
.

Unlike the Sadetov’s algebra
\mathcal{F}
, and similar to the algebra of shifts
\mathcal{F}_{a}
, the polynomials from
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{a}
are in bi-involution.

Theorem 1.3
(A. M. Izosimov,
[
7
]
).

For each regular
a\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, the extended Mischenko-Fomenko subalgebra
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{a}
is commutative w.r.t. the both brackets
\left\{\cdot,\cdot\right\}
and
\left\{\cdot,\cdot\right\}_{a}
.

The completeness criterion is formulated is follows.

•

Let
\operatorname{Sing}_{0}
be the union of all irreducible components of
\operatorname{Sing}
that have dimension
\dim\mathfrak{g}-1
. (If
\operatorname{codim}\operatorname{Sing}\geq 2
, then
\operatorname{Sing}_{0}=\emptyset
.)

•

Consider the subset

\operatorname{Sing}_{\mathfrak{b}}=\left\{y\in\operatorname{Sing}_{0}\,\,\bigr|\,\,\mathfrak{g}_{y}\simeq\operatorname{aff}(1)\oplus\mathbb{C}^{s}\right\}\subset\operatorname{Sing}_{0},

where
\operatorname{aff}(1)
is the
2
-dimensional non-abelian Lie algebra. In can be shown that
\operatorname{Sing}_{\mathfrak{b}}
is always Zariski open in
\operatorname{Sing}_{0}
, but might be empty.

Theorem 1.4
(A. M. Izosimov,
[
7
]
).

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional complex Lie algebra and
a\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
be a regular element. The extended Mischenko-Fomenko subalgebra
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{a}
is complete if and only if
\operatorname{Sing}_{\mathfrak{b}}
is dense in
\operatorname{Sing}_{0}
.

Remark 1.5
.

If
\operatorname{codim}\operatorname{Sing}\geq 2
, then
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{a}
is complete, because it contains the algebra of shifts
\mathcal{F}_{a}\subseteq\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{a}
. In the case
\operatorname{codim}\operatorname{Sing}\geq 2
we formally have

\operatorname{Sing}_{0}=\operatorname{Sing}_{\mathfrak{b}}=\emptyset.

(3)

We formally assume that (
3
) satisfies the condition “
\operatorname{Sing}_{\mathfrak{b}}
is dense in
\operatorname{Sing}_{0}
”, and Theorem
1.4
holds.

A non-zero element
g\in S(\mathfrak{g})
is a
semi-invariant with weight

\chi_{g}\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
if

\left\{f,g\right\}_{x}=\chi_{g}(df)g(x)

(4)

for any other
f\in S(\mathfrak{g})
. In this paper we study the following polynomial algebra.

Definition 1.6
.

The
algebra of shift of semi-invariants

\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{si}}_{a}
is a subalgebra of
S(\mathfrak{g})
generated by all elements of the algebra of shifts
\mathcal{F}_{a}
and all the functions
g(x+\lambda a)
, for all semi-invariants
g(x)
and all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
.

The next statement was proved in
[
7
, Section 4]
.

Theorem 1.7
(A. M. Izosimov,
[
7
]
).

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional Lie algebra,
a\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Then for any two semi-invariants
f,g\in S(\mathfrak{g})
and any
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{C}
,

\left\{f(a+\lambda x),g(a+\lambda x)\right\}=0,\qquad\left\{f(a+\lambda x),g(a+\lambda x)\right\}_{a}=0.

Thus, similar to
\mathcal{F}_{a}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{a}
, the polynomials in
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{si}}_{a}
are in bi-involution.

Corollary 1.8
.

For each regular
a\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
algebra of shift of semi-invariants
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{si}}_{a}
is commutative w.r.t. the both brackets
\left\{\cdot,\cdot\right\}
and
\left\{\cdot,\cdot\right\}_{a}
.

The difference between
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{si}}_{a}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{a}
is quite simple:

•

The extended Mischenko-Fomenko subalgebra
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{a}
is generated by
\mathcal{F}_{a}
and the shifts of the fundamental semi-invariant
p_{\mathfrak{g}}(a+\lambda x)
.

•

The algebra of shift of semi-invariants
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{si}}_{a}
is generated by
\mathcal{F}_{a}
and the shifts of all semi-invariants
g(a+\lambda x)
.

Obviously,
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{a}\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{si}}_{a}
. Our main result is

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional Lie algebra and
a\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
be a regular element. Then the algebra of shift of semi-invariants
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{si}}_{a}
and the extended Mischenko-Fomenko subalgebra
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{a}
have the same transcendence degree:

\operatorname{tr.deg.}\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{si}}_{a}=\operatorname{tr.deg.}\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{a}.

(5)

Consider the following subspace of
T^{*}_{x}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\simeq\mathfrak{g}
for the algebra of shifts
\mathcal{F}_{a}
:

d\mathcal{F}_{a}(x)=\left\{df(x)\,\,\bigr|\,\,f\in\mathcal{F}_{a}\right\},\qquad x\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

Also consider similar subspaces
d\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{a}(x)
and
d\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{si}}_{a}(x)
for the algebras
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{a}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{si}}_{a}
respectively. Since
\dim d\mathcal{F}_{a}(x)\leq\operatorname{tr.deg.}\mathcal{F}_{a}
(and the similar inequality holds for
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{a}(x)
and
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{si}}_{a}
) we get the following.

Corollary 1.10
.

Let
a\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
be regular. Then

d\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{a}(x)\subseteq d\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{si}}_{a}(x)\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\dim d\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{si}}_{a}(x)\leq\operatorname{tr.deg.}\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{a}

for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Thus, for all
x
such that
\dim d\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{a}(x)=\operatorname{tr.deg.}\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{a}
we have

d\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{a}(x)=d\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{si}}_{a}(x).

(6)

In particular, (
6
) holds on an open dense subset of
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.

Roughly speaking, the distribution
d\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{si}}_{a}
is obtained from the distribution
d\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{a}
by “increasing some singular subspaces”. The completeness criterion for
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{si}}_{a}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{a}
is, obviously, the same.

Corollary 1.11
.

For a regular
a\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
the algebra of shift of semi-invariants
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{si}}_{a}
is complete if and only if
\operatorname{Sing}_{\mathfrak{b}}
is dense in
\operatorname{Sing}_{0}
.

2
Roots of semi-invariants

In order to proof Theorem
1.9
we need the following Lemma
2.1
about the roots of semi-invariants. Below we consider Poisson brackets for (local) analytic functions. We extend the Lie–Poisson bracket
\left\{\cdot,\cdot\right\}
and the frozen argument bracket
\left\{\cdot,\cdot\right\}_{a}
to (local) analytic functions by

\left\{f,g\right\}=\langle x,[df(x),dg(x)]\rangle,\qquad\left\{f,g\right\}_{a}=\langle a,[df(x),dg(x)]\rangle.

Here we regard
df(x)
and
dg(x)
as elements of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\simeq T^{*}_{x}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. The following statement was proved in
[
11
]
, we repeat the proof for completeness sake.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
g(x)
be a semi-invariant with weight
\chi_{g}
on
\mathfrak{g}
and
a\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
be an arbitrary element. Assume that
\lambda(x)
is a solution of

g(x-\lambda(x)a)=0,

(7)

which is locally analytic on
U\subset\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Then for any locally analytic function
h(x)
on
U
we have the equality

\left\{\lambda,h\right\}=\lambda\left\{\lambda,h\right\}_{a}.

(8)

Remark 2.2
.

If we consider two matrices

\mathcal{A}_{x}=\left(\sum_{i}c^{i}_{jk}x_{i}\right),\qquad\mathcal{A}_{a}=\left(\sum_{i}c^{i}_{jk}a_{i}\right),

(9)

then (
8
) can be rewritten as

d\lambda(x)\in\operatorname{Ker}\left(\mathcal{A}_{x}-\lambda(x)\mathcal{A}_{a}\right).

(10)

The condition (
10
) for the characterisitc numbers (=eigenvalues) of compatible Poisson brackets is well-known, for more details see
[
11
]
.

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

Let us use the explicit formulas for the Lie-Poisson and frozen argument brackets:

\left\{\lambda,h\right\}=\langle x,[d\lambda,dh]\rangle,\qquad\left\{\lambda,h\right\}_{a}=\langle a,[d\lambda,dh]\rangle.

Then

\left\{\lambda,h\right\}-\lambda\left\{\lambda,h\right\}_{a}=\langle x-\lambda a,[d\lambda,dh]\rangle.

(11)

Differentianting (
7
) by
x
we get

d\lambda(x)=\frac{dg(x-\lambda a)}{\langle dg(x-\lambda a),a\rangle},

if
dg(x-\lambda a)\not=0
. Since
dg(x-\lambda a)\not=0
for generic
x\in U
, (
11
) takes the form

\left\{\lambda,h\right\}-\lambda\left\{\lambda,h\right\}_{a}=\frac{1}{\langle dg(x-\lambda a),a\rangle}\langle x-\lambda a,[dg(x-\lambda a),dh(x)]\rangle.

(12)

Note that the condition (
4
) in the definition of semi-invariants can be written as

\langle x,[df(x),dg(x)]\rangle=\chi_{g}(df(x))\cdot g(x)

(13)

In local coordinates (
13
) can be written as

\sum_{k,j}c_{ij}^{k}x_{k}\frac{\partial g}{\partial x^{j}}(x)=\left(\chi_{g}\right)_{i}\cdot g(x).

(14)

If in (
14
) we replace
x
with
x-\lambda a
, then for any function
h(x)
that is analytic on
U
we get

\langle x-\lambda a,[h(x),dg(x-\lambda a)]\rangle=\chi_{g}(dh(x))\cdot g(x-\lambda a).

(15)

Substituting (
15
) in (
12
) we get

\left\{\lambda,h\right\}-\lambda\left\{\lambda,h\right\}_{a}=-\frac{1}{\langle dg(x-\lambda a),a\rangle}\chi_{g}(dh(x))\cdot g(x-\lambda a)=0.

Here the last equation holds by (
7
). Lemma
2.1
is proved. ∎

Proof of Theorem
1.9

Fix a regular
a\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Let
g(x)
be a semi-invariant on
\mathfrak{g}
. Expand the polynomial
g(a+\lambda x)
in powers of
\lambda
:

g(a+\lambda x)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}g_{i}(x)\lambda^{i},\qquad m=\deg g(x).

Obviously, the algebra
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{si}}_{a}
contains the polynomials
g(a+\lambda x)
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
if and only if it contains all the elements
g_{i}(x),i=1,\dots,m
. Thus, it suffices to prove that each polynomial
g_{i}(x)
is algebraic over the fraction field
\operatorname{Frac}\left(\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{a}\right)
. If some
g_{i}(x)
is not algebraic over
\operatorname{Frac}\left(\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{a}\right)
, then

dg_{i}(x)\not\in d\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{a}(x)

on a Zariski open non-empty subset
U\subset\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Therefore, it suffices to prove the following.

Lemma 3.1
.

There exist a (non-empty) open subset
W_{g}\subset\mathfrak{g}^{*}
such that

dg_{i}(x)\in d\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{a}(x),\qquad i=1,\dots,m,\quad\forall x\in W_{g}.

(16)

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

Consider the equation

g(x-\lambda a)=0.

(17)

First, we show that (
16
) follows from a similar condition for the roots of (
17
). We use the following trivial statement.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
g(x)\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a homogeneous polynomial. Consider its factorization into irreducible factors:

g(x)=h_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdot\dots\cdot h_{p}^{k_{p}}.

Then there exists a Zariski open non-empty set
U_{g}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that the equation (
17
) has
d=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{p}\deg h_{j}
distinct roots
\lambda_{1}(x),\dots,\lambda_{d}(x)
for all
x\in U_{g}
.

It is well-known that the roots
\lambda_{i}(x)
of (
17
) are locally analytic functions on
U
, defined up to permutation (see e.g.
[
6
, p.362]
). Locally we have

g(x-\lambda a)=g(a)\prod_{i=1}^{d}(\lambda_{i}(x)-\lambda)^{s_{i}}.

It is easy to see that the functions
g_{1},\dots,g_{m}
are, up to a constant factor, elementary symmetric polynomials of the functions
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{d}
taken with multiplicities. Thus, similar to
[
7
, Proposition 5.1]
we get the following.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
U_{g}
be the subset from Proposition
3.2
,
\lambda_{i}(x),i=1,\dots,d
be the roots of (
17
), and
g_{j}(x),j=1,\dots,m
be given by (
11
). Then

\operatorname{span}\left(d\lambda_{1}(x),\dots,d\lambda_{d}(x)\right)=\operatorname{span}\left(dg_{1}(x),\dots,dg_{m}(x)\right),\qquad\forall x\in U_{g}.

By Proposition
3.3
, in order to prove (
16
), it suffices to show that

d\lambda_{i}(x)\in d\mathcal{F}_{a}(x),\qquad i=1,\dots,d,

(18)

on some open subset
W_{g}\subset U_{g}
. Next, let
U_{p_{\mathfrak{g}}}
be the open subset for the fundamental semi-invariant
p_{\mathfrak{g}}
from Proposition
3.2
. Consider an open subset
W_{g}\subset U_{g}\cap U_{p_{\mathfrak{g}}}
such that the following
2
conditions holds:

1.

The line
x+\lambda a
does not intersect the subset

\operatorname{Sing}_{1}=\operatorname{Sing}-\operatorname{Sing}_{0}

(note that
\operatorname{codim}\operatorname{Sing}_{1}\geq 2
).

2.

For each root
\lambda_{i}(x)
of the equation (
17
) for the semi-invariant
g(x)

•

either
p_{\mathfrak{g}}(x-\lambda_{i}(x)a)\equiv 0
for all
x\in W_{g}
,

•

or
p_{\mathfrak{g}}(x-\lambda_{i}(x)a)\not=0
for all
x\in W_{g}
.

We claim that (
18
) holds on
W_{g}
. There are
2
cases:

•

p_{\mathfrak{g}}(x-\lambda_{i}(x)a)\equiv 0
on
W_{g}
. Since
W_{g}\subset U_{p_{\mathfrak{g}}}
we can apply Proposition
3.3
for the fundamental semi-invariant
p_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)
. We get that

d\lambda_{i}(x)\in\operatorname{span}\left(dp_{1}(x),\dots,dp_{n}(x)\right)\subset d\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{a},\qquad\forall x\in W_{g}.

Here the functions
p_{j}(x)
are given by (
2
).

•

p_{\mathfrak{g}}(x-\lambda_{i}(x)a)\not=0
on
W_{g}
. By Lemma
2.1
and Remark
2.2

d\lambda_{i}(x)\in\operatorname{Ker}\left(\mathcal{A}_{x}-\lambda\mathcal{A}_{a}\right)

By
[
4
, Remark 1]
for any regular
a\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
the subspace

d\mathcal{F}_{a}(x)=\sum_{x-\lambda a\not\in\operatorname{Sing}}\operatorname{Ker}\left(\mathcal{A}_{x}-\lambda\mathcal{A}_{a}\right),

for any
x\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
such that the line
x+\lambda a
does not intersect
\operatorname{Sing}_{1}
. Thus,

d\lambda_{i}(x)\in d\mathcal{F}_{a}(x)\subseteq d\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{a}(x),\qquad\forall x\in W_{g}.

We proved that (
18
) holds on
W_{g}
. Hence, by Proposition
3.3
the required condition (
16
) also holds on
W_{g}
. This proves Lemma
3.1
, and thus Theorem
1.9
. ∎

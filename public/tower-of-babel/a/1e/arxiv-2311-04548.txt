Characterization of continuous homomorphisms on entire slice monogenic functions
Stefano Pinton, Peter Schlosser

Abstract
. This paper is inspired by a class of infinite order differential operators arising in quantum mechanics. They turned out to be an important tool in the investigation of evolution of superoscillations with respect to quantum fields equations. Infinite order differential operators act naturally on spaces of holomorphic functions or on hyperfunctions. Recently, infinite order differential operators have been considered and characterized on the spaces of entire monogenic functions, i.e., functions that are in the kernel of the Dirac operators. The focus of this paper is the characterization of infinite order differential operators that act continuously on a different class of hyperholomorphic functions, called slice hyperholomorphic functions with values in a Clifford algebra or also slice monogenic functions. This function theory has a very reach associated spectral theory and both the function theory and the operator theory in this setting are subjected to intensive investigations. Here we introduce the concept of proximate order and establish some fundamental properties of entire slice monogenic functions that are crucial for the characterization of infinite order differential operators acting on entire slice monogenic functions.



AMS Classification: 32A15, 32A10, 47B38.

Key words
: Infinite order differential operators, proximate order for slice monogenic functions, continuous homomorphisms.

Introduction

Extensive research has been conducted on infinite order differential operators for a considerable period of time. In recent years, their significance in examining the evolution of superoscillations as initial data for the Schrödinger equation has emerged as a fundamental area of study. Superoscillatory functions arise in various fields of science and technology. In quantum mechanics, they are the result of Aharonov’s weak values, as outlined in
[
1
,
7
]
. Investigating their time evolution as initial data for quantum field equations represents a crucial problem in the domain of quantum mechanics. Further details can be found in the monograph
[
5
]
, as well as in
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
6
,
14
,
15
,
20
,
21
,
38
]
. Moreover, we point out that superoscillations have been studied not only in quantum mechanics, but also in various fields including optics, antenna theory and signal processing. In particular, it is believed that they can be used to improve the resolution of imaging and spectral analysis techniques, as they allow for obtaining detailed information about structures at smaller scales than those allowed by conventional waves. Superoscillations are still an active area of study, and there are still many open questions about their nature and potential applications.

Investigating the evolution of superoscillatory functions under the time dependent Schrödinger equation presents highly intricate problems. A natural functional analytic framework for this purpose is the space of entire functions with specific growth conditions. In fact, addressing the Cauchy problem for the Schrödinger equation with superoscillatory initial data, we are naturally
led to study infinite order differential operators.

In order to explain how infinite order differential operators appear in quantum mechanics it is enough to consider the simple case of the free particle for Schrödinger equation with initial datum that is a superoscillatory function. Precisely, we consider the Cauchy problem

\begin{split}i\frac{\partial\psi(x,t)}{\partial t}&=-\frac{\partial^{2}\psi(x,t)}{\partial x^{2}}\\
\psi(x,0)&=F_{n}(x,a):=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\big(\frac{1+a}{2}\big)^{n-k}\big(\frac{1-a}{2}\big)^{k}e^{i(1-2k/n)x}\end{split}

(1.1)

for
a\in\mathbb{R}
,
a>1
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
where
F_{n}(x,a)
is a superoscillatory function outcome of Aharonov’s weak values. In
[
3
]
it has been shown that the solution
\psi_{n}(x,t)
, can be written as:

\psi_{n}(x,t)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(it)^{m}}{m!}\frac{d^{2m}}{dx^{2m}}F_{n}(x,a)

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
and to study the limit, as
n\to\infty
for
\psi_{n}(x,t)
entails the investigation of persistence of superoscillation during the time evolution. The crucial fact is to show the continuity of the operator

U\Big(\frac{d}{dx}\Big):=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(it)^{m}}{m!}\frac{d^{2m}}{dx^{2m}}

on a class of functions that contains
F_{n}(x,a)
. In order to study the continuity properly, we recall that we have to extend both the operator
U(\frac{d}{dx})
and the function

to the complex setting replacing the real variable
x
by the complex variable

. The natural settings for this problem are the spaces
\mathcal{A}_{p}

, i.e., the space of entire functions with either order lower than

or order equal to
p
and finite type, i.e., it consists of functions

for which there exist constants
B,C>0
such that

|f(z)|\leq Ce^{B|z|^{p}}.

(1.2)

The convergence in these spaces is defined as follows. Let
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}},f_{0}\in\mathcal{A}_{p}
. Then
f_{n}\to f_{0}
in
\mathcal{A}_{p}
if there exists some
B>0
such that

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sup_{z\in\mathbb{C}}\Big|(f_{n}(z)-f_{0}(z))e^{-B|z|^{p}}\Big|=0.

(1.3)

Proving the continuity of the infinite order differential operator
U(\frac{d}{dz})
on
\mathcal{A}_{1}
implies that

\lim_{n\to\infty}U\Big(\frac{d}{dz}\Big)F_{n}(z,a)=U\Big(\frac{d}{dz}\Big)\lim_{n\to\infty}F_{n}(z,a).

Thus we have
\lim_{n\to\infty}\psi_{n}(z,t)=e^{iaz-ia^{2}t}
in
\mathcal{A}_{1}
. Taking the restriction to the real axis for the complex variable
z
, we have
\lim_{n\to\infty}\psi_{n}(x,t)=e^{iax-ia^{2}t}
uniformly on the compact sets of
\mathbb{R}
for the space variable.

More generally when we investigate the Schrödinger evolution of superoscillatory functions under different potentials

2.
Preliminary results on slice monogenic functions

In this section we recall basic results on slice monogenic functions (see Chapter 2 in
[
19
]
) and also introduce the notion of proximate order functions (see the book
[
35
]
). We recall that
\mathbb{R}_{n}
is the real Clifford algebra over
n
imaginary units
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
, satisfying the commutation relation

e_{i}e_{j}=-e_{j}e_{i}\qquad\text{and}\qquad e_{i}^{2}=-1,\qquad i\neq j\in\{1,\dots,n\}.

Any
Clifford number

a\in\mathbb{R}_{n}
can uniquely be written as

a=a_{0}+a_{1}e_{1}+\ldots+a_{n}e_{n}+\dots+a_{12...n}e_{1}e_{2}...e_{n}=\sum\nolimits_{A}a_{A}e_{A},

where the last sum is over all ordered subsets
A=\{i_{1},\dots,i_{r}\}\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}
and the basis elements are
e_{A}:=e_{i_{1}}\dots e_{i_{r}}
. Note that for
A=\emptyset
we set
e_{\emptyset}:=1
. The
Euclidean norm
of a Clifford number
a\in\mathbb{R}_{n}
is

|a|^{2}:=\sum\nolimits_{A}|a_{A}|^{2}.

Any element of the form

x=x_{0}+\underline{x}=x_{0}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}x_{\ell}e_{\ell}

will be called
paravector
and we denote by
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
the set of all paravectors. Note that no ambiguity arises since we can identify any
(n+1)
-vector
(x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
of real numbers with
x_{0}+e_{1}x_{1}+\ldots+e_{n}x_{n}
. The
conjugate
of a paravector
x
is given by

\overline{x}:=x_{0}-\underline{x}=x_{0}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}x_{\ell}e_{\ell}.

Recall that
\mathbb{S}
is the sphere

\mathbb{S}=\{\underline{x}=e_{1}x_{1}+\ldots+e_{n}x_{n}\;|\;x_{1}^{2}+\dots+x_{n}^{2}=1\},

where every
j\in\mathbb{S}
satisfies
j^{2}=-1
. Given an element
x=x_{0}+\underline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
let us define

[x]:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\;|\;y=x_{0}+j|\underline{x}|,\,j\in\mathbb{S}\},

as the
(n-1)
-dimensional sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. For any
j\in\mathbb{S}
, the vector space

\mathbb{C}_{j}:=\{u+jv\;|\;u,v\in\mathbb{R}\}=\mathbb{R}+j\mathbb{R}

is an isomorphic copy of the complex numbers, passing through the real line in the direction of the imaginary unit
j
. Finally, a subset
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is called
axially symmetric
, if for every
x\in U
the whole
(n-1)
-sphere
[x]\subseteq U
is contained in
U
.

Next we define the set of slice monogenic functions (also called slice hyperholomorphic functions), acting on paravectors
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and having values in the full Clifford algebra
\mathbb{R}_{n}
.

Definition 2.1

(Slice monogenic functions)
.

Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be an axially symmetric open set and let
\mathcal{U}=\{(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:u+\mathbb{S}v\subseteq U\}
. A function
f:U\to\mathbb{R}_{n}
is called
left slice monogenic
, if it is of the form

f(x)=f_{0}(u,v)+jf_{1}(u,v),\qquad x=u+jv\in U,

(2.1)

where the two functions
f_{0},f_{1}:\mathcal{U}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{n}
satisfy the compatibility conditions

f_{0}(u,-v)=f_{0}(u,v)\qquad\text{and}\qquad f_{1}(u,-v)=-f_{1}(u,v),

(2.2)

as well as the Cauchy-Riemann-equations

\frac{\partial}{\partial u}f_{0}(u,v)=\frac{\partial}{\partial v}f_{1}(u,v)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\frac{\partial}{\partial v}f_{0}(u,v)=-\frac{\partial}{\partial u}f_{1}(u,v).

(2.3)

The set of left slice monogenic functions will be denoted by
\mathcal{S\!M}_{L}(U)
.

Remark 2.2
.

Analogously, one can also define right slice monogenic functions by simply replacing the decomposition (
2.1
) by

f(s)=f_{0}(u,v)+f_{1}(u,v)j,\qquad x=u+jv\in U.

However, for the rest of the paper we will only consider left slice monogenic functions, although all the results can also be written for right slice monogenic functions.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S\!M}_{L}(U)
. Then for any
x\in U
we define the
left slice derivative
as

\partial_{S}f(x):=\lim_{p\to x,p\in\mathbb{C}_{j}}(p-x)^{-1}(f(p)-f(x)),

(2.4)

where for nonreal
x
the imaginary unit
j\in\mathbb{S}
is chosen such that
x\in\mathbb{C}_{j}
and for real
x
one may choose
j\in\mathbb{S}
arbitrary.

We remark that
\partial_{S}f(x)
is uniquely defined and independent of the choice of
j\in\mathbb{S}
, even if
x
is real. Moreover, we note that the slice derivative of
f
coincides with

\partial_{S}f(x)=\partial_{x_{0}}f(x)=f_{j}^{\prime}(x),

(2.5)

where
\partial_{x_{0}}f
is the partial derivative with respect to
x_{0}
and
f_{j}^{\prime}
is the complex derivative of the restriction
f_{j}=f|_{\mathbb{C}_{j}}
.

Theorem 2.4
.

For
a\in\mathbb{R}
,
r>0
let
B_{r}(a)
the open ball with radius
r
centered at
a
. Then for every
f\in\mathcal{S\!M}_{L}(B_{r}(a))
there holds

f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(x-a)^{k}\frac{1}{k!}\partial_{x_{0}}^{k}f(a),\qquad x\in B_{r}(a).

(2.6)

We now recall the natural product of two functions, that preserves slice monogenicity.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be open and axially symmetric. Then for any
f,g\in\mathcal{S\!M}_{L}(U)
define their
star product

f\star_{L}g:=f_{0}g_{0}-f_{1}g_{1}+j(f_{1}g_{0}+f_{0}g_{1}),

with the functions
f_{0},f_{1},g_{0},g_{1}
from the decomposition (
2.1
).

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}x^{k}a_{k}
and
g(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}x^{k}b_{k}
be two left slice monogenic power series. Then their star product is given by

(f\star_{L}g)(x)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}x^{\ell}\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}a_{k}b_{\ell-k}.

(2.7)

For
x,s\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
x\not\in[s]
, the Cauchy kernel for left slice monogenic functions is given by

S_{L}^{-1}(s,x):=-(x^{2}-2s_{0}x+|s|^{2})^{-1}(x-\overline{s})=(s-\overline{x})(s^{2}-2x_{0}s+|x|^{2})^{-1}.

(2.8)

With this kernel there holds the following Clifford algebra version of the Cauchy integral formula.

Theorem 2.7

(The Cauchy formula)
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be axially symmetric, open, bounded and the boundary
\partial(U\cap\mathbb{C}_{j})
be a finite union of continuously differentiable Jordan curves. If we set
ds_{j}=-jds
for some
j\in\mathbb{S}
, then for
f
which is left slice monogenic on a neighborhood of
\overline{U}
, there holds

f(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\partial(U\cap\mathbb{C}_{j})}S_{L}^{-1}(s,x)ds_{j}f(s),\qquad x\in U.

(2.9)

Moreover, the integral depend neither on
U
nor on the imaginary unit
j\in\mathbb{S}
.

After the basic facts on slice monogenic functions we introduce the notion of proximate order functions which will then be used in Section
3
to introduce function spaces of exponentially bounded entire slice monogenic functions.

Definition 2.8

(Proximate order)
.

A differentiable function
\varrho:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is called a
proximate order function
for the order
\rho>0
, if it satisfies

(1)

\lim_{r\to\infty}\varrho(r)=:\rho>0
,

(2)

\lim_{r\to\infty}\varrho^{\prime}(r)r\ln(r)=0
.

It is also possible to define proximate order for the order
\rho=0
, but the upcoming results do not hold in this case. Any proximate order function admits the following properties, for a proof see
[
35
, Proposition 1.19]
.

Lemma 2.9

(Properties of proximate orders)
.

Let
\varrho
be a proximate order. Then there exists some
r_{0}>0
, such that

i)

r\mapsto r^{\varrho(r)}
is strictly increasing on
[r_{0},\infty)
,

ii)

\lim\limits_{r\to\infty}r^{\varrho(r)}=\infty
.

Definition 2.10

(Normalized proximate order)
.

A proximate order function
\varrho
is called
normalized
if

i)

r\mapsto r^{\varrho(r)}
is strictly increasing on
(0,\infty)
,

ii)

\lim\limits_{r\to 0^{+}}r^{\varrho(r)}=0

For a normalized proximate order it is then clear that
r\mapsto r^{\varrho(r)}
maps
(0,\infty)
bijectively onto
(0,\infty)
and we denote by

\varphi:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)\text{ the inverse function of }r\mapsto r^{\varrho(r)}.

(2.10)

Moreover, we set the numbers

G_{0}=G_{\varrho,0}:=1\qquad\text{and}\qquad G_{\ell}=G_{\varrho,\ell}:=\frac{\varphi(\ell)^{\ell}}{(e\rho)^{\ell/\rho}},\qquad\ell\in\mathbb{N}.

(2.11)

Remark 2.11
.

Note that by Lemma
2.9
it is possible to construct for any given proximate order function
\rho
a normalized proximate order
\hat{\rho}
such that
\rho(r)=\hat{\rho}(r)
for every
r\geq r_{0}
large enough. One possible choice is the function

\hat{\varrho}(r):=\begin{cases}\varrho(r_{0})-\frac{\rho}{4}\sin\big(4\frac{\varrho^{\prime}(r_{0})}{\rho}(r_{0}-r)\big),&r\in[0,r_{0}],\\
\varrho(r),&r\in[r_{0},\infty).\end{cases}

.

Let

(r+s)^{\varrho(r+s)}\leq 2^{\rho+\varepsilon}\big(r^{\varrho(r)}+s^{\varrho(s)}\big)+C_{\varepsilon},

.

.

The next lemma can be found in

.

.

Let

3.
Slice monogenic functions of proximate order

Let
\varrho(r)
be a proximate order for a positive order
\rho>0
according to Definition
2.8
. We will now introduce some function spaces of entire slice monogenic functions in the spirit of
[
35
,
11
]
, which treat the similar problem for entire functions of several complex variables. For constant proximate order functions
\varrho(r)=\rho
and slice monogenic functions in the quaternions we also refer to the results in
[
19
, Chapter 5]
. In particular we derive basic properties of these function spaces where most of them are already known in the complex and the monogenic setting, but which are new in the case of slice monogenic functions. Moreover, we give an alternative and more detailed proof of
[
35
, Theorem 1.23]
in Theorem
3.7
.

For any
\sigma>0
, we consider the Banach space

A_{\varrho,\sigma}:=\Big\{f\in\mathcal{S\!M}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})\;|\;\|f\|_{\varrho,\sigma}:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}|f(x)|\exp(-\sigma|x|^{\varrho(|x|)})<\infty\Big\}

with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\varrho,\sigma}
.

Remark 3.1
.

We observe that for any proximate function
\varrho(r)
and any normalization function
\hat{\varrho}(r)
according to Remark
2.11
, the spaces
A_{\varrho,\sigma}
and
A_{\hat{\varrho},\sigma}
coincide with equivalent norms, see
[
11
, Page 8]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
\sigma_{2}>\sigma_{1}>0
, then the inclusion map
A_{\varrho,\sigma_{1}}\xhookrightarrow{}A_{\varrho,\sigma_{2}}
is compact.

Proof.

We will show that
B:=\{f\in A_{\varrho,\sigma_{1}}\;|\;\|f\|_{\varrho,\sigma_{1}}\leq 1\}
is relatively compact in
A_{\varrho,\sigma_{2}}
, i.e., any sequence
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset B
has an accumulation point with respect to the norm of
A_{\varrho,\sigma_{2}}
.

First we will prove that the sequence
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset B
admits a subsequence which converges in the uniform convergence topology of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. By the Arzelá-Ascoli theorem, together with some standard diagonal sequence argument, it is sufficient to prove that
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is equicontinuous and uniformly bounded on any compact convex subset
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Let us now fix one of the compact convex subsets
K
. Since
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset B
, the sequence is uniformly bounded on
K
. Moreover, we have

|f_{k}(x)-f_{k}(y)|\leq C_{k}|x-y|,\qquad x,y\in K

where
C_{k}=\sup_{x\in K}|\nabla f_{k}(x)|
and
\nabla
is the usual gradient. We choose
r
large enough in a such way that
K\subset B(0,r)
. It is sufficient to prove that there exists a constant
C_{K}
, only depending on
K
, such that

|\partial_{x_{i}}f_{k}(x)|\leq C_{K},\qquad x\in K,\,k\in\mathbb{N},\,i=0,\dots,n.

To prove this fact we need to differentiate the integral representation formula (
2.9
) for
f_{k}
. Let
x\in K
and
j\in\mathbb{S}
be such that there exist
u,\,v\in\mathbb{R}
with
x=u+jv
. When
i=0
, there exists a positive constant
C^{\prime}_{K}
which depends only on
K
, such that

\displaystyle|\partial_{x_{0}}f_{k}(x)|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\Big|\int_{\partial(B(0,r)\cap\mathbb{C}_{j})}\partial_{x_{0}}S_{L}^{-1}(s,x)ds_{j}f_{k}(s)\Big|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\Big|\int_{\partial(B(0,r)\cap\mathbb{C}_{j})}\frac{1}{(s-x)^{2}}ds_{j}f_{k}(s)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M(r,f_{k})}{2\pi}\int_{\partial(B(0,r)\cap\mathbb{C}_{j})}\frac{1}{|x-s|^{2}}|ds_{j}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{rM(r,f_{k})}{(r-|x|)^{2}}\leq C^{\prime}_{K},

where from
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset B
and
\operatorname{dist}(K,\partial B(0,r))>0
the last inequality follows. When
i\neq 0
, using the second form for
S^{-1}_{L}(s,x)
in (
2.8
), we observe that

\partial_{x_{i}}S_{L}^{-1}(s,x)=(e_{i}s^{2}-2x_{0}e_{i}s+|x|^{2}e_{i}-2x_{i}s+2x_{i}\overline{x})(s^{2}-2x_{0}s+|x|^{2})^{-2}.

Thus, when
x\in\mathbb{C}_{j}\cap K
and
s\in\mathbb{C}_{j}\cap\partial B(0,r)
, there exists a positive constant
C_{2}
, which depends only on
K
, such that

|\partial_{x_{i}}S_{L}^{-1}(s,x)|\leq\frac{C_{2}}{|s-x|^{2}|s-\overline{x}|^{2}}.

By this inequality, there exists another positive constant
C^{\prime\prime}_{K}
, which only depends on
K
, such that

\displaystyle|\partial_{x_{i}}f_{k}(x)|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\Big|\int_{\partial(B(0,r)\cap\mathbb{C}_{j})}\partial_{x_{i}}S_{L}^{-1}(s,x)ds_{j}f_{k}(s)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M(r,f_{k})}{2\pi}\int_{\partial(B(0,r)\cap\mathbb{C}_{j})}|\partial_{x_{i}}S_{L}^{-1}(s,x)||ds_{j}|

\displaystyle\leq rC_{2}\frac{M(r,f_{k})}{(r-|x|)^{4}}\leq C^{\prime\prime}_{K}.

In particular, choosing
C_{K}=\max\{C^{\prime}_{K},C^{\prime\prime}_{K}\}
, for any
x,y\in K
and for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have

|f_{k}(x)-f_{k}(y)|\leq\sqrt{n}C_{K}|x-y|,

i.e.,
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is equicontinuous on
K
. Applying the Arzelá-Ascoli theorem and using some standard diagonal sequence argument, there exists a subsequence which converges to
f\in\mathcal{S\!M}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
in the topology of the uniform convergence on compact subsets. Without loss of generality we call this subsequence again by
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.

In the second step we prove that the sequence
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
A_{\varrho,\sigma_{2}}
. We fix
\delta>0
and choose
R>0
large enough such that

\exp\big((\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2})|x|^{\varrho(|x|)}\big)\leq\frac{\delta}{2},\qquad\text{for any }|x|\geq R.

Thus, since
f_{k},f_{\ell}\in B
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{|x|\geq R}

\displaystyle|f_{k}(x)-f_{\ell}(x)|\exp\big(-\sigma_{2}|x|^{\varrho(|x|)}\big)

\displaystyle=\sup_{|x|\geq R}|f_{k}(x)-f_{\ell}(x)|\exp\big(-\sigma_{1}|x|^{\varrho(|x|)}\big)\exp\big((\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2})|x|^{\varrho(|x|)}\big)

\displaystyle\leq 2\,\frac{\delta}{2}=\delta.

(3.1)

Moreover, by the uniform convergence of the sequence
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
on the compact subset
\overline{B(0,R)}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, there exists a positive integer
N
such that for any
k,\ell\geq N
we have

\sup_{|x|\leq R}|f_{k}(x)-f_{\ell}(x)|\exp\big(-\sigma_{2}|x|^{\varrho(|x|)}\big)\leq\sup_{|x|\leq R}|f_{k}(x)-f_{\ell}(x)|\leq\delta.

(3.2)

Thus, combining (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) we have proved that the sequence
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
A_{\varrho,\sigma_{2}}
.
∎

Definition 3.3

(The spaces
A_{\varrho}
and
A_{\varrho,\sigma+0}
)
.

We define the space

A_{\varrho}:=\lim_{\underset{\sigma>0}{\to}}A_{\varrho,\sigma},

i.e.
A_{\varrho}=\bigcup_{\sigma>0}A_{\varrho,\sigma}
and we say that a sequence
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq A_{\varrho}
converges to
f\in A_{\varrho}
if there exists
\sigma>0
such that
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq A_{\varrho,\sigma}
,
f\in A_{\varrho,\sigma}
and
\lim_{k\to\infty}\|f_{k}-f\|_{\varrho,\sigma}=0
.

For every
\sigma\geq 0
we also define the space

A_{\varrho,\sigma+0}:=\lim_{\underset{\epsilon>0}{\leftarrow}}A_{\varrho,\sigma+\epsilon},

i.e.,
A_{\varrho,\sigma+0}:=\bigcap_{\epsilon>0}A_{\varrho,\sigma+\epsilon}
and we say that a sequence
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq A_{\varrho,\sigma+0}
converges to
f\in A_{\varrho,\sigma+0}
if for any
\epsilon>0
we have
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq A_{\varrho,\sigma+\epsilon}
,
f\in A_{\varrho,\sigma+\epsilon}
and
\lim_{k\to\infty}\|f_{k}-f\|_{\varrho,\sigma+\epsilon}=0
. When
\sigma=0
, we denote
A_{\varrho,+0}:=A_{\varrho,0+0}
.

Note that
A_{\varrho}
is a (DFS)-space (see
[
16
, Definition 2.2.1 and Section 2.6]
) and
A_{\varrho,\sigma+0}
is a (FS)-space. Both
A_{\varrho}
and
A_{\varrho,\sigma+0}
are locally convex spaces.

Remark 3.4
.

Let
\varrho
be a proximate order function and
\hat{\rho}
its normalization according to Remark
2.11
. Then the spaces
A_{\varrho}
and
A_{\hat{\varrho}}
coincide and share the same locally convex topology. The same holds true for
A_{\varrho,\sigma+0}
and
A_{\hat{\varrho},\sigma+0}
.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
\varrho
be a proximate order function and
f\in A_{\varrho}
. Then we define the
type
of
f
with respect to
\varrho
as

\inf\{\beta>0\;|\;f\in A_{\varrho,\beta}\}.

Next we prove a preparatory lemma for Theorem
3.7
, which characterises the order.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\varrho
be a normalized proximate order for the positive order
\rho
. Let
f(x)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}x^{\ell}a_{\ell}\in A_{\varrho}
. Moreover, suppose that
\sigma\geq 0
which satisfies the property

\frac{1}{\rho}\ln(\sigma)\geq\limsup_{\ell\to\infty}\Big(\frac{1}{\ell}\ln|a_{\ell}|+\ln(\varphi(\ell))\Big)-\frac{1}{\rho}-\frac{\ln(\rho)}{\rho},

where we interpret
\ln(0)=-\infty
in the case
\sigma=0
. Then, for any
\tau^{\prime}>\sigma
, there exist positive constants
N
and
C
such that

\sup\limits_{\ell\geq N}\big(\ln|a_{\ell}|+\ell\ln(r)\big)\leq\tau^{\prime}r^{\rho(r)}+C,\qquad r>0.

(3.3)

Proof.

Choose
\sigma<\sigma^{\prime}<\sigma^{\prime\prime}<\tau<\tau^{\prime}
arbitrary. Then, by assumption there exists
N_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
, such that

|a_{\ell}|^{\frac{1}{\ell}}\varphi(\ell)\leq(e\rho\sigma^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{\rho}},\qquad\ell\geq N_{1}.

Moreover, by the limit
\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\frac{\varphi(\frac{\ell}{\rho\sigma^{\prime\prime}})}{\varphi(\ell)}=(\frac{1}{\rho\sigma^{\prime\prime}})^{\frac{1}{\rho}}
from Lemma
2.14
(ii), there exists some
N_{2}\geq N_{1}
such that
\frac{\varphi(\frac{\ell}{\sigma^{\prime\prime}\rho})}{\varphi(\ell)}\leq(\frac{1}{\rho\sigma^{\prime}})^{\frac{1}{\rho}}
and hence

|a_{\ell}|^{\frac{1}{\ell}}\leq\frac{(e\rho\sigma^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{\rho}}}{\varphi(\ell)}\leq\frac{e^{\frac{1}{\rho}}}{\varphi(\frac{\ell}{\sigma^{\prime\prime}\rho})},\qquad\ell\geq N_{2}.

(3.4)

Next, by Lemma
2.14
(i), there exists some
N\geq N_{2}
such that

-1\leq\frac{\frac{t}{\sigma^{\prime\prime}\rho}\varphi^{\prime}(\frac{t}{\sigma^{\prime\prime}\rho})}{\varphi(\frac{t}{\sigma^{\prime\prime}\rho})}-\frac{1}{\rho}\leq\frac{\tau-\sigma^{\prime\prime}}{\rho\sigma^{\prime\prime}},\qquad t\geq N.

(3.5)

We will now prove that there exists a positive constant
C
, which does not depend on
r
, and
r_{0}>0
, such that

\sup\limits_{\ell\geq N}\big(\ln|a_{\ell}|+\ell\ln(r)\big)\leq\tau r^{\rho(r)}+C,\quad r>r_{0}.

(3.6)

Due to the estimate (
3.4
) we get

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\ell\geq N}\big(\ln|a_{\ell}|+\ell\ln(r)\big)

\displaystyle\leq\sup\limits_{\mathbb{N}\ni\ell\geq N}\ell\Big(\frac{1}{\rho}-\ln\Big(\varphi\Big(\frac{\ell}{\sigma^{\prime\prime}\rho}\Big)\Big)+\ln(r)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\sup\limits_{\mathbb{R}\ni t\geq N}\underbrace{t\Big(\frac{1}{\rho}-\ln\Big(\varphi\Big(\frac{t}{\sigma^{\prime\prime}\rho}\Big)\Big)+\ln(r)\Big)}_{\eqqcolon\mu_{r}(t)}.

(3.7)

Let
t_{\text{max}}(r)
be the supremum of the points where the function
\mu_{r}
attains its maximum. We observe that this point exists and it is finite since
\mu_{r}
is continuous and converges to
-\infty
when
t\to\infty
. First we prove that
t_{\text{max}}(r)\to\infty
when
r\to\infty
. We observe that

\mu_{r}^{\prime}(t)=\frac{1}{\rho}-\ln\Big(\varphi\Big(\frac{t}{\sigma^{\prime\prime}\rho}\Big)\Big)+\ln(r)-\frac{\frac{t}{\sigma^{\prime\prime}\rho}\varphi^{\prime}(\frac{t}{\sigma^{\prime\prime}\rho})}{\varphi(\frac{t}{\sigma^{\prime\prime}\rho})}.

We assume by contradiction that
t_{\text{max}}(r)
is bounded. Since
\mu^{\prime}_{r}(t_{\text{max}}(r))\leq 0
, we have

\ln\bigg(\varphi\Big(\frac{t_{\text{max}}(r)}{\sigma^{\prime\prime}\rho}\Big)\bigg)\geq\frac{1}{\rho}+\ln(r)-\frac{\frac{t_{\text{max}}(r)}{\sigma^{\prime\prime}\rho}\varphi^{\prime}(\frac{t_{\text{max}}(r)}{\sigma^{\prime\prime}\rho})}{\varphi(\frac{t_{\text{max}}(r)}{\sigma^{\prime\prime}\rho})}.

(3.8)

The previous inequality gives a contradiction since the left hand side is bounded for any
r>0
, instead the right hand side tends to
\infty
when
r\to\infty
.

Since we just have proven that
t_{\text{max}}(r)\overset{r\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
there exists some
r_{0}
such that
t_{\text{max}}(r)>N
for any
r\geq r_{0}
and hence also
\mu_{r}^{\prime}(t_{\text{max}}(r))=0
has to be satisfied for any
r\geq r_{0}
. This means that the inequality (
3.8
) beomes an equation, i.e.

\ln\bigg(\frac{1}{r}\varphi\Big(\frac{t_{\text{max}}(r)}{\sigma^{\prime\prime}\rho}\Big)\bigg)=\frac{1}{\rho}-\frac{\frac{t_{\text{max}}(r)}{\sigma^{\prime\prime}\rho}\varphi^{\prime}(\frac{t_{\text{max}}(r)}{\sigma^{\prime\prime}\rho})}{\varphi(\frac{t_{\text{max}}(r)}{\sigma^{\prime\prime}\rho})}.

In view of Lemma
2.14
(i), we have

\lim_{r\to\infty}\ln\bigg(\frac{1}{r}\varphi\Big(\frac{t_{\text{max}}(r)}{\sigma^{\prime\prime}\rho}\Big)\bigg)=0.

Next we choose
\epsilon,\eta>0
such that
(1+\eta)(1+\epsilon)^{\rho}\tau\leq\tau^{\prime}
. By the previous limit, we can enlarge
r_{0}>0
such that

\varphi\Big(\frac{t_{\text{max}}(r)}{\sigma^{\prime\prime}\rho}\Big)\leq(1+\epsilon)r,\qquad r\geq r_{0}.

By applying the inverse function
\varphi^{-1}(r)=r^{\varrho(r)}
to both sides of the this inequality and by Lemma
2.12
ii) with the above chosen
\eta
, we get

t_{\text{max}}(r)\leq\sigma^{\prime\prime}\rho((1+\epsilon)r)^{\varrho((1+\epsilon)r)}\leq\sigma^{\prime\prime}\rho(1+\eta)(1+\epsilon)^{\rho}r^{\varrho(r)}+\sigma^{\prime\prime}\rho C_{\eta}.

(3.9)

Moreover, rearranging the equation
\mu_{r}^{\prime}(t_{\text{max}}(r))=0
and using the second inequality in (
3.5
), we have that

\ln\bigg(\varphi\Big(\frac{t_{\text{max}}(r)}{\sigma^{\prime\prime}\rho}\Big)\bigg)=\ln(r)+\frac{1}{\rho}-\frac{\frac{t_{\text{max}}(r)}{\sigma^{\prime\prime}\rho}\varphi^{\prime}(\frac{t_{\text{max}}(r)}{\sigma^{\prime\prime}\rho})}{\varphi(\frac{t_{\text{max}}(r)}{\sigma^{\prime\prime}\rho})}\geq\ln(r)-\frac{\tau-\sigma^{\prime\prime}}{\rho\sigma^{\prime\prime}}.

(3.10)

Using the inequalities (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) in (
3.7
), we obtain for every
r\geq r_{0}

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\ell\geq N}\big(

\displaystyle\ln|a_{\ell}|+\ell\ln(r)\big)=\mu_{r}(t_{\text{max}}(r))

\displaystyle=t_{\text{max}}(r)\Big(\frac{1}{\rho}-\ln\Big(\varphi\Big(\frac{t_{\text{max}}(r)}{\sigma^{\prime\prime}\rho}\Big)\Big)+\ln(r)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq(\sigma^{\prime\prime}\rho(1+\eta)(1+\epsilon)^{\rho}r^{\varrho(r)}+\sigma^{\prime\prime}\rho C_{\eta})\Big(\frac{1}{\rho}-\ln(r)+\frac{\tau-\sigma^{\prime\prime}}{\rho\sigma^{\prime\prime}}+\ln(r)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\tau^{\prime}r^{\rho(r)}+\tau^{\prime}C_{\eta}.

(3.11)

Since
\ln(r)
is increasing we furthermore get the estimate

\sup\limits_{\ell\geq N}\big(\ln|a_{\ell}|+\ell\ln(r)\big)\leq\sup\limits_{\ell\geq N}\big(\ln|a_{\ell}|+\ell\ln(r_{0})\big)\leq\tau^{\prime}r_{0}^{\rho(r_{0})}+\tau^{\prime}C_{\eta},\quad r\in(0,r_{0}].

Hence, choosing
C^{\prime}=\tau^{\prime}r_{0}^{\rho(r_{0})}+\tau^{\prime}C_{\eta}
, we finally have

\sup\limits_{\ell\geq N}\big(\ln|a_{\ell}|+\ell\ln(r)\big)\leq\tau^{\prime}r^{\rho(r)}+C^{\prime},\qquad r>0.\qed

Next we prove the main theorem of this section, a characterization of functions in the spaces
A_{\varrho,\sigma+0}
with respect to the order, their growth condition and their Taylor coefficients.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
\varrho
be a normalized proximate order function,
\sigma\geq 0
and
f(x)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}x^{\ell}a_{\ell}\in\mathcal{S\!M}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
. Then the following four statements are equivalent:

(1)

f\in A_{\varrho,\sigma+0}
;

(2)

\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{\sup_{|x|\leq r}\ln|f(x)|}{r^{\varrho(r)}}\leq\sigma
;

(3)

\limsup_{\ell\to\infty}|a_{\ell}|^{\frac{1}{\ell}}\varphi(\ell)\leq(e\rho\sigma)^{\frac{1}{\rho}}
;

(4)

\inf\{\beta>0\;|\;f\in A_{\varrho,\beta}\}\leq\sigma
.

Remark 3.8
.

Note that using the numbers
\{G_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
from (
2.11
), one can alternatively write
(3)
as

\limsup_{\ell\to\infty}(|a_{\ell}|G_{\varrho,\ell})^{\frac{\rho}{\ell}}\leq\sigma.

Proof of Theorem
3.7
.

In the first step we prove the equivalence
(2)\Leftrightarrow(4)
. First, we prove

\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{\sup_{|x|\leq r}\ln|f(x)|}{r^{\varrho(r)}}\geq\inf\{\beta>0\;|\;f\in A_{\varrho,\beta}\}.

(3.12)

If
\tau>\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{\sup_{|x|\leq r}\ln|f(x)|}{r^{\varrho(r)}}
, then we can choose
r_{0}>0
such that for any
|x|>r_{0}
we have

|f(x)|\leq\exp\big(\tau|x|^{\varrho(|x|)}\big).

Thus there exists a positive constant
C
such that
|f(x)|\leq C\exp(\tau|x|^{\varrho(|x|)})
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and
f\in A_{\varrho,\tau}
i.e
\tau\geq\inf\{\beta>0\;|\;f\in A_{\varrho,\beta}\}
. Hence (
3.12
) is proven. Now we prove that

\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{\sup_{|x|\leq r}\ln|f(x)|}{r^{\varrho(r)}}\leq\inf\{\beta>0\;|\;f\in A_{\varrho,\beta}\}.

If
\sigma<\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{\sup_{|x|\leq r}\ln|f(x)|}{r^{\varrho(r)}}
then, given an increasing divergent sequence of positive constant
\{C_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, there exists a sequence
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
such that

|f(x_{k})|\geq C_{k}\exp\big(\sigma|x_{k}|^{\varrho(|x_{k}|)}\big).

This can be proved observing that for any
C_{k}>0
we have

\sigma<\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{\sup_{|x|\leq r}\ln|f(x)|}{r^{\varrho(r)}}=\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{\sup_{|x|\leq r}\ln|f(x)|}{r^{\varrho(r)}}-\frac{\ln(C_{k})}{r^{\varrho(r)}}.

Thus
f\notin A_{\varrho,\sigma}
i.e
\sigma\leq\inf\{\beta>0\;|\;f\in A_{\varrho,\beta}\}
.

Next we prove the equivalence
(1)\Leftrightarrow(2)
. We have that
f\in A_{\varrho,\sigma+0}
if and only if for any
\epsilon>0
there exists a
D_{\epsilon}>0
such that

|f(x)|\leq D_{\epsilon}\exp\big((\sigma+\epsilon)|x|^{\varrho(|x|)}\big),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}.

(3.13)

This is equivalent to

\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{\sup_{|x|\leq r}\ln|f(x)|}{r^{\varrho(r)}}\leq\sigma.

(3.14)

Indeed, supposing (
3.13
) is true. For a fixed
\epsilon>0
there exists
D_{\epsilon}>0
such that

\ln|f(x)|\leq\ln(D_{\epsilon})+(\sigma+\epsilon)|x|^{\varrho(|x|)}.

This implies that

\sup_{|x|<r}\ln|f(x)|\leq\ln(D_{\epsilon})+(\sigma+\epsilon)r^{\varrho(r)}.

Since
r^{\varrho(r)}\to\infty
as
r\to\infty
, we have

\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{\sup_{|x|\leq r}\ln|f(x)|}{r^{\varrho(r)}}\leq\sigma+\epsilon.

(3.15)

Because inequality (
3.15
) holds to be true for any
\epsilon>0
, (
3.14
) is satisfied. Vice versa, if inequality (
3.14
) is supposed to be true, then for any
\epsilon>0
there exists
r_{0}>0
such that for any
r\geq r_{0}
we have

\frac{\sup_{|x|\leq r}\ln|f(x)|}{r^{\varrho(r)}}\leq\sigma+\epsilon.

Thus, for any
|x|\geq r_{0}
, we have

|f(x)|\leq\exp\big((\sigma+\epsilon)|x|^{\varrho(|x|)}\big).

Choosing
D_{\epsilon}>1
in such a way that
|f(x)|\leq D_{\epsilon}
for any
|x|\leq r_{0}
, we have that (
3.13
) is true for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. This verifies the equivalence of (
3.13
) and (
3.14
) and hence also
(1)\Leftrightarrow(2)
is proven.

Now we prove
(1)\Rightarrow(3)
. Let
f\in A_{\rho,\sigma+0}
. First we verify that

\frac{1}{\rho}\ln(\sigma)\geq\limsup_{\ell\to\infty}\Big(\frac{1}{q}\ln|a_{\ell}|+\ln(\varphi(\ell))\Big)-\frac{1}{\rho}-\frac{\ln(\rho)}{\rho},

where we use the convention
\ln(0)=-\infty
in the case
\sigma=0
. If follows from estimating the Cauchy integral formula

a_{\ell}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\partial(U_{r}(0)\cap\mathbb{C}_{j})}s^{-\ell-1}ds_{j}f(s),

that

|a_{\ell}|\leq\frac{M(r,f)}{r^{\ell}},\qquad\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

If
\tilde{\sigma}>\sigma
then for
r
large we have

M(r,f)\leq\exp\big(\tilde{\sigma}r^{\varrho(r)}\big),

and

\ln|a_{\ell}|\leq\tilde{\sigma}r^{\varrho(r)}-\ell\ln(r).

(3.16)

If
\ell
is large enough, then we define
r_{\ell}
to be the real number such that
\ell=\tilde{\sigma}\rho r_{\ell}^{\varrho(r_{\ell})}
and we have
\varphi(\frac{\ell}{\tilde{\sigma}\rho})=r_{\ell}
. Thus, for
\ell
large enough we use (
3.16
) for
r=r_{\ell}
and divide by
\ell
and sum
\ln(\varphi(\ell))
to both sides of inequality. Thus we have

\ln\big(\varphi(\ell)|a_{\ell}|^{\frac{1}{\ell}}\big)<\frac{1}{\rho}+\ln\bigg(\frac{\varphi(\ell)}{\varphi(\frac{\ell}{\tilde{\sigma}\rho})}\bigg).

(3.17)

By Lemma
2.14
(ii), we have

\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\frac{\varphi(\ell)}{\varphi(\frac{\ell}{\tilde{\sigma}\rho})}=(\tilde{\sigma}\rho)^{\frac{1}{\rho}}.

Moreover, taking the
\limsup\limits_{l\to\infty}
to both side of (
3.17
), we have

\limsup_{\ell\to\infty}\ln\big(\varphi(\ell)|a_{\ell}|^{\frac{1}{\ell}}\big)\leq\ln\big((e\tilde{\sigma}\rho)^{\frac{1}{\rho}}\big)

for any
\tilde{\sigma}>\sigma
. Thus we have proven that

\limsup_{\ell\to\infty}\ln\big(\varphi(\ell)|a_{\ell}|^{\frac{1}{\ell}}\big)\leq\ln\big((e\sigma\rho)^{\frac{1}{\rho}}\big).

For the implication
(3)\Rightarrow(1)
let
\tau>\sigma
and choose
\tau>\tau^{\prime}>\sigma
. We want to show that
f\in A_{\varrho,\tau}
by estimating in a suitable way
\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}|a_{\ell}||x|^{\ell}
. In what follows we are going to split the previous summation in three parts. We know that

\limsup_{\ell\to\infty}\ln\big(\varphi(\ell)|a_{\ell}|^{\frac{1}{\ell}}\big)\leq\ln\big((e\sigma\rho)^{\frac{1}{\rho}}\big).

Thus by Lemma
3.6
there exist positive constants
N
and
C
such that

\sup\limits_{\ell\geq N}\big(\ln|a_{\ell}|+\ell\ln(r)\big)\leq\tau^{\prime}r^{\varrho(r)}+C,\qquad r>0.

Moreover, let
\rho_{1}>\rho
and fix
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
r:=|x|\geq r_{0}
for some
r_{0}>0
large enough. Then define
m_{r}:=\lfloor 2e\tau\rho_{1}r^{\rho_{1}}\rfloor
. As it is shown in (
3.4
) in such a way that for any
\ell\geq m_{r}
we have

|a_{\ell}|^{\frac{1}{\ell}}\leq\frac{(e\rho_{1}\tau)^{\frac{1}{\rho_{1}}}}{\varphi(\ell)}\leq\frac{2^{-1}}{\varphi(\frac{\ell}{2e\tau\rho_{1}})}\leq\frac{2^{-1}}{\varphi(r^{\rho_{1}})}\leq\frac{2^{-1}}{\varphi(r^{\varrho(r)})}=\frac{2^{-1}}{r},\qquad l\geq m_{r}.

(3.18)

Hence we have

|f(x)|\leq\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{N-1}|a_{\ell}||x|^{\ell}+\sum\limits_{\ell=N}^{m_{r}}|a_{\ell}||x|^{\ell}+\sum\limits_{\ell=m_{r}+1}^{\infty}|a_{n}||x|^{\ell}.

We want to estimate all the three terms of the previous summation. Since the number of terms in the first summation is finite and it does not depend on
|x|
, there exists a positive constant
C^{\prime\prime}
that depends only on
N
such that for any
r>0
we have

\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{N-1}|a_{\ell}||x|^{\ell}\leq C^{\prime\prime}\exp\big(\tau^{\prime}|x|^{\varrho(|x|)}\big).

For the second summation, using (
3.3
), we get

\sum\limits_{\ell=N}^{m_{r}}|a_{\ell}||x|^{\ell}\leq m_{r}e^{C}\exp\big(\tau^{\prime}|x|^{\varrho(|x|)}\big)

For the third equation we use (
3.18
) to obtain the estimate

\sum\limits_{\ell=m_{r}+1}^{\infty}|a_{\ell}||x|^{\ell}\leq\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}2^{-\ell}=2.

Summing up the three previous inequalities, there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\displaystyle|f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime\prime}\exp\big(\tau^{\prime}|x|^{\varrho(|x|)}\big)+m_{r}C^{\prime\prime\prime}\exp\big(\tau^{\prime}|x|^{\varrho(|x|)}\big)+2

\displaystyle\leq(C^{\prime\prime}+CC^{\prime\prime\prime}+2)\exp\big(\tau|x|^{\varrho(|x|)}\big),

where in the last inequality we chose
C>0
independent of
r
large enough such that

m_{r}\exp\big(-(\tau-\tau^{\prime})r^{\rho(r)}\big)\leq C,

which is possible since
m_{r}=\lfloor 2e\tau\rho_{1}r^{\rho_{1}}\rfloor
grows polynomially in
r
. Since this is true for every
|x|\geq r_{0}
, this implies
f\in A_{\varrho,\tau}
.
∎

The following proposition is a direct consequence of Theorem
3.7
.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
\varrho
be a normalized proximate order function and consider
f(x)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}x^{\ell}a_{\ell}\in\mathcal{S\!M}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
. Then the following four statements are equivalent:

(1)

f\in A_{\varrho}
;

(2)

\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{\sup_{|x|\leq r}\ln|f(x)|}{r^{\varrho(r)}}<\infty
;

(3)

\limsup_{\ell\to\infty}|a_{\ell}|^{\frac{1}{\ell}}\varphi(\ell)<\infty
;

(4)

\inf\{\beta>0\;|\;f\in A_{\varrho,\beta}\}<\infty
.

In Section
4
we will need some estimates on the norms of monomials, which will be provided in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
\varrho
be a normalized proximate order function. Then for every
0<\sigma^{\prime}<\sigma
, there exists a constant
C(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})
such that

\|x^{\ell}\|_{\varrho,\sigma}\leq C(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})\frac{G_{\ell}}{\sigma^{\prime\ell/\varrho}},\qquad\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(3.19)

Proof.

First of all, by Lemma
2.14
there exists some
t_{0}\geq 0
, such that

\frac{\varphi(t)}{\varphi(t^{\prime})}\leq\frac{e^{\sigma\frac{t}{t^{\prime}}}}{(e\sigma^{\prime}\varrho)^{1/\varrho}},\qquad t,t^{\prime}\geq t_{0}.

(3.20)

Let now
r_{0}:=\varphi(t_{0})
. Since
\lim_{t\to\infty}\varphi(t)=\infty
, there exists another constant
t_{1}\geq 0
such that

\varphi(\ell)\geq r_{0}(e\rho\sigma^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{\rho}},\qquad\ell\geq t_{1}.

(3.21)

Let now
\ell\geq t_{1}
. Then for every
|x|\geq r_{0}
we set
t:=|x|^{\rho(|x|)}
and get

\frac{|x|^{\ell}}{G_{\ell}}\exp\big(-\sigma|x|^{\varrho(|x|)}\big)=\frac{|x|^{\ell}(e\rho)^{\frac{\ell}{\rho}}}{\varphi(\ell)^{\ell}}\exp\big(-\sigma|x|^{\varrho(|x|)}\big)=\frac{\varphi(t)^{\ell}(e\rho)^{\frac{\ell}{\rho}}}{\varphi(\ell)^{\ell}}e^{-\sigma t}.

Since
t=|x|^{\rho(|x|)}\geq r_{0}^{\rho(r_{0})}=t_{0}
, we are allowed to use (
3.20
) and estimate this equation by

\frac{|x|^{\ell}}{G_{\ell}}\exp\big(-\sigma|x|^{\varrho(|x|)}\big)\leq\frac{e^{\ell\sigma\frac{t}{\ell}}(e\rho)^{\frac{\ell}{\rho}}}{(e\sigma^{\prime}\rho)^{\frac{\ell}{\rho}}}e^{-\sigma t}=\frac{1}{\sigma^{\prime\frac{\ell}{\rho}}},\qquad|x|\geq r_{0},\,\ell\geq t_{1}.

(3.22)

For
|x|\leq r_{0}
on the other hand, we use (
3.21
) to get

\frac{|x|^{\ell}}{G_{\ell}}\exp\big(-\sigma|x|^{\varrho(|x|)}\big)\leq\frac{r_{0}^{\ell}}{G_{\ell}}=\frac{r_{0}^{\ell}(e\rho)^{\frac{\ell}{\rho}}}{\varphi(\ell)^{\ell}}\leq\frac{1}{\sigma^{\prime\frac{\ell}{\rho}}},\qquad|x|\leq r_{0},\ell\geq t_{1}.

(3.23)

Combining now (
3.22
) and (
3.23
) gives the estimate

\|x^{\ell}\|_{\varrho,\sigma}=\sup\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}|x|^{\ell}\exp\big(-\sigma|x|^{\varrho(|x|)}\big)\leq\frac{G_{\ell}}{\sigma^{\prime\frac{\ell}{\rho}}},\qquad\ell\geq t_{1}.

Finally, choosing the constant

C(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}):=\max\bigg\{\max\limits_{0\leq\ell\leq t_{1}}\frac{\|x^{\ell}\|_{\varrho,\sigma}\sigma^{\prime\frac{\ell}{\rho}}}{G_{\ell}},\,1\,\bigg\},

gives the stated estimate (
3.19
) for every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
∎

Lemma 3.11
.

There exists a constant
k
depending only on
\rho
for which the following statement holds: For any
\sigma>0
, we can take
C(\sigma)
such that for any
f\in A_{\hat{\varrho},\sigma}
, and any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the inequality

\frac{1}{\ell!}\|\partial^{\ell}_{x_{0}}f\|_{\hat{\varrho},k\sigma}\leq C(\sigma)\|f\|_{\hat{\varrho},\sigma}\frac{(2k\sigma)^{\ell/\rho}}{G_{\hat{\varrho},\ell}}

(3.24)

holds.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and
j\in\mathbb{S}
,
u,v\in\mathbb{R}
such that
x=u+jv
. then the Cauchy estimates for the restriction
f|_{\mathbb{C}_{j}}
give for any
s>0

\displaystyle|\partial^{\ell}_{x_{0}}f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\ell!}{s^{\ell}}\max\limits_{|\xi|=s,\xi\in\mathbb{C}_{j}}|f(x+\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\ell!}{s^{\ell}}\|f\|_{\varrho,\sigma}\max\limits_{|\xi|=s,\xi\in\mathbb{C}_{j}}\exp\big(\sigma|x+\xi|^{\varrho(|x+\xi|)}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\ell!}{s^{\ell}}\|f\|_{\varrho,\sigma}\exp\big(\sigma(|x|+s)^{\varrho(|x|+s)}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\ell!}{s^{\ell}}\|f\|_{\varrho,\sigma}e^{\sigma C_{\varepsilon}}\exp\big(2^{\rho+\varepsilon}\sigma\big(r^{\varrho(r)}+s^{\varrho(s)}\big)\big),

where in the last inequality we used Lemma
2.9
i). Choosing now the special value
s=\varphi(\frac{\ell}{2^{\rho+\varepsilon}\sigma\rho})
, i.e
s^{\varrho(s)}=\frac{\ell}{2^{\rho+\varepsilon}\sigma\rho}
, we can further estimate

|\partial^{\ell}_{x_{0}}f(x)|\leq\frac{\ell!}{\varphi(\frac{\ell}{2^{\rho+\varepsilon}\sigma\rho})^{\ell}}\|f\|_{\varrho,\sigma}e^{\sigma C_{\varepsilon}}\exp\big(2^{\rho+\varepsilon}\sigma r^{\varrho(r)}+\frac{\ell}{\rho}\big),\qquad\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

By Lemma
2.14
ii) there exists some
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\frac{\varphi(\ell)^{\ell}}{\varphi(\frac{\ell}{2^{\rho+\varepsilon}\sigma\rho})^{\ell}}\leq 2^{\frac{\ell}{\rho}}(2^{\rho+\varepsilon}\sigma\rho)^{\frac{\ell}{\rho}},\qquad\ell\geq N.

Hence we can choose a larger constant
C_{1}
such that this inequality holds for all
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, i.e.

\frac{\varphi(\ell)^{\ell}}{\varphi(\frac{\ell}{2^{\rho+\varepsilon}\sigma\rho})^{\ell}}\leq C_{1}(2^{\rho+1+\varepsilon}\sigma\rho)^{\frac{\ell}{\rho}},\qquad\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

This leads to the estimate

\displaystyle|\partial^{\ell}_{x_{0}}f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\frac{\ell!}{\varphi(\ell)^{\ell}}\big((2^{\rho+1+\varepsilon}e\sigma\rho)^{\frac{1}{\rho}}\big)^{\ell}\|f\|_{\varrho,\sigma}e^{\sigma C_{\varepsilon}}\exp\big(2^{\rho+\varepsilon}\sigma r^{\varrho(r)}\big)

\displaystyle=C_{1}e^{\sigma C_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\ell!}{G_{\ell}}(2^{\rho+1+\varepsilon}\sigma)^{\frac{\ell}{\rho}}\|f\|_{\varrho,\sigma}\exp\big(2^{\rho+\varepsilon}\sigma r^{\varrho(r)}\big),\qquad\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

This is exactly the stated inequality (
3.24
) for
\ell\geq N
.
∎

In view of the above stated properties, we can now prove the following crucial results.

Proposition 3.12
.

For an entire left slice monogenic function
f(x)
belonging to
A_{\varrho,\sigma+0}
for
\sigma\geq 0
, its Taylor expansion
\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}x^{\ell}a_{\ell}
converges to
f(x)
in the space
A_{\varrho,\sigma+0}
. In particular, the set of Fueter polynomials is dense in
A_{\varrho,+0}
and also dense in
A_{\varrho}
.

Proof.

For the former statement, it suffices to show that

\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\|x^{\ell}a_{\ell}\|_{\varrho,\sigma+\epsilon}

is finite for any
\epsilon>0
. By Lemma
3.10
, there exists a positive constant
C_{0}
such that for any
\ell\geq 0
we have

\|x^{\ell}\|_{\varrho,\sigma+\epsilon}\leq C_{0}(\sigma+\epsilon/2)^{-\ell/\rho}G_{\hat{\varrho},\ell}.

On the other hand, by Remark
3.8
, there exists a positive constant
C_{1}
such that for any
\ell\geq 0
we have

|a_{\ell}|G_{\hat{\varrho},\ell}\leq C_{1}(\sigma+\epsilon/4)^{\ell/\rho}.

Therefore, we have

\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\|x^{\ell}a_{\ell}\|_{\varrho,\sigma+\epsilon}\leq C_{0}C_{1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{\sigma+\epsilon/4}{\sigma+\epsilon/2}\Big)^{\ell/\rho}<\infty.

For the latter statement in the case
f\in A_{\varrho,+0}
, it follows from the former one with
\sigma=0
that

\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\sum_{q\leq\ell}x^{q}a_{q}=f(x)

in the space
A_{\varrho,+0}
. In the case
f\in A_{\varrho}
, there exists
\sigma>0
such that
f\in A_{\varrho,\sigma+0}
. Then the same convergence holds in the space
A_{\varrho,\sigma+0}
and therefore also in the space
A_{\varrho}
.
∎

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
\rho
be a proximate order function,
\sigma,\tau>0
,
f\in A_{\rho,\sigma}
,
g\in A_{\rho,\tau}
. Then
f\star_{L}g\in A_{\rho,\sigma+\tau}
and

\|f\star_{L}g\|_{\rho,\sigma+\tau}\leq 2^{\frac{n+4}{2}}\|f\|_{\rho,\sigma}\|g\|_{\rho,\tau},

where
n
is the number of imaginary units in the Clifford algebra
\mathbb{R}_{n}
.

Proof.

Due to Definition
2.1
, the function
f\in\mathcal{S\!M}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
does admit the decomposition

f(u+jv)=f_{0}(u,v)+jf_{1}(u,v),\qquad u,v\in\mathbb{R},\,j\in\mathbb{S},

where for any arbitrary
j\in\mathbb{S}
, the functions
f_{0},f_{1}
are given by

f_{0}(u,v)=\frac{f(u+jv)+f(u-jv)}{2}

and

f_{1}(u,v)=J\frac{f(u-jv)-f(u+jv)}{2},

for any
u,v\in\mathbb{R}
. Hence, since
f\in A_{\rho,\sigma}
, the functions
f_{0},f_{1}
admit the estimates

\displaystyle|f_{0}(u,v)|

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{\rho,\sigma}\exp\Big(\sigma(u^{2}+v^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}\rho\big((u^{2}+v^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)}\Big),

\displaystyle|f_{1}(u,v)|

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{\rho,\sigma}\exp\Big(\sigma(u^{2}+v^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}\rho\big((u^{2}+v^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)}\Big).

The same obviously holds true for the decomposition
g(u+jv)=g_{0}(u,v)+jg_{1}(u,v)
, i.e.

\displaystyle|g_{0}(u,v)|

\displaystyle\leq\|g\|_{\rho,\tau}\exp\Big(\tau(u^{2}+v^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}\rho\big((u^{2}+v^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)}\Big),

\displaystyle|g_{1}(u,v)|

\displaystyle\leq\|g\|_{\rho,\tau}\exp\Big(\tau(u^{2}+v^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}\rho\big((u^{2}+v^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)}\Big).

Altogether, for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with the decomposition
x=u+jv
, we then get for the star product

\displaystyle|(f\star g)(x)|

\displaystyle=\Big|f_{0}(u,v)g_{0}(u,v)-f_{1}(u,v)g_{1}(u,v)+j\big(f_{0}(u,v)g_{1}(u,v)+f_{1}(u,v)g_{0}(u,v)\big)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{n+4}{2}}\|f\|_{\rho,\sigma}\|g\|_{\rho,\tau}\exp\Big(\sigma(u^{2}+v^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}\rho\big((u^{2}+v^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)}\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 170.71652pt\times\exp\Big(\tau(u^{2}+v^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}\rho\big((u^{2}+v^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)}\Big)

\displaystyle=2^{\frac{n+4}{2}}\|f\|_{\rho,\sigma}\|g\|_{\rho,\tau}\exp\Big((\sigma+\tau)|x|^{\rho(|x|)}\Big),

where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and the factor
2^{\frac{n}{2}}
in the second lines comes from the fact that for two Clifford numbers
a,b\in\mathbb{R}_{n}
the modulus of the product estimates as
|ab|\leq 2^{\frac{n}{2}}|a||b|
.
∎

The results of this section will be used to characterize continuous homomorphisms in terms of differential operators in the sense that will be specified in the next section.

4.

In this section we study continuous homomorphism from
A_{\varrho_{1}}
and
A_{\varrho_{2}}
and those from
A_{\varrho_{1},+0}
and
A_{\varrho_{2},+0}
where
\varrho_{i}(r)
(

\rho_{i}=\lim_{r\to\infty}\varrho_{i}(r)>0
, satisfying

r^{\varrho_{1}(r)}=O(r^{\varrho_{2}(r)}),\quad\text{as }r\to\infty.

(4.1)

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\varrho_{i}
(

) be two proximate orders for orders
\rho_{i}>0
satisfying (

). We take normalization
\hat{\varrho}_{1}

where the sequence
\{u_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\subset A_{\varrho_{2}}
satisfies

\forall\lambda>0,\,\exists\sigma>0,\,\exists C>0,\,\forall\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\,\|u_{\ell}\|_{\varrho_{2},\sigma}\leq C\frac{G_{\hat{\varrho}_{1},\ell}}{\ell!}\lambda^{\ell}.

(4.2)

We denote by
\mathbf{D}_{\varrho_{1}\to\varrho_{2},0}
the set of all formal right linear differential operator
P
of the form

P=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}u_{\ell}\star_{L}\partial_{x_{0}}^{\ell}

where the sequence
\{u_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\subset A_{\varrho_{2}}
satisfies

\forall\sigma>0,\,\exists\lambda>0,\,\exists C>0,\,\forall\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\,\|u_{\ell}\|_{\varrho_{2},\sigma}\leq C\frac{G_{\hat{\varrho}_{1},\ell}}{\ell!}\lambda^{\ell}.

(4.3)

Note that in the latter case, each

belongs to

.

For the following remark see also

]
.

Remark 4.2
.

By adding
\ln(c)/\ln(r)
for a constant
c>0
(with a suitable modification near

) to a proximate order
\varrho_{2}(r)
with order
\rho_{2}
, we get a new proximate order
\tilde{\varrho}_{2}(r)
for the same order
\rho_{2}
satisfying

\tilde{\varrho}_{2}(r)=\varrho_{2}(r)+\ln(c)/\ln(r)

for

, that is,

r^{\tilde{\varrho}_{2}(r)}=cr^{\varrho_{2}(r)},

eventually. Then
\|\cdot\|_{\tilde{\varrho}_{2},\sigma}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\varrho_{2},c\sigma}
become equivalent norms for
\sigma>0
, and the spaces
A_{\tilde{\varrho}_{2}}
and
A_{\tilde{\varrho}_{2},+0}
are homeomorphic to
A_{\varrho_{2}}
and
A_{\varrho_{2},+0}
respectively. By taking
c
sufficiently large, we can take
\tilde{\varrho}_{2}
as

\varrho_{1}(r)\leq\tilde{\varrho}_{2}(r),\qquad r\geq r_{0}

for a suitable
r_{0}
, and we can choose normalizations
\hat{\varrho}_{1}
of
\varrho_{1}
and
\hat{\varrho}_{2}
of
\tilde{\varrho}_{2}
as

(i)

\hat{\varrho}_{1}(r)\leq\hat{\varrho}_{2}(r)
,

.

Since a proximate order and its normalization define equivalent norms, we have

(ii)

\|\cdot\|_{\hat{\varrho}_{2},\sigma}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\varrho_{2},c\sigma}
are equivalent for any
c>0
,

(iii)

\mathbf{D}_{\varrho_{1}\to\varrho_{2}}=\mathbf{D}_{\hat{\varrho}_{1}\to\hat{\varrho}_{2}}
,
\mathbf{D}_{\varrho_{1}\to\varrho_{2},0}=\mathbf{D}_{\hat{\varrho}_{1}\to\hat{\varrho}_{2},0}
.

Note further that Theorems
4.3
and
4.4
below are not affected by the replacement of
\varrho_{1}
and
\varrho_{2}
by
\hat{\varrho}_{1}
and
\hat{\varrho}_{2}
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\varrho_{1},\varrho_{2}
be two proximate orders satisfying (
4.1
). Then all continuous linear operators
T:A_{\varrho_{1}}\to A_{\varrho_{2}}
are characterized by operators of the form

T:=\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{\infty}u_{\ell}\star_{L}\frac{\partial^{\ell}}{\partial x_{0}^{\ell}},

(4.4)

with coefficients
\{u_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathcal{S\!M}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
satisfying

\forall\varepsilon>0,\,\exists\sigma,C>0:\|u_{\ell}\|_{\varrho_{2},\sigma}\leq C\frac{G_{\varrho_{1},\ell}}{\ell!}\varepsilon^{\ell},

(4.5)

i.e.,
\mathbf{D}_{\varrho_{1}\to\varrho_{2}}
is the set of all continuous operators from
A_{\varrho_{1}}
to
A_{\varrho_{2}}
.

Proof.

Due to Remark
4.2
we may assume that
\varrho_{1}(r)\leq\varrho_{2}(r)
for every
r\geq 0
, which implies
A_{\varrho_{1},\sigma}\subseteq A_{\varrho_{2},\sigma}
for every
\sigma>0
and

\|f\|_{\varrho_{2},\sigma}\leq\|f\|_{\varrho_{1},\sigma},\qquad f\in A_{\varrho_{1},\sigma}.

Step 1
. For the first implication, let
T
be an operator of the form (
4.4
) with coefficients
\{u_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfying (
4.5
). Let
\varepsilon>0
, then by (
4.5
) there exists
\sigma,C>0
such that

\|u_{\ell}\|_{A_{\varrho_{2},\sigma}}\leq C\frac{G_{\varrho_{1},\ell}}{\ell!}\varepsilon^{\ell},\qquad\ell\in\mathbb{N}.

Moreover, for
f\in A_{\varrho_{1}}
one has
f\in A_{\varrho_{1},\tau}
for some
\tau>0
and we can estimate

\displaystyle\|Tf\|_{\varrho_{2},\sigma+2^{\rho_{1}+1}\tau}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\|u_{\ell}\star_{L}\partial^{\ell}_{x_{0}}f\|_{\varrho_{2},\sigma+2^{\rho_{1}+1}\tau}

\displaystyle\leq 4\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\|u_{\ell}\|_{\varrho_{2},\sigma}\|\partial^{\ell}_{x_{0}}f\|_{\varrho_{2},2^{\rho_{1}+1}\tau}

\displaystyle\leq 4C\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{G_{\varrho_{1},\ell}}{\ell!}\varepsilon^{\ell}\|\partial^{\ell}_{x_{0}}f\|_{\varrho_{1},2^{\rho_{1}+1}\tau}

\displaystyle\leq 4C\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{G_{\varrho_{1},\ell}}{\ell!}\varepsilon^{\ell}C(\tau)\frac{\ell!(2^{\rho_{1}+2}\tau)^{\frac{\ell}{\rho_{1}}}}{G_{\varrho_{1},\ell}}\|f\|_{\varrho_{1},\tau}

\displaystyle=4CC(\tau)\|f\|_{\varrho_{1},\tau}\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\big(\varepsilon(2^{\rho_{1}+2}\tau)^{\frac{1}{\varrho_{1}}}\big)^{\ell}.

Choosing
\varepsilon<(2^{\rho_{1}+2}\tau)^{-\frac{1}{\varrho_{1}}}
, the right hand side is finite and we showed that
T
is bounded as an operator from
A_{\varrho_{1},\tau}
to
A_{\varrho_{2},\sigma+2^{\rho_{1}+1}\tau}
. Since
f
was arbitrary, this also proves the continuity of
T
as an operator from
A_{\rho_{1}}
to
A_{\rho_{2}}
according to the Definition
3.3
.

Step 2
. For the second implication, let
T:A_{\varrho_{1}}\to A_{\varrho_{2}}
be an everywhere defined continuous operator. Then, thanks to the theory of locally convex spaces, there exists for every
\tau>0
some
\tau^{\prime}>0
, such that

\|Tf\|_{\varrho_{2},\tau^{\prime}}\leq C_{\tau}\|f\|_{\varrho_{1},\tau},\qquad f\in A_{\varrho_{1},\tau},

(4.6)

(see
[
34
, Chap 4, Part 1, 5, Corollary 1]
). Let us now define the functions

u_{\ell}(x):=\frac{1}{\ell!}\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}{\ell\choose k}T(x^{k})\star_{L}(-x)^{\ell-k}.

(4.7)

For any
\varepsilon>0
choose
\tau_{0}:=(\frac{2}{\varepsilon})^{\rho_{1}}
and any
\tau>\tau_{0}
. Then the functions
u_{\ell}
admit the estimate

\displaystyle\|u_{\ell}\|_{\varrho_{2},\tau^{\prime}+\tau}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\ell!}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\ell}{\ell\choose k}\|T(x^{k})\star_{L}(-x)^{\ell-k}\|_{\varrho_{2},2^{\rho_{1}+1}(\tau^{\prime}+\tau)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{\frac{\ell+4}{2}}}{\ell!}\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}{\ell\choose k}\|T(x^{k})\|_{\varrho_{2},\tau^{\prime}}\|x^{\ell-k}\|_{\varrho_{2},\tau}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(\tau)2^{\frac{\ell+4}{2}}}{\ell!}\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}{\ell\choose k}\|x^{k}\|_{\varrho_{1},\tau}\|x^{\ell-k}\|_{\varrho_{1},\tau}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(\tau)C(\tau,\tau_{0})2^{\frac{\ell+4}{2}}}{\ell!\tau_{0}^{\frac{\ell}{\varrho_{1}}}}\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}{\ell\choose k}G_{k}G_{\ell-k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(\tau)C(\tau,\tau_{1})2^{\frac{\ell+4}{2}}G_{\ell}}{n!\tau_{0}^{\frac{\ell}{\varrho_{1}}}}2^{\ell}=\frac{C(\tau)C(\tau,\tau_{1})2^{\frac{\ell+4}{2}}G_{\ell}}{\ell!}\varepsilon^{\ell}.

This estimate shows that the functions
u_{\ell}
satisfy the assumptions (
4.5
). Next consider a function
f(x)=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{M}x^{m}a_{m}
be a polynomial. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}u_{\ell}(x)\star_{L}\partial_{x_{0}}^{\ell}f(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}u_{\ell}(x)\star_{L}\partial_{x_{0}}^{\ell}x^{m}a_{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{m=\ell}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}{\ell\choose k}{m\choose\ell}T(x^{k})\star_{L}(-x)^{\ell-k}\star_{L}x^{m-\ell}a_{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{m=\ell}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}(-1)^{\ell-k}{n\choose k}{m\choose\ell}T(x^{k})\star_{L}x^{m-k}a_{m}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{m}\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-k}(-1)^{\ell}{m-k\choose\ell}\,{m\choose k}T(x^{k})\star_{L}x^{m-k}a_{m}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{m}\delta_{m-k,0}{m\choose k}T(x^{k})\star_{L}x^{m-k}a_{m}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}T(x^{m})a_{m}=T\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}x^{m}a_{m}\right)=Pf(x).

(4.8)

Since the polynomials are dense by Lemma
3.12
and the operator
T
is continuous, the same result holds for any function
f\in A_{\varrho_{1}}
.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\varrho_{1},\varrho_{2}
be two proximate orders satisfying (
4.1
). Then all continuous linear operators
T:A_{\varrho_{1},+0}\to A_{\varrho_{2},+0}
are characterized by operators of the form

T:=\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{\infty}u_{\ell}\star_{L}\frac{\partial^{\ell}}{\partial x_{0}^{\ell}},

(4.9)

with coefficients
\{u_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathcal{S\!M}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
satisfying

\forall\sigma>0,\,\exists\varepsilon,C>0:\|u_{\ell}\|_{\varrho_{2},\sigma}\leq C\frac{G_{\varrho_{1},\ell}}{\ell!}\varepsilon^{\ell},

(4.10)

i.e.,
\mathbf{D}_{\varrho_{1}\to\varrho_{2},0}
is the set of all continuous operators from
A_{\varrho_{1},+0}
to
A_{\varrho_{2},+0}
.

Proof.

Due to Remark
4.2
we may assume that
\varrho_{1}(r)\leq\varrho_{2}(r)
for every
r\geq 0
, which implies
A_{\varrho_{1},\sigma}\subseteq A_{\varrho_{2},\sigma}
for every
\sigma>0
and

\|f\|_{\varrho_{2},\sigma}\leq\|f\|_{\varrho_{1},\sigma},\qquad f\in A_{\varrho_{1},\tau}.

We proceed in two steps.

Step 1
. For the first implication let
T
be an operator of the form (
4.9
) with coefficients
\{u_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfying (
4.10
). Let
\sigma>0
. Then with the coefficients
\varepsilon,C>0
from (
4.10
) we choose
0<\tau<\frac{1}{2^{\rho_{1}+2}\varepsilon^{\rho_{1}}}
. Then we obtain the estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\|u_{\ell}\star_{L}\partial^{\ell}_{x_{0}}f\|_{\varrho_{2},\sigma+2^{\rho_{1}+1}\tau}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{n+4}{2}}\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\|u_{\ell}\|_{\varrho_{2},\sigma}\|\partial^{\ell}_{x_{0}}f\|_{\varrho_{2},2^{\rho_{1}+1}\tau}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{n+4}{2}}C(\eta,\sigma,\tau)\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\|u_{\ell}\|_{\varrho_{2},\sigma}\|\partial^{\ell}_{x_{0}}f\|_{\varrho_{1},2^{\rho_{1}+1}\tau}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}(\eta,\sigma,\tau)\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\frac{G_{\varrho_{1},\ell}\varepsilon^{\ell}}{\ell!}\|f\|_{\varrho_{1},\tau}\frac{\ell!}{G_{\rho_{1},\ell}}(2^{\rho_{1}+2}\tau)^{\frac{\ell}{\rho_{1}}}

\displaystyle=C^{\prime}(\eta,\sigma,\tau)\|f\|_{\varrho_{1},\tau}\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\varepsilon^{\ell}(2^{\rho_{1}+2}\tau)^{\frac{l}{\rho_{1}}},

for
f\in A_{\varrho_{1},\tau}
. Note that the last sum is finite due to the choice of
\tau
. This implies that
P:A_{\varrho_{1},+0}\to A_{\varrho_{2},+0}
is a continuous operator.

Step 2
. For the second implication let
P:A_{\varrho_{1},+0}\to A_{\varrho_{2},+0}
be a continuous operator. Then for every
\varepsilon>0
there exist
\tau>0
and
C(\varepsilon)>0
, such that
P:A_{\varrho_{1},\tau}\to A_{\varrho_{2},\varepsilon}
is continuous with

\|Pf\|_{\varrho_{2},\varepsilon}\leq C(\varepsilon)\|f\|_{\varrho_{1},\tau},\qquad f\in A_{\varrho_{1},\tau},

(4.11)

(see
[
34
, Chap 4, Part 1, 5, Corollary 1]
). Defining the functions
u_{\ell}
as in (
4.7
), they admit the estimate

\begin{split}&\|u_{\ell}\|_{\varrho_{2},\sigma}\leq 2^{\frac{n+4}{2}}\sum_{k\leq\ell}\frac{\|x^{\ell-k}\|_{\varrho_{2},\sigma/2}\|P(x^{k})\|_{\varrho_{2},\sigma/2}}{(\ell-k)!k!}\\
&\leq\sum_{k\leq\ell}\frac{\|x^{\ell-k}\|_{\varrho_{1},\sigma/2}C(\tau_{1})\|x^{k}\|_{\varrho_{1},\tau_{1}}}{(\ell-k)!k!}\\
&\leq\sum_{k\leq\ell}\frac{C(\tau_{0},\sigma^{\prime}/2)\tau_{0}^{-(\ell-k)/\rho_{1}}G_{\varrho_{1},\ell-k}C(\tau_{1})C(\tau_{0},\tau_{1})\tau_{0}^{-k/\rho_{1}}G_{\varrho_{1},k}}{(\ell-k)!k!}\\
&\leq C(\tau_{1})C(\tau_{0},\sigma/2)C(\tau_{0},\tau_{1})\sum_{k\leq\ell}{\ell\choose k}\frac{G_{\varrho_{1},\ell}}{\ell!}\tau_{0}^{-\ell/\rho_{1}}\\
&=C(\tau_{1})C(\tau_{0},\sigma/2)C(\tau_{0},\tau_{1})\frac{G_{\varrho_{1},\ell}}{\ell!}2^{\ell}\tau_{0}^{-\ell/\rho_{1}}.\end{split}

(4.12)

Here we used Lemma
3.13
at the first inequality, (
4.11
) at the second with
\varepsilon=\sigma/2
, Lemma
3.10
twice with
0<\tau_{0}<\sigma/2
and
0<\tau_{0}<\tau_{1}
at the third, and Lemma
2.13
at the fourth. Therefore, by defining

\lambda:=2\tau_{0}^{-1/\rho_{1}},

we have

\|u_{\ell}\|_{\varrho_{2},\sigma}\leq C^{\prime}\frac{G_{\varrho_{1},\ell}}{\ell!}\lambda^{\ell}

for any
\ell
. Since
\sigma>0
can be chosen arbitrarily, the functions
u_{\ell}
satisfy all the conditions in (
4.10
). Moreover, the operator
P
admits the representation

P=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}u_{\ell}\star_{L}\partial_{x_{0}}^{\ell}

in the same way as in (
4.8
) for polynomials. Since the polynomials are dense due to Lemma
3.12
and the operator
T
is continuous, the same result holds for any function
f\in A_{\varrho_{1},+0}
. Thus
P\in\mathbf{D}_{\varrho_{1}\to\varrho_{2},0}
.
∎

On the homeomorphism and homotopy type of complexes of multichains
Shaheen Nazir, Volkmar Welker

Abstract
. In this paper we define and study for a finite partially ordered set P P a class of simplicial complexes on the set P r P_{r} of r r -element multichains from P P . The simplicial complexes depend on a strictly monotone function from [ r ] [r] to [ 2 ​ r ] [2r] . We show that there exactly 2 r 2^{r} such functions which yield subdivisions of the order complex of P P of which 2 r − 1 2^{r-1} are pairwise different. Within this class are for example the order complexes of the interval and the zig-zag poset of P P and the r r th edgewise subdivision of the order complex of P P . We also exhibit a large subclass for which our simplicial complexes are order complexes and homotopy equivalent to the order complex of P P .



1.
Introduction

Let
P
be a poset with order relation
\leq
. In this paper we study the homeomorphism and homotopy type of simplicial complexes associated to multichains in
P
. For a number
r\geq 1
we consider
the set
P_{r}
of all
r
-multichains

\mathfrak{p}:p_{1}\leq\cdots\leq p_{r}
in
P
.

If
r=1
then
P_{r}=P
and the order complex
\Delta(P)
of all linearly ordered subsets of
P
together with its
geometric realization are well studied geometric and topological objects. They have been shown to encode crucial information about
P

and have important applications in combinatorics and many other
fields in mathematics. We refer to reader to
[
Wac07
]
for further information and
background information.

In this paper we define for
r\geq 2
classes of simplicial
complexes associated to
P_{r}
. In contrast to
r=1
there does not
seem to be a canonical choice. In
[
Müh15
]
a poset structure
on
P_{r}
is defined whose order complex can be shown to be homotopy equivalent to
P
. We
provide this result which was missed in
[
Müh15
]
in
Proposition
4.3
. The main focus of our paper is a different construction which leads to a wide class of simplicial complexes associated to
P_{r}
. Our construction arose during a discussion of preliminary versions of
work of the first author in
[
Naz21
]
. As special cases the construction yields several well
studied and important poset and subdivision operations such as
interval posets (see
[
Wal88
]
),
the zig-zag poset (see
[
PRS98
]
), the
r
th
-edgewise subdivision (see
[
EG00
]
) and
a subdivision operation which arose in the work of Cheeger, Müller and Schrader
(see
[
CMS84
]
). We refer the reader to
[
Naz21
]
for a detailed account of how these arise from our construction (see also Example
3.7
).

For every strictly monotone map

\imath:[r]\rightarrow[2r]
we define a binary relation

\preceq_{i}
on
P_{r}
. Here for a natural number
n
we write
[n]
for
\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Through the undirected graph

G_{\imath}(P_{r})=(P_{r},E)
with edge set

E=\big\{\{\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q}\}\subseteq P_{r}\penalty\ :\penalty\ \mathfrak{p}\preceq_{\imath}\mathfrak{q}\text{ and }\mathfrak{p}\neq\mathfrak{q}\big\}

we associate to
P_{r}
and
\imath
the clique complex

\Delta(G_{\imath}(P_{r}))
of
G_{\imath}(P_{r})
; that is the simplicial complex of all
subsets
A\subseteq P_{r}
which form a clique in
G_{\imath}(P_{r})
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
r\geq 2
the following are equivalent

(1)

The relation
\preceq_{\imath}
is reflexive,

(2)

The clique complex
\Delta(G_{\imath}(P_{r}))
is a subdivision of
\Delta(P)
.

(3)

The clique complex
\Delta(G_{\imath}(P_{r}))
is homeomorphic to
\Delta(P)
.

In the formulation of the theorem and the rest of the paper, we use the term subdivision in
the sense of geometric subdivision (see e.g.
[
Sta92
]
). Also when we speak of
homotopy equivalent or homeomorphic simplicial complexes we mean that their geometric realizations
are homotopy equivalent or homeomorphic.

The cases treated in the theorem include the above mentioned
subdivision operations as special cases.
Indeed, in Lemma
2.3
(1) we show that for fixed
r
there are exactly
2^{r-1}
different

\imath
for which
\preceq_{\imath}
is reflexive and
\imath(1)=1
. We will see that the latter condition eliminates an obvious symmetry.

If
\preceq_{\imath}
is even a partial order then
\Delta(G_{\imath}(P_{r}))
coincides with the order complex
of
P_{r}
with respect to
\preceq_{\imath}
.
The following proposition shows that for each

there is exactly one
\imath
for which
\preceq_{\imath}
is a partial order.

Proposition 1.2
.

The relation
\preceq_{\imath}
is a partial order on
P_{r}
if and only if
for
2\leq t\leq r
we have
\imath(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2t,&\hbox{$t$ is even;}\\
2t-1,&\hbox{$t$ is odd.}\end{array}\right.

For the
\imath
from the above proposition and
r=2
we have that
\preceq_{\imath}
coincides with
the interval order on
P
and for
arbitrary
r\geq 2
we have that

\preceq_{\imath}
defines the zig-zag order on
P_{r}

(see
[
PRS98
]
and Example

). It can be seen (see
[
Naz21
]
) that the order complex of the zig-zag order on

coincides for even

with a subdivision operation studied in
[
CMS84
]
.

The condition that
\preceq_{\imath}
is reflexive provides the most restrictions on

\imath
when classifying the
\preceq_{\imath}
which are partial orders.
Thus instead of the relation
\preceq_{\imath}
, we may consider the relation
\preceq_{\imath}^{\prime}
which is
defined as follows:

\mathfrak{p}\preceq_{\imath}^{\prime}\mathfrak{q}:\Leftrightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{q}\text{ or }\\
\mathfrak{p}\neq\mathfrak{q}\text{ and }\mathfrak{p}\preceq_{\imath}\mathfrak{q}\end{array}\right.

We classify in Proposition

when
\preceq_{\imath}^{\prime}
is

Theorem 1.3
.

If
\preceq_{\imath}^{\prime}
is a partial order then the order complex of

with respect to

is homotopy equivalent to
\Delta(P)
.

Note that in the situation of

we have that the order complex of

coincides with

\Delta(G_{\imath}(P_{r}))
for the
\imath
defining

\preceq^{\prime}_{\imath}
. In case
\preceq_{\imath}
is neither reflexive nor transitive then
there are examples where the conclusion of Theorem

fails (see Example

).

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2

basic definitions are provided and reflexivity, antisymmetry, transitivity of the relation

In Section

, provide an example showing that the conclusion of Theorem

2.
The relation
\preceq_{i}
on
r
-multichains

For
1\leq r\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
\imath:[r]\rightarrow[2r]
be a strictly increasing map. On
P_{r}
we define the binary relation
\preceq_{\imath}
as
follows. For
\mathfrak{p}:p_{1}\leq\cdots\leq p_{r}
and

\mathfrak{q}:q_{1}\leq\cdots\leq q_{r}
we set:

\mathfrak{p}\preceq_{\imath}\mathfrak{q}:\iff\begin{array}[]{ll}p_{t}\geq q_{s},&\hbox{for $s\leq\imath(t)-t$;}\\
p_{t}\leq q_{s},&\hbox{for $s>\imath(t)-t$.}\end{array}.

for
\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q}\in P_{r}
.

Example 2.1
.

•

For
r=1
and
\imath(1)=1
the relation

\preceq_{\imath}
is the order
relation
\leq
on
P
.

•

If
r=2
and
\imath(1)=1,\imath(2)=4
then

\preceq_{\imath}
is the well studied interval order on
P

(see
[
Wal88
]
); that is the inclusion
order on
the set of intervals
[p_{1},p_{2}]:=\{q\in P\penalty\ :\penalty\ p_{1}\leq q\leq p_{2}\}
for
p_{1}\leq p_{2}
in
P
.

•

For arbitrary
r\geq 2
and
\imath(1)=1,\imath(2k)=4k,\imath(2k+1)=4k+1
,

k=1,\ldots,\lfloor\frac{r}{2}\rfloor
, we obtain a partial order
which appears naturally in the context of monoid algebras and is called zig-zag poset in
[
PRS98
, p. 390]
and coincides with the interval order for
r=2
.

The relation
\preceq_{\imath}
usually does not even define a partial order.
For example if
r>1
and
\imath(j)=j
for
j\in[r]
, then
if
P
contains a two element chain
p<q
we have
for
\mathfrak{p}:\underbrace{p\leq\cdots\leq p}_{r-1}<q
that

\mathfrak{p}\not\preceq_{\imath}\mathfrak{p}
and thus
\preceq_{\imath}
is not
reflexive.

For a given strictly increasing map
\imath:[r]\rightarrow[2r]
we write
\{\imath_{1}<\cdots<\imath_{r}\}
for the image of
\imath
. To
\imath

we associate another strictly increasing map
\imath^{*}:[r]\rightarrow[2r]
defined as

\imath^{*}(k)=\imath^{*}_{k}
, where
\{\imath^{*}_{1}<\imath^{*}_{2}<\cdots<\imath^{*}_{r}\}
is the complement of
\{\imath_{1}<\imath_{2}<\cdots<\imath_{r}\}
in
[2r]
. It can be observed that
\preceq_{\imath}
is the dual of
\preceq_{\imath^{*}}
, i.e.
\mathfrak{p}\preceq_{\imath}\mathfrak{q}
if and only if
\mathfrak{q}\preceq_{\imath^{*}}\mathfrak{p}
for all
\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q}\in P_{r}
. Therefore, for our purposes whenever it is convenient, we can restrict ourselves to strictly increasing maps
\imath
with
\imath(1)=1
.

Our first goal is to classify when
\preceq_{\imath}
is a partial order.
The next lemma is a first step towards the classification.

Lemma 2.2
.

(1)

The relation
\preceq_{\imath}
is antisymmetric.

(2)

The relation
\preceq_{i}
is an order for all
\imath
if and only if either
r=1
or

P
is an antichain.

Proof.

(1)

Let
\mathfrak{p}\preceq_{\imath}\mathfrak{q}
. If

\imath(j)\leq 2j-1
then
p_{j}\leq q_{j}
and if

\imath(j)\geq 2j-1
then
q_{j}\leq p_{j}
. If in

\mathfrak{q}\preceq_{\imath}\mathfrak{p}
then the fact that

is an

p_{j}=q_{j}
for all

and hence
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{q}
.

(2)

For
r=1
, by
\imath(1)=1
we have

and
\preceq_{\imath}=\leq
.
Thus we may assume

.

If

is antichain, then

consists of

. From the

Conversely, assume

.
Then the example right before this lemma shows that

∎

(1)

3.
Topology of Complexes of Multichains

In the first part of this section we will prove Theorem
1.1
. First note that if
P
is an antichain then by Lemma

(2)
\preceq_{\imath}
is reflexive for all
\imath
. In this situation

We first show that in this case

.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
P
be a poset which is not an antichain then
\Delta(G_{\imath}(P_{r}))
is a subdivision of
\Delta(P)
for every strictly increasing map
\imath:[r]\rightarrow[2r]

\imath(1)=1

In order to prove Proposition

we define the map

f(p_{1}\leq\cdots\leq p_{r})=\frac{1}{r}(p_{1}+p_{2}+\cdots+p_{r})
.
Assume
\preceq_{\imath}
is reflexive. Observe that if
\mathfrak{p}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{p}_{\ell}\in P_{r}
forms a clique in

then the union the elements of

\mathfrak{p}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{p}_{\ell}
is a chain in

. Thus we can extend the map

affinely to a piecewise linear, continuous map
|f|:|\Delta(G_{\imath}(P_{r}))|\rightarrow|\Delta(P)|

For a chain
\mathfrak{c}:p_{1}<\cdots<p_{n}

|\Delta(G_{\imath}(P_{r}))|_{\mathfrak{c}}

in strictly positive barycentric coordinates then
\mathfrak{p}_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\mathfrak{p}_{\ell}=\mathfrak{c}
. Building on ideas from

Lemma 3.2
.

For a chain
\mathfrak{c}:p_{1}<\cdots<p_{n}

\mathfrak{c}

Proof.

Let
z

\mathfrak{c}
.
If we write

.

Next we define a matrix

Q_{z,\imath}=(q_{i,j})\in P^{r\times m}
in the following way. For

we set

\alpha(\ell)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}r(\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{\ell})-(i-1),&\text{ if \, \,}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{i-1 < r(\lambda_1+\cdots+ \lambda_\ell) \leq i }{\text{and } \i(i)=2i-1}\\
1-\big[r(\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{\ell})-(i-1)\big],&\text{ if \,\,}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{i-1 < r(\lambda_1+\cdots+ \lambda_\ell) < i}{\text{and } \i(i)=2i}\\
1,&\text{ if \,\,}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{ r(\lambda_1+\cdots+ \lambda_\ell) =i}{\text{and } \i(i)=2i}\end{array}\right.

It is easily seen that
0<\alpha(\ell)\leq 1
. We denote by

(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m})

\alpha(1),\ldots,\alpha(n)

Note that by
\alpha(n)=1
we will always have
\alpha_{m}=1
.
For numbers

k=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\min\{k^{\prime}\penalty\ :\penalty\ r(\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{k^{\prime}})\geq i-1+\alpha_{j}\}&\text{ if }\imath(i)=2i-1\\
\min\{k^{\prime}\penalty\ :\penalty\ i-r(\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{k^{\prime}})<\alpha_{j}\}&\text{ if }\imath(i)=2i.\end{array}\right.

If we define
q_{i,j}=p_{k}
it follows that

(3.1)

\displaystyle q_{i,1}\leq q_{i,2}\leq\cdots\leq q_{i,n}

\displaystyle\text{ for }\imath(i)=2i-1

\displaystyle q_{i,m}\leq q_{i,m-1}\leq\cdots\leq q_{r,1}

\displaystyle\text{ for }\imath(i)=2i.

Every element in the

G_{\imath}(P_{r})
but
\{\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q}\}
is not an edge of
G_{\jmath}(P_{r})
.

∎

Example 3.7
.

For
\imath:[r]\rightarrow[2r]
with

then
\Delta(G_{\imath}(P_{r}))
is the edgewise subdivision of

(see
[

]
).

For
\imath:[r]\rightarrow[2r]
with

\imath(t)=2t
if

is even and
2t-1
if
t
is odd then we already know from Proposition
1.2
that then

\Delta(G_{\imath}(P_{r}))
is an order complex. By the comments after the proposition it is the order complex of the zig-zig order which coincides for even
r
with a subdivision operation studied in
[
CMS84
]
(see
[
Naz21
]
).

We have seen that even though postulating
\preceq_{\imath}
to be reflexive is quite restrictive, the resulting graphs
G_{\imath}(P_{r})
have interesting geometric properties. Next we want to prove Theorem
1.3
. Recall that the relation
\preceq^{\prime}_{\imath}
coincides with
\preceq_{\imath}
except that we impose reflexivity. Since
by Lemma
2.2
(1)
\preceq_{\imath}
is always antisymmetric it follows that
if
\preceq_{\imath}
is transitive, then
\preceq^{\prime}_{\imath}
becomes a partial order.
Next we will prove Theorem
1.3
and show that if
\preceq^{\prime}_{\imath}
is a partial order then
\Delta(P)
is homotopy equivalent to the order
complex
\Delta(P_{r}^{\prime})
of
P_{r}^{\prime}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

In the proof we will write
P_{r}^{\prime}
for
P_{r}
ordered by
\preceq^{\prime}_{\imath}
.

Consider the order complex
\Delta(P)\setminus\{\emptyset\}
as a poset. Define a map
g:\Delta(P_{r}^{\prime})\setminus\{\emptyset\}\rightarrow\Delta(P)\setminus\{\emptyset\}
as:

\mathfrak{p}^{(1)}\preceq^{\prime}_{\imath}\cdots\preceq^{\prime}_{\imath}\mathfrak{p}^{(k)}\mapsto\cup_{j=1}^{k}\{p_{1}^{(j)},\ldots,p_{r}^{(j)}\}

where
\mathfrak{p}^{(j)}:p_{1}^{(j)}\leq\cdots\leq p_{r}^{(j)}
. It is clear that
g
is a poset map. By Quillen’s Fiber Lemma, it is enough to show that the fibers
g^{-1}(\Delta(\{q_{0}<q_{1}<\cdots<q_{n}\})\setminus\{\emptyset\})=\{q_{0}<q_{1}<\cdots<1_{n}\}_{r}^{\prime}
of all chains
\{q_{0}<q_{1}<\cdots<q_{n}\}
in
P
, are contractible.

Set
Q=\{q_{0}<q_{1}<\cdots<q_{n}\}^{\prime}_{r}
.
Define a map

\mathrm{cl}:Q\rightarrow Q
by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}:p_{1}\leq\cdots\leq p_{r}

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle\mathrm{cl}(\mathfrak{p}):\underbrace{\underbrace{p_{b_{1}}\leq\cdots\leq p_{b_{1}}}_{b_{1}}\leq\underbrace{p_{b_{1}+1}\leq\cdots\leq p_{b_{1}+1}}_{k_{1}}}_{c_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\underbrace{\underbrace{p_{b_{1}+b_{2}}\leq\cdots\leq p_{b_{1}+b_{2}}}_{m_{1}}\leq\underbrace{p_{b_{1}+b_{2}+1}\leq\cdots\leq p_{b_{1}+b_{2}+1}}_{k_{2}}}_{c_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\underbrace{p_{b_{1}+b_{2}+b_{3}}\leq\cdots\leq p_{b_{1}+b_{2}+b_{3}}}_{m_{2}}\leq\cdots,

where
k_{t}
and
m_{t}
are the sequences of positive integers such that
\sum_{i=1}^{t}c_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{t}b_{i}+k_{t}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{t+1}b_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{t}c_{i}+m_{t}
for
t\geq 1
.

Claim 1:

The operator
\mathrm{cl}
is a closure operator on
Q
(i.e.,
\mathrm{cl}
is a poset map
such that
\mathfrak{p}\preceq_{\imath}\mathrm{cl}(\mathfrak{p})
and
\mathrm{cl}\circ\mathrm{cl}=\mathrm{cl}
)

\triangleleft

Proof of Claim 1:

First, we show that
\mathfrak{p}\preceq_{\imath}\mathrm{cl}(\mathfrak{p})
. Since
b_{1}<c_{1}<b_{1}+b_{2}<\cdots
, we have

\underbrace{p_{1}\leq\cdots\leq p_{b_{1}}}_{b_{1}}\leq\underbrace{p_{b_{1}}\leq\cdots p_{b_{1}}\leq p_{b_{1}+1}\leq\cdots\leq p_{b_{1}+1}}_{c_{1}}\leq\underbrace{p_{b_{1}+1}\leq\cdots\leq p_{b_{1}+b_{2}}}_{b_{2}}\\
\leq\underbrace{p_{b_{1}+b_{2}}\leq\cdots\leq p_{b_{1}+b_{2}}\leq p_{b_{1}+b_{2}+1}\leq\cdots\leq p_{b_{1}+b_{2}+1}}_{c_{2}}\leq\cdots

which shows that
\mathfrak{p}\preceq_{\imath}\mathrm{cl}(\mathfrak{p})
.

Next, we verify that
\mathrm{cl}
is a poset map. Suppose that
\mathfrak{p}\preceq_{\imath}\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
. Then we have

\underbrace{p_{1}\leq\cdots\leq p_{b_{1}}}_{b_{1}}\leq\underbrace{p_{1}^{\prime}\leq\cdots\leq p_{c_{1}}^{\prime}}_{c_{1}}\leq\underbrace{p_{b_{1}+1}\leq\cdots\leq p_{b_{1}+b_{2}}}_{b_{2}}\leq\underbrace{p_{c_{1}+1}^{\prime}\leq\cdots\leq p_{c_{1}+c_{2}}^{\prime}}_{c_{2}}\leq\cdots.

Now again using the relation of
b
’s and
c
’s, we get

\displaystyle\underbrace{p_{b_{1}}\leq\cdots\leq p_{b_{1}}}_{b_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{p_{b_{1}}^{\prime}\leq\cdots\leq p_{b_{1}}^{\prime}\leq p_{b_{1}+1}^{\prime}\leq\cdots\leq p_{b_{1}+1}^{\prime}}_{c_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{p_{b_{1}+1}\leq\cdots\leq p_{b_{1}+1}\leq p_{b_{1}+b_{2}}\leq\cdots\leq p_{b_{1}+b_{2}}}_{b_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{p_{b_{1}+b_{2}}^{\prime}\leq\cdots p_{b_{1}+b_{2}}^{\prime}\leq p_{b_{1}+b_{2}+1}^{\prime}\leq\cdots\leq p_{b_{1}+b_{2}+1}^{\prime}\leq}_{c_{2}}\cdots

which implies
\mathrm{cl}(\mathfrak{p})\preceq_{\imath}\mathrm{cl}(\mathfrak{p}^{\prime})
.

The fact that
\mathrm{cl}(\mathfrak{p})=\mathrm{cl}(\mathrm{cl}(\mathfrak{p}))
follows directly from the definition of
\mathrm{cl}
.
\triangleright

It is well known (see for example
[
Wac07
, Exercise 5.2.6(a)]
) that since

\mathrm{cl}
is closure operator the order complexes

\Delta(Q)
and
\Delta(\mathrm{cl}(Q))
are homotopy equivalent.

Every element
\mathfrak{p}:p_{1}\leq p_{2}\leq\cdots\leq p_{r}
of
\mathrm{cl}(Q)
satisfies the following relation:

(3.6)

p_{j}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}p_{2t-1},&\hbox{$c_{1}+\cdots+c_{t-1}<j\leq b_{1}+\cdots+b_{t}$;}\\
p_{2t},&\hbox{$b_{1}+\cdots+b_{t}<j\leq c_{1}+\cdots+c_{t}$.}\end{array}\right.

for
1\leq t\leq\ell
. Set
r_{0}=2\ell-2
if
c_{1}+\cdots+c_{\ell-1}=b_{1}+\cdots+b_{\ell}
;
r_{0}=2\ell-1
, otherwise. It is clear that
r_{0}\leq r
due to the transitivity of
\imath
. Let
\imath_{0}:[r_{0}]\rightarrow[2r_{0}]
be defined by
\imath_{0}(t)=2t
if
t
is
even and
2t-1
if
t
is odd.
Set
Q_{0}=\{q_{0}<q_{1}<\cdots<q_{n}\}_{r_{0}}
equipped with the relation
\preceq_{\imath_{0}}
. Then by Proposition
2.4

Q_{0}
is partially ordered by
\preceq_{\imath_{0}}
.

Claim 2:

The poset
\mathrm{cl}(Q)
is isomorphic to
Q_{0}
.

\triangleleft

Proof of Claim 2:

Define a map
h:\mathrm{cl}(Q)\rightarrow Q_{0}
by:

h(\mathfrak{p})=p_{1}\leq p_{2}\leq\cdots\leq p_{r_{0}}.

If
\mathfrak{p}\preceq^{\prime}_{\imath}\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
then we get
p_{1}\leq p_{1}^{\prime}\leq p_{2}^{\prime}\leq p_{2}\leq p_{3}\leq\cdots
which shows that
h(\mathfrak{p})\preceq_{\imath_{0}}h(\mathfrak{p}^{\prime})
. Thus,
h
is a poset map.
Now, define
h^{\prime}:Q_{0}\rightarrow\mathrm{cl}(Q)
as

p_{1}\leq p_{2}\leq\cdots\leq p_{r_{0}}\mapsto h^{\prime}(p),

where
h^{\prime}(p)
is defined as in (
3.6
) for
b
’s and
c
’s defined by the map
\imath
. This is clearly a poset map and an inverse of

. Thus
h
is a poset isomorphism between
\mathrm{cl}(Q)
and
Q_{0}
.

\triangleright

By Theorem
1.1
we know that
\Delta(Q_{0})
is a subdivision of the

-simplex. Hence,
Q
which is homotopy equivalent to

is contractible.
∎

4.
Further directions and open questions

The conclusion of Theorem
1.3
is false for arbitrary

\imath
as the following counterexample shows.

Example 4.1
.

Let
r=3
and let
P=\{1<2<3\}
be a three element chain.
Set
\imath(1)=1
,
\imath(2)=2
and
\imath(3)=4
. Then
\imath
is
neither reflexive nor transitive. The maximal simplices of

\Delta(G_{\imath}(P_{3}))
are

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\{111,112,122\}&\{111,112,123\}&\{111,112,133\}\\
\{111,113,133\}&\{112,123,233\}&\{113,133,333\}\\
\{113,233,333\}&\{123,233,333\}&\{223,233,333\}\end{array}

and

\{112,122,222,223,233\}.

Note that here we write
ijk
for element
i\leq j\leq k
from
P_{3}
.

A straightforward homology computation shows that the reduced homology group

\widetilde{H}_{i}(\Delta(G_{\imath}(P_{3})),{\mathbb{Z}})=0
for
i\neq 1
and
{\mathbb{Z}}

for
i=1
. In particular,
\Delta(G_{\imath}(P_{3}))
cannot be contractible. Indeed, by a sequence of elementary collapses one
can show that
\Delta(G_{\imath}(P_{3}))
is homotopy equivalent to a
circle.

Since
\Delta(P)
is a
2
-simplex and hence contractible we have
that
\Delta(G_{\imath}(P_{3}))
and
\Delta(P)
are not homotopy equivalent.

Question 4.2
.

(1)

Can one classify the
\imath:[r]\rightarrow[2r]
for which

\Delta(P)
and
\Delta(G_{\imath}(P_{r}))
are homotopy equivalent
for all
P
?

(2)

Can the homotopy type of
\Delta(G_{\imath}(P_{r}))
be described in terms of the homotopy type of
P
and
\imath
?

In
[
Müh15
]
Mühle defines a poset structure on

P_{r}
by setting

\mathfrak{p}\sqsubseteq\mathfrak{q}:\Leftrightarrow\,p_{i}\leq q_{i}\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,r

for multichains
\mathfrak{p}:p_{1}\leq\cdots\leq p_{r}
and

\mathfrak{q}:q_{1}\leq\cdots\leq q_{r}
.

It is easily seen that this partial order does not coincide with any of our constructions.
Nevertheless, it exhibits similar topological properties.

Proposition 4.3
.

The order complex
\Delta(P_{r})
of
P_{r}
with order relation
\sqsubseteq
is homotopy equivalent to the order complex of
P
.

Proof.

Consider the map

\phi:\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}P_{r}&\rightarrow&P_{r}\\
p_{1}\leq\cdots\leq p_{r}&\mapsto&p_{r}\leq\cdots\leq p_{r}\end{array}\right..

Clearly,
\mathfrak{p}\sqsubseteq\mathfrak{q}
implies

\phi(\mathfrak{p})\sqsubseteq\phi(\mathfrak{q})
for
\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q}\in P_{r}
.
The map
\phi
also satisfies
\mathfrak{p}\sqsubseteq\phi(\mathfrak{p})
and
\phi(\phi(\mathfrak{p}))=\phi(\mathfrak{p})

and hence is a closure operator. Thus the order complex of

P_{r}
is homotopy equivalent to
\phi(P_{r})
(see
[
Wac07
, Exercise 5.2.6(a)]
). But

\phi(P_{r})
is isomorphic to
P
which implies the assertion.
∎

Note that the preceding result in not implied by and does not imply
the topological consequences of
[
Müh15
, Proposition 3.5]
.
In that proposition
P
has a unique minimal and unique maximal
element which implies that
P_{r}
ordered
by
\sqsubset
has a unique minimal and a unique
maximal element. The order complex is then formed after
removing these elements.

One can imagine a common generalization of the relations
\sqsubseteq
and
\preceq_{\imath}
.
For that consider a strictly monotone map
\imath:[r]\rightarrow[2r]
with associated
blocks
B_{1},\ldots,B_{\ell}
. For a map
\kappa:[\ell]\rightarrow\{\pm 1\}
define the relation
\preceq_{i,\kappa}
as follows.
For
\mathfrak{p}:p_{1}\leq\cdots\leq p_{r}
and

\mathfrak{q}:q_{1}\leq\cdots\leq q_{r}
we set:

\mathfrak{p}\preceq_{\imath,\kappa}\mathfrak{q}:\iff\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}p_{t}\geq q_{s},&\text{ for }\imath(t)\in B_{r},\kappa(r)=0\text{ and }s\leq\imath(t)-t,\\
p_{t}\leq q_{s},&\text{ for }\imath(t)\in B_{r},\kappa(r)=0\text{ and }s>\imath(t)-t,\\
p_{t}\geq q_{t},&\text{ for }\imath(t)\in B_{r},\kappa(r)=1.\end{array}\right.

for
\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q}\in\mathcal{P}_{r}
.

If
\kappa(r)=1
for all
r\in[\ell]
then
\preceq_{\imath,\kappa}=\sqsubseteq
and if
\kappa(r)=0
for all
r\in[\ell]
then
\preceq_{\imath,\kappa}=\preceq_{\imath}
.
We can again form a graph
G_{\imath,\kappa}(P_{r})
on
P_{r}
whose edges are given by
r
-chains
\mathfrak{p}
and
\mathfrak{q}
such that either
\mathfrak{p}\prec_{\imath,\kappa}\mathfrak{q}
or
\mathfrak{q}\prec_{\imath,\kappa}\mathfrak{p}
.

The following question partly extends Question
4.2
.

Question 4.4
.

For which choices of
\kappa
and

\imath
is
\Delta(G_{\imath,\kappa}(P_{r}))
homeomorphic (resp., homotopy

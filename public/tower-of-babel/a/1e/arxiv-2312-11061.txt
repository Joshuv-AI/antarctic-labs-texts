Stability Analysis of Compartmental and Cooperative systems
Sondre Wiersdalen, Mike Pereira, Annika Lang, Gábor Szederkényi, Jean Auriol, Balázs Kulcsár

Abstract
The present article considers stability of the solutions to nonlinear and nonautonomous compartmental systems governed by ordinary differential equations (ODEs). In particular, compartmental systems with a right-hand side that can be written as a product of a matrix function and vector function. Sufficient, and on occasion necessary, conditions on the matrix function are provided to conclude exponential stability of the null solution. The conditions involve verifying that the matrix function takes its values in a set of compartmental matrices on a certain canonical form, and are easy to check. Similar conditions are provided to establish incremental exponential stability for compartmental systems governed by cooperative systems of ODEs. The solutions to such systems satisfy a so-called ordering. Systems that are cooperative in a box, are shown to be incrementally asymptotically stable if and only if every pair of initially ordered solutions converge to each other. Traffic Reaction Models are used to illustrate the results, which are numerical schemes to solve conservation laws in one spatial dimension. Suitable conditions on the flux function of the conservation law are given such that the numerical scheme gives rise to an incrementally exponentially stable system.

The goal of this paper is to derive sufficient (and on occasion necessary) conditions to establish stability for compartmental systems governed by systems of ordinary differential equations (ODEs).
In particular, we establish exponential stability of the null solution and incremental exponential stability for some compartmental systems governed by cooperative systems of ODEs.

The class ODEs called

The second class systems we consider are cooperative systems of ODEs, a special case of so-called monotone systems.
The solutions to such equations satisfy an ordering with respect to the initial conditions.
Monotone systems have been studied in several aspects concerning stability.
To conclude the asymptotic stability of the null solution, the construction of separable Lyapunov functions was introduced in

In the first contribution of this paper, we construct a linear Lyapunov function that confirms exponential stability of the null solution to a class compartmental systems.
These compartmental systems have a right-hand side that can be written as a product of a matrix-valued function, whose range is a set of compartmental matrices, and a vector-valued function, taking values in the nonnegative half-space.
A formula for the coefficients in the linear Lyapunov function and the exponential rate of decay are provided.
The second contribution is two-fold.

This result opens the possibility for the search of a linear Lyapunov function to establish the latter, which in turn can be used to confirm incremental exponential stability.
We introduce a class compartmental system that are governed by cooperative systems of ODEs as an example.
The latter class systems contain some discretizations of conservation laws arising in traffic flow modeling, called Traffic Reaction Models

V
Conclusion

The main contribution of this article concerns compartmental matrices.
In particular, it was shown that any outflow connected compartmental matrix can be rewritten (by permutation) to a canonical form coined outflow canonical form.
In the main
Theorem

1
, a special set of compartmental matrices on outflow canonical form where constructed, and leveraged to claim exponential stability for a general class compartmental systems that are in general nonlinear and nonautonomous.
The conditions are simple to check and a formula for the exponential rate of decay was provided.

Incremental asymptotic stability for cooperative systems of ODEs were considered.
Systems that are cooperative in a box (Cartesian interval of
n
intervals) where shown to be incrementally asymptotically stable if and only if all initially ordered solutions converge to each other in a
\mathcal{K}\mathcal{L}
sense.
This result was combined with the main theorem to establish incremental exponential stability for a class of compartmental systems governed by cooperative systems of ODEs.
Traffic Reaction Models were used to illustrate the results.

A Lieb-Robinson bound for quantum spin chains with strong on-site impurities
Martin Gebert, Alvin Moon and Bruno Nachtergaele

Abstract
. We consider a quantum spin chain with nearest neighbor interactions and sparsely distributed on-site impurities. We prove commutator bounds for its Heisenberg dynamics which incorporate the coupling strengths of the impurities. The impurities are assumed to satisfy a minimum spacing, and each impurity has a non-degenerate spectrum. Our results are proven in a broadly applicable setting, both in finite volume and in thermodynamic limit. We apply our results to improve Lieb-Robinson bounds for the Heisenberg spin chain with a random, sparse transverse field drawn from a heavy-tailed distribution.



1.
Introduction

Since the first demonstration of a finite group velocity for quantum spin systems in
[
LR72
]
, Lieb-Robinson bounds have played an important role in proving fundamental results in condensed matter theory and quantum information theory
[
BHV06
,
NOS06
,
BnHVC09
]
. The question of whether they can be improved in systems with distinguishing features, such as disorder
[
BEO09
,
BO07
,
HSS12
,
GL16
,
EKS18
]
, anomalous transport
[
DLLY14
]
or assumed rate of decay of interaction
[
TGS+19
,
EMNY20
,
WH20
]
, has received considerable attention in recent years. There is also interest in comparing observed velocities in experiments with the estimates one can prove
[
The14
]
. An overview of proofs and applications of Lieb-Robinson bounds can be found in Section 3 of
[
NSY19
]
.

In this paper we focus on spin chains. Propagation estimates of Lieb-Robinson type for classes of Hamiltonians are typically given in terms of a measure of the strength of the interactions, usually by using a norm. One naturally obtains such estimates that do not depend on the presence and magnitude of terms in the Hamiltonian supported on single sites since, by themselves, such terms do not generate propagation through the system. Here, we consider quantum spin chains with nearest neighbor interactions for which we are given such a propagation estimate that does not depend on single-site terms. We show that under certain conditions, taking into account single-site terms can lead to a sharper estimate. This improvement is manifested by a reduction of the pre-factor (amplitude of the propagation), and not in the Lieb-Robinson velocity.
More specifically, we can exploit large on-site terms (such a magnetic fields) supported on a subset of sites for which we assume a minimum spacing between sites
and a non-degeneracy condition on the eigenvalues of these single-site terms. As a consequence of our main result, Theorem
3.1
, we show that with our set-up, for time
t
and observables
A
and
B
, there exists a constant
C(A,B,t)
such that

(1.1)

\begin{split}\|{[A(t),B]}\|\leq\bigg(\frac{C(A,B,t)}{\lambda}\bigg)^{N}\|{A}\|\|{B}\|\exp(v|t|-\mu d_{A,B})\end{split}

where
N
is the number of impurities which are well-separated from and between the supports of
A
and
B
,
v,\mu>0
are parameters (see Section
2
),
d_{A,B}
is the distance between the supports of
A
and
B
and
\lambda>0
is the minimum impurity strength. The precise statement is found in Corollary
3.2
. The quantity
C(A,B,t)
can be determined explicitly and is independent of the system size and, hence, the estimate in (
1.1
) also holds in the thermodynamic limit (see Section
4.4
). Our result is non-trivial since the velocity term of a Lieb-Robinson bound generally diverges with the strength of the interaction. However, with our methods we do not see an effect of the impurities on the Lieb-Robinson velocity itself.

In Section
4.5
, we apply Theorem
3.1
to the Heisenberg model in the case when a sparse transverse field is coupled to the nearest neighbor interaction with i.i.d. couplings drawn from a heavy-tailed distribution. We show that with high probability,
\|{[A(t),B]}\|
is much smaller than one would expect
from the standard estimates, c.f. the commutator bound from Theorem
2.1
.

2.
Preliminaries and Notation

We consider the 1D lattice
\mathbb{Z}
and associate a copy of
\mathbb{C}^{D}
,
D\geq 2
, to each lattice site. We equip
\mathbb{Z}
with the natural distance
d(x,y)=|x-y|
and define
d(X,Y)=\inf_{x\in X,y\in Y}|x-y|
and
d(x,Y)=d(\left\{x\right\},Y)
for
x\in\mathbb{Z}
and
X,Y\subset\mathbb{Z}
. For any finite
X\subset\mathbb{Z}
, we define

(2.1)

\mathcal{A}_{X}=\bigotimes_{x\in X}M_{D}(\mathbb{C})

where
M_{D}(\mathbb{C})
is the set of
D\times D
matrices. When
X\subset Y
and
|Y|<\infty
, we identify
\mathcal{A}_{X}
as a subalgebra of
\mathcal{A}_{Y}
by the map
A\mapsto A\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{Y\setminus X}
, where
\mathbbm{1}_{Y\setminus X}
is the unit of
\mathcal{A}_{Y\setminus X}
. The algebra of local observables, the
local algebra
for short, is given by

(2.2)

\begin{split}\mathcal{A}_{\textnormal{loc}}=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}X\subset\mathbb{Z}\\
|X|<\infty\end{subarray}}\mathcal{A}_{X},\end{split}

and we will refer to the operator norm completion of
\mathcal{A}_{\textnormal{loc}}
, denoted by
\mathcal{A}
as the
quasi-local algebra
.

A mapping
\eta:\left\{X\subset\mathbb{Z}:|X|<\infty\right\}\to\mathcal{A}
is an
interaction
if
\eta(X)=\eta(X)^{*}\in\mathcal{A}_{X}
for all
X
.
\eta
is a
nearest neighbor
interaction when
\eta(X)\not=0
only if
X=\left\{x,x+1\right\}
for some
x\in\mathbb{Z}
. For a nearest neighbor interaction
\eta
we use the notation
\eta_{x,x+1}=\eta(\left\{x,x+1\right\})
and
\|\eta\|=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\eta_{x,x+1}\|
.

Let
\Phi:\left\{X\subset\mathbb{Z}:|X|<\infty\right\}\to\mathcal{A}
be a nearest neighbor interaction with
\|\Phi\|<\infty.

For
L\in\mathbb{N}
we define

(2.3)

H_{L}(\Phi)\equiv H_{L}=\sum_{x=-L}^{L-1}\Phi_{x,x+1}.

We are interested in on-site perturbations of the Hamiltonian
H_{L}
, which we will refer to as impurities.
To define these let
\emptyset\neq\mathcal{F}\subset\mathbb{Z}
and define the
minimal spacing
of
\mathcal{F}
as

(2.4)

\sigma_{\mathcal{F}}=\min\left\{|x-y|:x,y\in\mathcal{F},x\not=y\right\}.

Later on, we have to assume
\sigma_{\mathcal{F}}
is sufficiently large. Now for each
x\in\mathcal{F}
, let
V_{x}=V_{x}^{*}\in\mathcal{A}_{\left\{x\right\}}
be an operator of the form

(2.5)

\begin{split}V_{x}=\sum_{j=1}^{D}\gamma^{(x)}_{j}P_{j}^{(x)}\end{split}

where
P_{j}^{(x)}
are the eigenprojectors of
V_{x}
, and assume the eigenvalues
\gamma^{(x)}_{j}
are distinct at each site, i.e. for all
x\in\mathcal{F}
,
\gamma^{(x)}_{i}\neq\gamma^{(x)}_{j}
for all
i\neq j
.
We now consider perturbations of the original Hamiltonian
H_{L}
of the following form:

(2.6)

\begin{split}H_{L}(\vec{\lambda})=H_{L}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathcal{F}\cap[-L,L]\end{subarray}}\lambda_{x}V_{x},\end{split}

where
\lambda_{x}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
for all
x\in\mathcal{F}
and
\vec{\lambda}=\big(\lambda_{x}\big)_{x\in\mathcal{F}\cap[-L,L]}
is the vector consisting of the coupling constants.
We use the short-hand notation for the perturbation

(2.7)

V_{L}(\vec{\lambda})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathcal{F}\cap[-L,L]\end{subarray}}\lambda_{x}V_{x}.

For
L\in\mathbb{N}
, denote
\mathcal{A}_{L}=\mathcal{A}_{[-L,L]}
. We are interested in the Heisenberg evolution of an observable
A\in\mathcal{A}_{L}
, which for a Hamiltonian
H=H^{*}\in\mathcal{A}_{L}
is
defined by

(2.8)

\tau_{t}^{H}(A)=e^{itH}Ae^{-itH},

where
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Lastly, we fix some notation we frequently use in the following.
For
X\subset[-L,L]
, we define an enlarged version of
X
by
X(n)=\left\{x\in{[-L,L]}:d(x,X)\leq n\right\}
. For an observable
A\in\mathcal{A}_{L}
we denote by
S_{A}
the support of
A
, which we take to be the minimal length interval
[x,y]
such that
A\in\mathcal{A}_{[x,y]}
.

Since
\Phi
is a nearest neighbor interaction, the dynamics generated by
\Phi
satisfy a Lieb-Robinson bound. We parametrize the bound by a parameter
\mu>0
which is the rate of spatial decay, and the strength
\|{\Phi}\|
of the nearest neighbor interaction. The relevant statement of this commutator bound, Theorem
2.1
below, is implied by Corollary 2.2 of
[
NRSS09
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose
\Phi
is a nearest neighbor interaction with
\|{\Phi}\|<\infty
. For all
\mu>0
, there exist
C_{0},v>0
depending on
\mu
and
\|{\Phi}\|
such that for any operator of the form

(2.9)

\begin{split}\Psi_{L}=\sum_{x=-L}^{L}\Psi_{x},~~\Psi_{x}\in\mathcal{A}_{\left\{x\right\}},\end{split}

if
A,B\in\mathcal{A}_{L}
, then for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
:

(2.10)

\begin{split}\|{[\tau^{H_{L}(\Phi)+\Psi_{L}}_{t}(A),B]}\|\leq C_{0}\|{A}\|\|{B}\|(e^{v|t|}-1)e^{-\mu d(S_{A},S_{B})}.\end{split}

We note that since our definition of support uses a single interval, the bound in (
2.10
) does not depend on the support sizes of
A
and
B
.

Assuming
C_{0}\geq 1
will simplify the form of the constants in Theorem
3.1
without loss of generality. And the proof of Corollary 2.2 in
[
NRSS09
]
shows that the constants
C_{0}
and
v
in Theorem
2.1
can be taken as

(2.11)

\begin{split}C_{0}=\frac{10c_{\mu}}{K_{\mu}}\text{ and }v=8e^{\mu}K_{\mu}\|{\Phi}\|\end{split}

where

(2.12)

\begin{split}c_{\mu}=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-\mu x}\frac{1}{(1+|x|)^{2}}\text{ and }K_{\mu}=\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{e^{-\mu(|x-z|+|y-z|-|x-y|)}(1+|x-y|)^{2}}{(1+|x-z|)^{2}(1+|z-y|)^{2}}.\end{split}

Assumption 2.2
.

In the following, we will always assume
\Phi
is a nearest neighbor interaction with
\|{\Phi}\|<\infty
. For any
\mu>0
, we will take
C_{0}\geq 1
and
v>0
as defined in
(
2.11
)
.

3.
Main results

The main result of this work is the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

For any
\mu>0
, if
\sigma_{\mathcal{F}}>\max\left\{1/\mu,2\right\}
, then there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
L\in\mathbb{N}
and
A,B\in\mathcal{A}_{L}
with
\max S_{A}+3<\min S_{B}-3
,

(3.1)

\begin{split}\|{[\tau_{t}^{H_{L}(\vec{\lambda})}(A),B]}\|\leq\frac{C^{N}}{\prod_{x\in Z}|\lambda_{x}|\Gamma_{x}}\|{A}\|\|{B}\|\,G_{N}(t)\,F_{N}\big(d(S_{A},S_{B})\big)\end{split}

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\vec{\lambda}=\big(\lambda_{x}\big)_{x\in Z}
, where
N=|Z|
for
Z=[\max S_{A}+3,\min S_{B}-3]\cap\mathcal{F}
,

(3.2)

\Gamma_{x}=\min_{i\not=j}|\gamma^{(x)}_{i}-\gamma^{(x)}_{j}|

and

(3.3)

\begin{split}G_{N}(t)=v|t|(1+v|t|)^{N-1}e^{v|t|}\quad\text{ }\quad F_{N}(d)=(\mu d)^{N}e^{-\mu d}.\end{split}

The constant
C
can be taken as

(3.4)

\begin{split}C=\frac{444C_{0}^{2}e^{5\mu}}{\mu(1-e^{-\mu})}\|{\Phi}\|\binom{D}{2}^{2}\end{split}

We prove Theorem
3.1
by modifying the estimate in (
2.10
) by an inductive argument. In principle, we could assume that, under the assumptions of Theorem
2.1
, (
2.10
) holds for a monotone rapidly decreasing function
f
instead of
e^{-\mu d}
, e.g.
f(d)=e^{-\mu d\log(d)}
as in
[
WH20
]
, and derive a similar result without significant changes to the proof.

The velocity term of a Lieb-Robinson bound generally diverges with the strength of the interaction, so it is significant that large on-site terms can lead to
a stronger estimate in (
2.10
). However, our result does not show that a sparse field decreases the Lieb-Robinson velocity. This is because our
method introduces a prefactor polynomial in
t
and the
d
to the commutator bound which, for large times, diverges with the number of field sites. Proposition
4.5
shows that in the case
N=1
, we can choose
F_{1}
to have a marginally better dependence on
d(S_{A},S_{B})
. We leave
open the question of whether the bound in (
3.1
) would hold if
G_{N}(t)=e^{v|t|}
and
F_{N}(d)=e^{-\mu d}
.

Corollary
3.2
, below, follows immediately from Theorem
3.1
and makes the statement in (
1.1
) precise. Let
\mathcal{T}_{x}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
denote the translation operator which maps
\mathcal{A}_{\left\{0\right\}}
to
\mathcal{A}_{\left\{x\right\}}
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\mu>0
,
\sigma_{\mathcal{F}}>\max\left\{1/\mu,2\right\}
. Suppose
V_{x}=\mathcal{T}_{x}(V_{0})
and
\lambda_{x}=\lambda
for all
x\in\mathcal{F}
.
Then for all
A,B\in\mathcal{A}_{L}
with
\max S_{A}+3<\min S_{B}-3
and
Z=[\max S_{A}+3,\min S_{B}-3]\cap\mathcal{F}
, we obtain

(3.5)

\|{[\tau_{t}^{H_{L}(\vec{\lambda})}(A),B]}\|\leq\bigg(K\frac{\mu d(S_{A},S_{B})(1+v|t|)}{\lambda}\bigg)^{N}\|{A}\|\|{B}\|\,e^{v|t|}\,e^{-\mu d(S_{A},S_{B})}.

where we have set
N=|Z|
. The constant
K>0
depends on
\mu,D,\Phi
and
V_{0}
.

4.
Proofs

When the context is unambiguous, we omit the
L
dependence from the notation, since all estimates will be independent of
L
, and write
H_{L}=H
.

4.1.
Auxiliary results

For
x\in\mathcal{F}\cap[-L+2,L-2]
we define

(4.1)

\begin{split}\hat{H}_{x}=\sum_{y=-L}^{x-2}\Phi_{y,y+1}+\sum_{j=1}^{D}P_{j}^{(x)}&(\Phi_{x-1,x}+\Phi_{x,x+1})P_{j}^{(x)}+\sum_{y=x+1}^{L-1}\Phi_{y,y+1}\end{split}

and set for
\vec{\lambda}=\big(\lambda_{x}\big)_{x\in Z}

(4.2)

\hat{H}_{x}(\vec{\lambda})=\hat{H}_{x}+V(\vec{\lambda}).

We will use the short-hand notations

(4.3)

\overline{V}_{x}(\vec{\lambda})=\sum_{y\not=x}\lambda_{y}V_{y},\quad\tau_{t}=\tau_{t}^{H(\vec{\lambda})},\quad\text{and}\quad\hat{\tau}_{t}^{x}=\tau_{t}^{\hat{H}_{x}(\vec{\lambda})}.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{A}_{L}
and
x\in\mathcal{F}\cap[-L+2,L-2]
such that
\max S_{A}<x<\min S_{B}
. Then

(4.4)

\begin{split}[\hat{\tau}_{t}^{x}(A),B]=0.\end{split}

Proof.

From the definition of
\hat{H}_{x}
it follows that
V_{x}
commutes with
\hat{H}_{x}
and with
V_{y}
as well when
y\not=x
. And so, since
\max S_{A}<x

(4.5)

\begin{split}\hat{\tau}_{t}(A)=\tau^{\hat{H}_{x}+\overline{V}_{x}(\vec{\lambda})}_{t}\tau_{t}^{\lambda_{x}V_{x}}(A)=\tau^{\hat{H}_{x}+\overline{V}_{x}(\vec{\lambda})}_{t}(A).\end{split}

We write
\hat{H}_{x}=\hat{H}_{x}^{\ell}+\hat{H}_{x}^{r}
with
\hat{H}_{x}^{\ell}
supported on
[-L,x]
, and
\hat{H}_{x}^{r}
supported on
[x,L]
:

(4.6)

\begin{split}\hat{H}_{x}^{\ell}&=\sum_{y=-L}^{x-2}\Phi_{y,y+1}+\sum_{j=1}^{D}P_{j}^{(x)}\Phi_{x-1,x}P_{j}^{(x)}\\
\hat{H}_{x}^{r}&=\sum_{j=1}^{D}P_{j}^{(x)}\Phi_{x,x+1}P_{j}^{(x)}+\sum_{y=x+1}^{L-1}\Phi_{y,y+1}\end{split}.

Let
j\in\{1,...,D\}
. By assumption,
P_{j}^{(x)}=|\psi_{j}^{(x)}\rangle\langle\psi_{j}^{(x)}|
where
\psi_{j}^{(x)}\in\mathbb{C}^{D}
is a unit norm eigenvector to the simple eigenvalue
\gamma_{j}^{(x)}
of
V_{x}
. Expanding
\Phi_{x-1,x}
into a sum of elementary tensors
\Phi_{x-1,x}=\sum_{k}\Phi_{x-1}^{(k)}\otimes\Phi_{x}^{(k)}
,
\Phi_{x-1}^{(k)}\in\mathcal{A}_{\left\{x-1\right\}}
and
\Phi_{x}^{(k)}\in\mathcal{A}_{\left\{x\right\}}
, shows that

(4.7)

\begin{split}P_{j}^{(x)}\Phi_{x-1,x}P_{j}^{(x)}&=\sum_{k}\Phi_{x-1}^{(k)}\otimes\langle\psi_{j}^{(x)},\Phi_{x}^{(k)}\psi_{j}^{(x)}\rangle P_{j}^{(x)}\\
&=\bigg(\sum_{k}\langle\psi_{j}^{(x)},\Phi_{x}^{(k)}\psi_{j}^{(x)}\rangle\Phi_{x-1}^{(k)}\bigg)\otimes P_{j}^{(x)}.\end{split}

Similarly, there exists
\tilde{\Phi}_{x+1}\in\mathcal{A}_{\left\{x+1\right\}}
such that
P_{j}^{(x)}\Phi_{x,x+1}P_{j}^{(x)}=P_{j}^{(x)}\otimes\tilde{\Phi}_{x+1}
, which proves that
[P_{j}^{(x)}\Phi_{x-1,x}P_{j}^{(x)},P_{j}^{(x)}\Phi_{x,x+1}P_{j}^{(x)}]=0
and in turn
[\hat{H}_{x}^{\ell},\hat{H}_{x}^{r}]=0
. Hence

(4.8)

\begin{split}\bigg[\hat{H}_{x}^{\ell}+\sum_{y<x}\lambda_{y}V_{y},~\hat{H}_{x}^{r}+\sum_{y>x}\lambda_{y}V_{y}\bigg]=0\end{split}

and since
\max S_{A}<x
,

(4.9)

\begin{split}\hat{\tau}_{t}^{x}(A)&=\tau_{t}^{\hat{H}_{x}^{\ell}+\sum_{y<x}\lambda_{y}V_{y}}(A)\in\mathcal{A}_{[-L,x]}.\end{split}

As
x<\min S_{B}
, this implies
[\hat{\tau}_{t}^{x}(A),B]=0
.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{A}_{L}
, and suppose
x\in\mathcal{F}\cap[-L+2,L-2]
such that
\max S_{A}+1<x<\min S_{B}
. Then for all
t\in\mathbb{R}

(4.10)

\begin{split}\|{[\tau_{t}(A),B]}\|\leq\frac{1}{|\lambda_{x}|\Gamma_{x}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k\in\left\{1,\ldots,D\right\}\\
j\not=k\end{subarray}}\bigg(\|{f_{x}^{jk}(0,t)}\|+\int_{0}^{|t|}ds~\|{\frac{d}{ds}f_{x}^{jk}(s,t)}\|\bigg)\end{split}

where we have set for
s\in\mathbb{R}

(4.11)

\begin{split}f^{jk}_{x}(s,t)&=[[\tau^{\hat{H}_{x}+\overline{V}_{x}(\vec{\lambda})}_{s}(R_{jk}^{(x)}),\hat{\tau}_{s}^{x}\tau_{t-s}(A)],B]\\
R_{jk}^{(x)}&=P_{j}^{(x)}(\Phi_{x-1,x}+\Phi_{x,x+1})P_{k}^{(x)}\end{split}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume
t>0
.
Lemma
4.1
implies that
[\hat{\tau}^{x}_{t}(A),B]=0
. Hence Duhamel’s formula gives

\displaystyle[\tau_{t}(A),B]

\displaystyle=[\tau_{t}(A),B]-[\hat{\tau}^{x}_{t}(A),B]

(4.12)

\displaystyle=i\int_{0}^{t}ds~[\hat{\tau}^{x}_{s}([H(\vec{\lambda})-\hat{H}_{x}(\vec{\lambda}),\tau_{t-s}(A)]),B].

We write

(4.13)

H(\vec{\lambda})-\hat{H}_{x}(\vec{\lambda})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k\in\left\{1,\ldots,D\right\}:\\
j\not=k\end{subarray}}R_{jk}^{(x)}

with
R_{jk}^{(x)}=P^{(x)}_{j}(\Phi_{x-1,x}+\Phi_{x,x+1})P^{(x)}_{k}
for
j,k\in\{1,...,D\}
,
j\neq k
.
Since
[\hat{H}_{x}+\overline{V}_{x}(\vec{\lambda}),V_{x}]=0
, we obtain for
s\in\mathbb{R}

(4.14)

\displaystyle\hat{\tau}^{x}_{s}(R)=\tau_{s}^{\hat{H}_{x}+\overline{V}_{x}(\vec{\lambda})}\tau_{s}^{\lambda_{x}V_{x}}(R_{jk}^{(x)})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k\in\left\{1,\ldots,D\right\}\\
j\not=k\end{subarray}}e^{is\lambda_{x}(\gamma_{j}^{(x)}-\gamma_{k}^{(x)})}\tau_{s}^{\hat{H}_{x}+\overline{V}_{x}(\vec{\lambda})}(R_{jk}^{(x)}).

This implies

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}ds\,[\hat{\tau}^{x}_{s}([H(\vec{\lambda})-\hat{H}_{x}(\vec{\lambda}),\tau_{t-s}(A)]),B]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}ds\,[[\hat{\tau}^{x}_{s}(H(\vec{\lambda})-\hat{H}_{x}(\vec{\lambda})),\hat{\tau}^{x}_{s}\tau_{t-s}(A)]),B]

(4.15)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k\in\left\{1,\ldots,D\right\}\\
j\not=k\end{subarray}}\int_{0}^{t}ds\,e^{is\lambda_{x}(\gamma_{j}^{(x)}-\gamma^{(x)}_{k})}f^{jk}_{x}(s,t)

where we have set
f^{jk}_{x}(s,t)=[[\tau^{\hat{H}_{x}+\overline{V}_{x}(\vec{\lambda})}_{s}(R_{jk}^{(x)}),\hat{\tau}^{x}_{s}\tau_{t-s}(A)],B].
For
j,k\in\{1,...,D\}
with
j\neq k
integration by parts yields

\displaystyle\Big\|\int_{0}^{t}ds\,e^{is\lambda_{x}(\gamma_{j}-\gamma_{k})}f^{jk}_{x}(s,t)\Big\|

(4.16)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\lambda_{x}|}\frac{1}{|\gamma_{j}^{(x)}-\gamma_{k}^{(x)}|}\bigg(\|{f^{jk}_{x}(t,t)}\|+\|{f^{jk}_{x}(0,t)}\|+\int_{0}^{t}ds\,\|{\frac{d}{ds}f^{jk}_{x}(s,t)}\|\bigg).

Next we show
f^{jk}_{x}(t,t)=0
. Since
V_{x}
commutes with
\hat{H}_{x}+\overline{V}_{x}(\vec{\lambda})
, we can rewrite

(4.17)

\|f^{jk}_{x}(t,t)\|=\big\|[[\tau^{\hat{H}_{x}+\overline{V}_{x}(\vec{\lambda})}_{t}(R_{jk}^{(x)}),\hat{\tau}^{x}_{t}(A)],B]\big\|=\big\|[\hat{\tau}^{x}_{t}\big([\tau^{\lambda_{x}V_{x}}_{-t}(R_{jk}^{(x)}),A]\big),B]\big\|.

Now
\tau^{\lambda_{x}V_{x}}_{-t}(R_{jk}^{(x)})=e^{it\lambda_{x}(\gamma^{(x)}_{k}-\gamma^{(x)}_{j})}R_{jk}^{(x)}
, and so

(4.18)

\big\|[\hat{\tau}^{x}_{t}\big([\tau^{\lambda_{x}V_{x}}_{-t}(R_{jk}^{(x)}),A]\big),B]\big\|=\big\|[\hat{\tau}^{x}_{t}\big([R_{jk}^{(x)},A]\big),B]\big\|=0

where the last equality follows from the assumption that
S_{A}\cap[x-1,x+1]=\emptyset
.
∎

We will need the explicit form of the derivative
\frac{d}{ds}f_{x}^{jk}(s,t)
in the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{A}_{L}
,
\sigma_{\mathcal{F}}\geq 2
and
x\in\mathcal{F}\cap[-L+2,L-2]
. Then for all
j,k\in\{1,...,D\}
with
j\neq k
and

Suppose

In this appendix we prove the double commutator bound which we use in the proof of Theorem
3.1
. Our proof is a straightforward argument which we include for completeness. Let
\Phi_{1}
and
\Phi_{2}
denote two interactions such that
\Phi_{i}(X)\not=0
only for
X\subset\mathbb{Z}
with
\mathop{\rm diam}(X)=\max\{|x-y|:\ x,y\in X\}\leq 1
and

(5.1)

\|{\Phi_{i}}\|=\sup_{x}\|{\Phi_{i}(\left\{x,x+1\right\})}\|<\infty

for
i=1,2
.
We assume that
\|{\Phi_{1}}\|=\|{\Phi_{2}}\|=\|{\Phi}\|
, and we note that
\Phi_{1}
and
\Phi_{2}
are nearest neighbor interactions where arbitrary large on-site terms are added. Let
\tau_{t}^{(1)}
and
\tau_{t}^{(2)}
be the Heisenberg dynamics on
[-L,L]
generated by
\Phi_{1}
and
\Phi_{2}
, respectively.

Let
\rho_{x}:\mathcal{A}_{\left\{x\right\}}\to\mathbb{C}
denote the normalized trace
\rho_{x}(A)=\frac{1}{D}\textnormal{tr}(A)
, and for any finite subset
Z
, let
{\rm id}_{Z}
denote the identity map on
\mathcal{A}_{Z}
. Then for any finite set
X\subset[-L,L]
we define

\mathbb{E}_{X}={\rm id}_{X}\otimes\bigotimes_{y\in[-L,L]\setminus X}\rho_{y}
, which has the following approximation property.

Lemma 5.1
.

[
BHV06
,
NSW12
, Cor. 3.1]

Suppose
A\in\mathcal{A}_{L}
, and suppose there exist
X\subset[-L,L]
and
\varepsilon>0
such that for all
B\in\mathcal{A}_{[-L,L]\setminus X}
,

(5.2)

\begin{split}\|{[A,B]}\|\leq\varepsilon\|{A}\|\|{B}\|.\end{split}

Then
\|{({\rm id}_{[-L,L]}-\mathbb{E}_{X})(A)}\|\leq\varepsilon\|{A}\|
.

Lastly, we denote

(5.3)

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{r}=\mathbb{E}_{[-L,r)}.\end{split}

for any
r\in(-L,L]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{A}_{L}
such that
\max S_{A}<\min S_{B}
. Suppose there exists
k\geq 0
such that for all
T\in\mathcal{A}_{L}
with
\min S_{T}>\max S_{A}+k
,

(5.4)

\begin{split}\|{[\tau^{(1)}_{t}(A),T]}\|\leq C_{*}\|{A}\|\|{T}\|g(t)f(d(S_{A},S_{T}))\end{split}

for some constant
C_{*}>0
and
g,f
real-valued functions, where
f
is monotone decreasing. Then, for all
W\in\mathcal{A}_{L}
such that

(5.5)

\begin{split}\max S_{A}+k<\min S_{W}-1\leq\max S_{W}<\min S_{B}\end{split}

we have

(5.6)

\begin{split}&\|{[[W,\tau_{t}^{(1)}(A)],\tau_{s}^{(2)}(B)]}\|\leq\bigg(24C_{0}\frac{e^{\mu}}{1-e^{-\mu}}\bigg)C_{*}\|{A}\|\|{B}\|\|{W}\|g(t)e^{v|s|}h_{\mu}(S_{A},S_{W},S_{B})\end{split}

where

(5.7)

\begin{split}&h_{\mu}(S_{A},S_{W},S_{B})=f(d(S_{A},S_{B}))+f(d(S_{A},S_{W})-1)e^{-\mu d(S_{W},S_{B})}\\
&\hskip 156.49014pt+\sum_{m=1}^{D_{W}}f(d(S_{A},S_{W})+m-2)e^{-\mu(D_{W}-m)}\end{split}

Denote
{\rm id}={\rm id}_{[-L,L]}.
Without loss of generality, we suppose

. For any
X\subset[-L,L]

,

and
W

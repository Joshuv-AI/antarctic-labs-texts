Central Limit Theorem on Symmetric Kullback–Leibler (KL) Divergence
Helder Rojas, Artem Logachov

Abstract
In this paper we provide an asymptotic theory for the symmetric version of the Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence. We define a estimator for this divergence and study its asymptotic properties. In particular, we prove Law of Large Numbers (LLN) and the convergence to the normal law in the Central Limit Theorem (CLT) using this estimator.



Keywords—
Symmetric KL-Divergence estimation, Central Limit Theorem (CLT).

,

[

, information geometry
[
15
]
, among many other application fields. Let
\mathcal{X}=\{a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{r}\}
be a finite countable set, where
r\geq 2
. The probability measures on
\mathcal{X}
are finite dimensional vectors
\mathbf{p}
in

\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})=\bigg\{\mathbf{p}=(p_{a})_{a\in\mathcal{X}}\in\mathbb{R}^{r}:p_{a}\geq 0\hskip 9.24994pt\textrm{and}\hskip 9.24994pt\sum_{a\in\mathcal{X}}p_{a}=1\bigg\}.

If
\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})
, the KL divergence between
\mathbf{p}
and
\mathbf{q}
is defined, according to

, as following

\mathcal{D}_{KL}(\mathbf{p}||\mathbf{q})=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{X}}p_{a}\ln\Big(\frac{p_{a}}{q_{a}}\Big).

The KL divergence is possibly the most famous and used of a large family of divergences called
f
-Divergence. However, KL divergence is asymmetric, i.e., the values of
\mathcal{D}_{KL}(\mathbf{p}||\mathbf{q})
and
\mathcal{D}_{KL}(\mathbf{q}||\mathbf{p})
can be significantly different. This lack of symmetry, in some specific contexts, can be a disadvantage when measuring similarities between probability measures, see e.g.
[
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
]
. That is why it is often quite useful to work with a symmetrization of the KL divergence which is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{KL}^{sym}(\mathbf{p}||\mathbf{q})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{KL}(\mathbf{p}||\mathbf{q})+\mathcal{D}_{KL}(\mathbf{q}||\mathbf{p}),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in\mathcal{X}}(p_{a}-q_{a})\ln\Big(\frac{p_{a}}{q_{a}}\Big).

This symmetrization, also known as Jeffreys divergence, was introduced and used in
[
22
]
. For some benefits obtained by including symmetrization in KL divergence, see e.g.
[
23
,
24
,
25
]
. As a consequence of the desirable properties of
\mathcal{D}_{KL}^{sym}(\mathbf{p}||\mathbf{q})
, interest in estimating its value on a sample basis has been growing
[
26
,
27
,
28
,
29
,
30
]
. Therefore, we consider it important to establish good estimators for the symmetric KL divergence, and in turn, guarantee the efficiency and precision of their estimates through the study of their respective asymptotic properties. The statistical argument presented above is what motivated and guided our interest in obtaining our main results.

Before we state the main results we need a few definitions. Let
(X,Y)
be a bivariate random vector defined on the probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\mathbf{P})
with
\Omega=\mathcal{X}\times\{0,1\}
. We denote by
\mathbf{E}
the expectation with respect to
\mathbf{P}
. Let
\mathbf{p}=(p_{j})_{1\leq j\leq r}
and
\mathbf{q}=(q_{j})_{1\leq j\leq r}
two probability measures on
\mathcal{X}
such that, for any
j=1,\dots,r
, we have that

p_{j}=\mathbf{P}(X=a_{j}|Y=1)\hskip 9.24994pt\textrm{and}\hskip 9.24994ptq_{j}=\mathbf{P}(Y=a_{j}|Y=0).

Consider the empirical probability measures
\hat{\mathbf{p}}_{n}=(\hat{p}_{j,n})_{1\leq j\leq r}
and
\hat{\mathbf{q}}_{n}=(\hat{q}_{j,n})_{1\leq j\leq r}
, generated by the sequence i.i.d. random variables
(X_{1},Y_{1})\dots,(X_{n},Y_{n})
from the bivariate distribution,
(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})
, defined from

\hat{p}_{j,n}:=\frac{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{I}(X_{i}=a_{j},Y_{i}=1)}{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{I}(Y_{i}=1)},\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt\hat{q}_{j,n}:=\frac{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{I}(X_{i}=a_{j},Y_{i}=0)}{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{I}(Y_{i}=0)}.

(1.1)

Additionally, consider the following notation

\hat{p}_{n}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{I}(Y_{i}=1),\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt\hat{q}_{n}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{I}(Y_{i}=0).

(1.2)

Based on the Equation (
1.1
), we define an estimator for the symmetric KL divergence divergence
\mathcal{D}_{KL}^{sym}(\mathbf{p}||\mathbf{q})
which has the form

\mathcal{D}_{KL}^{sym}(\hat{\mathbf{p}}_{n}||\hat{\mathbf{q}}_{n}):=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{r}(\hat{p}_{j,n}-\hat{q}_{j,n})\ln\frac{\hat{p}_{j,n}}{\hat{q}_{j,n}}.

(1.3)

In this paper, we study the convergence properties, in particular LLN and CLT, of the estimator defined in (
1.3
). To this end, consider the following notation

\displaystyle\eta_{n}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{KL}^{sym}(\hat{\mathbf{p}}_{n}||\hat{\mathbf{q}}_{n})-\mathcal{D}_{KL}^{sym}(\mathbf{p}||\mathbf{q}),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{r}\left((\hat{p}_{j,n}-\hat{q}_{j,n})\ln\frac{\hat{p}_{j,n}}{\hat{q}_{j,n}}-(p_{j}-q_{j})\ln\frac{p_{j}}{q_{j}}\right).

It is true that there are some works on asymptotic theory for divergence measurements, especially for asymmetric KL divergence, see e.g.
[
31
,
32
,
33
,
34
]
. However, to the best of our knowledge, there are very few theoretical works about this symmetric version of the KL divergence. In this paper we have been careful with mathematical rigor and consequently we have obtained finer and simpler results than those available in the existing literature.

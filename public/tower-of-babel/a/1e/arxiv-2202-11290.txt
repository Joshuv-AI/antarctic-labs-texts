Equivariant Bordism of 2-Torus Manifolds and Unitary Toric Manifolds Thanks: This work is supported by grants from NSFC11801379
Bo Chen, Zhi Lü, Qiangbo Tan

Abstract
. The equivariant bordism classification of manifolds with group actions is an essential subject in the study of transformation groups. We are interesting in the action of 2-torus group ℤ 2 n \mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n} and torus group T n T^{n} , and study the equivariant bordism of 2-torus manifolds and unitary toric manifolds. In this paper, we give a new description of the group 𝒵 n ​ ( ℤ 2 n ) \mathcal{Z}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}) of 2-torus manifolds, and determine the dimention of 𝒵 n ​ ( ℤ 2 n ) \mathcal{Z}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}) as a ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} -vector space. With the help of toric topology, Lü and Tan proved that the bordism groups 𝒵 n ​ ( ℤ 2 n ) \mathcal{Z}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}) are generated by small covers. We will give a new proof to this result. These results can be generalized to the equivariant bordism of unitary toric manifolds, that is, we will give a new description of the group 𝒵 n U ​ ( T n ) \mathcal{Z}_{n}^{U}(T^{n}) of unitary torus manifolds, and prove that 𝒵 n U ​ ( T n ) \mathcal{Z}_{n}^{U}(T^{n}) can be generated by quasitoric manifolds with omniorientations.



1.
Introduction

An
n
-dimensional
2-torus manifold
is a smooth closed
n
-dimensional manifold equipped with an effective smooth
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-action, so its fixed point set is empty or consists of isolated points (see
[
12
,
13
]
). A
unitary
T^{n}
-manifold
is an oriented closed smooth manifold
M^{m}
with an effective
T^{n}
-action such that its tangent bundle admits a
T^{n}
-equivariant stable complex structure, where
T^{n}=(S^{1})^{n}
is the compact torus group of dimention
n
. A unitary
T^{n}
-manifold
M^{2n}
of dimension 2n with nonempty fixed set is called a
unitary torus manifold
or
unitary toric manifold
(see
[
15
,
10
]
). All the group actions in this article are assumed to be effective.

Davis and Januszkiewicz
[
7
]
developed the theory of small covers and quasitoric manifolds, which are topological versions of real toric varieties and toric varieties, respectively. A small cover is a special case of 2-torus manifolds. A quasitoric manifold can be equipped with an omniorientation structure, this omniorientation determines a stable complex structure (see
[
2
]
). So a quasitoric manifold with an omniorientation is a special case of unitary toric manifolds.

In Section 2, we will focus on the unoriented equivarint bordism of 2-torus manifolds. In the 1960s Conner and Floyd
[
4
]
begun the study of unoriented equivarint bordism theory of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
manifolds, they introduced a graded commutative algebra over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
with unit,
\mathcal{Z}_{*}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})=\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}\mathcal{Z}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})
, where the equivariant bordism group
\mathcal{Z}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})
is consists of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-equivariant unoriented bordism classes of all
m
-dimensional 2-torus manifolds with effective action and fixing finite set. The addition and multiplication on
\mathcal{Z}_{*}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})
are defined to be the disjoint union and the cartesian product with diagonal
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-actions, respectively. For
n\leq 2
, Conner and Floyd showed that
\mathcal{Z}_{*}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})\cong Z_{2}
and
\mathcal{Z}_{*}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}[u]
, where
u
denotes the class of
\mathbb{R}P^{2}
with the standard
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
-action. The equivariant unoriented bordism group of all
n
-dimensional 2-torus manifolds is
\mathcal{Z}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})
, which was also denoted by
\mathfrak{M}_{n}
in
[
12
]
. The group
\mathcal{Z}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})
was determined for
n=3
by Lü
[
12
]
and for general
n\geq 3
by Lü and Tan
[
14
]
. As showed in
[
14
]
,
\mathcal{Z}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})
is generated by all small covers of dimention
n
, and each class of
\mathcal{Z}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})
contains a small cover as its representative. A computer algorithm was given there to search for the generators of
\mathcal{Z}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})
and calculate the dimension
\text{dim}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(\mathcal{Z}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}))
. For
n>4
, the datas are too complex for the algorithm to work out. How to calculate
\text{dim}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(\mathcal{Z}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}))
remains a problem.

As stated by Davis and Januszkiewicz in
[
7
]
, there is a universal complex
X(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
such that every small cover can be obtained as a pullback from
X(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})
via a suitable map. To prove that
\mathcal{Z}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})
is generated by all small covers, Lü and Tan
[
14
]
defined a boundary operator
d_{*}
on the dual of the Conner–Floyd algebra
\mathcal{R}_{*}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})
, where
\mathcal{R}_{*}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})=\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}\mathcal{R}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})
is the graded polynomial algebra over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
generated by all irreducible
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-representations. Then the existence theorem of tom Dieck is equivalent to the condition
d_{*}f^{*}=0
(
[
14
]
Theorem 7.2). The differential
d_{*}
is almost the same as the differential
\partial_{*}
on the chain complex
(C_{*}(X(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})),\partial_{*})
with coefficient
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, and
f^{*}
corresponds to a closed chain, which represents an homology element in
H_{n-1}(X(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n});\mathbb{Z}_{2})
. With these observations, we will show the following main theorem for the equivariant bordism of 2-torus manifolds.

Theorem 1.1
.

For each
n\geq 1
, the equivariant bordism group
\mathcal{Z}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})
of 2-torus manifolds is isomorphic to the homology group
H_{n-1}(X(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n});\mathbb{Z}_{2})
of the universal
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-complex.

\mathcal{Z}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})\cong H_{n-1}(X(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n});\mathbb{Z}_{2})

It has already been shown in
[
1
]
that the homotopy type of
X(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})
is the wedge of
A_{n}
spheres
S^{n-1}
, where
A_{n}
is a number related to the dimension
n
. So as a corollary, the dimention
\text{dim}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(\mathcal{Z}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}))=A_{n}
can be given straightforward, this solve the problem left in
[
14
]
.

Corollary 1.2
.

The dimention of
\mathcal{Z}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})
as a linear space over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is

\dim_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(\mathcal{Z}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}))=A_{n}=(-1)^{n}+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{n-1-i}\frac{(2^{n}-2^{i})\cdots(2^{n}-2^{0})}{(i+1)!}

The homotopy type of
X(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})
and the isomorphism Theorem
1.1
inspire a new proof to a main theorem in
[
14
]
.

Corollary 1.3

(
[
14
]
, Theorem 2.5)
.

The group
\mathcal{Z}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})
is generated by equivariant unoriented bordism classes of small covers of dimention
n
.

In Section 3, we will deal with the the equivariant bordism of unitary toric manifolds. There have been many different ways to study the equivariant unitary bordism (see
[

,
5
,
8
,
11
,

). However, the explicit ring structure is still difficult to calculate. Let

denote the equivariant unitary bordism ring of unitary

-manifolds with finite fixed point set, then

generalized the methods in

to the study of

-representations. The boundary operator

can be defined on the dual of

still holds. But the unitary case turns out to be more difficult since the existence of an infinite number of irreducible representations. For

and 2, the group

was shown to be generated by quasitoric manifolds with omniorientations, and each class of

contains a quasitoric manifold with omniorientation as its representative. But for
n>2
, it can only be conjectured that each class of
\mathcal{Z}_{n}^{U}(T^{n})
contains a quasitoric manifold with omniorientation as its representative (see
[
6
]
).

There exists a universal complex
X(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
characterizing the quasitoric manifolds with omniorientations (
[
1
]
Proposition 2.1). As in the 2-torus case, the differential

on the dual of
\Lambda_{\mathbb{Z}}^{*}(J_{n}^{\mathbb{C}})
corresponds to the differential
\partial_{*}
on the chain complex
(C_{*}(X(\mathbb{Z}^{n}))
with coefficient
\mathbb{Z}
, and
f^{*}
corresponds to a closed chain, which represents an homology element in

. With these observations, we will show the following main theorem for equivariant bordism of unitary toric manifolds.

Theorem 1.4
.

For each
n\geq 1
, the equivariant bordism group
\mathcal{Z}_{2n}^{U}(T^{n})
of unitary torus manifolds is isomorphic to the homology group
H_{n-1}(X(\mathbb{Z}^{n});\mathbb{Z})
of the universal
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-complex
X(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
.

\mathcal{Z}_{2n}^{U}(T^{n})\cong H_{n-1}(X(\mathbb{Z}^{n});\mathbb{Z})

The homotopy type of
X(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
was proved to be the wedge of countably infinite spheres

(see
[
1
]
). So for

, we have
\mathcal{Z}_{2}^{U}(T^{1})\cong\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathcal{Z}_{2n}^{U}(T^{n})
is isomorphic to a countably infinite direct sum of

(Corollary
3.8
). The homotopy type of

and Theorem

inspire that
\mathcal{Z}_{2n}^{U}(T^{n})
is generated by quasitoric manifold with omniorientations (see Proposition
3.13
). The generalized connect sums introduced by Buchstaber-Ray

]
and Darby
[
6

suggest that the sum of two class

and
[M_{2}]
can still be represented by a quasitoric manifold with omniorientations, if
[M_{1}]
and
[M_{2}]
are represented by quasitoric manifolds with omniorientations. So we can prove the following proposition and solve the conjecture that

is generated by quasitoric manifolds with omniorientations .

Proposition 1.5
.

Each class of
\mathcal{Z}_{2n}^{U}(T^{n})
contains an omnioriented quasitoric manifold as its representative.

2.
Equivariant Bordism of 2-Torus Manifolds

Following
[
4
]
, let
\mathcal{R}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})
be the linear space over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, generated by the isomorphism classes of all
m
-dimensional real
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-representations. The direct sum of representations defines a multiplication on
\mathcal{R}_{*}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})=\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}\mathcal{R}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})
and makes it into a graded commutative algebra over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, with unit the representation of degree 0.
\mathcal{R}_{*}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})
is called the
Conner–Floyd unoriented
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-representation algebra
. As pointed out in
[
12
]
,
\mathcal{R}_{*}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})
is not the Grothendieck ring of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-representations. Every nontrivial irreducible real
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-representation is 1-dimensional and has the form
\tau_{\rho}:\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
with
\tau_{\rho}(g,x)=(-1)^{\rho(g)}
for
\rho\in\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n},\mathbb{Z}_{2})
, and
\tau_{\rho}
is trivial if
\rho(g)=0
for all
g\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
. Let
J_{n}^{\mathbb{R}}=\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n},\mathbb{Z}_{2})\setminus\{0\}
and regard it as the set of all nontrivial irreducible real
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-representations, let
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[J_{n}^{\mathbb{R}}]
denote the free polynomial algebra on
J_{n}^{\mathbb{R}}
over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, then
\mathcal{R}_{*}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})
can be identified with
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[J_{n}^{\mathbb{R}}]
.

The essential relation between the equivariant unoriented bordism ring
\mathcal{Z}_{*}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})=\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}\mathcal{Z}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})
and the Conner–Floyd unoriented
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-representation algebra
\mathcal{R}_{*}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})
is characterized by the Stong
[
18
]
.

Theorem 2.1

(Stong)
.

The ring homomorphism

\varphi_{*}:\mathcal{Z}_{*}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})\to\mathcal{R}_{*}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})

defined by
[M]\mapsto\sum_{p\in M^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}}}[\tau_{p}M]
is a monomorphism as algebras over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, where
\tau_{p}M
denotes the real
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-representation on the tangent space at the fixed point
p\in M^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}}
.

For each fixed point
p\in M^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}}
, the tangent
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-representation
\tau_{p}M
can be decomposed into a direct sum of 1-dimensional irreducible real representations
\tau_{p}M=\oplus_{j=1}^{n}\tau_{i,j}
, the set
\{[\tau_{p}M]\in\mathcal{R}_{*}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})|p\in M^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}}\}
is called the
fixed point data
of
M
. By Stong’s theorem, the equivariant bordism class
[M]
in
\mathcal{Z}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})
is determined by its fixed point data. But which polynomials in
\mathcal{R}_{*}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})
are polynomial from fixed point datas? In
[
8
]
, tom Dieck proved an existence theorem, showed that the elements in
\mathcal{R}_{*}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})
are determined by their equivariant characteristic numbers. In
[
14
]
, Lü and Tan gave a simple proof to this theorem, and formulated it into the following result in terms of Kosniowski and Stong’s localization formula.

Theorem 2.2

(tom Dieck-Kosniowski-Stong)
.

Let
\{\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{l}\}
be a collection of
m
-dimensional faithful
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-representations in
\mathcal{R}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})
. Then a necessary and sufficient condition that
\tau_{1}+\dots+\tau_{l}\in Im(\varphi_{m})
(or
\{\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{l}\}
is the fixed point data of a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-manifold
M^{m}
) is that for all symmetric polynomial functions
f(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})
over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
,

(2.1)

\sum_{i=1}^{l}\frac{f(\tau_{i})}{\chi^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}}(\tau_{i})}\in H^{*}(B\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n};\mathbb{Z}_{2})

where
\chi^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}}(\tau_{i})
denotes the equivariant Euler class of
\tau_{i}
, which is a product of
m
nonzero elements of
H^{1}(B\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
, and
f(\tau_{i})
means that variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{m}
in the function
f(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})
are replaced by those
m
degree-one factors in
\chi^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}}(\tau_{i})
.

If
{\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{l}}
is the fixed data of a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-manifold
M^{m}
, then the polynomial
2.1
is exactly an equivariant Stiefel–Whitney number of
M^{m}
. Although all elements of
Im(\varphi_{*})
can be characterized by the above localization formula, the ring structure of
\mathcal{Z}_{*}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})
is still far from settled. It was showed by Conner and Floyd in [12] that
\mathcal{Z}_{*}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, and
\mathcal{Z}_{*}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}[u]
, where
u
denotes the class of
\mathbb{R}P^{2}
with the standard
(\mathbb{Z}_{2})^{2}
-action. But for
n>2
, the structures and generators of
\mathcal{Z}_{*}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})
are still unknown.

When
m=n
, the group
\mathcal{Z}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})
is exactly formed by the classes of all
n
-dimensional 2-torus manifolds, and the graded ring is denoted by
\mathcal{Z}_{*}^{\mathbb{R}}=\bigoplus\mathcal{Z}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})
. The restriction of
\varphi_{*}|_{\mathcal{Z}_{*}^{\mathbb{R}}}:\mathcal{Z}_{*}^{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathcal{R}_{*}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})
is still a monomorphism. Suppose
[M^{m}]\in\mathcal{Z}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})
and
p\in M^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}}
is a fixed point, then the set
\{\tau_{p,1},\dots,\tau_{p,m}\}
contains a basis of
J_{n}^{\mathbb{R}}
. If
m=n
and
M^{n}
is a 2-torus manifold, then the monomial
\tau_{p}=\tau_{p,1}\cdots\tau_{p,n}
corresponds to a faithful representation, which means that
\{\tau_{p,1},\dots,\tau_{p,n}\}
is a basis of
J_{n}^{\mathbb{R}}
.

Definition 2.3
.

A homogeneous polynomial
g=\sum_{i}\tau_{i,1}\cdots\tau_{i,m}
of degree
m\leq n
in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[J_{n}^{\mathbb{R}}]
is called an
essential
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-polynomial
if for each
i
, the elements in
\{\tau_{i,1},\dots,\tau_{i,m}\}
are linearly independent in
\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n},\mathbb{Z}_{2})
. An essential
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-polynomial of degree
n
is also called
faithful
.

For a 2-torus manifold
M
, the image
\varphi_{*}([M])=\sum_{p\in M^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}}}[\tau_{p}M]\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}[J_{n}^{\mathbb{R}}]
is a faithful polynomial. The space
J_{n}^{\mathbb{R}}
has a dual space
J_{n}^{*\mathbb{R}}=\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{2},\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})\setminus\{0\}
, and they are both isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
, the is defined by the following composition of homomorphisms:

<\cdot,\cdot>:J_{n}^{*\mathbb{R}}\times J_{n}^{\mathbb{R}}\to Hom(\mathbb{Z}_{2},\mathbb{Z}_{2})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}

<\xi,\rho>=\rho\circ\xi

Similarly, we can define essential and faithful
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[J_{n}^{*\mathbb{R}}]
. If
g=\sum_{i}\tau_{i,1}\dots\tau_{i,n}
is a faithful
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-polynomial in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[J_{n}^{\mathbb{R}}]
, then each basis
\{\tau_{i,1},\cdots,\tau_{i,n}\}
has a dual basis
\{s_{i,1},\cdots,s_{i,n}\}
, and they determine a unique homogeneous polynomial
g^{*}=\sum_{i}s_{i,1}\dots s_{i,n}
in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[J_{n}^{*\mathbb{R}}]
, which is called the
dual
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-polynomial
of
g
. It is obvious that
g
is faithful in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[J_{n}^{\mathbb{R}}]
if and only if
g^{*}
is faithful in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[J_{n}^{*\mathbb{R}}]
.

In
[
14
]
, Lü and Tan defined a differential operator
d
on the graded ring
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[J_{n}^{*\mathbb{R}}]
as follows: for each monomial
\rho_{1}\dots\rho_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}[J_{n}^{*\mathbb{R}}]
with
k\geq 1
, let

(2.2)

d_{k}(\rho_{1}\dots\rho_{k})=\begin{cases}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\rho_{1}\dots\rho_{i-1}\hat{\rho_{i}}\rho_{i+1}\dots\rho_{k}&\text{if}\ k>1\\
1&\text{if}\ k=1\end{cases}

and
d_{0}(1)=0
, where the symbol
\hat{\rho_{i}}
means that
\rho_{i}
is deleted. It is easy to check that
d^{2}=0
, so it can be regarded as a boundary operator. For 2-torus manifolds, it was proved in
[
14
]
that the existence Theorem
2.2
of tom Dieck is equivalent to the following.

Theorem 2.4

(Lü-Tan)
.

Let
g=\sum_{i}t_{i,1}\dots t_{i,n}
be a faithful
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-polynomial in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[J_{n}^{\mathbb{R}}]
. Then

d_{n}(g^{*})=0
.

Let
0\leq m\leq n-1
, let
\mathcal{E}_{m}(J_{n}^{*\mathbb{R}})
be the set of all essential
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-polynomials of degree
m+1
in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[J_{n}^{*\mathbb{R}}]
. If we regard an element in
J_{n}^{*\mathbb{R}}
as a vertex, and an essential monomial
\tau_{i,1}\cdots\tau_{i,m}
as a simplex of dimention
m-1
, then the graded polynomial ring
\mathcal{E}_{*}(J_{n}^{*\mathbb{R}})=\bigoplus_{0\leq m\leq n-1}\mathcal{E}_{m}(J_{n}^{*\mathbb{R}})
can be regarded as a chian complex with chain map
d_{*}
. This point of view is similar to the definition of
universal
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-space

X(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})
, which was introduced and used to characterized the small covers by Davis and Januszkiewicz in
[
7
]
.

Definition 2.5
.

A subset
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\}
of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
is
unimodular
if span
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\}
is a direct summand of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
with dimension
m
. Note that a subset of a unimodular set is itself unimodular. The collection of all unimodular subsets of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
forms a simplicial complex
X(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})
on unimodular vertices, which is called the
universal
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-complex
.

As showed in
[
1
]
, the universal complex
X(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})
is a pure simplicial complex and an
(n-1)
-connected Cohen-Macaulay space. It is the universal simplicial complex classifying small covers of dimention
n
. Actually, it can be used to classify the equivariant bordism of 2-torus manifolds as stated in Theorem
1.1
and we now give a proof to that theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
0\leq m\leq n-1
. Let
r:J_{n}^{*\mathbb{R}}=Hom(\mathbb{Z}_{2},\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
be the standard isomorphim. For an essential monomial
s_{i}^{*}=s_{i,1}\cdots s_{i,m}\in\mathcal{E}_{m}(J_{n}^{*\mathbb{R}})
, the set
\{r(s_{i,1}),\dots,r(s_{i,m})\}
is unimodular in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
, it spans a simplex of dimention
m-1
in the universal complex
X(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})
, denoted by
r(s_{i}^{*})
. Let
(C_{*}(X(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})),\partial_{*})=\sum_{0\leq m\leq n-1}(C_{m}(X(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}),\partial_{m})
be the chain complex of
X(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})
, then the map

\pi^{-1}(F)
is submanifold of dimention

J_{n}^{*\mathbb{C}}
. Quasitoric manifolds can be equipped with an additional structure called

Definition 3.10
.

An
omniorientation
of a quasitoric manifold
M^{2n}
consists of a choice of an orientation for
M^{2n}
and for every facial submanifold
M^{2(n-1)}=\pi^{-1}(F_{i}),i=1,...,m
.

Note that a choice of orientation for
P^{n}
is equivalent to a choice of orientation for
M^{2n}
, and an omniorientation on
M^{2n}
is equivalent to a choice of orientations for
P^{n}
and all its facets. As proved by Buchstaber and Ray in
[
2
]

determines a stably complex structure on it, which is compatible with the action of the torus. So a quasitoric manifold

with an omniorientation is a special case of unitary toric manifolds. If we forget the group action, then the bordism class
[M]
is an element of the ordinary unitary bordism group
\Omega_{2n}^{U}
. For
n>1
, every complex cobordism class in
\Omega_{2n}^{U}
contains a quasitoric manifold with an omniorientation (
[
2
]

Definition 3.11

quasitoric pair

n

where

are some facets of

.

Two quasitoric pairs

\Lambda_{1}=\Lambda_{2}\Sigma

P^{n}

Proposition 3.13

Proof.

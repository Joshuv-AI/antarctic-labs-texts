On a Problem of Mező and its Generalizations to Three Classes of Rational Zeta Series


Abstract
We evaluate in closed form three special classes of alternating zeta series with one and two additional parameters. Two classes are expressed as linear combinations of polylogarithms while for the third class we prove an expression involving the incomplete gamma function and the exponential integral. We also present some related series that can be deduced from the main results as well as some series with Fibonacci and Lucas numbers as coefficients. Particular cases of the series presented here will be rediscoveries of identities established by Zhang and Williams, Choi and Srivastava, and Orr, among others. We will also rediscover a series identity published by Mező in 2015 as a problem proposal in the American Mathematical Monthly. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification : 41A58, 11M99, 11B39, 33B15. Keywords : Riemann zeta function; rational zeta series; polylogarithm; Clausen function; Fibonacci numbers; Lucas numbers; Bernoulli numbers.

1
Motivation

This paper has two sources of motivation. The first source is a problem proposal by Mező from 2015 that appeared in the American Mathematical Monthly
[
13
]
. It asks to prove the identity

\frac{1}{2\pi}\Li_{2}(e^{-2\pi})=\ln(2\pi)-1-\frac{5\pi}{12}-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}\zeta(2k)}{k(2k+1)},

(1.1)

where

\zeta(s)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{s}},\qquad\Re(s)>1,

is the Riemann zeta function and

\Li_{2}(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}}{k^{2}},\qquad|z|<1,

is the dilogarithm. During the course of solving this problem, we found an interesting generalization for which we could provide
two different proofs. Searching deeper in this direction we got familiar with the recent papers by Orr
[
12
,
14
]

which became the second source of motivation for writing this paper.
Orr has derived beautiful results for two families of rational zeta series which he expressed using
the Clausen functions
\Cl_{n}(x)
. One such evaluation involving
\zeta(2n)
is
[
14
, Eq. (2.4)]

-2\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta(2n)z^{2n}}{2n+p}=\sum_{k=0}^{p}\frac{p!(-1)^{\lfloor(k+3)/2\rfloor}}{(p-k)!(2\pi z)^{k}}\Cl_{k+1}(2\pi z)+\delta_{\lfloor p/2\rfloor,p/2}\frac{p!(-1)^{p/2}}{(2\pi z)^{p}}\zeta(p+1),

where
\delta_{j,k}
is the Kronecker delta function. Orr
[
14
, Eq. (3.5)]
also evaluates series of the form

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta(2n)z^{2n}}{(2n)(2n+1)\cdots(2n+m-1)(2n+m+p)}.

Here,
\Cl_{n}(x)
are the Clausen functions defined by

\Cl_{1}(x)=-\ln\left(2\sin\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)\right),\quad|x|<2\pi,

and, for
n\geq 2
, by
[
11
, Formulas (7.9) and (7.10)]

\Cl_{n}(x)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\Im\big(\Li_{n}(e^{ix})\big)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sin(kx)}{k^{n}},&\text{if $n$ is even;}\\
\displaystyle\Re\big(\Li_{n}(e^{ix})\big)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\cos(kx)}{k^{n}},&\text{if $n$ is odd.}\end{cases}

(1.2)

See also
[
16
]
for new information about the Clausen functions.

In this article, for a positive integer
n
, we first consider series of the form

P(n,z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}\zeta(2k)z^{2k}}{k(2k+n)},\qquad 0<|z|\leq 1.

In addition, for positive integers
m
and
n
with
m\neq n
, we also treat the class of rational series

P(m,n,z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}\zeta(2k)z^{2k}}{k(2k+m)(2k+n)},\qquad 0<|z|\leq 1,

and its degenerated counterpart for
m\geq 1

Q(m,z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}\zeta(2k)z^{2k}}{k(2k+m)^{2}},\qquad 0<z\leq 1.

We express
P(n,z)
and
P(m,n,z)
as linear combinations of polylogarithms
\Li_{s}(x)
.
For
Q(m,z)
we prove an identity involving the incomplete gamma function
\Gamma(a,x)
and the exponential integral
\Ein(x)
. We also present some related series that can be deduced from the main results as well as some series with Fibonacci and Lucas numbers as coefficients.
The paper concludes with a discussion of further possible generalizations of the present results to series with an arbitrary power
k^{p},p\geq 1,
in the denominator of
P(n,z),P(m,n,z)
and
Q(m,z)
, respectively.

2
A generalization of the zeta identity of Mező

First, we prove the following generalization of (
1.1
), for which we offer two proofs.

Theorem 1
.

Let
n
be a positive integer. For all
z
such that
0<|z|\leq 1
, we have the identity

\displaystyle P(n,z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{{(-1)^{k}\zeta(2k)z^{2k}}}{{k(2k+n)}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{n^{2}}-\frac{\pi z}{n+1}+\frac{\ln(2\pi z)}{n}+\frac{(n-1)!}{(2\pi z)^{n}}{\zeta(n+1)}-(n-1)!\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\Li_{j+1}(e^{-2\pi z})}{(n-j)!(2\pi z)^{j}},

(2.1)

where
\displaystyle\Li_{s}(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}}{k^{s}}
is the polylogarithm of order
s
evaluated at
z
.

First proof.
Let

S_{1}(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}\zeta(2k)}{k}z^{2k}.

(2.2)

For a variable
y
, we have

y^{n-1}S_{1}(zy)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{{(-1)^{k}\zeta(2k)}}{k}z^{2k}y^{2k+n-1}

and hence

P(n,z)=\int_{0}^{1}{y^{n-1}S_{1}(zy)dy}.

(2.3)

The evaluation of
S_{1}(z)
is a classical result and equals

\displaystyle S_{1}(z)

\displaystyle=-\ln\Big(\frac{\sinh(\pi z)}{\pi z}\Big)=\pi z+\ln\Big(\frac{2\pi z}{e^{2\pi z}-1}\Big)

\displaystyle=-\pi z+\ln(2\pi z)-\ln\left({1-e^{-2\pi z}}\right);

so that

S_{1}(zy)=-\pi zy+\ln(2\pi z)+\ln y-\ln\left({1-e^{-2\pi zy}}\right).

(2.4)

Thus, from (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) we have

\displaystyle P(n,z)

\displaystyle=-\pi z\int_{0}^{1}{y^{n}dy}+{\ln(2\pi z)}\int_{0}^{1}{y^{n-1}dy}+\int_{0}^{1}{y^{n-1}\ln y}\,dy

\displaystyle\quad\,-\int_{0}^{1}y^{n-1}\ln\!\big({1-e^{-2\pi yz}}\big)dy

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi z}{{n+1}}+\frac{{\ln(2\pi z)}}{n}-\frac{1}{n^{2}}-\int_{0}^{1}{y^{n-1}\ln\!\big({1-e^{-2\pi yz}}\big)dy}.

(2.5)

It now remains to evaluate the remaining integral in (
2
). Let

I(z)=-\int_{0}^{1}{y^{n-1}\ln\!\big({1-e^{-2\pi yz}}\big)dy}.

Using the identity

\ln\Big(1-\frac{1}{x}\Big)=-\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{mx^{m}},\qquad|x|>1,

we have

I(z)=\int_{0}^{1}y^{n-1}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-2\pi zym}}{m}dy=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m}\int_{0}^{1}{y^{n-1}e^{-2\pi zym}dy},

where the interchange of integration and summation is justified by uniform convergence.

Next, using the standard integral
[
8
, Entry 3.351]

\int_{0}^{1}x^{w}e^{-\mu x}dx=\frac{w!}{\mu^{w+1}}-e^{-\mu}\frac{w!}{\mu^{w+1}}\sum_{j=0}^{w}\frac{\mu^{j}}{j!},

(2.6)

we have

I^{\prime}(z)=\int_{0}^{1}{y^{n-1}e^{-2\pi zym}dy}=\frac{{(n-1)!}}{{(2\pi zm)^{n}}}-\frac{(n-1)!e^{-2\pi zm}}{(2\pi zm)^{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{(2\pi zm)^{j}}{j!}

and hence

I(z)=\frac{{(n-1)!}}{{(2\pi z)^{n}}}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}{\frac{1}{{m^{n+1}}}}-\frac{(n-1)!}{(2\pi z)^{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}{\frac{(2\pi z)^{j}}{j!}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}{\frac{e^{-2\pi mz}}{m^{n-j+1}}}}.

(2.7)

Plugging (
2.7
) into (
2
) gives the identity stated in the theorem.

Second proof
. We start with the obvious observation that

\frac{1}{k(2k+n)}=\frac{1}{n}\Big(\frac{1}{k}-\frac{2}{2k+n}\Big),

and hence
nP(n,z)=S_{1}(z)-2S_{2}(z)
,
where
S_{1}(z)
is defined and evaluated as in the first proof and
S_{2}(z)
equals

S_{2}(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}\zeta(2k)}{2k+n}z^{2k}.

We get

\displaystyle S_{2}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{2k+n}s^{-2k}z^{2k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\Big(\frac{z^{2}}{s^{2}}\Big)^{k}\int_{0}^{1}x^{2k+n-1}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}x^{n-1}\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Big(-\frac{z^{2}x^{2}}{s^{2}}\Big)^{k}-1\right)dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}x^{n+1}z^{2}\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{s^{2}+z^{2}x^{2}}.

In view of the known identity
[
8
, Entry 1.421]

\coth(\pi x)=\frac{1}{\pi x}+\frac{2x}{\pi}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{2}+x^{2}},

we get

\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{s^{2}+z^{2}x^{2}}=\frac{1}{2z^{2}x^{2}}\big(\pi zx\coth(\pi zx)-1\big),

and hence

\displaystyle S_{2}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2n}-\frac{\pi z}{2}\int_{0}^{1}x^{n}\coth(\pi zx)dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2n}-\frac{\pi z}{2}\int_{0}^{1}x^{n}\Big(1+\frac{2e^{-2\pi zx}}{1-e^{-2\pi zx}}\Big)dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2n}-\frac{\pi z}{2}\Big(\frac{1}{n+1}+2\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}x^{n}e^{-2\pi zmx}dx\Big).

Again we can use (
2.6
) to simplify. The result is

\displaystyle P(n,z)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi z}{n}+\frac{\ln(2\pi z)}{n}-\frac{\ln(1-e^{-2\pi z})}{n}-\frac{1}{n^{2}}+\frac{\pi z}{n(n+1)}

\displaystyle\quad\,+\frac{(n-1)!}{(2\pi z)^{n}}\Big(\zeta(n+1)-\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{(2\pi z)^{j}}{j!}\Li_{n+1-j}(e^{-2\pi z})\Big).

As
\Li_{1}(z)=-\ln(1-z)
the proof is completed.
\square

\displaystyle\Cl_{2}(n\pi)=0,\,\,\,n\in\mathbb{Z},\qquad\Cl_{2}\Big(\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)=G,\qquad\Cl_{2}\Big(\frac{3\pi}{2}\Big)=-\,G,

Theorem 8
.

For all
z
such that
0<|\ln z|\leq 2\pi
,

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{{(\ln z)^{2k+1}B_{2k}}}{k(2k+1)!}=\frac{{\pi^{2}}}{3}-\frac{1}{2}\ln^{2}z+2\ln z\,\big(1-\ln(-\ln z)\big)-2\Li_{2}(z),

where

Proof.

Evaluate
P(1,-\frac{\ln z}{2\pi})

Our analysis allows the evaluation of two other interesting series which are similar to the series considered by Orr

For positive integers
m
and

and its degenerated counterpart for

Q(m,z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}\zeta(2k)z^{2k}}{k(2k+m)^{2}},\qquad 0<z\leq 1,

Then, the following result holds true.

Theorem 20
.

For non-equal positive integers
m
,
n
and all
z
such that
0<|z|\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k-1}\zeta(2k)z^{2k}}{k(2k+m)(2k+n)}=\frac{m+n}{(mn)^{2}}+\frac{{\pi z}}{(m+1)(n+1)}-\frac{\ln(2\pi z)}{mn}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\,\,+\frac{1}{m-n}\left(\frac{(m-1)!}{(2\pi z)^{m}}\zeta(m+1)-\frac{(n-1)!}{(2\pi z)^{n}}\zeta(n+1)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\,\,-\frac{1}{m-n}\left((m-1)!\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\Li_{j+1}\big(e^{-2\pi z}\big)}{(m-j)!(2\pi z)^{j}}-(n-1)!\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\Li_{j+1}\big(e^{-2\pi z}\big)}{(n-j)!(2\pi z)^{j}}\right)

(4.1)

and, for all
0<z\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}\zeta(2k)z^{2k}}{k(2k+m)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{m^{3}}+\frac{\pi z}{(m+1)^{2}}-\frac{\ln(2\pi z)}{m^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(m-1)!}{(2\pi z)^{m}}\zeta(m+1)H_{m-1}+\frac{m!}{m^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\Li_{j+1}\left(e^{-2\pi z}\right)}{(m-j)!(2\pi z)^{j}}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{(m-1)!}{(2\pi z)^{m}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Ein(2\pi zk)+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\Gamma(j,2\pi zk)}{j!}}{k^{m+1}},

(4.2)

where
H_{n}=\sum\limits_{s=1}^{n}\frac{1}{s}
,
H_{0}=0
, are the harmonic numbers,

\Gamma(a,x)
is the incomplete gamma function

\Gamma(a,x)=\int\limits_{x}^{\infty}t^{a-1}\,e^{-t}\,dt,

and

\Ein(x)
being the exponential integral

\Ein(x)=\int\limits_{0}^{x}\frac{1-e^{-t}}{t}\,dt.

Proof.

From the partial fraction decomposition

\frac{1}{k(2k+m)(2k+n)}=\frac{2}{m(m-n)(2k+m)}-\frac{2}{n(m-n)(2k+n)}+\frac{1}{mnk}

we immediately see that we can write

P(m,n,z)=\frac{1}{mn}S_{1}(z)+\frac{2}{m-n}\left(\frac{1}{m}S_{2,m}(z)-\frac{1}{n}S_{2,n}(z)\right)\!,

where
S_{1}(z)
is defined in (
2.2
) and
S_{2,v}(z)
equals

S_{2,v}(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}\zeta(2k)z^{2k}}{2k+v},

which was also evaluated in Section 2. To complete the proof of (
4.1
) we simplify making use of the elementary identity

\frac{n(n+1)-m(m+1)}{(m+1)(n+1)(m-n)}+1=\frac{mn}{(m+1)(n+1)}.

For (
4.2
) we start with the partial fraction decomposition

\frac{1}{k(2k+m)^{2}}=\frac{1}{m^{2}k}-\frac{2}{m^{2}(2k+m)}-\frac{2}{m(2k+m)^{2}}.

This yields

Q(m,z)=\frac{1}{m^{2}}S_{1}(z)-\frac{2}{m^{2}}S_{2,m}(z)-\frac{2}{m}S_{3,m}(z)

(4.3)

with
S_{1}(z)
and
S_{2,m}(z)
as above and

S_{3,m}(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}\zeta(2k)z^{2k}}{(2k+m)^{2}}.

(4.4)

We have

\displaystyle S_{3,m}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}y^{m-1}S_{2,m}(zy)\,dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2m^{2}}-\frac{\pi z}{2(m+1)^{2}}-\frac{m!}{2^{m+1}(\pi z)^{m}}\zeta(m+1)\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dy}{y}

\displaystyle\quad+\pi z\sum_{j=0}^{m}\frac{m!}{j!}\frac{1}{(2\pi z)^{m+1-j}}\int_{0}^{1}y^{j-1}\Li_{m+1-j}(e^{-2\pi zy})\,dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2m^{2}}-\frac{\pi z}{2(m+1)^{2}}+\frac{m!}{2^{m+1}(\pi z)^{m}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\Li_{m+1}(e^{-2\pi zy})-\zeta(m+1)}{y}dy

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\pi z}{(2\pi z)^{m+1}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{m!}{j!}(2\pi z)^{j}\int_{0}^{1}y^{j-1}\Li_{m+1-j}(e^{-2\pi zy})\,dy.

Next,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{y}\left(\Li_{m+1}(e^{-2\pi zy})-\zeta(m+1)\right)dy

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{m+1}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{e^{-2\pi zyk}-1}{y}\,dy=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Ein(2\pi zk)}{k^{m+1}}.

Also,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}y^{j-1}\Li_{m+1-j}(e^{-2\pi zy})dy

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{m+1-j}}\int_{0}^{1}y^{j-1}e^{-2\pi zyk}dy

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{m+1-j}}\frac{(j-1)!-\Gamma(j,2\pi zk)}{(2\pi zk)^{j}},

as

\int_{0}^{1}x^{j-1}e^{-2\pi ax}\,dx=\frac{(j-1)!-\Gamma(j,2\pi a)}{(2\pi a)^{j}},\qquad\Re(a)>0.

The expression for
S_{3,m}(z)
becomes

\displaystyle S_{3,m}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2m^{2}}-\frac{\pi z}{2(m+1)^{2}}+\frac{m!}{2^{m+1}(\pi z)^{m}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\gamma-\Ein(2\pi zk)}{k^{m+1}}-\gamma\zeta(m+1)\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\pi z}{(2\pi z)^{m+1}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{m!}{j!}(2\pi z)^{j}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{m+1-j}}\frac{(j-1)!-\Gamma(j,2\pi zk)}{(2\pi zk)^{j}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2m^{2}}-\frac{\pi z}{2(m+1)^{2}}+\frac{m!}{2^{m+1}(\pi z)^{m}}\zeta(m+1)(H_{m}-\gamma)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{m!}{2^{m+1}(\pi z)^{m}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\gamma-\Ein(2\pi zk)}{k^{m+1}}-\frac{m!}{2^{m+1}(\pi z)^{m}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{j!}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(j,2\pi zk)}{k^{m+1}},

where
\gamma=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}(H_{n}-\ln n)
is the Euler–Mascheroni constant.

The expression for

follows from simplifications according to (

\displaystyle=-\frac{4}{9}+\frac{1}{3}\ln(2\pi)-\frac{{\zeta(3)}}{{4\pi^{2}}},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}{\frac{{\zeta(2k)}}{{k(k+1)(k+2)}}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{3}{8}+\frac{1}{2}\ln(2\pi)-\frac{{3\zeta(3)}}{{2\pi^{2}}},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}{\frac{{\zeta(2k)}}{{k(2k+3)(2k+5)}}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{8}{{225}}+\frac{1}{{15}}\ln(2\pi)-\frac{{\zeta(3)}}{{4\pi^{2}}}+\frac{{3\zeta(5)}}{{4\pi^{4}}}.

Corollary 22
.

If
m
is a positive even integer and
n
is a positive odd integer, then

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}{\frac{{\zeta(2k)}}{{k(2k+m)(2k+n)}}}=-\frac{{m+n}}{{(mn)^{2}}}+\frac{{\ln(2\pi)}}{{mn}}-\frac{i^{m}{(m-1)!}}{{m-n}}\frac{{\zeta(m+1)}}{{(2\pi)^{m}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\frac{1}{{m-n}}\left({(m-1)!\sum_{j=1}^{m/2}{\frac{{(-1)^{j}\zeta(2j+1)}}{{(m-2j)!(2\pi)^{2j}}}}-(n-1)!\sum_{j=1}^{(n-1)/2}{\frac{{(-1)^{j}\zeta(2j+1)}}{{(n-2j)!(2\pi)^{2j}}}}}\right)\!.

Example 10
.

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}{\frac{{\zeta(2k)}}{{k(k+2)(2k+3)}}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{7}{{72}}+\frac{\ln(2\pi)}{6}-\frac{{\zeta(3)}}{{2\pi^{2}}},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}{\frac{{\zeta(2k)}}{{k(k+1)(2k+5)}}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{7}{{50}}+\frac{\ln(2\pi)}{5}-\frac{{2\zeta(3)}}{{3\pi^{2}}}+\frac{{\zeta(5)}}{{\pi^{4}}}.

5
Concluding remarks

We conclude with the following observations. Another natural generalization of the function
P(n,z)
is one of the form

P(n,z,p)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}\zeta(2k)z^{2k}}{k^{p}(2k+n)},\qquad 0<|z|\leq 1,\,\,p\geq 1.

(5.1)

The recursion

\frac{1}{k^{p}(2k+n)}=\frac{1}{nk^{p}}-\frac{2}{n}\frac{1}{k^{p-1}(2k+n)}

can be solved by standard methods to get

\frac{1}{k^{p}(2k+n)}=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\frac{(-2)^{j}}{n^{j+1}k^{p-j}}+\Big(\frac{2}{n}\Big)^{p}\frac{(-1)^{p}}{2k+n}.

Then

P(n,z,p)=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\frac{(-2)^{j}}{n^{j+1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}\zeta(2k)z^{2k}}{k^{p-j}}+\left(-\frac{2}{n}\right)^{p}S_{2,n}(z).

(5.2)

Hence, to extend Theorem
1
to
P(n,z,p)
it suffices to find a closed form for

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}\zeta(2k)z^{2k}}{k^{p}}=\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\Li_{p}\left(-\left(\frac{z}{t}\right)^{2}\right),\quad p\geq 2,

(5.3)

as

\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\Li_{1}\left(-\left(\frac{z}{t}\right)^{2}\right)=-\ln\left(\frac{\sinh(\pi z)}{\pi z}\right)=S_{1}(z).

Similarly, from the partial fraction decomposition

\frac{1}{k(2k+m)(2k+n)}=\frac{1}{mnk}+\frac{2}{m(m-n)(2k+m)}-\frac{2}{n(m-n)(2k+n)},

we also get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k^{p}(2k+m)(2k+n)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{mnk^{p}}+\frac{2}{m(m-n)}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{p-2}\frac{(-2)^{j}}{m^{j+1}k^{p-1-j}}+\Big(\frac{2}{m}\Big)^{p-1}\frac{(-1)^{p-1}}{2k+m}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{2}{n(m-n)}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{p-2}\frac{(-2)^{j}}{n^{j+1}k^{p-1-j}}+\Big(\frac{2}{n}\Big)^{p-1}\frac{(-1)^{p-1}}{2k+n}\right).

This shows that a closed form evaluation of (
5.3
) would also allow us to evaluate the generalization of
P(m,n,z)
to

P(m,n,z,p)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}\zeta(2k)z^{2k}}{k^{p}(2k+m)(2k+n)},\qquad 0<|z|\leq 1,\,\,\,m\neq n,\,\,\,p\geq 1,

via

\displaystyle P(m,n,z,p)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{mn}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}\zeta(2k)z^{2k}}{k^{p}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2}{m(m-n)}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{p-2}\frac{(-2)^{j}}{m^{j+1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}\zeta(2k)z^{2k}}{k^{p-1-j}}+\left(-\frac{2}{m}\right)^{p-1}S_{2,m}(z)\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{2}{n(m-n)}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{p-2}\frac{(-2)^{j}}{n^{j+1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}\zeta(2k)z^{2k}}{k^{p-1-j}}+\left(-\frac{2}{n}\right)^{p-1}S_{2,n}(z)\right).

Finally, considering the generalization of
Q(m,z)
to
Q(m,z,p)
defined by

Q(m,z,p)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}\zeta(2k)z^{2k}}{k^{p}(2k+m)^{2}},\qquad 0<|z|\leq 1,\,\,p\geq 1,

we start with the partial fraction decomposition

\frac{1}{k(2k+m)^{2}}=\frac{1}{m^{2}k}-\frac{2}{m^{2}(2k+m)}-\frac{2}{m(2k+m)^{2}}.

The recursion is solved as

\frac{1}{k^{p}(2k+m)^{2}}=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\frac{(-2)^{j}}{m^{j+2}k^{p-j}}-2\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\frac{(-2)^{j}}{m^{j+2}k^{p-1-j}(2k+m)}+\left(\frac{2}{m}\right)^{p}\frac{(-1)^{p}}{(2k+m)^{2}}

giving the identity

\displaystyle Q(m,z,p)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\frac{(-2)^{j}}{m^{j+2}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}\zeta(2k)z^{2k}}{k^{p-j}}

\displaystyle\quad-2\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\frac{(-2)^{j}}{m^{j+2}}P(m,z,p-1-j)+\left(-\frac{2}{m}\right)^{p}S_{3,m}(z),

where
P(n,z,p)
is defined in (
5.1
) and
S_{3,m}(z)
is defined in (
4.4
).

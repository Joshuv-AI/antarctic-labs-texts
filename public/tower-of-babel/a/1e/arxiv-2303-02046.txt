Nodal sets of Dirichlet eigenfunctions in quasiconvex Lipschitz domains
Jiuyi Zhu, Jinping Zhuge

Abstract
. We introduce the class of quasiconvex Lipschitz domains, which covers both C 1 C^{1} and convex domains, to the study of boundary unique continuation for elliptic operators. In particular, we prove the upper bound of the size of nodal sets for Dirichlet eigenfunctions of general elliptic equations in bounded quasiconvex Lipschitz domains. Our result is new even for Laplace operator in convex domains.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
35A02, 35P05, 35J15.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation

In the study of boundary unique continuation for elliptic equations, there are two important questions. The first question originated from a conjecture of L. Bers. Consider a harmonic function
u\in C^{1}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}})
in the upper half space
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
. Does
u=\partial_{d}u=0
on a subset
U\subset\partial\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
with positive surface measure imply that
u
is identically zero in
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
? This conjecture is true in two dimensions by tools from complex analysis. However, for
d\geq 3
Bourgain-Wolff
[
4
]
constructed a striking counterexample in the class of
C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}})
(with
U
containing no open subsets). Given this failure,
a variant of the conjecture was asked by Lin
[
18
]
:

Let
u
be a bounded harmonic function in a Lipschitz domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Suppose that
u
vanishes on a relatively open set
U\subset\partial\Omega
and
\nabla u
vanishes on a subset of
U
with positive surface measure. Then
u=0
in
\Omega
.

The above full conjecture is still open for
d\geq 3
in general Lipschitz domains. But many efforts have been made on reducing the geometric condition of
\partial\Omega
and obtaining quantitative estimate of the singular sets on
\partial\Omega
. In
[
18
]
, Lin proved the conjecture for
C^{1,1}
domains by showing that the singular sets have Hausdorff dimension at most
d-2
. This result was extended to
C^{1,\alpha}
domains by Adolfsson-Escauriaza
[
1
]
, and to
C^{1}
-Dini domains by Kukavica-Nyström
[
16
]
. More recently, Tolsa in
[
24
]
proved the above conjecture for
C^{1}
domains (or Lipschitz domains with small constant).
More quantitative estimate of the
(d-2)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of the singular sets was obtained by Kenig-Zhao in
[
14
]
for
C^{1}
-Dini domains. They also showed that the
C^{1}
-Dini type condition is optimal for singular sets having finite
(d-2)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure
[
15
]
. Along another line, Adolfsson-Escauriaza-Kenig
[
2
]
confirmed the conjecture for arbitrary convex domains. Recently, Mccurdy
[
22
]
obtained more quantitative result in arbitrary convex domains.

The second question is the quantitative estimate of the nodal sets near the boundary with less regularity. This is particularly directed to Yau’s conjecture on manifolds, which states the following:

Let
(M,g)
be a
d
-dimensional smooth compact Riemannian manifold and
\varphi_{\lambda}
be the eigenfunction of
-\Delta_{g}
corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda>0
, i.e.,
-\Delta_{g}\varphi_{\lambda}=\lambda\varphi_{\lambda}
. Let
Z(\varphi_{\lambda})=\{x\in M|\varphi_{\lambda}(x)=0\}
be the nodal set of
\varphi_{\lambda}
. Then
c\sqrt{\lambda}\leq\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(Z(\varphi_{\lambda}))\leq C\sqrt{\lambda}
, where
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
denotes the
(d-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure.

In Yau’s conjecture, if
M
is a compact Riemannian manifold with boundary,
then both the regularity of the metric (or the coefficients of the elliptic equation) and the boundary will play essential roles. Donnelly-Fefferman first proved Yau’s conjecture for real analytic metric
[
6
]
and real analytic boundary
[
7
]
. See also results on the sharp upper bounds of nodal sets for various eigenfunctions on analytic domains by Lin and the first author
[
17
]
. For smooth manifolds without boundaries, Logunov remarkably proved the sharp lower bound
[
20
]
(thus the lower bound has been completely solved even with boundaries) and a polynomial upper bound
[
19
]
. The polynomial upper bound can be obtained for Laplace eigenfunctions with various boundary conditions in bounded smooth domains in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
as mentioned by the first author in
[
25
]
. Recently, Logunov-Malinnikava-Nadirashvili-Nazarov
[
21
]
obtained the sharp upper bound for Laplace eigenfunctions with Dirichlet boundary condition in
C^{1}
domains (or Lipschitz domains with small constant). Then Gallegos
[
9
]
extended the result to general second order elliptic operators with Lipschitz coefficients in
C^{1}
domains.

Comparing the above two types of problems, we see that the boundary uniqueness conjecture of Lin holds for both
C^{1}
and convex domains, while the quantitative estimates of nodal sets have only been established for

C^{1}
domains. Therefore, it is natural to ask if the quantitative estimate of nodal sets can be obtained in arbitrary convex domains.
The main contribution of this paper is to bring quasiconvex Lipschitz domains to the study of boundary unique continuation. The quasiconvex Lipschitz domain is a natural class of Lipschitz domains that contains both
C^{1}
and convex domains, first introduced by Jia-Li-Wang
[
10
]
for studying the global Carldrón-Zygmund esitmates. It has been used by the second author of this paper to study the weak maximum priniciple for biharmonic equations
[
26
]
. We emphasize that quasiconvex Lipschitz domains are not necessarily convex or
C^{1}
. In fact, we can construct a quasiconvex Lipschitz curve that is nowhere convex or
C^{1}
; see Appendix
A.1
. In this paper, we obtain quantitative estimates of the nodal sets in the new setting of quasiconvex Lipschitz domains for general second order elliptic equations, which particularly include
the previous results of the second question in
C^{1}
domains
[
21
,
9
]
as a special case. Moreover, our result is new (and sharp) even for Laplace operator in convex domains.

1.2.
Assumptions and main results

Consider the second order elliptic operator in the form of
\mathcal{L}=-\nabla\cdot A\nabla
. A function
u\in H^{1}(D)
will be called
A
-harmonic in
D
if
\mathcal{L}(u)=0
in
D
. Throughout this paper, we assume that the coefficient matrix
A
satisfies the following standard assmptions:

•

Ellipticity condition: there exists
\Lambda\geq 1
such that
\Lambda^{-1}I\leq A(x)\leq\Lambda I
for any
x\in\Omega
.

•

Symmetry:
A^{T}=A
.

•

Lipschitz continuity: there exists
\gamma\geq 0
such that
|A(x)-A(y)|\leq\gamma|x-y|
for every
x,y\in\Omega
.

If one would like to have the sharp upper bound estimate of nodal sets, then we need to assume additionally that the coefficients are real analytic; see Remark
6.2
for a detailed explanation.

Next, we introduce the assumption on the domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We say a domain
\Omega
is Lipschitz, if there exists
r_{0}>0
such that for every
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
, the boundary patch
\partial\Omega\cap B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})
, after a rigid transformation, can be expressed as a Lipschitz graph
x_{d}=\phi(x^{\prime})
such that
B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})\cap\Omega=B_{r}(x_{0})\cap\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{d}):x_{d}>\phi(x^{\prime})\}
. Moreover, the Lipschitz constants of these graphs are uniformly bounded by some constant
L
. It should be pointed out that the optimal Lipschitz constant
L
may depend on
r_{0}
. For example, for
C^{1}
domains, the Lipschitz constant can be arbitrarily small if we take
r_{0}\to 0
. However, for general Lipschitz domains (including convex domains), the Lipschitz constant may remain large independent of
r_{0}
.

In this paper, we will call
\omega(\rho):(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
a quasiconvexity modulus, which is a continuous nondecreasing function such that
\lim_{\rho\to 0}\omega(\rho)=0
.

Definition 1.1
.

A Lipschitz domain
\Omega
is called quasiconvex if it satisfies the following property: there exist a quasiconvexity modulus
\omega(\rho)
and
r_{0}>0
such that for each point
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
, one can translate and rotate the domain so that
x_{0}
is translated to
0
and the local graph of
\partial\Omega
,
x_{d}=\phi(x^{\prime})
, satisfies
\phi(0)=0
and

(1.1)

\phi(x^{\prime})\geq-|x^{\prime}|\omega(|x^{\prime}|),\quad\text{for all }x^{\prime}\text{ with }|x^{\prime}|<r_{0}.

An alternative equivalent definition of quasiconvex domains is as follows: for each
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
and
r<r_{0}
, there exists a unit vector
n
(coinciding with the outerward normal at
x_{0}
if exists) such that

\Omega\cap B_{r}(x_{0})\subset\{y:(y-x_{0})\cdot n\leq r\omega(r)\}.

It is shown in Lemma
2.3
that a quasiconvex Lipschitz domain is locally almost convex. In particular, if
\Omega
is convex, then
\omega(r)=0
. If
\Omega
is a
C^{1}
domain, then (
1.1
) should be strengthened to a two-sided condition

|x^{\prime}|\omega(|x^{\prime}|)\geq\phi(x^{\prime})\geq-|x^{\prime}|\omega(|x^{\prime}|),

which clearly implies the local flatness. On the other hand, if
\omega(\rho)\leq C\rho
, then the quasiconvex Lipschitz domains defined in Definition
1.1
satisfy the uniform exterior ball condition (which are called semiconvex domains in some literature, e.g.,
[
23
]
).

The following is the main result of this paper.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded quasiconvex Lipschitz domain and
A
satisfy the standard assumptions. Let
\varphi_{\lambda}
be a Dirichlet eigenfunction of
\mathcal{L}=-\nabla\cdot A\nabla
corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda
, i.e.,
\mathcal{L}(\varphi_{\lambda})=\lambda\varphi_{\lambda}
in
\Omega
and
\varphi_{\lambda}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
. Then there exists
\alpha\geq 1/2
depending only on
d
such that

(1.2)

\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(Z(\varphi_{\lambda}))\leq C\lambda^{\alpha},

where
C
depends only on
A
and
\Omega
. If in addition,
A
is real analytic, then (
1.2
) holds with the sharp exponent
\alpha=1/2
.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
relies on the interior results by Donnelly-Fefferman
[
6
]
for real analytic coefficients and Logunov
[
19
]
for Lipschitz coefficients. We follow the ideas developed recently in
[
21
]
that use the almost monotonicity of doubling index and a reduction to the estimate of nodal sets for
A
-harmonic functions with controlled doubling index. In order to do so, we prove the almost monotonicity of doubling index in quasiconvex domains. There is no obvious evidence showing that this property can be extended to more general domains.
The main novelties in our proof are: (1) reveal a geometric property of convex domains concerning the existence of (quantitative) almost flat spots, and apply this property to find large nonzero portions of
A
-harmonic functions near convex boundaries; (2) develop a general boundary perturbation argument in Lipschitz domains that allows us to pass from convex boundaries to quasiconvex boundaries.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In section 2, we introduce useful geometric properties of convex domains and quasiconvex domains. In section 3, we establish the almost monotonicity of doubling index in quasiconvex domains. In section 4, we prove the quantitative Cauchy uniqueness in arbitrary Lipschitz domains and a boundary layer lemma on the drop of large maximal doubling index. In section 5, we develop a boundary perturbation argument in finding nonzero portions of
A
-harmonic functions. In section 6, we estimate the nodal sets and prove the main theorem. Finally, some related results and useful lemmas are proved in Appendix
A
.

2.
Convex and quasiconvex domains

2.1.
Almost flat spots of convex functions

In this subsection, we show that the convex boundaries have (quantitative) almost flat spots at every scale. By a rigid transformation, it suffices to consider a general convex function
\phi
defined in
Q_{1}=(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})^{d}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with bounded Lipschitz character and
\phi(0)=0
. It has been shown in
[
8
]
that if
\phi
is a convex function, then
\nabla^{2}\phi
is a matrix-valued nonnegative Radon measure. In particular, for each
j
,
\partial_{j}^{2}\phi
is a scalar nonnegative Radon measure. This property is the essential reason for the existence of almost flat spots for convex functions. Nevertheless, the results in this subsection are proved in an elementary way without using measure theory. We will begin with smooth convex functions.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\phi
be a smooth convex function in
Q_{1}
with Lipschitz constant bounded by
L
. For any
r\in(0,1/2)
, there exists a point
y\in Q_{1/2}
such that for every
j
,

(2.1)

\int_{-r}^{r}\partial_{j}^{2}\phi(y+te_{j})dt\leq Cr,

where
C
depends only on
d
and
L
.

Proof.

In the proof, we will make sure that the constant
C
is independent of the smoothness of
\phi
. Since
\phi
is convex, we know that each
\partial_{j}^{2}\phi(x)
is a nonnegative scalar function on
Q_{1}
and

\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{Q_{1}}\partial_{j}^{2}\phi(x)dx\leq 2dL.

The last inequality follows from the fundamental theorem of calculus and the fact that
|\nabla\phi|\leq L
. Now consider the function

\psi(x)=\int_{-r}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{j}^{2}\phi(x+te_{j})dt.

By the Fubini theorem and the nonnegativity of
\partial_{j}^{2}\phi
,

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{1/2}}\psi(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{-r}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{Q_{1/2}}\partial_{j}^{2}\phi(x+te_{j})dxdt

\displaystyle\leq 2r\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{Q_{1}}\partial_{j}^{2}\phi(x)dx\leq 4dLr.

Due to the continuity of
\psi
, this implies that there exists some
y\in Q_{1/2}
such that

\psi(y)\leq Cr.

In view of the definition of
\psi
, this gives the desired result.
∎

Let
x_{d}=\phi(x^{\prime})
be a convex graph (function over
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
) in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For any
(x^{\prime}_{0},\phi(x_{0}^{\prime}))
on the graph, we say an affine function
x_{d}=P(x^{\prime})
is a support plane of
\phi
at
x^{\prime}_{0}
if
P(x^{\prime}_{0})=\phi(x^{\prime}_{0})
and
\phi(x^{\prime})\geq P(x^{\prime})
for all
x^{\prime}
. Note that if
\nabla\phi(x^{\prime}_{0})
exists, then the support plane is unique and
\nabla\phi(x^{\prime}_{0})=\nabla P
.

Lemma 2.2

(Existence of almost flat spots, quantitative version)
.

Let
x_{d}=\phi(x^{\prime})
be a convex function in
Q_{1}
with Lipschitz constant
L
. There exists
C=C(d,L)>0
such that for every
r\in(0,1/2)
, we can find a point
x_{0}^{\prime}\in\overline{Q_{1/2}}
and its support plane
P
such that

(2.2)

\sup_{|x^{\prime}-x^{\prime}_{0}|<r}|\phi(x^{\prime})-P(x^{\prime})|\leq Cr^{2}.

Proof.

Temporarily we assume that
\phi
is smooth with Lipschitz constant
L
.
First, by Lemma
2.1
we can find a point
x_{0}^{\prime}
such that (
2.1
) holds with
y=x_{0}^{\prime}
. Let
P
be the support plane of
\phi
at
x_{0}^{\prime}
. Note that
\nabla^{2}P=0
. This implies that

(2.3)

\sup_{t\in(-r,r)}|\partial_{j}\phi(x^{\prime}_{0}+te_{j})-\partial_{j}P|\leq Cr.

In fact, since
\phi
is smooth, then
\partial_{j}\phi(x^{\prime}_{0})=\partial_{j}P
.
Note that
\phi(x^{\prime}_{0}+se_{j})
is a 1-dimensional convex function in
s
and
x_{d}=P(x^{\prime}_{0}+se_{j})
is the support line at
s=0
. Observe that
\partial_{j}\phi(x^{\prime}_{0}+se_{j})
is nondecreasing in
s
. By the definition of support line (which could be self-explained), it is easy to see that for every
s>0
,
\partial_{j}\phi(x^{\prime}_{0}-se_{j})\leq\partial_{j}P\leq\partial_{j}\phi(x^{\prime}_{0}+se_{j})
. Now for any
t>0
with
-r<-t<t<r
, by Lemma
2.1
,

\partial_{j}\phi(x^{\prime}_{0}+te_{j})-\partial_{j}\phi(x^{\prime}_{0}-te_{j})=\int_{-t}^{t}\partial_{j}^{2}\phi(x^{\prime}_{0}+se_{j})ds\leq Cr.

As a result,

|\partial_{j}\phi(x^{\prime}_{0}\pm te_{j})-\partial_{j}P|\leq\partial_{j}\phi(x^{\prime}_{0}+te_{j})-\partial_{j}\phi(x^{\prime}_{0}-te_{j})\leq Cr.

This is exactly (
2.3
).

Integrating (
2.3
) in
t
again, we see that

(2.4)

\sup_{t\in(-r,r)}|\phi(x^{\prime}_{0}+te_{j})-P(x^{\prime}_{0}+te_{j})|\leq Cr^{2}.

By the convexity of
\phi-P
and the fact
\phi\geq P
, for any
t_{j}\in(-r,-r)
with
|t|_{1}:=\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}|t_{j}|<r
, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\phi(x^{\prime}_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}t_{j}e_{j})-P(x^{\prime}_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}t_{j}e_{j})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\frac{|t_{j}|}{|t|_{1}}\big(\phi(x_{0}^{\prime}+{\rm sgn}(t_{j})|t|_{1}e_{j})-P(x_{0}^{\prime}+{\rm sgn}(t_{j})|t|_{1}e_{j})\big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\frac{|t_{j}|}{|t|_{1}}Cr^{2}=Cr^{2},

where
{\rm sgn}(a)=a/|a|
is the sign function (with
{\rm sgn}(0)=0
) and we have used (
2.4
) in the third inequality.
This implies the desired result with possibly a smaller radius
cr
. But this can be fixed by adjusting the initial radius to
c^{-1}r
.

Finally, we need to pass from smooth convex functions to arbitrary convex functions. For any given convex function
\phi
with Lipschitz constant
L
, it is well-known that we can find a sequence of smooth convex functions
\{\phi_{k}:k\geq 1\}
, with the same Lipschitz constant, that converges uniformly to the given convex function
\phi
as
k\to\infty
.
Meanwhile, we can find a sequence of
x^{\prime}_{k}\in\overline{Q_{1/2}}
and their support planes
P_{k}
satisfying (
2.2
), namely, for each
k\geq 1
,

(2.5)

\sup_{|x^{\prime}-x_{k}^{\prime}|<r}|\phi_{k}(x^{\prime})-P_{k}(x^{\prime})|\leq Cr^{2},

where
C
depends only on
d
and
L
.
Due to the compactness of
\overline{Q_{1/2}}
and the set of support planes (since
|\nabla P_{k}|\leq L
), we can find subsequences of
x_{k}^{\prime}
and
P_{k}
such that
x_{k}^{\prime}\to x_{0}^{\prime}\in Q_{1/2}
and
P_{k}
converges uniformly to a plane
P_{0}
, which turns out to be a support plane of
\phi
at
x^{\prime}_{0}
. Hence, by taking limit in (
2.5
), we obtain (
2.2
) for a general convex function.
∎

2.2.
Quasiconvex domains

The following lemma shows that a quasiconvex Lipschitz domain can be well approximated locally by convex domains at all small scales.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a quasiconvex domain given by Definition
1.1
. Let
x\in\partial\Omega
and
r<r_{0}/2
. Let
V_{r}(x)
be the convex hull of
B_{r,+}(x)=B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega
. Then

{\rm dist}(\partial B_{r,+}(x),\partial V_{r}(x))\leq 2r\omega(2r).

Proof.

Let
y\in\partial B_{r,+}(x)
. If
y\in\partial B_{r}(x){\cap\Omega}
, then
y\in\partial V_{r}(x)
is trivial. Suppose
y\in\partial\Omega\cap B_{r}(x)
. Since
\Omega
is quasiconvex, by definition, we can find a vector
n
(coinciding with the outward normal if exists)
such that
B_{2r}(y)\cap\Omega
is entirely contained in the convex domain

\widetilde{V}_{2r}(y)=\{z:(y-z)\cdot n\ >-2r\omega(2r)\}\cap B_{2r}(y).

This implies
\text{dist}(y,\partial\widetilde{V}_{2r}(y))\leq 2r\omega(2r)
. Also, observe that
y\in\overline{V_{r}(x)}
and
V_{r}(x)\subset\widetilde{V}_{2r}(y)
. This is because
\widetilde{V}_{2r}(y)
is a convex set containing
B_{r,+}(x)
while the convex hull
V_{r}(x)
is the smallest convex set containing
B_{r,+}(x)
.
Hence,

\text{dist}(y,\partial V_{r}(x))\leq\text{dist}(y,\partial\widetilde{V}_{2r}(y))\leq 2r\omega(2r),

as desired.
∎

3.
Almost monotonicity of doubling index

In this section, we will show that for quasiconvex Lipschitz domains and variable coefficients, we have the almost monotonicity for the doubling index. We begin with two important facts.

Fact 1:
Affine transformations (a combination of linear transformations and translations) will not change the quasiconvexity (or convexity) of a domain (up to a constant on
\omega(r)
).

Fact 2:
A rescaling
x\to rx
for small
r<1
will make
\omega
and the Lipschitz constant of
A
small. This is because
\omega(r\cdot)\to 0
and
\lVert\nabla(A(r\cdot))\rVert_{L^{\infty}}=r\lVert\nabla A\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\to 0
as
r\to 0
. This fact is crucial for us as we will assume
\omega_{0}=\omega(1)
and
\gamma_{0}\simeq\lVert\nabla A\rVert_{L^{\infty}}
are sufficiently small later on. Note that for convex domains,
\omega_{0}=0
; for constant coefficients,
\gamma_{0}=0
.

3.1.
Frequency function and three-ball inequality

Let
\Omega
be a Lipschitz domain and
A
satisfy the standard assumptions. In this subsection, we further assume (up to an affine transformation) that
0\in\overline{\Omega}
and
A(0)=I
. Note that under this normalization, the constants for
\Omega
and
A
will change. In particular, the new quasiconvexity modulus
\tilde{\omega}(r)
will satisfy
\tilde{\omega}(r)\leq\Lambda\omega(\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}r)
which is harmless up to a constant. Similarly
\tilde{\gamma}\leq\Lambda\gamma
and
\tilde{L}\leq\Lambda L
.

Let
R>0
and
u
be an
A
-harmonic function in
B_{R,+}=B_{R}(0)\cap\Omega
and satisfy the Dirichlet boundary condition
u=0
on
B_{R}(0)\cap\partial\Omega
. Define

\mu(x)=\frac{x\cdot A(x)x}{|x|^{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad H(r)=\int_{\partial B_{r}(0)\cap\Omega}\mu u^{2}d\sigma.

Define

D(r)=\int_{B_{r,+}}A\nabla u\cdot\nabla u.

Define the frequency function of
u
centered at
0
as

(3.1)

\mathcal{N}_{u}(0,r)=\frac{rD(r)}{H(r)}.

The following monotonicity property is standard. For the reader’s convenience, we provide a proof in Appendix
A.2
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Under the above assumptions, if in addition,

(3.2)

n(x)\cdot A(x)x\geq 0,\quad\text{for almost every }x\in B_{R}(0)\cap\partial\Omega,

then there exists
C=C(\Lambda,d)>0
such that
e^{C\gamma r}\mathcal{N}_{u}(0,r)
is nondecreasing in
(0,R)
.

We say
B_{R,+}
is
A
-starshaped with respect to
0
if (
3.2
) holds. This additional geometric assumption on
\partial\Omega
is necessary in the proof of Proposition
3.1
and will be removed later.

The relationship between
H(r)
and the frequency function can be seen from

(3.3)

|\frac{H^{\prime}(r)}{H(r)}-{\frac{d-1}{r}}-\frac{2}{r}\mathcal{N}_{u}(0,r)|\leq C\gamma.

The proof of this estimate is contained in the proof of Proposition
3.1
; see (
A.7
).
Integrating (
3.3
) over
r\in(r_{1},r_{2})
, we have

(3.4)

\Big|\log\frac{H(r_{2})}{H(r_{1})}-(d-1)\log\frac{r_{2}}{r_{1}}-2\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\frac{\mathcal{N}_{u}(0,r)}{r}dr\Big|\leq C\gamma r_{2}.

Using Proposition
3.1
,
\mathcal{N}_{u}(0,r)\leq e^{C\gamma r_{2}}\mathcal{N}_{u}(0,r_{2})\leq e^{C\gamma r_{3}}\mathcal{N}_{u}(0,s)
for any
r_{1}<r<r_{2}<s<r_{3}
. Thus, for any
r_{1}<r_{2}<r_{3}<r_{0}
, it follows from (
3.4
) that

\log\frac{H(r_{2})}{H(r_{1})}\leq\beta\log\frac{H(r_{3})}{H(r_{2})}+(d-1)\log\frac{r_{2}^{1+\beta}}{r_{1}r_{3}^{\beta}}+C\gamma r_{3},

where
\beta=e^{C\gamma r_{3}}\frac{\log(r_{2}/r_{1})}{\log(r_{3}/r_{2})}
. Then

H(r_{2})\leq\exp\Big((d-1)\log\frac{r_{2}^{1+\beta}}{r_{1}r_{3}^{\beta}}+C\gamma r_{3}\Big)H(r_{3})^{\frac{\beta}{1+\beta}}H(r_{1})^{\frac{1}{1+\beta}}.

This is a three-sphere inequality. We would like to convert this into a three-ball inequality. The following argument, more or less, should be standard.
Replacing in the above inequality
r_{j}
with
tr_{j}
and
t\in(0,1)
, and then integrating in
t
on (0,1), we arrive at

(3.5)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}H(tr_{2})dt

\displaystyle\leq\exp\Big((d-1)\log\frac{r_{2}^{1+\beta}}{r_{1}r_{3}^{\beta}}+C\gamma r_{3}\Big)\int_{0}^{1}H(tr_{3})^{\frac{\beta}{1+\beta}}H(tr_{1})^{\frac{1}{1+\beta}}dt

\displaystyle\leq\exp\Big((d-1)\log\frac{r_{2}^{1+\beta}}{r_{1}r_{3}^{\beta}}+C\gamma r_{3}\Big)\Big(\int_{0}^{1}H(tr_{3})dt\Big)^{\frac{\beta}{1+\beta}}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}H(tr_{1})dt\Big)^{\frac{1}{1+\beta}},

where we also used the Hölder inequality. Note that

\int_{B_{r,+}}\mu u^{2}=\int_{0}^{r}H(s)ds=r\int_{0}^{1}H(tr)dt.

It follows from (
3.5
) that

(3.6)

\log\frac{\int_{B_{r_{2},+}}\mu u^{2}}{\int_{B_{r_{1},+}}\mu u^{2}}\leq\beta\log\frac{\int_{B_{r_{3},+}}\mu u^{2}}{\int_{B_{r_{2},+}}\mu u^{2}}+d\log\frac{r_{2}^{1+\beta}}{r_{3}^{\beta}r_{1}}+C\gamma r_{3}.

The last two terms on the right side will be small if
\gamma
or
r_{0}
is sufficiently small and
r_{2}/r_{1}
is sufficiently close to
r_{3}/r_{2}
.

3.2.
Non-identity matrix

This subsection is devoted to removing the assumption
A(0)=I
in order to define the doubling index at any point. Suppose now
A(0)\neq I
. Since
A(0)
is symmetric and positive definite, we can write
A(0)=\mathcal{O}D\mathcal{O}^{T}
, where
\mathcal{O}
is an (constant) orthogonal matrix and
D
is a (constant) diagonal matrix with entries contained in
[\Lambda^{-1},\Lambda]
. Let
S=\mathcal{O}{D^{\frac{1}{2}}}\mathcal{O}^{T}
. Thus
A(0)=S^{2}
(or formally
S=A^{\frac{1}{2}}(0)
) and
S
is also symmetric. Let
u
be
A
-harmonic in
B_{R}(0)\cap\Omega
. Then we can normalize the matrix
A
such that
A(0)=I
by a change of variable
x\to Sx
. Precisely, let

\tilde{u}(x)=u(Sx)\quad\text{ and }\quad\tilde{A}(x)=S^{-1}A(Sx)S^{-1}.

Then
\tilde{u}
is
\tilde{A}
-harmonic in
S^{-1}(B_{R}\cap\Omega)=S^{-1}(B_{R})\cap S^{-1}(\Omega)
. Obviously now
\tilde{A}(0)=I
.
Note that the
A
-starshape condition
\tilde{n}(x)\cdot\tilde{A}(x)x\geq 0
on
S^{-1}(B_{R}\cap\partial\Omega)
is equivalent to

(3.7)

n(x)\cdot A(x)A^{-1}(0)x\geq 0,\quad\text{on }B_{R}\cap\partial\Omega.

This condition is consistent with (
3.2
) if
A(0)=I
and invariant under linear transformations.

Let
\widetilde{\Omega}=S^{-1}(\Omega)
. We would like to find the right form of the doubling index under a change of variable. By definition of the weight
\tilde{\mu}
corresponding to
\tilde{A}
and the change of variable
y=Sx
,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(0)\cap\widetilde{\Omega}}\tilde{\mu}\tilde{u}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{r}(0)\cap\widetilde{\Omega}}\frac{x\cdot S^{-1}A(Sx)S^{-1}x}{|x|^{2}}(u(Sx))^{2}dx

\displaystyle=|\det A(0)|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{A^{\frac{1}{2}}(0)(B_{r}(0))\cap{\Omega}}\frac{y\cdot A(0)^{-1}A(y)A(0)^{-1}y}{y\cdot A(0)^{-1}y}u(y)^{2}dy.

Therefore, taking translations into consideration, we define

\mu(x_{0},y)=\frac{(y-x_{0})\cdot A(x_{0})^{-1}A(y)A(x_{0})^{-1}(y-x_{0})}{(y-x_{0})\cdot A(x_{0})^{-1}(y-x_{0})},

(3.8)

E(x_{0},r)=x_{0}+A^{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{0})(B_{r}(0)),

and

(3.9)

J_{u}(x_{0},r)=|\det A(x_{0})|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{E(x_{0},r)\cap{\Omega}}\mu(x_{0},y)u(y)^{2}dy.

Note that
\Lambda^{d}\geq\det A(x_{0})\geq\Lambda^{-d}
for any
x_{0}
and
J_{u}(x_{0},r)
is nondecreasing in
r
. We point out that
J_{u}(x_{0},r)
, invariant under affine transformations, is the right form of the weighted
L^{2}
norm for defining doubling index.

In the next lemma, we show the continuous dependence of
J_{u}(x_{0},r)
on both
x_{0}
and
r
. This will be useful when we shift the center of a doubling index from one point to a nearby point.

Lemma 3.2
.

There exists
C=C(\Lambda,d)>0
such that if
\theta=|x_{0}-x_{1}|<r/C
, then

(3.10)

(1-C\gamma\theta)J_{u}(x_{1},r-C\theta)\leq J_{u}(x_{0},r)\leq(1+C\gamma\theta)J_{u}(x_{1},r+C\theta).

Proof.

The proof follows from the Lipschitz continuity of
\mu(x_{0},y)
and
A^{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{0})
in
x_{0}
. Note that

\mu(x_{0},y)=1+\frac{(y-x_{0})\cdot A(x_{0})^{-1}(A(y)-A(x_{0}))A(x_{0})^{-1}(y-x_{0})}{(y-x_{0})\cdot A(x_{0})^{-1}(y-x_{0})}.

Then fixing
y
, one can show that
|\nabla_{x_{0}}\mu(x_{0},y)|\leq C\gamma.
Thus,

|\mu(x_{0},y)-\mu(x_{1},y)|\leq C\gamma\theta\mu(x_{1},y),

which yields

(3.11)

\mu(x_{0},y)\leq(1+C\gamma\theta)\mu(x_{1},y),

provided that
\theta
is suffciently small so that
C\gamma\theta<1/2
.

On the other hand, by the power series, the unique square root of
A
can be written as

(3.12)

A^{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{0})=\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{1/2}{n}(-1)^{n}\big(I-\Lambda^{-1}A(x_{0})\big)^{n},

where
\binom{1/2}{n}
are the binomial coefficients in the Taylor series
(1+X)^{1/2}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{1/2}{n}X^{n}
.
The power series (
3.12
) converges, in view of the Gelfand formula, since the all the eigenvalues of
I-\Lambda^{-1}A(x_{0})
are contained in
[0,1-\Lambda^{-2}]
. By taking derivatives in
x_{0}
, we see that
|\nabla A^{\frac{1}{2}}|\leq C|\nabla A|\leq C\gamma
, where
C
depends only on
d
and
\Lambda
. By choosing
\gamma
to be small enough if necessary, we have

(3.13)

E(x_{0},r)\subset E(x_{1},r(1+C_{0}\gamma\theta)+{C_{1}}\theta)\subset E(x_{1},r+C\theta).

Moreover,

|\det A(x_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}-\det A(x_{1})^{\frac{1}{2}}|\leq C\gamma\theta\leq C\gamma\theta\det A(x_{1})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

This implies

(3.14)

\det A(x_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq(1+C\gamma\theta)\det A(x_{1})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

It follows from (
3.11
), (
3.13
) and (
3.14
) that

\displaystyle J_{u}(x_{0},r)

\displaystyle=|\det A(x_{0})|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{E(x_{0},r)\cap{\Omega}}\mu(x_{0},y)u(y)^{2}dy

\displaystyle\leq(1+C\gamma\theta)^{2}|\det A(x_{1})|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{E(x_{1},r+C\theta)\cap{\Omega}}\mu(x_{1},y)u(y)^{2}dy

\displaystyle\leq(1+C\gamma\theta)J_{u}(x_{1},r+C\theta).

This proves one side of (
3.10
) and the other side follows from symmetry of the inequality.
∎

3.3.
Remove
A
-starshape condition

In the proof of the monotonicity of frequency function, we require the
A
-starshape condition (
3.7
) on the boundary, instead of quasiconvexity. To remove this geometric condition, the classical way is to introduce a nonlinear change of variables, see
[
1
]
or
[
14
]
for example. In this paper, however, we will use a different approach in which the quasiconvexity of the domain will play an essential role. The key idea of our approach is that if
\Omega\cap B_{r}
is close to be convex, we can recover the
A
-starshape condition if we shift the center from the boundary to an interior point, which is still relatively close to the boundary. This allows us to prove an almost monotonicity formula for the frequency function with small errors.

By a rigid transformation, we may assume
0\in\partial\Omega
and the local graph of
B_{r}(0)\cap\partial\Omega
is given by
x_{d}=\phi(x^{\prime})
such that
B_{r}(0)\cap\Omega=B_{r}(0)\cap\{x_{d}>\phi(x^{\prime})\}
, whose support plane at
0
is
x_{d}=0
.

Lemma 3.3
.

There exists
C=C(L,\Lambda)>0
such that if
r_{0}
satisfies
C({\omega}(2r_{0})+{\gamma}r_{0})\leq 1
, then
for every
r\in(0,r_{0})
,
\Omega\cap B_{r}(x_{1})
is
A
-starshaped with respect to its center
x_{1}=C(r{\omega}(2r)+{\gamma}r^{2})e_{d}
, namely,

(3.15)

n(x)\cdot A(x)A^{-1}(x_{1})(x-x_{1})\geq 0,\quad\text{for almost every }x\in\partial\Omega\cap B_{r}(x_{1}).

Proof.

Consider a point
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega\cap B_{2r}(0)
such that the normal
n=n(x_{0})
exists. By the definition of the quasiconvexity, the support plane at
x_{0}
, can be written as
x_{d}=P(x^{\prime})
whose downward normal direction is
n
. The length of the projection of vector
x_{0}
onto the support plane is
t=|(I-n\otimes n)x_{0}|\leq|x_{0}|
. By the definition of quasiconvexity centered at
x_{0}
(up to a rotation), we have

(3.16)

(x_{0}-0)\cdot n\geq-t{\omega}(t).

Now, we would like to find
k=k(r)>0
(independent of
x_{0}
) such that
x_{1}=ke_{d}
and

(3.17)

n\cdot A(x_{0})A^{-1}(x_{1})(x_{0}-x_{1})\geq 0.

In fact, using (
3.16
),
A(x_{1})A^{-1}(x_{1})=I
and
|A(x_{0})-A(x_{1})|\leq{\gamma}|x_{0}-x_{1}|
, we have

(3.18)

\displaystyle n\cdot A(x_{0})A^{-1}(x_{1})(x_{0}-x_{1})

\displaystyle\geq n\cdot(x_{0}-x_{1})-{\gamma}\Lambda|x_{0}-x_{1}|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq-t{\omega}(t)-kn_{d}-{\gamma}\Lambda(|x_{0}|+k)^{2}

Since
t\leq|x_{0}|<2r
and
n_{d}=\frac{-1}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla P|^{2}}}\leq\frac{-1}{\sqrt{1+{L}^{2}}}\leq\frac{-1}{1+{L}}
, where
{L}\geq|\nabla P|
is the Lipschitz constant of
\partial\Omega\cap B_{r_{0}}(0)
, we obtain

(3.19)

n\cdot A(x_{0})A^{-1}(x_{1})(x_{0}-x_{1})\geq\frac{k}{1+L}-2r\omega(2r)-\gamma\Lambda(2r+k)^{2}.

We would like
B_{r}(x_{1})
to contain a part of the boundary including the origin, which means
k\leq r
.
Note that the right-hand side of (
3.18
) is independent of
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega\cap B_{2r}(0)
. Now, choosing

k={9}\Lambda(1+{L})(r{\omega}(2r)+{\gamma}r^{2}),

we obtain the desired result (
3.17
) from (
3.19
) for almost every point on
B_{2r}(0)\cap\partial\Omega
. Therefore we pick
C=9\Lambda(1+{L})
and
x_{1}=C(r{\omega}(2r)+{\gamma}r^{2})e_{d}
in the statement of the lemma. Finally it suffices to make sure that
k\leq r
and
B_{r}(x_{1})\cap\partial\Omega\subset B_{2r}(0)\cap\partial\Omega
. Clearly this is satisfied if
r_{0}
is small enough such that
C({\omega}(2r_{0})+{\gamma}r_{0})\leq 1
.
∎

Given
r>0
and
x_{1}
as defined in Lemma
3.3
and by (
3.6
), if
r_{1}<r_{2}<r_{3}<r<r_{0}
,

(3.20)

\log\frac{J_{u}(x_{1},r_{2})}{J_{u}(x_{1},r_{1})}\leq\beta\log\frac{J_{u}(x_{1},r_{3})}{J_{u}(x_{1},r_{2})}+d\log\frac{r_{2}^{1+\beta}}{r_{3}^{\beta}r_{1}}+C\gamma r_{3}.

Now, we would like to obtain a monotonicity formula of the doubling index centered at
0
.
By Lemma
3.2
, for any
s<r/2
and
s>\theta(r):=C(r{\omega}(2r)+\gamma r^{2})
,

(3.21)

(1-\theta(r))J_{u}(0,s-\theta(r))\leq J_{u}(x_{1},s)\leq(1+\theta(r))J_{u}(0,s+\theta(r)).

Combining (
3.20
) and (
3.21
), we obtain

\displaystyle\log\frac{J_{u}(0,r_{2}-\theta)}{J_{u}(0,r_{1}+\theta)}

\displaystyle\leq\beta\log\frac{J_{u}(0,r_{3}+\theta)}{J_{u}(0,r_{2}-\theta)}

\displaystyle+(1+\beta)\log\frac{1+\theta}{1-\theta}+d\log\frac{r_{2}^{1+\beta}}{r_{3}^{\beta}r_{1}}+C\gamma r_{3}.

Without loss of generality, assume that
\theta=\theta(r)<\frac{r}{20}
. Let

r_{1}=\frac{1}{8}r-\theta,\quad r_{2}=\frac{1}{4}r+\theta,\quad r_{3}=\frac{1}{2}r-\theta.

It follows that
|\beta-1|\leq C(\gamma r+\theta/r)\leq C(r+{\omega}(2r))
and

(1+\beta)\log\frac{1+\theta}{1-\theta}+d\log\frac{r_{2}^{1+\beta}}{r_{3}^{\beta}r_{1}}+C\gamma r_{3}\leq C(r+{\omega}(2r)).

Consequently, we obtain

\log\frac{J_{u}(0,\frac{r}{4})}{J_{u}(0,\frac{r}{8})}\leq(1+C(\gamma r+{\omega}(2r)))\log\frac{J_{u}(0,\frac{r}{2})}{J_{u}(0,\frac{r}{4})}+C(\gamma r+{\omega}(2r)).

Define the doubling index by

N_{u}(x_{0},r)=\log\frac{J_{u}(x_{0},2r)}{J_{u}(x_{0},r)}.

Then we have

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}
. Then there exist
C=C(\Lambda,L)>0
and
r_{0}=r_{0}(\Lambda,L,\gamma,\omega)>0
(as in Lemma
3.3
) such that for
r<r_{0}

(3.22)

N_{u}(x_{0},r)\leq(1+C(\gamma r+\omega(16r)))N_{u}(x_{0},2r)+C(\gamma r+\omega(16r)).

Remark 3.5
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, if
r
is small enough (depending only on
\Lambda,\gamma,L
and
\omega
), then

N_{u}(x_{0},r)\leq(1+\varepsilon)N_{u}(x_{0},2r)+\varepsilon.

Or equivalently,

N_{u}(x_{0},r)+1\leq(1+\varepsilon)(N_{u}(x_{0},2r)+1).

This is a useful version of almost monotonicity for the doubling index.

Remark 3.6
.

The argument in this section actually gives stronger estimates if provided stronger assumptions on the domain or the coefficients. For example, if
\omega(r)
satisfies a Dini-type condition, then one can show

\sup_{0<s<r}N_{u}(x_{0},s)\leq C(N_{u}(x_{0},r)+1).

If
\Omega
is convex (i.e.,
\omega(r)=0
) or
C^{1,1}
(i.e.,
\omega(r)\leq Cr
), then one can show

\sup_{0<s<r}N_{u}(x_{0},s)\leq(1+C\gamma r)N_{u}(x_{0},r)+C\gamma r.

If
A
is constant (i.e.,
\gamma=0
) and
\Omega
is convex, then the above inequality recover the precise doubling inequality for harmonic functions over convex domains, i.e.,
N(x_{0},r)
is nondecreasing in
r
.

4.
Drop of maximal doubling index

The main result of this section is Lemma
4.2
.

4.1.
Quantitative Cauchy uniqueness

We first introduce a quantitative Cauchy uniqueness over Lipschitz domains. A different version of Cauchy uniqueness over Lipschitz boundary was proved in
[
3
]
, which unfortunately is not suitable for our purpose as we do not have the estimate for tangential derivatives on the boundary and
our estimate is
L^{\infty}
-based.

We briefly recall the regularity theory of elliptic equation over Lipschitz domain; see
[
11
,
13
]
and references therein. Assume that
A
is Hölder continuous and let
u
be an
A
-harmonic function in
\Omega\cap B_{2}
and
u|_{\partial\Omega\cap B_{2}}\in H^{1}(\partial\Omega\cap B_{2})
in the sense of trace. Then the nontangential maximal function
(\nabla u)^{*}|_{\partial\Omega\cap B_{1}}\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega\cap B_{1})
. This particularly implies that for almost every
x\in\partial\Omega\cap B_{1}
,
\nabla u(y)\to\nabla u(x)
as
\Omega\ni y\to x
nontangentially. Therefore,
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=n\cdot A\nabla u
exists almost everywhere. This fact will justify the following lemma and its proof.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a Lipschitz domain and
0\in\partial\Omega
. There exists
0<\tau=\tau(d,L,A)<1
such that if
u
is an
A
-harmonic function in
B_{1,+}
satisfying
\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}(B_{1,+})}=1
and
|u|+|n\cdot A\nabla u|\leq\varepsilon\leq 1
on
B_{1}\cap\partial\Omega
, then

\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B_{1/2,+})}\leq C\varepsilon^{\tau},

where
C
depends only on
d,L
and
A
.

Proof.

The proof is inspired by
[
21
]
. We first extend the Lipschitz coefficient matrix
A
from
B_{1,+}
to
B_{1}
, still denoted by
A
, such that it still satisfies the same assumptions as in
B_{1,+}
. Since
B_{1}
is a smooth domain, we may construct the Green function in
B_{1}
such that
\mathcal{L}(G(x,\cdot))=\delta_{x}
and
G(x,\cdot)=0
on
\partial B_{1}
. It is known (see, e.g.,
[
12
]
) that
G(x,y)
satisfies (for
d\geq 3
)

(4.1)

|G(x,y)|\leq\frac{C}{|x-y|^{d-2}},

and

(4.2)

|\nabla_{y}G(x,y)|\leq\min\bigg\{\frac{C}{|x-y|^{d-1}},\frac{C\text{dist}(x,\partial B_{1})}{|x-y|^{d}}\bigg\}.

For
d=2
, (
4.1
) should be replaced by
|G(x,y)|\leq C(1+\ln|x-y|)
.

Let
\Gamma_{1}=\partial\Omega\cap B_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}=\partial B_{1}\cap\Omega
. Now for the
A
-harmonic function
u
in
B_{1,+}
, by the Green formula, for
x\in B_{1,+}

\displaystyle u(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial B_{1,+}}\bigg\{u\frac{\partial G}{\partial\nu}(x,\cdot)-G(x,\cdot)\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\bigg\}d\sigma

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma_{1}}\bigg\{u\frac{\partial G}{\partial\nu}(x,\cdot)-G(x,\cdot)\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\bigg\}d\sigma+\int_{\Gamma_{2}}\bigg\{u\frac{\partial G}{\partial\nu}(x,\cdot)-G(x,\cdot)\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\bigg\}d\sigma

\displaystyle=:u_{1}(x)+u_{2}(x).

Note that the above calculation also makes sense for
x\in B_{1,-}=B_{1}\setminus\overline{B_{1,+}}
and
u(x)=0
for
x\in B_{1,-}
.
Moreover,
u_{1}
is
A
-harmonic in
B_{1}\setminus\Gamma_{1}
with a jump across
\Gamma_{1}
(this is a well-known fact in layer potential theory), while
u_{2}
is
A
-harmonic in the entire
B_{1}
.

We now estimate
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
separately. For
u_{1}(x)
with
x\in B_{1}\setminus\Gamma_{1}
, by the assumption

|u_{1}(x)|\leq\varepsilon\int_{\Gamma_{1}}|G(x,y)|+|\frac{\partial G}{\partial\nu}(x,y)|d\sigma_{y}.

It follows from (
4.1
) and the first part of (
4.2
) that

\int_{\Gamma_{1}}|G(x,y)|d\sigma_{y}\leq C,\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{\Gamma_{1}}|\nabla_{y}G(x,y)|d\sigma_{y}\leq C(1+|\ln\delta(x)|),

where
\delta(x)=\text{dist}(x,\Gamma_{1})
. Consequently, we obtain

|u_{1}(x)|\leq C{\varepsilon}(1+|\ln\delta(x)|),\quad\text{for any }x\in B_{1}\setminus\Gamma_{1},

which leads to

(4.3)

\lVert u_{1}\rVert_{L^{2}(B_{1,\pm})}\leq C\varepsilon,

where
B_{1,-}=B_{1}\setminus\overline{B_{1,+}}
.

For
u_{2}
, since
u=u_{1}+u_{2}=0
in
B_{1,-}
, we have

\lVert u_{2}\rVert_{L^{2}(B_{1,-})}\leq C\varepsilon,\quad\text{and}\quad\lVert u_{2}\rVert_{L^{2}(B_{1,+})}\leq\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}(B_{1,+})}+C\varepsilon.

Recall that
u_{2}
is
A
-harmonic in
B_{1}
. By the three-ball inequality in
B_{1}
, there exists
\tau
such that

\displaystyle\lVert u_{2}\rVert_{L^{2}(B_{3/4})}

\displaystyle\leq C\lVert u_{2}\rVert_{L^{2}(B_{1/2,-})}^{\tau}\lVert u_{2}\rVert_{L^{2}(B_{1})}^{1-\tau}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{\tau}(\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}(B_{1,+})}^{1-\tau}+\varepsilon^{1-\tau})

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon+C\varepsilon^{\tau}\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}(B_{1,+})}^{1-\tau}.

Combined with (
4.3
) and
\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}(B_{1,+})}=1
, we obtain

\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}(B_{3/4,+})}\leq C\varepsilon^{\tau}.

Finally, the De Giorgi-Nash estimate implies the desired estimate.
∎

4.2.
Domain decomposition

Let
L\geq 0
be the Lipschitz constant associated to the Lipschitz domain
\Omega
. Define a standard rectangular cuboid by
Q_{0}=[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})^{d-1}\times\{\sqrt{d-1}(1+L)[-1,1)\}
. Throughout this paper, all the cuboids considered are translated and rescaled versions of
Q_{0}
. Let
\pi(Q)
be the vertical projection of
Q
on
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
. For example,
\pi(Q_{0})=[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})^{d-1}
. Denote the side length of
Q_{0}
and
\pi(Q_{0})
by
s(Q_{0})=s(\pi(Q_{0}))=1
.

Let
0\in\partial\Omega
and
Q
be a cuboid centered at
0
. Assume that
\partial\Omega\cap Q
is given by the local graph
x_{d}=\phi(x^{\prime})
with
x^{\prime}\in Q^{\prime}=\pi(Q)
. By our assumption,
|\nabla\phi|\leq L
in
Q^{\prime}
. Let
k\geq 3
. We partition
Q^{\prime}
into
2^{k(d-1)}
small equal cubes
\{q^{\prime}\}
with side length
s(q^{\prime})=2^{-k}s(Q^{\prime})
. For each small cube
q^{\prime}
as above, we further cover
\pi^{-1}(q^{\prime})\cap Q
by at most
2^{k}+1
disjoint cuboids
\{q\}
similar to
Q_{0}
such that
s(q)=s(q^{\prime})
and one of these cuboids centers on
\partial\Omega\cap Q
. Let
\mathcal{B}_{k}(Q)
be the collection of boundary cuboids
q
that centered on
\partial\Omega\cap Q
and
\mathcal{I}_{k}(Q)
be the interior cuboids that intersect with
\Omega\cap Q
. Clearly, there are exactly
2^{k(d-1)}
boundary cuboids in
\mathcal{B}_{k}(Q)
. See Figure
1
for a demonstration.

Figure 1.
Standard domain decomposition

There is a simple fact about the above decomposition:

\min_{q\in\mathcal{I}_{k}(Q)}\text{dist}(q,Q\cap\partial\Omega)\geq\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{d-1}\cdot s(q).

This implies that a thin boundary layer of
Q\cap\partial\Omega
with width
\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{d-1}\cdot s(q)
is entirely contained in
\cup_{q\in\mathcal{B}_{k}(Q)}q
.

4.3.
A boundary layer lemma

Let
Q
be a cuboid centered at a point in
\overline{\Omega}
. Denote by
\ell(Q)
the diameter of
Q
(i.e., length of diagonal). Note that if
x_{Q}
is the center of
Q
, then
2Q\subset B_{\ell(Q)}(x_{Q})
. As in
[
21
]
, we define the maximal doubling index of
u
in
Q
by

N_{u}^{*}(Q)=\sup_{x\in Q\cap\overline{\Omega},\ell(Q)/2\leq r\leq\ell(Q)}N_{u}(x,r).

The following is the key lemma of this section which shows that the maximal doubling index drops when going down to some smaller boundary cuboids.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a Lipschitz domain whose local boundary near
0\in\partial\Omega
is given by
x_{d}=\phi(x^{\prime})
as in Section
4.2
and
|\nabla\phi|\leq L
. There exist
k_{0}\geq 5
and
N_{0}\geq 1
such that for any integer
k\geq k_{0}
, there exist
\gamma_{0}>0
and
\omega_{0}>0
(both only depending on
k,d
and
L
) such that the following statement holds. Suppose
\frac{1}{2}I\leq A\leq 2I,|\nabla A|\leq\gamma_{0}
,
\omega(1)\leq\omega_{0}
and
u
is an
A
-harmonic function in
B_{1,+}
with
u=0
on
B_{1}\cap\partial\Omega
. Let
Q\subset B_{1/64}
be a boundary cuboid centered on
\partial\Omega
. If
N_{u}^{*}(Q)>N_{0}
, then there exists
q\in\mathcal{B}_{k}(Q)
such that
N_{u}^{*}(q)\leq\frac{1}{2}N_{u}^{*}(Q)
.

Proof.

Let
\ell_{Q}=\ell(Q)
and
x_{Q}
be the center of
Q
on
\partial\Omega
. Given
k\geq 5
, let
\mathcal{B}_{k}(Q)
be the set of boundary cuboids in the standard decomposition of
Q
as in Section
4.2
. Then
\ell_{q}:=\ell(q)=2^{-k}\ell_{Q}
. Put
N=N^{*}_{u}(Q)
and
M^{2}=J_{u}(x_{Q},\ell_{Q})
.

We prove by contradiction. Assume for any
q\in\mathcal{B}_{k}(Q)
,
N_{u}^{*}(q)>N/2
. This means that for any
q\in\mathcal{B}_{k}(Q)
, there exists
x_{q}\in q
and
r_{q}\in[\ell_{q}/2,\ell_{q}]
such that
N_{u}(x_{q},r_{q})>N/2
. For the given
k
, we may let
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(k)
be sufficiently small so that
(1+\varepsilon)^{k}\leq 2
. By the almost monotonicity in Lemma
3.4
and Remark
3.5
, for such
\varepsilon>0
, by letting
\gamma_{0}
and
\omega_{0}
be small enough, we have for all
1\leq j\leq k
,

N_{u}(x_{q},r_{q})+1\leq(1+\varepsilon)^{j}(N_{u}(x_{q},2^{j}r_{q})+1)\leq 2(N_{u}(x_{q},2^{j}r_{q})+1).

As a result,

N_{u}(x_{q},2^{j}r_{q})>\frac{N-2}{4},\quad\text{for }1\leq j\leq k.

Thus,

\displaystyle J_{u}(x_{q},4\ell_{q})\leq J_{u}(x_{q},8r_{q})

\displaystyle=J_{u}(x_{q},2^{k-1}r_{q})\exp\Big({-\sum_{j=3}^{k-2}N_{u}(x_{q},2^{j}r_{q})}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq J_{u}(x_{q},\ell_{Q}/2)e^{-(N-2)(k-4)/4}

\displaystyle\leq 2M^{2}e^{-(N-2)(k-4)/4},

where the last inequality follows from Lemma
3.2
. In view of the definition of
J_{u}
in (
3.9
) and (
3.8
), as well as our assumption
\frac{1}{2}I\leq A\leq 2I
, we have

(4.4)

\int_{B_{2\ell_{q},+}(x_{q})}u^{2}\leq CM^{2}e^{-Nk/5},\quad\text{for each }q\in\mathcal{B}_{k}(Q),

where we have chosen
k_{0},N_{0}
large such that
(N-2)(k-4)/4\geq Nk/5
for all
k\geq k_{0},N\geq N_{0}
.

Now, let
\Gamma=Q\cap\partial\Omega=\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{d}):x^{\prime}\in\pi(Q),x_{d}=\phi(x^{\prime})\}
.
Then (
4.4
) implies

(4.5)

\sup_{y\in\Gamma}\sup_{B_{\ell_{q}}(y)}|u|\leq CM\ell_{q}^{-d/2}e^{-Nk/10}.

Define
\Gamma_{1}=\Gamma+\frac{1}{2}\ell_{q}e_{d}=\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{d}):x^{\prime}\in\pi(Q),x_{d}=\phi(x^{\prime})+\frac{1}{2}\ell_{q}\}
. Note that the distance from
\Gamma_{1}
to
\partial\Omega
is comparable to
\ell_{q}
. By the interior gradient estimate and (
4.5
), we have

(4.6)

\sup_{\Gamma_{1}}|u|+\ell_{q}\sup_{\Gamma_{1}}|\nabla u|\leq CM\ell_{q}^{-d/2}e^{-Nk/10}.

Let
\hat{x}_{Q}=x_{Q}+\frac{1}{2}\ell_{q}e_{d}\in\Gamma_{1}
and
\hat{B}_{r,+}(\hat{x}_{Q})=B_{r,+}(x_{Q})+\frac{1}{2}\ell_{q}e_{d}=\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{d}):|x-\hat{x}_{Q}|<r,\text{ and }x_{d}>\phi(x^{\prime})+\frac{1}{2}\ell_{q}\}
. Therefore, applying Lemma
4.1
in
\hat{B}_{s(Q)/2,+}(\hat{x}_{Q})
with Lipschitz boundary
\Gamma_{1}
, we obtain from (
4.6
) that

(4.7)

\sup_{\hat{B}_{s(Q)/4,+}(\hat{x}_{Q})}|u|\leq CMs(Q)^{-d/2}2^{\tau kd/2+k}e^{-\tau Nk/10},

where we also used the fact
s(Q)=C(d,L)2^{k}\ell_{q}
.
Combined with (
4.5
) again, (
4.7
) yields

(4.8)

J_{u}(x_{Q},s(Q)/8)\leq C\int_{{B}_{s(Q)/4,+}({x}_{Q})}u^{2}\leq CM^{2}2^{\tau kd+2k}e^{-\tau Nk/5}.

Finally, we relate
J_{u}(x_{Q},s(Q)/8)
to
J_{u}(x_{Q},\ell_{Q})
by a sequence of doubling inequalities. Let
m
be the smallest integer such that
2^{m}s(Q)/8>\ell_{Q}
. Note that
m
depends only on
d
and
L
. By the almost monotonicity of the doubling index, for
1\leq j\leq m
,

N_{u}(x_{Q},2^{-j}\ell_{Q})\leq 2N_{u}(x_{Q},\ell_{Q})\leq 2N.

Thus

(4.9)

\displaystyle J_{u}(x_{Q},s(Q)/8)

\displaystyle\geq J_{u}(x_{Q},2^{-m}\ell_{Q})

\displaystyle=J_{u}(x_{Q},\ell_{Q})\exp(-\sum_{j=1}^{m}N_{u}(x_{Q},2^{-j}\ell_{Q}))

\displaystyle\geq M^{2}e^{-2mN}.

Comparing (
4.8
) and (
4.9
), we obtain

(\frac{1}{5}-\frac{2m}{k})N\leq\frac{C}{k}+(\tau d+1)\log 2.

Clearly, this is a contradiction if
k\geq k_{0}
and
N\geq N_{0}
for
k_{0}
and
N_{0}
sufficiently large. Note that
k_{0}
and
N_{0}
depend only on
d
and
L
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

In Lemma
4.2
and several lemmas in the rest of the paper, we assume
\frac{1}{2}I\leq A\leq 2I
. This assumption is only for technical convenience and can be achieved by a linear transformation in a neighborhood of
0\in\partial\Omega
.

5.
Absence of nodal points

In this section, we will establish more boundary layer lemmas that show the absence of zeros near relatively large portion of the boundary for small maximal doubling index. The existence of flat spots for convex domains will be crucial in this section.

For a point
y
in
\Omega
, denote by
\delta(y)
the distance from
y
to
\partial\Omega
. The following boundary layer lemma rules out the possibility of concentration of zeros near any Lipschitz boundary.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a Lipschitz domain (with Lipschitz constant
L
and
\ell=\frac{1}{1+L}
) and
0\in\partial\Omega
. Let
u
be
A
-harmonic in
B_{2,+}=\Omega\cap B_{2}
and
u=0
on
\partial\Omega\cap B_{2}
. Suppose for any
B
centered in
\overline{B_{2,+}}
with
2B_{+}\subset B_{2,+}
and
\text{diam}(B)\geq\frac{\ell}{100}
,

(5.1)

\frac{\int_{2B_{+}}u^{2}}{\int_{B_{+}}u^{2}}\leq M.

Assume that
u
has zeros in
B_{1/16,+}=\Omega\cap B_{1/16}
. Then
u
has at least one zero in
B_{1/4,+}\cap\{\delta(y)>\rho\}
, where
\rho
is a positive constant depending only on
A,M
and
L
.

Proof.

Assume
\int_{B_{1/2,+}}|u|^{2}=1
. Let
\rho\in(0,\ell/100)
. Suppose that
u
has no zero in
B_{1/4,+}\cap\{\delta(y)>\rho\}
. Without loss of generalilty, assume
u>0
in
B_{1/4,+}\cap\{\delta(y)>\rho\}
(otherwise, replace
u
by
-u
). We show that if
\rho
is sufficiently small, then
u
has no zeros in
B_{1/16,+}
, which is a contradication.

By the local
L^{\infty}
estimate, we know
|u|\leq C_{0}
in
B_{1/4,+}
. Let
z_{0}
be a point on
\partial B_{1/8,+}\cap\Omega
and
\delta(z_{0})\geq\frac{\ell}{16}
(The existence of such
z_{0}
follows from a simple geometric observation). By our assumption that
u>0
in
B_{1/4,+}\cap\{\delta(y)>\rho\}
and the fact
B_{\ell/32}(z_{0})\subset B_{1/4,+}\cap\{\delta(y)>\rho\}
, we can apply the Hanack inequality to obtain

\inf_{y\in B_{\ell/64}(z_{0})}u(y)\geq c\bigg(\fint_{B_{\ell/32}(z_{0})}u^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\geq c_{M},

where the last inequality follows from (
5.1
) and our assumption
\int_{B_{1/2,+}}|u|^{2}=1
. Note that
B_{\ell/64}(z_{0})\cap B_{1/8,+}
contains a surface ball of
\partial B_{1/8,+}\cap\Omega
with positive measure greater than
c=c(d,L)>0
.

Now we construct a barrier function
v
in
B_{1/8,+}
such that
v
is
A
-harmonic in
B_{1/8,+}
,
v=c_{M}
on
\partial B_{1/8,+}\cap B_{\ell/64}(z_{0})
,
v=-C_{0}
on
\partial B_{1/8,+}\cap\{0<\delta(y)<\rho\}
and
v=0
otherwise on
\partial B_{1/8,+}
. By the maximum principle, we see that
u\geq v
in
B_{1/8,+}
. We show that
v>0
in
B_{1/16,+}\subset B_{1/8,+}
, provided that
\rho
is small enough.

Note that
B_{1/8,+}
is a Lipschitz domain. Using the elliptic measure
\omega^{x}_{A}
, we can express
v
as

v(x)=c_{M}v_{1}(x)-C_{0}v_{2}(x)\quad\text{for }x\in B_{1/8,+},

where
v_{1}(x)=\omega_{A}^{x}(\partial B_{1/8,+}\cap B_{\ell/64}(z_{0}))
and
v_{2}(x)=\omega_{A}^{x}(\partial B_{1/8,+}\cap\{0<\delta(y)<\rho\})
. We refer to
[
11
]
for the definition and properties of elliptic measures. Observe that both
v_{1},v_{2}
are positive
A
-harmonic functions. Let
z_{1}
be an interior point of
B_{1/8,+}
such that
\text{dist}(z_{1},\partial B_{1/8,+})\geq\ell/100
. Then by the estimate of
A
-harmonic measure (and Harnack inequality, if necessary),

v_{1}(z_{1})=\omega_{A}^{z_{1}}(\partial B_{1/8,+}\cap B_{\ell/64}(z_{0}))\geq c_{1},

for some positive constant
c_{1}
depending only on
d
and
L
; see
[
11
, Lemma 1.3.2]
. On the other hand, the
L^{2}
estimate of the nontangential maximal function
(v_{2})^{*}
(see, e.g.,
[
13
, Theorem 1.1]
with
\varepsilon=1
) implies that

\displaystyle\lVert v_{2}\rVert_{L^{2}(B_{1/8,+})}

\displaystyle\leq C\lVert(v_{2})^{*}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial B_{1/8,+})}{\leq C\lVert v_{2}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial B_{1/8,+})}}

\displaystyle=C|\partial B_{1/8,+}\cap\{0<\delta(y)<\rho\}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C_{1}\rho^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
C_{1}
depends only on
d
and
L
. By the local
L^{\infty}
estimate, this implies that
0<v_{2}(z_{1})\leq C_{2}\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Then it follows from the comparison principle for positive solutions (see, e.g.,
[
11
, Lemma 1.3.7]
) that

\frac{v_{2}(x)}{v_{1}(x)}\leq C_{3}\frac{v_{2}(z_{1})}{v_{1}(z_{1})}\leq\frac{C_{2}C_{3}\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}}{c_{1}}\quad\text{for all }x\in B_{1/16,+}.

Now if
\rho
is sufficiently small (depending on
A,M
and
L
) such that

c_{M}-\frac{C_{0}C_{2}C_{3}\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}}{c_{1}}>0,

then
v=c_{M}v_{1}-C_{0}v_{2}>0
in
B_{1/16,+}
. This implies
u>0
in
B_{1/16,+}
and contradicts to the assumption that
u
has zeros in
B_{1/16,+}
.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a convex domain (with Lipschitz constant
L
) and
0\in\partial\Omega
. Assume
\frac{1}{2}I\leq A\leq 2I
. Let
u
be
A
-harmonic in
B_{1,+}
and
u=0
on
\partial\Omega\cap B_{1}
. Assume

(5.2)

\qquad N_{u}(0,\frac{1}{4})\leq N.

Then there exist
\gamma_{0}=\gamma_{0}(d,L)>0,\mu=\mu(d,L,N)>0
,
\alpha=\alpha(L)\geq 1
and
z\in\partial\Omega\cap B_{1/80}
such that if
|\nabla A|\leq\gamma_{0}
, then

|u(x)|\geq c\delta(x)^{\alpha}\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}(B_{1/4,+})},\quad\text{for all }x\in B_{\mu,+}(z),

where
c
depends only on
d,L
and
N
.

Proof.

Recall that for convex domains,
\omega=0
. By Lemma
3.4
, we see that the almost monotonicity formula (
3.22
) holds for
r_{0}=1
if
\gamma_{0}
is small enough. By an iteration, this implies that for integers
0\leq k\leq 10

N_{u}(0,2^{-k-2})\leq N_{1},

with
N_{1}
depending on
N
. By a standard argument, this further implies

N_{u}(x,r)\leq N_{2},

for all
x\in B_{1/100,+}
and

r\in(0,1/25)
, which is equivalent to

\frac{J_{u}(x,2r)}{J_{u}(x,r)}\leq e^{N_{2}}.

In view of the definition of
J_{u}
, if
\frac{1}{2}I\leq A\leq 2I
, we have
B_{t/2}(x)\subset E(x,t)\subset B_{2t}(x)
. Consequently, for any
x\in B_{1/100,+}
and
t\in(0,1/50)

(5.3)

\frac{\int_{B_{2t}(x)}u^{2}}{\int_{B_{t}(x)}u^{2}}\leq C\frac{J_{u}(x,4t)}{J_{u}(x,2t)}\frac{J_{u}(x,2t)}{J_{u}(x,t)}\frac{J_{u}(x,t)}{J_{u}(x,t/2)}\leq Ce^{3N_{2}}.

Let
r\in(0,1/1000)
(to be determined later). By Lemma
2.2
, we can find a boundary point
z_{r}\in\partial\Omega\cap B_{1/100}
such that the distance from
\partial\Omega\cap B_{r}(z_{r})
to its support plane
x_{d}=P(x^{\prime})
at
z_{r}
is at most
Cr^{2}
. It is crucial to point out that
C
is independent of
r
.

Let
D_{r}(z_{r})=B_{r}(z_{r})\cap\{x_{d}>P(x^{\prime})\}
and
B_{r,+}(z_{r})=\Omega\cap B_{r}(z_{r})
. Notice that both
D_{r}(z_{r})
and
B_{r,+}(z_{r})
are convex,
B_{r,+}(z_{r})\subset D_{r}(z_{r})
and
D_{r}(z_{r})\setminus B_{r,+}(z_{r})
is a thin region. Let
v
be an
A
-harmonic function in
D_{r}(z_{r})
such that
v=u
on
\{\partial B_{r,+}(z_{r})=\partial D_{r}(z_{r})\}
and
v=0
on
\partial D_{r}(z_{r})\setminus\partial B_{r,+}(z_{r})
.

Without loss of generality, assume
r^{-d/2}\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}(B_{2r,+}(z_{r}))}=1
. The gradient estimate in convex domain
B_{2r,+}(z_{r})
implies that
\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B_{r,+}(z_{r}))}\leq C_{1}r^{-1}
. This can be shown by a standard barrier argument; see Lemma
A.1
or
[
5
, Theorem 1.4]
for a related result.
Thus,
|u(x)|\leq C_{1}\delta(x)r^{-1}
for any
x\in B_{r,+}(z_{r})
. Let
\tilde{\delta}(x)
be the distance from
x
to the support plane
x_{d}=P(x^{\prime})
. Then Lemma
A.5
implies that
|v(x)|\leq C_{2}\tilde{\delta}(x)r^{-1}
in
D_{r,+}(z_{r})
. Note that for
x\in\partial\Omega\cap B_{r}(z_{r})
,
\tilde{\delta}(x)\leq Cr^{2}
and thus
|v(x)|\leq C_{3}r
.
Now, comparing
u
and
v
, we obtain

(5.4)

\lVert u-v\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B_{r,+}(z_{r}))}\leq C_{3}r.

Since
r^{-d/2}\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}(B_{2r,+}(z_{r}))}=1
, by (
5.3
), there exists
M>1
depending on
N
such that

r^{-d/2}J_{u}(z_{r},r/4)\geq M^{-1}.

Consequently,

r^{-d/2}J_{v}(z_{r},r/4)\geq\frac{1}{2}M^{-1}-C_{3}r^{2}.

Choose
r
small so that
\frac{1}{2}M^{-1}-C_{3}r^{2}\geq\frac{1}{4}M^{-1}
. As a result,

(5.5)

N_{v}(z_{r},r/4)=\log\frac{J_{v}(z_{r},r/2)}{J_{v}(z_{r},r/4)}\leq N_{3},

where
N_{3}
depends on
N
. Note that, applying the almost monotonicity of doubling index, we have
N_{v}(z_{r},r/2^{k})\leq CN_{3}
at least for
2\leq k\leq 10
.

Since
D_{r,+}(z_{r})
has flat boundary on the support plane, it is proved in Lemma
A.4
that there exists
t_{0}>0
(depending only on
N
and
A
) and
w\in\partial D_{r,+}(z_{r})\cap B_{r/16}(z_{r})
, such that
v
does not change sign in
B_{t_{0}r}(w)\cap D_{r,+}(z_{r})
. Moreover, for every
x\in B_{t_{0}r}(w)\cap D_{r,+}(z_{r})
,

|v(x)|\geq c_{N}\tilde{\delta}(x)r^{-1}\lVert v\rVert_{L^{\infty}(D_{r/2,+}(z_{r}))}\geq c_{N}\tilde{\delta}(x)r^{-1}.

In view of (
5.4
), for any
x\in B_{t_{0}r}(w)\cap B_{r,+}(z_{r})
,

|u(x)|\geq c_{N}\tilde{\delta}(x)r^{-1}-C_{3}r.

Hence,
u(x)
has no zeros if
\tilde{\delta}(x)>C_{3}c_{N}^{-1}r^{2}
. Since
\delta(x)\leq\tilde{\delta}(x)
. Thus
u(x)
has no zeros if
\delta(x)>C_{3}c_{N}^{-1}r^{2}
and
x\in B_{t_{0}r}(w)\cap B_{r,+}(z_{r})
.

Next, since the distance between the flat boundary of
D_{r,+}(z_{r})
and
\partial\Omega\cap B_{r}(z_{r})
is bounded by
Cr^{2}
, we can find a point
w^{\prime}\in\partial\Omega\cap B_{r}(z_{r})
such that
|w-w^{\prime}|\leq Cr^{2}
. Therefore, if
r<\frac{1}{2}C^{-1}t_{0}
, then
B_{\frac{1}{2}t_{0}r,+}(w^{\prime})=B_{\frac{1}{2}t_{0}r}(w^{\prime})\cap\Omega\subset B_{t_{0}r}(w)\cap D_{r,+}(z_{r})
. Consequently,
u
has no zeros in
B_{\frac{1}{2}t_{0}r}(w^{\prime})\cap\{\delta(x)>C_{3}c_{N}^{-1}r^{2}\}
. Due to this fact, (
5.3
) and Lemma
5.1
(with rescaling), there exists a constant
\rho>0
such that if

C_{3}c_{N}^{-1}r^{2}<\rho\frac{1}{2}t_{0}r,

then
u
has no zeros in
B_{\frac{1}{2}t_{0}r,+}(w^{\prime})
. Definitely, this is possible if we take

r=r_{0}:=\min\{\frac{1}{3}C^{-1}t_{0},\frac{1}{3}\rho t_{0}c_{N}C_{3}^{-1}\}.

Note that when we take different
r
, the point
z_{r}
, as well as
w
and
w^{\prime}
will also vary. But as in the previous argument if we fix
r_{0}
as above, we can find a particular
z=w^{\prime}\in\partial\Omega\cap B_{1/80}
such that
u
has no zeros in
B_{\frac{1}{2}t_{0}r_{0},+}(z)
.

Finally, let
4\mu=\frac{1}{2}t_{0}r_{0}
. By the Harnack inequality and the doubling inequality (applied finitely many times depending on
\mu
), we can find
x_{1}\in B_{3\mu,+}(z)
, away from the boundary, such that
|u(x_{1})|\geq c\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}(B_{1/4,+})}
where
c=c(d,A,L,N)>0
. Another use of Harnack inequality in inward cones (see Lemma
A.3
) leads to

|u(x)|\geq c\delta(x)^{\alpha}u(x_{1})\geq c\delta(x)^{\alpha}\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}(B_{1/4,+})},

for all
x\in B_{\mu,+}(z)
, where
\alpha
depends only on
L
. This completes the proof.
∎

Using a similar argument, we can generalize the same result from convex domains to quaisconvex domains. The idea is that we can approximate quasiconvex domains by convex domains at every point on the boundary and at every small scale. Lemma
5.1
will again be useful in this argument.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a quasiconvex Lipschitz domain and
0\in\partial\Omega
. Moreover,
\partial\Omega\cap B_{32}(0)
can be expressed as a Lipschitz graph with constant
L
. Assume
\frac{1}{2}I\leq A\leq 2I
. Let

In this section, we prove the main theorem by combining Lemma

6.1.
Nodal sets of
A
-harmonic functions

We first recall the interior estimate of nodal sets for analytic or Lipschitz coefficients.

Lemma 6.1
.

There exists
r_{0}=r_{0}(A)>0
such that if
q
is a cuboid with
s(q)<r_{0}
and
u
is
A
-harmonic in
(2d\Lambda)q
, then

(6.1)

\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(Z(u)\cap q)\leq C_{0}N_{u}^{*}(q)^{\beta}s(q)^{d-1},

where
\beta=1
if
A
is real analytic and
\beta>1
if
A
is Lipschitz.

Remark 6.2
.

In the case of Lipschitz coefficients in the above lemma, the conclusion in (
6.1
) follows from
[
19
, Theorem 6.1]
.
In the case of analytic coefficients, the estimate is essentially contained in
[
6
]
. Here we briefly sketch the proof in our setting. Let the origin to be the center of
q
. By the analytic hypoellipticity, we have

(6.2)

\displaystyle\frac{|\partial^{\alpha}u(0)|}{\alpha!}\leq{\Big(\frac{C_{1}}{r}\Big)^{|\alpha|}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})},

for all
r\in(0,r_{0}]
, where
\alpha
is a multi-index, and
C_{1}
and
r_{0}
depend on the analyticity of
A
.
In terms of the Taylor series of
u(x)
in
B_{r}
, we can extend
u
to be a holomorphic function in
\mathbb{B}_{c_{1}r}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{d}:|z|<c_{1}r\}
such that

(6.3)

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\mathbb{B}_{c_{1}r}}|u(z)|\leq C_{2}\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})},

where
c_{1}<1
and
C_{2}
depending on
C_{1}
. By a finite number of iteration of interior doubling inequalities, we have

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})}\leq e^{CN_{u}^{*}(q)}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{cr/2})}.

Combining the above two estimates, we obtain

(6.4)

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\mathbb{B}_{c_{1}r}}|u(z)|\leq e^{CN_{u}^{*}(q)}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{c_{1}r/2})}.

Thus, an application of
[
6
, Proposition 6.7]
yields

(6.5)

\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(Z(u)\cap B_{c_{2}r})\leq C_{3}N_{u}^{*}(q){r^{d-1}},

where
c_{2}<1
is a constant depending on
C_{1}
. Note that (
6.5
) holds for all
r\leq\min\{r_{0},s(q)\}
. Hence,
applying (
6.5
) to a finite number of balls that cover
q
, we obtain (
6.1
) for real analytic coefficients.

The constant
C_{0}
in (
6.1
) (depending on
C_{1}
in (
6.2
), not on specific

) can be quantified in terms of the quantitative analyticity property of
A
, such as the radius of convergence for the Taylor series. In our later application of Lemma
6.1
,
q
could be small cuboids very close to the boundary. To guarantee that we have a uniform constant

in (

), we need a control of the analyticity of the coefficients as
q
approaching the boundary. A simple way to achieve this is to assume that
A
is analytic in
\overline{\Omega}
, or in other words,
A
is analytic up to the boundary (the Taylor series of
A
converges in a neighborhood of any boundary point). Thus, the compactness of
\overline{\Omega}
will give a uniform bound of
C_{0}
in (
6.1
). Nevertheless, for the sake of brevity, in this paper we will simply say that
A
is analytic (or real analytic).

Define
N_{u}^{**}(Q)=N_{u}^{*}(Q)+1.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
u
be
A
-harmonic in
B\cap\Omega
and vanishing on

. Let
Q
be a standard boundary cuboid such that
Q\subset\frac{1}{10}B
. Then there exists
r_{0}=r_{0}(A,\Omega)>0
and
C_{0}=C_{0}(A,\Omega)>0
such that if
s(Q)\leq r_{0}
,

(6.6)

\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(Z(u)\cap Q)\leq C_{0}N_{u}^{**}(Q)^{\beta}s(Q)^{d-1}.

where
\beta=1
if
A
is real analytic and
\beta>1
if
A
is Lipschitz.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume the center of
Q
is
0\in\partial\Omega
. By an affine transformation, we may assume
A(0)=I
and then
Q
is turned into a rescaled cuboid that is contained in some standard cuboid with comparable size, still denoted by
Q
. Therefore, it is sufficient to prove (
6.6

.

First let us explain how

is determined. Let
N_{0}
and
k_{0}
be given by Lemma
4.2
, which depends only on
A
and
\Omega
. Also let
N=N_{0}
in Lemma
5.4
and pick
k
to be the maximum of

in Lemma
4.2
and Lemma
5.4
. Now, we let
\gamma_{0}
and
\omega_{0}
be given by Lemma
4.2
and Lemma
5.4
, whichever is smaller. Note that all these parameters depend only on
A
and
\Omega
. Now, we can perform a rescaling

such that
\frac{1}{2}I\leq A\leq 2I,|\nabla A|\leq\gamma_{0}
in
Q
and
\omega(32)\leq\omega_{0}
in
B_{1,+}(0)
, where
r_{1}=Cr_{0}
and

. Note that

now depends only on
A
and

. Consequently, both Lemma
4.2
and Lemma
5.4

and all its boundary subcuboids. Again, we only need to show (

Let
K
be a compact set

. We would like to show

(6.7)

\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(Z(u)\cap Q\cap K)\leq C_{0}N_{u}^{**}(Q)^{\beta}s(Q)^{d-1},

for some constant
C_{0}
independent of
K
. Definitely if

is small enough such that

, then
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(Z(u)\cap Q\cap K)=0
. We prove (

) by induction from small boundary cuboids to large boundary cuboids.

Let
k\geq 1
to be determined. Let
(Q,\mathcal{B}_{k},\mathcal{I}_{k})
be a standard decomposition as in Section
4.2

\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(Z(u)\cap q\cap K)\leq C_{0}N_{u}^{**}(q)^{\beta}s(q)^{d-1}.

Note that the base case of induction

is trivial.

Now consider

\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(Z(u)\cap Q\cap K)\leq\sum_{q\in\mathcal{I}_{k}}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(Z(u)\cap q)+\sum_{q\in\mathcal{B}_{k}}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(Z(u)\cap q\cap K).

By the interior result in Lemma
6.1
,

(6.8)

\sum_{q\in\mathcal{I}_{k}}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(Z(u)\cap q)\leq C_{1}\sum_{q\in\mathcal{I}_{k}}N_{u}^{**}(q)^{\beta}s(q)^{d-1}\leq C_{k}N_{u}^{**}(Q)^{\beta}s(Q)^{d-1}.

where
\beta=1
if
A
is real analytic and
\beta>1
if
A
is Lipschitz.
For all other boundary cuboids
q\in\mathcal{B}_{k}
, we have by Remark
3.5

N_{u}^{**}(q)\leq(1+\varepsilon)^{k}N_{u}^{**}(Q),

if
Q
is small enough. Combining Lemma
4.2
and Lemma
5.4
(with

), we know that there exists
k\geq 1
, depending only on
d,L
, such that there is a cube
q_{0}\in\mathcal{B}_{k}
such that either
N_{u}^{*}(q_{0})\leq\frac{1}{2}N_{u}^{*}(Q)
or
Z(u)\cap q_{0}=\varnothing
. Without loss of generality, assume
N_{0}\geq 10
. Since the first case takes place if
N\geq N_{0}
, thus
N_{u}^{*}(q_{0})\leq\frac{1}{2}N_{u}^{*}(Q)
implies
N_{u}^{**}(q_{0})\leq\frac{2}{3}N_{u}^{**}(Q).

Recall that
\mathcal{B}_{k}
has
2^{k(d-1)}
subcuboids. Applying the induction argument to each boundary subcuboids, we have

(6.9)

\displaystyle\sum_{q\in\mathcal{B}_{k}}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(Z(u)\cap q\cap K)

\displaystyle=\sum_{q\in\mathcal{B}_{k},q\neq q_{0}}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(Z(u)\cap q\cap K)+\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(Z(u)\cap q_{0}\cap K)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{q\in\mathcal{B}_{k},q\neq q_{0}}C_{0}N_{u}^{**}(q)^{\beta}s(q)^{d-1}+C_{0}(2/3)^{\beta}N_{u}^{**}(Q)^{\beta}s(q_{0})^{d-1}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg\{\frac{2^{k(d-1)}-1}{2^{k(d-1)}}(1+\varepsilon)^{\beta k}+\frac{(2/3)^{\beta}}{2^{k(d-1)}}\bigg\}C_{0}N_{u}^{**}(Q)^{\beta}S(Q)^{d-1}.

Since
\beta\geq 1
and
k\geq 3
are fixed, we choose
\varepsilon
small (here we need to make
r_{0}=r_{0}(A,\Omega)>0
small enough) and
C_{0}
large enough such that

C_{k}+\bigg\{\frac{2^{k(d-1)}-1}{2^{k(d-1)}}(1+\varepsilon)^{\beta k}+\frac{(2/3)^{\beta}}{2^{k(d-1)}}\bigg\}C_{0}\leq C_{0}.

Note that
C_{0}
does not depend on the compact set
K
. Thus, the combination of (
6.8
) and (
6.9
) gives (
6.7
), which implies (
6.6
) by letting

exhaust

.
∎

6.2.
Dirichlet eigenfunctions

Let
\Omega
be a bounded quasiconvex Lipschitz domain. Let
\varphi_{\lambda}
be the Dirichlet eigenfunction of
\mathcal{L}
corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda>0
, namely,
\mathcal{L}(\varphi_{\lambda})=\lambda\varphi_{\lambda}
in
\Omega
and
\varphi_{\lambda}=0
on
\partial\Omega
. Let

u_{\lambda}(x,t)=e^{t\sqrt{\lambda}}\varphi_{\lambda}(x).

Then
u_{\lambda}
is
\widetilde{A}
-harmonic in
\widetilde{\Omega}:=\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
, where

\widetilde{A}(x,t)=\begin{bmatrix}A(x)&0\ \\
0&1\ \end{bmatrix}.

Lemma 6.4
.

If a Lipschitz
\Omega
is quasiconvex, then
\widetilde{\Omega}
is also quasiconvex.

Proof.

First note
\partial\widetilde{\Omega}=\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
. Let
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\partial\widetilde{\Omega}
. Then
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
.
Since
\Omega
is quasiconvex, we can rotate the domain
\Omega
such that the local graph of
\partial\Omega
is given by a Lipschitz graph
x_{d}=\phi(x^{\prime})
with
\phi(x^{\prime}_{0})=0
and for
|x^{\prime}-x^{\prime}_{0}|<r_{0}
,

\phi(x^{\prime})\geq-|x^{\prime}-x^{\prime}_{0}|\omega(|x^{\prime}-x^{\prime}_{0}|).

Clearly the local graph of
\partial\widetilde{\Omega}
is given by
x_{d}=\widetilde{\phi}(x^{\prime},t):=\phi(x^{\prime})
. Thus, for any
|(x^{\prime},t)-(x^{\prime}_{0},t_{0})|<r_{0}

\displaystyle\widetilde{\phi}(x^{\prime},t)

\displaystyle\geq-|x^{\prime}-x^{\prime}_{0}|\omega(|x^{\prime}-x^{\prime}_{0}|)

\displaystyle\geq-|(x^{\prime},t)-(x^{\prime}_{0},t_{0})|\omega(|(x^{\prime},t)-(x^{\prime}_{0},t_{0})|),

which, by definition, shows that
\widetilde{\Omega}
is quasiconvex with the same quasiconvexity modulus.
∎

The following lemma gives the bound of doubling index for the
\widetilde{A}
-harmonic extension

.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded quasiconvex Lipschitz domain. Let

be the
\widetilde{A}
-harmonic extension in

of the Dirichlet eigenfunction
\varphi_{\lambda}
. There exists

such that if
(x,t)\in\widetilde{\Omega}
and

, then

, where
C_{r}
depends only on

and

.

Proof.

The proof is standard. We refer to
[
21
]
for Lipschitz domains by using the three-ball inequality on a chain of balls and to
[
6
,
7
]
for the case of smooth domains. We skip the details.
∎

Finally, we prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
\varphi_{\lambda}
be the Dirichlet eigenfunction corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda>0
. Let
u_{\lambda}=e^{t\sqrt{\lambda}}\varphi_{\lambda}
be the
\widetilde{A}
-harmonic extension of
\varphi_{\lambda}
in
\widetilde{\Omega}=\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
. Note that
Z(u_{\lambda})=Z(\varphi_{\lambda})\times\mathbb{R}
. Thus it suffices to estimate the nodal set of
u_{\lambda}
in
\Omega\times[-1,1]\subset\widetilde{\Omega}
.

Let
r_{0}
be as in Theorem
6.3
or Lemma
6.5
, whichever is smaller. Let
\Omega_{I}=\{x\in\Omega:\delta(x)>cr_{0}\}
. Then
\Omega_{I}\times[-1,1]
can be covered (with finite overlaps) by a sequence of balls

with radius

such that
10B_{i}\subset\Omega\times[-2,2]
. Note that the number of these balls depends only on
cr_{0},d
and
\Omega

6.1
and Lemma
6.5

\mathcal{H}^{d}(Z(u_{\lambda})\cap\Omega_{I}\times[-1,1])\leq\sum_{i}\mathcal{H}^{d}(Z(u_{\lambda})\cap B_{i})\leq C\lambda^{\beta/2}.

On the other hand, let
\Omega_{B}=\Omega\setminus\Omega_{I}
. Then
\Omega_{B}\times[-1,1]
can be covered by a family of boundary balls
B_{k}^{\prime}
centered on
\partial\Omega\times[-1,1]
with radius

and
\Omega
. For each of these ball

with

and Lemma

show

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{d}(Z(u_{\lambda})\cap\Omega_{B}\times[-1,1])

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k}\mathcal{H}^{d}(Z(u_{\lambda})\cap Q_{k})

Hence,
\mathcal{H}^{d}(Z(u_{\lambda})\cap\Omega\times[-1,1])\leq C\lambda^{\beta/2}.
This implies the desired estimate of
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(Z(\varphi_{\lambda}))
.

Appendix A

A.1.
A quasiconvex Lipschitz curve that is nowhere convex or
C^{1}

We construct a quasiconvex Lipschitz curve in
(0,1)
which is neither
C^{1}
nor convex in any subinterval of
(0,1)
. First let
\{q_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
be the list of all rational number in
[0,1]
and define a nonnegative Radon measure by

\mu=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{-k}\delta_{q_{k}},

where
\delta_{q_{k}}
is the Dirac measure at
q_{k}
.
Now let
f(t)=\mu((0,t))
. Note that
f
is a bounded nondecreasing function with
f(0)=0
and
f(1)=1
. Moreover,
f
is not continuous in any subinterval of
(0,1)
. Let

\phi(x)=\int_{0}^{x}f(t)dt-x^{2}.

Since
\int_{0}^{x}f(t)dt
is convex and
x^{2}
is
C^{1}
, it is easy to see that
\phi(x)
is quasiconvex and Lipschitz.

Since
f
is not continuous in any subinterval of
(0,1)
, then clearly,
\phi
is not
C^{1}
in any subinterval of
(0,1)
. Now we show that
\phi
is not convex in any subinterval of
(0,1)
. It is sufficient to show that
\phi^{\prime\prime}
is not a nonnegative Radon measure on any subinterval of
(0,1)
. In fact,

\phi^{\prime\prime}=-2+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{-k}\delta_{q_{k}}.

Divided
(0,1)
into
2^{j}
equal subintervals with length
2^{-j}
. For any such interval
I
, if there is no
q_{k}
with
1\leq k\leq j
dropping in
I
, then

\phi^{\prime\prime}(I)\leq-2|I|+\sum_{k=j+1}^{\infty}2^{-k}\leq-2^{-j}<0.

In other words, there are at most
j
subintervals (out of
2^{j}
subintervals in total) such that
\phi^{\prime\prime}(I)\geq 0
. As
j
approaching infinity,
we see that
\phi^{\prime\prime}
cannot be nonnegative in any nonempty subintervals.

A.2.
Proof of Proposition
3.1

We adapt the arguments in
[
1
]
,
[
18
]
with emphasis on the role of Lipschitz coefficient.
Recall that we assumed
A(0)=I
and defined

(A.1)

\displaystyle\mu(x)=\frac{x\cdot A(x)x}{|x|^{2}}.

Using the standard assumption on
A
, we can check that

(A.2)

\displaystyle|\nabla\mu|\leq C\gamma\quad\ \mbox{and}\ \quad\Lambda^{-1}\leq\mu(x)\leq\Lambda.

We choose
w(x)=\frac{A(x)x}{\mu(x)}
and verify directly that

\displaystyle\partial_{j}w_{i}(x)=\delta_{ij}+O(\gamma|x|).

Differentiating
H(r)
with respect to
r
, we have

(A.3)

\displaystyle H^{\prime}(r)=\frac{d-1}{r}\int_{\partial B_{r}\cap\Omega}\mu u^{2}\,d\sigma+2\int_{\partial B_{r}\cap\Omega}\mu u\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\,d\sigma+\int_{\partial B_{r}\cap\Omega}\frac{\partial\mu}{\partial r}u^{2}\,\,d\sigma,

where
\frac{\partial}{\partial r}=\frac{x}{|x|}\cdot\nabla
.
The conormal derivative associated with
\mathcal{L}=-\nabla\cdot(A\nabla)
is defined as
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=n\cdot A\nabla u
, where
n
is the outer normal of the boundary. Since
\mathcal{L}(u)=0
in
B_{r}\cap\Omega
and
u=0
on
B_{r}\cap\partial\Omega
, an integration by parts gives

(A.4)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}\cap\Omega}A\nabla u\cdot\nabla u=\int_{\partial B_{r}\cap\Omega}u\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\,d\sigma.

Note that on
\partial B_{r}\cap\Omega
,
n(x)=\frac{x}{|x|}
.
Let

\displaystyle\tau=A\frac{x}{|x|}-\mu\frac{x}{|x|}.

Observe that
\tau
is a tangent vector field on
\partial B_{r}
since
\tau\cdot n=0
on
\partial B_{r}
. Moreover, using
n=\frac{x}{|x|}
on
\partial B_{r}\cap\Omega
, we can rewrite
\tau=(\tau_{i})
as

\displaystyle\tau_{i}=a_{ij}n_{j}-a_{ml}n_{m}n_{l}n_{i}=a_{ij}n_{j}n_{m}n_{m}-a_{ml}n_{m}n_{l}n_{i}.

Thus, we have

(A.5)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \tau\cdot\nabla&=a_{ij}n_{j}n_{m}n_{m}\partial_{i}-a_{ml}n_{m}n_{l}n_{i}\partial_{i}\\
&=a_{ij}n_{j}n_{m}n_{m}\partial_{i}-a_{ij}n_{i}n_{j}n_{m}\partial_{m}\\
&=a_{ij}n_{j}n_{m}(n_{m}\partial_{i}-n_{i}\partial_{m}),\end{aligned}

where we have interchanged the index
m
and
i
, and
l
and
j
in the second equality. The key is that
(n_{m}\partial_{i}-n_{i}\partial_{m})
is a tangential derivative on
\partial B_{r}\cap\Omega
that allows for integration by parts on the boundary. Since
u=0
on
B_{r}\cap\partial\Omega
, integrating by parts shows that

(A.6)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{\partial B_{r}\cap\Omega}\nabla(u^{2})\cdot\tau\,d\sigma&=\int_{\partial B_{r}\cap\Omega}a_{ij}n_{j}n_{m}(n_{m}\partial_{i}-n_{i}\partial_{m})u^{2}d\sigma\\
&=-\int_{\partial B_{r}\cap\Omega}u^{2}(n_{m}\partial_{i}-n_{i}\partial_{m})(a_{ij}n_{j}n_{m})d\sigma\\
&=\int_{\partial B_{r}\cap\Omega}u^{2}(n_{m}\partial_{i}-n_{i}\partial_{m})(n_{i}n_{m})d\sigma\\
&\qquad-\int_{\partial B_{r}\cap\Omega}u^{2}(n_{m}\partial_{i}-n_{i}\partial_{m})((a_{ij}-\delta_{ij})n_{j}n_{m})d\sigma\\
&=O(\gamma)H(r),\end{aligned}

where we have used
A(0)=I
, and the Lipschtiz continuity of
A
and
n
on the smooth boundary
\partial B_{r}\cap\Omega
, as well as the observation
(n_{m}\partial_{i}-n_{i}\partial_{m})(n_{i}n_{m})=0
.
Hence, from (
A.6
), we have

\displaystyle D(r)=\int_{\partial B_{r}\cap\Omega}u\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\,d\sigma=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{r}\cap\Omega}\mu u\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\,d\sigma+\int_{\partial B_{r}\cap\Omega}u\nabla u\cdot\tau\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{r}\cap\Omega}\mu u\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\,d\sigma+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial B_{r}\cap\Omega}\nabla(u^{2})\cdot\tau\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{r}\cap\Omega}\mu u\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\,d\sigma+O(\gamma)H(r).

It follows from (
A.3
) that

(A.7)

\displaystyle H^{\prime}(r)=\frac{d-1}{r}H(r)+2D(r)+O(\gamma)H(r).

Next we consider
D^{\prime}(r)
.
We use the following Rellich-Necas identity

(A.8)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\nabla\cdot(w(A\nabla u\cdot\nabla u))\\
&=2\nabla\cdot((w\cdot\nabla u)A\nabla u)+(\nabla\cdot w)A\nabla u\cdot\nabla u\\
&\qquad-2\partial_{i}w_{k}a_{ij}\partial_{j}u\partial_{k}u-2(w\cdot\nabla u)\nabla\cdot(A\nabla u)+w_{k}\partial_{k}a_{ij}\partial_{i}u\partial_{j}u.\end{aligned}

Direct calculations show that

(A.9)

\displaystyle w\cdot\frac{x}{|x|}=r\quad\mbox{and}\quad(w\cdot\nabla u)A\nabla u\cdot\frac{x}{|x|}=\frac{r}{\mu(x)}(\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu})^{2}\quad\mbox{for}\ x\in\partial B_{r}\cap\Omega.

On
B_{r}\cap\partial\Omega
, since
n=\frac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|}
(if
\nabla u\neq 0
), we have

(A.10)

\displaystyle(w\cdot n)A\nabla u\cdot\nabla u=(w\cdot\nabla u)A\nabla u\cdot n=\frac{(Ax\cdot n)(An\cdot n)}{\mu(x)}|\nabla u|^{2}.

This identity definitely holds if
\nabla u=0
.
We integrate the Rellich-Necas identity (
A.8
) to have

(A.11)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\int_{\partial B_{r}\cap\Omega}A\nabla u\cdot\nabla u\\
&=\frac{1}{r}\int_{B_{r}\cap\partial\Omega}\frac{(Ax\cdot n)(An\cdot n)}{\eta_{1}(x)}(\frac{\partial u}{\partial n})^{2}\,d\sigma+2\int_{\partial B_{r}\cap\Omega}\frac{1}{\mu}(\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu})^{2}\\
&\qquad+\frac{d-2}{r}\int_{B_{r}\cap\Omega}A\nabla u\cdot\nabla u+O(\gamma)\int_{B_{r}\cap\Omega}A\nabla u\cdot\nabla u,\end{aligned}

where we have used the fact that
\nabla\cdot(A\nabla u)=0
in
B_{r}\cap\Omega
,
|\nabla u|=|n\cdot\nabla u|=|\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}|
almost everywhere on
B_{r}\cap\partial\Omega
, and the Lipschitz continuity of
A
.
Note that

(A.12)

\displaystyle D^{\prime}(r)=\int_{\partial B_{r}\cap\Omega}A\nabla u\cdot\nabla u.

Using the assumption that
Ax\cdot n\geq 0
on
B_{r}\cap\partial\Omega
and (
A.12
), on one hand, we have

(A.13)

\displaystyle\frac{D^{\prime}(r)}{D(r)}\geq\frac{d-2}{r}+\frac{2\int_{\partial B_{r}\cap\Omega}\mu^{-1}(\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu})^{2}\,d\sigma}{\int_{B_{r}\cap\Omega}A\nabla u\cdot\nabla u}+O(\gamma).

On the other hand, with the aid of (
A.7
), we obtain that

(A.14)

\displaystyle\frac{H^{\prime}(r)}{H(r)}=\frac{d-1}{r}+\frac{2\int_{B_{r}\cap\Omega}A\nabla u\cdot\nabla u\,d\sigma}{\int_{\partial B_{r}\cap\Omega}\mu(x)u^{2}\,d\sigma}+O(\gamma).

Now we can establish the monotonicity property of the frequency function. Thanks to (
A.13
), (
A.14
), (
A.4
) and the definition of
\mathcal{N}_{u}(0,r)
in (
3.1
), we get

(A.15)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{\mathcal{N}_{u}^{\prime}(0,r)}{\mathcal{N}_{u}(0,r)}&=\frac{1}{r}+\frac{D^{\prime}(r)}{D(r)}-\frac{H^{\prime}(r)}{H(r)}\\
&\geq\frac{2\int_{\partial B_{r}\cap\Omega}\mu^{-1}(\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu})^{2}\,d\sigma}{\int_{\partial B_{r}\cap\Omega}u\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}d\sigma}-\frac{2\int_{\partial B_{r}\cap\Omega}u\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}d\sigma}{\int_{\partial B_{r}\cap\Omega}\mu u^{2}\,d\sigma}+O(\gamma)\\
&\geq-C\gamma,\end{aligned}

where
\mathcal{N}_{u}^{\prime}(0,r)=\frac{d}{dr}\mathcal{N}_{u}(0,r)
and we have used the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in the last inequality. Thus,
e^{C\gamma r}\mathcal{N}(0,r)
is nondecreasing with respect to
r
.

A.3.
Some useful results for elliptic equations

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a convex domain and
0\in\partial\Omega
. Suppose that
u
is
A
-harmonic in
B_{1,+}=B_{1}(0)\cap\Omega
with
u=0
on
\partial\Omega\cap B_{1}(0)
. Then

\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B_{1/2,+})}\leq C\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}(B_{1,+})}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume that
x_{d}=0
is the support plane of
\Omega
at
0
. Let
D_{3/4,+}:=B_{1}(0)\cap\{x_{d}>0\}
. By the De Giorgi-Nash estimate,

(A.16)

K:=\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B_{3/4,+})}\leq C\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}(B_{1,+})}.

Consider a positive barrier function
v
which is
A
-harmonic in
D_{3/4,+}
and satisfies
v=K
on
\partial B_{3/4}\cap\{x_{d}>0\}
and
v=0
on
B_{3/4}\cap\{x_{d}=0\}
. We show that
v(x)\leq CKx_{d}
for all
x\in D_{5/8,+}=B_{5/8}\cap\{x_{d}>0\}
. This follows easily from the gradient estimate of
v
over the flat boundary, i.e.,

\lVert\nabla v\rVert_{L^{\infty}(D_{5/8,+})}\leq C\lVert v\rVert_{L^{\infty}(D_{3/4,+})}\leq CK.

Here we need
A
to be at least Hölder continuous.

Now, observe that
-v\leq u\leq v
in
B_{3/4,+}
, due to the fact
|u|\leq v
on
\partial B_{3/4,+}
and the comparison principle. Then we have

|u(x)|\leq CKx_{d},\quad\text{for all }x\in B_{5/8,+}.

In particular, for any
x=(x^{\prime},x_{d})
with
x^{\prime}=0
, we have
|u(x)|\leq CK{\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega)
.
Note that this argument actually works for any points in
B_{1/2,+}
as any interior point can be connected to the nearest point on the boundary with a perpendicular support plane. Therefore, we obtain

|u(x)|\leq CK{\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega),\quad\text{for all }x\in B_{5/8,+}.

Finally, for any
x\in B_{1/2,+}
, let
\delta(x)={\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega)\leq 1/8
(the case
\delta(x)>1/8
is trivial). By the interior gradient estimate

|\nabla u(x)|\leq\frac{C}{\delta(x)}\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B_{\delta(x)}(x))}\leq\frac{C}{\delta(x)}CK\delta(x)\leq CK.

This, combined with (
A.16
), gives the desired estimate.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

Let
\beta>0
and
\mathcal{C}=\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{d}):0\leq|x^{\prime}|<\beta x_{d}\text{ and }x_{d}<1\}
. Let
u
be a positive
A
-harmonic function in
\mathcal{C}
. Then there exists
\alpha=\alpha(\beta,\Lambda)>0
such that for any
0<t<1/2

u(0,t)\geq t^{\alpha}u(0,1/2).

Proof.

Let
t\in(0,1/2)
and assume
u(0,1/2)=1
(without loss of generality). Note that
(0,t)
is an interior point of
\mathcal{C}
with
{\rm dist}((0,t),\partial\mathcal{C})\geq\frac{\beta t}{1+\beta}
. Then for
t\in[1/4,1/2)
,
{\rm dist}((0,t),\partial\mathcal{C})\geq\frac{\beta}{4(1+\beta)}
. By a Harnack chain of balls with radius
\frac{\beta}{8(1+\beta)}
, one can connect
(0,1/2)
to any point
(0,t)
for
1/4\leq t<1/2
. The number of such balls is at most
N=N(\beta)\geq 1
. Then the Harnack inequality implies

u(0,t)\geq c^{N}u(0,1/2)=c(\beta,\Lambda)>0,\quad\text{for all }1/4\leq t<1/2.

Note that
c(\beta,\Lambda)<1
.
Repeating this argument, we can obtain that, for
k\geq 1
,

u(0,t)\geq c^{k}(\beta,\Lambda),\quad\text{for all }2^{-k}\leq t<2^{-k+1}.

It follows that for all
t\in(0,1/2)
,

u(0,t)\geq 2^{(\log_{2}c(\beta,\Lambda))(-2\log_{2}t)}=t^{-2\log_{2}(c(\beta,\Lambda))}.

This gives the desired estimate with
\alpha=-2\log_{2}(c(\beta,\Lambda))>0
.
∎

Lemma A.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a Lipschitz domain and
0\in\partial\Omega
. Assume
\partial\Omega\cap B_{1}(0)
is given by the Lipschitz graph
x_{d}=\phi(x^{\prime})
and
B_{1,+}=\Omega\cap B_{1}(0)=B_{1}(0)\cap\{x=(x,x_{d}):x_{d}>\phi(x^{\prime})\}
. Then, there exists
\alpha,c>0
(depending only on
\Lambda
and the Lipschitz constnat of

) such that for any positive
A
-harmonic function
u
in
B_{1}^{+}
, we have

u(x)\geq c\delta(x)^{\alpha}u(0,1/2),\quad\text{for all }x\in B_{1/2,+}.

Proof.

Assume
u(0,1/2)=1
. First of all, in view of the Harnack inequality, we have
u(x)\geq c>0
for all
x\in B_{7/8,+}\cap\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{d}):x_{d}>\phi(x^{\prime})+1/8\}
. Thus, it suffices to consider an arbitrary point
x\in B_{1/2,+}\cap\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{d}):\phi(x^{\prime})<x_{d}<\phi(x^{\prime})+1/8\}
. Fix such an

and let
x_{0}=(x^{\prime},\phi(x^{\prime}))\in\partial\Omega
. Let

\mathcal{C}_{1/4}(x_{0})=\{y=(y^{\prime},y_{d}):0\leq|y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}|<\beta(y_{d}-\phi(x^{\prime}))\text{ and }y_{d}-\phi(x^{\prime})<1/4\},

where
\beta=\min\{1,L^{-1}\}
and

is the Lipschitz constant of
\phi
. Note that
\mathcal{C}_{1/4}(x_{0})
is a rescaled and translated version of the cone in Lemma
A.2
and is entirely contained in
B_{1,+}
. Also note that
x
in on the vertical axis of the cone
\mathcal{C}_{1/4}(x_{0})

Let
x_{1}=(x^{\prime},\phi(x^{\prime})+1/8)
. Using
u(x_{1})\geq c
and applying Lemma
A.2
in
\mathcal{C}_{1/4}(x_{0})
, we obtain
u(x)\geq c(x_{d}-\phi(x^{\prime}))^{\alpha}=c|x-x_{0}|^{\alpha}\geq c\delta(x)^{\alpha}
, where the last inequality follows from the fact that
x
is on the vertical axis of
\mathcal{C}_{1/4}(x_{0})
whose distance from the boundary is comparable to

∎

The following lemma is a generalization of
[

from harmonic functions (proved by using reflection) to

-harmonic functions. We point out that the assumptions
\frac{1}{2}I\leq A\leq 2I
and

Lemma A.4
.

There exists
\gamma_{0}=\gamma_{0}(d)>0
such that the following statement holds. If
\frac{1}{2}I\leq A\leq 2I,|\nabla A|\leq\gamma_{0}
and
u
is a continuous
A
-harmonic function in
B_{2,+}:=B_{2}\cap\{x_{d}>0\}
vanishing on
B_{2}\cap\{x_{d}=0\}
and satisfying
N_{u}(0,1/2)\leq N
. Then there exists
w\in B_{1/2}\cap\{x_{d}=0\}
and
t>0,C>0
depending only on
N
and
A
such that
u
does not change sign in
B_{t,+}(w)
. Moreover, for all
x\in B_{t,+}(w)
,

|u(x)|\geq Cx_{d}J_{u}(0,1)^{1/2}.

Proof.

By normalization, assume
J_{u}(0,1)=1
. If
|\nabla A|
is smaller than some absolute constant
\gamma_{0}=\gamma_{0}(d)>0
, Lemma
3.4
implies the almost monotonicity of
N_{u}(0,r)
. In particular, we have
N_{u}(0,2^{-k})\leq 2N_{u}(0,1/2)\leq 2N

\displaystyle\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}(B_{1/4,+})}^{2}\gtrsim J_{u}(0,\frac{1}{8})

, we have

\displaystyle\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}(B_{1/4,+})}

Lemma A.5
.

Assume
\Lambda^{-1}I\leq A\leq\Lambda I
. There exists
\gamma_{0}=\gamma_{0}(d,\Lambda)>0
such that the following statement holds. If
|\nabla A|\leq\gamma_{0}
and
u
is a continuous
A
-harmonic function in
B_{1,+}:=B_{1}(0)\cap\{x_{d}>0\}
satisfying
|u(x)|\leq x_{d}
for all

,

.

Consider

. Clearly

on
\partial B_{1,+}
. We check that

if
\gamma_{0}
is sufficiently small. In fact,

\nabla\cdot(A\nabla w)=-2a_{dd}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{i}a_{id}(2-2x_{d})\leq-2\Lambda^{-1}+2d\gamma_{0}.

, we have

. Similarly, one can show

∎

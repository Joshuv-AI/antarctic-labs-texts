The partitions whose members are finite and the permutations with at most n n non-fixed points of a set
Nattapon Sonpanow, Pimpen Vejjajiva

Abstract
. We write S ≤ n ​ ( A ) S_{\leq n}(A) and Part fin ​ ( A ) \textup{Part}_{\textup{fin}}(A) for the set of permutations with at most n n non-fixed points, where n n is a natural number, and the set of partitions whose members are finite, respectively, of a set A A . 1.
Introduction

With the Axiom of Choice (AC),
|\textup{Part}(A)|=2^{|A|}=|A|!
for any infinite set
A
, where
\textup{Part}(A)
is the set of partitions,
2^{|A|}
and
|A|!
are the cardinalities of the power set and the set of permutations of
A
respectively. Without AC, it follows from the results in
[
4
, Proposition 8.3]
and
[
1
]
that these equalities are not provable in the Zermelo-Fraenkel set theory (ZF).

Without AC, it is not hard to show that
2^{|A|}\leq|\textup{Part}(A)|
for any set
A
with
|A|\geq 5
. Halbeisen and Shelah showed in
[
3
, Theorem 3]
that “
|\textup{fin}(A)|<2^{|A|}
for any infinite set
A
” is provable in ZF, where
\textup{fin}(A)
is the set of finite subsets of
A
. As a result,
|\textup{fin}(A)|<|\textup{Part}(A)|
for any infinite set
A
. A stronger result in
[
9
, Theorem 3.7]
showed that “
|\textup{fin}(A)|<|\textup{Part}_{\textup{fin}}(A)|
for any set
A
with
|A|\geq 5
” is provable in ZF, where
\textup{Part}_{\textup{fin}}(A)
is the set of partitions of
A
whose members are finite. Moreover, “
|\textup{Part}_{\textup{fin}}(A)|<2^{|A|}
for some infinite set
A
” is consistent with ZF (cf.
[
9
, Corollary 4.7]
).

For a set
A
, we write
S_{\textup{fin}}(A)
,
S_{\leq n}(A)
, and
S_{n}(A)
, where
n
is a natural number, for the sets of permutations on
A
with finitely many non-fixed points, at most
n
non-fixed points, and exactly
n
non-fixed points respectively.
The results in
[
8
, Theorems 2.9 and 2.10]
showed that, under AC
{}_{<\aleph_{0}}
, the Axiom of Choice for families of nonempty finite sets,
|S_{\textup{fin}}(A)|\leq|\textup{fin}(A)|
if and only if
A
is a Dedekind infinite set. Therefore, under AC
{}_{<\aleph_{0}}
,
|S_{\leq n}(A)|<|\textup{Part}_{\textup{fin}}(A)|
for any Dedekind infinite set
A
.
Shen and Yuan also showed in ZF that
|S_{\leq n}(A)|<|A|!
for any infinite set
A
and any natural number
n
such that
|A|>n>0
(cf.
[
10
, Corollary 3.22]
).

In this paper, we investigate relationship between
|\textup{Part}_{\textup{fin}}(A)|
and
|S_{\leq n}(A)|
for infinite sets
A
. We show, in ZF, that
|\textup{Part}_{\textup{fin}}(A)|\nleq|S_{\leq n}(A)|
for any infinite set
A
and
|S_{\leq n}(A)|<|\textup{Part}_{\textup{fin}}(A)|
if
A
can be linearly ordered, while the statement “
|S_{n}(A)|\leq|\textup{Part}_{\textup{fin}}(A)|
for all infinite sets
A
” is not provable in ZF for
n\geq 3
. In addition, while the result in
[
7
, Theorem 3.2]
showed that “
|S_{n}(A)|\leq|S_{n+1}(A)|
for all infinite sets
A
”, where
n>1
, is not provable in ZF, we show that it is provable if the subscript
n+1
is replaced by any natural number
m
such that
m\geq 2n
.

2.
Results in ZF

In this section, we shall work in ZF without AC. We write
|A|
for the
cardinality
of a set
A
. For sets
A
and
B
, we say
|A|=|B|
if there is an explicit bijection from
A
onto
B
,
|A|\leq|B|
if there is an explicit injection from
A
to
B
, and
|A|<|B|
if
|A|\leq|B|
but
|A|\neq|B|
. A set
A
is
Dedekind-infinite
if
\aleph_{0}\leq|A|
, otherwise
A
is
Dedekind-finite
.

Throughout, let
n
be a natural number.

Apart from the notations introduced earlier, for a set
A
, let

(1)

[A]^{n}=\{X\subseteq A:|X|=n\}
,

(2)

\textup{m}(\varphi)=\{x\in A:\varphi(x)\neq x\}
where
\varphi
is a permutation on
A
,

(3)

(a_{0};a_{1};\ldots;a_{n})
, where
a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
are distinct elements of
A
, denote the cyclic permutation on A such that

a_{0}\mapsto a_{1}\mapsto\ldots\mapsto a_{n}\mapsto a_{0}.

First, we shall show that
|\textup{Part}_{\textup{fin}}(A)|\nleq|S_{\leq n}(A)|
for any infinite set
A
. The following facts are needed for the proof.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
2
, Theorem 5.19]

For any infinite ordinal
\alpha
,
|\alpha|=|\textup{fin}(\alpha)|
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For any infinite ordinal
\alpha
, there is an infinite ordinal
\beta
such that we can construct a bijection between
\alpha
and
3\cdot\beta
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha
be an infinite ordinal. Then
\alpha=3\cdot\beta+\gamma
for some infinite ordinal
\beta
and some
\gamma<3
. It is clear if
\gamma=0
. Otherwise, define
f\colon 3\cdot\beta\to\alpha
by

\displaystyle f(n)

\displaystyle=3\cdot\beta+n\quad

\displaystyle\text{ for }n<\gamma,

\displaystyle f(n+\gamma)

\displaystyle=n

\displaystyle\text{ for }n<\omega,

\displaystyle f(\xi)

\displaystyle=\xi

\displaystyle\text{ for }\omega\leq\xi<3\cdot\beta.

We can see that
f
is bijective as desired.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

For any natural number
k
, if
k\geq 2^{2n}
, where
n>0
, then
|\textup{Part}_{\textup{fin}}(k)|>|S_{\leq n}(k)|
.

Proof.

For any natural number
k>0
, since for each
X\in\mathcal{P}(k)\setminus\{\emptyset,k\}
,
\{X,k\setminus X\}
is a partition of
k
and
\{k\}
is also a partition of
k
, there are at least
2^{k-1}
partitions of
k
. Thus
|\textup{Part}_{\textup{fin}}(k)|\geq 2^{k-1}>k^{n}
for all
k\geq 2^{2n}
. Since each permutation in
S_{\leq n}(k)
can be obtained by permuting
n
elements chosen from
k
, for any
k\geq 2^{2n}
, we have

|S_{\leq n}(k)|\leq{k\choose n}n!=k(k-1)\ldots(k-n+1)\leq k^{n}<|\textup{Part}_{\textup{fin}}(k)|.

∎

Theorem 2.4
.

For any infinite set
A
,
|\textup{Part}_{\textup{fin}}(A)|\nleq|S_{\leq n}(A)|
.

Proof.

Let
A
be an infinite set. It is trivial if
n\leq 1
. Suppose there is an injection
F\colon\textup{Part}_{\textup{fin}}(A)\rightarrow S_{\leq n}(A)
where
n\geq 2
. We shall show that for any infinite ordinal
\alpha
, we can construct a one-to-one sequence of members of
\textup{Part}_{\textup{fin}}(A)
with length
\alpha
, which contradicts Hartogs’ Theorem.

In order to construct such one-to-one sequence with length
\omega
, we shall construct a family
\{A_{i}:i\in\omega\}\subseteq\textup{fin}(A)
such that
A_{i}\subsetneq A_{i+1}
for all
i\in\omega
.

Pick
A_{0}\subseteq A
such that
|A_{0}|=2^{2n}
. Suppose we have already defined a finite set
A_{i}
such that
A_{0}\subseteq A_{i}\subseteq A
. By Lemma
2.3
,

\left|\textup{Part}^{(A)}_{\textup{fin}}(A_{i})\right|=\left|\textup{Part}_{\textup{fin}}(A_{i})\right|>\left|S_{\leq n}(A_{i})\right|=\left|S^{(A)}_{\leq n}(A_{i})\right|,

where

\displaystyle\textup{Part}^{(A)}_{\textup{fin}}(A_{i})

\displaystyle=\{\Pi\cup[A\setminus A_{i}]^{1}:\Pi\in\textup{Part}_{\textup{fin}}(A_{i})\}\text{\ and}

\displaystyle S^{(A)}_{\leq n}(A_{i})

\displaystyle=\{\varphi\cup\textup{id}_{A\setminus A_{i}}:\varphi\in S_{\leq n}(A_{i})\}.

Let
B_{i}=F\left[\textup{Part}^{(A)}_{\textup{fin}}(A_{i})\right]\setminus S^{(A)}_{\leq n}(A_{i})
. Since
F
is injective,
\emptyset\neq B_{i}\subseteq S_{\leq n}(A)
. Moreover,
\textup{m}(\varphi)\not\subseteq A_{i}
for all
\varphi\in B_{i}
. Note that
B_{i}
is finite since
\textup{Part}^{(A)}_{\textup{fin}}(A_{i})
is finite. We define

A_{i+1}=A_{i}\cup\bigcup\{\textup{m}(\varphi):\varphi\in B_{i}\}.

Then
A_{i+1}\supsetneq A_{i}
and
A_{i+1}
is still a finite subset of
A
.

Now, for each
n\in\omega
, let

P_{n}=\{A_{n}\}\cup[A\setminus A_{n}]^{1}.

We can see that
\langle P_{0},P_{1},\ldots,P_{n},\ldots\rangle_{\omega}
is a one-to-one sequence as desired.

Next, assume there is a one-to-one sequence
\langle\Pi_{0},\Pi_{1},\ldots,\Pi_{i},\ldots\rangle_{\alpha}
of members of
\textup{Part}_{\textup{fin}}(A)
where the length
\alpha
is an infinite ordinal.

For each
i<\alpha
, let
\varphi_{i}=F(\Pi_{i})
and
A_{\alpha}=\bigcup\{\textup{m}(\varphi_{i}):i<\alpha\}
.

Define an equivalence relation
\sim
on
A_{\alpha}
by

x\sim y\text{ if and only if }\forall i<\alpha(x\in\textup{m}(\varphi_{i})\leftrightarrow y\in\textup{m}(\varphi_{i})).

Obviously,
\left|[x]_{\sim}\right|\leq n
for all
x\in A_{\alpha}
. Next, we shall show that there is a bijection between
\{[x]_{\sim}:x\in A_{\alpha}\}
and
\alpha
by using the idea from the proof of
[
3
, Theorem 3]
.

For each
x\in A_{\alpha}
and
\mu\leq\alpha
, define

D_{x,\mu}=\bigcap\{\textup{m}(\varphi_{i}):i<\mu\text{ and }x\in\textup{m}(\varphi_{i})\},

where
D_{x,\mu}=A_{\alpha}
if
x\notin\textup{m}(\varphi_{i})
for all
i<\mu
and define

g_{x}=\{\iota<\alpha:x\in\textup{m}(\varphi_{\iota})\text{ and }D_{x,\iota+1}\subsetneq D_{x,\iota}\}.

It is easy to see that, for any
x,y\in A_{\alpha}
,
x\sim y
implies
g_{x}=g_{y}
. For the converse, we can see that for any
x,y\in A_{\alpha}
, if
\nu<\alpha
is the least ordinal such that
x\in\textup{m}(\varphi_{\nu})
but
y\notin\textup{m}(\varphi_{\nu})
, then
D_{x,\nu+1}\subsetneq D_{x,\nu}=D_{y,\nu}=D_{y,\nu+1}
, which implies
\nu\in g_{x}\setminus g_{y}
, so
g_{x}\neq g_{y}
. Note that for each
x\in A_{\alpha}
, since
D_{x,\mu}
is finite for any
\mu\leq\alpha
,
g_{x}\in\textup{fin}(\alpha)
. Thus, by sending each
[x]_{\sim}
to
g_{x}
, we get an injection from
K=\{[x]_{\sim}:x\in A_{\alpha}\}
to
\textup{fin}(\alpha)
. As
|\textup{fin}(\alpha)|=|\alpha|
, we can construct an injection from
K
to
\alpha
. So
K
has a well order induced by
\alpha
with an order type, say
\gamma
. Hence, there is a bijection
p\colon K\rightarrow\gamma
. Since
K
is infinite, so is
\gamma
.
Note that, since
F
is injective, for each
i<\alpha
,
\{j<\alpha:\textup{m}(\varphi_{i})=\textup{m}(\varphi_{j})\}
has at most
n!
elements.
Thus, the map
q\colon\alpha\rightarrow\textup{fin}(\gamma)\times\gamma
defined by

q(i)=(\{p([x]_{\sim}):x\in\textup{m}(\varphi_{i})\},k_{i}),

where
i
is the
k_{i}
th ordinal in the set
\{j<\alpha:\textup{m}(\varphi_{i})=\textup{m}(\varphi_{j})\}
, is an injection.
Since
|\textup{fin}(\gamma)\times\gamma|=|\gamma|
, we obtain an injection from
\alpha
to
\gamma
.
Since
\gamma\leq\alpha
, by the Cantor-Bernstein Theorem, we get a bijection between
\alpha
and
\gamma
, and thus we can construct a bijection
h\colon\alpha\rightarrow K
.

By Lemma
2.2
, there are an infinite ordinal
\beta
and a bijection
t\colon 3\cdot\beta\rightarrow\alpha
. So
H=h\circ t\colon 3\cdot\beta\rightarrow K
is a bijection.

Next, we shall construct
\Pi_{\alpha}\in\textup{Part}_{\textup{fin}}(A)
which is distinct from
\Pi_{i}
for all
i<\alpha
.

Let us fix
\delta<\beta
and consider the following four partitions of
\bigcup\{H(3\cdot\delta+j):j<3\}
:

\displaystyle C_{0}^{\delta}

\displaystyle=\{H(3\cdot\delta),H(3\cdot\delta+1)\cup H(3\cdot\delta+2)\},

\displaystyle C_{1}^{\delta}

\displaystyle=\{H(3\cdot\delta+1),H(3\cdot\delta+2)\cup H(3\cdot\delta)\},

\displaystyle C_{2}^{\delta}

\displaystyle=\{H(3\cdot\delta+2),H(3\cdot\delta)\cup H(3\cdot\delta+1)\},

\displaystyle C_{3}^{\delta}

\displaystyle=\{\textstyle{\bigcup\{H(3\cdot\delta+j):j<3\}}\}.

Pick the least
m<4
such that
C_{m}^{\delta}\not\subseteq\Pi_{t(3\cdot\delta+j)}
for all
j<3
and write
C_{\delta}
for this
C_{m}^{\delta}
. After
C_{\delta}
’s are obtained for all
\delta<\beta
, we define

\Pi_{\alpha}=\bigcup\{C_{\delta}:\delta<\beta\}\cup[A\setminus A_{\alpha}]^{1}.

Note that
\bigcup\{C_{\delta}:\delta<\beta\}\in\textup{Part}_{\textup{fin}}(A_{\alpha})
and so
\Pi_{\alpha}\in\textup{Part}_{\textup{fin}}(A)
.

For each
\delta<\beta
and
j<3
, we have that

C_{\delta}\subseteq\Pi_{\alpha}
but
C_{\delta}\not\subseteq\Pi_{t(3\cdot\delta+j)}
,
so
\Pi_{\alpha}\neq\Pi_{t(3\cdot\delta+j)}
, which means
\Pi_{\alpha}
is distinct from
\Pi_{i}
for all
i<\alpha
.
Then we obtain a one-to-one sequence of members of
\textup{Part}_{\textup{fin}}(A)
with length
\alpha+1
.

We can see that the sequence constructed by the above process is an extension of the sequence previously constructed. Thus, we can define a sequence whose length is a limit ordinal as the union of all sequences constructed earlier.
∎

Corollary 2.5
.

For any infinite set
A
,
|S_{\leq 2}(A)|<|\textup{Part}_{\textup{fin}}(A)|
.

Proof.

This follows from Theorem
2.4
since for any nonempty set
A
, the map
F\colon S_{\leq 2}(A)\to\textup{Part}_{\textup{fin}}(A)
defined by

F(\varphi)=\begin{cases}\{\textup{m}(\varphi)\}\cup[A\setminus\textup{m}(\varphi)]^{1}&\text{if }\textup{m}(\varphi)\neq\emptyset\\
[A]^{1}&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

is injective.
∎

It follows from the results in
[
8
, Theorem 2.9]
and
[
9
, Theorem 3.7]
that if AC
{}_{<\aleph_{0}}
is assumed, then
|S_{\leq n}(A)|<|\textup{Part}_{\textup{fin}}(A)|
for any Dedekind-infinite set
A
. Note that AC
{}_{<\aleph_{0}}
is weaker than the Ordering Principle which states that “every set can be linearly ordered” (cf.
[
5
]
and
[
6
, page 104]
) but the statement “every infinite set is Dedekind-infinite” is independent from the Ordering Principle (cf.
[
5
]
). However, we obtain the same result for infinite linearly ordered sets.

Theorem 2.6
.

For any infinite linearly ordered set
A
,
|S_{\leq n}(A)|<|\textup{Part}_{\textup{fin}}(A)|
.

Proof.

Let
A
be an infinite set with a linear order
\lhd
. By Theorem
2.4
, it suffices to show that
|S_{\leq n}(A)|\leq|\textup{Part}_{\textup{fin}}(A)|
.
The result is trivial for
n<2
. Assume
n\geq 2
. Define
g\colon S_{\leq n}(A)\rightarrow S_{\leq n}(n)
as follows:

For
\varphi\in S_{\leq n}(A)
with
\textup{m}(\varphi)=\{a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell-1}\}
where
a_{0}\lhd a_{1}\lhd\ldots\lhd a_{\ell-1}
, define

g(\varphi)=\{(x,y)\in\ell\times\ell:(a_{x},a_{y})\in\varphi\}\cup\textup{id}_{n\setminus\ell}.

Let
p=|S_{\leq n}(n)|
,
f\colon S_{\leq n}(n)\rightarrow p
be a bijection,
D=\{X\subseteq A:1\neq|X|\leq n\}
, and define
h\colon S_{\leq n}(A)\rightarrow D\times p
by

h(\varphi)=(\textup{m}(\varphi),f(g(\varphi))).

To see that
h
is injective, suppose
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in S_{\leq n}(A)
are such that
\varphi_{1}\neq\varphi_{2}
and
\textup{m}(\varphi_{1})=\textup{m}(\varphi_{2})=\{a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell-1}\}
where
a_{0}\lhd a_{1}\lhd\ldots\lhd a_{\ell-1}
. Since
\varphi_{1}\neq\varphi_{2}
, there is some
(x,y)\in\ell\times\ell
such that
(a_{x},a_{y})\in(\varphi_{1}\setminus\varphi_{2})\cup(\varphi_{2}\setminus\varphi_{1})
, which implies
g(\varphi_{1})\neq g(\varphi_{2})
, and hence
f(g(\varphi_{1}))\neq f(g(\varphi_{2}))
since
f
is injective.

Next, we shall construct an injection
F\colon D\times p\rightarrow\textup{Part}_{\textup{fin}}(A)
. First, fix
p(n+1)^{2}
members of
A
and divide them into pairwise disjoint family
\{B^{i}_{j}\subseteq A:i\leq n\text{ and }j<p\}
such that
|B^{i}_{j}|=n+1
for each
i,j
. For each
(X,j)\in D\times p
, define

F(X,j)=\begin{cases}\{X,B^{k}_{j}\}\cup[A\setminus(X\cup B^{k}_{j})]^{1}&\text{if }X\neq\emptyset,\\
\{B^{k}_{j}\}\cup[A\setminus B^{k}_{j}]^{1}&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where
k=\min\{i\leq n:B^{i}_{j}\cap X=\emptyset\}
.
We can see that
F
is injective, so
F\circ h\colon S_{\leq n}(A)\rightarrow\textup{Part}_{\textup{fin}}(A)
is an injection.
∎

From
[
7
, Theorem 3.2]
, we know that “
|S_{n}(A)|\leq|S_{n+1}(A)|
for any infinite set
A
” is not provable in ZF for
n>1
. Surprisingly, the statement is provable when
n+1
is replaced by some large enough natural numbers.

Theorem 2.7
.

For any infinite set
A
and any natural number
m
, if
m\geq 2n
, where
n>1
, then
|S_{n}(A)|\leq|S_{m}(A)|
.

Proof.

Let
A
be an infinite set and
m
be a natural number such that
m\geq 2n
, where
n>1
. First, we assume
m>2n
. Fix a sequence of
m
distinct members of
A
, say
\langle y_{0},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m-1}\rangle
. For each
\varphi\in S_{n}(A)
, define a permutation
F_{\varphi}\in S_{m-n}(A)
by

F_{\varphi}=(x_{0};x_{1};\ldots;x_{m-n-1}),

where
x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{m-n-1}
are the first
m-n
entries of
\langle y_{0},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m-1}\rangle
which are not in
\textup{m}(\varphi)
and define
G\colon S_{n}(A)\rightarrow S_{m}(A)
by

G(\varphi)=\varphi\circ F_{\varphi}.

Observe that for each
\varphi\in S_{n}(A)
,
\textup{m}(\varphi)\cap\textup{m}(F_{\varphi})=\emptyset
and, as
|\textup{m}(F_{\varphi})|=m-n>n=|\textup{m}(\varphi)|
,
F_{\varphi}
is the only largest cycle in
G(\varphi)
. To see that
G
is injective, let
\varphi_{1}
,
\varphi_{2}\in S_{n}(A)
be such that
G(\varphi_{1})=G(\varphi_{2})
. Then their largest cycles are the same, which means
F_{\varphi_{1}}=F_{\varphi_{2}}
. So
\varphi_{1}=G(\varphi_{1})\circ F_{\varphi_{1}}^{-1}=G(\varphi_{2})\circ F_{\varphi_{2}}^{-1}=\varphi_{2}
.

Now, assume
m=2n
.
Fix an
n(n+1)
-element subset of
A
, say
B=\{x^{i}_{j}:i\leq n\text{ and }j<n\}
. Let
B_{i}=\{x^{i}_{0},x^{i}_{1},\ldots,x^{i}_{n-1}\}
and
\chi_{i}=(x^{i}_{0};x^{i}_{1};\ldots;x^{i}_{n-1})\in S_{n}(A)
for each
i\leq n
.

For each
\varphi\in S_{n}(A)
, if there is some (unique)
i\leq n
such that
\textup{m}(\varphi)=B_{i}
, then let
N_{\varphi}=i+1
(modulo
n+1
), otherwise let
N_{\varphi}=\min\{i\leq n:\textup{m}(\varphi)\cap B_{i}=\emptyset\}
. Define
G\colon S_{n}(A)\rightarrow S_{2n}(A)
by

G(\varphi)=\varphi\circ\chi_{N_{\varphi}}.

Observe that for each
\varphi\in S_{n}(A)
,
\textup{m}(\varphi)\cap\textup{m}(\chi_{N_{\varphi}})=\emptyset
and
|\textup{m}(\varphi)|=|\textup{m}(\chi_{N_{\varphi}})|=n
, so
G(\varphi)\in S_{2n}(A)
. To show that
G
is injective, suppose
\varphi,\psi\in S_{n}(A)
are such that
G(\varphi)=G(\psi)
.

Suppose
\varphi
and
\psi
are cycles with
\textup{m}(\varphi)=B_{p}
and
\textup{m}(\psi)=B_{q}
for some
p,q\leq n
. Then each of
G(\varphi)
and
G(\psi)
is a product of two disjoint cycles with length
n
. Suppose
\varphi\neq\psi
. Then
\varphi=\chi_{N_{\psi}}
and
\psi=\chi_{N_{\varphi}}
by the uniqueness of the decompositions of
G(\varphi)
and
G(\psi)
. From
\varphi=\chi_{N_{\psi}}
, we have
\textup{m}(\varphi)=B_{N_{\psi}}
. By the definition of
N_{\varphi}
, we have that
N_{\varphi}=N_{\psi}+1
(modulo
n+1
). Similarly,
N_{\psi}=N_{\varphi}+1
(modulo
n+1
). So
0=2
(modulo
n+1
), which is impossible since
n\geq 2
.

For the remaining cases, we may assume
\psi
is not a cycle or
\textup{m}(\psi)\neq B_{i}
for all
i\leq n
. Since
\chi_{N_{\varphi}}
is a cycle and
\textup{m}(\chi_{N_{\varphi}})=B_{N_{\varphi}}
,
\chi_{N_{\varphi}}\neq\psi
. Thus
\chi_{N_{\varphi}}=\chi_{N_{\psi}}
which implies
\varphi=\psi
.
∎

3.
Consistency Results and Summary

We have shown, in ZF, that if
X
is an infinite linearly ordered set, then
|S_{\leq n}(X)|<|\textup{Part}_{\textup{fin}}(X)|
(Theorem
2.6
). For arbitrary infinite sets
X
, we show that
|S_{\leq 2}(X)|<|\textup{Part}_{\textup{fin}}(X)|
(Corollary
2.5
) and
|\textup{Part}_{\textup{fin}}(X)|\nleq|S_{\leq n}(X)|
(Theorem
2.4
). Now, we shall show that the latter statement is the best possible result in ZF for arbitrary infinite sets
X
and for
n\geq 3
.

We shall use permutation models, which are models of ZFA, set theory with atoms. This theory admits objects which are not sets, called
urelements
or
atoms
. We provide sufficient details as follows:

Let
A
be an infinite set of atoms and
\mathcal{G}
be a group of permutations on
A
. Define
V_{0}=A
,
V_{\alpha+1}=\mathcal{P}(V_{\alpha})\cup V_{\alpha}
,
V_{\gamma}=\bigcup_{\alpha<\gamma}V_{\alpha}
for limits
\gamma
, and
V=\bigcup_{\alpha\in\textup{ON}}V_{\alpha}
. Each
\pi\in\mathcal{G}
is extended to a permutation on
V
so that
\pi x=x
whenever
x
is a
pure set
, a set whose transitive closure contains no atoms. For each
x\in V
, let
\textup{fix}_{\mathcal{G}}(x)=\{\pi\in\mathcal{G}:\pi y=y\text{\ for all\ }y\in x\}
and
\textup{sym}_{\mathcal{G}}(x)=\{\pi\in\mathcal{G}:\pi x=x\}
. For a normal ideal
I
on
A
, a set
E\in I
is a
support
of
x
if
\textup{fix}_{\mathcal{G}}(E)\subseteq\textup{sym}_{\mathcal{G}}(x)
. Given a normal ideal
I
on
A
, we define
\mathcal{V}=\{x\in V:x\text{\ has a support and\ }x\subseteq\mathcal{V}\}
. The class
\mathcal{V}
, which is determined by
A
,
\mathcal{G}
, and
I
, is called a
permutation model
. For more details, see
[
6
, Chapter 4]
.
We shall use the basic Fraenkel model
\mathcal{V}_{F_{0}}
which is a permutation model with a countably infinite set
A
of atoms, the group
\mathcal{G}
of all permutations on
A
, and the normal ideal
\textup{fin}(A)
.

In the following, we assume
n\geq 3
and for a set
X
, let
C_{n}(X)=\left\{\varphi\in S_{n}(X):\varphi\text{\ is a cycle}\right\}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

\mathcal{V}_{F_{0}}\vDash|C_{n}(A)|\nleq|\textup{Part}(A)|
.

Proof.

Suppose to the contrary that there is an injection
F:C_{n}(A)\rightarrow\textup{Part}(A)
with a finite support
E
. Pick
n
distinct elements
a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-1}
in
A\setminus E
. Define
\pi=(a_{0};a_{1};\ldots;a_{n-1})
and let
\Pi=F(\pi)
. Since
\pi
fixes all members of
E
and
\pi\pi=\pi
,

\pi\Pi=\pi(F\pi)=\pi F(\pi\pi)=F(\pi)=\Pi.

For
a,b\in A
, we say that
a\sim_{\Pi}b
whenever there is some
X\in\Pi
such that
a,b\in X
, and write
[a]_{\Pi}
for
\{c:a\sim_{\Pi}c\}
.

Case 1.
There are some distinct
i,j<n
such that
a_{i}\sim_{\Pi}a_{j}
.

Since
\pi\Pi=\Pi
, we have
\pi a_{i}\sim_{\Pi}\pi a_{j}
.
So there is some
P\in\Pi
such that
\pi a_{i},\pi a_{j}\in P
. Define
\rho=(\pi a_{i};\pi a_{j})
. Since
\textup{m}(\rho)=\{\pi a_{i},\pi a_{j}\}\subseteq P\setminus E
,
\rho F=F
and
\rho\Pi=\Pi
. Note that
\rho\pi\in C_{n}(A)
. Hence

F(\rho\pi)=\rho F(\rho\pi)=\rho(F\pi)=\rho\Pi=\Pi=F(\pi).

However,
\rho\pi\neq\pi
since
n\geq 3
. This contradicts the injectivity of
F
.

Case 2.
There are no distinct
i,j<n
such that
a_{i}\sim_{\Pi}a_{j}
.

Suppose there is some
i<n
such that
[a_{i}]_{\Pi}\neq\{a_{i}\}
, i.e.
\{a_{i}\}\subsetneq[a_{i}]_{\Pi}
. Let
X=[a_{i}]_{\Pi}\setminus\{a_{i}\}
. Then
\emptyset\neq X\subseteq A\setminus\{a_{k}:k<n\}
and so
\pi
fixes
X
pointwise. Since
\pi\Pi=\Pi
,
X\subseteq[a_{i}]_{\Pi}\cap[\pi a_{i}]_{\Pi}
but
a_{i}\not\sim_{\Pi}\pi a_{i}
, a contradiction. Thus
[a_{j}]_{\Pi}=\{a_{j}\}
for all
j<n
.
Let
\rho=(a_{0};a_{1})
. Then
\rho\Pi=\Pi
and hence, as in the previous case,
F(\rho\pi)=F(\pi)
while
\rho\pi\neq\pi
, contradicting the injectivity of
F
.
∎

From the above theorem, we have that “
|C_{n}(X)|\nleq|\textup{Part}(X)|
for some infinite set
X
” holds in
\mathcal{V}_{F_{0}}
which is a model of ZFA. This statement can be transferred to ZF by the Jech-Sochor First Embedding Theorem (cf.
[
6
, Theorem 6.1]
). As a result, “
|C_{n}(X)|\leq|\textup{Part}(X)|
for all infinite sets
X
” is not provable in ZF, provided ZF is consistent.
Since
C_{n}(X)\subseteq S_{n}(X)\subseteq S_{\leq n}(X)
and
\textup{Part}_{\textup{fin}}(X)\subseteq\textup{Part}(X)
for any set
X
,
C_{n}(A)
in the above theorem can be replaced by
S_{n}(A)
and
S_{\leq n}(A)
, and
\textup{Part}(A)
by
\textup{Part}_{\textup{fin}}(A)
as well. Therefore we can conclude that the best possible result for relationships between
S_{\leq n}(X)
and
\textup{Part}_{\textup{fin}}(X)
provable in ZF for arbitrary infinite sets
X
and for
n\geq 3
is that
|\textup{Part}_{\textup{fin}}(X)|\nleq|S_{\leq n}(X)|
.

For relationships among
S_{n}(X)
’s for infinite sets
X
, where
n>1
, it has been shown in
[
7
, Theorem 3.2]
that “
S_{n}(X)\leq S_{n+1}(X)
for all infinite sets
X
” is not provable in ZF. However, we show in Theorem
2.7
that the statement is provable if the subscript
n+1
is replaced by any natural number
m
such that
m\geq 2n
. We are still wondering whether the subscript
n+1
can be replaced by
2n-1
or not. This is left open for future research.

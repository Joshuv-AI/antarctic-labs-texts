Large-time behavior of unbounded solutions of the viscous Hamilton-Jacobi equation: quadratic and subquadratic cases
Alexander Quaas and Andrei Rodríguez-Paredes

Abstract
. We determine the large-time behavior of unbounded solutions for the so-called viscous Hamilton Jacobi equation , u t − Δ ​ u + | D ​ u | m = f ⁡ ( x ) u_{t}-\Delta u+|Du|^{m}=f(x) , in the quadratic and subquadratic cases (i.e., for 1 < m ≤ 2 1<m\leq 2 ), with a particular focus on allowing arbitrary growth at infinity for f f and the prescribed initial data. The lack of a comparison principle for the associated ergodic problem is overcome by proving that a generalized simplicity holds for sub- and supersolutions of the ergodic problem. Moreover, as the uniqueness of solutions of the parabolic problem remains open in the current setting, our result on large-time holds for any solution, even if multiple solutions exist.



Keywords:
Hamilton-Jacobi equations, viscous Hamilton-Jacobi equation, unbounded solutions, large-time behavior, ergodic behavior, viscosity solutions.

1.
Introduction

The following work deals with the parabolic
viscous Hamilton-Jacobi Equation
,

(1)

\displaystyle u_{t}-\Delta u+|Du|^{m}=f(x)

\displaystyle{}\quad\textrm{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,+\infty),

(2)

\displaystyle u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)

\displaystyle{}\quad\textrm{in }\mathbb{R}^{N},

in the the
subquadratic
and
quadratic
cases, i.e. when
1<m\leq 2
. The functions
f,u_{0}
are continuous and assumed bounded from below, but not from above. Thus, in this setting, solutions to (
1
)-(
2
) are in general unbounded. Further assumptions on
f,u_{0}
are stated as needed.

Our main interest is determining the large-time behavior of solutions to (
1
)-(
2
) for data—
u_{0}
and
f
—as general as possible, particularly with the most general growth conditions at infinity.

We recall that the problem of large-time behavior for (
1
) set on a
bounded
domain (along with the necessary boundary data) is addressed in Tabet
[
24
]
for the superquadratic case, then by this same author in collaboration with Barles and Porretta in
[
7
]
for the subquadratic case. While some key ideas from these works remain useful in the present setting, there is considerable difficulty in obtaining similar results for unbounded solutions over an unbounded domain.

There are several works that address the large-time behavior of solutions of (
1
) set in the whole space in the context of weak solutions, but by necessity these work within the class of bounded solutions; we refer the reader to
[
9
,
10
,
12
,
14
,
16
,
20
]
and the works cited therein, though this list is by no means exhaustive.

For unbounded solutions over the whole space, Ichihara
[
17
]
obtains results on large-time behavior for a class of equations which includes the model (
1
), for all
m>1
, by combining probabilistic and PDE techniques. Additionally, these results make use of precise growth assumptions for both
f
and
u_{0}
: roughly, it is assumed in
[
17
]
that
f(x)\approx|x|^{m^{*}}
, where
m^{*}=\frac{m}{m-1}
(the conjugate exponent of
m
) and that
u_{0}
has at most polynomial growth.

The results of
[
17
]
show that the large-time behavior of solutions of (
1
) is determined by the associated
ergodic problem
,

(
EP
)

\lambda-\Delta\phi+|D\phi|^{m}=f(x)\quad\textrm{in }\mathbb{R}^{N},

also studied therein, where both
\lambda
and
\phi
are unknown. More precisely, it is shown in
[
17
]
that the solution of (
1
) behaves like
\phi(x)+\lambda t
as
t\to+\infty
, where
(\lambda,\phi)
is the unique solution pair of (
EP
) within the class of bounded-from-below functions with at most polynomial growth (here,
\phi
is unique up to an additive constant). This result if commonly referred to as
ergodic large-time behavior
, and is in accordance with the bounded domain case.

Given the importance of the ergodic problem, we comment with some detail on some of the previous results in the unbounded setting. Also, we will sometimes write “(
EP
)
λ
” to stress the dependence of the problem on the constant
\lambda
; in particular, when a given value for
\lambda
is considered.

The results of
[
17
]
were extended by Barles and Meireles
[
6
]
in terms of the generality allowed for the data, though only the model equation (
1
) is addressed. In particular, in the subquadratic case, it is shown that uniqueness of solutions for (
EP
) holds if
f(x)\approx|x|^{\alpha}
—where
\alpha>0
is now arbitrary—and
Df
behaves accordingly (see
[
6
]
, Theorem 3.10 and Proposition 3.7).

In contrast, in a recent work by Arapostathis, Biswas and Caffarelli
[
1
]
, uniqueness of solutions for (
EP
) is obtained assuming only a technical hypothesis which relates
f
and
Df
, but still allows
f
to have arbitrary growth—see
(H2)
below. This is accomplished by means of the uniqueness of a measure related to the underlying dynamics of the problem. However, no comparison principle is proved for (
EP
), and in fact no information is provided on the behavior of
sub-
and
supersolutions
. These are crucial elements towards obtaining large-time behavior for (
1
) in our approach and in many of the cited works.

On this matter, we have found that a
generalized simplicity
holds for
sub-
and
supersolutions
of (
EP
). We use “simplicity” here in the spirit of similar results for e.g. the principal eigenfunction of second-order elliptic operators (see e.g.
[
11
]
); and “generalized” since the property holds for also for sub- and supersolutions, and not merely for solutions. These results are proved in Propositions
1.2
and
2.11
and, as expected, are an essential part of the proof of our main result, Theorem
1.3
.

Returning to the question of large-time behavior, the authors studied this problem in the superquadratic case (
m>2
) in
[
8
]
, in collaboration with G. Barles. In
[
8
]
it is shown that ergodic large-time behavior occurs assuming minimal hypotheses on
f
and
u_{0}
, although in this case we are able to prove a very general comparison principle for the parabolic problem, and a similar result was already available for the ergodic problem. Regarding the data, for
f
it is assumed that there exists a nondecreasing function
\varphi:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
and constants
\alpha,c>0
such that for all
r\geq 0
,
c^{-1}r^{\alpha}\leq\varphi(r)
, and for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
r=|x|
,
c^{-1}\varphi(r)-c\leq f(x)\leq c(\varphi(r)+1)
. In other words,
f
is controlled by some radial function that has
at least
some polynomial growth, but is not bounded in any way by above.

In the present setting, we are able to obtain the result on large-time behavior without the assumption of a “minimum growth rate” for
f
. We discuss this further after providing the precise statements of our results.

1.1.
Assumptions and main results

Our main result establishes, for
1<m\leq 2
, that the large-time behavior of solutions of (
1
)-(
2
) is determined by the solutions of (
EP
). To this end, we require the following assumptions on
f
.

(H1)

f\in W^{1,\infty}_{\textit{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is coercive; i.e.,
f(x)\to+\infty
as
|x|\to+\infty
.

(H2)

\limsup\limits_{x\to+\infty}\frac{|Df(x)|^{\frac{1}{2m-1}}}{|f(x)|^{\frac{1}{m}}}<+\infty.

Remark 1.1
.

Assumption
(H2)
may be interpreted as preventing
f
from having large oscillation at infinity. The fact that
\frac{1}{2m-1}<\frac{1}{m}
for all
m>1
allows
f
to have arbitrary growth.

We also introduce the
generalized ergodic constant
,

(3)

\lambda^{*}=\sup\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\ |\ \text{\eqref{erg_prob}${}_{\lambda}$ has a }C^{2}\textrm{-subsolution}\}.

The relevant properties of
\lambda^{*}
are provided in Section
2
.

The following is a version of the simplicity results for (
EP
); for more results of this type, see Section
2
below.

Proposition 1.2
.

If
\chi
and
v\in USC(\mathbb{R}^{N})
are respectively a solution and a subsolution of (
EP
)
{}_{\lambda^{*}}
, both bounded from below, then there exists
c\in\mathbb{R}
such that
v(x)=\chi(x)+c
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

The proof of Proposition
1.2
follows from combining an approximation of (
EP
) over bounded domains with an appropriately chosen perturbation of solutions; this perturbation relies on the homogeneity of the gradient term. In the end, these computations allow us to exploit the coercivity of
f
and “order solutions at infinity”. It is remarkable that this result can be obtained from such elementary techniques.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume
u_{0}\in C(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is bounded from below and assumptions
(H1)
and
(H2)
hold. Then, there exists a constant
\hat{c}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

u(\cdot,t)-\lambda^{*}t\to\phi\text{ locally uniformly over }\mathbb{R}^{N},

where
u=u(x,t)
is any solution of (
1
)-(
2
),
\lambda^{*}
is defined by (
3
) and
\phi
is the unique solution of (
EP
)
{}_{\lambda^{*}}
given by Proposition
1.2
.

Remark 1.4
.

Recall that in the case
1<m\leq 2
there is no comparison principle available for neither the ergodic nor the parabolic problems. In particular, the uniqueness of solutions for the parabolic problem—in the generality of the current setting—remains an open problem. Nevertheless, our result on large time behavior holds for any solution of (
1
).

The proof of our main result, Theorem
1.3
, begins by showing that a half-relaxed limit for
u-\lambda^{*}t
converges to the solution
\phi
of (
EP
) over a subsequence
t_{k}\to+\infty
; it is here that Proposition
1.2
is essential. From here we construct parabolic barriers for some new initial condition related to the previous subsequence, again using some of the ideas behind the proof of the simplicity results.

Notation and organization of the article.
The notation used in the article is entirely standard. Section
2
contains preliminary results concerning (
1
) and (
EP
), including the proof of Proposition
1.2
as well as others which are collected from previous works for convenience. Our main result is proved in Section

2.
Preliminaries

We start by establishing some existence results for the parabolic problem.

Theorem 2.1

(Existence of solutions)
.

Assume

Proof.

The proof is essentially the same as that of Proposition 3 in
[
8
]
for the case

u_{0}
in the is due to the lack of a comparison result for (

Let
\{T_{R}\}
be a sequence such that where

for all

\displaystyle u_{t}-\Delta u+|Du|^{m}=f(x)

\displaystyle{}\quad\textrm{ in }B_{R}\times(0,T_{R}),

(5)

\displaystyle u_{t}-\Delta u+|Du|^{m}\geq f(x)

\displaystyle{}\quad\textrm{ in }\partial B_{R}\times(0,T_{R}),

(6)

\displaystyle u(\cdot,0)=u_{0}

\displaystyle{}\quad\textrm{ in }\overline{B}_{R}.

By the results of
[

It can be shown that if

Consider a fixed

Furthermore, using Theorem

Thus, by using the Ascoli-Arzela Theorem, we have the uniform convergence of

on

)-(

Lemma 2.6
.

Assume
(H1)
.

(i)

If
\chi
is a bounded-from-below supersolution of (
EP
), then

\lim\limits_{|x|\to+\infty}\frac{\chi(x)}{|x|}=+\infty.

(ii)

Let
t_{0}>0
. If
v
is a bounded-from-below supersolution of (
1
), then

\lim\limits_{|x|\to+\infty}\frac{v(x,t_{0})}{|x|}=+\infty.

Proof.

We will prove only
(ii)
, since part
(i)
is analogous. Define, for all
r\geq 0
,
\varphi(r)=\inf_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{r}}f
. Observe that
\varphi
is non-decreasing and, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
f(x)\geq\varphi(|x|)
. Furthermore, assumption
(H1)
implies
\varphi
is coercive. We will argue by contradiction.

Assume that there exists some
t_{0}>0
and a sequence
(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
|y_{n}|\to+\infty
as
n\to+\infty
such that
\nicefrac{{v(y_{n},t_{0})}}{{|y_{n}|}}
remains bounded as
n\to+\infty
. Since
\varphi
is coercive, this implies

(9)

\liminf\limits_{n\to+\infty}\frac{v(y_{n},t_{0})}{|y_{n}|\varphi(y_{n})^{\frac{1}{m}}}=0.

Define for
y\in B_{1}
,

v_{n}(y,s)=\frac{v(y_{n}+\frac{|y_{n}|}{2}y,t_{0}+\frac{|y_{n}|^{2}}{4}s)}{|y_{n}|\varphi(\frac{1}{2}|y_{n}|)^{\frac{1}{m}}}.

Formally,

satisfies

\displaystyle{}2^{2-m}|y_{n}|^{-1}\varphi(\frac{1}{2}|y_{n}|)^{\frac{1}{m}-1}\left(\partial_{t}v_{n}-\Delta v_{n}\right)\,+\,|Dv_{n}|^{m}

\displaystyle{}\quad=2^{-m}\varphi(\frac{1}{2}|y_{n}|)^{-1}f\left(y_{n}+\frac{|y_{n}|}{2}y\right)\geq 2^{-m}

where the last inequality follows from the definition of
\varphi
and the fact that
|y_{n}+\frac{|y_{n}|}{2}y|\geq|y_{n}|-\frac{|y_{n}|}{2}|y|=\frac{1}{2}|y_{n}|
; it is not hard to pass the computation onto a suitable test function. Writing
\epsilon_{n}=2^{2-m}|y_{n}|^{-1}\varphi(\frac{1}{2}|y_{n}|)^{\frac{1}{m}-1}
, we have
\epsilon_{n}\to 0
since
\varphi
is coercive and
1-\frac{1}{m}<0
, hence

(10)

\epsilon_{n}(\partial_{t}v_{n}-\Delta v_{n})\,+\,|Dv_{n}|^{m}\geq 2^{-m}-o_{n}(1)\quad\hbox{in }B_{1}\times(t_{0},+\infty),

where

as

.

In order to pass to the limit, we lack some

-bound on
v_{n}
. To overcome this difficulty, we set

\tilde{v}_{n}(y,s)=\min\left(v_{n}(y,s),K(1-|y|)\right)\quad\hbox{in }B_{1}\times(0,+\infty),

for some suitably large

. For
n
large enough, the concave function
y\mapsto K(1-|y|)
is also a supersolution of (
10
) and therefore so is
\tilde{v}_{n}
, as the minimum of two supersolutions.

Hence, the half-relaxed limit
\tilde{v}=\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}\,\!\!^{*}\,\tilde{v}_{n}
is well defined and, by stability, we have in the limit

that

|D\tilde{v}|^{m}\geq 2^{-m}\quad\textrm{in }B_{1}\times(0,+\infty),

in the viscosity sense. For all

\tilde{v}_{n}\geq 0
, hence also
\tilde{v}\geq 0
(recall that

may be assumed at the outset). Thus
\tilde{v}
is a supersolution of the eikonal equation,
|Du|^{m}=2^{-m}
in
B_{1}
with homogeneous boundary condition. The latter has the unique solution
\frac{1}{2}d(y,\partial B_{1})
. Therefore, by comparison,
\tilde{v}(y,s)\geq\frac{1}{2}d(y,\partial B_{1})
for all

. We remark that comparison holds up to

by the (more general) results of
[
13
]
.

Finally, using (

This implies

, a contradiction.

Lemma 2.7

(“Simplicity”)
.

Let
(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\phi})
denote the solution pair of (
EP
) obtained in Theorem

and

We will first establish the following

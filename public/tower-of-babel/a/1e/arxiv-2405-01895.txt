1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{D}(a;r):=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z-a|<r\}
and let
\mathbb{D}:=\mathbb{D}(0;1)
be the open unit disk in the complex plane
\mathbb{C}.
For a given simply connected domain
\Omega
containing the unit disk
\mathbb{D},
we denote
\mathcal{H}\left(\Omega\right)
a class of analytic functions on
\Omega
, and
\mathcal{B}\left(\Omega\right)
be the class of functions
f\in\mathcal{H}\left(\Omega\right)
such that
f\left(\Omega\right)\subseteq\mathbb{D}.
The Bohr radius for the family
\mathcal{B}\left(\Omega\right)
is defined to be the positive real number
B_{\Omega}\in(0,1)
given by (see
[
36
]
)

\displaystyle B_{\Omega}=\sup\{r\in(0,1):M_{f}(r)\leq 1\;\mbox{for all}\;f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{B}\left(\Omega\right),z\in\mathbb{D}\},

where
M_{f}(r):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}
is the associated majorant series for
f\in\mathcal{B}\left(\Omega\right)
. If
\Omega=\mathbb{D},
then it is well-known that
B_{\mathbb{D}}=1/3,
and it is described precisely as follows.

Theorem A
.

(see
[
26
]
)
If
f\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathbb{D}\right)
, then
M_{f}(r)\leq 1
for
0\leq r\leq 1/3
. The number
1/3
is best possible.

The inequality
M_{f}(r)\leq 1
, which is known as the classical Bohr inequality for
f\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathbb{D}\right)
, fails to hold for any
r>1/3
. In view of this, the constant
1/3
is best possible. This can be seen by considering the function
f_{a}(z)=(a-z)/(1-az)
and by taking
a\in(0,1)
such that
a
is sufficiently close to
1
. Theorem A was originally obtained by H. Bohr in
[
26
]
for
0\leq r\leq 1/6
. The optimal value
1/3
, which is called the Bohr radius for the disk case, was later established independently by M. Riesz, I. Schur, and F.W. Wiener. Over the past three decades there has been significant interest in Bohr-type inequalities. See
[
30
,

,

,

,

]
and the references there.

The Bohr radius has also been investigated by many researchers in various multidimensional spaces. For example, in
1997
, generalizing a classical one-variable theorem of Bohr, Boas and Khavinson
[
25
]
showed that if an
n
-variable power series has modulus less than
1
in the unit polydisc, then the sum of the moduli of the terms is less than
1
in the polydisc of radius
1/(\sqrt{3}n)
. The result of Boas and Khavinson generates later extensive research in multidimensional Bohr inequalities. For different aspects of multidimensional Bohr inequalities, we refer to the articles
[
14
,
10
,
41
,
29
,
37
,
58
,
54
,
51
,
53
]
and references therein.

Determination of the Bohr radius has been extended to various classes of function for domains beyond the unit disk. For example, in
[
8
]
, Ali and Ng investigated Bohr phenomenon in the Poincaré disk model of the hyperbolic plane and develops the hyperbolic Bohr inequality for the class of functions mapping
\mathbb{D}^{h}
to any concentric subdisk of
\mathbb{D}
, where
\mathbb{D}^{h}
is the unit disk in the hyperbolic disk model. As a consequence, the hyperbolic Bohr radius is obtained for analytic self-maps of the hyperbolic unit disk
\mathbb{D}^{h}
. The hyperbolic Bohr inequality is studied for the class of functions mapping
\mathbb{D}^{h}
into a given set lying in an open half-plane of the unit disk. In
[
2
]
Muhanna and Ali have studied Bohr phenomenon to three classes of analytic functions mapping the unit disk respectively into the right half-plane, the slit region, and to the exterior of the unit disk using the properties of the hyperbolic metric. In
[
16
]
, Bohr property of bases is established in the space of entire functions defined on a compact subset of
\mathbb{C}^{n},

n\geq 1
. In
[
44
]
, Kaptanoglu and Sadik have studied Bohr Radii for class of functions defined in a domain bounded by the ellipse. The value of the Bohr radius estimated for elliptical domains of small eccentricity also shows that these domains do not exhibit the Bohr Phenomenon when the eccentricity is large. The Bohr radius was obtained in
[
3
]
for analytic functions that map from the unit disk to the punctured unit disk, showing that the radius same as the classical Bohr radius
1/3
.

Finding the Bohr radius for a class of functions defined in an arbitrary domain is a difficult task and interesting also. In this paper, our aim is to establish generalized Bohr inequalities for the classes of analytic, and harmonic mappings defined in simply connected domain
\Omega_{\gamma}
into
\mathbb{C}.
The paper is organized as follows: In Section 2, we have studied generalized version of Bohr inequality and its sharpness for the class of analytic functions
f\in\mathcal{B}\left(\Omega_{\gamma}\right)
,
0\leq\gamma<1
with the help of a sequence
\{\varphi_{n}(r)\}^{\infty}_{n=0}
of non-negative continuous function in
[0,1)
such that the series
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(r)
converges locally uniformly on the interval
[0,1)
. By several examples, we show that our results represent twofold generalizations corresponding to those existing results which are obtained for the classes
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D})
and
\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{\gamma})
. In Section 3, convolution counterpart of Bohr radius on simply connected domain
\Omega_{\gamma}
is established which generalized the result on unit disk
\mathbb{D}
. In Section 4, we focus on a class of harmonic mappings, namely,
K
-quasiconformal sense-preserving harmonic mappings, and derive generalized Bohr inequalities based on existing results. The proof of the main results has been discussed in detail in each section.

2.
Generalization of the Bohr inequality and its sharpness for the class
\mathcal{B}\left(\Omega_{\gamma}\right)

In order to present our results in this section, we need to introduce some basic notations. Following the recent study of the Bohr inequality (see
[
50

\mathcal{F}
denote the set of all sequence
\varphi=\{\varphi_{n}(r)\}^{\infty}_{n=0}

\displaystyle\Phi_{N}(r):=\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(r)\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\mathcal{A}\left(f_{0},\varphi,r\right):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2n}\left(\dfrac{\varphi_{2n}(r)}{1+|a_{0}|}+\Phi_{2n+1}(r)\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\left(f_{0},r\right):=\left(\dfrac{1}{1+|a_{0}|}+\dfrac{r}{1-r}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2n}r^{2n}

R^{k}_{2,\gamma}
is the best possible.

Remark 4.2
.

Corollary
4.2
is a generalized version of the result
[
48
, Theore 1.2]
from unit disk
\mathbb{D}
to simply connected domain
\Omega_{\gamma}
.

Further for
k\to 1
which corresponds to the limiting case
K\to\infty
in Theorem
4.1
, we obtain the following result for sense-preserving harmonic mappings.

Corollary 4.3
.

Assume that
p\in(0,2]
and let
f=h+\overline{g}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}+\overline{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}}
be a harmonic mapping in
\Omega_{\gamma}
with
|h(z)|\leq 1
on
\Omega_{\gamma}.
If
h(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
and
g(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}
in
\mathbb{D}
and
f=h+\overline{g}
is sense-preserving in
\mathbb{D}
and
\{\varphi_{n}(r)\}\in\mathcal{F}
satisfies the inequality

\displaystyle 4\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(r)<p(\gamma+1)\varphi_{0}(r),

then the following sharp inequality holds:

\displaystyle|a_{0}|^{p}\varphi_{0}(r)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|\right)\varphi_{n}(r)\leq\varphi_{0}(r)\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;r\leq R^{\gamma,1}_{p,\varphi},

where
R^{\gamma,1}_{p,\varphi}
is the minimal positive root in
(0,1)
of the equation

\displaystyle 4\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(r)=p(\gamma+1)\varphi_{0}(r).

In the case when

\displaystyle 4\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(r)>p(\gamma+1)\varphi_{0}(r),

in some interval
(R^{\gamma,1}_{p,\varphi},R^{\gamma,1}_{p,\varphi}+\epsilon)
, the number
R^{\gamma,1}_{p,\varphi}
cannot be improved.

Our next aim is to find the Bohr inequality for
K
-quasiconformal, sense-preserving harmonic mappings, where the analytic part
h
is subordinated to some analytic function
\psi
. Our purpose is to explore the cases in which
\psi
is univalent and convex in
\mathbb{D}
. We present the following result.

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose that
f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}+\overline{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}}
is a
K
- quasiconformal sense-preserving harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
and
h\prec\psi,
where
\psi
is a univalent and convex function in
\mathbb{D}.
If
\{\varphi_{n}(r)\}\in\mathcal{F}
and satisfies the inequality

\displaystyle 2(1+k)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(r)<\varphi_{0}(r),

then the following sharp inequality holds:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\varphi,f}(r):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|)\varphi_{n}(r)\leq\mathrm{dist}\left(\psi(0),\partial\psi(\mathbb{D})\right)\varphi_{0}(r)\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;|z|=r\leq R_{\varphi},

where
k\in[0,1)
and
R_{\varphi}
is the minimal positive root in
(0,1)
of the equation

(4.4)

\displaystyle 2(1+k)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(r)=\varphi_{0}(r).

In the case when

\displaystyle 2(1+k)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(r)>\varphi_{0}(r),

in some interval
(R_{\varphi},R_{\varphi}+\epsilon)
, the number
R_{\varphi}
cannot be improved.

As a consequence of Theorem
4.2
, in particular, when
\varphi_{n}(r)=r^{n}
, we obtain the following corollary. This shows that our result generalizes
[
56
, Theorem 1]
.

Corollary 4.4
.

(see
[
56
, Theorem 1]
)
Suppose that
f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}+\overline{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}}
is a
K
-quasiconformal sense-preserving harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
and
h\prec\psi,
where
\psi
is a univalent and convex function in
\mathbb{D}.

Then the following sharp inequality holds:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|)r^{n}\leq\mathrm{dist}\left(\psi(0),\partial\psi(\mathbb{D})\right)\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;|z|=r\leq\dfrac{K+1}{5K+1}.

The constant
(K+1)/(5K+1)
is sharp.

The following lemmas will play key roles in proving the Theorems
4.1
and
4.2
.

Lemma 4.1
.

(see
[

]
)
Let
\{\varphi_{n}(r)\}^{\infty}_{n=0}
be a decreasing sequence of non negative functions in
[0,r_{\varphi})
, and
g(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n},

h(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
analytic in
\mathbb{D}
such that
|g^{\prime}(z)|\leq k|h^{\prime}(z)|
in
\mathbb{D}
and for some
k\in[0,1).
Then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|b_{n}|^{2}\varphi_{n}(r)\leq k^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}\varphi_{n}(r)\;\;\mbox{ for}\;\;r\in[0,r_{\varphi}).

Lemma 4.2
.

(see
[
32
,
56
]
)
Let
\psi
be an analytic univalent map from
\mathbb{D}
onto a convex domain
\Omega=\psi(\mathbb{D}).
Then

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}|\psi^{\prime}(0)|\leq\mathrm{dist}\left(\psi(0),\partial\Omega\right)\leq|\psi^{\prime}(0)|.

If
g(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}\prec\psi(z),
then

\displaystyle|b_{n}|\leq|\psi^{\prime}(0)|\leq 2\mathrm{dist}\left(\psi(0),\partial\Omega\right).

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

By the assumption, we see that
h
is analytic with
|h(z)|\leq 1
on
\Omega_{\gamma}
. In view of Lemma
4.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|b_{n}|^{2}\varphi_{n}(r)\leq k^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}\varphi_{n}(r).

By applying Lemma A and the well-known Schwarz inequality, a straightforward calculation yields that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|b_{n}|\varphi_{n}(r)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|b_{n}|^{2}\varphi_{n}(r)}\sqrt{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(r)}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{k^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}\varphi_{n}(r)}\sqrt{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(r)}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{k^{2}\left(\dfrac{(1-|a_{0}|^{2})}{1+\gamma}\right)^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(r)}\sqrt{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(r)}

\displaystyle\leq k\dfrac{(1-|a_{0}|^{2})}{1+\gamma}\Phi_{1}(r).

As a result, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\varphi,f}(r)

\displaystyle\leq a^{p}\varphi_{0}(r)+(1+k)\dfrac{(1-a^{2})}{1+\gamma}\Phi_{1}(r)

\displaystyle=\varphi_{0}(r)-(1-a^{p})\varphi_{0}(r)+(1+k)\dfrac{(1-a^{2})}{1+\gamma}\Phi_{1}(r)

\displaystyle=\varphi_{0}(r)+\frac{(1-a^{2})}{(1+\gamma)}\bigg((1+k)\Phi_{1}(r)-\bigg(\frac{1-a^{p}}{1-a^{2}}\bigg)(1+\gamma)\varphi_{0}(r)\bigg).

By considering the inequality (
2.8
), it can be seen that

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\varphi,f}(r)

\displaystyle\leq\varphi_{0}(r)+\frac{(1-a^{2})}{(1+\gamma)}\bigg((1+k)\Phi_{1}(r)-\frac{p}{2}(1+\gamma)\varphi_{0}(r)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\varphi_{0}(r)+\frac{(1-a^{2})}{2(1+\gamma)}\bigg(2(1+k)\Phi_{1}(r)-p(1+\gamma)\varphi_{0}(r)\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\varphi_{0}(r),

for
|z|=r\leq R^{\gamma,k}_{p,\varphi},
where
R^{\gamma,k}_{p,\varphi}
is the minimal positive root in
(0,1)
of the equation given by (
4.2
).

To show the sharpness part of the result, we consider the function
f_{a}(z)=h_{a}(z)+\overline{g_{a}(z)}
in
\Omega_{\gamma}
(see
[
35
, Theorem 4]
), where

h_{a}
is given by (
2.9
), and
a_{n}

(n\geq 0)
are given by (
2.10
) and

\displaystyle f_{a}(z)=k\lambda\left(h_{a}(z)-a_{0}\right).

Consequently, it becomes clear that

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\varphi,f_{a}}(r)

\displaystyle=\left(\dfrac{a-\gamma}{1-a\gamma}\right)^{p}\varphi_{0}(r)+(1+k\lambda)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{1-a^{2}}{a(1-a\gamma)}\left(\dfrac{a(1-\gamma)}{1-a\gamma}\right)^{n}\varphi_{n}(r)

\displaystyle=\varphi_{0}(r)+\left(\bigg(\dfrac{a-\gamma}{1-a\gamma}\bigg)^{p}-1\right)\varphi_{0}(r)

\displaystyle\quad+(1+k\lambda)\dfrac{1-a^{2}}{a(1-a\gamma)}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\dfrac{a(1-\gamma)}{1-a\gamma}\right)^{n}\varphi_{n}(r)

\displaystyle=\varphi_{0}(r)+\frac{(1-a)}{(1-\gamma)}\bigg(2(1+k)\Phi_{1}(r)-p(1+\gamma)\varphi_{0}(r)\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad+\bigg(p(1-a)\frac{1+\gamma}{1-\gamma}+\bigg(\frac{a-\gamma}{1-a\gamma}\bigg)^{p}-1\bigg)\varphi_{0}(r)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(1-a)}{(1-\gamma)}\bigg(-2(1+k)\Phi_{1}(r)+(1+k\lambda)\dfrac{(1+a)(1-\gamma)}{a(1-a\gamma)}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\dfrac{a(1-\gamma)}{1-a\gamma}\right)^{n}\varphi_{n}(r)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\varphi_{0}(r)+\frac{(1-a)}{(1-\gamma)}\bigg(2(1+k)\Phi_{1}(r)-p(1+\gamma)\varphi_{0}(r)\bigg)+(1-a)A_{a,k,\gamma}(r),

where

\displaystyle A_{a,k,\gamma}(r)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)}\bigg(-2(1+k)\Phi_{1}(r)+(1+k\lambda)\dfrac{(1+a)(1-\gamma)}{a(1-a\gamma)}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\dfrac{a(1-\gamma)}{1-a\gamma}\right)^{n}\varphi_{n}(r)\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad+\bigg(\frac{p(1+\gamma)}{1-\gamma}+\frac{1}{1-a}\left(\bigg(\frac{a-\gamma}{1-a\gamma}\bigg)^{p}-1\right)\bigg)\varphi_{0}(r).

Now taking
\lambda\to 1^{-}
, it is a simple task to verify that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{a\to 1^{-}}

\displaystyle\bigg(-2(1+k)\Phi_{1}(r)+(1+k)\dfrac{(1+a)(1-\gamma)}{a(1-a\gamma)}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\dfrac{a(1-\gamma)}{1-a\gamma}\right)^{n}\varphi_{n}(r)\bigg)=0

and

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{a\to 1^{-}}\frac{1}{1-a}\left(\bigg(\frac{a-\gamma}{1-a\gamma}\bigg)^{p}-1\right)\;\;\;\left(\frac{0}{0}\;\mbox{form}\right)

\displaystyle=-\lim\limits_{a\to 1^{-}}p\bigg(\frac{a-\gamma}{1-a\gamma}\bigg)^{p-1}\frac{1-\gamma^{2}}{(1-a\gamma)^{2}}\;\;\;\;\left(\mbox{by L'H$\hat{o}$pital's rule}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{p(1+\gamma)}{1-\gamma}.

In view of the above estimates with
\lambda\to 1^{-}
, by a standard computation, we see that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{a\to 1^{-}}A_{a,k,\gamma}(r)=\left(\frac{p(1+\gamma)}{1-\gamma}-\frac{p(1+\gamma)}{1-\gamma}\right)\varphi_{0}(r)=0.

Hence, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\varphi,f_{a}}(r)=\varphi_{0}(r)+\frac{(1-a)}{(1-\gamma)}\bigg(2(1+k)\Phi_{1}(r)-p(1+\gamma)\varphi_{0}(r)\bigg)+O((1-a)^{2})

as
a
and
\lambda
tend to
1^{-}
. Clearly, in view of (
2.4
), it is easy to see that

\mathcal{N}_{\varphi,f_{a}}(r)>\varphi_{0}(r)

when
a
and
\lambda
tend to
1^{-}
and
r\in\left(R^{\gamma,k}_{p,\varphi},R^{\gamma,k}_{p,\varphi}+\epsilon\right)
, which shows that the radius
R^{\gamma,k}_{p,\varphi}
cannot be improved further. This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.2
.

By the assumption
h\prec\psi
and
\psi(\mathbb{D})
is convex.
By the Lemma
4.2
, we have
|a_{n}|\leq 2d,
where
d:=\left(\psi(0),\partial\psi(\mathbb{D})\right).
Since
f=h+\overline{g}
is a
K
- quasiconformal sense-preserving harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
so that
|g^{\prime}(z)|\leq k|h^{\prime}(z)|
in
\mathbb{D},
where
k\in[0,1)
, in view of Lemma
4.1
, it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|b_{n}|^{2}\varphi_{n}(r)\leq k^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}\varphi_{n}(r)\leq 4d^{2}k^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(r)=4d^{2}k^{2}\Phi_{1}(r).

Consequently, the classical Schwarz inequality leads to the conclusion that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|b_{n}|\varphi_{n}(r)\leq\sqrt{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|b_{n}|^{2}\varphi_{n}(r)}\sqrt{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(r)}\leq\sqrt{4k^{2}d^{2}\Phi_{1}(r)}\sqrt{\Phi_{1}(r)}=2kd\Phi_{1}(r).

Thus, the inequality

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\varphi,f}(r)\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2d\varphi_{n}(r)+k\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2d\varphi_{n}(r)=2(1+k)d\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(r)\leq d\varphi_{0}(r)

holds for
|z|=r\leq R_{\varphi},
where
R_{\varphi}
is the minimal root in
(0,1)
of the equation (
4.4
). With this the desired inequality is established.

Next part of the proof is to show that the sharpness of the radius. Hence, we consider the function
f_{a}=h_{a}+\overline{g_{a}},
where

\displaystyle\psi(z)=h_{a}(z)=\dfrac{1}{1-z}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}z^{n}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;g_{a}^{\prime}(z)=k\lambda h_{a}^{\prime}(z),\;|\lambda|=1,\;z\in\mathbb{D}.

It is well-known that
d=\mathrm{dist}\left(\psi(0),\partial\psi(\mathbb{D})\right)=1/2
(see
[
56
]
) and a simple computation shows that

\displaystyle g_{a}(z)=\lambda k\dfrac{z}{1-z}=k\lambda\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}z^{n}.

For the function
f_{a},
we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\varphi,f_{a}}(r)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1+|\lambda|k)\varphi_{n}(r)=(1+k)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(r)

which is bigger than or equal to
\varphi_{0}(r)d=\varphi_{0}(r)/2
if, and only if,

\displaystyle 2(1+k)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(r)>\varphi_{0}(r),

in some interval
(R_{\varphi},R_{\varphi}+\epsilon).
This shows that the constant
R_{\varphi}
is sharp. This completes the proof.
∎

Recurrence relations of poly-Cauchy numbers by the r r -Stirling transform
Takao Komatsu

Abstract
We give some formulas of poly-Cauchy numbers by the r r -Stirling transform. In the case of the classical or poly-Bernoulli numbers, the formulas are with Stirling numbers of the first kind. In our case of the classical or poly-Cauchy numbers, the formulas are with Stirling numbers of the second kind. We also discuss annihilation formulas for poly-Cauchy number with negative indices. Keywords: Stirling transform, r r -Stirling numbers, poly-Cauchy numbers, annihilation formulas MR Subject Classifications: Primary 11B75; Secondary 11B73, 11B65, 11B37, 11B68, 05A19

Introduction

A rich number of results by several authors have been developed concerning the
combinatorial and analytical properties of a family of numbers and polynomials called poly-Bernoulli and poly-Cauchy numbers and polynomials, respectively. These new sequences have applications in Number Theory (for example, relations with the generalized zeta function and multiple zeta function) and Enumerative Combinatorics (for example, lonesum matrices and set partitions).
Recently, Ohno and Sasaki
[
16
,
17
]
establish some formulae (including annihilation formulae) of poly-Bernoulli numbers and polynomials by using
r
-Stirling numbers. We study those of poly-Cauchy numbers and polynomials. On the other hand, expressing Bernoulli numbers or their generalizations in terms of Stirling numbers and harmonic numbers has been an active research.

In this paper, we mainly focus on the
r
-Stirling transform (analogous to the well-known binomial transform) of the poly-Cauchy numbers of both kinds. Expressing poly-Cauchy numbers in terms of Stirling numbers and harmonic numbers is also an active research. Some formulae are related to shifted poly-Cauchy numbers.
We can find annihilation formulas for poly-Cauchy numbers with negative indices (analogous to the results given by Ohno and Sasaki for the poly-Bernoulli case). To obtain the results we use some combinatorial properties of the
r
-Stirling numbers of the second kind.

The binomial transform takes the sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
to the sequence
\{b_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
via the transformation

b_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}a_{k}

with its inverse

a_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}\binom{n}{k}b_{k}

(see, e.g.,
[
6
,
20
]
). For example, for Fibonacci numbers
F_{n}
, defined by
F_{n}=F_{n-1}+F_{n-2}
(
n\geq 3
) with
F_{1}=F_{2}=1
, we have
a_{n}=F_{n}\rightarrow b_{n}=F_{2n}
and
a_{n}=(-1)^{n}F_{n}\rightarrow b_{n}=-F_{n}
.
Similarly, the Stirling transform takes the sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
to the sequence
\{b_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
via the transformation

b_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left[n\atop k\right]a_{k}

(1)

with its inverse

a_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}\left\{n\atop k\right\}b_{k}

(2)

(see, e.g.,
[
20
]
).
Here, the (unsigned) Stirling numbers of the first kind
\left[n\atop k\right]
are defined by the coefficients of the rising factorial:

(x)^{(n)}=x(x+1)\cdots(x+n-1)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left[n\atop k\right]x^{k}\,.

The Stirling numbers of the second kind are expressed as

\left\{n\atop k\right\}=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}\binom{k}{i}(k-i)^{n}\,.

For example, by the Stirling transform we have
a_{n}=\ell^{n}\rightarrow b_{n}=(n+\ell-1)!/(\ell-1)!
(
\ell\geq 1
) and
a_{n}=(n)^{(l)}\rightarrow b_{n}=l!\left[n+1\atop l+1\right]
(
l\geq 0
).
Many more examples for binomial transforms and Stirling transforms can be seen in
[
19
]
.

There are many kinds of generalizations of the Stirling numbers. One of the most interesting ones is the
r
-Stirling number in
[
2
]
.
The main purpose of this paper is to yield the relations associated with the inverse and extended transformation of (

b_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left\{n\atop k\right\}_{r}a_{k}

a_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}\left[n\atop k\right]_{r}b_{k}\,,

(4)

-Stirling numbers, which are used in this paper, are mentioned in the next section.
There are some different definitions for the
r
-Stirling numbers of both kinds. They can defined combinatorially as follows.
The (unsigned)
r

\left[n\atop m\right]_{r}

\{1,\dots,n\}
into

are in different subsets.

Bernoulli number
B_{n}
with its various generalizations is one of the most interesting and most popular numbers in history. For every integer

\mathbb{B}_{n}^{(k)}
are defined by

\frac{{\rm Li}_{k}(1-e^{-x})}{1-e^{-x}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{B}_{n}^{(k)}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\,,

where

{\rm Li}_{k}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{n^{k}}

is the polylogarithm function. When

\mathbb{B}_{n}=\mathbb{B}_{n}^{(1)}
is the Bernoulli number, defined by

\frac{xe^{x}}{e^{x}-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{B}_{n}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

In

\sum_{j=0}^{\mu}(-1)^{j}\left[\mu+2\atop j+2\right]_{2}\mathbb{B}_{k}^{(n-j)}=0

\sum_{j=0}^{\mu}(-1)^{j}\left[\mu+2\atop j+2\right]_{2}\mathbb{B}_{n+j}^{(-k)}=0\,.

On the other hand, similar formulas with positive index have been studied

and their generalization. For example, we have

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left[n\atop j\right]\mathbb{B}_{j}^{(k)}=\frac{n!}{(n+1)^{k}}\quad(n\geq 1)

\sum_{j=0}^{n}\left[n+1\atop j+1\right]\mathbb{B}_{j}^{(k)}=n!H_{n+1}^{(k)}\quad(n\geq 0)\,,

where
H_{n}^{(k)}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(1/i^{k})
is the higher order harmonic number.
Some other simpler cases are given by

\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{j}\left[n+1\atop j+1\right]B_{j}=n!H_{n+1}\quad(n\geq 1)

(5)

(

\frac{x}{e^{x}-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

As the inverse function of

is
\log(1+x)
, Cauchy number

is also enough interesting in history.
In

The poly-Cauchy numbers

have an explicit expression in terms of the Stirling numbers of the first kind as

c_{n}^{(k)}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{n-\ell}}{(\ell+1)^{k}}\left[n\atop\ell\right]\quad(n\geq 1)\,.

(6)

When

\sum_{j=r}^{n}\left\{n\atop j\right\}_{r}c_{j}^{(k)}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}\frac{(-1)^{r-\ell}}{(n-r+\ell+1)^{k}}\left[r\atop\ell\right]

\sum_{j=r-1}^{n}\left\{n+1\atop j+1\right\}_{r}c_{j}^{(k)}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}(-1)^{r-\ell}\left[r\atop\ell\right]\sum_{i=0}^{n-r+\ell}\binom{n-r+\ell}{i}\frac{1}{(i+1)^{k}}\,.

In the case of negative indices, we have for

\sum_{l=0}^{k}\left\{n-1\atop n-l-1\right\}_{n-k-1}c_{n-l}^{(-k)}=0\,.

We also give their analogous results for the poly-Cauchy numbers of the second kind

2
The
r
-Stirling numbers

In this section, we mention some basic properties of the
r
-Stirling numbers, which will be used in this paper.

By using the
r
-Stirling numbers,
the original Stirling numbers can be expressed as

\left[n\atop m\right]=\left[n\atop m\right]_{0},\quad\left\{n\atop m\right\}=\left[n\atop m\right]_{0}\,,

and

\left[n\atop m\right]=\left[n\atop m\right]_{1},\quad\left\{n\atop m\right\}=\left[n\atop m\right]_{1}\quad(n>0)\,,

There exist recurrence relations as

\left[n\atop m\right]_{r}=(n-1)\left[n-1\atop m\right]_{r}+\left[n-1\atop m-1\right]_{r}\quad(n>r)

(7)

with

\left[n\atop m\right]_{r}=0\quad(n<r)\quad\hbox{and}\quad\left[n\atop m\right]_{r}=\delta_{m,r}\quad(n=r)\,,

and

\left\{n\atop m\right\}_{r}=m\left\{n-1\atop m\right\}_{r}+\left\{n-1\atop m-1\right\}_{r}\quad(n>r)

with

\left\{n\atop m\right\}_{r}=0\quad(n<r)\quad\hbox{and}\quad\left\{n\atop m\right\}_{r}=\delta_{m,r}\quad(n=r)\,,

where
\delta_{m,r}=1
(
m=r
);
0
(
m\neq r
).
Note that the orthogonal properties
[
2
, Theorem 5, Theorem 6]
hold between two kinds of
r
-Stirling numbers too. Namely,

\sum_{k}(-1)^{n-k}\left[n\atop k\right]_{r}\left\{k\atop m\right\}_{r}=\begin{cases}\delta_{m,n}&\text{if $n\geq r$};\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(8)

and

\sum_{k}(-1)^{n-k}\left[k\atop n\right]_{r}\left\{m\atop k\right\}_{r}=\begin{cases}\delta_{m,n}&\text{if $n\geq r$};\\
0&\text{otherwise}\,.\end{cases}

(9)

The ordinary generating functions of both kinds of
r
-Stirling numbers
[
2
, Corollary 9, Corollary 10]
are given by

\sum_{k}\left[n\atop k\right]_{r}z^{k}=\begin{cases}z^{r}(z+r)(z+r+1)\cdots(z+n-1)&\text{if $n\geq r\geq 0$};\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

and

\sum_{k}\left\{k\atop m\right\}_{r}z^{k}=\begin{cases}\dfrac{z^{m}}{(1-rz)\bigl(1-(r+1)z\bigr)\cdots\bigl(1-mz\bigr)}&\text{if $m\geq r\geq 0$};\\
0&\text{otherwise}\,,\end{cases}

respectively.

This is our first main result.

Theorem 1
.

For integers
n
,

and

\sum_{j=r}^{n}\left\{n\atop j\right\}_{r}c_{j}^{(k)}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}\frac{(-1)^{r-\ell}}{(n-r+\ell+1)^{k}}\left[r\atop\ell\right]\,.

When

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\{n\atop j\right\}c_{j}^{(k)}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=2}^{n}\left\{n\atop j\right\}_{2}c_{j}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{n^{k}}+\frac{1}{(n+1)^{k}}\,,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=3}^{n}\left\{n\atop j\right\}_{3}c_{j}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{(n-1)^{k}}-\frac{3}{n^{k}}+\frac{1}{(n+1)^{k}}\,,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=4}^{n}\left\{n\atop j\right\}_{4}c_{j}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{6}{(n-2)^{k}}+\frac{11}{(n-1)^{k}}-\frac{6}{n^{k}}+\frac{1}{(n+1)^{k}}\,.

When

When

Proof of the main results

We need the following relations in order to prove Theorem

Lemma 1
.

Let
r
be any integer with
1\leq r\leq n
. Then for
1\leq m\leq n-r+1
,

\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\left[r\atop\ell\right]\left[n\atop r-\ell+m\right]_{r}=\left[n\atop m\right]\,,

and for

\sum_{\ell=1}^{n+1-\max\{m,r\}}\left[r\atop m-n+r-1+\ell\right]\left[n\atop n-\ell+1\right]_{r}=\left[n\atop m\right]\,.

Remark.

There are some different ways to express the Stirling numbers of the first kind in terms of the
r
-Stirling numbers of the first kind by choosing different

. For example,

\displaystyle\left[6\atop 3\right]=225

\displaystyle=\left[2\atop 1\right]\left[6\atop 4\right]_{2}+\left[2\atop 2\right]\left[6\atop 3\right]_{2}

\displaystyle=1\cdot 71+1\cdot 154

\displaystyle=\left[3\atop 1\right]\left[6\atop 5\right]_{3}+\left[3\atop 2\right]\left[6\atop 4\right]_{3}+\left[3\atop 3\right]\left[6\atop 3\right]_{3}

\displaystyle=2\cdot 12+3\cdot 47+1\cdot 60

\displaystyle=\left[4\atop 1\right]\left[6\atop 6\right]_{4}+\left[4\atop 2\right]\left[6\atop 5\right]_{4}+\left[4\atop 3\right]\left[6\atop 4\right]_{4}

\displaystyle=6\cdot 1+11\cdot 9+6\cdot 20\,.

Proof.

According to the definition of the
r
-Stirling numbers of the first kind, the relation

\left[6\atop 3\right]=\left[4\atop 1\right]\left[6\atop 6\right]_{4}+\left[4\atop 2\right]\left[6\atop 5\right]_{4}+\left[4\atop 3\right]\left[6\atop 4\right]_{4}

can be explained as follows. The permutations of the set
\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}
with

(1)

6

are in distinct cycles). Then, the cycles including

are collected into the same group (permutation).

(1)(2)(3)(4)(\ldots)(\ldots)\Longrightarrow\bigl((1)(2)(3)(4)\bigr)\bigl(\ldots\bigr)\bigl(\ldots\bigr)

(2)

6
numbers are divided into 5 different cycles so that
1,2,3,4
are in distinct cycles. Then, 4 cycles including
1,2,3,4
are collected into 2 different groups (permutation).

\displaystyle(1\ldots)(2\ldots)(3\ldots)(4\ldots)(\ldots)

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\bigl((a\ldots)(b\ldots)\bigr)\bigl((c\ldots)(d\ldots)\bigr)\bigl(\ldots\bigr)

or

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\bigl((a\ldots)(b\ldots)(c\ldots)\bigr)\bigl(d\ldots\bigr)\bigl(\ldots\bigr)\,,

where
\{a,b,c,d\}=\{1,2,3,4\}

(3)

6
numbers are divided into 4 different cycles so that
1,2,3,4
are in distinct cycles. Then, 4 cycles including
1,2,3,4
are collected into 3 different groups (permutation).

(1\ldots)(2\ldots)(3\ldots)(4\ldots)\Longrightarrow\bigl((a\ldots)(b\ldots)\bigr)\bigl(c\ldots\bigr)\bigl(d\ldots\bigr)

where
\{a,b,c,d\}=\{1,2,3,4\}

In general, the permutations of the set

with

,
n

(1\ldots)(2\ldots)\cdots(r\ldots)\underbrace{(\ldots)\cdots(\ldots)}_{m-\ell}\\

where

(1\ldots)(2\ldots)\cdots(r\ldots)\underbrace{(\ldots)\cdots(\ldots)}_{m-m^{\prime}-\ell}\\

where

Thus, equivalently, we shall prove that

\displaystyle c_{n}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=r-1}^{n}(-1)^{n-j}\left[n+1\atop j+1\right]_{r}\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}(-1)^{r-\ell}\left[r\atop\ell\right]\sum_{i=0}^{j-r+\ell}\binom{j-r+\ell}{i}\frac{1}{(i+1)^{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}\left[r\atop\ell\right]\sum_{m=\ell-1}^{n+\ell-r}(-1)^{n-m}\left[n+1\atop m-\ell+r+1\right]_{r}\sum_{i=0}^{m}\binom{m}{i}\frac{1}{(i+1)^{m}}\,.

Because of the expression in (
6
), it is sufficient to prove that for
n\geq r

\left[n\atop i\right]=\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}\left[r\atop\ell\right]\sum_{j=1}^{n-r+2}(-1)^{\ell+j-i}\binom{\ell+j-2}{i}\left[n+1\atop r+j-1\right]_{r}\,.

(12)

Put for
0\leq i\leq n
and
n\geq 1

a_{n,i}:=\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}\left[r\atop\ell\right]\sum_{j=1}^{n-r+2}(-1)^{\ell+j-i}\binom{\ell+j-2}{i}\left[n+1\atop r+j-1\right]_{r}\,.

First, for
i=0
and
r\geq 2
, by

\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}(-1)^{\ell}\left[r\atop\ell\right]=0\,,

we have

\displaystyle a_{n,0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}\left[r\atop\ell\right]\sum_{j=1}^{n-r+2}(-1)^{\ell+j}\binom{\ell+j-2}{0}\left[n+1\atop r+j-1\right]_{r}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n-r+2}(-1)^{j}\left[n+1\atop r+j-1\right]_{r}\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}(-1)^{\ell}\left[r\atop\ell\right]

\displaystyle=0\,.

For
i=0
and
r=1
,

a_{n,0}=\left[1\atop 1\right]\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{j+1}\left[n+1\atop j\right]=0\quad(n\geq 1)\,.

Next, let
i\geq 1
. For convenience, put

a_{n,i}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}\left[r\atop\ell\right]b_{n,i,\ell}

with for fixed
\ell

b_{n,i}=b_{n,i,\ell}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n-r+2}(-1)^{\ell+j-i}\binom{\ell+j-2}{i}\left[n+1\atop r+j-1\right]_{r}\,.

Then, using the recurrence relation (
7
),
by

\left[n\atop r+j-1\right]_{r}=0\quad(j=0,\,j=n-r+2)

and

\binom{\ell+j-1}{i}=\binom{\ell+j-2}{i}+\binom{\ell+j-2}{i-1}\,,

we have

\displaystyle b_{n,i}

\displaystyle=n\sum_{j=1}^{n-r+2}(-1)^{\ell+j-i}\binom{\ell+j-2}{i}\left[n\atop r+j-1\right]_{r}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{j=1}^{n-r+2}(-1)^{\ell+j-i}\binom{\ell+j-2}{i}\left[n\atop r+j-2\right]_{r}

\displaystyle=n\sum_{j=1}^{n-r+1}(-1)^{\ell+j-i}\binom{\ell+j-2}{i}\left[n\atop r+j-1\right]_{r}

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{j=0}^{n-r+1}(-1)^{\ell+j-i}\binom{\ell+j-1}{i}\left[n\atop r+j-1\right]_{r}

\displaystyle=(n-1)\sum_{j=1}^{n-r+1}(-1)^{\ell+j-i}\binom{\ell+j-2}{i}\left[n\atop r+j-1\right]_{r}

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{j=0}^{n-r+1}(-1)^{\ell+j-i}\binom{\ell+j-2}{i-1}\left[n\atop r+j-1\right]_{r}

\displaystyle=(n-1)b_{n-1,i}+b_{n-1,i-1}\,.

Hence,
a_{n,i}=(n-1)a_{n-1,i}+a_{n-1,i-1}
(
i\geq 1
).
Since
a_{n,i}
and
\left[n\atop i\right]
satisfy the same recurrence relation as (
7
) with
a_{n,0}=\left[n\atop 0\right]
(
n\geq 1
), the relation (
12
) holds.
∎

The proofs of Theorem
3
and Theorem
4
are similar to those of Theorem
1
and Theorem
2
, respectively, and omitted.

6
Shifted poly-Cauchy numbers and harmonic numbers

The right-hand sides of Theorem
1
and Theorem
3
can be written in terms of shifted poly-Cauchy numbers of both kinds, defined in
[
15
]
.

For integers
n\geq 0
and
k\geq 1
and a real number
\alpha>0
, the shifted poly-Cauchy numbers of the first kind
c_{n,\alpha}^{(k)}
are defined by

c_{n,\alpha}^{(k)}=\underbrace{\int_{0}^{1}\cdots\int_{0}^{1}}_{k}(x_{1}\cdots x_{k})^{\alpha}(x_{1}\cdots x_{k}-1)\cdots(x_{1}\cdots x_{k}-n+1)dx_{1}\dots dx_{k}

and can be expressed in terms of the Stirling numbers of the first kind as

c_{n,\alpha}^{(k)}=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\left[n\atop m\right]\frac{(-1)^{n-m}}{(m+\alpha)^{k}}\quad(n\geq 0,~k\geq 1)

(
[
15
, Theorem 1]
). When
\alpha=1
,
c_{n}^{(k)}=c_{n,1}^{(k)}
are the original poly-Cauchy numbers of the first kind.
Similarly, for integers
n\geq 0
and
k\geq 1
and a real number
\alpha>0
, the shifted poly-Cauchy numbers of the second kind
\widehat{c}_{n,\alpha}^{(k)}
are defined by

\widehat{c}_{n,\alpha}^{(k)}=\underbrace{\int_{0}^{1}\cdots\int_{0}^{1}}_{k}(-x_{1}\cdots x_{k})^{\alpha}(-x_{1}\cdots x_{k}-1)\cdots(-x_{1}\cdots x_{k}-n+1)dx_{1}\dots dx_{k}

and can be expressed in terms of the Stirling numbers of the first kind as

\widehat{c}_{n,\alpha}^{(k)}=(-1)^{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\left[n\atop m\right]\frac{1}{(m+\alpha)^{k}}\quad(n\geq 0,~k\geq 1)

(
[
15
, Proposition 2]
). When
\alpha=1
,
\widehat{c}_{n}^{(k)}=\widehat{c}_{n,1}^{(k)}
are the original poly-Cauchy numbers of the second kind.

By using the shifted poly-Cauchy numbers of the both kinds, the identities in Theorem
1
and Theorem
3
can be written in terms of shifted poly-Cauchy numbers of both kinds.

7
Annihilation formulas for poly-Cauchy numbers

Some annihilation formulas for poly-Bernoulli numbers with negative indices have been established in
[
16
,
17
]
. In this section, we show annihilation formulas for poly-Cauchy numbers with negative indices. In
[
14
]
, some annihilation formulas for poly-Cauchy numbers have been done, but the expressions are not so elegant. With the aid of the
r
-Stirling numbers, we can give more elegant forms.

Theorem 7
.

For
n\geq k+2
,

\sum_{l=0}^{k}\left\{n-1\atop n-l-1\right\}_{n-k-1}c_{n-l}^{(-k)}=0\,.

Remark.

For
k=1,2,3
we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\left\{n-1\atop n-1\right\}_{n-2}c_{n}^{(-1)}+\left\{n-1\atop n-2\right\}_{n-2}c_{n-1}^{(-1)}

\displaystyle=c_{n}^{(-1)}+(n-2)c_{n-1}^{(-1)}\quad(n\geq 3)\,,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\left\{n-1\atop n-1\right\}_{n-3}c_{n}^{(-2)}+\left\{n-1\atop n-2\right\}_{n-3}c_{n-1}^{(-2)}+\left\{n-1\atop n-3\right\}_{n-3}c_{n-2}^{(-2)}

\displaystyle=c_{n}^{(-2)}+(2n-5)c_{n-1}^{(-2)}+(n-3)^{2}c_{n-2}^{(-2)}\quad(n\geq 4)\,,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\left\{n-1\atop n-1\right\}_{n-4}c_{n}^{(-3)}+\left\{n-1\atop n-2\right\}_{n-4}c_{n-1}^{(-3)}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\{n-1\atop n-3\right\}_{n-4}c_{n-2}^{(-3)}+\left\{n-1\atop n-4\right\}_{n-4}c_{n-3}^{(-3)}

\displaystyle=c_{n}^{(-3)}+(3n-9)c_{n-1}^{(-3)}+(3n^{2}-21n+37)c_{n-2}^{(-3)}+(n-4)^{3}c_{n-3}^{(-3)}\quad(n\geq 5)\,.

Proof of Theorem
7
.

Since the
r
-Stirling numbers of the second kind can be expressed as

\left\{n+m\atop n\right\}_{r}=\sum_{r\leq i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{m}\leq n}i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{m}

(
[
2
, Theorem 8]
), together with
[
14
, Theorem 2.1]
, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{k}\left(\sum_{l+1\leq i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{l}\leq k+1}(n-i_{1})\cdots(n-i_{l})\right)c_{n-l}^{(-k)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{k}\left\{n-1\atop n-l-1\right\}_{n-k-1}c_{n-l}^{(-k)}\,.

∎

Similarly, concerning the poly-Cauchy numbers of the second kind
\widehat{c}_{n}^{(-k)}
, we have the following annihilation formula.

Theorem 8
.

For
n\geq k+2
,

\sum_{l=0}^{k}\left\{n+1\atop n-l+1\right\}_{n-k}\widehat{c}_{n-l}^{(-k)}=0\,.

Remark.

For
k=0,1,2
we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\left\{n+1\atop n+1\right\}_{n}\widehat{c}_{n}^{(0)}+\left\{n+1\atop n\right\}_{n}\widehat{c}_{n-1}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=\widehat{c}_{n}^{(0)}+n\widehat{c}_{n-1}^{(0)}\quad(n\geq 1)\,,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\left\{n+1\atop n+1\right\}_{n-1}\widehat{c}_{n}^{(-1)}+\left\{n+1\atop n\right\}_{n-1}\widehat{c}_{n-1}^{(-1)}+\left\{n+1\atop n-1\right\}_{n-1}\widehat{c}_{n-2}^{(-1)}

\displaystyle=\widehat{c}_{n}^{(-1)}+(2n-1)\widehat{c}_{n-1}^{(-1)}+(n-1)^{2}\widehat{c}_{n-2}^{(-1)}\quad(n\geq 2)\,,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\left\{n+1\atop n+1\right\}_{n-2}\widehat{c}_{n}^{(-2)}+\left\{n+1\atop n\right\}_{n-2}\widehat{c}_{n-1}^{(-2)}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\{n+1\atop n-1\right\}_{n-2}\widehat{c}_{n-2}^{(-2)}+\left\{n+1\atop n-2\right\}_{n-2}\widehat{c}_{n-3}^{(-2)}

\displaystyle=\widehat{c}_{n}^{(-2)}+(3n-3)\widehat{c}_{n-1}^{(-2)}+(3n^{2}-9n+7)\widehat{c}_{n-2}^{(-2)}+(n-2)^{3}\widehat{c}_{n-3}^{(-2)}\quad(n\geq 3)\,.

Proof of Theorem
8
.

By using
[
2
, Theorem 8]
) again, together with
[
14
, Theorem 3.1]
, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{k+1}\left(\sum_{l-1\leq i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{l}\leq k}(n-i_{1})\cdots(n-i_{l})\right)\widehat{c}_{n-l}^{(-k)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{k}\left\{n+1\atop n-l+1\right\}_{n-k}\widehat{c}_{n-l}^{(-k)}\,.

∎

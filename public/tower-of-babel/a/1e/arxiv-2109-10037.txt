A decomposition theorem for 0-cycles and applications
Rahul Gupta, Amalendu Krishna, Jitendra Rathore

Abstract




2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 14C25; Secondary 14F42, 19E15

Abstract.

We prove a decomposition theorem for the cohomological Chow group of 0-cycles on the
double of a quasi-projective
R_{1}
-scheme over a field along a closed subscheme, in
terms of the Chow groups, with and without modulus, of the scheme.
This yields a significant generalization of the decomposition theorem of
Binda-Krishna. As applications, we prove a moving lemma for Chow groups with modulus
and an analogue of
Bloch’s formula for 0-cycles with modulus on singular surfaces. The latter extends a
previous result of Binda-Krishna-Saito.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

Let
k
be a field and
X
an integral quasi-projective
R_{1}
-scheme
(i.e., a scheme which is regular in codimension one) of dimension
d\geq 1
over
k
.
Let
D\subset X
be a closed subscheme of
codimension one. Let
S_{X}
denote the join of two copies of
X
along
D
(see
§
2.3
).
Let
{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D)
denote the Chow group of 0-cycles with modulus
on
X
(see
[
16
, Definition 1.6]
)
and let
{\rm CH}_{0}(S_{X})
denote the cohomological (lci) Chow group of 0-cycles on

S_{X}
, introduced in
[
1
]
. Let
{\rm CH}^{F}_{0}(X)
denote the classical
(Borel-Moore type) homological
Chow group of 0-cycles on
X
(see
[
7
, Chapter 1]
).

When
k
is perfect and
X
is smooth over
k
,
a fundamental result of Binda-Krishna
[
1
]

connecting 0-cycles with and without modulus says that there exists a split
short exact sequence

(1.1)

0\to{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D)\to{\rm CH}_{0}(S_{X})\to{\rm CH}^{F}_{0}(X)\to 0.

This result plays a fundamental role in the current research on 0-cycles with
modulus on smooth varieties over perfect fields. Several new results have been found in the
subject of cycles with modulus using this exact sequence.
One reason for its usefulness is that it allows one to transport any
problem about 0-cycles with modulus on smooth schemes
to an analogous problem about 0-cycles without
modulus on schemes, albeit with mild singularities. In many cases, the latter
groups are well understood.

1.2.
Objective

The goal of this paper is to prove an analogue of (
1.1
)
when neither
k
is perfect nor
X
is smooth over
k
.
The motivation for this generalization comes from our desire to: (1) extend existing
results on 0-cycles with modulus on smooth schemes over perfect field to
those over imperfect fields, and (2) extend existing results on 0-cycles on
smooth schemes over perfect fields to singular schemes over such fields.

In this paper, we apply this generalization to prove
a new moving lemma for the Chow group of 0-cycles on
regular schemes, prove Bloch’s formula for 0-cycles with modulus on
singular schemes, and prove a moving lemma for 0-cycles on singular surfaces.
Further important applications are obtained in
[
3
]
(where
the 0-th Suslin homology is identified with the Chow group with 0-cycles with modulus
under some necessary conditions), and
[
14
]

(where a class field theory over finite fields is established for schemes with
singularities).

1.3.
Main result

Let
k
now be arbitrary and
X
an integral quasi-projective
R_{1}
-scheme of dimension
d\geq 1
over
k
.
Let
X^{o}
denote the regular locus of
X
. Note that
X^{o}
may not be smooth over
k

because the latter is not assumed to be perfect.
Let
D\subset X
be such that
D\cap X^{o}
is a Cartier
divisor (this may be empty).
We shall introduce a new 0-cycle group with modulus on
X
which we shall
denote by
{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D)
, following the classical notation.
It is an extension of the lci version
{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
of the Levine-Weibel Chow group
of
X
to the modulus world. Our motivation behind the introduction of
this group is the expectation that this would be useful in understanding the
already existing Chow group with modulus on non-proper smooth varieties, e.g.,
the regular loci of singular projective varieties.
(see, for instance,
[
14
]
whose main results are proven using this new Chow group
with modulus).
Let
{\rm CH}_{0}(X,D)
denote the lci Chow group of
X
relative to
D\cup X_{\rm sing}

(see
[
1
, § 3.3]
).
The new decomposition theorem reads as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

There is a split short exact sequence

0\to{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D)\to{\rm CH}_{0}(S_{X})\to{\rm CH}_{0}(X,D)\to 0.

When
X
is smooth over
k
, the group

{\rm CH}_{0}(X,D)
coincides with
{\rm CH}^{F}_{0}(X)
as a consequence of
the moving lemma for higher Chow groups by Bloch
(see
[
1
, Lemma 3.20]
). Hence, Theorem
1.1

is identical to (
1.1
) in this case.
The group
{\rm CH}_{0}(X,D)
coincides with
{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
for many singular
schemes as well (see Corollary
1.6
).

Remark 1.2
.

We emphasize to the reader that even when
X
is smooth over the base field
k
,
Theorem
1.1
asserts more than the decomposition theorem of
Binda-Krishna in that it does not assume
k
to be perfect.
This is achieved by virtue of some delicate new moving techniques
(see §
2.3
)
and new Bertini theorems proven recently by Ghosh-Krishna

[
8
]
.

1.4.
Application I : moving lemma for Chow group with modulus

The proof of Theorem
1.1
has the following consequence for the Chow group of
0-cycles with modulus (à la Kerz-Saito) on regular schemes.
Let us assume in Theorem
1.1
that
X
is regular and
D
is reduced.
Then we prove (a stronger version of) the following new moving lemma for
{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D)
.

Corollary 1.3
.

In the definition of
{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D)
, it suffices to consider only those
integral curves
C\subset X
not contained in
D
for which we can
assume that
C\cap D_{\rm sing}=\emptyset
and
\nu^{*}(D)
is regular,
where
\nu\colon C_{n}\to X
is the canonical map from the normalization of
C
.
If
k
is algebraically closed and
X
is affine or projective,
we can further assume that
C
is regular along
D
.

Remark 1.4
.

One may recall that if
X
is assumed to be complete in Corollary
1.3

(
D
still reduced), then there is a canonical surjection

\phi_{X|D}\colon{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D)\twoheadrightarrow H^{sus}_{0}(X\setminus D)
, where
the latter is the 0-th Suslin homology (see
[
2
, § 2.2]
or

[
9
, § 8]
). The reader is now warned that the above corollary
does not imply that
\phi_{X|D}
is an isomorphism. The question as to when
the latter happens, is the subject of
[
3
]
.

1.5.
Application II: Bloch’s formula and moving lemma for Chow group of
singular surfaces

Let us assume in Theorem
1.1
that
\text{\rm dim}(X)=2
.
Let
{\mathcal{K}}^{M}_{2,(X,D)}
be the kernel of the restriction map of Zariski sheaves
of Milnor
K
-groups
{\mathcal{K}}^{M}_{2,X}\twoheadrightarrow{\mathcal{K}}^{M}_{2,D}
on
X
. Every closed point

x\in X^{o}\setminus D
has a cycle class
cyc_{X}([x])\in H^{2}_{\rm zar}(X,{\mathcal{K}}^{M}_{2,(X,D)})
(e.g., see
[
15
, § 2]
).
This defines the well known cycle class map

{\mathcal{Z}}_{0}(X^{o}\setminus D)\to H^{2}_{\rm zar}(X,{\mathcal{K}}^{M}_{2,(X,D)})
.
As another application of Theorem
1.1
, we are able to prove the following
Bloch’s formulas for Chow groups, with and without modulus, for surfaces over
arbitrary fields.

Theorem 1.5
.

The cycle class map induces isomorphisms

{\operatorname{\rm cyc}}_{X}\colon{\rm CH}_{0}(X,D)\xrightarrow{\cong}H^{2}_{\rm zar}(X,{\mathcal{K}}^{M}_{2,X});

{\operatorname{\rm cyc}}_{X|D}\colon{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D)\xrightarrow{\cong}H^{2}_{\rm zar}(X,{\mathcal{K}}^{M}_{2,(X,D)}).

Binda-Krishna-Saito
[
4
]
had earlier proven the first isomorphism when

k
is perfect and
D=\emptyset
and the second isomorphism when
k
is perfect
and
X
is smooth over
k
.

One consequence of Bloch’s formula is the following moving lemma for

{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
D\subset X
be as in Theorem
1.1
.
Then the canonical map

{\rm CH}_{0}(X,D)\to{\rm CH}_{0}(X)

is an isomorphism in the following cases:

(1)

X
is smooth over
k
.

(2)

d=2
.

(3)

k
is algebraically closed and
X
is affine.

Remark 1.7
.

We shall show in the proof of Corollary
1.6
that its part (3) also holds
if
X
is projective, assuming a result from the yet unpublished paper

[
22
]
.

Note that the case (1) of the corollary is already known by

[
1
, Lemma 3.20]
.
So the new results are the other two cases.
The case (2) was earlier proven by Levine-Weibel
when
k
is algebraically closed and
X
is affine
(see
[
23
, Theorem 2.3]
).

The main application of Corollary
1.6
for us is that we can replace

{\rm CH}_{0}(X,D)
by
{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
in Theorem
1.1
when
X

satisfies one of the three conditions of the corollary.
We expect this is to be true in general
(see Question
5.7
) but do not know how to prove it.

1.6.
Overview of proofs

We now provide a brief outline of our proofs.
The proof of Theorem
1.1
presented here was undoubtedly inspired by the
previous decomposition theorem of Binda-Krishna
[
1
]
.
However, we had to make some critical advances in order to prove the new
decomposition theorem.

The first is to devise methods to get rid of the assumption that the base field
k
is
perfect.
Due to potential applications, some experts had asked the second author if this could be
possible.
In order to do so, we significantly refine the techniques of
[
1
]
.
This involves developing some delicate new moving techniques. We expect these new moving
techniques to be useful in future study of cycles over imperfect fields.
In particular, these also allow us to clarify some obscure steps in the proof of
the decomposition theorem of
[
1
]
.
Besides these movings, we employ the new Bertini
theorems proved recently by Ghosh-Krishna
[
8
]
over
arbitrary fields.

The second is to weaken the assumption that
X
is smooth over the base field.
Filling this part is very challenging and requires one to make very fine
choices of various intermediate hypersurface sections and curves that we
consider during the proofs. In particular, the reduction of the higher dimensions
case to surfaces becomes much subtle. §
2.3
provides many of
the new technical steps we need in order to achieve this reduction.

In §
2
, we review the definitions of the Levine-Weibel and
lci Chow groups of singular schemes. We then introduce the Chow group of
0-cycles with modulus on singular schemes.
In §
3
, we prove some results which show that the Chow group of
the double of a singular scheme along a closed subscheme has a very special
presentation. This is then used in §
4
to achieve the most
challenging piece of the proof of Theorem
1.1
. We also prove
a moving lemma in this section.
In §
5
, we complete the proofs of Theorem
1.1
and its
applications.

1.7.
Notations

Given a field
k
, a
k
-scheme will
mean a separated and essentially of finite type
k
-scheme.
We shall denote the category of such schemes by
{\operatorname{\mathbf{Sch}}}_{k}
.
We let
{\mathbf{Sm}}_{k}
denote the category of smooth essentially of finite type
k
-schemes.
A curve in this paper will mean a
k
-scheme of pure dimension one.

If
Y,Z\subset X
are two closed subschemes, then
Y\cap Z
will mean
the scheme theoretic intersection
Y\times_{X}Z
unless we say otherwise.
If
X
is reduced, we shall let
X_{n}
denote the normalization of
X
.
We shall let
k(X)
denote the total ring of quotients of
X
.
For a morphism
f\colon X^{\prime}\to X
of schemes and

D\subset X
a subscheme, we shall write
D\times_{X}X^{\prime}
as
f^{*}(D)
.
We shall let
{\mathcal{Z}}_{0}(X)
denote the free abelian group on the set of closed points on

X
.

2.
Levine-Weibel Chow group with modulus

We fix a field
k
of any characteristic.
We begin by very briefly recalling the definitions of the Levine-Weibel Chow group
and its improved version, the lci Chow group.
We refer to
[
1
, § 3]
for complete definitions.
If
X
is a reduced connected
k
-scheme of dimension
d
, then a point
x\in X

will be called a singular point if either it lies in an irreducible component of
X

of dimension
<d
or
{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}
is not a regular local ring.
We let
X_{\rm sing}
denote the locus of singular points of
X
. This is a closed
subset of
X
and we endow it with the reduced closed subscheme structure.
We let
X_{\rm reg}=X\setminus X_{\rm sing}
. If
X
is not necessarily reduced, we
shall let
X^{\dagger}=(X_{\rm red})_{\rm reg}
.

Throughout this paper, we shall use the following notations.
Once we have fixed a reduced
scheme
X\in{\operatorname{\mathbf{Sch}}}_{k}
, we shall write
X_{\rm reg}
as
X^{o}
.
For any subscheme
Z\subset X
, we shall write
Z^{o}=Z\cap X^{o}

and
Z^{\star}=Z\cup X_{\rm sing}
.
Keep in mind that
Z^{o}\neq Z_{\rm reg}
in general unless
Z=X
.

2.1.
Review of Levine-Weibel and lci Chow groups

Let
X
be a reduced and connected quasi-projective
k
-scheme of dimension
d\geq 1
.
Let
Y\subset X
be a closed subscheme which contains no
component of
X
of maximal dimension.

Let
C\subset X
be a curve. Recall (see
[
23
, § 1]
and

[
5
, § 2]
) that
C
is called Cartier relative to
Y

if no component of
C
lies in
Y^{\star}
, no embedded point of
C
lies away from

Y^{\star}
,
{\mathcal{O}}_{C,\eta}
is a field if
\overline{\{\eta\}}
is a component of
C

disjoint from
Y^{\star}
and,
C
is defined by a regular sequence at every point of

C\cap Y^{\star}
. If
Y\subset X_{\rm sing}
,
we shall simply say that
C
is a Cartier curve on
X
.
We let
k(C,C\cap Y^{\star})^{\times}
be the image of the natural map

{\mathcal{O}}^{\times}_{C,S}\to\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s}}{{\underset{i=1}{\oplus}}}{\mathcal{O}}^{\times}_{C,\eta_{i}}
,
where
\{\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{s}\}
is the set of generic points of
C
and

S
is the union of the closed subset

C\cap Y^{\star}
and the set of generic points
\eta_{i}
of
C

such that
\overline{\{\eta_{i}\}}
is
disjoint from
Y^{\star}
.

For
f\in k(C,C\cap Y^{\star})^{\times}
, we let
{\rm div}(f)=\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s}}{{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}}{\rm div}(f_{i})
, where

f_{i}
is the projection of
f
onto
{\mathcal{O}}^{\times}_{C,\eta_{i}}
, and

{\rm div}(f_{i})
is the divisor of the restriction of
f_{i}
to the maximal Cohen-Macaulay
subscheme
C_{i}
of
C
supporting
\eta_{i}
(see
[
5
, § 2]
).
If
C
is reduced, then
k(C,C\cap Y^{\star})^{\times}={\mathcal{O}}^{\times}_{C,S}
and
for
f\in{\mathcal{O}}^{\times}_{C,S}
,
{\rm div}(f)
is the sum of

{\rm div}(f_{i})
, where the sum runs through
the divisors (in the classical sense)
of the restrictions of
f
to the components of
C
.
If
f
is a rational function on two different curves
C
and
C^{\prime}
, we may
sometimes use notations such as
{\rm div}(f)_{C}
and
{\rm div}(f)_{C^{\prime}}
to make
clear as to where we consider the divisor of
f
.

Let
A\subset X
be a reduced closed subscheme such that

\text{\rm dim}(A)\leq d-2
. We let
{\mathcal{R}}^{LW}_{0}(X,Y;A)
denote the subgroup of

{\mathcal{Z}}_{0}(X\setminus(Y^{\star}\cup A))

generated by
{\rm div}(f)
, where
f\in k(C,C\cap Y^{\star})^{\times}
for
a Cartier curve
C
relative to
Y
which misses
A
.
The Levine-Weibel Chow group of
(X,Y;A)
is defined as the quotient

(2.1)

{\rm CH}^{LW}_{0}(X,Y;A)=\frac{{\mathcal{Z}}_{0}(X\setminus(Y^{\star}\cup A))}{{\mathcal{R}}^{LW}_{0}(X,Y;A)}.

We shall drop
Y
from our notations if
Y\subset X_{\rm sing}
.
Thus, the groups
{\mathcal{R}}^{LW}_{0}(X,X_{\rm sing};A)
and
{\rm CH}^{LW}_{0}(X,X_{\rm sing};A)
will be simply written
as
{\mathcal{R}}^{LW}_{0}(X;A)
and
{\rm CH}^{LW}_{0}(X;A)
, respectively.
When
A=\emptyset
, we drop
A
from our notations of the Chow groups.
Similar convention will be enforced on the lci Chow groups that we recall now.

One says that
C
a good curve relative to

Y
if
C
is reduced and there is a finite local complete intersection (lci)
morphism
\nu\colon C\to X
such that
\nu^{-1}(Y^{\star})
is nowhere dense in
C
.
If
Y\subset X_{\rm sing}
, we drop the adjective ‘relative to
Y
’
and simply say that
C
is a good curve.
We let
{\mathcal{R}}_{0}(X,Y;A)
be the subgroup of
{\mathcal{Z}}_{0}(X\setminus(Y^{\star}\cup A))

generated by
\nu_{*}({\rm div}(f))
, where

\nu\colon C\to X
is a good curve relative to
Y
whose image misses
A
and

f\in k(C,\nu^{-1}(Y^{\star}))^{\times}
.
The lci Chow group of 0-cycles of
(X,Y;A)
is defined as the quotient

(2.2)

{\rm CH}_{0}(X,Y;A)=\frac{{\mathcal{Z}}_{0}(X\setminus(Y^{\star}\cup A))}{{\mathcal{R}}_{0}(X,Y;A)}.

We have a canonical surjection

{\rm CH}^{LW}_{0}(X,Y;A)\twoheadrightarrow{\rm CH}_{0}(X,Y;A).

2.2.
Chow groups with modulus

Let
X\in{\operatorname{\mathbf{Sch}}}_{k}
be an integral scheme of dimension
d\geq 1
which is
regular in codimension one.
Let
D\subset X
be a closed subscheme such that
D^{o}
is a Cartier divisor on
X^{o}
.
We fix a reduced closed subscheme
A\subset X
such that

X_{\rm sing}\subset A
and
\text{\rm dim}(A)\leq d-2
.
Let
C
be an integral normal curve.
We shall say that a morphism
\nu\colon C\to X
is ‘admissible’ if
it is finite,
\nu(C)\not\subset D
and
\nu(C)\cap A=\emptyset
.
Given such a morphism and
f\in k(C)^{\times}
, we say that
f
has modulus
D

if it lies in
{\rm Ker}({\mathcal{O}}^{\times}_{C,E}\twoheadrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}^{\times}_{E})
, where
E=\nu^{*}(D)
.
We let
{\mathcal{R}}_{0}(X|D;A)
be the subgroup of
{\mathcal{Z}}_{0}(X^{o}\setminus(D\cup A))

generated by
\nu_{*}({\rm div}(f))
, where
\nu\colon C\to X
is admissible and

f\in k(C)^{\times}
has modulus
D
.
We let

(2.3)

{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D;A)=\frac{{\mathcal{Z}}_{0}(X^{o}\setminus(D\cup A))}{{\mathcal{R}}_{0}(X|D;A)}.

When
A=X_{\rm sing}
, we shall often write
{\mathcal{R}}_{0}(X|D;A)
and
{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D;A)
as

{\mathcal{R}}_{0}(X|D)
and
{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D)
, respectively.
Note that
{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D)
coincides with the original definition
(see
[
16
, Definition 1.6]
) of the Chow group with modulus when

X\in{\mathbf{Sm}}_{k}
.
We let
{\rm CH}^{\rm mod}_{0}(X|D;A)
be the quotient of
{\mathcal{Z}}_{0}(X^{o}\setminus(D\cup A))

by the subgroup
{\mathcal{R}}^{\rm mod}_{0}(X|D;A)
, where we allow only those admissible
morphisms
\nu\colon C\to X
which have the property that
\nu^{*}(D^{\dagger})

is regular.
We have then the canonical surjection

(2.4)

{\rm CH}^{\rm mod}_{0}(X|D;A)\twoheadrightarrow{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D;A).

We shall show that this map is in fact an isomorphism (see Corollary
4.6
).

In this paper, we shall consider a Levine-Weibel analogue of
{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D;A)
.
We let
{\mathcal{R}}^{LW}_{0}(X|D;A)
be the subgroup of
{\mathcal{Z}}_{0}(X^{o}\setminus(D\cup A))

generated by
{\rm div}(f)
, where:

(1)

C\subset X
is an integral curve with the property
that
C\not\subset D,\ C\cap A=\emptyset
;

(2)

C
is regular at every point of
E:=C\cap D
;

(3)

C\cap D^{\dagger}
is regular;

(4)

f\in{\rm Ker}({\mathcal{O}}^{\times}_{C,E}\twoheadrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}^{\times}_{E})
.

Note that the conditions (1) and (2) together
force
C
to be a Cartier curve on
X
relative to
D\cup A
. We let

{\rm CH}^{LW}_{0}(X|D;A)=\frac{{\mathcal{Z}}_{0}(X^{o}\setminus(D\cup A))}{{\mathcal{R}}^{LW}_{0}(X|D;A)}.

We shall write
{\rm CH}^{LW}_{0}(X|D;X_{\rm sing})
as
{\rm CH}^{LW}_{0}(X|D)
.
We shall call
{\rm CH}^{LW}_{0}(X|D;A)
the ‘Levine-Weibel Chow group with modulus’ of

(X,D)
. If
X
is projective over
k
, we let
{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D;A)_{0}
denote the
kernel of the degree map
{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D;A)\to{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Remark 2.1
.

We remark that just like the Levine-Weibel Chow group without modulus,

{\rm CH}^{LW}_{0}(X|D;A)
may not have
good functorial properties such as finite push-forward
(except for closed immersions). On the other hand,
it has some advantages over
{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D;A)
. For instance, it admits
cycle class map to
H^{d}_{\rm nis}(X,{\mathcal{K}}^{M}_{d,(X,D)})
when
k
is infinite.
At any rate, there is always a canonical surjection

(2.5)

\phi_{X|D}\colon{\rm CH}^{LW}_{0}(X|D;A)\twoheadrightarrow{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D;A),

which we expect to be an isomorphism
when
k
is infinite. We shall show in this
paper that this is indeed the case when
d=2
(see Remark
4.5
).

Remark 2.2
.

For a reduced closed subscheme
A\subset X
such that

X_{\rm sing}\subset A
and
\text{\rm dim}(A)\leq d-2
, we have a canonical map

{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D,A)\to{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D).

For a general
A
, we can not say much about this homomorphism. But it follows from Corollary
4.6
that the above map is an isomorphism when
A\subset X_{{\rm sing}}\cup D_{{\rm red}}
. Indeed, in the corollary, we shall prove the stronger claim that the composition of the maps

{\rm CH}^{\rm mod}_{0}(X|D;A)\twoheadrightarrow{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D,A)\to{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D).

is an isomorphism. A similar result for the Levine-Weibel Chow groups with modulus can be deduced from
Corollary
4.3
.

2.3.
The double and its Cartier curves

Let
D\subset X
be as in §
2.2
.
Recall from
[
1
, § 2.1]

that the double (or join) of
X
along
D
is the push-out
S(X,D):=X\amalg_{D}X
.
We shall also write
S(X,D)
as
S_{X}
. One knows that

(2.6)

\textstyle{D\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\iota}
\scriptstyle{\iota}

is a composition of monoidal transformations with centers away from

X_{\rm sing}
.
We conclude that

\alpha_{X}([\delta])\neq 0
. This finishes the proof.
∎

We end with the following question.

Question 5.7
.

Let
X
be an integral quasi-projective scheme of dimension
d\geq 2

over an infinite field which is regular in codimension one and let

Y\subset X
be a nowhere dense closed subscheme.
Is the canonical map

{\rm CH}^{LW}_{0}(X,Y)\to{\rm CH}^{LW}_{0}(X)

an isomorphism?

If the above question has a positive answer, then using the projective bundle
and pro-
\ell
-extension tricks we used above,
one can show that the same holds for the lci
Chow groups over any field. In this case, we can replace

{\rm CH}_{0}(X,D)
by
{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
in Theorem
1.1
.
In particular, we can replace
{\rm CH}_{0}(X,D)
by
{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
in
Theorem
1.1
when
d\leq 2
using Corollary
1.6
.

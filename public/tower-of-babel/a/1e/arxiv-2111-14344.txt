1.
Introduction

Throughout this article, we assume that
(\overline{K}(X)|K,v)
is an extension of valued fields, where
\overline{K}
is a fixed algebraic closure of
K
and
X
is an indeterminate. The extension
(K(X)|K,v)
satisfies the celebrated Abhyankar inequality:

(1)

\mbox{\rm rat rk}\,vK(X)/vK+\mbox{\rm trdeg}\,[K(X)v:Kv]\leq 1,

where
vK
and
Kv
denote respectively the value group and residue field of
(K,v)
and
\mbox{\rm rat rk}\,vK(X)/vK
is the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-dimension of the divisible hull
{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(vK(X)/vK)
. The above inequality is a consequence of [
References
, Chapter VI, §10.3, Theorem 1]. The extension
(K(X)|K,v)
is said to be
valuation algebraic
if we have strict inequality in (
1
). Otherwise, it is said to be
valuation transcendental
. Moreover, the extension is said to be
value transcendental
if we have
\mbox{\rm rat rk}\,vK(X)/vK=1
and
residue transcendental
if
\mbox{\rm trdeg}\,[K(X)v:Kv]=1
.

Several different objects have been introduced to study extensions of valuations to rational function fields. Minimal pairs of definition (cf. Section
3
) are central to the investigation of valuation transcendental extensions [cf.
References
,
References
,
References
,
References
]. The notion of implicit constant fields (cf. Section
6
) was introduced by Kuhlmann in [
References
] to construct
valuations with prescribed value groups and residue fields. The connections between these two notions were studied thoroughly in [
References
] and [
References
]. In particular, it has been observed in [
References
, Theorem 1.3] that given a minimal pair of definition
(a,\gamma)
for a valuation transcendental extension
(K(X)|K,v)
and an extension of
v
to
\overline{K(X)}
, we have

(K(a)\cap K^{r})^{h}\subseteq IC(K(X)|K,v)\subseteq K(a)^{h},

where
K^{r}
denotes the absolute ramification field of
(K,v)
and
K^{h}
denotes the henselization of
K
(cf. Section
2
).

Valuation algebraic extensions have been explored in detail in [
References
]. Following an idea of MacLane [
References
], the authors show that any valuation algebraic extension can be expressed as a limit of residue transcendental extensions. We employ similar observations to estimate the implicit constant fields of valuation algebraic extensions. When
(K(X)|K,v)
is valuation algebraic, we observe in Section
5
that there exist sequences
\{\gamma_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}\subseteq v(X-\overline{K})
and
\{a_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}\subseteq(\overline{K},v)
, where
v(X-\overline{K}):=\{v(X-a)\mid a\in\overline{K}\}
and
\lambda
is some limit ordinal, satisfying the following conditions for all
\nu<\lambda
:

•

\{\gamma_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is a cofinal well-ordered subset of
v(X-\overline{K})
,

•

v(X-a_{\nu})=\gamma_{\nu}
,

•

(a_{\nu},\gamma_{\nu})
is a minimal pair of definition for
v_{a_{\nu},\gamma_{\nu}}
over
K
,

•

v(X-a_{\nu})\geq v(X-\sigma a_{\nu})
for all
K
-conjugates
\sigma a_{\nu}
of
a_{\nu}
.

For each
\nu<\lambda
, we then choose
b_{\nu}\in\overline{K}
such that
K(b_{\nu})=K(a_{\nu})\cap K^{r}
. After fixing an extension of
v
to
\overline{K(X)}
, we obtain in Theorem
6.2
that

K(b_{\nu}\mid\nu<\lambda)^{h}\subseteq IC(K(X)|K,v)\subseteq K(a_{\nu}\mid\nu<\lambda)^{h}.

This observation is an analogue of Theorem 1.3 of [
References
], which in turn is a modification of Theorem 1.1 of [
References
].

Key polynomials (cf. Section
7
) are fundamental objects which play a pivotal role in the problem of describing extensions of valuations to rational function fields. They were introduced by MacLane [
References
] and later generalized by Vaquié [
References
]. An alternative form of key polynomials, also known as abstract key polynomials, was given by Spivakovsky and Novacoski in [
References
]. Throughout this article, we will work with abstract key polynomials and we will simply call them key polynomials for brevity. In Theorem
7.4
we obtain that when
(K(X)|K,v)
is a valuation algebraic extension, then

\{Q_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
forms a complete sequence of key polynomials for
v
over
K
,

where
Q_{\nu}
is the minimal polynomial of
a_{\nu}
over
K
. A special case of this result is Theorem 1.2 of [
References
].

2.
Ramification Theory

A valued field
(K,v)
is said to be
henselian
if
v
admits a unique extension to
\overline{K}
. Every valued field has a minimal separable-algebraic extension which is henselian. This extension is unique up to valuation preserving isomorphisms over
K
. We will call this extension the
henselization
of
(K,v)
and denote it by
K^{h}
. Henselization is an
immediate
extension, that is,
vK^{h}=vK
and
K^{h}v=Kv
. An algebraic extension of henselian valued fields is again henselian. For any algebraic extension
L
of
K
, we have that
L^{h}=L.K^{h}
.

We define the distinguished group:

G^{r}:=\{\sigma\in\mathrm{Gal}\,(\overline{K}|K)\mid v(\sigma a-a)>va\text{ for all }a\in K^{\mathrm{sep}}\setminus\{0\}\},

where
K^{\mathrm{sep}}
denotes the separable-algebraic closure of
K
. The corresponding fixed field in
K^{\mathrm{sep}}
will be denoted by
K^{r}
and we will call it the
absolute ramification field
of
(K,v)
. It is well-known that
K^{h}\subseteq K^{r}
and hence
K^{r}
is a henselian field. Further, the extension
(K^{r}|K^{h},v)
is
defectless
, that is, for any tower of field extensions
K^{h}\subseteq L\subseteq L^{\prime}\subseteq K^{r}
where
L^{\prime}|L
is finite, we have

[L^{\prime}:L]=(vL^{\prime}:vL)[L^{\prime}v:Lv].

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume that
(K(y)|K,v)
is an immediate extension. Then
v(y-K)
does not admit a maximum.

Proof.

Suppose that
\alpha:=v(y-a)=\max v(y-K)
for some
a\in K
. The fact that
(K(y)|K,v)
is an immediate extension implies that
v(y-K)\subset vK
. Take
b\in K
such that
vb=\alpha
. Then
v\frac{y-a}{b}=0
and
\frac{y-a}{b}v\in K(y)v=Kv
. Consequently, there exists
c\in K
such that
v(\frac{y-a}{b}-c)>0
, that is,
v(y-a-bc)>vb=\alpha
. The fact that
a+bc\in K
then contradicts the maximality of
\alpha
.
∎

3.
Minimal Pairs of Definition

Take any
a\in\overline{K}
and
\gamma
in some ordered abelian group containing
v\overline{K}
. Any polynomial
f(X)\in\overline{K}[X]
has a unique expression of the form
f(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}c_{i}(X-a)^{i}
where
c_{i}\in\overline{K}
. Consider the map
v_{a,\gamma}:\overline{K}[X]\to v\overline{K}+{\mathbb{Z}}\gamma
given by

v_{a,\gamma}f:=\min\{vc_{i}+i\gamma\},

and extend
v_{a,\gamma}
canonically to
\overline{K}(X)
. Then
v_{a,\gamma}
is a valuation transcendental extension of
v
from
\overline{K}
to
\overline{K}(X)
[
References
, Theorem 3.11]. Further,
(\overline{K}(X)|\overline{K},v_{a,\gamma})
is value transcendental whenever
\gamma\notin v\overline{K}
. Otherwise, the extension is residue transcendental.

It has further been observed in [
References
, Theorem 3.11] that
(K(X)|K,v)
is value (residue) transcendental if and only if
(\overline{K}(X)|\overline{K},v)
is also value (residue) transcendental, which in turn holds if and only if
v=v_{a,\gamma}
for some
a\in\overline{K}
and
\gamma\in v\overline{K}(X)
. Such a pair
(a,\gamma)\in\overline{K}\times v\overline{K}(X)
is said to be a
pair of definition for
v
over
K
. A valuation transcendental extension can have multiple pairs of definition. It has been observed in [
References
, Proposition 3] that
(a^{\prime},\gamma)
is also a pair of definition for
v
over
K
if and only if
v(a-a^{\prime})\geq\gamma
.

Definition 3.1
.

Assume that
(K(X)|K,v)
is a valuation transcendental extension. A pair
(a,\gamma)\in\overline{K}\times v\overline{K}(X)
is said to be a
minimal pair of definition for
v
over
K
if it satisfies the following conditions:

(MP1)
(a,\gamma)
is a pair of definition for
v
over
K
,

(MP2)
v(a-a^{\prime})\geq\gamma\Longrightarrow[K(a^{\prime}):K]\geq[K(a):K]
for all
a^{\prime}\in\overline{K}
.

In other words,
a
is of minimal degree over
K
with the property that
(a,\gamma)
is a pair of definition for
v
over
K
. It follows from [
References
, Theorem 3.11] that a valuation transcendental extension always admits a pair of definition. The well-ordering principle then implies that a valuation transcendental extension always admits a minimal pair of definition.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that
(K(X)|K,v)
is a valuation transcendental extension. Take a minimal pair of definition
(a,\gamma)
and a pair of definition
(a^{\prime},\gamma)
for
v
over
K
. Then,

vK(a)\subseteq vK(a^{\prime})\text{ and }K(a)v\subseteq K(a^{\prime})v.

Proof.

We first assume that
(K(X)|K,v)
is a value transcendental extension. It follows from [
References
, Remark 3.3] that

vK(X)=vK(a)\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}vQ,

where
Q(X)\in K[X]
is the minimal polynomial of
a
over
K
. Moreover, observe that
(a^{\prime},\gamma)
is a minimal pair of definition for
v
over
K(a^{\prime})
. As a consequence,

vK(a^{\prime},X)=vK(a^{\prime})\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}\gamma.

Take any
\alpha\in vK(X)
. The fact that
vK(X)\subseteq vK(a^{\prime},X)
then implies that
\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}+n\gamma
for some
\alpha^{\prime}\in vK(a^{\prime})
and
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Since
\gamma
is not a torsion element modulo
vK
, it follows that
n=0
and consequently,
\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}
. We have thus obtained that

vK(a)\subseteq vK(a^{\prime}).

It further follows from [
References
, Remark 3.3] that

K(a)v=K(X)v\subseteq K(a^{\prime},X)v=K(a^{\prime})v.

We now assume that
(K(X)|K,v)
is a residue transcendental extension. Then
\gamma\in v\overline{K}
. Take the ordered abelian group
v\overline{K}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}
endowed with the lexicographic order. Embed
v\overline{K}
into
(v\overline{K}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}})_{\text{lex}}
by setting

\alpha\mapsto(\alpha,0)\text{ for all }\alpha\in v\overline{K}.

Set

\Gamma:=(\gamma,-1).

Take the extension
w:=v_{a,\Gamma}
of
v
from
\overline{K}
to
\overline{K}(X)
. Observe that,

v(a-b)\geq\Gamma\Longleftrightarrow v(a-b)\geq\gamma\text{ for all }b\in\overline{K}.

It follows that
(a,\Gamma)
is a minimal pair of definition and
(a^{\prime},\Gamma)
is a pair of definition for
w
over
K
. The fact that
\Gamma\notin v\overline{K}
implies that
(K(X)|K,w)
is a value transcendental extension. It now follows from our preceding discussions that

vK(a)\subseteq vK(a^{\prime})\text{ and }K(a)v\subseteq K(a^{\prime})v.

∎

4.
Pseudo-Cauchy Sequences

A well-ordered set
\{z_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
in
(K,v)
, where
\lambda
is some limit ordinal, is said to form a
pseudo-Cauchy sequence (pCs)
if it satisfies the following condition:

v(z_{\nu_{1}}-z_{\nu_{2}})<v(z_{\nu_{2}}-z_{\nu_{3}})\text{ for all }\nu_{1}<\nu_{2}<\nu_{3}<\lambda.

Given a polynomial
f(X)\in K[X]
and a pCs
\{z_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
in
(K,v)
, the sequence
\{vf(z_{\nu})\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is either ultimately constant, or ultimately monotonically increasing [
References
, Lemma 5]. We say that
\{z_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is a
pCs of transcendental type
if
\{vf(z_{\nu})\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is ultimately constant for all
f(X)\in K[X]
. Otherwise, we say that
\{z_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is a
pCs of algebraic type
.

Observe that
v(z_{\nu}-z_{\nu+1})=v(z_{\nu}-z_{\mu})
for all
\nu<\mu<\lambda
. Set
\gamma_{\nu}:=v(z_{\nu}-z_{\nu+1})
. An element
\ell
in some valued field extension
(L,v)
of
(K,v)
is said to be a
limit
of
\{z_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
if

v(\ell-z_{\nu})=\gamma_{\nu}\text{ for all }\nu<\lambda.

If
\ell^{\prime}\in L
is another limit of
\{z_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
, then it follows from the triangle inequality that
v(\ell-\ell^{\prime})\geq\gamma_{\nu}
for all
\nu<\lambda
. The fact that
\{\gamma_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is a monotonically increasing sequence without a final element then implies the following:

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume that
\{z_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is a pCs in a valued field
(K,v)
. Take an extension
(L|K,v)
and assume that
\ell\in L
is a limit of
\{z_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
. Then
\ell^{\prime}\in L
is also a limit of
\{z_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
if and only if

v(\ell-\ell^{\prime})>\gamma_{\nu}\text{ for all }\nu<\lambda.

Definition 4.2
.

An extension
(K(X)|K,v)
is said to be a
pure extension
if it satisfies one of the following conditions:

(PE1)
v(X-a)
is not a torsion element modulo
vK
for some
a\in K
,

(PE2)
vb(X-a)=0
and
b(X-a)v
is transcendental over
Kv
for some
a,b\in K
,

(PE3)
X
is a limit of some pCs of transcendental type in
(K,v)
.

If
(K(X)|K,v)
is a pure extension satisfying (PE3), then it follows from [
References
, Theorem 2] that
(K(X)|K,v)
is immediate.

5.
Valuation Algebraic Extensions

Assume that
(K(X)|K,v)
is a valuation algebraic extension. Then
(\overline{K}(X)|\overline{K},v)
is an immediate extension [
References
, Lemma 3.3]. It follows from Lemma
2.1
that
v(X-\overline{K})
does not admit a maximum. Take a cofinal well-ordered subset
\{\delta_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
of
v(X-\overline{K})
, where
\lambda
is some limit ordinal. Further, for each
\nu<\lambda
, take
d_{\nu}\in\overline{K}
such that
v(X-d_{\nu})=\delta_{\nu}
.

Take a minimal pair of definition
(a,\delta_{1})
of
v_{d_{1},\delta_{1}}
over
K
. Then
v(a-d_{1})\geq\delta_{1}
and hence
v(X-a)\geq\delta_{1}
. Set
\gamma:=v(X-a)
. By definition,
(a,\gamma)
is a pair of definition for
v_{a,\gamma}
over
K
. Take another pair of definition
(a^{\prime},\gamma)
for
v_{a,\gamma}
over
K
. Then
v(a-a^{\prime})\geq\gamma\geq\delta_{1}
and hence
(a^{\prime},\delta_{1})
is a pair of definition for
v_{d_{1},\delta_{1}}
over
K
. The minimality of
(a,\delta_{1})
then implies that
[K(a):K]\leq[K(a^{\prime}):K]
. It follows that
(a,\gamma)
is a minimal pair of definition for
v_{a,\gamma}
over
K
. If
a\notin K
, assume that
\tau a\neq a
is a conjugate of
a
over
K
such that
v(X-\tau a)\geq v(X-a)
. It follows that
v(a-\tau a)\geq v(X-a)\geq\delta_{1}
and hence
(\tau a,\delta_{1})
is also a minimal pair of definition for
v_{d_{1},\delta_{1}}
over
K
. Replacing
a
by
\tau a
and employing the same arguments, we can thus further assume that
v(X-a)\geq v(X-\sigma a)
for all
K
-conjugates
\sigma a
of
a
. Set

a_{1}:=a\text{ and }\gamma_{1}:=\gamma.

Assume that we have obtained some
(a_{\nu},\gamma_{\nu})
. The fact that
\{\delta_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is a well-ordered cofinal subset of
v(X-\overline{K})
implies that there exists some minimal
\mu<\lambda
such that
\gamma_{\nu}<\delta_{\mu}
. We can then construct a pair
(a^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
such that
v(X-a^{\prime})=\gamma^{\prime}\geq\delta_{\mu}
,
(a^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
is a minimal pair of definition for
v_{a^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}}
over
K
and
v(X-a^{\prime})\geq v(X-\sigma a^{\prime})
for all
K
-conjugates
\sigma a^{\prime}
of
a^{\prime}
. We set

a_{\nu+1}:=a^{\prime}\text{ and }\gamma_{\nu+1}:=\gamma^{\prime}.

Observe that
\delta_{\nu}\leq\gamma_{\nu}
for all
\nu<\lambda
. Consequently, the fact that
\{\delta_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is a cofinal well-ordered subset of
v(X-\overline{K})
implies that
\{\gamma_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is a cofinal well-ordered subset of
v(X-\overline{K})
. We have thus constructed sequences
\{\gamma_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
and
\{a_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
satisfying the following conditions for all
\nu<\lambda
:

•

\{\gamma_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is a cofinal well-ordered subset of
v(X-\overline{K})
,

•

v(X-a_{\nu})=\gamma_{\nu}
,

•

(a_{\nu},\gamma_{\nu})
is a minimal pair of definition for
v_{a_{\nu},\gamma_{\nu}}
over
K
,

•

v(X-a_{\nu})\geq v(X-\sigma a_{\nu})
for all
K
-conjugates
\sigma a_{\nu}
of
a_{\nu}
.

We fix this choice of
\{\gamma_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
and
\{a_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}

for the rest of this article
. From [
References
, Theorem 2.3], we obtain that

vK(a_{\nu})\subseteq vK(a_{\mu})\text{ and }K(a_{\nu})v\subseteq K(a_{\mu})v\text{ for all }\nu<\mu<\lambda.

Further, it follows from [
References
, Theorem 5.1] that

vK(X)=\bigcup\limits_{\nu<\lambda}vK(a_{\nu})\text{ and }K(X)v=\bigcup\limits_{\nu<\lambda}K(a_{\nu})v.

By definition,
\{a_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is a pCs in
(K(a_{\nu}\mid\nu<\lambda),v)
with
X
as a limit. Suppose that
\{a_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is a pCs of algebraic type. In light of [
References
, Theorem 3], there exists some
\ell\in\overline{K}
such that
\ell
is also a limit of
\{a_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
. It then follows from Lemma
4.1
that

v(X-\ell)>\gamma_{\nu}\text{ for all }\nu<\lambda.

However, this contradicts the fact that
\{\gamma_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is a cofinal subset of
v(X-\overline{K})
. As a consequence,
\{a_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is a pCs of transcendental type in
(K(a_{\nu}\mid\nu<\lambda),v)
.

6.
Implicit Constant Fields

Fix an extension of
v
to
\overline{K(X)}
. The
implicit constant field
of the extension
(K(X)|K,v)
is defined as

IC(K(X)|K,v):=\overline{K}\cap K(X)^{h}.

Henselization being a separable-algebraic extension implies that
IC(K(X)|K,v)
is a separable-algebraic extension of
K
. Moreover, the fact that
K^{h}\subseteq K(X)^{h}
implies that
K^{h}\subseteq IC(K(X)|K,v)
. Consequently,
(IC(K(X)|K,v),v)
is a henselian valued field.

Lemma 6.1
.

Assume that
(K(X)|K,v)
is a valuation transcendental extension. Take a minimal pair of definition
(a,\gamma)
and a pair of definition
(a^{\prime},\gamma)
for
v
over
K
. Take
b\in\overline{K}
such that
K(b)=K(a)\cap K^{r}
. Then

vK(b,a^{\prime})=vK(a^{\prime})\text{ and }K(b,a^{\prime})v=K(a^{\prime})v.

Proof.

Fix an extension of
v
to
\overline{K(X)}
. We have observed in [
References
, Theorem 1.3] that
K(b)^{h}\subseteq IC(K(X)|K,v)
. Observe that
IC(K(X)|K,v)\subseteq IC(K(a^{\prime},X)|K(a^{\prime}),v)
by definition. It has been observed in [
References
, Lemma 5.1] that
IC(K(a^{\prime},X)|K(a^{\prime}),v)=K(a^{\prime})^{h}
. It then follows that
K(b)^{h}\subseteq K(a^{\prime})^{h}
. In particular,
b\in K(a^{\prime})^{h}
and consequently,

K(b,a^{\prime})^{h}=K(a^{\prime})^{h}.

The fact that the henselization is an immediate extension now yields the lemma.
∎

We are now ready to provide an estimate of
IC(K(X)|K,v)
when the extension
(K(X)|K,v)
is valuation algebraic.

Theorem 6.2
.

Assume that
(K(X)|K,v)
is a valuation algebraic extension. Fix an extension of
v
to
\overline{K(X)}
. Take
\{\gamma_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
and
\{a_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
as in Section
5
. For each
\nu<\lambda
, take
b_{\nu}\in\overline{K}
such that

K(b_{\nu})=K(a_{\nu})\cap K^{r}.

Then,

(2)

K(b_{\nu}\mid\nu<\lambda)^{h}\subseteq IC(K(X)|K,v)\subseteq K(a_{\nu}\mid\nu<\lambda)^{h}.

Proof.

We have observed in Section
5
that
\{a_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
is a pCs of transcendental type in
(L,v)
with limit
X
, where
L:=K(a_{\nu}\mid\nu<\lambda)
. Thus
(L(X)|L,v)
is a pure extension by definition. It follows from [
References
, Lemma 3.7] that
IC(L(X)|L,v)=L^{h}
. Consequently,

IC(K(X)|K,v)\subseteq IC(L(X)|L,v)=L^{h}=K(a_{\nu}\mid\nu<\lambda)^{h}.

Take some
\nu_{0}<\lambda
. Observe that
K(b_{\nu_{0}})=K(a_{\nu_{0}})\cap K^{r}
and hence
vK(b_{\nu_{0}})\subseteq vK(a_{\nu_{0}})
. It follows from [
References
, Theorem 2.3] and [
References
, Theorem 5.1] that
vK(X)=\bigcup\limits_{\nu<\lambda}vK(a_{\nu})
. Consequently,

vK(b_{\nu_{0}})\subseteq vK(a_{\nu_{0}})\subseteq vK(X).

We now obtain from [
References
, Theorem 3(1)] that

(3)

vK(b_{\nu_{0}},X)=vK(X).

Following the same arguments as in Section
5
, we construct sequences
\{\beta_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
and
\{c_{\nu}\}_{\nu<\lambda}
satisfying the following conditions for all
\nu<\lambda
:

•

\gamma_{\nu}\leq\beta_{\nu}=v(X-c_{\nu})
,

•

(c_{\nu},\beta_{\nu})
is a minimal pair of definition for
v_{c_{\nu},\beta_{\nu}}
over
K(b_{\nu_{0}})
.

It follows from [
References
, Theorem 2.3] and [

, Theorem 5.1] that

7.
Key Polynomials

Take a polynomial
f(X)\in K[X]
. Following [
References
], we define

\delta(f):=\max\{v(X-a)\mid a\in\overline{K}\text{ and }f(a)=0\}.

A root
a

f
such that
\delta(f)=v(X-a)
is said to be a
maximal root
of
f
. A monic polynomial
Q(X)\in K[X]
is said to be a
key polynomial for
v
over
K
if for any
f(X)\in K[X]
, we have

\deg f<\deg Q\Longrightarrow\delta(f)<\delta(Q).

Take any
f(X)\in K[X]
and a monic
Q(X)\in K[X]
. Then we have a unique expansion

f=f_{0}+f_{1}Q+\dotsc f_{r}Q^{r},

where
f_{i}(X)\in K[X]
such that
\deg f_{i}<\deg Q
. Consider the map
v_{Q}:K[X]\longrightarrow vK(X)
given by

v_{Q}f:=\min\{vf_{i}+ivQ\},

and extend it to
K(X)
by defining
v_{Q}(f/g):=v_{Q}f-v_{Q}g
. A sufficient condition for
v_{Q}
to be a valuation on
K(X)
is that
Q
be a key polynomial for
v
over
K
[
References
, Proposition 2.6], but it is not a necessary condition [
References
, Proposition 2.3].

Lemma 7.1
.

Take
a\in\overline{K}
and
f(X)\in K[X]
. Set
\gamma:=v(X-a)
. Then,

\displaystyle vf

\displaystyle>v_{a,\gamma}f\text{ if and only if }\delta(f)>\gamma,

\displaystyle vf

\displaystyle=v_{a,\gamma}f\text{ if and only if }\delta(f)\leq\gamma.

Proof.

Write
f(X)=z\prod_{i=1}^{n}(X-z_{i})
where
z,z_{i}\in\overline{K}
. By definition,
v_{a,\gamma}(X-z_{i})=\min\{\gamma,v(a-z_{i})\}
. It then follows from the triangle inequality that
v_{a,\gamma}(X-z_{i})\leq v(X-z_{i})
. As a consequence,

v_{a,\gamma}f\leq vf.

Further,
v_{a,\gamma}f<vf
if and only if
v_{a,\gamma}(X-z_{i})<v(X-z_{i})
for some
i
, which holds if and only if
v(X-z_{i})>\gamma=v(a-z_{i})
. The lemma now follows.
∎

Introduction

In last decades, a great attention has been focused on the study of problems involving exponential growth nonlinearities, which is related to the famous Trudinger-Moser inequality. Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and denote with
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
the standard first order Sobolev space given by

H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)=cl\Big\{u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)\ :\ \int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}{\rm d}x<\infty\Big\},\quad\ \ \|u\|=\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

This space is a limiting case for the Sobolev embedding theorem, which yields
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\Omega)
for all
1\leq p<\infty
, but one knows by easy examples that
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\not\subseteq L^{\infty}(\Omega)
such as,
u(x)=\log(1-\log|x|)
in
B_{1}(0)
. Hence, one is led to look for a function
g(s):\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
with maximal growth such that

\sup\limits_{u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\|u\|\leq 1}\int_{\Omega}g(u){\rm d}x<\infty.

It was shown by Trudinger
[
29
]
and Moser
[
26
]
that the maximal growth is of exponential type. More precisely, named the Trudinger-Moser inequality that

\exp(\alpha u^{2})\in L^{1}(\Omega),\quad\forall\ u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\ \ \forall\ \alpha>0,

and

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\|u\|\leq 1}\int_{\Omega}\exp(\alpha u^{2}){\rm d}x<\infty,\quad\mbox{if}\ \alpha\leq 4\pi,

where
4\pi
is the sharp constant in the sense that the supremum in the left is
\infty
if
\alpha>4\pi
.

In order to treat the system problems, here we give some definitions. For all
1\leq p<\infty
, we define
L^{p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})
as

L^{p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k}):=\underbrace{L^{p}(\Omega)\times\cdots\times L^{p}(\Omega)}_{k},

where
L^{p}(\Omega)
is the standard
L^{p}
-space, and since

\frac{1}{k}\left(\sum^{k}_{i=1}|u_{i}|^{p}\right)\leq\left(\sum^{k}_{i=1}|u_{i}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq k^{p}\left(\sum^{k}_{i=1}|u_{i}|^{p}\right),

(1.1)

we can know that
L^{p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})
is well defined and for
U\in L^{p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})
, we define
\|U\|_{p}=\left(\int_{\Omega}|U|^{p}dx\right)^{1/p}
where
|U|=(\sum^{k}_{i=1}|u_{i}|^{2})^{1/2}
. Moreover we denote

H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k}):=\underbrace{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times\cdots\times H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}_{k},

be the Sobolev space modeled in
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})
with the scalar product

\ \langle U,V\rangle=\sum^{k}_{i=1}\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{i}\nabla v_{i}dx,

where
U,V\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})
, to which corresponds the norm
\|U\|=\langle U,U\rangle^{1/2}=(\sum^{k}_{i=1}\|u_{i}\|^{2})^{1/2}
, then
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})
is well defined and also is a Hilbert space. For all
1\leq p<\infty
,
by the inequality (
1.1
) and the Sobolev embedding theorem, we can know that the embedding
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})
is compact and
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})\nsubseteq L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})
, where
L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k}):=L^{\infty}(\Omega)\times\cdots\times L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. In Section
2
, we will establish the Trudinger-Moser type inequality for
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})
.

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{e^{\alpha|u|^{2}}}{|x|^{\beta}}<\infty.

Moreover, there exists constant
C(\Omega)
depending only on
\Omega
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{||\nabla u||_{2}\leq 1}\int_{\Omega}\frac{e^{\alpha|u|^{2}}}{|x|^{\beta}}\leq C(\Omega),

if and only if
\frac{\alpha}{4\pi}+\frac{\beta}{2}\leq 1
.

Then, we give two useful algebraic inequalities that will be used systematically in the rest of the paper as the following:

Lemma 2.2
.

(
Improvement of the Young’s inequality
)
Let
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}>0
,
p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}>1
with
\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{p_{k}}=1
, then

\displaystyle a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{k}\leq\frac{a^{p_{1}}_{1}}{p_{1}}+\frac{a^{p_{2}}_{2}}{p_{2}}+\cdots+\frac{a^{p_{k}}_{k}}{p_{k}}.

(2.1)

Proof.

We will use the mathematical induction to proof this. When
k=2
, by Young inequality, we can know this conclusion is correct. Suppose that when
k=s-1
the conclusion is correct, we are going to show that when
k=s
the conclusion is still correct. Let

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q}=\sum^{s-1}_{i=1}\frac{1}{p_{i}},\ \ \frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p_{s}}=1,\ \ \mbox{then}\ \ \sum^{s-1}_{i=1}\frac{1}{p_{i}/q}=1.

Thus,

\displaystyle\prod^{s}_{i=1}a_{i}=\Big(\prod^{s-1}_{i=1}a_{i}\Big)a_{s}\leq\frac{1}{q}\Big(\prod^{s-1}_{i=1}a_{i}\Big)^{q}+\frac{1}{p_{s}}a^{p_{s}}_{s},

by the mathematical induction, we can get that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q}\Big(\prod^{s-1}_{i=1}a_{i}\Big)^{q}=\frac{1}{q}\Big(\prod^{s-1}_{i=1}a^{q}_{i}\Big)\leq\frac{1}{q}\sum^{s-1}\Big[\frac{1}{p_{i}/q}\big(a^{q}_{i})^{p_{i}/q}\Big]=\sum^{s-1}\frac{1}{p_{i}}a^{p_{i}}_{i}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\prod^{s}_{i=1}a_{i}\leq\sum^{s-1}_{i=1}\frac{1}{p_{i}}a^{p_{i}}_{i}+\frac{1}{p_{s}}a^{p_{s}}_{s}=\sum^{s}_{i=1}\frac{1}{p_{i}}a^{p_{i}}_{i}.

This lemma is proved. If we take
p_{1}=p_{2}=\cdots=p_{k}=k
, we can get that

\displaystyle a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{k}\leq\frac{1}{k}\sum^{k}_{i=1}a^{k}_{i}\leq\sum^{k}_{i=1}a^{k}_{i}.

(2.2)

∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}\geq 0
with
a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{k}<1
, then there exist
p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}>1
satisfying
\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{p_{k}}=1
, such that

\displaystyle p_{i}a_{i}<1,\quad\mbox{for all}\ \ i=1,2,\ldots,k.

(2.3)

Moreover, if
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}\geq 0
satisfying
a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{k}=1
, then we can take
p_{i}=\frac{1}{a_{i}}
such that
\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{p_{k}}=1
and

\displaystyle p_{i}a_{i}=1,\quad\mbox{for all}\ \ i=1,2,\ldots,k.

(2.4)

Proof.

For the case
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}\geq 0
with
a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{k}<1
. We also make use of the mathematical induction as previous. When
k=2
,
a_{1},a_{2}\geq 0
with
a_{1}+a_{2}<1
. If
a_{2}=0
(or
a_{1}=0
), then taking
p_{1}=\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2a_{1}}\right)>1
(or
p_{2}=\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2a_{2}}\right)>1
), we can obtain
p_{1}a_{1}<1,\ p_{2}a_{2}<1
, where
p_{2}=\frac{p_{1}}{p_{1}-1}
. If
a_{1},a_{2}>0
, then taking
p_{1}=\left(\frac{1}{2(1-a_{1})}+\frac{1}{2a_{2}}\right)>1
, we can obtain
p_{1}a_{1}<1,\ p_{2}a_{2}<1
, where
p_{2}=\frac{p_{1}}{p_{1}-1}
. Suppose that when
k=s-1
the conclusion is correct, if
k=s
the conclusion is still correct, then the lemma follows. Let

\displaystyle(a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{s-1})+a_{s}<1,

then there exist
q_{1},q_{2}>1
satisfying
\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}=1
such that

\displaystyle q_{1}(a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{s-1})<1,\ \ q_{2}a_{s}<1.

Then by the assumption, there exist
q_{3},q_{4},\ldots,q_{s+1}>1
satisfying
\frac{1}{q_{3}}+\frac{1}{q_{4}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{q_{s+1}}=1
such that

\displaystyle q_{3}(q_{1}a_{1})<1,\ q_{4}(q_{1}a_{2})<1,\ldots,\ q_{s+1}(q_{1}a_{s-1})<1,

i.e.

\displaystyle(q_{3}q_{1})a_{1}<1,\ (q_{4}q_{1})a_{2}<1,\ldots,\ (q_{s+1}q_{1})a_{s-1}<1.

Taking
p_{1}=q_{1}q_{3},\ p_{2}=q_{1}q_{4},\ldots,\ p_{s-1}=q_{1}q_{s+1},\ p_{s}=q_{2}
, then it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{p_{s}}=\frac{1}{q_{1}}\left(\frac{1}{q_{3}}+\frac{1}{q_{4}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{q_{s+1}}\right)+\frac{1}{q_{2}}=\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}=1,

and

\displaystyle p_{1}a_{1}<1,\ p_{2}a_{2}<1,\ldots,\ p_{s}a_{s}<1.

For the case
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}\geq 0
satisfying
a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{k}=1
. If
a_{i}>0
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
, then we can take
p_{i}=\frac{1}{a_{i}}
such that
\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{p_{k}}=1
and (
2.3
) holds. If
a_{i}=0
for some
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
which same as the case
(k-1)
.
The proof is complete.
∎

By the above inequalities, we begin to establish the singular Trudinger-Moser type inequalities in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})
:

Lemma 2.4
.

(
Improvement of the Trudinger-Moser inequality
)
Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
containing the origin and
U=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{k})\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})
. Then for every
\alpha>0
, and
\beta\in[0,2)
,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{e^{\alpha|U|^{2}}}{|x|^{\beta}}<\infty.

(2.5)

Moreover, it holds that

\displaystyle\sup_{U\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k}),\ \|U\|\leq 1}\int_{\Omega}\frac{e^{\alpha|U|^{2}}}{|x|^{\beta}}\leq C(\Omega).

(2.6)

if and only if
\frac{\alpha}{4\pi}+\frac{\beta}{2}\leq 1
, where
C(\Omega)
is given in Lemma
2.1
.

Proof.

Because
U=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{k})
, we can get
|U|^{2}=\sum^{k}_{i=1}|u_{i}|^{2}
. Thus, by using (
2.2
) and Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{e^{\alpha|U|^{2}}}{|x|^{\beta}}=\int_{\Omega}\frac{e^{\alpha|u_{1}|^{2}}\cdots e^{\alpha|u_{k}|^{2}}}{|x|^{\beta}}\leq\sum^{k}_{i=1}\int_{\Omega}\frac{e^{k\alpha|u_{i}|^{2}}}{|x|^{\beta}}<\infty.

Then we begin to proof (
2.6
). For each
U\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})
satisfying
\|U\|\leq 1
, then
\|U\|^{2}=\sum^{k}_{i=1}\|u_{i}\|^{2}\leq 1
, Lemma
2.3
shows that there exist
p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}>1
satisfying
\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{p_{k}}=1
such that

p_{i}\|u_{i}\|^{2}\leq 1
holds, for all
i=1,2,\ldots,k
. If
\frac{\alpha}{4\pi}+\frac{\beta}{2}\leq 1
, then it also holds that
\frac{p_{i}\|u_{i}\|^{2}\alpha}{4\pi}+\frac{\beta}{2}\leq 1
, for all
i=1,2,\ldots,k
. Then by using Lemma
2.2
, i.e. the improvement of the Young’s inequality, and from Lemma
2.1
we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\frac{e^{\alpha|U|^{2}}}{|x|^{\beta}}=\int_{\Omega}\frac{e^{\alpha|u_{1}|^{2}}\cdots e^{\alpha|u_{k}|^{2}}}{|x|^{\beta}}\leq\sum^{k}_{i=1}\int_{\Omega}\frac{e^{p_{i}\alpha|u_{i}|^{2}}}{p_{i}|x|^{\beta}}=\sum^{k}_{i=1}\int_{\Omega}\frac{e^{p_{i}\alpha\|u_{i}\|^{2}(\frac{u_{i}}{\|u_{i}\|})^{2}}}{p_{i}|x|^{\beta}}\leq\sum^{k}_{i=1}\frac{C(\Omega)}{p_{i}}=C(\Omega),\end{split}

then (
2.6
) follows. If
\frac{\alpha}{4\pi}+\frac{\beta}{2}>1
, we take
U=(u,0,\ldots,0)
, then Lemma
2.1
shows that the supremum for the integral in (
2.6
) is infinite. Thus the proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\{U_{n}\}
be a sequence of functions in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})
with
\|U_{n}\|=1
such that
U_{n}\rightharpoonup U\neq 0
weakly in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})
. Then for any
0<p<\frac{2\pi(2-\beta)}{{(1-\|U\|^{2})}}
and
\beta\in[0,2)
, we have

\sup_{n}\int_{\Omega}\frac{e^{p|U_{n}|^{2}}}{|x|^{\beta}}<\infty.

Proof.

Since
U_{n}\rightharpoonup U\neq 0
and
\|\nabla U_{n}\|_{2}=1
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\|U_{n}-U\|^{2}=1-2\langle U_{n},U\rangle+\|U\|^{2}\rightarrow 1-\|U\|^{2}<\frac{2\pi(2-\beta)}{p}

Thus, for large
n
we have

\displaystyle\frac{p\|U_{n}-U\|^{2}}{4\pi}+\frac{\beta}{2}<1.

Now we can choose
q>1
close to 1 and
\epsilon>0
such that

\displaystyle\frac{qp(1+\epsilon^{2})\|U_{n}-U\|^{2}}{4\pi}+\frac{q\beta}{2}\leq 1.

Lemmas
2.4
shows that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{e^{qp(1+\epsilon^{2})|U_{n}-U|^{2}}}{|x|^{q\beta}}\leq C(\Omega).

Moreover, since

\displaystyle p|U_{n}|^{2}\leq p(1+\epsilon^{2})|U_{n}-U|^{2}+p(1+1/\epsilon^{2})|U|^{2},

which can be proved by Young inequality, then it follows that

\displaystyle e^{p|U_{n}|^{2}}\leq e^{p(1+\epsilon^{2})|U_{n}-U|^{2}}e^{p(1+1/\epsilon^{2})|U|^{2}}.

Consequently, by Hölder inequality,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\frac{e^{p|U_{n}|^{2}}}{|x|^{\beta}}&\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{e^{qp(1+\epsilon^{2})|U_{n}-U|^{2}}}{|x|^{q\beta}}\right)^{1/q}\left(\int_{\Omega}e^{rp(1+1/\epsilon^{2})|U|^{2}}\right)^{1/r}\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}e^{rp(1+1/\epsilon^{2})|U|^{2}}\right),\end{split}

for large
n
, where
r=\frac{q}{q-1}
. By Lemma
2.4
, we know the second term in the last inequality is bounded, and this lemma is proved.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

Lemma
2.5
is actually an expression that Concentration-compactness principle for a singular Trudinger-Moser inequality which is better than (
[
16
]
, Lemma 2.3). However, it is still not clear whether
\frac{2\pi(2-\beta)}{1-\|U\|^{2}}
is sharp or not. This is still an open question.

Lemma 2.6
.

If
V\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k}),\ \alpha>0,\ q>0,\ \beta\in[0,2)
and
\|V\|\leq N
with
\frac{\alpha N^{2}}{4\pi}+\frac{\beta}{2}<1
, then there exists
C=C(\alpha,N,q)>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|V|^{q}\frac{e^{\alpha|V|^{2}}}{|x|^{\beta}}\leq C\|V\|^{q}.

(2.7)

Proof.

We consider
r>1
close to 1 such that
\frac{r\alpha N^{2}}{4\pi}+\frac{r\beta}{2}\leq 1
and
sq\geq 1
, where
s=\frac{r}{r-1}
. By using Hölder inequality and Lemma
2.4
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}|V|^{q}\frac{e^{\alpha|V|^{2}}}{|x|^{\beta}}&\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{e^{r\alpha|V|^{2}}}{|x|^{r\beta}}\right)^{1/r}\|V\|^{q}_{qs}\leq C(\Omega)\|V\|^{q}_{qs}.\end{split}

Finally, using the continuous embedding
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})\hookrightarrow L^{sq}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|V|^{q}\frac{e^{\beta|V|^{2}}}{|x|^{\beta}}\leq C\|V\|^{q}.

∎

3
The variational framework

We now consider the functional
I
given by

I_{\varepsilon}(U)=\frac{1}{2}M(\|U\|^{2})-\int_{\Omega}\frac{F(x,U)}{|x|^{\beta}}dx-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}U\cdot H(x)dx.

Lemma 3.1
.

Under our assumptions we have that
I
is well defined and
C^{1}
on
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})
. Moreover,

\langle I_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(U),\Phi\rangle_{*}=m(\|U\|^{2})\langle U,\Phi\rangle-\int_{\Omega}\frac{\Phi\cdot\nabla{F(x,U)}}{|x|^{\beta}}dx-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}\Phi\cdot Hdx,

where
\Phi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})
, here
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{*}
simply denotes the dual pairing between
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})
and its dual space
\left(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})\right)^{*}
.

Proof.

We have that
f_{i}
is continuous and has subcritical (or critical) growth at
\infty
, as defined in (
1.10
) (or (
1.11
)). Thus, given
\alpha>0
(or
\alpha>\alpha_{0}
), there exists
C>0
such that
|f_{i}(x,U)|\leq Ce^{\alpha|U|^{2}}
for all
(x,U)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Then,

|\nabla F(x,U)|\leq\sum^{k}_{i=1}|f_{i}(x,U)|\leq C_{1}e^{\alpha|U|^{2}},\ \ \text{for all }(x,U)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{k}.\\

(3.1)

By
(F_{1})
, given
\epsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that

|F(x,U)|\leq\frac{\lambda_{1}m_{0}-\epsilon}{2}|U|^{2}\ \ \text{always that }|U|<\delta.\\

(3.2)

Thus, using (
3.1
), (
3.2
) and
(F_{3})
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{|F(x,U)|}{|x|^{\beta}}dx\leq\frac{\lambda_{1}m_{0}-\epsilon}{2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|U|^{2}}{|x|^{\beta}}dx+C_{1}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|U|e^{\alpha|U|^{2}}}{|x|^{\beta}}dx.

Considering the continuous imbedding
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})\hookrightarrow L^{s}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})
for
s\geq 1
and using Lemma
2.6
, it follows that
\frac{F(x,U)}{|x|^{\beta}}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
which implies that
I_{\varepsilon}
is well defined. And we can see that
I\in C^{1}(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k}),\mathbb{R})
with

\langle I_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(U),\Phi\rangle_{*}=m(||U||^{2})\langle U,\Phi\rangle-\int_{\Omega}\frac{\Phi\cdot\nabla{F(x,U)}}{|x|^{\beta}}dx-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}\Phi\cdot Hdx,

for all
U,\Phi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})
.
∎

From Lemma
3.1
, we have that critical points of the functional
I_{\varepsilon}
are precisely weak solutions of problem (
1
). In the next three lemmas we check that the functional
I_{\varepsilon}
satisfies the geometric conditions of the Mountain-pass theorem.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose that
(M_{1})
and
(F_{1}),\ (F_{3})
hold and the function
f_{i}
has subcritical (or critical) growth at
\infty
. Then for small
\varepsilon
, there exist positive number
\rho_{\varepsilon}
and
\varsigma
such that

\displaystyle I_{\varepsilon}(U)\geq\varsigma,\ \ \forall U\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})\ \ \mbox{with}\ \ \|U\|=\rho_{\varepsilon}.

Moreover,
\rho_{\varepsilon}
can be chosen such that
\rho_{\varepsilon}\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Proof.

By
(F_{1})
, given
\kappa>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that

\displaystyle|\nabla F(x,U)|\leq(\lambda_{1}m_{0}-\kappa)|U|

always that
|U|<\delta
. On the other hand, for
\alpha>0
(subcritical case) or
\alpha>\alpha_{0}
(critical case), we have that there exists
C_{1}>0
such that
|f_{i}(x,U)|\leq C_{1}|U|^{q-1}e^{\alpha|U|^{2}}
for all
|U|\geq\delta
with
q>2
. Thus,

\displaystyle|\nabla F(x,U)|\leq\sum^{k}_{i=1}|f_{i}(x,U)|\leq(\lambda_{1}m_{0}-\kappa)|U|+C_{2}|U|^{q-1}e^{\alpha|U|^{2}},\ \ \forall(x,U)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{k}.

(3.3)

Thus, by
(M_{1}),\ (F_{3})
and (
3.3
),

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{\varepsilon}(U)=&\frac{1}{2}M(\|U\|^{2})-\int_{\Omega}\frac{F(x,U)}{|x|^{\beta}}dx-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}U\cdot H(x)dx\\
\geq&\frac{1}{2}m_{0}\|U\|^{2}-\int_{\Omega}\frac{F(x,U)}{|x|^{\beta}}dx-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}U\cdot H(x)dx\\
\geq&\frac{1}{2}m_{0}\|U\|^{2}-\frac{1}{\mu}\int_{\Omega}\frac{U\cdot\nabla F(x,U)}{|x|^{\beta}}dx-\varepsilon\|U\|\|H\|_{*}\\
\geq&\frac{1}{2}m_{0}\|U\|^{2}-\frac{\lambda_{1}m_{0}-\kappa}{\mu}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|U|^{2}}{|x|^{\beta}}dx-C_{2}\int_{\Omega}|U|^{q}\frac{e^{\alpha|U|^{2}}}{|x|^{\beta}}dx-\varepsilon\|U\|\|H\|_{*}\\
\geq&\Big(\frac{m_{0}}{2}-\frac{m_{0}}{\mu}+\frac{\kappa}{\mu\lambda_{1}}\Big)\|U\|^{2}-C_{2}\int_{\Omega}|U|^{q}\frac{e^{\alpha|U|^{2}}}{|x|^{\beta}}dx-\varepsilon\|U\|\|H\|_{*},\end{split}

By Lemma
2.6
, there exists
N>0
such that
\alpha N^{2}/4\pi+\beta/2<1
and we take
\|U\|\leq N
, there exists
C>0
such that
\int_{\Omega}|U|^{q}\frac{e^{\alpha|U|^{2}}}{|x|^{\beta}}dx\leq C\|U\|^{q}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle I_{\varepsilon}(U)\geq\Big(\frac{m_{0}}{2}-\frac{m_{0}}{\mu}+\frac{\kappa}{\mu\lambda_{1}}\Big)\|U\|^{2}-C_{3}\|U\|^{q}-\varepsilon\|U\|\|H\|_{*},

Since
m_{0}>0,\ \mu>2\theta>2,\ q>2
, then for small
\varepsilon
, there exists
\xi_{\varepsilon}>0
such that
\Big(\frac{m_{0}}{2}-\frac{m_{0}}{\mu}+\frac{\kappa}{\mu\lambda_{1}}\Big)\xi_{\varepsilon}^{2}-C_{3}\xi_{\varepsilon}^{q}-\varepsilon\xi_{\varepsilon}\|H\|_{*}>0
.
Consequently, taking
\rho_{\varepsilon}=\min\{N,\xi_{\varepsilon}\}>0
, we can get that
I_{\varepsilon}(U)\geq\varsigma
whenever
\|U\|=\rho_{\varepsilon}
where
\varsigma:=\Big(\frac{m_{0}}{2}-\frac{m_{0}}{\mu}+\frac{\kappa}{\mu\lambda_{1}}\Big)\rho_{\varepsilon}^{2}-C_{3}\rho_{\varepsilon}^{q}-\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}\|H\|_{*}>0
. And it is worth noting that,
\rho_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow 0
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume that
(M_{1}),\ (M_{3}),\ (F_{3})
hold and the function
f_{i}
has subcritical (or critical) growth at
\infty
. Then there exists
E\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})
with
\|E\|>\rho_{\varepsilon}
such that

\displaystyle I_{\varepsilon}(E)<\inf_{\|U\|=\rho_{\varepsilon}}I(U).

Proof.

We shall make use of the polar coordinate representation

\displaystyle U=(\nu,\phi)=(\nu,\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{k-1}),

where
\nu\geq 1,\ -\pi\leq\phi_{1}\leq\pi,\ 0\leq\phi_{2},\ldots,\phi_{k-1}\leq\pi
and

\displaystyle\begin{split}u_{1}&=\nu\sin(\phi_{1})\sin(\phi_{2})\cdots\sin(\phi_{k-1}),\\
u_{2}&=\nu\cos(\phi_{1})\sin(\phi_{2})\cdots\sin(\phi_{k-1}),\\
u_{3}&=\nu\cos(\phi_{2})\cdots\sin(\phi_{k-1}),\\
\vdots\\
u_{k}&=\nu\cos(\phi_{k-1}).\end{split}

Substituting in
(F_{3})
, we get
0<\mu F(x,U)\leq\nu F_{\nu}(x,U)
and hence

\displaystyle F(x,U)\geq\left(\min_{|W|=1}F(x,W)\right)|U|^{\mu},\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ x\in\Omega\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ |U|\geq 1.

Hence, for all
U\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})\setminus\{0\}
with
|U|\geq 1
, we have that

\displaystyle F(x,U)\geq C|U|^{\mu}.

From (
1.9
), we have that
0<M(t)\leq M(1)t^{\theta}
for all
t\geq 1
. Thus we have

\displaystyle I_{\varepsilon}(tU)\leq C_{1}t^{2\theta}\|U\|^{2\theta}-C_{2}t^{\mu}\int_{K}\frac{|U|^{\mu}}{|x|^{\beta}}dx-t\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}U\cdot Hdx,

for
t
large enough, where
C_{1},\ C_{2}>0,\ \mu>2\theta>2
,
K
is the compact subset of
\Omega
which yields
I_{\varepsilon}(tU)\rightarrow-\infty
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
. Setting
E=tU
with
t
large enough such that
I_{\varepsilon}(E)<0
with
\|E\|>\rho_{\varepsilon}
. Thus, the proof is finished.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

If
f_{i}
has subcritical (or critical) growth at
\infty
, there exists
\eta_{\varepsilon}>0
and
V\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})\backslash\{\mathbf{0}\}
such that
I_{\varepsilon}(tV)<0
for all
0<t<\eta_{\varepsilon}
. In particular,

\displaystyle\inf_{\|U\|\leq\eta_{\varepsilon}}I_{\varepsilon}(U)<0.

Proof.

Since
h_{i}\in\big(\big(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\big)^{*},\|\cdot\|_{*}\big)\backslash\{0\}
for some
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
, then by the Riesz representation theorem, the problem

\displaystyle-\Delta v_{i}=h_{i},\ \ x\in\Omega;\ \ v_{i}=0\ \ \mbox{on}\ \ \partial\Omega,

has a unique nontrivial weak solution
v_{i}
in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Thus,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}h_{i}v_{i}=\|v_{i}\|^{2}>0.

Since
f_{i}(x,0,\ldots,0)=0
, by continuity,
(F_{3})
and (
1.9
),
\theta>1
, it follows that there exists
\eta_{\varepsilon}>0
such that for all
0<t<\eta_{\varepsilon}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}[I_{\varepsilon}((0,\ldots,tv_{i},\ldots,0))]=&m(t^{2}\|v_{i}\|^{2})t\|v_{i}\|^{2}-\int_{\Omega}\frac{v_{i}f_{i}(x,(0,\ldots,tv_{i},\ldots,0))}{|x|^{\beta}}-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}h_{i}v_{i}\\

4
On Palais-Smale sequences

To prove that a Palais-Smale sequence converges to a weak solution of problem (
1
), we need to establish the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume that
(M_{1}),\ (M_{3}),\ (F_{3})
hold and
f_{i}
has subcritical (or critical) growth at
\infty
. Let
\{U_{n}\}\subset H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})
be the Palais-Smale sequence for functional
I_{\varepsilon}
at finite level. Then there exist
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\|U_{n}\|\leq C,\ \ \int_{\Omega}\frac{\left|U_{n}\cdot\nabla F(x,U_{n})\right|}{|x|^{\beta}}dx\leq C\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \int_{\Omega}\frac{F(x,U_{n})}{|x|^{\beta}}dx\leq C.

Proof.

Let
\{U_{n}\}\subset H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})
be a sequence such that
I_{\varepsilon}(U_{n})\rightarrow c
and
I_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(U_{n})\rightarrow 0
, where
|c|<\infty
, then we can take this as follows:

I_{\varepsilon}(U_{n})=\frac{1}{2}M(\|U_{n}\|^{2})-\int_{\Omega}\frac{F(x,U_{n})}{|x|^{\beta}}dx-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}U_{n}\cdot Hdx=c+\delta_{n},

(4.1)

where
\delta_{n}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, and

\langle I_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(U_{n}),U_{n}\rangle_{*}=m(\|U_{n}\|^{2})\|U_{n}\|^{2}-\int_{\Omega}\frac{U_{n}\cdot\nabla F(x,U_{n})}{|x|^{\beta}}dx-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}U_{n}\cdot Hdx=o(\|U_{n}\|).

(4.2)

Then for
n
large enough, by
(M_{1}),\ (F_{3})
and (
1.9
), it holds that

\displaystyle\begin{split}C+\|U_{n}\|\geq&I_{\varepsilon}(U_{n})-\frac{1}{\mu}\langle I^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(U_{n}),U_{n}\rangle_{*}\\
=&\frac{1}{2}M(\|U_{n}\|^{2})-\frac{1}{\mu}m(\|U_{n}\|^{2})\|U_{n}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{\mu}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\left(U_{n}\cdot\nabla F(x,U_{n})-\mu F(x,U_{n})\right)}{|x|^{\beta}}dx\\
&-\frac{\mu-1}{\mu}\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}U_{n}\cdot Hdx\\
\geq&\frac{1}{2\theta}\Big[\theta M(\|U_{n}\|^{2})-m(\|U_{n}\|^{2})\|U_{n}\|^{2}\Big]+\Big(\frac{1}{2\theta}-\frac{1}{\mu}\Big)m(\|U_{n}\|^{2})\|U_{n}\|^{2}\\
&-\frac{\mu-1}{\mu}\varepsilon\|U_{n}\|\|H\|_{*}\\
\geq&\Big(\frac{1}{2\theta}-\frac{1}{\mu}\Big)m(\|U_{n}\|^{2})\|U_{n}\|^{2}-\frac{\mu-1}{\mu}\varepsilon\|U_{n}\|\|H\|_{*}\\
\geq&\frac{\mu-2\theta}{2\theta\mu}m_{0}\|U_{n}\|^{2}-\frac{\mu-1}{\mu}\varepsilon\|U_{n}\|\|H\|_{*},\end{split}

for some
C>0
. Since
\mu>2\theta>2,\ m_{0}>0,\ \varepsilon>0
, we obtain
\|U_{n}\|
is bounded. From (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), it can be concluded directly that there exist
C>0
such that
\int_{\Omega}\frac{U_{n}\cdot\nabla F(x,U_{n})}{|x|^{\beta}}dx\leq C
and
\int_{\Omega}\frac{F(x,U_{n})}{|x|^{\beta}}dx\leq C
. Condition
(F_{3})
implies
U_{n}\cdot\nabla F(x,U_{n})\geq 0
for all
x\in\Omega
, thus we have
\int_{\Omega}\frac{\left|U_{n}\cdot\nabla F(x,U_{n})\right|}{|x|^{\beta}}dx\leq C
.
∎

In order to show that the limit of a sequence in

is a weak solution of problem (

) we will use the following convergence result due to Figueiredo-do Ó-Ref
[

]

Lemma 4.2
.

[
14
]
Let

be a bounded domain and

be a continuous function,
\beta\in[0,2)
. Then for any sequence

in

\displaystyle u_{n}\rightarrow u\ \ in\ \ L^{1}(\Omega),\ \ \frac{f(x,u_{n})}{|x|^{\beta}}\in L^{1}(\Omega)\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \int_{\Omega}\frac{|f(x,u_{n})u_{n}|}{|x|^{\beta}}dx\leq C,

up to a sequence we have

\displaystyle\frac{f(x,u_{n})}{|x|^{\beta}}\rightarrow\frac{f(x,u)}{|x|^{\beta}}\ \ \mbox{in}\ \ L^{1}(\Omega).

Lemma 4.3
.

[
19
]
Let
\{u_{n}\}
be a sequence of functions in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
strongly convergent. Then there exists a subsequence
\{u_{n_{k}}\}
of
\{u_{n}\}
and

such that

almost everywhere in

Lemma 4.4
.

Assume

hold and

has subcritical (or critical) growth at
\infty

at finite level, then there exists

such that

(4.3)

and

U_{n}\cdot H\rightarrow U\cdot H\ \ \mbox{in}\ \ L^{1}(\Omega).

(4.4)

Moreover,

\frac{F(x,U_{n})}{|x|^{\beta}}\rightarrow\frac{F(x,U)}{|x|^{\beta}}\ \ \mbox{in}\ \ L^{1}(\Omega).

(4.5)

Proof.

According to Lemma

, we know that
\{U_{n}\}

we can conclude that

∎

C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})
is dense in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})
, we conclude that
U_{M,\varepsilon}:=U
is a Mountain-pass type solution for problem (
1
) with
I_{\varepsilon}(U_{M,\varepsilon})=c_{M,\varepsilon}>0
and according to Claim 1, the proof is complete.
∎

Finally, let us to find out a minimum type solution
V_{0,\varepsilon}
with
I_{\varepsilon}(V_{0,\varepsilon})=c_{0,\varepsilon}<0
, where
c_{0,\varepsilon}
is given as in (
5.3
).

Lemma 5.7
.

For small
\varepsilon
, problem (
1
) has a nontrivial minimum type solution
V_{0,\varepsilon}
with
I_{\varepsilon}(V_{0,\varepsilon})=c_{0,\varepsilon}<0
.

Proof.

Let
\rho_{\varepsilon}
be as in Lemma
3.2
. Then we can choose
\varepsilon
sufficiently small such that

\displaystyle\rho_{\varepsilon}<\left(\frac{2\pi(2-\beta)}{\alpha_{0}}\right)^{1/2}.

Since
\overline{B}_{\rho_{\varepsilon}}
is a complete metric space with the metric given by the norm of
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})
, convex and the functional
I_{\varepsilon}
is of class
C^{1}
and bounded below on
\overline{B}_{\rho_{\varepsilon}}
, by the Ekeland variational principle, there exists a sequence
\{V_{n}\}
in
\overline{B}_{\rho_{\varepsilon}}
such that

I_{\varepsilon}(V_{n})\rightarrow c_{0,\varepsilon}=\inf_{\|V\|\leq\rho_{\varepsilon}}I_{\varepsilon}(V)<0\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ I^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(V_{n})\rightarrow 0.

Observing that

\displaystyle\|V_{n}\|^{2}\leq\rho_{\varepsilon}^{2}<\frac{2\pi(2-\beta)}{\alpha_{0}},

by Lemma
5.2
, there exists a strongly convergent subsequence and therefore, for some
V_{0,\varepsilon}
,
V_{n}\rightarrow V_{0,\varepsilon}
strongly in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})
. Consequently, we have
V_{0,\varepsilon}
is a minimum type solution of problem (
1
) with
I_{\varepsilon}(V_{0,\varepsilon})=c_{0,\varepsilon}<0
. We claim
V_{0,\varepsilon}\neq\mathbf{0}
. Indeed, suppose by a contradiction
V_{0,\varepsilon}=\mathbf{0}
, then
0>c_{0,\varepsilon}=I_{\varepsilon}(V_{0,\varepsilon})=I_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{0})=0
, what is absurd and this lemma is proved.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

By Lemmas
5.6
and
5.7
, there exists
\varepsilon_{c}>0
such that for each
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{c}
, there exist nontrivial critical points
U_{M,\varepsilon}
for
I_{\varepsilon}
at level
c_{M,\varepsilon}
and
V_{0,\varepsilon}
for
I_{\varepsilon}
at level
c_{0,\varepsilon}
. In the end, we claim
U_{M,\varepsilon}\neq V_{0,\varepsilon}
. Suppose by contradiction that
U_{M,\varepsilon}\equiv V_{0,\varepsilon}
, then
0>c_{0,\varepsilon}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}I_{\varepsilon}(V_{n})=I(V_{0,\varepsilon})=I(U_{M,\varepsilon})=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}I_{\varepsilon}(U_{n})=c_{M,\varepsilon}>0
, what is absurd. Thus, the proof of Theorem
1.2
is complete.
∎

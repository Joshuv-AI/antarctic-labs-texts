EXISTENCE OF TRAVELING WAVES OF LOTKA VOLTERRA TYPE MODELS WITH DELAYED DIFFUSION TERM AND PARTIAL QUASIMONOTONICITY
William Barker

Abstract
. This paper is concerned with the existence of traveling wave solutions for diffusive two-species Lotka-Volterra systems with delay in both the reaction and diffusion terms without monotonicity. We extend the partial or cross monotone iteration method to systems that satisfy the partial quasi-monotone condition via construction appropriate upper and lower solutions . This is done by using Schauder’s fixed point theorem.

Nonlinear parabolic partial differential equations play a vital role in mathematical modeling for engineering as well as the physical and biological sciences. The specific type of parabolic equations that we are interested in are called reaction diffusion equations. When no delay is present there is a myriad of scholarly work. The first work that studied the existence and properties of traveling waves in diffusion-reaction equations with delay in reaction term is attributed to Schaaf,
[

The seminal work for constructing monotone wave fronts for reaction diffusion is the paper by Wu and Zou

The existence of such solutions was established under a quasimonotone or exponential quasimonotone property via an iteration of appropriate upper and lower solutions. This is the so-called monotone iteration method.

The above results were extended by Ma,

The paper will be organized as follows: in Section 2 we will put forth preliminary information that will be utilized throughout the paper. In Section 3, we will show the existence of traveling waves for the system (

In this paper we will use some standard notations as
\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{n}

||f||:=\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|f(t)\|,

.

stands for the space of all
k

\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that all derivatives up to order

If the boundedness is dropped from the above function spaces we will simply denote them by
C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

f\leq g

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}

f(t)=\alpha

Moreover, we note that
f_{ic}(\phi_{\theta},\psi_{\theta}):\mathbb{X}_{c\tau}=C\left([-\tau,0],\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)\to\mathbb{R}

f_{ic}(\phi,\psi)=f_{i}\left(\phi^{c},\psi^{c}\right),\ \phi^{c}(\theta)=\phi(c\theta),\ \psi^{c}(\theta)=\psi(c\theta),\ \theta\in[\tau,0],\ i=1,2.

This allows us to see that the system (
1.2

(2.1)

\displaystyle D_{1}\phi^{\prime\prime}(t)-c\phi^{\prime}(t+r_{1})+f_{1c}(\phi_{t},\psi_{t})=0

\displaystyle D_{2}\psi^{\prime\prime}(t)-c\psi^{\prime}(t+r_{3})+f_{2c}(\phi_{t},\psi_{t})=0,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to-\infty}\phi(t)=0,\

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\phi(t)=k_{1}

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to-\infty}\psi(t)=0,\

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\psi(t)=k_{2},

f(\hat{0})=f(\hat{K})=0,

(C2)

\displaystyle\left|f_{1}(\phi_{1},\psi_{1})-f_{1}(\phi_{2},\psi_{2})\right|\leq L_{1}\left|\left|\Phi-\Psi\right|\right|

\displaystyle\left|f_{2}(\phi_{1},\psi_{1})-f_{2}(\phi_{2},\psi_{2})\right|\leq L_{2}\left|\left|\Phi-\Psi\right|\right|,

3.
The Case of Partial Quasimonotonicity

3.1.
Partial Quasimonotonicity Conditions

In this section, we develop sufficient conditions for the existence of traveling wave solutions for equations of the form (
2.1
). We invoke the following cross iteration scheme.

Definition 3.1
.

Partial Quasi-Monotone Condition (PQM)
Fix two constants
\beta_{1},\ \beta_{2}>0
such that

(P1)

f_{1}\left(\phi_{1},\psi_{1}\right)-f_{1}\left(\phi_{2},\psi_{1}\right)+\beta_{1}\left[\phi_{1}(0)-\phi_{2}(0)\right]\geq 0,

(P2)

f_{1}\left(\phi_{1},\psi_{1}\right)-f_{1}\left(\phi_{1},\psi_{2}\right)\leq 0,

(P3)

f_{2}\left(\phi_{1},\psi_{1}\right)-f_{2}\left(\phi_{2},\psi_{2}\right)+\beta_{2}\left[\psi_{1}(0)-\psi_{2}(0)\right]\geq 0.

(P4)

f_{2}\left(\phi_{1},\psi_{1}\right)-f_{2}\left(\phi_{2},\psi_{1}\right)\leq 0,

where
\phi_{1},\ \phi_{2},\ \psi_{1},\ \psi_{2}\in C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}),\ 0\leq\phi_{2}\leq\phi_{1}\leq k_{1},\ 0\leq\psi_{2}\leq\psi_{1}\leq k_{2}.

Let
\beta_{1},\ \beta_{2}
be the constants in (PQM), define the following operators

(3.1)

\displaystyle H_{1}(\phi,\psi)(t)=f_{1c}\left(\phi_{t},\psi_{t}\right)+\beta_{1}\phi(t+r_{1}),\ \phi,\psi\in C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})

(3.2)

\displaystyle H_{2}(\phi,\psi)(t)=f_{2c}\left(\phi_{t},\psi_{t}\right)+\beta_{2}\psi(t+r_{3}),\ \phi,\psi\in C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}).

The operators
H_{1},H_{2}
satisfy the following properties:

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume
(C1)
and (PQM) hold, then

i)

H_{1}(\phi_{2},\psi_{1})(t)\leq H_{1}(\phi_{1},\psi_{1})(t)

ii)

H_{1}(\phi_{1},\psi_{1})(t)\leq H_{1}(\phi_{1},\psi_{2})(t)

iii)

H_{2}\left(\phi,\psi\right)(t)\geq 0

iv)

H_{2}\left(\phi,\psi\right)(t)
is non-decreasing for
t\in\mathbb{R}

v)

H_{2}\left(\phi_{2},\psi_{2}\right)(t)\leq H_{2}\left(\phi_{1},\psi_{1}\right)(t).

Proof.

Looking towards
i.).
By (PQM) it is clear

\displaystyle H_{1}(\phi_{2},\psi_{1})(t)-H_{1}(\phi_{1},\psi_{1})(t)=f_{1c}\left(\phi_{2t},\psi_{1t}\right)+\beta_{1}\phi_{2}(t+r_{1})-\left(f_{1c}\left(\phi_{1t},\psi_{1t}\right)+\beta_{1}\phi_{1}(t+r_{1})\right)

\displaystyle=f_{1c}\left(\phi_{2t},\psi_{1t}\right)-f_{1c}\left(\phi_{1t},\psi_{1t}\right)+\beta_{1}\left(\phi_{2}(t+r_{1})-\phi_{1}(t+r_{1})\right)

\displaystyle=-\left[f_{1c}\left(\phi_{1t},\psi_{1t}\right)-f_{1c}\left(\phi_{2t},\psi_{1t}\right)+\beta_{1}\left(\phi_{1}(t+r_{1})-\phi_{2}(t+r_{1})\right)\right]\leq 0,

by (P1) pf (PQM). Thus,
H_{1}(\phi_{2},\psi_{1})(t)-H_{1}(\phi_{1},\psi_{1})(t)\leq 0
. This finishes
i.)
We will show
ii.)
via direct computation as well. Indeed,

\displaystyle H_{1}(\phi_{1},\psi_{1})(t)-H_{1}(\phi_{1},\psi_{2})(t)=f_{1c}\left(\phi_{1t},\psi_{1t}\right)+\beta_{1}\phi_{1}(t+r_{1})-\left(f_{1c}\left(\phi_{1t},\psi_{2t}\right)+\beta_{1}\phi_{1}(t+r_{1})\right)

\displaystyle=f_{1c}\left(\phi_{1t},\psi_{1t}\right)-f_{1c}\left(\phi_{1t},\psi_{2t}\right)\leq 0

by (P2) of (PQM). This proves
ii.)
Part
iii.)
follows directly from
(C_{1})
and each
\phi,\psi,\beta_{2}\geq 0.
In order to prove
iv.)
we fix
t\in\mathbb{R},s>0,
then

\displaystyle 0\leq\phi_{t}\leq\phi_{t+s}\leq k_{1}

\displaystyle 0\leq\psi_{t}\leq\psi_{t+s}\leq k_{2}

due to the fact that both
\phi
and
\psi
are non-decreasing. From here, we have

\displaystyle H_{2}\left(\phi,\psi\right)(t+s)-H_{2}\left(\phi,\psi\right)(t)=f_{2c}\left(\phi_{t+s},\psi_{t+s}\right)+\beta_{2}\phi(t+s)-\left(f_{2c}\left(\phi_{t},\psi_{t}\right)+\beta_{2}\phi(t)\right)

\displaystyle=f_{2c}\left(\phi_{t+s},\psi_{t+s}\right)-f_{2c}\left(\phi_{t},\psi_{t}\right)+\beta_{2}\left[\phi(t+s)-\phi(t)\right]

\displaystyle=f_{2c}\left(\phi_{t+s},\psi_{t+s}\right)-f_{2c}\left(\phi_{t},\psi_{t}\right)+\beta_{2}\left[\phi_{t+s}(0)-\phi_{t}(0)\right]\geq 0

by (P3) of (PQM). This proves
iv.)
Part
v.)
follows directly from (P3) of (PQM). This completes the proof.
∎

We can write the system (
2.1
) for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
as

(3.3)

\displaystyle D_{1}\phi^{\prime\prime}(t)-c\phi^{\prime}(t+r_{1})-\beta_{1}\phi(t+r_{1})+H_{1}\left(\phi,\psi\right)(t)=0

\displaystyle D_{2}\psi^{\prime\prime}(t)-c\psi^{\prime}(t+r_{3})-\beta_{2}\psi(t+r_{3})+H_{2}\left(\phi,\psi\right)(t)=0.

3.2.
Existence of Traveling Wave Solutions

In this section we will consider existence of positive solutions for the system (
3.3
). In
[
2
]
, it was shown that equations of the form
x^{\prime\prime}(t)-ax^{\prime}(t+r)-bx(t+r)=f(t)
have positive solutions where
r\in\mathbb{R},\ f\in BC\left(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}\right).
It was also noted that these results can be extended to higher dimensions.

In order to deal with the nonlinear part, we will show the existence of fixed point(s) by finding a set of appropriate convolution operators and applying Schrauder’s fixed point theorem.
Defining the set

(3.4)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}=\left\{\left(\phi,\psi\right)\in C\left(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{2}\right):\left(0,0\right)\leq\left(\phi,\psi\right)\leq\left(k_{1},k_{2}\right)\right\}

we rewrite Eq.(
3.3
) in the form

(3.5)

(\phi,\psi)^{T}=-{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}H(\phi,\psi)=\left(-{\mathcal{L}}_{1}^{-1}H_{1}(\phi,\psi),-{\mathcal{L}}_{2}^{-1}H_{2}(\phi,\psi)\right)^{T},\ \phi,\psi\in\Gamma_{1},

Taking
F:=-{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}H=-\left(F_{1}H_{1}(\phi,\psi),F_{2}H_{2}(\phi,\psi)\right)^{T}.
Thus, we can use the following Perron-Lyapunov integral operator

F=\left(F_{1}\left(\phi,\psi\right),F_{2}\left(\phi,\psi\right)\right):\Gamma_{1}\left(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)\to C\left(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)

defined by

(3.6)

\displaystyle F_{1}\left(\phi,\psi\right)

\displaystyle=\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}G_{1}(t-s,r)H_{1}(\phi(s),\psi(s))ds

\displaystyle F_{2}\left(\phi,\psi\right)

\displaystyle=\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}G_{2}(t-s,r)H_{2}(\phi(s),\psi(s))ds.

Here, we have positive constants
M_{1},M_{2},\delta_{1},\delta_{2}
that are uniformly bounded in
r
for some sufficiently small
r>0
such that for all
t\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle|G_{1}(t,r)|\leq M_{1}e^{-\delta_{1}|t|}

\displaystyle|G_{2}(t,r)|\leq M_{2}e^{-\delta_{2}|t|}.

See Theorem 4.1
[
20
]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Define
F=\left(F_{1}\left(\phi,\psi\right),F_{2}\left(\phi,\psi\right)\right)
as above, then for any
\left(\phi,\psi\right)\in\Gamma_{1},
then

i)

\left(F_{1}\left(\phi,\psi\right),F_{2}\left(\phi,\psi\right)\right):\Gamma_{1}\left(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)\to C\left(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)
is well defined.

ii)

F_{1}\left(\phi,\psi\right),F_{2}\left(\phi,\psi\right)
satisfy

(3.7)

\displaystyle D_{1}F_{1}^{\prime\prime}\left(\phi,\psi\right)-cF_{1}^{\prime}\left(\phi,\psi\right)-\beta_{1}F_{1}\left(\phi,\psi\right)+H_{1}\left(\phi,\psi\right)=0

\displaystyle D_{2}F_{2}^{\prime\prime}\left(\phi,\psi\right)-cF_{2}^{\prime}\left(\phi,\psi\right)-\beta_{2}F_{2}\left(\phi,\psi\right)+H_{2}\left(\phi,\psi\right)=0.

The proof is straight forward, and is hence omitted. Moreover, if
\left(\phi,\psi\right)
is a fixed point of

then the system (

enjoys similar properties as

in Lemma (

Lemma 3.4
.

Assume that
(C1)
and (PQM) hold, then

i)

F_{2}\left(\phi,\psi\right)(t)
is non-decreasing for
t\in\mathbb{R}

ii)

F_{1}(\phi_{2},\psi_{1})(t)\leq F_{1}(\phi_{1},\psi_{1})(t)

iii)

F_{1}(\phi_{1},\psi_{1})(t)\leq F_{1}(\phi_{1},\psi_{2})(t)

iv)

F_{2}\left(\phi_{2},\psi_{2}\right)(t)\leq F_{2}\left(\phi_{1},\psi_{1}\right)(t),

when

The proof follows from the inherited properties of

3.2.1.
Existence Via Schauder’s Fixed Point Theorem

We define the following set

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}\left(\left(\underline{\phi},\underline{\psi}\right),\left(\overline{\phi},\overline{\psi}\right)\right)=\bigl\{\left(\phi,\psi\right)\in C\left(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{2}\right):{}

\displaystyle i)\ \psi(t)\ \text{is nondecreasing in}\ \mathbb{R}.\ \text{and}

\displaystyle ii)\ \underline{\phi}(t)\leq\phi(t)\leq\overline{\phi},\ \underline{\psi}(t)\leq\psi(t)\leq\overline{\psi}(t)\bigr\}.

Furthermore, we define the exponential decay norm for some
\mu>0
as

|\Phi|_{\mu}=\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\ e^{-\mu|t|}||\Phi(t)||.

Now, define the ball as

B_{\mu}\left(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)=\left\{\Phi\in C\left(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{2}\right):|\Phi|_{\mu}<\infty\right\}.

Lemma 3.5
.

Define
\Gamma_{2}\left(\left(\underline{\phi},\underline{\psi}\right),\left(\overline{\phi},\overline{\psi}\right)\right),|\Phi|_{\mu},
and
B_{\mu}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{2})
as above, then

i)

\Gamma_{2}
is nonempty.

ii)

\Gamma_{2}
is closed, bounded and convex.

iii)

\Gamma_{2}\subset B_{\mu}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{2}).

iv)

\left(B_{\mu}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{2}),|\cdot|_{\mu}\right)
is a Banach space.

The result is classical, thus the proof is omitted. It is now possible to complete the requirements of Schauder’s theorem.

Lemma 3.6
.

Assume (C1), (C2) and (PQM) hold, then

i)

F:\Gamma_{2}\left(\left(\underline{\phi},\underline{\psi}\right),\left(\overline{\phi},\overline{\psi}\right)\right)\to\Gamma_{2}\left(\left(\underline{\phi},\underline{\psi}\right),\left(\overline{\phi},\overline{\psi}\right)\right).

ii)

F=(F_{1},F_{2})
is continuous with respect to
|\cdot|_{\mu}
in
B_{\mu}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{2}).

Proof.

For part
i.)
we only need to show that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\underline{\phi}\leq F_{1}(\underline{\phi},\overline{\psi})\leq F_{1}(\overline{\phi},\underline{\psi})\leq\overline{\phi}\\
\underline{\psi}\leq F_{2}(\overline{\phi},\underline{\psi})\leq F_{2}(\underline{\phi},\overline{\psi})\leq\overline{\psi}.\end{cases}

This is due to the fact

\displaystyle\begin{cases}F_{1}(\underline{\phi},\overline{\psi})\leq F_{1}(\phi,\psi)\leq F_{1}(\overline{\phi},\underline{\psi})\\
F_{2}(\overline{\phi},\underline{\psi})\leq F_{2}(\phi,\psi)\leq F_{2}(\underline{\phi},\overline{\psi}).\end{cases}

We only show the first inequality, because the second follows the same way. We first show that
\underline{\phi}\leq F_{1}(\underline{\phi},\overline{\psi})
.

\displaystyle F_{1}(\underline{\phi},\overline{\psi})(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}G_{1}(t-s,r)H_{1}(\underline{\phi},\overline{\psi})(s)ds

\displaystyle\geq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}G_{1}(t-s,r)\left(-D_{1}\underline{\phi}^{\prime\prime}(t)+c\underline{\phi}^{\prime}(t+r_{1})+\beta_{1}\underline{\phi}(t+r_{1})\right)ds=\underline{\phi}(t)

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}.

Next, we show
F_{1}(\overline{\phi},\underline{\psi})\leq\overline{\phi}
.

\displaystyle F_{1}(\overline{\phi},\underline{\psi})(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}G_{1}(t-s,r)H_{1}(\overline{\phi},\underline{\psi})(s)ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}G_{1}(t-s,r)\left(-D_{1}\overline{\phi}^{\prime\prime}(t)+c\overline{\phi}^{\prime}(t+r_{1})+\beta_{1}\overline{\phi}(t+r_{1})\right)ds=\overline{\phi}(t)

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}.
The proof is complete. The method of proof for part
ii.)
is similar to
[
15
]
, Lemma 3.4 with several modifications. To this end, we only need to show

F_{1}:B_{\mu}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{2})\to B_{\mu}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{2})

is continuous with respect to
|\cdot|_{\mu},
because the proof for
F_{2}(\phi,\psi)
is very similar. Take
\mu<\max\{\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\}

\displaystyle\overline{\phi}_{1}^{\prime\prime}(t)-c\overline{\phi}_{1}^{\prime}(t+r_{1})+\alpha_{1}\overline{\phi}_{1}(t+r_{1})\left(1-\overline{\phi}_{1}(t+r_{1})-a\underline{\phi_{2}}(t+(r_{1}-r_{2})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda_{1}^{2}}{2}e^{\lambda_{1}t}-c\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}e^{\lambda_{1}(t+r_{1})}+\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2}e^{\lambda_{1}(t+r_{1})}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}e^{\lambda_{1}(t+r_{1}-r_{2})}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2}e^{\lambda_{1}(t+r_{1})}\left(\frac{1}{2}e^{\lambda_{1}(t+r_{1}-r_{2})}\right)\leq 0.

For the second equation we have

\displaystyle\overline{\phi}_{2}^{\prime\prime}(t)-c\overline{\phi}_{2}^{\prime}(t+r_{3})+\alpha_{2}\overline{\phi}_{2}(t+r_{3})\left(1-\overline{\phi}_{2}(t+r_{3})-b\overline{\phi_{1}}(t+(r_{3}-r_{4}))\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu_{1}^{2}}{2}e^{\mu_{1}t}-c\frac{\mu_{1}}{2}e^{\mu_{1}(t+r_{3})}+\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2}e^{\mu_{1}(t+r_{3})}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}e^{\mu_{1}(t+r_{3})}-\frac{b}{2}e^{\lambda_{1}(t+(r_{3}-r_{4}))}\right)

\displaystyle=e^{\mu_{1}t}\left(\frac{\mu_{1}^{2}}{2}-c\frac{\mu_{1}}{2}e^{\mu_{1}r_{3}}+\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2}e^{\mu_{1}r_{3}}\right)-\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2}e^{\mu_{1}(t+r_{3})}\left(\frac{1}{2}e^{\mu_{1}(t+r_{3})}+\frac{b}{2}e^{\lambda_{1}(t+(r_{3}-r_{4}))}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2}e^{\mu_{1}(t+r_{3})}\left(\frac{1}{2}e^{\mu_{1}(t+r_{3})}+\frac{b}{2}e^{\lambda_{1}(t+(r_{3}-r_{4}))}\right)\leq 0.

Case 2:
-r\leq t\leq 0.
Direct substitution into the first equation of Eq.
4.2
yields:

\displaystyle\overline{\phi}_{1}^{\prime\prime}(t)-c\overline{\phi}_{1}^{\prime}(t+r_{1})+\alpha_{1}\overline{\phi}_{1}(t+r_{1})\left(1-\overline{\phi}_{1}(t+r_{1})-a\underline{\phi_{2}}(t+(r_{1}-r_{2}))\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda_{1}^{2}}{2}e^{\lambda_{1}t}-c\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}e^{-\lambda_{1}(t+r_{1})}+\alpha_{1}\overline{\phi}_{1}(t+r_{1})\left(1-\overline{\phi}_{1}(t+r_{1})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda_{1}^{2}}{2}e^{\lambda_{1}t}-c\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}e^{\lambda_{1}(t+r_{1})}+\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2}e^{\lambda_{1}(t+r_{1})}+c\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}e^{\lambda_{1}(t+r_{1})}-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2}e^{\lambda_{1}(t+r_{1})}-c\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}e^{-\lambda_{1}(t+r_{1})}

\displaystyle+\alpha_{1}\overline{\phi}_{1}(t+r_{1})\left(1-\overline{\phi}_{1}(t+r_{1})\right)

\displaystyle=c\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}e^{\lambda_{1}(t+r_{1})}-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2}e^{\lambda_{1}(t+r_{1})}-c\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}e^{-\lambda_{1}(t+r_{1})}+\alpha_{1}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}e^{-\lambda_{1}(t+r_{1})}\right)\frac{1}{2}e^{-\lambda_{1}(t+r_{1})}

\displaystyle=(c\lambda_{1}-\alpha_{1})\sinh(\lambda_{1}(t+r_{1}))-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{4}e^{-2\lambda_{1}(t+r_{1})}.

Using the power expansion of
\sinh x
we have

\sinh(\lambda_{1}(t+r_{1}))\approx\lambda_{1}(t+r_{1})+o(r^{2}_{1}).
Thus, for some small
r_{1}
we have

(c\lambda_{1}-\alpha_{1})\sinh(\lambda_{1}(t+r_{1}))-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{4}e^{-2\lambda_{1}(t+r_{1})}=(c\lambda_{1}-\alpha_{1})\left(\lambda_{1}(t+r_{1})+o(r^{2}_{1})\right)-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{4}e^{-2\lambda_{1}(t+r_{1})}\leq 0.

For the second equation we have

\displaystyle\overline{\phi}_{2}^{\prime\prime}(t)-c\overline{\phi}_{2}^{\prime}(t+r_{3})+\alpha_{2}\overline{\phi}_{2}(t+r_{3})\left(1-\overline{\phi}_{2}(t+r_{3})-b\overline{\phi_{1}}(t+(r_{3}-r_{4}))\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu_{1}^{2}}{2}e^{\mu_{1}t}-\frac{c\mu_{1}}{2}e^{-\mu_{1}(t+r_{3})}+\alpha_{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}e^{-\mu_{1}(t+r_{3})}\right)\left[1-\left(1-\frac{1}{2}e^{-\mu_{1}(t+r_{3})}\right)-\frac{b}{2}e^{\lambda_{1}(t+r_{3}-r_{4})}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu_{1}^{2}}{2}e^{\mu_{1}t}-\frac{c\mu_{1}}{2}e^{\mu_{1}(t+r_{3})}+\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2}e^{\mu_{1}(t+r_{3})}+\frac{c\mu_{1}}{2}e^{\mu_{1}(t+r_{3})}-\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2}e^{\mu_{1}(t+r_{3})}-\frac{c\mu_{1}}{2}e^{-\mu_{1}(t+r_{3})}

\displaystyle+\alpha_{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}e^{-\mu_{1}(t+r_{3})}\right)\left[1-\left(1-\frac{1}{2}e^{-\mu_{1}(t+r_{3})}\right)-\frac{b}{2}e^{\lambda_{1}(t+r_{3}-r_{4})}\right]

\displaystyle=c\mu_{1}\sinh(\mu_{1}r_{3})-\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2}\sinh(\mu_{1}r_{3})-\alpha_{2}\left[\frac{1}{4}e^{-2\mu_{1}(t+r_{3})}+\frac{b}{2}e^{\lambda_{1}(t+r_{3}-r_{4})}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}e^{-\mu_{1}(t+r_{3})}\right)\right].

This is less than zero as
r_{3}\to 0
, so we have the result.

Case 3:
0<t.
Direction substitution into the first equation of Eq.
4.2
yields:

\displaystyle\overline{\phi}_{1}^{\prime\prime}(t)-c\overline{\phi}_{1}^{\prime}(t+r_{1})+\alpha_{1}\overline{\phi}_{1}(t+r_{1})\left(1-\overline{\phi}_{1}(t+r_{1})-a\underline{\phi_{2}}(t+(r_{1}-r_{2}))\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{-\lambda_{1}^{2}}{2}e^{-\lambda_{1}t}-\frac{c\lambda_{1}}{2}e^{-\lambda_{1}(t+r_{1})}+\alpha_{1}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}e^{-\lambda_{1}(t+r_{1})}\right)\left(1-\left(1-\frac{1}{2}e^{-\lambda_{1}(t+r_{1})}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{-\lambda_{1}^{2}}{2}e^{-\lambda_{1}t}+\frac{c\lambda_{1}}{2}e^{-\lambda_{1}t+\lambda_{1}r_{1}}-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2}e^{-\lambda_{1}t+\lambda_{1}r_{1}}-\frac{c\lambda_{1}}{2}e^{-\lambda_{1}t+\lambda_{1}r_{1}}+\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2}e^{-\lambda_{1}t+\lambda_{1}r_{1}}

\displaystyle-\frac{c\lambda_{1}}{2}e^{-\lambda_{1}(t+r_{1})}+\alpha_{1}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}e^{-\lambda_{1}(t+r_{1})}\right)\left(1-\left(1-\frac{1}{2}e^{-\lambda_{1}(t+r_{1})}\right)\right)

It is clear that

-\alpha_{1}\left(1-\frac{e^{-\lambda_{1}t-\lambda_{1}r_{1}}}{2}\right)\left(1-\frac{e^{-\lambda_{1}(t+r_{1}-r_{2})}}{2}\right)\leq 0.

Furthermore,

\lim_{r_{1}\to 0}\alpha_{1}-c\lambda_{1}\cosh(\lambda_{1}r_{1})+\alpha_{1}\sinh(\lambda_{1}r_{1})=\alpha_{1}-c\lambda_{0},

because
\lambda_{1}\to\lambda_{0}.
A simple calculation shows

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}-c\lambda_{0}=\alpha_{1}-c\left(\frac{c+\sqrt{c^{2}-4\alpha_{1}}}{2}\right)\leq 0,

due to the fact that
c\geq 2\sqrt{\alpha_{1}}.
This means that there exists some
r^{*}>0
dependent on
c
such that
0<r_{1}\leq r^{*}(c)
and

\left(\alpha_{1}-c\lambda_{1}\cosh(\lambda_{1}r_{1})+\alpha_{1}\sinh(\lambda_{1}r_{1})\right)e^{-\lambda_{1}t}-\alpha_{1}\left(1-\frac{e^{-\lambda_{1}t-\lambda_{1}r_{1}}}{2}\right)\left(1-\frac{e^{-\lambda_{1}(t+r_{1})}}{2}\right)\leq 0.

For the second equation we have

\displaystyle\overline{\phi}_{2}^{\prime\prime}(t)-c\overline{\phi}_{2}^{\prime}(t+r_{3})+\alpha_{2}\overline{\phi}_{2}(t+r_{3})\left(1-\overline{\phi}_{2}(t+r_{3})-b\overline{\phi_{1}}(t+r_{3}-r_{4})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{-\mu_{1}^{2}}{2}e^{-\mu_{1}t}-c\frac{\mu_{1}}{2}e^{-\mu_{1}(t+r_{3})}+\alpha_{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}e^{-\mu_{1}(t+r_{3})}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(1-\left(1-\frac{1}{2}e^{-\mu_{1}(t+r_{3})}\right)-b\left(1-\frac{1}{2}e^{-\lambda_{1}(t+r_{3}-r_{4})}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\alpha_{2}-c\mu_{1}\cosh(\mu_{1}r_{3})+\alpha_{2}\sinh(\mu_{1}r_{3})\right)e^{-\mu_{1}t}

\displaystyle-\alpha_{2}\left(\left(1-\frac{1}{2}e^{-\mu_{1}(t+r_{3})}\right)+b\left(1-\frac{1}{2}e^{-\lambda_{1}(t+r_{3}-r_{4})}\right)\right)\leq 0

by the same reasoning as the first equation in this case. This proves the Lemma.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

For sufficiently small
r_{1},r_{2}
and
c>\max\{2\sqrt{\alpha_{1}},2\sqrt{\alpha_{2}}\}
where
\overline{\phi}_{1},\overline{\phi}_{2},\underline{\phi_{1}},\underline{\phi_{2}}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, then
\left(\underline{\phi_{1}},\underline{\phi_{2}}\right)^{T}
is a quasi-lower solution of Eq.
4.2
.

Proof.

We will show this Lemma in cases like above. In fact, we only need to show that the inequality is satisfied for the first equation in the model due to the fact for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
the second equation always is zero. This is shown by

\displaystyle\underline{\phi}_{2}^{\prime\prime}(t)-c\underline{\phi}_{2}^{\prime}(t+r_{3})+\alpha_{2}\underline{\phi}_{2}(t+r_{3})\left(1-\underline{\phi}_{2}(t+r_{3})-b\underline{\phi_{1}}(t+r_{3}-r_{4})\right)=0.

Case 1:
t\leq-T-r.
Direction substitution into the first equation of Eq.
4.2
yields:

\displaystyle\underline{\phi}_{1}^{\prime\prime}(t)-c\underline{\phi}_{1}^{\prime}(t+r_{1})+\alpha_{1}\underline{\phi}_{1}(t+r_{1})\left(1-\underline{\phi}_{1}(t+r_{1})-a\overline{\phi_{2}}(t+r_{1}-r_{2})\right)

\displaystyle=\left[\underline{\phi}_{1}^{\prime\prime}(t)-c\underline{\phi}_{1}^{\prime}(t+r_{1})+\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2}\underline{\phi}_{1}(t+r_{1})\right]+\alpha_{1}\underline{\phi}_{1}(t+r_{1})\left(\frac{1}{2}-\underline{\phi}_{1}(t+r_{1})-a\overline{\phi_{2}}(t+r_{1}-r_{2})\right)

\displaystyle=e^{\lambda_{2}t}\left[\lambda_{2}^{2}-c\lambda_{2}e^{\lambda_{2}r_{1}}+\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2}e^{\lambda_{2}r_{1}}\right]+\alpha_{1}\underline{\phi}_{1}(t+r_{1})\left(\frac{1}{2}-\underline{\phi}_{1}(t+r_{1})-a\overline{\phi_{2}}(t+r_{1}-r_{2})\right)

\displaystyle=\alpha_{1}\underline{\phi}_{1}(t+r_{1})\left(\frac{1}{2}-\underline{\phi}_{1}(t+r_{1})-a\overline{\phi_{2}}(t+r_{1}-r_{2})\right).

However, we can take
T
to be sufficiently large enough such that

\alpha_{1}\underline{\phi}_{1}(t+r_{1})\left(\frac{1}{2}-\underline{\phi}_{1}(t+r_{1})-a\overline{\phi_{2}}(t+r_{1}-r_{2})\right)\geq 0.

Case 2:
-r-T<t\leq T.
Direction substitution into the first equation of Eq.
4.2
yields:

\displaystyle\underline{\phi}_{1}^{\prime\prime}(t)-c\underline{\phi}_{1}^{\prime}(t+r_{1})+\alpha_{1}\underline{\phi}_{1}(t+r_{1})\left(1-\underline{\phi}_{1}(t+r_{1})-a\overline{\phi_{2}}(t+r_{1}-r_{2})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\left(f^{\prime\prime}(t)-cf^{\prime}(t+r_{1})+\alpha_{1}f(t+r_{1})\left(1-\frac{f(t+r_{1})}{4}-a\overline{\phi_{2}}(t+r_{1}-r_{2})\right)\right).

Again, we can take
T
large enough such that

\alpha_{1}f(t+r_{1})\left(1-\frac{f(t+r_{1})}{4}-a\overline{\phi_{2}}(t+r_{1}-r_{2})\right)\geq 0.

In
[
2
]
it was noted that on this interval

\displaystyle=\sup_{-T-r_{1}\leq t\leq T}|\underline{\phi}_{1}^{\prime\prime}(t)|+c|\underline{\phi}_{1}^{\prime}(t+r_{1})|

\displaystyle=\sup_{-T\leq t\leq T}|f^{\prime}(t)|+c|f^{\prime\prime}(t)|,

so we have that

\frac{1}{4}\left(f^{\prime\prime}(t)-cf^{\prime}(t+r_{1})+\alpha_{1}f(t+r_{1})\left(1-\frac{f(t+r_{1})}{4}-a\overline{\phi_{2}}(t+r_{1}-r_{2})\right)\right)\geq 0.

Case 3:
t\geq T.
Direction substitution into the first equation of Eq.
4.2
yields:

\displaystyle\underline{\phi}_{1}^{\prime\prime}(t)-c\underline{\phi}_{1}^{\prime}(t+r_{1})+\alpha_{1}\underline{\phi}_{1}(t+r_{1})\left(1-\underline{\phi}_{1}(t+r_{1})-a\overline{\phi_{2}}(t+r_{1}-r_{2})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}-a\overline{\phi_{2}}(t+r_{1}-r_{2})\right)\geq 0.

This concludes the proof.
∎

Fixed-Price Approximations in Bilateral Trade
Zi Yang Kang, Francisco Pernice, Jan Vondrák

Abstract
We consider the bilateral trade problem, in which two agents trade a single indivisible item. It is known that the only dominant-strategy truthful mechanism is the fixed-price mechanism: given commonly known distributions of the buyer’s value B B and the seller’s value S S , a price p p is offered to both agents and trade occurs if S ≤ p ≤ B S\leq p\leq B . The objective is to maximize either expected welfare 𝔼 ⁡ [ S + ( B − S ) ​ 𝟏 S ≤ p ≤ B ] \mathbb{E}[S+(B-S)\mathbf{1}_{S\leq p\leq B}] or expected gains from trade 𝔼 ⁡ [ ( B − S ) ​ 𝟏 S ≤ p ≤ B ] \mathbb{E}[(B-S)\mathbf{1}_{S\leq p\leq B}] . We improve the approximation ratios for several welfare maximization variants of this problem. When the agents’ distributions are identical, we show that the optimal approximation ratio for welfare is 2 + 2 4 \frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{4} . With just one prior sample from the common distribution, we show that a 3 / 4 3/4 -approximation to welfare is achievable. When agents’ distributions are not required to be identical, we show that a previously best-known ( 1 − 1 / e ) (1-1/e) -approximation can be strictly improved, but 1 − 1 / e 1-1/e is optimal if only the seller’s distribution is known.

Introduction

In this paper, we study the performance of fixed-price mechanisms in the canonical context of bilateral trade, in which a buyer and seller bargain over a single indivisible good. As is standard in the literature, agents have independent private values for the good,
B
for the buyer and
S
for the seller, and the mechanism designer is given some information about the distributions from which these values are drawn. We consider two basic settings in this paper: one where the distributions of the agents’ values are identical (the “symmetric” case), and one where the distributions are arbitrary (the “general” or “asymmetric” case).
We measure the performance of a mechanism by considering either (i) the “gains from trade”:
B-S
whenever trade occurs and
0
otherwise, or (ii) the “welfare”:
B
if trade occurs and
S
otherwise. Observe that the difference between welfare and gains from trade is simply the seller’s value.

The “first-best” optimum is considered to be
\max\{B,S\}
for welfare, and
\max\{B-S,0\}
for gains from trade. In their seminal work, Myerson and Satterthwaite
[
17
]
showed that no budget-balanced, individually rational Bayesian incentive-compatible (BIC) mechanism attains the first-best optimum in general. They also presented a “second-best” mechanism which is budget-balanced, individually rational, BIC, and achieves the best possible gains from trade attainable by any such mechanism. However, the second-best mechanism is complicated and difficult to implement in practical settings, so a recent growing literature seeks simpler mechanisms that maintain provably near-optimal approximation guarantees.

Motivated by the need for strategic simplicity, we focus on dominant-strategy incentive compatible (DSIC) mechanisms. A fundamental result of Hagerty and Rogerson
[
14
]
is that essentially every DSIC mechanism for bilateral trade has the following form: the mechanism designer chooses a (possibly random) price and offers trade at this price to the buyer and seller. Trade occurs if and only if the buyer’s value exceeds the chosen price and the seller’s value does not; this mechanism is DSIC. We refer to this as a
fixed-price
or
posted-price
mechanism. Given this explicit characterization of the allowed mechanisms under our incentive compatibility criterion, other questions arise. How close to the efficient outcome can fixed-price mechanisms get? What information about the agents is required to set the optimal price? In this paper we give a comprehensive answer to these questions in the special case of symmetric bilateral trade, and partial answers in the general case.

2
Model

Here we formally outline the model that was discussed above.

Bilateral Trade.

The bilateral trade problem is simple: two agents, a buyer and a seller, value an item that is held by the seller at
B
and
S
respectively. The specific realizations of
B
and
S
are unknown to us, but we assume that we have
B\sim F_{B}
and
S\sim F_{S}
, with
B
and
S
independent, for distribution functions
F_{S}
and
F_{B}
, about which we are given some information.

Bilateral Trade Mechanisms.

A bilateral trade mechanism consists of an allocation function
A:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to\{0,1\}
, which takes in as input the reported valuations of the buyer and seller and outputs 1 if a transaction should occur or 0 otherwise, and a payment function
\Pi:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
which, if a trade occurs, determines the price at which the item should be transacted.
By the revelation principle
[
13
]
,
we are interested in
incentive-compatible
mechanisms
(A,\Pi)
, where the agents are incentivized to report their true values. There are two standard notions of incentive compatibility that are considered in the literature: Bayesian incentive compatibility (BIC), and dominant-strategy incentive compatibility (DSIC). The former roughly says that reporting true values should be an optimal strategy for each agent,
in expectation
over the possible behavior of the other agent. The latter notion, which is what we will consider in this paper, means that reporting true values is
always
an optimal strategy for all agents, regardless of what the other agent does.
Hence the notion that agents have incentive to act according to their true preferences is fully described by the DSIC property of a mechanism, which is formally
defined as

\begin{cases}\Pi(s,b)-s\cdot A(s,b)\geq\Pi(s^{\prime},b)-s\cdot A(s^{\prime},b),\\
b\cdot A(s,b)-\Pi(s,b)\geq b\cdot A(s,b^{\prime})-\Pi(s^{\prime},b^{\prime})\end{cases}\quad\text{for all }s,s^{\prime},b,b^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}.

That is, both agents are always better off reporting their true preferences. It was shown by
[
14
]
that such mechanisms are essentially
fixed-price
mechanisms, where the payment function
\Pi
is taken to be a (possibly random) single value
p
, only depending on the distribution from which the valuations come from (and not on the valuations themselves). Then the allocation function is given by
A(b,s)={\bf 1}_{s\leq p\leq b}
: trade occurs if and only if the seller values the item less than
p
, and the buyer values it more.

Gains From Trade and Welfare.

As discussed above, we consider two benchmarks in this paper: gains from trade and welfare. The optimal gains from trade, given a distribution function
F
from which the valuations are drawn, is defined as

\mathsf{OPT\text{-}GFT}(F)=\mathbb{E}[{\bf 1}_{B\geq S}(B-S)],

while the gains from trade achieved by a particular, non-random posted price
p
is given by

\mathsf{GFT}(p,F)=\mathbb{E}[{\bf 1}_{B\geq p\geq S}(B-S)].

Similarly, the corresponding welfare measures are defined as

\displaystyle\mathsf{OPT\text{-}W}(F)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[S]+\mathbb{E}[{\bf 1}_{B\geq S}(B-S)]=\mathbb{E}[S]+\mathsf{OPT\text{-}GFT}(F),

\displaystyle\mathsf{W}(p,F)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[S]+\mathbb{E}[{\bf 1}_{B\geq p\geq S}(B-S)]=\mathbb{E}[S]+\mathsf{GFT}(p,F).

It is often useful to have simplified integral formulas for these quantities. We now state two such formulas, both initially derived by McAfee
[
16
]
; for completeness, we offer derivations for them in the appendix.

\displaystyle\mathsf{OPT\text{-}GFT}(F)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}F(x)(1-F(x))\ \mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle\mathsf{GFT}(p,F)

\displaystyle=F(p)\int_{p}^{\infty}(1-F(x))\ \mathrm{d}x+(1-F(p))\int_{0}^{p}F(x)\ \mathrm{d}x.

Main Results

We organize the results into three subsections. In the first subsection, we focus on the setting where only one sample from

In the second subsection, we work in the setting of full access to the common distribution

is not optimal; however, we show that
1-1/e

3.1
Single-Sample Approximation of Welfare

As mentioned before, Babaioff, Goldner and Gonczarowski
[
3
]
have shown that posting a single sample as a price in the symmetric setting achieves
exactly
a 1/2-approximation of the expected optimal gains from trade, under the assumption that the distribution is atomless. This matches the best-possible achievable ratio for this model even if full information about the distribution is given. We offer a somewhat different proof of this fact in the appendix (Theorem
12
).

By similar arguments, we obtain a 3/4 approximation of optimal welfare. Recall the representations of the welfare measures from the previous section:

\displaystyle\mathsf{OPT\text{-}W}(F)

\displaystyle=\mu+\mathsf{OPT\text{-}GFT}(F),

\displaystyle\mathsf{W}(F,p)

\displaystyle=\mu+\mathsf{GFT}(F,p),

where
\mu:=\mathbb{E}[S]=\mathbb{E}[B].
We then get the following result.

Theorem 1

The symmetric bilateral trade mechanism which under a valuation distribution
F
posts a price
p\sim F
achieves a 3/4-approximation of the optimal welfare.

Using that
\mathbb{E}_{p\sim F}[\mathsf{GFT}(F,p)]/\mathsf{OPT\text{-}GFT}(F)=1/2,
we can write

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{E}_{p\sim F}[\mathsf{W}(F,p)]}{\mathsf{OPT\text{-}W}(F)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu+\mathbb{E}_{p\sim F}[\mathsf{GFT}(F,p)]}{\mu+\mathsf{OPT\text{-}GFT}(F)}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathsf{OPT\text{-}GFT}(F)}{\mu+\mathsf{OPT\text{-}GFT}(F)}

\displaystyle=1-\lim_{a\to\infty}\frac{1}{2}\frac{\int_{0}^{a}F(x)(1-F(x))\ \mathrm{d}x}{\int_{0}^{a}xf(x)\ \mathrm{d}x+\int_{0}^{a}F(x)(1-F(x))\ \mathrm{d}x}

\displaystyle=1-\lim_{a\to\infty}\frac{1}{2}\frac{\int_{0}^{a}F(x)\ \mathrm{d}x-\int_{0}^{a}F(x)^{2}\ \mathrm{d}x}{aF(a)-\int_{0}^{a}F(x)^{2}\ \mathrm{d}x}.

Note that we have
\int_{0}^{a}F(x)\ \mathrm{d}x\leq a^{1/2}(\int_{0}^{a}F(x)^{2}\ \mathrm{d}x)^{1/2}
by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality. Thus letting
t:=(\int_{0}^{a}F(x)^{2}\ \mathrm{d}x)^{1/2},
and noting that
0\leq t\leq a^{1/2}
and
F(a)\leq 1,
we get

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{0}^{a}F(x)\ \mathrm{d}x-\int_{0}^{a}F(x)^{2}\ \mathrm{d}x}{aF(a)-\int_{0}^{a}F(x)^{2}\ \mathrm{d}x}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a^{1/2}t-t^{2}}{aF(a)-t^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{F(a)}\frac{t(a^{1/2}-t)}{(a^{1/2}+t)(a^{1/2}-t)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{F(a)}\frac{t}{a^{1/2}+t}.

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{F(a)}\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{a\to\infty}1/2.

Thus we have
\frac{\mathbb{E}_{p\sim F}[\mathsf{W}(F,p)]}{\mathsf{OPT\text{-}W}(F)}\geq 1-\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2}=3/4,
as desired.

The approximation lower bound of 3/4 from Theorem
1
for our mechanism is tight, as we show next.

Lemma 2

The 3/4-approximation bound of Theorem
1
is tight for the mechanism using a sample
p\sim F
as a price.

Take the distribution functions
F_{r}(x)=\min\{x^{r},1\}
parameterized by
r\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.
From the proof of Theorem
1
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{E}_{p\sim F}[\mathsf{W}(F_{r},p)]}{\mathsf{OPT\text{-}W}(F_{r})}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\int_{0}^{1}x^{r}(1-x^{r})dx}{r\int_{0}^{1}x^{r}dx+\int_{0}^{1}x^{r}(1-x^{r})dx}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\frac{1}{r+1}-\frac{1}{2r+1}}{\frac{r}{r+1}+\frac{1}{r+1}-\frac{1}{2r+1}}=1-\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{r}{2r^{2}+2r}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{r\to 0}1-\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2}=\frac{3}{4}.

3.2
Best-Possible Approximation of Welfare

We begin by showing that the optimal posted price in the symmetric setting is given simply by the mean of the common distribution.

Proposition 3

The welfare-maximizing mechanism for symmetric bilateral trade is given by posting the mean of the common distribution
F
:

p^{*}=\mathbb{E}[S]=\mathbb{E}[B].

For any positive price
p>0
, we may rewrite the welfare function as

\displaystyle\mathsf{W}(p,F)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[S]+\mathbb{E}[(B-S)\cdot{\bf 1}_{B\geq p\geq p}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[S]+\mathbb{E}[B\cdot{\bf 1}_{B\geq p}]\cdot F(p)-\mathbb{E}[S\cdot{\bf 1}_{S\leq p}]\cdot\left[1-F(p)\right].

Because both agents’ values are drawn from the same distribution,

\mathbb{E}[B\cdot{\bf 1}_{B\geq p}]=\mathbb{E}[S\cdot{\bf 1}_{S\geq p}]=\mathbb{E}[S]-\mathbb{E}[S\cdot{\bf 1}_{S\leq p}].

Thus:

\displaystyle\mathsf{W}(p,F)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[S]+\mathbb{E}[B\cdot{\bf 1}_{B\geq p}]\cdot F(p)-\mathbb{E}[S\cdot{\bf 1}_{S\leq p}]\cdot\left[1-F(p)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[S]\cdot\left[1+F(p)\right]-\mathbb{E}[S\cdot{\bf 1}_{S\leq p}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[S]\cdot\left[1+F(p)\right]-p\cdot F(p)+\int_{0}^{p}F(s)\ \mathrm{d}s.

We thus obtain that for
\delta\in[0,p^{*}]
,

\mathsf{W}(p^{*};F)-\mathsf{W}(p^{*}-\delta;F)=\left[F(p^{*})-F(p^{*}-\delta)\right]\cdot\left(\mathbb{E}[S]-p^{*}\right)-\delta\cdot F(p^{*}-\delta)+\int_{p^{*}-\delta}^{p^{*}}F(x)\ \mathrm{d}x,

and for
\delta\in[0,\infty)
,

\mathsf{W}(p^{*};F)-\mathsf{W}(p^{*}+\delta;F)=\left[F(p^{*})-F(p^{*}+\delta)\right]\cdot\left(\mathbb{E}[S]-p^{*}\right)+\delta\cdot F(p^{*}+\delta)-\int_{p^{*}}^{p^{*}+\delta}F(x)\ \mathrm{d}x.

In the first expression, since
F
is non-decreasing,

\int_{p^{*}-\delta}^{p^{*}}F(x)\ \mathrm{d}x\geq\int_{p^{*}-\delta}^{p^{*}}F(p^{*}-\delta)\ \mathrm{d}x=\delta\cdot F(p^{*}-\delta).

Likewise, in the second expression, the same observation yields

-\int_{p^{*}}^{p^{*}+\delta}F(x)\ \mathrm{d}x\geq-\int_{p^{*}}^{p^{*}+\delta}F(p^{*}+\delta)\ \mathrm{d}x=-\delta\cdot F(p^{*}+\delta).

Therefore, with
p^{*}=\mathbb{E}[S]=\mathbb{E}[B]
, substitution yields

\begin{cases}\mathsf{W}(p^{*};F)-\mathsf{W}(p^{*}-\delta;F)&\geq\left[F(p^{*})-F(p^{*}-\delta)\right]\cdot\left(\mathbb{E}[S]-p^{*}\right)=0,\\
\mathsf{W}(p^{*};F)-\mathsf{W}(p^{*}+\delta;F)&\geq\left[F(p^{*})-F(p^{*}+\delta)\right]\cdot\left(\mathbb{E}[S]-p^{*}\right)=0.\end{cases}

Therefore the optimal fixed-price mechanism is given by
p^{*}=\mathbb{E}[S]
.

Proposition
3
shows that only the first moment of the agents’ distribution is required by the mechanism designer to determine the optimal fixed price; all higher moments are inconsequential. Therefore, precise knowledge of the agents’ distribution, other than the mean of the distribution, is irrelevant.

In general, the fixed-price mechanism
p^{*}=\mathbb{E}[S]
is not uniquely optimal. Suppose, for example, that each agent’s value is 0 with probability
1/2
and 1 with probability
1/2
. Then any price
p\in(0,1)
is optimal. Nonetheless, under weak regularity conditions on
F
, the mean is the uniquely optimal price.

Corollary 4

Suppose that
F
is differentiable in a neighborhood of its mean, so that its density
f=F^{\prime}
is positive in that neighborhood. Then the optimal symmetric bilateral trade fixed-price mechanism that sets the price as the mean of the distribution
F
is uniquely optimal.

The proof of Corollary
4
can be directly obtained from the proof of Proposition
3
by noting that the inequalities must hold strictly under the stated assumptions; we thus omit the details. In particular, Corollary
4
applies under the usual assumption that
F
is continuously differentiable with positive density.

Despite Corollary
4
, we proceed without additional regularity assumptions on
F
to obtain a general analysis.
Given the mean
\mu:=\mathbb{E}[S]
of the agents’ distribution, we can now determine the expected welfare that the optimal fixed-price mechanism achieves,
\mathsf{W}(\mu;F)
:

\displaystyle\mathsf{W}(\mu;F)

\displaystyle=\mu\cdot\left[1+F(\mu)\right]-\mathbb{E}[S\cdot{\bf 1}_{S\leq\mu}]

\displaystyle=\mu+\left(\mu-\mathbb{E}[S\,|\,S\leq\mu]\right)\cdot F(\mu).

An interesting observation here is that the maximum expected welfare achieved by
any
fixed-price mechanism depends on the agents’ distribution
F
through only three quantities: (i) the mean of the distribution,
\mu
; (ii) the quantile of the mean,
F(\mu)
; and (iii) the mean of the distribution conditional on being no greater than
\mu
,
\mathbb{E}[S\,|\,S\leq\mu]
. Consequently, any modification to
F
that preserves these three quantities will lead to the same maximum expected welfare.

This observation motivates the following approach to find the approximation ratio we achieve by posting the mean as a price which, as we showed, is the best-possible among DSIC mechanisms:

(A)

\displaystyle\inf_{F}\frac{\mathsf{W}(\mu_{F};F)}{\mathsf{OPT\text{-}W}(F)}=\inf_{F}\sup_{p\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{\mathsf{W}(p,F)}{\mathsf{OPT\text{-}W}(F)}.

Define the subspace of probability distributions that fixes the three quantities from above:

\Delta_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mu,\mu_{1},\gamma):=\left\{F\in\Delta_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}):\mathbb{E}[S]=\mu,\,\mathbb{E}[S\,|\,S\leq\mu]=\mu_{1},\,F(\mu)=\gamma\right\}.

To compute (
A
), we first compute

(A’)

\adjustlimits{\inf}_{F\in\Delta_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mu,\mu_{1},\gamma)}{\sup}_{p\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{\mathsf{W}(p,F)}{\mathsf{OPT\text{-}W}(F)}.

The original problem (
A
) is thus equivalent to

\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\,\mu_{1}\,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\\
0\leq\mu_{1}\leq\mu;\,\mu>0;\,0<\gamma\leq 1\end{subarray}}\left\{\adjustlimits{\inf}_{F\in\Delta_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mu,\mu_{1},\gamma)}{\sup}_{p\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{\mathsf{W}(p,F)}{\mathsf{OPT\text{-}W}(F)}\right\}.

The objective of this decoupling is to reduce the dimension of our optimization problem. Instead of minimizing over the infinite-dimensional space
\Delta_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, our outer optimization problem is simplified to minimization over three variables. However, the inner optimization problem (
A’
) still requires minimizing over the infinite-dimensional space
\Delta_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mu,\mu_{1},\gamma)
. To reduce the dimension of (
A’
), define the space of distributions
supported on at most 4 points
that fixes the three quantities:

\displaystyle\Delta_{L^{1}}^{(4)}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mu,\mu_{1},\gamma):=\{F\in\Delta_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mu,\mu_{1},\gamma):F(x)=

\displaystyle q_{0}\cdot{\bf 1}_{x\geq 0}+q_{1}\cdot{\bf 1}_{x\geq x_{1}}

\displaystyle+q_{2}\cdot{\bf 1}_{x\geq x_{2}}+q_{3}\cdot{\bf 1}_{x\geq 1}\}.

The probability masses are given by
0\leq q_{0},q_{1},q_{2},q_{3}\leq 1
.

Lemma 5

For any fixed
0\leq\mu_{1}\leq\mu\leq 1
,
\mu>0
and
0<\gamma\leq 1
,

\adjustlimits{\inf}_{F\in\Delta_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mu,\mu_{1},\gamma)}{\sup}_{p\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{\mathsf{W}(p,F)}{\mathsf{OPT\text{-}W}(F)}=\inf_{F\in\Delta_{L^{1}}^{(4)}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mu,\mu_{1},\gamma)}\sup_{p\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{\mathsf{W}(p,F)}{\mathsf{OPT\text{-}W}(F)}.

We defer the proof of Lemma
5
to Appendix
A
and sketch the argument here. Fix
F\in\Delta_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mu,\mu_{1},\gamma)
. We may assume without loss of generality that
F
is a finite support discrete distribution because any
F\in\Delta_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mu,\mu_{1},\gamma)
can be approximated arbitrarily well by a corresponding distribution in
\Delta_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mu,\mu_{1},\gamma)
supported on finitely many points. Moreover, by rescaling if necessary, we may also assume that
F(1)=1
.

To solve the inner problem (
A’
), our earlier observation shows that, given
\mu
,
\mu_{1}
and
\gamma
,
\mathsf{W}(\mu;F)

\adjustlimits{\inf}_{F\in\Delta_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}{\sup}_{p\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{\mathsf{W}(p,F)}{\mathsf{OPT\text{-}W}(F)}\geq\frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{4}.

On the other hand, we can compute that

\mathsf{OPT\text{-}W}(F_{n})=\frac{4\,(\sqrt{2}-1)}{n}-\frac{4\sqrt{2}-1}{n^{2}}.

4
Concluding Remarks

Our results give a comprehensive description of the power of dominant-strategy truthful mechanisms for bilateral trade, in terms of two widely-studied objectives in mechanism design: welfare and gains from trade, together with the informational requirements needed to achieve those approximations.

We would like to distinguish our paper from previous work as follows. In our view, the main contribution of our paper is to show that fixed-price mechanisms can be effective
particularly in markets with symmetric agents
, for reasons other than strategic simplicity for agents. Two important properties of fixed-price mechanisms in such markets is that they are informationally simple from the designer’s perspective while being approximately efficient at the same time. We view robustness to different benchmarks (i.e., optimal welfare and optimal gains from trade) as an important criterion for “approximate efficiency.” In our view, previous approximability and inapproximability results in the asymmetric case, which crucially depend on benchmark adopted, constitute mainly a negative result for the asymmetric case.

A fundamental question remains in the asymmetric case, which is whether a constant-factor approximation to optimal gains from trade is possible with any Bayesian incentive-compatible mechanism. We already know that such a mechanism cannot be incentive-compatible in dominant strategies, i.e. it cannot be a fixed-price mechanism. There are other techniques to design Bayesian incentive-compatible mechanisms. In particular, the second-best mechanism is in some sense known, but its implicit description does not seem very useful for the derivation of approximation guarantees. It appears that a more specific Bayesian mechanism would be needed to resolve this question.

(when restricted to
S\leq\mu
) and
\mathbb{E}[S\,|\,S>\mu]
(when restricted to
S>\mu
).

By rescaling if necessary, we may also assume that
F(1)=1
. Let
F
put positive probability mass only on each of the points
x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\in[0,1]
.

Motivated by the procedure outlined in the proof sketch, we now construct
\tilde{F}\in\Delta_{L^{1}}^{(4)}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mu,\mu_{1},\gamma)
such that, for any
F\in\Delta_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mu,\mu_{1},\gamma)
,

\frac{\mathsf{W}(\mu;\tilde{F})}{\mathsf{OPT\text{-}W}(\tilde{F})}\leq\frac{\mathsf{W}(\mu;F)}{\mathsf{OPT\text{-}W}(F)}.

We write

\tilde{F}(x)=q_{0}\cdot{\bf 1}_{x\geq 0}+q_{1}\cdot{\bf 1}_{x\geq\mu}+q_{2}\cdot{\bf 1}_{x\geq\mu+\delta}+q_{3}\cdot{\bf 1}_{x\geq 1}.

To construct
\tilde{F}
, we choose
q_{0}
,
q_{1}
,
q_{2}
,
q_{3}
and
\delta
as follows:

•

\delta
is defined by

\delta=\min\{x_{i}-\mu:x_{i}>\mu,\,i\in\{1,2,\ldots,N\}\}.

•

q_{0}
and
q_{1}
are defined by

\begin{cases}q_{0}+q_{1}&=\gamma,\\
(q_{0}+q_{1})\mu_{1}&=q_{1}\mu.\end{cases}

That is,
q_{0}=\gamma(1-\mu_{1}/\mu)
and
q_{1}=\mu_{1}\gamma/\mu
.

•

If
\mu+\delta<1
,
q_{2}
and
q_{3}
are defined by

\begin{cases}q_{2}+q_{3}&=1-\gamma,\\
q_{2}(\mu+\delta)+q_{3}&=\mu-(q_{0}+q_{1})\mu_{1}.\end{cases}

That is,
q_{2}=(1-\gamma-\mu+\mu_{1}\gamma)/(1-\mu-\delta)
and
q_{3}=(\mu\gamma-\mu_{1}\gamma-\delta+\delta\gamma)/(1-\mu-\delta)
.

If
\mu+\delta=1
,
q_{2}
and
q_{3}
are defined by
q_{2}=0
and
q_{3}=1-\gamma
.

With
\tilde{F}
defined above, we first verify that
\tilde{F}\in\Delta_{L^{1}}^{(4)}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mu,\mu_{1},\gamma)
. Indeed:

\begin{cases}\tilde{F}(\mu)=q_{0}+q_{1}&=\gamma,\\
\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{F}}[S]=q_{1}\mu+q_{2}(\mu+\delta)+q_{3}&=\mu,\\
\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{F}}[S\,|\,S\leq\mu]=\frac{q_{1}\mu}{q_{0}+q_{1}}&=\mu_{1}.\end{cases}

Next, we show that, for any
F\in\Delta_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mu,\mu_{1},\gamma)
,

\mathsf{OPT\text{-}W}(\tilde{F})\geq\mathsf{OPT\text{-}W}(F).

To do so, we compute that

\displaystyle\mathsf{OPT\text{-}W}(F)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{F}[\max\{B,S\}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{F}[\max\{B,S\}\cdot{\bf 1}_{B,S\leq\mu}]+\mathbb{E}_{F}[\max\{B,S\}\cdot{\bf 1}_{B,S>\mu}]

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathbb{E}_{F}[\max\{B,S\}\cdot{\bf 1}_{\max\{B,S\}>\mu}{\bf 1}_{\min\{B,S\}\leq\mu}].

That is,
B,S
must be either: (i) both no greater than
\mu
; (ii) both greater than
\mu
; or (iii) on different sides of
\mu
. For the last case, observe that:

\mathbb{E}_{F}[\max\{B,S\}\cdot{\bf 1}_{\max\{B,S\}>\mu}{\bf 1}_{\min\{B,S\}\leq\mu}]=\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{F}}[\max\{B,S\}\cdot{\bf 1}_{\max\{B,S\}>\mu}{\bf 1}_{\min\{B,S\}\leq\mu}].

Therefore, it suffices to show that
\mathbb{E}_{F}[\max\{B,S\}\cdot{\bf 1}_{B,S\leq\mu}]\leq\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{F}}[\max\{B,S\}\cdot{\bf 1}_{B,S\leq\mu}]
and that
\mathbb{E}_{F}[\max\{B,S\}\cdot{\bf 1}_{B,S>\mu}]\leq\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{F}}[\max\{B,S\}\cdot{\bf 1}_{B,S>\mu}]
.

To show the former result, observe that

\begin{cases}\mathbb{E}_{F}[\max\{B,S\}\cdot{\bf 1}_{B,S\leq\mu}]=2\int_{0}^{\mu}x\,F(x)\ \mathrm{d}F(x)&=\mu\gamma^{2}-\int_{0}^{\mu}[F(x)]^{2}\ \mathrm{d}x,\\
\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{F}}[\max\{B,S\}\cdot{\bf 1}_{B,S\leq\mu}]=(q_{0}+q_{1})^{2}\mu-q_{0}^{2}\mu&=\mu\gamma^{2}-q_{0}^{2}\mu.\end{cases}

However, from the definition of
q_{0}
:

\int_{0}^{\mu}F(x)\ \mathrm{d}x=\mu\gamma-\int_{0}^{\mu}x\ \mathrm{d}F(x)=(\mu-\mu_{1})\gamma=q_{0}\mu.

Therefore

q_{0}^{2}\mu=\frac{1}{\mu}\left[\int_{0}^{\mu}F(x)\ \mathrm{d}x\right]^{2}\leq\int_{0}^{\mu}[F(x)]^{2}\ \mathrm{d}x.

This establishes that
\mathbb{E}_{F}[\max\{B,S\}\cdot{\bf 1}_{B,S\leq\mu}]\leq\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{F}}[\max\{B,S\}\cdot{\bf 1}_{B,S\leq\mu}]
. The latter result follows by a similar argument.

We have thus proved that
\mathsf{OPT\text{-}W}(\tilde{F})\geq\mathsf{OPT\text{-}W}(F)
. Moreover, since
\mathsf{W}(\mu;\tilde{F})=\mathsf{W}(\mu;F)
, we have

\frac{\mathsf{W}(p;\tilde{F})}{\mathsf{OPT\text{-}W}(\tilde{F})}\leq\frac{\mathsf{W}(p;F)}{\mathsf{OPT\text{-}W}(F)}.

Since
\tilde{F}\in\Delta_{L^{1}}^{(4)}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mu,\mu_{1},\gamma)\subset\Delta_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mu,\mu_{1},\gamma)
, hence this establishes:

\adjustlimits{\inf}_{F\in\Delta_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mu,\mu_{1},\gamma)}{\sup}_{p\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{\mathsf{W}(p;F)}{\mathsf{OPT\text{-}W}(F)}=\inf_{F\in\Delta_{L^{1}}^{(4)}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mu,\mu_{1},\gamma)}\sup_{p\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{\mathsf{W}(p;F)}{\mathsf{OPT\text{-}W}(F)}.

Lemma 14

The value of the minimax problem (
A
) is bounded below:

\adjustlimits{\inf}_{F\in\Delta_{L^{1}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}{\sup}_{p\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{\mathsf{W}(p;F)}{\mathsf{OPT\text{-}W}(F)}\geq\frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{4}.

[
Proof.
]From the argument given in the paper, it suffices to show that

\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\,\mu_{1}\,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\\
0\leq\mu_{1}\leq\mu;\,\mu,\gamma\in(0,1]\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu+(\mu-\mu_{1})\gamma}{\mu_{1}\gamma^{2}(2-\frac{\mu_{1}}{\mu})+2\gamma(\mu-\gamma\mu_{1})+(1-\gamma)^{2}-\frac{(1-\gamma-\mu+\mu_{1}\gamma)^{2}}{1-\mu}}=\frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{4}.

We begin with a change of variables. Define
x:=1-\mu_{1}/\mu\in[0,1]
; the minimization problem thus becomes

\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in[0,1]\\
\mu,\gamma\in(0,1]\end{subarray}}\frac{1+\gamma x}{1+2\gamma x-\frac{\gamma^{2}x^{2}}{1-\mu}}.

Observe that, for
x\in[0,1]
and
\mu,\gamma\in(0,1]
,

\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}\left[\frac{1+\gamma x}{1+2\gamma x-\frac{\gamma^{2}x^{2}}{1-\mu}}\right]=\frac{\gamma^{2}x^{2}(1+\gamma x)}{\left[1-\mu+\gamma x(2-2\mu-\gamma x)\right]^{2}}\geq 0.

That is, the objective function is non-decreasing in
\mu
.

Thus the infimum of the objective function is achieved in the limit as
\mu\to 0
:

\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in[0,1]\\
\mu,\gamma\in(0,1]\end{subarray}}\frac{1+\gamma x}{1+2\gamma x-\frac{\gamma^{2}x^{2}}{1-\mu}}=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in[0,1]\\
\gamma\in(0,1]\end{subarray}}\frac{1+\gamma x}{1+\gamma x(2-\gamma x)}.

We now make another change of variables. Define
y:=\gamma x\in[0,1]
. The minimization problem thus becomes a single-dimensional minimization problem:

\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in[0,1]\\
\gamma\in(0,1]\end{subarray}}\frac{1+\gamma x}{1+\gamma x(2-\gamma x)}=\inf_{y\in[0,1]}\frac{1+y}{1+y(2-y)}=\frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{4}.

Abstract.
This paper is concerned with the nonlinear elliptic problem

-\Delta u=\frac{\lambda}{(a-u)^{2}}
on a bounded domain
\Omega
of
\mathbb{R}^{N}

with Dirichlet boundary conditions. This problem arises from Micro-Electromechanical Systems devices
in the case that the elastic membrane contacts the ground plate on the boundary.
We analyze the properties of minimal solutions to this equation when
\lambda>0

and the function

a:\bar{\Omega}\to[0,1]
satisfying

a(x)\geq\kappa{\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega)^{\gamma}
for some
\kappa>0
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
.
Our results show how the boundary decay of the membrane works on the solutions and pull-in voltage
\lambda
.

1.
Introduction

Micro-Electromechanical Systems (MEMS) are often used to combine electronics with micro-size mechanical devices in the design
of various types of microscopic machinery. They
are successfully utilized in components of many commercial systems, including accelerometers for airbag deployment in automobiles, ink jet printer heads, optical switches, chemical sensors, etc.
In MEMS devices, a key component is called the electrostatic actuation, which
is based on an electrostatic-controlled tunable, it is a simple idealized electrostatic device. The upper part of this electrostatic device consists of a thin and deformable elastic membrane that is fixed along its
boundary and which lies above a rigid grounded plate. This elastic membrane is modeled as a dielectric with
a small but finite thickness. The upper surface of the membrane is coated with a negligibly thin metallic
conducting film. When a voltage
\lambda
is applied to the conducting film, the thin dielectric membrane deflects
towards the bottom plate, and when
\lambda
is increased beyond a certain critical value
\lambda^{*}-
known as pull-in
voltage
-
the steady state of the elastic membrane is lost, and proceeds to touchdown or snap through at a
finite time creating the so-called pull-in instability.

A mathematical model of the physical phenomena, leading to a partial
differential equation for the dimensionless deflection of the membrane, was
derived and analyzed in
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
12
,
15
]
and reference therein. In the damping-dominated limit, and using a
narrow-gap asymptotic analysis, the dimensionless deflection
u
of the membrane
on a bounded domain
\Omega
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is found to satisfy the
equation

-\Delta u=\frac{\lambda}{(1-u)^{2}}\qquad{\rm in}\quad\Omega

(1.1)

with the Dirichlet boundary condition. Here the term on the right hand side of equation (
1.1
) is the Coulomb force.
Later on, Ghoussoub and Guo in
[
4
,
5
]
studied the nonlinear elliptic problem

-\Delta u=\frac{\lambda f(x)}{(1-u)^{2}}\qquad{\rm in}\quad\Omega

(1.2)

with the Dirichlet boundary condition, which models a simple electrostatic
MEMS device consisting of a thin dielectric elastic membrane with boundary supported at
0
above a
rigid ground plate located at
1
.
Here
\Omega
is a bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
the function
f\geq 0
represents the permittivity profile and
\lambda>0
is a constant which is increasing with respect to the applied voltage.
We know that for any given suitable
f
, there exists a critical value

\lambda^{*}
(pull-in voltage) such that if
\lambda\in(0,\lambda^{*})
, problem (
1.2
)
is solvable, while for
\lambda>\lambda^{*}
, no solution for (
1.2
) exists.

In an effort to achieve better MEMS design, the
membrane can be technologically fabricated into non-flat shape like the surface of a semi-ball, which contacts the ground plate along the boundary.
In this paper, we study how the shape of membranes effects on the pull-in voltage.
In what follows,
we denote that
\Omega
is a
C^{2}
bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
N\geq 1
,
\rho(x)={\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega)
for
x\in\Omega
,
the function
a:\bar{\Omega}\to[0,1]
is in the class of
C^{\gamma}(\Omega)\cap C(\bar{\Omega})
and satisfies

a(x)\geq\kappa\rho(x)^{\gamma},\quad\forall\ x\in\Omega

(1.3)

for some
\kappa>0
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
. Our purpose of this paper is to consider the minimal solutions to
elliptic equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}-\Delta u=\frac{\lambda}{(a-u)^{2}}&{\rm in}\quad\ \Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{-}0<u<a&{\rm in}\quad\ \Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{-\Delta}u=0&{\rm on}\quad\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(1.4)

where parameter
\lambda>0
characterizes the relative strength of the electrostatic and mechanical forces in the system. Equation (
1.4
) models a MEMS device
that the static deformation of the surface of membrane when it is applied voltage
\lambda
,
where
a
is initially undeflected state of the elastic membrane that contacts the ground plate on the boundary.
For this equation, we have the following existence results.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
a\in C^{\gamma}(\Omega)\cap C(\bar{\Omega})
satisfies (
1.3
) with
\gamma\in(0,\frac{2}{3}]
and
\kappa>0
, then there exists a
finite pull-in voltage
\lambda^{*}:=\lambda^{*}(\kappa,\gamma)>0
such that

(i)
for
\lambda\in(0,\lambda^{*})
, problem (
1.4
) admits a minimal solution
u_{\lambda}
and the mapping:
\lambda\mapsto u_{\lambda}

is strictly increasing;

(ii)
for
\lambda>\lambda^{*}
, there is no solution for (
1.4
);

(iii)
assume more that there exists
c_{0}\geq\kappa
such that

a(x)\leq c_{0}\rho(x)^{\gamma},\quad x\in\Omega,

(1.5)

then there exists
\lambda_{*}:=\lambda_{*}(\kappa,\gamma)\in(0,\lambda^{*}]
such that for
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{*})
,
u_{\lambda}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and

{\rm for}\quad\gamma\not=\frac{1}{2},\qquad\frac{\lambda}{c_{1}}\rho(x)^{\min\{1,2-2\gamma\}}\leq u_{\lambda}(x)\leq c_{1}\lambda\rho(x)^{\min\{1,2-2\gamma\}},\qquad\forall x\in\Omega;

{\rm for}\quad\gamma=\frac{1}{2},\qquad\frac{\lambda}{c_{1}}\rho(x)\ln\frac{1}{\rho(x)}\leq u_{\lambda}(x)\leq c_{1}\lambda\rho(x)\ln\frac{1}{\rho(x)},\qquad\forall x\in A_{\frac{1}{2}},

where
c_{1}\geq 1
and
A_{\frac{1}{2}}=\{x\in\Omega:\ \rho(x)<\frac{1}{2}\}
.

In particular, assume more that
\Omega=B_{1}(0)
and

a(x)=\kappa(1-|x|^{2})^{\gamma},\qquad\forall x\in B_{1}(0),

then the mappings:
\gamma\mapsto\lambda_{*}(\kappa,\gamma)
,
\gamma\mapsto\lambda^{*}(\kappa,\gamma)
are decreasing and

(2/3)^{5}\kappa^{3}\leq\lambda_{*}(\kappa,2/3)\leq\lambda_{*}(\kappa,\gamma)\leq\lambda^{*}(\kappa,\gamma)\leq\lambda^{*}(\kappa,0)\leq(4/3)^{2}N\kappa^{3}.

We remark that the membrane contacts the ground plate on the boundary with decay rate
\rho^{\gamma}
,
\gamma\in(0,\frac{2}{3}]
,
there still has a positive finite pull-in voltage
\lambda^{*}
, but the decay of
a
plays an important role in decay of minimal solution,
the regularity of minimal solution and the estimate of
\lambda^{*}
. Theorem
1.1
shows that the membrane of the MEMS device could be
designed as the surface of the unit semi-ball,
that is,

\Omega=B_{1}(0)\quad{\rm and}\quad a(x)=(1-|x|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}},

which is equivalent to the case that
a(x)=\rho(x)^{\frac{1}{2}}
, so there exists a positive finite pull-in voltage
\lambda^{*}
.
However,
the decay rate of function
a
determined completely
nonexistence of pull-in voltage when
\gamma>\frac{2}{3}
. Precisely, we have the following non-existence result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that
a\in C(\bar{\Omega})
is positive and satisfies (
1.5
) with
\gamma\in(\frac{2}{3},1)
and
c_{0}>0
.
Then problem
(
1.4
) admits no nonnegative solution for any
\lambda>0.

We notice that for
\gamma\leq\frac{2}{3}
and fixed
\kappa
, the finite pull-in voltage
\lambda^{*}
depends on
\gamma
, however,
when
\gamma=\frac{2}{3}
,
\lambda^{*}>0
and
\lambda^{*}=0
for
\gamma>\frac{2}{3}
. Therefore, there is a gap at
\gamma=\frac{2}{3}
. From Theorem
1.2
,
we learn that the membrane of the MEMS device should be
made steeply enough near the boundary, otherwise the device does not work.

As normal, it is challenging to study the extremal solution, i.e., the solution of (
1.4
) when
\lambda=\lambda^{*}
. Especially, the decay of function
a

makes this issue more subtle. From Theorem
1.1
, we observe that the mapping
\lambda\mapsto u_{\lambda}
is increasing and
uniformly bounded by function
a
, then it is well-defined that

u_{\lambda^{*}}:=\lim_{\lambda\to\lambda^{*}}u_{\lambda}\quad{\rm in}\ \ \bar{\Omega},

(1.6)

where
u_{\lambda}
is the minimal solution of (
1.4
) with
\lambda\in(0,\lambda^{*})
.
Our final purpose in this paper is to
prove that
u_{\lambda^{*}}
is a solution of (
1.4
) in some weak sense and it is called the extremal solution. The extremal solution always is found in a weak sense and then it could be improved the regularity until
to the classical sense when the dimension
N
is suitable.

Definition 1.1
.

A function
u
is a weak solution of (
1.4
) if
0\leq u\leq a
and

\int_{\Omega}u(-\Delta\xi)dx=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda\xi}{(a-u)^{2}}dx,\quad\forall\xi\in C_{c}^{2}(\Omega),

where
C_{c}^{2}(\Omega)
is the space of all
C^{2}
functions with compact support in
\Omega
.

A solution (or weak solution)
u
of (
1.4
) is stable (resp. semi-stable) if

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}dx>\int_{\Omega}\frac{2\lambda\varphi^{2}}{(a-u)^{3}}dx,\quad({\rm resp.}\ \geq)\quad\forall\varphi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}.

We make use of the functions’ space
C^{2}_{c}(\Omega)
in Definition
1.1
replacing
C_{0}^{2}(\Omega):=C^{2}(\Omega)\cap C_{0}(\Omega)
, used in the case of
a\equiv 1
, to avoid the singularity at the boundary of
\frac{1}{(a-u_{\lambda})^{2}}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume that
\lambda\in(0,\lambda^{*})
, the function

a\in C^{\gamma}(\Omega)\cap C(\bar{\Omega})
satisfies (
1.3
) and(
1.5
) with

c_{0}\geq\kappa>0
,
\gamma\in(0,\frac{2}{3}]
,

u_{\lambda}
is the minimal solution of (
1.4
) and
u_{\lambda^{*}}
is given by (
1.6
).
Then

(i)

u_{\lambda^{*}}
is a weak solution of (
1.4
) and
u_{\lambda^{*}}\in W^{1,\frac{N}{N-\beta}}_{0}(\Omega)

for any
\beta\in(0,\gamma)
.

(ii)

u_{\lambda}
is a stable solution of (
1.4
) with
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{*})
.

(iii)
if
\gamma=\frac{2}{3}
, we have that
\lambda^{*}=\lambda_{*}
,

u_{\lambda^{*}}
is a semi-stable weak solution of (
1.4
).

Assume more that
1\leq N\leq 7
,
\Omega=B_{1}(0)
and
a(x)=\kappa(1-|x|^{2})^{\frac{2}{3}}
,
u_{\lambda^{*}}
is a classical solution of (
1.4
).

We note that
(i)
the extremal solution
u_{\lambda^{*}}
is a weak solution but in a
weaker sense comparing with the case
a\equiv 1
;

(ii)
the main difficulty to study
the stability of
u_{\lambda}
with
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{*})
arises from the decay on the boundary of
a
and to overcome this difficult,
we make use of the generalized Hardy’s inequality;

(iii)
in the case of
\gamma=\frac{2}{3}
, the regularity of the extremal solution could be improved into classical sense
for
1\leq N\leq 7
, the same range of the dimension with the case
a\equiv 1
.

The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 is devoted to do the estimate for
\mathbb{G}_{\Omega}[\rho^{\tau-2}]
when
\tau\in(0,2)
and
then we prove that there exists pull-in voltage
\lambda^{*}
such that problem (
1.4
) admits a minimal solution when
\lambda\in(0,\lambda^{*})
. We also analyze
the boundary decay of the minimal solution.
In Section 3, we do the estimate for
\lambda_{*}
and
\lambda^{*}
when
\Omega=B_{1}(0)
and
a(x)=\kappa(1-|x|)^{\gamma}
. In
Section 4, we study the properties of
u_{\lambda^{*}}
, including its regularity and stability.

2.
Existence

Denote by
G_{\Omega}
the Green kernel of
-\Delta
in
\Omega\times\Omega
and by
\mathbb{G}_{\Omega}[\cdot]
the
Green operator defined as

\mathbb{G}_{\Omega}[f](x)=\int_{\Omega}G_{\Omega}(x,y)f(y)dy,\quad\forall f\in L^{1}(\Omega,\rho^{-1}dx).

To do the existence of a minimal solution of problem (
1.4
), the following estimates play an important role.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\tau\in(0,2)
,
A_{\frac{1}{2}}=\{x\in\Omega:\ \rho(x)<\frac{1}{2}\}
. For
x\in A_{\frac{1}{2}}
, we denote

\varrho_{\tau}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\rho(x)^{\min\{1,\tau\}}&{\rm if}\ \ \tau\in(0,1)\cup(1,2),\\[5.69054pt]
\rho(x)\ln\frac{1}{\rho(x)}&{\rm if}\ \ \tau=1\end{array}\right.

(2.1)

and we make
C^{1}
extension of
\varrho_{\tau}
into
\Omega\setminus A_{\frac{1}{2}}
such that

\varrho_{\tau}>0
in
\Omega\setminus A_{\frac{1}{2}}
.

Then there exists
c_{\tau}>1
such that

\frac{1}{c}_{\tau}\varrho_{\tau}(x)\leq\mathbb{G}_{\Omega}[\rho^{\tau-2}](x)\leq c_{\tau}\varrho_{\tau}(x),\quad\forall x\in\Omega.

Proof.
Since the domain
\Omega
is
C^{2}
, then there exists
\delta_{1}>0
such that the distance function

\rho(\cdot)
is
C^{2}
in

A_{\delta_{1}}=\{x\in\Omega:\ \rho(x)<\delta_{1}\}.

Let us define

V_{\tau}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}l(x),&x\in\Omega\setminus A_{\delta_{1}},\\[5.69054pt]
\rho(x)^{\tau},&x\in A_{\delta_{1}},\end{array}\right.

(2.2)

where
\tau
is a parameter in
(0,2)
and
l
is a positive function such that

V_{\tau}
is
C^{2}
in
\Omega
.
Now we do the estimate of
-\Delta V_{\tau}
near the boundary of
\Omega
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
0\in\partial\Omega
and

e_{N}=(0,\cdots,0,1)
is the unit normal vector at
0
pointing inside. We observe that there exists
\delta_{2}\in(0,\delta_{1})
such that
for any
t\in(0,\delta_{2})
,
te_{N}\in A_{\delta_{1}}

and
V_{\tau}(te_{N})=\rho(te_{N})^{\tau}=t^{\tau}
. Then

\frac{\partial^{2}V_{\tau}(te_{N})}{\partial x_{N}^{2}}=\tau(\tau-1)t^{\tau-2}\quad{\rm and}\quad|\frac{\partial^{2}V_{\tau}(te_{N})}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}|\leq c_{2},\quad i=1,2,\cdots,N-1,

where
c_{2}>0
is independent of
t
.
Thus,

\tau(\tau-1)t^{\tau-2}-c_{2}\leq\Delta V_{\tau}(te_{N})\leq\tau(\tau-1)t^{\tau-2}+c_{2}.

Since
\Omega
is a
C^{2}
bounded domain,
\partial\Omega
is compact and then

\tau(\tau-1)\rho(x)^{\tau-2}-c_{2}\leq\Delta V_{\tau}(x)\leq\tau(\tau-1)\rho(x)^{\tau-2}+c_{2},\quad\forall x\in A_{\delta_{2}}.

(2.3)

For
\tau\in(0,1)
, we observe that
\tau(\tau-1)<0
and by (
2.3
),

\frac{1}{2}\tau(1-\tau)\rho(x)^{\tau-2}\leq-\Delta V_{\tau}(x)\leq 2\tau(1-\tau)\rho(x)^{\tau-2},\quad\forall x\in A_{\delta_{2}}.

Combining with the fact that

-\Delta\mathbb{G}_{\Omega}[\rho^{\tau-2}]=\rho^{\tau-2}
in
\Omega
,
we obtain that

\frac{1}{2\tau(1-\tau)}(-\Delta V_{\tau})\leq-\Delta\mathbb{G}_{\Omega}[\rho^{\tau-2}]\leq\frac{2}{\tau(1-\tau)}(-\Delta V_{\tau})\quad{\rm in}\ \ A_{\delta_{2}}.

For
x\in\partial\Omega
, we have that
V_{\tau}(x)=0
and
G_{\Omega}(x,y)=0
for
y\in\Omega
and then

\mathbb{G}_{\Omega}[\rho^{\tau-2}](x)=\int_{\Omega}G_{\Omega}(x,y)\rho(y)^{\tau-2}dy=0.

For
x\in\Omega
satisfying
\rho(x)=\delta_{2}
, we have that
V_{\tau}(x)={\delta_{2}}^{\tau}
and then

\frac{1}{c_{3}}V_{\tau}(x)\leq\mathbb{G}_{\Omega}[\rho^{\tau-2}](x)\leq c_{3}V_{\tau}(x)\quad{\rm for}\ \rho(x)=\delta_{2},

(2.4)

where
c_{3}>1
.
By Comparison Principle, there exists
c_{4}>1
depends on
\tau
such that

\frac{1}{c_{4}}V_{\tau}\leq\mathbb{G}_{\Omega}[\rho^{\tau-2}]\leq c_{4}V_{\tau}\quad{\rm in}\ \ A_{\delta_{2}}.

(2.5)

Since
\mathbb{G}_{\Omega}[\rho^{\tau-2}]
and
V_{\tau}
is bounded in
\Omega\setminus A_{\delta_{2}}
, then (
2.5
)
holds in
\Omega
.

For
\tau\in(1,2)
,
by the fact of (
2.3
), we observe that

\frac{1}{2}\tau(\tau-1)\rho(x)^{\tau-2}\leq\Delta V_{\tau}(x)\leq 2\tau(\tau-1)\rho(x)^{\tau-2},\quad\forall x\in A_{\delta_{2}}.

Letting
W_{\tau}=\mathbb{G}_{\Omega}[1]-V_{\tau}
, then there exists
c_{5}>1
depends on
\tau
such that

\frac{1}{c_{5}}\rho(x)^{\tau-2}\leq-\Delta W_{\tau}(x)=-1+\Delta V_{\tau}(x)\leq c_{5}\rho(x)^{\tau-2},\quad\forall x\in A_{\delta_{2}}.

By the fact that

-\Delta\mathbb{G}_{\Omega}[\rho^{\tau-2}]=\rho^{\tau-2}
in
\Omega
, we have

\frac{1}{c_{6}}W_{\tau}(x)\leq\mathbb{G}_{\Omega}[\rho^{\tau-2}](x)\leq c_{6}W_{\tau}(x),\ \ \ \ \forall x\in\partial A_{\delta_{2}}

(2.6)

for some
c_{6}>1
depends on
\tau
.
Using the Comparison Principle, there exists
c_{7},c_{8}>1
depend on
\tau
such that

\frac{1}{c_{8}}\rho(x)\leq\frac{1}{c_{7}}W_{\tau}(x)\leq\mathbb{G}_{\Omega}[\rho^{\tau-2}](x)\leq c_{7}W_{\tau}(x)\leq c_{8}\rho(x),\quad\ x\in A_{\delta_{2}},

and then

\frac{1}{c_{8}}\rho(x)\leq\mathbb{G}_{\Omega}[\rho^{\tau-2}](x)\leq c_{8}\rho(x),\quad\ x\in\Omega.

For
\tau=1
, we redefine

V_{1}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}l(x),&x\in\Omega\setminus A_{\delta_{1}},\\[5.69054pt]
\rho(x)\ln\frac{1}{\rho(x)},&x\in A_{\delta_{1}},\end{array}\right.

where
l
is a positive function such that

V_{1}
is
C^{2}
in
\Omega
. By direct computation, there exist
\delta_{3}\in(0,\delta_{1})
and
c_{9}>0
such that

\rho(x)^{-1}-c_{9}\leq-\Delta V_{1}(x)\leq\rho(x)^{-1}+c_{9},\quad\forall x\in A_{\delta_{3}}.

Then it follows by Comparison Principle that

\frac{1}{c_{10}}\rho\ln\frac{1}{\rho}\leq\mathbb{G}_{\Omega}[\rho^{-1}]\leq c_{10}\rho\ln\frac{1}{\rho}\qquad{\rm in}\quad A_{\delta_{3}}

for some
c_{10}>1
.
The proof is complete.
\Box

By Lemma
2.1
, we have the following results.

Corollary 2.1
.

For
\gamma\in(\frac{2}{3},1)

\lim_{x\in\Omega,x\to\partial\Omega}\mathbb{G}_{\Omega}[\rho^{-2\gamma}](x)\rho(x)^{-\gamma}=+\infty.

(2.7)

Proof.
Let
\tau=2-2\gamma
, by
\gamma\in(\frac{2}{3},1)
, we have that

\tau<\gamma<1
. Using Lemma

, we obtain that

\mathbb{G}_{\Omega}[\rho^{-2\gamma}](x)\geq c_{11}\rho(x)^{\tau},\quad\forall x\in\Omega

, which implies (

).

Now we are ready to show the existence of pull-in voltage
\lambda^{*}
to problem (
1.4
) such that
(
1.4
) admits a solution for
\lambda\in(0,\lambda^{*})
and no solution for
\lambda>\lambda^{*}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume that
a\in C^{\gamma}(\Omega)\cap C(\bar{\Omega})
satisfies (
1.3
) with
\gamma\in(0,\frac{2}{3}]
, then there exists
\lambda^{*}>0
such that
problem (
1.4
) admits at least one solution for
\lambda\in(0,\lambda^{*})
and no solution
for
\lambda>\lambda^{*}
.
Moreover,

\lambda^{*}\leq\frac{\int_{\Omega}a(x)dx}{\int_{\Omega}\frac{\mathbb{G}_{\Omega}[1](x)}{a(x)^{2}}dx}.

(2.8)

Proof.
Let
v_{0}\equiv 0
in
\bar{\Omega}
and

v_{1}=\lambda\mathbb{G}_{\Omega}[\frac{1}{a^{2}}]>0,

by (
1.3
) and Lemma
2.1
,

v_{1}=\lambda\mathbb{G}_{\Omega}[\frac{1}{a^{2}}]\leq\frac{\lambda}{\kappa^{2}}\mathbb{G}_{\Omega}[\rho^{-2\gamma}]\leq\frac{\lambda}{\kappa^{2}}c_{12}\varrho_{2-2\gamma},

(2.9)

where
c_{12}>0
depending on
\gamma
and
\varrho_{2-2\gamma}
is given by (
2.1
).

For
0<\gamma\leq\frac{2}{3}
and
\gamma\not=\frac{1}{2}
, we observe that
\min\{1,2-2\gamma\}\geq\gamma
and by (
2.9
),

v_{1}(x)\leq\frac{\lambda}{\kappa^{2}}c_{12}\rho(x)^{\min\{1,2-2\gamma\}}\leq\frac{\lambda}{\kappa^{2}}c_{13}\rho(x)^{\gamma},\qquad x\in\Omega.

For
\gamma=\frac{1}{2}
, we see that
2-2\gamma=1
and by (
2.9
),

v_{1}(x)\leq\frac{\lambda}{\kappa^{2}}c_{12}\rho(x)\log\frac{1}{\rho(x)}\leq\frac{\lambda}{\kappa^{2}}c_{13}\rho(x)^{\gamma},\qquad x\in\Omega.

Then

v_{1}(x)\leq\frac{\lambda}{\kappa^{2}}c_{13}\rho(x)^{\gamma},\qquad x\in\Omega.

Fixed
\mu\in(0,\kappa)
, there exists
\lambda_{1}>0
such that

\frac{\lambda}{\kappa^{2}}c_{13}\leq\mu<\kappa,\ \ \ \forall\lambda<\lambda_{1},

then for any
\lambda<\lambda_{1}
,

v_{1}(x)\leq\mu\rho(x)^{\gamma},\qquad\forall x\in\Omega.

Let
v_{2}=\lambda\mathbb{G}_{\Omega}[\frac{1}{(a-v_{1})^{2}}]
, by the fact that
a(x)\geq\kappa\rho(x)^{\gamma}>\mu\rho(x)^{\gamma}\geq v_{1}(x)>0
for
x\in\Omega
and and Lemma
2.1
,
we have that

v_{1}=\lambda\mathbb{G}_{\Omega}[\frac{1}{a^{2}}]\leq v_{2}\leq\frac{\lambda}{(\kappa-\mu)^{2}}\mathbb{G}_{\Omega}[\rho^{-2\gamma}]\leq\frac{\lambda}{(\kappa-\mu)^{2}}c_{12}\varrho_{2-2\gamma}\leq\frac{\lambda}{(\kappa-\mu)^{2}}c_{14}\rho^{\gamma}\quad{\rm in}\ \ \Omega

for
\gamma\in(0,\frac{2}{3}]
. We observe that there exists
\lambda_{2}\in(0,\lambda_{1}]

\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda^{*}\rho^{1-\beta}}{(a-u_{\lambda}^{*})^{2}}dx\leq\frac{\bar{c}_{\beta}}{c_{25}},

(4.5)

thus, for any
\xi\in C_{c}^{2}(\Omega)
, we have that

\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda\xi}{(a-u_{\lambda})^{2}}dx\to\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda^{*}\xi}{(a-u_{\lambda^{*}})^{2}}dx\ \ \ {\rm as}\ \ \lambda\to\lambda^{*}.

(4.6)

Since
u_{\lambda}
is the minimal solution of (
1.4
),

\int_{\Omega}u_{\lambda}(-\Delta\xi)dx=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda\xi}{(a-u_{\lambda})^{2}}dx,\ \ \ \ \ \forall\,\xi\in C_{c}^{2}(\Omega),

passing to the limit as
\lambda\to\lambda^{*}
, combining with (
4.4
) and (
4.6
),
then

u_{\lambda^{*}}
is a weak solution of (
1.4
) with
\lambda=\lambda^{*}
.

By
[
2
, Theorem 2.6]
, for any
\beta^{\prime}\in(\beta,\gamma)
, there exists
c_{27}>0
such that

\lVert|\nabla u_{\lambda^{*}}|\rVert_{M^{\frac{N}{N-\beta^{\prime}}}(\Omega)}\leq c_{27}\lVert(a-u_{\lambda^{*}})^{-2}\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega,\,\rho^{1-\beta^{\prime}}dx)},

where
{M^{\frac{N}{N-\beta^{\prime}}}(\Omega)}
is the Marcinkiewicz space of
exponent
\frac{N}{N-\beta^{\prime}}
.
Then, by (
4.5
), we have that

\lVert|\nabla u_{\lambda^{*}}|\rVert_{L^{\frac{N}{N-\beta}}(\Omega)}\leq c_{27}c_{25}^{-1}\bar{c}_{\beta}(\lambda^{*})^{-1}

and then

\lVert u_{\lambda}^{*}\rVert_{W^{1,\frac{N}{N-\beta}}(\Omega)}\leq c_{\beta}

for some
c_{\beta}>0.

Thus, (
4.1
) holds and (
4.2
) is obvious by (
4.5
).
This ends the proof.
\Box

4.2.
Stability

In this subsection, we introduce the stability of the minimal solution
u_{\lambda}
for problem (
1.4
). By the definition of
\lambda_{*}
, we observe that for
any
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{*})
, there exists
\theta\in(0,1)
such that
u_{\lambda}\leq\theta a
. Therefore,
by the fact that
\gamma\in(0,\frac{2}{3}]
and
a\geq\kappa\rho^{\gamma}
in
\Omega
, we have that

\frac{1}{(a-u_{\lambda})^{3}}\leq\frac{1}{(1-\theta)^{3}a^{3}}\leq(1-\theta)^{-3}\kappa^{-3}\rho^{-3\gamma}\leq c_{28}\rho^{-2}\quad{\rm in}\quad\Omega,

(4.7)

where
c_{28}>0
depends on
\theta,\kappa,\gamma
.
This enables us to consider the first eigenvalue of
-\Delta-\frac{2\lambda}{(a-u_{\lambda})^{3}}
in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, that is,

\mu_{1}(\lambda)=\inf_{\varphi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}\frac{\int_{\Omega}(|\nabla\varphi|^{2}-\frac{2\lambda\varphi^{2}}{(a-u_{\lambda})^{3}})dx}{\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{2}\,dx}

for
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{*})
. It is well-known that
u_{\lambda}
is stable if
\mu_{1}(\lambda)>0
and semi-stable if
\mu_{1}(\lambda)\geq 0
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume that
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{*})
,

a\in C^{\gamma}(\Omega)\cap C(\bar{\Omega})
satisfies (
1.3
) and (
1.5
)
with
c_{0}\geq\kappa>0
,
\gamma\in(0,\frac{2}{3}]
. Suppose that
u_{\lambda}
is the minimal solution of (
1.4
)
and
v_{\lambda}
is a super solution of (
1.4
).

If
\mu_{1}(\lambda)>0
, then

u_{\lambda}\leq v_{\lambda}\quad{\rm in}\quad\Omega.

If
\mu_{1}(\lambda)=0
, then

u_{\lambda}=v_{\lambda}\quad{\rm in}\quad\Omega.

Proof.
We observe that
a>u_{\lambda}
in
\Omega
for
\lambda\in(0,\lambda^{*})
and
\mu_{1}(\lambda)
is well-defined for
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{*})
, then it follows the procedure of the proof of Lemma 4.1 in
[
4
]
just replacing

\frac{f}{(1-u)^{2}}
by
\frac{1}{(a-u)^{2}}
.

\Box

Proposition 4.2
.

Assume that
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{*})
,

a\in C^{\gamma}(\Omega)\cap C(\bar{\Omega})
satisfies (
1.3
) and (
1.5
)
with
c_{0}\geq\kappa>0
,
\gamma\in(0,\frac{2}{3}]
. Let
u_{\lambda}
be the minimal solution of (
1.4
),
then
u_{\lambda}
is stable.

Proof.

We first claim that the mapping
\lambda\mapsto\mu_{1}(\lambda)
is locally Lipschitz continuous and strictly decreasing
in
(0,\lambda_{*})
.
Observing that the mapping
\lambda\mapsto u_{\lambda}
is strictly increasing and so is
\lambda\mapsto\frac{2\lambda}{(a-u_{\lambda})^{3}}
,
which implies that the mapping
\lambda\mapsto\mu_{1}(\lambda)
is strictly decreasing
in
(0,\lambda_{*})
. Let
0<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}<\lambda_{*}
and
\phi_{\lambda_{2}}
be the achieved function of
\mu_{1}(\lambda_{2})
with
\lVert\phi_{\lambda_{2}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1
,
then we have that

\displaystyle 0<\mu_{1}(\lambda_{1})-\mu_{1}(\lambda_{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla\phi_{\lambda_{2}}|^{2}-\frac{2\lambda_{1}\phi_{\lambda_{2}}^{2}}{(a-u_{\lambda_{1}})^{3}}\right)dx

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla\phi_{\lambda_{2}}|^{2}-\frac{2\lambda_{2}\phi_{\lambda_{2}}^{2}}{(a-u_{\lambda_{2}})^{3}}\right)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1})\int_{\Omega}\frac{\phi_{\lambda_{2}}^{2}}{(a-u_{\lambda_{2}})^{3}}dx,

thus, the mapping
\lambda\mapsto\mu_{1}(\lambda)
is locally Lipschitz continuous.

We next prove that
u_{\lambda}
is stable and
\mu_{1}(\lambda)>0
for
\lambda>0
small.
It follows by
[
10
, Theorem 1]
that there exists constant
c_{29}>0
such that

\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{2}\rho^{-2}dx\leq c_{29}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}dx,\quad\forall\varphi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

(4.8)

For
\lambda<\lambda_{*}
, it follows from
(
4.7
) and (
4.8
) that

\int_{\Omega}\frac{2\lambda\varphi^{2}}{(a-u_{\lambda})^{3}}dx\leq 2\lambda c_{28}\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{2}\rho^{-2}dx\leq 2\lambda c_{28}c_{29}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}dx,\quad\forall\varphi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

For
\lambda>0
small such that
2\lambda c_{28}c_{29}<1
, we obtain that

\int_{\Omega}\frac{2\lambda\varphi^{2}}{(a-u_{\lambda})^{3}}dx<\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}dx,\quad\forall\varphi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\},

that is,
\mu_{1}(\lambda)>0
and
u_{\lambda}
is stable if
\lambda>0
small.

Finally, we prove that
u_{\lambda}
is stable for
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{*})
. We have obtained that
\mu_{1}(\lambda)>0
for
\lambda>0
small and
the mapping
\lambda\mapsto\mu_{1}(\lambda)
is locally Lipschitz continuous, strictly decreasing
in
(0,\lambda_{*})
, so if there exists
\lambda_{0}\in(0,\lambda_{*})
such that
\mu_{1}(\lambda_{0})=0
, then
\mu_{1}(\lambda)>0
for
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{0})
.
Letting
\lambda_{1}\in(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{*})
, the minimal solution
u_{\lambda_{1}}
is
a super solution of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}-\Delta u=\frac{\lambda_{0}}{(a-u)^{2}}&{\rm in}\quad\ \Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{-\Delta}u=0&{\rm on}\quad\partial\Omega\end{array}\right.

and it infers from Lemma
4.1
that

u_{\lambda_{1}}=u_{\lambda_{0}},

which contradicts that the mapping
\lambda\mapsto u_{\lambda}
is strictly increasing.
Therefore,
\mu_{1}(\lambda)>0
and
u_{\lambda}
is stable for
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{*})
.

\Box

Lemma 4.2

Proposition 4.3
.

Assume that

Proof.
When

4.2
and Proposition
4.2
, we have that

\lambda\in(0,\lambda^{*})

\frac{\rho^{2}}{(a-u_{\lambda})^{3}}\to\frac{\rho^{2}}{(a-u_{\lambda^{*}})^{3}}\quad{\rm as}\quad\lambda\to\lambda^{*}\quad{\rm in}\quad L^{1}(\Omega)

\Box

We next improve the regularity of

Lemma 4.3
.

Assume that

\lVert\frac{1}{a-u}\rVert_{L^{\frac{3N}{2}}(K)}\leq c_{32}.

Then there exists
c_{33}>0
depending on

\inf_{x\in K}(a(x)-u(x))>c_{33}.

(4.10)

Proof.
By (

), we have that

\frac{1}{(a-u)^{2}}\in L^{\frac{3N}{4}}(K)

which contradicts (

\Box

Proposition 4.4
.

Assume that
1\leq N\leq 7
,
\Omega=B_{1}(0)
,
the function

a

with

is a classical solution of (
1.4

Proof.
Since the mapping

is strictly increasing and bounded by
a

4.3
,
we only have to improve the regularity of

in any compact set of

u_{\lambda}
is stable, then

\int_{B_{1}(0)}\frac{2\lambda\varphi^{2}}{(a-u_{\lambda})^{3}}dx<\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}dx,\quad\forall\varphi\in H^{1}_{0}(B_{1}(0))\setminus\{0\}.

(4.11)

We claim that the minimal solutions
u_{\lambda}
is radially symmetric for
\lambda\in(0,\lambda^{*}]
. Indeed, the minimal solution
u_{\lambda}
could be approximated

v_{n}=\lambda\mathbb{G}_{B_{1}(0)}[\frac{1}{(a-v_{n-1})^{2}}]\ \ \ {\rm with}\ \ v_{0}=0.

It follows by radially symmetry of

and
a
that

u_{\lambda^{*}}
is radially symmetric.

By (
4.2

Otherwise, there exists

which contradicts (
4.2

Let us denote

\varphi_{\theta}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}(a-u_{\lambda})^{\theta}-\epsilon_{\lambda}^{\theta}&{\rm in}\quad B_{r_{n}}(0),\\[4.2679pt]
\phantom{}0&{\rm in}\quad B_{1}(0)\setminus B_{r_{n}}(0),\end{array}\right.

(4.13)

Multiplying (

\frac{\theta^{2}}{1-2\theta}[(a-u_{\lambda})^{2\theta-1}-\epsilon_{\lambda}^{2\theta-1}]
and applying integration by parts yields that

\displaystyle\frac{\theta^{2}}{1-2\theta}\int_{B_{r_{n}}(0)}[-\Delta a-\frac{\lambda}{(a-u_{\lambda})^{2}}][(a-u_{\lambda})^{2\theta-1}-\epsilon_{\lambda}^{2\theta-1}]dx

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{\theta^{2}}{1-2\theta}\int_{B_{r_{n}}(0)}\nabla(a-u_{\lambda})\cdot\nabla\left((a-u_{\lambda})^{2\theta-1}\right)dx

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=-\theta^{2}\int_{B_{r_{n}}(0)}(a-u_{\lambda})^{2\theta-2}|\nabla(a-u_{\lambda})|^{2}dx,

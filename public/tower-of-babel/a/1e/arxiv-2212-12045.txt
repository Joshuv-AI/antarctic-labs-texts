Random block coordinate methods for inconsistent convex optimisation problems
MathiasStaudigl, PaulinJacquot

Abstract
We develop a novel randomised block coordinate primal-dual algorithm for a class of non-smooth ill-posed convex programs. Lying in the midway between the celebrated Chambolle-Pock primal-dual algorithm and Tseng’s accelerated proximal gradient method, we establish global convergence of the last iterate as well optimal O ⁡ ( 1 / k ) O(1/k) and O ⁡ ( 1 / k 2 ) O(1/k^{2}) complexity rates in the convex and strongly convex case, respectively, k k being the iteration count. Motivated by the increased complexity in the control of distribution level electric power systems , we test the performance of our method on a second-order cone relaxation of an AC-OPF problem. Distributed control is achieved via the distributed locational marginal prices (DLMPs), which are obtained as dual variables in our optimisation framework.

Introduction

In this paper we study non-smooth convex composite optimisation problems of the form

\min_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\{\Psi(\bm{x}):=\Phi(\bm{x})+R(\bm{x})\},

(P)

where
\mathcal{X}:=\arg\min\left\{\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{A}\bm{z}-\bm{b}\|^{2}|\bm{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\right\},
and the functions
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
and
R:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to(-\infty,\infty]
are convex and additively decomposable of the form
\Phi(\bm{x}):=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\Phi_{i}(\bm{x}_{i})
and
R(\bm{x}):=\sum_{i=1}^{d}r_{i}(\bm{x}_{i})
. We assume that each function
\phi_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}}\to\mathbb{R}
is smooth, whereas
r_{i}(\cdot)
is only required to be proper convex and lower semi-continuous. We typically think of the smooth component
\phi_{i}(\cdot)
as a convex loss function, whereas
r_{i}(\cdot)
can take over to role of a regularising or penalty function. In particular,
r_{i}(\cdot)
can represent an indicator function of a closed convex set
\mathcal{K}_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}}
, representing individual membership constraints of the decision variable
\bm{x}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}}
. Thus, problem (
P
) can include certain separable block constraints in addition to the non-separable constraints embodied in the set
\mathcal{X}
. This setting gains relevance in distributed optimisation problems with joint coupling constraints, such as multi-agent optimisation problems
Facchinei and Kanzow 2007
. Other examples include convex penalty functions, like the
\ell_{1}
-norm on
\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}}
. The matrix
\bm{A}=[\bm{A}_{1};\ldots;\bm{A}_{d}]
is decomposed in
q\times m_{i}
matrices
\bm{A}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{q\times m_{i}}
. Accordingly, we use the notation
\bm{x}=[\bm{x}_{1};\ldots;\bm{x}_{d}]
with each
\bm{x}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}}
to represent the blocks of coordinates of
\bm{x}
, and
m=m_{1}+\ldots+m_{d}
. The joint restriction
\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}
state that a feasible decision variable is a solution to a linear least squares problem and could be equivalently described as the set of solutions to the normal equations
\mathcal{X}=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}|\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{x}=\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{b}\}
. When
\bm{b}
is in the range of
\bm{A}
, then we can solve the linear system
\bm{A}\bm{x}=\bm{b}
exactly, and the optimisation problem reduces to a linearly constrained convex non-smooth minimisation problem

\min_{\bm{x}}\{\Psi(\bm{x})=\Phi(\bm{x})+R(\bm{x})\}\quad\text{s.t.: }\bm{A}\bm{x}=\bm{b}.

(1)

We call this the
consistent case
. Problems of type (
1
) are very general and include all generic conic optimisation problems, such as linear programming, second-order cone optimisation and semi-definite programming. In particular, partitioning the matrix
\bm{A}
into suitably defined blocks, problem (
1
) is a canonical formulation of various distributed optimisation problems, as the next examples illustrate.

Example 1

(Consensus)
.

Consider the problem

\min_{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}(\bm{x})

where
r_{1},\ldots,r_{d}
are closed convex functions on
\mathbb{R}^{p}
. This problem is equivalent to

\min_{\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}(\bm{x}_{i})\quad\text{ s.t.: }\bm{x}_{1}=\bm{x}_{2}=\ldots=\bm{x}_{n}

This can be written as a linear constrained optimisation problem with matrix
\bm{A}
corresponding to the Laplacian of a connected undirected graph and linear constraint
\bm{A}\bm{x}=0
.

Example 2

(Distributed model fitting)
.

Problem (
P
) can also cover composite minimisation problems which canonically arise in machine learning. Consider the problem

\min_{\bm{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}\ell(\bm{K}\bm{u}-\bm{b})+\rho(\bm{u}),

where
\ell(\cdot)
is a convex and smooth statistical loss function of the form

\ell(\bm{K}\bm{u}-\bm{b})=\sum_{i=1}^{q}\ell_{i}(\bm{K}_{i}^{\top}\bm{u}-b_{i})

where
\ell_{i}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is the loss for the
i
-th training example,
\bm{K}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
is the feature vector for example
i
, and
b_{i}
is the output or response for example
i
. Define the variable
\bm{x}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\bm{u}\\
\bm{v}\end{array}\right]\in\mathbb{R}^{p+q}=\mathbb{R}^{m}
and the linear operator
\bm{A}\bm{x}=\bm{K}\bm{u}-\bm{v}
. Define the functions
\phi_{i}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
and
r_{i}:\mathbb{R}\to(-\infty,\infty]
by

\phi_{i}(\bm{x}_{i}):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\ell_{i}(v_{i})&\text{if }p+1\leq i\leq p+d\\
0&\text{ if }1\leq i\leq p\end{array}\right.\quad r_{i}(\bm{x}_{i}):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\rho_{i}(u_{i})&\text{if }1\leq i\leq p\\
0&\text{ if }p+1\leq i\leq m\end{array}\right.

Setting
\Phi(\bm{x})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\phi_{i}(\bm{x}_{i})
and
R(\bm{x})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}r_{i}(\bm{x}_{i})
yields the convex optimisation problem

\min_{\bm{x}}\Phi(\bm{x})+R(\bm{x})\quad\text{s.t.: }\bm{A}\bm{x}=\bm{b}.

Problem (
P
) is more general than the examples just presented, since we do not insist on
\bm{b}
belonging to the range of
\bm{A}
. Hence, the optimisation problems of interest to us are non-smooth convex problems with
inconsistent
linear restrictions under which the exact condition
\bm{A}\bm{x}=\bm{b}
is replaced by the approximate condition
\bm{A}\bm{x}\approx\bm{b}
. This assumption gains relevance in inverse problems and PDE-constrained optimisation, where problems of the from (
P
) appear in the method of residuals (Morozov regularisation)
Grasmair et al. 2011
. Another instance where ill-posed linear restrictions appear is studied in an application to power systems in Section
5
of this work. Motivated by all these different optimisation problems, this paper derives a unified random
block-coordinate method
which solves problem (
P
) under very general assumptions on the problem data.

1.1
Related Methods

Our algorithms are closely related to randomised coordinate descent methods, primal–dual coordinate update methods, and accelerated primal–dual methods. In this subsection, let us briefly review these three classes of methods and discuss their relations to our work.

Linear constrained minimisation

From the theoretical standpoint, one could formulate problem (
P
) as a linear constrained optimisation problem of the type (
1
), with linear restriction
\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{x}=\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{b}
(the normal equations). Hence, one could approach problem (
P
) via primal-dual techniques directly. While we will show that our main algorithmic scheme (Algorithm
1
) is indeed equivalent to a suitably defined primal-dual method, it can be argued that this connection is not always a recommended solution strategy in practice. First, a primal-dual implementation has to deal with the symmetric matrix
\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{A}
, whose dimension is
m\times m
. If
q
is much smaller than
m
then primal-dual methods would have to process many more data points than direct approaches. Second, if
\bm{A}
is a sparse matrix, then
\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{A}
is usually not sparse anymore, which leads to heavy use of numerical linear algebra techniques. Finally, it is often not known a-priori whether the linear system is de facto consistent with the given inputs.This is in particular the case in the application motivating the development of the numerical scheme to be presented in this paper, which is concerned with the distributed
optimisation
of an electrical distribution system within an AC-optimal power flow framework
Wood et al. 2013
;
Bienstock 2015
. A basic stability desideratum on a numerical solution method (acting as a decentralised coordination mechanism in our application) is therefore that overall system convergence is guaranteed even if the linear constraints are not satisfied with equality. Our method is exactly achieving this. Section
5
illustrates the performance of our method on a 15-bus AC-OPF problem taken from
Papavasiliou 2018
.

Primal penalty methods

An alternative and popular approach to solve (
P
) is the penalty method. It consists in solving a sequence of unconstrained optimisation problems

\min_{\bm{x}}\Psi(\bm{x})+\frac{\sigma_{k}}{2}\|\bm{A}\bm{x}-\bm{b}\|^{2},

where
\sigma_{k}
is a positive and increasing penalty parameter sequence. Intuitively it is clear that, by choosing
\sigma_{k}
larger, the more importance the optimisation gives to the constraints. Since penalty methods are entirely primal, they do not use duality arguments, and hence they are in principle able to solve the inconsistent case as well. However, their implementation usually involves two loops: an inner loop solving the minimisation problem for a fixed parameter
\sigma_{k}
to some desired accuracy, followed by an outer loop describing how the penalty parameter is updated. Viewed from this angle, our algorithm is performing these operations in a single-loop fashion.

Randomised coordinate descent methods

In the absence of the linear constraints, our algorithm specialises to randomised coordinate descent (RCD), which was first proposed in
Nesterov 2012
, and later generalised to the non-smooth case in
Lu and Xiao 2015
;
Richtárik and Takáč 2014
. It was shown that RCD features sublinear rates of convergence with rate
O(1/k)
,
k
being the iteration counter. Acceleration to a
O(1/k^{2})
complexity and even linear rates for strongly convex problems has been obtained. Extensions to parallel computations, important for large-scale optimization problems, were first proposed in
Richtárik and Takáč 2016
.

Primal-dual coordinate update methods

To cope with linear constraints, a very popular approach is the alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM). Originally, ADMM
Glowinski and Marroco 1975
;
Gabay and Mercier 1976
was proposed for two-block structured problems with separable objective. The convergence and complexity analysis of this method is well documented in the literature
Han et al. 2017
; see
Eckstein and Yao 2015
for a survey. Direct extensions of the ADMM to multi-block settings such as (
P
) is not straightforward, and indeed even may fail to converge
Chen et al. 2016
. Very recently,
Gao et al. 2019
proposed a randomised primal–dual coordinate (RPDC) update method, whose asynchronous parallel version was then studied in
Gao and Zhang 2017
. It was shown that RPDC converges with rate
O(1/k)
under convexity assumption. Improved complexity statementes for multi-block ADMM can be found in
Deng et al. 2016
.

Accelerated primal-dual methods

It is possible to accelerate the rate of convergence from
O(1/k)
to
O(1/k^{2})
for gradient type methods. The first acceleration result was shown by
Nesterov 1983
for solving smooth unconstrained problems. The technique has been generalised to accelerated gradient-type methods on possibly non-smooth convex programs
Beck and Teboulle 2009
;
Nesterov 2013
. Primal–dual methods on solving linearly constrained problems can also be accelerated by similar techniques. Under convexity assumption, the augmented Lagrangian method (ALM) is accelerated from
O(1/k)
to
O(1/k^{2})
in
Kang et al. 2015
.

1.2
Contribution

Methodological contributions

We propose a block-coordinate implementation of the method developed by
Malitsky 2019
for linearly constrained optimisation, lying midway between the celebrated primal-dual hybrid gradient algorithm
Chambolle and Pock 2011
and Tseng’s accelerated proximal gradient method
Tseng 2008
. Specifically, our proposed method is a distributed interpretation of the primal-dual algorithm of
Chambolle and Pock 2011
which operates on randomly selected coordinate blocks. A parallel between the Chambolle-Pock method
Chambolle and Pock 2011
and the accelerated proximal gradient of
Tseng 2008
was already drawn in
Malitsky 2017
;
Malitsky 2019
. Reducing the primal-dual algorithm to an implementation of Tseng’s method enabled
Malitsky 2017
to derive new convergence results based on primal arguments, thus departing from strong duality requirements and the ergodic rates typically issued for primal-dual methods. Our developments revisit the coordinate-descent implementation proposed in
Luke and Malitsky 2018
for the basic algorithm of
Malitsky 2019
, to a block-coordinate descent setting featuring Nesterov type of acceleration. In particular, in the strongly convex case, we derive a new step size policy that achieves an accelerated rate of
O(k^{-2})
. Besides the recent preprint
Tran-Dinh and Liu 2020
, we are not aware of a similar algorithm achieving the accelerated convergence rates
O(k^{-2})
in a fully distributed computational setting. Thus, our main contributions in this paper can be summarised as follows:

(i)

In the convex case, provided problem (
P
) possesses a saddle point (defined in Section
2.6
) our main result is Theorem
6
which establishes an
O(1/k)
iteration complexity in terms of the objective function gap and convergence of the last iterate.

(ii)

In the strongly convex case and uniform sampling of coordinate blocks, our main result is Theorem
8
, which proves an accelerated
O(k^{-2})
convergence rate in the primal objective function values.

We remark that RCD methods have been shown to exhibit linear convergence rates in the strongly convex regime
Nesterov 2012
. Such fast convergence is, however, not to be expected in the presence of linear constraints. While strong convexity of the primal objective ensures smoothness of the Lagrangian dual function, but not its strong concavity. Hence, in general, we do not expect to see linear convergence rates by only assuming strong convexity in the primal. However, we note that
Xu and Zhang 2018
obtain linear convergence rates in the
consistent
regime if there is one block variable that is independent of all others in the objective (but coupled in the linear constraint) and also the corresponding component function is smooth.

Related to this paper is also the very recent work
Tran-Dinh and Liu 2022
. They consider a larger class of convex optimisation problems with linear constraints and design a new randomized primal-dual algorithm with single block activation in each iteration and similar complexity results as reported in the present work. However, our method is able to solve
inconsistent
convex optimisation problems with general sampling techniques.

2.2
Block structure

We first describe the block setup which has become standard in the analysis of block coordinate methods
Richtárik and Takáč 2014
;
Fercoq and Richtárik 2015
;
Richtárik and Takáč 2016
;
Ryu and Yin 2022
. The block structure of (
P
) is given by a decomposition of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
into
d

\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}},1\leq i\leq d
, so that
\mathbb{R}^{m}=\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}^{m_{d}}
. Let
\bm{U}=[\bm{U}_{1},\ldots,\bm{U}_{d}]
be the
m\times m

\bm{U}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m_{i}}
. For
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, let
\bm{x}_{i}=\bm{U}^{\top}_{i}\bm{x}
be the block of coordinates corresponding to the columns of
\bm{U}_{i}
. Any vector
\bm{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
can be written as
\bm{s}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bm{U}_{i}\bm{s}_{i}
. For
\emptyset\neq I\subseteq[d]

\bm{s}_{[I]}=\sum_{i\in I}\bm{U}_{i}\bm{s}_{i}.

We denote the

\|\cdot\|_{i}
. If
\bm{Q}=\blkdiag[\bm{Q}_{1};\ldots,\bm{Q}_{d}]

\bm{Q}_{i}\in\mathbb{S}^{m_{i}}_{++}
, we define a weighted norm on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
by

\|\bm{s}\|_{\bm{Q}}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\|\bm{s}_{i}\|_{\bm{Q}_{i}}\qquad\forall\bm{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

2.3
Smoothness of
\Phi

We assume throughout the paper that
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
is convex and possesses a Lipschitz continuous partial gradient. Specifically, we assume that for each block
i\in[d]
there exists a matrix
\Lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{S}^{m_{i}}_{++}
so that

0\leq\phi_{i}(\bm{x}_{i}+\bm{t}_{i})-\phi_{i}(\bm{x}_{i})-\langle{\nabla\phi_{i}(\bm{x}_{i})},{\bm{t}_{i}}\rangle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{t}_{i}\|^{2}_{\Lambda_{i}},

(2)

A typical situation is that
\Lambda_{i}=L_{i}\bm{I}_{m_{i}}
for a scalar
L_{i}>0
, so that the gradient
\nabla\phi
is Lipschitz continuous with modulus
L_{i}>0
. Allowing for matrix-valued parameters increases generality and takes into account that norms on the factors
\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}}
might differ from block to block. Collecting all the matrices
\Lambda_{i}
in one block diagonal matrix
\Lambda:=\blkdiag[\Lambda_{1};\ldots;\Lambda_{d}]
, it follows

\Phi(\bm{x}^{\prime})-\Phi(\bm{x})-\langle{\nabla\Phi(\bm{x})},{\bm{x}^{\prime}-\bm{x}}\rangle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{x}^{\prime}-\bm{x}\|^{2}_{\Lambda}.

2.4
Properties of
R

We assume that
R:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to(-\infty,\infty]
is block separable
R(\bm{x})=\sum_{i=1}^{d}r_{i}(\bm{x}_{i})
, where the functions
r_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}}\to(-\infty,\infty]

\mu_{i}\geq 0

(3)

Typical examples for the regulariser
r_{i}
are indicator functions of closed convex sets, i.e.
r_{i}(\bm{x}_{i})=\delta_{\mathcal{K}_{i}}(\bm{x}_{i})
, for
\mathcal{K}_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}}

-penalty
r_{i}(\bm{x}_{i})=\|\bm{x}_{i}\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}})}

\Upsilon=\blkdiag[\Upsilon_{1};\ldots;\Upsilon_{d}]
be the
m\times m

2.6
On Saddle points

The optimization problem can be equivalently expressed as the linear constrained optimisation problem

\min_{\bm{x}=(\bm{x}_{1},\ldots,\bm{x}_{d})}\Psi(\bm{x})=\Phi(\bm{x})+R(\bm{x})\quad\text{s.t.: }\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{x}=\bm{A}^{\top}b

(7)

The Lagrangian associated with this non-smooth convex optimization problem is

\mathcal{L}(\bm{x},\bm{y})=\Psi(\bm{x})+\langle{\bm{y}},{\bm{A}^{\top}b-\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{x}}\rangle.

Definition 1
.

A pair
(\bm{x}^{\ast},\bm{y}^{\ast})
is called a
saddle-point
if

0\in\partial\Psi(\bm{x}^{\ast})-\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{y}^{\ast},\quad\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{x}^{\ast}-\bm{A}^{\top}b=0.

(8)

For convex programs, the conditions (
8
) are sufficient for
\bm{x}^{\ast}
to be a solution of (
P
). They are also necessary if a constraint qualification condition holds (e.g. the Slater condition, stating that there exists
\bm{x}
in the interior of the domain of
\Psi
such that
\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{x}=\bm{A}^{\top}b
).

2.7
Random Sampling

We next introduce our random sampling strategy. Our approach is very general, and allows for virtually all existing sampling strategies considered in the literature. We refer the reader to
Qu and Richtárik 2016a
;
Qu and Richtárik 2016b
for an in-depth systematic overview on this topic.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be a probability space. By a sampling we mean a random set-valued mapping with values in
2^{[d]}
. We will call the random variable
\mathcal{I}:\Omega\to 2^{[d]}
a
random sampling
.
\mathcal{I}(\omega)
defines the selection of blocks employed in a single iteration of our method. The set
\mathcal{I}(\Omega)=\Sigma
is the set of all possible realisations of the random selection mechanism. Let
\{\iota_{k}\}_{k\in\mathcal{N}}
represent the stochastic process on
2^{[d]}
in which each random variable
\iota_{k}
is an i.i.d copy of
\mathcal{I}
. We refer to such a sampling as an
i.i.d sampling
. We assume that the sampling
\mathcal{I}
is
proper
: There exists a vector
\bm{\pi}=[\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{d}]\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with

\pi_{i}=\mathbb{P}(i\in\mathcal{I})\in(0,1)\qquad\forall i\in[d].

With the sampling
\mathcal{I}
, we associate the matrix
\bm{\Pi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
defined as

(\bm{\Pi})_{ij}:=\mathbb{P}(\{i,j\}\subseteq\mathcal{I})\qquad\forall i\neq j,\text{ and }(\bm{\Pi})_{ii}=\pi_{i}.

We note that
\bm{\Pi}\succ 0

(
Qu and Richtárik 2016b
, Thm 3.1)
. We further define the weighting matrix

\bm{P}:=\blkdiag[\frac{1}{\pi_{1}}\bm{I}_{m_{1}},\ldots,\frac{1}{\pi_{d}}\bm{I}_{m_{d}}].

We emphasise that the random sampling model we adopt here is capable of capturing many stationary randomised activation mechanisms. To illustrate this, consider the following activation mechanisms:

•

Single coordinate activation:
at each iteration, one coordinate block is activated. This means that
\mathcal{I}(\omega)
takes values in the discrete set
[d]
only, i.e.
\Sigma=[d]
. In this case, we necessarily have
\sum_{i=1}^{d}\pi_{i}=1
.

•

Uniform Sampling:
for all
i\in[d]
it holds
\mathbb{P}(i\in\mathcal{I})=\mathbb{P}(j\in\mathcal{I})
. This implies
\pi_{i}=\frac{\mathbb{E}[\lvert\mathcal{I}\rvert]}{d}
for all
i\in[d]
. A special case of a uniform sampling is the popular class of
m
-nice samplings, arising under the specification where
\Sigma
is the set of all subsets of
[d]
containing exactly
m
elements, each of which is activated with uniform probability. Clearly, in this case one has
\pi_{i}=\frac{m}{d}
for all
i\in[d]
.

•

Full Sampling:

\Sigma=\{1,\ldots,d\}
, which means that all coordinates are updated in parallel.

We will choose the matrices

4
Convergence Analysis

This section is concerned with the convergence properties of Algorithm
1
. We start with a basic descent property of the primal forward-backward step. This will involve the identification of a Lyapunov function to obtain energy decay estimates in a variable metric. Building on this result, we investigate two important scenarios in isolation: First, we consider the merely convex case, which is obtained when
\Upsilon=0
. If
\Upsilon\succ 0
, then accelerated rates in the primal iterates can be obtained.
This, however, requires the derivation of a suitable step-size policy that exploits strong convexity for boosting the performance of the method. Understanding the mechanics of this step-size regime involves a delicate analysis of the thus-constructed step-size policy, which is relegated to Appendix
8
.

\displaystyle=h(\bm{x}^{\ast})-h(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1})-\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{A}(\bm{x}^{*}-\bm{w}^{k})\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{A}(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{w}^{k})\|^{2},

\displaystyle\langle{\nabla h(\bm{x}^{k})},{\bm{x}^{*}-\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}}\rangle

\displaystyle=h(\bm{x}^{\ast})-h(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1})-\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{A}(\bm{x}^{*}-\bm{x}^{k})\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{A}(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k})\|^{2}.

Combined with the previous display, this shows for
\bm{x}^{\ast}\in\arg\min_{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}h(\bm{x})
,

\displaystyle\Psi(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1})-\Psi(\bm{x}^{*})

\displaystyle\leq-S_{k}[h(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1})-h(\bm{x}^{*})]-\frac{S_{k-1}}{2}\|\bm{A}(\bm{x}^{*}-\bm{w}^{k})\|^{2}+\frac{S_{k-1}}{2}\|\bm{A}(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{w}^{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{\sigma_{k}}{2}\|\bm{A}(\bm{x}^{*}-\bm{x}^{k})\|^{2}+\frac{\sigma_{k}}{2}\|\bm{A}(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{x}^{*}\|^{2}_{\bm{Q}^{k}}-\frac{1}{2}\|\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{*}\|^{2}_{\bm{Q}^{k}+\Upsilon}-\frac{1}{2}\|\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k}\|^{2}_{\bm{Q}^{k}-\Lambda}

\displaystyle=-\left(S_{k}h(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1})+S_{k-1}h(\bm{w}^{k})+\sigma_{k}h(\bm{x}^{k})-2S_{k}h(\bm{x}^{*})\right)+\frac{S_{k-1}}{2}\|\bm{A}(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{w}^{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{x}^{*}\|^{2}_{\bm{Q}^{k}}-\frac{1}{2}\|\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{*}\|^{2}_{\bm{Q}^{k}+\Upsilon}-\frac{1}{2}\|\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k}\|^{2}_{\bm{Q}^{k}-\Lambda-\sigma_{k}\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{A}}.

Finally, applying identity (
16
), one sees

\displaystyle-\left(S_{k}h(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1})+S_{k-1}h(\bm{w}^{k})+\sigma_{k}h(\bm{x}^{k})-2S_{k}h(\bm{x}^{*})\right)+\frac{S_{k-1}}{2}\|\bm{A}(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{w}^{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\left(S_{k}h(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1})+S_{k-1}h(\bm{w}^{k})+\sigma_{k}h(\bm{x}^{k})-2S_{k}h(\bm{x}^{*})\right)

\displaystyle+S_{k-1}\left(\frac{S_{k}}{\sigma_{k}}h(\bm{w}^{k})+\frac{\sigma_{k}}{2S_{k-1}}\|\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k}\|^{2}_{\Xi-\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{A}}-\frac{S^{2}_{k}}{\sigma_{k}S_{k-1}}\mathbb{E}_{k}[h(\bm{w}^{k+1})]+\frac{S_{k}}{S_{k-1}}h(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{S^{2}_{k-1}}{\sigma_{k}}h(\bm{w}^{k})-\frac{S^{2}_{k}}{\sigma_{k}}\mathbb{E}_{k}[h(\bm{w}^{k+1})]+2S_{k}h(\bm{x}^{*})-\sigma_{k}h(\bm{x}^{k})+\frac{\sigma_{k}}{2}\|\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k}\|^{2}_{\Xi-\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{A}}

\displaystyle=\frac{S^{2}_{k-1}}{\sigma_{k}}(h(\bm{w}^{k})-h(\bm{x}^{*}))-\frac{S^{2}_{k}}{\sigma_{k}}\mathbb{E}_{k}[h(\bm{w}^{k+1})-h(\bm{x}^{*})]-\sigma_{k}(h(\bm{x}^{k})-h(\bm{x}^{*}))+\frac{\sigma_{k}}{2}\|\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k}\|^{2}_{\Xi-\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{A}},

where the last step uses the identity
2S_{k}=\frac{S^{2}_{k}}{\sigma_{k}}-\frac{S^{2}_{k-1}}{\sigma_{k}}+\sigma_{k}
. Plugging this expression into the penultimate display, we arrive that

\begin{split}\Psi(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1})-\Psi(\bm{x}^{*})&\leq\frac{S^{2}_{k-1}}{\sigma_{k}}(h(\bm{w}^{k})-h(\bm{x}^{*}))-\frac{S^{2}_{k}}{\sigma_{k}}\mathbb{E}_{k}[h(\bm{w}^{k+1})-h(\bm{x}^{*})]-\sigma_{k}(h(\bm{x}^{k})-h(\bm{x}^{*}))\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{x}^{*}\|^{2}_{\bm{Q}^{k}}-\frac{1}{2}\|\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{*}\|^{2}_{\bm{Q}^{k}+\Upsilon}-\frac{1}{2}\|\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k}\|^{2}_{\bm{Q}^{k}-\Lambda-\sigma_{k}\Xi}.\end{split}

(20)

Define the vector-valued function
\vec{\psi}(\bm{x}):=[\phi_{1}(\bm{x}_{1})+r_{1}(\bm{x}_{1}),\ldots,\phi_{d}(\bm{x}_{d})+r_{d}(\bm{x}_{d})]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let

\hat{\Psi}_{k}:=\sum_{t=0}^{k}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\gamma_{i}^{k,t}\psi_{i}(\bm{x}^{t}_{i})=\sum_{t=0}^{k}\gamma^{k,t}\cdot\vec{\psi}(\bm{x}^{t}),

where
\gamma^{k,t}\cdot\vec{\psi}(\bm{x}^{t}):=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\gamma_{i}^{k,t}\psi_{i}(\bm{x}^{t}_{i})
. Thanks to Lemma
2
, we have
\hat{\Psi}_{k}\geq\Psi(\bm{w}^{k})
for all
k\geq 0
.

Lemma 3
.

Let
\bm{M}:=\blkdiag[\bm{M}_{1};\ldots;\bm{M}_{d}]
with
\bm{M}_{i}\in\mathbb{S}^{m_{i}}_{++}
. We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{*}\|^{2}_{\bm{P}\bm{M}}]

\displaystyle=\|\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{*}\|^{2}_{\bm{M}}+\|\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{x}^{*}\|^{2}_{(\bm{P}-\bm{I})\bm{M}},

(21)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[\hat{\Psi}_{k+1}]

\displaystyle=(1-\theta_{k})\hat{\Psi}_{k}+\theta_{k}\Psi(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}).

(22)

Proof.

Eq. (
21
) can be easily seen by observing

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\bm{x}^{k+1}_{i}-\bm{x}^{*}_{i}\|^{2}_{\frac{1}{\pi_{i}}\bm{M}_{i}}]

\displaystyle=\pi_{i}\|\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}_{i}-\bm{x}^{*}_{i}\|^{2}_{\frac{1}{\pi_{i}}\bm{M}_{i}}+(1-\pi_{i})\|\bm{x}^{k}_{i}-\bm{x}^{\ast}_{i}\|^{2}_{\frac{1}{\pi_{i}}\bm{M}_{i}}

\displaystyle=\|\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}_{i}-\bm{x}^{*}_{i}\|^{2}_{\bm{M}_{i}}+(\pi^{-1}_{i}-1)\|\bm{x}^{k}_{i}-\bm{x}^{*}_{i}\|^{2}_{\bm{M}_{i}}.

Summing over all
i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
gives the result. To prove (
22
), we first observe

\mathbb{E}_{k}[\psi_{i}(\bm{x}^{k+1}_{i})]=\pi_{i}\psi_{i}(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}_{i})+(1-\pi_{i})\psi_{i}(\bm{x}^{k}_{i}),

so that
\mathbb{E}_{k}[\vec{\psi}(\bm{x}^{k+1})]=\bm{P}^{-1}\vec{\psi}(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1})+(\bm{I}-\bm{P}^{-1})\vec{\psi}(\bm{x}^{k})
. It follows

\displaystyle\hat{\Psi}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\gamma^{k+1,t}\cdot\vec{\psi}(\bm{x}^{t})+\gamma^{k+1,k}\cdot\vec{\psi}(\bm{x}^{k})+\gamma^{k+1,k+1}\cdot\vec{\psi}(\bm{x}^{k+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}(1-\theta_{k})\gamma^{k,t}\cdot\vec{\psi}(\bm{x}^{t})+\left((1-\theta_{k})\gamma^{k,k}+\theta_{k}(1-\pi^{-1})\right)\cdot\vec{r}(\bm{x}^{k})+\theta_{k}\pi^{-1}\cdot\vec{\psi}(\bm{x}^{k+1})

\displaystyle=(1-\theta_{k})\hat{\Psi}_{k}+\theta_{k}\left((1-\pi^{-1})\cdot\vec{\psi}(\bm{x}^{k})+\pi^{-1}\cdot\vec{\psi}(\bm{x}^{k+1})\right).

Taking conditional expectations on both sides, it follows

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[\hat{\Psi}_{k+1}]

\displaystyle=(1-\theta_{k})\hat{\Psi}_{k}+\theta_{k}\left((1-\pi^{-1})\cdot\vec{\psi}(\bm{x}^{k})+\Psi(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1})+(\pi^{-1}-1)\cdot\vec{\psi}(\bm{x}^{k})\right)

\displaystyle=(1-\theta_{k})\hat{\Psi}_{k}+\theta_{k}\Psi(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}).

∎

The next result characterises a suitable Lyapunov function in an adapted variable metric. We set
\bm{B}:=\blkdiag[\bm{B}_{1};\ldots;\bm{B}_{d}]
.

Lemma 4
.

Assume that
\theta_{0}\in(0,\min_{i\in[d]}\pi_{i}]
, and that the sequences
(\sigma_{k})_{k\geq 0},(\tau_{k})_{k\geq 0}
satisfy the matrix inequalities

\displaystyle\bm{P}\bm{B}\succeq\tau_{k}(\Lambda+\sigma_{k}\Xi),

(23)

\displaystyle\bm{P}^{2}\bm{B}+\tau_{k}\bm{P}\Upsilon\succeq\frac{\tau_{k}\sigma_{k+1}}{\tau_{k+1}\sigma_{k}}(\bm{P}^{2}\bm{B}-\tau_{k+1}(\bm{I}-\bm{P})\Upsilon).

(24)

Define the matrix-valued sequence
(\bm{W}_{k})_{k\geq 0}\subset\mathbb{S}^{m}_{++}
by
\bm{W}_{k}=\frac{\sigma_{k}}{\tau_{k}}\bm{P}^{2}\bm{B}+\sigma_{k}(\bm{P}-\bm{I})\Upsilon
, and introduce the functions

\displaystyle F_{k}:=\hat{\Psi}_{k}+S_{k-1}(h(\bm{w}^{k})-h(\bm{x}^{*})),\text{ and }

(25)

\displaystyle V_{k}(\bm{x}):=\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{x}\|^{2}_{\bm{W}_{k}}+S_{k-1}(F_{k}-\Psi(\bm{x})),

(26)

We have for all
\bm{x}^{*}\in\mathcal{X}
,

\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[V_{k+1}(\bm{x}^{*})\right]\leq V_{k}(\bm{x}^{*})-\sigma^{2}_{k}(h(\bm{x}^{k})-h(\bm{x}^{*}))-\frac{1}{2}\|\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k}\|^{2}_{\frac{\sigma_{k}}{\tau_{k}}(\bm{P}\bm{B}-\tau_{k}(\sigma_{k}\Xi+\Lambda))}.

(27)

Proof.

Using identity (
21
) with
\bm{M}=\bm{Q}_{k}+\Upsilon
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{*}\|^{2}_{\bm{P}\bm{Q}^{k}+\bm{P}\Upsilon}\right]=\frac{1}{2}\|\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{*}\|^{2}_{\bm{Q}^{k}+\Upsilon}+\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{x}^{*}\|^{2}_{(\bm{P}-\bm{I})(\bm{Q}^{k}+\Upsilon)}

Furthermore, eq. (
22
) yields

\displaystyle\frac{S_{k}}{\sigma_{k}}\mathbb{E}_{k}[\hat{\Psi}_{k+1}-\Psi(\bm{x}^{*})]=\frac{S_{k-1}}{\sigma_{k}}\left(\hat{\Psi}_{k}-\Psi(\bm{x}^{*})\right)+\left(\Psi(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1})-\Psi(\bm{x}^{*})\right),

and (
20
) shows

\displaystyle\frac{S^{2}_{k}}{\sigma_{k}}\mathbb{E}_{k}[h(\bm{w}^{k+1})-h(\bm{x}^{*})]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{S^{2}_{k-1}}{\sigma_{k}}[h(\bm{w}^{k})-h(\bm{x}^{*})]-[\Psi(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1})-\Psi(\bm{x}^{*})]-\sigma_{k}[h(\bm{x}^{k})-h(\bm{x}^{*})]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{x}^{*}\|^{2}_{\bm{Q}^{k}}-\frac{1}{2}\|\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{*}\|^{2}_{\bm{Q}^{k}+\Upsilon}-\frac{1}{2}\|\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k}\|^{2}_{\bm{Q}^{k}-\Lambda-\sigma_{k}\Xi}.

Adding these three expression together, we obtain the recursion

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{*}\|^{2}_{\bm{P}\bm{Q}^{k}+\bm{P}\Upsilon}+\frac{S_{k}}{\sigma_{k}}\left(\hat{\Psi}_{k+1}-\Psi(\bm{x}^{*})+S_{k}(h(\bm{w}^{k+1})-h(\bm{x}^{*}))\right)\right]\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{x}^{*}\|^{2}_{\bm{P}\bm{Q}^{k}+(\bm{P}-\bm{I})\Upsilon}+\frac{S_{k-1}}{\sigma_{k}}\left(\hat{\Psi}_{k}-\Psi(\bm{x}^{*})+S_{k-1}(h(\bm{w}^{k})-h(\bm{x}^{*}))\right)\\
&-\frac{1}{2}\|\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k}\|^{2}_{\bm{Q}^{k}-(\Lambda+\sigma_{k}\Xi)}-\sigma_{k}(h(\bm{x}^{k})-h(\bm{x}^{*})).\end{split}

(28)

Recall that
\bm{Q}^{k}=\frac{1}{\tau_{k}}\bm{P}\bm{B}
. Condition (
23
) guarantees that
\bm{Q}^{k}\succeq\Lambda+\sigma_{k}\Xi
. Multiplying both sides of (
28
) by
\sigma_{k}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{*}\|^{2}_{\frac{\sigma_{k}}{\tau_{k}}\bm{P}^{2}\bm{B}+\sigma_{k}\bm{P}\Upsilon}+S_{k}\left(\hat{\Psi}_{k+1}-\Psi(\bm{x}^{*})+S_{k}(h(\bm{w}^{k})-h(\bm{x}^{*}))\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{x}^{*}\|^{2}_{\frac{\sigma_{k}}{\tau_{k}}\bm{P}^{2}\bm{B}+\sigma_{k}(\bm{P}-\bm{I})\Upsilon}+S_{k-1}\left(\hat{\Psi}_{k}-\Psi(\bm{x}^{*})+S_{k-1}(h(\bm{w}^{k})-h(\bm{x}^{*}))\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\|\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k}\|^{2}_{\frac{\sigma_{k}}{\tau_{k}}\bm{P}\bm{B}-\sigma_{k}(\Lambda+\sigma_{k}\Xi)}-\sigma^{2}_{k}(h(\bm{x}^{k})-h(\bm{x}^{*})).

Under condition (
24
), it follows that

\|\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{*}\|^{2}_{\bm{W}_{k+1}}\leq\|\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{*}\|^{2}_{\frac{\sigma_{k}}{\tau_{k}}\bm{P}^{2}\bm{B}+\sigma_{k}\bm{P}\Upsilon}.

Exploiting this relation in the penultimate display, using definitions (
25
) and (
26
), one readily obtains

\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[V_{k+1}(\bm{x}^{*})\right]\leq V_{k}(\bm{x}^{*})-\sigma^{2}_{k}(h(\bm{x}^{k})-h(\bm{x}^{*}))-\frac{1}{2}\|\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k}\|^{2}_{\frac{\sigma_{k}}{\tau_{k}}(\bm{P}\bm{B}-\tau_{k}(\sigma_{k}\Xi+\Lambda))}

∎

4.2
The Convex case

Suppose that
\Upsilon=0
, so that
\bm{W}_{k}=\frac{\sigma_{k}}{\tau_{k}}\bm{P}^{2}\bm{B}
. In this case, the matrix inequality (
24
) is satisfied for any sequences
(\sigma_{k})_{k\geq 0},(\tau_{k})_{k\geq 0}
satisfying
\frac{\sigma_{k}}{\tau_{k}}\geq\frac{\sigma_{k+1}}{\tau_{k+1}}
. In particular, this can be realised with the policy
\sigma_{k}=\sigma\tau_{k}
for all
k\geq 0
, and some constant
\sigma\in(0,\min_{i\in[d]}\pi_{i}]
. This implies
\theta_{k}=\frac{\sigma}{1+k\sigma}
for all
k\geq 0
. This specification satisfies all conditions needed for Lemma
2
to hold. We further set
\tau_{k}\equiv 1
. It only remains to see if matrix inequality (
23
) is satisfied. In fact, this condition gives us a restriction on
\sigma
, reading as

\bm{P}\bm{B}\succeq\sigma\Xi+\Lambda.

(29)

We show later in this section that this matrix inequality is achievable. In this regime, we will show that we get an
O(1/k)
iteration complexity estimate of the averaged sequence
(\bm{w}^{k})_{k}
, in terms of the objective function value. This extends the results reported in
Malitsky 2017
;
Malitsky 2019
;
Luke and Malitsky 2018
to general random block-coordinate activation schemes. The following Assumptions shall be in place throughout this section:

Assumption 1
.

Problem (
P
) admits a saddle point, i.e. a pair
(\bm{x}^{\ast},\bm{y}^{\ast})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
satisfying (
8
).

Assumption 2
.

The solution set of problem (
P
)
\mathcal{X}^{\ast}
is nonempty.

Thanks to eq. (
17
), we know that
\bm{w}^{k}\in\texttt{Conv}(\bm{x}^{0},\ldots,\bm{x}^{k})\subseteq\texttt{dom}(r)
. Therefore,

\displaystyle R(\bm{w}^{k})-R(\bm{x}^{\ast})

\displaystyle\geq\langle{-\nabla\phi(\bm{x}^{*})-\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{y}^{*}},{\bm{w}^{k}-\bm{x}^{*}}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\phi(\bm{x}^{*})-\phi(\bm{w}^{k})+\langle{\bm{A}\bm{y}^{*}},{\bm{A}(\bm{x}^{*}-\bm{w}^{k})}\rangle

Whence,

\Psi(\bm{w}^{k})-\Psi(\bm{x}^{*})\geq-\|\bm{A}\bm{y}^{*}\|\cdot\|\bm{A}(\bm{w}^{k}-\bm{x}^{*})\|=-\delta\sqrt{h(\bm{w}^{k})-h(\bm{x}^{*})},

(30)

where
\delta:=\sqrt{2}\|A\bm{y}^{*}\|
. We assume that
\tau_{k}\equiv 1
and
\sigma_{k}=\sigma>0
. The energy functions (
25
) and (
26
) take the form
F_{k}=\hat{\Psi}_{k}+(1+(k-1)\sigma)(h(\bm{w}^{k})-h(\bm{x}^{*}))
, and
V_{k}(\bm{x}):=\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{x}\|^{2}_{\bm{P}^{2}\bm{B}}+(1+(k-1)\sigma)(F_{k}-\Psi(\bm{x}))
.

Lemma 5
.

Suppose that Assumption
1
applies. Then, the process
\left(S_{k-1}(F_{k}-\Psi(\bm{x}^{*}))\right)_{k\geq 0}
is almost surely bounded.

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle S_{k-1}(F_{k}-\Psi(\bm{x}^{*}))

\displaystyle=S_{k-1}(\hat{\Psi}_{k}-\Psi(\bm{x}^{*}))+S^{2}_{k-1}(h(\bm{w}^{k})-h(\bm{x}^{*}))

\displaystyle\geq S_{k-1}(\Psi(\bm{w}^{k})-\Psi(\bm{x}^{*}))+S^{2}_{k-1}(h(\bm{w}^{k})-h(\bm{x}^{*}))

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:LBw}}{\geq}-\delta S_{k-1}\sqrt{h(\bm{w}^{k})-h(\bm{x}^{*})}+S^{2}_{k-1}(h(\bm{w}^{k})-h(\bm{x}^{*}))

\displaystyle=\frac{S^{2}_{k-1}}{2}(h(\bm{w}^{k})-h(\bm{x}^{*}))+\left(\frac{S^{2}_{k-1}}{2}(h(\bm{w}^{k})-h(\bm{x}^{*}))-\delta S_{k-1}\sqrt{h(\bm{w}^{k})-h(\bm{x}^{*})}\right)

The convex function
t\mapsto\frac{S^{2}}{2}t^{2}-\delta St
attains the global minimum at the value
-\frac{\delta^{2}}{2}
. Hence,

S_{k-1}(F_{k}-\Psi(\bm{x}^{*}))\geq\frac{S^{2}_{k-1}}{2}(h(\bm{w}^{k})-h(\bm{x}^{*}))-\frac{\delta^{2}}{2}\geq-\frac{\delta^{2}}{2}.

(31)

The last inequality uses the fact that
\bm{x}^{\ast}\in\mathcal{X}
, so that
h(\bm{w}^{k})\geq h(\bm{x}^{\ast})
. This completes the proof.
∎

We are now in the position to give the proof of the first main result of this paper.

Theorem 6
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
and
2
apply. Consider the sequence
(\bm{z}^{k},\bm{w}^{k},\bm{x}^{k})_{k\geq 0}
generated by Algorithm
1
with
\sigma_{k}\equiv\sigma
and
\tau_{k}\equiv 1
for all
k\geq 1
. Then, the following holds:

(i)

h(\bm{w}^{k})-h(\bm{x}^{*})=O(1/k^{2})
a.s.

(ii)

(\bm{x}^{k})_{k}
and
(\bm{w}^{k})_{k}
converge a.s. to a random variable with values in
\mathcal{X}^{\ast}
.

(iii)

\lvert\Psi(\bm{w}^{k})-\Psi(\bm{x}^{*})\rvert=O(1/k)
a.s.

Proof.

(i) From Lemma
5
, it follows that the process
\mathcal{E}_{k}:=V_{k}(\bm{x}^{*})+\frac{\delta^{2}}{2}
is non-negative. Eq. (
27
) shows that
\mathcal{E}_{k}
satisfies the recursion

\mathbb{E}_{k}[\mathcal{E}_{k+1}]\leq\mathcal{E}_{k}-\sigma^{2}(h(\bm{x}^{k})-h(\bm{x}^{*}))-\frac{1}{2}\|\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k}\|^{2}_{\sigma(\bm{P}\bm{B}-(\sigma\Xi+\Lambda))}.

(32)

Lemma
10
implies that
(\mathcal{E}_{k})_{k\geq 0}
converges a.s. to a limiting random variable
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}\in[0,\infty)
and

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left[\sigma^{2}(h(\bm{x}^{k})-h(\bm{x}^{*}))+\frac{1}{2}\|\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k}\|^{2}_{\sigma(\bm{P}\bm{B}-(\sigma\Xi+\Lambda))}\right]<\infty\quad\text{a.s.}

Since
h(\bm{x}^{k})-h(\bm{x}^{*})\geq 0
, it follows
\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{2}\|\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k}\|^{2}_{\sigma(\bm{P}\bm{B}-(\sigma\Xi+\Lambda))}=0
. Consequently, the sequence
\left(\frac{1}{2}\|\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k}\|^{2}_{\sigma(\bm{P}\bm{B}-(\sigma\Xi+\Lambda))}\right)_{k\geq 0}
is bounded. By definition of the energy function
V_{k}(\bm{x}^{*})
, the sequence
(\bm{x}^{k})_{k\geq 0}
is bounded and (
31
) yields

h(\bm{w}^{k})-h(\bm{x}^{*})\geq-\frac{\delta^{2}}{S^{2}_{k-1}}.
Hence,
h(\bm{w}^{k})-h(\bm{x}^{*})=O(1/k^{2})
a.s. Moreover, it is easy to see that
\lim_{k\to\infty}\sum_{t=0}^{k}\gamma_{i}^{k,t}=1
and
\lim_{k\to\infty}\gamma_{i}^{k,t}=0
for all
i\in[d]
. Hence, by Lemma
2
and the Silverman-Toeplitz theorem, the sequence
(\bm{w}^{k})_{k}
converges to the same limit point as
(\bm{x}^{k})_{k}
, a.s.

By definition of the energy function
V_{k}
, the sequence
(\bm{x}^{k})_{k\geq 0}
is a.s. bounded. We show next that all accumulation bounds are contained in the set of solutions
\mathcal{X}^{\ast}
of (
P
).

Let
\Omega_{1}
be a set of probability 1 on which the subsequence
(\bm{x}^{k_{j}}(\omega))_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to a limiting random variable
\bar{\bm{x}}(\omega)
for all
\omega\in\Omega_{1}
. It follows from
\lim_{j\to\infty}\|\hat{\bm{x}}^{k_{j}}(\omega)-\bm{x}^{k_{j}}(\omega)\|=0
on
\Omega_{1}
that
(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k_{j}})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
\bar{\bm{x}}
as well on
\Omega_{1}
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle h(\bm{z}^{k})-h(\bm{x}^{\ast})

\displaystyle=\theta_{k}[h(\bm{w}^{k})-h(\bm{x}^{\ast})]+(1-\theta_{k})[h(\bm{x}^{k})-h(\bm{x}^{\ast})]

\displaystyle-\frac{\theta_{k}(1-\theta_{k})}{2}\|\bm{A}(\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{w}^{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=O(k^{-2}),

and for all
\bm{x}^{\ast}\in\mathcal{X}
,

\displaystyle\langle{\nabla h(\bm{z}^{k})},{\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{\ast}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle{\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{A}(\bm{z}^{k}-\bm{x}^{\ast})},{\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{\ast}}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\|\bm{A}(\bm{z}^{k}-\bm{x}^{\ast})\|\cdot\|\bm{A}(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{\ast})\|

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{h(\bm{z}^{k})-h(\bm{x}^{\ast})}\cdot\sqrt{h(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1})-h(\bm{x}^{\ast})}=o(k^{-1})

Hence,
\lim_{k\to\infty}S_{k}\langle{\nabla h(\bm{z}^{k})},{\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{\ast}}\rangle=0
for all
\bm{x}^{\ast}\in\mathcal{X}
a.s. Using Lemma
1
at the point
\bm{x}^{\ast}\in\mathcal{X}^{\ast}\subset\mathcal{X}
shows that

\displaystyle R(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k_{j+1}})+\langle{\nabla\Phi(\bm{x}^{k_{j}})},{\hat{\bm{x}}^{k_{j+1}}-\bm{x}^{k_{j}}}\rangle+S_{k}\langle{\nabla h(\bm{z}^{k_{j}})},{\hat{\bm{x}}^{k_{j+1}}-\bm{z}^{k_{j}}}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\|\hat{\bm{x}}^{k_{j+1}}-\bm{x}^{k_{j}}\|^{2}_{\bm{Q}}

\displaystyle\leq R(\bm{x}^{\ast})+\langle{\nabla\Phi(\bm{x}^{k_{j}})},{\bm{x}^{\ast}-\bm{x}^{k_{j}}}\rangle+S_{k}\langle{\nabla h(\bm{z}^{k_{j}})},{\bm{x}^{\ast}-\bm{z}^{k_{j}}}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\|\hat{\bm{x}}^{\ast}-\bm{x}^{k_{j}}\|^{2}_{\bm{Q}}-\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{x}^{\ast}-\hat{\bm{x}}^{k_{j+1}}\|_{\bm{Q}}.

Let

, one arrives that the inequality

R(\bar{\bm{x}})\leq R(\bm{x}^{\ast})+\langle{\nabla\Phi(\bar{\bm{x}})},{\bm{x}^{\ast}-\bar{\bm{x}}}\rangle.

Since

is an element of
\mathcal{X}
. Convexity of

\Phi(\bm{x}^{\ast})\geq\Phi(\bar{\bm{x}})+\langle{\nabla\Phi(\bar{\bm{x}})},{\bm{x}^{\ast}-\bar{\bm{x}}}\rangle

and
\bar{\bm{x}}\in\mathcal{X}
. Therefore,

.

(ii) We next show that cluster points are unique and thereby demonstrate almost sure convergence of the last iterate. We argue by contradiction. Suppose there are converging subsequences
(\bm{x}^{k})_{k\in\mathcal{K}_{1}}
and
(\bm{x}^{k})_{k\in\mathcal{K}_{2}}
with limit points
\bm{x}^{\prime}
and

. Following the above argument, we see that

a.s. The point

chosen in the previous argument was arbitrary, so we can replace it by
\bm{x}^{\prime}

converges almost surely, we see

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}V_{k}(\bm{x}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty,k\in\mathcal{K}_{1}}V_{k}(\bm{x}^{\prime})=\lim_{k\to\infty,k\in\mathcal{K}_{1}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{x}^{\prime}\|^{2}_{\bm{W}}+a_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty,k\in\mathcal{K}_{1}}a_{k}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}V_{k}(\bm{x}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty,k\in\mathcal{K}_{2}}V_{k}(\bm{x}^{\prime})=\lim_{k\to\infty,k\in\mathcal{K}_{2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{x}^{\prime}\|^{2}_{\bm{W}}+a_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\|\bm{x}^{\prime\prime}-\bm{x}^{\prime}\|_{\bm{W}}+\lim_{k\to\infty,k\in\mathcal{K}_{2}}a_{k}.

(iii) Taking expectations on both sides of (

4.3
The strongly convex case

In this section we study the performance of Algorithm
1
in the strongly convex regime.

Assumption 3
.

\Upsilon\succ 0
.

The main challenge we need to overcome is to design step size sequences which obey the matrix inequalities (
23
) and (
24
). Let us focus on (
23

\bm{B}=\bm{P}^{-2}\Upsilon
and
\bm{M}^{1/2}:=\bm{P}^{-1/2}\Upsilon^{1/2}

\bm{M}=\bm{P}^{-1}\Upsilon

\bm{P}^{-1}\Upsilon\succeq\tau_{k}\Lambda+\sigma_{k}\tau_{k}\bm{\Xi}\iff\frac{1}{\tau_{k}}\bm{I}\succeq\bm{M}^{-1/2}\Lambda\bm{M}^{-1/2}+\sigma_{k}\bm{M}^{-1/2}\bm{\Xi}\bm{M}^{-1/2}.

(33)

It is easy to see that
\bm{\Xi}
is symmetric. Furthermore, for
\bm{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\{0\}
, we see
\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{\Xi}\bm{u}=\mathbb{E}[\|\bm{A}\bm{t}\|^{2}]\geq 0
, where
\bm{t}:=\bm{P}\bm{E}_{k}\bm{u}
is a random vector in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
with mean
\bm{u}
. It follows
\bm{\Xi}\in\mathbb{S}^{m}_{+}
. This suggests to relate the step size parameters to the spectra of the involved matrices. The rest of this section assumes the following parametric model on the coupling between
\sigma_{k}
and
\tau_{k}
.

Assumption 4
.

The sequence
(\tau_{k})_{k}
is non-increasing and positive. They are related by the coupling equation

\sigma_{k}\tau_{k}=\alpha-\beta\tau_{k}\qquad\forall k\geq 0,

(34)

where
\alpha
and
\beta
are positive numbers.

We note that this coupling relation automatically means that
\sigma_{k}=\alpha/\tau_{k}-\beta
is non-decreasing. A sufficient condition to satisfy eq. (

\frac{1}{\tau_{k}}\geq\lambda_{\max}(\bm{M}^{-1/2}\Lambda\bm{M}^{-1/2})+\sigma_{k}\lambda_{\max}(\bm{M}^{-1/2}\Xi\bm{M}^{-1/2}).

Using the model (
34
), this boils down to

\frac{1}{\tau_{k}}(1-\alpha\lambda_{\max}(\bm{M}^{-1/2}\bm{\Xi}\bm{M}^{-1/2}))\geq\lambda_{\max}(\bm{M}^{-1/2}\Lambda\bm{M}^{-1/2})-\beta\lambda_{\max}(\bm{M}^{-1/2}\bm{\Xi}\bm{M}^{-1/2}).

Choosing

\alpha:=\lambda_{\max}(\bm{M}^{-1/2}\bm{\Xi}\bm{M}^{-1/2})^{-1},

(35)

we get

\beta\geq\frac{\lambda_{\max}(\bm{M}^{-1/2}\Lambda\bm{M}^{-1/2})}{\lambda_{\max}(\bm{M}^{-1/2}\bm{\Xi}\bm{M}^{-1/2})}=\alpha\lambda_{\max}(\bm{M}^{-1/2}\Lambda\bm{M}^{-1/2}).

We make the choice

\beta=\alpha\lambda_{\max}(\bm{M}^{-1/2}(\Lambda+\Upsilon)\bm{M}^{-1/2}),

so that the coupling equation (

23

Remark 3
.

To get some intuition behind the derived conditions for
\alpha
and
\beta

factorises to
\bm{\Xi}=\blkdiag(\frac{1}{\pi_{1}}\bm{A}_{1}^{\top}\bm{A}_{1},\ldots,\frac{1}{\pi_{d}}\bm{A}_{d}^{\top}\bm{A}_{d})

\displaystyle\alpha=\left(\max_{i\in[d]}\frac{1}{\mu_{i}\pi_{i}^{2}}\|\bm{A}_{i}\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{-1},\text{and }

\displaystyle\kappa:=\frac{\beta}{\alpha}=\max_{i\in[d]}\frac{L_{i}+\mu_{i}}{\pi_{i}\mu_{i}}.

Let us fix these identified values for

\beta

6
Conclusion

In this paper we developed a novel random block-coordinate descent method for ill-posed convex non-smooth optimisation problems. Our scheme gives optimal iteration complexity guarantees in terms of the last iterate of the sequence generated by the algorithm. As such, we directly generalise results from
Luke and Malitsky 2018
to a fully distributed optimisation setting. Motivated by the need to achieve a distributed optimisation of the power system, we follow a data-driven approach leading to a potentially inconsistent AC-OPF formulation. We show that our Algorithmic scheme is immediately applicable to such a challenging and important setting, and achieves distributed control of the power grid via distributed locational marginal prices as price signals to independent agents (i.e. aggregators). Future work should consist in extending the method to non-convex optimisation problems, so that exact formulation of the power flow constraints can be implemented. We also plan to conduct extensions of this work where the electric network is exposed to stochastic uncertainty.

\mathbb{E}_{k}[X]=\bm{A}(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{w}^{k})
. Furthermore,

X-\mathbb{E}_{k}[X]=\bm{A}\left((\bm{I}-\bm{P})\bm{x}^{k}+\bm{P}\bm{x}^{k+1}-\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}\right)=\bm{A}(\bm{x}^{k}+\bm{P}(\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k})-\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}).

(48)

Using (
45
), we get

\bm{P}\bm{x}^{k+1}-\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}=(\bm{P}\bm{E}_{k}-\bm{I})\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}+\bm{P}(\bm{I}-\bm{E}_{k})\bm{x}^{k},

which implies

\bm{A}(\bm{x}^{k}+\bm{P}(\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k})-\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1})=X-\mathbb{E}_{k}[X]=\bm{A}(\bm{P}\bm{E}_{k}-\bm{I})(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k}).

(49)

Collecting all therms above, one obtains

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\|\bm{A}(\bm{x}^{k}+\bm{P}(\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k})-\bm{w}^{k})\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{A}(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{w}^{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\|\bm{A}(\bm{P}\bm{E}_{k}-\bm{I})(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k})\|^{2}\right].

∎

The next Lemma provides a compact expression for the correction term in eq. (
47
).

Lemma 12
.

Let
\bm{\Xi}=\mathbb{E}[\bm{E}_{k}\bm{P}\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{P}\bm{E}_{k}]
, which is a symmetric time-independent matrix, thanks to iid sampling. We have

\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\|\bm{A}(\bm{P}\bm{E}_{k}-\bm{I})(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k})\|^{2}\right]=\|\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k}\|^{2}_{\Xi-\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{A}}.

(50)

Proof.

By definition of the sampling procedure, we have
\mathbb{E}_{k}[\bm{P}\bm{E}_{k}]=\bm{I}_{m}
. Some simple algebra shows then that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\|\bm{A}(\bm{P}\bm{E}_{k}-\bm{I})(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k})\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k})^{\top}\bm{M}_{k}(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k})\right],

where
\bm{M}_{k}:=\bm{E}_{k}\bm{P}\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{P}\bm{E}_{k}-\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{P}\bm{E}_{k}-\bm{E}_{k}\bm{P}\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{A}+\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{A}
is a symmetric matrix, satisfying
\mathbb{E}_{k}\bm{M}_{k}=\bm{\Xi}-\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{A}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k})^{\top}\bm{M}_{k}(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{k}\left[\tr\left(\bm{M}_{k}(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k})(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k})^{\top}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\tr\left(\mathbb{E}_{k}[\bm{M}_{k}](\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k})(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k})^{\top}\right)

\displaystyle=\tr\left((\Xi-\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{A})(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k})(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k})^{\top}\right)

\displaystyle=\|\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k}\|_{\bm{\Xi}-\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{A}}.

∎

Remark 4
.

The matrix
\bm{\Xi}
can be given a simple expression in terms of the probability matrix
\bm{\Pi}
. A direct computation shows that
\bm{\Xi}
is a block-symmetric matrix with
d^{2}
blocks
\bm{\Xi}[i,j],1\leq i,j\leq d,
each block having the form

\bm{\Xi}[i,j]=\frac{(\bm{\Pi})_{ij}}{\pi_{i}\pi_{j}}\bm{A}^{\top}_{i}\bm{A}_{j}^{\top}\text{ if }i\neq j,\text{ and }\bm{\Xi}[i,i]=\frac{1}{\pi_{i}}\bm{A}^{\top}_{i}\bm{A}_{i}\quad\forall i\in[d].

In special instances this matrix can be simplified even further:

1.

If
\mathbb{P}(\lvert\mathcal{I}\rvert=1)=1
the random sampling is a single coordinate activation mechanism. In this case the associated matrix
\bm{\Pi}
is diagonal with entries
\pi_{i}
. It follows that
\bm{\Xi}[i,i]=\frac{1}{\pi_{i}}\bm{A}_{i}^{\top}\bm{A}_{i}
for all
i\in[d]
and
\bm{\Xi}[i,j]=0
for
i\neq j
.

2.

We say that the matrix
\bm{A}
has
orthogonal design
if
\bm{A}_{i}^{\top}\bm{A}_{j}
is the zero matrix for
i\neq j
. Also in this case the matrix
\bm{\Xi}
is block diagonal, with the same coordinate expression as above.

7.3
Properties of the penalty function
h

We collect some important identities involving the penalty function
h
in this subsection. From the definition of the iterate
\bm{z}^{k}
, we see

\begin{split}\nabla h(\bm{z}^{k})&=\bm{A}^{\top}(\bm{A}\bm{z}^{k}-\bm{b})=\frac{1}{S_{k}}\left(S_{k-1}\bm{A}^{\top}(\bm{A}\bm{w}^{k}-\bm{b})+\sigma_{k}\bm{A}^{\top}(\bm{A}\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{b})\right)\\
&=(1-\theta_{k})\nabla h(\bm{w}^{k})+\theta_{k}\nabla h(\bm{x}^{k}).\end{split}

(51)

Furthermore, we note that the definition of the iterate
\bm{w}^{k+1}
implies

\begin{split}h(\bm{w}^{k+1})&=h\left((1-\theta_{k})\bm{w}^{k}+\theta_{k}(\bm{x}^{k}+\bm{P}(\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k}))\right)\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:hstrongconvex}}{=}(1-\theta_{k})h(\bm{w}^{k})+\theta_{k}h(\bm{x}^{k}+\bm{P}(\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k}))\\
&-\frac{\theta_{k}(1-\theta_{k})}{2}\|\bm{A}(\bm{x}^{k}+\bm{P}(\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k})-\bm{w}^{k})\|^{2}.\end{split}

(52)

Lemma 13
.

We have

\mathbb{E}_{k}[h(\bm{x}^{k}+\bm{P}(\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k}))]=h(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1})+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{k}[\|\bm{A}(\bm{x}^{k}+\bm{P}(\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k}))-\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}\|^{2}].

(53)

Proof.

The Pythagorean identity gives

\displaystyle h(\bm{x}^{k}+\bm{P}(\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k}))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|(\bm{A}\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{b})+\bm{A}(\bm{x}^{k}+\bm{P}(\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k})-\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=h(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1})+\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{A}(\bm{x}^{k}+\bm{P}(\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k})-\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1})\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\langle{\bm{A}\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1}-\bm{b}},{\bm{A}(\bm{x}^{k}+\bm{P}(\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k})-\hat{\bm{x}}^{k+1})}\rangle.

Taking conditional expectations on both sides, using eq. (
46
), establishes the claimed identity.
∎

8
Step size policy for the accelerated algorithm

Assume that parameter coupling equation (
34
) holds, where

\alpha=\lambda_{\max}(\bm{M}^{-1/2}\Xi\bm{M}^{-1/2})^{-1}\text{ and }\beta=\alpha\lambda_{\max}(\bm{M}^{-1/2}(\Lambda+\Upsilon)\bm{M}^{-1/2}).

Set
\kappa=\frac{\beta}{\alpha}
. With the choice
\bm{B}=\bm{P}^{-2}\Upsilon
, the matrix inequality (
24
) reads as

[(\alpha-\beta\tau_{k})(\bm{I}+\tau_{k}\bm{P})-\beta\tau^{2}_{k}(\bm{I}-\bm{P})]\tau_{k+1}^{2}+\tau_{k}^{2}[\beta\bm{I}+\alpha(\bm{I}-\bm{P})]\tau_{k+1}-\alpha\tau_{k}^{2}\bm{I}\succeq 0.

Exploiting the block structure of the involved matrices, we can equivalently write this condition as a system of quadratic inequalities

\tau^{2}_{k+1}\left[(\alpha-\beta\tau_{k})(\pi_{i}+\tau_{k})+\beta(1-\pi_{i})\tau^{2}_{k}\right]-\tau_{k+1}\tau^{2}_{k}[\alpha(1-\pi_{i})-\beta\pi_{i}]-\tau^{2}_{k}\alpha\pi_{i}\geq 0,

(54)

holding for all
i\in[d]
simultaneously. This defines a quadratic inequality in
\tau_{k+1}
of the form
c_{i,1}(\tau_{k})\tau^{2}_{k+1}+c_{i,2}(\tau_{k})\tau_{k+1}-c_{i,3}(\tau_{k})\geq 0
, with coefficients

\displaystyle c_{i,1}(y):=(\alpha-\beta y)(\pi_{i}+y)+\beta(1-\pi_{i})y^{2}=\alpha\pi_{i}\left[1-\kappa y^{2}-y(\kappa-1/\pi_{i})\right],

\displaystyle c_{i,2}(y):=y^{2}[\beta\pi_{i}-\alpha(1-\pi_{i})]=\alpha\pi_{i}y^{2}(\kappa-1/\pi_{i}+1),

\displaystyle c_{i,3}(y):=y^{2}\alpha\pi_{i}

The structure of the coefficients shows that we can eliminate the parameters
\alpha
and
\pi_{i}
, and just continue with the coefficients

\displaystyle a_{i,1}(y):=1-\kappa y^{2}-y(\kappa-1/\pi_{i}),\;a_{i,2}(y):=y^{2}(\kappa-1/\pi_{i}+1),\;a_{i,3}(y):=y^{2}.

Define the function
F:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
by

F(x,y):=\max_{i\in[d]}\{a_{i,1}(y)x^{2}+a_{i,2}(y)x-a_{i,3}(y)\}\equiv\max_{i\in[d]}F_{i}(x,y).

This gives us an implicit relation for the step size policy (
40
) by
F(\tau_{k+1},\tau_{k})=0
from which we take the positive solution, if it exists. To analyse this implicit relation, we proceed as follows.

Lemma 14
.

The equation
F(x,0)=0
has the unique solution
x=0
. Furthermore, the equation
F(x,\alpha/\beta)=0
has the unique positive solution
x=\frac{\alpha}{\beta}=\frac{1}{\kappa}
.

Proof.

This is a direct numerical computation.
∎

By definition we have
\kappa=\max_{i\in[d]}\frac{L_{i}+\mu_{i}}{\mu_{i}\pi_{i}}\geq\frac{1}{\pi_{i}}>1
for all
i\in[d]
as well. Consequently,

\delta_{i}:=\kappa-\frac{1}{\pi_{i}}\geq 0\qquad\forall i\in[d],

and we obtain the concise representation
a_{i,1}(y)=1-\kappa y^{2}-y\delta_{i}
, as well as,
a_{i,2}(y)=y^{2}(\delta+1)
. It is clear that
a_{i,1}(y)>0
for
0<y<\frac{-\delta_{i}+\sqrt{\delta_{i}^{2}+4\kappa}}{2\kappa}
. Since
\pi_{i}\in(0,1)
, it can be readily verified that restricting
y\in(0,1/\kappa)
is a sufficient condition for
a_{i,1}(y)>0
. Hence, if
\tau_{k}\in(0,1/\kappa)
, then the characterisation of the step size reduces to finding the root of the polynomial equation

\tau^{2}_{k+1}+\frac{a_{2}(\tau_{k})}{a_{1}(\tau_{k})}\tau_{k+1}-\frac{a_{3}(\tau_{k})}{a_{1}(\tau_{k})}=0.

Since
\frac{a_{2}(y)}{a_{1}(y)}=y^{2}\frac{\delta+1}{1-\kappa y^{2}-y\delta}
, and
\frac{a_{3}(y)}{a_{1}(y)}=y^{2}\frac{1}{1-\kappa y^{2}-\delta y}
, we set

b_{k}:=\frac{\delta+1}{1-\tau_{k}^{2}\kappa-\delta\tau_{k}}\text{ provided that }\tau_{k}\in(0,1/\kappa),

so that our polynomial becomes

\tau^{2}_{k+1}+\tau^{2}_{k}b_{k}\tau_{k+1}-\tau^{2}_{k}\frac{b_{k}}{\delta+1}=0.

(55)

This has the unique positive solution

\tau_{k+1}=\frac{\tau_{k}\left[-b_{k}\tau_{k}+\sqrt{b_{k}^{2}\tau^{2}_{k}+4b_{k}/(\delta+1)}\right]}{2}=\frac{2}{\delta+1}\frac{1}{1+\sqrt{1+\frac{4}{b_{k}\tau^{2}_{k}(1+\delta)}}}.

(56)

Setting
d_{k}:=1-\delta_{k}\tau_{k}-\kappa\tau^{2}_{k}
, and
s_{k}:=(1+\delta)\tau_{k}
, this recursion can be equivalently stated as

s_{k+1}=\frac{2s_{k}}{s_{k}+\sqrt{s^{2}_{k}+4d_{k}}}.

To wit, we note that

\displaystyle s_{k+1}=\frac{2}{1+\sqrt{1+\frac{4d_{k}}{\tau^{2}_{k}(1+\delta)^{2}}}}=\frac{2}{1+\sqrt{1+\frac{4d_{k}}{s^{2}_{k}}}}=\frac{2s_{k}}{s_{k}+\sqrt{s^{2}_{k}+4d_{k}}}.

Hence,
\frac{s_{k+1}}{s_{k}}=\frac{2}{1+\sqrt{1+\frac{4d_{k}}{s^{2}_{k}}}}\leq 1
using that
d_{k}>0
. It follows by induction that
s_{k}\leq s_{0}=(1+\delta)\tau_{0}
. We deduce that the sequence
(\tau_{k})_{k}
is monotonically decreasing and non-negative. Therefore,
\lim_{k\to\infty}\tau_{k}=\tau_{\infty}\geq 0
exists. We argue that
\tau_{\infty}=0
. Suppose that
\tau_{\infty}\geq t>0
. Then
s_{\infty}=\lim_{k\to\infty}s_{k}=(1+\delta)\tau_{\infty}
, and
\lim_{k\to\infty}(s_{k+1}-s_{k})=0
. But then

s_{\infty}=\frac{2s_{\infty}}{s_{\infty}+\sqrt{s^{2}_{\infty}+4d_{\infty}}}<s_{\infty},

a contradiction! We conclude
\tau_{\infty}=0
. If follows that
\lim_{k\to\infty}b_{k}=1+\delta
, and
\lim_{k\to\infty}d_{k}=1
. Moreover,
d_{k}\uparrow 1
. Hence,

s_{k+1}=\frac{2s_{k}}{s_{k}+\sqrt{s^{2}_{k}+4d_{k}}}\geq\frac{2s_{k}}{s_{k}+\sqrt{s^{2}_{k}+4}}=\frac{2}{1+\sqrt{1+4/s_{k}^{2}}},

which is equivalent to
\frac{1-s_{k+1}}{s^{2}_{k+1}}\leq\frac{1}{s^{2}_{k}}
. It follows from
Tseng 2008
that
s_{k}\geq\frac{2s_{0}}{ks_{0}+2}
for all
k\geq 0
, which implies

\tau_{k}\geq\frac{2\tau_{0}}{(1+\delta)\tau_{0}k+2},\text{ provided }\tau_{0}\in(0,1/\kappa).

This implies via our parameter coupling
\sigma_{k}=\alpha/\tau_{k}-\beta
for
k\geq 1
, that

\displaystyle S_{k}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\alpha}{\tau_{i}}-k\beta\leq 1+\frac{\alpha(1+\delta)}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{k}i+k\left(\frac{\alpha}{\tau_{0}}-\beta\right)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{\alpha(1+\delta)}{4}k(k+1)+k\sigma_{0}.

Hence,
S_{k}=O(k^{2})
.

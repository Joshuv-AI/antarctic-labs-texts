On the Phase Retrievable Sequences
F. Javadi 1 and M. J. Mehdipour 1

Abstract
In this paper, we study phase retrievable sequences and give a characterization of phase retrievability of a sequence of bounded linear operators on a Hilbert space H H ; in particular, for H = ℓ 2 d ​ ( ℂ ) H=\ell_{2}^{d}(\mathbb{C}) . We also give several approaches for constructing phase retrievable sequences. Then, we investigate the property of phase retrieval for g g -frames and frames.

1
Introduction

Throughout this paper,
H
is a complex Hilbert space and

\mathcal{L}(H)
is the space of all bounded linear operators on
H
.
A sequence
\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}

in
\mathcal{L}(H)
is called
phase retrieval
if for every

x,y\in H
satisfying

\displaystyle\|\Lambda_{k}(x)\|=\|\Lambda_{k}(y)\|

for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, then

\displaystyle x=\alpha y~~~\text{with}~~~|\alpha|=1.

It is routine to check that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\{\Lambda^{n}_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is also phase retrieval.
Also,
if
\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a phase retrievable sequence of
\Lambda_{k}
normal operators on
H
,
then
\{\Lambda^{*}_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is phase retrieval.
Note that if
\Lambda_{k}(x)=0
, then

\displaystyle\|\Lambda_{k}(x)\|=\|\Lambda_{k}(0)\|.

So phase retrievability of

\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
implies that
x=0
.
Thus
\Lambda_{k}
is injective for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

A sequence
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
in
H
is called
phase retrieval
, if for every
x,y\in H
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, from

|\langle x,x_{k}\rangle|=|\langle y,x_{k}\rangle|

implies that there exists
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\alpha|=1
and

x=\alpha y.

We define the equivalence relation “
\sim
” on
H\times H
as follows:

x\sim y
if and only if there exists
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\alpha|=1
such that
x=\alpha y
.
Put
H_{r}:=H/\sim
. It is easy to see that
H_{r}=H/\mathbb{T}
, where
\mathbb{T}
is the complex unit circle. Balan et al. [3] introduced the function
\mathbb{M}:H_{r}\rightarrow\ell^{2}(\mathbb{I})
which is defined by

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}(\hat{x})=\{|\langle x,x_{k}\rangle|\}_{k\in\mathbb{I}},

where
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{I}}
is a frame for
H
and
\mathbb{I}
is a finite or countable subset of
\mathbb{Z}
. They gave conditions under which
\mathbb{M}
is injective. These results have applications in quantum mechanic; see [16]. Note that if
H
is a real Hilbert space, then
H_{r}=H/\{\pm 1\}
. In the case where,
\hbox{dim}(H)=M
, Cahill et al. [7] considered the function
\mathbb{A}:\mathbb{R}^{M}/\{\pm 1\}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}
defined by

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}(x)(n)=\|P_{n}(x)\|^{2},

where
P_{n}:\mathbb{R}^{M}\rightarrow W_{n}
is the orthogonal projection on a subspace
W_{n}
of
H
for
n=1,...,N
. They characterized the subspaces
\{W_{n}\}_{n=1}^{N}

for which
\{\|P_{n}(x)\|\}_{n=1}^{N}

are injective for all
x\in H
. They also used the phrase “
\{W_{n}\}_{n=1}^{N}

allows phase retrieval” instead of the phrase “
\mathbb{A}
is injective”.
Some authors continued these investigations [1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 12, 15]. For example, Han and Juste [11] studied phase-retrievable operator-valued frames and gave several characterizations for them. They found some relations between phase-retrievable operator-valued frames, projective group representation frames and representations of quantum channels. These results naturally leads us to study injectivity of the function
\mathbb{A}:H_{r}/\mathbb{T}\rightarrow\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
defined by

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}(x)(k)=\|\Lambda_{k}(x)\|^{2},

where
\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a sequence in
\mathcal{L}(H)
.

The outline of the paper is as follows.
In Section 2, we investigate phase retrievability of a sequence
\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
in
\mathcal{L}(H)
and provide some necessary and sufficient conditions under which
\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
to be phase retrieval. We also show that
\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is phase retrieval
(a phase retrievable g-frame) if and only if
\{\Lambda_{k}T\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is phase retrieval (a phase retrievable g-frame) when
T\in\mathcal{L}(H)
is injective (surjective).
Section 3 is devoted to the study of a phase retrievable frame
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
in
H
. We establish that if
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a phase retrievable frame, then
\{Tx_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a phase retrievable frame, where
T\in\mathcal{L}(H)
is injective. The converse remaind valid if
T
is injective and self-adjoint. In particular,
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a phase retrievable frame if and only if the canonical dual of
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is phase retrieval.

2
Characterization of phase retrievable sequences

We commence this section with the main result of this paper.

Theorem 2.1
.

A sequence
\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
in

{\cal L}(H)
is not phase retrieval if and only if there exist nonzero elements
x,y\in H

such that
x\not\in i\mathbb{R}y
and

\hbox{Re}\langle\Lambda_{k}(x),\Lambda_{k}(y)\rangle=0

for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

First, assume that

\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}

is not phase retrieval.
Then there exist
f,g\in H
such that for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}

with

|\alpha|=1

we have
f\not=\alpha g
and for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}

(2.1)

\displaystyle\langle\Lambda_{k}(f),\Lambda_{k}(f)\rangle=\langle\Lambda_{k}(g),\Lambda_{k}(g)\rangle.

Let

x=\frac{1}{2}(f+g)

and

y=\frac{1}{2}(f-g)
.
Then

f=x+y

and

g=x-y
.
It follows from (
2.1
) that for every

k\in\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle\hbox{Re}\langle\Lambda_{k}(x),\Lambda_{k}(y)\rangle=0.

Clearly,
x
and
y
are nonzero.
If
x=iay
for some
a\in\mathbb{R}
, then

f=(ia+1)y
and
g=(ia-1)y.

This shows that

\displaystyle\frac{f}{g}=\frac{ia+1}{ia-1}\,\,\,\,\,\hbox{and}\,\,\,\,\,\bigg|\frac{ia+1}{ia-1}\bigg|=1,

a contradiction.
Therefore,
x\not\in i\mathbb{R}y
.

Conversely, let

\hbox{Re}\langle\Lambda_{k}(x),\Lambda_{k}(y)\rangle=0

for some nonzero elements

x,y\in H

with
x\not\in i\mathbb{R}y
.
Then

\langle\Lambda_{k}(f),\Lambda_{k}(f)\rangle=\langle\Lambda_{k}(g),\Lambda_{k}(g)\rangle,

where

f=x+y
and
g=x-y
.
Suppose that

f=\alpha g
with
|\alpha|=1
.
Then

\|f\|=\|g\|
and
x=\beta y
with
\beta\not=0
.
Thus

|\beta+1|=|\beta-1|,

\displaystyle f=(\beta+1)y\quad\hbox{and}\quad g=(\beta-1)y.

If
H
is a real Hilbert space,
then
\beta

is real and hence
\beta=0
.
This is a contradiction.
If
H
is a complex Hilbert space, then
\beta\in\mathbb{C}
.
Thus

|\beta+1|=|\beta-1|

holds only if

\beta=ia

for some
a\in\mathbb{R}
,
which implies that
x\in i\mathbb{R}y
.
These contradictions lead to
\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is not phase retrieval.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a sequence in

{\cal L}(H)
and
\hbox{dim}\;H>1
.
If
\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}

have a nonzero common root,
then
\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is not phase retrieval.

Proof.

Let
x
be a nonzero element of
H
such that
\Lambda_{k}(x)=0
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Since

\hbox{dim}\;H>1
,
there exists a nonzero element
y\in H
with

y\not\in i\mathbb{R}x
.
The proof will be completed if we only note that
Re\langle\Lambda_{k}(x),\Lambda_{k}(y)\rangle=0

for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

Every phase retrievable sequence in
{\cal L}(H)

separates points of
H
.

Proof.

Let
\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a phase retrievable sequence.
If
x
is a nonzero element of
H
,
then by Theorem
2.1
there exists
k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}

such that
\Lambda_{k_{0}}(x)\neq 0
.
This implies that

\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}

separates points of
H
.
∎

Let us recall that

\ell_{2}^{d}(\mathbb{C})

is the set of all

(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d})

such that

x_{i}\in\mathbb{C}

and
\sum_{i=1}^{d}|x_{i}|^{2}<\infty.

For a sequence

\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}

in

{\cal L}(\ell_{2}^{d}(\mathbb{C}))
and
x\in\ell_{2}^{d}(\mathbb{C})
,
let

\displaystyle W_{x}:=\overline{span}\{Re(\Lambda_{k}(x))\oplus Im(\Lambda_{k}(x)):k\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

For every
x,y\in\ell_{2}^{d}(\mathbb{C})
we have

\displaystyle 2Re\langle\Lambda_{k}(y),\Lambda_{k}(x)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\Lambda_{k}(y),\Lambda_{k}(x)\rangle+\overline{\langle\Lambda_{k}(y),\Lambda_{k}(x)\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle Re(\Lambda_{k}(y))\oplus Im(\Lambda_{k}(y)),Re(\Lambda_{k}(x))\oplus Im(\Lambda_{k}(x))\rangle.

This implies that

\displaystyle Re\langle\Lambda_{k}(y),\Lambda_{k}(x)\rangle=0

if and only if

\displaystyle Re(\Lambda_{k}(y))\oplus Im(\Lambda_{k}(y))\in W_{x}^{\perp}

for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
;
or equivalently,

\displaystyle W_{y}\subseteq W_{x}^{\perp}.

If
z=ix
,
then

Re\langle\Lambda_{k}(z),\Lambda_{k}(x)\rangle=0
.
This shows that
W_{ix}
is a one dimensional subspace of
W_{x}^{\perp}
.
So

W_{x}^{\perp}
is non-empty and

dimW_{x}^{\perp}\geq 1
.
Note that
dimW_{x}^{\perp}>1
if and only if
W_{x}^{\perp}\setminus W_{ix}
is non-empty.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a sequence in
{\mathcal{L}}(\ell_{2}^{d}(\mathbb{C}))
.
Then
\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}

is not phase retrieval if and only if there exist nonzero elements

x,y\in\ell_{2}^{d}(\mathbb{C})

such that
y\not\in i\mathbb{R}x

and

W_{y}
is contained in
W_{x}^{\perp}.

Proof.

Suppose that

\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is not phase retrieval.
By Theorem
2.1
,
there are nonzero elements

x,y\in\ell^{2}_{d}(\mathbb{C})

such that
y\not\in i\mathbb{R}x

and

\displaystyle Re\langle\Lambda_{k}(y),\Lambda_{k}(x)\rangle=0.

This shows that
W_{y}\subseteq W_{x}^{\perp}.
A similar argument proves the converse.
∎

As an immediate consequence of
Corollary
2.2

we have the following result.

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}

be a sequence in
{\mathcal{L}}(\ell_{2}^{d}(\mathbb{C}))

and
d>1
.
If there exists a nonzero element

x\in\ell_{2}^{d}(\mathbb{C})

such that
W_{y}=\{0\}
.
Then

\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}

is not phase retrieval.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a sequence in
{\mathcal{L}}(\ell_{2}^{d}(\mathbb{C}))
.
If there exist nonzero elements

x,y\in\ell_{2}^{d}(\mathbb{C})

such that
W_{y}
is contained in

W_{x}^{\perp}\setminus W_{ix}
,
then
\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}

is not phase retrieval.
The converse holds if

\Lambda_{k_{0}}

is injective for some
k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
In this case,
dimW_{x}^{\perp}>1
.

Proof.

Let there exist
k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
with
\Lambda_{k_{0}}
be injective.
Suppose that
\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}

is not phase retrieval.
By Theorem
2.4
, there are nonzero elements
x,y\in\ell^{2}_{d}(\mathbb{C})

such that

y\not\in i\mathbb{R}x

and

W_{y}
is contained in
W_{x}^{\perp}.

Let

\displaystyle Re(\Lambda_{k_{0}}(y))\oplus Im(\Lambda_{k_{0}}(y))\in W_{ix}.

This shows that

\displaystyle Re(\Lambda_{k_{0}}(y))=-aIm(\Lambda_{k_{0}}(x))~~~\text{and}~~~Im(\Lambda_{k_{0}}(y))=aRe(\Lambda_{k_{0}}(x))

for some
a\in\mathbb{R}
.
So

\displaystyle\Lambda_{k_{0}}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Re(\Lambda_{k_{0}}(y))+iIm(\Lambda_{k_{0}}(y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-aIm(\Lambda_{k_{0}}(x))+iaRe(\Lambda_{k_{0}}(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle aRe(\Lambda_{k_{0}}(ix))+aIm(\Lambda_{k_{0}}(ix))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Lambda_{k_{0}}(iax).

By injectivity of
\Lambda_{k_{0}}
,
we have
y=iax
, a contradiction.
Thus

W_{y}\subset W^{\perp}_{x}\setminus W_{ix}.

Therefore,

W^{\perp}_{x}\setminus W_{ix}

is non-empty which implies that

dim{W_{x}^{\perp}}>1
.
The remain of proof follows from Theorem
2.4
.
∎

Let us recall that for every
x,y\in H
, the bounded operator
x\otimes y
on
H
is defined by

(x\otimes y)(z)=\langle z,y\rangle x

for all
z\in H
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n}
be a sequence in

{\cal L}(H)
.
If
\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n}

is phase retrieval, then

\sum_{k=1}^{n}(\Lambda_{k}^{*}\Lambda_{k}(x)\otimes\Lambda_{k}^{*}\Lambda_{k}(x))

is injective for all nonzero vector
x\in H
.

Proof.

Suppose that

\sum_{k=1}^{n}(\Lambda_{k}^{*}\Lambda_{k}(x)\otimes\Lambda_{k}^{*}\Lambda_{k}(x))

is not injective for some

x\not=0
.
Then there exists
y\not=0
such that

\displaystyle\langle\Lambda_{k}(y),\Lambda_{k}(x)\rangle=0.

It follows that

\displaystyle Re\langle\Lambda_{k}(y),\Lambda_{k}(x)\rangle=0

for all
k=1,\ldots,n
.
If
y=iax
for some
a\in\mathbb{R}
,
then
\langle\Lambda_{k}(y),\Lambda_{k}(x)\rangle=0
,
which shows that
\Lambda_{k}(x)=0
.
This contradicts Corollary
2.3
.
So
y\not\in i\mathbb{R}x
.
Therefore,
\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is
not phase retrieval, a contradiction.
∎

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a sequence in
\mathcal{L}(H)
and let
T\in\mathcal{L}(H)
be injective.
Then
\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is phase retrieval if and only if
\{\Lambda_{k}T\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is phase retrieval.

Proof.

Suppose that there exist nonzero elements
x,y\in H
such that
y\not\in i\mathbb{R}x
and
Re\langle\Lambda_{k}T(x),\Lambda_{k}T(y)\rangle=0
. Since
T
is injective,
Ty\not\in i\mathbb{R}Tx
. Thus if
\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is phase retrieval, then
\{\Lambda_{k}T\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is phase retrieval.

Conversely, if for every
x,y\in H
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\|\Lambda_{k}(x)\|=\|\Lambda_{k}(y)\|
,
then there exists

\tilde{T}\in\mathcal{L}(H)

such that

\|\Lambda_{k}T(\tilde{T}x)\|=\|\Lambda_{k}T(\tilde{T}y)\|
. Since

\{\Lambda_{k}T\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is phase retrieval
and
T
is injective,

x=\alpha y
with
|\alpha|=1
. Therefore,
\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is phase retrieval.
∎

In the following, let
H
denote a separable infinite dimensional Hilbert space and
\{H_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a sequence of subspaces of
H
.
Let
\Lambda_{k}
be a linear operator from
H
into
H_{k}
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then the sequence
\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is said to be a

g
-frame for
H
with respect to

\{H_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
(briefly,
g
-frame)
if there exist two constants
0<A\leq B
such that

\displaystyle A~\|x\|^{2}\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\Lambda_{k}(x)\|^{2}\leq B~\|x\|^{2}.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a sequence in
\mathcal{L}(H)
and let
T\in\mathcal{L}(H)
be surjective.
Then
\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a phase retrievable
g
-frame if and only if
\{\Lambda_{k}T\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a phase retrievable
g
-frame.

Proof.

Let
\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a
g
-frame for
H
.
Then there exists
A,B>0
such that for every
\in H
,

\displaystyle A\|x\|^{2}\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\Lambda_{k}(x)\|^{2}\leq B\|x\|^{2}.

If
T
is surjective,
then
\|x\|=\|\overline{T}Tx\|\leq\|\overline{T}\|\|Tx\|
for some
\overline{T}\in\mathcal{L}(H)
. So

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\Lambda_{k}T(x)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\Lambda_{k}(Tx)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle B\|Tx\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle B\|T\|^{2}\|x\|^{2}

and

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\Lambda_{k}T(x)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle A\|Tx\|^{2}\geq A\|\overline{T}\|^{-2}\|x\|^{2}

for all
x\in H
. Hence

\{\Lambda_{k}T\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a
g
-frame. Now, let for every
x,y\in H
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\|\Lambda_{k}(Tx)\|=\|\Lambda_{k}(Ty)\|
.
Since

\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is phase retrieval,
there exists
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
such that

|\alpha|=1

and

Tx=\alpha Ty
.
Surjectivity of
T
implies that
x=\alpha y
.
Therefore,
\{\Lambda_{k}T\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a phase retrievable
g
-frame.

To prove the converse, let
z\in H
. Then
Tz^{\prime}=z
for some
z^{\prime}\in H
. If
\{\Lambda_{k}T\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a
g
-frame, then there exist
A,B>0
such that

\displaystyle A\|z\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A\|Tz^{\prime}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\Lambda_{k}T(z^{\prime})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\Lambda_{k}(z)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle B\|z\|^{2}.

This shows that
\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a
g
-frame. Suppose that
\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}

is not phase retrieval. Thus there exists nonzero elements
x,y\in H
such that
y\not\in i\mathbb{R}x
and

\displaystyle Re\langle\Lambda_{k}(x),\Lambda_{k}(y)\rangle=0.

Choose
x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in H
with

\displaystyle Tx^{\prime}=x\quad\hbox{and}\quad Ty^{\prime}=y.

Note that

\displaystyle Re\langle\Lambda_{k}T(x^{\prime}),\Lambda_{k}T(y^{\prime})\rangle=0

and
Ty^{\prime}\not\in Tx^{\prime}
. Hence
\{\Lambda_{k}T\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is not phase retrieval.
∎

Let
\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a

-frame for
H
. The operator

defined by

\displaystyle S(x):=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}{\Lambda}_{k}^{*}\,{\Lambda}_{k}\,(x)

is called the

of

.
It has been proved that the operator

is bounded, positive and invertible.
For

-frame

, the sequence

is called the

of

; see [17, 18] for more details.

Corollary 2.10
.

Let
\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a
g
-frame for
H
.
Then the canonical dual of

is phase retrieval.

3
Phase retrievable frames

A sequence
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
of elements of a separable infinite dimensional Hilbert space
H
is called
frame
if there exist constants

0<A\leq B
such that

\displaystyle A\,\|x\|^{2}\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle x,x_{k}\rangle|^{2}\leq B\,\|x\|^{2}

for all
x\in H
. For extensive of frames see [9]; see also [10] for
K
-frames, see [8] for fusion frames and [13, 14, 17] for
K
-
g
-frames and
g
-frames. Let the linear operator

\Lambda_{k}:H\rightarrow H
defined by

\displaystyle\Lambda_{k}(x)=\langle x,x_{k}\rangle x_{k}.

One can easy prove that
\{\Lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a phase retrievable
g
-frame if and only if

\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a phase retrievable frame.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{L}(H)
be an injective operator.
If
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a phase retrievable frame for
H
, then

\{Tx_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a phase retrievable frame.
The converse holds when
T
is self-adjoint.

Proof.

Let
T
be injective. Then there exists
\overline{T}\in\mathcal{L}(H)
such that

\displaystyle T\overline{T}=I=\overline{T}^{*}T^{*}

on
H
.
So for every
x\in H
we have

\displaystyle\|x\|=\|T\overline{T}x\|\leq\|T\|\|\overline{T}x\|\quad\hbox{and}\quad\|x\|=\|\overline{T}^{*}T^{*}x\|\leq\|\overline{T}^{*}\|\|T^{*}x\|.

It follows that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\|T\|^{-1}\|x\|\leq\|\overline{T}x\|

and
\|\overline{T}^{*}\|^{-1}\|x\|\leq\|T^{*}x\|
.
Assume that
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a frame for
H
.
Then there exist
A,B>0
such that for every
x\in H

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle x,Tx_{k}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle T^{*}x,x_{k}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle A\|T^{*}x\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle A\|\overline{T}^{*}\|^{-2}\|x\|^{2}

and

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle x,Tx_{k}\rangle|^{2}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle T^{*}x,x_{k}\rangle|^{2}\leq B\|T^{*}\|^{2}\|x\|^{2}.

Hence
\{Tx_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a frame for
H
.
Let

|\langle x,Tx_{k}\rangle|=|\langle y,Tx_{k}\rangle|
for all
x,y\in H
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then

|\langle T^{*}x,x_{k}\rangle|=|\langle T^{*}y,x_{k}\rangle|
.
Now, since
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is phase-retrieval, there exists
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\alpha|=1
such that

\displaystyle T^{*}x=\alpha T^{*}y.

Then
\overline{T}^{*}T^{*}x=\alpha\overline{T}^{*}T^{*}y
. Therefore,
x=\alpha y
.
That is,
\{Tx_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a phase-retrieval frame.

Conversely, let
\{Tx_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a frame.
Then there exists
A,B>0
such that for every
x\in H

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle x,x_{k}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle T\overline{T}x,x_{k}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle\overline{T}x,T^{*}x_{k}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle\overline{T}x,Tx_{k}\rangle|^{2}.

From (
3.1
) we obtain

\displaystyle A\|T\|^{-2}\|x\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle A\|\overline{T}x\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle x,x_{k}\rangle|^{2}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle\overline{T}x,Tx_{k}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle B\|\overline{T}\|^{2}\|x\|^{2}.

So,
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a frame for
H
.
Now, let

|\langle x,x_{k}\rangle|=|\langle y,x_{k}\rangle|
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
x,y\in H
.
Since
T
is injective and self-adjoint, it follows that

\displaystyle|\langle\overline{T}x,Tx_{k}\rangle|=|\langle\overline{T}y,Tx_{k}\rangle|.

If
\{Tx_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is phase retrieval, then there exists
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\alpha|=1
and

\displaystyle\overline{T}x=\alpha\overline{T}y.

Consequently,

T\overline{T}x=\alpha T\overline{T}y
.
Thus
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a phase retrievable frame.
∎

As a consequence of Theorem
3.1
we give the following result.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a frame for
H
.
The canonical dual of
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is
phase retrieval if and only if
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is phase retrieval.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a phase retrievable frame for
H
with dual frame
\{y_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
.
If
\{y_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is an orthogonal sequence,
then
\{y_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a phase retrievable frame.

Proof.

Let

|\langle x,y_{k}\rangle|=|\langle y,y_{k}\rangle|
for all
x,y\in H
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Then

\displaystyle\langle x,y_{k}\rangle\overline{\langle x,y_{k}\rangle}=\langle y,y_{k}\rangle\overline{\langle y,y_{k}\rangle},

i.e.,

\displaystyle\langle x,y_{k}\rangle\langle y_{k},x\rangle=\langle y,y_{k}\rangle\langle y_{k},y\rangle.

Put
\alpha_{k}=\langle y_{k},x\rangle
and

\beta_{k}=\langle y_{k},y\rangle
. Then

\displaystyle\langle x,\overline{\alpha_{k}}\,y_{k}\rangle=\langle y,\overline{\beta_{k}}\,y_{k}\rangle.

Since
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
and
\{y_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}

are dual frame, we have

\displaystyle x=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle x,x_{j}\rangle y_{j}\,\,\,\,\,\hbox{and}\,\,\,\,\,\,y=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle y,x_{j}\rangle y_{j}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\bigg\langle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle x,x_{j}\rangle y_{j},\overline{\alpha_{k}}y_{k}\bigg\rangle=\bigg\langle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle y,x_{j}\rangle y_{j},\overline{\beta_{k}}y_{k}\bigg\rangle.

This together with the orthogonality of
\{y_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
shows that for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle\langle x,x_{k}\rangle\,\alpha_{k}\,\langle y_{k},y_{k}\rangle=\langle y,x_{k}\rangle\,\beta_{k}\,\langle y_{k},y_{k}\rangle.

Thus

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle x,x_{k}\rangle\langle y_{k},x\rangle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle y,x_{k}\rangle\langle y_{k},y\rangle.

From Lemma
6.3.2
in [9] we infer that
x=y
.
Therefore,
\{y_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a phase retrievable frame.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{L}(H)
be an injective self-adjoint operator and
\lambda>0
.
Let also
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
and
\{y_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
be sequences in
H
such that
for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}

(3.2)

\displaystyle|\langle x,x_{k}\pm y_{k}\rangle|=\lambda|\langle x,x_{k}\rangle|.

If either

\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
or
\{Tx_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}

is a phase retrievable frame,
then
\{x_{k}\pm y_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a phase retrievable frame.

Proof.

Let
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a frame for
H
with bounds
A
and
B
.
It is easy to see that

\{x_{k}\pm y_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}

is a frame for
H

with bounds
\lambda A
and
\lambda B
.
To complete the proof, let
x,y\in H
such that

|\langle x,x_{k}\pm y_{k}\rangle|=|\langle y,x_{k}\pm y_{k}\rangle|

for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then by (
3.2
)

\displaystyle\lambda|\langle x,x_{k}\rangle|=\lambda|\langle y,x_{k}\rangle|.

So if
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a phase retrievable frame for
H
,
then
\{x_{k}\pm y_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a phase retrievable frame for
H
.
In the case where,

\{Tx_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a phase retrievable frame,
the result is proved if we only note that by Theorem
3.1
,
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a phase retrievable frame for
H
.
∎

As an immediate consequence of Theorem
3.1
and Proposition
3.4
we have the following result.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a phase retrievable frame for
H
and
\{y_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a sequence in
H
.
Let
T\in\mathcal{L}(H)

be injective.
If there exists
\lambda>0
such that for every
k

\displaystyle|\langle x,T(x_{k}\pm y_{k})\rangle|=\lambda|\langle x,Tx_{k}\rangle|,

then
\{T(x_{k}\pm y_{k})\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}

is a phase retrievable frame for
H
.

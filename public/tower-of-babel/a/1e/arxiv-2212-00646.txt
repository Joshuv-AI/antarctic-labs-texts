Calderón Preconditioning for Acoustic Scattering at Multi-Screens
Kristof Cools, Carolina Urzúa-Torres

Abstract
We propose a preconditioner for the Helmholtz exterior problems on multi-screens. For this, we combine quotient-space BEM and operator preconditioning. For a class of multi-screens (which we dub type A multi-screens), we show that this approach leads to block diagonal Calderón preconditioners and results in a spectral condition number that grows only logarithmically with h h , just as in the case of simple screens. Since the resulting scheme contains many more DoFs than strictly required, we also present strategies to remove almost all redundancy without significant loss of effectiveness of the preconditioner. We verify these findings by providing representative numerical results. Further numerical experiments suggest that these results can be extended beyond type A multi-screens and that the numerical method introduced here can be applied to essentially all multi-screens encountered by the practitioner, leading to a significantly reduced simulation cost.

Introduction

We are interested in the scattering of acoustic waves at multi-screens, which
are geometries composed of essentially two-dimensional piecewise smooth
surfaces joined together, as shown in Figure
1
. Hence, we consider
the following Dirichlet and Neumann Helmholtz boundary value problems (BVPs)
in the exterior of the multi-screen
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
, with wave number

\kappa\in\mathbb{C}
,
\operatorname{Re}\kappa\geq 0
,

\displaystyle-\Delta U-\kappa^{2}U=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\overline{\Gamma},\qquad U=g_{D}\quad\text{ or }\quad\dfrac{\partial U}{\partial\mathbf{n}}=f_{N}\quad\text{ on }\Gamma,

(1)

plus the
Sommerfield radiation condition

\lim_{r\to\infty}r\left(\dfrac{\partial U}{\partial r}-i\kappa U\right)=0,\quad r=\|\mathbf{x}\|,

(2)

where
\|\mathbf{x}\|
designates the Euclidean norm of a point
\mathbf{x}
in

\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and
g_{D}
and
f_{N}
are suitable boundary data.

:
Two examples of multi-screen geometries

Our goal is to solve these exterior BVPs efficiently by means of Galerkin
boundary element methods (BEM)
[
26
]
and Calderón preconditioning

[
28
,
6
]
. For this, we recast the BVPs as variational

first-kind
boundary integral equations (BIEs) defined for densities on
the surface of the multi-screen.

For
simple screens
this approach is well established

[
26
, Section 3.5.3]
. Here, we call a simple screen an orientable,
piecewise smooth two-dimensional manifold with boundary
\mathcal{S}
embedded in

\mathbb{R}^{3}
. For these geometries, the arising variational first-kind BIEs
are known to be coercive
[
30
,
16
,
15
]
in Sobolev spaces of

jumps
of suitable field traces, in
\widetilde{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}([\Gamma])
and
\widetilde{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}([\Gamma])
,
respectively
[
24

. For these trace spaces, conforming boundary
element spaces are easily available, and they lead to Galerkin approximations
and Calderón preconditioning whose numerical analysis is well-understood

[
25
,
21
,
23
]
.

In contrast, the notion of jumps becomes problematic in multi-screens, since
they are not globally orientable. For this reason, the tools from simple
screens cannot be used verbatim on multi-screens. Many alternatives have been
proposed to tackle this problem.
[
4
,
31
,
11
,
10
,
12
,
13
]
. It
is worth pointing out that at the time of writing this article, a rigorous
analysis of these approaches in suitable trace spaces is not available.
Furthermore, these approaches lead to ill-conditioned linear systems, yet are
not amenable to preconditioning.

Fortunately, recent work by Claeys and Hiptmair offers the mathematical
framework to overcome these difficulties
[
8
]
. The key idea is to see
trace spaces from the perspective of quotient-spaces and to work with on multi-valued
traces. This new paradigm not only allows for a rigorous analysis, but it also
paves the way for conforming Galerkin discretization by means of quotient-space
BEM, as proposed in
[
7
]
.
Indeed, instead of trying to approximate jumps directly, the new approach relies on the Galerkin discretization of multi-trace boundary element spaces.
With this approach, the related BIEs give rise to Galerkin matrices with large
null spaces comprised of single-trace functions. Since the right-hand-sides
of the linear systems of equations are consistent, Krylov subspace iterative
solvers like GMRES still converge to the right solution. We summarize these
ideas and results in Section
2
.

Now that the most fundamental issues have been solved, we are in the position to
investigate how to improve the computational performance of quotient-space BEM
for multi-screens.
Indeed, one should note that the arising linear systems are ill-conditioned and
that the number of GMRES iteration counts increases with mesh refinement. Hence,
a natural next step – and the main focus of this paper – is to devise
preconditioners for multi-screen problems. In Section
3
,
we propose a simple preconditioning strategy based on opposite-order preconditioning,
also known as Calderón preconditioning on closed surfaces. Moreover, we present
the tools to understand the new preconditioner in the context of operator
preconditioning. Numerical experiments confirm that this approach reduces considerably
the number of GMRES iterations required to solve the system.

It is worth mentioning that an advantage of the quotient-space BEM approach is
that minimal geometrical information is required. However, the disadvantage is
that one pays with unnecessary computations due to the “doubling of degrees of
freedom” underlying the discretization of multi-valued traces. As an
alternative, we dedicate Section
4
to discuss reduced
quotient-space representations that require slightly more geometrical information,
but minimize computational effort while still rendering efficient Calderón preconditioning. Furthermore, we use the tools derived in Section
3

to provide some insight about the requirements that such reductions need to fulfil.

3
Calderón Preconditioning for Quotient-Space BEM

As already mentioned in the introduction, the linear systems arising from
the discretization of (
12
) and (
14
) using Quotient-space
BEM are ill-conditioned, which causes that the number of GMRES iteration counts
increases with mesh refinement. One should note that this is not a particularity

low-order BEM discretization of first-kind integral equations on simple screens
and closed surfaces. In those cases, one typically fixes the problem by using so-called
Calderón preconditioning, which combines Calderón identities with operator
preconditioning to build a very convenient and effective preconditioner

6

20
]

In this paper, we will extend this approach and devise Calderón
preconditioners for the problem at hand.
Following the policy of operator preconditioning (see, for instance

[
20
]
), we introduce the following more general notation in order to
state the results that will hold for both BIEs under consideration (i.e. (

Let

spaces such that

\mathbb{Y}(\Gamma)=\left(\mathbb{X}(\Gamma)\right)^{\prime}

•

Let

and

and

•

Let

and

be

\widetilde{X}(\Gamma)=\mathbb{X}(\Gamma)/X(\Gamma)
and
\widetilde{Y}(\Gamma)=\mathbb{Y}(\Gamma)/Y(\Gamma)

What these spaces will be exactly, depends on whether we are solving the Dirichlet or Neumann problem. For clarity, we will consider each case separately
in the next Subsections.

Now, in both cases, we are interested in continuous sesquilinear forms

parameter

Let us begin by introducing the

3.1.1
A flawed idea

With these choices and Corollary

\displaystyle\sup_{\mu_{h}\in Y_{h}(\Gamma)}\dfrac{|\ll x_{h},\mu_{h}\gg|}{\|\mu_{h}\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}([\Gamma])}}\geq C_{1}\|x_{h}\|_{\widetilde{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}([\Gamma])},\qquad\text{ and}\qquad\sup_{u_{h}\in\widetilde{X}_{h}(\Gamma)}\dfrac{|\ll\mathsf{W}_{\kappa}\,x_{h},u_{h}\gg|}{\|u_{h}\|_{\widetilde{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}([\Gamma])}}\geq C_{\mathsf{W}}\|x_{h}\|_{\widetilde{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}([\Gamma])},

Changing perspective

In order to find the right operator

Let

4
Calderón preconditioning on Reduced Quotient-Space BEM

The above analysis has been carried out for the case where the finite element space is chosen to approximate the entire multi-trace space. Since the solution is determined in the jump space, it can be worth while to investigate whether (combinations of) degrees of freedom (DoFs) can be deleted and whether the resulting method remains amenable to operator preconditioning schemes.

In this section we will introduce several ways in which the number of DoFs can be reduced, what mileage can be expected from the resulting methods, and we discuss what the ramifications are for implementations in code of these methods.

The most straightforward approach to building a well-conditioned boundary element method on multi-screens is to introduce a finite element space for the multi-trace space that is contained in the direct product space. Two key ingredients for the success of this approach are that

•

the discrete left/right nullspace
X_{h}(\Gamma)
equals
H^{+\frac{1}{2}}([\Gamma])\cap\mathbb{X}_{h}(\Gamma)
; and that

•

the quotient
\mathbb{X}_{h}(\Gamma)/X_{h}(\Gamma)
approximates
\widetilde{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}([\Gamma])
.

However, because we are interested in finding an approximate solution in the quotient space
\widetilde{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}([\Gamma])
, we are free to consider boundary element spaces
\mathbb{X}_{h}(\Gamma)^{\circ}
that do not approximate all of
\mathbb{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
as long as the corresponding discrete nullspace
X_{h}(\Gamma)^{\circ}=H^{+\frac{1}{2}}([\Gamma])\cap\mathbb{X}_{h}(\Gamma)^{\circ}
is still a subset of
H^{+\frac{1}{2}}([\Gamma])\cap\mathbb{X}_{h}(\Gamma)
and the quotient spaces
\mathbb{X}_{h}(\Gamma)^{\circ}/X_{h}(\Gamma)^{\circ}
and
\mathbb{X}_{h}(\Gamma)/X_{h}(\Gamma)
are equal. Similar choices can be made to select a reduced dual finite element space
\mathbb{Y}_{h}(\Gamma)^{\circ}\subseteq\mathbb{Y}_{h}(\Gamma)
. The quality of the resulting operator preconditioning depends on the stability of the restriction of the duality form to this subspace.

How does this work in practice? In the case of nodal elements in
\mathcal{S}_{0}^{1,0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
, any given basis function relates to a function in
X_{h}(\Gamma)
by completing it with its counterpart(s) on the opposite side(s) of the multi-screen. By removing one of the basis functions from the standard nodal basis for
\mathbb{X}_{h}(\Gamma)^{\circ}
, the dimension of the discrete nullspace
X_{h}(\Gamma)^{\circ}
goes down by one. The dimension of the complement of
X_{h}(\Gamma)^{\circ}
remains unchanged and so necessarily
\mathbb{X}_{h}(\Gamma)^{\circ}/X_{h}(\Gamma)^{\circ}=\mathbb{X}_{h}(\Gamma)/X_{h}(\Gamma)
. To put it in more physical terminology: the reduced discrete multi-trace space
\mathbb{X}_{h}(\Gamma)^{\circ}

radiates
the same fields as the original one.

There are a number of reduction strategies that are fairly straightforward to implement. We will discuss here three strategies that can be applied to a multi-screen
\Gamma
comprising a single junction where an odd number
m
simple screens meet.

(i)

Partial reduction
: In the partition
[\Gamma]=\cup_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{I}_{i}
, degrees of freedom based on the terms
i=3,5,7,...
can be discarded. This is extremely easy to implement and boils down to using
\mathbb{X}_{h}(\Gamma)^{\circ}=\mathcal{S}^{0,-1}(\mathcal{I}_{1,h})\times\prod_{i=1}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}\mathcal{S}^{0,-1}(\mathcal{I}_{2i,h})
instead of
\mathbb{X}_{h}(\Gamma)=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{S}^{0,-1}(\mathcal{I}_{i,h})
.

(ii)

Single strip
: The partial reduction described above in essence removes the
back
from part of
[\Gamma]
. This still leaves significant redundancy in
\mathbb{X}_{h}(\Gamma)^{\circ}
. In our example leaving out
\mathcal{S}^{0,-1}(\mathcal{I}_{i,h})
for
i=3,5,7,...
still leaves all the DoFs on
\Gamma_{2}
(excluding DoFs on the junction) that can be completed by DoFs on
the other side
to yield functions in
X_{h}(\Gamma)
. As a result, we can further discard DoFs in
\mathcal{S}^{0,-1}(\mathcal{I}_{1,h})
that lie in the interior of
\Gamma_{2}
. If the implementer has access to node-triangle adjacency information this approach requires minimal coding effort. The resulting finite element space is
X_{h}(\Gamma)^{\circ}=\mathcal{S}^{0,-1}(\mathcal{I}_{1,h})^{\circ}\times\prod_{i=1}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}\mathcal{S}^{0,-1}(\mathcal{I}_{2i,h})
, where the
\circ
superscript on the first factor denotes that this finite element space is reduced by leaving out redundant DoFs linked to nodes that are in
\mathcal{I}_{1,h}\cap\Gamma_{2}
but not on the junction.

(iii)

Fixed overlap
: Note that the efficiency of the resulting preconditioning method depends on the lower bound for the duality form
\ll.,.\gg
on
\mathbb{X}_{h}(\Gamma)^{\circ}\times\mathbb{Y}_{h}(\Gamma)^{\circ}
, which may depend on the geometry and hence may indirectly depend on
h
when using a single strip reduction. In those situations where this is undesirable, one can opt to leave in not only those DoFs in
\mathcal{S}^{0,-1}(\mathcal{I}_{1,h})
that are positioned on
\Gamma_{1}
or on the junction, but also those on
\Gamma_{2}
inside a strip within a fixed mesh independent distance from the junction. Likely, this will require manipulations to the code at the level of mesh generation. The resulting method will lead to an increasing redundancy in DoFs as
h
tends to zero, but the user is guaranteed that the preconditioner efficiency will not be limited by degradation of the duality pairing stability.

, the other two proposed strategies are not computationally attractive.

5.1
Preconditioning the hypersingular operator (Neumann problem)

Consider the geometry in Figure

The numerical experiments will be run in the low-frequency regime (

Linear systems are solved using GMRES with the tolerance set to
2.0e-5
. To build the preconditioners, application of the inverse Gram matrix is required. This action is computed by running a second, inner GMRES solver within the outer, primal solver. Numerical experiments have shown that it is important to set the tolerance for this inner GMRES sufficiently low. In the experiments presented here the tolerance is set to
2.0e-12
. Fortunately the Gram matrices are well conditioned and application of their inverses through GMRES can be computed in a small and linear number of operations, even at these very small tolerances.

Another important aspect of implementing the preconditioning strategies presented above is the use of high quality quadrature rules, especially for interactions between geometric elements that are close together. Specifically, it is important that left and right nullspaces of the discrete bilinear forms are invariant upon introduction of the quadrature error. One can either choose to adopt highly accurate quadrature rules or to use rules that are symmetric with respect to back-front mirroring across the multi-screen. Here we have opted for the highly accurate and kernel independent Sauter-Schwab rules described in Chapter 5 of
[

6
Conclusions

We have presented an effective Calderón-type preconditioner
for Helmholtz equations at multi-screens that builds on quotient-space
BEM and operator preconditioning. Moreover, we have
proved and confirmed numerically that it performs as
standard Calderón preconditioning does on simple screens.

From a computational point of view, quotient-space
BEM considering the full discretization of multi-valued traces
has the advantage of requiring minimal geometrical information
but the disadvantage of ”doubling DoFs”. As an alternative, we
proposed different strategies to work with reduced multi-trace
discretizations that use less DoFs but require more adaptations when using a standard BEM code. We gave details regarding
the additional data requirements in the implementation of all
these strategies, and used the developed
framework to identify the requirements that reduced spaces need
to meet in order to still have efficient Calderón-type preconditioning.

Finally, we briefly presented an heuristic strategy to precondition multi-screens that also appear in applications but that are not covered by our
theory. Although in essence our approach follows the same principles of our
Calderón-type preconditioner for type A multi-screens, rigorous analysis has been elusive and therefore has not been treated in this article. Indeed, the key missing piece is an extension of Lemma 8 for this case. Nevertheless, we offered numerical experiments to investigate its effectivity.

Current and future work also involves extending the analysis of this preconditioning approach to Maxwell equations, where numerical results
are promising
[
14
]
.

\displaystyle\|\tilde{Q}_{h}^{j}u\|_{H^{1/2}(S_{j})}\leq c_{Qj}\|u\|_{H^{1/2}(S_{j})},\qquad\forall u\in H^{1/2}(S_{j}),

(54)

and that the following inf-sup condition holds

\displaystyle\underset{v_{h}\in\mathcal{S}^{1,0}(\check{S}_{jh})}{\sup}\frac{|\langle\varphi_{h},v_{h}\rangle|}{\|v_{h}\|_{H^{1/2}(S_{j})}}\geq\frac{1}{c_{1}}\|\varphi_{h}\|_{\widetilde{H}^{-1/2}(S_{j})}\>,\qquad\forall\>\varphi_{h}\>\in\>\mathcal{S}^{0,-1}(S_{jh}),

(55)

with constants
c_{1}>0
independent of
h
.

Remark 5
.

The local mesh conditions
[
27
, Assumption 2.1]
are considered mild because they are fulfilled by a broad set of meshes used in applications, including geometrically graded meshes, algebraically 2-graded meshes and families of meshes generated by adaptive red-green algorithms
[
17
]
.

Let us also introduce the projection operator
\Pi_{h}:\mathbb{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)\to\mathbb{Z}_{h}^{-1/2}(\Gamma)\subset\mathbb{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)

\displaystyle\ll\Pi_{h}u,w_{h}\gg=(u,w_{h})_{\mathbb{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}.

(56)

From
[
27
, Thm. 2.1 and 2.2]
, we know that under certain (mild) local mesh conditions
[
27
, Assumption 2.1]
we satisfy the discrete inf-sup condition of the duality pairing of multi-trace spaces. Moreover, these mesh conditions also guarantee the continuity of
\Pi_{h}
,
i.e.

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{h}u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}\leq c_{\pi}\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)},\qquad\forall u\in\mathbb{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma).

(57)

Therefore, in order to use the continuity of both projection operators,
we will need to satisfy the following mesh assumption:

Assumption 2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a multi-screen as in Assumption
1
.

We assume that for each
j=0,..,m
, the family of meshes
\{S_{jh}\}_{h\in\mathcal{H}},h>0

of
S_{j}
agree at the junction(s), are uniformly shape-regular, locally quasi-uniform and satisfy
the (mild) local mesh conditions from
[
27
, Assumption 2.1]
.

Lemma 7

(Inverse inequality in
\mathbb{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a multi-screen as in Assumption
1
and

\mathbb{Z}_{h}^{1/2}(\Gamma)
such that for all
v_{h}\in\mathbb{Z}_{h}^{1/2}(\Gamma)
it holds that
(v_{h})_{|S_{j}}\in\widetilde{H}^{1/2}(S_{j})
. Then, we have that for all
\varphi_{h}\in\mathbb{Z}_{h}^{-1/2}(\Gamma)

(58)

with mesh size
h\leq 1
, and
\widetilde{C}_{N}
¿0 independent of
h
.

Proof.

By definition of dual norm and Lemma
3
, we get

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{h}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle=\underset{v\in\mathbb{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)\setminus\{0\}}{\sup}\dfrac{|\ll v,\varphi_{h}\gg|}{\|v\|_{\mathbb{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}}=\underset{v\in\mathbb{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)\setminus\{0\}}{\sup}\dfrac{|\sum_{j=0}^{m}\langle v_{j},\varphi_{hj}\rangle_{S_{j}}|}{\|v\|_{\mathbb{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}},

(59)

where we have set
v_{j}=v_{|S_{j}}
and
\varphi_{hj}=(\varphi_{h})_{|S_{j}}
.
Then, let us consider the index set
\mathcal{J}
of all the indices
0\leq k\leq m
such that

. Thus, we have that

\displaystyle\dfrac{|\sum_{j=0}^{m}\langle v_{j},\varphi_{hj}\rangle_{S_{j}}|}{\|v\|_{\mathbb{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}}=\dfrac{|\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\langle v_{j},\varphi_{hj}\rangle_{S_{j}}|}{\|v\|_{\mathbb{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}}.

(60)

Next, we derive two inequalities that will help us proceed. First, using the

2
and then Young’s inequality

m

\displaystyle\|v\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}\geq\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\|v_{j}\|^{2}_{H^{1/2}(S_{j})}\geq\dfrac{1}{|\mathcal{J}|}\left(\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\|v_{j}\|_{H^{1/2}(S_{j})}\right)^{2}

(61)

where
|\mathcal{J}|
is the size of the index set
\mathcal{J}
.
Second, we remark that for a sum
\sum_{i=1}^{m^{*}}a_{i}
with all coefficients

and
m^{*}\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that
\dfrac{1}{\sum_{i=1}^{m^{*}}a_{i}}\leq\dfrac{1}{a_{k}}
for

Plugging these in (

) gives

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{h}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:aux1}}{=}\underset{v\in\mathbb{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)\setminus\{0\}}{\sup}\dfrac{|\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\langle v_{j},\varphi_{hj}\rangle_{S_{j}}|}{\|v\|_{\mathbb{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}}\quad\quad\quad\overset{\eqref{eq:aux2}}{\leq}|\mathcal{J}|^{1/2}\underset{v\in\mathbb{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)\setminus\{0\}}{\sup}\dfrac{|\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\langle v_{j},\varphi_{hj}\rangle_{S_{j}}|}{\sum_{k\in\mathcal{J}}\|v_{k}\|_{H^{1/2}(S_{k})}}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1}{|\mathcal{J}|^{1/2}}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\>\underset{w_{j}\in H^{1/2}(S_{j})\setminus\{0\}}{\sup}\dfrac{|\langle w_{j},\varphi_{hj}\rangle_{S_{j}}|}{\|w_{j}\|_{H^{1/2}(S_{j})}}=\dfrac{1}{|\mathcal{J}|^{1/2}}\sum_{j=0}^{m}\>\underset{w_{j}\in H^{1/2}(S_{j})\setminus\{0\}}{\sup}\dfrac{|\langle w_{j},\varphi_{hj}\rangle_{S_{j}}|}{\|w_{j}\|_{H^{1/2}(S_{j})}}.

(63)

Then, using

from (

) and its continuity

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{h}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1}{|\mathcal{J}|^{1/2}}\sum_{j=0}^{m}\>\underset{w_{j}\in H^{1/2}(S_{j})\setminus\{0\}}{\sup}\dfrac{|\langle\tilde{Q}_{h}^{j}w_{j},\varphi_{hj}\rangle_{S_{j}}|}{\|w_{j}\|_{H^{1/2}(S_{j})}}\leq\dfrac{1}{|\mathcal{J}|^{1/2}}\sum_{j=0}^{m}\>c_{Qj}\underset{w_{j}\in H^{1/2}(S_{j})\setminus\{0\}}{\sup}\dfrac{|\langle\tilde{Q}_{h}^{j}w_{j},\varphi_{hj}\rangle_{S_{j}}|}{\|\tilde{Q}_{h}^{j}w_{j}\|_{H^{1/2}(S_{j})}}

(64)

Using Cauchy-Schwarz, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{h}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1}{|\mathcal{J}|^{1/2}}\sum_{j=0}^{m}\>c_{Qj}\underset{w_{hj}\in\mathcal{S}^{0,1}(S_{jh})\setminus\{0\}}{\sup}\dfrac{\|w_{hj}\|_{H^{1/2}(S_{j})}\|\varphi_{hj}\|_{\widetilde{H}^{-1/2}(S_{j})}}{\|w_{hj}\|_{H^{1/2}(S_{j})}}\leq\dfrac{1}{|\mathcal{J}|^{1/2}}\sum_{j=0}^{m}\>c_{Qj}\|\varphi_{hj}\|_{\widetilde{H}^{-1/2}(S_{j})}

Using the inverse inequality on simple screens (

), we can further

with

For convenience, we can work the estimate a bit further

\displaystyle\leq c_{N}^{2}(1+|\log h|)^{2}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{m}\>\|\varphi_{hj}\|_{H^{-1/2}(S_{j})}\right)^{2}\>\overset{\eqref{eq:aux2}}{\leq}|\mathcal{J}|c_{N}^{2}(1+|\log h|)^{2}\sum_{j=0}^{m}\>\|\varphi_{hj}\|_{H^{-1/2}(S_{j})}^{2},

(67)

which gives (

) with

∎

Lemma 8

(Inverse inequality in
\mathbb{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a multi-screen as in Assumption

and

\mathbb{Z}_{h}^{-1/2}(\Gamma)
such that for all

(\mu_{h})_{|S_{j}}\in\widetilde{H}^{-1/2}(S_{j})
. Then, we have that for all

\|u_{h}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}\leq\widetilde{C}_{D}(1+|\log h|)\left(\sum_{j=0}^{m}\|u_{h}\|_{H^{1/2}(S_{j})}^{2}\right)^{1/2},

with mesh size

, and

¿0 independent of

Proof.

By definition and continuity of

(

\displaystyle\|u_{h}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}=\dfrac{|(u_{h},u_{h})_{\mathbb{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}|}{\|u_{h}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}}=\dfrac{|\ll u_{h},\Pi_{h}u_{h}\gg|}{\|u_{h}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}}\leq c_{\pi}\dfrac{|\ll u_{h},\Pi_{h}u_{h}\gg|}{\|\Pi_{h}u_{h}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}}.

In the following, we will proceed in analogy to what we did in the proof of

A.2
Norm equivalences

Recall that

\|u\|_{\widetilde{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}([\Gamma])}=\inf_{x\in H^{+\frac{1}{2}}([\Gamma])}\|u+x\|_{\mathbb{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)},

and that
\mathcal{S}^{1,0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
is the space spanned by piecewise
linear “continuous” functions on (the virtual mesh)
\mathcal{T}_{h}
. We define
the norm

\|u\|_{\widetilde{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}([\Gamma]),D}:=\inf_{x_{h}\in H^{+\frac{1}{2}}([\Gamma])\cap\mathcal{S}^{1,0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}\|u+x_{h}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)},

(73)

and study its relation with the continuous jump norm.

Lemma 9
.

Assume that there exists an operator
\mathsf{R}_{h}^{+}\,:\,\mathbb{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)\to\mathcal{S}^{1,0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
such that

(i)

\mathsf{R}_{h}^{+}
is a h-uniformly bounded projection,

(ii)

\mathsf{R}_{h}^{+}(H^{+\frac{1}{2}}([\Gamma]))\subseteq H^{+\frac{1}{2}}([\Gamma])\cap\mathcal{S}^{1,0}(\mathcal{T}_{h}).

Then
\|\cdot\|_{\widetilde{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}([\Gamma])}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\widetilde{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}([\Gamma]),D}
are equivalent norms

Proof.

By definition, we have

\|u\|_{\widetilde{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}([\Gamma])}\leq\|u\|_{\widetilde{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}([\Gamma]),D}

(74)

Choose
u_{h}=\mathsf{R}_{h}^{+}u
where
u\in\widetilde{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}([\Gamma])\subset\mathbb{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
.
Then we have that

\|u_{h}\|_{\widetilde{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}([\Gamma]),D}=\inf_{x_{h}\in H^{+\frac{1}{2}}([\Gamma])\cap\mathcal{S}^{1,0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}\|u_{h}+x_{h}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}.

Note that, by surjectivity of
\mathsf{R}_{h}^{+}
and property (ii), there exists an
x\in H^{+\frac{1}{2}}([\Gamma])
such that

x_{h}=\mathsf{R}_{h}^{+}x
. This allows us to write

\|u_{h}\|_{\widetilde{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}([\Gamma]),D}=\inf_{x_{h}\in H^{+\frac{1}{2}}([\Gamma])}\|\mathsf{R}_{h}^{+}(u+x)\|_{\mathbb{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}.

Since
\mathsf{R}_{h}^{+}
is continuous, we further get

\|u_{h}\|_{\widetilde{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}([\Gamma]),D}\leq\|\mathsf{R}_{h}^{+}\|\inf_{x_{h}\in H^{+\frac{1}{2}}([\Gamma])}\|u+x\|_{\mathbb{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}=\|\mathsf{R}_{h}^{+}\|\|u_{h}\|_{\widetilde{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}([\Gamma])}

(75)

for all
v_{h}\in\widetilde{H}^{+\frac{1}{2}}([\Gamma])\cap\mathcal{S}^{1,0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
.

From this, we conclude that the two norms are equivalent.
Furthermore, the fact that
\mathsf{R}_{h}^{+}
is
h
-uniformly bounded guarantees that the
related constants are
h
-independent.
∎

Similarly, one shows that for the norm

\|\mu\|_{\widetilde{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}([\Gamma]),D}:=\inf_{\xi_{h}\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}}([\Gamma])\cap\mathcal{S}^{0,-1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}\|\mu+\xi_{h}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)},

(76)

we have

Lemma 10
.

Assume that there exists an operator
\mathsf{R}_{h}^{-}\,:\,\mathbb{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)\to\mathcal{S}^{0,-1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
such that

(i)

\mathsf{R}_{h}^{-}
is a h-uniformly bounded projection,

(ii)

\mathsf{R}_{h}^{-}(H^{-\frac{1}{2}}([\Gamma]))\subseteq H^{-\frac{1}{2}}([\Gamma])\cap\mathcal{S}^{0,-1}(\mathcal{T}_{h}).

Then, there exists
C_{N}>0
independent of
h
such that

\|\varphi_{h}\|_{\widetilde{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}([\Gamma])}\leq\|\varphi_{h}\|_{\widetilde{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}([\Gamma]),D}\leq c_{N}\|\varphi_{h}\|_{\widetilde{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}([\Gamma])}

(77)

for all
\varphi_{h}\in\widetilde{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}([\Gamma])\cap\mathcal{S}^{0,-1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
.

A.3
Condition number estimates for quotient space discretisations

Following the policy of operator preconditioning, we need to bound the spectral
condition number
\kappa_{sp}(\mathbf{M}^{-1}\mathbf{B}_{h}\mathbf{M}^{-T}\mathbf{A}_{h})
for the pairs

(\mathbf{A}_{h},\mathbf{B}_{h})=(\mathbf{W}_{\kappa,h},\mathbf{B}^{\mathsf{W}}_{\kappa,h})
, and
(\mathbf{A}_{h},\mathbf{B}_{h})=(\mathbf{V}_{\kappa,h},\mathbf{B}^{\mathsf{V}}_{\kappa,h})
. In other words, we are assuming that we
have that

•

the Galerkin matrix
\mathbf{B}_{h}
arises from a continuous sesquilinear form
\mathsf{b}

that satisfies a discrete inf-sup condition on the whole space
\mathbb{Y}_{h}(\Gamma)
;

•

the Galerkin matrix
\mathbf{M}_{h}
arises from a continuous sesquilinear form
\mathsf{m}

that satisfies a discrete inf-sup condition on the whole space
\mathbb{X}_{h}(\Gamma)
;

•

the Galerkin matrix
\mathbf{A}_{h}
has a non-trivial nullspace
N_{h}(\Gamma):=\ker\mathsf{A}_{h}
. Moreover,
its corresponding sesquilinear form
\mathsf{a}
satisfies a discrete inf-sup
condition only on
\mathbb{X}_{h}(\Gamma)/N_{h}(\Gamma)
.

Since
\mathbf{A}_{h}
is either
\mathbf{W}_{\kappa,h}
or
\mathbf{V}_{\kappa,h}
, we will directly
use that
N_{h}(\Gamma)=X_{h}(\Gamma)
to avoid unnecessary extra notation.

As usual, this entails bounding
\lambda_{\max}:=\lambda_{\max}(\mathbf{M}^{-1}\mathbf{B}_{h}\mathbf{M}^{-T}\mathbf{A}_{h})
from above and
\lambda_{\min}:=\lambda_{\min}(\mathbf{M}^{-1}\mathbf{B}_{h}\mathbf{M}^{-T}\mathbf{A}_{h})
from below. In order to write these bounds,
we need to introduce some notation first.

Let
\mathsf{A}_{h}\,:\,\mathbb{X}_{h}(\Gamma)\to\mathbb{X}_{h}(\Gamma)^{\prime}
,
\mathsf{B}_{h}\,:\,\mathbb{Y}_{h}(\Gamma)\to\mathbb{Y}_{h}(\Gamma)^{\prime}
and

\mathsf{M}_{h}\,:\,\mathbb{X}_{h}(\Gamma)\to\mathbb{Y}_{h}(\Gamma)^{\prime}
be the bounded linear operators associated to
the sesquilinear forms
\mathsf{a}
,
\mathsf{b}
and
\mathsf{m}
, respectively.

For
\lambda_{\max}
we proceed in the classical way and arrive to

\displaystyle\lambda_{\max}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathsf{M}^{-1}\|^{2}\|\mathsf{B}\|\|\mathsf{A}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{\mathsf{M}}^{2}\|\mathsf{B}\|\|\mathsf{A}\|,

(78)

where

\displaystyle\small\alpha_{\mathsf{M}}:=\inf_{v_{h}\in\mathbb{X}_{h}(\Gamma)\setminus\{0\}}\dfrac{\|\mathsf{M}_{h}v_{h}\|_{\mathbb{Y}(\Gamma)^{\prime}}}{\|v_{h}\|_{\mathbb{X}(\Gamma)}}.

For
\lambda_{\min}
we have to take a slightly different approach since we need to restrict
\mathsf{A}_{h}

to the space where its corresponding bilinear form
\mathsf{a}
satisfies a discrete inf-sup
condition. Moreover, we need to establish when the discrete inf-sup condition will
bound the smallest eigenvalue. We study this in the next Lemma.

Lemma 11

(Discrete inf-sup constant in the quotient space norm)
.

Let
\mathsf{a}
be a continuous bilinear form on
\mathbb{X}(\Gamma)\times\mathbb{X}(\Gamma)
. Let
X(\Gamma)\subseteq\mathbb{X}(\Gamma)
be both the left and right nullspace of
\mathsf{a}
.

Let
\mathbb{X}_{h}(\Gamma)
be a finite dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{X}(\Gamma)
and
\mathsf{A}_{h}\,:\,\mathbb{X}_{h}(\Gamma)\to\mathbb{X}_{h}(\Gamma)^{\prime}
the bounded linear operator associated to
\mathsf{a}
.
We assume that
\mathbb{X}_{h}(\Gamma)
is nullspace conforming to
\mathsf{a}
in the sense that

X_{h}(\Gamma):=\ker\mathsf{A}_{h}=\ker\mathsf{A}^{\prime}_{h}
is a linear subspace of
X(\Gamma)\cap\mathbb{X}_{h}(\Gamma)\subseteq X(\Gamma)
.

If
\mathsf{a}
satisfies a discrete inf-sup condition in the quotient space
\mathbb{X}_{h}(\Gamma)/X_{h}(\Gamma)\times\mathbb{X}_{h}(\Gamma)/X_{h}(\Gamma)
with constant
\alpha_{\mathsf{a}}>0
and if the norms on
\mathbb{X}(\Gamma)/X(\Gamma)
and
\mathbb{X}(\Gamma)/X_{h}(\Gamma)
are equivalent, then

\sup_{v_{h}\in\mathbb{X}_{h}(\Gamma)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\mathsf{a}(u_{h},v_{h})}{\|v_{h}\|_{\mathbb{X}(\Gamma)}}\geq\tilde{\alpha}_{\mathsf{a}}\|u_{h}\|_{\mathbb{X}(\Gamma)/X(\Gamma)}.

(79)

Proof.

We note that due to the kernel, the supremum is not attained at
v_{h}\in X_{h}(\Gamma)
,
and thus

\displaystyle\sup_{v_{h}\in\mathbb{X}_{h}(\Gamma)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\mathsf{a}(u_{h},v_{h})}{\|v_{h}\|_{\mathbb{X}(\Gamma)}}\geq\sup_{\tilde{v}_{h}\in X_{h}(\Gamma)^{\perp}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\mathsf{a}(u_{h},\tilde{v}_{h})}{\|\tilde{v}_{h}\|_{\mathbb{X}(\Gamma)}}

(80)

Let
\mathbb{Q}_{h}:\mathbb{X}_{h}(\Gamma)\to X_{h}(\Gamma)^{\perp}
be an orthogonal projection with
respect to the
\mathbb{X}(\Gamma)
-inner product. Using that
\tilde{v}_{h}=\mathbb{Q}_{h}\tilde{v}_{h}
, and the identification of
X_{h}(\Gamma)^{\perp}
with
\mathbb{X}_{h}(\Gamma)/X_{h}(\Gamma)
, we have that by property of quotient spaces
[
5
, Eq. (4)]

\displaystyle\sup_{\tilde{v}_{h}\in X_{h}(\Gamma)^{\perp}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\mathsf{a}(u_{h},\tilde{v}_{h})}{\|\tilde{v}_{h}\|_{\mathbb{X}(\Gamma)}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\tilde{v}_{h}\in X_{h}(\Gamma)^{\perp}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\mathsf{a}(u_{h},\tilde{v}_{h})}{\|\mathbb{Q}_{h}\tilde{v}_{h}\|_{\mathbb{X}(\Gamma)}}=\sup_{\tilde{v}_{h}\in\mathbb{X}_{h}(\Gamma)/X_{h}(\Gamma)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\mathsf{a}(u_{h},\tilde{v}_{h})}{\|\tilde{v}_{h}\|_{\mathbb{X}(\Gamma)/X_{h}(\Gamma)}}.

(81)

Finally, by norm equivalence, this becomes the discrete inf-sup condition, i.e.

\displaystyle\sup_{v_{h}\in\mathbb{X}_{h}(\Gamma)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\mathsf{a}(u_{h},v_{h})}{\|v_{h}\|_{\mathbb{X}(\Gamma)}}

\displaystyle\geq c\,\sup_{\tilde{v}_{h}\in\mathbb{X}_{h}(\Gamma)/X_{h}(\Gamma)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\mathsf{a}(u_{h},\tilde{v}_{h})}{\|\tilde{v}_{h}\|_{\mathbb{X}(\Gamma)/X(\Gamma)}}

(82)

\displaystyle\geq c\alpha_{\mathsf{a}}|u_{h}\|_{\mathbb{X}(\Gamma)/X(\Gamma)},

(83)

and therefore the required result follows with
\tilde{\alpha}_{\mathsf{a}}=c\alpha_{\mathsf{a}}
,
where
c
is the constant from the norm-equivalence.
∎

Using all the above, we can bound
\lambda_{\min}
as follows

\displaystyle\lambda_{\min}

Remark 6
.

Note that the nullspace conformity requirement forces us to use meshes that agree on the
front
and
back
of the structure. In practice this does not pose a major limitation because in a typical usage scenario the simple screens
\partial\Omega_{j}\cap\Gamma
are built by fusing together two or more unique meshes for the interfaces
\partial\Omega_{i}\cup\partial\Omega_{j}
. The interface meshes are used both as front and back and thus necessarily agree.

Remark 7
.

For the discrete quotient norm to be bounded from below by the continuous quotient norm, it suffices that
X_{h}(\Gamma)\subseteq X(\Gamma)\cap\mathbb{X}_{h}(\Gamma)
; equality is not required. This opens the door to reduction schemes for the multi-trace space variational formulation. A reduction scheme is a choice
\mathbb{X}_{h}(\Gamma)^{\circ}\subseteq\mathbb{X}_{h}(\Gamma)\subseteq\mathbb{X}(\Gamma)
with corresponding discrete left/right nullspace
X_{h}(\Gamma)^{\circ}
such that (i)
X_{h}(\Gamma)^{\circ}\subseteq X(\Gamma)
and (ii)
\mathbb{X}_{h}(\Gamma)^{\circ}/X_{h}(\Gamma)^{\circ}=\mathbb{X}_{h}(\Gamma)/X_{h}(\Gamma)
. Such a choice leads on one hand to approximations in the jump space of equal quality and on the other hand does not preclude the construction of efficient operator preconditioners.

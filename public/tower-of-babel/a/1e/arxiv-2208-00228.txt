Sharp and strong non-uniqueness for the magneto-hydrodynamic equations
Yao Nie and, Weikui Ye

Abstract
In this paper, we prove a sharp and strong non-uniqueness for a class of weak solutions to the three-dimensional magneto-hydrodynamic (MHD) system. More precisely, we show that any weak solution ( v , b ) ∈ L t p ​ L x ∞ (v,b)\in L^{p}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x} is non-unique in L t p ​ L x ∞ L^{p}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x} with 1 ≤ p < 2 1\leq p<2 , which reveals the strong non-uniqueness, and the sharpness in terms of the classical Ladyzhenskaya-Prodi-Serrin criteria at endpoint ( 2 , ∞ ) (2,\infty) . Moreover, for any ϵ > 0 \epsilon>0 , we can also construct infinitely many weak solutions of the ideal MHD system in L t 1 ​ C x 1 − ϵ L^{1}_{t}C^{1-\epsilon}_{x} . Our result shows the non-uniqueness for any weak solution ( v , b ) (v,b) including non-trivial magnetic field b b .

Introduction

In many natural phenomena, magnetic fields are closely related to a large number of natural and man-made fluids. The model of magneto-hydrodynamics (MHD) system is already widely used to study the interaction between magnetic fields and moving, conducting flows. In this paper, we consider the MHD equations on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{3}=\mathbb{R}^{3}/\mathbb{Z}^{3}
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}v-\Delta v+\div(v\otimes v)+\nabla p=\div(b\otimes b),\\
&\partial_{t}b-\Delta b+\div(v\otimes b)=\div(b\otimes v),\\
&\div v=0,~\div b=0,\end{aligned}\right.

When

Compared with the Navier-Stokes equations, the anti-symmetric structure

Before giving our results, we show two non-uniqueness definitions introduced in

\displaystyle\bullet~\text{``Weak non-uniqueness'': there exists a non-unique weak solution in the class X.}

\displaystyle\bullet~\text{``Strong non-uniqueness'': any weak solution in the class X is non-unique.}

We can prove the non-uniqueness of weak solutions in a strong sense and our main results are as follows:

Theorem 1.4

(Sharp and strong non-uniqueness)
.

Let

. Any weak solution
(v,b)

1.1
) in

is non-unique. Moreover, there exist non-Leray-Hopf weak solutions
(v,b)
in
L^{p}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x}

Remark 1.5

Given

, Cheskidov and Luo

is not unique

has at least one interval regularity

\displaystyle\|(\partial^{M}_{t}D^{N}\mathring{\bar{R}}_{q},\,\partial^{M}_{t}D^{N}\mathring{\bar{M}}_{q})\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{3}_{x}}\leq\lambda^{55+15N}_{q},\quad\forall N\geq 0,\,M\in\{0,1\}.

(3.28)

Proof.

By the definition of
(\mathring{\bar{R}}_{q},\mathring{\bar{M}}_{q})
in (
3.20
) and (
3.21
), we have

\displaystyle\|(\mathring{\bar{R}}_{q},\mathring{\bar{M}}_{q})\|_{L^{1}_{t,x}}\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N_{q}}\|\partial_{t}\chi_{i}\|_{L^{1}_{t}}\|\mathcal{R}(\textsf{{v}}_{i-1}-\textsf{{v}}_{i})\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{i};L^{1})}+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{q}}\|\partial_{t}\chi_{i}\|_{L^{1}_{t}}\|\mathcal{R}_{a}(\textsf{{b}}_{i-1}-\textsf{{b}}_{i})\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{i};L^{1})}

\displaystyle+\|\textsf{{v}}_{i-1}-\textsf{{v}}_{i}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(I_{i};L^{2})}+\|\textsf{{b}}_{i-1}-\textsf{{b}}_{i}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(I_{i};L^{2})}).

With aid of Proposition
3.3
, choosing
0<\varepsilon<\frac{\alpha}{3}
and sufficient large
a
, we deduce from the above inequality and (
2.6
) that,

\displaystyle\|(\mathring{\bar{R}}_{q},\mathring{\bar{M}}_{q})\|_{L^{1}_{t,x}}\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{i=1}^{N_{q}}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+2}}\|(\mathring{R}_{q},\mathring{M}_{q})\|_{B^{0}_{1,1}}ds+C\lambda^{-3}_{q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|(\mathring{R}_{q},\mathring{M}_{q})\|^{1-{\varepsilon}}_{L^{1}_{t,x}}\|(\mathring{R}_{q},\mathring{M}_{q})\|^{{\varepsilon}}_{L^{1}_{t}H^{3}_{x}}+C\|(\mathring{R}_{q},\mathring{M}_{q})\|_{L^{1}_{t,x}}+C\lambda^{-3}_{q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\delta_{q+1}\lambda^{-6\alpha})^{1-{\varepsilon}}(\lambda^{5}_{q})^{{\varepsilon}}+C\lambda^{-3}_{q}\leq\delta_{q+1}\lambda^{-4\alpha}_{q}.

In view of Corollary
3.2
, one obtains that

\displaystyle\|(\mathring{\bar{R}}_{q},\mathring{\bar{M}}_{q})\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{3}_{x}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\tau^{-1}_{q}\|(\textsf{{v}}_{i-1}-\textsf{{v}}_{i},\textsf{{b}}_{i-1}-\textsf{{b}}_{i})\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{i};H^{2})}+\|(\textsf{{v}}_{i-1}-\textsf{{v}}_{i},\textsf{{b}}_{i-1}-\textsf{{b}}_{i})\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(I_{i};H^{2})}\leq\lambda^{22}_{q}.

Furthermore, noting the fact that
\mathring{\bar{R}}_{q}
and
\mathring{\bar{M}}_{q}
support on

, where

possess

regularity, we obtain by (

) that for

,
\forall N\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\|(\partial^{M}_{t}D^{N}\mathring{\bar{R}}_{q},\,\,\partial^{M}_{t}D^{N}\mathring{\bar{M}}_{q})\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{3}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\tau^{-3}_{q}\sup_{1\leq i\leq N_{q}}\|\partial^{M}_{t}(\textsf{{v}}_{i-1}-\textsf{{v}}_{i})\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{i};H^{N+2})}+\tau^{-3}_{q}\sup_{1\leq i\leq N_{q}}\|\partial^{M}_{t}(\textsf{{v}}_{i-1}-\textsf{{v}}_{i})\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{i};H^{N+2})}

\displaystyle+\tau^{-2}_{q}(\|\partial^{M}_{t}D^{N}(\textsf{{v}}_{i-1},\textsf{{v}}_{i},\textsf{{b}}_{i-1},\textsf{{b}}_{i})\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(I_{i};H^{3})}+\|(\textsf{{v}}_{i-1},\textsf{{v}}_{i},\textsf{{b}}_{i-1},\textsf{{b}}_{i})\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(I_{i};H^{3})})

\displaystyle+\tau^{-2}_{q}(\|\partial^{M}_{t}(\textsf{{v}}_{i-1},\textsf{{v}}_{i},\textsf{{b}}_{i-1},\textsf{{b}}_{i})\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(I_{i};H^{3})}+\|D^{N}(\textsf{{v}}_{i-1},\textsf{{v}}_{i},\textsf{{b}}_{i-1},\textsf{{b}}_{i})\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(I_{i};H^{3})})

where the last inequality holds by sufficient large

4
Convex integration step: construction and estimates for the perturbation

To reduce the size of the Reynolds and magnetic stresses at
q+1
level, the construction of the perturbation
heavily depends on the two geometric lemmas. For the convenience of readers, we review the two geometric lemmas firstly.

4.1
Geometric lemmas

Lemma 4.1

(First Geometric Lemma
[
2
]
)
.

Let
B_{\delta}(0)
denote the ball of radius
\delta
centered at
0
in the space of
3\times 3
skew-symmetric matrices.
There exists a set
\Lambda_{b}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\cap\mathbb{Q}^{3}
that consists of vectors
k
with associated orthonormal basis
(k,\bar{k},\bar{\bar{k}}),~\epsilon_{b}>0
and smooth positive functions
a_{b,k}:B_{\epsilon_{b}}(0)\rightarrow\mathbb{R},
such that, for
M_{b}\in B_{\epsilon_{b}}(0)
we have the following identity:

M_{b}=\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{b}}a_{b,k}(M_{b})(\bar{k}\otimes\bar{\bar{k}}-\bar{\bar{k}}\otimes\bar{k}).

Lemma 4.2

(Second Geometric Lemma
[
2
]
)
.

Let
B_{\sigma}(0)
denote the ball of radius
\sigma
centered at
\rm Id
in the space of
3\times 3
symmetric matrices.
There exists a set
\Lambda_{u}\subset\mathbb{S}^{2}\cap\mathbb{Q}^{3}
that consists of vectors
k
with associated orthonormal basis
(k,\bar{k},\bar{\bar{k}}),~\epsilon_{u}>0
and smooth positive functions
a_{u,k}:B_{\epsilon_{u}}(\rm Id)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that, for
R_{u}\in B_{\epsilon_{u}}(\rm Id)
we have the following identity:

R_{u}=\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{u}}a_{u,k}(R_{u})\bar{k}\otimes\bar{k}.

Remark 4.3
.

It is worth noting that, once we choose a vector
k
in
\Lambda_{u}\cup\Lambda_{b}
, a unique triple
(k,\bar{k},\bar{\bar{k}})
could be obtained, see
[
2
]
for more details.

4.2
Amplitudes

Let
\eta_{q}\in C_{c}^{\infty}([2T,3T];[0,1])
satisfy

\text{supp}\,\eta_{q}=\big(2T+\tfrac{7\tau_{q}}{6},3T-\tfrac{\tau_{q}}{6}\big)\quad\text{and}\quad\eta_{q}(t)\equiv 1\,\text{for}\,\,t\in\big[2T+\tfrac{4\tau_{q}}{3},3T-\tfrac{\tau_{q}}{3}\big].

It is easy to check that for
N\geq 1
,
\|\partial_{t}^{N}\eta_{q}\|_{C^{0}_{t}}\lesssim\tau_{q}^{-N}.

Note that the geometric lemmas are valid for skew-symmetric matrices perturbed near
0
matrix and symmetric matrices perturbed near
\rm Id
matrix, we need the following smooth function
\chi
introduced in
[
2
]
such that the stresses are pointwise small. More precisely: Let
\chi:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be a smooth function satisfying

\chi(z)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &1,\quad 0\leq z\leq 1,\\
&z,\quad z\geq 2,\end{aligned}\right.

(4.1)

with
z\leq 2\chi(z)\leq 4z
for
z\in(1,2)
. Next, we define

\rho_{b}=\delta_{q+1}\lambda^{-2\alpha}_{q}\chi\Big(\Big\langle\tfrac{\mathring{\bar{M}}_{q}}{\delta_{q+1}\lambda^{-4\alpha}_{q}}\Big\rangle\Big),\,\,{\rho_{v}=\delta_{q+1}\lambda^{-2\alpha}_{q}}\chi\Big(\Big\langle\tfrac{R_{v}}{\delta_{q+1}\lambda^{-3\alpha}_{q}}\Big\rangle\Big),

where

R_{v}=\mathring{\bar{R}}_{q}+\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{b}}\eta^{2}_{q}\rho_{b}a^{2}_{b,k}\Big(-\tfrac{\mathring{\bar{M}}_{q}}{\rho_{b}}\Big)(\bar{k}\otimes\bar{k}-\bar{\bar{k}}\otimes\bar{\bar{k}}).

Now we define amplitudes
a_{(v,k)}
and
a_{(b,k)}
as follows:

a_{(v,k)}:=\eta_{q}\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}_{v}a_{v,k}({\rm Id}-\tfrac{R_{v}}{\rho_{v}}),\quad a_{(b,k)}:=\eta_{q}\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}_{b}a_{b,k}(-\tfrac{\mathring{\bar{M}}_{q}}{\rho_{b}}),

(4.2)

where
a_{b,k}
and
a_{v,k}
stem from Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
4.2
, respectively.

4.3
Constructing the perturbation

Assume that
\phi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a smooth cutoff function supported on the interval
[0,\frac{1}{2}]
. We normalize it in such a way that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=1.

For any small positive parameter
r
, we denote by
\phi_{r}
the following rescaled functions:

\phi_{r}:=r^{-\frac{1}{2}}\phi(r^{-1}x).

We periodize
\phi_{r}
so that the resulting functions are periodic functions defined on
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}=\mathbb{T}
.
Next, letting
k\in\Lambda_{v}\cup\Lambda_{b}
, we choose a large number
N_{\Lambda}
such that

N_{\Lambda}{k},N_{\Lambda}\bar{k},N_{\Lambda}\bar{\bar{k}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}.

Here and below, we use the following denotations:

\phi_{(\gamma,\tfrac{1}{2},k)}(x):=\phi_{\lambda^{-\gamma}_{q+1}}(\lambda^{\tfrac{1}{2}}_{q+1}N_{\Lambda}k\cdot x),~~g_{{(2,\iota)}}(t):=\phi_{\lambda^{-2}_{q+1}}(\lambda^{\sigma}_{q+1}t).

Without ambiguity, we denote
\phi_{(\gamma,\tfrac{1}{2},k)}(x)
by
\phi_{(\gamma,\tfrac{1}{2},k)}
only depending on spatial variable and denote
g_{{(2,\sigma)}}(t)
by
g_{{(2,\sigma)}}
only depending on time variable in the rest of this paper. Let
\psi=\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x^{2}}\Psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
. We define

\psi_{k}:=\psi(\lambda_{q+1}N_{\Lambda}k\cdot x)

and normalize it such that
\|\psi_{k}\|_{L^{2}}=1
.

The construction of perturbation are involved in the so-called
intermittent shear flows

\phi_{(\gamma,\tfrac{1}{2},k)}\bar{k}
or
\phi_{(\gamma,\tfrac{1}{2},k)}\bar{\bar{k}}
. One can easily check that the intermittent shear flows have zero mean and support on sheets with size
\thickapprox\lambda^{-\gamma}_{q+1}\times 1\times 1
. In summary, we have the following estimates:

Proposition 4.4
.

For
p\in[1,\infty]
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we have the following estimations

\big\|{D}^{m}_{x}\phi_{(\gamma,\tfrac{1}{2},k)}\big\|_{L^{p}_{x}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq\lambda^{\frac{m}{2}-\gamma(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})}_{q+1},\qquad\big\|\tfrac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{m}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}g_{{(2,\sigma)}}\big\|_{L^{p}_{t}([0,1])}\leq\lambda^{{\sigma m}-2(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})}_{q+1},

and the following estimates for the size of support of
\phi_{(\gamma,\tfrac{1}{2},k)}
:

\Big|{\rm supp}\big(\phi_{(\gamma,\tfrac{1}{2},k)}\big)\Big|\lesssim\lambda^{-\gamma}_{q+1},\quad\Big|{\rm supp}\big(\phi_{(\gamma,\tfrac{1}{2},k)}\phi_{(\gamma,\tfrac{1}{2},k^{\prime})}\big)\Big|\lesssim\lambda^{-2\gamma}_{q+1}\,\,\,{\rm if}\,\,k\neq k^{\prime}.

Based on the above intermittent shear flows, we define the
principal parts
of the perturbations
w^{(p)}_{q+1}
and
d^{(p)}_{q+1}
as follows:

\displaystyle w^{(p)}_{q+1}:=\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{v}}a_{(v,k)}\phi_{{(\gamma,\tfrac{1}{2},k)}}g_{{(2,\sigma)}}\psi_{k}\bar{k}+\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{b}}a_{(b,k)}\phi_{{(\gamma,\tfrac{1}{2},k)}}g_{{(2,\sigma)}}\psi_{k}\bar{k},

\displaystyle d^{(p)}_{q+1}:=\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{b}}a_{(b,k)}\phi_{{(\gamma,\tfrac{1}{2},k)}}g_{{(2,\sigma)}}\psi_{k}\bar{\bar{k}},

where
a_{(b,k)}
and
a_{(v,k)}
are defined in (
4.2
).

Next, we need to construct the
incompressibility correctors
to ensure that the perturbation is divergence-free.
Noting the fact that
\psi_{k}\bar{k}
is zero mean and divergence free, we have

\psi_{k}\bar{k}=\lambda^{-1}_{q+1}N^{-1}_{\Lambda}\curl\Big(-\big(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x}\Psi\big)(\lambda_{q+1}N_{\Lambda}k\cdot x)k\times\bar{k}\Big):=\lambda^{-1}_{q+1}\curl F_{\bar{k}}.

The
incompressibility correctors

w^{(c)}_{q+1}
and
d^{(c)}_{q+1}
are defined as

\displaystyle w^{(c)}_{q+1}=

\displaystyle\lambda^{-1}_{q+1}\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{v}}{\nabla\big(a_{(v,k)}\phi_{{(\gamma,\tfrac{1}{2},k)}}g_{{(2,\sigma)}}\big)}\times F_{\bar{k}}+\lambda^{-1}_{q+1}\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{b}}{\nabla\big(a_{(b,k)}\phi_{{(\gamma,\tfrac{1}{2},k)}}g_{{(2,\sigma)}}\big)}\times F_{\bar{k}},

and

\displaystyle d^{(c)}_{q+1}=\lambda^{-1}_{q+1}\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{b}}{\nabla\big(a_{(v,k)}\phi_{{(\gamma,\tfrac{1}{2},k)}}g_{{(2,\sigma)}}\big)}\times F_{\bar{\bar{k}}}.

Owning to
\curl(aF)=\nabla a\times F+a\curl F
, it is easy to verify that

\displaystyle w^{(p)}_{q+1}+w^{(c)}_{q+1}=

\displaystyle\lambda^{-1}_{q+1}\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{v}}{\curl\big(a_{(v,k)}\phi_{{(\gamma,\tfrac{1}{2},k)}}g_{{(2,\sigma)}}F_{\bar{k}}\big)}

(4.3)

\displaystyle+\lambda^{-1}_{q+1}\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{b}}{\curl\big(a_{(v,k)}\phi_{{(\gamma,\tfrac{1}{2},k)}}g_{{(2,\sigma)}}F_{\bar{k}}\big)},

and

d^{(p)}_{q+1}+d^{(c)}_{q+1}=\lambda^{-1}_{q+1}\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{b}}{\curl\big(a_{(v,k)}\phi_{{(-\gamma,\tfrac{1}{2},k)}}g_{{(2,\sigma)}}F_{\bar{\bar{k}}}\big)}.

(4.4)

Therefore,
\div(w^{(p)}_{q+1}+w^{(c)}_{q+1})=\div(d^{(p)}_{q+1}+d^{(c)}_{q+1})=0
.

Finally, Let
h_{\sigma}(t):[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
be defined by

h_{\sigma}(t)=\lambda^{-\sigma}_{q+1}\int_{0}^{\lambda^{\sigma}_{q+1}t}(\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}g^{2}_{{(2,\sigma)}})(s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,

where
\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}
is the projection onto functions with zero mean, that is
\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}f(s):=f(s)-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}f\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s
.

The
temporal correctors
are defined by

\displaystyle{w^{(t)}_{q+1}}=

\displaystyle-\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{v}}\mathbb{P}_{H}\div(a^{2}_{(v,k)}\bar{k}\otimes\bar{k})h_{\sigma}(t)-\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{b}}\mathbb{P}_{H}\div(a^{2}_{(b,k)}(\bar{k}\otimes\bar{k}-\bar{\bar{k}}\otimes\bar{\bar{k}}))h_{\sigma}(t)

(4.5)

\displaystyle{d^{(t)}_{q+1}}=

\displaystyle-\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{b}}\div(a^{2}_{(b,k)}(\bar{k}\otimes\bar{\bar{k}}-\bar{k}\otimes\bar{\bar{k}}))h_{\sigma}(t),

where
\mathbb{P}_{H}:={\rm Id}-\frac{\nabla\div}{\Delta}
represents the Leray projector. Therefore, we have
\div{w^{(t)}_{q+1}}=0
. Moreover,
{d^{(t)}_{q+1}}
is divergence-free because
\div\div A=0
for any anti-symmetric matrix
A
.

Moreover, we deduce by the support of
\eta_{q}(t)
that

(d_{p+1},w_{q+1})(t,x)=0,\quad\forall t\in[0,2T+\tfrac{7\tau_{q}}{6}]\cup[3T-\tfrac{\tau_{q}}{6},1\big].

(4.6)

Proposition 4.6
.

\displaystyle\|(a_{(b,k)},a_{(v,k)})\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}\lesssim\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}_{q+1}\lambda^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}_{q},\quad\,\,\,\|(a_{(b,k)},a_{(v,k)})\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\leq\lambda^{12}_{q},

\displaystyle\|(\partial^{M}_{t}a_{(b,k)},\partial^{M}_{t}a_{(v,k)})\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{5}_{x}}\leq\lambda^{1000}_{q},\,M=0,1.

Taking advantage of Proposition

\displaystyle\|\rho_{b}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\leq

\displaystyle\|a_{(v,k)}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\leq\|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}_{v}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\lesssim\Big(\delta_{q+1}\lambda^{-2\alpha}_{q}(1+\frac{(\|\mathring{\bar{R}}_{q}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}+\|a^{2}_{(b,k)}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}})}{\delta_{q+1}\lambda^{-3\alpha}_{q}})\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\lambda^{11+\frac{3\alpha}{2}}_{q}.

\displaystyle\|\rho^{-1}_{b}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}\dot{H}^{5}_{x}}

An easy computation yields that

\displaystyle\|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}_{b}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}\dot{H}^{5}_{x}}

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}a_{(b,k)}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}\dot{H}^{5}_{x}}\lesssim

\displaystyle+\|\eta_{q}\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}_{b}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\|\partial_{t}(a_{b,k}(-\tfrac{\mathring{\bar{M}}_{q}}{\rho_{b}}))\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}\dot{H}^{5}_{x}}+\|\eta_{q}\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}_{b}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}\dot{H}^{5}_{x}}\|\partial_{t}(a_{b,k}(-\tfrac{\mathring{\bar{M}}_{q}}{\rho_{b}}))\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}.

(7.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{a}u=\Delta^{-1}(\nabla u+\nabla u^{\mathsf{T}})-\frac{1}{2}\Delta^{-2}\nabla^{2}\nabla\cdot u-\frac{1}{2}\Delta^{-1}(\nabla\cdot u)I_{3\times 3},

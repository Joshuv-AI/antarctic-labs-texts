Almost Polynomial Factor Inapproximability for Parameterized k k -Clique
Karthik C. S, Subhash Khot

Abstract
The k k -Clique problem is a canonical hard problem in parameterized complexity. In this paper, we study the parameterized complexity of approximating the k k -Clique problem where an integer k k and a graph G G on n n vertices are given as input, and the goal is to find a clique of size at least k / F ⁡ ( k ) k/F(k) whenever the graph G G has a clique of size k k . When such an algorithm runs in time T ​ ( k ) ⋅ poly ​ ( n ) T(k)\cdot\text{poly}(n) (i.e., FPT-time) for some computable function T T , it is said to be an F ⁡ ( k ) F(k) -FPT-approximation algorithm for the k k -Clique problem. Although the non-existence of an F ⁡ ( k ) F(k) -FPT-approximation algorithm for any computable sublinear function F F is known under gap-ETH [Chalermsook et al., FOCS 2017], it has remained a long standing open problem to prove the same inapproximability result under the more standard and weaker assumption, W[1] ≠ \neq FPT. In a recent breakthrough, Lin [STOC 2021] ruled out constant factor (i.e., F ⁡ ( k ) = O ⁡ ( 1 ) F(k)=O(1) ) FPT-approximation algorithms under W[1] ≠ \neq FPT. In this paper, we improve this inapproximability result (under the same assumption) to rule out every F ⁡ ( k ) = k 1 / H ⁡ ( k ) F(k)=k^{1/H(k)} factor FPT-approximation algorithm for any increasing computable function H H (for example H ⁡ ( k ) = log ∗ ⁡ k H(k)=\log^{\ast}k ). Our main technical contribution is introducing list decoding of Hadamard codes over large prime fields into the proof framework of Lin.

Our Framework.

We are now ready to describe our proof framework. At a high level, the gap created by Lin mainly arrives from the distance of the Hadamard code. Since the gap generated by using Hadamard codes over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
is at most
1/2

\mathbb{F}_{q}

\mathbb{F}_{q}
in the low acceptance regime, has its own challenges, such as:

\bullet
First, in Lin’s case, local testability of Hadamard codes in the high acceptance regime is just the standard BLR Linearity testing
[
BLR93
]
, which can be used off the shelf. However, we need to test the Hadamard code in the low acceptance regime over
\mathbb{F}_{q}

\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Such results appear implicitly in literature, and we make them explicit through our Theorems

and
3.2

\bullet
Second, because we deal with list decoding instead of standard decoding, all the relationships in our proofs have some “noise” and therefore the arguments in our soundness analysis of Theorem
1.1
are very intricate.

We have described above, the challenges that we had to address to prove Theorem
1.1
over the result of
[
Lin21
]

Our starting point is the same as Lin’s starting point, i.e., the

]

(i) We have 3-ary constraints arising from the 3-query list decoding of Hadamard codes. These constraints enforce that the assignments satisfying them can themselves be viewed as a Hadamard codeword. In particular, for every
k

k
-Vector Sum instance, our assignment is supposed to be the Hadamard encoding of the sum of the

(ii) We have 2-ary constraints arising from a pair of points on any axis parallel line in

\vec{0}

After constructing this CSP, we build an instance of the

The satisfying pairs of the alphabet set of the constraints in items (ii) and (iii) appear directly as edges in the graph. Since every variable appear in multiple clouds of vertices, we only put an edge between pairs of vertices that are “consisitent” on their assigment to a variable.

Unlike

In this section, we prove a linearity testing result which is a key technical component in proving our inapproximability result.

Let
q
be a prime number and
d,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
.
Given a function
f:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ell}

\mathcal{T}

Pick
\vec{\bm{\alpha}},\vec{\bm{\beta}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
uniformly and independently at random. Accept if
f(\vec{\bm{\alpha}})+f(\vec{\bm{\beta}})=f(\vec{\bm{\alpha}}+\vec{\bm{\beta}})

S_{f,\mathcal{T}}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}\times\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}

S_{f,\mathcal{T}}:=\{(\vec{\bm{\alpha}},\vec{\bm{\beta}})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}\times\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}:f(\vec{\bm{\alpha}})+f(\vec{\bm{\beta}})=f(\vec{\bm{\alpha}}+\vec{\bm{\beta}})\},

We say that a function

\vec{\bm{\alpha}},\vec{\bm{\beta}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
we have
c(\vec{\bm{\alpha}})+c(\vec{\bm{\beta}})=c(\vec{\bm{\alpha}}+\vec{\bm{\beta}})
.
Moreover, we say that a function

\vec{\bm{\alpha}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}

\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
we have
f(\gamma\cdot\vec{\bm{\alpha}})=\gamma\cdot f(\vec{\bm{\alpha}})
.

We prove below a couple of theorems in the flavor of the many list-decoding results known in literature for Hadamard codes
[
GL89
,
GRS00
,
Gur09
]
.

Theorem 3.1

(Linearity Testing)
.

Let
q
be a prime number and
d\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
f:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a scalar respecting function. Let
\varepsilon,\delta>0
be parameters such that

\varepsilon\gg\delta\gg\frac{1}{q^{1/3}}
.
If
f
passes
\mathcal{T}
with probability
\varepsilon
, then there exists an integer
r=O(1/\delta^{2})
and

linear
functions
c_{1},\ldots,c_{r}:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}
, such that the following holds.

\underset{(\vec{\bm{\alpha}},\vec{\bm{\beta}})\sim S_{f,\mathcal{T}}}{\Pr}\left[\exists\ \text{unique }j\in[r]\text{ such that }f(\vec{\bm{\alpha}})=c_{j}(\vec{\bm{\alpha}}),f(\vec{\bm{\beta}})=c_{j}(\vec{\bm{\beta}})\right]\geqslant 1-O\left(\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}\right).

The proof of the above theorem follows by combining known ideas in literature, more precisely, we combine the arguments made in
[
AS03
]
and
[
KS13
]
to obtain the theorem. We include a proof of the above theorem in Appendix

for the sake of completeness.

Next, we extend the above theorem to functions from
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
to
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ell}
for any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
by using the above theorem as a blackbox result.

Theorem 3.2

(Piecing Together)
.

Let
q
be a prime number and
d,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
f:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ell}
be a scalar respecting function.
Let
\varepsilon,\tau>0
be parameters such that

1/2\geqslant\tau\geqslant\varepsilon\gg\frac{1}{q^{1/24}}
.
If
f
passes
\mathcal{T}
with probability
\varepsilon
, then there exists a
linear
function
c:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ell}
, such that the following holds.

\underset{\vec{\bm{\alpha}}\sim\mathsf{var}(f,\mathcal{T})}{\Pr}\left[\|f(\vec{\bm{\alpha}})-c(\vec{\bm{\alpha}})\|\leqslant\tau\right]\geqslant\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Proof.

The proof outline of this theorem is as follows. We consider
f_{i}:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}
, which is the restriction of
f
to its
i^{\text{th}}
output coordinate/symbol, and invoke Theorem
3.1
to obtain a list of linear functions that agree with it uniquely on most of the pairs
(\vec{\bm{\alpha}},\vec{\bm{\beta}})
that pass the linearity test. Next we argue that since linear functions agree on at most
1/q
fraction of the domain, it is possible to assume that for most
\vec{\bm{\alpha}}\in\mathsf{var}(f,\mathcal{T})
, all its corresponding pairs in
S(f,\mathcal{T})
are consistent on the
i^{\text{th}}
coordinate w.r.t. a single linear function from the list. Then, by an averaging argument, we identify
\vec{\bm{\alpha}}^{*}\in\mathsf{var}(f,\mathcal{T})
and
T_{\vec{\bm{\alpha}}^{*}}\subseteq\mathsf{var}(f,\mathcal{T})
such that (i) for all
\vec{\bm{\beta}}\in T_{\vec{\bm{\alpha}}^{*}}
, we have that
(\vec{\bm{\alpha}}^{*},\vec{\bm{\beta}})
agrees uniquely with a linear function from the list on most of the
\ell
coordinates, and (ii)
|T_{\vec{\bm{\alpha}}^{*}}|=\Omega(\varepsilon\cdot q^{d})
. Thus, by stitching together the linear functions in the list that agree with
\vec{\bm{\alpha}}^{*}
on each coordinate, we obtain the global linear function claimed in the theorem statement. We now present the formal proof.

For every
i\in[\ell]
, let
f_{i}:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a scalar respecting function obtained by only looking at the
i^{\text{th}}
coordinate output of
f
. It immediately follows that

f_{i}
passes
\mathcal{T}
with probability
\varepsilon_{i}\geqslant\varepsilon
, since
S_{f,\mathcal{T}}\subseteq S_{f_{i},\mathcal{T}}
. Moreover, each
f_{i}
is also scalar respecting, and thus from Theorem
3.1
we have that there are
r_{i}=O(1/\delta_{i}^{2})
many linear functions
c_{1}^{i},\ldots,c_{r_{i}}^{i}:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}
, such that the following holds.

\underset{(\vec{\bm{\alpha}},\vec{\bm{\beta}})\sim S_{f_{i},\mathcal{T}}}{\Pr}\left[\exists\ \text{unique }j\in[r_{i}]\text{ such that }f_{i}(\vec{\bm{\alpha}})=c_{j}^{i}(\vec{\bm{\alpha}}),f_{i}(\vec{\bm{\beta}})=c_{j}^{i}(\vec{\bm{\beta}})\right]\geqslant 1-O\left(\frac{\delta_{i}}{\varepsilon_{i}}\right).

Let
R_{i}\subseteq S_{f_{i},\mathcal{T}}
be the largest set such that for all
(\vec{\bm{\alpha}},\vec{\bm{\beta}})\in R_{i}
, the event ‘
\exists\ \text{unique }j\in[r_{i}]\text{ such that }f_{i}(\vec{\bm{\alpha}})=c_{j}^{i}(\vec{\bm{\alpha}}),f_{i}(\vec{\bm{\beta}})=c_{j}^{i}(\vec{\bm{\beta}})
’ does not happen. We note that
|R_{i}|=O\left(\frac{\delta_{i}}{\varepsilon_{i}}\right)\cdot|S_{f_{i},\mathcal{T}}|\leqslant O\left(\frac{\delta_{i}}{\varepsilon_{i}}\right)\cdot q^{2d}=O\left(\frac{\delta_{i}}{\varepsilon_{i}}\right)\cdot\frac{|S_{f,\mathcal{T}}|}{\varepsilon}
.

We set
\delta_{i}=O(\varepsilon^{4}\cdot\varepsilon_{i})
, to obtain a subset
\widetilde{S}_{f_{i},\mathcal{T}}:=S_{f,\mathcal{T}}\setminus R_{i}
, for which we know
|\widetilde{S}_{f_{i},\mathcal{T}}|\geqslant|{S}_{f,\mathcal{T}}|-|R_{i}|\geqslant(1-\varepsilon^{3})\cdot|{S}_{f,\mathcal{T}}|
. Note that for every
(\vec{\bm{\alpha}},\vec{\bm{\beta}})\in\widetilde{S}_{f_{i},\mathcal{T}}
there exists unique
j\in[r_{i}]

f_{i}(\vec{\bm{\alpha}})=c_{j}^{i}(\vec{\bm{\alpha}})
and
f_{i}(\vec{\bm{\beta}})=c_{j}^{i}(\vec{\bm{\beta}})

For every
i\in[\ell]
, let
A_{i}\subseteq\mathsf{var}(f,\mathcal{T})

\vec{\bm{\alpha}}\in A_{i}
there are two distinct
j,j^{\prime}\in[r_{i}]
such that
c_{j}^{i}(\vec{\bm{\alpha}})=c_{j^{\prime}}^{i}(\vec{\bm{\alpha}})
.
Since every two linear functions on the domain
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}

q^{d-1}
points of the domain, we have that:

|A_{i}|\leqslant{q^{d-1}}\cdot\binom{r_{i}}{2}=O\left(\frac{q^{d-1}}{\delta_{i}^{4}}\right)=O\left(\frac{q^{d-1}}{\varepsilon^{16}\cdot\varepsilon_{i}^{4}}\right)=O\left(\frac{q^{d-1}}{\varepsilon^{20}}\right)\leqslant\varepsilon^{4}\cdot q^{d},

where in the last inequality we used that
\varepsilon\gg\frac{1}{q^{1/24}}
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a directed graph where
V
is associated with
\mathsf{var}(f,\mathcal{T})
, and we have an edge from
\vec{\bm{\alpha}}
to
\vec{\bm{\beta}}
in

if and only if
(\vec{\bm{\alpha}},\vec{\bm{\beta}})\in S_{f,\mathcal{T}}
(i.e.,
|E|=|S_{f,\mathcal{T}}|
). For every
i\in[\ell]
, let
\widetilde{G}_{i}=(V,\widetilde{E}_{i})
be a subgraph of

where we have an edge from
\vec{\bm{\alpha}}
to
\vec{\bm{\beta}}
in
\widetilde{E}_{i}
if and only if
(\vec{\bm{\alpha}},\vec{\bm{\beta}})\in\widetilde{S}_{f_{i},\mathcal{T}}
and
\vec{\bm{\alpha}}\notin A_{i}

\widetilde{E}_{i}

|\widetilde{E}_{i}|\geqslant|\widetilde{S}_{f_{i},\mathcal{T}}|-\left(|A_{i}|\cdot q^{d}\right)\geqslant\left((1-\varepsilon^{3})\cdot\varepsilon-\varepsilon^{4}\right)\cdot q^{2d}\geqslant(1-\varepsilon^{2})\cdot\varepsilon\cdot q^{2d}=(1-\varepsilon^{2})\cdot|E|,

where we used that
\varepsilon\leqslant 1/2
.

We now claim the following:

Claim 3.3
.

There is a vertex
\vec{\bm{\alpha}}^{*}
in
V
and a subset
T_{\vec{\bm{\alpha}}^{*}}
of its out-neighbors in

such that the following holds:

•

|T_{\vec{\bm{\alpha}}^{*}}|\geqslant(1-\varepsilon)\cdot\varepsilon\cdot q^{d}
.

•

\forall\vec{\bm{\beta}}\in T_{\vec{\bm{\alpha}}^{*}},
we have
\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}i\sim[\ell]\end{subarray}}{\Pr}\left[\left(\vec{\bm{\alpha}}^{*},\vec{\bm{\beta}}\right)\in\widetilde{E}_{i}\right]\geqslant 1-\varepsilon
.

Assuming the above claim, we will first complete the proof of the theorem. We now define
s_{1},\ldots,s_{\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}

if there is some
\vec{\bm{\beta}}\in T_{\vec{\bm{\alpha}}^{*}}
such that
(\vec{\bm{\alpha}}^{*},\vec{\bm{\beta}})\in\widetilde{E}_{i}

s_{i}\in[r_{i}]
be the unique index such that

(the uniqueness follows from the observation that

s_{i}
to be 0.
We build a linear function

denote the all zeroes function. Then,

\forall\vec{\bm{\alpha}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d},\ c(\vec{\bm{\alpha}}):=\left(c_{s_{1}}^{1}(\vec{\bm{\alpha}}),c_{s_{2}}^{2}(\vec{\bm{\alpha}}),\ldots,c_{s_{\ell}}^{\ell}(\vec{\bm{\alpha}})\right).

For every
\vec{\bm{\beta}}\in T_{\vec{\bm{\alpha}}^{\ast}}

\|f(\vec{\bm{\beta}})-c(\vec{\bm{\beta}})\|\leqslant\varepsilon.

To see this, note that for every

such that
(\vec{\bm{\alpha}}^{*},\vec{\bm{\beta}})\in\widetilde{E}_{i}

agrees with

on

\|f(\vec{\bm{\beta}})-c(\vec{\bm{\beta}})\|\leqslant 1-\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}i\sim[\ell]\end{subarray}}{\Pr}\left[\left(\vec{\bm{\alpha}}^{*},\vec{\bm{\beta}}\right)\in\widetilde{E}_{i}\right]\leqslant\varepsilon\leqslant\tau.

Finally, we have that

\displaystyle\underset{\vec{\bm{\alpha}}\sim\mathsf{var}(f,\mathcal{T})}{\Pr}\left[\|f(\vec{\bm{\alpha}})-c(\vec{\bm{\alpha}})\|\leqslant\tau\right]\geqslant\frac{|T_{\vec{\bm{\alpha}}^{\ast}}|}{|\mathsf{var}(f,\mathcal{T})|}\geqslant(1-\varepsilon)\cdot\varepsilon\geqslant\frac{\varepsilon}{2},

where in the last inequality we used that
\varepsilon\leqslant 1/2
. Thus, we return to the proof of Claim

Proof of Claim

.

Recall that for all
i\in[\ell]

|\widetilde{E}_{i}|\geqslant(1-\varepsilon^{2})\cdot|E|

Let

Let
\widetilde{G}
be the subgraph of

\widetilde{E}

\vec{\bm{\alpha}}^{*}

whose degree is

of neighbors of

in the claim statement is exactly the neighbors of
\vec{\bm{\alpha}}^{*}
in

and we note that for every
\vec{\bm{\beta}}\in T_{\vec{\bm{\alpha}}^{*}}

\underset{i\in[\ell]}{\Pr}[(\vec{\bm{\alpha}}^{*},\vec{\bm{\beta}})\in\widetilde{E}_{i}]\geqslant 1-\varepsilon
.

4
Almost Polynomial Factor FPT Inapproximability of k-Clique

In this section we prove Theorem
1.1
. More precisely, we prove the following.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be the set of all prime numbers.
For every increasing
5
computable function
F:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that
\forall k\in\mathbb{N},\ F(k)\leqslant 1+\lfloor\log k\rfloor
, there exist computable functions
\Lambda:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
and
\hat{q}:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{P}
such that the following holds. For every fixed parameter
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there is a randomized reduction running in
\Lambda(k)^{O(1)}\cdot\text{poly}(n)
time which given an instance
(U_{1},U_{2},\ldots,U_{k})
of
k
-vector sum as input, where for all
i\in[k]
we have that
U_{i}
is a collection of
n
vectors in
\mathbb{F}_{\hat{q}(k)}^{O(k^{2}\log n)}
, outputs a graph
G
such that the following holds.

Completeness:

If there exist
\vec{u}_{1}\in U_{1},\ldots,\vec{u}_{k}\in U_{k}
such that

\sum_{i\in[k]}\vec{u}_{i}=\vec{0},

then, there is a clique in
G
of size exactly
\Lambda(k)
.

Soundness:

If for all
\vec{u}_{1}\in U_{1},\ldots,\vec{u}_{k}\in U_{k}
we have that

\sum_{i\in[k]}\vec{u}_{i}\neq\vec{0},

then, there is no clique in
G
of size
\Lambda(k)^{1-\frac{1}{F(\Lambda(k))}}
.

Size:

The number of vertices in
G
is at most
\Lambda(k)\cdot\text{poly}(n)
.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
then follows by invoking the above theorem and noting the W[1]-hardness of
k
-Vector Sum problem (Theorem
2.2
).

The proof outline of Theorem
4.1
in the subsequent subsections is as follows. In Section
4.1
we introduce a few definitions and results which will be useful for the design and analysis of our reduction. In Section
4.2
, we outline a randomized reduction from the
k
-vector sum to the
\Lambda(k)
-clique problem. In Section

, we prove the completeness, soundness, and claims on the reduction parameters.

4.1
Notations and Definitions

In this subsection we introduce a few definitions and prove some basic results which will come in handy in the subsequent subsections.

For any finite field
\mathbb{F}
we define the operator
\langle\cdot\rangle:\mathbb{F}^{d}\times\mathbb{F}^{d}\to\mathbb{F}
(for every
d\in\mathbb{N}
) as follows. For all
\vec{a}:=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}),\vec{b}:=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{d})\in\mathbb{F}^{d}
we have

\langle\vec{a},\vec{b}\rangle=\sum_{i\in[d]}\left(a_{i}\cdot b_{i}\right),

where the sum is over
\mathbb{F}
.

Next, we define an operator
\mathcal{M}
which mimics matrix multiplication but by treating the matrices as vectors. Formally, for any field
\mathbb{F}
and
t,d\in\mathbb{N}
, we define
\mathcal{M}:\mathbb{F}^{d}\times\mathbb{F}^{t\cdot d}\to\mathbb{F}^{t}
as follows. For all
\vec{a}\in\mathbb{F}^{d},\vec{b}:=(\vec{b}_{1},\ldots,\vec{b}_{t})\in\mathbb{F}^{t\cdot d}
(where
\vec{b}_{i}\in\mathbb{F}^{d}
for all
i\in[t]
) we have:

\mathcal{M}(\vec{a},\vec{b}):=\left(\langle\vec{a},\vec{b}_{1}\rangle,\ldots,\langle\vec{a},\vec{b}_{t}\rangle\right).

We now define a linear transformation
g
that will be useful later on.
Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
q\in\mathbb{P}
. Let
B\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m}
, where
m=\Theta(k^{2}\log n)
and
|B|=n
. Let
\ell:=12\log_{q}n
. In the next subsection, we will fix
k
, set
q
to be a prime depending on
k
, and use the notations
m
and
\ell
as specified here.

Select
\ell
matrices
A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{\ell}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k\times m}
uniformly and independently at random. For every
\vec{b}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m}
, let

g(\vec{b}):=(A_{1}\vec{b},\cdots,A_{\ell}\vec{b})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k\cdot\ell}.

Let
\widetilde{B}_{r}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m}
be the
r
-sumset of
B
, i.e.,

\widetilde{B}_{r}:=\left\{\sum_{i\in[r]}\gamma_{i}\cdot\vec{b}_{i}\bigg|\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{r}\in\mathbb{F}_{q},\ \text{and }\vec{b}_{1},\ldots,\vec{b}_{r}\in B\right\}.

We next show that if
q
is large but only a function of
k
(independent of
n
), then with very high probability, the relative Hamming weight of the images of all vectors in
\widetilde{B}_{k}
under
g
is high.

Proposition 4.2
.

Suppose
q>2^{12k}
but
q=O_{k}(1)
. Then with probability at least
1-\frac{O_{k}(1)}{n^{k}}
, for every
\vec{b}\in\widetilde{B}_{k}\setminus\{\vec{0}\}
, we have that
\|g(\vec{b})\|\geqslant 2/3
.

Proof.

For every
i\in[\ell]
, let
\vec{a}_{i}^{1},\ldots,\vec{a}_{i}^{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m}
be the row vectors of
A_{i}
.
Fix
\vec{b}\in\widetilde{B}_{k}\setminus\{\vec{0}\}
.
For any fixed
i\in[\ell]
and
j\in[k]
, we have

\Pr\left[\langle\vec{a}_{i}^{j},\vec{b}\rangle\neq 0\right]=1-\frac{1}{q},

where the probability is over the selection of the random matrix row
\vec{a}_{i}^{j}
.

Next, the probability that for a fixed
\vec{b}
we have
\|g(\vec{b})\|<2/3
is upper bounded by the probability that there exists a subset
S\subseteq[\ell]\times[k]
of size
\ell k/3
such that for every

\langle\vec{a}_{i}^{j},\vec{b}\rangle=0

\Pr\left[\|g(\vec{b})\|<\frac{2}{3}\right]\leqslant\binom{\ell k}{|S|}\cdot q^{-\ell k/3}.

By union bound, the probability that for every
\vec{b}\in\widetilde{B}_{k}\setminus\{\vec{0}\}

\|g(\vec{b})\|\geqslant\frac{2}{3}

\displaystyle 1-|\widetilde{B}_{k}|\cdot\binom{\ell k}{\ell k/3}\cdot q^{-\ell k/3}.

(1)

Note that
|\widetilde{B}_{k}|\leqslant(qn)^{k}=O_{k}(1)\cdot n^{k}
,
\binom{\ell k}{\ell k/3}\leqslant 2^{\ell k}=n^{12k/\log q}<n
, and

. Thus we have expression in (

1-\frac{O_{k}(1)}{n^{k}}
.
∎

We saw above that any two vectors in
B
disagree on most coordinates under

. We see below that this continues to hold even when projected to a fixed smaller subspace.

Proposition 4.3
.

Suppose
q>2^{12k}
but

\vec{b}_{1},\vec{b}_{2}\in\widetilde{B}_{2}

\vec{a}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}
,

\displaystyle\Pr\left[\vec{a}^{T}A_{i}=\vec{\rho}^{T}\right]=\frac{1}{q^{m}},

\displaystyle\Pr\left[\langle\vec{a}^{T}A_{i},\vec{b}\rangle=\gamma\right]=\frac{1}{q}.

(2)

Next the probability that for fixed distinct

S\subseteq[\ell]

such that for every

\Pr\left[\langle\vec{a}_{1}^{T}A_{i},\vec{b}_{1}\rangle=\langle\vec{a}_{2}^{T}A_{i},\vec{b}_{2}\rangle\right]=\sum_{\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\Pr\left[\langle\vec{a}_{1}^{T}A_{i},\vec{b}_{1}\rangle=\langle\vec{a}_{2}^{T}A_{i},\vec{b}_{2}\rangle=\gamma\right]=\sum_{\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\frac{1}{q^{2}}=\frac{1}{q},

\vec{b}_{1},\vec{b}_{2}\in\widetilde{B}_{2}\setminus\{\vec{0}\}

\vec{a}_{1},\vec{a}_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}

\displaystyle 1-n^{4}\cdot q^{2k}\cdot\binom{\ell}{\ell/2}\cdot q^{-\ell/2}.

(3)

Note that
\binom{\ell}{\ell/2}\leqslant 2^{\ell}=n^{12/\log q}\leqslant n
and

. Thus, we have expression in (
3
) is lower bounded by
1-\frac{O_{k}(1)}{n}
.

Finally, we consider the case that either
\vec{b}_{1}
or
\vec{b}_{2}
is
\vec{0}
. Then the proposition amounts to proving that for every
\vec{b}\in\widetilde{B}_{2}\setminus\{\vec{0}\}
, and
\vec{a}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}\setminus\{\vec{0}\}
, we have that
\left\|\mathcal{M}\left(\vec{a},g(\vec{b})\right)\right\|\geqslant\frac{1}{2}
. For fixed
\vec{b}\in\widetilde{B}_{2}\setminus\{\vec{0}\}
and
\vec{a}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}\setminus\{\vec{0}\}
we have the probability that
\left\|\mathcal{M}\left(\vec{a},g(\vec{b})\right)\right\|<\frac{1}{2}
is upper bounded by the probability that there exists a subset
S\subseteq[\ell]

5
Open Problems

The main open problem left behind from this work is to prove the total FPT-inapproximability of the
k
-Clique problem. Apart from this open problem, we would like to highlight the following two open problems too.

Parameterized Inapproximability Hypothesis (PIH).

The PIH was putforth in
[
LRSZ20
]
and asserts that it is W[1]-hard parameterized by
k
, to decide the satisfiability of gap 2-CSP on
k
variables and alphabet size
n
. It is easy to show that assuming Gap-ETH, the above gap 2-CSP instances do not admit FPT-approximation algorithms (for example see
[
BGKM18
]
). Previously, many researchers belived that the way to obtain inapproximability results for the parameterized
k
-Clique problem must be to first resolve PIH. However, Lin
[
Lin21
]
surprisingly found a route to prove inapproximability of the
k
-Clique problem while circumventing past PIH. Nevertheless, since one may see PIH as a parameterized complexity analogue of the PCP theorem (for NP), it remains an outstanding open problem to be settled.

ETH lower bound for approximating
k
-Clique.

In
[
Lin21
]
and this paper, we are primarily interested in proving strong hardness of approximation factors for the
k
-Clique problem under the W[1]
\neq
FPT assumption. However, can we prove tighter running time lower bounds for approximating
k
-Clique problem under stronger assumptions such as ETH? For example, assuming ETH, can we rule out constant factor approximation algorithms for
k
-Clique problem running in
n^{o(k)}
time?
Both
[
Lin21
]
and this paper can only prove a time lower bound of
n^{(\log k)^{\Omega(1)}}
under ETH, for approximating the
k
-Clique to constant factors.

Appendix A
Linearity Testing over
\mathbb{F}_{q}

In this section we prove the following theorem.

Theorem A.1

(Linearity Testing)
.

[Restatement of Theorem
3.1
]
Let
q
be a prime number and
d\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
f:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a scalar respecting function. Let
\varepsilon,\delta>0
be parameters such that

\varepsilon\gg\delta\gg\frac{1}{q^{1/3}}
.
If
f
passes
\mathcal{T}
with probability
\varepsilon
, then there exists an integer
r=O(1/\delta^{2})
and

linear
functions
c_{1},\ldots,c_{r}:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}
, such that the following holds.

\underset{(\vec{\bm{\alpha}},\vec{\bm{\beta}})\sim S_{f,\mathcal{T}}}{\Pr}\left[\exists\ \text{unique }j\in[r]\text{ such that }f(\vec{\bm{\alpha}})=c_{j}(\vec{\bm{\alpha}}),f(\vec{\bm{\beta}})=c_{j}(\vec{\bm{\beta}})\right]\geqslant 1-O\left(\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}\right).

Before we prove the above theorem, we note some basic facts in Fourier analysis.
Let
\omega:=e^{2\pi i/q}
be the complex
q^{\text{th}}
root of unity and
\Omega:=\{1,\omega,\ldots,\omega^{q-1}\}
.
In this section, we are interested in the complex vector space of all complex valued functions on
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
, equipped with the inner product,

\langle g_{1},g_{2}\rangle:=\underset{\vec{\bm{\alpha}}\in F_{q}^{d}}{\mathbb{E}}[g_{1}(\vec{\bm{\alpha}})\overline{g_{2}(\vec{\bm{\alpha}})}].

Fact A.2
.

For
z\in\Omega
we have
\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}z^{j}=q
if
z=1
and
\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}z^{j}=0
if
z\neq 1
.

Let
g:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}\to\Omega
be a function which is scalar respecting (i.e.
g(j\vec{\bm{\alpha}})=g(\vec{\bm{\alpha}})^{j}
). We write

g(\vec{\bm{\alpha}})=\underset{\vec{\bm{\rho}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}}{\sum}\hat{g}(\vec{\bm{\rho}})\chi_{\vec{\bm{\rho}}}(\vec{\bm{\alpha}}),

where
\hat{g}:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
is the Fourier transform of
g
. Here

\chi_{\vec{\bm{\rho}}}(\vec{\bm{\alpha}})=\omega^{\vec{\bm{\rho}}\cdot\vec{\bm{\alpha}}}
are the Fourier characters (
\vec{\bm{\rho}}\cdot\vec{\bm{\alpha}}
is the inner product over
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
)
and we have:

\forall\vec{\bm{\rho}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d},\ \hat{g}(\vec{\bm{\rho}}):=\langle g,\chi_{\vec{\bm{\rho}}}\rangle.

By Parseval, we have
\sum_{\vec{\bm{\rho}}}|\hat{g}(\vec{\bm{\rho}})|^{2}=1
.
Next we show that the Fourier coefficients are all real numbers for scalar respecting functions

Proposition A.3

For every
\vec{\bm{\rho}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}

Proof.

We note that:

We used the fact that
g
is scalar respecting and that the distribution of

\vec{\bm{\alpha}}
and
j\vec{\bm{\alpha}}
is the same for

. The proposition follows by noting that
the inner sum is always a real number, either
q-1
or
-1
.
∎

We note that the agreement of a function

Proposition A.4

\displaystyle=\underset{\vec{\bm{\alpha}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{1}{q}\cdot\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}(g(\vec{\bm{\alpha}})\overline{\chi_{\vec{\bm{\rho}}}(\vec{\bm{\alpha}})})^{j}\right]

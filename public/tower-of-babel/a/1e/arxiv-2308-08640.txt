Non-Rigid Designators in Epistemic and Temporal Free Description Logics (Extended Version)
Alessandro Artale, Andrea Mazzullo

Abstract
Definite descriptions, such as ‘the smallest planet in the Solar System’, have been recently recognised as semantically transparent devices for object identification in knowledge representation formalisms. Along with individual names, they have been introduced also in the context of description logic languages, enriching the expressivity of standard nominal constructors. Moreover, in the first-order modal logic literature, definite descriptions have been widely investigated for their non-rigid behaviour, which allows them to denote different objects at different states. In this direction, we introduce epistemic and temporal extensions of standard description logics, with nominals and the universal role, additionally equipped with definite descriptions constructors. Regarding names and descriptions, in these languages we allow for: possible lack of denotation , ensured by partial models, coming from free logic semantics as a generalisation of the classical ones; and non-rigid designation features, obtained by assigning to terms distinct values across states, as opposed to the standard rigidity condition on individual expressions. In the absence of the rigid designator assumption, we show that the satisfiability problem for epistemic free description logics is NExpTime -complete, while satisfiability for temporal free description logics over linear time structures is undecidable.

Introduction

Definite descriptions
,
like ‘the smallest planet in the Solar System’,
are expressions having form ‘the
x
such that
\varphi
’. Together with
individual names
,
such as ‘Mercury’,
they are used as
referring expressions
to identify objects in a given domain
Borgida et al. 2016
;
Borgida et al. 2017
;
Toman and Weddell 2018
.
Definite description and individual names can also
fail to denote
any object at all, as in the cases of the definite description ‘the planet between Mercury and the Sun’ or the individual name ‘Vulcan’.
Formal accounts that address these aspects and still admit definite descriptions as genuine terms of the language, on a par with individual names, are usually based on so-called
free logics

Bencivenga 2002
;
Lehmann 2002
;
Indrzejczak 2021
;
Indrzejczak and Zawidzki 2021
.
These are in contrast with classical logic approaches, in which individual names are assumed to always designate, and where definite descriptions are paraphrased in terms of sentences expressing existence and uniqueness conditions (an approach dating back to Russell
Russell 1905
).
Recently,
definite descriptions have been introduced into description logic (DL) formalisms
Neuhaus et al. 2020
;
Artale et al. 2020
;
Artale et al. 2021
, by enriching standard languages with nominals like
\smash{\mathcal{ALCO}}
and
\smash{\mathcal{ELO}}
.
In particular, the DLs
\smash{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}
and
\smash{\mathcal{ELO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}
from
Artale et al. 2020
;
Artale et al. 2021
, which include the universal role and are based on a free logic semantics allowing for non-denoting terms, have been shown to have, respectively,
ExpTime
-complete ontology satisfiability and
PTime
-complete subsumption checking problem, hence matching the complexities of the classical
DL counterparts.

In addition, expressions referring to the same object cannot always be mutually substituted in a sentence while preserving the truth value.
For instance, even if everybody is aware that Mercury is very close to the Sun, not everyone thereby knows that also the smallest planet in the Solar System is very close to the Sun, despite the fact that ‘the smallest planet in the Solar System’ and ‘Mercury’ denote the same object.
Indeed,
modal
settings, like the temporal or the epistemic ones, are

referentially opaque
contexts, where the
intension
(i.e., the meaning) of a term might not coincide with its
extension
(that is, its referent)
Fitting 2004
.
In these cases, referring expressions can behave as
non-rigid designators
, meaning that they can denote different individuals across different states (epistemic alternatives, instants of time, etc.).

For example, in an epistemic scenario, ‘the smallest planet’ can be conceived as denoting another astronomical object, by someone unaware of its actual reference to Mercury. Similarly, on a temporal scale, ‘the smallest planet’ refers to Mercury nowadays, but denoted Pluto less than twenty years ago
Fitting and Mendelsohn 2012
.
Due to this problematic interplay between designation and modalities,
non-rigid descriptions and names
have been widely investigated in first-order modal and temporal logics
Cocchiarella 1984
;
Garson 2001
;
Braüner and Ghilardi 2007
;
Kröger and Merz 2008
;
Fitting and Mendelsohn 2012
;
Corsi and Orlandelli 2013
;
Indrzejczak 2020
;
Orlandelli 2021
,
as
individual concepts
or
flexible terms

capable of taking different values across states.
However, despite the wide body of research both on temporal
Wolter and Zakharyaschev 1998
;
Artale and Franconi 2005
;
Lutz et al. 2008
and epistemic
Donini et al. 1998
;
Calvanese et al. 2008
;
Console and Lenzerini 2020
extensions,
non-rigid designators have received, to the best of our knowledge,
little attention in modal DLs. In an epistemic DL context, non-rigid
individual names appear in
Mehdi and Rudolph 2011
, under an approach that
involves abstract individual names interpreted on an infinite common
domain, but without definite descriptions.

In this paper, we extend the free DLs proposed for the non-modal case
in
Artale et al. 2020
;
Artale et al. 2021
, by: (i) adding
epistemic
modalities
, such as
\Box
(
box
, read as ‘it is known
that’), or
temporal
ones, like
\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}
(
until
)applicable both to formulas and concepts of the
language; (ii) introducing nominals built from
definite
descriptions
of the form
\iota C
(read as ‘the object that is

C
’), where
C
is a concept, alongside the standard ones based on

individual names
;
(iii) dropping the
rigid designator assumption
, hence allowing
terms to behave as flexible individual concepts across states. We
study the complexity of formula satisfiability, showing that, without
the rigid designator assumption, this problem for epistemic free DLs
is
NExpTime
-complete (same as the logic
\mathbf{S5}\times\mathbf{S5}

(
Gabbay et al. 2003
, Theorem
5.26)
), whereas it becomes undecidable for temporal free
DLs interpreted on linear time structures (while it is
decidable without definite descriptions and with the RDA
(
Gabbay et al. 2003
, Theorem
14.12)
).

Section
2
provides the necessary background on the
epistemic and temporal free DLs introduced in this paper. In
Section
3
, we motivate with examples the
syntactical and semantical choices for these
languages. Section
4
studies the complexity of
formula satisfiability in epistemic free DLs, while
Section
5
focuses on the undecidability of temporal
free DLs over time flows consisting of finite or infinite traces.
Finally, Section
6
concludes the paper, discussing open
problems and future research directions.

2
Epistemic and Temporal Free Description Logics

In this section, we introduce the syntax of

\smash{\smash{\mathcal{ML}}_{\smash{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}}}
and
\smash{\smash{\mathcal{TL}}_{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}}
, respectively a modal and a temporal
extension of the free DL
\smash{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}
language
Artale et al. 2020
;
Artale et al. 2021
, as well as their semantics based on epistemic and temporal frames, respectively.

2.1
Epistemic free description logics

The

\smash{\smash{\mathcal{ML}}_{\smash{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}}}

language
is a modalised extension of the free DL

\smash{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}

language.
Let
{\sf N_{C}}
,
{\sf N_{R}}
and
{\sf N_{I}}
be countably infinite and pairwise disjoint sets
of
concept names
,
role names
, and
individual
names
, respectively. The
\smash{\smash{\mathcal{ML}}_{\smash{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}}}

terms
and

concepts
are defined as:

\displaystyle\tau::=a\mid\smash{\iota}C,\qquad C::=A\mid\{\tau\}\mid\lnot C\mid C\sqcap C\mid\exists r.C\mid\exists u.C\mid\Diamond C

where
a\in{\sf N_{I}}
,
A\in{\sf N_{C}}
,
r\in{\sf N_{R}}
,
and
u
is the

universal role
.
A term of the form
\smash{\iota}C
is called a
definite
description
, with the concept
C
being the
body of

\smash{\iota}C
, and a concept
\{\tau\}
is called a
(term)
nominal
. All the usual syntactic abbreviations are assumed:

\bot=A\sqcap\lnot A
,
\top=\lnot\bot
,

C\sqcup D=\lnot(\lnot C\sqcap\lnot D)
,

C\Rightarrow D=\lnot C\sqcup D
,

\forall s.C=\lnot\exists s.\lnot C
, with

s\in{\sf N_{R}}\cup\{u\}
, and
\Box C=\lnot\Diamond\lnot C
. We
will consider also the
reflexive diamond
operator,

\Diamond^{+}C=C\sqcup\Diamond C
, and the
reflexive box

operator,
\Box^{+}C=C\sqcap\Box C
.

An
\smash{\smash{\mathcal{ML}}_{\smash{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}}}
axiom
, denoted as
\alpha
, is either a

concept inclusion
(
CI
) of the form
C\sqsubseteq D
,
or an
\smash{\smash{\mathcal{ML}}_{\smash{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}}}
assertion
of the form
C(\tau)
or

r(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})
, where
C,D
are concepts,
r\in{\sf N_{R}}
, and

\tau,\tau_{1},\tau_{2}
are terms.
An
\smash{\smash{\mathcal{ML}}_{\smash{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}}}
formula
is an expression of the following form:

\varphi::=(\alpha)\mid\neg\varphi\mid\varphi\land\varphi\mid\Diamond\varphi

Given a
frame

\mathfrak{F}=(W,\lhd)
, with
W
being a non-empty
set of
worlds
(or
states
) and

\lhd\subseteq W\times W
being a binary
accessibility
relation
on
W
, a
partial modal interpretation
based on

\mathfrak{F}
is a triple
\mathfrak{M}=(\mathfrak{F},\Delta,\mathcal{I})
, where:
\mathfrak{F}
is
the frame of
\mathfrak{M}
;
\Delta
is a non-empty set, called the

domain
of
\mathfrak{M}
(we adopt the so-called
constant domain
assumption

Gabbay et al. 2003
); and
\mathcal{I}
is a function associating
with every
w\in W
a
partial interpretation

\mathcal{I}_{w}=(\Delta,\cdot^{\mathcal{I}_{w}})
that maps every
A\in{\sf N_{C}}
to
a subset of
\Delta
, every
r\in{\sf N_{R}}
to a subset of

\Delta\times\Delta
, the universal role
u
to the set

\Delta\times\Delta
itself, and every
a
in a
subset
of

{\sf N_{I}}
to an element in
\Delta
. In other words, every

\cdot^{\mathcal{I}_{w}}
is a total function on
{\sf N_{C}}\cup{\sf N_{R}}
and a

partia
l function on
{\sf N_{I}}
.
We say that

\mathfrak{M}=(\mathfrak{F},\Delta,\mathcal{I})
is a
total modal
interpretation
if every
\mathcal{I}_{w}
, with
w\in W
, is a
total

interpretation,
meaning that
\cdot^{\mathcal{I}_{w}}
is defined as above, except that it maps
every

a\in{\sf N_{I}}
to an element of
\Delta
.

Definition 1
.

Given
\mathfrak{M}=(\mathfrak{F},\Delta,\mathcal{I})
, with
\mathfrak{F}=(W,\lhd)
, we
say that
\mathfrak{M}
satisfies the
rigid designator assumption

(
RDA
) if, for every individual name
a\in{\sf N_{I}}
and every

w,v\in W
,
the following condition holds:
if
a^{\mathcal{I}_{w}}
is defined, then

a^{\mathcal{I}_{w}}=a^{\mathcal{I}_{v}}
, i.e.,
a
is a
rigid
designator
.
An individual name
a\in{\sf N_{I}}
is said
to
denote in
\mathcal{I}_{w}
if
a^{\mathcal{I}_{w}}
is defined,
and we say that it
denotes in
\mathfrak{M}
if
a
denotes in
\mathcal{I}_{w}
, for some
w\in W
.
Moreover,
a
is called a
ghost in

\mathfrak{M}
if,
for every
w\in W
,
a
does not denote in
\mathcal{I}_{w}
.

Remark 1
.

Dropping the RDA is the most general
assumption, since rigid designators can be enforced by the CI

\Diamond^{+}\{a\}\sqsubseteq\Box^{+}\{a\}
. Moreover, partial interpretations are a generalisation of the classical ones: an individual
can be forced to denote at some state (i.e., not being a ghost) with the CI

\top\sqsubseteq\Diamond^{+}\exists u.\{a\}
, and to denote at all states with the formula
\Box^{+}(\top\sqsubseteq\exists u.\{a\})
.
Note that a ghost individual is vacuously rigid.

Given

\mathfrak{M}=(\mathfrak{F},\Delta,\mathcal{I})
, with
\mathfrak{F}=(W,\lhd)
,
and a
world
w\in W
,
we
define the
value

\tau^{\mathcal{I}_{w}}
of a term
\tau
in
w
as
a^{\mathcal{I}_{w}}
, if
\tau=a
, and as follows, for
\tau=\smash{\iota}C
:

\displaystyle(\smash{\iota}C)^{\mathcal{I}_{w}}=\begin{cases}d,&\text{if}\ C^{\mathcal{I}_{w}}=\{d\},\ \text{for some}\ d\in\Delta;\\
\text{undefined},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

As for the
extension
of a concept
C
in
w
,
C^{\mathcal{I}_{w}}
is
as usual with the following additions:

\displaystyle(\Diamond C)^{\mathcal{I}_{w}}

\displaystyle=\{d\in\Delta\mid\exists v\in W,w\lhd v\colon d\in C^{\mathcal{I}_{v}}\},\quad\{\tau\}^{\mathcal{I}_{w}}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\{\tau^{\mathcal{I}_{w}}\},&\text{if $\tau$ denotes in $\mathcal{I}_{w}$},\\
\,\emptyset,&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

where a term
\tau
is said to
denote
in
\mathcal{I}_{w}
if

\tau^{\mathcal{I}_{w}}
is defined.
A concept
C
is
satisfied at
w
of
\mathfrak{M}
if

C^{\mathcal{I}_{w}}\neq\emptyset
. An

\smash{\smash{\mathcal{ML}}_{\smash{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}}}
formula
\varphi
is satisfied at
w
of
\mathfrak{M}
,
written
\mathfrak{M},w\models\varphi
, when:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M},w\models C\sqsubseteq D

\displaystyle\text{ \, iff \, }C^{\mathcal{I}_{w}}\subseteq D^{\mathcal{I}_{w}},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M},w\models C(\tau)

\displaystyle\text{ \, iff \, }\tau~\text{denotes in}~{\mathcal{I}_{w}}~\text{and}~\tau^{\mathcal{I}_{w}}\in C^{\mathcal{I}_{w}},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M},w\models r(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})

\displaystyle\text{ \, iff \, }\tau_{1},\tau_{2}~\text{denotes in}~{\mathcal{I}_{w}}~\text{and}~(\tau_{1}^{\mathcal{I}_{w}},\tau_{2}^{\mathcal{I}_{w}})\in r^{\mathcal{I}_{w}},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M},w\models\Diamond\psi

\displaystyle\text{ \, iff \, }\exists v\in W,w\lhd v\colon\mathfrak{M},v\models\psi,

together with the usual interpretation of Boolean operators. An

\smash{\smash{\mathcal{ML}}_{\smash{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}}}
formula
\varphi
is
satisfied in
\mathfrak{M}
if there
exists a world
w
in
\mathfrak{M}
such that
\mathfrak{M},w\models\varphi
, and it
is
partial
(
total
)
satisfiable
if there is a
partial (total) modal interpretation
\mathfrak{M}
such that
\varphi
is
satisfied in
\mathfrak{M}
.

2.2
Temporal free description logics

For the temporal DL language
\smash{\smash{\mathcal{TL}}_{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}}
, we build
\smash{\smash{\mathcal{TL}}_{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}}

terms
,
concepts
, and
formulas
similarly to the

\smash{\smash{\mathcal{ML}}_{\smash{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}}}
case, by using the temporal operator
until
,

\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}
, for the construction of concepts,
C\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}D
, and
formulas,
\varphi\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}\psi
.

We call
flow of time
a frame
\mathfrak{F}=(T,<)
, where
T
is a
non-empty set of
instants
and
<\ \subseteq T\times T
is a
strict linear order on
T
. A
partial temporal interpretation
,
or
partial trace
, based on a flow of time
\mathfrak{F}
, is a triple

\mathfrak{M}=(\mathfrak{F},\Delta,\mathcal{I})
, defined as in the modal case. We
similarly define the notion of
total trace
. If
\mathfrak{M}
is based
on the flow of time
(\mathbb{N},<)
, where
<
is the natural strict
linear order on
\mathbb{N}
, we call it an
infinite trace
, and
we often denote it (with an abuse of notation) by

\mathfrak{M}=(\Delta,(\mathcal{I}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}})
; whereas, if it is
based on
(\{0,\ldots,n\},<)
, with
n\in\mathbb{N}
, it is
called a
finite trace
, and it is simply denoted by

\mathfrak{M}=(\Delta,(\mathcal{I}_{t})_{t\in T})
, with
T=[0,n]
. Given a partial trace
\mathfrak{M}=(\mathfrak{F},\Delta,\mathcal{I})
, with

\mathfrak{F}=(T,<)
and
t\in T
(that we call an
instant of

\mathfrak{M}
), the
value
of an
\smash{\smash{\mathcal{TL}}_{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}}
term
\tau
at
t
, and
the
extension
of an
\smash{\smash{\mathcal{TL}}_{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}}
concept
C
at
t
, are defined
as for the modal case, by replacing the semantics of the
\Diamond

modal operator with the following one for the
\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}
temporal
operator:

\displaystyle(C\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}D)^{\mathcal{I}_{t}}=\{d\in\Delta\mid\text{there is}\ u\in T,t<u\colon d\in D^{\mathcal{I}_{u}}\ \text{and, for all}\ v\in(t,u),d\in C^{\mathcal{I}_{v}}\}.

Similarly, the
satisfaction
of a
\smash{\smash{\mathcal{TL}}_{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}}
formula
\varphi
at

t
of
\mathfrak{M}
is defined as for the modal case, with the following
semantics of
\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}
replacing the one for the

\Diamond
operator:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M},t\models\varphi\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}\psi\ \text{iff}\ \text{there is}\ u\in T,t<u\colon\mathfrak{M},u\models\psi\ \text{and, for all $v\in(t,u)$,}\ \mathfrak{M},v\models\varphi.

As usual, we use the until operator to define the other temporal
operators, as follows.
For concepts:
(strong) next
operator,

{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\text{\scriptsize{$\bigcirc$}}}}C=\bot\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}C
;
diamond
operator,

\Diamond C=\top\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}C
; and
box
operator,

\Box C=\lnot\Diamond\lnot C
;
reflexive diamond
operator,

\Diamond^{+}C=C\sqcup\Diamond C
;
reflexive box
operator,

\Box^{+}C=C\sqcap\Box C
. Similar abbreviations are used for
formulas.

We say that a
\smash{\smash{\mathcal{TL}}_{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}}
formula
\varphi
(respectively, a concept
C
)
is
satisfiable on partial (total) traces
, if
\varphi

(respectively,
C
) is satisfied at time
0
in some partial (total)
trace
\mathfrak{M}
.

Remark 2
.

Assertions are syntactic sugar, since
C(\tau)
and

r(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})
are captured by the following CIs, respectively:

\top\sqsubseteq\exists u.\{\tau\},\{\tau\}\sqsubseteq C
;
and

\top\sqsubseteq\exists u.\{\tau_{1}\},\{\tau_{1}\}\sqsubseteq\exists r.\{\tau_{2}\}
. To avoid ambiguities, we use
parentheses when applying Boolean or modal operators to
assertions. Thus, for instance, the formulas
\lnot(C(\tau))
and

\Diamond(C(\tau))
abbreviate, respectively,

\lnot(\top\sqsubseteq\exists u.\{\tau\}\land\{\tau\}\sqsubseteq C)
and

\Diamond(\top\sqsubseteq\exists u.\{\tau\}\land\{\tau\}\sqsubseteq C)
, whereas the assertions
\lnot C(\tau)
and

\Diamond C(\tau)
stand, respectively, for

\top\sqsubseteq\exists u.\{\tau\}\land\{\tau\}\sqsubseteq\lnot C
and

\top\sqsubseteq\exists u.\{\tau\}\land\{\tau\}\sqsubseteq\Diamond C
.
Finally, as already observed for
\smash{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}

Artale et al. 2021
,
we point out that formulas are just syntactic sugar in
\smash{\smash{\mathcal{ML}}_{\smash{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}}}
,
since a CI
C\sqsubseteq D
can be internalised
Baader et al. 2003
;
Rudolph 2011
as a concept of
the form
\forall u.(C\Rightarrow D)
.

Remark 3
.

We point out a counter-intuitive behaviour of formulas
without the RDA
assumption. Let us consider the following:

(\{a\}\sqsubseteq\Box C)\land\Diamond(\{a\}\sqsubseteq\lnot C)
. This formula, while unsatisfiable if the RDA is assumed, is
satisfiable
without the RDA,
since it is
satisfied
in
a modal
interpretation that interprets the individual name
a
differently in
different states.

Formula satisfiability problems and reductions

)

, the

(

)

(

or

, respectively) is the
problem of deciding, given an
\smash{\smash{\mathcal{TL}}_{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}}
formula
\varphi
, whether there

that satisfies
\varphi
.

The
partial
(

)
\mathbf{S5}_{\smash{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}}
formula
satisfiability problem
(

or

,

on

.

The
partial
(

)
\smash{\mathbf{LTL}_{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}}

satisfiability problem
(
with
or
without the RDA
,

\smash{\smash{\mathcal{TL}}_{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}}
formula
satisfiability problem (with or without the RDA, respectively) over

\{(\mathbb{N},<)\}
. The

(

)

\smash{\mathbf{LTL}^{\boldsymbol{f}}_{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}}
formula satisfiability problem
(

or

, respectively) is the partial (total)

\smash{\smash{\mathcal{TL}}_{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}}

We first illustrate a polynomial-time reduction of formula
satisfiability on total modal or temporal interpretations without the

Lemma 1
.

Total

Motivations and examples

In the following, we provide some motivating examples to illustrate
the novel features of the modal and temporal free DL
languages introduced in the previous section.

Epistemic scenario.

We discuss here the main features of the language
\smash{\smash{\mathcal{ML}}_{\smash{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}}}

Let us consider the following individual names:
\mathsf{clark}
standing
for the ‘Clark Kent’,
\mathsf{superman}
standing for ‘Superman’, and

\mathsf{lois}
standing for ‘Lois Lane’. In a given state of an epistemic

\mathsf{clark}

captures those individuals that are

Superman, whereas
\Diamond\{\mathsf{superman}\}
would
be the set of individuals
suspectable of being

\{\mathsf{lois}\}\sqsubseteq\Box\exists\mathsf{loves}.\{\mathsf{superman}\}

\{\mathsf{lois}\}\sqsubseteq\lnot\Box\exists\mathsf{loves}.\{\smash{\iota}(\mathsf{Journalist}\sqcap\exists\mathsf{worksWith}.\{\mathsf{lois}\}\}

Temporal scenario.

In the following, we exemplify and discuss the main features of the
language
\smash{\smash{\mathcal{TL}}_{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}}

Let us consider the individual name

\Diamond^{+}\{\smash{\iota}\exists\mathsf{isGCof}.\{\mathsf{dl}\}\}

\Diamond^{+}(\{\mathsf{dl22}\}\sqcap\Diamond\{\mathsf{dl22}\})\sqsubseteq\bot

is not gost and that no objects can be the denotation

\mathsf{dl22}

To state that the (current) General Chair of DL will be one of next

({\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\text{\scriptsize{$\bigcirc$}}}}\exists{\sf isPCMof}.\{{\sf dl}\})(\{\smash{\iota}\exists{\sf isGCof}.\{{\sf dl}\}\})

4
Reasoning in epistemic free description logics

In this section, we show that the formula satisfiability problem for
the epistemic free DL
\mathbf{S5}_{\smash{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}}

is
decidable and
NExpTime
-complete.
After reducing, thanks to Lemmas
2
and
3
, satisfiability of a formula
\varphi
in
\mathbf{S5}_{\smash{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}}
to
the satisfiability of a concept
C_{\varphi}
in
\mathbf{S5}_{\smash{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}}}
, we can show
how to decide the satisfiability of the latter by adapting the technique of

quasimodels

to the case where individual names
can be left uninterpreted and do not respect the RDA.

Given an

\smash{\smash{\mathcal{ML}}_{\smash{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}}}}

, let

\mathsf{con}(C_{\varphi})
be the closure under single negation of the set of

C_{\varphi}
.
A
type
for

is a subset
{\boldsymbol{t}}
of
\mathsf{con}(C_{\varphi})

such that:

C1

\neg C\in{\boldsymbol{t}}
iff
C\not\in{\boldsymbol{t}}
, for all

\neg C\in\mathsf{con}(C_{\varphi})
;

C2

C\sqcap D\in{\boldsymbol{t}}
iff
C,D\in{\boldsymbol{t}}

C\sqcap D\in\mathsf{con}(C_{\varphi})
.

Note that there are at most exponentially many types, i.e., there are

2^{|\mathsf{con}(C_{\varphi})|}
types for
C_{\varphi}
.

A
quasistate for
C_{\varphi}
is a non-empty set
\boldsymbol{T}
of types
for
C_{\varphi}

Q1

for every
\{a\}\in\mathsf{con}(C_{\varphi})

{\boldsymbol{t}}\in\boldsymbol{T}

\{a\}\in{\boldsymbol{t}}

Q2

for every
{\boldsymbol{t}}\in\boldsymbol{T}

\exists r.C\in{\boldsymbol{t}}

{\boldsymbol{t}}^{\prime}\in\boldsymbol{T}
such
that
\{\lnot D\mid\lnot\exists r.D\in{\boldsymbol{t}}\}\cup\{C\}\subseteq{\boldsymbol{t}}^{\prime}

Q3

for every
{\boldsymbol{t}}\in\boldsymbol{T}
,
\exists u.C\in{\boldsymbol{t}}
iff
there exists
{\boldsymbol{t}}^{\prime}\in\boldsymbol{T}
such that
C\in{\boldsymbol{t}}^{\prime}
.

A
basic structure for

is a pair
(W,\boldsymbol{q})

is a non-empty set, and
\boldsymbol{q}
is a function associating with

w\in W

for

B1

there exists a world

and a type
{\boldsymbol{t}}\in\boldsymbol{q}(w^{\prime})

.

A
run through

\Diamond C\in\rho(w)
iff there exists
v\in W
such that

C\in\rho(v)
.

An
\mathbf{S5}_{\smash{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}}}

is a triple
\mathfrak{Q}=(W,\boldsymbol{q},\mathfrak{R})
, where
(W,\boldsymbol{q})
is a basic structure for

, and

for every

Reasoning in temporal free description logics

In the following, we show that temporal DLs
without RDA are undecidable, differently from the case with RDA which
has been shown to be decidable in the absence of definite
descriptions
(
Gabbay et al. 2003
, Theorem 14.12)
.
We start considering the DL
\smash{\mathbf{LTL}^{\boldsymbol{f}}_{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}}}
, obtained from
\smash{\mathbf{LTL}^{\boldsymbol{f}}_{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}}

by dropping definite descriptions. We show that, without the RDA for
individual names, the formula satisfiability problem is
undecidable. This result holds already for
total

interpretations (hence also for the

partial
case).
The undecidability is due to the interaction between temporal
operators, the universal role, and nominals interpreted non-rigidly
over time.
The main proof, that adapts an analogous one due to Degtyarev et
al.
Degtyarev et al. 2002
, goes by a reduction of the halting problem for
Minsky machines to total
\smash{\mathbf{LTL}^{\boldsymbol{f}}_{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}}}
formula satisfiability without
the RDA.

Theorem 3
.

\smash{\mathbf{LTL}^{\boldsymbol{f}}_{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}}}
formula satisfiability without
the RDA is undecidable.

Proof.

We consider the simpler case of a total
interpretation. The result is obtained by a reduction of the
(undecidable) halting problem for (two-counter) Minsky machines
starting with
0
as initial counters
values
Degtyarev et al. 2002
;
Baader et al. 2017
, that can be encoded
in

\smash{\mathbf{LTL}^{\boldsymbol{f}}_{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}}}

without
the RDA.

A
(two-counter) Minsky machine

is a pair
M=(Q,P)
, where
Q=\{q_{0},\ldots,q_{L}\}
is a set of
states
and
P=(I_{0},\ldots,I_{L-1})
is a sequence of
instructions
.
We assume
q_{0}
to be the
initial state
, and
q_{L}
to be the
halting state
.
Moreover, the instruction
I_{i}
is executed at state
q_{i}
, for
0\leq i<L
.
Each instruction
I
can have one of the following forms, where
r\in\{r_{1},r_{2}\}
is a
register
, that stores non-negative integers as values,
and
q,q^{\prime}
are states:

1.

I=+(r,q)
– add
1
to the value of register
r
and go to state
q
;

2.

I=-(r,q,q^{\prime})
– if the value of register
r
is (strictly) greater than
0
, subtract
1
to the value of
r
and go to state
q
; otherwise, go to state
q^{\prime}
.

Given a Minsky machine
M
,
a
configuration of
M
is a triple
(q,v_{r_{1}},v_{r_{2}})
, where
q
is a state of
M
and
v_{r_{1}},v_{r_{2}}\in\mathbb{N}
are the
values
of registers
r_{1},r_{2}
, respectively.
In the following, we set

\overline{r_{k}}=r_{3-k}
, for
k\in\{1,2\}
.
Given
i,j\geq 0
, we write
(q_{i},v_{r_{1}},v_{r_{2}})\Rightarrow_{M}(q_{j},v^{\prime}_{r_{1}},v^{\prime}_{r_{2}})
iff one of the following conditions hold:

•

I_{i}=+(r,q_{j})
,
v^{\prime}_{r}=v_{r}+1
and
v^{\prime}_{\overline{r}}=v_{\overline{r}}
;

•

I_{i}=-(r,q_{j},q^{\prime})
,
v_{r}>0
,
v^{\prime}_{r}=v_{r}-1
, and
v^{\prime}_{\overline{r}}=v_{\overline{r}}
;

•

I_{i}=-(r,q,q_{j})
,
v_{r}=v^{\prime}_{r}=0
,
and

v^{\prime}_{\overline{r}}=v_{\overline{r}}
.

Given an
input

(u,t)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
, the
computation of
M
on input
(u,t)
is the (unique) longest sequence of configurations

(p_{0},v^{0}_{r_{1}},v^{0}_{r_{2}})\Rightarrow_{M}(p_{1},v^{1}_{r_{1}},v^{1}_{r_{2}})\Rightarrow_{M}\ldots
,
such that
p_{0}=q_{0}
,
v^{0}_{r_{1}}=u
and
v^{0}_{r_{2}}=t
.
We say that
M

halts

on input

(0,0)
if the computation of
M
on input
(0,0)
is finite: thus, its initial configuration takes the form
(q_{0},0,0)
, while its last configuration has the form
(q_{L},v_{r_{1}},v_{r_{2}})
.
The
halting problem for Minsky machines
is the problem of deciding, given a Minsky machine
M
, whether
M
halts on input
(0,0)
.
This problem is known to be undecidable
Degtyarev et al. 2002
;
Baader et al. 2017
.

To represent the computation of a Minsky machine
M
, we use the temporal dimension to model successive configurations in the computation of
M
on input
(0,0)
. We introduce a concept name
Q_{i}
, for each state
q_{i}
of
M
. Concept names
R_{1},R_{2}
are used to represent the registers
r_{1},r_{2}
, respectively: the cardinality of their extensions at a given instant
will capture the values
of the registers
at one step
of the computation.
The incrementation, respectively, the decrementation, by one unit to the value of register
r_{k}

is modelled by requiring the extensions of
concepts
\lnot R_{k}\sqcap{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\text{\scriptsize{$\bigcirc$}}}}R_{k}
, respectively
R_{k}\sqcap{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\text{\scriptsize{$\bigcirc$}}}}\lnot R_{k}
, to be included in those of nominals
\{a_{r_{k}}\}
and
\{b_{r_{k}}\}
, respectively.
Since the individual names
a_{r_{k}}
and
b_{r_{k}}
are interpreted
non-rigidly
,
the (unique) element added or subtracted from the extension of
R_{k}
is forced to vary over time.
In the following, for
k\in\{1,2\}
, we set
\overline{R_{k}}=R_{3-k}
.

The initial configuration
(q_{0},0,0)
of
M
, together with the assumptions that at each configuration the machine is in exactly one state, and that the halting state is reached only at the last configuration, are represented by the conjunction of the following formulas (recall that
\textit{last}:=\Box\bot
):

(\textbf{S1})

R_{1}\sqsubseteq\bot
;

(\textbf{S2})

R_{2}\sqsubseteq\bot
;

(\textbf{S3})

\top\sqsubseteq Q_{0}
;

(\textbf{S4})

\Box^{+}(\top\sqsubseteq\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{L}Q_{i})
;

(\textbf{S5})

\bigwedge_{i=1}^{L}\Box^{+}(Q_{i}\sqsubseteq\bigsqcap_{j\neq i}\lnot Q_{j})
;

(\textbf{S6})

\Box^{+}(Q_{L}\equiv\textit{last})
.

Instructions of the form
I_{i}=+(r_{k},q_{j})
are represented by the conjunction of the following formulas:

(\textbf{I1})

\Box^{+}(Q_{i}\sqsubseteq\exists u.(\lnot R_{k}\sqcap{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\text{\scriptsize{$\bigcirc$}}}}R_{k}))
;

(\textbf{I2})

\Box^{+}(Q_{i}\sqsubseteq\forall u.(\lnot R_{k}\sqcap{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\text{\scriptsize{$\bigcirc$}}}}R_{k}\Rightarrow\{a_{r_{k}}\}))
;

(\textbf{I3})

\Box^{+}(Q_{i}\sqsubseteq\forall u.(R_{k}\Rightarrow{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\text{\scriptsize{$\bigcirc$}}}}R_{k}))
;

(\textbf{I4})

\Box^{+}(Q_{i}\sqsubseteq\forall u.(\overline{R_{k}}\Leftrightarrow{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\text{\scriptsize{$\bigcirc$}}}}\overline{R_{k}}))
;

(\textbf{I5})

\Box^{+}(Q_{i}\sqsubseteq{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\text{\scriptsize{$\bigcirc$}}}}Q_{j})
.

Instructions of the form
I_{i}=-(r_{k},q_{j},q_{h})
are
represented by the conjunction of the following formulas:

(\textbf{D1})

\Box^{+}(Q_{i}\sqcap\exists u.R_{k}\sqsubseteq\exists u.(R_{k}\sqcap{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\text{\scriptsize{$\bigcirc$}}}}\lnot R_{k}))
;

(\textbf{D2})

\Box^{+}(Q_{i}\sqcap\exists u.R_{k}\sqsubseteq\forall u.(R_{k}\sqcap{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\text{\scriptsize{$\bigcirc$}}}}\lnot R_{k}\Rightarrow\{b_{r_{k}}\}))
;

(\textbf{D3})

\Box^{+}(Q_{i}\sqcap\exists u.R_{k}\sqsubseteq\forall u.({\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\text{\scriptsize{$\bigcirc$}}}}R_{k}\Rightarrow R_{k}))
;

(\textbf{D4})

\Box^{+}(Q_{i}\sqcap\exists u.R_{k}\sqsubseteq\forall u.(\overline{R_{k}}\Leftrightarrow{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\text{\scriptsize{$\bigcirc$}}}}\overline{R_{k}}))
;

(\textbf{D5})

\Box^{+}(Q_{i}\sqcap\lnot\exists u.R_{k}\sqsubseteq\forall u.({R_{1}}\Leftrightarrow{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\text{\scriptsize{$\bigcirc$}}}}{R_{1}})\sqcap\forall u.({R_{2}}\Leftrightarrow{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\text{\scriptsize{$\bigcirc$}}}}{R_{2}}))
;

(\textbf{D6})

\Box^{+}(Q_{i}\sqcap\exists u.R_{k}\sqsubseteq{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\text{\scriptsize{$\bigcirc$}}}}Q_{j})
;

(\textbf{D7})

\Box^{+}(Q_{i}\sqcap\lnot\exists u.R_{k}\sqsubseteq{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\text{\scriptsize{$\bigcirc$}}}}Q_{h})
.

Let
\chi^{M}
be the conjunction of the CIs above, for
0\leq i<L
. We require the following claim.

Claim 2

A
Minsky machine

on input
(0,0)

is satisfiable
on
finite

without the RDA.

\smash{\mathbf{LTL}^{\boldsymbol{f}}_{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}}}

is satisfiable on finite, respectively partial or total, traces iff
\varphi^{\prime\dagger}
is satisfiable on infinite traces, respectively partial or total (the notion of partial trace, given for
\smash{\mathbf{LTL}_{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}}}
, can be naturally extended to the
{\mathcal{T}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}}\mathcal{QL}}
case).
∎

Given that
\smash{\mathbf{LTL}_{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}}}
is a syntactic fragment of
\smash{\mathbf{LTL}_{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}}
, as an
immediate consequence of Theorem
3
and the
reductions given in Lemmas
1

and
4
, we obtain the following result, holding for
total, and hence partial, interpretations.

Theorem 4
.

\smash{\mathbf{LTL}_{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}}}
,
\smash{\mathbf{LTL}_{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}}
and
\smash{\mathbf{LTL}^{\boldsymbol{f}}_{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}}
formula satisfiability
without the RDA are undecidable.

Preserving RDA is not enough to regain
decidability for the temporal DLs with definite descriptions. Indeed,
by exploiting definite descriptions that behave non-rigidly we can
still encode incrementation and decrementation of counters’ values (in
place of non-rigid nominals), yielding the following result,
proved for total interpretations, and hence holding for
partial interpretations, too.

Corollary 1
.

\smash{\mathbf{LTL}_{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}}
and
\smash{\mathbf{LTL}^{\boldsymbol{f}}_{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}}
formula satisfiability with the RDA is undecidable.

6
Discussion and Future Work

We moved first steps in the study of modal free description logics, focussing in particular on the epistemic free DL
\mathbf{S5}_{\smash{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}}
, and on the
temporal free DLs
\smash{\mathbf{LTL}_{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}}
and
\smash{\mathbf{LTL}^{\boldsymbol{f}}_{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}}
.
Syntactically, these DLs extend the classical
\smash{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}}
, with nominals and the universal role, by including definite descriptions and
modal
or temporal operators.
Semantically, adapting ideas from the non-modal
\smash{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}
case, we interpret these DLs over modal (epistemic or temporal) interpretations
that allow for:
(i)
non-denoting
terms, that is, individual expressions that can be left uninterpreted at certain states, hence encoding “error” or “empty” values;
and
(ii)
non-rigid
designators, making individual names and definite descriptions referential devices capable of picking different objects at different states, to represent “dynamic” or “flexible” values.
The main technical results concern formula satisfiability: we show that, while this problem is
NExpTime
-complete for
\mathbf{S5}_{\smash{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}}
on epistemic frames, it becomes undecidable
both for

\smash{\mathbf{LTL}_{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}}
and
\smash{\mathbf{LTL}^{\boldsymbol{f}}_{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}}

on, respectively, infinite and finite temporal traces.

As future work, we intend to strengthen our results, as well as deepen the connections between free DLs with definite descriptions, on the one hand, and modal operators, on the other.
On the epistemic side, we are interested in:
(i) considering frames for the propositional modal logics
\mathbf{K4}
,
\mathbf{T}
,
\mathbf{S4}
, or
\mathbf{KD45}
,
to model different doxastic or epistemic attitudes that might satisfy or not the so-called
factivity
and
introspection
principles
Gabbay et al. 2003
;
(ii) investigating of non-rigid descriptions and names
in the context of
non-normal
modal DLs, such as the ones obtained from the systems
\mathbf{E}
,
\mathbf{M}
,
\mathbf{C}
, and
\mathbf{N}

Dalmonte et al. 2019
;
Dalmonte et al. 2022
;
Dalmonte et al. 2023
,
to avoid the
logical omniscience
problem (i.e., an agent knows all the logical truths and all the consequences of their background knowledge), which affects all the systems extending
\mathbf{K}

Vardi 1986
;
Vardi 1989
;
(iii) addressing less expressive DL languages, such as
\smash{\mathcal{ELO}_{\!u}^{\smash{\iota}}}
, in an epistemic setting, and connect them with the recently investigated
standpoint DL
family
Gómez Álvarez et al. 2022
;
Gómez Álvarez et al. 2023
.

On the temporal side, and in particular concerning our undecidability proofs, we observe the following.

Degtyarev et al. 2002

Degtyarev et al. 2002
use equality
to encode a one-unit addition or subtraction from a register’s value at subsequent steps of a two-counter Minsky machine computation.
In our case, the same effect is due to the interplay between non-rigid (and possibly non-denoting) nominals and the universal role.
Related results appear also in
Hampson and Kurucz 2015

Hampson and Kurucz 2015
,
where the undecidability of
one-variable first-order temporal logic with counting to two
, denoted by FOLTL
≠
, is proved.
In their setting, the
difference
existential quantifier
\exists^{\neq}x
extends the language of one-variable (monadic) first-order temporal logic: formulas like
\exists^{\neq}x\varphi
enforce that
\varphi
holds for some individual different from the one assigned to
x
.
Their undecidability proof for satisfiability of FOLTL
≠
on finite or infinite traces with constant domains is also based on an encoding of the computation of two-counter Minsky machines.

It seems thus that a form of interaction between the universal role and counting constructors (even simply up to one, as in
\smash{\mathbf{LTL}_{\mathcal{ALCO}_{\!u}}}
, with non-rigid and possibly non-denoting nominals) plays a role in these kinds of undecidability proofs.
However, it is not yet clear whether already
\smash{\mathbf{LTL}_{\mathcal{ALCO}}}
or
\smash{\mathbf{LTL}_{\mathcal{ALCO}^{\smash{\iota}}}}
satisfiability, on partial temporal interpretations and with non-rigid nominals, but
without
the universal quantifier, is undecidable.
To obtain a similar proof, one would have to suitably replace the universal role with dedicated roles for counters, while forcing them to stay “global” enough not to lose any information about the values of the counters at subsequent computation steps.
We leave this open question for future work.

Finally, we remark that the preliminary negative results presented here for temporal free DLs might not entirely undermine the use of definite descriptions on a temporal dimension.
For applications in temporal conceptual modelling and ontology-mediated query answering
Lutz et al. 2008
;
Artale et al. 2017
, it is worth exploring whether more encouraging
results can be obtained in fragments restricting the use of temporal operators (limited, e.g., to the
\Box
operator only), or constraining the DL dimension (such as the already mentioned
\smash{\mathbf{LTL}_{\mathcal{ALCO}^{\smash{\iota}}}}
, without the universal role, or lightweight members of the
TDL-Lite
family
Artale et al. 2014
).
Any of these restrictions should, of course, be designed to preserve an interesting degree of interaction between the DL constructors and the non-rigidity of names and descriptions.

Finally, we are interested in studying in this setting the complexities of other problems than formula satisfiability. Related to
interpolant
and
explicit definition existence

Artale et al. 2021
;
Artale et al. 2023
;
Kurucz et al. 2023
,
the
referring expression existence
problem
Artale et al. 2021
,
which asks whether there exists a description for an individual under an ontology and a given signature,
is of particular interest to epistemic and temporal scenarios with definite descriptions.

1.
Symmetric means of monomial functions

A
nonnegative vector
is a vector with nonnegative coordinates.
The
nonnegative octant
in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
is

\mathbf{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}=\left\{\mathbf{a}=\vectoran\in\mathbf{R}^{n}:\text{$a_{i}\geq 0$ for all $i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}$}\right\}.

A
positive vector
is a vector with positive coordinates.
The
positive octant
in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
is

\mathbf{R}^{n}_{>0}=\left\{\mathbf{x}=\vectorxn\in\mathbf{R}^{n}:\text{$x_{i}>0$ for all $i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}$}\right\}.

Every nonnegative vector
\mathbf{a}
defines the
monomial function

\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{a}}
on the positive octant as follows:
For
\mathbf{a}=\vectorsmallan\in\mathbf{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
and
\mathbf{x}=\vectorsmallxn\in\mathbf{R}^{n}_{>0}
,
let

\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{a}}=x_{1}^{a_{1}}x_{2}^{a_{2}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}.

We call
\mathbf{a}
the
exponent vector

of the monomial
\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{a}}
. Note that
\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{a}}>0
for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}_{>0}
.

Let
S_{n}
be the symmetric group.
The
symmetric mean
of the monomial function
\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{a}}
is the function

[\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{a}}]_{S_{n}}=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}x_{\sigma(1)}^{a_{1}}x_{\sigma(2)}^{a_{2}}\cdots x_{\sigma(n)}^{a_{n}}.

For every subgroup
G
of
S_{n}
, the
G
-symmetric mean

of the monomial
\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{a}}
is the function

[\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{a}}]_{G}=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\sigma\in G}x_{\sigma(1)}^{a_{1}}x_{\sigma(2)}^{a_{2}}\cdots x_{\sigma(n)}^{a_{n}}.

Let
\sigma\in S_{n}
. If
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
j=\sigma(i)
, then

x_{\sigma(i)}^{a_{i}}=x_{j}^{a_{\sigma^{-1}(j)}}

and

\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{\sigma(i)}^{a_{i}}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}^{a_{\sigma^{-1}(j)}}.

Because
\{\sigma\in G\}=\{\sigma^{-1}\in G\}
, we have

\displaystyle[\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{a}}]_{G}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\sigma\in G}\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{\sigma(i)}^{a_{i}}=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\sigma\in G}\prod_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}^{a_{\sigma^{-1}(j)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\sigma\in G}\prod_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}^{a_{\sigma(j)}}=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\sigma\in G}x_{1}^{a_{\sigma(1)}}x_{2}^{a_{\sigma(2)}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{\sigma(n)}}.

If
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}_{>0}
is the constant vector with all coordinates equal to
x
, then

(1)

[\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{a}}]_{G}=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\sigma\in G}\prod_{j=1}^{n}x^{a_{\sigma(j)}}=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\sigma\in G}x^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}}=x^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}}.

Let
G
be a subgroup of
S_{n}
and let
\mathbf{a}=\vectorsmallan
and
\mathbf{b}=\vectorsmallbn

be nonnegative vectors such that

\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{j}.

If

(2)

\left[\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{b}}\right]_{G}<\left[\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{a}}\right]_{G}

for all vectors
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}_{>0}
,
then (
2
) is called a
monomial inequality
with respect to the subgroup
G
determined by
\mathbf{a}
and
\mathbf{b}
.
A
monomial inequality
is a monomial inequality
with respect to the symmetric group
S_{n}
.
Here are some examples.
If
\mathbf{a}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}7\\
3\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathbf{R}^{2}_{\geq 0}
,
then
\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{a}}=x_{1}^{7}x_{2}^{3}
.
The
S_{2}
-symmetric mean of
\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{a}}
is

[\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{a}}]_{S_{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(x_{1}^{7}x_{2}^{3}+x_{2}^{7}x_{1}^{3}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(x_{1}^{7}x_{2}^{3}+x_{1}^{3}x_{2}^{7}\right).

If
\mathbf{b}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}6\\
4\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathbf{R}^{2}_{\geq 0}
,
then
\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{b}}=x_{1}^{6}x_{2}^{4}
and
the
S_{2}
-symmetric mean of
\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{b}}
is

[\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{b}}]_{S_{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(x_{1}^{6}x_{2}^{4}+x_{1}^{4}x_{2}^{6}\right).

It is straightforward to check that

(3)

x_{1}^{6}x_{2}^{4}+x_{1}^{4}x_{2}^{6}<x_{1}^{7}x_{2}^{3}+x_{1}^{3}x_{2}^{7}

for all nonconstant positive vectors

\mathbf{x}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right)

and so
[\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{b}}]_{S_{2}}<[\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{a}}]_{S_{2}}
is a monomial inequality.

Another example.
Associated to the vector
\mathbf{e}_{1}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1\\
0\\
\vdots\\
0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathbf{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}

is the monomial function

\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{e}_{1}}=x_{1}^{1}x_{2}^{0}\cdots x_{n}^{0}=x_{1}.

For every ordered pair of integers
(i,j)
with
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, there are
(n-1)!
permutations

\sigma\in S_{n}
with
\sigma(i)=j
.
In particular, for all
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, there are
(n-1)!
permutations

\sigma\in S_{n}
with
\sigma(1)=j
. For all
\mathbf{x}=\vectorsmallxn\in\mathbf{R}^{n}_{>0}
, we have

\displaystyle[\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{e}_{1}}]_{S_{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}x_{\sigma(1)}^{1}x_{\sigma(2)}^{0}\cdots x_{\sigma(n)}^{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}x_{\sigma(1)}=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(n-1)!x_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}.

Thus, the symmetric mean of the monomial
\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{e}_{1}}

is the
arithmetic mean
of the
positive numbers
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
.

Associated to the vector
\mathbf{f}_{n}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1/n\\
1/n\\
\vdots\\
1/n\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathbf{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}

is the monomial function

\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{f}_{n}}=x_{1}^{1/n}x_{2}^{1/n}\cdots x_{n}^{1/n}=\left(x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{n}\right)^{1/n}

which is is the
geometric mean
of the
positive numbers
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
. Because

x_{\sigma(1)}x_{\sigma(2)}\cdots x_{\sigma(n)}=x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{n}

for all
\sigma\in S_{n}
, the symmetric mean

\displaystyle[\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{f}_{n}}]_{S_{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\left(x_{\sigma(1)}x_{\sigma(2)}\cdots x_{\sigma(n)}\right)^{1/n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\left(x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{n}\right)^{1/n}

\displaystyle=\left(x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{n}\right)^{1/n}

is also the geometric mean of the numbers
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
.
The arithmetic and geometric mean inequality is equivalent to the monomial inequality

[\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{f}_{n}}]_{S_{n}}<[\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{e}_{1}}]_{S_{n}}

for all nonconstant vectors
\vectorsmallxn\in\mathbf{R}^{n}_{>0}
.

There are infinitely many ordered pairs
(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})
of nonnegative vectors such that

[\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{b}}]_{S_{n}}<[\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{a}}]_{S_{n}}

for all nonconstant vectors
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}_{>0}
.
Muirhead’s inequality (Theorem
4
)
and its converse (Theorem
5
)
classify the pairs of vectors
\mathbf{a}
and
\mathbf{b}
that determine monomial inequalities. Richard Rado
(Theorems
9
and
10
)
classified monomial inequalities with respect to a subgroup
G
of
S_{n}
.
The purpose of this paper is to prove these results.

2.
Simple inequalities

Here is a trivial fact, from which we obtain infinitely many monomial inequalities.

Theorem 1
.

The product of two real numbers is positive if and only if both numbers are positive
or both numbers are negative.

Corollary 1
.

Let
\mathbf{a}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a_{1}\\
a_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathbf{R}^{2}_{>0}
and

\mathbf{x}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathbf{R}^{2}_{>0}
.
If
x_{1}\neq x_{2}
, then

(4)

x_{1}^{a_{1}}x_{2}^{a_{2}}+x_{1}^{a_{2}}x_{2}^{a_{1}}<x_{1}^{a_{1}+a_{2}}+x_{2}^{a_{1}+a_{2}}.

If
x_{1}=x_{2}
, then

x_{1}^{a_{1}}x_{2}^{a_{2}}+x_{1}^{a_{2}}x_{2}^{a_{1}}=x_{1}^{a_{1}+a_{2}}+x_{2}^{a_{1}+a_{2}}.

Proof.

If
0<x_{2}<x_{1}
, then
x_{1}^{a_{1}}-x_{2}^{a_{1}}>0
and
x_{1}^{a_{2}}-x_{2}^{a_{2}}>0
.
If
0<x_{1}<x_{2}
, then
x_{1}^{a_{1}}-x_{2}^{a_{1}}<0
and
x_{1}^{a_{2}}-x_{2}^{a_{2}}<0
.
Thus, the numbers
x_{1}^{a_{1}}-x_{2}^{a_{1}}
and
x_{1}^{a_{2}}-x_{2}^{a_{2}}

are both positive or both negative.
Applying Theorem
1

to these numbers gives

x_{1}^{a_{1}+a_{2}}+x_{2}^{a_{1}+a_{2}}-x_{1}^{a_{1}}x_{2}^{a_{2}}-x_{1}^{a_{2}}x_{2}^{a_{1}}=(x_{1}^{a_{1}}-x_{2}^{a_{1}})(x_{1}^{a_{2}}-x_{2}^{a_{2}})>0.

This proves (
4
). If
x_{1}=x_{2}
, then

x_{1}^{a_{1}}x_{2}^{a_{2}}+x_{1}^{b}x_{2}^{a_{1}}=x_{1}^{a_{1}+a_{2}}+x_{2}^{a_{1}+a_{2}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 2
.

Let
\rho
,
\delta
, and
\Delta
satisfy the inequalities

\rho>0\qqand|\delta|<\Delta.

Let
\mathbf{x}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathbf{R}^{2}_{>0}
.
If
x_{1}\neq x_{2}
, then

(5)

x_{1}^{\rho+\delta}x_{2}^{\rho-\delta}+x_{1}^{\rho-\delta}x_{2}^{\rho+\delta}<x_{1}^{\rho+\Delta}x_{2}^{\rho-\Delta}+x_{1}^{\rho-\Delta}x_{2}^{\rho+\Delta}.

Proof.

We have
\Delta+\delta>0
and
\Delta-\delta>0
.
Applying Corollary
1

with
\mathbf{a}=\left(\begin{matrix}\Delta+\delta\\
\Delta-\delta\end{matrix}\right)\in\mathbf{R}^{2}_{>0}
gives

x_{1}^{\Delta+\delta}x_{2}^{\Delta-\delta}+x_{1}^{\Delta-\delta}x_{2}^{\Delta+\delta}<x_{1}^{2\Delta}+x_{2}^{2\Delta}.

Multiplying this inequality by
(x_{1}x_{2})^{-\Delta}
gives

x_{1}^{\delta}x_{2}^{-\delta}+x_{1}^{-\delta}x_{2}^{\delta}<x_{1}^{\Delta}x_{2}^{-\Delta}+x_{1}^{-\Delta}x_{2}^{\Delta}.

Multiplying by
(x_{1}x_{2})^{\rho}
completes the proof.
∎

For example, letting
\rho=5
,
\delta=1
, and
\Delta=2

in inequality (
5
) gives inequality (
3
).
Similarly, for all nonconstant vectors
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{R}^{2}_{>0}
, we have
the chain of inequalities

\displaystyle 2x_{1}^{5}x_{2}^{5}

\displaystyle<x_{1}^{6}x_{2}^{4}+x_{1}^{4}x_{2}^{6}<x_{1}^{7}x_{2}^{3}+x_{1}^{3}x_{2}^{7}

\displaystyle<x_{1}^{8}x_{2}^{2}+x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{8}<x_{1}^{9}x_{2}+x_{1}x_{2}^{9}<x_{1}^{10}+x_{2}^{10}.

These inequalities fail if
x_{1}=1
and
x_{2}=-1

or, more generally, if
x_{1}x_{2}\leq 0
.

Theorem 2
.

Let vectors
\mathbf{a}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a_{1}\\
a_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathbf{R}^{2}_{\geq 0}

and
\mathbf{b}=\vectorsmallb 2\in\mathbf{R}^{2}_{\geq 0}
satisfy

a_{2}<b_{2}\leq b_{1}<a_{1}\qqand a_{1}+a_{2}=b_{1}+b_{2}.

If
\mathbf{x}=\vectorsmallx 2\in\mathbf{R}^{2}_{>0}
with
x_{1}\neq x_{2}
, then

[\mathbf{x}]_{S_{2}}^{\mathbf{b}}<[\mathbf{x}]_{S_{2}}^{\mathbf{a}}
or, equivalently,

(6)

x_{1}^{b_{1}}x_{2}^{b_{2}}+x_{1}^{b_{2}}x_{2}^{b_{1}}<x_{1}^{a_{1}}x_{2}^{a_{2}}+x_{1}^{a_{2}}x_{2}^{a_{1}}.

Proof.

Let

\rho=\frac{a_{1}+a_{2}}{2}=\frac{b_{1}+b_{2}}{2}

and

\delta=\frac{b_{1}-b_{2}}{2}\qqand\Delta=\frac{a_{1}-a_{2}}{2}.

We have

\rho>0\qqand 0\leq\delta<\Delta.

Applying inequality (
5
) with

\rho+\delta=b_{1}\qqand\rho-\delta=b_{2}

and

\rho+\Delta=a_{1}\qqand\rho-\Delta=a_{2}

gives inequality (
6
).
This completes the proof.
∎

This is Muirhead’s inequality for monomials in two variables.
Inequality (
3
) is the special case

\mathbf{a}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}7\\
3\end{smallmatrix}\right)
and

\mathbf{b}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}6\\
4\end{smallmatrix}\right)
.

3.
Symmetric means of functions of
n
variables

Recall the
Kronecker delta

\delta_{i,j}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $i=j$ }\\
0&\text{if $i\neq j$ .}\end{cases}

The
standard basis
for
\mathbf{R}^{n}
is
\mathcal{E}=\{\mathbf{e}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{e}_{n}\}
,
where
\mathbf{e}_{j}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\delta_{1,j}\\
\vdots\\
\delta_{n,j}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
.
Every permutation
\sigma\in S_{n}
defines a linear operator on
\mathbf{R}^{n}
by

\sigma(\mathbf{e}_{j})=\mathbf{e}_{\sigma(j)}
.
If
\mathbf{x}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}\mathbf{e}_{j}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
, then

\sigma(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}\sigma\left(\mathbf{e}_{j}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}\mathbf{e}_{\sigma(j)}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{\sigma^{-1}(j)}\mathbf{e}_{j}

and so

(7)

\sigma\vectorxn=\left(\begin{matrix}x_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}\\
\vdots\\
x_{\sigma^{-1}(n)}\end{matrix}\right).

Let
\Omega
be a subset of
\mathbf{R}^{n}
that is closed under the action of
S_{n}
,
that is,
\mathbf{x}\in\Omega
implies
\sigma(\mathbf{x})\in\Omega
for all
\sigma\in S_{n}
.
Let
\mathcal{F}(\Omega)
be the set of real-valued functions defined on
\Omega
.

For every function
f\in\mathcal{F}(\Omega)
and every permutation
\sigma

in the symmetric group
S_{n}
, define the function
\sigma f\in\mathcal{F}(\Omega)
by

(8)

(\sigma f)(\mathbf{x})=f\left(\sigma^{-1}(\mathbf{x})\right).

For all
\sigma,\tau\in S_{n}
we have

\displaystyle\left(\tau(\sigma f)\right)(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=(\sigma f)\left(\tau^{-1}(\mathbf{x})\right)=f\left(\sigma^{-1}\left(\tau^{-1}(\mathbf{x})\right)\right)

\displaystyle=f\left(\left(\sigma^{-1}\tau^{-1}\right)(\mathbf{x})\right)=f\left(\left(\tau\sigma\right)^{-1}(\mathbf{x})\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\left(\tau\sigma\right)f\right)\left(\mathbf{x}\right).

Thus,

\left(\tau\sigma\right)f=\tau(\sigma f)

and (
8
) defines an action of the group
S_{n}

on the function space
\mathcal{F}(\Omega)
.

Let
G
be a subgroup of
S_{n}
of order
|G|
.
The
G
-symmetric mean

of the function
f

is the function
[f]_{G}\in\mathcal{F}(\Omega)
defined by

[f]_{G}=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\sigma\in G}\sigma f.

Because
G=\{\sigma^{-1}:\sigma\in G\}
, we have

\displaystyle[f]_{G}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\sigma\in G}(\sigma f)(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\sigma\in G}f\left(\sigma^{-1}(\mathbf{x})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\sigma\in G}f\left(\sigma(\mathbf{x})\right).

The
symmetric mean
of the function
f

is the
S_{n}
-symmetric mean.

Lemma 1
.

Let
G
be a subgroup of
S_{n}
and let
\tau
in
G
.
For all functions
f\in\mathcal{F}(\Omega)
,

[\tau f]_{G}=[f]_{G}

Proof.

From the identity

G\tau=\{\sigma\tau:\sigma\in G\}=G

we obtain

\displaystyle[\tau f]_{G}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\sigma\in G}\sigma(\tau f)=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\sigma\in G}(\sigma\tau)f=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\sigma\in G}\sigma f=[f]_{G}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2
.

Let
G
be a subgroup of
S_{n}
.
For every nonnegative vector
\mathbf{a}=\vectorsmallan\mathbf{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}

and every permutation
\tau\in G
,

[\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{a}}]_{G}=[\mathbf{x}^{\tau(\mathbf{a})}]_{G}.

Proof.

From (
7
) we have

\tau(\mathbf{a})=\left(\begin{matrix}a_{\tau^{-1}(1)}\\
\vdots\\
a_{\tau^{-1}(n)}\end{matrix}\right)\qqand\mathbf{x}^{\tau(\mathbf{a})}=x_{1}^{a_{\tau^{-1}(1)}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{\tau^{-1}(n)}}.

Because
G\tau=\{\sigma\tau:\sigma\in G\}=G
for all
\tau\in G
, we have

\displaystyle[\mathbf{x}^{\tau(\mathbf{a})}]_{G}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in G}x_{\sigma(1)}^{a_{\tau^{-1}(1)}}\cdots x_{\sigma(n)}^{a_{\tau^{-1}(n)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in G}x_{1}^{a_{\tau^{-1}\sigma^{-1}(1)}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{\tau^{-1}\sigma^{-1}(n)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in G}x_{1}^{a_{(\sigma\tau)^{-1}(1)}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{(\sigma\tau)^{-1}(n)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in G}x_{1}^{a_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{\sigma^{-1}(n)}}

\displaystyle=[\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{a}}]_{G}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3
.

Let
G
be a subgroup of
S_{n}
that contains the transposition
\tau=(j,k)
with
j<k
.
For every nonnegative vector
\mathbf{a}=\vectorsmallan\in\mathbf{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}

and positive vector
\mathbf{x}=\vectorsmallxn\in\mathbf{R}^{n}_{>0}
,

[\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{a}}]_{G}=\frac{1}{2|G|}\sum_{\sigma\in G}\left(x_{\sigma(j)}^{a_{j}}x_{\sigma(k)}^{a_{k}}+x_{\sigma(j)}^{a_{k}}x_{\sigma(k)}^{a_{j}}\right)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j,k\end{subarray}}^{n}x_{\sigma(i)}^{a_{i}}.

Proof.

The transposition
\rho=(j,k)
acts as follows:

\text{if}\quad\mathbf{a}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a_{1}\\
\vdots\\
a_{j-1}\\
a_{j}\\
a_{j+1}\\
\vdots\\
a_{k-1}\\
a_{k}\\
a_{k+1}\\
\vdots\\
a_{n}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\qquad\text{then}\quad\rho(\mathbf{a})=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a_{1}\\
\vdots\\
a_{j-1}\\
a_{k}\\
a_{j+1}\\
\vdots\\
a_{k-1}\\
a_{j}\\
a_{k+1}\\
\vdots\\
a_{n}\end{smallmatrix}\right).

By Lemma
2
,

\displaystyle 2[\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{a}}]_{G}

\displaystyle=[\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{a}}]_{G}+[\mathbf{x}^{\tau(\mathbf{a})}]_{G}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\sigma\in G}x_{\sigma(1)}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{\sigma(j)}^{a_{j}}\cdots x_{\sigma(k)}^{a_{k}}\cdots x_{\sigma(n)}^{a_{n}}

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt+\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\sigma\in G}x_{\sigma(1)}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{\sigma(j)}^{a_{k}}\cdots x_{\sigma(k)}^{a_{j}}\cdots x_{\sigma(n)}^{a_{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\sigma\in G}\left(x_{\sigma(j)}^{a_{j}}x_{\sigma(k)}^{a_{k}}+x_{\sigma(j)}^{a_{k}}x_{\sigma(k)}^{a_{j}}\right)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j,k\end{subarray}}^{n}x_{\sigma(i)}^{a_{i}}.

Dividing by 2 completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4
.

Let
G
be a subgroup of
S_{n}
that contains the transposition
\tau=(j,k)
with
j<k
.
Let
\mathbf{u}=\vectorsmallun
be a nonnegative vector such that
u_{j}>u_{k}
and let

\rho=\frac{u_{j}+u_{k}}{2}\qqand\Delta=\frac{u_{j}-u_{k}}{2}.

Then

0<\Delta\leq\rho.

Let

0\leq\delta<\Delta.

Define the nonnegative vector
\mathbf{v}=\vectorsmallvn
by

v_{j}=\rho+\delta\qqand v_{k}=\rho-\delta

and

v_{i}=u_{i}\qquad\text{if $i\neq j,k$}.

If
\mathbf{x}=\vectorsmallxn\in\mathbf{R}^{n}_{>0}
is a nonconstant vector such that

x_{\sigma(j)}\neq x_{\sigma(k)}
for some
\sigma\in G
, then

[\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{v}}]_{G}<[\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{u}}]_{G}.

Proof.

We have
v_{i}=u_{i}\geq 0
for
i\neq j,k
and

0\leq u_{k}=\rho-\Delta<\rho-\delta=v_{k}\leq v_{j}=\rho+\delta<\rho+\Delta=u_{j}

and so the vector is
\mathbf{v}
is nonnegative.

Let
\mathbf{x}
be a nonconstant vector in
\mathbf{R}^{n}_{>0}
.
Applying Lemma
3
, we have

\displaystyle[\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{u}}]_{G}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2|G|}\sum_{\sigma\in G}\left(x_{\sigma(j)}^{u_{j}}x_{\sigma(k)}^{u_{k}}+x_{\sigma(j)}^{u_{k}}x_{\sigma(k)}^{u_{j}}\right)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j,k\end{subarray}}^{n}x_{\sigma(i)}^{u_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2|G|}\sum_{\sigma\in G}\left(x_{\sigma(j)}^{\rho+\Delta}x_{\sigma(k)}^{\rho-\Delta}+x_{\sigma(j)}^{\rho-\Delta}x_{\sigma(k)}^{\rho+\Delta}\right)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j,k\end{subarray}}^{n}x_{\sigma(i)}^{u_{i}}

and

\displaystyle[\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{v}}]_{G}

5.

Let

be a subgroup of
S_{n}

\mathbf{a}
be
a vector in
\mathbf{R}^{n}

The
G

generated by
\mathbf{a}

\{\gamma(\mathbf{a}):\gamma\in G\}

\mathbf{R}^{n}

If the vector
\mathbf{a}

\gamma(\mathbf{a})

We recall the following separation theorem from convexity theory.

Theorem 7

Theorem 8

The following result of Richard Rado extends Muirhead’s inequality to subgroups of
S_{n}
.

Theorem 9

(Rado)
.

Let
G
be a subgroup of the symmetric group
S_{n}
.
Let
\mathbf{a}
be a nonnegative vector in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
and let
K_{G}(\mathbf{a})
be the
G
-permutohedron,
that is, the convex hull of the set of vectors
\{\gamma(\mathbf{a}):\gamma\in G\}
.
If

\mathbf{b}\in K_{G}(\mathbf{a})

then
\mathbf{b}
is nonnegative and

\left[\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{b}}\right]_{G}\leq\left[\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{a}}\right]_{G}

for all positive vectors
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{a}=\vectorsmallan\in\mathbf{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
and
\mathbf{b}=\vectorsmallbn\in K_{G}(\mathbf{a})
.
Then

\mathbf{b}=\sum_{\gamma\in G}t_{\gamma}\ \gamma(\mathbf{a})

where
t_{\gamma}\in[0,1]
for all
\gamma\in G
and

\sum_{\gamma\in G}t_{\gamma}=1
.
By (
7
), the
i
th component of the vector
\mathbf{b}
is

b_{i}=\sum_{\gamma\in G}t_{\gamma}(\gamma\mathbf{a})_{i}=\sum_{\gamma\in G}t_{\gamma}\ a_{\gamma^{-1}(i)}.

Let
\mathbf{x}=\vectorsmallxn
be a positive vector.
For every permutation
\sigma\in G
, the arithmetic and geometric mean inequality
(Theorem
8
) gives

\prod_{\gamma\in G}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{\sigma(i)}^{a_{\gamma^{-1}(i)}}\right)^{t_{\gamma}}\\
\leq\sum_{\gamma\in G}t_{\gamma}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{\sigma(i)}^{a_{\gamma^{-1}(i)}}\right)

and so

\displaystyle\left[\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{b}}\right]_{G}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\sigma\in G}\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{\sigma(i)}^{b_{i}}=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\sigma\in G}\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{\sigma(i)}^{\sum_{\gamma\in G}t_{\gamma}a_{\gamma^{-1}(i)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\sigma\in G}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{\gamma\in G}x_{\sigma(i)}^{t_{\gamma}a_{\gamma^{-1}(i)}}=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\sigma\in G}\prod_{\gamma\in G}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{\sigma(i)}^{a_{\gamma^{-1}(i)}}\right)^{t_{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\sigma\in G}\sum_{\gamma\in G}t_{\gamma}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{\sigma(i)}^{a_{\gamma^{-1}(i)}}\right)=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\gamma\in G}t_{\gamma}\sum_{\sigma\in G}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{\sigma(i)}^{a_{\gamma^{-1}(i)}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\gamma\in G}t_{\gamma}\sum_{\sigma\in G}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}x_{\sigma\gamma(j)}^{a_{j}}\right)=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\gamma\in G}t_{\gamma}\sum_{\sigma\in G}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}x_{\sigma(j)}^{a_{j}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\sigma\in G}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}x_{\sigma(j)}^{a_{j}}\right)\sum_{\gamma\in G}t_{\gamma}=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\sigma\in G}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}x_{\sigma(j)}^{a_{j}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left[\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{a}}\right]_{G}.

This completes the proof.

Next we prove the converse of Theorem
9
.

Theorem 10

(Rado)
.

Let
G
be a subgroup of the symmetric group
S_{n}
.
Let
\mathbf{a}
be a nonnegative vector in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
and let
K_{G}(\mathbf{a})
be the
G
-permutohedron.
If
\mathbf{b}
is a nonnegative vector in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
such that

\left[\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{b}}\right]_{G}\leq\left[\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{a}}\right]_{G}

for all positive vectors
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
, then

\mathbf{b}\in K_{G}(\mathbf{a}).

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{a}=\vectorsmallan
and
\mathbf{b}=\vectorsmallbn
.
We shall prove that if
\mathbf{b}\notin K_{G}(\mathbf{a})
, then there is a positive
vector
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
such that
\left[\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{b}}\right]_{G}>\left[\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{a}}\right]_{G}
.

By Theorem
7
,
if
\mathbf{b}\notin K_{G}(\mathbf{a})
, then the compact convex set
K_{G}(\mathbf{a})

and the vector
\mathbf{b}
are
strictly separated by a hyperplane
H
and so there is a nonzero linear functional

H(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}x_{i}

and scalars
c
and
\delta
with
\delta>0
such that

H(\mathbf{x})\leq c\qquad\text{for all $\mathbf{x}\in K_{G}(\mathbf{a})$}

and

H(\mathbf{b})\geq c+\delta.

For all
\gamma\in G
we have

\gamma^{-1}(\mathbf{a})=\left(\begin{matrix}a_{\gamma(1)}\\
\vdots\\
a_{\gamma(n)}\end{matrix}\right)\in K_{G}(\mathbf{a})

and so

\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}a_{\gamma^{-1}(i)}=H(\gamma(\mathbf{a}))\leq c\leq H(\mathbf{b})-\delta=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}b_{i}-\delta.

Let

M>|G|^{1/\delta}>1\qqand x_{i}=M^{u_{i}}

for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
The vector
\mathbf{x}=\vectorsmallxn
is positive.
The subgroup
G
contains the identity permutation, and so

\displaystyle M^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}b_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\gamma\in G}M^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}b_{\gamma(i)}}=\sum_{\gamma\in G}\prod_{i=1}^{n}M^{u_{i}b_{\gamma(i)}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma\in G}\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{b_{\gamma(i)}}=|G|\left[\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{b}}\right]_{G}.

We have
\gamma(\mathbf{a})\in K_{G}(\mathbf{a})
and
H(\gamma(\mathbf{a}))\geq H(\mathbf{b})-\delta
.
It follows that

\displaystyle\left[\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{a}}\right]_{G}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\gamma\in G}\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{a_{\gamma^{-1}(i)}}=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\gamma\in G}\prod_{i=1}^{n}M^{u_{i}a_{\gamma^{-1}(i)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\gamma\in G}M^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}a_{\gamma^{-1}(i)}}=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\gamma\in G}M^{H(\gamma(\mathbf{a}))}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\gamma\in G}M^{H(\mathbf{b})-\delta}=\frac{1}{|G|}M^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}b_{i}-\delta}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M^{\delta}}\frac{1}{|G|}M^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}b_{i}}\leq\frac{1}{M^{\delta}}\left[\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{b}}\right]_{G}

\displaystyle<\left[\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{b}}\right]_{G}.

This completes the proof.
∎

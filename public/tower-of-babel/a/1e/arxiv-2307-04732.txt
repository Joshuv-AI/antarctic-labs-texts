Moduli spaces of (co)closed G 2 \mathrm{G}_{2} -structures on nilmanifolds
Giovanni Bazzoni, Alejandro Gil-García

Abstract
. We compute the dimensions of some moduli spaces of left-invariant closed and coclosed G 2 \mathrm{G}_{2} -structures on 7-dimensional nilmanifolds, showing that they are not related to the third Betti number. We also prove that, in contrast to the case of closed G 2 \mathrm{G}_{2} -structures, the group of automorphisms of a coclosed G 2 \mathrm{G}_{2} -structure is not necessarily abelian.

Primary: 53C10, Secondary: 22E25, 53C38, 58D27

1.
Introduction and Preliminaries

A
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure on a 7-dimensional manifold is a reduction of the structure group of the frame bundle from
\operatorname{GL}(7,\mathbb{R})
to
\mathrm{G}_{2}\subset\operatorname{SO}(7)
; hence, such a manifold is oriented and Riemannian. The existence of a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure is topological, and equivalent to the vanishing of the first and the
second Stiefel-Whitney class of the manifold (thus to orientability and spinnability); this is due to Gray
[
15
]
. Since
\mathrm{G}_{2}
is the stabilizer of a (positive) 3-form in
\mathbb{R}^{7}
, a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure on a 7-manifold
M
can equivalently be defined as a positive 3-form
\varphi\in\Omega^{3}_{+}(M)
, one for which it is possible to find, at each point
p\in M
, local coordinates
\{x^{1},\ldots,x^{7}\}
such that

\varphi_{p}=\mathrm{d}x^{127}+\mathrm{d}x^{347}+\mathrm{d}x^{567}+\mathrm{d}x^{135}-\mathrm{d}x^{236}-\mathrm{d}x^{146}-\mathrm{d}x^{245}\,,

where
\mathrm{d}x^{ijk}=\mathrm{d}x^{i}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{j}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{k}
. When one thinks of a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure in this way, the metric and the orientation are usually denoted
g_{\varphi}
and
\mathrm{vol}_{\varphi}
, since they are determined by the positive 3-form
\varphi
. In fact,
g_{\varphi}
and
\mathrm{vol}_{\varphi}
can be recovered from
\varphi
via the formula

6g_{\varphi}(X,Y)\mathrm{vol}_{\varphi}=\iota_{X}\varphi\wedge\iota_{Y}\varphi\wedge\varphi\,,\quad X,Y\in{\mathfrak{X}}(M)\,.

\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures are related to manifolds with Riemannian holonomy contained in
\mathrm{G}_{2}
: by a classical result of Fernández and Gray
[
8
]
, the metric
g_{\varphi}
has holonomy contained in
\mathrm{G}_{2}
if and only if
\mathrm{d}\varphi=0=\mathrm{d}*_{\varphi}\varphi
, namely, if
\varphi
is both
closed
and
coclosed
. Notice that
\mathrm{G}_{2}
is one of the exceptional holonomy groups appearing in the celebrated Berger list
[
3
]
.

Furthermore, the differential and the codifferential of a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-form
\varphi
on a 7-manifold
M
are governed by the so-called
torsion forms

\tau_{i}\in\Omega^{i}(M)
,
i=0,1,2,3
, according to the following equations (see
[
4
, Proposition 1]
):

(1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}\mathrm{d}\varphi&=&\tau_{0}\psi+3\tau_{1}\wedge\varphi+*_{\varphi}\tau_{3}\\
\mathrm{d}\psi&=&4\tau_{1}\wedge\psi+\tau_{2}\wedge\varphi\end{array}\right.

where
\psi=*_{\varphi}\varphi
. Thus, there are 16 classes of
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures, and the holonomy reduction is equivalent to the vanishing of all torsion forms, so that Riemannian manifolds with holonomy contained in
\mathrm{G}_{2}
are also known as torsion-free
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-manifolds. The
pure
classes are those for which all torsion forms vanish, but one. Thus, looking at (
1
), we get four pure classes:

\tau_{i}=0,i=1,2,3
,
\tau_{0}\neq 0

nearly parallel

\mathrm{d}\varphi=\tau_{0}*_{\varphi}\varphi

\tau_{i}=0,i=0,2,3
,
\tau_{1}\neq 0

locally conformally parallel

\mathrm{d}\varphi=3\tau_{1}\wedge\varphi
,
\mathrm{d}\psi=4\tau_{1}\wedge\psi

\tau_{i}=0,i=0,1,3
,
\tau_{2}\neq 0

closed

\mathrm{d}\psi=\tau_{2}\wedge\varphi

\tau_{i}=0,i=0,1,2
,
\tau_{3}\neq 0

purely coclosed

\mathrm{d}\varphi=*_{\varphi}\tau_{3}\Leftrightarrow\varphi\wedge\mathrm{d}\varphi=0

Notice that coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures, those for which
\mathrm{d}\psi=0
, form a class which contains both nearly parallel and purely coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures. Since one has
\tau_{0}=\frac{1}{7}*_{\varphi}(\varphi\wedge\mathrm{d}\varphi)
, purely coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures are precisely the coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures for which
\tau_{0}=0
.

In this paper we are mainly interested in left-invariant
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures on nilmanifolds. A
nilmanifold
is the quotient of a connected, simply connected, nilpotent Lie group
G
by a lattice
\Gamma
, i.e. a discrete, cocompact subgroup. Recall that a Lie group
G
is nilpotent if and only if its Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
is. Nilpotency of a Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
is defined as follows: the
lower central series
of a Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
is defined by
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}={\mathfrak{g}}
and
{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}=[{\mathfrak{g}}_{i-1},{\mathfrak{g}}]
, for
i\geq 1
. A Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
is
nilpotent
if there exists
m
such that
{\mathfrak{g}}_{m}=0
. The smallest such
m
is the
nilpotency step
of a nilpotent Lie algebra.

Let
G
be a connected, simply connected, nilpotent Lie group. Tensors on
{\mathfrak{g}}
correspond to left-invariant tensors on
G
, which descend to tensors on the quotient nilmanifold
\Gamma\backslash G
; with a slight abuse of notation, we call such tensors on
\Gamma\backslash G
left-invariant. For instance, left-invariant forms know a great deal about the topology of a nilmanifold: Nomizu
[
24
]
proved that
H^{*}_{dR}(\Gamma\backslash G)
is isomorphic to the Lie algebra cohomology of
{\mathfrak{g}}
. A
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure on a nilmanifold
\Gamma\backslash G
is called
left-invariant
if it is induced by a left-invariant 3-form
\varphi\in\Omega^{3}(\Gamma\backslash G)
; hence, such a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure can be described by an element
\varphi\in\Lambda^{3}{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
.

Nilmanifolds are parallelizable, so that they admit
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures, and also coclosed ones, by a result of Crowley and Nordström
[
6
]
; not all of them are left-invariant. It is known that nilmanifolds (not tori) can not admit torsion-free
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures. As for pure, left-invariant
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures on nilmanifolds, we have the following facts:

•

they can not be nearly parallel; this is because the metric of a nearly parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure is Einstein
[
12
, Proposition 3.10]
. By a result of Milnor, nilmanifolds (not tori) do not admit left-invariant Einstein metrics
[
22
, Theorem 2.4]
.

•

they can not be locally conformally parallel; by
[
17
, Theorem A]
, compact manifolds with locally conformally parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures are mapping tori of simply connected nearly Kähler 6-manifolds, hence they have
b_{1}=1
. However, a nilmanifold has
b_{1}\geq 2
.

We are therefore naturally led to consider left-invariant closed and (purely) coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures on nilmanifolds. The classification of 7-dimensional nilpotent Lie algebras which admit closed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures was obtained by Conti and Fernández
[
5
]
. In
[
1
]
Bagaglini, Fino and Fernández considered coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures on decomposable and 2-step nilpotent 7-dimensional Lie algebras (a Lie algebra is
decomposable
if it is a direct sum of two non-trivial Lie algebras). Purely coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures on 2-step nilpotent Lie algebras have been studied in
[
7
]
. Purely coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures on nilpotent Lie algebras which are either decomposable or have nilpotency step
\leq 4
have been tackled in
[
2
]
.

The main focus of this paper is on moduli spaces of closed and coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures on nilpotent Lie algebras, and of (left-invariant) closed and coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures on nilmanifolds (we refer to Sections
2
and
3
for all the relevant definitions). The starting points of our analysis are the following:

•

by a result of Joyce
[
18
]
, the moduli space of torsion-free
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures on a compact manifold
M
is a smooth manifold of dimension
b_{3}(M)
;

•

it is known that, on a compact manifold, both the moduli space of closed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures and that of coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures are infinite-dimensional; we include a proof of these facts in Sections
2
and
3
.

In Section
2
, we compute the dimension of the moduli space
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{c}}({\mathfrak{g}})
of closed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures on a 7-dimensional nilpotent Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
which admits such a structure, and of the moduli space of left-invariant closed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures on any nilmanifold
\Gamma\backslash G
,
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{c}}_{\mathrm{inv}}(\Gamma\backslash G)
. Furthermore, we prove that
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{c}}({\mathfrak{g}})
embeds into
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{c}}(\Gamma\backslash G)
, the moduli space of closed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures on
\Gamma\backslash G
, thus the former can be considered as a finite-dimensional approximation of the latter. Finally, we show that the dimension of
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{c}}({\mathfrak{g}})
is not related to
b_{3}({\mathfrak{g}})=b_{3}(\Gamma\backslash G)
.

In Section
3
we follow the same train of thoughts in the coclosed setting; the results we get are tantamount to those obtained in Section
2
. As an application of our study, we show that the automorphism group of a coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure on a compact manifold need not be abelian. This differs from the closed situation; indeed, as proved by Podestà and Raffero in
[
25
]
, the automorphism group of a closed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure on a compact manifold is abelian.

The appendices contain an overview of the computations, which were executed with
SageMath

[
27
]
.

2.
Moduli spaces of closed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures on nilmanifolds

In this section we consider three moduli spaces of closed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures:

•

closed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures on (nilpotent) Lie algebras,

•

left-invariant closed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures on nilmanifolds,

•

closed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures on 7-manifolds,

and prove some relations among them. Given a 7-dimensional Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
with a closed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure, we set

V^{\mathrm{c}}({\mathfrak{g}})\coloneqq\left\{\varphi\in\Lambda^{3}{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}\mid\text{$\varphi$ is a $\mathrm{G}_{2}$-structure and }\mathrm{d}\varphi=0\right\}\,.

Thus
\varphi\in\Lambda^{3}{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
is a closed 3-form and the formula

6g_{\varphi}(X,Y)\operatorname{vol}_{\varphi}\coloneqq\iota_{X}\varphi\wedge\iota_{Y}\varphi\wedge\varphi

defines a scalar product
g_{\varphi}
and a volume
\operatorname{vol}_{\varphi}
on
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Since being positive-definite is an open condition,
V^{\mathrm{c}}({\mathfrak{g}})
is an open subset of
Z^{3}({\mathfrak{g}}^{*})
, and its dimension coincides with that of
Z^{3}({\mathfrak{g}}^{*})
.
\Aut({\mathfrak{g}})
, the group of Lie algebra automorphisms of
{\mathfrak{g}}
, is a closed subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}({\mathfrak{g}})
, and acts naturally on
V^{\mathrm{c}}({\mathfrak{g}})
; we denote by
\mathcal{O}(\varphi)\cong\Aut({\mathfrak{g}})/\Aut({\mathfrak{g}})_{\varphi}
the orbit of the
\Aut({\mathfrak{g}})
-action passing through
\varphi
; here

\Aut({\mathfrak{g}})_{\varphi}\coloneqq\Aut({\mathfrak{g}})\cap\{A\in\operatorname{GL}({\mathfrak{g}})\mid A^{*}\varphi=\varphi\}\,,

which is a compact subgroup.

Definition 2.1
.

The moduli space of closed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures on
{\mathfrak{g}}
is

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{c}}({\mathfrak{g}})\coloneqq V^{\mathrm{c}}({\mathfrak{g}})/\Aut({\mathfrak{g}})=\{\mathcal{O}(\varphi)\mid\varphi\in V^{c}({\mathfrak{g}})\}\,.

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{c}}({\mathfrak{g}})
is not smooth in general, rather a stratified space, since the orbits of different elements of
V^{\mathrm{c}}({\mathfrak{g}})
under the action of
\Aut({\mathfrak{g}})
may have different dimensions.

Definition 2.2
.

The dimension of
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{c}}({\mathfrak{g}})
is

(2)

\dim\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{c}}({\mathfrak{g}})\coloneqq\dim V^{\mathrm{c}}({\mathfrak{g}})-\dim\mathcal{O}(\varphi)=\dim V^{\mathrm{c}}({\mathfrak{g}})-(\dim\Aut({\mathfrak{g}})-\dim\Aut({\mathfrak{g}})_{\varphi})\,,

where
\mathcal{O}(\varphi)
is a principal orbit, i.e. one of maximal dimension; equivalently, the stabilizer of
\varphi
has minimal dimension.

On a nilmanifold
\Gamma\backslash G
one has special kinds of diffeomorphisms, which are called
G
-induced
in the literature: these are precisely automorphisms of
G
which preserve the lattice
\Gamma
; we denote them by
\Aut(G,\Gamma)
. Thus, a meaningful definition of
moduli space of left-invariant closed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures
on
\Gamma\backslash G
is

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{c}}_{\mathrm{inv}}(\Gamma\backslash G)\coloneqq V^{\mathrm{c}}_{\mathrm{inv}}(\Gamma\backslash G)/\Aut(G,\Gamma)=\{\mathcal{O}(\varphi_{\mathrm{inv}})\mid\varphi_{\mathrm{inv}}\in V^{\mathrm{c}}_{\mathrm{inv}}(\Gamma\backslash G)\}\,,

where
V^{\mathrm{c}}_{\mathrm{inv}}(\Gamma\backslash G)
is the space of left-invariant closed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures on
\Gamma\backslash G
, so that
V^{\mathrm{c}}_{\mathrm{inv}}(\Gamma\backslash G)\cong V^{\mathrm{c}}({\mathfrak{g}})
. According to
[
23
, Proposition 1.1]
,
\Aut(G,\Gamma)
is a discrete subgroup of
\Aut(G)
, hence

(3)

\dim\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{c}}_{\mathrm{inv}}(\Gamma\backslash G)=\dim V^{\mathrm{c}}({\mathfrak{g}})=\dim\left(\ker\left(\mathrm{d}\colon\Lambda^{3}{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}\to\Lambda^{4}{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}\right)\right)\geq b_{3}({\mathfrak{g}})=b_{3}(\Gamma\backslash G)\,.

A similar dimension count appears, in the context of symplectic structures on nilpotent Lie algebras and nilmanifolds, in
[
26
, Equation 31]
. We can compare
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{c}}_{\mathrm{inv}}(\Gamma\backslash G)
with the space of
all
closed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures on
\Gamma\backslash G
. More generally, we can give the following definition.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
M
be a manifold endowed with closed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures. The moduli space of closed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures on
M
is

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{c}}(M)\coloneqq\{\varphi\in\Omega^{3}_{+}(M),\mathrm{d}\varphi=0\}/\Diff_{0}(M)=\{\mathcal{O}(\varphi)\mid\varphi\in\Omega^{3}_{+}(M),\mathrm{d}\varphi=0\}\,,

where
\mathcal{O}(\varphi)
is the orbit of
\varphi
under the action of
\Diff_{0}(M)
, so that
\mathcal{O}(\varphi)\cong\Diff_{0}(M)/\Diff_{0}(M)_{\varphi}
.

Here
\Diff_{0}(M)_{\varphi}
is the stabilizer of
\varphi
in
\Diff_{0}(M)
. The following result is well-known, but we include a proof for completeness. We are grateful to Lothar Schiemanowski for pointing this argument out to us.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
M
be a compact manifold endowed with closed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures. Then
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{c}}(M)
is infinite-dimensional.

Proof.

\{\varphi\in\Omega^{3}_{+}(M),\mathrm{d}\varphi=0\}
is an open set in the infinite-dimensional vector space
Z^{3}(M)
of closed 3-forms on
M
, acted on by
\Diff_{0}(M)
. Pick
[\varphi]\in\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{c}}(M)
. Then

T_{[\varphi]}\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{c}}(M)=Z^{3}(M)/T_{\varphi}(\Diff_{0}(M)\cdot\varphi)\,,

where
T_{\varphi}(\Diff_{0}(M)\cdot\varphi)=\{\mathcal{L}_{X}\varphi\mid X\in{\mathfrak{X}}(M)\}
. Since
\mathrm{d}\varphi=0
,

T_{\varphi}(\Diff_{0}(M)\cdot\varphi)=\{\mathrm{d}(\iota_{X}\varphi)\mid X\in{\mathfrak{X}}(M)\}=\mathrm{d}\left(\Omega^{2}_{7}(M)\right)\,,

where
\Omega^{2}(M)=\Omega^{2}_{7}(M)\oplus\Omega^{2}_{14}(M)
under the natural action of
\mathrm{G}_{2}
. By harmonic theory,
Z^{3}(M)=\mathcal{H}^{3}(M)\oplus\mathrm{d}\left(\Omega^{2}(M)\right)
, hence
T_{[\varphi]}\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{c}}(M)\cong\mathcal{H}^{3}(M)\oplus\mathrm{d}\left(\Omega^{2}_{14}(M)\right)
, which is infinite-dimensional.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

The group
\Diff_{0}(M)_{\varphi}
is non-trivial, in general. As proved in
[
25
, Theorem 2.1]
, it is an abelian Lie group with
\dim\Diff_{0}(M)_{\varphi}\leq\min\{b_{2}(M),6\}
.

Closed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures are sometimes considered the symplectic analogue of
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures. Indeed, they are both defined by closed differential forms satisfying the same non-degeneracy condition, namely that at each point of the manifold their orbit under the action of the general linear group in the corresponding tangent space is open; in other words, they are
stable forms
in the sense of Hitchin
[
16
]
. If
M
is a compact symplectic manifold, let us denote by
\mathcal{S}(M)
the space of symplectic structures on
M
; it is an open subset of
Z^{2}(M)
, acted on by the diffeomorphism group
\Diff(M)
of
M
and by its identity component
\Diff_{0}(M)
. Setting

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{s}}(M)\coloneqq\mathcal{S}(M)/\Diff_{0}(M)\,,

one has that
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{s}}(M)
is a smooth manifold of dimension
b_{2}(M)
(see for instance
[
11
, Proposition 3.1]
). With respect to this, closed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures and symplectic structures behave quite differently.

If
M=\Gamma\backslash G
is a nilmanifold admitting closed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures, we have a natural inclusion
\imath\colon V^{\mathrm{c}}({\mathfrak{g}})\hookrightarrow\{\varphi\in\Omega^{3}_{+}(M)\mid\mathrm{d}\varphi=0\}
.

Theorem 2.6
.

\imath
induces an inclusion
\bar{\imath}\colon\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{c}}({\mathfrak{g}})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{c}}(M)
.

Proof.

Consider
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in V^{\mathrm{c}}({\mathfrak{g}})
, and view them as elements of
\{\varphi\in\Omega^{3}_{+}(M)\mid\mathrm{d}\varphi=0\}
via
\imath
. Suppose that they define the same class in
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{c}}(M)
. We must show that
[\varphi_{1}]=[\varphi_{2}]\in\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{c}}({\mathfrak{g}})
. By hypothesis, there exists
f\in\Diff_{0}(M)
such that
f^{*}\varphi_{2}=\varphi_{1}
. Since the projection
\pi\colon G\to M
is the universal cover, we can lift
f
to a diffeomorphism
\tilde{f}\colon G\to G
such that
\tilde{f}^{*}\tilde{\varphi}_{2}=\tilde{\varphi}_{1}
; here
\tilde{\varphi}_{j}
,
j=1,2
, denotes the left-invariant form on
G
which projects to
\varphi_{j}
. Notice that
\tilde{\varphi}_{j}
induces a left-invariant Riemannian metric
\tilde{g}_{j}
on
G
,
j=1,2
, and that
\tilde{f}\colon(G,\tilde{g}_{1})\to(G,\tilde{g}_{2})
is an isometry. Composing with a left-translation if necessary, we can suppose that
\tilde{f}
fixes the identity of
G
. By a theorem of Wilson
[
28
]
(see also
[
21
, Proposition 1.3]
), it follows that
\tilde{f}\in\mathrm{Aut}(G)\cong\Aut({\mathfrak{g}})
. But this implies that
[\varphi_{1}]=[\varphi_{2}]
in
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{c}}({\mathfrak{g}})
.
∎

Remark 2.7
.

Wilson’s theorem
[
28
, Theorem 3]
was generalized to the case of completely solvable and unimodular Lie algebras
[
14
, Theorem 4.3]
(see also
[
20
, Theorem 4.2]
). Hence Theorem
2.6
(and Theorem
3.6
below) holds also in this broader class of Lie algebras.

Let
M
be a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-manifold, that is, a 7-manifold with torsion-free
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures, and consider the
moduli space of torsion-free
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures
on
M
,

\mathcal{M}(M)\coloneqq\mathcal{T}(M)/\Diff_{0}(M)\,,

where

\mathcal{T}(M)=\{\varphi\in\Omega^{3}_{+}(M)\mid\mathrm{d}\varphi=0=\mathrm{d}*_{\varphi}\varphi\}\,.

The following theorem, due to Joyce, summarizes the properties of
\mathcal{M}(M)
(see for instance
[
19
, Theorem 7.5]
):

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
M
be a compact
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-manifold. Then
\mathcal{M}(M)
is a smooth manifold of dimension
b_{3}(M)
. In fact, the period map
\mathcal{M}(M)\to H^{3}(M;\mathbb{R})
,
[\varphi]\mapsto[\varphi]_{dR}
is a local diffeomorphism.

Remark 2.9
.

According to Theorem
2.8
the moduli space of torsion-free
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures on the 7-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{7}
has dimension
b_{3}(\mathbb{T}^{7})=35
. On the other hand
\dim\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{c}}_{\mathrm{inv}}(\mathbb{T}^{7})=\dim V^{\mathrm{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{7})=35=b_{3}(\mathbb{T}^{7})
. Note that in this case a left-invariant closed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure on the torus is also coclosed (and vice versa), hence torsion-free by
[
8
]
.

If
M=\Gamma\backslash G
is a nilmanifold, Theorem
2.6
shows that
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{c}}(M)
contains a full copy of
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{c}}({\mathfrak{g}})
. We can ask whether this “finite-dimensional approximation” of
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{c}}(M)
has some relationship with
b_{3}(M)=b_{3}({\mathfrak{g}})
. We address this question next.

According to (
2
), to compute
\dim\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{c}}({\mathfrak{g}})
we need to compute
\dim V^{\mathrm{c}}({\mathfrak{g}})
,
\dim\Aut({\mathfrak{g}})
and
\dim\Aut({\mathfrak{g}})_{\varphi}
.

•

As we remarked above,
\dim V^{\mathrm{c}}({\mathfrak{g}})=\dim Z^{3}({\mathfrak{g}}^{*})
;

•

to compute
\dim\Aut({\mathfrak{g}})
, we simply compute the dimension of its Lie algebra
\Der({\mathfrak{g}})=\{D\in\mathfrak{gl}({\mathfrak{g}})\mid D[X,Y]=[DX,Y]+[X,DY]\ \forall~X,Y\in{\mathfrak{g}}\}
;

•

to compute
\dim\Aut({\mathfrak{g}})_{\varphi}
it is enough to calculate the dimension of its Lie algebra
\Der({\mathfrak{g}})_{\varphi}=\{D\in\Der({\mathfrak{g}})\mid D\cdot\varphi=0\}
, where, for
X,Y,Z\in{\mathfrak{g}}
,

(D\cdot\varphi)(X,Y,Z)=-\varphi(DX,Y,Z)-\varphi(X,DY,Z)-\varphi(X,Y,DZ)\,.

To compute the dimension of
\Aut({\mathfrak{g}})_{\varphi}
we need to start from a background closed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure on
{\mathfrak{g}}
. In
[
5
]
, Conti and Fernández classified the 7-dimensional NLAs admitting a closed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure: they showed that only 12 of the 7-dimensional NLAs admit such a structure; 3 of them are decomposable and 9 are indecomposable. For each NLA
{\mathfrak{g}}
with a closed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure
\varphi
, the authors exhibit an explicit coframe
\{\eta^{1},\ldots,\eta^{7}\}
of
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
on which

3.
Moduli spaces of coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures on NLAs

In this section we focus on moduli spaces of coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures, using the same approach as in Section
2
. We consider three moduli spaces of coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures:

•

coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures on (nilpotent) Lie algebras,

•

left-invariant coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures on nilmanifolds,

•

coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures on 7-manifolds.

Given a 7-dimensional NLA
{\mathfrak{g}}
with a coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure, we set

V^{\mathrm{cc}}({\mathfrak{g}})\coloneqq\left\{\psi\in\Lambda^{4}{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}\mid\text{$\psi$ determines a $\mathrm{G}_{2}$-structure and }\mathrm{d}\psi=0\right\}\,.

Observe that starting with a 3-form
\varphi
defining a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure, one has
\psi=\ast_{\varphi}\varphi
. The stabilizer of such 4-form is
\pm\mathrm{G}_{2}
; therefore, the 4-form
\psi
alone does determine the
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-metric, but not the orientation. An explicit formula, which gives the metric directly from
\psi
, can be found in
[
10
, Page 5]
. Notice that
V^{\mathrm{cc}}({\mathfrak{g}})
is an open subset of
Z^{4}({\mathfrak{g}}^{*})
, hence its dimension coincides with that of
Z^{4}({\mathfrak{g}}^{*})
.
\Aut({\mathfrak{g}})
acts naturally on
V^{\mathrm{cc}}({\mathfrak{g}})
. We denote by
\mathcal{O}(\psi)\cong\Aut({\mathfrak{g}})/\Aut({\mathfrak{g}})_{\psi}
the orbit of
\psi\in V^{\mathrm{cc}}({\mathfrak{g}})
under the
\Aut({\mathfrak{g}})
-action; here
\Aut({\mathfrak{g}})_{\psi}
is the stabilizer of
\psi
; notice that
\Aut({\mathfrak{g}})_{\psi}
is compact.

Definition 3.1
.

The moduli space of coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures on
{\mathfrak{g}}
is

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{cc}}({\mathfrak{g}})\coloneqq V^{\mathrm{cc}}({\mathfrak{g}})/\Aut({\mathfrak{g}})=\{\mathcal{O}(\psi)\mid\psi\in V^{\mathrm{cc}}({\mathfrak{g}})\}\,.

As in the closed case, it is a stratified space, since the orbits may have different dimensions.

Definition 3.2
.

The dimension of
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{cc}}({\mathfrak{g}})
is

(4)

\dim\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{cc}}({\mathfrak{g}})\coloneqq\dim V^{\mathrm{cc}}({\mathfrak{g}})-\dim\mathcal{O}(\psi)=\dim V^{\mathrm{cc}}({\mathfrak{g}})-(\dim\Aut({\mathfrak{g}})-\dim\Aut({\mathfrak{g}})_{\psi})\,,

where
\mathcal{O}(\psi)
is a principal orbit; equivalently,
\psi
has minimal stabilizer.

If
\Gamma\backslash G
is a nilmanifold, the
moduli space of left-invariant coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures
on
\Gamma\backslash G
is

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{cc}}_{\mathrm{inv}}(\Gamma\backslash G)\coloneqq V^{\mathrm{cc}}_{\mathrm{inv}}(\Gamma\backslash G)/\Aut(G,\Gamma)=\{\mathcal{O}(\psi_{\mathrm{inv}})\mid\psi_{\mathrm{inv}}\in V^{\mathrm{cc}}_{\mathrm{inv}}(\Gamma\backslash G)\}\,,

where
V^{\mathrm{cc}}_{\mathrm{inv}}(\Gamma\backslash G)
is the space of left-invariant coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures on
\Gamma\backslash G
, thus
V^{\mathrm{cc}}_{\mathrm{inv}}(\Gamma\backslash G)\cong V^{\mathrm{cc}}({\mathfrak{g}})
. Arguing exactly as in Section
2
, we see that on a nilmanifold
\Gamma\backslash G
equipped with a left-invariant coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure we have

\dim\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{cc}}_{\mathrm{inv}}(\Gamma\backslash G)=\dim V^{\mathrm{cc}}({\mathfrak{g}})=\dim\left(\ker\left(\mathrm{d}\colon\Lambda^{4}{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}\to\Lambda^{5}{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}\right)\right)\geq b_{4}({\mathfrak{g}})=b_{3}({\mathfrak{g}})=b_{3}(\Gamma\backslash G)\,.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
M
be a manifold endowed with coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures. The moduli space of coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures on
M
is

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{cc}}(M)\coloneqq\{\psi\in\Omega^{4}_{+}(M),\mathrm{d}\psi=0\}/\Diff_{0}(M)=\{\mathcal{O}(\psi)\mid\psi\in\Omega^{4}_{+}(M),\mathrm{d}\psi=0\}\,,

where
\mathcal{O}(\psi)
is the orbit of
\psi
under the action of
\Diff_{0}(M)
, so that
\mathcal{O}(\psi)\cong\Diff_{0}(M)/\Diff_{0}(M)_{\psi}
.

We also have:

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
M
be a compact manifold endowed with coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures. Then
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{cc}}(M)
is infinite-dimensional.

Proof.

\{\psi\in\Omega^{4}_{+}(M),\mathrm{d}\psi=0\}
is an open set in the infinite-dimensional vector space
Z^{4}(M)
of closed 4-forms on
M
, acted on by
\Diff_{0}(M)
. Pick
[\psi]\in\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{cc}}(M)
. Then

T_{[\psi]}\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{cc}}(M)=Z^{4}(M)/T_{\psi}(\Diff_{0}(M)\cdot\psi)\,,

where
T_{\psi}(\Diff_{0}(M)\cdot\psi)=\{\mathcal{L}_{X}\psi\mid X\in{\mathfrak{X}}(M)\}
. Since
\mathrm{d}\psi=0
,

T_{\psi}(\Diff_{0}(M)\cdot\psi)=\{\mathrm{d}(\iota_{X}\psi)\mid X\in{\mathfrak{X}}(M)\}=\mathrm{d}\left(\Omega^{3}_{7}(M)\right)\,,

where
\Omega^{3}(M)=\langle\varphi\rangle\oplus\Omega^{3}_{7}(M)\oplus\Omega^{3}_{27}(M)
under the natural action of
\mathrm{G}_{2}
. By harmonic theory,
Z^{4}(M)=\mathcal{H}^{4}(M)\oplus\mathrm{d}\left(\Omega^{3}(M)\right)
, hence
T_{[\psi]}\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{cc}}(M)\cong\mathcal{H}^{4}(M)\oplus\mathrm{d}\left(\langle\varphi\rangle\oplus\Omega^{3}_{27}(M)\right)
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Not much seems to be known about the group
\Diff_{0}(M)_{\psi}
, except that it is non-trivial, in general. Below, we will show that it needs not be abelian.

If
M=\Gamma\backslash G
is a nilmanifold admitting left-invariant coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures, we have a natural inclusion
\imath\colon V^{\mathrm{cc}}({\mathfrak{g}})\hookrightarrow\{\psi\in\Omega^{4}_{+}(M)\mid\mathrm{d}\psi=0\}

2.6
, we obtain:

Theorem 3.6
.

\imath
induces an inclusion
\bar{\imath}\colon\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{cc}}({\mathfrak{g}})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{cc}}(M)

As we did in the previous section, we can ask whether, on a nilmanifold
\Gamma\backslash G

\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures, there is any relationship between
\dim\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{cc}}({\mathfrak{g}})
and

By (

we need the dimensions of

\Aut({\mathfrak{g}})
and
\Aut({\mathfrak{g}})_{\psi}

•

We noticed above that
\dim V^{\mathrm{cc}}({\mathfrak{g}})=\dim Z^{4}({\mathfrak{g}}^{*})

•

•

\Der({\mathfrak{g}})_{\psi}=\{D\in\Der({\mathfrak{g}})\mid D\cdot\psi=0\}

\displaystyle(D\cdot\psi)(X,Y,Z,W)=

\displaystyle-\psi(DX,Y,Z,W)-\psi(X,DY,Z,W)

\displaystyle-\psi(X,Y,DZ,W)-\psi(X,Y,Z,DW)\,.

\Aut({\mathfrak{g}})_{\psi}

\mathrm{G}_{2}

Theorem 3.10
.

On a 7-dimensional non-abelian nilpotent Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
with a coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure there is no relation between the dimensions of
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{cc}}({\mathfrak{g}})
and
b_{3}({\mathfrak{g}})=b_{4}({\mathfrak{g}})
. More precisely:

•

\dim\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{cc}}({\mathfrak{g}})<b_{3}({\mathfrak{g}})
,
\dim\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{cc}}({\mathfrak{g}})=b_{3}({\mathfrak{g}})
and
\dim\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{cc}}({\mathfrak{g}})>b_{3}({\mathfrak{g}})
are possible on decomposable Lie algebras;

•

\dim\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{cc}}({\mathfrak{g}})<b_{3}({\mathfrak{g}})
if
{\mathfrak{g}}
is 2-step indecomposable.

Remark 3.11
.

In contrast with the closed case, for Lie algebras with coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure we do not have a relation between the dimension of the moduli space and the nilpotency step of
{\mathfrak{g}}
. For example, the Lie algebras
{\mathfrak{n}}_{20}
,
{\mathfrak{n}}_{21}
and
{\mathfrak{n}}_{23}
are 4-step nilpotent, but

\dim\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{cc}}({\mathfrak{n}}_{20})>b_{3}({\mathfrak{n}}_{20}),\quad\dim\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{cc}}({\mathfrak{n}}_{21})=b_{3}({\mathfrak{n}}_{21}),\quad\dim\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{cc}}({\mathfrak{n}}_{23})<b_{3}({\mathfrak{n}}_{23}).

One can wonder whether the uniform bound
\dim\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{cc}}({\mathfrak{g}})<b_{3}({\mathfrak{g}})
on indecomposable Lie algebras holds because we restrict to nilpotency step 2. This is indeed the case, as the following example shows:

.

The Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}=137A=(0,0,0,0,12,34,15+36)
is 7-dimensional, 3-step nilpotent, has
\dim V^{\mathrm{cc}}({\mathfrak{g}})=24
,

and

. It carries the (purely) coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure

with

•

\omega=e^{13}-e^{24}+e^{56}
;

•

, hence
\dim\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{cc}}({\mathfrak{g}})=24-14=10>8=b_{3}({\mathfrak{g}})
. Similarly, one can check that
\dim\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{cc}}(247Q)=10=b_{3}(247Q)
and
\dim\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{cc}}(357A)=8<14=b_{3}(357A)
, where

and

are also 3-step nilpotent indecomposable Lie algebras carrying the (purely) coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}

.

Corollary 3.13
.

On a 7-dimensional nilmanifold
M=\Gamma\backslash G
endowed with left-invariant coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures there is no relation between the dimension of
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{cc}}({\mathfrak{g}})
and

.

As a by-product of our study of the automorphism group of coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures on nilpotent Lie algebras we obtain the following result:

Theorem 3.14
.

The automorphism group of a coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure need not be abelian.

Proof.

Consider the decomposable Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{n}}_{2}
with the coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure
\psi
given in Appendix
B
. As we saw in Table
3
, the Lie algebra
\Der({\mathfrak{n}}_{2})_{\psi}
has dimension 4; moreover, it contains
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
as a subalgebra, hence it is not abelian. Then
\Aut({\mathfrak{n}}_{2})_{\psi}
is also not abelian. Notice that
{\mathfrak{n}}_{2}\cong\mathbb{R}^{4}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}_{3}
, where
{\mathfrak{h}}_{3}
denotes the Heisenberg Lie algebra. One can pick two non-commuting derivations in
\Der({\mathfrak{n}}_{2})_{\psi}
to give the following automorphisms of the abelian factor
\mathbb{R}^{4}
in
\mathbb{R}^{4}\times H_{3}
:

A_{t}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos t&0&\sin t&0\\
0&\cos t&0&-\sin t\\
-\sin t&0&\cos t&0\\
0&\sin t&0&\cos t\end{pmatrix}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad B_{s}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos s&0&0&-\sin s\\
0&\cos s&-\sin s&0\\
0&\sin s&\cos s&0\\
\sin s&0&0&\cos s\end{pmatrix}.

Choosing
t=s=\frac{\pi}{2}
, we obtain two non-commuting automorphisms of the Lie group
\mathbb{R}^{4}
which preserve the standard lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{4}\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}
. Together with the identity on the factor
H_{3}
, which preserves any given lattice
\Gamma\subset H_{3}
, they define non-commuting elements in
\Diff(M)_{\psi}
, where
M
is the nilmanifold
(\mathbb{Z}^{4}\times\Gamma)\backslash(\mathbb{R}^{4}\times H_{3})
.
∎

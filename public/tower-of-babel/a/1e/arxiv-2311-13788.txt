Non-Linear Additive Twists of GL 3 \mathrm{GL}_{3} Hecke Eigenvalues Thanks: IK acknowledges the support of the Masason Foundation.
Ikuya Kaneko, Wing Hong Leung

Abstract
. We bound non-linear additive twists of GL 3 \mathrm{GL}_{3} Hecke eigenvalues, improving upon the work of Kumar–Mallesham–Singh (2022). The proof employs the DFI circle method with standard manipulations (Voronoĭ, Cauchy–Schwarz, lengthening, and additive reciprocity). The main novelty includes the conductor lowering mechanism, albeit sacrificing some savings to remove an analytic oscillation, followed by the iteration ad infinitum of Cauchy–Schwarz and Poisson. The resulting character sums are estimated via the work of Adolphson–Sperber (1993). As an application, we prove nontrivial bounds for the first moment of GL 3 \mathrm{GL}_{3} Hardy’s function, which corresponds to the cubic moment of Hardy’s function studied by Ivić (2012).

1.2.
Heuristic Sketch

This section overviews the method of the proof of Theorem

1.2.1.
Trivial Bound

By Cauchy–Schwarz and the Rankin–Selberg bound (also known as the Ramanujan conjecture on average), the trivial bound amounts to

\mathcal{A}_{\pi}(T,\alpha,\beta)\ll_{\pi}T

uniformly in
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. In what follows, we shall implicitly employ the Rankin–Selberg bound to visualise how much we ought to save from the trivial bound at each step.

3.1.
Classical Triumphs

Hardy’s function plays a crucial rôle in the study of the zeros of the Riemann zeta function
\zeta(s)
on the critical line
\mathrm{Re}(s)=\frac{1}{2}
. Given
t\in\mathbb{R}
, it is defined by

Z(t)\coloneqq\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\chi\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}},\qquad\zeta(s)=\chi(s)\zeta(1-s).

As Ivić discusses in
[
Ivi13
]
, Hardy’s function
Z(t)
is real-valued, even, and smooth. Moreover,
|Z(t)|=|\zeta(\frac{1}{2}+it)|
implies that its zeros match the zeros of
\zeta(s)
on the critical line. Hardy’s original application of
Z(t)
is to prove that
\zeta(s)
has infinitely many nontrivial zeros; see the book of Titchmarsh
[
Tit51
]
. The proof begins with an assumption that
Z(t)
does not change sign for sufficiently large
t>0
and then yields a contradiction. Hardy
[
Har14
]
later refines on his reasoning to show that
\zeta(s)
has
\gg T
zeros
\beta+i\gamma
satisfying
|\gamma|\leq T
. Selberg
[
Sel89
]
then improves upon this estimate to
\gg T\log T
, followed by its further refinements by successors.

We are interested in the moments of
Z(t)
, but only odd moments represent a novelty since
|Z(t)|=|\zeta(\frac{1}{2}+it)|
. Given
k\in\mathbb{N}
fixed and
T\geq 1
, consider the
k

, namely

\mathcal{F}_{k}(T)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{T}Z(t)^{k}dt.

When
k=1
, Korolev
[
Kor08
]
and Jutila
[

Jut11
]
prove independently the true order of the first moment

\mathcal{F}_{1}(T)=O(T^{\frac{1}{4}}),\qquad\mathcal{F}_{1}(T)=\Omega_{\pm}(T^{\frac{1}{4}}),

Nonetheless, higher odd moments, where one also expects some nontrivial cancellations, still remain unamenable. In particular, the following problem has been unsolved since being posed as a question of study during the conference “Elementary and Analytic Number Theory” at the Mathematisches Forschungsinstitut Oberwolfach.

The approximate functional equation shows

For the cubic moment of Hardy’s function, there seems to exist no better estimate than

3.2.
\mathrm{GL}_{3}
Hardy’s Function

Given

Z(t,\pi)\coloneqq L\left(\frac{1}{2}+it,\pi\right)\chi\left(\frac{1}{2}+it,\pi\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}},\qquad L(s,\pi)=\chi(s,\pi)L(1-s,\widetilde{\pi}).

We then define the first moment of

\mathcal{F}_{1}(T,\pi)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{T}Z(t,\pi)dt.

The key difference from the cubic moment of Hardy’s function for the Riemann zeta function is that general

-functions. This precludes us from using Cauchy–Schwarz as in (
3.4
). Instead, if one assumes the second moment bound

\int_{0}^{T}\left|L\left(\frac{1}{2}+it,\pi\right)\right|^{2}dt\ll_{\pi,\varepsilon}T^{\frac{3}{2}-\delta+\varepsilon}

for some
\delta>0
, then

(3.5)

\mathcal{F}_{1}(T,\pi)\ll_{\pi,\varepsilon}T^{\frac{5}{4}-\frac{\delta}{2}+\varepsilon}.

Conforming to
[
Ivi13
, Chapter 7]

mutatis mutandis
, one can establish the following reduction.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{3}
. Assume that

\sum_{n\leq T}\lambda_{\pi}(n)e(\alpha n^{\frac{2}{3}})\ll_{\pi,\alpha,\varepsilon}T^{1-\eta+\varepsilon}

for some
\eta>0
. Then we have for any
\varepsilon>0
that

(3.6)

\mathcal{F}_{1}(T,\pi)\ll_{\pi,\varepsilon}T^{\frac{5}{4}-\frac{3\eta}{2}+\varepsilon}.

Substituting
\eta=\frac{1}{18}
from Corollary
1.2
into (
3.6
) yields a refined exponent
\frac{7}{6}=1.16666\cdots
.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{3}
. Then we have for any
\varepsilon>0
that

\mathcal{F}_{1}(T,\pi)\ll_{\pi,\varepsilon}T^{\frac{7}{6}+\varepsilon}.

If
\eta>\frac{1}{6}
, then one can prove the infinitude of zeros of
\mathrm{GL}_{3}

L
-functions on the critical line; see the Ph.D. thesis of Rajkumar
[
Raj12
]
. It is commensurate with Weyl-strength subconvex bounds for
\mathrm{GL}_{3}

L
-functions (at least) in the
t
-aspect.

4.
Arithmetic Preliminaries

This section reviews arithmetic tools that we shall adopt in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

4.1.
Circle Method

There are two oscillatory components in the non-linear additive twist that we address. The idea is to separate these oscillations via the circle method. One seeks for a Fourier expansion that matches the Kronecker symbol
\delta(n=0)
.

Lemma 4.1

(Leung
[
Leu22
]
)
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}
be such that
|n|\ll N
,
q\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
C>N^{\varepsilon}
. Let
U\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and
W\in C_{c}^{\infty}([-2,-1]\cup[1,2])
be nonnegative even functions such that
U(x)=1
for
x\in[-2,2]
. Then we have that

\delta(n=0)=\frac{1}{\mathcal{C}}\sum_{c=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{cq}\sum_{a{\@displayfalse\pmod{cq}}}e\left(\frac{an}{cq}\right)V_{0}\left(\frac{c}{C},\frac{n}{cCq}\right),

where

\mathcal{C}\coloneqq\sum_{c=1}^{\infty}W\left(\frac{c}{C}\right)\sim C,

and

V_{0}(x,y)\coloneqq W(x)U(x)U(y)-W(y)U(x)U(y)

is a smooth function localising to
\delta(|x|,|y|\ll 1)
.

In view of the result of Heath-Brown
[
HB96
]
, Lemma
4.1
is the Duke–Friedlander–Iwaniec circle method
[
DFI94
]
with a simpler weight function that restricts
|n|\ll cCQ
. This feature facilitates the forthcoming integral analysis once we make use of the
\mathrm{GL}_{3}
Voronoĭ summation formula (Lemma
5.4
). We direct the reader to
[
KL23
,
Leu22
]
for further discussions.

4.2.
Poisson Summation

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and an integrable function
w\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
, denote its Fourier transform by

\widehat{w}(y)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}w(x)e(-\langle x,y\rangle)dx,

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the standard inner product on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Furthermore, if
c\in\mathbb{N}
and
K\colon\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{C}
is a periodic function of period
c
, then its Fourier transform
\widehat{K}
is again the periodic function of period
c
, defined on
\mathbb{Z}
by

\widehat{K}(n)\coloneqq\ \sum_{a{\@displayfalse\pmod{c}}}K(a)e\left(-\frac{an}{c}\right).

There is a minor inconsistency in sign choices, and
\widehat{\widehat{K}}(n)=K(-n)
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Lemma 4.2

(Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel
[
FKM15
, Lemma 2.1]
)
.

For any
c\in\mathbb{N}
, any
c
-periodic function
K
, and any even smooth function
V
compactly supported on
\mathbb{R}
, we have that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}K(n)V(n)=\frac{1}{c}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{K}(n)\widehat{V}\left(\frac{n}{c}\right).

4.3.
The Gamma Function

For fixed
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
, real
|\tau|\geq 3
, and any
M>0
, we often make use of Stirling’s formula

(4.1)

\Gamma(\sigma+i\tau)=e^{-\frac{\pi|\tau|}{2}}|\tau|^{\sigma-\frac{1}{2}}\exp\left(i\tau\log\frac{|\tau|}{e}\right)g_{\sigma,M}(\tau)+O_{\sigma,M}(|\tau|^{-M}),

where

g_{\sigma,M}(\tau)=\sqrt{2\pi}\exp\left(\frac{\pi}{4}(2\sigma-1)i\operatorname{sgn}(\tau)\right)+O_{\sigma,M}(|\tau|^{-1})

and

|\tau|^{j}g_{\sigma,M}^{(j)}(\tau)\ll_{j,\sigma,M}1

for all fixed
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

5.
Automorphic Preliminaries

This section compiles automorphic tools that we shall adopt in the proof of Theorem
1.1
; see the book of Goldfeld
[
Gol06
]
for a thorough account of the theoretical background.

5.1.
Hecke Eigenvalues

Denote by
\pi
a Hecke–Maaß cusp form of type
(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})
for
\mathrm{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
with the Fourier–Whittaker coefficients
\lambda_{\pi}(m,n)
normalised so that
\lambda_{\pi}(1,1)=1
. As usual, one abbreviates
\lambda_{\pi}(1,n)\coloneqq\lambda_{\pi}(n)
. The corresponding Langlands parameters are given by

\mu_{1}=-\nu_{1}-2\nu_{2}+1,\qquad\mu_{2}=-\nu_{1}+\nu_{2},\qquad\mu_{3}=2\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}-1.

The Ramanujan conjecture then states that
\mathrm{Re}(\mu_{i})=0
. The work of Jacquet–Shalika
[
JS90
]
shows
|\mathrm{Re}(\mu_{i})|<\frac{1}{2}
. The Fourier–Whittaker coefficients obey the Hecke relation

(5.1)

\lambda_{\pi}(n_{1},1)\lambda_{\pi}(1,n_{2})=\sum_{d\mid(n_{1},n_{2})}\lambda_{\pi}\left(\frac{n_{1}}{d},\frac{n_{2}}{d}\right).

The Rankin–Selberg bound is known in the following form.

Lemma 5.1
.

We have for any
\varepsilon>0
that

\underset{n_{1}^{2}n_{2}\leq N}{\sum\sum}|\lambda_{\pi}(n_{2},n_{1})|^{2}\ll_{\pi,\varepsilon}N^{1+\varepsilon}.

As a corollary, we deduce the following estimate.

Corollary 5.2
.

We have for any
\varepsilon>0
that

\sum_{n_{1}\leq N_{1}}\sum_{n_{2}\leq N_{2}}|\lambda_{\pi}(n_{1},n_{2})|^{2}\ll(N_{1}N_{2})^{1+\varepsilon}.

Proof.

Applying the Hecke relation (
5.1
) in tandem with Möbius inversion yields

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}\leq N_{1}}\sum_{n_{2}\leq N_{2}}|\lambda_{\pi}(n_{1},n_{2})|^{2}=

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}\leq N_{1}}\sum_{n_{2}\leq N_{2}}\left|\sum_{d\mid(n_{1},n_{2})}\mu(d)\lambda_{\pi}\left(\frac{n_{1}}{d},1\right)\lambda_{\pi}\left(1,\frac{n_{2}}{d}\right)\right|^{2}.

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality to take out the
d
-sum, the right-hand side is bounded by

X^{\varepsilon}\sum_{n_{1}\leq N_{1}}\sum_{n_{2}\leq N_{2}}\sum_{d\mid(n_{1},n_{2})}\left|\lambda_{\pi}\left(\frac{n_{1}}{d},1\right)\lambda_{\pi}\left(1,\frac{n_{2}}{d}\right)\right|^{2}\\
=X^{\varepsilon}\sum_{d}\sum_{n_{1}\leq\frac{N_{1}}{d}}\sum_{n_{2}\leq\frac{N_{2}}{d}}|\lambda_{\pi}(n_{1},1)\lambda_{\pi}(1,n_{2})|^{2}.

Corollary
5.2
now follows from Lemma
5.1
.
∎

We record the best known bound for individual Fourier–Whittaker coefficients
\lambda_{\pi}(n_{1},n_{2})
.

Lemma 5.3

(Kim–Sarnak
[
Kim03
]
)
.

We have for any
\varepsilon>0
that

|\lambda_{\pi}(n_{1},n_{2})|\ll_{\pi,\varepsilon}(n_{1}n_{2})^{\frac{5}{14}+\varepsilon}.

5.2.
\mathrm{GL}_{3}
Voronoĭ Summation

In conjunction with Poisson summation in Section
4.2
, the proof of Theorem
1.1
requires the Voronoĭ summation formula attached to Hecke–Maaß cusp forms on
\mathrm{Z}(\mathbb{R})\mathrm{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})/\mathrm{O}(3)
. Let
h
be a compactly supported smooth function on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
and denote the corresponding Mellin transform by

\widetilde{h}(s)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{\infty}h(x)x^{s-1}dx.

Given
\sigma>-1+\max\{-\mathrm{Re}(\alpha_{1}),-\mathrm{Re}(\alpha_{2}),-\mathrm{Re}(\alpha_{3})\}
and
a\in\{0,1\}
, define

\gamma_{a}(s)\coloneqq\frac{\pi^{-3s-\frac{3}{2}}}{2}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1+s+\alpha_{i}+a}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{-s-\alpha_{i}+a}{2})}.

Let
\gamma_{\pm}(s)\coloneqq\gamma_{0}(s)\mp i\gamma_{1}(s)
, and define

(5.2)

\mathcal{H}_{\pm}(x)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\sigma)}x^{-s}\gamma_{\pm}(s)\widetilde{h}(-s)ds.

We now formulate a version of the
\mathrm{GL}_{3}
Voronoĭ summation formula; cf.
[
Blo12
,
Li11
,
MS06
]
.

Lemma 5.4

(
\mathrm{GL}_{3}
Voronoĭ summation)
.

Let
a,\overline{a}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
q\in\mathbb{N}
with
(a,q)=1
,
a\overline{a}\equiv 1{\@displayfalse\pmod{q}}
. Let
h
be a compactly supported smooth function on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Then we have that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{\pi}(n)e\left(\frac{an}{q}\right)h(n)=q\sum_{\pm}\sum_{n_{0}\mid q}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{\pi}(n,n_{0})}{nn_{0}}S\left(\overline{a},\pm n;\frac{q}{n_{0}}\right)\mathcal{H}_{\pm}\left(\frac{nn_{0}^{2}}{q^{3}}\right).

The behaviour of the integral transform
\mathcal{H}_{\pm}
on the dual side is determined in the literature.

Lemma 5.5

(Blomer
[
Blo12
, Lemma 6]
)
.

Let
h
be a compactly supported smooth function on
[a,b]\subset(0,\infty)
, and let
\mathcal{H}_{\pm}
be defined by (
5.2
). Then there exist constants
\gamma_{\ell}
depending only on the Langlands parameters
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})
such that

\mathcal{H}_{\pm}(x)=x\int_{0}^{\infty}h(y)\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\frac{\gamma_{\ell}}{(xy)^{\frac{\ell}{3}}}e(\pm 3(xy)^{\frac{1}{3}})dy+\mathcal{R}(x),

where the error term
\mathcal{R}(x)
satisfies

x^{k}\frac{d^{k}}{dx^{k}}\mathcal{R}(x)\ll\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{\infty}^{1+\frac{k-L}{3}}

with an implicit constant depending at most on
a,b,\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3},k
, and
L
.

6.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we embark on the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Recall that our goal is to estimate

\mathcal{A}_{\pi}(T,\alpha,\beta)\coloneqq\sum_{n\leq T}\lambda_{\pi}(n)e(\alpha n^{\beta})\ll_{\pi,\alpha,\beta,\varepsilon}T^{\frac{9+10\beta}{17}+\varepsilon}+T^{\frac{19}{14}-\beta+\varepsilon}.

We will consider
\pi
,
\alpha
,
\beta
fixed, and employ the usual convention that
\varepsilon
is an arbitrarily small positive quantity, which may change from line to line. We will then drop the dependence on
\pi

\alpha
,
\beta

\varepsilon

.

6.1.
Smoothing

A dyadic subdivision yields

\mathcal{A}_{\pi}(T,\alpha,\beta)\ll T^{\varepsilon}\sup_{1\leq N\leq T}\sum_{\frac{N}{2}<n\leq N}\lambda_{\pi}(n)e(\alpha n^{\beta}).

Let
Y\geq 1
be a parameter at our disposal. Approximating the indicator function
\mathbf{1}_{(\frac{N}{2},N]}
by a

-inert function

that takes
1
on
[\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{Y},1-\frac{1}{Y}]
and
0
outside
[\frac{1}{2},1]
, and bounding the error term with Lemma
5.3
, we deduce

(6.1)

\mathcal{A}_{\pi}(T,\alpha,\beta)\ll T^{\varepsilon}\sup_{1\leq N\leq T}\left(|\mathcal{S}(N)|+\frac{N^{\frac{19}{14}+\varepsilon}}{Y}\right),

where

\mathcal{S}(N)\coloneqq\sum_{n}\lambda_{\pi}(n)e(\alpha n^{\beta})W\left(\frac{n}{N}\right).

6.2.
Applying the
\delta
-Symbol

We now use Lemma
4.1
to separate the oscillation of
\lambda_{\pi}(n)
from
e(\alpha n^{\beta})W(\frac{n}{N})
. Let
C\geq 1
be a parameter at our disposal, and fix a smooth function
U
that takes
1
on
[1,2]
and
0
outside
[\frac{1}{2},\frac{5}{2}]
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(N)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m}\lambda_{\pi}(m)U\left(\frac{m}{N}\right)\sum_{n}e(\alpha n^{\beta})W\left(\frac{n}{N}\right)\delta(n=m)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{C}}\sum_{c}\frac{1}{c}\sum_{m}\lambda_{\pi}(m)U\left(\frac{m}{N}\right)\sum_{n}e(\alpha n^{\beta})W\left(\frac{n}{N}\right)\sum_{a{\@displayfalse\pmod{c}}}e\left(\frac{a(m-n)}{c}\right)V_{0}\left(\frac{c}{C},\frac{m-n}{cC}\right)

for some
\mathcal{C}\sim C
and a fixed smooth function
V_{0}
satisfying
V_{0}(x,y)\ll\delta(|x|,|y|\ll 1)
. Pulling out the divisor
(a,c)
implies

\mathcal{S}(N)\ll\frac{1}{C}\sum_{b}\sum_{c}\frac{1}{bc}\mathop{\sum\nolimits^{\mathrlap{\ast}}}_{a{\@displayfalse\pmod{c}}}\sum_{m}\lambda_{\pi}(m)e\left(\frac{am}{c}\right)U\left(\frac{m}{N}\right)\\
\times\sum_{n}e\left(\alpha n^{\beta}-\frac{an}{c}\right)W\left(\frac{n}{N}\right)V_{0}\left(\frac{bc}{C},\frac{m-n}{bcC}\right).

6.3.
Voronoĭ Summation in
m

Applying
\mathrm{GL}_{3}
Voronoĭ summation (Lemma
5.4
together with Lemma
5.5
) to the
m
-sum, we deduce

\sum_{m}\lambda_{\pi}(m)e\left(\frac{am}{c}\right)U\left(\frac{m}{N}\right)V_{0}\left(\frac{bc}{C},\frac{m-n}{bcC}\right)\\
=\frac{N^{\frac{2}{3}}}{c}\sum_{\pm}\sum_{m_{0}\mid c}\sum_{m}\lambda_{\pi}(m,m_{0})\left(\frac{m_{0}}{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}S\left(\overline{a},\pm m;\frac{c}{m_{0}}\right)\mathcal{J}_{1}(m_{0}^{2}m,n,c),

where
4

4

We suppress less important variables from the notation, and it applies to the ensuing integral transforms.

(6.2)

\mathcal{J}_{1}(m_{0}^{2}m,n,c)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi\left(y,\frac{m_{0}^{2}m}{c^{3}}\right)V_{0}\left(\frac{bc}{C},\frac{Ny-n}{bcC}\right)e\left(\pm\frac{3(m_{0}^{2}mNy)^{\frac{1}{3}}}{c}\right)dy

for some flat function

. This leads to the expression

\mathcal{S}(N)\ll\frac{N^{\frac{2}{3}}}{C}\sum_{\pm}\sum_{b}\sum_{c}\frac{1}{bc^{2}}\mathop{\sum\nolimits^{\mathrlap{\ast}}}_{a{\@displayfalse\pmod{c}}}\sum_{m_{0}\mid c}\sum_{m}\lambda_{\pi}(m,m_{0})\left(\frac{m_{0}}{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}S\left(\overline{a},\pm m;\frac{c}{m_{0}}\right)\\
\times\sum_{n}e\left(\alpha n^{\beta}-\frac{an}{c}\right)W\left(\frac{n}{N}\right)\mathcal{J}_{1}(m_{0}^{2}m,n,c).

6.4.
Poisson Summation in

Applying Poisson summation (Lemma
4.2
) to the

\sum_{n}e\left(\alpha n^{\beta}-\frac{an}{c}\right)W\left(\frac{n}{N}\right)\mathcal{J}_{1}(m_{0}^{2}m,n,c)=\frac{N}{c}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\gamma{\@displayfalse\pmod{c}}}e\left(\frac{(n-a)\gamma}{c}\right)\mathcal{J}_{2}(m_{0}^{2}m,n,c),

(6.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{J}_{2}(m_{0}^{2}m,n,c)&\coloneqq\int_{0}^{\infty}W(x)\mathcal{J}_{1}(m_{0}^{2}m,Nx,c)e\left(\alpha(Nx)^{\beta}-\frac{nNx}{c}\right)dx\\

\mathcal{S}(N)\ll\frac{N^{\frac{5}{3}}}{C}\sum_{\pm}\sum_{b}\sum_{c}\frac{1}{bc^{2}}\sum_{m_{0}}\sum_{m}\frac{\lambda_{\pi}(m,m_{0})}{m_{0}^{\frac{5}{3}}m^{\frac{1}{3}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}\\

6.5.
Rewriting the Integral Transform

In anticipation of the Cauchy–Schwarz step, it is convenient to rewrite slightly the integral

and extract some of its oscillations. Making a change of variables

we obtain

\mathcal{J}_{2}(m_{0}^{2}m,n,cm_{0})=\frac{bcm_{0}C}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{\infty}W(x)\Phi\left(x+\frac{bcm_{0}Cu}{N},\frac{m}{c^{3}m_{0}}\right)V_{0}\left(\frac{bcm_{0}}{C},u\right)\\

Remark 6.1
.

This is one of the strengths of the circle method that we choose. The bump function

being fixed allows one to extract the oscillations via a simple change of variables.

Note that for any

and
-1<\sigma<0

(6.4)

e^{ix}-1=\int_{(\sigma)}\Gamma(s)e\left(\frac{s}{4}\right)x^{-s}\frac{ds}{2\pi i}.

This is immediate from shifting the contour to the left and then comparing it with the Taylor expansion of

. The identity (
6.4
) gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{2}(m_{0}^{2}m,n,cm_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{bcm_{0}C}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{\infty}W(x)\Phi\left(x+\frac{bcm_{0}Cu}{N},\frac{m}{c^{3}m_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times V_{0}\left(\frac{bcm_{0}}{C},u\right)e\left(\alpha(Nx)^{\beta}-\frac{nNx}{cm_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(1+\int_{(-\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon)}\Gamma(s)e\left(\frac{s}{4}\right)\left(\pm\frac{3(m(Nx+bcm_{0}Cu))^{\frac{1}{3}}}{cm_{0}^{\frac{1}{3}}}\right)^{-s}\frac{ds}{2\pi i}\right)dxdu.

It follows from Stirling’s formula (
4.1
) that the
s
-integral converges absolutely. Furthermore, repeated integration by parts in the

-integral ensures an arbitrary saving unless

|\mathrm{Im}(s)|\ll\left(\frac{mN}{c^{3}m_{0}}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}T^{\varepsilon}.

6.6.
Cauchy–Schwarz in

Applying dyadic subdivisions on

and

to take out the

where

(n,cm_{0})=1\end{subarray}}S(\pm m,\overline{n};c)\mathcal{J}_{3}^{\eta}(m,n,c)\right|^{2}

with

(6.6)

\mathcal{J}_{3}^{\eta}(m,n,c)\coloneqq V_{0}\left(\frac{bcm_{0}}{C},u\right)\int_{0}^{\infty}W(x)\Phi\left(x+\frac{bcm_{0}Cu}{N},\frac{m}{c^{3}m_{0}}\right)\\
\times e\left(\alpha(Nx)^{\beta}-\frac{nNx}{cm_{0}}\right)\left(\frac{(MN)^{\frac{1}{6}}}{\sqrt{m_{0}}}\left(\frac{x+\frac{bcm_{0}Cu}{N}}{c^{3}}\right)^{\frac{1}{6}+\frac{\varepsilon}{3}-\frac{it}{3}}\right)^{\eta}dx.

Before we open the square, we lengthen the
m
-sum in anticipation of Poisson summation in the ensuing analysis. Let
L\geq 1
be a parameter at our disposal, and fix a smooth function

\mathcal{R}(N)\leq\sum_{m}U_{0}\left(\frac{m_{0}^{2}m}{LM}\right)\left|\sum_{\frac{C_{0}}{m_{0}}\leq c<\frac{2C_{0}}{m_{0}}}\frac{1}{c}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\frac{C_{0}T^{-\varepsilon}}{N^{1-\beta}}\ll n\ll\frac{C_{0}T^{\varepsilon}}{N^{1-\beta}}\\
(n,cm_{0})=1\end{subarray}}S(\pm m,\overline{n};c)\mathcal{J}_{3}^{\eta}(m,n,c)\right|^{2}.

This step counterweights zero frequency and nonzero frequencies in Poisson summation, and the optimisation of the parameter

thus governs the quality of the final bound.

6.7.

Opening the square, the

-sum boils down to

\sum_{m}U_{0}\left(\frac{m_{0}^{2}m}{LM}\right)S(\pm m,\overline{n_{1}};c_{1})S(\pm m,\overline{n_{2}};c_{2})\mathcal{J}_{3}^{\eta}(m,n_{1},c_{1})\overline{\mathcal{J}_{3}^{\eta}(m,n_{2},c_{2})}.

Poisson summation (Lemma

\frac{LM}{c_{1}c_{2}}\sum_{m}\mathcal{C}^{\pm}(m,n_{1},n_{2};c_{1},c_{2})\mathcal{J}_{4}^{\eta}(m,n_{1},n_{2},c_{1},c_{2}),

where

and

(6.8)

\mathcal{J}_{4}^{\eta}(m,n_{1},n_{2},c_{1},c_{2})\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}}U_{0}(y)\mathcal{J}_{3}^{\eta}(LMy,n_{1},c_{1})\overline{\mathcal{J}_{3}^{\eta}(LMy,n_{2},c_{2})}e\left(-\frac{mLMy}{c_{1}c_{2}}\right)dy.

Repeated integration by parts in the

Inserting the above manipulations back into (

\mathcal{R}(N)\ll LM\mathop{\sum\sum}_{\frac{C_{0}}{m_{0}}\leq c_{1},c_{2}<\frac{2C_{0}}{m_{0}}}\frac{1}{(c_{1}c_{2})^{2}}\mathop{\sum\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\frac{C_{0}T^{-\varepsilon}}{N^{1-\beta}}\ll n_{1},n_{2}\ll\frac{C_{0}T^{\varepsilon}}{N^{1-\beta}}\\
(n_{1},c_{1}m_{0})=(n_{2},c_{2}m_{0})=1\end{subarray}}\\
\times\sum_{|m|\ll\frac{C_{0}^{2}T^{\varepsilon}}{m_{0}^{2}LM}}\mathcal{C}^{\pm}(m,n_{1},n_{2};c_{1},c_{2})\mathcal{J}_{4}^{\eta}(m,n_{1},n_{2},c_{1},c_{2})+T^{-2023}.

For the character sum

, summing over

Write

,

,
c_{2,0}=(\frac{c_{2}}{c_{0}},c_{0}^{\infty})
,

(n_{1}n_{2},cm_{0})=1\end{subarray}}\mathcal{J}_{4}^{\eta}(0,n_{1},n_{2},c_{1},c_{2}).

Applying the second derivative test on the integral transform

\displaystyle\quad\times\mathop{\mathop{\sum\nolimits^{\mathrlap{\ast}}}_{\alpha_{1}{\@displayfalse\pmod{c_{0}c_{1,0}}}}\mathop{\sum\nolimits^{\mathrlap{\ast}}}_{\alpha_{2}{\@displayfalse\pmod{c_{0}c_{2,0}}}}}\sum_{(c_{1}^{\prime},mn_{0}n_{1}^{\prime})=1}\frac{m_{0}mn_{0}n_{1}^{\prime}n_{2}^{\prime}}{a^{2}c_{0}^{2}c_{1,0}c_{2,0}^{2}C_{0}\sqrt{(mn_{0})_{\square}}}U_{0}\left(\frac{ac_{0}c_{1,0}c_{1}^{\prime}m_{0}}{C_{0}R_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\Bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\frac{C_{0}}{c_{0}m_{0}}\leq ac_{2,0}c_{2}^{\prime}<\frac{2C_{0}}{c_{0}m_{0}}\\

for some

-inert function

.

6.10.

Opening the square, the

\sum_{(c_{1}^{\prime},mn_{0}n_{1}^{\prime})=1}U_{0}\left(\frac{ac_{0}c_{1,0}c_{1}^{\prime}m_{0}}{C_{0}R_{1}}\right)\varphi(c_{1}^{\prime},c_{2}^{\prime},x_{2})\overline{\varphi(c_{1}^{\prime},c_{3}^{\prime},x_{3})}e\left(\pm\frac{c_{2,0}\overline{c_{1}^{\prime}}(c_{2}^{\prime}-c_{3}^{\prime})}{c_{1,0}mn_{1}}\pm\frac{c_{1,0}c_{1}^{\prime}(\overline{c_{2}^{\prime}}-\overline{c_{3}^{\prime}})}{c_{2,0}mn_{2}}\right).

\frac{n_{0}C_{0}R_{1}}{ac_{0}c_{1,0}^{2}c_{2,0}m_{0}mn_{1}n_{2}}\sum_{c_{1}^{\prime}}\mathcal{C}^{\prime}(c_{1}^{\prime},c_{2}^{\prime},c_{3}^{\prime})\int_{\mathbb{R}}U_{0}(y)\varphi\left(\frac{C_{0}R_{1}y}{ac_{0}c_{1,0}m_{0}},c_{2}^{\prime},x_{2}\right)\\

where

Repeated integration by parts in the

-integral ensures an arbitrary saving unless

Choosing

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma{\@displayfalse\pmod{c_{1,0}c_{2,0}mn_{1}n_{2}/n_{0}}}\\

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{(n_{0},ac_{0}c_{1}^{\prime}c_{2}^{\prime}m_{0})=1}\mathop{\sum\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1,0},n_{2,0}\mid n_{0}^{\infty}\\

\displaystyle\quad\times\mathop{\mathop{\sum\nolimits^{\mathrlap{\ast}}}_{\alpha_{1}{\@displayfalse\pmod{c_{0}c_{1,0}}}}\mathop{\sum\nolimits^{\mathrlap{\ast}}}_{\alpha_{2}{\@displayfalse\pmod{c_{0}c_{2,0}}}}}\frac{\varphi(d)m_{1}m_{2}n_{2}^{\prime}R_{1}}{a^{3}c_{0}^{3}c_{1,0}^{2}c_{2,0}^{2}\sqrt{(mn_{0})_{\square}}}\sum_{b_{0}b_{1}=n_{1}^{\prime}}\mu(b_{0})b_{1}

\displaystyle\quad\times\mathop{\sum\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\frac{C_{0}}{ac_{0}c_{2,0}m_{0}}\leq c_{2}^{\prime},c_{3}^{\prime}<\frac{2C_{0}}{ac_{0}c_{2,0}m_{0}}\\

where

with the notation

and

\mathcal{I}_{1}(c_{2}^{\prime},\ell)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}W(x_{2})W(x_{3})U_{0}(y)\varphi\left(\frac{C_{0}R_{1}y}{ac_{0}c_{1,0}m_{0}},b_{2}c_{2}^{\prime},x_{2}\right)\overline{\varphi\left(\frac{C_{0}R_{1}y}{ac_{0}c_{1,0}m_{0}},b_{2}c_{3}^{\prime},x_{3}\right)}\\
\times e\left(-\alpha(Nx_{2})^{\beta}+\frac{n_{2}Nx_{2}}{ac_{0}c_{2,0}c_{2}^{\prime}}+\alpha(Nx_{3})^{\beta}-\frac{n_{2}Nx_{3}}{ac_{0}c_{2,0}c_{3}^{\prime}}\right)dx_{2}dx_{3}dy.

For any

Endgame

By Lemma

, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{R}(N)&\ll\frac{LM^{\frac{4}{3}}T^{\varepsilon}}{N^{\frac{2}{3}}}+LMT^{\varepsilon}\frac{R}{N^{\beta}}\left(\frac{(MN)^{\frac{1}{3}}}{C_{0}}\right)^{\eta}\\

) and choosing

Appendix A
Computations of Character Sums

To handle the character sum that we encounter, we need the following lemmata.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
A,B,C,k,u,v\in\mathbb{N}
with
C
squarefree. Consider the character sum defined recursively by

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{1}(A,B)

\displaystyle=S(u(\overline{A}-\overline{B}),v(A-B);C),

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{k+1}(A,B)

\displaystyle=\mathop{\sum\nolimits^{\mathrlap{\ast}}}_{\gamma{\@displayfalse\pmod{C}}}\mathcal{C}_{k}(\gamma,A)\mathcal{C}_{k}(\gamma,B).

Then we have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{k}(A,B)\ll((C,uv)C)^{2^{k-1}-\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{(C,A-B)}.

Proof.

We first assume that
C=p
is prime. If
p\mid uv
, then trivial bound
\mathcal{C}_{k}\ll p^{2^{k}-1}
follows. On the other hand, if
p\nmid uv(A-B)
, then we apply the method of Adolphson–Sperber
[
AS93
]
. For
k=1
, the Weil bound for Kloosterman sums implies
\mathcal{C}_{1}(A,B)\ll\sqrt{p}
. For
k=2
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{2}(A,B)=\mathop{\mathop{\sum\nolimits^{\mathrlap{\ast}}}\ \mathop{\sum\nolimits^{\mathrlap{\ast}}}\ \mathop{\sum\nolimits^{\mathrlap{\ast}}}}_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta{\@displayfalse\pmod{p}}}e\left(\frac{f_{2}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta)}{p}\right),

where

f_{2}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta)=u\alpha_{1}\overline{\beta}+v\overline{\alpha_{1}}\beta-u\alpha_{1}\overline{A}-v\overline{\alpha_{1}}A+u\alpha_{2}\overline{\beta}+v\overline{\alpha_{2}}\beta-u\alpha_{2}\overline{B}-v\overline{\alpha_{2}}B.

Considering
f_{2}(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}[x,y,z,(xyz)^{-1}]
, we see that the locus of
\frac{\partial f_{2,\sigma}}{\partial x}=\frac{\partial f_{2,\sigma}}{\partial y}=\frac{\partial f_{2,\sigma}}{\partial z}=0
is empty in
(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times})^{3}
for any face
\sigma
of the Newton polyhedron
\Delta(f)
since
p\nmid uv(A-B)
. Hence the work of Adolphson–Sperber
[
AS93
]
yields
\mathcal{C}_{2}(A,B)\ll p^{\frac{3}{2}}
.

For
k\geq 3
, we observe that the character sum
\mathcal{C}_{k}(A,B)
can be written in the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{k}(A,B)=\mathop{\mathop{\sum\nolimits^{\mathrlap{\ast}}}\cdots\mathop{\sum\nolimits^{\mathrlap{\ast}}}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{2^{k-1}}{\@displayfalse\pmod{p}}\\
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{2^{k-2}}{\@displayfalse\pmod{p}}\\
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{2^{k-2}-1}{\@displayfalse\pmod{p}}\end{subarray}}e\left(\frac{f_{k}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{2^{k-1}},\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{2^{k-2}},\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{2^{k-2}-1})}{p}\right),

where

f_{k}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{2^{k-1}},\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{2^{k-2}},\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{2^{k-2}-1})\\
=\sum_{j=1}^{2^{k-1}}\left(u\alpha_{j}\overline{\beta_{\lceil\frac{j}{2}\rceil}}+v\overline{\alpha_{j}}\beta_{\lceil\frac{j}{2}\rceil}-u\alpha_{j}\overline{\gamma_{g(j)}}-v\overline{\alpha_{j}}\gamma_{g(j)}\right)-u\alpha_{2^{k-2}}\overline{A}-u\overline{\alpha_{2^{k-2}}}A-v\alpha_{2^{k-1}}\overline{B}-v\overline{\alpha_{2^{k-1}}}B.

For
j=2^{\ell}+2^{\ell+2}h,\,2^{\ell}+2^{\ell+1}(2h+1)
with
\ell,h\geq 0
, the function
g(j)
above is given by

\displaystyle g(j)=2^{k-2}-2^{k-\ell-2}+h.

Considering
f_{k}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{2^{k}-1})\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{2^{k}-1},(x_{1}\cdots x_{2^{k}-1})^{-1}]
, we see that locus of
\frac{\partial f_{k,\sigma}}{\partial x_{j}}=0
for all
1\leq j\leq 2^{k}-1
is empty in
(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times})^{2^{k}-1}
for any face
\sigma
of the Newton polyhedron
\Delta(f_{k})
since
p\nmid uv(A-B)
. Hence the work of Adolphson–Sperber
[
AS93
]
yields
\mathcal{C}_{k}(A,B)\ll p^{2^{k-1}-\frac{1}{2}}
.

Finally, we perform induction to prove that

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{k}(A,B)\ll p^{2^{k-1}-\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{(p,A-B)}

when
p\nmid uv
. We are left with the case where
p\mid(A-B)
. Note that when
k=1
, the Weil bound for Kloosterman sums justifies the desired claim. For
k\geq 2
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{k}(A,B)=\mathop{\sum\nolimits^{\mathrlap{\ast}}}_{\gamma{\@displayfalse\pmod{p}}}\mathcal{C}_{k-1}(\gamma,A)\mathcal{C}_{k-1}(\gamma,B).

By induction hypothesis,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{k-1}(\gamma,A)\ll p^{2^{k-2}-\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{(p,\gamma-A)}.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{k}(A,B)\ll p^{2^{k-1}}=p^{2^{k-1}-\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{(p,A-B)}.

The Chinese Remainder Theorem completes the proof of Lemma
A.1
for squarefree
C
.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

Let
A,B,C,k,u,v,q_{1},q_{2},q_{3},q,Q\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
C\mid Q
,
q_{1},q_{2},q_{3}\mid q\mid Q
, and
(q_{3},\frac{CQ}{q_{3}})=1
. Consider the character sum defined recursively by

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{1}(A,B)

\displaystyle=\delta(A\equiv B{\@displayfalse\pmod{q}})S\left(\frac{u(\overline{A}-\overline{B})}{q_{1}},\frac{v(A-B)}{q_{2}};C\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{k+1}(A,B)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma{\@displayfalse\pmod{Q}}\\
(\gamma,\frac{Q}{q_{3}})=1\end{subarray}}\mathcal{C}_{k}(\gamma,A)\mathcal{C}_{k}(\gamma,B).

Then we have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{k}(A,B)\ll\left(\frac{Q}{q}C_{1}(C_{2},uv)(C,q^{\infty})\right)^{2^{k-1}},

where
C_{1}C_{2}=\frac{C}{(C,q^{\infty})}
with
C_{1}
squarefull,
C_{2}
squarefree, and
(C_{1},C_{2})=1
.

Proof.

Write
C_{0}=(C,q^{\infty})
,
C=C_{0}C^{\prime}
,
Q_{0}=(Q,q^{\infty})
and
Q=Q_{0}Q^{\prime}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{k}(A,B)=\mathcal{C}_{k,1}(A,B)\mathcal{C}_{k,2}(A,B),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{1,1}(A,B)

\displaystyle=\delta(A\equiv B{\@displayfalse\pmod{q}})S\left(\overline{C^{\prime 2}}\frac{u(\overline{A}-\overline{B})}{q_{1}},\frac{v(A-B)}{q_{2}};C_{0}\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{1,2}(A,B)

\displaystyle=S(\overline{C_{0}^{2}q_{1}q_{2}}u(\overline{A}-\overline{B}),v(A-B);C^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{k+1,1}(A,B)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma{\@displayfalse\pmod{Q_{0}}}\\
(\gamma,\frac{Q_{0}}{q_{3}})=1\end{subarray}}\mathcal{C}_{k,1}(\gamma,A)\mathcal{C}_{k,1}(\gamma,B),

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{k+1,2}(A,B)

\displaystyle=\mathop{\sum\nolimits^{\mathrlap{\ast}}}_{\gamma{\@displayfalse\pmod{Q^{\prime}}}}\mathcal{C}_{k,2}(\gamma,A)\mathcal{C}_{k,2}(\gamma,B)

\displaystyle=\varphi\left(\frac{Q^{\prime}}{(Q^{\prime},C^{\infty})}\right)\frac{(Q^{\prime},C^{\infty})}{C^{\prime}}\mathop{\sum\nolimits^{\mathrlap{\ast}}}_{\gamma{\@displayfalse\pmod{C^{\prime}}}}\mathcal{C}_{k,2}(\gamma,A)\mathcal{C}_{k,2}(\gamma,B).

For
\mathcal{C}_{k,1}(A,B)
, bounding the Kloosterman sum trivially implies

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{k,1}(A,B)\ll\left(\frac{Q_{0}}{q}\right)^{2^{k-1}-1}C_{0}^{2^{k-1}}.

For
\mathcal{C}_{k,2}(A,B)
, note that the character sum is an iteration of Kloosterman sums. Factorising
C^{\prime}=C_{1}C_{2}
uniquely with
C_{1}
squarefull,
C_{2}
squarefree, and
(C_{1},C_{2})=1
, and then applying the Chinese Remainder Theorem accordingly, Lemma
A.1
yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{k,2}(A,B)\ll\left(\varphi\left(\frac{Q^{\prime}}{(Q^{\prime},C^{\infty})}\right)\frac{(Q^{\prime},C^{\infty})}{C^{\prime}}\right)^{2^{k-1}-1}C_{1}^{2^{k}-1}\left(\left(C_{2},uv\right)C_{2}\right)^{2^{k-1}-\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{(C_{2},A-B)}.

Combining the above estimates completes the proof of Lemma
A.2
.
∎

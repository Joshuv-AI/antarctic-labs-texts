1.
Introduction

The slicing problem for functions asks for the greatest constant
s_{n},
depending only on the dimension
n,
such that for every origin-symmetric convex body
K
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of volume 1 and any continuous probability density
f
supported in
K
there exists a hyperplane
H
passing through the origin for which

\int_{H}f\geq s_{n}.

This problem was put forward in
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
and further investigated in
[
3
,
1
]
. The case where
f\equiv 1
is the slicing problem of Bourgain, for which the best lower bound so far, due to Klartag
[
7
]
, is
c(\log n)^{-1/2}
.

More precisely, let us define

s_{n}=\inf_{f,K}\max_{\xi\in S^{n-1}}\int_{K\cap\xi^{\perp}}f,

where the infimum is taken over the class of all even continuous probability densities defined on symmetric convex bodies
K
of volume
1
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Here
\xi^{\perp}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:(x,\xi)=0\}
is the central hyperplane perpendicular to
\xi.

It was proved in
[
10
]
that
s_{n}\geq\frac{1}{2}n^{-1/2}.
A more general result was proved in
[
11
]
,
as follows.

Theorem 1
.

Let
K
be an origin-symmetric convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and let
f
be an even continuous non-negative function on
K
. Then

\int_{K}f\leq d_{\operatorname{ovr}}(K,\mathcal{I}_{n})\frac{n}{n-1}c_{n}|K|_{n}^{1/n}\max_{\xi\in S^{n-1}}\int_{K\cap\xi^{\perp}}f,

where
c_{n}=\frac{|B_{2}^{n}|_{n}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}}{|B_{2}^{n-1}|_{n-1}}<1

and
d_{\operatorname{ovr}}(K,\mathcal{I}_{n})
is the outer volume ratio distance from
K
to the class of intersection bodies
\mathcal{I}_{n}
(see Section
2
for the definition).

The outer volume ratio distance to a class of convex bodies
\mathcal{C}
is defined by

d_{\operatorname{ovr}}(K,\mathcal{C})=\inf\left\{\left(\frac{|D|_{n}}{|K|_{n}}\right)^{1/n}:K\subseteq D,D\in\mathcal{C}\right\}.

Since the class of intersection bodies contains ellipsoids and
K
is symmetric, by John’s theorem one always has
d_{\operatorname{ovr}}(K,\mathcal{I}_{n})\leq\sqrt{n}
.
The constant
d_{\operatorname{ovr}}(K,\mathcal{I}_{n})
may be smaller for special classes of bodies
K
. For example,
if
K
is an unconditional convex body then
d_{\operatorname{ovr}}(K,\mathcal{I}_{n})\leq e
(see
[
11
]
), and if
K
is the unit ball of a subspace of
L_{p}([0,1])
then
d_{\operatorname{ovr}}(K,\mathcal{I}_{n})\leq\sqrt{p}
(see
[
16
,
13
]
).

On the other hand, the upper bound
s_{n}\leq cn^{-1/2}
was established in
[
5
]
up to a logarithmic term, and the proof was refined in

[
6
]
to remove the logarithmic factor.

Theorem 2
.

There exists a symmetric convex body
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and an even continuous function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
for which

\int_{K}f\geq c,\qquad\int_{K\cap\xi^{\perp}}f\leq\frac{c}{\sqrt{n}}\text{ and }\qquad|K|_{n}\leq c^{n}

for every
\xi\in S^{n-1}
, where
c>0
is an absolute constant.
Moreover,
f
belongs to the Schwartz space, and we have
\sqrt{n}\mathbb{B}\subseteq K
.

It immediately follows from Theorems
1
and
2
that

c\sqrt{n}\leq\sup_{K}d_{\operatorname{ovr}}(K,\mathcal{I}_{n})\leq\sqrt{n},

where
c>0
is a universal constant, and the supremum is taken over all origin-symmetric convex bodies
K
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}.

By applying a similar strategy to moments of functions on convex sets, it was shown in
[
1
]
(lower bound) and
[
12
]
(upper bound) that

c_{1}\sqrt{\frac{n}{p}}\leq\sup_{K}d_{\operatorname{ovr}}(K,L_{p}^{n})\leq c_{2}\sqrt{\frac{n}{p}},

where
c_{1},c_{2}
are absolute constants, and
L_{p}^{n}
is the class of unit balls of
n
-dimensional spaces that embed in
L_{p}([0,1]),\ p\geq 1
.

For a continuous function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
, the Radon transform of
f
is defined by

{\mathcal{R}}f(\xi,t)=\int_{\xi^{\perp}+t\xi}f,\qquad\xi\in S^{n-1},\ t\in\mathbb{R}.

Clearly, Theorems
1
and
2
can be stated as bounds for
{\mathcal{R}}f(\xi,0)
, and

s_{n}=\inf_{f,K}\max_{\xi\in S^{n-1}}{\mathcal{R}}(\chi_{K}f)(\xi,0)

where
\chi_{K}
is the indicator function of
K
.

We denote by
\mathcal{R}f(\xi,\cdot)_{t}^{(q)}(0)
the fractional derivative of order
q
of the function
t\mapsto{\mathcal{R}}f(\xi,t)
at
t=0
(see Section
2
for the definition).

The following Theorem is a variant of
[
4
, Theorem 2]
.

Theorem 3
.

Suppose
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an infinitely smooth origin-symmetric convex body,
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is a non-negative even infinitely smooth function and
q\in(-1,n-1)
is not an odd integer. Then

(1)

\int_{K}f\leq\frac{n}{(n-q-1)2^{q}\pi^{\frac{q-1}{2}}\Gamma(\frac{q+1}{2})}|K|_{n}^{\frac{q+1}{n}}\\
\times d_{\operatorname{ovr}}(K,L_{-1-q}^{n})^{q+1}\max_{\xi\in S^{n-1}}\frac{1}{\cos(\pi q/2)}\mathcal{R}f(\xi,\cdot)_{t}^{(q)}(0)

where
L_{-1-q}^{n}
is the class of star bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
that embed in
L_{-1-q}
(see Section
2
).

Note that Theorem
3
, as stated in
[
4
, Theorem 2]
requires
f
to have support inside
K
.
But a quick inspection of the proof shows that this is actually not necessary, and the proof carries over without modifications.

In this note we adapt the example of Theorem
2
to obtain a function with small fractional derivatives of the Radon transform, thus complementing Theorem
3
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
q\in[0,n-1),

q
is not an odd integer.
There exists an infinitely smooth, symmetric convex body
D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
of volume
1
, and an even continuous function
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
for which
\int_{D}g=1
and

\max_{\xi\in S^{n-1}}\frac{1}{\cos(\pi q/2)}\mathcal{R}g(\xi,\cdot)_{t}^{(q)}(0)\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}(c(q+1))^{\frac{q+1}{2}},\qquad\forall\xi\in S^{n-1}.

Note that the body
D
does not depend on
q
.

The reader should compare Theorem
4
with
[
4
, Formula (7)]
. Namely, for every
f,K
under the conditions of Theorem
3
,

(2)

\max_{\xi\in S^{n-1}}\frac{1}{\cos(\pi q/2)}\mathcal{R}f(\xi,\cdot)_{t}^{(q)}(0)\geq\frac{(c(q+1))^{\frac{q+1}{2}}}{(d_{\operatorname{ovr}}(K,L_{-1-q}^{n}))^{q+1}}\geq\left(\frac{c(q+1)}{n}\right)^{\frac{q+1}{2}}.

When
q=2k-1,\ k\in\mathbb{N},
we have

(3)

\lim_{q\to 2k-1}\frac{1}{\cos(\pi q/2)}\mathcal{R}f(\xi,\cdot)_{t}^{(q)}(0)\\
=(-1)^{k}(2k-1)!\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-2k}\left(Rf(\xi,t)-\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{t^{2j}}{(2j)!}(Rf(\xi,t))_{t}^{(2j)}(0)\right)dt,

and Theorems
3
and
4
hold with this quantity.

As a consequence of Theorem
4
we obtain

Corollary 5
.

For
q\in[0,n-1)
there exists an origin-symmetric convex body
K
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

d_{\operatorname{ovr}}(K,L_{-1-q}^{n})\geq c_{1}n^{\frac{1}{2(q+1)}}.

Combining this estimate with the upper estimate from
[
4
, Propositions 2 and 3]
, we get

c_{1}n^{\frac{1}{2(q+1)}}\leq\sup_{K}d_{\operatorname{ovr}}(K,L_{-1-q}^{n})\leq\min\left\{c_{2}\sqrt{\frac{n\log^{3}(\frac{ne}{q+1})}{q+1}},\sqrt{n}\right\},

where
c_{1},c_{2}
are absolute constants, and the supremum is taken over all origin-symmetric convex bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{n}.

2.
Notation and Auxiliary Results

A star body is a set of the form

K=\{0\}\cup\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}:|x|_{2}\leq\rho_{K}(x/|x|_{2})\}

where
\rho_{K}:\mathcal{S}^{n-1}\to(0,\infty)
is continuous and bounded.
The radial function
\rho_{K}
is extended to
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
as an homogeneous function of degree
-1
, this is
\rho_{K}(\lambda x)=\lambda^{-1}\rho_{K}(x)
.
We denote the volume of
K
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by
|K|_{n}.

The Minkowsky functional of a star body
K
is
\|x\|_{K}=\rho_{K}(x)^{-1}
.
A convex star body
K
is called a convex body, and
K
is said to be infinitely smooth if
\rho_{K}
is an infinitely smooth function of the sphere.

The radial metric in the class of star bodies is defined by

d_{\rho}(K,L)=\sup_{\xi\in S^{n-1}}|\rho_{K}(\xi)-\rho_{L}(\xi)|.

We use the Fourier transform of distributions and refer the reader to
[
14
]
for details.
For
f\in\mathcal{S}
, the Schwartz space of rapidly decreasing infinitely differentiable functions, the Fourier transform is defined by

\hat{f}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)e^{-i(x,\xi)}dx.

The Fourier transform of a distribution
f
is defined by
\langle\hat{f},\varphi\rangle=\langle f,\hat{\varphi}\rangle
for every
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}
.
With this normalization we have
\hat{\hat{f}}=(2\pi)^{n}f
for every even distribution
f
.

A star body
K
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
embeds (isometrically) in
L_{p},\ p>0
if there exists a finite Borel measure
\mu
on
S^{n-1}
so that

\|x\|_{K}^{p}=\int_{S^{n-1}}|(x,\xi)|^{p}d\mu(\xi),\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

We denote by
L_{p}^{n}
the set of all such bodies.

Following Lutwak
[
15
]
, we define the intersection body of a star body
K
as the star body
IK
for which
\rho_{IK}(\xi)=|K\cap\xi^{\perp}|_{n-1}
.
A more general class of bodies was introduced in
[
8
]
.
Given two star bodies
K,L\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and an integer
k\in[1,n]
we say that
K
is the
k
-intersection body of
L
if

|K\cap H^{\perp}|_{k}=|L\cap H|_{n-k},

for all
n-k
dimensional subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
The class of
k
-intersection bodies is defined as the closure in the radial metric of
k
-intersection bodies of star bodies.
For
k=1
we get the class of intersection bodies
{\mathcal{I}}_{n}.

We say that a star body
K
embeds in
L_{-p}
if
(\|\cdot\|_{K}^{-p})^{\wedge}
is a positive distribution, i.e.
\langle\|\cdot\|_{K}^{-p},\varphi\rangle\geq 0
for every non-negative test function
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}.
By
[
14
, Theorem 6.16]
, for integers
k\in[1,n-1]
,
K
embeds in
L_{-k}
if and only if it is a
k
-intersection body (note that 1-intersection body is the usual intersection body of Lutwak). Also, every origin-symmetric convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
embeds in
L_{-n+3};
see
[
14
, Corollary 4.9]
. Note that
the unit balls of finite dimensional subspaces of
L_{p},\ 0<p\leq 2
embed in
L_{-p}
for every
p\in(0,n).
In particular, all ellipsoids embed in
L_{-p}.

For details, we refer the reader to
[
14
, Section 6.3]
. We denote the class of bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
that embed in
L_{-p}
by
L_{-p}^{n}.

For a test function
\varphi:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
the fractional derivative of order
q\in\mathbb{C}
at
0
, is defined as the analytic continuation to
q\in\mathbb{C}
of

\varphi^{(q)}(0)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(-q)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-1-q}\varphi(t)dt.

If
\varphi
is
m
-times continuously differentiable and integrable, then
\varphi^{(q)}(0)
can be extended to an analytic function at
-1<\Re(q)<m
.
Moreover, one has

(4)

\varphi^{(q)}(0)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(-q)}\int_{0}^{1}t^{-1-q}\left(h(t)-h(0)-\cdots-h^{(m-1)}(0)\frac{t^{m-1}}{(m-1)!}\right)dt\\
+\frac{1}{\Gamma(-q)}\int_{1}^{\infty}t^{-1-q}h(t)dt+\frac{1}{\Gamma(-q)}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{h^{(k)(0)}}{k!(k-q)}

for
-1<\Re(q)<m
, and the definition does not depend on the choice of
m
as long as
\varphi
is
m
-times continuously differentiable.

The Fourier transform of negative powers of the Euclidean norm
|\cdot|_{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
are given by

(5)

(|\cdot|_{2}^{-q})^{\wedge}(\xi)=\frac{\pi^{n/2}\Gamma{\left(\frac{n-q}{2}\right)}2^{n-q}}{\Gamma{(q/2)}}|\xi|_{2}^{q-n}

for
q\in(0,n);
see
[
2
]
.

Definition 6
.

For a function
f\in\mathcal{S}
and
q\in(-n,\infty)
the fractional Laplacian of the order
q/2
is defined by

(-\Delta)^{{q}/2}f=(|\xi|^{q}\hat{f}(\xi))^{\vee},

where
\vee
stands for the inverse Fourier transform.

By (
5
), for
q\in(0,n)
we have the representation

(6)

(-\Delta)^{{-q}/2}f=c_{n,q}|\cdot|^{-n+q}*f,

where

c_{n,q}=\frac{\pi^{n/2}2^{n-q}\Gamma\left(\frac{n-q}{2}\right)}{(2\pi)^{n}\Gamma\left(\frac{q}{2}\right)}.

Lemma 7
.

For
q\in(-1,\infty)
not an odd integer, the fractional Laplacian and the Radon transform are related by

\frac{1}{\cos(\pi q/2)}\mathcal{R}f(\xi,\cdot)_{t}^{(q)}(0)={\mathcal{R}}((-\Delta)^{{q}/2}f)(\xi,0)

Proof.

By Lemma 2.23 in
[
14
]
, if
q\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{-1,-3,-5,\ldots\}
and
t,\xi\in\mathbb{R}
,

(|t|^{-1-q})^{\wedge}(\xi)=2\Gamma(-q)\cos(\pi q/2)|\xi|^{q}

in the sense of analytic continuation of distributions.

We use the well-known relation between the Radon and Fourier transforms; see
[
14
, Lemma 2.11]
.
For every fixed
\xi\in S^{n-1}
and any Schwartz test function
f
, the Fourier transform of the function
t\to\mathcal{R}f(\xi,t)
is the function
z\to\hat{f}(z\xi)
. Now, for
q\in(-1,0)
, we have

(7)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\cos(\pi q/2)}\mathcal{R}f(\xi,\cdot)_{t}^{(q)}(0)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\cos(\pi q/2)}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2\Gamma(-q)}|t|^{-1-q}{\mathcal{R}}f(\xi,t)dt

(8)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|z|^{q}{\mathcal{R}}f(\xi,\cdot)_{t}^{\wedge}(z)dz

(9)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|z\xi|_{2}^{q}\hat{f}(z\xi)dz

(10)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}((-\Delta)^{{q}/2}f)^{\wedge}(z\xi)dz

(11)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\mathcal{R}}{((-\Delta)^{{q}/2}f)}(\xi,\cdot)_{z}^{\wedge}(z)dz

(12)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{R}}{((-\Delta)^{{q}/2}f)}(\xi,0).

By analytic continuation with respect to
q
we obtain the equality for every
q\in(-1,\infty),
not an odd integer.
∎

For the proof of the main results we need two lemmas.

Lemma 8
.

For positive measurable functions
f,g
, and measurable sets
A,B
,

\int_{A+B}f*g(x)dx\geq\int_{A}f(x)dx\int_{B}g(x)dx

Proof.

Since the support of
(f\chi_{A})*(g\chi_{B})
is contained in
A+B
, we have

(13)

\displaystyle\int_{A+B}f*g(x)dx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{A+B}(f\chi_{A})*(g\chi_{B})(x)dx

(14)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(f\chi_{A})*(g\chi_{B})(x)dx

(15)

\displaystyle=\int_{A}f(x)dx\int_{B}g(x)dx

∎

Lemma 9
.

For
0\leq\mu\leq\lambda
we have

\lambda^{\mu}\Gamma(\lambda-\mu)\geq\Gamma(\lambda).

Proof.

The equality holds for
\mu=0
. Since the left-hand side is log-convex with respect to
\mu
, it suffices to show that
its logarithmic derivative at
\mu=0
is non-negative.
By Jensen’s inequality,

(17)

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}\left[\mu\log(\lambda)+\log\Gamma(\lambda-\mu)\right]\right|_{\mu=0}

\displaystyle=\log(\lambda)-\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)}{\Gamma(\lambda)}

(18)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\lambda)}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}-\log(t/\lambda)t^{\lambda-1}e^{-t}dt\right)

(19)

\displaystyle\geq-\log\left(\frac{1}{\lambda\Gamma(\lambda)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\lambda}e^{-t}dt\right)

(20)

\displaystyle=0

where we used that
\Gamma(\lambda+1)=\lambda\Gamma(\lambda)
.
∎

Proof of the Main Results

Proof of Theorem
4
.

Consider
K
and

from Theorem
2
, and take

.
By Lemma
8
and equation (

),

(21)

\displaystyle\int_{2K}h

\displaystyle\geq c_{n,q}\int_{\sqrt{n}\mathbb{B}}|x|_{2}^{-n+q}dx\int_{K}fdx

(22)

\displaystyle\geq c_{n,q}n\omega_{n}\frac{n^{q/2}}{q}c=cd_{n,q}.

where
d_{n,q}=\frac{2^{-q}n^{q/2}\Gamma\left(\frac{n-q}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{q}{2}+1\right)}
.

By Lemma

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\cos(\pi q/2)}\mathcal{R}h(\xi,\cdot)_{t}^{(q)}(0)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\cos(\pi q/2)}\mathcal{R}((-\Delta)^{{-q}/2}f)(\xi,\cdot)_{t}^{(q)}(0)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{R}}f(\xi,0)\leq\frac{c}{\sqrt{n}}.

We consider

so that

and

1
.
Combining (

), (

) and applying Lemma

, the inequality
|K|_{n}\leq c^{n}
and Stirling’s formula

(25)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\cos(\pi q/2)}\mathcal{R}g(\xi,\cdot)_{t}^{(q)}(0)

(26)

\displaystyle\leq c^{q}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\frac{2^{q}\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{q}{2}+1\right)}{n^{q/2}\Gamma\left(\frac{n-q}{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq c^{q}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\Gamma\left(\frac{q}{2}+1\right)

(28)

\displaystyle\leq c^{q}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}(q+1)^{\frac{q+1}{2}}.

∎

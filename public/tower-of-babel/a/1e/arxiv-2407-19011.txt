Field-induced magnon decays in dipolar quantum magnets
Andrew D. Kim, Ahmed Khalifa, Shubhayu Chatterjee

Abstract
We investigate the spontaneous disintegration of magnons in two-dimensional ferromagnets and antiferromagnets dominated by long-range dipolar interactions. Analyzing kinematic constraints, we show that the unusual dispersion of dipolar ferromagnets in a uniform magnetic field precludes magnon-decay at all fields, in sharp contrast to short-range exchange-driven magnets. However, in a staggered magnetic field, magnons can decay in both dipolar ferromagnets and antiferromagnets. Remarkably, such decays do not require a minimum threshold field, and happen over a nearly fixed fraction of the Brillouin Zone in the XY limit, highlighting the significant role played by dipolar interactions. In addition, topological transitions in the decay surfaces lead to singularities in the magnon spectrum. Regularizing such singular behavior via a self-consistent approach, we make predictions for dynamical spin correlations accessible to near-term quantum simulators and sensors.



The dynamics of a quantum phase of matter are typically governed by its low-energy excitations.
These elementary excitations, such as phonons in solids, electrons or holes in metals, and magnons in ferromagnets or antiferromagnets, are conventionally assumed to be stable and long-lived
Ashcroft and Mermin 1976
;
Simon 2013
;
Ziman 2001
.
However, collisions with other quasiparticles or spontaneous disintegration into two or more quasiparticles can render a quasiparticle unstable
Lifshitz and Pitaevskii 2013
.
At low temperatures, the vanishing density of quasiparticles implies that an excitation cannot decay via collisions.
Nevertheless, subtle quantum many-body effects can still lead to spontaneous decay of a quasiparticle.
Such decay can have drastic consequences, such as a termination of the spectrum in bosonic superfluids
Pitaevskii 1959
;
Lifshitz and Pitaevskii 2013
;
Graf et al. 1974
;
Glyde et al. 1998
, or the destruction of long-lived excitations at the Fermi surface in non-Fermi liquids
Schofield 1999
;
Senthil 2008
;
Lee 2018
;
Chowdhury et al. 2022
, that render the quasiparticle desciption invalid.

In short-range interacting quantum magnets with collinear magnetic order, e.g. XY ferromagnets and Néel antiferromagnets, external magnetic fields are known to induce magnon decays

.
For instance, in Heisenberg antiferromagnets on a cubic lattice, all magnons remain perturbatively stable up to a threshold magnetic field

As one increases

On the materials front, dipolar couplings describe lattices of charge-neutral excitons with electrical dipole moments in moiré heterostructures

;

While a flurry of work

Specifically, the long-range tail of the dipolar interaction fundamentally alters the dispersion of magnons
Peter et al. 2012

Understanding potential instabilities of magnons is a necessary pre-requisite to decipher the quantum dynamics of spins in long-range interacting magnets.

In this paper, we study the stability of magnons in long-range interacting quantum magnets in the presence of an external magnetic field.
Specifically, we focus on quantum fluctuation-induced decays at zero temperature in 2D collinear ferromagnets (FM) and antiferromagnets (AFM), and consider both uniform and staggered magnetic fields motivated by recent experiments in Rydberg arrays
Chen et al. 2023a
;
Chen et al. 2023b
.
Our central results are as follows.
First, using non-linear spin-wave theory, we show that in dipolar XY FMs, magnons are absolutely stable in uniform magnetic fields due to kinematic constraints.
At non-zero staggered fields, magnons can decay.
Such decays occur at infinitesimal fields, and the decay region does not evolve as a function of the field, both in contrast to previously studied short-range interacting magnets in uniform fields.
Remarkably, dipolar XY AFMs, expected to behave like short-range interacting magnets as the dipolar interactions frustrate AFM order, also lack a threshold field
H^{*}
for magnon decay.
Further, for dipolar XY AFMs in staggered fields, the decay region decreases with increasing
H
, in contrast to short range AFMs.
Second, we identify singularities in the magnon spectrum due to divergent decay-rates.
We show that such singularities originate from a transfer of spectral weight to the single magnon mode from the two-magnon continuum, and correspond precisely to topological transitions in the decay contour with a Van Hove singularity in the two-magnon density of states.
Third, we investigate the implications of magnon decay, and demonstrate that the dynamical spin correlations show striking deviations from mean-field predictions.

.-

Further, in the vicinity of the Van Hove singularity, the structure factor broadens out, carrying clear signatures of magnon-instability due to mixing of transverse and longitudinal (two-magnon) modes.

Conclusion and outlook
.-
In this work, we critically examined the stability of magnons in dipolar ferromagnets and antiferromagnets on a square lattice in presence of an external magnetic field.
Our results can be explored both in 2D VdW materials, as well as existing and near-term quantum simulators.
In fact, the stability of ferromagnetic magnons relative to antiferromagnetic magnons at zero-field has been observed in a recent quench experiment in a 2D Rydberg array realizing XY-magnetism
Chen et al. 2023b
.
Our work predicts that the antiferromagnetic magnons can be stabilized in the Heisenberg limit in a weak uniform field
5
5

5

Note that this also requires the 1-3 magnon decays to be suppressed by the field, which we demonstrate in the supplement
sup
, enabling a better experimental characterization of their dispersion.
Our results can also be leveraged to identify appropriate phase spaces for stable magnons, a pre-requisite to transmit information via spin degrees of freedom.

On the theoretical front, our work sets the stage for further investigations of spin-dynamics in long-range interacting magnets.
While our results apply to the thermodynamic limit, a natural next step is to consider the interplay of magnons with the Anderson tower of states
Roscilde et al. 2023
in finite-sized systems of experimental relevance.
Further, our formalism can be directly extended to study magnon-dynamics in non-collinear dipolar magnets on non-bipartite lattices
Goetze et al. 2016
;
Homeier et al. 2024
;
Liu et al. 2024
, provided magnetic order is not precluded by valence bond solids or quantum spin liquids
Bintz et al. 2024
;
Machado et al 2024
.
Finally, our work opens the door to studying the effect of tunable power-law interactions on magnon dynamics, as realized in driven trapped ion-chains
Richerme et al. 2014
.

For the staggered field dipolar XXZ FM, we take
H_{i}=\eta_{i}H
and
J>0

S1

The ground state has all spins aligned in-plane along an arbitrary direction, which we take to be
\hat{x}_{0}
for concreteness.
At nonzero
H

\pm\hat{z}_{0}
direction depending on the sign of
H_{i}
, as seen in Figure

Then, to simplify calculations, we move to a sublattice-dependent twisted reference frame for the spins

\displaystyle S^{x}_{j}

\displaystyle=S^{x_{0}}_{j}\cos\theta_{j}+S^{z_{0}}_{j}\sin\theta_{j}

\displaystyle S^{y}_{j}

\displaystyle=S^{y_{0}}_{j}

\displaystyle S^{z}_{j}

\displaystyle=-S^{x_{0}}_{j}\sin\theta_{j}+S^{z_{0}}_{j}\cos\theta_{j}

(S2)

where
\theta_{j}=\eta_{j}\theta
and
\eta_{i}=e^{i{\mathbf{Q}}\cdot{\mathbf{r}}_{j}}
, such that all spins are aligned along
\hat{x}
in the twisted frame.
We then use a Holstein-Primakoff transformation in the twisted frame, introducing bosonic operators
a_{j},a^{\dagger}_{j}
in the large-

\displaystyle S^{x}_{j}

\displaystyle=S-a^{\dagger}_{j}a_{j}

\displaystyle S^{+}_{j}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{(2S-a^{\dagger}_{j}a_{j})}a_{j}=\sqrt{2S}\sqrt{\left(1-\frac{a^{\dagger}_{j}a_{j}}{2S}\right)}a_{j}\approx\sqrt{2S}\left(1-\frac{a^{\dagger}_{j}a_{j}}{4S}+\cdots\right)a_{j}

\displaystyle S^{-}_{j}

\displaystyle=a^{\dagger}_{j}\sqrt{(2S-a^{\dagger}_{j}a_{j})}\approx\sqrt{2S}a^{\dagger}_{j}\left(1-\frac{a^{\dagger}_{j}a_{j}}{4S}+\cdots\right)

The mean-field result is found by considering terms up to

II
Decay threshold boundary for XY FM in staggered fields

In this section, we provide a formal proof for the two-magnon decay boundary for the dipolar XY FM in a staggered field
H_{i}=\eta_{i}H
.
Specifically, we show that the kinematic constraint for
1\to 2
magnon-decays,
\omega_{\mathbf{k}}=\omega_{\mathbf{q}}+\omega_{{\mathbf{k}}-{\mathbf{q}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}
, is satisfied for some
{\mathbf{q}}
in the BZ only if
{\mathbf{k}}
lies outside the diamond shaped region defined by
|k_{x}\pm k_{y}|=\pi
containing the point
\Gamma
.

To start, we note that the dispersion
\omega_{\mathbf{k}}
is non-negative everywhere and
\omega_{{\mathbf{k}}}\xrightarrow{{\mathbf{k}}\to 0}0
.
Therefore, if
\omega_{\mathbf{k}}>\omega_{{\mathbf{k}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}
, by continuity we must have some small
{\mathbf{q}}
that satisfies
\omega_{\mathbf{k}}=\omega_{\mathbf{q}}+\omega_{{\mathbf{k}}-{\mathbf{q}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}
.
Further, positivity of
\omega_{\mathbf{k}}
implies

\omega_{\mathbf{k}}>\omega_{{\mathbf{k}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}\Leftrightarrow\omega_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}>\omega_{{\mathbf{k}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}^{2}
.
We can substitute the actual dispersion relation to simplify the condition:

\displaystyle(\omega_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}-\omega_{{\mathbf{k}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}^{2})/(JS)^{2}

\displaystyle=(\varepsilon_{0}-\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{k}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}\sin^{2}{\theta})(\varepsilon_{0}-\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}})-(\varepsilon_{0}-\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{k}}}\sin^{2}{\theta})(\varepsilon_{0}-\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{k}}+{\mathbf{Q}}})=\varepsilon_{0}\cos^{2}\theta(\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{k}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}-\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}})>0

(S24)

Since we are considering
H<H_{c}
, we know that
0\leq\theta<\pi/2
, meaning that
\varepsilon_{0}\cos^{2}\theta>0
(
\varepsilon_{0}\approx 9.033
). Therefore,
\omega_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}-\omega_{{\mathbf{k}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}^{2}>0
if and only if
\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{k}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}>\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}
.
Thus, the condition for nontrivial two-magnon decays to exist can be simplified into a condition on the dipolar sum
\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}
.
Rewriting, we have that:

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{k}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}-\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{k}}}=\sum_{|{\mathbf{r}}_{j}|\not=0}=\frac{1}{|{\mathbf{r}}_{j}|^{3}}e^{i{\mathbf{k}}\cdot{\mathbf{r}}_{j}}(e^{i{\mathbf{Q}}\cdot{\mathbf{r}}_{j}}-1)=\sum_{m,n}^{{}^{\prime}}\frac{1}{(m^{2}+n^{2})^{3/2}}e^{i(k_{x}m+k_{y}n)}(e^{i\pi(m+n)}-1)

(S25)

To proceed, we note that

e^{i\pi(m+n)}-1=\begin{cases}0&2\mid(m+n)\\
-2&2\nmid(m+n)\end{cases}

Then, we may rewrite the sum again as

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{k}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}-\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{k}}}=-2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\\
2\nmid(m+n)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(m^{2}+n^{2})^{3/2}}e^{i(k_{x}m+k_{y}n)}

(S26)

From numerical observations, we are inspired to look at the constraint
k_{y}=\pi-k_{x}
, giving us:

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{k}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}-\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{k}}}=-2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\\
2\nmid(m+n)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(m^{2}+n^{2})^{3/2}}e^{i(k_{x}(m-n)+\pi n)}

(S27)

At this point we shall consider
m=a
and
n=b
, which are arbitrary. The term in the sum that these two values for
m,n
give is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(a^{2}+b^{2})^{3/2}}e^{ik_{x}(a-b)}e^{i\pi b}.

(S28)

To find a set of different values for
m,n
in the sum whose term cancels this one, consider
m=-b
and
n=-a
. The term in the sum that these two values for
m,n
give is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{((-b)^{2}+(-a)^{2})^{3/2}}e^{i(k_{x}((-b)-(-a))+\pi(-a))}=\frac{1}{(a^{2}+b^{2})^{3/2}}e^{ik_{x}(a-b)}e^{-i\pi a}

(S29)

By the summation constraint, we know that
2\nmid(a+b)
, meaning
a
and
b
have different parity, which implies that

\displaystyle e^{i\pi b}=-e^{i\pi a}=-e^{-i\pi a}

(S30)

Therefore, the term in the sum from
m=a,n=b
is exactly cancelled by the term from
m=-b,n=-a
. Since
a,b
were arbitrary, this cancellation occurs for all values of
m,n
where
2\nmid(m+n)
, meaning that
\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{k}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}-\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{k}}}=0
under the constraint that
k_{y}=\pi-k_{x}
.
Similarly, we can show that the same condition also holds for
k_{y}=-\pi-k_{x}
and
k_{y}=\pm\pi+{\mathbf{k}}_{x}
, thereby completing our proof.

II.1
Proof of nearest-neighbor XY decay region boundary

Following similar logic to that used in the previous subsection, we conclude that the kinematically allowed decays for an arbitrary
{\mathbf{k}}
are those where
{\mathbf{q}}\approx 0
and
{\mathbf{q}}\approx{\mathbf{k}}+{\mathbf{Q}}
.
We then propose the condition that if
E_{\mathbf{k}}>E_{{\mathbf{k}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}
, then there exists a nontrivial value of
{\mathbf{q}}
for which the two-magnon decay condition is satisfied.

Again, we note that the dispersion
E_{\mathbf{k}}
is non-negative everywhere; therefore,
E_{\mathbf{k}}>E_{{\mathbf{k}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}\Leftrightarrow E_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}>E_{{\mathbf{k}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}^{2}
.
Then there exist nontrivial decays if
E_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}-E_{{\mathbf{k}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}^{2}>0
.
We can substitute the actual dispersion to simplify the condition:

\displaystyle E_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}-E_{{\mathbf{k}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}^{2}

\displaystyle=(\gamma_{0}+\gamma_{{\mathbf{k}}}\sin^{2}{\theta})(\gamma_{0}-\gamma_{\mathbf{k}})-(\gamma_{0}+\gamma_{{\mathbf{k}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}\sin^{2}{\theta})(\gamma_{0}-\gamma_{{\mathbf{k}}+{\mathbf{Q}}})

\displaystyle=\gamma_{0}(1-\sin^{2}\theta)(\gamma_{{\mathbf{k}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}-\gamma_{\mathbf{k}})+\sin^{2}\theta(\gamma_{{\mathbf{k}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}^{2}-\gamma_{{\mathbf{k}}}^{2})

\displaystyle=\gamma_{0}(1-\sin^{2}\theta)(\gamma_{{\mathbf{k}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}-\gamma_{\mathbf{k}})>0

(S31)

Since we are considering
\delta<\delta_{c}
, we know that
0\leq\theta<\pi/2
, meaning that
1-\sin^{2}\theta>0
. Also,
\gamma_{0}=4
, so
\gamma_{0}(1-\sin^{2}\theta)>0
. So
E_{\mathbf{k}}^{2}-E_{{\mathbf{k}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}^{2}>0
if and only if
\gamma_{{\mathbf{k}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}>\gamma_{\mathbf{k}}
.

Thus, the condition for nontrivial two-magnon decays to exist can be simplified into a condition on the nearest-neighbor sum
\gamma_{\mathbf{k}}
. The curve along which
\gamma_{\mathbf{k}}=\gamma_{{\mathbf{k}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}
is the curve along which the decay region boundary lies, since
\gamma_{\mathbf{k}}
monotonically decreases, and
\gamma_{{\mathbf{k}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}
monotonically increases, from
0
to
\pi
. Again, we look at the line
k_{y}=\pi-k_{x}
and observe that

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mathbf{k}}

\displaystyle=2\big[\cos{k_{x}}+\cos{(\pi-k_{x})}\big]

\displaystyle\gamma_{{\mathbf{k}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}

\displaystyle=2\big[\cos{(k_{x}+\pi)}+\cos{(\pi-k_{x}+\pi)}\big]=2\big[\cos{(k_{x}-\pi)}+\cos{k_{x}}\big]=\gamma_{\mathbf{k}}

(S32)

Thus, we see that the decay region boundary is along the line
k_{y}=\pi-k_{x}
.

IV
Threshold behavior

In this section, we provide a detailed derivation of the magnon-decay rate at the threshold, i.e., near the decay boundary for the dipolar XY FM in a staggered field.
Because of the
C_{4}
symmetry of the problem, we can restrict ourselves to the first quadrant of the BZ.
Consider a magnon with initial momentum
{\mathbf{k}}={\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{p}}
, where
{\mathbf{k}}_{0}
is a point on the decay boundary (i.e.,
k_{0,x}+k_{0,y}=\pi
), and
|{\mathbf{p}}|
is small.
In the one-loop approximation, its decay rate is given by

\Gamma_{{\mathbf{k}}={\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{p}}}=\pi\int\frac{d^{2}q}{(2\pi)^{2}}\,|V^{(3)}_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{p}},{\mathbf{q}}}|^{2}\,\delta(\omega_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{p}}}-\omega_{\mathbf{q}}-\omega_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{p}}-{\mathbf{q}}+{\mathbf{Q}}})

(S33)

It is intuitively expected and numerically confirmed that the magnons near the threshold decay by emitting two magnons, one of which is a Goldstone magnon and the other one is a near-threshold magnon.
W.l.o.g., we can hence evaluate the integral when
{\mathbf{q}}
is small, and multiply the final result by 2.
Taylor expanding the argument of the delta function (that imposes energy conservation) everywhere except
{\mathbf{q}}=0
where it is non-analytic, we find that

\omega_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{p}}}-\omega_{\mathbf{q}}-\omega_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{p}}-{\mathbf{q}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}\approx({\mathbf{v}}_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}}+{\mathbf{v}}_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{Q}}})\cdot{\mathbf{p}}-{\mathbf{v}}_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{Q}}}\cdot{\mathbf{q}}-\bar{c}\sqrt{|{\mathbf{q}}|}=(v_{x}+v_{y})(p_{x}+p_{y})-(v_{y}q_{x}+v_{x}q_{y})-\bar{c}\sqrt{|{\mathbf{q}}|}

(S34)

where
\bar{c}=JS\sqrt{2\pi(\varepsilon_{0}-\varepsilon_{\mathbf{Q}}\sin^{2}\theta)}
, and in the last step we defined
v_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}}\equiv(v_{x},v_{y})
, then
{\mathbf{v}}_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{Q}}}=-(v_{y},v_{x})
using
{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathbf{k}}
is an odd function of
{\mathbf{k}}
and its reflection about the diagonal
k_{x}=k_{y}
interchanges the x and y components.
We note that the argument of the delta function only depends on
p_{x}+p_{y}=\sqrt{2}{\mathbf{p}}\cdot\hat{{\mathbf{Q}}}
. i.e., the normal distance of the point
{\mathbf{k}}
from the decay threshold line.
Further, for small
|{\mathbf{q}}|
, the integrand is dominated by the square root dispersion and the linear term in
q_{x},q_{y}
can be neglected, leading to the following simplification for the decay rate:

\Gamma_{{\mathbf{k}}={\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{p}}}\approx\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{2}(v_{x}+v_{y})}\int d^{2}{\mathbf{q}}\,|V^{(3)}_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{p}},{\mathbf{q}}}|^{2}\,\delta({\mathbf{p}}\cdot\hat{{\mathbf{Q}}}-c\sqrt{|{\mathbf{q}}|})

(S35)

which is Eq. (
3
) in the main text.

From the above analysis, it is evident that we only need to consider the cubic vertex squared for momenta
{\mathbf{q}}
that satisfy
c\sqrt{q}={\mathbf{p}}\cdot\hat{{\mathbf{Q}}}
, which we turn to next.
We begin with the definition of the vertex.

V^{(3)}_{{\mathbf{k}},{\mathbf{q}}}=C\left[f({\mathbf{k}},{\mathbf{q}})-g({\mathbf{k}},{\mathbf{q}})-g({\mathbf{k}},{\mathbf{k}}-{\mathbf{q}}+{\mathbf{Q}})\right]

where

\displaystyle f({\mathbf{k}},{\mathbf{q}})

\displaystyle\equiv\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{k}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}(u_{\mathbf{k}}-v_{\mathbf{k}})(u_{\mathbf{q}}v_{{\mathbf{k}}-{\mathbf{q}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}+v_{\mathbf{q}}u_{{\mathbf{k}}-{\mathbf{q}}+{\mathbf{Q}}})

\displaystyle g({\mathbf{k}},{\mathbf{q}})

\displaystyle\equiv\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{q}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}(u_{\mathbf{q}}-v_{\mathbf{q}})(u_{\mathbf{k}}u_{{\mathbf{k}}-{\mathbf{q}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}+v_{\mathbf{k}}v_{{\mathbf{k}}-{\mathbf{q}}+{\mathbf{Q}}})

\displaystyle g({\mathbf{k}},{\mathbf{k}}-{\mathbf{q}}+{\mathbf{Q}})

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{k}}-{\mathbf{q}}}(u_{{\mathbf{k}}-{\mathbf{q}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}-v_{{\mathbf{k}}-{\mathbf{q}}+{\mathbf{Q}}})(u_{\mathbf{k}}u_{\mathbf{q}}+v_{\mathbf{k}}v_{\mathbf{q}})

and
C=\frac{J}{2}\sqrt{2S}\sin\theta\cos\theta
is a complex constant.
Now, we consider a point near the decay threshold
{\mathbf{k}}={\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{p}}
, where
{\mathbf{k}}_{0}
lies on the decay boundary and
|{\mathbf{p}}|
is small.
Since
|{\mathbf{p}}|
is small, we also need
|{\mathbf{q}}|
(or
|{\mathbf{k}}-{\mathbf{q}}+{\mathbf{Q}}|
) to be small for energy conservation to be satisfied.
Since the vertex is symmetric under
{\mathbf{q}}\to{\mathbf{k}}-{\mathbf{q}}+{\mathbf{Q}}
, we can only look at the case where
|{\mathbf{q}}|
is small.
In this limit, we expand each of the functions above, and keep only the leading order term in
|{\mathbf{q}}|
.
If the term can be Taylor expanded, we will only keep the first (constant) term, while if it is non-analytic we will keep the leading non-analytic dependence.
We will come back to examine the validity of this later - it will turn out that is not quite valid (we need to keep derivative terms) but it still gives us the correct scaling.

We first note that,

\displaystyle u_{\mathbf{q}}-v_{\mathbf{q}}=\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{0}-\varepsilon_{\mathbf{q}}}{\varepsilon_{0}-\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{q}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}\sin^{2}\theta}\right)^{1/4}\implies u_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}}-v_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}}=u_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{Q}}}-v_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{Q}}}

(S36)

since
\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{Q}}}=\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}}
for
{\mathbf{k}}_{0}
lying on the decay boundary.
This, together with the constraint
u_{\mathbf{q}}^{2}-v_{\mathbf{q}}^{2}=1
for all
{\mathbf{q}}
implies that
u_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}}+v_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}}=u_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{Q}}}+v_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{Q}}}
.
Combining these two results, we find that
u_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}}=u_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{Q}}}
and
v_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}}=v_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{Q}}}
when
{\mathbf{k}}_{0}
lies on the boundary.
We also note that for small
q
,

\displaystyle u_{\mathbf{q}}-v_{\mathbf{q}}\approx\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{0}-\varepsilon_{\mathbf{q}}}{\varepsilon_{0}-\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{Q}}}\sin^{2}\theta}\right)^{1/4}\approx\left(\frac{2\pi a|{\mathbf{q}}|}{\varepsilon_{0}-\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{Q}}}\sin^{2}\theta}\right)^{1/4}\implies u_{\mathbf{q}}+v_{\mathbf{q}}=\frac{1}{u_{\mathbf{q}}-v_{\mathbf{q}}}\approx\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{0}-\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{Q}}}\sin^{2}\theta}{2\pi a|{\mathbf{q}}|}\right)^{1/4}

(S37)

Thus, both
u_{\mathbf{q}}
and
v_{\mathbf{q}}
will diverge as
|{\mathbf{q}}|^{-1/4}
for small
|{\mathbf{q}}|
, but the combination
u_{\mathbf{q}}-v_{\mathbf{q}}\sim|{\mathbf{q}}|^{1/4}
does not diverge.

Using the simplifications above, we have, to leading order

\displaystyle f({\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{p}},{\mathbf{q}})

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{p}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}(u_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{p}}}-v_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{p}}})(u_{\mathbf{q}}v_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{Q}}+{\mathbf{p}}-{\mathbf{q}}}+v_{\mathbf{q}}u_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{Q}}+{\mathbf{p}}-{\mathbf{q}}})\approx\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}}(u_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}}-v_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}})(u_{\mathbf{q}}v_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}}+v_{\mathbf{q}}u_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}})

\displaystyle g({\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{p}},{\mathbf{q}})

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{q}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}(u_{\mathbf{q}}-v_{\mathbf{q}})(u_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{p}}}u_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{p}}-{\mathbf{q}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}+v_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{p}}}v_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{p}}-{\mathbf{q}}+{\mathbf{Q}}})\approx\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{Q}}}(u_{\mathbf{q}}-v_{\mathbf{q}})(u^{2}_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}}+v^{2}_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}})

\displaystyle g({\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{p}},{\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{p}}-{\mathbf{q}}+{\mathbf{Q}})

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{p}}-{\mathbf{q}}}(u_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{p}}-{\mathbf{q}}+{\mathbf{Q}}}-v_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{p}}-{\mathbf{q}}+{\mathbf{Q}}})(u_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{p}}}u_{\mathbf{q}}+v_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{p}}}v_{\mathbf{q}})\approx\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}}(u_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}}-v_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}})(u_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}}u_{\mathbf{q}}+v_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}}v_{\mathbf{q}})

In all of the above, we have expanded in a Taylor series in
{\mathbf{q}}
and
{\mathbf{p}}-{\mathbf{q}}
, which are both small, except the terms are non-analytic at small
{\mathbf{q}}
, and also repeatedly used
\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}}=\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{Q}}},u_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}}=u_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{Q}}},v_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}}=v_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{Q}}}
.
Within this approximation:

\displaystyle f({\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{p}},{\mathbf{q}})-g({\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{p}},{\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{p}}-{\mathbf{q}}+{\mathbf{Q}})\approx-\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}}(u_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}}-v_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}})^{2}(u_{\mathbf{q}}-v_{\mathbf{q}})

(S38)

Therefore, the vertex is given by

\displaystyle V^{(3)}({\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{p}},{\mathbf{q}})\approx-C\left[\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}}(u_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}}-v_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}})^{2}+\varepsilon_{\mathbf{Q}}(u^{2}_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}}+v^{2}_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}})\right](u_{\mathbf{q}}-v_{\mathbf{q}})

(S39)

In this approximation, therefore,
|V^{(3)}({\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{p}},{\mathbf{q}})|^{2}
scales as
(u_{\mathbf{q}}-v_{\mathbf{q}})^{2}\approx q^{1/2}
.

Next, we consider the terms which we neglected in the Taylor expansion, and show that we cannot neglect them altogether.
The reason is as follows: the energy conservation constraint sets
\sqrt{q}\sim p
, implying that a linear term in
p
in the Taylor expansion may multiply
u_{\mathbf{q}}\sim q^{-1/4}
or
v_{\mathbf{q}}\sim q^{-1/4}
to give a term
\sim q^{1/4}
in
V^{(3)}({\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{p}},{\mathbf{q}})
.
Keeping leading order terms in
{\mathbf{p}}
in the Taylor expansion (but not in
{\mathbf{q}}
, as these would give subleading corrections) and carrying out the tedious algebra, we find the linear in
p
term to be

\displaystyle{\mathbf{p}}\cdot({\mathbf{v}}_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{Q}}}-{\mathbf{v}}_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}})\bigg[(u_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}}-v_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}})(u_{\mathbf{q}}u_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}}+v_{\mathbf{q}}v_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}})+\frac{(u_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}}-v_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}})[\varepsilon_{0}(1+\sin^{2}\theta)-2\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}}\sin^{2}\theta]}{4(\varepsilon_{0}-\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}})(\varepsilon_{0}-\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}}\sin^{2}\theta)}\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}}(u_{\mathbf{q}}u_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}}+v_{\mathbf{q}}v_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}})

\displaystyle+\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}}(u_{\mathbf{q}}+v_{\mathbf{q}})\frac{[\varepsilon_{0}(1+\sin^{2}\theta)-2\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}}\sin^{2}\theta]}{8(\varepsilon_{0}-\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}})(\varepsilon_{0}-\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}}\sin^{2}\theta)}\bigg]

Since
u_{\mathbf{q}}
and
v_{\mathbf{q}}
scale as
q^{-1/4}
, for
p\sim\sqrt{q}
from the kinematic constraint, both the p-independent and p-linear term in the vertex
V^{(3)}({\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{p}},{\mathbf{q}})
scale as
q^{1/4}
.
Thus, we have derived that
|V^{(3)}({\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{p}},{\mathbf{q}})|^{2}
asymptotically scales as
\sqrt{q}
near the threshold for
p\sim\sqrt{q}
, used in the main text.
We have also shown that the singular part of the term linear in
{\mathbf{p}}
is proportional to
{\mathbf{p}}\cdot({\mathbf{v}}_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}}-{\mathbf{v}}_{{\mathbf{k}}_{0}+{\mathbf{Q}}})
, implying that at this level of approximation the vertex depends only on the component of
{\mathbf{p}}
normal to the decay boundary, as claimed in the main text.

V
Dynamical structure factor

In this section, we provide detailed expressions for the dynamical spin-structure factor in terms of the renormalized magnon Green’s functions
G({\mathbf{q}},\omega)
.
We note that our expressions for twisted-frame spin correlation functions are similar to Ref.
Mourigal et al. 2010
, the main differences being that our ordering direction is along the x-axis and that we neglect Hartree-Fock correction factors:

\displaystyle S^{xx}({\mathbf{k}},\omega)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathbf{q}}(u_{{\mathbf{k}}-{\mathbf{q}}}v_{\mathbf{q}}+u_{\mathbf{q}}v_{{\mathbf{k}}-{\mathbf{q}}})^{2}\text{Im}\left\{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dxG({\mathbf{q}},x)G({\mathbf{k}}-{\mathbf{q}},\omega-x)\right\},

(S41)

\displaystyle S^{yy}({\mathbf{k}},\omega)

\displaystyle=-S(u_{\mathbf{k}}+v_{\mathbf{k}})^{2}\text{Im}G({\mathbf{k}},\omega),

(S42)

\displaystyle S^{zz}({\mathbf{k}},\omega)

\displaystyle=-S(u_{\mathbf{k}}-v_{\mathbf{k}})^{2}\text{Im}G({\mathbf{k}},\omega).

(S43)

The correlation functions above are for twisted-frame spin operators, so we move back to the lab frame using (
S2
):

\displaystyle S^{x_{0}x_{0}}({\mathbf{k}},\omega)

\displaystyle=S^{xx}({\mathbf{k}},\omega)\cos^{2}\theta+S^{zz}({\mathbf{k}}+{\mathbf{Q}},\omega)\sin^{2}\theta,

\displaystyle S^{y_{0}y_{0}}({\mathbf{k}},\omega)

\displaystyle=S^{yy}({\mathbf{k}},\omega),

\displaystyle S^{z_{0}z_{0}}({\mathbf{k}},\omega)

\displaystyle=S^{xx}({\mathbf{k}}+{\mathbf{Q}},\omega)\sin^{2}\theta+S^{zz}({\mathbf{k}},\omega)\cos^{2}\theta.

(S44)

Evaluating the integral in
S^{xx}({\mathbf{k}},\omega)
is generally challenging, but the form of
G({\mathbf{k}},\omega)
is simplified by the fact that we get a frequency-independent self-energy from solving Dyson’s equation off-shell.
Letting
\Sigma({\mathbf{k}},\omega)=\Sigma({\mathbf{k}})=\bar{\omega}_{\mathbf{k}}-i\Gamma_{\mathbf{k}}
,

\displaystyle G({\mathbf{q}},x)=\frac{1}{x-\bar{\omega}_{\mathbf{q}}+i\Gamma_{\mathbf{q}}},\hskip 7.22743ptG({\mathbf{k}}-{\mathbf{q}},\omega-x)=\frac{1}{\omega-x-\bar{\omega}_{{\mathbf{k}}-{\mathbf{q}}}+i\Gamma_{{\mathbf{k}}-{\mathbf{q}}}}.

(S45)

Then defining
\tau=x-\bar{\omega}_{\mathbf{q}}
, the integrand in (
S41
) becomes

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau+i\Gamma_{\mathbf{q}}}\frac{1}{(\omega-\bar{\omega}_{\mathbf{q}}-\bar{\omega}_{{\mathbf{k}}-{\mathbf{q}}}-\tau)+i\Gamma_{{\mathbf{k}}-{\mathbf{q}}}}=\frac{1}{\tau+i\Gamma_{\mathbf{q}}}\frac{1}{(\omega^{\prime}-\tau)+i\Gamma_{{\mathbf{k}}-{\mathbf{q}}}}

(S46)

where
\omega^{\prime}\equiv\omega-\bar{\omega}_{\mathbf{q}}-\bar{\omega}_{{\mathbf{k}}-{\mathbf{q}}}
.
Then,

\displaystyle\text{Im}\left\{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dxG({\mathbf{q}},x)G({\mathbf{k}}-{\mathbf{q}},\omega-x)\right\}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tau\left[\Gamma_{\mathbf{q}}\omega^{\prime}-(\Gamma_{\mathbf{q}}-\Gamma_{{\mathbf{k}}-{\mathbf{q}}})\tau\right]\frac{1}{(\tau^{2}+\Gamma_{\mathbf{q}}^{2})}\frac{1}{((\omega^{\prime}-\tau)^{2}+\Gamma_{{\mathbf{k}}-{\mathbf{q}}}^{2})}

\displaystyle=\frac{-\pi\omega^{\prime}}{\Gamma_{{\mathbf{k}}-{\mathbf{q}}}}\left\{\frac{\Gamma_{\mathbf{q}}+\Gamma_{{\mathbf{k}}-{\mathbf{q}}}}{\omega^{\prime 2}+(\Gamma_{\mathbf{q}}+\Gamma_{{\mathbf{k}}-{\mathbf{q}}})^{2}}-\frac{\Gamma_{\mathbf{q}}-\Gamma_{{\mathbf{k}}-{\mathbf{q}}}}{\omega^{\prime 2}+(\Gamma_{\mathbf{q}}-\Gamma_{{\mathbf{k}}-{\mathbf{q}}})^{2}}\right\}.

(S47)

The integral in
S^{xx}({\mathbf{k}},\omega)
becomes the difference of two integrals: a convolution of two Lorentzians and the same thing with one power of
\tau
in the integrand.
Both of these integrals are analytically evaluable, giving us the final expression in (
S47
).
Using it, we compute
S({\mathbf{k}},\omega)
as defined in the main text using a
600\times 600

{\mathbf{q}}
-grid for 50 values of
{\mathbf{k}}
along the
\Gamma M
line cut, evaluating at 100 values of
\omega
for each
{\mathbf{k}}
.
The result is shown as a log plot in Fig.
1
(c) in the main text.

VI
Three-magnon decays

In the main text, we predict that antiferromagnetic magnons may be stabilized in the Heisenberg limit, but one-loop decay rates shown in Figure
4
only take into account contributions from 1 to 2 magnon decays.
Then, to justify this prediction, we briefly examine the on-shell 3-magnon density of states (DOS)
\rho_{3}({\mathbf{k}})
, defined as

\displaystyle\rho_{3}({\mathbf{k}})\equiv\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{{\mathbf{q}},{\mathbf{p}}}\delta(\omega_{\mathbf{k}}-\omega_{{\mathbf{q}}}-\omega_{{\mathbf{p}}}-\omega_{{\mathbf{k}}-{\mathbf{q}}-{\mathbf{p}}}).

(S48)

This quantity is closely related to contributions from the quartic vertex (
S23
) to the magnon decay rate
\Gamma_{\mathbf{k}}
at zero temperature.
The leading order two-loop contribution is as follows
Zhitomirsky and Chernyshev 2013
:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mathbf{k}}^{(4)}\sim\sum_{{\mathbf{q}},{\mathbf{p}}}|V_{{\mathbf{k}},{\mathbf{q}},{\mathbf{p}}}^{(4)}|^{2}\delta(\omega_{\mathbf{k}}-\omega_{{\mathbf{q}}}-\omega_{{\mathbf{p}}}-\omega_{{\mathbf{k}}-{\mathbf{q}}-{\mathbf{p}}}).

(S49)

Figure S4:
Plots of
\log[\rho_{3}({\mathbf{k}})]
for the dipolar HAFM in a uniform field for multiple values of
H/H_{c}
(increasing from left to right).

Note that the uniform-field XXZ AFM dispersion (
S17
) is gapless at
{\mathbf{Q}}
.
Since the vertex
V_{{\mathbf{k}},{\mathbf{p}},{\mathbf{q}}}^{(4)}
is not expected to be singular at
{\mathbf{k}}={\mathbf{Q}}
, the stability of low-energy magnons requires a small DOS at
{\mathbf{k}}={\mathbf{Q}}
.
Figure
S4
shows an increased DOS near
{\mathbf{k}}=\mathbf{0}
with increased
H/H_{c}
, but it remains suppressed for the low-energy magnons at
{\mathbf{Q}}
.
This, in turn, indicates that the low-energy magnons of the HAFM in a uniform field are stable against 1 to 3 magnon decays.
Taken together with the fact that 1 to 2 magnon decays are kinematically forbidden
H/H_{c}<0.29
(Table.
1
), we conclude that the low-energy magnons of the HAFM in a uniform field are perturbatively stable at small
H
, as claimed in the main text.

On solutions of the Diophantine equation L n + L m = 3 a L_{n}+L_{m}=3^{a}
Pagdame TIEBEKABE & \& Ismaïla DIOUF

Abstract
Let ( L n ) n ≥ 0 (L_{n})_{n\geq 0} be the Lucas sequence given by L 0 = 2 , L 1 = 1 L_{0}=2,L_{1}=1 and L n + 2 = L n + 1 + L n L_{n+2}=L_{n+1}+L_{n} for n ≥ 0 n\geq 0 . In this paper, we are interested in finding all powers of three which are sums of two Lucas numbers, i.e., we study the exponential Diophantine equation L n + L m = 3 a L_{n}+L_{m}=3^{a} in nonnegative integers n , m , n,m, and a a . The proof of our main theorem uses lower bounds for linear forms in logarithms, properties of continued fractions, and a version of the Baker-Davenport reduction method in Diophantine approximation.



Keywords and phrases
: Linear forms in logarithm; Diophantine equations; Fibonacci sequence; Lucas sequence; perfect powers.

Preliminaries

2.1
Generalities

Definition 2.1
.

Let
k\geq 1
. The sequence
\{H_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
is called a recurrent linear sequence of order
k
if the sequence satisfies

H_{n+k}=a_{1}H_{n+k-1}+a_{2}H_{n+k-2}+\cdots+a_{k}H_{n}

for all
n\geq 0
with
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\in\mathbb{C}
, fixed.

We suppose that
a_{k}\neq 0
(otherwise, the sequence
\{H_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
satisfies a recurrence of order less than
k
). If
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
H_{0},\ldots,H_{k-1}\in\mathbb{Z}
, then we can easily prove by induction on
n
that
H_{n}
is an integer for all
n\geq 0
. The polynomial

f(X)=X^{k}-a_{1}X^{k-1}-a_{2}X^{k-2}-\cdots-a_{k}\in\mathbb{C},

is called the characteristic polynomial of
(H_{n})_{n\geq 0}
. We suppose that

f(X)=\prod_{i=1}^{m}(X-\alpha_{i})^{\sigma_{i}},

where
\alpha_{1}
, …,
\alpha_{m}
are distinct roots of
f(X)
with respectively
\sigma_{1}
, …,
\sigma_{m}
their multiplicities.

Definition 2.2
.

We define the sequences
(A_{n})_{n\geq 0}
and
(B_{n})_{n\geq 0}
for all positive integers
\mathbb{N}
by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lllll}A_{n+2}&=&aA_{n+1}+A_{n},&A_{0}=0,&A_{1}=1\\
B_{n+2}&=&aB_{n+1}+B_{n},&B_{0}=2,&B_{1}=a.\\
\end{array}\right.

For
a=1
,
(A_{n})_{n\geq 0}=(F_{n})_{n\geq 0}
and
(B_{n})_{n\geq 0}=(L_{n})_{n\geq 0}
, which are Fibonacci and Lucas sequences respectively, defined above.

Remark 2.1
.

If
k=2
, the sequence
(H_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is called a binary recurrent sequence. In this case, the characteristic polynomial is of the form

f(X)=X^{2}-a_{1}X-a_{2}=(X-\alpha_{1})(X-\alpha_{2}).

Suppose that
\alpha_{1}\neq\alpha_{2}
, then
H_{n}=c_{1}\alpha_{1}^{n}+c_{2}\alpha_{2}^{n}

for all
n\geq 0
.

Definition 2.3
.

The binary recurrent sequence
\{H_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is said to be non degenerated if
c_{1}c_{2}\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\neq 0
and
\alpha_{1}/\alpha_{2}
is not a root of unity.

Binet’s formula for the general term of Fibonacci and Lucas sequences is obtained using standard methods for solving recurrent sequences, which are given by :

F_{n}=\dfrac{\alpha^{n}-\beta^{n}}{\alpha-\beta}\quad\text{and}\quad L_{n}=\alpha^{n}+\beta^{n}

where
\left(\alpha,\beta\right)=\left(\dfrac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2},\dfrac{1-\sqrt{5}}{2}\right)
are the zeros of the characteristic polynomial
X^{2}-X-1
.

Definition 2.4
.

For all algebraic numbers
\gamma
, we define its measure by the following identity :

{\rm M}(\gamma)=|a_{d}|\prod\limits_{i=1}^{d}\max\{1,|\gamma_{i}|\},

where
\gamma_{i}
are the roots of
f(x)=a_{d}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{d}(x-\gamma_{i})
is the minimal polynomial of
\gamma
.

Let us define now another height, deduced from the last one, called the absolute logarithmic height. It is the most used one.

Definition 2.5
.

( Absolute logarithmic height)

For a non-zero algebraic number of degree
d
on
\mathbb{Q}
where the minimal polynomial on
\mathbb{Z}
is
f(x)=a_{d}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{d}(x-\gamma_{i})
, we denote by

h(\gamma)=\dfrac{1}{d}\left(\log|a_{d}|+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}\log\max\{1,|\gamma_{i}|\}\right)=\dfrac{1}{d}\log{\rm M}(\gamma).

the usual logarithmic absolute height of
\gamma
.

The following properties of the logarithmic height, will also be used in the next section:

•

h(\gamma\pm\eta)\leq h(\gamma)+h(\eta)+\log 2
.

•

h(\gamma\eta^{\pm 1})\leq h(\gamma)+h(\eta)
.

•

h(\gamma^{s})=|s|h(\gamma)
.

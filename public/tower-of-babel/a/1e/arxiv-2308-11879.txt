Theoretical interpretation of the Ξ ⁡ ( 1620 ) \Xi(1620) and Ξ ⁡ ( 1690 ) \Xi(1690) resonances seen in Ξ c + → Ξ − ​ π + ​ π + \Xi_{c}^{+}\to\Xi^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{+} decay
Hai-Peng Li, Gong-Jie Zhang, Wei-Hong Liang, E. Oset

Abstract
We study the Belle reaction Ξ c + → Ξ − ​ π + ​ π + \Xi_{c}^{+}\to\Xi^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{+} looking at the mass distribution of π + ​ Ξ \pi^{+}\Xi , where clear signals for the Ξ ⁡ ( 1620 ) \Xi(1620) and Ξ ⁡ ( 1690 ) \Xi(1690) resonances are seen. These two resonances are generated dynamically from the interaction in coupled channels of π ​ Ξ , K ¯ ​ Λ , K ¯ ​ Σ \pi\Xi,\bar{K}\Lambda,\bar{K}\Sigma and η ​ Ξ \eta\Xi within the chiral unitary approach. Yet, the weak decay process at the quark level, together with the hadronization to produce pairs of mesons, does not produce the π ​ π ​ Ξ \pi\pi\Xi final state. In order to produce this state one must make transitions from the K ¯ ​ Λ , K ¯ ​ Σ \bar{K}\Lambda,\bar{K}\Sigma and η ​ Ξ \eta\Xi components to π ​ Ξ \pi\Xi , and this interaction is what produces the resonances. So, the reaction offers a good test for the molecular picture of these resonances. Adding the contribution of the Ξ ∗ ​ ( 1530 ) \Xi^{*}(1530) and some background we are able to get a good reproduction of the mass distribution showing the signatures of the two resonances as found in the experiment.

II
Generation of the
\Xi(1620)
and
\Xi(1690)
states

Using the chiral unitary approach, we follow closely the work of Ref.
Bennhold
and consider the
\pi\Xi
scattering
with the
\pi\Xi,\bar{K}\Lambda,\bar{K}\Sigma,\eta\Xi
coupled channels.
The
s
-wave scattering amplitudes in matrix form are given by means of the Bethe-Salpeter (BS) equation

T=[1-VG]^{-1}\,V,

(1)

where

Here we differ a bit from Ref.

\Xi_{c}^{+}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{+}\Xi^{-}

Here we follow closely the formalism of Ref.
Miyahara
.
At the quark level the reaction to produce the
\Xi(1620)
and
\Xi(1690)
proceeds as depicted in Fig.
1
,
followed by the hadronization of the final quarks,
where a
\bar{q}q

Figure 1:

The original
s,u

\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(su-us)

The weak process is doubly Cabibbo favored and the hadronization must necessarily involve the created
s
quark after the weak vertex
because in order to have the
\Xi(1620)

Yet, when the pseudoscalar-baryon coupled channels of section

which forces this

Bookkeeping the meson and baryon states coming from the hadronization,
it is found in Ref.
Miyahara

H=K^{-}\,\Sigma^{+}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,\bar{K}^{0}\,\Sigma^{0}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\,\bar{K}^{0}\,\Lambda-\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\,\eta\,\Xi^{0},

(3)

where the
\eta^{\prime}

\Xi(1620)

\Xi(1690)

3

but it is precisely this feature what stresses the production of a dynamically generated resonance,
because the
\pi\Xi

3

\pi\Xi
in the final state,
and it is precisely this interaction the one that produces the resonances.

2

V
Conclusions

In this work, we have studied
\Xi^{-}\pi^{+}_{L}
invariant mass distribution for
\Xi^{+}_{c}\to\pi_{H}^{+}\pi^{+}_{L}\Xi^{-}
decay,
considering the
\Xi(1620)
and
\Xi(1690)
as meson-baryon molecular states,
under the framework of the chiral unitary approach.
We can dynamically generate double resonance states simultaneously,
\Xi(1620)
and
\Xi(1690)
,
from the
s
-wave interaction of
\pi\Xi
and other coupled channels,
when using the cutoff method to regularize the
G
functions.
We provide the coupling constants of
\Xi(1620)
and
\Xi(1690)
to different channels in Table
2
.
From the coupling constants, it can be known that
\Xi(1620)
is a molecular state with main components
\pi\Xi
and
\bar{K}\Lambda
,
and
\Xi(1690)
is a molecular state with main component
\bar{K}\Sigma
.
The mechanism of
\Xi^{+}_{c}\to\pi^{+}MB
decay was analyzed in Ref.
Miyahara
.
We follow closely this work and find that the meson and baryon states from hadronization after the weak process do not contain the final state
\pi^{+}\Xi^{-}
, so it must come from rescattering.
But what might be a problem to describe the reaction becomes actually a strong point to look into the nature of the
\Xi(1620)
and
\Xi(1690)
resonances,
which are produced by this final state interaction in the chiral unitary approach.

In order to compare with the experimental data from Belle,
we take into account contributions from
\Xi^{*}(1530)
and other backgrounds,
and get the
\Xi^{-}\pi^{+}_{L}
invariant mass distribution for
\Xi_{c}^{+}\to\pi^{+}_{H}\pi^{+}_{L}\Xi^{-}
decay.
Results in comparison with experimental data strongly support that the
\Xi(1620)
and
\Xi(1690)
are molecular states,
with spin-parity being both
\frac{1}{2}^{-}
.
Other reactions requiring the need of final state interaction to produce these resonances would be most welcome to further support this picture.

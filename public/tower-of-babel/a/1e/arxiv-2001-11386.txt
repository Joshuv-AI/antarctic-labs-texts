Toric one-skeletons for complexity-one spaces
Isabelle Charton

Abstract
A complexity-one space is a compact symplectic manifold ( M , ω ) (M,\omega) endowed with an effective Hamiltonian action of a torus T T of dimension 1 2 ​ dim ( M ) − 1 \frac{1}{2}\dim(M)-1 . In this note we prove that for a certain class of complexity-one spaces the Poincaré dual of the Chern class c n − 1 c_{n-1} can be represented by a collection of n 2 ​ χ ​ ( M ) \frac{n}{2}\chi(M) symplectic embedded 2 2 -spheres, where χ ⁡ ( M ) \chi(M) is the Euler characteristic of M M and dim ( M ) = 2 ​ n \dim(M)=2n . We call such a collection a toric one-skeleton. The classification of complexity-one spaces is an important subject in symplectic geometry. A nice subcategory of those spaces are the ones which are monotone. The existence of a toric one-skeleton is a useful tool to understand six-dimensional monotone complexity-one spaces. In particular, we will show that the existence of a toric one-skeleton for such a space implies that the second Betti number of M M is at most seven. This is a simple application of results by Sabatini-Sepe and Lindsay-Panov.

Theorem 2.2

(local normal forms near fixed points)

One of most the important theorems for Hamiltonian

.

Proof of Theorem

and Corollary

In this section we prove the Theorem
1.4
. Moreover, in Lemma

we prove that the existence of a toric one-skeleton for a monotone complexity-one space of dimension six implies that the second Betti number is bounded by
7
. Finally, we see that Corollary
1.6
is a simple consequence of Theorem
1.4
and Lemma
6.1
.
So let us prove Theorem
1.4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Let
(M,\omega,T,\phi)
be a complexity-one space of dimension
2n
that satisfies the Extension-Property
(\mathcal{P}_{E})
.
Hence, the connected components of
M^{T}
are isolated points or
2
-spheres.

\bullet
For each fat edge
e\in E^{fat}_{\phi(M)}
, we pick one reduced toric one-skeleton
\mathcal{\bar{S}}_{F_{e}}
of
F_{e}=\phi^{-1}(e)
.

\bullet
Let
\mathcal{S}_{pre}
be the pre-toric one-skeleton of
(M,\omega,T,\phi)
.

\bullet
Let
M_{2}^{T}
be the set of fixed
2
-spheres of
M^{T}
.

Now we want to show that

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{S}_{pre}\bigcup M_{2}^{T}\bigcup\left(\bigcup_{e\in E^{fat}_{\phi(M)}}\bar{\mathcal{S}}_{F_{e}}\right)

(37)

is a toric one-skeleton of
(M,\omega,T,\phi)
.
Therefore, let
\bar{\xi}\in Lie(T)
generic,
S^{1}\cong\exp(\mathbb{R}\bar{\xi})
and let
\Phi\colon\ell_{T}^{*}\rightarrow\ell_{S^{1}}^{*}
.

Then Proposition
4.14
and
5.4
imply

\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}}\int_{S}\mu^{S^{1}}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\Sigma\in M_{2}^{T}}\left(\int_{\Sigma}\mu^{S^{2}}+2\dfrac{\pi_{\Sigma}(\mu^{S^{1}}|_{\Sigma})}{x}\left(\frac{1}{\Phi(\alpha_{\Sigma,1})}+\dots+\frac{1}{\Phi(\alpha_{\Sigma,n-1})}\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{p\in M^{T}_{0}}\dfrac{\mu^{S^{1}}(p)}{x}\left(\frac{1}{\Phi(\alpha_{p,1})}+\dots+\frac{1}{\Phi(\alpha_{p,n})}\right)

for all
\mu^{S^{1}}\in H_{S^{1}}^{*}(M;\mathbb{Z})
. By Corollary
3.4
, this is equal to
\int_{M}\mu^{S^{1}}c_{n-1}^{S^{1}}
.

Moreover, since the fixed point set
M^{T}
is torsion-free the restriction map

H_{S^{1}}^{*}(M;\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow H^{*}(M;\mathbb{Z})
is surjective (
[
11
, Kirwan]
). Let
\mu\in H^{2}(M;\mathbb{Z})
, then
\mu
admits an
S^{1}
-equivariant extension
\mu^{S^{1}}\in H_{S^{1}}^{2}(M;\mathbb{Z})
. We obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}}\int_{S}\mu=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\int_{S}\mu^{S^{1}}=\int_{M}\mu^{S^{1}}c_{n-1}^{S^{1}}=\int_{M}\mu c_{n-1}.

(38)

Hence, the union of the
2
-spheres of
\mathcal{S}
in
H_{2}(M;\mathbb{Z})
is the Poincaré dual to the Chern class
c_{n-1}
.

Moreover, by Lemma
4.13
and
5.5
we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{S}|=\dfrac{1}{2}n\cdot\left(|M_{0}^{T}|+2|M_{2}^{T}|\right).

(39)

So it is left to show that the Euler Characteristic
\chi(M)
is equal to
|M_{0}^{T}|+2|M_{2}^{T}|
.
Therefore let
\phi^{\bar{\xi}}\colon M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be the
\bar{\xi}
-component of the moment map. Since
\bar{\xi}
is generic, we have that
\phi^{\bar{\xi}}
is a perfect Morse-Bott function whose critical submanifolds are precisely the connected components of

(Kirwan
[
11
]
). Hence,

\displaystyle H^{*}(M;\mathbb{R})=\bigoplus_{F\subset M^{T}}H^{*-2d_{F}}(F;\mathbb{R}),

where

is the number of negative weights of the normal bundle of

. Since all connected components of

are isolated points or

\displaystyle\chi(M)=|M_{0}^{T}|+2|M_{2}^{T}|.

∎

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
(M,\omega,T,\phi)
be a complexity-one space of dimension six, which is monotone and admits a toric one-skeleton
\mathcal{S}
. Then the second Betti number of
M
is at most
7
.

Proof.

By a result of Sabatini-Sepe
[
15
]
, the odd Betti numbers of
M
are equal to zero. So let
b_{0},\dots,b_{6}
be the Betti numbers of
M
. Since
M
is connected we have
b_{0}=1
. Moreover, by the Poincaré duality we have
b_{0}=b_{6}
and
b_{2}=b_{4}
. Hence, the Euler Characteristic of
M
is

\displaystyle\chi(M)=b_{0}-b_{1}+b_{2}-b_{3}+b_{4}-b_{5}+b_{6}=2(1+b_{2}).

(40)

Since each element
S\in\mathcal{S}
is a smoothly embedded, symplectic surface of
(M,\omega)
we have

\displaystyle\int_{S}\omega>0\quad\text{for all }S\in\mathcal{S}.

(41)

Now let
c_{1}\in H^{2}(M;\mathbb{Z})
be the first Chern class of
(M,\omega)
. Since
(M,\omega)
is monotone, the integer

\int_{S}c_{1}
is positive for all
S\in\mathcal{S}
. This implies

\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}}\int_{S}c_{1}\geq|\mathcal{S}|=\frac{3\cdot\chi(M)}{2}=3(1+b_{2}).

Since
\mathcal{S}
is the Poincaré dual of the second Chern class
c_{2}\in H^{4}(M;\mathbb{Z})
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}c_{1}c_{2}=\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}}c_{1}\geq 3(1+b_{2})

(42)

Moreover, by the work of Lindsay-Panov
[
12
]
we have that
\int_{M}c_{1}c_{2}=24
.
Hence,

\displaystyle 24=\int_{M}c_{1}c_{2}\geq 3(1+b_{2}),

(43)

and this implies
b_{2}\leq 7
.

∎

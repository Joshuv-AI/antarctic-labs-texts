Homogenization of quadratic convolution energies in periodically perforated domains
Andrea Braides, Andrey Piatnitski

Abstract




Abstract.

We prove a homogenization theorem for quadratic convolution energies defined in perforated domains.
The corresponding limit is a Dirichlet-type quadratic energy, whose integrand is defined by a non-local cell-problem formula.
The proof relies on an extension theorem from perforated domains belonging to a wide class containing compact periodic perforations.

Keywords.
Homogenization, convolution functionals, perforated domains, extension theorem, non-local energies

AMS Classifications.
49J45, 49J55, 74Q05, 35B27, 35B40, 45E10

Introduction

In this paper we perform a limit analysis for a class of convolution functionals that may be interpreted as describing macroscopic features of biological systems. Indeed, the study of macroscopic properties for complex biological systems and models of population dynamics can be reduced to studying the evolution of the so-called one-point correlation function describing the population density in the system. An important feature of the corresponding equation is that it is nonlocal with respect to spatial variables, and that the nonlocal operator is of convolution type, see
[
13
,
10
]
for further details.
On a fixed domain
\Omega
the energy of such a system in the stationary regime is given by

\displaystyle{1\over\varepsilon^{d+2}}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}a\Bigl({y-x\over\varepsilon}\Bigr)(u(y)-u(x))^{2}dy\,dx,

(1)

where
a
is a convolution kernel with good integrability properties.

We note that if
u\in C^{1}(\Omega)
then

u(y)-u(x)\approx\langle\nabla u(x),y-x\rangle
and, using the change of variables
y=x+\varepsilon\xi
,

\displaystyle{1\over\varepsilon^{d+2}}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}a\Bigl({y-x\over\varepsilon}\Bigr)(\langle\nabla u(x),y-x\rangle)^{2}dy\,dx\longrightarrow\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a(\xi)(\langle\nabla u(x),\xi\rangle)^{2}d\xi\,dx,

(2)

as
\varepsilon\to 0
, so that the quadratic functional

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\langle A\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle\,dx,\qquad\hbox{ with }\qquad\langle Az,z\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a(\xi)(\langle z,\xi\rangle)^{2}d\xi,

(3)

gives an approximation of (
1
), or, conversely, (
1
) gives a more general form of quadratic energies allowing for interactions between points at scale
\varepsilon
. In terms of
\Gamma
-convergence this computation can be reworked as a
\Gamma
-limit and the corresponding convergence of minumum problems. The convergence of the corresponding operators, also with periodic perturbations of the convolution kernel, has been studied in
[
15
]
.

In the case of inhomogeneous media with a periodic microstructure and with zones where we do not have interactions, the model may be set in a perforated domain, where the energies are integrated only on the complement of a ‘perforation’. In the corresponding equations we will obtain a homogeneous (non-local) Neumann boundary condition on the perforation.
A general periodically perforated domain is obtained by intersecting
\Omega
with a periodic connected Lipschitz set
E_{\delta}=\delta E
with small period
\delta
. In the case of energies of convolution type the relevant scale of the period
\delta
is of order
\varepsilon
. Indeed, otherwise, if
\varepsilon<\!<\delta
then we may apply a separation of scales argument and reduce to the classical problem of the asymptotic description of perforated domain for the quadratic form (
3
), while if
\delta<\!<\varepsilon
the effect of the perforation is averaged out giving as a limit the energy in (
3
) multiplied by the constant
|E\cap(0,1)^{d}|
(i.e., the constant density of the weak limit of
\chi_{\delta E}
). We will then directly consider energies of the form

\displaystyle F_{\varepsilon}(u)={1\over\varepsilon^{d+2}}\int_{(\Omega\cap E_{\varepsilon})\times(\Omega\cap E_{\varepsilon})}a\Bigl({y-x\over\varepsilon}\Bigr)(u(y)-u(x))^{2}dy\,dx

(4)

(i.e., with
\delta=\varepsilon
). In order to avoid technicalities, we will restrict to the simplified case of ‘compact perforations’; i.e., when

\displaystyle E=\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus(K_{0}+\mathbb{Z}^{d}),

(5)

where
K_{0}
is a Lipschitz compact set such that
(K_{0}+j)\cap(K_{0}+j^{\prime})=\emptyset
if
j,j^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
j\neq j^{\prime}
.

In the asymptotic analysis of usual (quadratic) integral functionals on perforated domains one considers energies

\displaystyle E_{\varepsilon}(u)=\int_{\Omega\cap E_{\varepsilon}}\langle A\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle\,dx

(6)

or the corresponding elliptic differential operators with Neumann conditions on the perforation boundary.
Early homogenization results for such operators were obtained in
[
14
,
12
,
8
,
16
]
, from which many other works on the topic followed.
The key argument in the asymptotic analysis both of energies and operators is an extension lemma, which allows to extend functions in
H^{1}(\Omega\cap E_{\varepsilon})
to functions in
H^{1}(\Omega^{\prime})
for
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega
controlling the
H^{1}
and
L^{2}
norms of the extension with those of the original function in the perforated domain independently of
\varepsilon
(see
[
1
,
5
]
). In this way we may define an
L^{2}_{\rm loc}
-limit of sequences of functions with bounded energy as the limit of their extensions. Our first result is a similar Extension Theorem, which can be stated as follows: for a fixed
r_{0}>0
there exist
r>0
and a constant
C
such that for every fixed
\Omega^{\prime}
and for each function
u
with bounded energy there exists an extension
v
of function
u
that satisfies for all sufficiently small
\varepsilon
the inequality

\int_{(\Omega^{\prime}\times\Omega^{\prime})\cap\{|x-y|\leq\varepsilon r\}}(v(y)-v(x))^{2}dy\,dx\leq C\int_{(\Omega\cap E_{\varepsilon})\times(\Omega\cap E_{\varepsilon})\cap\{|y-x|<\varepsilon r_{0}\}}(u(y)-u(x))^{2}dy\,dx\,.

(7)

The construction of the extended function
v
can be achieved by a ‘reflection’ argument close to
\partial E_{\varepsilon}
. Note however that the estimate of the energy is a little trickier than in the usual ‘local’ case, since for the extended function we will have interactions between the function inside and outside the perforation.

Under the assumption that

\displaystyle\hbox{there exist a constant $c>0$ and $r_{0}>0$ such that
}a(z)\geq c\hbox{ if }|z|\leq r_{0}

(8)

the term on the right-hand side of (
7
) can be estimated in terms of
F_{\varepsilon}(u)
.
Note that if hypothesis (
8
) fails, the set of points
(x,y)\in E\times E
with
a(y-x)\neq 0
may be composed of disconnected components and as a result no controlled extension be possible.

Since, unlike for the usual Dirichlet integrals, inequality (
7
) does not imply the boundedness of the extensions in
H^{1}
, in order to conclude the argument, the extension theorem must be coupled with a Compactness Result for non-perforated energies (see
[
7
]
), now applied to the extended functions, which allows to conclude that from each sequence with equi-bounded energy we can extract a subsequence such that the corresponding extensions converge in
L^{2}_{\rm loc}
to some
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
.

Once this result is obtained, we can compute the
\Gamma
-limit of
F_{\varepsilon}
with respect to the convergence described above.
The limit is a ‘classical’ local quadratic energy

F_{\rm hom}(u)=\int_{\Omega}\langle A_{\rm hom}\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle\,dx,

(9)

with domain
H^{1}(\Omega)
, where
A_{\rm hom}
is a symmetrix matrix
given by the cell-problem formula

\langle A_{\rm hom}z,z\rangle=\inf\Bigl\{\int_{(0,1)^{d}\cap E}\int_{E}a(\xi)(w(y+\xi)-w(y))^{2}d\xi\,dy:w(y)-\langle z,y\rangle\hbox{ is $1$-periodic}\Bigr\}.

(10)

It must be noted that in formula (
10
) the inner integral is performed on the whole
E
, highlighting that long-range interactions cannot be neglected. The treatment of these long-range interactions is the source of most of the technical points in the proof of the homogenization results. In order to control them we have to make some assumption on the decay of
a
, which we state as

\displaystyle 0\leq a(\xi)\leq C{1\over(1+|\xi|)^{d+2+\kappa}}.

(11)

for some
\kappa>0
. Note that this is slightly more restrictive that assuming the finiteness of second moments of
a
, which is a necessary condition by (
3
).

This homogenization theorem is achieved by first using the blow-up method of Fonseca and Müller
[
11
,
6
]
, and then reducing to one-periodic interactions by a convexity argument. The use of formula (
10
) allows the construction of recovery sequence for which we can control long-range interactions. It is worth remarking that the use of the blow-up method is possible thanks to the ‘vanishing non-locality’ of the energies. We note the analogy with the results on ‘perforated’ discrete domains (i.e., defined on scaled periodic lattices with some missing sites) studied in
[
4
]
Section 3.

2
Setting of the problem

We consider a convolution kernel
a:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a(\xi)\,d\xi>0
and such that (
11
) holds
for some
\kappa>0
.

We suppose that

\bullet

K_{0}
is a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with Lipschitz boundary
such that
(K_{0}+j)\cap(K_{0}+j^{\prime})=\emptyset
if
j,j^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
j\neq j^{\prime}
.

We then define the
perforated domain

E=\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus(K_{0}+\mathbb{Z}^{d}).

(12)

Let
\Omega
be an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For all
\varepsilon>0
and
u\in L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\varepsilon E)
we set

\displaystyle F_{\varepsilon}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over\varepsilon^{d+2}}\int_{\Omega\cap\varepsilon E}\int_{\Omega\cap\varepsilon E}a\Bigl({y-x\over\varepsilon}\Bigr)(u(y)-u(x))^{2}dy\,dx.

(13)

Equivalently, we will write

\displaystyle F_{\varepsilon}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over\varepsilon^{d}}\int_{\Omega\cap\varepsilon E}\int_{\Omega\cap\varepsilon E}a\Bigl({y-x\over\varepsilon}\Bigr)\Bigl({u(y)-u(x)\over\varepsilon}\Bigr)^{2}dy\,dx

(14)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over\varepsilon^{d+2}}\int_{\Omega\cap\varepsilon E}\int_{\Omega\cap\varepsilon E-x}a\Bigl({\xi\over\varepsilon}\Bigr)(u(x+\xi)-u(x))^{2}d\xi\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\cap\varepsilon E}\int_{{1\over\varepsilon}(\Omega\cap\varepsilon E-x)}a(\xi)\Bigl({u(x+\varepsilon\xi)-u(x)\over\varepsilon}\Bigr)^{2}d\xi\,dx,

this last version being useful when integrability properties of
a
are used.

In the following we will prove extension, compactness and convergence properties for the functionals
F_{\varepsilon}
under
hypothesis (
8
).
Note that if such a hypothesis is removed the functionals
F_{\varepsilon}
may be ‘degenerate’, as shown in Example
4.3
.

2.2
Preliminaries for ‘solid domains’

In this section we consider ‘solid domains’; i.e., the case when the perforation is not present.
In the following we will extend functions from
\Omega\cap\varepsilon E
to the whole
\Omega
maintaining
the boundedeness of some convolution energy. In this way we will be able to use arguments for functions defined on solid domains.

2.2.1
A Compactness Theorem

Let
\Omega
be an open set with Lipschitz boundary. The following result, proved in
[
7
]
show that families of functions that have bounded energies of the type (
1
) is compact in
L^{2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
.

Theorem 2.1

(compactness theorem)
.

Let
\Omega
be an open set with Lipschitz boundary, and assume that for a family
\{w_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
,
w_{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
the estimate

F_{\varepsilon}^{k\varepsilon,r}(w_{\varepsilon}):=\int_{\Omega(k\varepsilon)}\int_{\{|\xi|\leq r\}}\Bigl({w_{\varepsilon}(x+\varepsilon\xi)-w_{\varepsilon}(x)\over\varepsilon}\Bigr)^{2}d\xi\,dx\leq C

(15)

is satisfied with some
k>0
and
r>0
.
Assume moreover that the family
\{w_{\varepsilon}\}
is bounded in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Then for any sequence
{\varepsilon_{j}}
such that
\varepsilon_{j}>0
and
\varepsilon_{j}\to 0
, as
j\to\infty
, and for any open subset

\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega
the set
\{w_{\varepsilon_{j}}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is relatively compact in
L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})
and every its limit point is in
H^{1}(\Omega)
.

2.2.2
Treatment of boundary data

In this section we prove a classical lemma that allows to match boundary data. For future reference we prove it for general integrands
b_{\varepsilon}
which only satisfy an estimate from above. In the following it will be applied to

b_{\varepsilon}(x,y)=b(x,y)=\chi_{E}(x)\chi_{E}(y)a(x-y).

(16)

With this requirement of generality in mind, let
b_{\varepsilon}:\Omega\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
be Borel functions satisfying

\displaystyle 0\leq b_{\varepsilon}(x,y)\leq C{1\over(1+|x-y|)^{d+2+\kappa}}.

(17)

and set

\displaystyle F^{b}_{\varepsilon}(u)={1\over\varepsilon^{d+2}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}b_{\varepsilon}\Bigl({x\over\varepsilon},{y\over\varepsilon}\Bigr)(u(y)-u(x))^{2}\,dy\,dx.

(18)

Proposition 2.2

(treatment of boundary values)
.

Let

be a bounded open set with Lipschitz boundary, let

For every

\displaystyle B_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigl(A\setminus A\Bigl(\delta\Bigl(1+{k-1\over N}\Bigr)\Bigr)\Bigr)\times\Bigl(A\setminus A\Bigl(\delta\Bigl(1+{k-1\over N}\Bigr)\Bigr)\Bigr)

\displaystyle B_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigl(A\Bigl(\delta\Bigl(1+{k-1\over N}\Bigr)\Bigr)\setminus A\Bigl(\delta\Bigl(1+{k\over N}\Bigr)\Bigr)\times A

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigl(A\times\Bigl(\Bigl(A\Bigl(\delta\Bigl(1+{k-1\over N}\Bigr)\Bigr)\setminus A\Bigl(\delta\Bigl(1+{k\over N}\Bigr)\Bigr)\Bigr)\Bigr)\setminus B_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A\Bigl(\delta\Bigl(1+{k\over N}\Bigr)\Bigr)\times\Bigl(A\setminus A\Bigl(\delta\Bigl(1+{k-1\over N}\Bigr)\Bigr)\Bigr)

\displaystyle B^{\prime}_{4}

3
An extension theorem

The following result states that any function
u
defined on the perforated domain
\Omega\cap\varepsilon E
can be extended to a function defined on the whole domain
\Omega
such that, upon restricting to the interior of the domain (more precisely, to points at distance greater than some multiple of
\varepsilon
) a reference convolution energy of the extension is bounded by
F_{\varepsilon}(u)
and the
L^{2}
-norm of the extension is bounded by the
L^{2}
-norm of
u
on the perforated domain. This result mirrors the analog where
F_{\varepsilon}
is the Dirichlet energy on the
perforated domain and the reference convolution energy is replaced with the Dirichlet energy on the solid domain
[
1
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

We say that an open subset
\cal F
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
has a uniformly Lipschitz countinuous boundary if
there exist constants
L>0
and
\rho_{1},\,\rho_{2}>0
such that for any point
x\in\partial\mathcal{F}
there exists a set
\mathbb{S}
which, up to translation by
x
and rotation, is of the form
(-\rho_{1},\rho_{1})^{d-1}\times(-\rho_{2},\rho_{2})
such that
\mathbb{S}\cap\mathcal{F}
is the sub-graph of a
L
-Lipschitz function defined on
(-\rho_{1},\rho_{1})^{d-1}
.

Notice that we can assume without loss of generality that the
L
-Lipschitz function in the last definition takes
on values in the interval
(-\frac{\rho_{2}}{2},\frac{\rho_{2}}{2})
.

Theorem 3.2

(extension theorem)
.

Let
E
be an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with the following properties

1
.
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus E
is a union of bounded open sets in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
;

2
. the diameters of these sets are uniformly bounded;

3
. the distance between any two distinct sets is bounded from below by a positive constant;

4
. the boundary of the set
E
is uniformly Lipschitz continuous in the sense of Definition
3.1
.

Let
\Omega
be an open set with Lipschitz boundary. For each
r_{0}>0
there exist
k>0
and
r>0
such that
for all
u\in L^{2}(\Omega\cap\varepsilon E)
there exists
v\in L^{2}(\Omega)
such that

v=u\ \ \hbox{\rm on }\Omega\cap\varepsilon E,

(19)

\int_{\Omega(k\varepsilon)\times\Omega(k\varepsilon)\cap\{|x-y|\leq\varepsilon r\}}\Bigl({v(y)-v(x)\over\varepsilon}\Bigr)^{2}dy\,dx\leq C\int_{(\Omega\cap\varepsilon E)\times(\Omega\cap\varepsilon E)\cap\{|x-y|\leq\varepsilon r_{0}\}}\Bigl({u(y)-u(x)\over\varepsilon}\Bigr)^{2}dy\,dx

(20)

and

\int_{\Omega(k\varepsilon)}|v|^{2}\,dx\leq C\int_{\Omega\cap\varepsilon E}|u|^{2}\,dx.

(21)

Note that if (
8
) holds then (
20
) implies that

\int_{\Omega(k\varepsilon)}\int_{\{|\xi|\leq r\}}\Bigl({v(x+\varepsilon\xi)-v(x)\over\varepsilon}\Bigr)^{2}d\xi\,dx\leq CF_{\varepsilon}(u)

(22)

Our arguments rely on the following statement.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
A
be a connected bounded Lipschitz set.
For any
r>0
there exists a constant

We now state and prove a homogenization result using

Definition 4.1

We say that a sequence

Theorem 4.2

Let
\Omega
be an open set with Lipschitz boundary, and let

be given by (

Then
F_{\varepsilon}

\Gamma
-converge with respect to the convergence

(30)

From this theorem, thanks to the compactness results of the previous sections and the validity of Poincaré inequalities (see
[

The example below shows that if condition (

Proposition 4.6

Proposition 4.9
.

(upper bound)

\displaystyle\int_{(\Omega_{1}\setminus\Omega_{1}^{\delta})\cap\varepsilon E}\int_{{1\over\varepsilon}((\Omega_{2}\setminus\Omega_{2}^{\delta})\cap\varepsilon E-x)}a(\xi)\Bigl({u^{2,\delta}_{\varepsilon}(x+\varepsilon\xi)-u^{1,\delta}_{\varepsilon}(x)\over\varepsilon}\Bigr)^{2}d\xi\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{(\Omega_{1}\setminus\Omega_{1}^{\delta})\cap\varepsilon E}\int_{{1\over\varepsilon}((\Omega_{2}\setminus\Omega_{2}^{\delta})\cap\varepsilon E-x)}a(\xi)\Bigl({u(x+\varepsilon\xi)-u(x)\over\varepsilon}\Bigr)^{2}d\xi\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{(\Omega_{1}\setminus\Omega_{1}^{\delta})}\int_{{1\over\varepsilon}((\Omega_{2}\setminus\Omega_{2}^{\delta})-x)}a(\xi)|\xi|^{2}d\xi\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\delta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a(\xi)|\xi|^{2}d\xi\,dx=C\delta

Gathering the previous estimates and letting
\varepsilon\to 0
we obtain

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega_{1}\cap\varepsilon E}\int_{{1\over\varepsilon}(\Omega_{2}\cap\varepsilon E-x)}a(\xi)\Bigl({u^{2,\delta}_{\varepsilon}(x+\varepsilon\xi)-u^{1,\delta}_{\varepsilon}(x)\over\varepsilon}\Bigr)^{2}d\xi\,dx\leq C\delta,

which proves (
60
).

A geometric interpretation of Krull dimensions of 𝑻 \boldsymbol{T} -algebras
Song JuAe, Yasuhito Nakajima

Abstract
. We investigate Krull dimensions of semirings and semifields dealt in tropical geometry. For a congruence C C on a tropical Laurent polynomial semiring 𝑻 ⁡ [ X 1 ± , … , X n ± ] \boldsymbol{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}] , a finite subset T T of C C is called a finite congruence tropical basis of C C if the congruence variety 𝑽 ⁡ ( T ) \boldsymbol{V}(T) associated with T T coincides with 𝑽 ⁡ ( C ) \boldsymbol{V}(C) . For C C proper, we prove that the Krull dimension of the quotient semiring 𝑻 ⁡ [ X 1 ± , … , X n ± ] / C \boldsymbol{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]/C coincides with the maximum of the dimension of 𝑽 ⁡ ( C ) \boldsymbol{V}(C) as a polyhedral complex plus one and that of 𝑽 ⁡ ( C 𝑩 ) \boldsymbol{V}(C_{\boldsymbol{B}}) when both C C and C 𝑩 C_{\boldsymbol{B}} have finite congruence tropical bases, respectively. Here C 𝑩 C_{\boldsymbol{B}} is the congruence on 𝑻 ⁡ [ X 1 ± , … , X n ± ] \boldsymbol{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}] generated by { ( f 𝑩 , g 𝑩 ) | ( f , g ) ∈ C } \{(f_{\boldsymbol{B}},g_{\boldsymbol{B}})\,|\,(f,g)\in C\} and f 𝑩 f_{\boldsymbol{B}} is defined as the tropical Laurent polynomial obtained from f f by replacing the coefficients of all non − ∞ -\infty terms of f f with the real number zero. With this fact, we also show that rational function semifields of tropical curves that do not consist of only one point have Krull dimension two.

1.
Introduction

Tropical geometry is an algebraic geometry over the tropical semifield
\boldsymbol{T}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(\boldsymbol{R}\cup\{-\infty\},\operatorname{max},+)
.
By an operation
\operatorname{trop}
called tropicalization, a
d
-dimensional irreducible subvariety of an algebraic torus over a valuation field
K
corresponds to the support of a balanced, pure,
d
-dimensional,
\boldsymbol{R}
-rational polyhedral complex (see
[
18
, Theorem 3.3.5]
for more details).
In
[
4
]
, Giansiracusa–Giansiracusa introduced a concept of scheme-theoretic tropicalization, which sends an ideal
I
in a Laurent polynomial ring
K[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
over
K
to a congruence
E(I)
on the tropical Laurent polynomial semiring
\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
over
\boldsymbol{T}
, and proved that the congruence variety
\boldsymbol{V}(E(I))
associated with
E(I)
equals the image of the algebraic set
\boldsymbol{V}(I)
associated with
I
by
\operatorname{trop}
.
For finitely generated congruences on tropical polynomial semirings, Bertram–Easton (
[
1
]
) and Joó–Mincheva (
[
13
]
) proved a tropical analogue of Nullstellensatz.
As mentioned above, there is much research on tropical polynomial semirings, tropical Laurent polynomial semirings, and congruences on them.

In this paper, we determine the Krull dimension of the quotient semiring by a proper congruence
C
on a tropical Laurent polynomial semiring when
C
satisfies some mild conditions:

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
3.3
)
.

Let
C
be a proper congruence on
\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
.
If both
C
and
C_{\boldsymbol{B}}
have finite congruence tropical bases, respectively, then the equality

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim}\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]/C=\operatorname{max}\{\operatorname{dim}\boldsymbol{V}(C)+1,\operatorname{dim}\boldsymbol{V}(C_{\boldsymbol{B}})\}

holds.

Here a finite congruence tropical basis of
C
is a finite subset of
C
whose congruence variety coincides with
\boldsymbol{V}(C)
and
C_{\boldsymbol{B}}
is the congruence on
\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
generated by
\{(f_{\boldsymbol{B}},g_{\boldsymbol{B}})\,|\,(f,g)\in C\}
, where
f_{\boldsymbol{B}}
is defined as the tropical Laurent polynomial obtained from
f
by replacing the coefficients of all non
-\infty
terms of
f
with the real number zero.
For
C
above, since
\boldsymbol{V}(C)
is a finite union of
\boldsymbol{R}
-rational polyhedral sets, we can define its dimension
\operatorname{dim}\boldsymbol{V}(C)
as the dimension of its polyhedral structure.
On the other hand, in
[
13
]
, Joó–Mincheva defined prime congruences on additively idempotent semirings as an analogue of prime ideals and the Krull dimension
\operatorname{dim}S
of an additively idempotent semiring
S
as the maximum length of strict inclusions of prime congruences on
S
.
Also in the same paper, they proved that both the tropical polynomial semiring and the tropical Laurent polynomial semiring in
n
-variables have Krull dimension
n+1
(and with this fact, proved the Nullstellenstz mentioned above).
For a more general setting, the polynomial semiring and the Laurent polynomial semiring in
n
-variables over an additively idempotent semiring
A
of a finite Krull dimension has Krull dimension
\operatorname{dim}A+n
by
[
14
]
.
Mincheva in
[
20
]
verified that the quotient semiring
\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]/E(I)
has the dimension of
\boldsymbol{V}(I)
plus one as its Krull dimension.
Since not all the supports of
\boldsymbol{R}
-rational polyhedral complexes are given by tropicalization, Theorem
1.1
is a proper generalization of Mincheva’s result.
Theorem
1.1
is a tropical analogue of the classical fact that the dimension of an affine algebraic set is equal to the Krull dimension of its affine coordinate ring over an algebraically closed field (
[
8
, Proposition 1.7 in Chapter I]
).

We also investigate algebraic properties of rational function semifields
\operatorname{Rat}(\varGamma)
of (abstract) tropical curves
\varGamma
and with Theorem
1.1
, give a tropical analogue of the fact that the function field of a Riemann surface has transcendental degree one:

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
3.25
)
.

Let
\varGamma
be a tropical curve which is not one point.
Then the equality

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Rat}(\varGamma)=2

holds.

A tropical curve is the extended distance space obtained from a finite connected graph by identifying each edge with a closed segment of
\boldsymbol{T}
(see Subsection
2.8
for more details).
As an analogue of linear systems on algebraic curves, the concept of linear systems on tropical curves was introduced and studied by several researchers (cf.
[
7
]
).
A rational function (other than
-\infty
) on a tropical curve
\varGamma
is a continuous integral affine function with a finite number of pieces and the set
\operatorname{Rat}(\varGamma)
of all rational functions on
\varGamma
forms a semifield with natural operations and called the rational function semifield of
\varGamma
.
In
[
11
]
, Song proved that
\operatorname{Rat}(\varGamma)
is finitely generated as a semifield over
\boldsymbol{T}
.
Hence there exists a sujective
\boldsymbol{T}
-algebra homomorphism
\psi
from a tropical rational function semifield
\overline{\boldsymbol{T}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})}
to
\operatorname{Rat}(\varGamma)
(
[
10
, Lemma 3.11]
), and thus
\operatorname{Rat}(\varGamma)
is isomorphic to the quotient semifield
\overline{\boldsymbol{T}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})}/\operatorname{Ker}(\psi)
by the kernel congruence
\operatorname{Ker}(\psi)
of
\psi
.
As in the tropical Laurent polynomial semiring case, we can define congruence varieties associated with congruences on tropical rational function semifields.
By
[
10
, Proposition 3.12]
, (the natural compactification of) the congruence variety associated with
\operatorname{Ker}(\psi)
(to be regarded as a tropical curve by lattice length) is isomorphic to
\varGamma
.
More generally, for a finitely generated semifield
S
over
\boldsymbol{T}
, since we do not fix a finite generating set of
S
, we can obtain distinct congruence varieties from
S
.
Song also verified that these congruence varieties correspond to each other with invertible maps defined by finite numbers of tropical rational functions (
[
10
, Corollary 3.20]
).
In addition, by
[
9
, Corollary 3.19]
, Song characterized rational function semifields of tropical curves with surjective
\boldsymbol{T}
-algebra homomorphisms from tropical rational function semifields and geometric properties (i.e., dimensions as supports of polyhedral complexes, connectivity, the existence of distinct parallel rays) of congruence varieties associated with the kernel congruences.

In the current paper, we directly extract these geometric properties without passing through congruence varieties.
Theorem
1.2
is one of them, which corresponds to the dimension property above.
Connectivity is described in terms of pseudodirect product, i.e., in the setting in
[
9
, Corollary 3.19]
, a finitely generated semifield
S
over
\boldsymbol{T}
defines (an isomorphism class of) a connected congruence variety if and only if there exist no two finitely generated semifields
S_{1},S_{2}
over
\boldsymbol{T}
whose pseudodirect product
S_{1}\bowtie S_{2}
is isomorphic to
S
.
It is a tropical analogue of the classical fact that the affine scheme of a (unital commutative) ring
A
is connected if and only if there exist no two nonzero (unital commutative) rings
A_{1},A_{2}
whose direct product
A_{1}\times A_{2}
is isomorphic to
A
(
[
8
, Exercise 2.19 in Chapter II]
).
The existence of distinct parallel rays (of a fixed
\boldsymbol{R}
-rational polyhedral structure) is translated into the terms of numbers of unbounded cells of (
\boldsymbol{R}
-rational polyhedral structures of) congruence varieties and their recession fans, which depend on only the
\boldsymbol{T}
-algebra structure of
S
.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we prepare several concepts that we need such as polyhedral sets, congruences on semirings and Krull dimensions of additively idempotent semirings, tropicalizations, and tropical curves and rational functions on them.
Section
3
is our main section, and we prove all assertions above.

2.1.
Polyhedral sets

A
polyhedral set
in
\boldsymbol{R}^{n}
is the solution set of a system of a finite number of linear inequalities.
The polyhedral set
\{\boldsymbol{x}\in\boldsymbol{R}^{n}\,|\,A\boldsymbol{x}\geq\boldsymbol{0}\}
is called the
recession cone
of the polyhedral set
P\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{\boldsymbol{x}\in\boldsymbol{R}^{n}\,|\,A\boldsymbol{x}\geq\boldsymbol{b}\}
defined by an
l\times n
-matrix
A
and a vector
\boldsymbol{b}\in\boldsymbol{R}^{l}
when
P
is nonempty.
Here
\boldsymbol{0}
stands for the zero vector.
A
polyhedral complex

X
is a complex consisting of a finite number of polyhedral sets.
Each poluhedral set in
X
is a
cell
.
Its
support

|X|
is the union of its polyhedral sets.
The
recession fan
of
X
is the union of all recession cones of polyhedral sets in
X
.
By definition, we can say that it is also the
recession fan

\operatorname{rec}(|X|)
of
|X|
.
A finite union of polyhedral sets in
\boldsymbol{R}^{n}
is the support of a polyhedral complex in
\boldsymbol{R}^{n}
, and vice versa (cf.
[
19
, Proposition 4.1.1(a)]
).

For a nonempty polyhedral set
P
in
\boldsymbol{R}^{n}
, its
dimension

\operatorname{dim}P
is defined by the dimension of the smallest affine subspace of
\boldsymbol{R}^{n}
containing
P
.
If
P
is empty, we define that it has dimension
-\infty
.
The
dimension

\operatorname{dim}X
of a polyhedral complex
X
consisting of polyhedral sets
P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}
is defined as
\operatorname{max}\{\operatorname{dim}P_{i}\,|\,i=1,\ldots,m\}
.
We say that
|X|
has dimension
\operatorname{dim}X
, i.e.,
\operatorname{dim}(|X|)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{dim}X
.
Since
X
consists of only a finite number of polyhedral sets,
\operatorname{dim}(|X|)
is independent of the choice of its
polyhedral structures
, i.e., polyhedral complexes with
|X|
as their supports.
Clearly
\operatorname{dim}X\geq\operatorname{dim}(\operatorname{rec}(|X|))
holds.

Let
G
be a subgroup of the abelian group
(\boldsymbol{R},+)
.
A polyhedral set
P
in
\boldsymbol{R}^{n}
is
G
-rational
if
P
is of the form
\{\boldsymbol{x}\in\boldsymbol{R}^{n}\,|\,A\boldsymbol{x}\geq\boldsymbol{b}\}
with some
l\times n
-matrix
A
with entries in
\boldsymbol{Q}
and some vector
\boldsymbol{b}\in G^{l}
.
A polyhedral complex is
G
-rational
if it consists only of
G
-rational polyhedral sets.

2.2.
Semirings, algebras and semifields

In this paper, a
semiring

S
is a commutative semiring with the absorbing neutral element
0_{S}
for addition
+
and the identity
1_{S}
for multiplication
\cdot
, i.e., a nonempty set
S
with two binary operations
+
and
\cdot
satisfying the following four conditions:

(1)
the pair
(S,+)
is a commutative monoid with an identity
0_{S}
,

(2)
the pair
(S,\cdot)
is a commutative monoid with an identity
1_{S}
,

(3)
the triple
(S,+,\cdot)
has the distributive property, i.e.,
(a+b)\cdot c=a\cdot c+b\cdot c
holds for any
a,b,c\in S
, and

(4)
the zero element
0_{S}
is absorbing, i.e.,
0_{S}\cdot a=0_{S}
holds for any
a\in S
.

If every nonzero element of a semiring
S
is multiplicatively invertible and
0_{S}\not=1_{S}
, then
S
is called a
semifield
.

The set
\boldsymbol{T}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\boldsymbol{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
with two tropical operations:

\displaystyle a\oplus b\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{max}\{a,b\}\quad\text{and}\quad a\odot b\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}a+b,

where
a,b\in\boldsymbol{T}
and
a+b
stands for the usual sum of
a
and
b
, becomes a semifield.
Here, for any
a\in\boldsymbol{T}
, we handle
-\infty
as follows:

\displaystyle a\oplus(-\infty)=(-\infty)\oplus a=a\quad\text{and}\quad a\odot(-\infty)=(-\infty)\odot a=-\infty.

This triple
(\boldsymbol{T},\odot,\oplus)
is called the
tropical semifield
.
The subset
\boldsymbol{B}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{0,-\infty\}
of
\boldsymbol{T}
becomes a semifield with tropical operations of
\boldsymbol{T}
and is called the
boolean semifield
.

The
tropical polynomials
are defined in the usual way and the set of all tropical polynomials in
n
-variables is denoted by
\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
.
It becomes a semiring with two tropical operations and is called the
tropical polynomial semiring
.
Similarly, we define the
tropical Laurent polynomials
and the
tropical Laurent polynomial semiring

\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
.
Throughout this paper, our usage of the symbol
c\odot\boldsymbol{X}^{\odot\boldsymbol{m}}
with
c\in\boldsymbol{T}
and
\boldsymbol{m}=\,{}^{t}\!(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n})\in\boldsymbol{Z}^{n}
means the tropical monomial
c\odot X_{1}^{\odot m_{1}}\odot\cdots\odot X_{n}^{\odot m_{n}}
.

A map
\varphi\colon S_{1}\to S_{2}
between semirings is a
semiring homomorphism
if for any
a,b\in S_{1}
,

\displaystyle\varphi(a+b)=\varphi(a)+\varphi(b),\ \varphi(a\cdot b)=\varphi(a)\cdot\varphi(b),\ \varphi(0)=0,\ \text{and}\ \varphi(1)=1.

When
\varphi
is a bijective semiring homomorphism, it is a
semiring isomorphism
.
Then
S_{1}
is
isomorphic to

S_{2}
as a semiring.
Given a semiring homomorphism
\varphi\colon S_{1}\to S_{2}
, we call the pair
(S_{2},\varphi)
(for short,
S_{2}
) a
S_{1}
-algebra
.
In particular, if both
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
are semifields and
\varphi
is injective, then
S_{2}
is a
semifield over
S_{1}
.
Then
S_{1}
is frequently identified with its image by
\varphi
in
S_{2}
.
For a semiring
S_{1}
, a map
\psi\colon(S_{2},\varphi)\to(S_{2}^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})
between
S_{1}
-algebras is a
S_{1}
-algebra homomorphism
if
\psi
is a semiring homomorphism and
\varphi^{\prime}=\psi\circ\varphi
.
When there is no confusion, we write
\psi\colon S_{2}\to S_{2}^{\prime}
simply.
A bijective
S_{1}
-algebra homomorphism
S_{2}\to S_{2}^{\prime}
is a
S_{1}
-algebra isomorphism
.
Then
S_{2}
and
S_{2}^{\prime}
are said to be
isomorphic
and denoted by
S_{2}\cong S_{2}^{\prime}
.

A semiring
S
is a
\boldsymbol{B}
-algebra if and only if it is
additively idempotent
, i.e.,
a+a=a
holds for any
a\in S
.
Both
\boldsymbol{T}
and
\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
are
\boldsymbol{B}
-algebras.
When
S
is additively idempotent, we can define a partial order as
a\geq b
if and only if
a+b=a
.
If this partial order is a total order, then
S
is said to be
totally ordered
.
A
\boldsymbol{B}
-algebra
S
is said to be
cancellative
if whenever
a\cdot b=a\cdot c
for some
a,b,c\in S
, then either
a=0_{S}
or
b=c
.
If
S
is cancellative, then we can define the semifield
Q(S)
of fractions as within the case of integral domains.
In this case, the map
S\to Q(S);x\mapsto x/1_{S}
becomes an injective
\boldsymbol{B}
-algebra homomorphism.

2.3.
Congruences

A
congruence

E
on a semiring
S
is a subset of
S^{2}=S\times S
satisfying

(1)

(a,a)\in E
holds for any
a\in S
,

(2)
if
(a,b)\in E
, then
(b,a)\in E
,

(3)
if
(a,b)\in E
and
(b,c)\in E
, then
(a,c)\in E
,

(4)
if
(a,b)\in E
and
(c,d)\in E
, then
(a+c,b+d)\in E
, and

(5)
if
(a,b)\in E
and
(c,d)\in E
, then
(a\cdot c,b\cdot d)\in E
.

The diagonal set of
S^{2}
is denoted by
\Delta
and called the
trivial
congruence on
S
.
It is the unique smallest congruence on
S
.
The set
S^{2}
is a congruence on
S
called the
improper
congruence on
S
.
Congruences other than the improper congruence are said to be
proper
.
Quotients by congruences can be considered in the usual sense and the quotient semiring of
S
by the congruence
E
is denoted by
S/E
.
Then the natural surjection
\pi_{E}\colon S\twoheadrightarrow S/E
is a semiring homomorphism.

The intersection of (possibly infinitely many) congruences is again a congruence.
For a subset
T
of
S^{2}
, let
\langle T\rangle_{S}=\langle T\rangle
be the smallest congruence on
S
containing
T
, i.e., the intersection of all congruences on
S
containing
T
.
A congruence
E
on
S
is
finitely generated
if there exist a finite number of element
(a_{1},b_{1}),\ldots,(a_{n},b_{n})\in E
such that
\langle\{(a_{1},b_{1}),\ldots,(a_{n},b_{n})\}\rangle=E
.

For a semiring homomorphism
\psi\colon S_{1}\to S_{2}
, the
kernel congruence

\operatorname{Ker}(\psi)
of
\psi
is the congruence
\{(a,b)\in S_{1}^{2}\,|\,\psi(a)=\psi(b)\}
on
S_{1}
.
For semirings and congruences on them, the fundamental homomorphism theorem holds (
[
4
, Proposition 2.4.4]
).
Then, for the above
\pi_{E}
, we have
\operatorname{Ker}(\pi_{E})=E
.

For two congruences
E,F
on a semiring
S
such that
E\subset F
, the image
F/E
of
F
in
S/E\times S/E
is a congruence on
S/E
and
(S/E)/(F/E)
is isomorphic to
S/E
as a semiring (
[
4
, Proposition 2.4.4(3)]
).
This yields a bijection between congruences on
S/E
and congruences on
S
containing
E
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
S
be a semiring.
For a subset
E
of
S^{2}
, the equality

\displaystyle\langle E\rangle=\bigcup_{F\subset E:\,\#F<\infty}\langle F\rangle

holds.

Proof.

For any finite subset
F
of
E
, clearly
\langle E\rangle\supset\langle F\rangle
holds, and thus
\langle E\rangle\supset\bigcup_{F\subset E:\#F<\infty}\langle F\rangle
holds.

Clearly
E\subset\bigcup_{F\subset E:\#F<\infty}\langle F\rangle
, hence
\langle E\rangle\subset\left\langle\bigcup_{F\subset E:\#F<\infty}\langle F\rangle\right\rangle
.
Since it is easy to check that
\bigcup_{F\subset E:\#F<\infty}\langle F\rangle
is a congruence on
S
, the equality
\left\langle\bigcup_{F\subset E:\#F<\infty}\langle F\rangle\right\rangle=\bigcup_{F\subset E:\#F<\infty}\langle F\rangle
holds.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

Let
C
be a congruence on
\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
.
Then since
f\oplus m=f
and
m\oplus(-\infty)=m
for any term
m
of
f
, by
[
13
, Proposition 2.2(iii)]
, we have
(f,m),(m,-\infty)\in C
if
(f,-\infty)\in C
with
f\not=-\infty
.
As
m
is multiplicatively invertible in
\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
, this implies
(0,-\infty)\in C
, and hence
C
is improper (cf.
[
9
, the proof of Lemma 3.3]
).
Conversely, if
C
is improper, cleary
(f,-\infty)\in C
does not imply
f=-\infty
.
Thus
C
is proper if and only if
(f,-\infty)\in C
implies
f=-\infty
.

2.4.
Prime congruences and Krull dimensions

Let
S
be a semiring.
For two pairs
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}),\beta=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\in S^{2}
, we define

\displaystyle\alpha\rtimes\beta\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}+\alpha_{2}\beta_{2},\alpha_{1}\beta_{2}+\alpha_{2}\beta_{1}).

A congruence
E
on
S
is a
prime congruence
if
E
is proper and
\alpha\rtimes\beta\in E
implies that
\alpha\in E
or
\beta\in E
holds.

For two congruence
E
and
F
on
S
, the congruence on
S
generated by
\{\alpha\rtimes\beta\,|\,\alpha\in E,\beta\in F\}
is denoted by
E\rtimes F
.

For a
\boldsymbol{B}
-algebra
A
, we define its
Krull dimension
as the maximum length of strict inclusions of prime congruences on
A
.
For instance, the Krull dimension of
\boldsymbol{B}
is zero, and that of
\boldsymbol{T}
is one.
Like this, the Krull dimension of a semifield over
\boldsymbol{B}
is not always zero.
By
[
13
, Proposition 2.10]
, prime congruences are characterized as follows:
let
E
be a congruence on
A
.
Then
E
is a prime congruence if and only if the quotient
\boldsymbol{B}
-algebra is cancellative and totally ordered.

Let

be an
l\times(n+1)
-matrix with entries in
\boldsymbol{R}
with an integer
l
such that
1\leq l\leq n+1
.
This matrix

if

(1)
its first column
\boldsymbol{u}_{1}
is the zero vector
\boldsymbol{0}\in\boldsymbol{R}^{l}
or the first nonzero entry of
\boldsymbol{u}_{1}
is positive, and

(2)
for any integer

such that
1\leq i\leq l
, its
i
-th row is
irredundant
on
\boldsymbol{R}\times\boldsymbol{Z}^{n}
, i.e., there exist
c\in\boldsymbol{R}
and
\boldsymbol{n}\in\boldsymbol{Z}^{n}
such that the first nonzero entry of
U\begin{pmatrix}c\\
\boldsymbol{n}\end{pmatrix}
is
i
-th entry.

When

above is t-admissible, then the rank of
U
is
l
and
U
defines

as the congruence on
\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
generated by the set of pairs
\left(c_{1}\odot\boldsymbol{X}^{\odot\boldsymbol{n}_{1}}\oplus c_{2}\odot\boldsymbol{X}^{\odot\boldsymbol{n}_{2}},c_{1}\odot\boldsymbol{X}^{\odot\boldsymbol{n}_{1}}\right)
such that
U\begin{pmatrix}c_{1}-c_{2}\\
\boldsymbol{n}_{1}-\boldsymbol{n}_{2}\end{pmatrix}
is
\boldsymbol{0}
or its first nonzero entry is positive.
Note that

is written as

in
[

]
.
Then
\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]/P(U)
is a totally ordered
\boldsymbol{B}
-algebra by the definition of

and cancellative, and hence

is in fact a prime congruence on
\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
.
For
1\leq i\leq l
, let

denote the
i\times(n+1)
-matrix consisting of the first
i
rows of
U
, and
U(0)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\begin{pmatrix}0&\cdots&0\end{pmatrix}
the
1\times(n+1)
-zero matrix.
Note that
U(l)
is
U
itself.
By definition,
U(i)
is t-admissible for any
1\leq i\leq l
, and

\displaystyle P(U)\subsetneqq P(U(l-1))\subsetneqq\cdots\subsetneqq P(U(1))\subsetneqq P(U(0))

hold, where

is defined by the maximum proper congruence on

, i.e.,
\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]/P(U(0))
is isomorphic to
\boldsymbol{B}
.
Note that

is a prime congruence and admits the definition of

above.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
13
, Theorem 4.13(iii)]
)
.

In the above setting, every congruence of
\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
containing

is of the form

for some
0\leq i\leq l
.
In particular,
\operatorname{dim}\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]/P(U)
coincides with the rank

of

.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
13
, Theorem 4.14(i)]
)
.

For every prime congruence
P
on
\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
, there exists a t-admissible matrix

such that

or

.

Remark 2.5
.

By Theorems
2.3
and
2.4
, for a congruence
E
on
\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
, to calculate the Krull dimension of

of maximum rank such that

.

Remark 2.6
.

Since the quotient semiring
\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]/P
by a prime congruence

has a (possibly not unique) term

such that

.
Note that

Hence the pair of tropical Laurent polynomials

2.5.
Tropical rational function semifields and congruence varieties

If
\overline{E}
denotes the set

\displaystyle\left\{(f,g)\in\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]^{2}\,\middle|\,\forall x\in\boldsymbol{T}^{n},f(x)=g(x)\right\},

then it is a congruence on
\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
and the semiring
\overline{\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]/\overline{E}

\boldsymbol{B}
-algebra by
[
1
, Theorem 1]
and
[
13
, Proposition 5.5 and Theorem 4.14(v)]
.

tropical polynomial function semiring
.
We call its semifield of fractions the

and write it as
\overline{\boldsymbol{T}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})}
.
In what follows, by abuse of notation, the image of each
X_{i}
in

is again written by
X_{i}
.
For a subset

of
\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]^{2}
or
\overline{\boldsymbol{T}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})}^{2}
, we define
\boldsymbol{V}(T)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{x\in\boldsymbol{R}^{n}\,|\,\forall(f,g)\in T,f(x)=g(x)\}

associated with

.
Note here that

For an element

or

.
By defining subsets of

.
For a subset

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{E}_{\pm}(V)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left\{(f,g)\in\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]^{2}\,\middle|\,\forall x\in V,f(x)=g(x)\right\}

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{E}(V)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left\{(f,g)\in\overline{\boldsymbol{T}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})}^{2}\,\middle|\,\forall x\in V,f(x)=g(x)\right\}.

Then these are congruences on
\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
and
\overline{\boldsymbol{T}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})}
, respectively.

The
\boldsymbol{B}
-algebra homomorphism from
\boldsymbol{T}
to
\boldsymbol{B}
sending any real number to

and

to

is naturally extended to the
\boldsymbol{B}
-algebra homomorphism from
\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
(resp.

) to
\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
(resp.
\overline{\boldsymbol{T}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})}

In this paper, the image of

(resp.

) by this

.

Remark 2.7
.

Let
U\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\begin{pmatrix}1&a_{1}&\cdots&a_{n}\end{pmatrix}

2.6.
Tropicalization

Let
K
be a field.
A map
v\colon K\to\boldsymbol{T}
is a
valuation
if

(1)
v(0_{K})=-\infty
and
v(1_{K})=0
hold,

(2)
v(ab)=v(a)+v(b)
holds for any
a,b\in K
, and

(3)
v(a+b)\leq\operatorname{max}\{v(a),v(b)\}
holds for any
a,b\in K
.
Moreover,
v(a)\not=v(b)
implies
v(a+b)=\operatorname{max}\{v(a),v(b)\}
.

The pair
(K,v)
(for short,
K
) is called a
valuation field
.
For a valuation field
K
, we can define the map
\operatorname{trop}
from the Laurent polynomial ring
K[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
in
n
-variables with coefficiencts in
K
to
\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
by the correspondence

\displaystyle\sum_{\boldsymbol{n}\in A}c_{\boldsymbol{n}}\boldsymbol{X}^{\boldsymbol{n}}\mapsto\bigoplus_{\boldsymbol{n}\in A}v(c_{\boldsymbol{n}})\odot\boldsymbol{X}^{\odot\boldsymbol{n}},

where
A
is a nonempty finite subset of
\boldsymbol{Z}^{n}
.
The map
\operatorname{trop}
is called a
tropicalization map
and the image of an ideal
I
of
K[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
by
\operatorname{trop}
is called the
tropicalization
of
I
.

Every tropical Laurent polynomial
F
defines the
tropical hypersurface

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{V}(F)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{x\in\boldsymbol{R}^{n}\,|\,F\text{ has at least two terms which achieve its maximum at }x\}.

If
F
is of degree one, then
\boldsymbol{V}(F)
is called a
tropical hyperplane
.
When
F
is of degree one and homogeneous, it is a
tropical linear form
.

If
I
is an ideal in the Laurent polynomial ring over a valuation field
K
in
n
-variables and defines an algebraic subvariety
X
in the algebraic torus
(K^{\ast})^{n}
, where
K^{\ast}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}K\setminus\{0_{K}\}
, then the intersection

\displaystyle\bigcap_{f\in I}\boldsymbol{V}(\operatorname{trop}(f))\subset\boldsymbol{R}^{n}

is called the
tropicalization
of
X
and denoted by
\operatorname{trop}(X)
.
Then there exist a finite number of Laurent polynomials
f_{1},\ldots,f_{l}
in
I
satisfying
\bigcap_{i=1}^{l}\boldsymbol{V}(\operatorname{trop}(f_{i}))=\operatorname{trop}(X)
(
[
18
, Theorem 2.6.6]
).
When
I
is prime, it is the support of a
v(K^{\ast})
-rational polyhedral complex of dimension
\operatorname{dim}X
(in fact, it has a more rich structure by
[
18
, Theorem 3.3.5]
).

2.7.
Scheme-theoretic tropicalization

For a subset
T
of
\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
, the
bend congruence

\operatorname{Bend}(T)
is defined by the congruence generated by all
(f,f_{\hat{m}})
with any
f\in T
and its any term
m
, where
f_{\hat{m}}
denotes the tropical Laurent polynomial obtained from
f
by removing
m
.
Then the intersection of the tropical hypersurfaces defined by all elements of
T
coincides with the congruence variety
\boldsymbol{V}(\operatorname{Bend}(T))
.
Moreover, in
[
16
, Theorem 1.1]
, it was proved that the tropicalization
\operatorname{trop}(I)
of an ideal
I
in
K[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
determines
\operatorname{Bend}(\operatorname{trop}(I))
and vice versa, where
K
is a valuation field.
This
\operatorname{Bend}(\operatorname{trop}(I))
is called the
scheme-theoretic tropicalization
of
I
.

2.8.
Tropical curves and rational functions

In this paper, a
graph
means an unweighted, undirected, finite, connected nonempty multigraph that may have loops.
For a graph
G
, the set of edges is denoted by
E(G)
.
A vertex
v
of
G
is a
leaf end
if
v
is incident to only one edge and this edge is not loop.
A
leaf edge
is an edge of
G
incident to a leaf end.

A
tropical curve
is the topological space associated with the pair
(G,l)
of a graph
G
and a function
l\colon E(G)\to{\boldsymbol{R}}_{>0}\cup\{\infty\}
, where
l
can take the value
\infty
only on leaf edges, by identifying each edge
e
of
G
with the closed interval
[0,l(e)]
.
The interval
[0,\infty]
is the one-point compactification of the interval
[0,\infty)
.
We regard
[0,\infty]
not just as a topological space but as an extended metric space.
The distance between
\infty
and any other point is infinite.
When
l(e)=\infty
, the leaf end of
e
must be identified with
\infty
.
If
E(G)=\{e\}
and
l(e)=\infty
, then we can identify either leaf ends of
e
with
\infty
.
For a point
x
of a tropical curve
\varGamma
, if
x
is identified with
\infty
, then
x
is called a
point at infinity
.

Let
\varGamma
be a tropical curve.
A map
f\colon\varGamma\to\boldsymbol{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
is a
rational function
on
\varGamma
if
f
is a constant function of
-\infty
or a continuous piecewise affine function with integer slopes, with a finite number of pieces, that can take the values
\pm\infty
at only points at infinity.
Let
\operatorname{Rat}(\varGamma)
denote the set of all rational functions on
\varGamma
.
For rational functions
f,g\in\operatorname{Rat}(\varGamma)
and a point
x
of
\varGamma
that is not a point at infinity, we define

\displaystyle(f\oplus g)(x)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{max}\{f(x),g(x)\}\quad\text{and}\quad(f\odot g)(x)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}f(x)+g(x).

We extend
f\oplus g
and
f\odot g
to points at infinity to be continuous on the whole of
\varGamma
.
Then both are rational functions on
\varGamma
.
Note that for any
f\in\operatorname{Rat}(\varGamma)
, we have

\displaystyle f\oplus(-\infty)=(-\infty)\oplus f=f

and

\displaystyle f\odot(-\infty)=(-\infty)\odot f=-\infty.

Then
\operatorname{Rat}(\varGamma)
becomes a semifield with these two operations.
Also,
\operatorname{Rat}(\varGamma)
becomes a
\boldsymbol{T}
-algebra and a semifield over
\boldsymbol{T}
with the natural inclusion
\boldsymbol{T}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Rat}(\varGamma)
.

A vector
\boldsymbol{b}\in\boldsymbol{R}^{n}
is
primitive
if all its components are integers and their greatest common divisor is one.
When
\boldsymbol{b}
is primitive, for
\lambda\geq 0
, the
lattice length
of
\lambda\boldsymbol{b}
is defined as
\lambda
(cf.
[
12
, Subsection 2.1]
).

3.
Main results

In this section, we prove all assertions in Section
1
.

First, we give an easy proposition:

Proposition 3.1
.

The congruence variety
\boldsymbol{V}(P)
associated with a prime congruence
P
on
\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
or
\overline{\boldsymbol{T}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})}
is empty or consists of only one point.

Proof.

For a point
\boldsymbol{z}=\,{}^{t}\!(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})\in\boldsymbol{R}^{n}
, let

\displaystyle f_{\boldsymbol{z}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\left(z_{i}^{\odot(-1)}\odot X_{i}\oplus\left(z_{i}^{\odot(-1)}\odot X_{i}\right)^{\odot(-1)}\right)\right)^{\odot(-1)}\in\overline{\boldsymbol{T}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})}.

Note that
f_{\boldsymbol{z}}
is a tropical rational function that attains the maximum value zero at and only at
\boldsymbol{z}
.
Assume that
\boldsymbol{V}(P)
has two distinct points
\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in\boldsymbol{R}^{n}
.
Then there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that the set of points where at least one of
\varepsilon\odot f_{\boldsymbol{x}}\oplus 0
and
\varepsilon\odot f_{\boldsymbol{y}}\oplus 0
takes the value zero is
\boldsymbol{R}^{n}
.
Since both
\varepsilon\odot f_{\boldsymbol{x}}\oplus 0
and
\varepsilon\odot f_{\boldsymbol{y}}\oplus 0
are tropical rational functions, there exists
g\in\overline{\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]}\setminus\{-\infty\}
such that
h_{\boldsymbol{x}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(\varepsilon\odot f_{\boldsymbol{x}}\oplus 0)\odot g,h_{\boldsymbol{y}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(\varepsilon\odot f_{\boldsymbol{y}}\oplus 0)\odot g\in\overline{\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]}
.
It is easy to check that
(h_{\boldsymbol{x}},g),(h_{\boldsymbol{y}},g)\not\in P
but
(h_{\boldsymbol{x}},g)\rtimes(h_{\boldsymbol{y}},g)\in\Delta\subset P
when
P
is a prime congruence on
\overline{\boldsymbol{T}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})}
, which is a contradiction.
By choosing proper preimages of
h_{\boldsymbol{x}},h_{\boldsymbol{y}},g
in
\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
, the same argument holds, and thus it is also a contradiction in the case that
P
is a prime congruence on
\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
.
∎

By Proposition
3.1
and by deducing from the classical setting (cf.
[
8
, Proposition 1.7 in Chapter I]
), we wonder that for a congruence
C
on
\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
or
\overline{\boldsymbol{T}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})}
whose congruence variety
\boldsymbol{V}(C)
is a finite union of
\boldsymbol{R}
-rational polyhedral sets, prime congruences on
\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
or
\overline{\boldsymbol{T}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})}
containing
C
is hardly related to the dimension of
\boldsymbol{V}(C)

However, Mincheva proved the following theorem, which implies that the Krull dimension of
\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]/C
may reflect the dimension
\boldsymbol{V}(C)
:

Theorem 3.2

(
[
20
, Theorem 7.2.1]
)
.

Let
K
be a valuation field and
I
an ideal in
K[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
that defines a
d
-dimensional subvariety of the algebraic torus
(K^{\ast})^{n}
.
Then the equality

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim}\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]/\operatorname{Bend}(\operatorname{trop}(I))=d+1

holds.

In this paper, we generalize this theorem as follows:

Theorem 3.3

(Theorem
1.1
)
.

Let
C
be a proper congruence on
\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
and
C_{\boldsymbol{B}}
the congruence
\langle\{(f_{\boldsymbol{B}},g_{\boldsymbol{B}})\,|\,(f,g)\in C\}\rangle
on
\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
.
If both
C
and
C_{\boldsymbol{B}}
have finite congruence tropical bases, respectively, then the equality

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim}\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]/C=\operatorname{max}\{\operatorname{dim}\boldsymbol{V}(C)+1,\operatorname{dim}\boldsymbol{V}(C_{\boldsymbol{B}})\}

holds.

Here the definition of finite congruence tropical bases is given as follows:

Definition 3.4
.

Let
C
be a congruence on
\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
.
A finite subset

\displaystyle\{(f_{1},g_{1}),\ldots,(f_{l},g_{l})\}

of
C
is a
finite congruence tropical basis
of
C
if the equality

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{V}(C)=\bigcap_{i=1}^{l}\boldsymbol{V}((f_{i},g_{i}))

holds.

Lemma 3.5

(cf.
[
9
, the proof of Proposition 3.4]
)
.

For a congruence on
\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
, if it has a finite congruence tropical basis, then it has a finite congruence tropical basis consisting of only one element.

Proof.

Let
C
be a congruence on
\boldsymbol{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
that has a finite congruence tropical basis
\{(f_{1},g_{1}),\ldots,(f_{l},g_{l})\}
.
Since
(f_{i}\oplus g_{i},f_{i})=(f_{i}\oplus g_{i},f_{i}\oplus f_{i}),(f_{i}\oplus g_{i},g_{i})=(f_{i}\oplus g_{i},g_{i}\oplus g_{i})\in C
for any
i
, the pair
((f_{i}\oplus g_{i})^{\odot 2},f_{i}\odot g_{i})=((f_{i}\oplus g_{i})\odot(f_{i}\oplus g_{i}),f_{i}\odot g_{i})
also belongs to
C
.
Hence
\left(\bigodot_{i=1}^{l}(f_{i}\oplus g_{i})^{\odot 2},\bigodot_{i=1}^{l}(f_{i}\odot g_{i})\right)\in C
follows.
Therefore the inclusion

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{V}\left(\left(\bigodot_{i=1}^{l}(f_{i}\oplus g_{i})^{\odot 2},\bigodot_{i=1}^{l}(f_{i}\odot g_{i})\right)\right)\supset\boldsymbol{V}(C)

holds.
Assume
x\in\boldsymbol{V}\left(\left(\bigodot_{i=1}^{l}(f_{i}\oplus g_{i})^{\odot 2},\bigodot_{i=1}^{l}(f_{i}\odot g_{i})\right)\right)
, i.e.,
\bigodot_{i=1}^{l}(f_{i}(x)\oplus g_{i}(x))^{\odot 2}=\bigodot_{i=1}^{l}(f_{i}(x)\odot g_{i}(x))
.
This implies

must hold for each

Hence

In conclusion,

holds.
∎

Peano and Osgood theorems via effective infinitesimals
Karel Hrbacek, Mikhail G. Katz

Abstract
. We provide choiceless proofs using infinitesimals of the global versions of Peano’s existence theorem and Osgood’s theorem on maximal solutions. We characterize all solutions in terms of infinitesimal perturbations. Our proofs are more effective than traditional non-infinitesimal proofs found in the literature. The background logical structure is the internal set theory 𝐒𝐏𝐎𝐓 \mathbf{SPOT} , conservative over 𝐙𝐅 \mathbf{ZF} .

1.
Introduction

Nonstandard analysis (NSA) is sometimes criticized for its implicit
dependence on strong forms of the Axiom of Choice (
\mathbf{AC}
). Indeed,
if
\ast
is the mapping that assigns to each
X\subseteq\mathbb{N}
its
nonstandard extension
{}^{\ast}\!X
, and if
\nu\in{}^{\ast}\mathbb{N}\setminus\mathbb{N}
is an unlimited integer, then the set
U=\{X\subseteq\mathbb{N}\,\mid\,\nu\in{}^{\ast}\!X\}
is a nonprincipal ultrafilter over
\mathbb{N}
.
Of course strong forms of
\mathbf{AC}
, such as Zorn’s Lemma, are a staple of
modern set-theoretic mathematics, but it is undesirable to have to
rely on them for results in ordinary mathematics dealing with Calculus
or differential equations (see Simpson
[
18
]
for a discussion of
the distinction between
set-theoretic
and
ordinary

mathematics). The traditional proofs of most theorems in ordinary
mathematics are
effective
: they do not use
\mathbf{AC}
.
1
1

1

In this paper the word
effective
means
without
the Axiom of Choice
. In reverse mathematics, constructive
mathematics and other areas, it usually has more restrictive meaning.

A few results, such as the equivalence of the
\varepsilon
-
\delta

definition and the sequential definition of continuity for functions

f:X\subseteq\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, require weak forms of
\mathbf{AC}
, notably the Axiom of
Countable Choice (
\mathbf{ACC}
) or the stronger Axiom of Dependent Choice
(
\mathbf{ADC}
). These weak forms are generally accepted in ordinary
mathematics; they do not imply the strong consequences of
\mathbf{AC}
such
as the existence of nonprincipal ultrafilters or the Banach–Tarski
paradox (see Jech
[
12
]
, Howard and Rubin
[
7
]
). We refer to
such proofs as
semi-effective
.

An answer to the above criticism of NSA is offered by recent
developments in the axiomatic/syntactic approach that dates back to
the work of Hrbacek
[
8
]
and Nelson
[
15
]
. A number of
axiomatic systems for NSA have been proposed, of which Nelson’s
\mathbf{IST}

is the best known. We refer to Kanovei and Reeken’s
monograph
[
13
]
for a comprehensive discussion of such axiomatic
frameworks. An accessible introduction to
\mathbf{IST}
is
Robert
[
16
]
.

The theory
\mathbf{IST}
includes the axioms of
\mathbf{ZFC}
, so one could ask
whether the dependence on
\mathbf{AC}
could be avoided by deleting
\mathbf{AC}

from the axioms constituting
\mathbf{IST}
. It turns out that in the
resulting theory one can still prove the existence of nonprincipal
ultrafilters, by an argument similar to the one given above for the
model-theoretic approach (see
[
9
]
and the paragraph following
Lemma
2.5
below).

In
[
10
]
the authors have developed an axiomatic system for NSA
with the acronym
\mathbf{SPOT}
, a subtheory of
\mathbf{IST}
. The theory
\mathbf{SPOT}

is a conservative extension of
\mathbf{ZF}
. This means that every statement
in the
\in
-language provable in
\mathbf{SPOT}
is provable already in

\mathbf{ZF}
. In particular,
\mathbf{AC}
and the existence of nonprincipal
ultrafilters are not provable is
\mathbf{SPOT}
, because they are not
provable in
\mathbf{ZF}
. A stronger theory
\mathbf{SCOT}
which is a conservative
extension of
\mathbf{ZF}+\mathbf{ADC}
is also considered there. Hence proofs in

\mathbf{SPOT}
are effective, and proofs in
\mathbf{SCOT}
are semi-effective.

Some examples of constructions in nonstandard analysis formalized in
these theories are given in
[
10
]
. In particular, it is shown
there how the Riemann integral can be defined in
\mathbf{SPOT}
using
partitions into infinitesimal subintervals, and the countably additive
Lebesgue measure in
\mathbf{SCOT}
using counting measures. The expository
article
[
11
]
presents in
\mathbf{SCOT}
various nonstandard arguments
related to compact sets and continuity.

In Section
2
we state the axioms of
\mathbf{SPOT}
, list some of
their consequences, and prove a stronger version of the Standard Part
principle
\mathbf{SP}
that is crucial in the preliminary
Section
3
.

In Sections
4
-
6
we give nonstandard
proofs in
\mathbf{SPOT}
of the global versions of Peano’s and Osgood’s
theorems concerning the existence of solutions of ordinary
differential equations. While the nonstandard approach using Euler
approximations with an infinitesimal step that we employ is well known
for local solutions (see e.g.
[
1
]
, p. 30), we offer three
innovations:

•

The axiomatic system
\mathbf{SPOT}
enables us to use infinitesimal methods
without the underlying assumption of the existence of nonprincipal
ultrafilters or any other strong form of
\mathbf{AC}
.

•

We construct global, ie, noncontinuable, solutions rather than local
solutions.

•

Traditional proofs of the existence of noncontinuable solutions
typically depend on
\mathbf{ADC}
; see Remark
7.1
. By contrast, our
proof does not assume any form of
\mathbf{AC}
at all.

We first prove (Theorem
4.1
) that every infinitesimal
perturbation
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}
determines a unique global solution
y_{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}

(some or all of these solutions may be the same). We next prove
(Lemma
5.1
) that every solution that is not global is a
restriction of some
y_{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}
. Hence every solution is either global
or can be extended to a global one (Corollary
5.2
) and
every global solution is of the form
y_{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}
for some infinitesimal
perturbation
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}
(Theorem
5.3
). Finally we state the
global Osgood’s theorem (Theorem
6.2
). The proof shows first
that there is a local maximal solution (Lemma
6.5
and the last
part of the sentence that precedes it). The last paragraph of the
proof obtains the global maximal solution as the union of all local
ones.

2.
Theory
SPOT

By an
\in
-language we mean the language that contains a binary
membership predicate
\in
and is enriched by defined symbols for
constants, relations, functions and operations customary in
traditional mathematics. For example, it contains names
\mathbb{N}
and

\mathbb{R}
for the sets of natural and real numbers; they are viewed as
defined in the traditional way (
\mathbb{N}
is the least inductive set,

\mathbb{R}
is defined in terms of Dedekind cuts or Cauchy sequences). The
symbols
<,+
and
\times
denote the ordering, addition and
multiplication of real numbers, and so on without further explanation.
The classical theories
\mathbf{ZF}
and
\mathbf{ZFC}
are formulated in the

\in
-language.

The language of
\mathbf{SPOT}
contains an additional unary predicate

\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}
.
\mathbf{SPOT}
is a subtheory of
IST
and its bounded version

BST
(see
[
13
]
).
We use

\forall
and
\exists
as quantifiers over sets and
\forall^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}

and
\exists^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}
as quantifiers over standard sets. The theory

\mathbf{SPOT}
has the following axioms.

\mathbf{ZF}
(Zermelo - Fraenkel Set Theory)

\mathbf{T}
(Transfer)
Let
\phi
be an
\in
-formula with standard parameters. Then

\forall^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}x\;\phi(x)\rightarrow\forall x\;\phi(x).

\mathbf{O}
(Nontriviality)
\exists\nu\in\mathbb{N}\;\forall^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}n\in\mathbb{N}\;(n\neq\nu)
.

\mathbf{SP}^{\prime}
(Standard Part)

\forall A\subseteq\mathbb{N}\;\exists^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}B\subseteq\mathbb{N}\;\forall^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}n\in\mathbb{N}\;(n\in B\longleftrightarrow n\in A).

The theory
\mathbf{SPOT}
proves the following results (see
[
10
]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Standard natural numbers precede all nonstandard ones:

\forall^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}n\in\mathbb{N}\;\forall m\in\mathbb{N}\;(m<n\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}(m)).

Note that
\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}
is a finite set for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
; it is nonstandard when
n
is nonstandard.

Lemma 2.2

(Countable Idealization)
.

Let
\phi
be an
\in
-formula with arbitrary parameters.

\forall^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}n\in\mathbb{N}\;\exists x\;\forall m\in\mathbb{N}\;(m\leq n\;\rightarrow\phi(m,x))\longleftrightarrow\exists x\;\forall^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}n\in\mathbb{N}\;\phi(n,x).

The dual form of Countable Idealization is

\exists^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}n\in\mathbb{N}\;\forall x\;\exists m\in\mathbb{N}\;(m\leq n\,\wedge\,\phi(m,x))\longleftrightarrow\forall x\;\exists^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}n\in\mathbb{N}\;\phi(n,x).

Countable Idealization easily implies the following more familiar form. We use
\forall^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}\operatorname{\mathbf{fin}}}
and
\exists^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}\operatorname{\mathbf{fin}}}
as quantifiers
over standard finite sets.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
\phi
be an
\in
-formula with arbitrary parameters.
For every standard countable
set
A

\forall^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}\operatorname{\mathbf{fin}}}a\subseteq A\,\exists x\,\forall y\in a\;\phi(x,y)\longleftrightarrow\exists x\,\forall^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}y\in A\;\phi(x,y).

The axiom
\mathbf{SP}^{\prime}
is often stated and used in the
form

(
\mathbf{SP}
)

\forall x\in\mathbb{R}\;(x\text{ limited }\rightarrow\exists^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}r\in\mathbb{R}\;(x\approx r))

where
x
is
limited
iff
|x|\leq n
for some standard
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
x\approx r
iff
|x-r|\leq 1/n
for all standard
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\neq 0
;
x
is
infinitesimal
if
x\approx 0\,\wedge\,x\neq 0
.
The unique standard real number
r
in
\mathbf{SP}
is called the
standard part of

x
or the
shadow of

x
; notation
r=\operatorname{\mathbf{sh}}(x)
.

We have the following equivalence.

Lemma 2.4
.

The statements
\mathbf{SP}^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{SP}
are equivalent (over the theory
\mathbf{ZF}+\mathbf{O}+\mathbf{T}
).

\mathbf{SP}^{\prime}
can also be reformulated as an axiom schema (
Countable
Standardization for
\in
-formulas
):

\displaystyle\text{Let $\phi$ }

\displaystyle\text{be an $\in$-formula with arbitrary
parameters. Then}

\displaystyle\exists^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}S\;\forall^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}n\;(n\in S\longleftrightarrow n\in\mathbb{N}\,\wedge\,\phi(n)).

Lemma 2.5
.

The statement
\mathbf{SP}^{\prime}
and the schema
\mathbf{SP}^{\prime\prime}
are equivalent (over the theory

\mathbf{ZF}+\mathbf{O}+\mathbf{T}
).

Proof.

Apply
\mathbf{SP}^{\prime}
to the set
A=\{n\in\mathbb{N}\,\mid\,\phi(n)\}
(
A
exists because
\phi
is an
\in
-formula).
∎

Standardization in full strength, as postulated in
\mathbf{IST}
,
\mathbf{BST}
, etc., implies the existence of nonprincipal ultrafilters over
\mathbb{N}
: take a nonstandard
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
and let
U
be the standard subset of
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})
such that
\forall^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}X\subseteq\mathbb{N}\;(X\in U\longleftrightarrow\nu\in X)
. Nonetheless, two important special cases of Standardization can be proved in
\mathbf{SPOT}
.

The scope of Countable Standardization can be expanded to a larger class of formulas.

Definition 2.6
.

An
\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}
-
\in
-formula
\Phi(v_{1},\ldots,v_{r})
is
\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}
-
prenex
if it is of the form

\mathsf{Q}^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}u_{1}\ldots\mathsf{Q}^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}u_{s}\,\psi(u_{1},\ldots,u_{s},v_{1},\ldots,v_{r})

where
\psi
is an
\in
-formula and each
\mathsf{Q}
stands for
\exists
or
\forall
.

In other words, all occurrences of
\forall^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}
or
\exists^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}
in
\Phi

appear before all occurrences of
\forall
or
\exists
.

We use
\forall^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}_{\mathbb{N}}\,u\ldots
and
\exists^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}_{\mathbb{N}}\,u\ldots
as quantifiers over standard natural numbers; i.e. as shorthand for respectively

\forall u\,(u\in\mathbb{N}\,\wedge\,\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}(u)\rightarrow\ldots)
and
\exists u\,(u\in\mathbb{N}\,\wedge\,\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}(u)\,\wedge\,\ldots)
.

An
\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}_{\mathbb{N}}
-
prenex
formula is a formula of the form

\mathsf{Q}^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}_{\mathbb{N}}u_{1}\ldots\mathsf{Q}^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}_{\mathbb{N}}u_{s}\,\psi(u_{1},\ldots u_{s},v_{1},\ldots,v_{r})

where
\psi
is an
\in
-formula.

The theory
\mathbf{SPOT}
proves the following stronger version of Countable Standardization that is used repeatedly in this paper.

Proposition 2.7
.

(Countable Standardization for
\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}_{\mathbb{N}}
-prenex formulas)

Let
\Phi
be an
\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}_{\mathbb{N}}
-prenex formula with arbitrary parameters. Then

\exists^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}S\;\forall^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}n\;(n\in S\longleftrightarrow n\in\mathbb{N}\,\wedge\,\Phi(n)).

Of course,
\mathbb{N}
can be replaced by any standard countable set.

Proof.

We give the argument for a typical case

\forall^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}_{\mathbb{N}}u_{1}\,\exists^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}_{\mathbb{N}}u_{2}\,\forall^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}_{\mathbb{N}}u_{3}\;\psi(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},v).

By
\mathbf{SP}^{\prime\prime}
there is a standard set
R
such that for all standard
n_{1},n_{2},n_{3},n

\langle n_{1},n_{2},n_{3},n\rangle\in R\longleftrightarrow\langle n_{1},n_{2},n_{3},n\rangle\in\mathbb{N}^{4}\,\wedge\,\psi(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3},n).

We let
R_{n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}}=\{n\in\mathbb{N}\,\mid\,\langle n_{1},n_{2},n_{3},n\rangle\in R\}
and

S=\bigcap_{n_{1}\in\mathbb{N}}\,\bigcup_{n_{2}\in\mathbb{N}}\,\bigcap_{n_{3}\in\mathbb{N}}\,R_{n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}}.

Then
S
is standard and for all standard
n
:

\displaystyle n\in S

\displaystyle\longleftrightarrow\forall n_{1}\in\mathbb{N}\,\exists n_{2}\in\mathbb{N}\,\forall n_{3}\in\mathbb{N}\;(n\in R_{n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}})

\displaystyle\longleftrightarrow\text{(by Transfer) }\forall^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}_{\mathbb{N}}n_{1}\,\exists^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}_{\mathbb{N}}n_{2}\;\forall^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}_{\mathbb{N}}n_{3}\;(n\in R_{n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}})

\displaystyle\longleftrightarrow\text{ (by definition of $R$) }\forall^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}_{\mathbb{N}}n_{1}\,\exists^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}_{\mathbb{N}}n_{2}\;\forall^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}_{\mathbb{N}}n_{3}\;\psi(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3},n)

\displaystyle\longleftrightarrow\Phi(n).

∎

The second special case of Standardization involves
\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}
-prenex formulas with only the standard parameters.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\Phi(v_{1},\ldots,v_{r})
be an
\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}
-prenex formula
with standard parameters.
Then

\forall^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}S\;\exists^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}P\;\forall^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}\;

\langle v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}\rangle\in P\longleftrightarrow\langle v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}\rangle\in S\;\wedge\;\Phi(v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}).

Proof.

Let
\Phi(v_{1}\ldots,v_{r})
be
\mathsf{Q}_{1}^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}u_{1}\ldots\mathsf{Q}_{s}^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}u_{s}\,\psi(u_{1},\ldots,u_{s},v_{1},\ldots,v_{r})

and let
\phi(v_{1}\ldots,v_{r})
be

\mathsf{Q}_{1}u_{1}\ldots\mathsf{Q}_{s}u_{s}\,\psi(u_{1},\ldots,u_{s},v_{1},\ldots,v_{r})
.
Since
\Phi
has standard parameters,

\Phi(v_{1}\ldots,v_{r})\longleftrightarrow\phi(v_{1}\ldots,v_{r})
holds for all standard
v_{1}\ldots,v_{r}

by the Transfer principle.

The set
P=\{\langle v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}\rangle\in S\,\mid\,\phi(v_{1},\ldots,v_{r})\}
exists by the Separation Principle of
\mathbf{ZF}
, it is standard, and has the required property.
∎

Remark 2.9
.

This result has twofold importance:

(1)

The meaning of every predicate that for standard inputs is defined by an
\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}
-prenex formula
\mathsf{Q}_{1}^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}u_{1}\ldots\mathsf{Q}_{s}^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}u_{s}\,\psi
with standard parameters is automatically extended to all inputs, where it is given by the
\in
-formula
\mathsf{Q}_{1}u_{1}\ldots\mathsf{Q}_{s}u_{s}\,\psi
.

(2)

Standardization holds for all
\in
-formulas with additional predicate symbols, as long as all these additional predicates are defined by
\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}
-prenex formulas with standard parameters.

3.
Two examples

Formulas that occur in practice are usually not in the
\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}
-prenex form, but they can often be converted to it using Countable Idealization.

Definition 3.1
.

[
Integral of continuous functions
]

We fix a positive infinitesimal
h
and the corresponding “hyperfinite line”

\{x_{i}\,\mid\,i\in\mathbb{Z}\}
where
x_{i}=i\cdot h
. Let
f
be a standard real-valued function continuous on the standard interval
[a,b]
. Let
i_{a},i_{b}
be such that
i_{a}\cdot h-h<a\leq i_{a}\cdot h
and

i_{b}\cdot h<b\leq i_{b}\cdot h+h
. We define

(1)

\int_{a}^{b}f(x)\,dx=\operatorname{\mathbf{sh}}\left(\Sigma_{i=i_{a}}^{i_{b}}f(x_{i})\cdot h\right).

It is easy to show that the value of the integral does not depend on the choice of
h
.

Lemma 3.2
.

There is an
\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}_{\mathbb{N}}
-prenex formula
\Phi(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4})
such that

\int_{a}^{b}f(x)\,dx=r\longleftrightarrow\Phi(f,a,b,r)
holds
for all standard
f,a,b,r
.

Proof.

For standard
f,a,b,r
we have
\int_{a}^{b}f(x)\,dx=r
iff

\forall h\,\left[\forall^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}_{\mathbb{N}}n\;(|h|<\tfrac{1}{n})\rightarrow\,\forall^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}_{\mathbb{N}}m\left(|\Sigma_{i=i_{a}}^{i_{b}}f(x_{i})\cdot h-r|<\tfrac{1}{m}\right)\right]

(it is understood that
h
,
n
,
m
are not
0
).
This expression can be rewritten as

\forall h\;\forall^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}_{\mathbb{N}}m\;\exists^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}_{\mathbb{N}}n\,\left[|h|\geq\tfrac{1}{n}\,\vee\,|\Sigma_{i=i_{a}}^{i_{b}}f(x_{i})\cdot h-r|<\tfrac{1}{m}\right].

We swap the outmost universal quantifiers and apply the dual version of Countable Idealization
(Lemma
2.2
) to get

\forall^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}_{\mathbb{N}}m\;\exists^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}_{\mathbb{N}}n\,\forall h\;\exists k\leq n\;\left[|h|\geq\tfrac{1}{k}\,\vee\,|\Sigma_{i=i_{a}}^{i_{b}}f(x_{i})\cdot h-r|<\tfrac{1}{m}\right],

which is an
\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}_{\mathbb{N}}
-prenex formula, clearly equivalent to

\forall^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}_{\mathbb{N}}m\;\exists^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}_{\mathbb{N}}n\,\forall h\;\left[|h|\geq\tfrac{1}{n}\,\vee\,|\Sigma_{i=i_{a}}^{i_{b}}f(x_{i})\cdot h-r|<\tfrac{1}{m}\right].

∎

One can now use Standardization for
\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}
-prenex formulas with standard parameters to conclude that, for example, for every standard
f
,
a
there exists a standard function
F
such that
F(z)=\int_{a}^{z}f(x)\,dx
for all standard
z\in[a,b]
. By Remark

(1), the last equation holds for all
z\in[a,b]
.
Of course, the usual arguments show that the above definition of the integral agrees with the traditional
\epsilon
-
\delta
one for all standard
f,a,b,r
.

The following observation is crucial for the proof of Proposition
3.6
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
w
be a function,
\operatorname{dom}w=D_{w}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
and
\operatorname{ran}w\subseteq\mathbb{R}
. Then the formula

\Psi(x,y):\quad\exists\alpha\in D_{w}\;[x\approx\alpha\,\wedge\,(y\approx w(\alpha)\,\vee\,y\geq w(\alpha))]

is equivalent to an

\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}_{\mathbb{N}}
-prenex formula (with the parameter
w
).

Proof.

The formula
\Psi(x,y)
can be written as

\exists\alpha\in D_{w}\;[\forall^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}_{\mathbb{N}}i\,(|x-\alpha|<\tfrac{1}{i+1})\,\wedge\,(\forall^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}_{\mathbb{N}}j\,(|y-w(\alpha)|<\tfrac{1}{j+1})\,\vee\,y\geq w(\alpha))],

which is equivalent to

\exists\alpha\in D_{w}\;\forall^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}_{\mathbb{N}}i\;\forall^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}_{\mathbb{N}}j\,[(|x-\alpha|<\tfrac{1}{i+1})\,\wedge\,(|y-w(\alpha)|<\tfrac{1}{j+1}\,\vee\,y\geq w(\alpha))].

This is equivalent to

\exists\alpha\in D_{w}\;\forall^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}_{\mathbb{N}}n\,\ [(|x-\alpha|<\tfrac{1}{n+1})\,\wedge\,(|y-w(\alpha)|<\tfrac{1}{n+1}\,\vee\,y\geq w(\alpha))]

(let
n=\min{\{i,j}\}
), and finally (Countable Idealization, Lemma
2.2
) to the
\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}_{\mathbb{N}}
-prenex formula

\forall^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}_{\mathbb{N}}n\,\exists\alpha\in D_{w}\;\forall m\leq n\ [(|x-\alpha|<\tfrac{1}{m+1})\,\wedge\,(|y-w(\alpha)|<\tfrac{1}{m+1}\,\vee\,y\geq w(\alpha))].

The last formula of course simplifies to

\forall^{\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}}_{\mathbb{N}}n\,\exists\alpha\in D_{w}\;[(|x-\alpha|<\tfrac{1}{n+1})\,\wedge\,(|y-w(\alpha)|<\tfrac{1}{n+1}\,\vee\,y\geq w(\alpha))].

∎

Definition 3.4
.

Let
w
be a function,
\operatorname{dom}w=D_{w}\subseteq I
where
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
is a standard interval, and
\operatorname{ran}w\subseteq\mathbb{R}

•

The function

The function

\alpha\approx\beta

Lemma 3.5
.

A function

is S-continuous iff for every standard
\epsilon>0

\alpha,\beta\in D_{w}

|\alpha-\beta|<\delta

Proof.

The usual arguments work in
\mathbf{SPOT}
; see e.g.
[

]
.

The next proposition follows immediately from the Standardization
principle of
\mathbf{IST}
or
\mathbf{BST}
, but to prove it in
\mathbf{SPOT}
we need to

W
on the rationals, to which we

\operatorname{\mathbf{st}}_{\mathbb{N}}
-prenex formulas.

Proposition 3.6
.

If
w
is S-continuous and densely defined on
I
, then there is a standard function
W
such that, for all standard
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
,

\langle x,y\rangle\in W
if and only if
x\approx\alpha

Remark 7.1
.

The proofs of the global Peano theorem we found in the literature often simply appeal to Zorn’s lemma (eg. Ganesh
[
3
]
, Theorem 4.7). The more careful proofs depend on
\mathbf{ADC}
, usually without mentioning it explicitly.
Hale
[
4
]
in his proof of global Peano theorem (Theorem 2.1, p. 17) writes:

“… there is a monotone increasing sequence
\{b_{n}\}

constructed as above so that the solution
x(t)
of (1.1) on
[a,b]
has an extension to the interval
[a,b_{n}]
and
(b_{n},x(b_{n}))
is not in
\bar{V_{n}}
. Since the
b_{n}
are bounded above,
let
\omega=\lim_{n\to\infty}b_{n}
. It is clear that
x
has
been extended to the interval
[a,\omega)
…”

What is actually clear is that his construction yields solutions
x_{n}(t)
on
[a,b_{n}]
for each
n
, and each
x_{n}(t)
has extensions to some
x_{n+1}(t)
.
The axiom
\mathbf{ADC}
is needed to justify the existence of
x(t)
.
Similarly Hartman
[
6
, II, 3.1, p. 13]
constructs an increasing sequence
\{b_{n}\}
such that any solution on
[a,b_{n}]
has an extension to a solution on
[a,b_{n+1}]
.
Here
\mathbf{ADC}
is needed to justify the existence of a solution on
[a,\omega_{+}]
for
\omega_{+}=\lim_{n\to\infty}b_{n}
. In Hartman’s proof of III, Lemma 2.1, a key step to the proof of III, Theorem 2.1 (Osgood’s theorem),
\mathbf{ACC}
is used implicitly to obtain the sequence
\{u_{n}(t)\}
.
Similar unacknowledged use of
\mathbf{ADC}
appears in Kurzweil
[
14
]
, pp. 355–356.

Remark 7.2
.

Simpson
[
17
]
carried out a thorough study of the axioms needed to prove the
local
versions of Peano and Osgood theorems. He showed that (over
\mathbf{RCA}_{0}
) the local Peano theorem is equivalent to
\mathbf{WKL}_{0}
and the local Osgood theorem is equivalent to
\mathbf{ACA}_{0}
(see Simpson
[
18
]
for the description of these systems of second order arithmetic and additional information). In particular, the proofs of local versions of these theorems do not need any form of
\mathbf{AC}
.

Remark 7.3
.

The conservativity of
\mathbf{SPOT}
over
\mathbf{ZF}
and the results of this paper imply that global Peano and Osgood theorems are provable in
\mathbf{ZF}
.

In a discussion on MathOverflow
[
5
]
, James Hanson pointed out
that the same conclusion follows from Shoenfield’s absoluteness
theorem. A consequence of this theorem is that every
\Pi^{1}_{4}

sentence provable in
\mathbf{ZFC}
is provable in
\mathbf{ZF}
alone. The global
Peano theorem can be expressed by a
\Pi^{1}_{4}
sentence, and therefore
it is provable in
\mathbf{ZF}
. The
\mathbf{ZF}
proof obtained by conversion of the

\mathbf{ZFC}
proof by this method is far from elementary; in addition to
Shoenfield’s absoluteness theorem, it relies on the notion of
relatively constructible sets.

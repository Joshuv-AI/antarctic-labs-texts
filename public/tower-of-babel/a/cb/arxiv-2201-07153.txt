Braid groups and symplectic Steinberg groups
François Digne, Christian Kassel

Abstract
. We construct a homomorphism f f from the braid group B 2 ​ n + 2 B_{2n+2} on 2 ​ n + 2 2n+2 strands to the Steinberg group St ⁡ ( C n , ℤ ) \operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}}) associated with the Lie type C n C_{n} and with integer coefficients. This homomorphism lifts the well-known symplectic representation (aka the integral Burau representation) of the braid group. We also describe the image and the kernel of f f .

1.
Introduction

In this article we provide a connection between low-dimensional topology and algebraic
K
-theory.
More precisely, let
B_{2n+2}
be the braid group on
2n+2
strands (
n\geq 2
).
In
[
17
]
, following work by Arnold, Magnus & Peluso, Birman, A’Campo
et al
. (see
[
1
,
2
,
4
,
5
,
21
]
),
the second-named author investigated an action of
B_{2n+2}
on the free group
F_{2n}
on
2n
generators
obtained by viewing a twice-punctured surface of genus
n
as a double covering of the disk
via an hyperelliptic involution.
Linearizing this action, one obtains a homomorphism
\bar{f}:B_{2n+2}\to\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{Z}})

from the braid group to the symplectic modular group
\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{Z}})
.
This symplectic representation was shown by Gambaudo and Ghys
to be the Burau representation specialized at
t=-1
(see
[
16
, Prop. 2.1]
).

Now
\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{Z}})
is a Chevalley group of Lie type
C_{n}
. By
[
24
,
25
,
26
]
it has a natural extension,
its Steinberg group
\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})
,
which is defined by means of a presentation by generators and relations.
(Steinberg groups are basic ingredients in algebraic
K
-theory; see for instance
[
23
]
.)

Our main observation is that the symplectic representation
\bar{f}
(aka the integral Burau representation mentioned above)
can be lifted to a homomorphism

f:B_{2n+2}\to\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})

from the braid group to the Steinberg group.

When
n\geq 3
the lifting
f
is not surjective; we shall describe its image.
We also show how to extend
f
to a surjective homomorphism with a bigger Artin group as domain.

We further describe the kernel of
f
.
As an application we obtain a simple braid-like presentation of the image of
f
(resp. of the image of
\bar{f}
),
which is a subgroup of finite index of
\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})
(resp. of
\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{Z}})
).

The paper is a continuation of
[
18
]
, which dealt with the case
n=2
.
It is organized as follows.
In Section
2
we give a presentation of the Steinberg group
\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})

and list a few properties of the special elements
w_{\gamma}
.
In Section
3
we construct the lifting
f
from the braid group to the Steinberg group.
In Section
4
we determine its image (see Theorem
4.1
).
Section
5
is devoted to a description of the kernel of
f
:
we highlight a braid
\alpha_{n}\in B_{2n+2}
which together with two other braids generate the kernel of
f
as a normal subgroup
(see Theorem
5.8
).
In Section
6

we extend
f
to an epimorphism
\widehat{f}:\widehat{B}_{2n+2}\to\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})
,
where
\widehat{B}_{2n+2}
is an Artin group (see Theorem
6.1
)
slightly bigger than the braid group
B_{2n+2}
.

2.
The Steinberg group
\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})

With any irreducible root system
\Phi
Steinberg
[
25
,
26
]
associated the so-called Steinberg group,
which is an extension of the simple complex algebraic group of type
\Phi
.
Later Stein
[
24
]
extended Steinberg’s construction over any commutative ring
R
,
thus leading to the Steinberg group
\operatorname{St}(\Phi,R)
.
We are interested in the case when the root system
\Phi
is of type
C_{n}
(
n\geq 2
) and
R={\mathbb{Z}}
is the ring
of integers. The corresponding Steinberg group
\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})
is an extension of the symplectic modular group
\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{Z}})
.

2.1.
The symplectic modular group
\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{Z}})

Let
n
be an integer
\geq 2
.
Recall that
\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{Z}})
is the group of
2n\times 2n
matrices
M
with integral entries satisfying
the relation
M^{T}J_{2n}M=J_{2n}
, where
M^{T}
is the transpose of
M
,

J_{2n}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathrm{Id}_{n}\cr-\mathrm{Id}_{n}&0\end{pmatrix}

and
\mathrm{Id}_{n}
is the identity matrix of size
n
.

Let
\{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{n}\}
be the canonical basis of the free abelian group
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
.
In Bourbaki’s notation the root system of type
C_{n}
consists of the following elements (see
[
8
, Chap. VI, § 4.6]
):
the elements
\pm\varepsilon_{i}\pm\varepsilon_{j}
(
1\leq i,j\leq n
,
i\neq j
) are the
short roots
and

\pm 2\varepsilon_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq n
) are the
long roots
.

Denote by
E_{i,j}
the
2n\times 2n
matrix which has all
entries equal to
0
except the
(i,j)
-entry which is equal to
1
.
The group
\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{Z}})
is generated by the following matrices (see
[
9
]
):

•

X_{i,j}=\mathrm{Id}_{2n}+E_{i,j}-E_{j+n,i+n}
for
1\leq i,j\leq n
,
i\neq j
,

•

Y_{i,j}=\mathrm{Id}_{2n}+E_{i,j+n}+E_{j,i+n}
for
1\leq i,j\leq n
,
i\neq j
,

•

Y^{\prime}_{i,j}=Y_{i,j}^{T}
for
1\leq i,j\leq n
,
i\neq j
,

•

Z_{i}=\mathrm{Id}_{2n}+E_{i,i+n}
for
1\leq i\leq n
,

•

Z^{\prime}_{i}=Z_{i}^{T}
for
1\leq i\leq n
.

Note that
Y_{i,j}=Y_{j,i}
and
Y^{\prime}_{i,j}=Y^{\prime}_{j,i}
.

Each of these matrices generates a root subgroup corresponding to a root in the following way:

X_{i,j}
corresponds to the root
\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j}
,

Y_{i,j}
to the root
\varepsilon_{i}+\varepsilon_{j}
,

Y^{\prime}_{i,j}
to
-\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j}
,

Z_{i}
to
2\varepsilon_{i}
, and
Z^{\prime}_{i}
to
-2\varepsilon_{i}
.

We now list the commutation relations between pairs of these matrices corresponding to non-opposite roots.
In the following relations, the indices
i
,
j
and
k
are pairwise
distinct and run over
\{1,\ldots,n\}
:

[X_{i,j},X_{j,k}]=X_{i,k}\,,\quad[X_{i,j},Y_{j,k}]=Y_{i,k}\,,\quad

[X_{i,j},Y^{\prime}_{i,k}]=Y_{j,k}^{\prime-1}\,,\quad[Y_{i,j},Y^{\prime}_{j,k}]=X_{i,k}\,,

[X_{i,j},Y_{i,j}]=Z_{i}^{2}\,,\quad[X_{i,j},Y^{\prime}_{i,j}]=Z_{j}^{\prime-2}\,,

[X_{i,j},Z_{j}]=Z_{i}Y_{i,j}=Y_{i,j}Z_{i}\,,\quad[X_{i,j},Z^{\prime}_{i}]=Z^{\prime}_{j}Y_{i,j}^{\prime-1}=Y_{i,j}^{\prime-1}Z^{\prime}_{j}\,,

[Y_{i,j},Z^{\prime}_{i}]=X_{j,i}Z_{j}^{-1}=Z_{j}^{-1}X_{j,i}\,,\quad[Y^{\prime}_{i,j},Z_{i}]=X_{i,j}^{-1}Z_{j}^{\prime-1}=Z_{j}^{\prime-1}X_{i,j}^{-1}\,.

The matrices commute for all other pairs of generators, except for

(X_{i,j},X_{j,i})
,
(Y_{i,j},Y^{\prime}_{i,j})
and
(Z_{i},Z^{\prime}_{i})
, which are pairs corresponding to opposite roots.

2.2.
A presentation of the Steinberg group

By
[
3
, Sect. 3]
and
[
24
]
the Steinberg group
\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})
has a presentation
with the same generators and relations as above, namely with generators

x_{i,j}
,
y_{i,j}
,
y^{\prime}_{i,j}
(
1\leq i,j\leq n
and
i\neq j
),

z_{i}
,
z^{\prime}_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq n
)
subject to the following relations (where
i,j,k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
are pairwise distinct):

(2.1)

y_{i,j}=y_{j,i}\,,\qquad y^{\prime}_{i,j}=y^{\prime}_{j,i}\,,

(2.2)

[x_{i,j},x_{j,k}]=x_{i,k}\,,

(2.3)

[x_{i,j},y_{j,k}]=y_{i,k}\,,

(2.4)

[x_{i,j},y^{\prime}_{i,k}]=y_{j,k}^{\prime-1}\,,

(2.5)

[y_{i,j},y^{\prime}_{j,k}]=x_{i,k}\,,

(2.6)

[x_{i,j},y_{i,j}]=z_{i}^{2}\,,

(2.7)

[x_{i,j},y^{\prime}_{i,j}]=z_{j}^{\prime-2}\,,

(2.8)

[x_{i,j},z_{j}]=z_{i}y_{i,j}=y_{i,j}z_{i}\,,

(2.9)

[x_{i,j},z^{\prime}_{i}]=z^{\prime}_{j}y_{i,j}^{\prime-1}=y_{i,j}^{\prime-1}z^{\prime}_{j}\,,

(2.10)

[y_{i,j},z^{\prime}_{i}]=x_{j,i}z_{j}^{-1}=z_{j}^{-1}x_{j,i}\,,

(2.11)

[y^{\prime}_{i,j},z_{i}]=x_{i,j}^{-1}z_{j}^{\prime-1}=z_{j}^{\prime-1}x_{i,j}^{-1}\,,

and all remaining pairs of generators commuting, except the pairs

(x_{i,j},x_{j,i})
,
(y_{i,j},y^{\prime}_{i,j})
and
(z_{i},z^{\prime}_{i})
for which we do not prescribe any relation.

Note that in view of (
2.9
) and (
2.10
) the generators
x_{i,j}
and
y^{\prime}_{i,j}
can be expressed
in terms of the other generators.

By construction there is a surjective homomorphism

\pi:\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})\to\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{Z}})

sending each generator of
\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})
represented by a lower-case letter to the symplectic matrix
represented by the corresponding upper-case letter.

By
[
22
, Th. 6.3]
and
[
3
, Kor. 3.2]
the kernel of the epimorphism
\pi:\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})\to\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{Z}})

is infinite cyclic generated by
(x_{2\varepsilon_{i}}\,x_{-2\varepsilon_{i}}^{-1}\,x_{2\varepsilon_{i}})^{4}
,
where
x_{2\varepsilon_{i}}
(resp.
x_{-2\varepsilon_{i}}
) is the generator corresponding to the long root
2\varepsilon_{i}

(resp. to
-2\varepsilon_{i}
); this generator is independent of
i
and central (see Lemma
2.1
below;
see also
[
20
]
).

2.3.
The elements
w_{\gamma}

For a root
\gamma
let
x_{\gamma}
be the generator of the Steinberg group corresponding to
\gamma
.
Set

(2.12)

w_{\gamma}=x_{\gamma}\,x_{-\gamma}^{-1}\,x_{\gamma}\in\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})\,.

In particular, we have

(2.13)

w_{2\varepsilon_{i}}=z_{i}\,z_{i}^{\prime-1}\,z_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad w_{-2\varepsilon_{i}}=z^{\prime}_{i}\,z_{i}^{-1}\,z^{\prime}_{i}\,.

For simplicity we write
w_{i}
for
w_{2\varepsilon_{i}}
.
Since
z_{i}
commutes with
z_{j}
and with
z^{\prime}_{j}
when
i\neq j
,
we have
w_{i}w_{j}=w_{j}w_{i}
for all
(i,j)\in\{1,\ldots,n\}^{2}
.

The following equality holds for all roots
\gamma
:

(2.14)

w_{\gamma}=w_{-\gamma}^{-1}

(for a proof, see
[
18
, Lemma 2.2]
).
As a consequence, we have

(2.15)

w_{\gamma}x_{\gamma}w_{\gamma}^{-1}=w_{-\gamma}^{-1}x_{\gamma}w_{\gamma}^{-1}=x_{-\gamma}^{-1}\,x_{\gamma}\,x_{-\gamma}^{-1}x_{\gamma}x_{\gamma}^{-1}\,x_{-\gamma}\,x_{\gamma}^{-1}=x_{-\gamma}^{-1}.

Similarly,

(2.16)

w_{\gamma}x_{-\gamma}w_{\gamma}^{-1}=x_{\gamma}^{-1}.

It follows from (
2.15
) and (
2.16
) that the square
w_{\gamma}^{2}
commutes with
x_{\gamma}

and with
x_{-\gamma}
for all roots
\gamma
.

We also need the subsequent relation between an element
w_{\gamma}
and the generator
x_{\delta}

associated with a root
\delta
such that
\gamma+\delta\neq 0
, namely

(2.17)

w_{\gamma}\,x_{\delta}\,w_{\gamma}^{-1}=x_{\delta^{\prime}}^{c}\,,

where
\delta^{\prime}
is the image of
\delta
under the reflection
s_{\gamma}

in the hyperplane orthogonal to
\gamma
and
c=\pm 1

(see Relation (R7) in
[
26
, Chap. 3, p. 23]
).
Recall that
s_{\gamma}
is given by

s_{\gamma}(\delta)=\delta-2\frac{(\gamma,\delta)}{(\gamma,\gamma)}\gamma\,,

where
(-,-)
is the inner product of the Euclidean vector space of which
the set
\{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{n}\}
forms an orthonormal basis.
To determine the sign
c
(and the root
\delta^{\prime}
) in (
2.17
) it is enough to compute the image

\pi(w_{\gamma}\,x_{\delta}\,w_{\gamma}^{-1})
in
\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{Z}})
.

In particular, for any long root
2\varepsilon_{i}
the element
w_{i}=w_{2\varepsilon_{i}}
commutes with all
generators
x_{k,\ell}
,
y_{k,\ell}
and
y^{\prime}_{k,\ell}
such that
k\neq i\neq\ell
.
By contrast we have the non-trivial relations (
i\neq j
)

(2.18)

w_{i}\,y_{i,j}\,w_{i}^{-1}=x_{j,i}\,,\qquad w_{i}\,x_{j,i}\,w_{i}^{-1}=y_{i,j}^{-1}\,,

and

(2.19)

w_{i}\,y^{\prime}_{i,j}\,w_{i}^{-1}=x_{i,j}\,,\qquad w_{i}\,x_{i,j}\,w_{i}^{-1}=y_{i,j}^{\prime-1}

in the Steinberg group
\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})
.
Hence, the conjugation by the square
w_{i}^{2}
turns each generator
x_{i,j}
,
x_{j,i}
,
y_{i,j}
,
y^{\prime}_{i,j}
into its inverse,
namely

(2.20)

w_{i}^{2}\,y_{i,j}\,w_{i}^{-2}=y_{i,j}^{-1}\,,\qquad w_{i}^{2}\,x_{j,i}\,w_{i}^{-2}=x_{j,i}^{-1}\,,

and

(2.21)

w_{i}^{2}\,y^{\prime}_{i,j}\,w_{i}^{-2}=y_{i,j}^{\prime-1}\,,\qquad w_{i}^{2}\,x_{i,j}\,w_{i}^{-2}=x_{i,j}^{-1}\,.

The following lemma will be used in the sequel.

Lemma 2.1
.

For each
i=1,\ldots,n

the element
w_{i}^{4}
is central in
\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})
and we have
w_{i}^{4}=w_{1}^{4}
.

Proof.

The centrality of
w_{i}^{4}
follows from (
2.15
), (
2.16
), (
2.20
) and (
2.21
).
Now all elements
w_{i}
are conjugate as a consequence of the following special cases of (
2.17
), where
i\neq j
:

w_{\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j}}\,z_{i}\,w_{\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j}}^{-1}=z_{j}\quad\text{and}\quad w_{\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j}}\,z^{\prime}_{i}\,w_{\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j}}^{-1}=z^{\prime}_{j}\,.

The conclusion follows.
∎

3.
From the braid group to the Steinberg group

Let
B_{2n+2}
be the braid group on
2n+2
strands, where
n\geq 2
is a fixed integer.
It has a standard presentation with
2n+1
generators

\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\ldots,\sigma_{2n+1}

and the following relations (
1\leq i,j\leq 2n+1
):

(3.1)

\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\quad\text{if}\;|i-j|=1,

and

(3.2)

\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}=\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i}\qquad\;\;\text{otherwise}.

Let us now construct a homomorphism from
B_{2n+2}
to the symplectic Steinberg group
\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})
.

Theorem 3.1
.

There exists a homomorphism

f:B_{2n+2}\to\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})
such that

f(\sigma_{1})=z_{1}\,,\qquad f(\sigma_{2n+1})=z_{n}\,,

f(\sigma_{2i})=z_{i}^{\prime-1}\qquad\text{for}\;\,i=1,\ldots,n,

f(\sigma_{2i+1})=z_{i}z_{i+1}y_{i,i+1}^{-1}\qquad\text{for}\;\,i=1,\ldots,n-1.

The homomorphism
f
is surjective if and only if
n=2
.

Remarks 3.2
.

(a) By
[
17
]
the homomorphism
\bar{f}:B_{2n+2}\to\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{Z}})
mentioned in the introduction is
defined on the generators
\sigma_{i}
by

\bar{f}(\sigma_{1})=Z_{1}\,,\qquad\bar{f}(\sigma_{2n+1})=Z_{n}\,,

\bar{f}(\sigma_{2i})=Z_{i}^{\prime-1}\qquad\text{for}\;\,i=1,\ldots,n,

\bar{f}(\sigma_{2i+1})=Z_{i}Z_{i+1}Y_{i,i+1}^{-1}\quad\text{for}\;\,i=1,\ldots,n-1,

where
Y_{i,i+1}
,
Z_{i}
and
Z^{\prime}_{i}
are the symplectic matrices defined in Section
2.1
.
It follows from these formulas and from the definition of
f
in Theorem
3.1

that the latter is a natural lifting of
\bar{f}
, i.e. we have
\bar{f}=\pi\circ f
,
where
\pi:\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})\to\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{Z}})
is the natural projection.

(b) By definition of
f
and of
w_{1}=w_{2\varepsilon_{1}}
(see (
2.13
)) we have

(3.3)

w_{1}=f(\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1})\,.

It follows from this equality and the remark at the end of Section
2.2

that the kernel of
\pi:\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})\to\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{Z}})
,
which is generated by
w_{1}^{4}
, belongs to the image of
f
.
We thus recover the known fact that the integral Burau representation is not injective.

(c) Note that all three factors in the product
z_{i}z_{i+1}y_{i,i+1}^{-1}
expressing
f(\sigma_{2i+1})
commute.

Proof.

It suffices to check that the values of
f(\sigma_{i})
(
1\leq i\leq 2n+1
)
in the Steinberg group
\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})
satisfy the braid relations (
3.1
) and (
3.2
).

(i) Let us first check the trivial commutation relations (
3.2
).

•

Commutation of
f(\sigma_{1})
with
f(\sigma_{2n+1})
.

This follows from the fact that
z_{1}
and
z_{n}
commute.

•

Commutation of
f(\sigma_{1})
with
f(\sigma_{2i})
when
i\geq 2
.

Indeed,
z_{1}
commutes with
z^{\prime}_{i}
when
i\neq 1
.

•

Commutation of
f(\sigma_{1})
with all
f(\sigma_{2i+1})
.

This is implied by the commuting of
z_{1}
with the other
z_{i}
and with the generators
y_{i,j}
.

Similarly for the trivial braid relations involving
f(\sigma_{2n+1})=z_{n}
.

•

Commutation of
f(\sigma_{2i})
with
f(\sigma_{2j})
.

This follows from the fact that the generators
z^{\prime}_{i}
commute with one another.

•

Commutation of
f(\sigma_{2i+1})
with
f(\sigma_{2j+1})
.
Indeed, the
z_{i}
’s commute with one another,
as do the
y_{i,j}
’s. Moreover, the
z_{i}
’s commute with the
y_{i,j}
’s.

•

Commutation of
f(\sigma_{2i})
with
f(\sigma_{2j+1})
when
i\notin\{i-1,i\}
.
This is implied by the facts that
z^{\prime}_{i}
commutes with
y_{j,j+1}
when
i\neq j
and that

z^{\prime}_{i}
commutes with
z_{j}z_{j+1}
when
j\notin\{i-1,i\}
.

(ii) The relation
f(\sigma_{1})f(\sigma_{2})f(\sigma_{1})=f(\sigma_{2})f(\sigma_{1})f(\sigma_{2})
reads as

z_{1}z_{1}^{\prime-1}z_{1}=z_{1}^{\prime-1}z_{1}z_{1}^{\prime-1}\,,

which is equivalent to
w_{2\varepsilon_{1}}=w_{-2\varepsilon_{1}}^{-1}
,
where we use the notation of Section
2.3
. The latter equality holds by (
2.14
).

(iii) The relation
f(\sigma_{2i})f(\sigma_{2i+1})f(\sigma_{2i})=f(\sigma_{2i+1})f(\sigma_{2i})f(\sigma_{2i+1})

reads for

as

z_{i}^{\prime-1}z_{i}z_{i+1}y_{i,i+1}^{-1}z_{i}^{\prime-1}=z_{i}z_{i+1}y_{i,i+1}^{-1}z_{i}^{\prime-1}z_{i}z_{i+1}y_{i,i+1}^{-1}\,.

Let
\operatorname{LHS}
(resp.
\operatorname{RHS}
) be the element of
\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})
represented by the left-hand (resp. right-hand) side of the
previous equation.

Since
z_{i}
commutes with
y_{i,i+1}
and with
z_{i+1}
, and the latter with

, we have

\operatorname{LHS}=z_{i+1}z_{i}^{\prime-1}y_{i,i+1}^{-1}z_{i}z_{i}^{\prime-1}\,.

By (

), (
2.10
), (
2.14
) and the trivial commutation relations we obtain

\displaystyle\operatorname{LHS}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z_{i+1}z_{i}^{\prime-1}y_{i,i+1}^{-1}z^{\prime}_{i}w_{-2\varepsilon_{i}}^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z_{i+1}y_{i,i+1}^{-1}z_{i}^{\prime-1}z_{i+1}x_{i+1,i}^{-1}z^{\prime}_{i}w_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z_{i+1}y_{i,i+1}^{-1}z_{i+1}x_{i+1,i}^{-1}w_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z_{i+1}^{2}y_{i,i+1}^{-1}x_{i+1,i}^{-1}w_{i}\,.

Let us now deal with
\operatorname{RHS}
.
Since
z_{i}
commutes with
y_{i,i+1}
, and
z_{i+1}
with
z_{i}
,
z^{\prime}_{i}
and
y_{i,i+1}
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{RHS}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z_{i+1}^{2}y_{i,i+1}^{-1}z_{i}z_{i}^{\prime-1}z_{i}y_{i,i+1}^{-1}=z_{i+1}^{2}y_{i,i+1}^{-1}w_{i}y_{i,i+1}^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z_{i+1}^{2}y_{i,i+1}^{-1}x_{i+1,i}^{-1}w_{i}=\operatorname{LHS}.

For the third equality we have used (
2.18
).

(iv) Applying the automorphism
\sigma_{i}\mapsto\sigma_{2n+2-i}
of
B_{2n+2}
, we reduce
the relations

f(\sigma_{2i-1})f(\sigma_{2i})f(\sigma_{2i-1})=f(\sigma_{2i})f(\sigma_{2i-1})f(\sigma_{2i})

(
2\leq i\leq n
) and

f(\sigma_{2n})f(\sigma_{2n+1})f(\sigma_{2n})=f(\sigma_{2n+1})f(\sigma_{2n})f(\sigma_{2n+1})

to the previous cases.

(v) The surjectivity for
n=2
was established in
[
18
]
.
Let us now prove that
f
is not surjective when
n\geq 3
.
We remark that under the composition
B_{2n+2}\rightarrow\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{Z}})\rightarrow\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})

of
\bar{f}
with the reduction modulo
2

the images of the generators
\sigma_{i}
of

have order
2
. Hence
this morphism factors through the symmetric group
{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{2n+2}
of all permutations of the set
\{1,\ldots,2n+2\}
.
If
f
is surjective, then
\bar{f}
is surjective too and we obtain a surjective morphism

{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{2n+2}\rightarrow\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})
since the reduction modulo

is surjective.
Now for

the symmetric group
{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{2n+2}
has no non-commutative proper quotient,
which implies that the map
{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{2n+2}\rightarrow\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})
is an isomorphism. But this is impossible
as
\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})
is a simple group when

and
{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{2n+2}
is not.
(See also
[
2
, Proof of Statement B]
.)
∎

4.
The image of the homomorphism
f

As noted in Theorem
3.1
, the homomorphism
f:B_{2n+2}\to\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})

is not surjective when
n\geq 3
. We can nevertheless determine its image
f(B_{2n+2})

inside
\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})
.

Consider the
level
2
congruence subgroup

\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{Z}})[2]
defined as the kernel of the homomorphism

\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{Z}})\rightarrow\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})

induced by reduction modulo
2
. We lift
\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{Z}})[2]
to the Steinberg group by taking its preimage

(4.1)

\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})[2]=\pi^{-1}\left(\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{Z}})[2]\right)

under the canonical projection
\pi:\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})\to\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{Z}})
.
Thus
\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})[2]
is the kernel of the composition

\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})\overset{\pi}{\longrightarrow}\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{Z}})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{F}}_{2}).

The group
\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})
being finite,
\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})[2]
is of finite index in
\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})
.

Since by
[
3
, Kor. 3.2]
the kernel of the projection
\pi:\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})\to\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{Z}})

is the infinite cyclic group
\langle w_{1}^{4}\rangle
generated by
w_{1}^{4}
, we have the short exact sequence

1\to\langle w_{1}^{4}\rangle\longrightarrow\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})[2]\overset{\pi}{\longrightarrow}\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{Z}})[2]\to 1.

Recall the surjective homomorphism
p:B_{2n+2}\to{\mathfrak{S}}_{2n+2}
sending each generator
\sigma_{i}
of
B_{2n+2}

to the simple transposition
s_{i}\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{2n+2}
, where
s_{i}
permutes
i
and
i+1
and leaves the remaining
elements of
\{1,\ldots,2n+2\}
fixed. The kernel of
p
is the
pure braid group

P_{2n+2}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume
n\geq 2
.
(a) The images under
f
of the pure braid groups
P_{2n+2}
and
P_{2n+1}

are both equal to
\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})[2]

f(P_{2n+2})=f(P_{2n+1})=\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})[2].

(b) The image of the full braid group
B_{2n+2}
fits into the short exact sequence

1\to\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})[2]\longrightarrow f(B_{2n+2})\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{S}}_{2n+2}\to 1.

When
n=2
the homomorphism
f
is surjective, that is
f(B_{6})=\operatorname{St}(C_{2},{\mathbb{Z}})
.
One recovers in this way the well-known isomorphism
\operatorname{Sp}_{4}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})\cong{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{6}
.

In general
f(B_{2n+2})
is of finite index in
\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})
with index

equal to

i_{n}=\frac{\mathrm{card}\,\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})}{\mathrm{card}\,{\mathfrak{S}}_{2n+2}}=2^{n^{2}}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(2^{2i}-1)}{(2n+2)!}\

(see
[
12
, p. 64]
).
The values of

grow very rapidly; for low subscripts they are:

i_{2}=1
,
i_{3}=36
,
i_{4}=13\,056
,
i_{5}=51\,806\,208
,

i_{6}=2\,387\,230\,064\,640
.

Proof.

By Remark
3.2
(b) the kernel
\langle w_{1}^{4}\rangle
of
\pi:\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})\to\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{Z}})

is in the image of
f
.
Therefore, in view of the definitions of
\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})[2]
and of
P_{2n+2}

it is enough to check that the image of
\bar{f}=\pi\circ f:B_{2n+2}\to\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{Z}})
satisfies the following two properties:
(i)
\bar{f}(P_{2n+2})=\bar{f}(P_{2n+1})=\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{Z}})[2]
and (ii) there is an isomorphism

\bar{f}(B_{2n+2})/\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{Z}})[2]\cong{\mathfrak{S}}_{2n+2}
.

As we observed in Part (v) of the proof of Theorem
3.1
,
the composition
B_{2n+2}\overset{\bar{f}}{\longrightarrow}\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{Z}})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})

Hence we have the inclusions
\bar{f}(P_{2n+1})\subset\bar{f}(P_{2n+2})\subset\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{Z}})[2]

The induced morphism
{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{2n+2}\rightarrow\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})

(which is non-trivial since the image of
\sigma_{1}
in
\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})
is not the identity)
is injective since the images of
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
do not commute
and
{{\mathfrak{S}}}_{2n+2}
has no non-commutative proper quotient when

.
This implies that Property (ii) above follows from (i). Let us now prove the latter.

In order to establish the opposite inclusion
\bar{f}(P_{2n+1})\supset\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{Z}})[2]
,
it is sufficient to prove
\bar{f}(B_{2n+1})\supset\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{Z}})[2]
.
We appeal to
[
1
]
. In
loc. cit
. A’Campo considers the monodromy representation of
B_{2n+1}

in the free
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module
V
with basis
\{\delta_{i},\,i=1,\ldots,2n\}
,
endowed with an alternating form

satisfying
I(\delta_{i},\delta_{i+1})=1
for

.
This form is non-degenerate.
The monodromy representation maps each generator
\sigma_{i}
of
B_{2n+1}

to the automorphism

of

defined by

T_{i}(\delta_{j})=\begin{cases}\delta_{j}&\text{ if }j\neq i-1,i+1,\\
\delta_{j}+\delta_{i}&\text{ if }j=i-1,\\
\delta_{j}-\delta_{i}&\text{ if }j=i+1.\end{cases}

One thus obtains a representation of

in the symplectic group
\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{Z}})
.

Now by
[
1
, Th. 1 (2)]
the image of this monodromy representation contains the congruence subgroup
\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{Z}})[2]
.
To conclude, it suffices to check that this representation is isomorphic to our
\bar{f}
. Indeed,
If we take

as a basis of

, the matrix of the form

becomes in

the matrix

considered in §

.
One easily checks that in this new basis the matrix of

is equal to that of
\bar{f}(\sigma_{i})
for

.
∎

As noted by a referee, Conditions (i)–(ii) above may also be derived from recent results of

).

The squares of all the generators

Remark 4.4
.

Given a prime

, the level

is

By

5.
The kernel of
f

In this section we determine the kernels of
\bar{f}:B_{2n+2}\to\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{Z}})
and of
f:B_{2n+2}\to\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})
.

5.1.
The elements
\Delta_{k}
and their images

We start with a few simple-looking elements of the kernel of
f
.

For
k\in\{2,\ldots,2n+2\}

let
\Delta_{k}\in B_{k}
be the longest reduced positive braid in the group
B_{k}
of braids with
k
strands.
It is defined inductively by
\Delta_{2}=\sigma_{1}
and

\Delta_{k+1}=(\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\cdots\sigma_{k})\,\Delta_{k}
for
k\geq 2
.
It is well known (see
[
6
]
or
[
19
, Sect. 1.3.3]
) that its square
\Delta_{k}^{2}
generates the center of
B_{k}
.

We now consider
\Delta_{k}
as an element of
B_{2n+2}
under the natural inclusion
B_{k}\to B_{2n+2}

sending each generator
\sigma_{i}\in B_{k}
(
1\leq i\leq k-1
) to
\sigma_{i}\in B_{2n+2}
.
We may thus look for the image of
\Delta_{k}
under the homomorphism
f:B_{2n+2}\to\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})
.

Let us first compute the image
f(\Delta_{k}^{2})
of the squares of some of the braids
\Delta_{k}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
f:B_{2n+2}\to\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})
be the homomorphism defined in Theorem
3.1
.
For each
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
we have

f(\Delta_{2i+1}^{2})=\left(w_{1}^{4}\right)^{i(i-1)/2}\prod_{j=1}^{i}\,w_{j}^{2}\,.

Moreover,

f(\Delta_{2n+2}^{2})=\left(w_{1}^{4}\right)^{n(n+1)/2}.

Remark 5.2
.

The element
\Delta_{2n+2}^{2}
generating the center of
B_{2n+2}
belongs to the kernel of
\bar{f}
;
it does not belong to the kernel of
f

since the order of
w_{1}^{4}
is infinite in the Steinberg group
\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})
(see
[
22
, Th. 6.3]
).

The following consequence of Proposition
5.1
provides us with
non-trivial elements of the kernel of
f
.

Corollary 5.3
.

(a) If
1\leq i\leq n
, then

f\left(\Delta_{2i+1}^{4}\,\Delta_{3}^{-4i^{2}}\right)=1.

(b) We also have

f\left(\Delta_{2n+2}^{2}\,\Delta_{3}^{-2n(n+1)}\right)=1.

Proof.

By Proposition
5.1
and Lemma
2.1
we have

f\left(\Delta_{2i+1}^{4}\right)=\left(w_{1}^{4}\right)^{i^{2}}
. In the special case
i=1

we obtain
f(\Delta_{3}^{4})=w_{1}^{4}
. Therefore

f\left(\Delta_{2i+1}^{4}\right)=\left(w_{1}^{4}\right)^{i^{2}}=f\left(\Delta_{3}^{4i^{2}}\right).

Similarly,
f\left(\Delta_{2n+2}^{2}\right)=f(\Delta_{3}^{2n(n+1)})
.
∎

To prove Proposition
5.1
we need two preliminary lemmas.

Lemma 5.4
.

We have

f(\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\cdots\sigma_{2i})=\begin{cases}w_{1}z_{1}^{-1}&\text{ if }i=1,\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\left(\prod_{j=1}^{i}\,w_{j}\right)\left(x_{1,2}x_{2,3}\cdots x_{i-1,i}\right)z_{i}^{-1}&\text{ if }1<i\leq n,\end{cases}

and

f(\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\cdots\sigma_{2i+1})=\begin{cases}z_{1}&\text{ if }i=0,\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr w_{1}y_{1,2}^{-1}z_{2}&\text{ if }i=1,\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\left(\prod_{j=1}^{i}\,w_{j}\right)\left(x_{1,2}x_{2,3}\cdots x_{i-1,i}\right)y_{i,i+1}^{-1}z_{i+1}&\text{ if }1<i<n,\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}\,w_{j}\right)\left(x_{1,2}x_{2,3}\cdots x_{n-1,n}\right)&\text{ if }i=n.\end{cases}

Proof.

We proceed by induction on the length
k
of the braid word
\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{k}
.
For
k=1,2
we have

f(\sigma_{1})=z_{1}
and
f(\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2})=z_{1}z_{1}^{\prime-1}=w_{1}z_{1}^{-1}
by definition of
f

and of
w_{1}
.

For
k\geq 3
, using the induction and (
2.18
), we obtain

\displaystyle f(\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{2i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{2i-1})f(\sigma_{2i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}\,w_{j}\right)\left(x_{1,2}x_{2,3}\cdots x_{i-2,i-1}\right)y_{i-1,i}^{-1}z_{i}z_{i}^{\prime-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}\,w_{j}\right)\left(x_{1,2}x_{2,3}\cdots x_{i-2,i-1}\right)y_{i-1,i}^{-1}w_{i}z_{i}^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}\,w_{j}\right)\left(x_{1,2}x_{2,3}\cdots x_{i-2,i-1}\right)w_{i}x_{i-1,i}z_{i}^{-1}.

Observing that
w_{i}
commutes with the
x_{j,j+1}

To obtain the formula for
f(\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{2i+1})
it suffices to multiply the formula for
f(\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{2i})

on the right by
f(\sigma_{2i+1})
which is equal to
z_{i}^{-1}z_{i}y_{i,i+1}^{-1}z_{i+1}
if
1<i<n

and to
z_{n}
if
i=n
and to cancel the product
z_{i}^{-1}z_{i}
.
∎

A similar computation yields the following result.

Lemma 5.5
.

We have

f(\sigma_{2i}\cdots\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1})=\begin{cases}z_{1}^{-1}w_{1}&\text{ if }i=1,\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr z_{i}^{\prime-1}z_{i}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}\,w_{j}\right)\left(x_{i,i-1}\cdots x_{3,2}x_{2,1}\right)&\text{ if }1<i\leq n,\end{cases}

5.1
.

(a) For the first equality we argue by induction on

\displaystyle\hskip-40.0ptf(\sigma_{2i-1}\cdots\sigma_{1}\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{2i-1})=

\displaystyle z_{i}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{i-2}w_{j}^{2}\right)w_{i-1}x_{i,i-1}w_{i-1}\times

\displaystyle f(\Delta_{2i+1}^{2})

\displaystyle=

which is the desired formula.

(b) We now prove the second formula of Proposition
5.1
.
By Lemmas
5.4
and
5.5

f(\sigma_{2n+1}\cdots\sigma_{1}\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{2n+1})\\
=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}\,w_{i}\right)\left(x_{n,n-1}\cdots x_{2,1}\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}\,w_{i}\right)\left(x_{1,2}x_{2,3}\cdots x_{n-1,n}\right).

Pushing the

f(\sigma_{2n+1}\cdots\sigma_{1}\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{2n+1})=\prod_{j=1}^{i=n}\,w_{j}^{2}\,.

Hence, by the induction formula, the first formula of the proposition for the case

,
and Lemma
2.1
we have

\displaystyle f(\Delta_{2n+2}^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(\Delta_{2n+1}^{2})f(\sigma_{2n+1}\cdots\sigma_{1}\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{2n+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(w_{1}^{4})^{n(n-1)/2}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}\,w_{j}^{2}\right)^{2}=(w_{1}^{4})^{n(n-1)/2}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\,w_{j}^{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(w_{1}^{4})^{n(n-1)/2}(w_{1}^{4})^{n}=(w_{1}^{4})^{n(n+1)/2}.

∎

\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})\xrightarrow{\pi}\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{Z}})\rightarrow\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})
.
Hence, if the image of
H
is the whole group
\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})
, we obtain the equality
H=\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})
.

Let us denote the images in
\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})
of the generators
x_{i,j}
,
y_{i,j}
,
y^{\prime}_{i,j}
,
z_{i}
,
z^{\prime}_{i}
of
\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})

by
\bar{x}_{i,j}
,
\bar{y}_{i,j}
,
\bar{y}^{\prime}_{i,j}
,
\bar{z}_{i}
,
\bar{z}^{\prime}_{i}
respectively.
Note that the latter generate
\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})
and that each of them is of order
2
, hence equal to its inverse.

Define
E_{n}
to be the set of the images
of
\{\bar{f}(\sigma_{1}),\bar{f}(\sigma_{2}),\ldots,\bar{f}(\sigma_{2n})\}
in
\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})
. We have

E_{n}=\left\{\bar{z}_{1},\bar{z}^{\prime}_{1},\bar{z}_{1}\bar{z}_{2}\bar{y}_{1,2},\bar{z}^{\prime}_{2},\bar{z}_{2}\bar{z}_{3}\bar{y}_{2,3},\bar{z}^{\prime}_{3},\ldots,\bar{z}^{\prime}_{n}\right\}.

In order to prove Theorem
6.1
, it is sufficient to check that

E_{n}\cup\{\bar{z}_{2}\}
generates
\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})
.
We will establish this assertion by induction on
n
.

Note that the assertion holds for
n=2
: indeed,
z_{2}=\bar{f}(\sigma_{5})
so that
the set
E_{2}\cup\{\bar{z}_{2}\}
generates the image of
\bar{f}
, which by
the surjectivity result of Theorem
3.1
is equal to the whole group
\operatorname{Sp}_{4}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})
.

We now prove the assertion for
n=3
.

Lemma 6.2
.

The set
E_{3}\cup\{\bar{z}_{2}\}
generates
\operatorname{Sp}_{6}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})
.

Proof.

Let
G_{3}
be the subgroup of
\operatorname{Sp}_{6}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})
generated by

E_{3}\cup\{\bar{z}_{2}\}=\{\bar{z}_{1},\bar{z}^{\prime}_{1},\bar{z}_{1}\bar{z}_{2}\bar{y}_{1,2},\bar{z}^{\prime}_{2},\bar{z}_{2}\bar{z}_{3}\bar{y}_{2,3},\bar{z}^{\prime}_{3},\bar{z}_{2}\}.

Obviously,
G_{3}
contains
\bar{y}_{1,2}
and
\bar{z}_{3}\bar{y}_{2,3}
.
We have the following relations deduced
from Equations (
2.2
), (
2.3
), (
2.8
) and (
2.10
):

[\bar{y}_{1,2},\bar{z}^{\prime}_{2}]=\bar{x}_{1,2}\bar{z}_{1}\,,\;\;\text{whence}\;\bar{x}_{1,2}\in G_{3}\,;

[\bar{x}_{1,2},\bar{z}_{3}\bar{y}_{2,3}]=[\bar{x}_{1,2},\bar{y}_{2,3}]=\bar{y}_{1,3}\,,\;\;\text{whence}\;\bar{y}_{1,3}\in G_{3}\,;

[\bar{y}_{1,3},\bar{z}^{\prime}_{1}]=\bar{x}_{3,1}\bar{z}_{3}\,,\;\;\text{whence}\;\bar{x}_{3,1}\bar{z}_{3}\in G_{3}\,;

[\bar{y}_{1,2},\bar{z}^{\prime}_{1}]=\bar{x}_{2,1}\bar{z}_{2}\,,\;\;\text{whence}\;\bar{x}_{2,1}\in G_{3}\,;

[\bar{y}_{1,3},\bar{z}^{\prime}_{3}]=\bar{x}_{1,3}\bar{z}_{1}\,,\;\;\text{whence}\;\bar{x}_{1,3}\in G_{3}\,;

[\bar{x}_{3,1}\bar{z}_{3},\bar{x}_{1,2}]=[\bar{x}_{3,1},\bar{x}_{1,2}]=\bar{x}_{3,2}\,,\;\;\text{whence}\;\bar{x}_{3,2}\in G_{3}\,;

[\bar{x}_{3,2},\bar{x}_{2,1}]=\bar{x}_{3,1}\,,\;\;\text{whence}\;\bar{x}_{3,1}\in G_{3}\,.

Since
\bar{x}_{3,1}
and
\bar{x}_{3,1}\bar{z}_{3}
belong to
G_{3}
, so does
\bar{z}_{3}
.
Now we know that all
\bar{y}_{i,j}
,
\bar{z}_{i}
and
\bar{z}^{\prime}_{i}
with
i,j\in\{1,2,3\}
and
i\neq j
belong to
G_{3}
.
As follows from a remark in Section
2.2
, these elements generate the whole group
\operatorname{Sp}_{6}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})
.
∎

We resume Part (b) of the proof of Theorem
6.1
.
Assume that the assertion above holds for
n\geq 3
and let us prove it for
n+1
.

Let
G_{n+1}
be the subgroup of
\operatorname{Sp}_{2n+2}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})
generated by
E_{n+1}\cup\{\bar{z}_{2}\}
.
By the induction hypothesis, since
E_{n}
is a subset of
E_{n+1}
,
the group
G_{n+1}
contains
\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})
, viewed as the group of matrices in
\operatorname{Sp}_{2n+2}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})

with entries equal to
0
for the indices
(i,n+1)
and
(n+1,i)
with
i\neq n+1
and
for the indices
(i,2n+2)
and
(2n+2,i)
with

In particular,
G_{n+1}
contains
\bar{z}_{3}
by Lemma
6.2
.

Now consider the subgroup of

generated by

\{\bar{z}_{2},\bar{z}^{\prime}_{2},\bar{z}_{2}\bar{z}_{3}\bar{y}_{2,3},\bar{z}^{\prime}_{3},\bar{z}_{3}\bar{z}_{4}\bar{y}_{3,4},\bar{z}^{\prime}_{4},\ldots,\bar{z}^{\prime}_{n+1}\}\cup\{\bar{z}_{3}\}.

Since
\bar{z}_{3}\in G_{n+1}
, this subgroup is a subgroup of
G_{n+1}
.

By the induction hypothesis applied to all subscripts increased by
1
, this subgroup
is isomorphic to the symplectic group
\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})
, now viewed
as the group of matrices in
\operatorname{Sp}_{2n+2}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})

with entries equal to

for the indices
(i,1)
and
(1,i)
with
i\neq 1

and for the indices
(i,n+2)
and
(n+2,i)
with

.

It follows that all generators
\bar{x}_{i,j}
,
\bar{y}_{i,j}
,
\bar{y}^{\prime}_{i,j}
,
\bar{z}_{i}
,
\bar{z}^{\prime}_{i}
of
\operatorname{Sp}_{2n+2}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})

belong to

, except possibly
\bar{x}_{1,n+1}
,
\bar{x}_{n+1,1}
,

But the latter also belong to

in view of the commutator relations

[\bar{x}_{1,n},\bar{x}_{n,n+1}]=\bar{x}_{1,n+1}\,,\quad[\bar{x}_{n+1,n},\bar{x}_{n,1}]=\bar{x}_{n+1,1}\,,

6.2.
Elements of the kernel of
\widehat{f}

Let
\widehat{f}_{2n+1}:\widehat{B}_{2n+1}\rightarrow\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})
be the restriction of
\widehat{f}
to
\widehat{B}_{2n+1}
.
Recall the restrictions
f_{2n+1}:B_{2n+1}\to\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})
and
\bar{f}_{2n+1}:B_{2n+1}\to\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{Z}})

We consider the element

(6.2)

\alpha_{0}=(\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1})^{2}(\sigma_{1}\sigma_{3}^{-1}\sigma_{0})(\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1})^{-2}(\sigma_{1}\sigma_{3}^{-1}\sigma_{0})

of the Artin group
\widehat{B}_{2n+1}

since

\widehat{f}_{2n+1}(\alpha_{0})=w_{1}^{2}y_{1,2}w_{1}^{-2}y_{1,2}=y_{1,2}^{-2}y_{1,2}=1

Note that mapping
\sigma_{0}
to
\sigma_{5}
and
\sigma_{i}
to
\sigma_{i}
for

,
we obtain an isomorphism
j
from the subgroup
\widehat{B}_{5}
of
\widehat{B}_{2n+1}

The composed morphism

Theorem 6.3
.

The kernel of the restriction of
\widehat{f}_{2n+1}
to the pure Artin group
\widehat{P}_{2n+1}
of type
\Gamma_{2n+1}

is the normal closure of
\alpha_{0}
,
[\sigma_{3},\Delta_{3}^{4}]
and
\Delta_{5}^{4}\Delta_{3}^{-16}
in
\widehat{B}_{2n+1}
.

Proof.

It follows from Proposition
5.10
that

\mathrm{Ker}(\widehat{f}_{2n+1})\cap P_{2n+1}=\mathrm{Ker}(f_{2n+1})\cap P_{2n+1}

is the normal closure
of
[\sigma_{3},\Delta_{3}^{4}]
and
\Delta_{5}^{4}\Delta_{3}^{-16}
in
B_{2n+1}
.

To determine the kernel in
\widehat{P}_{2n+1}
we apply
[
14
, Corollary 8]
(and its proof).
This result states that if
\Gamma
is a Coxeter graph and
i
a vertex of
\Gamma
,
the pure Artin group of type
\Gamma
is the semi-direct product of a normal subgroup
generated by conjugates of the squares
\sigma_{j}^{2}
with
j\in\Gamma

and of the pure Artin group of type
\Gamma\setminus\{i\}
.

We apply this to
\Gamma=\Gamma_{k}
and
i=0
. All generators
\sigma_{j}

are conjugate in
\widehat{B}_{2n+1}
; in particular their squares are conjugate to
\sigma_{0}^{2}
.
Consequently,
\widehat{P}_{2n+1}
is the semi-direct product of a normal subgroup
generated by elements conjugate to
\sigma_{0}^{2}
in
\widehat{B}_{2n+1}
and of
P_{2n+1}
.
Since
\alpha_{0}
lies in
P_{4}\sigma_{0}^{2}\subset P_{2n+1}\sigma_{0}^{2}

(see the proof of Theorem
5.8
)
and
\alpha_{0}\in\mathrm{Ker}\widehat{f}_{2n+1}
, we deduce the theorem.
∎

Remark 6.4
.

By Theorem
6.1
the morphism
\widehat{f}_{2n+1}:\widehat{B}_{2n+1}\to\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})
is surjective.
Composing it with the natural surjections
\operatorname{St}(C_{n},{\mathbb{Z}})\to\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{Z}})\to\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})
, we obtain
a surjective morphism
\widehat{B}_{2n+1}\to\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})
.
The latter epimorphism factors through
W(\Gamma_{2n+1})\rightarrow\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})
,
where
W(\Gamma_{2n+1})
is the Coxeter group associated with the graph
\Gamma_{2n+1}
,
since the image of each generator
\sigma_{i}
has order
2
for
i=0,\ldots,2n
.

Remark 6.5
.

We can say a little more about the kernel of
\widehat{f}_{2n+1}
when
n=3
.
In this case the Coxeter group
W(\Gamma_{7})
is of type
E_{7}
and its center has order
2
.
Let
w_{0}
be its non-trivial central element. Since the center of
\operatorname{Sp}_{6}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})
is trivial,
the element
w_{0}
has to be in the kernel of the above surjective morphism

W(\Gamma_{7})\rightarrow\operatorname{Sp}_{6}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})
.
Since the order of
W(\Gamma_{7})
is twice
the order of
\operatorname{Sp}_{6}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})
, this kernel is exactly
\{1,w_{0}\}
.
We deduce that any element of
\mathrm{Ker}\widehat{f}_{7}
is either in the pure
Artin group
\widehat{P}_{7}
of type
\Gamma_{7}
, or in the coset
{\mathbf{w}}_{0}\widehat{P}_{7}
, where
{\mathbf{w}}_{0}
is a fixed preimage of
w_{0}
in
\widehat{B}_{7}
.

Since we know
\mathrm{Ker}\widehat{f}_{7}\cap\widehat{P}_{7}
by Theorem
6.3
,
it remains to determine the intersection of
\mathrm{Ker}\widehat{f}_{7}
with the coset
{\mathbf{w}}_{0}\widehat{P}_{7}
.
A computation shows that the image of
{\mathbf{w}}_{0}
in
\operatorname{Sp}_{6}({\mathbb{Z}})
is trivial.
Thus
\widehat{f}_{7}({\mathbf{w}}_{0})=w_{1}^{4k}\in\operatorname{St}(C_{3},{\mathbb{Z}})
for some integer
k
.
Consequently,
{\mathbf{w}}_{0}\Delta_{3}^{-4k}
belongs to
\mathrm{Ker}\widehat{f}_{7}

and the full kernel
\mathrm{Ker}\widehat{f}_{7}:\widehat{B}_{7}\to\operatorname{St}(C_{3},{\mathbb{Z}})
is the normal closure of

\alpha_{0}
,
[\sigma_{3},\Delta_{3}^{4}]
,
\Delta_{5}^{4}\Delta_{3}^{-16}
and
{\mathbf{w}}_{0}\Delta_{3}^{-4k}
.
We have not been able to determine the exponent
k
.

Pfaff’s Method Revisited
Aritram Dhar

Abstract
. In 1797, Pfaff gave a simple proof of a F 2 3 {}_{3}F_{2} hypergeometric series summation formula which was much later reproved by Andrews in 1996. In the same paper, Andrews also proved other well-known hypergeometric identities using Pfaff’s method. In this paper, we prove a number of terminating q q -hypergeometric series-product identities using Pfaff’s method thereby providing a detailed account of its wide applicability.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{N}
be the set of natural numbers. For complex variables
a
and
q
, define the conventional
q
-shifted factorial by

\displaystyle(a;q)_{n}:=\prod\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}(1-aq^{k})

for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
(a;q)_{0}=1
. For
|q|<1
, define

\displaystyle(a;q)_{\infty}:=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}(a;q)_{n}.

We can also define
(a;q)_{n}
for all real numbers
n
by

\displaystyle(a;q)_{n}:=\dfrac{(a;q)_{\infty}}{(aq^{n};q)_{\infty}}.

The
q
-shifted factorial for negative integers can be defined by

(1.1)

\displaystyle(a;q)_{-n}:=\dfrac{1}{(aq^{-n};q)_{n}}=\dfrac{(-q/a)^{n}}{(q/a;q)_{n}}q^{\binom{n}{2}}.

Following Gasper and Rahman’s text
[
3
]
, the
{}_{r+1}F_{r}
hypergeometric series and the
{}_{r+1}\phi_{r}
unilateral basic hypergeometric series are defined, respectively, by

(1.2)

\begin{multlined}{}_{r+1}F_{r}\left[\begin{matrix}a_{0},\ldots,a_{r}\\
\lx@intercol\hfil\begin{matrix}b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}\end{matrix}\hfil\lx@intercol\end{matrix}\hskip 2.0pt;z\right]:=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(a_{0},\ldots,a_{r})_{n}}{(1,b_{1},\ldots,b_{r})_{n}}z^{n},\end{multlined}

where

\displaystyle(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k})_{n}:=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k}\prod\limits_{j=0}^{n-1}(a_{i}+j),

and

(1.3)

\begin{multlined}{}_{r+1}\phi_{r}\left[\begin{matrix}a_{0},\ldots,a_{r}\\
\lx@intercol\hfil\begin{matrix}b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}\end{matrix}\hfil\lx@intercol\end{matrix}\hskip 2.0pt;q,z\right]:=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(a_{0},\ldots,a_{r};q)_{n}}{(q,b_{1},\ldots,b_{r};q)_{n}}z^{n},\quad\lvert z\rvert<1,\end{multlined}

where

\displaystyle(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k};q)_{n}:=\prod_{i=1}^{k}(a_{i};q)_{n}.

The series (
1.3
) is called a
terminating series
if one of its numerator parameters is of the form
q^{-m}
where
m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. The series (
1.3
) is called
balanced
or
Saalschützian
if

\displaystyle b_{1}b_{2}\ldots b_{r}=qa_{0}a_{1}\ldots a_{r}.

The series (
1.3
) is said to be
well-poised
if the parameters satisfy the relations

\displaystyle qa_{0}=a_{1}b_{1}=a_{2}b_{2}=\ldots=a_{r}b_{r},

very-well-poised
if, in addition,

\displaystyle a_{1}=q\sqrt{a_{0}},\,a_{2}=-q\sqrt{a_{0}}.

In 1797, Pfaff
[
6
]
gave “the simplest proof” (as Andrews writes in
[
1
]
) of the following
{}_{3}F_{2}
hypergeometric identity,

{}_{3}F_{2}\left[\begin{matrix}-n,\quad\,\,\,\,\,a,\quad\,\,\,\,\,b\\
\lx@intercol\hfil\begin{matrix}\,\,\,\,\,c,\,\,\,1+a+b-n-c\end{matrix}\hfil\lx@intercol\end{matrix}\hskip 8.0pt;1\right]=\dfrac{(c-a,c-b)_{n}}{(c,c-a-b)_{n}}.

Pfaff’s result was ignored until Saalschütz rediscovered it in 1890. In the second part of a series of three papers (which he refers to as “the Pfaff Trilogy”), Andrews
[
1
]
then recreated the proof of Pfaff’s
{}_{3}F_{2}
sum and also proved some other hypergeometric identities using Pfaff’s method in the same paper, for instance, the terminating Kummer theorem, Bailey’s theorem, the theorems of Dougall and Lakin, to name a few.

Towards the end of his paper, Andrews
[
1
]
proved a
q
-analog of Pfaff’s original theorem which was due to Jackson
[
4
]
. He then concludes with a brief description saying Pfaff’s method is mostly effective for balanced and well-poised hypergeometric series (and
q
-series). In this paper, we give proofs of a plethora of well-known terminating
q
-hypergeometric series-product identities using Pfaff’s method.

We now present the statements of the main terminating
q
-hypergeometric identites which we prove in this paper.

2.
Proofs

In this section, we present the proofs of Theorems
1.1

2.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Consider
S_{n}(z;q)
to be the the left-hand side of (
1.4
). Then, we have

\displaystyle\quad S_{n}(z;q)-S_{n-1}(z;q)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(q^{-n};q)_{j}}{(q;q)_{j}}z^{j}-\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(q^{-n+1};q)_{j}}{(q;q)_{j}}z^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{z^{j}}{(q;q)_{j}}\left((q^{-n};q)_{j}-(q^{-n+1};q)_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=-q^{-n}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{(q^{-n+1};q)_{j-1}}{(q;q)_{j-1}}z^{j}

\displaystyle=-zq^{-n}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(q^{-n+1};q)_{j}}{(q;q)_{j}}z^{j}

\displaystyle=-zq^{-n}S_{n-1}(z;q).

Thus, we have

(2.1)

\displaystyle S_{n}(z;q)=(1-zq^{-n})S_{n-1}(z;q).

This already implies Theorem
1.1
by iteration since
S_{0}(z;q)=1
, but for the sake of completeness we continue with Pfaff’s method. Now, let
S_{n}^{\prime}(z;q)
denote the right-hand side of (
1.4
). Then, we have

\displaystyle\quad S_{n}^{\prime}(z;q)-S_{n-1}^{\prime}(z;q)

\displaystyle=(zq^{-n};q)_{n}-(zq^{-n+1};q)_{n-1}

\displaystyle=-zq^{-n}(zq^{-n+1};q)_{n-1}

\displaystyle=-zq^{-n}S_{n-1}^{\prime}(z;q).

Thus, we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle S_{n}^{\prime}(z;q)=(1-zq^{-n})S_{n-1}^{\prime}(z;q).

Now, observe that both
S_{n}(z;q)
and
S_{n}^{\prime}(z;q)
have the same initial conditions
S_{0}(z;q)=S_{0}^{\prime}(z;q)=1
and both obey the same recurrences (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) respectively. Hence,

\displaystyle S_{n}(z;q)=S_{n}^{\prime}(z;q)

and we have a proof of the
q
-binomial theorem (Theorem
1.1
) using Pfaff’s method.∎

2.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

Consider
S_{n}(a,c;q)
to be the the left-hand side of (
1.5
). Then, we have

\displaystyle\quad S_{n}(a,c;q)-S_{n-1}(a,c;q)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(a,q^{-n};q)_{j}}{(q,c;q)_{j}}q^{j}-\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(a,q^{-n+1};q)_{j}}{(q,c;q)_{j}}q^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{(a;q)_{j}q^{j}}{(q,c;q)_{j}}\left((q^{-n};q)_{j}-(q^{-n+1};q)_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=-q^{-n}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{(a;q)_{j}(q^{-n+1};q)_{j-1}}{(q;q)_{j-1}(c;q)_{j}}q^{j}

\displaystyle=-q^{-n+1}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(a;q)_{j+1}(q^{-n+1};q)_{j}}{(q;q)_{j}(c;q)_{j+1}}q^{j}

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{q^{-n+1}(1-a)}{(1-c)}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(aq,q^{-n+1};q)_{j}}{(q,cq;q)_{j}}q^{j}

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{q^{-n+1}(1-a)}{(1-c)}S_{n-1}(aq,cq;q).

Thus, we have

(2.3)

\displaystyle S_{n}(a,c;q)-S_{n-1}(a,c;q)=-\dfrac{q^{-n+1}(1-a)}{(1-c)}S_{n-1}(aq,cq;q).

Now, let
S_{n}^{\prime}(a,c;q)
denote the right-hand side of (
1.5
). Then, we have

\displaystyle\quad S_{n}^{\prime}(a,c;q)-S_{n-1}^{\prime}(a,c;q)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(c/a;q)_{n}}{(c;q)_{n}}a^{n}-\dfrac{(c/a;q)_{n-1}}{(c;q)_{n-1}}a^{n-1}

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{(1-a)(c/a;q)_{n-1}}{(c;q)_{n}}a^{n-1}

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{q^{-n+1}(1-a)(cq/aq;q)_{n-1}}{(1-c)(cq;q)_{n-1}}(aq)^{n-1}

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{q^{-n+1}(1-a)}{(1-c)}S_{n-1}^{\prime}(aq,cq;q).

Thus, we have

(2.4)

\displaystyle S_{n}^{\prime}(a,c;q)-S_{n-1}^{\prime}(a,c;q)=-\dfrac{q^{-n+1}(1-a)}{(1-c)}S_{n-1}^{\prime}(aq,cq;q).

Now, observe that both
S_{n}(a,c;q)
and
S_{n}^{\prime}(a,c;q)
have the same initial conditions
S_{0}(a,c;q)=S_{0}^{\prime}(a,c;q)=1
and both obey the same recurrences (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) respectively. Hence,

\displaystyle S_{n}(a,c;q)=S_{n}^{\prime}(a,c;q)

and we have a proof of
q
-Chu-Vandermonde sum (Theorem
1.2
) using Pfaff’s method.∎

2.3.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

Consider
S_{n}(a,b,c;q)
to be the the left-hand side of (
1.6
). Then, we have

\displaystyle\quad S_{n}(a,b,c;q)-S_{n-1}(a,b,c;q)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(a,b,q^{-n};q)_{j}}{(q,c,abq^{-n+1}/c;q)_{j}}q^{j}-\dfrac{(a,b,q^{-n+1};q)j}{(q,c,abq^{-n+2}/c;q)_{j}}q^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{(a,b;q)_{j}q^{j}}{(q,c;q)_{j}}\left(\dfrac{(q^{-n};q)_{j}}{(abq^{-n+1}/c;q)_{j}}-\dfrac{(q^{-n+1};q)_{j}}{(abq^{-n+2}/c;q)_{j}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{q^{-n}(1-abq/c)}{(1-abq^{-n+1}/c)}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{(a,b;q)_{j}(q^{-n+1};q)_{j-1}}{(q;q)_{j-1}(c,abq^{-n+2}/c;q)_{j}}q^{j}

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{q^{-n}(1-abq/c)}{(1-abq^{-n+1}/c)}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(a,b;q)_{j+1}(q^{-n+1};q)_{j}}{(q;q)_{j}(c,abq^{-n+2}/c;q)_{j+1}}q^{j+1}

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{q^{-n+1}(1-a)(1-b)(1-abq/c)}{(1-c)(1-abq^{-n+1}/c)(1-abq^{-n+2}/c)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(aq,bq,q^{-n+1};q)_{j}}{(q,cq,abq^{-n+3}/c;q)_{j}}q^{j}

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{q^{-n+1}(1-a)(1-b)(1-abq/c)}{(1-c)(1-abq^{-n+1}/c)(1-abq^{-n+2}/c)}S_{n-1}(aq,bq,cq;q).

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\quad S_{n}(a,b,c;q)-S_{n-1}(a,b,c;q)

(2.5)

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{q^{-n+1}(1-a)(1-b)(1-abq/c)}{(1-c)(1-abq^{-n+1}/c)(1-abq^{-n+2}/c)}S_{n-1}(aq,bq,cq;q).

Remark:

(
2.5
) was also proved by Andrews
[
1
, p.
22
, eq.
(7.3)
]
.

Now, let
S_{n}^{\prime}(a,b,c;q)
denote the right-hand side of (
1.6
). Then, we have

\displaystyle\quad S_{n}^{\prime}(a,b,c;q)-S_{n-1}^{\prime}(a,b,c;q)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(c/a,c/b;q)_{n}}{(c,c/ab;q)_{n}}-\dfrac{(c/a,c/b;q)_{n-1}}{(c,c/ab;q)_{n-1}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{cq^{n-1}(1-1/a)(1-1/b)(c/a,c/b;q)_{n-1}}{(c,c/ab;q)_{n}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{cq^{n-1}(1-a)(1-b)(c/abq;q)_{n-1}(cq/aq,cq/bq;q)_{n-1}}{ab(1-c)(1-c/ab)(cq/ab;q)_{n-1}(cq,c/abq;q)_{n-1}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{cq^{n-1}(1-a)(1-b)(1-c/abq)}{ab(1-c)(1-cq^{n-2}/ab)(1-cq^{n-1}/ab)}S_{n-1}^{\prime}(aq,bq,cq;q)

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{q^{-n+1}(1-a)(1-b)(1-abq/c)}{(1-c)(1-abq^{-n+1}/c)(1-abq^{-n+2}/c)}S_{n-1}^{\prime}(aq,bq,cq;q).

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\quad S_{n}^{\prime}(a,b,c;q)-S_{n-1}^{\prime}(a,b,c;q)

(2.6)

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{q^{-n+1}(1-a)(1-b)(1-abq/c)}{(1-c)(1-abq^{-n+1}/c)(1-abq^{-n+2}/c)}S_{n-1}^{\prime}(aq,bq,cq;q).

Now, observe that both
S_{n}(a,b,c;q)
and
S_{n}^{\prime}(a,b,c;q)
have the same initial conditions
S_{0}(a,b,c;q)

=

S_{0}^{\prime}(a,b,c;q)=1
and both obey the same recurrences (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) respectively. Hence,

\displaystyle S_{n}(a,b,c;q)=S_{n}^{\prime}(a,b,c;q)

and we have a proof of
q
-Pfaff-Saalschütz sum (Theorem
1.3
) using Pfaff’s method.∎

2.4.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

Consider
S_{n}(a,b;q)
to be the the left-hand side of (
1.7
). Then, we have

\displaystyle\quad S_{n}(a,b;q)-S_{n-1}(a,b;q)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(a,-q\sqrt{a},b,q^{-n};q)_{j}}{(q,-\sqrt{a},aq/b,aq^{n+1};q)_{j}}\left(\dfrac{q^{n+1}\sqrt{a}}{b}\right)^{j}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(a,-q\sqrt{a},b,q^{-n+1};q)_{j}}{(q,-\sqrt{a},aq/b,aq^{n};q)_{j}}\left(\dfrac{q^{n}\sqrt{a}}{b}\right)^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{(a,-q\sqrt{a},b;q)_{j}(q^{n}\sqrt{a}/b)^{j}}{(q,-\sqrt{a},aq/b;q)_{j}}\left(\dfrac{(q^{-n};q)_{j}q^{j}}{(aq^{n+1};q)_{j}}-\dfrac{(q^{-n+1};q)_{j}}{(aq^{n};q)_{j}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{1}{(1-aq^{n})}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{(a,-q\sqrt{a},b;q)_{j}(q^{-n+1};q)_{j-1}(1-aq^{j})}{(-\sqrt{a},aq/b;q)_{j}(q,aq^{n+1};q)_{j-1}(1-aq^{n+j})}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\left(\dfrac{q^{n}\sqrt{a}}{b}\right)^{j}

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{(1-a)}{(1-aq^{n})}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{(aq,-q\sqrt{a},b;q)_{j}(q^{-n+1};q)_{j-1}}{(q;q)_{j-1}(-\sqrt{a},aq/b,aq^{n+1};q)_{j}}\left(\dfrac{q^{n}\sqrt{a}}{b}\right)^{j}

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{(1-a)}{(1-aq^{n})}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(aq,-q\sqrt{a},b;q)_{j+1}(q^{-n+1};q)_{j}}{(q;q)_{j}(-\sqrt{a},aq/b,aq^{n+1};q)_{j+1}}\left(\dfrac{q^{n}\sqrt{a}}{b}\right)^{j+1}

Transverse Momentum-Dependent Heavy-Quark Fragmentation at Next-to-Leading Order
and Zhiquan Sun

Abstract
The transverse momentum-dependent fragmentation functions (TMD FFs) of heavy (bottom and charm) quarks, which we recently introduced, are universal building blocks that enter predictions for a large number of observables involving final-state heavy quarks or hadrons. They enable the extension of fixed-order subtraction schemes to quasi-collinear limits, and are of particular interest in their own right as probes of the nonperturbative dynamics of hadronization. In this paper we calculate all TMD FFs involving heavy quarks and the associated TMD matrix element in heavy-quark effective theory (HQET) to next-to-leading order in the strong interaction. Our results confirm the renormalization properties, large-mass, and small-mass consistency relations predicted in our earlier work. We also derive and confirm a prediction for the large- z z behavior of the heavy-quark TMD FF by extending, for the first time, the formalism of joint resummation to capture quark mass effects in heavy-quark fragmentation. Our final results in position space agree with those of a recent calculation by another group that used a highly orthogonal organization of singularities in the intermediate momentum-space steps, providing a strong independent cross check. As an immediate application, we present the complete quark mass dependence of the energy-energy correlator (EEC) in the back-to-back limit at 𝒪 ⁡ ( α s ) \mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}) .

In this context, TMD FFs are unique in the wealth of information

The main goal of the present paper is to evaluate all unpolarized heavy-quark TMD FFs
at perturbative transverse momenta to complete next-to-leading order (NLO)
in the strong coupling,
with the aim of improving the baseline perturbative precision

Importantly, the factorization paradigm ensures

In ref.

We stress that isolating this singular behavior at fixed order

but instead is of immediate interest to extend fixed-order subtraction

2
Theoretical framework

2.1
Heavy-quark TMD FFs

We study the fragmentation of a heavy quark
Q
with pole mass
m\gg\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}

into an unpolarized heavy hadron
H
and additional fragmentation products
X
.
We work in QCD with
n_{f}=n_{\ell}+1
flavors,
where
n_{\ell}
is the number of massless quark flavors.
We work in the “hadron frame” for fragmentation
[
33
]

where
P_{H}^{\mu}=(P_{H}^{-},M_{H}^{2}/P_{H}^{-},0)
,
with
M_{H}
the hadron mass,
and
P_{H,\perp}=0
by definition.
(We refer to
Appendix
A.1
for our lightcone conventions.)

In ref.
[
1
]
we showed
that up to a factor of the total probability
\chi_{H}
for
Q
to fragment into
H
,
the distribution differential in
k_{T}=P_{X,T}\sim M_{H}

and the lightcone momentum fraction
z_{H}
of the original quark retained by
H

is governed by a new perturbative matching coefficient,
the
partonic heavy-quark TMD FF

d_{1\,Q/Q}(z,k_{T},\mu,\zeta)
,

\displaystyle D_{1\,H/Q}(z_{H},b_{T},\mu,\zeta)=d_{1\,Q/Q}(z_{H},b_{T},\mu,\zeta)\,\chi_{H}+\mathcal{O}\Bigl(\frac{\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}}{m}\Bigr)+\mathcal{O}(\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}b_{T})\,.

(2.1)

Making all regulators explicit,
the formal definition of
d_{1\,Q/Q}

at the bare level reads

\displaystyle d_{1\,Q/Q}^{\mathrm{bare}}(z,b_{T},\epsilon,\eta,\zeta/\nu^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2z^{1-2\epsilon}N_{c}}\int\!\frac{\mathrm{d}b^{+}}{4\pi}\,e^{\mathrm{i}b^{+}(p^{-}/z)/2}

(2.2)

\displaystyle\quad\times\operatorname{Tr}\SumInt_{X}\operatorname{tr}\Bigl[\frac{\not{{\bar{n}}}}{2}\,\big\langle 0\big\lvert W_{\eta}^{\dagger}(b)\,\psi_{Q}(b)\big\rvert QX\big\rangle\big\langle QX\big\lvert\bar{\psi}_{Q}(0)\,W_{\eta}(0)\big\rvert 0\big\rangle\Bigr]\,,

where we work in
d=4-2\epsilon
spacetime dimensions,

N_{c}
is the number of colors,

\operatorname{Tr}
(
\operatorname{tr}
) indicates a trace over color (spin),
and
\SumInt_{X}
indicates a sum over all possible partonic final states
combined with an integral over their phase space.
The fields in the first matrix element are evaluated
at a spacetime position
b\equiv(0,b^{+},b_{\perp})

with
b_{\perp}
Fourier conjugate to
k_{\perp}
,
and
b_{T}^{2}\equiv-b_{\perp}^{2}
and
k_{T}^{2}=-k_{\perp}^{2}
, respectively.
The heavy quark in the external state carries momentum

p^{\mu}=(p^{-},m^{2}/p^{-},0)
,
i.e., the above definition is equal to the hadron-level definition
of the heavy-quark TMD FF
D_{1\,H/Q}
,
but with
H
replaced by
Q
itself in the external state
and restricting to partonic final states
X

[
1
]
.
The definition in eq. (
2.2
) is given
in terms of the heavy-quark field renormalized on shell,

\displaystyle\psi^{\mathrm{bare}}_{Q}(x)=Z_{\psi,\mathrm{OS}}^{1/2}(m,\mu,\epsilon)\,\psi_{Q}(x)\,,

(2.11)

and in terms of Wilson lines
W_{\eta}(x)
defined as anti-path ordered exponentials of gauge fields
extending to positive infinity along the lightcone direction
\bar{n}^{\mu}
,

\displaystyle W_{\eta}(x)=\bar{P}\Bigl[\exp\Bigl(-\mathrm{i}g\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\mathrm{d}s\,{\bar{n}}\cdot A(x+{\bar{n}}s)\Bigr)\Bigr]_{\eta}\,.

(2.12)

The subscript
\eta
indicates the presence of an additional rapidity regulator.
In explicit calculations,
we will use the so-called
\eta
regulator of refs.
[
43
,
44
]

whose action on a single gluon with momentum
\ell
attached to the Wilson line
amounts to inserting a factor of
\lvert\ell^{-}/\nu\rvert^{\eta}

under phase-space or loop integrals over
\ell
,
with
\nu
a dimensionful rapidity scale.
The final result for the bare matrix element depends on
\zeta/\nu^{2}
,
with
\zeta\equiv(p^{-}/z)^{2}
the Collins-Soper scale.

In terms of the bare collinear matrix element in eq. (
2.2
),
the renormalized partonic heavy-quark TMD FF is given by

\displaystyle d_{1\,Q/Q}(z,b_{T},\mu,\zeta)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}Z_{\mathrm{UV}}(\mu,\zeta,\epsilon)\lim_{\eta\to 0}\Bigl[d_{1\,Q/Q}^{\mathrm{bare}}(z,b_{T},\epsilon,\eta,\zeta/\nu^{2})\,\sqrt{S}(b_{T},m,\epsilon,\eta,\nu)\Bigr]\,,

(2.13)

where
S
is the universal bare TMD soft function for the
\eta
regulator,
which cancels all poles of
\eta
and the associated
\nu
dependence,
and is independent of the heavy-quark mass
up to secondary quark mass effects starting at two loops.
By contrast, the
\overline{\text{MS}}
renormalization factor
Z_{\mathrm{UV}}

for quark TMDs is independent of the mass to all orders
by RG consistency with the hard matching coefficient
at the hard scattering energy
Q\sim p^{-}/z\gg m\sim k_{T}\sim 1/b_{T}
.
While soft subtractions (known as zero bins in the SCET literature)
generally need to be accounted for in both virtual and real collinear diagrams
when computing the bare collinear matrix element itself,
we have explicitly verified that
they lead to scaleless integrals for our choice of regulator,
also in the presence of the quark mass.

2.2
Nonvalence contributions

There are two further ways in which heavy quarks can participate
in the TMD fragmentation process for
m\sim k_{T}\sim 1/b_{T}\gg\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}
;
these were sketched in ref.
[
1
]
, but we spell them out here explicitly.
In one case, the resolved final state is a heavy hadron
H
,
while the parent parton
i=g,q,\bar{q}
is light,

\displaystyle D_{1\,H/i}(z_{H},b_{T},\mu,\zeta)=d_{1\,Q/i}(z_{H},b_{T},\mu,\zeta)\,\chi_{H}+\mathcal{O}(\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}})\,.

(2.14)

Here
\chi_{H}
is again the universal inclusive fragmentation probability for
Q\to H
,
while the bare matching coefficient
d_{1\,Q/i}
is obtained
from eqs. (
2.2
) and (
2.13
)
after replacing the heavy-quark fields in the bare correlator
by suitable unpolarized combinations of gluon or light-quark fields.

In the other case, the resolved final-state hadron
h
is light.
In this case the TMD FF
D_{1\,h/i}
for
i=g,q,\bar{Q},Q,\bar{Q}

has to be matched onto collinear fragmentation functions
D_{h/j}

at the scale
\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}
where all degrees of freedom
j=g,q,\bar{q}
are light,

\displaystyle D_{1\,h/i}(z_{H},b_{T},\mu,\zeta)=\frac{1}{z_{H}^{2}}\sum_{j}\int\!\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{z}\,\mathcal{J}_{j/i}(z,b_{T},m,\mu,\zeta)\,D_{h/j}\Bigl(\frac{z_{H}}{z},\mu\Bigr)+\mathcal{O}(\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}})\,.

(2.15)

This has the standard form of matching TMD FFs onto collinear FFs,
but note that we include the mass as an additional third argument of the matching coefficient
to make explicit that even when
i
is light, the heavy quark
may in general contribute to the matching at
\mu\sim m\sim k_{T}

through closed loops.

2.3
bHQET fragmentation factors and the large-mass limit

In ref.
[
1
]
, we showed that for transverse momenta
k_{T}\ll m
,
the TMD dynamics are governed by new (and in general nonperturbative)
matrix elements defined in boosted HQET
[
45
,
46
]

that we dubbed TMD fragmentation factors.
Specifically, for
\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}\lesssim k_{T}\ll m
and counting

\displaystyle 1-z_{H}\sim 1\,,

(2.16)

which amounts to integrating over wide bins in
z_{H}

or taking low
z_{H}
moments,
the TMD FF for producing a heavy hadron
H
off a heavy quark
Q

factorizes as
[
1
]

\displaystyle D_{1\,H/Q}(z_{H},b_{T},\mu,\zeta)=\delta(1-z_{H})\,C_{m}(m,\mu,{\zeta})\,\chi_{1,H}\Bigl(b_{T},\mu,{\frac{\sqrt{\zeta}}{m}}\Bigr)+\mathcal{O}\Bigl(\frac{1}{m}\Bigr)\,,

(2.17)

where
C_{m}(m,\mu,\zeta)
is generated by separately
matching collinear and soft modes at the scale
\mu\sim m
onto HQET
and QCD with
n_{\ell}
flavors, respectively
[
47
]
;
explicit expressions for
C_{m}
and other perturbative ingredients
to the order required for our perturbative checks
are given in
appendix
B
.
We note that eq. (
2.16
) may appear counterintuitive,
given that the prediction of HQET in eq. (
2.17
)
– after assuming this counting – comes out proportional to
\delta(1-z_{H})
.
The proper way to understand eq. (
2.16
) in this context
is that it may be satisfied not just point by point in
z_{H}

far from the endpoint (where HQET predicts that the heavy-quark TMD FF is suppressed),
but in particular is also satisfied
when integrating over a wide bin in
z_{H}
from
1
down to some bin boundary
z_{H}^{\mathrm{cut}}

with
1-z_{H}^{\mathrm{cut}}\sim 1
,
or when taking Mellin moments
z_{H}^{N}
with
N\ll m/\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}
not large.
In these latter two cases, the prediction from HQET proportional to
\delta(1-z_{H})

can be easily understood as all bin integrals and Mellin moments
becoming equal and independent of
z_{H}^{\mathrm{cut}}
and
N
up to corrections of
\mathcal{O}(1/m)

[
1
]
.
We point out that the assumption in eq. (
2.16
)
in fact also underlies the “full theory” factorization for
k_{T}\sim m

in eq. (
2.1
).
Lowering
1-z_{H}\ll 1
at fixed
k_{T}\sim m
in that case
requires further resummation and – in the extreme limit –
a different factorization of nonperturbative physics,
which we present in
Section
6
.

In eq. (
2.17
), the TMD dynamics are encoded
in the unpolarized TMD fragmentation factor
\chi_{1,H}
.
For
\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}\ll k_{T}\sim 1/b_{T}
, we showed in ref.
[
1
]

that it is given by a product of the total fragmentation probability
\chi_{H}

and a perturbative matching coefficient
C_{1}
,

\displaystyle\chi_{1,H}(b_{T},\mu,{\rho})=C_{1}(b_{T},\mu,{\rho})\,\chi_{H}+\mathcal{O}(\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}^{2}b_{T}^{2})\,,

(2.18)

where
C_{1}
will be the target of our calculation in
section
4.3
.
Here we have also introduced the shorthand
\rho\equiv v^{-}=\sqrt{\zeta}/m

for the boost of the hadron; in ref.
[
1
]
we argued from consistency
with TMD factorization that
\chi_{1,H}
develops an anomalous dependence on this variable
governed by the Collins-Soper kernel,
and we will make this expectation more explicit in
section
4.1
.

To evaluate
C_{1}
, we will use that – for fully general
\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}\lesssim 1/b_{T}
–
the unpolarized TMD fragmentation factor
\chi_{1,H}
reduces to a vacuum matrix element of Wilson lines
when summed over all hadrons
H
containing
Q

[
1
]
.
Since
\sum_{H}\chi_{H}=1
and
C_{1}
in eq. (
2.18
) is independent
of the hadronic state
H
,
it is then straightforward to obtain
C_{1}

by evaluating the Wilson line correlator at perturbative
b_{T}
.

At the partonic level, eqs. (
2.17
) and (
2.18
) together imply
the following consistency condition for the partonic heavy-quark TMD FF
in the limit
m\gg k_{T}\sim 1/b_{T}

[
1
]
,

\displaystyle d_{1\,Q/Q}(z,b_{T},\mu,\zeta)=\delta(1-z)\,C_{m}(m,\mu,{\zeta})\,C_{1}\Bigl(b_{T},\mu,{\frac{\sqrt{\zeta}}{m}}\Bigr)+\mathcal{O}\Bigl(\frac{1}{b_{T}m}\Bigr)\,,

(2.19)

and in
section
5.1
we will use this relation as a check of our one-loop results.
By contrast,
nonvalence (or disfavored) partonic heavy-quark TMD FFs become power-suppressed in the heavy-quark limit,

\displaystyle i\neq Q\,:\quad d_{1\,Q/i}(z,b_{T},\mu,\zeta)=\mathcal{O}\Bigl(\frac{1}{b_{T}m}\Bigr)\,.

(2.20)

The indirect effect (
D_{1\,h/i}
,
i\neq Q,\bar{Q}
) of the heavy quark
on light-hadron production for
m\gg k_{T}

is leading in
1/m
.
It is governed by virtual contributions from collinear and soft so-called mass modes
and thus follows exactly the TMD PDF case
[
48
]
.
Finally, the direct contribution (
D_{1\,h/Q}
) to light-hadron production
becomes strongly peaked at
z_{h}\to 0
in the limit
m\gg k_{T}\gtrsim\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}
,
but for any finite value of
z_{h}
(or argument
z
of
\mathcal{J}_{j/Q}
at the partonic level)
is kinematically suppressed as
m\gg k_{T}
;
we will verify this latter behavior in
section
5.1
,
but leave a dedicated analysis of its
z_{h}\to 0
behavior
– which is known to be subtle in fragmentation
[
49
]
–
to future work.

2.4
Consistency conditions in the light-quark limit

In the case when the heavy-quark is light compared to
k_{T}
,
the matching at the scale
\mu\sim k_{T}

takes exactly the standard form
for matching TMD FFs onto twist-2 collinear FFs
[
1
]
,

\displaystyle D_{1\,\mathfrak{h}/Q}(z_{\mathfrak{h}},b_{T},\mu,\zeta)=\frac{1}{z_{\mathfrak{h}}^{2}}\sum_{i}\int\!\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{z}\,\mathcal{J}_{i/q}(z,b_{T},\mu,\zeta)\,D_{\mathfrak{h}/i}^{(n_{\ell}+1)}\Bigl(\frac{z_{\mathfrak{h}}}{z},\mu\Bigr)+\mathcal{O}(m^{2}b_{T}^{2})\,,

(2.21)

where
\mathfrak{h}=h,H
may be both a heavy or a light hadron
and
D_{\mathfrak{h}/j}^{(n_{\ell}+1)}
are collinear FFs
in a theory with
n_{\ell}
light and one massive flavor,
i.e., the mass is the highest IR scale in the twist-2 matching.
Importantly, the TMD FF matching coefficients
\mathcal{J}_{i/q}

are independent of these details in the IR
and are thus given by the universal TMD FF matching coefficients
in a theory with
n_{f}=n_{\ell}+1

light
degrees of freedom,
which are known to N
3
LO
[
50
,
51
]
.
The problem of the mass dependence is thus reduced
to the well-understood behavior of the collinear FFs,
which differs depending on whether
\mathfrak{h}
is heavy or light
[
52
,
53
]
,

\displaystyle D_{H/i}^{(n_{\ell}+1)}(z_{H},\mu)

\displaystyle=d_{Q/i}(z_{H},\mu)\,\chi_{H}+\mathcal{O}\Bigl(\frac{\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}}{m}\Bigr)\,,

\displaystyle D_{h/i}^{(n_{\ell}+1)}(z_{h},\mu)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\int\!\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{z}\,\mathcal{M}_{j/i,T}(z,m,\mu)\,D_{h/j}^{(n_{\ell})}\Bigl(\frac{z_{h}}{z},\mu\Bigr)\,.

(2.22)

Here
d_{Q/i}(z_{H},\mu)
is the partonic
collinear

For ease of calculation and to make contact
with the splitting probability in eq. (

we will perform our calculation of the bare collinear matrix element

Passing to momentum space,

\displaystyle=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\biggl\{\delta(1-z)\,\frac{1}{\pi}\delta(k_{T}^{2})\,Z_{\mathrm{UV}}^{(1)}(\mu,\zeta,\epsilon)

\displaystyle\quad+\lim_{\eta\to 0}\Bigl[d_{1\,Q/Q}^{\mathrm{bare}\,(1)}(z,k_{T},\epsilon,\eta,\zeta/\nu^{2})+\delta(1-z)\,\tfrac{1}{2}S^{(1)}(k_{T},\epsilon,\eta,\nu)\Bigr]\biggr\}\,,

where we have used
that

at tree level.
(Here and in the following
we use the same symbol for functions of

and their Fourier transforms,
as the meaning is always clear from the argument.)
Note that in our implementation of dimensional regularization in the transverse plane,
we make use of the azimuthal symmetry of the unpolarized TMD FF

55

\displaystyle d_{1\,Q/Q}^{\mathrm{bare}}(z,k_{T},\epsilon,\eta,\zeta/\nu^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2z^{1-2\epsilon}N_{c}}\int\!\frac{\mathrm{d}\Omega_{2-2\epsilon}}{2\pi}\,k_{T}^{-2\epsilon}\operatorname{Tr}\SumInt_{X}\operatorname{tr}\Bigl[\frac{\not{{\bar{n}}}}{2}\,\big\langle 0\big\lvert W_{\eta}^{\dagger}\,\psi_{Q}\big\rvert QX\big\rangle

\displaystyle\quad\times\big\langle QX\big\lvert\bigl[\delta\bigl(p^{-}/z+\mathrm{i}\partial^{-}\bigr)\,\delta^{(2-2\epsilon)}\bigl(k_{\perp}^{\mu}+\mathrm{i}\partial_{\perp}^{\mu}\bigr)\,\bar{\psi}_{Q}\,W_{\eta}\bigr]\big\rvert 0\big\rangle\Bigr]\,.

Here

in

dimensions
and the

To proceed, we first expand the Wilson line diagram (a)

\displaystyle\frac{z^{\eta}}{\bar{z}^{1+\eta}}=-\frac{\delta(\bar{z})}{\eta}+\mathcal{L}_{0}(\bar{z})+\mathcal{O}(\eta)\,.

(3.13)

(including its exact
\epsilon

as it must for it to cancel the pole in the
\eta

For our implementation of dimensional regularization, the soft function is given by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}S^{(1)}(k_{T},\epsilon,\eta,\nu)=\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{4\pi}\frac{e^{\epsilon\gamma_{E}}}{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}\Bigl(\frac{\mu}{k_{T}}\Bigr)^{2\epsilon}\frac{1}{\pi k_{T}^{2}}\Bigl[+\frac{4}{\eta}+2\ln\frac{\nu^{2}}{k_{T}^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(\eta)\Bigr]\,.

\displaystyle f(x,\epsilon)=\bigl[f(x,\epsilon)\bigr]_{+}+\delta(x)\,F(\epsilon)\,,\qquad F(\epsilon)=\int_{0}^{1}\!\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}\,f(x^{\prime},\epsilon)\,,

whereas the explicit poles are typically isolated

\displaystyle f(x,z,\epsilon)=\bigl[f(x,z,\epsilon)\bigr]_{+,+}+\delta(x)\bigl[F_{x}(z,\epsilon)\bigr]_{+}+\delta(\bar{z})\bigl[F_{z}(x,\epsilon)\bigr]_{+}+\delta(x)\,\delta(\bar{z})\,F_{xz}(\epsilon)\,,

Virtual contributions and UV renormalization

The virtual diagram (c) from

is common to any collinear matrix element involving massive fermions

So far our calculation was performed in terms of bare quark fields,

Using the standard on-shell renormalization factor

\displaystyle Z_{\psi,\mathrm{OS}}\equiv 1+Z_{\psi,\mathrm{OS}}^{(1)}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})\,,\qquad Z_{\psi,\mathrm{OS}}^{(1)}=-\,\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{4\pi}\Big(\frac{3}{\epsilon}+3\ln\frac{\mu^{2}}{m^{2}}+4\Big)

on the second line of eq. (

)

\displaystyle\lim_{\eta\to 0}\Bigl[d_{1\,Q/Q}^{\mathrm{bare}\,(1)}(z,k_{T},\epsilon,\eta,\zeta/\nu^{2})+\delta(1-z)\,\tfrac{1}{2}S^{(1)}(k_{T},\epsilon,\eta,\nu)\Bigr]

\displaystyle=d_{1\,Q/Q}^{\mathrm{bare}\,(a+s)}+d_{1\,Q/Q}^{\mathrm{bare}\,(b)}+d_{1\,Q/Q}^{\mathrm{bare}\,(c)}+\delta(1-z)\frac{1}{\pi}\delta(k_{T}^{2})Z_{\psi}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{4\pi}\frac{1}{\pi z^{2}}\,\biggl\{\delta(\bar{z})\delta(k_{T}^{2})\Bigl(\frac{2}{\epsilon^{2}}+\frac{3}{\epsilon}-\frac{2}{\epsilon}\ln\frac{\zeta}{\mu^{2}}\Bigr)+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{0})\biggr\}\,,

(3.30)

and we continue to only quote the poles for brevity.
The
\overline{\text{MS}}
UV renormalization factor for quark TMD PDFs and FFs is likewise well known
and independent of the mass
[
58
]
,

\displaystyle Z_{\mathrm{UV}}^{(1)}(\mu,\zeta,\epsilon)=-\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{4\pi}\biggl\{\frac{2}{\epsilon^{2}}+\frac{3}{\epsilon}-\frac{2}{\epsilon}\ln\frac{\zeta}{\mu^{2}}\biggr\}\,.

(3.31)

It precisely cancels the poles from eq. (
3.30

when inserting both into eq. (

We thus confirm by an explicit one-loop calculation
that the TMD FF for heavy quarks obeys standard TMD evolution equations,
with secondary mass effects in the Collins-Soper kernel
at higher loop orders understood
[
48

Taking the

limit, we obtain our final result
for the

correction to the renormalized partonic heavy-quark TMD FF:

\displaystyle d_{1\,Q/Q}^{(1)}(z,k_{T},\mu,\zeta)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{4\pi}\frac{1}{\pi z^{2}}\,\biggl\{\delta(\bar{z})\Bigl[2\ln\frac{\zeta}{\mu^{2}}\mathcal{L}_{0}(k_{T}^{2},\mu^{2})+2\ln\frac{\mu^{2}}{m^{2}}\mathcal{L}_{0}(k_{T}^{2},m^{2})-\ln^{2}\frac{\mu^{2}}{m^{2}}\delta(k_{T}^{2})+3\ln\frac{\mu^{2}}{m^{2}}\delta(k_{T}^{2})\Bigr]

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{m^{2}}\biggl[\frac{2xz^{4}(1+z^{2})+2z^{2}\bar{z}^{4}}{\bar{z}(xz^{2}+\bar{z}^{2})^{2}}\biggr]_{+,+}+\delta(k_{T}^{2})\biggl[\frac{2(1+z^{2})}{\bar{z}}\ln\Bigl(1+\frac{z^{2}}{\bar{z}^{2}}\Bigr)-\frac{4z^{3}}{\bar{z}(1-2\bar{z}z)}\biggr]_{+}

\displaystyle\qquad+\delta(\bar{z})\,\frac{1}{m^{2}}\biggl[-\frac{2+3x+4x^{2}+3x^{3}+\pi\sqrt{x}(2+7x+x^{2})+x(1+7x+2x^{2})\ln x}{x(1+x)^{3}}\biggr]_{+}

\displaystyle\qquad+\delta(\bar{z})\,\delta(k_{T}^{2})\Bigl(5-\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{2\pi^{2}}{3}\Bigr)\biggr\}\,.

(3.32)

Here we still use the dimensionless variable
x\equiv k_{T}^{2}/m^{2}
.
To simplify the result we have combined terms under
+,+
and
+
brackets
as much as possible, exploiting their linearity,
reexpressed plus distributions
\mathcal{L}_{n}(k_{T}^{2},\mu^{2})

in terms of
\mathcal{L}_{n}(k_{T}^{2},m^{2})
and logarithms of
\mu^{2}/m^{2}
,
and used that products of one-dimensional plus distributions
can be written as (trivial) two-dimensional ones, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{0}(\bar{z})\,\mathcal{L}_{0}(k_{T}^{2},m^{2})=\mathcal{L}_{0}(\bar{z})\,\frac{1}{m^{2}}\mathcal{L}_{0}(k_{T}^{2}/m^{2})=\frac{1}{m^{2}}\Bigl[\frac{1}{x}\frac{1}{\bar{z}}\Bigr]_{+,+}\,.

(3.33)

Eq. (
3.32
) is the main result of this section.
Up to the terms on the first line of the right-hand side,
which are predicted by the RGE and vanish for
\zeta=\mu^{2}=m^{2}
,
we have cast the heavy-quark TMD FF precisely in the form of eq. (
3.18
),
i.e., as a two-dimensional plus distribution
with nontrivial functions of
z
and
k_{T}

living on the respective boundaries
and an overall boundary contribution at
z=1
and
k_{T}=0
.
This structure was already illustrated in
figure
1
,
where the partial integrals over one of the variables

Of course, the total contribution in the bulk at

and
k_{T}>0

which can simply be read off from the content of the

brackets
accounting for the prefactor of

,
is still equal to the simple result we found in eq. (

)
and thus to the quasi-collinear splitting probability in eq. (

require the cumulative distribution in
k_{T}
integrated
over transverse momenta
\vec{k}_{T}
with
\lvert\vec{k}_{T}\rvert\leq{k_{T}^{\mathrm{cut}}}
as an input,

\displaystyle d_{1\,Q/Q}(z,{k_{T}^{\mathrm{cut}}},\mu,\zeta)\equiv\pi\!\int^{({k_{T}^{\mathrm{cut}}})^{2}}\!\mathrm{d}(k_{T}^{2})\,d_{1\,Q/Q}(z,k_{T},\mu,\zeta)

(3.34)

often simply referred to as the cumulant.
As for the Fourier transform we will indicate the cumulative distribution
simply through its argument
{k_{T}^{\mathrm{cut}}}
.

The organization of our final result in eq. (
3.32
)
in terms of plus distributions defined on rectangular domains in
(z,k_{T}^{2})

makes it very straightforward to compute the cumulative distribution
because the cumulant constitutes a special case of eq. (
3.20
) for a unit test function.
Specifically, for plus-bracketed functions
f

of either a single variable
x=k_{T}^{2}/m^{2}

or of both
x
and
z
,
we have the following integrals up to
x_{\mathrm{cut}}\equiv({k_{T}^{\mathrm{cut}}}/m)^{2}
:

\displaystyle\int^{x_{\mathrm{cut}}}\!\mathrm{d}x\,\bigl[f(x,z)\bigr]_{+,+}

\displaystyle=\biggl[\int_{1}^{x_{\mathrm{cut}}}\!f(x,z)\biggr]_{+}\,,

\displaystyle\int^{x_{\mathrm{cut}}}\!\mathrm{d}x\,\bigl[f(x)\bigr]_{+}

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{x_{\mathrm{cut}}}\!f(x)\,,

(3.35)

where the remaining one-dimensional plus bracket on the right-hand side of the first line
refers to the
z
dependence of the integral inside it,
while the latter’s
x_{\mathrm{cut}}
dependence is function valued.

The integrals in eq. (
3.35
)
are straightforward to evaluate
for the respective terms in eq. (
3.32
).
For purposes of our presentation, we find it convenient
to further decompose the
z
plus distribution arising
from the cumulant of the
[\dots]_{+,+}
term as

\displaystyle\bigl[s(x_{\mathrm{cut}},z)+r(x_{\mathrm{cut}},z)\bigr]_{+}=\bigl[s(x_{\mathrm{cut}},z)\bigr]+r(x_{\mathrm{cut}},z)-\delta(\bar{z})\int_{0}^{1}\!\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}\,r(x_{\mathrm{cut}},z^{\prime})\,,

(3.36)

where
s(x_{\mathrm{cut}},z)
is defined as the leading term
under the brackets for
\bar{z}\ll x_{\mathrm{cut}}\sim 1
,
with homogeneous
\sim 1/\bar{z}
scaling,
while
r(x_{\mathrm{cut}},z)
is defined as the remainder
that is at most logarithmically divergent as
z\to 1
.
Notably, we find that in this operation, the
x_{\mathrm{cut}}
dependence of
s(z)

is purely logarithmic, while the power-like
x_{\mathrm{cut}}
dependence
of the integrated
r(x_{\mathrm{cut}},z^{\prime})
multiplying
\delta(\bar{z})

precisely compensates that of the complicated cumulant integral
of the one-dimensional plus distribution in
x
in eq. (
3.32
).

Collecting terms, our final result for the NLO correction
to the renormalized heavy-quark TMD FF
in cumulant space reads

\displaystyle d_{1\,Q/Q}^{\,(1)}(z,{k_{T}^{\mathrm{cut}}},\mu,\zeta)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{4\pi}\frac{1}{z^{2}}\bigg\{\delta(1-z)\bigg[-4\ln\frac{\mu}{{k_{T}^{\mathrm{cut}}}}\ln\frac{\zeta}{m^{2}}+4\ln^{2}\frac{\mu}{m}+6\ln\frac{\mu}{m}-4\ln^{2}\frac{{k_{T}^{\mathrm{cut}}}}{m}+4-\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad-4\Bigl(1-2\ln\frac{{k_{T}^{\mathrm{cut}}}}{m}\Bigr)\,\mathcal{L}_{0}(1-z)-8\mathcal{L}_{1}(1-z)+\mathcal{R}\Bigl[z,\Bigl(\frac{{k_{T}^{\mathrm{cut}}}}{m}\Bigr)^{\!2\,}\Bigr]\bigg\}\,,

(3.37)

where we defined

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(z,x_{\mathrm{cut}})\equiv

\displaystyle\,\,\frac{4-4z(1-x_{\mathrm{cut}}z)}{\bar{z}^{2}+x_{\mathrm{cut}}z^{2}}+4\ln\frac{\bar{z}^{2}}{x_{\mathrm{cut}}}+\frac{1}{\bar{z}}\Bigl[2(1+z^{2})\ln\Bigl(1+\frac{x_{\mathrm{cut}}z^{2}}{\bar{z}^{2}}\Bigr)-4z\ln\frac{x_{\mathrm{cut}}}{\bar{z}^{2}}\Bigr]\,.

(3.38)

We note that
\mathcal{R}(z,x_{\mathrm{cut}})=\mathcal{O}(\bar{z}^{0})
for
z\to 1
and also
\mathcal{R}(z,x_{\mathrm{cut}})=\mathcal{O}(z^{0})

for
z\to 0
, so it is integrable in all limits.
For later reference we note its total integral,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\!\mathrm{d}z\,\mathcal{R}(z,x_{\mathrm{cut}})

\displaystyle=-4\mathrm{Li}_{2}\Bigl(1+\frac{1}{\mathrm{i}\sqrt{x_{\mathrm{cut}}}}\Bigr)-4\mathrm{Li}_{2}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{\mathrm{i}\sqrt{x_{\mathrm{cut}}}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{3x_{\mathrm{cut}}^{2}+3x_{\mathrm{cut}}+2}{(1+x_{\mathrm{cut}})^{2}}\ln x_{\mathrm{cut}}+\frac{2x_{\mathrm{cut}}(x_{\mathrm{cut}}-\pi\sqrt{x_{\mathrm{cut}}}+1)}{(1+x_{\mathrm{cut}})^{2}}\,.

(3.39)

To check eq. (
3.37
), we have performed an independent calculation
of the bare collinear correlator in dimensional regularization
and renormalized it directly in cumulant space,
This approach
involves more complicated hypergeometric functions of combinations of
x_{\mathrm{cut}}
and
z

in intermediate steps;
in particular, the challenge in this case is that their expansion in
\epsilon
produces
terms that are singular as
z\to 1
, even after subtracting terms
that scale as
1/\bar{z}^{1+\epsilon}
for general
\epsilon
.
This means that the full hypergeometric result in dimensional regularization
needs to be treated as in eq. (
3.17
) before expanding in
\epsilon
.
The final result is in full agreement with eq. (
3.37
).

Eq. (
3.37
) has the – at first sight – remarkable property
that the dependence of the coefficient of
\delta(1-z)
on
{k_{T}^{\mathrm{cut}}}/m

is purely logarithmic, while a power-like dependence on
{k_{T}^{\mathrm{cut}}}/m

would in general be allowed by our power counting assumption

for the heavy-quark TMD FF.
Instead, after the cancellations discussed below eq. (
3.36

the nontrivial power-like dependence on
{k_{T}^{\mathrm{cut}}}/m

arises solely in the
\mathcal{R}(z,x_{\mathrm{cut}})
term, which is regular as
z\to 1
.
In
section
6
we will explain this fact from the perspective
of joint threshold and
k_{T}
factorization,
and give all-order expressions for the
z\to 1
limit of

at fixed
k_{T}/m\sim b_{T}m\sim 1
.

3.4
Results in position space

We now move on to position-space results in terms of the
\vec{b}_{T}

variable Fourier-conjugate to
\vec{k}_{T}
,
which are the key input for producing resummed predictions
from solving multiplicative
b_{T}
-space RGEs
and Fourier-transforming the final result.
As is standard, the two-dimensional Fourier transform
of the azimuthally symmetric renormalized TMD FF at hand
is simply given by a Fourier-Bessel (or Hankel) transform of zeroth order,

\displaystyle d_{1\,Q/Q}(z,b_{T},\mu,\zeta)=2\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\mathrm{d}k_{T}\,k_{T}\,J_{0}(b_{T}k_{T})\,d_{1\,Q/Q}(z,k_{T},\mu,\zeta)\,,

(3.40)

where
J_{n}
is the
n
th-order Bessel function of the first kind.
It is convenient to integrate the above relation by parts,

\displaystyle d_{1\,Q/Q}(z,b_{T},\mu,\zeta)=b_{T}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\mathrm{d}{k_{T}^{\mathrm{cut}}}\,J_{1}(b_{T}{k_{T}^{\mathrm{cut}}})\,d_{1\,Q/Q}(z,{k_{T}^{\mathrm{cut}}},\mu,\zeta)\,.

(3.41)

This lets us compute the Fourier-space result directly
from the cumulant in eq. (
3.37
) obtained in the previous section,
which already had function-valued (and simpler)
{k_{T}^{\mathrm{cut}}}
dependence,
cf. the more complicated original momentum-space result in eq. (
3.32
).

Evaluating the relevant Hankel transforms,
our result for the NLO correction to the renormalized heavy-quark TMD FF
in position space reads

\displaystyle d_{1\,Q/Q}^{\,(1)}(z,b_{T},\mu,\zeta)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{4\pi}\frac{1}{z^{2}}\bigg\{\delta(1-z)\bigg[-2L_{b}\ln\frac{\zeta}{m^{2}}+4\ln^{2}\frac{\mu}{m}+6\ln\frac{\mu}{m}-L_{y}^{2}+4-\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-4(1+L_{y})\,\mathcal{L}_{0}(1-z)-8\mathcal{L}_{1}(1-z)+\tilde{\mathcal{R}}(z,b_{T}m)\bigg\}\,,

(3.42)

where we have used the shorthands

\displaystyle L_{b}

\displaystyle\equiv 2\ln\frac{b_{T}\mu}{2e^{-\gamma_{E}}}\,,\qquad y\equiv b_{T}m\,,\quad L_{y}\equiv 2\ln\frac{y}{2e^{-\gamma_{E}}}\,,

(3.43)

and the regular term
\tilde{\mathcal{R}}(y)
is again integrable for
z\to 0,1

and given by

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{R}}(z,y)

\displaystyle\equiv y\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\mathrm{d}t\,J_{1}(ty)\,\mathcal{R}(z,t^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{\bar{z}}\Bigl[1+L_{y}+(1+z^{2})K_{0}\Bigl(\frac{y\bar{z}}{z}\Bigr)-y\bar{z}K_{1}\Bigl(\frac{y\bar{z}}{z}\Bigr)+2\ln\bar{z}\Bigr]\,.

(3.44)

While we were not able to find a closed-form expression
for the total
z
integral of
\tilde{\mathcal{R}}
,
it can readily be obtained numerically
or analytically in asymptotic limits
through the Hankel transform of eq. (
3.39
).

bHQET TMD fragmentation factors at NLO

4.1
All-order renormalization properties

In ref.
[
1
]
we showed that up to a normalization factor
N_{H}

involving Clebsch-Gordan coefficients,
the unpolarized bHQET TMD fragmentation factor
\chi_{1,H}

in eq. (
2.17
)
for a heavy hadron state
H
is given
by the trace of the spin density matrix
\rho_{\ell}

encoding the dynamics of the light constituents
\ell
of the hadron,

\displaystyle\chi_{1,H}(b_{T},\mu,\rho)=N_{H}\sum_{h_{\ell}}\rho_{\ell,h_{\ell}h_{\ell}}(b_{\perp},\mu,\rho)\,.

(4.1)

Here we use the shorthand
\rho\equiv\bar{n}\cdot v

as introduced below eq. (
2.18
),
which can be interpreted as the total boost of the fragmenting heavy-quark system
with velocity
v
in the frame
where the Wilson line direction
\bar{n}
has unit spatial component.
A priori, the matrix element is invariant under boosts
– or reparametrization invariance type-III transformations (RPI-III) in SCET
[
61
,
62
]
–
that would take
\bar{n}\to\alpha\,\bar{n}
for an arbitrary factor
\alpha
,
but similarly to the case of relativistic TMD FFs,
the presence of rapidity divergences breaks this invariance
such that
\chi_{1,H}
and
\rho_{\ell}
develop an anomalous dependence on
\rho
.
In this subsection, we make the discussion of this point in ref.
[
1
]

fully explicit, allowing us to cross check our predictions
for the renormalization properties at one loop
in
section
4.3
.

Spelling out all regulators and its open light helicity indices
h_{\ell},h_{\ell}^{\prime}
,
the renormalized light-spin density matrix is defined as

\displaystyle\rho_{\ell,h_{\ell}h_{\ell}^{\prime}}(b_{\perp},\mu,\rho)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}Z_{\chi_{1}}^{-1}(\mu,\rho,\epsilon)\lim_{\eta\to 0}\Bigl[\rho_{\ell,h_{\ell}h_{\ell}^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{bare}}\Bigl(b_{\perp},\epsilon,\eta,\frac{\rho}{\nu}\Bigr)\,\sqrt{S^{(n_{\ell})}}(b_{T},\epsilon,\eta,\nu)\Bigr]\,,

(4.2)

where
S^{(n_{\ell})}
is the bare TMD soft function in the theory
where only the
n_{\ell}
light quark degrees of freedom are active.
This is the same soft function as appears in light-quark TMD PDFs or FFs
at transverse momenta below the heavy-quark threshold:
The fact that it also arises here and cancels the rapidity divergences as
\eta\to 0

in the bare light-spin density matrix
follows from consistency of the TMD factorization
for flavor-changing hard processes,
e.g. charged-current DIS
e^{-}\bar{s}\to\nu_{e}\,\bar{c}
.

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{c}}\operatorname{Tr}\SumInt_{X}\big\langle 0\big\lvert W_{\eta}^{\dagger}(b_{\perp})\,Y_{v}(b_{\perp})\big\lvert s_{\ell},h_{\ell},f_{\ell};X\big\rangle

We conclude this section by summarizing the renormalization group equations
for the renormalized

that follow from the above renormalization procedure:

\displaystyle\mu\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\mu}\rho_{\ell,h_{\ell}h_{\ell}^{\prime}}(b_{\perp},\mu,\rho)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{\chi_{1}}\bigl[\alpha_{s}(\mu),\rho\bigr]\,\rho_{\ell,h_{\ell}h_{\ell}^{\prime}}(b_{\perp},\mu,\rho)\,,

\displaystyle\rho\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\rho}\rho_{\ell,h_{\ell}h_{\ell}^{\prime}}(b_{\perp},\mu,\rho)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{\zeta}^{(n_{\ell})}(b_{T},\mu)\,\rho_{\ell,h_{\ell}h_{\ell}^{\prime}}(b_{\perp},\mu,\rho)\,,

(4.16)

where the
\mu

of the standard TMD UV anomalous dimension
\gamma_{\mu}^{q}

and the anomalous dimension of
C_{m}
,
evaluating both at
\zeta=\rho^{2}m^{2}

\displaystyle\gamma_{\chi_{1}}\bigl[\alpha_{s}(\mu),\rho\bigr]=\gamma_{\mu}^{q}(\mu,\rho^{2}m^{2})-\gamma_{C_{m}}(\mu,m^{2},\rho^{2}m^{2})=\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{4\pi}\Bigl(-8\ln\rho+4\Bigr)+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})\,.

(4.17)

We stress that when extracting the anomalous dimension at higher orders,
care must be taken to perturbatively reexpress
\alpha_{s}^{(n_{\ell}+1)}

in terms of
\alpha_{s}(\mu)\equiv\alpha_{s}^{(n_{\ell})}(\mu)
.

From the second line of eq. (
4.16
), we see that
\rho_{\ell}
develops
an anomalous dependence on the boost parameter
\rho
governed
by the Collins-Soper CS kernel
\gamma_{\zeta}^{(n_{\ell})}

of the theory where only
n_{\ell}
light quarks are active.
For perturbative

, it is given in our normalization by

\displaystyle\gamma_{\zeta}^{(n_{\ell})}(b_{T},\mu)=\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{4\pi}\,\biggl(-8\ln\frac{b_{T}\mu}{2e^{-\gamma_{E}}}\biggr)+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})+\mathcal{O}(\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}^{2}b_{T}^{2})\,.

(4.18)

A natural boundary condition for
\rho_{\ell}
, where it is free of large rapidity logarithms,
is given by
\rho\sim 1
, i.e., the rest frame of the heavy quark.
By contrast, in physical cross sections, the boost is fixed to
\rho=Q/m\gg 1
,
i.e., the lab frame in the
e^{+}e^{-}
case, or the Breit frame for SIDIS.
The
\rho
evolution between those scales resums
the associated large rapidity logarithms.
As in the relativistic case, the two-dimensional evolution equations satisfy
an integrability condition,

\displaystyle\rho\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\rho}\gamma_{\chi_{1}}\bigl[\alpha_{s}(\mu),\rho\bigr]=\mu\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mu}\gamma_{\zeta}^{(n_{\ell})}(\mu,b_{T})=-2\Gamma_{\mathrm{cusp}}\bigl[\alpha_{s}(\mu)\bigr]

(4.19)

which involves the lightlike-lightlike cusp anomalous dimension

\Gamma_{\mathrm{cusp}}(\alpha_{s})=\tfrac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\,4C_{F}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})

and can readily be verified at one loop.

4.2
Sum over hadronic states

An important property of the unpolarized bHQET fragmentation factors
is that their description greatly simplifies
when summing over all hadrons
H
containing the heavy quark,

\displaystyle\chi_{1}(b_{T},\mu,\rho)=\sum_{H}\chi_{1,H}(b_{T},\mu,\rho)=\sum_{\ell}\sum_{h_{\ell}}\rho_{\ell}(b_{\perp},\mu,\rho)\,.

(4.20)

As indicated, this sum in turn is equal to the sum of traces of the light-spin density matrices
for all the possible light states
\ell
.
Unlike the case of inclusive fragmentation,
\chi_{1}
does not simply
evaluate to unity by probability conservation.
However,
as
in the inclusive case, the complete sum over states
can be performed also here, leaving behind a simple vacuum
Wilson line correlator
[
1
]
,

\displaystyle\chi_{1}^{\mathrm{bare}}(b_{T},\epsilon,\eta,\bar{n}\cdot v)\equiv\frac{1}{N_{c}}\operatorname{Tr}\big\langle 0\big\lvert W_{\eta}^{\dagger}(b_{\perp})\,Y_{v}(b_{\perp})\,Y^{\dagger}_{v}(0)\,W_{\eta}(0)\big\rvert 0\big\rangle\,.

(4.21)

Its renormalization proceeds exactly as for the whole
\rho_{\ell}
in eq. (
4.2
),

\displaystyle\chi_{1}(b_{T},\mu,\rho)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}Z_{\chi_{1}}^{-1}(\mu,\rho,\epsilon)\lim_{\eta\to 0}\Bigl[\chi_{1}^{\mathrm{bare}}\Bigl(b_{T},\epsilon,\eta,\frac{\rho}{\nu}\Bigr)\,\sqrt{S^{(n_{\ell})}}(b_{T},\epsilon,\eta,\nu)\Bigr]\,.

(4.22)

While eqs. (
4.21
) and (
4.22
) are valid nonperturbatively
and likely exhibit interesting properties at long distances
b_{T}\sim 1/\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}
,
an immediate application of their simple operator structure is
to evaluate the correlator perturbatively at
b_{T}\ll 1/\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}
,
which we do in the next section.
As a byproduct of this, we may then read off the universal
matching coefficient
C_{1}
in eq. (
2.18
)
by summing eq. (
2.18
) over all hadrons
H

such that
\sum\chi_{H}=1
drops out,

\displaystyle\chi_{1}(b_{T},\mu,\rho)=C_{1}(b_{T},\mu,\rho)+\mathcal{O}(\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}^{2}b_{T}^{2})\,.

(4.23)

Of course, one can also determine
C_{1}
by a standard matching calculation,
e.g. using non-decoupled heavy-quark fields
h_{v}
in the operators
and heavy-quark external states in both the theory at the scale
\mu\sim 1/b_{T}

and at the low scale
\mu\sim\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}
to read off the matching coefficient,
and the procedure (in pure dimensional regularization) involves equivalent diagrams
and leads to the same result.

7
Application: EEC with heavy quarks in the back-to-back limit

7.1
Mass-dependent EEC jet functions

In this section we use our results to produce explicit results
for the effect of heavy quarks
on the energy-energy correlator (EEC) observable
[
76
]

as measured in
e^{+}e^{-}
collisions,

\displaystyle\mathrm{EEC}(\chi)\equiv\frac{1}{\sigma_{\mathrm{total}}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{d\cos\chi}=\frac{1}{\sigma_{\mathrm{total}}}\sum_{a,b\,\in X}\int\!\mathrm{d}\sigma_{ee\to X}\frac{E_{a}E_{b}}{Q^{2}}\delta\bigl[\cos(\theta_{ab})-\cos(\chi)\bigr]\,,

(7.1)

where the sum runs over all pairs of hadrons
a
and
b
in the event
with energies
E_{a},E_{b}
and
Q
is the center-of-mass energy.
We are specifically interested in the so-called back-to-back limit
\chi\to\pi
,
where a parton-level factorization theorem has been proven
[
25
]

and can be directly related to TMD factorization.
This precisely complements the study of ref.
[
77
]
,
where the NLO mass effects in the opposite (collinear) limit
\chi\to 0
of the EEC were calculated,
and can serve as data for a future calculation of the heavy-quark EEC
for general angles.

To do so, it is convenient to rewrite the EEC as a differential distribution
in
z_{\chi}=\bigl[1-\cos(\theta_{ab})\bigr]/2\to 1
,
i.e., the average pairwise angle of energy-weighted hadron pairs,

\mathrm{EEC}(\chi)=(1/\sigma_{\mathrm{total}})\,\mathrm{d}\sigma/\mathrm{d}z_{\chi}
.
We are interested in the modifications of the fixed-order EEC
from the presence of heavy quarks in this limit,
counting
(1-z_{\chi})Q^{2}\sim 1/b_{T}^{2}\sim m^{2}
,
where
b_{T}
– precisely as in TMD factorization –
is the typical transverse spacetime separation
of fields in the relevant correlators.
The
z_{\chi}
-differential distribution is given
by
z_{\mathfrak{h}}
-moments of the TMD FFs for identified hadrons
[
25
]
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma_{0}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\mathrm{d}z_{\chi}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\mathcal{H}_{ee\to ij}(Q^{2},\mu)~Q^{2}\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\mathrm{d}b_{T}\,b_{T}\,J_{0}\bigl(\sqrt{1-z_{\chi}}\,b_{T}Q\bigr)

(7.2)

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}}\int\!\mathrm{d}z_{\mathfrak{h}}\,z_{\mathfrak{h}}^{3}D_{1\,\mathfrak{h}/i}(z_{\mathfrak{h}},b_{T},\mu,Q^{2})\int\!\mathrm{d}z_{\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}}\,z_{\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}}^{3}D_{1\,\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}/j}(z_{\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}},b_{T},\mu,Q^{2})

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{i,j}\mathcal{H}_{ee\to ij}(Q^{2},\mu)~Q^{2}\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\mathrm{d}b_{T}\,b_{T}\,J_{0}\bigl(\sqrt{1-z_{\chi}}\,b_{T}Q\bigr)

\displaystyle\quad\times J_{i}(b_{T},m,\mu,Q/\nu)\,J_{j}(b_{T},m,\mu,Q/\nu)\,S(b_{T},m,\mu,\nu)\,,

where the sum runs over all possible pairs of partons
i,j
,
with nonzero contributions for
i,j=q,\bar{q}
a light quark pair
or
i,j=Q,\bar{Q}
a heavy quark pair,
and it is understood that
\mathcal{H}_{ee\to gg}=0
.
Here our normalization is such that
\mathrm{d}\sigma/\mathrm{d}z_{\chi}=\sigma_{0}\,\delta(1-z_{\chi})+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s})

in the limit where the process is mediated only by virtual photons,
and the hard function
\mathcal{H}_{ee\to ij}
is given in
appendix
B
.
On the second equality we defined the EEC back-to-back jet function

J_{i}(b_{T},m,\mu,\nu)
for a – possibly massive – parent parton

and we followed the usual convention in the EEC literature
to factor off a rapidity and UV-renormalized TMD soft function

Both

and

acquire mass dependence for
1/b_{T}^{2}\sim m^{2}

from primary and secondary heavy-quark effects,
which we now classify and evaluate further.
Note that while the energy fractions entering eq. (
7.1

in general differ from the lightcone momentum fractions
z_{\mathfrak{h}}

that the TMD FFs depend on, in particular for massive particles,
the difference is power-suppressed in the back-to-back limit

To evaluate the sums over hadrons, we break each of them down
into two terms, a sum over light hadrons
\mathfrak{h}=h

and one over heavy hadrons
\mathfrak{h}=H

For light-hadron final states, the standard manipulations
of the collinear FF using its momentum sum rule apply
[
25
]

such that also in the presence of intermediate heavy quarks
and for all of

\displaystyle\sum_{h}\int\!\mathrm{d}z_{h}\,z_{h}^{3}\,D_{1\,h/i}(z_{h},b_{T},\mu,Q^{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=q,\bar{q},g}\int\!\mathrm{d}z\,z\,\mathcal{J}_{k/i}(z,b_{T},m,\mu,Q^{2})+\mathcal{O}(\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}^{2}b_{T}^{2})\,,

(7.3)

where

introduced in eq. (

Importantly, at the scale

On the other hand, for heavy-hadron final states
H

\bar{Q}

\displaystyle\sum_{H}\int\!\mathrm{d}z_{H}\,z_{H}^{3}\,D_{1\,H/i}(z_{H},b_{T},\mu,Q^{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{K=Q,\bar{Q}}\int\!\mathrm{d}z\,z^{3}\,d_{1\,K/i}(z,b_{T},\mu,Q^{2})+\mathcal{O}(\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}^{2}b_{T}^{2})\,,

which again holds for all of
i=q,Q,g

While the end result looks similar to eq. (

the moment of the perturbative coefficient arises
in a different (and much simpler) way,

from eq. (

are independent of the state

We note in passing that bound states containing multiple heavy quarks

and e.g. for a

onto NRQCD long-distance matrix elements calculated

but this contribution to the jet function

only starts at

The expressions above are particular (

and the TMD FF matching coefficients,
which we in general define as

\displaystyle d_{1\,Q/i}^{[N]}(b_{T},\mu,\zeta)

\displaystyle\equiv\int\!\mathrm{d}z\,z^{N+2}d_{1\,Q/i}(z,b_{T},\mu,\zeta)\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{k/i}^{[N]}(b_{T},m,\mu,\zeta)

\displaystyle\equiv\int\!\mathrm{d}z\,z^{N}\mathcal{J}_{k/i}(z,b_{T},m,\mu,\zeta)\,,

In summary, we have

\displaystyle J_{i}(b_{T},m,\mu,Q/\nu)\bigl[S(b_{T},m,\mu,\nu)\bigr]^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{K=Q,\bar{Q}}d_{1\,K/i}^{[1]}(b_{T},\mu,Q^{2})+\sum_{k=q,\bar{q},g}\mathcal{J}_{k/i}^{[1]}(b_{T},m,\mu,Q^{2})\,,

[

We collect the Mellin moments of our new results

7.2
Jet function consistency in the small and large-mass limits

The all-order consistency relation for the mass-dependent EEC jet functions
in the small-mass limit follows from eq. (
2.23
) and reads

\displaystyle J_{i}(b_{T},m,\mu,Q/\nu)=J_{i}(b_{T},\mu,Q/\nu)+\mathcal{O}(mb_{T})\,,

(7.7)

i.e., they simply reduce to their massless counterparts
because the partonic heavy-quark collinear FFs
and the FF flavor matching kernels preserve
the momentum sum rule.
(In other words, the measurement becomes fully inclusive
over the details of the IR also in this case, which now includes the mass.)

On the other hand, in the large-mass limit we can infer
from the discussion in
section
2.3

that the heavy-quark jet function to all orders satisfies

\displaystyle J_{Q}(b_{T},m,\mu,Q/\nu)\bigl[S(b_{T},m,\mu,\nu)\bigr]^{1/2}=C_{m}(m,\mu,Q^{2})\,C_{1}\Bigl(b_{T},\mu,\frac{Q}{m}\Bigr)\,.

(7.8)

Note that it is not power suppressed in this regime
because even at scales below the quark mass,
the heavy quark continues to radiate light modes and pick up recoil,
leading to a nontrivial angular dependence, and thus a nonzero EEC.
As for the TMD FFs, the mass effects
in the light-parton jet functions
J_{q}
and
J_{g}

for
(1-z_{\chi})Q^{2}\ll m^{2}
are of purely virtual origin
and encoded in collinear and soft mass matching coefficients
onto their counterparts in the
n_{\ell}
(purely light) theory,
see refs.
[
48
,
60
]
.

i.e., it has to involve the genuine TMD
FF
matching coefficients
onto twist-2 collinear FFs because the UV matching coefficients
are independent of the details of the IR,
that is, whether one matches onto a theory at
\mu\sim\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}
or
\mu\sim m
.

A second and final difference between the power counting assumptions
is phenomenological and regards the kinds of differential distributions
one may describe in either approach.
Our stance here is that while – again – the assumption of ref.
[
13
]

is sufficient and entirely correct to describe specific regions
of the multi-differential
(z_{H},k_{T})
distribution,
it is not the unique power-counting assumption
on
1-z_{H}
,
k_{T}
, and
m

that would yield a leading-power factorization theorem involving bHQET modes,
in particular not when different possible scalings of
\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}
are also considered.
As one example, we point to our analysis in
section
6
,
where we continued to count
m\sim k_{T}
, but still took
1-z_{H}
small,
and likewise obtained a leading-power factorization theorem,
in that case also involving a purely collinear bHQET shape function,
but nontrivial
k_{T}
-dependent correlators of lightlike Wilson lines at the scale
\mu\sim k_{T}
.
In light of this, we believe that capturing the full
(z_{H},k_{T})
dependence
will require dedicated future studies combining all of these results,
while for the time being and within the scope of exploratory theory studies
(or, necessarily less differential, initial experimental measurements)
the restriction to wide bins or low-moment integrals over
z_{H}
is reasonable.

9
Conclusions

In this paper, we calculated all heavy-quark transverse-momentum dependent
fragmentation functions (TMD FFs) to next-to-leading order (NLO)
in the strong coupling.
We provided explicit results
in transverse momentum (
k_{T}
) space
– both for the differential and the cumulative distribution –
and in the conjugate position (
b_{T}
) space,
retaining the exact dependence on
k_{T}/m
and
b_{T}m
, respectively,
with
m
the mass of the heavy quark.
Our calculations provide the last missing, key ingredient
for a complete next-to-next-to-leading logarithmic (NNLL)
description of the transverse momentum distributions
of observed heavy hadrons, capturing all quark mass effects.
Our results also enable the extension of fixed-order subtraction methods
to quasi-collinear limits involving final-state heavy quarks.

We further studied the corresponding TMD fragmentation factors
and the TMD light-spin density matrix
that arise in boosted Heavy-Quark Effective Theory (bHQET)
when the transverse momentum is small compared to the mass.
We demonstrated explicitly that they satisfy a two-dimensional set
of renormalization group equations (RGEs)
with respect to the standard virtuality scale
\mu

and a dimensionless analog
\rho=\sqrt{\zeta}/m
of the usual Collins-Soper scale
\zeta
.
Here
\rho
has an immediate physical interpretation as the boost
of the heavy hadron in the lab frame.
We then proceeded to also evaluate the unpolarized TMD fragmentation factor
at NLO, allowing us to cross check our results for the heavy-quark TMD FF
in the large-mass and – using known results from the literature –
the small-mass limit, finding full agreement.
We further showed how to extend the theory of joint threshold and
k_{T}
resummation
to fully account for quark mass effects of
\mathcal{O}(m^{n}b_{T}^{n})
,
and used these novel factorization theorems for the heavy-quark TMD FF
to obtain a third independent cross check on our NLO result in an asymptotic limit.
We also compared our final results for the exact heavy-quark TMD FF
in
b_{T}
space to a recent independent calculation
by another group. We found full agreement despite the highly orthogonal ways
in which singularities were isolated in the two intermediate momentum-space calculations,
which provides a strong independent cross check of this key universal ingredient
for heavy-quark TMD predictions.
Finally, and as a first application,
we leveraged our results to obtain the exact quark-mass dependence
of the energy-energy correlator (EEC) in
e^{+}e^{-}
collisions in the back-to-back limit,
where the associated jet functions are given by Mellin moments of our results.
As a side note, we pointed out that if the EEC
is restricted to heavy hadrons (
\mathrm{EEC}_{H}
),
substantial simplifications occur in the back-to-back limit
such that the
\mathrm{EEC}_{H}
at values below the quark mass
is given by a simple product of Wilson-line vacuum expectation values,
even extending down to nonperturbative values of the observable.

Our results strengthen the theoretical underpinnings of heavy-quark TMD FFs
and pave the way towards precision studies of their nonperturbative dynamics,
as well as for improving the description of heavy flavor differential distributions
in general-purpose Monte-Carlo programs.
We look forward to future applications of our results
in precision predictions for transverse momentum distributions
of heavy quarks and hadrons,
and to comparing them to future experimental measurements
to shed light on the three-dimensional dynamics of heavy-quark hadronization.

A.1

We decompose four-vectors

{\bar{n}}^{\mu}

A.2

One-dimensional plus distributions are defined through their action
on test functions

\displaystyle\int\!\mathrm{d}x\bigl[f(x)\bigr]_{+}\,g(x)\equiv\int\!\mathrm{d}x\,f(x)\bigl[g(x)-g(0)\,\Theta(1-x)\bigr]\,,

(A.2)

Integrals of

against the constant test function

The generalization of eq. (
A.2
) to two dimensions

) in the main text.
For logarithmic plus distributions with homogeneous power counting
\sim 1/x

and their analogs for dimensionful variables,
we further define the shorthands

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{n}(x)\equiv\Bigl[\frac{\Theta(x)\,\ln^{n}x}{x}\Bigr]_{+}\,,\quad\mathcal{L}_{n}(k,\mu)\equiv\frac{1}{\mu}\mathcal{L}_{n}\Bigl(\frac{k}{\mu}\Bigr)\,,\quad\mathcal{L}_{n}(k_{T}^{2},\mu^{2})\equiv\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}\mathcal{L}_{n}\Bigl(\frac{k_{T}^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\Bigr)\,.

(A.3)

The definition in eq. (
A.2
)
enables expansions in a regulator

as follows,

\displaystyle\frac{\Theta(x)}{x^{1+\alpha}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\delta(x)}{\alpha}+\Bigl[\frac{\Theta(x)}{x^{1+\alpha}}\Bigr]_{+}

\displaystyle\frac{\Theta(x)\,\ln x}{x^{1+\alpha}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\delta(x)}{\alpha^{2}}+\Bigl[\frac{\Theta(x)\,\ln x}{x^{1+\alpha}}\Bigr]_{+}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\delta(x)}{\alpha^{2}}+\mathcal{L}_{1}(x)+\mathcal{O}(\alpha)\,.

(A.5)

To relate plus distributions

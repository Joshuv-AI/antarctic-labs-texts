Semiclassics with ’t Hooft flux background for QCD with 2 2 -index quarks Preprint: YITP-22-45
Yuya Tanizaki, Mithat Ünsal

Abstract
We study quantum chromodynamics including the two-index symmetric or anti-symmetric quark (QCD(Sym/ASym)) on small ℝ 2 × T 2 \mathbb{R}^{2}\times T^{2} with a suitable magnetic flux. We first discuss the ’t Hooft anomaly of these theories and claim that discrete chiral symmetry should be spontaneously broken completely to satisfy the anomaly matching condition. The T 2 T^{2} compactification with the magnetic flux preserves the ’t Hooft anomaly, and the 2 2 d effective theory is constrained by the same anomaly of 4 4 d QCD(Sym/ASym). We demonstrate the spontaneous breakdown of chiral symmetry using the dilute gas of center vortices, which confirms the prediction of the ’t Hooft anomaly. We also find that each vacuum maintains the charge conjugation symmetry, and this gives affirmative support for the nonperturbative large- N N orientifold equivalence between QCD(Sym/ASym) and 𝒩 = 1 \mathcal{N}=1 supersymmetric S ​ U ​ ( N ) SU(N) Yang-Mills theory.

Introduction

In our recent work
Tanizaki:2022ngt
, we have proposed a new semiclassical formalism to study four-dimensional non-Abelian gauge theories by compactifying the theory on small
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times T^{2}
including ’t Hooft flux.
Our framework provides the weak-coupling description of confinement and reproduces many qualitative features of strongly coupled confining vacua, and thus it is reasonable to expect that its dynamics show adiabatic continuity to the strong dynamics on
\mathbb{R}^{4}
.

Technically, the ’t Hooft flux lifts the classical moduli space of gauge holonomies on
T^{2}
completely
tHooft:1979rtg
, and the
0
-form center symmetry is unbroken.
As the
T^{2}
size is much smaller than the strong scale, the center-symmetric vacuum is stable under perturbative corrections.
On the other hand, the
1
-form center symmetry is spontaneously broken at the classical level, and we need to consider the nonperturbative corrections to restore it.

The center vortex
tHooft:1977nqb
;
Cornwall:1979hz
;
Nielsen:1979xu
;
Ambjorn:1980ms
is the key player that dominates the leading nonperturbative correction.
In this
T^{2}
compactified setup with ’t Hooft flux, center vortices are realized as the BPS configuration having the fractional topological charge
Q=\pm\frac{1}{N}
, and its classical action is given by
S=\frac{S_{I}}{N}
, where
S_{I}=\frac{8\pi^{2}}{g^{2}}
is the 4d instanton action
Gonzalez-Arroyo:1998hjb
;
Montero:1999by
;
Montero:2000pb
.
The distinguishing feature of the center vortex is its nontrivial mutual statistics with Wilson loops.
We can show that the dilute-gas approximation of center vortices describes the confinement for
1
-form center symmetry, and this provides the first semiclassical computations of
4
d confining gauge theories based on center vortices.

In this paper, we generalize our earlier work to quantum chromodynamics (QCD) with two-index representation fermions, e.g. symmetric or anti-symmetric representation, and we denote them as QCD(Sym/ASym).
QCD(ASym) provides an alternative large-
N
limit of QCD because of the simple kinematic fact that fundamental and anti-symmetric representations are the same for
N=3
.
Unlike the ’t Hooft large-
N
limit
tHooft:1973alw
, quark loops are not suppressed compared with gluon loops in QCD(ASym), and this feature is similar to the large-
N
limit of QCD with
N_{f}
fundamental quarks with
N_{f}/N
fixed.
QCD(Sym/ASym) received much attention in the context of orientifold equivalence with
{\cal N}=1
supersymmetric Yang-Mills theory
Armoni:2003gp
;
Armoni:2004ub
;
Unsal:2006pj
.

We examine the nonperturbative dynamics of
N_{f}=1
QCD(Sym/ASym) by using compactification with magnetic flux.
We shall demonstrate that the discrete chiral symmetry is spontaneously broken completely, by performing the dilute gas approximation with center vortices dressed with gapless fermions.
By adding a soft mass deformation, we obtain the multi-branch structure of the ground states as in the case of confining vacua of pure Yang-Mills theory.
A remarkable feature here is that even though the theta angle is fractionalized as
\theta/N
, the dilute gas of center vortices produces the correct
N+2
(and
N-2
) vacua and multi-branched structure once a soft mass term is added.
As these theories are non-supersymmetric, there have not been powerful techniques to study QCD(Sym/ASym) directly (except with double-trace deformations on
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times S^{1}

Shifman:2008ja
), and thus our result provides a novel insight into their dynamics and the validity of nonperturbative orientifold equivalence.

Global symmetry and ’t Hooft anomaly of QCD(Sym)

We consider the

\overline{\psi}\gamma_{\mu}D_{\mu}\psi=\overline{\psi}\gamma_{\mu}\left(\partial_{\mu}+\mathsf{sym}(\mathrm{i}a_{\mu})\right)\psi.

The classical theory has the chiral symmetry,
U(1)_{\mathrm{L}}\times U(1)_{\mathrm{R}}\simeq[U(1)_{\mathrm{V}}\times U(1)_{\mathrm{A}}]/\mathbb{Z}_{2}

\psi\mapsto\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\alpha\gamma_{5}}\psi,\,\overline{\psi}\mapsto\overline{\psi}\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\alpha\gamma_{5}},

{\mathcal{D}}\overline{\psi}{\mathcal{D}}\psi\mapsto{\mathcal{D}}\overline{\psi}{\mathcal{D}}\psi\exp\left(\mathrm{i}\frac{2\alpha}{8\pi^{2}}\int\mathrm{tr}_{\mathsf{sym}}(f\wedge f)\right),

Therefore, the axial symmetry

\bigl(\mathbb{Z}_{2(N+2)}\bigr)_{\mathrm{A}}

We also need to care about the periodicity of the vector symmetry and its relation to the

2

\sum_{i=1}^{N+1}\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\int_{M}\phi_{i}\wedge(\mathrm{d}A_{2\mathrm{d}}+2B_{2\mathrm{d}})=\frac{\mathrm{i}(N+1)}{2\pi}\int_{M}\phi\wedge(\mathrm{d}A_{2\mathrm{d}}+2B_{2\mathrm{d}}).

(42)

This is the standard topological coupling for the vector-like gauge fields in the bosonized description, where the vector-like gauge field strength is made manifestly
1
-form gauge invariant.
However, this does not reproduce the second term of the anomaly correctly and we need an extra contribution.
To see where it comes from, we should note that there is the dynamical
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
gauge
a^{\prime}
with the gas of center-vortex defects:
\mathrm{d}a^{\prime}
can be nonzero and
\int_{V_{c}}\mathrm{d}a^{\prime}=\frac{2\pi}{N}
for the integration over the vortex core
V_{c}
.
This should couple to the
\theta
parameter in the following standard form,

\frac{\mathrm{i}\theta}{2\pi}\int_{M}\mathrm{d}a^{\prime},

(43)

but this does not respect the
U(1)_{\mathrm{A}}
spurious symmetry,
\theta\to\theta+2(N+2)\alpha
,
\phi\to\phi+2\alpha
, and
\mathrm{d}a^{\prime}
is not invariant under
1
-form gauge transformation.
These requirements fix the form of couplings in a unique manner:

\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\int_{M}(\theta-(N+2)\phi)\wedge(\mathrm{d}a^{\prime}+B_{2\mathrm{d}}).

(44)

Combining (
42
) and (
44
), the discrete chiral transformation,
\phi\to\phi+\frac{2\pi}{N+2}
, shifts the local counter term as

\frac{\mathrm{i}}{N+2}\int\left((N+1)(\mathrm{d}A_{2\mathrm{d}}+2B_{2\mathrm{d}})-(N+2)B_{2\mathrm{d}}\right)=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{N+2}\int\left((N+1)\mathrm{d}A_{2\mathrm{d}}+NB_{2\mathrm{d}}\right),

(45)

and this reproduces the ’t Hooft anomaly.

2.2.4
Center vortices and chiral symmetry breaking for QCD(Sym)

To go beyond the perturbation theory, we should perform the dilute gas approximation of the semiclassical configuration.
In the

From the BPS bound, the YM action is given by

The center-vortex vertex is then given by

2.3
Mass deformation of QCD(Sym) on
M\times T^{2}

So far, we have considered the massless fermion and obtained the spontaneous chiral symmetry breaking,
\left(\mathbb{Z}_{2(N+2)}\right)_{\mathrm{A}}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{SSB}}\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, using the semiclassical method.
Let us add the mass deformation to the Lagrangian,

\Delta\mathcal{L}_{m}=m\left((\overline{\psi}_{\rm L}\psi_{\rm R})+(\overline{\psi}_{\rm R}\psi_{\rm L})\right),

(54)

and assume that the mass
m
is sufficiently small.
The deformation breaks the

\mathbb{Z}_{2N+4}^{[0]}
discrete chiral symmetry softly.
After the bosonization, the mass deformation becomes

\Delta\mathcal{L}_{m}\sim-m\frac{(\Lambda L)^{3-\frac{4}{3N}}}{L^{3}}\cos(\phi).

(55)

The ground-state energy densities can be obtained by substituting the vacuum configurations for
\phi
at
m=0
.

For odd
N
, we then obtain

E_{k}(\theta)\sim-m\Lambda^{3}(\Lambda L)^{-\frac{4}{3N}}\cos\left(\frac{\theta+2\pi Nk}{N+2}\right),

(56)

with
k=0,1,\ldots,N+1
. At generic values of
\theta
, the system has the unique and gapped ground state. That is, the degeneracy between chirally broken vacua is lifted by the mass deformation.
Concretely, the ground state is given by
k=0
when
-\pi<\theta<\pi
.
At
\theta=\pi
, there are two degenerate vacua,
k=0
and
Nk=-1
. Here, we note that
Nk=-1
has the unique solution in
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{N+2}
as
\gcd(N,N+2)=1
for odd
N
.

For even
N
, there are two universes due to
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{[1]}
labelled by
\ell\in\{0,1\}
.
For the
\ell=0
universe, the multi-branch ground-state energies are given by

E^{(\ell=0)}_{k}(\theta)\sim-m\Lambda^{3}(\Lambda L)^{-\frac{4}{3N}}\cos\left(\frac{\theta+2\pi Nk}{N+2}\right),

(57)

with
k=0,1,\ldots,\frac{N}{2}
. For the
\ell=1
universe, they are given by

E^{(\ell=1)}_{k^{\prime}}(\theta)\sim-m\Lambda^{3}(\Lambda L)^{-\frac{4}{3N}}\cos\left(\frac{\theta+2\pi(Nk^{\prime}+1)}{N+2}\right),

(58)

with
k^{\prime}=0,1,\ldots,\frac{N}{2}
.
At generic values of
\theta
, the system again has the unique and gapped ground state.
As a result, the fundamental Wilson line shows the area law. The string tension
T(\theta)
of the fundamental Wilson loop is given by

T(\theta)=L^{2}\left(\min_{k^{\prime}}E^{(\ell=1)}_{k^{\prime}}(\theta)-\min_{k}E^{(\ell=0)}_{k}(\theta)\right),

(59)

and it is indeed nonzero for generic values of
\theta
.

2.4
Semiclassical study of QCD(ASym) on small
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times T^{2}
with magnetic flux

In this section, we study the dynamics of QCD(ASym) using the center-vortex effective theory.
As the discussion is almost parallel to that of QCD(Sym), we explain it very briefly.

The structure of the vector-like symmetry
G_{\mathrm{V}}
is completely identical to (
6

The ABJ anomaly explicitly breaks

to
\left(\mathbb{Z}_{2(N-2)}\right)_{\mathrm{A}}

\mathrm{tr}_{\mathsf{asym}}(T^{a}T^{b})=(N-2)\mathrm{tr}(T^{a}T^{b})

As the dimension of the anti-symmetric representation is

(N+1)
” in QCD(Sym) is also replaced by “
(N-1)
”.

To compute the mixed ’t Hooft anomaly between
G_{\mathrm{V}}
and the discrete chiral symmetry, let us calculate the index of the Dirac operator under the presence of the
G_{\mathrm{V}}
background gauge field
(A,B)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ind}(\not{D})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\int\left(\mathrm{tr}_{\mathsf{asym}}((\widetilde{f}-B)^{2})+\dim(\mathsf{asym})(\mathrm{d}A+2B)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\int\left((N-2)\mathrm{tr}((\widetilde{f}-B)^{2})+\frac{N(N-1)}{2}(\mathrm{d}A+2B)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{N-2}{8\pi^{2}}\int\mathrm{tr}(\widetilde{f}\wedge\widetilde{f})+\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\int\left(\frac{N(N-1)}{2}(\mathrm{d}A)^{2}+2(N-1)\mathrm{d}A\wedge NB+(NB)^{2}\right).

(60)

As a result, under the discrete axial transformation,

\bigl(\mathbb{Z}_{2(N-2)}\bigr)_{\mathrm{A}}:\psi\mapsto\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi}{2(N-2)}\mathrm{i}\gamma_{5}}\psi,\,\overline{\psi}\mapsto\overline{\psi}\,\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi}{2(N-2)}\mathrm{i}\gamma_{5}},

(61)

the partition function of QCD(ASym) is shifted by the local counterterm of the
G_{\mathrm{V}}

\displaystyle\bigl(\mathbb{Z}_{2(N-2)}\bigr)_{\mathrm{A}}:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}[A,B]\mapsto\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}}{8\pi^{2}(N-2)}\int\left(\frac{N(N-1)}{2}(\mathrm{d}A)^{2}+2(N-1)\mathrm{d}A\wedge NB+(NB)^{2}\right)}\mathcal{Z}[A,B].

(62)

This is the mixed ’t Hooft anomaly, and the anomaly matching condition can be satisfied by the complete spontaneous breakdown of the discrete axial symmetry,

\left(\mathbb{Z}_{2(N-2)}\right)_{\mathrm{A}}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{SSB}}\mathbb{Z}_{2}.

(63)

In order to apply the semiclassical description with center vortices, we would like to perform the
T^{2}
compactification to maintain the above ’t Hooft anomaly as much as possible.
To this end, we again consider the magnetic flux background (
20
). It effectively introduces the minimal ’t Hooft flux in the gauge sector, and thus the gauge field has the perturbative mass gap because of the Higgsing
SU(N)\to\mathbb{Z}_{N}

For Dirac fermions, there are perturbative gapless modes following the index theorem on

given by (
30

(N-1)
flavors of
2
d Dirac fermions at the classical level.

By performing the Abelian bosonization,
2

2\pi

\phi_{i}
, with

.
The

33

We again assume that its classical moduli is given by
\phi\equiv\phi_{1}=\cdots=\phi_{N-1}
, and then we only have a single compact scalar as a result of the perturbative analysis.

The center-vortex vertex takes the following form,

K\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{S_{I}}{N}}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\mathrm{i}}{N}(\theta-(N-2)\phi)}\sim\frac{(\Lambda L)^{3+\frac{4}{3N}}}{L^{2}}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\mathrm{i}}{N}(\theta-(N-2)\phi)},

(64)

and this satisfies the spurious symmetry,
\phi\to\phi+2\alpha
associated with

By performing the dilute gas approximation of center vortices, the effective potential,

V_{\mathrm{vortex},\ell}(\phi)=-2K\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{S_{I}}{N}}\cos\left(\frac{(N-2)\phi+2\pi\ell-\theta}{N}\right),

(65)

is generated with the discrete label,
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}

\phi
is then understood as

\displaystyle(\phi,\ell+N)\sim(\phi,\ell),

(66)

\displaystyle(\phi+2\pi,\ell)\sim(\phi,\ell+(N-2))\sim(\phi,\ell-2).

(67)

With this identification, the field

is extended to

-periodic field for odd

For even

3
Implications on the large-
N
orbifold/orientifold equivalence

There is a conjecture called as the large-
N
orientifold equivalence
Armoni:2003gp
;
Armoni:2004ub
, which states that
C
-invariant bosonic sectors of QCD(Sym/ASym) becomes equivalent to those of
\mathcal{N}=1

SU(N)
super Yang-Mills (SYM) theory in the large-
N
limit.
This is a striking conjecture, which is interesting and important not only theoretically but also phenomenologically.
For
N=3
, two-index anti-symmetric representation becomes identical to the fundamental representation, and thus QCD(ASym) for
N=3
is the ordinary QCD that describes the strong interaction of our world.
If the conjecture is true and
N=3
is regarded as a large parameter, the orientifold equivalence may provide a direct path to apply the knowledge of supersymmetric gauge theories to the actual QCD
Armoni:2003fb
;
Armoni:2003yv
.

It is therefore an important question if the large-
N
orientifold equivalence is true at the nonperturbative level.
In Ref.
Unsal:2006pj
, it is uncovered that the unbroken charge-conjugation symmetry is the necessary and sufficient condition for its validity.
In order to see this, it is useful to think of both
\mathcal{N}=1

SU(N)
SYM and QCD(ASym) as
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
projections of
\mathcal{N}=1

SO(2N)
SYM theory.
To obtain
\mathcal{N}=1

SU(N)
SYM theory from
SO(2N)
theory, we introduce the symplectic matrix
J=\mathrm{i}\sigma_{2}\otimes\bm{1}_{N}\in SO(2N)
and impose the constraint
A_{\mu}=JA_{\mu}J^{t}
and
\lambda=J\lambda J^{t}
for the gluons and gluinos of
SO(2N)
theory.
Then, the gauge group becomes
U(N)
and
\lambda
becomes the Weyl fermion in the adjoint representation, so we obtain
\mathcal{N}=1

U(N)
SYM theory.
To obtain QCD(ASym), we replace the constraint for the gluino as
\lambda=-J\lambda J^{t}
, then the fermion becomes the Dirac fermion in the anti-symmetric representation of
U(N)
.
In the large-
N
limit, the difference between
U(N)
and
SU(N)
becomes negligible, and we obtain the correspondence.
In this way, the large-
N
orientifold equivalence proposed in Refs.
Armoni:2003gp
;
Armoni:2004ub
has been decomposed into two large-
N
orbifold-type equivalences
Bershadsky:1998cb
;
Schmaltz:1998bg
;
Kachru:1998ys
;
Strassler:2001fs
between the parent and daughter theories with the same parent theory: It is an example of the daughter-daughter equivalence.

The necessary and sufficient condition for the large-
N
orbifold equivalence has been derived in Refs.
Kovtun:2003hr
;
Kovtun:2004bz
;
Kovtun:2005kh
, and it shows that the equivalence holds for correlation functions of neutral sectors if and only if the ground state also belongs to the neutral sector.
Applying it to the present case, the ground states must have unbroken charge-conjugation symmetry, and then the orientifold equivalence is true for observables in the
C
-even bosonic sector.
Since it is widely accepted that
\mathcal{N}=1
SYM theory does not break
C
while it breaks the discrete axial symmetry, the question becomes whether QCD(ASym) maintains
C
-symmetry or not.
This is a nontrivial question as it involves the strong dynamics of
4
d gauge theories.

So far, we pay attention to QCD(ASym), but we can perform the same analysis for QCD(Sym) starting from the
\mathcal{N}=1

USp(2N)
SYM theory, and we do not repeat it here.
Again, the criterion for the large-
N
orientifold equivalence is given by the unbroken
C
-symmetry for QCD(Sym).

Our analysis on small
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times T^{2}
predicts that
C
symmetry is unbroken.
We have also observed the complete spontaneous breakdown of discrete chiral symmetry, which suggests the adiabatic continuity between our semiclassical description and the strong dynamics on
\mathbb{R}^{4}
.
This gives positive support for the nonperturbative orientifold equivalence.

We would like to emphasize the magnetic flux on small
T^{2}
plays the pivotal role to achieve the adiabatic continuity
Tanizaki:2022ngt
(see also Refs.
vanBaal:1984ra
;
Yamazaki:2017ulc
;
Cox:2021vsa
).
In Ref.
Unsal:2006pj
, QCD(Sym/ASym) on small
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times S^{1}
has been studied, and it was found that these theories have chiral symmetric vacua with broken
C
symmetry.
Since the anomaly matching on
\mathbb{R}^{4}
requires the spontaneous breaking of chiral symmetry, this suggests that the naive compactification must have phase transitions and it is not suitable to study the dynamics of strongly coupled regimes.

In order to achieve the adiabatic continuity on
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times S^{1}
, the double-trace deformed gauge theories are more suitable choice
Unsal:2007vu
;
Unsal:2007jx
;
Unsal:2008ch
.
By adding the double-trace deformations to QCD(ASym) on
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times S^{1}
, we can find that
C
becomes unbroken and instead the chiral symmetry is spontaneously broken
Shifman:2008ja
.
Therefore, in both
S^{1}
and
T^{2}
compactifications with adiabatic continuity, we obtained the evidence for unbroken
C
symmetry, suggesting the validity of nonperturbative large-
N
orientifold equivalence.

4
Summary

We performed the semiclassical analysis of QCD(Sym/ASym) on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times T^{2}
by introducing the magnetic flux in
T^{2}
.
QCD(Sym/ASym) have the mixed ’t Hooft anomaly between its vector symmetry and discrete chiral symmetry, and the magnetic flux in
T^{2}
allows us to preserve the information of ’t Hooft anomaly.
As a result, the dynamics of the
2
d effective theory is constrained by the same anomaly of
4
d QCD(Sym/ASym).

We find that discrete chiral symmetry is spontaneously broken completely thanks to the dilute gas of center vortices, and this satisfies the anomaly matching constraint.
This leads to the multi-branch structure of the ground states after the tiny mass deformation, and this leads to the same vacuum structure with the pure YM theory.

Each ground state has unbroken
C
symmetry, and this gives an important implication for the large-
N
orientifold equivalence between QCD(Sym/ASym) and
\mathcal{N}=1

SU(N)
SYM theory.
Since the unbroken
C
symmetry gives the necessary and sufficient condition for its nonperturbative justification, our result gives a strong positive support for the nonperturbative orientifold equivalence, assuming the adiabatic continuity.

As we showed in this work, the chiral condensate obtained in the small symmetric
T^{2}
regime (
52
) matches exactly the result on
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times S^{1}_{\rm small}
in
Shifman:2008ja
.
At the same time, the microscopic configurations which lead to this result are genuinely distinct, even though they have the same topological charge
Q=1/N
and action.
Monopole-instantons and center vortices have different mutual statistics with Wilson loops. It would be very interesting to establish how these two reliable semiclassical regimes are adiabatically connected.

Lastly, let us comment on several future directions.
One of them is about the domain wall that connects different chiral broken vacua.
As the phase of chiral condensate can be regarded as an effective
\theta
angle via the ABJ anomaly, the domain-wall theory can be thought of
3
d QCD(Sym/ASym) with a Chern-Simons term.
This problem has been studied in Ref.
Choi:2018tuh
, and they suggest the domain wall connecting
n
-th and
0
-th chiral broken vacua is described by
U(n)_{(N\pm 2)-n,(N\pm 2)-2n}
Chern-Simons theory, where
n=1,2,\ldots,(n\pm 2)-1
.
It would be an interesting future work to study if the
T^{2}
compactification with magnetic flux can give a nontrivial check of this proposal on the wall.

Another curious problem is to extend our work of vector-like QCD to some chiral gauge theories
Bolognesi:2017pek
;
Bolognesi:2019wfq
;
Bolognesi:2019fej
;
Sulejmanpasic:2020zfs
.
Recently, Ref.
Anber:2021iip

Appendix A
Group theoretic factors of two-index representations

Let
T^{a}_{\mathsf{rep}}
be the generators of
\mathfrak{su}(N)
Lie algebra with the representation
\mathsf{rep}
.
We take the orthonormal basis,

\mathrm{tr}(T^{a}_{\mathsf{rep}}T^{b}_{\mathsf{rep}})=T(\mathsf{rep})\delta^{ab},

(68)

where
T(\mathsf{rep})
is the trace normalization factor.
For the defining (or fundamental) representation,
\mathsf{rep}=\mathsf{fd}
, we set

T(\mathsf{fd})=\frac{1}{2}.

(69)

The quadratic Casimir invariant
C_{2}(\mathsf{rep})
is defined by

\sum_{a=1}^{N^{2}-1}T^{a}_{\mathsf{rep}}T^{a}_{\mathsf{rep}}=C_{2}(\mathsf{rep})\,\mathrm{id}_{\mathsf{rep}}.

(70)

By taking the trace of both sides,
C_{2}(\mathsf{rep})
is related to
T(\mathsf{rep})
by the formula,

C_{2}(\mathsf{rep})\dim(\mathsf{rep})=(N^{2}-1)T(\mathsf{rep}).

(71)

Let us write down the table of these factors for the fundamental, adjoint, two-index symmetric and antisymmetric representations:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{c|ccc}\mathsf{rep}&\dim(\mathsf{rep})&T(\mathsf{rep})&C_{2}(\mathsf{rep})\\
\hline\cr\mathsf{fd}&N&\frac{1}{2}&\frac{N^{2}-1}{2N}\\
\mathsf{adj}&N^{2}-1&N&N\\
\mathsf{sym}&\frac{N(N+1)}{2}&\frac{N+2}{2}&\frac{(N-1)(N+2)}{N}\\
\mathsf{asym}&\frac{N(N-1)}{2}&\frac{N-2}{2}&\frac{(N+1)(N-2)}{N}\end{array}

The perturbative beta function,
\beta(g)=\frac{\mathrm{d}g}{\mathrm{d}\ln\mu}
, is given by

\beta(g)=-\frac{\beta_{0}}{(4\pi)^{2}}g^{3}-\frac{\beta_{1}}{(4\pi)^{4}}g^{5}+O(g^{7}).

(77)

The leading coefficient is

\displaystyle\beta_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{11}{3}N-\frac{4}{3}T(\mathsf{rep})N_{f},

\displaystyle\beta_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{34}{3}N^{2}-\frac{20}{3}NT(\mathsf{rep})N_{f}-4C_{2}(\mathsf{rep})T(\mathsf{rep})N_{f},

(78)

for
SU(N)
gauge theory with
N_{f}
Dirac fermions in the representation
\mathsf{rep}
.
The normalization of the coefficient is the standard one for non-supersymmetric gauge theories, and then the instanton factor within the
1
-loop renormalization group is given by

\exp\left(-\frac{8\pi^{2}}{g^{2}(\mu)}\right)=\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\mu}\right)^{\beta_{0}},

(79)

where
\Lambda
is the strong scale.
For
1
-flavor QCD(Sym/Asym),
\beta_{0}
and
\beta_{1}
are given by

\displaystyle\beta_{0}

\displaystyle=3N\mp\frac{4}{3},

(80)

\displaystyle\beta_{1}

\displaystyle=6N^{2}\mp\left(\frac{38}{3}N-\frac{8}{N}\right),

(81)

and they are identical to those of
\mathcal{N}=1
super Yang-Mills theory in the large-
N
limit,
\beta_{0}=3N
and
\beta_{1}=6N^{2}
.

Appendix B
Dirac zero modes on
T^{2}
with the magnetic flux

In this Appendix, we confirm the result of the index theorem (
30
) by solving the Dirac zero-mode equation (
29
) explicitly.
We first define the chirality of small
T^{2}
by

(\mathrm{i}\gamma_{3}\gamma_{4})\psi_{\pm}=\pm\psi_{\pm},

(82)

and then the Dirac equation for each chirality becomes

\left[\partial_{3}\mp\mathrm{i}\left(\partial_{4}+\mathrm{i}\frac{4\pi}{N}\frac{x_{3}}{L^{2}}\right)\right]\psi_{\pm}=0,

(83)

or

(\partial_{3}\mp\mathrm{i}\partial_{4})\left(\mathrm{e}^{\pm\frac{2\pi}{N}\frac{x_{3}^{2}}{L^{2}}}\psi_{\pm}\right)=0.

(84)

This shows that

\psi_{\pm}(x_{3},x_{4})=\mathrm{e}^{\mp\frac{2\pi}{N}\frac{x_{3}^{2}}{L^{2}}}u_{\pm}((x_{3}\mp\mathrm{i}x_{4})/L).

(85)

We can check that there is no zero modes for
\mathrm{i}\gamma_{3}\gamma_{4}=-1
by the following equality,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{T^{2}}\left|\left(\partial_{3}\mp\mathrm{i}\left(\partial_{4}+\mathrm{i}Bx_{3}\right)\right)\psi_{\pm}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{T^{2}}\left[|\partial_{3}\psi_{\pm}|^{2}+|(\partial_{4}+\mathrm{i}Bx_{3})\psi_{\pm}|^{2}\mp B|\psi_{\pm}|^{2}\right].

(86)

Here, we set
B=\frac{4\pi}{NL^{2}}
for simplicity of notation.
In the first equality, we use the equation for Dirac zero modes. The second equality can be obtained using integration by parts. For
\mathrm{i}\gamma_{3}\gamma_{4}=-1
, all the three terms are positive semi-definite, and it requires that
\psi_{-}=0
.
We therefore only consider the case
\mathrm{i}\gamma_{3}\gamma_{4}=1
in the following discussion.

Introducing the dimensionless coordinate
z=(x_{3}-\mathrm{i}x_{4})/L
, we can rewrite the boundary condition as

\displaystyle u_{+}(z+1)=\mathsf{sym}(S^{\dagger})\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi}{N}+\frac{4\pi}{N}z}u_{+}(z),

(87)

\displaystyle u_{+}(z-\mathrm{i})=\mathsf{sym}(C^{\dagger})u_{+}(z).

(88)

Writing color components explicitly, we find that

\displaystyle u_{+,ab}(z+1)=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi}{N}+\frac{4\pi}{N}z}u_{+,a-1\,b-1}(z),

(89)

\displaystyle u_{+,ab}(z-\mathrm{i})=\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}}{N}((a-1)+(b-1))}u_{+,ab}(z).

(90)

We can perform the Fourier expansion to satisfy (
90
),

u_{+,ab}(z)=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi}{N}((a-1)+(b-1))z}\sum_{n}c_{n,ab}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi nz}.

(91)

We then use the boundary condition (
89
) for the
x_{3}
direction. When
1<a,b\leq N
, the condition becomes

\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi}{N}(a-1+b-1)(z+1)}\sum_{n}c_{n,ab}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi n(z+1)}=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi}{N}+\frac{4\pi}{N}z}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi}{N}(a-2+b-2)z}\sum_{n}c_{n,a-1\,b-1}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi nz},

(92)

and we have

c_{n,ab}=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi}{N}(1-(a-1)-(b-1))-2\pi n}c_{n,a-1\,b-1}.

(93)

When
a=1
but
b>1
, the condition becomes

\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi}{N}(0+b-1)(z+1)}\sum_{n}c_{n,1\,b}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi n(z+1)}=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi}{N}+\frac{4\pi}{N}z}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi}{N}(N-1+b-2)z}\sum_{n}c_{n,N\,b-1}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi nz},

(94)

and we have

c_{n,1\,b}=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi}{N}(1-(b-1))-2\pi n}c_{n-1,N\,b-1}.

(95)

Similarly, when
a>1
and
b=1
,

c_{n,a\,1}=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi}{N}(1-(a-1))-2\pi n}c_{n-1,a-1\,N}.

(96)

When
a=b=1
, we get

c_{n,11}=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi}{N}-2\pi n}c_{n-2,NN}.

(97)

This gives
c_{n,a\,b}\propto\exp\left(-\frac{2\pi N}{4}n^{2}-\pi((a-1)+(b-1))n\right)
, and we can find that the solutions can be expressed with theta functions.

7

7

The Fourier series expansion of Jacobi’s theta function is given by
\theta_{3}(z)\equiv\theta_{3}(z|\tau)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\pi\tau n^{2}+2\mathrm{i}nz}=1+2\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\pi\tau n^{2}}\cos(2nz)
with
\mathrm{Im}(\tau)>0
. Importantly, it satisfies the quasi-periodicity,
\theta_{3}(z+(m+n\tau)\pi)=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\pi\tau n^{2}-2\mathrm{i}nz}\theta_{3}(z)
, and it is the key property to solve the quasi-periodicity of
u_{+}
coming out of (
89
),
u_{+}(z+N)=\mathrm{e}^{2\pi N+4\pi z}u_{+}(z)
, combined with (
90
).
We note that the theta functions are holomorphic, i.e. do not have poles or singularities, and thus they give normalizable zero modes.

Let us count the number of zero modes. By the shift matrix,
a-b\bmod N
is preserved, so the diagonal directions are related in mod
N
. That means, when we consider
k
-diagonal from the top-left to the bottom-right (i.e.
c_{n,i\,i+k}
wit
i:1\to N-k
), we jump to the
-(N-k)
-diagonal elements (i.e.
c_{n+1,N-k+j\,j}
with
j:1\to k
), and we come back to the
k
-diagonal elements,
c_{n+2,1\,1+k}
.
As a result,
c_{n,ab}
is related to
c_{n\pm 2,ab}
, which means that we can have
2
zero modes, corresponding to even and odd
n
: For example, the
1
-
1
component is given by

u_{+,11}(z)=a\sum_{n\in 2\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{2\pi N}{4}n^{2}}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi nz}+b\sum_{n\in 2\mathbb{Z}+1}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{2\pi N}{4}n^{2}}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi nz},

(98)

where
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
.
For the main diagonal, we thus have
2
zero modes, but for off-diagonals, the symmetry condition imposes the restriction, and thus we only have
1
zero mode for each. In total, we get
2+(N-1)=N+1
zero modes as indicated by the index theorem (
30
).

The discussion is completely parallel also for the case of the two-index anti-symmetric representation. The crucial difference appears only in the last step and it comes from the absence of the diagonal elements. As a result, we get
0+(N-1)=N-1
zero modes.

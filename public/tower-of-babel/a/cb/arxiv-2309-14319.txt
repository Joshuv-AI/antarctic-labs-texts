Regularity theory for parabolic operators in the half-space with boundary degeneracy
G. Metafune, L. Negro, C. Spina

Abstract
We study elliptic and parabolic problems governed by the singular elliptic operators ℒ = y α 1 ​ Tr ​ ( Q ​ D x 2 ​ u ) + 2 ​ y α 1 + α 2 2 ​ q ⋅ ∇ x D y + γ ​ y α 2 ​ D y ​ y + c ​ y α 2 − 1 ​ D y \displaystyle\mathcal{L}=y^{\alpha_{1}}\mbox{Tr }\left(QD^{2}_{x}u\right)+2y^{\frac{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}{2}}q\cdot\nabla_{x}D_{y}+\gamma y^{\alpha_{2}}D_{yy}+{cy^{\alpha_{2}-1}D_{y}} under Neumann boundary condition, in the half-space ℝ + N + 1 = { ( x , y ) : x ∈ ℝ N , y > 0 } \mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}=\{(x,y):x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},y>0\} . We prove elliptic and parabolic L p L^{p} -estimates and solvability for the associated problems. In the language of semigroup theory, we prove that ℒ \mathcal{L} generates an analytic semigroup, characterize its domain as a weighted Sobolev space and show that it has maximal regularity. Mathematics subject classification (2020): 35K67, 35B45, 47D07, 35J70, 35J75. Keywords: degenerate elliptic operators, boundary degeneracy, vector-valued harmonic analysis, maximal regularity.

Introduction

In this paper we study solvability and regularity of elliptic and parabolic problems associated to the degenerate operators

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=y^{\alpha_{1}}\mbox{Tr }\left(QD^{2}_{x}u\right)+2y^{\frac{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}{2}}q\cdot\nabla_{x}D_{y}+\gamma y^{\alpha_{2}}D_{yy}+{cy^{\alpha_{2}-1}D_{y}}

(1)

and
D_{t}-\mathcal{L}
in the half-space
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}=\{(x,y):x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},y>0\}
or in
(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}
and under Neumann boundary condition at
y=0
.

Here
c\in\mathbb{R}
and

\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}Q&{q}^{t}\\[4.30554pt]
\hline\cr q&\gamma\end{array}\right)
is
a constant real elliptic matrix. The real numbers
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
satisfy
\alpha_{2}<2
and
\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1}<2
but are not assumed to be nonnegative.

We write

B_{y}
to denote the 1-dimensional Bessel operator
D_{yy}+\frac{c}{y}D_{y}
. With this notation the special case where

\mathcal{L}=y^{\alpha_{1}}\Delta_{x}+y^{\alpha_{2}}B_{y}

has been already studied in
[
15
]

2y^{\frac{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}{2}}q\cdot\nabla_{x}D_{y}
in the operator
\mathcal{L}
which is a crucial step for treating degenerate operators in domains, through a localization procedure.

Our main result is the following,
see Theorems
6.1
,
6.2

Theorem
.

Let
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\alpha_{2}<2
,
\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1}<2
and

\alpha_{1}^{-}<\frac{m+1}{p}<\frac{c}{\gamma}+1-\alpha_{2}.

Then the operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=y^{\alpha_{1}}\mbox{Tr }\left(QD^{2}_{x}u\right)+2y^{\frac{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}{2}}q\cdot\nabla_{x}D_{y}+\gamma y^{\alpha_{2}}D_{yy}+{cy^{\alpha_{2}-1}D_{y}}

generates a bounded analytic semigroup in

,

It turns out that when

When

Let us briefly describe the previous literature on these operators. In

Another source of non-commutativity comes from the presence of mixed derivatives. In

This paper is devoted to a final step in this direction, by adding (different) powers of

This is, by no means, an immediate generalization of the previous results or methods and many extra difficulties appear. Here we consider only constant matrices

are bounded and uniformly continuous is however straightforward and allows to treat operators in smooth domains, whose degeneracy in the top order coefficients behaves like a power of the distance from the boundary. We shall treat these topics in a forthcoming paper.

As for the simpler operator

The operators

\|y^{\alpha}\Delta_{x}u\|_{p,m}+\|y^{\alpha}\nabla_{x}D_{y}u\|_{p,m}+\|y^{\alpha}B_{y}u\|_{p,m}\leq C\|\mathcal{L}u\|_{p,m},\quad

(2)

Let us explain how to obtain (

y^{\alpha}(\Delta_{x}u+2a\cdot\nabla_{x}D_{y}u+B_{y}u)=f

Denoting by

\displaystyle y^{\alpha}\Delta_{x}\mathcal{L}^{-1}=-{\cal F}^{-1}\left(y^{\alpha}|\xi|^{2}(|\xi|^{2}-2ia\xi D_{y}-B_{y})^{-1}y^{-\alpha}\right){\cal F}

\displaystyle y^{\alpha}\nabla_{x}D_{y}\mathcal{L}^{-1}=-{\cal F}^{-1}\left(y^{\alpha}\xi D_{y}(|\xi|^{2}-2ia\xi D_{y}-B_{y})^{-1}y^{-\alpha}\right){\cal F}

and the boundedness of

are equivalent to that of the multipliers
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\to y^{\alpha}|\xi|^{2}(|\xi|^{2}-2ia\cdot\xi D_{y}-B_{y})^{-1}y^{-\alpha}
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\to y^{\alpha}\xi D_{y}(|\xi|^{2}-2ia\cdot\xi D_{y}-B_{y})^{-1}y^{-\alpha}

L^{p}_{m}

The structure of these multipliers show the difficulties connected to the presence of the mixed operators. The parameter

and variants like

Both the elliptic and parabolic estimates above share the name “maximal regularity” even though this term is often restricted to the parabolic case.

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|b|}{(\beta+1)Q_{a}(\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\textrm{\emph{Re}\,}{\mathfrak{\tilde{a}}_{b}}(u,u)=\frac{|a|}{\sqrt{1-|a|^{2}}}\textrm{\emph{Re}\,}{\mathfrak{\tilde{a}}_{b}}(u,u).

Proof.
Properties (i) and (ii) are immediate consequences of the definition. Since

To prove (iii), we use Young’s inequality and the elementary identity

for

\displaystyle\left|\textrm{\emph{Im}\,}{\mathfrak{\tilde{a}}_{b}}(u,u)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|-\frac{b}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}D_{y}\left(|u|^{2}\right)y^{c+\beta}\,dy\right|=\left|-b\int_{0}^{\infty}\textrm{\emph{Re}\,}\left(\overline{u}D_{y}u\right)y^{c+\beta}\,dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq|b|\left(\|D_{y}u\|_{L^{2}_{c}}\|u\|_{L^{2}_{c+2\beta}}\right)=\frac{|b|}{(\beta+1)Q_{a}(\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left(\|D_{y}u\|_{L^{2}_{c}}\;{(\beta+1)Q_{a}(\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|u\|_{L^{2}_{c+2\beta}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|b|}{2(\beta+1)Q_{a}(\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left(\|D_{y}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{c}}+(\beta+1)^{2}Q_{a}(\xi)\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{c+2\beta}}\right)

In view of the latter equality, the required identity becomes trivial.

Lemma 3.15
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
\alpha^{-}<\frac{m+1}{p}<c+1-\alpha
. Then there exists

such that for every

,

\|y^{\alpha}D_{y}u\|_{L^{p}_{m}}\leq C\|y^{\alpha}Bu\|_{L^{p}_{m}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|y^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{p}_{m}}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

It follows that for every
\varepsilon>0
,
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

\||\xi|y^{\alpha}D_{y}u\|_{L^{p}_{m}}\leq\varepsilon\|y^{\alpha}Bu\|_{L^{p}_{m}}+\frac{C}{\varepsilon}\||\xi|^{2}y^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{p}_{m}}.

Proof.
We apply
[
16

with

in place of

thus obtaining

\displaystyle\|D_{y}u\|_{L^{p}_{m+\alpha p}}\leq C\|Bu\|_{L^{p}_{m+\alpha p}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u\|_{L^{p}_{m+\alpha p}}^{\frac{1}{2}},\qquad u\in W^{2,p}_{\mathcal{N}}(0,m+\alpha p).

The first inequality then follows since by Lemma

\displaystyle W^{2,p}_{\mathcal{N}}(\alpha,m)\cap L^{p}_{m+\alpha p}=W^{2,p}_{\mathcal{N}}(0,m+\alpha p)\cap L^{p}_{m}.

The second one follows by Young inequality.

Lemma 3.16
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
\alpha^{-}<\frac{m+1}{p}<c+1-\alpha
. Then there exists
C>0
, independent of
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
|a|<1
, such that for every

,
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
,
\textrm{\emph{Re}\,}\lambda>0
,

\||\xi|^{2}y^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{p}_{m}}\leq C\|(\lambda-y^{\alpha}L_{2a\cdot\xi}+|\xi|^{2}y^{\alpha})u\|_{L^{p}_{m}}.

Proof
.
Let
u\in W^{2,p}_{\mathcal{N}}(\alpha,m)\cap L^{p}_{m+\alpha p}
and set

. Then, by Corollary
3.13
(i),

\displaystyle\||\xi|^{2}y^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{p}_{m}}

\displaystyle=\||\xi|^{2}y^{\alpha}(\lambda-y^{\alpha}L_{2a\cdot\xi}+|\xi|^{2}y^{\alpha})^{-1}f\|_{L^{p}_{m}}=\left\||\xi|^{2}y^{\alpha}\left(\frac{\lambda}{y^{\alpha}}-L_{2a\cdot\xi}+|\xi|^{2}\right)^{-1}\frac{f}{y^{\alpha}}\right\|_{L^{p}_{m}}.

By Proposition
3.10

\displaystyle\left\||\xi|^{2}y^{\alpha}\left(\frac{\lambda}{y^{\alpha}}-L_{2a\cdot\xi}+|\xi|^{2}\right)^{-1}\frac{f}{y^{\alpha}}\right\|_{L^{p}_{m}}

\displaystyle=\left\||\xi|^{2}\left(\frac{\lambda}{y^{\alpha}}-L_{2a\cdot\xi}+|\xi|^{2}\right)^{-1}\frac{f}{y^{\alpha}}\right\|_{L^{p}_{m+\alpha p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\frac{f}{y^{\alpha}}\right\|_{L^{p}_{m+\alpha p}}=C\|f\|_{L^{p}_{m}}

for some
C
independent of
\xi
.

Theorem 3.17
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
\alpha^{-}<\frac{m+1}{p}<c+1-\alpha
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
. Then the generator of
(e^{z(y^{\alpha}(L_{2a\cdot\xi}-|\xi|^{2}))})

y^{\alpha}(L_{2a\cdot\xi}-|\xi|^{2})
with domain

. The set

\mathcal{D}
defined in (
4
)
is a core.

Proof.
We fix
0\neq\xi
and first prove that the equation

,

, is uniquely solvable in
W^{2,p}_{\mathcal{N}}(\alpha,m)\cap L^{p}_{m+\alpha p}
. Let
u\in W^{2,p}_{\mathcal{N}}(\alpha,m)\cap L^{p}_{m+\alpha p}
. By Theorem
2.1
(i) in the first inequality and then by Lemma
3.15
and Lemma
3.16
, there exists a positive constant

such that for every

,
0\leq t\leq 1

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{2,p}_{\mathcal{N}}(\alpha,m)}+\||y^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{p}_{m}}\leq C\|u-y^{\alpha}Bu\|+\|y^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{p}_{m}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|u-y^{\alpha}L_{2ta\cdot\xi}u+|\xi|^{2}y^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{p}_{m}}+|2ta\cdot\xi|\|y^{\alpha}D_{y}u\|_{L^{p}_{m}}+\|(1+|\xi|^{2})y^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{p}_{m}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|u-y^{\alpha}L_{2ta\cdot\xi}u+|\xi|^{2}y^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{p}_{m}}+\varepsilon\|y^{\alpha}Bu\|_{L^{p}_{m}}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\|(1+|\xi|^{2})y^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{p}_{m}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|u-y^{\alpha}L_{2ta\cdot\xi}u+|\xi|^{2}y^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{p}_{m}}+\varepsilon\|u\|_{W^{2,p}_{\mathcal{N}}(\alpha,m)}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}(1+|\xi|^{-2})\||\xi|^{2}y^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{p}_{m}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|u-y^{\alpha}L_{2ta\cdot\xi}u+|\xi|^{2}y^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{p}_{m}}+\varepsilon\|u\|_{W^{2,p}_{\mathcal{N}}(\alpha,m)}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}(1+|\xi|^{-2})\|u-y^{\alpha}L_{2ta\cdot\xi}u+|\xi|^{2}y^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{p}_{m}}\right).

Note that for the last inequality we used the fact that the estimate in Lemma
3.16
is uniform in
\xi
and

.
By choosing

we deduce for some

depending on
\xi
but independent of

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{2,p}_{\mathcal{N}}(\alpha,m)}+\|y^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{p}_{m}}\leq C\|u-y^{\alpha}L_{2ta\cdot\xi}u+|\xi|^{2}y^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{p}_{m}}.

Since, for
t=0
, the operator

is invertible in

(ii), the same holds for

Let

be the generator of

in

and consider the set

\mathcal{D}=\left\{u\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,\infty)):u\ {\rm constant\ in\ a\ neighborhood\ of}\ 0\right\}

which is dense in

, by Theorem
8.4
.
By using the definition of

through the form
\mathfrak{a}
as in the beginning of Section 3, it is easy to see that

and that

on
\mathcal{D}
. Since
\mathcal{D}
is dense in

,

is closed on

Take now

and let

and that

on

and, as before, one concludes the proof for

.

We remark that Proposition
3.8
assures that

As consequence we deduce the domain of the operator

Remark 3.20

Let

such that

4
Multipliers

In this section we investigate the boundedness of some multipliers related to the degenerate operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=y^{\alpha}\left(\Delta_{x}+2\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}D_{iy}+D_{yy}+\frac{c}{y}D_{y}\right),\qquad a\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\ |a|<1,\qquad\alpha<2

(13)

Assuming that

and taking the Fourier transform (denoted by
\mathcal{F}
or

We
consider the operator

of Section
3
. The latter computation shows that formally

\displaystyle(\lambda-\mathcal{L})^{-1}={\cal F}^{-1}\left(\lambda-y^{\alpha}L_{2a\cdot\xi}+y^{\alpha}|\xi|^{2}\right)^{-1}{\cal F}.

In order to prove that
\mathcal{L}
generates an analytic semigroup and to prove regularity for the associated parabolic problem, we investigate the boundedness of the operator-valued multiplier

\displaystyle\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\to R_{\lambda}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\left(\lambda-y^{\alpha}L_{2a\cdot\xi}+y^{\alpha}|\xi|^{2}\right)^{-1}.

To characterize the domain of

we also consider the multipliers

,
\xi y^{\alpha}D_{y}R_{\lambda}
which are associated with the operators

,
y^{\alpha}D_{xy}(\lambda-\mathcal{L})^{-1}
, respectively. In the next results we prove that the above multipliers satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem
7.5
.

We also need the operator-valued multiplier
\tilde{R}_{\lambda}(\xi)
defined by

\displaystyle\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\tilde{R}_{\lambda}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\left(|\xi|^{2}-L_{2a\cdot\xi}+\frac{\lambda}{y^{\alpha}}\right)^{-1}\in\mathcal{B}\left(L^{p}_{m+\alpha p}\right).

Note that the role of

and

is a spectral parameter in the first and plays a role of a complex potential in the second, where instead is

the spectral parameter. Nevertheless, we keep the same notation.

By Corollary
3.13
we have

\displaystyle y^{\alpha}R_{\lambda}(\xi)f

\displaystyle=y^{\alpha}\tilde{R}_{\lambda}(\xi)\left(\frac{f}{y^{\alpha}}\right),\qquad y^{\alpha}D_{y}R_{\lambda}(\xi)f=y^{\alpha}D_{y}\tilde{R}_{\lambda}(\xi)\left(\frac{f}{y^{\alpha}}\right),\qquad f\in L^{p}_{m}.

(14)

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
\alpha<2
be such that

.
Then the families

\displaystyle\left\{\lambda R_{\lambda}(\xi),\;|\xi|^{2}y^{\alpha}R_{\lambda}(\xi),\;\xi y^{\alpha}D_{y}R_{\lambda}(\xi)\in\mathcal{B}(L^{p}_{m})\,:\,\lambda\in\mathbb{C}_{+},\;\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}\right\}

Proof.
The
\mathcal{R}
-boundedness of

The
\mathcal{R}

\displaystyle=y^{\alpha}\left(|\xi|^{2}\tilde{R}_{\lambda}(\xi)\right)\left(\frac{\cdot}{y^{\alpha}}\right)

\displaystyle\tilde{R}_{\lambda}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\left(|\xi|^{2}-L_{2a\cdot\xi}+\frac{\lambda}{y^{\alpha}}\right)^{-1}

Lemma 4.2

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in{\mathcal{S}}_{n}}y^{\alpha}R_{\lambda}(\xi)\prod_{k=1}^{n}\Big(2ia_{j_{\sigma(k)}}y^{\alpha}D_{y}R_{\lambda}(\xi)-2\xi_{j_{\sigma(k)}}y^{\alpha}R_{\lambda}(\xi)\Big)

(15)

\displaystyle D_{\xi_{j_{1}}}\cdots D_{\xi_{j_{n}}}y^{\alpha}D_{y}R_{\lambda}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in{\mathcal{S}}_{n}}y^{\alpha}D_{y}R_{\lambda}(\xi)\prod_{k=1}^{n}\Big(2ia_{j_{\sigma(k)}}y^{\alpha}D_{y}R_{\lambda}(\xi)-2\xi_{j_{\sigma(k)}}y^{\alpha}R_{\lambda}(\xi)\Big).

Let us fix
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
. Let us prove the first equality in (

) for

that is, for

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi_{j}}(R_{\lambda}(\xi))=R_{\lambda}(\xi)\Big(2ia_{j}y^{\alpha}D_{y}R_{\lambda}(\xi)-2\xi_{j}y^{\alpha}R_{\lambda}(\xi)\Big),\qquad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}.

Indeed let us write for

\displaystyle R_{\lambda}

\displaystyle(\xi+he_{j})-R_{\lambda}(\xi)=\left(\lambda+y^{\alpha}|\xi+he_{j}|^{2}-y^{\alpha}L_{2a\cdot(\xi+he_{j})}\right)^{-1}-\left(\lambda+y^{\alpha}|\xi|^{2}-y^{\alpha}L_{2a\cdot\xi}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=R_{\lambda}(\xi)\,y^{\alpha}\left(L_{2a\cdot(\xi+he_{j})}-L_{2a\cdot\xi}+|\xi|^{2}-|\xi+he_{j}|^{2}\right)\left(\lambda+y^{\alpha}|\xi+he_{j}|^{2}-y^{\alpha}L_{2a\cdot(\xi+he_{j})}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=R_{\lambda}(\xi)\,y^{\alpha}\left(2ia_{j}hD_{y}-2\xi_{j}h-h^{2}\right)R_{\lambda}(\xi+he_{j})

\displaystyle=2ia_{j}h\,R_{\lambda}(\xi)\,y^{\alpha}D_{y}R_{\lambda}(\xi+he_{j})-(2\xi_{j}h+h^{2})\,R_{\lambda}(\xi)\,y^{\alpha}R_{\lambda}(\xi+he_{j}).

Using the
\mathcal{R}
-boundedness of

|\xi|^{2}y^{\alpha}R_{\lambda}(\xi)
,

Let

\|\sum_{i}\varepsilon_{i}S_{i}x_{i}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;X)}\leq C\|\sum_{i}\varepsilon_{i}x_{i}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;X)}

is a bounded subset of

. The smallest constant

for which the above definition holds is the
\mathcal{R}
-bound of
\mathcal{S}
, denoted by
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{S})
.
It is well-known that this definition does not depend on

(however, the constant
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{S})
does) and that
\mathcal{R}
-boundedness is equivalent to boundedness when

is an Hilbert space.
When

Proposition 7.1

Let

. Then

-bounded if and only if there is a constant

such that for every finite family

\left\|\left(\sum_{i}|S_{i}f_{i}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Sigma)}\leq C\left\|\left(\sum_{i}|f_{i}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Sigma)}.

for which the above square functions estimates hold satisfies

for a suitable

-boundedness follows from domination by a positive
\mathcal{R}
-bounded family.

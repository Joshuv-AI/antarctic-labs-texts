Repeated Integration and Explicit Formula for the n n -th Integral of x m ​ ( ln ⁡ x ) m ′ x^{m}(\ln x)^{m^{\prime}}
Roudy El Haddad

Abstract
Repeated integration is a major topic of integral calculus. In this article, we study repeated integration. In particular, we study repeated integrals and recurrent integrals. For each of these integrals, we develop reduction formulae for both the definite as well as indefinite form. These reduction formulae express these repetitive integrals in terms of single integrals. We also derive a generalization of the fundamental theorem of calculus that expresses a definite integral in terms of an indefinite integral for repeated and recurrent integrals. From the recurrent integral formulae, we derive some partition identities. Then we provide an explicit formula for the n n -th integral of x m ​ ( ln ⁡ x ) m ′ x^{m}(\ln x)^{m^{\prime}} in terms of a shifted multiple harmonic star sum. Additionally, we use this integral to derive new expressions for the harmonic sum and repeated harmonic sum.

2
Repeated integration

In this section, we consider two techniques of repeated integration: repeated integrals and recurrent integrals. We begin by defining these types of repetitive integrals and presenting some notation.

Definition
.

We define the indefinite repeated integral of order
n
of the function
f(x)
, denoted
I_{n}[f(x)](x)
or simply
I_{n}
, by the following structure:

I_{n}=\int{\cdots\int{f(x)\,dx\cdots dx}}=\int{\cdots\int{f(x)\,dx^{n}}}.

(1)

It can also be defined by the following recurrent relation:

\begin{cases}I_{n+1}=\int{I_{n}\,dx},\\
I_{0}\,\,\,\,\,\,=f(x).\end{cases}

Definition
.

We define the
n
-th order definite repeated integral of the function
f(x)
, denoted
I_{n,a,b}[f(x)]
or simply
I_{n,a,b}
, by the following structure:

I_{n,a,b}=\int_{a}^{b}{\cdots\int_{a}^{x_{3}}{\int_{a}^{x_{2}}{f(x_{1})\,dx_{1}dx_{2}\cdots dx_{n}}}}.

(2)

Definition
.

We define the indefinite recurrent integral of order
n
of the function
f(x)
, denoted
J_{n}[f(x)](x)
or simply
J_{n}
, by the following recurrent relation:

\begin{cases}J_{n+1}=\int{f(x)J_{n}\,dx},\\
J_{0}\,\,\,\,\,\,=1.\end{cases}

Explicitly, it can also be expressed as follows:

J_{n}=\int{f(x)\int{f(x)\cdots\int{f(x)\,dx^{n}}}}.

(3)

Definition
.

We define the
n
-th order definite recurrent integral of the function
f(x)
, denoted
J_{n,a,b}[f(x)]
or simply
J_{n,a,b}
, by the following structure:

J_{n,a,b}=\int_{a}^{b}{f(x_{n})\int_{a}^{x_{n}}{f(x_{n-1})\cdots\int_{a}^{x_{2}}{f(x_{1})\,dx_{1}\cdots dx_{n}}}}.

(4)

Remark
.

As we can see, repeated integrals are analogous to repeated sums
[
3
]
while recurrent integrals are analogous to recurrent sums
[
1
]
.

Likewise, before we proceed, we need to generalize the concept of constant of integration:
Let us define
C_{n}(x)
as

C_{n}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}{c_{i}\frac{x^{i}}{i!}}

where the
c_{i}
’s are constants of integration.

Remark
.

If we are using
C_{n}(x)
as a function of
x
, for simplicity, we will denote it as
C_{n}
.

2.1
Repeated integrals

In this section, we begin by providing a way to compute indefinite repeated integrals in terms of simple indefinite integrals. Then we generalize the concept of primitive and present a generalization of the fundamental theorem of calculus. Finally, we present a formula for computing definite repeated integrals in terms of simple definite integrals.

2.1.1
Indefinite repeated integrals

We begin by proving a formula for expressing indefinite repeated integrals in terms of single integrals. We refer to this theorem as the reduction formula for indefinite repeated integrals.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
f(x)
be a function of
x
, for any
n\in\mathbb{N^{*}}
, we have that

\int{\cdots\int{f(x)dx^{n}}}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}{(-1)^{n-k}\frac{x^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}\left(\int{\frac{x^{n-k}}{(n-k)!}f(x)dx}\right)}+C_{n}.

Remark
.

By substituting
k
by
n-k
and performing a few manipulations, this theorem can be rewritten as follows:

\int{\cdots\int{f(x)dx^{n}}}=\frac{x^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{\binom{n-1}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{x^{k}}\left(\int{x^{k}f(x)dx}\right)}+C_{n}.

Proof.

1. Base case: verify true for
n=1
.

\sum_{k=1}^{1}{(-1)^{1-k}\frac{x^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}\left(\int{\frac{x^{1-k}}{(1-k)!}f(x)dx}\right)}+C_{1}=\int{f(x)\,dx}+C_{1}.

2. Induction hypothesis: assume the statement is true until
n
.

\int{\cdots\int{f(x)dx^{n}}}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}{(-1)^{n-k}\frac{x^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}\left(\int{\frac{x^{n-k}}{(n-k)!}f(x)dx}\right)}+C_{n}.

3. Induction step: we will show that this statement is true for
(n+1)
.

We have to show the following statement to be true:

\int{\cdots\int{f(x)dx^{n+1}}}=\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}{(-1)^{n-k+1}\frac{x^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}\left(\int{\frac{x^{n-k+1}}{(n-k+1)!}f(x)dx}\right)}+C_{n+1}.

To be concise, we denote the left hand side term by
I
. By applying the induction hypothesis,

\begin{split}I&=\int{\left(\int{\cdots\int{f(x)\,dx^{n}}}\right)dx}\\
&=\int{\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}{(-1)^{n-k}\frac{x^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}\left(\int{\frac{x^{n-k}}{(n-k)!}f(x)dx}\right)}+C_{n}\right)dx}\\
&=\int{\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}{(-1)^{n-k}\frac{x^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}\left(\int{\frac{x^{n-k}}{(n-k)!}f(x)dx}\right)}\right)dx}+\int{\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}{c_{i}\frac{x^{i}}{i!}}\right)\,dx}+C\\
&=\sum_{k=1}^{n}{(-1)^{n-k}\int{\frac{x^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}\left(\int{\frac{x^{n-k}}{(n-k)!}f(x)dx}\right)dx}}+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}{c_{i}\frac{x^{i+1}}{(i+1)!}}+C.\end{split}

Setting
du=\frac{x^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}dx
and
v=\int{\frac{x^{n-k}}{(n-k)!}f(x)\,dx}
, then applying integration by parts, we have

\begin{split}\int{\frac{x^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}\left(\int{\frac{x^{n-k}}{(n-k)!}f(x)dx}\right)dx}&=\frac{x^{k}}{k!}\int{\frac{x^{n-k}}{(n-k)!}f(x)\,dx}-\int{\frac{x^{k}}{k!}\frac{x^{n-k}}{(n-k)!}f(x)\,dx}\\
&=\frac{x^{k}}{k!}\int{\frac{x^{n-k}}{(n-k)!}f(x)\,dx}-\binom{n}{k}\int{\frac{x^{n}}{n!}f(x)\,dx}.\end{split}

Substituting back, we get

\begin{split}I&=\sum_{k=1}^{n}{(-1)^{n-k}\frac{x^{k}}{k!}\int{\frac{x^{n-k}}{(n-k)!}f(x)\,dx}}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}{(-1)^{n-k}\binom{n}{k}\int{\frac{x^{n}}{n!}f(x)\,dx}}+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}{c_{i}\frac{x^{i+1}}{(i+1)!}}+C\\
&\resizebox{22609920}{}{$\displaystyle{=\sum_{k=1}^{n}{(-1)^{n-k}\frac{x^{k}}{k!}\int{\frac{x^{n-k}}{(n-k)!}f(x)\,dx}}-(-1)^{n}\left(\int{\frac{x^{n}}{n!}f(x)\,dx}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{n}{(-1)^{k}\binom{n}{k}}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}{c_{i-1}\frac{x^{i}}{i!}}+C.}$}\end{split}

Knowing that
\sum_{k=0}^{n}{(-1)^{k}\binom{n}{k}}=0
,

\sum_{k=1}^{n}{(-1)^{k}\binom{n}{k}}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{(-1)^{k}\binom{n}{k}}-1=-1.

Also, let us consider the set of constants
b_{i}
such that
b_{0}=C
and, for
i\geq 1
,
b_{i}=c_{i-1}
. Hence,

\begin{split}I&=\sum_{k=1}^{n}{(-1)^{n-k}\frac{x^{k}}{k!}\int{\frac{x^{n-k}}{(n-k)!}f(x)\,dx}}+(-1)^{n}\left(\int{\frac{x^{n}}{n!}f(x)\,dx}\right)+\sum_{i=0}^{n}{b_{i}\frac{x^{i}}{i!}}\\
&=\sum_{k=2}^{n+1}{(-1)^{n-k+1}\frac{x^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}\int{\frac{x^{n-k+1}}{(n-k+1)!}f(x)\,dx}}+(-1)^{n}\left(\int{\frac{x^{n}}{n!}f(x)\,dx}\right)+\sum_{i=0}^{n}{b_{i}\frac{x^{i}}{i!}}\\
&=\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}{(-1)^{n-k+1}\frac{x^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}\int{\frac{x^{n-k+1}}{(n-k+1)!}f(x)\,dx}}+\sum_{i=0}^{n}{b_{i}\frac{x^{i}}{i!}}.\end{split}

Noting that
\sum_{i=0}^{n}{b_{i}\frac{x^{i}}{i!}}
represents
C_{n+1}
, the theorem is proven by induction.
∎

Hence, to find the
n
-th integral of
f(x)
according to Theorem
2.1
, it is enough to find an expression for the integral of
x^{k}f(x)
in terms of
k
. Thus, the problem is reduced from computing an
n
-th integral to computing a simple integral.
Using this theorem, we find two propositions that will be useful later on.

Proposition 2.1
.

For
f(x)=1
, using Theorem
2.1
, we get that

\int{\cdots\int{1}\,dx^{n}}=\frac{x^{n}}{n!}+C_{n}.

Proof.

Applying Theorem
2.1
for
f(x)=1
, we get

\begin{split}\int{\cdots\int{1\,dx^{n}}}&=\frac{x^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{\binom{n-1}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{x^{k}}\left(\int{x^{k}dx}\right)}+C_{n}\\
&=\frac{x^{n}}{(n-1)!}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{\binom{n-1}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k+1}}+C_{n}\\
&=\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{\binom{n}{k+1}(-1)^{k}}+C_{n}\\
&=\frac{x^{n}}{n!}+C_{n}.\end{split}

∎

Proposition 2.2
.

For
f(x)=\ln x
, using Theorem
2.1
, we get that

\int{\cdots\int{\ln x}\,dx^{n}}-C_{n}=\int{\cdots\int{\frac{1}{x}}\,dx^{n+1}}-C_{n+1}=\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\left[\ln x+\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\binom{n}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k}}\right].

Remark
.

Theorem
2.1
can also be generalized to non-integer orders to obtain:

I_{\alpha}=\frac{x^{\alpha-1}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\binom{\alpha-1}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{x^{k}}\int{x^{k}f(x)\,dx}}

where

\binom{\alpha-1}{k}=\frac{(\alpha-1)\cdots(\alpha-k)}{k!}.

2.1.2
Relation between definite and indefinite repeated integrals

The fundamental theorem of calculus relates the definite integral of a function to its primitive. In this section, we develop a generalization of the fundamental theorem of calculus that relates the definite repeated integral of a function to its repeated primitives.

We begin by defining and generalizing the concept of primitive.

Definition
.

We denote by
F(x)
the primitive of
f(x)
. We use primitive to mean the expression of the indefinite integral without the constant of integration (In other words, the case where the constant of integration is zero).

Definition
.

We define the primitive of order
n
of the function
f(x)
, denoted
F_{n}[f(x)](x)
or
F_{n}(x)
, as the expression of the
n
-th indefinite integral of
f(x)
excluding the constants of integration (In other words, the case where the constants of integration are zero).

The following theorem illustrates how to compute the
n
-th primitive of a function
f(x)
in terms of a simple primitive, specifically, the primitive of
x^{k}f(x)
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\Phi_{k}(x)
be the primitive of
x^{k}f(x)
. The
n
-th primitive of
f(x)
can be expressed as follows:

F_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}{(-1)^{n-k}\frac{x^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}\frac{\Phi_{n-k}(x)}{(n-k)!}}=\frac{x^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{\binom{n-1}{k}\frac{\Phi_{k}(x)}{(-x)^{k}}}.

Proof.

Using the definition of the
n
-th primitive presented as well as the notation presented, we can see, from Theorem
2.1
, that the
n
-th primitive of
f(x)
can be expressed as stated by this theorem.
∎

Example 2.1
.

The
n
-th primitive of
f(x)=1
is

F_{n}(x)=\frac{x^{n}}{n!}.

(5)

Example 2.2
.

The
n
-th primitive of
f(x)=\ln x
is

F_{n}[\ln x](x)=F_{n+1}\left[\frac{1}{x}\right](x)=\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\left[\ln x+\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\binom{n}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k}}\right].

(6)

In the following lemma, we present a modified version of the fundamental theorem of calculus which is needed to derive a generalization of the fundamental theorem of calculus for repeated integrals.

Lemma 2.1
.

We have that

\int_{a}^{b}{F_{n}(x)dx}=F_{n+1}(x)|_{a}^{b}=F_{n+1}(b)-F_{n+1}(a).

Proof.

F_{n+1}(x)
is the primitive of
F_{n}(x)
. Hence, from the fundamental theorem of calculus, we have the lemma.
∎

In what follows, we prove a generalization of the fundamental theorem of calculus.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
f(x)
be a function defined in the interval
[a,b]
, for any
n\in\mathbb{N^{*}}
, we have

\int_{a}^{b}{\cdots\int_{a}^{x_{3}}{\int_{a}^{x_{2}}{f(x_{1})dx_{1}dx_{2}\cdots dx_{n}}}}=F_{n}(b)-\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\frac{(b-a)^{n-k}}{(n-k)!}F_{k}(a)}.

Remark
.

By applying the binomial theorem, this theorem can be expressed as follows:

\int_{a}^{b}{\cdots\int_{a}^{x_{3}}{\int_{a}^{x_{2}}{f(x_{1})dx_{1}dx_{2}\cdots dx_{n}}}}=F_{n}(b)-\sum_{k=1}^{n}{F_{k}(a)\frac{b^{n-k}}{(n-k)!}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-k}{\binom{n-k}{\ell}(-1)^{\ell}\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{\ell}}}.

Proof.

1. Base case: verify true for
n=1
.

F_{1}(b)-\sum_{k=1}^{1}{\frac{(b-a)^{1-k}}{(1-k)!}F_{k}(a)}=F_{1}(b)-F_{1}(a)=\int_{a}^{b}{f(x_{1})dx_{1}}.

2. Induction hypothesis: assume the statement is true until
n
.

\int_{a}^{b}{\cdots\int_{a}^{x_{3}}{\int_{a}^{x_{2}}{f(x_{1})dx_{1}dx_{2}\cdots dx_{n}}}}=F_{n}(b)-\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\frac{(b-a)^{n-k}}{(n-k)!}F_{k}(a)}.

3. Induction step: we will show that this statement is true for
(n+1)
.

We have to show the following statement to be true:

\int_{a}^{b}{\cdots\int_{a}^{x_{3}}{\int_{a}^{x_{2}}{f(x_{1})dx_{1}dx_{2}\cdots dx_{n+1}}}}=F_{n+1}(b)-\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}{\frac{(b-a)^{n-k+1}}{(n-k+1)!}F_{k}(a)}.

\int_{a}^{b}{\cdots\int_{a}^{x_{3}}{\int_{a}^{x_{2}}{f(x_{1})dx_{1}dx_{2}\cdots dx_{n+1}}}}=\int_{a}^{b}{\left[\int_{a}^{x_{n+1}}{\cdots\int_{a}^{x_{3}}{\int_{a}^{x_{2}}{f(x_{1})dx_{1}dx_{2}\cdots dx_{n}}}}\right]dx_{n+1}}.

Letting
t=x_{n+1}
and applying the induction hypothesis,

\begin{split}\int_{a}^{b}{\cdots\int_{a}^{x_{3}}{\int_{a}^{x_{2}}{f(x_{1})dx_{1}dx_{2}\cdots dx_{n+1}}}}&=\int_{a}^{b}{\left[F_{n}(x_{n+1})-\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\frac{(x_{n+1}-a)^{n-k}}{(n-k)!}F_{k}(a)}\right]dx_{n+1}}\\
&=\int_{a}^{b}{F_{n}(t)dt}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}{F_{k}(a)\int_{a}^{b}{\frac{(t-a)^{n-k}}{(n-k)!}dt}}.\end{split}

Let us note that, letting
u=(t-a)
and, hence,
du=dt
, we have

\int_{a}^{b}{\frac{(t-a)^{n-k}}{(n-k)!}dt}=\int_{a}^{b}{\frac{u^{n-k}}{(n-k)!}du}=\left[\frac{u^{n-k+1}}{(n-k+1)!}\right]_{a}^{b}=\frac{(b-a)^{n-k+1}}{(n-k+1)!}.

Using Lemma 2.1, we get

\begin{split}\int_{a}^{b}{\cdots\int_{a}^{x_{3}}{\int_{a}^{x_{2}}{f(x_{1})dx_{1}dx_{2}\cdots dx_{n+1}}}}&=F_{n+1}(b)-F_{n+1}(a)-\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\frac{(b-a)^{n-k+1}}{(n-k+1)!}F_{k}(a)}\\
&=F_{n+1}(b)-\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}{\frac{(b-a)^{n-k+1}}{(n-k+1)!}F_{k}(a)}.\end{split}

The case for
(n+1)
is proven. Hence, the theorem is proven by induction.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

For
f(x)=1
, from Theorem
2.3
, we have that

\int_{a}^{b}{\cdots\int_{a}^{x_{3}}{\int_{a}^{x_{2}}{1\,dx_{1}dx_{2}\cdots dx_{n}}}}=\frac{(b-a)^{n}}{n!}.

Proof.

By applying Theorem
2.3
for
f(x)=1
, we have that

\begin{split}\int_{a}^{b}{\cdots\int_{a}^{x_{3}}{\int_{a}^{x_{2}}{1\,dx_{1}dx_{2}\cdots dx_{n}}}}&=\frac{b^{n}}{n!}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\frac{(b-a)^{n-k}}{(n-k)!}\frac{a^{k}}{k!}}\\
&=\frac{b^{n}}{n!}-\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\binom{n}{k}(b-a)^{n-k}a^{k}}\\
&=\frac{b^{n}}{n!}-\left[\frac{(b-a+a)^{n}}{n!}-\frac{(b-a)^{n}}{n!}\right]\\
&=\frac{(b-a)^{n}}{n!}.\end{split}

∎

2.1.3
Definite repeated integrals

In this section, we develop a reduction formula for definite repeated integrals which expresses such integrals in terms of definite single integrals. According to Theorem
2.4
, it is enough to determine an expression for the definite integral of
x^{k}f(x)
in function of
k
to compute the
n
-th definite integral of
f(x)
. Hence, the problem is reduced from computing an
n
-th integral to computing a single integral.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
f(x)
be a function defined in the interval
[a,b]
, for any
n\in\mathbb{N^{*}}
, we have

\int_{a}^{b}{\cdots\int_{a}^{x_{3}}{\int_{a}^{x_{2}}{f(x_{1})dx_{1}dx_{2}\cdots dx_{n}}}}=\frac{b^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{\binom{n-1}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{b^{k}}\int_{a}^{b}{x^{k}f(x)\,dx}}.

Proof.

1. Base case: verify true for
n=1
.

\frac{b^{1-1}}{(1-1)!}\sum_{k=0}^{1-1}{\binom{1-1}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{b^{k}}\int_{a}^{b}{x^{k}f(x)\,dx}}=\int_{a}^{b}{f(x)\,dx}.

2. Induction hypothesis: assume the statement is true until
n
.

\int_{a}^{b}{\cdots\int_{a}^{x_{3}}{\int_{a}^{x_{2}}{f(x_{1})dx_{1}dx_{2}\cdots dx_{n}}}}=\frac{b^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{\binom{n-1}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{b^{k}}\int_{a}^{b}{x^{k}f(x)\,dx}}.

3. Induction step: we will show that this statement is true for
(n+1)
.

We have to show the following statement to be true:

\int_{a}^{b}{\cdots\int_{a}^{x_{3}}{\int_{a}^{x_{2}}{f(x_{1})dx_{1}dx_{2}\cdots dx_{n+1}}}}=\frac{b^{n}}{n!}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{\binom{n}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{b^{k}}\int_{a}^{b}{x^{k}f(x)\,dx}}.

To be concise, we denote the left hand side term by
I
. By applying the induction hypothesis,

\begin{split}I&=\int_{a}^{b}{\left[\int_{a}^{x_{n+1}}{\cdots\int_{a}^{x_{3}}{\int_{a}^{x_{2}}{f(x_{1})dx_{1}dx_{2}\cdots dx_{n}}}}\right]dx_{n+1}}\\
&=\int_{a}^{b}{\left[\frac{(x_{n+1})^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{\binom{n-1}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(x_{n+1})^{k}}\int_{a}^{x_{n+1}}{x^{k}f(x)\,dx}}\right]dx_{n+1}}\\
&=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{\binom{n-1}{k}(-1)^{k}\int_{a}^{b}{(x_{n+1})^{n-k-1}\int_{a}^{x_{n+1}}{x^{k}f(x)\,dx}dx_{n+1}}}.\end{split}

Let
t=x_{n+1}
. Setting
du=t^{n-k-1}dt
and
v=\int_{a}^{t}{x^{k}f(x)\,dx}
, then applying integration by parts, we have

\begin{split}\int_{a}^{b}{vdu}=[uv]_{a}^{b}-\int_{a}^{b}{udv}&=\left[\frac{t^{n-k}}{n-k}\int_{a}^{t}{x^{k}f(x)\,dx}\right]_{t=a}^{t=b}-\int_{a}^{b}{\frac{t^{n-k}}{n-k}[t^{k}f(t)]\,dt}\\
&=\frac{b^{n-k}}{n-k}\int_{a}^{b}{x^{k}f(x)\,dx}-\int_{a}^{b}{\frac{t^{n}}{n-k}f(t)\,dt}.\end{split}

Hence, substituting back, we get

\begin{split}I&\resizebox{22609920}{}{$\displaystyle{=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{\binom{n-1}{k}(-1)^{k}\frac{b^{n-k}}{n-k}\int_{a}^{b}{x^{k}f(x)\,dx}}-\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{\binom{n-1}{k}(-1)^{k}\int_{a}^{b}{\frac{t^{n}}{n-k}f(t)\,dt}}}$}\\
&=\frac{b^{n}}{n!}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{\binom{n}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{b^{k}}\left(\int_{a}^{b}{x^{k}f(x)\,dx}\right)}-\left(\int_{a}^{b}{\frac{t^{n}}{n!}f(t)\,dt}\right)\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}}.\end{split}

Noticing that

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}}-(-1)^{n}=-(-1)^{n}

and that

\frac{b^{n}}{n!}\sum_{k=n}^{n}{\binom{n}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{b^{k}}\left(\int_{a}^{b}{x^{k}f(x)\,dx}\right)}=(-1)^{n}\left(\int_{a}^{b}{\frac{t^{n}}{n!}f(t)\,dt}\right),

then substituting back, we obtain the theorem.
∎

Remark
.

Using Theorem
2.4
, we can provide an additional proof for Proposition
2.3
:

\begin{split}\int_{a}^{b}{\cdots\int_{a}^{x_{3}}{\int_{a}^{x_{2}}{1\,dx_{1}dx_{2}\cdots dx_{n}}}}&=\frac{b^{n-1}}{n!}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{\binom{n}{k+1}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{b^{k}}[b^{k+1}-a^{k+1}]}\\
&=\frac{b^{n}}{n!}\left[\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k+1}}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k+1}\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{k}}\right]\\
&=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}b^{n-k}a^{k}}\\
&=\frac{(b-a)^{n}}{n!}.\end{split}

Remark
.

Theorem
2.4
can also be generalized to non-integer orders to obtain:

I_{\alpha,a,b}=\frac{b^{\alpha-1}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\binom{\alpha-1}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{b^{k}}\int_{a}^{b}{x^{k}f(x)\,dx}}

where

\binom{\alpha-1}{k}=\frac{(\alpha-1)\cdots(\alpha-k)}{k!}.

2.2
Recurrent integrals

In this section, we present formulas for computing indefinite and definite recurrent integrals of a function in terms of the primitive of this same function. We also introduce the concept of recurrent primitive and present a generalization of the fundamental theorem of calculus for recurrent integrals. We finish by deriving a few partition identities from this type of integrals.

2.2.1
Indefinite recurrent integrals

Now we prove a formula for expressing indefinite recurrent integrals in terms of single integrals. We can call this the reduction formula for indefinite recurrent integrals.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
f(x)
be a function of
x
, for any
n\in\mathbb{N^{*}}
, we have that

\int{f(x)\cdots\int{f(x)\int{f(x)\,dx^{n}}}}=\frac{1}{n!}\left(\int{f(x)dx}\right)^{n}+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}{c_{i}\frac{1}{i!}\left(\int{f(x)dx}\right)^{i}}.

Remark
.

Using the notation introduced, this theorem can be rewritten as

\int{f(x)\cdots\int{f(x)\int{f(x)\,dx^{n}}}}=\frac{1}{n!}\left(F(x)\right)^{n}+C_{n}(F(x)).

Proof.

Let
X=\int{f(x)\,dx}
and
dX=f(x)\,dx
, we can rewrite the recurrent integral as follows:

\int{f(x)\cdots\int{f(x)\int{f(x)\,dx^{n}}}}=\int{\cdots\int{\int{1\,dX^{n}}}}.

Using Proposition
2.1
, we get

\int{f(x)\cdots\int{f(x)\int{f(x)\,dx^{n}}}}=\frac{X^{n}}{n!}+C_{n}(X)=\frac{1}{n!}\left(\int{f(x)dx}\right)^{n}+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}{c_{i}\frac{1}{i!}\left(\int{f(x)dx}\right)^{i}}.

∎

Example 2.3
.

For the recurrent integral of order
3
of
\sin x
, we have

\begin{split}\int{\sin x\int{\sin x\int{\sin x\,dx}dx}dx}&=\int{\sin x\int{\sin x\left(-\cos x+c_{2}\right)\,dx}dx}\\
&=\int{\sin x\left(\frac{\cos^{2}x}{2}-c_{2}\cos x+c_{1}\right)\,dx}\\
&=-\frac{\cos^{3}x}{6}+c_{2}\frac{\cos^{2}x}{2}-c_{1}\cos x+c_{0}\\
&=\frac{1}{3!}\left(\int{\sin x\,dx}\right)^{3}+\sum_{i=0}^{2}{c_{i}\frac{1}{i!}\left(\int{\sin x\,dx}\right)^{i}}.\end{split}

Example 2.4
.

For the recurrent integral of order
3
of
e^{x}
, we have

\begin{split}\int{e^{x}\int{e^{x}\int{e^{x}\,dx}dx}dx}&=\int{e^{x}\int{e^{x}\left(e^{x}+c_{2}\right)\,dx}dx}\\
&=\int{e^{x}\left(\frac{e^{2x}}{2}+c_{2}e^{x}+c_{1}\right)\,dx}\\
&=\frac{e^{3x}}{6}+c_{2}\frac{e^{2x}}{2}+c_{1}e^{x}+c_{0}\\
&=\frac{1}{3!}\left(\int{e^{x}\,dx}\right)^{3}+\sum_{i=0}^{2}{c_{i}\frac{1}{i!}\left(\int{e^{x}\,dx}\right)^{i}}.\end{split}

2.2.2
Relation between definite and indefinite recurrent integrals

We begin by introducing the concept of recurrent primitive.

Definition
.

We define the recurrent primitive of order
n
of the function
f(x)
, denoted
\rho_{n}(x)
, as the resulting expression of the
n
-th indefinite recurrent integral of
f(x)
excluding the constants of integration (In other words, the case where the constants of integration are zero).

Theorem 2.6
.

The
n
-th recurrent primitive of
f(x)
can be expressed as follows:

\rho_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{n!}\left(\rho_{1}(x)\right)^{n}=\frac{1}{n!}\left(F(x)\right)^{n}.

Proof.

Using the definition of the
n
-th recurrent primitive presented as well as the notation presented, we can see, from Theorem
2.5
, that the
n
-th recurrent primitive of
f(x)
can be expressed as follows:

\rho_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{n!}\left(F(x)\right)^{n}.

Noticing that
\rho_{1}(x)
is simply
F(x)
, the proof is complete.
∎

This definition of
\rho_{n}(x)
will be used in the next section to derive a reduction formula for the definite recurrent integral of a given function
f(x)
.

Now we prove a variant of the fundamental theorem of calculus for recurrent integration. This is needed to prove an expression for definite recurrent integrals in terms of indefinite recurrent integrals.

Lemma 2.2
.

We have that

\int_{a}^{b}{f(x)\rho_{n}(x)\,dx}=\rho_{n+1}(b)-\rho_{n+1}(a).

Proof.

By definition of a recurrent integral,
\rho_{n+1}(x)=\int{f(x)\rho_{n}(x)\,dx}
. Hence,
\rho_{n+1}(x)
is the primitive of
f(x)\rho_{n}(x)
. Therefore, by the fundamental theorem of calculus, we obtain the lemma.
∎

Before we can proceed, we need to present the concept of partition of an integer as partitions are involved in the generalized fundamental theorem of calculus for recurrent integrals. As defined by the author in
[
1
,
2
]
, a partition is defined as follows:

Definition
.

A partition of a non-negative integer
m
is a set of positive integers whose sum equals
m
.
We can represent a partition of
m
as an ordered set
(y_{k,1},\ldots,y_{k,m})
that verifies

y_{k,1}+2y_{k,2}+\cdots+my_{k,m}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}{i\,y_{k,i}}=m.

(7)

The coefficient
y_{k,i}
is the multiplicity of the integer
i
in the
k
-th partition of
m
. Note that
0\leq y_{k,i}\leq m
while
1\leq i\leq m
. Also note that the number of partitions of an integer
m
is given by the partition function denoted
p(n)
. Another way of representing a partition of an integer is with a set
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell})
that satisfies:

\begin{cases}\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{\ell}=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}{\lambda_{i}}=m,\\
\lambda_{i}\leq\lambda_{i+1}.\end{cases}

(8)

Note that the second condition is necessary to ensure a unique representation for each partition of the integer. The
\lambda_{i}
’s are called the parts of this partition and
\ell
is called the length of this given partition. Note that
1\leq\lambda_{i}\leq m
and that the
\lambda_{i}
’s are not necessarily all distinct. Also note that the partitions of an integer do not all have the same length. In terms of the first definition,
\ell
is the sum of the multiplicities:
\ell=\sum_{i=1}^{m}{y_{i}}=y_{1}+\cdots+y_{m}
.
As partitions are not a fundamental part of this article, we will not go into more details. However, for readers interested in a detailed explanation of partitions, see
[
14
]
.

Now that the concept of partition of an integer has been presented, we introduce the generalized fundamental theorem of calculus for recurrent integrals.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
f(x)
be a function defined in the interval
[a,b]
, for any
n\in\mathbb{N^{*}}
, we have

\begin{split}&\int_{a}^{b}{f(x_{n})\int_{a}^{x_{n}}{f(x_{n-1})\cdots\int_{a}^{x_{2}}{f(x_{1})\,dx_{1}\cdots dx_{n}}}}\\
&\,\,\,\,=\sum_{k=1}^{n}{(\rho_{k}(b)-\rho_{k}(a))\sum_{\sum{iy_{i}}=n-k}}{\,\,\prod_{i=1}^{n-k}{(-1)^{y_{i}}(\rho_{i}(a))^{y_{i}}}}.\end{split}

Remark
.

This theorem can also be rewritten using the second partition notation:

\begin{split}\int_{a}^{b}{f(x_{n})\int_{a}^{x_{n}}{f(x_{n-1})\cdots\int_{a}^{x_{2}}{f(x_{1})\,dx_{1}\cdots dx_{n}}}}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum{\lambda_{i}}=n-k\\
\lambda_{i}\leq\lambda_{i+1}\end{subarray}}{(\rho_{k}(b)-\rho_{k}(a))(-1)^{\ell}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}{\rho_{\lambda_{i}}(a)}}}.\end{split}

This sum is over all partitions
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell})
of all integers
n-k
with
1\leq k\leq n
. It is important to note that it is not equivalent to a sum over all partitions
(k,\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell})
of the integer
n
as
k
does not necessarily satisfy
k\leq\lambda_{1}
.

Proof.

1. Base case: verify true for
n=1

\begin{split}\sum_{k=1}^{1}{(\rho_{k}(b)-\rho_{k}(a))\sum_{\sum{iy_{i}}=1-k}}{\,\,\prod_{i=1}^{1-k}{(-1)^{y_{i}}(\rho_{i}(a))^{y_{i}}}}=\rho_{1}(b)-\rho_{1}(a).\end{split}

Likewise, from Lemma
2.2
,

\int_{a}^{b}{f(x_{1})\,dx_{1}}=\int_{a}^{b}{f(x_{1})\rho_{0}(x_{1})\,dx_{1}}=\rho_{1}(b)-\rho_{1}(a).

2. Induction hypothesis: assume the statement is true until
n
.

\begin{split}J_{n,a,b}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}{(\rho_{k}(b)-\rho_{k}(a))\sum_{\sum{iy_{i}}=n-k}}{\,\,\prod_{i=1}^{n-k}{(-1)^{y_{i}}(\rho_{i}(a))^{y_{i}}}}.\end{split}

3. Induction step: we will show that this statement is true for
(n+1)
.

We have to show the following statement to be true:

\begin{split}J_{n+1,a,b}=\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}{(\rho_{k}(b)-\rho_{k}(a))\sum_{\sum{iy_{i}}=n-k+1}}{\,\,\prod_{i=1}^{n-k+1}{(-1)^{y_{i}}(\rho_{i}(a))^{y_{i}}}}.\end{split}

Using the induction hypothesis,

\begin{split}J_{n+1,a,b}&=\int_{a}^{b}{f(x_{n+1})\left(\int_{a}^{x_{n+1}}{f(x_{n})\cdots\int_{a}^{x_{2}}{f(x_{1})\,dx_{1}\cdots dx_{n}}}\right)dx_{n+1}}\\
&=\int_{a}^{b}{f(x_{n+1})\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}{(\rho_{k}(x_{n+1})-\rho_{k}(a))\sum_{\sum{iy_{i}}=n-k}}{\,\,\prod_{i=1}^{n-k}{(-1)^{y_{i}}(\rho_{i}(a))^{y_{i}}}}\right)dx_{n+1}}\\
&=\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\left(\int_{a}^{b}{f(x_{n+1})\rho_{k}(x_{n+1})dx_{n+1}}\right)\sum_{\sum{iy_{i}}=n-k}}{\,\,\prod_{i=1}^{n-k}{(-1)^{y_{i}}(\rho_{i}(a))^{y_{i}}}}\\
&-\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\rho_{k}(a)\left(\int_{a}^{b}{f(x_{n+1})dx_{n+1}}\right)\sum_{\sum{iy_{i}}=n-k}}{\,\,\prod_{i=1}^{n-k}{(-1)^{y_{i}}(\rho_{i}(a))^{y_{i}}}}.\end{split}

From Lemma
2.2
,

\int_{a}^{b}{f(x_{n+1})\rho_{k}(x_{n+1})\,dx_{n+1}}=\rho_{k+1}(b)-\rho_{k+1}(a),

\int_{a}^{b}{f(x_{n+1})\,dx_{n+1}}=\int_{a}^{b}{f(x_{n+1})\rho_{0}(x_{n+1})\,dx_{n+1}}=\rho_{1}(b)-\rho_{1}(a).

Hence,

\begin{split}J_{n+1,a,b}&=\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\left(\rho_{k+1}(b)-\rho_{k+1}(a)\right)\sum_{\sum{iy_{i}}=n-k}}{\,\,\prod_{i=1}^{n-k}{(-1)^{y_{i}}(\rho_{i}(a))^{y_{i}}}}\\
&-\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\rho_{k}(a)\left(\rho_{1}(b)-\rho_{1}(a)\right)\sum_{\sum{iy_{i}}=n-k}}{\,\,\prod_{i=1}^{n-k}{(-1)^{y_{i}}(\rho_{i}(a))^{y_{i}}}}\\
&=\sum_{k=2}^{n+1}{\left(\rho_{k}(b)-\rho_{k}(a)\right)\sum_{\sum{iy_{i}}=n-k+1}}{\,\,\prod_{i=1}^{n-k+1}{(-1)^{y_{i}}(\rho_{i}(a))^{y_{i}}}}\\
&+\left(\rho_{1}(b)-\rho_{1}(a)\right)\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\sum_{\sum{iy_{i}}=n-k}}{(-1)\rho_{k}(a)\,\,\prod_{i=1}^{n-k}{(-1)^{y_{i}}(\rho_{i}(a))^{y_{i}}}}.\end{split}

Let us define a partition
(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n})
such that
Y_{k}=y_{k}+1
and, for
i\neq k
, if
1\leq i\leq n-k
,
Y_{i}=y_{i}
, otherwise,
Y_{i}=0
. Noticing that
\sum{i\,Y_{i}}=\sum{i\,y_{i}}+k=n
, hence,
(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n})
is a partition of
n
for all
k
. Thus, we have

\begin{split}&\left(\rho_{1}(b)-\rho_{1}(a)\right)\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\sum_{\sum{iy_{i}}=n-k}}{(-1)\rho_{k}(a)\,\,\prod_{i=1}^{n-k}{(-1)^{y_{i}}(\rho_{i}(a))^{y_{i}}}}\\
&=\left(\rho_{1}(b)-\rho_{1}(a)\right)\sum_{\sum{iY_{i}}=n}{\,\,\prod_{i=1}^{n}{(-1)^{Y_{i}}(\rho_{i}(a))^{Y_{i}}}}\\
&=\sum_{k=1}^{1}{\left(\rho_{k}(b)-\rho_{k}(a)\right)\sum_{\sum{iY_{i}}=n-k+1}}{\,\,\prod_{i=1}^{n-k+1}{(-1)^{Y_{i}}(\rho_{i}(a))^{Y_{i}}}}.\end{split}

Hence, substituting back, we get the
(n+1)
case and the theorem is proven.
∎

2.2.3
Definite recurrent integrals

In this section, we develop two reduction formulae for definite recurrent integrals: the first involves a sum over partitions while the second does not. Then, in the later section, by equating the expressions from these two formulae, we derive partition identities.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
f(x)
be a function defined in the interval
[a,b]
, for any
n\in\mathbb{N^{*}}
, we have

\begin{split}&\int_{a}^{b}{f(x_{n})\int_{a}^{x_{n}}{f(x_{n-1})\cdots\int_{a}^{x_{2}}{f(x_{1})\,dx_{1}\cdots dx_{n}}}}\\
&\,\,\,\,=\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\frac{(F(b))^{k}-(F(a))^{k}}{k!}(F(a))^{n-k}\sum_{\sum{iy_{i}}=n-k}}{\,\,\prod_{i=1}^{n-k}{\frac{(-1)^{y_{i}}}{i!^{y_{i}}}}}.\end{split}

Remark
.

This theorem can also be expressed using the second partition notation:

\begin{split}&\int_{a}^{b}{f(x_{n})\int_{a}^{x_{n}}{f(x_{n-1})\cdots\int_{a}^{x_{2}}{f(x_{1})\,dx_{1}\cdots dx_{n}}}}\\
&\,\,\,\,=\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum{\lambda_{i}}=n-k\\
\lambda_{i}\leq\lambda_{i+1}\end{subarray}}{\frac{(F(b))^{k}-(F(a))^{k}}{k!}(F(a))^{n-k}(-1)^{\ell}\,\,\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}{\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}!}}}}.\end{split}

Proof.

To be concise, we will represent the recurrent integral by
J_{n,a,b}
. From Theorem
2.7
,

\begin{split}J_{n,a,b}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}{(\rho_{k}(b)-\rho_{k}(a))\sum_{\sum{iy_{i}}=n-k}}{\,\,\prod_{i=1}^{n-k}{(-1)^{y_{i}}(\rho_{i}(a))^{y_{i}}}}.\end{split}

Applying Theorem
2.6
, we get

\begin{split}J_{n,a,b}&=\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\frac{(\rho_{1}(b))^{k}-(\rho_{1}(a))^{k}}{k!}\sum_{\sum{iy_{i}}=n-k}}{\,\,\prod_{i=1}^{n-k}{(-1)^{y_{i}}\frac{(\rho_{1}(a))^{iy_{i}}}{i!^{y_{i}}}}}\\
&=\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\frac{(\rho_{1}(b))^{k}-(\rho_{1}(a))^{k}}{k!}(\rho_{1}(a))^{n-k}\sum_{\sum{iy_{i}}=n-k}}{\,\,\prod_{i=1}^{n-k}{\frac{(-1)^{y_{i}}}{i!^{y_{i}}}}}.\end{split}

Noticing that
\rho_{1}(x)
is simply the primitive
F(x)
of
f(x)
, we get the theorem.
∎

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
f(x)
be a function defined in the interval
[a,b]
, for any
n\in\mathbb{N^{*}}
, we have

\int_{a}^{b}{f(x_{n})\int_{a}^{x_{n}}{f(x_{n-1})\cdots\int_{a}^{x_{2}}{f(x_{1})\,dx_{1}\cdots dx_{n}}}}=\frac{1}{n!}\left(\int_{a}^{b}{f(x)\,dx}\right)^{n}=\frac{\left(F(b)-F(a)\right)^{n}}{n!}.

Proof.

Let
X_{i}=\int{f(x_{i})\,dx_{i}}=F(x_{i})
and
dX_{i}=f(x_{i})\,dx_{i}
, we can rewrite the definite recurrent integral as follows (Notice that the limits of integration should also be modified accordingly):

\int_{a}^{b}{f(x_{n})\int_{a}^{x_{n}}{f(x_{n-1})\cdots\int_{a}^{x_{2}}{f(x_{1})\,dx_{1}\cdots dx_{n}}}}=\int_{F(a)}^{F(b)}{\int_{F(a)}^{X_{n}}{\cdots\int_{F(a)}^{X_{2}}{1\,dX_{1}\cdots dX_{n}}}}.

Using Proposition
2.3
, we get the theorem.
∎

2.2.4
Partition identities derived from recurrent integrals

By equation the expressions of the definite recurrent integral from Theorem
2.8
and Theorem
2.9
, we get the following theorem.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
F(x)
be a function defined in the interval
[a,b]
, for any
n\in\mathbb{N^{*}}
, we have

\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\frac{(F(b))^{k}-(F(a))^{k}}{k!}(F(a))^{n-k}\sum_{\sum{iy_{i}}=n-k}}{\,\,\prod_{i=1}^{n-k}{\frac{(-1)^{y_{i}}}{i!^{y_{i}}}}}=\frac{\left(F(b)-F(a)\right)^{n}}{n!}.

Equivalently, we have

\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum{\lambda_{i}}=n-k\\
\lambda_{i}\leq\lambda_{i+1}\end{subarray}}{\frac{(F(b))^{k}-(F(a))^{k}}{k!}(F(a))^{n-k}(-1)^{\ell}\,\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}{\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}!}}}}=\frac{\left(F(b)-F(a)\right)^{n}}{n!}.

From this general partition identity, we derive some special partition identities.

Corollary 2.1
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N^{*}}
, we have that

\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\sum{iy_{i}}=n-k}}{\,\,\prod_{i=1}^{n-k}{\frac{(-1)^{y_{i}}}{i!^{y_{i}}}}}=\frac{\left(-1\right)^{n+1}}{n!}.

Equivalently, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{k+\lambda_{1}\cdots+\lambda_{\ell}=n}\\
\lambda_{i}\leq\lambda_{i+1}\end{subarray}}{\frac{(-1)^{\ell+1}}{k!\lambda_{1}!\cdots\lambda_{\ell}!}}=\frac{\left(-1\right)^{n}}{n!}.

Proof.

Letting
F(x)
be such that
F(a)=1
and
F(b)=0
, Theorem
2.10
reduces to this corollary. The second equality is obtained by using the second partition notation and combining the two sums into one.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N^{*}}
and any
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
, we have that

\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\frac{b^{k}a^{n-k}}{k!}\sum_{\sum{iy_{i}}=n-k}}{\,\,\prod_{i=1}^{n-k}{\frac{(-1)^{y_{i}}}{i!^{y_{i}}}}}=\frac{(b-a)^{n}-(-a)^{n}}{n!}.

Equivalently, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{k+\lambda_{1}\cdots+\lambda_{\ell}=n}\\
\lambda_{i}\leq\lambda_{i+1}\end{subarray}}{\frac{b^{k}a^{n-k}}{k!}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\lambda_{1}!\cdots\lambda_{\ell}!}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{k+\lambda_{1}\cdots+\lambda_{\ell}=n}\\
\lambda_{i}\leq\lambda_{i+1}\end{subarray}}{\frac{b^{k}}{k!}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}a^{\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{\ell}}}{\lambda_{1}!\cdots\lambda_{\ell}!}}=\frac{(b-a)^{n}-(-a)^{n}}{n!}.

Proof.

Letting
F(x)
be such that
F(a)=a
and
F(b)=b
, from Theorem
2.10
, we have

\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\frac{b^{k}a^{n-k}}{k!}\sum_{\sum{iy_{i}}=n-k}}{\,\,\prod_{i=1}^{n-k}{\frac{(-1)^{y_{i}}}{i!^{y_{i}}}}}-a^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\sum{iy_{i}}=n-k}}{\,\,\prod_{i=1}^{n-k}{\frac{(-1)^{y_{i}}}{i!^{y_{i}}}}}=\frac{\left(b-a\right)^{n}}{n!}.

Applying Corollary
2.1
, we obtain this corollary.
The second equality is obtained by using the second partition notation and combining the two sums into one.
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N^{*}}
and any
b\in\mathbb{C}
, we have that

\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\frac{b^{k}}{k!}\sum_{\sum{iy_{i}}=n-k}}{\,\,\prod_{i=1}^{n-k}{\frac{(-1)^{y_{i}}}{i!^{y_{i}}}}}=\frac{(b-1)^{n}-(-1)^{n}}{n!}.

Equivalently, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{k+\lambda_{1}\cdots+\lambda_{\ell}=n}\\
\lambda_{i}\leq\lambda_{i+1}\end{subarray}}{\frac{b^{k}}{k!}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\lambda_{1}!\cdots\lambda_{\ell}!}}=\frac{(b-1)^{n}-(-1)^{n}}{n!}.

Proof.

By setting
a=1
in Corollary
2.2
, we obtain this corollary.
∎

2.2.5
Generalized recurrent integrals

The recurrent integral structure is one where all functions are the same. It is analogous to the simple recurrent sum (presented in
[
1
]
) where all sequences are the same. However, that last article also presents a more general sum structure where all sequences are distinct. Similarly, we can introduce a generalized recurrent integral structure where all functions are distinct.

Definition
.

We define the general indefinite recurrent integral of order
n
and denote it as:

J_{n}[f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}](x)=\int{f_{n}(x)\int{f_{n-1}(x)\cdots\int{f_{1}(x)\,dx^{n}}}}.

(9)

Definition
.

We define the
n
-th order general definite recurrent integral and denote it as:

J_{n,a,b}[f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}]=\int_{a}^{b}{f_{n}(x_{n})\int_{a}^{x_{n}}{f_{n-1}(x_{n-1})\cdots\int_{a}^{x_{2}}{f_{1}(x_{1})\,dx_{1}\cdots dx_{n}}}}.

(10)

In what follows, we conjecture formulas for simplifying each of the indefinite and definite general recurrent integrals. But first, we define the follow notation:
The permutation group
S_{n}
is the set of all permutations of the set
\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Let
\sigma\in S_{n}
be a permutation of the set
\{1,\ldots,n\}
and let
\sigma(i)
represent the
i
-th element of this given permutation.
Let
C_{n,k}
be the set of all combinations (without repetition) of
k
elements from the set
\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Let
\varepsilon\in C_{n,k}
be a combination of
k
elements from the set
\{1,\ldots,n\}
and let
\varepsilon(i)
represent the
i
-th element of this given combination.

Conjecture 2.1
.

Let
f(x)
be a function of
x
, for any
n\in\mathbb{N^{*}}
, we have that

\begin{split}&\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}{\left(\int{f_{\sigma(n)}(x)\int{f_{\sigma(n-1)}(x)\cdots\int{f_{\sigma(1)}(x)\,dx^{n}}}}\right)}\\
&=\prod_{i=1}^{n}{\left(\int{f_{i}(x)\,dx}\right)}+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{\sum_{\varepsilon\in C_{n,k}}{c_{k,\varepsilon(1),\ldots,\varepsilon(k)}\prod_{i=1}^{k}{\left(\int{f_{\varepsilon(i)}(x)dx}\right)}}}\end{split}

where the
c_{k,\varepsilon(1),\ldots,\varepsilon(k)}
are constants of integration.

Example 2.5
.

For
f_{1}(x)=e^{x}
and
f_{2}(x)=\sin x
, we have

\begin{split}&\int{\sin x\int{e^{x}\,dx^{2}}}+\int{e^{x}\int{\sin x\,dx^{2}}}\\
&=\left(\int{e^{x}}\,dx\right)\left(\int{\sin x}\,dx\right)+c_{1,1}\left(\int{\sin x}\,dx\right)+c_{1,2}\left(\int{e^{x}}\,dx\right)+c_{0}\\
&=-e^{x}\cos x-c_{1,1}\cos x+c_{1,2}e^{x}+c_{0}.\end{split}

Conjecture 2.2
.

Let
f(x)
be a function defined in the interval
[a,b]
, for any
n\in\mathbb{N^{*}}
, we have

\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}{\left(\int_{a}^{b}{f_{\sigma(n)}(x_{n})\int_{a}^{x_{n}}{f_{\sigma(n-1)}(x_{n-1})\cdots\int_{a}^{x_{2}}{f_{\sigma(1)}(x_{1})\,dx_{1}\cdots dx_{n}}}}\right)}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}{\left(\int_{a}^{b}{f_{i}(x)\,dx}\right)}.

Example 2.6
.

For
f_{1}(x)=e^{x}
and
f_{2}(x)=\sin x
, we have

\begin{split}&\int_{a}^{b}{\sin x_{2}\int_{a}^{x_{2}}{e^{x_{1}}\,dx_{1}dx_{2}}}+\int_{a}^{b}{e^{x}_{2}\int_{a}^{x_{2}}{\sin x_{1}\,dx_{1}dx_{2}}}\\
&=\left(\int_{a}^{b}{e^{x}}\,dx\right)\left(\int_{a}^{b}{\sin x}\,dx\right)=-e^{b}\cos b-e^{a}\cos a+e^{b}\cos a+e^{a}\cos b.\end{split}

3
Explicit formula for the
n
-th integral of
x^{m}(\ln x)^{m^{\prime}}

An integral table can be found at the back of any calculus textbook. Such integral tables contain general solutions to some general integrals. However, one integral that cannot be found is the
n
-th order integral of
x^{m}(\ln x)^{m^{\prime}}
,

\int{x^{m}(\ln x)^{m^{\prime}}dx^{n}}.

This integral is of great importance due to its connection with multiple harmonic star sums (MHSSs). In this section, we develop an explicit formula for this general integral. Through the formulas we derive in this section, we further contribute to strengthening the connection between logarithmic integrals and multiple harmonic star sums. We derive two expressions for this integral: the first in terms of shifted MHSSs and the second in terms of a sum over partitions of shifted harmonic sums. Hence, further highlighting the number theoretical nature of this integral. But, before we begin, for the sake of conciseness, let us define the following notation:

\int{f(x)\,dx^{n}}=\int{f(x)\,d^{n}x}=\underbrace{\int\cdots\int}_{n}\,f(x)\,\underbrace{dx\cdots dx}_{n}.

3.1
Lemmas

Before we proceed to prove the needed lemmas, we need to present the gamma function. The gamma function
[
15
,
16
]
, denoted
\Gamma(z)
, is an extension of the factorial to complex numbers excluding the non-positive integers as it satisfies the following property:
\Gamma(z+1)=z\Gamma(z)
. It was developed by Euler and is defined by the following expression:

\Gamma(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}{x^{z-1}e^{-x}\,dx}.

If
z\in\mathbb{N}
, the gamma function reduces back to the following factorial:

\Gamma(z)=(z-1)!.

We will denote by
\mathbb{Z^{-}}
the set of negative integers. Hence,
\Gamma(z+1)
is defined for
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z^{-}}
.

Now that we have introduced the gamma function, we proceed in proving the lemmas. The first lemma consists of proving an expression for a simpler version of the integral.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
m\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{-1\}
and for any
m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that

\int{x^{m}(\ln x)^{m^{\prime}}\,dx}=m^{\prime}!\frac{x^{m+1}}{m+1}\sum_{k=0}^{m^{\prime}}{(-1)^{m^{\prime}-k}\frac{(\ln x)^{k}}{k!}\left(\frac{1}{(m+1)^{m^{\prime}-k}}\right)}+C_{1}.

Proof.

1. Base case: verify true for
m^{\prime}=0
.

\begin{split}0!\frac{x^{m+1}}{m+1}\sum_{k=0}^{0}{(-1)^{-k}\frac{(\ln x)^{k}}{k!}\left(\frac{1}{(m+1)^{-k}}\right)}&=\frac{x^{m+1}}{m+1}=\int{x^{m}\,dx}.\end{split}

2. Induction hypothesis: assume the statement is true until
m^{\prime}
.

\int{x^{m}(\ln x)^{m^{\prime}}\,dx}=m^{\prime}!\frac{x^{m+1}}{m+1}\sum_{k=0}^{m^{\prime}}{(-1)^{m^{\prime}-k}\frac{(\ln x)^{k}}{k!}\left(\frac{1}{(m+1)^{m^{\prime}-k}}\right)}.

3. Induction step: we will show that this statement is true for
(m^{\prime}+1)
.

We have to show the following statement to be true:

\int{x^{m}(\ln x)^{m^{\prime}+1}\,dx}=(m^{\prime}+1)!\frac{x^{m+1}}{m+1}\sum_{k=0}^{m^{\prime}+1}{(-1)^{m^{\prime}-k+1}\frac{(\ln x)^{k}}{k!}\left(\frac{1}{(m+1)^{m^{\prime}-k+1}}\right)}.

We set

\begin{cases}u=(\ln x)^{m^{\prime}+1},&du=(m^{\prime}+1)\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)(\ln x)^{m^{\prime}}\,dx\\
v=\frac{x^{m+1}}{m+1},&dv=x^{m}\,dx.\end{cases}

Applying integration by parts, we get

\begin{split}\int{x^{m}(\ln x)^{m^{\prime}+1}\,dx}&=\frac{x^{m+1}}{m+1}(\ln x)^{m^{\prime}+1}-\frac{m^{\prime}+1}{m+1}\int{x^{m}(\ln x)^{m^{\prime}}\,dx}.\end{split}

Applying the induction hypothesis,

\begin{split}&\int{x^{m}(\ln x)^{m^{\prime}+1}\,dx}\\
&\,\,\,\,=\frac{x^{m+1}}{m+1}(\ln x)^{m^{\prime}+1}-\frac{m^{\prime}+1}{m+1}m^{\prime}!\frac{x^{m+1}}{m+1}\sum_{k=0}^{m^{\prime}}{(-1)^{m^{\prime}-k}\frac{(\ln x)^{k}}{k!}\left(\frac{1}{(m+1)^{m^{\prime}-k}}\right)}\\
&\,\,\,\,=\frac{x^{m+1}}{m+1}(\ln x)^{m^{\prime}+1}+(m^{\prime}+1)!\frac{x^{m+1}}{m+1}\sum_{k=0}^{m^{\prime}}{(-1)^{m^{\prime}-k+1}\frac{(\ln x)^{k}}{k!}\left(\frac{1}{(m+1)^{m^{\prime}-k+1}}\right)}\\
&\,\,\,\,=(m^{\prime}+1)!\frac{x^{m+1}}{m+1}\sum_{k=0}^{m^{\prime}+1}{(-1)^{m^{\prime}-k+1}\frac{(\ln x)^{k}}{k!}\left(\frac{1}{(m+1)^{m^{\prime}-k+1}}\right)}.\end{split}

Hence, the lemma is proven by induction.
∎

In
[
1
]
, the author proved the following theorem (called the variation formula for recurrent sums),

\sum_{N_{m}=q}^{n+1}{\cdots\sum_{N_{2}=q}^{N_{3}}{\sum_{N_{1}=q}^{N_{2}}{a_{N_{m}}\cdots a_{N_{2}}a_{N_{1}}}}}=\sum_{k=0}^{m}{\left(a_{n+1}\right)^{m-k}\left(\sum_{N_{k}=q}^{n}{\cdots\sum_{N_{2}=q}^{N_{3}}{\sum_{N_{1}=q}^{N_{2}}{a_{N_{k}}\cdots a_{N_{2}}a_{N_{1}}}}}\right)}.

(11)

This relation needs to be adapted to a certain form in order for it to be used in the proof of Theorem
3.1
. This needed adaptation is illustrated by the following lemma. This lemma consists of a multiple harmonic star sum identity expressing a MHSS in terms of lower order MHSSs.

Lemma 3.2
.

We have that

\begin{split}&\sum_{N_{m^{\prime}-i}=1+m}^{n+m+1}{\cdots\sum_{N_{2}=1+m}^{N_{3}}{\sum_{N_{1}=1+m}^{N_{2}}{\frac{1}{N_{m^{\prime}-i}}\cdots\frac{1}{N_{2}}\frac{1}{N_{1}}}}}\\
&\,\,\,\,=\sum_{k=i}^{m^{\prime}}{\frac{1}{(m+n+1)^{k-i}}\sum_{N_{m^{\prime}-k}=1+m}^{n+m}{\cdots\sum_{N_{2}=1+m}^{N_{3}}{\sum_{N_{1}=1+m}^{N_{2}}{\frac{1}{N_{m^{\prime}-k}}\cdots\frac{1}{N_{2}}\frac{1}{N_{1}}}}}}.\end{split}

Remark
.

Using the notation, we can write the lemma as follows:

\zeta^{\star}_{1+m,n+m+1}(\{1\}_{m^{\prime}-i})=\sum_{k=i}^{m^{\prime}}{\frac{\zeta^{\star}_{1+m,n+m}(\{1\}_{m^{\prime}-k})}{(n+m+1)^{k-i}}}.

Proof.

From equation (
11
) with
m
substituted by
m^{\prime}-i
, with
a_{N_{k}}=\frac{1}{N_{k}}
, with
q=1+m
, and with
n
substituted by
n+m
, we have that

\begin{split}&\sum_{N_{m^{\prime}-i}=1+m}^{n+m+1}{\cdots\sum_{N_{2}=1+m}^{N_{3}}{\sum_{N_{1}=1+m}^{N_{2}}{\frac{1}{N_{m^{\prime}-i}}\cdots\frac{1}{N_{2}}\frac{1}{N_{1}}}}}\\
&\,\,\,\,=\sum_{k=0}^{m^{\prime}-i}{\frac{1}{\left(m+n+1\right)^{m^{\prime}-k-i}}\left(\sum_{N_{k}=1+m}^{n+m}{\cdots\sum_{N_{2}=1+m}^{N_{3}}{\sum_{N_{1}=1+m}^{N_{2}}{\frac{1}{N_{k}}\cdots\frac{1}{N_{2}}\frac{1}{N_{1}}}}}\right)}.\end{split}

For the sake of conciseness, let us denote the first term as
S
. Substituting
k
by
m^{\prime}-k
, we get

\begin{split}S=\sum_{m^{\prime}-k=m^{\prime}-i}^{0}{\frac{1}{\left(m+n+1\right)^{k-i}}\left(\sum_{N_{m^{\prime}-k}=1+m}^{n+m}{\cdots\sum_{N_{2}=1+m}^{N_{3}}{\sum_{N_{1}=1+m}^{N_{2}}{\frac{1}{N_{m^{\prime}-k}}\cdots\frac{1}{N_{2}}\frac{1}{N_{1}}}}}\right)}.\end{split}

Noticing that the interval
m^{\prime}-i\leq m^{\prime}-k\leq 0
is equivalent to the interval
i\leq k\leq m^{\prime}
,

\begin{split}S=\sum_{k=i}^{m^{\prime}}{\frac{1}{\left(m+n+1\right)^{k-i}}\left(\sum_{N_{m^{\prime}-k}=1+m}^{n+m}{\cdots\sum_{N_{2}=1+m}^{N_{3}}{\sum_{N_{1}=1+m}^{N_{2}}{\frac{1}{N_{m^{\prime}-k}}\cdots\frac{1}{N_{2}}\frac{1}{N_{1}}}}}\right)}.\end{split}

The relation is proven.
∎

3.2
Theorems

Now that the needed lemmas have been proven, we can develop the following explicit expression for the
n
-th integral of
x^{m}(\ln x)^{m^{\prime}}
in terms of a shifted multiple harmonic star sum.

Theorem 3.1
.

For any
m\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z^{-}}
, any
m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
, and any
n\in\mathbb{N^{*}}
, we have that

\int{x^{m}(\ln x)^{m^{\prime}}d^{n}x}=(m^{\prime})!\Gamma(m+1)\frac{x^{n+m}}{\Gamma(n+m+1)}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{m^{\prime}}{(-1)^{m^{\prime}-k}\frac{(\ln x)^{k}}{k!}\zeta_{1+m,n+m}^{\star}(\{1\}_{m^{\prime}-k})}\right]+C_{n}

where

\zeta^{\star}_{1+m,n+m}(\{1\}_{m^{\prime}-k})=\sum_{N_{m^{\prime}-k}=1+m}^{n+m}{\cdots\sum_{N_{2}=1+m}^{N_{3}}{\sum_{N_{1}=1+m}^{N_{2}}{\frac{1}{N_{m^{\prime}-k}}\cdots\frac{1}{N_{2}}\frac{1}{N_{1}}}}}.

Remark
.

If
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we replace the gamma function by the corresponding factorial.

\int{x^{m}(\ln x)^{m^{\prime}}d^{n}x}=(m)!(m^{\prime})!\frac{x^{n+m}}{(n+m)!}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{m^{\prime}}{(-1)^{m^{\prime}-k}\frac{(\ln x)^{k}}{k!}\zeta^{\star}_{1+m,n+m}(\{1\}_{m^{\prime}-k})}\right]+C_{n}.

Remark
.

If
m\in\mathbb{Z^{-}}
but
(n+m)<0
, by taking the limit of the gamma functions ratio,

\int{x^{m}(\ln x)^{m^{\prime}}d^{n}x}=(m^{\prime})!\frac{x^{n+m}}{(m+1)\cdots(n+m)}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{m^{\prime}}{(-1)^{m^{\prime}-k}\frac{(\ln x)^{k}}{k!}\zeta^{\star}_{1+m,n+m}(\{1\}_{m^{\prime}-k})}\right]+C_{n}.

Remark
.

As the reason for the constants of integration represented by
C_{n}
is trivial, we will neglect this term in the proof of the theorem for simplicity.

Proof.

1. Base case: verify true for
n=1
.

\begin{split}&\Gamma(m+1)(m^{\prime})!\frac{x^{1+m}}{\Gamma(2+m)}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{m^{\prime}}{(-1)^{m^{\prime}-k}\frac{(\ln x)^{k}}{k!}\zeta_{1+m,1+m}^{\star}(\{1\}_{m^{\prime}-k})}\right]\\
&=m^{\prime}!\frac{x^{m+1}}{m+1}\sum_{k=0}^{m^{\prime}}{(-1)^{m^{\prime}-k}\frac{(\ln x)^{k}}{k!}\left(\frac{1}{(m+1)^{m^{\prime}-k}}\right)}.\end{split}

Likewise, from Lemma
3.1
,

\int{x^{m}(\ln x)^{m^{\prime}}\,dx}=m^{\prime}!\frac{x^{m+1}}{m+1}\sum_{k=0}^{m^{\prime}}{(-1)^{m^{\prime}-k}\frac{(\ln x)^{k}}{k!}\left(\frac{1}{(m+1)^{m^{\prime}-k}}\right)}.

2. Induction hypothesis: assume the statement is true until
n
.

\int{x^{m}(\ln x)^{m^{\prime}}d^{n}x}=\Gamma(m+1)(m^{\prime})!\frac{x^{n+m}}{\Gamma(n+m+1)}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{m^{\prime}}{(-1)^{m^{\prime}-k}\frac{(\ln x)^{k}}{k!}\zeta_{1+m,n+m}^{\star}(\{1\}_{m^{\prime}-k})}\right].

3. Induction step: we will show that this statement is true for
(n+1)
.

We have to show the following statement to be true

\int{x^{m}(\ln x)^{m^{\prime}}d^{n+1}x}=\Gamma(m+1)(m^{\prime})!\frac{x^{n+m+1}}{\Gamma(n+m+2)}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{m^{\prime}}{(-1)^{m^{\prime}-k}\frac{(\ln x)^{k}}{k!}\zeta_{1+m,n+m+1}^{\star}(\{1\}_{m^{\prime}-k})}\right].

Using the induction hypothesis,

\begin{split}\int{x^{m}(\ln x)^{m^{\prime}}d^{n+1}x}&=\int{\left(\int{x^{m}(\ln x)^{m^{\prime}}d^{n}x}\right)dx}\\
&=\int{\Gamma(m+1)\frac{(m^{\prime})!x^{n+m}}{\Gamma(n+m+1)}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{m^{\prime}}{(-1)^{m^{\prime}-k}\frac{(\ln x)^{k}}{k!}\zeta_{1+m,n+m}^{\star}(\{1\}_{m^{\prime}-k})}\right]dx}\\
&=\frac{\Gamma(m+1)(m^{\prime})!}{\Gamma(n+m+1)}\sum_{k=0}^{m^{\prime}}{\frac{(-1)^{m^{\prime}-k}}{k!}\zeta_{1+m,n+m}^{\star}(\{1\}_{m^{\prime}-k})}\left(\int{x^{n+m}(\ln x)^{k}\,dx}\right).\end{split}

Using Lemma
3.1
, we have

\int{x^{n+m}(\ln x)^{k}\,dx}=k!\frac{x^{m+n+1}}{m+n+1}\sum_{i=0}^{k}{(-1)^{k-i}\frac{(\ln x)^{i}}{i!}\left(\frac{1}{(m+n+1)^{k-i}}\right)}.

Substituting back and simplifying, we get

\begin{split}\int{x^{m}(\ln x)^{m^{\prime}}\,dx}=\frac{m^{\prime}!\Gamma(m+1)x^{m+n+1}}{\Gamma(m+n+2)}\sum_{k=0}^{m^{\prime}}{\sum_{i=0}^{k}{(-1)^{m^{\prime}-i}\frac{(\ln x)^{i}}{i!}\left\{\frac{\zeta_{1+m,n+m}^{\star}(\{1\}_{m^{\prime}-k})}{(m+n+1)^{k-i}}\right\}}}.\end{split}

Now we need to invert the order of summation. From Theorem 3.1 of
[
1
]
, we have

\sum_{k=0}^{m^{\prime}}{b_{k}\sum_{i=0}^{k}{a_{i}}}=\sum_{i=0}^{m^{\prime}}{a_{i}\sum_{k=i}^{m^{\prime}}{b_{k}}}.

Let

a_{i}=(-1)^{m^{\prime}-i}\frac{(\ln x)^{i}}{i!}\frac{1}{(m+n+1)^{-i}},\,\,\,\,b_{k}=\frac{1}{(m+n+1)^{k}}\zeta_{1+m,n+m}^{\star}(\{1\}_{m^{\prime}-k}).

Hence, applying this formula to interchange the order of summation, we get

\begin{split}&\sum_{k=0}^{m^{\prime}}{\sum_{i=0}^{k}{(-1)^{m^{\prime}-i}\frac{(\ln x)^{i}}{i!}\left\{\frac{\zeta_{1+m,n+m}^{\star}(\{1\}_{m^{\prime}-k})}{(m+n+1)^{k-i}}\right\}}}\\
&=\sum_{i=0}^{m^{\prime}}{(-1)^{m^{\prime}-i}\frac{(\ln x)^{i}}{i!}\frac{1}{(m+n+1)^{-i}}\sum_{k=i}^{m^{\prime}}{\frac{\zeta_{1+m,n+m}^{\star}(\{1\}_{m^{\prime}-k})}{(m+n+1)^{k}}}}\\
&=\sum_{i=0}^{m^{\prime}}{(-1)^{m^{\prime}-i}\frac{(\ln x)^{i}}{i!}\sum_{k=i}^{m^{\prime}}{\frac{\zeta_{1+m,n+m}^{\star}(\{1\}_{m^{\prime}-k})}{(m+n+1)^{k-i}}}}.\end{split}

Applying Lemma
3.2
, we get

\begin{split}\sum_{k=0}^{m^{\prime}}{\sum_{i=0}^{k}{(-1)^{m^{\prime}-i}\frac{(\ln x)^{i}}{i!}\left\{\frac{\zeta_{1+m,n+m}^{\star}(\{1\}_{m^{\prime}-k})}{(m+n+1)^{k-i}}\right\}}}=\sum_{i=0}^{m^{\prime}}{(-1)^{m^{\prime}-i}\frac{(\ln x)^{i}}{i!}\left\{\zeta_{1+m,n+m+1}^{\star}(\{1\}_{m^{\prime}-i})\right\}}.\end{split}

Substituting back into the expression of the integral, we get

\int{x^{m}(\ln x)^{m^{\prime}}d^{n+1}x}=\Gamma(m+1)(m^{\prime})!\frac{x^{n+m+1}}{\Gamma(n+m+2)}\left[\sum_{i=0}^{m^{\prime}}{(-1)^{m^{\prime}-i}\frac{(\ln x)^{i}}{i!}\zeta_{1+m,n+m+1}^{\star}(\{1\}_{m^{\prime}-i})}\right].

Hence, the case for
(n+1)
is proven. Thus, the relation is proven by induction.
∎

Before we proceed to prove a second expression for this logarithmic integral, we present a few corollaries that will be useful later.

Corollary 3.1
.

For any
m\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{-1\}
, we have that

\int{x^{m}\ln x\,dx}=\frac{x^{m+1}}{m+1}\left[\ln x-\frac{1}{m+1}\right]+C_{1}.

Corollary 3.2
.

For any
m\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z^{-}}
and for any
n\in\mathbb{N^{*}}
, we have that

\int{x^{m}\ln x\,d^{n}x}=\Gamma(m+1)\frac{x^{n+m}}{\Gamma(n+m+1)}\left[\ln x-\sum_{N=1+m}^{n+m}{\frac{1}{N}}\right]+C_{n}.

Remark
.

If
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we replace the gamma function by the corresponding factorial.

\int{x^{m}\ln x\,d^{n}x}=m!\frac{x^{n+m}}{(n+m)!}\left[\ln x-\sum_{N=1+m}^{n+m}{\frac{1}{N}}\right]+C_{n}.

Remark
.

If
m\in\mathbb{Z^{-}}
but
(n+m)<0
, by taking the limit of the gamma functions ratio,

\int{x^{m}\ln x\,d^{n}x}=\frac{x^{n+m}}{(n+m)\cdots(m+1)}\left[\ln x-\sum_{N=1+m}^{n+m}{\frac{1}{N}}\right]+C_{n}.

Using the reduction theorem for recurrent sums presented in
[
1
]
, we can develop the following explicit expression for the
n
-th integral of
x^{m}(\ln x)^{m^{\prime}}
in terms of a sum over partitions.

Theorem 3.2
.

For any
m\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z^{-}}
, any
m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
, and any
n\in\mathbb{N^{*}}
, we have that

\int{x^{m}(\ln x)^{m^{\prime}}d^{n}x}=\frac{(m^{\prime})!\Gamma(m+1)x^{n+m}}{\Gamma(n+m+1)}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{m^{\prime}}{(-1)^{m^{\prime}-k}\frac{(\ln x)^{k}}{k!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\\
\sum{i\,y_{k,i}}={m^{\prime}-k}\end{subarray}}{\prod_{i=1}^{m^{\prime}-k}{\frac{1}{y_{k,i}!i^{y_{k,i}}}\left(\sum_{N=1+m}^{n+m}{\frac{1}{N^{i}}}\right)^{y_{k,i}}}}}\right]+C_{n}.

Remark
.

If
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we replace the gamma function by the corresponding factorial.

\int{x^{m}(\ln x)^{m^{\prime}}d^{n}x}=(m)!(m^{\prime})!\frac{x^{n+m}}{(n+m)!}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{m^{\prime}}{(-1)^{m^{\prime}-k}\frac{(\ln x)^{k}}{k!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\\
\sum{i\,y_{k,i}}={m^{\prime}-k}\end{subarray}}{\prod_{i=1}^{m^{\prime}-k}{\frac{1}{y_{k,i}!i^{y_{k,i}}}\left(\sum_{N=1+m}^{n+m}{\frac{1}{N^{i}}}\right)^{y_{k,i}}}}}\right]+C_{n}.

Remark
.

If
m\in\mathbb{Z^{-}}
but
(n+m)<0
, by taking the limit of the gamma functions ratio,

\int{x^{m}(\ln x)^{m^{\prime}}d^{n}x}=\frac{(m^{\prime})!x^{n+m}}{(n+m)\cdots(m+1)}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{m^{\prime}}{(-1)^{m^{\prime}-k}\frac{(\ln x)^{k}}{k!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\\
\sum{i\,y_{k,i}}={m^{\prime}-k}\end{subarray}}{\prod_{i=1}^{m^{\prime}-k}{\frac{1}{y_{k,i}!i^{y_{k,i}}}\left(\sum_{N=1+m}^{n+m}{\frac{1}{N^{i}}}\right)^{y_{k,i}}}}}\right]+C_{n}.

Proof.

In
[
1
]
, the author proved the following reduction formula,

\sum_{N_{m}=q}^{n}{\cdots\sum_{N_{1}=q}^{N_{2}}{a_{N_{m}}\cdots a_{N_{1}}}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\\
\sum{i.y_{k,i}}=m\end{subarray}}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}{\frac{1}{(y_{k,i})!}\left(\frac{1}{i}\sum_{N=q}^{n}{(a_{N})^{i}}\right)^{y_{k,i}}}}.

Applying this formula (with
m
substituted by
m^{\prime}
,
n
substituted by
n+m
,
q=1+m
, and
a_{N_{k}}=\frac{1}{N_{k}}
) to Theorem
3.1
, we obtain this theorem.
∎

Logarithms and logarithmic integrals have always held a special place in number theory. The greatest symbol of this connection is the prime number theorem. This connection is further highlighted here as a logarithmic integral was shown to be related to two major number theoretical concepts: MHSSs in Theorem
3.1
and partitions in Theorem
3.2
.

Although it is not of major importance, we can use the theorems previously developed for definite repeated integrals to develop some formulae for the
n
-th definite integral of
x^{m}(\ln x)^{m^{\prime}}
.

Definition
.

We define
L_{n,m,m^{\prime}}(x)
as the
n
-th primitive of
x^{m}(\ln x)^{m^{\prime}}
whose expression can be seen from Theorem
3.1
or Theorem
3.2
.

Theorem 3.3
.

For any
m\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z^{-}}
, any
m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
, and any
n\in\mathbb{N^{*}}
, we have that

\int_{a}^{b}{\cdots\int_{a}^{x_{3}}{\int_{a}^{x_{2}}{x_{1}^{m}(\ln(x_{1}))^{m^{\prime}}dx_{1}dx_{2}\cdots dx_{n}}}}=L_{n,m,m^{\prime}}(b)-\sum_{k=1}^{n}{(b-a)^{n-k}L_{k,m,m^{\prime}}(a)}.

Proof.

By applying Theorem
2.1

, we get this theorem.

4
Application to the expression of harmonic sums

In this section, we use the connection between logarithmic integrals and harmonic sums established in the previous section to prove some results related to the harmonic sum, repeated harmonic sum, and binomial-harmonic sum.

4.1
An alternating expression for the harmonic sum

In this section, we derive a new expression for the harmonic sum as an alternating sum. We present a particularly original proof for this expression using the logarithmic integral from Corollary
3.2
.

We begin by proving the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N^{*}}
and any
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that

\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}(x^{n+m}\ln x)=\frac{(n+m)!}{m!}x^{m}\ln x+n!x^{m}\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{k}\binom{m+n}{m+k}}.

Proof.

Let
u=x^{n+m}
,
v=\ln x
.
From Leibniz’s theorem, we get

\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}\left(x^{n+m}\ln x\right)=\left(uv\right)^{(n)}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{\binom{n}{k}u^{(n-k)}v^{(k)}}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{\binom{n}{k}[x^{n+m}]^{(n-k)}[\ln x]^{(k)}}.

For
k=0
,
\binom{n}{k}[x^{n+m}]^{(n-k)}[\ln x]^{(k)}=\frac{(n+m)!}{m!}x^{m}\ln x
. Hence,

\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}\left(x^{n+m}\ln x\right)=\frac{(n+m)!}{m!}x^{m}\ln x+\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\binom{n}{k}[x^{n+m}]^{(n-k)}[\ln x]^{(k)}}.

We also know the following:

\forall z\in\mathbb{N},[x^{n+m}]^{(z)}=\frac{(m+n)!}{(m+n-z)!}x^{m+n-z},\text{ hence, }[x^{n+m}]^{(n-k)}=\frac{(m+n)!}{(m+k)!}x^{m+k}.

\forall k\in\mathbb{N^{*}},v^{(k)}=\frac{(-1)^{k+1}(k-1)!}{x^{k}}.

Hence, by substituting back, we get

\begin{split}\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}\left(x^{n+m}\ln x\right)&=\frac{(n+m)!}{m!}x^{m}\ln x+\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\binom{n}{k}\left[\frac{(m+n)!}{(m+k)!}x^{m+k}\right]\left[\frac{(-1)^{k+1}(k-1)!}{x^{k}}\right]}\\
&=\frac{(n+m)!}{m!}x^{m}\ln x+x^{m}\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{k}\frac{(m+n)!n!}{(m+k)!(n-k)!}}\\
&=\frac{(n+m)!}{m!}x^{m}\ln x+n!x^{m}\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{k}\binom{m+n}{m+k}}.\end{split}

The formula is proven.
∎

Using the lemma, we develop an alternating sum expression for the harmonic sum.

Theorem 4.1
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N^{*}}
and any
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that

\binom{m+n}{m}\sum_{k=1+m}^{n+m}{\frac{1}{k}}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{k}\binom{m+n}{m+k}}.

Proof.

From Corollary
3.2
, we have that

\int{x^{m}\ln x\,dx^{n}}=m!\frac{x^{n+m}}{(n+m)!}\left[\ln x-\sum_{N=1+m}^{n+m}{\frac{1}{N}}\right]+C_{n}.

We rearrange the terms as follows,

x^{n+m}\sum_{N=1+m}^{n+m}{\frac{1}{N}}=x^{n+m}\ln x-\frac{(n+m)!}{m!}\int{x^{m}\ln x\,dx^{n}}+C_{n}.

By differentiating
n
times, we get

\left(\sum_{N=1+m}^{n+m}{\frac{1}{N}}\right)\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}(x^{n+m})=\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}(x^{n+m}\ln x)-\frac{(n+m)!}{m!}\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}\left(\int{x^{m}\ln x\,dx^{n}}\right)+\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}(C_{n}).

We know the following:

\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}(C_{n})=0,\,\,\,\,\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}(x^{n+m})=\frac{(n+m)!}{m!}x^{m},\,\,\,\,\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}\left(\int{x^{m}\ln x\,dx^{n}}\right)=x^{m}\ln x,

and, from Lemma
4.1
,

\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}(x^{n+m}\ln x)=\frac{(n+m)!}{m!}x^{m}\ln x+n!x^{m}\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{k}\binom{m+n}{m+k}}.

Hence, substituting back, we get

\begin{split}\left(\sum_{N=1+m}^{n+m}{\frac{1}{N}}\right)\frac{(n+m)!}{m!}x^{m}&=\frac{(n+m)!}{m!}x^{m}\ln x+n!x^{m}\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{k}\binom{m+n}{m+k}}-\frac{(n+m)!}{m!}x^{m}\ln x\\
&=n!x^{m}\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{k}\binom{m+n}{m+k}}.\end{split}

Dividing both sides by
n!x^{m}
, we get the theorem.
∎

Corollary 4.1
.

For
m=0
, Theorem
4.1
becomes

\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\frac{1}{k}}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{k}\binom{n}{k}}.

\sum_{N_{k}=1}^{n}{\cdots\sum_{N_{1}=1}^{N_{2}}{\left[\binom{N_{1}+m}{m}\sum_{i=1+m}^{N_{1}+m}{\frac{1}{i}}\right]}}=\binom{n+m+k}{m+k}\sum_{i=1+m+k}^{n+m+k}{\frac{1}{i}}.

4.2.4
Divergence of the alternating series

The harmonic series was proven to diverge by several mathematicians including Nicole Oresme
[
17
]
, Pietro Mengoli
[
18
]
, Johann Bernoulli
[
19
]
, and Jacob Bernoulli
[
20
,
21
]
. Twenty additional proofs can also be found in
[
22
]
. In this section, we use this fact to proof the divergence of the alternating series presented in this section.

Theorem 4.5
.

For any
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\left[\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{k}\binom{m+n}{m+k}}\right]}=\infty.

Proof.

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\binom{n+m}{m}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}{\frac{(n+m)!}{m!n!}}=\frac{1}{m!}\lim_{n\to\infty}{(n+m)\cdots(n+1)}=\infty.

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\sum_{k=1+m}^{n+m}{\frac{1}{k}}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}{\left[\sum_{k=1}^{n+m}{\frac{1}{k}}-\sum_{k=1}^{m}{\frac{1}{k}}\right]}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}{\frac{1}{k}}-\sum_{k=1}^{m}{\frac{1}{k}}=\infty.

Hence, by using Theorem
4.1
, we prove that this alternating series diverges.
∎

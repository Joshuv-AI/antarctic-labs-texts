Introduction

Let
\mathbb{N}
be the set of nonnegative integers and let
A
be a subset of nonnegative integers.
We use
A^{n}
to denote the Cartesian product of
n
sets
A
, that is,

A^{n}=\{(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}):~a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}\in A\}.

Let

R_{A,k}(n)=|\{(a_{1},a_{2},\dots{},a_{k})\in A^{n}:a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{k}=n\}|,

R^{<}_{A,k}(n)=|\{(a_{1},a_{2},\dots{},a_{k})\in A^{n}:a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{k}=n,a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots{}<a_{k}\}|,

R^{\leq}_{A,k}(n)=|\{(a_{1},a_{2},\dots{}a_{k})\in A^{n}:a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{k}=n,a_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq\cdots{}\leq a_{k}\}|,

where
|.|
denotes the cardinality of a finite set. We say that
R_{A,k}(n)
is monotonous increasing in
n
from a certain point on (or eventually monotone increasing), if there exists an integer
n_{0}
such that
R_{A,k}(n+1)\geq R_{A,k}(n)
for all integers
n\geq n_{0}
.

We define the monotonicity of the two other representation functions
R^{<}_{A,k}(n)
and
R^{\leq}_{A,k}(n)
in the same way. We denote the counting function of the set
A
by

A(n)=\sum_{\overset{a\in\mathcal{A}}{a\leq n}}1.

We define the lower asymptotic density of a set
A
of natural numbers by

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{A(n)}{n}

and the asymptotic density by

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{A(n)}{n}

whenever the limit exists. The generating function of a set
A
of natural numbers is denoted by

G_{A}(x)=\sum_{a\in A}x^{a}.

Obviously, if
\mathbb{N}\setminus A
is finite, then each of the functions
R_{A,2}(n),R^{<}_{A,2}(n)
and

R^{\leq}_{A,2}(n)
is eventually monotone increasing. In
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
, Erdős, Sárközy and V.T. Sós investigated whether there exists a set
A
for which
\mathbb{N}\setminus A
is infinite and the representation functions are monotone increasing from a certain point on. Namely, they proved the following theorems.

Theorem A
.

The function
R_{A,2}(n)
is monotonous increasing from a certain point on, if and only if the sequence
A
contains all the integers
from a certain point on, i.e., there exists an integer
n_{1}
with

A\cap\{n_{1},n_{1}+1,n_{1}+2,\dots{}\}=\{n_{1},n_{1}+1,n_{1}+2,\dots{}\}.

Theorem B
.

There exists an infinite set
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}
such that

A(n)<n-cn^{1/3}
for
n>n_{0}
and
R^{<}_{A,2}(n)
is monotone increasing from a certain point on.

Theorem C
.

If

A(n)=o\Big(\frac{n}{\log n}\Big),

then the functions
R^{<}_{A,2}(n)
and

R^{\leq}_{A,2}(n)
cannot be monotonous increasing in
n
from a certain point on.

Theorem D
.

If
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}
is an infinite set with

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n-A(n)}{\log n}=\infty,

then
R^{\leq}_{A,2}(n)
cannot be monotone increasing from a certain point on.

The latter theorem was proved by Balasubramanian
[
2
]
independently.

Very little is known when
k>2
. Many years ago, it was proved in
[
9
]
and independently in
[
7
]
that

Theorem E
.

If
k
is an integer with
k>2
,
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}
and

R_{A,k}(n)
is monotonous increasing in
n
from a certain point on,
then

A(n)=o\bigg(\frac{n^{2/k}}{(\log n)^{2/k}}\bigg)

cannot hold.

Furthermore, Dombi
[
4
]
constructed sets
A
of asymptotic density
\frac{1}{2}
such that for
k>4
, the function
R_{A,k}(n)
is monotone increasing from a certain point on. His constructions are based on the Rudin-Shapiro sets and Thue-Morse sequences. On the other hand, Dombi gave the following
conjecture.

Dombi’s Conjecture.
If
\mathbb{N}\setminus A
is infinite, then
R_{A,k}(n)
cannot be strictly increasing.

For
k\geq 3
, Bell and Shallit recently gave a counterexample
[
3
]
of Dombi’s conjecture by applying tools from automata theory and logic. They proved the following result.

Theorem F
.

Let
k
be integer with
k\geq 3
and let
F\subseteq\mathbb{N}
with
0\notin F
. If
F(n)=o(n^{\alpha})
for
\alpha<(k-2)/k
and
A=\mathbb{N}\setminus F
, then
R_{A,k}(n)
is eventually strictly increasing.

In this paper, we improve their result in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
k
be an integer with
k\geq 3
. If
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}
satisfies

A(n)\leqslant\frac{n^{\frac{k-2}{k-1}}}{\sqrt[k-1]{(k-2)!}}-2

for all sufficiently large integers
n
, then
R_{\mathbb{N}\setminus A,k}(n)
is eventually strictly increasing.

In particular, for
k=3
we have the following Corollary.

Corollary 1.2
.

If
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}
satisfies
A(n)\leqslant\sqrt{n}-2

for all sufficiently large integers
n
, then
R_{\mathbb{N}\setminus A,3}(n)
is eventually strictly increasing.

We improve the constant factor above in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}
satisfies
A(n)\leqslant\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\sqrt{n}-2
for all sufficiently large integers
n
, then
R_{\mathbb{N}\setminus A,3}(n)
is eventually strictly increasing.

It turns out from the next theorem that the upper bound for the counting function of
A
in Theorem 1.1 is tight up to a constant factor.

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose that
f(n)
be a function satisfying
f(n)\rightarrow\infty

as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Then
there is a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}
such that
A(n)<\sqrt[k-1]{k-1}n^{\frac{k-2}{k-1}}+f(n)
for
all sufficiently large integers
n
and
R_{\mathbb{N}\setminus A,k}(n)<R_{\mathbb{N}\setminus A,k}(n-1)

for infinitely many positive integers
n
.

Note that Shallit
[
8
]
recently constructed a set
A
with positive lower asymptotic density such that the function
R_{\mathbb{N}\setminus A,3}(n)

is strictly increasing.

Proofs

The proofs of theorems are based on the next lemma, coming from Bell and Shallit’s paper
[
3
]
yet not explicitly stated there.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any positive integers
n
and
k
with
k\geq 3
, we have

\displaystyle R_{\mathbb{N}\setminus A,k}(n)-R_{\mathbb{N}\setminus A,k}(n-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\binom{n+k-2}{k-2}+\sum_{i=1}^{k-2}\binom{k}{i}(-1)^{i}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{n}\binom{m+k-i-2}{k-i-2}R_{A,i}(n-m)\right)

\displaystyle+(-1)^{k-1}kR_{A,k-1}(n)+(-1)^{k}(R_{A,k}(n)-R_{A,k}(n-1)).

Proof of Lemma 2.1.

Clearly,

\displaystyle(1-x)(G_{\mathbb{N}\setminus A}(x))^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}R_{\mathbb{N}\setminus A,k}(n)x^{n}-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}R_{\mathbb{N}\setminus A,k}(n)x^{n+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R_{\mathbb{N}\setminus A,k}(0)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(R_{\mathbb{N}\setminus A,k}(n)-R_{\mathbb{N}\setminus A,k}(n-1))x^{n}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle(1-x)((G_{\mathbb{N}\setminus A)}(x))^{k}=(1-x)\left(\frac{1}{1-x}-G_{A}(x)\right)^{k}=(1-x)\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{k}{i}\frac{(-1)^{i}}{(1-x)^{k-i}}G_{A}(x)^{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-x)^{k-1}}+\sum_{i=1}^{k-2}\binom{k}{i}\frac{(-1)^{i}}{(1-x)^{k-i-1}}G_{A}(x)^{i}+(-1)^{k-1}kG_{A}(x)^{k-1}+(-1)^{k}(1-x)G_{A}(x)^{k}.

It is well known that

\frac{1}{(1-x)^{m}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{n+m-1}{m-1}x^{n}.

It follows that

\displaystyle R_{\mathbb{N}\setminus A,k}(0)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(R_{\mathbb{N}\setminus A,k}(n)-R_{\mathbb{N}\setminus A,k}(n-1))x^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{n+k-2}{k-2}x^{n}+\sum_{i=1}^{k-2}(-1)^{i}\binom{k}{i}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{n}\binom{m+k-i-2}{k-i-2}R_{A,i}(n-m)\right)x^{n}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{k-1}k\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}R_{A,k-1}(n)x^{n}+(-1)^{k}R_{A,k}(0)+(-1)^{k}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(R_{A,k}(n)-R_{A,k}(n-1))x^{n}.

Comparing the coefficient of
x^{n}
on both sides of the equation, Lemma 2.1 follows immediately.
∎

Proof of Theorem 1.1.

Clearly,

\displaystyle R_{A,i}(n)=|\{(a_{1},a_{2},\dots{},a_{i})\in A^{i}:a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots{}+a_{i}=n\}|

\displaystyle\leq|\{(a_{1},a_{2},\dots{},a_{i-1})\in A^{i-1}:a_{1},a_{2},\dots{},a_{i-1}\leq n\}|=A(n)^{i-1}.

Then by Lemma 1, there exist constants
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4}
only depending on
k
such that

\displaystyle R_{\mathbb{N}\setminus A,k}(n)-R_{\mathbb{N}\setminus A,k}(n-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\binom{n+k-2}{k-2}+\sum_{i=1}^{k-2}\binom{k}{i}(-1)^{i}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{n}\binom{m+k-i-2}{k-i-2}R_{A,i}(n-m)\right)

\displaystyle+(-1)^{k-1}kR_{A,k-1}(n)+(-1)^{k}(R_{A,k}(n)-R_{A,k}(n-1))

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{n^{k-2}}{(k-2)!}-\sum_{i=1}^{k-2}2^{k}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\binom{m+k-i-2}{k-i-2}A(n)^{i-1}-kR_{A,k-1}(n)-A(n)^{k-1}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{n^{k-2}}{(k-2)!}-\sum_{i=1}^{k-2}2^{k}A(n)^{i-1}\binom{n+k-i-1}{k-i-2}-k\bigg(\frac{n^{\frac{k-2}{k-1}}}{\sqrt[k-1]{(k-2)!}}\bigg)^{k-2}-\bigg(\frac{n^{\frac{k-2}{k-1}}}{\sqrt[k-1]{(k-2)!}}-2\bigg)^{k-1}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{n^{k-2}}{(k-2)!}-c_{1}\sum_{i=1}^{k-2}A(n)^{i-1}n^{k-i-2}-k\cdot\frac{n^{\frac{(k-2)^{2}}{k-1}}}{((k-2)!)^{\frac{k-2}{k-1}}}

\displaystyle-\bigg(\frac{n^{k-2}}{(k-2)!}-2(k-1)\frac{n^{\frac{(k-2)^{2}}{k-1}}}{((k-2)!)^{\frac{k-2}{k-1}}}+c_{2}n^{\frac{(k-2)(k-3)}{k-1}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{n^{k-2}}{(k-2)!}-c_{3}n^{k-3}-k\frac{n^{\frac{(k-2)^{2}}{k-1}}}{((k-2)!)^{\frac{k-2}{k-1}}}-\bigg(\frac{n^{k-2}}{(k-2)!}-\frac{2(k-1)n^{\frac{(k-2)^{2}}{k-1}}}{((k-2)!)^{\frac{k-2}{k-1}}}+c_{2}n^{\frac{(k-2)(k-3)}{k-1}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{k-2}{((k-2)!)^{\frac{k-2}{k-1}}}\cdot n^{\frac{(k-2)^{2}}{k-1}}-c_{4}n^{k-3}.

Hence
R_{\mathbb{N}\setminus A,k}(n)-R_{\mathbb{N}\setminus A,k}(n-1)>0
when
n
is large enough.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

For any set
A
of natural numbers and for any natural number
n
, one has
R_{A,3}(n)\leqslant\frac{3}{4}A(n)^{2}+\left\{\frac{A(n)^{2}}{4}\right\},
where
\{x\}
denotes the
fractional part of
x
.

Note that Lemma 2.2 is sharp: if
A=\{0,1,\dots{},m\}
, then

R_{A,3}\left(\left\lfloor\frac{3m}{2}\right\rfloor\right)=\frac{3}{4}A\left(\left\lfloor\frac{3m}{2}\right\rfloor\right)^{2}+\left\{\frac{A\left(\left\lfloor\frac{3m}{2}\right\rfloor\right)^{2}}{4}\right\},

where
\lfloor y\rfloor
denotes the maximal integer not greater than
y
.

Proof of Lemma 2.2.

Fix a natural number
n
. Let
A\cap[1,n]=\left\{a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{m}\right\}
and
\overline{A}=\{n-a_{m}<n-a_{m-1}<\cdots<n-a_{1}\}
.
For
i=1,2,\ldots,m
, we define

A_{i}=\{a_{i}+a_{1}<a_{i}+a_{2}<\cdots<a_{i}+a_{m+1-i}<a_{i+1}+a_{m+1-i}<\cdots<a_{m}+a_{m+1-i}\}.

Clearly,

\displaystyle R_{A,3}(n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left|A_{i}\cap\overline{A}\right|\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{m}\min\{2m-2i+1,m\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\left\lfloor\frac{m}{2}\right\rfloor}m+\sum_{i=\left\lfloor\frac{m}{2}\right\rfloor+1}^{m}(2m-2i+1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m\left\lfloor\frac{m}{2}\right\rfloor+\left(m-\left\lfloor\frac{m}{2}\right\rfloor\right)^{2}=\frac{3}{4}m^{2}+\left\{\frac{m^{2}}{4}\right\}.

∎

Proof of Theorem 1.3.

Applying Lemma 2.1 for
k=3
, we have

R_{\mathbb{N}\backslash A,3}(n)-R_{\mathbb{N}\backslash A,3}(n-1)=n+1-3\sum_{m=0}^{n}R_{A,1}(n-m)+3R_{A,2}(n)-(R_{A,3}(n)-R_{A,3}(n-1))

=n+1-3A(n)+3R_{A,2}(n)-(R_{A,3}(n)-R_{A,3}(n-1)).

Hence by Lemma 2.2,

R_{\mathbb{N}\backslash A,3}(n)-R_{\mathbb{N}\backslash A,3}(n-1)\geq n+1-3A(n)-R_{A,3}(n)

\geq n+1-3\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\sqrt{n}-2\right)-\frac{3}{4}\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\sqrt{n}-2\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{4}=\frac{15}{4}>0,

which completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem 1.4.

We may suppose that
f(n)<\sqrt[k-1]{k-1}n^{\frac{k-2}{k-1}}
.
We define an infinite sequence of natural numbers
N_{1},N_{2},\dots{}
by induction.
Let
N_{1}=100k^{4}
. Assume that
N_{1},\dots{},N_{j}
is already defined. Let
N_{j+1}
be an even number with
N_{j+1}>100k^{4}N_{j}^{k-1}
and
f(n)>(k-1)(N_{1}^{k-2}+\cdots{}+N_{j}^{k-2})
for every
n\geq N_{j+1}
. We define the set
A
in the following way.

A=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}\{N_{j},2N_{j},3N_{j},\dots{},(k-1)N_{j}^{k-1}\}.

First, we give an upper estimation for
A(n)
. Let
n\geq 100k^{4}
. Then there exists an index
j
such that

N_{j}\leq n<N_{j+1}
. Define
l
as the largest integer with
l\leq(k-1)N_{j}^{k-2}
and
lN_{j}\leq n
. Then we have

\displaystyle A(n)-\sqrt[k-1]{k-1}n^{\frac{k-2}{k-1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(k-1)(N_{1}^{k-2}+\cdots{}+N_{j}^{k-2})+l-\sqrt[k-1]{k-1}(lN_{j})^{\frac{k-2}{k-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(k-1)(N_{1}^{k-2}+\cdots{}+N_{j}^{k-2})+l^{\frac{k-2}{k-1}}(l^{\frac{1}{k-1}}-(k-1)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}N_{j}^{\frac{k-2}{k-1}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f(n),

which implies that

A(n)<\sqrt[k-1]{k-1}n^{\frac{k-2}{k-1}}+f(n).

Next we shall prove that there exist infinitely many positive integers
n
such that

R_{\mathbb{N}\setminus A,k}(n)<R_{\mathbb{N}\setminus A,k}(n-1)
. In order to prove this,
we divide into two cases according to the parity of
k
.

Suppose that
k
is an odd integer. For
j=1,2,\ldots
, we define

u_{j}=(k-1)N_{j}^{k-1}+100(k-2)(k-1)^{3}N_{j}^{k-2}.

Now, we show that
R_{\mathbb{N}\setminus A,k}(u_{j})<R_{\mathbb{N}\setminus A,k}(u_{j}-1)
when
j
is large enough.

Since all the elements of
A
are even and
u_{j}-1
is odd, it follows that
R_{A,k}(u_{j}-1)=0
.
By Lemma 2.1, we have

\displaystyle R_{\mathbb{N}\setminus A,k}(u_{j})-R_{\mathbb{N}\setminus A,k}(u_{j}-1)

(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\binom{u_{j}+k-2}{k-2}+\sum_{i=1}^{k-2}\binom{k}{i}(-1)^{i}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{u_{j}}\binom{m+k-i-2}{k-i-2}R_{A,i}(u_{j}-m)\right)

\displaystyle+(-1)^{k-1}kR_{A,k-1}(u_{j})+(-1)^{k}(R_{A,k}(u_{j})-R_{A,k}(u_{j}-1))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\binom{u_{j}+k-2}{k-2}+k^{2}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{u_{j}}\binom{m+k-4}{k-4}R_{A,2}(u_{j}-m)\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=3}^{k-2}2^{k}\sum_{m=0}^{u_{j}}\binom{m+k-i-2}{k-i-2}A(u_{j})^{i-1}+kR_{A,k-1}(u_{j})-R_{A,k}(u_{j}).

Next we shall give a bound for each term of the right hand side of (1) respectively.

There exists an constant
c_{5}
only depending on
k
such that

\binom{u_{j}+k-2}{k-2}\leq\frac{(k-1)^{k-2}N_{j}^{k^{2}-3k+2}+100(k-2)^{2}(k-1)^{k}N_{j}^{k^{2}-3k+1}+c_{5}N_{j}^{k^{2}-3k}}{(k-2)!}

(2)

and

\displaystyle k^{2}\sum_{m=0}^{u_{j}}\binom{m+k-4}{k-4}R_{A,2}(u_{j}-m)\leq k^{2}\sum_{m=0}^{u_{j}}\binom{m+k-4}{k-4}A(u_{j}-m)

(3)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle k^{2}\sum_{m=0}^{u_{j}}\binom{m+k-4}{k-4}A(kN_{j}^{k-1})\leq k^{2}\sum_{m=0}^{u_{j}}\binom{m+k-4}{k-4}2\sqrt[k-1]{k-1}(kN_{j}^{k-1})^{\frac{k-2}{k-1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle k^{2}\sum_{m=0}^{u_{j}}\binom{m+k-4}{k-4}2kN_{j}^{k-2}=2k^{3}N_{j}^{k-2}\binom{u_{j}+k-3}{k-3}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2k^{3}N_{j}^{k-2}\binom{kN_{j}^{k-1}}{k-3}\leq\frac{2k^{k}}{(k-3)!}N_{j}^{k^{2}-3k+1}.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=3}^{k-2}2^{k}\sum_{m=0}^{u_{j}}\binom{m+k-i-2}{k-i-2}A(u_{j})^{i-1}

(4)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{6}\sum_{i=3}^{k-2}2^{k}\sum_{m=0}^{u_{j}}\binom{m+k-i-2}{k-i-2}((N_{j}^{k-1})^{\frac{k-2}{k-1}})^{i-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{6}\sum_{i=3}^{k-2}2^{k}N_{j}^{(k-2)(i-1)}\sum_{m=0}^{u_{j}}\binom{m+k-i-2}{k-i-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{6}\sum_{i=3}^{k-2}2^{k}N_{j}^{(k-2)(i-1)}\binom{u_{j}+k-i-1}{k-i-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{7}\sum_{i=3}^{k-2}N_{j}^{(k-2)(i-1)}\cdot N_{j}^{(k-1)(k-i-1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{7}\sum_{i=3}^{k-2}N_{j}^{k^{2}-3k-i+3}\leq c_{8}N_{i}^{k^{2}-3k},

where
c_{6},c_{7}
and
c_{8}
are constants only depending on
k
.
Moreover,

\displaystyle R_{A,k-1}(u_{j}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle A(u_{j})^{k-2}\leq A(kN_{j}^{k-1})^{k-2}

(5)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(2\sqrt[k-1]{k-1}(kN_{j}^{k-1})^{\frac{k-2}{k-1}})^{k-2}\leq(2k)^{k-2}N_{j}^{(k-2)^{2}}.

Obviously,

\displaystyle R_{A,k}(u_{j})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle|\{(x_{1},\dots{},x_{k})\in(\mathbb{Z}^{+})^{k}:\sum_{t=1}^{k}x_{t}=u_{j},N_{j}\mid x_{t},x_{t}\leq(k-1)N_{j}^{k-1}~\text{for}~t=1,\ldots,k\}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\{(y_{1},\dots{},y_{k})\in(\mathbb{Z}^{+})^{k}:\sum_{t=1}^{k}y_{t}=\frac{u_{j}}{N_{j}},y_{t}\leq(k-1)N_{j}^{k-2}~\text{for}~t=1,\ldots,k\}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\{(y_{1},\dots{},y_{k})\in(\mathbb{Z}^{+})^{k}:\sum_{t=1}^{k}y_{t}=\frac{u_{j}}{N_{j}}\}|

\displaystyle-|\{(y_{1},\dots{},y_{k})\in(\mathbb{Z}^{+})^{k}:\sum_{t=1}^{k}y_{t}=\frac{u_{j}}{N_{j}},~y_{t}>(k-1)N_{j}^{k-2}~\text{for some}~t\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\}|.

We see that

\displaystyle|\{(y_{1},\dots{},y_{k})\in(\mathbb{Z}^{+})^{k}:y_{1}+\cdots{}+y_{k}=\frac{u_{j}}{N_{j}}\}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\binom{\frac{u_{j}}{N_{j}}-1}{k-1}\geq\frac{N_{j}^{k^{2}-3k+2}+100(k-2)(k-1)^{k+2}N_{j}^{k^{2}-3k+1}+c_{9}N_{j}^{k^{2}-3k}}{(k-1)!},

where
c_{9}
is a constant only depending on
k
, and

\displaystyle|\{(y_{1},\dots{},y_{k})\in(\mathbb{Z}^{+})^{k}:\sum_{t=1}^{k}y_{t}=\frac{u_{j}}{N_{j}},~y_{t}>(k-1)N_{j}^{k-2}~\text{for some}~t\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k|\{(z_{1},\dots{},z_{k})\in(\mathbb{Z}^{+})^{k}:z_{1}+\cdots{}+z_{k}=100(k-2)(k-1)^{3}N_{j}^{k-3}\}|

\displaystyle\leq

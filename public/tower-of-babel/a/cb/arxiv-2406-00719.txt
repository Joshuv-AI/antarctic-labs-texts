Linear Degeneracy in a Class of Nonlinear Second-Order Hyperbolic Systems
Heinrich Freistühler

Abstract




1
Formation of singularities in first-order systems

A most prominent feature of quasilinear hyperbolic systems

\displaystyle V_{t}+A(V)V_{x}=0,

(1.1)

A:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n},\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
is the property that many smooth solutions to them develop singularities in finite time.
Such solutions exist whenever the system has a genuinely nonlinear mode, i.e.,
there is a smooth (eigenvalue,eigenvector) pair

(\lambda,R):\Omega^{*}\to\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
,

\displaystyle(-\lambda(V)I+A(V))R(V)=0,\hskip 10.00002ptV\in\Omega^{*},

(1.2)

defined on an open neighborhood
\Omega^{*}\subset\Omega

of some state

V^{*}\in\Omega
with

\displaystyle(R(V)\cdot\nabla\lambda(V))|_{V=V^{*}}\neq 0.

(1.3)

The condition (
1.3
) of
genuine nonlinearity
goes back to Lax
[
10
]
, and first general results on
singularity formation to him
[
11
]
, John
[
8
]
, and Liu
[
12
]
.
For inhomogeneous systems

\displaystyle V_{t}+A(V)V_{x}=G(V),

(1.4)

with source
G:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
Bärlin has recently shown
[
1
]
that
under the sole assumptions that

\displaystyle G(V^{*})=0

(1.5)

and
(
1.3
) hold, there exist, for any Euclidean neighborhood
\Omega^{*}
of
V^{*}
,
smooth data
V_{0}:\mathbb{R}\to\Omega^{*}
such that the associated regular solution, while its values
stay in
\Omega^{*}
,
ceases to be differentiable after finite time.

The present note has been prompted by the fact that this classical mechanism of singularity
formation due to genuine nonlinearity will generically transfer to second-order hyperbolic systems.
However, we show that this is not the case for a class of systems of the special form

B^{00}(U)U_{tt}+C^{j}(U)U_{tx^{j}}+B^{jk}(U)U_{x^{j}x^{k}}=H(U,U_{t},U_{x}).

Systems of this form have recently been introduced as models for dissipative relativistic fluid dynamics,
and an application of the main result of this paper – Proposition 1 below – is to provide an indication
that solutions to these models may avoid singularity formation, similarly to what is generically
expected for the hyperbolic-parabolic models of dissipative fluid dynamics such as the
Navier-Stokes-Fourier system for compressible fluids
[
9
]
.

3
Quasisemilinear second-order systems

From now on we consider nonlinear systems of the form

\displaystyle B^{00}(U)U_{tt}+C^{j}(U)U_{tx^{j}}+B^{jk}(U)U_{x^{j}x^{k}}=H(U,U_{t},U_{x}),

(3.1)

i.e., the differential operator is linear in the highest, second-order, derivatives,
with coefficients depending only on the unknown functions itself, not its first-order derivatives.
The unknown/solution
U
ranges in some open subset
\mathcal{U}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

We assume that the operator on the left-hand side of (
3.1
) is
pointwise
semi-strictly definitely hyperbolic
, in the sense that every linear operator

U\mapsto B^{00}(U^{*})U_{tt}+C^{j}(U^{*})U_{tx^{j}}+B^{jk}(U^{*})U_{x^{j}x^{k}},\hskip 10.00002ptU^{*}\in\mathcal{U},

is semi-strictly definitely hyperbolic

With again
Q=U_{x}
, system (
3.1
) has the first-order counterpart

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} B^{00}(U)P_{t}+C^{j}(U)P_{x^{j}}+B^{jk}(U)(Q_{j})_{x^{k}}&=H(U,P,Q)\\
(Q_{j})_{t}-P_{x^{j}}&=0\\
U_{t}&=P,\end{aligned}

(3.2)

which we write as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{0}(\mathcal{V})\mathcal{V}_{t}+\mathcal{A}^{k}(\mathcal{V})\mathcal{V}_{x^{k}}=\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{V}).

\mathcal{V}=(P,Q_{1},...,Q_{d},U),

&&&I&\\

Potts and random cluster measures on locally regular-tree-like graphs
Anirban Basak, Amir Dembo, Allan Sly

Abstract
. Fixing β ≥ 0 \beta\geq 0 and an integer q ≥ 2 q\geq 2 , consider the ferromagnetic q q -Potts measures μ n β , B \mu_{n}^{\beta,B} on finite graphs 𝖦 n {\sf G}_{n} on n n vertices, with external field strength B ≥ 0 B\geq 0 and the corresponding random cluster measures φ n q , β , B \varphi^{q,\beta,B}_{n} . Suppose that as n → ∞ n\to\infty the uniformly sparse graphs 𝖦 n {\sf G}_{n} converge locally to an infinite d d -regular tree 𝖳 d {\sf T}_{d} , d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 . We show that the convergence of the Potts free energy density to its Bethe-Peirles replica symmetric prediction (which has been proved in case d d is even, or when B = 0 B=0 ), yields the local weak convergence of φ n q , β , B \varphi^{q,\beta,B}_{n} and μ n β , B \mu_{n}^{\beta,B} to the corresponding free or wired random cluster measure, Potts measure, respectively, on 𝖳 d {\sf T}_{d} . The choice of free versus wired limit is according to which has the larger Potts Bethe functional value, with mixtures of these two appearing as limit points on the critical line β c ​ ( q , B ) \beta_{c}(q,B) where these two values of the Bethe functional coincide. For B = 0 B=0 and β > β c \beta>\beta_{c} , we further establish a pure-state decomposition by showing that conditionally on the same dominant color 1 ≤ k ≤ q 1\leq k\leq q , the q q -Potts measures on such edge-expander graphs 𝖦 n {\sf G}_{n} converge locally to the q q -Potts measure on 𝖳 d {\sf T}_{d} with a boundary wired at color k k .

1.
Introduction

For a finite graph
{\sf G}=(V,E)
, parameters
\beta,B\in\mathbb{R}
and an integer
q\geq 2
,
the Potts measure
\mu_{\sf G}^{\beta,B}(\cdot)
on
{\sf G}
is a probability measure on
[q]^{V}
,
given by

\mu_{{\sf G}}^{\beta,B}(\underline{\sigma}):=\frac{1}{Z_{\sf G}(\beta,B)}\exp\Big\{\beta\sum_{(i,j)\in E}\delta_{\sigma_{i},\sigma_{j}}+B\sum_{i\in V}\delta_{\sigma_{i},1}\Big\},\quad\underline{\sigma}\in[q]^{V},

where
\delta_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}:={\bf 1}\left\{\sigma=\sigma^{\prime}\right\}
and
Z_{\sf G}(\beta,B)
is the normalizing constant (commonly known as the
partition function
).
Setting
[n]:=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
, our goal here is to characterize the limits, as
n\to\infty
, of Potts measures in the ferromagnetic regime
\beta,B\geq 0
, for
uniformly sparse

graphs
\{{\sf G}_{n}:=([n],E_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
that converge
locally
to the
d
-regular infinite tree
{\sf T}_{d}:=({\sf T}_{d},o)

rooted at a distinguished vertex
o
, as defined next.

Definition 1.1

(Uniform sparsity and local weak convergence of graphs)
.

A sequence of graphs
{\sf G}_{n}=([n],E_{n})
(possibly random) is
uniformly sparse
, if

\lim_{L\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{n}[\Delta_{I_{n}}{\bf 1}\{\Delta_{I_{n}}\geq L\}]=0,

(1.1)

where
I_{n}
is uniform on
[n]
,
\mathbb{E}_{n}
denotes the expectation with respect to the (possible) randomness of the graph
{\sf G}_{n}
and the randomly chosen vertex
I_{n}
, and
\Delta_{i}
denotes the degree of a vertex
i\in[n]
.
Given a graph
{\sf G}
(possibly infinite), and
t\in\mathbb{N}
, let
{\sf B}_{v,{\sf G}}(t)
be the
subgraph induced by

\{v^{\prime}\in V({\sf G}):{\rm dist}_{\sf G}(v,v^{\prime})\leq t\}
, for the graph distance
{\rm dist}_{\sf G}(\cdot,\cdot)
on
{\sf G}
, writing

{\sf B}_{v}(t)
when

{\sf G}
is clear from the context and
{\sf T}_{d}(t):={\sf B}_{o,{\sf T}_{d}}(t)
.
A sequence
{\sf G}_{n}=([n],E_{n})
is said to
converge locally weakly
to

{\sf T}_{d}
,
if

{\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{n}\big[{\bf 1}({\sf B}_{I_{n}}}(t)\ncong{\sf T}_{d}(t))\big]=0,\quad\quad\text{ for all }t\in\mathbb{N}\,,

(1.2)

and by a slight abuse of notation, we write
{\sf G}_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle loc}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\sf T}_{d}
when both
(
1.1
) and (
1.2
) hold.

It is widely believed that statistical physics models on large locally tree-like graphs are a good proxy for models on the integer lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for large
d
or for those with long interaction range, and therefore the study of such models on locally tree-like graphs is considered
a test bed for

mean-field theory
. Based on the
cavity method
physicists predict that, for a wide class of ferromagnetic spin systems, the asymptotic
free energy density

is given by the
Bethe-Peirles prediction
, the maximum of the
Bethe free energy functional
(see Definition
1.3
below) over all ‘meaningful’ fixed points of the
belief propagation equations

(cf.
[
17
, Chapter 17]
and the references therein. See also
[
8
, Chapter 3]
for a mathematical
treatment of this prediction, in the replica-symmetric regime, which is expected to hold for
all ferromagnetic models).
In the large
n
limit, the relative probabilities of these meaningful fixed points, commonly termed as the ‘pure states’, are further conjectured to be dictated by their respective Bethe free energies. In particular, if the Bethe free energy is maximized by a unique pure state then the ferromagnetic spin system, in the large
n
limit,
is governed by that pure state, while if there are multiple maximizers
then the system be governed in the limit
by a mixture of such maximizers.
While these conjectures
been
in the physics literature for quite
some time,
verifying them rigorously poses
serious mathematical challenges. In this paper, we prove the latter conjecture for the ferromagnetic Potts model, with an external magnetic field, on locally tree-like graphs when the limiting tree is a regular tree.

After a series of works, the Bethe-Peirles prediction for the free energy density of Ising measures
(namely, Potts measures with
q=2
), on locally tree-like graphs was rigorously established in
[
10
]
in full generality (see the references in
[
9
]

for a historical account).
In the context of the limit of Ising measures on locally tree-like graphs, the first success was attained in
[
18
]
, where they showed that under the assumption

{\sf G}_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle loc}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\sf T}_{d}
the measures

\mu_{{\sf G}_{n}}^{\beta,B}
, for
q=2
, locally weakly convergence to the free or wired/plus
Ising measures on
{\sf T}_{d}
(the choice of limit depends on whether both
B=0

and
\beta>\beta_{c}
, with
\beta_{c}
being the critical inverse temperature, or not). Such local weak convergence has been strengthened in
[
1
]
to show that it holds for a much larger collection of graph sequences
\{{\sf G}_{n}\}
, covering
also sequences that converge locally to certain non-regular, possibly random trees
(e.g. to a multi-type Galton-Watson tree of minimum degree
d_{\min}\geq 3
).

As mentioned above, this phenomenon should extend
to Potts measures with
q\geq 3
. However, as explained in the sequel,
proving such a convergence is way more challenging, especially
for the two-dimensional region of parameters
\sf R_{\neq}
,
where a-priori two different scenarios
are possible for such a limit law. The emergence of the two-dimensional region
\sf R_{\neq}
is due to the presence of two distinct phase transitions for the Potts measures with
q\geq 3
on
{\sf T}_{d}
: the uniqueness/non-uniqueness transition and the disordered/ordered transition, a phenomenon absent in the Ising setting. Moreover, on the critical line
\sf R_{c}
within
\sf R_{\neq}
,
where the Bethe free energies of both meaningful belief propagation fixed points are the same, the predicted limit is a mixture of both pure states, a behavior also absent in the Ising case.

We note in passing that the phase diagram of Potts measures, and in particular the local weak
convergence of
\mu_{n}^{\beta,B}:=\mu^{\beta,B}_{{\sf G}_{n}}
, is related to algorithmic questions of much
current interest (see
[
13
, Section 1.2]
and the references therein, where
B=0
).
Such convergence is also related to the metastability of the Glauber dynamics and of the Swendsen-Wang chain for
\mu^{\beta,B}_{n}
, as well as the non-reconstructions of the paramagnetic and ferromagnetic states
when
(\beta,B)\in\sf R_{\neq}
(for
B=0
, see
[
6
]
and the references therein for a list representative works in this area).

To state our result about such convergence of Potts measures

\mu_{n}^{\beta,B}
when
{\sf G}_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle loc}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\sf T}_{d}

to define the Potts measures on
{\sf T}_{d}

Definition 1.2

(Potts on trees, with given boundary conditions)
.

For each

\displaystyle\mu^{\beta,B}_{\ddagger,t}(\underline{\sigma})=\mu^{\beta,B}_{\ddagger,{\sf T}_{d},t}(\underline{\sigma})

\displaystyle\propto

\displaystyle\exp\Big\{\beta\sum_{(i,j)\in E({\sf T}_{d}(t))}\delta_{\sigma_{i},\sigma_{j}}+B\sum_{i\in V({\sf T}_{d}(t))}\delta_{\sigma_{i},1}\Big\}\prod_{u\in\partial{\sf T}_{d}(t)}{\widetilde{\nu}_{\ddagger}}(du)

,
{\widetilde{\nu}_{\free}}
is the uniform measure on
[q]
, and

\ddagger\in[q]

The Potts measure with

\mu_{\ddagger}^{\beta,B}:=\displaystyle{\lim_{t\uparrow\infty}}\mu_{\ddagger,{\sf T}_{d},t}^{\beta,B}

exists

and
\ddagger\in\{\free,1\}
, and

[

Remark

For

plays a significant role in describing our results. Indeed, within this region, the limit is
determined by the relation between the

\mu_{n}^{\beta,B}

\Phi_{n}(\beta,B):=\frac{1}{n}\log Z_{{\sf G}_{n}}(\beta,B),

(1.3)

and the Bethe (free energy) functional at certain Bethe recursion fixed points,

Definition 1.3

(Bethe functional; Bethe recursion and its fixed points)
.

Denoting by
\mathcal{P}([q])
the set of all probabilities on
[q]
, the Bethe functional

\Phi:\mathcal{P}([q])\mapsto\mathbb{R}
is given by

\Phi(\nu)=\Phi^{\beta,B}(\nu):=\log\Big\{\sum_{\sigma\in[q]}e^{B\delta_{\sigma,1}}\left((e^{\beta}-1)\nu(\sigma)+1\right)^{d}\Big\}-\frac{d}{2}\log\Big\{(e^{\beta}-1)\sum_{\sigma\in[q]}\nu(\sigma)^{2}+1\Big\}.

(1.4)

Of primary interest to us
is the value of
\Phi(\cdot)
at
\nu_{\free}^{\beta,B}
and at
\nu_{1}^{\beta,B}

{\sf BP}:\mathcal{P}([q])\mapsto\mathcal{P}([q])

({\sf BP}\nu)(\sigma):=\frac{1}{z_{\nu}}e^{B\delta_{\sigma,1}}\left((e^{\beta}-1)\nu(\sigma)+1\right)^{d-1},\quad\sigma\in[q],

(1.5)

(with

[q]

[

Throughout this paper we make the assumption that

∎

Recall that, by Lemma

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose
\mathscr{M}:=(\mathscr{M}_{1},\mathscr{M}_{2},\ldots,\mathscr{M}_{q})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}{\rm Mult}_{q}(M,q^{-1},q^{-1},\ldots,q^{-1})

M

for each of the
q

\mathscr{M}

[M]

to
distinguished sets
\{B_{k},\widetilde{B}_{k}\}

|\widetilde{B}_{k}|=\mathscr{M}_{k}-\mathscr{M}_{\star}

\underline{\gamma}

[q]

whenever

\{Y_{i}\}_{i=1}^{M}

\dU([q])

[q]

Proof.

It suffices to show that for
{\bm{Y}}:=(Y_{1},Y_{2},\ldots,Y_{M})

any fixed
{\bm{k}}:=(k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{M})\in[q]^{M}
and a fixed permutation

{\bm{\gamma}}^{0}
of
[q]

\mathbb{P}({\bm{Y}}={\bm{k}}\,|\,\underline{\gamma}={\bm{\gamma}}^{0})=q^{-M}.

(4.4)

Now, given
\underline{\gamma}={\bm{\gamma}}^{0}
,
the event
\Omega_{{\bm{k}}}:=\{{\bm{Y}}={\bm{k}}\}
induces the partition
\widehat{B}_{i}:=\{j\in[M]:k_{j}=i\}

of
[M]
with
\widehat{B}_{i}=B_{i}\cup\widetilde{B}_{{\bm{\gamma}}^{0}(i)}
. Setting
|\widehat{B}_{i}|=y_{i}
, since

|B_{i}|=y_{\star}:=\min_{j}\{y_{j}\}
for all
i\in[q]
, we also have
|\widetilde{B}_{{\bm{\gamma}}^{0}(i)}|=y_{i}-y_{\star}
.
That is, given
\underline{\gamma}
, the event
\Omega_{\bm{k}}
determines the

\{B_{i},\widetilde{B}_{i}\}

indeterminate. In fact, any such choice of
\{B_{i}\}

with

.
There are

and the probability of observing each realization of

is then

\frac{1}{\binom{M}{y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{q}}\prod_{i=1}^{q}\binom{y_{i}}{y_{\star}}}\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{M}_{i}=y_{i},i\in[q])=\frac{1}{\prod_{i=1}^{q}\binom{y_{i}}{y_{\star}}}q^{-M}\,.

Hence, taking a union over the set of all possible choices of

we arrive at (
4.4
). ∎

In Lemma

the dominant color is completely determined by the

,

\underline{\gamma}

) in case
q=2
is well known (e.g. see
[
7
, Lemma 4.1]
),
and the extension for
q\geq 3
(which we now give), follows a similar scheme.
With
\mu^{\beta,B}_{\ddagger}
a translation invariant measure, it suffices to consider

i=o
and
j=1
a specific neighbor of the root
o
of
{\sf T}_{d}
. Fixing
t\in\mathbb{N}
,
as there are no cycles in
{\sf T}_{d}(t)
, we have under
\mu_{\ddagger,t}^{\beta,B}
of
Definition
1.2
, that for some

\widehat{\nu}_{t}^{1},\widehat{\nu}_{t}^{o}\in\mathcal{P}([q])

\mu_{\ddagger,t}^{\beta,B}(\sigma_{o},\sigma_{1})\propto e^{\beta\delta_{\sigma_{o},\sigma_{1}}}\widehat{\nu}^{1}_{t}(\sigma_{1})\widehat{\nu}^{o}_{t}(\sigma_{o})\,,\qquad\forall\sigma_{o},\sigma_{1}\in[q].

Further, both
T_{o\rightarrow 1}
and
T_{1\rightarrow o}
are infinite
(d-1)
-ary trees (i.e. each vertex has
(d-1)
children). Hence, by induction we deduce from Definitions
1.2

and
1.3
, that
\widehat{\nu}_{t}^{o}
and
\widehat{\nu}_{t}^{1}
are the probability measures obtained by running the
{\sf BP}
recursion
t
and
(t-1)
times, respectively, starting from

\nu_{\ddagger}
(i.e. starting at the uniform measure if
\ddagger=\free
and at Dirac at
1

when
\ddagger=1
). By definition the limit of these recursions is
\nu_{\ddagger}^{\beta,B}

and (
3.6
) follows.
∎

Remark A.1
.

By a similar reasoning one finds also that for any
\beta,B\geq 0
and
\ddagger\in\{\free,1\}
,
all finite dimensional marginals of
\mu_{\ddagger,t}^{\beta,B}
converge as
t\to\infty
. Furthermore, it can be checked that these marginals are consistent, thus implying the existence of
\mu_{\ddagger}^{\beta,B}
for
\ddagger\in\{\free,1\}
. The existence of
\mu_{i}^{\beta,0}
for all
i\in[q]

is proved similarly (now starting the
{\sf BP}
recursion at Dirac at
i
).

Next we prove that
\nu_{\free}
and
\nu_{1}
, when viewed as functions of
\beta
and
B
, are continuously differentiable except on
\partial\sf R_{\neq}^{+}
and
\partial\sf R_{\neq}^{\free}
, respectively.

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

(i)
Since

\nu_{1}^{\beta,B}
is a fixed point of the
{\sf BP}
recursion of (
1.5
), starting from the probability measure on
[q]
that is Dirac at
1
, necessarily
\nu_{1}^{\beta,B}(\sigma)=\nu_{1}^{\beta,B}(\sigma^{\prime})
for all
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in[q]\backslash\{1\}
and as
\nu_{1}^{\beta,B}\in\mathcal{P}([q])
,
it suffices to show that
(\beta,B)\mapsto\nu_{1}^{\beta,B}(1)
is continuously differentiable
at any fixed
(\beta_{0},B_{0})\notin\partial\sf R_{\neq}^{\free}
. Further, after some algebra we
deduce from (
1.5
) that

r_{1}(\beta,B):=\log\frac{\nu_{1}^{\beta,B}(1)}{\nu_{1}^{\beta,B}(2)}=\log\frac{(1-q)\nu_{1}^{\beta,B}(1)}{1-\nu_{1}^{\beta,B}(1)}\,,

satisfies the fixed point equation

F(r;\beta,B)=r,

(A.1)

where

F(r;\beta,B):=B+(d-1)\log\left(\frac{e^{\beta+r}+q-1}{e^{r}+e^{\beta}+q-2}\right).

Using this representation it suffices to show that
(\beta,B)\mapsto r_{1}(\beta,B)
is continuously differentiable,
and this follows by an application of the implicit function theorem, once we have verified that

\frac{\partial}{\partial r}F(r;\beta,B)\neq 1,\qquad\text{ for }(r,\beta,B)=(r_{1}(\beta_{0},B_{0}),\beta_{0},B_{0}).

(A.2)

To obtain (
A.2
) we borrow results from
[
10
]
. From the proof of
[
10
, Lemma 4.6]
it follows that for
d\geq 3
there is some
\beta_{-}\in(0,\infty)
such that for
\beta<\beta_{-}
there does not exist any solution to the equation

\frac{\partial}{\partial r}F(r;\beta,B)=\frac{\partial}{\partial r}F(r;\beta,0)=1.

(A.3)

In contrast, for
\beta\geq\beta_{-}
there exist solutions
\rho_{-}(\beta)\leq\rho_{+}(\beta)
of the equation (
A.3
), with equality if and only if
\beta=\beta_{-}
. Thus, to complete the proof of (
A.2
) we need to show that if
(\beta_{0},B_{0})\in\sf R_{\neq}\backslash\partial\sf R_{\neq}^{\free}
(and therefore
\beta_{0}>\beta_{-}
, see
[
10
, Lemma 4.6]
) then
r_{1}(\beta_{0},B_{0})\neq\rho_{\pm}(\beta_{0})
.

If possible, let us assume that
r_{1}(\beta_{0},B_{0})=\rho_{+}(\beta_{0})
. Denoting

B_{\pm}(\beta):=\rho_{\mp}(\beta)-F(\rho_{\mp}(\beta);\beta,0),

(A.4)

as
r_{1}(\beta,B)
is a fixed point of the equation (
A.1
), we note that
B_{0}=B_{-}(\beta_{0})
. From the proof of
[
10
, Theorem 1.11(a)]
we have that the map
B\mapsto\beta_{\free}(B)
is the inverse of the map
\beta\mapsto B_{-}(\beta)
. So,
\beta_{0}=\beta_{\free}(B_{0})
implying that
(\beta_{0},B_{0})\in\partial\sf R_{\neq}^{\free}
. As
(\beta_{0},B_{0})\notin\partial\sf R^{\free}_{\neq}
we arrive at a contradiction.

To rule out the other possibility that
r_{1}(\beta_{0},B_{0})=\rho_{-}(\beta_{0})
we again proceed by contradiction. As
\beta_{0}>\beta_{-}
we have that
\rho_{-}(\beta_{0})<\rho_{+}(\beta_{0})
. Now note

B_{+}(\beta)-B_{-}(\beta)=\int_{\rho_{-}(\beta)}^{\rho_{+}(\beta)}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial r}F(r;\beta,0)-1\right]dr.

It can be checked that
\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial r^{2}}F(r;\beta,0)
is negative for sufficiently large
r
and has a single change of sign. As
\rho_{\pm}(\beta)
are the solutions of (
A.3
) one therefore have that
\frac{\partial}{\partial r}F(r;\beta,0)>1
for
r\in(\rho_{-}(\beta),\rho_{+}(\beta))
. Hence, it follows from above that
B_{-}(\beta_{0})<B_{+}(\beta_{0})
.
Thus

F(\rho_{+}(\beta_{0});\beta_{0},B_{0})=\rho_{+}(\beta_{0})+B_{0}-B_{-}(\beta_{0})=\rho_{+}(\beta_{0})+B_{+}(\beta_{0})-B_{-}(\beta_{0})>\rho_{+}(\beta_{0}),

(A.5)

where in the penultimate step we have used the fact that the assumption
r_{1}(\beta_{0},B_{0})=\rho_{-}(\beta_{0})
implies that
B_{0}=B_{+}(\beta_{0})
.

Noting that
\lim_{r\to\infty}F(r;\beta_{0},B_{0})<\infty
, (
A.5
) implies that there exists some
r_{\star}\in(\rho_{+}(\beta_{0}),\infty)
such that
r_{\star}=F(r_{\star};\beta_{0},B_{0})
. However, this contradicts the fact that
r_{1}(\beta_{0},B_{0})
is the largest root of that fixed point equation (
A.1
).

(ii) As in part (i), it now suffices to show that
(\beta,B)\mapsto r_{\free}(\beta,B)
is continuously differentiable for
(\beta,B)\in\sf R_{\neq}\backslash\partial\sf R_{\neq}^{+}
,
where
r_{\free}(\beta,B):=\log\frac{(1-q)\nu_{\free}^{\beta,B}(1)}{1-\nu_{\free}^{\beta,B}(1)}

for
\nu_{\free}^{\beta,B}(\cdot)
of Definition
1.3
.
To this end, fixing
(\beta_{0},B_{0})\in\sf R_{\neq}\backslash\partial\sf R_{\neq}^{+}

we only need to show that (
A.3
) fails at
(r,\beta,B)=(r_{\free}(\beta_{0},B_{0}),\beta_{0},B_{0})
which analogously to part (i) be a direct consequence of having that

r_{\free}(\beta_{0},B_{0})\neq\rho_{\pm}(\beta_{0})
.

Proceeding to do this task, the same argument as in part (i) shows that

r_{\free}(\beta_{0},B_{0})\neq\rho_{-}(\beta_{0})
, but a slightly different argument is needed
for ruling out the other choice. Specifically, we claim that

r_{\free}(\beta_{0},B_{0})=\rho_{+}(\beta_{0})
would imply

\rho_{-}(\beta_{0})>0
. To see this, from the definition of
B_{\pm}(\beta)
we find that
B_{0}=B_{-}(\beta_{0})
. From the proof of
[
10
, Theorem 1.11]
we also have that the map
B\mapsto\beta_{\free}(B)
is the inverse of the map
\beta\mapsto B_{-}(\beta)
. Moreover, the assumption
(\beta_{0},B_{0})\in\sf R_{\neq}\backslash\partial\sf R_{\neq}^{+}
implies that
B_{0}\in[0,B_{+})
. Therefore,

\beta_{0}=\beta_{\free}(B_{0})<\beta_{+}(B_{0})\leq\beta_{+}(0)=:\beta_{+},

(A.6)

where the first inequality follows from the fact that
\beta_{\free}(B)<\beta_{+}(B)
for
B\in[0,B_{+})
and the second inequality follows from the fact that the map
\beta_{+}(\cdot)
is decreasing in
B
. Using
[
10
, Eqn. (4.10)]
we thus deduce that
\rho_{-}(\beta_{0})>0
whenever

r_{\free}(\beta_{0},B_{0})=\rho_{+}(\beta_{0})
. Now, similarly to part (i),
if both
\rho_{-}(\beta_{0})>0
and
r_{\free}(\beta_{0},B_{0})=\rho_{+}(\beta_{0})
, then
there exists a solution
r_{\star}\in[0,r_{\free}(\beta_{0},B_{0}))
of (
A.1
). From Definition
1.3
we know that
r_{\free}(\beta_{0},B_{0})
is the smallest
nonnegative solution of (
A.1
), so having arrived at a contradiction,
the proof of the lemma is thus complete.
∎

Proof of Proposition
1.5
.

From
[
10
, Theorem 1.11(a)]
we have the existence of the smooth curves
\beta_{\free}(B)
and
\beta_{+}(B)
with the desired properties. It also follows from there that for
(\beta,B)\in[0,\infty)^{2}\backslash\sf R_{\neq}
one has
\nu_{\free}^{\beta,B}=\nu_{1}^{\beta,B}
. This together with Lemma
3.3
implies that all two dimensional marginals of
\mu_{\free}^{\beta,B}
and
\mu_{1}^{\beta,B}
coincide.
Further, as described in Remark
A.1
, any finite dimensional marginal of
\mu_{\free}^{\beta,B}
then coincides with that of
\mu_{1}^{\beta,B}
, thereby yielding our claim (
1.8
) from which (
1.9
) also follows (since at
B=0
the Potts measures are invariant with respect to permutations on
[q]
). Since
r_{\free}^{\beta,B}<r_{1}^{\beta,B}
for
(\beta,B)\in\sf R_{\neq}
our claim that
\nu_{\free}^{\beta,B}(1)<\nu_{1}^{\beta,B}(1)
follows from the fact that map
r\mapsto e^{r}/(e^{r}+q-1)
is strictly increasing on
\mathbb{R}
.

Now, for the existence of smooth
B\to\beta_{c}(B)
of the required properties,
it suffices to verify that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\Phi(\nu_{\free}^{\beta,B})<\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\Phi(\nu_{1}^{\beta,B}),\qquad

\displaystyle\text{ for all }(\beta,B)\in{\rm Int}(\sf R_{\neq})\,,

(A.7)

\displaystyle\Phi(\nu_{\free}^{\beta,B})>\Phi(\nu_{1}^{\beta,B}),\qquad

\displaystyle\text{ for }(\beta,B)\in\partial\sf R_{\neq}^{\free},

(A.8)

\displaystyle\Phi(\nu_{\free}^{\beta,B})<\Phi(\nu_{1}^{\beta,B}),\qquad

\displaystyle\text{ for }(\beta,B)\in\partial\sf R_{\neq}^{+}.

(A.9)

Indeed, (
A.7
)-(
A.9
) imply that (
1.11
) holds
for some
B\mapsto\beta_{c}(B)
such that
\beta_{\free}(B)<\beta_{c}(B)<\beta_{+}(B)
for
B\in[0,B_{+})
,
with smoothness of
\beta_{c}(\cdot)
due to the implicit function theorem
(where

\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\Phi(\nu_{\free}^{\beta,B})\neq\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\Phi(\nu_{1}^{\beta,B})

throughout
{\rm Int}(\sf R_{\neq})
thanks to (
A.7
)).

Turning to prove (
A.7
), we set
\bar{\nu}:=\frac{1-\nu}{q-1}

and note that the map

\nu\mapsto\frac{\bar{\nu}(1+\nu-\bar{\nu})}{\bar{\nu}^{2}+(q-1)\nu^{2}}

is strictly decreasing on
[\frac{1}{q},1)
(having positive numerator and denominator,
whose derivatives are strictly negative and positive, respectively, on the interval
(\frac{1}{q},1)
). Using Lemma
3.3
and the fact that
\nu_{\ddagger}(i)=\nu_{\ddagger}(2)
for
\ddagger\in\{\free,1\}

and all
i\in[q]\backslash\{1\}
, we also have at any
i\in\partial o
,

\mu_{\ddagger}^{\beta,B}(\sigma_{o}=\sigma_{i})=\frac{e^{\beta}\left[\nu_{\ddagger}(1)^{2}+(q-1)\nu_{\ddagger}(2)^{2}\right]}{e^{\beta}\left[\nu_{\ddagger}(1)^{2}+(q-1)\nu_{\ddagger}(2)^{2}\right]+2(q-1)\nu_{\ddagger}(1)\nu_{\ddagger}(2)+(q-1)(q-2)\nu_{\ddagger}(2)^{2}}\,.

With
\frac{1}{q}\leq\nu_{\free}^{\beta,B}(1)<\nu_{1}^{\beta,B}(1)
at any
(\beta,B)\in\sf R_{\neq}
,
combining the last two observations results with

\sum_{i\in\partial o}\mu_{\free}^{\beta,B}(\sigma_{o}=\sigma_{i})<\sum_{i\in\partial o}\mu_{1}^{\beta,B}(\sigma_{o}=\sigma_{i}),\qquad\qquad\forall\,(\beta,B)\in\sf R_{\neq}

from which (
A.7
) follows upon using (
3.10
).

Moving next to the proof of (
A.8
), for any
\beta\in[\beta_{-},\beta_{\free}]
let

\Psi_{-}(\beta):=\Phi^{\beta,B_{-}(\beta)}(r_{1}(\beta,B_{-}(\beta)))-\Phi^{\beta,B_{-}(\beta)}(r_{\free}(\beta,B_{-}(\beta)))\,,

with the convention that
\Phi^{\beta,B}(r)=\Phi^{\beta,B}(r(\nu)):=\Phi^{\beta,B}(\nu)

for
r(\nu):=\log(\nu(1)/\nu(2))
and any
\nu\in\mathcal{P}([q])
such that
\nu(2)=\cdots=\nu(q)
.
Fix any
(\beta_{0},B_{0})\notin\partial\sf R_{\neq}^{+}
. By Lemma
3.2
(ii), proceeding as in the steps leading to (
3.10
), and using the definition of
r(\nu)
we obtain that

\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\Phi^{\beta_{0},B_{0}}(r_{\free}(\beta_{0},B_{0}))=h_{1}(r_{\free}(\beta_{0},B_{0});\beta_{0})\text{ and }\frac{\partial}{\partial B}\Phi^{\beta_{0},B_{0}}(r_{\free}(\beta_{0},B_{0}))=h_{2}(r_{\free}(\beta_{0},B_{0});\beta_{0}),

(A.10)

where

h_{1}(r;\beta):=\frac{d}{2}\cdot\frac{e^{\beta}(e^{2r}+q-1)}{e^{r}(e^{\beta+r}+q-1)+(q-1)(e^{r}+e^{\beta}+q-2)}

and

h_{2}(r;\beta):=\frac{e^{r}(e^{\beta+r}+q-1)}{e^{r}(e^{\beta+r}+q-1)+(q-1)(e^{r}+e^{\beta}+q-2)}.

By (
A.4
) we see that
\rho_{+}(\beta_{0})
is a fixed point of (
A.1
) for
(\beta,B)=(\beta_{0},B_{-}(\beta_{0}))
. Recall that
\rho_{+}(\beta)
is the largest solution of (
A.3
) and since
r_{1}(\beta,B)
is the largest solution of (
A.1
), we deduce that
\rho_{+}(\beta_{0})=r_{1}(\beta_{0},B_{-}(\beta_{0}))
for
\beta_{0}\in[\beta_{-},\beta_{\free}]
. From (
A.3
) it can be further checked that
\rho_{+}(\beta)
is the
\log
of the largest solution of a quadratic equation with coefficients smooth in
\beta
that does not admit a double root for
\beta>\beta_{-}
. Therefore, the map
\beta\mapsto\rho_{+}(\beta)
is differentiable on
(\beta_{-},\beta_{\free}]
. Hence, repeating a computation
as in the steps leading to (
3.10
), we find that (
A.10
) continues to hold when
B_{0}
is replaced by
B_{-}(\beta_{0})
and
r_{\free}(\beta_{0},B_{0})
is replaced by
r_{1}(\beta_{0},B_{-}(\beta_{0}))
. Moreover, by (
A.4
) we have that
\partial_{\beta}B_{-}(\beta_{0})=-\partial_{\beta}F(\rho_{+}(\beta_{0});\beta_{0},0)
for
\beta_{0}\in(\beta_{-},\beta_{\free}]
. Thus, using the chain rule of differentiation, we find that

\Psi_{-}^{\prime}(\beta_{0})=\Delta h_{1}(\beta_{0})-\Delta h_{2}(\beta_{0})\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}F(\rho_{+}(\beta_{0});\beta_{0},0)\\
=\int_{r_{\free}(\beta_{0},B_{-}(\beta_{0}))}^{r_{1}(\beta_{0},B_{-}(\beta_{0}))}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial r}h_{1}(r;\beta_{0})-\frac{\partial}{\partial r}h_{2}(r,\beta_{0})\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}F(\rho_{+}(\beta_{0});\beta_{0},0)\right)dr,

(A.11)

where

\Delta h_{i}(\beta_{0}):=h_{i}(r_{1}(\beta_{0},B_{-}(\beta_{0}));\beta_{0})-h_{i}(r_{\free}(\beta_{0},B_{-}(\beta_{0}));\beta_{0}),\quad\text{ for }i=1,2.

Next denoting

g_{\beta_{0}}(r):=\frac{\frac{\partial}{\partial r}h_{1}(r;\beta_{0})}{\frac{\partial}{\partial r}h_{2}(r;\beta_{0})}=d\cdot\frac{e^{2r}+(q-2)e^{r}-(q-1)}{e^{2r}+2(e^{\beta_{0}}+q-2)e^{r}+(q-1)(1+e^{-\beta_{0}}(q-2))}

we find that
\sgn(g^{\prime}_{\beta_{0}}(r))=\sgn(2e^{\beta_{0}}+q-2)\cdot\sgn(Q_{\beta_{0}}(e^{r}))
, where
Q_{\beta_{0}}(t):=t^{2}+2(q-1)e^{-\beta_{0}}+(q-1)(1+(q-2)e^{-\beta_{0}})
. Since the roots of
Q_{\beta_{0}}
are either negative or complex conjugates of each other we deduce that
g^{\prime}_{\beta_{0}}(r)>0
for all
r\in\mathbb{R}
. This entails that

g_{\beta_{0}}(r)<g_{\beta_{0}}(r_{1}(\beta_{0},B_{-}(\beta_{0})))=\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}F(\rho_{+}(\beta_{0});\beta_{0},0)\text{ for }r\in[0,r_{1}(\beta_{0},B_{-}(\beta_{0}))),

(A.12)

where to obtain the right most equality we use the fact that
\rho_{+}(\beta_{0})=r_{1}(\beta_{0},B_{-}(\beta_{0}))
satisfies (
A.3
). So, noticing that
\frac{\partial}{\partial r}h_{2}(\cdot;\beta_{0})
is strictly positive, by (
A.12
), we find that the integrand in the
rhs
of (
A.11
) is strictly negative for
r\in[r_{\free}(\beta_{0},B_{-}(\beta_{0}),r_{1}(\beta_{0},B_{-}(\beta_{0})))
, and consequently
that
\Psi_{-}^{\prime}(\beta_{0})<0
for
\beta_{0}\in(\beta_{-},\beta_{\free}]
. By definition of the non-uniqueness regime, necessarily
\Psi_{-}(\beta_{-})=0
and (
A.8
) follows.

The proof of (
A.9
) is similar. Indeed, noting that
r_{\free}(\beta,B)=\rho_{-}(\beta)
for any
(\beta,B)\in\partial\sf R_{\neq}^{+}
, denoting
\Psi_{+}(\beta):=\Phi^{\beta,B_{+}(\beta)}(r_{1}(\beta,B_{+}(\beta)))-\Phi^{\beta,B_{+}(\beta)}(r_{\free}(\beta,B_{+}(\beta)))
for any
\beta\in[\beta_{-},\beta_{+}]
, and proceeding as in the steps leading to (
A.11
) we find that (
A.11
) continues to hold for
\Psi_{+}^{\prime}(\beta_{0})

at any
\beta_{0}\in(\beta_{-},\beta_{+}]
, provided we replace there

\rho_{+}(\beta_{0})
and
B_{-}(\beta_{0})
by
\rho_{-}(\beta_{0})
and
B_{+}(\beta_{0})
, respectively. Moreover, a same reasoning as in (
A.12
) yields that

g_{\beta_{0}}(r)>g_{\beta_{0}}(r_{\free}(\beta_{0},B_{+}(\beta_{0})))=\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}F(\rho_{-}(\beta_{0});\beta_{0},0)\text{ for }r\in(r_{\free}(\beta_{0},B_{+}(\beta_{0})),\infty).

Repeating now the rest of the argument in the proof of (
A.8
)
we obtain (
A.9
) and thereby complete the proof of the proposition.

Abstract
. For Lebesgue generic ( x 1 , x 2 ) ∈ ℝ 2 ({x}_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2} , we investigate the distribution of small values of products q ⋅ ‖ q ​ x 1 ‖ ⋅ ‖ q ​ x 2 ‖ q\cdot\|qx_{1}\|\cdot\|qx_{2}\| with q ∈ ℕ q\in\mathbb{N} , where ∥ ⋅ ∥ \|\cdot\| denotes the distance to the closest integer. The main result gives an asymptotic formula for the number of 1 ≤ q < T 1\leq q<T such that a < q ⋅ ‖ q ​ x 1 ‖ ⋅ ‖ q ​ x 2 ‖ ≤ b and ‖ q ​ x 1 ‖ , ‖ q ​ x 2 ‖ ≤ c a<q\cdot\|qx_{1}\|\cdot\|qx_{2}\|\leq b\quad\textrm{and}\quad\|qx_{1}\|,\|qx_{2}\|\leq c for given parameters a , b , c a,b,c satisfying certain growth conditions.



1.
Introduction

In this paper we will be interested in the basic problem of multiplicative Diophantine approximation regarding existence of small values for products

q\cdot\|qx_{1}\|\cdot\|qx_{2}\|,\quad\hbox{with $q\in\mathbb{N}$,}

for Lebesgue generic
\underline{x}=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
where
\|\cdot\|
denotes the distance to the closest integer.
The main result in this direction
was established by Gallagher
[
8
]
, who showed that for any non-increasing function
\psi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}

satisfying
\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}\psi(q)\frac{\log q}{q}=\infty
, the inequality

q\cdot\|qx_{1}\|\cdot\|qx_{2}\|\leq\psi(q)

has infinitely many solutions
q\in\mathbb{N}
for almost all
\underline{x}=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. For instance,
for almost all
\underline{x}=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, there are infinitely many solutions
q\in\mathbb{N}
to the inequality

q\cdot\|qx_{1}\|\cdot\|qx_{2}\|\leq(\log q)^{-2}.

The next natural question involves the distribution of the set of solutions

M(\underline{x}):=\big\{q\in\mathbb{N}\,:\,q\cdot\|qx_{1}\|\cdot\|qx_{2}\|\leq\psi(q)\big\}.

Wang and Yu
[
21
]
derived an explicit asymptotic formula for the cardinality

|M(\underline{x})\cap[1,T)|
as
T\to\infty
. Inhomogeneous versions of these problems were also studied by
Chow and Technau
[
4
,
5
]
. The above results are usually described as
asymptotic
Diophantine
problems.

Here we explore the analogous
uniform
Diophantine problem that involves
solutions to the inequalities

q\cdot\|qx_{1}\|\cdot\|qx_{2}\|\leq b,\quad 1\leq q<T

for given parameters
b
and
T
.
It should be noted that there is an essential difference between asymptotic problems and uniform problems.
For instance, establishing a uniform version of the classical Khinchin theorem was addressed only recently
in
[
15
,
12
,
13
]
. A somewhat different related uniform problem
was also considered by Widmer
[
22
]
. His result concerns
the sets

N(\underline{x};b):=\left\{q\in\mathbb{N}\,:\,\|qx_{1}\|\cdot\|qx_{2}\|\leq b\right\},

and it follows from
[
22
]
that if
b\gg(\log T)^{4+\varepsilon}/T
for some
\varepsilon>0
, then
for almost all
\underline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

\big|N(\underline{x};b)\cap[1,T)\big|=4\cdot b(1-\log(4b))\cdot T+O_{\underline{x},\varepsilon}\Big(b^{2/3}(-\log b)\cdot T^{2/3}(\log T)^{4/3+\varepsilon/3}\Big).

The condition on the parameter
b
is probably not optimal and is needed to ensure that
the required Diophantine condition in
[
22
]
holds for almost all
\underline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
However, it is not clear whether this method can give an asymptotic formula in a larger range.

Our first main result gives an asymptotic formula for the growth of the sets

L(\underline{x};b):=\big\{q\in\mathbb{N}\,:\,q\cdot\|qx_{1}\|\cdot\|qx_{2}\|\leq b\big\}.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\eta\in(1,2)
. Then for every
\varepsilon>0
and for almost every
\underline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

•

when

(\ln T)^{-\eta}\leq b\leq(\ln T)^{-\eta/(3-\eta)}
,

\displaystyle\big|L(\underline{x};b)\cap[1,T)\big|=2\cdot b\cdot(\ln T)^{2}+O_{\underline{x},\varepsilon}\left(b^{1-1/\eta}\cdot(\ln\ln T)^{6+\varepsilon}\ln T\right),

•

when
b\geq(\ln T)^{-\eta/(3-\eta)}
,

\displaystyle\big|L(\underline{x};b)\cap[1,T)\big|=2\cdot b\cdot(\ln T)^{2}+O_{\underline{x},\varepsilon}\left(b^{2/\eta}\cdot(\ln\ln T)^{6+\varepsilon}(\ln T)^{2}\right).

Theorem
1.1
is proved in Section
11
.
It will be deduced from a more general result which we now describe.

Given parameters
T\geq 1
and
a,b,c>0
satisfying

a<b<1\quad\textrm{and}\quad c<\frac{1}{2},

we consider the domains

\Omega=\Omega_{[1,T)}^{(a,b],c}:=\left\{(\underline{u},y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,T)\,:\,\begin{array}[]{c}\max(|u_{1}|,|u_{2}|)\leq c\\[5.69046pt]
a<|u_{1}u_{2}|\cdot y\leq b\end{array}\right\},

(1.1)

and, for
\underline{x}=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the corresponding sets

Q_{\Omega}(\underline{x}):=\left\{q\in[1,T)\cap\mathbb{N}\,:\,\begin{array}[]{c}\max(\|qx_{1}\|,\|qx_{2}\|)\leq c\\[5.69046pt]
a<q\cdot\|qx_{1}\|\cdot\|qx_{2}\|\leq b\end{array}\right\}.

It is natural to expect that the cardinality
|Q_{\Omega}(\underline{x})|
grows as
the volume of
\Omega
.
Our next theorem proves such an asymptotic formula:

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that

a\geq(\ln T)^{-\theta}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\zeta\cdot b\leq c^{2}\quad\hbox{for some $\zeta,\theta>0$.}

Let
\eta\in(1,2)
. Then for every
\varepsilon>0
and for almost every
\underline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

•

when

(c\cdot\ln T)^{-\eta}\leq b\leq(c\cdot\ln T)^{-\eta/(3-\eta)}
,

\displaystyle\big|Q_{\Omega}(\underline{x})\big|=\Vol_{3}(\Omega)+O_{\underline{x},\zeta,\theta,\varepsilon}\left(c^{-1}b^{1-1/\eta}\cdot(\ln\ln T)^{6+\varepsilon}\ln T+1\right),

•

when
b\geq(c\cdot\ln T)^{-\eta/(3-\eta)}
,

\displaystyle\big|Q_{\Omega}(\underline{x})\big|=\Vol_{3}(\Omega)+O_{\underline{x},\zeta,\theta,\varepsilon}\left(b^{2/\eta}\cdot(\ln\ln T)^{6+\varepsilon}(\ln T)^{2}+1\right).

To compare the above main term with the error term,
we note that according to (
A.4
) in
Appendix
A
,

\Vol_{3}(\Omega)\sim 2\cdot(\ln T)^{2}\cdot(b-a)\quad\hbox{as $T\to\infty$.}

(1.2)

Due to the assumption on the parameter
a
, we cannot directly take
a=0
in the Theorem

1.2
to deduce almost sure asymptotics for the sets
L(\underline{x};b)
in Theorem
1.1
.
Instead we analyze the existence of lattice points in thin hyperbolic strips
and show that when
a
decays sufficiently fast, the sets
L(\underline{x},a)

are empty for almost all
\underline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
(see Section
11
).

Our method also allows us to study the distribution
of the scaled set
Q_{\Omega}(\underline{x})/T
in the interval
[0,1]
.
For a compactly supported Lipschitz function
h:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
consider the weighted counting function

S_{\Omega}h(\underline{x}):=\sum_{q\in Q_{\Omega}(\underline{x})}h\left(\frac{q}{T}\right),\quad\underline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

We shall show that
S_{\Omega}h(\underline{x})
is asymptotic on average to
the weighted mean

\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}(h):=\int_{1}^{T}h\left(\frac{y}{T}\right)\cdot\Vol_{2}(\Omega(y))\,dy,

(1.3)

where

\Omega(y):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,:\,(x,y)\in\Omega\}.

We note that
\Vol_{2}(\Omega(y))
can be computed explicitly (see Lemma
A.1
).
When
h=1
on the interval
[0,1]
, the sum
S_{\Omega}h(\underline{x})
is simply
|Q_{\Omega}(\underline{x})|
and

\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}(h)=\Vol_{3}(\Omega)
. For different oscillatory functions
h
, the
asymptotics of
S_{\Omega}h(\underline{x})
describe the distribution of
Q_{\Omega}(\underline{x})
inside
[1,T)
.

We establish a bound for ”sub-quadratic” moments defined in terms of the function

\theta_{\kappa}(s):=s^{2}/\ln(e+|s|)^{1+\kappa}\quad\hbox{with $\kappa>0$}.

(1.4)

Theorem 1.3
.

With the assumption as in Theorem
1.2
, for every compactly supported Lipschitz function
h:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
\kappa>0
,

\int_{[0,1)^{2}}\theta_{\kappa}\Big(S_{\Omega}h(\underline{x})-\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}(h)\Big)\,d\underline{x}\ll_{h,\zeta,\theta,\kappa}c^{-2}b\cdot(\ln\ln T)^{3+\kappa}(\ln T)^{2}.

In particular,

\int_{[0,1)^{2}}\theta_{\kappa}\Big(|Q_{\Omega}(\underline{x})|-\Vol_{3}(\Omega)\Big)\,d\underline{x}\ll_{\zeta,\theta,\kappa}c^{-2}b\cdot(\ln\ln T)^{3+\kappa}(\ln T)^{2}.

Remark 1.4
.

In view of the volume asymptotics (
1.2
),
for certain ranges of the parameters, the formula in Theorem
1.2
establishes an error term with essentially ”square-root cancellation”.

Theorem
1.3
is proved in Section
9
.
Then Theorem
1.2
is deduced from it in Section
10

using a Borel-Cantelli argument.
We outline the structure of the paper in Section
2
below.

2.
Organisation of the paper

It will be convenient to consider a more geometric framework.
For
\underline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
we consider the unimodular lattice

\Lambda_{\underline{x}}:=\{(\underline{p}+q\underline{x},q)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}\,:\,(\underline{p},q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}\}.

(2.1)

Since
c<\frac{1}{2}
, the map

\Lambda_{\underline{x}}\cap\Omega\rightarrow Q_{\Omega}(\underline{x}):\enskip(\underline{p}+q\underline{x},q)\mapsto q

is a bijection. In particular, Theorem
1.2
can be viewed as an asymptotic
lattice point counting problem for the cardinality
|\Lambda_{\underline{x}}\cap\Omega|
.

It order to produce an asymptotic formula for
|\Lambda_{\underline{x}}\cap\Omega|
,
one usually aims to establish a bound on the
second moment
\int_{[0,1)^{2}}\big||\Lambda_{\underline{x}}\cap\Omega|-\Vol_{3}(\Omega)\big|^{2}\,d\underline{x}
.
This idea goes back, for instance, to the work of
W. Schmidt
[
17
]
.
We also refer to the work Kleinbock, Shi, and Weiss
[
14
]
, where this approach
was developed for ergodic sums.
However, our analysis in Section
5
suggests that
a needed bound for the second moment is not attainable.
As a substitute we establish
a bound for ”sub-quadratic” moments

\int_{[0,1)^{2}}\theta_{\kappa}\big(|\Lambda_{\underline{x}}\cap\Omega|-\Vol_{3}(\Omega)\big)\,d\underline{x}
,
with
\theta_{\kappa}
as in (
1.4
) and
generalize the argument of
[
16
,
17
]

to such sub-quadratic moments.

It will be convenient to view the domains
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
as disjoint union
of smaller domains. To construct such a decomposition, we use the action by the diagonal matrices

a(t):=\hbox{diag}(e^{t_{1}},e^{t_{2}},e^{-t_{1}-t_{2}}),\quad t=(t_{1},t_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}.

In Section
3
, we show that

\Omega=\bigsqcup_{n\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}}a(n)^{-1}\Delta_{\Omega,n},

(2.2)

where
\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}
is a finite subset of
\mathbb{N}_{o}^{2}
and

\Delta_{\Omega,n}
are finite subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
We note that this tessellation procedure is different compared to
the one used in our previous work
[
3
]
. While in
[
3
]

we used varying tessellations defined for each
a(t)
-orbit,
here we construct a tessellation directly in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
This has advantages and disadvantages.
In particular, in our present construction the tiles
\Delta_{\Omega,n}

have more complicated shape. This necessitates a new notion of regularity
(
(\varepsilon,\gamma,M)
-controlled sets), which we develop in Section
4
.

The decomposition (
2.2
) allows us to write

|\Lambda_{\underline{x}}\cap\Omega|=\sum_{n\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}}\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,n}}(a(n)\Lambda_{\underline{x}}),

(2.3)

where
\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,n}}
denote the Siegel transforms of
the characteristic function of the sets
\Delta_{\Omega,n}
.
Hence, the original counting question is reformulated in terms of ergodic
sums for the action of the group
\{a(t):t\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\}
, albeit these sums are computed along a varying
family of functions. The crucial ingredient of our proof is a quasi-independence
estimate (Theorem
9.2
) established in our previous work
[
2
]
.
It gives a quantitative bound for the correlations of
\varphi\circ a(n)
,
n\in\mathbb{N}_{o}^{2}
,
for smooth compactly supported functions
\varphi
on the space space of unimodular lattices. It should be noted that this bound is only useful when
\min(n_{1},n_{2})

is large, and one of the hardest parts of the present paper is consists in
treating the part of the sum (
2.3
) where
\min(n_{1},n_{2})
is small.

In Section
5
we establish various non-divergence estimates for lattices

a(t)\Lambda_{\underline{x}}
with
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
and
\underline{x}\in[0,1)^{2}
(and somewhat more general lattices). Although this question fits in the general framework of non-divergence
of unipotent flows (cf.
[
6
,
19
,
10
]
), we need much more precise information
about the height functions along
a(t)\Lambda_{\underline{x}}
(see, for instance, Corollary
5.5
). The results of Section
5

will be used in the proof of the main theorem to estimate the part of the sum with small

\min(n_{1},n_{2})
(this corresponds to the term
\mathcal{C}^{(1)}_{\Omega}
in Section
9
) and also in the construction of smooth approximations.

In Section
8
we build smooth compactly supported approximations
for the functions
\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,n}}
. Our main technical result here, used in the proof of the main theorem, is Lemma
8.5

(this corresponds to the term
\mathcal{C}^{(2)}_{\Omega}
in Section
9
).
The proof of this lemma uses the notion of

(\varepsilon,\gamma,M)
-controlled sets and the non-divergence estimates from Section
5
.
Ultimately, its proof reduces to an arithmetic problem of estimating the number
of points from
a(t)\Lambda_{\underline{x}}
contained in an
(\varepsilon,\gamma,M)
-controlled set (see Lemma
7.1
)
and to an estimate for correlations of certain lattice counting functions (see Lemma
6.1
).
Those are handled in Sections
6
–
7
.

The proof of Theorem
1.3
is completed in Section
9
.
In Section
10
we deduce Theorem
1.2
from Theorem
1.3
using a Borel-Cantelli argument.
Finally, Theorem
1.1
is deduced from
1.2

in Section
11
.

3.
Tessellations

In this section we show how to decompose the ”hyperboloid strips”

\Omega:=\left\{(\underline{x},y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}\,:\,\begin{array}[]{c}a<|x_{1}x_{2}|\cdot y\leq b,\\[8.5359pt]
\enskip\max(|x_{1}|,|x_{2}|)\leq c,\\[8.5359pt]
\enskip T_{0}\leq y<T\end{array}\right\}.

(3.1)

defined for
1\leq T_{0}<T
,
0<a<b
, and
c>0
. We use the action of the semigroup of diagonal matrices of the form

a(t):=\left(\begin{matrix}e^{t_{1}}&&\\
&e^{t_{2}}&\\
&&e^{-(t_{1}+t_{2})}\end{matrix}\right),\quad\textrm{for $t=(t_{1},t_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}$}.

(3.2)

Our aim below is to show that
\Omega
can be written as a union of certain

a(t)
-translates of smaller pieces
\Delta_{\Omega,n}
, where
n=(n_{1},n_{2})\in\mathbb{N}_{o}^{2}
, and whose dependence
on the parameters is rather mild. More precisely, let

\Delta_{\Omega,n}:=\left\{(\underline{x},y)\,:\,\begin{array}[]{c}a<|x_{1}x_{2}|\cdot y\leq b\\[8.5359pt]
c\,e^{-1}<|x_{i}|\leq c,\enskip\textrm{for $i=1,2$}\\[8.5359pt]
T_{0}e^{-(n_{1}+n_{2})}\leq y<Te^{-(n_{1}+n_{2})}\end{array}\right\}

(3.3)

and

\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}:=\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}_{o}^{2}\,:\,\alpha_{\Omega}\leq n_{1}+n_{2}<\beta_{\Omega}\right\},

(3.4)

where

\alpha_{\Omega}:=\ln\left(\frac{T_{0}c^{2}}{be^{2}}\right)\quad\textrm{and}\quad\beta_{\Omega}:=\ln\left(\frac{Tc^{2}}{a}\right).

(3.5)

Then, we have the following lemma:

Lemma 3.1
.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}_{o}^{2}
,

\Delta_{\Omega,n}\neq\emptyset\implies n\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}

and

\Omega=\bigsqcup_{n\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}}a(n)^{-1}\Delta_{\Omega,n},

Furthermore, for all
n\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}
,

\Delta_{\Omega,n}\subset\left[\shortminus c,c\right]^{2}\times\left(\frac{a}{c^{2}},\frac{be^{2}}{c^{2}}\right].

Proof.

We first note that for every
n=(n_{1},n_{2})\in\mathbb{N}_{o}^{2}
,

a(n)^{-1}\Delta_{\Omega,n}=\left\{(\underline{x},y)\,:\,\begin{array}[]{c}a<|x_{1}x_{2}|y\leq b\\[8.5359pt]
c\,e^{-(n_{i}+1)}<|x_{i}|\leq ce^{-n_{i}},\enskip\textrm{for $i=1,2$}\\[8.5359pt]
T_{0}\leq y<T\end{array}\right\},

so in particular,
a(n)^{-1}\Delta_{\Omega,n}\subset\Omega
and the sets
\{a(n)^{-1}\Delta_{\Omega,n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}
are disjoint. Pick a point
(\underline{x},y)\in\Omega
. Then there
is clearly a unique
n\in\mathbb{N}_{o}^{2}
such that

c\cdot e^{-(n_{i}+1)}<|x_{i}|\leq c\cdot e^{-n_{i}},\quad\textrm{for both $i=1,2$},

and thus
(\underline{x},y)\in a(n)^{-1}\Delta_{\Omega,n}
. Since
(\underline{x},y)
is arbitrary, this shows that

\Omega=\bigsqcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{o}^{2}}a(n)^{-1}\Delta_{\Omega,n}.

(3.6)

However, most of the sets
\Delta_{\Omega,n}
in this union are empty. Indeed, suppose that

\Delta_{\Omega,n}\neq\emptyset
, and pick
(\underline{x},y)\in\Delta_{\Omega,n}
. Then,

a<|x_{1}x_{2}|\cdot y<c^{2}\cdot T\cdot e^{-(n_{1}+n_{2})},

and thus
n_{1}+n_{2}<\ln\left(\frac{Tc^{2}}{a}\right)
. Similarly,

c^{2}\cdot T_{0}\cdot e^{-2}\cdot e^{-(n_{1}+n_{2})}\leq|x_{1}x_{2}|y\leq b,

and thus
\ln\left(\frac{T_{0}c^{2}}{be^{2}}\right)\leq n_{1}+n_{2}
.

In other words,
\Delta_{\Omega,n}
is non-empty only if
n\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}
, and thus the union in (
3.6
) can without loss of generality be restricted to
\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}
.

Finally, suppose that
(\underline{x},y)\in\Delta_{\Omega,n}
for some
n
. Then
\underline{x}\in[\shortminus c,c]^{2}
and

\frac{a}{c^{2}}<\frac{a}{|x_{1}x_{2}|}<y\leq\frac{b}{|x_{1}x_{2}|}\leq\frac{be^{2}}{c^{2}},

and thus

\Delta_{\Omega,n}\subset\left[\shortminus c,c\right]^{2}\times\left(\frac{a}{c^{2}},\frac{be^{2}}{c^{2}}\right]

independently of
n
.
∎

4.
Group perturbations and controlled sets

The aim of this section is to establish
regularity under small
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})
-perturbations for families sets of the as in (
3.3
). Since the sets relevant to our problem degenerate
in some directions, this will need to introduce a new notion of regularity.

We begin with some notation. If
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
, let

E^{y}=\{\underline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,:\,(\underline{x},y)\in E\}\quad\textrm{and}\quad E_{\underline{x}}=\{y\in\mathbb{R}\,:\,(\underline{x},y)\in E\},

for
y\in\mathbb{R}
and
\underline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. We refer to
E^{y}
as the
y
-section
of

E
, and to
E_{\underline{x}}
as the
\underline{x}
-section
of
E
.

The following definition captures a convenient
form of regularity that we will use in the paper.

Definition 4.1

(Controlled set)
.

Let
M>1
and
0<\varepsilon<\gamma<M
. We say that a Borel set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
is
(\varepsilon,\gamma,M)
-controlled
if either

E\subset[-M,M]^{2}\times(\gamma,M]\quad\textrm{and}\quad\sup_{y\in[\gamma,1]}\Vol_{2}(E^{y})\ll_{M}\max\left(\varepsilon,-\frac{\varepsilon}{\gamma}\ln\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\gamma}\right)\right),

or if there is an interval
[\alpha,\beta]
such that

E\subset[-M,M]^{2}\times[\alpha,\beta],\quad\beta-\alpha\ll_{M}\varepsilon,\quad\textrm{and}\quad\alpha\geq\gamma/2

with implicit constants that are independent of
\varepsilon
and
\gamma
. If
E

satisfies the first set of conditions, we say that
E
is
type I
and if
E

satisfies the second set of conditions, we say that
E
is
type II
. We refer
to the implicit constants above as the
bounds for
E
.

Roughly speaking, a controlled set is type I if all of its
y
-sections have uniformly small volumes and it is type II if all of its
\underline{x}
-sections have uniformly small volumes.

Remark 4.2
.

Note that if
\varepsilon<\gamma^{\prime}<\gamma
,
\varepsilon/\gamma^{\prime}<1/e
, and
E
is an
(\varepsilon,\gamma,M)
-controlled set, then
E
is also an
(\varepsilon,\gamma^{\prime},M)
-controlled set.

Let
M>1
.
For the rest of this section, let us fix the following real parameters:

0<a<b\quad\textrm{and}\quad u_{1}^{-}<u_{1}^{+}\leq 1/2,\enskip u_{2}^{-}<u_{2}^{+}\leq 1/2\quad\textrm{and}\quad 0<\gamma<\delta\leq M.

(4.1)

We define the set

\Delta:=\left\{(\underline{x},y)\,:\,\begin{array}[]{c}a<|x_{1}x_{2}|\cdot y\leq b\\[8.5359pt]
u_{i}^{-}<|x_{i}|\leq u_{i}^{+}\enskip\textrm{for $i=1,2$}\\[8.5359pt]
\gamma\leq y<\delta\end{array}\right\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}.

(4.2)

We fix the
\max
-norm on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and denote by
\|\cdot\|_{\textrm{op}}

the operator norm on
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})
induced by the
\max
-norm, in particular,

\|g\cdot\underline{x}\|_{\infty}\leq\|g\|_{\textrm{op}}\cdot\|\underline{x}\|_{\infty},\quad\hbox{for $g\in\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})$ and $\underline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}$}

For
\varepsilon>0
, we denote by
V_{\varepsilon}
the symmetric open neighborhoods around the identity in

\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})
:

V_{\varepsilon}:=\left\{g\in\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})\,:\,\|g-\id\|_{\textrm{op}}<\varepsilon\quad\textrm{and}\quad\|g^{-1}-\id\|_{\textrm{op}}<\varepsilon\right\}.

(4.3)

In other words,

\|g^{\pm 1}.(\underline{x},y)-(\underline{x},y)\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon\cdot\|(\underline{x},y)\|_{\infty},\quad\textrm{for all $g\in V_{\varepsilon}$ and $(\underline{x},y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}$}.

(4.4)

The following lemma is the main result of this section.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\Delta
be as in (
4.2
) with the assumptions (
4.1
).
Then, for every

0<\varepsilon<\min\left(1/(2M),\gamma/(2M),a/(M^{2}+1)\right),

there
are
(\varepsilon,\gamma/2,M+1)
-controlled sets
E_{s}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
,

, such that

\left(g^{-1}\Delta\setminus\Delta\right)\sqcup\left(\Delta\setminus g^{-1}\Delta\right)\subset\bigcup_{s}E_{s}\quad\hbox{for all $g\in V_{\varepsilon}$. }

Furthermore, the bounds in Definition
4.1
for the sets

are independent of the parameters
a,b,u_{1}^{\pm},u_{2}^{\pm},\delta
.

Proof.

Let us fix
\varepsilon
as above. For
g\in V_{\varepsilon}
and
(\underline{x},y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
, we define
(\underline{x}(g),y(g))
by

g.(\underline{x},y):=(\underline{x}(g),y(g))=({x}_{1}(g),x_{2}(g),y(g)).

(4.5)

Then

\max(|x_{1}(g)-x_{1}|,|x_{2}(g)-x_{2}|,|y(g)-y|)<\varepsilon\cdot\|(\underline{x},y)\|_{\infty},

(4.6)

for all
(\underline{x},y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
.
In particular, for
(\underline{x},y)\in\Delta
,

\max(|x_{1}(g)-x_{1}|,|x_{2}(g)-x_{2}|,|y(g)-y|)<M\varepsilon.

Hence, it follows that

u_{i}^{-}-M\varepsilon<|x_{i}(g)|\leq u_{i}^{+}+M\varepsilon\enskip\textrm{for $i=1,2$}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\gamma-M\varepsilon\leq y(g)\leq\delta+M\varepsilon.

Also using that
\varepsilon<1/(2M)
,

\displaystyle\big||x_{1}x_{2}|\cdot y-|x_{1}(g)x_{2}(g)|\cdot y(g)\big|\leq

\displaystyle\big||x_{1}x_{2}|\cdot y-|x_{1}(g)x_{2}|\cdot y\big|+\big||x_{1}(g)x_{2}|\cdot y-|x_{1}(g)x_{2}(g)|\cdot y\big|

\displaystyle+\big||x_{1}(g)x_{2}(g)|\cdot y-|x_{1}(g)x_{2}(g)|\cdot y(g)\big|\leq\varepsilon M^{2}.

Therefore, we also have that

a-\varepsilon M^{2}<|x_{1}(g)x_{2}(g)|\cdot y(g)\leq b+\varepsilon M^{2}.

We conclude that for
g\in V_{\varepsilon}
,

g^{-1}\Delta\subset\Delta_{\varepsilon}^{+},

where

\Delta_{\varepsilon}^{+}:=\left\{(\underline{x},y)\,:\,\begin{array}[]{c}a-\varepsilon M^{2}<|x_{1}x_{2}|\cdot y\leq b+\varepsilon M^{2}\\[8.5359pt]

and

We note that

(4.7)

because
\varepsilon<\gamma/(2M)
and
\varepsilon<1/(2M)
.

Let

E_{1}:=\{(\underline{x},y)\in\Delta_{\varepsilon}^{+}:\,a-\varepsilon M^{2}<|x_{1}x_{2}|\cdot y\leq a\}.

For all

E_{1}^{y}\subset\left(\begin{tabular}[]{ll}$u_{1}^{+}(\varepsilon)$&0\\
0&$u_{2}^{+}(\varepsilon)$\end{tabular}\right)\cdot\left(\Xi\left(\frac{a}{y\cdot u_{1}^{+}(\varepsilon)u_{2}^{+}(\varepsilon)}\right)\setminus\Xi\left(\frac{a-\varepsilon M^{2}}{y\cdot u_{1}^{+}(\varepsilon)u_{2}^{+}(\varepsilon)}\right)\right),

where the set

is defined as in (

It follows from (

and
u_{1}^{+}(\varepsilon),u_{2}^{+}(\varepsilon)<1

\frac{a-\varepsilon M^{2}}{u_{1}^{+}(\varepsilon)u_{2}^{+}(\varepsilon)}>\varepsilon,

and
thus

\Vol_{2}((E_{1})^{y})\ll_{M}-\frac{\varepsilon}{y}\cdot\ln^{-}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{y}\right)\ll-\frac{2\varepsilon}{\gamma}\cdot\ln^{-}\left(\frac{2\varepsilon}{\gamma}\right)

(4.8)

for all

is
(\varepsilon,\gamma/2,M+1)
-controlled.

Let

E_{2}:=\{(\underline{x},y)\in\Delta_{\varepsilon}^{+}:\,u_{1}^{-}(\varepsilon)<x_{1}\leq u_{1}^{-}\}.

Then for all

so that this set is also
(\varepsilon,\gamma/2,M+1)
-controlled.
The set

-controlled of type II.

Finally, we observe that

To handle the set

5.
Height function estimates

In this section we define a height function on the space
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{3}
consisting of all lattices in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
prove some technical level set estimates for this function that will be used later in our analysis of Siegel transforms.

Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{3}
denote the space of all lattices in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, endowed with
the standard action of
\operatorname{GL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})
. Given a bounded Borel function
f:\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

such that the set
\{f\neq 0\}
is bounded, we define the
Siegel transform

\widehat{f}:\widetilde{\mathcal{L}_{3}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

\widehat{f}(\Lambda):=\sum_{\underline{\lambda}\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}}f(\underline{\lambda}),\quad\textrm{for $\Lambda\in\widetilde{\mathcal{L}_{3}}$}.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{3}\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{3}
denote the subspace of all
unimodular
lattices. This subspace is preserved by the restricted
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})
-action, and it is
well-known that
\mathcal{L}_{3}
carries a unique
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})
-invariant Borel probability measure, which we denote by
\mu
. The following classical theorem of Siegel will play an important role in
our analysis.

Theorem 5.1

(Siegel’s theorem)
.

Let
f
be a bounded Borel function with compact support.
Then
\widehat{f}\in L^{1}(\mu)
and

\int_{\mathcal{L}_{3}}\widehat{f}\,d\mu=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(\underline{z})\,d\underline{z},

where the volume measure on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
has been normalized so that
\Vol_{3}([0,1]^{3})=1
.

5.1.
A height function on
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{3}

Let
\{\underline{e}_{1},\underline{e}_{2},\underline{e}_{3}\}
denote the standard
(ordered) basis of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. We extend the
\max
-norm with respect to this basis to the second exterior power
\mathbb{R}^{3}\wedge\mathbb{R}^{3}
as follows. If
\underline{u},\underline{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and

w=\underline{u}\wedge\underline{v}=w_{12}\,\underline{e}_{1}\wedge\underline{e}_{2}+w_{13}\,\underline{e}_{1}\wedge\underline{e}_{3}+w_{23}\,\underline{e}_{2}\wedge\underline{e}_{3},

then
\|w\|_{\infty}=\max(|w_{12}|,|w_{13}|,|w_{23}|)
.

Let
\Lambda
be a (not necessarily unimodular) lattice in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. We define
the functions

\displaystyle s_{1}(\Lambda)

\displaystyle:=\inf\left\{\|\underline{\lambda}\|_{\infty}\,:\,\underline{\lambda}\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}\right\},

\displaystyle s_{2}(\Lambda)

\displaystyle:=\inf\left\{\|\underline{\lambda}_{1}\wedge\underline{\lambda}_{2}\|_{\infty}\,:\,\underline{\lambda}_{1}\wedge\underline{\lambda}_{2}\neq 0,\enskip\underline{\lambda}_{1},\underline{\lambda}_{2}\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}\right\},

\displaystyle s_{3}(\Lambda)

\displaystyle:=\inf\left\{|\alpha|\,:\,\alpha\,\underline{e}_{1}\wedge\underline{e}_{2}\,\wedge\underline{e}_{3}=\underline{\lambda}_{1}\wedge\underline{\lambda}_{2}\wedge\underline{\lambda}_{3}\neq 0,\enskip\underline{\lambda}_{1},\underline{\lambda}_{2},\underline{\lambda}_{3}\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}\right\},

and the
height function

\height(\Lambda):=\min\big(s_{1}(\Lambda),s_{2}(\Lambda),s_{3}(\Lambda)\big)^{-1}.

(5.1)

Since

\|g\cdot\underline{w}\|_{\infty}\leq\|g\|_{\textrm{op}}\cdot\|\underline{w}\|_{\infty}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\|g\cdot\underline{w}\|_{\infty}\geq\|g^{-1}\|^{-1}_{\textrm{op}}\cdot\|\underline{w}\|_{\infty},

for all
g\in\operatorname{GL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})
and
\underline{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
(where
\|\cdot\|_{\textrm{op}}
denotes the operator norm with respect to the
\max
-norm on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
), we have

\|g\|^{-1}_{\textrm{op}}\cdot\height(\Lambda)\leq\height(g.\Lambda)\leq\|g^{-1}\|_{\textrm{op}}\cdot\height(\Lambda),

(5.2)

for all
g\in\operatorname{GL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})
and
\Lambda\in\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{3}
. Before we proceed to the main topic of this section, we recall an important inequality due to Schmidt
[
18
, Lemma 2]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

For every bounded Borel function with compact support,

|\widehat{f}(\Lambda)|\ll_{\supp(f)}\|f\|_{\infty}\cdot\height(\Lambda),\quad\textrm{for all $\Lambda\in\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{3}$},

where the implicit constants only depend on
\supp(f)
.

5.2.
Main results

We recall that

a(t)=\left(\begin{matrix}e^{t_{1}}&&\\
&e^{t_{2}}&\\
&&e^{-(t_{1}+t_{2})}\end{matrix}\right),\quad\textrm{for $t=(t_{1},t_{2})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}$}.

Given
\underline{x}=({x}_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
r>0
, we define the lattice

\Lambda_{\underline{x},r}:=\left\{\underline{p}+q(\iota(\underline{x})+r\underline{e}_{3})\,:\,\underline{p}\in\Span_{\mathbb{Z}}(\underline{e}_{1},\underline{e}_{2}),\enskip q\in\mathbb{Z}\right\},

(5.3)

where
\iota(\underline{x}):=({x}_{1},x_{2},0)
.
We note that
\Lambda_{\underline{x},r}
is a unimodular lattice if and only if
r=1
.

Our first theorem in this section provides uniform upper bounds of the height function along
a(t)
-orbits of lattices of the form
\Lambda_{\underline{x},r}
. Here and later in the paper,
we use the notation

\lfloor t\rfloor:=\min(t_{1},t_{2})\quad\hbox{for $t=(t_{1},t_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}$.}

Theorem 5.3
.

For all
r>0
and
t=(t_{1},t_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
,

\sup\left\{\height(a(t)\Lambda_{\underline{x},r})\,:\,\underline{x}\in[0,1)^{2}\right\}\leq\max\left(e^{t_{1}+t_{2}}/r,e^{-\lfloor t\rfloor}\right).

Our second theorem roughly tells us that the map

t\mapsto\height(a(t)\Lambda_{\underline{x},r})
is not large on a big volume set.

Theorem 5.4
.

For every
r>0
,
L>\max(1,1/r)
and
t\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
,

\Vol_{2}\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in[0,1)^{2}\,:\,\height(a(t)\Lambda_{\underline{x},r})\geq L\right\}\right)\ll\max(r^{-1},r^{-2})L^{-3}+r^{-1}L^{-2}e^{-\lfloor t\rfloor},

where the implicit constants are independent of
L,r
and
t
.

The theorems above provide useful upper bounds on certain integrals involving the
height functions restricted to super-level sets. To state these bounds, we define the sets

M_{t,r}(\eta):=\left\{\underline{x}\in[0,1)^{2}\,:\,\height(a(t)\Lambda_{\underline{x},r})\geq\eta\right\}

(5.4)

for
\eta\geq 0
. We then have the following corollary, which we prove at the end of this section:

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
\rho>0
and
\theta:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be an increasing measurable function such that
u\mapsto\frac{\theta(u)}{u^{2}}
is decreasing on
[1,\infty)
. Then, for every
t\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
,
r>\rho
and
\eta\geq e^{2}\rho^{-1}
, we have

\int_{M_{t,r}(\eta)}\theta(\height(a(t)\Lambda_{\underline{x},r}))\,d\underline{x}\ll\left(\max(r^{-1},r^{-2})\eta^{-1}+r^{-1}e^{-\lfloor t\rfloor}\right)\cdot\int_{e^{-2}\eta}^{e^{t_{1}+t_{2}+1}\max(1,\rho^{-1})}\frac{\theta(u)}{u^{3}}\,du,

where the implicit constants are independent of
\rho,\eta,r
and
t
.

5.3.
Lemmas about heights

Lemma 5.6

(Uniform Lower Bound for
s_{1}
)
.

For all
r>0
and
t=(t_{1},t_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
,

\inf\left\{s_{1}(a(t)\Lambda_{\underline{x},r})\,:\,\underline{x}\in[0,1)^{2}\right\}\geq\min\left(e^{\lfloor t\rfloor},r\cdot e^{-(t_{1}+t_{2})}\right).

Proof.

Let
\underline{x}=({x}_{1},x_{2})\in[0,1)^{2}
,
r>0
, and
t=(t_{1},t_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
.
We note that for every
\underline{p}=(p_{1},p_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and

, we have

\|a(t)(\underline{p}+q(\iota(\underline{x})+r\underline{e}_{3}))\|_{\infty}=\max\left(e^{t_{1}}\cdot|p_{1}+qx_{1}|,e^{t_{2}}\cdot|p_{2}+qx_{2}|,r\cdot|q|\cdot e^{-(t_{1}+t_{2})}\right).

We assume that
\underline{p}+q(\iota(\underline{x})+r\underline{e}_{3})\neq 0
. If
q=0
, then
\underline{p}\neq 0
, and thus

\|a(t)(\underline{p}+q(\iota(\underline{x})+r\underline{e}_{3}))\|_{\infty}\geq e^{\lfloor t\rfloor}.

If

, then

\|a(t)(\underline{p}+q(\iota(\underline{x})+r\underline{e}_{3}))\|_{\infty}\geq r\cdot e^{-(t_{1}+t_{2})}.

We get a uniform lower bound by taking the minimum of these two bounds.
∎

Lemma 5.7

(Volume Bound for
s_{1}
)
.

For every
0<\varepsilon<1
,
r>0
and
t\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
, we have

\Vol_{2}\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in[0,1)^{2}\,:\,s_{1}(a(t)\Lambda_{\underline{x},r})\leq\varepsilon\right\}\right)\ll\frac{\varepsilon^{3}}{r},

where the implicit constants are independent of
\varepsilon,r
and
t
.

Proof.

Let
0<\varepsilon<1
and
r>0
and
t=(t_{1},t_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
. Pick
\underline{x}=({x}_{1},x_{2})\in[0,1)^{2}
such that

s_{1}(a(t)\Lambda_{\underline{x},r})\leq\varepsilon.

This means that we can find a non-zero vector
\underline{p}+q(\iota(\underline{x})+r\underline{e}_{3})\in\Lambda_{\underline{x},r}

such that

\Lambda_{\underline{x}}(t):=e^{t_{1}}(p_{1}+qx_{1})\,\underline{e}_{1}+e^{t_{2}}(p_{2}+qx_{2})\,\underline{e}_{2}+re^{-(t_{1}+t_{2})}q\,\underline{e}_{3}\in a(t)\Lambda_{\underline{x},r}

satisfies
\|\Lambda_{\underline{x}}(t)\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon
. Writing this information coordinate-wise, we get the inequalities

|p_{1}+qx_{1}|\leq\varepsilon\cdot e^{-t_{1}},\enskip|p_{2}+qx_{2}|\leq\varepsilon\cdot e^{-t_{2}},\enskip|q|\leq\frac{\varepsilon\cdot e^{t_{1}+t_{2}}}{r}.

(5.5)

Since
\varepsilon<1
and
t_{1},t_{2}\geq 0
, there are no non-zero solutions for
p
when
q=0
.

For
q\neq 0
, we note that since
\underline{x}=({x}_{1},x_{2})\in[0,1)^{2}
, there are at most
O(q^{2})
choices for
\underline{p}=(p_{1},p_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Furthermore, for each such choice
of
\underline{p}
,

\Vol_{2}\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in[0,1)^{2}\,:\,|p_{1}+qx_{1}|\leq\varepsilon\cdot e^{-t_{1}},\enskip|p_{2}+qx_{2}|\leq\varepsilon\cdot e^{-t_{2}}\right\}\right)\ll\frac{\varepsilon^{2}\cdot e^{-(t_{1}+t_{2})}}{q^{2}},

where the implicit constants are independent of
\underline{p}
,
\varepsilon
and
t
. In particular, upon summing over all possible
\underline{p}
and
q
, we see that the volume of the set of
\underline{x}\in[0,1)^{2}
for which the inequalities in (

Let us introduce some notation which will be used in the proofs below. Given

r>0,\enskip\underline{x}\in[0,1)^{2},\enskip\underline{p}^{(1)},\underline{p}^{(2)}\in\Span_{\mathbb{Z}}(\underline{e}_{1},\underline{e}_{2}),\enskip q_{1},q_{2}\in\mathbb{Z},

(5.6)

Furthermore, given

, we define

\displaystyle\omega_{\underline{x}}(t)

\displaystyle:=a(t)\left(\left(\underline{p}^{(1)}+q_{1}(\iota(\underline{x})+r\underline{e}_{3})\right)\wedge\left(\underline{p}^{(2)}+q_{2}(\iota(\underline{x})+r\underline{e}_{3})\right)\right)

\displaystyle=a(t)\left(\underline{p}^{(1)}\wedge\underline{p}^{(2)}+\left(q_{2}\underline{p}^{(1)}-q_{1}\underline{p}^{(2)}\right)\wedge\underline{x}+r\left(q_{2}\underline{p}^{(1)}-q_{1}\underline{p}^{(2)}\right)\wedge\underline{e}_{3}\right).

\omega_{\underline{x}}(t)=e^{t_{1}+t_{2}}\,\omega_{1,2}(\underline{x})\,\underline{e}_{1}\wedge\underline{e}_{2}+r\,e^{-t_{2}}w_{1}\,\underline{e}_{1}\wedge\underline{e}_{3}+r\,e^{-t_{1}}\,w_{2}\,\underline{e}_{2}\wedge\underline{e}_{3},

\|\omega_{\underline{x}}(t)\|_{\infty}=\max\left(e^{t_{1}+t_{2}}\cdot|m(\underline{p})+(w_{1}x_{2}-w_{2}x_{1})|,e^{-t_{2}}\cdot r\cdot|w_{1}|,e^{-t_{1}}\cdot r\cdot|w_{2}|\right).

(5.7)

Note that if

Lemma 5.8

(Uniform Lower Bound for

Note that

Lemma 5.9

(Volume Bound for
s_{2}
)
.

For every

,
r>0
and
t\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}

\Vol_{2}\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in[0,1)^{2}\,:\,s_{2}(a(t)\Lambda_{\underline{x},r})\leq\varepsilon\right\}\right)\ll\frac{\varepsilon^{3}}{r^{2}}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}\cdot e^{-\lfloor t\rfloor}}{r},

where the implicit constants are independent of
\varepsilon,r
and

Proof.

Let
0<\varepsilon<1
,
r>0
and
t=(t_{1},t_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}

such

This means that we can find two linearly independent vectors
\underline{v}_{1}
and
\underline{v}_{2}

as in (
5.6
) such that
\|\omega_{\underline{x}}(t)\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon
. By (

), this results in the bounds

|m(\underline{p})+(w_{1}x_{2}-w_{2}x_{1})|\leq\varepsilon\cdot e^{-(t_{1}+t_{2})},\enskip|w_{1}|\leq\frac{\varepsilon\cdot e^{t_{2}}}{r},\enskip|w_{2}|\leq\frac{\varepsilon\cdot e^{t_{1}}}{r}.

We make two observations:

•

If

\underline{w}=0
and

. Since
\varepsilon<1
and

and
m(\underline{p})
is an integer,

. This readily implies that
\underline{p}^{(1)}\wedge\underline{p}^{(2)}=0
, so

\underline{p}^{(1)}
and
\underline{p}^{(2)}
are linearly dependent, and thus
\underline{v}_{1}
and
\underline{v}_{2}
are linearly dependent as well, contrary to our assumption.

•

If
\underline{w}=0
and
(q_{1},q_{2})\neq(0,0)

q_{2}\underline{v}_{1}-q_{1}\underline{v}_{2}=0
, which contradicts our assumption that
\underline{v}_{1}
and
\underline{v}_{2}
are linearly independent.

We can thus without loss of generality assume that
\underline{w}\neq 0
and

(q_{1},q_{2})\neq(0,0)
.

For a fixed
\underline{w}\neq 0
and
m(\underline{p})\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\Vol_{2}\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in[0,1)^{2}\,:\,|m(\underline{p})+(w_{1}x_{2}-w_{2}x_{1})|\leq\varepsilon\cdot e^{-(t_{1}+t_{2})}\right\}\right)\ll\frac{\varepsilon\cdot e^{-(t_{1}+t_{2})}}{\max(|w_{1}|,|w_{2}|)},

where the implicit constants are independent of
\underline{p}
and
t
. Furthermore,
since
0<\varepsilon<1
and
t_{1},t_{2}\geq 0

choices for

. We also note from (

•

O\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{2}\cdot e^{t_{1}+t_{2}}}{r^{2}}\right)

\underline{w}
with
w_{1},w_{2}\neq 0

•

O\left(\frac{\varepsilon\cdot e^{t_{2}}}{r}\right)

\underline{w}
with

\underline{w}
with

Summing over all of these choices, we get

\displaystyle\Vol_{2}\left(\left\{\underline{x}\in[0,1)^{2}\,:\,s_{2}(a(t)\Lambda_{\underline{x},r})\leq\varepsilon\right\}\right)\ll\frac{\varepsilon^{3}}{r^{2}}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}\cdot e^{-t_{1}}}{r}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}\cdot e^{-t_{2}}}{r},

which implies the claim.
∎

6.
Correlations between the number of shifted lattice points in boxes

If
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a bounded Borel set, we define the counting function

N_{B}(\underline{x})=\left|\left(\mathbb{Z}^{2}+\underline{x}\right)\cap B\right|,\quad\textrm{for $\underline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}$}.

(6.1)

Note that
N_{B}
is
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-periodic, and thus completely determined by its values on

[0,1)^{2}
.

Our main result in this section reads as follows.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
M>0
and let
D^{(1)}
and
D^{(2)}
be Borel subsets of the square
[\shortminus M,M]^{2}
.
Let
t=(t_{1},t_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
with
t_{1}\leq t_{2}
and define

D^{(i)}_{t}=\left(\begin{matrix}e^{-t_{1}}&0\\
0&e^{-t_{2}}\end{matrix}\right)D^{(i)}\quad\textrm{for $i=1,2$}.

Then, for all
(q_{1},q_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
,

\int_{[0,1)^{2}}N_{D^{(1)}_{t}}(q_{1}\underline{x})N_{D^{(2)}_{t}}(q_{2}\underline{x})\,d\underline{x}\leq F_{t}\left(\frac{\max(q_{1},q_{2})}{\gcd(q_{1},q_{2})}\right)\cdot\max\left(\Vol_{2}(D^{(1)}),\Vol_{2}(D^{(2)})\right),

where
F_{t}
is defined as in (
B.3
).

We will derive Lemma
6.1
from the following general result.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
be bounded Borel sets in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and let
q_{1}
and
q_{2}

be relatively prime positive integers. Then,

\int_{[0,1)^{2}}N_{B_{1}}(q_{1}\underline{x})N_{B_{2}}(q_{2}\underline{x})\,d\underline{x}\leq\left|\mathbb{Z}^{2}\cap\left(q_{2}B_{1}-q_{1}B_{2}\right)\right|\cdot\min\left(\frac{\Vol_{2}(B_{1})}{q_{1}^{2}},\frac{\Vol_{2}(B_{2})}{q_{2}^{2}}\right).

Proof.

Note that
\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2}=\bigsqcup_{\underline{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}E_{\underline{k}}(q_{1},q_{2})
, where

E_{\underline{k}}(q_{1},q_{2}):=\big\{(\underline{p}_{1},\underline{p}_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2}\,:\,q_{2}\underline{p}_{1}-q_{1}\underline{p}_{2}=\underline{k}\big\},\quad\textrm{for $\underline{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}$}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1)^{2}}N_{B_{1}}(q_{1}\underline{x})\,N_{B_{2}}(q_{2}\underline{x})\,d\underline{x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\underline{p}_{1},\underline{p}_{2}}\int_{[0,1)^{2}}\chi_{B_{1}}(\underline{p}_{1}+q_{1}\underline{x})\,\chi_{B_{2}}(\underline{p}_{2}+q_{2}\underline{x})\,d\underline{x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\underline{k}}C_{\underline{k}}(q_{1},q_{2}),

where
C_{\underline{k}}(q_{1},q_{2}):=\sum_{(\underline{p}_{1},\underline{p}_{2})\in E_{\underline{k}}(q_{1},q_{2})}\int_{[0,1)^{2}}\chi_{B_{1}}(\underline{p}_{1}+q_{1}\underline{x})\,\chi_{B_{2}}(\underline{p}_{2}+q_{2}\underline{x})\,d\underline{x}
.

Fix
\underline{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and
(\underline{p}_{1}^{\prime},\underline{p}_{2}^{\prime})\in E_{\underline{k}}(q_{1},q_{2})
. Then,

E_{\underline{k}}(q_{1},q_{2})=\left\{\left(\underline{p}_{1}^{\prime}+q_{1}\underline{l},\underline{p}_{2}^{\prime}+q_{2}\underline{l}\right)\,:\,\underline{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\right\}.

(6.2)

Indeed, if
(\underline{p}_{1},\underline{p}_{2})
is any point in
E_{\underline{k}}(q_{1},q_{2})
, then
q_{2}(\underline{p}_{1}-\underline{p}_{1}^{\prime})=q_{1}(\underline{p}_{2}-\underline{p}_{2}^{\prime})
. Since
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
are relatively prime integers, we must have
\underline{p}_{1}-\underline{p}_{2}^{\prime}=q_{1}\underline{l}
and
\underline{p}_{2}-\underline{p}_{2}^{\prime}=q_{2}\underline{l}
for some (unique)
\underline{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, thus proving (
6.2
).

Hence, for a fixed choice of
(\underline{p}_{1}^{\prime},\underline{p}_{2}^{\prime})\in E_{\underline{k}}(q_{1},q_{2})
, the identity (
6.2
) allows us to rewrite the term
C_{\underline{k}}(q_{1},q_{2})
as follows:

\displaystyle C_{\underline{k}}(q_{1},q_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\underline{l}}\int_{[0,1)^{2}}\chi_{B_{1}}(\underline{p}_{1}^{\prime}+q_{1}(\underline{l}+\underline{x}))\chi_{B_{2}}(\underline{p}_{2}^{\prime}+q_{2}(\underline{l}+\underline{x}))\,d\underline{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\chi_{B_{1}}(\underline{p}_{1}^{\prime}+q_{1}\underline{x})\chi_{B_{2}}(\underline{p}_{2}^{\prime}+q_{2}\underline{x})\,d\underline{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\chi_{B_{1}}(\underline{p}_{1}^{\prime}-q_{1}\underline{p}_{2}^{\prime}/q_{2}+q_{1}\underline{x})\chi_{B_{2}}(q_{2}\underline{x})\,d\underline{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\chi_{B_{1}}(\underline{k}/q_{2}+q_{1}\underline{x})\chi_{B_{2}}(q_{2}\underline{x})\,d\underline{x},

where, in the last step, we have used the fact that
q_{2}\underline{p}_{1}^{\prime}-q_{1}\underline{p}_{2}^{\prime}=\underline{k}
. We conclude
that

\displaystyle C_{\underline{k}}(q_{1},q_{2})

\displaystyle=\Vol_{2}\left(\left(\frac{1}{q_{1}}B_{1}-\frac{\underline{k}}{q_{1}q_{2}}\right)\cap\frac{1}{q_{2}}B_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{q_{1}q_{2}}\right)^{2}\cdot\Vol_{2}\left(\left(q_{2}B_{1}-\underline{k}\right)\cap q_{1}B_{2}\right).

In particular,

\displaystyle C_{\underline{k}}(q_{1},q_{2})

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{q_{1}q_{2}}\right)^{2}\cdot\min\left(\Vol_{2}\left(q_{2}B_{1}\right),\Vol_{2}\left(q_{1}B_{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\min\left(\frac{\Vol_{2}(B_{1})}{q_{1}^{2}},\frac{\Vol_{2}(B_{2})}{q_{2}^{2}}\right)\quad\textrm{for all $\underline{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}$},

and

C_{\underline{k}}(q_{1},q_{2})=0,\quad\textrm{for all $\underline{k}\notin q_{2}B_{1}-q_{1}B_{2}$}.

Hence,

\sum_{\underline{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}C_{\underline{k}}(q_{1},q_{2})\leq\left|\mathbb{Z}^{2}\cap\left(q_{2}B_{1}-q_{1}B_{2}\right)\right|\cdot\min\left(\frac{\Vol_{2}(B_{1})}{q_{1}^{2}},\frac{\Vol_{2}(B_{2})}{q_{2}^{2}}\right),

which finishes the proof.
∎

Proof of Lemma
6.1
.

Fix
(q_{1},q_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
and write
q_{1}=sq^{\prime}_{1}
and
q_{2}=sq_{2}^{\prime}
, where

s=\gcd(q_{1},q_{2})
and
q_{1}^{\prime}
and
q_{2}^{\prime}
are relatively prime. Since multiplication
by positive integers on the torus

preserves the Lebesgue measure, we have

\int_{[0,1)^{2}}N_{D^{(1)}_{t}}(q_{1}\underline{x})N_{D^{(2)}_{t}}(q_{2}\underline{x})\,d\underline{x}=\int_{[0,1)^{2}}N_{D^{(1)}_{t}}(q^{\prime}_{1}\underline{x})N_{D^{(2)}_{t}}(q^{\prime}_{2}\underline{x})\,d\underline{x}.

If we apply Lemma
6.2
to the right-hand side with

B_{1}=D^{(1)}_{t}\quad\textrm{and}\quad B_{2}=D^{(2)}_{t},

and note that
q^{\prime}_{2}D^{(1)}_{t}-q^{\prime}_{1}D^{(2)}_{t}\subset[\shortminus M_{1},M_{1}]\times[\shortminus M_{2},M_{2}]
, where

M_{i}=2\max(q_{1}^{\prime},q_{2}^{\prime})\cdot M\cdot e^{-t_{i}}\quad\textrm{for $i=1,2$}.

we get

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1)^{2}}N_{D^{(1)}_{t}}(q^{\prime}_{1}\underline{x})N_{D^{(2)}_{t}}(q^{\prime}_{2}\underline{x})\,d\underline{x}

\displaystyle\leq\left|\mathbb{Z}^{2}\cap\left(q^{\prime}_{2}D^{(1)}_{t}-q^{\prime}_{1}D^{(2)}_{t}\right)\right|\cdot\min\left(\frac{\Vol_{2}\left(D^{(1)}_{t}\right)}{(q^{\prime}_{1})^{2}},\frac{\Vol_{2}\left(D^{(2)}_{t}\right)}{(q^{\prime}_{2})^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{G(M_{1},M_{2})}{\max(q^{\prime}_{1},q_{2}^{\prime})^{2}}\cdot e^{-(t_{1}+t_{2})}\cdot\max\left(\Vol_{2}\left(D^{(1)}\right),\Vol_{2}\left(D^{(2)}\right)\right),

where
G
is defined as in (

). Hence,

\int_{[0,1)^{2}}N_{D^{(1)}_{t}}(q^{\prime}_{1}\underline{x})N_{D^{(2)}_{t}}(q^{\prime}_{2}\underline{x})\,d\underline{x}\leq F_{t}(\max(q_{1}^{\prime},q_{2}^{\prime}))\cdot\max\left(\Vol_{2}(D^{(1)}),\Vol_{2}(D^{(2)})\right),

where
F_{t}
is defined as in (
B.3
). This finishes the proof.
∎

7.
Mean counting within controlled sets

In this section we prove
L^{2}
-bounds for Siegel transforms of indicator functions of controlled sets. These bounds will be useful later in Section
8
when we
analyze smooth approximations of counting functions.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
M>1
and
0<\varepsilon<3\varepsilon<\gamma<1
.
We suppose that

E\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
is an
(\varepsilon,\gamma,M)
-controlled set.
Then, for all
t=(t_{1},t_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
such that

t_{1}+t_{2}>\max(1,-\ln\left({\gamma}/{2}\right)),

\int_{[0,1)^{2}}\widehat{\chi}_{E}(a(t)\Lambda_{\underline{x}})^{2}\,d\underline{x}\ll_{M}e^{-(t_{1}+t_{2})}+\max\left(\varepsilon,-\frac{\varepsilon}{\gamma}\ln\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\gamma}\right)\right)\cdot\max(1,(t_{1}+t_{2}))^{2},

where the implicit constants depend only on
M
.

Proof.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
be a bounded Borel set, let
E(t)=a(t)^{-1}E
, and note that

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1)^{2}}\widehat{\chi}_{E}(a(t)\Lambda_{\underline{x}})^{2}\,d\underline{x}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{q_{1},q_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}}\,\sum_{\underline{p}_{1},\underline{p}_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\int_{[0,1)^{2}}\chi_{E(t)^{q_{1}}}(\underline{p}_{1}+q_{1}\underline{x})\chi_{E(t)^{q_{2}}}(\underline{p}_{2}+q_{2}\underline{x})\,d\underline{x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{q_{1},q_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{[0,1)^{2}}N_{D_{q_{1}}}(q_{1}\underline{x})N_{D_{q_{2}}}(q_{2}\underline{x})\,d\underline{x},

where
N_{\bullet}
is defined as in (
6.1
) and
D_{q}=E(t)^{q}
for
q\in\mathbb{Z}
. We define

J:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}\,:\,E^{y}\neq\emptyset\},

and note that for every
q\in\mathbb{Z}
,

E(t)^{q}=\left(\begin{matrix}e^{-t_{1}}&\\
&e^{-t_{2}}\end{matrix}\right)E^{q(t)},

where
q(t)=e^{-(t_{1}+t_{2})}q
. In particular,

E(t)^{q}\neq\emptyset\iff q\in J_{t}:=e^{t_{1}+t_{2}}J.

Hence,

\int_{[0,1)^{2}}\widehat{\chi}_{E}(a(t)\Lambda_{\underline{x}})^{2}\,d\underline{x}\ll\sum_{q_{1},q_{2}\in J_{t}}\int_{[0,1)^{2}}N_{D^{(1)}_{t}}(q_{1}\underline{x})N_{D^{(2)}_{t}}(q_{2}\underline{x})\,d\underline{x},

where
D^{(i)}=E^{q_{i}(t)}
for
i=1,2
, and
D^{(i)}_{t}
is defined as in Lemma
6.1
. The same lemma now tells us that

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1)^{2}}\widehat{\chi}_{E}(a(t)\Lambda_{\underline{x}})^{2}\,d\underline{x}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{q_{1},q_{2}\in J_{t}}F_{t}\left(\frac{\max(q_{1},q_{2})}{\gcd(q_{1},q_{2})}\right)\cdot\max\left(\Vol_{2}\left(E^{q_{1}(t)}\right),\Vol_{2}\left(E^{q_{2}(t)}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\ll\left(\sum_{q_{1},q_{2}\in J_{t}}F_{t}\left(\frac{\max(q_{1},q_{2})}{\gcd(q_{1},q_{2})}\right)\right)\cdot\sup_{y\in J}\Vol_{2}(E^{y}),

where
F_{t}
is defined as in (
B.3
).

The arguments up to this point have not made use of any special properties of
E
.
In what follows, we will fix
0<\varepsilon<3\varepsilon<\gamma
and assume that

E
is an
(\varepsilon,\gamma,M)
-controlled set (see Definition
4.1
).
The analysis will depend on whether
E
is type I or type II.

Let us first assume that
E
is type I. Then,

J\subset(\gamma,M]\quad\textrm{and}\quad\sup_{y\in J}\Vol_{2}(E^{y})\ll_{M}\max\left(\varepsilon,-\frac{\varepsilon}{\gamma}\ln\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\gamma}\right)\right).

Furthermore, by Lemma
B.1
(with
\alpha=\gamma
and
\beta=M
),

\sum_{q_{1},q_{2}\in J_{t}}F_{t}\left(\frac{\max(q_{1},q_{2})}{\gcd(q_{1},q_{2})}\right)\ll_{M}e^{-(t_{1}+t_{2})}+\max(1,-\ln(\gamma))\cdot\max(1,t_{1}+t_{2}),

provided that

(7.1)

We conclude that if the conditions (

) hold, then

\int_{[0,1)^{2}}\widehat{\chi}_{E}(a(t)\Lambda_{\underline{x}})^{2}\,d\underline{x}\ll(t_{1}+t_{2})^{2}\cdot\max\left(\varepsilon,-\frac{\varepsilon}{\gamma}\ln\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\gamma}\right)\right).

(7.2)

Let us now assume that

is type II. Then,

E\subset[-M,M]^{2}\times[\alpha,\beta],

where
\frac{\gamma}{2}\leq\alpha
, and
\beta-\alpha\ll\varepsilon
. In particular,

J\subset[\alpha,\beta]\quad\textrm{and}\quad\sup_{y\in J}\Vol_{2}(E^{y})\ll M^{2}.

By Lemma
B.1
,

\displaystyle\sum_{q_{1},q_{2}\in J_{t}}F_{t}\left(\frac{\max(q_{1},q_{2})}{\gcd(q_{1},q_{2})}\right)

\displaystyle\ll_{M}e^{-(t_{1}+t_{2})}+(\beta-\alpha)\cdot\max\left(1,\ln\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)\right)\cdot\max(1,t_{1}+t_{2})

(7.3)

If

. Otherwise , the right-hand side is bounded from above by

\frac{\gamma}{2}\leq\alpha
. Hence,

\int_{[0,1)^{2}}\widehat{\chi}_{E}(a(t)\Lambda_{\underline{x}})^{2}\,d\underline{x}\ll e^{-(t_{1}+t_{2})}+\max\left(\varepsilon,\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\gamma}\right)\cdot\max(1,(t_{1}+t_{2})),

(7.4)

provided that (
7.3
) hold.

Now we combine (
7.2
) and (
7.4
).
Since
3\varepsilon<\gamma
, we have

\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\gamma}\leq-\frac{\varepsilon}{\gamma}\ln\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\gamma}\right).

Hence, if we combine (
7.2
) and (
7.4
), we get the uniform estimate (independent of whether
E
is type I or type II):

\int_{[0,1)^{2}}\widehat{\chi}_{E}(a(t)\Lambda_{\underline{x}})^{2}\,d\underline{x}\ll e^{-(t_{1}+t_{2})}+\max\left(\varepsilon,-\frac{\varepsilon}{\gamma}\ln\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\gamma}\right)\right)\cdot\max(1,(t_{1}+t_{2}))^{2},

provided that (
7.1
) and (
7.3
) both hold. Note that since

\frac{\gamma}{2}\leq\alpha
, the second conditions in (
7.1
) and (
7.3
) are both satisfied if

t_{1}+t_{2}>\max(1,-\ln\left({\gamma}/{2}\right)).

This finishes the proof.
∎

8.
Smooth approximations

Let
\mathcal{L}_{3}
denote the space of unimodular lattices in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. We can identify

\mathcal{L}_{3}
with the homogeneous space
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})/\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
via the map
g\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})\mapsto g\mathbb{Z}^{3}
. Fix a basis
\{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{8}\}
of the Lie algebra

\mathfrak{sl}_{3}(\mathbb{R})
. We adopt the following slight abuse of notation: for every

i=1,\ldots,8
, let
D_{i}
both denote the differential operator

D_{i}\rho(g)=\frac{d}{dt}\rho(\exp(tY_{i})g)|_{t=0}

on
C_{b}^{\infty}(\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R}))
and the differential operator

D_{i}\varphi=\frac{d}{dt}\rho(\exp(tY_{i})\Lambda)|_{t=0}

on
C_{b}^{\infty}(\mathcal{L}_{3})
(which we identify with the space of bounded and smooth right
\Gamma
-invariant functions on the group
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})
via the map above). Differential operators can clearly be composed, and any composition of the
D_{1},\ldots,D_{8}

can be rewritten as a linear combination of compositions of the form

D_{m}:=D_{1}^{m_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ D_{8}^{m_{8}}
for some vector

m=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{8})\in\mathbb{N}_{o}^{8}
(with the convention that
D_{0}=\id
).
We define the norms

\|\rho\|_{C_{b}^{s}(\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})}:=\max\{\|D_{m}\rho\|_{\infty}\,:\,m_{1}+\ldots+m_{8}\leq s\},\quad\textrm{for $\rho\in C_{b}^{\infty}(\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R}))$}

and

\|\varphi\|_{C^{s}(\mathcal{L}_{3})}:=\max\{\|D_{m}\varphi\|_{\infty}\,:\,m_{1}+\ldots+m_{8}\leq s\},\quad\textrm{for $\varphi\in C_{b}^{\infty}(\mathcal{L}_{3})$}.

Fix a right-invariant Riemannian metric on
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})
, and denote by
\Lip
the corresponding Lipschitz semi-norm on
\mathcal{L}_{3}
(viewed as the right quotient space
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})/\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
). We define

\mathcal{N}_{s}(\varphi):=\max\big(\|\varphi\|_{C_{b}^{s}(\mathcal{L}_{3})},\Lip(\varphi)\big),\quad\textrm{for $\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{L}_{3})$}.

(8.1)

For
\varepsilon>0
, let
V_{\varepsilon}
denote
the symmetric open neighborhoods around the identity in
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})

defined in (
4.3
).
For the rest of this paper, we fix a non-negative smooth function
\rho_{\varepsilon}
on
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})
whose support is
contained in
V_{\varepsilon}
and has integral one with respect to the Haar measure on
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})
. We leave it to the reader to verify that
\rho_{\varepsilon}
can be chosen so that for every integer
s\geq 1
,
there is an integer
\sigma_{s}>0
such that

\|\rho_{\varepsilon}\|_{C_{b}^{s}(\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R}))}\ll\varepsilon^{-\sigma_{s}},\quad\textrm{for all $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$}

(8.2)

and where the implicit constants do not depend on
\varepsilon
.

By Lemma
[
1
, Lemma 4.11]
, for every
L>1
there exists
a smooth function
\eta_{L}:\mathcal{L}_{3}\rightarrow[0,1]
such that

\{\height\leq L/2\}\subset\{\eta_{L}=1\}\subset\supp(\eta_{L})\subset\{\height\leq 2L\},

(8.3)

where
\height
is the height function on
\mathcal{L}_{3}
defined in (
5.1
), with
the property that for every
s\geq 1
and

(8.4)

where the implicit constants only depend on
s
, but not on
L
.

If
F
is a locally bounded Borel function on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
\rho\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G)
, we denote by
\rho*F
the (action) convolution

(\rho*F)(u):=\int_{G}\rho(g)\,F(g^{-1}u)\,dm(g),\quad\textrm{for $u\in\mathbb{R}^{3}$},

where
m
is a (fixed) Haar measure on
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})
. We note that

D_{m}(\rho*F):=(D_{m}\rho)*F,\quad\textrm{for every $m\in\mathbb{N}_{o}^{8}$}.

(8.5)

Finally, if
\varphi
is a locally bounded function on
\mathcal{L}_{3}
, we write

(\rho*\varphi)(u)=\int_{G}\rho(g)\,\varphi(g^{-1}\Lambda)\,dm(g),\quad\textrm{for $\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}_{3}$}.

Our first main result in this section now reads as follows.

Let us now discuss Lipschitz semi-norms. By definition, if
\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}_{3}
and

\dist
is a right-invariant distance on
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})
, we can induce a (non-invariant) distance
on
\mathcal{L}_{3}
by

\dist(\Lambda,h.\Lambda)=\inf\left\{\dist(\id,h\gamma)\,:\,\gamma\in\Stab_{\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})}(\Lambda)\right\},\quad\textrm{for $h\in\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})$}.

The corresponding Lipschitz semi-norms on
C_{b}^{\infty}(\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R}))
and
C_{b}^{\infty}(\mathcal{L}_{3})
are thus given by

\Lip_{\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})}(\rho)=\sup\left\{\frac{|\rho(g)-\rho(hg)|}{\dist(\id,h)}\,:\,g,h\in\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R}),\enskip h\neq\id\right\},

for
\rho\in C_{b}^{\infty}(\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R}))
and

\Lip_{\mathcal{L}_{3}}(\varphi)=\sup\left\{\frac{|\varphi(\Lambda)-\varphi(h.\Lambda)|}{\dist(\Lambda,h.\Lambda)}\,:\,\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}_{3},\enskip h\notin\Stab_{\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})}(\Lambda)\right\},

for
\varphi\in C_{b}^{\infty}(\mathcal{L}_{3})
.

One checks that there is a constant
\theta>0
such that

\Lip_{\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})}(\rho_{\varepsilon})\ll\varepsilon^{-\theta},

(8.6)

with implicit constants that are independent of
\varepsilon
. Upon possibly enlarging
\sigma_{s}
, we can (and will) always assume that
\theta<\sigma_{s}
. We also leave
to the reader to show that the function
\eta_{L}
can be chosen so that

\left|\eta_{L}(\Lambda)-\eta_{L}(h.\Lambda)\right|\ll\dist(\Lambda,h.\Lambda),

(8.7)

with implicit constants that are independent of
L
.

Lemma 8.3
.

Let
B
be a bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Then, for every
0<\varepsilon<1
and for every bounded Borel function
f:\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that
\{f\neq 0\}\subset B
and
L\geq 1
,

\Lip_{\mathcal{L}_{3}}((\rho_{\varepsilon}*\widehat{f})\cdot\eta_{L})\ll_{B}\varepsilon^{-\theta}\cdot L,

where the implicit constants only depend on
B
.

Proof.

We use in the proof that

\|\rho_{\varepsilon}\|_{C_{b}^{s}(\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R}))}\ll\varepsilon^{-\theta}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\Lip_{\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})}(\rho_{\varepsilon})\ll\varepsilon^{-\theta}.

For every
h\in G
and
\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}_{3}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|(\rho_{\varepsilon}*\widehat{f})(\Lambda)-(\rho_{\varepsilon}*\widehat{f})(h.\Lambda)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{(V_{\varepsilon}\cup h^{-1}V_{\varepsilon})}\left|\rho_{\varepsilon}(g)-\rho_{\varepsilon}(hg)\right|\cdot\widehat{f}(g^{-1}.\Lambda)\,dg

\displaystyle\leq\Lip_{\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})}(\rho_{\varepsilon})\cdot\dist(\id,h)\cdot\int_{(V_{\varepsilon}\cup h^{-1}V_{\varepsilon})}|\widehat{f}(g^{-1}.\Lambda)|\,dm(g)

\displaystyle\ll\varepsilon^{-\theta}\cdot\dist(\id,h)\cdot\int_{(V_{\varepsilon}\cup h^{-1}V_{\varepsilon})}|\widehat{f}(g^{-1}.\Lambda)|\,dm(g),

where we in the last estimate have used (
8.6
). Note that the same computation goes through with
h_{\gamma}:=h\gamma
for
\gamma\in\Stab_{\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})}(\Lambda)
, and thus

\left|(\rho_{\varepsilon}*\widehat{f})(\Lambda)-(\rho_{\varepsilon}*\widehat{f})(h.\Lambda)\right|\leq\varepsilon^{-\theta}\cdot\dist(\Lambda,h.\Lambda)\cdot\int_{(V_{\varepsilon}\cup h_{\gamma}^{-1}V_{\varepsilon})}|\widehat{f}(g^{-1}.\Lambda)|\,dm(g).

(8.8)

We also note that

\displaystyle((\rho_{\varepsilon}*\widehat{f})\cdot\eta_{L})(\Lambda)-((\rho_{\varepsilon}*\widehat{f})\cdot\eta_{L})(h.\Lambda)=

\displaystyle\left((\rho_{\varepsilon}*\widehat{f})(\Lambda)-(\rho_{\varepsilon}*\widehat{f})(h.\Lambda)\right)\cdot\eta_{L}(\Lambda)

\displaystyle+(\rho_{\varepsilon}*\widehat{f})(h.\Lambda)\cdot\left(\eta_{L}(\Lambda)-\eta_{L}(h.\Lambda)\right).

(8.9)

We can without loss of generality assume that at least one of the points
\Lambda

and
h.\Lambda
belong to
\supp(\eta_{L})
; otherwise the Lipschitz condition is trivially satisfied. The rest of our analysis is now divided into two cases.

Case I:

\Lambda,h.\Lambda\in\supp(\eta_{L})
, so that
\hbox{\rm ht}(\Lambda),\hbox{\rm ht}(h\Lambda)\leq 2L
.

By Lemma
5.2
and (
5.2
), we have

|\widehat{f}(g^{-1}h.\Lambda)|\ll_{B}\|f\|_{\infty}\cdot\height(g^{-1}h.\Lambda)\leq\|f\|_{\infty}\cdot\|g\|_{\textrm{op}}\cdot\height(h.\Lambda),\quad\textrm{for all $g,h\in\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})$},

and thus,

\displaystyle\int_{(V_{\varepsilon}\cup h_{\gamma}^{-1}V_{\varepsilon})}|\widehat{f}(g^{-1}.\Lambda)|\,dg

\displaystyle\leq\int_{V_{\varepsilon}}|\widehat{f}(g^{-1}.\Lambda)|\,dg+\int_{V_{\varepsilon}}|\widehat{f}(g^{-1}h.\Lambda)|\,dg

\displaystyle\ll_{B}(1+\varepsilon)\cdot\|f\|_{\infty}\cdot\height(\Lambda)+(1+\varepsilon)\cdot\height(h.\Lambda)

\displaystyle\ll\|f\|_{\infty}\cdot\max(\height(\Lambda),\height(h.\Lambda)),

(8.10)

for all
g\in V_{\varepsilon}
and for all
h\in\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})
, where the implicit constants only depend on
B
(and not on
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
). Hence, we deduce from (
8.8
) and (
8.10
) that the first term on the right-hand side in (
8.9
) is bounded above in absolute value by

\ll\|f\|_{\infty}\cdot\max(\height(\Lambda),\height(h.\Lambda))\cdot\eta_{L}(\Lambda)\cdot\dist(\Lambda,h.\Lambda)\cdot\varepsilon^{-\theta},

(8.11)

while the second term is bounded above in absolute value by

\ll\|f\|_{\infty}\cdot\height(h.\Lambda)\cdot\left|\eta_{L}(\Lambda)-\eta_{L}(h.\Lambda)\right|\ll\|f\|_{\infty}\cdot\height(h.\Lambda)\cdot\varepsilon^{-\theta}\cdot\dist(\Lambda,h.\Lambda),

(8.12)

where we in the last inequality have used (
8.7
). By our assumption,
\height(\Lambda)\leq 2L
and
\height(h.\Lambda)\leq 2L
, so we conclude that

\left|(\rho_{\varepsilon}*\widehat{f})(\Lambda)-(\rho_{\varepsilon}*\widehat{f})(h.\Lambda)\right|\ll\|f\|_{\infty}\cdot\varepsilon^{-\theta}\cdot L\cdot\dist(\Lambda,h.\Lambda).

Case II:

\Lambda\in\supp(\eta_{L})

but
h.\Lambda\notin\supp(\eta_{L})
or the opposite
.

We split this case into two sub-cases. Let us first assume that
\dist(\Lambda,h.\Lambda)\geq 1
. Then, by the same analysis as above,

\displaystyle|(\rho_{\varepsilon}*\widehat{f})(\Lambda)\eta_{L}(\Lambda)|

\displaystyle\ll\varepsilon^{-\theta}\cdot\int_{V_{\varepsilon}}|\widehat{f}(g^{-1}.\Lambda)|\,dm(g)

\displaystyle\ll_{B}\varepsilon^{-\theta}\cdot\|f\|_{\infty}\cdot\height(\Lambda)\leq\varepsilon^{-\theta}\cdot\|f\|_{\infty}\cdot L

\displaystyle\ll\varepsilon^{-\theta}\cdot\|f\|_{\infty}\cdot L\cdot\dist(\Lambda,h.\Lambda).

If
\dist(\Lambda,h.\Lambda)\leq 1
, we can choose
h_{\gamma}
such that
\dist(\id,h_{\gamma})\leq 1
, and hence there is a compact set
K
, which is independent of
\varepsilon
(and
h
as long as

\dist(\Lambda,h.\Lambda)\leq 1
), with the property that

V_{\varepsilon}\cup h_{\gamma}^{-1}V_{\varepsilon}\subset K.

Hence, in this case, by (
8.6
), Lemma
5.2
and (
5.2
),

\left|(\rho_{\varepsilon}*\widehat{f})(\Lambda)-(\rho_{\varepsilon}*\widehat{f})(h.\Lambda)\right|\ll_{B}\varepsilon^{-\theta}\cdot\dist(\Lambda,h.\Lambda)\cdot\|f\|_{\infty}\cdot\left(\int_{K}\|g^{-1}\|_{\textrm{op}}\,dm(g)\right)\cdot\height(\Lambda).

Similarly, when

,

|(\rho_{\varepsilon}*\widehat{f})(h.\Lambda)|\ll_{K}\varepsilon^{-\theta}\cdot\|f\|_{\infty}\cdot\height(\Lambda).

We conclude that in the difference (
8.9
) both terms are bounded above in absolute value by

\ll\|f\|_{\infty}\cdot\varepsilon^{-\theta}\cdot L\cdot\dist(\Lambda,h.\Lambda),

where we for the second term have used (

). In both sub-cases, we see that

\left|(\rho_{\varepsilon}*\widehat{f})(\Lambda)\eta_{L}(\Lambda)-(\rho_{\varepsilon}*\widehat{f})(h.\Lambda)\eta_{L}(h.\Lambda)\right|\ll\|f\|_{\infty}\cdot\varepsilon^{-\theta}\cdot L\cdot\dist(\Lambda,h.\Lambda).

The opposite case is handled in the same way by interchanging

, and we are done.
∎

The proof of Lemma

Lemma 8.5

\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}\leq 3\cdot\left(\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{(1)}+\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{(2)}+\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{(3)}\right),

where

\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{(k)}:=\int_{\mathcal{L}_{3}}\theta_{\kappa}\left(D_{\Omega}^{(k)}\right)\,d\nu,\quad\textrm{for $k=1,2,3$}.

In what follows, we will estimate these partial moments separately.

9.4.
An upper bound on
\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{(1)}

Since
\theta_{\kappa}
is convex and satisfies
(P2)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{L}_{3}}\theta_{\kappa}\left(\sum_{n\in\mathcal{G}^{-}_{\Omega}(\xi_{T})}\phi_{\Omega,n}\circ a(n)\,d\nu\right)

\displaystyle\leq|\mathcal{G}^{-}_{\Omega}(\xi_{T})|^{2}\cdot\int_{\mathcal{L}_{3}}\theta_{\kappa}\left(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}^{-}_{\Omega}(\xi_{T})|}\sum_{n\in\mathcal{G}^{-}_{\Omega}(\xi_{T})}\phi_{\Omega,n}\circ a(n)\,d\nu\right)

\displaystyle\leq|\mathcal{G}^{-}_{\Omega}(\xi_{T})|\cdot\sum_{n\in\mathcal{G}^{-}_{\Omega}(\xi_{T})}\int_{\mathcal{L}_{3}}\theta_{\kappa}\big(\phi_{\Omega,n}\circ a(n)\big)\,d\nu.

(9.8)

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{L}_{3}}\theta_{\kappa}\big(\phi_{\Omega,n}\circ a(n)\big)\,d\nu

\displaystyle\leq 2\max\left(\int_{\mathcal{L}_{3}}\theta_{\kappa}\big(\widehat{h}_{\Omega,n}\circ a(n)\big)\,d\nu,\theta_{\kappa}\left(\int_{\mathcal{L}_{3}}\widehat{h}_{\Omega,n}\,d\mu\right)\right)

(9.9)

By Siegel’s Theorem (Theorem
5.1
) and (
9.4
),

\int_{\mathcal{L}_{3}}\widehat{h}_{\Omega,n}\,d\mu=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}}h_{\Omega,n}(\underline{u},y)\,d\underline{u}dy\leq\|h\|_{\infty}\Vol_{3}(\Delta_{\Omega,n})\ll_{h}b\ll 1.

(9.10)

We stress that the implicit constants only depend on
\|h\|_{\infty}
(which is assumed to be fixed throughout the proof).

Now, we observe that

\big|\widehat{h}_{\Omega,n}(a(n)\Lambda_{\underline{x}})\big|\leq\|h\|_{\infty}\cdot\widehat{\chi}_{\Delta_{\Omega,n}}(a(n)\Lambda_{\underline{x}}),\quad\textrm{for all $\underline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}$}.

Furthermore, by (
9.4
),

\chi_{\Delta_{\Omega,n}}(\underline{u},y)\leq\chi_{[-1,1]^{2}\times[0,1]}(\underline{u},r_{\Omega}),\quad\textrm{where $r_{\Omega}:=\frac{c^{2}}{be^{2}}$},

so we conclude that

\big|\widehat{h}_{\Omega,n}(a(n)\Lambda_{\underline{x}})\big|\leq\|h\|_{\infty}\cdot\widehat{\chi}_{[\shortminus 1,1]^{2}\times[0,1]}(a(n)\Lambda_{\underline{x},r_{\Omega}}),

where the lattice
\Lambda_{\underline{x},r_{\Omega}}
is defined as in (
5.3
). Thus, by Lemma
5.2
,

\big|\widehat{h}_{\Omega,n}(a(n)\Lambda_{\underline{x}})\big|\ll_{h}\height(a(n)\Lambda_{\underline{x},r_{\Omega}}).

(9.11)

We note that
r_{\Omega}\geq e^{-2}\zeta
by (
9.1
).

Let us now use these estimates to bound
\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{(1)}
. We introduce the set

E_{T,n}:=\left\{\underline{x}\in[0,1)^{2}\,:\,\height(a(n)\Lambda_{\underline{x},r_{\Omega}})\geq e^{4}\zeta^{-1}\right\}.

By Corollary
5.5
, applied with
\rho=e^{-2}\zeta
and
\eta=e^{4}\zeta^{-1}
, we have
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{o}^{2}
,

\int_{E_{T,n}}\theta_{\kappa}\big(\height(a(n)\Lambda_{\underline{x},r_{\Omega}})\big)\,d\underline{x}\ll_{\zeta}r_{\Omega}^{-1}\cdot\int_{e^{2}\zeta^{-1}}^{\infty}\frac{\theta_{\kappa}(u)}{u^{3}}\,du\ll_{\kappa,\zeta}r_{\Omega}^{-1},

where the implicit constants are independent of
T
and
n
. Here we used that the function
u\mapsto\frac{\theta_{\kappa}(u)}{u^{3}}
is integrable on
[1,\infty)
. Hence, from (
9.11
), since
\theta_{\kappa}

is increasing and satisfies (P2), we conclude that

\int_{E_{T,n}}\theta_{\kappa}\big(\widehat{h}_{\Omega,n}(a(n)\Lambda_{\underline{x}})\big)\,d\underline{x}\ll_{h}\int_{E_{T,n}}\theta_{\kappa}\big(\height(a(n)\Lambda_{\underline{x},r_{\Omega}})\big)\,d\underline{x}\ll_{\kappa,\zeta}r_{\Omega}^{-1},

(9.12)

for all
n
.

It remains to estimate the integral over
E_{T,n}^{c}
.
To do so, note that

\int_{E_{T,n}^{c}}\theta_{\kappa}\big(\widehat{h}_{\Omega,n}(a(n)\Lambda_{\underline{x}})\big)\,d\underline{x}\ll_{h}\int_{E_{T,n}^{c}\cap P_{\Omega,n}}\theta_{\kappa}\big(\height(a(n)\Lambda_{\underline{x},r_{\Omega}})\big)\,d\underline{x}\ll_{\kappa,\zeta}\Vol_{2}(P_{\Omega,n}),

where

P_{\Omega,n}:=\left\{\underline{x}\in[0,1)^{2}\,:\,\widehat{h}_{\Omega,n}(a(n)\Lambda_{\underline{x}})>0\right\}.

Using that
c<\frac{1}{2}
, and the upper bounds, which follow from (
9.4
), we obtain that

\big|\widehat{h}_{\Omega,n}(a(n)\Lambda_{\underline{x}})\big|\leq\|h\|_{\infty}\cdot\widehat{\chi}_{\left[\shortminus\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]^{2}\times\left[0,\frac{1}{2}\right]}\big(a(n)\Lambda_{\underline{x},{r_{\Omega}}/{2}}\big),

we see that

\displaystyle P_{\Omega,n}

\displaystyle\subseteq\left\{\underline{x}\in[0,1)^{2}\,:\,a(n)\Lambda_{\underline{x},{r_{\Omega}}/{2}}\cap\left(\left[\shortminus\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]^{2}\times\left[0,\frac{1}{2}\right]\right)\neq\{0\}\right\}

\displaystyle\subseteq\left\{\underline{x}\in[0,1)^{2}\,:\,s_{1}\big(a(n)\Lambda_{\underline{x},r_{\Omega}/2}\big)\leq\frac{1}{2}\right\},

where
s_{1}
is defined as in Subsection
5.1
. Hence, by Lemma
5.7
, applied with
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{2}
and
r=r_{\Omega}/2
, we have

\Vol_{2}(P_{\Omega,n})\ll r_{\Omega}^{-1},\quad\textrm{for all $n$}.

Therefore,

\int_{E_{T,n}^{c}}\theta_{\kappa}\big(\widehat{h}_{\Omega,n}(a(n)\Lambda_{\underline{x}})\big)\,d\underline{x}\ll_{h,\kappa,\zeta}r_{\Omega}^{-1}.

If we combine this estimate with
(
9.10
) and (
9.12
), we deduce from (
9.9
) that

\int_{\mathcal{L}_{3}}\theta_{\kappa}\big(\varphi_{\Omega,n}\circ a(n)\big)\,d\nu\ll_{h,\kappa,\zeta}r_{\Omega}^{-1}\ll\frac{b}{c^{2}}

(9.13)

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{o}^{2}
. Hence, by (
9.8
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{(1)}\leq|\mathcal{G}^{-}_{\Omega}(\xi_{T})|\cdot\sum_{n\in\mathcal{G}^{-}_{\Omega}(\xi_{T})}\int_{\mathcal{L}_{3}}\theta_{\kappa}\big(\varphi_{\Omega,n}\circ a(n)\big)\,d\nu\ll_{h,\kappa,\zeta}|\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}^{-}(\xi_{T})|^{2}\cdot\frac{b}{c^{2}}.

Since

|\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}^{-}(\xi_{T})|\leq 2\xi_{T}\cdot(\beta_{\Omega}-\alpha_{\Omega})\quad\textrm{and}\quad(\beta_{\Omega}-\alpha_{\Omega})^{2}\leq|\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}|,

we conclude that

|\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}^{-}(\xi_{T})|^{2}\ll\xi_{T}^{2}\cdot|\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}|,

and

\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{(1)}\ll_{h,\kappa,\zeta}\frac{b\cdot\xi^{2}_{T}}{c^{2}}\cdot|\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}|.

(9.14)

9.5.
An upper bound on
\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{(2)}

Since
\theta_{\kappa}(u)\leq u^{2}
for all
u
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{L}_{3}}\theta_{\kappa}\big(D_{\Omega}^{(2)}\big)\,d\nu

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathcal{L}_{3}}\left(\sum_{n\in\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}^{+}(\xi_{T})}(\phi_{\Omega,n}-\varphi_{\Omega,n})\circ a(n)\right)^{2}\,d\nu

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{n\in\mathcal{G}_{T}^{+}}\big\|(\phi_{\Omega,n}-\varphi_{\Omega,n})\circ a(n)\big\|_{L^{2}(\nu)}\right)^{2}.

Let us assume that

\xi_{T}>\max(1,-\ln\left({a}/{2}\right)).

(9.15)

Then for all
n\in\mathcal{G}^{+}_{\Omega}(\xi_{T})
, we have

n_{1}+n_{2}>\max(1,-\ln\left({a}/{2}\right))
.
In particular, if we additionally assume that

\varepsilon_{T}<a\zeta^{2}/100\quad\textrm{and}\quad L_{T}\geq 2e^{2}\zeta^{-1},

(9.16)

then
the conditions of Lemma
8.5
are satisfied, from which we conclude that

\displaystyle\big\|(\phi_{\Omega,n}-\varphi_{\Omega,n})\circ a(n)\big\|_{L^{2}(\nu)}\ll_{h,\zeta}

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon_{T}}{a}\cdot\max(1,n_{1}+n_{2})^{1/2}

\displaystyle+e^{-(n_{1}+n_{2})/2}+\max\Big(\varepsilon_{T},-\frac{\varepsilon_{T}}{a}\ln\Big(\frac{\varepsilon_{T}}{a}\Big)\Big)^{1/2}\cdot\max(1,n_{1}+n_{2})

\displaystyle+\left(L_{T}^{-1/2}+e^{-\lfloor n\rfloor/2}\right)\cdot\max(1,n_{1}+n_{2})^{1/2}

for all
n\in\mathcal{G}^{+}_{\Omega}(\xi_{T})
.
We note that

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}^{+}(\xi_{T})}\max(1,n_{1}+n_{2})^{1/2}\leq\beta_{\Omega}^{1/2}\cdot|\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}^{+}(\xi_{T})|,

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}^{+}(\xi_{T})}\max(1,n_{1}+n_{2})\leq\beta_{\Omega}\cdot|\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}^{+}(\xi)|,

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}^{+}(\xi_{T})}e^{-(n_{1}+n_{2})/2}\leq 2e^{-\xi_{T}/2}\cdot|\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}^{+}(\xi_{T})|,

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}^{+}(\xi_{T})}e^{-\lfloor n\rfloor/2}\max(1,n_{1}+n_{2})^{1/2}\leq 2\beta_{\Omega}^{1/2}\cdot e^{-\xi_{T}/2}\cdot|\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}^{+}(\xi_{T})|,

and thus

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{L}_{3}}\theta_{\kappa}\big(D_{\Omega}^{(2)}\big)\,d\nu

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{n\in\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}^{+}(\xi_{T})}\big\|(\phi_{\Omega,n}-\varphi_{\Omega,n})\circ a(n)\big\|_{L^{2}(\nu)}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\ll_{h,\zeta}\gamma\cdot|\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}^{+}(\xi_{T})|^{2}\leq\gamma\cdot\beta_{\Omega}^{2}\cdot|\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}|,

(9.17)

where

\gamma:=\beta_{\Omega}\cdot\max\left(\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{T}}{a}\right)^{2},\beta_{\Omega}\cdot\max\left(\varepsilon_{T},-\frac{\varepsilon_{T}}{a}\ln\Big(\frac{\varepsilon_{T}}{a}\Big)\right),L_{T}^{-1},e^{-\xi_{T}}\right).

9.6.
An upper bound on
\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{(3)}

We have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{L}_{3}}\left(D_{\Omega}^{(3)}\right)^{2}\,d\nu=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{L}_{3}}\sum_{m,n\in\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}^{+}(\xi_{T})}\varphi_{\Omega,m}\circ a(m)\cdot\varphi_{\Omega,n}\circ a(n)\,d\nu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\in\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}^{+}(\xi_{T})\\[2.84544pt]
\|m-n\|<\xi_{T}\end{subarray}}\int_{\mathcal{L}_{3}}\varphi_{\Omega,m}\circ a(m)\cdot\varphi_{\Omega,n}\circ a(n)\,d\nu

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\in\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}^{+}(\xi_{T})\\[2.84544pt]
\|m-n\|\geq\xi_{T}\end{subarray}}\int_{\mathcal{L}_{3}}\varphi_{\Omega,m}\circ a(m)\cdot\varphi_{\Omega,n}\circ a(n)\,d\nu.

(9.18)

We estimate the two sums on the right-hand side separately.

By Lemma
5.2
and (
9.4
),

|\varphi_{\Omega,n}|\ll_{h,\zeta}L_{T}
for all
n
.
Then, by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we deduce

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{L}_{3}}\left|\varphi_{\Omega,m}\circ a(m)\cdot\varphi_{\Omega,n}\circ a(n)\right|\,d\nu

\displaystyle\ll_{h,\zeta}(\ln L_{T})^{1+\kappa}\int_{\mathcal{L}_{3}}\ln\left(e+|\varphi_{\Omega,m}\circ a(m)|\right)^{-(1+\kappa)/2}\cdot\left|\varphi_{\Omega,m}\circ a(m)\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\cdot\ln\left(e+|\varphi_{\Omega,n}\circ a(n)|\right)^{-(1+\kappa)/2}\cdot\left|\varphi_{\Omega,n}\circ a(n)\right|\,d\nu

\displaystyle\leq(\ln L_{T})^{1+\kappa}\left(\int_{\mathcal{L}_{3}}\theta_{\kappa}(\varphi_{\Omega,m}\circ a(m))\,d\nu\cdot\int_{\mathcal{L}_{3}}\theta_{\kappa}(\varphi_{\Omega,n}\circ a(n))\,d\nu\right)^{1/2}.

Hence, by (
9.13
), we have that

\int_{\mathcal{L}_{3}}\left|\varphi_{\Omega,m}\circ a(m)\cdot\varphi_{\Omega,n}\circ a(n)\right|\,d\nu\ll_{h,\zeta}\frac{b{\cdot(\ln L_{T})^{1+\kappa}}}{c^{2}}.

so that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\in\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}^{+}(\xi_{T})\\[2.84544pt]
\|m-n\|<\xi_{T}\end{subarray}}\int_{\mathcal{L}_{3}}\left|\varphi_{\Omega,m}\circ a(m)\cdot\varphi_{\Omega,n}\circ a(n)\right|\,d\nu\ll_{h,\zeta}\frac{b{\cdot(\ln L_{T})^{1+\kappa}}}{c^{2}}\cdot|\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}(\xi_{T})|,

(9.19)

where

\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}(\xi_{T}):=\left\{(m,n)\in\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}^{+}(\xi_{T})\,:\,\|m-n\|<\xi_{T}\right\}.

For every
m\in\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}^{+}(\xi_{T})
, there are
O(\xi_{T}^{2})
elements
n
in
\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}^{+}(\xi_{T})
such that
\|m-n\|<\xi_{T}
, and thus
|\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}(\xi_{T})|\ll\xi_{T}^{2}\cdot|\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}^{+}(\xi_{T})|
, where the implicit constants are independent of
\Omega
. We thus see that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\in\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}^{+}(\xi_{T})\\[2.84544pt]
\|m-n\|<\xi_{T}\end{subarray}}\int_{\mathcal{L}_{3}}\varphi_{\Omega,m}\circ a(m)\cdot\varphi_{\Omega,n}\circ a(n)\,d\nu\ll_{h,\zeta}\frac{b{\cdot(\ln L_{T})^{1+\kappa}}}{c^{2}}\cdot\xi_{T}^{2}\cdot|\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}|.

(9.20)

Let us now turn to the second sum in (
9.18
). By construction, the function
\varphi_{\Omega,n}

belongs to
\mathbb{R}\cdot 1+C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathcal{L}_{3})
and satisfies
\int_{\mathcal{L}_{3}}\varphi_{\Omega,n}\,d\mu=0
. Hence, by Theorem
9.2
, there exist

s\geq 1
and
\delta>0
such that

\left|\int_{\mathcal{L}_{3}}\varphi_{\Omega,m}\circ a(m)\cdot\varphi_{\Omega,n}\circ a(n)\,d\nu\right|\ll e^{-\delta\min(\lfloor m\rfloor,\lfloor n\rfloor,\|m-n\|)}\,\mathcal{N}_{s}(\varphi_{\Omega,m})\cdot\mathcal{N}_{s}(\varphi_{\Omega,n}),

where the implicit constants are independent of
m,n,T
. Furthermore, by Lemma

8.1
, there exists a constant
\sigma_{s}>0
such that

\mathcal{N}_{s}(\varphi_{\Omega,n})\ll_{h}\varepsilon_{T}^{-\sigma_{s}}\cdot L_{T},\quad\textrm{for all $n$},

where the implicit constants are independent of
\Omega
and
n
. We conclude that for
all
m,n\in\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}^{+}(\xi_{T})
such that
\|m-n\|\geq\xi_{T}
, we have

\left|\int_{\mathcal{L}_{3}}\varphi_{\Omega,m}\circ a(m)\cdot\varphi_{\Omega,n}\circ a(n)\,d\nu\right|\ll_{h}e^{-\delta\xi_{T}}\cdot\varepsilon_{T}^{-2\sigma_{s}}\cdot L_{T}^{2},

and thus

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\in\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}^{+}(\xi_{T})\\[2.84544pt]
\|m-n\|\geq\xi_{T}\end{subarray}}\int_{\mathcal{L}_{3}}\varphi_{\Omega,m}\circ a(m)\cdot\varphi_{\Omega,n}\circ a(n)\,d\nu\ll_{h}e^{-\delta\xi_{T}}\cdot\varepsilon_{T}^{-2\sigma_{s}}\cdot L_{T}^{2}\cdot|\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}^{+}(\xi_{T})|^{2}.

(9.21)

If we now plug our estimates (
9.20
) and (
9.21
) into (
9.18
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{L}_{3}}\theta_{\kappa}\big(D_{\Omega}^{(3)}\big)\,d\nu\ll_{h,\zeta}

\displaystyle\frac{b\cdot(\ln L_{T})^{1+\kappa}\cdot\xi_{T}^{2}}{c^{2}}\cdot|\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}|+e^{-\delta\xi_{T}}\cdot\varepsilon_{T}^{-2\sigma_{s}}\cdot L_{T}^{2}\cdot|\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}^{+}(\xi_{T})|^{2}.

Since
|\mathcal{G}_{\Omega}^{+}(\xi_{T})|\ll\beta_{\Omega}^{2}
, where the implicit constants are independent of
\Omega
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{L}_{3}}\theta_{\kappa}\big(D_{\Omega}^{(3)}\big)\,d\nu\ll_{h,\zeta}\left(\frac{b\cdot(\ln L_{T})^{1+\kappa}\cdot\xi_{T}^{2}}{c^{2}}++e^{-\delta\xi_{T}}\cdot\varepsilon_{T}^{-2\sigma_{s}}\cdot L_{T}^{2}\cdot\beta_{\Omega}^{2}\right)\cdot|\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}|.

(9.22)

9.7.
Putting it all together

Let us now summarize what we have so far:

\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}\leq 3\cdot\left(\mathcal{C}^{(1)}_{\Omega}+\mathcal{C}^{(2)}_{\Omega}+\mathcal{C}^{(3)}_{\Omega}\right),

and by (
9.14
), (
9.5
), (
9.22
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{(1)}

\displaystyle\ll_{h,\kappa,\zeta}\frac{b\cdot\xi^{2}_{T}}{c^{2}}\cdot|\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}|,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{(2)}

\displaystyle\ll_{h,\zeta}\beta_{\Omega}^{3}\cdot\max\left(\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{T}}{a}\right)^{2},\beta_{\Omega}\cdot\max\left(\varepsilon_{T},-\frac{\varepsilon_{T}}{a}\ln\Big(\frac{\varepsilon_{T}}{a}\Big)\right),{L_{T}}^{-1},e^{-\xi_{T}}\right)\cdot|\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}|,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{(3)}

\displaystyle{\ll_{h}\Big(\frac{b\cdot(\ln L_{T})^{1+\kappa}\cdot\xi_{T}^{2}}{c^{2}}+e^{-\delta\xi_{T}}\cdot\varepsilon_{T}^{-2\sigma_{s}}\cdot L_{T}^{2}\cdot\beta_{\Omega}^{2}\Big)\cdot|\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}|},

provided that the parameters have been chosen so that

\displaystyle\xi_{T}\geq\max(1,-\ln\left({a}/{2}\right)),\quad\varepsilon_{T}<a\zeta^{2}/100,\quad L_{T}\geq e^{2}\zeta^{-1},\quad c^{2}>\zeta\cdot b.

(9.23)

We further assume that

a\geq(\ln T)^{-\theta},\quad\textrm{for some $\theta>0$}.

Then, in particular,
\beta_{\Omega}=\ln\left(\frac{Tc^{2}}{a}\right)\ll_{\theta}\ln T.

In what follows, we will choose the parameters
\xi_{T},\varepsilon_{T}
, and
L_{T}
, so that

\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}\ll_{h,\kappa,\zeta}\left(\frac{b\cdot(\ln L_{T})^{1+\kappa}\cdot\xi_{T}^{2}}{c^{2}}+O_{\theta,\rho}\big((\ln T)^{-\rho}\big)\right)\cdot|\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}|

(9.24)

for all
\rho>0
.

To prove (
9.24
), let

\xi_{T}=\xi_{o}\cdot\ln(\ln T),\quad L_{T}=(\ln T)^{3+\eta},\quad\varepsilon_{T}=(\ln T)^{-\gamma}

for some
positive
constants
\xi_{o},\eta
and
\gamma
that will be chosen later.
With these choices, we see that

\displaystyle\beta^{3}_{\Omega}\cdot\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{T}}{a}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\ll_{\theta}(\ln T)^{3-2(\gamma-\theta)},

\displaystyle\beta_{\Omega}^{4}\cdot\max\left(\varepsilon_{T},-\frac{\varepsilon_{T}}{a}\ln\Big(\frac{\varepsilon_{T}}{a}\Big)\right)

\displaystyle\ll_{\theta}(\ln T)^{4-(\gamma-\theta)}\cdot\ln(\ln T),

\displaystyle\beta_{\Omega}^{3}\cdot\max\left({L_{T}}^{-1},e^{-\xi_{T}}\right)

\displaystyle\ll_{\theta}\max\left((\ln T)^{-\eta},(\ln T)^{3-\xi_{o}}\right),

\displaystyle e^{-\delta\xi_{T}}\cdot\varepsilon_{T}^{-2\sigma_{s}}\cdot L_{T}^{2}\cdot\beta_{\Omega}^{2}

\displaystyle\ll_{\theta}(\ln T)^{-\delta\xi_{o}+2\sigma_{s}\gamma+2(3+\eta)+2},

for all sufficiently large
T
. We think of
\theta
as fixed throughout the argument. Hence, if we pick
\eta,\gamma
sufficiently large
and next
\xi_{o}
sufficiently large,
then all of the right-hand sides above are
O_{\theta,\rho}((\ln T)^{-\rho})
for any
\rho>0
.
Furthermore, one verifies that all the conditions in (
9.23
) are
satisfied. Hence, (
9.24
) follows. Since

\frac{b}{c^{2}}\gg a\geq(\ln T)^{-\theta},

we obtain that

\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}\ll_{h,\kappa,\zeta,\theta}\frac{b\cdot(\ln L_{T})^{1+\kappa}\cdot\xi_{T}^{2}}{c^{2}}\cdot|\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}|.

(9.25)

Furthermore,

\xi_{T}\ll\ln\ln T\quad\textrm{and}\quad|\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}|\ll\beta_{\Omega}^{2}-\alpha_{\Omega}^{2},

where

\alpha_{\Omega}=\ln\left(\frac{T_{0}c^{2}}{e^{2}b}\right)\quad\textrm{and}\quad\beta_{\Omega}=\ln\left(\frac{Tc^{2}}{a}\right).

Since, according to our assumptions,

\zeta\leq\frac{c^{2}}{b}<\frac{c^{2}}{a}\leq(\ln T)^{\theta},

we conclude that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}|\ll\left(\ln T+\ln\left(\frac{c^{2}}{a}\right)\right)^{2}-\left(\ln T_{0}+\ln\left(\frac{c^{2}}{e^{2}b}\right)\right)^{2}\ll_{\zeta}\mathcal{L}([T_{0},T))+\mathcal{M}([T_{0},T)),

where

\mathcal{L}([T_{0},T)):=(\ln T)^{2}-(\ln T_{0})^{2}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\mathcal{M}([T_{0},T)):=\ln T\ln\ln T.

Hence, it follows from (
9.5
) and (
9.25
) that

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1)^{2}}\theta_{\kappa}\Big(S_{\Omega}h(\underline{x})-\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}(h)\Big)\,d\underline{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{L}_{3}}\theta_{\kappa}\left(D_{\Omega}\right)\,d\nu

\displaystyle\ll_{h,\kappa,\zeta,\theta}c^{-2}\cdot b\cdot(\ln\ln T)^{3+\kappa}\cdot\Big(\mathcal{L}([T_{0},T))+\mathcal{M}([T_{0},T))\Big).

(9.26)

In particular, in the case when
T_{0}=1
, we obtain Theorem
1.3
.

10.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

We work with the sets

\Omega=\Omega^{(a,b],c}_{[T_{0},T)}:=\left\{(u,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[T_{0},T)\,:\,\begin{array}[]{c}\max(|u_{1}|,|u_{2}|)\leq c\\[5.69046pt]
a<|u_{1}u_{2}|\cdot y\leq b\end{array}\right\}

that depend on parameters
0<a<b<1
,
0<c<1/2
,
1\leq T_{0}<T
.
For a lattice
\Lambda
, we consider the discrepancy function

D^{(a,b],c}_{[T_{0},T)}(\Lambda):=\left|\Lambda\cap\Omega^{(a,b],c}_{[T_{0},T)}\right|-\Vol_{3}\left(\Omega^{(a,b],c}_{[T_{0},T)}\right).

Let us take in (
9.26
) a Lipschitz function
h
such that
h=1
on
[0,1]
. Then

S_{\Omega}h(\underline{x})=\left|\Lambda_{\underline{x}}\cap\Omega\right|\quad\textrm{and}\quad\mathcal{M}_{\Omega}(h)=\Vol_{3}(\Omega),

so that it follows that

\int_{\mathcal{L}_{3}}\theta_{\kappa}\left(\left|D^{(a,b],c}_{[T_{0},T)}\right|\right)\,d\nu\ll_{\kappa,\zeta,\theta}c^{-2}\cdot b\cdot(\ln\ln T)^{3+\kappa}\cdot\Big(\mathcal{L}([T_{0},T))+\mathcal{M}([T_{0},T))\Big).

(10.1)

We use this estimate for a Borel–Cantelli argument below.

For
s\in\mathbb{N}
, let us denote by
\mathcal{I}_{s}

the collection of intervals

I_{i,j}:=\left[e^{2^{i}j},e^{2^{i}(1+j)}\right).

(10.2)

with
(i,j)\in\mathbb{N}_{o}^{2}
satisfying
2^{i}(1+j)<2^{s}
.
The following lemma is essentially
[
16
, Lemma 1]
, although this lemma takes place in a slightly different setting. We provide a proof of our version
for completeness.

Lemma 10.1
.

For every
1\leq N<2^{s}
, there exists a
subset
\mathcal{H}_{N}\subset\mathcal{I}_{s}
such that

|\mathcal{H}_{N}|\leq s\quad\textrm{and}\quad[1,e^{N})=\bigsqcup_{I\in\mathcal{H}_{N}}I.

Lemma 10.2

By Borel-Cantelli’s Lemma, there exists a conull subset
Z\subset[0,1)^{2}
such that for every
\underline{x}\in Z
, there is an index
k(\underline{x})
such that

\Lambda_{\underline{x}}\cap\Upsilon_{T_{k(\underline{x})}}\neq\emptyset
.
This finishes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Take a non-increasing function
a_{T}
such that

a_{T}=o((\ln T)^{-2})
and consider the sets
\Omega=\Omega_{[1,T)}^{(a_{T},b],1/2}
.
Then

L(\underline{x};b)\cap[1,T)=L(\underline{x};a_{T})\cap[1,T)\bigsqcup Q_{\Omega}(\underline{x}).

By Lemma
11.1
,
L(\underline{x};a_{T})=\emptyset
for all almost all
\underline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and sufficiently large
T
(depending on
\underline{x}
), and thus
L(\underline{x};b)\cap[1,T)=Q_{\Omega}(\underline{x})
. Hence, Theorem
1.1
follows from Theorem
1.2

and the volume formula (
A.4
).
∎

Appendix A
Volume estimates

In this section we discuss some basic facts concerning the volumes of the sets

\Omega=\left\{(\underline{x},y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,T)\,:\,\begin{array}[]{c}\max(|x_{1}|,|x_{2}|)\leq c\\[5.69046pt]
a<|x_{1}x_{2}|\cdot y\leq b\end{array}\right\}

with
0<a<b<1
and
c\leq 1/2
.
We observe that these domains can be represented in terms of
more basic sets

\Xi(\gamma):=\left\{\underline{x}\in[\shortminus 1,1]^{2}\,:\,|x_{1}x_{2}|\leq\gamma\right\},\quad\gamma>0.

(A.1)

Direct computation gives

\Vol_{2}(\Xi(\gamma))=4\max\big(1,\gamma\cdot(1-\ln\gamma)\big)\quad\hbox{for $\gamma>0$}.

(A.2)

In particular, it follows from the Mean Value Theorem that

\Vol_{2}(\Xi(\gamma_{2}))-\Vol_{2}(\Xi(\gamma_{1}))\leq 4|\ln\min(1,\gamma_{1})|\cdot(\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1})\quad\hbox{for $\gamma_{2}>\gamma_{1}>0$}.

(A.3)

We observe that the
y
-sections

\Omega(y):=\{\underline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,:\,(\underline{x},y)\in\Omega\},\quad\hbox{for $y\in[1,T).$}

can be written as

\Omega(y)=c\cdot\left(\Xi\left(\frac{b}{c^{2}\cdot y}\right)\setminus\Xi\left(\frac{a}{c^{2}\cdot y}\right)\right),

and thus

\Vol_{2}(\Omega(y))=c^{2}\cdot\left(\Vol_{2}\left(\Xi\left(\frac{b}{c^{2}\cdot y}\right)\right)-\Vol_{2}\left(\Xi\left(\frac{a}{c^{2}\cdot y}\right)\right)\right),\quad\textrm{for all $y\in[1,T)$}.

In view of this, the following lemma can be deduced from (
A.2
) by a direct computation:

Lemma A.1
.

For
\max(1,\frac{b}{c^{2}})\leq y<T
,

\displaystyle\Vol_{2}(\Omega(y))=

\displaystyle\frac{4\cdot\ln y}{y}\cdot(b-a)

\displaystyle+\frac{4}{y}\cdot\Big((b-a)\cdot(1+2\cdot\ln c)-b\ln b+a\ln a\Big),

so that when
b\ll c^{2}
,

\displaystyle\Vol_{3}(\Omega)=

\displaystyle 2\cdot(\ln T)^{2}\cdot(b-a)

\displaystyle+4\cdot\ln T\cdot\Big((b-a)\cdot(1+2\cdot\ln c)-b\ln b+a\ln a\Big)+O(1).

In particular, if in addition
a\gg(\ln T)^{-\theta}
for some
\theta>0
, then

\displaystyle\Vol_{3}(\Omega)=2\cdot(\ln T)^{2}\cdot(b-a)+O\Big(\ln T(\ln\ln T)\cdot(b-a)+1\Big).

(A.4)

Appendix B
An auxiliary double sum

This is a largely technical section where we collect some estimates on certain multi-parameter sums that are used in Section
7
. This part can be safely skipped
on a first read.

We begin with a simple observation. For
\underline{u}=(u_{1},u_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
, let

N(\underline{u}):=\left|\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap\left([\shortminus u_{1},u_{1}]\times[\shortminus u_{2},u_{2}]\right)\right|.

(B.1)

A simple counting argument that we leave to the reader shows that
N(\underline{u})\ll G(\underline{u})
, where

G(\underline{u})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}1&\textrm{if $\lceil\underline{u}\rceil<1$}\\[5.69046pt]
\lceil\underline{u}\rceil&\textrm{if $\lfloor\underline{u}\rfloor<1\leq\lceil\underline{u}\rceil$}\\[5.69046pt]
u_{1}u_{2}&\textrm{if $1\leq\lfloor\underline{u}\rfloor$}\end{array}\right..

(B.2)

Let us fix a constant
M>0
for the rest of the section. For
t=(t_{1},t_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
, we define the function

F_{t}(q)=\frac{G(2Mqe^{-t_{1}},2Mqe^{-t_{2}})}{q^{2}}\cdot e^{-(t_{1}+t_{2})},\quad q\geq 1.

(B.3)

The explicit formula for
G
above tells us that

F_{t}(q)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\frac{e^{-(t_{1}+t_{2})}}{q^{2}}&\textrm{if \, $q<\frac{e^{\lfloor t\rfloor}}{2M}$}\\[5.69046pt]
2M\frac{e^{-(2\lfloor t\rfloor+\lceil t\rceil)}}{q}&\textrm{if \, $\frac{e^{\lfloor t\rfloor}}{2M}\leq q<\frac{e^{\lceil t\rceil}}{2M}$}\\[5.69046pt]
4M^{2}e^{-2(t_{1}+t_{2})}&\textrm{if \, $\frac{e^{\lceil t\rceil}}{2M}\leq q$}\end{array}\right..

(B.4)

Our main goal in this section is to prove the following upper bound on an auxiliary double sum which involves the function
F_{t}
.

Lemma B.1
.

Let
0<\alpha<\beta\leq M
and let
t=(t_{1},t_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
. Define

\alpha_{t}=\alpha\cdot e^{t_{1}+t_{2}}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\beta_{t}=\beta\cdot e^{t_{1}+t_{2}}

and suppose that
\alpha<1
,
\alpha_{t}\geq 1
. Then,

\sum_{q_{1},q_{2}=\alpha_{t}}^{\beta_{t}}F_{t}\left(\frac{\max(q_{1},q_{2})}{\gcd(q_{1},q_{2})}\right)\ll_{M}e^{-(t_{1}+t_{2})}+(\beta-\alpha)\cdot\max\left(1,\ln\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)\right)\cdot\max(1,t_{1}+t_{2}),

where the implicit constants depend only on
M
.

Remark B.2
.

We adopt the following sum convention: If
1\leq\gamma<\delta
, and

m
and
n
are integers such that

m<\gamma\leq m+1\quad\textrm{and}\quad n\leq\delta<n+1,

then
\sum_{q=\gamma}^{\delta}:=\sum_{q=m+1}^{n}
, where the right-hand side
is defined to be zero if
m=n
.

B.1.
Proof of Lemma
B.1

The following standard estimates will be used in the proof. For

1\leq\gamma<\gamma+1<\delta
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{q=\gamma}^{\delta}\frac{1}{q}

\displaystyle=\ln\left(\frac{\delta}{\gamma}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma}\right),

(B.5)

where the implicit constants are independent of
\gamma
and
\delta
. In addition, the following elementary upper bound on the sum-of-divisors function holds

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=1\\
m\mid n\end{subarray}}^{n}m\ll\ln(n).

(B.6)

Let us begin with the proof. We fix
t=(t_{1},t_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
and
0<\alpha<\beta\leq M
such that

1>\alpha\quad\textrm{and}\quad\alpha_{t}\geq 1\quad\textrm{and}\quad e^{\lfloor t\rfloor}>2M,

and we want to bound the double sum

S_{t}(\alpha,\beta):=\sum_{q_{1},q_{2}=\alpha_{t}}^{\beta_{t}}F_{t}\left(\frac{\max(q_{1},q_{2})}{\gcd(q_{1},q_{2})}\right)

from above. Note that
F_{t}(1)\ll_{M}e^{-(t_{1}+t_{2})}
.

We first consider the case when
\beta_{t}-\alpha_{t}<1
. Then, the double sum above contains at most one term (necessarily with
q_{1}=q_{2}
), and thus

S_{t}(\alpha,\beta)\leq F_{t}(1)\ll_{M}e^{-(t_{1}+t_{2})}.

(B.7)

Let us from now on assume that
\beta_{t}-\alpha_{t}\geq 1
, and define the sets

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{0}

\displaystyle:=\left\{(q_{1},q_{2})\in[\alpha_{t},\beta_{t}]^{2}\cap\mathbb{N}^{2}\,:\,\frac{\max(q_{1},q_{2})}{\gcd(q_{1},q_{2})}<\frac{e^{\lfloor t\rfloor}}{2M}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{1}

\displaystyle:=\left\{(q_{1},q_{2})\in[\alpha_{t},\beta_{t}]^{2}\cap\mathbb{N}^{2}\,:\,\frac{e^{\lfloor t\rfloor}}{2M}\leq\frac{\max(q_{1},q_{2})}{\gcd(q_{1},q_{2})}<\frac{e^{\lceil t\rceil}}{2M}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{2}

\displaystyle:=\left\{(q_{1},q_{2})\in[\alpha_{t},\beta_{t}]^{2}\cap\mathbb{N}^{2}\,:\,\frac{e^{\lceil t\rceil}}{2M}\leq\frac{\max(q_{1},q_{2})}{\gcd(q_{1},q_{2})}\right\},

and the functions

S_{t}^{(k)}(\alpha,\beta):=\sum_{(q_{1},q_{2})\in\mathcal{E}_{k}}F_{t}\left(\frac{\max(q_{1},q_{2})}{\gcd(q_{1},q_{2})}\right),\quad\textrm{for $k=0,1,2$}.

Note that

S_{t}(\alpha,\beta)=S_{t}^{(0)}(\alpha,\beta)+S_{t}^{(1)}(\alpha,\beta)+S_{t}^{(2)}(\alpha,\beta).

We will estimate these three partial sums separately below.

An upper bound for
S_{t}^{(0)}(\alpha,\beta)

Note that if
\gcd(q_{1},q_{2})=d
, then

\left(\frac{\gcd(q_{1},q_{2})}{\max(q_{1},q_{2})}\right)^{2}=\frac{d^{2}}{\max(q_{1},q_{2})^{2}}\leq\frac{d}{q_{1}\cdot q_{2}^{\prime}},

where
q_{2}=d\cdot q_{2}^{\prime}
. Hence,

\displaystyle S_{t}^{(0)}(\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(q_{1},q_{2})\in\mathcal{E}_{o}}\left(\frac{\gcd(q_{1},q_{2})}{\max(q_{1},q_{2})}\right)^{2}\cdot e^{-(t_{1}+t_{2})}\leq\sum_{q_{1},q_{2}=\alpha_{t}}^{\beta_{t}}\left(\frac{\gcd(q_{1},q_{2})}{\max(q_{1},q_{2})}\right)^{2}\cdot e^{-(t_{1}+t_{2})}

\displaystyle\leq 2\cdot\sum_{q_{1}=\alpha_{t}}^{\beta_{t}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d=1\\
d\mid q_{1}\end{subarray}}^{q_{1}}\,\sum_{q_{2}^{\prime}=\frac{\alpha_{t}}{d}}^{\frac{\beta_{t}}{d}}\frac{d}{q_{1}\cdot q_{2}^{\prime}}\right)\cdot e^{-(t_{1}+t_{2})}.

By (
B.5
) (with
\gamma=\frac{\alpha_{t}}{d}
and
\delta=\frac{\beta_{t}}{d}
),
we now see that

\displaystyle S_{t}^{(0)}(\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{q_{1}=\alpha_{t}}^{\beta_{t}}\left(\frac{1}{q_{1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q_{1}=1\\
d\mid q\end{subarray}}^{q_{1}}d\right)\cdot\left(1+\ln\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)\right)\cdot e^{-(t_{1}+t_{2})},

and by
(
B.6
),

\displaystyle\sum_{q=\alpha_{t}}^{\beta_{t}}\left(\frac{1}{q}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q=1\\
d\mid q\end{subarray}}d\right)\cdot e^{-(t_{1}+t_{2})}

\displaystyle\ll\left(\sum_{q=\alpha_{t}}^{\beta_{t}}\ln(q)\right)\cdot e^{-(t_{1}+t_{2})}\leq(\beta_{t}-\alpha_{t})\cdot\ln(\beta_{t})\cdot e^{-(t_{1}+t_{2})}

\displaystyle\ll_{M}(\beta-\alpha)\cdot\max(1,t_{1}+t_{2}),

since
\beta\leq M
.
We conclude that

S_{t}^{(0)}(\alpha,\beta)\ll(\beta-\alpha)\cdot\max\left(1,\ln\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)\right)\cdot\max(1,t_{1}+t_{2}).

(B.8)

An upper bound for
S_{t}^{(1)}(\alpha,\beta)

To simplify notation, let us assume that
t_{1}\leq t_{2}
so that
\lfloor t\rfloor=t_{1}

and
\lceil t\rceil=t_{2}
. Then,

S_{t}^{(1)}(\alpha,\beta)=\left(\sum_{(q_{1},q_{2})\in\mathcal{E}_{1}}\frac{\gcd(q_{1},q_{2})}{\max(q_{1},q_{2})}\right)\cdot e^{-(2t_{1}+t_{2})},

If
(q_{1},q_{2})\in\mathcal{E}_{1}
, then

\frac{e^{t_{1}}}{2M}\leq\frac{\max(q_{1},q_{2})}{\gcd(q_{1},q_{2})}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\max(q_{1},q_{2})\leq\beta_{t}=\beta\cdot e^{t_{1}+t_{2}},

and thus
\gcd(q_{1},q_{2})\leq 2M\cdot\beta\cdot e^{t_{2}}
. Let
\gamma_{t}=\min(\alpha_{t},2M\cdot\beta\cdot e^{t_{2}})
, and note that

\displaystyle S_{t}^{(1)}(\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{d=1}^{\gamma_{t}}\left(\sum_{q_{1},q_{2}=\frac{\alpha_{t}}{d}}^{\frac{\beta_{t}}{d}}\frac{1}{\max(q_{1},q_{2})}\right)\cdot e^{-(2t_{1}+t_{2})}

\displaystyle+\sum_{d=\alpha_{t}}^{\beta_{t}}\left(\sum_{q_{1},q_{2}=1}^{\frac{\beta_{t}}{d}}\frac{1}{\max(q_{1},q_{2})}\right)\cdot e^{-(2t_{1}+t_{2})}

\displaystyle\ll\left(\sum_{d=1}^{\gamma_{t}}\frac{\beta_{t}-\alpha_{t}}{d}\right)\cdot e^{-(2t_{1}+t_{2})}+\left(\sum_{d=\alpha_{t}}^{\beta_{t}}\frac{\beta_{t}}{d}\right)\cdot e^{-(2t_{1}+t_{2})}

\displaystyle\ll(\beta-\alpha)\cdot(1+\ln(\gamma_{t}))\cdot e^{-t_{1}}+\beta\cdot\left(\ln\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha_{t}}\right)\right)\cdot e^{-t_{1}},

where we in the last inequality have used (
B.5
) (with parameters
\gamma=1,\delta=\gamma_{t}
and
\gamma=\alpha_{t},\delta=\beta_{t}
). Since

\ln(\gamma_{t})\ll 1+t_{2},

we have

(\beta-\alpha)\cdot(1+\ln(\gamma_{t}))\cdot e^{-t_{1}}\ll_{M}(\beta-\alpha)\cdot\max(1,t_{1}+t_{2})\cdot e^{-\lfloor t\rfloor}.

For the term

\beta\cdot\left(\ln\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha_{t}}\right)\right)\cdot e^{-t_{1}},

we consider two separate cases. If
\beta/\alpha\geq e
, then
\ln(\beta/\alpha)
dominates, and since
\beta-\alpha\gg\beta
, we find

\beta\cdot\left(\ln\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha_{t}}\right)\right)\cdot e^{-t_{1}}\ll_{M}(\beta-\alpha)\cdot\ln\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)\cdot e^{-t_{1}}.

If
\beta/\alpha<e
, on the other hand, we deduce

\beta\cdot\left(\ln\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha_{t}}\right)\right)\cdot e^{-t_{1}}\ll_{M}\beta\cdot\ln\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)\cdot e^{-t_{1}}+\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\cdot e^{-2t_{1}-t_{2}}.

On observing that for
1<\beta/\alpha\leq e

\beta\cdot\ln\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)\ll(\beta-\alpha),

we now obtain

\beta\cdot\left(\ln\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha_{t}}\right)\right)\cdot e^{-t_{1}}\ll_{M}(\beta-\alpha)\cdot e^{-t_{1}}+e^{-2t_{1}-t_{2}}.

Then, by combining the previous estimates, we conclude that

S_{t}^{(1)}(\alpha,\beta)\ll_{M}(\beta-\alpha)\cdot\max\left(1,\ln\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)\right)\cdot\max(1,t_{1}+t_{2})\cdot e^{-\lfloor t\rfloor}+e^{-(t_{1}+t_{2})}.

(B.9)

An upper bound for
S_{t}^{(2)}(\alpha,\beta)

The following crude estimate will suffice:

\displaystyle S^{(2)}_{t}(\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{(q_{1},q_{2})\in\mathcal{E}_{2}}1\right)\cdot e^{-2(t_{1}+t_{2})}\leq\left(\sum_{q_{1}=\alpha_{t}}^{\beta_{t}}\sum_{q_{2}=\alpha_{t}}^{\beta_{t}}1\right)\cdot e^{-2(t_{1}+t_{2})}

\displaystyle\ll(\beta_{t}-\alpha_{t})^{2}\cdot e^{-2(t_{1}+t_{2})}\ll_{M}(\beta-\alpha)^{2}\ll\beta-\alpha,

(B.10)

since
\beta\leq M
.

Putting it all together

If we now combine (
B.7
), (
B.8
), (
B.9
) and (
B.10
),
we get

S_{t}(\alpha,\beta)\ll_{M}e^{-(t_{1}+t_{2})}+(\beta-\alpha)\cdot\max\left(1,\ln\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)\right)\cdot\max(1,t_{1}+t_{2}),

where the implicit constants are independent of
\alpha
and
\beta
, and hence the
proof of Lemma
B.1
is complete.

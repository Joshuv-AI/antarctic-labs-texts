Bounds for discrete moments of Weyl sums and applications
Karin Halupczok

Abstract
. We prove two bounds for discrete moments of Weyl sums. The first one can be obtained using a standard approach. The second one involves an observation how this method can be improved, which leads to a sharper bound in certain ranges. The proofs both build on the recently proved main conjecture for Vinogradov’s mean value theorem. We present two selected applications: First, we prove a new k k -th derivative test for the number of integer points close to a curve by an exponential sum approach. This yields a stronger bound than existing results obtained via geometric methods, but it is only applicable for specific functions. As second application we prove a new improvement of the polynomial large sieve inequality for one-variable polynomials of degree k ≥ 4 k\geq 4 .

2.
Discrete moments of Weyl sums

It is known that VMVT has the following impact on
Weyl sum estimates:

Theorem 6

(Weyl sum estimate)
.

Let
P\in\mathbb{R}[X]
be a polynomial of degree
k\geq 2
, and for
the leading coefficient
\alpha_{k}
of
P
let
u,q
be integers with

(u,q)=1
,
q\geq 1
and
|\alpha_{k}-u/q|<q^{-2}
.
Then we have

\sum_{n\leq x}\mathrm{e}(P(n))\ll x\Big(\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{x}+\frac{q}{x^{k}}\Big)^{1/k(k-1)}x^{\varepsilon}.

A proof can easily be found using Montgomery’s
exposition
[
17
, §4]
. Our analysis of this proof yields a
generalization of this estimate for discrete moments of Weyl sums.
By changing a small aspect, it comes
with an extra-improvement, stated below as Theorem
8
.
Compared to this, Theorem

9
below is just a straight forward generalization of Theorem

6
that stems from Montgomery’s original approach
presented in
[
17
, §4]
.

We give the definition of the discrete moments we look at.

Definition 7
.

Let
k\geq 2
be a fixed integer and
consider a fixed polynomial
P_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\in\mathbb{R}[X]
of degree
k
with

P_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(0)=0
, say

P_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(X)=\alpha_{k}X^{k}+\alpha_{k-1}X^{k-1}+\dots+\alpha_{1}X

with
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Let
x>1
be a sufficiently large real number
and for
a\in\mathbb{N}
let

S_{a}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}):=\sum_{m\leq x}\mathrm{e}(aP_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(m))

be a corresponding Weyl sum of
P_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
. The twist with
a
allows us
to consider discrete moments of the form

\sum_{a\leq T}|S_{az}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})|^{2s}

(1)

with large real
T>1
and with
fixed numbers
z,s\in\mathbb{N}
.

The role of
z
is to control a possible dependence of a further
factor in the argument of the exponential. We might think of a small

z
, or even
z=1
.

Sums of the shape (
1
) occur in numerous applications,
like in Dirichlet’s divisor problem, counting integer points close to curves,
or, as we will see below, in the polynomial large sieve
inequality (for one variable polynomials). We will restrict on
presenting just the latter two applications which work well.

Our first goal is to give good estimates for the expression in
(
1
) depending on
x
and
T
.

Note that bounds for other moments can then easily be derived by Hölder’s
inequality

\sum_{a\leq T}|S_{az}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})|^{\ell}\leq T^{1-\ell/2s}\Big(\sum_{a\leq T}|S_{az}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})|^{2s}\Big)^{\ell/2s},\ 0<\ell<2s.

(2)

Since our results use different values of
s
, it is convenient
to state bounds for the first moment, which makes the statements
easy to compare. Therefore, the results Theorem
8
and
Theorem
9
below are stated for the first moment.

3.
Improved moment estimate

We are following the estimate of Weyl sums along the
lines of Montgomery’s so-called alternative derivation in
[
17
, §4.4]

and carry it over to the situation of discrete moments.
This approach yields
the following result for Weyl sums as given in Definition
7
.
We call it the improved moment estimate.
The direct approach leading to Theorem
9

yields a bound that is weaker in certain ranges. This is discussed in

Theorem 8

(Improved moment estimate)
.

Let
k\geq 3
,
s_{0}=(k-1)(k-2)/2+1
and
u,q
be integers with
q\geq 1
,

(u,q)=1
and
|\alpha_{k}-u/q|<q^{-2}

\sum_{a\leq T}|S_{az}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})|\ll Tx\Big(\frac{zx^{k-1}}{q}+\frac{zx^{k-1}\log(q)}{T}+\frac{1}{x}+\frac{q\log(q)}{Tx}\Big)^{1/2s_{0}}x^{\varepsilon}.

In the bound, we ordered the factors on the right hand side:
it starts with
the trivial estimate

, then we give the improvement factor and
then a small additional factor

.

Proof.

We need to introduce some of the notations from
[
17
, §4.4]
,
but writing
x

N

Thus for

and the difference of two power sums as

d_{j}=d_{j}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}):=s_{j}(\mathbf{u})-s_{j}(\mathbf{v})

u_{i},v_{i}\in\{-x,\dots,x\}

Multiplying

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt\cdot\mathrm{e}(-azP(n_{1}))\cdots\mathrm{e}(-azP(n_{s}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}\\

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt+(s_{k-1}(\mathbf{m})-s_{k-1}(\mathbf{n}))az\alpha_{k-1}\big)

Theorem 9

(Standard approach estimate)
.

Let
k\geq 2
,
s_{1}=k(k-1)/2
and

be integers with
q\geq 1
,
(u,q)=1
and

|\alpha_{k}-u/q|<q^{-2}
. Then

\sum_{a\leq T}|S_{az}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})|\ll Tx\Big(\frac{z}{q}+\frac{z}{x}+\frac{q}{Tx^{k}}\Big)^{1/2s_{1}}(Txz)^{\varepsilon}.

Note that with
T=1

, we get back Theorem

Proof.

Montgomery’s original approach in
[
17
, §4.4,p.81,l.15]
yields

\sum_{a\leq T}|S_{az}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})|^{2s}\\
\ll x^{(k-1)(k-2)/2}x^{-1}J_{k-1}(3x,s)\sum_{a\leq T}\sum_{0\leq h\leq 2sx^{k-1}}\min(x,\|akhz\alpha_{k}\|^{-1}),

where the last double sum can be estimated using Lemma
1
.
Together with the substitution

this yields

\ll\sum_{w\leq 2sTzkx^{k-1}}\tau_{3}(w)\min(x,\|w\alpha_{k}\|^{-1})\ll\Big(\frac{zTx^{k}}{q}+zTx^{k-1}+q\Big)(Txz)^{\varepsilon},

where there exist integers
u,q
with
q\geq 1
,
(u,q)=1
and

. We proceed with

\sum_{a\leq T}|S_{az}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})|^{2s}\ll x^{2s}T\Big(\frac{J_{k-1}(3x,s)}{x^{2s-k(k-1)/2}}\Big)\Big(\frac{z}{q}+\frac{z}{x}+\frac{q}{Tx^{k}}\Big)(Txz)^{\varepsilon}.

Next, using VMVT (Theorem

\sum_{a\leq T}|S_{az}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})|^{2s_{1}}\ll x^{2s_{1}}T\Big(\frac{z}{q}+\frac{z}{x}+\frac{q}{Tx^{k}}\Big)(Txz)^{\varepsilon}.

(6)

Applying Hölder’s inequality (

3.1.
Comparison and conjectural considerations

The expressions in large brackets in Theorems
8
and

9
show the improvements compared
to the trivial estimate
Tx
.
They lead to a nontrivial assertion
if

in Theorem

respectively if
z\lll q\lll Tx^{k}
in Theorem

Let
s_{0}=(k-1)(k-2)/2+1
and
s_{1}=k(k-1)/2
.

We compare these improvement expressions
(supposing

is small in this comparison) and obtain
the following assertions.

1.)

In these expressions, we compare the typical dominant

(for

)

(for

2.)

The dominant term

(7)

To summarize, with Theorem

3.)

The dominant term

4.)

To summarize, Theorem
8
yields an improvement

for

from which on we obtain improvements.
Moreover, above conditions on

(8)

For any other

9

An observation is that in (

For

Compared to Theorem

(Second improved moment estimate)

Proof.

We start as in the proof of Theorem

with

\displaystyle\sum_{a\leq T}\mathcal{T}(a)

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{d\ll x^{k}}\sum_{h\ll x^{k-1}}\Big(\sum_{j\ll zx^{k}}\min(T,\|\alpha_{k}j+hz\alpha_{k-1}\|^{-1})\Big)\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\\

(9)

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\\

uniformly in

\displaystyle\sum_{h\ll x^{k-1}}

\displaystyle\sum_{j\ll zx^{k}}\min(T,\|\alpha_{k}j+hz\alpha_{k-1}\|^{-1})

\displaystyle\ll(zx^{k}/q+1)\sum_{h\ll x^{k-1}}\sum_{1\leq j\leq q}\min(T,\|\alpha_{k}j+hz\alpha_{k-1}\|^{-1})

\displaystyle\ll(zx^{k}/q+1)\sum_{h\ll x^{k-1}}(\min(T,q\|hzq\alpha_{k-1}\|^{-1})+q\log q),

\ll(zx^{k}/q+1)x^{\varepsilon}q(T/q+w\log w)(x^{k-1}z/w+1)+zx^{2k-1+\varepsilon}+x^{k-1+\varepsilon}q\\

\displaystyle\sum_{a\leq T}

\displaystyle|S_{az}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})|^{2s}

\displaystyle\ll Tx^{(k-1)(k-2)/2+k+\varepsilon}(J_{k-1}(x,s-1)J_{k-1}(x,s))^{1/2}

4.
Discrete moments of exponential sums

We turn now to discrete moments of general exponential sums
with smooth functions
f
. The main idea is to approximate
f
with a
polynomial using Taylor’s theorem and apply the bounds of the previous
sections.

We proceed similar as in Bordellès’ book
[
6
, §6.6.7]
,
or in Heath-Brown’s recent article
[
14
]
.
The first result is as follows.

Theorem 12
.

Let
N
be a large positive integer,
and let
f\in\mathbb{C}^{k}((0,3N))
,
k\geq 3
.
Suppose that there exists real numbers

\lambda,A
such that
0<\lambda\leq f^{(k)}(x)\leq A\lambda
holds for
all
x\in(0,3N)
. Let
\rho=1/((k-2)(k-3)+2)
and

\mu=1+A\lambda N
.
Let
z
be a positive integer that is considered
to be small, and let
T
be a real number with

N^{-k}(zA\lambda)^{-1}\leq T\leq(zA\lambda)^{-1}
.
Then

\sum_{a\leq T}\Big|\sum_{N<m<2N}\mathrm{e}(azf(m))\Big|\ll NT(zA\lambda T)^{\rho/k+\varepsilon}+T(zA\lambda T)^{-1/k}\\
+T\mu z^{2}(zA\lambda T)^{2/k-2}+\mu z(zA\lambda T)^{1/k-1}\lambda^{-1}.

(10)

We note that
\lambda
as well as
A
and
z
may depend on
N

and
T
. In the case if
A
and
z
is depending on
k
only, we may hide
A

and
z
in the implicit constant leading to a slightly easier expression.
Additionally assuming
\mu=1
, the upper bound simplifies to

NT(\lambda T)^{\rho/k}+T(\lambda T)^{-1/k}+T(\lambda T)^{2/k-2}+(\lambda T)^{1/k-1}\lambda^{-1}.

(11)

The proof uses an adapted circle method.
The first term in the bound (
10
) respectively (
11
)
comes from the the minor arc contribution, the
second gives a trivial contribution from a Weyl-shift,
and the last two terms come from the major arc contribution.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{f}
denote the left hand side of (
10
).

We start with a Weyl-shift with
1\leq H\leq N
. For this,
let
\beta_{m}=\mathrm{e}(azf(m))
if
N<m<2N
, and
\beta_{m}=0

otherwise. Then for each
h^{\prime}\leq H
,

\sum_{N<m<2N}\mathrm{e}(azf(m))=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\beta_{m+h^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{H}\sum_{h\leq H}\ \sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\beta_{m+h}\\
=\frac{1}{H}\sum_{m=N-H+1}^{2N-1}\ \sum_{h\leq H}\beta_{m+h}=\frac{1}{H}\sum_{m=N+1}^{2N-1}\ \sum_{h\leq H}\mathrm{e}(azf(m+h))+O(H).

We obtain

\mathcal{L}_{f}=\frac{1}{H}\sum_{a\leq T}\ \sum_{N<m<2N}\Big|\sum_{h\leq H}\mathrm{e}(azf(m+h))\Big|+O(TH).

(12)

An application of Taylor’s theorem provides the expansion

f(m+h)=Q_{m}(h)+u_{m}(h)
with

Q_{m}(h)=hf^{\prime}(m)+h^{2}f^{\prime\prime}(m)/2!+\dots+h^{k-1}f^{(k-1)}(m)/(k-1)!.

Note that
f^{(k-1)}(m)/(k-1)!
is the leading
coefficient of this polynomial of degree
k-1
in
h
, and that

u_{m}(h)=f(m)+\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\int_{0}^{h}(h-v)^{k-1}f^{(k)}(m+v)dv,

so that
\mathrm{e}(azf(m+h))=\mathrm{e}(azQ_{m}(h))\mathrm{e}(azu_{m}(h))
.

We separate the exponential expressions containing
Q_{m}
and
u_{m}

by partial summation, this yields

\mathcal{L}_{f}\leq\mathcal{S}_{1}+\mathcal{S}_{2}+O(TH)

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{H}\sum_{N<m<2N}\sum_{a\leq T}\Big|\sum_{h\leq H}\mathrm{e}(azQ_{m}(h))\Big|

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{a\leq T}\frac{2\pi az}{H}\sum_{N<m<2N}\int_{1}^{H}\Big|\sum_{h\leq x}\mathrm{e}(azQ_{m}(h))\Big|\cdot|u^{\prime}_{m}(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\ll zH^{k-2}\sum_{N<m<2N}\int_{1}^{H}\sum_{a\leq T}a\Big|\sum_{h\leq x}\mathrm{e}(azQ_{m}(h))\Big|\,\mathrm{d}x\cdot\sup_{v\in(0,H)}|f^{(k)}(m+v)|

\displaystyle\ll zA\lambda H^{k-2}\int_{1}^{H}\sum_{N<m<2N}\sum_{a\leq T}a\Big|\sum_{h\leq x}\mathrm{e}(azQ_{m}(h))\Big|\,\mathrm{d}x.

Next, we abbreviate

S_{a,m}(x):=\sum_{h\leq x}\mathrm{e}(azQ_{m}(h)),

summarize the bounds for
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{2}
and arrive at

\mathcal{L}_{f}\ll TH+\frac{1}{H}\sum_{N<m<2N}\sum_{a\leq T}|S_{a,m}(H)|\\
+zA\lambda H^{k-2}T\int_{1}^{H}\sum_{N<m<2N}\sum_{a\leq T}|S_{a,m}(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x.

(13)

For the next argument,
fix
x
with
x\leq H\leq N

and let
\Delta_{0}:=z^{-1}T^{-1}H^{1-k}
.
Consider
m\in(N,2N)\cap\mathbb{Z}
and let

\mathcal{A}_{m}:=\Big\{\alpha\in[0,1];\ \Big\|\frac{f^{(k-1)}(m)}{(k-1)!}-\alpha\Big\|\leq\Delta_{0}\Big\}.

Fix an
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}_{m}
.

We replace the leading coefficient in
Q_{m}(h)
by
b_{k-1}\in\mathbb{R}
such
that
\frac{f^{(k-1)}(m)}{(k-1)!}-b_{k-1}\in\mathbb{Z}
, so that

|b_{k-1}-\alpha|\leq\Delta_{0}
. Like this, we look at

f_{m}^{*}(h):=hf^{\prime}(m)+\dots+h^{k-2}f^{(k-2)}(m)/(k-2)!+b_{k-1}h^{k-1}.

Let
S_{a,m}^{*}(x):=\sum_{h\leq x}\mathrm{e}(azf_{m}^{*}(h))
, so that

|S_{a,m}^{*}(x)|=|S_{a,m}(x)|
and we are able to work with
S_{a,m}^{*}(x)

instead of
S_{a,m}(x)
in (
13
). Moreover, let

\tilde{f}_{m,\alpha}(h):=hf^{\prime}(m)+\dots+h^{k-2}f^{(k-2)}(m)/(k-2)!+\alpha h^{k-1}

and

\tilde{S}_{a,m}(\alpha,x):=\sum_{h\leq x}\mathrm{e}(az\tilde{f}_{m,\alpha}(h)).

Then we have

\frac{d}{dx}(f_{m}^{*}(x)-\tilde{f}_{m,\alpha}(x))\ll|b_{k-1}-\alpha|x^{k-2}\ll\Delta_{0}x^{k-2},

and we conclude by a partial summation, that

S_{a,m}^{*}(x)\ll|\tilde{S}_{a,m}(\alpha,x)|+az\int_{1}^{x}y^{k-2}\Delta_{0}|\tilde{S}_{a,m}(\alpha,y)|\,\mathrm{d}y.

(14)

Our task is reduced to prove good upper bounds for the term

\mathcal{T}_{x}:=\sum_{N<m<2N}\sum_{a\leq T}|\tilde{S}_{a,m}(\alpha,x)|

with
x\leq H
. For each
m
in the sum there is a chosen

\alpha\in\mathcal{A}_{m}
.
We intend to apply Theorem
8
. We expect a good
result if we assume

T
to be much bigger than
zx^{k-2}
. (Note that
\deg\tilde{S}_{a,m}(\alpha,x)=k-1

For this purpose, introduce appropriate major and minor arcs.
Let

\mathfrak{M}=\bigcup_{q\leq zx^{k-1}}\bigcup_{(u,q)=1}\Big[\frac{u}{q}-\frac{1}{qT},\frac{u}{q}+\frac{1}{qT}\Big]

denote the set of major arcs, and
\mathfrak{m}=[0,1]\setminus\mathfrak{M}
.

Now we distinguish two cases:

Say case (
\mathfrak{m}
) occurs if
m
is such that
there exists a real number
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}_{m}\cap\mathfrak{m}
.
We choose then such an
\alpha
for each such
m
.
By Dirichlet’s approximation theorem, there exists
coprime integers
u
and
q

with
1\leq q\leq T
such that

\Big|\alpha-\frac{u}{q}\Big|\leq\frac{1}{qT}.

Since
\alpha
is contained in
\mathfrak{m}
, we conclude that even
q\geq zx^{k-1}
holds true.

A closer look at the improvement expression in Theorem

8
yields

\Big(\frac{zx^{k-2}}{q}+\frac{zx^{k-2}}{T}+\frac{1}{x}+\frac{q}{Tx}\Big)^{\rho}\ll x^{-\rho}

with
\rho=1/((k-2)(k-3)+2)
.
Therefore by Theorem
8
,

\sum_{a\leq T}|\tilde{S}_{a,m}(\alpha,x)|\ll Tx^{1-\rho+\varepsilon},

hence, summing up over these
m
,

\mathcal{T}_{x,\text{($\mathfrak{m}$)}}:=\sideset{}{{}^{(\mathfrak{m})}}{\sum}_{N<m<2N}\ \sum_{a\leq T}|\tilde{S}_{a,m}(\alpha,x)|\ll NTx^{1-\rho+\varepsilon}.

(15)

In the major arc case,

\mathcal{A}_{m}
is contained completely in a major arc interval.
Then we conclude for
m
with
N<m<2N
in case (
\mathfrak{M}
),
that there exist
q\leq zx^{k-1}
and

(u,q)=1
such that
\|f^{(k-1)}(m)/(k-1)!-u/q\|<1/qT
.
Summing up over these
m
, we obtain

\mathcal{T}_{x,\text{($\mathfrak{M}$)}}:=\sideset{}{{}^{(\mathfrak{M})}}{\sum}_{N<m<2N}\ \sum_{a\leq T}|\tilde{S}_{a,m}(\alpha,x)|\\
\ll Tx\sum_{q\leq zx^{k-1}}\sum_{(u,q)=1}\#\{m\in(N,2N);\ \\
\hskip 142.26378pt\|f^{(k-1)}(m)/(k-1)!-u/q\|<1/qT\}\\
\ll Tx\sum_{q\leq zx^{k-1}}\varphi(q)(1+A\lambda N)(1+1/\lambda qT),

where in the last step we applied Lemma
3
with

g(x):=f^{(k-1)}(x)/(k-1)!-u/q
.
From now on we make use of the abbreviation
\mu=1+A\lambda N
.
This yields the bound

\mathcal{T}_{x,\text{($\mathfrak{M}$)}}\ll Tx(\mu z^{2}x^{2k-2}+\mu zx^{k-1}\lambda^{-1}T^{-1}),

hence, in the major arc case,

\mathcal{T}_{x,\text{($\mathfrak{M}$)}}\ll T\mu z^{2}x^{2k-1}+\mu zx^{k}\lambda^{-1}.

(16)

Then joining the estimates (
15
) and (
16
)
together yields

\mathcal{T}_{x}\ll\mathcal{T}_{x,\text{($\mathfrak{m}$)}}+\mathcal{T}_{x,\text{($\mathfrak{M}$)}}\ll NTx^{1-\rho+\varepsilon}+T\mu z^{2}x^{2k-1}+\mu zx^{k}\lambda^{-1}.

(17)

Next, from estimate (
14
) together with (

)
we obtain

\sum_{N<m<2N}\sum_{a\leq T}|S_{a,m}^{*}(x)|\ll\mathcal{T}_{x}+Tz\Delta_{0}\int_{1}^{x}y^{k-2}\mathcal{T}_{y}\,\mathrm{d}y\\
\ll NTx^{1-\rho+\varepsilon}+Tz\Delta_{0}\int_{1}^{x}y^{k-2}NTy^{1-\rho+\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}y\\
+T\mu z^{2}x^{2k-1}+Tz\Delta_{0}\int_{1}^{x}y^{k-2}T\mu z^{2}y^{2k-1}\,\mathrm{d}y\\
+\mu zx^{k}\lambda^{-1}+Tz\Delta_{0}\int_{1}^{x}y^{k-2}\mu zy^{k}\lambda^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}y\\
\ll NTx^{1-\rho+\varepsilon}+T\mu z^{2}x^{2k-1}+\mu zx^{k}\lambda^{-1},

where only in the last step we used
\Delta_{0}=z^{-1}T^{-1}H^{1-k}

and

.

Therefore, by (

), we arrive at

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{f}

\displaystyle\ll TH+H^{-1}\sum_{m}\sum_{a\leq T}|S_{a,m}^{*}(H)|

\displaystyle\qquad+zA\lambda H^{k-2}T\int_{1}^{H}\sum_{m}\sum_{a\leq T}|S_{a,m}^{*}(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\ll TH+H^{-1}NTH^{1-\rho+\varepsilon}+zA\lambda H^{k-2}T\int_{1}^{H}NTx^{1-\rho+\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+H^{-1}T\mu z^{2}H^{2k-1}+zA\lambda H^{k-2}T\int_{1}^{H}T\mu z^{2}x^{2k-1}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+H^{-1}\mu zH^{k}\lambda^{-1}+zA\lambda H^{k-2}T\int_{1}^{H}\mu zx^{k-1}\lambda^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}x

First application: Integer points close to smooth curves

In this application, we will use Theorem

.
We introduce the following quantity.

Definition 15
.

Let
N
be a large positive integer, let

f\in\mathbb{C}^{k}((0,3N))
,
k\geq 3
and
0<\delta<1/4
.
Define

\mathcal{R}(f,N,\delta):=\#\{n\in[N,2N]\cap\mathbb{Z};\ \|f(n)\|<\delta\}.

Like this, we count lattice points in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
close to the
graph of

.
Bordellès gives in
[
6
, Ch. 5]
an overview of
several known nontrivial bounds for this quantity and their
applications.
In Lemma
3
we gave already a bound for
\mathcal{R}(f,N,\delta)

in the case

, it is also known as the first derivative test
for
\mathcal{R}(f,N,\delta)
.

In what follows, we use a property of the set which is
counted by
\mathcal{R}(f,N,\delta)
. It is proved in
[
6
, Thm. 5.11]
.

Lemma 16
.

Suppose that there exists real numbers

\lambda,A>0

such that
\lambda\leq f^{(k)}(x)\leq A\lambda\leq 1/4
holds for
all
x\in(0,3N)
.
In the set
\{n\in[N,2N]\cap\mathbb{Z};\ \|f(n)\|<\delta\}
there exists a

H^{\prime}
-spaced subset
\mathcal{R}
such that
\mathcal{R}(f,N,\delta)\leq(k+1)(1+\#\mathcal{R})
,
where
H^{\prime}=(A\lambda)^{-2/k(k+1)}
.

A set is called
H^{\prime}
-spaced
if any two elements differ by more than
H^{\prime}>0
.

Theorem
13
of Section
4

allows us to prove a strong bound for
\mathcal{R}(f,N,\delta)

which is the following.

Theorem 17
.

Suppose that there exists real numbers

\lambda,A>0

such that
\lambda\leq f^{(k)}(x)\leq A\lambda\leq c_{0}
holds for
all
x\in(0,3N)
and some small constant
c_{0}<1/4
,
and assume
(A\lambda)^{-2/k(k+1)}\leq N
.
Let
\lambda_{1}>0
be such that
|f^{\prime}(x)|\leq\lambda_{1}
holds for all

x\in(0,3N)
.
Assume that
\delta>0
is such that

A\lambda\ll\delta+\lambda_{1}\ll(A\lambda)^{1-2(k-1)/k(k+1)},

(23)

then we have the bound

\mathcal{R}(f,N,\delta)\ll 1+A(1+A\lambda N)((\delta+\lambda_{1})/A\lambda)^{1/(k-1)}.

(24)

Proof.

We begin the proof as indicated in
[
6
, Ex. 6.7.4]
.
Let
m\in\mathbb{Z}
with
N\leq m\leq 2N
and
\|f(m)\|<\delta
,
then

\Big|\sum_{a=0}^{T-1}\mathrm{e}(af(m))\Big|^{2}=\sum_{a_{1},a_{2}}\mathrm{e}((a_{1}-a_{2})f(m))\\
=\sum_{a_{1},a_{2}}\Re(\mathrm{e}((a_{1}-a_{2})f(m)))\geq T^{2}/2,

(25)

since we have
\Re(\mathrm{e}(af(m)))\geq\sqrt{2}/2
for all
a\in\mathbb{Z}
with
|a|<T
,
provided that
1\leq T\leq[1/8\delta]+1
.

From Lemma
16
we know that there is a
H^{\prime}
-spaced subset
\mathcal{R}
of

\{m;\ N\leq m\leq 2N,\|f(m)\|<\delta\}
with
\mathcal{R}(f,N,\delta)\leq(k+1)(1+\#\mathcal{R})
, where
H^{\prime}=(A\lambda)^{-2/k(k+1)}
.

Hence

\mathcal{R}(f,N,\delta)\ll 1+\sum_{m\in\mathcal{R}}1\leq 1+\frac{2}{T^{2}}\sum_{m\in\mathcal{R}}\Big|\sum_{a=0}^{T-1}\mathrm{e}(af(m))\Big|^{2}.

Now opening the square and separating the summand for
a=0
shows

\Big|\sum_{a=0}^{T-1}\mathrm{e}(af(m))\Big|^{2}=\sum_{|a|\leq T-1}(T-|a|)\mathrm{e}(af(m))\\
=T+\sum_{0<|a|\leq T-1}(T-|a|)\mathrm{e}(af(m))=:T+a_{m}

(26)

say. Clearly
a_{m}>0
for large
T
and for
m\in\mathcal{R}
due to (
25
).
We proceed with

\mathcal{R}(f,N,\delta)\ll 1+\mathcal{R}(f,N,\delta)T^{-1}+T^{-2}\sum_{m\in\mathcal{R}}a_{m}\ll 1+T^{-2}\sum_{m\in\mathcal{R}}a_{m},

(27)

by an application of Lemma
4
if we take
T\gg 1
(in fact

T\geq 4(k+1)
suffices).

Let
m\in\mathcal{R}
,
then there exists an integer
n
with
|f(m)-n|<\delta
. Thus,
by an application of the mean value theorem,

|f(m-h)-f(m)|=|f^{\prime}(t)|h\leq\lambda_{1}H

for some
t\in(-h,0)
.
We conclude
|f(m-h)-n|\leq|f(m-h)-f(m)|+|f(m)-n|\leq\lambda_{1}H+\delta
, so

\|f(m-h)\|\leq\lambda_{1}H+\delta
for all
h\leq H
.

This argument shows that for
m\in\mathcal{R}

we have
a_{m-h}\geq T^{2}/2-T\gg T^{2}
by (
25
)
assuming

T\leq\frac{1}{8(\lambda_{1}H+\delta)}.

(28)

Since
T^{2}\geq a_{m}
we conclude that

a_{m}\ll\frac{1}{H}\sum_{h\leq H}a_{m-h}.

This implies

\sum_{m\in\mathcal{R}}a_{m}\ll\sum_{m\in\mathcal{R}}\frac{1}{H}\sum_{h\leq H}a_{m-h}.

Now we have

\sum_{m\in\mathcal{R}}\frac{1}{H}\sum_{h\leq H}a_{m-h}=\frac{1}{H}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}\\
\exists h\leq H:n+h\in\mathcal{R}\end{subarray}}a_{n},

since for each
m\in\mathcal{R}
, the summands
a_{m-1},\dots,a_{m-h}
occur
in the sum on the right hand side. Assuming
H\leq H^{\prime}
, the elements
of
\mathcal{R}
are
H
-spaced, so each such summand occurs exactly once on
both sides of this equality.

This improves upon the Weyl step in the proofs above, namely

\sum_{m\in\mathcal{R}}a_{m}\ll H^{-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}\\
\exists h^{\prime}\leq H:n+h^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}\end{subarray}}a_{n}=H^{-2}\sum_{h\leq H}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}\\
\exists h^{\prime}\leq H:n+h+h^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}\end{subarray}}a_{n+h}\\
=H^{-2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}\\
\exists h_{0}\in[1,2H]:n+h_{0}\in\mathcal{R}\end{subarray}}\ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h\leq H\\

=H^{-2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}\cap[N-2H,2N]\\

h\in[h_{0}-H,h_{0}-1]\end{subarray}}a_{n+h}.

Note that the number of

in this sum is
\ll\min\{H\#\mathcal{R},N\}=:R

\mathcal{R}

.
Further,
h

of length at most
H
. The extra factor

With above definition (

we derive

\sum_{m\in\mathcal{R}}a_{m}\ll TH^{-2}\sum_{a\leq T}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in[N-2H,2N]\\
\exists h_{0}\leq 2H:m+h_{0}\in\mathcal{R}\end{subarray}}\Big|\sum_{h\in\mathcal{H}}\mathrm{e}(af(m+h))\Big|.

(29)

in the proofs of the Theorems

and

For this, let us denote in analogy to

Thus

\mathcal{R}(f,N,\delta)\ll 1+T^{-1}H^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{f}^{\prime}

)
and (

.

Next, for appropriate

with the well-known theorem of Huxley and Sargos from

Theorem 18

(Huxley and Sargos, explicit in
A
)
.

Let
k\geq 3
be an integer and
f\in C^{k}([N,2N])
be such that there
exist
\lambda,A>0
with
\lambda\leq|f^{(k)}(x)|\leq A\lambda

for all
x\in[N,2N]
. Let
0<\delta<1/4
. Then

\mathcal{R}(f,N,\delta)\ll N(A\lambda)^{2/k(k+1)}+N(A\delta)^{2/k(k-1)}+(\delta/\lambda)^{1/k}+1.

In Theorem
18
, the first term

N(A\lambda)^{2/k(k+1)}
dominates if

\delta\leq\lambda^{1-2/(k+1)}

A^{-2/(k+1)}

and
\delta\ll N^{k}A^{2/(k+1)}\lambda^{1+2/(k+1)}
,
especially when examining very small
\delta
.
The main term

is commonly called the smoothness term
and being regarded as very difficult to improve,
compare also
[
6
, p.275]
.

A comparison of the term

in the bound of Theorem

with the smoothness term
shows that under the stated assumptions,

gives a sharper bound.
Also note that if we let

in Theorem

, we obtain the improved bound

\mathcal{R}(f,N,0)\ll 1+A(1+A\lambda N)(\lambda_{1}/A\lambda)^{1/(k-1)}
,
compared to Theorem

which yields

\mathcal{R}(f,N,0)
.

The bound of Theorem

is quite satisfying,
the only obstruction lies in the restrictive assumption

and

.
This shows that Theorem

is in fact of limited use in applications since this condition
is true only for certain appropriate functions. Unfortunately,

, which are occurring in interesting applications,

And if

is so big such that (

can be quite weak.

In this context, we state the following theorem of Gorny

]

Theorem 19

(Gorny)
.

Let

,

such that

with implicit constant that depends on

only.

By this theorem, we conclude that (
33
) is true for all
sufficiently large
N
provided that
M\leq(A\lambda)^{1-2/(k+1)}
.
Therefore, functions on
[1,1+3N]
that are much smaller in absolute value
compared to the maximum of the absolute value of the

k
-th derivative are admissible for Theorem
17
.
This gives a nice criterion for Theorem
17
to hold, but it
seems to be hard to find easy examples.

Remark.

Applying Theorem
12
instead of Theorem
13
in
the proof of Theorem
17
would
also lead to a vanishing of the minor arc contribution in the bound,
but the resulting bound for
\mathcal{R}(f,N,\delta)

would be weaker due to the bigger major arc contribution.
Instead, the presented vanishing trick may be used with even
bigger minor arc contributions probably leading to further refinements.

6.
Second application:
The polynomial large sieve inequality
(LSI) in the one-dimensional case

In this section we present an application of Theorem
8
,
namely to the polynomial large sieve inequality. This generalization of the
classical large sieve inequality to sparse moduli sets, usually given as values
of some fixed polynomial, has been studied intensely in past research and has
already influenced some other areas of Number Theory, especially the
version with
k=2
from
[
2
]
.
To name a few such topics, it has been found useful to find
variants of Bombieri–Vinogradov’s theorem
[
4
,
13
]
,
new results on
primes of polynomial shape
[
9
]
or primes in APs to spaced
moduli
[
4
]
,
divisibility questions with Fermat quotients
[
8
]
,
and mean value estimates for character sums with
applications
[
1
,
3
,
5
,
16
]
. Furthermore, the
multidimensional polynomial LSI can be used
for sieving with high powers like seen in
[
12
]
and reaches questions
related to the abc-conjecture.

We start by giving the setting and basic assumptions in the polynomial
LSI in the one-dimensional case.

Setting.
Let
P\in\mathbb{R}[x]
be a fixed monic polynomial of
degree
k\geq 2
with
P(0)=0
.
Assume that
P
has only positive values in
[Q,2Q]
for each real
Q\geq 1

and let
M_{Q}:=\max\{P(q);\ q\in[Q,2Q]\}
be the maximal value for
integers
q\in[Q,2Q]
. Clearly
M_{Q}\ll Q^{k}
,
assume also that
P(q)\gg Q^{k}
holds true for all
integers
q\in[Q,2Q]
and some implicit constant that may depend only on
k
.
Let
N,M
be integers and
(v_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of complex
numbers.

In the theory of the large polynomial LSI, see

[
2
,
11
,
21
]
,
we aim to give upper bounds for the quantity

\Sigma_{P}:=\sum_{q\leq Q}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a\leq P(q)\\
(a,P(q))=1\end{subarray}}\Big|\sum_{M<n\leq M+N}v_{n}\mathrm{e}\Big(\frac{an}{P(q)}\Big)\Big|^{2}.

When we put the current form for Weyl’s inequality,
Theorem
6
, in the machinery of
[
11
,
12
]
,
the bound
\Sigma_{P}\ll Q^{\varepsilon}\|v\|^{2}(Q^{k+1}+A_{k}(Q,N))
is easily derived with

A_{k}(Q,N):=NQ^{1-1/k(k-1)}+N^{1-1/k(k-1)}Q^{1+1/(k-1)}.

The interesting range for
N
is
Q^{k}\ll N\ll Q^{2k}
, since outside
that, it is already known by an application of the standard large
sieve inequality that the sharp bound

\Sigma_{P}\ll Q^{\varepsilon}\|v\|^{2}(Q^{k+1}+N)
holds true. So we
assume without loss of generality that
N
lies in this range.

This result already offers an improvement compared to
[
3
]

in the range
Q^{k}\ll N\ll Q^{2k-2+2/k(k-1)}
when
k\geq 3
. We
will now improve on that.

6.1.
The connection of the Polynomial LSI with Weyl sums

From
[
11
, Lemma 1]
, we know that

\Sigma_{P}\ll Q^{\varepsilon}\|v\|^{2}\bigg(\sum_{Q<q\leq 2Q}P(q)+\max_{Q<r\leq 2Q}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq b<P(r)\\
\operatorname{gcd}(b,P(r))=1\end{subarray}}\int_{1/N}^{1/2}\#\mathcal{F}_{b,P(r)}(x)\frac{dx}{x^{2}}\bigg).

(34)

The first term in large brackets
can be estimated as
\ll QM_{Q}
and is admissible.
It is not necessary to repeat the definition of
\#\mathcal{F}_{b,P(r)}(x)

since we will just make use of the upper bound

\#\mathcal{F}_{b,P(r)}(x)\ll B^{-1}Q+B^{-1}\sum_{1\leq a\leq B}|S_{a}|

with
B^{-1}=2M_{Q}x
and

S_{a}:=\sum_{q\leq 2Q}\mathrm{e}\Big(\frac{ab}{P(r)}P(q)\Big),

which has been shown in the deduction of
[
11
, (8)]
.

To consider the integral expression in (
34
),
fix a pair
b,r
with
r\in[Q,2Q]
,
1\leq b<P(r)
and

\operatorname{gcd}(b,P(r))=1
.
We substitute
B^{-1}=2M_{Q}x

and estimate as follows.

\begin{aligned} \int_{1/N}^{1/2}&\#\mathcal{F}_{b,P(r)}(x)\frac{dx}{x^{2}}\\
&\ll\int_{1/4M_{Q}}^{N/2M_{Q}}\bigg(B^{-1}Q+B^{-1}\sum_{a\leq B}|S_{a}|\bigg)M_{Q}\,dB\\
&\ll QM_{Q}\log N+M_{Q}\int_{1/4M_{Q}}^{N/2M_{Q}}B^{-1}\sum_{a\leq B}|S_{a}|\,dB\\
&\ll QM_{Q}\log N+M_{Q}\sum_{a\leq N/2M_{Q}}|S_{a}|\int_{a}^{N/2M_{Q}}B^{-1}\,dB\\
&\ll QM_{Q}\log N+M_{Q}\log N\sum_{a\leq N/2M_{Q}}|S_{a}|.\end{aligned}

Here the last sum is a discrete moment of a Weyl sum
with the polynomial
bP(x)/P(r)
and leading term
b/P(r)
since
P

is monic.
We are able to apply Theorem
8
directly with
P(r)
as approximating
denominator (when
k\geq 3
). By this, we have

\sum_{a\leq N/2M_{Q}}|S_{a}|\ll\frac{N}{M_{Q}}Q^{1+\varepsilon}\Big(\frac{Q^{k-1}}{P(r)}+\frac{Q^{k-1}}{N/M_{Q}}+\frac{1}{Q}+\frac{P(r)}{QN/M_{Q}}\Big)^{1/2s_{0}}

with

s_{0}=(k-1)(k-2)/2+1,\text{ so that }2s_{0}=k(k-1)-2k+4.

Let
\omega:=1/2s_{0}
.

In the big bracket expression, the last summand
P(r)M_{Q}/NQ

dominates since
P(r)\gg Q^{k}
and
1/Q\ll P(r)M_{Q}/NQ
for
N\ll Q^{2k}
.

So we continue with

\displaystyle\int_{1/N}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\#\mathcal{F}_{b,P(r)}(x)\frac{dx}{x^{2}}

\displaystyle\ll QM_{Q}\log Q+N^{1-\omega}M_{Q}^{\omega}Q^{1-\omega+\varepsilon}P(r)^{\omega}

\displaystyle\ll QM_{Q}\log Q+N^{1-\omega}M_{Q}^{2\omega}Q^{1-\omega+\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\ll Q^{k+1}\log Q+N^{1-\omega}Q^{1+2k\omega-\omega+\varepsilon},

where we used
M_{Q}\ll Q^{k}
in the last step.

Compared to the dominating term
NQ^{1-1/k(k-1)}
in the former bound

A_{k}(Q,N)
,
we get an advantage if
N^{1-\omega}Q^{1+(2k-1)\omega}\leq NQ^{1-1/k(k-1)}
,
which is the case if

N\geq Q^{2k-2/(k-1)+4/k(k-1)}
, so when
N
is close to
Q^{2k}
,
but still in the interesting range
Q^{k}\ll N\ll Q^{2k}
.
We have therefore shown the following new improved bound
for the polynomial LSI.

Theorem 20
.

In the setting of Section
6
when
k\geq 3
,

\Sigma_{P}\ll Q^{\varepsilon}\|v\|^{2}(Q^{k+1}+\min\{A_{k}(Q,N),N^{1-\omega}Q^{1+(2k-1)\omega}\})

(35)

with
\omega=1/((k-1)(k-2)+2)
.

Theorem
20
offers an improvement compared to known previous
results when
k\geq 4
. This is since for
k=3
, the additional bound from

[
3
]
is still stronger in this case.

It is interesting what we would obtain having Conjecture
10
.
In this case, we would be able to
gain a factor
Q^{(1-k)\omega}
.
Then, we would arrive at the following result.

Conjecture 21
.

In the setting of Section
6
when
k\geq 3
,

\Sigma_{P}\ll Q^{\varepsilon}\|v\|^{2}(Q^{k+1}+\min\{A_{k}(Q,N),N^{1-\omega}Q^{1+k\omega}\})

with
\omega=1/((k-1)(k-2)+2)
.

Note that if we could take even
1/k(k-1)
at the place of
\omega
,
the expression
N^{1-1/k(k-1)}Q^{1+1/(k-1)}
coincides with the
second summand in
A_{k}(Q,N)
.

Still, these conjectural bounds are far from Zhao’s conjecture
in
[
21
]
stating

\Sigma_{P}\ll Q^{\varepsilon}\|v\|^{2}(Q^{k+1}+N).

Conjecture
21
might be rather within reach
of further refinements of the methods
presented in this article.

We remark that an attempt to use Theorem
11
will not give
more if more on the coefficient of
q^{k-1}
in
P(q)
, say

\alpha_{k-1}
, is known. This is since the coefficient of
q^{k-1}

in the polynomial
bP(q)/P(r)
is then
\alpha_{k-1}b/P(r)
. By
Theorem
11
, one needs to look then at the rational approximations
to
\alpha_{k-1}b
. Since
b
is supposed to be
any
coprime
residue mod
P(r)
, there will always be one with small denominator
which offers no advantage in Theorem
11
.

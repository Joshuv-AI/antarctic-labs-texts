Bianchi modular symbols and p p -adic L L -functions
JAESUNG KWON

Abstract
. In the present paper, we construct the integral special L L -value and integral valued p p -adic L L -function of p p -ordinary Bianchi Hecke eigenforms. We obtain the functional equation of the L L -function of Bianchi modular forms. By using the functional equation, we compute the approximate functional equation, which is a fast convergent series of L L -values. By studying the fast convergent series of special L L -values, we prove that the first homology group of the Bianchi modular manifold is generated by the special Bianchi modular symbols. As a corollary of the generation result, we have that the μ \mu -invariant of some isotopic components of the p p -adic L L -function of certain Bianchi Hecke eigenforms vanishes for a positive proportion of ordinary prime ideals. By studying a ℓ \ell -adic representation attached to a Bianchi Hecke eigenform, we show that the density of the set of ordinary prime ideals is one. Also we obtain the result of residual non-vanishing of the integral L L -values.

1.4.
Skectch of the proof of Theorem A

Let

1.5.
Lattice estimation

To obtain the equation (
1.1
), we have to compute the estimation on the number of points in certain lattice in bounded region. To do this, we have to study the properties of the norm map
N_{F/\mathbb{Q}}:F^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
. If
F
is imaginary quadratic, then the norm map is given by the square of the absolute value, which is easy. But the difficulty arises for the case of general number fields. For example, if

is real quadratic, then the norm form
(a,b)\mapsto N(a+b\sqrt{D})a=a^{2}-b^{2}D
is not positive definite.

1.6.
\ell
-adic Galois representations and ordinary primes

In Theorem A, we consider the ordinary primes in some arithmetic progression. Therefore, we have to prove that the density of the ordinary primes is positive.
If a normalized Bianchi Hecke eigenform

1.7.
Technical Difficulties

To deal with the general class number, we use the adelic language. In Kwon-Sun
[

]
, we can see the similar techniques.

Consider the module

i
.
We define the
m
-th component
f_{m}
of a
L_{2k-2}(\mathbb{C})
-valued function
f
by

f(g)(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{m=0}^{2k-2}f_{m}(g)X^{2k-2-m}Y^{m}.

Let us fix a branch of the square root function on
\mathbb{C}
. For
x\in\mathbb{C}
and
y\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, define an element
(x;y)\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
by

(x;y):=\begin{pmatrix}y^{\frac{1}{2}}&xy^{-\frac{1}{2}}\\
0&y^{-\frac{1}{2}}\end{pmatrix}.

By Proposition
2.2
, we observe that

\displaystyle f_{k-1}\big(t_{i}(0;y)t(u)\big)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2k-2\\
k-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)|a_{i}|_{\mathbb{A}_{F}}\sum_{\xi\in F^{\times}}a_{f}(\xi\mathfrak{a}_{i}\mathfrak{d}_{F})|y|^{k}K_{0}(4\pi|\xi y|)\mathbf{e}_{F}(\xi a_{i}u).

So the Mellin transform of the function
y\mapsto f_{k-1}\big(t_{i}(0;y)t(u)\big)
on
\mathbb{R}^{\times}_{>0}
is given by

(3.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&|a_{i}|_{\mathbb{A}_{F}}^{s-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{k-1}\big(t_{i}(0;y)t(u)\big)y^{2s-k}\frac{dy}{y}\\
=&\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2k-2\\
k-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)|D_{F}|^{s}\sum_{\xi\in F^{\times}}\frac{a_{f}(\xi\mathfrak{a}_{i}\mathfrak{d}_{F})\mathbf{e}_{F}(\xi a_{i}u)}{N(\xi\mathfrak{a}_{i}\mathfrak{d}_{F})^{s}}\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{2s}K_{0}(4\pi y)\frac{dy}{y}\end{split}

for
\text{Re}(s)>\frac{k+2}{2}
. By the following integration formula

\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{s}{K_{j}(ay)}\frac{dy}{y}=\frac{1}{4}\Big(\frac{a}{2}\Big)^{-s}\Gamma\Big(\frac{s+j}{2}\Big)\Gamma\Big(\frac{s-j}{2}\Big)\text{ for }a>0\text{ and }\mathfrak{R}(s\pm j)>0,

the equation (
3.1
) becomes

\displaystyle|a_{i}|_{\mathbb{A}_{F}}^{s-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{k-1}\big(t_{i}(0;y)t(u)\big)y^{2s-k}\frac{dy}{y}=\frac{\Gamma_{F,k}(s)}{|\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}|}\sum_{\xi\in F^{\times}}\frac{a_{f}(\xi\mathfrak{a}_{i}\mathfrak{d}_{F})\mathbf{e}_{F}(\xi a_{i}u)}{N(\xi\mathfrak{a}_{i}\mathfrak{d}_{F})^{s}}

for
\mathfrak{R}(s)>\frac{k+2}{2}
, where
\Gamma_{F,k}(s):=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2k-2\\
k-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\frac{|D_{F}|^{s}|\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}|}{4(2\pi)^{2s}}\Gamma(s)^{2}.

Finally, we obtain the integral representation of
L(s,f,u)
:

(3.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Gamma_{F,k}(s)L(s,f,u)=\sum_{i=1}^{h_{F}}|a_{i}|_{\mathbb{A}_{F}}^{s-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{k-1}\big(t_{i}(0;y)t(u)\big)y^{2s-k}\frac{dy}{y}\end{split}

for
\mathfrak{R}(s)>\frac{k+2}{2}
.

3.2.
Functional equations

Let
\omega_{\chi}:=\chi|\cdot|_{\mathbb{A}_{F}}^{k-2}
be the standard normalization of
\chi
. Let
\mathfrak{q}
be an integral ideal of
F
coprime to
\mathfrak{a}_{i}^{-1}\mathfrak{d}_{F}\mathfrak{N}
for each
i
.
Let
R_{\mathfrak{q}}
be the set of elements of
\prod_{v|\mathfrak{q}}F_{v}^{\times}
corrensponding to a set of representatives of
(\mathfrak{q}^{-1}/\mathcal{O}_{F})^{\times}
. Let us embed
u\in R_{\mathfrak{q}}
into
\mathbb{A}_{F}^{(\infty)}
by
u\mapsto(0,\cdots,0,u,0,\cdots,0)
.
Note that we have the following bijection:

R_{\mathfrak{q}}\rightarrow(\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{F}/\widehat{\mathfrak{q}})^{\times},\ u\mapsto\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}u+\widehat{\mathfrak{q}}.

For
u\in R_{\mathfrak{q}}
, let us define a
completed L-function

\Lambda(s,f,u)
of
L(s,f,u)
by

\Lambda(s,f,u):=|\varpi_{\mathfrak{N}}\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}^{2}|_{\mathbb{A}_{F}}^{-\frac{s}{2}}\Gamma_{F,k}(s)L(s,f,u).

Let us define the
Fricke involution

W_{\mathfrak{N}}
by taking the right translation with the element
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\
\varpi_{\mathfrak{N}}&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F}^{(\infty)})
:

W_{\mathfrak{N}}f(g)(\mathbf{x}):=(-1)^{k-1}|\varpi_{\mathfrak{N}}|_{\mathbb{A}_{F}}^{\frac{k}{2}-1}\omega_{\chi}^{-1}\big(\det(g)\big)f\bigg(g\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
\varpi_{\mathfrak{N}}&0\end{pmatrix}\bigg)(\mathbf{x}).

Then
W_{\mathfrak{N}}f
is a cusp form in
S_{k}(\mathfrak{N},\overline{\chi})
. Note that for
u\in R_{\mathfrak{q}}
, we can consider
u\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}
to be an element of
(\mathcal{O}_{F,\mathfrak{q}}/\mathfrak{q}^{2})^{\times}
. For
u\in R_{\mathfrak{q}}
, let us denote by
u^{\prime}\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}
a representative of the mod
\mathfrak{q}^{2}
inverse of
-u\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}
. Then we obtain a functional equation of
\Lambda(s,f,u)
as follows:

Proposition 3.1
.

For
u\in R_{\mathfrak{q}}
, we have the following functional equation:

(3.3)

\Lambda(s,f,u)=\omega_{\chi}(\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}})\Lambda(k-s,W_{\mathfrak{N}}f,u^{\prime}).

Also we have the anlaytic continuation of
\Lambda(s,f,u)
to the entire
s\in\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

Let us denote
\mathfrak{Q}=\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{q}^{2}
. Then there exists a
h_{F}
-permutation
\sigma
and elements
\xi_{i}\in F^{\times}
such that
\mathfrak{a}_{i}^{-1}\mathfrak{Q}=\xi_{i}\mathfrak{a}_{\sigma(i)}
for each
i
. For
\xi\in F^{\times}
, let us denote
w_{\xi}:=\xi^{(\infty)}\varpi_{\xi\mathcal{O}_{F}}^{-1}
. Then we obtain

w_{\xi_{i}}\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{F}^{\times},\ (w_{\xi_{i}})_{\mathfrak{q}}=1,\ a_{i}^{-1}\varpi_{\mathfrak{Q}}=a_{\sigma(i)}\xi_{i}^{(\infty)}w_{\xi_{i}}^{-1},\text{ and }|\xi_{i}|=|a_{i}^{2}a_{\sigma(i)}^{2}\varpi_{\mathfrak{Q}}^{-2}|_{\mathbb{A}_{F}}^{1/4}.

Let us denote by
f_{(i),j}^{u}
the function
y\mapsto f_{j}\big(t_{i}(0;y)t(u)\big)
on
\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\times}
. Then by the equation (
3.2
), we obtain

(3.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Lambda(s,f,u)=\sum_{i=1}^{h_{F}}\frac{|\varpi_{\mathfrak{Q}}|_{\mathbb{A}_{F}}^{-\frac{s}{2}}}{|a_{i}|_{\mathbb{A}_{F}}^{1-s}}\bigg(\int_{|a_{i}^{-2}\varpi_{\mathfrak{Q}}|_{\mathbb{A}_{F}}^{1/4}}^{\infty}+\int_{0}^{|a_{i}^{-2}\varpi_{\mathfrak{Q}}|_{\mathbb{A}_{F}}^{1/4}}\bigg)f_{(i),k-1}^{u}(y)y^{2s-k}\frac{dy}{y}.\end{split}

Let us set
\gamma_{i}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\
\xi_{i}&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}(F)
. Let
b(u)\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{F}
be the element such that
b(u)_{\mathfrak{q}}\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}^{2}-(u\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}})(u^{\prime}\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}})=1
and
b(u)_{v}=1
for
v
coprime to
\mathfrak{q}
.
Under the isomorphism
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\cong\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\times\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F}^{(\infty)})
,
\gamma_{i}t_{i}(0;y)t(u)
can be decomposed into

\displaystyle\gamma_{i}t_{i}(0;y)t(u)=\big(\xi_{i}^{1/2}(0;|\xi_{i}|^{-1}y^{-1})U_{i},\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}a_{\sigma(i)}^{-1}t_{\sigma(i)}t(u^{\prime})A_{\mathfrak{N}}B_{u}C_{i}\big)

for each
y\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and
i
, where
U_{i}=(0;|\xi_{i}|\xi_{i}^{-1})\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\operatorname{SU}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
,
A_{\mathfrak{N}}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\
\varpi_{\mathfrak{N}}&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
,

B_{u}=\begin{pmatrix}\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}&u\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}\\
u^{\prime}\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}&b(u)\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}\end{pmatrix}\in U_{1}(\mathfrak{N}),\text{ and }C_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}w_{\xi_{i}}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\in U_{1}(\mathfrak{N}).

Note that
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(F)
acts on
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\cong\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\times\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F}^{(\infty)})
diagonally.
By using the above equations and the properties of
f
, we have

\displaystyle f_{(i),k-1}^{u}(y)

\displaystyle=f_{k-1}\big(t_{i}(0;y)t(u)\big)=f_{k-1}\big(\gamma_{i}t_{i}(0;y)t(u)\big)

\displaystyle=|\xi_{i}|^{2-k}\chi_{\mathfrak{N}}(B_{u}C_{i})\chi(\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}a_{\sigma(i)}^{-1})\Big(f\big(t_{\sigma(i)}(0;|\xi_{i}|^{-1}y^{-1})t(u^{\prime})A_{\mathfrak{N}}\big)\big(U_{i}\mathbf{x}\big)\Big)_{k-1}

\displaystyle=|\xi_{i}|^{2-k}|a_{\sigma(i)}|_{\mathbb{A}_{F}}^{k-2}|\varpi_{\mathfrak{Q}}|_{\mathbb{A}_{F}}^{1-\frac{k}{2}}\omega_{\chi}(\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}})(W_{\mathfrak{N}}f)^{u^{\prime}}_{(\sigma(i)),k-1}(|\xi_{i}|^{-1}y^{-1}).

By using the above equations, we can write the second term of the integral (
3.4
) as

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{h_{F}}\frac{\omega_{\chi}(\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}})|\varpi_{\mathfrak{Q}}|_{\mathbb{A}_{F}}^{\frac{s-k}{2}}}{|a_{\sigma(i)}|^{1-k+s}_{\mathbb{A}_{F}}}\int_{0}^{|a_{i}^{-2}\varpi_{\mathfrak{Q}}|_{\mathbb{A}_{F}}^{1/4}}(W_{\mathfrak{N}}f)^{u^{\prime}}_{(\sigma(i)),k-1}\big(|\xi_{i}|^{-1}y^{-1}\big)(|\xi_{i}|y)^{2s-k}\frac{dy}{y}.

By applying the change of variable to the above equation, we can rewrite the equation (
3.4
) as follows:

(3.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Lambda(s,f,u)=&\sum_{i=1}^{h_{F}}\bigg(\int_{|a_{i}^{-2}\varpi_{\mathfrak{Q}}|_{\mathbb{A}_{F}}^{1/4}}^{\infty}r_{i}(s)f^{u}_{(i),k-1}(y)y^{2s-k}\frac{dy}{y}\\
&+\int_{|a_{i}^{-2}\varpi_{\mathfrak{Q}}|_{\mathbb{A}_{F}}^{1/4}}^{\infty}\omega_{\chi}(\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}})r_{i}(k-s)(W_{\mathfrak{N}}f)^{u^{\prime}}_{(i),k-1}(y)y^{k-2s}\frac{dy}{y}\bigg),\end{split}

where
r_{i}(s)=|\varpi_{\mathfrak{Q}}|_{\mathbb{A}_{F}}^{-\frac{s}{2}}|a_{i}|_{\mathbb{A}_{F}}^{s-1}
. Note that
W_{\mathfrak{N}}f\in S_{2}(\mathfrak{N},\overline{\chi})
,
W_{\mathfrak{N}}^{2}f=f
for
f\in S_{2}(\mathfrak{N},\chi)
, and
(u^{\prime})^{\prime}=u
.
By the straightforward computation, we can obtain the desired funtional equation from the integral representation (
3.5
).
Also the equation (
3.5
) implies that
\Lambda(s,f,u)
has the analytic continuation to the entire
s\in\mathbb{C}
.
∎

By using our functional equation
(\ref{func:eq})
, we obtain an approximate functional equation of
\Lambda(s,f,u)
for
u\in R_{\mathfrak{q}}
in a spirit of Luo-Ramakrishnan
[
10
]
. Let us fix an infinitely differentiable function
\Phi

on
\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\times}
with compact support such that
\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi(y)\frac{dy}{y}=1
. Define functions

\displaystyle V_{1,s}(x)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{2-i\infty}^{2+i\infty}\kappa(t)\Gamma_{F,k}(s+t)x^{-t}\frac{dt}{t},

\displaystyle V_{2,s}(x)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{2-i\infty}^{2+i\infty}\kappa(-t)\Gamma_{F,k}(s+t)x^{-t}\frac{dt}{t},

where
\kappa(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi(y)y^{t}\frac{dy}{y}
. By shifting the contour, one can show that for
s\neq 1/2
,
V_{1,s}
and
V_{2,s}
satisfy following:

(3.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}V_{j,s}(x)&=O(\Gamma_{F,k}(\mathfrak{R}(s)+j)x^{-j})\text{ for all }j\geq 1\text{ as }x\rightarrow\infty,\\
V_{j,s}(x)&=\Gamma_{F,k}(s)+O\Big(\Gamma_{F,k}\Big(\mathfrak{R}(s)-\frac{1}{2}\Big)x^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)\text{ as }x\rightarrow 0,\end{split}

where the implicit constants depend only on
j
and
\Phi
. Let
u
be an element of
R_{\mathfrak{q}}
. Let us recall that
\mathfrak{q}
is coprime to
\mathfrak{a}_{i}^{-1}\mathfrak{N}
for each
i
. For
\mathfrak{R}(s)>\frac{k+2}{2}
and
y>0
, we obtain

(3.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{2-i\infty}^{2+i\infty}\kappa\big((-1)^{j-1}t\big)\Gamma_{F,k}(s+t)L(s+t,f,u)y^{t}\frac{dt}{t}\\
=&\frac{1}{|\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}|}\sum_{i=1}^{h_{F}}\sum_{\xi\in F^{\times}}\frac{a_{f}(\xi\mathfrak{a}_{i}\mathfrak{d}_{F})\mathbf{e}_{F}(\xi a_{i}u)}{N(\xi\mathfrak{a}_{i}\mathfrak{d}_{F})^{s}}V_{j,s}\Big(\frac{N(\xi\mathfrak{a}_{i}\mathfrak{d}_{F})}{y}\Big).\end{split}

by the definition of
L(s,f,u)
. From the Residue theorem, we have

\displaystyle\Gamma_{F,k}(s)L(s,f,u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{2-i\infty}^{2+i\infty}\kappa(t)\Gamma_{F,k}(s+t)L(s+t,f,u)y^{t}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{-2+i\infty}^{-2-i\infty}\kappa(t)\Gamma_{F,k}(s+t)L(s+t,f,u)y^{t}\frac{dt}{t}.

Putting the equations (
3.3
), (
3.7
) in the above equation and applying Proposition
2.2
.(1), we obtain

(3.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Lambda(s,f,u)&=\sum_{i=1}^{h_{F}}\bigg(N(\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{q}^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\sum_{0\neq\alpha\in\mathfrak{a}_{i}^{-1}}\frac{a_{f}(\alpha\mathfrak{a}_{i})\mathbf{e}_{F}(\alpha u)}{N(\alpha\mathfrak{a}_{i})^{s}|\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}|}V_{1,s}\Big(\frac{N(\alpha\mathfrak{a}_{i})}{y}\Big)\\
+\omega_{\chi}(\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}})&N(\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{q}^{2})^{\frac{k-s}{2}}\sum_{0\neq\alpha\in\mathfrak{a}_{i}^{-1}}\frac{a_{{W_{\mathfrak{N}}}f}(\alpha\mathfrak{a}_{i})\mathbf{e}_{F}(\alpha u^{\prime})}{N(\alpha\mathfrak{a}_{i})^{k-s}|\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}|}V_{2,k-s}\Big(\frac{N(\alpha\mathfrak{a}_{i})y}{N(\mathfrak{N}\mathfrak{q}^{2})}\Big)\bigg).\end{split}

Note that
\mathbf{e}_{F}(\alpha u)=\mathbf{e}_{F}(\alpha a_{i}d_{F}^{-1}u)
as
\mathfrak{q}
is coprime to

for each

.

3.3.
Twisted cusp forms

Let
f\in S_{k}(\mathfrak{N},\chi)
be a cusp form. For a finite order Hecke character
\phi:F^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
of conductor
\mathfrak{q}
, the
twisted cusp form

f\otimes\phi:\text{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\rightarrow L(2k-2,\mathbb{C})
is defined by

(3.9)

f\otimes\phi(g)(\mathbf{x}):=G(\phi)^{-1}\phi\big(\det(g)\big)\sum_{u\in R_{\mathfrak{q}}}\phi(\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}u)f\big(gt(u)\big)(\mathbf{x}),

where
G(\phi)
is the Gauss sum of
\phi
(see Hida
[
5
, Section 6]
).
Then we have
f\otimes\phi\in S_{k}(\mathfrak{N}\cap\mathfrak{q}^{2},\chi\phi^{2})
and
a_{f\otimes\phi}(\mathfrak{a})=a_{f}(\mathfrak{a})\phi(\mathfrak{a})
, where

\phi(\mathfrak{a}):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\phi(\varpi_{\mathfrak{a}})&\textrm{if $\mathfrak{a}$ is coprime to $\mathfrak{f}(\phi)$},\\
0&\textrm{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

From the equation (
3.2
), the special value
L(\frac{k}{2},f\otimes\phi)
of the
L
-function of
f\otimes\phi
is given by

(3.10)

\Gamma_{F,k}\Big(\frac{k}{2}\Big)L\Big(\frac{k}{2},f\otimes\phi\Big)=\sum_{i=1}^{h_{F}}\sum_{u\in R_{\mathfrak{q}}}\frac{\phi(a_{i})\phi(\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}u)}{G(\phi)N(\mathfrak{a}_{i})^{\frac{k}{2}-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{k-1}\big(t_{i}(0;y)t(u)\big)\frac{dy}{y}.

4.
Modular symbols and Eichler-Shimura isomorphism

In this section, we will give the definition and the properties of modular symbols. Also we discuss the homology and cohomology groups of Bianchi modular manifolds. To associate the cusp forms and the cohomology classes, we introduce the generalized Eichler-Shimura isomorphism.

.

4.2.
Modular symbols of weight (2,2)

Let
\Gamma
be a torsion free congruence subgroup of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{F})
. For
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(F)
, let
\{\alpha,\beta\}
be the geodesic from
\alpha
to
\beta
in
\overline{\mathscr{H}_{3}}
. Let
\{\alpha,\beta\}_{\Gamma}
be the image of
\{\alpha,\beta\}
in
X_{\Gamma}
. Then we can consider
\{\alpha,\beta\}_{\Gamma}
to be an element of
H_{1}(X_{\Gamma},C_{\Gamma},\mathbb{Z})
. The classes
\{\alpha,\beta\}_{\Gamma}
are called
modular symbols
of level
\Gamma
and weight
(2,2)
. We have the following relations on the modular symbols:

(1)

\{\alpha,\alpha\}_{\Gamma}=0
in
H_{1}(X_{\Gamma},C_{\Gamma},\mathbb{Z})
for
\alpha\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(F)
.

(2)

\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\}_{\Gamma}=\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{3}\}_{\Gamma}+\{\alpha_{3},\alpha_{2}\}_{\Gamma}
for
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(F)
.

(3)

\{\alpha,\beta\}_{\Gamma}=\{\gamma\alpha,\gamma\beta\}_{\Gamma}
for
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(F)
and
\gamma\in\Gamma
.

By the definition of the relative homology group,
H_{1}(X_{\Gamma},C_{\Gamma},\mathbb{Z})
is generated by the symbols
\{\alpha,\beta\}_{\Gamma}
.
From the exact sequence (
4.1
), we have

\ker(\partial)=H_{1}(X_{\Gamma},\mathbb{Z})
, where
\partial
is the boundary map defined by

\partial:H_{1}(X_{\Gamma},C_{\Gamma},\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow H_{0}(C_{\Gamma},\mathbb{Z}),\ \{\alpha,\beta\}_{\Gamma}\mapsto\{\alpha\}_{\Gamma}-\{\beta\}_{\Gamma}.

Note that we have the following map:

\text{pr}_{\Gamma}:\Gamma\rightarrow H_{1}(X_{\Gamma},\mathbb{Z}),\ \gamma\mapsto\{0,\gamma\cdot 0\}_{\Gamma}.

Then
\text{pr}_{\Gamma}
is a surjective group homomorphism, which is trivial on the parabolic subgroups
\Gamma_{s}
of
\Gamma
.

4.3.
Special modular symbols

For each
1\leq i\leq h_{F}
, let us denote

\Gamma_{1}^{i}(\mathfrak{N}):=\operatorname{GL}_{2}(F)\cap t_{i}\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})U_{1}(\mathfrak{N})t_{i}^{-1},\ \overline{\Gamma_{1}^{i}(\mathfrak{N})}:=\operatorname{SL}_{2}(F)\cap\Gamma_{1}^{i}(\mathfrak{N}),

which are congruence subgroups of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{F})
and
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{F})
, respectively.
Since
[\mathbb{Z}:\mathfrak{N}\cap\mathbb{Z}]>3
, we observe that
\overline{\Gamma_{1}^{i}(\mathfrak{N})}
has no torsion element (see Urban
[
22
, Lemme 2.3.1]
). Therefore, for
\Gamma=\overline{\Gamma_{1}^{i}(\mathfrak{N})}
,
Y_{\Gamma}
is a manifold, not an orbifold.
Let us denote
Y_{1}^{i}(\mathfrak{N}):=Y_{\Gamma}
and
X_{1}^{i}(\mathfrak{N}):=X_{\Gamma}
for
\Gamma=\overline{\Gamma_{1}^{i}(\mathfrak{N})}
. Let us define the Bianchi modular manifold
Y_{1}(\mathfrak{N})
of level
U_{1}(\mathfrak{N})
by

Y_{1}(\mathfrak{N}):=\operatorname{GL}_{2}(F)\backslash\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})/\mathbb{C}^{\times}\operatorname{U}_{2}(\mathbb{C})U_{1}(\mathfrak{N}).

Let us recall that
\mathfrak{a}_{i}^{-1}
is coprime to
\mathfrak{N}
for each
i
. Then by the strong approximation theorem on
\operatorname{GL}(2)
, we obtain the following isomorphism:

Y_{1}(\mathfrak{N})\rightarrow\coprod_{i=1}^{h_{F}}Y_{1}^{i}(\mathfrak{N}),\ g=\gamma t_{j}g^{\prime}h\mapsto\overline{\Gamma_{1}^{j}(\mathfrak{N})}\frac{g^{\prime}}{\det(g^{\prime})}\operatorname{SU}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\in Y_{1}^{j}(\mathfrak{N}),

where
\gamma\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}(F)
,
g^{\prime}\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
,
h\in U_{1}(\mathfrak{N})
and
1\leq j\leq h_{F}
.
Note that

H_{1}(X_{1}(\mathfrak{N}),\mathbb{Z})\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{h_{F}}H_{1}(X_{1}^{i}(\mathfrak{N}),\mathbb{Z}).

Let
\mathfrak{q}
be an integral ideal of
F
. For
u\in R_{\mathfrak{q}}
and
i
, consider the following set:

\{t_{i}(0;y)t(u):y\in\mathbb{R}^{\times}_{>0}\}\subset\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F}).

Then we have the following proposition:

Proposition 4.2
.

Assume that
\mathfrak{q}
is coprime to
\mathfrak{a}_{i}^{-1}
. Then for each
u\in R_{\mathfrak{q}}
, there is an element
\alpha_{u}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&r_{u}\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(F)
and
c^{(i)}_{u}\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{F}
such that
(c^{(i)}_{u})_{\mathfrak{q}}=0
and

\alpha_{u}t_{i}gt(u)=t_{i}\alpha_{u,\infty}gt(c^{(i)}_{u})

in
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
for any
g\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
.

Proof.

Choose an element
r_{u}
of
(\mathfrak{q}^{-1}/\mathcal{O}_{F})^{\times}
corresponding to the element
-\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}u\in(\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{F}/\widehat{\mathfrak{q}})^{\times}

under the following well-defined bijection:

(\mathfrak{q}^{-1}/\mathcal{O}_{F})^{\times}\rightarrow(\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{F}/\widehat{\mathfrak{q}})^{\times},\ r+\mathcal{O}_{F}\mapsto\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}r_{\mathfrak{q}}+\widehat{\mathfrak{q}}.

Then we have the equation as follows:

\begin{pmatrix}1&r_{u}\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}t_{i}(0;y)t(u)=t_{i}\begin{pmatrix}1&r_{u}\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}_{\infty}(0;y)\begin{pmatrix}1&u+r_{u}^{(\infty)}a_{i}^{-1}\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

Note that
(u+r_{u}^{(\infty)}a_{i}^{-1})_{v}=0
for each
v|\mathfrak{q}
, and
(u+r_{u}^{(\infty)}a_{i}^{-1})_{v}\in\mathcal{O}_{v}
for each
v
coprime to
\mathfrak{q}
. So we conclude the proof.
∎

Remark 4.3

Note that we can observe that
\alpha_{u}
and

does not depend on

if

is coprime to
\mathfrak{a}_{i}^{-1}
.

Let
\mathfrak{l}

coprime to

for each

4.4.
Generalized Eichler-Shimura isomorphism

Let us follow Hida
[
5
]
to define the generalized Eichler-Shimura map. Let
S_{2}\big(\Gamma_{1}^{i}(\mathfrak{N})\big)
be the space of cusp forms on
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
of weight
(2,2)
and level
\Gamma_{1}^{i}(\mathfrak{N})
(for the definition, see Hida
[

, define a function

on

by

f^{\prime}(g,\mathbf{a}):=\begin{pmatrix}f_{0}(g)&f_{1}(g)&f_{2}(g)\end{pmatrix}\Psi\big({}^{t}j\big(g,(0,1)\big)\mathbf{a}\big),

where
\mathbf{a}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A\\

,
\Psi(\mathbf{a})={}^{t}\begin{pmatrix}A^{2}&AB&B^{2}\end{pmatrix}
and

j\bigg(\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix},(x,y)\bigg):=\begin{pmatrix}cx+d&-cy\\
\overline{c}y&\overline{cx+d}\end{pmatrix}

for

.
Let us define a differential

\delta^{(i)}(f)
on

by

\delta^{(i)}(f)\big((x,y)\big):=f^{\prime}\big((x;y),\mathbf{a}\big)|_{A^{2}=dx,\ AB=-dy,\ B^{2}=-d\overline{x}},

where

are the local coordinates on

. Note that we have following:

\overline{\Gamma_{1}^{i}(\mathfrak{N})}\backslash\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})/\operatorname{SU}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\cong Y_{1}^{i}(\mathfrak{N}),\ \overline{\Gamma_{1}^{i}(\mathfrak{N})}g\operatorname{SU}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\mapsto\overline{\Gamma_{1}^{i}(\mathfrak{N})}\big(g\cdot(0,1)\big).

Then we obtain the
generalized Eichler-Shimura isomorphism
:

Proposition 4.4
.

For
1\leq i\leq h_{F}
, the map
\delta^{(i)}
is a Hecke equivariant isomorphism of
\mathbb{C}
-vector spaces:

\delta^{(i)}:S_{2}\big(\Gamma_{1}^{i}(\mathfrak{N})\big)\rightarrow H^{1}_{\operatorname{par}}(Y_{1}^{i}(\mathfrak{N}),\mathbb{C})\cong H^{1}(Y_{1}^{i}(\mathfrak{N}),\mathbb{C}).

Proof.

By Hida
[
5
, Corollary 2.2, Proposition 3.1]
, we are done.
∎

Note that for
(x,y)\in Y_{1}^{i}(\mathfrak{N})
, we have

(4.7)

\delta^{(i)}(f)\big((x,y)\big)=f_{0}\big((x;y)\big)\frac{dx}{y}-f_{1}\big((x;y)\big)\frac{dy}{y}-f_{2}\big((x;y)\big)\frac{d\overline{x}}{y}.

and level

Then for a cusp form

(4.8)

\xi_{u}^{(i)}\cap\iota\big(\delta^{(i)}(f_{(i)})\big)=\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{1}\big(t_{i}(r_{u};y)\big)\frac{dy}{y}=\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{1}\big(t_{i}(0;y)t(u)\big)\frac{dy}{y}

5.
Lattice estimation

In this section, we estimate the number of the elements in the lattices of bounded norm. Also we find the elements of the smallest norm in certain lattices.

Let
n>0
be an integer and
\mathfrak{l}
a prime ideal of
F
. Then we have the following lemma:

Lemma 5.1
.

For each
\beta\in\mathcal{O}_{F}
, we have

\displaystyle\#\{\alpha\in\beta+\mathfrak{l}^{n}:\ N(\alpha)\leq x\}\ll_{F}\max\Big\{\frac{x}{N(\mathfrak{l})^{n}},1\Big\}.

Proof.

By Kwon-Sun
[
9
, Proposition 5.2]
, we can conclude the proof since the size of the unit group
\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}
is finite. Note that
[
9
, Proposition 5.2]
comes from Rohrlich
[
16
]
, which is about the coherent cone decomposition of lattices in the Minkowski space of
F
.
∎

For
\beta\in\mathcal{O}_{F}
, let
[\beta]_{n,\mathfrak{l}}
be the set consists of the elements
\alpha\in\beta+\mathfrak{l}^{n}
such that

N(\alpha)=\min_{\alpha_{1}\in\beta+\mathfrak{l}^{n}}N(\alpha_{1}).

We show that
[\beta]_{n,\mathfrak{l}}
is a singleton set for sufficiently large
N(\mathfrak{l})^{n}
:

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\beta\in\mathcal{O}_{F}
such that
4N(\beta)<N(\mathfrak{l})^{n}
. Then we have
[\beta]_{n,\mathfrak{l}}=\{\beta\}
.

Proof.

By the definition, the set
[\beta]_{n,\mathfrak{l}}
is non-empty. Thus we can choose an element
\beta_{1}\in[\beta]_{n,\mathfrak{l}}
. Let us assume that
\beta_{1}\neq\beta
. Note that we have
0\neq\beta_{1}-\beta\in\mathfrak{l}^{n}
and
N(\beta_{1})\leq N(\beta)
by the definition of
[\beta]_{n,\mathfrak{l}}
.
Then by the triangle inequality, we obtain

N(\mathfrak{l})^{n}\leq N(\beta-\beta_{1})\leq(N(\beta)^{\frac{1}{2}}+N(\beta_{1})^{\frac{1}{2}})^{2}\leq 4N(\beta)<N(\mathfrak{l})^{n},

which is a contradiction. Therefore,
[\beta]_{n,\mathfrak{l}}=\{\beta\}
, which concludes the proof.
∎

6.
Generation of homology groups

6.1.
Estimation of integrals along special modular symbols

Let
n>0
be an integer and
\mathfrak{l}
a prime ideal of
F
whose norm
N(\mathfrak{l})
is coprime to
\mathfrak{a}_{i}^{-1}\mathfrak{N}
for each
i
. Let us denote
\ell:=N(\mathfrak{l})
.
In this section, we prove that the module
M_{n,\mathfrak{l}}
defined in Section
4
is of full-rank in
H_{1}(X_{1}(\mathfrak{N}),\mathbb{Z})
for sufficiently large
\ell^{n}
. Before proving the generation result, we need the following lemma about the estimation on the Kloosterman-like exponential sum:

Lemma 6.1
.

Le
v>0
be an integer such that
v\leq\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
. For
b,c\in\mathcal{O}_{F,\mathfrak{l}}
with
v\leq n-\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-\min\{v_{\mathfrak{l}}(b),v_{\mathfrak{l}}(c)\}
, we have

(6.1)

\sum_{a\equiv a_{0}(\mathfrak{l}^{v})}\mathbf{e}_{F}\bigg(\frac{ab+a^{\prime}c}{\varpi_{\mathfrak{l}}^{n}}\bigg)\leq 2\ell^{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil+\min\{v_{\mathfrak{l}}(b),v_{\mathfrak{l}}(c)\}},

where
a^{\prime}
is the mod
\mathfrak{l}^{n}
inverse of
a
.

Proof.

Let us denote
\Gamma_{n}=1+\mathfrak{l}^{n}\mathcal{O}_{F,\mathfrak{l}}
,
Q(j)=a_{0}bj+a_{0}^{\prime}cj^{\prime}
,
h=\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
, and
w=\min\{v_{\mathfrak{l}}(b),v_{\mathfrak{l}}(c)\}
.
Let us set
d\in\mathcal{O}_{F,\mathfrak{l}}
and
r\in\Gamma_{v}
. As the map
a\mapsto\operatorname{\textbf{e}}_{F}\big(\frac{a}{\varpi_{\mathfrak{l}}^{n}}\big)
is an additive character on
\mathcal{O}_{F,\mathfrak{l}}
, we have

\displaystyle Q\big(r(1+\varpi_{\mathfrak{l}}^{h}d)\big)

\displaystyle\equiv a_{0}br+a_{0}br\varpi_{\mathfrak{l}}^{h}d+a_{0}^{\prime}cr^{\prime}\big(1-\varpi_{\mathfrak{l}}^{h}d+O(\varpi_{\mathfrak{l}}^{2h})\big)

\displaystyle\equiv Q(r)+\varpi_{\mathfrak{l}}^{h}d(a_{0}br-a_{0}^{\prime}cr^{\prime})\ (\text{mod }\mathfrak{l}^{n}).

Note that we have the following group isomorphism:

\displaystyle\begin{split}r(1+\varpi_{\mathfrak{l}}^{h}d)\in\Gamma_{v}/\Gamma_{n}\cong\Gamma_{v}/\Gamma_{h}\times\Gamma_{h}/\Gamma_{n}\cong\Gamma_{v}/\Gamma_{h}\times\mathcal{O}_{F,\mathfrak{l}}/\mathfrak{l}^{n-h}\mathcal{O}_{F,\mathfrak{l}}\ni(r,d).\end{split}

From the above equations, we can rewrite (
6.1
) as

(6.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{r\in\Gamma_{v}/\Gamma_{h}}\textbf{e}_{F}\bigg(\frac{Q(r)}{\varpi_{\mathfrak{l}}^{n}}\bigg)\sum_{d\ \text{mod }\mathfrak{l}^{n-h}}\textbf{e}_{F}\bigg(\frac{(a_{0}br-a_{0}^{\prime}cr^{\prime})d}{\varpi_{\mathfrak{l}}^{n-h}}\bigg).\end{split}

By the orthogonality of characters of finite group,
the above equation becomes

(6.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\ell^{n-h}\sum_{a_{0}br\equiv a_{0}^{\prime}cr^{\prime}\ (\text{mod }\mathfrak{l}^{n-h})}\textbf{e}_{F}\bigg(\frac{Q(r)}{\varpi_{\mathfrak{l}}^{n}}\bigg),\end{split}

where

6.2.

From the estimation in the previous subsection, we can prove the full-rankness of

Lemma 6.4

Let

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:X\times Y\rightarrow\mathbb{C}

For any sequence of proper subspaces

of

and a sequence

in

Let us choose

M_{n,\mathfrak{p}}
are well defined. Also we have
\xi^{(i)}_{n,\mathfrak{p}}(\beta)=\{\infty,\beta\alpha^{-n}\}_{\overline{\Gamma^{i}_{1}(\mathfrak{N})}}
and
\alpha^{n}=\alpha_{n}^{n}+BCN
for some
C\in\mathfrak{C}
and
N\in\mathfrak{N}
.

Consider the action of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(F)
on

, then
\gamma_{c}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
c&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}(F)
and
\gamma_{c,\infty}\in\Gamma_{1}^{i}(\mathfrak{N})
. For

and

, we have

t_{i}(b;y)=\gamma_{c}t_{i}(b;y)=t_{i}\gamma_{c,\infty}(b;y)\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
a_{i}c^{(\infty)}&1\end{pmatrix}=t_{i}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
c&1\end{pmatrix}_{\infty}(b;y)

in
Y_{1}(\mathfrak{N})
. Note that
\{\infty,b\}_{\Gamma_{1}^{i}(\mathfrak{N})}
is represented by the set
\{t_{i}(b;y):y\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\times}\}
. Also note that the action of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
on
Y_{1}^{i}(\mathfrak{N})
preserve the metric, and

\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
c&1\end{pmatrix}_{\infty}(b;y)\mapsto\bigg(\frac{\overline{c}y^{2}+b\overline{(bc+1)}}{|c|^{2}y^{2}+|bc+1|^{2}},\frac{y}{|c|^{2}y^{2}+|bc+1|^{2}}\bigg)

under the bijection
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})/\operatorname{SU}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\rightarrow\mathscr{H}_{3}
.
Thus we have

\{\infty,b\}_{\overline{\Gamma^{i}_{1}(\mathfrak{N})}}=\Big\{\frac{1}{c},\frac{b}{bc+1}\Big\}_{\overline{\Gamma^{i}_{1}(\mathfrak{N})}}=\gamma_{c}\{\infty,0\}_{\overline{\Gamma^{i}_{1}(\mathfrak{N})}}+\{0,\infty\}_{\overline{\Gamma^{i}_{1}(\mathfrak{N})}}+\Big\{\infty,\frac{b}{bc+1}\Big\}_{\overline{\Gamma^{i}_{1}(\mathfrak{N})}}.

Let us set
b=\beta\alpha_{n}^{-n}\in F^{\times}
and
c=\beta^{-1}BCN\in\mathfrak{N}\prod_{i=1}\mathfrak{a}_{i}^{-1}
. Note that
\gamma_{c}\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}(F)
and
\gamma_{c,\infty}\in\bigcap_{i=1}^{h_{F}}\Gamma_{1}^{i}(\mathfrak{N})
. Then by the above equations, we obtain

\xi=\xi_{n,\mathfrak{p}_{n}}^{(i)}(\beta)=\{\infty,b\}_{\overline{\Gamma^{i}_{1}(\mathfrak{N})}}=\{\infty,\beta\alpha^{-n}\}_{\overline{\Gamma^{i}_{1}(\mathfrak{N})}}=\xi_{n,\mathfrak{p}}^{(i)}(\beta),

which is a generator of
M_{n,\mathfrak{p}}
.
Thus
M_{n,\mathfrak{p}_{n}}
is a submodule of
M_{n,\mathfrak{p}}
, which finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 6.7

Let us follow the notations in Theorem

Let us define a positive integer as follows:

Let

Proposition 6.8
.

Let us follow the notations in Theorem

and Remark

\mathfrak{p}
be an

Proof.

For each open subset

, let us denote

Lemma 7.2

Let

T_{\mathfrak{p}}\eta_{f,i}=a_{f}(\mathfrak{p})\eta_{f,j},\ T_{\mathfrak{p}}\eta_{f,\operatorname{c},i}=a_{f}(\mathfrak{p})\eta_{f,\operatorname{c},j}.

By the definition of

\mathcal{L}_{f}(\phi)=0

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h_{F}}\sum_{\psi\in H_{F}}\mathcal{L}_{f}(\psi\phi)=\sum_{i=1}^{h_{F}}\bigg(\frac{1}{h_{F}}\sum_{\psi\in H_{F}}\psi(a_{i})\bigg)\sum_{u\in R_{\mathfrak{f}(\phi)}}\phi(a_{i})\phi(\varpi_{\mathfrak{f}(\phi)}u)\xi_{u}^{(i)}\cap\eta_{f,{\operatorname{c}},i}

9.
Density of ordinary primes

Let
\ell
be a rational prime. By using the result of Serre
[
18
]
and the prime number theorem, the set of ordinary rational primes has density one for any classical elliptic modular forms with no CM. In this section, we prove the similar result.

Let
f\in S_{2}\big(U_{0}(\mathfrak{N})\big)=S_{2}(\mathfrak{N},\mathbf{1})
be a normalized Hecke eigenform, which is neither CM nor the lifting of classical elliptic modular newform. For simplicity, we call such
f

Genuine newform
. Let us denote by
\mathbb{Q}_{f}
the coefficient field of
f
By Friedberg-Hoffstein
[
3
, Theorem A, Theorem B]
and Taylor
[
21
, Theorem 2, Corollary 4]
, we obtain a continuous irreducible representation

\rho_{f,\ell}:G_{F}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})

such that if
v
is an unramified place of
F
which does not divide
\mathfrak{N}\ell
, then

(1)

\rho_{f,\ell}
is unramified at
v
,

(2)

Im
(\rho_{f,\ell}\oplus\rho^{\operatorname{c}}_{f,\ell})
is dense in
\{(a,b)\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}^{2}:\det(a)=\det(b)\}
, and

(3)

either

\text{char}\big(\rho_{f,\ell}(\text{Frob}_{v})\big)(X)=X^{2}-a_{f}(v)X+N(v)

or
a_{f}(v^{\operatorname{c}})=0
and

\text{char}\big(\rho_{f,\ell}(\text{Frob}_{v})\big)(X)=X^{2}+N(v),

where the first possibility occurs outside a set
\{v\}_{v}
of density zero.

From this, we have the following proposition:

Proposition 9.1
.

For
x>0
, the number of places
v
of
F
such that
\operatorname{Tr}\big(\rho_{f,\ell}(\operatorname{Frob}_{v})\big)=0
and
N(v)<x
is given by

O\bigg(\frac{x}{\operatorname{log}(x)^{\frac{3}{2}-\varepsilon}}\bigg)

for any
\varepsilon>0
.

Proof.

Let
G:=\text{Im}(\rho_{f,\ell})/\big(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}^{\times}\cap\text{Im}(\rho_{f,\ell})\big)
,
C
the image of the set
\{s\in\text{Im}(\rho_{f,\ell}):\text{Tr}(s)=0\}
in
G
. Note that Im
(\rho_{f,\ell})
is dense in
\operatorname{GL}_{2}
, thus
\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}(G)=3
and
\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}(C)\leq 2
. By the argument of Serre
[
18
, Théorème 15]
, we have

\inf_{s\in C}\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}(\text{Ad}(s)\rm{Id})=2,

where
Ad
is the adjoint representation of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
induced from
\rho_{f,\ell}
. Applying
[
18
, Théorème 12]
for a pair
(G,C)
, we obtain the desired estimation.
∎

From this, we obtain the result toward Conjecture
8.3
:

Theorem 9.2
.

If
\mathbb{Q}_{f}=\mathbb{Q}
, then
f
is
\mathfrak{l}
-ordinary for a set
\{\mathfrak{l}\}_{\mathfrak{l}}
of prime ideals of
F
of density one.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{l}
be a non-ordinary prime ideal of
F
lying above a rational prime
\ell
such that
N(\mathfrak{l})>2^{\frac{64}{25}}
. Then we obtain
a_{f}(\mathfrak{l})\in N(\mathfrak{l})\mathbb{Z}
. Assume that
a_{f}(\mathfrak{l})\neq 0
, then we have

N(\mathfrak{l})\leq|a_{f}(\mathfrak{l})|\leq 2N(\mathfrak{l})^{\frac{39}{64}}

by Nakasuji
[
13
, Corollary 1.1]
, which is a contradiction. Therefore, we have
a_{f}(\mathfrak{l})=0
if
\mathfrak{l}
is non-ordinary and
N(\mathfrak{l})>2^{\frac{64}{25}}
. The converse direction is clear. Therefore, if

is sufficiently large, then

is zero if and only if
\mathfrak{l}
is non-ordinary.

On the other hand, by the prime number theorem and Proposition

, the density of prime ideals
\mathfrak{l}
of

such that
\text{Tr}\big(\rho_{f,\ell}(\text{Frob}_{\mathfrak{l}})\big)=0

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\operatorname{log}(x)^{\varepsilon-\frac{1}{2}}=0.

Note that the equation
\text{Tr}\big(\rho_{f,\ell}(\text{Frob}_{\mathfrak{l}})\big)=a_{f}(\mathfrak{l})
holds for a set of prime ideals
\{\mathfrak{l}\}_{\mathfrak{l}}
of density one. Hence we obtain the desired result.
∎

Admissible ideals for 𝕜 \Bbbk -linear categories
Karin M. Jacobsen and Job D. Rock

Abstract
. We generalize the notion of an admissible ideal from path algebras to (small) 𝕜 \Bbbk -linear categories that satisfy the Krull–Remak–Schmidt–Azumaya assumption. In our treatment we first prove some general results that are analogous to general results for path algebras and admissible ideals. We then cover generalizations of relations generated by paths of length two, which we call point relations, and more general length relations. We conclude the paper with several examples and an appendix containing further discussion on length relations.



1.
Introduction

Quivers with relations play a fundamental role in the representation theory of finite dimensional algebras.

Every basic finite dimensional algebra
\Lambda
over an algebraically closed field
\Bbbk
is isomorphic to a path algebra
\Bbbk Q/R
of a finite quiver
Q
with admissible relations
R
. Moreover, the modules of
\Lambda
are in bijection with the representations of
(Q,I)
over
\Bbbk
. Without relations, we get a correspondence only to hereditary algebras
[
G
]
; see also
[
ARS
, Ch. III.1]
.

Non-hereditary algebras are central in most fields of modern representation theory of algebras. For one, higher homological algebra requires algebras of global dimension at least 2
[
J
,
Kv
]
. There is a rich tradition of studying classes of non-hereditary algebras, such as gentle
[
AH
,
AS
]
, clannish
[
C-B
]
, Schur
[
E
]
, preprojective
[
R
]
, and self–injective
[
SY
]
algebras.

Continuous quivers and their representations were first explicitly studied in
[
IRT
]
. They are a natural generalisation of quivers, replacing finite sets of vertices with uncountably infinite sets. In the process, one gains intuition about what characteristics of representation theory come from innate properties of algebraic structures, and what comes from the discrete examples that are usually studied.

One parameter persistence modules are often defined over the real line so that persistence modules coincide with pointwise finite-dimensional representations of a continuous quiver of type
\mathbb{A}
(see, for example,
[
CdSGO
]
).
In
[
BBOS
]
the authors consider
m\times n
rectangular grid quivers which have the commutativity relation on each square.
The authors of
[
BBH
]
study homological approximations in order to obtain new invariants of these representations (persistence modules).

Given the important role of quiver relations in the representation theory of finite-dimensional algebra, it is natural to ask if relations can be extended to the continuous setting. This has already been done in a restricted sense by the second author and Zhu in
[
RZ
]
. We give a more general definition that works with any underlying quiver. To capture the full generality we actually go beyond quivers and consider categories instead.

In starting this work, we were motivated by two areas of study that we intend to lift to the continuous setting: gentle algebras and
d
-cluster-tilting subcategories. In gentle algebras, the relations appear in the definition, and are always generated by compositions of two arrows. This type of relations are generalized as
point relations
in
Section

3.1
. An important class of
d
-cluster-tilting subcategories appear in the module category of type A algebras, with relations consisting of all paths above a certain length
[
V
]
. This type of relations is generalized as
length relations
in
Section

3.2
.

2.1.
\Bbbk
-linear categorization

Definition 2.1
.

Let
Q
be a (finite) quiver and
\Bbbk Q
its path algebra.
Let

be the category whose indecomposable objects are the vertices of

\Hom_{\mathcal{Q}}(i,j)=e_{j}\Bbbk Qe_{i}.

The objects in
\mathcal{Q}
are finite direct sums of the indecomposables (and 0).
The morphisms in
\mathcal{Q}
are given by extending bilinearly.
We call
\mathcal{Q}
the
\Bbbk
-linear categorification
of

.

Example 2.2
.

Let

be the following quiver:

1
2
3
4
\alpha_{1}
\beta_{1}
\alpha_{2}
\beta_{2}

Then the
\Bbbk
-linear categorification

is a category with indecomposable objects

and
4
. The morphisms in
\mathcal{Q}
are given by paths in

, so for example we have
\Hom(1,4)\cong\Bbbk^{2}
, while
\Hom(4,1)=0

Proposition 2.3
.

There is a bijection between nonzero elements in
\Bbbk Q
and nonzero morphisms in
\mathcal{Q}
.

Proof.

A non-zero element in
\Bbbk Q
is a finite sum of paths in
Q
. We can therefore define a map
F
from the elements in
\Bbbk Q
to morphisms in
\mathcal{Q}
by specifying the action of
F
on paths in
Q
. We let this mapping be determined by
\Hom_{\mathcal{Q}}(i,j)=e_{j}\Bbbk Qe_{i}
. This map is a bijection by bilinearity of
\mathcal{Q}
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mathsf{Mod}(\mathcal{Q})
be the category of functors
\mathcal{Q}\to\mathsf{Vec}(\Bbbk)
.
Then there exists an isomorphism of categories
\Phi:\mathsf{Mod}(\mathcal{Q})\to\mathsf{Rep}(Q)
.

Proof.

Let
F
be a functor in
\mathsf{Mod}(\mathcal{Q})
.
We now define the corresponding representation
V=\Phi(F)
.
Let
M
be the representation of
Q
over
\Bbbk
where
V(i)=F(i)
for each
i\in Q_{0}
.
For a path
\rho
in
Q
, let
V(\rho)
be the
\Bbbk
-linear map

.

Let

be a morphism in
\mathsf{Mod}(\mathcal{Q})
.
Then
\Phi(f):\Phi(F)\to\Phi(G)
is defined by the
f_{i}:F(i)\to G(i)
for each

.
Straightforward computations show that

respects composition and so it is a functor.

Define

in the following way.
For a representation

of

be determined by

, for each

, and
F(\rho)=V(\rho)
for each path in

.
Morphisms are defined similarly.
One may check
\Phi^{-1}\Phi
and
\Phi\Phi^{-1}
are the identity functors on
\mathsf{Mod}(\mathcal{Q})
and
\mathsf{Rep}(Q)
, respectively.
∎

2.2.
The Jacobson Radical

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a category.
The
radical

\mathsf{Rad}(\mathcal{C})
of
\mathcal{C}
is the ideal consisting of

\mathsf{Rad}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y):=\left\{f\in\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)\mid\forall g\in\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(Y,X),\,f\circ g\in\mathsf{J}(\End_{\mathcal{C}}(Y))\right\},

for each pair of objects
X,Y
in
\mathcal{C}
.

Proposition 2.6

(
[
Kr
]
)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be objects in
\mathcal{C}
.
Then
\mathsf{Rad}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)=\mathsf{J}(\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y))
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be an morphism for indecomposable objects
X,Y
in
\mathcal{C}
.
Then
f\in\mathsf{Rad}(\mathcal{C})
if and only if
f
is not an isomorphism.

Proof.

Suppose
f
is not an isomorphism.
If
\mathcal{C}
does not have cycles we are done.
If
\mathcal{C}
has cycles, let
g:Y\to X
be a nonzero morphism.
Then
f\circ g\in\mathsf{J}(\End_{\mathcal{C}}(Y))
and so
f\in\mathsf{Rad}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)
.
Reversing the argument shows that if
f\in\mathsf{Rad}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)
then
f
is not an isomorphism.
∎

Recall that
\mathcal{C}
is semi-simple if every object in
\mathcal{C}
is a finite direct sum of simple objects and all such direct sums exist.

Proposition 2.8
.

If
\mathcal{C}
is Krull–Remak–Schmidt, then
\mathcal{C}/\mathsf{Rad}(\mathcal{C})
is semi-simple.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a Krull–Remak–Schmidt category.
Let
X
and
Y
be indecomposables in
\mathcal{C}
such that
X\not\cong Y
.
Then
\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)=\mathsf{Rad}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)
and so
\Hom_{\mathcal{C}/\mathsf{Rad}(\mathcal{C})}(X,Y)=0
.
Extending bilinearly we see
\mathcal{C}/\mathsf{Rad}(\mathcal{C})
is semi-simple.
∎

Remark 2.9
.

It follows immediately from
Proposition

2.8
that if
Q
is a finite acyclic quiver then
\mathcal{Q}/\mathsf{Rad}(\mathcal{Q})
is semi-simple.

Definition 2.10

(

)

Let

We say

is the trivial one.

An

such that for any

and for any

and

gfh
is defined, the composition
gfh\in\mathcal{I}
.
For an ideal

of
\mathcal{C}
, we denote by

.

Remark 2.11
.

For an ideal
\mathcal{I}
of
\mathcal{C}
, the category
\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{I}
has the same objects as

.
The morphisms of

are given by

A representation

is also a representation of

by precomposition with the quotient functor

\widetilde{V}:\mathcal{C}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\to}}\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{I}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle V}}{{\to}}\Bbbk\text{vec}

Hence we may consider the representations of

as a subcategory of the representations of
\mathcal{C}
.
In particular, representations of
\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{I}
are those representations

\mathcal{C}
such that if

then

Definition 2.12

Let

We say

(1)

(2)

We remark that, in

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a Krull–Remak–Schmidt category and
\mathcal{I}
an ideal in
\mathcal{C}
.
If
\mathcal{I}
is admissible, then
\mathcal{I}
is contained in the radical of
\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

Let

be a morphism in
\mathcal{I}
between indecomposable objects. Then we know by

that if

is not contained in the radical, it is an isomorphism. However, if

is an isomorphism, we have

.
Then
every

is in
\mathcal{I}
.
Thus, if

for any composition, both

, which contradicts condition

2.12
(1).
Hence

for indecomposable

.

Now let

and
Y=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{n}Y_{j}
, where each
X_{i}
and

is indecomposable. Consider

. We can rewrite

as

, where

Suppose

We see

Proposition 2.14
.

Let

be a finite quiver and

an ideal of
\Bbbk Q
as a path algebra.
Let
\mathcal{Q}
be the

Let

Now, let

2.4.
General Results

Proposition 2.17
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be connected, and let
\mathcal{I}
be an admissible ideal of morphisms. Then
\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{I}
is connected.

Proof.

Assume towards a contradiction that
\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{I}
is not connected; then there exists a decomposition of
\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{I}
into mutually orthogonal subcategories
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{n}
. We can lift these subcategories to subcategories
\mathcal{C}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{C}_{n}
of
\mathcal{C}
. As
\mathcal{C}
is connected, these subcategories cannot all be mutually orthogonal, so assume that there exists some morphism
f:X_{i}\rightarrow X_{j}
, with
X_{i}\in\mathcal{C}_{i},X_{j}\in\mathcal{C}_{j}
and
i\neq j
. To preserve mutual orthogonality in
\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{I}
, we must have
f\in\mathcal{I}
. Then since
I
is an admissible ideal, we can write
f=g_{m}\circ\cdots\circ g_{1}
, where each of the
g_{1},\cdots,g_{m}
are not in
\mathcal{I}
.

Consider
g_{1}:X_{i}\rightarrow Y
and denote its image in
\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{I}
by
\overline{g_{1}}
. As
\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{I}
is not connected, we can write
Y=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}
, with
Y_{i}\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{i}
and
\overline{g_{1}}=(g^{1}_{1},\cdots g_{1}^{n})
. Now, as
\overline{g_{1}}
is nonzero,
g_{1}^{k}
is nonzero for some
k
. If
k\neq i
, we have reached a contradiction. If
k=i
, we can repeat the argument with
\overline{g_{2}}|_{Y_{i}}
, eventually reaching a contradiction.
∎

Proposition 2.18
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be Krull–Remak–Schmidt–Azumaya and
\mathcal{I}
an admissible ideal.
Then
\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{I}
is Krull–Remak–Schmidt–Azumaya.

Proof.

Let
X
be an object in
\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{I}
. Then
X
is an object in
\mathcal{C}
, which we assume to be Krull–Remak–Schmidt–Azumaya. It follows that
X=\bigoplus_{\alpha}X_{\alpha}
, where
\End_{\mathcal{C}}(X_{\alpha})
is local. If we can show that
\End_{\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{I}}(X_{\alpha})
is local, we are done.

We know that
\End_{\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{I}}(X_{\alpha})=\End_{\mathcal{C}}(X_{\alpha})/\mathcal{I}(X_{\alpha},X_{\alpha})
. By property 1 of
Definition

2.12
, the identity on
X_{\alpha}
cannot be an element of
\mathcal{I}(X_{\alpha},X_{\alpha})
, so
\End_{\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{I}}(X_{\alpha})
is a nonzero quotient ring of a local ring, which is local by the ideal correspondence theorem for quotient rings.
∎

Proposition 2.19
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a category such that all endomorphism rings are artinian and
\mathcal{I}
an admissible ideal.
Then

\mathsf{Rad}(\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{I})=\mathsf{Rad}(\mathcal{C})/\mathcal{I}.

Proof.

The equation
\mathsf{Rad}(\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{I})=\mathsf{Rad}(\mathcal{C})/\mathcal{I}
holds if and only if
\mathsf{Rad}_{\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{I}}(X,Y)=\mathsf{Rad}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)/\mathcal{I}
holds for all pairs of objects
X,Y\in\mathcal{C}
.

First note that if
\End_{\mathcal{C}}(Y)
is artinian, then

\displaystyle\mathsf{Rad}_{\mathcal{C}}(Y,Y)/\mathcal{I}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{J}(\End_{\mathcal{C}}(Y))/\mathcal{I}=\mathsf{J}(\End_{\mathcal{C}}(Y)+\mathcal{I})/\mathcal{I}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{J}(\End_{\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{I}}(Y))=\mathsf{Rad}_{\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{I}}(Y,Y).

Now we can see that

\displaystyle f\in\mathsf{Rad}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)/\mathcal{I}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow f=f^{\prime}+\mathcal{I}(X,Y)\text{, with }f^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Rad}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow f=f^{\prime}+\mathcal{I}(X,Y)\text{, s.t.\ }f^{\prime}\circ g^{\prime}\in\mathsf{J}(\End_{\mathcal{C}}(Y))\,\forall\,g^{\prime}\in\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(Y,X)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow f\circ(g^{\prime}+\mathcal{I}(Y,X))\in\mathsf{J}(\End_{\mathcal{C}}(Y))/\mathcal{I}\,\forall\,g^{\prime}\in\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(Y,X)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow f\circ g\in\mathsf{J}(\End_{\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{I}}(Y))\,\forall\,g\in\Hom_{\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{I}}(Y,X)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow f\in\mathsf{Rad}_{\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{I}}(X,Y).

∎

In particular, if
\mathcal{C}
is
\Bbbk
-linear and Krull–Remak–Schmidt, then all endomorphism rings are artinian and the above Proposition holds.

The following lemma is useful for our examples in
Section

4
.

Lemma 2.20
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a small,
\Bbbk
-linear, Krull–Remak–Schmidt–Azumaya category and
\mathcal{I}
an admissible ideal in
\mathcal{C}
.
Let
\mathcal{J}
be an admissible ideal in
\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{I}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}
the set of morphisms
f
in
\mathcal{C}
such that
f+\mathcal{I}\in\mathcal{J}
in
\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{I}
. Then the following hold:

(1)

\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}
contains
\mathcal{I}
.

(2)

\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}
is an ideal.

(3)

\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}
is admissible in
\mathcal{C}
.

(4)

(\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{I})/\mathcal{J}\simeq\mathcal{C}/\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}
.

Proof.

1.
Let
f\in\mathcal{I}
.
Then
f\mapsto 0
in
\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{I}
.
Since all zero morphisms in
\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{I}
are in
\mathcal{J}
, we see
f\in\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}
.

2.
Let
f:X\to Y
be nonzero in
\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}
and let
g:Y\to Z
be nonzero in
\mathcal{C}
.
Then
f+\mathcal{I}
and
g+\mathcal{I}
are in
\mathsf{Mor}(\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{I})
.
So,
(g+\mathcal{I})\circ(f+\mathcal{I})
is in
\mathcal{J}
and is equal to
gf+\mathcal{I}
.
Then
gf\in\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}
.

3.
Let
f\in\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}
.
Then
f+\mathcal{I}\in\mathcal{J}
and, by assumption, there exists
(g_{1}+\mathcal{I}),\ldots,(g_{n}+\mathcal{I})
in
\mathsf{Mor}(\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{I})\setminus\mathcal{J}
such that

f+\mathcal{I}=(g_{n}+\mathcal{I})\circ(g_{n-1}+\mathcal{I})\circ\cdots\circ(g_{2}+\mathcal{I})\circ(g_{1}+\mathcal{I}).

Then for each
g_{i}
there is
h_{i}\in\mathcal{I}
such that
g_{i}+h_{i}\mapsto g_{i}+\mathcal{I}
and

f=(g_{n}+h_{n})\circ(g_{n-1}+h_{n-1})\circ\cdots\circ(g_{2}+h_{2})\circ(g_{1}+h_{1}).

Since each
g_{i}\notin\mathcal{J}
, we know each
g_{i}+h_{i}\notin\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}
and so
f
is a finite composition of morphisms not in
\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}
.
Additionally, for any nonzero, nonisomorphism endomorphism
f
, we have
f^{n}\in\mathcal{I}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then
f^{n}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}
by statement 1.
Therefore,
\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}
is admissible.

4.
Recall
\mathsf{Ob}((\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{I})/\mathcal{J})=\mathsf{Ob}(\mathcal{C}/\widetilde{\mathcal{J}})
.
We now produce a bijection between
\mathsf{Mor}((\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{I})/\mathcal{J})
and
\mathsf{Mor}(\mathcal{C}/\widetilde{\mathcal{J}})
by producing bijections

\phi_{X,Y}:\Hom_{\mathcal{C}/\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}}(X,Y)\to\Hom_{(\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{I})/\mathcal{J}}(X,Y)

for each ordered pair
X,Y
of objects.

Let
f+\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}\in\Hom_{\mathcal{C}/\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}}(X,Y)
.
Then there exists
g\in\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}\subset\mathsf{Mor}(\mathcal{C})
such that
f+g\mapsto f+\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}\in\mathsf{Mor}(\mathcal{C}/\widetilde{\mathcal{J}})
.
If
g\in\mathcal{I}
then
f+g\mapsto f+\mathcal{I}\in\mathsf{Mor}(\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{I})
; otherewise
f+g\mapsto f+g+\mathcal{I}\in\mathsf{Mor}(\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{I})
.
In either case,
f+g\mapsto f+\mathcal{J}
in
\Hom_{(\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{I})/\mathsf{J}}(X,Y)
.
We define
\phi_{X,Y}(f+\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}):=f+\mathcal{J}
.

It is immediate that
\phi_{X,Y}
is injective.
Suppose
f+\mathcal{J}\in\Hom_{(\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{I})/\mathcal{J}}
.
Then there exists
g+\mathcal{I}
in
\Hom_{\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{I}}(X,Y)
such that
f+g+\mathcal{I}\mapsto f+\mathcal{J}
.
Then there exists
h\in\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)
such that
f+g+h\mapsto f+g+\mathcal{I}
.
But this means
g+h\in\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}
and so
f+(g+h)\mapsto f+\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}
in
\Hom_{\mathcal{C}/\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}}(X,Y)
.
Thus,
\phi_{X,Y}
is surjective.
∎

3.
Relations

In this section we look at two types of admissible ideals: those generated by point relations (
Section

3.1
) and those generated by length relations (
Section

3.2
).
These generalize a relation generated by a single path of length two and relations generated by all paths of a particular length, respectively.

3.1.
Point Relations

Here we generalize relations generated by a single path of length 2 to a point relation (
Definition

3.3
) and prove this generates an admissible ideal (
Theorem

3.5
).
We then give examples that point to a continuous version of a gentle algebra (
Examples

3.6
and
3.7
).

Definition 3.1
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a nonisomorphism between indecomposables in
\mathcal{C}
.
A
decomposition point

Z
in
\mathcal{C}
is an indecomposable object such that there exists nonisomorphisms
g:X\to Z
and
h:Z\to Y
where
f=h\circ g
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a nonisomorphism of indecomposables in
\mathcal{C}
,
Z
a decomposition point of
f
, and
f=g\circ h
such a decomposition.
We call
f
an
acyclic morphism
if for all pairs
g^{\prime}:X\to Z
and
h^{\prime}:Z\to Y
such that
f=h^{\prime}\circ g^{\prime}
then
h^{\prime}
and
g^{\prime}
are scalar multiples of
h
and
g
, respectively.

Note that an acyclic morphism cannot be irreducible. (I.e., it must be a path of length at least 2 in a quiver.)

Definition 3.3
.

Let
f
be an acyclic morphism and
Z
a decomposition point of
f
.
Let
P
be the set of all nonisomorphisms
g
between indecomposables satisfying the following.

•

There exists
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
morphisms of indecomposables such that
f=h_{2}\circ g\circ h_{1}
.

•

We have
Z
as a decomposition point of
g
.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{f,Z}
be the ideal in
\mathcal{C}
generated by
P
.
We call
\mathcal{P}_{f,Z}
the
point relation through
Z
by
f
.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\{\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}\}
be a collection of point relations in
\mathcal{C}
.
We say
\{\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}\}
is
admissible
if each morphism of indecomposables appears in at most finitely-many
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\{\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}\}
be an admissible collection of point relations in
\mathcal{C}
and let
\mathcal{I}=\langle\bigcup_{\alpha}P_{\alpha}\rangle
.
Suppose also that for each indecomposable
X
in
\mathcal{C}
, we have
\End_{\mathcal{C}}(X)/\mathcal{I}(X,X)
is finite dimensional.
Then
\mathcal{I}
is an admissible ideal.

Proof.

We satisfy
Definition

2.12
(2) by assumption.

Now suppose
f\in\mathcal{I}
; we show that
f
can be written as a finite composition of morphisms not in
\mathcal{I}
.
Since
f
is a finite sum of morphisms of indecomposables, we assume without loss of generality
f
is a morphism of indecomposables.
Then, by assumption there are at most finitely-many
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}
such that
f\in\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}
.

We proceed by induction beginning with
f
is only in
\mathcal{P}_{1}
.
Let
f_{1}
,
P_{1}
, and
Z_{1}
be as in
Definition

3.3
.
Then there exists morphisms
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
in
Mor(\mathcal{C})
and
g\in P_{1}
such that
f=h_{2}\circ g\circ h_{1}
.
Further,
g=h^{\prime}_{2}\circ h^{\prime}_{1}
where the target of
h^{\prime}_{1}
is
Z_{\alpha}
and the source of
h^{\prime}_{2}
is
Z_{\alpha}
.
So, let
g_{1}=h^{\prime}_{1}\circ h_{1}
and let
g_{2}=h_{2}\circ h^{\prime}_{2}
.
Note that neither
g_{1}
nor
g_{2}
is in
\mathcal{P}_{1}
.
Further, neither
g_{1}
nor
g_{2}
is in
\mathcal{I}
or else
f
would be in another
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}
as well.
Thus, we have our desired decomposition.

Now assume that if
f
is in
n
of the
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}
, then
f
is a finite composition of morphisms not in
\mathcal{I}
.
Suppose
f
is in
n+1
of the
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}
and denote one of them by
\mathcal{P}_{1}
.
Let
f_{1}
,
P_{1}
, and
Z_{1}
be as before for
\mathcal{P}_{1}
.
We find
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
as before, but they may be in
\mathcal{I}
.
However, each
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
may only be in
n
or fewer
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}
and so are a finite composition of morphisms not in
\mathcal{I}
.
Therefore,
f
is a finite composition of morphisms not in
\mathcal{I}
.
∎

In

Recall a commutative monoid
\Lambda
is a set with an associative, commutative, binary operation
+_{\Lambda}:\Lambda\times\Lambda\to\Lambda
and an identity 0.

Definition 3.9
.

Let
\Lambda
be a commutative monoid.
We say
\Lambda
is
weakly Archimedian
if it satisfies the following.

•

There is a total order
\leq
on
\Lambda
.

•

If
\lambda\neq 0
then
\lambda>0
.

•

If
\lambda_{1}>\lambda_{2}
then, for any
\lambda_{3}
, we have
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{3}>\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}
or
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{3}=\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}=\max\Lambda
.

•

For all
0<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}
in
\Lambda
, there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

n\lambda_{1}:=\underbrace{\lambda_{1}+_{\Lambda}\lambda_{1}+_{\Lambda}\cdots+_{\Lambda}\lambda_{1}}_{n}\geq\lambda_{2}.

Example 3.10
.

We give three examples, two of which the reader might expect.

(1)

The set
\mathbb{N}
with the usual total order and
+_{\mathbb{N}}
given in the usual way is weakly Archimedian.

(2)

The set
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
with the usual total order and
+_{\mathbb{R}}
given in the usual way is weakly Archimedian.

(3)

Let
\Lambda=\{0,1,2,\ldots,n-1,n,\infty\}
. Let
+_{\Lambda}
be given by

\lambda_{1}+_{\Lambda}\lambda_{2}=\begin{cases}\lambda_{1}+_{\mathbb{N}}\lambda_{2}&(\lambda_{1}+_{\mathbb{N}}\lambda_{2})\leq n\\
\infty&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

For the total order, we say
0<1<2<\cdots<n-1<n<\infty
.
Then
\Lambda
is weakly Archimedian.

When the weakly Archimedian monoid

is clear, we write

instead of

.

Definition 3.11
.

Let
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
be a small category and ignore any additional structure.
The

-linearization

.

\mathcal{C}

\Bbbk
-linear category.

.

.

Let
\mathcal{C}

We now consider a continuous version of the grid quiver with commutativity relations as examined in

Let

be the category whose objects are points in

.

We now define the
\Hom

(x,y)
and
(z,w)

Hom sets are given by considering paths made of up of horizontal and vertical line segments.
For a pair
(x,y)
and
(z,w)
, consider the set
P_{x,y}^{z,w}
of all finite sequences
\{(x_{i},y_{i})\}_{i=i}^{n}
such that

•

(x_{1},y_{1})=(x,y)
and
(x_{n},y_{n})=(z,w)
,

•

x_{1}\leq x_{2}\leq\cdots\leq x_{n}
and
y_{1}\leq y_{2}\leq\cdots\leq y_{n}
,

•

(x_{i},y_{i})\neq(x_{i+1},y_{i+1})
for all
1\leq i<n
,

•

x_{1}=x_{2}
or
y_{1}=y_{2}
,

•

for all
1\leq i<n-1
, if
x_{i}=x_{i+1}
then
y_{i+1}=y_{i+2}
, and

•

for all
1\leq i<n-1
, if
y_{i}=y_{i+1}
then
x_{i+1}=x_{i+2}

We define

\Hom_{\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}}((x,y),(z,w))=P_{x,y}^{z,w}.

Note that
P_{x,y}^{z,w}
may be empty.
If either
x>z
or
y>w
, then

\Hom_{\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}}((x,y),(z,w))=\emptyset.

If (i)
x=z
and
y<w
or (ii)
x<z
and
y=w
, then

\Hom_{\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}}((x,y),(z,w))=\left\{\{(x,y),(z,w)\}\right\}.

If
(x,y)=(z,w)
then

\Hom_{\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}}((x,y),(x,y))=\left\{\{(x,y)\}\right\}.

Composition is given by concatenating sequences and, if necessary, deleting a repeated term.

Let
\mathcal{Q}
be the additive
\Bbbk
-linearization of
\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}
and let
(x,y),(z,w)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
x<z
and
y<w
.
Then, we define

\mathcal{I}((x,y),(z,w))=\left\langle\{(x_{i},y_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m}-\{(x^{\prime}_{j},y^{\prime}_{j})\}_{j=1}^{n}\mid\{(x_{i},y_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m}\neq\{(x^{\prime}_{j},y^{\prime}_{j})\}_{j=1}^{n}\right\rangle.

If
x=z
or
y=w
, then
\mathcal{I}((x,y),(z,w))=0
.

Consider

\displaystyle\{(x_{i},y_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m}-\{(x^{\prime}_{j},y^{\prime}_{j})\}_{j=1}^{n}

\displaystyle\in\mathcal{I}((x,y),(z,w))

\displaystyle\{(z_{k},y_{k})\}_{k=1}^{p}-\{(z^{\prime}_{l},z^{\prime}_{l})\}_{l=1}^{q}

\displaystyle\in\mathcal{I}((z,w),(u,v)).

We show the composition is in
\mathcal{I}((x,y),(u,v))
.

\displaystyle(\{(z_{k},y_{k})\}_{k=1}^{p}-\{(z^{\prime}_{l},z^{\prime}_{l})\}_{l=1}^{q})\circ(\{(x_{i},y_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m}-\{(x^{\prime}_{j},y^{\prime}_{j})\}_{j=1}^{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{(z_{k},y_{k})\}\circ\{(x_{i},y_{i})\}-\{(z_{k},y_{k})\}\circ\{(x^{\prime}_{j},y^{\prime}_{j})\}

\displaystyle-\{(z^{\prime}_{l},z^{\prime}_{l})\}\circ\{(x_{i},y_{i})\}+\{(z^{\prime}_{l},z^{\prime}_{l})\}\circ\{(x^{\prime}_{j},y^{\prime}_{j})\}.

We know

\displaystyle\{(z_{k},y_{k})\}\circ\{(x_{i},y_{i})\}

\displaystyle\neq\{(z_{k},y_{k})\}\circ\{(x^{\prime}_{j},y^{\prime}_{j})\}

and

\displaystyle\{(z^{\prime}_{l},z^{\prime}_{l})\}\circ\{(x_{i},y_{i})\}

\displaystyle\neq\{(z^{\prime}_{l},z^{\prime}_{l})\}\circ\{(x^{\prime}_{j},y^{\prime}_{j})\}.

Thus, the composition is in
\mathcal{I}((x,y),(u,v))
.

Consider
\{(x_{i},y_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m}-\{(x^{\prime}_{j},y^{\prime}_{j})\}_{j=1}^{n}\in\mathcal{I}((x,y),(z,w))
.
By assumption there is
1\leq k\leq m
and
1\leq l\leq n
such that
(x_{k},y_{k})\neq(x^{\prime}_{l},y^{\prime}_{l})
.
Let

\displaystyle f:(x,y)\to(x_{k},y_{k})\oplus(x^{\prime}_{l},y^{\prime}_{l})

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{matrix}\{(x_{i},y_{i})\}_{i=1}^{k}\\
-\{(x^{\prime}_{j},y^{\prime}_{j})\}_{j=1}^{l}\end{matrix}\right]

\displaystyle g:(x_{k},y_{k})\oplus(x^{\prime}_{l},y^{\prime}_{l})\to(z,w)

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{matrix}\{(x_{i},y_{i})\}_{i=k}^{m}&\{(x^{\prime}_{j},y^{\prime}_{j})\}_{j=l}^{n}\end{matrix}\right].

Notice
f\notin\mathcal{I}((x,y),(x_{k},y_{k})\oplus(x^{\prime}_{l},y^{\prime}_{l}))
,
g\notin\mathcal{I}((x_{k},y_{k})\oplus(x^{\prime}_{l},y^{\prime}_{l}),(z,w))
, and

\{(x_{i},y_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m}-\{(x^{\prime}_{j},y^{\prime}_{j})\}_{j=1}^{n}=g\circ f.

Thus, every morphism in
\mathcal{I}
is given by a finite composition of morphisms not in
\mathcal{I}
.
Furthermore, there are no cycles in
\mathcal{Q}
.
Thus,
\mathcal{I}
is an admissible ideal.

The resulting
\mathcal{Q}/\mathcal{I}
is the category where the objects are fnite direct sums of points in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and Hom spaces between points is given by

\Hom_{\mathcal{Q}/\mathcal{I}}((x,y),(z,w))=\begin{cases}\Bbbk&x\leq z\text{ and }y\leq w\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

This means that
\mathcal{Q}/\mathcal{I}
is the continuous generalization of (finite) discrete commutative grid quivers as examined in
[
BBOS
]
.

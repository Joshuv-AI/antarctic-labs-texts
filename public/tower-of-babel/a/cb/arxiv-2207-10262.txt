A Labelled Sequent Calculus for Public Announcement Logic
Hao Wu, Hans van Ditmarsch, Jinsheng Chen

Abstract
Public announcement logic ( 𝖯𝖠𝖫 \mathsf{PAL} ) is an extension of epistemic logic ( 𝖤𝖫 \mathsf{EL} ) with some reduction axioms. In this paper, we propose a cut-free labelled sequent calculus for 𝖯𝖠𝖫 \mathsf{PAL} , which is an extension of that for 𝖤𝖫 \mathsf{EL} with sequent rules adapted from the reduction axioms. This calculus admits cut and allows terminating proof search.



1
Introduction

The modern modal approach to the logic of knowledge and belief was extensively developed by Hintikka
[
10
]
to interpret epistemic notions utilizing possible world semantics. The standard multi-agent epistemic logic
\mathsf{EL}
is usually identified with the poly-modal modal logic
\mathsf{S5}
with a group of agents. Public announcement logic (PAL), introduced in Plaza
[
17
]
, is an extension of
\mathsf{EL}
that studies logical dynamics of epistemic information after the action of public announcement. More general actions are studied in action model logic (see e.g.
[
5
,
7
,
4
]
).

\mathsf{PAL}
is an extension of
\mathsf{EL}
with announcement operators of the form
[\varphi]
. As
\mathsf{EL}
, formulas in
\mathsf{PAL}
are interpreted in Kripke models in which all relations are reflexive, transitive and symmetric. In particular, formulas of the form
[\varphi]\psi
are interpreted in the restrictions of Kripke models induced by the announcement
\varphi
. The Hilbert-style axiomatization of
\mathsf{PAL}
is obtained by adding to that of
\mathsf{EL}
the so called
reduction axioms
for announcement operators, which can be used to eliminate announcement operators in a
\mathsf{PAL}
-formula.

Generally speaking, it is difficult to prove whether proof search using a Hilbert-style axiomatization is decidable. In view of these, many proof systems for
\mathsf{PAL}
are proposed in the literature, e.g., tableau systems
[
3
]
, labelled sequent calculi
[
2
,
16
]
.

In this paper, we propose another labelled sequent calculus for
\mathsf{PAL}
. This calculus is based on a labelled sequent calculus for
\mathsf{EL}
proposed in Hakli and Negri
[
9
]
and rules for announcement operators designed according to the reduction axioms. This calculus admits structural rules (including cut) and allows terminating proof search. Unlike
[
2
,
16
]
, which are based on another semantics for
\mathsf{PAL}
, our calculus is based on the original semantics and takes into account the conditions of reflexivity, transitivity and symmetry in
\mathsf{EL}
.

The rest of the paper is structured as follows: Section 2 presents some basic notions and concepts. Section 3 presents our labelled sequent calculus
G_{\mathsf{PAL}}
for
\mathsf{PAL}
. Section 4 shows that
G_{\mathsf{PAL}}
admits some structural rules, including cut. Section 5 shows that
G_{\mathsf{PAL}}
allows terminating proof search.
Section 6 compares
G_{\mathsf{PAL}}
with related works, concludes the paper and discusses future work.

2.1
\mathsf{EL}

Let
\mathsf{Prop}

\mathcal{L}::=p\mid\neg\varphi\mid\varphi\land\varphi\mid\varphi\to\varphi\mid K_{a}\varphi

Let

\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathcal{M},w\Vdash p&~\text{iff}&w\in V(p)\\
\mathcal{M},w\Vdash\neg\varphi&~\text{iff}&\mathcal{M},w\nVdash\varphi\\

\mathcal{M},w\Vdash K_{a}\varphi&~\text{iff}&\text{for~all}~v\in W,w\sim_{a}v~\text{implies}~\mathcal{M},v\Vdash\varphi\\

where

2.2
Labelled sequent calculus

A labelled sequent calculus for a logic with Kripke semantics is based on the internalization of Kripke semantics.

Let
\mathcal{F}=(W,\{\sim_{a}\}_{a\in\mathsf{Ag}})
be an epistemic frame. A
relational atom
is of the form
x\sim_{a}y
, where
x,y\in W
and
a\in\mathsf{Ag}
. A
labelled formula
is of the form
x:\varphi
, where
x\in W
and
\varphi
is an
\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{EL}}
-formula. We use
\sigma,\delta
with or without subscript to denote relational atoms or labelled formulas.

A
multiset
is a ‘set with multiplicity’, or put the other way round, a sequence modulo the ordering. A
labelled sequent
is of the form
\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta
where
\Gamma,\Delta
are finite multisets of relational atoms and labelled formulas. A
sequent rule
is of the form

(\mathcal{R})~\frac{\Gamma_{1}\Rightarrow\Delta_{1}~\ldots~\Gamma_{m}\Rightarrow\Delta_{m}}{\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta}

where
m\geq 0
.
\Gamma_{1}\Rightarrow\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{m}\Rightarrow\Delta_{m}
are called
premises
of this rule and
\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta
is called the conclusion. If
m=0
, we simply write
\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta
and call it an
initial sequent
.
The formula with the connective in a rule is the
principal
formula of that rule, and its components in the premisses are the
active
formulas.
A
labelled sequent calculus
is a set of sequent rules. A derivation in a labelled sequent calculus
G
is defined as usual. The
derivation height h
of a sequent is defined as the length of longest branch in the derivation of the sequent.
We use
G\vdash\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta
to denote that
\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta
is derivable in
G
and
G\vdash_{h}\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta
to denote that
\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta
is derivable in
G
with a derivation the height of which is at most
h
.

A sequent rule
(\mathcal{R})
is
admissible
in
G
if
G\vdash\Gamma_{i}\Rightarrow\Delta_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq m
implies
G\vdash\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta
. It is
height-preserving admissible
if
G\vdash_{h}\Gamma_{i}\Rightarrow\Delta_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq m
implies
G\vdash_{h}\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=(W,\{\sim_{a}\}_{a\in\mathsf{Ag}},V)
be an epistemic model. The
interpretation

\tau_{\mathcal{M}}
of relational atoms and labelled formulas are defined as follows:

\displaystyle\tau_{\mathcal{M}}(x\sim_{a}y)

\displaystyle=\quad

\displaystyle x\sim_{a}y;

\displaystyle\tau_{\mathcal{M}}(x:\varphi)

\displaystyle=\quad

\displaystyle\mathcal{M},x\Vdash\varphi.

A labelled sequent
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m}\Rightarrow\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{n}
is
valid
if

\forall\mathcal{M}\forall x_{1}\ldots\forall x_{k}[\tau_{\mathcal{M}}(\sigma_{1})\land\ldots\land\tau_{\mathcal{M}}(\sigma_{m})\to\tau_{\mathcal{M}}(\delta_{1})\lor\ldots\lor\tau_{\mathcal{M}}(\delta_{n})]

is true, where
\mathcal{M}=(W,\{\sim_{a}\}_{a\in\mathsf{Ag}},V)
is an epistemic model, and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}
are variables occurring in
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m}\Rightarrow\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{n}
ranging over
W
.

A sequent rule
\mathcal{R}

is
valid
if the validity of all the premises implies the validity of the conclusion.

Given a labelled sequent calculus
G
and a logic
\Lambda
, we say
G
is a
labelled sequent calculus for
\Lambda
if for all
\varphi
,
G\vdash{}\Rightarrow\varphi
if and only if
\varphi\in\Lambda
.

3
Labelled sequent calculus for
\mathsf{PAL}

In this section, we introduce a labelled sequent calculus for
\mathsf{PAL}
. The Hilbert-style axiomatization for
\mathsf{PAL}
is the extension of that for
\mathsf{EL}
with reduction axioms. Can we obtain a labelled sequent calculus for
\mathsf{PAL}
by adding some rules adapted from reduction axioms to the labelled sequent calculus
G_{\mathsf{EL}}
for
\mathsf{EL}
? The answer is yes and this is what we do.

Take reduction axiom (R1)
[\varphi]p\leftrightarrow(\varphi\to p)
as an example. The equivalence symbol in the axiom means that
[\varphi]p
and
\varphi\to p
are equivalent in
\mathsf{PAL}
. Therefore, the most direct sequent rules for (R1) are

\frac{x:\varphi\to p,\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta}{x:[\varphi]p,\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta}\text{~~~and~~~}\frac{\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta,x:\varphi\to p}{\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta,x:[\varphi]p}

The rule on the left is sound because of
[\varphi]p\to(\varphi\to p)
and the rule on the right is sound because of
(\varphi\to p)\to[\varphi]p
. These rules can be written in a more neat way if we see
\varphi\to p,\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta
as the conclusion of an application of
(\to\Rightarrow)
and
\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta,\varphi\to p
as the conclusion of an application of
(\Rightarrow\to)
. Applying the reverse of
(\to\Rightarrow)
and
(\Rightarrow\to)
to their premises, we have the rules for (R1) that will be added to
G_{\mathsf{EL}}
:

\frac{\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta,x:\varphi\quad x:p,\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta}{x:[\varphi]p,\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta}\text{~~~and~~~}\frac{x:\varphi,\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta,p}{\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta,x:[\varphi]p}

In a similar way, we can have sequent rules for other reduction axioms. Therefore, we have:

8

Definition 19

A derivation in

\varphi

The potential sources of non-terminating proof search in
G_{\mathsf{PAL}}
are as follows:

(1)

(Trans_{a})
,
(Sym_{a})
(read root-first) can be applied infinitely on the same principal formulas.

(2)

(Ref_{a})
(read root-first) can be applied infinitely to introduce new relational atoms.

(3)

{(K_{a}\Rightarrow)}
(read root-first) can be applied infinitely on the same principal formulas.

(4)

By
(Trans_{a})
and its iteration with
(\Rightarrow K_{a})
that brings in new accessible worlds, we can build chains of accessible worlds on which
(K_{a}\Rightarrow)
can be applied infinitely.

To show that the space of root-first proof search is finite, it is useful to look at
minimal derivations
, that is, derivations where shortenings are not possible.

For (1), since no rules, read root first, can change a relational atom, any application of
(Trans_{a})
or
(Sym_{a})
on the same principal formulas will make the derivation fail to be minimal. Therefore, this case should be excluded when searching for minimal derivation.

For (2), we need the following lemma:

All variables in relational atoms of the form

Proof

For (3), we need to show the following lemma:

Lemma 21

Rule

permutes down with respect to
(\Rightarrow\!K_{a})
in case the principal relational atom of

Proposition 22

(K_{a}\!\Rightarrow)

6
Conclusion, comparison and future research

In this paper we proposed a labelled sequent calculus for
\mathsf{PAL}
. It is based on a proof system for
\mathsf{EL}
, namely poly-modal
\mathsf{S5}
, extended with sequent rules to deal with announcement operators that directly mirror the
\mathsf{PAL}
reduction rules. We also determined that the obtained system allows terminating proof search, and compared our system with the calculus for
\mathsf{EL}
on matters such a height preservation of derivations.

\alpha
or

of formulas, and by

\alpha=(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n})

\mathcal{M}^{\alpha}:=\mathcal{M}

\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathcal{M}^{\alpha,\varphi},w\Vdash p&~\text{iff}&\mathcal{M}^{\alpha},w\Vdash\varphi\text{~and~}\mathcal{M}^{\alpha},w\Vdash p\\

\mathcal{M}^{\alpha},w\Vdash\varphi\to\psi&~\text{iff}&\mathcal{M}^{\alpha},w\nVdash\varphi~\text{or}~\mathcal{M}^{\alpha},w\Vdash\psi\\
\mathcal{M}^{\alpha},w\Vdash K_{a}\varphi&~\text{iff}&\text{for~all}~v\in W,w\sim^{\alpha}_{a}v~\text{implies}~\mathcal{M}^{\alpha},v\Vdash\varphi\\
\mathcal{M}^{\alpha},w\Vdash[\varphi]\psi&~\text{iff}&\mathcal{M}^{\alpha},w\Vdash\varphi~\text{implies}~\mathcal{M}^{\alpha,\varphi},w\Vdash\psi\end{array}

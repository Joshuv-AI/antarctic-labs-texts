Abstract
In this paper, we obtain some sufficient conditions for the D D -completion of a T 0 T_{0} space to be the well-filterification of this space, the well-filterification of a T 0 T_{0} space to be the sobrification of this space and the D D -completion of a T 0 T_{0} space to be the sobrification, respectively. Moreover, we give an example to show that a tapered closed set may be neither the closure of a directed set nor the closed K ​ F KF -set, respectively. Because the tapered closed set is a closed W ​ D WD -set, the example also gives a negative answer to a problem proposed by Xu. Meantime, a new direct characterization of the D D -completion of a T 0 T_{0} space is given by using the notion of pre- c c -compact elements.

Sufficient conditions

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X
be a
T_{0}
space. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)
If
A\in\mathbf{KF}(X)
, then
\mathord{\downarrow}(A\cap K)\in\Gamma(X)
for any
K\in Q(X)
;

(2)

A
is a directed closed subset of
X
.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
It suffices to prove that
A
is directed. Suppose
A\in\mathbf{KF}(X)
. Then there exists
\{K_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subseteq_{flt}Q(X)
such that
A
is a minimal closed set that intersects all members of
\{K_{i}\}_{i\in I}
by Definition
2.5
.

Claim 1:
A=\mathord{\downarrow}(A\cap K_{j})
for any
j\in I
.

For any
i\in I
, since
\{K_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subseteq_{flt}Q(X)
, there exists
r\in I
such that
K_{r}\subseteq K_{i}\cap K_{j}
. This implies that
\emptyset\neq A\cap K_{r}\subseteq A\cap K_{j}\cap K_{i}\subseteq\mathord{\downarrow}(A\cap K_{j})\cap K_{i}
. Then we have
A=\mathord{\downarrow}(A\cap K_{j})
by the minimality of
A
.

Claim 2:
\mathord{\uparrow}x\cap K_{i}\cap A\neq\emptyset
for any
x\in A
and
i\in I
.

x\in A=\mathord{\downarrow}(A\cap K_{i})
by Claim 1. Thus
\mathord{\uparrow}x\cap A\cap K_{i}\neq\emptyset
.

Claim 3:
A
is directed.

Let
x,y\in A
. Then we have
\mathord{\downarrow}(\mathord{\uparrow}x\cap A)\cap K_{i}\neq\emptyset
by Claim 2. It follows that
\mathord{\downarrow}(\mathord{\uparrow}x\cap A)=A
by the minimality of
A
. Note that
y\in A=\mathord{\downarrow}(\mathord{\uparrow}x\cap A)
, thus
\mathord{\uparrow}y\cap\mathord{\uparrow}x\cap A\neq\emptyset
.

(2)\Rightarrow(1)
It remains to prove that
\mathord{\downarrow}(A\cap K)\in\Gamma(X)
.

Claim 1:
\mathord{\downarrow}(\mathord{\uparrow}x\cap A)\in\Gamma(X)
for any
x\in X
.

If
\mathord{\uparrow}x\cap A=\emptyset
, then we have
\mathord{\downarrow}(\mathord{\uparrow}x\cap A)=\emptyset\in\Gamma(X)
. Else
\mathord{\uparrow}x\cap A\neq\emptyset
. Then
x\in A
because
A
is a lower set. Obviously,
\mathord{\downarrow}(\mathord{\uparrow}x\cap A)\subseteq A
. Conversely,
\mathord{\uparrow}a\cap\mathord{\uparrow}x\cap A\neq\emptyset
for any
a\in A
since
A
is directed. So we have
\mathord{\downarrow}(\mathord{\uparrow}x\cap A)=A\in\Gamma(X)
.

Claim 2:
\mathord{\downarrow}(K\cap A)\in\Gamma(X)
.

If
K\cap A=\emptyset
, then
\mathord{\downarrow}(K\cap A)=\emptyset\in\Gamma(X)
.
Else
K\cap A\neq\emptyset
. Pick
k\in K\cap A
. This means that
A\subseteq\mathord{\downarrow}(\mathord{\uparrow}k\cap A)\subseteq\mathord{\downarrow}(K\cap A)\subseteq A
by Claim 1. Hence,
\mathord{\downarrow}(K\cap A)=A\in\Gamma(X)
.
∎

The following proposition reveals some order-theoretic properties of the lattice

Proposition 5.4
.

Let P be a poset. Then for any
\mathcal{D}\in\mathcal{H}
,
\sup_{\Gamma(P)}\mathcal{D}=\bigcup\mathcal{D}
.

Proof.

Note that each member of
\mathcal{H}
is a Scott-closed subset of
P
. So to prove the equation, it suffices to show that
\bigcup\mathcal{D}\in\Gamma(P)
. Obviously,
\bigcup\mathcal{D}
is a lower set. Now Let
D
be any directed subset of
P
contained in
\bigcup\mathcal{D}
such that
\sup D
exists in
P
. We want to prove that
\sup D\in C
for some
C\in\mathcal{D}
. First note that
\mathcal{C}=\{\mathord{\downarrow}d~|~d\in D\}
is a directed subset of
\Gamma(P)
. Moreover,
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
by the definition of
\mathcal{D}
. Since
\mathcal{D}
is a
d
-closed set, so
\sup\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{D}
. But
\sup\mathcal{C}
is precisely
\mathord{\downarrow}\sup D
. Hence
\sup D\in C
for some
C\in\mathcal{D}
.
∎

Proposition 5.5

Let
P

Proof.

Suppose

Lemma 5.6

Let

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
P

A\in\mathcal{K}(\Gamma(P))

Proof.

Assume

(P^{\vee},j)

is continuous. As
(P^{\vee},j)
is the

[

]
. We need to prove

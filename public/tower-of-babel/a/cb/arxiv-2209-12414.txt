Abstract
. In this paper we describe the irreducible decomposition of the facet ideal ℱ ⁡ ( Δ m , n ) {\mathcal{F}}(\Delta_{m,n}) of the chessboard complex Δ m , n \Delta_{m,n} with n ≥ m n\geq m . We also provide some lower bounds for depth and regularity of the facet ideal ℱ ⁡ ( Δ m , n ) {\mathcal{F}}(\Delta_{m,n}) . When m ≤ 3 m\leq 3 , we prove that these lower bounds can be obtained.



1.
Introduction

Graph complexes have provided an important link between combinatorics and algebra, topology, and geometry (see
[
14
,
22
]
).
The two most important graph complexes are the matching complex and independence complex; their algebraic and topological properties
have been widely studied by many authors (see
[
2
,
8
,
14
,
17
,
22
,
23
,
26
]
).
The chessboard complex, which is the matching complex
\mathcal{M}(K_{m,n})
of a complete bipartite graph
K_{m,n}
, is first introduced in the thesis of Garst
[
9
]
.
He showed that
\mathcal{M}(K_{m,n})
is Cohen–Macaulay if and only if
n\geq 2m-1
. Ziegler in
[
23
]
strengthened this result by showing that
\mathcal{M}(K_{m,n})
is shellable if
n\geq 2m-1
. Hence
\mathcal{M}(K_{m,n})
has the homotopy type of a wedge of
(m-1)
-spheres if
n\geq 2m-1
. Freidman and Hanlon studied Betti numbers of chessboard complex in
[
8
]
. They showed that
b_{r-1}(\Delta_{m,n})=0
if and only if
(m-r)(n-r)>r
, and
b_{v-1}(\Delta_{m,n})>0
if and only if
n\geq 2m-4
or
(m,n)=(6,6),(7,7),(8,9)
,
where
b_{i}(\Delta_{m,n})
is the
i
-th Betti number of
\Delta_{m,n}
, it equals the rank of the homology group
H_{i}(\Delta_{m,n})
.
The chessboard complex may be regarded as a simplicial complex formed by all admissible rook configurations on an
m\times n
chessboard, while an admissible rook configuration on a chessboard refers to
a subset of squares of the chessboard such that no two squares lie in the same row or in the same column. It is for this reason
that the name “chessboard complex” is used.

Given a simplicial complex
\Delta
on the vertex set
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
, we consider the polynomial ring
S=k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
in
n
variables over a field
k
.
The Stanley-Reisner ideal of
\Delta
, denoted by
{\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta}
, is the squarefree monomial ideal of
S
given by

{\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta}=(x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{s}}\mid\{x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{s}}\}\text{\ is not a face of \ }\Delta).

The
facet ideal
of
\Delta
, denoted by
{\mathcal{F}}(\Delta)
, is the squarefree monomial ideal of
S
given by

{\mathcal{F}}(\Delta)=(x_{\ell_{1}}\cdots x_{\ell_{s}}\mid\{x_{\ell_{1}},\ldots,x_{\ell_{s}}\}\text{\ is a facet of \ }\Delta).

The Stanley-Reisner ring of
\Delta
is
k[\Delta]=S/{\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta}
.
For any positive integers
m,n
, Björner et al. in
[
2
]
showed that a chessboard complex
\Delta_{m,n}
is
(v-2)
-connected
and
\operatorname{depth}\,(k[\Delta_{m,n}])=v
for any positive integers
m,n
, where
v=\min\{m,n,\lfloor\frac{m+n+1}{3}\rfloor\}
and
\left\lfloor\frac{m+n+1}{3}\right\rfloor
is the largest integer
\leq\frac{m+n+1}{3}
.

Hà and Van Tuyl in
[
11
]
studied edge ideals of
m
-uniform
hypergraphs, they showed that if
H
is a properly-connected hypergraph with the edge ideal
I(H)
and
c
is
the maximal number of pairwise
(m+1)
-disjoint edges of
H
, then

\operatorname{reg}\,(S/I(H))\geq c(m-1)

and the equality holds if
H
is triangulated. Obviously,
{\mathcal{F}}(\Delta_{m,n})
can be regarded as the edge ideal of an
m
-uniform hypergraph, but this
m
-uniform hypergraph is not properly-connected and has no pairwise
(m+1)
-disjoint edges.

As far as we know, little is known about algebraic properties of the facet ideal of a chessboard complex. In this article,
we focus on properties corresponding to the irreducible decomposition (see Theorem
3.3
), depth and regularity of the facet ideal of a chessboard complex. Our main results are as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Delta_{m,n}
be a chessboard complex with
n\geq m\geq 1
.
Let
V
be its vertex set and
{\mathcal{F}}(\Delta_{m,n})
be its facet ideal.
A prime ideal
P
of
S
is a minimal prime of
{\mathcal{F}}(\Delta_{m,n})
if and only if
P
is generated by a set
C
, which is obtained from
V
by deleting all elements in
s
rows and all elements in
m-1-s
columns for some
0\leq s\leq m-1
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
\Delta_{m,n}
be a chessboard complex with
n\geq m\geq 1
, one has

(a)

\operatorname{height}\,({\mathcal{F}}(\Delta_{m,n}))=n
,

(b)

\dim\,(S/{\mathcal{F}}(\Delta_{m,n}))=(m-1)n
,

(c)

\operatorname{bight}\,({\mathcal{F}}(\Delta_{m,n}))=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l@{\ \ }l}\lfloor\frac{(n+1)^{2}}{4}\rfloor&\text{if $n<2m-1$,}\\
{(n-m+1)m}&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Delta_{m,n}
be a chessboard complex with
n\geq m\geq 1
, then

(a)

\operatorname{reg}(S/{\mathcal{F}}(\Delta_{m,n}))\geq 2(m-1)
.

(b)

\operatorname{depth}\,(S/{\mathcal{F}}(\Delta_{m,n}))\geq 2(m-1)
.

The equalities hold if
m\leq 3
.

Moradi in
[
17
]
showed that if
G
is a graph with
m
-clique ideal
K_{m}(G)
and
c_{m}(G)
is the minimum number
of co-chordal subgraphs of
G
required to cover the
m
-cliques of
G
, then

\operatorname{reg}\,(S/K_{m}(G))\leq c_{m}(G)(m-1)
. One can regard
{\mathcal{F}}(\Delta_{m,n})
as the
m
-clique ideal of the complement
G
of the line graph
of the complete bipartite graph
K_{m,n}
where
c_{m}(G)\geq 2
. This shows that the upper bound of
\operatorname{reg}\,(S/K_{m}(G))
given by Moradi
can be strict.

In this section, we gather together the needed notations and basic facts, which will

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex on the vertex set
V
. We denote the set of its facets (maximal faces under inclusion) by
Facets\,(\Delta)
.
If
Facets\,(\Delta)=\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{q}\}
, we write
\Delta
as
\Delta=\langle F_{1},\ldots,F_{q}\rangle
.
A
subcomplex
of
\Delta
is a simplicial complex whose
facet set is a subset of the facet set of
\Delta
.
If
A\subseteq V
,
the
induced subcomplex
of
\Delta
over
A
is the subcomplex
\Delta|_{A}=\{F\in\Delta\mid F\subseteq A\}
.

Let
n
be a positive integer, we set
[n]=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
. Given a chessboard complex
\Delta_{m,n}
with a vertex set
\{x_{ij}\mid i\in[m],j\in[n]\}
, we consider
the polynomial ring
S=k[x_{ij}\mid i\in[m],j\in[n]]
in the entries of a generic matrix
(x_{ij})_{m\times n}
over a field
k
.
The facet ideal of
\Delta_{m,n}
is the squarefree monomial ideal of
S
given by

{\mathcal{F}}(\Delta_{m,n})=(x_{1\ell_{1}}\cdots x_{m\ell_{m}}\mid\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{m}\in[n]\text{\ are pairwise different}).

Example 2.1
.

The facet ideal of the chessboard complex
\Delta_{3,3}
is

{\mathcal{F}}(\Delta_{3,3})=(x_{11}x_{22}x_{33},x_{11}x_{23}x_{32},x_{12}x_{21}x_{33},x_{12}x_{23}x_{31},x_{13}x_{21}x_{32},x_{13}x_{22}x_{31}).

Let
S=k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a polynomial ring in
n
variables over a field
k
,
I\subset S
a nonzero homogeneous ideal and

0\rightarrow\bigoplus\limits_{j}S(-j)^{\beta_{p,j}(I)}\rightarrow\bigoplus\limits_{j}S(-j)^{\beta_{p-1,j}(I)}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow\bigoplus\limits_{j}S(-j)^{\beta_{0,j}(I)}\rightarrow I\rightarrow 0

is a
minimal graded free resolution
of
I
, and
S(-j)
is an
S
-module obtained by shifting
the degrees of
S
by
j
. The number

\beta_{i,j}(I)
, the
(i,j)
-th graded Betti number of
I
, is
an invariant of
I
that equals the minimal number of generators of degree
j
in the

i
th syzygy module of
I
.
Of particular interest are the following invariants which measure the size of the minimal graded
free resolution of
I
.
The
projective dimension
of
I
, denoted by
\operatorname{pd}\,(I)
, is defined to be

\mbox{pd}\,(I):=\mbox{max}\,\{i\ |\ \beta_{i,j}(I)\neq 0\}.

The
regularity
of
I
, denoted by
\mbox{reg}\,(I)
, is defined by

\mbox{reg}\,(I):=\mbox{max}\,\{j-i\ |\ \beta_{i,j}(I)\neq 0\}.

By looking at the minimal free resolution and Auslander-Buchsbaum formula (see Theorem 1.3.3 of
[
3
]
), it is easy to obtain the following lemma:

Lemma 2.2
.

(
[
13
, Lemma 1.3]
)
Let
I\subset S
be a nonzero proper homogeneous ideal. Then

(1)

\operatorname{depth}\,(S/I)=n-\operatorname{pd}\,(S/I)
,

(2)

\operatorname{depth}\,(S/I)=\operatorname{depth}\,(I)-1
,

(3)

\operatorname{reg}\,(S/I)=\operatorname{reg}\,(I)-1
,

(4)

\operatorname{pd}\,(S/I)=\operatorname{pd}\,(I)+1
.

Hence we shall work with
\operatorname{reg}\,(I)
(resp.
\operatorname{depth}\,(I)
) and
\operatorname{reg}\,(S/I)
(resp.
\operatorname{depth}\,(S/I)
) interchangeably.

For a monomial ideal
I
,
\mathcal{G}(I)
denotes the unique minimal set
of monomial generators of
I
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let

be a squarefree monomial ideal with irreducible decomposition

where

and

The Alexander dual of

.

(
[

Let

3.
Irreducible decomposition of the facet ideal

In this section, we provide the irreducible decomposition of the facet ideal
{\mathcal{F}}(\Delta_{m,n})
of a chessboard complex
\Delta_{m,n}
with
n\geq m
.
We first give a definition of the vertex cover.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex on the vertex set
V
. A vertex cover for
\Delta
is a subset
A
of
V
that intersects
every facet of
\Delta
. If
A
is a minimal element (under inclusion) of the set of vertex covers of
\Delta
, it is called a minimal vertex cover.

We need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

(
[
6
, Proposition 1.8]
) Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex,
{\mathcal{F}}(\Delta)
its facet ideal.
Then an ideal
P=(x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{s}})
is a minimal prime of
{\mathcal{F}}(\Delta)
if and only if
\{x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{s}}\}
is a minimal vertex cover of
\Delta
.

Now, we describe the irreducible decomposition of
{\mathcal{F}}(\Delta_{m,n})
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\Delta_{m,n}
be a chessboard complex with
n\geq m\geq 1
.
Let
V
be its vertex set and
{\mathcal{F}}(\Delta_{m,n})
be its facet ideal.
A prime ideal
P
of
S
is a minimal prime of
{\mathcal{F}}(\Delta_{m,n})
if and only if
P
is generated by a set
C
, which is obtained from
V
by deleting all elements in
s
rows and all elements in
m-1-s
columns for some
0\leq s\leq m-1
.

Proof.

The case
m=1
is trivial. Now we assume that
m\geq 2
. It is enough to show that the set
C
is exactly a minimal vertex cover of
\Delta_{m,n}
by Lemma
3.2
.

First, we show that the set
C
is a vertex cover of
\Delta_{m,n}
.

For any
F\in Facets(\Delta_{m,n})
, we know that
|F|=m
and all elements in
F
are taken from different rows and columns.
We may write
F
as
F=\{x_{1t_{1}},\ldots,x_{mt_{m}}\}
, where
t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}\in[n]
are different.
Now, we show that there exists some
x_{kt_{k}}\in F
such that
x_{kt_{k}}\in C
, i.e.,
F\cap C\neq\emptyset
. Assume that the set
C
is obtained from
V
by deleting all elements in
i_{1},\ldots,i_{s}
rows and all elements in
j_{1},\ldots,j_{m-1-s}
columns, where

Given an ideal

\operatorname{bight}\,(I)=\sup\{\operatorname{height}\,(P)\mid P\text{ is a minimal prime ideal of }S\text{ over }I\}.

As a consequence of the above theorem, we have

\operatorname{height}\,({\mathcal{F}}(\Delta_{m,n}))=n

(b)

(c)

\operatorname{bight}\,({\mathcal{F}}(\Delta_{m,n}))=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l@{\ \ }l}\lfloor\frac{(n+1)^{2}}{4}\rfloor&\text{if $n<2m-1$,}\\
{(n-m+1)m}&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Proof.

(a) follows from Theorem
3.3

(b) follows from a fact that

(c) The case

Now suppose

for some nonnegative integer

\operatorname{bight}\,({\mathcal{F}}(\Delta_{m,n}))=\sup\{-[s+\frac{n-(2m-1)}{2}]^{2}+\frac{(n+1)^{2}}{4}\mid\,0\leq s\leq m-1\}.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(\lfloor\frac{(2m-1)-n}{2}\rfloor)

\displaystyle=

5.
The facet ideal

In this section, we provide some lower bounds for depth and regularity of the facet ideal
{\mathcal{F}}(\Delta_{m,n})
of a chessboard complex
\Delta_{m,n}
under the condition that
n\geq m\geq 3
,
we also prove that these lower bounds can be attained if

.

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex on the vertex set

,
Facets\,(\Delta)

the set of its facets. A subset

of

is called a

of

if the facets in

are pairwise disjoint.
Moreover, if

for

is called an

of

Lemma 5.1
.

(
[
5
, Corollary 3.5]
and
[

, Corollary 3.9]
)
Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex with facet ideal
{\mathcal{F}}(\Delta)
.
Then

\operatorname{reg}\,(S/{\mathcal{F}}(\Delta))\geq\Big\{|\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{k}F_{i}|-k\mid\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{k}\}\text{\ is an induced matching in }\Delta\Big\}.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
\Delta_{m,n}
be a chessboard complex with

, then

\operatorname{reg}(S/{\mathcal{F}}(\Delta_{m,n}))\geq 2(m-1).

Proof.

Let
F_{1}=\{x_{11},x_{22},\ldots,x_{mm}\}
and
F_{2}=\{x_{12},x_{23},\ldots,x_{(m-1)m},x_{m1}\}
, then

\{F_{1},F_{2}\}
is an induced matching of
\Delta_{m,n}
. The result follows from Lemma
5.1
.

∎

Theorem 5.3

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
\Delta_{m,n}
be a chessboard complex with
n\geq m
and

\Omega=\{{\mathcal{D}}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}\mid 1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m}\leq n\}.

Then

\operatorname{depth}\,\left(\frac{S}{\sum\limits_{{\mathcal{D}}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}\in\Omega^{\prime}}{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{D}}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}})}\right)\geq 2(m-1)

for any nonempty subcollection
\Omega^{\prime}
of
\Omega
.

Proof.

We apply induction on
m
. Case
m=1
is trivial. Now suppose
m\geq 2
.
Let
V=\{x_{m1},x_{m2},\ldots,x_{mn}\}
,
W\subseteq V
and denote

{\mathcal{F}}_{W}=(\sum\limits_{{\mathcal{D}}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}\in\Omega^{\prime}}{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{D}}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}):\prod\limits_{x_{m\ell}\in W}x_{m\ell})+P_{W^{c}}

where
P_{W^{c}}
is an ideal generated by
V\setminus W
.
By Corollary
2.12
, it is enough to show that

\operatorname{depth}\,(S/{\mathcal{F}}_{W})\geq 2(m-1).

If
W=\emptyset
, then
{\mathcal{F}}_{W}=\sum\limits_{{\mathcal{D}}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}\in\Omega^{\prime}}{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{D}}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}})+P_{W^{c}}=P_{W^{c}}=(x_{m1},\ldots,x_{mn})
. In this case,

\operatorname{depth}\,(S/{\mathcal{F}}_{W})=\operatorname{depth}\,(S/(x_{m1},\ldots,x_{mn}))=n(m-1)\geq 2(m-1).

If
W\neq\emptyset
. We may assume
W=\{x_{ms_{1}},x_{ms_{2}},\ldots,x_{ms_{k}}\}

and

\Omega^{\prime\prime}=\{{\mathcal{D}}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}\in\Omega^{\prime}\mid\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}\}\cap\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{k}\}\neq\emptyset\}.

We consider the following two cases:

(a) If
\Omega^{\prime\prime}=\emptyset
, then
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}\}\cap\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{k}\}=\emptyset
for any
{\mathcal{D}}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}\in\Omega^{\prime}
. It follows that

({\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{D}}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}):\prod\limits_{x_{m\ell}\in W}x_{m\ell})={\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{D}}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}})\subset P_{W^{c}}

because of
\{x_{mi_{1}},\ldots,x_{mi_{m}}\}\subseteq W^{c}
. Thus

{\mathcal{F}}_{W}=(\sum\limits_{{\mathcal{D}}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}\in\Omega^{\prime}}{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{D}}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}):\prod\limits_{x_{m\ell}\in W}x_{m\ell})+P_{W^{c}}=P_{W^{c}}.

Thus, we have
\operatorname{depth}\,(S/{\mathcal{F}}_{W})=\operatorname{depth}\,(S/P_{W^{c}})=n(m-1)+k>2(m-1)
.

(b) If
\Omega^{\prime\prime}\neq\emptyset
, then

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}_{W}\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!(\sum\limits_{{\mathcal{D}}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}\in\Omega^{\prime}}{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{D}}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}):\prod\limits_{x_{m\ell}\in W}x_{m\ell})+P_{W^{c}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\sum\limits_{{\mathcal{D}}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}\in\Omega^{\prime\prime}}\!\!\!({\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{D}}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}):\!\!\prod\limits_{x_{m\ell}\in W}\!\!x_{m\ell})+\!\!\!\!\sum\limits_{{\mathcal{D}}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}\in\Omega^{\prime}\setminus\Omega^{\prime\prime}}({\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{D}}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}):\!\!\prod\limits_{x_{m\ell}\in W}\!\!x_{m\ell})+P_{W^{c}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\sum\limits_{{\mathcal{D}}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}\in\Omega^{\prime\prime}}\!\!\!({\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{D}}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}})\!:\!\!\prod\limits_{x_{m\ell}\in W}x_{m\ell})+P_{W^{c}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\sum\limits_{{\mathcal{D}}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}\in\Omega^{\prime\prime}}\!\sum\limits_{s_{\ell}\in A_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}}\!\!\!\!\!{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{B}}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m},s_{\ell}})\!+\!P_{W^{c}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{W}+P_{W^{c}},

where the third and fourth equalities hold because of
({\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{D}}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}):\prod\limits_{x_{m\ell}\in W}x_{m\ell})={\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{D}}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}})\subset P_{W^{c}}
for any
{\mathcal{D}}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}\in\Omega^{\prime}\setminus\Omega^{\prime\prime}
and Remark
5.6
, where
A_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}\}\cap\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{k}\}
,

{\mathcal{B}}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m},s_{\ell}}
is a chessboard complex obtained by removing the
m
-th row cells and
s_{\ell}
-th column cells of the chessboard corresponding to
{\mathcal{D}}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}
and

{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{W}=\sum\limits_{{\mathcal{D}}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}\in\Omega^{\prime\prime}}\sum\limits_{s_{\ell}\in A_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}}{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{B}}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m},s_{\ell}}).

We distinguish into the following two cases:

(i) If
k=1
, then
A_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}=\{s_{1}\}
for any
{\mathcal{D}}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}\in\Omega^{\prime\prime}
. Thus there exists some

such that

. In this case,
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{W}=\sum\limits_{{\mathcal{D}}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}\in\Omega^{\prime\prime}}{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{B}}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m},s_{\ell}})
and

is a chessboard complex obtained by deleting the

-th row cells and the

-th column cells of the

By induction hypothesis, we obtain

\operatorname{depth}\,(S/{\mathcal{F}}_{W})=\operatorname{depth}\,(S_{1}/{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{W})+m\geq 2(m-2)+m\geq 2(m-1)

(ii) If

with

i_{j}\neq s_{\ell}\\
\end{subarray}}^{m}\{x_{1i_{j}},x_{2i_{j}},\ldots,x_{mi_{j}}\}\}

is a chessboard complex obtained by removing the

-th row cells of the

chessboard.
By induction hypothesis, we obtain

\operatorname{depth}\,(S/{\mathcal{F}}_{W})=\operatorname{depth}\,(S_{2}/{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{W})+k\geq 2(m-2)+k\geq 2(m-1)

.

∎

J_{s}:u_{s}=(x_{2p})+\sum\limits_{j\in[n-1]\setminus\{p\}}\left(\frac{x_{11}\cdots x_{1(n-1)}}{x_{1p}x_{1j}}\right)

and
\operatorname{reg}\,(J_{s}:u_{s})=n-3
. This finishes the proof.

∎

Theorem 5.13
.

Let
\Delta_{3,n}
be a chessboard complex with
n\geq 3
, then

\operatorname{depth}\,(S/{\mathcal{F}}(\Delta_{3,n}))=4.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}^{\,\vee}
be the Alexander dual of the facet ideal
{\mathcal{F}}(\Delta_{3,n})
. Then, by Lemma
2.4
and Auslander-Buchsbaum formula, we have

\operatorname{depth}\,(S/{\mathcal{F}}(\Delta_{3,n}))=3n-\text{pd}\,(S/{\mathcal{F}}(\Delta_{3,n}))=3n-\operatorname{reg}\,({\mathcal{F}}^{\,\vee}).

We will prove that
\operatorname{reg}\,({\mathcal{F}}^{\,\vee})=3n-4
, the desired result follows.

By Theorem
3.3
, one has

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}^{\,\vee}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n}x_{1k},\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n}x_{2k},\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n}x_{3k}\right)+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\in[n],\\
i<j\end{subarray}}\left(\prod\limits_{k=1}^{3}\frac{x_{k1}\cdots x_{kn}}{x_{ki}x_{kj}}\right)+

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\left(\prod\limits_{k=1,2}\frac{x_{k1}\cdots x_{kn}}{x_{kj}},\prod\limits_{k=1,3}\frac{x_{k1}\cdots x_{kn}}{x_{kj}},\prod\limits_{k=2,3}\frac{x_{k1}\cdots x_{kn}}{x_{kj}}\right).

Let
J
be a monomial ideal with
\mathcal{G}(J)=\mathcal{G}({\mathcal{F}}^{\,\vee})\setminus\{\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n}x_{3k}\}
.
Claim:
\operatorname{reg}\,(J:\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n}x_{3k})=2n-3
and
\operatorname{reg}(J)\leq 3n-4
. Thus the asserted results hold by Corollary
2.10
.

First, we compute
\operatorname{reg}\,(J:\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n}x_{3k})
.
Note that

J:\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n}x_{3k}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\left(\frac{x_{11}\cdots x_{1n}}{x_{1j}},\frac{x_{21}\cdots x_{2n}}{x_{2j}}\right)+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\in[n],\\
i<j\end{subarray}}\left(\prod\limits_{k=1}^{2}\frac{x_{k1}\cdots x_{kn}}{x_{ki}x_{kj}}\right).

If
n=3
, then
J:\prod\limits_{k=1}^{3}x_{3k}=\sum\limits_{k=1,2}(x_{k1}x_{k2},x_{k1}x_{k3},x_{k2}x_{k3})+(x_{11}x_{21},x_{12}x_{22},x_{13}x_{23})
.
Thus
\operatorname{reg}\,(J:\prod\limits_{k=1}^{3}x_{3k})=3=2n-3
by Lemma
5.10
.

If
n\geq 4
. Set

W=\left\{\prod\limits_{k=1}^{2}\frac{x_{k1}\cdots x_{k(n-1)}}{x_{kp}x_{kq}}\mid p,q\in[n-1],p<q\right\}
,
we sort all the elements of
W
in lexicographic order with
x_{11}>x_{12}>\cdots>x_{1n}>x_{21}>x_{22}>\cdots>x_{2n}
, that is:

\prod\limits_{k=1}^{2}\frac{x_{k1}\cdots x_{k(n-1)}}{x_{k(n-2)}x_{k(n-1)}}>\prod\limits_{k=1}^{2}\frac{x_{k1}\cdots x_{k(n-1)}}{x_{k(n-3)}x_{k(n-1)}}>\cdots>\prod\limits_{k=1}^{2}\frac{x_{k1}\cdots x_{k(n-1)}}{x_{k1}x_{k3}}>\prod\limits_{k=1}^{2}\frac{x_{k1}\cdots x_{k(n-1)}}{x_{k1}x_{k2}}.

Let
u_{s}
be the
s

W
and
I_{s}=(J:\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n}x_{3k})+(u_{1},\ldots,u_{s})
for
s\in[r]
. Then

I_{r}=(J:\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n}x_{3k})+(W)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\left(\frac{x_{11}\cdots x_{1n}}{x_{1j}},\frac{x_{21}\cdots x_{2n}}{x_{2j}}\right)+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\in[n-1],\\
i<j\end{subarray}}\left(\prod\limits_{k=1}^{2}\frac{x_{k1}\cdots x_{k(n-1)}}{x_{ki}x_{kj}}\right).

It follows from Lemma
5.12
that
\operatorname{reg}\,(I_{r})=2n-5.\hskip 142.26378pt(1)

Now, we compute
\operatorname{reg}\,(I_{s-1}:u_{s})
for any
s\in[r]
.

Set
\prod\limits_{k=1}^{2}\frac{x_{k1}\cdots x_{k(n-1)}}{x_{kp}x_{kq}}
for some
p,q\in[n-1]
and
p<q
. By simple calculations, one has

\displaystyle(J:\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n}x_{3k}):u_{s}\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\left(\frac{x_{11}\cdots x_{1n}}{x_{1j}},\frac{x_{21}\cdots x_{2n}}{x_{2j}}\right):u_{s}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\in[n],\\
i<j\end{subarray}}\left(\prod\limits_{k=1}^{2}\frac{x_{k1}\cdots x_{kn}}{x_{ki}x_{kj}}\right):u_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1,2}(x_{kp}x_{kq},x_{kp}x_{kn},x_{kq}x_{kn})+(x_{1p}x_{2p},x_{1q}x_{2q},x_{1n}x_{2n}).

Hence, it is easy to check that

\displaystyle I_{s-1}:u_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle((J:\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n}x_{3k})+(u_{i}\mid i\in[s-1])):u_{s}

\displaystyle(x_{1p}x_{1q},x_{1p}x_{1n},x_{1q}x_{1n},x_{2p}x_{2q},x_{2p}x_{2n},x_{2q}x_{2n},x_{1p}x_{2p},x_{1q}x_{2q},x_{1n}x_{2n}),

.

Finally, we prove

.

as

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal. The

-invariant of

, denoted by

, is the degree (as a rational function) of

.

Corollary 5.14
.

Let
\Delta_{m,n}
be a chessboard complex with
n\geq m
. If

, then

a(S/{\mathcal{F}}(\Delta_{m,n}))=0.

Proof.

By
[

, we have

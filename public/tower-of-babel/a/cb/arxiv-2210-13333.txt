Transcendental entire solutions of several general quadratic type PDEs and PDDEs in ℂ 2 \mathbb{C}^{2}
Molla Basir Ahamed, Sanju Mandal

Abstract
. The functional equations f 2 + g 2 = 1 f^{2}+g^{2}=1 and f 2 + 2 ​ α ​ f ​ g + g 2 = 1 f^{2}+2\alpha fg+g^{2}=1 are respectively called Fermat-type binomial and trinomial equations. It is of interest to know about the existence and form of the solutions of general quadratic functional equations. Utilizing Nevanlinna’s theory for several complex variables, in this paper, we study the existence and form of the solutions to the general quadratic partial differential or partial differential-difference equations of the form a ​ f 2 + 2 ​ α ​ f ​ g + b ​ g 2 + 2 ​ β ​ f + 2 ​ γ ​ g + C = 0 af^{2}+2\alpha fg+bg^{2}+2\beta f+2\gamma g+C=0 in ℂ 2 \mathbb{C}^{2} . Consequently, we obtain certain corollaries of the main results of this paper concerning binomial equations which generalize many results in [ Rocky Mountain J. Math. 51 (6) (2021), 2217-2235] in the sense of arbitrary coefficients.

The study of the Fermat type functional equation has been the interesting subject in the field of complex analysis in connection with extensions of Nevanlinna theory. Finding the precise solutions of different Fermat-type equations in several complex variables now-a-days becomes a central objectives in the value distribution theory of Nevanlinna. 2.
Finding solutions to the general quadratic equation with the help of some transformations

The most general quadratic first order partial differential equation
P(u_{x},u_{y})=0
over
\mathbb{C}
can be written as

(2.1)

P(u_{x},u_{y}):=au_{x}^{2}+2\alpha u_{x}u_{y}+bu_{y}^{2}+2\beta u_{x}+2\gamma u_{y}+C=0,

where
a,\alpha,b,\beta,\gamma,c
are complex constants and
(x,y)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
. It is easy to see that (
2.1
) is associated with the algebraic equation

(2.2)

\displaystyle ax^{2}+2\alpha xy+by^{2}+2\beta x+2\gamma y+C=0.

This will represent a pair of straight lines only if the L.H.S of (
2.2
)
can be resolve into two linear factors. Arranging the equation as a quadratic in
x
, we have

\displaystyle ax^{2}+2(\alpha y+\beta)x+(by^{2}+2\gamma y+C)=0.

Therefore, if
a\neq 0
a simple computation shows that

\displaystyle x=\frac{-(\alpha y+\beta)\pm\sqrt{(\alpha y+\beta)^{2}-a(by^{2}+2\gamma y+C)}}{a}.

Hence left hand side of (
2.2
) can be resolve into two linear factors, if
(\alpha y+\beta)^{2}-a(by^{2}+2\gamma y+C)
, i.e,
y^{2}(\alpha^{2}-ab)+2(\alpha\beta-a\gamma)y+(\beta^{2}-aC)
would be a perfect square. This is a quadratic in
y
, and hence is a perfect square, if its discriminant be zero, i.e, if
4(\alpha\beta-a\gamma)^{2}-4(\alpha^{2}-ab)(\beta^{2}-aC)=0
or

(2.3)

\displaystyle a(abC+2\alpha\beta\gamma-a\gamma^{2}-b\beta^{2}-C\alpha^{2})=0.

The companion matrix of the quadratic equation (
2.2
) is the following

A=\begin{bmatrix}a&\alpha&\beta\\
\alpha&b&\gamma\\
\beta&\gamma&C\end{bmatrix}.

Let
\Delta:=\det(A)
, where
\Delta
is known to be an invariant of the conic as
\Delta=0
in invariant under linear transformations (see
[
10
]
). A simple computation shows that
\Delta=abC+2\alpha\beta\gamma-a\gamma^{2}-b\beta^{2}-C\alpha^{2}
. Now since
a\neq 0
, from (
2.3
), we see that
\Delta=0
. Therefore, when
\Delta=0
the equation (
2.2
) represent a pair of straight lines. These pair of straight lines will be parallel if
\alpha^{2}=ab
and be intersecting if
\alpha^{2}\neq ab
.

In addition, when
\Delta\neq 0
, the equation (
2.2
) is transformed into a circle
x^{2}+y^{2}=1
, or, an ellipse
x^{2}/a^{2}+y^{2}/b^{2}=1
, or, a parabola,
y^{2}=x
. Define,
D=\det\begin{bmatrix}a&\alpha\\
\alpha&b\end{bmatrix}=ab-\alpha^{2}
. Let
\alpha^{2}\neq ab
. Then
under the transformation

(2.4)

\displaystyle x=X+x_{1}\;\mbox{and}\;y=Y+y_{1},\;\mbox{where}\;x_{1}=\frac{\alpha\gamma-b\beta}{ab-\alpha^{2}}\;\mbox{and}\;y_{1}=\frac{\alpha\beta-a\gamma}{ab-\alpha^{2}}

a straightforward computation shows that equation
(
2.4
)
reduces to

(2.5)

\displaystyle aX^{2}+2\alpha XY+bY^{2}+C_{1}=0,

where
C_{1}=ax^{2}_{1}+2\alpha x_{1}y_{1}+by^{2}_{1}+2\beta x_{1}+2\gamma y_{1}+C.
In view of the values of
x_{1}
and
y_{1}
, we see that
C_{1}
can be written as
C_{1}=\frac{abC+2\alpha\beta\gamma-a\gamma^{2}-b\beta^{2}-C\alpha^{2}}{ab-\alpha^{2}}=\frac{\Delta}{D}.
Hence, the equation
(
2.5
)
can be written as

(2.6)

\displaystyle aX^{2}+2\alpha XY+bY^{2}+\frac{\Delta}{D}=0,

where
D\neq 0
as
\alpha^{2}\neq ab
. To remove the

[(D_{11}-iD_{12})c_{1}+(E_{11}+iE_{12})c_{2}]H^{\prime}
and
[(D_{11}+iD_{12})c_{1}+(E_{11}-iE_{12})c_{2}]H^{\prime}
are constants. Since
(c_{1},c_{2})\neq(0,0)
, it follows that
H^{\prime}
is a constant, that is
\deg_{s_{1}}H\leq 1
. Hence, it is easy to see that
L(z)+H(z)+B
is still linear form of
\alpha_{1}z_{1}+\alpha_{2}z_{2}+B
, which means that
H(z)\equiv 0
. Therefore it is easy to see that
h(z)=L(z)+B=\alpha_{1}z_{1}+\alpha_{2}z_{2}+B
. Now, in view of (
4.64
) and (
4.65
), we see that

\begin{cases}\dfrac{(E_{11}+iE_{12})\alpha_{1}-(D_{11}-iD_{12})\alpha_{2}}{(iD_{11}+D_{12})-(iE_{11}-E_{12})}e^{iL(c)}=1;\vskip 5.69054pt\\
\dfrac{(D_{11}+iD_{12})\alpha_{2}-(E_{11}-iE_{12})\alpha_{1}}{(iD_{11}+D_{12})-(iE_{11}-E_{12})}e^{-iL(c)}=1,\par\end{cases}

which implies that

\begin{cases}e^{2iL(c)}=\dfrac{(D_{11}+iD_{12})\alpha_{2}-(E_{11}-iE_{12})\alpha_{1}}{(E_{11}+iE_{12})\alpha_{1}-(D_{11}-iD_{12})\alpha_{2}},\vskip 5.69054pt\\
(E^{2}_{11}+E^{2}_{12})\alpha^{2}_{1}+(D^{2}_{11}+D^{2}_{12})\alpha^{2}_{2}-2(D_{11}E_{11}-D_{12}E_{12})\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\\
\quad+[i(D_{11}-E_{11})+(D_{12}+E_{12})]^{2}=0.\end{cases}

Substituting
h(z)=\alpha_{1}z_{1}+\alpha_{2}z_{2}+B
into (
4.59
), we obtain

(4.66)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}-\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial z_{2}}

\displaystyle=(D_{11}-E_{11})\cos(\alpha_{1}z_{1}+\alpha_{2}z_{2}+B)

\displaystyle-(D_{12}+E_{12})\sin(\alpha_{1}z_{1}+\alpha_{2}z_{2}+B)+(T_{1}-T_{2}).

The characteristic equations of (
4.66
) are
\frac{dz_{1}}{dt}=1,\;\frac{dz_{2}}{dt}=-1
and we see that

(4.67)

\displaystyle\dfrac{df}{dt}=(D_{11}-E_{11})\cos(\alpha_{1}z_{1}+\alpha_{2}z_{2}+B)-(D_{12}+E_{12})\sin(\alpha_{1}z_{1}+\alpha_{2}z_{2}+B)+(T_{1}-T_{2}).

Using the initial conditions:
z_{1}=0
,
z_{2}=s
and
f=f(0,s):=\psi_{0}(s)
with a parameter
s
. Therefore, it is easy to see that
z_{1}=t,\;z_{2}=-t+s,

and

\displaystyle f(t,s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}[(D_{11}-E_{11})\cos(\alpha_{1}z_{1}+\alpha_{2}z_{2}+B)-(D_{12}+E_{12})\sin(\alpha_{1}z_{1}+\alpha_{2}z_{2}+B)]dt

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}(T_{1}-T_{2})dt+\psi_{0}(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}((D_{11}-E_{11})\cos[(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})t+\alpha_{2}s+B]-(D_{12}+E_{12})\sin[(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})t+\alpha_{2}s+B])dt

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}(T_{1}-T_{2})dt+\psi_{0}(s)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(D_{11}-E_{11})}{(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})}\sin[(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})t+\alpha_{2}s+B]+\dfrac{(D_{12}+E_{12})}{(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})}\cos[(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})t+\alpha_{2}s+B]

\displaystyle\quad+(T_{1}-T_{2})t+\psi_{1}(s)

where
\psi_{1}(s)
is a transcendental entire function with finite order in
s
such that

\displaystyle\psi_{1}(s)=\psi_{0}(s)-\dfrac{(D_{11}-E_{11})}{(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})}\sin(\alpha_{2}s+B)-\dfrac{(D_{12}+E_{12})}{(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})}\cos(\alpha_{2}s+B).

Therefore, we have

(4.68)

\displaystyle f(z_{1},z_{2})

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(D_{11}-E_{11})}{(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})}\sin(\alpha_{1}z_{1}+\alpha_{2}z_{2}+B)+\dfrac{(D_{12}+E_{12})}{(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})}\cos(\alpha_{1}z_{1}+\alpha_{2}z_{2}+B)

\displaystyle+(T_{1}-T_{2})z_{1}+\psi_{1}(s).

which can be written as

\displaystyle f(z_{1},z_{2})=\dfrac{(D_{11}-E_{11})}{(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})}\sin(L(z)+B)+\dfrac{(D_{12}+E_{12})}{(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})}\cos(L(z)+B)+(T_{1}-T_{2})z_{1}+\psi_{1}(s).

Substituting (
4.68
) into (
4.57
), we can obtain that
\psi_{1}(s)
satisfies

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime}_{1}(s)+\psi_{1}(s+s_{0})

\displaystyle=\left(\dfrac{E_{11}\alpha_{1}-D_{11}\alpha_{2}}{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}}\right)\cos(L(z)+B)+\left(\dfrac{E_{12}\alpha_{1}-D_{12}\alpha_{2}}{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}}\right)\sin(L(z)+B)

\displaystyle-\left(\dfrac{D_{11}-E_{11}}{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}}\right)\sin(L(z)+L(c)+B)-\left(\dfrac{D_{12}-E_{12}}{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}}\right)\cos(L(z)+L(c)+B)

\displaystyle-(T_{1}-T_{2})(z_{1}+c)+T_{2}.

This completes the proof of the Theorem
3.4
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.5
.

Suppose that
f
is a transcendental entire solution of (
3.5
) with finite order. The equation (
3.5
) can be written as

(4.69)

\displaystyle\left(\dfrac{DA^{\pm}}{-\Delta}\right)u^{2}+\left(\dfrac{DB^{\mp}}{-\Delta}\right)v^{2}=1

Now we will discuss two cases below.

Case 1
: If
\sqrt{\frac{DA^{\pm}}{-\Delta}}u
is a constant, then it follows from (
4.69
) that
\sqrt{\frac{DB^{\mp}}{-\Delta}}v
is also constant. Denote

\displaystyle\sqrt{\dfrac{DA^{\pm}}{-\Delta}}u=K_{9},\;\;\;\;\sqrt{\dfrac{DB^{\mp}}{-\Delta}}v=K_{10}

In view of (
4.69
), it is easy to see that
K^{2}_{9}+K^{2}_{10}=1
. So we have

(4.70)

\displaystyle M_{1}(f)=f(z+c)+\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}=\dfrac{K_{9}\xi^{\pm}_{1}}{\sqrt{\frac{DA^{\pm}}{-\Delta}}}-\dfrac{K_{10}\eta^{\pm}_{1}}{\sqrt{\frac{DB^{\mp}}{-\Delta}}}+\dfrac{\alpha\gamma-b\beta}{ab-\alpha^{2}}:=A_{5},

(4.71)

\displaystyle M_{3}(f)=f(z+c)+\dfrac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial z^{2}_{1}}=\dfrac{K_{9}\eta^{\pm}_{1}}{\sqrt{\frac{DA^{\pm}}{-\Delta}}}+\dfrac{K_{10}\xi^{\pm}_{1}}{\sqrt{\frac{DB^{\mp}}{-\Delta}}}+\dfrac{\alpha\beta-a\gamma}{ab-\alpha^{2}}:=B_{5}.

A simple computation using (
4.70
) and (
4.71
) shows that

(4.72)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}-\dfrac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial z^{2}_{1}}=A_{5}-B_{5}.

Differentiating (
4.70
) with respect to
z_{1}
we obtain

(4.73)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial f(z+c)}{\partial z_{1}}+\dfrac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial z^{2}_{1}}=0

Thus it follows from (
4.71
) and (
4.73
) that

(4.74)

\displaystyle f(z+c)-\dfrac{\partial f(z+c)}{\partial z_{1}}=B_{5}

Differentiating (
4.74
) with respect to
z_{1}
, we obtain

(4.75)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial f(z+c)}{\partial z_{1}}-\dfrac{\partial^{2}f(z+c)}{\partial z^{2}_{1}}=0

In view of (
4.72
) and (
4.75
) it is easy to see that
A_{5}=B_{5}
. Using
K^{2}_{9}+K^{2}_{10}=1
,
A_{5}=B_{5}
and from (
4.70
), (
4.71
), a simple computation shows that
K_{2}=\frac{-R_{2}R_{3}\pm R_{1}\sqrt{R^{2}_{1}+R^{2}_{2}-R^{2}_{3}}}{R^{2}_{1}+R^{2}_{2}}
, where

\displaystyle R_{1}=\dfrac{\xi^{\pm}_{1}-\eta^{\pm}_{1}}{\sqrt{\frac{DA^{\pm}}{-\Delta}}},\;R_{2}=\dfrac{\xi^{\pm}_{1}+\eta^{\pm}_{1}}{\sqrt{\frac{DB^{\mp}}{-\Delta}}}\;\mbox{and}\;R_{3}=\dfrac{\beta(\alpha+b)-\gamma(\alpha+a)}{ab-\alpha^{2}}.

In view of (
4.72
), using
A_{5}=B_{5}
, we obtain

(4.76)

\displaystyle f(z_{1},z_{2})=e^{z_{1}+\psi_{1}(z_{2})}+\psi_{2}(z_{2}).

where
\psi_{1}(z_{2})
,
\psi_{2}(z_{2})
are two function in
z_{2}
. From (
4.70
) and (
4.76
), it follows that
e^{(z_{1}+c_{1})+\psi_{1}(z_{2}+c_{2})}+e^{z_{1}+\psi_{1}(z_{2})}+\psi_{2}(z_{2}+c_{2})=A_{5}

which implies that
\psi_{2}(z_{2})=A_{5},\;\;\psi_{1}(z_{2})=Az_{2}+B,\;\;\mbox{where}\;\;A=\frac{2k\pi i\pm\pi i-c_{1}}{c_{2}}

Therefore, from (
4.76
) we obtain
f(z_{1},z_{2})=A_{5}+e^{(z_{1}+Az_{2}+B)}.

Case 2
: If
\sqrt{\frac{DA^{\pm}}{-\Delta}}u
is not a constant. By the similar arguments being used in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, there exists an entire function
h(z)
such that

(4.77)

\displaystyle M_{1}(f)=f(z+c)+\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}=D_{11}\cos h(z)-D_{12}\sin h(z)+T_{1},

(4.78)

\displaystyle M_{3}(f)=f(z+c)+\dfrac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial z^{2}_{1}}=E_{11}\cos h(z)+E_{12}\sin h(z)+T_{2}.

In view of (
4.77
) and (
4.78
), we obtain

(4.79)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}-\dfrac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial z^{2}_{1}}=(D_{11}-E_{11})\cos h(z)-(D_{12}+E_{12})\sin h(z)+(T_{1}-T_{2}).

Differentiating (
4.77
) with respect to
z_{1}
, we obtain

(4.80)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial f(z+c)}{\partial z_{1}}+\dfrac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial z^{2}_{1}}=-D_{11}\sin h(z)\dfrac{\partial h}{\partial z_{1}}-D_{12}\cos h(z)\dfrac{\partial h}{\partial z_{1}}.

Thus it follows from (
4.78
) and (
4.80
) that

(4.81)

\displaystyle f(z+c)-\dfrac{\partial f(z+c)}{\partial z_{1}}=\left(D_{12}\dfrac{\partial h}{\partial z_{1}}+E_{11}\right)\cos h(z)+\left(D_{11}\dfrac{\partial h}{\partial z_{1}}+E_{12}\right)\sin h(z)+T_{2}

Differentiating (
4.81
) with respect to
z_{1}
and using (
4.79
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left(D_{11}\dfrac{\partial^{2}h}{\partial z^{2}_{1}}-D_{12}\left(\dfrac{\partial h}{\partial z_{1}}\right)^{2}-E_{11}\dfrac{\partial h}{\partial z_{1}}\right)\sin h(z)+\left(D_{12}\dfrac{\partial^{2}h}{\partial z^{2}_{1}}+D_{11}\left(\dfrac{\partial h}{\partial z_{1}}\right)^{2}+E_{12}\dfrac{\partial h}{\partial z_{1}}\right)\cos h(z)

\displaystyle=(D_{11}-E_{11})\cos h(z+c)-(D_{12}+E_{12})\sin h(z+c)+(T_{1}-T_{2}).

This can be expressed as the following

(4.82)

\displaystyle\left(\dfrac{\{(D_{12}-iD_{11})+(E_{12}+iE_{11})\}e^{ih(z+c)}+2i(T_{2}-T_{1})}{(iD_{11}+D_{12})-(iE_{11}-E_{12})}\right)e^{ih(z+c)}

\displaystyle+\left(\dfrac{(D_{11}+iD_{12})\dfrac{\partial^{2}h}{\partial z^{2}_{1}}+(iD_{11}-D_{12})\left(\dfrac{\partial h}{\partial z_{1}}\right)^{2}-(E_{11}-iE_{12})\dfrac{\partial h}{\partial z_{1}}}{(iD_{11}+D_{12})-(iE_{11}-E_{12})}\right)e^{i[h(z+c)+h(z)]}

\displaystyle+\left(\dfrac{(iD_{12}-D_{11})\dfrac{\partial h}{\partial z_{2}}+(iD_{11}+D_{12})\left(\dfrac{\partial h}{\partial z_{1}}\right)^{2}+(iE_{12}+E_{11})\dfrac{\partial h}{\partial z_{1}}}{(iD_{11}+D_{12})-(iE_{11}-E_{12})}\right)e^{i[h(z+c)-h(z)]}=1.

If
\left(\frac{(D_{11}+iD_{12})\frac{\partial^{2}h}{\partial z^{2}_{1}}+(iD_{11}-D_{12})\left(\frac{\partial h}{\partial z_{1}}\right)^{2}-(E_{11}-iE_{12})\frac{\partial h}{\partial z_{1}}}{(iD_{11}+D_{12})-(iE_{11}-E_{12})}\right)=0
, then from (
4.82
) it is easy to see that
\left(\frac{(iD_{12}-D_{11})\frac{\partial h}{\partial z_{2}}+(iD_{11}+D_{12})\left(\frac{\partial h}{\partial z_{1}}\right)^{2}+(iE_{12}+E_{11})\frac{\partial h}{\partial z_{1}}}{(iD_{11}+D_{12})-(iE_{11}-E_{12})}\right)\neq 0
. Otherwise, it follows that
\left(\frac{\{(D_{12}-iD_{11})+(E_{12}+iE_{11})\}e^{ih(z+c)}+2i(T_{2}-T_{1})}{(iD_{11}+D_{12})-(iE_{11}-E_{12})}\right)=1
, thus, a simple computation shows that
e^{ih(z+c)}=\frac{i(T_{1}-T_{2})\pm i\sqrt{(T_{2}-T_{1})^{2}+[(D_{12}-iD_{11})+(E_{12}+iE_{11})][(iE_{11}-E_{12})-(iD_{11}+D_{12})]}}{(D_{12}-iD_{11})+(E_{12}+iE_{11})}
, which implies
h(z)
is a constant. Thus, the equation (
4.82
) reduces to

\displaystyle\left(\dfrac{\{(D_{12}-iD_{11})+(E_{12}+iE_{11})\}e^{ih(z+c)}+2i(T_{2}-T_{1})}{(iD_{11}+D_{12})-(iE_{11}-E_{12})}\right)e^{ih(z+c)}

\displaystyle+\left(\dfrac{(iD_{12}-D_{11})\dfrac{\partial h}{\partial z_{2}}+(iD_{11}+D_{12})\left(\dfrac{\partial h}{\partial z_{1}}\right)^{2}+(iE_{12}+E_{11})\dfrac{\partial h}{\partial z_{1}}}{(iD_{11}+D_{12})-(iE_{11}-E_{12})}\right)e^{i[h(z+c)-h(z)]}=1.

By the similar argument being used previously, in view of the Second Fundamental Theorem of Nevanlinna for several complex variables, we obtain
T(r,e^{2ih(z+c)})=o\left(T(r,e^{h})\right)
which is a contradiction. Hence,
\frac{(D_{11}+iD_{12})\frac{\partial^{2}h}{\partial z^{2}_{1}}+(iD_{11}-D_{12})\left(\frac{\partial h}{\partial z_{1}}\right)^{2}-(E_{11}-iE_{12})\frac{\partial h}{\partial z_{1}}}{(iD_{11}+D_{12})-(iE_{11}-E_{12})}\neq 0
. Similarly, we obtain
\frac{(iD_{12}-D_{11})\frac{\partial h}{\partial z_{2}}+(iD_{11}+D_{12})\left(\frac{\partial h}{\partial z_{1}}\right)^{2}+(iE_{12}+E_{11})\frac{\partial h}{\partial z_{1}}}{(iD_{11}+D_{12})-(iE_{11}-E_{12})}\neq 0
. Therefore, in view of the Lemma
4.3
using (
4.82
), we obtain

(4.83)

\displaystyle\left(\dfrac{(iD_{12}-D_{11})\dfrac{\partial h}{\partial z_{2}}+(iD_{11}+D_{12})\left(\dfrac{\partial h}{\partial z_{1}}\right)^{2}+(iE_{12}+E_{11})\dfrac{\partial h}{\partial z_{1}}}{(iD_{11}+D_{12})-(iE_{11}-E_{12})}\right)e^{i[h(z+c)-h(z)]}\equiv 1.

From (
4.82
) and (
4.83
) it follows that

(4.84)

\displaystyle\left(\dfrac{(D_{11}+iD_{12})\dfrac{\partial^{2}h}{\partial z^{2}_{1}}+(iD_{11}-D_{12})\left(\dfrac{\partial h}{\partial z_{1}}\right)^{2}-(E_{11}-iE_{12})\dfrac{\partial h}{\partial z_{1}}}{(iD_{11}+D_{12})-(iE_{11}-E_{12})}\right)e^{i[h(z)-h(z+c)]}\equiv 1.

Since
h(z)
is a polynomial, then equation (
4.83
)(or (
4.84
)) implies
h(z+c)-h(z)=\eta
, where
\eta
is a constant in
\mathbb{C}
. Therefore, it follows that
h(z)=L(z)+H(z)+B
, where
L(z)=\alpha_{1}z_{1}+\alpha_{2}z_{2}
,
H(z):=H(s_{1})
,
H(s_{1})
is a polynomial in
s_{1}=c_{2}z_{1}-c_{1}z_{2}
. Now, we show that
H(z)\equiv 0
. From (
4.62
) and (
4.63
), it follows that

(4.85)

\displaystyle(iD_{11}+D_{12})\alpha^{2}_{1}+(iE_{12}+E_{11})\alpha_{1}+c_{2}[2\alpha_{1}(iD_{11}+D_{12})+(iE_{12}+E_{11})]H^{\prime}

\displaystyle+c^{2}_{2}(iD_{11}+D_{12})(H^{\prime})^{2}=e^{-iL(c)}[(iD_{11}+D_{12})-(iE_{11}-E_{12})]

and

(4.86)

\displaystyle(iD_{11}-D_{12})\alpha^{2}_{1}-(E_{11}-iE_{12})\alpha_{1}+c_{2}[2\alpha_{1}(iD_{11}-D_{12})-(E_{11}-iE_{12})]H^{\prime}

\displaystyle+c^{2}_{2}(iD_{11}-D_{12})(H^{\prime})^{2}=e^{iL(c)}[(iD_{11}+D_{12})-(iE_{11}-E_{12})].

Combining with the fact that
(c_{1},c_{2})\neq(0,0)
, it follows that
H^{\prime}
is a constant, that is
\deg_{s_{1}}H\leq 1
. Hence,
L(z)+H(z)+B
is still linear form of
\alpha_{1}z_{1}+\alpha_{2}z_{2}+B
, which means that
H(z)\equiv 0
. Therefore it is easy to see that
h(z)=L(z)+B=\alpha_{1}z_{1}+\alpha_{2}z_{2}+B
. Thus, the equations (
4.85
) and (
4.86
) become

(4.87)

\displaystyle\dfrac{(iD_{11}+D_{12})\alpha^{2}_{1}+(iE_{12}+E_{11})\alpha_{1}}{(iD_{11}+D_{12})-(iE_{11}-E_{12})}e^{iL(c)}=1,

and

(4.88)

\displaystyle\dfrac{(iD_{11}-D_{12})\alpha^{2}_{1}-(E_{11}-iE_{12})\alpha_{1}}{(iD_{11}+D_{12})-(iE_{11}-E_{12})}e^{-iL(c)}=1.

From (
4.87
) and (
4.88
), a simple computation shows that

\displaystyle e^{2iL(c)}=\dfrac{(iD_{11}-D_{12})\alpha_{1}-(E_{11}-iE_{12})}{(iD_{11}+D_{12})\alpha_{1}+(iE_{12}+E_{11})}

and

\displaystyle(D^{2}_{11}+D^{2}_{12})\alpha^{4}_{1}

\displaystyle+2(D_{11}E_{12}+D_{12}E_{11})\alpha^{3}_{1}+(E^{2}_{11}+E^{2}_{12})\alpha^{2}_{1}

\displaystyle+[(iD_{11}+D_{12})-(iE_{11}-E_{12})]=0.

The equation (
4.81
) can be written as

\displaystyle f(z+c)-\dfrac{\partial f(z+c)}{\partial z_{1}}

\displaystyle=\left(D_{12}\alpha_{1}+E_{11}\right)\cos(\alpha_{1}z_{1}+\alpha_{2}z_{2}+B)

\displaystyle+\left(D_{11}\alpha_{1}+E_{12}\right)\sin(\alpha_{1}z_{1}+\alpha_{2}z_{2}+B)+T_{2},

that is

(4.89)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}-f(z)

\displaystyle=-\left(D_{12}\alpha_{1}+E_{11}\right)\cos[L(z)-L(c)+B]

\displaystyle-\left(D_{11}\alpha_{1}+E_{12}\right)\sin[L(z)-L(c)+B]-T_{2},

Therefore, a simple computation shows that

(4.90)

\displaystyle f(z_{1},z_{2})=\phi(z_{2})e^{z_{1}}+R_{11}\sin[L(z)-L(c)+B]+R_{12}\cos[L(z)-L(c)+B],

where
\phi(z_{2})
is an entire function in
z_{2}
and
R_{11}=\frac{(D_{11}\alpha_{1}+E_{12})-\alpha_{1}(D_{12}\alpha_{1}+E_{11})}{\alpha^{2}_{1}+1},\;\;R_{12}=\frac{(D_{12}\alpha_{1}+E_{11})+\alpha_{1}(D_{11}\alpha_{1}+E_{12})}{\alpha^{2}_{1}+1}
. Substituting (
4.90
) into (
4.77
), it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\phi(z_{2}+c_{2})e^{c_{1}}e^{z_{1}}+(R_{11}+D_{12})\sin(L(z)+B)+(R_{12}-D_{11})\cos(L(z)+B)

\displaystyle+R_{11}\alpha_{1}\cos[L(z)-L(c)+B]-R_{12}\alpha_{1}\sin[L(z)-L(c)+B]=T_{1}-\phi(z_{2})e^{z_{1}}.

Comparing both side the coefficient of
e^{z_{1}}
we obtain
\phi(z_{2}+c_{2})=-e^{-c_{1}}\phi(z_{2}),

which implies that
\phi(z_{2})=(-e^{-c_{1}})^{\frac{z_{2}}{c_{2}}}\pi(z_{2}),\;\;\mbox{where}\;\;\pi(z_{2}+c_{2})=\pi(z_{2}).
Therefore, from (
4.90
), we see that the precise form of the solution is

\displaystyle f(z_{1},z_{2})=(-e^{-c_{1}})^{\frac{z_{2}}{c_{2}}}\pi(z_{2})e^{z_{1}}+R_{11}\sin[L(z)-L(c)+B]+R_{12}\cos[L(z)-L(c)+B].

This completes the proof of the Theorem
3.5
.

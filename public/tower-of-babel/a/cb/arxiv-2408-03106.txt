Hybrid approach to perfect and dissipative spin hydrodynamics
Zbigniew Drogosz, Wojciech Florkowski, Mykhailo Hontarenko

Abstract
A hybrid framework of spin hydrodynamics is proposed that combines the results of kinetic theory for particles with spin 1/2 with the Israel-Stewart method of introducing nonequilibrium dynamics. The framework of kinetic theory is used to define the perfect-fluid description that conserves baryon number, energy, linear momentum and spin part of angular momentum. This leads to the entropy conservation although, in the presence of spin degrees of freedom, the perfect-fluid formalism includes extra terms whose structure is usually attributed to dissipation. The genuine dissipative terms appear from the condition of positive entropy production in nonequilibrium processes. They are responsible for the transfer between the spin and orbital parts of angular momentum, with the total angular momentum being conserved.

II
Thermodynamic relations

In this section, we review the relativistic forms of thermodynamic relations used in relativistic hydrodynamics. We start with the standard case without spin degrees of freedom and then switch to a popular form used in many formulations of spin hydrodynamics. The latter approach is often called the phenomenological version of spin thermodynamic relations. In the next sections, we demonstrate that it must be modified to be consistent with the results of microscopic calculations obtained within the kinetic theory.

II.1
Spinless case

The standard thermodynamic relations used in relativistic hydrodynamics include the identity

\displaystyle\varepsilon+P=T\sigma+\mu n

(1)

and the first law of thermodynamics

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\varepsilon=T\mathrm{d}\sigma+\mu\mathrm{d}n.

(2)

Here the letters
\varepsilon
,
P
,
T
,
\sigma
,
\mu
and
n
denote the local energy density, pressure, temperature, entropy density, baryon chemical potential, and baryon number density, respectively. Equation (
1
) follows from the extensivity of energy, entropy and baryon number. Equations (
1
) and (
2
) imply the Gibbs-Duhem relation

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}P=\sigma\mathrm{d}T+n\mathrm{d}\mu.

(3)

Following the seminal works of Israel and Stewart, one often rewrites Eqs. (
1
)–(
2
) in a tensor (four-vector) form, which is convenient for further incorporation of dissipative phenomena. This is done by multiplication of Eqs. (
1
)–(
3
) by the local four-velocity of the fluid
u^{\mu}
, which leads to the following expressions

\displaystyle S^{\mu}_{\rm eq}=\sigma u^{\mu}=P\beta^{\mu}-\xi N^{\mu}_{\rm eq}+\beta_{\lambda}T^{\lambda\mu}_{\rm eq},

(4)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}S^{\mu}_{\rm eq}=-\xi\mathrm{d}N^{\mu}_{\rm eq}+\beta_{\lambda}\mathrm{d}T^{\lambda\mu}_{\rm eq},

(5)

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}(P\beta^{\mu})=N^{\mu}_{\rm eq}\mathrm{d}\xi-T^{\lambda\mu}_{\rm eq}\mathrm{d}\beta_{\lambda}.

(6)

Here we have introduced a common notation,

\displaystyle\beta^{\mu}=\frac{u^{\mu}}{T},\quad\beta=\sqrt{\beta^{\lambda}\beta_{\lambda}}=\frac{1}{T},\quad\xi=\frac{\mu}{T}.

(7)

The four-vector
S^{\mu}_{\rm eq}
represents the entropy current, while
N^{\mu}_{\rm eq}
and
T^{\lambda\mu}_{\rm eq}
are the baryon current and energy-momentum tensors for a perfect fluid

\displaystyle N^{\mu}_{\rm eq}=nu^{\mu}

(8)

and

\displaystyle T^{\lambda\mu}_{\rm eq}=(\varepsilon+P)u^{\lambda}u^{\mu}-Pg^{\lambda\mu}=\varepsilon u^{\lambda}u^{\mu}-P\Delta^{\lambda\mu},

(9)

where the tensor

\displaystyle\Delta^{\lambda\mu}=g^{\lambda\mu}-u^{\lambda}u^{\mu}

(10)

projects on the space orthogonal to flow. Below, we also introduce the projector
\Delta^{\mu\nu}_{\alpha\beta}
defined by the expression

\displaystyle\Delta^{\mu\nu}_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\Delta^{\mu}_{\alpha}\Delta^{\nu}_{\beta}+\Delta^{\nu}_{\alpha}\Delta^{\mu}_{\beta}-\frac{2}{3}\Delta^{\mu\nu}\Delta_{\alpha\beta}\right).

(11)

The contraction
\Delta^{\mu\nu}_{\alpha\beta}A^{\alpha\beta}\equiv A^{\langle\alpha\beta\rangle}
picks up the orthogonal, symmetric, and traceless part of the tensor
A^{\alpha\beta}
. We also introduce the standard notation where round (squared) brackets denote symmetrization (antisymmetrization) of the Lorentz indices:
A^{(\mu\nu)}=\frac{1}{2}(A^{\mu\nu}+A^{\nu\mu})
and
A^{[\mu\nu]}=\frac{1}{2}(A^{\mu\nu}-A^{\nu\mu})
. Moreover, we introduce an orthogonal projection
T^{\alpha\beta\langle\gamma\rangle\delta...}=\Delta^{\gamma}_{\rho}T^{\alpha\beta\rho\delta...}
. If the symbols
\langle\rangle
are used to the right of the differential symbol, the contractions with
\Delta
’s should be done after the derivative is calculated first. The transverse gradient
\nabla^{\mu}
is defined by contraction
\Delta^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}
.

II.2
Including spin degrees of freedom—phenomenological version

Many approaches to relativistic spin hydrodynamics extend Eqs. (
1
)–(
3
) to include the tensor spin chemical potential
\Omega_{\alpha\beta}
and the spin density tensor
S^{\alpha\beta}
(which are both rank-2 antisymmetric tensors). They read

\displaystyle\varepsilon+P=T\sigma+\mu n+\frac{1}{2}\Omega_{\alpha\beta}S^{\alpha\beta},

(12)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\varepsilon=T\mathrm{d}\sigma+\mu\mathrm{d}n+\frac{1}{2}\Omega_{\alpha\beta}\mathrm{d}S^{\alpha\beta},

(13)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}P=\sigma\mathrm{d}T+n\mathrm{d}\mu+\frac{1}{2}S^{\alpha\beta}\mathrm{d}\Omega_{\alpha\beta}.

(14)

By multiplying Eqs. (
12
)–(
14
) by the hydrodynamic flow vector
u^{\mu}
, we obtain

\displaystyle S^{\mu}_{\rm eq}=P\beta^{\mu}-\xi N^{\mu}_{\rm eq}+\beta_{\lambda}T^{\lambda\mu}_{\rm eq}-\frac{1}{2}\omega_{\alpha\beta}S^{\mu,\alpha\beta}_{\rm eq},

(15)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}S^{\mu}_{\rm eq}=-\xi\mathrm{d}N^{\mu}_{\rm eq}+\beta_{\lambda}\mathrm{d}T^{\lambda\mu}_{\rm eq}-\frac{1}{2}\omega_{\alpha\beta}\mathrm{d}S^{\mu,\alpha\beta}_{\rm eq},

(16)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}(P\beta^{\mu})=N^{\mu}_{\rm eq}\mathrm{d}\xi-T^{\lambda\mu}_{\rm eq}\mathrm{d}\beta_{\lambda}+\frac{1}{2}S^{\mu,\alpha\beta}_{\rm eq}\mathrm{d}\omega_{\alpha\beta}.

(17)

The spin polarization tensor that appears here is defined as the ratio

\displaystyle\omega_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{\Omega_{\alpha\beta}}{T}.

(18)

Below we use the parametrization introduced in
[
12
]

\displaystyle\omega_{\alpha\beta}=k_{\alpha}u_{\beta}-k_{\beta}u_{\alpha}+t_{\alpha\beta},

(19)

where

\displaystyle t_{\alpha\beta}=\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}u^{\gamma}\omega^{\delta},

(20)

and the four-vectors
k
and
\omega
are orthogonal to the flow vector
u
(i.e.,
k\cdot u=0
and
\omega\cdot u=0
). One can easily check the following property
1
1

1

We use a colon to denote contraction of two rank-2 tensors, e.g.,
A:B=A_{\alpha\beta}B^{\alpha\beta}
. Note the order of the indices.

\displaystyle\omega:\omega\equiv\omega^{\alpha\beta}\omega_{\alpha\beta}=2(k^{2}-\omega^{2}).

(21)

Below, we also frequently use the definition

\displaystyle t^{\mu}=t^{\mu\nu}k_{\nu}=\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}k_{\nu}u_{\alpha}\omega_{\beta}.

(22)

The four-vector
t
is orthogonal to the vectors
u,k
and
\omega
. In the local rest frame (LRF), where
u^{\mu}=(1,0,0,0)
, one finds that
{\bm{t}}={\bm{k}}\times{\bm{\omega}}
. Moreover, in Eqs. (
15
)–(
17
) we have introduced the spin tensor
S^{\mu,\alpha\beta}_{\rm eq}
defined by the expression

\displaystyle S^{\mu,\alpha\beta}_{\rm eq}=u^{\mu}S^{\alpha\beta},

(23)

which is an analog of the perfect-fluid forms of
N^{\mu}_{\rm eq}
and
T^{\lambda\mu}_{\rm eq}
given by Eqs. (
8
) and (
9
).

Equation (
16
) as a direct consequence implies the entropy conservation for a system that conserves baryon number, energy, linear momentum and spin, that is to say, the conservation laws
\partial_{\mu}N^{\mu}_{\rm eq}=0
,
\partial_{\mu}T^{\mu\lambda}_{\rm eq}=0
, and
\partial_{\mu}S^{\mu,\alpha\beta}_{\rm eq}~=~0
imply
\partial_{\mu}S^{\mu}_{\rm eq}=0
. We note that the spin conservation is a direct consequence of using a symmetric energy-momentum tensor in the considered formalism. In general, only the total angular momentum is conserved,

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}J^{\mu,\alpha\beta}=0,

(24)

with
J
given as a sum of the orbital (
L
) and spin (
S
) parts

\displaystyle J^{\mu,\alpha\beta}=x^{\alpha}T^{\mu\beta}-x^{\beta}T^{\mu\alpha}+S^{\mu,\alpha\beta}\equiv L^{\mu,\alpha\beta}+S^{\mu,\alpha\beta}.

(25)

The last equation implies

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}S^{\mu,\alpha\beta}=T^{\beta\alpha}-T^{\alpha\beta}.

(26)

Thus, we find that the divergence of the spin tensor is determined by the antisymmetric part of the energy-momentum tensor. The latter vanishes in our case (for perfect fluid).

At this point it is important to note that the use of the expression (
23
) can be traced back to the very first model of a spinning fluid by Weyssenhoff and Raabe
[
53
]
. It was also used in Ref.
[
12
]
, where the first formulation of relativistic hydrodynamics for particles with spin
{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}
was proposed. The form (
23
) has been subsequently used in many works that followed the methods of Israel and Stewart (positivity of the entropy current) to construct the framework of dissipative spin hydrodynamics
[
28
,
29
,
32
,
31
,
36
,
39
]
.

Despite the numerous applications of Eq. (
23
), its structure is not justified by microscopic calculations; see, for example, Refs.
[
54
,
13
,
50
]
. The way to solve this problem is to introduce generalized thermodynamic relations as in Ref.
[
52
]
. In this work we further develop these ideas.

III
Kinetic-theory results

In this section, we discuss the state of local thermodynamic equilibrium as defined within the framework of kinetic theory describing particles with spin
{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}
. The framework of perfect spin hydrodynamics is based on a combination of two concepts: local equilibrium for particles with spin and the conservation laws for the baryon number, energy, linear momentum and the spin part of the angular momentum.

Our kinetic-theory approach uses a classical description of spin. It has been shown in
[
50

that for small values of the polarization tensor
\omega_{\mu\nu}
the expression for the spin tensor obtained with classical treatment of spin is consistent with the result obtained from the calculations using a semiclassical expansion of the Wigner function. It was also found in
[

that the classical spin treatment guarantees that the value of the Pauli-Lubański vector—as expected—never exceeds the value of
\sqrt{3/4}
. This is in contrast with the popular approach based on the Wigner function, which allows for infinite spin polarization

III.1
Classical spin description

In the classical treatment of spin
[
55
,
56
]
, one introduces the particle internal angular momentum tensor
s^{\alpha\beta}
defined by the formula

\displaystyle s^{\alpha\beta}=\frac{1}{m}\epsilon^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}p_{\gamma}s_{\delta}.

(27)

Here
p
is the particle four-momentum satisfying the on-mass-shell condition
p^{\mu}p_{\mu}=m^{2}
(with
m
being the particle mass) and
s
is the particle spin four-vector. Equation (
27
) implies that
s^{\alpha\beta}=-s^{\beta\alpha}
and
s^{\alpha\beta}p_{\beta}=0
. The spin four-vector is orthogonal to four-momentum
s\cdot p=0
, hence we can write

\displaystyle s^{\alpha}=\frac{1}{2m}\,\epsilon^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}p_{\beta}s_{\gamma\delta}.

(28)

In the particle rest frame (PRF), where
p^{\mu}=(m,0,0,0)
, the four-vector
s^{\alpha}
has only space components,
s^{\alpha}=(0,{\bm{s}}_{*})
, with the normalization
|{\bm{s}}_{*}|=\,{\gothfamily s:}
. For particles with spin
{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}
we use the value of the Casimir operator
\,{\gothfamily s:}^{2}={\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\left(1+{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right)={\nicefrac{{3}}{{4}}}
.

The basic object used in the kinetic theory is the phase-space distribution function
f(x,{\bm{p}})
. For particles with spin,
f(x,{\bm{p}})
is generalized to a spin-dependent distribution
f(x,{\bm{p}},s)
. One commonly uses the notation
f(x,p,s)
for
f(x,{\bm{p}},s)
remembering that the energy
p^{0}
is on the mass shell, namely,
p^{0}=E_{p}=\sqrt{m^{2}+{\bm{p}}^{2}}
. Alongside the distribution function, we introduce the integration measures in momentum space

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}P=\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}E_{p}}

(29)

and spin space
[
50
]

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}S=\frac{m}{\pi\,{\gothfamily s:}}\,\mathrm{d}^{4}s\,\delta(s\cdot s+\,{\gothfamily s:}^{2})\,\delta(p\cdot s).

(30)

More discussion of the measure and corresponding integrals is given in Appendix B.

III.2
Equilibrium distribution functions and currents

In local equilibrium, the spin-dependent distribution functions for particles (
+
) and antiparticles (
-
) have the Fermi-Dirac form

\displaystyle f^{\pm}_{\rm eq}(x,p,s)=\left[\exp\left(\mp\xi(x)+p\cdot\beta(x)-\frac{1}{2}\,\omega(x):s\right)+1\right]^{-1}.

(31)

We shall also use the compact notation

\displaystyle f^{\pm}_{\rm eq}=\frac{1}{e^{y^{\pm}}+1}

(32)

with

\displaystyle y^{\pm}=\mp\xi(x)+p\cdot\beta(x)-\frac{1}{2}\omega(x):s.

(33)

Equation (
31
) follows from the microscopic picture that baryon number, energy, linear momentum and the spin part of angular momentum are conserved in particle binary collisions. Similarly as the conservation of the baryon charge introduces a Lagrange multiplier that is the baryon chemical potential
\mu=T\xi
, the conservation of
s^{\alpha\beta}
introduces six Lagrange multipliers grouped into a tensor spin chemical potential
\Omega_{\alpha\beta}=T\omega_{\alpha\beta}
. The arguments for this form of the local equilibrium function (in the case of Boltzmann statistics) were originally given in Sec. 6.3 of Ref.
[
50
]
. Subsequent important developments by the Frankfurt group, see Refs.
[
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
]
, allowed for the inclusion of nonlocal collisions.

The macroscopic currents and tensors are obtained as moments of the distribution functions. In this way we obtain the baryon current

\displaystyle N^{\mu}_{\rm eq}=\int\mathrm{d}P\,\mathrm{d}S\,p^{\mu}\,\left[f_{\rm eq}^{+}(x,p,s)-f_{\rm eq}^{-}(x,p,s)\right],

(34)

the energy-momentum tensor

\displaystyle T^{\mu\nu}_{\rm eq}=\int\mathrm{d}P\,\mathrm{d}S\,p^{\mu}p^{\nu}\,\left[f_{\rm eq}^{+}(x,p,s)+f_{\rm eq}^{-}(x,p,s)\right],

(35)

and the spin tensor

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636ptS^{\lambda,\mu\nu}_{\rm eq}\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\int\!\mathrm{d}P\,\mathrm{d}S\,\,p^{\lambda}\,s^{\mu\nu}\left[f_{\rm eq}^{+}(x,p,s)+f_{\rm eq}^{-}(x,p,s)\right].

(36)

In addition, we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{\mu}\!=\!-\int\mathrm{d}P\,\mathrm{d}S\,p^{\mu}\,\left[\ln(1-f_{\rm eq}^{+})+\ln(1-f_{\rm eq}^{-})\right].

(37)

In the traditional hydrodynamics, the current
\mathcal{N}^{\mu}
can be directly expressed by local pressure and hydrodynamic flow,
\mathcal{N}^{\mu}=P\beta^{\mu}
, see Appendix
C
. However, this is not the case for spin hydrodynamics. Finally, we introduce the entropy current
[
57
]

\displaystyle\begin{split}S^{\mu}_{\rm eq}&=-\int\mathrm{d}P\,\mathrm{d}S\,p^{\mu}\,\left[f_{\rm eq}^{+}\ln{f_{\rm eq}^{+}}-f_{\rm eq}^{+}\ln(1-f_{\rm eq}^{+})+\ln(1-f_{\rm eq}^{+})\right]\\
&-\int\mathrm{d}P\,\mathrm{d}S\,p^{\mu}\left[\,f_{\rm eq}^{-}\ln{f_{\rm eq}^{-}}-f_{\rm eq}^{-}\ln(1-f_{\rm eq}^{-})+\ln(1-f_{\rm eq}^{-})\right].\end{split}

(38)

Using the identity

\displaystyle f_{\rm eq}\ln f_{\rm eq}-f_{\rm eq}\ln(1-f_{\rm eq})=-yf_{\rm eq},

(39)

one can show that the entropy current
S^{\mu}_{\rm eq}
can be expressed as a linear combination of other tensors and currents. Inserting (
39
) into (
38
), we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}S^{\mu}_{\rm eq}&=\int\mathrm{d}P\,\mathrm{d}S\,p^{\mu}\left[f_{\rm eq}^{+}y^{+}-f_{\rm eq}^{-}y^{-}\right]-\int\mathrm{d}P\,\mathrm{d}S\,p^{\mu}\left[\ln(1-f_{\rm eq}^{+})+\ln(1-f_{\rm eq}^{-})\right]\\
&=\int\mathrm{d}P\,\mathrm{d}S\,p^{\mu}\left[f_{\rm eq}^{+}\left(-\xi+p\cdot\beta-{\scriptstyle{\frac{1}{2}}}\omega:s\right)+f_{\rm eq}^{-}\left(\xi+p\cdot\beta-{\scriptstyle{\frac{1}{2}}}\omega:s\right)\right]+\mathcal{N}^{\mu}\end{split}

(40)

or

\displaystyle S^{\mu}_{\rm eq}=-N^{\mu}_{\rm eq}\xi+T^{\mu\alpha}_{\rm eq}\beta_{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}S^{\mu,\alpha\beta}_{\rm eq}\omega_{\alpha\beta}+\mathcal{N}^{\mu}.

(41)

The last equation is almost identical to Eq. (
15
), however, as noted above, the relation
\mathcal{N}^{\mu}=P\beta^{\mu}
does not hold for particles with spin. This property has been recently emphasized in Ref.
[
52
]
for the case of the classical statistics. In this work, we find that Eq. (
41
) holds also for the Fermi-Dirac statistics if the current
\mathcal{N}^{\mu}
is defined by Eq. (
37
). In the future it would be interesting to analyze if Eq. (
41
) can be alternatively derived using the concepts introduced in Ref.
[
58
]
.

III.3
Generalized thermodynamic relations

For the distribution function of the form (
32
), one finds useful relations

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}f_{\rm eq}\!=\!-\frac{e^{y}}{(e^{y}+1)^{2}}\mathrm{d}y=-\frac{1}{e^{y}+1}\,\frac{e^{y}+1-1}{e^{y}+1}\,\mathrm{d}y=-f_{\rm eq}(1-f_{\rm eq})\mathrm{d}y,

(42)

They can be used to obtain the expression for
d\mathcal{N}^{\mu}
directly from (
37
), namely,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{N}^{\mu}&=-\mathrm{d}\int\mathrm{d}P\,\mathrm{d}S\,p^{\mu}\,\left[\ln(1-f_{\rm eq}^{+})+\ln(1-f_{\rm eq}^{-})\right]=\int\mathrm{d}P\,\mathrm{d}S\,p^{\mu}\,(f_{\rm eq}^{+}\!-\!f_{\rm eq}^{-})\mathrm{d}\xi\\
&-\!\!\!\int\mathrm{d}P\,\mathrm{d}S\,p^{\mu}p^{\alpha}(f_{\rm eq}^{+}\!+\!f_{\rm eq}^{-})\mathrm{d}\beta_{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}\int\mathrm{d}P\,\mathrm{d}S\,p^{\mu}s^{\alpha\beta}(f_{\rm eq}^{+}\!+\!f_{\rm eq}^{-})\mathrm{d}\omega_{\alpha\beta},\end{split}

(43)

which leads to

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\mathcal{N}^{\mu}=N^{\mu}_{\rm eq}\mathrm{d}\xi-T^{\mu\alpha}_{\rm eq}\mathrm{d}\beta_{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}S^{\mu,\alpha\beta}_{\rm eq}\mathrm{d}\omega_{\alpha\beta}.

(44)

Finally, we use the form (
38
) to calculate
\mathrm{d}S^{\mu}_{\rm eq}
. Combining the obtained result with
\mathrm{d}\mathcal{N}^{\mu}
given by (
44
), we find

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}S^{\mu}_{\rm eq}=-\xi dN^{\mu}_{\rm eq}+\beta_{\alpha}\mathrm{d}T^{\mu\alpha}_{\rm eq}-\frac{1}{2}\omega_{\alpha\beta}\mathrm{d}S^{\mu,\alpha\beta}_{\rm eq}.

(45)

Thermodynamic relations of the form (
44
) and (
45
) have been recently derived for the case of classical statistics
[
52
]
. Herein, we show that they are valid also for the Fermi-Dirac statistics, similarly as Eq. (
38
).

IV
Small spin density expansion

It should be emphasized that our derivation of Eqs. (
44
) and (
45
) does not require that the spin polarization tensor be small. However, since the experimentally observed spin polarization effects typically are small, it makes sense to consider expansion in
\omega^{\mu\nu}
or, alternatively, in
k^{\mu}
and
\omega^{\mu}
that are treated as small parameters (note that
\omega^{\mu\nu}
is dimensionless in natural units). As pointed out in Ref.
[
52
]
, a nontrivial and thermodynamically consistent treatment of spin can be achieved if we take into account at least quadratic corrections in
k
and
\omega
. Thus, we expand the FD distribution functions around
y_{s}=0
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{e^{y^{\pm}_{0}+y_{s}}+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{e^{y^{\pm}_{0}}+1}-\frac{e^{y^{\pm}_{0}}}{(e^{y^{\pm}_{0}}+1)^{2}}\,y_{s}+\frac{e^{y^{\pm}_{0}}(e^{y^{\pm}_{0}}-1)}{2\,(e^{y^{\pm}_{0}}+1)^{3}}\,y_{s}^{2}\,+\cdots.

(46)

Here
y^{\pm}_{0}
is the spin-independent part of
y^{\pm}
defined by Eq. (
33
),

\displaystyle y^{\pm}_{0}=\mp\xi(x)+p\cdot\beta(x),

(47)

while

\displaystyle y_{s}=-\frac{1}{2}\,\omega(x):s.

(48)

With
f^{\pm}_{0}
denoting the FD distribution with
\omega_{\alpha\beta}=0
, i.e., for the spinless case, we may write

\displaystyle f_{\rm eq}^{\pm}=f^{\pm}_{0}-f^{\pm}_{0}(1-f^{\pm}_{0})y_{s}+\frac{1}{2}f^{\pm}_{0}(1-f^{\pm}_{0})(1-2f^{\pm}_{0})y_{s}^{2}+\cdots

(49)

or

\displaystyle f_{\rm eq}^{\pm}=f^{\pm}_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\,f^{\pm}_{1}\,\omega:s+\frac{1}{8}\,f^{\pm}_{2}\,(\omega:s)^{2}+\cdots,

(50)

where we have introduced the notation

\displaystyle\begin{split}f^{\pm}_{1}&=f^{\pm}_{0}(1-f^{\pm}_{0})=\frac{e^{y^{\pm}_{0}}}{(e^{y^{\pm}_{0}}+1)^{2}}=\pm\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}f^{\pm}_{0},\\
f^{\pm}_{2}&=f^{\pm}_{0}(1-f^{\pm}_{0})(1-2f^{\pm}_{0})=\frac{e^{y^{\pm}_{0}}(e^{y^{\pm}_{0}}-1)}{(e^{y^{\pm}_{0}}+1)^{3}}=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\xi^{2}}f^{\pm}_{0}.\end{split}

(51)

IV.1
Baryon current

The expansion in
\omega:s
may be used in our definitions of the macroscopic currents. In this case, one can analytically integrate over the spin degrees of freedom first. Starting from the definition of the baryon current,

\displaystyle N_{\rm eq}^{\mu}=\int\mathrm{d}P\,\mathrm{d}S\,p^{\mu}\,\left[f_{\rm eq}^{+}(x,p,s)-f_{\rm eq}^{-}(x,p,s)\right],

(52)

and using the expansion (
50
) for the FD distribution functions as well as Eq. (
170
), we find

\displaystyle N_{\rm eq}^{\mu}=\int\mathrm{d}P\,\mathrm{d}S\,p^{\mu}\left(f^{+}_{0}-f^{-}_{0}\right)+\frac{1}{8}\int\mathrm{d}P\,\mathrm{d}S\,p^{\mu}\left(f^{+}_{2}-f^{-}_{2}\right)(\omega:s)^{2}.

(53)

With the help of the spin integration rules (
167
) and (
172
), we further find

\displaystyle\begin{split}N_{\rm eq}^{\mu}&=2\int\mathrm{d}P\,p^{\mu}\left(f^{+}_{0}-f^{-}_{0}\right)+\,{\gothfamily s:}^{2}\frac{\omega:\omega}{6}\int\mathrm{d}P\,p^{\mu}\left(f^{+}_{2}-f^{-}_{2}\right)\\
&+\frac{\,{\gothfamily s:}^{2}}{3m^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}P\,p^{\mu}p^{\alpha}p^{\beta}\omega^{\gamma}_{\phantom{\gamma}\alpha}\omega_{\beta\gamma}\left(f^{+}_{2}-f^{-}_{2}\right).\end{split}

(54)

Here we introduce functions
Z^{\pm\alpha\beta...}_{n}
, which appear in computations and are computed in Appendix A for several values of
n
,

\displaystyle Z^{\pm\alpha\beta...}_{n}=\int\mathrm{d}Pp^{\alpha}p^{\beta}...f^{\pm}_{n}.

(55)

Then, the baryon current can be written in a compact form as

\displaystyle N_{\rm eq}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(Z^{+\mu}_{0}-Z^{-\mu}_{0})+\,{\gothfamily s:}^{2}\frac{\omega:\omega}{6}(Z^{+\mu}_{2}-Z^{-\mu}_{2})+\frac{\,{\gothfamily s:}^{2}}{3m^{2}}(Z^{+\mu\alpha\beta}_{2}-Z^{-\mu\alpha\beta}_{2})\omega^{\gamma}_{\phantom{\gamma}\alpha}\omega_{\beta\gamma}.

(56)

IV.2
Energy-momentum tensor

In the case of the energy-momentum tensor we use the expression

T_{\rm eq}^{\mu\nu}=\int\mathrm{d}P\,\mathrm{d}S\,p^{\mu}p^{\nu}\,\left[f_{\rm eq}^{+}(x,p,s)+f_{\rm eq}^{-}(x,p,s)\right].

(57)

Similarly, as in the case of the baryon current, the terms linear in the spin polarization tensor vanish. Thus, we obtain the following form of the energy-momentum tensor

\displaystyle T_{\rm eq}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\mathrm{d}P\,\mathrm{d}S\,p^{\mu}p^{\nu}(f_{0}^{+}+f_{0}^{+})+\frac{1}{8}\int\mathrm{d}P\,\mathrm{d}S\,p^{\mu}p^{\nu}(f^{+}_{2}+f^{-}_{2})(\omega:s)^{2}.

(58)

Integration over the spin degrees of freedom yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}T_{\rm eq}^{\mu\nu}&=2\int\mathrm{d}P\,p^{\mu}p^{\nu}(f^{+}_{0}+f^{-}_{0})+\frac{\,{\gothfamily s:}^{2}}{6m^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}P\,p^{\mu}p^{\nu}(f^{+}_{2}+f^{-}_{2})(m^{2}\omega:\omega+2p^{\alpha}p^{\beta}\omega^{\gamma}_{\phantom{\gamma}\alpha}\omega_{\beta\gamma})\\
&=2\int\mathrm{d}P\,p^{\mu}p^{\nu}(f^{+}_{0}+f^{-}_{0})+\,{\gothfamily s:}^{2}\frac{\omega:\omega}{6}\int\mathrm{d}P\,p^{\mu}p^{\nu}(f^{+}_{2}+f^{-}_{2})\\
&+\frac{\,{\gothfamily s:}^{2}}{3m^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}Pp^{\mu}p^{\nu}p^{\alpha}p^{\beta}(f^{+}_{2}+f^{-}_{2})\omega^{\gamma}_{\phantom{\gamma}\alpha}\omega_{\beta\gamma},\end{split}

(59)

which can be rewritten in terms of the tensors
Z
, namely
as

\displaystyle T_{\rm eq}^{\mu\nu}=2\left(Z^{+\mu\nu}_{0}\!+\!Z^{-\mu\nu}_{0}\right)\!+\!\,{\gothfamily s:}^{2}\,\frac{\omega:\omega}{6}\left(Z^{+\mu\nu}_{2}\!+\!Z_{2}^{-\mu\nu}\right)\!+\!\frac{\,{\gothfamily s:}^{2}}{3m^{2}}\,\left(Z^{+\mu\nu\alpha\beta}_{2}\!+\!Z^{-\mu\nu\alpha\beta}_{2}\right)\omega^{\gamma}_{\phantom{\gamma}\alpha}\omega_{\beta\gamma}.

(60)

IV.3
Spin tensor

In the case of the spin tensor, we use the formula

\displaystyle S_{\rm eq}^{\lambda,\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\!\mathrm{d}P\,\mathrm{d}S\,\,p^{\lambda}\,s^{\mu\nu}\left[f_{\rm eq}^{+}(x,p,s)+f_{\rm eq}^{-}(x,p,s)\right]

(61)

and expand the FD distribution functions up to linear terms in the spin polarization tensor, which leads to the expression

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{\rm eq}^{\lambda,\mu\nu}\!&=\!\!\frac{1}{2}\int\!\mathrm{d}P\,\mathrm{d}S\,\,p^{\lambda}\,s^{\mu\nu}(f^{+}_{1}+f^{-}_{1})(\omega:s)\\
&=\frac{2\,{\gothfamily s:}^{2}}{3}\int\mathrm{d}Pp^{\lambda}(f_{1}^{+}+f_{1}^{-})\omega^{\mu\nu}+\frac{2\,{\gothfamily s:}^{2}}{3m^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}Pp^{\lambda}p^{\alpha}(p^{\mu}\omega^{\nu}_{\,\,\,\alpha}-p^{\nu}\omega^{\mu}_{\,\,\,\alpha})(f^{+}_{1}+f^{-}_{1}).\end{split}

(62)

Then, one obtains the compact expression

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{\rm eq}^{\lambda,\mu\nu}=&\frac{2\,{\gothfamily s:}^{2}}{3}\,\omega^{\mu\nu}\,(Z^{+\lambda}_{1}+Z^{-\lambda}_{1})+\frac{2\,{\gothfamily s:}^{2}}{3m^{2}}\left[\,(Z^{+\lambda\alpha\mu}_{1}+Z^{-\lambda\alpha\mu}_{1})\omega^{\nu}_{\,\,\,\alpha}-(Z^{+\lambda\alpha\nu}_{1}+Z^{-\lambda\alpha\nu}_{1})\omega^{\mu}_{\,\,\,\alpha}\right].\end{split}

(63)

We note that the baryon current and the energy-momentum tensor contain even terms in the expansion, while the spin tensor includes the odd terms. However, because in the thermodynamic relations the spin tensor is multiplied by the spin polarization tensor, only even terms in
\omega_{\alpha\beta}
appear there.

V
Boltzmann Statistics

Since the explicit expressions for the tensors
Z^{\pm\alpha\beta\ldots}_{n}
are rather complicated, in the next sections we restrict our considerations to the Boltzmann statistics. We come back to the discussion of the FD case in Appendix
A
. The classical statistics is obtained by neglecting the term
+1

\displaystyle f_{\rm eq}^{\pm}(x,p,s)=\exp\left[\pm\xi(x)-p\cdot\beta(x)+\frac{1}{2}\omega(x):s\right].

(64)

In addition, as the classical limit corresponds to a dilute system, we can always neglect

(1-f)

50

\displaystyle f_{\rm eq}^{\pm}=f^{\pm}_{0}-f^{\pm}_{0}y_{s}+\frac{1}{2}f^{\pm}_{0}y_{s}^{2}+\cdots

(65)

or

\displaystyle f_{\rm eq}^{\pm}=f^{\pm}_{0}\left[1+\frac{1}{2}\,\omega:s+\frac{1}{8}\,(\omega:s)^{2}+\cdots\right].

(66)

Clearly, the terms in the squared brackets directly come from the expansion of the exponential function (

V.1
Baryon and particle currents

Factorization of the Boltzmann distribution function into the “momentum” and “spin” parts makes the calculation of macroscopic quantities quite straightforward. First we integrate over the spin degrees of freedom and then over momentum, which leads to expressions involving modified Bessel functions. In the case of the baryon current, including terms up to the second order, we find

\displaystyle\begin{split}N_{\rm eq}^{\mu}&=2\sinh{\xi}\int\mathrm{d}P\,p^{\mu}e^{-p\cdot\beta}\left[2+\frac{1}{8}\int dS(\omega:s)^{2}\right]\\
&=4\sinh{\xi}\int\mathrm{d}P\,p^{\mu}e^{-p\cdot\beta}\left[\left(1+\frac{\,{\gothfamily s:}^{2}}{12}\omega:\omega\right)+\frac{\,{\gothfamily s:}^{2}}{6m^{2}}p^{\alpha}p^{\beta}\omega^{\gamma}_{\phantom{\gamma}\alpha}\omega_{\beta\gamma}\right]\\
&=4\sinh{\xi}\left(1+\frac{\,{\gothfamily s:}^{2}}{12}\,\omega:\omega\right)\underset{Z^{\mu}}{\underbrace{\int\mathrm{d}P\,p^{\mu}e^{-\beta\cdot p}}}+\frac{2{\gothfamily s:}^{2}\sinh{\xi}}{3m^{2}}\underset{Z^{\mu\alpha\beta}}{\underbrace{\int\mathrm{d}P\,p^{\mu}p^{\alpha}p^{\beta}e^{-\beta\cdot p}}}\omega^{\gamma}_{\phantom{\gamma}\alpha}\omega_{\beta\gamma}.\end{split}

(67)

We note that this formula is the classical (Boltzmann) limit of Eq. (
56
). The integration over the spin degrees of freedom is done according to the rules (
170
) and (
172
), see Ref.
[
50
]

Z^{\mu}
and
Z^{\mu\alpha\beta}

Z^{\pm\alpha\beta\ldots}_{n}
defined above in the context of the FD statistics,

\displaystyle Z^{\pm\alpha\beta\ldots}_{n}\quad\longrightarrow\quad e^{\pm\xi}Z^{\alpha\beta\ldots},\qquad n=0,1,2.

(68)

The explicit forms of
Z^{\mu}
and
Z^{\mu\alpha\beta}

[

\displaystyle Z^{\mu}=\frac{T^{3}}{2\pi^{2}}z^{2}K_{2}(z)u^{\mu}.

Here and below the functions

V.2
Energy-momentum tensor

Using the definition of the energy-momentum tensor (

\displaystyle\begin{split}T_{\rm eq}^{\mu\nu}&=4\cosh{\xi}\left(1+\frac{\,{\gothfamily s:}^{2}}{12}\omega:\omega\right)\underset{Z^{\mu\nu}}{\underbrace{\int\mathrm{d}P\,p^{\mu}p^{\nu}e^{-\beta\cdot p}}}\\
&+\frac{2\,{\gothfamily s:}^{2}\cosh{\xi}}{3m^{2}}\underset{Z^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}}{\underbrace{\int\mathrm{d}P\,p^{\mu}p^{\nu}p^{\alpha}p^{\beta}e^{-\beta\cdot p}}}\,\omega^{\gamma}_{\phantom{\gamma}\alpha}\omega_{\beta\gamma}.\end{split}

(74)

Here we underlined the tensors
Z^{\mu\nu}
and
Z^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}
, whose explicit forms are given in
[

]
.
Combining all the expressions (
176

), we find the formula

\displaystyle\begin{split}T_{\rm eq}^{\mu\nu}&={\bar{\varepsilon}}u^{\mu}u^{\nu}-{\bar{P}}\Delta^{\mu\nu}+P_{k}\,k^{\mu}k^{\nu}+P_{\omega}\,\omega^{\mu}\omega^{\nu}+P_{t}\,(t^{\mu}u^{\nu}+t^{\nu}u^{\mu})\\

&+P_{k}k^{\mu}k^{\nu}+P_{\omega}\omega^{\mu}\omega^{\nu}+P_{t}(t^{\mu}u^{\nu}+t^{\nu}u^{\mu}),\end{split}

(75)

where the coefficient functions read

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\varepsilon}_{0}&=\frac{2\cosh{\xi}}{\pi^{2}}z^{2}T^{4}\left[zK_{3}(z)-K_{2}(z)\right],\\

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}{P_{0}}&=\frac{2\cosh{\xi}}{\pi^{2}}z^{2}T^{4}K_{2}(z),\\

\displaystyle\begin{split}T^{\mu\nu}_{\rm eq}&={\bar{\varepsilon}}(T,\xi,k^{2},\omega^{2})u^{\mu}u^{\nu}-{\bar{P}}_{k\omega}(T,\xi,k^{2},\omega^{2})\Delta^{\mu\nu}\\

V.3
Spin tensor

In the next step, we consider the spin tensor
S^{\lambda,\mu\nu}_{\rm eq}
given by Eq. (
36
). Using Eq. (
171
), we can again express it in terms of the tensors
Z
,

\displaystyle S_{\rm eq}^{\lambda,\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\cosh{\xi}\int\mathrm{d}P\,p^{\lambda}e^{-p\cdot\beta}\int\mathrm{d}Ss^{\mu\nu}\left[1+\frac{1}{2}\omega:s\right]

(79)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cosh{\xi}\int\mathrm{d}P\,p^{\lambda}e^{-p\cdot\beta}\,\int\mathrm{d}S\,s^{\mu\nu}\,\omega:s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4\,{\gothfamily s:}^{2}\cosh{\xi}}{3m^{2}}\left[m^{2}\omega^{\mu\nu}\underset{Z^{\lambda}}{\underbrace{\int\mathrm{d}P\,p^{\lambda}e^{-\beta\cdot p}}}+\omega^{\nu}_{\phantom{\nu}\alpha}\underset{Z^{\lambda\alpha\mu}}{\underbrace{\int\mathrm{d}P\,p^{\lambda}p^{\alpha}p^{\mu}e^{-\beta\cdot p}}}-\omega^{\mu}_{\phantom{\mu}\alpha}\underset{Z^{\lambda\alpha\nu}}{\underbrace{\int\mathrm{d}P\,p^{\lambda}p^{\alpha}p^{\nu}e^{-\beta\cdot p}}}\right].

To obtain the final form, we need an explicit expression for the contraction (
177
). We also introduce the tensor

\displaystyle\begin{split}t^{\lambda\mu\nu}&=\omega^{\nu\lambda}u^{\mu}-\omega^{\mu\lambda}u^{\nu}+g^{\lambda\mu}k^{\nu}-g^{\lambda\nu}k^{\mu}\\
&=u^{\lambda}\left(u^{\mu}k^{\nu}-u^{\nu}k^{\mu}\right)+u^{\mu}t^{\nu\lambda}-u^{\nu}t^{\mu\lambda}+g^{\lambda\mu}k^{\nu}-g^{\lambda\nu}k^{\mu}.\end{split}

(80)

Then, the spin tensor
S_{\rm eq}^{\lambda,\mu\nu}
can be written as

\displaystyle S_{\rm eq}^{\lambda,\mu\nu}=A_{1}u^{\lambda}\omega^{\mu\nu}+\frac{A_{2}}{2}u^{\lambda}\left(u^{\mu}k^{\nu}-u^{\nu}k^{\mu}\right)+\frac{A_{3}}{2}\,t^{\lambda\mu\nu},

(81)

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}A_{1}&=\frac{2\,{\gothfamily s:}^{2}\cosh{\xi}}{3\pi^{2}}zT^{3}\left[zK_{2}(z)+2K_{3}(z)\right],\\
A_{2}&=\frac{4\,{\gothfamily s:}^{2}\cosh{\xi}}{3\pi^{2}}z^{2}T^{3}K_{4}(z),\\
A_{3}&=-\frac{4\,{\gothfamily s:}^{2}\cosh{\xi}}{3\pi^{2}}zT^{3}K_{3}(z).\end{split}

(82)

This result is consistent with the decomposition used in Ref.
[
60
]
. It is also convenient to introduce a coefficient
A
defined by the expression

\displaystyle A=A_{1}-\frac{A_{2}}{2}-A_{3}=-\frac{4\,{\gothfamily s:}^{2}\cosh{\xi}}{3\pi^{2}}zT^{3}K_{3}(z)=A_{3},

(83)

which allows us to rewrite the spin tensor in a compact form as

\displaystyle S_{\rm eq}^{\lambda,\mu\nu}=u^{\lambda}\left[A\left(k^{\mu}u^{\nu}-k^{\nu}u^{\mu}\right)+A_{1}t^{\mu\nu}\right]+\frac{A}{2}\left(t^{\lambda\mu}u^{\nu}-t^{\lambda\nu}u^{\mu}+\Delta^{\lambda\mu}k^{\nu}-\Delta^{\lambda\nu}k^{\mu}\right).

(84)

An important feature of Eq. (
84
) is that it does not agree with the phenomenological version (
23
). Moreover, even if the term on the right-hand side of Eq. (
84
) that is orthogonal to
u^{\lambda}
is neglected, still the spin density tensor defined by Eq. (
84
) cannot be written in the form
S^{\mu\nu}=A\,\omega^{\mu\nu}
, as the coefficients
A
and
A_{1}
are different.

As the matter of fact, the spin equation of state of the form
S^{\mu\nu}=A\,\omega^{\mu\nu}
has been recently analyzed and excluded
[
37
]
as leading to unstable behavior of rest frame modes in the first-order
[
32
,
31
]
and second-order
[
36
,
39
]
dissipative spin hydrodynamics. The conclusion of these works is that the spin density tensor
S^{\mu\nu}
should depend differently on the electriclike and magneticlike components of the spin tensor, with the electric susceptibility being negative and the magnetic being positive. We emphasize that this is really the case for our kinetic expression (
84
) since
A<0
and
A_{1}>0
. In particular, for small values of
z=m/T
we find

\displaystyle\frac{A_{1}}{A}=-1-\frac{z^{2}}{8}+\cdots,

(85)

while for large
z=m/T
we have

\displaystyle\frac{A}{A_{1}}=-\frac{2}{z}-\frac{1}{z^{2}}+\cdots.

(86)

Thus, for small
z
the coefficients
A
and
A_{1}
have almost the same magnitude but opposite signs, whereas for large
z
the coefficient
A
is much smaller than
A_{1}
. In the latter case, the magneticlike component of the spin density tensor dominates the system’s behavior, as first noted in Ref.
[
13
]
.

Entropy current

In addition to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\omega_{\alpha\beta}S_{\rm eq}^{\mu,\alpha\beta}=u^{\mu}(Ak^{2}-A_{1}\omega^{2})+A_{3}t^{\mu}\equiv{\bar{s}}u^{\mu}+s_{t}t^{\mu}.

\displaystyle S_{\rm eq}^{\mu}={\bar{\sigma}}u^{\mu}+\sigma_{t}t^{\mu},

\displaystyle\bar{\sigma}=\frac{\bar{\varepsilon}}{T}+(\coth\xi-\xi)\bar{n}-\bar{s},\hskip 19.91684pt\sigma_{t}=\frac{P_{t}}{T}+(\coth{\xi}-\xi)n_{t}-s_{t}.

(93)

VI.1
Nonequilibrium entropy current

To extend the formalism presented above to a theory including dissipative phenomena, we follow the method initiated by Israel and Stewart

\displaystyle S^{\mu}=T^{\mu\alpha}\beta_{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}\omega_{\alpha\beta}S^{\mu,\alpha\beta}-\xi N^{\mu}+{\cal N}^{\mu}.

(94)

Next, we calculate the divergence of the entropy current defined by Eq. (

4

4

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}N^{\mu}=0,\qquad\partial_{\mu}T^{\mu\nu}=0,\qquad\partial_{\mu}S^{\mu,\alpha\beta}=T^{\beta\alpha}-T^{\alpha\beta}.

VI.4
Systematics of expansion

Our expressions for the baryon current (
70
), the energy-momentum tensor (
75
), and the spin tensor (
84
) were based on the expansion in the spin polarization tensor,
\omega_{\alpha\beta}=\Omega_{\alpha\beta}/T
, which in natural units is a dimensionless quantity, similarly as the ratio
\xi=\mu/T
. Hence, the expansion in
k
and
\omega
is well defined as controlled by dimensionless parameters. On the other hand, inclusion of dissipation introduces gradient terms, whose importance is quantified by the Knudsen number.

Since some of the dissipative currents are determined by a combination of the gradient terms and the spin polarization components, many works consider
\omega_{\alpha\beta}
to be a quantity of the same order as the gradient terms (see Ref.
[
28
]
and works based on that paper). This implies that the gradients of
\omega_{\alpha\beta}
appearing on the right-hand sides of Eqs. (
125
) and (
126
) are considered to be second-order corrections and neglected. This also directly leads to the conclusion that the terms of the form
u_{\lambda}S^{\lambda,\alpha\beta}\omega_{\alpha\beta}
should be at least of the second order and neglected. However, the latter conclusion is usually ignored by stating that the spin density tensor
S^{\alpha\beta}=u_{\lambda}S^{\lambda,\alpha\beta}
is of the zeroth order in gradients. Clearly, the assumption that the magnitude of
\omega_{\alpha\beta}
is fixed by the magnitude of gradients cannot hold in general
7
7

7

For example, let us consider the dimensionless expression
\beta Du^{\mu}-\beta^{2}\nabla^{\mu}T-2k^{\mu}
in Eq. (
121
). For boost-invariant flows
Du^{\mu}=0
and
\nabla^{\mu}T=0
, however,
k^{\mu}
may be noticeably different from zero.
. Our approach suggests that the gradient corrections and the corrections arising due to the appearance of the spin polarization tensor components should be treated independently. The paper
[
52
]
shows that an expansion in
\omega_{\alpha\beta}
to second-order terms is crucial for obtaining a nontrivial and consistent treatment of thermodynamic relations. In this work we show how to extend
[
52
]
by additions of dissipative (gradient) terms.

VII
Summary and conclusions

In this work we have introduced a unified framework of spin hydrodynamics that combines the results of kinetic theory for particles with spin
{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}
with the IS approach for including nonequilibrium processes. The latter is used to incorporate what is usually referred to as the first-order terms (in gradients). The inclusion of the second-order terms is straightforward but quite lengthy, so we leave it as a separate project. The framework of the kinetic theory has been used to define the perfect-fluid description. Interestingly, in the presence of spin degrees of freedom, it includes terms whose form is usually attributed to dissipation. The genuine dissipative terms appear from the condition of positive entropy production that forms the basis of the IS method. They are responsible for transfers between the spin and orbital parts of the total angular momentum.

The proposed framework solves long-standing problems encountered in previous studies based on the positive-entropy production principle (inconsistent expansion in the spin polarization tensor, problems with thermodynamic identities) and local kinetic theory (neglecting the spin-orbit coupling). It also seems to be straightforward to implement in practical applications/codes, as it circumvents possible technical difficulties connected with the use of the nonlocal collision kernel.

Appendix A
Construction of the tensors
Z^{\pm\alpha\beta\ldots}_{n}

The tensors
Z^{\pm\alpha\beta\ldots}_{n}\equiv\int\mathrm{d}Pp^{\alpha}p^{\beta}\cdots f_{n}^{\pm}

symmetries (i.e., dependence on the product
p\cdot u
and symmetry under exchange of any Lorentz indices) as the tensors
Z^{\alpha\beta\ldots}\equiv\int\mathrm{d}Pp^{\alpha}p^{\beta}\cdots e^{-\beta\cdot p}
that appear in the classical (Boltzmann) approach, and therefore they admit the same
decomposition in terms of generic
tensors of the same symmetry built out of the hydrodynamic flow vector
u^{\alpha}
and the metric tensor
g^{\alpha\beta}
(see Eqs. (5.225)–(5.228) in Ref.
[
59
]
).

The general method for determining the functional form of the coefficients that appear in such a decomposition is to calculate appropriate traces and/or tensor contractions with the flow
u_{\alpha}
, so as to obtain a set of linear scalar equations, which can be easily solved. In the FD case, after a suitable variable change, this directly leads to the coefficients expressed as combinations of integrals of the following two types
[
59
]

\displaystyle J_{nm}(\xi,z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sinh^{n}y\cosh^{m}y}{e^{-\xi+z\cosh y}-\epsilon}\mathrm{d}y,\quad I_{n}(\xi,z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\cosh{(ny)}}{e^{z\cosh{y}}-\epsilon}\mathrm{d}y.

(127)

The parameter
\epsilon
introduced here is equal to
-1
for the FD statistics.

Within the coefficients, the functions

always appear with even

by using the hyperbolic identity
\sinh^{2}y=\cosh^{2}y-1
and replacing powers of
\cosh y

\displaystyle\begin{split}J_{20}=\frac{1}{2}(I_{2}-I_{0}),\quad J_{21}=\frac{1}{4}(I_{3}-I_{1}),\quad J_{22}=\frac{1}{8}(I_{4}-I_{0}),\quad\\

(128)

Appendix D
Contraction of rank-3 tensors antisymmetric in two indices

As per Eq. (
100
), the spin tensor can be parametrized in terms of simpler vectors and tensors in the following way:

\displaystyle S^{\lambda,\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u^{\lambda}\left[\left(f^{\mu}u^{\nu}-f^{\nu}u^{\mu}\right)+h^{\mu\nu}\right]+i^{\lambda\mu}u^{\nu}-i^{\lambda\nu}u^{\mu}+j^{\lambda\mu\nu},

(183)

with the simplifying constraints

\displaystyle\begin{split}f^{\mu}u_{\mu}=0,\quad h^{\mu\nu}=-h^{\nu\mu},\quad h^{\mu\nu}u_{\mu}=0,\quad h^{\mu\nu}=\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}u_{\rho}w_{\sigma},\ \text{where}\ w\cdot u=0,\\
i^{\lambda\mu}u_{\lambda}=i^{\lambda\mu}u_{\mu}=0,\quad j^{\lambda\mu\nu}=-j^{\lambda\nu\mu},\quad j^{\lambda\mu\nu}u_{\lambda}=j^{\lambda\mu\nu}u_{\mu}=j^{\lambda\mu\nu}u_{\nu}=0.\end{split}

(184)

Now, let us compute the contraction of two such tensors.

\displaystyle\begin{split}S^{\lambda,\mu\nu}S^{\prime}_{\lambda,\mu\nu}&=\big[u^{\lambda}\left(\left(f^{\mu}u^{\nu}-f^{\nu}u^{\mu}\right)+h^{\mu\nu}\right)+i^{\lambda\mu}u^{\nu}-i^{\lambda\nu}u^{\mu}\\
&\quad+j^{\lambda\mu\nu}\big]\left[u_{\lambda}\left(\left(f^{\prime}_{\mu}u_{\nu}-f^{\prime}_{\nu}u_{\mu}\right)+h^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}\right)+i^{\prime}_{\lambda\mu}u_{\nu}-i^{\prime}_{\lambda\nu}u_{\mu}+j^{\prime}_{\lambda\mu\nu}\right]\\
&=u^{\lambda}u_{\lambda}[2f^{\mu}f^{\prime}_{\mu}u^{\nu}u_{\nu}-2f^{\mu}u_{\mu}\ f^{\prime}_{\nu}u^{\nu}-2h^{\mu\nu}u_{\mu}\ f^{\prime}_{\nu}-2h^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}u^{\mu}\ f^{\nu}+h^{\mu\nu}h^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}]\\
&\quad+\left(i^{\lambda\mu}(...)-i^{\lambda\nu}(...)\right)u_{\lambda}+\left(i^{\prime}_{\lambda\mu}(...)-i^{\prime}_{\lambda\nu}(...)\right)u^{\lambda}-2i^{\lambda\nu}u_{\nu}\ i^{\prime}_{\lambda\mu}u^{\mu}\\
&\quad+j^{\lambda\mu\nu}\left(u_{\lambda}(...)+u_{\nu}(...)-u_{\mu}(...)\right)+j^{\prime}_{\lambda\mu\nu}\left(u^{\lambda}(...)+u^{\nu}(...)-u^{\mu}(...)\right)\\
&\quad+2i^{\lambda\mu}i^{\prime}_{\lambda\mu}\ u^{\nu}u_{\nu}+j^{\lambda\mu\nu}j^{\prime}_{\lambda\mu\nu}\\
&=2f^{\mu}f^{\prime}_{\mu}+h^{\mu\nu}h^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}+2i^{\mu\nu}i^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}+j^{\lambda\mu\nu}j^{\prime}_{\lambda\mu\nu}.\end{split}

(185)

Constraints (
184
) were used to eliminate most of the terms. Using the definition of
h^{\mu\nu}
leads to further simplification,

\displaystyle h^{\mu\nu}h^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}u_{\rho}w_{\sigma}u^{\alpha}w^{\prime\beta}=-2(g^{\rho}_{\alpha}g^{\sigma}_{\beta}-g^{\rho}_{\beta}g^{\sigma}_{\alpha})u_{\rho}w_{\sigma}u^{\alpha}w^{\prime\beta}=-2w_{\mu}w^{\prime\mu},

(186)

whereas the tensor
i
can be split into the antisymmetric and the symmetric part, in the latter of which, in turn, we can isolate a part with nonzero trace and a traceless part
i^{\langle\mu\nu\rangle}
,

i^{\mu\nu}=i^{[\mu\nu]}+i^{(\mu\nu)}=i^{[\mu\nu]}-\tilde{i}\Delta^{\mu\nu}+i^{\langle\mu\nu\rangle},

(187)

where
\tilde{i}=-(1/3)i^{(\mu}{}_{\mu)}
and
\Delta^{\mu\nu}=g^{\mu\nu}-u^{\mu}u^{\nu}
. A contraction of a symmetric and an antisymmetric tensor is equal to zero, as is

\displaystyle\Delta^{\mu\nu}i_{\langle\mu\nu\rangle}^{\prime}=\Delta^{\mu\nu}\left(i^{\prime}_{(\mu\nu)}+\tilde{i^{\prime}}\Delta_{\mu\nu}\right)=g^{\mu\nu}i^{\prime}_{(\mu\nu)}-u^{\mu}u^{\nu}i^{\prime}_{(\mu\nu)}+3\tilde{i^{\prime}}=-3\tilde{i^{\prime}}+3\tilde{i^{\prime}}=0.

(188)

Hence, finally, we can write down the contraction of spin tensors in a simple way as a sum of contractions of their constituent parts:

\displaystyle S^{\lambda,\mu\nu}S^{\prime}_{\lambda,\mu\nu}=2f^{\mu}f^{\prime}_{\mu}-2w_{\mu}w^{\prime\mu}+2i^{[\mu\nu]}i^{\prime}_{[\mu\nu]}+2i^{\langle\mu\nu\rangle}i^{\prime}_{\langle\mu\nu\rangle}+3\tilde{i}\tilde{i^{\prime}}+j^{\lambda\mu\nu}j^{\prime}_{\lambda\mu\nu}.

(189)

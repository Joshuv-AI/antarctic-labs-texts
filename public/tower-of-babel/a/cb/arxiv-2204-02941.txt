A note on boundedness of singular integral operators on functions spaces over Local Fields
Salman Ashraf and Qaiser Jahan

Abstract
. In this article we consider the classical singular integral operator over a local field with rough kernels. We study the boundedness of such an operator on different function spaces by relaxing the smoothness condition on kernels.



1.
Introduction

In the fundamental paper
[
3
]
, Singular integral theory were introduced by A. P. Calderón and A. Zygmund, which plays an important role in Harmonic analysis. Let
\Omega:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a Lebesgue measurable function with
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\Omega(y)dy=0.
We say that a linear operator
T_{\Omega}:S(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow S^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a rough singular integral operator if

\displaystyle T_{\Omega}f(x)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{|y|>\epsilon}\dfrac{\Omega(y/|y|)}{|y|^{n}}f(x-y)dy.

The rough singular integral operators are one of the main topics in the theory of singular integral operators. In a series of remarks below we will see some known results about
L^{p}
boundedness of
T_{\Omega}.

(i)

In 1956, Calderón and Zygmund
[
4
]
proved that if
\Omega\in L\log^{+}L(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
is even or
\Omega\in L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
is odd then
T_{\Omega}
is of type
(p,p)
for
1<p<\infty.

(ii)

In 1979,
L^{p}
boundedness of
T_{\Omega}
for
1<p<\infty
proved independently by Connett
[
5
]
and Ricci-Weiss
[
21
]
under less restrictive condition that
\Omega\in H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
since

\displaystyle L\log^{+}L(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})\subset H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}).

Here
H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
is the Hardy space on the unit sphere.

(iii)

In 1998, Grafakos and Stefanov in
[
10
]
, considered the condition

(1.1)

\displaystyle\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\big|\Omega(\theta)\big|\bigg(\log\dfrac{1}{|\theta\cdot\xi|}\bigg)^{1+\alpha}d\theta\penalty\ <\penalty\ +\infty

for
\alpha>0
and gave
L^{p}
boundedness of
T_{\Omega}
if
\Omega
satisfies (
1.1
). Also in
[
11
]
, Grafakos and Stefanov gave a survey of known results in the theory of Calderón-Zygmund singular integral operator
T_{\Omega}.

The boundedness of
T_{\Omega}
on homogeneous Besov space
\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}
and on homogeneous Triebel-Lizorkin space
\dot{F}^{s}_{p,q}
were studied by Chen, Fan and Ying in
[
6
]
. The result of
[

]
is as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

(See
[
6
]
) Let

and

If

with mean value zero, then

(i)

\|T_{\Omega}f\|_{\dot{F}_{p,q}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{\dot{F}_{p,q}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

(ii)

\|T_{\Omega}f\|_{\dot{B}_{p,q}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{p,q}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

The result of
[
6
]
then improved by Chen and Zhang in
[

Theorem 1.2
.

(See
[
7
]
) Let

and

If

\displaystyle\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\big|\Omega(\theta)\big|\bigg(\log\dfrac{1}{|\theta\cdot\xi|}\bigg)^{1+\alpha}d\theta\penalty\ <\penalty\ +\infty

for all

Chen and Ding in

and proved the following result:

Theorem 1.3
.

(See
[
8
]
) Let
1<p,q<\infty
and

If
\Omega\in H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
with mean value zero, then

(i)

\|T_{\Omega}f\|_{\dot{F}_{p,q}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{\dot{F}_{p,q}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

(ii)

\|T_{\Omega}f\|_{\dot{B}_{p,q}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{p,q}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

In this paper, our main aim is to establish the boundedness of singular integral operator with Hardy space kernel on Lebesgue spaces, Triebel–Lizorkin spaces, and Besov spaces over local fields. In
[

A local field

2.
Preliminary definitions and lemmas

2.1.
Local Fields

Let
K
be a field and a topological space. If the additive group
K^{+}
and multiplicative group
K^{*}
of
K
both are locally compact Abelian group, then

is called
locally compact topological field
, or
local field.

If
K
is any field and is endowed with the discrete topology, then
K
is a local field. Further, if
K
is connected, then
K
is either
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
. If
K
is not connected, then it is totally disconnected. So, here local fields means a field
K
which is locally compact, non-discrete and totally disconnected. If
K
is of characteristic zero, then K is either a
p
-adic field for some prime number
p
or a finite algebraic extension of such a field. If
K
is of positive characteristic, then
K
is either a field of formal Laurent series over a finite field of characteristic
p
or an algebraic extension of such a field.

Let
K
be a local field. Since
K^{+}
is a locally compact abelian group, we choose a Haar measure
dx
for
K^{+}
. If
\alpha\neq 0,\penalty\ \alpha\in K
, then
d(\alpha x)
is also a Haar measure and by the uniqueness of Haar measure we have
d(\alpha x)=|\alpha|dx
. We call
|\alpha|
the
absolute value
or
valuation
of
\alpha
which is, in fact, a natural non-Archimedian norm on
K
. A mapping
|\cdot|\penalty\ :K\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfies:

(a)

|x|=0
if and only if
x=0
;

(b)

|xy|=|x||y|
for all
x,y\in K
;

(c)

|x+y|\leq\text{max}\{|x|,|y|\},
for all
x,y\in K
,

The property
(c)
is called the
ultrametric inequality
. It follows that

|x+y|=\text{max}\{|x|,|y|\}\penalty\ \text{if}\penalty\ |x|\neq|y|.

The set
\mathfrak{D}=\{x\in K:|x|\leq 1\}
is called the ring of integers in

. It is the unique maximal compact subring of
K
. The set
\mathfrak{P}=\{x\in K:|x|<1\}
is called the prime ideal in
K
. It is the unique maximal ideal in
\mathfrak{D}
. It can be proved that
\mathfrak{D}
is compact and open and hence
\mathfrak{P}
is also compact and open. Therefore, the residue space
\mathfrak{D}/\mathfrak{P}
is isomorphic to a finite field

, where

for some prime number

and

.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a fixed element of maximum absolute value in,
\mathfrak{P}
and it is called a
prime element
of
K
. For a measurable subset
E
of
K
, let
|E|=\int_{K}\mathbb{1}_{E}(x)dx
, where
\mathbb{1}
is the indicator function of

and
dx
is the Haar measure of
K

|\mathfrak{D}|=1
. Then it is easy to prove that
|\mathfrak{P}|=q^{-1}
and

(see

25
]
).

Let
\mathcal{U}=\{a_{i}\}_{i=0}^{q-1}
be any fixed full set of coset representative of
\mathfrak{P}
in,
\mathfrak{D}
then each

can be written uniquely as

x=\sum\limits_{l=k}^{\infty}c_{l}\mathfrak{p}^{k}
, where

.
If

then
|x|=q^{k}
for some

Let

;
\mathfrak{D}^{*}
is the group of units in

. If

, we can write as

, with

. Let
\mathfrak{P}^{k}=\mathfrak{p}^{k}\mathfrak{D}=\{x\in K:|x|\leq q^{-k}\}\penalty\ ,k\in\mathbb{Z}
. These are called

.

The set
\{\mathfrak{P}^{k}\subset K:k\in\mathbb{Z}\}
satisfies the following:

(i)

\{\mathfrak{P}^{k}\subset K:k\in\mathbb{Z}\}
is a base for neighborhood system of identity in

, and
\mathfrak{P}^{k+1}\subset\mathfrak{P}^{k},\penalty\ k\in\mathbb{Z};

(ii)

\mathfrak{P}^{k},\penalty\ k\in\mathbb{Z},
is open, closed and compact in

;

(iii)

K=\bigcup\limits_{{k=-\infty}}^{+\infty}\mathfrak{P}^{k}\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ \penalty\ \{0\}=\bigcap\limits_{{k=-\infty}}^{+\infty}\mathfrak{P}^{k}.

Let
\Gamma
be the character group of the additive group

of

. There is a nontrivial character

which is trivial on
\mathfrak{D}
but is non-trivial on

be a non-trivial character on

, then the corresponding relationship

and the topological isomorphism is established for

For

be the annihilator of

that is,

\displaystyle\Gamma^{k}=\{\chi\in\Gamma:\forall x\in\mathfrak{P}^{k}\implies\chi(x)=1\}.

The locally compact group

can be endowed with the non-Archimedian norm as

For the Haar measure

The set

satisfies the following properties:

2.2.
Function classes

In this subsection we will introduce some function classes:

Test function class
S(K)
:
It is the linear space in which functions have the form

\phi(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}\Phi_{\mathfrak{P}^{j}}(x-h_{j}),\penalty\ c_{j}\in\mathbb{C},h_{j}\in K,j\in\mathbb{Z},n\in\mathbb{N},

The space

is an algebra of continuous functions with compact support that separates points. Consequently,

is dense in

as well as

Similarly, the test function class

on
\Gamma

is isomorphic to
\Gamma

and

can be regarded as equivalent with respect to absolute value or valuation.

The space

is equipped with a topology as a topological vector space as follows: Define a null sequence in

as a sequence

of functions on

in such a way that each

is constant on cosets of
\mathfrak{P}^{l}
and is supported on
\mathfrak{P}^{k}
for a fixed pair of integers

and

and the sequence converges to zero uniformly. The space

is complete and separable and is called the

.

Since

is dense in

, thus the Fourier transformation of

is defined by

and the inverse Fourier transformation of

is defined by the formula

\widecheck{\phi}(x)\equiv(\mathcal{F}^{-1}\phi)(x)=\int_{\Gamma}\phi(\xi)\chi_{x}(\xi)d\xi,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ x\in K.

S^{\prime}(K)
, the

, is a collection of continuous linear functional on

.
S^{\prime}(K)
is also a complete topological linear space. The action of

in

is denoted by

. The distribution space

is given the weak

is defined in the following way:

converges to

converges to

The Fourier transformation

of a distribution

is defined by

\langle\widehat{f},\phi\rangle\penalty\ =\penalty\ \langle f,\widehat{\phi}\rangle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \text{for\penalty\ all}\penalty\ \phi\in S(K),

the inverse Fourier transformation

is defined by

\langle\widecheck{g},\psi\rangle=\langle g,\widecheck{\psi}\rangle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \text{for\penalty\ all}\penalty\ \psi\in S(K).

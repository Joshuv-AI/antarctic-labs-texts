Combinatorial Burnside groups
Yuri Tschinkel, Kaiqi Yang, Zhijia Zhang

Abstract
. We study the structure of combinatorial Burnside groups, which receive equivariant birational invariants of actions of finite groups on algebraic varieties.



1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a finite group, acting regularly on a smooth projective variety over an algebraically closed field
k
, of characteristic zero. The study of such actions, up to
G
-equivariant birationality, is a classical and active area in higher-dimensional algebraic geometry (see, e.g.,
[
16
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
14
]
). A new type of birational invariants of
G
-actions was introduced in
[
8
]
.
These take values in the
Burnside group

\mathrm{Burn}_{n}(G),

defined by explicit generators and relations. The invariant is computed on an appropriate birational model
X
(standard form), where

•

all stabilizers are abelian,

•

a translate of an irreducible component
Y
of a locus with nontrivial stabilizer is either equal to
Y
or is disjoint from it.

The invariant takes into account information about

•

subvarieties
Y\subset X
with nontrivial (abelian) stabilizers
H
,

•

the induced action of the centralizer
Z_{G}(H)
of
H
on
Y
, and

•

the representation of
H
in the normal bundle to
Y
.

A purely combinatorial version of these constructions was introduced in
[
12
]
. It keeps track of the
group-theoretic
information extracted as above, while forgetting the
field-theoretic
information, i.e., the birational type of the action on irreducible components of loci with nontrivial stabilizers.

Formally, combinatorial birational invariants of
G
-actions on
algebraic varieties of dimension
n
take values in the

combinatorial Burnside group

{\mathcal{BC}}_{n}(G),

defined in Section
4
.

When
G
is abelian, there is
a surjective homomorphism

{\mathcal{BC}}_{n}(G)\to{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(G),

a group introduced in
[
7
]
(and in Section
3
below),
which in turn has remarkable arithmetic properties
[
7
]
,
[
9
]
. For example,

{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(G)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}={\mathrm{H}}_{0}(\Gamma(n,G),{\mathcal{F}}_{n})\otimes{\mathbb{Q}},

where
\Gamma(n,G)\subset\mathrm{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{Z}})
is
a certain congruence subgroup and

{\mathcal{F}}_{n}
is the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-vector space generated by characteristic functions of convex rational polyhedral cones in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, modulo
functions of support less than
n

[
7
, Section 9]
. In particular, the groups
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(G)
carry Hecke operators. For
n=2
, there is a relation between
{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(G)
and Manin symbols.

In this note, we investigate arithmetic properties of the
a priori
richer groups
{\mathcal{BC}}_{n}(G)
,
which may be viewed as analogs of Manin symbols for nonabelian groups
G
, and of the
finitely generated
ring

\mathcal{BC}_{*}(G)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{BC}_{n}(G).

Our main result, Theorem
5.2
, is the construction of an isomorphism

(1.1)

\mathcal{BC}_{n}(G)\simeq\bigoplus_{[H,Y]}{\mathcal{B}}_{n}([H,Y]),

where the sum is over
G
-conjugacy classes
[H,Y]
of pairs
(H,Y)
, with
H\subseteq G
an abelian subgroup and
H\subseteq Y\subseteq Z_{G}(H)
, and

{\mathcal{B}}_{n}([H,Y])\simeq{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(H)/(\mathbf{C}_{(H,Y)})

is the quotient by a conjugation relation
which depends on the representative
(H,Y)
of the conjugacy class of the pair (see Section
4
). For
G

abelian
,
we have

{\mathcal{B}}_{n}([H,Y])={\mathcal{B}}_{n}(H),\,\text{ and }\,\mathcal{BC}_{n}(G)=\bigoplus_{H^{\prime}\subseteq G}\bigoplus_{H^{\prime\prime}\subseteq H^{\prime}}{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(H^{\prime\prime});

in particular, the groups
\mathcal{BC}_{n}(G)
also carry Hecke operators, as defined in
[
7
, Section 6]
and
[
9
, Section 3]
.

2.
Moebius inversion

Let
G
be a finite group and
\mathcal{H}
the poset of abelian subgroups of
G
under the inclusion relation. Let
\mathcal{S}
be the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module,
freely generated by
{\mathcal{H}}
; we will view
{\mathcal{H}}
as a subset of
{\mathcal{S}}
. For
H\in{\mathcal{H}}
we let
(H)
be its image in
{\mathcal{S}}
:

{\mathcal{S}}=\bigoplus_{H\in{\mathcal{H}}}{\mathbb{Z}}(H).

Let

\Psi
be the
{\mathcal{S}}
-valued function on
{\mathcal{S}}
, defined on generators by

\Psi((H))=\sum_{H^{\prime}\subseteq H}(H^{\prime}),\quad\forall H\in{\mathcal{H}},

and extended to all of
{\mathcal{S}}
by
{\mathbb{Z}}
-linearity.
Then there exists a unique
{\mathbb{Z}}
-valued function, the
Moebius function

(2.1)

\mu=\mu_{{\mathcal{H}}}:{\mathcal{H}}\times{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}

such that

\Phi((H)):=\sum_{H^{\prime}\subseteq H}\mu(H^{\prime},H)(H^{\prime}),\quad\forall H\in{\mathcal{H}},

is the inverse of
\Psi
, i.e.,

\Psi\circ\Phi=\Phi\circ\Psi=\mathrm{Id}.

The Moebius function
\mu
is constructed recursively by rules

•

\mu(H,H)=1
, for all
H\in{\mathcal{H}}
,

•

\mu(H^{\prime},H)=0
, for all
H^{\prime},H\in{\mathcal{H}}
with
H^{\prime}\not\subseteq H
,

•

\mu(H^{\prime\prime},H)=\displaystyle{-\sum_{H^{\prime\prime}\subseteq H^{\prime}\subsetneq H}}\mu(H^{\prime\prime},H^{\prime}),

for all
H^{\prime\prime},H\in{\mathcal{H}}
with
H^{\prime\prime}\subsetneq H
.

When
G
is
abelian
the poset
{\mathcal{H}}
contains all subgroups of
G
and
is a
lattice
,
with
join
and
meet
operations defined by

\displaystyle H^{\prime}\wedge H

\displaystyle:=H^{\prime}\cap H,

\displaystyle\quad H^{\prime}\vee H

\displaystyle:=\text{subgroup generated by }H^{\prime}\text{ and }H.

In Section
5
,
we will use the following result concerning the Moebius function on lattices (see, e.g.,
[
17
]
, or
[
15
, Sect 5]
):

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group and
H^{\prime\prime},H^{\prime}\subseteq G
subgroups satisfying

H^{\prime\prime}\subseteq H^{\prime}\subsetneq G.

Let
\mu
be the Moebius function of the subgroup lattice of
G
.
Then

\sum_{H\subseteq G,\,H\cap H^{\prime}=H^{\prime\prime}}\mu(H,G)=0.

In Section
6
, we will also need a corollary of Lemma
2.1
:

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
H,H^{\prime}
be subgroups of a fixed finite abelian group, and

H^{\prime\prime}\subseteq H\cap H^{\prime}.

Then

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\widetilde{H}\subseteq H,\widetilde{H}^{\prime}\subseteq H^{\prime},\,\widetilde{H}\cap\widetilde{H}^{\prime}=H^{\prime\prime}\end{subarray}}\mu(\widetilde{H},H)\mu(\widetilde{H}^{\prime},H^{\prime})=\begin{cases}\mu(H^{\prime\prime},H)&\text{if }H=H^{\prime},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

We rearrange the summation

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\widetilde{H}\subseteq H,\widetilde{H}^{\prime}\subseteq H^{\prime},\,\widetilde{H}\cap\widetilde{H}^{\prime}=H^{\prime\prime}\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle\mu(\widetilde{H},H)\mu(\widetilde{H}^{\prime},H^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\widetilde{H}\subseteq H}\mu(\widetilde{H},H)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\widetilde{H}^{\prime}\subseteq H^{\prime},\,\widetilde{H}^{\prime}\cap\widetilde{H}=H^{\prime\prime}\end{subarray}}\mu(\widetilde{H}^{\prime},H^{\prime}),

and apply Lemma
2.1
.
When
H=H^{\prime}
, the right side
equals

\mu(H,H)\mu(H^{\prime\prime},H^{\prime})=\mu(H^{\prime\prime},H).

When
H\neq H^{\prime}
, the right side
equals

\displaystyle\sum_{H^{\prime}\subseteq\widetilde{H}\subseteq H}\mu(\widetilde{H},H)\mu(H^{\prime\prime},H^{\prime})=\mu(H^{\prime\prime},H^{\prime})\sum_{H^{\prime}\subseteq\widetilde{H}\subseteq H}\mu(\widetilde{H},H)=0.

∎

3.
Symbols groups

Let
G
be a finite
abelian
group,

G^{\vee}=\mathrm{Hom}(G,{\mathbb{C}}^{\times})

its character group, and

{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(G)

the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module generated by
n
-tupels of characters of
G
,

\beta=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}),\quad b_{j}\in G^{\vee},\text{ for all }j,

generating

G^{\vee}
,
modulo the relation

(O)
reordering: for all
\beta=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})
and all
\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
we have

\beta=\beta^{\sigma}:=(b_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,b_{\sigma(n)}).

Consider the quotient

{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(G)\to{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(G)

by the
blowup relation

(B)
: for all
\beta=(b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{n})
, one has

\beta=\beta_{1}+\beta_{2},

where

(3.1)

\beta_{1}:=(b_{1}-b_{2},b_{2},\ldots,b_{n}),\quad\beta_{2}:=(b_{1},b_{2}-b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}),\quad n\geq 2.

For
H\subseteq G
and
\beta=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})
we
put

(3.2)

\beta|_{H}:=(b_{1}|_{H},\ldots,b_{n}|_{H}).

The groups
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(G)
were introduced in
[
7
]
; they capture
equivariant birational invariants
of
G
-actions on
n
-dimensional algebraic varieties. Combining constructions in
[
7
]
and
[
9
]
, we know that the groups

{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(G)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}:={\mathcal{B}}_{n}(G)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}

have an interesting internal structure, e.g., they carry:

•

Hecke operators,

•

multiplication and co-multiplication arising from exact sequences

0\to G^{\prime}\to G\to G^{\prime\prime}\to 0,

e.g., multiplication

\nabla:{\mathcal{B}}_{n^{\prime}}(G^{\prime})_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\otimes{\mathcal{B}}_{n^{\prime\prime}}(G^{\prime\prime})_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\to{\mathcal{B}}_{n^{\prime}+n^{\prime\prime}}(G)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}.

4.
Combinatorial Burnside groups

Definitions

Let
G
be a finite group and
n
a positive integer.
The

combinatorial symbols group
is the

{\mathbb{Z}}
-module

\mathcal{SC}_{n}(G),

generated by triples

(H,Y,\beta),

where

•

H\subseteq G
is an abelian group,

•

Y\subseteq G
is a subgroup satisfying
H\subseteq Y\subseteq Z_{G}(H)
, and

•

\beta=(b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{r})
is a sequence of
nontrivial
characters of
H
of length
r=r(\beta)
, with
1\leq r\leq n
,
generating

H^{\vee}
,

subject to relation

(O)

reordering: for all
(H,Y,\beta)
, with

\beta=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{r})
, and
\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{r}
we have

(H,Y,\beta)=(H,Y,\beta^{\sigma}),\quad\beta^{\sigma}:=(b_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,b_{\sigma(r)}).

By convention,

{\mathcal{SC}}_{0}(G):={\mathbb{Z}}.

The
combinatorial Burnside group
is a quotient of
the combinatorial symbols group,

{\mathcal{SC}}_{n}(G)\to\mathcal{BC}_{n}(G),

obtained by imposing additional relations
[
12
, Definition 8.1]
:

(
\mathbf{C}
)

conjugation: for all
H
,
Y
, and
\beta
, we have

(H,Y,\beta)=(gHg^{-1},gYg^{-1},\beta^{g}),

where
\beta^{g}
is the image of
\beta
under the conjugation by
g\in G
,

(
\mathbf{V}
)

vanishing:

(H,Y,\beta)=0

in each of the following cases:

\circ

H=1
,

\circ

b_{1}+b_{2}=0
, for some characters
b_{1},b_{2}
in
\beta
,

(
\mathbf{B2}
)

blowup relation:

for
b_{1}=b_{2}
, put:

(4.1)

(H,Y,(b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}))=(H,Y,(b_{2},\ldots,b_{r}));

for
b_{1}\neq b_{2}
, put:

\displaystyle(H,Y,\beta)=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(H,Y,\beta_{1})+(H,Y,\beta_{2})&\text{ if }b_{i}\in\langle b_{1}-b_{2}\rangle,\text{ for some }i,\\
\underbrace{(H,Y,\beta_{1})+(H,Y,\beta_{2})}_{\Theta_{1}}+\underbrace{(\bar{H},Y,\bar{\beta})}_{\Theta_{2}}&\text{ otherwise. }\end{cases}

Here we put

(4.2)

\beta_{1}:=(b_{1}-b_{2},b_{2},b_{3},\ldots,b_{r}),\quad\beta_{2}:=(b_{1},b_{2}-b_{1},b_{3},\ldots,b_{r}),

\bar{H}:=\ker(\langle b_{1}-b_{2}\rangle)\subseteq H,\quad\bar{\beta}:=\beta|_{\bar{H}}.

The notation
\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2}
was used in
[
8
, Section 4]
and
[
12
, Section 2]
.

Relation (
4.1
) allows to shorten the length of
\beta
in the presence of repeated characters; we call a symbol
reduced
if the characters in
\beta
are pairwise distinct.

Filtration

The blowup relation (
\mathbf{B2}
) does not increase
r(\beta)
, the number of characters in
\beta
. This allows to introduce

\mathcal{BC}_{n,r}(G)\subset\mathcal{BC}_{n}(G)

as the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-submodule generated by reduced symbols where

\beta
satisfies

r(\beta)\leq r
.
We have surjective homomorphisms

\mathcal{BC}_{r}(G)\to\mathcal{BC}_{n,r}(G),\quad 1\leq r\leq n,

which need not be isomorphisms, for
r<n
.

Vanishing

Relation (
\mathbf{V}
) implies (see
[
8
, Proposition 4.7]
) that

(H,Y,\beta)=0\in\mathcal{BC}_{n}(G),

provided there exist a nonempty
I\subseteq[1,\ldots,r]

and characters
b_{i},i\in I
, such that

(4.3)

\sum_{i\in I}b_{i}=0\in H^{\vee}.

Proposition 4.1
.

For a fixed
G
, we have

\mathcal{BC}_{n}(G)=0,\quad n\gg 0.

Proof.

Let
\ell=\ell(G)
be the maximal order of an element of
G
.
We have

0=(H,Y,(\underbrace{b_{1},\ldots,b_{1}}_{\ell{\,{\rm times}}},b_{2},\ldots,b_{n-\ell}))=(H,Y,(b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{n-\ell}))\in\mathcal{BC}_{n}(G),

for any choices of
b_{i}
, which implies that

\mathcal{BC}_{n,r}(G)=0,\quad 1\leq r\leq n-\ell.

It suffices to observe that for
n\gg 0
, every

reduced
symbol has
r(\beta)\leq n-\ell
.
∎

We define the
combinatorial dimension
:

(4.4)

\mathrm{cd}(G):=\min\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}\,|\,\mathcal{BC}_{m}(G)=0\quad\forall m>n\}.

We may also consider versions of this for

\mathcal{BC}_{m}(G)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}},\quad\text{ respectively, }\quad\mathcal{BC}_{m}(G)\otimes{\mathbb{F}}_{p},

and denote the corresponding smallest
n
as in (
4.4
) by

\mathrm{cd}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}(G),\quad\text{ respectively, }\quad\mathrm{cd}_{p}(G).

Computer experiments and Theorem
5.2
suggest the following:

Conjecture 4.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group, and
H\subseteq G

a maximal abelian subgroup. Then

\mathrm{cd}(G)\leq\log_{2}({|H|}),\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathrm{cd}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}(G)\leq\log_{3}({|H|})+1.

In particular, for
G=\mathfrak{S}_{m}
, based on the determination of maximal abelian subgroups of
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
in
[
3
]
, we have

\mathrm{cd}(G)\leq\frac{m}{3}\log_{2}({3}),\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathrm{cd}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}(G)\leq\frac{m}{3}+1.

Restriction

Section 7 in
[
12
]
introduced
the
restriction homomorphism
;
in our context, for
G^{\prime}\subseteq G
, it takes the form:

\mathrm{res}_{G^{\prime}}^{G}:{\mathcal{BC}}_{n}(G)\to{\mathcal{BC}}_{n}(G^{\prime}).

The group
G
acts by conjugation on the set of generating symbols as in
\mathbf{(C)}
. For any symbol

\mathfrak{s}=(H,Y,\beta),

the conjugation action by
G^{\prime}
partitions the conjugacy class of
\mathfrak{s}
into finitely many orbits. The restriction map is given by

\mathfrak{s}\mapsto\sum_{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}}(H^{\prime}\cap G^{\prime},Y^{\prime}\cap G^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}|_{H^{\prime}\cap G^{\prime}}),

where the sum is over orbit representatives
\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}=(H^{\prime},Y^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})
. The map respects relations, by construction. It is not
surjective, in general; e.g.,

{\mathcal{BC}}_{2}(\mathfrak{S}_{3})={\mathbb{Z}}/2,\quad{\mathcal{BC}}_{2}({\mathfrak{C}}_{3})={\mathbb{Z}}.

Ring structure

There is a product map

{\mathcal{BC}}_{n}(G)\times{\mathcal{BC}}_{n^{\prime}}(G)\rightarrow{\mathcal{BC}}_{n+n^{\prime}}(G),

given as the composition of

\displaystyle{\mathcal{BC}}_{n}(G)\times{\mathcal{BC}}_{n^{\prime}}(G)

\displaystyle\rightarrow{\mathcal{BC}}_{n+n^{\prime}}(G\times G)

\displaystyle(H,Y,\beta)\times(H^{\prime},Y^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})

\displaystyle\mapsto(H\times H^{\prime},Y\times Y^{\prime},\beta\cup\beta^{\prime})

with restriction to the diagonal.

We obtain a finitely-generated graded ring

{\mathcal{BC}}_{*}(G):=\oplus_{n\geq 0}\,\,\mathcal{BC}_{n}(G),

with
{\mathcal{BC}}_{0}(G)={\mathbb{Z}}
,
subject to various functoriality properties.

5.
Structure theory

In this section, we establish an isomorphism of
\mathcal{BC}_{n}(G)
with a simpler quotient of the combinatorial symbols group

{\mathcal{SC}}_{n}(G)\to\mathcal{BC}_{n}^{\prime}(G),

defined via relations
(\mathbf{C}),(\mathbf{V})
, together with the following modification of the blowup relation:

(\mathbf{B2^{\prime}})

for
b_{1}=b_{2}
, put:

(5.1)

(H,Y,(b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{r}))=(H,Y,(b_{2},\ldots,b_{r}));

for
b_{1}\neq b_{2}
, put:

(H,Y,\beta)=(H,Y,\beta_{1})+(H,Y,\beta_{2}),

where
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}
are as in (
4.2
).

For clarity, we will write

(H,Y,\beta)^{\prime},

when we view the corresponding symbol as an element in
\mathcal{BC}_{n}^{\prime}(G)
.

The relations respect the
G
-conjugacy class
[H,Y]
of the pair
(H,Y)
; so that

(5.2)

\mathcal{BC}_{n}^{\prime}(G)=\bigoplus_{[H,Y]}\,\,\,{\mathcal{B}}_{n}([H,Y]),

where

{\mathcal{B}}_{n}([H,Y]):=\bigoplus_{H^{\prime},Y^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}\,\,{\mathbb{Z}}(H^{\prime},Y^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})/(\mathbf{C}),(\mathbf{V}),(\mathbf{B2^{\prime}}),\quad(H^{\prime},Y^{\prime})\in[H,Y].

Consider the following conjugation relation on
{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(H)
:

(\mathbf{C}_{(H,Y)})
: for all
\beta\in{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(H)
and
g\in N_{G}(H)\cap N_{G}(Y)
we have

\beta=\beta^{g}.

Lemma 5.1
.

We have have an isomorphism of abelian groups

(5.3)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}_{n}([H,Y])\simeq{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(H)/(\mathbf{C}_{(H,Y)}).

Proof.

Fix a representative
(H,Y)
of the conjugacy class and
consider

(H^{\prime},Y^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}),\quad(H^{\prime},Y^{\prime})\in[H,Y],\quad\beta^{\prime}=(b_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,b_{r}^{\prime}).

Let
g\in G
be such that

H=gH^{\prime}g^{-1}\quad\text{and}\quad Y=gY^{\prime}g^{-1},

and put

(b_{i}^{\prime})^{g}:=\text{image of }b_{i}^{\prime}\text{ under the conjugation by }g,\quad i=1,\ldots,r.

Consider the homomorphism given on symbols in
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}([H,Y])
by

(5.4)

\displaystyle(H^{\prime},Y^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})\mapsto((b_{1}^{\prime})^{g},\ldots,(b_{r}^{\prime})^{g},\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{n-r})\in{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(H).

This is independent of the choice of
g
: given

g,g^{\prime}\in G,

such that

H^{\prime}=g^{-1}Hg=g^{\prime-1}Hg^{\prime}\quad\text{and}\quad Y^{\prime}=g^{-1}Yg=g^{\prime-1}Yg^{\prime},

we have

g^{\prime}g^{-1}\in N_{G}(H)\cap N_{G}(Y).

Therefore, by definition of
(\mathbf{C}_{(H,Y)})
, we have

((b_{1}^{\prime})^{g},\ldots,(b_{r}^{\prime})^{g},\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{n-r})=((b_{1}^{\prime})^{g^{\prime}},\ldots,(b_{r}^{\prime})^{g^{\prime}},\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{n-r}),

since

((b_{i}^{\prime})^{g})^{(g^{\prime}g^{-1})}=(b_{i}^{\prime})^{g^{\prime}},\quad i=1,\ldots,r.

The mapping (
5.4
) respects
\mathbf{(C)}
, by construction. Indeed, for any
g\in G
, the symbols

(H^{\prime},Y^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})\quad\text{and}\quad(gH^{\prime}g^{-1},gY^{\prime}g^{-1},\beta^{\prime g})

will be mapped to the same element in
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(H)/(\mathbf{C}_{(H,Y)})

since

H=g^{\prime}H^{\prime}g^{\prime-1}\quad\text{implies}\quad H=g^{\prime}g^{-1}\cdot(gH^{\prime}g^{-1})\cdot gg^{\prime-1}.

To see its compatibility with
\mathbf{(V)}
and
\mathbf{(B2^{\prime})},
it suffices to observe that the conjugation action is linear, i.e.,

(b_{1}+b_{2})^{g}=b_{1}^{g}+b_{2}^{g},\quad\text{ for all }b_{1},b_{2}\in H^{\vee},\,g\in G,

and we have the following identities in
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(H)
, by definition:

•

(b_{1},b_{1},b_{2},\ldots)=(0,b_{1},b_{2},\ldots)
,

•

(b_{1},b_{2},\ldots)=(b_{1}-b_{2},b_{2},\ldots)+(b_{1},b_{2}-b_{1},\ldots)
,

•

(b_{1},-b_{1},\ldots)=0.

On the other hand, by conjugation relations, the map defined by

(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})\mapsto(H,Y,\beta),

where
\beta
is obtained by removing all 0’s in the sequence of
b_{i}
, is the inverse of the map (
5.4
). It is clearly compatible with
\mathbf{(B)}
and
\mathbf{(C}_{(H,Y)})
. Therefore
(
5.4
) induces the desired isomorphism (
5.3
).
∎

We will now construct an isomorphism of
{\mathbb{Z}}
-modules

\mathcal{BC}_{n}(G)\simeq\mathcal{BC}_{n}^{\prime}(G).

The decomposition (
5.2
) above allows us to efficiently compute
\mathcal{BC}_{n}(G)
,
and to import further structures into
\mathcal{BC}_{n}(G)
.

We start by defining a poset relation on the set of symbols:

\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}:=(H^{\prime},Y^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})\leq(H,Y,\beta)=:\mathfrak{s}

if and only if

•

Y=Y^{\prime}
,

•

H^{\prime}\subseteq H
, and

•

\beta^{\prime}=\beta|_{H^{\prime}}
.

We observe that the
intervals
in this poset relation are isomorphic, as posets, to intervals in the poset
\mathcal{H}
of abelian subgroups of
G
. Locally, these intervals are isomorphic to intervals in posets of subgroups of finite abelian groups; the corresponding Moebius function is
the one in (
2.1
).

Consider the following homomorphisms of
{\mathbb{Z}}
-modules

\Psi,\Phi:{\mathcal{SC}}_{n}(G)\to{\mathcal{SC}}_{n}(G)

defined on symbols by

\Psi:(H,Y,\beta)\mapsto\sum_{H^{\prime}\subseteq H}(H^{\prime},Y,\beta^{\prime})^{\prime},

respectively,

\Phi:(H,Y,\beta)^{\prime}\mapsto\sum_{H^{\prime}\subseteq H}\mu(H^{\prime},H)(H^{\prime},Y,\beta^{\prime}),

where

\beta^{\prime}=\beta|_{H^{\prime}},

and extended by linearity. By convention, if
\beta^{\prime}
contains a zero, the symbol is
considered to be zero.

These are isomorphisms (see Section
2
), we have

\Psi\circ\Phi=\Phi\circ\Psi=\mathrm{Id}.

Theorem 5.2
.

For all
n\geq 1
and all
G
, the homomorphism
\Psi
descends to the respective quotients of the combinatorial symbols group, yielding a commutative diagram of abelian groups

\textstyle{{\mathcal{SC}}_{n}(G)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Proof.

\Psi

\mathbf{(C)}
and
\mathbf{(V)}

It remains to show their compatibility with
(\mathbf{B2})
, respectively,
\mathbf{(B2^{\prime})}
.

First, we show

\mathbf{(B2^{\prime})}
, i.e. for any symbol

we have

\Psi(\mathfrak{s})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{=}}\Psi((H,Y,\beta_{1}))+\Psi((H,Y,\beta_{2}))+\Psi(\Theta_{2}(\mathfrak{s}))\in\mathcal{BC}_{n}^{\prime}(G),

with
\beta_{1}
,
\beta_{2}

defined in (

). Assume

and put

then

\Psi((H,Y,\beta_{i}))=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}H^{\prime}\subseteq H,H^{\prime}\not\subseteq\bar{H}\end{subarray}}(H^{\prime},Y,\beta_{i}|_{H^{\prime}})^{\prime},\quad i=1,2,

since when

, the restriction of
\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}
to

will have nontrivial space of invariants
(i.e., a zero in the sequence of characters).

On the other hand, by definition, we have

\displaystyle\Psi(\mathfrak{s})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}H^{\prime}\subseteq H,H^{\prime}\not\subseteq\bar{H}\end{subarray}}(H^{\prime},Y,\beta|_{H^{\prime}})^{\prime}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}H^{\prime}\subseteq H\cap\bar{H}\end{subarray}}(H^{\prime},Y,\beta|_{H^{\prime}})^{\prime}.

Observe that

Applying
\mathbf{(B2^{\prime})}
to the right side of (

\displaystyle\Psi(\mathfrak{s})=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}H^{\prime}\subseteq H,H^{\prime}\not\subseteq\bar{H}\end{subarray}}(H^{\prime},Y,\beta_{1}|_{H^{\prime}})^{\prime}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}H^{\prime}\subseteq H,H^{\prime}\not\subseteq\bar{H}\end{subarray}}(H^{\prime},Y,\beta_{2}|_{H^{\prime}})^{\prime}

6.
Examples and applications

6.1.
Abelian groups

Classification of abelian subgroups of the plane Cremona group, i.e.,
of actions of abelian groups on rational surfaces, is well-understood (see
[
1
]
, and the references therein). Much less is known in higher dimensions. First applications of the Burnside group formalism to the classification of such actions, in particular to actions of cyclic groups on cubic fourfolds, can be found in
[
5
]
.

When
G
is abelian, Theorem
5.2
, combined with decomposition (
5.2
), shows that

(6.1)

\mathcal{BC}_{n}(G)=\bigoplus_{H^{\prime}\subseteq G}\bigoplus_{H^{\prime\prime}\subseteq H^{\prime}}{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(H^{\prime\prime}).

For elementary abelian
p
-groups
G\simeq{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{r}

{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(G)=0,\quad n<r,

since a sequence of characters of length
<r
cannot generate the character group.
By (
6.1
), the computation of
{\mathcal{BC}}_{n}(G)
reduces to
the computation of
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(H^{\prime\prime})
, where
H^{\prime\prime}={\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{m}
with
m\leq n
.
The number of
H^{\prime\prime}\subseteq G
such that
H^{\prime\prime}\cong{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{m}
is

\#\mathrm{Gr}(m,r)({\mathbb{F}}_{p}).

Results in
[
7
, Section 5]
, especially Theorem 14, yield finer structural information about
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(H^{\prime\prime})\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Problem 6.1
.

Determine the ring structure of
\mathcal{BC}_{*}(G)
, where
G={\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{r}
.

The isomorphisms
\Phi
and
\Psi
induce a ring structure on

\mathcal{BC}^{\prime}_{*}(G):=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{BC}^{\prime}_{n}(G),

with the product map
defined on symbols by

(6.2)

\displaystyle(H,Y,\beta)^{\prime}\widetilde{\times}(H^{\prime},Y^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})^{\prime}\mapsto\Psi(\Phi((H,Y,\beta)^{\prime}){\times}\Phi((H^{\prime},Y^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})^{\prime})).

By construction,
\Psi
and
\Phi
are ring isomorphisms

{\mathcal{BC}}_{*}(G)\simeq{\mathcal{BC}}_{*}^{\prime}(G).

Proposition 6.2
.

When
G
is abelian, the product (
6.2
) takes the form:

(H,Y,\beta)^{\prime}\widetilde{\times}(H^{\prime},Y^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})^{\prime}\longmapsto\begin{cases}0&\text{if }H\neq H^{\prime},\\
(H,Y\cap Y^{\prime},\beta\cup\beta^{\prime})^{\prime}&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

When
G
is abelian, the conjugacy relation plays no role. For any generating symbols

(H,Y,\beta)^{\prime},\quad(H^{\prime},Y^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})^{\prime}\in\mathcal{BC}^{\prime}_{*}(G)

we have, by definition,

\displaystyle\Phi((H,Y,\beta)^{\prime})\times\Phi((H^{\prime},Y^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{\widetilde{H}\subseteq H}\mu(\widetilde{H},H)(\widetilde{H},Y,\beta|_{\widetilde{H}})\right){\times}\left(\sum_{\widetilde{H}^{\prime}\subseteq H^{\prime}}\mu(\widetilde{H}^{\prime},H^{\prime})(\widetilde{H}^{\prime},Y^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}|_{\widetilde{H}^{\prime}})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\widetilde{H}\subseteq H,\widetilde{H}^{\prime}\subseteq H^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\mu(\widetilde{H},H)\mu(\widetilde{H}^{\prime},H^{\prime})\cdot(\widetilde{H}\cap\widetilde{H}^{\prime},Y\cap Y^{\prime},(\beta\cup\beta^{\prime})|_{\widetilde{H}\cap\widetilde{H}^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{H^{\prime\prime}\subseteq H\cap H^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\widetilde{H}\subseteq H,\widetilde{H}^{\prime}\subseteq H^{\prime}\\
\widetilde{H}\cap\widetilde{H}^{\prime}=H^{\prime\prime}\end{subarray}}\mu(\widetilde{H},H)\mu(\widetilde{H}^{\prime},H^{\prime})\right)\cdot(H^{\prime\prime},Y\cap Y^{\prime},(\beta\cup\beta^{\prime})|_{H^{\prime\prime}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\sum_{\widetilde{H}\subseteq H}\mu(\widetilde{H},H)(\widetilde{H},Y\cap Y^{\prime},(\beta\cup\beta^{\prime})|_{\widetilde{H}})}&\text{if }H=H^{\prime},\\
0&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

where the last equality follows from Corollary
2.2
. Applying
\Psi
to the equality above completes the proof.
∎

Abstract.
The free metaplectic transformation (FMT) or the nonseparable linear canonical transformation (NSLCT) has gained much popularity in recent times because of its various application in signal processing, paraxial optical systems, digital algorithms, optical encryption and so on. However, the NSLCT is
inadequate for localized analysis of non-transient
signals, as such, it is
imperative to introduce a unique localized transform coined as the short-time nonseparable linear canonical transformation (ST-NSLCT). In
this paper, we investigate the ST-NSLCT. Firstly, we propose the definition of
the ST-NSLCT, and provide the time-frequency analysis of the proposed transform in
the NSLCT domain. Secondly, we investigate the basic properties of the proposed
transform including the reconstruction formula,
Moyal’s formula. The emergence of the ST-NSLCT definition and its properties
broadens the development of time-frequency representation of higher-dimensional
signals theory to a certain extent. Finally, we extend some different uncertainty principles (UP) from quantum mechanics
including Lieb’s inequality, Pitt’s inequality, Hausdorff-Young inequality, Heisenberg’s uncertainty principle, Hardy’s
UP, Beurling’s UP, Logarithmic UP, and Nazarov’s UP.

Keywords: Short-time non-seperable transformation; Moyals formula; Uncertainty Principle; Nazarov’s UP; Hardy’s UP; Logarithmic’s UP .

2.
Preliminary

This section give some useful definitions and lemmas about the multi-dimensional Fourier transform and non-separable linear canonical transform.

2.1.
Multi-dimensional Fourier transform (FT)

For any
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the n-dimensional
Fourier transform (FT) of
f(x)
is given by
[
32
]

(2.1)

\mathcal{F}[f](w)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)e^{-iw^{T}x}dx,

and its inversion is given by

(2.2)

f(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{F}[f](w)e^{iw^{T}x}dw

where
x=(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
w=(w_{1},w_{2},...,w_{n})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Based on the definition of short-time Fourier transform (STFT)
[
34
]
, we can define n-dimensional STFT as:

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\phi
be a window function in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
for any
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the n-dimensional short-time
Fourier transform (STFT) of
f(x)
with respect to the
window function
\phi
is given by
[
33
]

(2.3)

\mathcal{V}_{\phi}[f](w,u)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}e^{-iw^{T}x}dx.

2.2.
The non-separable linear canonical transform

For typographical convenience, we shall denote a
2n\times 2n
matrix
{\bf M}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&B\\
C&D\\
\end{array}\right)
as
{\bf M}=(A,B:C,D),
where
A
,
B
,
C
and
D
are real
n\times n
sub-matrices. Moreover, we recall that the matrix
\bf M
is said to be a free
symplectic matrix if
{\bf M}^{T}\bf\Omega M=\bf\Omega,
and
|det(B)|\neq 0
, where
{\bf\Omega}=({0,I_{n}:-I_{n},0})
and
I_{n}
represents the
n\times n
identity matrix. The sub-matrices of
{\bf M}
satisfying the following constraints:

AB^{T}=BA^{T},\quad CD^{T}=DC^{T},\quad AD^{T}-BC^{T}=I_{n}.

The transpose of
{\bf M}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&B\\
C&D\\
\end{array}\right)
is given by
{\bf M}^{T}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A^{T}&C^{T}\\
B^{T}&D^{T}\\
\end{array}\right).

Also inverse of the free
symplectic matrix is given by
{\bf M}^{-1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}D^{T}&-B^{T}\\
-C^{T}&A^{T}\\
\end{array}\right).

It is clear that
\bf MM^{-1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}I_{n}&0\\
0&I_{n}\\
\end{array}\right)
.

Definition 2.2

(NSLCT)
.

[
39
]
The non-separable
linear canonical transform or the free metaplectic transform of a function
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with respect
2n\times 2n
real, free symplectic matrix
{\bf M}=(A,B:C,D)
(with
|det(B)|\neq 0
) is defined by

(2.4)

\mathcal{L}_{\bf M}[f](w)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\mathcal{K}_{M}(x,w)dx

where
\mathcal{K}_{\bf M}(x,w)
denotes the kernel and is given by

(2.5)

\mathcal{K}_{\bf M}(x,w)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}\sqrt{|det(B)|}}e^{\frac{i}{2}(w^{T}DB^{-1}w-2w^{T}B^{-T}x+x^{T}B^{-1}Ax)}

where
x=(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
w=(w_{1},w_{2},...,w_{n})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Definition 2.3
.

Suppose
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then the inversion of the non-separable linear canonical transform of

f
is given by

(2.6)

f(x)=\mathcal{L}_{\bf M^{-1}}\left\{\mathcal{L}_{\bf M}[f](w)\right\}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{L}_{\bf M}[f](w)\mathcal{K}_{\bf M^{-1}}(w,x)dw,

where
{\bf M}^{-1}=(D^{T},-B^{T}:-C^{T},A^{T}).

For real, symplectic matrix
{\bf M}=(A,B:C,D)
, the non-separable linear canonical transform
kernel (
2.5
) satisfies the following important properties:

(i)
\mathcal{K}_{\bf M^{-1}}(w,x)=\overline{\mathcal{K}_{\bf M}(x,w)},

(ii)
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{K}_{\bf M}(x,w)\mathcal{K}_{\bf M^{-1}}(z,x)dx=\delta(z-w).

(iii)
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{K}_{\bf M}(x,w)\mathcal{K}_{\bf N}(x,z)dx=\mathcal{K}_{\bf MN}(w,z).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then the non-separable linear canonical transform satisfies the following parseval’s formula:

(2.7)

\langle f,g\rangle_{2}=\langle\mathcal{L}_{\bf M}[f],\mathcal{L}_{\bf M}[g]\rangle_{2}.

For
f=g
, one have

(2.8)

\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{n})}=\|\mathcal{L}_{\bf M}[f]\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{n})}.

The non-separable linear canonical transform (defined in (
2.4
)) of a function
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
can be computed via associated n-dimensional FT, namely

(2.9)

\mathcal{L}_{\bf M}[f](w)=\frac{e^{\frac{i(w^{T}DB^{-1}w)}{2}}}{\sqrt{det(B)}}\mathcal{F}\left\{e^{\frac{i(x^{T}B^{-1}Ax)}{2}}f(x)\right\}(B^{-1}w),

where
\mathcal{F}\{f\}(w)
represents the n-dimensional FT defined in (
2.1
)

(2.10)

\mathcal{F}\{f\}(w)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)e^{-iw^{T}x}dx.

Eq.(
2.9
) can be rewritten as

(2.11)

e^{\frac{-i(w^{T}DB^{-1}w)}{2}}\mathcal{L}_{\bf M}[f](w)=\mathcal{F}\left\{H\right\}(B^{-1}w),

where
H(x)

is given by

(2.12)

H(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{det(B)}}e^{\frac{i(x^{T}B^{-1}Ax)}{2}}f(x)

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
H(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{det(B)}}e^{\frac{i(x^{T}B^{-1}Ax)}{2}}f(x)
, then we have following relationship between n-dimensional FT and NSLCT

(2.13)

|\mathcal{F}[H](w)|=|\mathcal{L}_{\bf M}[f](Bw)|.

Proof.

The proof follows by
changing
w=Bw
and taking modulus in (
2.11
).

∎

Remark 2.1
.

: If we change
w=Bw
and take modulus in (
2.9
), we have

(2.14)

|\mathcal{F}[H_{0}](w)|=\sqrt{det(B)}|\mathcal{L}_{\bf M}[f](Bw)|,

where
H_{0}(x)={e^{\frac{i(x^{T}B^{-1}Ax)}{2}}}f(x)
.

3.
The Short-time Non-separable Linear Canonical Transform

In this section, we introduce the novel short-time non-separable linear canonical transform
(ST-NSLCT) and discuss several basic properties
of the ST-NSLCT. These properties play important roles in signal representation of multi-dimensional signals.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
{\bf M}=(A,B:C,D)
be a real, free symplectic matrix. Let
\phi
be a window function in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the short-time non-separable linear canonical transform
(ST-NSLCT) of the function
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
respect to
\phi
is defined by

(3.1)

\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}\mathcal{K}_{\bf M}(x,w)dx,

where
x=(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
u=(u_{1},u_{2},...,u_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
w=(w_{1},w_{2},...,w_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathcal{K}_{\bf M}(x,w)
is given by (
2.5
).

In terms of classic convolution, formula (
3.1
) can be written as

\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)=(f(u)\mathcal{K}_{\bf M}(u,w))\ast\overline{\phi(-u)}.

Also by applying the properties of non-separable linear canonical transform, (
3.1
) can be rewritten in the form of an
inner product as

(3.2)

\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)=\langle f,\Psi^{\bf M}_{x,w,u}\rangle,

where
\Psi^{\bf M}_{x,w,u}=\phi(x-u)\mathcal{K}_{\bf M}(u,w).

The prolificacy of the short-time non-separable linear canonical transform given in Definition
3.1
can be ascertained
from the following important deductions:

(i)

When
{\bf M}=(I_{n}\cos\alpha,I_{n}\sin\alpha:-I_{n}\sin\alpha,I_{n}\cos\alpha)

the ST-NSLCT (
3.1
) yields the
n-dimensional non-separable short-time fractional Fourier transform:

\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{\phi}[f](w,u)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}|\sin\alpha|^{n/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}e^{\frac{i}{2}(w^{T}w+x^{T}x)\cot\alpha-iw^{T}x\csc\alpha}dx.\\
\\

(ii)

For
{\bf M}=({\bf 0},I_{n}\>-I_{n},{\bf 0})

the ST-NSLCT (
3.1
) boils to
n-dimensional short-time Fourier transform:

\mathcal{F}_{\phi}[f](w,u)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}e^{-iw^{T}x}dx.\\
\\

(iii)

If the sub-matrices
A=diag(a_{11},...,a_{nn})
,
B=diag(b_{11},...,b_{nn})
,
C=diag(c_{11},...,c_{nn})
and
D=diag(d_{11},...,d_{nn})
of the real, symplectic matrix
{\bf M}
are taken then ST-NSLCT (
3.1
)yields short-time separable
linear canonical transform:

\mathcal{L}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}|\Pi_{j=1}^{n}b_{jj}|^{1/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}e^{i\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\frac{d_{jj}w^{2}_{j}-2w_{j}x_{j}+a_{jj}x^{2}_{j}}{2b_{jj}}\right)}dx.\\

(iv)

For
{\bf M}=(I_{n},B:{\bf 0},I_{n})

the ST-NSLCT (
3.1
) boils down to
n-dimensional short-time Fresnel transform:

\mathcal{F}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}\sqrt{|det(B)|}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}e^{i\frac{(w^{T}B^{-1}w-2w^{T}B^{-T}x+x^{T}B^{-1}x)}{2}}dx.\\
\\

Now we shall establish relationsip between NSLCT and ST-NSLCT, which will be helpful in establishing some UP’s in section
4
.

For fixed
u,
we have

(3.3)

\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)=\mathcal{L}_{\bf M}\left\{f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}\right\}(w)

Applying inverse non-separable linear canonical transform
(
2.6
), we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\bf M^{-1}}\left\{\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)\mathcal{K}_{\bf M^{-1}}(w,x)dw

(3.5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)\overline{\mathcal{K}_{\bf M}(x,w)}dw.

Now, we discuss several basic properties of the ST-NSLCT given by (
3.1
). These properties
play important roles in multi-dimensional signal processing.

The properties which follows directly from definition of the ST-NSLCT viz: Linearity; Anti-linearity; Translation; Modulation and Scaling are omitted. We shall focus on boundedness, Moyal’s formula and inversion.

Lemma 3.1

( Relation with n-dimensional STFT)
.

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}\sqrt{|det(B)|}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}e^{\frac{i(w^{T}DB^{-1}w+x^{T}B^{-1}Ax-2w^{T}B^{-T}x)}{2}}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{i\frac{w^{T}DB^{-1}w}{2}}}{(2\pi)^{n/2}\sqrt{|det(B)|}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{\frac{i(x^{T}B^{-1}Ax)}{2}}f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}e^{-i(B^{-1}w)^{T}x}dx

\displaystyle={e^{i\frac{w^{T}DB^{-1}w}{2}}}\mathcal{V}_{\phi}[H](B^{-1}w,u),

where
\mathcal{V}_{\phi}[f]
represents n-dimensional STFT given in Definition
2.1
.

And

(3.6)

H(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|det(A)|}}e^{\frac{i(x^{T}B^{-1}Ax)}{2}}f(x).\\

Next we prove the lemma which is very important in establishing various uncertainty principles in Section
4

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
F(x)=e^{\frac{i(x^{T}B^{-1}Ax)}{2}}f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}
, then we have following relationship between n-dimensional FT and ST-NSLCT.

(3.7)

|\mathcal{F}[F](w)|=\sqrt{|det(B)|}|\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](Bw,u)|.

Proof.

From (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}\sqrt{|det(B)|}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}e^{\frac{i(w^{T}DB^{-1}w+x^{T}B^{-1}Ax-2w^{T}B^{-T}x)}{2}}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{i\frac{w^{T}DB^{-1}w}{2}}}{(2\pi)^{n/2}\sqrt{|det(B)|}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{\frac{i(x^{T}B^{-1}Ax)}{2}}f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}e^{-i(B^{-1}w)^{T}x}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|det(B)|}}{e^{i\frac{w^{T}DB^{-1}w}{2}}}\mathcal{F}[F](B^{-1}w),

On changing

\sqrt{|det(B)|}|\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](Bw,u)|=|\mathcal{F}[F](w)|

.

Remark 3.1
.

: If we take
F_{0}(x)=\frac{e^{\frac{i(x^{T}B^{-1}Ax)}{2}}}{\sqrt{det(B)}}f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}

(3.8)

Theorem 3.2

(Boundedness)
.

Let
f,\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where
\phi
is a non zero window function then we have

\left|\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)\right|\leq\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}\sqrt{|det(B)|}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

Proof.

By using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in Definition
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}\mathcal{K}_{\bf M}(x,w)dx\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}\mathcal{K}_{\bf M}(x,w)\right|dx\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}\sqrt{|det(B)|}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}e^{\frac{i(w^{T}DB^{-1}w+x^{T}B^{-1}Ax-2w^{T}B^{-T}x)}{2}}\right|dx\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}\sqrt{|det(B)|}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}\right|dx\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}{|det(B)|}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}\right|dx\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}{|det(B)|}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|f(x)\right|^{2}dx\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\overline{\phi(x-u)}\right|^{2}dx\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}{|det(B)|}}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

On further simplification, we obtain

\left|\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)\right|\leq\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}\sqrt{|det(B)|}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

which completes the proof.

∎

In the following theorem, we show that the proposed ST-NSLCT is reversible in the sense
that the input signal
f
can be recovered easily from the transformed domain.

Theorem 3.3

(Inversion)
.

For any fixed window function
\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).
Then, any
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
can be reconstructed by the formula

(3.9)

f(x)=\frac{1}{\|\phi(x)\|^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)\phi(x-u)\mathcal{K}_{\bf M^{-1}}(x,w)dwdu

Proof.

From (
3.4
), we have

\displaystyle f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\bf M^{-1}}\left\{\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)\mathcal{K}_{\bf M^{-1}}(x,w)dw

Multiplying both sides of last equation by
\phi(x-u)
and integrating
with respect to
du
, yields

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}\phi(x-u)du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)\mathcal{K}_{\bf M^{-1}}(x,w)\phi(x-u)dwdu

\displaystyle\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)|\phi(x-u)|^{2}du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)\phi(x-u)\mathcal{K}_{\bf M^{-1}}(x,w)dwdu

\displaystyle\ f(x)\|\phi(x)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)\phi(x-u)\mathcal{K}_{\bf M^{-1}}(x,w)dwdu,

which implies

f(x)=\frac{1}{\|\phi(x)\|^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)\phi(x-u)\mathcal{K}_{\bf M^{-1}}(x,w)dwdu.\\

∎

Theorem 3.4

(Moyal’s formula)
.

Let
\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi_{1}}[f](w,u
and
\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi_{2}}[g](w,u)
be the ST-NSLCT transforms of
f
and
g
, respectively. Then, we have

(3.10)

\left\langle\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi_{1}}[f](w,u),\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi_{2}}[g](w,u)\right\rangle=\langle f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\langle\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

Proof.

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi_{1}}[f](w,u),\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi_{2}}[g](w,u)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi_{1}}[f](w,u)\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi_{2}}[g](w,u)dwdu

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi_{1}}[f](w,u)\left[\overline{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)\overline{\phi_{2}(x-u)}\mathcal{K}_{\bf M}(x,w)dx}\right]dwdu

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi_{1}}[f](w,u)\overline{\mathcal{K}_{\bf M}(x,w)}.\overline{g(x)}\phi_{2}(x-u)dxdudw

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi_{1}}[f](w,u)\overline{\mathcal{K}_{\bf M}(x,w)}dw\right]\overline{g(x)}\phi_{2}(x-u)dxdu.

Now applying (
3.4
) in above equation, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi_{1}}[f](w,u),\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi_{2}}[g](w,u)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\overline{\phi_{1}(x-u)}.\overline{g(x)}\phi_{2}(x-u)dxdu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\overline{g(x)}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\overline{\phi_{1}(x-u)}\phi_{2}(x-u)du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\langle\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

which completes the proof.
∎

From the above theorem, we obtain the following consequences.

(i) If
\phi_{1}=\phi_{2},
then

(3.11)

\left\langle\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u),\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[g](w,u)\right\rangle=\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\langle f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.\\
\\

(ii) If
f=g
and
\phi_{1}=\phi_{2},
then

(3.12)

\left\langle\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u),\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)\right\rangle=\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(iii) If
f=g
and
\phi_{1}=\phi_{2}=1,
then

(3.13)

\left\langle\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u),\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)\right\rangle=\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Equation (
3.13
) states that the proposed ST-NSLCT(
3.1
) becomes an isometry from
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
into
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In
other words, the total energy of a signal computed in the in the
short-time non-seperable linear canonical domain is equal to the total energy computed in
the spatial domain.

4.
Uncertainty Principles for the Short-time Non-seperable Linear canonical Transformation

The uncertainty principle lies at the heart of harmonic analysis, which asserts that
“the position and the velocity of a particle cannot be both determined precisely at the
same time”. Authors in
[
32
,
39
]
studied various uncertainty principles associated with non-seperable LCT, since ST-NSLCT is the generalized version of NSLCT and keeping in view the fact that the theory of UP’s for the short-time non-separable
linear canonical transform is yet to be explored exclusively; therefore, it is natural and interesting to study different forms of UP’s in the ST-NSLCT domain.

4.1.
Pitt’s inequality

The Pitt’s inequality in the Fourier domain expresses a fundamental relationship between
a sufficiently smooth function and the corresponding Fourier transform (Beckner 1995)
[
27
]
. In
[
32
]
authors introduced the Pitt’s inequality can be extended to the free metaplectic transformation domain, we here extend it to the ST-NSLCT domain as:

Lemma 4.1

(Pitt’s inequality for the NSLCT
[
35
]
)
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the Schwartz class in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then we have the inequality

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w|^{-\alpha}|\mathcal{L}_{\bf M}[f](w)|^{2}dw\leq C_{\alpha}|det(B)|^{-\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{\alpha}|f(x)|^{2}dx,\\
\\

where
C_{\alpha}=\pi^{\alpha}\left[\Gamma\left(\frac{n-\alpha}{4}\right)/\Gamma\left(\frac{n+\alpha}{4}\right)\right]^{2}
and
0\leq\alpha<n.

Based on Pitt’s inequality for the NSLCT, we obtain Pitt’s
inequality for the ST-NSLCT.

Theorem 4.1

(Pitt’s inequality for the ST-NSLCT)
.

Under the assumptions of Lemma
4.1
, we have

(4.1)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w|^{-\alpha}|\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)|^{2}dwdu

(4.2)

\displaystyle=C_{\alpha}|det(B)|^{-\alpha}\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{\alpha}|f(x)|^{2}dx.

Proof.

Let
f^{\phi}_{u}(x)=f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}

then by virtue of (
3.3
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w|^{-\alpha}|\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)|^{2}dw=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w|^{-\alpha}|\mathcal{L}_{\bf M}[f^{\phi}_{u}(x)]|^{2}dw.

Now applying Lemma
4.1
to the L.H.S of above equation, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w|^{-\alpha}|\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)|^{2}dw

\displaystyle\leq C_{\alpha}|det(B)|^{-\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{\alpha}|f^{\phi}_{u}(x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq C_{\alpha}|det(B)|^{-\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{\alpha}|f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}|^{2}dx.

On integrating both sides of (
4.1
)with respect to
du
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w|^{-\alpha}|\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)|^{2}dwdu

\displaystyle\leq C_{\alpha}|det(B)|^{-\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{\alpha}|f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}|^{2}dxdu

\displaystyle=C_{\alpha}|det(B)|^{-\alpha}\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{\alpha}|f(x)|^{2}dx,

which completes the proof.
∎

4.2.
Lieb’s UP

Here we shall establish Lieb’s
Uncertainty Principle for the ST-NSLCT by using the relation between ST-NSLCT with the short-time Fourier transform in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Theorem 4.2

(Lieb’s)
.

Let
\phi,f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
2\leq p<\infty
. Then following inequality holds

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)|^{p}dwdx\leq\frac{2}{p}\frac{|B|}{{|det(A)|}^{p/2}}(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})})^{p}.

Proof.

For every
f\in\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),
the Leib’s inequality for the STFT states that

(4.4)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\mathcal{V}_{\phi}[f](w,u)|^{p}dwdx\leq\frac{2}{p}\left(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\right)^{p},

where
\mathcal{V}_{\phi}[f](w,u)
denotes the STFT of
f
given by (
2.1
) and
\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the Schwartz class in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. To obtain an analogue of the Leib’s inequality for the ST-NSLCT, we replace
f
in above equation by
H
[defined in
2.12
], we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\mathcal{V}_{\phi}[H](w,u)|^{p}dwdx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{p}\left(\|H\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\right)^{p}

(4.5)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{p}\left(\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{|det(A)|}}e^{\frac{i(x^{T}B^{-1}Ax)}{2}}f(x)\right|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\right)^{p}

Setting
w=B^{-1}w
in (
4.5
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|B^{-1}||\mathcal{V}_{\phi}[H](B^{-1}w,u)|^{p}dwdx

(4.6)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{p}\frac{1}{{|det(A)|}^{p/2}}\left(\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|f(x)\right|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\right)^{p}.

Now using Lemma
3.1
to L.H.S of (
4.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|B^{-1}||e^{\frac{-iw^{T}DB^{-1}w}{2}}\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)|^{p}dwdx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{p}\frac{1}{{|det(A)|}^{p/2}}\left(\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|f(x)\right|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\right)^{p}

on further simplifying, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)|^{p}dwdx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2|B|}{p}\frac{1}{{|det(A)|}^{p/2}}\left(\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|f(x)\right|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\right)^{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{p}\frac{|B|}{{|det(A)|}^{p/2}}(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})})^{p}.

Which completes the proof.

∎

4.3.
Heisenberg’s UP

In
[
32
]
, authors introduced Heisenberg’s UP to observe
the lower bound of uncertainty corresponding to the free metaplectic transformation. We extend it to the propose Heisenberg’s UP fot the ST-NSLCT.

Theorem 4.3

(Heisenberg’s UP)
.

Let
\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f]
be the short time non-separable linear canonical transform of any
non-trivial function
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with respect to a real free symplectic matrix
{\bf M}=(A,B:C,D),
then following inequality holds:

\displaystyle\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w|^{2}\left|\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)\right|^{2}dw\right\}^{1/2}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x^{2}\left|f(x)\right|^{2}dx\right\}^{1/2}

(4.7)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n\sigma_{min}(B)}{4\pi}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Proof.

For any
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the Heisenberg–Pauli–Weyl uncertainty inequality for NSLCT domain is given by
[
32
]
:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x^{2}|f(x)|^{2}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w|^{2}|\mathcal{L}_{\bf M}[f](w)|^{2}dw\geq\frac{n^{2}\sigma^{2}_{min}(B)}{16\pi^{2}}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|^{2}dx\right\}^{2}

Using the inversion of NSLCT (
2.6
) into LHS and (
2.8
) to the RHS, above inequality becomes

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x^{2}\left|\mathcal{L}_{\bf M^{-1}}\{\mathcal{L}_{\bf M}[f](w)\}\right|^{2}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w|^{2}\left|\mathcal{L}_{\bf M}[f](w)\right|^{2}dw

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n^{2}\sigma^{2}_{min}(B)}{16\pi^{2}}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\mathcal{L}_{\bf M}[f](w)\right|^{2}dw\right\}^{2}

since
\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f]\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),
therefore replacing
\mathcal{L}_{\bf M}[f](w)
by
\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f]\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x^{2}\left|\mathcal{L}_{\bf M^{-1}}\{\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)\}\right|^{2}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w|^{2}\left|\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)\right|^{2}dw

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n^{2}\sigma^{2}_{min}(B)}{16\pi^{2}}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)\right|^{2}dw\right\}^{2}

First taking square of above equation and then integrating both sides with respect
du
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x^{2}\left|\mathcal{L}_{\bf M^{-1}}\{\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)\}\right|^{2}dx\right\}^{1/2}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w|^{2}\left|\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)\right|^{2}dw\right\}^{1/2}du

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n\sigma_{min}(B)}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)\right|^{2}dwdu

As a consequence of the Cauchy–Schwartz’s inequality and Fubini theorem, above inequality yields

\displaystyle\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x^{2}\left|\mathcal{L}_{\bf M^{-1}}\{\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)\}\right|^{2}dxdu\right\}^{1/2}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w|^{2}\left|\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)\right|^{2}dw\right\}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n\sigma_{min}(B)}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)\right|^{2}dwdu

Now using (
3.4
) in L.H.S and (
3.12
) in R.H.S of above equation, we have

\displaystyle\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x^{2}\left|f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}\right|^{2}dxdu\right\}^{1/2}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w|^{2}\left|\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)\right|^{2}dw\right\}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n\sigma_{min}(B)}{4\pi}\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Simplifying above, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\{\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x^{2}\left|f(x)\right|^{2}dx\right\}^{1/2}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w|^{2}\left|\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)\right|^{2}dw\right\}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n\sigma_{min}(B)}{4\pi}\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

dividing both sides by
\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},
we will get the desired result.
∎

Theorem 4.4

(Hausdorff–Young)
.

For
Let
1\leq p\leq 2
and
q
be such that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1.

Then for
\phi\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),
following inequality holds

(4.8)

\|\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|\phi\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Proof.

The definition of NSLCT
2.2
and procedure defined in
[
36
]
[Theorem 5.1] together led to

(4.9)

\|\mathcal{L}_{\bf M}[f]\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

For
p=1
, we get

(4.10)

\|\mathcal{L}_{\bf M}[f]\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Now using (
4.10
) and taking
\|\phi\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=1
, equation (
3.3
) yields

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{L}_{\bf M}\{f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}\}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\{f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}\}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|{\phi}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Now for
p=2
, we obtain

\|\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

\|\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(4.12)

\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{g}[f](w,u)=\frac{1}{\|\phi\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u),

on taking
\phi=g
in (
4.11
), we obtain

(4.13)

\|\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{g}[f](w,u)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

which on simplification becomes

\|\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|\phi\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Hence completes the proof.
∎

4.4.
Logarithmic UP

In 1995

[
37
]
W. Beckner introduced Logarithmic uncertainty principle. In this subsection we obtain the concept of Logarithmic
uncertainty principle for the Short-time non-separable linear canonical transform as follows:

Theorem 4.5

(Logarithmic uncertainty principle )
.

Let
\phi
be a window function in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and let
\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)\in\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),
then the short-time
non-separable linear canonical transform satisfies the
following logarithmic uncertainty inequality:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\ln|wB^{-T}||\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)|^{2}dwdu+\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\ln|x||f(x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}(n/2)}{\Gamma(n/2)}-\ln\pi\right)\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Proof.

For any
f\in\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the logarithmic uncertainty principle for the non-separable linear canonical transform domain is given by
[
32
]
:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\ln|x||f(x)|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\ln|wB^{-T}||\mathcal{L}_{\bf M}[f](w)|^{2}dw

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}(n/2)}{\Gamma(n/2)}-\ln\pi\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|^{2}dx.

Now invoking the inversion formula of the non-separable linear canonical transform on the L.H.S and Parseval’s formula on R.H.S, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\ln|x||\mathcal{L}_{\bf M^{-1}}\{\mathcal{L}_{\bf M}[f](w)\}|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\ln|wB^{-T}||\mathcal{L}_{\bf M}[f](w)|^{2}dw

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}(n/2)}{\Gamma(n/2)}-\ln\pi\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\mathcal{L}_{\bf M}[f](w)|^{2}dw.

Since both
\mathcal{L}_{\bf M}[f](w)
and
\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)
are in
\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
thus we can replace
\mathcal{L}_{\bf M}[f](w)
by
\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)

on the both sides of above, to get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\ln|x||\mathcal{L}_{\bf M^{-1}}\{\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)\}|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\ln|wB^{-T}||\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)|^{2}dw

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}(n/2)}{\Gamma(n/2)}-\ln\pi\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)|^{2}dw.

Integrating above inequality with respect
du
on both sides and then by virtue of Fubini’s theorem, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\ln|x||\mathcal{L}_{\bf M^{-1}}\{\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)\}|^{2}dxdu+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\ln|wB^{-T}||\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)|^{2}dwdu

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}(n/2)}{\Gamma(n/2)}-\ln\pi\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)|^{2}dwdu.

Now using (
3.4
) in L.H.S and (
3.12
) in R.H.S of above inequality, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\ln|wB^{-T}||\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)|^{2}dwdu+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\ln|x||f(x)\phi(x-u)|^{2}dxdu

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}(n/2)}{\Gamma(n/2)}-\ln\pi\right)\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Further simplifying left-hand side, we obtain the desired result as

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\ln|wB^{-T}||\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)|^{2}dwdu+\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\ln|x||f(x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}(n/2)}{\Gamma(n/2)}-\ln\pi\right)\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

∎

4.5.
Hardy’s UP

G.H. Hardy first introduced the Hardy’s uncertainty principle in 1933
[
40
]
. Hardy’s uncertainty principle says that it
is impossible for a function and its Fourier transform to decrease very rapidly simultaneously. Hardy’s UP in the Fourier transform domain
[
41
]
was
given as follows.

Lemma 4.2

(Hardy’s UP in the Fourier transform
[
41
]
)
.

f(x)=\mathcal{O}(e^{-|x|^{2}/\beta^{2}})

\mathcal{F}[f](w)=\mathcal{O}((2\pi)^{n/2}e^{-16\pi^{2}|w|^{2}/\alpha^{2}})

1/\alpha\beta>1/4,
then
f\equiv 0.
If
1/\alpha\beta=1/4,
then

f=Ce^{-|x|^{2}/\beta^{2}}.

Where
C
is a constant in
\mathbb{C}

Based on Lemma
4.2
, we derive the corresponding Hardy’s UP for the ST-NSLCT.

Theorem 4.6

(Hardy’s UP in the ST-NSLCT)
.

If
f(x)=\mathcal{O}(e^{-|x|^{2}/\beta^{2}})
,
\mathcal{F}[f](w)=\mathcal{O}((2\pi)^{n/2}e^{-16\pi^{2}|B^{-1}w|^{2}/\alpha^{2}})
and
1/\alpha\beta>1/4,
then
f\equiv 0.
If
1/\alpha\beta=1/4,
then

f=Ce^{-|x|^{2}/\beta^{2}-\frac{i(x^{T}B^{-1}Ax)}{2}}.

Where
C
is a constant in
\mathbb{C}

Proof.

Consider then function
F(x)=e^{\frac{i(x^{T}B^{-1}Ax)}{2}}f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}
.

On setting
u=x
then we have

|F(x)|=|f(x)||\overline{\phi(0)}|=\mathcal{O}(e^{-|x|^{2}/\beta^{2}})

Also by Lemma
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}[F](w)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{det(B)}|\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](Bw,u)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}((2\pi)^{n/2}e^{-16\pi^{2}|B^{-1}w|^{2}/\alpha^{2}}).

Following from Lemma
4.2
:

If

we have
F\equiv 0
which implies

.

Also,if

then

F(x)=Ce^{-|x|^{2}/\beta^{2}}

implies

f(x)=Ce^{-|x|^{2}/\beta^{2}-\frac{i(x^{T}B^{-1}Ax)}{2}}.

∎

4.6.
Beurling’s UP

Beurling’s uncertainty principle is a more general version of Hardy’s uncertainty principle, which is given by A.Beurling. It implies the weak form of Hardy’s UP immediately.
Beurling’s UP in the Fourier transform domain is as follows

Lemma 4.3

(Beurling’s UP in the Fourier transform domain
[
25
]
)
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
d\geq 0
satisfy

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\dfrac{|f(x)||\mathcal{F}[f](w)|}{(1+\|x\|+\|w\|)^{d}}e^{2\pi|\langle x,w\rangle|}dxdw\leq\infty.

then

f(x)=P(x)e^{-\pi\langle Ax,x\rangle},

where
A
is a real positive definite symmetric matrix and
P(x)
is a polynomial of degree
<\frac{d-n}{2}.

According to Lemma
4.3
, we derive Beurling’s uncertainty principle for the ST-NSLCT.

Theorem 4.7

(Beurling’s UP )
.

Let
f,\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
where
\phi
is a nonzero window function and
\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f]\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfy

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\dfrac{|f(x)||\overline{\phi(x-u)}||\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)|}{\sqrt{|det(B)|}(1+\|x\|+\|B^{-1}w\|)^{d}}e^{2\pi|\langle x,B^{-1}w\rangle|}dxdw<\infty,\quad where\quad d\geq 0.\\
\

Then

f(x)=P(x)/\overline{\phi(x-u)}e^{\frac{-i(x^{T}B^{-1}Ax)}{2}-\pi\langle Ax,x\rangle},

where
A
is a real positive definite symmetric matrix and
P(x)
is a polynomial of degree
<\frac{d-n}{2}.

Proof.

Consider then function
F(x)=e^{\frac{i(x^{T}B^{-1}Ax)}{2}}f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}
, then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\dfrac{|F(x)||\mathcal{F}[F](w)|}{(1+\|x\|+\|w\|)^{d}}e^{2\pi|\langle x,w\rangle|}dxdw

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\dfrac{|f(x)||\overline{\phi(x-u)}|\sqrt{|det(B)|}|\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](Bw,u)|}{(1+\|x\|+\|w\|)^{d}}e^{2\pi|\langle x,w\rangle|}dxdw

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\dfrac{|f(x)||\overline{\phi(x-u)}||\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)|}{\sqrt{|det(B)|}(1+\|x\|+\|B^{-1}w\|)^{d}}e^{2\pi|\langle x,B^{-1}w\rangle|}dxdw<\infty.

Therefore by Lemma
4.3
, we have

F(x)=P(x)e^{-\pi\langle Ax,x\rangle},

where
A
is a real positive definite symmetric matrix and
P(x)
is a polynomial of degree
<\frac{d-n}{2}.

Furthermore
f(x)=P(x)/\overline{\phi(x-u)}e^{\frac{-i(x^{T}B^{-1}Ax)}{2}-\pi\langle Ax,x\rangle}.

∎

This completes the proof.

4.7.
Nazarov’s UP for the ST-NSLCT

Nazarov’s UP was first proposed by F.L. Nazarov in 1993
[
23
]
. It
measures the localization of a nonzero function by taking into consideration the notion of support of the function instead of the dispersion. In other words it argues what happens if a non-trival function and its Fourier transform are only small outside a compact
set.Let us start with Nazarov’s UP for the Fourier transform.

Lemma 4.4

(Nazarov’s UP for the Fourier transform
[
24
]
)
.

There exists a constant
K
, such that for finite Lebesgue measurable sets
S
,
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and for every
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),
we have

Ke^{K(S,E)}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash S}|f(x)|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash E}|\mathcal{F}[f](w)|^{2}dw\right)\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|^{2}dx

where
K(S,E)={Kmin(|S||E|,|S|^{1/n},\mu(E),\mu(S)|E|^{1/n})},

\mu(S)
is the mean width of
S
and
|S|
denotes the Lebesgue measure of
S.

Now we shall establish Nazarov’s UP to the short-time non-sperable linear canonical transform domain.

Theorem 4.8

(Nazarov’s UP)
.

Let
\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f]
be the short-stime NSLCT, then under the assumptions of Lemma
4.4
for every
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq Ke^{K(S,E)}\left(\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash S}|f(x)|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}(\backslash EB)}|\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}[f](w,u)|^{2}dwdu\right).

Proof.

Applying Lemma
4.4
to the function
F(x)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
defined in Lemma
3.2
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|F(x)|^{2}dx\leq Ke^{K(S,E)}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash S}|F(x)|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash E}|\mathcal{F}[F](w)|^{2}dw\right)

Now with the help of Lemma
3.7
, above yields

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq Ke^{K(S,E)}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash S}|f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash E}|\sqrt{|det(B)|}\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}(Bw,u)|^{2}dw\right).

Integrating above equation both sides with respect to
u
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}|^{2}dxdu

\displaystyle\leq Ke^{K(S,E)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash S}|f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash E}|\sqrt{|det(B)|}\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}(Bw,u)|^{2}dw\right)du.

Implementing Fubini’s theorem, we have

\displaystyle\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq Ke^{K(S,E)}\left(\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash S}|f(x)|^{2}dx+{|det(B)|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash E}|\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}(Bw,u)|^{2}dwdu\right)

\displaystyle=Ke^{K(S,E)}\left(\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash S}|f(x)|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}(\backslash EB)}|\mathcal{V}^{\bf M}_{\phi}(w,u)|^{2}dwdu\right),

which completes the proof.

∎

Alternative proof:

For any function

of

5.
Conclusion

In this paper we presented a novel concept of short-time non-separable linear canonical transform. Based on the properties of ST-NSLCT and NSLCT, the relationship between these two notations are presented. Important properties such as boundedness, reconstruction formula, Moyals formula and are derived. Finally, we extend some different uncertainty principles (UP) from quantum mechanics
including Lieb’s, Pitt’s UP, Heisenberg’s uncertainty principle, Hausdorff-Young, Hardy’s
UP, Beurling’s UP, Logarithmic UP, and Nazarov’s UP which have already been well studied in the
ST-NSLC domain.

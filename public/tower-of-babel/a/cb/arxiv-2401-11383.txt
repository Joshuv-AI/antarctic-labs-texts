Entropic conditional central limit theorem and Hadamard Compression
Zhi-Ming Malabel=e1]mazm@amt.ac.cn, Liu-Quan Yaolabel=e2]yaoliuquan20@mails.ucas.ac.cn, Shuai Yuanlabel=e3]yuanshuai2020@amss.ac.cn, Hua-Zi Zhanglabel=e4]zhanghuazi@huawei.com

Abstract
We make use of an entropic property to establish a convergence theorem (Main Theorem), which reveals that the conditional entropy measures the asymptotic Gaussianity. As an application, we establish the entropic conditional central limit theorem (CCLT), which is stronger than the classical CCLT. As another application, we show that continuous input under iterated Hadamard transform, almost every distribution of the output conditional on the values of the previous signals will tend to Gaussian, and the conditional distribution is in fact insensitive to the condition. The results enable us to make a theoretic study concerning Hadamard compression, which provides a solid theoretical analysis supporting the simulation results in previous literature. We show also that the conditional Fisher information can be used to measure the asymptotic Gaussianity.

Introduction

1.1
Entropic Central Limit Theorem

Suppose
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\cdots,\xi_{n}
are independent and identically distributed (
i.i.d.
) random variables with mean
\mu
and variance
\sigma^{2}
. Central limit theorem (CLT) states that the normalized partial sum

W_{n}=\frac{\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}(\xi_{k}-\mu)}{\sqrt{n\sigma^{2}}}

(1)

is asymptotically Gaussian distributed in weak sense. Different from classical proof of CLT based on tools of characteristic functions, the entropic method, which is started by Linnik (1959)
[
26
]
, provides an information-theoretic proof of CLT by studying the behaviour of entropy
h(W_{n})
. Recall that for a random variable
X
with continuous density
f
, the
differential entropy
of
X
is defined as

h(X)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}-f(x)\log f(x)dx,

(2)

where
\log
denotes the natural logarithm. The
Kullback-Leibler divergence
(KL-divergence) between two probability densities
f_{1},f_{2}
is given by

\text{KL}(f_{1}||f_{2})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{1}(x)\log\frac{f_{1}(x)}{f_{2}(x)}dx.

(3)

To measure the Gaussianity of
X
, let
D(X)=\text{KL}(f||g)
, where
g
is the Gaussian density with the same mean and variance as
X
. Barron(1986)
[
6
]
showed that

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}D(W_{n})=0.

(4)

Let
f_{n}
denote the density of
W_{n}
, using Pinsker’s inequality and (
4
) we obtain that

\|f_{n}-g\|_{L^{1}}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f_{n}(x)-g(x)|dx\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}0,

(5)

which implies CLT directly. Note that
(
5
)
provides a strong convergence of the density of
W_{n}
in the sense of
L^{1}
norm, while only weak convergence is obtained by classical CLT.

The proof of (
4
) is based on the well-known entropy jump inequality:

h\left(\frac{X+Y}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\geq\frac{h(X)+h(Y)}{2},

(6)

where the equality holds if and only if
X
and
Y
are Gaussian with the same variance. The difference
h\left((X+Y)/\sqrt{2}\right)-(h(X)+h(Y))/2
is called
entropy jump
. Let
W_{n}^{\prime}
be an independent copy of
W_{n}
, taking
X=W_{n}
,
Y=W_{n}^{\prime}
in (
6
) we obtain

h(W_{2n})=h\left(\frac{W_{n}+W_{n}^{\prime}}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\geq\frac{h(W_{n})+h(W_{n}^{\prime})}{2}=h(W_{n}).

(7)

This indicates that
h(W_{n})
will increase to its supremum, and
W_{n}
should converge to a ”stable distribution” that makes the ”=” hold in (
7
). On the other hand, the equality in (
7
) holds if and only if
W_{n}
is Gaussian distributed, and it is also known that Gaussian maximizes the entropy over all random variables with bounded variance. Therefore, it suggests that

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}h(W_{n})=\frac{1}{2}\log 2\pi e,

(8)

which is equivalent to (
4
) by some simple calculation (see
[
9
]
). This briefly illustrates the main picture of entropic method.

Because of the importance of entropy jump inequality (
7
), more researchers focused on intensive study on the amount of entropy jump, which leads to entropic CLT as a direct corollary. To describe it we recall an important parameter for random variables called Fisher information, which has close connection with entropy (as stated in
[
24
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
21
]
, etc.). More precisely,
for a random variable
X
with absolutely continuous density
f
, its Fisher information is defined to be

J(X)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{f^{\prime}(x)^{2}}{f(x)}dx.

(9)

The tail variance
L_{X}(R)
of
X
is defined to be

L_{X}(R)=\mathbb{E}[(X-\mathbb{E}X)^{2}\bm{1}_{\{|X-\mathbb{E}X|\geq R\}}].

(10)

Using tools from Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup, Carlen (1991)
[
9
]
showed that for any
\lambda\in(0,1)
and normalized random variables
X,Y
(
i.e.
,
X,Y
have zero mean and unit variance), if their Fisher information and tail variance are uniformly bounded, then there exists a non-negative
\delta
such that

h\left(\lambda X+\sqrt{1-\lambda^{2}}Y\right)\geq\lambda^{2}h(X)+(1-\lambda^{2})h(Y)+\delta,

(11)

where
\delta
relies only on the uniform bounds of Fisher information and tail variance, as well as the value of
\left(\frac{1}{2}\log 2\pi e-h(X)\right)
and
\left(\frac{1}{2}\log 2\pi e-h(Y)\right)
. In other words, the entropy jump
\delta
relies only on some bounds in regular conditions and the entropy gaps of
X
and
Y
to Gaussian, but not on the particular distributions of
X
and
Y
. In particular,
\delta=0
if and only if
h(X)=h(Y)=\frac{1}{2}\log 2\pi e
, or equivalently,
X,Y
are Gaussian with the same variance. Ball
et al.
(2003)
[
5
]
proved a variational inequality for Fisher information and used it to deduce a lower bound on the entropy jump
\delta
. Johnson
et al.
(2004)
[
23
]
used the theory of projection in
L^{1}
space to obtain an estimate on the decrease of Fisher information, which immediately leads to a lower bound on the entropy jump using de Bruijn identity. Furthermore, the convergence rate of
h(W_{n})
was also estimated in
[
23
]
.

Entropic CLT has drawn more and more attention in the past decade. Bobkov
et al.
(2014)
[
7
]
proved a Berry-Esseen type bound for entropic CLT. Bobkov
et al.
(2014)
[
8
]
showed a more accurate convergence rate on the standard Fisher information by the Chebyshev-Hermite polynomials. More recently, Li
et al.
(2019)
[
25
]
discussed the CLT in the sense of R
\acute{\mbox{e}}
nyi entropy. Johnson (2020)
[
22
]
relaxed the finite Poincar
\acute{\mbox{e}}
constant condition for entropy gap theory. Gavalakis (2021)
[
15
]
extended the entropic method to the CLT for lattice-valued discrete random variables.

1.2
Conditional Central Limit Theorem

As emphasized in
[
36
]
, the random nature of many problems arising in the applied sciences leads to mathematical models where conditioning is
present, thus it is necessary to study the properties of the conditional distribution.

Holst (1979) proved a CCLT for a special conditional distribution in
[
17
]
, which has a simple form that under some regular assumptions for
(\xi_{i},\eta_{i})
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\xi_{i}}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\text{Var}(\xi_{i})}}\leq t|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\eta_{i}=n\mathbb{E}\eta_{1}+o(1)\right)\to\Phi(t),\;\;n\to\infty,

where
\eta_{i}
’s are
i.i.d.
and discrete,
\xi_{i}
’s are also
i.i.d.
with
E\xi_{i}=0,\forall i
,
\Phi
is standard Gaussian distribution function. The result was strengthened in
[
19
]
to
n\mathbb{E}\eta_{1}+O(\sqrt{n}).

In
[
31
]
Rubshtein proved a CCLT for ergodic stationary process. Using martingale method, Rubshtein showed that for a two-dimensional ergodic stationary process
(\bm{\xi},\bm{\eta}):=\{(\xi_{n},\eta_{n}),n\geq 1\}
satisfying some regular conditions, the conditional distribution function

F_{n,\bm{z}}(t)=\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{S_{n}-\mathbb{E}(S_{n}|\bm{\eta}=\bm{z})}{\sqrt{\text{Var}(S_{n}|\bm{\eta}=\bm{z})}}\leq t|\bm{\eta}=\bm{z}\right),t\in\mathbb{R}

(12)

converges weakly to the distribution function of standard Gaussian for realizations
\bm{z}=\bm{\eta}(\omega)=\{\eta_{n}(\omega)\}
almost surely, where
S_{n}=\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}+\cdots+\xi_{n}
.

More recently,
[
36
]
showed a limit theorem for the conditionally independent case.
[
14
]
used Stein method to deduce an upper bound for Wasserstein distance between conditional distribution and Gaussian distribution.

1.3
Hadamard Compression

In 2009, Arikan found Polar code with successive cancelation (SC) decoding in
[
1
]
, which can achieve the Shannon limitation in GF(2) when block length tends to infinity.
Afterwords, Polar method was also applied in lossless source compression
[
2
]
.
In the discrete inputs case with polar transform and SC decoding, the conditional entropy of the
k
th signal
Y_{k}
under the realization of the previous signals
Y_{1}=y_{1},Y_{2}=y_{2},\cdots,Y_{k-1}=y_{k-1}
becomes either 0 or 1, which means that some signals can be uniquely determined and thus can be compressed (see
[
2
]
for details). While when inputs are continuous random variables with densities,
[
35
]
proved that any linear encoding can not achieve lossless compression.

On the other hand, the design of the encoding matrix in continuous input compression has been studied for long time.
The designation of encoding matrix can be divided into two approaches.
One approach is random matrix, such as Gaussian matrix and LDPC, that can facilitate theoretical analysis (
[
30
]
,
[
28
]
and
[
37
]
etc.). Another approach is deterministic matrix, such as Hadamard matrix, unitary geometry method and Reed-Muller based matrix (
[
29
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
34
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
33
]
,
[
18
]
and
[
27
]
etc.). The first approach has rich theoretical results but poor practicality since its encoding is random.
The second approach shows directly some practical results with applications, but to the best of our knowledge, most of the practical results were obtained by simulation experiments and there were lack of theoretical analysis.

To investigate analog compression of continuous random inputs,
[
29
]
employed deterministic
Hadamard matrix transform to replace Polar transform, which is also considered in
[
34
]
and
[
3
]
.

In Chapter 4 of
[
29
]
, the signal
\bm{\eta}=(\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\cdots,\eta_{2^{n}})^{T}
to be compressed by Hadamard transform was supposed to have the form below

\bm{\eta}=H_{n}\bm{\tilde{\xi}}+\bm{G}=H_{n}(\tilde{\xi}_{1},\tilde{\xi}_{2},\cdots,\tilde{\xi}_{2^{n}})^{T}+(G_{1},G_{2},\cdots,G_{2^{n}})^{T}\overset{d}{=}H_{n}(\bm{\tilde{\xi}}+\bm{G}),

(13)

where
\bm{\tilde{\xi}}
is the original signal and
\bm{G}
is an independent Gaussian noise, and both have
i.i.d.
components respectively.
[
29
]
aimed to use
high-entropy position to recover low-entropy position. From numerical examples the author
deduced that a high entropy subset of
\eta_{i}
’s are sufficient to recover all
\bm{\eta}
. This deduction was
verified by further examples of sampling and obtained a satisfactory performance.

Before moving ahead, we introduce some notations. Let
(X,Y)
be a random pair where
X
is a random variable and
Y
may be a multiple-dimensional random vector. If for any realization of
Y=y
, the conditional density of
X
given
Y=y,
denoted by
f_{X|Y}(x|y),
exists and is continuous, then the conditional entropy of
X
given
Y=y
is defined as

h(X|Y=y):=-\int f_{X|Y}(x|y)\log f_{X|Y}(x|y)dx.

(14)

In this paper we use
h(X|Y)
to denote the corresponding random variable, and the traditional conditional entropy, i.e. the expectation of
h(X|Y)
, is denoted
explicitly as

\mathbb{E}h(X|Y)=-\iint f_{X|Y}(x|y)\log f_{X|Y}(x|y)dxdP_{Y}(y).

Similar notations will also be used for conditional variance and Fisher information.

For independent random variables
X_{1},X_{2}
with densities
f_{1},f_{2}
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
, we write
X_{1}*_{\lambda}X_{2}:=\lambda X_{1}+\sqrt{1-\lambda^{2}}X_{2}
, and denote the density of
X_{1}*_{\lambda}X_{2}
by
f_{1}*_{\lambda}f_{2}
.

Below we consider a slightly more general Hadamard transform. Let
\lambda\in(0,1)
,

H_{\lambda}=\begin{bmatrix}\lambda&\sqrt{1-\lambda^{2}}\\
\sqrt{1-\lambda^{2}}&-\lambda\end{bmatrix},

(15)

H_{\lambda,n}=B_{N}(H_{\lambda})^{\otimes n},
where

,
n
is the block length,

is the bit permutation matrix,
\otimes
is the Kronnecker product. We simply denote
H_{1/\sqrt{2}}=H,H_{1/\sqrt{2},n}=H_{n}.

Given a two dimensional vector input
\xi=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})^{T},
through Hadamard transform we will get a two dimensional vector output
\eta=(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})^{T}=H_{\lambda}\xi.
We call
\eta_{1}
the upper perform or
"0"
perform, and call
\eta_{2}
the lower perform or
"1"
perform. For the n-iteration of Hadamard transform

H_{\lambda,n}=B_{N}(H_{\lambda})^{\otimes n},
given an
N

N

Each

In fact,

(16)

where

We denote

(17)

Due to the action of the bit permutation matrix

one can check that for each polarization path
(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\cdots\omega_{n}),~\eta^{n}_{k}(\omega)
is exactly the

\eta^{n}_{k}

\eta^{n}.

We now suppose that the input
\xi^{n}=(\xi^{n}_{1},\xi^{n}_{2},\cdots,\xi^{n}_{N})^{T}
satisfies
\{\xi^{n}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\overset{\textit{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\xi
, where
\xi
has density

. Let

For

\eta_{i:j}^{n}

We denote the density of
\eta_{k}^{n}

f_{k}^{n}

\eta_{k}^{n}
given
\eta_{1:k-1}^{n}=(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{k-1})
as

with
y
stands for
(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{k-1})
.
Note that
H_{\lambda}
is an orthogonal transform. Repeatedly employing the chain rule of entropies and the fact that conditional entropies are invariant under orthogonal transformations. We will find that for all

If we fix

2
Paper Structure and Main Results

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows.

In Section
3
, we provide a Main Theorem (Theorem
3.1
) of this paper. We show that if a sequence of random pairs
\{(X_{n},Y_{n})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
satisfies (
20
), then with some mild additional conditions, the average of the conditional entropy
h_{n}:=\mathbb{E}h(X_{n}|Y_{n})
will converge to the entropy of Gaussian with the variance
\sigma^{2}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\text{Var}(X_{n}|Y_{n})
(see Theorem
3.1
for details). Thus, the conditional entropy measures the asymptotic Gaussianity. This theorem reveals a phenomena that for a sequence of random pairs
\{(X_{n},Y_{n})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
satisfying the entropic property (
20
), one may deduce some results similar to CCLT. We emphasize that the phenomena revealed by this Main Theorem is valid not only on Hadamard transform, but also on any sequence which satisfies the entropic property (
20
).

The proof of the Main Theorem relies heavily on a Key Lemma (Lemma
3.2
), in which we extend Theorem 1.2 in
[
9
]
(see (
11
)) to random variables with distinct variances. We show that for
X,Y
with uniformly lower bounded variance, the entropy jump
\delta
depends only on the difference between their variance and their gaps to Gaussian. Note that without equal variance condition, the proof of Lemma
3.2
is quite different from that of
[
9
]
. We treat the variance, as well as the differential entropy and the KL divergence as functionals in density space and find its continuity with respect to the density. See Proposition
3.11
for details.

Section
4
is devoted to establish the
entropic conditional central limit theorem
, which is an important application of the Main Theorem. Let
(X_{n},Y_{n})
be defined by (
19
), if we specify the path
\omega=(0,0,\cdots,0),
then the conditional distribution of
X_{n}
given
Y_{n}=y,
denoted by
(X_{n}|Y_{n}=y),
will take the form

(X_{n}|Y_{n}=y)=(\frac{\xi_{0}^{1}+\xi_{0}^{2}+\cdots+\xi_{0}^{2^{n}}}{2^{n/2}}|\eta_{0}^{1}=y_{1},\eta_{0}^{2}=y_{2},\cdots,\eta_{0}^{2^{n}}=y_{2^{n}}),

(21)

where
\{(\xi_{0}^{i},\eta_{0}^{i}),i=1,2,\cdots,2^{n}\}
are
i.i.d.
,
(\xi_{0}^{i},\eta_{0}^{i})\overset{d}{=}(\xi_{0},\eta)
,
y=(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{2^{n}})
. Note that
\eta_{0}^{i}
can be considered as a ’side information’ of input signal
\xi_{i}
.
Therefore, we can make use of Theorem
3.1
to establish the CCLT.

To be more precise, suppose that
(\xi,\eta)
is a two-dimensional random variable such that for almost sure realization
\eta=y
, the regular conditional distribution
\xi|\eta=y
exists with finite variance and is absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure. Let
(\xi_{1},\eta_{1}),(\xi_{2},\eta_{2}),\cdots,(\xi_{n},\eta_{n})
be
i.i.d.
samples from
(\xi,\eta)
,
\bm{\eta}_{n}=(\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\cdots,\eta_{n})
be the random vector of the first
n
samples of
\eta
, and
\bm{y}_{n}=(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{n})
be a realization of
\bm{\eta}_{n}
. Let
W_{n}=(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}+\cdots+\xi_{n})/\sqrt{n}
be the normalized partial sum, and
\tilde{g}
be a Gaussian density with the same mean and variance as the conditional distribution
(W_{n}|\bm{\eta}_{n}=\bm{y}_{n})
. Denote the conditional density of
(W_{n}|\bm{\eta}_{n}=\bm{y}_{n})
by

. Let

D(W_{n}|\bm{y}_{n}):=\text{KL}(f_{n}(x|\bm{y}_{n})||\tilde{g})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{n}(x|\bm{y}_{n})\log\frac{f_{n}(x|\bm{y}_{n})}{\tilde{g}(x)}dx.

(22)

Equivalently,
D(W_{n}|\bm{y}_{n})
is also given by

D(W_{n}|\bm{y}_{n})=\text{KL}(\tilde{W}_{n}(\bm{y}_{n})||\mathcal{N}(0,1)|\bm{\eta}_{n}=\bm{y}_{n}),

(23)

where

\tilde{W}_{n}(\bm{y}_{n})=\frac{\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}(\xi_{k}-\mathbb{E}(\xi|\eta_{k}=y_{k}))}{\sqrt{\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\text{Var}(\xi|\eta_{k}=y_{k})}}

(24)

stands for the conditional normalized partial sum. Then, with some mild conditions, in Section
4
we will prove the following result (see Theorem
4.1
and Corollary
4.2
for details).

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}D(W_{n}|\bm{\eta}_{n})=0.

(25)

Note that the entropic CLT (
4
) is covered by (
25
) if we take
\eta
to be independent of
\xi
. Since the Gaussianity of
W_{n}
conditioning on
\bm{\eta}_{n}=\bm{y}_{n}
is measured by (

), hence (
25
) provides also the asymptotic Gaussianity of
(W_{n}|\bm{y}_{n})
which can not be easily deduced by classical CLT. Moreover, our result is in some sense stronger than the corresponding result in
[
31
]

where only the weak convergence of conditional distribution
\tilde{W}_{n}(\bm{y}_{n})
is obtained. However, the convergence in KL-divergence given by (
25
) leads to
L^{1}
-convergence of the density of
(W_{n}|\bm{\eta}_{n})
by Pinsker’s inequality (cf.
(
5
)
).

In Section
4
we prove also a convergence rate for the entropic conditional CLT, which is stimulated by the rate theorem for entropic CLT in
[
7
]
. See Theorem
4.5
for details.

In Section
5
, we study the conditional Fisher information.
Recall that for a random variable X with differentiable density, the Cramer-Rao bound (CR bound)
reads

J(X)\geq\frac{1}{\text{Var}(X)},

(26)

where the equality holds if and only if X is Gaussian (see e.g. Lemma 1.19 in
[
21
]
).
Therefore apart from differential entropy, the Fisher information is another useful quantity to measure Gaussianity.
It is
natural to ask whether the conditional Fisher information can also be used to measure the asymptotic
Gaussianity. To answer this question we need some kind of continuity of Fisher information against
the densities.
To our knowledge the continuity of Fisher information has not been investigated yet. Although the lower semi-continuity under weak topology was shown in
[
8
]
, it is not applicable to
our purpose, since we need the continuity of Fisher information against densities equipped with
L^{1}
-topology. The difficulty lies here is that the definition of Fisher information involves derivatives
and it is notably known that
L^{1}
norm does not coincide with
H^{1}
norm. Thus, we consider only
the densities of random variables with small Gaussian perturbations (cf. (
129
)). We show that for such random variables the continuity of Fisher information holds (see Lemma
5.2
), and then the expectation of the conditional Fisher information
\mathbb{E}J(X_{n}|Y_{n})

converges to
1/\sigma^{2}
where
\sigma^{2}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\text{Var}(X_{n}|Y_{n})
. See Theorem
5.1
for details.

In Section
6
, we investigate Hadamard compression for continuous input. We show that if input signals are the same as in
[
29
]
, then under Hadamard transform and SC decoding frame, almost every distribution of the received signal conditional on the knowledge of the previous signals tends to Gaussian, when the block length tends to infinity, see Theorem
6.2
for details. Moreover, the conditional distribution is insensitive to the values of previous received signals (cf. (
158
)). We studied also the limit relation between variance and Fisher information (Theorem
6.8
). In Theorem
6.9
we make a rough estimate of the limit variance, which has close link to the error analysis in compression.

As we have mentioned in Section
1.3
, from numerical examples the author in
[
29
]

deduced that a high entropy subset of received signal
\eta_{i}
’s are sufficient to recover all received signals. The deduction was
verified by further examples of sampling and obtained a satisfactory performance. However, there was no theoretical deduction supporting this performance. It is not clear why those subset of
\eta_{i}
’s with lower entropy can be compressed.
Note that for continuous signals, low entropy does not necessarily imply small variance, and hence does not necessarily mean small uncertainty (see the example in Appendix
N
). Indeed, in the literature (see e.g.
[
29
]
,
[
34
]
,
[
3
]
and references therein ), there was no theoretic study concerning Hadamard compression.

Based on the research in this section, we can provide a theoretic study concerning Hadamard compression. Firstly, by (
156
) or (
157
), for almost every polarization path
\omega,
when
n
tends to
\infty,
the distribution of received signal
\eta^{n}_{k}(\omega)
conditional on the values of previous received signals converges to a Gaussian distribution with corresponding conditional variance. Secondly, by (
158
) we know that the corresponding conditional variance
\text{Var}(\eta^{n}_{k}(\omega)|\eta^{n}_{1:k(\omega)-1})
converges to a deterministic value
V_{\infty}(\omega).
Third, the conditional distribution
(\eta^{n}_{k}(\omega)|\eta^{n}_{1:k(\omega)-1})
is insensitive to the values of previous received signals in the sense that, for almost every realization
\eta^{n}_{1}=y_{1},\eta^{n}_{2}=y_{2},\cdots,\eta^{n}_{k-1}=y_{k-1},
the conditional distribution of
(\eta^{n}_{k}(\omega)|\eta^{n}_{1}=y_{1},\eta^{n}_{2}=y_{2},\cdots,\eta^{n}_{k-1}=y_{k-1})
converges to the Gaussian distribution with same variance
V_{\infty}(\omega).
This can be seen from the deduction below. For almost every
\omega\in\Omega
, when
n
is sufficiently large, there exists
\sigma^{n}_{k}(\omega)>0
, where
k=k(n,\omega)
, such that for almost every realization
(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{k-1})
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}h(\eta^{n}_{k}(\omega)|\eta^{n}_{1},\eta^{n}_{2},\cdots,\eta^{n}_{k-1})\overset{(a)}{\approx}\frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi e(\sigma^{n}_{k}(\omega))^{2})

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{\approx}\frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi e\text{Var}(\eta^{n}_{k}|\eta^{n}_{1}=y_{1},\eta^{n}_{2}=y_{2},\cdots,\eta^{n}_{k-1}=y_{k-1})).

where
(a)
comes from (
156
), and
(b)
comes from (
158
).

Now, we can conduct a solid theoretical analysis supporting the simulation results of
[
29
]
. When
n
is large enough, for any
k,~1\leq k\leq 2^{n},
the received signal
\eta^{n}_{k}
is roughly Gaussian distributed with variance, say
\sigma^{n}_{k},
and
\sigma^{n}_{k}
is irrelevant to the previous received signals. It is well known that the entropy
h(\eta)
of a Gaussian variable
\eta
is uniquely determined by its variance
\sigma
, that is,
h(\eta)=\frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi e\sigma^{2}).
Consequently, those
\eta^{n}_{k}
’s with lower entropy have less variance, and hence have smaller uncertainty. Therefore one can safely use high entropy subset of
\eta^{n}_{k}
’s to recover all the received signals, as performed in
[
29
]
. Further more, since the conditional distribution is insensitive to the realization of previous signals, when
n
is large enough, the choice of compressed positions can be fixed for whatever input signals. This means that the experimental Hadamard compression performed in
[
29
]
exhibits universal applicability across diverse input signals.

3
Main Theorem and Key Lemma

3.1
Statement of Main Theorem

We first recall some notations and conventions used in this paper. Throughout this paper, ”
\log
” denotes the natural logarithm.
g_{a}
stands for the Gaussian density with mean 0 and variance
a
, and
g
for standard Gaussian density. The letter
G
will always stands for a standard Gaussian random variable. We denote by
\mathcal{T}
the collection of all absolutely continuous probability densities,
i.e.
,

\mathcal{T}=\{f:\ f\text{ is absolutely continuous and }f(x)\geq 0,\ \int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)dx=1\}.

(27)

We write
L^{1}_{f(x)}
for
L^{1}(\mathbb{R},f(x)dx)
, in
particular, we write
L^{1}
for
L^{1}(\mathbb{R},dx)
.

For a random pair
(X,Y)
with
X
being a random variable, we always assume that the conditional density
f_{X|Y}(\cdot|y)
exists and
f_{X|Y}(\cdot|y)\in\mathcal{T}
for almost surely
y
. We write

\text{Var}(X|Y)=V(Y):=\mathbb{E}(X^{2}|Y)-[\mathbb{E}(X|Y)]^{2}

(28)

for the random variable representing the conditional variance under random realization, and we write

\text{Var}(X|y)=V(y)
for the conditional variance under given realization
y
.
In addition, the average of conditional variance is denoted by
\mathbb{E}\text{Var}(X|Y)
. We make the same convention for all functionals operating on conditional distributions. For example,
h(X|Y)
is a random variable standing for the conditional entropy under random realization, and the average of
h(X|Y)
is given by

\mathbb{E}h(X|Y)=\int\int_{\mathbb{R}}-f_{X|Y}(x|y)\log f_{X|Y}(x|y)dxP_{Y}(dy).

(29)

Similarly, The average of conditional Fisher information
J(X|Y)
is denoted by

\mathbb{E}J(X|Y)=\int\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{f_{X|Y}^{\prime}(x|y)^{2}}{f_{X|Y}(x|y)}dxP_{Y}(dy).

(30)

Throughout this paper,
l
stands for a decreasing function
l(R)
such that
l(R)\rightarrow 0
as
R\rightarrow\infty
. For
\lambda\in(0,1)
and independent random variables
X,Y
, their entropy jump is defined by

\triangle_{\lambda}(X,Y):=h\left(\lambda X+\sqrt{1-\lambda^{2}}Y\right)-(\lambda^{2}h(X)+(1-\lambda^{2})h(Y)).

(31)

For a random pair
(X,Y)
, the Gaussianity of
X
conditioning on
Y=y
is measured by

D(X|Y=y):=\text{KL}[(X|Y=y)\|\mathcal{N}(\mu_{y},\sigma^{2}_{y})]=\int f(x|y)\log\frac{f(x|y)}{g(x)}dx,

(32)

where
\mu_{y}=\mathbb{E}(X|Y=y),\sigma^{2}_{y}=\text{Var}(X|Y=y),f(x|y)
is the density of
X
given
Y=y
, and
g
is the density of
\mathcal{N}(\mu_{y},\sigma^{2}_{y})
.

In the Main Theorem stated below, instead of using traditional tools from probability theory such as characteristic function or martingale, we make use of the entropic property (
20
) to establish a result similar to conditional CLT. In fact it is even stronger than traditional CCLT, because by Pinsker’s inequality the convergence in KL divergence sense implies the convergence in total variance sense rather than in weak sense (cf. (
5
))

Theorem 3.1

(Main Theorem)
.

Let
\{(X_{n},Y_{n})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of random pairs such that the conditional distribution
X_{n}|Y_{n}=y_{n}
has absolute continuous density for
y_{n}
almost surely. Assume the following conditions hold,

1.

There exists
a>0
such that
\text{Var}(X_{n}|Y_{n})\geq a,\ a.s.,\ \forall n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
(
here and henceforth,

\mathbb{N}^{+}
stands for positive integers).

2.

There exists
J_{0}<\infty
such that
\sup\limits_{n}\mathbb{E}J(X_{n}|Y_{n})\leq J_{0}
.

3.

There exists function
l
decreasing to 0 such that

\mathbb{E}[L_{X_{n}|Y_{n}}(R)]\leq l(R),\ \forall n\in\mathbb{N}^{+},R\geq 0.

Denote by
(X_{n}^{\prime},Y_{n}^{\prime})
an independent copy of
(X_{n},Y_{n})
. If

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}h(X_{n}|Y_{n})\ \ \text{exists},

(33)

and

|\mathbb{E}h(X_{n+1}|Y_{n+1})-\mathbb{E}h(X_{n}|Y_{n})|=\mathbb{E}\left[h(X_{n}*_{\lambda}X^{\prime}_{n}|Y_{n},Y^{\prime}_{n})-h(X_{n}|Y_{n})\right]

(34)

for some
\lambda\in(0,1)
. Then

D(X_{n}|Y_{n})\xrightarrow{\text{Pr.}}0,\ \ n\rightarrow\infty,

(35)

and

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}|\text{Var}(X_{n}|Y_{n})-\mathbb{E}\text{Var}(X_{n}|Y_{n})|=0.

(36)

Furthermore, If
\{h(X_{n}|Y_{n}),n\geq 1\}
is uniformly integrable, then there exists
\sigma^{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\text{Var}(X_{n}|Y_{n})=\sigma^{2},

(37)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}h(X_{n}|Y_{n})=\frac{1}{2}\log 2\pi e\sigma^{2}.

(38)

Remark 3.1
.

Here are examples for the conditions in Theorem
3.1
. As stated in Section
1.3
, condition (
34
) is held in Hadamard transform. Further, if
\omega=(0,0,\cdots,0)
, the conditions, that the uniformly integrability of
\{h(X_{n}|Y_{n}),n\geq 1\}
and the convergence property (
33
), are held immediately, as proved in Section
4
. While for general
\omega
,
if the input signal of Hadamard compression
\xi
satisfies the form

\xi=\hat{\xi}+G,

(39)

where
G
is Gaussian and independent with
\hat{\xi}
, as assumed in
[
29
]
, then the uniformly integrability of
\{h(X_{n}|Y_{n}),n\geq 1\}
and the convergence property (
33
) are both held for almost any
\omega
(on a specific probability space). See details in Appendix
G.2
.

3.2
Key Lemma and its Proof

As a core step for proving Main Theorem, in this subsection we prepare a Key Lemma (Lemma
3.2
), which is an Extension of Theorem 1.2 of
[
9
]
. The results in this subsection are similar with those in
[
9
]
, the main difference is that we treat the case where the random variables have distinct variances. Note that without equal variance condition, the proof of Lemma
3.2
is quite different from that of
[
9
]
. We treat the variance, as well as the differential entropy and the K-L divergence as functionals in density space and find its continuity with respect to the density (see Proposition
3.11
) .

Lemma 3.2

(Key Lemma)
.

Suppose
X
and
Y
are independent random variables with absolutely continuous densities. Assume the following conditions satisfy,

1.

\min\{J(X),J(Y)\}\leq J_{0}
for some
J_{0}>0
.

2.

\max\{\text{Var}(X),\text{Var}(Y)\}\geq a
for some
a>0
.

3.

\min\{L_{X}(R),L_{Y}(R)\}\leq l(R)
for some non-negative decreasing function
l(R)
such that
l(R)\rightarrow 0
as
R\rightarrow\infty
.

If

\max\{D(X),D(Y)\}+|\text{Var}(X)-\text{Var}(Y)|\geq\epsilon>0,

(40)

then for any
\lambda\in(0,1)
, there exists a positive number
\delta=\delta(\lambda,J_{0},a,l,\epsilon)
which does not rely on the distributions of
X
and
Y,
such that

h\left(\lambda X+\sqrt{1-\lambda^{2}}Y\right)\geq\lambda^{2}h(X)+(1-\lambda^{2})h(Y)+\delta.

(41)

Before the proof of Lemma
3.2
,
we first list some useful results that will be exploited in our proof.

The next lemma shows that Fisher information decreases after convolution. We refer to
[
9
, Lemma 3.1]
for its proof.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}
be independent random variables with densities
f_{1},\cdots,f_{n}\in\mathcal{T}
. Suppose
0<\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n}<1
and
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}^{2}=1
, then

J\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}X_{i}\right)\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}^{2}J(X_{i}),

(42)

and the equality holds if and only if
f_{1},f_{2},\cdots,f_{n}
are Gaussian densities with the same variance.

The de Bruijn’s identity has been widely quoted in the literature of information theory (
e.g.
see
[
13
, Theorem 17.7.2]
), which connects Fisher information and differential entropy. We write de Bruijn’s identity as the following lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
X
be a continuous random variable with finite variance,
G
be an independent standard Gaussian random variable. Then

\frac{d}{dt}h(X+\sqrt{t}G)=\frac{1}{2}J(X+\sqrt{t}G).

(43)

The variational formula for KL-divergence, which has also been extensively explored (
e.g.
see
[
32
, Corollary 4.15]
), will be used several times in our proof. We state it as the next lemma.

Lemma 3.5
.

For any probability densities
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
,

\text{KL}(f_{1}||f_{2})=\sup\limits_{\phi}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{1}(x)\phi(x)dx-\log\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{\phi(x)}f_{2}(x)dx\right),

(44)

where the supremum runs over all bounded measurable functions
\phi
.

Recall that for
\lambda\in(0,1)
and independent random variables
X_{1},X_{2}
with densities

, we write
X_{1}*_{\lambda}X_{2}=\lambda X_{1}+\sqrt{1-\lambda^{2}}X_{2}
, and denote the density of

by

. Recall that

stands for the Gaussian density with mean 0 and variance

Barrow from the idea of
[

]
, we will frequently use the tools of Ornstein-Unlenbeck Semigroup, which is defined as follows.

Definition 3.6
.

Set
t\geq 0
, define the map

(p_{t}^{*}f)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(y)g_{(1-e^{-2t})}(x-e^{-t}y)dy.

\{p_{t}^{*}:t\geq 0\}
is the Lebesgue adjoint to Ornstein-Unlenbeck semigroup. It is well known that

is strongly continuous on

. Let

be a random variable with density

to be the random variable with density

p^{*}_{t}X\overset{d}{=}e^{-t}X+\sqrt{1-e^{-2t}}G=X*_{e^{-t}}G

(46)

and

(p^{*}_{t}f)(x)=\mathbb{E}[g_{(1-e^{-2t})}(x-e^{-t}X)]=f*_{e^{-t}}g,

(47)

where
\overset{d}{=}
means equal in distribution. From (

and

,
i.e.
, for any

(p^{*}_{t}f_{1})*_{\lambda}(p^{*}_{t}f_{2})=p_{t}^{*}(f_{1}*_{\lambda}f_{2}).

(48)

Finally, we note here that for a random variable
X
with density
f
, we usually indistinguishably write

and

when handling with some functionals operating on the distribution of

. For example, we write
\text{Var}(f)=\text{Var}(X),\ h(f)=h(X),L_{f}(R)=L_{X}(R),\triangle_{\lambda}(f_{X},f_{Y})=\triangle_{\lambda}(X,Y)
and so on.

We now start to prove our Key Lemma. We begin with defining some functional spaces. Recall that in this paper
l
always stands for a decreasing function

such that

Lemma 3.10

Proof.

For any

Next proposition shows the continuity of differential entropy, variance and KL-divergence from Gaussian.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let

Lemma 3.12
.

For any
f\in\mathcal{T}
, the function
t\mapsto h(p_{t}^{*}f)
is continuous on
[0,\infty)
, differentiable on
(0,\infty)
, and

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}h(p_{t}^{*}f)=h(g)=\frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi e)\ \ ,\ \ \frac{d}{dt}h(p_{t}^{*}f)=J(p_{t}^{*}f)-1.\ \

Proof.

Notice that
\{p_{t}^{*},t\geq 0\}
is a strongly continuous semigroup, thus it is enough to show the continuity at
t=0
. Let
X
be the random variable with density
f
. On the one hand,

h(p_{t}^{*}f)=h(e^{-t}X+\sqrt{1-e^{-2t}}G)=-t+h(X+\sqrt{e^{2t}-1}G)\geq-t+h(X),

(58)

which implies
\liminf\limits_{t\rightarrow 0}h(p_{t}^{*}f)\geq h(X)=h(f).

On the other hand, for any
\epsilon>0
, Lemma
3.5
implies that there exists a bounded measurable function
\phi(x)
such that

\frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi e\text{Var}(f))-h(f)=D(f)\leq\epsilon+\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\phi(x)dx-\log\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{\phi(x)}g_{\text{Var}(f)}(x)dx.

(59)

Let
V_{t}=e^{-2t}\text{Var}(f)+1-e^{-2t}
, use Lemma
3.5
again and we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi eV_{t})-h(p_{t}^{*}f)\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(x)(p_{t}^{*}f)(x)dx-\log\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{\phi(x)}g_{V_{t}}(x)dx

(60)

\displaystyle=\int_{R}\phi(x)[(p_{t}^{*}f)(x)-f(x)]dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\phi(x)dx-\log\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{\phi(x)}g_{V_{0}}(x)dx

\displaystyle+\log\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{\phi(x)}g_{V_{0}}(x)dx-\log\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{\phi(x)}g_{V_{t}}(x)dx

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi eV_{0})-h(f)-\epsilon-\|\phi\|_{\infty}\|p_{t}^{*}f-f\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle+\log\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{\phi(x)}g_{V_{0}}(x)dx-\log\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{\phi(x)}g_{V_{t}}(x)dx.

Since
\sqrt{V_{t}}G
converges weakly to
\sqrt{V_{0}}G
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{\phi(x)}g_{V_{t}}(x)dx=\mathbb{E}e^{\phi(\sqrt{V_{t}}G)}\xrightarrow{t\rightarrow 0}\mathbb{E}e^{\phi(\sqrt{V_{0}}G)}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{\phi(x)}g_{V_{0}}(x)dx.

(61)

As a result, we have
\limsup\limits_{t\rightarrow 0}h(p_{t}^{*}f)\leq h(f)+\epsilon
. This completes the proof of the continuity at

.

For the second part of Lemma
3.12
, since

\displaystyle h(p_{t}^{*}f)=h(e^{-t}+\sqrt{1-e^{-2t}}G)\geq h(\sqrt{1-e^{-2t}}G)=h(g)+\frac{1}{2}\log(1-e^{-2t}),

(62)

\displaystyle h(p_{t}^{*}f)\leq\frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi e\text{Var}(p_{t}^{*}f))=\frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi e(e^{-2t}\text{Var}(f)+1-e^{-2t})),

we have
\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}h(p_{t}^{*}f)=h(g).

Finally, according to Lemma
3.4
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}h(p_{t}^{*}f)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}(-t+h(X+\sqrt{e^{2t}-1}G))=-1+\frac{1}{2}J(X+\sqrt{e^{2t}-1}G)2e^{2t}

(63)

\displaystyle=-1+J(e^{-t}X+\sqrt{1-e^{-2t}}G)=J(p_{t}^{*}f)-1,

which completes the whole proof.∎

From Lemma
3.12
we obtain the following useful corollary.

Corollary 3.13
.

Suppose

4
Entropic Conditional Central Limit Theorem

This section we apply Main Theorem to derive the
entropic conditional central limit theorem
(entropic CCLT). Suppose
(\xi,\eta)
is a two-dimensional random variable such that for almost sure realization
\eta=y
, the regular conditional distribution
\xi|\eta=y
exists with finite variance and is absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure. Let
(\xi_{1},\eta_{1}),(\xi_{2},\eta_{2}),\cdots,(\xi_{n},\eta_{n})
be
i.i.d.
samples from
(\xi,\eta)
,
\bm{\eta}_{n}=(\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\cdots,\eta_{n})
be the random vector of the first
n
samples of
\eta
, and
\bm{y}_{n}=(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{n})
be a realization of
\bm{\eta}_{n}
. Let
W_{n}=(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}+\cdots+\xi_{n})/\sqrt{n}
be the normalized partial sum, and
\tilde{g}
be a Gaussian density with the same mean and variance as the conditional distribution
(W_{n}|\bm{\eta}_{n}=\bm{y}_{n})
. Denote the conditional density of
(W_{n}|\bm{\eta}_{n}=\bm{y}_{n})
by
f_{n}(x|\bm{y}_{n})
. The Gaussianity of
W_{n}
conditioning on
\bm{\eta}_{n}=\bm{y}_{n}
is measured by

D(W_{n}|\bm{y}_{n}):=\text{KL}(f_{n}(x|\bm{y}_{n})||\tilde{g})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{n}(x|\bm{y}_{n})\log\frac{f_{n}(x|\bm{y}_{n})}{\tilde{g}(x)}dx.

(93)

Equivalently,
D(W_{n}|\bm{y}_{n})
is also given by

D(W_{n}|\bm{y}_{n})=\text{KL}(\tilde{W}_{n}(\bm{y}_{n})||\mathcal{N}(0,1)|\bm{\eta}_{n}=\bm{y}_{n}),

(94)

where

\tilde{W}_{n}(\bm{y}_{n})=\frac{\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}(\xi_{k}-\mathbb{E}(\xi|\eta_{k}=y_{k}))}{\sqrt{\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\text{Var}(\xi|\eta_{k}=y_{k})}}

(95)

stands for the conditional normalized partial sum. Notice that
D(W_{n}|\bm{y}_{n})
is a measurable function of
\bm{y}_{n}
and hence
D(W_{n}|\bm{\eta}_{n})
is a random variable representing the divergence under a random realization. In this paper, it is shown that
D(W_{n}|\bm{\eta}_{n})
converges to 0 in expectation under some regular conditions. The specific result is stated in Theorem
4.1
.

Theorem 4.1

(Entropic CCLT)
.

Let
(\xi,\eta)
be a two-dimensional random variable. Suppose
(\xi_{1},\eta_{1}),(\xi_{2},\eta_{2}),\cdots,(\xi_{n},\eta_{n})
are independent and identically distributed as
(\xi,\eta)
. Let
W_{n}=(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}+\cdots+\xi_{n})/\sqrt{n}
, and
\bm{\eta}_{n}=(\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\cdots,\eta_{n})
. Assume the following conditions hold.

1.

\text{Var}(\xi)<\infty,(\xi|\eta)\prec\mathcal{L},\mathbb{P}_{\eta}-a.s.
, where
\mathcal{L}
denotes Lebesgue measure and
\mu\prec\mathcal{L}
means
\mu
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mathcal{L}
.

2.

\mathbb{E}J(\xi|\eta)<\infty,\ \mathbb{E}h(\xi|\eta)>-\infty
(see Section
2
for the definition).

3.

(Uniform lower bound on conditional variance) There exists
a>0
such that
\text{Var}(\xi|\eta)\geq a,\ \mathbb{P}_{\eta}-a.s.

Denote
h_{n}=\mathbb{E}h(W_{n}|\bm{\eta}_{n})
and
\sigma^{2}=\mathbb{E}\text{Var}(\xi|\eta)
, then we have

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}h_{n}=\frac{1}{2}\log\left(2\pi e\sigma^{2}\right).

(96)

We make some remarks on the assumptions in Theorem
4.1
. The assumption 1 and 2 are fundamental regular conditions ensuring that the conditional distribution and related quantities are well-defined. We require uniform lower bound on conditional variance in assumption 3 to exclude some singular points of the conditional distribution. For example, let
\eta\sim\text{Uniform}(-1,1)
and
\xi|\eta\sim\text{Uniform}(0,|\eta|)
, then
\text{Var}(\xi|y)=y^{2}/12
vanishes and
h(\xi|y)=\log|y|
tends to
-\infty
as
y\rightarrow 0
. In this case
y=0
is a singular point, which makes the behaviour of
D(W_{n}|\bm{y}_{n})
hard to analyze for some extreme realizations
\bm{y}_{n}
.

The following corollary can be deduced directly.

Corollary 4.2
.

Under the same conditions in Theorem
4.1
,

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}D(W_{n}|\bm{\eta}_{n})=0.

(97)

Proof.

Note that from Jensen’s inequality we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}D(W_{n}|\bm{\eta}_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\log\left(2\pi e\text{Var}(W_{n}|\bm{\eta}_{n})\right)-h_{n}

(98)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\log\left(2\pi e\mathbb{E}\left[\text{Var}(W_{n}|\bm{\eta}_{n})\right]\right)-h_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\log\left(2\pi e\sigma^{2}\right)-h_{n}.

Use Theorem
4.1
and then completes the proof.
∎

This indicates that the KL-divergence between the normalized conditional distribution

and standard Gaussian converges to 0 in probability, as

),

.

Clearly, (

) and (
34
) follow from (

) and (

). All of the discussion above show that
(U_{n},T_{n})
satisfies all conditions of Theorem
3.1
. Consequently, from (

D(U_{n}|T_{n})\xrightarrow{\text{Pr.}}0,\ n\rightarrow\infty.

(108)

Let
N=2^{n}
,
h_{N}(\bm{\eta})=h(W_{N}|\bm{\eta}_{N}),V_{N}(\bm{\eta})=\text{Var}(W_{N}|\bm{\eta}_{N})
, then (
108
) is equivalent to

\frac{1}{2}\log 2\pi eV_{N}(\bm{\eta})-h_{N}(\bm{\eta})\xrightarrow{\text{Pr.}}0.

Using the law of large numbers again we have

\mathbb{E}\left[\liminf\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}(h_{N}(\bm{\eta})-H_{N})\right]\leq\liminf\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[h_{N}(\bm{\eta})-H_{N}\right]=\liminf\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}h_{N}-\mathbb{E}h(\xi|\eta).

Without loss of generality, we can assume

(114)

which shows that (

since

is superadditive.

Rate of Convergence with Independent Component

We can establish the rate of convergence for the limit theorem. Indeed, we note that there is a further form for (

(\xi_{n}|\eta_{n}=y)=(\frac{\xi_{0}^{1}+\xi_{0}^{2}+\cdots+\xi_{0}^{2^{n}}}{2^{n/2}}|\eta_{0}^{1}=y_{1},\eta_{0}^{2}=y_{2},\cdots,\eta_{0}^{2^{n}}=y_{2^{n}})\overset{d}{=}\frac{\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}+\cdots+\eta_{2^{n}}}{2^{n/2}},

(115)

where
\{\eta_{i},i=1,2,\cdots,2^{n}\}
are independent with distribution

\eta_{i}\overset{d}{=}(\xi_{0}|\eta_{0}=y_{i}).

Thus, Theorem
3.1
is closely related with CLT for classical independent sum.
[

, Theorem 1.2, (12.6)]
gave the speed of convergence in KL-divergence sense for independent sum.

Lemma 4.3
.

For independent sum
T_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}
,
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\text{Var}(\xi_{i}):=B_{n}
, if
\sup_{i}D(\xi_{i})\leq D<\infty
, then

D(T_{n})\leq ce^{62D}L_{4},

where

is a constant,

D(\xi):=\text{KL}(\xi\|\mathcal{N}(\mathbb{E}\xi,\text{Var}(\xi))),

L_{s}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}|\xi_{i}-\mathbb{E}\xi_{i}|^{s}/B_{n}^{s/2}.

Since Lemma
4.3
needs an upper bound for
D(\xi_{i})
, we shall use another lemma
[
12
, (10)]
to go through it.

Lemma 4.4
.

Given
\xi
,
\mathbb{E}\xi=0,\text{Var}(\xi)<\infty
, then

D(\xi)\leq\frac{1}{2}\log(\text{Var}(\xi)J(\xi)).

(116)

Thanks to Lemma
4.3
and Lemma
4.4
, we can deduce the rate theorem as follows.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
(\xi,\eta)
be a two-dimensional random variable, and suppose that
(\xi_{1},\eta_{1}),(\xi_{2},\eta_{2}),\cdots,(\xi_{n},\eta_{n})
are independent and identically distributed as
(\xi,\eta)
. Let
W_{n}=(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}+\cdots+\xi_{n})/\sqrt{n}
, and
\bm{\eta}_{n}=(\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\cdots,\eta_{n})
. Assume the following conditions hold.

1.

(\xi|\eta)\prec\mathcal{L},\ \mathbb{P}_{\eta}-a.s.
, where
\mathcal{L}
denotes Lebesgue measure and
\mu\prec\mathcal{L}
means
\mu
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mathcal{L}
. Further, for
\mathbb{P}_{\eta}-a.s.
, the conditional random variable
(\xi|\eta)
has absolutely continuous density.

2.

(Finite
s
th moment for variance and Fisher information) There exists
s>64
s.t.

\mathbb{E}(\text{Var}(\xi|y))^{s}<\infty,\;\;\mathbb{E}(J(\xi|y))^{s}<\infty.

(117)

Then
\forall\varepsilon>0
,

\mathbb{E}D(W_{n}|\bm{\eta}_{n})=O\left(\frac{1}{n^{\frac{s-64}{s+64}-\varepsilon}}\right).

(118)

Proof.

For any realization
\bm{\eta}_{n}=\bm{y}_{n}=(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{n})
, we can write the conditional distribution as

(W_{n}|\bm{\eta}_{n}=\bm{y}_{n})\overset{d}{=}\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}+\cdots+\eta_{n},

(119)

where
\eta_{i}
s are independent and

\eta_{i}\overset{d}{=}\frac{(\xi|\eta=y_{i})}{\sqrt{n}}.

First we control the upper bound for
D(\eta_{i})
in order to satisfy the uniformly upper bound condition for
D
in Lemma
4.3
. In fact by Lemma
4.4

\displaystyle D(\eta_{i})=\frac{1}{2}\log(\text{Var}(\xi|\eta=y_{i})J(\xi|\eta=y_{i})),\;\forall i.

(120)

Next, define

A(M):=\{y:\text{Var}(\xi|\eta=y)\leq M,J(\xi|\eta=y)\leq M\},

then by Lemma
4.3
,
\forall\bm{y}_{n}=(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n})\in(A(M))^{n}
,

\displaystyle D(W_{n}|\bm{\eta}_{n}=\bm{y}_{n})

\displaystyle\leq cM^{62}\dfrac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\eta_{i}-\mathbb{E}\eta_{i}|^{4}}{(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\text{Var}(\eta_{i}))^{2}}

(121)

\displaystyle=cM^{62}\dfrac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\eta_{i}|^{4}+(\mathbb{E}\eta_{i})^{4}}{(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\text{Var}(\eta_{i}))^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq cM^{64}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\eta_{i}|^{4}+(\mathbb{E}\eta_{i})^{4},

where the last inequality holds since
\text{Var}(\xi)J(\xi)\geq 1
. On the other hand,
use condition 4 and Chebyshev inequality, we obtain

\mathbb{P}(A(M))\geq 1-\dfrac{\mathbb{E}(\text{Var}(\xi|y))^{s}+\mathbb{E}(J(\xi|y))^{s}}{M^{s}}.

(122)

In addition, for any
\bm{y}_{n}
, we use Lemma
4.4
and Lemma 1.3 in
[
9
]
to obtain
\forall t>0
,

\displaystyle D(W_{n}|\bm{\eta}_{n})=\bm{y}_{n})

\displaystyle C(t)(\text{Var}^{t}(W_{n}|\bm{\eta}_{n})+J^{t}(W_{n}|\bm{\eta}_{n}))

(123)

\displaystyle\leq C(t)(\text{Var}^{t}(W_{n}|\bm{\eta}_{n})+\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}J(\xi_{i}|y_{i})\right)^{t}).

Combine the above results, we have for all
t\in(0,1),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}D(W_{n}|\bm{\eta}_{n})=\mathbb{E}D(W_{n}|\bm{\eta}_{n}=\bm{y}_{n})

\displaystyle 1_{\bm{y}_{n}\in(A(M))^{n}}+\mathbb{E}D(W_{n}|\bm{\eta}_{n}=\bm{y}_{n})1_{\bm{y}_{n}\notin(A(M))^{n}}

(124)

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\leq}\mathbb{E}cM^{64}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\eta_{i}(y_{i})|^{4}+(\mathbb{E}\eta_{i})^{4})

\displaystyle 1_{\bm{y}_{n}\in(A(M))^{n}}+\mathbb{E}C(t)(\text{Var}^{t}(W_{n}|\bm{\eta}_{n})

(125)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}J(\xi_{i}|y_{i})\right)^{t})1_{\bm{y}_{n}\notin A(M)}

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{\leq}\mathbb{E}cM^{64}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\eta_{i}(y_{i})|^{4}+(\mathbb{E}\eta_{i})^{4})

\displaystyle+C(t)\left(\text{Var}(\xi)+\mathbb{E}J(\xi|\eta)\right)^{t}\mathbb{P}^{1-t}\left(\bm{y}_{n}\notin A(M)\right)

(126)

\displaystyle\overset{(c)}{\leq}cM^{64}\dfrac{\mathbb{E}|\xi|^{4}}{n}+C

\displaystyle\left(1-(1-\dfrac{\mathbb{E}(\text{Var}(\xi|y))^{s}+\mathbb{E}(J(\xi|y))^{s}}{M^{s}})^{n}\right)^{1-t}

(127)

\displaystyle\overset{(d)}{\leq}\dfrac{C_{1}M^{64}}{n}+

\displaystyle C_{2}\left(\frac{n}{M^{s}}\right)^{1-t}.

(128)

where
(a)
holds due to (
121
) and (

(b)
comes from Cauchy inequality;
(c)
is deduced by the definition of
\eta_{i}

122

(d)
is from Taylor expansion of

around

∎

Lemma 6.4

Let

J(\xi*_{\lambda}\eta)\leq\lambda^{2}J(\xi)+(1-\lambda^{2})J(\eta),

\displaystyle J(\xi*_{\lambda}(-\eta)|\xi*_{\lambda}\eta)

\displaystyle=4\int\int\left(\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x}f(x,y)\right)^{2}dxdy=\lambda^{2}J(\xi)+(1-\lambda^{2})J(\eta).

For the conditional variance, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
\xi,\eta,Z
be three random variables with densities, then

\text{Var}(\xi|\eta)\geq\text{Var}(\xi|\eta,Z),

Proof.

It is sufficient to prove

In fact,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{(\eta,Z)}[\mathbb{E}^{2}(\xi|\eta,Z)]

\displaystyle=\int_{\eta}\int_{Z}\mathbb{E}^{2}(\xi|\eta=y,Z=z)p_{(\eta,Z)}(y,z)dzdy

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\eta}[\int_{Z}\mathbb{E}(\xi|\eta=y,Z=z)p_{Z|\eta}(z|y)dz]^{2}p_{\eta}(y)dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\eta}[\mathbb{E}(\xi|\eta=y)]^{2}p_{\eta}(y)dy

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\eta}[\mathbb{E}^{2}(\xi|\eta)].

where

6.3
Theoretical study of Hadamard compression

In Chapter 4 of
[
29
]
, the author studied Hadamard compression for continuous input
\xi
with form (
155
).

Let
\bm{\eta}:=(\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\cdots,\eta_{2^{n}})^{T}=H_{n}\bm{\xi}:=H_{n}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\cdots,\xi_{2^{n}})^{T}
be the received signals of
n
-iteration Hadamard transform. From numerical examples the author in
[
29
]

deduced that a high entropy subset of
\eta_{i}
’s are sufficient to recover all
\bm{\eta}
. This deduction was
verified by further examples of sampling and obtained a satisfactory performance. However, there was no theoretical deduction supporting this performance. For example, a natural question is why those subset of
\eta_{i}
’s with lower entropy can be compressed.
Note that for continuous signals, low entropy does not necessarily imply small variance, and hence does not necessarily mean small uncertainty (see the example in Appendix
N
). Indeed, in the literature (see e.g.
[
29
]
,
[
34
]
,
[
3
]
and references therein), there was no theoretic study concerning Hadamard compression.

Based on the research in this article, we can now conduct some theoretical analysis on Hadamard compression. Our analysis will consist of three recursive statements. The statements below are valid for almost every polarization path
\omega\in\Omega
.

Statement 1.

When
n
tends to
\infty,
the distribution of received signal
\eta^{n}_{k}(\omega)
conditional on the values of previous received signals converges to a Gaussian distribution with corresponding conditional variance. This statement follows from (
156
) or (
157
).

Statement 2.
The corresponding conditional variance
\text{Var}(\eta^{n}_{k}(\omega)|\eta^{n}_{1:k(\omega)-1})
converges to a deterministic value
V_{\infty}(\omega).
This statement follows directly from (
158
).

Statement 3.
The conditional distribution
(\eta^{n}_{k}(\omega)|\eta^{n}_{1:k(\omega)-1})
is insensitive to the values of previous received signals in the sense that, for almost every realization
\eta^{n}_{1}=y_{1},\eta^{n}_{2}=y_{2},\cdots,\eta^{n}_{k-1}=y_{k-1},
the conditional distribution of
(\eta^{n}_{k}(\omega)|\eta^{n}_{1}=y_{1},\eta^{n}_{2}=y_{2},\cdots,\eta^{n}_{k-1}=y_{k-1})
converges to the Gaussian distribution with the same variance
V_{\infty}(\omega).
This statement can be seen from the deduction below. For almost every
\omega\in\Omega
, when
n
is sufficiently large, there exists
\sigma^{n}_{k}(\omega)>0
, where
k=k(n,\omega)
, such that for almost any realization
(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{k-1})
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}h(\eta^{n}_{k}(\omega)|\eta^{n}_{1},\eta^{n}_{2},\cdots,\eta^{n}_{k-1})\overset{(a)}{\approx}\frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi e(\sigma^{n}_{k}(\omega))^{2})

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{\approx}\frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi e\text{Var}(\eta^{n}_{k}|\eta^{n}_{1}=y_{1},\eta^{n}_{2}=y_{2},\cdots,\eta^{n}_{k-1}=y_{k-1})).

where
(a)
comes from (
156
), and
(b)
comes from (
158
).

With the above statements, we can now conduct some theoretical analysis about the simulation results of
[
29
]
. By Statement 1, when
n
is large enough, we may roughly regard the received signals conditional on the knowledge of previous signals are Gaussian distributed variables. It is notably known that the entropy
h(\eta)
of a Gaussian variable
\eta
is uniquely determined by its variance
\sigma
, that is,
h(\eta)=\frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi e\sigma^{2}).
Therefore, lower conditional entropy implies smaller conditional variance. Statement 2 says that, when
n
is large enough, the conditional variance is nearly deterministic, and hence nearly does not depend on the condition of previous signals. Further, by Statement 3, the conditional distribution
\eta^{n}_{k}(\omega)
will always converge to the Gaussian distribution with variance
V_{\infty}(\omega),
and will not be influenced by the specific values of previous signals.

We now provide a solid theoretical analysis explaining the simulation results of
[
29
]
. When
n
is large enough, for any
k,~1\leq k\leq 2^{n},
the received signal
\eta^{n}_{k}
is roughly Gaussian distributed with variance, say
\sigma^{n}_{k},
and
\sigma^{n}_{k}
is irrelevant to the previous received signals. It implies that, those
\eta^{n}_{k}
’s with lower entropy have less variance, and hence have smaller uncertainty. Consequently we can safely use high entropy subset of
\eta^{n}_{k}
’s to recover all the received signals. Further more, since the conditional distribution is insensitive to the realization of previous signals, when
n
is large enough, the choice of compressed positions can be fixed for whatever input signals. This means that the experimental Hadamard compression performed in
[
29
]
exhibits universal applicability across diverse input signals.

Uniformly integrability of

Lemma L.1
.

Suppose that
\xi_{n}
are i.i.d. samples from
\xi
and
\mathbb{E}\xi=0,\mathbb{E}\xi^{2}=\sigma^{2}<\infty
, then the sequence
\{W^{2}_{n},n\geq 1\}
is uniformly integrable, where

W_{n}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}.

By CLT and Skorohod representation theorem, there exists a Gaussian random variable
G_{\sigma}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})
such that
W_{n}\overset{a.s.}{\rightarrow}G_{\sigma}
, and thus

W^{2}_{n}\overset{Pr.}{\longrightarrow}G^{2}_{\sigma}.

(205)

Since we also have that

\mathbb{E}W_{n}^{2}=\sigma^{2}=\mathbb{E}G_{\sigma}^{2},\;\;\forall n\geq 1,

we obtain the uniformly integrability of
\{W^{2}_{n},n\geq 1\}
.
∎

2.

\displaystyle L_{Y}(R)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[Y^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{|Y|\geq R\}}]\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{P}(|Y|\geq R)\mathbb{E}Y^{4}}\leq\sqrt{(\mathbb{E}X^{4}+3(\mathbb{E}X^{2})^{2})\frac{\mathbb{E}Y^{2}}{R^{2}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{(\mathbb{E}X^{4}+3(\mathbb{E}X^{2})^{2})(\mathbb{E}X^{2})}}{R}.

Thus
L_{Y}(R)\leq\min\{L_{Y}(0),\frac{\sqrt{(\mathbb{E}X^{4}+3(\mathbb{E}X^{2})^{2})(\mathbb{E}X^{2})}}{R}\}=\min\{\mathbb{E}X^{2},\frac{\sqrt{(\mathbb{E}X^{4}+3(\mathbb{E}X^{2})^{2})(\mathbb{E}X^{2})}}{R}\}
.
∎

An Example for Theorem
6.9

Given a distribution
A
, let
h_{0}:=h(A)
and
J_{0}:=J(A)
. We assume that the density function
p
of
A
is continuous and differentiable, and there exists
x_{1}<x_{0}<x_{2}

s.t.

p(x_{1})>0,\;\;p(x_{0})=0,\;\;p^{\prime}(x_{0})=0,\;\;p(x_{2})>0.

We define
A_{i}:=\frac{1}{i}A
, then it follows that
\text{Var}(A_{i})\rightarrow 0
as
i\to\infty
. We further define a random variable
B(i,j)
using the function

q_{i,j}(x):=\begin{cases}p_{i}(x+j),&x\leq x_{0}-j;\\
0,&x_{0}-j<x<x_{0};\\
p_{i}(x),&x\geq x_{0},\end{cases}

as its density, where
p_{i}
is the density function of
A_{i}
. It is not difficult to observe that for any
i
,

\text{Var}(B(i,j))\rightarrow\infty\;\text{as}\;j\to\infty

and

h(B(i,j))=h(A_{i})=h_{0}-\log(i);\;J(B(i,j))=J(A_{i})=i^{2}J_{0}.

Now, for any
\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
, we can find large
i
such that

\dfrac{h(B(i,j))-\frac{1}{2}\log[2\pi e(1/J(B(i,j)))]}{\frac{1}{2}\log[2\pi e\varepsilon]-\frac{1}{2}\log[2\pi e(1/J(B(i,j)))]}=\dfrac{h_{0}-\frac{1}{2}\log[2\pi e(1/J_{0})]}{\frac{1}{2}\log(\varepsilon i^{2}J_{0})}\leq\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{\prime}.

(206)

Note that (
206
) holds uniformly about
j
.
Finally, we note that for
B(\tau):=B(i,j)+a_{\tau},\;\;a_{\tau}\sim N(0,\tau),
the entropy, the Fisher information and the variance are all continuous (differentiable) with respect to
\tau
, therefore, we can find a sufficiently small
\tau
such that when using
B=B(\tau)
as input, the Hadamard transformation effectively reduces the large variance to be almost smaller than
\varepsilon
,
i.e.

\mathbb{P}_{\Omega}(V_{\infty}(\omega)\leq\varepsilon)\geq 1-\varepsilon^{\prime}.

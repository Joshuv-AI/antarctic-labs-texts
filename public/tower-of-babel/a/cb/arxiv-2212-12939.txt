Foldy–Wouthuysen Transformation in Strong Magnetic Fields and Relativistic Corrections for Quantum Cyclotron Energy Levels
Albert Wienczek, Christopher Moore, Ulrich D. Jentschura

Abstract
We carry out a direct, iterative Foldy–Wouthuysen transformation of a general Dirac Hamiltonian coupled to an electromagnetic field, including the anomalous magnetic moment. The transformation is carried out through an iterative disentangling of the particle and antiparticle Hamiltonians, in the expansion for higher orders of the momenta. The time-derivative term from the unitary transformation is found to be crucial in supplementing the transverse component of the electric field in higher orders. Final expressions are obtained for general combined electric and magnetic fields, including strong magnetic fields. The time-derivative of the electric field is shown to enter only in the seventh order of the fine-structure constant if the transformation is carried out in the standard fashion. We put special emphasis on the case of strong fields, which are important for a number of applications, such as electrons bound in Penning traps.



I
Introduction

The purpose of this paper is threefold:
First, to discuss the role of nonstandard
and standard Foldy–Wouthuysen transformations,
up to seventh order in the momenta,
second, to clarify the role
of higher-order terms in the magnetic fields
which become relevant for particles bound
in strong magnetic fields (Penning traps),
and third, to apply the results to
the calculation of quantum cyclotron energy
levels.

Let us start with the first purpose, which requires
some background discussion.
The Foldy–Wouthuysen transformation
FoWu1950

is a cornerstone of the description of electronic bound states in
simple atomic systems.
The purpose of the transformation is to start from
a (generalized) Dirac Hamiltonian,
and to disentangle the particle and antiparticle
degrees of freedom.
We recall that the Dirac Hamiltonian describes
particles and antiparticles simultaneously,
and the Foldy–Wouthuysen transformation
is used to find separate effective Hamiltonians for the
particle (positive-energy) and antiparticle (negative-energy) states.
In simple cases, such as a free electron,
one can disentangle the particle and antiparticle
Hamiltonians to all orders in
the coupling parameter
BjDr1964
,
but this is, in general, not possible when the Dirac
particle is bound in external fields, because of difficulties
in expressing infinite series of multi-commutators
in closed analytic form.
One can do the exact transformation
(to all orders in the momenta) only in rare cases.
As a consequence, for atomic bound states,
one resorts to a perturbative
scheme, which involves an expansion in higher
orders of the momenta, or in powers of a suitably chosen
coupling parameter. The coupling parameter can be
the fine-structure constant
\alpha=\alpha_{\mathrm{QED}}\approx 1/137.036

or a suitable generalization
(see Ref.
Je2014pra
).

For an electron bound to a nucleus,
in the fourth order in the momenta,
starting from the Dirac–Coulomb Hamiltonian,
one obtains
BjDr1964
;
ItZu1980
the relativistic corrections
to the hydrogen bound states, i.e.,
the relativistic
p^{4}
correction,
the zitterbewegung term (the Darwin term),
and the spin-orbit coupling (Russell–Saunders coupling).
The Foldy–Wouthuysen method was
generalized to sixth order in the momenta in
Ref.
Pa2005
, using a non-standard
transformation given in Eq. (8) of Ref.
Pa2005
,
which makes the decoupling transformation
computationally easier. It
gives rise to a term [see Eq. (18) of Ref.
Pa2005
]
which involves the time-derivative of the electric field,

H\sim-\frac{e}{16m^{3}}\{\sigma\cdot\vec{\pi},\,\sigma\cdot\dot{\vec{E}}\}\,,

(1)

where
\vec{\pi}=\vec{p}-e\,\vec{A}
is the
kinetic momentum
(throughout this paper,
e=-|e|
is the electron charge).
Furthermore,

\vec{E}
is the electric field, and

\{A,B\}=A\,B+B\,A
denotes the anticommutator.
One may eliminate
the time-derivative of the electric field by an
additional unitary transformation given in Eq. (19)
of Ref.
PaYePa2016
.

Throughout this paper, we use Coulomb gauge, so that
one can easily identify the longitudinal and
transverse parts of the electric field
\vec{E}
,
which are related to the vector potential
\vec{A}
as

\vec{A}=\vec{A}_{\perp}\,,\qquad\vec{E}_{\parallel}=-\vec{\nabla}A^{0}\,,\qquad\vec{E}_{\perp}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\vec{A}\,,

(2)

where
\perp
denotes the transverse (divergence-free)
field, and
\parallel
denotes the longitudinal
(curl-free) field component.
Furthermore, we shall use the convention

V=e\,A^{0}

(3)

for the binding potential (here,
e
is the electron charge).
In the treatment of atomic bound states,
the binding potential is often approximated by the
Coulomb potential
V(r)=-Z\alpha/r
,
where
Z
is the nuclear charge number,

\alpha
is the fine-structure constant,
and
r
is the distance of the orbiting particle
and the nucleus. However, in the treatment of
bound states in a Penning trap
BrGa1982
;
Br1985aop
;
BrGa1986
,
the binding potential is given by the electric quadrupole
field of the Penning trap, while an additional
strong magnetic field provides the
axial confinement. The binding Coulomb field
is replaced by the binding field of the Penning trap.

The problem of the calculation
of the higher-order corrections to the
Foldy–Wouthuysen transformation
has been considered, quite recently, in Ref.
ZaPa2010
,
where the general Hamiltonian for particles with
arbitrary spin has been investigated.
The special case of
s=1/2
has been treated
in Eqs. (36)–(38) of Ref.
ZaPa2010
.
Furthermore, in Eq. (7) of Ref.
HaZhKoKa2020
,
a Hamiltonian has been indicated which has been obtained from
the nonrelativistic quantum electrodynamics (NRQED)
approach outlined in Ref.
HiLePaSo2013
.
Indeed, in Ref.
HiLePaSo2013
,
the coefficients in the effective Hamiltonian
were obtained by matching of the NRQED Hamiltonian,
given in Eq. (1) of Ref.
HiLePaSo2013
,
with scattering amplitude calculations.
It is an interesting question to compare the NRQED approach to the
sixth-order generalization of the standard Foldy–Wouthuysen
transformation, outlined in Refs.
FoWu1950
;
BjDr1964
;
ItZu1980
.

Thus, we here present an application of the

standard
Foldy–Wouthuysen
transformation
FoWu1950
;
BjDr1964
;
ItZu1980
,
which is based on the
iterative elimination of “odd” operators in the
Hamiltonian via unitary transformations,
to sixth order in the momenta,
for general electric and magnetic fields.
Our approach is sufficiently general to be valid for strong
magnetic fields, and is thus applicable
to electrons bound to Penning traps
BrGa1982
;
Br1985aop
;
BrGa1986
.
The bound states in Penning traps differ from atomic
bound states in the sense that
the primary binding fields are the static
magnetic field, directed along the trap axis,
and the electric quadrupole field of the trap.
The standard Foldy–Wouthuysen approach, in higher
orders, offers technical difficulties which
are overcome in the current investigation.

In particular, we do not rely on any
non-standard transformations, which
were otherwise used in Ref.
Pa2005
.
As a consequence, we are able to compare the
standard sixth-order Foldy–Wouthuysen approach
to the generalized nonstandard Foldy–Wouthuysen transformations
outlined in Ref.
Pa2005
.
In the standard approach,
one eliminates odd operators
by a well-defined iterative procedure.
Here, the odd operators are
understood as the off-diagonal entries
in the bispinor basis. Let us consider an example.
For a Hermitian
Hamiltonian
H
, of the form

H=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathcal{E}&\mathcal{O}\\
\mathcal{O}^{\dagger}&\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\\
\end{array}\right)\,,\qquad H^{\dagger}=H\,,

(4)

where
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}^{\dagger}
,
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=\mathcal{E}^{\prime\dagger}

and
\mathcal{O}
are
2\times 2
matrices,
the odd operator is just
\mathcal{O}
. The iterative elimination
of
\mathcal{O}
, through successive applications of the unitary
transformations, is the aim of the Foldy–Wouthuysen method.

Units with
\hbar=c=\epsilon_{0}=1
are employed.
This paper is organized as follows.
In Sec.
II
, we consider the scaling
of operators for an electron bound in a Penning trap.
This scaling is different from that encountered in
an atom, because the binding fields (in particular,
the magnetic field) have to be given more weight.
In particular, the magnetic field enters at a lower
order in a generalized coupling parameter
(generalized fine-structure constant) than in
atomic bound systems. In fact, in Sec.
II
,
we define suitable generalized coupling parameters for the
electron bound in the Penning trap.
In Sec.
III
, we carry out the main part of the
calculations for the sixth-order, and seventh-order,
Foldy–Wouthuysen transformation pertaining to
bound electrons.
We obtain general results which allow us to
carry out a detailed comparison between the
different approaches previously pursued in
Refs.
Pa2005
;
ZaPa2010
;
PaYePa2016
;
HiLePaSo2013
;
HaZhKoKa2020
.
We then specialize the general expressions to the
case of a Penning trap (Sec.
IV
),
and derive a few higher-order terms,
supplementing previous investigations
BrGa1982
;
Br1985aop
;
BrGa1986
.
These are important for the determination
of the fine-structure constant from measurements
of the anomalous magnetic moment of the electron.
Finally, in Sec.
V
, we draw some conclusions.

II
Preparatory Considerations

II.1
Penning Trap

We shall attempt to devise a formalism for the
systematic analysis of higher-order corrections to
the bound energy levels for electrons
bound in field configurations where the
wave functions are spatially confined by the field
geometry, and a discrete spectrum of bound states
results.
Due to the spatial confinement,
one obtains a discrete spectrum of bound states.
Our investigations are motivated, to a large extent,
by the necessity to extend the usual Foldy–Wouthuysen
formalism to situations with strong confining magnetic
fields. An example is given by an electron confined
in a Penning trap (see
Refs.
BrGa1982
;
Br1985aop
;
BrGa1986
).

In a Penning trap, one has a
strong, constant, uniform,
confining, magnetic field along the trap axis
(the
z
axis), given as
\vec{B}_{\mathrm{T}}=\hat{\mathrm{e}}_{z}\,B_{\mathrm{T}}
.
The corresponding vector potential is

\vec{A}_{\mathrm{T}}=\frac{1}{2}\,(\vec{B}_{\mathrm{T}}\times\vec{r})=\frac{1}{2}\,(\vec{B}_{\mathrm{T}}\times\vec{\rho})\,,

(5)

where
\vec{\rho}
is the position vector in the

xy
plane,

\vec{\rho}=\vec{r}_{\parallel}=x\,\hat{\mathrm{e}}_{x}+y\,\hat{\mathrm{e}}_{y}\,.

(6)

We decompose the momentum operator as

\vec{p}=\vec{p}_{\parallel}+\vec{p}_{\perp}
,
where
\vec{p}_{\parallel}=p_{x}\hat{\mathrm{e}}_{x}+p_{y}\,\hat{\mathrm{e}}_{y}
,
and
\vec{p}_{\perp}=p_{z}\hat{\mathrm{e}}_{z}
.
The kinetic trap momentum
\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}}
is

\displaystyle\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}}=

\displaystyle\;\vec{p}-e\vec{A}_{\mathrm{T}}=\vec{p}_{\parallel}-\frac{e}{2}\,(\vec{B}_{\mathrm{T}}\times\vec{r})+\vec{p}_{\perp}=\vec{\pi}_{\parallel}+\vec{p}_{\perp}\,,

(7)

\displaystyle\vec{\pi}_{\parallel}=

\displaystyle\;\vec{p}_{\parallel}-\frac{e}{2}\,(\vec{B}_{\mathrm{T}}\times\vec{r})\,.\qquad

(8)

The
scalar potential is
A^{0}
.
The quadrupole potential
V
of the Penning
trap is

\displaystyle V=

\displaystyle\;e\,A^{0}=V_{0}\,\frac{z^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}\rho^{2}}{2d^{2}}=V_{z}+V_{\parallel}\,,

(9a)

\displaystyle V_{z}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{1}{2}m\omega_{z}^{2}z^{2}\qquad V_{\parallel}=-\frac{1}{4}m\omega_{z}^{2}\rho^{2}\,,

(9b)

\displaystyle\omega_{z}^{2}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{V_{0}}{md^{2}}\,,

(9c)

where
V_{0}>0
and
d>0
are constants.
Note that
V_{\parallel}
is repulsive, while

V_{z}
is an attractive harmonic potential.
We also note that

V_{0}
has physical dimension of energy,
and
d
has a physical dimension of length.
We found it convenient to absorb
the elementary charge
e

in the definition of
V_{0}
, which leads to
a slight change in the notation as compared
to Ref.
BrGa1986
.
We write the spin
g
factor of the
electron as
g=2(1+\kappa)
, where
\kappa\approx\alpha/(2\pi)
is the anomalous magnetic-moment term
Sc1948
.
The nonrelativistic Hamiltonian, including the
anomalous magnetic-moment term, can be written as

\displaystyle H_{0}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{(\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}})^{2}}{2m}+V-\frac{e}{2m}\,\kappa\,\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{B}_{\mathrm{T}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\frac{(\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi}_{\parallel})^{2}}{2m}-\frac{e}{2m}\,\kappa\,\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{B}_{\mathrm{T}}+\frac{p_{z}^{2}}{2m}+V\,.

(10)

Using the result

\displaystyle(\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi}_{\parallel})^{2}=

\displaystyle\;\vec{p}_{\parallel}^{\,2}-e\vec{L}\cdot\vec{B}_{\mathrm{T}}+\frac{m^{2}\omega_{c}^{2}}{4}\,\rho^{2}-e\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{B}_{\mathrm{T}}\,,

(11)

\displaystyle\omega_{c}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{|e|\,B_{\mathrm{T}}}{m}\,,

(12)

where
\omega_{c}
is the cyclotron frequency
and
\vec{L}
is the angular momentum operator,
one can write
H_{0}=H_{\parallel}+H_{\sigma}+H_{z}

as the sum of an
orbital Hamiltonian
H_{\parallel}
which acts in the

xy
plane, of a magnetic Hamiltonian
H_{\sigma}

which couples to the spin,
and of a Hamiltonian
H_{z}
which
confines the particle along the
z
axis,
in a harmonic potential due to the quadrupole
field of the trap,

\displaystyle H_{0}=

\displaystyle\;H_{\parallel}+H_{\sigma}+H_{z}\,,

(13a)

\displaystyle H_{\parallel}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{\vec{p}_{\parallel}^{\,2}}{2m}-\frac{e}{2m}\vec{L}\cdot\vec{B}_{\mathrm{T}}+\frac{m\omega_{c}^{2}}{8}\,\rho^{2}+V_{\parallel}\,,

(13b)

\displaystyle H_{\sigma}=

\displaystyle\;-\frac{e}{2m}\,(1+\kappa)\,\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{B}_{\mathrm{T}}\,,

(13c)

\displaystyle H_{z}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{p_{z}^{2}}{2m}+V_{z}\,.

(13d)

Due to its harmonic-oscillator structure,

can be written as

(14)

\displaystyle\;\sqrt{\frac{m\omega_{z}}{2}}\,z+\mathrm{i}\left(\frac{1}{2m\omega_{z}}\right)^{1/2}\,p_{z}\,,

(15a)

\displaystyle a_{z}^{\dagger}=

\displaystyle\;\sqrt{\frac{m\omega_{z}}{2}}\,z-\mathrm{i}\left(\frac{1}{2m\omega_{z}}\right)^{1/2}\,p_{z}\,.

(15b)

With reference to Eq. (

\displaystyle\;\frac{\vec{p}_{\parallel}^{\,2}}{2m}+\frac{\omega_{c}}{2}L_{z}+\frac{m(\omega_{c}^{2}-2\omega_{z}^{2})}{8}\,\rho^{2}

\displaystyle\;\left(\frac{m}{2\,\left(\omega_{{\mbox{\scriptsize$(+)$}}}-\omega_{{\mbox{\scriptsize$(-)$}}}\right)}\right)^{1/2}\,\left(V_{{\mbox{\scriptsize$(+)$}}x}+\mathrm{i}\,V_{{\mbox{\scriptsize$(+)$}}y}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\;\left(\frac{m}{2\,\left(\omega_{{\mbox{\scriptsize$(+)$}}}-\omega_{{\mbox{\scriptsize$(-)$}}}\right)}\right)^{1/2}\,\left(V_{{\mbox{\scriptsize$(-)$}}x}+\mathrm{i}\,V_{{\mbox{\scriptsize$(-)$}}y}\right)\,,

II.2
Unperturbed Eigenfunctions

Eigenfunctions of the unperturbed, nonrelativistic Hamiltonian

H_{0}
[see Eqs. (
II.1
) and (

)]
are described by the spin projection quantum number
s
, the
axial quantum number
k
, and the magnetron quantum number
\ell
,
illustrating the fact that an electron bound in a
Penning trap merely constitutes an “artificial atom”,
with the trap fields replacing the binding Coulomb field.
The quantum numbers take the following values:

\displaystyle k=

\displaystyle\;0,1,2,\dots\,,

\displaystyle\mbox{(axial)}\,,

(23)

\displaystyle\ell=

\displaystyle\;0,1,2,\dots\,,

\displaystyle\mbox{(magnetron)}\,,

(24)

\displaystyle n=

\displaystyle\;0,1,2,\dots

\displaystyle\mbox{(cyclotron)}\,,

\displaystyle s=

\displaystyle\;\pm 1\,,

\displaystyle E_{k\ell ns}=

II.3
Scaling for Strong Fields and Penning Trap

In order to illustrate the analogy of the
bound spectrum inside a Penning trap
and an electron bound in an atom,
we introduce coupling parameters.
With reference to the QED coupling
\alpha_{\mathrm{QED}}=e^{2}/(4\pi)\approx 1/137.036
, we refer to these
as the cyclotron coupling parameter

\alpha_{c}
(which could otherwise be referred to as the
cyclotron fine-structure constant),
and the axial coupling parameter
\alpha_{z}
,

\alpha_{c}=\sqrt{\frac{\omega_{c}}{m}}\,,\qquad\alpha_{z}=\sqrt{\frac{\omega_{z}}{m}}\,.

(38)

A third coupling parameter,
pertaining to the magnetron frequency,
is defined in Eq. (
42
).
Because of the hierarchy of
typical frequencies in a trap

, one has

\alpha_{z}\ll\alpha_{c}\,.

(39)

Once the cyclotron and the axial frequencies are defined,
we can calculate the magnetron frequency
based on Eq. (

).
One can define a trap fine-structure constant

in terms of the maximum of the coupling parameters

and

\alpha_{\mathrm{T}}=\max(\alpha_{z},\alpha_{c})\,,\qquad\omega_{\mathrm{T}}=\max(\omega_{z},\omega_{c})\,.

(40)

Then, we can define scaling parameters
\xi_{c}
and
\xi_{z}
by

\alpha_{c}=\xi_{c}\,\alpha_{\mathrm{T}}\,,\qquad\alpha_{z}=\xi_{z}\,\alpha_{\mathrm{T}}\,,\qquad\max(\xi_{c},\xi_{z})=1\,.

(41)

For the magnetron coupling parameter
\alpha_{\mathrm{m}}
,
it follows that

\displaystyle\alpha_{\mathrm{m}}=

\displaystyle\;\sqrt{\frac{\omega_{\mbox{\scriptsize$(-)$}}}{m}}=\xi_{\mathrm{m}}\,\alpha_{\mathrm{T}}\,,

(42a)

\displaystyle\xi_{\mathrm{m}}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\xi_{c}^{2}-\sqrt{\xi_{c}^{4}-2\xi_{z}^{4}}\right)^{1/2}\approx\frac{\xi_{z}^{2}}{\sqrt{2}\,\xi_{c}}\,,

where
\xi_{\mathrm{m}}
is smaller than either

\xi_{c}
or
\xi_{z}
.
Electron momenta in the trap can be shown to be of order

p_{\mathrm{T}}\sim\alpha_{\mathrm{T}}\,m\,,

(43)

in analogy to an atom, where
\alpha_{\mathrm{T}}
would be
replaced by
\alpha_{\mathrm{QED}}
.
(By
\sim
we indicate that
the quantities on the right and left are
of the same order-of-magnitude, while

\approx
is reserved to indicate approximate equality.)
In atoms, the wave function is spread over a length scale
commensurate with the Bohr radius

a_{0}=\hbar/p
, where
p
is a characteristic momentum.
We conclude that the “trapped Bohr radius”
a_{0{\mathrm{T}}}
is

a_{0{\mathrm{T}}}=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{m\,\omega_{\mathrm{T}}}}=\frac{1}{\alpha_{\mathrm{T}}\,m}\sim\frac{\hbar}{p_{\mathrm{T}}}\,.

With these definitions, we can establish the scaling
of frequencies, momenta and position operators inside a

12
) and (
38

we have

\omega_{c}=\frac{|e|\,B_{\mathrm{T}}}{m}=\alpha_{c}^{2}\,m\sim\alpha_{\mathrm{T}}^{2}\,m\,.

(45)

It is clear that the position vector

scales as

|\vec{r}|\sim a_{0{\mathrm{T}}}=\frac{1}{\alpha_{\mathrm{T}}m}\,.

(46)

The scaling of the quadrupole potential follows as

V\sim\omega_{z}^{2}(z^{2}-\rho^{2})\sim\alpha_{\mathrm{T}}^{4}\;\alpha_{\mathrm{T}}^{-2}m=\alpha_{\mathrm{T}}^{2}m\,,

(47)

and also

Finally, the kinetic momentum in the trap,
defined in Eq. (
7

(49)

So, the appropriate scaling for the trap implies the
following relations, which we summarize for convenience.

\displaystyle\vec{\pi}\sim

\displaystyle\;\alpha_{\mathrm{T}}\,,\qquad e\vec{A}_{\mathrm{T}}\sim\alpha_{\mathrm{T}}\,,\qquad e\,\vec{B}_{\mathrm{T}}\sim\alpha_{\mathrm{T}}^{2}\,,

(50a)

\displaystyle e\,\vec{E}_{\mathrm{T}}=

\displaystyle\;-\vec{\nabla}V\sim\alpha_{\mathrm{T}}^{3}\,,\qquad e\,\partial_{t}\vec{E}\sim\alpha_{\mathrm{T}}^{5}\,.

(50b)

The second of these implies that, if we wish to
calculate Penning trap energy levels to
order
\alpha_{\mathrm{T}}^{2}
, then we need to keep all terms
quadratic in the magnetic trap field,
and if we wish to calculate them to order
\alpha_{\mathrm{T}}^{6}
, then
we need to keep all terms cubic in the magnetic fields.
The scaling with the coupling parameters is
notably different from atomic systems
BeSa1957
.
An expansion to third order in the magnetic fields
is not necessary for atoms, where terms
of higher than the second order in the magnetic
fields can be safely
discarded

;
ZaPa2010
;
PaYePa2016
;
HiLePaSo2013
;
HaZhKoKa2020
.
This necessity, in addition to the other aspects
described in Sec.
I
, motivates revisiting
the Foldy–Wouthuysen transformation for general fields.
The scaling of the magnetic field in a Penning trap
is completely different from that in an atom.
In typical atoms, the only important magnetic field is
the dipole magnetic field generated
by the atomic nucleus, which leads to the hyperfine splitting.
In the Penning trap, by contrast, the magnetic field
provides for the binding of the electron,
which is why it needs to be taken out in higher orders.

II.4
Time Derivative and Foldy–Wouthuysen

Let us briefly review the formalism of the
Foldy–Wouthuysen transformation, with a particular
emphasis on the time derivative term.
The Foldy–Wouthuysen method
FoWu1950
is based on
a unitary transformation

U=\exp(\mathrm{i}S)\,.

(51)

In order to consistently derive the formalism,
it is necessary to realize that
the time derivative of an operator does not necessarily
commute with the operator itself.
The transformation is constructed so that,
iteratively, the Foldy–Wouthuysen
transformed Dirac Hamiltonian
is given as follows,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\rm FW}=

\displaystyle\;\exp(\mathrm{i}S)\,\left[\mathcal{H}-\mathrm{i}\partial_{t}\right]\,\exp(-\mathrm{i}S)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\mathcal{H}+[\mathrm{i}S,\mathcal{H}-\mathrm{i}\partial_{t}]+\frac{1}{2!}\,[\mathrm{i}S,[\mathrm{i}S,\mathcal{H}-\mathrm{i}\partial_{t}]]

\displaystyle\;+\frac{1}{3!}\,[\mathrm{i}S,[\mathrm{i}S,[\mathrm{i}S,\mathcal{H}-\mathrm{i}\partial_{t}]]]+\ldots

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\mathcal{H}+\delta\mathcal{H}^{(1)}+\delta\mathcal{H}^{(2)}+\delta\mathcal{H}^{(3)}+\ldots

(52)

Here, the differential operator
\mathrm{i}\partial_{t}

is understood to exclusively act on the unitary operator
\exp(-\mathrm{i}S)
,
but not on the wave function.
The time derivatives add additional terms,
which, in first order, read as follows,

\delta\mathcal{H}^{(1)}=[\mathrm{i}S,\mathcal{H}-\mathrm{i}\partial_{t}]=\mathrm{i}[S,\mathcal{H}]-\partial_{t}S\,.

(53)

Note that one can iteratively
calculate the multi-commutators in Eq. (
II.4
),

\delta\mathcal{H}^{(n+1)}=\frac{1}{n+1}\;[\mathrm{i}S,\delta\mathcal{H}^{(n)}]\,.

(54)

For a typical generalized Dirac Hamiltonian,
the Foldy–Wouthuysen transformation operator
S
is
proportional to

S\sim\vec{\alpha}\cdot\vec{\pi}\sim\alpha_{\mathrm{T}}\,,\qquad\partial_{t}S\sim\vec{\alpha}\cdot\partial_{t}\vec{\pi}\sim\alpha_{\mathrm{T}}^{3}\,.

(55)

Here, the
\vec{\alpha}
and
\beta
matrices are used in the
Dirac representation,

\vec{\alpha}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\vec{\sigma}\\
\vec{\sigma}&0\\
\end{array}\right)\,,\qquad\beta=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbbm{1}_{2\times 2}&0\\
0&-\mathbbm{1}_{2\times 2}\end{array}\right)\,,

(56)

where
\vec{\sigma}
denotes the vector of
2\times 2
Pauli matrices.
It becomes clear that the higher-order terms in the
multi-commutator expansion (
II.4
)
represent higher orders in the momenta.

In the Coulomb gauge, one can separate the electric field
into its longitudinal and transverse components,

e\vec{E}_{\parallel}=-\vec{\nabla}V\,,\qquad\vec{E}_{\perp}=-\partial_{t}\vec{A}\,.

(57)

The longitudinal component is obtained as the
commutator of kinetic momentum and potential
V
,

[\vec{\pi},V]=-\mathrm{i}\,\vec{\nabla}V=-\mathrm{i}\,e\,\vec{\nabla}A^{0}=\mathrm{i}\,e\,\vec{E}_{\parallel}\,,

(58)

while the transverse component is obtained
via the time derivative of the vector potential,

\partial_{t}\vec{\pi}=-e\,\partial_{t}\vec{A}=e\,\vec{E}_{\perp}\,.

(59)

The time derivative term
in Eq. (
53
) is decisive in ensuring that the
Foldy–Wouthuysen transformed Dirac Hamiltonian
contains the complete electric field.

which in turn has been derived from the
NRQED approach outlined in Ref.
HiLePaSo2013
.
The
\alpha^{7}
terms attain the following structure,

\displaystyle H^{[7]}=

\displaystyle\;\,\frac{e^{2}\,\kappa}{8m^{3}}\,\vec{E}^{\,2}-\frac{e\,\kappa}{16m^{3}}\,\;\{\,\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi},\vec{\sigma}\cdot\partial_{t}\vec{E}\,\}

\displaystyle\;+\frac{\mathrm{i}\,e\,\kappa}{16m^{4}}\;\{\,(\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi})^{2},[\,\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi},\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{E}\,]\}

\displaystyle\;-\,\frac{e\,\kappa}{32m^{5}}\;[\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi},[\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi},\{\,\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi},\{\,\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi},\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{B}\,\}\}\,]]

\displaystyle\;-\,\frac{3e\,\kappa}{256m^{5}}\;\{\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi},\{\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi},\{\,\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi},\{\,\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi},\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{B}\,\}\}\,\}\}

\displaystyle\;+\frac{\mathrm{i}\,e^{2}\,\kappa}{16\,m^{4}}\;[\,\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{E},\{\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi},\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{B}\}\,]\,.

(82)

In general, we have not found ways to simplify the

\alpha^{7}
terms further than the expression given by Eq. (
III.2
).

IV
Quantum Cyclotron Energy Levels

IV.1
Leading Term

Now, we return to the problem considered in Sec.
II.1

and perform simplifications for a Penning trap configuration.
The trap field
\vec{B}_{\mathrm{T}}
is assumed to be directed along the

z
axis, as a constant, uniform field, so that

\vec{\pi}=\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}}=\vec{p}-\frac{e}{2}(\vec{B}_{\mathrm{T}}\times\vec{r})\,,\quad\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\,2}=\vec{p}^{\,2}-e\vec{L}\cdot\vec{B}_{\mathrm{T}}+\frac{1}{4}m^{2}\omega_{c}^{2}\rho^{2}\,,

(83)

where we note the identity (
\vec{B}_{\mathrm{T}}\times\vec{r})^{2}=B_{\mathrm{T}}^{2}\,\rho^{2}
.
Furthermore, we ignore the radiative (transverse) electric field and set

e\vec{E}=e\vec{E}_{\parallel}=-\vec{\nabla}V\,.

(84)

Let us specialize the terms
H^{[k]}

with
k=2,\dots,6
, discussed in Sec.
III.2
,
to the case of a Penning trap.
The sum of the terms
H^{[2]}
and
H^{[3]}

is just the Hamiltonian
H_{0}
given in
Eqs. (
II.1
) and (
13
),

\displaystyle H_{0}=

\displaystyle\;H^{[2]}+H^{[3]}

(85)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\frac{\vec{p}^{\,2}}{2m}-\frac{e}{2m}\vec{L}\cdot\vec{B}_{\mathrm{T}}+\frac{m\omega_{c}^{2}}{8}\,\rho^{2}+V-\frac{e(1+\kappa)}{2m}\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{B}_{\mathrm{T}}\,.

We recall, from Eq. (
II.2
), the unperturbed energy
E^{[2+3]}
of the
unperturbed level (eigenket
|k\ell ns\rangle
) as

\displaystyle E^{[2+3]}=

\displaystyle\;E_{k\ell ns}=\langle k\ell ns|H_{0}|k\ell ns\rangle=\langle H^{[2]}+H^{[3]}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\omega_{c}(1+\kappa)\,\frac{s}{2}+\omega_{\mbox{\scriptsize$(+)$}}\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\;+\omega_{z}\left(k+\frac{1}{2}\right)-\omega_{\mbox{\scriptsize$(-)$}}\left(\ell+\frac{1}{2}\right)\,.

(86)

The energy
E^{[2+3]}=E_{0}
is the unperturbed (nonrelativistic)
energy.

IV.2
Relativistic Corrections

In a Penning trap,
the expression for
H^{[4]}
simplifies as follows,

H^{[4]}=-\frac{(\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}})^{\,4}}{8m^{3}}+\frac{\vec{\nabla}^{2}V}{8m^{2}}+\frac{\vec{\sigma}\,\cdot\,(\vec{\nabla}V\times\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}})}{4m^{2}}\,.

(87)

It is adequate to treat
H^{[4]}
and
H^{[5]}
together.
For the fifth-order Hamiltonian
H^{[5]}
, we need the
following relation, which is valid for a constant,
uniform magnetic field,

\{\,\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}},\{\,\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}},\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{B}_{\mathrm{T}}\,\}\}=4(\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}})^{2}\,(\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}}\cdot\vec{B}_{\mathrm{T}})\,.

(88)

For the Penning trap, this implies that

H^{[5]}=\frac{\kappa\vec{\nabla}^{2}V}{4m^{2}}+\frac{\kappa\vec{\sigma}\,\cdot\,(\vec{\nabla}V\times\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}})}{2m^{2}}\;+\frac{e\kappa\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}}\vec{B}_{\mathrm{T}}\cdot\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}}}{4m^{3}}\,.

(89)

It is convenient to express
the sum of
H^{[4]}
and
H^{[5]}
as follows,

H^{[4]}+H^{[5]}=-\frac{(\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}})^{\,4}}{8m^{3}}+(1+2\kappa)\frac{\vec{\nabla}^{2}V}{8m^{2}}\\
+(1+2\kappa)\frac{\vec{\sigma}\,\cdot\,(\vec{\nabla}V\times\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}})}{4m^{2}}+\frac{e\kappa\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}}\vec{B}_{\mathrm{T}}\cdot\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}}}{4m^{3}}\,.

(90)

If we are in a charge-free region, then

\vec{\nabla}^{2}V=0
, and
we have three contributions,

H^{[4]}+H^{[5]}=H_{1}+H_{2}+H_{3}

(91)

The first is the relativistic correction to the
kinetic energy,

H_{1}=-\frac{(\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}})^{4}}{8m^{3}}\,.

(92)

It leads to an energy shift
E_{1}=\langle H_{1}\rangle=\langle k\ell ns|H_{1}|k\ell ns\rangle

which reads as follows,

E_{1}=-\frac{\left[\frac{\omega_{\mbox{\scriptsize$(+)$}}^{2}\left(n+\tfrac{1}{2}\right)+\omega_{\mbox{\scriptsize$(-)$}}^{2}\left(\ell+\tfrac{1}{2}\right)}{\omega_{\mbox{\scriptsize$(+)$}}-\omega_{\mbox{\scriptsize$(-)$}}}+\frac{\omega_{z}}{2}(k+\tfrac{1}{2})+\frac{\omega_{c}s}{2}\right]^{2}}{2m}\\
-\frac{\omega_{z}^{4}\left[(n+\tfrac{1}{2})(\ell+\tfrac{1}{2})+\tfrac{1}{4}\right]}{4m(\omega_{\mbox{\scriptsize$(+)$}}-\omega_{\mbox{\scriptsize$(-)$}})^{2}}-\frac{\omega_{z}^{2}}{16m}\left[\left(k+\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{2}+\tfrac{3}{4}\right].

(93)

As compared to Eq. (7.48) of Ref.
BrGa1986
,
we take the opportunity to correct an apparent
misprint,

\underbrace{(n+\tfrac{1}{2})(\ell+\tfrac{1}{2})-\tfrac{1}{4}}_{\mbox{[Eq.~(7.48) of Ref.~\cite[cite]{\@@bibref{Authors Phrase1YearPhrase2}{BrGa1986}{\@@citephrase{(}}{\@@citephrase{)}}}]}}\to\underbrace{(n+\tfrac{1}{2})(\ell+\tfrac{1}{2})+\tfrac{1}{4}}_{\mbox{[Result obtained here]}}.

(94)

The spin-orbit coupling leads to the term

H_{2}=(1+2\kappa)\,\frac{1}{4m^{2}}\,\vec{\sigma}\cdot(\vec{\nabla}V\times\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}})\,.

(95)

The energy shift
E_{2}=\langle H_{2}\rangle=\langle k\ell ns|H_{2}|k\ell ns\rangle

reads as follows,

E_{2}=-\frac{\omega_{z}^{2}s(1+2\kappa)}{4m}\frac{\omega_{\mbox{\scriptsize$(+)$}}(n+\tfrac{1}{2})+\omega_{\mbox{\scriptsize$(-)$}}(\ell+\tfrac{1}{2})}{\omega_{\mbox{\scriptsize$(+)$}}-\omega_{\mbox{\scriptsize$(-)$}}}\,.

(96)

Finally, there is an additional correction, due to the
higher-order interaction of the electron spin
with the magnetic field,

H_{3}=\frac{e\kappa}{4m^{3}}\,(\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}})\,(\vec{B}_{\mathrm{T}}\cdot\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}})\,.

(97)

The corresponding energy shift

E_{3}=\langle H_{3}\rangle
is

E_{3}=-\frac{s\kappa\omega_{c}\omega_{z}}{4m}(k+\tfrac{1}{2})\,.

(98)

The terms of fourth and fifth order lead to the joint correction

E^{[4+5]}=E_{1}+E_{2}+E_{3}\,,

(99)

where the energy corrections
E_{1}
,
E_{2}

and
E_{3}
are given in Eqs. (
93
), (
96
)
and (
98
), respectively.
In terms of the coupling parameters
defined in Sec.
II.3
, we can express

E^{[4+5]}
differently,

E^{[4+5]}=\alpha_{\mathrm{T}}^{4}m\left(-\frac{(1+s+2n)^{2}}{8}\,\xi_{c}^{4}\right.\\[0.4878pt]
-\frac{(1+2k)\,[1+2n+s(1+\kappa)]}{8}\,\xi_{c}^{2}\,\xi_{z}^{2}\\[0.4878pt]
\left.-\frac{3+6k(1+k)+4s(1+2n)(1+2\kappa)}{32}\,\xi_{z}^{4}+\mathcal{O}(\xi_{z}^{8})\right).

(100)

The term of order
\xi_{z}^{6}
vanishes.
If the dominant (angular) frequency in the trap
is the cyclotron frequency, then
we have
\xi_{c}=1
according
to Eq. (
41
).

IV.3
Sixth–Order Corrections

For the Penning trap, the sixth-order terms assume the form

\displaystyle H^{[6]}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{(\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}})^{6}}{16m^{5}}-\frac{5\mathrm{i}}{128m^{4}}\left[\left(\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}}\right)^{2},\left\{\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\nabla}V,\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}}\right\}\right]

\displaystyle\;-\frac{3\mathrm{i}}{64m^{4}}\;\{\,(\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}})^{2},[\,\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}},\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\nabla}V\,]\}+\frac{(\vec{\nabla}V)^{2}}{8m^{3}}\,.

(101)

One has two further useful relations.
The first of these is

[(\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}})^{2},\{\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\nabla}V,\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}}\}]=\mathrm{i}\left[\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\,2},\left[\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\,2},V\right]\right]

(102)

and the second,

\{(\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}})^{2},[\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}},\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\nabla}V]\}\\
=-\mathrm{i}\left\{(\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}})^{2},\vec{\nabla}^{2}V+2\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\nabla}V\times\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}}\right\}\,.

(103)

Hence, one can express the sixth-order terms as follows,

\displaystyle H^{[6]}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{(\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}})^{6}}{16m^{4}}+\frac{5}{128m^{4}}\left[\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\,2},\left[\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\,2},V\right]\right]

\displaystyle\;-\frac{3}{64m^{4}}\;\{\,(\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}})^{2},[\,\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}},\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\nabla}V\,]\}+\frac{(\vec{\nabla}V)^{2}}{8m^{3}}\,.

(104)

Overall, in the sixth order, one has two terms, which are

(i)
directly due to the sixth-order Hamiltonian
and
(ii)
due to a second-order effect,
involving the fourth-order Hamiltonian,

E^{[6]}=\langle k\ell ns|H^{[6]}|k\ell ns\rangle\\
+\langle k\ell ns|H^{[4]}\,\left(\frac{1}{E_{0}-H_{0}}\right)^{\prime}\,H^{[4]}|k\ell ns\rangle\,,

(105)

where
[1/(E_{0}-H_{0})]^{\prime}

is the reduced Green function.
A remark is in order.
For a Penning trap, the unperturbed eigenstates
are separately eigenstates of the Hamiltonian
H^{[3]}
,
and so, a conceivable additional sixth-order
term vanishes,

\left<k\ell ns\left|H^{[3]}\,\left(\frac{1}{E_{0}-H_{0}}\right)^{\prime}\,H^{[5]}\right|k\ell ns\right>=0\,.

(106)

The exact expression for
\langle H^{[6]}\rangle

is very lengthy; however, an expansion into the
coupling parameters defined in Sec.
II.3

leads to the compact expression

\langle H^{[6]}\rangle=\alpha_{\mathrm{T}}^{6}m\biggl[\frac{(1+s+2n)^{3}}{16}\,\xi_{c}^{6}\\[0.4878pt]
+\frac{3(1+2k)\,(1+2n+s)^{3}}{32}\,\xi_{c}^{4}\,\xi_{z}^{2}\\
+\frac{3}{64}\bigl[(5+6k(1+k))(1+2n)\\
+(5+6k(1+k)+8n(1+n))s\bigr]\,\xi_{c}^{2}\xi_{z}^{4}\\
+\frac{1}{128}(1+2k)(13+10k(1+k)+6(1+2n)s)\xi_{z}^{6}+\mathcal{O}(\xi_{z}^{8})\biggr]\,.

(107)

After expansion in
\xi_{z}
, the second-order shift is

\left<k\ell ns\left|H^{[4]}\,\left(\frac{1}{E_{0}-H_{0}}\right)^{\prime}\,H^{[4]}\right|k\ell ns\right>\\
=\alpha_{\mathrm{T}}^{6}m\biggl[-\frac{1}{32}(1+2k)(1+s+2n(1+n+s))\xi_{c}^{4}\,\xi_{z}^{2}\\
-\frac{1}{64}(5+6k(1+k))(1+2n+s)\,\xi_{c}^{2}\,\xi_{z}^{4}\\
-\frac{1}{512}(1+2k)(45+17k(1+k)+24(1+2n)s)\,\xi_{z}^{6}+\mathcal{O}(\xi_{z}^{8})\biggr]\,.

(108)

The total sixth-order energy shift is obtained
as the sum of the results given in
Eqs. (
107
) and (
108
),

E^{[6]}=\alpha_{\mathrm{T}}^{6}m\biggl[\frac{1}{16}(1+2n+s)^{3}\xi_{c}^{6}\\
+\frac{(1+2k)(10n(1+n+s)+2+5s+3s^{2})\xi_{c}^{4}\xi_{z}^{2}}{32}\\
+\frac{(5+6k(1+k))(1+2n+s)+12n(1+n)s)\xi_{c}^{2}\xi_{z}^{4}}{32}\\
+\frac{1}{512}(1+2k)(7+23k(1+k))\xi_{z}^{6}+\mathcal{O}(\xi_{z}^{8})\biggr]\,.

(109)

We leave the evaluation of the seventh-order corrections for
a Penning trap to a future investigation.
These, otherwise, also include higher-order corrections
to the Lamb shift (self-energy) of the quantum states.

V
Conclusions

The main results of the current investigation can be
summarized as follows.
We have carried out, in Sec.
III.1
, the
full iterative Foldy–Wouthuysen transformation
of the single-particle Dirac Hamiltonian, coupled to general
electromagnetic fields,
up to seventh order in the momenta.
The results, for the
4\times 4
matrices
that combine the particle and antiparticle
states, are given in Eqs. (
66
)–(
III.1
).
The effective Hamiltonian for the particle
(as opposed to the antiparticle) is given in
Eqs. (
78
)–(
III.2
).
We have clarified
that the standard Foldy–Wouthuysen
method, iteratively applied, reproduces the
effective Hamiltonian, to order
\alpha^{7}
,
which has been derived based on NRQED methods in Ref.
HiLePaSo2013
.
So, we have shown that the Hamiltonian
first obtained by a nonstandard Foldy–Wouthuysen
transformation in Ref.
Pa2005
,
and then augmented by an additional
unitary transformation given in Eq. (19) of Ref.
PaYePa2016
,
is exactly equal to the gauge-covariant Hamiltonian used
recently in Eq. (7) of Ref.
HaZhKoKa2020
.
It is instructive to recall that the
kinetic momentum
\vec{\pi}
is gauge covariant,
but not gauge invariant, because
\vec{A}

transforms nontrivially under gauge transformations.
As a result of the investigations reported here,
complete agreement between the various methods
has been achieved, and
the calculations have been extended to the seventh
order in
\alpha
, including effects due
to strong magnetic fields.

Our results are valid for cases where the
binding of the electron proceeds in strong external
fields such as those encountered in Penning traps.
In such cases, the term
|e|B_{\mathrm{T}}/m=\omega_{c}

(cyclotron frequency),
where
B_{\mathrm{T}}
is the trap magnetic field,
is of order
\alpha_{\mathrm{T}}^{2}m
where
\alpha_{\mathrm{T}}

is a suitably defined coupling constant
for the trap [see Eq. (
38
)].
In a Penning trap, the magnetic field is not an external perturbation,
but provides the decisive energy scale for the
bound states inside the trap.
After an initial discussion of
the separation of the electron Hamiltonian
inside the trap, carried out in Sec.
II.1
,
and the discussion of scaling relations in Sec.
II.3
,
we discuss the relevant expressions for
relativistic corrections to
electron energy levels in quantum cyclotrons
in Sec.
IV
.

Our results, given for the Penning trap in
Eqs. (
93
)–(
98
), (
100
) and (
109
),
enable a more accurate evaluation of
the relativistic corrections to quantum cyclotron
states, which are important for the determination
of the fine-structure
constant
BrGa1982
;
BrGaHeTa1985
;
GaEtAl2006everything
;
HaFoGa2008
;
FaGa2021prl
;
FaGa2021pra
.
Terms of seventh order in
\alpha_{\mathrm{T}}
can be obtained
from Eq. (
III.2
) under the
substitutions
\vec{\pi}\to\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}}
,
and
e\vec{E}\to-\vec{\nabla}V
.
However, these are of the same order as
the relativistic corrections to the Lamb shift,
notably, to the relativistic Bethe logarithm
(order
\alpha_{\mathrm{T}}^{7}\,m
).
Hence, we leave these terms for a future work.
We do not indicate them separately.

In a more general context, our calculations show that it is possible
to generalize the standard, direct
calculation of the Foldy–Wouthuysen transformation
to seventh order in the coupling parameters,
under the intensive use of computer algebra
Wo1999
.

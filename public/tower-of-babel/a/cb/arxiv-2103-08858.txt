A Whipple F 6 7 {}_{7}F_{6} formula revisited
Wen-Ching Winnie Li † , Ling Long ‡ and Fang-Ting Tu ‡

Abstract
. A well-known formula of Whipple relates certain hypergeometric values F 6 7 ​ ( 1 ) {}_{7}F_{6}(1) and F 3 4 ​ ( 1 ) {}_{4}F_{3}(1) . In this paper we revisit this relation from the viewpoint of the underlying hypergeometric data H ​ D HD , to which there are also associated hypergeometric character sums and Galois representations. We explain a special structure behind Whipple’s formula when the hypergeometric data H ​ D HD are primitive and self-dual. If the data are also defined over ℚ \mathbb{Q} , by the work of Katz, Beukers, Cohen, and Mellit, there are compatible families of ℓ \ell -adic representations of the absolute Galois group of ℚ \mathbb{Q} attached to H ​ D HD . For specialized choices of H ​ D HD , these Galois representations are shown to be decomposable and automorphic. As a consequence, the values of the corresponding hypergeometric character sums can be explicitly expressed in terms of Fourier coefficients of certain modular forms. We further relate the hypergeometric values F 6 7 ​ ( 1 ) {}_{7}F_{6}(1) in Whipple’s formula to the periods of these modular forms.

2.2.
Hypergeometric character sums and Galois representations

To a hypergeometric datum
HD=\{\alpha,\beta;\lambda\}
consisting of a primitive pair of multi-sets
\alpha=\{a_{1},...,a_{n}\},\beta=\{1,b_{2},...,b_{n}\}
with
M=lcd(\alpha\cup\beta)
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{M},1/M]\setminus\{0\}
, and a prime
\ell
, we associate the
\ell
-adic representation
\rho_{HD,\ell}
of
G(M)=G_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{M})}
as in Theorem
1
. We are interested in when it can be extended to a representation of
G_{K}
for a subfield
K
of
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{M})
, in particular
K=\mathbb{Q}
. This happens if and only if
\rho_{HD,\ell}
is isomorphic to its conjugation by each element in Gal
(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{M})/K)
. This is a standard result by the Clifford theory
[
15
]
and Frobenius reciprocity. For related discussions, see
[
37
, §2.6]
by Li, Liu and Long. If this happens we say
\{\alpha,\beta;\lambda\}
can be
extended
to
K
.

We now explain the Galois action. An element
\tau\in
Gal
(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{M})/\mathbb{Q})
sends
\zeta_{M}
to
\zeta_{M}^{c}
for some
c=c(\tau)\in(\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. The conjugation of
\rho_{HD,\ell}
by
\tau
, denoted by
\rho_{HD,\ell}^{\tau}
, satisfies

\text{Tr}\rho_{HD,\ell}^{\tau}(\text{Frob}_{\wp})=\text{Tr}\rho_{HD,\ell}(\text{Frob}_{\tau(\wp)})=\text{Tr}\rho_{c\cdot HD,\ell}(\text{Frob}_{\wp})

for almost all prime ideals
\wp
of
\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{M}]
. Regard the pair
\alpha,\beta
in HD as a multi-set of column vectors
\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}\\
1\end{pmatrix},\cdots,\begin{pmatrix}a_{n}\\
b_{n}\end{pmatrix}\right\}
. Here
c\cdot HD
denotes the datum

\{c\cdot\alpha,c\cdot\beta;\lambda\}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}ca_{1}\\
c\end{pmatrix},...,\begin{pmatrix}ca_{n}\\
cb_{n}\end{pmatrix};\lambda\right\}.

This motivates the following definitions.

Definition 1
.

For a primitive pair
\alpha,\beta
with
M=lcd(\alpha\cup\beta)
as above and
c\in(\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
, the pairs
\alpha,\beta
and
c\cdot\alpha,c\cdot\beta
are
congruent mod
\mathbb{Z}
if
the two multi-sets

\left\{\begin{pmatrix}ca_{1}\\
c\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}ca_{2}\\
cb_{2}\end{pmatrix},...,\begin{pmatrix}ca_{n}\\
cb_{n}\end{pmatrix}\right\}
and
\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}\\
1\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}a_{2}\\
b_{2}\end{pmatrix},...,\begin{pmatrix}a_{n}\\
b_{n}\end{pmatrix}\right\}
are congruent mod
\mathbb{Z}
. The pair
\alpha,\beta
is said to be
self-dual
if it is congruent to the pair
-\alpha,-\beta
mod
\mathbb{Z}
, and it is
defined over a subfield
K
of
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{M})
if it is congruent to
c\cdot\alpha,c\cdot\beta
mod
\mathbb{Z}
for all
c=c(\tau)
, where
\tau\in
Gal
(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{M})/K)
. A hypergeometric datum
HD=\{\alpha,\beta;\lambda\}
is said to be
self-dual
if the pair
\alpha,\beta
is self-dual and
\lambda
is totally real; it is
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
if the pair
\alpha,\beta
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
.

Clearly, if the pair
\alpha,\beta
in
HD
above is defined over
K
and
\lambda\in K^{\times}
, then the representation
\rho_{HD,\ell}
can be extended to
G_{K}
.

Remark 2.
Let
K
be a number field with the ring of integers
\mathbb{Z}_{K}
. Since the prime ideals of
\mathbb{Z}_{K}
of degree 1 have density one among all prime ideals of
\mathbb{Z}_{K}
, by the Chebotarev density theorem, if two
\ell
-adic representations of
G_{K}
ramified only at finitely many prime ideals have the same traces at
\text{Frob}_{\wp}
for almost all prime ideals
\wp
of
\mathbb{Z}_{K}
of degree 1, then they are isomorphic as
G_{K}
representations provided that they are semi-simple. In particular, if
K=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{M})
is a cyclotomic field, the semi-simplification of an
\ell
-adic representation of
G_{K}
ramified only at finitely many prime ideals, such as those in Theorems
1
and
8
, is determined by its traces at
\text{Frob}_{\wp}
for almost all prime ideals
\wp
of
\mathbb{Z}_{K}
above primes
p\equiv 1\mod M
.

2.3.

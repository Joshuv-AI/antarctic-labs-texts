Error Exponent and Strong Converse for Quantum Soft Covering
Hao-Chung Cheng 1,2,3,4 and Li Gao 5

Abstract
. How well can we approximate a quantum channel output state using a random codebook with a certain size? In this work, we study the quantum soft covering problem. Namely, we use a random codebook with codewords independently sampled from a prior distribution and send it through a classical-quantum channel to approximate the target state. When using a random independent and identically distributed codebook with a rate above the quantum mutual information, we show that the expected trace distance between the codebook-induced state and the target state decays with exponent given by the sandwiched Rényi information. On the other hand, when the rate of the codebook size is below the quantum mutual information, the trace distance converges to one exponentially fast. We obtain similar results when using a random constant composition codebook, whereas the sandwiched Augustin information expresses the error exponent. In addition to the above large deviation analysis, our results also hold in the moderate deviation regime. That is, we show that even when the rate of the codebook size approaches the quantum mutual information moderately quickly, the trace distance still vanishes asymptotically.

Let

The goal of quantum soft covering is to approximate the marginal state at the channel output, i.e.

\displaystyle{\rho}_{B}^{\mathcal{C}}:=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{C}}\rho_{B}^{x}.

and the true marginal state

as the figure of merit:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathds{E}_{\mathcal{C}}\left\|{\rho}_{B}^{\mathcal{C}}-\rho_{B}\right\|_{1}.

x:\Omega\to\mathcal{X}
be a random codeword with respect to the distribution
p_{X}
, where
\Omega
is the event space. We can rewrite the classical-quantum state
\rho_{XB}
as

\rho_{\Omega B}=\sum_{x}1_{A_{x}}\otimes\rho_{x}\in L_{\infty}(\Omega,\mathcal{B(H)})\hskip 2.84544pt,

where
1_{A_{x}}
is the characteristic function on the mutually disjoint set
A_{x}
, which satisfying
\Pr(A_{x})=p_{X}(x)
and
\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\Pr(A_{x})=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}p_{X}(x)=1
. In particular, we have
\mathds{E}_{\Omega}\rho_{\Omega B}=\rho_{B}
.
Take
1<{\alpha}<2
and
\frac{1}{{\alpha}}+\frac{1}{{\alpha}^{\prime}}=1
. The Rényi information can be expressed as

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}^{*}\left(X{\,:\,}B\right)_{\rho}

\displaystyle=\inf_{\sigma}\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\left(\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}p_{X}(x)\left\|\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2{\alpha}^{\prime}}}\rho_{x}\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2{\alpha}^{\prime}}}\right\|_{\alpha}^{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})}\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\log\parallel\sigma_{\Omega B}^{-\frac{1}{2{\alpha}^{\prime}}}\rho_{\Omega B}\sigma_{\Omega B}^{-\frac{1}{2{\alpha}^{\prime}}}\parallel_{L_{\alpha}(\Omega,S_{{\alpha}}({\mathcal{H}}))}

where
\sigma_{\Omega B}:=1_{\Omega}\otimes\sigma
is interpreted as a constant function on
L_{\infty}(\Omega,\mathcal{B}({\mathcal{H}}))
. In other words,

\displaystyle\e^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}I_{\alpha}^{*}\left(X{\,:\,}B\right)_{\rho}}=\inf_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})}\parallel\sigma_{\Omega B}^{-\frac{1}{2{\alpha}^{\prime}}}\rho_{\Omega B}\sigma_{\Omega B}^{-\frac{1}{2{\alpha}^{\prime}}}\parallel_{L_{\alpha}(\Omega,S_{{\alpha}}({\mathcal{H}}))}\hskip 2.84544pt.

Now using the construction in the Lemma
2
, we have

\displaystyle\mathds{E}_{\mathcal{C}}\left\|\rho_{B}^{\mathcal{C}}-\rho_{B}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathds{E}_{\mathcal{C}}\left\|\frac{1}{M}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{C}}\rho_{x}-\rho_{B}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\parallel\Theta(\rho_{\Omega B})\parallel_{L_{1}(\Omega^{M},S_{1}(\mathcal{H}))}.

Note that for any state
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})
,

\displaystyle\parallel\Theta(\rho_{\Omega B})\parallel_{L_{1}(\Omega^{\times M},S_{1}(\mathcal{H}))}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{(a)}}{\leq}\;\parallel\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2{\alpha}^{\prime}}}\Theta(\rho_{\Omega B})\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2{\alpha}^{\prime}}}\parallel_{L_{\alpha}(\Omega^{M},S_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H}))}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{(b)}}{=}\;\parallel\Theta(\sigma_{\Omega B}^{-\frac{1}{2{\alpha}^{\prime}}}\rho_{\Omega B}\sigma_{\Omega B}^{-\frac{1}{2{\alpha}^{\prime}}})\parallel_{L_{\alpha}(\Omega^{M},S_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H}))}

\displaystyle\leq\;\parallel\Theta:L_{\alpha}(\Omega,S_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H}))\to L_{\alpha}(\Omega^{M},S_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H}))\parallel\cdot\parallel\sigma_{\Omega B}^{-\frac{1}{2{\alpha}^{\prime}}}\rho_{\Omega B}\sigma_{\Omega B}^{-\frac{1}{2{\alpha}^{\prime}}}\parallel_{L_{\alpha}(\Omega^{M},S_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H}))}.

Here, (b) used the fact
\Theta=\Theta\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{B(H)}}
is identity on the operator part , and (a) used Hölder inequality
{\parallel AXB\parallel_{1}\leq\parallel A\parallel_{2{\alpha}^{\prime}}\parallel X\parallel_{{\alpha}}\parallel B\parallel_{2{\alpha}^{\prime}}}
with

\parallel\sigma^{\frac{1}{2{\alpha}^{\prime}}}\parallel_{L_{\alpha}(\Omega^{M},S_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H}))}=\int_{\Omega^{\times M}}\parallel\sigma^{\frac{1}{2{\alpha}^{\prime}}}\parallel_{2{\alpha}^{\prime}}^{2{\alpha}^{\prime}}d\mu^{M}=1

Then the assertion follows from Lemma
2
and taking infimum over
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})
, i.e.

\displaystyle\mathds{E}_{\mathcal{C}}\left\|\rho_{B}^{\mathcal{C}}-\rho_{B}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}M^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}\cdot\inf_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})}\parallel\sigma_{\Omega B}^{-\frac{1}{2{\alpha}^{\prime}}}\rho_{\Omega B}\sigma_{\Omega B}^{-\frac{1}{2{\alpha}^{\prime}}}\parallel_{L_{\alpha}(\Omega^{\times M},S_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H}))}

\displaystyle=2^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}M^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}\e^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}I_{\alpha}^{*}\left(X{\,:\,}B\right)_{\rho}}.

That finishes the proof.
∎

3.1.
Random I.I.D. Codebook

We consider the
n
-shot extension of quantum soft covering, where the c-q channel is now
n
-fold product:

(3.7)

\displaystyle x^{n}\mapsto\rho_{B^{n}}^{x^{n}}:=\rho_{B}^{x_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\rho_{B}^{x_{n}},\quad\forall x^{n}\in\mathcal{X}^{n}.

In this section, we investigate the case that input distribution is i.i.d., i.e.
p_{X^{n}}=p_{X}^{\otimes n}
.
Hence, the joint c-q state is the
n
-fold product state
\rho_{X^{n}B^{n}}=\rho_{XB}^{\otimes n}
and our targeted true marginal state is
\rho_{B}^{\otimes n}
.

With the i.i.d. prior
p_{X}^{\otimes n}
,
we use the codebook
\mathcal{C}^{n}
with size
|\mathcal{C}^{n}|=\exp(nR)
, where
each codeword in
\mathcal{C}^{n}
is i.i.d. drawn according to
p_{X}^{\otimes n}
.
We term this the
random i.i.d. codebook
.
Now the goal is to use the codebook-induced state
\rho_{B^{n}}^{\mathcal{C}^{n}}
to approximate
\rho_{B}^{\otimes n}
.

We apply the one-shot achievability established in Theorem
1
to show that the expected value of the trace distance between the induced state
{\rho}_{B^{n}}^{{\mathcal{C}}^{n}}
and the true marginal state
{\rho}_{B}^{\otimes n}
decays exponentially fast.

Proposition 3

(
n
-shot achievability using random i.i.d. codebook)
.

For any
n\in\mathds{N}
, let
R=\frac{1}{n}\log|{\mathcal{C}}^{n}|
.
Then,

(3.8)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathds{E}_{{\mathcal{C}}^{n}}\left\|{\rho}_{B^{n}}^{{\mathcal{C}}^{n}}-{\rho}_{B}^{\otimes n}\right\|_{1}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-n\sup_{\alpha\in(1,2)}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\left({I}_{\alpha}^{*}\left(X{\,:\,}B\right)_{\rho}-R\right)}.

Moreover, the exponent
\sup_{\alpha\in(1,2)}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\left({I}_{\alpha}^{*}\left(X{\,:\,}B\right)_{\rho}-R\right)
is positive if and only if
R>I(X{\,:\,}B)_{\rho}
.

Proof.

Recall the additivity of sandwiched Rényi information
[
25
, Lemma 4.8]
:

(3.9)

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}^{*}\left(X^{n};B^{n}\right)_{\rho}=nI_{\alpha}^{*}\left(X{\,:\,}B\right)_{\rho}.

The exponential upper bound is a direct consequence of the one-shot achievability proved in Theorem
1
and (
3.9
).
The positivity follows from the monotone non-decreasing map
\alpha\mapsto I_{\alpha}^{*}

[
28
, Proposition 4]
and (
2.9
).
∎

3.2.
Random Constant Composition Codebook

Our Theorem
1
also applies to achievability using random constant composition codebook as well. Let
\rho_{XB}=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}p_{X}(x)|x\rangle\langle x|\otimes\rho_{B}^{x}
be a c-q state. Fix
n
such that
np_{X}(x)\in 0\cup\mathds{N}
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
.
We define the type class of length-
n
sequences under
p_{X}
as

(3.10)

\displaystyle T_{p}^{n}:=\set{x^{n}\in\mathcal{X}^{n}:P_{x^{n}}=p_{X}},

where the empirical distribution of sequence
x^{n}\in\mathcal{X}^{n}
is

(3.11)

\displaystyle P_{{x}^{n}}(x):=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{1}_{\{x=x_{i}\}},\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{X}.

Then, we define a uniform distribution on the type class as:

(3.12)

\displaystyle\breve{p}_{X^{n}}(x^{n}):=\frac{1}{\left|T_{p}^{n}\right|}\mathbf{1}_{x^{n}\in T_{p}^{n}},\quad x^{n}\in\mathcal{X}^{n}.

A
random constant composition codebook

(3.13)

\displaystyle\breve{\mathcal{C}}^{n}:=\{x^{n}(m)\}_{m=1}^{M},\quad x^{n}(m)\sim\breve{p}_{X^{n}}(x^{n})

consists of
M
codewords, where each codeword
x^{n}(m)
is independently drawn according to distribution
\breve{p}_{X^{n}}
. For a c-q channel
x\mapsto\rho_{B}^{x}
(induced by the c-q state
\rho_{XB}
), we define the c-q state generated from
\breve{p}_{X^{n}}
as

(3.14)

\displaystyle\breve{\rho}_{X^{n}B^{n}}:=\sum_{x^{n}\in\mathcal{X}^{n}}\breve{p}_{X^{n}}(x^{n})|x^{n}\rangle\langle x^{n}|\otimes\rho_{B^{n}}^{x^{n}},

and its marginal state

\breve{\rho}_{B^{n}}:=\sum_{x^{n}\in\mathcal{X}^{n}}\breve{p}_{X^{n}}(x^{n})\rho_{B^{n}}^{x^{n}}\hskip 2.84544pt.

The induced output state via the random constant composition codebook
\breve{\mathcal{C}}^{n}
is then

(3.15)

\displaystyle{\rho}_{B^{n}}^{\breve{\mathcal{C}}^{n}}:=\frac{1}{|\breve{\mathcal{C}}^{n}|}\sum_{x^{n}\in\breve{\mathcal{C}}^{n}}\rho_{B^{n}}^{x^{n}}.

Theorem 4

(
n
-shot achievability using random constant composition codebook)
.

For any
n\in\mathds{N}
, consider a c-q state
\rho_{XB}=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}p_{X}(x)|x\rangle\langle x|\otimes\rho_{B}^{x}
, where
np_{X}(x)\in 0\cup\mathds{N}
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
, and let
R:=\frac{1}{n}\log|\breve{\mathcal{C}}^{n}|
for a random constant composition codebook given in (
3.13
).
The trace distance between the induced state
{\rho}_{B^{n}}^{\breve{\mathcal{C}}^{n}}
and the true marginal state
\breve{\rho}_{B^{n}}
is upper bounded by

(3.16)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathds{E}_{\breve{\mathcal{C}}^{n}}\left\|{\rho}_{B^{n}}^{\breve{\mathcal{C}}^{n}}-\breve{\rho}_{B^{n}}\right\|_{1}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-n\sup_{\alpha\in(1,2)}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\left(\breve{I}_{\alpha}^{*}\left(X{\,:\,}B\right)_{\rho}-R\right)}.

Here, the order-
\alpha
sandwiched Augustin information
\breve{I}_{\alpha}^{*}(X{\,:\,}B)_{\rho}
is defined in (
2.6
). Moreover, the exponent
\sup_{\alpha\in(1,2)}\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}(\breve{I}_{\alpha}^{*}(X{\,:\,}B)_{\rho}-R)
is positive if and only if
R>I(X{\,:\,}B)_{\rho}
.

Remark 3.1
.

Jensen’s inequality together with the concavity of logarithmic function show that

(3.17)

\displaystyle\tfrac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}{I}_{\alpha}^{*}(X{\,:\,}B)_{\rho}\leq\tfrac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}{I}_{\alpha}^{*}(X{\,:\,}B)_{\rho},\quad\forall\alpha>1.

Hence, the expected value of the trace distance decays faster when using random constant composition codebook compared to that of using random i.i.d. codebook.

Proof.

The idea is similar to Theorem
1
.
We write
\mathcal{H}\equiv\mathcal{H}_{B}
throughout the proof. For
x^{n}=x_{1}\cdots x_{n}\in\mathcal{X}^{n}
,
we write the output state as
\rho_{x^{n}}:=\rho_{x_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\rho_{x_{n}}\in\mathcal{B(H)}^{\otimes n}
.
Let
x^{n}:\Omega\to\mathcal{X}^{n}
be a random codeword with respect to the uniform distribution on type class
\breve{p}_{X^{n}}
.
We introduce the classical-quantum state as

\breve{\rho}_{\Omega B^{n}}=\sum_{x^{n}\in T_{p}^{n}}1_{A_{x^{n}}}\otimes\rho_{x^{n}}\in L_{\infty}\left(\Omega,\mathcal{B(H)}^{\otimes n}\right)\hskip 2.84544pt,

where
1_{A_{x^{n}}}
is the characteristic function on the mutually disjoint set
A_{x^{n}}
such that

\Pr(A_{x^{n}})=\frac{1}{|T_{p}^{n}|}
. It is clear that
\mathds{E}_{\Omega}\left[\breve{\rho}_{\Omega B^{n}}\right]=\breve{\rho}_{B^{n}}
.
Take
1<{\alpha}<2
and
\frac{1}{{\alpha}}+\frac{1}{{\alpha}^{\prime}}=1
.
Let
n_{x}:=np_{X}(x)
for some integer
n_{x}\in\mathds{N}
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
.
The Augustine information
\breve{I}_{\alpha}^{*}\left(X{\,:\,}B\right)_{\rho}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\inf_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})}\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\frac{n_{x}}{n}\log\left\|\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2{\alpha}^{\prime}}}\rho_{x}\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2{\alpha}^{\prime}}}\right\|_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\overset{}{=}\inf_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})}\frac{1}{n}\cdot\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\log\left\|(\sigma^{\otimes n})^{-\frac{1}{2{\alpha}^{\prime}}}\rho_{x^{n}}(\sigma^{\otimes n})^{-\frac{1}{2{\alpha}^{\prime}}}\right\|_{\alpha}^{n_{x}}

for any
x_{n}\in T_{p}^{n}
. Then we further have

\displaystyle\breve{I}_{\alpha}^{*}\left(X{\,:\,}B\right)_{\rho}

\displaystyle=\inf_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})}\frac{1}{n}\cdot\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\log\parallel\sigma_{\Omega B^{n}}^{-\frac{1}{2{\alpha}^{\prime}}}\breve{\rho}_{\Omega B^{n}}\sigma_{\Omega B^{n}}^{-\frac{1}{2{\alpha}^{\prime}}}\parallel_{L_{\alpha}(\Omega,S_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}))}.

where
\sigma_{\Omega B^{n}}=1_{\Omega}\otimes\sigma_{B}^{\otimes n}
is interpreted as a constant function with value
\sigma_{B}^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal{B(H)}^{\otimes n}\cong\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n})
. In other words,

\displaystyle\e^{n\cdot\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\breve{I}_{\alpha}^{*}\left(X{\,:\,}B\right)_{\rho}}=\inf_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})}\parallel\sigma_{\Omega B_{n}}^{-\frac{1}{2{\alpha}^{\prime}}}\breve{\rho}_{\Omega B^{n}}\sigma_{\Omega B_{n}}^{-\frac{1}{2{\alpha}^{\prime}}}\parallel_{L_{\alpha}(\Omega,S_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}))}.

Denote by
M:=|\breve{\mathcal{C}}^{n}|
.
Now using the construction given in the Lemma
2
, we have

\displaystyle\mathds{E}_{\breve{\mathcal{C}}^{n}}\left\|{\rho}_{B^{n}}^{\breve{\mathcal{C}}^{n}}-\breve{\rho}_{B^{n}}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\parallel\Theta(\rho_{\Omega B^{n}})\parallel_{L_{1}(\Omega^{M},S_{1}(\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}))}.

Note that for any
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})
,

\displaystyle\parallel\Theta(\rho_{\Omega B^{n}})\parallel_{L_{1}(\Omega^{M},S_{1}(\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}))}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{(a)}}{\leq}\;\parallel\sigma_{\Omega B^{n}}^{-\frac{1}{2{\alpha}^{\prime}}}\breve{\rho}_{\Omega B^{n}}\sigma_{\Omega B^{n}}^{-\frac{1}{2{\alpha}^{\prime}}}\parallel_{L_{\alpha}(\Omega^{M},S_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}))}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{(b)}}{=}\;\parallel\Theta(\sigma_{\Omega B^{n}}^{-\frac{1}{2{\alpha}^{\prime}}}\breve{\rho}_{\Omega B^{n}}\sigma_{\Omega B^{n}}^{-\frac{1}{2{\alpha}^{\prime}}})\parallel_{L_{\alpha}(\Omega^{M},S_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}))}

\displaystyle\leq\;\parallel\Theta:L_{\alpha}(\Omega,S_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}))\to L_{\alpha}(\Omega^{M},S_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}))\parallel\cdot\parallel\sigma_{\Omega B^{n}}^{-\frac{1}{2{\alpha}^{\prime}}}\breve{\rho}_{\Omega B^{n}}\sigma_{\Omega B^{n}}^{-\frac{1}{2{\alpha}^{\prime}}}\parallel_{L_{\alpha}(\Omega,S_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}))}.

Here, (b) follows from the fact
\Theta=\Theta\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{B(H)}}
is identity on the operator part, and (a) uses Hölder inequality with

\displaystyle\parallel\sigma_{B^{n}}^{\frac{1}{2{\alpha}^{\prime}}}\parallel_{L_{2{\alpha}}(\Omega^{M},S_{2{\alpha}}(\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}))}

\displaystyle=\Big(\int_{\Omega^{M}}\parallel(\sigma^{\frac{1}{2{\alpha}^{\prime}}})^{\otimes n}\parallel_{S_{2{\alpha}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n})}^{2{\alpha}^{\prime}}d\mu^{M}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2{\alpha}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=1\hskip 2.84544pt.

Then the assertion follows from Lemma
2
and taking infimum over
\sigma\in\mathcal{S(H)}
, i.e.

\displaystyle\mathds{E}_{\breve{\mathcal{C}}^{n}}\left\|{\rho}_{B^{n}}^{\breve{\mathcal{C}}^{n}}-\breve{\rho}_{B^{n}}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}M^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}\inf_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})}\parallel\sigma_{\Omega B_{n}}^{-\frac{1}{2{\alpha}^{\prime}}}\breve{\rho}_{\Omega B^{n}}\sigma_{\Omega B_{n}}^{-\frac{1}{2{\alpha}^{\prime}}}\parallel_{L_{\alpha}(\Omega,S_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}))}

\displaystyle=2^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}M^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}\e^{n\cdot\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\breve{I}_{\alpha}^{*}\left(X{\,:\,}B\right)_{\rho}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\e^{-n\cdot\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}(\breve{I}_{\alpha}^{*}\left(X{\,:\,}B\right)_{\rho}-R)},

where
R=\frac{1}{n}\log|{\mathcal{C}}^{n}|
. The assertion of exponential decay follows from taking infimum of the right-hand side for
\alpha\in(1,2)
.
The positivity again follows from the monotone non-decreasing of the map
\alpha\mapsto\breve{I}_{\alpha}^{*}

[
28
, Proposition 5]
and (
2.9
).
∎

4.
Strong Converse

In the previous Section
3
, we have presented that as long as the rate of the random codebook size is
above
the quantum mutual information
I(X{\,:\,}B)_{\rho}
, the trace distances using both the random i.i.d. codebook and the random constant composition codebook exponentially decay.
In this section, we show that, on the other hand, when the rate of the random codebook size is
below
the quantum mutual information
I(X{\,:\,}B)_{\rho}
, the trace distances using both the two random codebooks converge to
1
exponentially fast, reflecting the
exponential strong converse
.

Using the notation as in Section
3
, we first prove the following one-shot strong converse bound.

Theorem 5

(A one-shot strong converse)
.

The trace distance between the induced state
{\rho_{B}^{\mathcal{C}}}
and the true state
\rho_{B}
is lower bounded by,

(4.1)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathds{E}_{\mathcal{C}}\left\|\rho_{B}^{\mathcal{C}}-\rho_{B}\right\|_{1}\geq 1-4\,\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left(I_{2-\nicefrac{{1}}{{\alpha}}}^{\downarrow}(X{\,:\,}B)_{\rho}-\log M\right)},\quad\forall\alpha\in(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}},1).

Here,
I^{\downarrow}_{2-\nicefrac{{1}}{{\alpha}}}(X{\,:\,}B)_{\rho}
is defined in (
2.7
).

In Sections
4.1
and
4.2
later, we will apply the one-shot strong converse, Theorem
5
, to the random i.i.d. codebook and the random constant composition codebook.

Proof.

Using the Holevo–Helstrom theorem
[
29
,
30
]
, i.e.

(4.2)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\rho-\sigma\|_{1}=\sup_{0\leq\Pi\leq\mathds{1}}\Tr\left[(\rho-\sigma)\Pi\right],

we have

(4.3)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho_{B}^{\mathcal{C}}-\rho_{B}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\sup_{0\leq\Pi_{B}\leq\mathds{1}_{B}}\Tr\left[\left(\rho_{B}^{\mathcal{C}}-\rho_{B}\right)\Pi_{B}\right]

(4.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\geq\Tr\left[\rho_{B}^{\mathcal{C}}\left(\rho_{B}^{\mathcal{C}}+\rho_{B}\right)^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\rho_{B}^{\mathcal{C}}\left(\rho_{B}^{\mathcal{C}}+\rho_{B}\right)^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right]\\
&\quad-\Tr\left[\rho_{B}\left(\rho_{B}^{\mathcal{C}}+\rho_{B}\right)^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\rho_{B}^{\mathcal{C}}\left(\rho_{B}^{\mathcal{C}}+\rho_{B}\right)^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right].\end{split}

We will then lower bound the two terms in (
4.4
) subsequently.

Recalling
{\rho}_{B}^{\mathcal{C}}=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{C}}\rho_{B}^{x}
, we rewrite the first term in (
4.4
) as follows:

\displaystyle\Tr\left[\rho_{B}^{\mathcal{C}}\left(\rho_{B}^{\mathcal{C}}+\rho_{B}\right)^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\rho_{B}^{\mathcal{C}}\left(\rho_{B}^{\mathcal{C}}+\rho_{B}\right)^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right]

(4.5)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{C}}\Tr\left[\rho_{B}^{x}\left(\rho_{B}^{\mathcal{C}}+\rho_{B}\right)^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\rho_{B}^{\mathcal{C}}\left(\rho_{B}^{\mathcal{C}}+\rho_{B}\right)^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right]

(4.6)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{C}}\Tr\left[\rho_{B}^{x}\left(\sum_{\bar{x}\in\mathcal{C}}\rho_{B}^{\bar{x}}+M\rho_{B}\right)^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\left(\rho_{B}^{x}+\sum_{\bar{x}\in\mathcal{C},\bar{x}\neq x}\rho_{B}^{\bar{x}}\right)\left(\sum_{\bar{x}\in\mathcal{C}}\rho_{B}^{\bar{x}}+M\rho_{B}\right)^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right]

(4.7)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{M}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{C}}\Tr\left[\rho_{B}^{x}\left(\sum_{\bar{x}\in\mathcal{C}}\rho_{B}^{\bar{x}}+M\rho_{B}\right)^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\rho_{B}^{x}\left(\sum_{\bar{x}\in\mathcal{C}}\rho_{B}^{\bar{x}}+M\rho_{B}\right)^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right]

(4.8)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{M}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{C}}\left(1-\Tr\left[(\rho_{B}^{x})^{1-s}\left(\sum_{\bar{x}\in\mathcal{C},\bar{x}\neq x}\rho_{B}^{\bar{x}}+M\rho_{B}\right)^{s}\right]\right),\quad\forall s\in(0,1),

where we have applied Lemma
6
given below with
K=\rho_{B}^{x}
and
L=\sum_{{\bar{x}\in\mathcal{C},\bar{x}\neq x}}\rho_{B}^{\bar{x}}+M\rho_{B}
to the last inequality.

Recalling the fact that each codeword (e.g.
x,\bar{x}\in\mathcal{C}
) is drawn independently, and using the operator concavity of
(\,\cdot\,)^{s}
for
s\in(0,1)
, we average the above inequality over the random codebook
\mathcal{C}
to arrive at

\displaystyle\mathds{E}_{\mathcal{C}}\Tr\left[\rho_{B}^{\mathcal{C}}\left(\rho_{B}^{\mathcal{C}}+\rho_{B}\right)^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\rho_{B}^{\mathcal{C}}\left(\rho_{B}^{\mathcal{C}}+\rho_{B}\right)^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right]

(4.9)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{M}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{C}}\left(1-\mathds{E}_{x\sim p_{X}}\Tr\left[(\rho_{B}^{x})^{1-s}\mathds{E}_{\bar{x}\sim p_{X}}\left(\sum_{{\bar{x}\in\mathcal{C},\bar{x}\neq x}}\rho_{B}^{\bar{x}}+M\rho_{B}\right)^{s}\right]\right)

(4.10)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{M}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{C}}\left(1-\mathds{E}_{x\sim p_{X}}\Tr\left[(\rho_{B}^{x})^{1-s}\left(\sum_{{\bar{x}\in\mathcal{C},\bar{x}\neq x}}\mathds{E}_{\bar{x}\sim p_{X}}\rho_{B}^{x^{\prime}}+M\rho_{B}\right)^{s}\right]\right)

(4.11)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{C}}\left(1-\mathds{E}_{x\sim p_{X}}\Tr\left[(\rho_{B}^{x})^{1-s}\left(\sum_{{x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C},\bar{x}\neq x}}(M-1)\rho_{B}+M\rho_{B}\right)^{s}\right]\right)

(4.12)

\displaystyle\geq 1-(2M)^{s}\mathds{E}_{x\sim p_{X}}\Tr\left[(\rho_{B}^{x})^{1-s}\rho_{B}^{s}\right],\quad\forall s\in(0,1).

Next, we lower bound the second term in (
4.4
). Using the cyclic property of trace, we have

\displaystyle-\Tr\left[\rho_{B}\left(\rho_{B}^{\mathcal{C}}+\rho_{B}\right)^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\rho_{B}^{\mathcal{C}}\left(\rho_{B}^{\mathcal{C}}+\rho_{B}\right)^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right]

(4.13)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{M}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{C}}\Tr\left[\rho_{B}^{x}\left(\rho_{B}^{\mathcal{C}}+\rho_{B}\right)^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\rho_{B}\left(\rho_{B}^{\mathcal{C}}+\rho_{B}\right)^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right]

(4.14)

\displaystyle-\mathds{E}_{\mathcal{C}}\Tr\left[\rho_{B}\left(\rho_{B}^{\mathcal{C}}+\rho_{B}\right)^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\rho_{B}^{\mathcal{C}}\left(\rho_{B}^{\mathcal{C}}+\rho_{B}\right)^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right]

(Exponential strong converse using random i.i.d. codebook)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathds{E}_{\mathcal{C}^{n}}\left\|\rho_{B^{n}}^{\mathcal{C}^{n}}-\rho_{B}^{\otimes n}\right\|_{1}

is positive if and only if

Proof.

The estimate follows from

5.
Moderate Deviation Analysis

6.
Conclusions

In this work, we establish achievability and strong converse for quantum soft covering using the random i.i.d. codebook and the random constant composition codebook with the codebook size being fixed. In both settings, we obtain exponential convergence of the trace distance to

,
28
,
41
,
42
,

,

,

,

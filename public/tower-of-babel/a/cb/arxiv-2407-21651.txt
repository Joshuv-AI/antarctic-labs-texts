On Minimal Predictable Intensity of Point Processes
Haoming Wang

Abstract
. An adapted, right‐continuous, non-decreasing, integer‐valued process with unit jumps and starting at zero has a minimal predictable intensity if and only if it is a standard Poisson process under an absolutely continuous transformation of measures.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 60A05; Secondary 60G05, 60G55

1.
Introduction

This paper studies a problem arising in the change of time on semimartingales by transformation of measures. For the continuous semimartingale,
Monroe (1978)
proved that any continuous local martingale is a time-changed Brownian motion, although possibly under an enlargement of the filtration. By the Zorn lemma, a minimal filtration always exists in such a problem. For the Brownian motion, this problem is resolved by many authors, such as
Kakutani (1948)
,
Girsanov (1960)
,
Clark (1970)
,
Novikov (1972)
, and
Kazamaki (1994)
. In parallel with the Brownian motion, the Poisson process is another prototype in many stochastic applications. In fact, the point process, which is an adapted, right‐continuous, non-decreasing, integer‐valued stochastic process with unit jumps and starting at zero, can be represented as a time-changed Poisson process. However, an enlargement of the filtration is still required.
Brémaud (1972)
once asked when the minimal filtration is natural and conjectured that if and only if it is a standard Poisson process under an absolutely continuous transformation of measures. In this article, we answer this question in the affirmative sense.

Although on many occasions, an enlargement of filtration helps us represent point processes, it brings some troubles in the analysis of such processes, e.g., including more null sets. Converse to the filtration expansion problem, like expositions in
Protter (2005)
,
Jeulin (2006)
, and
Tella and Jeanblanc (2021)
, the minimal filtration was first studied by
Jacod (1975)
, and subsequently by
Brémaud (1981)
,
Daley and Vere-Jones (2003, 2007)
and
Karr (2017)
. Their methods are all based on the hazard function of conditional expectation. For example, Theorem 14.1 in
Daley and Vere-Jones (2003, 2007)
and Theorem 2.31 in
Karr (2017)
. In this method, it is unavoidable to assume the null complete assumption in the sense of
Dellacherie (1972)
, i.e., all terminal null sets are contained in the initial filter. However, the explicit construction based on null completeness limits the practicality of the minimal filtration. In our paper, we adopt a function-analytic approach to establish the existence of a minimal predictable intensity using the Hahn-Banach theorem, which only involves the property of closed subspaces of semimartingales. This method helps us get rid of unnecessary null completeness, so that we can obtain a natural restriction of filtration.

Further applications to the Cox and Hawkes processes implemented the main theorem. Based on our main Theorems and the Lebesgue decomposition theorem, we establish a canonical decomposition Theorem
3
for the Cox process. With application to the Hawkes process, Theorem
4
fills existing gaps between Poisson processes and self-exciting processes.

The present work studies a class of stochastic processes defined in the real half line

In fact, from a theorem of

The definition of (

\boldsymbol{P}(\boldsymbol{\varOmega}^{\prime})>0

Coming to a simple point process
\underline{\underline{N}}=\{N(\omega,t),\boldsymbol{F},\boldsymbol{P}\}

coincides the natural filtration generated by all

(S2)

,

\boldsymbol{G}\leq\boldsymbol{F}\Leftrightarrow\mathscr{G}_{t}\in\boldsymbol{G}\subseteq\mathscr{F}_{t}\in\boldsymbol{F},

Brémaud (1972)
asked a question: Should the minimal filtration

if there exists a probability measure

relative to which the point process

Recall that a simple point process
\underline{\underline{N}}
is not necessarily orderly, by which we mean that for any stopping time
\tau(\omega)\geq 0

\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{1}{h}\boldsymbol{P}(N(\cdot,\tau(\cdot)+h)-N(\cdot,\tau(\cdot))>1\mid\mathscr{F}_{\tau})(\omega)=0,

(2.2)

while orderliness implies simplicity (e.g., the mixed Poisson process of Exercise 3.3.2 in

). Here,
\mathscr{F}_{\tau}
denotes the stopped
\sigma
-subalgebra of
\mathscr{F}

\mathscr{F}_{\tau}=\{A:A\cap\{\omega:\tau(\omega)\leq t\}\in\mathscr{F}_{t},\text{ for all }t\geq 0\},

The intensity
\lambda(\omega,t)
of an orderly point process
\underline{\underline{N}}
is a non-negative process satisfying for any predictable stopping time

\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{1}{h}\boldsymbol{P}(N(\cdot,\tau(\cdot)+h)-N(\cdot,\tau(\cdot))=1\mid\mathscr{F}_{\tau})(\omega)=\lambda(\omega,\tau(\omega)).

Here, we use a different definition from

) for fixed

\boldsymbol{P}(\lambda(\cdot,t)\neq\lambda^{\prime}(\cdot,t)\text{ for some }t\geq 0)=0.

This is guaranteed by the predictable section theorem (Theorem 85 of Chapter IV in

), while in Brémaud’s definition, only version uniqueness is assumed, i.e.,

\boldsymbol{P}(\lambda(\cdot,t)\neq\lambda^{\prime}(\cdot,t))=0,\text{ for all }t\geq 0.

5.
Applications to the Cox and Hawkes Processes

The Cox process, also known as the doubly stochastic Poisson process, is usually introduced as a random measure when some random measure is given. In fact,
Prohorov (1956)
furnished a complete and separable metric on such space of measures, which permits conditioning. Here we adopt a different definition. Suppose
\underline{\underline{N}}=\{N(\omega,t),\boldsymbol{F},\boldsymbol{P}\}
satisfies (P0)-(P5). A Cox process
\underline{\underline{N}}
characterized is by its compensator
A(\omega,t)
in the following way

(C0)

(adapted)
N(\omega,t),A(\omega,t)
is adapted to
\boldsymbol{F}
;

(C1)

(initial adapted)
\mathscr{F}_{0}
contains the
\sigma
-algebra generated by all
A(\cdot,t),t\geq 0
;

(C2)

(conditional Poisson law) the random variable
N(\cdot,t)-N(\cdot,s),0\leq s<t
has the conditional Poisson law for
n=0,1,2,\dots
,

\boldsymbol{P}(N(\cdot,t)-N(\cdot,s)=n|\mathscr{F}_{s})(\omega)=\frac{(A(\omega,t)-A(\omega,s))^{n}e^{A(\omega,s)-A(\omega,t)}}{n!};

(C3)

(conditional independent increment) the random variables
N(\cdot,t)-N(\cdot,s)
and
N(\cdot,u)-N(\cdot,v)
are conditionally independent given
\mathscr{F}_{0}
for
0\leq s<t\leq u<v
.

From Theorem
1
, Theorem
2
and the Lebesgue decomposition theorem, we have

Theorem 3
.

Any Cox process decomposes into three parts

N+A+M

is a Cox process admitting an intensity,

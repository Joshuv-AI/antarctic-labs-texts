Abstract
A connected graph G G is said to be k k -connected if it has more than k k vertices and remains connected whenever fewer than k k vertices are deleted. In this paper, for a connected graph G G with sufficiently large order, we present a tight sufficient condition for G G with fixed minimum degree to be k k -connected based on the Q Q -index. Our result can be viewed as a spectral counterpart of the corresponding Dirac type condition.

-index; Minimum degree;

Introduction

All graphs considered in this paper are simple connected and undirected. The notations we used are standard. Let
G

V(G)
and edge set

such that
|V(G)|=n

. Let

be the degree of a vertex

in
G
, and the minimum degree be
\delta(G)=\delta
. For two vertex-disjoint graphs
G
and
H,
we denote
G\cup H
the disjoint union of
G
and
H,

G\vee H
the join of
G
and
H,
which is a graph obtained by adding all possible edges between
G
and
H.
Throughout this paper, we use the symbol

to denote the vertices
i
and
j
are adjacent, and
i\nsim j
otherwise.

A graph
G
is said to be
k
-connected
if it has more than
k
vertices and remains connected whenever fewer than
k
vertices are deleted.
In the meantime, a vertex-cut
X
of

is a subset of
V(G)
such that
G-X
is disconnected. The vertex connectivity
\kappa
is the minimum vertex-cut
X.
We say

is
k
-connected when
\kappa\geq k,

\kappa=0
if

is either trivial or disconnected. In other words,
G
is
k
-connected if the minimum vertex-cut

satisfies
|X|\geq k.

The
adjacency matrix
of

with
a_{ij}=1
if

and

are adjacent, and
a_{ij}=0
otherwise. The largest eigenvalue of

of

of

. The
signless Laplacian matrix

of

is defined as

The largest eigenvalue of
Q(G)
, denoted by
q(G)
, is called the
Q
-
index
(or the signless Laplacian spectral radius) of

.

\displaystyle\left\{G\subseteq{L}_{k}(n)-E^{\prime},\mbox{where $E^{\prime}\subset E_{1}({L}_{k}(n))$ with $|E^{\prime}|\leq\lfloor\frac{k^{2}}{4}\rfloor$}\right\}.

Preliminaries

In this section, we present some basic notations and lemmas.

Let
x=(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n})^{T}\neq 0,
by Rayleigh’s principle, we have

q(G)=\max_{x}\frac{\langle Q(G)x,x\rangle}{\langle x,x\rangle}=\max_{x}\frac{x^{T}Q(G)x}{x^{T}x}.

By the definition of
Q(G),
we have

\langle Q(G)x,x\rangle=\sum_{i\sim j}(x_{i}+x_{j})^{2}.

If
z
is the corresponding unit positive eigenvector (usually called Perron vector) of
q(G),
then

Q(G)z=q(G)z.

According to the Perron-Frobenius theorem,
we have
x_{i}>0
for each

i\in V(G)
if
G
is connected.
Taking the
i
-th entry of both sides and rearranging terms, we have

\displaystyle(q(G)-d(i))z_{i}=\sum_{i\sim j}z_{j}.

(1)

Let
N(i)
denote the set of neighbours of
i,

N[i]=N(i)\cup\{i\}
. From the above, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1

[
19
]

For any
i,j\in V(G),
we have

\displaystyle(q(G)-d(i))(z_{i}-z_{j})=(d(i)-d(j))z_{j}+\sum_{k\in N(i)\setminus N(j)}z_{k}-\sum_{l\in N(j)\setminus N(i)}z_{l}.

(2)

Lemma 2.2

[
10
]

Let
G
be a graph of order
n.
Then

q(G)\leq\frac{2m}{n-1}+n-2.

Using the ideas in
[
14
]
, we obtain

Lemma 2.3

Let
G
be a connected graph of order
n\geq 2\delta-k+5,
size
m,
minimum degree
\delta(G)=\delta\geq k\geq 2.
If

m>\frac{1}{2}n(n-1)-(\delta-k+3)(n-\delta-2),

then
G
is
k
-connected unless
G
is a subgraph of
{A}(n,k,\delta).

Proof.

Suppose on the contrary that
G
is not
k
-connected. Let
X
be a minimum vertex-cut with
1\leq|X|\leq k-1.
Assume that
C_{1},C_{2},...,C_{t}

(t>1)
are the components of
G-X,
where
|C_{1}|\leq|C_{2}|\leq...\leq|C_{t}|.

Clearly, for
1\leq i\leq t,
each vertex in
C_{i}
is adjacent to at most
|C_{i}|-1
vertices of
C_{i}
and
|X|
vertices of
X.
Thus

\delta|C_{i}|\leq\sum_{x\in C_{i}}d(x)\leq(|C_{i}|-1+|X|)|C_{i}|,

hence
|C_{i}|\geq\delta-|X|+1,
and therefore
\delta-|X|+1\leq|C_{i}|\leq n-|X|-(\delta-|X|+1),
which implies

\displaystyle\delta-|X|+1\leq|C_{i}|\leq n-\delta-1.

Let
S=\cup_{i=2}^{t}C_{i}.
Then from above,
\delta-|X|+1\leq|S|\leq n-\delta-1.
Since
G-X
is disconnected, there are no edges between
C_{1}
and
S
in
G,
we obtain

m\leq\frac{1}{2}n(n-1)-|C_{1}||S|.

In order to prove that
G
is a subgraph of
{A}(n,k,\delta),
it suffices to show that
|C_{1}|=\delta-k+2.

If
|C_{1}|\geq\delta-k+3,
as
|C_{1}|\leq|C_{2}|\leq...\leq|C_{t}|
and
S=\cup_{i=2}^{t}C_{i},
we have
|C_{1}|\leq\frac{n-|X|}{2}.
Therefore for
\delta-k+3\leq|C_{1}|\leq\frac{n-|X|}{2},
we have
|C_{1}||S|=|C_{1}|(n-|X|-|C_{1}|)\geq(\delta-k+3)(n-|X|-(\delta-k+3))
, the equality is attained when
|C_{1}|=\delta-k+3.
Since
|X|\leq k-1,

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}n(n-1)-|C_{1}||S|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}n(n-1)-(\delta-k+3)(n-|X|-(\delta-k+3))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}n(n-1)-(\delta-k+3)(n-\delta-2).

From the assumption,
we get a contradiction. Thus
|C_{1}|\leq\delta-k+2.

Combining this with
|C_{1}|\geq\delta-|X|+1\geq\delta-k+2,
we have
|C_{1}|=\delta-k+2.

Hence, as the minimum degree of
G
is
\delta
, we have
|X|=k-1

and
d_{G}(i)=\delta
for each
i\in C_{1},
therefore each vertex of
C_{1}
is adjacent to each vertex of
X.
We obtain the result.

3.6
.

Lemma 3.6

Assume
G\in\mathcal{A}_{2}(n,k,\delta)
as defined above. We have

\max\limits_{i\in V(G)}x_{i}-\min\limits_{j\in Y\cup Z}x_{j}\leq\frac{(\delta-k+2)(k+3)+4}{2(q(G)-n+1)}.

Proof.

We distinguish the proof into two cases.

Case 1
:

Y_{1}=\emptyset.
Notice that
\max\limits_{i\in V(G)}x_{i}
is attained at the vertices in
Z_{1}
. For each
i\in Z_{1},

d(i)=n-\delta+k-3,
hence the vertex
i
is adjacent to all other vertices in
Y\cup Z.

Subcase 1.1
:
If
j\in Z_{2},
we have
N(i)\setminus N[j]=\{k:k\in Y\cup Z
and
k\nsim j\}.
Thus we have

d(i)-d(j)\leq\left\lfloor\frac{(\delta-k+2)(k-1)}{4}\right\rfloor+1
and
|N(i)\setminus N[j]|\leq\left\lfloor\frac{(\delta-k+2)(k-1)}{4}\right\rfloor+1.

Note that
N(j)\setminus\{i\}\subset N(i)\setminus\{j\},

applying equation (
3
), we obtain

\displaystyle(q(G)-d(i)+1)(x_{i}-x_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(d(i)-d(j))x_{j}+\sum_{k\in N(i)\setminus N[j]}x_{k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\lfloor\frac{(\delta-k+2)(k-1)}{2}\right\rfloor+2.

Since
i\in Z_{1},\delta\geq k,
we have

x_{i}-x_{j}\leq\frac{\frac{(\delta-k+2)(k-1)}{2}+2}{q(G)-(n-\delta+k-3)+1}=\frac{(\delta-k+2)(k-1)+4}{2(q(G)-n+\delta-k+4)}<\frac{(\delta-k+2)(k+3)+4}{2(q(G)-n+1)}.

Subcase 1.2
:
If
j\in Y_{2},
we have
N(i)\setminus(N[j])=\{k:k\in Y\cup Z
and
k\nsim j\},
and
N(j)\setminus N[i]=X.
Thus

|N(i)\setminus N[j]|\leq\left\lfloor\frac{(\delta-k+2)(k-1)}{4}\right\rfloor+1.
Meanwhile, note that
|d(i)-d(j)|\leq\left\lfloor\frac{(\delta-k+2)(k-1)}{4}\right\rfloor+\delta-k+3.

Similarly, we obtain

\displaystyle(q(G)-d(i)+1)(x_{i}-x_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(d(i)-d(j))x_{j}+\sum_{k\in N(i)\setminus N[j]}x_{k}-\sum_{l\in X}x_{l}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(d(i)-d(j))x_{j}+\sum_{k\in N(i)\setminus N[j]}x_{k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(\delta-k+2)(k-1)}{2}+\delta-k+2+2

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(\delta-k+2)(k+1)+4}{2}.

Since
i\in Z_{1},\delta\geq k,
we easily have

\displaystyle x_{i}-x_{j}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{(\delta-k+2)(k+1)}{2}+2}{q(G)-(n-\delta+k-3)+1}=\frac{(\delta-k+2)(k+1)+4}{2(q(G)-n+\delta-k+4)}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{(\delta-k+2)(k+3)+4}{2(q(G)-n+1)}.

Case 2
:

Y_{1}\neq\emptyset.
Notice that
\max\limits_{i\in V(G)}x_{i}
is attained at the vertices in
Y_{1}
. When
i\in Y_{1},

d(i)=n-1,
hence the vertex
i
is adjacent to all other vertices in
V(G).

Subcase 2.1
:
If
j\in Y_{2},
we have
N(i)\setminus N[j]=\{k:k\in Y\cup Z\mbox{ and }k\nsim j\}.
Thus we have

d(i)-d(j)\leq\left\lfloor\frac{(\delta-k+2)(k-1)}{4}\right\rfloor+1
and
|N(i)\setminus N[j]|\leq\left\lfloor\frac{(\delta-k+2)(k-1)}{4}\right\rfloor+1.

Note that
N(j)\setminus\{i\}\subset N(i)\setminus\{j\},

applying equation (
3
), we obtain

\displaystyle(q(G)-d(i)+1)(x_{i}-x_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(d(i)-d(j))x_{j}+\sum_{k\in N(i)\setminus N[j]}x_{k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\lfloor\frac{(\delta-k+2)(k-1)}{2}\right\rfloor+2.

Since
i\in Y_{1},\delta\geq k,
we easily have

\displaystyle x_{i}-x_{j}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{(\delta-k+2)(k-1)}{2}+2}{q(G)-(n-1)+1}=\frac{(\delta-k+2)(k-1)+4}{2(q(G)-n+2)}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{(\delta-k+2)(k+3)+4}{2(q(G)-n+1)}.

Subcase 2.2
:
If
j\in Z_{1},
we have
N(i)\setminus N[j]=X-\{j\},
and
N(j)\setminus N[i]=\emptyset.
Thus

|N(i)\setminus N[j]|=(n-1)-(n-\delta+k-3+1)=\delta-k+1.
Note that
d(i)-d(j)=\delta-k+2
,
we similarly obtain

\displaystyle(q(G)-d(i)+1)(x_{i}-x_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(d(i)-d(j))x_{j}+\sum_{k\in N(i)\setminus N[j]}x_{k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\delta-k+2+\delta-k+1

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle 2(\delta-k+2).

Since
i\in Y_{1},\delta\geq k,
we have

\displaystyle x_{i}-x_{j}<\frac{2(\delta-k+2)}{q(G)-(n-1)+1}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{4(\delta-k+2)}{2(q(G)-n+1)}<\frac{(\delta-k+2)(k+3)+4}{2(q(G)-n+1)}.

Subcase 2.3
:
If
j\in Z_{2},
we have
N(i)\setminus N[j]=\{k:k\in X\cup Y\cup Z
and
k\nsim j\},
and
N(j)\setminus N[i]=\emptyset.
Thus we have

|N(i)\setminus N[j]|\leq\delta-k+2+\left\lfloor\frac{(\delta-k+2)(k-1)}{4}\right\rfloor+1.
Meanwhile, note that
d(i)-d(j)\leq\left\lfloor\frac{(\delta-k+2)(k-1)}{4}\right\rfloor+\delta-k+3.

Similarly, we obtain

\displaystyle(q(G)-d(i)+1)(x_{i}-x_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(d(i)-d(j))x_{j}+\sum_{k\in N(i)\setminus N[j]}x_{k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(\delta-k+2)(k-1)}{2}+2(\delta-k+2)+2

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(\delta-k+2)(k+3)+4}{2}.

Since
i\in Y_{1},\delta\geq k,
we have

\displaystyle x_{i}-x_{j}\leq\frac{\frac{(\delta-k+2)(k+3)}{2}+2}{q(G)-(n-1)+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(\delta-k+2)(k+3)+4}{2(q(G)-n+2)}<\frac{(\delta-k+2)(k+3)+4}{2(q(G)-n+1)}.

The proof is completed.

Now we can prove Lemma
3.7
, which is crucial for Theorem
1.5
.

Lemma 3.7

Let
G
be a connected graph of order
n\geq F(k,\delta)
and minimum degree
\delta\geq k\geq 3.

For each graph
G\in\mathcal{A}_{2}(n,k,\delta),

we have
q(G)<2(n-\delta+k-3).

Proof.

We assume
G\in\mathcal{A}_{2}(n,k,\delta)
such that
G
has the largest
Q
-index
q(G)
among
\mathcal{A}_{2}(n,k,\delta)
and
G[Y]
contains the largest number of edges.
Let
x
be the eigenvector corresponding to
q(G),
and
G^{\prime}[X]
be the complete graph
K_{\delta-k+2}
induced by
X.
Lemmas
3.3
and
3.6
imply that

\displaystyle\langle Q(G)x,x\rangle-\langle Q(\overline{K_{\delta-k+2}}+K_{n-\delta+k-2})x,x\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j\in E(G^{\prime}[X])}(x_{i}+x_{j})^{2}+\sum_{i\in X,j\in Y}(x_{i}+x_{j})^{2}-\sum_{{i,j}\in E^{\prime}}(x_{i}+x_{j})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(\delta-k+2)(\delta-k+1)}{2}\left(2\frac{k-1}{q(G)-(2\delta-k+1)}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+(k-1)(\delta-k+2)\left(1+\frac{k-1}{q(G)-(2\delta-k+1)}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle-4|E^{\prime}|\left(1-\frac{(\delta-k+2)(k+3)+4}{2(q(G)-n+1)}\right)^{2}.

As
|E^{\prime}|=\left\lfloor\frac{(\delta-k+2)(k-1)}{4}\right\rfloor+1\geq\frac{(\delta-k+2)(k-1)+1}{4},
we have

\displaystyle\langle Q(G)x,x\rangle-\langle Q(\overline{K_{\delta-k+2}}+K_{n-\delta+k-2})x,x\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(\delta-k+2)(\delta-k+1)}{2}\left(2\frac{k-1}{q(G)-(2\delta-k+1)}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+(k-1)(\delta-k+2)\left(1+\frac{k-1}{q(G)-(2\delta-k+1)}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle-\left((k-1)(\delta-k+2)+1\right)\left(1-\frac{(\delta-k+2)(k+3)+4}{2(q(G)-n+1)}\right)^{2}.

Since
n\geq F(k,\delta),
and
q(G)>2(n-\delta+k-3)-1
by Lemma
3.2
, we have

\langle Q(G)x,x\rangle-\langle Q(\overline{K_{\delta-k+2}}+K_{n-\delta+k-2})x,x\rangle<0.

According to the Rayleigh’s principle,

\frac{\langle Q(\overline{K_{\delta-k+2}}+K_{n-\delta+k-2})x,x\rangle}{\langle x,x\rangle}\leq q(\overline{K_{\delta-k+2}}+K_{n-\delta+k-2})=2(n-\delta+k-3).

Therefore, we have
q(G)=\frac{\langle Q(G)x,x\rangle}{\langle x,x\rangle}<2(n-\delta+k-3).

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.5
.

By Lemma
2.2
, we have

2(n-\delta+k-3)\leq q(G)\leq\frac{2m}{n-1}+n-2.

Therefore

the last inequality holds as

k

3.1
and

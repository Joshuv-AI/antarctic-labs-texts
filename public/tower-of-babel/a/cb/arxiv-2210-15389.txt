Stability and related zero viscosity limit of steady plane Poiseuille-Couette flows with no-slip boundary condition
Song Jiang Chunhui Zhou, [4mm] Department of Mathematics, Southeast University, Nanjing, 210096, China. Email: zhouchunhui@seu.edu.cn

Abstract
We prove the existence and stability of smooth solutions to the steady Navier-Stokes equations near plane Poiseuille-Couette flow. Consequently, we also provide the zero viscosity limit of the 2D steady Navier-Stokes equations to the steady Euler equations. First, in the absence of any external force, we prove that there exist smooth solutions to the steady Navier-Stokes equations which are stable under infinitesimal perturbations of plane Poiseuille-Couette flow. In particular, if the basic flow is the Couette flow, then we can prove that the flow is stable for any finite perturbation o ⁡ ( 1 ) o(1) . Moreover, we also show that for any smooth shear flow satisfying ( 1.27 ), if we put a proper external force to control the flow, then we can also obtain a smooth solution of the steady Navier-Stokes equations which is stable for infinitesimal perturbation of the external force. Finally, based on the same linear estimates, we establish the zero viscosity limit of all the solutions obtained above to the solutions of the Euler equations.



Keywords:
Steady Navier-Stokes equations, stability of shear flows,
zero viscous limit, weak boundary layers, strong solutions.

Introduction

Hydrodynamic stability has been recognized as one of the central problems in fluid mechanics. There have been plenty of works
including theoretical, experimental and numerical results on the stability characteristics of different classes of basic flows.
These include flows in channels, boundary layers, jets and shear layers.
Concerning the stability of viscous unsteady shear flows, the authors in
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
27
,
31
,
32
]
show
that the 2D or 3D Couette flow and pipe Poiseuille flow are linear stable for infinitesimal perturbations at high Reynolds number.
In
[
15
]
, the authors prove that generic plane shear profiles other than the plane Couette
flow are linearly unstable for sufficiently large Reynolds number. More recently, the authors in
[
28
]
use a new energy method to prove the instability of plane Couette flow for some perturbation of size
\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}
with any small
\delta>0
.

In this paper, we shall study the stability of shear flows in the
steady setting
. First we prove that, in contrast with
the unsteady case, a steady plane Poiseuille flow is stable under infinitesimal perturbations in the absence of external forces.
In fact, we can also prove that the basic flows of plane Poiseuille-Couette family defined in (
1.1
) are stable under infinitesimal perturbations. In particular, if the basic flow is Couette flow, then through a formal asymptotic expansion including the Euler correctors and weak boundary layer correctors, we can show that the flow is stable for any finite perturbation
o(1)
.
Moreover, we also show that any shear flow
\mathbf{u}_{0}=(\mu(y),0)
satisfying (
1.27
), which is a solution to the steady
Navier-Stokes equations with an external force
\mathbf{f}_{0}=(-\mu^{\prime\prime},0)
, is stable under an infinitesimal perturbation
of the external force.
Finally, based on the same linear estimates, we prove the zero viscosity limit of all the obtained solutions to the solutions of
the Euler equations.

Let
\mathbf{U}_{0}=(U(y),0)
be the basic flow of Poiseuille-Couette family with

U(y)=\alpha_{1}y+\alpha_{2}y(2-y),\ \ 0\leq y\leq 2,

(1.1)

where
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\geq 0
are given constants and assumed always to satisfy

\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}>0
. If
\alpha_{1}\equiv 0
, then we get the plane Poiseuille flow,
while
\alpha_{2}\equiv 0
, the flow is called the plane Couette flow.

For the characteristic quantities of the flow: the density
\rho^{*}
, the viscosity
\mu
, the characteristic velocity such as the free
stream velocity
V
, and the characteristic length
d
, we consider the dimensionless spatial coordinates
x=x^{*}/d,\ y=y^{*}/d
,

(x,y)\in\Omega=(0,L)\times(0,2)
, the dimensionless velocity
\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}=\mathbf{u}^{*}/V

and the dimensionless normal stress
P^{\varepsilon}=P^{*}/\rho^{*}V^{2}
. The steady incompressible Navier-Stokes equations can be written
into the following dimensionless form:

\begin{cases}\mathbb{u}^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\mathbb{u}^{\varepsilon}+\nabla P^{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon\Delta\mathbb{u}^{\varepsilon}=\mathbb{f}^{\varepsilon},\\
\nabla\cdot\mathbb{u}^{\varepsilon}=0,\end{cases}

(1.2)

where
\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}=(u^{\varepsilon},v^{\varepsilon})
,
\varepsilon=1/Re
,
Re=\frac{\rho^{*}V^{*}d}{\mu}
is the Reynolds number,
P^{\varepsilon}
is the pressure,
and
\mathbb{f}^{\varepsilon}=(f_{1}^{\varepsilon},f_{2}^{\varepsilon})
is the external force.

Besides, we consider an Euler flow:

\displaystyle\mathbb{u}^{0}=(\mu(y),0),

(1.3)

which, for any sufficiently smooth function
\mu
, satisfies the stationary Euler equations:

\begin{cases}\mathbb{u}^{0}\cdot\nabla\mathbb{u}^{0}+\nabla P^{0}=0\\
\nabla\cdot\mathbb{u}^{0}=0\\
\mathbb{u}^{0}\cdot\mathbb{n}|_{y=0,y=2}=0.\end{cases}

(1.4)

Here the pressure
P^{0}
is a constant,
\mathbb{n}
denotes the unit normal vector on
\{y=0\}
and
\{y=2\}
, and the boundary condition
\mathbb{u}^{0}\cdot\mathbb{n}=0
is known as the no-penetration condition.

We are interested in the asymptotic behavior of
\mathbb{u}^{\varepsilon}
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
with no-slip boundary condition on
y=0
and
y=2
.
In the presence of boundaries, if there is a mismatch between the no-slip boundary condition for the Navier-Stokes equations
and the no-penetration boundary condition satisfied by the Euler equations, then the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0

would be rectified by the presence of the Prandtl boundary layer. There have been many works on the well-posedness
of the Prandtl boundary layer equations. Concerning the instability of the Prandtl boundary layer
in the non-steady setting, we can refer, for instance, to
[

,
9
,
10
,
12
,
14
,
16
,
26
,
29
]
and the references therein.
Considering the well-posedness of steady Prandtl boundary layers,
we refer to
[
11
,
18
,
19
,
17
,
21
,
22
,
23
]
and the references therein.

In this article, we shall study the situation when the Euler flow
\mathbb{u}^{0}=(\mu(y),0)
itself is a shear flow
satisfying the no-slip boundary condition:

\displaystyle\mu(0)=0,\ \mu(2)=2\alpha_{1},

(1.5)

and is a small perturbation of the basic flow
\mathbf{U}_{0}
(cf. (
1.1
)).

In the 2D steady setting, under the assumption that
\mu^{\prime\prime}/\mu
vanishes at high order at
y=0,2
, and
in the absence of external forces, the authors in
[
24
]
investigate the inviscid limit of weak solutions to
the steady Navier-Stokes equations in a channel around the shear flows
(\mu(y),0)
with homogeneous no-slip boundary condition
on the rigid wall and the stress-free condition on the outflow part of the boundary.
The weak solutions in
[
24
]
can not be strong ones since one has no adequate regularity
near the corners of the domain due to the stress free boundary condition.
In
[
11

, under the proper control
of the external forces, the authors establish the
H^{1}
stability of shear flows of Prandtl type with periodic boundary condition in
x

The aim of this paper is to study the existence and stability of smooth solutions to the steady Navier-Stokes equations around the plane
Poiseuille-Couette family under the perturbations of both viscous stresses and external forces. Except for the no-slip boundary
condition on the rigid walls
y=0
and
y=2
, we assume further that the curl of the flow is well controlled
on the inflow and outflow parts of the boundary.

Let us give a few comments on our results. Considering solutions to the steady Navier-Stokes equations in the absence of external forces,
the class of strictly parallel flows
\mathbf{U}_{0}=(\mu(y),0)
is limited, since
\mu(y)
has to satisfy the equation of motion:

\varepsilon\frac{d^{2}\mu}{dy^{2}}=\frac{dP}{dx}.

This includes two important special cases:

{\it Plane\ Couette\ flow}:\qquad\mu(y)=y,\ P=\text{constant},\ 0<y<2,

and

{\it Plane\ Poiseuille\ flow}:\qquad\mu(y)=y(2-y),\ \frac{dP}{dx}=\text{constant},\ 0<y<2.

We have therefore obtained a family of strictly parallel flows, say the plane Poiseuille-Couette flow
\mathbf{U}_{0}=(U(y),0)
with

U(y)
defined in (
1.1
).

Compared with
[
11
,
18
]
, where the authors studied the stability of the Prandtl expansions for the steady Navier-Stokes equations,
the Euler flow
\mathbb{u}^{0}=(\mu(y),0)
in this paper satisfies the no-slip boundary condition:

\mu(0)=0,\ \mu(2)=2\alpha_{1}
. Thus, there would be no strong boundary layers around the rigid walls
y=0
and
y=2
.
In the absence of any external force, we could construct an Euler corrector to balance the perturbation of the internal viscous
stress of the fluid. Due to the no-slip boundary condition
\mu(0)=0
, the equation for the Euler corrector would have
a degenerate coefficient. It seems that for Poiseuille-Couette flows with
\alpha_{2}>0
, we can not continue the expansion
to obtain arbitrary small remainders due to the eigenvalue problem of the Laplace operator. However,
if
\alpha_{2}=0
, and
\mu^{\prime\prime}
degenerate rapidly near
y=0
, then we can continue the expansion
to obtain arbitrary small remainders by using weak boundary layer correctors to rectify the mismatch between the no-slip boundary
condition for the Navier-Stokes equations and the no-penetration boundary condition for Euler correctors.
So, we can prove that the Couette flow is stable under finite small perturbation.

Our proof is inspired by the method in
[
18
]
. Compared with the boundary layer profiles in
[
18
]
,
we would have one more boundary
y=2
with no-slip boundary condition, and as mentioned above,
the equations for the Euler correctors would have degenerate coefficients
near the rigid walls. If the basic flow is a Couette flow, considering the weak boundary layer correctors in the expansion,
the dominating equation is a linear parabolic equation with a degenerate coefficient
\mu(y)\sim y
near the boundary
y=0
and
non-degenerate coefficients near
y=2
. So in the process of multi-scale analysis, we have to use different scales to balance
the weak boundary layer terms, i.e., we utilize the scale
Y_{-}:=y/\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}
near
y=0
and

Y_{+}:=(2-y)/\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}
near
y=2
. Consequently, the boundary conditions for the corresponding Euler correctors

\mathbf{u}_{e}^{i,\pm}
are also different near the rigid walls. Moreover, the linear estimates in
[
18
]
failed in obtaining the third order derivative
v_{yyy}
as we do not have a good sign for
u_{sy}
on the upper bound
y=2
.
Instead, we shall use the theory of the Dirichlet boundary value problem for biharmonic equations as well as the Sobolev imbedding theory
to close the estimates. Finally, let us describe the main steps in the proof of the theorems.

In Section 2, we give the construction of the asymptotic solution
\mathbf{u}_{s}
when
\alpha_{2}=0
in the absence of any external force. First, we use an Euler corrector
\mathbf{u}_{e}^{1}
to offset the perturbation of the viscous stress of the flow. Then we use weak boundary layer correctors to rectify the mismatch between the no-slip condition for Navier-Stokes equations and the no-penetration condition for Euler correctors.
Thanks to the condition that
\mu^{\prime\prime}
degenerates rapidly when
y\rightarrow 0
,
we can continue to make as many expansions as we want and the remainders
(\mathcal{F}_{u},\mathcal{F}_{v})
can be arbitrary small.
For the weak boundary layer correctors in the expansion, as mentioned above,
we use the scale
Y_{-}:=y/\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}
near
y=0
and the scale
Y_{+}:=(2-y)/\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}
near
y=2
. Consequently, we have to construct the weak boundary layer correctors
u_{p}^{i,\pm}
separately.

In Section 3, we study the linearized system. First by introducing the stream function, we prove the existence of solutions to
the linearized system. Then, we derive the uniform-in-
\varepsilon
estimates of solutions to the linearized system.
We first use
v
as a multiplier to act on the curl equation to obtain the
L^{2}
norm of
v

bounded in order
O(1)
. Then, we use the multiplier
q_{x}=(v/{u_{s}})_{x}
to act on the curl equation to get the estimates
for second order derivatives. Broadly speaking, the multiplier
q_{x}
works well in controlling the convection terms,
but also brings complicated calculations in controlling the viscous terms.
Next, as mentioned above, we can not use the method in
[
18
]
to obtain the estimates for the third order derivatives
of the solutions as we do not have good signs for
u_{sy}
on the upper bound
y=2
.
Instead, we shall employ the multiplier
v_{xxx}
to act on the curl equation to obtain a bound for
\|\nabla^{2}v_{x}\|

which implies the boundedness of the convection terms in the biharmonic equation for
v
. Finally,
by the compatibility condition from the curl equation, we can obtain the estimates
of fourth order derivatives of
v
by employing the theory of the Dirichlet boundary value problem for biharmonic equations.
In this way, we can close the estimates by employing a careful bootstrap argument and the Sobolev imbedding theory.

Finally in Section 4, based on the linear estimates in Section 3, we prove the main theorems by the contract mapping principle.
We point out here that the only difference between the proofs of Theorems 1-3 lies in the remainders

in the linearized
system. Besides, the restriction on the exponent

in the expansion (

2
Formal asymptotic expansion around the Couette flow

The goal of this section is to construct approximate solutions
(\mathbf{u}_{s},P_{s})
in (
1.8
). In the case when
\alpha_{2}>0
in the absence of any external force, or there is a suitable external force

to control the flow, we will directly take
\mathbf{u}_{s}=\mathbf{u}^{0}
. So in this section we will only study the special case when
\alpha_{2}=0
in the absence of any external force, i.e., the basic flow is Couette flow. Without loss of generality, we will assume
\alpha_{1}=1
. Due to the fact that the Euler profile
\mathbf{u}^{0}
satisfies the no-slip boundary condition, there would be no strong boundary layers near the boundaries
y=0
and
y=2

First, we shall use an Euler corrector
\mathbf{u}_{e}^{1}
to offset the perturbation of the viscous stress of the fluid.
Then there would be, similar to the case of Prandtl boundary layers, a mismatch between the no-slip condition for Navier-Stokes equations and the no-penetration condition for the Euler corrector. This mismatch can be rectified by the presence of the weak boundary layer corrector. As the dominating equation for the weak boundary layer corrector is a linear parabolic equation including a degenerate term near
y=0
, in the process of multi-scale analysis near the boundaries, we will broke the boundary layer profile into two components, one supported near
y=0
with scale
Y_{-}:=y/\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}
,
\mathbf{u}_{p}^{1,-}
, and one supported near
y=2
with scale

\mathbf{u}_{p}^{1,+}
. Thanks to the degeneration of
\mu^{\prime\prime}
near

, we can continue the construction of
\mathbf{u}_{e}^{i,\pm}
and
\mathbf{u}_{p}^{i,\pm,}
till the remainders defined in (

)-(
1.13
) are small enough.

In what follows,
the Eulerian profiles are functions of
(x,y)
, whereas the boundary layer profiles are functions of
(x,Y)
, where

\displaystyle Y=\left\{\begin{aligned} &Y_{+}:=\frac{2-y}{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}\text{ if }1\leq y\leq 2,\\
&Y_{-}:=\frac{y}{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}}\text{ if }0\leq y\leq 1.\end{aligned}\right.

As we have used different scales
Y_{\pm}
in the construction of the weak boundary layer profiles, we will have to use different Euler correctors
\mathbf{u}_{e}^{i,\pm}
in the following expansion. The expansion will be continued till the remainders are small enough. More precisely,
we expand the solution in

\begin{cases}u^{\varepsilon}=\mu+\varepsilon u_{e}^{1}+\varepsilon u_{p}^{1,+}+\varepsilon u_{p}^{1,-}+\sum_{i=2}^{M}\varepsilon^{1+\frac{i-1}{2}}(u^{i,+}_{e}+u^{i,+}_{p})\\

\end{cases}

We shall also introduce the notation

\begin{cases}u_{s}^{E}:=\mu+\varepsilon u_{e}^{1}+\sum_{i=2}^{M}\varepsilon^{1+\frac{i-1}{3}}u^{i,-}_{e}+\sum_{i=2}^{M}\varepsilon^{1+\frac{i-1}{2}}u^{i,+}_{e},\\

(2.3)

The main result of this section reads as:

and

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}u_{s}|+|\partial_{y}v_{s}|+|u_{s}-\mu|\lesssim\min\{O(\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}})y,O(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}})(2-y),O(\varepsilon)\},

(2.5)

(2.7)

where

Remark 2.2
.

In the case when
\alpha_{2}>0
in the absence of any external force, as the Euler profile
\mathbf{u}^{0}
satisfies

and

2.1
Euler correctors

In this subsection we will study the construction of Euler correctors. The equations satisfied by the first Euler corrector are obtained by collecting the
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
order Euler terms from (
1.12
) - (
1.13
), and is now shown:

\begin{cases}\mu\partial_{x}u^{1}_{e}+\mu^{\prime}v^{1}_{e}+\partial_{x}P^{1}_{e}=\mu^{\prime\prime}(y)\\
\mu\partial_{x}v^{1}_{e}+\partial_{y}P^{1}_{e}=0,\\
\partial_{x}u^{1}_{e}+\partial_{y}v^{1}_{e}=0,\\
\partial_{x}v^{1}_{e}|_{x=0}=0,v^{1}_{e}|_{x=L}=0,\quad v^{1}_{e}|_{y=0}=v^{1}_{e}|_{y=2}=0.\end{cases}

(2.11)

By going to the vorticity formulation, we arrive at the following problem:

\displaystyle-\mu\Delta v^{1}_{e}+\mu^{\prime\prime}v^{1}_{e}=\mu^{\prime\prime\prime}(y),\hskip 8.53581pt\hskip 8.53581ptu^{1}_{e}:=-\int_{0}^{x}v^{1}_{ey}(s,y)ds.

(2.12)

Then we divide (
2.12
) by
\mu

\displaystyle-\Delta v^{1}_{e}+\frac{\mu^{\prime\prime}}{\mu}v^{1}_{e}=\frac{\mu^{\prime\prime\prime}}{\mu}.

(2.13)

Thanks to the degeneration of
\mu^{\prime\prime}
near
y=0
, If we take
|\frac{\mu^{\prime\prime}}{\mu}|_{L^{\infty}}
small enough, equation (
2.13
) will be well-posed. Next we turn to the construction of the rest Euler correctors. Due to the different scales of the weak boundary layer correctors near
y=0
and
y=2

v_{p}^{i,\pm}
from weak boundary layers correctors. The system satisfied by the rest Euler correctors are shown as
(i=2,...M)

\begin{cases}\mu\partial_{x}u^{i,+}_{e}+\mu^{\prime}v^{i,+}_{e}+\partial_{x}P^{i,+}_{e}=0\\
\mu\partial_{x}v^{i,+}_{e}+\partial_{y}P^{i,+}_{e}=0,\\

\partial_{x}v^{i,+}_{e}|_{x=0}=0,\ v^{i,+}_{e}|_{x=L}=0,\ v^{i,+}_{e}|_{y=2}=-v^{i-1,+}_{p}|_{y=2},\hskip 8.53581pt\partial_{y}v^{i,+}_{e}|_{y=0}=0.\end{cases}

(2.14)

and

\begin{cases}\mu\partial_{x}u^{i,-}_{e}+\mu^{\prime}v^{i,-}_{e}+\partial_{x}P^{i,-}_{e}=0\\

(2.18)

\mathbf{u}_{e}^{i,-}
will not cause the mismatch of the velocity on

Precisely, we have the following Lemma for Euler correctors:

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume that
\mu(y)
satisfies the conditions in Theorem
2.1
, then there exists a unique triple
(u_{e}^{1},v_{e}^{1},P_{e}^{1})
satisfying system (
2.11
), and unique triples

(u^{i,\pm}_{e},v^{i,\pm}_{e},P^{i,\pm}_{e})
,
i=2,...,M
, satisfying system (
2.14
) and (
2.15
). Besides, we have

u_{e}^{i,+}=v_{e}^{i,+}=0\ \text{on}\ y=0;\ \ u_{e}^{i,-}=v_{e}^{i,-}=0\ \text{on}\ y=2,\ i=2,...M,

(2.19)

and

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}^{l}\partial_{y}^{m}\{u^{1}_{e},v^{1}_{e},P^{1}_{e}\}\|+\|\partial_{x}^{l}\partial_{y}^{m}\{u^{i,\pm}_{e},v^{i,\pm}_{e},P^{i,\pm}_{e}\}\|\leq C(m,l)|\frac{\mu^{\prime\prime\prime}}{\mu}|_{C^{k}},

(2.20)

where
m,l\geq 0
are integers satisfying
m+l\leq k+2
.

Proof.

First of all, under the assumption that
|\frac{\mu^{\prime\prime}}{\mu}|_{L^{\infty}}
is small enough, the existence of
(u_{e}^{1},v_{e}^{1},P_{e}^{1})
,
(u^{i,\pm}_{e},v^{i,\pm}_{e},P^{i,\pm}_{e})
and estimates (
2.20
) can be easily obtained by theory of elliptic equations.

Then we need to prove
u_{e}^{i,+}=v_{e}^{i,+}=0
on
y=0
and
u_{e}^{i,-}=v_{e}^{i,-}=0
on
y=2
.

We will first prove
u_{e}^{i,+}=v_{e}^{i,+}=0
on
y=0
by weak maximum principle of elliptic equations.

First, by (
2.16
), we easily have

u_{e}^{i,+}(x,0)=-\int_{0}^{x}v_{ey}^{i,+}dx=0.

Next, for fixed
\sigma>0
small enough, since we have already known by (
2.20
) that
v_{e}^{i,+}
is bounded, we can define a barrier function

\phi^{\sigma}=K(y+\sigma)^{\alpha},\ 0<\alpha<1,

where
K
is a constant satisfying

K\geq C(|\frac{\mu^{\prime\prime}}{\mu}|_{L^{\infty}}+\alpha_{0})

with
C
being a sufficiently large constant.
Then we have

\Delta\phi^{\sigma}=\alpha(\alpha-1)K(y+\sigma)^{\alpha-2}<0.

(2.21)

Now we cut
v_{e}^{i,+}
by the cut-off function
\chi(y)
defined in (
1.6
).
Thanks to the boundary conditions
\partial_{x}v_{e}^{i,+}|_{x=0}=0
, we can make an even extension of
\chi(y)v_{e}^{i,+}
with respect to
x=0
and the new function, still denoted by
\chi(y)v_{e}^{i,+}
, belong to
C^{2}(\Omega^{*})\cap C^{0}(\bar{\Omega}^{*})
, here
\Omega^{*}=(-L,L)\times(0,2)
.
Recalling that
K
is large enough, we have by (
2.16
) and (
2.21
)

\Delta(\chi v_{e}^{i,+}-\phi^{\sigma})\geq 0\ \text{ in}\ \Omega^{*}.

Besides, direct computation shows that

\partial_{y}(\chi v_{e}^{i,+}-\phi^{\sigma})|_{y=0}<0.

So we can not obtain the maximum value of
\chi v_{e}^{i,+}-\phi^{\sigma}
on
y=0
. More over, we also have

\ (\chi v_{e}^{i,+}-\phi^{\sigma})|_{\{y=2\}\cup\{x=L\}\cup\{x=-L\}}<0.

The weak maximum principle for elliptic equations implies that

\chi v_{e}^{i,+}-\phi^{\sigma}\leq 0\ \text{ in}\ \Omega^{*}.

If we replace
\chi v_{e}^{i,+}
by
-\phi^{\sigma}
, and
\phi^{\sigma}
by
-\chi v_{e}^{i,+}
above, we can obtain

-\phi^{\sigma}\leq-\chi v_{e}^{i,+}\ \text{ in}\ \Omega^{*}.

Combing above, we have proved that for any
0<\alpha<1

|v_{e}^{i,+}|\leq K(y+\sigma)^{\alpha},\ \text{when}\ 0\leq y\leq\frac{1}{2},

for any
\sigma>0
small enough, which implies immediately
v_{e}^{i,+}=0
on
y=0
. Similarly we can also prove
u_{e}^{i,-}=v_{e}^{i,-}=0
on
y=2
and we complete the proof of the Lemma.
∎

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}q_{x}\Delta(u_{y}-v_{x})dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}u_{yyy}q_{x}dxdy+\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}(2v_{xyy}+v_{xxx})q_{x}dxdy

\displaystyle\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}u_{yy}q_{xy}dxdy-2\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}v_{xy}q_{xy}dxdy-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}v_{xx}q_{xx}dxdy-\varepsilon\int_{0}^{2}v_{xx}q_{x}|_{x=0}dy

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}u_{xyy}q_{y}dxdy-2\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}(u_{s}q_{xy}+u_{sy}q_{x}+u_{sx}q_{y})q_{xy}dxdy

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}(u_{s}q_{xx}+2u_{sx}q_{x}+u_{sxx}q)q_{xx}dxdy-\varepsilon\int_{0}^{2}\frac{u_{sx}}{u_{s}}v_{xx}q|_{x=0}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}v_{yyy}q_{y}dxdy-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}(2u_{s}q_{xy}^{2}+u_{s}q_{xx}^{2})dxdy+\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}u_{syy}q_{x}^{2}dxdy

(3.63)

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}(2u_{sx}q_{x}q_{xx}+u_{sxx}qq_{xx}+2u_{sx}q_{y}q_{xy})dxdy-\varepsilon\int_{0}^{2}\frac{u_{sx}}{u_{s}}v_{xx}q|_{x=0}dy,

where from Lemma
3.6
and (
3.56
) one gets

\displaystyle|\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}[u_{syy}q_{x}^{2}-(2u_{sx}q_{x}q_{xx}+u_{sxx}qq_{xx}+2u_{sx}q_{y}q_{xy})]dxdy-\varepsilon\int_{0}^{2}\frac{u_{sx}}{u_{s}}v_{xx}q|_{x=0}dy|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\varepsilon\|q_{x}\|^{2}+\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\|\sqrt{u_{s}}q_{xx}\|^{2}+\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\|\sqrt{u_{s}}q_{xy}\|^{2}++\varepsilon L^{\frac{1}{2}}|u_{sx}u_{s}^{-\frac{3}{4}}|_{L^{\infty}}|u_{s}^{-\frac{1}{4}}v_{xx}(0,\cdot)|\|q_{x}\|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\varepsilon L^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\sqrt{u_{s}}\nabla^{2}q\|^{2}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{8}}L^{\frac{1}{2}}(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\sqrt{u_{s}}\nabla^{2}q\|+\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\|v_{xxx}\|)\|\sqrt{u_{s}}\nabla^{2}q\|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle L^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\sqrt{u_{s}}\nabla^{2}q\|^{2}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}+3}\|v_{xxx}\|^{2},

(3.64)

while

\displaystyle\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}v_{yyy}q_{y}dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\int_{0}^{L}(u_{s}q_{yy}+2u_{sy}q_{y})q_{y}|_{y=0}dx+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{L}(u_{s}q_{yy}+2u_{sy}q_{y})q_{y}|_{y=2}dx

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}(u_{s}q_{yy}+2u_{sy}q_{y}+u_{syy}q)q_{yy}dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\int_{0}^{L}(u_{s}q_{yy}+u_{sy}q_{y})q_{y}|_{y=0}dx+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{L}(u_{s}q_{yy}+u_{sy}q_{y})q_{y}|_{y=2}dx

(3.65)

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}u_{s}q_{yy}^{2}+\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}(u_{syy}q_{y}^{2}-u_{syy}qq_{yy})dxdy.

If
u_{s}>0
on

, while when

\displaystyle\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}v_{yyy}q_{y}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq-\varepsilon\int_{0}^{L}u_{sy}q_{y}^{2}|_{y=0}dx-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}u_{s}q_{yy}^{2}+\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}(u_{syy}q_{y}^{2}-u_{syy}qq_{yy})dxdy

\displaystyle\leq-\varepsilon\int_{0}^{L}u_{sy}q_{y}^{2}|_{y=0}dx-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}u_{s}q_{yy}^{2}+\varepsilon\|q_{y}\|^{2}+\varepsilon\|\sqrt{u_{s}}q_{yy}\|_{L^{2}}\|u_{s}^{-\frac{1}{2}}q\|

\displaystyle\leq-\varepsilon\int_{0}^{L}u_{sy}q_{y}^{2}|_{y=0}dx-\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}u_{s}q_{yy}^{2}+\varepsilon L^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\sqrt{u_{s}}\nabla^{2}q\|.

(3.67)

(3.68)

Furthermore, by virtue of (

) and Lemma
3.6

\displaystyle|\int_{\Omega}S(u,v)q_{x}dxdy|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\int_{\Omega}[-\partial_{y}(u_{sx}u+v_{s}u_{y})+\partial_{x}(v_{s}v_{y}+v_{sx}u)]q_{x}dxdy|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\int_{\Omega}[(u_{sx}u+v_{s}u_{y})q_{xy}+v_{sxx}uq_{x}-v_{sy}v_{x}q_{x}-v_{s}v_{x}q_{xy}]dxdy|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|(\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits{\mathbf{F}},q_{x})|=|(\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits{\mathcal{F}},q_{x})|

to control the flow, we take

\mathbf{u}_{s}=\mathbf{u}^{0},\quad P_{s}=C,

where
C
is any constant. By (
1.12
) and (
1.13
) we have

F_{1}=\varepsilon^{-M_{0}}f^{\varepsilon}_{1}+\mathcal{F}_{u}=\varepsilon^{-M_{0}}(\varepsilon\mu^{\prime\prime}-f^{\varepsilon}_{1})=\varepsilon^{-M_{0}}g_{1}^{\varepsilon};

F_{2}=\varepsilon^{-M_{0}}f^{\varepsilon}_{2}+\mathcal{F}_{v}=\varepsilon^{-M_{0}}f^{\varepsilon}_{2}=\varepsilon^{-M_{0}}g_{2}^{\varepsilon}.

Then, from (
1.28
) in Theorem
1.3
we get

\displaystyle|(\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits{\mathbf{F}},q_{x})|=

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{-\frac{11}{8}-\gamma}|(\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits{\mathbf{g}^{\varepsilon}},q_{x})|=\varepsilon^{-\frac{11}{8}-\gamma}|\int_{0}^{2}\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits{\mathbf{g}^{\varepsilon}}q|_{x=0}dy|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{-\frac{11}{8}-\gamma}\|\mathbf{g}^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{2}}(|u_{s}q_{y}(0,\cdot)|_{L^{2}}+\|\sqrt{u_{s}}v_{x}\|_{L^{2}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\alpha_{0}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{\mathcal{X}},

(4.3)

where the constant
C
does not depend on
\varepsilon
.

Step II: Solutions to the nonlinear systems.

First of all, let
(\mathbf{u}_{s},P_{s})
be one of the cases in Step I,
then we see that

\|\mathcal{\mathbf{F}}\|_{H_{1}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{3}\|\mathcal{\mathbf{F}}\|_{H_{2}}^{2}+|(\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits{\mathbf{F}},q_{x})|\leq C(\alpha_{0}^{2}+\alpha_{0}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{\mathcal{X}})

(4.4)

with the constant
C
being independent of
\varepsilon
. So we have from Theorem
3.5

\|\mathbf{u}\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{2}\leq C(\varepsilon^{\gamma}\|\bar{\mathbf{u}}\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{4}+\alpha_{0}^{2}).

(4.5)

In view of Theorems
3.1
and (
4.5
), we see that
for any
\bar{\mathbf{u}}=(\bar{u},\bar{v})\in\mathcal{X}
, there is a unique solution
(\mathbf{u},P)
satisfying (
3.1
)-(
3
)
with the following estimate:

\|\mathbf{u}\|_{\mathcal{X}}\leq C(\varepsilon^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\|\bar{\mathbf{u}}\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{2}+\alpha_{0}).

(4.6)

Now, for
\alpha_{0}
small enough, we define

V=\{\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{X}|\|\mathbf{u}\|_{\mathcal{X}}<2C\alpha_{0}\}.

Then we can define a mapping
T:V\rightarrow V
by
T(\bar{\mathbf{u}})=\mathbf{u}
.

Now, for any
\bar{\mathbf{u}}_{1},\bar{\mathbf{u}}_{2}\in V
, we denote
\mathbf{u}_{1}=(u_{1},v_{1})=T(\bar{\mathbf{u}}_{1})
and
\mathbf{u}_{2}=(u_{2},v_{2})=T(\bar{\mathbf{u}}_{2})
.
Then,
\mathbf{u}_{1}-\mathbf{u}_{2}
satisfies the following system:

\displaystyle-u_{s}\Delta(v_{1}-v_{2})+\Delta u_{s}(v_{1}-v_{2})+S(u_{1}-u_{2},v_{1}-v_{2})-\varepsilon\Delta\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits(\mathbf{u}_{1}-\mathbf{u}_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits\mathbf{N}(\bar{u}_{1},\bar{v}_{1})-\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits\mathbf{N}(\bar{u}_{2},\bar{v}_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{y}[(\bar{v}_{1}-\bar{v}_{2})\bar{u}_{1y}+(\bar{u}_{1}-\bar{u}_{2})\bar{u}_{1x}]-\partial_{x}[(\bar{v}_{1}-\bar{v}_{2})\bar{v}_{1y}+(\bar{u}_{1}-\bar{u}_{2})\bar{v}_{1x}]

(4.7)

\displaystyle+\partial_{y}[\bar{v}_{2}(\bar{u}_{1}-\bar{u}_{2})_{y}+\bar{u}_{2}(\bar{u}_{1}-\bar{u}_{2})_{x}]-\partial_{x}[v_{2}(\bar{v}_{1}-\bar{v}_{2})_{y}+\bar{u}_{2}(\bar{v}_{1}-\bar{v}_{2})_{x}].

If we further denote
\bar{q}=\frac{v_{1}-v_{2}}{u_{s}}
, and

\displaystyle B_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{u_{s}}(v_{1}-v_{2})_{y}\|^{2}+\|\sqrt{u_{s}}(v_{1}-v_{2})_{x}\|^{2},

\displaystyle B_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon\|\sqrt{u_{s}}\bar{q}_{xx}\|^{2}+\varepsilon\|\sqrt{u_{s}}\bar{q}_{xy}\|^{2}+\varepsilon\|\sqrt{u_{s}}\bar{q}_{yy}\|^{2}+|u_{s}\bar{q}_{y}(0,\cdot)|^{2},

then we have in a manner similar to (
3.82
) that

B_{1}^{2}+B_{2}^{2}\leq\varepsilon^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\|\mathbf{u}_{1}-\mathbf{u}_{2}\|_{\mathcal{X}}\|\bar{\mathbf{u}}_{1}-\bar{\mathbf{u}}_{2}\|_{\mathcal{X}}(\|\bar{\mathbf{u}}_{1}\|_{\mathcal{X}}+\|\bar{\mathbf{u}}_{2}\|_{\mathcal{X}}).

(4.8)

Similarly to the proof of Lemma
3.14
, we obtain

\varepsilon^{\frac{5}{2}}\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{H^{4}}\lesssim B_{2}+\varepsilon\|\mathbf{u}_{1}-\mathbf{u}_{2}\|_{\mathcal{X}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{7}{8}}\|\bar{\mathbf{u}}_{1}-\bar{\mathbf{u}}_{2}\|_{\mathcal{X}}(\|\bar{\mathbf{u}}_{1}\|_{\mathcal{X}}+\|\bar{\mathbf{u}}_{2}\|_{\mathcal{X}}).

(4.9)

Combing (
4.8
) with (
4.9
), we argue similarly to the proof of Theorem
3.5
to deduce

\|\mathbf{u}_{1}-\mathbf{u}_{2}\|_{\mathcal{X}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\|\bar{\mathbf{u}}_{1}-\bar{\mathbf{u}}_{2}\|_{\mathcal{X}}(\|\bar{\mathbf{u}}_{1}\|_{\mathcal{X}}+\|\bar{\mathbf{u}}_{2}\|_{\mathcal{X}}).

Thus,
T
is a compact mapping if
\alpha_{0}
is taken to be small enough. We finish the proof of the main theorems.

Appendix A
Sketch of the construction of
\mathbf{u}_{p}^{i,-}

In this section, we sketch the construction of
\mathbf{u}_{p}^{i,-}
, and refer to
[
25
]
for the details.

As the equations for the weak boundary layer correctors are linear parabolic equations including degenerate terms
\mu(y)\partial_{x}u_{p}^{i,-}

near
y=0
, we would have to use the scale
Y_{-}=\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{3}}y
near
y=0
.
Recalling that
\mu(y)\sim y
as
y\rightarrow 0
, and that the boundary layer correctors would degenerate rapidly when
Y_{-}>1
, we first collect from (
1.12
) the leading
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{\frac{4}{3}})
terms for the boundary layer profiles near
y=0
:

\displaystyle R^{1,-}_{u}=\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{3}}\mu\partial_{x}u_{p}^{1,-}+v_{p}^{1,-}\mu^{\prime}+\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{3}}\partial_{x}P_{p}^{1,-}-\partial_{Y_{-}Y_{-}}u_{p}^{1,-},

(A.1)

and the leading
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}})
terms for (
1.13
) is

\partial_{Y_{-}}P_{p}^{1,-}=0.

(A.2)

Then to construct
\mathbf{u}_{p}^{1,-}
, we first consider the following system (using the fact that
\mu(y)\sim y
when
y\rightarrow 0
):

\displaystyle\begin{cases}Y_{-}\partial_{x}u_{p}^{1,0,-}+v_{p}^{1,0,-}+\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{3}}\partial_{x}P_{p}^{1,0,-}-\partial_{Y_{-}Y_{-}}u_{p}^{1,0,-}=0,\ \ \partial_{Y_{-}}P_{p}^{1,0,-}=0,\\
u_{p}^{1,0,-}|_{x=0}=0,\ u_{p}^{1,0,-}|_{Y_{-}=0}=-u_{e}^{1}(x,0),\ u_{p}^{1,0,-}|_{Y_{-}\rightarrow\infty}=0,\\
v_{p}^{1,0,-}=\int_{Y_{-}}^{\infty}\partial_{x}u_{p}^{1,0,-}.\end{cases}

(A.3)

It is easy to obtain
P_{p}^{1,-}\equiv 0
, and thus,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}Y_{-}\partial_{x}u_{p}^{1,0,-}+v_{p}^{1,0,-}-\partial_{Y_{-}Y_{-}}u_{p}^{1,0,-}=0,\\
u_{p}^{1,0,-}|_{x=0}=0,\ u_{p}^{1,0,-}|_{Y_{-}=0}=-u_{e}^{1}(x,0),\ u_{p}^{1,0,-}|_{Y_{-}\rightarrow\infty}=0,\\
v_{p}^{1,0,-}=\int_{Y_{-}}^{\infty}\partial_{x}u_{p}^{1,0,-}.\end{cases}

(A.4)

Note that we construct
u^{1,0,-}_{p},v^{1,0,-}_{p}
on
(0,L)\times(0,\infty)
. We now cut-off these layers and
make an
O(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}})
-order error:

\displaystyle u^{1,-}_{p}=\chi(\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}Y_{-}}{a_{0}})u^{1,0,-}_{p}-\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}}{a_{0}}\chi^{\prime}(\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}Y_{-}}{a_{0}})\int_{0}^{x}v^{1,0,-}_{p},\;\;v^{1,-}_{p}:=\chi(\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}Y_{-}}{a_{0}})v^{1,0,-}_{p}

(A.5)

where
a_{0}>0
is a fixed constant small enough, and
\chi
is defined in (
1.6
).

When
i=2
, let
u_{p}^{1,0,-},\ v_{p}^{1,0,-}
be solutions to the system (
A.4
), then after cutting off (
A.5
),
the contribution to the next layer is

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{cut}^{1,-}=\frac{1}{a_{0}}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}Y_{-}\chi^{\prime}v_{p}^{1,0,-}+3\frac{1}{a_{0}}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\chi^{\prime}\partial_{Y_{-}}u_{p}^{1,0,-}+3\frac{1}{a_{0}^{2}}\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}\chi^{\prime\prime}u_{p}^{1,0,-}+\frac{1}{a_{0}^{3}}\varepsilon\chi^{\prime\prime\prime}\int_{0}^{x}v_{p}^{1,0,-}.

We also obtain another error, due to approximating
\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{3}}\mu
by
Y_{-}
in the support of the cut-off function
\chi(\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}Y}{a_{0}})
and by approximating
\mu^{\prime}
by
1
. This error is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{approx}^{1,-}:=

\displaystyle(\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{3}}\mu(y)-Y_{-})(\chi(\frac{y}{a_{0}})\partial_{x}u^{1,0,-}_{p}+\frac{1}{a_{0}}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\chi^{\prime}u_{p}^{1,0,-})+(\mu^{\prime}-1)\chi(\frac{y}{a_{0}})v^{1,0,-}_{p}

(A.7)

Finally, we have higher order terms that contribute to the error:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{1,-}_{quad}:=

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}(u^{1}_{e}+u^{1,-}_{p})\partial_{x}u^{1,-}_{p}+\varepsilon^{2}u^{1,-}_{p}\partial_{x}u^{1}_{e}+\varepsilon^{\frac{5}{3}}v^{1}_{e}u^{1,-}_{pY_{-}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{7}{3}}v^{1,-}_{p}u^{1}_{ey}

(A.8)

\displaystyle+\varepsilon^{2}v^{1}_{p}u^{1,-}_{pY_{-}}-\varepsilon^{2}u^{1,-}_{pxx}.

For the higher order terms in the second equation, we shall use our auxiliary pressure to move it to the top equation.
This is achieved by defining the first auxiliary pressure
P^{1,a,-}_{p}
to zero out the terms contributed from

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{4}{3}}(\mu+\varepsilon u^{1}_{e})v^{1,-}_{px}+\varepsilon u^{1,-}_{p}(\varepsilon v^{1}_{ex}+\varepsilon^{\frac{4}{3}}v^{1,-}_{px})+\varepsilon^{2}v^{1}_{e}v^{1,-}_{pY_{-}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{7}{3}}v^{1}_{p}v^{1,-}_{pY_{-}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{7}{3}}v^{1,-}_{p}v^{1}_{ey}

\displaystyle-\varepsilon^{\frac{5}{3}}v^{1,-}_{pY_{-}Y_{-}}-\varepsilon^{\frac{7}{3}}v^{1,-}_{pxx}+\varepsilon^{\frac{4}{3}}P^{1,a,-}_{pY_{-}}=0,

(A.9)

which therefore motivates our following definition:

\displaystyle-\varepsilon^{\frac{4}{3}}P^{1,a,-}_{P}:=

\displaystyle\int_{Y_{-}}^{\infty}\Big(\varepsilon^{\frac{4}{3}}(\mu+\varepsilon u^{1}_{e})v^{1,-}_{px}+\varepsilon u^{1,-}_{p}(\varepsilon v^{1}_{ex}+\varepsilon^{\frac{4}{3}}v^{1,-}_{px})+\varepsilon v^{1}_{e}v^{1,-}_{pY_{-}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{7}{3}}v^{1,-}_{p}v^{1,-}_{pY_{-}}

\displaystyle+\varepsilon^{\frac{7}{3}}v^{1,-}_{p}v^{1}_{ey}-\varepsilon^{\frac{5}{3}}v^{1,-}_{pY_{-}Y_{-}}-\varepsilon^{\frac{7}{3}}v^{1,-}_{pxx}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}Y^{\prime}.

(A.10)

As a result, we can define the force for the next order weak boundary layer via

\displaystyle F^{2,-}:=\varepsilon^{-\frac{5}{3}}\Big(-\varepsilon^{\frac{4}{3}}\mathcal{C}^{1,-}_{cut}+\varepsilon^{\frac{4}{3}}\mathcal{C}^{1,-}_{approx}+\mathcal{C}^{1,-}_{quad}-\varepsilon^{\frac{5}{3}}\partial_{x}P^{1,a,-}_{P}\Big).

(A.11)

Remark A.1
.

For
i=3,...,M
,
F^{i,-}
can be constructed similarly.
Since we shall put the terms with
H^{2}
-norm smaller than
\varepsilon^{\frac{7}{4}+\gamma}
into the remainders
(\mathcal{F}_{u},\mathcal{F}_{v})
,
we only need the auxiliary pressure
P_{p}^{i,a,-}
for
i=1,...,5
. When
i>5
, we shall take
P_{p}^{i,a,+}=0
.
We remark here that all the interaction terms with form
u_{e}^{i,+}u_{p}^{j,-}
will be put into the remainders
(\mathcal{F}_{u},\mathcal{F}_{v})
.

Let us therefore consider the abstract problem (dropping indices):

\displaystyle Y\partial_{x}u+v-\partial_{Y}^{2}u=F,\qquad(x,Y)\in(0,L)\times(0,\infty)

(A.12)

\displaystyle v:=\int_{Y}^{\infty}\partial_{x}u\,\mathrm{d}Y^{\prime},

(A.13)

\displaystyle u|_{x=0}=0,\qquad u|_{Y=0}=g(x),\qquad u|_{Y\rightarrow\infty}=0.

(A.14)

For this abstract problem, we have the following estimates:

Lemma A.2
.

Assume that
F(x,Y)
decays rapidly at infinity, i.e., for any
m\geq 0
, there is a constant
M>0
,
such that

\|(1+Y)^{m}\partial_{x}^{n}\partial_{Y}^{l}F\|\leq M\ \text{ for }0\leq 2n+l\leq K,

where
K>0
is a sufficiently large constant. Then, there exists a unique solution
(u,v)
to (
A.12
)–(
A.14
) satisfying

\|(1+Y)^{m}\partial_{x}^{n}\partial_{Y}^{l}\{u,v\}\|\leq C(m,n,l)(M+\|g\|_{H^{K+1}})\ \text{ for any }2n+l\leq K+2,

where the constant
C
does not depend on
Y
.

For
i=M
, we need to slightly modify the abstract problem. Consider

\displaystyle Y\partial_{x}u+v-\partial_{Y}^{2}u=F,\qquad(x,Y)\in(0,L)\times(0,\infty)

\displaystyle v:=-\int_{0}^{Y}\partial_{x}u\,\mathrm{d}Y^{\prime},

(A.15)

\displaystyle u|_{x=0}=0,\qquad u|_{Y=0}=g(x),\qquad\partial_{Y}u|_{Y\rightarrow\infty}=0,

and we have

Lemma A.3
.

Assume that
F(x,Y)
decays rapidly at infinity, i.e., for any
m\geq 0
, there is a constant
M>0
,
such that

\displaystyle\|(1+Y)^{m}\partial_{x}^{n}\partial_{Y}^{l}F\|\leq M\text{ for }0\leq 2n+l\leq K,

(A.16)

then there exists a unique solutions
(u,v)
of (
A.15
), satisfying

\displaystyle\|(1+Y)^{m}\partial_{x}^{n}\partial_{Y}^{l}u\|\leq C(m,n,l)(M+\|g\|_{H^{K+1}})\text{ for any }l\geq 1,1\leq 2n+l\leq K+2,

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}^{n}\{u,\frac{v}{Y}\}\|\leq C(m,n,l)(M+\|g\|_{H^{K+1}})\text{ for any }0\leq 2n\leq K+2.

Remark A.4
.

The main difference between the proofs of Lemma
A.2
and Lemma
A.3
lies in that one does not
have the decay of
v
as
Y\rightarrow\infty
in Lemma
A.3
.

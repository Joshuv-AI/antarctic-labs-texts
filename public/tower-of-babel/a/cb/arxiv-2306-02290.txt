Simultaneous supersingular reductions of Hecke orbits
Xiaoyu Zhang

Abstract
. Fix a finite set of primes 𝒮 \mathcal{S} , for an abelian variety A 0 A_{0} over ℚ ¯ \overline{\mathbb{Q}} (with extra structures) which has supersingular good reductions at all ℓ ∈ 𝒮 \ell\in\mathcal{S} , we consider the simultaneous reduction modulo ℓ ∈ 𝒮 \ell\in\mathcal{S} of several copies of p p -adic/prime-to- N N Hecke orbits of A 0 A_{0} . We show that this simultaneous reduction map is always surjective under certain rationality condition on the copies of the Hecke orbits.

1.
Introduction

Abelian varieties are one of the central objects of study in algebraic geometry and algebraic number theory. Shimura varieties are moduli spaces of families of abelian varieties (with extra structures) and this is of fundamental importance in Langlands program and automorphic representations. In this article, we study the simultaneous reductions (modulo primes) of Hecke orbits of abelian varieties in a Shimura varieties and in particular we classify the distribution properties of these reductions.

We write
Sh_{k}
for a PEL modular variety over
k=\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
or
\mathbb{C}
associated to a PEL datum
(B,\dagger,V,\langle-,-\rangle)
(
[
Lan13
, Chapter 1]
).
Fix a prime number
p\neq\mathrm{char}(k)
. The
p
-adic Hecke orbit
\mathcal{H}_{p}(x_{0})
of a point
x_{0}\in Sh_{k}
is the set of points
x\in Sh_{k}
related to
x
by
p
-power isogenies. This notion is very important in the study of geometric properties of Shimura varieties in positive characteristic. For example, the well-known Hecke orbit conjecture says that for any
x_{0}
, its
p
-adic Hecke orbit is Zariski dense in the central leaf of
Sh_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}}
containing
x_{0}
(
[
CO19
]
). Similarly one can show that the
p
-adic Hecke orbit
\mathcal{H}_{p}(x_{0})
for
x_{0}\in Sh_{\mathbb{C}}
is dense in the archimedean topology (
[
EY03
, § 6]
). In this article, we would like to relate these two orbits from characteristic
0
to characteristic
\ell
, in particular, after fixing a finite set
\mathcal{S}
of primes
\ell\neq p
such that
x_{0}\in Sh_{\mathbb{C}}
has good reduction

at all
\ell\in\mathcal{S}
, we consider the distribution property of the simultaneous reduction
(x_{\ell})_{\ell\in\mathcal{S}}
for
x\in\mathcal{H}_{p}(x_{0})
, that is,

Question 1.1
.

(including CM abelian varieties), assuming that the reduction

. We give a complete answer to the following question which refines the preceding one (
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}:=\{\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\ell}\mid\ell\in\mathcal{S}\}
)

Question 1.2

Is the following simultaneous reduction map surjective?

\mathbf{R}_{\mathcal{S},\widetilde{\mathcal{T}};p}\colon\mathcal{H}_{p}(x_{0})\mapsto\prod_{\ell\in\mathcal{S},\sigma\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\ell}}\mathcal{H}_{p}(x_{0,\ell}),\quad x\mapsto((\sigma x)_{\ell})_{\ell\in\mathcal{S},\sigma\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\ell}}.

\langle-,-\rangle\colon L\times L\to\mathbb{Z}

Theorem 1.3

(Theorem
3.5
)
.

Denote the image of each
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
in

\prod_{\ell\in\mathcal{S},\overline{\sigma}\in\overline{\mathcal{T}}(\ell)}\mathcal{H}_{p}(\underline{A}_{0,\ell})

In particular, suppose that for each
\ell\in\mathcal{S}

\sigma\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
in

\mathbf{R}_{\mathcal{S},\mathcal{T};p}

In the case where

has CM by a CM field

L^{\prime}
for the reflex field of

determined by the preceding isomorphism. Then we have group homomorphisms

\mathrm{Gal}((L^{\prime})^{\mathrm{ab}}/L^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\mathrm{Art}_{L^{\prime}}}\overline{(L^{\prime})^{\times}}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{L^{\prime},f}^{\times}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{Nm}}\mathbf{G}_{\underline{A}_{0}}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash\mathbf{G}_{\underline{A}_{0}}(\mathbb{A}_{f})

where
\mathrm{Art}_{L^{\prime}}
is the Artin reciprocity map and
\mathrm{Nm}\colon L^{\prime}\rightarrow L
is the reflex type norm. For each prime
\ell\in\mathcal{S}
,
fix a finite set
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{\ell}
of the preimage of
\mathbf{G}_{\underline{A}_{0}}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash\mathbf{G}_{\underline{A}_{0}}^{1}(\mathbb{A}_{f})
under the above map

.
Again we have the simultaneous reduction map

\mathbf{R}_{\mathcal{S},\widetilde{\mathcal{T}};p}\colon\mathcal{H}_{p}(\underline{A}_{0})\to\prod_{\ell\in\mathcal{S},\overline{\sigma}\in\overline{\mathcal{T}}(\ell)}\mathcal{H}_{p}(\underline{A}_{0}),\quad\underline{A}\mapsto((\sigma\underline{A})_{\ell})_{\ell\in\mathcal{S},\sigma\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}}.

(Theorem
7.2
)
.

Suppose that for each
\ell\in\mathcal{S}
, the images of the elements

\mathrm{Gal}((L^{\prime})^{\mathrm{ab}}/L^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\mathrm{Nm}\circ\mathrm{Art}_{L^{\prime}}}\mathbf{G}_{\underline{A}_{0}}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash\mathbf{G}_{\underline{A}_{0}}(\mathbb{A}_{f})\to\mathbf{G}_{\underline{A}_{0}}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash\mathbf{G}_{\underline{A}_{0}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})

2.
Polarized abelian varieties

We recall the notion polarized abelian varieties with level structures. A very convenient reference for this section is
[
Yu10
, §2]
.

We fix a non-zero free
\mathbb{Z}
-module of finite type
L
and write
V=L\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
. We assume that there is a non-degenerate symplectic bilinear pairing

\langle-,-\rangle\colon L\times L\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}.

We define the similitude symplectic group scheme
\mathbf{GSp}_{L}
over
\mathbb{Z}
the symplectic pairing
(L,\langle-,-\rangle)
: for any
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra
R
,

\mathbf{GSp}_{L}(R):=\{(g,r)\in\mathrm{GL}_{R}(L\otimes R)\times R^{\times}\mid\langle gx,gy\rangle=\mu(g)\langle x,y\rangle,\forall x,y\in L\otimes R\}.

Note that
\mathbf{GSp}_{L}
is not necessarily a smooth group scheme over
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{Z})
.
Since
L\neq 0
, the pair
(g,\mu(g))
is uniquely determined by the first component
g
, so we will also write
g
for
(g,\mu(g))
. Moreover
\mu
is the similitude factor of
g
and defines a morphism of group schemes over
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{Z})
:

\mu\colon\mathbf{GSp}_{L}\rightarrow\mathbb{G}_{m}.

The isometry subgroup
\mathbf{Sp}_{L}
of
\mathbf{GSp}_{L}
is given by

\mathbf{Sp}_{L}(R):=\{g\in\mathbf{GSp}_{L}(R)\mid\mu(g)=1\}.

For an abelian scheme
A
over a scheme
S
, we write
A^{\vee}
for the dual abelian scheme of
A
. For two abelian schemes
A,B
over
S
, we write

\mathrm{Hom}_{S}(A,B)^{\circ}=\mathrm{Hom}_{S}(A,B)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}.

Similarly
\mathrm{End}_{S}(A)^{\circ}=\mathrm{End}_{S}(A)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
.

A
polarized abelian scheme over
S
is a pair
\underline{A}=(A,\lambda)
where
A
is an abelian scheme over
S
and
\lambda\colon A\rightarrow A^{\vee}
is a polarization. We assume in the following

\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{Q}}(V)=2\mathrm{dim}(A/S).

In other words, for
S=\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{Z})
, then these
\underline{A}
are objects in the Shimura varieties of PEL type attached to the PEL datum
(\mathbb{Q},\dagger=\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbb{Q}},V,\langle-,-\rangle)
.

Let
\underline{A}=(A,\lambda)
be as above. For any prime number
\ell
, we write
A[\ell^{\infty}]
for the
\ell
-divisible group associated to the abelian scheme
A
and similarly

\underline{A}[\ell^{\infty}]=(A[\ell^{\infty}],\lambda_{\ell}),

the
\ell
-divisible group associated to
\underline{A}
. Here
\lambda_{\ell}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{S}(A[\ell^{\infty}],A^{\vee}[\ell^{\infty}])
is the quasi-polarization induced from
\lambda
(when there is no confusion, we also write
\lambda
for
\lambda_{\ell}
). For any two polarized abelian varieties
\underline{A}_{1}
and
\underline{A}_{2}
over a field
K
, we write

(1)

\mathrm{Q}\text{-}\mathrm{isog}_{K}(\underline{A}_{1},\underline{A}_{2})
(resp,
\mathrm{Isom}_{K}(\underline{A}_{1},\underline{A}_{2})
) for the set of quasi-isogenies (resp, isomorphisms)
\phi\colon A_{1}\rightarrow A_{2}
over
K
such that
\phi^{\ast}\lambda_{2}=\lambda_{1}
.

(2)

For a prime
\ell
,
\mathrm{Q}\text{-}\mathrm{isog}_{K,\ell}(\underline{A}_{1},\underline{A}_{2})
is the subset of
\mathrm{Q}\text{-}\mathrm{isog}_{K}(\underline{A}_{1},\underline{A}_{2})
consisting of
\phi
whose degree is of the form
\ell^{r}
for some
r\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(3)

\mathrm{Q}\text{-}\mathrm{isog}_{K}(\underline{A}_{1}[\ell^{\infty}],\underline{A}_{2}[\ell^{\infty}])
(resp,
\mathrm{Isom}_{K}(\underline{A}_{1}[\ell^{\infty}],\underline{A}_{2}[\ell^{\infty}])
) for the set of quasi-isogenies (resp, isomorphisms)
\phi\colon A_{1}[\ell^{\infty}]\rightarrow A_{2}[\ell^{\infty}]
such that
\phi^{\ast}\lambda_{2}=\lambda_{1}
.

Here
\phi^{\ast}\lambda_{2}:=\phi^{\vee}\lambda_{2}\phi
.

We fix in the following a polarized abelian variety
\underline{A}_{0}=(A_{0},\lambda_{0})
over
K
.
We then denote
\mathbf{G}_{\underline{A}_{0}}
for the group scheme over
\mathbb{Z}
of automorphisms of
\underline{A}_{0}
defined as follows: for any
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra
R
,

\mathbf{G}_{\underline{A}_{0}}(R):=\{g\in\mathrm{End}_{K}(\underline{A}_{0})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}R|g^{\prime}g=\mu(g)\in R^{\times}\}.

Here

is the Rosati involution induced by
\lambda_{0}
.
We write
\mathbf{G}_{\underline{A}_{0}}^{1}
for the subgroup scheme of
\mathbf{G}_{\underline{A}_{0}}
defined by

\mathbf{G}_{\underline{A}_{0}}^{1}(R)=\{g\in\mathbf{G}_{\underline{A}_{0}}(R)\mid\mu(g)=1\}.

It is easy to see

\mathbf{G}_{\underline{A}_{0}}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})\simeq\mathrm{Q}\text{-}\mathrm{isog}_{K}(\underline{A}_{0},\underline{A}_{0}),\quad\mathbf{G}_{\underline{A}_{0}}^{1}(\mathbf{Z}_{\ell})\simeq\mathrm{Q}\text{-}\mathrm{isog}_{K,\ell}(\underline{A}_{0}[\ell^{\infty}],\underline{A}_{0}[\ell^{\infty}]).

From now on we assume
S=\mathrm{Spec}(K)
with
K
an algebraically closed field.

Let
U
be a compact open subgroup of
\mathrm{Isom}_{K}(\underline{A}_{0}[p^{\infty}],\underline{A}_{0}[p^{\infty}])
:

U\subset\mathrm{Isom}_{K}(\underline{A}_{0}[p^{\infty}],\underline{A}_{0}[p^{\infty}]).

A
U
-level structure
on a polarized abelian scheme
\underline{A}=(A,\lambda)
over
K
is a
non-empty

-orbit of isomorphisms in

\mathrm{Isom}_{K}(\underline{A}_{0}[p^{\infty}],\underline{A}[p^{\infty}])

\underline{A}
for the triple
\underline{A}=(A,\lambda,\overline{\eta})
where
(A,\lambda)
is a polarized abelian variety and
\overline{\eta}
is a
U
-level structure on
(A,\lambda)
. Moreover, we write

\underline{A}_{0}=(A_{0},\lambda_{0},\overline{\eta}_{0})

where
\overline{\eta}_{0}

-orbit of the identity in

, we write, for two polarized abelian varieties with

(1)

\mathrm{Q}\text{-}\mathrm{isog}_{K}(\underline{A}_{1},\underline{A}_{2})
(resp,
\mathrm{Q}\text{-}\mathrm{isog}_{K,\ell}(\underline{A}_{1},\underline{A}_{2})

\mathrm{Q}\text{-}\mathrm{isog}_{K}((A_{1},\lambda_{1}),(A_{2},\lambda_{2}))

\mathrm{Q}\text{-}\mathrm{isog}_{K,\ell}((A_{1},\lambda_{1}),(A_{2},\lambda_{2}))

(2)

For a prime
\ell\neq p

\mathrm{Isom}_{K}((A_{1},\lambda_{1})[\ell^{\infty}],(A_{2},\lambda_{2})[\ell^{\infty}])

(3)

\mathrm{Isom}_{K}(\underline{A}_{1}[p^{\infty}],\underline{A}_{2}[p^{\infty}])
(resp.
\mathrm{Isom}_{K}(\underline{A}_{1},\underline{A}_{2})

We write again

Definition 3.1

The

Theorem 5.2
.

For distinct
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathcal{S}

and on

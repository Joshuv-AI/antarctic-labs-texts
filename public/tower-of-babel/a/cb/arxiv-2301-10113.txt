Extremes of regularly varying stochastic volatility fields
Mads Stehr, Anders Rønn–Nielsen

Abstract
We consider a stationary stochastic volatility field Y v ​ Z v Y_{v}Z_{v} with v ∈ ℤ d v\in\mathbb{Z}^{d} , where Z Z is regularly varying and Y Y has lighter tails and is independent of Z Z . We make—relative to existing literature—very general assumptions on the dependence structure of both fields. In particular this allows Y Y to be non-ergodic, in contrast to the typical assumption that it is i.i.d., and Z Z to be given by an infinite moving average. Considering the stochastic volatility field on a (rather general) sequence of increasing index sets, we show the existence and form of a Y Y -dependent extremal functional generalizing the classical extremal index. More precisely, conditioned on the field Y Y , the extremal functional shows exactly how the extremal clustering of the (conditional) stochastic volatility field is given in terms of the extremal clustering of the regularly varying field Z Z and the realization of Y Y . Secondly, we construct two different cluster counting processes on a fixed, full-dimensional set with boundary of Lebesgue measure zero: By means of a coordinate-dependent upscaling of subsets, we systematically count the number of relevant clusters with an extreme observation. We show that both cluster processes converge to a Poisson point process with intensity given in terms of the extremal functional.

Introduction

In this paper we consider, and study the extremal behavior of, a very general stochastic volatility field

X_{v}=Y_{v}Z_{v},\qquad v\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}},\qquad d\in\mathbb{N},

(1)

which, in the case of
d=1
plays an important role in econometrics and mathematical finance; see e.g.
[
1
,
3
]
and references therein. Among many other applications they are used for option pricing, insurance mathematics and time series modeling. Allowing for a non-constant volatility can be seen as a very relevant generalization of the classical Black–Scholes setting, since many empirical studies have shown the necessity of this extension: Observed features of implied volatility such as the volatility smile can only be explained by a non-constant volatility; see e.g.
[
28
]
.

A main assumption in stochastic volatility models is that
(Y_{v})
and
(Z_{v})
are independent. This is in contrast to the popular one-dimensional, i.e.
d=1
, GARCH model, where one of the processes, say,
Z_{v}
will feed into future values
Y_{v+k}
,
k\geq 1
of the other. As both
(Z_{v})
and
(Y_{v})
will in practice be unobserved, the choice between GARCH models or stochastic volatility models will often depend on the varying technical difficulties due the desired application, e.g. estimation and prediction. As we consider a potentially multi-dimensional setting, only the stochastic volatility models have a natural generalization.

So far, most literature on the extremal behavior of stochastic volatility models has focused on the one-dimensional case
d=1
and furthermore assumed that the volatility process, say
(Z_{v})
, is a stationary
\log
-linear Gaussian process, and that the independent noise process
(Y_{v})
consists of i.i.d. random variables with either light- or heavy-tailed marginal distribution; see e.g.
[
4
,
8
,
9
,
17
]
and references therein. In particular, these scenarios imply that the stochastic volatility process
(X_{v})
does not exhibit extremal clustering no-matter the dependence structure of the underlying
\log
-Gaussian volatility.

A, in some sense, more general set-up than the above is found in
[
21
]
where the authors allow the volatility process to have power law tails and then require that the independent noise process has a lighter tail. More specifically, they assume that
(Z_{v})
is a stationary, non-negative and regularly varying process of some index
\alpha>0
, and that the marginal distribution of the i.i.d. noise process
(Y_{v})
satisfy
\mathbb{E}\lvert Y\rvert^{\gamma}<\infty
for some
\gamma>\alpha
. Based on the general result
[
7
, Theorem 2.7]
concerning extremes of stationary processes, they show under standard mixing conditions of the volatility process that extremal clustering of the stochastic volatility process
(X_{v})
is in fact essentially inherited by its volatility process.

The contribution of the present paper in the literature of stochastic volatility models is twofold.
First, we consider the extremal behavior of the field
(X_{v})
on the
d
-dimensional discrete index set
D_{n}\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
, which is allowed to expand in a very general way. Results on extremes of stationary random fields are to the best of the authors’ knowledge mostly formulated under the assumption of
(D_{n})
being a sequence of increasing boxes (
[
13
,
20
,
24
,
29
]
). However, there are recent papers in which extremes are considered under a more general geometric regime; see for instance
[
23
,
25
,
26
]
.
In this paper we simply assume that
D_{n}\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
can be approximated from the in- and outside by unions of certain slowly increasing boxes, where the approximations asymptotically have the same size as
D_{n}
; please see Assumption
1
for details.

Secondly, and perhaps most importantly, we allow for very general volatility and noise fields in the stochastic volatility model (
1
). We assume that the fields
(Y_{v})_{v\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}
and
(Z_{v})_{v\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}
are independent and stationary, however, in contrast to existing literature, we do not require any of them to be i.i.d. It is important to emphasize that we even allow the field
(Y_{v})
to be non-ergodic, which makes long and infinite memory possible within the framework; see the considerations on ergodicity, long memory and mixing in
[
27
, Chapters 2 & 5]
.
We require that
(Z_{v})
can be sufficiently approximated by a sequence of random fields
(Z_{v}^{t})
,
t\in\mathbb{N}
, all of which are assumed to be strongly mixing, regularly varying of index
\alpha>0
, and satisfy an anti-clustering condition in the spirit of e.g.
[
7
,
21
]
. We also assume that
(Y_{v})
has lighter tails than
(Z_{v})
by requiring that
\lvert Y\rvert^{\gamma}<\infty
for some
\gamma>\alpha
; see Assumption
2
for details. Note that we do not assume any of the fields to be non-negative—also in contrast to existing literature. This allow us to model both of the mentioned scenarios from the literature where the volatility field is light-tailed (the volatility being
(Y_{v})
) and heavy-tailed (the volatility being
(Z_{v})
), respectively. In particular, the stochastic volatility field may in fact show extremal clustering inherited by the noise. This special case not present in the literature can for instance be obtained by having a stationary
\log
-Gaussian volatility field
(Y_{v})
and a regularly varying noise
(Z_{v})
.

A stationary random field
(\xi_{v})_{v\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
, that is regularly varying with index
\alpha
(the precise definition of regular variation for random fields is given in Section
2
), is said to have extremal index
\theta
if for a sequence
(a_{n})
of norming constants chosen such that
\lvert D_{n}\rvert^{-1}\sim\mathbb{P}(\xi_{0}>a_{n})
as
n\to\infty
, it holds that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(\max_{v\in D_{n}}\xi_{v}\leq a_{n}x)=\exp(-x^{-\alpha}\theta)

for all
x>0
. Here,
\lvert D_{n}\rvert
denotes the cardinality of
D_{n}
and
\sim
denotes asymptotic equivalence. See e.g.
[
11
,
19
]
for the general definition of the extremal index, beyond the regularly varying case.

Under the assumptions mentioned above on the index sets and fields, our first main result, Theorem
3.1
, states the existence (and form) of an
extremal functional

\eta:\mathbb{R}^{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\bigl(\max_{v\in D_{n}}Y_{v}Z_{v}\leq a_{n}x\mid(Y_{v})=(y_{v})\bigr)=\exp\Bigl(-x^{-\alpha}\eta((y_{v}))\Bigr)

(2)

for all
x>0
, where, again,
(a_{n})
is a sequence chosen such that
\lvert D_{n}\rvert^{-1}\sim\mathbb{P}(Z_{0}>a_{n})
as
n\to\infty
.
The term
extremal functional
comes from the fact that, up to a finite and non-vanishing constant, the functional
\eta(\cdot)
generalizes the classical extremal index. In fact we show that if
(Y_{v})
is just ergodic,
\eta(\cdot)=\eta
is a constant related to the extremal index
\theta_{X}
of the stochastic volatility field
(X_{v})
by

\theta_{X}=\frac{\eta}{\lim_{n\to\infty}\lvert D_{n}\rvert\,\mathbb{P}(X_{0}>a_{n})}.

We furthermore demonstrate how the extremal functional can be derived using the spectral distribution of
(Z_{v})
and the distribution of
(Y_{v})
.

It is well known in the literature that a regularly varying random field satisfying certain mixing and anti-clustering conditions has a well defined extremal index that furthermore is determined by its spectral distribution; see the seminal paper
[
7
]
for the one-dimensional regularly varying case and
[
26
,
29
]
for conditions ensuring an extremal index for random fields that are not necessarily regularly varying. In
[
21
]
a one-dimensional stochastic volatility model of the form
X_{t}=Y_{t}Z_{t}
is studied, where
(Z_{t})
is regularly varying and
(Y_{t})
is i.i.d. Assuming that
(Z_{t})
is on the form of an exponential AR(1)-process, a finite moving average or given by a stochastic recurrence equation, it is shown
(under further model-specific assumptions)
that
(X_{t})
satisfies relevant mixing and anti-clustering conditions that ensures the existence of the extremal index. Furthermore, more concrete model-specific formulas for determining the index are given.

The present paper supplies a substantial generalization of the results from
[
21
]
: The i.i.d.-requirement on the
(Y_{v})
-process is reduced to that of stationarity, and the field
(Z_{v})
simply needs to be suitably approximated by mixing fields obeying to an anti-clustering condition.

Only requiring stationarity of
(Y_{v})
instead of, e.g., ergodicity makes it possible for
(X_{v})
to have long or even infinite memory. This is reflected in the result, where we find a possibly random
(Y_{v})
-dependent extremal index, when conditioning on
(Y_{v})
. That means that the long/infinite memory coming from
(Y_{v})
in this modeling framework enters in a clear and specified way in the extremal behavior. Another example from the litterature, where the interplay between long memory and extremal theory is studied, can be found in
[
6
]
. Here a stable random field with long memory is constructed, and a functional extremal theorem is derived with a limit that is not necessarily the Fréchet distribution.

We make assumptions on
(Z_{v})
requiring that it can be approximated by fields exhibiting a sufficient mixing and anti-clustering behavior. Although this set of assumptions at first may look elaborate and specific, it is in fact very general. It covers easily the important example, where
(Z_{v})
is given as an infinite moving average: The field can be approximated by finite moving averages, that clearly satisfies requirements on the dependence structure. On the other hand, the set of assumptions also makes solutions to stochastic recurrence equations, as e.g. the volatility process of a GARCH(1,1) model, possible to handle. Here
(Z_{v})
will in itself meet the necessary requirements regarding mixing and anti-clustering behavior.

As mentioned above, a usual proof technique when showing extremal results for stochastic volatility processes is to show that the full product process
(X_{v})=(Y_{v}Z_{v})
satisfies certain mixing and anti-clustering conditions. The approach in the present paper will be rather different, and this is the reason for the increased generality of the results: We consider
(X_{v})

conditioned
on
(Y_{v})=(y_{v})
and then use a spatial version of the Birkhoff–Khinchin theorem to handle the
(y_{v})
-dependence. This makes it possible to only require (approximate) mixing and anti-clustering of
(Z_{v})
.

A second set of results (see Section
4
) concern the asymptotic distribution of a range of cluster counting processes. Here, we assume that
C\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
is a fixed, full-dimensional set with boundary of Lebesgue measure zero. We then consider the sequence of index sets
(D_{n})
given by

D_{n}=(\boldsymbol{c}_{n}C)\cap{\mathbb{Z}^{d}},

(3)

where
\boldsymbol{c}_{n}
is a
d
-dimensional vector with each element tending to
\infty
, and
\boldsymbol{c}_{n}C
denotes the coordinate wise multiplication of
C
with the elements of
\boldsymbol{c}_{n}
. First we construct two cluster counting processes on the fixed set
C
as follows: Let
\Phi_{n}\subseteq D_{n}
denote the (random) set of indices
v\in D_{n}
where
X_{v}=Y_{v}Z_{v}
has an exceedance over the threshold
a_{n}x
for fixed
x>0
, i.e.

\Phi_{n}=\{v\in D_{n}\>:\>Y_{v}Z_{v}>a_{n}x\}.

Then, according to some
cluster rule
, we make a partition of
\Phi_{n}
into a (random) number
\Gamma_{n}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
of clusters
\mathcal{C}_{1}^{n},\dots,\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma_{n}}^{n}
. For any measurable set
A\subseteq C
we then construct the cluster counting processes by counting the number of clusters
\mathcal{C}_{i}^{n}
intersecting
\boldsymbol{c}_{n}A
, that is

N_{n}(A)=\sum_{i=1}^{\Gamma_{n}}1_{\{\mathcal{C}_{i}^{n}\cap\boldsymbol{c}_{n}A\neq\emptyset\}}.

(4)

The first cluster rule is a spatial version of the classical one-dimensional definition, e.g. seen in
[
12
]
and
[
18
]
: We divide
{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
into disjoint (increasing)
boxes and count a box as a cluster if there is an
a_{n}x
-exceedance which is within the box and within
D_{n}
. Typically, for
d=1
, such a counting process is defined on the unit interval
(0,1]
via a rescaling of the indices, where our geometric
construction in (
3
) allow us to define it on a much more general set
C
. The second cluster counting process, similar to that of
[
26
]
, is relatively new in the context of extreme value theory. The process is based on the more intuitive rule that a cluster comprises of the indices in
\Phi_{n}
which are relatively close to one another.
Being close in this setting corresponds to being within a box of the same size as in the first cluster process. For both cluster rules we show that the counting process, conditioned on
(Y_{v})=(y_{v})
converges (in the vague topology) towards a homogeneous Poisson process on
C
with intensity
x^{-\alpha}\eta((y_{v}))
, where
\eta(\cdot)
is the extremal functional appearing in (
2
); see
[
14
, Chapter 4]
for details on vague convergence.

Related to the cluster counting process in (
4
) we define a similar process defined on the original scale in
{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
,

L_{n}(A)=\sum_{i=1}^{\Gamma_{n}}1_{\{\mathcal{C}_{i}^{n}\cap A\neq\emptyset\}}

for all
A\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
. Utilizing the convergence above we show (for either cluster rule) the following joint convergence:
If
(B_{n}^{1}),\dots,(B_{n}^{G})
are disjoint sequences of subsets of
D_{n}\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
each satisfying a slightly modified geometric assumption (see Assumption
3
), then, conditioned on
(Y_{v})=(y_{v})
,

\bigl(L_{n}(B_{n}^{1}),\dots,L_{n}(B_{n}^{G})\bigr)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{D}}}{{\to}}\bigl(L^{1},\ldots,L^{G}\bigr),

where
(L^{1},\dots,L^{G})
are independent random variables each being Poisson distributed with parameter
x^{-\alpha}\eta((y_{v}))\lim_{n\to\infty}\lvert B_{n}^{g}\rvert/\lvert D_{n}\rvert
(provided that the limit exists). Here
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{D}}}{{\to}}
denotes weak convergence.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section

\frac{\mathbb{P}(\lVert\boldsymbol{R}^{m}\rVert>xt,\boldsymbol{R}^{m}/\lVert\boldsymbol{R}^{m}\rVert\in{}\cdot{})}{\mathbb{P}(\lVert\boldsymbol{R}^{m}\rVert>t)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle v}}{{\to}}x^{-\alpha}\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{m}\in{}\cdot{})

. Here,

3
Results on the extremal functional

In this section we formally present the underlying assumptions of the paper after
which we can present the first set of main results.

For the sequence
(D_{n})
of index sets under consideration, we assume that they increase and can be sufficiently approximated by a union of slowly increasing boxes. To formally define this, let
\boldsymbol{t}_{n}=(t_{n,1},\ldots,t_{n,d})
be a sequence in
\mathbb{N}^{d}
of scaling vectors, and let
x_{n}=(x_{n,1},\ldots,x_{n,d})
with
x_{n,\ell}\leq t_{n,\ell}
for
\ell=1,\ldots,d
be a sequence in
(0,\infty)^{d}
of translation (or shift) vectors. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we then define the system of boxes
(J_{z}^{\boldsymbol{t}_{n},x_{n}})_{z\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}
by

J_{z}^{\boldsymbol{t}_{n},x_{n}}=\Bigl(x_{n}+\boldsymbol{t}_{n}\bigl(z+[0,1)^{d}\bigr)\Bigr)\cap{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}.

For a sequence
(D_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of sets in
{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
we define the approximations

D_{n,\boldsymbol{t}_{n},x_{n}}^{-}=\bigcup_{J^{\boldsymbol{t}_{n},x_{n}}_{z}\subseteq D_{n}}J^{\boldsymbol{t}_{n},x_{n}}_{z}\qquad\text{and}\qquad D_{n,\boldsymbol{t}_{n},x_{n}}^{+}=\bigcup_{J^{\boldsymbol{t}_{n},x_{n}}_{z}\cap D_{n}\neq\emptyset}J^{\boldsymbol{t}_{n},x_{n}}_{z},

which obviously satisfy

D_{n,\boldsymbol{t}_{n},x_{n}}^{-}\subseteq D_{n}\subseteq D_{n,\boldsymbol{t}_{n},x_{n}}^{+}.

As the choice of scalings
(\boldsymbol{t}_{n})
and translations
(x_{n})
will be given and fixed by Assumption
1
below, we will write
J_{z}^{n}
,
D_{n}^{-}
and
D_{n}^{+}
for ease of notation.

Furthermore, we say that a sequence
(D_{n})
is regular if there exists an increasing sequence of boxes
D_{n}^{\prime}
, i.e. on the form
\bigtimes_{\ell}[a_{\ell},b_{\ell}]\cap{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
,
such that for all
n
it holds that
D_{n}\subseteq D_{n}^{\prime}
and
\lvert D_{n}^{\prime}\rvert\leq c\,\lvert D_{n}\rvert
uniformly in
n
for some finite constant
c
.
For a regular sequence
(D_{n})
, we write
D_{n}\to{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
, if the corresponding sequence
(D_{n}^{\prime})
satisfies

\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}D_{n}^{\prime}={\mathbb{Z}^{d}}.

Note in particular that
\lvert D_{n}\rvert\to\infty
for a regular sequence
(D_{n})
satisfying
D_{n}\to{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
as

.

Assumption 1
.

The sequence of index sets
(D_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
is regular with
D_{n}~\to~{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
,
and there exist
(\boldsymbol{t}_{n})
and
(x_{n})

\boldsymbol{t}_{n}\to\infty

\boldsymbol{t}_{n}^{*}=o(\lvert D_{n}\rvert)
,
such that

\frac{\lvert D_{n}^{+}\rvert-\lvert D_{n}^{-}\rvert}{\lvert D_{n}\rvert}\to 0

(6)

as
n\to\infty
.

Assumption
1
can be seen as a relaxation of the geometrical assumption on the sequence of index sets in
[
26
, Section 2]
, where a certain type of convexity is required. We let
\preceq
be an arbitrary translation invariant total order on
{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
(e.g. the lexicographical order) and define

{A_{v}^{n}}=\{z\in v+(\boldsymbol{t}_{n}[-1,1]^{d}\cap{\mathbb{Z}^{d}})\,:\,v\prec z\}

and

{A_{v}^{(m)}}=\{z\in v+([-m,m]^{d}\cap{\mathbb{Z}^{d}})\,:\,v\prec z\},

see
[
26
]
for similar definitions.

Assumption 2
.

We say that the stochastic volatility field
(Y_{v}Z_{v})_{v\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}
satisfies Assumption
2
if all of the following conditions are satisfied:

The field
(Z_{v})_{v\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}
is stationary and it satisfies the tail-balance condition

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}(Z_{0}<-x)}{\mathbb{P}(Z_{0}>x)}=\frac{1-p}{p}

(7)

for some
p\in(0,1]
. Furthermore, it can be approximated by a sequence of fields
(Z_{v}^{t})_{v\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}
,
t\in\mathbb{N}
, with the following properties (some of which are relative to the geometry given by Assumption
1
):

(i)

(Z_{v}^{t})_{v\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}
is stationary and regularly varying with index
\alpha>0
,

(ii)

(Z_{v}^{t})_{{v\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}}
is strongly mixing with mixing parameters
(\alpha^{t}(\boldsymbol{h}))_{\boldsymbol{h}\in(0,\infty)^{d}}
such that there exists a sequence
(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{n}^{t})
with
\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{n}^{t}\to\infty
and
\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{n}^{t}=o(\boldsymbol{t}_{n})
, where

\alpha^{t}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{n}^{t})\,\lvert D_{n}\rvert/\boldsymbol{t}_{n}^{*}\to 0,

(iii)

(Z_{v}^{t})_{v\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}
satisfies the anti-clustering condition

\lim_{m\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\Bigl(\max_{{A_{0}^{n}}\setminus{A_{0}^{(m)}}}\lvert Z_{v}^{t}\rvert>sa_{n}\mid\lvert Z_{0}^{t}\rvert>sa_{n}\Bigr)=0

for all
s>0
, where
\lvert D_{n}\rvert^{-1}\sim\mathbb{P}(Z_{0}^{t}>a_{n})
,

(iv)

there exist
C_{t}^{+}>0
and
C_{t}^{-}>0
such that

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}(Z_{0}^{t}>x)}{\mathbb{P}(Z_{0}>x)}=C_{t}^{+}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}(Z_{0}^{t}<-x)}{\mathbb{P}(Z_{0}<-x)}=C_{t}^{-},

(8)

where
C_{t}^{+}
and
C_{t}^{-}
additionally satisfy

\lim_{t\to\infty}C_{t}^{+}=\lim_{t\to\infty}C_{t}^{-}=1,

(9)

(v)

and lastly

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to\infty}\liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}(Z_{0}^{t}>x,Z_{0}>x)}{\mathbb{P}(Z_{0}>x)}=\liminf_{t\to\infty}\liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}(Z_{0}^{t}<-x,Z_{0}<-x)}{\mathbb{P}(Z_{0}<-x)}=1.

(10)

The field
(Y_{v})_{v\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}
is stationary and independent of
(Z_{v})
and all the approximating fields
(Z_{v}^{t})
,
t\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover

\exists\ \gamma>\alpha:\quad\mathbb{E}\lvert Y_{0}\rvert^{\gamma}<\infty.

(11)

Note that
(i)
and
(iii)
above imply that the right tail of

,
{v\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}
, is (marginally) regularly varying with index
\alpha
. In particular, there exists a sequence of norming constants
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

\lvert D_{n}\rvert\mathbb{P}(Z_{0}>a_{n}x)\to x^{-\alpha}

for all

as

. Since

is regularly varying this is equivalent to the asymptotic equality
\lvert D_{n}\rvert^{-1}\sim\mathbb{P}(Z_{0}>a_{n})
. This particular sequence

will be used in the results below, and with this choice of sequence,
(iii)
will still be satisfied for each
(Z_{v}^{t})
because of

.

There are many cases in which certain elements of the assumption are obviously satisfied. For example, if the field
(Z_{v})
itself satisfies the regular variation, mixing, and anti-clustering required by

, one can choose

for all
{v\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}
and

. Furthermore, if the approximating fields
(Z_{v}^{t})
are
m_{t}
-dependent for some

, that is, if the families

and

are independent for all

-separated sets

, then

and

is so-called

-

, the approximating fields are

-dependent and furthermore

Example 1
.

Let

be an i.i.d. field of regularly varying random variables of index

In a one-dimensional setting, i.e.

As opposed to our model (

Defining

More generally, a stochastic process

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(D_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of sets in
{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
satisfying Assumption
1
. Let
(Y_{v}Z_{v})_{v\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}
be a stochastic volatility field satisfying Assumption
2
, and choose the norming constants
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\lvert D_{n}\rvert^{-1}\sim\mathbb{P}(Z_{0}>a_{n})
. Then, for almost all realizations
(y_{v})
of
(Y_{v})
, it holds that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\bigl(\max_{v\in D_{n}}Y_{v}Z_{v}\leq a_{n}x\mid(Y_{v})=(y_{v})\bigr)=\exp\Bigl(-x^{-\alpha}\eta((y_{v}))\Bigr)

(14)

for all
x>0
. Here the functional
\eta:\mathbb{R}^{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\to[0,\infty)
is given (and is well defined) by the limit

\eta((y_{v}))=\lim_{t\to\infty}\lim_{K\to\infty}\lim_{m\to\infty}\eta^{t,K,m}((y_{v}))

with

\displaystyle\eta^{t,K,m}((y_{v}))

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{E}^{\boldsymbol{Y}}\Bigl[\mathbb{E}\Bigl(\bigl((Y_{0}1_{\{\lvert Y_{0}\rvert\leq K\}}\Theta^{t,m}_{0})_{+}^{\alpha}-(\underset{v\in A^{(m)}_{0}}{\max}Y_{v}1_{\{\lvert Y_{v}\rvert\leq K\}}\Theta^{t,m}_{v})_{+}^{\alpha}\bigr)_{+}\mid\boldsymbol{Y}=\hat{\boldsymbol{Y}}\Big)\mid\hat{\mathbb{I}}\Bigr]((y_{v}))}{\mathbb{E}(\Theta_{0}^{t,m})_{+}^{\alpha}},

where
(\Theta^{t,m}_{v})_{v\in B^{(m)}}
represents the spectral field of
(Z^{t}_{v})_{v\in B^{(m)}}
.

Corollary
3.2
below demonstrates that the extremal functional

satisfies properties similar to properties met by the classical extremal index under sufficient mixing and anti-clustering conditions. See e.g. the discussions in
[

, Section 5]
,
[

, Section 4]
and
[

, Section 4]

Corollary 3.2
.

Let the assumptions of Theorem
3.1
be satisfied. Then the functional
\eta:\mathbb{R}^{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\to[0,\infty)
can equivalently be found as

\displaystyle\eta((y_{v}))

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{{J_{z}^{n}}\subseteq D_{n}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(\max_{v\in{J_{z}^{n}}}y_{v}Z_{v}>a_{n}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{{J_{z}^{n}}\cap D_{n}\neq\emptyset}\mathbb{P}\bigl(\max_{v\in{J_{z}^{n}}}y_{v}Z_{v}>a_{n}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{v\in D_{n}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(\max_{u\in{A_{v}^{n}}}y_{u}Z_{u}\leq a_{n}<y_{v}Z_{v}\bigr)

as
n\to\infty
.

Next we consider the special case, where

(15)

as

for all

.

4
Results on cluster counting processes

In this section we consider only index sets
D_{n}
given as follows:
Let
C\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
be a bounded set with non-empty interior
\mathrm{int}\,C\neq\emptyset
satisfying that the boundary has Lebesgue measure zero in the sense that
\lvert\mathrm{cl}\,C\setminus\mathrm{int}\,C\rvert=0
. Without loss of generality we may assume that
C
has unit volume
\lvert C\rvert=1
. Let
\boldsymbol{c}_{n}\to\infty
and
\boldsymbol{k}_{n}\to\infty
be any pair of sequences of vectors satisfying
\boldsymbol{k}_{n}=o(\boldsymbol{c}_{n})
, and define

D_{n}=\boldsymbol{c}_{n}C\cap{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
\boldsymbol{t}_{n}
be given by its entries
t_{n,\ell}=\lfloor c_{n,\ell}/k_{n,\ell}\rfloor
, where
\lfloor\cdot\rfloor
is the floor function. Then the sequence
(D_{n})
satisfies Assumption
1
with this choice of scalings
\boldsymbol{t}_{n}
and arbitrary translations
x_{n}
. In particular, the approximation of
D_{n}
is constructed with boxes
{J_{z}^{n}}\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
of side-lengths
t_{n,\ell}
.

As mentioned in the introduction, we will construct two counting processes,

N_{n}
and
\tilde{N}_{n}
, each counting the number of clusters of indices at which the stochastic volatility field
(Y_{v}Z_{v})_{v\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}
exceeds the threshold
a_{n}x
.
Here, as in the previous section, the norming constants
(a_{n})
are chosen such that

\lvert D_{n}\rvert\mathbb{P}(Z_{0}>a_{n})\to 1\qquad\text{or equivalently}\qquad\boldsymbol{c}_{n}^{*}\mathbb{P}(Z_{0}>a_{n})\to 1

as
n\to\infty
. According to a given cluster rule, both counting processes are constructed by making a partition of the random set

\Phi_{n}=\{v\in D_{n}\>:\>Y_{v}Z_{v}>a_{n}x\}

into a random number of clusters.

First we define the cluster measure
N_{n}
on
C
by counting the box
{J_{z}^{n}}
as a cluster if there is an
a_{n}x
-exceedance within
{J_{z}^{n}}\cap D_{n}
, and if such an exceedance lies in the
\boldsymbol{c}_{n}
-upscaled set at hand, i.e.

N_{n}(A)=\sum_{z\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}1_{\{{J_{z}^{n}}\cap\Phi_{n}\cap\boldsymbol{c}_{n}A\neq\emptyset\}}

for all
x>0
and all
A\in\mathcal{B}(C)
. Note that this indeed has the general form introduced in (
4
) with well-defined clusters
{J_{z}^{n}}\cap\Phi_{n}
.

Secondly we define the cluster measure
\tilde{N}_{n}
on
C
, which is based on the more intuitive interpretation of a cluster as being a collection of indices sufficiently close to one another at which there is an exceedance of the threshold
a_{n}x
.
We then say that the elements
u_{1},\dots,u_{R}\in\Phi_{n}
form a cluster if (possibly after a reordering) it for all
i=2,\dots,R
holds that

\lVert\boldsymbol{t}_{n}^{-1}(u_{i}-u_{i-1})\rVert<1,

and if

\lVert\boldsymbol{t}_{n}^{-1}(u_{i}-v)\rVert\geq 1

for all
i=1,\dots,R
and all
v\in\Phi_{n}\setminus\{u_{1},\dots,u_{R}\}
. Note that this cluster rule is comparable to the first one, as it simply collects all indices with an
a_{n}x
-exceedances which can be covered by an overlapping sequence of non-empty
J^{n}
-boxes.

If
\Phi_{n}\neq\emptyset
, this uniquely partitions
\Phi_{n}
into
\Gamma_{n}\geq 1
disjoint clusters
\mathcal{C}_{1}^{n},\dots,\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma_{n}}^{n}
(with arbitrary ordering). We now define the random point measure
\tilde{N}_{n}
on
C
as the number of clusters
\mathcal{C}_{i}^{n}
intersecting the
\boldsymbol{c}_{n}
-upscaled set at hand, i.e.

\tilde{N}_{n}(A)=\sum_{i=1}^{\Gamma_{n}}1_{\{\mathcal{C}_{i}^{n}\cap\boldsymbol{c}_{n}A\neq\emptyset\}}

for all
A\in\mathcal{B}(C)
, with the convention that
\tilde{N}_{n}(A)=0
if
\Phi_{n}=\emptyset
(that is,
\Gamma_{n}=0
).

For the Theorems
4.1
and
4.2
below, we refer to Theorem
3.1
and Corollary
3.3
for the definition of the extremal functional
\eta((y_{v}))
and the corresponding constant
\eta
in the case of ergodic
(Y_{v})
. However, in a number of special cases, it may be more convenient to consult Corollaries
3.4
–
3.5
for, in the relevant situation, equivalent definitions.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
C\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
and
(D_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be as above, and let
(Y_{v}Z_{v})_{v\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}
be a stochastic volatility field satisfying Assumption 2. For almost all realizations
(y_{v})
of
(Y_{v})
it holds that

\displaystyle N_{n}({}\cdot{})\mid(Y_{v})=(y_{v})\overset{v}{\to}N({}\cdot{};(y_{v}))\qquad\text{and}

(17)

\displaystyle\tilde{N}_{n}({}\cdot{})\mid(Y_{v})=(y_{v})\overset{v}{\to}N({}\cdot{};(y_{v}))

as

, where

is a homogeneous Poisson measure on
C
with intensity
x^{-\alpha}\eta((y_{v}))
.

If additionally the field
(Y_{v})
is ergodic, then (unconditionally)

N_{n}({}\cdot{})\overset{v}{\to}N({}\cdot{})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\tilde{N}_{n}({}\cdot{})\overset{v}{\to}N({}\cdot{})

(18)

as
n\to\infty
, where

is a homogeneous Poisson measure on
C
with intensity

.

In contrast to the cluster measures considered so far, which are defined on
C
by means of a
\boldsymbol{c}_{n}
-upscaling, we can naturally also consider corresponding cluster counting measures defined on the original index set scale
{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
as follows:

L_{n}(A)=\sum_{z\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}1_{\{{J_{z}^{n}}\cap\Phi_{n}\cap A\neq\emptyset\}}

and

\tilde{L}_{n}(A)=\sum_{i=1}^{\Gamma_{n}}1_{\{\mathcal{C}_{i}^{n}\cap A\neq\emptyset\}}

for
A\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
. Using these processes, we can count the number of clusters in more general sequences of sets contained in
D_{n}=\boldsymbol{c}_{n}C\cap{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
. However, it turns out that we need a slight adjustment of the former geometrical Assumption
1
. With the vector
\boldsymbol{c}_{n}
in mind, we define for each

the side-lengths

\tilde{t}_{n,k}^{\ell}=\frac{c_{n,\ell}}{k^{1/d}}

Theorem 4.2

Let

T_{\ell}\big((x_{v_{1},\ldots,v_{d}})_{(v_{1},\ldots,v_{d})\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}\big)=\big((x_{v_{1},\ldots,v_{\ell-1},v_{\ell}+1,v_{\ell+1},\ldots,v_{d}})_{(v_{1},\ldots,v_{d})\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}\big)

for each
\ell=1,\ldots,d
. Let
\hat{\mathbb{I}}
be the invariant
\sigma
-algebra on
S
, i.e. the
\sigma
-algebra consisting of all sets in
\mathcal{B}(S)
invariant to
T_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,d
.

For a random field
\boldsymbol{Y}=(Y_{v})_{v}
on
(S,\mathcal{B}(S))
with distribution
\boldsymbol{Y}(\mathbb{P})
and a measurable function
h:S\to\mathbb{R}
, we let
\mathbb{E}^{\boldsymbol{Y}}(h\mid\hat{\mathbb{I}})
denote the conditional expectation on
(S,\mathcal{B}(S),\boldsymbol{Y}(\mathbb{P}))

of
h
given
\hat{\mathbb{I}}
. When the concrete definition of
h
is relevant, the conditional expectation will be written on the form
\mathbb{E}^{\boldsymbol{Y}}(h((\hat{Y}_{v}))\mid\hat{\mathbb{I}})
, where
\hat{\boldsymbol{Y}}=(\hat{Y}_{v})_{v\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}
is the identity map on
(S,\mathcal{B}(S))
.

It should be noted that
\mathbb{E}^{\boldsymbol{Y}}(h\mid\hat{\mathbb{I}})
is an
\hat{\mathbb{I}}
-measurable function
S\to\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\boldsymbol{Y}=(Y_{v})_{v\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}
be a stationary random field, and assume that
(D_{n})
is a sequence satisfying Assumption
1
. If
h:S\to\mathbb{R}
is a measurable function satisfying
\mathbb{E}\lvert h((Y_{u})_{u\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}})\rvert<\infty
, then it holds that

\frac{1}{\lvert D_{n}\rvert}\sum_{v\in D_{n}}h((y_{u+v})_{u\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}})\to\mathbb{E}^{\boldsymbol{Y}}(h\mid\hat{\mathbb{I}})((y_{u})_{u\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}})

for almost all realizations
(y_{v})_{v}
of
(Y_{v})_{v}
.

We say that
\boldsymbol{Y}=(Y_{v})_{v}
is ergodic, if
\hat{\mathbb{I}}
is trivial. That is
\boldsymbol{Y}(\mathbb{P})(A)\in\{0,1\}
for all
A\in\hat{\mathbb{I}}
. Note that if
\boldsymbol{Y}
is ergodic, then the limit is non-random, i.e. independent of the specific realization of
\boldsymbol{Y}
.

In the case, where
(D_{n})
are boxes in
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
, Lemma
A.1
is identical to
[
16
, Theorem 6.2.8]
. Extending that theorem to boxes in
{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
follows by dividing the index set along the coordinate axes into
2^{d}
subsets and applying the result to each sequence of subsets separately. That the conditional expectation in the limit is the same in all sub-directions follows from the fact that, for each
\ell=1,\ldots,d
, invariance to
T_{\ell}
is equivalent to invariance to
T_{\ell}^{-1}
.

Extending the result to allow for more general sequences
(D_{n})
, as given in Assumption
1
, is possible, since the proof of
[
16
, Theorem 6.2.8]
only relies on
[
16
, Corollary 6.2.7]
, which is clearly satisfied in the extended framework as well, and on the geometrical requirement

\frac{\lvert D_{n}\Delta(D_{n}+\boldsymbol{e}_{\ell})\rvert}{\lvert D_{n}\rvert}\to 0

(36)

for each
\ell=1,\ldots,d
. Here
A\Delta B=(A\cup B)\setminus(A\cap B)
and
\boldsymbol{e}_{\ell}
denotes the unit vector in direction
\ell
.

To see that (
36
) is satisfied, let
K
be the number of neighboring boxes in all directions, including via edges and corners, of the box
J_{0}^{n}
. Now define
E_{n}
to be the union of
D_{n}^{+}\setminus D_{n}^{-}
with all its neighboring boxes, and note that
\lvert E_{n}\rvert\leq K\lvert D_{n}^{+}\setminus D_{n}^{-}\rvert
. Also note that for
n
large enough it holds that

D_{n}\Delta(D_{n}+\boldsymbol{e}_{\ell})\subseteq E_{n}.

Now (
36
) follows from (
6
).
∎

B
Results on regular variation

In this section we derive a limit of

\sum_{v\in D_{n}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{yZ}({A_{v}^{(m)}})\leq a_{n}x<y_{v}Z_{v}\bigr)

for almost all realizations
(y_{v})
of
(Y_{v})
, and a limit for the same expression with the approximating fields
(Z_{v}^{t})
for each
t\in\mathbb{N}
. The proof strategy will be to use the ergodic result Lemma
A.1
from Appendix
A
in combination with the spectral behavior of
(Z_{v})
. There will be some similarities to the proof of the multivariate version of Breiman’s lemma. See e.g. the proof of
[
2
, Proposition A.1]
. The results will be formulated and proven in terms of a general field
(R_{v})
acting as a place-holder for either
(Z_{v})
or
(Z_{v}^{t})
.

Lemma B.1
.

Let
(R_{v})_{v\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}
be a stationary and regularly varying random field with index
\alpha
, and, for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, let
(\Theta_{v}^{m})_{v\in B^{(m)}}
represent the spectral field of
\boldsymbol{R}^{m}=(R_{v})_{v\in B^{(m)}}
. Furthermore, let
(y_{v})_{v\in B^{(m)}}
be real constants. If
(a_{n})
is chosen such that
\lvert D_{n}\rvert^{-1}\sim\mathbb{P}(R_{0}>a_{n})
, then

\lvert D_{n}\rvert\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{yR}({A_{0}^{(m)}})>a_{n}x\bigr)\to x^{-\alpha}\frac{\mathbb{E}[(\max_{v\in{A_{0}^{(m)}}}y_{v}\Theta_{v}^{m})_{+}^{\alpha}]}{\mathbb{E}(\Theta_{0}^{m})_{+}^{\alpha}}

as
n\to\infty
.

The result is trivial in the case with
y_{v}=0
for all
v\in B^{(m)}
, so it can without loss of generality be assumed that
y_{v}\neq 0
for at least one
v\in B^{(m)}
. With standard extension arguments it follows from (
5
) that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{P}(M_{yR}({A_{0}^{(m)}})>a_{n}x)}{\mathbb{P}(\lVert\boldsymbol{R}^{m}\rVert>a_{n})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{P}\bigl(\max_{v\in{A_{0}^{(m)}}}\big(y_{v}\frac{R_{v}}{\lVert\boldsymbol{R}^{m}\rVert}\big)_{+}\lVert\boldsymbol{R}^{m}\rVert>a_{n}x\bigr)}{\mathbb{P}(\lVert\boldsymbol{R}^{m}\rVert>a_{n})}

\displaystyle\to x^{-\alpha}\mathbb{E}((\max_{v\in{A_{0}^{(m)}}}y_{v}\Theta_{v}^{m})_{+}^{\alpha})

and

\frac{\mathbb{P}(R_{0}>a_{n})}{\mathbb{P}(\lVert\boldsymbol{R}^{m}\rVert>a_{n})}=\frac{\mathbb{P}\bigl(\bigl(\tfrac{R_{0}}{\lVert\boldsymbol{R}^{m}\rVert}\bigr)_{+}\lVert\boldsymbol{R}^{m}\rVert>a_{n})}{\mathbb{P}(\lVert\boldsymbol{R}^{m}\rVert>a_{n})}\to\mathbb{E}(\Theta_{0}^{m})_{+}^{\alpha}.

(37)

Now the desired result follows from the choice
\lvert D_{n}\rvert\mathbb{P}(R_{0}>a_{n})\to 1
.
∎

Lemma B.2
.

Let
(R_{v})_{v\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}
be a stationary and regularly varying random field with index
\alpha
, and, for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, let
(\Theta_{v}^{m})_{v\in B^{(m)}}
represent the spectral field of
\boldsymbol{R}^{m}=(R_{v})_{v\in B^{(m)}}
. Let
(Y_{v})
be a stationary random field such that
\mathbb{E}\lvert Y_{0}\rvert^{\gamma}<\infty
for some
\gamma>\alpha
. Assume that
(Y_{v})
is independent of
(R_{v})
and
(\Theta^{m}_{v})_{v\in B^{(m)}}
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
. If
(a_{n})
is chosen such that
\lvert D_{n}\rvert^{-1}\sim\mathbb{P}(R_{0}>a_{n})
, then for almost all realizations
(y_{v})
of
(Y_{v})
it holds that

\sum_{v\in D_{n}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{yR}({A_{v}^{(m)}})\leq a_{n}x<y_{v}R_{v})\to x^{-\alpha}\eta_{\boldsymbol{R}^{m}}((y_{v})),

where

\eta_{\boldsymbol{R}^{m}}((y_{v}))=\frac{\mathbb{E}^{\boldsymbol{Y}}\Big[\mathbb{E}\Bigl(\bigl((Y_{0}\Theta^{m}_{0})_{+}^{\alpha}-(\max_{v\in{A_{0}^{(m)}}}Y_{v}\Theta^{m}_{v})_{+}^{\alpha}\bigr)_{+}\mid\boldsymbol{Y}=\hat{\boldsymbol{Y}}\Bigr)\mid\hat{\mathbb{I}}\Big]((y_{v}))}{\mathbb{E}(\Theta_{0}^{m})_{+}^{\alpha}}.

We write

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in D_{n}}\mathbb{P}(M_{yR}(A^{(m)}_{v})\leq a_{n}x<y_{v}R_{v})

(38)

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in D_{n}}\mathbb{P}(M_{yR}(A^{(m)}_{v}\cup\{v\})>a_{n}x)-\sum_{v\in D_{n}}\mathbb{P}(M_{yR}(A^{(m)}_{v})>a_{n}x)

and handle the two terms separately but in the same way. To keep notation simple, we focus on the second term.
Let
\boldsymbol{y}_{v}^{m}=(y_{z})_{z\in B_{v}^{(m)}}
and
\boldsymbol{R}_{v}^{m}=(R_{z})_{z\in B_{v}^{(m)}}
for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
v\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
, where
B_{v}^{(m)}
is defined in Section
2
. For
K\in\mathbb{N}
we now have

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in D_{n}}\mathbb{P}(M_{yR}(A^{(m)}_{v})>a_{n}x)

(39)

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in D_{n}}1_{\{\lVert\boldsymbol{y}^{m}_{v}\rVert<K\}}\mathbb{P}(M_{yR}(A^{(m)}_{v})>a_{n}x)

\displaystyle+\sum_{v\in D_{n}}1_{\{\lVert\boldsymbol{y}^{m}_{v}\rVert\geq K\}}\mathbb{P}(M_{yR}(A^{(m)}_{v})>a_{n}x).

For the second term we find for
n
large that

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in D_{n}}1_{\{\lVert\boldsymbol{y}^{m}_{v}\rVert\geq K\}}\mathbb{P}(M_{yR}(A^{(m)}_{v})>a_{n}x)

(40)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{v\in D_{n}}1_{\{\lVert\boldsymbol{y}^{m}_{v}\rVert\geq K\}}\mathbb{P}(\lVert\boldsymbol{y}^{m}_{v}\rVert\lVert\boldsymbol{R}^{m}_{v}\rVert>a_{n}x)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{v\in D_{n}}1_{\{\lVert\boldsymbol{y}^{m}_{v}\rVert\geq K\}}\mathbb{P}(\lVert\boldsymbol{R}^{m}_{0}\rVert>a_{n}x)c\lVert\boldsymbol{y}^{m}_{v}\rVert^{\gamma},

where we in the second inequality have used the stationarity of
(R_{v})
and the fact that the tail of
\lVert\boldsymbol{R}^{m}_{0}\rVert
is regularly varying: There exist
c>0
and
x_{0}>0
such that

\frac{\mathbb{P}(\lVert\boldsymbol{R}^{m}_{0}\rVert>x/y)}{\mathbb{P}(\lVert\boldsymbol{R}^{m}_{0}\rVert>x)}\leq cy^{\gamma}

for all
x\geq x_{0}
and
y\geq 1
. Utilizing (
37
) and the choice of
(a_{n})
, we find from Lemma
A.1
that the upper bound in (
40
) has limit

x^{-\alpha}C\frac{\mathbb{E}^{\boldsymbol{Y}}\Bigl[1_{\{\lVert\hat{\boldsymbol{Y}}^{m}_{0}\rVert\geq K\}}\lVert\hat{\boldsymbol{Y}}^{m}_{0}\rVert^{\gamma}\mid\hat{\mathbb{I}}\Bigr]((y_{v}))}{\mathbb{E}(\Theta^{m}_{0})_{+}^{\alpha}}

as
n\to\infty
.
By dominated convergence, this tends to 0 as
K\to\infty
, since
\mathbb{E}\lVert\boldsymbol{Y}^{m}_{0}\rVert^{\gamma}<\infty
.

We now consider the first term in (
39
). When
\lVert\boldsymbol{y}_{v}^{m}\rVert<K
, the field
\boldsymbol{y}_{v}^{m}
takes its values in the space
\mathcal{S}=[-K,K)^{\lvert B^{(m)}\rvert}
. Now fix
p\in\mathbb{N}
and construct the set
\mathcal{A}_{p}
of grid points of
\mathcal{S}
with a distance
K/p
apart, i.e.

\mathcal{A}_{p}=(\tfrac{K}{p}\mathbb{Z})^{{}^{\lvert B^{(m)}\rvert}}\cap\mathcal{S}.

We only highlight the dependence on
p
, as
K
will be fixed throughout the use of the set.
For each point
\boldsymbol{x}_{p}\in\mathcal{A}_{p}
, we let
\mathcal{B}_{\boldsymbol{x}_{p}}
denote the box in
\mathbb{R}^{\lvert B^{(m)}\rvert}
with “left” corner point
\boldsymbol{x}_{p}
,

\mathcal{B}_{\boldsymbol{x}_{p}}=\boldsymbol{x}_{p}+\bigl[0,K/p\bigr)^{\lvert B^{(m)}\rvert}.

These boxes make up a partition of the set
\mathcal{S}
, and thus when
\lVert\boldsymbol{y}_{v}^{m}\rVert<K
, the field
\boldsymbol{y}_{v}^{m}
lies in exactly one such box. As a last piece of convenient notation, we let
\overline{\boldsymbol{x}}_{p}
and
\underline{\boldsymbol{x}}_{p}
denote the coordinate-wise numerically largest respectively smallest value in (the closure of)
\mathcal{B}_{\boldsymbol{x}_{p}}
. That is, with
x_{p,z}
denoting the
z
’th coordinate of
\boldsymbol{x}_{p}
and similarly for
\overline{\boldsymbol{x}}_{p}
and
\underline{\boldsymbol{x}}_{p}
,

\overline{x}_{p,z}=x_{p,z}+\tfrac{K}{p}1_{\{x_{p,z}\geq 0\}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\underline{x}_{p,z}=x_{p,z}+\tfrac{K}{p}1_{\{x_{p,z}<0\}}.

Having introduced the notation above, we can write the first term in (
39
) as

\sum_{\boldsymbol{x}_{p}\in\mathcal{A}_{p}}\sum_{v\in D_{n}}1_{\{\boldsymbol{y}^{m}_{v}\in\mathcal{B}_{\boldsymbol{x}_{p}}\}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{yR}({A_{v}^{(m)}})>a_{n}x\bigr).

(41)

This is bounded from above by

\sum_{\boldsymbol{x}_{p}\in\mathcal{A}_{p}}\sum_{v\in D_{n}}1_{\{\boldsymbol{y}^{m}_{v}\in\mathcal{B}_{\boldsymbol{x}_{p}}\}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{\overline{\boldsymbol{x}}_{p}R}({A_{v}^{(m)}})>a_{n}x\bigr),

which by Lemmas
A.1
and
B.1
converges to

x^{-\alpha}\sum_{\boldsymbol{x}_{p}\in\mathcal{A}_{p}}\frac{\mathbb{E}^{\boldsymbol{Y}}\bigl(1_{\{\hat{\boldsymbol{Y}}_{0}^{m}\in\mathcal{B}_{\boldsymbol{x}_{p}}\}}\mid\hat{\mathbb{I}}\bigr)((y_{v}))\,\mathbb{E}(\max_{v\in A_{0}^{(m)}}\overline{x}_{p,v}\Theta^{m}_{v})_{+}^{\alpha}}{\mathbb{E}(\Theta^{m}_{0})_{+}^{\alpha}}

(42)

as
n\to\infty
. Similarly, (
41
) is bounded from below by an expression that tends to

x^{-\alpha}\sum_{\boldsymbol{x}_{p}\in\mathcal{A}_{p}}\frac{\mathbb{E}^{\boldsymbol{Y}}\bigl(1_{\{\hat{\boldsymbol{Y}}_{0}^{m}\in\mathcal{B}_{\boldsymbol{x}_{p}}\}}\mid\hat{\mathbb{I}}\bigr)((y_{v}))\,\mathbb{E}(\max_{v\in A_{0}^{(m)}}\underline{x}_{p,v}\Theta^{m}_{v})_{+}^{\alpha}}{\mathbb{E}(\Theta^{m}_{0})_{+}^{\alpha}}

(43)

as
n\to\infty
. Clearly, both (
42
) and (
43
) tends to

x^{-\alpha}\frac{\mathbb{E}^{\boldsymbol{Y}}\Big[\mathbb{E}\Big(1_{\lVert\boldsymbol{Y}^{m}_{0}\rVert<M}(\max_{v\in A_{0}^{(m)}}Y_{v}\Theta^{m}_{v})_{+}^{\alpha}\mid\boldsymbol{Y}=\hat{\boldsymbol{Y}}\Big)\mid\hat{\mathbb{I}}\Big]((y_{v}))}{\mathbb{E}(\Theta^{m}_{0})_{+}^{\alpha}}

as
p\to\infty
. Letting
K\to\infty
, using that
\mathbb{E}\lVert\boldsymbol{Y}^{m}_{0}\rVert^{\alpha}<\infty
, gives that

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in D_{n}}\mathbb{P}(M_{yR}(A^{(m)}_{v})>a_{n}x)

\displaystyle\to x^{-\alpha}\frac{\mathbb{E}^{\boldsymbol{Y}}\Bigl[\mathbb{E}\Bigl((\max_{v\in A^{(m)}_{0}}Y_{v}\Theta^{m}_{v})_{+}^{\alpha}\mid\boldsymbol{Y}=\hat{\boldsymbol{Y}}\Bigr)\mid\hat{\mathbb{I}}\Bigr]((y_{v}))}{\mathbb{E}(\Theta^{m}_{0})_{+}^{\alpha}}.

Handling the first term in (
38
) similarly, gives the desired result.
∎

C
Extremal representation

Throughout this appendix we assume that
(D_{n})
satisfies Assumption
1
and that
(Z_{v})
, with approximating fields
(Z_{v}^{t})
, and
(Y_{v})
satisfy Assumption
2
. We set out to prove (in Lemma
C.5
below) that

\mathbb{P}\bigl(\max_{v\in D_{n}}y_{v}Z_{v}\leq a_{n}x\bigr)=\exp\Bigl(-\sum_{v\in D_{n}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{yZ}({A_{v}^{n}})\leq a_{n}x<y_{v}Z_{v}\bigr)\Bigr)+o(1)

for almost all realizations
(y_{v})
of
(Y_{v})
, which is also satisfied for the approximating fields
(Z_{v}^{t})
for each
t\in\mathbb{N}
.
The proof follows by arguments similar to those of
[
26
]
, however with some modifications due to lack of stationarity caused by conditioning on
(Y_{v})=(y_{v})
. This is handled using the ergodic-like convergence of Lemma
A.1
in Appendix
A
.

The results herein will, again, be formulated and proven in terms of a general field
(R_{v})
acting as a place-holder for either
(Z_{v})
or
(Z_{v}^{t})
. Therefore, by assumption and by Lemma
5.1
, we have that there exist
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
and
C_{0}>0
such that

\mathbb{P}(yR_{0}>a_{n}x)\leq\frac{C_{0}\,x^{-\alpha}}{\lvert D_{n}\rvert}\lvert y\rvert^{\gamma}

(44)

for all
n\geq n_{0}
and all
\lvert y\rvert>1
.

To ease notation throughout, we let
P_{n}
and
Q_{n}
be the set of indices used in the geometrical approximation
D_{n}^{-}\subseteq D_{n}\subseteq D_{n}^{+}
of
D_{n}
appearing in Assumption
1
, i.e.

P_{n}=\bigl\{z\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\>:\>{J_{z}^{n}}\subseteq D_{n}\bigr\}

and

Q_{n}=\bigl\{z\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\>:\>{J_{z}^{n}}\cap D_{n}\neq\emptyset\bigr\}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence,
\bigcup_{P_{n}}{J_{z}^{n}}=D_{n}^{-}
and
\bigcup_{Q_{n}}{J_{z}^{n}}=D_{n}^{+}
, and note that the assumption (
6
) is equivalent to the asymptotic equivalences

\lvert P_{n}\rvert\sim\lvert Q_{n}\rvert\sim\frac{\lvert D_{n}\rvert}{\lvert J_{z}^{n}\rvert}

as
n\to\infty
.

The following lemma, which states that the extremal behavior of
(y_{v}R_{v})
on a collection of
{J_{z}^{n}}
-sets is essentially equal to the extremal behavior on corresponding subsets of asymptotically equivalent sizes, will be useful throughout the section.

Lemma C.1
.

Let
(R_{v})
be either
(Z_{v})
or
(Z_{v}^{t})
for some fixed
t\in\mathbb{N}
. For
z\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
let
H_{z}^{n}\subseteq{J_{z}^{n}}
be a set which expands in size as
{J_{z}^{n}}
, i.e.
\lvert H_{z}^{n}\rvert\sim\lvert{J_{z}^{n}}\rvert
as
n\to\infty
. Then, for almost all realizations
(y_{v})
of
(Y_{v})
,

\sum_{z\in{P_{n}}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{yR}({J_{z}^{n}}\setminus H_{z}^{n})>a_{n}x\bigr)\to 0

(45)

as
n\to\infty
. The result is also true if
P_{n}
is replaced by
Q_{n}
. Moreover,

\sum_{v\in D_{n}^{+}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(y_{v}R_{v}>a_{n}x\bigr)-\sum_{v\in D_{n}^{-}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(y_{v}R_{v}>a_{n}x\bigr)\to 0

(46)

and

\sum_{z\in{Q_{n}}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{yR}({J_{z}^{n}})>a_{n}x\bigr)-\sum_{z\in{P_{n}}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{yR}({J_{z}^{n}})>a_{n}x\bigr)\to 0

(47)

as
n\to\infty
.

It is enough to show (
45
) with summation over
P_{n}
, as the case of
Q_{n}
follows identically.

Let
b\geq 1
be given. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{z\in{P_{n}}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{yR}({J_{z}^{n}}\setminus H_{z}^{n})>a_{n}x\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{z\in{P_{n}}}\sum_{v\in{J_{z}^{n}}\setminus H_{z}^{n}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(y_{v}R_{v}>a_{n}x\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{z\in{P_{n}}}\sum_{v\in{J_{z}^{n}}\setminus H_{z}^{n}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(y_{v}R_{v}>a_{n}x\bigr)1_{\{\lvert y_{v}\rvert\leq b\}}+\sum_{z\in{P_{n}}}\sum_{v\in{J_{z}^{n}}\setminus H_{z}^{n}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(y_{v}R_{v}>a_{n}x\bigr)1_{\{\lvert y_{v}\rvert>b\}}.

We consider the two terms separately. For the former term we see by stationarity of
(R_{v})
that

\displaystyle\sum_{z\in{P_{n}}}\sum_{v\in{J_{z}^{n}}\setminus H_{z}^{n}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(y_{v}R_{v}>a_{n}x\bigr)1_{\{\lvert y_{v}\rvert\leq b\}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{z\in{P_{n}}}\sum_{v\in{J_{z}^{n}}\setminus H_{z}^{n}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(\lvert R_{v}\rvert>a_{n}x/b\bigr)1_{\{\lvert y_{v}\rvert\leq b\}}

\displaystyle\leq\lvert P_{n}\rvert\,\lvert{J_{z}^{n}}\setminus H_{z}^{n}\rvert\,\mathbb{P}\bigl(\lvert R_{0}\rvert>a_{n}x/b\bigr)

\displaystyle\to 0

as
n\to\infty
due to the choice of
a_{n}
and the tail-balance of
(R_{v})
. For the second term above we use (
44
) and Lemma
A.1
of Appendix
A
to obtain (for sufficiently large
n
)

\displaystyle\sum_{z\in{P_{n}}}\sum_{v\in{J_{z}^{n}}\setminus H_{z}^{n}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(y_{v}R_{v}>a_{n}x\bigr)1_{\{\lvert y_{v}\rvert>b\}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{z\in{P_{n}}}\sum_{v\in{J_{z}^{n}}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(y_{v}R_{v}>a_{n}x\bigr)1_{\{\lvert y\rvert>b\}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in D_{n}^{-}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(y_{v}R_{v}>a_{n}x\bigr)1_{\{\lvert y\rvert>b\}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{0}\,x^{-\alpha}}{\lvert D_{n}^{-}\rvert}\sum_{v\in D_{n}^{-}}\lvert y_{v}\rvert^{\gamma}1_{\{\lvert y_{v}\rvert>b\}}

\displaystyle\to C_{0}\,x^{-\alpha}\mathbb{E}^{\boldsymbol{Y}}[\lvert\hat{Y}_{0}\rvert^{\gamma}1_{\{\lvert\hat{Y}_{0}\rvert>b\}}\mid\hat{\mathbb{I}}]((y_{v}))

as
n\to\infty
. Letting
b\to\infty
proves (
45
).

The claims (
46
) and (
47
) follow by similar arguments utilizing that
P_{n}\subseteq Q_{n}
,
D_{n}^{-}\subseteq D_{n}^{+}
and
\lvert D_{n}^{+}\setminus D_{n}^{-}\rvert=o(\lvert D_{n}\rvert)
.
∎In the proof of Lemma
C.4
below, we will use an asymptotic equivalence between a product of probabilities, where each factor is
y_{v}
-dependent, and the exponential of the sum of the opposite probabilities. In the process of obtaining this, the next result will be useful. The asymptotic equivalence can be seen as the the
y_{v}
-dependent counterpart of the formula
a_{n}^{n}=\exp(-n(1-a_{n}))+o(1)
used in classical extremal theory, cf.
[
22
, Formula (2.8)]
.

Lemma C.2
.

Let
(R_{v})
be either
(Z_{v})
or
(Z_{v}^{t})
for some fixed
t\in\mathbb{N}
. For almost all realizations
(y_{v})
of
(Y_{v})
,

\sum_{z\in{P_{n}}}\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j}\Bigl(\sum_{v\in{J_{z}^{n}}}\mathbb{P}(y_{v}R_{v}>a_{n}x)\Bigr)^{j}\to 0

as
n\to\infty
. The result is also true if
P_{n}
is replaced by
Q_{n}
.

Let
b\geq 1
large enough be given. Later in the proof we will explicitly choose
b
such that

2C_{0}\,x^{-\alpha}\mathbb{E}^{\boldsymbol{Y}}[\lvert\hat{Y}_{0}\rvert^{\gamma}1_{\{\lvert\hat{Y}_{0}\rvert>b\}}\mid\hat{\mathbb{I}}]((y_{v}))<1,

where
C_{0}
is the constant from (
44
). This
b
can be chosen for almost all
(y_{v})
due to the integrability of
\lvert Y_{0}\rvert^{\gamma}
.

By considering the cases
\lvert y_{v}\rvert\leq b
and
\lvert y_{v}\rvert>b
separately as in the previous proof, we can write

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in{J_{z}^{n}}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(y_{v}R_{v}>a_{n}x\bigr)\leq\lvert{J_{z}^{n}}\rvert\,\mathbb{P}\bigl(\lvert R_{0}\rvert>a_{n}x/b\bigr)+\sum_{v\in{J_{z}^{n}}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(y_{v}R_{0}>a_{n}x\bigr)1_{\{\lvert y_{v}\rvert>b\}}.

Since
(a_{1}+a_{2})^{j}\leq 2^{j}(a_{1}^{j}+a_{2}^{j})
for positive
a_{1},a_{2}
, The result especially follows if the following can be proven true for the considered realization
(y_{v})
:

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j}\lvert P_{n}\rvert\Bigl(2\,\lvert{J_{z}^{n}}\rvert\,\mathbb{P}(\lvert R_{0}\rvert>a_{n}x/b)\Bigr)^{j}=0,

(48)

\displaystyle\lim_{b\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j}\sum_{z\in{P_{n}}}\Bigl(2\sum_{v\in{J_{z}^{n}}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(y_{v}R_{0}>a_{n}x\bigr)1_{\{\lvert y_{v}\rvert>b\}}\Bigr)^{j}=0.

(49)

We start by showing (
48
). Due to the choice of
a_{n}
and the tail-balance of
R_{0}
we have
\limsup_{n}\lvert D_{n}\rvert\mathbb{P}(\lvert R_{0}\rvert>a_{n}x/b)<\infty
for all
x>0
. In particular we find for any
j\geq 2
that

\lvert P_{n}\rvert\Bigl(2\,\lvert{J_{z}^{n}}\rvert\,\mathbb{P}(\lvert R_{0}\rvert>a_{n}x/b)\Bigr)^{j}\sim\Bigl(2\,\lvert D_{n}\rvert\,\mathbb{P}(\lvert R_{0}\rvert>a_{n}x/b)\Bigr)^{j}\frac{\lvert P_{n}\rvert}{\lvert P_{n}\rvert^{j}}\to 0

as
n\to\infty
. Moreover, for
n
sufficiently large,

\displaystyle\lvert P_{n}\rvert^{1/j}\,2\,\lvert{J_{z}^{n}}\rvert\,\mathbb{P}(\lvert R_{0}\rvert>a_{n}x/b)

\displaystyle\leq\lvert P_{n}\rvert^{1/2}\,2\,\lvert{J_{z}^{n}}\rvert\,\mathbb{P}(\lvert R_{0}\rvert>a_{n}x/b)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{2\,\lvert D_{n}\rvert\,\mathbb{P}(\lvert R_{0}\rvert>a_{n}x/b)}{\sqrt{\lvert P_{n}\rvert}}<1

for all
j\geq 2
. Since
\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\tfrac{1}{j}a^{j}<\infty
exactly when
\lvert a\rvert<1
, we conclude (
48
) by dominated convergence.

To show (
49
) we use the inequality
\sum_{i}a_{i}^{j}\leq(\sum_{i}a_{i})^{j}
to obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{z\in{P_{n}}}\Bigl(2\sum_{v\in{J_{z}^{n}}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(y_{v}R_{0}>a_{n}x\bigr)1_{\{\lvert y_{v}\rvert>b\}}\Bigr)^{j}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl(2\sum_{z\in{P_{n}}}\sum_{v\in{J_{z}^{n}}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(y_{v}R_{0}>a_{n}x\bigr)1_{\{\lvert y_{v}\rvert>b\}}\Bigr)^{j}

\displaystyle=\Bigl(2\sum_{v\in D_{n}^{-}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(y_{v}R_{0}>a_{n}x\bigr)1_{\{\lvert y_{v}\rvert>b\}}\Bigr)^{j}.

Exactly as in the previous proof we find that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}2\sum_{v\in D_{n}^{-}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(y_{v}R_{0}>a_{n}x\bigr)1_{\{\lvert y_{v}\rvert>b\}}\leq 2\,C_{0}\,x^{-\alpha}\mathbb{E}^{\boldsymbol{Y}}[\lvert\hat{Y}_{0}\rvert^{\gamma}1_{\{\lvert\hat{Y}_{0}\rvert>b\}}\mid\hat{\mathbb{I}}]((y_{v})).

Since
b
is chosen such that this limit is
<1
, we find by dominated convergence that

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j}\sum_{z\in{P_{n}}}\Bigl(2\sum_{v\in{J_{z}^{n}}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(y_{v}R_{0}>a_{n}x\bigr)1_{\{\lvert y_{v}\rvert>b\}}\Bigr)^{j}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j}\Bigl(2\sum_{z\in{P_{n}}}\sum_{v\in{J_{z}^{n}}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(y_{v}R_{0}>a_{n}x\bigr)1_{\{\lvert y_{v}\rvert>b\}}\Bigr)^{j}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j}\Bigl(2\,C_{0}\,x^{-\alpha}\mathbb{E}^{\boldsymbol{Y}}[\lvert\hat{Y}_{0}\rvert^{\gamma}1_{\{\lvert\hat{Y}_{0}\rvert>b\}}\mid\hat{\mathbb{I}}]((y_{v}))\Bigr)^{j}.

Letting
b\to\infty
the convergence (
49
) follows.

(R_{v})
. In its formulation,
\overline{B}_{z}^{n}
denotes the union of the
3^{d}-1
neighboring
J^{n}
-boxes of
{J_{z}^{n}}
, i.e.

\overline{B}_{z}^{n}=\{u\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\setminus{J_{z}^{n}}\>:\>(z_{\ell}-1)t_{n,\ell}\leq u_{\ell}\leq(z_{\ell}+2)t_{n,\ell}-1,\ \text{for all }\ell\}.

Lemma C.3
.

Let
(R_{v})
be either
(Z_{v})
or
(Z_{v}^{t})
for some fixed
t\in\mathbb{N}
. There exists
\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{n}\to\infty
with
\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{n}=o(\boldsymbol{t}_{n})
such that

\Big\lvert\mathbb{P}\Bigl(\bigcap_{z\in P_{n}}\bigl\{M_{yR}({H_{z}^{n}})\leq a_{n}x\bigr\}\Bigr)-\prod_{z\in P_{n}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{yR}({H_{z}^{n}})\leq a_{n}x\bigr)\Big\rvert\to 0

(51)

as
n\to\infty

for all
\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{n}
-separated sets
{H_{z}^{n}}\subseteq{J_{z}^{n}}
.
Moreover, for all
\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{n}
-separated sets
H_{z}^{n}\subseteq{J_{z}^{n}}
and
B_{z}^{n}\subseteq\overline{B}_{z}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\Big\lvert

\displaystyle\sum_{z\in P_{n}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{yR}({H_{z}^{n}})>a_{n}x,M_{yR}(B_{z}^{n})>a_{n}x\bigr)

(52)

\displaystyle-\sum_{z\in P_{n}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{yR}({H_{z}^{n}})>a_{n}x\bigr)\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{yR}(B_{z}^{n})>a_{n}x\bigr)\Big\rvert\to 0

as
n\to\infty
. Both results remain true if
P_{n}
is replaced by
Q_{n}
.

If
(R_{v})=(Z_{v}^{t})
for some
t\in\mathbb{N}
both results follow easily from Assumption
2
(ii)
.

The results for
(R_{v})=(Z_{v})
follow by appropriately approximating
(Z_{v})
with
(Z_{v}^{t})
and utilizing its strong mixing.
Let
n
be given and let for each
t\in\mathbb{N}
the function
\alpha^{t}(\cdot)
denote the strong mixing rate of
(Z_{v}^{t})
. According to Assumption
2
(ii)
, let
\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{t}_{n}\to\infty
satisfy
\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{n}^{t}=o(\boldsymbol{t}_{n})
and define

\alpha^{t}_{n}:=\alpha^{t}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{n}^{t})

which satisfies
\lvert P_{n}\rvert\alpha_{n}^{t}\to 0
. Moreover, let

\delta(t,n)=\sum_{v\in D_{n}^{+}}\mathbb{P}(y_{v}Z_{v}^{t}>a_{n}x,\,y_{v}Z_{v}\leq a_{n}x)+\sum_{v\in D_{n}^{+}}\mathbb{P}(y_{v}Z_{v}>a_{n}x,\,y_{v}Z_{v}^{t}\leq a_{n}x),

which, due to (
21
), satisfies

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\delta(t,n)=0.

Let
(N_{t}^{1})_{t\in\mathbb{N}},\ (N_{t}^{2})_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(N_{t}^{3})_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
be increasing sequences chosen such that

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{n}^{t}/\boldsymbol{t}_{n}

\displaystyle\leq 1/{t}\qquad\forall n\geq N_{t}^{1},

(53)

\displaystyle\lvert P_{n}\rvert\alpha_{n}^{t}

\displaystyle\leq 1/{t}\qquad\forall n\geq N_{t}^{2},

\displaystyle\delta(t,n)

\displaystyle\leq 1/{t}\qquad\forall n\geq N_{t}^{3}.

Using these, define

r_{n}^{i}=\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}t1_{[N_{t}^{i},N_{t+1}^{i})}(n)\qquad\text{for }i=1,2,3,

and
r_{n}=\min\{r_{n}^{1},r_{n}^{2},r_{n}^{3}\}\to\infty
. Then

N_{t_{n}}^{i}\leq N_{r_{n}}^{i}\leq N_{r_{n}^{i}}^{i}\leq n

for all sequences
(t_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
where
t_{n}\leq r_{n}
(not to be confused with the vector
\boldsymbol{t}_{n}
). In particular, all the inequalities in (
53
) are satisfied for all
n
if
t
is replaced by
t_{n}
such that
t_{n}\leq r_{n}
. Hence, for all such
(t_{n})
,

\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{n}^{t_{n}}/\boldsymbol{t}_{n}\to 0,\qquad\lvert P_{n}\rvert\alpha_{n}^{t_{n}}\to 0,\qquad\delta(t_{n},n)\to 0

(54)

as
n\to\infty
. Now let such a sequence be fixed and define

\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{n}=\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{n}^{t_{n}}\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha_{n}=\alpha_{n}^{t_{n}}=\alpha^{t_{n}}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{n}^{t_{n}}).

This
\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{n}
fulfills the requirement of the lemma.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let the sets
{H_{z}^{n}}
appearing in (
51
) be
\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{n}
-separated. Using the strong mixing of
(Z_{v}^{t_{n}})
recursively (in the last inequality), we find that

\displaystyle\Big\lvert\mathbb{P}\Bigl(\bigcap_{z\in P_{n}}\bigl\{M_{yZ}({H_{z}^{n}})\leq a_{n}x\bigr\}\Bigr)-\prod_{z\in P_{n}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{yZ}({H_{z}^{n}})\leq a_{n}x\bigr)\Big\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle\Big\lvert\mathbb{P}\Bigl(\bigcap_{z\in P_{n}}\bigl\{M_{yZ}({H_{z}^{n}})\leq a_{n}x\bigr\}\Bigr)-\mathbb{P}\Bigl(\bigcap_{z\in P_{n}}\bigl\{M_{yZ^{t_{n}}}({H_{z}^{n}})\leq a_{n}x\bigr\}\Bigr)\Big\rvert

\displaystyle+\Big\lvert\prod_{z\in P_{n}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{yZ}({H_{z}^{n}})\leq a_{n}x\bigr)-\prod_{z\in P_{n}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{yZ^{t_{n}}}({H_{z}^{n}})\leq a_{n}x\bigr)\Big\rvert

\displaystyle+\Big\lvert\mathbb{P}\Bigl(\bigcap_{z\in P_{n}}\bigl\{M_{yZ^{t_{n}}}({H_{z}^{n}})\leq a_{n}x\bigr\}\Bigr)-\prod_{z\in P_{n}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{yZ^{t_{n}}}({H_{z}^{n}})\leq a_{n}x\bigr)\Big\rvert

as

Lemma C.4
.

Let
(R_{v})
be either
(Z_{v})
or
(Z_{v}^{t})
for some fixed
t\in\mathbb{N}
. For almost all realizations
(y_{v})
of
(Y_{v})
,

\mathbb{P}\bigl(\max_{v\in D_{n}}y_{v}R_{v}\leq a_{n}x\bigr)=\exp\Bigl(-\sum_{z\in{P_{n}}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{yR}({J_{z}^{n}})>a_{n}x\bigr)\Bigr)+o(1)

(55)

as
n\to\infty
. In particular,

\mathbb{P}\bigl(\max_{v\in D_{n}}y_{v}R_{v}\leq a_{n}x\bigr)\geq\exp\Bigl(-\sum_{v\in D_{n}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(y_{v}R_{v}>a_{n}x\bigr)\Bigr)+o(1).

(56)

For any
z\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
we define
{H_{z}^{n}}\subseteq{J_{z}^{n}}
to be the subset of
{J_{z}^{n}}
with distance
\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{n}
to the “right” boundary,

{H_{z}^{n}}=\bigl\{u\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\>:\>z_{\ell}t_{n,\ell}\leq u_{\ell}\leq(z_{\ell}+1)t_{n,\ell}-1-\gamma_{n,\ell},\ \text{for all }\ell=1,\dots,d\bigr\},

where
\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{n}
is the vector given in Lemma
C.3
.
It will be used shortly that
{H_{z}^{n}}
is asymptotically of the same size as
{J_{z}^{n}}
, i.e.
\lvert{H_{z}^{n}}\rvert\sim\lvert{J_{z}^{n}}\rvert
.
First we see that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\Bigl(\bigcap_{z\in P_{n}}\bigl\{M_{yR}({H_{z}^{n}})\leq a_{n}x\bigr\}\Bigr)-\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{yR}(D_{n}^{-})\leq a_{n}x\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\Bigl(\bigcup_{z\in P_{n}}\bigl\{M_{yR}({J_{z}^{n}}\setminus{H_{z}^{n}})>a_{n}x\bigr\}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{z\in P_{n}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{yR}({J_{z}^{n}}\setminus{H_{z}^{n}})>a_{n}x\bigr).

Secondly, using (
51
) above,

\displaystyle\Big\lvert\mathbb{P}\Bigl(\bigcap_{z\in P_{n}}\bigl\{M_{yR}({H_{z}^{n}})\leq a_{n}x\bigr\}\Bigr)-\prod_{z\in P_{n}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{yR}({H_{z}^{n}})\leq a_{n}x\bigr)\Big\rvert\to 0

as
n\to\infty
. As

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{z\in P_{n}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{yR}({H_{z}^{n}})\leq a_{n}x\bigr)-\prod_{z\in P_{n}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{yR}({J_{z}^{n}})\leq a_{n}x\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{z\in P_{n}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{yR}({J_{z}^{n}}\setminus{H_{z}^{n}})>a_{n}x\bigr),

which tends to
0
by Lemma
C.1
, these considerations show that

\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{yR}(D_{n}^{-})\leq a_{n}x\bigr)=\prod_{z\in P_{n}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{yR}({J_{z}^{n}})\leq a_{n}x\bigr)+o(1).

Note that this is also true with
P_{n}
replaced by
Q_{n}
and
D_{n}^{-}
replaced by
D_{n}^{+}
, respectively. Consequently, as
D_{n}^{-}\subseteq D_{n}\subseteq D_{n}^{+}
, this implies that

\displaystyle\prod_{z\in{Q_{n}}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{yR}({J_{z}^{n}})\leq a_{n}x\bigr)+o(1)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\bigl(\max_{v\in D_{n}}y_{v}R_{v}\leq a_{n}x\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{z\in{P_{n}}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{yR}({J_{z}^{n}})\leq a_{n}x\bigr)+o(1)

as
n\to\infty
. Utilizing a series representation of the logarithm in combination with Lemma
C.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\prod_{z\in{P_{n}}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{yR}({J_{z}^{n}})\leq a_{n}x\bigr)

\displaystyle=\exp\Bigl(\sum_{z\in P_{n}}\log\bigl(1-\mathbb{P}(M_{yR}({J_{z}^{n}})>a_{n}x)\bigr)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\exp\Bigl(-\sum_{z\in P_{n}}\mathbb{P}(M_{yR}({J_{z}^{n}})>a_{n}x)-\sum_{z\in{P_{n}}}\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j}\bigl(\mathbb{P}(M_{yR}({J_{z}^{n}})>a_{n}x)\bigr)^{j}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\exp\Bigl(-\sum_{z\in P_{n}}\mathbb{P}(M_{yR}({J_{z}^{n}})>a_{n}x)\Bigr)+o(1)

as
n\to\infty
, and again similarly for
Q_{n}
. Due to (
47
), this shows (
55
).

The inequality (
56
) is a consequence of (
55
), Boole’s inequality and (
46
).

Lemma C.5
.

Let
(R_{v})
be either
(Z_{v})
or
(Z_{v}^{t})
for some fixed
t\in\mathbb{N}
. For almost all realizations
(y_{v})
of

\sum_{z\in{P_{n}}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{yR}({J_{z}^{n}})>a_{n}x\bigr)=\sum_{v\in D_{n}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{yR}({A_{v}^{n}})\leq a_{n}x<y_{v}R_{v}\bigr)+o(1)

(57)

as

. In particular,

\mathbb{P}\bigl(\max_{v\in D_{n}}y_{v}R_{v}\leq a_{n}x\bigr)=\exp\Bigl(-\sum_{v\in D_{n}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{yR}({A_{v}^{n}})\leq a_{n}x<y_{v}R_{v}\bigr)\Bigr)+o(1)

(58)

.

Clearly (
58

C.4

For all

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{yR}({J_{z}^{n}})>a_{n}x\bigr)

\displaystyle\sum_{z\in P_{n}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{yR}({J_{z}^{n}})>a_{n}x\bigr)

46
) since

is the union of the

.

) in the second inequality below we find that

\displaystyle\sum_{z\in P_{n}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{yR}({J_{z}^{n}})>a_{n}x,M_{yR}(\overline{B}_{z}^{n})>a_{n}x\bigr)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{z\in P_{n}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{yR}({J_{z}^{n}})>a_{n}x,\,M_{yR}(\overline{B}_{z}^{n}\setminus E_{z,n}^{+})>a_{n}x\bigr)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{z\in P_{n}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{yR}({J_{z}^{n}}\setminus E_{z,n}^{-})>a_{n}x,\,M_{yR}(\overline{B}_{z}^{n})>a_{n}x\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{z\in P_{n}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{yR}(E_{z,n}^{-})>a_{n}x\bigr)

\displaystyle\quad+2\sum_{z\in P_{n}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{yR}({J_{z}^{n}})>a_{n}x\bigr)\,\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{yR}(\overline{B}_{z}^{n})>a_{n}x\bigr)+o(1).

. Since

, the second term can also be seen to converge to

A.1
of Appendix
A
and arguments similar to those in the proof of Lemma
C.1
thus concluding the proof.

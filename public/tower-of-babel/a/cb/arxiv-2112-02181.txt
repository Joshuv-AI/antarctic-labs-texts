Projections onto hyperbolas or bilinear constraint sets in Hilbert spaces
Heinz H. Bauschke, Manish Krishan Lal, Xianfu Wang

Abstract
Sets of bilinear constraints are important in various machine learning models. Mathematically, they are hyperbolas in a product space. In this paper, we give a complete formula for projections onto sets of bilinear constraints or hyperbolas in a general Hilbert space.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification:

Primary 41A50, 90C26; Secondary 90C20, 46C05.

Keywords:
bilinear constraint set,
hyperbola, orthogonal projection, nonconvex minimization, prox-regularity.

Introduction

Throughout this paper, we assume that

X
is a Hilbert space
with inner product

\left\langle{\cdot},{\cdot}\right\rangle\colon X\times X\to\mathbb{R}
,

(1)

and induced norm
\|\cdot\|
. In various learning models
[
9
,

]
, Elser utilizes

C_{\gamma}:=\big\{{(x,y)\in X\times X}~\big|~{\left\langle{x},{y}\right\rangle=\gamma}\big\}

(2)

where
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
is a fixed constant. Mathematically speaking, up to a rotation, this is just a hyperbola or
quadratic surface in

. Finding projections onto quadratic curves or surfaces algorithmically
have many practical applications;
see, e.g.,
[
6
,
7
,
9
,
10
]
.
If
\gamma=0
in

, then

becomes the set ‘cross’ in

.
The projection formula for the ‘cross’ has been thoroughly investigated
[
2
]
.
In
[

,
10

results on projections

The goal of this paper is to give a complete analysis of the projection onto the set
C_{\gamma}
with
\gamma\neq 0
,
and extend the results to a general Hilbert space.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows.

2
General properties of
C_{\gamma}

In an infinite-dimensional space, the existence of projection onto a set
often requires the weak closedness of the set.
Our first result says that
although the set
C_{\gamma}
is norm closed it is not weakly closed in
X\times X
.

Proposition 2.1
.

The set
C_{\gamma}
is closed in the
norm
topology but
not closed in the weak topology in
X\times X
.
In fact,
\overline{C_{\gamma}}^{\rm weak}=X\times X
.

Proof
. Evidently,
C_{\gamma}
is norm closed. We show that

\overline{C_{\gamma}}^{\rm weak}=X\times X
.
Let
(x,y)\in X\times X
. Consider
S=\mbox{span}\{x,y\}
.
The orthogonal decomposition theorem gives
X=S\oplus S^{\perp}
.
Because
X
is infinite-dimensional and
S
is at most two-dimensional,
S^{\perp}
is infinite dimensional, so
any orthonormal base of
S^{\perp}
must have a sequence
(e_{n})_{{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}}
which converges weakly to
0
, i.e.,

e_{n}\rightharpoonup 0
; see, e.g.,
[
11
]
.
Also
e_{n}\perp x
and

e_{n}\perp y.

Set
\xi=\gamma-\langle x,y\rangle
. Then

\displaystyle\left\langle{x-\xi e_{n}},{y-e_{n}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle{x},{y}\right\rangle-\left\langle{x},{e_{n}}\right\rangle-\xi\left\langle{e_{n}},{y}\right\rangle+\xi\left\langle{e_{n}},{e_{n}}\right\rangle

(3)

\displaystyle=\left\langle{x},{y}\right\rangle+\xi=\gamma,

(4)

so
(x-\xi e_{n},y-e_{n})\in C_{\gamma}
.
Since
(x-\xi e_{n},y-e_{n})\rightharpoonup(x,y)
, we have

(x,y)\in\overline{C_{\gamma}}^{\rm weak}
.
Because
(x,y)\in X\times X
was arbitrary, we conclude that

X\times X\subseteq\overline{C}^{\rm weak}
.

\hfill\quad\blacksquare

Proposition
2.1
indicates that finding
P_{C_{\gamma}}
, i.e., projections onto
C_{\gamma}
,
might be complicated in a general Hilbert space.
This seemingly difficult issue can be completely avoided by utilizing the structures of
the optimization problem. See Subsection

below.

Our next result says that when
\gamma\neq 0
, locally around
the set

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\gamma\neq 0
, and
C_{\gamma}=\big\{{(x,y)\in X\times X}~\big|~{\left\langle{x},{y}\right\rangle=\gamma}\big\}.

For every
(x,y)\in C_{\gamma}
,
the the following hold:

(i)

C_{\gamma}
is prox-regular at
(x,y)
.

(ii)

There exists a neighborhood of
(x,y)

on which
the projection mapping onto
C_{\gamma}
is monotone and Lipschitz continuous.

Proof
.
(i)
: Write

\iota_{C_{\gamma}}(x,y)=\iota_{\{\gamma\}}(h(x,y))
where
h(x,y)=\left\langle{x},{y}\right\rangle
. Let

(x,y)\in C_{\gamma}
. Then

\nabla h(x,y)=(y,x)\neq(0,0)
because of
\gamma\neq 0
.
Being a composition of a convex function
\iota_{\{\gamma\}}

and a twice
differentiable function
h
that is qualified at
(x,y)
,
[
3
, Proposition 2.4]
shows that

\iota_{C_{\gamma}}
is prox-regular at
(x,y)
, so is
C_{\gamma}
.

(ii)
: Apply
(i)
and
[
3
, Proposition 4.4]
.

\hfill\quad\blacksquare

Observe that Proposition
2.2

also follow from
[
13
, Proposition 13.32]
and
[

when

; and that
when
\gamma=0

C_{0}
is not prox-regular at
(0,0)
. Although the projection
exists locally around
C_{\gamma}
, it is still not clear for the global existence.

3
Projections onto hyperbolas

For ease of analysis, we start with

C_{\gamma}:=\big\{{(x,y)\in X\times X}~\big|~{h(x,y):=\left\langle{x},{y}\right\rangle-\gamma=0}\big\}

where
\gamma>0
.
Our goal is to find the projection formula

P_{C_{\gamma}}(x_{0},y_{0})
for every
(x_{0},y_{0})\in X\times X
. That is,

\text{minimize}\quad f(x,y):=\|x-x_{0}\|^{2}+\|y-y_{0}\|^{2}\quad\text{subject to}\quad(x,y)\in C_{\gamma}.

(P)

3.1
Auxiliary problems and existence of projections

To determine the projection operator
{\thinspace P\thinspace}_{\negthinspace\negthinspace C_{\gamma}}
of the set
C_{\gamma}
,
we shall introduce two
equivalently
reformulated problems.
First, for every
(u_{0},v_{0})\in X\times X
, we solve the problem

\text{minimize}\quad f_{1}(u,v):=\|u-u_{0}\|^{2}+\|v-v_{0}\|^{2}\quad\text{subject to}\quad(u,v)\in\tilde{C}_{1},

(
\tilde{P}_{1}
)

where
\tilde{C}_{1}:=\big\{{(u,v)\in{X}\times{X}}~\big|~{h_{1}(u,v):=\norm{u}^{2}-\norm{v}^{2}-2=0}\big\}.

Next, for every
(\tilde{u}_{0},\tilde{v}_{0})\in X\times X
we solve the problem

\text{minimize}\quad f_{\gamma}(\tilde{u},\tilde{v}):=\|\tilde{u}-\tilde{u}_{0}\|^{2}+\|\tilde{v}-\tilde{v}_{0}\|^{2}\quad\text{subject to}\quad(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})\in\tilde{C}_{\gamma},

(
\tilde{P}_{\gamma}
)

where
\tilde{C}_{\gamma}:=\big\{{(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})\in{X}\times{X}}~\big|~{h_{\gamma}(\tilde{u},\tilde{v}):=\norm{\tilde{u}}^{2}-\norm{\tilde{v}}^{2}-2\gamma=0}\big\}
.
Both
\tilde{C}_{1}
and
\tilde{C}_{\gamma}
are hyperbolas.

\tilde{P}_{1}
and
\tilde{P}_{\gamma}
solve for projections
P_{\tilde{C}_{1}}
and
P_{\tilde{C}_{\gamma}}
respectively,
and their connections to

P_{C_{\gamma}}
are given by the
Proposition

3.2
below. Recall

Definition 3.1

(Rotation with an angle
\phi
)
.

A change of coordinates
(u,v)\in{X}\times{X}
, by rotation through an angle
\phi
, is defined by

\begin{bmatrix}x\\
y\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\phi\operatorname{Id}&-\sin\phi\operatorname{Id}\\
\sin\phi\operatorname{Id}&\cos\phi\operatorname{Id}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}u\\
v\end{bmatrix}.

Put

A_{\phi}:=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\phi\operatorname{Id}&-\sin\phi\operatorname{Id}\\
\sin\phi\operatorname{Id}&\cos\phi\operatorname{Id}\end{bmatrix}.

Then
A_{\phi}A_{-\phi}=\operatorname{Id}=A_{-\phi}A_{\phi}
and
A^{-1}_{\phi}=A_{-\phi}
.
The relationships among
P_{C_{\gamma}},P_{\tilde{C}_{\gamma}}
and
P_{\tilde{C}_{1}}
are summarized below.

Proposition 3.2
.

The following hold:

(i)

P_{\tilde{C}_{\gamma}}=\sqrt{\gamma}P_{\tilde{C}_{1}}(\operatorname{Id}/\sqrt{\gamma}).

(ii)

P_{C_{\gamma}}=A_{\pi/4}P_{\tilde{C}_{\gamma}}A_{-\pi/4}.

Proof
.
(i)
:
(\tilde{P}_{1})
is equivalent to
(\tilde{P}_{\gamma})

by a change of variables of scaling

\operatorname{Id}/\sqrt{\gamma}
. Indeed, using

\begin{bmatrix}u\\
v\end{bmatrix}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma}}\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{u}\\
\tilde{v}\end{bmatrix},\text{ and }\begin{bmatrix}u_{0}\\
v_{0}\end{bmatrix}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma}}\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{u}_{0}\\
\tilde{v}_{0}\end{bmatrix}

h_{\gamma}(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})=0
becomes
h_{1}(u,v)=0
, and

f_{\gamma}(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})
becomes

\gamma f_{1}(u,v)=\gamma(\|u-u_{0}\|^{2}+\|v-v_{0}\|^{2})
.

(ii)
:
(P)
is equivalent to
(\tilde{P}_{\gamma})
by a change of variables of
rational
A_{\pi/4}
. Indeed, with

\begin{bmatrix}x\\
y\end{bmatrix}=A_{\pi/4}\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{u}\\
\tilde{v}\end{bmatrix},\text{ and }\begin{bmatrix}x_{0}\\
y_{0}\end{bmatrix}=A_{\pi/4}\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{u}_{0}\\
\tilde{v}_{0}\end{bmatrix}

the objective
f(x,y)=\|x-x_{0}\|^{2}+\|y-y_{0}\|^{2}=\|(x,y)-(x_{0},y_{0})\|^{2}
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle f(x,y)

\displaystyle=\norm{A_{\frac{\pi}{4}}\begin{bmatrix}
\tilde{u}-\tilde{u}_0 \\
\tilde{v}-\tilde{v}_0 \end{bmatrix}
}^{2}=\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{u}-\tilde{u}_{0}\\
\tilde{v}-\tilde{v}_{0}\end{bmatrix}^{T}A_{\frac{\pi}{4}}^{T}A_{\frac{\pi}{4}}\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{u}-\tilde{u}_{0}\\
\tilde{v}-\tilde{v}_{0}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\norm{\tilde{u}-\tilde{u}_0}^{2}+\norm{\tilde{v}-\tilde{v}_0}^{2},

and

h(x,y)=\left\langle{x},{y}\right\rangle-\gamma=0
becomes
h_{\gamma}(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})=\|\tilde{u}\|^{2}-\|\tilde{v}\|^{2}-2\gamma=0
.

\hfill\quad\blacksquare

In view of Proposition
3.2
(ii)
, to see
that
P_{C_{\gamma}}(x,y)\neq\varnothing
for every
(x,y)\in X\times X
,
the following observation is crucial.

Proposition 3.3
.

For every
(\tilde{u}_{0},\tilde{v}_{0})\in{X}\times{X}
,
the minimization problem

minimize

\displaystyle\quad f_{\gamma}(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})=\|\tilde{u}-\tilde{u}_{0}\|^{2}+\|\tilde{v}-\tilde{v}_{0}\|^{2}

(5)

subject to

\displaystyle\quad h_{\gamma}(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})=\norm{\tilde{u}}^{2}-\norm{\tilde{v}}^{2}-2\gamma=0

(6)

always has a solution, i.e.,
P_{\tilde{C}_{\gamma}}(\tilde{u}_{0},\tilde{v}_{0})\neq\varnothing
.

Proof
. We shall illustrate only the case
\tilde{u}_{0}\neq 0,\tilde{v}_{0}\neq 0
, since
the other cases are similar. We claim that the optimization problem is essentially

2
-dimensional. To this end, we expand

\displaystyle f_{\gamma}(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\|\tilde{u}\|^{2}-2\left\langle{\tilde{u}},{\tilde{u}_{0}}\right\rangle+\|\tilde{u}_{0}\|^{2}}+\underbrace{\|\tilde{v}\|^{2}-2\left\langle{\tilde{v}},{\tilde{v}_{0}}\right\rangle+\|\tilde{v}_{0}\|^{2}}.

(7)

The constraint

h_{\gamma}(\tilde{u},\tilde{v}):=\norm{\tilde{u}}^{2}-\norm{\tilde{v}}^{2}-2\gamma=0

means that only the norms
\|\tilde{u}\|
and
\|\tilde{v}\|
matter.
With
\|\tilde{u}\|
fixed, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in a Hilbert space, see, e.g.,
[
11
]
,
shows that
\tilde{u}\mapsto\left\langle{\tilde{u}},{\tilde{u}_{0}}\right\rangle

will be larger so that
the first underlined part in
f_{\gamma}
will be smaller when

\tilde{u}
and
\tilde{u}_{0}
are positively co-linear, i.e,
\tilde{u}=\alpha\tilde{u}_{0}
for some

\alpha\geq 0
. Similarly, for fixed
\|v\|
the second underlined part in

f_{\gamma}
will be smaller when

\tilde{v}=\beta\tilde{v}_{0}
for some
\beta\geq 0
. It follows that the optimization
problem given by (
5
)-(
6
) is equivalent to

minimize

\displaystyle\quad g(\alpha,\beta):=(1-\alpha)^{2}\|\tilde{u}_{0}\|^{2}+(1-\beta)^{2}\|\tilde{v}_{0}\|^{2}

(8)

subject to

\displaystyle\quad g_{1}(\alpha,\beta):=\alpha^{2}\norm{\tilde{u}_{0}}^{2}-\beta^{2}\norm{\tilde{v}_{0}}^{2}-2\gamma=0,\alpha\geq 0,\beta\geq 0.

(9)

Because
g:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is continuous and coercive, and
g_{1}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is
continuous, we conclude that the optimization problem given by
(
8
)-(
9
) has a solution.

\hfill\quad\blacksquare

We are now ready for the investigation of projections onto
\tilde{C}_{1},\tilde{C}_{\gamma}
and
C_{\gamma}
.

3.2
Finding the projection
P_{\tilde{C}_{1}}

Note that

\nabla f_{1}(u,v)=(2(u-u_{0}),2(v-v_{0}))\quad\text{and}\quad\nabla h_{1}(u,v)=(2u,-2v).

(10)

By
[
4
, Proposition 4.1.1]
, every solution of (
\tilde{P}_{1}
) satisfies
the necessarily conditions, i.e., the KKT system of (
\tilde{P}_{1}
), given by

\displaystyle(1+\lambda)u=u_{0}

(11)

\displaystyle(1-\lambda)v=v_{0}

(12)

\displaystyle\|u\|^{2}-\|v\|^{2}-2=0.

(13)

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
u_{0}\neq 0,v_{0}\neq 0
. If
(u,v,\lambda)
verifies (
11
)–(
13
) and

(u,v)
is an optimal solution of problem (
\tilde{P}_{1}
), then
the Lagrange multiplier
\lambda

satisfies
|\lambda|<1
.

Proof
. The constraint set

\|u\|^{2}-\|v\|^{2}=2,

has a special structure: replacing
u
by
-u
, or
v
by
-v

the constraint is still verified.
Also consider

f(u,v)=\|u-u_{0}\|^{2}+\|v-v_{0}\|^{2}=\|u_{0}\|^{2}-2\left\langle{u_{0}},{u}\right\rangle+\|u\|^{2}+\|v_{0}\|^{2}-2\left\langle{v_{0}},{v}\right\rangle+\|v\|^{2},

Given
u_{0}\text{ and }v_{0}
, for fixed
\|u\|
and
\|v\|
,
f(u,v)
becomes smaller if one choose

\left\langle{u_{0}},{u}\right\rangle\geq 0
, and

\left\langle{v_{0}},{v}\right\rangle\geq 0
. Indeed, one can do so by replacing
u
by
-u
or
v
by
-v
if needed.
Now by (
11
) and (
12
),

(1+\lambda)\left\langle{u_{0}},{u}\right\rangle=\|u_{0}\|^{2},\text{ and }(1-\lambda)\left\langle{v_{0}},{v}\right\rangle=\|v_{0}\|^{2}.

Because
u_{0}\neq 0,v_{0}\neq 0
, we have
\left\langle{u_{0}},{u}\right\rangle>0,\left\langle{v_{0}},{v}\right\rangle>0
so that

1+\lambda>0,1-\lambda>0
. Hence

|\lambda|<1.

\hfill\quad\blacksquare

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
u_{0}\neq 0,v_{0}\neq 0
. Define
p\coloneqq\norm{u_0}^{2}-\norm{v_0}^{2}
and
q\coloneqq\norm{u_0}^{2}+\norm{v_0}^{2}
.
Suppose that
u,v\in{X}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
verify (
11
)-(
13
) and
(u,v)
is an optimal solution
to (
\tilde{P}_{1}
).
Then
the following hold:

(i)

u=\frac{u_{0}}{(1+\lambda)}
,
v=\frac{v_{0}}{(1-\lambda)}
, and

(1-\lambda)^{2}\norm{u_0}^{2}-(1+\lambda)^{2}\norm{v_0}^{2}=2(1-\lambda^{2})^{2}
.

(ii)

The objective function has

f_{1}(u,v)=\lambda^{2}\bigg(\frac{\|u_{0}\|^{2}}{(1+\lambda)^{2}}+\frac{\|v_{0}\|^{2}}{(1-\lambda)^{2}}\bigg).

(iii)

\lambda
is the unique solution of

H(\lambda)\coloneqq\frac{(\lambda^{2}+1)p-2\lambda q}{2(1-\lambda^{2})^{2}}-1=0

(14)

in
\left]-1,1\right[
.

Proof
.
Item

(i)
: Because
u_{0}\neq 0,v_{0}\neq 0
, we obtain
|\lambda|<1
,

u=\frac{u_{0}}{(1+\lambda)}
and
v=\frac{v_{0}}{(1-\lambda)}
by
Lemma
3.4
and (

)-(

).
Then we have the following equivalences

\displaystyle\norm{u}^{2}-\norm{v}^{2}=2

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\frac{\norm{u_0}^{2}}{(1+\lambda)^{2}}-\frac{\norm{v_0}^{2}}{(1-\lambda)^{2}}=2

(15)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow(1-\lambda)^{2}\norm{u_0}^{2}-(1+\lambda)^{2}\norm{v_0}^{2}=2(1-\lambda)^{2}(1+\lambda)^{2}

(16)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow(1-\lambda)^{2}\norm{u_0}^{2}-(1+\lambda)^{2}\norm{v_0}^{2}=2(1-\lambda^{2})^{2}.

(17)

Item

(ii)
: Substitute
u=\frac{u_{0}}{(1+\lambda)}
and
v=\frac{v_{0}}{(1-\lambda)}
in
f_{1}
.

Item

(iii)
:
By
Item

(i)
, we have

(1-\lambda)^{2}\norm{u_0}^{2}-(1+\lambda)^{2}\norm{v_0}^{2}=2(1-\lambda^{2})^{2}.

(18)

Since

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt(1-\lambda)^{2}\norm{u_0}^{2}-(1+\lambda)^{2}\norm{v_0}^{2}

(19)

\displaystyle=\big(1+\lambda^{2}-2\lambda\big)\|u_{0}\|^{2}-\big(1+\lambda^{2}+2\lambda\big)\|v_{0}\|^{2}

(20)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}\big(\|u_{0}\|^{2}-\|v_{0}\|^{2}\big)+\big(\|u_{0}\|^{2}-\|v_{0}\|^{2}\big)-2\lambda\big(\|u_{0}\|^{2}+\|v_{0}\|^{2}\big)

(21)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}p+p-2\lambda q,

(22)

using
Eq.

22
on left side of
Eq.

18
, we obtain
\lambda^{2}p+p-2\lambda q=2(1-\lambda^{2})^{2}
,
a univariate quartic equation in
\lambda

\displaystyle H(\lambda)\coloneqq\frac{(\lambda^{2}+1)p-2\lambda q}{2(1-\lambda^{2})^{2}}-1=0.

(23)

We show that

has a unique solution in
]-1,1[
.
Because
u_{0}\neq 0,v_{0}\neq 0
,
we know that

,
and using it along-with

from

,
we get
p\lambda\leq|p||\lambda|\leq q|\lambda|
, and

\displaystyle H^{\prime}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-\lambda^{2})^{3}}\big(-q(1+3\lambda^{2})+p(\lambda^{3}+3\lambda)\big)

(24)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(1-\lambda^{2})^{3}}\big(-q(1+3\lambda^{2})+|p||\lambda|(\lambda^{2}+3)\big)

(25)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(1-\lambda^{2})^{3}}\big(-q(1+3\lambda^{2})+q|\lambda|(\lambda^{2}+3)\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{q}{(1-\lambda^{2})^{3}}\big((|\lambda|-1)^{3}\big)

(27)

\displaystyle=\frac{-q}{((1-|\lambda|)(1+|\lambda|))^{3}}\big((1-|\lambda|)^{3}\big)

Since

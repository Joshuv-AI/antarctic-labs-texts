Infinitesimal deformations of opers in positive characteristic and the de Rham cohomology of symmetric products
Yasuhiro Wakabayashi

Abstract
. The Eichler-Shimura isomorphism describes a certain cohomology group with coefficients in a space of polynomials by using holomorphic modular/cusp forms. It determines a canonical decomposition of the corresponding de Rham cohomology group associated to a specific oper on a Riemann surface. One purpose of the present paper is to establish its analogue for opers in positive characteristic. We first discuss some basic properties on the (parabolic) de Rham cohomology groups and deformation spaces of G G -opers (where G G is a semisimple algebraic group of adjoint type) in a general formulation. In particular, it is shown that the deformation space of a G G -oper induced from an SL 2 \mathrm{SL}_{2} -oper decomposes into a direct sum of the (parabolic) de Rham cohomology groups of its symmetric products. As a consequence, we obtain an Eichler-Shimura-type decomposition for dormant opers on general pointed stable curves by considering a transversal intersection of related spaces in the de Rham moduli space.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
The Eichler-Shimura isomorphism for a modular curve

The Eichler-Shimura isomorphism describes a certain cohomology group with coefficients in a space of polynomials by using holomorphic modular/cusp forms.
To be precise, let

N
be an integer with
N\geq 5
, and

\Gamma

the level
N
congruence subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.
Given each
l\geq 2
,
we have the
\mathbb{C}
-vector space

(1)

\displaystyle V_{l-2}:=\mathrm{Sym}^{l-2}(\mathbb{C}X+\mathbb{C}Y)

of homogenous degree
l-2
polynomials in
\mathbb{C}[X,Y]
.
It has a
\Gamma
-action given by
\gamma(P)(X,Y)=P(aX+cY,bX+dY)
for any
P\in V_{l-2}
and
\gamma:=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma
.

The Eichler-Shimura isomorphism (cf.
[
Eic
]
,
[
Shi
]
) asserts a canonical isomorphism for the cohomology group
H^{1}(\Gamma,V_{l-2})
of the resulting
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
-module
V_{l-2}
:

(2)

\displaystyle M_{l}(\Gamma,\mathbb{C})\oplus\overline{S_{l}(\Gamma,\mathbb{C})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}H^{1}(\Gamma,V_{l-2}),

where
\overline{(-)}
denotes the complex conjugation and

M_{l}(\Gamma,\mathbb{C})
(resp.,
S_{l}(\Gamma,\mathbb{C})
) denotes the
\mathbb{C}
-vector space of modular forms (resp., cusp forms) of weight
l
and level
N
.

This isomorphism can be interpreted in geometric terms, as follows.
Let
Y_{\Gamma}
denote the Riemann surface defined as the quotient
\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}
of
the upper half plane
\mathbb{H}:=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}\,|\,\mathrm{Im}(z)>0\right\}

by the natural
\Gamma
-action.
Also, let
X_{\Gamma}:=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^{*}
, where
\mathbb{H}^{*}:=\mathbb{H}\cup\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
(as a subset of
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
, equipped with a natural
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-action) has a topology inducing
a structure of compact Riemann surface on the quotient
X_{\Gamma}
.
The moduli interpretation of
Y_{\Gamma}

brings us
a universal
family of complex tori

\pi:E\rightarrow Y_{\Gamma}

of level
N
.

The relative de Rham cohomology of this family forms
a holomorphic vector bundle
\mathcal{F}_{Y}:=\mathbb{R}^{1}\pi_{*}\Omega_{E/Y_{\Gamma}}^{\bullet}
of rank
2
; it carries a (flat) connection
\nabla_{Y}
(called the
Gauss-Manin connection
), as well as a
2
-step decreasing filtration
\{\mathcal{F}_{Y}^{j}\}_{j=0}^{2}
(called the
Hodge filtration
) given by
\mathcal{F}^{0}_{Y}:=\mathcal{F}_{Y}
,
\mathcal{F}^{2}_{Y}:=0
, and
\mathcal{F}^{1}_{Y}:=\pi_{*}(\Omega_{E/Y_{\Gamma}})
, considered as a line subbundle of
\mathcal{F}_{Y}
via the injection
\mathcal{F}^{1}_{Y}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{F}_{Y}
arising from the Hodge to de Rham spectral sequence
{}^{\prime}E_{1}^{a,b}:=\mathbb{R}^{b}\pi_{*}\Omega^{a}_{E/Y_{\Gamma}}\Rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{a+b}\pi_{*}\Omega_{E/Y_{\Gamma}}^{\bullet}
.

Each object in the data
(\mathcal{F}_{Y},\nabla_{Y},\{\mathcal{F}^{j}_{Y}\}_{j=0}^{2})
has a natural extension over
X_{\Gamma}

by admitting simple poles along the cusps
D:=X_{\Gamma}\setminus Y_{\Gamma}
;
the resulting collection

(3)

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{X}^{\heartsuit}:=(\mathcal{F}_{X},\nabla_{X},\{\mathcal{F}_{X}^{j}\}_{j=0}^{2})

specifies a canonical example of an
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
-oper
(cf. Remark
2.1
), and
the line bundle
\varTheta:=\mathcal{F}_{X}^{1}
satisfies
\varTheta^{\otimes 2}\cong\Omega_{X}(D)
.

We shall write

\mathrm{Sym}^{l-2}\mathscr{F}_{X}

for the
(l-2)
-nd symmetric power of the flat bundle
\mathscr{F}_{X}:=(\mathcal{F}_{X},\nabla_{X})
.
The
1
-st de Rham cohomology group

H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{1}(X,\mathrm{Sym}^{l-2}\mathscr{F}_{X})

of

\mathrm{Sym}^{l-2}\mathscr{F}_{X}

fits into the short exact sequence

(4)

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow H^{0}(X,\varTheta^{\otimes l})\longrightarrow H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{1}(X,\mathrm{Sym}^{l-2}\mathscr{F}_{X})\longrightarrow H^{0}(X,\varTheta^{\otimes l}(-D))^{\vee}\longrightarrow 0

arising from the filtration on
\mathrm{Sym}^{l-2}\mathscr{F}_{X}
induced by
\{\mathcal{F}_{X}^{j}\}_{j}
.
Since

\mathrm{Sym}^{l-2}\mathscr{F}_{X}
corresponds to the local system determined by the
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
-module
V_{l-2}
via the Riemann-Hilbert correspondence,
there exists a canonical identification

(5)

\displaystyle H^{1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\mathrm{Sym}^{l-2}\mathscr{F}_{X})=H^{1}(\Gamma,V_{l-2})

(cf.
[
Del
, Chap. II, Corollaire 6.10]
).
On the other hand,
the elements of
H^{0}(X,\varTheta^{\otimes l})
can be interpreted, via descent along the covering map
\mathbb{H}\twoheadrightarrow Y_{\Gamma}
, as those of
M_{l}(\Gamma,\mathbb{C})
,
and moreover, the Petersson inner product yields

H^{0}(X,\varTheta^{\otimes l}(-D))^{\vee}=\overline{S_{l}(\Gamma,\mathbb{C})}
.

Under these identifications, the Eichler-Shimura isomorphism (
2
) may be regarded as a canonical choice of a splitting of the short exact sequence (
4
) given by taking periods.
See, e.g.,
[
BrHa
]
,
[
KaSc
]
,
[
Sch
]
for algebraic treatments of the Eichler-Shimura isomorphism in terms of de Rham cohomology.

1.2.
Analogous decomposition in positive characteristic

The present paper aims to
investigate the short exact sequence (
4
) for opers in positive characteristic (including the case of parabolic de Rham cohomology) in relation to their deformation spaces, as well as
to establish an analogue of the Eichler-Shimura type decomposition for such opers.
The present paper contains many arguments based on those in
[
Wak4
]
and can be positioned as a continuation of that work.

Let
p
be an odd prime,
l
an integer with
3\leq l\leq p-1
,
k
an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p
,

(g,r)
a pair of nonnegative integers with
2g-2+r>0
,
and
\mathscr{X}:=(X,\{\sigma_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r})

an
r
-pointed stable curve over
k
of genus
g
, where
X
denotes a nodal curve over
k
and
\{\sigma_{i}\}_{i}
is a set of marked points on
X
.
(The present paper deals not only with smooth curves but also with
pointed
and
stable
curves, since the same arguments can be applied.
However, it is not so essential to consider such a fairly general situation.)

Suppose that we are given an
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
-oper
\mathscr{F}^{\heartsuit}:=(\mathcal{F},\nabla,\{\mathcal{F}^{j}\}_{j})
on
\mathscr{X}
.
Then, the
2
-fold tensor product of the line bundle
\varTheta:=\mathcal{F}^{1}\left(\subseteq\mathcal{F}\right)
is isomorphic to
the sheaf
\Omega_{X^{\mathrm{log}}}
of logarithmic
1
-forms on the log curve
X^{\mathrm{log}}
determined by
\mathscr{X}
.
Just as in the complex case,

\mathscr{F}^{\heartsuit}
associates
the
1
-st de Rham cohomology
H^{1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\mathrm{Sym}^{l-2}\mathscr{F})
of the
(l-2)
-nd symmetric power
\mathrm{Sym}^{l-2}\mathscr{F}
of
\mathscr{F}:=(\mathcal{F},\nabla)
, fitting into the
short exact sequence

(6)

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow H^{0}(X,\varTheta^{\otimes l})\longrightarrow H^{1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\mathrm{Sym}^{l-2}\mathscr{F})\longrightarrow H^{0}(X,\varTheta^{\otimes l}(-D))^{\vee}\longrightarrow 0

(cf. Theorem
5.2
, (i)), where
D
denotes the divisor on
X
given by the union of the
\sigma_{i}
’s.
(Similarly, a version for the parabolic de Rham cohomology of
\mathrm{Sym}^{l-1}\mathscr{F}
holds, as asserted in Theorem
5.2
, (ii).)

Our question regarding this sequence is described as follows:

For what kind of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
-opers
\mathscr{F}^{\heartsuit}
can we construct a
canonical
splitting of (
6
) arising from geometry such as the classical Eichler-Shimura isomorphism?

Even when

\mathscr{F}^{\heartsuit}
is taken to be the mod
p
reduction of the “
\mathscr{F}_{X}^{\heartsuit}
” defined on a modular curve as above,
the classical Eichler-Shimura isomorphism would not be appropriate in constructing a canonical splitting
because of its non-algebraic construction.

To get an answer to the above question, we discuss
opers with vanishing
p
-curvature,
called
dormant opers
.
Here, recall that
p
-curvature is an invariant for (flat) connections measuring the obstruction to the compatibility of
p
-power structures appearing in certain associated spaces of infinitesimal symmetries; this is a key ingredient of the
p
-adic Teichmuller theory developed by S. Mochizuki (cf.
[
Moc1
]
,
[
Moc2
]
), in which various moduli spaces of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
-opers (or
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
-opers) characterized by the degree of vanishing
p
-curvature were investigated.
That work suggests that
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
-opers whose
p
-curvature are close to zero
may be thought of as candidates for suitable analogues of the canonical
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
-opers on modular curves.
In fact, we prove the following assertion, which is
one of the main results in the present paper (cf. Theorem
7.7
for a similar assertion with slightly relaxed assumptions).

Theorem A

(cf. Corollary
7.8
)
.

Let us keep the above notation.
Moreover,
suppose that
l
is even,
\mathscr{F}^{\heartsuit}
is dormant (i.e. has vanishing
p
-curvature), and
\mathscr{X}
is general in the moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,r}
of
r
-pointed stable curves of genus
g
.
Then, there exists a
canonical
splitting of (
6
).
In particular, we obtain
a
canonical
decomposition

(7)

\displaystyle H^{1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\mathrm{Sym}^{l-2}\mathscr{F})=H^{0}(X,\varTheta^{\otimes l})\oplus H^{0}(X,\varTheta^{\otimes l}(-D))^{\vee}

of the
1
-st de Rham cohomology
H^{1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\mathrm{Sym}^{l-2}\mathscr{F})
of
\mathrm{Sym}^{l-2}\mathscr{F}
.

Other relatively important results are Theorems
3.1
,
4.9
,
4.12
,
5.2
,
5.3
,
7.2
,
7.7
, and Corollaries
7.5
,
7.6
.

1.3.
Outline of our argument

The present paper develops a general theory of opers on pointed stable curves and their infinitesimal deformations, that may be positioned as a continuation of the related work in
[
Wak4
]
.
(The first half of our discussion proceeds under the situation where the characteristic of the base field is not necessarily positive.)
Here, we briefly describe the arguments and results leading to
the above theorem.
(There are many assertions proved in this text that are not directly involved in proving Theorem
A
. For example, the study of parabolic de Rham cohomology groups and their self-duality assertion are included; see Corollary
4.6
.
Regarding the self-duality, we can find some previous results involved, e.g.,
[
Can
, Theorem 5.2, (a)]
,
[

, Remark 3.2]
, and
[

, Theorem 2.7, (i)]
.)

Let

be a semisimple algebraic group of adjoint type with certain properties (cf.
the assumption imposed at the beginning of §
1.4
).
The GIT quotient of its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}:=\mathrm{Lie}(G)
by the adjoint

-action defines a

-scheme

.
We shall fix an element

of
\mathfrak{c}(k)^{\times r}
, which lifts to an element
\widetilde{\rho}\in\mathfrak{g}^{\times r}
via the Kostant section (cf. (
39
)).

Denote by

(8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}onn_{G}

(cf. (
20
))
the moduli stack
classifying flat
G
-bundles
on
\mathscr{X}
.
As proved in Theorem

,
the moduli stack

(9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}p_{G}\ \left(\text{resp.,}\ \mathcal{O}p_{G,\rho}\right)

-opers (resp.,
G
-opers of radii

) forms a locally closed substack of

.

If we are given a

-oper
\mathscr{E}^{\spadesuit}
classified by a closed point

,
then the inclusion

induces, via differentiation, a

We investigate these spaces, as well as the relationship between them, by describing relevant infinitesimal deformations in terms of various (de Rham) cohomology groups associated to
\mathscr{E}^{\spadesuit}
.
The resulting description allows us to obtain
a short exact sequence

(10)

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow T_{q}\mathcal{O}p_{G}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\mathrm{inclusion}}T_{q}\mathcal{C}onn_{G}\longrightarrow T_{q}^{\vee}\mathcal{O}p_{G,\rho}\longrightarrow 0

(cf. Theorem

), where

denotes the cotangent space at

.

Next, since the base field

,
one can define
the closed substack

(11)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}onn_{G}^{\psi=0}

(cf. (
222
)) of

The intersection

(12)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}p_{G}^{{}^{\mathrm{Zzz...}}}:=\mathcal{O}p_{G}\cap\mathcal{C}onn_{G}^{\psi=0}\

(cf. (

-opers
and was substantially investigated in
[

]
from a viewpoint of enumerative geometry.
One important fact proved in
loc. cit.

is the étaleness of
\mathcal{O}p_{G}^{{}^{\mathrm{Zzz...}}}\!
for a sufficiently general
\mathscr{X}
(cf.
[
Wak4
, Theorem 8.19]
).
(This fact also played an essential role in the proof of Joshi’s conjecture; see
[
Wak4
, Theorem 9.10]
.)
Since the equality of dimensions

(13)

\displaystyle\mathrm{dim}(T_{q}\mathcal{O}p_{G})+\mathrm{dim}(T_{q}\mathcal{C}onn_{G}^{\psi=0})=\mathrm{dim}(T_{q}\mathcal{C}onn_{G})

holds (cf. Proposition

),
the ètaleness means that

and

Hence,
the composite

(14)

\displaystyle T_{q}\mathcal{C}onn_{G}^{\psi=0}\hookrightarrow T_{q}\mathcal{C}onn_{G}\twoheadrightarrow T^{\vee}_{q}\mathcal{O}p_{G,\rho}

is an isomorphism, and the composite of its inverse and the inclusion

determines a split injection of (

It follows that
there exists a canonical decomposition

(15)

\displaystyle T_{q}\mathcal{O}p_{G}\oplus T_{q}^{\vee}\mathcal{O}p_{G}=T_{q}\mathcal{C}onn_{G}\

Finally, let us consider the case of

with

(This is only the setting considered here for simplicity, and the main text will deal with a general

\displaystyle T_{q}\mathcal{O}p_{G}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n-1}H^{0}(X,\varTheta^{\otimes 2j}),\hskip 28.45274ptT_{q}^{\vee}\mathcal{O}p_{G,\rho}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n-1}H^{0}(X,\varTheta^{\otimes 2j}(-D))^{\vee}

.

As observed in

.

Flat

inducing
\alpha_{G}
via change of structure group by

.

(\mathcal{E}_{G},\nabla)
is a flat
G
-bundle on
\mathscr{X}
, then a
B
-reduction
\mathcal{E}_{B}
of
\mathcal{E}_{G}

for which the pair
(\mathcal{E}_{B},\nabla)
defines a
G
-oper is (if it exists) uniquely determined up to isomorphism.

Proof.

We may assume, without loss of generality, that

,
\nabla:=\nabla_{1}=\nabla_{2}
, and that
\alpha_{G}

coincides with the identity morphism of
\mathcal{E}
.
The two Borel reductions
\mathcal{E}_{B,1}
,
\mathcal{E}_{B,2}
of
\mathcal{E}
define the
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-submodules of
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{E}}
, i.e.,
\mathfrak{g}^{0}_{\mathcal{E}_{B,1}}\left(=\mathfrak{b}_{\mathcal{E}_{B,1}}\right)
and
\mathfrak{g}^{0}_{\mathcal{E}_{B,2}}\left(=\mathfrak{b}_{\mathcal{E}_{B,2}}\right)

and

Hence, by using

we can verify the equality

Next, let us take a closed point

of
X
, and fix an identification

\mathcal{E}_{B,1}|_{x}=B
, which extends to

\mathcal{E}|_{x}=G
and moreover induces

for some

It follows that

of
\mathfrak{g}^{1}
via the adjoint operator

given by

.

denotes the unipotent radical of
B

(cf.
[

, § 23.1, Corollary D]
), and we have

(cf.
[

Therefore, the equality

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

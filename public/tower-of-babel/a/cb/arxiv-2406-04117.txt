All crepant resolutions of hyperpolygon spaces via their Cox rings. Thanks: Email: ah2941@bath.ac.uk , Affiliation: University of Bath
Austin Hubbard

Abstract
. We construct and enumerate all crepant resolutions of hyperpolygon spaces, a family of conical symplectic singularities arising as Nakajima quiver varieties associated to a star-shaped quiver. We provide an explicit presentation of the Cox ring of any such crepant resolution. Using techniques developed by Arzhantsev-Derenthal-Hausen-Laface we construct all crepant resolutions of the hyperpolygon spaces, including those which are not projective over the singularity. We find that the number of crepant resolutions equals the Hoşten-Morris numbers. In proving these results, we obtain a description of all complete geometric quotients associated to the classical GIT problem constructing moduli spaces of ordered points on the projective line. These moduli spaces appear as the Lagrangian subvarieties of crepant resolutions of hyperpolygon spaces fixed under the conical action.



1.
Introduction

Hyperpolygon spaces are Nakajima quiver varieties associated to an
n
-pointed star-shaped quiver for a fixed dimension vector
v
, for
n\geq 4
. They form a family of symplectic varieties of dimension
2n-6
, generalising the crepant partial resolutions of the Kleinian singularity of type
D_{4}
. First introduced by Konno
[
22
]
as hyperkähler analogues of polygon spaces, hyperpolygon spaces have since been investigated by Harada-Proudfoot
[
14
]
and Godinho-Mandini
[
13
]
.
Polygon spaces arise as compactifications of the moduli space of
n
-distinct ordered points on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
and have served as foundational examples in the theory of GIT. Much like the well-studied polygon spaces, hyperpolygon spaces have provided a ripe testing ground for conjectures pertaining to Nakajima quiver varieties and algebraic symplectic reduction.

We provide explicit presentations of the Cox rings of smooth hyperpolygon spaces
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}
, for
n\geq 5
. As an application, we construct all crepant resolutions of the most singular hyperpolygon spaces
\mathfrak{M}_{0}
. Work of Bellamy-Craw-Rayan-Schedler-Weiss
[
3
]
establishes a construction of all projective crepant resolutions of
\mathfrak{M}_{0}
as Nakajima quiver varieties, using Mumford’s GIT. To produce more general quotients we use the
A_{2}
-quotient construction developed by Arzhantsev-Derenthal-Hausen-Laface
[
1
]
.
This allows the construction of crepant resolutions that are not projective over
\mathfrak{M}_{0}
, which have been difficult to study until now.

We introduce maximally-biconnected complexes, a combinatorial object which we show parameterises the crepant resolutions of
\mathfrak{M}_{0}
. The number of maximally-biconnected complexes on the set
\{1,\ldots,n\}
is given by the
n
-th Hoşten-Morris number
[
16
]
. We find that the number of crepant resolutions of
\mathfrak{M}_{0}
up to isomorphism over
\mathfrak{M}_{0}
equals
\lambda(n)
.

Any crepant resolution of
\mathfrak{M}_{0}
admits a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action extending the conical
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on
\mathfrak{M}_{0}
. There is a unique Lagrangian subvariety inside the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-fixed locus, which is in almost all cases a polygon space. We see that the small birational transformation relating a pair of crepant resolutions is completely determined by the induced transformation on this Lagrangian subvariety, providing a close link between the crepant resolutions of
\mathfrak{M}_{0}
and polygon spaces. We study the polygon space GIT problem of
n
ordered points on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, extending the classical results to include non-projective quotients. This GIT problem is given by the action of
T:=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
on the affine variety
Y
given as the spectrum of the Cox ring of
\mathbb{P}^{n-3}
blown-up at
n-1
general points, for
n\geq 5
. We provide a combinatorial characterisation of the open subsets
U\subseteq Y
which admit geometric quotients
U\rightarrow U\operatorname{\!/\!\!/\!}T
with
U\operatorname{\!/\!\!/\!}T
complete. We show that the number of such open subsets equals
\lambda(n)-n
.

1.1.
A presentation of the Cox ring.

The hyperpolygon spaces
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}
are constructed as GIT quotients
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}:=\mu^{-1}(0)\operatorname{\!/\!\!/\!}_{\theta}G
, dependent on a choice of parameter
\theta\in\Theta\cong G^{\vee}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
. The most singular hyperpolygon space
\mathfrak{M}_{0}
, given as the affine GIT quotient
\mu^{-1}(0)\operatorname{\!/\!\!/\!}G
, is a conical symplectic singularity (see Definition
2.33
). It is shown by Bellamy-Craw-Rayan-Schedler-Weiss
[
3
]
, that for generic
\theta\in\Theta
, the natural morphism
\pi_{\theta}:\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}\rightarrow\mathfrak{M}_{0}
, induced by varying the parameter in
\Theta
, is a projective crepant resolution (see Theorem
2.41
). By work of Namikawa
[
30
]
, for generic
\theta\in\Theta
,
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}
is a Mori dream space (see Definition
2.14
), i.e. it has finitely-generated Cox ring. Even in cases where the Cox ring of a variety is finitely-generated, it is in general a difficult problem to provide explicit generators and relations for it.

The case of
n=4
is extremely well-studied. The graph underlying the star-shaped quiver with
4
external vertices is the affine Dynkin diagram of type
\widetilde{D}_{4}
. By work of Kronheimer
[
23
]
, the affine quotient
\mathfrak{M}_{0}
is the Kleinian singularity of type
D_{4}
. For
\theta\in\Theta
generic,
\pi_{\theta}:\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}\rightarrow\mathfrak{M}_{0}
is the minimal resolution of
\mathfrak{M}_{0}
. In this case, the Cox ring of the minimal resolution of
\mathfrak{M}_{0}
is computed by Donten-Bury
[
9
, Example 6.14]
.

In order to describe the Cox ring of
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}
, we introduce auxilliary geometric and algebraic objects (see Definition
3.1
): the affine variety
\mathcal{X}
is the affinisation of a principal bundle over
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}
; the
\mathbb{C}
-algebra
R
is a
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-graded polynomial ring, and
I\subseteq R
is a homogeneous ideal.

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix
n\geq 5
. The following
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-graded
\mathbb{C}
-algebras are isomorphic

(1)

\mathrm{Cox}(\mathfrak{M}_{\theta})
, for
\theta\in\Theta
generic.

(2)

\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{X}]^{\mathrm{SL}(2)}
.

(3)

R/I
.

The variety
\mathcal{X}
is a complete intersection of codimension 3 in
\mathbb{A}^{3n}
with a particularly simple description, suggesting that if one wants to work with a coordinate ring for
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}
defined by simple equations, then
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{X}]
can be seen as a better candidate than
\mathrm{Cox}(\mathfrak{M}_{\theta})
.

1.2.
All crepant resolutions of
\mathfrak{M}_{0}
.

In
[
3
]
, it is shown that any projective crepant resolution of
\mathfrak{M}_{0}
arises as a morphism
\pi_{\theta}:\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}\rightarrow\mathfrak{M}_{0}
, for generic
\theta
in a unique GIT chamber within the positive orthant
F\subseteq\Theta
. The positive orthant
F
is a fundamental domain for the action of the Namikawa-Weyl group on
\Theta
. They further provide an explicit hyperplane arrangement
\mathcal{A}
which determines the GIT fan for the action of
G
on
\mu^{-1}(0)
(see discussion above Definition
2.32
). This gives a combinatorial method for computing the number of projective crepant resolutions: it is the number of chambers of the GIT fan contained in
F
.

However, a crepant resolution need not be a projective morphism. We provide a construction of all the crepant resolutions of
\mathfrak{M}_{0}
, including non-projective resolutions. As an application, we show that for
n\geq 6
, there are many examples of non-projective crepant resolutions of
\mathfrak{M}_{0}
. Each crepant resolution arises as the geometric quotient of an open subset of
X:=\mathrm{Spec}(\mathrm{Cox}(\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}))
under the natural torus action induced by the
\mathrm{Pic}(\mathfrak{M}_{\theta})
-grading. We utilise the construction of
A_{2}
-quotients via bunches of orbit cones given by Arzhantsev-Derenthal-Hausen-Laface
[
1
, Section 3.1]
. The construction takes as input a bunch of orbit cones
\Psi\subseteq\Omega_{X}
(see Definition
2.5
), and produces an open subset
U(\Psi)\subseteq X
admitting a good quotient
U(\Psi)\rightarrow U(\Psi)\operatorname{\!/\!\!/\!}T
.
We define maximally-biconnected complexes on
[n]:=\{1,\ldots,n\}
(see Definition
4.6
), and describe a map associating to every such complex
\Delta
a maximal bunch of orbit cones
\Psi_{\Delta}
.

.

1.3.
All polygon spaces.

Polygon spaces can be constructed as GIT quotients of an affine variety
Y
isomorphic to the affine cone over the Grassmannian
\mathrm{Gr}(2,n)
, by an action of
T:=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
(see Definition
2.21
). We define full maximally-biconnected complexes on
[n]
(see Definition
4.6
), and describe a map associating to every such complex
\Delta
, a maximal bunch of orbit cones
\Phi_{\Delta}\subseteq\Omega_{Y}
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Fix
n\geq 5
. Let
\mathscr{U}
denote the set of
T
-invariant open subschemes
U\subseteq Y
admitting a geometric quotient
U\rightarrow U\operatorname{\!/\!\!/\!}T
, such that
U\operatorname{\!/\!\!/\!}T
is complete. There is a bijection

\displaystyle\{\text{Full maximally-biconnected complexes on }[n]\}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathscr{U},

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle\longmapsto U(\Phi_{\Delta})/T.

Corollary 1.5
.

We have
\#\mathscr{U}=\lambda(n)-n
, where
\lambda(n)
denotes the
n
-th Hoşten-Morris number (see Definition
4.10
).

For each hyperpolygon space
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}
, there is a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action contracting
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}
into the zero fiber
\pi_{\theta}^{-1}(0)
. If
\theta
is generic, there is a unique Lagrangian subvariety fixed under this action which is almost always a polygon space (see Definition
2.21
). If
\mathfrak{M}
is an arbitrary crepant resolution of
\mathfrak{M}_{0}
, there remains a contracting
\mathbb{C}^{*}
action and a unique Lagrangian subvariety fixed under this action, which may not be projective.

The quotients arising from Theorem
1.4
describe the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-fixed Lagrangians inside arbitrary crepant resolutions
\mathfrak{M}
of
\mathfrak{M}_{0}
. In fact, Theorem
1.4
is a vital component in the proof of Theorem
1.2
. It follows from the constructions involved in Theorems
1.2
and
1.4
that a crepant resolution
\mathfrak{M}
is projective if and only if the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-fixed Lagrangian it contains is projective. This explains the fact that for
n=5
, in which case
\dim(\mathfrak{M}_{0})=4
, all crepant resolutions
\mathfrak{M}
are projective, since the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-fixed Lagrangian in
\mathfrak{M}
is a smooth surface, which must be projective.

As an example, let
Z_{3}
be the projective 3-fold in
\mathbb{P}^{5}
defined by the equations

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{5}x_{i}=0,\ \ \sum_{i=0}^{5}x_{i}^{3}=0.

This is the Segre cubic; it is constructed as a projective GIT quotient of
Y
(see Example
4.19
). Its singular locus consists of 10 ordinary double points, each of which locally admits a pair of crepant resolutions. Since each singular point can be resolved independently,
Z_{3}
has
2^{10}
crepant resolutions. Work of Finkelnberg
[
11
]
establishes that only 332 of them are projective. All
2^{10}
minimal resolutions arise as quotients of
Y
described in Theorem
1.4
.

There is a 5-dimensional analogue of the Segre cubic, which we denote by
Z_{5}
, constructed as a projective GIT quotient of
Y
(see Example
4.20
). Its singular locus consists of 35 ordinary double points. Thus
Z_{5}
admits
2^{35}
crepant resolutions, of which only 495504 are projective (see Corollary
2.27
). All minimal resolutions can again be constructed as quotients of
Y
described by Theorem
1.4
.

2.
Background

2.1.
GIT quotients.

We begin with a summary of various GIT quotient constructions, following the development given by Arzhantsev-Derenthal-Hausen-Laface
[
1
]
.

A variety is an integral separated scheme of finite-type over
\mathbb{C}
. Let
G
be a reductive algebraic group acting on an affine variety
X=\mathrm{Spec}(R)
. Finite generation of the subring of
G
-invariant functions
R^{G}\subseteq R
provides the construction of an affine quotient variety
X\operatorname{\!/\!\!/\!}G=\mathrm{Spec}(R^{G})
.

There is an induced
G
-action on the coordinate ring
R
. The
R^{G}
-module of semi-invariant functions of weight
\theta\in\Theta
is

R_{\theta}:=\{f\in R\ |\ g\cdot f=\theta(g^{-1})f\}\subseteq R.

Let
\Theta:=G^{\vee}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
. If
\theta\in\Theta\setminus G^{\vee}
, set
R_{\theta}=0
.
Given
\theta\in\Theta
, Mumford’s GIT
[
27
]
gives a construction of refined quotient spaces
X\operatorname{\!/\!\!/\!}_{\theta}G
, projective over
X\operatorname{\!/\!\!/\!}G
.

Definition 2.1
.

Given
\theta\in\Theta
, a point
x\in X
is
\theta
-semistable
if there exists some
n>0
, and some homogeneous
f\in R_{n\theta}
, such that
f(x)\neq 0
. Let
X^{\theta\text{-ss}}
denote the
G
-invariant open subset of
\theta
-semistable points in
X
.

For all
\theta\in\Theta
, the
\theta
-semistable locus admits a good quotient given by

X\operatorname{\!/\!\!/\!}_{\theta}G:=\mathrm{Proj}\Big(\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}R_{n\theta}\Big).

The inclusion of
R_{0}
into
\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}R_{n\theta}
induces a projective morphism
X\operatorname{\!/\!\!/\!}_{\theta}G\rightarrow X\operatorname{\!/\!\!/\!}G
.

From now on we consider the action of an algebraic torus
T
on
X
. In this case the coordinate ring
R
is graded by
T^{\vee}
. We have
R\cong\bigoplus_{\theta\in T^{\vee}}R_{\theta}
. Suppose
f\in R_{\theta}
for some
\theta\in T^{\vee}\subseteq\Theta
, then let
\deg(f)=\theta
.

Definition 2.2
.

The
orbit cone
of
x\in X
is the convex polyhedral cone

\omega_{x}:=\mathbb{Q}_{>0}\cdot\{\theta\in\Theta\ |\ f(x)\neq 0\ \text{for some}\ f\in R_{\theta}\}\subseteq\Theta.

We denote by
\Omega_{X}
the set of all orbit cones of points in
X
. This is a finite set
[
1
, Proposition 3.1.1.10]
. Given an orbit cone
\omega\in\Omega_{X}
, we denote its relative interior by
\omega^{\circ}
.
The
GIT cone
associated to
\theta\in\Theta
is the intersection

C(\theta):=\bigcap_{\{\omega_{x}\in\Omega_{X}|\theta\in\omega_{x}\}}\omega_{x}.

We say
\theta
is
generic
if
C(\theta)
is top dimensional. We call the interior of a top dimensional GIT cone a GIT chamber.

Definition 2.3
.

A
quasi-fan
in
\Theta
is a finite collection of convex polyhedral cones in
\Theta
, closed under taking faces, and such that the intersection of any two cones is a face of both. A
fan
is a quasi-fan consisting of strongly-convex polyhedral cones.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
1
]
, Theorem 3.1.2.8)
.

The collection of all GIT cones gives a quasi-fan in
\Theta
. Given
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\Theta
, we have that
X^{\theta_{1}\text{-ss}}=X^{\theta_{2}\text{-ss}}
if and only if
C(\theta_{1})=C(\theta_{2})
.

We are interested in constructing quotients that are not projective over
X\operatorname{\!/\!\!/\!}T
. Since the natural morphism
X\operatorname{\!/\!\!/\!}_{\theta}T\rightarrow X\operatorname{\!/\!\!/\!}T
is always projective we require a construction that produces a larger class of quotients.

Definition 2.5
.

A
bunch of orbit cones
is a non-empty collection of orbit cones
\Phi\subseteq\Omega_{X}
, such that:

(1)

Given
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\in\Phi
, then
\omega_{1}^{\circ}\cap\omega_{2}^{\circ}\neq\emptyset
.

(2)

Given
\omega_{1}\in\Phi
, and
\omega_{2}\in\Omega_{X}
such that
\omega_{1}^{\circ}\subseteq\omega_{2}^{\circ}
, then
\omega_{2}\in\Phi
.

A
maximal bunch of orbit cones
is a bunch of orbit cones that is not contained in any strictly larger bunch of orbit cones.

Definition 2.6
.

Given a bunch of orbit cones
\Phi\subseteq\Omega_{X}
, we define an open subscheme

U(\Phi)=\{x\in X\ |\ \omega_{x}\in\Phi\}.

Definition 2.7
.

A variety
Y
has the
A_{2}
-property
if for all
y_{0},y_{1}\in Y
there exists an affine open subset of
Y
containing both
y_{0}
and
y_{1}
.

Proposition 2.8

(
[
1
]
, Proposition 3.1.3.8)
.

Let
\Phi\subseteq\Omega_{X}
be a bunch of orbit cones. Then
U(\Phi)
is a
T
-invariant open subscheme admitting a good quotient
U(\Phi)\rightarrow U(\Phi)\operatorname{\!/\!\!/\!}T
. The quotient
U(\Phi)\operatorname{\!/\!\!/\!}T
has the
A_{2}
-property.

Let
x\in X
, and let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{r}\in R
be a collection of homogeneous functions such that
f_{i}(x)\neq 0
and
\omega_{x}
is generated by
\deg(f_{i})
. We define a
T
-invariant open subscheme
U_{x}:=\mathrm{Spec}(R[f_{1}^{-1},\cdots,f_{r}^{-1}])\subseteq U(\Phi)
. This open subscheme does not depend on the choice of
f_{i}

[
1
, Construction 3.1.3.7]
. Since
x\in U_{x}
, we have that the collection of

for

forms an open covering of

. The affine quotients
U_{x}\operatorname{\!/\!\!/\!}T
glue together appropriately to form a good quotient.

Remark 2.9
.

Given
\theta\in\Theta
, define
\Phi_{\theta}:=\{\omega\in\Omega_{X}\ |\ \theta\in\omega^{\circ}\}
. This is a maximal bunch of orbit cones, and

. Hence the set of quotients defined via bunches of orbit cones includes the set of projective GIT quotients.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
U\subseteq X
be a
T
-invariant open subset admitting a good quotient
\pi:U\rightarrow U\operatorname{\!/\!\!/\!}T
. Given

, we say

is

.

Let
\mathscr{A}
denote the collection of

-invariant open subschemes

such that

admits a good quotient

, where

-property, and

Theorem 2.11

(

]
, Theorem 3.1.4.4)
.

There is a bijection

2.2.
Cox rings and Mori dream spaces.

In this paper, we consider complete varieties and resolutions of singular affine varieties, both of which fit into the following class.

Definition 2.13
.

A variety
X
is called
proper (projective) over affine
if
\Gamma(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{C}
-algebra, and the natural affinisation morphism

X\rightarrow\mathrm{Spec}(\Gamma(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}))
is proper (projective).

Definition 2.14
.

Let
X
be a smooth, proper over affine variety, with finitely generated and free Picard group of rank
n
. Let
L_{1},\ldots,L_{n}
be a collection of line bundles chosen such that their isomorphism classes form a basis of
\mathrm{Pic}(X)
. Given
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, let
L_{\lambda}:=L_{1}^{\otimes\lambda_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes L_{n}^{\otimes\lambda_{n}}
.
The
Cox ring
of
X
is the
\mathrm{Pic}(X)
-graded
\mathbb{C}
-algebra given by

\mathrm{Cox}(X)=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\Gamma(X,L_{\lambda}).

In the case that
\mathrm{Cox}(X)
is a finitely-generated
\mathbb{C}
-algebra, then
X
is a
Mori dream space
. When
X
is a Mori dream space the
total coordinate space
of
X
is
\overline{X}:=\mathrm{Spec}(\mathrm{Cox}(X))
.

Suppose
X
is a Mori dream space. Then
X
carries an ample line bundle
L
, satisfying

X\cong\mathrm{Proj}\Big(\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\Gamma(X,L^{\otimes k})\Big).

Let
T
be the algebraic torus with character group
T^{\vee}=\mathrm{Pic}(X)
. Since
\mathrm{Cox}(X)
is
\mathrm{Pic}(X)
-graded, the total coordinate space
\overline{X}:=\mathrm{Spec}(\mathrm{Cox}(X))
is equipped with a
T
-action. Letting
\theta=[L]\in T^{\vee}
, we have that
\overline{X}\operatorname{\!/\!\!/\!}_{\theta}T\cong X
. Hence a Mori dream space is recovered from its total coordinate space as a GIT quotient.

Definition 2.15
.

Let
X
and
L_{1},\ldots,L_{n}
be as in Definition
2.14
. The
Cox sheaf
of
X
is the
\mathrm{Pic}(X)
-graded sheaf of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-algebras given by

\mathscr{C}ox(X)\vcentcolon=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}L_{\lambda}.

The relative spectrum
\widehat{X}=\mathrm{Spec}_{X}(\mathscr{C}ox(X))
carries a
T
action induced by the
\mathrm{Pic}(X)
-grading on
\mathscr{C}ox(X)
. The
total coordinate space
of
\overline{X}\vcentcolon=\mathrm{Spec}(\mathrm{Cox}(X))
is the affinisation of
\widehat{X}
.

Lemma 2.16

(
[
1
]
, Construction 1.6.3.1)
.

Let
X
be as in Definition
2.14
. There is a
T
-equivariant open immersion
\widehat{X}\rightarrow~\overline{X}
, with complement of codimension
\geq 2
. If
X
is projective over affine and
L
is an ample line bundle with
\theta=[L]

Proposition 3.8

We begin by constructing the morphism. A presentation of

is given above Definition
2.32
. Consider the algebra map

The ideal defining

in

We have
f(\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}z_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}y_{i}^{2}\in J
, and
f(x_{i}z_{i}+y_{i}w_{i})=x_{i}c_{i}y_{i}-y_{i}c_{i}x_{i}=0
, for all
i\in[n]
. Similarly all generators of the ideal can be seen to have image inside
J
, hence
\iota^{*}
factors through
\mathbb{C}[\mu^{-1}(0)]

respects the
G
-action.

Let
\iota:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mu^{-1}(0)
denote the
G
-equivariant morphism induced by
\iota^{*}
.
Let

On

Let

. Suppose

, we have that

.

We claim

is normal. Let

denote the variety given by the same presentation as

, but with

, for all

. Suppose
(p_{i},q_{i})\neq(0,0)
, for all
i\in[n]
. Suppose the induced partition
P
has size 1, then there is a subgroup
\mathbb{G}_{a}\subseteq G
, which acts at the vertex
0\in Q_{0}
, which fixes the 1-dimensional linear subspace given by
\mathrm{Span}((p_{i},q_{i})\ |\ i\in[n])
, which stabilises
p
. Suppose
P
has size 2, then there is a maximal torus in
\mathrm{GL}(2)
with image in
G
that stabilises
p
. Finally supposing
P
has size
\geq 3
, then supposing
[g_{0},g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}]\in G
stabilises
p
, we must have that
g_{0}=\lambda I
(since
g_{0}
must have
\geq 3
linearly independent eigenvectors), and we must have
g_{i}=\lambda
. Thus
(g_{0},g_{1},\ldots,g_{n})\in\Delta
, hence
p
has trivial stabiliser. It follows that the closed points with trivial stabiliser lie in the complement of a closed subset. Denote this complement by
\mathcal{Y}^{\mathrm{free}}
.

By
[
27
, Proposition 3.1]
, we have that the restriction of
\pi_{Y}
to
\pi_{Y}^{-1}(Y\setminus\{0\})
is a locally trivial
\mathrm{SL}(2)
-bundle over

. Moreover
\mathcal{Y}^{\mathrm{free}}\subseteq\pi_{Y}^{-1}(Y\setminus\{0\})
. Since the restricted morphism
\pi_{Y}^{-1}(Y\setminus\{0\})\rightarrow Y\setminus\{0\}
is a geometric quotient of the
\mathrm{SL}(2)
-action, it follows that a closed point

has trivial stabiliser if and only if
\pi_{Y}(p)
has trivial stabiliser. Hence
\pi_{Y}^{-1}(Y^{\mathrm{free}})=\mathcal{Y}^{\mathrm{free}}
. The description of
\mathcal{Y}^{\mathrm{free}}
as the locus where
(p_{i},q_{i})\neq 0
, for all

, and such that the induced partition has size
\geq 3
implies statement (2).
∎

Definition 4.3
.

Let
\omega\subseteq\Theta
be a convex cone. The dual cone to
\omega
is

\omega^{\vee}\vcentcolon=\{L\in\Theta^{*}\ |\ L(z)\geq 0,\ \text{for all }z\in\omega\}\subseteq\Theta^{*}.

Where
\Theta^{*}
denotes the vector space dual to
\Theta
. Recall
\Theta
is endowed with a fixed basis
e_{i}
, for
i\in[n]
. Let
f_{i}\in\Theta^{*}
be the dual basis to
e_{i}
. For
I\subseteq[n]
, let

v_{I}\vcentcolon=\sum_{j\notin I}f_{j}-\sum_{i\in I}f_{i}\in\Theta^{*}.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
I\subseteq[n]
. Let

\eta_{I}\vcentcolon=\mathrm{Cone}\big(e_{i}+e_{j}\ |\ \{i,j\}\not\subseteq I,\ \text{for }i,j\in[n]\big).

Note
\eta_{I}=\omega_{P_{I}}
, where
P_{I}
denotes the partition of
[n]
consisting of
I
together with the set of singleton sets in
I^{c}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\omega_{P},\omega_{Q}\in\Omega_{Y}^{\mathrm{free}}
. Then

(1)

\omega_{P}^{\vee}={\mathrm{Cone}}(v_{I}\ |\ I\in P)+{\mathrm{Cone}}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})\subseteq\Theta^{\vee}
.
Where
+
denotes the Minkowski sum.

(2)

\omega_{P}\subseteq\omega_{Q}
if and only if
Q
is a refinement of
P
.

(3)

if
\omega_{P}^{\circ}\cap\omega_{Q}^{\circ}=\emptyset
, then there exists some
I\subseteq[n]
such that
\omega_{P}\subseteq\eta_{I}
and
\omega_{Q}\subseteq\eta_{I^{c}}
.

Proof.

We first prove statement (1). Let

\chi\vcentcolon={\mathrm{Cone}}(v_{I}\ |\ I\in P)+{\mathrm{Cone}}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})\subseteq\Theta^{\vee}.

Let
I\in P
, and let
i,j\in[n]
such that
\{i,j\}\not\subseteq J
, for all
J\in P
. Then

v_{I}(e_{i}+e_{j})=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }i\ \text{or}\ j\ \text{lies in}\ I,\\
2,&i,j\notin I.\end{cases}

Moreover, given
k\in[n]
, then

f_{k}(e_{i}+e_{j})=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }i,j\neq k,\\
1,&\text{if }i\text{ or }j=k.\end{cases}

It follows that
\chi\subseteq\omega_{P}^{\vee}
, since the cone generators of
\chi
are non-negative on the cone generators of
\omega_{P}
. For the opposite inclusion let
z\in\chi^{\vee}
. Let
I\in P
, then by definition
v_{I}(z)\geq 0
. We can subtract a non-negative multiple of
e_{i}+e_{j}
from
z
, where
i\in I
and
j\notin I
, such that the result remains in
\chi^{\vee}
. We can repeat this process for each
I\in P
, when we reach the final
I
we have that
\sum_{j\notin I}z_{j}=0
. Hence
z
is a non-negative linear combination of the generators of
\omega
. Thus
\chi^{\vee}\subseteq\omega
. This completes the proof of statement (1).

To prove statement (2) assume that
Q
is a refinement of
P
, i.e. for all
I\in Q
, there is some
J\in P
with
I\subseteq J
. Therefore

v_{I}=v_{J}+2\sum_{i\in J\setminus I}f_{i}\in\omega_{P}^{\vee}.

Thus
\omega_{Q}^{\vee}\subseteq\omega_{P}^{\vee}
. For the opposite inclusion suppose
\omega_{P}\subseteq\omega_{Q}
. Let
I\in Q
, then by assumption
v_{I}\in\omega_{P}^{\vee}
. Suppose for a contradiction that
I\not\subseteq J
, for all
J\in P
. Then there exists distinct
J_{0},J_{1}\in P
with
j_{0}\in I\cap J_{0}
and
j_{1}\in I\cap J_{1}
. We have

v_{I}=\sum_{J\in P}\lambda_{J}v_{J}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{i}f_{i},\ \ \lambda_{J},\mu_{i}\geq 0.

Taking the
j_{0}
-th and
j_{1}
-th coordinates we obtain the following inequalities.

\displaystyle-1\geq-\lambda_{J_{0}}+\sum_{J\neq J_{0}}\lambda_{J},\ \ -1\geq-\lambda_{J_{1}}+\sum_{J\neq J_{1}}\lambda_{J}.

Summing gives

-2\geq\sum_{J\neq J_{0},J_{1}}\lambda_{J}
.
Therefore
\lambda_{J}=0
, for all
J\neq J_{0},J_{1}
. Considering the
j_{0}
-th and
j_{1}
-th coordinates again we have

\displaystyle-1

\displaystyle=-\lambda_{J_{0}}+\lambda_{J_{1}}+\mu_{j_{0}},

\displaystyle-1

\displaystyle=\lambda_{J_{0}}-\lambda_{J_{1}}+\mu_{j_{1}}.

Summing implies that
\mu_{j_{0}}=\mu_{j_{1}}=0
, which implies a contradiction. Hence there exists some
J\in P
with
I\subseteq J
, and
Q
is a refinement of
P
. This proves statement (2).

We now prove statement (3). Since
\omega\in\Omega_{Y}^{\mathrm{free}}
is a top dimensional cone we have that
\omega_{P}^{\circ}\cap\omega_{Q}^{\circ}\neq\emptyset
if and only if
\omega_{P}\cap\omega_{Q}
is top dimensional. Hence we have
\omega_{P}^{\circ}\cap\omega_{Q}^{\circ}=\emptyset
if and only if
\omega_{P}\cap\omega_{Q}
is contained in a proper linear subspace; if and only if
(\omega_{P}\cap\omega_{Q})^{\vee}=\omega_{P}^{\vee}+\omega_{Q}^{\vee}
contains a non-zero linear subspace.

Note that by statement (2) we have that
\omega_{P}\subseteq\eta_{I}
and
\omega_{Q}\subseteq\eta_{I^{c}}
if and only if
P_{I}
is a refinement of
P
and
P_{I^{c}}
is a refinement of
Q
. Equivalently
I\subseteq I^{\prime}
, for some
I^{\prime}\in P
and
I^{c}\subseteq J^{\prime}
, for some
J^{\prime}\in Q
. In this case we have
(I^{\prime})^{c}\cap(J^{\prime})^{c}=\emptyset
. We show that if
(I^{\prime})^{c}\cap(J^{\prime})^{c}\neq\emptyset
, for all
I^{\prime}\in P
and
J^{\prime}\in Q
, then
\omega_{P}^{\vee}+\omega_{Q}^{\vee}
does not contain a non-zero linear subspace.

The Minkowski sum
\omega_{P}^{\vee}+\omega_{Q}^{\vee}
contains a non-zero linear subspace if and only if there exists some non-trivial linear combination with non-negative coefficients:

\sum_{I\in P}\lambda_{I}v_{I}+\sum_{J\in Q}\lambda_{J}v_{J}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{i}f_{i}=0.

Suppose there exists such a linear combination.
Given any
i\in[n]
, let
I_{i}
and
J_{i}
denote the unique sets in
P
and
Q
respectively, containing
i
. Fix
i\in[n]
, taking the
i
-th coordinate of the above linear combination we obtain the inequality:

\lambda_{I_{i}}+\lambda_{J_{i}}\geq\sum_{I\neq I_{i}}\lambda_{I}+\sum_{J\neq J_{i}}\lambda_{J}.

By assumption there exists some
j\in I_{i}^{c}\cap J_{i}^{c}
. Taking the
j
-th coordinate of the linear combination we obtain:

\displaystyle\lambda_{I_{j}}+\lambda_{J_{j}}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{I\neq I_{j}}\lambda_{I}+\sum_{J\neq J_{j}}\lambda_{J},

\displaystyle=\lambda_{I_{i}}+\lambda_{J_{i}}+\sum_{I\neq I_{i},I_{j}}\lambda_{I}+\sum_{J\neq J_{i},J_{j}}\lambda_{J},

\displaystyle\geq\lambda_{I_{j}}+\lambda_{J_{j}}+2\left(\sum_{I\neq I_{i},I_{j}}\lambda_{I}+\sum_{J\neq J_{i},J_{j}}\lambda_{J}\right).

Where we have applied the inequality obtained from taking the
i
-th coordinate.
Thus we have that:

\sum_{I\neq I_{i},I_{j}}\lambda_{I}+\sum_{J\neq J_{i},J_{j}}\lambda_{J}=0.

Hence
\lambda_{I}=0
, for all
I\neq I_{i},I_{j}
, and
\lambda_{J}=0
, for all
J\neq J_{i},J_{j}
.

Finally, let
k\in(I_{i}\cup I_{j})^{c}
, which is non-empty since
\omega_{P}\in\Omega_{Y}^{\mathrm{free}}
. We are reduced to two cases. Let
k\in(J_{i}\cup J_{j})^{c}
, then

0\geq\lambda_{I_{i}}+\lambda_{J_{i}}+\lambda_{I_{j}}+\lambda_{J_{j}},

hence all the coefficients in the linear combination vanish. In the other case, we may assume without loss of generality that
k\in J_{i}
, which gives the inequality:

\displaystyle\lambda_{J_{i}}

\displaystyle\geq\lambda_{I_{i}}+\lambda_{I_{j}}+\lambda_{J_{j}},

\displaystyle\geq 2\lambda_{I_{i}}+\lambda_{J_{i}}.

Thus
\lambda_{J_{i}}=0
. Let
k\in I_{j}^{c}\cap J_{i}^{c}
. Again there are two cases. If
k\in J_{j}^{c}
then all coefficients vanish. If
k\in J_{j}
then taking the
k
-th coordinate gives:

\lambda_{J_{j}}\geq\lambda_{I_{j}}+\lambda_{J_{i}}.

Which implies
\lambda_{I_{j}}=0
. Hence
\lambda_{I}=0
, for all
I\in P
. Thus the linear combination lies in
\omega_{Q}^{\vee}
, which contains no linear subspace, so all coefficients must vanish. This completes the proof of statement (3).
∎

4.2.
Maximal bunches and complexes.

Recall the definition of a maximal bunch of orbit cones (see Definition
2.5
). Here we introduce maximally-biconnected complexes and show they parameterise the maximal bunches of orbit cones consisting of orbit cones in
Y^{\mathrm{free}}
. We show that the number of maximally-biconnected complexes on
[n]
equals the
n
-th Hoşten-Morris number.

Definition 4.6
.

A
complex
(on
[n]
) is a set
\Delta
of non-empty subsets
I\subseteq[n]
, such that if
J\subseteq I
and
I\in\Delta
, then
J\in\Delta
.
A complex
\Delta
is
biconnected
if for all
I,J\in\Delta
then
I\cup J\neq[n]
. A complex
\Delta
is
maximally-biconnected
if it is biconnected and not contained in any larger biconnected complex. A complex
\Delta
is
full
if
\{i\}\in\Delta
, for all
i\in[n]
.

Let
\Delta
be a complex. We associate a collection of cones

\Phi_{\Delta}\vcentcolon=\{\omega_{P}\in\Omega_{Y}^{\mathrm{free}}\ |\ P\subseteq\Delta\}\subseteq\Omega_{Y}^{\mathrm{free}}.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
\Delta
be a full maximally-biconnected complex. Then
\Phi_{\Delta}
is a maximal bunch of orbit cones. The induced map

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\text{Full maximally-biconnected}\\
\text{complexes}\end{array}\right\}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\text{Maximal bunches of orbit cones }\\
\Phi\subseteq\Omega_{Y}^{\mathrm{free}}\end{array}\right\},

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle\longmapsto\Phi_{\Delta},

is a bijection.

Proof.

We begin by showing that
\Phi_{\Delta}
is a maximal bunch of orbit cones. Let
P\subseteq\Delta
be a partition of
[n]
, by definition
\omega_{P}\in\Phi_{\Delta}
. Suppose
\omega\in\Omega_{Y}
such that
\omega_{P}^{\circ}\subseteq\omega^{\circ}
. This implies that
\omega
is top-dimensional, hence
\omega\in\Omega^{\mathrm{free}}_{Y}
and
\omega=\omega_{Q}
for some partition
Q
of
[n]
. Since
\omega_{P}
and
\omega_{Q}
are both top dimensional we have that
\omega_{P}\subseteq\omega_{Q}
, thus by Lemma
4.5
,
Q
is a refinement of
P
. Since
\Delta
is closed under taking subsets we have that
Q\subseteq\Delta
, therefore
\omega_{Q}\in\Phi_{\Delta}
. Hence
\Phi_{\Delta}
satisfies Definition
2.5
(2) in the definition of a bunch of orbit cones.

To show that
\Phi_{\Delta}
satisfies Definition
2.5
(1), let
\omega_{P},\omega_{Q}\in\Omega_{Y}^{\mathrm{free}}
such that
\omega_{P}^{\circ}\cap\omega_{Q}^{\circ}=\emptyset
. By Lemma
4.5
, there exists some
I\subseteq[n]
such that
\omega_{P}\subseteq\eta_{I}
and
\omega_{Q}\subseteq\eta_{I^{c}}
. If
\omega_{P},\omega_{Q}\in\Phi_{\Delta}
, then by definition we have that
I,I^{c}\in\Delta
, which is absurd since
\Delta
is biconnected. Therefore for all
\omega_{P},\omega_{Q}\in\Delta
we have that
\omega_{P}^{\circ}\cap\omega_{Q}^{\circ}\neq\emptyset
, thus
\Phi_{\Delta}
is a bunch of orbit cones.

We claim
\Phi_{\Delta}
is a
maximal
bunch of orbit cones. Suppose that
\Phi
is a bunch of orbit cones containing
\Phi_{\Delta}
. Let
\omega_{P}\in\Phi
. We first treat the case that
\omega_{P}
is not top dimensional; the following two subcases can occur:

(1)

P
is a partition of
I
, with
I\neq[n]
.

(2)

P
is a partition of
[n]
, with
\#P\leq 2
.

In the first case
\omega_{P}^{\circ}\cap\omega_{Q}^{\circ}=\emptyset
, for all
\omega_{Q}\in\Omega_{Y}^{\mathrm{free}}
, since
\omega_{P}
is contained in the coordinate hyperplanes given by the vanishing of the
i
-th coordainte, for all
i\in I^{c}
. In the second case we may assume
\#P=2
, otherwise
\omega_{P}=\{0\}
. In this case
\omega_{P}=\eta_{I}\cap\eta_{I^{c}}
, for some
I\subseteq[n]
. It is a property of maximally-biconnected complexes that they contain precisely one of
I
or
I^{c}
, for all
I\subseteq[n]
. Without loss of generality suppose
I\in\Delta
, then
\eta\in\Phi_{\Delta}
, and
\eta_{I}^{\circ}\cap\omega_{P}^{\circ}=\emptyset
, giving a contradiction. Therefore
\omega_{P}
must be top dimensional, i.e.
\omega_{P}\in\Omega_{Y}^{\mathrm{free}}
.

Let
\Delta_{P}
be the complex consisting of the partition
P
and its subsets. Since
\Phi
is a bunch of orbit cones contains
\Delta_{P}
by Definition
2.5
(2). The union
\Delta\cup\Delta_{P}
remains a full complex. Suppose
\{I,I^{c}\}\subseteq\Delta\cup\Delta_{P}
, then
\eta_{I},\eta_{I^{c}}\in\Phi
, which cannot occur since
\eta_{I}^{\circ}\cap\eta_{I^{c}}^{\circ}=\emptyset
. Thus
\Delta\cup\Delta_{P}
is a full biconnected complex. It follows, by maximality of
\Delta
, that
\Delta\cup\Delta_{P}=\Delta
, and hence
P\subseteq\Delta
and
\omega_{P}\in\Phi_{\Delta}
. Thus
\Phi_{\Delta}
is a maximal bunch of orbit cones.

Finally we show the map is a bijection. Distinct maximally-biconnected complexes contain distinct partitions, hence their images contain distinct orbit cones, thus the map is injective. To show surjectivity, let
\Phi\subseteq\Omega_{Y}^{\mathrm{free}}
be a maximal bunch of orbit cones. Let

\Delta_{\Phi}=\bigcup_{\omega_{P}\in\Phi}\Delta_{P}.

Since
\Phi\neq\emptyset
we must have
\omega_{P}\in\Phi
for some partition
P
. Since
\Delta_{P}
is full we have that
\Delta_{\Phi}
is full. Again
\Delta_{\Phi}
is biconnected since we cannot have that
\{\eta_{I},\eta_{I^{c}}\}\subseteq\Phi
.
It is maximally-biconnected by the maximality of
\Phi
: the image of
\Delta_{\Phi}
clearly contains
\Phi
, and by the maximality of
\Phi
it is equal to it. Thus the map in the statement is a bijection.
∎

Lemma 4.8
.

There are exactly

is covered by
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}
, for
\theta\in F
generic, there exists some
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}
containing
q^{\prime}
and
\zeta_{\omega}
. Since
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}
is a union of
\mathcal{Z}_{\chi}
we have that
q\in\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}
, and since
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}
is proper over
\mathfrak{M}_{0}
, we have that the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Spec}(K)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Spec}(R)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathfrak{M}_{0}}}

admits a unique completion, hence
q^{\prime}=q
. Therefore since
\mathfrak{M}
is proper over
\mathfrak{M}_{0}
, it follows that
q\in\mathfrak{M}
. Thus
\pi^{-1}(0)
, and hence
\mathfrak{M}
, is a union of strata
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
.

Step 2
. we claim that
\Psi
satisfies Definition
2.5
(2). By Lemma
5.16
, we have that
\overline{\mathcal{Z}}_{\omega}=\bigcup_{\chi\subseteq\omega}\mathcal{Z}_{\chi}
, hence
\bigcup_{\omega\subsetneq\omega_{0}}\mathcal{Z}_{\chi}
is open. Since
\mathfrak{M}\subseteq\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{tot}}
is an open subscheme, if
\omega\in\Psi
and
\omega\subseteq\omega_{0}
then
\omega_{0}\in\Psi
.
If
\omega\in\Psi
and
\omega^{\circ}\subseteq\omega_{0}^{\circ}
, then
\omega\subseteq\omega_{0}
and thus
\omega_{0}\in\Psi
. Therefore
\Psi
satisfies Definition
2.5
(2).

Step 3
. Given
\omega\in\Omega_{X}^{\mathrm{free}}
, let

\rho(\omega)\vcentcolon=\omega\cap F
. Given
\Psi\subseteq\Omega_{X}^{\mathrm{free}}
, let

\rho(\Psi)\vcentcolon=\{\rho(\omega)\ |\ \omega\in\Psi\}.

We claim that
C_{i}\in\rho(\Psi)
for precisely one
i=0,\ldots,n
.

By Lemma
5.3
,
\rho(\omega)
is a top dimensional cone in
\Theta
. Suppose that
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}\subseteq\mathfrak{U}
, then for all generic
\theta\in F
, we have
\theta\in\omega
. Therefore
F\subseteq\omega
, and
\rho(\omega)=F
. In general
\rho(\omega)
is not a cone in
\Omega_{X}
. Given
\omega\in\Omega_{X}^{\mathrm{free}}
, we claim that
\rho(\omega)=C_{i}
if and only if
\omega=\omega_{Q_{i},K}
, with
K\subseteq\{i\}^{c}
. This follows from the fact that
\omega_{P,K}\cap F
is generated by
e_{j}
, where
j\in I\in P
, such that
K\cap I^{c}\neq\emptyset
, together with
e_{j_{1}}+e_{j_{2}}
, where
\{j_{1},j_{2}\}\not\subseteq I
, for all
I\in P
.

Let
R
be a discrete valuation ring with fraction field
K
, together with a morphism
\alpha:\mathrm{Spec}(R)\rightarrow\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{tot}}
sending the open point to the generic point of
\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{tot}}
, and the closed point to the generic point of
\mathcal{Z}_{C_{0}}
. We have an induced morphism
\mathrm{Spec}(K)\rightarrow\mathfrak{M}
. By properness this admits a unique extension
\mathrm{Spec}(R)\rightarrow\mathfrak{M}
over
\mathfrak{M}_{0}
. If this extension has the same image as
\alpha
, then
C_{0}\in\Psi
, hence
C_{0}\in\rho(\Psi)
. If not, then the image lies in
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}
, for some generic
\theta\in F
, such that
\theta\notin C_{0}
. Since
F=\bigcup_{i=0}^{n}C_{i}
, we have that
\theta\in C_{i}^{\circ}
, for some
i=1,\ldots,n
. Therefore the image of the closed point lies in
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
, such that
\omega\cap F=C_{i}
, and
C_{i}\in\rho(\Psi)
.

Step 4
. Suppose
C_{i}\in\rho(\Psi)
for
i\neq 0
, we claim that
\Psi=\Psi_{\Delta}
, where
\Delta
is the unique maximally-biconnected complex such that
\{i\}^{c}\in\Delta
.

Let
\omega\in\Psi
such that
\rho(\omega)=C_{i}
. We have that
\omega=\omega_{Q_{i},K}
, where
K\subseteq\{i\}^{c}
. Since
\omega_{Q_{i},K}\subseteq\omega_{Q_{i},\{i\}^{c}}
, we have that
\omega_{Q_{i},\{i\}^{c}}\in\Psi
. Let
\omega_{0}=\omega_{Q_{i},\{i\}^{c}}
. With respect to the open covering

\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{tot}}=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta\in F\\
\mathrm{generic}\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}.

the stratum
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega_{0}}
is contained in a unique open subscheme given by
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}
, where
\theta\in C_{i}^{\circ}
(since
\omega_{0}
contains a unique GIT chamber inside
F
). In particular

\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{tot}}\setminus\bigcup_{\omega_{0}\subsetneq\chi}\mathcal{Z}_{\chi}\subseteq\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}.

Therefore
\overline{\mathcal{Z}}_{\omega_{0}}\subseteq\mathfrak{M}
, which implies
\omega_{Q,J}\in\Psi
, for all
J\subseteq\{i\}^{c}
.

By Lemma
5.5
, together with Definition
2.5
(2) we have shown that any cone
\omega
containing the GIT chamber
C_{i}
is contained in
\Psi
. Hence
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}\subseteq\mathfrak{M}
, and by properness of
\mathfrak{M}
we obtain equality. Thus
\Psi=\Psi_{\Delta}
, where
\Delta
is the unique maximally-biconnected complex containing
\{i\}^{c}
.

Step 5
. Suppose
C_{0}\in\rho(\Psi)
, we claim that
\Psi=\Psi_{\Delta}
, where
\Delta
is a
full
maximally-biconnected complex.

We have seen that if
C_{0}\in\rho(\Psi)
, then
C_{0}\in\Psi
. In this case
\overline{\mathcal{Z}}_{C_{0}}\subseteq\mathfrak{M}
is a complete geometric GIT quotient of some open subset of
Y
(see Theorem
4.13
). We see this by noting that

\mathcal{V}_{C_{0}}=\{x\in X^{\mathrm{free}}\ |\ c_{k}(x)=0,\ \text{for all }k,\ \varphi_{i,j}(x)\neq 0,\ \text{for all }i,j\}.

Which is a dense open subset of the closed subscheme
Y\subseteq X
defined as the vanishing locus
\mathbb{V}(c_{i}\ |\ i\in[n])
.
Since
\overline{\mathcal{Z}}_{C_{0}}\rightarrow\pi^{-1}(0)
is a closed immersion of an irreducible variety into complete scheme, we obtain that
\overline{\mathcal{Z}}_{C_{0}}
is a complete. Hence by Proposition
4.7
there is a full maximally-biconnected complex
\Delta
, such that
\overline{\mathcal{Z}}_{C_{0}}=U(\Phi_{\Delta})\operatorname{\!/\!\!/\!}T
. Therefore
\Phi_{\Delta}\subseteq\Psi
, since
\Psi
satisfies Definition
2.5
(2) and we have that
\Psi_{\Delta}\subseteq\Psi
.

Let
\omega_{P,K}\in\Psi
. Then
\omega_{P,K}\cap C_{0}\neq\emptyset
, else
\omega_{P,K}\cap C_{i}\neq\emptyset
for some
i=1,\ldots,n
, which implies
C_{i}\in\rho(\Psi)
, contradicting our assumption. Thus
\#P\geq 3
, and
\omega_{P,K}\cap C_{0}=\omega_{P}
.

Since
\omega_{P}\subseteq\omega_{P,K}
, for all generic
\theta\in F
, we have that
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega_{P}}\subset\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}
implies that
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega_{P,K}}\subseteq\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}
. Thus let
\mathrm{Spec}(R)\rightarrow\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{tot}}
, sending its open point to the generic point of
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega_{P,K}}
and its closed point to the generic point of
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega_{P}}
, then the induced morphism
\mathrm{Spec}(K)\rightarrow\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{tot}}
admits a unique extension over
\mathfrak{M}_{0}
. Therefore by properness of
\mathfrak{M}
we have that
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega_{P}}\subseteq\mathfrak{M}
, hence
\omega_{P}\in\Psi
. Again since
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega_{P}}
is contained inside the polygon space contained in
\mathfrak{M}
, we have that
\omega_{P}\in\Phi_{\Delta}
, hence
\omega_{P,K}\in\Psi_{\Delta}
. Therefore
\Psi=\Psi_{\Delta}
.

Step 6
. Given a maximally-biconnected complex
\Delta
, let
\mathfrak{M}_{\Delta}\vcentcolon=U(\Psi_{\Delta})\operatorname{\!/\!\!/\!}T
. To finish the proof it suffices to show that
\mathfrak{M}_{\Delta}\subseteq\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{tot}}
together with its natural morphism
\pi_{\Delta}:~\mathfrak{M}_{\Delta}\rightarrow\mathfrak{M}_{0}
is a crepant resolution of
\mathfrak{M}_{0}
. The map in the statement of the Theorem is then well-defined, and by the previous steps it is surjective. We have seen that if
\{i\}^{c}\in\Delta
then
\mathfrak{M}_{\Delta}=\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}
, for
\theta\in C_{i}^{\circ}
. Thus we may assume
\Delta
is full.

First note that
U(\Psi_{\Delta})\operatorname{\!/\!\!/\!}T
is an open subscheme of
\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{tot}}
, and
\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{tot}}
is covered by
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}
, for
\theta\in F
generic. Hence each point of
\mathfrak{M}_{\Delta}
is smooth, and locally around each point the natural morphism
\pi_{\Delta}
is crepant. Since
\mathfrak{M}_{\Delta}
is separated (since it has the
A_{2}
-property), to show
\pi_{\Delta}
is a crepant resolution it suffices to show that it satisfies the existence part of the valuative criterion for properness over
\mathfrak{M}_{0}
.

By the definition of
\Psi_{\Delta}
, if
\omega\in\Omega_{X}^{\mathrm{free}}
and
\omega\supseteq F
then
\omega\in\Psi_{\Delta}
, since
\omega
necessarily contains some
\omega_{0}\in\Phi_{\Delta}
. It follows that

\bigcup_{\omega\supseteq F}\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}\subseteq\mathfrak{M}_{\Delta}.

The open subscheme
\bigcup_{\omega\supseteq F}\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}\subseteq\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{tot}}
is precisely the preimage of
\mathfrak{M}_{0}\setminus\{0\}
under
\pi_{\mathrm{tot}}
, and by Corollary
5.15
we have that it is proper over
\mathfrak{M}_{0}\setminus\{0\}
. Therefore to prove properness of
\mathfrak{M}_{\Delta}
it suffices to verify the properness of
\pi_{\Delta}^{-1}(0)
.

Let
R
be a discrete valuation ring fraction field
K
. Suppose we are given a morphism
\alpha:\mathrm{Spec}(K)\rightarrow\pi_{\Delta}^{-1}(0)
, with the image lying in
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
. Since
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}\subseteq\pi_{\Delta}^{-1}(0)
, by Lemma
5.13
and Corollary
5.15
, we have that
\omega=\omega_{P,K}
, where
K\subseteq I
, for some

.

For all generic
\theta\in\omega\cap F
, we have that
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}\subseteq\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}
, and since
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}
is projective, there is a unique extension of
\alpha
to a morphism
\mathrm{Spec}(R)\rightarrow\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}
. Thus for all GIT chambers

such that
\overline{C}\subseteq\omega
, there is a unique
\chi_{C}\in\Omega_{X}^{\mathrm{free}}
, with

, such that, given

, there is a unique extension
\beta_{C}:\mathrm{Spec}(R)\rightarrow\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}
, with the image of the closed point lying in
\mathcal{Z}_{\chi_{C}}
. The union of these cones contains
\omega
and their interiors have pairwise empty intersections.

Label by
\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{N}
the subset of cones
\chi_{C}
such that
\chi_{C}^{\circ}\cap C_{0}^{\circ}\neq\emptyset
. We claim that some
\chi_{i}\in\Psi_{\Delta}
, in which case the image of some extension of
\alpha

\mathfrak{M}_{\Delta}

\mathfrak{M}_{\Delta}
is proper.

If
\omega=\omega_{P,K}
and

\omega_{P,K}\cap C_{0}=\eta_{I}\in\Omega_{Y}^{\mathrm{free}}

\Psi_{\Delta}

\omega_{P,K}\supseteq\omega_{0}

\omega_{0}\subseteq C_{0}

(2).

2.5
(2), applied to

The Logical Essence of Compiling With Continuations
José Espírito Santo, Filipa Mendes

Abstract
The essence of compiling with continuations is that conversion to continuation-passing style (CPS) is equivalent to a source language transformation converting to administrative normal form (ANF). Taking as source language Moggi’s computational lambda-calculus ( λ ​ 𝖢 \lambda{\mathsf{C}} ), we define an alternative to the CPS-translation with target in the sequent calculus LJQ, named value-filling style (VFS) translation, and making use of the ability of the sequent calculus to represent contexts formally. The VFS-translation requires no type translation: indeed, double negations are introduced only when encoding the VFS target language in the CPS target language. This optional encoding, when composed with the VFS-translation reconstructs the original CPS-translation. Going back to direct style, the “essence” of the VFS-translation is that it reveals a new sublanguage of ANF, the value-enclosed style (VES), next to another one, the continuation-enclosing style (CES): such an alternative is due to a dilemma in the syntax of λ ​ 𝖢 \lambda{\mathsf{C}} , concerning how to expand the application constructor. In the typed scenario, VES and CES correspond to an alternative between two proof systems for call-by-value, LJQ and natural deduction with generalized applications, confirming proof theory as a foundation for intermediate representations.

Introduction

The conversion of a program in a source call-by-value language to continuation-passing style (CPS) by an optimizing translation that reduces on the fly the so-called administrative redexes produces programs which can be translated back to direct style, so that the final result, obtained by composing the two stages of translation, is a new program in the source language which can be obtained from the original one by reduction to administrative normal form (ANF) – a program transformation in the source language
[
9
,
23
]
. This fact has been dubbed the “essence” of compiling with continuations and has had a big impact and generated an on-going debate in the theory and practice of compiler design
[
10
,
15
,
17
]
.

Our starting point is the refinement of that “essence”, obtained in
[
24
]
, in the form of a reflection of the CPS target in the computational
\lambda
-calculus
[
19
]
, the latter playing the role of source language and here denoted
\lambda{\mathsf{C}}
– see Fig.
1
. Then we ask: What is the proof-theoretical meaning of this reflection? What is the logical reading of this reflection in the typed setting? Of course, the CPS-translation has a well-known logical reading as a negative translation, based on the introduction of double negations, capable of translating a classical source calculus with control operators
[
18
,
11
,
25
]
. But it is not clear how this reading is articulated with the reflection in Fig.
1
, which provides a
decomposition
of the CPS-translation as the reduction to ANF followed by a “kernel” translation that relates the “kernel” ANF with CPS.

It is also well-known that the CPS-translation can be decomposed in several ways: indeed in the reference
[
24
]
alone we may find two of them, one through the monadic meta-language
[

]
, the other through the linear

[

. Here we will propose another intermediate language, the sequent calculus
LJQ

[

. The calculus

[

Languages of proof terms for the sequent calculus handle contexts (

\lambda
-terms with a hole) formally
[

7

5

. This seems most convenient, since a continuation may be seen as a certain kind of context, and suggests that we can write an alternative translation into the sequent calculus, as if we were CPS-translating, but without the need to pass around a reification of the current continuation as a
\lambda
-abstraction, nor the concomitant need to translate types by the insertion of double negations, to make room for a type
A^{\sim}
of values, a type
\neg A^{\sim}
of continuations and a type
\neg\neg A^{\sim}
of programs, out of a source type
A
.

We develop this in detail, which requires: to rework entirely the term calculus for
LJQ
and obtain a system, named
\lambda Q
, more manageable for our purposes; and to identify the kernel and the sub-kernel of
\lambda Q
, the latter being the target system, named
VFS
after
value-filling style
, of the new translation. In the end, we are rewarded with an isomorphism between
VFS
and the target of the CPS-translation,
which, when composed with the alternative translation, reconstructs the CPS-translation. The isomorphism is a negative translation, reduced to the role of optional and late stage of translation.

Going back to direct style, the “essence” of the VFS-translation is that it reveals a new sublanguage of ANF, the
value-enclosed style
(VES), next to another sublanguage of ANF, the
continuation-enclosing style
(CES): such alternative between VES and CES is due to a dilemma in the syntax of
\lambda{\mathsf{C}}
, concerning how to
expand
the application constructor. Hence, these two sub-kernels of
\lambda{\mathsf{C}}
are under a layer of expansion – and the same was already true for the passage from the kernel to the sub-kernel of
\lambda Q
.

While VES corresponds to the sub-kernel VFS of
\lambda Q
, CES corresponds to a fragment of
\lambda\mathsf{J}_{v}

[
6
]
, a call-by-value
\lambda
-calculus with generalized applications; the fragment is that of
commutative normal forms
(CNF), that is, normal forms w. r. t. the commutative conversions, naturally arising when application is generalized, which reduce both the head term and the argument in an application to the form of values. So the alternative between VES and CES is also a reflection, in the source language, of the alternative between two proof systems for call-by-value: the sequent calculus
LJQ
and the natural deduction system behind
\lambda\mathsf{J}_{v}
.

Preliminaries.
Simple types (=formulas) are given by
A,B,C::=a|A\supset B
. In typing systems, a context
\Gamma

consistent
set of declarations
x:A
; consistency here means that no variable can be declared with two different types in

We recall the concepts of equational correspondence, pre-Galois connection and reflection
[

,
23
,

characterizing different forms of relationship between two calculi.

Definition 1
.

Let
(\Lambda_{1},\to_{1})
and
(\Lambda_{2},\to_{2})
be two calculi and, for each

\twoheadrightarrow_{i}
(resp.

) be the reflexive-transitive (resp. reflexive-transitive-symmetric) closure of
\to_{i}
. Consider the mappings
f:\Lambda_{1}\to\Lambda_{2}
and
g:\Lambda_{2}\to\Lambda_{1}
.

•

f
and
g
form an

between

and
\Lambda_{2}
if the following conditions hold: (1) If

then
f(M)\leftrightarrow_{2}f(N)
; (2) If
M\to_{2}N
then
g(M)\leftrightarrow_{1}g(N)
; (3)
M\leftrightarrow_{1}g(f(M))
; (4)
f(g(M))\leftrightarrow_{2}M
.

•

f
and
g
form a
pre-Galois connection
from
\Lambda_{1}
to
\Lambda_{2}
if the following conditions hold: (1) If
M\to_{1}N
then
f(M)\twoheadrightarrow_{2}f(N)
; (2) If
M\to_{2}N
then
g(M)\twoheadrightarrow_{1}g(N)
; (3)
M\twoheadrightarrow_{1}g(f(M))
.

•

f
and
g
form a
reflection
in
\Lambda_{1}
of
\Lambda_{2}
if the following conditions hold: (1) If
M\to_{1}N
then
f(M)\twoheadrightarrow_{2}f(N)
; (2) If
M\to_{2}N
then
g(M)\twoheadrightarrow_{1}g(N)
; (3)
M\twoheadrightarrow_{1}g(f(M))
; (4)
f(g(M))=M
.

Note that if
f
and
g
form a pre-Galois connection from
\Lambda_{1}
to
\Lambda_{2}
and
\to_{2}
is confluent, then
\to_{1}
is also confluent. Besides, it is also important to observe that if
f
and
g
form a reflection from
\Lambda_{1}
to
\Lambda_{2}
, then
g
and
f
form a pre-Galois connection from
\Lambda_{2}
to
\Lambda_{1}
.

The computational
\lambda

For the reduction of

4

[

24

In this paper, we leave the

CPS-translation.
We present in this subsection the call-by-value CPS-translation of
\lambda{\mathsf{C}}
. It is a “refined ” translation
[

, in the sense that it reduces “administrative redexes” at translation time, as already done in

The target of the translation is the system

In the first sub-kernel, named
{\scriptstyle{CES}}
, terms
M
are given by the grammar

M\,::=\,V\,|\,\mathsf{let}\,x:=VW\,\mathsf{in}\,M\kern 5.0pt.

We call this representation
continuation enclosing
style, since the “serious” (=non-value) terms have the form of an application
VW
enclosed in a let-expression. The unique reduction rule of
{\scriptstyle{CES}}
is

(\beta_{v})\qquad\mathsf{let}\,y:=(\lambda x.M)V\,\mathsf{in}\,P\to\mathsf{LET}\,y:=[V/x]M\,\mathsf{in}\,P

In
\underline{ANF}

B_{v}
-step followed by
let_{v}
-step. The operation
\mathsf{LET}\,y:=M\,\mathsf{in}\,P

\underline{ANF}

let_{v}
-step:
\mathsf{LET}\,y:=V\,\mathsf{in}\,P=[V/y]P

In the second sub-kernel, named
VES

\begin{array}[]{rcl}M,N&::=&V\,|\,\mathsf{let}\,x:=V\,\mathsf{in}\,c_{x}\\

\begin{array}[]{rrcl}(B_{v})&\mathsf{let}\,y:=(\lambda x.M)\,\mathsf{in}\,\mathsf{let}\,z:=yV\,\mathsf{in}\,P&\to&\mathsf{let}\,x:=V\,\mathsf{in}\,\mathsf{LET}\,z:=M\,\mathsf{in}\,P\\

In

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathsf{LET}\,y:=V\,\mathsf{in}\,P&=&\mathsf{let}\,y:=V\,\mathsf{in}\,P\\

6
Conclusions

Contributions.
We list our main contribution: the VFS-translation; the negative translation as an isomorphism between the VFS and CPS targets; the decomposition of the CPS-translation in terms of the VFS-translation and the negative translation; the two sub-kernels of
\lambda{\mathsf{C}}
and their perfect relationship with appropriate fragments of the sequent calculus
LJQ
and natural deduction with general eliminations; the reworking of the term calculus for
LJQ
.

In all, we took the polished account of the essence of CPS, obtained in
[
24
]
and illustrated in Fig.
1
, and revealed a rich proof-theoretical background, as in Fig.
2
, with a double layer of sub-kernels, under a layer of expansions (see the dotted lines in Fig.
2
and recall (
1
), (
2
), and (
3
)), intersecting an intermediate zone, between the source language and the CPS targets, of calculi corresponding to proof systems.

Related work.
In
[
4
]
,
LJQ
is studied as a source language, while the CPS translation of
LJQ
is a tool to establish indirectly a connection with
\lambda{\mathsf{C}}
, through their respective kernels, in order to confirm that cut-elimination in
LJQ
is connected with call-by-value computation. There is nothing wrong with using the sequent calculus as source language and translating it with CPS: this has been done abundantly, even by the first author
[
1
,
26
,
4
,
8
]
. But the point made here is that the sequent calculus should also be used as a tool to analyze the CPS-translation, and is able to play a special role as an intermediate language.

The sequent calculus was put forward as an intermediate representation for compilation of functional programs in
[
2
]
. This study addresses compilation of programs for a real-world language; designs an intermediate language
Sequent Core
(SC) inspired in the sequent calculus for such source language; and compares SC with CPS heuristically w. r. t. several desirable properties in the context of optimized compilation. In the present paper, we address the foundations of compilation, employing theoretical languages; pick the sequent calculus
LJQ
, which is a standard systems with decades of history in proof-theory
[
3
]
; and compare
LJQ
and CPS, not through a benchmarking of competing languages, but through mathematical results showing their intimate connection.

Future work.
We know an appropriate CPS target will be capable of interpreting a classical extension of our chosen source language. The problem in moving in this direction is that there is no standard extension of
\lambda{\mathsf{C}}
with control operators readily available. Source languages with let-expressions and control operators can be found in
[
13
,
5
]
, but adopting them means to redo all that we have done here – that is another project. On the other hand, maybe a system with generalized applications will make a good source language. The system
\lambda\mathsf{J}_{v}
performed well in this paper, since its sub-kernel of administrative normal forms (
{\scriptstyle{CNF}}
) is reachable without consideration of expansions – a sign of a well calibrated syntax.

Our presentation of the kernel of
\lambda{\mathsf{C}}
given in Table

is very close to the original one in

[
24

\begin{array}[]{rcl}M,N,P&::=&\mathbb{K}[V]|\mathbb{K}[VW]\\
V,W&::=&x|\lambda x.M\\
\mathbb{K}&::=&[\_]|\mathsf{let}\,x:=[\_]\,\mathsf{in}\,P\end{array}

We take for granted the sets of terms and values of
\lambda{\mathsf{C}}
, together with the set of contexts of
\lambda{\mathsf{C}}
, which are
\lambda{\mathsf{C}}
-terms with a single hole, and the concept of hole filling in such contexts. This grammar defines simultaneously a subset of the terms of
\lambda{\mathsf{C}}
, a subset of the values of
\lambda{\mathsf{C}}
, and a subset of the contexts of
\lambda{\mathsf{C}}
.

The second production in the grammar of terms,
\mathbb{K}[VW]
, should be understood thus: given in the kernel values
V
,
W
and a context
\mathbb{K}
, the
\lambda{\mathsf{C}}
-term
\mathbb{K}[VW]
, obtained by filling the hole of
\mathbb{K}
with the
\lambda{\mathsf{C}}
-term
VW
, is in the kernel. In
\lambda{\mathsf{C}}
,
VW
is a subterm of
\mathbb{K}[VW]

2
, in the kernel, the term
VW
is not an immediate subterm of
\mathbb{K}[VW]
– the immediate subexpressions are just
V
,
W
, and
\mathbb{K}
. Notice the
\lambda{\mathsf{C}}
-term

is a term in the kernel, generated by the second production of the grammar with
\mathbb{K}=[\_]
. But that second production
should not
be interpreted as
\mathbb{K}[M]
with
M=VW
.

There is no primitive
\mathbb{K}[M]
in the kernel. Instead, there is the operation
(M:\mathbb{K})
, defined by recursion on
M
as follows:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}(V:\mathbb{K})&=&\mathbb{K}[V]\\
(VW:\mathbb{K})&=&\mathbb{K}[VW]\\
(\mathsf{let}\,x:=V\,\mathsf{in}\,M:\mathbb{K})&=&\mathsf{let}\,x:=V\,\mathsf{in}\,(M:\mathbb{K})\\
(\mathsf{let}\,x:=VW\,\mathsf{in}\,M:\mathbb{K})&=&\mathsf{let}\,x:=VW\,\mathsf{in}\,(M:\mathbb{K})\end{array}

It is easy to see that
(M:\mathsf{let}\,x:=[\_]\,\mathsf{in}\,P)=\mathsf{LET}\,x:=M\,\mathsf{in}\,P
and that
(M:[\_])=M
.

In
[
24
]
, the kernel has the following reduction rule

(\beta.v)\qquad\mathbb{K}[(\lambda x.M)V]\to([V/x]M:\mathbb{K})\kern 5.0pt.

There is no need for the requirement of maximal
\mathbb{K}
in this rule, as done in
[
24
]

\mathbb{K}[VW]
is obtained. We now see the relationship between
\beta.v
and our
B_{v}
and
B_{v}^{\prime}
.

Let
\mathbb{K}=\mathsf{let}\,y:=[\_]\,\mathsf{in}\,P
. Then rule
B_{v}
can re written as

\mathbb{K}[(\lambda x.M)V]\to\mathsf{let}\,x:=V\,\mathsf{in}\,(M:\mathbb{K})\kern 5.0pt.

The contractum is a
let_{v}
-redex, which could be immediately reduced, to achieve the effect of
\beta.v
. Here we prefer to delay this

-step, and the same applies to our rule

is also seen in Section

Finally, rule
\eta_{let}
in

24
]
reads

If

Theorem 7

\lambda Q
of
\underline{LNF}

.

1.

in

\lambda Q

in
\underline{LNF}

(\sigma_{v})
.

\begin{array}[]{rcll}\mathsf{C}(\uparrow\!(\lambda x.M),y.\uparrow\!y)&\to_{\eta_{cut}}&\lambda x.M\\
&=&[\lambda x.M/y]y\end{array}

and

\begin{array}[]{rcll}\mathsf{C}(\uparrow\!V,y.N)&\to_{\sigma_{v}}&[V/y]N\end{array}

The proof of the item 2 is by induction on the relation
M\to N
in
\lambda Q
. We proceed by cases:

Case
(B_{v})
.

\begin{array}[]{rcll}\mathsf{C}(\uparrow\!(\lambda x.M),y.y(V,z.N))^{\triangledown}&=&\mathsf{C}_{v}(\lambda x.M^{\triangledown},y.y(V^{\triangledown\!\!\!\triangledown},z.N^{\triangledown}))\\

Item 3 follow by simultaneous induction on
V
and
M\in\lambda Q

\begin{array}[]{rcll}\uparrow\!W&\twoheadrightarrow&\uparrow\!W^{\triangledown\!\!\!\triangledown}&\text{(by IH)}\\

\begin{array}[]{rcll}x(W,y.N)&\twoheadrightarrow&x(W^{\triangledown\!\!\!\triangledown},y.N^{\triangledown})&\text{(by IH)}\\

Lemma 10
.

For all
V,W,M\in\lambda{\mathsf{C}}
,
P\in VFS
:

1.

([V/x]W)^{\circ}=[V^{\circ}/x]W^{\circ}
.

2.

[V^{\circ}/x](M;y.P)=([V/x]M;y.[V^{\circ}/x]P)
.

3.

([V/x]M)^{\bullet}=[V^{\circ}/x]M^{\bullet}

Proof.

Notice that, if item 2 holds for some
M
, then item 3 holds for the same
M
. Item 3 is obtained using item 2.

\begin{array}[]{rcll}([V/x]M)^{\bullet}&=&([V/x]M;z.\uparrow\!z)\\
&=&([V/x]M;z.[V^{\circ}/x](\uparrow\!z))\\
&=&[V^{\circ}/x](M;z.\uparrow\!z)&\text{(by IH)}\\
&=&[V^{\circ}/x]M^{\bullet}\end{array}

Hence, it suffices to prove items 1 and 2, and this is done by simultaneous induction on
W
and
M
.

Case
W=x
.

\begin{array}[]{rcll}([V/x]x)^{\circ}&=&V^{\circ}\\
&=&[V^{\circ}/x]x\\
&=&[V^{\circ}/x]x^{\circ}\end{array}

Case
W=y
.

\begin{array}[]{rcll}([V/x]y)^{\circ}&=&y^{\circ}\\
&=&y\\
&=&[V^{\circ}/x]y\\
&=&[V^{\circ}/x]y^{\circ}\end{array}

Case
W=\lambda y.P
.

\begin{array}[]{rcll}([V/x](\lambda y.P))^{\circ}&=&(\lambda y.[V/x]P)^{\circ}\\
&=&\lambda y.([V/x]P)^{\bullet}\\
&=&\lambda y.[V^{\circ}/x]P^{\bullet}&\text{(by IH)}\\
&=&[V^{\circ}/x](\lambda y.P)^{\circ}\end{array}

Case
M=W
.

\begin{array}[]{rcll}[V^{\circ}/x](W;y.P)&=&[V^{\circ}/x]\mathsf{C}_{v}(W^{\circ},y.P)\\
&=&\mathsf{C}_{v}([V^{\circ}/x]W^{\circ},y.[V^{\circ}/x]P)\\
&=&\mathsf{C}_{v}(([V/x]W)^{\circ},y.[V^{\circ}/x]P)&\text{(by IH)}\\
&=&([V/x]W;y.[V^{\circ}/x]P)\end{array}

Case
M=WW^{\prime}
.

\begin{array}[]{rcll}[V^{\circ}/x]((WW^{\prime});y.P)&=&[V^{\circ}/x]\mathsf{C}_{v}(W^{\circ},(W^{\prime\circ},y.N))\\
&=&\mathsf{C}_{v}([V^{\circ}/x]W^{\circ},([V^{\circ}/x]W^{\prime\circ},y.[V^{\circ}/x]N))\\
&=&\mathsf{C}_{v}(([V/x]W)^{\circ},(([V/x]W^{\prime})^{\circ},y.[V^{\circ}/x]N))&\text{(by IH)}\\
&=&([V/x]W[V/x]W^{\prime};y.[V^{\circ}/x]N)\\
&=&([V/x](WW^{\prime});y.[V^{\circ}/x]N)\end{array}

Case
M=WQ
, where
Q
is not a value.

\begin{array}[]{rcll}[V^{\circ}/x]((WQ);y.P)&=&[V^{\circ}/x](Q;n.\mathsf{C}_{v}(W^{\circ},(n^{\circ},y.P)))\\
&=&([V/x]Q;n.[V^{\circ}/x]\mathsf{C}_{v}(W^{\circ},(n^{\circ},x.P)))&\text{(by IH)}\\
&=&([V/x]Q;n.\mathsf{C}_{v}([V^{\circ}/x]W^{\circ},(n^{\circ},y.[V^{\circ}/x]P)))\\
&=&([V/x]Q;\mathsf{C}_{v}(([V/x]W)^{\circ},(n^{\circ},y.[V^{\circ}/x]P)))&\text{(by IH)}\\
&=&([V/x]W[V/x]Q;y.[V^{\circ}/x]P)\\
&=&([V/x](WQ);y.[V^{\circ}/x]P)\end{array}

Cases
M=NQ

is not a value, and
M=\mathsf{let}\,z:=N\,\mathsf{in}\,Q

M\to_{R}N

in
VFS

Item 1. Let
R\in\{B,let_{v},\eta_{let}\}

M\to_{R}N
. The base cases are as follows.

Case
(B)
.

\begin{array}[]{rcll}((\lambda x.M)V)^{\bullet}&=&\mathsf{C}_{v}(\lambda x.M^{\bullet},(V^{\circ},z.\uparrow\!z))\\
&\to_{B_{v}}&\mathsf{C}_{v}(V^{\circ},x.\mathsf{C}_{v}(M^{\bullet}:z.\uparrow\!z))\\

is not a value.

&\to_{\sigma_{v}}&(M;x.[x/z](\uparrow\!z))&\text{(by Lemma \ref{lem:vfs-red})}\\
&=&(M;x.\uparrow\!x)\\
&=&M^{\bullet}\end{array}

The inductive cases are easy, because
\twoheadrightarrow
is compatible.

R\in\{let_{1},let_{2},assoc\}

(let_{1})

Theorem
2
(Decomposition of the CPS-translation)
.

1.

For all
V\in\lambda{\mathsf{C}}
,
{V^{\circ}}^{\sim}=V^{\dagger}
.

2.

For all
M\in\lambda{\mathsf{C}}
,
N\in VFS
,
(M;x.N)^{\wr}=(M:\lambda x.N^{\wr})
.

3.

For all
M\in\lambda{\mathsf{C}}
,
{M^{\bullet}}^{\wr}=M^{\star}
.

4.

For all
M\in\lambda{\mathsf{C}}
,
{M^{\bullet}}^{-}=\overline{M}
.

Proof.

Notice that, if item 2 holds for some
M
, then item 3 and 4 hold for the same
M
. Item 3 is obtained using item 2.

\begin{array}[]{rcll}{M^{\bullet}}^{\wr}&=&(M;x.\uparrow\!x)^{\wr}\\
&=&(M:\lambda x.(\uparrow\!x)^{\wr})&\text{(by item 2)}\\
&=&(M:\lambda x.kx)\\
&=&M^{\star}\end{array}

Item 4 is obtained using item 3.

\begin{array}[]{rcll}{M^{\bullet}}^{-}&=&\lambda k.{M^{\bullet}}^{\wr}\\
&=&\lambda k.M^{\star}&\text{(by item 3)}\\
&=&\overline{M}\end{array}

Hence, it suffices to prove items 1 and 2, and this is done by simultaneous induction on
V
and
M
.

Case
V=x
.

\begin{array}[]{rcll}{x^{\circ}}^{\sim}&=&x^{\sim}\\
&=&x\\
&=&x^{\dagger}\end{array}

Case
V=\lambda x.M
. By IH, item 4 holds of
M
. Then:

\begin{array}[]{rcll}{(\lambda x.M)^{\circ}}^{\sim}&=&(\lambda x.M^{\bullet})^{\sim}\\
&=&\lambda x.{M^{\bullet}}^{-}\\
&=&\lambda x.\overline{M}&\text{(by item 4)}\\
&=&(\lambda x.M)^{\dagger}\end{array}

Case
M=V
.

\begin{array}[]{rcll}(V;x.N)^{\wr}&=&\mathsf{C}_{v}(V^{\circ},x.N)^{\wr}\\
&=&(\lambda x.M^{\wr}){V^{\circ}}^{\sim}\\
&=&(\lambda x.M^{\wr})V^{\dagger}&\text{(by IH)}\\
&=&(M:\lambda x.N^{\wr})\end{array}

Case
M=PQ
, with
P
not a value.

\begin{array}[]{rcll}(PQ;x.N)^{\wr}&=&(P;m.(mQ;x.N))^{\wr}\\
&=&(P:\lambda m.(mQ;x.N)^{\wr})&\text{(by IH)}\\
&=&(P:\lambda m.(mQ:\lambda x.N^{\wr}))&\text{(by IH)}\\
&=&(PQ:\lambda x.N^{\wr})\end{array}

Case
M=VQ
, with
Q
not a value.

\begin{array}[]{rcll}(VQ;x.N)^{\wr}&=&(Q;n.(Vn;x.N))^{\wr}\\
&=&(Q:\lambda n.(Vn;x.N)^{\wr})&\text{(by IH)}\\
&=&(Q:\lambda n.(Vn:\lambda x.N^{\wr}))&\text{(by IH)}\\
&=&(VQ:\lambda x.N^{\wr})\end{array}

Case
M=VW
.

\begin{array}[]{rcll}(VW;x.N)^{\wr}&=&\mathsf{C}_{v}(V^{\circ},(W^{\circ},x.N))^{\wr}\\
&=&{V^{\circ}}^{\sim}{W^{\circ}}^{\sim}(\lambda x.N^{\wr})\\
&=&V^{\dagger}V^{\dagger}(\lambda x.N^{\wr})&\text{(by IH)}\\
&=&(VW:\lambda x.N^{\wr})\end{array}

Case
M=\mathsf{let}\,y:=P\,\mathsf{in}\,Q
.

\begin{array}[]{rcll}&&(\mathsf{let}\,y:=P\,\mathsf{in}\,Q;x.N)^{\wr}\\
&=&(P;y.(Q;x.N))^{\wr}\\
&=&(P:\lambda y.(Q;x.N)^{\wr})&\text{(by IH)}\\
&=&(P:\lambda y.(Q:\lambda x.N^{\wr}))&\text{(by IH)}\\
&=&(\mathsf{let}\,y:=P\,\mathsf{in}\,Q:\lambda x.N^{\wr})\end{array}

∎

&=&\Phi([[W/y]M\backslash x]P)&\text{(by Lemma \ref{lem:phi-LET})}\end{array}

∎

Lemma 23
.

For all
V,W,M\in{\scriptstyle{CNF}}
:

1.

([V/x]W)^{\sim}=[V^{\sim}/x]W^{\sim}
.

2.

([V/x]M)^{\wr}=[V^{\sim}/x]M^{\wr}
.

Proof.

By simultaneous induction on
W
and
M
.
∎

Lemma 24
.

For all
M,N\in{\scriptstyle{CNF}}
,
([N\backslash x]M)^{\wr}=[\lambda x.M^{\wr}/k]N^{\wr}
.

Proof.

By induction on
N
.

Case
N=V
.

\begin{array}[]{rclcrcl}([V\backslash x]M)^{\wr}&=&([V/x]M)^{\wr}\\
&=&[V^{\sim}/x]M^{\wr}&\text{(by Lemma \ref{lem:subs-neg})}\\
&=&[\lambda x.M^{\wr}/k](kV^{\sim})\\
&=&[\lambda x.M^{\wr}/k]V^{\wr}\end{array}

Case
N=V(W,y.N)
.

\begin{array}[]{rclcrcl}([V(W,y.N)\backslash x]M)^{\wr}&=&(V(W,y.[N\backslash x]M))^{\wr}\\
&=&V^{\sim}W^{\sim}(\lambda y.([N\backslash x]M)^{\wr})\\
&=&V^{\sim}W^{\sim}(\lambda y.[\lambda x.M^{\wr}/k]N^{\wr})&\text{(by IH)}\\
&=&[\lambda x.M^{\wr}/k](V^{\sim}W^{\sim}(\lambda y.N^{\wr}))\\
&=&[\lambda x.M^{\wr}/k](V(W,y.N))^{\wr}\end{array}

∎

Lemma 25
.

For all
V,W,M,P\in{\scriptstyle{CPS}}
:

1.

([V/x]W)^{\times\!\!\!\times}=[V^{\times\!\!\!\times}/x]W^{\times\!\!\!\times}
.

2.

([V/x]M)^{\times}=[V^{\times\!\!\!\times}/x]M^{\times}
.

3.

([V/x]P)^{+}=[V^{\times\!\!\!\times}/x]P^{+}
.

Proof.

By simultaneous induction on
W,M
and
P
.
∎

Lemma 26
.

For all
M,N\in{\scriptstyle{CPS}}
,
([\lambda x.N/k]M)^{\times}=[M^{\times}\backslash x]N^{\times}
.

Proof.

By induction on
M
.
Case
M=kV
.

\begin{array}[]{rclcrcl}([\lambda x.N/k](kV))^{\times}&=&([V/x]N)^{\times}\\
&=&[V^{\times\!\!\!\times}/x]N^{\times}&\text{(by Lemma \ref{lem:subs-cps})}\\
&=&[V^{\times\!\!\!\times}\backslash x]N^{\times}\\
&=&[(kV)^{\times}\backslash x]N^{\times}\end{array}

Case
M=VW(\lambda y.P)
.

\begin{array}[]{rclcrcl}([\lambda x.N/k](VW(\lambda y.P)))^{\times}&=&VW(\lambda y.[\lambda x.N/k]P)^{\times}\\
&=&V^{\times\!\!\!\times}(W^{\times\!\!\!\times},y.([\lambda x.N/k]P)^{\times})\\
&=&V^{\times\!\!\!\times}(W^{\times\!\!\!\times},y.[P^{\times}\backslash x]N^{\times})&\text{(by IH)}\\
&=&[V^{\times\!\!\!\times}(W^{\times\!\!\!\times},y.P^{\times})\backslash x]N^{\times}\\
&=&[(VW(\lambda y.P))^{\times}\backslash x]N^{\times}\end{array}

∎

Theorem
5
.

{\scriptstyle{CNF}}\cong{\scriptstyle{CPS}}
.

1.

For all
M,V\in{\scriptstyle{CNF}}
,
{M^{-}}^{+}=M
and
{M^{\wr}}^{\times}=M
and
{V^{\sim}}^{\times\!\!\!\times}=V
.

2.

For all
P,M,V\in{\scriptstyle{CPS}}
,
{P^{+}}^{-}=P
and
{M^{\times}}^{\wr}=M
and
{V^{\times\!\!\!\times}}^{\sim}=V
.

3.

If
M_{1}\to M_{2}
in
{\scriptstyle{CNF}}
then
M_{1}^{\wr}\to M_{2}^{\wr}
in
{\scriptstyle{CPS}}
(hence
M_{1}^{-}\to M_{2}^{-}
in
{\scriptstyle{CPS}}
).

4.

If
M_{1}\to M_{2}
in
{\scriptstyle{CPS}}
then
M_{1}^{\times}\to M_{2}^{\times}
in
{\scriptstyle{CNF}}
. Hence If
P_{1}\to P_{2}
in
{\scriptstyle{CPS}}
then
P_{1}^{+}\to P_{2}^{+}
in
{\scriptstyle{CNF}}
.

Proof.

Item 1 is straightforward by simultaneous induction on
M
and
V
. Similarly, item 2 is straightforward by simultaneous induction on
P,M
and
V
. Item 3 follows by induction on the relation
M_{1}\to M_{2}
in
{\scriptstyle{CNF}}
. We just show the base case.

Case
(\beta_{v})
.

\begin{array}[]{rcll}((\lambda y.M)(V,x.P))^{\wr}&=&(\lambda y.\lambda k.M^{\wr})V^{\sim}(\lambda x.P^{\wr})\\
&\to_{\beta_{v}}&[\lambda x.P^{\wr}/k][V^{\sim}/y]M^{\wr}\\
&=&[\lambda x.P^{\wr}/k]([V/y]M)^{\wr}&\text{(by Lemma \ref{lem:subs-neg})}\\
&=&([[V/y]M\backslash x]P)^{\wr}&\text{(by Lemma \ref{lem:col-neg})}\end{array}

Analogously, item 4 follows by induction on the relation
M_{1}\to M_{2}
in
{\scriptstyle{CPS}}
. We just show the base case.

Case
(\beta_{v})
.

\begin{array}[]{rcll}((\lambda y.\lambda x.M)W(\lambda x.N))^{\times}&=&(\lambda y.M^{\times})(W^{\times\!\!\!\times},x.N^{+})\\
&\to_{\beta_{v}}&[[W^{\times\!\!\!\times}/y]M^{\times}\backslash x]N^{+}\\
&=&[([W/y]M)^{\times}\backslash x]N^{+}&\text{(by Lemma \ref{lem:subs-cps})}\\
&=&([\lambda x.N/k][W/y]M)^{\times}&\text{(by Lemma \ref{lem:col-cps})}\end{array}

∎

Abstract
. We prove that a trace inequality holds for John domains Ω \Omega satisfying ℋ n − 1 ​ ( ∂ Ω ∖ ∂ ∗ Ω ) = 0 , \mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial\Omega\setminus\partial_{*}\Omega)=0, where ∂ ∗ Ω \partial_{*}\Omega denotes the measure-theoretic boundary, together with an upper density bound on ∂ Ω \partial\Omega . This class of domains includes ( ϵ , r ) (\epsilon,\,r) -perimeter minimizers of Wulff perimeter P K P_{K} which are close to the associated convex body K K . Particularly, this result is established without requiring ∂ Ω \partial\Omega to be Ahlfors regular. As a consequence, we give an alternative proof for a crucial step in the quantitative Wulff inequality, thereby providing a meaningful commentary on the seminal work of Figalli, Maggi, and Pratelli [ 11 ] .

1.
Introduction

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},n\geq 2
be a convex (open) set containing the origin such that
|K|=|B|
.
Here
B
denotes the standard Euclidean unit ball and
|\cdot|
denotes the Lebesgue measure. We denote by
\mathcal{H}^{k}
the
k
-dimensional Hausdorff measure, and define

P_{K}(E)=\int_{\partial^{*}E}\|\nu_{E}(x)\|_{*}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)

where
\|\cdot\|_{*}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is defined as

\|y\|_{*}:=\sup_{x\in K}x\cdot y\ \text{ for any }y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

which is convex, positive, and
1
-homogeneous. Here,
E
represents a set of finite perimeter,
\partial^{*}E
denotes its reduced boundary, and
\nu_{E}
refers to the (measure-theoretic) unit outer normal; see the beginning of SubSection
2.1
for more specific definitions. We further assume that

\int_{K}x=0.

This assumption is not restrictive for our problem; see Remark
1.1
below for a detailed explanation.

We usually refer to

P_{K}(E)
as the
Wulff perimeter
of
E
, and
K
as the
Wulff shape
corresponding to the surface tension

\|\cdot\|_{*}
.
The (open) set
K
can be characterized via
\|\cdot\|_{*}
by

K=\bigcap_{\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon x\cdot\nu<\|\nu\|_{*}\right\}.

In particular, when
K=B
, or equivalently
\|\cdot\|_{*}\equiv 1
on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, one obtains the standard Euclidean norm and denotes by

P(E):=P_{B}(E)=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}E)

the perimeter of
E
with respect to the Euclidean norm.

Analogous to the standard Euclidean case, we have the Wulff inequality: For any set of finite perimeter
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

P_{K}(E)\geq n|K|^{\frac{1}{n}}|E|^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.

Moreover, the equality holds if and only if
E
is congruent to
K
up to translation and dilation.

In their distinguished work
[
11
]
, Figalli, Maggi, and Pratelli employed a mass transportation approach to demonstrate a quantified version of the Wulff inequality: For every set of finite perimeter
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|K|=|E|=|B|
, one has

(1.1)

P_{K}(E)-P_{K}(K)\geq c(n)\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|E\Delta(x+K)|^{2},

where the constant is independent of
K
.
Their result holds significant implications for exploring the geometric configurations of small liquid droplets and crystalline structures; see e.g.
[
9
,
12
]
.

Remark 1.1
.

For any affine map
L:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
, (
1.1
) is invariant under affine maps according to
[
11
, Step 2, Proof of Theorem 1.1]
. Hence, up to a translation, we may assume the barycenter of
K
is the origin.

Moreover, recall John’s lemma
[
17
, Theorem III]
yields that, for any convex set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there exists an affine map
L_{0}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
so that

B_{1}\subset L_{0}(K)\subset B_{n},\quad\det L_{0}>0.

Thus, we also assume that

(1.2)

1\leq\frac{M_{K}}{m_{K}}\leq n.

where
M_{k}
and
m_{k}
are defined as

(1.3)

m_{K}:=\inf\{\|v\|_{*}:v\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\}\quad\text{and}\quad M_{K}:=\sup\{\|v\|_{*}:v\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\}.

An indispensable step in their proof, presented in
[
11
, Theorem 3.4]
, requires identifying, for any set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
close to the Wulff shape
K
with small isoperimetric deficit

\delta(E):=\frac{P_{K}(E)}{n|K|^{\frac{1}{n}}|E|^{\frac{n-1}{n}}}-1\ll 1,

a corresponding set
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This set
F
, while is close to
E
, supports either a Sobolev-Poincaré inequality of the form:

(1.4)

\int_{E}\|-Du(x)\|_{*}\,dx\geq c(n)\inf_{a\in\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{E}|u(x)-a|^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\quad\text{ for all }\ u\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

wherein one can derive a variant of (
1.1
) for
F
with a non-optimal exponent of
4
on the right-hand side; or a trace inequality of the form

(1.5)

\int_{E}\|-Du(x)\|_{*}\,dx\geq c(n)\inf_{a\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{\partial E}|u(x)-a|\|\nu_{E}(x)\|_{*}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)\quad\text{ for all }\ u\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

wherein one can precisely establish the inequality (
1.1
) for
F
with the optimal exponent of 2; see
[
11
, Section 1.6]
for more discussions.
We emphasize that the constants in (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) are independent of
K
once the ratio
\frac{M_{k}}{m_{k}}
is bounded uniformly.
The construction of
F
in
[
11
]
is well-executed, utilizing a ’maximal critical set’ to derive an explicit constant in the trace inequality.

On the other hand, a sharp quantitative isoperimetric inequality for the standard Euclidean norm was proven by Fusco, Maggi, and Pratelli
[
10
]
through the quantification of Steiner symmetrization. Later, Cicalese and Leonardi provided an alternative argument in
[
7
]
(see also
[
1
]
), based on the regularity of minimal surfaces. In their approach, they also needed to slightly modify a set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
that is close to the unit ball
B
, in order to obtain a set
F
which is
W^{1,\,\infty}
-close to
B
. To achieve this, they first established a penalized variational problem based on
E
to obtain the set
F
, and then apply the regularity results for
(\epsilon,\,r)
-minimizers (see Definition
1.4
below) to show that
F
is even
C^{1,\,\alpha}
-close to
B
for some
0<\alpha<\frac{1}{2}
.
This method is now known as the
selection principle
and has found wide application; see, for example,
[
5
,
14
,
12
]
.

However, a direct application of the regularity method, namely the selection principle, in the argument presented in
[
11
]
encounters a challenge: the Lipschitz regularity (uniformly under appropriate normalization) for
(\epsilon,r)
-minimizers with respect to
P_{K}
still poses an unresolved problem when
n\geq 3
; we draw attention to
[
22
]
for a related endeavor in this direction. Indeed, the shape
K
itself may lack
C^{1,\,\alpha}
regularity and uniform convexity, thereby complicating prospects for enhanced regularity. To our knowledge, the most notable advancement in this regard can be found in
[
3
]
and
[
2
, Proposition 4.6]
, where a Lipschitz approximation property was demonstrated for
(\epsilon,r)
-minimizers of
P_{K}
associated with a convex set
K
of arbitrary form. One may consult the references therein for further literature on this topic.

1.1.
Literature: Sobolev-Poincaré meets John

Nevertheless, Lipschitz regularity is not fully necessary to support (
1.4
) (or (
1.5
)). Indeed, there have been numerous studies on the geometric characterizations of domains that support the Sobolev-Poincaré inequality. In particular, it was shown in
[
4
]
and
[
15
]
that a John domain admits a
(p^{*},\,p)
-Sobolev-Poincaré inequality with the same possible exponents
p^{*}
as the one for a ball, where
1\leq p<n
and
p^{*}=\frac{np}{n-p}
. Later, Buckely and Koskela
[
6
]
also proved that the John property is necessary under some mild geometric assumptions on the domain.
This relation was also generalized to other metric measure spaces including Carnot groups; see e.g.
[
16
]
for a comprehensive study.

However, it appears to us that there is currently no direct discussion on the relationship between the trace inequality and John domains until now.
To be specific, let us introduce the concept of John domains.

Definition 1.2
.

For
J\geq 1
, a (bounded) domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be
J
-John provided that, there exists a distinguished point
x_{0}\in\Omega
so that, for any
x\in\Omega
, there exists a curve
\gamma\subset\Omega
starting from
x
ending at
x_{0}
satisfying the following condition:

(1.6)

\ell(\gamma[x,\,y])\leq J{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(y,\,\partial\Omega)\quad\text{ for any $y\in\gamma$},

where
\ell(\gamma[x,\,y])
denotes the Euclidean length of the subcurve of
\gamma
joining
x
to
y
.
We usually call
x_{0}
the John center of
\Omega
and
\gamma
the John curve joining
x_{0}
and
x
.

In addition, a domain is
(J,\,s)
-John if there exists
s>0
and
J\geq 1
so that, whenever
z\in\partial E
and
0<r<s
, for all points
x\in B_{r}(z)\cap\Omega
, there exists a point
w_{x}\in B_{Jr}(z)\cap\Omega
with

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(w_{x},\,\partial\Omega)\geq J^{-1}r

and a curve
\gamma\subset\Omega
joining
x
to
w_{x}
so that (
1.6
) is satisfied for any
y\in\gamma
.

It is worthy to note that
(J,s)
-John domain might not be John as the center
w_{x}
depends on
x
. However, when the boundary of a
(J,s)
-John domain
\Omega
is sufficiently close to that of a John domain in the Hausdorff distance, one can prove
\Omega
is also a John domain, quantitatively; see Lemma
3.1
below.

1.2.
Trace inequality

In order to attain the precise exponent
2
, we establish the validity of (
1.5
) for functions in the space

W^{1,\,1}(\Omega):=\{u\in L^{1}(\Omega):|Du|\in L^{1}(\Omega)\}

within a John domain
\Omega
, subject to certain supplementary conditions.

For a Borel set
E
and

x\in E
, we denote by
E^{(\lambda)}
the set

E^{(\lambda)}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{|E\cap B_{r}(x)|}{|B_{r}(x)|}=\lambda\right\}.

Then the
measure-theoretic boundary

\partial_{*}E
of
E
is defined as
\partial_{*}E:=\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus(E^{(0)}\cup E^{(1)})
.
Now we illustrate the following theorem, where such a theorem has beed studied under the Ahlfors–David regular assumption in
[
8
, Section 7]
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded
J
-John domain and of finite perimeter. Suppose that
\Omega
satisfies

(1.7)

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial\Omega\setminus\partial_{*}\Omega)=0,

and there exists a constant
a_{0}>0
so that, for any
x\in\partial\Omega
and any
0<r<r_{0}=c(n){\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,diam\,}}(\Omega),

(1.8)

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial\Omega\cap B_{r}(x))\leq a_{0}r^{n-1}.

Then for any
u\in W^{1,\,1}(\Omega)
and
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-almost every
x\in\partial\Omega
, one has

(1.9)

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\bf\kern 5.17493pt--\hss}\int}\ignorespaces_{\Omega\cap B_{r}(x)}|u(y)-Tu(x)|dy=0

exists, wherein the trace

Tu(x):=\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\bf\kern 5.17493pt--\hss}\int}\ignorespaces_{\Omega\cap B_{r}(x)}u(y)dy

of
u

is well-defined for
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-almost every
x\in\partial\Omega
.

Moreover, for any
u\in W^{1,\,1}(\Omega)
, there exists a constant
C=C(n,\,a_{0},\,J)
such that

(1.10)

\inf_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{\partial\Omega}|Tu(x)-c|\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)\leq C\int_{\Omega}|Du|\,dx.

1.3.
Application: A selection principle for the quantitative Wulff inequality

Later, we apply Theorem
1.3
to
(\epsilon,\,r)
-minimizers, and conclude a selection principle for the quantitative Wulff inequality in
[
11
]
. Let us recall the definition of
(\epsilon,\,r)
-minimizers.

Definition 1.4
.

Given
\epsilon,\,r>0
, a set of finite perimeter
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an
(\epsilon,\,r)
-minimizer of
P_{K}
, if for any set
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying
E\Delta G\subset\subset x+rK
with
x\in E
, one has

P_{K}(E)\leq P_{K}(G)+\epsilon|E\Delta G|.

Observe that
an
(\epsilon,\,r)
-minimizer
E
is a quasi-minimizer of the Euclidean perimeter
P
in the sense of
[
18
, Definition 3.1]
: for any open set
U\subset\subset B_{\rho}(x)
with
\rho\in(0,r)
and
x\in E
, and every set
G
satisfying
E\Delta G\subset\subset U

, there exists
\kappa=\kappa(\epsilon,\,r,\,n)\geq 1
so that

(1.11)

P(E;U)\leq\kappa P(G;U),

where, for the distributional gradient
D\chi_{E}
of the characteristic function
\chi_{E}
of
E
,

P(E;V)=\|D\chi_{E}\|(V)\quad\text{ for a measurable set }\ V\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},

i.e. the total variation of
D\chi_{E}
in
V
. We note that, in accordance with
[
20
, Theorem 9.6.4]
, the trace inequality (
1.10
) can also be derived under the condition that

\min\{P(G;\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega),\,P(\Omega\setminus G;\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega)\}\leq CP(G;\,\Omega)\quad\text{ for any measurable set }\ G\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Despite their similarities, this condition does not directly follow from (
1.11
) above.

Now the following theorem is a corollary of
[
8
]
; see also the discussion in
[
18
, Page 1620]
.

Theorem 1.5

(
[
8
]
)
.

Let
\epsilon,\,r>0
, and
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an
(\epsilon,\,r)
-minimizer of
P_{K}
. Then there exists a constant
J_{0}=J_{0}\left(n,\epsilon,r\right)>0
so that each component of
E
is a
(J_{0},\,cr)
-John domain with
c=c\left(n,\epsilon,r\right)>0
.

For an
(\epsilon,\,r)
-minimizer
E
of
P_{K}
, we may regard it as a topologically open set, and equivalently integrate with respect to
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
on either its topological boundary or reduced boundary, according to
[
2
, Corollary 3.6]
.

Let us expound briefly on how the conditions in Theorem
1.3
are fulfilled by
(\epsilon,\,r)
-minimizers of
P_{K}
, thereby enabling the application of the selection principle. As one shall see in the proof of Proposition
1.6
,
\epsilon
can be chosen depending only on
n
while
r
is absolute.
Then the standard density estimates
[
19
, Theorem 21.11]
yields (
1.7
) and (
1.8
) with
a_{0}=a_{0}(n)
.
Recall from Remark
1.1
that we may assume that
K
satisfies

1\leq\frac{M_{K}}{m_{K}}\leq n.

Moreover, when the set
E
is close to
K
in the sense of (
1.12
) below, Lemma
3.1
establishes the (global)
J
-John property of
E
.
Then Theorem
1.3
immediately implies that (
1.5
) also holds for
(\epsilon,\,r)
-minimizers of
P_{K}
close to
K
.
Consequently, we are empowered to utilize Theorem
1.3
and conclude the following proposition.

Proposition 1.6
.

Let
1\leq\frac{M_{K}}{m_{K}}\leq n
,
\delta_{k}\to 0^{+}
and
E_{k}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a sequence of sets of finite perimeter for which

(1.12)

(1-\delta)K\subset E_{k}\subset(1+\delta)K

for some
\delta=\delta(n)>0
small,
|E_{k}|=|K|
,

E_{k}\to K
in measure and

(1.13)

P_{K}(E_{k})-P_{K}(K)\leq\delta_{k}\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|E_{k}\Delta(x+K)|^{2}.

We additionally assume that

\int_{E_{k}}x=0.

Then there exists a sequence of
J
-John domains
F_{k}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying
|F_{k}|=|K|
and

(1.14)

\int_{F_{k}}x=0.

In addition,

(1.15)

P_{K}(F_{k})-P_{K}(K)\leq\alpha_{k}\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|F_{k}\Delta(x+K)|^{2}

with
J=J\left(n\right)
and
\alpha_{k}\to 0
as
k\to 0
.

Given that the proof of Proposition
1.6
follows a standard approach and closely mirrors the one found in
[
13

, we have included it in the Appendix for completeness. To some extent, this proposition indicates that the stability of the (anisotropic) isoperimetric inequality derives from the stability of the John property of the minimizers under small perturbations.

2.
Trace inequality in John domains

2.1.
Preliminary

Let us establish some notation.
For a (rectifiable) curve
\gamma
, we denote by
\ell(\gamma)
the Euclidean length of
\gamma
. When
\gamma
is a curve, for any pair of points
x,\,y\in\gamma
, denote the subcurve joining
x
to
y
by
\gamma[x,\,y]
. For any measurable set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define

{\hbox to0.0pt{\bf\kern 5.17493pt--\hss}\int}\ignorespaces_{A}u\,dx=\frac{1}{|A|}\int_{A}u\,dx\quad\text{for any }u\in L^{1}(A).

Recall that a measurable set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to have
finite perimeter
if the distributional gradient of its characteristic function
\mathbf{\chi}_{E}
is an
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued Radon measure
D\mathbf{\chi}_{E}
with finite total variation, i.e.,
|D\mathbf{\chi}_{E}|(\mathbb{R}^{n})<\infty
.
According to the Lebesgue-Besicovitch differentiation theorem for measures, for
|D\mathbf{\chi}_{E}|
-a.e.
x
, it holds

(2.1)

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}-\frac{D\mathbf{\chi}_{E}(x+rB^{n})}{|D\mathbf{\chi}_{E}|(x+rB^{n})}=\nu_{E}(x)\quad\text{and}\quad|\nu_{E}(x)|=1.

The set of points
x
where this condition holds is called the
reduced boundary
of
E
and is denoted by
\partial^{*}E
.
At points on the reduced boundary,
\nu_{E}(x)
represents the
measure-theoretic outward unit normal
to
E
at
x
.
Also, up to changing
E
in a set of measure zero, one can assume that
\overline{\partial^{*}E}=\partial E
.
We refer the interested reader to
[
19
, Sections 12 and 15]
for more details on sets of finite perimeter.

2.2.
Trace inequality

In this section, we always consider
K
so that (
1.2
) is satisfied.
We first record the following observation.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a John domain with John center
x_{0}\in\Omega
. Then for any
x\in\partial\Omega
, there exists (at least) one curve
\gamma_{x}\subset\overline{\Omega}
with
{\gamma}_{x}\setminus\{x\}\subset\Omega
from
x
to
x_{0}
for which (
1.6
) holds.

Proof.

Take a sequence of points
x_{i}\in\Omega
approaching
x
, and the corresponding John curve
{\gamma}_{x_{i}}\subset\Omega
joining
x
to
x_{0}
. Then
\ell({\gamma}_{x_{i}})
is uniformly bounded according to (
1.6
). Now by parametrizing
\gamma_{x_{i}}
via arc length and up to relabeling the sequence, Arzelá-Ascoli lemma yields the uniform convergence of
{\gamma}_{i}
to some curve
{\gamma}_{x}\subset\overline{\Omega}
joining
x
to
x_{0}
.

Moreover, the uniform convergence also implies that
{\gamma}_{x}
satisfies (
1.6
). This gives

{\gamma}_{x}\setminus\{x\}\subset\Omega.

This concludes the lemma.
∎

Now we introduce the definition of an admissible domain.

Definition 2.2
.

A bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with finite perimeter is said to be admissible provided

(i)

The measure-theoretic boundary of
\Omega
almost coincides with its topological boundary, i.e.

(2.2)

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial\Omega\setminus\partial_{*}\Omega)=0.

(ii)

For any
\widetilde{x}\in\partial\Omega
, there exists a positive constant
\Theta=\Theta(\Omega)
and a ball
B_{r}(\widetilde{x})
so that

(2.3)

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}((\partial_{*}\Omega)\cap(\partial_{*}E))\leq\Theta\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\Omega\cap(\partial_{*}E))

holds for each measurable set
E\subset\overline{\Omega}\cap B_{r}(\widetilde{x})
.

In
[
25
]
, it is shown that for an admissible domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the trace
Tu
of the function
u\in W^{1,1}(\Omega)
can be defined in
L^{1}(\partial\Omega)
. To be more precise, as long as we prove that all the John domains
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying the assumptions in Theorem
1.3
are admissible,
[
25
, Theorem 5.14.4]
immediately ensures the existence of the trace for each
u\in W^{1,1}(\Omega)
. Additionally,
[
25
, Theorem 5.10.7]
gives

\inf_{c}\int_{\partial\Omega}|Tu(x)-c|\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)\leq C(\Omega)\int_{\Omega}|Du(x)|\,dx.

However, the inequality above is
insufficient
for our purposes, even for the standard Euclidean norm, as the dependence of
C(\Omega)
on
\Omega
is not explicit in
[
25
, Theorem 5.10.7]
. To address this issue, we present an alternative proof based on the John property, which provides an explicit dependence on the parameters.

Let us introduce the Whitney decomposition for an open set
\Omega\subsetneqq\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For a constant
c>0
and any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with center
x_{Q}=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})
and sides parallel to the coordinate axis, we let
\ell(Q)
be the edge length of
Q
and then rewrite

\{x=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:-c\ell(Q)/2\leq x_{i}-a_{i}\leq c\ell(Q)/2\}

as
cQ
for brevity. Now we can state the following lemma on Whitney decomposition, see e.g.
[
23
, Chapter VI]
.

Lemma 2.3

(Whitney decomposition)
.

For any open set
\Omega\neq\mathbb{R}^{n}
there exists a collection

\mathscr{F}=\{Q_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of countably many closed dyadic cubes such that

(2.4)

Appendix A
Proof of the Selection Principle.

Proof of Proposition
1.6
.

Set

A(E):=\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|E\Delta(x+K)|

for any measurable set
E
, and observe that this minimum is attained for some
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
whenever
E
is bounded.
Moreover, for any bounded measurable set
E,F\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

(A.1)

|A(E)-A(F)|\leq|E\Delta F|

since, by assuming
A(E)\geq A(F)
without loss of generality and observing that

A(F)=|F\Delta(y+K)|\quad\text{ for some }\ y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

the triangle inequality gives

A(E)-A(F)\leq|E\Delta(y+K)|-|F\Delta(y+K)|\leq|E\Delta F|.

Step 1:

We first claim that, up to translations,
K
is the unique minimizer of the problem:

(A.2)

\min\{P_{K}(U)+\Lambda\big||U|-|K|\big|:U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\}\quad\text{ for $\Lambda>n$}.

Indeed, by the Wulff inequality, we may replace
U
in (
A.2
) with some
(x+rK)
satisfying
|(x+rK)|=|U|
. Then (
A.2
) is equivalent to finding where the minimum of

h(r):=nr^{n-1}+\Lambda|r^{n}-1|

can be reached. Since
h
has a unique minimum when
r=1
in case
\Lambda>n
, our claim holds.

Step 2: Replace
E_{k}
by a
(\Lambda+1,R_{0})
-minimizer
.
Let

(A.3)

\Lambda=n+1\quad\text{and}\quad R_{0}=10.

To set up the new set, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
we consider a minimizer
F_{k}^{\prime}
of the following problem:

(A.4)

\min\Big\{P_{K}(U)+\big|A(U)-A(E_{k})\big|+\Lambda\big||U|-|K|\big|:U\subset R_{0}K\Big\},

where
\Lambda>n
is fixed. Since the variational energy (
A.4
) is invariant under translation, we may assume

(A.5)

\int_{F_{k}^{\prime}}x=0

up to a translation. In addition, up to extracting a subsequence, the point
y_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying

(A.6)

A(F_{k}^{\prime})=|F_{k}^{\prime}\Delta(y_{k}+K)|

converges to some point
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Moreover,
by
[
19

, up to passing to a subsequence, there is a set
F
satisfying

(A.7)

\int_{F}x=0,\quad\lim_{k\to+\infty}|F_{k}^{\prime}\Delta F|=0\quad\text{and}\quad P_{K}(F)\leq\liminf_{k\to+\infty}P_{K}(F_{k}^{\prime}).

As a result, recalling that

since

A.4
), (
A.6
) and (

) to obtain the minimality of

, that is, for any

with finite perimeter,

\displaystyle P_{K}(F)+A(F)+\Lambda\big||F|-|K|\big|\leq P_{K}(F)+|F\Delta(y_{0}+K)|+\Lambda\big||F|-|K|\big|

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{k\to+\infty}P_{K}(F_{k}^{\prime})+|F_{k}^{\prime}\Delta(y_{k}+K)|-A(E_{k})+\Lambda\big||F_{k}^{\prime}|-|K|\big|

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{k\to+\infty}P_{K}(F_{k}^{\prime})+\big|A(F_{k}^{\prime})-A(E_{k})\big|+\Lambda\big||F_{k}^{\prime}|-|K|\big|

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{k\to+\infty}P_{K}(U)+\big|A(U)-A(E_{k})\big|+\Lambda\big||U|-|K|\big|

(A.8)

\displaystyle=P_{K}(U)+A(U)+\Lambda\big||U|-|K|\big|.

Further recalling (
A.2
) and that, up to a translation,
K
is the unique solution of

\min\{A(U):U\subset R_{0}K\},

it implies that, up to translations,
K
is the unique solution of

\min\{P_{K}(U)+A(U)+\Lambda\big||U|-|K|\big|:U\subset R_{0}K\}.

As a result, from (
A.8
), (
A.7
) and (
A.6
) it follows that

with
\int_{K}x=0
.

Observe that
\partial F_{k}^{\prime}
converges to
\partial K
in the Hausdorff metric since (

) and
F_{k}^{\prime}\subset R_{0}K
. Next, we show that

are all

-minimizers.

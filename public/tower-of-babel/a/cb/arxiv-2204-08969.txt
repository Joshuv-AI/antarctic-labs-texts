Couplings and primitives on topological spaces
Jürgen Voigt

Abstract
For an open covering 𝒰 \mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{U} of a topological space and a mapping d : ℐ → 𝕂 d\colon\mathcal{I}\to\mathbb{K}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt , where ℐ : = { ( U , V ) ∈ 𝒰 × 𝒰 ; U ∩ V ≠ ∅ } \mathcal{I}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigl\{(U,V)\in\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{U}\times\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{U}{;}\;U\cap V\neq\varnothing\bigr\} , we present conditions for the existence of a mapping C : 𝒰 → 𝕂 C\colon\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{U}\to\mathbb{K}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt satisfying C V − C U = d U ​ V C_{V}-C_{U}=d_{UV} for all ( U , V ) ∈ ℐ (U,V)\in\mathcal{I} . The result is applied to a Poincaré type theorem concerning distributional potentials. We also put the result into the context of algebraic topology. MSC 2010: 54C20, 46F10, 55U99 Keywords: topological space, simply connected, distribution, cocycle



1
Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a topological space,
\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
an open covering of
\Omega
, and denote by

\mathcal{I}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigl\{(U,V)\in\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{U}\times\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{U}{;}\;U\cap V\neq\varnothing\bigr\}

the set of intersecting pairs. A mapping
d\colon\mathcal{I}\to\mathbb{K}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt
, where
\mathbb{K}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt
denotes the field of real or complex numbers, will be called a
coupling
. A mapping
C\colon\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{U}\to\mathbb{K}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt
will be called a
primitive
of
d
if

d_{UV}=C_{V}-C_{U}
for all
(U,V)\in\mathcal{I}
. (We write the arguments of the mappings
d
and
C
as subscripts.)

Clearly, if
C\colon\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{U}\to\mathbb{K}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt
is any mapping, then
C
is a primitive of the coupling
d\colon\mathcal{I}\to\mathbb{K}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt
given by

d_{UV}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=C_{V}-C_{U}\qquad((U,V)\in\mathcal{I}).

Note that this coupling
d
has the properties

\displaystyle\begin{split}d_{UV}=-d_{VU}\qquad&((U,V)\in\mathcal{I}),\\
d_{UW}=d_{UV}+d_{VW}\qquad&(U,V,W\in\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{U},\ U\cap V\cap W\neq\varnothing).\end{split}

(1.1)

This means that the properties (
1.1
) are minimal requirements for the existence of a
primitive for a coupling.

1.1 Theorem
.

Let
\Omega
be a simply connected topological space, and let
\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{U}
be a covering of
\Omega
consisting of path-connected open sets. Let
d\colon\mathcal{I}\to\mathbb{K}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt
be a coupling
satisfying the compatibility conditions (
1.1
). Then there exists a primitive
C
of
d
.

This theorem will be proved in Section
2
. In order to motivate the result we indicate the following two applications.

1.2 Corollary
.

Let
\Omega
and
\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{U}
be as in Theorem
1.1
. For each
U\in\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{U}
let
f_{U}\colon U\to\mathbb{K}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt
be a function, and suppose that for all
(U,V)\in\mathcal{I}
the function

f_{V}-f_{U}
is constant on
U\cap V
.
Then there exists a function
f\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{K}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt
such that
f_{U}-f
is constant on
U
for all
U\in\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{U}
.

1.3 Corollary
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt^{n}
be a simply connected open set, and let
\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{U}
be a covering of
\Omega
consisting of path-connected
open sets. For each
U\in\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{U}
let
F_{U}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(U)

be a distribution. Suppose that for all
(U,V)\in\mathcal{I}
the restriction
F_{V}-F_{U}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(U\cap V)
to

\mathcal{D}(U\cap V)
is generated by a constant function. Then there exist
a mapping
C\colon\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{U}\to\mathbb{K}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt
and a distribution
F\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)
such that
F_{U}=F+C_{U}
on
\mathcal{D}(U)
for all
U\in\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{U}
.

Proofs of these corollaries will be provided in Section
3
.
A proof of Corollary
1.3
, in a special context, is given in
[
3
, pp. 528–532]
, and our proof of Theorem
1.1
is inspired by this proof.
Applying the ideas of
[
3
]
in order to prove Corollary
1.2
, the author realised that the proofs of Corollaries
1.2
and
1.3
became rather similar, and this
was the motivation for finding a context, now stated in Theorem
1.1
, from which both corollaries can be derived.

In Section
4
we sketch the application of Corollary
1.3
in the proof of a distributional version of Poincaré’s lemma.

In Section
5
we outline how Theorem
1.1
fits into the context of algebraic topology.

2
Existence of primitives for couplings

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
U_{0}\in\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{U}
and
x_{0}\in U_{0}
be fixed throughout this proof.
Let
U\in\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{U}
,
x\in U
and a path
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to\Omega
connecting
x_{0}
with

x
, i.e.
\gamma
is continuous, and
\gamma(0)=x_{0}
,
\gamma(1)=x
, be fixed throughout parts (i)–(iii)
of the proof.

(i) As
\bigl(\gamma^{-1}(V)\bigr)_{V\in\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{U}}
is an open covering of
[0,1]
, there exist

t_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=0<t_{1}<\dots<t_{n-1}<1=\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}t_{n}
and
U_{1},\dots,U_{n}\in\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{U}
such that

\gamma([t_{k-1},t_{k}])\subseteq U_{k}\qquad(k=1,\dots,n).

(2.1)

Put

C_{U,x,\gamma,t_{1},\dots,t_{n-1}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}d_{U_{k}U_{k+1}},

(2.2)

with the convention
U_{n+1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=U
.
We are going to show that
C_{U,x,\gamma,t_{1},\dots,t_{n-1}}
does not depend on the choice of the sets
U_{1},\dots,U_{n}
and the intermediate points
t_{1},\dots,t_{n-1}
.

First, replacing one of the sets
U_{k}
by another set
V_{k}\in\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{U}
containing
\gamma([t_{k-1},t_{k}])
, we apply (
1.1
)
and obtain

d_{U_{k-1}V_{k}}+d_{V_{k}U_{k+1}}=d_{U_{k-1}U_{k}}+d_{U_{k}V_{k}}+d_{V_{k}U_{k}}+d_{U_{k}U_{k+1}}=d_{U_{k-1}U_{k}}+d_{U_{k}U_{k+1}}.

This implies that
C_{U,x,\gamma,t_{1},\dots,t_{n-1}}
does not depend on the choice of the sets
U_{k}
satisfying (
2.1
).

Next suppose that we include an additional intermediate point
s\in(0,1)
, say
t_{k-1}<s<t_{k}
, for some

k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. Then, observing that
\gamma([t_{k-1},s])\subseteq U_{k}
and
\gamma([s,t_{k}])\subseteq U_{k}
, the
sum in (
2.2
) corresponding to the intermediate points
t_{0}<\dots<t_{k-1}<s<t_{k}<\dots t_{n}
will just contain the additional term
d_{U_{k}U_{k}}=0
, hence is unchanged.

Now let
s_{0}=0<s_{1}<\dots<s_{m-1}<1=s_{m}
together with sets

V_{1},\dots,V_{m}\in\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{U}
satisfy the conditions
analogous to (
2.1
), and let
C_{U,x,\gamma,s_{1},\dots,s_{m-1}}
denote the corresponding sum (
2.2
). Then we can augment
one by one the original intermediate points to the set containing all the points

t_{1},\dots,t_{n-1},s_{1},\dots,s_{m-1}
, without changing the value of the sum in (
2.2
). Since we can do
the same, starting with the intermediate points
0<s_{1}<\dots s_{m-1}<1
, we conclude that

C_{U,x,\gamma,s_{1},\dots,s_{m-1}}=C_{U,x,\gamma,t_{1},\dots,t_{n-1}}=\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}C_{U,x,\gamma}.

(ii) Let

\widetilde{\gamma}\colon[0,1]\to\Omega

be another path connecting
x_{0}
with
x
. By hypothesis, there exists an FEP-homotopy

\Gamma\colon[0,1]^{2}\to\Omega

connecting
\gamma
with
\widetilde{\gamma}
, i.e.
\Gamma
is continuous,
\gamma=\Gamma(\cdot,0)
,

\widetilde{\gamma}=\Gamma(\cdot,1)
,
\Gamma(0,\cdot)=x_{0}
,
\Gamma(1,\cdot)=x
. We show that

C_{U,x,\widetilde{\gamma}}=C_{U,x,\gamma}
.

To show this, let
s_{0}\in[0,1]
. Then for the path
\Gamma(\cdot,s_{0})
there exist intermediate points

0<t_{1}<\dots<t_{n-1}<1
and sets
U_{1},\dots,U_{n}\in\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{U}
satisfying the properties analogous to (
2.1
), and
then

C_{U,x,\Gamma(\cdot,s_{0})}=C_{U,x,\Gamma(\cdot,s_{0}),t_{1},\dots,t_{n-1}}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}d_{U_{k}U_{k+1}}.

The continuity of
\Gamma
implies that the same intermediate points, with the same sets
U_{1},\dots,U_{n}
can be
taken for paths
\Gamma(\cdot,s)
with the homotopy parameter
s
in a neighbourhood of
s_{0}
. This shows that the function

[0,1]\ni s\mapsto C_{U,x,\Gamma(\cdot,s)}
is locally constant, hence constant, and

C_{U,x,\widetilde{\gamma}}=C_{U,x,\Gamma(\cdot,1)}=C_{U,x,\Gamma(\cdot,0)}=C_{U,x,\gamma}=\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}C_{U,x}.

(iii)
Let
y\in U
. As
U
is path-connected by hypothesis, there exists a path
\gamma_{1}\colon[1,2]\to U

connecting
x
with
y
. We combine
\gamma
with
\gamma_{1}
to a path
\widehat{\gamma}
connecting
x_{0}
with
y
. Evaluating
C_{U,x}
and

C_{U,y}
by the expression (
2.1
), we see that the sums only differ by the last term in the expression for

C_{U,y}
, given by
d_{UU}=0
. We conclude that

C_{U,x}=C_{U,y}=\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}C_{U}.

(iv) It remains to show that
C
is a primitive of
d
, i.e.
d_{UV}=C_{V}-C_{U}
for all
(U,V)\in\mathcal{I}
.
Let
x\in U\cap V
,
y\in V
. Then an argument analogous to the previous step (iii) yields

C_{V}=C_{V,y,\widehat{\gamma}}=C_{U,x,\gamma}+d_{UV}=C_{U}+d_{UV}
.
∎

3
Proofs of the corollaries

Proof of Corollary
1.2
.

For all
(U,V)\in\mathcal{I}
let
d_{UV}
be the constant satisfying
f_{V}=f_{U}+d_{UV}
on
U\cap V
. Then clearly
d_{VU}=-d_{UV}
.
If
U,V,W\in\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{U}
are such that
U\cap V\cap W\neq\varnothing
, then
f_{V}=f_{U}+d_{UV}
and

f_{W}=f_{V}+d_{VW}=f_{U}+d_{UV}+d_{VW}
on
U\cap V\cap W
. Since also
f_{W}=f_{U}+d_{UW}
on

U\cap W\supseteq U\cap V\cap W
, one obtains
d_{UW}=d_{UV}+d_{VW}
. Hence Theorem
1.1
implies that
there exists a primitive
C
of
d
. Then the function
f\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{K}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt
, given by

f\vphantom{f}\mskip 1.0mu\vrule\mskip 2.0mu_{U}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=f_{U}-C_{U}\qquad(U\in\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{U})

is well-defined. Indeed, if
(U,V)\in\mathcal{I}
, then on
U\cap V
one obtains

f_{V}-C_{V}=(f_{U}+d_{UV})-(C_{U}+d_{UV})=f_{U}-C_{U}.\qed

Proof of Corollary
1.3
.

For all
(U,V)\in\mathcal{I}
let
d_{UV}
be the constant satisfying
F_{V}=F_{U}+d_{UV}
on
\mathcal{D}(U\cap V)
. (Here the
constant
d_{UV}
stands for the distribution generated by the constant function
d_{UV}
. More explicitly, the
equality
F_{V}=F_{U}+d_{UV}
means that

\left\langle F_{V},\varphi\right\rangle=\left\langle F_{U},\varphi\right\rangle+\int_{U\cap V}d_{UV}\varphi(x)\mathclose{}\,\mathrm{d}x

for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(U\cap V)
.) As in the proof of Corollary
1.2
one shows that
(d_{UV})_{(U,V)\in\mathcal{I}}

is a coupling satisfying (
1.1
), and for the primitive
C
of
d
resulting from Theorem
1.1

one concludes that
(F_{U}-C_{U})_{U\in\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{U}}
is a consistent family of distributions, i.e.

F_{U}-C_{U}=F_{V}-C_{V}
on
\mathcal{D}(U\cap V)
for all
(U,V)\in\mathcal{I}
. It is a standard fact from the theory of
distributions that – using a suitable partition of unity – one can then construct a distribution

F\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)
satisfying
F=F_{U}-C_{U}
on
\mathcal{D}(U)
for all
U\in\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{U}
.
∎

4
A distributional version of Poincaré’s lemma

The present note was motivated by the following version of Poincaré’s lemma; see
[
3
, Theorem 2.1]
,
[
4
, Theorem 2.1]
.

4.1 Theorem
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt^{n}
be a simply connected open set, and let
G=(G_{1},\dots,G_{n})\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)^{n}
be such that

\partial_{j}G_{k}=\partial_{k}G_{j}\qquad(j,k\in\{1,\dots,n\}).

(4.1)

Then there exists
F\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)
such that

\operatorname{grad}F=G
.

The first step in the proof is to show that the theorem holds if
\Omega
is an open rectangle
\prod_{j=1}^{n}(a_{j},b_{j})
; see

[
3
, pp. 526–528]
or
[
4
, part (i) of the proof of Theorem 2.1]
. (In the latter reference it is shown for more general convex open sets.)

For each open rectangle
U\subseteq\Omega
let
F_{U}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(U)
be a distribution satisfying

\operatorname{grad}F_{U}=G
on
\mathcal{D}(U)
. If
U,V\subseteq\Omega
are open rectangles satisfying
U\cap V\neq\varnothing
, then

\operatorname{grad}(F_{V}-F_{U})=G-G=0
on
\mathcal{D}(U\cap V)
, and this implies that there exists
d_{UV}\in\mathbb{K}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt
such that

F_{V}-F_{U}=d_{UV}
on
\mathcal{D}(U\cap V)
.
Choosing a covering
\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{U}
of
\Omega
by open rectangles one concludes that for each
(U,V)\in\mathcal{I}
there exists

d_{UV}
as described above, and Corollary
1.3
can be applied to provide
F
as asserted in Theorem
4.1
.

5
Context algebraic topology

The author is indebted to Friedrich Martin Schneider (TU Bergakademie Freiberg) for pointing out how Theorem
1.1
relates to algebraic topology and in fact
can be proved in this context. We refer to
[
2
, Section 3.1]
for the notions of algebraic topology used below.

With the covering
\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{U}
of
\Omega
one associates the
nerve
(or
nerve complex
)

N(\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{U})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigl\{\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{V}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{U}){;}\;\bigcap\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{V}\neq\varnothing\bigr\}

(where
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{U})
stands for the collection of finite subsets of
\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{U}
). For this simplicial complex
\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{S}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=N(\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{U})
(over the set
\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{U}
) one considers the cochain
complex
\mathcal{C}^{\ast}(\mathcal{S},\mathbb{K})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigl((C^{*}_{n}(\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{S},\mathbb{K}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt))_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\nonscript\hskip 0.24582pt},(\partial^{*}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\nonscript\hskip 0.24582pt}\bigr)
, where
\partial^{*}_{n}\colon C^{*}_{n-1}(\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{S},\mathbb{K}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt)\to C^{*}_{n}(\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{S},\mathbb{K}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt)
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt
(and
C^{*}_{n}(\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{S},\mathbb{K}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt)=\{0\}

for
n\in\{-2,-3,\dots\}
). The hypothesis that
\Omega
is simply connected and
that (its open covering)
\mathcal{U}
consists of path-connected sets implies (via
standard techniques, more precisely
[
2
, Theorem 3.2 (p. 195) and
Theorem 2A.1 (p. 166)]
and
[
1
, Theorem 8]
) that the cohomology group
H_{1}(\mathcal{C}^{*}(\mathcal{S},\mathbb{K}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt))
is trivial, and this means that the set

B_{1}(\mathcal{C}^{*}(\mathcal{S},\mathbb{K}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt))=\partial^{*}_{1}(C_{0}^{*}(\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{S},\mathbb{K}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt))
of
1
-coboundaries coincides with the set
Z_{1}(\mathcal{C}^{*}(\mathcal{S},\mathbb{K}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt))=\ker(\partial^{*}_{2})
of
1
-cocycles.

Now, a coupling
d
satisfying the compatibility conditions (
1.1
) can be interpreted as a
1
-cocycle
g
. A corresponding element
f\in C^{*}_{0}(\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{S},\mathbb{K}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt)
satisfying

\partial^{*}_{1}f=g
can then be interpreted as a primitive
C
of
d
.

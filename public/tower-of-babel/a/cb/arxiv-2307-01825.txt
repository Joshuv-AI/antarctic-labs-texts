Abstract
. We construct a self-similar solution of the heat equation for the fractional Laplacian with Dirichlet boundary conditions in every fat cone. Furthermore, we give the entrance law from the vertex and the Yaglom limit for the corresponding killed isotropic stable Lévy process and precise large-time asymptotics for solutions of the Cauchy problem in the cone.

Primary: 60G18, 60J35; secondary: 60G51, 60J50.

1.
Introduction

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}:=\{1,2,\ldots\}
. Consider an arbitrary non-empty open cone
\Gamma\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
. Thus,
ry\in\Gamma
whenever
y\in\Gamma
and
r>0
.
Let
p_{t}^{\Gamma}(x,y)
,
t>0
,
x,y\in\Gamma
, be the Dirichlet heat kernel of the cone for the fractional Laplacian. More specifically, for
\alpha\in(0,2)
,

p^{\Gamma}
is the transition density of the isotropic
\alpha
-stable Lévy process in
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
killed upon leaving
\Gamma
; see, e.g., Bogdan and Grzywny
[
9
]
. Let
M_{\Gamma}\colon{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\to[0,\infty)
be the Martin kernel of
\Gamma
with the pole at infinity (for definitions, see Section
2
). The function is homogeneous (or self-similar) of some degree
\beta\in[0,\alpha)
.
Our first result captures the asymptotics of
p_{t}^{\Gamma}
at the vertex
0
of
\Gamma
, as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

If the cone
\Gamma
is fat, then for
s,t>0
,
x\in\Gamma
, we have

\Psi_{t}(x):=\lim\limits_{\Gamma\in y\to 0}p_{t}^{\Gamma}(x,y)/M_{\Gamma}(y)\in(0,\infty),

(1.1)

\Psi_{t}(x)=t^{-(d+\beta)/\alpha}\Psi_{1}\big(t^{-1/\alpha}x\big),

and

(1.2)

\int_{\Gamma}\Psi_{s}(y)p^{\Gamma}_{t}(x,y)\,{\textrm{d}}y=\Psi_{s+t}(x).

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is given in Section
3
.
In view of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
),
\Psi_{t}(x)

is a self-similar semigroup solution of the heat equation for the fractional Laplacian with Dirichlet conditions. By
(
1.2
),

\Psi_{t}
is also an entrance law for
p^{\Gamma}
at the origin, see, e.g., Blumenthal
[
6
, p. 104]
, Haas and Rivero
[
23
]
or Bañuelos et al.
[
2
]
.

The result is the next step in the program
for the
boundary potential theory
sketched in the Introduction of Bogdan at al.
[
10
]
, building on the boundary Harnack principle, Green function, and Dirichlet heat kernel estimates.
In Theorems
3.11
and
3.12
below, we make further applications to Probability by obtaining the so-called Yaglom limit for
\Gamma
. We also give applications to Functional Analysis and Partial Differential Equations, as follows.

Let
1\leqslant q\leqslant\infty
and
L^{q}(\Gamma):=L^{q}(\Gamma,{\textrm{d}}x)
. For a weight function
w>0
, we denote
L^{q}(w):=L^{q}(\Gamma,w(x)\,{\textrm{d}}x)
. For instance,
L^{1}(M_{\Gamma})=\{f/M_{\Gamma}\colon f\in L^{1}\}
. Then, for
1\leqslant q<\infty
, we define

\|f\|_{q,M_{\Gamma}}:=\|f/M_{\Gamma}\|_{L^{q}(M_{\Gamma}^{2})}=\bigg(\int_{\Gamma}|f(x)|^{q}M_{\Gamma}^{2-q}(x)\,{\textrm{d}}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}=\|f\|_{L^{q}(M_{\Gamma}^{2-q})},

and, for
q=\infty
, we let

\|f\|_{\infty,M_{\Gamma}}:=\esssup_{x\in\Gamma}|f(x)|/M_{\Gamma}(x).

Of course,
\|f\|_{1,M_{\Gamma}}=\|f\|_{L^{1}(M_{\Gamma})}
. For a non-negative or integrable function
f
we let

P_{t}^{\Gamma}f(x):=\int_{\Gamma}p_{t}^{\Gamma}(x,y)f(y)\,{\textrm{d}}y,\quad t>0,\,x\in\Gamma.

We say that the cone
\Gamma
is
smooth
if its boundary is
C^{1,1}
outside of origin, to wit, there is
r>0
such that at every boundary point of
\Gamma
on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, there exist inner and outer tangent balls for
\Gamma
, with radii
r
. Put differently, for
d\geqslant 2
, the
spherical cap
,
\Gamma\cap\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, is a
C^{1,1}
subset of
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. For instance, the right-circular cones (see Section
2
) are smooth.

The second result describes the large-time asymptotic behavior of the semigroup
P_{t}^{\Gamma}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
q\in[1,\infty)
. Assume that the cone
\Gamma
is smooth with
\beta\geqslant\alpha/2
. Then for every
f\in L^{1}(M_{\Gamma})
and
A=\int_{\Gamma}f(x)M_{\Gamma}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x
we have

(1.3)

\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{\frac{d+2\beta}{\alpha}\frac{q-1}{q}}\|P_{t}^{\Gamma}f-A\Psi_{t}\|_{q,M_{\Gamma}}=0.

As stated in Lemma
4.9
, the condition
\beta\geqslant\alpha/2
is sharp for smooth cones and
d\geqslant 2
; see also Example
4.12
. Theorem
1.2
follows from the more general Theorem
4.6
, by means of Corollary
4.10
.

Let us comment on previous developments in the literature and our methods.
If
\Gamma={\mathbb{R}^{d}}
, then
\beta=0
,
M_{\Gamma}=1
and
p^{\Gamma}_{t}(x,y)=p_{t}(x,y)
is the transition density of the fractional Laplacian on
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
(see below). In this case,
\Psi_{t}(x)=p_{t}(0,y)
and Theorem
1.2
was resolved by Vázquez
[
36
]
, see also Bogdan et al.
[
13
]
with
\kappa=0
in
[
13
, Eq. (1.1)]
; see Example
4.4
below, too.

For general cones
\Gamma
, the behavior of
p_{t}^{\Gamma}
is intrinsically connected to properties of
M_{\Gamma}
, see, e.g.,
[
9
]
,
[
15
]
, or Kyprianou et al.
[
28
]
. The identification of the Martin kernel
M_{\Gamma}
was accomplished by Bañuelos and Bogdan
[
3
]
. Its crucial property is the homogeneity of order
\beta\in[0,\alpha)
, which is also reflected in the behavior of the Green function studied by Kulczycki
[
25
]
and Michalik
[
29
, Lemma 3.3]
, at least when
\Gamma
is a right-circular cone. As we see in Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, the exponent
\beta
determines the self-similarity of the semigroup solution and the asymptotic behavior of the semigroup
P_{t}^{\Gamma}
, too. For more information on
\beta
we refer to
[
3
]
and Bogdan et al.
[
17
]
.

If
\Gamma
is a Lipschitz cone, then Theorem
1.1
follows from
[
15

. However, the method presented in
[
15
]
does not apply to general fat cones, in particular, to
\Gamma=\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
or
\Gamma=\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus([0,\infty)\times\{0\})
, which are of interest for
\alpha\in(1,2)
.

Instead, in this work we develop an approach suggested by
[
13
]
, where the authors employ a stationary density of an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck type semigroup corresponding to a homogeneous (self-similar) heat kernel in a different setting (on the whole of
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
). Another key tool in their analysis is the so-called Doob conditioning using an invariant function or the heat kernel.
Due to Theorem
3.1
below, the Martin kernel
M_{\Gamma}
is invariant with respect to
P_{t}^{\Gamma}
, which indeed allows for Doob conditioning. Then we form the corresponding Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup and prove existence of a stationary density
\varphi
in Theorem
3.4
by using the Schauder-Tychonoff fixed-point theorem. As we shall see in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, the self-similar semigroup solution
\Psi_{t}
is directly expressed by
\varphi
and
M_{\Gamma}
.

The remaining developments in our paper are as follows. In Subsection
3.3
we obtain an asymptotic relation between the Martin kernel and the survival probability near the vertex of the cone (see Corollary
3.10
). We also obtain a Yaglom limit (quasi-stationary distribution) in Theorem
3.11
, which describes the behavior of the stable process starting from a fixed point
x\in\Gamma
and conditioned to stay in a cone, generalizing Theorem 1.1 of
[
15
]
. In Theorem
3.12
we considerably extend both results by allowing arbitrary initial distributions with finite moment of order
\alpha
.

We note that the Yaglom limit for random walks in cones is discussed by Denisov and Wachtel
[
27
]
,
but generally there are very few results in literature on unbounded sets. For a broad survey on quasi-stationary distributions, we refer to van Doorn and Pollet
[
35
]
; see also Champagnat and Villemonais
[
19
]
.
Self-similar solutions for general homogeneous semigroups are discussed in Cholewa and Rodriguez-Bernal
[
21
]
. Patie and Savov
[
30
]
discuss generalized Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroups, which they call generalized Laguerre semigroups.
Results related to Theorem
1.2
, but for fractal Burgers equation and fractional
p
-Laplacian can be found in Biler et al.
[
5
, Theorem 2.2]
and Vázquez
[
37
, Theorem 1.2]
, respectively.
For an approach to entrance laws based on fluctuation theory of Markov additive processes, we refer to
[
27
]
, see also Chaumont et al.
[
20
]
.

This brief review shows that the asymptotics of the heat kernel is in a busy intersection between Probability, Partial Differential Equations and Functional Analysis even though the fields do not always communicate. Our approach should apply to rather general self-similar operator semigroup kernels, at least when they enjoy an invariant function and suitable upper and lower bounds.

Here is a simple example to illustrate our findings.

Example 1.3
.

If
d=1
,
\alpha\in(0,2)
, and
\Gamma=(0,\infty)
, then
\beta=\alpha/2
,
M_{\Gamma}(x)=(0\vee x)^{\alpha/2}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}
, so, by Theorem
4.6
,
\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{(1+\alpha)/(2\alpha)}\|P_{t}^{\Gamma}f\|_{2}=0
if
\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x)x^{\alpha/2}\,{\textrm{d}}x=0
; see Example
4.11
. Furthermore, by Remark
3.10
and (
4.10
),
the survival probability is
\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{\Gamma}>t)\approx(1\wedge t^{-1/\alpha}x)^{\alpha/2}
and the heat kernel satisfies
p_{1}^{\Gamma}(x,y)\approx(1+|x-y|)^{-1-\alpha}(1\wedge x)^{\alpha/2}(1\wedge y)^{\alpha/2}
, so
\Psi_{t}(x)\approx(t^{1/\alpha}\vee x)^{-1-\alpha}(t^{1/\alpha}\wedge x)^{\alpha/2}
. Here and below
x,y,t>0
. By Lemma
3.6
, the stationary density of the corresponding Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup is
\varphi(x)\approx(1+x)^{-1-3\alpha/2}
and the Yaglom limit has density
\varphi(x)x^{\alpha/2}/\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(y)y^{\alpha/2}\,{\textrm{d}}y\approx x^{\alpha/2}(1+x)^{-1-3\alpha/2}\approx\Psi_{1}(x)
; see Theorem
3.11
. We refer to
[
23
, Example 5]
for an exact but less explicit expression for the Yaglom limit by means of exponential functionals. See also Example
4.11
for the case of
\Gamma=\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
and Example
4.12
for
d
-dimensional extensions.

2.
Preliminaries

For
x,z\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
, the standard scalar product of is denoted by
x\cdot z
and
|z|
is the Euclidean norm. For
x\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
and
r\in(0,\infty)
, we let
B(x,r)=\{y\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\colon|x-y|<r\}
, the ball centered at
x
with radius
r
, and write
B_{r}:=B(0,r)
. All the considered sets, functions and measures are Borel. For non-negative functions
f,g
, we write
f\approx g
if there is a number
c\in(0,\infty)
, i.e., a
constant
, such that
c^{-1}f\leqslant g\leqslant cf
, and write
f\lesssim g
if there is a constant
c
such that
f\leqslant cg
. As usual, for two real numbers
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, we write
a\wedge b:=\min\{a,b\}
and
a\vee b:=\max\{a,b\}
.

Recall that
\alpha\in(0,2)
and let

\nu(z)=c_{d,\alpha}|z|^{-d-\alpha},\quad z\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}},

where the constant
c_{d,\alpha}
is such that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\big(1-\cos(\xi\cdot z)\big)\nu(z)\,{\textrm{d}}z=|\xi|^{\alpha},\quad\xi\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}.

For
t>0
we let

(2.1)

p_{t}(x):=(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}e^{-t|\xi|^{\alpha}}e^{-i\xi\cdot x}\,{\textrm{d}}\xi,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}.

By the Lévy-Khintchine formula,
p_{t}
is a probability density function and

\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}e^{i\xi\cdot x}\,p_{t}(x)\,{\textrm{d}}x=e^{-t|\xi|^{\alpha}},\quad\xi\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}},\;t>0.

We consider the isotropic
\alpha
-stable Lévy process
\mathbf{X}=(X_{t},t\geqslant 0)
in
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
, with

p_{t}(x,y):=p_{t}(y-x),\quad x,y\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}},\ t>0,

as transition density. Thus,

\mathbb{E}_{x}e^{i\xi\cdot X_{t}}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}e^{i\xi\cdot y}\,p_{t}(x,y)\,{\textrm{d}}y=e^{i\xi\cdot x-t|\xi|^{\alpha}},\quad\xi\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}},\,x\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}},\;t>0.

The Lévy-Khintchine exponent of
\mathbf{X}
is, of course,
|\xi|^{\alpha}
and
\nu
is the intensity of jumps.
By (
2.1
),

(2.2)

\displaystyle p_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle=t^{-d/\alpha}p_{1}\big(t^{-1/\alpha}x,t^{-1/\alpha}y\big),\quad x,y\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}},\;t>0,

and

(2.3)

\displaystyle p_{t}\left(Tx,Ty\right)

\displaystyle=p_{t}(x,y),\quad x,y\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}},\;t>0,

for every isometry
T
on
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
.
It is well known that

(2.4)

p_{t}(x,y)\approx t^{-d/\alpha}\wedge t|y-x|^{-d-\alpha},\quad x,y\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}},\;t>0,

see, e.g.,
[
7
]
.
We then consider the time of the first exit of
\mathbf{X}
from the cone
\Gamma
,

\tau_{\Gamma}:=\inf\{t\geqslant 0\colon X_{t}\notin\Gamma\},

and we define
the Dirichlet heat kernel for
\Gamma
,

p_{t}^{\Gamma}(x,y):=p_{t}(x,y)-\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\tau_{\Gamma}<t;p_{t-\tau_{\Gamma}}\left(X_{\tau_{\Gamma}},y\right)\right],\quad x,y\in\Gamma,\;t>0,

see
[
9
,
14
,
22
]
.
It immediately follows that
p_{t}^{\Gamma}(x,y)\leqslant p_{t}(x,y)
for all
x,y\in\Gamma
and
t>0
. The Dirichlet heat kernel is non-negative, and symmetric:
p_{t}^{\Gamma}(x,y)=p_{t}^{\Gamma}(y,x)
for
x,y\in\Gamma
,
t>0
. It satisfies the Chapman-Kolmogorov equations:

(2.5)

p_{t+s}^{\Gamma}(x,y)=\int_{\Gamma}p_{t}^{\Gamma}(x,z)p_{s}^{\Gamma}(z,y)\,{\textrm{d}}z,\quad x,y\in\Gamma,\;s,t>0.

For nonnegative or integrable functions
f
we define the
killed semigroup

by

P_{t}^{\Gamma}f(x):=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\tau_{\Gamma}>t;f(X_{t})\right]=\int_{\Gamma}p_{t}^{\Gamma}(x,y)f(y)\,{\textrm{d}}y,\quad x\in\Gamma,\;t>0.

In particular, for
f\equiv 1
we obtain
the
survival probability
:

(2.6)

\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{\Gamma}>t)=\int_{\Gamma}p_{t}^{\Gamma}(x,y)\,{\textrm{d}}y,\quad x\in\Gamma,\,t>0,

see
[
11
, Remark 1.9]
.
Since
t^{-1/\alpha}\Gamma=\Gamma
, the scaling (
2.2
) extends to the Dirichlet heat kernel:

p_{t}^{\Gamma}(x,y)=t^{-d/\alpha}p_{1}^{\Gamma}\big(t^{-1/\alpha}x,t^{-1/\alpha}y\big),\quad x,y\in\Gamma,\;t>0.

As a consequence,

(2.7)

\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{\Gamma}>t)=\mathbb{P}_{t^{-1/\alpha}x}(\tau_{\Gamma}>1),\quad x\in\Gamma,\;t>0.

Furthermore, by (
2.3
),

(2.8)

p_{t}^{T\Gamma}(Tx,Ty)=p_{t}^{\Gamma}(x,y),\quad x,y\in\Gamma,\;t>0.

The operators
P_{t}^{\Gamma}
and the kernel
p_{t}^{\Gamma}(x,y)
are the main subject of the paper. In view of (
2.8
), without loss of generality we may assume that
{\bf 1}:=(0,\ldots,0,1)\in\Gamma
.
By
[
3
, Theorem 3.2]
, there is a unique non-negative function
M_{\Gamma}
on
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
such that
M_{\Gamma}({\bf 1})=1
,
M_{\Gamma}=0
on
\Gamma^{c}
, and for every open bounded set
B\subseteq\Gamma
,

(2.9)

M_{\Gamma}(x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}M_{\Gamma}(X_{\tau_{B}}),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}.

Moreover,
M_{\Gamma}
is locally bounded on
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
and homogeneous of some order
\beta\in[0,\alpha)
, i.e.,

(2.10)

M_{\Gamma}(x)=|x|^{\beta}M_{\Gamma}(x/|x|),\quad x\in\Gamma.

We call
M_{\Gamma}
the Martin kernel of
\Gamma
with the pole at infinity.

Example 2.1
.

By
[
3
]
,
\beta=\alpha/2
if
\Gamma
is a half-space and
\beta=\alpha-1
if
\Gamma=\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
and
1<\alpha<2
.
By
[
12
]
,
\beta=(\alpha-1)/2
if
\Gamma=\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus([0,\infty)\times\{0\})
and
1<\alpha<2
.

Below we often assume that
\Gamma
is
fat
, i.e.,
\kappa\in(0,1)
exists such that for all
Q\in\overline{\Gamma}
and
r\in(0,\infty)
, there is a point
A=A_{r}(Q)\in\Gamma\cap B(Q,r)
such that
B(A,\kappa r)\subseteq\Gamma\cap B(Q,r)
, see
[
10
, Definition 1]
.
Recall that
\Gamma
is
smooth
if
d=1
or
d\geqslant 2
and
\Gamma\cap\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
is a
C^{1,1}
subset of
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.
Furthermore, a cone
\Gamma
is called
right-circular
, if
\Gamma=\{x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\setminus\{0\}:x_{d}>|x|\cos\eta\}
, with
\eta\in(0,\pi)
called the angle of the cone. Of course, every right-circular cone is smooth, and every smooth cone is fat.

By
[
10
, Theorem 1]
, the following approximate factorization holds true for fat cones:

(2.11)

p_{t}^{\Gamma}(x,y)\approx\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{\Gamma}>t)p_{t}(x,y)\mathbb{P}_{y}(\tau_{\Gamma}>t),\quad x,y\in\Gamma,\;t>0.

For
R\in(0,\infty)
, we let
\Gamma_{R}:=\Gamma\cap B_{R}
, the
truncated cone
.

3.
Doob conditioning

The Martin kernel
M_{\Gamma}
is invariant for the semigroup
P_{t}^{\Gamma}
, as follows.

Theorem 3.1
.

For all
x\in\Gamma
and
t>0
, we have
P_{t}^{\Gamma}M_{\Gamma}(x)=M_{\Gamma}(x)
.

Proof.

Fix
t>0
and
x\in\Gamma
. We have

(3.1)

P_{t}^{\Gamma}M_{\Gamma}(x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\Big[\tau_{\Gamma}>t;\,M_{\Gamma}(X_{t})\Big].

Let
R>0
and
\tau_{R}:=\tau_{\Gamma_{R}}
. By (
2.9
) and the strong Markov property,

(3.2)

M_{\Gamma}(x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}M_{\Gamma}(X_{\tau_{\Gamma_{R}}})=\mathbb{E}_{x}M_{\Gamma}\big(X_{t\wedge\tau_{R}}\big)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\Big[X_{t\wedge\tau_{R}}\in\Gamma;\,M_{\Gamma}\big(X_{t\wedge\tau_{R}}\big)\Big],

where the last equality follows from the fact that
M_{\Gamma}=0
outside
\Gamma
. We note that
\mathbb{P}_{x}-a.s.
,
\tau_{R}\to\tau_{\Gamma}
as
R\to\infty
(see, e.g.,
[
1
, proof of Proposition A.1]
). We consider two scenarios. On
\{\tau_{\Gamma}=\infty\}
, for
R
large enough, we have:
\tau_{R}>t
,
\mathds{1}_{X_{t\wedge\tau_{R}}\in\Gamma}=1=\mathds{1}_{t<\tau_{\Gamma}}
, and

M_{\Gamma}\big(X_{t\wedge\tau_{R}}\big)\mathds{1}_{X_{t\wedge\tau_{R}}\in\Gamma}=M_{\Gamma}(X_{t})=M_{\Gamma}(X_{t})\mathds{1}_{t<\tau_{\Gamma}}.

On
\{\tau_{\Gamma}<\infty\}
, for
R
large enough we have:
\tau_{R}=\tau_{\Gamma}
,
\mathds{1}_{X_{t\wedge\tau_{R}}\in\Gamma}=\mathds{1}_{t<\tau_{\Gamma}}
, and

M_{\Gamma}\big(X_{t\wedge\tau_{R}}\big)\mathds{1}_{X_{t\wedge\tau_{R}}\in\Gamma}=M_{\Gamma}(X_{t})\mathds{1}_{t<\tau_{\Gamma}},

too. In both cases, the integrand on the right-hand side of (
3.2
) converges
a.s.
to the integrand on the right-hand side of (
3.1
) as
R\to\infty
. By the local boundedness of
M_{\Gamma}
and (
2.10
),

\Big|M_{\Gamma}\big(X_{t\wedge\tau_{R}}\big)\mathds{1}_{X_{t\wedge\tau_{R}}\in\Gamma}\Big|\leqslant c\big|X_{t\wedge\tau_{R}}\big|^{\beta}\leqslant c(X_{t}^{*})^{\beta},

where

X_{t}^{*}:=\sup_{0\leqslant s\leqslant t}|X_{s}|.

Using
[
4
, Theorem 2.1]
and the fact that
\beta\in[0,\alpha
), we conclude that
\mathbb{E}_{x}(X_{t}^{*})^{\beta}<\infty
. An application of the dominated convergence theorem ends the proof.
∎

3.1.
Renormalized kernel

We define the renormalized (Doob-conditioned) kernel

(3.3)

\rho_{t}(x,y)=\frac{p_{t}^{\Gamma}(x,y)}{M_{\Gamma}(x)M_{\Gamma}(y)},\quad x,y\in\Gamma,\;t>0.

Note that
\rho
is jointly continuous. By Theorem
3.1
,

(3.4)

\int_{\Gamma}\rho_{t}(x,y)M_{\Gamma}^{2}(y)\,{\textrm{d}}y=1,\quad x\in\Gamma,\;t>0,

and by (
2.5
),

(3.5)

\int_{\Gamma}\rho_{t}(x,y)\rho_{s}(y,z)M_{\Gamma}^{2}(y)\,{\textrm{d}}y=\rho_{t+s}(x,z),\quad x,y\in\Gamma,\;s,t>0.

In other words,
\rho_{t}
is a symmetric transition probability density on
\Gamma
with respect to the measure
M^{2}_{\Gamma}(y)\,{\textrm{d}}y
. Furthermore, the following scaling property holds true: for all
x,y\in\Gamma
and all
t>0
,

(3.6)

\rho_{t}(x,y)=\frac{t^{-d/\alpha}p_{1}^{\Gamma}(t^{-1/\alpha}x,t^{-1/\alpha}y)}{t^{2\beta/\alpha}M_{\Gamma}(t^{-1/\alpha}x)M_{\Gamma}(t^{-1/\alpha}y)}=t^{-(d+2\beta)/\alpha}\rho_{1}(t^{-1/\alpha}x,t^{-1/\alpha}y).

Therefore,

(3.7)

\rho_{st}(t^{1/\alpha}x,t^{1/\alpha}y)=t^{-(d+2\beta)/\alpha}\rho_{s}(x,y),\quad x,y\in\Gamma,\;s,t>0.

By (
2.11
), for fat cones we have

(3.8)

\rho_{t}(x,y)\approx\frac{\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{\Gamma}>t)}{M_{\Gamma}(x)}p_{t}(x,y)\frac{\mathbb{P}_{y}(\tau_{\Gamma}>t)}{M_{\Gamma}(y)},\quad x,y\in\Gamma,\,t>0.

The boundary behavior of
\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{\Gamma}>t)/M_{\Gamma}(x)
is important due to (
3.8
), but it is rather elusive.
The next lemma strengthens the upper bound from
[
3
, Lemma 4.2]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

There exists a constant
c
depending only on
\alpha
and
\Gamma
, such that

(3.9)

\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{\Gamma}>t)\leqslant c\Big(t^{-\beta/\alpha}+t^{-1}|x|^{\alpha-\beta}\Big)M_{\Gamma}(x),\quad t>0,\,x\in\Gamma.

Remark 3.3
.

(1) For
t=1
, (
3.9
) reads as follows,

(3.10)

\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{\Gamma}>1)\leqslant c(1+|x|^{\alpha-\beta})M_{\Gamma}(x),\quad x\in\Gamma.

(2) The estimate (
3.9
) applies to arbitrary cones and arguments
t,x
,
however, it is not optimal. For example, for the right-circular cones, we can confront (
3.8
) with

M_{\Gamma}(x)\approx\delta_{\Gamma}(x)^{\alpha/2}|x|^{\beta-\alpha/2},\quad x\in\Gamma,

and

\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{\Gamma}>1)\approx\big(1\wedge\delta_{\Gamma}(x)\big)^{\alpha/2}\big(1\wedge|x|\big)^{\beta-\alpha/2},\quad x\in\Gamma,

as provided by
[
29
, Lemma 3.3]
and
[
10
, Example 7]
.

(3) For the right-circular cones, the ratio

\frac{\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{\Gamma}>1)}{M_{\Gamma}(x)}\approx\frac{\big(1+\delta_{\Gamma}(x))^{-\alpha/2}}{(1+|x|)^{\beta-\alpha/2}},\quad x\in\Gamma,

is bounded if and only if
\beta\geqslant\alpha/2
.

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

We slightly modify the proof of
[
3
, Lemma 4.2]
. First, suppose that
t=1
. The case
x\in\Gamma_{1}
in (
3.10
) is resolved by
[
3
, Lemma 4.2]
, so we assume that
x\in\Gamma\setminus\Gamma_{1}
. For every
z\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\setminus\{0\}
we define its projection on the unit sphere
\tilde{z}:=z/|z|
. By (
2.7
),

\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{\Gamma}>1)=\mathbb{P}_{\tilde{x}}(\tau_{\Gamma}>|x|^{-\alpha}).

Then we have

\mathbb{P}_{\tilde{x}}(\tau_{\Gamma}>|x|^{-\alpha})\leqslant\mathbb{P}_{\tilde{x}}(\tau_{\Gamma_{2}}>|x|^{-\alpha})+\mathbb{P}_{\tilde{x}}(\tau_{\Gamma_{2}}<\tau_{\Gamma}).

By the boundary Harnack principle (BHP), see Song and Wu
[
34
, Theorem 3.1]
, and the homogeneity of
M_{\Gamma}
(
2.10
),

(3.11)

\mathbb{P}_{\tilde{x}}(\tau_{\Gamma_{2}}<\tau_{\Gamma})\leqslant\mathbb{P}_{{\bf 1}}(\tau_{\Gamma_{2}}<\tau_{\Gamma})M_{\Gamma}(\tilde{x})=c_{1}|x|^{-\beta}M_{\Gamma}(x).

We let

c_{2}=\inf_{y\in\Gamma_{2}}\int_{\Gamma\setminus\Gamma_{2}}\nu(y-z)\,{\textrm{d}}z.

Clearly,
c_{2}>0
. We recall the Ikeda-Watanabe formula:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}[\tau_{D}\in I,\;Y_{\tau_{D}-}\in A,\;Y_{\tau_{D}}\in B]=\int_{I}\int_{A}\int_{B}\,p_{s}^{D}(x,v)\nu(v,z)\,{\textrm{d}}z\,{\textrm{d}}v\,{\textrm{d}}s,

where
x\in D
,
I\subseteq[0,\infty)
,
A\subseteq D
and
B\subseteq D^{c}
, see, e.g., Bogdan et al.
[
16
, Section 4.2]
. By Markov inequality and BHP,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\tilde{x}}\big(\tau_{\Gamma_{2}}>|x|^{-\alpha}\big)

\displaystyle\leqslant|x|^{\alpha}\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{x}}\tau_{\Gamma_{2}}=|x|^{\alpha}\int_{\Gamma_{2}}G_{\Gamma_{2}}(\tilde{x},y)\,{\textrm{d}}y

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{2}^{-1}|x|^{\alpha}\int_{\Gamma\setminus\Gamma_{2}}\int_{\Gamma_{2}}G_{\Gamma_{2}}(\tilde{x},y)\nu(y-z)\,{\textrm{d}}y\,{\textrm{d}}z

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{2}^{-1}|x|^{\alpha}\mathbb{P}_{\tilde{x}}\big(X_{\tau_{\Gamma_{2}}}\in\Gamma\big)

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{1}c_{2}^{-1}|x|^{\alpha}\mathbb{P}_{{\bf 1}}\big(X_{\tau_{\Gamma_{2}}}\in\Gamma\big)M_{\Gamma}(\tilde{x})

\displaystyle=c_{1}c_{2}^{-1}c|x|^{\alpha-\beta}M_{\Gamma}(x).

By (
3.11
), we get (
3.10
) when
x\in\Gamma\setminus\Gamma_{1}
. For arbitrary
t>0
, we use (
2.7
) and (
3.10
):

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{\Gamma}>t)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{t^{-1/\alpha}x}(\tau_{\Gamma}>1)

\displaystyle\leqslant c\left(1+\left(t^{-1/\alpha}|x|\right)^{\alpha-\beta}\right)M_{\Gamma}(t^{-1/\alpha}x)

\displaystyle=c\left(t^{-\beta/\alpha}+t^{-1}|x|^{\alpha-\beta}\right)M_{\Gamma}(x).

∎

By the proof of
[
3
, Lemma 4.2]
, for every
R\in(0,\infty)
there exists a constant
c
, depending only on
\alpha
,
\Gamma
and
R
, such that

c^{-1}M_{\Gamma}(x)t^{-\beta/\alpha}\leqslant\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{\Gamma}>t)\leqslant cM_{\Gamma}(x)t^{-\beta/\alpha},\quad x\in\Gamma_{Rt^{1/\alpha}},\;t>0.

In particular, for fat cones, in view of (
2.4
) and (
3.8
),

(3.12)

\rho_{1}(x,y)\approx(1+|y|)^{-d-\alpha}\frac{\mathbb{P}_{y}(\tau_{\Gamma}>1)}{M_{\Gamma}(y)},\quad x\in\Gamma_{R},\;y\in\Gamma,

with comparability constant depending only on
\alpha
,
\Gamma
and
R
.
Using Lemma
3.2
we also conclude that for every
R\geqslant 1
there is a constant
c
depending only on
R
,
\alpha
and
\Gamma
, such that

(3.13)

\rho_{1}(x,y)\leqslant c(1+|y|)^{-d-\beta},\quad x\in\Gamma_{R},\;y\in\Gamma.

3.2.
Ornstein-Uhlenbeck kernel

Encouraged by
[
13
]
, we let

(3.14)

\ell_{t}(x,y):=\rho_{1-e^{-t}}(e^{-t/\alpha}x,y),\quad x,y\in\Gamma,\;t>0,

and, by (
3.7
), we get the Chapman-Kolmogorov property for
\ell_{t}
:

\int_{\Gamma}\ell_{t}(x,y)\ell_{s}(y,z)M_{\Gamma}^{2}(y)\,{\textrm{d}}y=\ell_{t+s}(x,z),\quad x,z\in\Gamma,\;s,t>0.

By (
3.4
),

\int_{\Gamma}\ell_{t}(x,y)M_{\Gamma}^{2}(y)\,{\textrm{d}}y=1,\quad x\in\Gamma,\;t>0.

Thus,
\ell_{t}
is a transition probability density on
\Gamma
with respect to
M_{\Gamma}^{2}(y)\,{\textrm{d}}y
.
We define the corresponding Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup:

L_{t}f(y)=\int_{\Gamma}\ell_{t}(x,y)f(x)M_{\Gamma}^{2}(x)\,{\textrm{d}}x,\quad x\in\Gamma,t>0.

We easily see that the operators are bounded on
L^{1}(M^{2}_{\Gamma}(y)\,{\textrm{d}}y)
. In fact, they preserve densities, i.e., functions
f\geqslant 0
such that
\int_{\Gamma}f(x)M^{2}_{\Gamma}(x)\,{\textrm{d}}x=1
.

Before we immerse into details, let us note that the relations (
3.12
) and (
3.13
) will be crucial in what follows. Both of them rely on the factorization of the Dirichlet heat kernel (
2.11
), which is valid for fat sets. So, below in this section we assume (sometimes tacitly) that
\Gamma
is a fat cone.

Theorem 3.4
.

Assume
\Gamma
is a fat cone. Then there is a unique stationary density
\varphi
for the operators
L_{t}
,
t>0
.

Proof.

Fix
t>0
and consider the family
F
of non-negative functions on
\Gamma
that have the form

f(y)=\int_{\Gamma_{1}}\rho_{t}(x,y)\,\mu({\textrm{d}}x),\quad y\in\Gamma,

for some sub-probability measure
\mu
concentrated on
\Gamma_{1}
. By (
3.4
),
F\subseteq L^{1}(M_{\Gamma}^{2}(y))
. By the scaling (
3.7
) and the same reasoning as in the proof of
[
13
, Theorem 3.2]
,
L_{t}F\subseteq F
. Since
L_{t}
is continuous, we also have
L_{t}\overline{F}\subseteq\overline{F}
, where
\overline{F}
is the closure of
F
in the norm topology of
L^{1}(\Gamma,M^{2}_{\Gamma}(y)\,{\textrm{d}}y)
. Next, we observe that
F
is convex, therefore by
[
18
, Theorem 3.7]
,
\overline{F}
is equal to the closure of
F
in the weak topology. In view of (
3.12
),

(3.15)

f(y)\lesssim(1+|y|)^{-d-\alpha}\frac{\mathbb{P}_{y}(\tau_{\Gamma}>1)}{M_{\Gamma}(y)},\quad y\in\Gamma,

uniformly for
f\in F
. Moreover, (
3.4
) and (
3.12
) show that the right-hand side of (
3.15
) is integrable with respect to
M_{\Gamma}^{2}(y)\,{\textrm{d}}y
. Therefore, the family
F
is uniformly integrable with respect to
M^{2}_{\Gamma}(y)\,{\textrm{d}}y
. By
[
8
, Theorem 4.7.20]
,
F
is weakly pre-compact in
L^{1}(M^{2}_{\Gamma}(y))
, so
\overline{F}
is weakly compact.
Furthermore, we invoke
[
18
, Theorem 3.10]
to conclude that
L_{t}
is weakly continuous. By the Schauder-Tychonoff fixed point theorem
[
32
, Theorem 5.28]
,
there is a density
\varphi\in\overline{F}
satisfying
L_{t}\varphi=\varphi
. It is unique by the strict positivity of the kernel
\ell_{t}
, and the same for every
t>0
, see the proof of
[
13
, Theorem 3.2]
.
∎

Let us note that by Theorem
3.4
and
[
26
, Theorem 1 and Remark 2]
, the following stability result for kernels
l_{t}
in
L^{1}(M_{\Gamma}^{2}(y)\,{\textrm{d}}y)
holds true for every
x\in\Gamma
:

(3.16)

\int_{\Gamma}\big|\ell_{t}(x,y)-\varphi(y)\big|M_{\Gamma}^{2}(y)\,{\textrm{d}}y\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad t\to\infty.

We claim that the convergence in (
3.16
) is in fact uniform for
x
in any bounded subset
A\subseteq\Gamma
. Indeed, let
x,x_{0}\in A
. In view of (
3.14
) and (
3.8
) we may write

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\big|\ell_{1+t}(x,y)-\varphi(y)\big|M_{\Gamma}^{2}(y)\,{\textrm{d}}y

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma}\bigg|\int_{\Gamma}\ell_{1}(x,z)\big(\ell_{t}(z,y)-\varphi(y)\big)M_{\Gamma}^{2}(z)\,{\textrm{d}}z\bigg|M_{\Gamma}^{2}(y)\,{\textrm{d}}y

(3.17)

\displaystyle\leqslant c\int_{\Gamma}\ell_{1}(x_{0},z)\int_{\Gamma}\big|\ell_{t}(z,y)-\varphi(y)\big|M_{\Gamma}^{2}(y)\,{\textrm{d}}y\,M_{\Gamma}^{2}(z)\,{\textrm{d}}z.

By (
3.16
), for every
z\in\Gamma
,

I_{t}(z):=\int_{\Gamma}\big|\ell_{t}(z,y)-\varphi(y)\big|M_{\Gamma}^{2}(y)\,{\textrm{d}}y\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad t\to\infty.

Moreover,
I_{t}(z)\leqslant\int_{\Gamma}\big(\ell_{t}(z,y)+\varphi(y)\big)M_{\Gamma}^{2}(y)\,{\textrm{d}}y=2
. Since

\int_{\Gamma}2\ell_{1}(x_{0},z)M_{\Gamma}^{2}(z)\,{\textrm{d}}z=2<\infty,

by the dominated convergence theorem the iterated integral in (
3.17
) tends to
0
as
t\to\infty
, so the convergence in (
3.16
) is uniform for all
x\in A
, as claimed. By rewriting (
3.16
) in terms of
\rho
, we get that, uniformly for
x\in A
,

(3.18)

\int_{\Gamma}\Big|\rho_{1-e^{-t}}\big(e^{-t/\alpha}x,y\big)-\varphi(y)\Big|M_{\Gamma}^{2}(y)\,{\textrm{d}}y\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad t\to\infty.

This leads to the following
spacial asymptotics for
\rho_{1}
.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
\Gamma
be a fat cone. If
\Gamma\ni x\to 0
then
\int_{\Gamma}\big|\rho_{1}(x,y)-\varphi(y)\big|M_{\Gamma}^{2}(y)\,{\textrm{d}}y\to 0
.

Proof.

By the scalings (
3.6
) and (
3.7
),

\rho_{1-e^{-t}}\big(e^{-t/\alpha}x,y\big)=\big(1-e^{-t}\big)^{-(d+2\beta)/\alpha}\rho_{1}\Big(\big(e^{t}-1\big)^{-1/\alpha}x,\big(1-e^{-t}\big)^{-1/\alpha}y\Big),

thus, in view of (
3.18
),

(3.19)

\int_{\Gamma}\bigg|\rho_{1}\Big(\big(e^{t}-1\big)^{-1/\alpha}x,\big(1-e^{-t}\big)^{-1/\alpha}y\Big)-\varphi(y)\bigg|M_{\Gamma}^{2}(y)\,{\textrm{d}}y\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad t\to\infty.

By the continuity of dilations in
L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
,

\int_{\Gamma}\bigg|\varphi\Big(\big(1-e^{-t}\big)^{1/\alpha}y\Big)M_{\Gamma}^{2}(y)-\varphi(y)M_{\Gamma}^{2}(y)\bigg|\,{\textrm{d}}y\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad t\to\infty.

Thus, by a change of variables in (
3.19
) and the triangle inequality, we conclude that

\int_{\Gamma}\bigg|\rho_{1}\Big(\big(e^{t}-1\big)^{-1/\alpha}z,y\Big)-\varphi(y)\bigg|M_{\Gamma}^{2}(y)\,{\textrm{d}}y\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad t\to\infty

uniformly for all
z\in A
. To end the proof, we take
A=B_{1}
and
x=\big(e^{t}-1\big)^{-1/\alpha}z
, where
t=\ln\big(1+|x|^{-\alpha}\big)
and
z=x/|x|\in A
. .
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

After a modification on set of Lebesgue measure
0
,
\varphi
is continuous on
\Gamma
and

\varphi(y)\approx(1+|y|)^{-d-\alpha}\frac{\mathbb{P}_{y}(\tau_{\Gamma}>1)}{M_{\Gamma}(y)},\quad y\in\Gamma.

Proof.

By Corollary
3.5
and (
3.12
),

\varphi(y)\approx(1+|y|)^{-d-\alpha}\frac{\mathbb{P}_{y}(\tau_{\Gamma}>1)}{M_{\Gamma}(y)}

on
\Gamma
less a set of Lebesgue measure zero. Theorem
3.4
entails that
\varphi=L_{1}\varphi

a.e.
, so it suffices to verify that
L_{1}\varphi
is continuous on
\Gamma
. To this end we note that
\ell_{1}(x,y)
is continuous in
x,y\in\Gamma
. Next, by (
3.14
) and (
3.8
),

\displaystyle\ell_{1}(x,y)\approx\frac{\mathbb{P}_{e^{-1/\alpha}x}\big(\tau_{\Gamma}>1-e^{-1}\big)}{M_{\Gamma}\big(e^{-1/\alpha}x\big)}p_{1-e^{-1}}\big(e^{-1/\alpha}x,y\big)\frac{\mathbb{P}_{y}\big(\tau_{\Gamma}>1-e^{-1}\big)}{M_{\Gamma}(y)},\quad x,y\in\Gamma.

Let
R>1
. By (
2.4
) and (
3.9
),

[
10
, Remark 3]
and (
2.7
), and the homogeneity (
2.10
) of
M_{\Gamma}
,

\ell_{1}(x,y)\lesssim(1+|x|)^{-d-\alpha}\frac{\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{\Gamma}>1)}{M_{\Gamma}(x)},\quad x\in\Gamma,y\in\Gamma_{R}.

By the dominated convergence theorem,
L_{1}\varphi
is continuous on
\Gamma_{R}
.
∎

In what follows,
\varphi
denotes the continuous modification from Lemma
3.6
.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
\Gamma
be a fat cone. For every
t>0
, uniformly in
y\in\Gamma
we have

\rho_{t}(0,y):=\lim_{\Gamma\ni x\to 0}\rho_{t}(x,y)=t^{-(d+2\beta)/\alpha}\varphi\big(t^{-1/\alpha}y\big).

Proof.

If
\beta=0
then
\rho_{t}(x,y)=p_{t}(x,y)
and the claim is simply the continuity property of the heat kernel
p_{t}
. Thus, we assume that
\beta>0
.

We only prove the claim for
t=1
; the extension to arbitrary
t
is a consequence of the scaling (
3.6
). By (
3.7
) and the Chapman-Kolmogorov property, for
x,y\in\Gamma
,

\displaystyle\rho_{1}(x,y)

\displaystyle=2^{(d+2\beta)/\alpha}\rho_{2}\big(2^{1/\alpha}x,2^{1/\alpha}y\big)

\displaystyle=2^{(d+2\beta)/\alpha}\int_{\Gamma}\rho_{1}\big(2^{1/\alpha}x,z\big)\rho_{1}\big(z,2^{1/\alpha}y\big)M_{\Gamma}^{2}(z)\,{\textrm{d}}z.

We will prove that, uniformly in
y\in\Gamma
,

(3.20)

\int_{\Gamma}\rho_{1}\big(2^{1/\alpha}x,z\big)\rho_{1}\big(z,2^{1/\alpha}y\big)M_{\Gamma}^{2}(z)\,{\textrm{d}}z\to\int_{\Gamma}\varphi(z)\rho_{1}\big(z,2^{1/\alpha}y\big)M_{\Gamma}^{2}(z)\,{\textrm{d}}z

as
\Gamma\ni x\to 0
. To this end we first claim that there is
c\in(0,\infty)
dependent only on
\alpha
and
\Gamma
, such that for all
x\in\Gamma_{1}
and
y\in\Gamma
,

(3.21)

\int_{\Gamma}\big|\rho_{1}\big(2^{1/\alpha}x,z\big)-\varphi(z)\big|\rho_{1}\big(z,2^{1/\alpha}y\big)M_{\Gamma}^{2}(z)\,{\textrm{d}}z\leqslant c(1+|y|)^{-\beta}.

Indeed, denote
\tilde{y}=2^{1/\alpha}y
. By (
3.8
), Lemma
3.6
and (
3.10
), there is
c>0
such that for all
z,y\in\Gamma
and
x\in\Gamma_{1}
,

\big|\rho_{1}\big(2^{1/\alpha}x,z\big)-\varphi(z)\big|\rho_{1}\big(z,\widetilde{y}\big)M_{\Gamma}^{2}(z)\lesssim(1+|z|)^{-d-\alpha}(1+|z-\widetilde{y}|)^{-d-\alpha}(1+|y|)^{\alpha-\beta}.

We split the integral in (
3.21
) into two integrals. For
z\in A:=B(\widetilde{y},|\widetilde{y}|/2)
we use the fact that
|z|\approx|\widetilde{y}|\approx|y|
and
1+|z-\widetilde{y}|\geqslant 1
, therefore

(3.22)

\displaystyle\int_{A}\big|\rho_{1}\big(2^{1/\alpha}x,z\big)-\varphi(z)\big|\rho_{1}\big(z,\widetilde{y}\big)M_{\Gamma}^{2}(z)\,{\textrm{d}}z

\displaystyle\lesssim|y|^{d}(1+|y|)^{-d-\beta}\leqslant(1+|y|)^{-\beta}.

For
z\in\Gamma\setminus A
we simply have
1+|z|\geqslant 1
, thus,

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma\setminus A}\big|\rho_{1}\big(2^{1/\alpha}x,z\big)-\varphi(z)\big|\rho_{1}\big(z,\widetilde{y}\big)M_{\Gamma}^{2}(z)\,{\textrm{d}}z

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+|y|)^{\alpha-\beta}\int_{\Gamma\setminus A}(1+|z-\widetilde{y}|)^{-d-\alpha}\,{\textrm{d}}z

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+|y|)^{\alpha-\beta}\int_{|\widetilde{y}|/2}^{\infty}(1+r)^{-1-\alpha}\,{\textrm{d}}r

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+|y|)^{-\beta}.

Combining it with (
3.22
), we arrive at (
3.21
), as claimed.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. In view of (
3.21
) and the fact that
\beta>0
, there is
R\in(0,\infty)
depending only on
\alpha
,
\beta
,
\Gamma
and
\varepsilon
such that

(3.23)

\int_{\Gamma}\big|\rho_{1}\big(2^{1/\alpha}x,z\big)-\varphi(z)\big|\rho_{1}\big(z,2^{1/\alpha}y\big)M_{\Gamma}^{2}(z)\,{\textrm{d}}z<\varepsilon,

provided that
y\in\Gamma\setminus\Gamma_{R}
. For
y\in\Gamma_{R}
, by (
3.13
) we get

\int_{\Gamma}\big|\rho_{1}\big(2^{1/\alpha}x,z\big)-\varphi(z)\big|\rho_{1}\big(z,2^{1/\alpha}y\big)M_{\Gamma}^{2}(z)\,{\textrm{d}}z\lesssim\int_{\Gamma}\big|\rho_{1}\big(2^{1/\alpha}x,z\big)-\varphi(z)\big|M_{\Gamma}^{2}(z)\,{\textrm{d}}z,

with the implied constant dependent only on
\alpha
,
\beta
,
\Gamma
and
R
, but not otherwise dependent of
y
. Thus, by Corollary
3.5
,

(3.24)

\int_{\Gamma}\big|\rho_{1}\big(2^{1/\alpha}x,z\big)-\varphi(z)\big|\rho_{1}\big(z,2^{1/\alpha}y\big)M_{\Gamma}^{2}(z)\,{\textrm{d}}z<\varepsilon

for all
y\in\Gamma_{R}
and
x\in\Gamma_{1}
small enough. Putting (
3.24
) together with (
3.23
) we arrive at (
3.20
). Using the scaling property (
3.7
) and Theorem
3.4
,

\displaystyle\lim_{\Gamma\ni x\to 0}\rho_{1}(x,y)

\displaystyle=2^{(d+2\beta)/\alpha}\int_{\Gamma}\varphi(z)\rho_{1}\big(z,2^{1/\alpha}y\big)M_{\Gamma}^{2}(z)\,{\textrm{d}}z

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma}\varphi(z)\rho_{1/2}\big(2^{-1/\alpha}z,y\big)M_{\Gamma}^{2}(z)\,{\textrm{d}}z=L_{\ln 2}\varphi(y)=\varphi(y).

The proof is complete.
∎

Note that by the symmetry of
\rho_{t}
, for
x\in\Gamma
,

\rho_{t}(x,0):=\lim_{\Gamma\ni y\to 0}\rho_{t}(x,y)=\rho_{t}(0,x)=t^{-(d+2\beta)/\alpha}\varphi\big(t^{-1/\alpha}x\big).

Recall also that by (

) and (
3.4
),

\rho_{1}(x,y)\approx(1+|y|)^{-d-\alpha}\frac{\mathbb{P}_{y}(\tau_{\Gamma}>1)}{M_{\Gamma}(y)}\in L^{1}(M_{\Gamma}^{2}(y)\,{\textrm{d}}y).

Thus, by Theorem
3.7
and the dominated convergence theorem,

(3.25)

\int_{\Gamma}\varphi(x)M_{\Gamma}^{2}(x)\,\,{\textrm{d}}x=1.

Let us summarize the results of this section in one statement.

Theorem 3.8
.

Assume
\Gamma
is a fat cone. Then the function
\rho
has a continuous extension to
(0,\infty)\times(\Gamma\cup\{0\})\times(\Gamma\cup\{0\})
and

(3.26)

\rho_{t}(0,y):=\lim_{\Gamma\ni x\to 0}\rho_{t}(x,y)\in(0,\infty),\quad t>0,\,y\in\Gamma,

satisfies

(3.27)

\rho_{t}(0,y)=t^{-(d+2\beta)/\alpha}\rho_{1}(0,t^{-1/\alpha}y),\quad t>0,\,y\in\Gamma,

and

(3.28)

\int_{\Gamma}\rho_{t}(0,y)\rho_{s}(y,z)M_{\Gamma}^{2}(y)\,{\textrm{d}}y=\rho_{t+s}(0,z),\quad s,t>0,\,z\in\Gamma.

Proof.

The existence of the limit (
3.26
) and the scaling property (
3.27
) are proved in Theorem
3.7
, see also Lemma
3.6
. For the proof of (
3.28
) we employ (
3.5
) to write

\displaystyle\rho_{t+s}(0,z)=\lim_{\Gamma\ni y\to 0}\rho_{t+s}(y,z)=\lim_{\Gamma\ni y\to 0}\int_{\Gamma}\rho_{t}(y,w)\rho_{s}(w,z)M_{\Gamma}^{2}(w)\,{\textrm{d}}w,

and use (
3.4
), (
3.6
), (
3.13
), and the dominated convergence theorem.

Thus, it remains to prove the continuity of
\rho
on
(0,\infty)\times(\Gamma\cup\{0\})\times(\Gamma\cup\{0\})
. By symmetry and the Chapman-Kolmogorov property (
3.5
) of
\rho_{1}
,

(3.29)

\rho_{1}(x,y)=\int_{\Gamma}\rho_{1/2}(x,z)\rho_{1/2}(y,z)M_{\Gamma}^{2}(z)\,{\textrm{d}}z,\quad x,y\in\Gamma.

3.3.
Yaglom limit

The above results quickly lead to calculation of the
Yaglom limit
for the stable process (conditioned to stay in a cone).
Note that our proof is different from that in
[
15
]
. We also cover more general cones, e.g.,
\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
and
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus([0,\infty)\times\{0\})
.
First, we obtain the following extension of
[
15
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
\Gamma
be a fat cone. For every
t>0
,

\lim_{\Gamma\ni x\to 0}\frac{\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{\Gamma}>t)}{M_{\Gamma}(x)}=C_{1}t^{-\beta/\alpha}\quad\mbox{where}\quad C_{1}=\int_{\Gamma}\varphi(z)M_{\Gamma}(z)\,{\textrm{d}}z\in(0,\infty).

Proof.

It is enough to prove the claim for
t=1
; the general case follows by the scalings (
2.7
) and (
2.10
). We have

\frac{\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{\Gamma}>1)}{M_{\Gamma}(x)}=\int_{\Gamma}\frac{p_{1}^{\Gamma}(x,y)}{M_{\Gamma}(x)}\,{\textrm{d}}y=\int_{\Gamma}\rho_{1}(x,y)M_{\Gamma}(y)\,{\textrm{d}}y,\quad x\in\Gamma.

We use (
3.12
), the dominated convergence theorem, and Theorem
3.7
to get the conclusion.
∎

The first identity below is the
Yaglom limit.

Theorem 3.11
.

Assume
\Gamma
is a fat cone and
B
is a bounded subset of
\Gamma
. Then,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(t^{-1/\alpha}X_{t}\in A\;\middle|\;\tau_{\Gamma}>t\right)=\mu(A),\quad A\subseteq\Gamma,

uniformly in
x\in B
,
where

\mu(A):=\frac{1}{C_{1}}\int_{A}\varphi(y)M_{\Gamma}(y)\,{\textrm{d}}y,\quad A\subseteq\Gamma.

Proof.

By (
2.6
) and the scaling property (
2.7
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(t^{-1/\alpha}X_{t}\in A\;\middle|\;\tau_{\Gamma}>t\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\tau_{\Gamma}>t,t^{-1/\alpha}X_{t}\in A\right)}{\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{\Gamma}>t)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{P}_{t^{-1/\alpha}x}\left(\tau_{\Gamma}>1,X_{1}\in A\right)}{\mathbb{P}_{t^{-1/\alpha}x}(\tau_{\Gamma}>1)}

\displaystyle=\int_{A}\frac{p_{1}^{\Gamma}\big(t^{-1/\alpha}x,y\big)}{M_{\Gamma}\big(t^{-1/\alpha}x\big)}\,{\textrm{d}}y\cdot\frac{M_{\Gamma}(t^{-1/\alpha}x)}{\mathbb{P}_{t^{-1/\alpha}x}(\tau_{\Gamma}>1)}.

The claim follows by Corollary
3.10
, (
3.12
), and the dominated convergence theorem.
∎

Theorem 3.12
.

If
\Gamma
is a fat cone and
\gamma
is a probability measure on
\Gamma
with
\int_{\Gamma}(1+|y|)^{\alpha}\,\gamma({\rm d}y)<\infty
, then

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\gamma}\left(t^{-1/\alpha}X_{t}\in A\;\middle|\;\tau_{\Gamma}>t\right)=\mu(A),\quad A\subseteq\Gamma.

Proof.

Let
t\geqslant 1
. In view of
[
24
, Lemma 8.7]
, we may write

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\gamma}\left(t^{-1/\alpha}X_{t}\in A\;\middle|\;\tau_{\Gamma}>t\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{P}_{\gamma}\left(t^{-1/\alpha}X_{t}\in A,\tau_{\Gamma}>t\right)}{\mathbb{P}_{\gamma}\left(\tau_{\Gamma}>t\right)}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma}\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(t^{-1/\alpha}X_{t}\in A\;\middle|\;\tau_{\Gamma}>t\right)\frac{\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\tau_{\Gamma}>t\right)}{\mathbb{P}_{\gamma}\left(\tau_{\Gamma}>t\right)}\,\gamma({\textrm{d}}x).

We first prove that for all
x\in\Gamma
,

(3.31)

\frac{\mathbb{P}_{\gamma}\left(\tau_{\Gamma}>t\right)}{\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\tau_{\Gamma}>t\right)}:=\int_{\Gamma}\frac{\mathbb{P}_{y}\left(\tau_{\Gamma}>t\right)}{\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\tau_{\Gamma}>t\right)}\,\gamma(\,{\textrm{d}}y)\to\int_{\Gamma}\frac{M_{\Gamma}(y)}{M_{\Gamma}(x)}\,\gamma({\textrm{d}}y),

as
t\to\infty
.
Indeed, fix
x\in\Gamma
. First we note that by local boundedness of
M_{\Gamma}
and (
2.10
),

\int_{\Gamma}M_{\Gamma}(y)\,\gamma({\rm d}y)\leqslant c\int_{\Gamma}(1+|y|)^{\beta}\,\gamma({\rm d}y)<\infty,

so the right-hand side of (
3.31
) is finite. Next, by Corollary
3.10
, (
2.7
), and (
2.10
),

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}_{y}\left(\tau_{\Gamma}>t\right)}{\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\tau_{\Gamma}>t\right)}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}_{t^{-1/\alpha}y}\left(\tau_{\Gamma}>1\right)M_{\Gamma}(t^{-1/\alpha}x)}{\mathbb{P}_{t^{-1/\alpha}x}\left(\tau_{\Gamma}>1\right)M_{\Gamma}(t^{-1/\alpha}y)}\frac{M_{\Gamma}(y)}{M_{\Gamma}(x)}=\frac{M_{\Gamma}(y)}{M_{\Gamma}(x)},\quad x,y\in\Gamma.

Moreover, since
x
is fixed, we may assume that
t\geqslant 1\vee|x|^{\alpha}
. Thus, by
[
3
, Lemma 4.2]
, Lemma
3.2
, the local boundedness of
M_{\Gamma}
and (
2.10
),

\frac{\mathbb{P}_{y}\left(\tau_{\Gamma}>t\right)}{\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\tau_{\Gamma}>t\right)}\leqslant c\frac{\big(t^{-\beta/\alpha}+t^{-1}|y|^{\alpha-\beta}\big)M_{\Gamma}(y)}{t^{-\beta/\alpha}M_{\Gamma}(x)}\leqslant c\frac{\big(1+|y|^{\alpha-\beta}\big)M_{\Gamma}(y)}{M_{\Gamma}(x)}\leqslant c\frac{(1+|y|)^{\alpha}}{M_{\Gamma}(x)}.

Thus, the dominated convergence theorem yields (
3.31
), as desired.

Next, we consider a family
\mathcal{F}_{1}
of functions
f_{t}
of the form

f_{t}(x)=\frac{\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{\Gamma}>t)}{\mathbb{P}_{\gamma}(\tau_{\Gamma}>t)},\quad x\in\Gamma,\,t\geqslant 1.

Denote

f(x)=\frac{M_{\Gamma}(x)}{\int_{\Gamma}M_{\Gamma}(y)\,\gamma({\rm d}y)},\quad x\in\Gamma.

By virtue of (
3.31
),
f_{t}\to f
everywhere in
\Gamma
as
t\to\infty
. Thus,
f_{t}\to f
in measure
\gamma
as
t\to\infty
, see
[
33
, Definition 22.2]
. Moreover, we have

\int_{\Gamma}f(x)\,\gamma({\rm d}x)=1=\lim_{t\to\infty}1=\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{\Gamma}f_{t}(x)\,\gamma({\rm d}x).

Therefore, by Vitali’s theorem
[
33
, Theorem 22.7]
, the family
\mathcal{F}_{1}
is uniformly integrable. If we now consider the family
\mathcal{F}_{2}
of functions
\tilde{f}_{t}
of the form

\tilde{f}_{t}(x)=\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(t^{-1/\alpha}X_{t}\in A\;\middle|\;\tau_{\Gamma}>t\right)f_{t}(x),\quad x\in\Gamma,\,t\geqslant 1,

then a trivial bound
\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(t^{-1/\alpha}X_{t}\in A\;\middle|\;\tau_{\Gamma}>t\right)\leqslant 1
shows that
\mathcal{F}_{2}
is uniformly integrable as well (see, e.g.,
[
33
, Theorem 22.9]
). By Theorem
3.11
, (
3.31
) and
[
33
, Theorem 22.7]
,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{\Gamma}\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(t^{-1/\alpha}X_{t}\in A\;\middle|\;\tau_{\Gamma}>t\right)\frac{\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\tau_{\Gamma}>t\right)}{\mathbb{P}_{\gamma}\left(\tau_{\Gamma}>t\right)}\,\gamma({\textrm{d}}x)=\int_{\Gamma}\mu(A)\frac{M_{\Gamma}(x)}{\int_{\Gamma}M_{\Gamma}(y)\,\gamma({\rm d}y)}\,\gamma({\rm d}x)=\mu(A).

The proof is complete.
∎

4.
Asymptotic behavior for the killed semigroup

This section is devoted to examples and applications in Functional Analysis and Partial Differential Equations. Note that in Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
we do not assume that
\Gamma
is fat.

Lemma 4.1
.

\{P_{t}^{\Gamma}\}_{t>0}

is a strongly continuous contraction semigroup on
L^{1}(M_{\Gamma})
and

(4.1)

\int_{\Gamma}P_{t}^{\Gamma}f(x)M_{\Gamma}(x)\,{\textrm{d}}x=\int_{\Gamma}f(x)M_{\Gamma}(x)\,{\textrm{d}}x,\quad t>0,\quad f\in L^{1}(M_{\Gamma}).

Proof.

Let
f\geqslant 0
. By the Fubini-Tonelli theorem, the symmetry of
p_{t}^{\Gamma}
and Theorem
3.1
,

(4.2)

\int_{\Gamma}P_{t}^{\Gamma}f(x)M_{\Gamma}(x)\,{\textrm{d}}x=\int_{\Gamma}\int_{\Gamma}p_{t}^{\Gamma}(x,y)f(y)M_{\Gamma}(y)\,{\textrm{d}}y\,{\textrm{d}}x=\int_{\Gamma}f(y)M_{\Gamma}(y)\,{\textrm{d}}y.

Since
|P_{t}^{\Gamma}f|\leqslant P_{t}^{\Gamma}|f|
, the contractivity follows. Furthermore, for arbitrary
f\in L^{1}(M_{\Gamma})
we write
f=f_{+}-f_{-}
and use (
4.2
) to prove (
4.1
). The semigroup property follows from (
2.5
).

To prove the strong continuity, we fix
f\in L^{1}(M_{\Gamma})
and let
G:=fM_{\Gamma}\in L^{1}(\Gamma)
. There is a sequence
g_{n}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Gamma)
such that
\|g_{n}-G\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma)}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. For
f_{n}:=g_{n}/M_{\Gamma}
we get
f_{n}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Gamma)
and
\|f_{n}-f\|_{L^{1}(M_{\Gamma})}=\|g_{n}-G\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma)}\to 0
. By the first part of the proof,

\displaystyle\|P_{t}^{\Gamma}f-f\|_{L^{1}(M_{\Gamma})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|P_{t}^{\Gamma}f-P_{t}^{\Gamma}f_{n}\|_{L^{1}(M_{\Gamma})}+\|P_{t}^{\Gamma}f_{n}-f_{n}\|_{L^{1}(M_{\Gamma})}+\|f_{n}-f\|_{L^{1}(M_{\Gamma})}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\|f_{n}-f\|_{L^{1}(M_{\Gamma})}+\|P_{t}^{\Gamma}f_{n}-f_{n}\|_{L^{1}(M_{\Gamma})}.

It remains to prove that
\|P_{t}^{\Gamma}f-f\|_{L^{1}(M_{\Gamma})}\to 0
as
t\to 0^{+}
for every
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Gamma)
. To this end we let
\varepsilon>0
and choose
R>0
such that
\supp f\in B_{R}
and

\int_{\Gamma\setminus\Gamma_{R}}P_{t}^{\Gamma}|f|(x)M_{\Gamma}(x)\,{\textrm{d}}x<\varepsilon
.
Then,

(4.3)

\|P_{t}^{\Gamma}f-f\|_{L^{1}(M_{\Gamma})}<\int_{\Gamma_{R}}\big|P_{t}^{\Gamma}f(x)-f(x)\big|M_{\Gamma}(x)\,{\textrm{d}}x+\varepsilon.

Considering the integrand in (
4.3
), for all
x\in\Gamma_{R}
we have

(4.4)

\big|P_{t}^{\Gamma}f(x)-f(x)\big|\leqslant\int_{\Gamma}p_{t}^{\Gamma}(x,y)|f(y)-f(x)|\,{\textrm{d}}y+|f(x)|\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{\Gamma}\leqslant t).

Since
P_{t}f\to f
uniformly as
t\to 0^{+}
, for
t>0
small enough we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}p_{t}^{\Gamma}(x,y)|f(y)-f(x)|\,{\textrm{d}}y\leqslant\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}p_{t}(x,y)|f(y)-f(x)|\,{\textrm{d}}y<\varepsilon.

On the other hand,

|f(x)|\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{\Gamma}\leqslant t)\leqslant\|f\|_{\infty}\sup_{x\in K}\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{\Gamma}\leqslant t),

where
K:=\supp f
. We have
r:=\dist(K,\Gamma^{c})>0
, so

\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{\Gamma}\leqslant t)\leqslant\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{B(x,r)}\leqslant t)=\mathbb{P}_{0}(\tau_{B_{r}}\leqslant t)\leqslant ctr^{-\alpha}<\varepsilon,

for
t
small enough, see, e.g.,
[
31
]
. By (
4.3
) and (
4.4
) we get, as required,

\|P_{t}^{\Gamma}f-f\|_{L^{1}(M_{\Gamma})}<\varepsilon+(\varepsilon+\|f\|_{\infty}\varepsilon)|\Gamma_{R}|\sup_{\Gamma_{R}}M_{\Gamma}.

∎

Recall that

\|f\|_{q,M_{\Gamma}}:=\|f/M_{\Gamma}\|_{L^{q}(M_{\Gamma}^{2})}=\bigg(\int_{\Gamma}|f(x)|^{q}M_{\Gamma}^{2-q}(x)\,{\textrm{d}}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}=\|f\|_{L^{q}(M_{\Gamma}^{2-q})},

if
1\leqslant q<\infty
, and

\|f\|_{\infty,M_{\Gamma}}:={\rm ess}\sup_{x\in\Gamma}|f(x)|/M_{\Gamma}(x).

The following characterization of
hypercontractivity
of
P^{\Gamma}_{t}
is crucial for the proof of (
1.3
).

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
q\in[1,\infty)
. We have

(4.5)

\|P_{t}^{\Gamma}f\|_{q,M_{\Gamma}}\leqslant Ct^{-\frac{d+2\beta}{\alpha}\frac{q-1}{q}}\|f\|_{1,M_{\Gamma}}

for all
t>0
and all non-negative functions
f
on
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
if and only if

(4.6)

\sup_{y\in\Gamma}\int_{\Gamma}\rho_{1}(x,y)^{q}M_{\Gamma}^{2}(x)\,{\textrm{d}}x<\infty.

Proof.

Assume (
4.6
). Let
f\geqslant 0
. With the notation
F:=f/M_{\Gamma}
we get

\|P_{1}^{\Gamma}f\|_{q,M_{\Gamma}}=\Bigg(\int_{\Gamma}\bigg(\int_{\Gamma}\rho_{1}(x,y)F(y)M_{\Gamma}^{2}(y)\,{\textrm{d}}y\bigg)^{q}M_{\Gamma}^{2}(x)\,{\textrm{d}}x\Bigg)^{1/q}.

Let
c
be the supremum in (
4.6
).
By Minkowski integral inequality,

\displaystyle\Bigg(\int_{\Gamma}\bigg(\int_{\Gamma}\rho_{1}(x,y)F(y)M_{\Gamma}^{2}(y)\,{\textrm{d}}y\bigg)^{q}M_{\Gamma}^{2}(x)\,{\textrm{d}}x\Bigg)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\Gamma}\bigg(\int_{\Gamma}\rho_{1}(x,y)^{q}M_{\Gamma}^{2}(x)\,{\textrm{d}}x\bigg)^{1/q}F(y)M_{\Gamma}^{2}(y)\,{\textrm{d}}y

\displaystyle\leqslant c\int_{\Gamma}F(y)M_{\Gamma}^{2}(y)\,{\textrm{d}}y=c\|f\|_{1,M_{\Gamma}}.

For
t>0
, by scaling we get (
4.5
) as follows:

\displaystyle\|P_{t}^{\Gamma}f\|_{q,M_{\Gamma}}

\displaystyle=t^{\frac{d+\beta(2-q)}{\alpha q}}\|P_{1}^{\Gamma}f(t^{1/\alpha}\,\cdot\,)\|_{q,M_{\Gamma}}\leqslant ct^{\frac{d+\beta(2-q)}{\alpha q}}\|f(t^{1/\alpha}\,\cdot\,)\|_{1,M_{\Gamma}}

\displaystyle=ct^{\frac{d+\beta(2-q)}{\alpha q}}t^{-\frac{d+\beta}{\alpha}}\|f\|_{1,M_{\Gamma}}=ct^{-\frac{d+2\beta}{\alpha}\frac{q-1}{q}}\|f\|_{1,M_{\Gamma}}.

Conversely, assume (
4.5
). Let
y\in\Gamma
. Let
g_{n}\geqslant 0
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, be functions in
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Gamma)
approximating
\delta_{y}
, the Dirac measure at
y
, as follows:

\int_{\Gamma}g_{n}(x)\,{\textrm{d}}x=1,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Gamma}h(x)g_{n}(x)\,{\textrm{d}}x=h(y),

for every function
h
continuous near
y
. For
f_{n}:=g_{n}/M_{\Gamma}
,
\|f_{n}\|_{1,M_{\Gamma}}=\|g_{n}\|_{1}=1
and

P_{1}^{\Gamma}f_{n}(x)=\int_{\Gamma}p_{1}^{\Gamma}(x,z)\frac{g_{n}(z)}{M_{\Gamma}(z)}\,{\textrm{d}}z\to\frac{p_{1}^{\Gamma}(x,y)}{M_{\Gamma}(y)},

as
n\to\infty
. By (
4.5
) and Fatou’s lemma,

\displaystyle C^{q}

\displaystyle\geqslant\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|P_{1}^{\Gamma}f_{n}\|_{q,M_{\Gamma}}^{q}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Gamma}\bigg|\int_{\Gamma}\frac{p_{1}^{\Gamma}(x,z)}{M_{\Gamma}(z)}g_{n}(z)\,{\textrm{d}}z\bigg|^{q}M_{\Gamma}^{2-q}(x)\,{\textrm{d}}x

\displaystyle\geqslant\int_{\Gamma}\rho_{1}(x,y)^{q}M_{\Gamma}^{2}(x)\,{\textrm{d}}x.

Since
y\in\Gamma
was arbitrary, we obtain (
4.6
).
∎

Remark 4.3
.

Of course, (
4.5
) extends to arbitrary
f\in L^{1}(M_{\Gamma})
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
q\in[1,\infty)
, assume (

) and suppose
\Gamma
is fat. If

,
\int_{\Gamma}f(x)M_{\Gamma}(x)\,{\textrm{d}}x=0

(4.7)

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}t^{\frac{d+2\beta}{\alpha}\frac{q-1}{q}}\|P_{t}^{\Gamma}f\|_{q,M_{\Gamma}}=0.

If, additionally,

has compact support, then (
4.7
) is true for

, too.

Proof.

Let
\omega>0
. First, we prove prove (
4.7
) for a compactly supported function

\int_{\Gamma}f(x)M_{\Gamma}(x)\,{\textrm{d}}x=0.

Step 1. Case
q=\infty
.

For
t>0

\displaystyle I(t)

\displaystyle:=t^{\frac{d+2\beta}{\alpha}}\|P_{t}^{\Gamma}f\|_{\infty,M_{\Gamma}}=t^{\frac{d+2\beta}{\alpha}}\sup_{x\in\Gamma}\left|\int_{\Gamma}\rho_{t}(x,y)M_{\Gamma}(y)f(y)\,{\textrm{d}}y\right|.

\displaystyle I(t)

\displaystyle=t^{\frac{d+2\beta}{\alpha}}\sup_{x\in\Gamma}\left|\int_{|y|\leqslant t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\omega}\left(\rho_{t}(x,y)-\rho_{t}(x,0)\right)M_{\Gamma}(y)f(y)\,{\textrm{d}}y\right|

\displaystyle=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\Gamma\\
|y|\leqslant\omega\end{subarray}}\left|\rho_{1}\left(x,y\right)-\rho_{1}\left(x,0\right)\right|\int_{\Gamma}M_{\Gamma}(y)|f(y)|\,{\textrm{d}}y,

where in the last line we used scaling (
3.6
) of
\rho
.

, we can make it arbitrary small by choosing small

) follows in this case.

Step 2. Case

J(t):=\|P_{t}^{\Gamma}f\|_{1,M_{\Gamma}}=\int_{\Gamma}\left|\int_{\Gamma}p^{\Gamma}_{t}(x,y)M_{\Gamma}(x)f(y)\,{\textrm{d}}y\right|\,{\textrm{d}}x=\int_{\Gamma}\Big|\int_{\Gamma}\rho_{t}(x,y)M_{\Gamma}^{2}(x)f(y)M_{\Gamma}(y)\,{\textrm{d}}y\Big|\,{\textrm{d}}x.

\displaystyle J(t)

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\Gamma}\int_{|y|\leqslant t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\omega}\big|\rho_{t}(x,y)-\rho_{t}(x,0)\big|M_{\Gamma}^{2}(x)|f(y)|M_{\Gamma}(y)\,{\textrm{d}}y\,{\textrm{d}}x

\displaystyle\leqslant\sup_{|y|\leqslant t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\omega}\|\rho_{t}(\cdot,y)-\rho_{t}(\cdot,0)\|_{L^{1}(M_{\Gamma}^{2})}\int_{\Gamma}M_{\Gamma}(y)|f(y)|\,{\textrm{d}}y,

for sufficiently large
t
. In view of (
2.10
) and (
3.6
), by changing variables
t^{-1/\alpha}x\to x
and
t^{-1/\alpha}y\to y
we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{|y|\leqslant t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\omega}\|\rho_{t}(\cdot,y)-\rho_{t}(\cdot,0)\|_{L^{1}(M_{\Gamma}^{2})}

\displaystyle=t^{-\frac{d+2\beta}{\alpha}}\sup_{|y|\leqslant t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\omega}\int\left|\rho_{1}\left(t^{-{1}/{\alpha}}x,t^{-{1}/{\alpha}}y\right)-\rho_{1}\left(t^{-{1}/{\alpha}}x,0\right)\right|M_{\Gamma}^{2}(x)\,{\textrm{d}}x

\displaystyle=\sup_{|y|\leqslant\omega}\|\rho_{1}(\cdot,y)-\rho_{1}(\cdot,0)\|_{L^{1}(M_{\Gamma}^{2})}.

By Corollary
3.5
, we can make it arbitrary small by choosing small
\omega
, so (
4.7
) is true.

Step 3. Case
q\in(1,\infty)
.

By Hölder inequality we get that, as
t\to\infty
,

\displaystyle t^{\frac{d+2\beta}{\alpha}\frac{q-1}{q}}\|P_{t}^{\Gamma}f\|_{q,M_{\Gamma}}

\displaystyle=t^{\frac{d+2\beta}{\alpha}\frac{q-1}{q}}\bigg(\int_{\Gamma}\big|P_{t}^{\Gamma}f(x)/M_{\Gamma}(x)\big|^{q-1}\big|P_{t}^{\Gamma}f(x)M_{\Gamma}(x)\big|\,{\textrm{d}}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(t^{\frac{d+2\beta}{\alpha}}\|P_{t}^{\Gamma}f\|_{\infty,M_{\Gamma}}\right)^{\frac{q-1}{q}}\|P_{t}^{\Gamma}f\|_{1,M_{\Gamma}}^{\frac{1}{q}}\to 0,

since both factors converge to zero as
t\rightarrow\infty
by
Step 1.
and
Step 2.

Finally, consider arbitrary
f\in L^{1}(M_{\Gamma})
with
\int_{\Gamma}f(x)M_{\Gamma}(x)\,{\textrm{d}}x=0
. Let
R>0
and
f_{R}(x)=(f(x)-c_{R})\mathbf{1}_{|x|\leqslant R}
, where
c_{R}=\int_{|x|\leqslant R}f(x)M_{\Gamma}(x)\,{\textrm{d}}x/\int_{|x|\leqslant R}M_{\Gamma}(x)\,{\textrm{d}}x
. Of course,

(4.9)

\int_{\Gamma}M_{\Gamma}(x)f_{R}(x)\,{\textrm{d}}x=0,

and
f_{R}
is compactly supported. Furthermore, due to our assumptions,

\displaystyle\|f-f_{R}\|_{L^{1}(M_{\Gamma})}

\displaystyle=|c_{R}|\int_{|x|\leqslant R}M_{\Gamma}(x)\,{\textrm{d}}x+\int_{|x|>R}M_{\Gamma}(x)|f(x)|\,{\textrm{d}}x

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{|x|\leqslant R}M_{\Gamma}(x)f(x)\,{\textrm{d}}x\Big|+\int_{|x|>R}M_{\Gamma}(x)|f(x)|\,{\textrm{d}}x\to 0

as
R\rightarrow\infty
.
Let
{\varepsilon}>0
and choose
R>0
so large that

\|f-f_{R}\|_{1,M_{\Gamma}}<{\varepsilon}.

For
q=1
, by using the triangle inequality and Lemma
4.1
, we get

\displaystyle\|P_{t}^{\Gamma}f\|_{1,M_{\Gamma}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|P_{t}^{\Gamma}f_{R}\|_{1,M_{\Gamma}}+\|P_{t}^{\Gamma}(f-f_{R})\|_{1,M_{\Gamma}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|P_{t}^{\Gamma}f_{R}\|_{1,M_{\Gamma}}+\|f-f_{R}\|_{1,M_{\Gamma}},

and
Step 2.
yields

\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\|P_{t}^{\Gamma}f\|_{1,M_{\Gamma}}\leqslant{\varepsilon},

which proves (
4.7
) in this case.

If
1<q<\infty
, then using the triangle inequality and Lemma
4.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle t^{\frac{d+2\beta}{\alpha}\frac{q-1}{q}}\|P_{t}^{\Gamma}f\|_{q,M_{\Gamma}}

\displaystyle\leqslant t^{\frac{d+2\beta}{\alpha}\frac{q-1}{q}}\|P_{t}^{\Gamma}f_{R}\|_{q,M_{\Gamma}}+t^{\frac{d+2\beta}{\alpha}\frac{q-1}{q}}\|P_{t}^{\Gamma}(f-f_{R})\|_{q,M_{\Gamma}}

\displaystyle\leqslant t^{\frac{d+2\beta}{\alpha}\frac{q-1}{q}}\|P_{t}^{\Gamma}f_{R}\|_{q,M_{\Gamma}}+C\|f-f_{R}\|_{1,M_{\Gamma}}.

By (
4.9
) and
Step 3.
,

\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}t^{\frac{d+2\beta}{\alpha}\frac{q-1}{q}}\|P_{t}^{\Gamma}f\|_{q,M_{\Gamma}}\leqslant 2C{\varepsilon}.

This completes the proof of (
4.7
) for
q\in(1,\infty)
.
∎

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
q\in[1,\infty)
, assume (
4.6
) and suppose
\Gamma
is fat. Then for
f\in L^{1}(M_{\Gamma})
and
A=\int_{\Gamma}f(x)M_{\Gamma}(x)\,{\textrm{d}}x
,

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}t^{\frac{d+2\beta}{\alpha}\frac{q-1}{q}}\|P_{t}^{\Gamma}f-A\Psi_{t}\|_{q,M_{\Gamma}}=0.

Remark 4.7
.

In view of (
3.30
) and Lemma
4.1
, the constant
A
in Theorem
4.6
satisfies

\int_{\Gamma}\big(P_{t}^{\Gamma}f(x)-A\Psi_{s}(x)\big)M_{\Gamma}(x)\,{\textrm{d}}x=0,\quad s,t>0.

Proof of Theorem
4.6
.

By (
2.5
), (
1.2
), Remark
4.7
and Lemma
4.5
,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}t^{\frac{d+2\beta}{\alpha}\frac{q-1}{q}}\|P_{t}^{\Gamma}f-A\Psi_{t}\|_{q,M_{\Gamma}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}t^{\frac{d+2\beta}{\alpha}\frac{q-1}{q}}\|P_{t+1}^{\Gamma}f-A\Psi_{t+1}\|_{q,M_{\Gamma}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}t^{\frac{d+2\beta}{\alpha}\frac{q-1}{q}}\|P_{t}^{\Gamma}\big(P_{1}^{\Gamma}f-A\Psi_{1}\big)\|_{q,M_{\Gamma}}=0.

∎

4.1.
Applications

We conclude the article by providing several applications and examples which apply to our results. In particular, we draw the reader’s attention to Lemma
4.9
, which provides sharp distinction between cones bigger and smaller than the half-space
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}:=\{x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\colon x_{d}>0\}
. The same behavior is displayed by the bigger class of smooth cones, as we assert in Corollary
4.10
. First, we note a simple observation.

Example 4.8
.

Let
q=1
. By (
3.4
), the condition (
4.6
) holds for every fat cone
\Gamma
.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
q\in(1,\infty)
and suppose
\Gamma
is a right-circular cone. Then (
4.6
) holds if
\beta\geqslant\alpha/2
. Conversely, if
d\geqslant 2
and
\beta<\alpha/2
, then (
4.6
) does not hold.

Proof.

Recall that by
[
10
, Theorem 2 and Eq. (3)]
,

(4.10)

p_{1}^{\Gamma}(x,y)\approx p_{1}(x,y)\frac{\big(1\wedge\delta_{\Gamma}(x)\big)^{\alpha/2}\big(1\wedge\delta_{\Gamma}(y)\big)^{\alpha/2}}{\big(1\wedge|x|)^{\alpha/2-\beta}\big(1\wedge|y|)^{\alpha/2-\beta}},\quad x,y\in\Gamma.

Moreover,
[
29
, Lemma 3.3]
entails that

M_{\Gamma}(x)\approx\delta_{\Gamma}(x)^{\alpha/2}|x|^{\beta-\alpha/2},\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}.

Using this together with (
4.10
) and (
2.4
), we infer that, for
x,y\in\Gamma
,

(4.11)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \rho_{1}(x,y)&\approx p_{1}(x,y)\frac{\big(1\wedge\delta_{\Gamma}(x)\big)^{\alpha/2}\big(1\wedge\delta_{\Gamma}(y)\big)^{\alpha/2}}{\big(1\wedge|x|)^{\alpha/2-\beta}\big(1\wedge|y|)^{\alpha/2-\beta}\delta_{\Gamma}(x)^{\alpha/2}|x|^{\beta-\alpha/2}\delta_{\Gamma}(y)^{\alpha/2}|y|^{\beta-\alpha/2}}\\
&\approx\big(1+|x-y|\big)^{-d-\alpha}\frac{\big(1+\delta_{\Gamma}(x))^{-\alpha/2}\big(1+\delta_{\Gamma}(y))^{-\alpha/2}}{(1+|x|)^{\beta-\alpha/2}(1+|y|)^{\beta-\alpha/2}}.\end{aligned}

Let
q\in(1,\infty)
and assume
\beta\geqslant\alpha/2
. Then it follows from (
4.11
) that
\rho_{1}(x,y)\lesssim 1
for
x,y\in\Gamma
, and (
3.4
) entails that

(4.12)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{\Gamma}\rho_{1}(x,y)^{q}M_{\Gamma}^{2}(x)\,{\textrm{d}}x&\leqslant\|\rho_{1}(x,\,\cdot\,)\|_{\infty}^{q-1}\int_{\Gamma}\rho_{1}(x,y)M_{\Gamma}^{2}(y)\,{\textrm{d}}y\\
&=\|\rho_{1}(x,\,\cdot\,)\|_{\infty}^{q-1}\lesssim 1.\end{aligned}

Thus, we get (
4.6
) as claimed.

Now assume that
d\geqslant 2
and
\beta<\alpha/2
. Let
y\in\Gamma
be such that
\delta_{\Gamma}(y)=2
, so that
A:=B(y,1)\subseteq\Gamma
. Then for
x\in A
one clearly has that
1+|x-y|\approx 1
and
\delta_{\Gamma}(x)\approx 1
. Then it follows from (
4.11
) that

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\rho_{1}(x,y)^{q}M_{\Gamma}^{2}(x)\,{\textrm{d}}x

\displaystyle\geqslant\int_{A}\rho_{1}(x,y)^{q}M_{\Gamma}^{2}(x)\,{\textrm{d}}x

\displaystyle\approx\int_{A}(1+|x|)^{q(\alpha/2-\beta)}(1+|y|)^{q(\alpha/2-\beta)}\delta_{\Gamma}(x)^{\alpha}|x|^{2\beta-\alpha}\,{\textrm{d}}x

\displaystyle\approx|y|^{(q-1)(\alpha-2\beta)}.

Since
\alpha-2\beta>0
and
q>1
, by taking
|y|\to\infty
we see that (
4.6
) cannot hold in this case.
∎

Corollary 4.10
.

For
d\geqslant 2
and smooth cone
\Gamma
, (
4.6
) holds if and only if
\beta\geqslant\alpha/2
.

Proof.

Recall that
\Gamma
is open and
C^{1,1}
outside of the origin.
From the harmonicity and homogeneity of
M_{\Gamma}
, by the boundary Harnack principle we get, as in
[
29
, Lemma 3.3]
, that

M_{\Gamma}(x)\approx\delta_{\Gamma}(x)^{\alpha/2}|x|^{\beta-\alpha/2},\quad x\in\Gamma.

Moreover, since a smooth cone is fat, its Dirichlet heat kernel satisfies (
4.10
). Thus, one can directly repeat the proof of Lemma
4.9
to conclude the claim.
∎

Example 4.11
.

For
d=1
, either
\Gamma=(0,\infty)
or
\Gamma=\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
and both cases are (trivially) smooth cones, with
\delta_{\Gamma}(y)=|y|
for
y\in\Gamma
.

When
\Gamma=(0,\infty)
, then (
4.11
) yields the boundedness of
\rho_{1}
and (
4.6
) holds through (
4.12
), since this
\Gamma
is right-circular. Recall that here one has
\beta=\alpha/2
and
M_{\Gamma}(x)=(0\vee x)^{\alpha/2}
by
[
3
, Example 3.2]
, and we refer to Example
1.3
for the rest of the summary of this case.

If
\alpha\in(1,2)
and
\Gamma=\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
, then
\Gamma^{c}=\{0\}
is a non-polar set,
\beta=\alpha-1
, and
M_{\Gamma}(x)=|x|^{\alpha-1}
; see
[
3
, Example 3.3]
. Note that in this example,
\Gamma
is not right-circular anymore. Nevertheless, by
[
10
, Example 8]
, the survival probability is
\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{\Gamma}>t)\approx(1\wedge t^{-1/\alpha}|x|)^{\alpha-1}
and

p_{1}^{\Gamma}(x,y)\approx(1+|x-y|)^{-1-\alpha}(1\wedge|x|)^{\alpha-1}(1\wedge|y|)^{\alpha-1},\quad x,y\in\Gamma.

Thus,

\rho_{1}(x,y)\approx(1+|x-y|)^{-1-\alpha}(1+|x|)^{1-\alpha}(1+|y|)^{1-\alpha}\lesssim 1,\quad x,y\in\Gamma,

and one may apply (
4.12
) to get (
4.6
), too. It then follows from Theorem
4.6
with
q=2
that
\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{(2\alpha-1)/(2\alpha)}\|P_{t}^{\Gamma}f\|_{2}=0
if
\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)|x|^{\alpha-1}\,{\textrm{d}}x=0
. Accordingly, by Lemma
3.6
, the stationary density
\varphi
of Theorem
3.4
satisfies
\varphi(x)\approx(1+|x|)^{-2\alpha}
. Here and below,
x\in\Gamma
and
t>0
. Then,
\Psi_{t}(x)\approx t^{-1}(1+t^{-1/\alpha}|x|)^{-1-\alpha}(1\wedge t^{-1/\alpha}|x|)^{\alpha-1}
and the density of the Yaglom limit is comparable with
\varphi(x)|x|^{\alpha-1}\approx(1+|x|)^{-2\alpha}|x|^{\alpha-1}\approx\Psi_{1}(x)
.

Example 4.12
.

Let
d\geqslant 2
and
\Gamma
be a right-circular cone which is a subset of the half-space
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
. Then by
[
3

, we have
\beta\geqslant\alpha/2
and Lemma
4.9
gives (
4.6
). On the other hand, if
\Gamma
is such that
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}\subsetneq\Gamma

\beta<\alpha/2
by
[
3
, Lemma 3.3]
, and Lemma
4.9
asserts that (
4.6
) does not hold.

The following extends Example
1.3
. Let
\Gamma=\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
, so that
M_{\Gamma}(x)=(0\vee x_{d})^{\alpha/2}
. By Theorem
4.6
,
\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{(d+\alpha)/(2\alpha)}\|P_{t}^{\Gamma}f\|_{2}=0
if
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}f(x)x_{d}^{\alpha/2}\,{\textrm{d}}x=0

\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{\Gamma}>t)\approx 1\wedge t^{-1/2}(0\vee x_{d})^{\alpha/2}
and

p_{1}^{\Gamma}(x,y)\approx(1+|x-y|)^{-d-\alpha}(1\wedge x_{d})^{\alpha/2}(1\wedge y_{d})^{\alpha/2},

see
[
10

. Here and below,
x,y\in\Gamma

\Psi_{t}(x)\approx(t^{1/\alpha}\vee|x|)^{-d-\alpha}(t^{1/\alpha}\wedge x_{d})^{\alpha/2}

t>0

\varphi
is comparable to
(1+|x|)^{-d-\alpha}(1+x_{d})^{-\alpha/2}
and the density of the Yaglom distribution is
\varphi(x)x_{d}^{\alpha/2}/\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}\varphi(y)y_{d}^{\alpha/2}\,{\textrm{d}}y\approx(1+|x|)^{-d-\alpha}(1\wedge x_{d})^{\alpha/2}\approx\Psi_{1}(x)
.

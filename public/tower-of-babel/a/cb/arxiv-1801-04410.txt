1.
Introduction

Wide subcategories of an abelian category are analogous to thick subcategories of a triangulated category.
This notion was introduced by Hovey in [Hov] and provides a significant interface between representation theory and combinatorics [IT]. Wide subcategories are closely related to other important notions in representation theory, such as semibricks [As], torsion classes [AIR, DIRRT, MS], silting complexes [AIR], semistable subcategories [Y] and support
\tau
-tilting modules [AIR]. Wide subcategories of
d
-abelian categories was introduced and studied in [HJV].

Recollements of abelian categories were introduced by Beilinson, Bernstein and Deligne in [BBD] for the construction of the category of perverse sheaves on a singular space, arising from recollements of triangulated categories. The notion of recollements is very useful in gluing and in reduction, see for example [AKL, BBD, C, FP, LVY, P].
The main result of this paper is the following reduction result for wide subcategories. This is similar to Calabi-Yau reduction in [IY2], silting reduction in [AI] and [IY1], and reduction of support
\tau
-tilting modules in [EJR] and [J].

Theorem 1.1
.

(Theorems 3.4 and 3.5)

Let (
\mathcal{A}^{\prime},\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime},i^{*},i_{*},i^{!},j_{!},j^{*},j_{*}
) be a recollement (see Definition 2.4 for details). Then we have

(1)

There is a bijection between wide subcategories in
\mathcal{A}
containing
i_{*}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
and wide subcategories in
\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime}
.

(2)

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a subcategory of
\mathcal{A}
. If
i_{*}i^{*}(\mathcal{C})\subset\mathcal{C}
(resp.
i_{*}i^{!}(\mathcal{C})\subset\mathcal{C})
, then
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{A}
is wide implies that
i^{*}(\mathcal{C})
(resp.
i^{!}(\mathcal{C})
)
\subset\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
is wide.

Note that
i_{*}i^{*}(\mathcal{C})\subset\mathcal{C}
(resp.
i_{*}i^{!}(\mathcal{C})\subset\mathcal{C})
is weaker than
i_{*}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})\subset\mathcal{C}
.
It is a general interesting problem to construct a new recollement from a known one, see for example [CL, LL, LW] for some study on this problem. As an application of Theorem
1.1
, we can construct a new recollement from the given one as follows:

Theorem 1.2
.

(Theorem 3.8)

Let (
\mathcal{A}^{\prime},\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime},i^{*},i_{*},i^{!},j_{!},j^{*},j_{*}
) be a recollement of abelian categories and
\mathcal{C}
be a wide subcategory of
\mathcal{A}
. If
i_{*}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})\subset\mathcal{C}
, then the given recollement restricts to a new recollement of wide subcategories (
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
,
\mathcal{C},j^{*}(\mathcal{C}),\overline{i^{*}},\overline{i_{*}},\overline{i^{!}},\overline{j_{!}},\overline{j^{*}},\overline{j_{*}}
).

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we collect some basic material that will be used later. We begin this section by introducing the definition of wide subcategories.

Definition 2.1
.

[Hov, Definition 1.1]
A full subcategory
\mathcal{C}
of an abelian category
\mathcal{A}
is called
wide
if it is abelian and closed under extensions.

Note that when we say a full subcategory
\mathcal{C}
is abelian, we mean that
\mathcal{C}
is closed under kernels and cokernels, more precisely, if
f:M\rightarrow N
is a morphism of
\mathcal{C}
, then both the kernel and cokernel of
f
are also in
\mathcal{C}
. We denote by
\operatorname{\mathsf{wide}}\mathcal{A}
the set of wide subcategories of
\mathcal{A}
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{wide}}_{\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{A}
the set of wide subcategories of
\mathcal{A}
containing a given subcategory
\mathcal{C}
.

Example 2.2
.

[Hov]
Let
R
be a ring.

(1)

The empty subcategory, the zero subcategory and the entire category of
R
-modules are all wide subcategories.

(2)

The category of all
R
-modules of cardinality
\leq\kappa
for some infinite cardinal
\kappa
is a wide subcategory.

(3)

The category of finite-dimensional rational vector spaces, as a subcategory of the category of abelian groups, is a wide subcategory.

For the convenience of the reader, we recall the definition of adjoint pairs [ARS] before the definition of recollements.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
be categories. An

from

to
\mathcal{B}
is a triple
\langle F,G,\phi\rangle:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
, where

is a functor from
\mathcal{A}
to
\mathcal{B}
,

\mathcal{B}
to
\mathcal{A}
, and

which is functorial in

and

. The functor

is called a
left

adjoint
of

,
G
is called a
right

adjoint
of
F
, and the pair
(F,G)
is called an
adjoint

pair
from
\mathcal{A}
to
\mathcal{B}
.

3.
Recollements of wide subcategories

In this section we prove Theorems

Throughout this section, let (

We first establish the following important result.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{wide}}_{i_{*}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})}\mathcal{A}
. Then we have

(1)

i_{*}i^{*}(\mathcal{C})\subset\mathcal{C}

i_{*}i^{!}(\mathcal{C})\subset\mathcal{C}

(2)

If

Proof.

(1) Since

i_{*}i^{*}(\mathcal{C})\subset\mathcal{C}
and
i_{*}i^{!}(\mathcal{C})\subset\mathcal{C}
are obvious.
For any

and two exact sequences

0\rightarrow i_{*}(M^{\prime})\rightarrow j_{!}j^{*}(C)\rightarrow C\rightarrow i_{*}i^{*}(C)\rightarrow 0,

0\rightarrow i_{*}i^{!}(C)\rightarrow C\rightarrow j_{*}j^{*}(C)\rightarrow i_{*}(N^{\prime})\rightarrow 0.

Since

is wide and
i_{*}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})\subset\mathcal{C}
, it follows that
j_{*}j^{*}(C)\in\mathcal{C}
and
j_{!}j^{*}(C)\in\mathcal{C}
. Therefore
j_{*}j^{*}(\mathcal{C})\subset\mathcal{C}
and
j_{!}j^{*}(\mathcal{C})\subset\mathcal{C}
.

(2) By Remark 2.5(3) there exists an exact sequence

0\rightarrow i_{*}(C^{\prime})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\rightarrow}}j_{!}j^{*}(C)\rightarrow C\rightarrow i_{*}i^{*}(C)\rightarrow 0,

where

. Since
j^{*}(C)\in j^{*}({\mathcal{C}})

, both

and

are in

. Therefore

is a morphism in
{\mathcal{C}}
, and thus its cokernel

belongs to
{\mathcal{C}}
because
{\mathcal{C}}
is closed under cokernels. Now
C
, being an extension of
i_{*}i^{*}(C)
by
\mathrm{Coker}f
, belongs to
{\mathcal{C}}
, because
{\mathcal{C}}
is closed under extensions.
∎

j^{*}
is exact, we have the following exact sequence:

0\longrightarrow j^{*}(M)\longrightarrow j^{*}(N)\longrightarrow j^{*}(P)\longrightarrow 0.

Note that
j^{*}(M)\in\mathcal{W}
and
j^{*}(P)\in\mathcal{W}
, it follows that
j^{*}(N)\in\mathcal{W}
since
\mathcal{W}
is wide. Thus
N\in\mathcal{C}
. We conclude that
\mathcal{C}
is a wide subcategory of
\mathcal{A}
. It is obvious that
i_{*}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})\subset\mathcal{C}
since
j^{*}i_{*}=0
.
∎

In conclusion, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 3.4
.

There is a bijection

\operatorname{\mathsf{wide}}_{i_{*}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})}\mathcal{A}\leftrightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{wide}}\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime}

given by
\operatorname{\mathsf{wide}}_{i_{*}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})}\mathcal{A}\ni\mathcal{C}\mapsto j^{*}(\mathcal{C})\in\operatorname{\mathsf{wide}}\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime}
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{wide}}\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime}\ni\mathcal{W}\mapsto\mathcal{C}=\{M\in\mathcal{A}|j^{*}(M)\in\mathcal{W}\}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{wide}}_{i_{*}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})}\mathcal{A}
.

Proof.

By Proposition 3.2 and Proposition 3.3, we only have to prove that the maps above are inverse to each other.
Let
\mathcal{W}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{wide}}\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime}
and
\mathcal{C}=\{M\in\mathcal{A}|j^{*}(M)\in\mathcal{W}\}
. We claim that
j^{*}(\mathcal{C})=\mathcal{W}
. Clearly
j^{*}(\mathcal{C})\subset\mathcal{W}
. For any
N\in\mathcal{W}
,
N\cong j^{*}j_{*}(N)\in\mathcal{W}
, then
j_{*}(N)\in\mathcal{C}
and therefore
N\in j^{*}(\mathcal{C})
.

Conversely, let
\mathcal{C}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{wide}}_{i_{*}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})}\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\{M\in\mathcal{A}|j^{*}(M)\in j^{*}(\mathcal{C})\}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{wide}}_{i_{*}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})}\mathcal{A}
. We claim that
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
. Clearly
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
. For any
M\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{A}
, by Lemma 3.1(2) we have
M\in\mathcal{C}
. We have finished to prove that
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
.
∎

Theorem 3.5
.

If
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{A}
is wide and satisfies
i_{*}i^{*}(\mathcal{C})\subset\mathcal{C}
(resp.
i_{*}i^{!}(\mathcal{C})\subset\mathcal{C})
), then
i^{*}(\mathcal{C})
(resp.
i^{!}(\mathcal{C})
)
\subset\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
is wide.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{A}
is wide and satisfies
i_{*}i^{*}(\mathcal{C})\subset\mathcal{C}
. We prove that
i^{*}(\mathcal{C})\subset\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
is wide. The proof that
i^{!}(\mathcal{C})\subset\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
is wide when
i_{*}i^{!}(\mathcal{C})\subset\mathcal{C}
is similar.

Step 1:
i^{*}(\mathcal{C})
is closed under kernels and cokernels. If
f^{\prime}:M^{\prime}\rightarrow N^{\prime}
is a morphism of
i^{*}(\mathcal{C})
, we have to prove that
{\rm Ker}f^{\prime},~{\rm Coker}f^{\prime}\in i^{*}(\mathcal{C})
. Applying the functor
i_{*}
to
f^{\prime}
, by Remark 2.5(1) we have the following exact sequence:

0\longrightarrow i_{*}({\rm Ker}f^{\prime})\longrightarrow i_{*}(M^{\prime})\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{{i_{*}(f^{\prime})}}}i_{*}(N^{\prime})\longrightarrow i_{*}({\rm Coker}f^{\prime})\longrightarrow 0.

Note that
i_{*}i^{*}(\mathcal{C})\subset\mathcal{C}
, it follows that
i_{*}(f^{\prime})
is a morphism of
\mathcal{C}
. Since
\mathcal{C}
is wide, it follows that
i_{*}({\rm Ker}f^{\prime})
and
i_{*}({\rm Coker}f^{\prime})
belong to
\mathcal{C}
. Thus
{\rm Ker}f^{\prime}\cong i^{*}i_{*}({\rm Ker}f^{\prime})\in i^{*}(\mathcal{C})
and
{\rm Coker}f^{\prime}\cong i^{*}i_{*}({\rm Coker}f^{\prime})\in i^{*}(\mathcal{C})
.

Step 2:
i^{*}(\mathcal{C})
is closed under extensions. Let
0\rightarrow M^{\prime}\rightarrow N^{\prime}\rightarrow P^{\prime}\rightarrow 0
be an exact sequence in
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
where
M^{\prime},P^{\prime}\in i^{*}(\mathcal{C})
, we have to prove that
N^{\prime}\in i^{*}(\mathcal{C})
. Applying the functor
i_{*}
to the above exact sequence, by Remark 2.5(1) we have the following exact sequence:

0\longrightarrow i_{*}(M^{\prime})\longrightarrow i_{*}(N^{\prime})\longrightarrow i_{*}(P^{\prime})\longrightarrow 0.

Note that
i_{*}i^{*}(\mathcal{C})\subset\mathcal{C}
, it follows that
i_{*}(M^{\prime}),i_{*}(P^{\prime})\in\mathcal{C}
. Since
\mathcal{C}
is wide,
i_{*}(N^{\prime})\in\mathcal{C}
. Thus
N^{\prime}\cong i^{*}i_{*}(N^{\prime})\in i^{*}(\mathcal{C})
.

We have finished to prove that
i^{*}({\mathcal{C}})
is wide.
∎

The next result states that an adjoint pair of two categories can be restricted to an adjoint pair of subcategories under an additional condition.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
(F,G)
be an adjoint pair from
\mathcal{A}
to
\mathcal{B}
as in Definition 2.3. If
\mathcal{C}
is a subcategory of
\mathcal{A}
satisfying
GF(\mathcal{C})\subset\mathcal{C}
, then the restricted functors
(\overline{F},\overline{G})(=
(F|_{\mathcal{C}},G|_{F(\mathcal{C})}))
is an adjoint pair from
\mathcal{C}
to
F(\mathcal{C})
.

Dually, if
\mathcal{D}
is a subcategory of
\mathcal{B}
satisfying
FG(\mathcal{D})\subset\mathcal{D}
, then the restricted functors
(\overline{F},\overline{G})(=
(F|_{G(\mathcal{D})},G|_{\mathcal{D}}))
is an adjoint pair from
G(\mathcal{D})
to
\mathcal{D}
.

Proof.

For any
C,D\in\mathcal{C}
, since
(F,G)
is an adjoint pair from
\mathcal{A}
to
\mathcal{B}
and
GF(\mathcal{C})\subset\mathcal{C}
, we have the following bijection:

{\rm Hom}_{F(\mathcal{C})}(\overline{F}C,FD)={\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{B}}(FC,FD)\cong{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(C,GFD)={\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(C,\overline{G}FD),

which is functorial in
C
and
D
. Therefore
(\overline{F},\overline{G})
is an adjoint pair from to
\mathcal{C}
to
F(\mathcal{C})
.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

If
i_{*}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})\subset\mathcal{C}
, then
i^{*}(\mathcal{C})=i^{!}(\mathcal{C})=\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

In fact, if
i_{*}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})\subset\mathcal{C}
, then
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\cong i^{*}i_{*}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})\subset i^{*}(\mathcal{C})
and
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\cong i^{!}i_{*}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})\subset i^{!}(\mathcal{C})
. Thus
i^{*}(\mathcal{C})=i^{!}(\mathcal{C})=\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
.
∎

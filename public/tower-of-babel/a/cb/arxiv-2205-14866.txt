Abstract
. In this article, we study an inverse problem for the following convective Brinkman-Forchheimer (CBF) equations: 𝒖 t − μ Δ 𝒖 + ( 𝒖 ⋅ ∇ ) 𝒖 + α 𝒖 + β | 𝒖 | r − 1 𝒖 + ∇ p = 𝑭 := f 𝒈 , ∇ ⋅ 𝒖 = 0 , \displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}_{t}-\mu\Delta\boldsymbol{u}+(\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{u}+\alpha\boldsymbol{u}+\beta|\boldsymbol{u}|^{r-1}\boldsymbol{u}+\nabla p=\boldsymbol{F}:=f\boldsymbol{g},\ \ \ \nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}=0, in a bounded domain Ω ⊂ ℝ 2 \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2} with smooth boundary ∂ Ω \partial\Omega , where α , β , μ > 0 \alpha,\beta,\mu>0 and r ∈ [ 1 , 3 ] r\in[1,3] . The investigated inverse problem consists of reconstructing the vector-valued velocity function 𝒖 \boldsymbol{u} , the pressure field p p and the scalar function f f . For the divergence free initial data 𝒖 0 ∈ 𝕃 2 ​ ( Ω ) \boldsymbol{u}_{0}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega) , we prove the existence of a solution to the inverse problem for two-dimensional CBF equations with the integral overdetermination condition, by showing the existence of a unique fixed point for an equivalent operator equation (using an extension of the contraction mapping theorem). Moreover, we establish the uniqueness and Lipschitz stability results of the solution to the inverse problem for 2D CBF equations with r ∈ [ 1 , 3 ] r\in[1,3] .



1.
Introduction

The convective Brinkman-Forchheimer (CBF) equations describe the motion of incompressible fluid flows in a saturated porous medium (cf.
[
5
]
). This article’s primary goal is to discuss the well-posedness of an inverse problem to CBF equations in two dimensions for a divergence free initial data in
\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)
.

1.1.
The mathematical model and the direct problem

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a bounded domain with a smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
(at least
\mathrm{C}^{2}
-boundary). The CBF equations are given by

(1.1)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}_{t}-\mu\Delta\boldsymbol{u}+(\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{u}+\alpha\boldsymbol{u}+\beta|\boldsymbol{u}|^{r-1}\boldsymbol{u}+\nabla p=\boldsymbol{F}

\displaystyle:=f\boldsymbol{g},\ \text{ in }\ \Omega\times(0,T),

(1.2)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}

\displaystyle=0,\ \ \ \ \text{ in }\ \Omega\times(0,T),

with initial condition

(1.3)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}=\boldsymbol{u}_{0},\ \text{ in }\ \Omega\times\{0\},

and boundary condition

(1.4)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}=\boldsymbol{0},\ \ \text{ on }\ \partial\Omega\times[0,T).

Here
\boldsymbol{u}(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
represents the velocity field at position
x
and time
t
,
p(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}
denotes the pressure field and
\boldsymbol{F}(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
stands for the external forcing. The constant
\mu
denotes the positive Brinkman coefficient (effective viscosity), the positive constants
\alpha
and
\beta
stand for the Darcy coefficient (permeability of porous medium) and the Forchheimer coefficient (proportional to the porosity of the material), respectively (cf.
[
23
]
). The absorption exponent
r\in[1,\infty)
and the cases,
r=3
and
r>3
, are known as the critical exponent and the fast growing nonlinearity, respectively. For
\alpha=\beta=0
, we obtain the classical
2\mathrm{D}
Navier-Stokes equations (NSE). Thus, one can consider the equations (
1.1
)-(
1.4
) as a modification (by introducing an absorption term
\alpha\boldsymbol{u}+\beta|\boldsymbol{u}|^{r-1}\boldsymbol{u}
) of the classical NSE. Thus, one may consider the model (
1.1
)-(
1.4
) as NSE with damping. In order to obtain the uniqueness of the pressure
p
, one can impose the condition
\int_{\Omega}p(x,t)\/\mathrm{d}\/x=0,
for
t\in[0,T]
. The model given in (
1.1
)-(
1.4
) is recognized to be more accurate when the flow velocity is too large for the Darcy’s law to be valid alone, and apart from that, the porosity is not too small, so that we call these types of models as
non-Darcy models
(cf.
[
30
]
). It has been proved in Proposition 1.1,
[
18
]
that the critical homogeneous CBF equations have the same scaling as NSE only when
\alpha=0
and no scale invariance property for other values of
\alpha
and
r
.

Let us now discuss some results available in the literature on the global solvability of the system (
1.1
)-(
1.4
) (direct problem). The existence and uniqueness of weak as well as strong solutions in two and three dimensional bounded domains are available in
[
3
,
16
,
23
,
31
]
, etc., for the global solvability results in periodic domains and whole space, one can refer to
[
7
,
18
,
43
]
, etc. The Navier-Stokes problem modified an absorption term
|\boldsymbol{u}|^{r-1}\boldsymbol{u}
, for
r>1
, in bounded domains with compact boundary is considered in
[
3
]
. The existence of Leray-Hopf weak solutions, for any dimension
d\geq 2
, and its uniqueness for
d=2
is established in
[
3
]
. For
d=2,r\in[1,\infty)
and
d=3,r\in[3,\infty)
(
2\beta\mu\geq 1
for
r=3
), the existence and uniqueness of global Leray-Hopf weak as well as strong solution is established in
[
31
]
.

1.2.
The inverse problem

Even though the direct problem is important, but it requires the knowledge of physical parameters such as the Brinkman coefficient
\mu
, Darcy coefficient
\alpha
, Forchheimer coefficient
\beta
and the forcing term
\boldsymbol{F}:=f\boldsymbol{g}
. When in addition to the solution of the equation, recovery of some physical properties of the investigated object or the effects of the external sources are needed, we require inverse problems to determine a coefficient or to handle the right hand side of the differential equation arising in mathematical modeling of physical phenomena. In such modeled problems, it is more efficient to consider the inverse source problems. However, posing an inverse problem requires some additional information of the solution besides the given initial and boundary conditions. An additional information on a solution of the inverse problem can be integral overdetermination condition, which is the case considered in this paper. The integral overdetermination condition considered in this work is given by

(1.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{u}(x,t)\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}(x)\/\mathrm{d}\/x=\varphi(t),\ \ t\in[0,T],

where
\varphi(t)
is the measurement data, which stands for the average velocity on the domain
\Omega
and
\boldsymbol{\omega}
is given quantity, which corresponds to the type of device used to measure the velocity.

Let the vector-valued external forcing
\boldsymbol{F}
appearing in (
1.1
) be represented by

\boldsymbol{F}(x,t):=f(t)\boldsymbol{g}(x,t),

where the scalar function
f
is unknown and
\boldsymbol{g}
is a given vector-valued function. The investigation of the nonlinear inverse problem in this article consists of reconstructing the vector-valued velocity function
\boldsymbol{u}
, the pressure field
p
and the scalar function
f
from the system (
1.1
)-(
1.4
), with the integral overdetermination condition (
1.5
), and the given functions
\boldsymbol{u}_{0},\boldsymbol{\omega},\varphi
and
\boldsymbol{g}
.

The inverse problems with final overdetermination (cf.

1.3.
Technical difficulties

We emphasize that, due to a technical difficulty in working with bounded domains, the method employed in
[

,
34
,

]
, etc. (for the initial data

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}(x)|^{r-1}\boldsymbol{u}(x)\/\mathrm{d}\/x+4\bigg(\frac{r-1}{(r+1)^{2}}\bigg)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla|\boldsymbol{u}(x)|^{\frac{r+1}{2}}|^{r}\/\mathrm{d}\/x

2.2.
Equivalent formulation

We require the following input data function requirements:

\boldsymbol{u}_{0}\in\mathbb{H},\ \boldsymbol{g}\in\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)),\ \boldsymbol{\omega}\in\mathbb{H}^{2}(\Omega)\cap\mathbb{V},\ \nabla\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty},\ \varphi\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(0,T),

\text{and}\ \ \mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{g}(x,t)\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}(x)\/\mathrm{d}\/x}}\right|\geq g_{0}>0,\ \ (g_{0}\equiv\text{constant}),\ \ \ 0\leq t\leq T.

Under the above assumptions, we derive an operator equation of the second kind for the scalar function

(2.1)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{A}f)(t):=\frac{1}{g_{1}(t)}\bigg\{\int_{\Omega}\big(\mu\nabla\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\nabla\boldsymbol{\omega}+(\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}+\alpha\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}+\beta|\boldsymbol{u}|^{r-1}\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}\big)\/\mathrm{d}\/x+\varphi^{\prime}(t)\bigg\},

(2.2)

\displaystyle g_{1}(t)=\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{g}(x,t)\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}(x)\/\mathrm{d}\/x.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
(\boldsymbol{u}(\cdot),p(\cdot))
be the unique weak solution of the CBF equations (
1.1
)-(
1.4

\boldsymbol{u}_{0}\in\mathbb{H}

(A.1)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\boldsymbol{u}(t)\|_{\mathbb{H}}\leq\|\boldsymbol{u}_{0}\|_{\mathbb{H}}+T^{1/2}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\boldsymbol{g}(t)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,T)},

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\boldsymbol{u}(t)\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+2\mu\int_{0}^{T}\|\boldsymbol{u}(t)\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{2}\/\mathrm{d}\/t+\alpha\int_{0}^{T}\|\boldsymbol{u}(t)\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\/\mathrm{d}\/t+2\beta\int_{0}^{T}\|\boldsymbol{u}(t)\|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}}^{r+1}\/\mathrm{d}\/t

\displaystyle\leq\|\boldsymbol{u}_{0}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\boldsymbol{g}(t)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,T)}^{2}.

Proof.

Taking the inner product with
\boldsymbol{u}(\cdot)
in the equation (
1.1
) and use the fact that
\langle(\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u}\rangle=0
and
\langle\nabla p,\boldsymbol{u}\rangle=0
to obtain

(A.3)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\/\mathrm{d}\/}{\/\mathrm{d}\/t}\|\boldsymbol{u}(t)\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+\mu\|\boldsymbol{u}(t)\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{2}+\alpha\|\boldsymbol{u}(t)\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+\beta\|\boldsymbol{u}(t)\|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}}^{r+1}=(f\boldsymbol{g}(t),\boldsymbol{u}(t)),

for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
. Simplifying (
A.3
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\/\mathrm{d}\/}{\/\mathrm{d}\/t}\|\boldsymbol{u}(t)\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\leq|f(t)|\|\boldsymbol{g}(t)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\|\boldsymbol{u}(t)\|_{\mathbb{H}},

which further reduces to

(A.4)

\displaystyle\frac{\/\mathrm{d}\/}{\/\mathrm{d}\/t}\|\boldsymbol{u}(t)\|_{\mathbb{H}}\leq|f(t)|\|\boldsymbol{g}(t)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}.

Integrating the inequality (
A.4
) from
0
to
t
, and taking supremum over
0
to
T
on both sides, one can easily deduce (
A.1
).

Using Hölder’s and Young’s inequalities, we estimate
|(f\boldsymbol{g},\boldsymbol{u})|
as

(A.5)

\displaystyle|(f\boldsymbol{g},\boldsymbol{u})|\leq|f|\|\boldsymbol{g}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathbb{H}}\leq\frac{1}{2\alpha}|f|^{2}\|\boldsymbol{g}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}.

Substituting (
A.5
) in (
A.3
), and then integrating it from
0
to
t
, we find

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{u}(t)\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+2\mu\int_{0}^{t}\|\boldsymbol{u}(s)\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{2}\/\mathrm{d}\/s+\alpha\int_{0}^{t}\|\boldsymbol{u}(s)\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\/\mathrm{d}\/s+2\beta\int_{0}^{t}\|\boldsymbol{u}(s)\|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}}^{r+1}\/\mathrm{d}\/s

\displaystyle\quad\leq\|\boldsymbol{u}_{0}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}|f(s)|^{2}\|\boldsymbol{g}(s)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\/\mathrm{d}\/s,

for all
t\in[0,T]
and (
A.1
) follows.
∎

The next lemma establishes stability estimates for the CBF equations (
1.1
)-(
1.4
).

Lemma A.2
.

Let
(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}(\cdot),p_{1}(\cdot))
be the solution of the direct problem (
1.1
)-(
1.4
) corresponding to the external forcing
f_{1}\boldsymbol{g}
and the initial velocity
\boldsymbol{u}_{01}
; and
(\boldsymbol{u}_{2}(\cdot),p_{2}(\cdot))
be the solution of the same problem corresponding to the external forcing
f_{2}\boldsymbol{g}
and the initial velocity
\boldsymbol{u}_{02}
. Then, for
\boldsymbol{u}_{01},\boldsymbol{u}_{02}\in\mathbb{H},

f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,T)
and
\boldsymbol{g}\in\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega))
, the following estimate holds:

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2})(t)\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+\mu\int_{0}^{T}\|(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2})(t)\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{2}\/\mathrm{d}\/t

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha\int_{0}^{T}\|(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2})(t)\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\/\mathrm{d}\/t+\frac{\beta}{2^{r-2}}\int_{0}^{T}\|(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2})(t)\|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}}^{r+1}\/\mathrm{d}\/t

(A.6)

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\|\boldsymbol{u}_{01}-\boldsymbol{u}_{02}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\boldsymbol{g}(t)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\|f_{1}-f_{2}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,T)}^{2}\bigg)\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{2}{\mu}\int_{0}^{T}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{2}(t)\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{2}\/\mathrm{d}\/t}}\right).

Moreover, for
\boldsymbol{u}_{01}=\boldsymbol{u}_{02}
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2})(t)\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}

(A.7)

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\frac{1}{\alpha}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\boldsymbol{g}(t)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\|f_{1}-f_{2}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,T)}^{2}\bigg)\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{2}{\mu}\int_{0}^{T}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{2}(t)\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{2}\/\mathrm{d}\/t}}\right).

Proof.

It is clear that the pair
\big((\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2})(\cdot),(p_{1}-p_{2})(\cdot)\big)
satisfies the following system:

(A.8)

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{aligned} (\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2})_{t}-\mu\Delta(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2})+\alpha(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2})+\nabla(p_{1}-p_{2})&=\boldsymbol{F}_{k},\ \ \ \text{in}\ \Omega\times(0,T),\\
\nabla\cdot(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2})&=0,\ \ \ \ \ \text{in}\ \Omega\times(0,T),\\
\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2}&=\boldsymbol{0},\ \ \ \ \ \text{on}\ \partial\Omega\times[0,T),\\
\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2}&=\boldsymbol{u}_{01}-\boldsymbol{u}_{02},\ \ \text{in}\ \Omega\times\{0\},\end{aligned}}}\right.

in
\mathbb{V}^{\prime}
, where

(A.9)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{F}_{k}=(f_{1}-f_{2})\boldsymbol{g}-(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{u}_{1}+(\boldsymbol{u}_{2}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{u}_{2}-\beta\big(|\boldsymbol{u}_{1}|^{r-1}\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-|\boldsymbol{u}_{2}|^{r-1}\boldsymbol{u}_{2}\big).

Taking the inner product with
(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2})(\cdot)
to the first equation in (
A.8
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\/\mathrm{d}\/}{\/\mathrm{d}\/t}\|(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2})(t)\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+\mu\|(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2})(t)\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{2}+\alpha\|(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2})(t)\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle=((f_{1}-f_{2})(t)\boldsymbol{g}(t),(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2})(t))-\langle((\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2})(t)\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{u}_{2}(t),(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2})(t)\rangle

(A.10)

\displaystyle\quad-\beta\langle|\boldsymbol{u}_{1}(t)|^{r-1}\boldsymbol{u}_{1}(t)-|\boldsymbol{u}_{2}(t)|^{r-1}\boldsymbol{u}_{2}(t),(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2})(t)\rangle,

for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
. It is worth emphasizing that, for
r\geq 1
(see Sec. 2.4,
[
31
,
32
]
)

\displaystyle\beta\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(|\boldsymbol{u}_{1}|^{r-1}\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-|\boldsymbol{u}_{2}|^{r-1}\boldsymbol{u}_{2},\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2}}}\right)

(A.11)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\beta}{2}\big\||\boldsymbol{u}_{1}|^{\frac{r-1}{2}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2})\big\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+\frac{\beta}{2}\big\||\boldsymbol{u}_{2}|^{\frac{r-1}{2}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2})\big\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}.

It can be easily seen that

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2}\|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}}^{r+1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\boldsymbol{u}_{1}(x)-\boldsymbol{u}_{2}(x)|^{r-1}|\boldsymbol{u}_{1}(x)-\boldsymbol{u}_{2}(x)|^{2}\/\mathrm{d}\/x

\displaystyle\leq 2^{r-2}\int_{\Omega}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(|\boldsymbol{u}_{1}(x)|^{r-1}+|\boldsymbol{u}_{2}(x)|^{r-1}}}\right)|\boldsymbol{u}_{1}(x)-\boldsymbol{u}_{2}(x)|^{2}\/\mathrm{d}\/x

\displaystyle\leq 2^{r-2}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\big\||\boldsymbol{u}_{1}|^{\frac{r-1}{2}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2})\big\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+\big\||\boldsymbol{u}_{2}|^{\frac{r-1}{2}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2})\big\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}}\right).

From the above inequality, we have

(A.12)

\displaystyle\frac{2^{2-r}\beta}{2}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2}\|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}}^{r+1}\leq\frac{\beta}{2}\big\||\boldsymbol{u}_{1}|^{\frac{r-1}{2}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2})\big\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+\frac{\beta}{2}\big\||\boldsymbol{u}_{2}|^{\frac{r-1}{2}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2})\big\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}.

Thus, from (
A
) and (
A.12
), one can easily deduce that

(A.13)

\displaystyle\beta\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(|\boldsymbol{u}_{1}|^{r-1}\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-|\boldsymbol{u}_{2}|^{r-1}\boldsymbol{u}_{2},\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2}}}\right)\geq\frac{\beta}{2^{r-1}}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2}\|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}}^{r+1}.

Using the Cauchy-Schwarz and Young’s inequalities, we get

(A.14)

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left((f_{1}-f_{2})\boldsymbol{g},\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2}}}\right)\leq\frac{1}{2\alpha}|f_{1}-f_{2}|^{2}\|\boldsymbol{g}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}.

Making use of Hölder’s, Ladyzhenskaya’s, and Young’s inequalities, we have

\displaystyle\langle((\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2})\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{u}_{2},\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\|\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2}\|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{4}}^{2}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{2}\|_{\mathbb{V}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2}\|_{\mathbb{H}}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2}\|_{\mathbb{V}}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{2}\|_{\mathbb{V}}

(A.15)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mu}{2}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2}\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{2}+\frac{1}{\mu}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{2}\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{2}.

Substituting (
A.13
)-(
A
) in (
A
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\/\mathrm{d}\/}{\/\mathrm{d}\/t}\|(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2})(t)\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+\frac{\mu}{2}\|(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2})(t)\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\alpha}{2}\|(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2})(t)\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+\frac{\beta}{2^{r-1}}\|(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2})(t)\|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}}^{r+1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\alpha}|(f_{1}-f_{2})(t)|^{2}\|\boldsymbol{g}(t)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{\mu}\|(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2})(t)\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{2}(t)\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{2}.

Integrating the above inequality from
0
to
t
, we deduce

\displaystyle\|(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2})(t)\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+\mu\int_{0}^{t}\|(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2})(s)\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{2}\/\mathrm{d}\/s

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha\int_{0}^{t}\|(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2})(s)\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\/\mathrm{d}\/s+\frac{\beta}{2^{r-2}}\int_{0}^{t}\|(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2})(s)\|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{r+1}}^{r+1}\/\mathrm{d}\/s

\displaystyle\leq\|\boldsymbol{u}_{01}-\boldsymbol{u}_{02}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}|(f_{1}-f_{2})(t)|^{2}\|\boldsymbol{g}(s)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\/\mathrm{d}\/s

(A.16)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2}{\mu}\int_{0}^{t}\|(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2})(s)\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{2}(s)\|_{\mathbb{V}}^{2}\/\mathrm{d}\/s,

for all
t\in[0,T]
. Applying Gronwall’s inequality in (
A
) and then taking supremum over
0
to
T
on both sides, we finally have (
A.2
). One can easily deduce (
A.2
) by taking
\boldsymbol{u}_{01}=\boldsymbol{u}_{02}
in (
A.2
) and it completes the proof.
∎

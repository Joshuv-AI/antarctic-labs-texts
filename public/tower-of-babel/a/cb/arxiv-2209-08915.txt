Abstract
. This article concerns the random dynamics and asymptotic analysis of the well known mathematical model, ∂ 𝒗 ∂ t − ν Δ 𝒗 + ( 𝒗 ⋅ ∇ ) 𝒗 + ∇ p = 𝒇 , ∇ ⋅ 𝒗 = 0 , \displaystyle\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{v}}{\partial t}-\nu\Delta\boldsymbol{v}+(\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{v}+\nabla p=\boldsymbol{f},\ \nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v}=0, the Navier-Stokes equations. We consider the two-dimensional stochastic Navier-Stokes equations (SNSE) driven by a linear multiplicative white noise of Itô type on the whole space ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} . Firstly, we prove that the non-autonomous 2D SNSE generates a bi-spatial ( 𝕃 2 ​ ( ℝ 2 ) , ℍ 1 ​ ( ℝ 2 ) ) (\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})) -continuous random cocycle. Due to the bi-spatial continuity property of the random cocycle associated with SNSE, we show that if the initial data is in 𝕃 2 ​ ( ℝ 2 ) \mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}) , then there exists a unique bi-spatial ( 𝕃 2 ​ ( ℝ 2 ) , ℍ 1 ​ ( ℝ 2 ) ) (\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})) -pullback random attractor for non-autonomous SNSE which is compact and attracting not only in 𝕃 2 \mathbb{L}^{2} -norm but also in ℍ 1 \mathbb{H}^{1} -norm. Next, as a consequence of the existence of pullback random attractors, we prove the existence of a family of invariant sample measures for non-autonomous random dynamical system generated by 2D non-autonomous SNSE. Moreover, we show that the family of invariant sample measures satisfies a stochastic Liouville type theorem. Finally, we discuss the existence of an invariant measure for the random cocycle associated with 2D autonomous SNSE. We prove the uniqueness of invariant measures for 𝒇 = 𝟎 \boldsymbol{f}=\mathbf{0} and for any ν > 0 \nu>0 by using the linear multiplicative structure of the noise coefficient and exponential stability of solutions. The above results for SNSE defined on ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} are totally new, especially the results on bi-spatial random attractors and stochastic Liouville type theorem for 2D SNSE with linear multiplicative noise are obtained in any kind of domains for the first time. We observe that in contrast to Stratonovich noise, which is used widely in the literature to study the random dynamics of SNSE, Itô noise is more adequate in the case of whole space. This work settles down several open problems regarding random attractors, invariant measures and ergodicity for 2D SNSE on the whole space.

Introduction

1.1.
Literature survey and motivations

It is well known that the explicit solutions of ordinary/partial differential equations (ODE/PDE) are very difficult to find (cf.
[

]
and references therein). Therefore, one requires a qualitative theory to understand the asymptotic behavior of their solutions. The theory of attractors plays an important role to capture the long time behavior of the solutions of ODE/PDE. The theory of global attractors for the deterministic infinite-dimensional dynamical systems (DS) is well-investigated in
[
56
,
63
]
, etc. The theory of global attractors (for deterministic DS) has been extended to random attractors (for compact random DS, cf.
[

]
) in the works
[
10
,
11
]
etc., and it has been used for several physically relevant stochastic models (cf.
[
18
,
37
,
40
,
74
]
etc. and references therein). The author in
[
66
]
introduced a
sufficient and necessary criteria for the existence of a unique pullback random attractor
for non-compact non-autonomous random DS and applied it to stochastic reaction-diffusion equations driven by additive noise. Later, it has been applied to several stochastic models, cf.
[
29
,
39
,
67
,
68
,
70
]
, etc., and references therein.

The current work is mainly focused on the random dynamics of
autonomous and non-autonomous 2D stochastic Navier-Stokes equations (SNSE) driven by a linear multiplicative white noise
defined on the whole space. Given
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, we consider the following non-autonomous 2D SNSE on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
:

(1.1)

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{aligned} \/\mathrm{d}\/\boldsymbol{v}&=-\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[-\nu\Delta\boldsymbol{v}+(\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{v}+\nabla p-\boldsymbol{f}}}\right]\/\mathrm{d}\/t+\sigma\boldsymbol{v}\/\mathrm{d}\/\mathrm{W},\hskip 28.45274pt\text{ in }\ \mathbb{R}^{2}\times(\tau,\infty),\\
\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v}&=0,\hskip 240.42578pt\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{2}\times(\tau,\infty),\\
\boldsymbol{v}|_{t=\tau}&=\boldsymbol{v}_{\tau},\hskip 237.5805ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\ \text{ and }\ \tau\in\mathbb{R},\\
\boldsymbol{v}(x)&\to 0\hskip 242.70201pt\text{ as }\ |x|\to\infty,\end{aligned}}}\right.

where
\boldsymbol{v}(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
stands for the velocity field and
p(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}
denotes the pressure field, for all
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times(\tau,+\infty)

\boldsymbol{f}
is either autonomous (
\boldsymbol{f}(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{2},
for

\boldsymbol{f}(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times(\tau,+\infty)
). The coefficient
\nu>0
represents the

of the fluid and

\mathrm{W}=\mathrm{W}(t,\omega)

The dynamics of 2D deterministic/stochastic NSE including the existence of unique global/ pullback/random attractors also have a plenty of literature, cf.
[

,
29
,

,

,
68

[
12
,
57
,
63
]
etc.), bounded (
[

,

,

]
etc.), and unbounded Poincaré (
[

30
,
59
]
etc.) domains, and not on the whole space. Until now, the existence of random attractors is based on a transformation which converts a stochastic system into an equivalent pathwise deterministic system. Such types of transformations are available in the literature when the noise is either
additive or linear multiplicative

Itô sense
which is one of the main motivations to do this work on the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
The existence of global/pullback attractors for deterministic NSE and global/pullback random attractors for SNSE driven by additive noise on the whole space is still an open problem
.

The first aim of this work is to show the existence of a unique bi-spatial pullback random attractor for the non-autonomous SNSE (
1.1
) on the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Stochastic PDE such as 2D SNSE (cf.
[
49
]
), stochastic convective Brinkman-Forchheimer equations (cf.
[

), stochastic fractional power dissipative equation (cf.
[
75
]
), stochastic semilinear Laplacian equations (cf.
[
45

[
45
]
introduced the concept of
bi-spatial random attractor
which is an invariant and compact random set attracting the subsets of the initial space in the topology of the terminal space. Later, the authors in
[
14
]
generalized the work of
[
45
]
for non-autonomous random DS also, which has been applied to several physically relevant models, cf.
[
69
,
71
]
, etc., and references therein. Recently, the author in
[
75

introduced an abstract theory of bi-spatial pullback attractors for the bi-spatial continuous cocycle and successfully applied it to the stochastic fractional power dissipative equation. To the best of our knowledge, there is no result available in the literature on the
existence of a unique bi-spatial pullback random attractor for 2D SNSE either on Poincaré domains (bounded or unbounded) or on the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

The second goal of this paper is to prove the existence of
a family of invariant sample measures
(see Definition
4.5
below) which satisfies
a stochastic Liouville type theorem
(cf.
[
9
]
) for the 2D non-autonomous SNSE (
1.1
) on the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. This result is obtained with the aid of the existence of pullback random attractors and an abstract theory available in
[
9
, Theorem 3.1]
. Sufficient criteria for the existence of a family of invariant sample measures for autonomous and non-autonomous random DS (under the assumption on the existence of global/pullback random attractors) is established in
[
77
,
9
]
, respectively. In addition, the works
[
77
,
9
]
reveal that the family of invariant sample measures will be supported by the global/pullback random attractors. Note that if the random statistical equilibrium (that is, the instantaneous quantities still vary widely in time but average quantities seem stationary) has been reached by the 2D SNSE (
1.1
) (or the system (
2.11
) below), then the statistical information do not change with time (cf.
[
60
]
). In this situation, the shape of the pullback random attractor
\mathscr{A}(t,\omega)
changes randomly with respect to time along with the sample points
\omega\in\Omega,
but the measures of
\mathscr{A}(t,\omega)
on each time are the same with respect to the family of invariant sample measures. This is the stochastic (or random) version of the Liouville Theorem in Statistical Mechanics (cf.
[
77
]
for autonomous random DS and
[
9
]
for non-autonomous random DS). We refer readers to the works
[
60
,
76
]
for more detailed information about statistical equilibrium, trajectory statistical solutions and Liouville theorem in Statistical Mechanics (see Remark
4.14
also). An abstract theory for a
random Liouville type theorem
has been established in
[
9
, Theorem 5.1]
. Due to the fact that Brownian paths are continuous but nowhere differentiable, we cannot apply the abstract theory in
[
9
, Theorem 5.1]
directly to the system (
2.5
) with white noise. Therefore, we prove a stochastic version of Liouville type theorem and use the terminology
a stochastic Liouville type theorem
(cf.
[
9
, Subsection 6.3]
and Theorem
4.12
).

The final objective of this article is to demonstrate the
ergodic properties (via proving the existence of a unique invariant measure)
for the 2D autonomous SNSE (
1.1
) which is one of the most considerable qualitative properties of a dynamical system. The ergodic properties of the randomly forced NSE on torus or bounded domains have been extensively studied by several researchers during the past two decades. The first work on the ergodicity for 2D SNSE on bounded domains (for any
\nu>0
) driven by additive noise (see

[

]
, the author proved the same results as in
[
23
]
under weaker assumptions on the noise. Later, instead of taking extra assumptions on the noise, the author in
[

proved the ergodicity for 2D SNSE driven by additive noise for sufficiently large

. The papers
[
20
,
31
,
32
,
41
,
42
,
53
,
54
,
72
,
73

etc., discussed various results on the ergodic behavior of different types of partial differential equations covering 2D SNSE and the works
[

]

[
26
,
42
]

\nu>0
. In the literature, most of the works regarding the existence and uniqueness of invariant measures as well as ergodic behavior of 2D SNSE have been considered either on a torus or on bounded domains. In some of the works such as
[
2
,

etc., the authors established the existence of invariant measures for 2D SNSE driven by additive or multiplicative Itô noise on unbounded Poincaré domains, but they have not discussed the uniqueness. Recently, in

the existence and uniqueness of ergodic and strongly mixing invariant measures for 2D autonomous SNSE driven by a linear multiplicative noise in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is discussed for any
\nu>0
and the results are new in the context of multiplicative noise as well as for 2D SNSE on the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{2}

1.2.
Difficulties and approaches

If one considers a damped 2D deterministic/stochastic NSE with linear damping, that is,

\displaystyle\/\mathrm{d}\/\boldsymbol{v}+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[-\nu\Delta\boldsymbol{v}+(\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{v}+\alpha\boldsymbol{v}+\nabla p}}\right]=\boldsymbol{f}\/\mathrm{d}\/t+\sigma\boldsymbol{v}\/\mathrm{d}\/\mathrm{W},\ \nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v}=0,

where
\alpha>0
and
\sigma\geq 0
, then the existence of global/pullback/random attractors can be proved on the whole space (cf.
[
34
,
35
]
etc.). Even though we do not have any linear damping term in (
1.1
), by using a suitable transformation (see (
2.10
) below), we obtain an equivalent pathwise deterministic system (see (
2.11
) below) which helps us to prove the results on the whole space. It is an advantage of considering the stochastic system (
1.1
) in the sense of Itô, rather than Stratonovich.

In order to prove the bi-spatial
(\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
-pullback random attractors for the system (
1.1
), we need to estimate the nonlinear functional
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\boldsymbol{u}(x)\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{u}(x)\cdot\Delta\boldsymbol{u}(x)\/\mathrm{d}\/x
(see (
3
) below) in an appropriate way. We observe that one cannot use the estimates obtained in
[
64
, Chapter 2, section 2.3]
to estimate the above nonlinear term. In view of Hölder’s, Ladyzhenskaya’s (
[
43
, Lemma 1, Chapter I]
), Sobolev’s (
[
50
, Theorem 7, pp. 190]
) and interpolation (
[
50
, Theorem 6, pp. 200]
) inequalities, we estimate the above nonlinear term (see Remark
2.2
below). Note that for the periodic case, this integral is zero
[
64
, Lemma 3.1]
, and for the bounded or unbounded Poincaré domains case, one can use the estimates form
[
64
, Chapter 2, Section 2.3]
to obtain the results similar to this work. As the existence of pullback attractors for 2D deterministic NSE ((
1.1
) with
\sigma=0
) is still an open problem, the upper semicontinuity (as
\sigma\to 0
) of the obtained pullback random attractors is also an open problem (Remark
3.13
).

The existence of random attractors helps us to obtain the existence of invariant measures (see
[
11
, Corollary 4.6]
and Definition
4.3
below), and a family of invariant sample measures (
[
77
, Theorem 2.1]
,
[
9
, Theorem 5.1]
and Definition

1.1
) (cf.
[
11
,
77
]
etc.). As discussed above, due to the nowhere differentiability of Brownian paths, we do not apply
[
9
, Theorem 5.1]
directly. We use the Itô formula in infinite dimensions along with the invariant property of invariant sample measures to show that the family of invariant sample measures satisfies a stochastic Liouville type equation (see (
4.12

[

and
[

The main difficulties arise in the uniqueness of invariant measures part. We consider the deterministic forcing term
\boldsymbol{f}=\mathbf{0}
and obtain the uniqueness of invariant measure (Dirac measure centered at zero) for any
\nu>0,
where the linear multiplicative structure of the white noise coefficient and exponential stability of solutions (see Lemma

\boldsymbol{f}\neq\mathbf{0}
is still an open problem for 2D SNSE in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Novelties

Most of the results regarding the random dynamics and asymptotic analysis of 2D SNSE available in the literature are either on bounded domains (cf.

2.
Mathematical Formulation

In this section, we provide the necessary function spaces and operators needed to obtain the main results of this work. Further, we provide an abstract formulation of the system (
1.1
), a pathwise deterministic system equivalent to the system (
1.1
) (which helps us to define the non-autonomous random dynamical system) and the universe of tempered sets.

2.1.
Function spaces

We define the space
\mathcal{V}:=\{\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathrm{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2}):\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v}=0\},
where
\mathrm{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2})
denotes the space of all
\mathbb{R}^{2}
-valued infinite times differentiable functions with compact support in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Let
\mathbb{H}
and
\mathbb{V}
denote the completion of
\mathcal{V}
in
\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}):=\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
\mathbb{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}):=\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2})
norms, respectively. The spaces
\mathbb{H}
and
\mathbb{V}
are endowed with the norms
\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\boldsymbol{v}(x)|^{2}\/\mathrm{d}\/x
and
\|\boldsymbol{v}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{V}}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\boldsymbol{v}(x)|^{2}\/\mathrm{d}\/x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}(x)|^{2}\/\mathrm{d}\/x
, respectively. The inner product in the Hilbert space
\mathbb{H}
is represented by
(\cdot,\cdot)
, and the duality pairing between the spaces
\mathbb{V}
and
\mathbb{V}^{*}
is denoted by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle.
The
m^{\textrm{th}}
-order Sobolev spaces are denoted by
\mathbb{H}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2}):=\mathrm{H}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2})
with the norm
\|\boldsymbol{v}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}:=\sum\limits_{|\boldsymbol{k}|\leq m}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\mathrm{D}^{\boldsymbol{k}}\boldsymbol{v}(x)|^{2}\/\mathrm{d}\/x
, where
\boldsymbol{k}=(k_{1},k_{2})
is the multiindex and
|\boldsymbol{k}|=k_{1}+k_{2}
.

2.2.
Linear operator

Let
\mathcal{P}:\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\to\mathbb{H}
denote the Helmholtz-Hodge orthogonal projection (cf.
[
43
]
). The projection operator
\mathcal{P}:\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\to\mathbb{H}
can be expressed in terms of the Riesz transform (cf.
[
52
]
), and the operators
\mathcal{P}
and
\Delta
commutes, that is,
\mathcal{P}\Delta=\Delta\mathcal{P}
. Let us define the Stokes operator

\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{v}:=-\mathcal{P}\Delta\boldsymbol{v}=-\Delta\boldsymbol{v},\;\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{A}):=\mathbb{V}\cap\mathbb{H}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).

The operator
\mathrm{A}:\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{V}^{*}
is linear and continuous. Moreover, the usual norm of
\mathbb{H}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is equivalent to (cf.
[
50
, Proposition 1, pp. 169]
)

(2.1)

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{m}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\widehat{\boldsymbol{v}}(\xi)}}\right|^{2}\/\mathrm{d}\/\xi}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\|(\mathrm{I}-\Delta)^{\frac{m}{2}}\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\mathbb{H}}=\|(\mathrm{I}+\mathrm{A})^{\frac{m}{2}}\boldsymbol{v}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},

where
\widehat{\boldsymbol{v}}(\cdot)
is the Fourier transform of
\boldsymbol{v}(\cdot)
.

2.3.
Bilinear operator

Let us define the
trilinear form

b(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot):\mathbb{V}\times\mathbb{V}\times\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{R}
by

b(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{w})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\boldsymbol{u}(x)\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{v}(x)\cdot\boldsymbol{w}(x)\/\mathrm{d}\/x=\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\boldsymbol{u}_{i}(x)\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{v}_{j}(x)}{\partial x_{i}}\boldsymbol{w}_{j}(x)\/\mathrm{d}\/x.

An integration by parts gives

(2.2)

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{aligned} b(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{u})&=0,\ \text{ for all }\ \boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{V},\\
b(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{w})&=-b(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{w},\boldsymbol{v}),\ \text{ for all }\ \boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{w}\in\mathbb{V}.\end{aligned}}}\right.

If
\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}
are such that the linear map

is continuous on
\mathbb{V}
, the corresponding element of
\mathbb{V}^{*}
is denoted by
\mathrm{B}(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v})
. We also denote
\mathrm{B}(\boldsymbol{v})=\mathrm{B}(\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{v})=\mathcal{P}[(\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{v}]
.

Remark 2.1
.

Note that
\langle\mathrm{B}(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v}),\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v}\rangle=0
, which implies that

(2.3)

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{B}(\boldsymbol{u})-\mathrm{B}(\boldsymbol{v}),\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v}\rangle=\langle\mathrm{B}(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{v}),\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v}\rangle=-\langle\mathrm{B}(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v}),\boldsymbol{v}\rangle.

Remark 2.2
.

The following estimate on
b(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)
plays a crucial role in the sequel. Applying Hölder’s, Ladyzhenskaya’s (
[
43
, Lemma 1, Chapter I]
), Sobolev’s (
[
50
, Theorem 7, pp. 190]
) and interpolation (
[
50
, Theorem 6, pp. 200]
) inequalities, and (

), respectively, we obtain

\displaystyle|b(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{w})|

\displaystyle\leq\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|\boldsymbol{w}\|_{\mathbb{H}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\boldsymbol{u}\|^{1/2}_{\mathbb{H}}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}\|^{1/2}_{\mathbb{H}}\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|\boldsymbol{w}\|_{\mathbb{H}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\boldsymbol{u}\|^{1/2}_{\mathbb{H}}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}\|^{1/2}_{\mathbb{H}}\|\boldsymbol{v}\|^{1/2}_{\mathbb{V}}\|\boldsymbol{v}\|^{1/2}_{\mathbb{H}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|\boldsymbol{w}\|_{\mathbb{H}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\boldsymbol{u}\|^{1/2}_{\mathbb{H}}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}\|^{1/2}_{\mathbb{H}}\big(\|\boldsymbol{v}\|^{1/2}_{\mathbb{H}}+\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}\|^{1/2}_{\mathbb{H}}\big)\|(\mathrm{I}+\mathrm{A})\boldsymbol{v}\|^{1/2}_{\mathbb{H}}\|\boldsymbol{w}\|_{\mathbb{H}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\boldsymbol{u}\|^{1/2}_{\mathbb{H}}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}\|^{1/2}_{\mathbb{H}}\|\boldsymbol{v}\|^{1/2}_{\mathbb{H}}\big(\|\boldsymbol{v}\|^{1/2}_{\mathbb{H}}+\|\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{v}\|^{1/2}_{\mathbb{H}}\big)\|\boldsymbol{w}\|_{\mathbb{H}}

(2.4)

\displaystyle\quad+C\|\boldsymbol{u}\|^{1/2}_{\mathbb{H}}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}\|^{1/2}_{\mathbb{H}}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}\|^{1/2}_{\mathbb{H}}\big(\|\boldsymbol{v}\|^{1/2}_{\mathbb{H}}+\|\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{v}\|^{1/2}_{\mathbb{H}}\big)\|\boldsymbol{w}\|_{\mathbb{H}}.

Remark 2.5
.

One can use an another transformation to change the system (

) into an equivalent pathwise deterministic system. For a given

and
\omega\in\Omega
, let
\hat{z}(t,\omega)=e^{-\sigma\omega(t)}
. Then,
\hat{z}
satisfies the equation

\displaystyle\/\mathrm{d}\/\hat{z}=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\hat{z}\/\mathrm{d}\/t-\sigma\hat{z}\/\mathrm{d}\/\mathrm{W}.

Let
\boldsymbol{u}
be a new function given by

(2.15)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}(t;\tau,\omega,\boldsymbol{u}_{\tau})=\hat{z}(t,\omega)\boldsymbol{v}(t;\tau,\omega,\boldsymbol{v}_{\tau})\ \ \ \text{ with }\ \ \ \boldsymbol{u}_{\tau}=\hat{z}(\tau,\omega)\boldsymbol{v}_{\tau},

where
\boldsymbol{v}(t;\tau,\omega,\boldsymbol{v}_{\tau})
and
\hat{z}(t,\omega)
are the solutions of (

) and (
2.14
), respectively. Then
\boldsymbol{u}(\cdot;\tau,\omega,\boldsymbol{u}_{\tau})
satisfies the following system:

(2.16)

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\/\mathrm{d}\/\boldsymbol{u}}{\/\mathrm{d}\/t}+\nu\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{u}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\boldsymbol{u}+\frac{1}{\hat{z}(t,\omega)}\mathrm{B}\big(\boldsymbol{u}\big)&=\hat{z}(t,\omega)\boldsymbol{f},\quad t>\tau,\\

which is a pathwise deterministic system and is equivalent to the system (
2.5
). Even though the system (

) is easier to handle than the system (
2.11
), there is a technical difficulty in using the transformation (

). Since

is identified with
\mathrm{W}(t,\omega)
, the change of variable given by (
2.15
) involves the Wiener process explicitly. Therefore,
\hat{z}(t,\omega)
is not a stationary process and the change of variable given by (
2.15

2.15

is stationary, the change of variable is given by a homeomorphism (also called conjugation) which transforms one random dynamical system in an another equivalent one (cf.
[
25
, Proposition B.9]
). For this reason, it is appropriate to use the change of variable mentioned in (

). We also point out here that the authors in
[
27
]
used the transformation given (
2.14
) to study the global (or local) existence of smooth pathwise solutions 2D (or 3D) Euler equations. But they are not investigating any qualitative properties of the solutions (cf.
[
58
]
also).

Remark 3.13
.

We observe that the method introduced in
[
65
]
for the upper semicontinuity is not suitable for proving the upper semicontinuity of random attractors obtained in Theorem
3.12
as
\sigma\to 0
. Because the right hand side of (
3.8
) will tend to
\infty
as
\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\to 0
and we will not able to find a random set which contains
\bigcup\limits_{0<\sigma\leq 1}\mathcal{K}_{\sigma}(\tau,\omega)
, where

represents the absorbing set corresponding to each
\sigma
. Therefore the existence of pullback attractors for 2D deterministic NSE as well as the upper semicontinuity of random attractors for 2D SNSE on the whole space are still challenging open problems.

Remark 3.14
.

In
[
38
]
, the upper semicontinuity of the random attractors with respect to domains, that is, when domain changes from bounded to unbounded (Poincaré) domain is proved for stochastic convective Brinkman-Forchheimer equations (cf.
[
38
, Theorem 6.10]
). The authors have also discussed the upper semicontinuity of the random attractors with respect to domains for SNSE driven by additive noise as a remark (cf.
[
38
, Remark 6.11]
). Using the similar arguments as in the work
[
38
]
, one can establish the upper semicontinuity of the random attractors with respect to domains for the 2D non-autonomous SNSE (
2.5
) on the whole space.

Remark 3.15
.

The authors in
[
68
]
proved the asymptotic autonomy of pullback random attractors for 2D SNSE on unbounded Poincaré domains. They consider the additive as well as linear multiplicative white noise where the stochastic integration has been taken in the sense of Stratonovich. Using the similar ideas as in the work
[
68
]
, one can obtain the asymptotic autonomy robustness of random attractors for the 2D non-autonomous SNSE (
2.5
). It means that if
\mathscr{A}_{\infty}(\omega)
is the unique random attractor for the 2D autonomous SNSE (
2.5
), then one can prove that

(3.51)

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\to-\infty}\mathrm{dist}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathscr{A}(\tau,\omega),\mathscr{A}_{\infty}(\omega)}}\right)=0,\ \text{ for all }\ \omega\in\Omega,

where
\mathrm{dist}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes the Hausdorff semi-distance between two non-empty subsets of
\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
(cf.
[
68
]
for the detailed proof).

4.2.
A stochastic Liouville type theorem

Our next aim is to study

for the 2D non-autonomous SNSE (

). For this purpose, we require the definition of a class of test functions. Let us rewrite the first equation of the system (
2.5
) as

(4.17)

\displaystyle\/\mathrm{d}\/\boldsymbol{v}=L(\boldsymbol{v},t)\/\mathrm{d}\/t+\sigma\boldsymbol{v}\/\mathrm{d}\/\mathrm{W}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[-\nu\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{v}-\mathrm{B}(\boldsymbol{v})+\mathcal{P}\boldsymbol{f}}}\right]\/\mathrm{d}\/t+\sigma\boldsymbol{v}\/\mathrm{d}\/\mathrm{W}.

4.2.1.
Class of test function and its properties

Let us define the class
\mathscr{J}
of cylindrical test functions
\Lambda:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{R}
of the form

\displaystyle\Lambda(\boldsymbol{v}):=\psi((\boldsymbol{v},e_{1}),(\boldsymbol{v},e_{2}),\ldots,(\boldsymbol{v},e_{m})),

where
\psi
is a two times differentiable scalar valued function with compact support defined on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

, and
\{e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{m}\}\subset\mathcal{V}_{2}:=\{\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathrm{C}^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2}):\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v}=0\}
, where
\mathrm{C}^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2})
is two times differentiable
\mathbb{R}^{2}
-valued function with compact support (see
[
8
]
for detailed information of such types of test functions).
Moreover, the chain rule of differential calculus gives

(4.18)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{v})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\partial_{i}\psi((\boldsymbol{v},e_{1}),(\boldsymbol{v},e_{2}),\ldots,(\boldsymbol{v},e_{m}))e_{i},

(4.19)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\prime\prime}(\boldsymbol{v})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\partial_{i,j}\psi((\boldsymbol{v},e_{1}),(\boldsymbol{v},e_{2}),\ldots,(\boldsymbol{v},e_{m})){e_{i}\cdot e_{j}},

where
\partial_{i}\psi
and
\partial_{i,j}\psi
are the first-order and second-order derivatives of
\psi
with respect to
i
-th variable, and
i
-th and
j
-th variables, respectively.
Now, let
\boldsymbol{v}(\cdot)
be the solution of (
2.5
). Then, we infer from Itô’s formula (cf.
[
4
, Theorem 2.4]
) that for
t\geq\tau
and
\omega\in\Omega
,

\displaystyle\Lambda(\boldsymbol{v}(t))-\Lambda(\boldsymbol{v}(\tau))

\displaystyle=\int_{\tau}^{t}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\Lambda^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{v}(\xi)),\sigma\boldsymbol{v}(\xi)}}\right)\/\mathrm{d}\/\mathrm{W}(\xi)+\int_{\tau}^{t}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle\Lambda^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{v}(\xi)),L(\boldsymbol{v}(\xi),\xi)}}\right\rangle\/\mathrm{d}\/\xi

(4.20)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\tau}^{t}\Lambda^{\prime\prime}(\boldsymbol{v}(\xi))(\sigma\boldsymbol{v}(\xi))(\sigma\boldsymbol{v}(\xi))\/\mathrm{d}\/\xi.

Next lemma is useful to prove that the family of invariant sample measures obtained in Theorem

g\in\mathrm{C}_{b}(\mathbb{H})
,
\mathrm{T}_{t}g(\boldsymbol{x})\to g(\boldsymbol{x})
as
t\downarrow 0
.

Definition 5.3
.

A Borel probability measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{H}
is called an
invariant measure
for a Markov semigroup
\{\mathrm{T}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
of Feller operators on
\mathrm{C}_{b}(\mathbb{H})
if and only if

\mathrm{T}_{t}^{*}\mu=\mu,\ t\geq 0,

where
(\mathrm{T}_{t}^{*}\mu)(\Gamma)=\int_{\mathbb{H}}\mathrm{P}_{t}(\boldsymbol{y},\Gamma)\mu(\/\mathrm{d}\/\boldsymbol{y}),
for
\Gamma\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{H})
and
\mathrm{T}_{t}(\boldsymbol{y},\cdot)
is the transition probability,
\mathrm{T}_{t}(\boldsymbol{y},\Gamma)=\mathrm{T}_{t}(\chi_{\Gamma})(\boldsymbol{y}),\ \boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{H}
.

One can establish that
\Phi
is a Markov random DS (cf.
[
11
, Theorem 5.6]
), that is,
\mathrm{T}_{s_{1}+s_{2}}=\mathrm{T}_{s_{1}}\mathrm{T}_{s_{2}}
, for all
s_{1},s_{2}\geq 0
. It is known by Theorem
5.1
that there exists a Feller invariant probability measure
\mu
for a Markov random DS
\Phi
which has an invariant compact random set on a Polish space. Hence we have the following result due to Theorems
3.12
and
5.1
.

Theorem 5.4
.

Assume that
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Then, the Markov semigroup
\{\mathrm{T}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
induced by the flow
\Phi
on
\mathbb{H}
has an invariant measure
\upmu
. The associated flow-invariant Markov measure
\uprho
on
\Omega\times\mathbb{H}
has the property that its disintegration
\omega\mapsto\uprho_{\omega}
is supported by the attractor
\mathscr{A}(\omega)
, where
\mathscr{A}(\omega)
is a random attractor for autonomous SNSE (
1.1
).

Remark 5.5
.

Theorem
5.4
establishes the existence of invariant measures in
\mathbb{H}
. Since we have proved the existence of a unique
(\mathbb{H},\mathbb{V})
-pullback
\mathfrak{D}
-random attractor, it can be shown in a similar way (same as in the case of
\mathbb{H}
) that there exists an invariant measure for the 2D SNSE (
2.5
) in
\mathbb{V}
also.

5.2.
Uniqueness

In this section, we prove the uniqueness of invariant measures for the system (
2.5
) by using the linear structure of multiplicative noise and the exponential stability of solutions. For this purpose, we consider the external forcing
\boldsymbol{f}=\textbf{0}
in the system (
2.5
).

Lemma 5.6
.

Assume that
\boldsymbol{f}=\textbf{0}
. Then, there exists
T(\omega)>0
such that the solution of the system (
2.5
) satisfies the following exponential estimate:

(5.3)

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{v}_{1}(t)-\boldsymbol{v}_{2}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\leq e^{-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}t}\|\boldsymbol{v}_{1,0}-\boldsymbol{v}_{2,0}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}},\ \ \text{ for all }\ t\geq T(\omega),

where
\boldsymbol{v}_{1}(t):=\boldsymbol{v}_{1}(t;0,\omega,\boldsymbol{v}_{1,0})
and
\boldsymbol{v}_{2}(t):=\boldsymbol{v}_{2}(t;0,\omega,\boldsymbol{v}_{2,0})
be two solutions of the system (
2.11
) with respect to the initial data
\boldsymbol{v}_{1,0}
and
\boldsymbol{v}_{2,0}
at
0
, respectively.

Proof.

Let
\boldsymbol{u}_{1}(t):=z(t,\omega)\boldsymbol{v}_{1}(t)
and
\boldsymbol{u}_{2}(t):=z(t,\omega)\boldsymbol{v}_{2}(t)
be two solutions of the system (
2.11
) with respect to the initial data
\boldsymbol{u}_{1,0}=z(0,\omega)\boldsymbol{v}_{1,0}
and
\boldsymbol{u}_{2,0}=z(0,\omega)\boldsymbol{v}_{2,0}
at
0
, respectively. Then
\mathfrak{X}(\cdot)=\boldsymbol{u}_{1}(\cdot)-\boldsymbol{u}_{2}(\cdot)
with
\mathfrak{X}(0)=\boldsymbol{u}_{1,0}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2,0}
satisfies

(5.4)

\displaystyle\frac{\/\mathrm{d}\/\mathfrak{X}(t)}{\/\mathrm{d}\/t}+\nu\mathrm{A}\mathfrak{X}(t)+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}-\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)}}\right]\mathfrak{X}(t)=-z^{-1}(t,\omega)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\mathrm{B}\big(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}(t)\big)-\mathrm{B}\big(\boldsymbol{u}_{2}(t)\big)}}\right\},

for a.e.
t\geq 0
in
\mathbb{V}^{*}
. Multiplying (
5.4
) with
\mathfrak{X}(\cdot)
and then integrating over
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we infer

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\/\mathrm{d}\/}{\/\mathrm{d}\/t}\|\mathfrak{X}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}+\nu\|\nabla\mathfrak{X}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}-\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)}}\right]\|\mathfrak{X}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}

(5.5)

\displaystyle=-z^{-1}(t,\omega)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle\mathrm{B}\big(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}(t)\big)-\mathrm{B}\big(\boldsymbol{u}_{2}(t)\big),\mathfrak{X}(t)}}\right\rangle,

for a.e.
t\geq 0
. Using (
2.2
), (
2.3
), Hölder’s, Ladyzhenskaya’s and Young’s inequalities, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|z^{-1}(t,\omega)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle\mathrm{B}\big(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}\big)-\mathrm{B}\big(\boldsymbol{u}_{2}\big),\mathfrak{X}}}\right\rangle}}\right|

\displaystyle=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|z^{-1}(t,\omega)b(\mathfrak{X},\mathfrak{X},\boldsymbol{u}_{2})}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{1}{2}}z^{-1}(t,\omega)\|\mathfrak{X}\|_{\mathbb{H}}\|\nabla\mathfrak{X}\|_{\mathbb{H}}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{2}\|_{\mathbb{H}}

(5.6)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\nu}{2}\|\nabla\mathfrak{X}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}+\frac{z^{-2}(t,\omega)}{\nu}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{2}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\|\mathfrak{X}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}.

Making use of (
5.2
) in (
5.2
), we infer

\displaystyle\frac{\/\mathrm{d}\/}{\/\mathrm{d}\/t}\|\mathfrak{X}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\sigma^{2}-2\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)-\frac{2z^{-2}(t,\omega)}{\nu}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{2}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}}}\right]\|\mathfrak{X}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\leq 0,

for a.e.
t\geq 0
and for all
\omega\in\Omega
, and an application of variation of constant formula implies

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{X}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{-\sigma^{2}t+2\sigma\int_{0}^{t}y(\theta_{r}\omega)\/\mathrm{d}\/r+\frac{2}{\nu}\int_{0}^{t}e^{2\sigma y(\theta_{r}\omega)}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{2}(r)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\/\mathrm{d}\/r}}\right\}\|\mathfrak{X}(0)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}},

for all
t\geq 0
and
\omega\in\Omega
. Using the transformation (
5.1
) and the properties (
2.8
) of the continuous process
y(\cdot)
, we obtain that there exists a time
T_{1}(\omega)>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{v}_{1}(t)-\boldsymbol{v}_{2}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{-\sigma^{2}t+2\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)+2\sigma\int_{0}^{t}y(\theta_{r}\omega)\/\mathrm{d}\/r+\frac{2}{\nu}\int_{0}^{t}e^{2\sigma y(\theta_{r}\omega)}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{2}(r)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\/\mathrm{d}\/r-2\sigma y(\omega)}}\right\}

\displaystyle\quad\times\|\boldsymbol{v}_{1,0}-\boldsymbol{v}_{2,0}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}

(5.7)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}t+\frac{2}{\nu}\int_{0}^{t}e^{2\sigma y(\theta_{r}\omega)}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{2}(r)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\/\mathrm{d}\/r-2\sigma y(\omega)}}\right\}\|\boldsymbol{v}_{1,0}-\boldsymbol{v}_{2,0}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}},

for all
t\geq T_{1}(\omega)
and
\omega\in\Omega
. From the first equation of the system (
2.11
), (
2.2
) and Young’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\/\mathrm{d}\/}{\/\mathrm{d}\/t}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{2}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}-2\sigma y(\theta_{t}\omega)}}\right]\|\boldsymbol{u}_{2}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}+2\nu\|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{2}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\leq 0,

which gives (by variation of constant formula)

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{u}_{2}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}e^{\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}t-2\sigma\int_{0}^{t}y(\theta_{r}\omega)\/\mathrm{d}\/r}+2\nu\int_{0}^{t}e^{\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}s-2\sigma\int_{0}^{s}y(\theta_{r}\omega)\/\mathrm{d}\/r}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{2}(s)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\/\mathrm{d}\/s\leq\|\boldsymbol{u}_{2,0}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}},

\displaystyle\Rightarrow 2\nu\int_{\xi}^{t}e^{\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}s-2\sigma\int_{0}^{s}y(\theta_{r}\omega)\/\mathrm{d}\/r}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{2}(s)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\/\mathrm{d}\/s\leq\|\boldsymbol{u}_{2,0}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}},

for all
t\geq\xi\geq 0
and
\omega\in\Omega
. By the ergodicity property (
2.8
) of the continuous process
y(\cdot)
, we find
T_{2}(\omega)>0
such that

(5.8)

\displaystyle\int_{T_{2}}^{t}e^{\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}s}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{2}(s)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\/\mathrm{d}\/s\leq\int_{T_{2}}^{t}e^{\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}s-2\sigma\int_{0}^{s}y(\theta_{r}\omega)\/\mathrm{d}\/r}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{2}(s)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\/\mathrm{d}\/s

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\nu}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{2,0}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}},\ \ \mbox{ for all $t\geq T_{2}(\omega)$.}

From
\lim\limits_{t\to\pm\infty}\frac{|y(\theta_{t}\omega)|}{|t|}=0
, there exists a time
T_{3}(\omega)\geq T_{2}(\omega)
such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{2\sigma y(\theta_{r}\omega)}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{2}(r)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\/\mathrm{d}\/r

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T_{3}}e^{2\sigma y(\theta_{r}\omega)}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{2}(r)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\/\mathrm{d}\/r+\int_{T_{3}}^{t}e^{2\sigma y(\theta_{r}\omega)}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{2}(r)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\/\mathrm{d}\/r

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{r\in[0,T_{3}]}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[e^{2\sigma y(\theta_{r}\omega)}}}\right]\int_{0}^{T_{3}}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{2}(r)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\/\mathrm{d}\/r+\int_{T_{3}}^{t}e^{\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}r}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{2}(r)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\/\mathrm{d}\/r

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{r\in[0,T_{3}]}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[e^{2\sigma y(\theta_{r}\omega)}}}\right]\int_{0}^{T_{3}}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{2}(r)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\/\mathrm{d}\/r+\int_{T_{2}}^{t}e^{\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}r}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{2}(r)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\/\mathrm{d}\/r

(5.9)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}}\sup_{r\in[0,T_{3}]}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[e^{2\sigma y(\theta_{r}\omega)}}}\right]\rho(T_{3},\omega,\boldsymbol{u}_{2,0},\boldsymbol{f})+\frac{1}{2\nu}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{2,0}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}:=\rho^{*}(T_{3},\omega,\boldsymbol{u}_{2,0},\boldsymbol{f}),

for all
t\geq T_{3}(\omega)
and
\omega\in\Omega
, where we have used (
3
) and (
5.8
) in the final inequality. Let us choose
T_{4}(\omega)\geq\max\{T_{1}(\omega),T_{3}(\omega)\}
, then (
5.2
) and (
5.2
) imply

(5.10)

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{v}_{1}(t)-\boldsymbol{v}_{2}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\leq\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}t+\frac{2}{\nu}\rho^{*}(T_{3},\omega,\boldsymbol{u}_{2,0},\boldsymbol{f})-2\sigma y(\omega)}}\right\}\|\boldsymbol{v}_{1,0}-\boldsymbol{v}_{2,0}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}},

for all
t\geq T_{4}(\omega)
and
\omega\in\Omega
. Now, there exists a time
T(\omega)\geq T_{4}(\omega)
such that

(5.11)

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\nu}\rho^{*}(T_{3},\omega,\boldsymbol{u}_{2,0},\boldsymbol{f})-2\sigma y(\omega)\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}t,

for all
t\geq T(\omega)
and
\omega\in\Omega
. By (
5.10
)-(
5.11
), we have

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{v}_{1}(t)-\boldsymbol{v}_{2}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}\leq e^{-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}t}\|\boldsymbol{v}_{1,0}-\boldsymbol{v}_{2,0}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}},\ \ \ \text{ for all }t\geq T(\omega)\ \text{ and }\ \omega\in\Omega,

which completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 5.7
.

Assume that
\boldsymbol{f}=\textbf{0}
and
\boldsymbol{v}_{0}\in\mathbb{H}
be given. Then, there is a unique invariant measure to the system (
2.5
) which is ergodic and strongly mixing.

Proof.

For
\psi\in\text{Lip}(\mathbb{H})
(Lipschitz
\psi
) and an invariant measure
\upmu
, we have for all
t\geq T(\omega)
,

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\mathrm{T}_{t}\psi(\boldsymbol{v}_{0})-\int_{\mathbb{H}}\psi(\boldsymbol{u}_{0})\upmu(\/\mathrm{d}\/\boldsymbol{u}_{0})}}\right|

\displaystyle=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\mathbb{E}[\psi(\boldsymbol{v}(t,\boldsymbol{v}_{0}))]-\int_{\mathbb{H}}\mathrm{T}_{t}\psi(\boldsymbol{u}_{0})\upmu(\/\mathrm{d}\/\boldsymbol{u}_{0})}}\right|

\displaystyle=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\int_{\mathbb{H}}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\psi(\boldsymbol{v}(t,\boldsymbol{v}_{0}))-\psi(\boldsymbol{v}(t,\boldsymbol{u}_{0}))}}\right]\upmu(\/\mathrm{d}\/\boldsymbol{u}_{0})}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq L_{\psi}\int_{\mathbb{H}}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\boldsymbol{v}(t,\boldsymbol{v}_{0})-\boldsymbol{v}(t,\boldsymbol{u}_{0})}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}}}}\right]\upmu(\/\mathrm{d}\/\boldsymbol{u}_{0})

\displaystyle\leq L_{\psi}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[e^{-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{8}t}}}\right]\int_{\mathbb{H}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\boldsymbol{v}_{0}-\boldsymbol{u}_{0}}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}}\upmu(\/\mathrm{d}\/\boldsymbol{u}_{0})\to 0\ \text{ as }\ t\to\infty,

since
\int_{\mathbb{H}}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{0}\|_{\mathbb{H}}\upmu(\/\mathrm{d}\/\boldsymbol{u}_{0})+\int_{\mathbb{H}}\|\boldsymbol{v}_{0}\|_{\mathbb{H}}\upmu(\/\mathrm{d}\/\boldsymbol{u}_{0})<+\infty
. Hence, we conclude

(5.12)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathrm{T}_{t}\psi(\boldsymbol{v}_{0})=\int_{\mathbb{H}}\psi(\boldsymbol{u}_{0})\/\mathrm{d}\/\upmu(\boldsymbol{u}_{0}),\ \upmu\text{-a.s., for all }\ \boldsymbol{v}_{0}\in\mathbb{H}\ \text{ and }\ \psi\in\mathrm{C}_{b}(\mathbb{H}),

by the density of
\text{Lip}(\mathbb{H})
in
\mathrm{C}_{b}(\mathbb{H})
. Since we have a stronger result that
\mathrm{T}_{t}\psi(\boldsymbol{v}_{0})
converges exponentially fast to the equilibrium, this property is known as the
exponential mixing property
. Now suppose that
\widetilde{\upmu}
is an another invariant measure, then we have for all
t\geq T(\omega)
,

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\int_{\mathbb{H}}\psi(\boldsymbol{v}_{0})\upmu(\/\mathrm{d}\/\boldsymbol{v}_{0})-\int_{\mathbb{H}}\psi(\boldsymbol{u}_{0})\widetilde{\upmu}(\/\mathrm{d}\/\boldsymbol{u}_{0})}}\right|

\displaystyle=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\int_{\mathbb{H}}\mathrm{T}_{t}\psi(\boldsymbol{v}_{0})\upmu(\/\mathrm{d}\/\boldsymbol{v}_{0})-\int_{\mathbb{H}}\mathrm{T}_{t}\psi(\boldsymbol{u}_{0})\widetilde{\upmu}(\/\mathrm{d}\/\boldsymbol{u}_{0})}}\right|

\displaystyle=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\int_{\mathbb{H}}\int_{\mathbb{H}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathrm{T}_{t}\psi(\boldsymbol{v}_{0})-\mathrm{T}_{t}\psi(\boldsymbol{u}_{0})}}\right]\upmu(\/\mathrm{d}\/\boldsymbol{v}_{0})\widetilde{\upmu}(\/\mathrm{d}\/\boldsymbol{u}_{0})}}\right|

\displaystyle=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\int_{\mathbb{H}}\int_{\mathbb{H}}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\psi(\boldsymbol{v}(t,\boldsymbol{v}_{0}))-\psi(\boldsymbol{v}(t,\boldsymbol{u}_{0}))}}\right]\upmu(\/\mathrm{d}\/\boldsymbol{v}_{0})\widetilde{\upmu}(\/\mathrm{d}\/\boldsymbol{u}_{0})}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq L_{\psi}\int_{\mathbb{H}}\int_{\mathbb{H}}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\boldsymbol{v}(t,\boldsymbol{v}_{0})-\boldsymbol{v}(t,\boldsymbol{u}_{0})}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}}}}\right]\upmu(\/\mathrm{d}\/\boldsymbol{v}_{0})\widetilde{\upmu}(\/\mathrm{d}\/\boldsymbol{u}_{0})

\displaystyle\leq L_{\psi}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[e^{-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{8}t}}}\right]\int_{\mathbb{H}}\int_{\mathbb{H}}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\boldsymbol{v}_{0}-\boldsymbol{u}_{0}}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}}}}\right]\upmu(\/\mathrm{d}\/\boldsymbol{v}_{0})\widetilde{\upmu}(\/\mathrm{d}\/\boldsymbol{u}_{0})

(5.13)

\displaystyle\leq L_{\psi}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[e^{-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{8}t}}}\right]\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{H}}\|\boldsymbol{v}_{0}\|_{\mathbb{H}}\upmu(\/\mathrm{d}\/\boldsymbol{v}_{0})+\int_{\mathbb{H}}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{0}\|_{\mathbb{H}}\widetilde{\upmu}(\/\mathrm{d}\/\boldsymbol{u}_{0})\bigg)\to 0\ \text{ as }\ t\to\infty,

since
\int_{\mathbb{H}}\|\boldsymbol{v}_{0}\|_{\mathbb{H}}\upmu(\/\mathrm{d}\/\boldsymbol{v}_{0})<+\infty
and
\int_{\mathbb{H}}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{0}\|_{\mathbb{H}}\widetilde{\upmu}(\/\mathrm{d}\/\boldsymbol{u}_{0})<+\infty
. Since
\upmu
is the unique invariant measure for
(\mathrm{T}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, it follows from
[
15
, Theorem 3.2.6]
that
\upmu
is ergodic also.
∎

Remark 5.8
.

Since, for
\boldsymbol{f}=\mathbf{0}
, Theorem
5.7
demonstrates the unique invariant measure in
\mathbb{H}
. As
\mathbb{V}\subset\mathbb{H}
is a closed subspace, the uniqueness of invariant measure in
\mathbb{V}
also follows from Theorem
5.7
.

Remark 5.9
.

As zero solution is always a solution to the system (
1.1
) with zero initial data and
\boldsymbol{f}=\boldsymbol{0}
, it infers from Lemma
5.6
that the non-zero solutions to the system (
1.1
) are converging to
0
exponentially fast when time goes to
\infty
. Particularly, the unique ergodic measure of the system (
1.1
) with
\boldsymbol{f}=\boldsymbol{0}
is just the trivial one, that is, the Dirac delta measure centered at zero, which is obviously invariant since the zero solution is the steady state of the system. Similarly, the pullback random attractor is just a singleton set
\{0\}
.

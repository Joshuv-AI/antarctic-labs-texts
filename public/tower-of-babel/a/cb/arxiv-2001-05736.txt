1
Introduction

Let
G
be an infinite connected graph with vertex set
V
and let
\{S_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
be the simple random walk on
G
started at a distinguished vertex
o\in V
. Denote the law and expectation of this walk by
P
and
E
. Let
\{\xi(v):v\in V\}
be independent copies of a random variable
\xi
, which we denote as the scenery. Denote the law of the scenery by
\mathbb{P}
and the expectation with respect to this law by
\mathbb{E}
. We will always assume
\mathbb{E}\xi=0
and
\sigma^{2}=\mathbb{E}\xi^{2}>0
. The random walk in random scenery (RWRS) is the process
\{T_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
defined by

\displaystyle T_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\xi(S_{k})=\sum_{v\in V}\ell_{n}(v)\xi(v),

where
\ell_{n}(v)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathbbm{1}{\{S_{k}=v\}}
is the local time of
v
at time
n
. This process was introduced for the case
G=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
by Kesten and Spitzer
[
10
]
, and by Borodin
[
5
]
independently and at the same time in order to introduce new scaling and self-similar laws. However for
d\geq 3
, when the random walk by time
n
visits most points a constant amount of times,
[
10
]
showed under appropriate assumptions on the distribution of
\xi
that
T_{n}/\sqrt{n}
converges in distribution to a Gaussian random variable. More recently, large and moderate deviations of
T_{n}
have been studied for
G=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
in
[
2
,
3
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
. Fleischmann, Mortërs and Wachtel
[
8
]
proved a moderate deviations principle (MDP) for
d\geq 3
. Assuming Cramér’s condition, i.e. that there exists
\theta>0
such that
\mathbb{E}e^{\theta|\xi|}<\infty
, they showed that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}y_{n}^{-2}\log\mathbb{P}\otimes P(T_{n}/\sqrt{n}\geq y_{n})=-\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}(2G(0)-1)}

(1.1)

for
y_{n}=o(n^{1/6})
, where
G(\cdot)
is the Green’s function of the random walk. We write
a_{n}=o(b_{n})
or
a_{n}\ll b_{n}
if
\lim_{n\to\infty}a_{n}/b_{n}=0
for positive sequences
a_{n}
and
b_{n}
. In contrast with moderate deviations of sums of i.i.d. random variables, in
[
3
]
it was shown that this regime is maximal under Cramér’s condition. That is, more assumptions need to be made on the scenery in order to get moderate deviations when
y_{n}
grows faster than
n^{1/6}
.

There has been a recent interest in proving large deviations for sums of i.i.d. random variables under minimal moment assumptions. It is well understood that if one replaces the normalization constant by self-normalization, this is possible. In
[
13
]
, Shao provided self-normalized large and moderate deviations for the partial sum of i.i.d. random variables, while only making assumptions on the second moment. In
[
7
]
, Feng, Shao and Zeitouni extended this framework to RWRS by proving a Crámer type moderate deviations. Define

\displaystyle V_{n}^{2}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\xi^{2}(S_{k})=\sum_{v\in V}\ell_{n}(v)\xi^{2}(v)

and

\displaystyle L^{2}_{n,2}=\sum_{v\in V}\ell^{2}_{n}(v).

For the simple random walk on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for
d\geq 3
, it is known (see
[
8
,
10
]
) that

\displaystyle T_{n}/\sqrt{n}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{d}N(0,\sigma^{2}(2G(0)-1)),\kern 5.0ptL^{2}_{n,2}/n\xrightarrow{p}2G(0)-1\kern 5.0pt\text{and}\kern 5.0ptV_{n}^{2}/n\xrightarrow{p}\sigma^{2},

so that we have the self-normalized central limit theorem

\displaystyle\frac{T_{n}\sqrt{n}}{V_{n}L_{n,2}}\xrightarrow{d}N(0,1).

In
[
7
]
, they proved that if
d\geq 4
and there exists
\alpha>0
and
c_{\alpha}>0
such that
\mathbb{P}(\xi\geq t)\leq 2e^{-c_{\alpha}t^{\alpha}}
for
t>0
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\otimes P\left(\frac{T_{n}\sqrt{n}}{V_{n}L_{n,2}}\geq x\right)\sim 1-\Phi(x)

(1.2)

uniformly for
x\in[0,O(n^{\tau})]
and any
0<\tau<\alpha/(6\alpha+4)
. Here
\Phi(\cdot)
is the standard normal distribution function, and we write
a_{n}\sim b_{n}
if
\lim_{n\to\infty}a_{n}/b_{n}=1
for positive sequences
a_{n}
and
b_{n}
. By self-normalizing,
[
7
]
was able to achieve a MDP while only assuming sub-exponential tails on the scenery, which contrasts (
1.1
). However, for a suitable range of
y_{n}
, one would expect

\displaystyle-\infty<\liminf_{n\to\infty}y_{n}^{-2}\log\mathbb{P}\otimes P\left(\frac{T_{n}\sqrt{n}}{V_{n}L_{n,2}}\geq y_{n}\right)\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}y_{n}^{-2}\log\mathbb{P}\otimes P\left(\frac{T_{n}\sqrt{n}}{V_{n}L_{n,2}}\geq y_{n}\right)<0

(1.3)

under only finite moment assumptions on the scenery, i.e.
\mathbb{E}|\xi|^{\kappa}<\infty
for some fixed
\kappa>0
. In the same paper, the authors showed that if
\xi
has the probability density function
\frac{1}{2}\alpha(1+|t|)^{-1-\alpha}
for some
\alpha>0
and
(\log n)^{1/2}\ll y_{n}\ll n^{1/2}
, then

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}y_{n}^{-2d/(d+2)}(\log n)^{-2/(d+2)}\log\mathbb{P}\otimes P\left(\frac{T_{n}\sqrt{n}}{V_{n}L_{n,2}}\geq y_{n}\right)>-\infty.

(1.4)

Since
y_{n}^{2}\gg y_{n}^{2d/(d+2)}(\log n)^{2/(d+2)}
, this lower bound shows that moderate deviations of the form (
1.3
) does not hold when
y_{n}\gg(\log n)^{1/2}
if one is only to assume finite moment conditions on
\xi
.

The lower bound is achieved by considering the event in which the random walk occupies the ball
\{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:|z|\leq R\}
for time
y_{n}^{2}
, and taking each of the scenery values inside the ball to be of size
O(n)
. The probability of the former event is roughly
\exp(-y_{n}^{2}/R^{2})
, and for the latter event
n^{-R^{d}}=\exp(-R^{d}\log n)
. Since the random walk and the scenery are independent of one other, we get the lower bound
\exp(-y_{n}^{2}/R^{2}-R^{d}\log n)
which, optimized over
R
, yields (
1.4
). From this example, we observe that deviations of the self-normalized RWRS depend on the interplay between the scenery and the random walk. Furthermore, we see that this process is sensitive to the correlation of the local times due to the heavy tails of the scenery.

Motivated by this phenomenon, in this paper we study self-normalized moderate deviations for graphs other than the lattice. We expect that for graphs where the simple random walk has weakly-dependent local times, moderate deviations of the form (
1.3
) are attainable. A natural candidate for such graphs are trees, since regeneration epochs of the random walk on the tree have exponential tails (see Section
3
for definitions). Lastly, we also provide an upper bound that complements (
1.4
).

2
Main results

Let
\mathbb{T}_{d}
be the
d
-ary tree rooted at
o
, i.e.
\deg(v)=d+1
for
v\neq o
and
\deg(o)=d
. In this paper we will always assume
d\geq 2
. Since the simple random walk on
\mathbb{T}_{d}
is transient, it follows that for
G=\mathbb{T}_{d}
we have
T_{n}/\sqrt{n}
converges weakly to a normal random variable. The following theorem provides precise asymptotics for the self-normalized RWRS on
\mathbb{T}_{d}
while only making finite moment assumptions on the scenery.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
G=\mathbb{T}_{d}
and let
y_{n}
be a positive sequence such that
y_{n}\to\infty
.

1.

Suppose that
\mathbb{E}\xi^{4}<\infty
and
y_{n}=o(n^{1/6})
. We then have

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}y_{n}^{-2}\log\mathbb{P}\otimes P\left(\frac{T_{n}\sqrt{n}}{V_{n}L_{n,2}}\geq y_{n}\right)\leq-\frac{c_{d}}{2},

(2.1)

where
c_{d}
is a positive constant independent of
n
, and
c_{d}\uparrow 1
as
d\to\infty
.

2.

Suppose
\mathbb{E}\xi^{6}<\infty
and
y_{n}=o(n^{1/6})
. We then have

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}y_{n}^{-2}\log\mathbb{P}\otimes P\left(\frac{T_{n}\sqrt{n}}{V_{n}L_{n,2}}\geq y_{n}\right)\geq-\frac{1}{2}.

(2.2)

In light of (
1.4
), the following theorem provides a sharp upper bound for the self-normalized RWRS on the lattice when only assuming finite moment conditions on the scenery.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
G=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for
d\geq 3
and let
y_{n}
be a positive sequence such that
(\log n)^{1/2}\ll y_{n}\ll n^{1/6}
. If
\mathbb{E}\xi^{4}<\infty
, then

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}y_{n}^{-2d/(d+2)}(\log n)^{-2/(d+2)}\log\mathbb{P}\otimes P\left(\frac{T_{n}\sqrt{n}}{V_{n}L_{n,2}}\geq y_{n}\right)<0.

(2.3)

Remark 2.1
.

For Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
we assume that
y_{n}\ll n^{1/6}
. In this regime, the deviation comes from the moderate deviation of the scenery. When
y_{n}\gg n^{1/6}
, the deviation comes from large deviations for the local time statistics
L_{n,2}
and
L_{n,3}
, see Section
3
for definitions.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
3
, we study concentration inequalities for different local time statistics that will aid us in the proof of Theorem

2.1
. Our main tool will be the regeneration structure of the random walk on the tree. In Sections
4
and
5
, we prove the upper and lower bound of Theorem
2.1
. In Section
6
we review the necessary concentration inequalities for local time in the lattice, and in Section
7
we prove Theorem
2.2
.

3.1
Regeneration times

Our proofs for the concentration of local times will utilize the regenerative structure of the random walk on the tree. For
v\in V
, denote the level of
v
by
|v|
, which is the length of the unique geodesic between
v
and
o
. We define regeneration times as

\displaystyle\tau_{1}=\inf\{n\in\mathbb{N}:|S_{n}|\neq|S_{k}|\>\text{for all}\>k<n\>\text{and}\>|S_{k}|\neq|S_{n-1}|\>\text{for all}\>k>n\}

and for
j\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\tau_{j+1}=\inf\{n>\tau_{j}:|S_{n}|\neq|S_{k}|\>\text{for all}\>k<n\>\text{and}\>|S_{k}|\neq|S_{n-1}|\>\text{for all}\>k>n\}.

Regeneration times were studied in
[
6
,
11
]
for biased random walks on Galton-Watson trees. Rerunning the same proofs, we can conclude that the simple random walk on
\mathbb{T}_{d}
has infinitely many regeneration times such that
\{\tau_{j+1}-\tau_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
is an i.i.d. sequence which is independent of
\tau_{1}
, and that
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}-\tau_{1}
have exponential moments. The following lemma shows that as
d
tends to infinity, the tails of the regeneration times become lighter.

Lemma 3.1
.

Define

\displaystyle s_{d}=\sup\{\lambda\geq 0:Ee^{\lambda\tau_{1}}<\infty\>\>\text{and}\>\>Ee^{\lambda(\tau_{2}-\tau_{1})}<\infty\}.

We then have
s_{d}\geq\frac{1}{3}\log((d+1)/3)+\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1}{d+1}
.

Proof.

Since we are only concerned with the levels of
\mathbb{T}_{d}
, we can consider our random walk as a Markov chain on
\mathbb{N}_{0}
starting at
0
, with transition probabilities
p_{0,1}=1
,
p_{j,j+1}=d/(d+1)
and
p_{j,j-1}=1/(d+1)
for
j\in\mathbb{N}
. We claim that

\displaystyle\{\tau_{1}=k\}\subset\{\text{RW took at least $\lfloor k/3\rfloor$ steps backwards by time $k$}\}.

Observe that
|S_{\tau_{1}}|\leq\lfloor\tau_{1}/3\rfloor
. This is because for every
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
m<|S_{\tau_{1}}|
, the random walk must visit
m
at least twice. Now suppose by contradiction the random walk took less than
\lfloor k/3\rfloor
steps backwards, which means the random walk took more than
k-\lfloor k/3\rfloor
steps forward. This implies
|S_{\tau_{1}}|>\lfloor k/3\rfloor
, which is a contradiction. Applying the Chernoff inequality to the binomial random variable
Z
with parameters
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
1/(d+1)
, it follows that for all
x\geq k/(d+1)

\displaystyle P(Z>x)\leq\exp\left(-x\log\left(\frac{x}{k/(d+1)}\right)+x-\frac{k}{d+1}\right).

By our inclusion and the above inequality, we have

\displaystyle P(\tau_{1}=k)\leq P(Z\geq k/3)\leq\exp\left(-\frac{k}{3}\log((d+1)/3)+\frac{k}{3}-\frac{k}{d+1}\right).

Hence when
\lambda<\frac{1}{3}\log((d+1)/3)+\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1}{d+1}
, which is strictly positive when
d>2
, we have
Ee^{\lambda\tau_{1}}<\infty
. We are left to bound
P(\tau_{2}-\tau_{1}=k)
. Rerunning the proof of Lemma 4.3 in
[
6
]
, we conclude that there exists a constant
C>0
such that for every
k
we have
P(\tau_{2}-\tau_{1}=k)\leq C\cdot P(\tau_{1}=k)
. This finishes the proof.
∎

3.2
Concentration inequalities

We introduce the various local time statistics of the simple random walk on
\mathbb{T}_{d}
used throughout the remainder of this paper. The one we are most interested in is the size of the level sets of the local time

\displaystyle L_{n}(t)=|\{v\in V:\ell_{n}(v)>t\}|.

Let
\mathcal{R}_{n}=\{S_{0},\ldots,S_{n-1}\}
be the range of the random walk at time
n
. Setting
\lambda_{d}=s_{d}/2
, we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{(n)}=\mathcal{L}=\left\{v\in\mathcal{R}_{n}:\ell_{n}(v)<\frac{4}{\lambda_{d}}\log n\right\}

to be the set of vertices with small local time. Another statistic that appears throughout the proof is

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{n}=\sum_{v\in\mathcal{L}^{c}}\ell_{n}(v).

For
q\in\mathbb{N}
, denote the
q
-fold self-intersection local time by

\displaystyle L_{n,q}=\left(\sum_{v\in V}\ell_{n}^{q}(v)\right)^{1/q}.

The
q
-fold self-intersection local times often appear in the study of RWRS because they quantify the number of times the random walk visits the same sites, see
[
3
]
for a discussion for the case
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Throughout the paper, we will frequently use the fact that

\displaystyle L_{n,q}^{q}\geq n.

(3.1)

Lastly, denote the maximum of the local times by

\displaystyle L_{n,\infty}=\max_{v\in\mathcal{R}_{n}}\ell_{n}(v).

We will denote
\theta_{k}=\tau_{k}-\tau_{k-1}
for
k\geq 1
and
\theta_{1}=\tau_{1}
to be the regeneration epochs. Our main ingredient for deriving concentration inequalities for the local time will be the existence of regeneration epochs, and that they have exponential moments.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose
u\geq 1
and
t\geq 1
. There exists a constant
M>0
independent of
u
and
t
such that for
\beta\in(0,\lambda_{d}/2]
we have

\displaystyle E\exp\left(\beta\sum_{k=1}^{n}\theta_{k}\cdot\mathbbm{1}\{\theta_{k}>t\}\right)\leq\exp(Mn\exp(-\beta t/2)),

(3.2)

which implies

\displaystyle P(L_{n}(t)\geq u)\leq\exp(Mn\exp(-\beta t/2)-\beta tu).

(3.3)

Proof.

We begin by making a few observations. Firstly, at the
k
th regeneration epoch
\theta_{k}
, there are at most
\lfloor\theta_{k}/t\rfloor
vertices
v
satisfying
\ell_{n}(v)>t
. Secondly, any visited site is visited during a single regeneration epoch. Since by time
n
there are at most
n
regeneration times, we get

\displaystyle L_{n}(t)\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\lfloor\theta_{k}/t\right\rfloor\cdot\mathbbm{1}\{\theta_{k}>t\}.

Combining this inequality with the Chebyshev inequality, we have for any
\beta\in(0,\lambda_{d}]

\displaystyle P(L_{n}(t)\geq u)

\displaystyle\leq P\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\theta_{k}\cdot\mathbbm{1}\{\theta_{k}>t\}\geq ut\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp(-\beta ut)\prod_{k=1}^{n}E\exp\left(\beta\theta_{k}\cdot\mathbbm{1}\{\theta_{k}>t\}\right),

where the last inequality uses the fact that the regeneration epochs are independent. We are left to bound the exponential moment. Again by the Chebyshev inequality, we have
P(\theta_{k}>t)\leq M\exp(-\beta t)
for some positive constant
M
. Assuming
\beta\in(0,\lambda_{d}/2]
, we have

\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{n}E\exp\left(\beta\theta_{k}\cdot\mathbbm{1}\{\theta_{k}>t\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{k=1}^{n}\bigg(E[\exp\left(\beta\theta_{k}\right)\mathbbm{1}\{\theta_{k}>t\}]+1\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{k=1}^{n}\bigg((E\exp\left(2\beta\theta_{k}\right))^{1/2}P(\theta_{k}>t)^{1/2}+1\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\exp(Mn\exp(-\beta t/2)).

∎

Lemma 3.3
.

There exists an
M>0
such that for any
u\geq 1

\displaystyle P\left(\mathscr{L}_{n}\geq u\right)\leq M\exp(-\lambda_{d}u/2).

Proof.

We have the inequality
\mathscr{L}_{n}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\theta_{k}\cdot\mathbbm{1}\{\theta_{k}>\frac{4}{\lambda_{d}}\log n\}
. The Chebyshev inequality combined with an application of (
3.2
) finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose
x>1
. Then there exists a constant
c_{1}>0
independent of
n
and
x
such that

\displaystyle P(L_{n,\infty}\geq x)\leq n\exp(-c_{1}(x-1)).

Proof.

For
v\in V
, define
T^{+}_{v}=\inf\{n\geq 1:S_{n}=v\}
to be the return time of
v
and let
p_{v}=P_{v}(T^{+}_{v}=+\infty)
be the escape probability starting at
v
. Observe that the probability of escaping from
o
is the same as the probability of escaping from
v
conditioned on the event of
\{|S_{1}|>|v|\}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle p_{o}=P_{v}(T^{+}_{v}=+\infty||S_{1}|>|v|)=\frac{P_{v}(T_{v}^{+}=+\infty,|S_{1}|>|v|)}{P_{v}(|S_{1}|>|v|)}\leq\frac{P_{v}(T_{v}^{+}=+\infty)}{P_{v}(|S_{1}|>|v|)}=\frac{d+1}{d}p_{v}.

We now have the uniform lower bound
p_{v}\geq p_{o}d/(d+1).
By the strong Markov property, we have

\displaystyle P_{v}(\ell_{n}(v)\geq x)\leq(1-p_{v})^{x-1}\leq\left(1-\frac{d}{d+1}p_{o}\right)^{x-1}.

Using the same proof as Lemma 18 in
[
8
]
, we have

\displaystyle P(L_{n,\infty}\geq x)\leq\sum_{v\in V}P(\ell_{n}(v)\geq x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in V}\sum_{k=1}^{n}P(T_{v}^{+}=k)P_{v}(\ell_{n-k}(v)\geq x)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{v\in V}P_{v}(\ell_{n}(v)\geq x)\sum_{k=1}^{n}P(T_{v}^{+}=k)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\frac{d}{d+1}p_{o}\right)^{x-1}\sum_{v\in V}P(T_{v}^{+}\leq n).

We finish by observing
\sum_{v\in V}P(T_{v}^{+}\leq n)\leq\sum_{v\in V}\sum_{k=1}^{n}P(S_{k}=v)=n
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Fix an integer
q\geq 2
. There exist positive constants
B_{q}
and
c_{q}
such that

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}n^{-1/q}\log P\bigg(L_{n,q}^{q}\geq B_{q}n\bigg)\leq-c_{q}.

Proof.

Fix
B_{q}>0
and define the events
E=\{L_{n,q}^{q}\geq B_{q}n\}
and
F=\{L_{n,\infty}>n^{1/q}\}
. By Lemma
3.4
, we are left to bound
P(E\cap F^{c})
, which we will do by bounding the probability that the level sets of the local time are large. Define the sets

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{k}=\left\{v\in V:2^{k-1}<\ell_{n}(v)\leq 2^{k}\right\}

for
k=0,\ldots,\lceil\log_{2}(n^{1/q})\rceil=K_{2}
, and define the events

\displaystyle D_{k}

\displaystyle=\left\{|\mathcal{D}_{k}|>\frac{8Mn}{\lambda_{d}}\frac{e^{-\lambda_{d}2^{k-1}/8}}{2^{k-1}}\right\}\kern 5.0pt

\displaystyle\text{for}\kern 5.0ptk=0,\ldots,K_{1}=\lfloor\log_{2}(\frac{8}{\lambda_{d}}\log n)\rfloor

\displaystyle D_{k}

\displaystyle=\left\{|\mathcal{D}_{k}|>\frac{n^{1/q}}{2^{k-1}}\right\}\kern 5.0pt

\displaystyle\text{for}\kern 5.0ptk=K_{1}+1,\ldots,K_{2}.

For
k=1,\ldots,K_{1}
, we apply (
3.3
) for
\beta=\lambda_{d}/4
and get

\displaystyle P(D_{k})\leq P\left(L_{n}(2^{k-1})\geq\frac{8Mn}{\lambda_{d}}\frac{e^{-\lambda_{d}2^{k-1}/8}}{2^{k-1}}\right)\leq\exp\left(-Mne^{-\lambda_{d}2^{k-1}/8}\right)\leq\exp(-Mn^{1/2}).

For
v\in\mathcal{D}_{k}
for
k=K_{1}+1,\ldots,K_{2}
we have
\ell_{n}(v)\geq 4\log n/\lambda_{d}
, so we can apply (
3.3
) with parameter
\beta=\lambda_{d}/2
to get

\displaystyle P(D_{k})\leq P\left(L_{n}(2^{k-1})\geq\frac{n^{1/q}}{2^{k-1}}\right)\leq\exp\left(M-\frac{\lambda_{d}}{2}n^{1/q}\right).

By the union bound it follows that there exists
c_{q}>0
independent of
n
such that

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}n^{-1/q}\log P\left(\cup_{k=0}^{K_{2}}D_{k}\right)\leq-c_{q}.

It is left to show
E\cap F^{c}\subset\left(\cup_{k=0}^{K_{2}}D_{k}\right)\cap F^{c}
. On the event
F^{c}
, we have
V=\cup_{k=0}^{K_{2}}\mathcal{D}_{k}
. Therefore, on the event
\left(\cup_{k=0}^{K_{2}}D_{k}\right)^{c}\cap F^{c}
, we get

We define the partial sums

\displaystyle T_{n,1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{z\in\mathcal{L}\cap\mathcal{E}}\ell_{n}(z)\xi(z),

\displaystyle V^{2}_{n,1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{z\in\mathcal{L}\cap\mathcal{E}}\ell_{n}(z)\xi^{2}(z),

\displaystyle T_{n,2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{z\in\mathcal{L}\cap\mathcal{E}^{c}}\ell_{n}(z)\xi(z),

\displaystyle V^{2}_{n,2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{z\in\mathcal{L}\cap\mathcal{E}^{c}}\ell_{n}(z)\xi^{2}(z)

\displaystyle T_{n,3}

\displaystyle=\sum_{z\in\mathcal{L}^{c}}\ell_{n}(z)\xi(z),

\displaystyle V^{2}_{n,3}

\displaystyle=\sum_{z\in\mathcal{L}^{c}}\ell_{n}(z)\xi^{2}(z).

Applying (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\otimes P\left(T_{n}\sqrt{n}/(V_{n}L_{n,2})\geq y_{n}\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\otimes P\left(T_{n}/V_{n}\geq y_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\otimes P(T_{n,1}/V_{n}\geq y_{n}/3)+\mathbb{P}\otimes P(T_{n,2}/V_{n}\geq y_{n}/3)+\mathbb{P}\otimes P(T_{n,3}/V_{n}\geq y_{n}/3).

As in the proof of (
2.1
), we apply the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality and get that this bounded by

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\otimes P(T_{n,1}/V_{n}\geq y_{n}/3)+\mathbb{P}\otimes P\left(|\mathcal{E}^{c}|\geq y_{n}^{2d/(d+2)}\log n^{-d/(d+2)}/9\right)+P\left(\sum_{z\in\mathcal{L}^{c}}\ell_{n}(z)\geq y_{n}^{2}/9\right).

Reviewing the proof of Lemma
4.2
, we see that the conclusion holds for any random walk that satisfies
|\mathcal{R}_{n}|\leq n
. Since this holds for the simple random walk on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we can apply Lemma
4.2
with the assumption
\mathbb{E}\xi^{4}<\infty
, to get

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}y_{n}^{-2d/(d+2)}(\log n)^{-2/(d+2)}\log\mathbb{P}\otimes P(|\mathcal{E}^{c}|\geq y_{n}^{2d/(d+2)}\log n^{-d/(d+2)}/9)<0.

By Lemma
6.2
, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}y_{n}^{-2d/(d+2)}(\log n)^{-2/(d+2)}\log P\left(\sum_{z\in\mathcal{L}^{c}}\ell_{n}(z)\geq y_{n}^{2}/9\right)<0.

The rest of the proof is nearly identical to the proof of Lemma
4.1
. For
B>2G(0)-1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\otimes P\left(T_{n,1}/V_{n}\geq y_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\otimes P\left(\{V_{n}^{2}\leq n/4\}\cap\{L_{n,2}^{2}\leq Bn\}\right)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}\otimes P\left(\{T_{n,1}/n^{1/2}\geq y_{n}/2\}\cap\{L^{2}_{n,2}\leq Bn\}\right)+P(L_{n,2}^{2}>Bn)

\displaystyle\eqqcolon I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

By Lemma
6.3
and that
y_{n}=o(n^{1/6})
, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}y_{n}^{-2d/(d+2)}(\log n)^{-2/(d+2)}\log I_{3}=-\infty,

and by the same proof as in Lemma
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}y_{n}^{-2d/(d+2)}(\log n)^{-2/(d+2)}\log I_{1}=-\infty,

and so we are left to bound
I_{2}
. By Chebyshev’s inequality, for any
\alpha_{n}>0
we have

\displaystyle I_{2}\leq\exp(-y_{n}\alpha_{n}/2)\cdot E\left[\prod_{z\in\mathcal{L}}\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\ell_{n}(z)\xi(z)\frac{\alpha_{n}}{n^{1/2}}\mathbbm{1}\{z\in\mathcal{E}\}\right)\right]\mathbbm{1}\{L_{n,2}^{2}\leq B_{2}n\}\right].

We set
\alpha_{n}=y_{n}^{(d-2)/(d+2)}(\log n)^{2/(d+2)}
, and observe that there exists
C>0
such that for
z\in\mathcal{L}
, we have

\displaystyle\ell_{n}(z)\xi(z)\frac{\alpha_{n}}{n^{1/2}}\mathbbm{1}\{z\in\mathcal{E}\}\leq C/\log n.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for
x\leq 1
we have
e^{x}\leq 1+x+Cx^{2}
. Combining both facts yields the inequality

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\ell_{n}(z)\xi(z)\frac{\alpha_{n}}{n^{1/2}}\mathbbm{1}\{z\in\mathcal{E}\}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[1+\ell_{n}(z)\xi(z)\frac{\alpha_{n}}{n^{1/2}}\mathbbm{1}\{z\in\mathcal{E}\}+C\frac{\ell^{2}_{n}(z)}{n}\xi^{2}(z)\alpha_{n}^{2}\mathbbm{1}\{z\in\mathcal{E}\}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(C\frac{\ell_{n}^{2}(z)}{n}\alpha_{n}^{2}\right).

We thus have

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\exp(-y_{n}^{2d/(d+2)}(\log n)^{2/(d+2)}/2+CB_{2}y_{n}^{(2d-4)/(d+2)}(\log n)^{4/(d+2)}).

Observe that
y_{n}^{2d/(d+2)}(\log n)^{2/(d+2)}\gg y_{n}^{(2d-4)/(d+2)}(\log n)^{4/(d+2)}
precisely when
y^{2}_{n}\gg\log n
, which completes the proof.

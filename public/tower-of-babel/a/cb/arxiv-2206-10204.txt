Controllability of the Schrödinger equation on unbounded domains without geometric control condition
Matthias Täufer

Abstract
. We prove controllability of the Schrödinger equation in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} in any time T > 0 T>0 with internal control supported on nonempty, periodic, open sets. This demonstrates in particular that controllability of the Schrödinger equation in full space holds for a strictly larger class of control supports than for the wave equation and suggests that the control theory of Schrödinger equation in full space might be closer to the diffusive nature of the heat equation than to the ballistic nature of the wave equation. Our results are based on a combination of Floquet-Bloch theory with Ingham-type estimates on lacunary Fourier series.

\mathbb{R}^{d}

Consider the Schrödinger equation in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with internal control

(1)

\begin{cases}i\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}u+\Delta u=\mathbf{1}_{S}f&\quad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T]$},\\

\end{cases}

where

,

For any choice of

u(t)=\mathrm{e}^{it\Delta}u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{i(t-s)\Delta}\mathbf{1}_{S}f(s)\mathrm{d}s.

Definition 1

Equation (

Theorem 2

Let

Then (

Remark 3
.

Since the free Schrödinger group

is unitary, controllability in time

T

such that

Indeed, a null-control for the initial state

Therefore, from now on, we will focus on null-controllability and assume that the target state

Remark 5
.

The GCC is a strictly stronger condition that thickness. One example of thick sets not satisfying the GCC are periodic arrangements of balls with small radii as permitted in Theorem
2

In the literature, one often finds the GCC formulated in terms of geodesics for a Riemannian metric, i.e. tailored to controllability of differential operators of the form
\operatorname{div}A\nabla
instead of the pure Laplacian, and there are versions for domains or manifolds with non-empty boundary.
Since we are interested in the pure Laplacian in full space, we refrain from formulating these more general variants.
Also note that the GCC reflects the ballistic nature of the wave equation whereas thickness reflects the diffusive nature of the heat equation.

In 2016, Burq and Joly proved that the GCC is the necessary and sufficient criterion on the set

Now, in order to return to the interpretation of Theorem

Proofs

Without loss of generality, we may assume that the periodic set

is a union of balls of radius

S=\bigcup_{k\in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}^{d}}B_{R}(k).

By the Hilbert Uniqueness method

\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\mathbf{1}_{S}\mathrm{e}^{it\Delta}u\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\mathrm{d}t\quad\text{for all}\quad u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

2.1.
Floquet-Bloch decomposition

We use Floquet-Bloch theory to reduce the observability estimate (
4
) to a family of observability estimates on compact domains with varying boundary conditions.
Let
\mathbb{T}^{d}:=\mathbb{R}^{d}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z}^{d})\cong(-\pi,\pi]^{d}
denote the
d
-dimensional torus.

Definition 11
.

Denote by
\mathcal{F}\colon L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d})
the
Floquet transform

\displaystyle(\mathcal{F}u)(y,\theta)

\displaystyle={(2\pi)^{-d/2}}\sum_{k\in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\mathrm{e}^{i\theta k}u(y+k).

This is an isometric isomorphism that commutes with periodic functions in the sense that if
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is
2\pi\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-periodic, then

\mathcal{F}(f\cdot u)(y,\theta)=f\mid_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(y)\cdot(\mathcal{F}u)(y,\theta).

One also has for
u\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

\mathcal{F}(\Delta u)(y,\theta)=\Delta_{\theta}(\mathcal{F}u)(y,\theta)

for almost all
\theta\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
where
\Delta_{\theta}
is the
self-adjoint Laplacian on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
(in the
y
-coordinate) with
\theta
-pseudoperiodic boundary conditions
, that means

\mathcal{D}(\Delta_{\theta})=\left\{\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\colon\begin{aligned} &\text{there exists\ }\tilde{\phi}\in H^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\ \text{with}\ \tilde{\phi}\mid_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}=\phi,\ \text{and}\\
&\tilde{\phi}(x+2\pi k)=\exp(ik\cdot\theta)\tilde{\phi}(x)\ \text{for all $k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}$, $x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$}\end{aligned}\right\}.

Indeed, these statements can be seen by straightforward calculations on sufficiently smooth functions and the general case follows by density.
We also refer to
[
Sjö91
,
Kuc93
]
for further reading on Floquet theory for partial differential operators.
In particular, one also has for all
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{e}^{it\Delta}u)(y,\theta)=\mathrm{e}^{it\Delta_{\theta}}(\mathcal{F}u)(y,\theta).

The spectral decomposition of the operator
\Delta_{\theta}
is well-known:

Lemma 12
.

The spectral decomposition of
\Delta_{\theta}
is given by the eigenbasis

(\mathrm{e}^{i\gamma y})_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\theta}},\text{where}\ \Gamma_{\theta}:=\frac{\theta}{2\pi}+\mathbb{Z}^{d}

with corresponding eigenvalues

(\lvert\gamma\rvert^{2})_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\theta}}.

Proof.

Since
(\mathrm{e}^{iky})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
is an orthogonal basis of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, so is
(\mathrm{e}^{i\gamma y})_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\theta}}
.
The eigenvalue property and the
\theta
-pseudoperiodicity are verified by explicit computation.
∎

By isometry of the the Floquet transform, the l.h.s. of (
4
) becomes

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{S}\lvert(\mathrm{e}^{it\Delta}u)(x)\rvert^{2}\mathrm{d}x\ \mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}t\left\langle\mathrm{e}^{it\Delta}u,\mathbf{1}_{S}\mathrm{e}^{it\Delta}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}t\left\langle\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{e}^{it\Delta}u),\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{1}_{S}\mathrm{e}^{it\Delta}u)\right\rangle_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathrm{d}\theta\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}t\left\langle\mathrm{e}^{it\Delta_{\theta}}(\mathcal{F}u)(\cdot,\theta),\mathbf{1}_{B_{R}}\mathrm{e}^{it\Delta_{\theta}}(\mathcal{F}u)(\cdot,\theta)\right\rangle_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathrm{d}\theta\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}t\int_{B_{R}}\mathrm{d}y\lvert\mathrm{e}^{it\Delta_{\theta}}(\mathcal{F}u)(y,\theta)\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathrm{d}\theta\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}t\int_{B_{R}}\mathrm{d}y\big\lvert\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\theta}}\mathrm{e}^{i(t\lvert\gamma\rvert^{2}+y\gamma)}\alpha_{\theta,\tilde{\gamma}}\big\rvert^{2}

where the
\alpha_{\theta}=(\alpha_{\theta,\tilde{\gamma}})_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\theta}}\in\ell^{2}(\tilde{\Gamma}_{\theta})
are the Fourier coefficients of

\mathbb{T}^{d}\ni y\mapsto\mathcal{F}(u)(y,\theta)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})

with respect to the eigenbasis from Lemma
12
for fixed

.
Writing

\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\gamma\\

\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle=:z\cdot\tilde{\gamma},

3.
Thickness is necessary for Schrödinger

We give an elementary proof of Theorem
9
(previously demonstrated using a different method in
[
MPS20
]
) stating that in the case of controllability of the Schrödinger equation the control set
S
must be thick.

Proof.

By the Hilbert uniqueness method
[
Lio88
]
, (null-)controllability in time
T
is equivalent to the observability inequality

\lVert u\rVert^{2}\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\mathbf{1}_{S}\mathrm{e}^{it\Delta}u\rVert^{2}\mathrm{d}t\quad\text{for all}\quad u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Thus, it suffices to find for any
\epsilon>0
a function
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with

\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\mathbf{1}_{S}\mathrm{e}^{i\Delta t}u\rVert^{2}\mathrm{d}t\leq\epsilon\lVert u\rVert^{2}.

Clearly, we have for any
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\mathbf{1}_{S}\mathrm{e}^{i\Delta t}u\rVert^{2}\mathrm{d}t\leq 2\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\mathbf{1}_{S}\left[\mathrm{e}^{i\Delta t}-\mathrm{e}^{\Delta t}\right]u\rVert^{2}\mathrm{d}t+2\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\mathbf{1}_{S}\mathrm{e}^{\Delta t}u\rVert^{2}\mathrm{d}t=(A)+(B).

For
E>0
to be chosen below let
u=u_{1}+u_{2}
with
u_{1}
having Fourier support in
B_{E}(0)
and
u_{2}
Fourier support in
B_{E}^{c}(0)
.
Since
0\leq\mathbf{1}_{S}\leq\operatorname{Id}
and since the operator norm of
\mathbf{1}_{S}\left[\mathrm{e}^{i\Delta t}-\mathrm{e}^{\Delta t}\right]
is at most
2
, we can estimate

(A)\leq 2\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\left[\mathrm{e}^{i\Delta t}-\mathrm{e}^{\Delta t}\right]u_{1}\rVert^{2}\mathrm{d}t+4T\lVert u_{2}\rVert^{2}=(A_{1})+(A_{2})

By Plancherel, we further estimate

(A_{1})=2\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\left[\mathrm{e}^{-i\xi^{2}t}-\mathrm{e}^{-\xi^{2}t}\right]\widehat{u_{1}}\rVert^{2}\mathrm{d}t\leq 2T\max_{\lvert\xi\rvert\leq E,t\in[0,T]}\lvert\mathrm{e}^{-i\xi^{2}t}-\mathrm{e}^{-\xi^{2}t}\rvert\cdot\lVert u\rVert^{2}\leq cT^{2}E^{2}\lVert u\rVert^{2}.

Taking
E
sufficiently small (depending only on
T
) this can be made smaller than
\frac{\epsilon}{3}\lVert u\rVert^{2}
, independently of
u
.

We now choose
u
as a (square root of a) normalized Gaussian with standard deviation
\nu
, and center
x_{0}
that is

u(x):=\frac{1}{\left(2\pi\nu\right)^{d/4}}\exp\left(-\frac{\lVert x-x_{0}\rVert^{2}}{4\nu}\right).

where
\nu>0
,
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
will be chosen below.
We have

\widehat{u}(\xi)=\left(\frac{2\nu}{\pi}\right)^{d/4}\exp\left(-\xi^{2}\nu\right),\quad\text{and}\quad\widehat{u}_{2}(\xi)=\chi_{\lvert\xi\rvert\geq E}\left(\frac{2\nu}{\pi}\right)^{d/4}\exp\left(-\xi^{2}\nu\right).

The function
\hat{u}
is the (square root of a) Gaussian with standard deviation proportional to
\nu^{-1}
and for
\nu\gg 1
, its mass will concentrate on
B_{E}(0)
.
Hence, choosing
\nu
sufficiently large (depending on
E
which is already fixed), we obtain
(A_{2})=4T\lVert\widehat{u}_{2}\rVert^{2}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{3}\lVert u\rVert^{2}
.

It remains to estimate
(B)
.
This is the time evolution part of the heat equation and can be made small by putting
x_{0}
into a center of a sufficiently large (depending on
\epsilon
and
\nu
) ball on which
\omega
has little mass, as has been done for the heat equation, see for instance
[
EV18
]
for details.
Such a clearing with center
x_{0}
exists by assumption since
S
is assumed not to be thick.
∎

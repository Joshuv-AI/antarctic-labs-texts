Introduction

1.1.
Model description

Under suitable assumptions on amplitude, wavelength, wave steepness, and so on, the study on asymptotic models for water waves has been extensively investigated to understand the full water wave system; see, for instance,
[
1
,
6
,
7
]
and references therein for a rigorous justification of various asymptotic models for surface and internal waves.

Formulating the waves as a free boundary problem of the incompressible, irrotational Euler equation in an appropriate non-dimensional form, one has two non-dimensional parameters
\delta:=\frac{h}{\lambda}
and
\varepsilon:=\frac{a}{h}
, where the water depth, the wavelength and the amplitude of the free surface are parameterized as
h,\lambda
and
a
, respectively. Moreover, another non-dimensional parameter
\mu
is called the Bond number, which measures the importance of gravitational forces compared to surface tension forces. The physical condition
\delta\ll 1
characterizes the waves, called long waves or shallow water waves. In particular, considering the relations between
\varepsilon
and
\delta
, we can have two well-known regimes:

1.

Korteweg-de Vries (KdV):
\varepsilon=\delta^{2}\ll 1
and
\mu\neq\frac{1}{3}
.
Under this regime, Korteweg and de Vries
[
24
]

1

This equation was first introduced by Boussinesq
[
9
]
, and Korteweg and de Vries rediscovered it twenty years later.
derived the following well-known equation as a central equation among other dispersive or shallow water wave models called the KdV equation from the equations for capillary-gravity waves:

\pm 2\eta_{t}+3\eta\eta_{x}+\left(\frac{1}{3}-\mu\right)\eta_{xxx}=0.

2.

Kawahara:
\varepsilon=\delta^{4}\ll 1
and
\mu=\frac{1}{3}+\nu\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
In connection with the critical Bond number
\mu=\frac{1}{3}
, Hasimoto
[
16
]
derived a fifth-order KdV equation of the form

\pm 2\eta_{t}+3\eta\eta_{x}-\nu\eta_{xxx}+\frac{1}{45}\eta_{xxxxx}=0,

which is nowadays called the Kawahara equation.

In the last years, many authors have been interested in finding the behavior of solutions for the time-delayed KdV equation, time-delayed Kawahara equation, and other time-delayed dispersive systems see, for instance,
[
2
,
3
,
11
,
12
,
21
,
32
]
and the reference therein. In this article, our goal is to study general results for a higher-order dispersive equation which in some sense recovers the equations mentioned in the cited articles. Due to this advance for this type of dispersive equation, our main focus is to investigate the stabilization of the higher-order extension, for example, of KdV and Kawahara equations.

To be precise, we will consider the Cauchy problem for the following higher-order KdV-type equation posed in
\mathbb{R}
:

(1.1)

\begin{cases}u_{t}(x,t)+(-1)^{j+1}\partial_{x}^{2j+1}u(x,t)+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}(u^{2})=0,&(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R},\\

Specifically, (

and

(1.2)

\begin{cases}u_{t}(x,t)+(-1)^{j+1}\partial_{x}^{2j+1}u(x,t)+(-1)^{m}\partial_{x}^{2m}u(x,t)+\lambda_{0}(x)u(x,t)\\

u(x,s)=u_{0}(x,s)\,&\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}\times[-\tau,0],\end{cases}

where we often write
u_{0}(x)
instead of
u_{0}(x,0)
with
u_{0}
the initial value

Here, we consider the following compatibility condition
u_{0}(x,0)=u_{0}(x)
.
and
m\leq j
,
j,m\in\mathbb{N}
,
1\leq p<2j
. Here, the constant
\tau>0
is the time delay and the coefficients are considered with the following regularity

\lambda_{0}(x),\lambda(x)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}).

Thus, our main intention is to furnish sufficient conditions on the coefficients
\lambda,\lambda_{0}
to have well-posedness and exponential decay estimates for the model (
1.2
).

It is crucial to emphasize that the initial condition
u(x,s)=u_{0}(x,s)
must be taken into account, as it arises from the initial-boundary value problem for a transport equation. This is a classical setup, as detailed in
[
27

1.2
) is studied using the following change of variable:
z(x,\rho,t)=u(x,t-\rho\tau),
where
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
\rho\in(0,1)
, with
t>0
. Substituting this, the function
z(x,\rho,t)
satisfies the transport equation:

(1.3)

\begin{cases}\tau\partial_{t}z(x,\rho,t)+\partial_{\rho}z(x,\rho,t)=0,&x\in\mathbb{R},\rho\in(0,1),t>0,\\
z(x,0,t)=u(x,t),&x\in\mathbb{R},t>0,\\
z(x,\rho,0)=z_{0}(x,\rho,-\rho\tau),&x\in\mathbb{R},\rho\in(0,1),\end{cases}

where
z_{0}(x,\rho,-\rho\tau):=u_{0}(x,-\rho\tau)
. With this formulation, the linear system associated with (
1.2
) can be rewritten as an abstract problem. Let
U(t)=(u(\cdot,t),z(\cdot,\cdot,t))
and denote
z(1):=z(x,1,t)
. Incorporating the linear system derived from (
1.2
) and (
1.3

\begin{cases}u_{t}+(-1)^{j+1}\partial_{x}^{2j+1}u+(-1)^{m}\partial_{x}^{2m}u+\lambda_{0}(x)u+\lambda(x)z(1)=0,&(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty),\\

u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R},\\
z(x,\rho,0)=z_{0}(x,\rho,-\rho\tau),&x\in\mathbb{R},\rho\in(0,1),\end{cases}

which is equivalent to the abstract Cauchy problem:

\begin{cases}\frac{d}{dt}U(t)=AU(t),\\
U(0)=\left(u_{0}(x),z_{0}(x,-\rho\tau)\right),\end{cases}

where

and the dense domain
D(A)=\left\{U\in\mathcal{H}:AU\in\mathcal{H}\right\}
, where
\mathcal{H}
is an appropriately chosen Hilbert space. Henceforth, we will focus on analyzing the stabilization properties of the system (

1.2.
Review of the results in the literature

Let us briefly discuss the preceding works
[
2
,
3
,
11
,
12
,
21
,
32
]
and the results concerning the well-posedness of (
1.1
).

The local and global well-posedness of (
1.1
) has been widely studied.
The local well-posedness result was first proved by Gorsky and Himonas
[
15
]
for
s\geq-\frac{1}{2}
, and Hirayama
[
17
]
improved this to
s\geq-\frac{j}{2}
.
Both works were based on the standard Fourier restriction norm method.
Hirayama improved the bilinear estimate by using the factorization of the resonant function. The global well-posedness of (
1.1
) was established for
j=1,2
by Colliander et al.
[
13
]
and Kato
[
19
]
, respectively, via the “I-method”.
In
[
18
]
the authors extended the results of
[
13
]
and
[
19
]
to
j\geq 3
.
Their method follows the argument in
[
13
]
for the periodic KdV equation, while some estimates are slightly different.
They proved that the IVP (
1.1
) is globally well-posed in
H^{s}({\mathbb{T}})
for
j\geq 3
and
s\geq-\frac{j}{2}
.

To our knowledge, the only work concerning the control and stabilization properties for the system (
1.1
) was done by the first author with two collaborators in
[
10
]
.
The authors studied the local and global control results for (
1.1
) posed on the unit circle.
More precisely, they considered the system

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+(-1)^{j+1}\partial_{x}^{2j+1}u+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}(u^{2})=f(t,x),&(t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{T}},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),&x\in H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}),\end{cases}

posed on a periodic domain
\mathbb{T}
.
They showed the globally controllability in
H^{s}({\mathbb{T}})
, for
s\geq 0
by the forcing term

f(t,x):=g(x)\left(h(t,x)-\int_{\mathbb{T}}g(y)h(t,y)\;dy\right),

supported in a given open set
\omega\subset\mathbb{T}
, where
g
is a given nonnegative smooth function satisfying

\int_{\mathbb{T}}g\left(x\right)dx=1\quad\text{and}\quad\omega:=\left\{g>0\right\},

and
h
is the control input.

Considering the particular cases of the system (
1.2
), without delay and damping terms, we recall some results from the literature concerning the asymptotic properties.
The well-known Korteweg–de Vries–Burgers equation

(1.4)

u_{t}+u_{xxx}-u_{xx}+uu_{x}=0\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty),

corresponding to the case
j=m=1
, was extensively studied. For example, in
[
4
]
Amick
et. al
proved that the
L^{2}
-norm of the solutions to (
1.4
) tends to zero as
t\rightarrow\infty
in a polynomial way, namely

\left\lVert u(\cdot,t)\right\rVert_{2}\leq Ct^{-\frac{1}{2}}\quad\text{for all}\quad t>0,

with a positive constant
C.
More recently, Cavalcanti
et al.

[
12
]
studied the following damped KdV–Burgers equation:

(1.5)

\begin{cases}u_{t}(x,t)+u_{xxx}(x,t)-u_{xx}(x,t)+\lambda_{0}(x)u(x,t)+u(x,t)u_{x}(x,t)=0,&\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty),\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),&\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}.\end{cases}

Under appropriate conditions on the damping coefficient
\lambda_{0}
, the authors established its well-posedness and exponential stability for indefinite damping
\lambda_{0}(x),
giving exponential decay estimates on the
L^{2}-
norm of solutions.
In
[
14
]
, the results of
[
12
]
were extended by generalizing the nonlinear term of (
1.5
) and proving the global well-posedness and exponential stabilization of the generalized KdV-Burger equation under the presence of the localized and indefinite damping.
In
[
21
]
the authors consider the KdV-Burgers equation (
1.5
) in the presence of a delayed feedback
\lambda(x)u(x,t-\tau)
.
They considered the system with damping and delay feedback, showing the exponential decay estimates under appropriate conditions on the damping coefficients.

It is important to point out the exponential decay estimates obtained in
[
26
,
29
]
for the KdV equation posed in an interval with localized damping.
Also, periodic conditions have been considered in
[
20
,
23
]
while more general nonlinearities have been considered in
[
31
]
.
Additionally, the robustness concerning the delay of the boundary stability of the nonlinear KdV equation has been studied in
[
5
]
.
The authors obtain, under an appropriate condition on the feedback, with and without delay, the local stabilization results for the KdV equation with noncritical length.
Moreover, in
[
32
]
, the authors extended this result for the nonlinear Korteweg-de Vries equation in the presence of an internal delayed term.

In two recent articles
[
2
,
3
]
the authors studied the qualitative and numerical analysis of the following nonlinear fourth-order delayed dispersive equation in a bounded domain
I=[0,L]
with boundary and initial conditions:

(1.6)

\begin{cases}u_{t}(x,t)-\sigma u_{xx}(x,t)+\mu u_{xxxx}(x,t)+u(x,t-\tau)u_{x}(x,t)=0,&(x,t)\in I\times(0,\infty),\\
u(0,t)=u(L,t)=u_{x}(0,t)=u_{x}(L,t)=0,&t>0,\\
u(x,h)=f(x,h),&(x,h)\in I\times[-\tau,0].\end{cases}

The well-posedness, as well as the exponential stability of the zero solution of (
1.6
), was established in
[
2
]
.
The main ingredient of the proof was the exploitation of the Schauder Fixed Point Theorem.
This improved an earlier result
[
3
]
in the sense that no interior damping control was required.
Additionally, numerical simulations were also presented in this article to illustrate the theoretical result.

Finally, in a recent paper
[
11
]
the authors considered the Kawahara equation posed on a bounded interval under the presence of localized damping (
a\left(x\right)u(x,t)
) and delay (
b(x)u(x,t-h)
) terms. They proved its exponential stabilization under suitable assumptions. First, they showed the Kawahara system is exponentially stable under some restriction of the spatial length of the domain. Next, they introduced a more general delayed system and suitable energy functions, they proved, via the Lyapunov approach, the exponential stability for small initial data, under a restriction on the spatial length of the domain. Then they used a compactness-uniqueness argument to remove these hypotheses, thereby obtaining a semi-global stabilization result.

We end our review with a recent paper
[
22
]
where abstract linear and nonlinear evolutionary systems with feedback were studied.
Specifically, the authors considered the system

(1.7)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}U^{\prime}(t)=AU(t)+k(t)BU(t-\tau)+F(U(t)),&\text{ in }(0,+\infty).\\
U(0)=U_{0},&\text{ for }t\in(0,\tau),\\
BU(t-\tau)=f(t),&\end{array}\right.

where
A
generates an exponentially stable semigroup in a Hilbert space
H
,
B
is a continuous linear operator of
H
into itself,
k(t)\in L^{1}_{loc}(0,+\infty)
and
\tau>0
is a delay parameter, with
F:H\rightarrow H
is a Lipschitz function. The main purpose of
[
22
]
was to give a well-posedness result and an exponential decay estimate for the model (
1.7
) with a damping coefficient
k(t)
belonging only to
L^{1}_{loc}
. By using the semigroup approach combined with Gronwall’s inequality, under some mild assumptions on the involved functions and parameters, the well-posedness of the problem was established and an exponential decay estimate was obtained.

1.3.
Main results

With this state of the art, let us now present our main results which give a necessary next step to understanding the asymptotic behavior for generalized dispersive systems.

From now on, for the sake of simplicity, the norms in the spaces
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
and
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
will be denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{p}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
, respectively.
Furthermore, we introduce the Banach space

{\mathcal{B}}_{s,T}:=C([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{s+j}(\mathbb{R}))

with the natural norm

\|u\|_{\mathcal{B}_{s,T}}=\|u\|_{C([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))}+\|\partial^{s+j}_{x}u\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}.

Here, we will consider the

\|u\|_{H^{s}({\mathbb{R}})}=\|(1+\xi^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\hat{u}(\xi)\|_{2},

where
\hat{u}
denotes the Fourier transform of

[
25
]

For

The second main result is related to the exponential stabilization of (

(1.8)

\lambda_{0}(x)\geq\gamma_{0}\quad\text{for a.e.}\quad x\in\mathbb{R}

If the coefficient of the delay term

we can obtain the exponential decay estimates for the

{\mathcal{E}}(t):={\mathcal{E}}(u(t))=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}(x,t)dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{t-\tau}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-(t-s)}|\lambda(x)|u^{2}(x,s)\ dx\ ds.

In that case, the delay effect is compensated by the undelayed damping term (cf.

27
]

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\lambda_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying (
1.8
) and
\lambda\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying

\frac{e^{\tau}+1}{2}|\lambda(x)|\leq\gamma+\beta(x)\quad\text{for a.e.}\quad x\in\mathbb{R}

(1.10)

0\leq\gamma<\gamma_{0}\quad\text{and}\quad\left\lVert\beta\right\rVert_{q}<\Big(\frac{\gamma_{0}-\gamma}{c_{q}}\Big)^{1-\frac{1}{2qj}},\quad\text{where}\quad c_{q}:=\Big(1-\frac{1}{2qj}\Big)\Big(\frac{2^{2j-1}C^{2j}}{qj}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2qj-1}}.

Thus, the solution of (

{\mathcal{E}}(t)\leq C(u_{0},\tau)e^{-\nu_{j}t}.

(1.11)

\nu_{j}=\min\left\{2\Big(\gamma_{0}-\gamma-c_{q}\|\beta\|_{p}^{\frac{2qj}{2qj-1}}\Big),1\right\}

\lambda_{0}
is bounded from below by a positive constant, the next issue is to extend our results to the case where the coefficient of the undelayed feedback

Theorem 1.5
.

, the system (
1.2
) is exponentially stable. In other words, there exist a time

(1.17)

\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq\tilde{\gamma}(\|u_{0}\|_{2},T_{0})\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}({\mathbb{R}})}e^{-\eta(s)t},\quad\text{for $t\geq T_{0}$}.

Here,

is a continuous function.

Well-posedness theory

, for

The strategy is to establish the well-posedness results in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
and
H^{2j+1}({\mathbb{R}})
, respectively. After that, we will use interpolation arguments, due to Tartar
[

]
and adapted by Bona and Scott

, to achieve Theorem

.

2.1.
Linear system

For simplicity, consider
m=j\in\mathbb{N}
in the system (
1.2
). Let us start studying the following linear model associated with (
1.2
), namely

(2.1)

\begin{cases}u_{t}(x,t)+(-1)^{j+1}\partial_{x}^{2j+1}u(x,t)+(-1)^{j}\partial_{x}^{2j}u(x,t)+\lambda_{0}u(x,t)\\
\hskip 113.81102pt+\lambda u(x,t-\tau)=0,&\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty),\\
u(x,s)=u_{0}(x,s),&\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}\times[-\tau,0].\end{cases}

This section is devoted to proving the well-posedness via
semigroup theory
.We first take a look at the properties of the following operator

(2.2)

A_{\lambda_{0}}u:=-(-1)^{j+1}\partial_{x}^{2j+1}u(x,t){\color[rgb]{0,0,0}-}(-1)^{j}\partial_{x}^{2j}u(x,t)-\lambda_{0}u.

The following well-posedness result can be proved.

Proposition 2.1
.

If
\lambda_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}),
then the operator
A_{\lambda_{0}}
defined by (
2.2
) on
{\mathcal{D}}(A_{\lambda_{0}}):=H^{2j+1}(\mathbb{R})
generates a strongly continuous semigroup in the Hilbert space
H:=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}).

Proof.

Note that
A_{\lambda_{0}}=A+\bar{A}_{\lambda_{0}}
, where

Au=-(-1)^{j+1}\partial_{x}^{2j+1}u(x,t)-(-1)^{j}\partial_{x}^{2j}u(x,t)

and

\bar{A}_{\lambda_{0}}=-\lambda_{0}u.

It suffices to observe that
\bar{A}_{\lambda_{0}}
is a bounded perturbation of the operator
A
and to prove that
A
generates a strongly continuous semigroup in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}).
Indeed,
according to the Lumer-Phillips theorem it is sufficient to check that
A
is dissipative and that
I-A
is onto. The dissipativity follows by a direct computation: if
u_{0}\in H^{2j+1}(\mathbb{R})
is real-valued, then
u
is also real-valued and

\displaystyle(Au,u)_{H}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(-(-1)^{j+1}\partial_{x}^{2j+1}u{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}-}(-1)^{j}\partial_{x}^{2j}u\right)udx=-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(\partial^{j}_{x}u)^{2}dx\leqslant 0,

since we have

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{j+1}(\partial_{x}^{2j+1}u)udx=0.

Thanks to the fact that
\Re Av=A(\Re v)
for all
v\in H^{2j+1}(\mathbb{R})
, it follows that

\Re(Au,u)_{H}=-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(\partial^{j}_{x}u)^{2}dx\leqslant 0

for all
u_{0}\in H^{2j+1}(\mathbb{R})
.

In order to prove that
I-A
is onto, it remains to show that for every
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
there exists
u\in H^{2j+1}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying the equality

(-1)^{j+1}\partial_{x}^{2j+1}u(x,t)+(-1)^{j}\partial_{x}^{2j}u(x,t)+u=f.

Taking the Fourier transform in the previous equation, it is equivalent to

\hat{u}(\xi)=\frac{\hat{f}(\xi)}{1+((-1)^{j}(i\xi)^{2j})+(-1)^{j+1}(i\xi)^{2j+1}}.

This is possible by the fact that the denominator

h(\xi):=1+((-1)^{j}(i\xi)^{2j})+(-1)^{j+1}(i\xi)^{2j+1}

never vanishes. Since, moreover,
h(\xi)
is a continuous function satisfying
|h(\xi)|\rightarrow\infty
as
|\xi|\rightarrow\infty,1/h
is bounded, and therefore the last equation has a unique solution
\hat{u}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
Finally, since the function

\frac{(1+\xi^{2})^{2j+1}}{|h(\xi)|^{2}}=\frac{(1+\xi^{2})^{2j+1}}{\left|1+((-1)^{j}(i\xi)^{2j})+(-1)^{j+1}(i\xi)^{2j+1}\right|}

tends to 1 as
|\xi|\rightarrow\infty
and hence it is bounded by some constant
M
on
\mathbb{R}
, we conclude that

\|u\|_{H^{2j+1}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}=\|(1+\xi^{2})^{\frac{2j+1}{2}}\hat{u}\|^{2}_{2}\leqslant M\|\hat{f}\|_{2}^{2},\quad\text{for any }j\in\mathbb{N}.

Since
\hat{f}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, this implies the regularity property
u\in H^{2j+1}(\mathbb{R})
.
∎

Let us now use an iterative procedure (see e.g.
[
28
]
) and semigroup theory (see e.g.
[
30
]
), to prove that (
2.1
) is well-posed.

Theorem 2.2
.

If
\lambda_{0},\lambda\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and
u_{0}\in C([-\tau,0];H)
, then there exists a unique solution
u\in C([-\tau,+\infty);H)
of the problem (
2.1
).

Proof.

Consider the interval
[0,\tau].
Note that (
2.1
) can be seen as an inhomogeneous Cauchy problem of the form

\begin{cases}u_{t}(t)-A_{\lambda_{0}}u(t)=g_{0}(t),&\quad\text{in}\quad(0,\tau),\\
u(0)=u_{0},\end{cases}

with
g_{0}(t)=-\lambda u_{0}(t-\tau),
for
t\in[0,\tau],
which have unique solution
u(\cdot)\in C([0,\tau),H).
Now, considering the interval
t\in[\tau,2\tau]
, problem (
2.1
) can be rewritten as

\begin{cases}u_{t}(t)-A_{\lambda_{0}}u(t)=g_{1}(t),&\quad\text{in}\quad(\tau,2\tau),\\
u(\tau)=u({\tau}_{-}),\end{cases}

where
g_{1}(t)=-\lambda u(t-\tau).
Thanks to the first step of the proof the function
u(t)
, for
t\in[0,\tau]
, its known, thus
g_{1}(t)
can be considered as a known function for
t\in[\tau,2\tau].
Therefore, this analysis yields the existence of a solution
u(\cdot)\in C([0,2\tau],H).
By a bootstrap argument we get a solution
u\in C([0,\infty),H).

∎

2.2.
Non-homogeneous system

We are interested in extending the previous results to the nonlinear system (
1.2
). In this way, we first consider the corresponding linear inhomogeneous initial value problem

(2.3)

\begin{cases}u_{t}(x,t)+(-1)^{j+1}\partial_{x}^{2j+1}u(x,t)+(-1)^{j}\partial_{x}^{2j}u(x,t)+\lambda_{0}u(x,t)\\
\hskip 113.81102pt+\lambda u(x,t-\tau)=f(x,t),&\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}\times(0,T),\\
u(x,s)=u_{0}(x,s),&\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}\times[-\tau,0],\end{cases}

for some
T>0.
Consider the operator
A_{\lambda_{0}}
defined by Proposition
2.1
. So, we may rewrite (
2.3
) in the following way

(2.4)

\begin{cases}u_{t}(x,t)+\lambda u(x,t-\tau)=A_{\lambda_{0}}u(x,t)+f(x,t),&\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}\times(0,T),\\
u(x,s)=u_{0}(x,s),&\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}\times[-\tau,0].\end{cases}

Since
A_{\lambda_{0}}
generates a strongly continuous semigroup of contractions in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
by Proposition
2.1
, for any given value
u_{0}\in C([-\tau,0],H)
and
f\in L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R})),
problem (
2.4
) has a unique mild solution
u\in C([-\tau,T];L^{2}(\mathbb{R})),
satisfying Duhamel’s formula:

(2.5)

u(t)=S(t)u_{0}(0)-\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)\lambda u(s-\tau)\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)f(s)\,ds,\quad t\in[0,T].

Therefore, we can prove that the mild solution of (
2.4
) depends continuously on the initial data.

Proposition 2.3
.

If
u_{0}\in C([-\tau,0],L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))
and
f\in L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R})),
then the solution of (
2.4
)
satisfies the following estimate:

(2.6)

\|u\|_{C([0,T];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}\leq e^{\|\lambda\|_{\infty}T}\left(\|u_{0}\|_{2}+\|f\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}+\|\lambda\|_{\infty}\int_{-\tau}^{0}\|u(s)\|_{2}\,ds\right)

and

(2.7)

\|u\|_{C([-\tau,T];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}\leq C\left(\|u_{0}\|_{C([-\tau,0];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}+\|f\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}\right),

where
\lambda
is a function in
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
(see (
1.9
)) and
C
is a positive constant given by
C=C(\|\lambda\|_{\infty},T,\tau)
.

Proof.

The Duhamel’s formula (
2.5
) give us that

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{2}\leq

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{2}+\|\lambda\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s-\tau)\|_{2}\,ds+\|f\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{2}+\|f\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}+\|\lambda\|_{\infty}\int_{-\tau}^{0}\|u(s)\|_{2}\,ds+\|\lambda\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)\|_{2}\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{2}+\|f\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}+\|\lambda\|_{\infty}\int_{-\tau}^{t}\|u(s)\|_{2}\,ds.

Thus, the result follows as a direct application of Gronwall’s lemma.
∎

As a consequence of the previous inequality, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
u_{0}\in C([-\tau,0],L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))
and
f\in L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R})),
then the solution of (
2.4
) belongs to
{\mathcal{B}}_{T}
and the following estimate holds true:

(2.8)

\begin{split}\|u\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{T}}\leq C_{T}\left\{\|u_{0}\|_{2}+\|f\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}+\|\lambda\|_{\infty}\|u\|_{L^{1}(-\tau,0;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}\right.\\
\left.+\|\lambda\|_{\infty}^{1/2}\|u\|_{L^{2}(-\tau,0;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}\right\}\end{split}

where

(2.9)

C_{T}=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}\left(1+e^{2\|\lambda\|_{\infty}T}\right)^{1/2}e^{(\|\lambda\|_{\infty}+\|\lambda_{0}\|_{\infty})T}.

Moreover, we have

(2.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\|u(t)\|_{2}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial^{j}_{x}u\|^{2}_{2}ds&+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lambda_{0}u^{2}(x,s)dxds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lambda u(x,s-\tau)u(x,s)dxds\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x,s)u(x,s)dxds,\end{split}

for all
t\in[0,T]
,

Proof.

Multiplying the equation (
2.3
) by
u
and integrating by parts, taking into account that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}(-1)^{j+1}(\partial_{x}^{2j+1}u)udx=0\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}}(-1)^{j}\partial_{x}^{2j}u(x,t)udx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial^{j}_{x}u)^{2}dx,

the relation (
2.10
) holds.

Let us now prove (
2.8
). Thanks to (
2.6
) we infer that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|u(t)\|_{2}^{2}&+2\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial^{j}_{x}u\|_{2}^{2}ds\leq\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\\
&+2\|f\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}e^{\|\lambda\|_{\infty}T}\left(\|u_{0}\|_{2}+\|f\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}+\|\lambda\|_{\infty}\int_{-\tau}^{0}\|u(s)\|_{2}\,ds\right)\\
&+2\|\lambda_{0}\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)\|_{2}^{2}ds+\|\lambda\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s-\tau)\|_{2}^{2}ds+\|\lambda\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)\|_{2}^{2}ds.\end{split}

The previous inequality together with the following one

\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s-\tau)\|_{2}^{2}\,ds\leq\int_{-\tau}^{0}\|u(s)\|_{2}^{2}\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)\|_{2}^{2}\,ds,

ensures that

(2.11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|u(t)\|_{2}^{2}&+2\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial^{j}_{x}u\|_{2}^{2}ds\leq\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+\|f\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}^{2}\\
&+e^{2\|\lambda\|_{\infty}T}\left(\|u_{0}\|_{2}+\|f\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}+\|\lambda\|_{\infty}\|u\|_{L^{1}(-\tau,0;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}\right)^{2}\\
&+\|\lambda\|_{\infty}\|u\|_{L^{2}(-\tau,0;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}^{2}+2(\|\lambda\|_{\infty}+\|\lambda_{0}\|_{\infty})\int_{0}^{t}\|u\|^{2}_{2}ds.\end{split}

From (
2.11
) we have

(2.12)

\begin{split}\|u(t)\|_{2}^{2}&+2\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial^{j}_{x}u\|_{2}^{2}ds\leq 2(\|\lambda\|_{\infty}+\|\lambda_{0}\|_{\infty})\int_{0}^{t}\|u\|^{2}_{2}ds\\
&+\Big(1+e^{2\|\lambda\|_{\infty}T}\Big)\left\{\|u_{0}\|_{2}+\|f\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}\right.\\
&\left.+\|\lambda\|_{\infty}\|u\|_{L^{1}(-\tau,0;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}+\|\lambda\|_{\infty}^{1/2}\|u\|_{L^{2}(-\tau,0;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}\right\}^{2}.\end{split}

An application of Gronwall’s lemma gives the following:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|u(t)\|_{2}^{2}&+2\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial^{j}_{x}u\|_{2}^{2}\,ds\leq\left(1+e^{2\|\lambda\|_{\infty}T}\right)e^{2(\|\lambda\|_{\infty}+\|\lambda_{0}\|_{\infty})T}\times\\
&\times\Big\{\|u_{0}\|_{2}+\|f\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}+\|\lambda\|_{\infty}\|u\|_{L^{1}(-\tau,0;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}+\|\lambda\|_{\infty}^{1/2}\|u\|_{L^{2}(-\tau,0;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}\Big\}^{2},\end{split}

thus

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|u\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{T}}^{2}\leq&\frac{3}{2}\left(1+e^{2\|\lambda\|_{\infty}T}\right)e^{2(\|\lambda\|_{\infty}+\|\lambda_{0}\|_{\infty})T}\times\\
&\times\Big\{\|u_{0}\|_{2}+\|f\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}+\|\lambda\|_{\infty}\|u\|_{L^{1}(-\tau,0;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}+\|\lambda\|_{\infty}^{1/2}\|u\|_{L^{2}(-\tau,0;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}\Big\}^{2},\end{split}

and so

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|u\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{T}}\leq&\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}\left(1+e^{2\|\lambda\|_{\infty}T}\right)^{1/2}e^{(\|\lambda\|_{\infty}+\|\lambda_{0}\|_{\infty})T}\times\\
&\times\Big\{\|u_{0}\|_{2}+\|f\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}+\|\lambda\|_{\infty}\|u\|_{L^{1}(-\tau,0;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}+\|\lambda\|_{\infty}^{1/2}\|u\|_{L^{2}(-\tau,0;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}\Big\}\end{split}

showing (
2.8
) with
C_{T}
defined by (
2.9
).
∎

2.3.
Nonlinear estimates

In this subsection, we present some nonlinear estimates that will be used to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
1\leq p<2j
with
j\geq 1
. Then, there exists a positive constant
C
, such that, for any
T>0
and
u,v\in\mathcal{B}_{T}
, we have

\|u^{p}v_{x}\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}))}\leq 2^{\frac{p}{2}}CT^{\frac{2j-p}{4j}}T^{\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{j}\right)}\|u\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{p}\|v\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}.

Proof.

Recall that
H^{j}({\mathbb{R}})\hookrightarrow H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
for
j\geq 1
. On the other hand, note that
the following inequality holds:

(2.13)

\|v\|_{\infty}^{2}\leq 2\|v\|_{2}\|v_{x}\|_{2},

for all
v\in H^{j}(\mathbb{R})
. Indeed, if
v\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and
y\in\mathbb{R}
, then we have the following inequality:

(2.14)

\left|v(y)^{2}\right|=\left|\int_{-\infty}^{y}2vv_{x}dx\right|\leqslant 2\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|v|\cdot\left|v_{x}\right|dx\leqslant 2\|v\|_{2}\left\|v_{x}\right\|_{2},

proving our estimate for smooth functions. Note that the general case, that is, for
v\in H^{j}(\mathbb{R})
, follows by density. Thus, we have that

\displaystyle\|u^{p}v_{x}\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t)\|_{\infty}^{p}\|v_{x}(t)\|_{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{p}{2}}C\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t)\|_{2}^{\frac{p}{2}}\|u_{x}(t)\|_{2}^{\frac{p}{2}}\|v_{x}(t)\|_{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{p}{2}}C\|u\|_{C([0,T];L^{2})}^{\frac{p}{2}}\int_{0}^{T}\|u_{x}(t)\|_{2}^{\frac{p}{2}}\|v_{x}(t)\|_{2}dt.

We recall the following case of the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality:

(2.15)

\|\partial^{m}_{x}u\|_{2}\leq C\|\partial^{j}_{x}u\|^{\frac{m}{j}}_{2}\|u\|_{2}^{1-\frac{m}{j}},\quad m\leq j.

Hence, taking into account (
2.13
) and considering
m=1
in (
2.15
), we get that

(2.16)

\|v\|_{\infty}^{2}\leq 2\|v\|_{2}\|v_{x}\|_{2}\leq 2C\|\partial_{x}^{j}v\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{j}}\|v\|_{2}^{2-\frac{1}{j}}.

Moreover, by using (
2.15
) and Hölder inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\|u^{p}v_{x}\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}))}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{p}{2}}C\|u\|_{C([0,T];L^{2})}^{\frac{p}{2}}\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{x}^{j}u(t)\|_{2}^{\frac{p}{2j}}\|u(t)\|_{2}^{\frac{p}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{j}\right)}\|v_{x}(t)\|_{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{p}{2}}C\|u\|_{C([0,T];L^{2})}^{\frac{p}{2}\left(2-\frac{1}{j}\right)}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{x}^{j}u(t)\|_{2}^{\frac{p}{2j}.\frac{4j}{p}}\right)^{\frac{p}{4j}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|v_{x}(t)\|_{2}^{2}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}dt\right)^{\frac{2j-p}{4j}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{p}{2}}CT^{\frac{2j-p}{4j}}\|u\|_{C([0,T];L^{2})}^{\frac{p}{2}\left(2-\frac{1}{j}\right)}\|\partial_{x}^{j}u\|_{L^{2}([0,T];L^{2})}^{\frac{p}{2j}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{x}^{j}v(t)\|_{2}^{\frac{2}{j}}\|v(t)\|_{2}^{2\left(1-\frac{1}{j}\right)}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{p}{2}}CT^{\frac{2j-p}{4j}}\|u\|_{C([0,T];L^{2})}^{\frac{p}{2}\left(2-\frac{1}{j}\right)}\|\partial_{x}^{j}u\|_{L^{2}([0,T];L^{2})}^{\frac{p}{2j}}\|v\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{1-\frac{1}{j}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{x}^{j}v(t)\|_{2}^{\frac{2}{j}}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{p}{2}}CT^{\frac{2j-p}{4j}}\|u\|_{C([0,T];L^{2})}^{\frac{p}{2}\left(2-\frac{1}{j}\right)}\|\partial_{x}^{j}u\|_{L^{2}([0,T];L^{2})}^{\frac{p}{2j}}\|v\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{1-\frac{1}{j}}\left(\|\partial_{x}^{j}v(t)\|_{L^{2}([0,T];L^{2})}^{\frac{1}{j}}T^{\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{j}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{p}{2}}CT^{\frac{2j-p}{4j}}T^{\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{j}\right)}\|u\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{p}\|v\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}},

proving the result.
∎

The next lemma gives the estimates for the nonlinear terms.

Lemma 2.6
.

For any
T>0
,
1\leq p<2j
,
\lambda_{0}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
and
u,v,w\in\mathcal{B}_{T}
, we have

(i)

\|\lambda_{0}u\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}))}\leq T^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\lambda_{0}\|_{\infty}\|u\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}
;

(ii)

\|uw_{x}\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}))}\leq CT^{\frac{4j-3}{4j}}\|u\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}\|w\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}
;

(iii)

\|u|v|^{p-1}w_{x}\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}))}\leq 2^{\frac{p}{2}}CT^{\frac{2j-p}{4j}}T^{\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{j}\right)}\|u\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}\|w\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}\|v\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{p-1}
;

(iv)

The map

M:\mathcal{B}_{T}\rightarrow L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})),

defined by
Mu:=u^{p}u_{x}
, is locally Lipschitz continuous and satisfies the inequality

\begin{split}\|Mu-Mv\||_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}))}\leq&2^{\frac{p}{2}}T^{\frac{2j-p}{4j}}T^{\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{j}\right)}C\left(\|u\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{p}+\|u\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}\|v\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{p-1}+\|v\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{p}\right)\|u-v\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}\\
&+2^{\frac{1}{2}}T^{\frac{4j-3}{4j}}C\|u\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}\|u-v\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}},\end{split}

with a positive constant
C
.

Proof.

Note that (i) follows using Hölder inequality.
For (ii), observe that thanks to (
2.13
) and (
2.15
), we have

\displaystyle\|uw_{x}\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}))}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t)\|_{\infty}\|w_{x}(t)\|_{2}dt\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t)\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}\left(2-\frac{1}{j}\right)}\|\partial_{x}^{j}u(t)\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2j}}\|w_{x}(t)\|_{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq C\|u(t)\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{\frac{1}{2}\left(2-\frac{1}{j}\right)}\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{x}^{j}u(t)\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2j}}\|w(t)\|_{2}^{1-\frac{1}{j}}\|\partial_{x}^{j}w(t)\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{j}}dt

\displaystyle\leq C\|u(t)\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{\frac{1}{2}\left(2-\frac{1}{j}\right)}\|w(t)\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{1-\frac{1}{j}}\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{x}^{j}u(t)\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2j}}\partial_{x}^{j}\|w(t)\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{j}}dt

\displaystyle\leq C\|u(t)\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{\frac{1}{2}\left(2-\frac{1}{j}\right)}\|w(t)\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{1-\frac{1}{j}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{x}^{j}u(t)\|_{2}^{2}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{4j}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{x}^{j}w(t)\|_{2}^{2}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2j}}T^{\frac{4j-3}{4j}}

\displaystyle\leq CT^{\frac{4j-3}{4j}}\|u(t)\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{\frac{1}{2}\left(2-\frac{1}{j}\right)}\|w(t)\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{1-\frac{1}{j}}\|u(t)\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{\frac{1}{2j}}\|w(t)\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{\frac{1}{j}}.

Let us now prove (iii). Note that (
2.15
) implies that

\displaystyle\|u|v|^{p-1}w_{x}\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}))}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{p}{2}}\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t)\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{x}(t)\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|v(t)\|_{2}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\|v_{x}(t)\|_{2}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\|w_{x}(t)\|_{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{p}{2}}C\|u\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|v\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{x}^{j}u(t)\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2j}}\|u(t)\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{j}\right)}\|\partial_{x}^{j}v(t)\|_{2}^{\frac{p-1}{2j}}\|v(t)\|_{2}^{\frac{p-1}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{j}\right)}\|w_{x}(t)\|_{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{p}{2}}C\|u\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|v\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\|u\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{j}\right)}\|v\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{\frac{p-1}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{j}\right)}\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{x}^{j}u(t)\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2j}}\|\partial_{x}^{j}v(t)\|_{2}^{\frac{p-1}{2j}}\|w_{x}(t)\|_{2}dt

\displaystyle=2^{\frac{p}{2}}C\|u\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{\frac{1}{2}\left(2-\frac{1}{j}\right)}\|v\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{\frac{p-1}{2}\left(2-\frac{1}{j}\right)}\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{x}^{j}u(t)\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2j}}\|\partial_{x}^{j}v(t)\|_{2}^{\frac{p-1}{2j}}\|w_{x}(t)\|_{2}dt.

Thus, Hölder’s inequality ensures that

\|u|v|^{p-1}w_{x}\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}))}\\
\leq 2^{\frac{p}{2}}C\|u\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{\frac{1}{2}\left(2-\frac{1}{j}\right)}\|v\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{\frac{p-1}{2}\left(2-\frac{1}{j}\right)}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{x}^{j}u\|_{2}^{2}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{4j}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{x}^{j}v\|_{2}^{2}dt\right)^{\frac{p-1}{4j}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|w_{x}\|_{2}^{2}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}T^{\frac{2j-p}{4j}}.

The previous inequality and (
2.15
) give us

\displaystyle\|u|v|^{p-1}w_{x}\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}))}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 2^{\frac{p}{2}}CT^{\frac{2j-p}{4j}}\|u\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{\frac{1}{2}\left(2-\frac{1}{j}\right)}\|v\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{\frac{p-1}{2}\left(2-\frac{1}{j}\right)}\|u\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{\frac{1}{2j}}\|v\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{\frac{p-1}{2j}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|w\|_{2}^{2\left(1-\frac{1}{j}\right)}\|\partial_{x}^{j}w\|_{2}^{\frac{2}{j}}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 2^{\frac{p}{2}}CT^{\frac{2j-p}{4j}}\|u\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}\|v\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{p-1}\|w\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{1-\frac{1}{j}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{x}^{j}w\|_{2}^{2}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2j}}T^{\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{j}\right)}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 2^{\frac{p}{2}}CT^{\frac{2j-p}{4j}}T^{\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{j}\right)}\|u\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}\|v\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{p-1}\|w\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{1-\frac{1}{j}}\|w\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{\frac{1}{j}},

which allows us to get (iii). Finally,
using the mean value theorem, (ii), (iii) and Lemma
2.5
, we have

\displaystyle\|Mu-Mv\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2})}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C\|(1+|u|^{p-1}+|v|^{p-1})|u-v|u_{x}\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2})}+\|v^{p}(u-v)_{x}\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2})}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C\left\{T^{\frac{4j-3}{4j}}\|u-v\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}\|u\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}+2^{\frac{p}{2}}T^{\frac{2j-p}{4j}}T^{\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{j}\right)}\|u-v\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}\|u\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{p}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left.+2^{\frac{p}{2}}T^{\frac{2j-p}{4j}}T^{\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{j}\right)}\|u-v\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}\|u\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}\|v\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{p-1}+2^{\frac{p}{2}}T^{\frac{2j-p}{4j}}T^{\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{j}\right)}\|u-v\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}\|v\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{p}\right\},

and (iv) holds.
∎

2.4.
Nonlinear system

We are in a position to consider the nonlinear model (
1.2
), with
u_{0}\in C([-\tau,0];L^{2}(\mathbb{R})).
Before presenting it, let us introduce the following definitions:

Definition 2.7
.

A mild solution of (
1.2
) is a function
u\in{\mathcal{B}}_{T},

T>0,
which satisfies

\displaystyle u(t)

\displaystyle=S(t)u_{0}(0)-\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)\lambda u(s-\tau)ds-\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)u^{p}(s)\partial_{x}u(s)ds,\quad t\in[0,T].

A global mild solution of (
1.2
) is a function
u:[0,\infty)\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathbb{R})

whose restriction to every bounded interval
[0,T]
is a mild solution of (
1.2
).

2.4.1.
Well-posedness theory in
L^{2}({{\mathbb{R}}})

With these definitions and the previous estimates in hand, the following result gives the local well-posedness for the higher-order dispersive equation and an a priori estimate for the solutions of (
1.2
).

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
1\leq p<2j
with
j\geq 1
, and
\lambda_{0},\lambda\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
. For
u_{0}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
, there exist
T>0
and a unique mild solution
u\in\mathcal{B}_{T}
of (
1.2
), such that

\|u\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{T}}\leq C_{T}\left\{\|u(0)\|_{2}+\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\|\lambda\|_{\infty}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(-\tau,0;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}+\|\lambda\|_{\infty}^{1/2}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(-\tau,0;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}\right\}.

Here,
C_{T}
is given by (
2.9
).

Proof.

Let
T>0
be determined later and consider the operator
\Gamma:\mathcal{B}_{T}\longrightarrow\mathcal{B}_{T}
given by
\Gamma(u)=v
. For each
u\in\mathcal{B}_{T}
consider the problem

(2.17)

\begin{cases}v_{t}=A_{\lambda_{0}}v-\lambda v(x,t-\tau)-Mu,&\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}\times(0,T),\\
v(x,s)=u_{0}(x,s),&\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}\times[-\tau,0].\end{cases}

Note that
A_{\lambda_{0}}
generates a strongly continuous semigroup
\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
of contractions in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
. Moreover, Proposition
2.4
allows us to conclude that (
2.17
) has a unique mild solution
v\in B_{0,T}
, such that

(2.18)

\begin{split}\|v\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{T}}\leq&C_{T}\left\{\|u_{0}\|_{2}+\|Mu\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}\right.\\
&\left.+\|\lambda\|_{\infty}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(-\tau,0;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}+\|\lambda\|_{\infty}^{1/2}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(-\tau,0;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}\right\}\end{split}

where
C_{T}
is given by (
2.9
).
Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\|v(t)\|_{2}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial^{j}_{x}v\|^{2}_{2}ds&+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lambda_{0}v^{2}(x,s)dxds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lambda v(x,s-\tau)v(x,s)dxds\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}Mu(x,s)v(x,s)dxds.\end{split}

The idea now is to prove that the operator
\Gamma:\mathcal{B}_{T}\longrightarrow\mathcal{B}_{T}
is a contraction mapping. To see it, first, note that, thanks to the Lemma
2.5
and (
2.18
), we have

\|\Gamma u\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}\leq C_{T}\{\|u_{0}\|_{2}+2^{p/2}CT^{\frac{2j-p}{4j}}\|u\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{p+1}+\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\|\lambda\|_{\infty}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(-\tau,0;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}+\|\lambda\|_{\infty}^{1/2}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(-\tau,0;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}\},

and, for
u\in B_{R}(0):=\{u\in\mathcal{B}_{T}:\|u\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}\leq R\}
, it follows that

\|\Gamma u\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}\leq C_{T}\{\|u_{0}\|_{2}+2^{p/2}CT^{\frac{2j-p}{4j}}R^{p+1}+\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\|\lambda\|_{\infty}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(-\tau,0;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}+\|\lambda\|_{\infty}^{1/2}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(-\tau,0;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}\}.

Choosing

R=2C_{T}\left(\|u_{0}\|_{2}+\left(\|\lambda\|_{\infty}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(-\tau,0;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}+\|\lambda\|_{\infty}^{1/2}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(-\tau,0;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}\right)\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(-\tau,0;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}\right),

we obtain the following estimate

\|\Gamma u\|_{0,T}\leq\left(K_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\right)R,

where
K_{1}=K_{1}(T)=2^{p/2}C_{T}CT^{\frac{2j-p}{4j}}R^{p}.
On the other hand, note that
\Gamma u-\Gamma w
is solutions of

\begin{cases}v_{t}=A_{\lambda_{0}}v-\lambda v(t-\tau)-(Mu-Mv),&\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}\times(0,T),\\
v(x,s)=0,&\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}\times[-\tau,0].\end{cases}

We will now prove that
\Gamma
has a unique fixed point. To do that, note that thanks to Proposition
2.4
, we have

\displaystyle\|\Gamma u-\Gamma w\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}\|Mu-Mw\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2})}.

Lemma
2.6
, precisely estimate (iv), allows us to conclude that

\displaystyle\|\Gamma u-\Gamma w\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}\leq C_{T}C\left\{2^{\frac{p}{2}}T^{\frac{2j-p}{4j}}\left(\|u\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{p}+\|u\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}\|w\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{p-1}+\|w\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}^{p}\right)+2^{\frac{1}{2}}T^{\frac{1}{4}}\|u\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}\right\}\|u-w\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}.

Suppose that
u,w\in B_{R}(0)
defined above. Then,

\displaystyle\|\Gamma u-\Gamma w\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}

\displaystyle\leq K_{2}\|u-w\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}},

where

K_{2}=K_{2}(T)=C_{T}C\{2^{\frac{1}{2}}T^{\frac{1}{4}}R+3(2^{\frac{p}{2}})T^{\frac{2j-p}{4j}}R^{p}\}.

Since
K_{1}\leq K_{2}
, we can choose
T>0
to obtain
K_{2}<\frac{1}{2}
,

\|\Gamma u\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}\leq R\quad\text{and}\quad\|\Gamma u-\Gamma w\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}<\frac{1}{2}\|u-w\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}},

for all
u,w\in B_{R}(0)\subset B_{0,T}
. Hence,
\Gamma:B_{R}(0)\rightarrow B_{R}(0)
is a contraction and, by Banach fixed point theorem, we obtain a unique
u\in B_{R}(0)
, such that
\Gamma(u)=u
and consequently, the local well-posedness result for
0<T\leq\tau
small enough to the system. Thus,
u
is a unique local mild solution to the problem, and the estimate (
2.8
) holds.
∎

We are now able to present the main result of this subsection.

Theorem 2.9
.

Consider
\lambda_{0},\lambda\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying the hypotheses of Theorem
1.4
(or Theorem
1.5
). Then the system (
1.2
) admits a unique global mild solution for every initial datum
u_{0}\in C([-\tau,0];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))
satisfying

(2.19)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\|u(0)\|_{2}^{2}=&\frac{1}{2}\|u(t)\|_{2}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{x}\|^{2}_{2}\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lambda_{0}u^{2}(x,s)dxds\\
&+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lambda u(x,s-\tau)u(x,s)dxds\end{split}

for all
t\geq 0
. Moreover, there exists a non-decreasing continuous function
\beta_{0}:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, such that the solution
u
(
1.2
) satisfies

\|u\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{T}}\leq C_{T}\left\{\|u(0)\|_{2}+\|\lambda\|_{\infty}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(-\tau,0;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}+\|\lambda\|_{\infty}^{1/2}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(-\tau,0;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}\right\}

and

\|u\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{T}}\leq\beta_{0}(\|u_{0}\|_{2})\|u_{0}\|_{C([-\tau,0];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))},

with
\beta_{0}=C_{T}\left(1+\left(\|\lambda\|_{\infty}\tau^{1/2}+\|\lambda\|_{\infty}^{1/2}\right)\right)
and
C_{T}
given by (
2.9
).

Proof.

To prove the global well-posedness we need to prove that the norms of the solutions of (
1.2
) remain bounded in the existence time interval. To do that, let us consider the functional

(2.20)

{\mathcal{E}}(t):={\mathcal{E}}(u(t))=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}(x,t)dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{t-\tau}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-(t-s)}|\lambda(x)|u^{2}(x,s)\ dx\ ds.

Taking the time derivative in time of this function, we have that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d{\mathcal{E}}}{dt}(t)=&\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(t)((-1)^{j}\partial^{2j}_{x}u(t)-\lambda_{0}u(t)-\lambda u(t-\tau)+u^{p}(t)u_{x}(t))dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\lambda|u^{2}(t)dx\\
&-\frac{1}{2}e^{-\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\lambda|u^{2}(t-\tau)dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{t-\tau}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-(t-s)}|\lambda|u^{2}(x,s)dxds.\end{split}

Integrating by parts, using the Young inequality and taking into account the hypotheses (
1.8
), (
1.9
), and (
1.10
) we get that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d{\mathcal{E}}}{dt}(t)\leq&-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial^{j}_{x}u)^{2}(t)dx-\gamma_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}(t)dx+\frac{e^{\tau}+1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\lambda(x)|u^{2}(t)dx\\
&-\frac{1}{2}\int_{t-\tau}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-(t-s)}|\lambda|u^{2}(x,s)dxds\\
\leq&-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial^{j}_{x}u)(t)dx-(\gamma_{0}-\gamma)\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}(t)dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\beta(x)u^{2}(t)dx.\end{split}

Now, the Hölder inequality ensures that

(2.21)

\frac{d{\mathcal{E}}}{dt}(t)\leq-\|\partial^{j}_{x}u(t)\|_{2}^{2}-(\gamma_{0}-\gamma)\|u(t)\|_{2}^{2}+\|\beta\|_{q}\|u\|_{2q^{\prime}}^{2}

with
q^{\prime}=\frac{q}{q-1}.
Observing that

(2.22)

\|u\|_{2{q^{\prime}}}^{2}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}(u(t))^{2{q^{\prime}}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}(t)(u(t))^{\frac{2}{q-1}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\leq\|u\|_{2}^{\frac{2}{q^{\prime}}}\|u\|_{\infty}^{\frac{2}{{q^{\prime}}(q-1)}}=\|u\|_{2}^{\frac{2}{q^{\prime}}}\|u\|_{\infty}^{\frac{2}{q}}

and using (
2.22
) in (
2.21
), yields

(2.23)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d{\mathcal{E}}}{dt}(t)\leq&-\|\partial^{j}_{x}u(t)\|_{2}^{2}-(\gamma_{0}-\gamma)\|u(t)\|_{2}^{2}+\|\beta\|_{q}\|u(t)\|_{2}^{\frac{2}{q^{\prime}}}\|u(t)\|_{\infty}^{\frac{2}{q}}.\end{split}

Using (
2.13
) and (
2.15
) and applying Young’s inequality, we deduce from (
2.23
) for every fixed
\delta>0
the following estimates:

\begin{split}\frac{d{\mathcal{E}}}{dt}(t)\leq&-\|\partial^{j}_{x}u(t)\|_{2}^{2}-(\gamma_{0}-\gamma)\|u(t)\|_{2}^{2}+2^{1/q}\|\beta\|_{q}\|u\|_{2}^{\frac{2q-1}{q}}\|u_{x}\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{q}}\\
\leq&-\|\partial^{j}_{x}u(t)\|_{2}^{2}-(\gamma_{0}-\gamma)\|u(t)\|_{2}^{2}+2^{\frac{1}{q}}C^{\frac{1}{q}}\|\beta\|_{q}\|u\|_{2}^{\frac{2qj-1}{qj}}\|\partial_{x}^{j}u\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{qj}}\\
\leq&-\|\partial^{j}_{x}u(t)\|_{2}^{2}-(\gamma_{0}-\gamma)\|u(t)\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\Big(\frac{1}{\delta}\|\beta\|_{q}\|u\|_{2}^{\frac{2qj-1}{qj}}\Big)^{\frac{2qj}{2qj-1}}}{\frac{2qj}{2qj-1}}+\frac{\left(\delta 2^{\frac{1}{q}}C^{\frac{1}{q}}\|\partial_{x}^{j}u\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{qj}}\right)^{2qj}}{2qj}.\end{split}

Picking
\delta>0
such that
2^{2j}C^{2j}\delta^{2qj}=2qj,
this gives

\displaystyle\frac{d{\mathcal{E}}}{dt}(t)\leq

\displaystyle-\Big(\gamma_{0}-\gamma-\frac{2qj-1}{2qj}\Big(\frac{2^{2j-1}C^{2j}}{qj}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2qj-1}}\|\beta\|_{q}^{\frac{2qj}{2qj-1}}\Big)\|u(t)\|_{2}^{2}\leq 0.

This inequality ensures that
\|u(t)\|_{2}
remains bounded for
t\in[0,T].

Finally, thanks to the estimate (
2.19
), we deduce that
\|u\|_{\mathcal{B}_{T}}
remains bounded for
t\in[0,T]
, and so, the local solution
u
given by Proposition
2.8
can be extended on
[0,\tau].
Now, once we have a solution
u\in{\mathcal{B}}_{\tau}
we can apply the same arguments as we did on Theorem
2.2
to prove the existence of a global mild solution of (
1.2
). Finally, note that the proof of Proposition
2.8
guarantees the function
\beta_{0}
is given by

\displaystyle\beta_{0}(s)=C_{T}\left(1+\left(\|\lambda\|_{\infty}\tau^{1/2}+\|\lambda\|_{\infty}^{1/2}\right)\right),

completing the proof.
∎

2.4.2.
Well-posedness theory in
H^{2j+1}({\mathbb{R}})

We will analyze the well-posedness in
{\mathcal{B}}_{2j+1,T}
, with
1\leq p<2j
with
j\geq 1
. To do that, let us first consider the following linearized problem:

(2.24)

\begin{cases}v_{t}+(-1)^{j+1}\partial_{x}^{2j+1}v+(-1)^{m}\partial_{x}^{2m}v+\partial_{x}(u^{p}v)\\
\hskip 113.81102pt+\lambda_{0}(x)v+\lambda(x)v(x,t-\tau)=0,&\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty),\\
v(x,s)=v_{0}(x,s),&\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}\times[-\tau,0].\end{cases}

We can establish the following proposition:

Proposition 2.10
.

For
T>0
,
\lambda_{0},\lambda\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
, and
u\in{\mathcal{B}}_{T}
, if
v_{0}\in C([-\tau,0];L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}))
, then the system (
2.24
) admits a unique solution
v\in\mathcal{B}_{0,T}
, such that

(2.25)

\|v(t)\|_{C([-\tau,T];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}\leq C(\|\lambda\|_{\infty},T,\tau)\left(\|v_{0}\|_{C([-\tau,0];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}+(p+1)\|u\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{T}}^{p}\|v\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{T}}\right),

where
C(\|\lambda\|_{\infty},T,\tau)
is a positive constant and

(2.26)

\|v\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{T}}\leq C_{T}\left\{\|v(0)\|_{2}+\|u\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{T}}^{p}+\|\lambda\|_{\infty}\|v_{0}\|_{L^{1}(-\tau,0;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}+\|\lambda\|_{\infty}^{1/2}\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}(-\tau,0;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}\right\}

and

(2.27)

\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\|v\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{T}}\leq\sigma(\|u\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{T}},T)\left\{\|v(0)\|_{2}+\|\lambda\|_{\infty}\|v_{0}\|_{L^{1}(-\tau,0;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}+\|\lambda\|_{\infty}^{1/2}\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}(-\tau,0;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}\right\}.

Here,
1\leq p<2j
, with
j\geq 1
, and
\sigma:{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
is a non-decreasing continuous function.

Using them we obtain that

(2.43)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{x}^{j}(\lambda_{0}u)(t)\|_{2}^{2}dt\leq&\sum_{k=0}^{j}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}j\\
k\end{array}\right)\|\partial_{x}^{k}(\lambda_{0})\partial_{x}^{j-k}(u)(t)\|_{2}^{2}dt\\
=&\int_{0}^{T}\|\lambda_{0}\|_{\infty}^{2}\|\partial_{x}^{j}(u)(t)\|_{2}^{2}dt\\
&+\sum_{k=1}^{j}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}j\\
k\end{array}\right)\|\partial_{x}^{k}(\lambda_{0})\|_{2}^{2}\|\partial_{x}^{j-k}(u)(t)\|_{\infty}^{2}dt\\
\leq&C\|\lambda_{0}\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{x}^{j}(u)(t)\|_{2}^{2}dt\\
&+\sum_{k=1}^{j}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}j\\
k\end{array}\right)\|\lambda_{0}\|_{H^{k}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}\|u(t)\|_{H^{1+j-k}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}dt\\
\leq&CT\|\lambda_{0}\|_{H^{j}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}\|u\|_{C([0,T];H^{2j+1}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2},\end{split}

and similarly,

(2.44)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{x}^{j}(\lambda_{0}u)(t-\tau)\|_{2}^{2}dt\leq&C\|\lambda_{0}\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{x}^{j}(u)(t-\tau)\|_{2}^{2}dt\\
&+\sum_{k=1}^{j}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}j\\
k\end{array}\right)\|\lambda_{0}\|_{H^{k}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}\|u(t-\tau)\|_{H^{1+j-k}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}dt\\
=&C\|\lambda_{0}\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}\int_{-\tau}^{T-\tau}\|\partial_{x}^{j}(u)(t)\|_{2}^{2}dt\\
&+\sum_{k=1}^{j}\int_{-\tau}^{T-\tau}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}j\\
k\end{array}\right)\|\lambda_{0}\|_{H^{k}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}\|u(t)\|_{H^{1+j-k}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}dt\\
\leq&CT\|\lambda_{0}\|_{H^{j}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}\left(\|u\|_{C([0,T];H^{2j+1}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}+\int_{-\tau}^{0}\|u_{0}(t)\|_{H^{j}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}dt\right).\end{split}

Now, we concentrate on the estimate of the first integral on the right-hand side of (
2.42
).
Observing that

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{j}(u^{p}u_{x})=\sum_{k=0}^{j}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}j\\
k\end{array}\right)\partial_{x}^{k}(u^{p})\partial_{x}^{j-k}(u_{x}),

we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{x}^{j}(u^{p}u_{x})(t)\|_{2}^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{j}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}j\\
k\end{array}\right)\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{x}^{k}(u^{p})(t)\|_{\infty}^{2}\|\partial_{x}^{j-k}(u_{x})(t)\|_{2}^{2}dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{j}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}j\\
k\end{array}\right)\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{x}^{k}(u^{p})(t)\|_{\infty}^{2}\|u(t)\|_{H^{1+j-k}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k=0}^{j}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}j\\
k\end{array}\right)\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{x}^{k}(u^{p})(t)\|_{\infty}^{2}\|u(t)\|_{H^{2j+1}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}dt.

On the other hand, note that the
k
-order derivative of the function
u^{p}
can be written in the following way:

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{k}(u^{p})=C_{0,p}u^{p-1}\partial_{x}^{k}u+\sum_{n=1}^{k-1}C_{n,p}u^{p-n}F_{n}(u)+C_{k,p}u^{p-k}u_{x}^{k},

where the constants
C_{n,p}
are given by the formula

C_{n,p}=M_{n}\prod_{i=0}^{n}(p-i)

with
M_{n}\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and
F_{n}(u)
is a differential operator involving sums and products of derivatives of
u
with order less than
n+1
. Thanks to this fact, we have the following estimate:

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}^{k}(u^{p})\|_{\infty}\leq

\displaystyle\ |C_{0,p}|\|u\|_{\infty}^{p-1}\|\partial_{x}^{k}u\|_{\infty}+\sum_{n=1}^{k-1}|C_{n,p}|\|u\|_{\infty}^{p-n}\|F_{n}(u)\|_{\infty}+|C_{k,p}|\|u\|_{\infty}^{p-k}\|u_{x}\|_{\infty}^{k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ C\left(|C_{0,p}|\|u\|_{H^{2j+1}({\mathbb{R}})}^{p-1}\|u\|_{H^{k+1}({\mathbb{R}})}+\sum_{n=1}^{k-1}|C_{n,p}|\|u\|_{H^{2j+1}({\mathbb{R}})}^{p-n}\|u\|_{H^{n+2}({\mathbb{R}})}^{m(k)}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+|C_{k,p}|\|u\|_{H^{2j+1}({\mathbb{R}})}^{p-k}\|u\|_{H^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}^{k}\right),

where
m(n)\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
C>0
is a constant. Since

H^{2j+1}({\mathbb{R}})\subset H^{k+1}({\mathbb{R}}),\quad H^{2j+1}({\mathbb{R}})\subset H^{n+1}({\mathbb{R}})\ \text{ and }\ H^{2j+1}({\mathbb{R}})\subset H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}),

it follows that

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}^{k}(u^{p})\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(|C_{0,p}|\|u\|_{H^{2j+1}({\mathbb{R}})}^{p}+\sum_{n=1}^{k-1}|C_{n,p}|\|u\|_{H^{2j+1}({\mathbb{R}})}^{p-n+m(k)}+|C_{k,p}|\|u\|_{H^{2j+1}({\mathbb{R}})}^{p}\right).

It follows that

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{x}^{j}(u^{p}u_{x})(t)\|_{2}^{2}dt\leq&C|C_{0,p}|^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t)\|_{H^{2j+1}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2(p+1)}dt\\
&+C\sum_{k=0}^{j}\sum_{n=0}^{k}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}j\\
k\end{array}\right)|C_{n,p}|^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t)\|_{H^{2j+1}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2(p-n+m(k)+1)}dt\\
&+C\sum_{k=0}^{j}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}j\\
k\end{array}\right)|C_{k,p}|^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t)\|_{H^{2j+1}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2(p+1)}dt.\end{split}

Finally, we obtain that

(2.45)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{x}^{j}(u^{p}u_{x})(t)\|_{2}^{2}dt\leq&CT\left(|C_{0,p}|^{2}\|u\|_{C([0,T];H^{2j+1}({\mathbb{R}}))}^{2(p+1)}\right.\\
&+\sum_{k=0}^{j}\sum_{n=0}^{k}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}j\\
k\end{array}\right)|C_{n,p}|^{2}\|u(t)\|_{C([0,T];H^{2j+1}({\mathbb{R}}))}^{2(p-n+m(k)+1)}\\
&\left.+\sum_{k=0}^{j}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}j\\
k\end{array}\right)|C_{k,p}|^{2}\|u(t)\|_{C([0,T];H^{2j+1}({\mathbb{R}}))}^{2(p+1)}\right).\end{split}

Step 3 and thus the proof of the theorem is completed by putting the estimates (
2.43
), (
2.44
), and (
2.45
) together into (
2.42
).
∎

2.5.
Interpolation arguments

In this part of the work we prove the well-posedness of the system (
1.2
). To do that, let us introduce an interpolation argument due to Tartar
[
33
]
and adapted by Bona and Scott
[
8
, Theorem 4.3]
.

Let
B_{0}
and
B_{1}
be two Banach spaces, where
B_{1}\subset B_{0}
with the inclusion map continuous. Consider
f\in B_{0}
and define

\displaystyle K(f,t)=\inf_{g\in B_{1}}\left\{\|f-g\|_{B_{0}}+t\|g\|_{B_{1}}\right\},

for
t\geq 0
.
For
0<\theta<1
and
1\leq p\leq+\infty
, we introduce the set

\displaystyle B_{\theta,p}:=[B_{0},B_{1}]_{\theta,p}=\left\{f\in B_{0}:\|f\|_{\theta,p}:\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}K(f,t)t^{-\theta p-1}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty\right\},

with the usual modification for the case
p=\infty
. Then,
B_{\theta,p}
is a Banach space with norm
\|\cdot\|_{\theta,p}
. Given two pairs
(\theta_{1},p_{1})
and
(\theta_{2},p_{2})
as above,
(\theta_{1},p_{1})\prec(\theta_{2},p_{2})
will denote

\left\{\begin{tabular}[]{l l}$\theta_{1}<\theta_{2},$&or\\
$\theta_{1}=\theta_{3},$&and $p_{1}>p_{2}$.\end{tabular}\right.

If
(\theta_{1},p_{1})\prec(\theta_{2},p_{2})
, then
B_{\theta_{2},p_{2}}\subset B_{\theta_{1},p_{1}}
with a continuous inclusion.
Then, the following result, proved by
[
8
, Theorem 4.3]
, holds:

Theorem 2.12
.

Let
B_{0}^{j}
and
B_{1}^{j}
be Banach spaces such that
B_{1}^{j}\subset B_{0}^{j}
with continuous inclusion mappings, for
j=1,2
. Let
\alpha
and
q
lie in the ranges
0<\alpha<1
and
1\leq q\leq\infty
. Suppose that
\mathcal{A}
is a mapping satisfying:

(i)

\mathcal{A}:B_{\alpha,q}^{1}\rightarrow B_{0}^{2}
and, for
f,g\in B_{\alpha,q}^{1}
,

\|\mathcal{A}f-\mathcal{A}g\|_{B_{0}^{2}}\leq C_{0}\left(\|f\|_{B_{\alpha,q}^{1}}+\|g\|_{B_{\alpha,q}^{1}}\right)\|f-g\|_{B_{0}^{1}}.

(ii)

\mathcal{A}:B_{1}^{1}\rightarrow B_{1}^{2}
and, for
h\in B_{1}^{1}
,

\|\mathcal{A}h\|_{B_{1}^{2}}\leq C_{1}\left(\|h\|_{B_{\alpha,q}^{1}}\right)\|h\|_{B_{1}^{1}},

where
C_{j}:{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
are continuous nondecreasing functions, for
j=0,1
. Then, if
(\theta,p)\geq(\alpha,q)
,
\mathcal{A}
maps
B_{\theta,p}^{1}
into
B_{\theta,p}^{2}
and, for
f\in B_{\theta,p}^{1}
, we have

\|\mathcal{A}f\|_{B_{\theta,p}^{2}}\leq C\left(\|f\|_{B_{\alpha,q}^{1}}\right)\|f\|_{B_{\theta,p}^{1}},

where
C(r)=4C_{0}(4r)^{1-\theta}C_{1}(3r)^{\theta}
, with
r>0
.

It follows from Theorem
2.9
that, for each fixed
T>0
, the solution map

(2.46)

\mathcal{A}:C([-\tau,0];L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}))\rightarrow{\mathcal{B}}_{0,T},\quad\mathcal{A}u_{0}=u

is well-defined. Moreover, we have the following result:

Proposition 2.13
.

The solution map (
2.46
) is locally Lipschitz continuous, that is, there exists a continuous function
\mathcal{C}_{0}:{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\times(0,\infty)\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
, nondecreasing in its first variable, such that, for all
u_{0},v_{0}\in C([-\tau,0];L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}))
, we have

\|\mathcal{A}u_{0}-\mathcal{A}v_{0}\|_{0,T}\leq\mathcal{C}_{0}\left(\|u_{0}\|_{2}+\|v_{0}\|_{2},T\right)\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{C([-\tau,0];L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}))}.

Proof.

Let
0<\theta\leq T
and
n=\left[\frac{T}{\theta}\right]
. Theorem
2.9
ensures that

(2.47)

\|\mathcal{A}u_{0}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{0,\theta}}\leq\beta_{0}(\|u_{0}\|_{2})\|u_{0}\|_{C([-\tau,0];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))},

where
\beta_{0}(s)
is a constant function given by
\beta_{0}=C_{T}\left(1+\left(\|\lambda\|_{\infty}\tau^{1/2}+\|\lambda\|_{\infty}^{1/2}\right)\right)
and

\|\mathcal{A}u_{0}-\mathcal{A}v_{0}\|_{0,\theta}\leq C_{\theta}\left\{\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{2}+\|M(\mathcal{A}u_{0})-M(\mathcal{A}v_{0})\|_{L^{1}(0,\theta;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}))}\right\},

where
C_{\theta}=2e^{\theta\|b\|_{\infty}}
. Moreover, thanks to Lemma
2.6
we have

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{A}u_{0}-\mathcal{A}v_{0}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{0,\theta}}\leq&C_{\theta}\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{2}+C_{\theta}C\left\{2^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta^{\frac{2j-p}{4j}}\left(\|\mathcal{A}u_{0}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{0,\theta}}^{p}+\|\mathcal{A}u_{0}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{0,\theta}}\|\mathcal{A}v_{0}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{0,\theta}}^{p-1}\right.\right.\\
&\left.\left.+\|\mathcal{A}v_{0}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{0,\theta}}^{p}\right)+2^{\frac{1}{2}}\theta^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\mathcal{A}u_{0}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{0,\theta}}\right\}\|\mathcal{A}u_{0}-\mathcal{A}v_{0}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{0,\theta}}.\end{split}

Now, inequality (
2.47
) together with the following estimate:

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{A}u_{0}-\mathcal{A}v_{0}\|_{0,\theta}\leq&C_{\theta}\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{2}+C_{\theta}C\left\{2^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta^{\frac{2j-p}{4j}}\beta_{0}^{p}\left(\|u_{0}\|_{C([-\tau,0];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}^{p}\right.\right.\\
&\left.\left.+\|\ u_{0}\|_{C([-\tau,0];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}\|\ v_{0}\|_{C([-\tau,0];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}^{p-1}+\|\ v_{0}\|_{C([-\tau,0];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}^{p}\right)\right.\\
&\left.+2^{\frac{1}{2}}\beta_{0}\theta^{\frac{1}{4}}\|u_{0}\|_{C([-\tau,0];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}\right\}\|\mathcal{A}u_{0}-\mathcal{A}v_{0}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{0,\theta}},\end{split}

yields that

(2.48)

\|\mathcal{A}u_{0}-\mathcal{A}v_{0}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{0,\theta}}\leq 2C_{T}\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{2},

if
\theta
is chosen small enough. Observe that setting

B_{0,[k\theta,(k+1)\theta]}:=C\left([k\theta,(k+1)\theta];L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\right)\cap L^{2}(k\theta,(k+1)\theta;H^{j}({\mathbb{R}})).

with norm

, in a analogously way as done to (

), we can deduce thanks to estimate (

) that

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{A}u_{0}-\mathcal{A}v_{0}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{0,[k\theta,(k+1)\theta]}}\leq&C_{\theta}\|u(k\theta)-v(k\theta)\|_{2}+C_{\theta}C\beta_{0}\left\{2^{\frac{p}{2}}\theta^{\frac{2j-p}{4j}}\beta_{0}^{p}\left(\|u_{0}\|_{C([-\tau,0];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}^{p}\right.\right.\\
&\left.\left.+\|\ u_{0}\|_{C([-\tau,0];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}\|\ v_{0}\|_{C([-\tau,0];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}^{p-1}+\|\ v_{0}\|_{C([-\tau,0];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}^{p}\right)\right.\\
&\left.+2^{\frac{1}{2}}\beta_{0}\theta^{\frac{1}{4}}\|u_{0}\|_{C([-\tau,0];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}\right\}\|\mathcal{A}u_{0}-\mathcal{A}v_{0}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{0,[k\theta,(k+1)\theta]}}.\end{split}

Lastly, we get

(2.49)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}u_{0}-\mathcal{A}v_{0}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{0,[k\theta,(k+1)\theta]}}\leq 2C_{T}\|u(k\theta)-v(k\theta)\|_{2},\quad k=0,1,...,n-1.

On the other hand, note that (
2.48
) and (
2.49
) imply that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}u_{0}-\mathcal{A}v_{0}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{0,[k\theta,(k+1)\theta]}}\leq 2^{k}C_{T}^{k}\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{2},\quad k=0,1,...,n-1,

and, therefore,

\|\mathcal{A}u_{0}-\mathcal{A}v_{0}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{0,[k\theta,(k+1)\theta]}}\leq 2^{n}C_{T}^{n}\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{2}.

Finally,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}u_{0}-\mathcal{A}v_{0}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{0,T}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\|\mathcal{A}u_{0}-\mathcal{A}v_{0}\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{0,[k\theta,(k+1)\theta]}}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}2^{n}C_{T}^{n}\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{n}C_{T}^{n}n\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{2}\leq\mathcal{C}_{0}(\|u_{0}\|_{2}+\|v_{0}\|_{2})\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{C([-\tau,0];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}

with
\mathcal{C}_{0}(s)=\frac{T}{\theta(s)}\left[2C_{T}\right]^{\frac{T}{\theta(s)}}
, completing the proof of the proposition.
∎

We are in a position to prove the main result of this section.

and
(ii)
in Theorem

3.
Exponential stabilization: damped and delayed systems

This section is devoted to the proof of the exponential stability of the system (

) in

.
First, we prove the result for the linear system, and then we extend it to the nonlinear one.
To do this we consider the following Lyapunov functional:

(3.1)

E(t):=E(u(t))=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}(x,t)dx,

and we recall for
\lambda\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
the definition of

, given in (

3.1.
Linear system

The first result ensures the exponential stability of the linear system (
2.1
) with localized damping and delay terms:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\lambda,\lambda_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and
\lambda_{0}
satisfy (
1.8
).
If there exist a constant
\gamma>0
and a function
\beta\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}),
for

, such that the function
\lambda
satisfies (
1.9
) and (
1.10
), then the system (
2.1
) is exponentially stable.
More precisely, the solutions
u
of (

) satisfy the inequality

(3.2)

{\mathcal{E}}(t)\leq C(u_{0})e^{-\nu_{j}}t,

where
\nu_{j}
and
C(u_{0})
are defined by (
1.11
) and (
1.12
), respectively.

Proof.

Using the same argument as in
[
12
, Theorem 4.7]
, we consider
u_{0}\in C([-\tau,0];H^{2j+1}({\mathbb{R}}));
then
u\in C([0,T];H^{2j+1}({\mathbb{R}}))
. Taking the derivative in
t
of
{\mathcal{E}}(t)
we get

\begin{split}\frac{d{\mathcal{E}}}{dt}(t)=&\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(t)({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}-}(-1)^{j}\partial^{2j}_{x}u(t)-\lambda_{0}u(t)-\lambda u(t-\tau))dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\lambda|u^{2}(t)dx\\
&-\frac{1}{2}e^{-\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\lambda|u^{2}(t-\tau)dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{t-\tau}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-(t-s)}|\lambda(x)|u^{2}(x,s)dx\,ds,\end{split}

since we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}}(-1)^{j+1}(\partial_{x}^{2j+1}u)udx=0.\quad\text{for all}\quad u\in H^{2j+1}.

Integrating by parts, and using the Young inequality, remembering that (
1.8
) and (
1.9
) are satisfied, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d{\mathcal{E}}}{dt}(t)\leq

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial^{j}_{x}u)^{2}(t)dx-\gamma_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}(t)dx+\frac{e^{\tau}+1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\lambda(x)|u^{2}(t)dx

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{t-\tau}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-(t-s)}|\lambda(x)|u^{2}(x,s)dxds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial^{j}_{x}u)^{2}(t)dx-(\gamma_{0}-\gamma)\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}(t)dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\beta(x)u^{2}(t)dx

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{t-\tau}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-(t-s)}|\lambda(x)|u^{2}(x,s)\ dx\ ds;

we have used the relations

(-1)^{j}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u\partial^{2j}_{x}udx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial^{j}_{x}u)^{2}dx.

Now the Hölder inequality ensures that

(3.3)

\frac{d{\mathcal{E}}}{dt}(t)\leq-\|\partial^{j}_{x}u(t)\|_{2}^{2}-(\gamma_{0}-\gamma)\|u(t)\|_{2}^{2}+\|\beta\|_{q}\|u\|_{2q^{\prime}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{t-\tau}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-(t-s)}|\lambda(x)|u^{2}(x,s)dx\,ds

with
q^{\prime}=\frac{q}{q-1}.
Hence, using (
2.22
) in (
2.21
) in (
3.3

4.1

C(u_{0},0)^{1/2}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2j+1}}
, we have

\begin{split}\frac{1}{4}\|\partial_{x}^{2j+1}u(t)\|_{2}\leq&C\eta_{1}(\|u_{0}\|_{2})\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2j+1}({\mathbb{R}})}e^{-\delta_{1}t}\\
&+\left(C+\|\lambda_{0}\|_{\infty}\right)e^{-\frac{\tilde{\nu}}{2}t}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2j+1}}+e^{-\frac{\tilde{\nu}}{2}\left(\frac{4j(p+1)+p}{4j-p}\right)t}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2j+1}},\end{split}

showing the inequality (
4.2
).
∎

4.2.
Stabilization in
H^{s}({\mathbb{R}})

Finally, we prove the third main result of this work.

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

We already know that there exists a unique solution
u
of the system (
1.2
) in the class
\mathcal{B}_{s,T}
for every
T>0
. Moreover, if
0<\varepsilon<T
then it follows from Corollary
1.3
,
u\in\mathcal{B}_{2j+1,[\varepsilon,T]}
. On the other hand, from the interpolation inequality
[
25
, inequality (2.43)]
, we have

\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}({\mathbb{R}})}=\|u(t)\|_{[L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}),H^{2j+1}({\mathbb{R}})]_{2,\frac{s}{2j+1}}}\leq C\|u(t)\|_{2}^{1-\frac{s}{2j+1}}\|u(t)\|_{H^{2j+1}({\mathbb{R}})}^{\frac{s}{2j+1}},\quad\forall t\geq\varepsilon,

where
C>0
is a constant that comes from the interpolation argument. Thus, Proposition
4.2
and Theorems
1.4
and
1.5
, with
\lambda=\tau=0
, imply the existence of
T_{0}>0
,
\nu>0
and
\gamma>0
such that

\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq Ce^{-\left(1-\frac{s}{2j+1}\right)\nu t}\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{\left(1-\frac{s}{2j+1}\right)}\alpha_{2j+1}^{\frac{s}{2j+1}}(\|u(\varepsilon)\|_{2},T_{0})\|u(\varepsilon)\|^{\frac{s}{2j+1}}_{H^{2j+1}({\mathbb{R}})}e^{-\frac{s}{2j+1}\gamma t},\qquad\forall t\geq T_{0}.

Hence, (
1.17
) holds with

\eta(s)=\left(1-\frac{s}{2j+1}\right)\nu+\frac{s}{2j+1}\gamma>0

and

\gamma(s,T_{0})=Cs^{-\frac{s}{2j+1}}\alpha_{3}^{\frac{s}{2j+1}}(\|u(\varepsilon)\|_{2},T_{0})\|u(\varepsilon)\|^{\frac{s}{2j+1}}_{H^{2j+1}},

and the proof is complete.
∎

5.
Concluding remarks

In this work, we gave a more general framework to treat stabilization problems for a general higher-order dispersive system, which extends several previous results that appear in the literature. So, in terms of generality, we can consider the nonlinear general differential operator

(5.1)

\mathcal{V}u:=(-1)^{j+1}\partial_{x}^{2j+1}u(x,t)+(-1)^{m}\partial_{x}^{2m}u(x,t)+\frac{1}{p+1}\partial_{x}u^{p+1}(x,t),

with
1\leq p<2j
, instead of the typical KdV equation (or co-related systems) as is usual in the literature
3
3

3

One may generalize the linear operator associated to
\mathcal{V}
as

\mathcal{V}u=\sum_{m=0}^{j}\alpha_{m}\partial_{x}^{2m+1}u+\sum_{m=0}^{j}\beta_{m}\partial_{x}^{2m}u,

where
\alpha_{m},\beta_{m}\in\mathbb{R}
. However, the main analyses in the paper are almost analogous without additional difficulties, thus the operator (
5.1
) does not lose the generality in a sense of the aim of this paper.
. Thus, summarizing, we studied the asymptotic behavior of the equation (
1.2
) posed on an unbounded domain
\mathbb{R}
with the constant
\tau>0
as a time delay, and with the coefficients

\lambda_{0}(x),\lambda(x)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\quad\text{and}\quad j,m\in\mathbb{N}.

Considering
p=1
, when we have
j=m=1
, in (
5.1
), we recover the result proved in
[
21
]
for the KdV-Burgers operator. Additionally, when
j=2
and
m=1
, we have that the results of this manuscript are still valid for the fifth order KdV-Burgers type operator. Finally, we can take, without loss of generality,
j=m\in\mathbb{N}
in (
5.1
). So, we can define Lyapunov functionals associated with the solution of (
1.2
)

E(t):=E(u(t))=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}(x,t)dx

and, for
\lambda\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}),

{\mathcal{E}}(t):={\mathcal{E}}(u(t))=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}(x,t)dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{t-\tau}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-(t-s)}|\lambda(x)|u^{2}(x,s)\ dx\ ds.

thanks to the damping mechanism and the delay term, we showed that solutions of (
1.2
) satisfy

{\mathcal{E}}(t)\leq C(u_{0})e^{-\nu t}.

Additionally to that, it is important to note that Section
4
extends to
the operator (

) and the space
H^{s}

.
It also extends for an unbounded domain the results showed in
[

]
with appropriated choice of

), that is, the system with damping and delayed terms, is still an open problem to prove the stabilization result in

5.1.
Weak versus strong damping mechanism

Observe that taking
\beta(x)=0
in Theorems
1.4
and
1.5
, the results are still valid. However, we keep this term for a more general framework. Keeping in mind that
\beta(x)\neq 0
, additionally, we can consider
\lambda_{0}
and
\lambda
are constants such that
|\lambda|<\lambda_{0},
and the delay
\tau
is sufficiently small, so Theorem
1.4
gives us the exponential stability for the solution of (
1.2
). This is possible using the method introduced in
[
27
]
for wave equations. In fact, in this case, we choose a sufficiently small delay
\tau
and a constant
\gamma
such that

\frac{e^{\tau}+1}{2}|\lambda|<\gamma<\lambda_{0}.

Since now
\lambda_{0}=\gamma_{0}
(see (
1.8
)), the conditions (
1.9
) and (
1.10
) are satisfied, and the Theorem
1.4
follows.
The same remark applies if
\lambda,\lambda_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
with
\lambda_{0}
satisfying (
1.8
), and
|\lambda|<\gamma_{0}.

With respect to the Theorem
1.5
, for a more general framework, if
\lambda_{0}
satisfies (
1.13
) and (
1.14
) instead of
\lambda_{0}(x)\geq\gamma_{0}
for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}
, then for the same function,
\beta
satisfying

\frac{e^{\tau}+1}{2}|\lambda(x)|\leq\gamma+\beta(x)\quad\text{for a.e.}\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

we expect a smaller decay rate
\tilde{\nu}
than
\nu
in Theorem
1.5
. The explanation for this is the fact that there is a “good” part
\gamma
of
\lambda_{0}
that will compensate for the delay feedback and its indefinite component
\beta_{0}
.

5.2.
General framework

One may generalize the system (
1.2
) as follows:

(5.2)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(x,t)+\sum\limits_{m=0}^{j}\alpha_{m}\partial_{x}^{2m+1}u(x,t)+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\beta_{k}\partial_{x}^{2k}u(x,t)+\lambda_{0}(x)u(x,t)\\
\hskip 113.81102pt+\lambda(x)u(x,t-\tau)+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}u^{2}(x,t)=0,&\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty),\\
u(x,s)=u_{0}(x,s),&\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}\times[-\tau,0],\end{cases}

where
\alpha_{m},\beta_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
and
j,n\in\mathbb{N}
. The previous system, which depends on the parameters
\alpha_{m}
and
\beta_{k}
, recovers various delayed dispersive equations. Note that depending on the choice of the constants, we have:

1.

Burgers equation (
\alpha_{m}=0
and
n=1
);

2.

KdV equation (
j=1
and
\beta_{k}=0
);

3.

Kawahara equation (
j=2
,
\alpha_{0}=1
,
\alpha_{1}=-1
and
\beta_{k}=0
);

4.

KdV–Burgers equation (
j=1
and
n=1
);

5.

Kawahara–Burgers equation (
j=2
,
\alpha_{0}=1
,
\alpha_{1}=-1
and
n=1
);

6.

Fourth-order dispersive equation (
\alpha_{m}=0
,
n=2
,
\beta_{1}=-1
and
\beta_{2}=1
).

However, we point out that for the system (
5.2
) the main analyses in the paper are almost analogous without additional difficulties, thus the equation (
1.2
) does not lose the generality in a sense of the aim of this paper.

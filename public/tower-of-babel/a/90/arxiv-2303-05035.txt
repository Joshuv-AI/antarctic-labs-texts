Abstract




ON STABILITY OF SOLITONS FOR A ROTATING

CHARGE AT REST IN THE MAXWELL FIELD

We consider the system of Maxwell equations and Lorentz torque equation which describes a motion of charge in electromagnetic field. Under certain symmetry conditions on charge distribution and on initial fields the charge remains at rest but rotates. The system admits stationary soliton-type solutions. We study the Lyapunov and the orbital stability of solitons exploiting the energy conservation law the angular momentum argument for the system. The bibliography: 20 refs.

Introduction

In this note we study some dynamical aspects of the Abraham model for a classical charge with spin coupled to the Maxwell field. The model is known since early 1900-ths, cf.
[
2
,
3
]
, and was the subject of a number of papers, both at the physical and mathematical levels of rigor, see below the comment on previous works and results.

First let us write down the equations of motion. The Maxwell field consists of the electric field
E(x,t)
and the magnetic field
B(x,t)
. The charge has the center of mass
q
with the velocity
\dot{q}
. We assume that the mass distribution,
m\,\rho(x)
, and the charge distribution,
e\,\rho(x)
, are proportional to each other. Here
m
is the total mass,
e
is the total charge, and we use a system of units, where
m=1
and
e=1
;
\rho(x)
is a smooth radially symmetric smearing function of compact support, explicitly,

\rho\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\rm I\kern-1.56693ptR}^{3}),\,\,\,\,\rho(x)=\rho_{rad}(|x|),\,\,\,\,\rho(x)=0,\,\,\,{\rm for}\,\,\,|x|>R_{\rho}>0.

(1.1)

The angular velocity of the charge is denoted by
\omega(t)\in{\rm I\kern-1.56693ptR}^{3}
. In detail,
\omega(t)
is the angular velocity “in space” (in
the terminology of
[
4
]
) of the charge. Namely, let us fix a
“center” point
O
of the rigid body. Then the trajectory of each
particular point of the body is described by

x(t)=q(t)+R(t)(x(0)-q(0)),

where
q(t)
is the position of
O
at the time
t
, and
R(t)\in SO(3)
. Respectively, the velocity reads

\dot{x}(t)=\dot{q}(t)+\dot{R}(t)(x(0)-q(0))=\dot{q}(t)+\dot{R}(t)R^{-1}(t)(x(t)-q(t))=\dot{q}(t)+\omega(t)\wedge(x(t)-q(t)),

(1.2)

where
\omega(t)\in{\rm I\kern-1.56693ptR}^{3}
corresponds to the skew-symmetric matrix
\dot{R}(t)R^{-1}(t)
by the rule

\dot{R}(t)R^{-1}(t)={\cal J}\omega(t):=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&-\omega_{3}(t)&\omega_{2}(t)\\
\omega_{3}(t)&0&-\omega_{1}(t)\\
-\omega_{2}(t)&\omega_{1}(t)&0\end{array}\right).

(1.3)

We assume that
x
and
q
refer to a certain Euclidean coordinate
system in
{\rm I\kern-1.56693ptR}^{3}
, and the vector product
\wedge
is defined in this
system by standard formulas. The identification (
1.3
) of a
skew-symmetric matrix and the corresponding angular velocity
vector is true in any Euclidean coordinate system of the same
orientation as the initial one.

Then the Maxwell equations read,
[
3
]

\dot{E}(x,t)=\nabla\wedge B(x,t)-(\dot{q}(t)+\omega(t)\wedge(x-q(t)))\rho(x-q(t)),\quad\dot{B}(x,t)=-\nabla\wedge E(x,t),

(1.4)

where the current has a contribution also from the internal rotation; together with the constraints,

\nabla\cdot E(x,t)=\rho(x-q(t)),\,\nabla\cdot B(x,t)=0.

(1.5)

The back reaction of the field onto the charge is given through the Lorentz force equation

\ddot{q}(t)=\int\,[E(x,t)+(\dot{q}(t)+\omega(t)\wedge(x-q(t)))\wedge B(x,t)]\rho(x-q(t))\,dx

(1.6)

and the Lorentz torque equation

I\,\dot{\omega}(t)=\int\,(x-q(t))\wedge[E(x,t)+(\dot{q}(t)+\omega(t)\wedge(x-q(t)))\wedge B(x,t)]\rho(x-q(t))\,dx,

(1.7)

with the moment of inertia

I=\frac{2}{3}\int\,x^{2}\rho(x)d\,x.

(1.8)

The important question is to obtain solutions having constant velocity and of the form

q(t)=q+vt,\,\omega(t)\equiv\omega,\,E(x,t)=E_{v,\omega}(x-vt),\,B(x,t)=B_{v,\omega}(x-vt).

(1.9)

We will call them the “soliton solutions”, in brief, the “solitons”. If in (
1.4
), (
1.6
), (
1.14
) we set
\omega=0
, by hand, then for every
v\in{\rm I\kern-1.56693ptR}^{3}
there is a unique solution of the form (
1.9
). However, for the Abraham model including spin,
the equation
(
1.14
) can be satisfied only if either
\omega\|v
or
\omega\bot v

[
3
]
. This result is surprising at first sight,
but reflects the semirelativistic nature of the Abraham model. The velocity singles out a direction, which is then
taken by
\omega
. Eventually one has to understand the domain of attraction of this soliton-like solutions. In this paper we restrict ourselves however to a simpler situation, where the charge remains at rest for all times,
q\equiv 0
. This can be achieved by assuming the (anti-) symmetry conditions

E(-x)=-E(x),\quad B(-x)=B(x)

(1.10)

for the initial fields.
Then this property would persist for all times:

E(-x,t)=-E(x,t),\,\,\,B(-x,t)=B(x,t).

(1.11)

The Lorentz force equation is automatically satisfied, the Maxwell equations simplify to

\dot{E}(x,t)=\nabla\wedge B(x,t)-(\omega(t)\wedge x)\rho(x),\quad\dot{B}(x,t)=-\nabla\wedge E(x,t),

(1.12)

with the constraints

\nabla\cdot E(x,t)=\rho(x),\,\,\,\nabla\cdot B(x,t)=0,

(1.13)

and the Lorentz torque equation simplifies to

I\dot{\omega}(t)=\int x\wedge[E(x,t)+(\omega(t)\wedge x)\wedge B(x,t)]\rho(x)\,dx.

(1.14)

The system (
1.11
)–(
1.14
) is the subject of study of the present paper. We will consider solutions of finite energy

H(\omega,E,B)=\frac{I\omega^{2}}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int\,\Big(|E(x)|^{2}+|B(x)|^{2}\Big)\,dx<\infty.

(1.15)

The corresponding phase space will be equipped with a suitable topology below and the existence and uniqueness of finite energy solutions will be briefly explained. Note that the total momentum of the system

P:=\int E(x,t)\wedge B(x,t)\,dx=0

(1.16)

in view of the symmetry conditions (
1.11
).

The solitons for the system (
1.11
)-(
1.14
) have the form

E(x,t)=E_{\omega}(x),\,\,\,B(x,t)=B_{\omega}(x),\,\,\,\omega(t)=\omega=const\in{\rm I\kern-1.56693ptR}^{3}.

The solitons satisfy the stationary equations

E_{\omega}(-x)=-E_{\omega}(x),\,\,\,B_{\omega}(-x)=B_{\omega}(x),

(1.17)

\nabla\wedge B_{\omega}(x)-(\omega\wedge x)\rho(x)=0,\,\,\,\nabla\wedge E_{\omega}(x)=0,

(1.18)

\nabla\cdot E_{\omega}(x)=\rho(x),\,\,\,\nabla\cdot B_{\omega}(x)=0,

(1.19)

\int x\wedge[E_{\omega}(x)+(\omega\wedge x)\wedge B_{\omega}(x)]\rho\,dx=0.

(1.20)

To write down the exact formulas of solitons let us specify the version of Fourier transform we use. The Fourier transform

of a function

\hat{f}(k):=(2\pi)^{-3/2}\int e^{-ikx}f(x)\,dx.

(1.21)

Then by the Fourier transform

xf(x)\mapsto i\nabla_{k}\hat{f}(k),\,\,\,\nabla f(x)\mapsto ik\hat{f}(k).

(1.22)

The Parseval equality holds,

\int f(x)\overline{g(x)}\,dx=\int\hat{f}(k)\overline{\hat{g}(k)}\,dk.

(1.23)

Note that the conditions (
1.1
) imply some special properties of the Fourier transform
\hat{\rho}(k)
of
\rho(x)
. First,

\hat{\rho}(k)\,\,\,{\rm is\,\,\,a\,\,\,real-valued\,\,\,spherically\,\,\,symmetric\,\,\,function:}\,\,\,\overline{\hat{\rho}(k)}=\hat{\rho}(k),\,\,\,\rho(k)=\rho_{r}(r),\,\,r:=|k|.

(1.24)

Second, as for the spatial decay of
\hat{\rho}(k)
,

\hat{\rho}(k)\,\,\,{\rm is\,\,\,at\,\,\,least\,\,\,a\,\,\,fast\,\,\,decaying\,\,\,function\,\,\,of\,\,\,the\,\,\,Schwarz\,\,\,space}.

(1.25)

Now, in Fourier space, the soliton fields are expressed
[
3

5

\hat{E}_{\omega}(k)=\frac{-ik\hat{\rho}(k)}{k^{2}},\,\,\,\hat{B}_{\omega}(k)=-\frac{k\wedge(\omega\wedge\nabla_{k}\hat{\rho}(k))}{k^{2}}.

(1.26)

It follows from (
1.24

1.26

\hat{E}_{\omega}\in L^{2},\hat{B}_{\omega}\in L^{2}\,\,\,\,{\rm in}\,\,\,k-{\rm space,\,\,\,hence}\,\,\,\,E_{\omega}\in L^{2},B_{\omega}\in L^{2}\,\,\,\,{\rm in}\,\,\,x-{\rm space}.

(1.27)

2
Existence of dynamics and the main results

Consider the space
L=({\rm I\kern-1.56693ptR}^{3},L^{2}({\rm I\kern-1.56693ptR}^{3};{\rm I\kern-1.56693ptR}^{3}),L^{2}({\rm I\kern-1.56693ptR}^{3};{\rm I\kern-1.56693ptR}^{3}))
with the norm

\|(\omega,E,B)\|_{L}:=|\omega|+\|E\|_{L^{2}({\rm I\kern-1.24918ptR}^{3};{\rm I\kern-1.24918ptR}^{3})}+\|B\|_{L^{2}({\rm I\kern-1.24918ptR}^{3};{\rm I\kern-1.24918ptR}^{3})}.

Definition 2.1

The phase space for the system (
1.11
)-(
1.14
) is the nonlinear submanifold
{\cal M}
of states
(\omega,E,B)\in L
, where

satisfy (
1.11

1.13

Let us state a proposition on existence of dynamics for the system (

)-(

Proposition 2.1

Let the assumptions (
1.1

i) for any
(\omega_{0},E_{0},B_{0})\in{\cal M}

1.14

(\omega(t),E(x,t),B(x,t))\in C({\rm I\kern-1.56693ptR};{\cal M})
with initial conditions
\omega(0)=\omega_{0},E(x,0)=E_{0},B(x,0)=B_{0}
;

ii) for any

the map
U_{T}:(\omega_{0},E_{0},B_{0})\mapsto(\omega(T),E(\cdot,T),B(\cdot,T))

{\cal M}
;

iii) the energy
H(t):=H(\omega(t),E(\cdot,t),B(\cdot,t))
is conserved along the solutions of the system:

H(t)\equiv H(0),\,\,\,\,t\in{\rm I\kern-1.56693ptR}.

(2.28)

The proof is similar to that of

Now let us specify common definitions of Lyapunov stability, orbital stability, and asymptotic stability for the particular case of soliton solutions to the system (
1.11
)-(
1.14
).

Definition 2.2

1) A soliton solution
Y_{\omega}=(\omega,E_{\omega},B_{\omega})
is called Lyapunov stable if
\forall\varepsilon>0

\exists\delta>0
such than for any solution
Y(t)=(\omega(t),E_{(}t),B(t))
from the condition
\|Y(0)-Y_{\omega}(0)\|_{L}<\delta
it follows that
\|Y(t)-Y_{\omega}(t)\|_{L}<\varepsilon

\forall t\in{\rm I\kern-1.56693ptR}
.

2) A soliton solution
Y_{\omega}=(\omega,E_{\omega},B_{\omega})
is called orbital stable if
\forall\varepsilon>0

\exists\delta>0
such than for any solution
Y(t)=(\omega(t),E_{(}t),B(t))
with
\|Y(0)-Y_{\omega}(0)\|_{L}<\delta
the solution
Y(\cdot)
remains in
\varepsilon
-neighbourhood of
Y_{\omega}(\cdot)
in the space
C({\rm I\kern-1.56693ptR};L)
.

3) A soliton solution
Y_{\omega}=(\omega,E_{\omega},B_{\omega})
is called asymptotic stable if it is Lyapunov stable and
\|Y(t)-Y_{\omega}(t)\|_{L}\to 0
as
t\to\infty
.

Note that in view of the energy conservation (
2.28
) one could not expect the asymptotic stability of the solitons in the phase space
{\cal M}
of finite energy states. Our main result is then the Lyapunov and orbital stability. In fact, for our case the properties of Lyapunov stability and orbital stability of a soliton coincide, since solitons do not depend on
t
and thus are stationary solutions (fixed points of the dynamical system).

Further, the method of exploiting energy conservation arguments we use imposes some restrictions on the class of possible perturbations. Namely, we assume that the perturbations are of
uniformly compact supports
, see the exact formulation below. The main result on stability is the following theorem.

Theorem 2.3

a) The zero soliton with
\omega=0
is Lyapunov stable and orbital stable.

b) Let us fix a non-zero
\omega\in{\rm I\kern-1.56693ptR}^{3}
. Consider solutions to the Cauchy problem for the system (
1.11
)-(
1.14
) with initial data
\omega+\Omega_{0},\,\,E_{\omega}(x)+e_{0}(x),\,\,B_{\omega}(x)+b_{0}(x)
, where
(\Omega_{0},e_{0},b_{0})\in L
,
e_{0}(x)
is odd,
b_{0}(x)
is even,
\nabla\cdot e_{0}=0
,
\nabla\cdot b_{0}=0
.

The soliton
(\omega,E_{\omega}(x),B_{\omega}(x))
is Lyapunov stable and as well orbital stable with respect to perturbations of uniformly compact support

i.e.
\forall\varepsilon>0
and
\forall R>0

\exists\delta>0
such that for any solution
(\omega(t),E(t),B(t))
with the initial data
\omega_{0}=\omega+\Omega_{0},E_{0}=E_{\omega}+e_{0},B_{0}=B_{\omega}+b_{0}
from the condition
\|(\Omega_{0},e_{0},b_{0})\|_{L}<\delta
it follows that
\|(\omega(t)-\omega,E(t)-E_{\omega},B(t)-B_{\omega})\|_{L}<\varepsilon

\forall t\in{\rm I\kern-1.56693ptR}

\forall(\Omega_{0},e_{0}(x),b_{0}(x))
such that
{\rm supp}\,e_{0}\subset\{|x|\leq R\}
and
{\rm supp}\,b_{0}\subset\{|x|\leq R\}
.

Angular momentum

The angular momentum is defined by

M(\omega,E,B):=I\omega+\int\,x\wedge(E(x)\wedge B(x))\,dx.

Note that for

-\frac{\omega(k\cdot\nabla_{k}\rho_{r}(r))-\nabla_{k}\rho_{r}(r)(k\cdot\omega)}{r^{2}}=\frac{\rho^{\prime}_{r}(r)((k\omega)k-k^{2}\omega)}{r^{3}}.

Note that
\hat{B}_{\omega}(k)
is a real-valued function by (
1.24
), thus,
\overline{\hat{B}_{\omega}}(k)=\hat{B}_{\omega}(k)
.
For
j=1,2,3
put
\alpha_{j}=(k\omega)k_{j}-k^{2}\omega_{j}
, then
\partial_{j}\alpha_{j}=k\omega-k_{j}\omega_{j}
. One has

\partial_{j}\frac{\rho_{r}^{\prime}\alpha_{j}}{r^{3}}=\frac{\rho_{r}^{\prime\prime}r^{2}k_{j}\alpha_{j}+\rho_{r}^{\prime}r^{3}(k\omega-k_{j}\omega_{j})-3\rho_{r}^{\prime}rk_{j}\alpha_{j}}{r^{6}}.

Note that
\sum_{j}k_{j}\alpha_{j}=0
. Then

\nabla_{k}\cdot{\hat{B}_{\omega}}=\frac{\rho_{r}^{\prime\prime}r^{2}\sum_{j}k_{j}\alpha_{j}-3\rho_{r}^{\prime}r\sum_{j}k_{j}\alpha_{j}+2\rho_{r}^{\prime}r^{3}k\omega}{r^{6}}=\frac{2\rho_{r}^{\prime}r^{3}k\omega}{r^{6}}=\frac{2\rho_{r}^{\prime}k\omega}{r^{3}}.

Thus, the first summand of the integrand in (
4.79
) equals
\hat{e}\frac{\displaystyle 2\rho_{r}^{\prime}k\omega}{\displaystyle r^{3}}
. Now, the whole integral (
4.79
) equals

i\,\int\left[\omega\cdot\hat{e}\frac{2\rho_{r}^{\prime}k\omega}{r^{3}}-\omega\cdot(\hat{e}\cdot\nabla_{k}){\hat{B}_{\omega}}\right]dk.

(4.80)

In the integrand, the component
\hat{e}_{1}
is multiplied by the expression

\frac{2\omega_{1}\rho_{r}^{\prime}k\omega}{r^{3}}-\omega\cdot(\partial_{1}{\hat{B}_{\omega}}).

(4.81)

One has

\partial_{1}({\hat{B}_{\omega}})_{1}=\frac{\rho_{r}^{\prime\prime}r^{2}k_{1}\alpha_{1}+\rho_{r}^{\prime}r^{3}(k\omega-k_{1}\omega_{1})-3\rho_{r}^{\prime}rk_{1}\alpha_{1}}{r^{6}},

and

\partial_{1}({\hat{B}_{\omega}})_{j}=\frac{\rho_{r}^{\prime\prime}r^{2}k_{1}\alpha_{j}+\rho_{r}^{\prime}r^{3}(\omega_{1}k_{j}-2k_{1}\omega_{j})-3\rho_{r}^{\prime}rk_{1}\alpha_{j}}{r^{6}}

for
j=2,3
. Then in (
4.80
)
\hat{e}_{1}
is multiplied by

k_{1}\cdot\beta(k),\,\,\,\,\beta(k):=\frac{\rho_{r}^{\prime\prime}r^{2}\sum_{j}\omega_{j}\alpha_{j}-3\rho_{r}^{\prime}r\sum_{j}\omega_{j}\alpha_{j}-2\rho_{r}^{\prime}r^{3}\omega^{2}}{r^{6}}.

(4.82)

Note that
\beta(k)
does not depend on the number of the coordinate. Similarly, in (
4.80
)
\hat{e}_{j}
is multiplied by
k_{j}\cdot\beta(k)
,
j=2,3
. Finally, in (
4.80
)
\hat{e}
is multiplied by the vector
k\cdot\beta(k)
which is proportional to
k
. Hence, the scalar product equals zero. We have proved that the soliton satisfies the first variational equation (
4.77
).

Let us proceed to the equation (
4.78
). Insert
E=E_{\omega}
,
B=B_{\omega}
and obtain

\int b\cdot B_{\omega}dx-\omega\cdot\int x\wedge(E_{\omega}\wedge b)dx=\int b\cdot B_{\omega}dx-\omega\cdot\int\left(E_{\omega}(x\cdot b)-b(x\cdot E_{\omega})\right)dx=

\int\hat{b}\cdot\hat{B}_{\omega}dk-\omega\cdot\int\left((i\nabla_{k}\cdot\hat{b})\overline{\hat{E}}_{\omega}-\hat{b}(\overline{i\nabla_{k}\cdot\hat{E}_{\omega}})\right)dk.

(4.83)

Here the first integral equals

\int\hat{b}\cdot\frac{\rho_{r}^{\prime}((k\omega)k-r^{2}\omega)}{r^{3}}dk=-\int\omega\cdot\hat{b}\frac{\rho_{r}^{\prime}}{r}dk,

since
\hat{b}
is orthogonal to
k
. Insert
\hat{E}_{\omega}=-\frac{\textstyle ik\rho_{r}}{r^{2}}
and obtain, first,

\int(i\nabla_{k}\cdot\hat{b})\overline{\hat{E}}_{\omega}dk=\int(i\nabla_{k}\cdot\hat{b})\frac{ik\rho_{r}}{r^{2}}=\int(\hat{b}\cdot\nabla_{k})\frac{k\rho_{r}}{r^{2}}

by partial integration. Within this integral,
\hat{b}_{1}
is multiplied by
\partial_{1}\frac{\textstyle k\rho_{r}}{\textstyle r^{2}}
. Note that

\partial_{1}\frac{k_{1}\rho_{r}}{r^{2}}=\frac{\rho_{r}(k^{2}-2k_{1}^{2})+\rho_{r}^{\prime}rk_{1}^{2}}{r^{4}},\,\,\,\,\partial_{1}\frac{k_{j}\rho_{r}}{r^{2}}=\frac{\rho_{r}^{\prime}rk_{1}k_{j}-\rho_{r}2k_{1}k_{j}}{r^{4}},\,\,\,\,j=2,3.

Second,

\int\hat{b}(\overline{i\nabla_{k}\cdot\hat{E}_{\omega}})dk=\int\hat{b}(-i\nabla_{k}\cdot\frac{ik\rho_{r}}{r^{2}})dk=\int\hat{b}\cdot\frac{\rho_{r}+\rho_{r}^{\prime}r}{r^{2}}dk,

since

\nabla_{k}\cdot\frac{k\rho_{r}}{k^{2}}=\frac{\rho_{r}r^{2}+\rho_{r}^{\prime}r^{3}}{r^{4}},

Finally, in (
4.83
)
\hat{b}_{1}
is multiplied by

-\omega_{1}\frac{\rho_{r}^{\prime}}{r}-\omega_{1}\frac{\rho_{r}(r^{2}-2k_{1}^{2})+\rho_{r}^{\prime}rk_{1}^{2}}{r^{4}}-\omega_{2}\frac{\rho_{r}^{\prime}rk_{1}k_{2}-2\rho_{r}k_{1}k_{2}}{r^{4}}-

\omega_{3}\frac{\rho_{r}^{\prime}rk_{1}k_{3}-2\rho_{r}k_{1}k_{3}}{r^{4}}+\omega_{1}\frac{\rho_{r}+\rho_{r}^{\prime}r}{r^{2}}=k_{1}\cdot\gamma(k),\,\,\,\gamma(k):=\frac{2\rho_{r}k\omega-\rho_{r}^{\prime}rk\omega}{r^{4}}.

Here
\gamma(k)
does not depend on the number of coordinate. Similarly,
\hat{b}_{j}
is multiplied by
k_{j}\cdot\gamma(k)
,
j=2,3
. As the result, in (
4.83
)
\hat{b}
is multiplied by the vector
k\cdot\gamma(k)
which is proportional to
k
. This equals zero and the variational equation (
4.78
) is satisfied.
\Box

4.2
Instability of the soliton on the surface of constant angular momentum

4.2.1
Energy increment at the soliton

Let us compute

H_{M}(E_{\omega}+e,B_{\omega}+b)-H_{M}(E_{\omega},B_{\omega})=\frac{1}{2I}(M-\int x\wedge(E_{\omega}\wedge B_{\omega})dx-m)^{2}-\frac{1}{2I}(M-\int x\wedge(E_{\omega}\wedge B_{\omega})dx)^{2}+

\frac{1}{2}\int(|E_{\omega}+e|^{2}+|B_{\omega}+b|^{2})dx-\frac{1}{2}\int(|E_{\omega}|^{2}+|B_{\omega}|^{2})dx,

where we put

m=m_{1}+m_{2}+m_{3},\,\,\,\,m_{1}=\int x\wedge(E_{\omega}\wedge b)dx,\,\,\,m_{2}=\int x\wedge(e\wedge B_{\omega})dx,\,\,\,m_{3}=\int x\wedge(e\wedge b)dx.

Note that
M-\int x\wedge(E_{\omega}\wedge B_{\omega})dx=I\omega
. Thus, we obtain
H_{M}(E_{\omega}+e,B_{\omega}+b)-H_{M}(E_{\omega},B_{\omega})=

\frac{1}{2I}(I\omega-m)^{2}-\frac{1}{2I}(I\omega)^{2}+\int E_{\omega}\cdot e\,dx+\int B_{\omega}\cdot b\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int(|e|^{2}+|b|^{2})dx=

\frac{1}{2I}m^{2}-\omega\cdot m+\int E_{\omega}\cdot e\,dx+\int B_{\omega}\cdot b\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int(|e|^{2}+|b|^{2})dx=

\frac{1}{2I}m^{2}-\omega\cdot m_{1}-\omega\cdot m_{2}-\omega\cdot m_{3}+\int E_{\omega}\cdot e\,dx+\int B_{\omega}\cdot b\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int(|e|^{2}+|b|^{2})dx.

We have
\int E_{\omega}\cdot e\,dx=0
;
\omega\cdot m_{2}=0
by the variational equation (
4.77
),
-\omega\cdot m_{1}+\int B_{\omega}\cdot b\,dx=0
by the variational equation (
4.78
). Finally, we obtain

H_{M}(E_{\omega}+e,B_{\omega}+b)-H_{M}(E_{\omega},B_{\omega})=\frac{1}{2I}m^{2}-\omega\cdot m_{3}+\frac{1}{2}\int(|e|^{2}+|b|^{2})dx.

(4.84)

Note that for
\omega=0
the increment is nonnegative which corresponds to stability of the zero soliton.

4.2.2
Energy increment alternation as instability argument

For instability of a soliton of finite angular momentum we have to prove that

\exists\varepsilon>0\,\,\forall\delta>0\,\,\exists(\Omega,e,b):\,\,|\Omega|+\|e\|_{L^{2}({\rm I\kern-1.24918ptR}^{3};{\rm I\kern-1.24918ptR}^{3})}+\|b\|_{L^{2}({\rm I\kern-1.24918ptR}^{3};{\rm I\kern-1.24918ptR}^{3})}<\delta\,\,\,{\rm and}

\exists t\in{\rm I\kern-1.56693ptR}:\,\,|\omega(t)-\omega|+\|E(\cdot,t)-E_{\omega}\|_{L^{2}({\rm I\kern-1.24918ptR}^{3};{\rm I\kern-1.24918ptR}^{3})}+\|B(\cdot,t)-B_{\omega}\|_{L^{2}({\rm I\kern-1.24918ptR}^{3};{\rm I\kern-1.24918ptR}^{3})}\geq\varepsilon.

(4.85)

Clearly, it is sufficient to prove that

\exists\varepsilon>0\,\,\forall\delta>0\,\,\exists(e,b):\,\,\|e\|_{L^{2}({\rm I\kern-1.24918ptR}^{3};{\rm I\kern-1.24918ptR}^{3})}+\|b\|_{L^{2}({\rm I\kern-1.24918ptR}^{3};{\rm I\kern-1.24918ptR}^{3})}<\delta\,\,\,{\rm and}\,\,\,

|H_{M}(E_{\omega}+e,B_{\omega}+b)-H_{M}(E_{\omega},B_{\omega})|\geq\varepsilon.

(4.86)

Indeed, consider the solution
(\omega(t),E(x,t),B(x,t))
with the initial data
(\omega,E_{\omega}+e,B_{\omega}+b)
. Then by the energy conservation (
2.28
) one has
H(\omega(t),E(x,t),B(x,t))=H_{M}(E_{\omega}+e,B_{\omega}+b)
and thus (
4.86
) implies (
4.85
).

To obtain (
4.86
) we construct special perturbation fields
(e,b)
.

The idea of the construction is rather simple. Let us concentrate ourselves on the term
\omega\cdot m_{3}
of (
4.84
). This term is linear while all the rest terms are quadratic. Further,
m_{3}=\int x\wedge(e\wedge b)dx
. Suppose that compact supports of
e
and
b
tend to infinity i.e. they are in the region of large
|x|
. Then
m_{3}
can be made sufficiently large compared to the other terms such that
\omega\cdot m_{3}
(
\omega\neq 0
) makes the increment (
4.84
) alternating. We only have to provide that
e
is odd,
b
is even and
\nabla\cdot e=0
,
\nabla\cdot b=0
. So, it is natural to start with potentials of the fields
e
and
b
. The exact construction is given below.

Consider a smooth non-increasing function
\alpha(r)
,
r\in{\rm I\kern-1.56693ptR}
,
r\geq 0
such that

\alpha(r)\equiv d\,\,\,{\rm for}\,\,\,0\leq r\leq 1,\,\,\,\alpha(r)\equiv 0\,\,\,{\rm for}\,\,\,r\geq 2,

(4.87)

where
d>0
will be chosen later. Set

\tilde{e}(x):=(\alpha(|x-a|)+\alpha(|x+a|),0,0),\,\,\,\,\tilde{b}(x):=(0,\alpha(|x-a|)-\alpha(|x+a|),0),

where
a:=(0,c,0)
,
c>0
is sufficiently large. Then
\tilde{e}(x)
is even in
x
,
\tilde{b}(x)
is odd in
x
. Further, define the perturbation fields as

e(x)=\nabla\wedge\tilde{e}(x),\,\,\,\,b(x)=\nabla\wedge\tilde{b}(x).

Then

\nabla\cdot e=0,\,\,\,\,\nabla\cdot b=0,

and the field
e
is odd in
x
, the field
b
is even in
x
. Now let us compute
e
and
b
in detail:

e=(0,\partial_{3}(\alpha(|x-a|)+\alpha(|x+a|),-\partial_{2}(\alpha(|x-a|)+\alpha(|x+a|))=

(0,\,\,f(|x-a|)\frac{x_{3}}{|x-a|}+f(|x+a|)\frac{x_{3}}{|x+a|},\,\,-f(|x-a|)\frac{x_{2}-c}{|x-a|}-f(|x+a|)\frac{x_{2}+c}{|x+a|}),

where we put
f(r)=\alpha^{\prime}_{r}
. By the definition of
\alpha
, the summands of the components of
e
are non zero for either
|x-a|\leq 2
or
|x+a|\leq 2
, i.e.
|(x_{1},x_{2}-c,x_{3})|\leq 2
or
|(x_{1},x_{2}+c,x_{3})|\leq 2
. Hence,
\|e\|_{L^{2}}
is bounded uniformly in
c
. Further,

b=(-\partial_{3}(\alpha(|x-a|)-\alpha(|x+a|),0,\partial_{1}(\alpha(|x-a|)-\alpha(|x+a|))=

(-f(|x-a|)\frac{x_{3}}{|x-a|}+f(|x+a|)\frac{x_{3}}{|x+a|},\,\,0,\,\,f(|x-a|)\frac{x_{1}}{|x-a|}-f(|x+a|)\frac{x_{1}}{|x+a|});

\|b\|_{L^{2}}
is bounded uniformly in
c
.

Let us compute the term

m_{1}=\int\,x\wedge(E_{\omega}\wedge b)\,dx=\int\,E_{\omega}(x\cdot b)-b(x\cdot E_{\omega})\,dx.

Since
x_{j}(E_{\omega})_{k}\in L^{2}({\rm I\kern-1.56693ptR},{\rm I\kern-1.56693ptR})
by (
2.32
),
|m_{1}|
is bounded uniformly in
c
.

Similarly,

m_{2}=\int\,x\wedge(e\wedge B_{\omega})\,dx=\int\,e(x\cdot B_{\omega})-B_{\omega}(x\cdot e)\,dx.

Since
x_{j}(B_{\omega})_{k}\in L^{2}({\rm I\kern-1.56693ptR},{\rm I\kern-1.56693ptR})
by (
2.32
),
|m_{2}|
is bounded uniformly in
c
.

Let us compute the term

m_{3}=\int\,x\wedge(e\wedge b)\,dx=\int\,e(x\cdot b)-b(x\cdot e)\,dx.

We start with the first component
m_{3,1}
. The first component of
e(x\cdot b)
is clearly zero. Compute

-\int\,b_{1}(x\cdot e)\,dx=\int\,dx\left(f(|x-a|)\frac{x_{3}}{|x-a|}-f(|x+a|)\frac{x_{3}}{|x+a|}\right)(x\cdot e).

(4.88)

First,

(x\cdot e)=f(|x-a|)\frac{x_{2}x_{3}}{|x-a|}+f(|x+a|)\frac{x_{2}x_{3}}{|x+a|}-f(|x-a|)\frac{x_{3}(x_{2}-c)}{|x-a|}-f(|x+a|)\frac{x_{3}(x_{2}+c)}{|x+a|}=

cx_{3}\left(\frac{f(|x-a|)}{|x-a|}-\frac{f(|x+a|)}{|x+a|}\right).

Now, the integral of (
4.88
) is the integral of four summands. Two of them are of the kind
f(|x-a|)f(|x+a|)[...]=0
for sufficiently large
c
, since the supports of
f(|x-a|)
and
f(|x+a|)
do not intersect. Then

m_{3,1}=-\int\,b_{1}(x\cdot e)\,dx=c\int\,dx\,x_{3}^{2}\left(\frac{f^{2}(|x-a|)}{|x-a|^{2}}\right)+c\int\,dx\,x_{3}^{2}\left(\frac{f^{2}(|x+a|)}{|x+a|^{2}}\right).

For the first integral let us change the variables as
y=x-a
, in detail,

y_{1}=x_{1},\,\,\,\,y_{2}=x_{2}-c,\,\,\,\,y_{3}=x_{3}.

(4.89)

Then we obtain

c\int\,dx\,x_{3}^{2}\left(\frac{f^{2}(|x-a|)}{|x-a|^{2}}\right)=c\int\,dy\,y_{3}^{2}\left(\frac{f^{2}(|y|)}{|y|^{2}}\right).

For the second integral we change the variables as
z=x+a
:

z_{1}=x_{1},\,\,\,\,z_{2}=x_{2}+c,\,\,\,\,z_{3}=x_{3}.

(4.90)

Then

c\int\,dx\,x_{3}^{2}\left(\frac{f^{2}(|x+a|)}{|x+a|^{2}}\right)=c\int\,dz\,z_{3}^{2}\left(\frac{f^{2}(|z|)}{|z|^{2}}\right).

Finally, after redesignating the variables again by
x
, we obtain

m_{3,1}=2c\int\,dx\,x_{3}^{2}\left(\frac{f^{2}(|x|)}{|x|^{2}}\right)=2c\cdot\frac{1}{3}\int\,dx\,|x|^{2}\cdot\frac{f^{2}(|x|)}{|x|^{2}}=c\cdot\frac{2}{3}\int\,dx\,f^{2}(|x|).

(4.91)

Let us proceed to the second component

m_{3,2}=\int\,e_{2}(x\cdot b)-b_{2}(x\cdot e)\,dx=\int\,e_{2}(x\cdot b)\,dx,

since
b_{2}=0
. Note that

x\cdot b=-f(|x-a|)\frac{x_{1}x_{3}}{|x-a|}+f(|x+a|)\frac{x_{1}x_{3}}{|x+a|}+f(|x-a|)\frac{x_{1}x_{3}}{|x-a|}-f(|x+a|)\frac{x_{1}x_{3}}{|x+a|}=0.

Thus,
m_{3,2}=0
.

For the third component we have

m_{3,3}=\int\,e_{3}(x\cdot b)-b_{3}(x\cdot e)\,dx=-\int\,b_{3}(x\cdot e)\,dx,

since
x\cdot b=0
. Insert
b_{3}
and
x\cdot e
and obtain

m_{3,3}=-c\int\,dx\left(\frac{f^{2}(|x-a|)x_{1}x_{3}}{|x-a|^{2}}\right)-c\int\,dx\left(\frac{f^{2}(|x+a|)x_{1}x_{3}}{|x+a|^{2}}\right).

For the first integral we apply the change of variables (
4.89
) and obtain

\int\,dx\left(\frac{f^{2}(|x-a|)x_{1}x_{3}}{|x-a|^{2}}\right)=\int\,dy\left(\frac{f^{2}(|y|)y_{1}y_{3}}{|y|^{2}}\right)=0,

since the integrand is odd w.r.t
y_{1}
or
y_{3}
. Similarly, the second integral

\int\,dx\left(\frac{f^{2}(|x+a|)x_{1}x_{3}}{|x+a|^{2}}\right)=0

by the change of variables (
4.90
). As the result,
m_{3,3}=0
.

Further, we modify the perturbation fields as follows:

\tilde{e}_{c}(x):=c^{-1/2}(\alpha(|x-a|)+\alpha(|x+a|),0,0),\,\,\,\,\tilde{b}_{c}(x):=c^{-1/2}(0,\alpha(|x-a|)-\alpha(|x+a|),0),

e_{c}(x)=\nabla\wedge\tilde{e}_{c}(x),\,\,\,\,b_{c}(x)=\nabla\wedge\tilde{b}_{c}(x).

Then, as
c\to+\infty
,

\|e_{c}\|_{L^{2}}={\cal O}(c^{-1/2}),\,\,\,\,\|b_{c}\|_{L^{2}}={\cal O}(c^{-1/2});

m_{1,c}:=\int\,x\wedge(E_{\omega}\wedge b_{c})\,dx={\cal O}(c^{-1/2}),\,\,\,\,m_{2,c}:=\int\,x\wedge(e_{c}\wedge B_{\omega})\,dx={\cal O}(c^{-1/2}),

while

\omega\cdot m_{3,c}=\omega_{1}\cdot m_{3,1,c}=\omega_{1}\cdot\frac{2}{3}\int\,dx\,f^{2}(|x|)

by (
4.91
), thus,
\omega\cdot m_{3,1,c}
does not depend on
c
.

Let us compute in detail the energy increment (
4.84
) with
m_{j}=m_{j,c}
,
j=1,2,3
,
e=e_{c}
,
b=b_{c}
; for simplicity of notation we omit the index
c
:

\frac{1}{2I}m^{2}-\omega\cdot m_{3}+\frac{1}{2}\int(|e|^{2}+|b|^{2})dx=

\frac{1}{2I}(m_{1}^{2}+m_{2}^{2}+2m_{1}\cdot m_{2}+2m_{1}\cdot m_{3}+2m_{2}\cdot m_{3})+\frac{1}{2}\int(|e|^{2}+|b|^{2})dx+

\frac{1}{2I}m_{3,1}^{2}-\omega_{1}\cdot m_{3,1},

we take into account that
m_{3,2}=0
and
m_{3,3}=0
. Here the second line tends to zero as
c\to+\infty
and can be ignored for sufficiently large
c
. We have to analyze the third line. Put
\tilde{m}=m_{3,1}
. The quadratic function
g
of
\tilde{m}
,
g=\frac{1}{2I}\tilde{m}^{2}-\omega_{1}\cdot\tilde{m}
has the zeros at the points
\tilde{m}_{1}=0
and
\tilde{m}_{2}=2I\omega_{1}\neq 0
, the point of minimum is
\tilde{m}_{0}=I\omega_{1}
, the minimal value equals
-I\omega_{1}^{2}/2
. Fix a
\varepsilon>0
such that
-I\omega_{1}^{2}<-\varepsilon<0
, then the equation
g(\tilde{m})=-\varepsilon
has two roots
\tilde{m}_{1}^{\prime}
and
\tilde{m}_{2}^{\prime}
between
\tilde{m}_{1}
and
\tilde{m}_{2}
.

Finally, we choose a
d
in (
4.87
) such that
\tilde{m}\in(\tilde{m}_{1}^{\prime},\tilde{m}_{2}^{\prime})
. Then (
4.86
) follows and hence the statement of Theorem
2.5
.

5
Appendix. Angular momentum of a soliton

Let us compute the angular momentum
M_{\omega}
of a soliton
(E_{\omega},B_{\omega},\omega)
given by (
2.35
), in detail. We have

\int x\wedge(E_{\omega}\wedge B_{\omega})dx=\int(E_{\omega}(x\cdot B_{\omega})-B_{\omega}(x\cdot E_{\omega}))dx=

\int\left[(i\nabla_{k}\cdot\hat{B}_{\omega})\overline{\hat{E}_{\omega}}-\hat{B}_{\omega}\overline{(i\nabla_{k}\cdot\hat{E}_{\omega})}\right]dk=i\int\left[(\nabla_{k}\cdot\hat{B}_{\omega})\overline{\hat{E}_{\omega}}+\hat{B}_{\omega}(\nabla_{k}\cdot\overline{\hat{E}_{\omega}})\right]dk.

(5.92)

Since

\hat{B}_{\omega}=\frac{\rho_{r}^{\prime}((k\omega)k-k^{2}\omega)}{r^{3}},\,\,\,\,\overline{\hat{E}_{\omega}}=\frac{ik\overline{\tilde{\rho}}}{k^{2}},

we obtain by the computation of Section 4.1 that

\nabla_{k}\cdot\hat{B}_{\omega}=\frac{2\rho_{r}^{\prime}(k\omega)}{r^{3}},\,\,\,\,\nabla_{k}\cdot\overline{\hat{E}_{\omega}}=i\frac{\rho_{r}+\rho_{r}^{\prime}r}{r^{2}}.

Then the expression in (
5.92
) equals

-\int\left[\frac{2\rho_{r}^{\prime}\rho_{r}(k\omega)k}{r^{5}}+((k\omega)k-k^{2}\omega)\rho_{r}^{\prime}\frac{\rho_{r}+\rho_{r}^{\prime}r}{r^{5}}\right]dk.

Since
\rho_{r}
and
\rho_{r}^{\prime}
are even in
k
, the last expression simplifies to

\frac{2}{3}\omega\int\frac{(\rho^{\prime}_{r})^{2}}{r^{2}}dk.

Finally, the angular momentum of the soliton equals

M_{\omega}=\omega\left(I+\frac{2}{3}\int\frac{(\rho_{r}^{\prime})^{2}}{r^{2}}dk\right).

(5.93)

Remark 5.1

In
x
-representation (
5.93
) reads

M_{\omega}=\omega\left(I+\frac{2}{3}\int\,x\rho\cdot\Delta^{-1}(x\rho)dx\right).

Strong Rates of Convergence for Space-Time Discretization of the Backward Stochastic Heat Equation, and of a Linear-Quadratic Control Problem for the Stochastic Heat Equation Thanks: This work is supported in part by the National Natural Science Foundation of China (11801467), and the Chongqing Natural Science Foundation (cstc2018jcyjAX0148).
Andreas Prohl, and, Yanqing Wang

Abstract
We introduce a time-implicit, finite-element based space-time discretization scheme for the backward stochastic heat equation, and for the forward-backward stochastic heat equation from stochastic optimal control, and prove strong rates of convergence. The fully discrete version of the forward-backward stochastic heat equation is then used within a gradient descent algorithm to approximately solve the linear-quadratic control problem for the stochastic heat equation driven by additive noise.



Keywords:
Strong error estimate, backward stochastic heat equation, stochastic linear quadratic problem,

Introduction

Let
D\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be a bounded domain with
C^{2}
boundary,
T>0
, and a (deterministic)
function
\widetilde{X}\equiv\{\widetilde{X}(t);\,t\in[0,T]\}\in C([0,T];\mathbb{H}_{0}^{1}\cap{\mathbb{H}}^{2})
be given.
Our goal is to numerically approximate the
{\mathbb{L}}^{2}
-valued,
{\mathbb{F}}
-adapted control process

U^{*}\equiv\{U^{*}(t);\,t\in[0,T]\}
on the filtered probability space

(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},{\mathbb{F}},{\mathbb{P}})
that minimizes the functional (
\alpha\geq 0
)

{\mathcal{J}}(X,U)=\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\int_{0}^{T}\left(\|X(t)-\widetilde{X}(t)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}+\|U(t)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\right)\,{\rm d}t+\alpha\|X(T)-\widetilde{X}(T)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\Bigr]

(1.1)

subject to the (controlled forward) stochastic heat equation (
SPDE
for short)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rm d}X(t)=\bigl[\Delta X(t)+U(t)\bigr]\,{\rm d}t+\sigma(t){\rm d}W(t)&\forall\,t\in[0,T]\,,\\
X(0)=X_{0}\,,\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

which is supplemented by homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition. Here

W\equiv\{W(t);\,t\in[0,T]\}
is an
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
-valued Wiener process, and
\sigma\equiv\{\sigma(t);\,t\in[0,T]\}\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}\bigl(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H}_{0}^{1}\cap\mathbb{H}^{2})\bigr)
, which both are given on the same filtered probability space. For every given
U\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}\bigl(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;{\mathbb{L}}^{2})\bigr)
, there exists a unique
{\mathbb{H}}^{1}_{0}
-valued strong (variational) solution
X\equiv X(U)
in (
1.2
) such that

X(0)=X_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{H}^{1}_{0})
, and a
unique minimizer
(X^{*},U^{*})\in L^{2}_{{\mathbb{F}}}\bigl(\Omega;C([0,T];{\mathbb{L}}^{2})\cap L^{2}(0,T;{\mathbb{H}}^{1}_{0})\times L^{2}(0,T;{\mathbb{L}}^{2})\bigr)
of the stochastic optimal control problem: ’minimize (
1.1
) subject to (
1.2
)’ – which we below refer to as
SLQ
; see e.g.
[
2
]
.

We consider problem
SLQ
as a prototype example of a (linear-quadratic) stochastic optimal control problem involving a stochastic PDE, for which corresponding numerical analyses so far are rare in the existing literature; see e.g.
[
23
,
8
]
. This is in contrast to the deterministic counterpart problem
LQ
which involves a linear PDE,
where optimal rates of convergence are available for (finite element based) space-time discretization of related optimality conditions (see e.g.
[
17
,
16
,
21
,
18
,
11
]
), which may then be used as part of a gradient descent algorithm with step size control

[
12
]

to approximate
the minimizing tuple
(X^{*},U^{*})
, which here consists of deterministic state and control functions. If compared to problem
LQ
, problem
SLQ
owns some distinctive characters and additional difficulties caused by the driving
Wiener process in the SPDE (
1.2
), which make the generalization of the
numerical results for the deterministic control problem to

a non-trivial task.
For example, a crucial difficulty consists in solving the adjoint equation in the context of

, which here is a backward stochastic PDE (
BSPDE
for short) of the form

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rm d}Y(t)=\Bigl[-\Delta Y(t)+\bigl[X(t)-\widetilde{X}(t)\bigr]\Bigr]{\rm d}t+Z(t){\rm d}W(t)&\forall\,t\in[0,T]\,,\\
Y(T)=-\alpha\left(X(T)-\widetilde{X}(T)\right)\,,\end{array}\right.

(1.3)

having a solution tuple
(Y,Z)\in L^{2}_{{\mathbb{F}}}\bigl(\Omega;C([0,T];{\mathbb{H}}^{1}_{0})\cap L^{2}(0,T;{\mathbb{H}}^{1}_{0}\cap{\mathbb{H}}^{2})\bigr)\times L^{2}_{{\mathbb{F}}}\bigl(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;{\mathbb{H}}^{1}_{0})\bigr)

[

The adjoint variable

is then related to the optimal control

is

0=U^{*}(t)-Y(t)\qquad\forall\,t\in(0,T)\,.

(1.4)

The combination of equations (
1.2
), (
1.3
), and (
1.4

(X^{*},U^{*})
of problem

.

Preliminaries

2.1
Notation — involved processes and the finite element method

Let
\bigl({\mathbb{K}},(\cdot,\cdot)_{{\mathbb{K}}}\bigr)
be a separable Hilbert space.
By
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}
resp.
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}
,
we denote the norm resp. the scalar product in
{\mathbb{L}}^{2}:={\mathbb{L}}^{2}(D)
.
The norm in
{\mathbb{H}}^{1}_{0}:=H_{0}^{1}(D)
,
\mathbb{H}^{2}:=H^{2}(D)
is denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{H}_{0}^{1}}
,
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}

respectively. Let
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},{\mathbb{F}},{\mathbb{P}})
be a complete filtered probability space, where

{\mathbb{F}}
is the filtration generated by the
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
-valued Wiener process
W
, which is augmented by
all the
{\mathbb{P}}
-null sets. Below, we set
m=1
for simplicity. The space of all
{\mathbb{F}}
-adapted processes

X:\Omega\times[0,T]\rightarrow{\mathbb{K}}
satisfying

{\mathbb{E}}[\int_{0}^{T}\|X(t)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{K}}}\,{\rm d}t]<\infty
is denoted by

L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;{\mathbb{K}}))
; the space of all
{\mathbb{F}}
-adapted processes

X:\Omega\times[0,T]\rightarrow{\mathbb{K}}
satisfying

{\mathbb{E}}[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|X(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{K}}]<\infty
is denoted by

L^{2}_{{\mathbb{F}}}\bigl(\Omega;C([0,T];{\mathbb{K}})\bigr)
.

We partition the bounded domain
D\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
via a regular triangulation
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
into elements
K
with maximum mesh size

h:=\max\{{\rm diam}(K):\,K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\}
, and consider spaces

{\mathbb{V}}_{h}^{1}:=\{\phi\in{\mathbb{H}}^{1}_{0}:\,\phi\bigl|_{K}\in{\mathbb{P}}_{1}(K)\quad\forall\,K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\}\,,\qquad{\mathbb{V}}_{h}^{0}:=\{\phi\in{\mathbb{L}}^{2}:\,\phi\bigl|_{K}\in{\mathbb{P}}_{0}(K)\quad\forall\,K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\}\,,

where
{\mathbb{P}}_{i}(K)
denotes the space of polynomials of degree
i\,(i=0,1)
.
The
{\mathbb{L}}^{2}
-projection
\Pi_{h}^{i}:{\mathbb{L}}^{2}\rightarrow{\mathbb{V}}^{i}_{h}
is defined by
(\Pi^{i}_{h}\xi-\xi,\phi_{h})_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}=0
for all
\phi_{h}\in{\mathbb{V}}_{h}^{i}
.
We define the discrete Laplacean
\Delta_{h}:{\mathbb{V}}_{h}^{1}\rightarrow{\mathbb{V}}_{h}^{1}
by
(-\Delta_{h}\xi_{h},\phi_{h})_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}=(\nabla\xi_{h},\nabla\phi_{h})_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}
for all
\xi_{h},\phi_{h}\in{\mathbb{V}}_{h}^{1}
.

We use approximation estimates for the projection
\Pi_{h}^{1}
, and an inverse estimate (cf.
[
5
]
) to conclude that

\|\Delta_{h}\Pi_{h}^{1}\xi\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\leq C\|\nabla^{2}\xi\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\qquad\forall\,\xi\in{\mathbb{H}}^{1}_{0}\cap{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\,,

(2.1)

since

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{h}\Pi_{h}^{1}\xi\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\bigl(\nabla[\Pi_{h}^{1}\xi-\xi],\nabla\Delta_{h}\Pi_{h}^{1}\xi\bigr)_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}-(\nabla\xi,\nabla\Delta_{h}\Pi_{h}^{1}\xi)_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch\|\nabla^{2}\xi\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\|\nabla\Delta_{h}\Pi_{h}^{1}\xi\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}+(\Delta\xi,\Delta_{h}\Pi_{h}^{1}\xi)_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\bigl(\|\nabla^{2}\xi\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}+\|\Delta\xi\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\bigr)\|\Delta_{h}\Pi_{h}^{1}\xi\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\,.

We denote by
I_{\tau}=\{t_{n}\}_{n=0}^{N}\subset[0,T]
a time mesh with maximum step size
\tau:=\max\{t_{n+1}-t_{n}:\,n=0,1,\cdots,N-1\}
, and

\Delta_{n}W=W(t_{n})-W(t_{n-1})
for all
n=1,\cdots,N
.
For simplicity, we choose a uniform partition, i.e.
\tau=T/N
and
\tau\leq 1
.
The results in this work still hold for quasi-uniform partitions.

2.2
The stochastic heat equation — strong convergence rates for a space-time discretization

For a given
U\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}\bigl(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2})\bigr)
, and

X_{0}\in\mathbb{H}^{1}_{0}
in (
1.2
), there exist a strong solution

X\in L^{2}_{{\mathbb{F}}}\bigl(\Omega;C([0,T];{\mathbb{H}}^{1}_{0})\cap L^{2}(0,T;{\mathbb{H}}^{1}_{0}\cap{\mathbb{H}}^{2})\bigr)

solving
{\mathbb{P}}
-a.s. for all
t\in[0,T]

\displaystyle\bigl(X(t),\phi\bigr)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}-(X_{0},\phi)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+\int_{0}^{t}\left[\bigl(\nabla X(s),\nabla\phi\bigr)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}-\bigl(U(s),\phi\bigr)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\right]\,{\rm d}s

(2.2)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\sigma(s),\phi\bigr)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\,{\rm d}W(s)\qquad\forall\,\phi\in{\mathbb{H}}^{1}_{0}\,,

and a constant
C\equiv C(D,T)>0
such that

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|X(t)\|_{{\mathbb{H}}_{0}^{1}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\|X(t)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}\,{\rm d}t\right]\leq C{\mathbb{E}}\left[\|X_{0}\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{H}}_{0}^{1}}+\int_{0}^{T}\|U(t)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\,{\rm d}t\right]\,.

(2.3)

A finite element discretization of (
2.2
) then reads: For all
t\in[0,T]
, find

X_{h}\in L^{2}_{{\mathbb{F}}}\bigl(\Omega;C([0,T];{\mathbb{V}}_{h}^{1})\bigr)
such that
{\mathbb{P}}
-a.s. and for all times
t\in[0,T]

\displaystyle\bigl(X_{h}(t),\phi_{h}\bigr)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}-(X_{0},\phi_{h})_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\nabla X_{h}(s),\nabla\phi_{h}\bigr)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}-\bigl(U(s),\phi_{h}\bigr)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\,{\rm d}s

(2.4)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\sigma(s),\phi_{h}\bigr)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\,{\rm d}W(s)\qquad\forall\,\phi_{h}\in{\mathbb{V}}_{h}^{1}\,.

Equation (
2.4
) may be recast into the following SDE system,

\left\{\begin{aligned} &dX_{h}(t)=\bigl[\Delta_{h}X_{h}(t)+\Pi_{h}^{1}U(t)\bigr]{\rm d}t+\Pi_{h}^{1}\sigma(t){\rm d}W(t)\,,\\
&X_{h}(0)=\Pi_{h}^{1}X_{0}\,.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.5)

The derivation of a strong error estimate is standard, and uses the improved (spatial) regularity properties of the strong variational solution,

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[\|X_{h}(t)-X(t)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\bigr]+{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\int_{0}^{T}\bigl\|\nabla\bigl[X_{h}(t)-X(t)\bigr]\bigr\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\,{\rm d}t\Bigr]\leq Ch^{2}\,.

(2.6)

We now consider a time-implicit discretization of (
2.4
) on a partition
I_{\tau}
of
[0,T]
. The problem then reads: For every
0\leq n\leq N-1
, find a solution
X^{n+1}_{h}\in L^{2}_{{\mathcal{F}}_{t_{n+1}}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{V}}_{h}^{1})
such that
{\mathbb{P}}
-a.s.

(X^{n+1}_{h}-X^{n}_{h},\phi_{h})_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+\tau\Bigl[(\nabla X_{h}^{n+1},\nabla\phi_{h})_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}-(U(t_{n}),\phi_{h})_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\Bigr]=\bigl(\sigma(t_{n}),\phi_{h}\bigr)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\Delta_{n+1}W\,,

(2.7)

where
\Delta_{n+1}W:=W(t_{n+1})-W(t_{n})
.
The verification of the error estimate (see
[
24
]
)

\max_{0\leq n\leq N}{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[\|X_{h}(t_{n})-X^{n}_{h}\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\bigr]+\tau\sum_{n=1}^{N}{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\bigl\|\nabla\bigl[X_{h}(t_{n})-X^{n}_{h}\bigr]\bigr\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\Bigr]\leq C\tau

(2.8)

rests on stability properties of the implicit Euler, as well as the bound

\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|X_{h}(t)-X_{h}(t_{n})\|_{\mathbb{H}_{0}^{1}}^{2}\bigr]\,\mathrm{d}t\leq C\tau\,,

(2.9)

which requires additional regularity properties of involved data, i.e.,

X_{0}\in\mathbb{H}^{1}_{0}\cap\mathbb{H}^{2}
, as well as

\sigma\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H}_{0}^{1}\cap\mathbb{H}^{2}))
,
U\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H}_{0}^{1}))
,
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\mathbb{E}\left[\|U(t)-U(t_{n})\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\|\sigma(t)-\sigma(t_{n})\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]\,\mathrm{d}t\leq C\tau\,,

and the
{\mathbb{H}}^{1}
-stability of the
{\mathbb{L}}^{2}
-projection
\Pi_{h}^{1}
; cf.
[
6
,
4
]
.

2.3
The backward stochastic heat equation — a finite element based spatial discretization

Let
Y_{T}\in L^{2}_{{\mathcal{F}}_{T}}\bigl(\Omega;{\mathbb{H}}^{1}_{0}\bigr)
and
f\in L^{2}_{{\mathbb{F}}}\bigl(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;{\mathbb{L}}^{2})\bigr)
. A strong solution to the backward stochastic heat equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rm d}Y(t)=\bigl[-\Delta Y(t)+f(t)\bigr]{\rm d}t+Z(t){\rm d}W(t)&\forall\,t\in[0,T]\\
Y(T)=Y_{T}\end{array}\right.

(2.10)

is a pair of square integrable
{\mathbb{F}}
-adapted processes

(Y,Z)\in L^{2}_{{\mathbb{F}}}\Bigl(\Omega;C([0,T];{\mathbb{H}}^{1}_{0})\cap L^{2}(0,T;{\mathbb{H}}^{1}_{0}\cap{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\bigr)\times L^{2}(0,T;{\mathbb{H}}^{1}_{0})\Bigr)

such that
{\mathbb{P}}
-a.s. for all times
t\in[0,T]

\displaystyle\bigl(Y_{T},\phi\bigr)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}-(Y(t),\phi)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}-\int_{t}^{T}\left[\bigl(\nabla Y(s),\nabla\phi\bigr)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+\bigl(f(s),\phi\bigr)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\right]\,{\rm d}s

(2.11)

\displaystyle=\int_{t}^{T}\bigl(Z(s),\phi\bigr)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\,{\rm d}W(s)\quad\forall\,\phi\in{\mathbb{H}}^{1}_{0}\,,

and there exists a constant
C\equiv C(D,T)>0
such that

{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Y(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}_{0}^{1}}\bigr]+{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\int_{0}^{T}\|Y(t)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}+\|Z(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}_{0}^{1}}\,{\rm d}t\Bigr]\leq C{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\|Y_{T}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}_{0}^{1}}+\int_{0}^{T}\|f(t)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\,{\rm d}t\Bigr]\,.

(2.12)

The existence of a strong solution to (
2.10
) in the above sense, as well as its uniqueness are shown in
[
7
]
.

We now consider a finite element discretization of the
BSPDE
(
2.10
). Let
Y_{T,h}\in L^{2}_{{\mathcal{F}}_{T}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{V}}_{h}^{1})

be an approximation of
Y_{T}
. The problem
BSPDE
h
then reads: Find
(Y_{h},Z_{h})\in L^{2}_{{\mathbb{F}}}\bigl(\Omega;C([0,T];{\mathbb{V}}_{h}^{1})\bigr)\times L^{2}_{{\mathbb{F}}}\bigl(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;{\mathbb{V}}_{h}^{1})\bigr)
such that
{\mathbb{P}}
-a.s. for all
t\in[0,T]

\displaystyle(Y_{T,h},\phi_{h})_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}-\bigl(Y_{h}(t),\phi_{h}\bigr)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}-\int_{t}^{T}\bigl(\nabla Y_{h}(s),\nabla\phi_{h}\bigr)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+\bigl(f(s),\phi_{h}\bigr)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\,{\rm d}s

(2.13)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\int_{t}^{T}\bigl(Z_{h}(s),\phi_{h}\bigr)_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\,{\rm d}W(s)\qquad\forall\,\phi_{h}\in{\mathbb{V}}^{1}_{h}\,.

Equation (
2.13
) is equivalent to the following system of BSDEs:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\rm d}Y_{h}(t)=\bigl[-\Delta_{h}Y_{h}(t)+\Pi_{h}^{1}f(t)\bigr]{\rm d}t+Z_{h}(t){\rm d}W(t)\quad\forall\,t\in[0,T]\\
&Y_{h}(T)=Y_{T,h}\,.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.14)

The existence and uniqueness of a solution tuple
(Y_{h},Z_{h})
e.g. follows from
[

. Moreover, there exists
C\equiv C(f,T)>0
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[\|\nabla Y_{h}(t)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\bigr]+{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\int_{0}^{T}\|\Delta_{h}Y_{h}(t)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}+\|\nabla Z_{h}(t)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\,{\rm d}t\Bigr]

(2.15)

\displaystyle\leq C{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[\left\|\nabla Y_{T,h}\right\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}+\int_{0}^{T}\|f(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t\bigr]\,;

cf.
[

, Lemma 3.1]
. — The following result is taken from

Theorem 2.1
.

Let

2.4
Temporal discretization of the backward stochastic heat equation — the role of the Malliavin derivative

The numerical analysis of a temporal discretization of (
2.14
) requires Malliavin calculus to bound temporal increments
\mathbb{E}\|Z_{h}(t)-Z_{h}(s)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}
in terms of
|t-s|
, where
s,t\in[0,T]
. We therefore recall the definition of the Malliavin derivative
of processes, and the crucial connection between the Malliavin derivative of
Y_{h}
and
Z_{h}
from (
2.14
). For further details, we refer to
[
20
,
9
]
.

Let us recall that
{\cal F}_{T}=\sigma\{W(t);0\leq t\leq T\}
, and that
{\mathbb{K}}
denotes a separable Hilbert space. We define the Itô isometry
W:L^{2}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}})\to L^{2}_{{\cal F}_{T}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}})
by

\displaystyle W(h)=\int_{0}^{T}h(t)\,{\mathrm{d}}W(t)\,.

For
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we denote by
C_{p}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{\ell})
the space of all smooth functions
g:\mathbb{R}^{\ell}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
g
and all of its partial derivatives have polynomial growth.
Let
{\cal P}
be the set of
{\mathbb{R}}
-valued random variables of the form

F=g\bigl(W(h_{1}),\cdots,W(h_{\ell})\bigr)

(2.16)

for some
g\in C_{p}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{\ell})
,
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and
h_{1},\ldots,h_{\ell}\in L^{2}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}})
.
To any
F\in{\cal P}
we define its
{\mathbb{R}}
-valued Malliavin derivative

DF:=\{D_{t}F;\,0\leq t\leq T\}
process via

\displaystyle D_{t}F=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{i}}(W(h_{1}),\cdots,W(h_{\ell}))h_{i}(t)\,.

In general, we can define the
k
-th iterated derivative of
F
by
D^{k}F=D(D^{k-1}F)
, for any

k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Now we extend the derivative operator to
\mathbb{K}
-valued variables. For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and
u
in the set of
\mathbb{K}
-valued variables:

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{\mathbb{K}}=\Bigl\{u=\sum_{j=1}^{n}F_{j}\phi_{j}:F_{j}\in{\cal P},\,\phi_{j}\in\mathbb{K},\,n\in\mathbb{N}\Bigr\}\,,

we can define the
k
-th iterated derivative of
u
by

\displaystyle D^{k}u=\sum_{j=1}^{n}D^{k}F_{j}\otimes\phi_{j}\,.

For
p\geq 1
, we define the norm
\|\cdot\|_{k,p}
via

\displaystyle\|u\|_{k,p}:=\bigg(\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|u\|_{\mathbb{K}}^{p}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left\|D^{j}u\right\|_{\left({L^{2}(0,T;{\mathbb{R}})}\right)^{\otimes j}\otimes\mathbb{K}}^{p}\Bigr]\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,.

Then
\mathbb{D}^{k,p}({\mathbb{K}})
is the completion of
{\cal P}
under the norm
\|\cdot\|_{k,p}
.

We may now express
Z_{h}
in BSDE (
2.14
) in terms of the Malliavin derivative of
Y_{h}
.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
9
]
, Prop. 5.3)
.

Suppose that
Y_{T,h}\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{L}^{2})
,
f\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}\bigl(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2})\bigr)
, and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int_{0}^{T}\|D_{\theta}Y_{T,h}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\,{\rm d}\theta\Bigr]+\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\|D_{\theta}f(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\,{\rm d}t{\rm d}\theta\Bigr]<\infty\,.

Let
(Y_{h},Z_{h})
be the solution to BSDE (
2.14
). Then

(Y_{h},Z_{h})\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}\Bigl(\Omega;C\bigl([0,T];\mathbb{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{L}^{2})\bigr)\times L^{2}\bigl(0,T;\mathbb{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{L}^{2})\bigr)\Bigr)\,,

and its Malliavin derivative
(D_{\theta}Y_{h},D_{\theta}Z_{h})
solves

\left\{\begin{aligned} &D_{\theta}Y_{h}(t)-D_{\theta}Y_{h}(T)+\int_{t}^{T}-\Delta_{h}D_{\theta}Y_{h}(s)+\Pi_{h}^{1}D_{\theta}f(s)\,{\rm d}s\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad=-\int_{t}^{T}D_{\theta}Z_{h}(s)\,{\rm d}W(s)\qquad 0\leq\theta\leq t\leq T\,,\\
&{D_{\theta}Y_{h}(t)=D_{\theta}Z_{h}(t)=0\qquad 0\leq t<\theta\leq T}\,.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.17)

Moreover,
\{D_{t}Y_{h}(t):\,0\leq t\leq T\}
is a version of
\{Z_{h}(t):\,0\leq t\leq T\}
.

(

3.1
Uniform bounds for temporal increments of the solution
(Y_{h},Z_{h})
to
(
2.14
)

We start with the derivation of uniform estimates for
Y_{h}
which control its temporal increments.
We note again that all involved generic constants
C>0
do not depend on
h
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that
Y_{T,h}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{H}_{0}^{1}),\,f\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H}_{0}^{1}))
,
I_{\tau}
is a time
partition of
[0,T]
.
Let
(Y_{h},Z_{h})
be the solution to (
2.14
). Then

(i)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\|Y_{h}(t)-Y_{h}(t_{n})\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]\leq C\tau\mathbb{E}\left[\|Y_{T,h}\|_{\mathbb{H}_{0}^{1}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\|f(t)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]\,.

(ii)

Assume further
\sup_{h>0}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\Delta_{h}Y_{T,h}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\bigr]<\infty
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\|\nabla\left(Y_{h}(t)-Y_{h}(t_{n})\right)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]\leq C\tau\mathbb{E}\left[\|\Delta_{h}Y_{T,h}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla Y_{T,h}\|_{\mathbb{H}_{0}^{1}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\|f(t)\|_{\mathbb{H}_{0}^{1}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]\,.

(iii)

Assume further
\sup_{h>0}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\Delta_{h}Y_{T,h}\|_{\mathbb{H}_{0}^{1}}^{2}\bigr]<\infty
and
f\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H}_{0}^{1}\cap\mathbb{H}^{2}))
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\|\Delta_{h}\left(Y_{h}(t)-Y_{h}(t_{n})\right)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]\leq C\tau\mathbb{E}\left[\|\Delta_{h}Y_{T,h}\|_{\mathbb{H}_{0}^{1}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\|f(t)\|_{\mathbb{H}_{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right].

Here, the constant
C>0
only depends on
Y_{T,h},\,f
and
T
.

Proof.

We only prove (i). The other statements can be proved in a similar vein.

By BSDE (
2.14
), we get

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\|Y_{h}(t)-Y_{h}(t_{n})\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]\leq C\tau\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\Delta_{h}Y_{h}(t)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\|\Pi_{h}^{1}f(t)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\|Z_{h}(t)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]\,\mathrm{d}t.

Applying Itô’s formula for
\|Y_{h}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}
and
\|\nabla Y_{h}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}
in (
2.14
), we see

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\|Z_{h}(t)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]\leq

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\left[\|Y_{T,h}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\|\Pi_{h}^{1}f(t)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right],

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\|\Delta_{h}Y_{h}(t)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]\leq

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla Y_{T,h}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\|\Pi_{h}^{1}f(t)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right].

Then (i) can be deduced by the above estimates.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose that
Y_{T,h}\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{H}_{0}^{1})
, and
f\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}))
satisfy

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|D_{t}Y_{T,h}\|_{\mathbb{H}_{0}^{1}}^{2}\bigr]+\sup_{0\leq\theta\leq T}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|D_{\theta}D_{t}Y_{T,h}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\bigr]<C,

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int_{t}^{T}\|D_{t}f(\tau)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\,{\rm d}\tau\Bigr]+\sup_{0\leq\theta\leq T}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int_{\theta\vee t}^{T}\|D_{\theta}D_{t}f(\tau)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\,{\rm d}\tau\Bigr]\leq C\,,

and for any
s,t\in[0,T]
with
s\leq t
,

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\left(D_{t}-D_{s}\right)Y_{T,h}\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\bigr]+\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int_{t}^{T}\|\left(D_{t}-D_{s}\right)f(\tau)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\,{\rm d}\tau\Bigr]\leq C|t-s|\,.

(3.1)

Then, it holds that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[\|Z_{h}(t)-Z_{h}(s)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\bigr]\leq C|t-s|\,.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2
,
we know that
Z_{h}(t)=D_{t}Y_{h}(t)
for all
0\leq t\leq T
, and therefore, for
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
,

\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[\|Z_{h}(t)-Z_{h}(s)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\bigr]\leq\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|D_{t}Y_{h}(t)-D_{s}Y_{h}(t)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\bigr]+\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|D_{s}Y_{h}(t)-D_{s}Y_{h}(s)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\bigr]\,.

(3.2)

In what follows, we estimate the two terms on the right-hand side of (
3.2
) independently.

Step 1.

Fix two
0\leq\theta_{2}\leq\theta_{1}\leq t\leq T
and define
\delta_{\theta}=D_{\theta_{1}}-D_{\theta_{2}}
.
By (
2.17
), we have the BSDE

\displaystyle\delta_{\theta}Y_{h}(t)-\delta_{\theta}Y_{h}(T)+\int_{t}^{T}\left[-\Delta_{h}\delta_{\theta}Y_{h}(s)+\Pi_{h}^{1}\delta_{\theta}f(s)\right]\,{\rm d}s=-\int_{t}^{T}\delta_{\theta}Z_{h}(s)\,{\rm d}W(s)\,\,\,\forall\,t\in[\theta_{1},T]\,.

(3.3)

Itô’s formula and Poincaré’s inequality lead to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\delta_{\theta}Y_{h}(t)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\bigr]+\int_{t}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla\delta_{\theta}Y_{h}(s)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}+\|\delta_{\theta}Z_{h}(s)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\right]\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\|\delta_{\theta}Y_{h}(T)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}+\int_{t}^{T}\|\Pi_{h}^{1}\delta_{\theta}f(s)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\,{\rm d}s\Bigr]\,.

Taking
\theta_{2}=s
and
\theta_{1}=t
and using (
3.1
) then lead to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|D_{t}Y_{h}(t)-D_{s}Y_{h}(t)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\bigr]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\|\left(D_{t}-D_{s}\right)Y_{h,T}\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}+\int_{t}^{T}\|\left(D_{t}-D_{s}\right)f(\tau)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\Bigr]

(3.4)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C|t-s|\,.

Step 2.
By (
2.17
), Itô’s isometry together with Poincaré’s inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|D_{s}Y_{h}(t)-D_{s}Y_{h}(s)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\bigr]

(3.5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\bigl\|\int_{s}^{t}\left[-\Delta_{h}D_{s}Y_{h}(\tau)+\Pi_{h}^{1}D_{s}f(\tau)\right]\,{\rm d}\tau+\int_{s}^{t}D_{s}Z_{h}(\tau)\,{\rm d}W(\tau)\bigr\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2|t-s|\,\int_{s}^{T}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\Delta_{h}D_{s}Y_{h}(\tau)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}+\|\Pi_{h}^{1}D_{s}f(\tau)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\bigr]\,{\rm d}\tau+2\,\int_{s}^{t}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|D_{s}Z_{h}(\tau)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\bigr]\,{\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C|t-s|\,\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\|\nabla D_{s}Y_{h}(T)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}+\int_{s}^{T}\|\Pi_{h}^{1}D_{s}f(\tau)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\,{\rm d}\tau\Bigr]

\displaystyle+C|t-s|\,\sup_{0\leq\theta\leq T}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\|D_{\theta}D_{t}Y_{T,h}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\int_{\theta\vee t}^{T}\|D_{\theta}D_{t}f(\tau)\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\,{\rm d}\tau\Bigr]\,.

Inserting (
3.5
) and (
3.4
) into (
3.2
) then settles the proof of the lemma.

3.2
A time-implicit space-time discretization of the
BSPDE (
2.10
)

We use an implicit time discretization on the mesh
I_{\tau}
to approximate
BSPDE
h
(
2.14
); we refer to it as
BSPDE
hτ
, and the discretization reads as follows: For every
0\leq n\leq N-1
, find
\bigl(Y^{n}_{h},Z^{n}_{h}\bigr)\in L^{2}_{{\mathcal{F}}_{t_{n}}}\bigl(\Omega;{\mathbb{V}}_{h}^{1}\times{\mathbb{V}}_{h}^{1}\bigr)
such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &[\mathds{1}-\tau\Delta_{h}]Y_{h}^{n}={\mathbb{E}}\bigl[Y^{n+1}_{h}\bigl|{\mathcal{F}}_{t_{n}}\bigr]-{\tau}\Pi_{h}^{1}f(t_{n})\,,\\
&Z_{h}^{n}=\frac{1}{\tau}{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[Y_{h}^{n+1}\Delta_{n+1}W\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{n}}\Bigr]\\
&Y_{h}^{N}=Y_{T,h}\,.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.6)

We introduce an auxiliary BSDE system on each time interval
[t_{n},t_{n+1}]
for the convergence analysis of (
3.6
): Find
(\overline{Y}_{h,n},\overline{Z}_{h,n})\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}\bigl(\Omega;C([t_{n},t_{n+1}];{\mathbb{V}}^{1}_{h})\times L^{2}(t_{n},t_{n+1};{\mathbb{V}}_{h}^{1})\bigr)
such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\overline{Y}_{h,n}(t)-\overline{Y}_{h,n}(t_{n+1})+\int_{t}^{t_{n+1}}\left[-\Delta_{h}{Y^{n}_{h}}+\Pi_{h}^{1}f({t_{n}})\right]\,{\rm d}s=-\int_{t}^{t_{n+1}}\,\overline{Z}_{h,n}(s)\,{\rm d}W(s)\\
&\overline{Y}_{h,n}(T)=Y_{T,h}\,.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.7)

We now construct
(\overline{Y}_{h},\overline{Z}_{h})\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}\bigl(\Omega;C([0,T];{\mathbb{V}}^{1}_{h})\times L^{2}(0,T;{\mathbb{V}}_{h}^{1})\bigr)
via

\bigl(\overline{Y}_{h}\bigl|_{[t_{n},t_{n+1}]},\overline{Z}_{h}\bigl|_{[t_{n},t_{n+1}]}\bigr):=(\overline{Y}_{h,n},\overline{Z}_{h,n})
.
Note that the integrand in the drift is evaluated with the help of
the solution part
\{Y^{n}_{h}\}_{n=0}^{N}
from (
3.7
).

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\{(Y^{n}_{h},Z^{n}_{h})\}_{n=0}^{N-1}
solve (
3.6
), and

(\overline{Y}_{h},\overline{Z}_{h})
solve (
3.7
). For all
0\leq n\leq N-1
,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} Y^{n}_{h}=\overline{Y}_{h}(t_{n})\,,\qquad{Z^{n}_{h}=\frac{1}{\tau}{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\overline{Z}_{h}(s)\,{\rm d}s\Bigl|{\mathcal{F}}_{t_{n}}\Bigl]}\,.\end{aligned}

Proof.

The first identity is immediate; the second follows from multiplication of (
3.7
) with the admissible
\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}1\,{\rm d}W(s)
, and
application of conditional expectation
{\mathbb{E}}[\cdot|{\mathcal{F}}_{t_{n}}]
.

We may now prove a strong error estimate for the first component
of
(Y_{h},Z_{h})
that solves (
2.14
).

Theorem 3.4
.

Suppose that
Y_{T,h}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{H}_{0}^{1}),\,\Delta_{h}Y_{T,h}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{L}^{2}),\,f\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H}_{0}^{1}))
as
well as

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[\|f(t)-f(t_{n})\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\bigr]\leq C\tau\,.

Let
(Y_{h},Z_{h})
solve (
2.14
), and
\{(Y^{n}_{h},Z^{n}_{h})\}_{n=0}^{N-1}
solve (
3.6
). There exists a constant
C\equiv(Y_{T,h},f,T)>0
such that

\max_{0\leq n\leq N}{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[\|Y_{h}(t_{n})-Y_{h}^{n}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}\bigr]+\tau\,\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\nabla(Y_{h}(t_{n})-Y_{h}^{n})\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\bigr]\leq C\tau\,.

(3.8)

Proof.

Consider
(\overline{Y}_{h},\overline{Z}_{h})
from (
3.7
), and define

\{e_{n}\}_{n=0}^{N-1}
, where each
e_{n}=Y_{h}(t_{n})-\overline{Y}_{h}(t_{n})
is a
{\mathbb{V}}_{h}^{1}
-valued random variable. Subtracting (
3.7
) from (
2.14
) yields
{\mathbb{P}}
-a.s.

\displaystyle e_{n}-e_{n+1}-\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\Delta_{h}e_{n}\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\Delta_{h}\bigl[Y_{h}(s)-Y_{h}(t_{n})\bigr]-\Pi_{h}^{1}[f(s)-f(t_{n})]\,{\rm d}s

(3.9)

\displaystyle+\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\bigl[Z_{h}(s)-\overline{Z}_{h}(s)\bigr]\,{\rm d}W(s)\,.

Fixing one realization
\omega\in\Omega
, testing with the admissible

e_{n}(\omega)\in{\mathbb{V}}_{h}^{1}
, using binomial formula, and then taking expectation, and Poincare’s and Young’s inequality lead to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\|e_{n}\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}-\|e_{n+1}\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}+\|e_{n}-e_{n+1}\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}+2\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\|\nabla e_{n}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}\,{\rm d}s\Bigr]

(3.10)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\frac{1}{2}\bigl\|\nabla\bigl[Y_{h}(s)-Y_{h}(t_{n})\bigr]\bigr\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\|f(s)-f(t_{n})\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\Bigr]\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\|\nabla e_{n}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+\|e_{n}-e_{n+1}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\Bigr]\,{\rm d}s+\frac{\tau}{2}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|e_{n+1}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\bigr]^{2}\,.

Subsequently, the discrete Gronwall inequality leads to

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq n\leq N}{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[\|e_{n}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}\bigr]\leq 2e^{T}\,\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\bigl\|\nabla\bigl[Y_{h}(s)-Y_{h}(t_{n})\bigr]\bigr\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}+\|f(s)-f(t_{n})\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\Bigr]\,{\rm d}s\,.

(3.11)

Then, summing up over all steps of (
3.10
) yields

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\|\nabla e_{n}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right]

(3.12)

\displaystyle\leq\tau\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|e_{n+1}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\bigr]^{2}+2\,\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\bigl\|\nabla\bigl[Y_{h}(s)-Y_{h}(t_{n})\bigr]\bigr\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}+\|f(s)-f(t_{n})\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\Bigr]\,{\rm d}s\,.

Then, (
3.11
), (
3.12
) together with (ii) of Lemma
3.1
, and
3.3
lead to the desired estimate.

By Theorems
2.1
,
3.4
and Lemma
3.1
(i), we thus get the following convergence rate for the approximation
\{Y_{h}^{n}\}_{n=0}^{N}
of the first solution component
Y
to (
2.11
) via
the space-time discretization scheme (
3.6
),

\max_{0\leq n\leq N}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|Y(t_{n})-Y_{h}^{n}\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\bigr]+\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla\big(Y(t)-Y_{h}^{n}\big)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\big]\,\mathrm{d}t\leq C\left(\tau+h^{2}\right)\,.

(3.13)

Remark 3.5
.

If the drift term of (
2.10
) is
-\Delta Y(t,x)+f\bigl(t,x,Y(t,x)\bigr)

appears in the drift term.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
(Y_{h},Z_{h})
solve (
2.13
), where data satisfy the assumptions in
Lemma
3.1
(ii), Lemma
3.2
, as well as

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[\|f(t)-f(t_{n})\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\bigr]\leq C\tau\,.

Let
\{(Y^{n}_{h},Z^{n}_{h})\}_{n=0}^{N-1}
solve (
3.6
).
There exists a constant
C\equiv(Y_{T},f,T)>0
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|Z_{h}(t)-Z_{h}^{n}\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\bigr]\,{\rm d}t\leq C\tau\,.

The proof begins with an estimate for
Z_{h}-\overline{Z}_{h}
, which exploits time regularity properties of the solution

(Y_{h},Z_{h})
in stronger norms; cf. Lemma
3.1
, (ii).
Moreover, the following technical result is needed; see also
[
23
]
.

Lemma 3.7
.

For any
\varphi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{K})
and
0\leq s<t\leq T
, define

\varphi_{0}=\frac{1}{t-s}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int_{s}^{t}\varphi(\tau)\,{\rm d}\tau\big|\mathcal{F}_{s}\Bigr]\,.

For any
\xi\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{s}}(\Omega;\mathbb{K})
there holds

Proof.

Let
\{\phi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{K}
, and
\Pi_{n}
be the projection from
\mathbb{K}

to
\mbox{span}\{\phi_{i}:i=1,2,\cdots,n\}
.
For any

, one has

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int_{s}^{t}\|\varphi(\tau)-\xi\|^{2}_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{\,}{\rm d}\tau\Bigr]\geq\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int_{s}^{t}\|\Pi_{n}\bigl(\varphi(\tau)-\xi\bigr)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{K}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\Bigr]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int_{s}^{t}\|\Pi_{n}\bigl(\varphi(\tau)-\varphi_{0}\bigr)\|_{\mathbb{K}}^{2}+\|\Pi_{n}(\varphi_{0}-\xi)\|_{\mathbb{K}}^{2}+2\bigl(\Pi_{n}\bigl(\varphi(\tau)-\varphi_{0}\bigr),\Pi_{n}(\varphi_{0}-\xi)\bigr)_{{\mathbb{K}}}\,\mathrm{\rm d}\tau\Bigr]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int_{s}^{t}\|\Pi_{n}(\varphi(\tau)-\varphi_{0})\|_{\mathbb{K}}^{2}+\|\Pi_{n}(\varphi_{0}-\xi)|_{\mathbb{K}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\Bigr]

\displaystyle+2\mathbb{E}\Biggl[\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\bigl(\int_{s}^{t}\Pi_{n}\varphi(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau-\mathbb{E}\bigl[\int_{s}^{t}\Pi_{n}\varphi(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\big|\mathcal{F}_{s}\bigr],\Pi_{n}(\varphi_{0}-\xi)\bigr)_{{\mathbb{K}}}\big|\mathcal{F}_{s}\Bigr]\Biggr]

Since,
\varphi_{0}
and
\xi
are
\mathcal{F}_{s}
-measurable, the last term vanishes, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\bigl(\int_{s}^{t}\Pi_{n}\varphi(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau-\mathbb{E}\bigl[\int_{s}^{t}\Pi_{n}\varphi(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\big|\mathcal{F}_{s}\bigr],\Pi_{n}(\varphi_{0}-\xi)\bigr)_{{\mathbb{K}}}\big|\mathcal{F}_{s}\Bigr]\right]

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathbb{E}\left[\bigl(\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int_{s}^{t}\Pi_{n}\varphi(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau-\mathbb{E}\bigl[\int_{s}^{t}\Pi_{n}\varphi(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\big|\mathcal{F}_{s}\bigr]\Bigl|{\mathcal{F}}_{s}\Bigr],\Pi_{n}(\varphi_{0}-\xi)\bigr)_{{\mathbb{K}}}\right]=0\,.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int_{s}^{t}\|\varphi(\tau)-\xi\|^{2}_{\mathbb{K}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\Bigr]\geq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int_{s}^{t}\|\Pi_{n}(\varphi(\tau)-\varphi_{0})\|_{\mathbb{K}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\Bigr]+\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int_{s}^{t}\|\Pi_{n}(\varphi_{0}-\xi)|_{\mathbb{K}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\Bigr]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int_{s}^{t}\|\Pi_{n}(\varphi(\tau)-\varphi_{0})\|_{\mathbb{K}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\Bigr]\,.

By letting

, we may therefore conclude

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int_{s}^{t}\|\varphi(\tau)-\varphi_{0}\|_{\mathbb{K}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\Bigr]=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int_{s}^{t}\|\Pi_{n}\bigl(\varphi(\tau)-\varphi_{0}\bigr)\|_{\mathbb{K}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\Bigr]\leq\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int_{s}^{t}\|\varphi(\tau)-\xi\|^{2}_{\mathbb{K}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\Bigr]\,,

which completes the proof.

\displaystyle\left\{X\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}\bigl(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{V}_{h}^{1})\bigr):\ X(t)=X(t_{n}),\,\,\forall t\in[t_{n},t_{n+1}),\,\,n=0,1,\cdots,\,N-1\right\}\,,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{U}}_{h\tau}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left\{U\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}\bigl(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{V}_{h}^{0})\bigr):\ U(t)=U(t_{n}),\,\,\forall t\in[t_{n},t_{n+1}),\,\,n=0,1,\cdots,\,N-1\right\}\,,

and for any

\displaystyle\|X\|_{\mathbb{X}_{h\tau}}:=\bigg(\tau\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\big[\|X(t_{n})\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\big]\bigg)^{1/2},\,\,\|U\|_{\mathbb{U}_{h\tau}}:=\bigg(\tau\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\big[\|U(t_{n})\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\big]\bigg)^{1/2}.

Problem

hτ
then reads as follows: Find an optimal pair

which minimizes the cost functional

(4.13)

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|X_{h\tau}(t_{n})-\widetilde{X}(t_{n})\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\bigr]+\frac{\tau}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|U_{h\tau}(t_{n})\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\bigr]+\frac{\alpha}{2}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|X_{h\tau}(T)-\widetilde{X}(T)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\bigr]\,,

subject to the difference equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} &X_{h\tau}(t_{n+1})-X_{h\tau}(t_{n})=\tau\bigl[\Delta_{h}X_{h\tau}(t_{n+1})+{\Pi_{h}^{1}U_{h\tau}(t_{n})}\bigr]+\Pi_{h}^{1}\sigma(t_{n})\Delta_{n+1}W\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad n=0,1,\cdots,N-1\,,\\

.

Theorem 4.2

(4.16)

Step 1.

\displaystyle A_{0}:=\left(\mathds{1}-\tau\Delta_{h}\right)^{-1}

(4.17)

Hence, by iteration we arrive at

\displaystyle X_{h\tau}(t_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{0}^{n}X_{h\tau}({0})+\tau\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}A_{0}^{n-j}\Pi_{h}^{1}U_{h\tau}(t_{j})+\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{0}^{n+1-j}\Pi_{h}^{1}\sigma(t_{j-1})\Delta_{j}W

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle(\Gamma{\Pi_{h}^{1}X_{0}})(t_{n})+(LU_{h\tau})(t_{n})+f(t_{n})\,.

Here,
\Gamma:\mathbb{V}_{h}^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{X}_{h\tau}
and

Below, we use the abbreviations

\widehat{\Gamma}{\Pi_{h}^{1}X_{0}}:=\Gamma{\Pi_{h}^{1}X_{0}}(T)\,,\qquad\widehat{L}{U_{h\tau}}:=(LU_{h\tau})(T)\,,\qquad\widehat{f}=f(T)\,.

(4.19)

Claim
: For any

, and any

(4.20)

where
(Y_{0},Z_{0})
solves the following backward stochastic difference equation:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &Y_{0}(t_{n+1})-Y_{0}(t_{n})=\tau\bigl(-\Delta_{h}Y_{0}(t_{n})+\xi(t_{n+1})\bigr)+\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}Z_{0}(t)\,\mathrm{d}W(t)\,\quad n=0,1,\cdots,N-1\,,\\
&Y_{0}(t_{N})=Y_{0}(T)=0\,,\end{aligned}\right.

(4.21)

and

&Y_{1}(T)=-\eta\,.\end{aligned}\right.

Proof of Claim:
The existence and the uniqueness of solutions to (

Note that

Y_{0}(t_{j})=\mathbb{E}\bigl[A_{0}Y_{0}(t_{j+1})-\tau A_{0}\xi(t_{j+1})\big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{j}}\bigr].

(4.22)

With the similar procedure as that in
(
4.17

) and (
4.21
)
2
,

\displaystyle Y_{0}(t_{j})=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[A_{0}^{N-j}Y_{0}(t_{N})\big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{j}}\bigr]-\mathbb{E}\bigl[\tau\sum_{k=j+1}^{N}A_{0}^{k-j}\xi(t_{k})\big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{j}}\bigr]

(4.23)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\tau\sum_{k=j+1}^{N}A_{0}^{k-j}\xi(t_{k})\big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{j}}\Bigr]\,.

Let
U_{h\tau}\in{\mathbb{U}}_{h\tau}
be arbitrary. By the definition of

) and the fact

, we can calculate that

\displaystyle\tau\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\bigl((LU_{h\tau})(t_{n}),\xi(t_{n})\bigr)_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\Bigr]=\tau\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\bigl(\tau\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}A_{0}^{n-j}\Pi_{h}^{1}U_{h\tau}(t_{j}),\xi(t_{n})\bigr)_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\qquad{=}\tau\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\Bigl({\Pi_{h}^{1}U_{h\tau}(t_{j})},\mathbb{E}\bigl[\tau\sum_{n=j+1}^{N}A_{0}^{n-j}\xi(t_{n})\big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{j}}\bigr]\Bigr)_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\Bigr]\,.

\displaystyle\qquad=\tau\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\Bigl({U_{h\tau}(t_{j})},{\Pi_{h}^{0}}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\tau\sum_{k=j+1}^{N}A_{0}^{k-j}\xi(t_{k})\big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{j}}\bigr]\Bigr)_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\Bigr]\,.\qquad\qquad\quad

Because of (

which is the first part of the claim.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[\big(\widehat{L}U_{h\tau},\eta\big)_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\bigr]=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\Bigl(\Pi_{h}^{1}U_{h\tau}(t_{j}),\mathbb{E}\bigl[A_{0}^{N-j}\eta\big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{j}}\bigr]\Bigr)_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\Bigr]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\Bigl(U_{h\tau}(t_{j}),-\Pi_{h}^{0}Y_{1}(t_{j})\Bigr)_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\Bigr]\qquad\forall\,{U_{h\tau}\in{\mathbb{U}}_{h\tau}}\,.

Step 2.
By (

\displaystyle{\cal J}_{\tau}(X_{h\tau},U_{h\tau})

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{2}\left[\|X_{h\tau}-\Pi_{\tau}{\widetilde{X}}\|_{L^{2}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;{\mathbb{L}}^{2}))}^{2}+\|U_{h\tau}\|_{L^{2}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;{\mathbb{L}}^{2}))}^{2}+\alpha\|{X_{h\tau}(T)}-{\widetilde{X}}(T)\|_{L^{2}_{{\mathcal{F}}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{L}^{2})}^{2}\right]

Theorem 4.3
.

Suppose that

(4.25)

Let
(X_{h}^{*},Y_{h},Z_{h},U^{*}_{h})

\displaystyle{\rm(i)}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\left\|U^{*}_{h}(t)-U^{*}_{h\tau}(t_{k})\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\,{\rm d}t\Bigr]\leq C\tau\,;

\displaystyle{\rm(ii)}

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq k\leq N}{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[\|X_{h}^{*}(t_{k})-X_{h\tau}^{*}(t_{k})\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\bigr]+{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\tau\sum_{k=1}^{N}\|X_{h}^{*}(t_{k})-X^{*}_{h\tau}(t_{k})\|_{\mathbb{H}_{0}^{1}}^{2}\Bigr]\leq C\tau\,;

Proof.

We divide the proof into three steps.

Step 1.

We follow the argumentation in the proof of Theorem

\displaystyle\quad\leq{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\bigl(D\widehat{\mathcal{J}}_{h\tau}(U^{*}_{h\tau}),U^{*}_{h\tau}-\Pi_{\tau}U^{*}_{h}\bigr)_{{L^{2}(0,T;{\mathbb{L}}^{2})}}-\bigl(D\widehat{\mathcal{J}}_{h\tau}(\Pi_{\tau}U^{*}_{h}),U^{*}_{h\tau}-\Pi_{\tau}U^{*}_{h}\bigr)_{{L^{2}(0,T;{\mathbb{L}}^{2})}}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\quad={\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\bigl(D\widehat{\mathcal{J}}_{h}(U^{*}_{h})-D\widehat{\mathcal{J}}_{h}(\Pi_{\tau}U^{*}_{h}),U^{*}_{h\tau}-\Pi_{\tau}U^{*}_{h}\bigr)_{L^{2}(0,T;{\mathbb{L}}^{2})}\Bigr]

(4.27)

\displaystyle\qquad+{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\bigl(D\widehat{\mathcal{J}}_{h}(\Pi_{\tau}U^{*}_{h})-D\widehat{\mathcal{J}}_{h\tau}(\Pi_{\tau}U^{*}_{h}),U^{*}_{h\tau}-\Pi_{\tau}U^{*}_{h}\bigr)_{L^{2}(0,T;{\mathbb{L}}^{2})}\Bigr]\,.

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[\left\|U^{*}_{h\tau}-{\Pi_{\tau}U^{*}_{h}}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T;{\mathbb{L}}^{2})}^{2}\bigr]

\displaystyle\quad\leq 2\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|D\widehat{\mathcal{J}}_{h}(U^{*}_{h})-D\widehat{\mathcal{J}}_{h}(\Pi_{\tau}U^{*}_{h})\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2})}^{2}\bigr]+2\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|D\widehat{\mathcal{J}}_{h}(\Pi_{\tau}U^{*}_{h})-D\widehat{\mathcal{J}}_{h\tau}(\Pi_{\tau}U^{*}_{h})\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2})}^{2}\bigr]

\displaystyle\quad=:2I^{\prime}+2II^{\prime}\,.

\displaystyle I^{\prime}=

(4.29)

\displaystyle\leq

5
The gradient descent method to solve
SLQ
hτ

By Theorem
4.2
, solving minimization problem
SLQ
hτ
is
equivalent to solving the system of coupled forward-backward difference equations
(
4.15
) and (
4.16
). We may exploit the variational
character of problem
SLQ
hτ
to construct a gradient descent method

SLQ
{}_{h\tau}^{\rm grad}

where approximate iterates of the optimal control
U^{*}_{h\tau}
in the
Hilbert space
\mathbb{U}_{h\tau}
are obtained; see also
[
19
,
14
]
.

Algorithm 5.1
.

(SLQ
{}_{h\tau}^{\rm grad}
)

Let
U_{h\tau}^{(0)}\in\mathbb{U}_{h\tau}
, and fix
\kappa>0
. For any
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, update
U_{h\tau}^{(\ell)}\in{\mathbb{U}}_{h\tau}
as follows:

1.

Compute
X_{h\tau}^{(\ell)}\in\mathbb{X}_{h\tau}
by

\left\{\begin{aligned} &[\mathds{1}-\tau\Delta_{h}]X^{(\ell)}_{h\tau}(t_{n+1})=X^{(\ell)}_{h\tau}(t_{n})+\tau\Pi_{h}^{1}U^{(\ell)}_{h\tau}(t_{n})+\Pi_{h}^{1}\sigma(t_{n})\Delta_{n+1}W\quad n=0,1,\cdots,N-1\,,\\
&X_{h\tau}^{(\ell)}(0)=\Pi_{h}^{1}X_{0}\,.\end{aligned}\right.

2.

Use
X_{h\tau}^{(\ell)}\in\mathbb{X}_{h\tau}
to compute
Y_{h\tau}^{(\ell)}\in\mathbb{X}_{h\tau}
via

\left\{\begin{aligned} &[\mathds{1}-\tau\Delta_{h}]Y^{(\ell)}_{h\tau}(t_{n})={\mathbb{E}}\left[Y^{(\ell)}_{h\tau}(t_{n+1})-{\tau}\bigl(X^{(\ell)}_{h\tau}(t_{n+1})-\Pi_{h}^{1}\widetilde{X}(t_{n+1})\bigr)\bigl|{\mathcal{F}}_{t_{n}}\right]\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad n=0,1,\cdots,N-1\,,\\
&Y_{h\tau}^{(\ell)}(T)=-\alpha\bigl(X_{h\tau}^{(\ell)}(T)-\Pi_{h}^{1}\widetilde{X}(T)\bigr)\,.\end{aligned}\right.

3.

Compute the update
U_{h\tau}^{(\ell+1)}\in{\mathbb{U}}_{h\tau}
via

\displaystyle U^{(\ell+1)}_{h\tau}=U^{(\ell)}_{h\tau}-\frac{1}{\kappa}\left(U^{(\ell)}_{h\tau}-\Pi_{h}^{0}Y_{h\tau}^{(\ell)}\right)\,.

Note that Steps 1 and 2 are now decoupled: the first step requires to solve a space-time discretization (
2.7
) of
SPDE
(
2.2
), while the second requires to solve the space-time discretization (
3.6
)
1
of the
BSPDE
(
4.1
)
2
.
We refer to related works on how to approximate conditional expectations

[

,

,
1
,

]
;
a similar method to

{}_{h\tau}^{\rm grad}
to solve problem
SLQ
hτ

has been proposed in
[

]
.

We want to show convergence of
SLQ
{}_{h\tau}^{\rm grad}
for
\kappa>0
sufficiently large and
\ell\uparrow\infty
. For this purpose, we recall the notations
{\mathcal{S}}_{h\tau},{\mathcal{T}}_{h\tau},\widehat{\mathcal{J}}_{h\tau}
introduced in Section
4.2
. For this purpose, we first recall Lipschitz continuity of

D\widehat{\mathcal{J}}_{h\tau}
:
since

D^{2}\widehat{\cal J}_{h\tau}(U_{h\tau})=\left(\mathds{1}+L^{*}L+\alpha\widehat{L}^{*}\widehat{L}\right)U_{h\tau}\,,

where operators

are defined in (

\displaystyle\|D\widehat{\cal J}_{h\tau}\left(U^{1}_{h\tau}\right)-D\widehat{\cal J}_{h\tau}\left(U^{2}_{h\tau}\right)\|_{\mathbb{U}_{h\tau}}\leq K\left\|U^{1}_{h\tau}-U^{2}_{h\tau}\right\|_{\mathbb{U}_{h\tau}}\,.

Indeed,
noting that
\|(\mathds{1}-\tau\Delta_{h})^{-1}\|_{{\cal L}(\mathbb{V}_{h}^{1};\mathbb{V}_{h}^{1})}\leq 1
,
we conclude

\displaystyle\|LU_{h\tau}\|_{\mathbb{X}_{h\tau}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tau\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|LU_{h\tau}(t_{n})\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\bigr]=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tau\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\|\tau\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\left[(\mathds{1}-\tau\Delta_{h})^{-1}\right]^{n-j}\Pi_{h}^{1}U_{h\tau}(t_{j})\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\Bigr]

\displaystyle T^{2}\|U_{h\tau}\|_{\mathbb{U}_{h\tau}}^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\|\widehat{L}U_{h\tau}\|_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{L}^{2})}^{2}=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\big\|\tau\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\left[(\mathds{1}-\tau\Delta_{h})^{-1}\right]^{N-j}\Pi_{h}^{1}U_{h\tau}(t_{j})\big\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\Bigr]\leq T\|U_{h\tau}\|_{\mathbb{U}_{h\tau}}^{2}\,.

\displaystyle K=\|\mathds{1}+L^{*}L+\alpha\widehat{L}^{*}\widehat{L}\|_{{\cal L}(\mathbb{U}_{h\tau};\mathbb{U}_{h\tau})}\leq 1+\alpha T+T^{2}.

.

Non-Hermitian Floquet-Free Analytically Solvable Time Dependant Systems
Hamed Ghaemi-Dizicheh, Hamidreza Ramezani

Abstract
The non-Hermitian models, which are symmetric under parity ( P P ) and time-reversal ( T T ) operators, are the cornerstone for the fabrication of new ultra-sensitive optoelectronic devices. However, providing the gain in such systems usually demands precise contorol of nonlinear processes, limiting their application. In this paper, to bypass this obstacle, we introduce a class of time-dependent non-Hermitian Hamiltonians (not necessarily Floquet) that can describe a two-level system with temporally modulated on-site potential and couplings. We show that implementing an appropriate non-Unitary gauge transformation converts the original system to an effective one with a balanced gain and loss. This will allow us to derive the evolution of states analytically. Our proposed class of Hamiltonians can be employed in different platforms such as electronic circuits, acoustics, and photonics to design structures with hidden P ​ T PT -symmetry potentially without imaginary onsite amplification and absorption mechanism to obtain an exceptional point.



I
Introduction

The non-Hermitian models in physics have become a vast research area in recent years, including condensed matter physics
Rui et al. 2019
, photonics
Longhi 2010
;
Ding et al. 2015
, biophysics
Chou et al. 2011
, and acoustic

, distinct transport effects

, relocation of topological edge states

A well-known approach to making a tight-binding model non-Hermitian is introducing complex onsite potential, playing the role of gain (loss) in photonic models
Schomerus 2013
. One can also stagger inter (intra) cell hopping amplitudes and develop non-reciprocity through the system

;

;

Among non-Hermitian hamiltonians with gain and loss, the ones with balanced amplification and absorption are known as parity time-reversal (
PT
) symmetric hamiltonians and attracted more intention.
PT
-symmetric systems have been studied in both continuous and discrete models
Feng et al. 2017
;
El-Ganainy et al. 2018
and extensively investigated theoretically and experimentally in photonic lattices
El-Ganainy et al. 2007
;
Makris et al. 2008

;
Regensburger et al. 2012
, microring resonator
Peng et al. 2014a
;

Peng et al. 2014b

;
Chitsazi et al. 2017
;
León-Montiel et al. 2018
.

In
PT
-symmetric non-Hermitian systems, specific singular points are known as exceptional points (EPs) where at least two or more eigenvalues and corresponding eigenvectors of the hamiltonians coalesce. In recent years, the EPs have led to many interesting phenomena observed in different physical systems
Berry 2004
;
Bender 2007
;
Rotter 2009
;
Moiseyev 2011
;
Heiss 2012
;
Cao and Wiersig 2015

Indeed, EPs separate the broken
PT
-symmetric regions, in which the eigenvalues of the non-Hermitian Hamiltonian become complex, from the unbroken
PT
-symmetric phase with real-valued eigenvalues.
Many nontrivial features such as unidirectional invisibility
Lin et al. 2011
, invisible sensor
Fleury et al. 2015
, single-mode lasing
Feng et al. 2014
, sensitive readout
Dong et al. 2019
and robust wireless power transfer
Assawaworrarit and Fan 2020
can be investigated by creating and developing
PT
-symmetric structure. However, from a practical point of view, constructing delicate balanced gain and loss is a rigorous requirement that hinders the possibility of implementing
PT
-symmetry and studying its interesting features in specifically quantum systems.

In addition, fundamental obstacles such as gain-induced noise and instabilities arise motivation to look for a different route of achieving
PT
-symmetry without gain. The gain-free models with a hidden
PT
-symmetry capture various research interests
Li et al. 2020a
;
Li et al. 2020b
;
Guo et al. 2009
;
Ornigotti and Szameit 2014
;
Feng et al. 2013
;
Peng et al. 2014a
;
Liu et al. 2019
.

The key approaches to design a system benefit hidden
PT
-symmetry, are based on nonlinear phenomena
Jiang et al. 2019
or by making use of a temporally modulated coupling. In the latter method, a unitary gauge transformation leads to an effective system whose Hamiltonian is invariant under parity and time reversal. A well-known platform to explore such a gauge transformation is an electronic system. For example, a transient
PT
-symmetry can be triggered by the switching on and off of electronic devices
Yang et al. 2022
.

A larger class of Hamiltonian with hidden
PT
-symmetry can be developed and studied by releasing the unitarity condition on the transformation to reach the effective Hamiltonian. This approach shapes the main idea of this paper.

In this study, we introduce a general class of time-dependent two-level non-Hermitian Hamiltonians with a hidden
PT
-symmetry which can be revealed by implementing a non-unitary gauge transformation. The main aspect of this approach is its generality. Indeed, our proposed Hamiltonian is not
necessarily floquet
which makes it applicable to the vast range of time-dependent two-level systems. In addition, we formulated our two-level Hamiltonian with an arbitrary onsite potential. In the case of onsite potential with zero imaginary part, the system can be transformed into the effective one with balanced gain and loss. Therefore our proposed method can bypass the usual difficulties in realizing amplification and absorption mechanism. The two-level Hamiltonian investigated in this letter can model acoustics, electronics, and photonics systems whose couplings are time or coordinate dependant.

Besides, we show that the analysis of state evolution described by this class of Hamiltonian can be analytically obtained regardless of adiabatic condition. The exact solutions are helpful to predict and manipulate the system’s behavior precisely when it encircles EPs
Hassan et al. 2017
.

PT
-symmetry in the model. In section
III

PT
-symmetric phases. The system’s time evolution is studied analytically in the appendix.

\displaystyle H(t)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\omega_{1}(t)&\dfrac{\nu f_{1}(t)}{f_{2}(t)}\\
\dfrac{\nu^{\prime}f_{2}(t)}{f_{1}(t)}&\omega_{2}(t)\end{array}\right),

i\partial_{t}\Psi(t)=H(t)\Psi(t),

(3)

\Psi(t)=(\psi_{1}(t),\psi_{2}(t))^{T}

3

We introduce time-dependent transformation
\Psi(t)=A(t)\chi(t)

II

(4)

\|\Psi(t)\|=\|\chi(t)\|

A(t)

\displaystyle A(t)=\exp\left(-i\int^{t}\Omega_{-}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\sqrt{\frac{f_{1}(t)}{f_{2}(t)}}&0\\
0&\sqrt{\frac{f_{2}(t)}{f_{1}(t)}}\end{array}\right),

where,
\Omega_{\pm}(t):=(\omega_{1}(t)\pm\omega_{2}(t))/2

A(t)

4

(7)

where

(8)

\chi(t)=(\zeta_{-}(t),\zeta_{+}(t))^{T}

By choosing a specific form of time transformation

II

\dfrac{d^{2}\zeta_{\mp}}{d\tau^{2}}\pm\left[\partial_{\tau}\Gamma(\tau)\mp i\Gamma(\tau)^{2}\pm 1\right]\zeta_{\mp}=0.

(10)

In driving the above differential equations, we scaled variables such as

\left(\dfrac{\Gamma(t)}{\sqrt{\nu^{\prime}\nu}},\sqrt{\nu^{\prime}\nu}t\right)\rightarrow(\Gamma(\tau),\tau).

(11)

IV
Conclusion

In summary, in this paper, we propose a class of time-dependent Hamiltonian describing a two-level system with the hidden
PT
-symmetry. The hidden symmetric can be revealed by implementing a specific non-Unitary gauge transformation which transforms the original Hamiltonian with temporally modulated couplings and onsite potentials to an effective one with balanced gain and loss. The dynamics of the effective system can be analytically extracted. Time-dependent systems are not usually analytically solvable. Thus, using our method we can propose a class of systems that one can analytically drive the condition for the existence of exceptional points and thus use it for determining other applications of exceptional points including sensing. The approach of this paper can provide a protocol to design and develop a different type of system with a
PT
-symmetric characteristic without encountering the obstacles of injecting gain. We demonstrated how our method can be applied to electronic devices. However, photonics models may have a potential application. The main experimental challenge in developing a photonics application is designing a system with time-varying couplings and onsite potentials. Microwave Smoothly deformed metallic waveguides in the microwave and helical waveguides in optics have the potential to be used in the design of driven photonics systems. In such a system, the deformed waveguide is extended along the
x
-axis, allowing us to achieve the spatially modulated couplings
Rechtsman et al. 2013
;
Doppler et al. 2016
, where
x
now represents time in our approach.

Case (b).
In the second scenario, we choose

\zeta_{\mp}

(43)

Case (c).
The following relation gives the third model we study here for effective on-site potential

\Gamma(\tau)=\alpha e^{i\gamma\tau}-\beta.

(47)

By introducing
\zeta_{\mp}:=e^{-\eta_{\mp}/2}W_{\mp}(\eta_{\mp})
, the equation (
10
) transforms to

\eta_{\mp}^{2}\dfrac{d^{2}W_{\mp}}{d\eta_{\mp}^{2}}+\eta_{\mp}(1-\eta_{\mp})\dfrac{dW_{\mp}}{d\eta_{\mp}}\pm\left[\dfrac{\alpha_{1}^{-1}+\alpha_{2}}{\gamma}\mp\frac{\alpha_{2}^{2}}{\gamma^{2}}\right]W_{\mp}=0,

(48)

where

\displaystyle\eta_{\mp}:=\mp\dfrac{2i\alpha}{\gamma}e^{i\gamma\tau},

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}:=(-i\beta+\sqrt{1-\beta^{2}})^{-1},

(49)

\displaystyle\alpha_{2}:=\sqrt{1-\beta^{2}}.

(50)

From here, one can obtain the general solution for
W_{\mp}
such that

\displaystyle W_{-}=\eta_{-}^{\alpha_{2}/\gamma}[c_{1}^{(-)}U(\dfrac{\alpha_{1}}{\gamma},1+\dfrac{2\alpha_{2}}{\gamma},\eta_{-})+

\displaystyle~~~~~~c_{2}^{(-)}L(\dfrac{\alpha_{1}^{-1}+2\alpha_{2}}{\gamma},\dfrac{2\alpha_{2}}{\gamma},\eta_{-})],

(51)

\displaystyle W_{+}=\eta_{+}^{\alpha_{2}/\gamma}[c_{1}^{(+)}U(\dfrac{\alpha_{1}^{-1}}{\gamma},1+\dfrac{2\alpha_{2}}{\gamma},\eta_{+})+

\displaystyle~~~~~~c_{2}^{(+)}L(-\dfrac{\alpha_{1}^{-1}}{\gamma},\dfrac{2\alpha_{2}}{\gamma},\eta_{+})],

(52)

where
U
represents confluent hypergeometric functions of the second kind, and
L
stands for Laguerre polynomials which satisfy the following properties
Zwillinger and Jeffrey 2007

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dz}U(m_{1},m_{2},z)=-m_{1}U(m_{1}+1,m_{2}+2,z),

(53)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dz}L(m_{3},m_{2},z)=-m_{3}L(m_{3}-1,m_{2}+1,z),

(54)

\displaystyle L(m_{3},m_{2},z)=\frac{(m_{2}+1)_{m_{3}}}{m_{1}!}F_{1}(-m_{3},m_{2}+1,z).

(55)

In the third relation,
(m_{2}+1)_{m_{1}}
is Pochhammer symbol, and
F_{1}
represents confluent hypergeometric functions of the first kind. The coefficients
c_{1,2}^{({\mp})}
depend on initial conditions. By following the method given in
Hassan et al. 2017
, we can obtain constants by using the Transfer Matrix method

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}c_{1}^{(-)}\\
c_{2}^{(-)}\end{array}\right)=z_{0}^{-m}e^{z_{0}}\left[\mathbf{M}_{0}-z_{1}\mathbf{M}_{1}\right]^{-1}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a\\
b\end{array}\right),

(56)

where the transfer matrix
\mathbf{M}_{j}
is obtained as

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}_{0}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbf{U}^{(0)}&\mathbf{L}^{(0)}\\
z_{3}\mathbf{U}^{(0)}&z_{3}\mathbf{L}^{(0)}\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}_{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
\mathbf{U}^{(1)}&\mathbf{L}^{(1)}\end{array}\right).

In the above relations, we introduce

\displaystyle z_{0}:=\dfrac{-2i\alpha}{\gamma},

\displaystyle z_{1}:=2\alpha,

(63)

\displaystyle z_{3}:=(\dfrac{z_{1}m-z_{1}z_{0}}{2z_{0}}-i\beta),

\displaystyle m:=\dfrac{\alpha_{2}}{\gamma},

(64)

and in the entries of transfer matrices,
\mathbf{U}
and
\mathbf{L}
stands for

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}^{(j)}:=U(m_{1}+j,m_{2}+j,z=0),

(65)

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}^{(j)}:=L(m_{3}-j,m_{2}+j,z=0).

(66)

For
\alpha=-\rho
and
\beta=-1
, the exact solution is reduced to the one given in
Hassan et al. 2017
.

Abstract
Let 𝕂 \mathbb{K} be a discrete field and ( V , ϕ ) (V,\phi) a flow over the category of locally linearly compact 𝕂 \mathbb{K} -spaces. Here we give the formulas to compute the topological entropy of ( V , ϕ ) (V,\phi) subject to the extension or the restriction of scalars.

Preliminaries and basic properties

Throughout, the topology on arbitrary fields will always be the discrete topology. We summarise here some of the properties of linearly compact vector spaces and the topological entropy
\mathrm{ent}^{*}

[
5
]

2.1
Linearly compact vector spaces

A topological
\mathbb{K}
-space is said to be
linearly topologized
if it admits a local basis at 0 of
\mathbb{K}
-linear subspaces.
For linearly topologized
\mathbb{K}
-spaces

and

(P1)

If

V

(P2)

If

is linearly compact and

W
is linearly compact.

(P3)

Linear compactness is preserved by continuous homomorphisms.

(P4)

If
V

V
is linearly compact if and only if
V
has finite dimension.

(P5)

If
W
is closed, then
V
is linearly compact if and only if
W
and
V/W
are linearly compact.

(P6)

The direct product of linearly compact
\mathbb{K}
-spaces is linearly compact.

(P7)

An inverse limit of linearly compact
\mathbb{K}
-spaces is linearly compact.

(P8)

If
V
is linearly compact, then
V
is complete.

(P9)

Let
V
be linearly compact. Every continuous
\mathbb{K}
-linear
f\colon V\to V^{\prime}
is open onto its image.

(P10)

Linearly compact
\mathbb{K}
-spaces satisfy Lefschetz duality.

(P11)

Every linearly compact
\mathbb{K}
-space is topologically a direct product of copies of
\mathbb{K}

\mathbb{K}

.

3
Change of fields

Firstly, we face the case of linearly compact vector spaces and then we deduce from it the general case concerning locally linearly compact spaces.

3.1
Restriction of scalars over linearly compact vector spaces

Let
\mathbb{F}\leq\mathbb{K}
be a field extension of finite degree
[\mathbb{K}:\mathbb{F}]\in\mathbb{N}
. Since any
\mathbb{K}
-space can be regarded as
\mathbb{F}

\mathbb{F}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{K}

\mathrm{res}^{\mathbb{K}}_{\mathbb{F}}(-)\colon{}_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{Vect}\to{}_{\mathbb{F}}\mathrm{Vect},

(3.1)

which is usually called

. Now let
V
be a linearly compact
\mathbb{K}
-space, i.e.,
V
is topologically a direct product of (discrete) 1-dimensional
\mathbb{K}
-spaces (endowed with the Tychonoff topology). Since restriction commutes with products, the underlying abstract
\mathbb{F}

\mathrm{res}^{\mathbb{K}}_{\mathbb{F}}(V)=\prod\mathrm{res}^{\mathbb{K}}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{K})\cong\prod\mathbb{F}^{[\mathbb{K}:\mathbb{F}]}.

(3.2)

From the topological point of view, the abstract
\mathbb{F}
-space
\mathrm{res}^{\mathbb{K}}_{\mathbb{F}}(V)
comes equipped with the product topology of discrete finite-dimensional
\mathbb{F}
-spaces. Therefore
V
is a linearly compact
\mathbb{F}
-space. Thus one also obtains a
restriction functor

\mathrm{Res}^{\mathbb{K}}_{\mathbb{F}}(-)\colon{}_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LC}\to{}_{\mathbb{F}}\mathrm{LC}.

(3.3)

for linearly compact vector spaces and field extensions.

Notice that
[\mathbb{K}:\mathbb{F}]<\infty
is a necessary condition, since a discrete linearly compact vector space has to be finite-dimensional.

4
Topological entropy after induction and restriction

4.1
Linearly compact case

Let
\mathbb{F}\leq\mathbb{K}\leq\mathbb{L}
be field extensions such that
[\mathbb{L}:\mathbb{F}]<\infty
. Let
V
be a linearly compact vector space and let

denote the left Bernoulli shift of
V
. When

then
{}_{\mathbb{K}}\beta
is the one-dimensional left Bernoulli shift that has topological entropy

. Clearly, its image under restriction is nothing but the (

\mathrm{Res}^{\mathbb{K}}_{\mathbb{F}}(V)=\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{F}^{[\mathbb{K}:\mathbb{F}]}

\mathrm{ent}_{\mathbb{F}}^{*}(\mathrm{Res}^{\mathbb{K}}_{\mathbb{F}}(V,{}_{\mathbb{K}}\beta))=\mathrm{ent}^{*}_{\mathbb{F}}(\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{F}^{[\mathbb{K}:\mathbb{F}]},{}_{\mathbb{F}^{[\mathbb{K}:\mathbb{F}]}}\beta)=[\mathbb{K}:\mathbb{F}]=[\mathbb{K}:\mathbb{F}]\cdot\mathrm{ent}_{\mathbb{K}}^{*}(V,{}_{\mathbb{K}}\beta),

by
[
5
, Example 3.13(b)]
.
On the other hand, the induction gives rise to the (1-dimensional) left Bernoulli shift on
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathbb{K}}^{\mathbb{L}}(V)=\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{L}
, i.e.,

\mathrm{ent}_{\mathbb{L}}^{*}(\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathbb{K}}^{\mathbb{L}}(V,{}_{\mathbb{K}}\beta))=\mathrm{ent}^{*}_{\mathbb{L}}(\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{L},{}_{\mathbb{L}}\beta)=1=\mathrm{ent}_{\mathbb{K}}^{*}(V,{}_{\mathbb{K}}\beta).

The following result shows that the formulas above hold for every flow
(V,\phi)
over
{}_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LC}

form a neighbourhood basis at 0 for the Tychonoff topology on
\prod\mathbb{E}
. We will refer to such a basis as
good basis
of
\prod\mathbb{E}
and its elements will be said to be
good
.

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

For an arbitrary flow
(V,\phi)
over
{}_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LC}
, let
U=\prod A_{i}
be a good
\mathbb{K}
-subspace of
V
. Thus

\displaystyle H_{\mathbb{F}}^{*}(\mathrm{Res}^{\mathbb{K}}_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi,U))

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\cdot\mathrm{codim}_{\mathbb{F}}\,C^{\mathbb{F}}_{n}(\mathrm{Res}^{\mathbb{K}}_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi,U))=

(4.1)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{[\mathbb{K}:\mathbb{F}]}{n}\cdot\mathrm{codim}_{\mathbb{K}}\,C^{\mathbb{K}}_{n}(\phi,U)=[\mathbb{K}:\mathbb{F}]\cdot H_{\mathbb{K}}^{*}(\phi,U).

(4.2)

and notice that
\mathrm{Res}^{\mathbb{K}}_{\mathbb{F}}(U)
is a good
\mathbb{F}
-subspace of
V
. Since good subspaces form a neighbourhood basis at 0, one has that

[\mathbb{K}:\mathbb{F}]\cdot\mathrm{ent}_{\mathbb{K}}^{*}(V,\phi)\leq\mathrm{ent}_{\mathbb{F}}^{*}(\mathrm{Res}^{\mathbb{K}}_{\mathbb{F}}(V,\phi)).

(4.3)

Finally, the equality holds since every good
\mathbb{F}
-subspace of
\mathrm{Res}^{\mathbb{K}}_{\mathbb{F}}(V)
can be realised as the restriction of a good
\mathbb{K}
-subspace of
V
. In order to prove the second part of the statement, notice that

\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(-)=\dim_{\mathbb{L}}(\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathbb{K}}^{\mathbb{L}}(-))\quad\text{and}\quad C^{\mathbb{L}}_{n}(\mathrm{Ind}^{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathbb{K}}(\phi,U))=\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathbb{K}}^{\mathbb{L}}(C^{\mathbb{K}}_{n}(\phi,U)),\ \text{($U\in\mathcal{B}(V),n\in\mathbb{N}$)}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle H^{*}(\mathrm{Ind}^{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathbb{K}}(\phi,U))

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\cdot\mathrm{codim}_{\mathbb{L}}\,C^{\mathbb{L}}_{n}(\mathrm{Ind}^{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathbb{K}}(\phi,U))=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\cdot\mathrm{codim}_{\mathbb{L}}\,\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathbb{K}}^{\mathbb{L}}(C_{n}(\phi,U))=

(4.4)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\cdot\mathrm{codim}_{\mathbb{K}}\,C_{n}(\phi,U)=H^{*}(\phi,U).

(4.5)

Same reasoning as above yields
\mathrm{ent}^{*}(\mathrm{ind}^{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathbb{K}}(V,\phi))=\mathrm{ent}^{*}(V,\phi).

∎

Remark 4.2
.

By latter result, it follows that

\mathrm{ent}_{\mathbb{K}}^{*}(\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{K}}(\mathrm{Res}_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathbb{K}}(V,\phi)))=[\mathbb{K}:\mathbb{F}]\cdot\mathrm{ent}_{\mathbb{K}}^{*}(V,\phi)=\mathrm{ent}_{\mathbb{K}}^{*}(\mathrm{Res}_{\mathbb{K}}^{\mathbb{L}}(\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathbb{K}}^{\mathbb{L}}(V,\phi))).

This gives back an intuitive method to generate examples of topological flows with some finite topological entropy
n\in\mathbb{N}
. E.g., let
(V,{}_{\mathbb{K}}\beta)
be the 1-dimensional left Bernoulli shift defined above. One easily generates the
n
-dimensional left Bernoulli shift over
\mathbb{K}
by combining induction and restriction with respect to a field extension of
\mathbb{K}
of degree
n
.

4.2
General case

Recall that every locally linearly compact
\mathbb{K}
-space
V
can be split into a topological direct sum of a linearly compact open
\mathbb{K}
-space
V_{c}
and a discrete
\mathbb{K}
-space
V_{d}
; namely,
V\cong_{top}V_{c}\oplus V_{d}
. Such a decomposition is available whenever we consider a linearly compact open subspace
U
of
V
. Indeed,
V\cong_{top}U\oplus V/U
.

Now let
(V,\phi)
be a flow over
{}_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LLC}
. Any decomposition
V\cong_{top}V_{c}\oplus V_{d}
induces a decomposition

\phi=\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{cc}&\phi_{dc}\\
\phi_{cd}&\phi_{dd}\end{pmatrix},

(4.6)

where
\phi_{\bullet*}:V_{\bullet}\to V_{*}
is the composition
\phi_{\bullet*}=p_{*}\circ\phi\circ\iota_{\bullet}
for
\bullet,*\in\{c,d\}
.
Therefore,
\phi_{\bullet*}
is continuous being composition of continuous homomorphisms.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mathbb{F}\leq\mathbb{K}\leq\mathbb{L}
be finite field extensions and
V\cong_{top}V_{c}\oplus V_{d}
a locally linearly compact
\mathbb{K}
-space together with a continuous endomorphism
\phi\colon V\to V
. Thus

1.

\mathrm{Res}^{\mathbb{K}}_{\mathbb{F}}(V)
admits a decomposition such that

\mathrm{Res}^{\mathbb{K}}_{\mathbb{F}}(V)_{c}=\mathrm{Res}^{\mathbb{K}}_{\mathbb{F}}(V_{c})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{Res}^{\mathbb{K}}_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi)_{cc}=\mathrm{Res}^{\mathbb{K}}_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi_{cc}).

2.

\mathrm{Ind}^{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathbb{K}}(V)
admits a decomposition such that

\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathbb{K}}^{\mathbb{L}}(V)_{c}=\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathbb{K}}^{\mathbb{L}}(V_{c})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{Ind}^{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathbb{K}}(\phi)_{cc}=\mathrm{Ind}^{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathbb{F}}(\phi_{cc}).

Proof.

Notice that
\mathrm{Res}^{\mathbb{K}}_{\mathbb{F}}(V)
(or
\mathrm{Ind}^{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathbb{K}}(V)
) can be obtained by restricting (or extending)
V_{c}
and
V_{d}
first in the corresponding categories and then summing them up in
{}_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LLC}
. Indeed both restriction functor and induction functor are additive.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\mathbb{F}\leq\mathbb{K}\leq\mathbb{L}
such that
[\mathbb{L}:\mathbb{F}]<\infty
. For every flow
(V,\phi)
over
{}_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{LLC}
one has

\mathrm{ent}_{\mathbb{F}}^{*}(\mathrm{Res}^{\mathbb{K}}_{\mathbb{F}}(V,\phi))=[\mathbb{K}:\mathbb{F}]\cdot\mathrm{ent}_{\mathbb{K}}^{*}(V,\phi)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{ent}_{\mathbb{L}}^{*}(\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathbb{K}}^{\mathbb{L}}(V,\phi))=\mathrm{ent}_{\mathbb{K}}^{*}(V,\phi).

Proof.

By the previous lemma, it follows directly from Proposition
4.1
and the reduction to linearly compact vector spaces devolopped in
[
5
]
.
∎

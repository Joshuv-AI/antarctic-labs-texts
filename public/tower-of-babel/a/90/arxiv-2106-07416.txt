Weak solutions for time-fractional evolution equations in Hilbert spaces
Paola Loreti, Daniela Sforza

Abstract
We introduce a notion of weak solution for abstract fractional differential equations, motivated by the definition of Caputo derivative. We prove existence results for weak and strong solutions. We also give two examples as application of our results: time-fractional wave equations and time-fractional Petrovsky systems.



Keywords:
Abstract evolution equations, fractional analysis, strong and weak solutions.

Mathematics Subject Classification
: 35R11, 34A08.

1
Introduction

Our aim is to establish existence and regularity results regarding weak solutions of
the abstract fractional equation

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u+Au=0\qquad\mbox{in}\ (0,T),

(1)

where
u
takes its values in a Hilbert space
H
,
A
is a densely defined, linear self-adjoint positive operator on
H
and the Caputo derivative
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u
of order
\alpha\in(1,2)
is defined as

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(2-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{1-\alpha}\frac{d^{2}u}{d\tau^{2}}(\tau)\ d\tau\,.

Our study is inspired from the literature concernig partial differential equations of the type
1
in the case the operator
A
is a concrete differential operator. Typically, the operator
A
is the Laplacian or, more general, a uniformly elliptic operator. To our knowledge, the biharmonic operator has not been studied yet in the framework of the time-fractional differential equations.

We mention
[
3
]
where the author gives a first analysis about evolution equations, related to fractional differential ones, that interpolates the heat equation and the wave equation in the domain
(0,+\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}
.
In the survey
[
8
]
the interested reader can find a historical note on the origin of the fractional derivative in the Caputo sense. Morever, the authors provide some arguments showing the usefulness of the Caputo derivatives in the theory of viscoelasticity.

To begin with, we introduce strong solutions of (
1
) as functions
u
belonging to
C([0,T];D(A))\cap C^{1}([0,T];H)
,
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u\in C([0,T];H)
and
u
satisfies
equation (
1
) for any
t\in[0,T]
.

In this paper we are interested to solutions with lower regularity, so the main difficulty
is an appropriate definition for weak solutions. The definition of weak solutions that we propose is suggested by the formula

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u(t)=\frac{d}{dt}I^{2-\alpha}\big(u^{\prime}-u^{\prime}(0)\big)(t)\,,

(2)

where
I^{2-\alpha}
is the Riemann–Liouville operator of order
2-\alpha
, see formula (
12
) in Subsection
2.2
.

We define a weak solution of the fractional differential equation (
1
) as a
function
u
belonging to
C([0,T];D(\sqrt{A}))
,
u^{\prime}\in L^{2}(0,T;H)\cap C([0,T];D(A^{-\theta}))
, for some
\theta\in(0,1)
,
and for any
v\in D(\sqrt{A})
one has
\langle I^{2-\alpha}\big(u^{\prime}-u^{\prime}(0)\big)(t),v\rangle\in C^{1}([0,T])
and

\frac{d}{dt}\langle I^{2-\alpha}\big(u^{\prime}-u^{\prime}(0)\big)(t),v\rangle+\langle\sqrt{A}u(t),\sqrt{A}v\rangle=0\qquad t\in[0,T]\,.

(3)

Our main result is the following (see Section
3
)

Theorem 1.1

If
u_{0}\in D(\sqrt{A})
and
u_{1}\in H
, then the function

u(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\big[\langle u_{0},e_{n}\rangle E_{\alpha,1}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})+\langle u_{1},e_{n}\rangle tE_{\alpha,2}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})\big]e_{n}

(4)

is the unique weak solution of (
1
) satisfying
the initial conditions

u(0)=u_{0},\quad u^{\prime}(0)=u_{1}.

Formula (
4
) is suggested by the spectral approach given in
[
12
,
13
]
, where the authors deal with uniformly elliptic operators in a different setting. It is worthwile to mention the paper
[
6
]
(see also references therein), where the authors give the representation of classical solutions by means of the
\alpha
-resolvent family.

Our abstract results can be applied to various partial differential equations to obtain the existence of weak solutions.
The last section is devoted to the discussion of two examples: time-fractional wave equations and time-fractional Petrovsky systems.

2
Preliminaries

In this section we collect some notations, definitions and known results that we use to prove our main results.

2.1
Abstract operators

Let
H
be a real Hilbert space with inner product

\langle\cdot\,,\,\cdot\rangle
and norm
\|\cdot\|
.

A
is a linear self-adjoint positive
operator on
H
with dense domain
D(A)
, satisfying

\langle Ax,x\rangle\geq a\|x\|^{2}\qquad\forall x\in D(A)

(5)

for some
a>0
. We assume that the spectrum of
A
consists of a sequence of positive eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
such that
\lambda_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits_{n}}\infty
. Moreover,
\lambda_{n}
are all distinct numbers, whence the eigenspace generated by every single
\lambda_{n}
has dimension one.

Moreover, the eigenfunctions
e_{n}
of
A

(
Ae_{n}=\lambda_{n}e_{n}
)
constitute an orthonormal basis of
H
.

The fractional powers
A^{\theta}
are defined for
\theta>0
, see e.g.
[
10
,
7
]
.
The domain
D(A^{\theta})
of
A^{\theta}
consists of
u\in H
such that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{2\theta}|\langle u,e_{n}\rangle|^{2}<+\infty

and

A^{\theta}u=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{\theta}\langle u,e_{n}\rangle e_{n},\quad u\in D(A^{\theta}).

Moreover
D(A^{\theta})
is a Hilbert space with the norm given by

\|u\|_{D(A^{\theta})}=\|A^{\theta}u\|=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{2\theta}|\langle u,e_{n}\rangle|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\quad u\in D(A^{\theta}),

(6)

and for any
0<\theta_{1}<\theta_{2}
we have
D(A^{\theta_{2}})\subset D(A^{\theta_{1}})
.

In particular, the norm of the space
D(\sqrt{A})
is given by

\|u\|_{D(\sqrt{A})}=\|\sqrt{A}u\|=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}|\langle u,e_{n}\rangle|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\qquad u\in D(\sqrt{A}).

(7)

If we identify the dual
H^{\prime}
with
H
itself, then we have
D(A^{\theta})\subset H\subset(D(A^{\theta}))^{\prime}
.
From now on we set

D(A^{-\theta}):=(D(A^{\theta}))^{\prime},

(8)

whose elements are bounded linear functionals on
D(A^{\theta})
. If
u\in D(A^{-\theta})
and
\varphi\in D(A^{\theta})
the value
u(\varphi)
is denoted
by

\langle u,\varphi\rangle_{-\theta,\theta}:=u(\varphi)\,.

(9)

In addition,

D(A^{-\theta})
is a Hilbert space with the norm given by

\|u\|_{D(A^{-\theta})}=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{-2\theta}|\langle u,e_{n}\rangle_{-\theta,\theta}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\quad u\in D(A^{-\theta})\,,

(10)

and for any
0<\theta_{1}<\theta_{2}
we have
D(A^{-\theta_{1}})\subset D(A^{-\theta_{2}})
.
We also recall that

\langle u,\varphi\rangle_{-\theta,\theta}=\langle u,\varphi\rangle\qquad\mbox{for}\ u\in H\,,\varphi\in D(A^{\theta}),

(11)

see e.g.
[
2
, Chapitre V]
.

2.2
Fractional derivatives

Definition 2.1

For any
\beta>0

we denote the Riemann–Liouville fractional integral operator of order
\beta
by

I^{\beta}(f)(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{\beta-1}f(\tau)\ d\tau,\qquad f\in L^{1}(0,T),\ \mbox{a.e.}\ t\in(0,T),

(12)

where
T>0
and
\Gamma(\beta)=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\beta-1}e^{-t}\ dt
is the Euler Gamma function.

The Caputo fractional derivative of order
\alpha\in(1,2)
is given by

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}f(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(2-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{1-\alpha}\frac{d^{2}f}{d\tau^{2}}(\tau)\ d\tau\,.

(13)

By means of the Riemann–Liouville integral operator
I^{2-\alpha}
we can write

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}f(t)=I^{2-\alpha}\Big(\frac{d^{2}f}{dt^{2}}\Big)(t)\,.

(14)

We also note that if
f^{\prime}
is absolutely continuous, then

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}f(t)=\frac{d}{dt}I^{2-\alpha}\big(f^{\prime}-f^{\prime}(0)\big)(t)\,.

(15)

For arbitrary constants
\alpha,\beta>0
, we denote the Mittag–Leffler functions by

E_{\alpha,\beta}(z):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}}{\Gamma(\alpha k+\beta)},\quad z\in{\mathbb{C}}.

(16)

By the power series, one can note that
E_{\alpha,\beta}(z)
is an entire function
of
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
. We note that
E_{\alpha,1}(0)=1
.

The proof of the following result can be found in
[
11
, p. 35]
, see also
[
12
, Lemma 3.1]
.
In the following we denote the Laplace transform of a function
f(t)
by the symbol

{\cal L}[f(t)](z):=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-zt}f(t)\ dt\qquad z\in{\mathbb{C}}.

(17)

Lemma 2.2

1.

Let
\alpha\in(1,2)
and
\beta>0
be. Then for any
\mu\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\pi\alpha/2<\mu<\pi
there exists a constant
C=C(\alpha,\beta,\mu)>0
such that

\big|E_{\alpha,\beta}(z)\big|\leq\frac{C}{1+|z|},\qquad z\in{\mathbb{C}},\ \mu\leq|\arg(z)|\leq\pi.

(18)

2.

For
\alpha\,,\beta\,,\lambda>0
one has

{\cal L}\big[t^{\beta-1}E_{\alpha,\beta}(-\lambda t^{\alpha})\big)\big](z)=\frac{z^{\alpha-\beta}}{z^{\alpha}+\lambda}\qquad\Re z>\lambda^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\,.

(19)

3.

If
\alpha\,,\lambda>0
, then we have

\frac{d}{dt}E_{\alpha,1}(-\lambda t^{\alpha})=-\lambda t^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-\lambda t^{\alpha}),\qquad t>0,

(20)

\frac{d}{dt}\Big(t^{k}E_{\alpha,k+1}(-\lambda t^{\alpha})\Big)=t^{k-1}E_{\alpha,k}(-\lambda t^{\alpha}),\qquad k\in{\mathbb{N}}\,,t\geq 0,

(21)

\frac{d}{dt}\Big(t^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-\lambda t^{\alpha})\Big)=t^{\alpha-2}E_{\alpha,\alpha-1}(-\lambda t^{\alpha}),\qquad t\geq 0.

(22)

We also exhibit an elementary result that will be useful in the estimates.

Lemma 2.3

For any
0<\beta<1
the function
x\to\frac{x^{\beta}}{1+x}
gains its maximum on
[0,+\infty[
at point
\frac{\beta}{1-\beta}
and the maximum value is given by

\max_{x\geq 0}\frac{x^{\beta}}{1+x}=\beta^{\beta}(1-\beta)^{1-\beta},\qquad\beta\in(0,1)\,.

(23)

Now we recall the definition of fractional vector-valued Sobolev spaces.
For
\beta\in(0,1)
,
T>0
and a Hilbert space
H
, endowed with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{H}
,
H^{\beta}(0,T;H)
is the space of all
u\in L^{2}(0,T;H)
such that

[u]_{H^{\beta}(0,T;H)}:=\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\|u(t)-u(\tau)\|_{H}^{2}}{|t-\tau|^{1+2\beta}}\ dtd\tau\right)^{1/2}<+\infty\,,

(24)

that is
[u]_{H^{\beta}(0,T;H)}
is the so-called Gagliardo semi-norm of
u
.

H^{\beta}(0,T;H)
is endowed with the norm

\|\cdot\|_{H^{\beta}(0,T;H)}:=\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}+[\ \cdot\ ]_{H^{\beta}(0,T;H)}.

(25)

The following result is a generalization to the case of vector valued functions of

[
4
, Theorem 2.1]
.
We will use the symbol
\sim
to indicate equivalent norms.

Theorem 2.4

Let
H
be a separable Hilbert space.

(i)

The Riemann–Liouville operator
I^{\beta}:L^{2}(0,T;H)\to L^{2}(0,T;H)
,
0<\beta\leq 1
, is injective and the range
{\cal R}(I^{\beta})
of
I^{\beta}
is given by

{\cal R}(I^{\beta})=\begin{cases}H^{\beta}(0,T;H),\hskip 156.49014pt0<\beta<\frac{1}{2},\\
\Big\{v\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(0,T;H):\int_{0}^{T}t^{-1}|v(t)|^{2}dt<\infty\Big\},\qquad\beta=\frac{1}{2},\\
{}_{0}H^{\beta}(0,T;H),\hskip 147.95424pt\frac{1}{2}<\beta\leq 1,\end{cases}

(26)

where
{}_{0}H^{\beta}(0,T)=\{u\in H^{\beta}(0,T):u(0)=0\}
.

(ii)

For the Riemann–Liouville operator
I^{\beta}
and its inverse operator
I^{-\beta}
the norm equivalences

\begin{split}\|I^{\beta}(u)\|_{H^{\beta}(0,T;H)}&\sim\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)},\qquad u\in L^{2}(0,T;H),\\
\|I^{-\beta}(v)\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}&\sim\|v\|_{H^{\beta}(0,T;H)},\qquad v\in{\cal R}(I^{\beta}),\end{split}

(27)

hold true.

3
Existence and regularity of solutions

First, we introduce the notions of weak and strong solutions, see . Let
\alpha\in(1,2)
and
T>0
.

Definition 3.1

1.

A function
u
is called a weak solution of the abstract time-fractional equation

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u+Au=0

(28)

if
u\in C([0,T];D(\sqrt{A}))
,
u^{\prime}\in L^{2}(0,T;H)\cap C([0,T];D(A^{-\theta}))
, for some
\theta\in(0,1)
, and for any
v\in D(\sqrt{A})
one has
\langle I^{2-\alpha}\big(u^{\prime}-u^{\prime}(0)\big)(t),v\rangle\in C^{1}([0,T])
and

\frac{d}{dt}\langle I^{2-\alpha}\big(u^{\prime}-u^{\prime}(0)\big)(t),v\rangle+\langle\sqrt{A}u(t),\sqrt{A}v\rangle=0\qquad t\in[0,T]\,.

(29)

2.

A function
u
is called a strong solution of (
28
) if
u\in C([0,T];D(A))\cap C^{1}([0,T];H)
,
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u\in C([0,T];H)
and satisfies
equation (
28
) for any
t\in[0,T]
.

Remark 3.2

We observe that a strong solution is also a weak solution.

Also, we note that for a weak solution
u
of (
28
) we have
\partial_{t}^{\beta}u\in H^{1-\beta}(0,T;H)
,
\beta\in(0,1)
, where
the Caputo fractional derivative of order
\beta\in(0,1)
is defined as

\partial_{t}^{\beta}u(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\beta)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{-\beta}u^{\prime}(\tau)\ d\tau=I^{1-\beta}\big(u^{\prime}\big)(t)\,.

(30)

Indeed, since
u^{\prime}\in L^{2}(0,T;H)
we can apply Theorem
2.4
to obtain
I^{1-\beta}\big(u^{\prime}\big)\in H^{1-\beta}(0,T;H)
, that is

\partial_{t}^{\beta}u\in H^{1-\beta}(0,T;H)
.

In particular, for
\beta=\alpha/2
and
\beta=1-\alpha/2
we have
\partial_{t}^{\alpha/2}u\in H^{1-\alpha/2}(0,T;H)
and

\partial_{t}^{1-\alpha/2}u\in H^{\alpha/2}(0,T;H)
respectively.

A classical approach to solve scalar fractional differential equations is using Laplace transform methods.

Lemma 3.3

For any
\lambda>0
and
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}
the solution of problem

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u(t)+\lambda u(t)=0\,,\quad t\geq 0,\\
u(0)=x,\quad u^{\prime}(0)=y,\end{cases}

(31)

is given by

u(t)=x\ E_{\alpha,1}(-\lambda t^{\alpha})+y\ tE_{\alpha,2}(-\lambda t^{\alpha}),\qquad t\geq 0\,.

(32)

Proof.

First, we compute the Laplace transform of
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u(t)
, that is

{\cal L}[\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u(t)](z)=z^{\alpha-2}{\cal L}\big[u^{\prime\prime}(t)\big](z)=z^{\alpha-2}\big(z^{2}{\cal L}[u(t)](z)-x\ z-y\big)\\
=z^{\alpha}{\cal L}[u(t)](z)-x\ z^{\alpha-1}-y\ z^{\alpha-2}\,.

Therefore, taking the Laplace transform of the equation in (
31
) we obtain

{\cal L}[\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u(t)](z)+\lambda{\cal L}[u(t)](z)=(z^{\alpha}+\lambda){\cal L}[u(t)](z)-x\ z^{\alpha-1}-y\ z^{\alpha-2}=0

and hence

{\cal L}[u(t)](z)=x\ \frac{z^{\alpha-1}}{z^{\alpha}+\lambda}+y\ \frac{z^{\alpha-2}}{z^{\alpha}+\lambda}\,.

From (
19
) we have

{\cal L}\big[E_{\alpha,1}(-\lambda t^{\alpha})\big](z)=\frac{z^{\alpha-1}}{z^{\alpha}+\lambda},\qquad{\cal L}\big[tE_{\alpha,2}(-\lambda t^{\alpha})\big](z)=\frac{z^{\alpha-2}}{z^{\alpha}+\lambda},\quad\Re z>\lambda^{1/\alpha},

whence we deduce

{\cal L}[u(t)](z)=x\ {\cal L}\big[E_{\alpha,1}(-\lambda t^{\alpha})\big](z)+y\ {\cal L}\big[tE_{\alpha,2}(-\lambda t^{\alpha})\big](z)\\
={\cal L}\big[x\ E_{\alpha,1}(-\lambda t^{\alpha})+y\ tE_{\alpha,2}(-\lambda t^{\alpha})\big](z)\,.

In conclusion, by Lerch’s Theorem that assures the uniqueness of inverse Laplace transforms
u
is given by (
32
).

\square

Theorem 3.4

(i)

If
u_{0}\in D(\sqrt{A})
and
u_{1}\in H
, then the function

u(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\big[\langle u_{0},e_{n}\rangle E_{\alpha,1}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})+\langle u_{1},e_{n}\rangle tE_{\alpha,2}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})\big]e_{n}

(33)

is the unique weak solution of (
28
) satisfying
the initial conditions

u(0)=u_{0},\quad u_{t}(0)=u_{1}.

(34)

In addition

u^{\prime}(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\big[-\lambda_{n}\langle u_{0},e_{n}\rangle t^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})+\langle u_{1},e_{n}\rangle E_{\alpha,1}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})\big]e_{n}\,,

(35)

and
u^{\prime}\in C([0,T];D(A^{-\theta}))
for
\theta\in\big(\frac{2-\alpha}{2\alpha},\frac{1}{2}\big)

(ii)

For
u_{0}\in D({A})
and
u_{1}\in D(\sqrt{A})

the weak solution given by (
33

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u(t)=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\big[\lambda_{n}\langle u_{0},e_{n}\rangle E_{\alpha,1}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})+\lambda_{n}\langle u_{1},e_{n}\rangle tE_{\alpha,2}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})\big]e_{n}\,.

(36)

Proof.

To begin with, we intend to justify the expression of the solution given by the series (
33
).
To this end we search the solution in the form

u(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\ u_{n}(t)e_{n}

where the functions
u_{n}(t)=\langle u(t),e_{n}\rangle
are unknown. First, we assume that there exists a strong solution
u
, see Definition
3.1
-(2). By means of the scalar product in
H
we multiply the equation
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u+\Delta^{2}u=0
by
e_{n}
and take into account that the operator
A
is self-adjoint, so we have

0=\langle\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u,e_{n}\rangle+\langle Au,e_{n}\rangle=\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u_{n}+\langle u,Ae_{n}\rangle=\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u_{n}+\lambda_{n}\langle u,e_{n}\rangle=\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u_{n}+\lambda_{n}u_{n}\,.

Therefore, in virtue of the initial conditions
(
34
)
u_{n}(t)
is the solution of problem

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u_{n}(t)+\lambda_{n}u_{n}(t)=0\,,\quad t\geq 0,\\
u_{n}(0)=\langle u_{0},e_{n}\rangle,\quad u_{n}^{\prime}(0)=\langle u_{1},e_{n}\rangle,\end{cases}

(37)

u_{n}(t)=\langle u_{0},e_{n}\rangle\ E_{\alpha,1}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})+\langle u_{1},e_{n}\rangle\ tE_{\alpha,2}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha}),\qquad t\geq 0\,.

(38)

(i) Now, we take
u_{0}\in D(\sqrt{A})
,
u_{1}\in H
and show that

u(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}u_{n}(t)e_{n},

with
u_{n}(t)
given by (
38
), is a weak solution of (
28
) satisfying the initial conditions (
34
). First, we note that for any
t\in[0,T]
we have
u(t)\in D(\sqrt{A})
. Indeed, since

\|\sqrt{A}u(t)\|^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}|u_{n}(t)|^{2}\leq 2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}\big|\langle u_{0},e_{n}\rangle E_{\alpha,1}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})\big|^{2}+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}\big|\langle u_{1},e_{n}\rangle tE_{\alpha,2}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})\big|^{2},

thanks to (

\begin{split}\lambda_{n}\big|\langle u_{0},e_{n}\rangle E_{\alpha,1}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})\big|^{2}&\leq C\lambda_{n}\big|\langle u_{0},e_{n}\rangle\big|^{2},\\
\lambda_{n}\big|\langle u_{1},e_{n}\rangle tE_{\alpha,2}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})\big|^{2}&\leq Ct^{2-\alpha}\big|\langle u_{1},e_{n}\rangle\big|^{2}\frac{\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha}}{(1+\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})^{2}}\leq Ct^{2-\alpha}\big|\langle u_{1},e_{n}\rangle\big|^{2},\end{split}

\|\sqrt{A}u(t)\|^{2}\leq C\|\sqrt{A}u_{0}\|^{2}+CT^{2-\alpha}\|u_{1}\|^{2}.

(39)

Following the same reasoning pursued to get (

\leq Ct^{2\alpha\theta+\alpha-2}\lambda_{n}|\langle u_{0},e_{n}\rangle\big|^{2}\bigg(\frac{(\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})^{\frac{1}{2}-\theta}}{1+\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha}}\bigg)^{2}\leq Ct^{2\alpha\theta+\alpha-2}\lambda_{n}|\langle u_{0},e_{n}\rangle\big|^{2}.

Therefore, taking into account that
\theta>\frac{2-\alpha}{2\alpha}
, for any
t\in[0,T]
we get

\Big\|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}u_{n}^{\prime}(t)e_{n}\Big\|_{D(A^{-\theta})}^{2}\leq CT^{2\alpha\theta+\alpha-2}\|\sqrt{A}u_{0}\|^{2}+C\|u_{1}\|^{2}\,.

Arguing as above, we can show that
the series
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}u_{n}^{\prime}(t)e_{n}

is convergent in
D(A^{-\theta})
uniformly in
t\in[0,T]
.
For that reason, the function
u
is differentiable and

u^{\prime}(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}u_{n}^{\prime}(t)e_{n},

that is, formula (
35
) holds for
u^{\prime}
. Moreover,
u^{\prime}\in C([0,T];D(A^{-\theta}))
and

u^{\prime}(0)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle u_{1},e_{n}\rangle e_{n}=u_{1}.

In addition,

\|u^{\prime}(t)\|^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\big|u_{n}^{\prime}(t)\big|^{2}\\
\leq 2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\big|-\lambda_{n}\langle u_{0},e_{n}\rangle t^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})\big|^{2}+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\big|\langle u_{1},e_{n}\rangle E_{\alpha,1}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})\big|^{2}\,.

(41)

Using (
18
) and (
23
) we obtain

\big|-\lambda_{n}\langle u_{0},e_{n}\rangle t^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})\big|^{2}\leq Ct^{\alpha-2}\lambda_{n}|\langle u_{0},e_{n}\rangle\big|^{2}\bigg(\frac{(\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})^{\frac{1}{2}}}{1+\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha}}\bigg)^{2}\leq Ct^{\alpha-2}\lambda_{n}|\langle u_{0},e_{n}\rangle\big|^{2}.

Putting the above estimate into (
41
) we get

\int_{0}^{T}\|u^{\prime}(t)\|^{2}\ dt\leq C\frac{T^{\alpha-1}}{\alpha-1}\|\sqrt{A}u_{0}\|^{2}+C\|u_{1}\|^{2}\,,

so
u^{\prime}\in L^{2}(0,T;H)
.

To evaluate
I^{2-\alpha}\big(u^{\prime}-u_{1}\big)(t)
,
where

u^{\prime}(t)-u_{1}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\big[-\lambda_{n}\langle u_{0},e_{n}\rangle t^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})+\langle u_{1},e_{n}\rangle\big(E_{\alpha,1}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})-1\big)\big]e_{n}\,,

we observe that

{\cal L}\big[I^{2-\alpha}\big(t^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})\big)\big](z)=\frac{z^{\alpha-2}}{z^{\alpha}+\lambda_{n}}={\cal L}\big[tE_{\alpha,2}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})\big)\big](z),

{\cal L}\big[I^{2-\alpha}\big(E_{\alpha,1}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})-1\big)\big](z)=z^{\alpha-2}\Big(\frac{z^{\alpha-1}}{z^{\alpha}+\lambda_{n}}-\frac{1}{z}\Big)\\
=-\lambda_{n}\frac{z^{\alpha-3}}{z^{\alpha}+\lambda_{n}}=-\lambda_{n}{\cal L}\big[t^{2}E_{\alpha,3}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})\big)\big](z).

Therefore, by Lerch’s Theorem that assures the uniqueness of inverse Laplace transforms and repeating the previous argumentations one proves

I^{2-\alpha}\big(u^{\prime}-u_{1}\big)(t)=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}\big[\langle u_{0},e_{n}\rangle tE_{\alpha,2}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})+\langle u_{1},e_{n}\rangle t^{2}E_{\alpha,3}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})\big]e_{n}\,,

(42)

and
I^{2-\alpha}\big(u^{\prime}-u_{1}\big)(t)\in C([0,T];H)
.

Next, if
v=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\ \langle v,e_{n}\rangle e_{n}
belongs to
D(\sqrt{A})
, then we have

\langle I^{2-\alpha}\big(u^{\prime}-u_{1}\big)(t),v\rangle=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big[\langle u_{0},e_{n}\rangle tE_{\alpha,2}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})+\langle u_{1},e_{n}\rangle t^{2}E_{\alpha,3}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})\big]\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle v,e_{n}\rangle

We observe that by (
21
) for
k=1
and
k=2
we have

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{d}{dt}\Big[\langle u_{0},e_{n}\rangle tE_{\alpha,2}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})+\langle u_{1},e_{n}\rangle t^{2}E_{\alpha,3}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})\Big]\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle v,e_{n}\rangle\\
=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big[\langle u_{0},e_{n}\rangle E_{\alpha,1}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})+\langle u_{1},e_{n}\rangle tE_{\alpha,2}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})\big]\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle v,e_{n}\rangle\,.

Thanks to (
18
) and (
23
) we get

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}\big|\langle u_{0},e_{n}\rangle E_{\alpha,1}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})+\langle u_{1},e_{n}\rangle tE_{\alpha,2}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})\big|^{2}\\
\leq C\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}\big|\langle u_{0},e_{n}\rangle\big|^{2}\frac{1}{(1+\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})^{2}}+Ct^{2-\alpha}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\big|\langle u_{1},e_{n}\rangle\big|^{2}\Big(\frac{\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}{1+\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha}}\Big)^{2}\\
\leq C\big(\|\sqrt{A}u_{0}\|^{2}+t^{2-\alpha}\|u_{1}\|^{2}\big)\,.

Therefore, since
\alpha<2
for any
t\in[0,T]
we have

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Big|\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{d}{dt}\Big[\langle u_{0},e_{n}\rangle tE_{\alpha,2}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})+\langle u_{1},e_{n}\rangle t^{2}E_{\alpha,3}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})\Big]\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle v,e_{n}\rangle\Big|\\
\leq C\big(\|\sqrt{A}u_{0}\|^{2}+\|u_{1}\|^{2}+\|\sqrt{A}v\|^{2}\big)\,,

whence

\frac{d}{dt}\langle I^{2-\alpha}\big(u^{\prime}-u_{1}\big)(t),v\rangle\\
=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{d}{dt}\Big[\langle u_{0},e_{n}\rangle tE_{\alpha,2}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})+\langle u_{1},e_{n}\rangle t^{2}E_{\alpha,3}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})\Big]\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle v,e_{n}\rangle\\
=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big[\langle u_{0},e_{n}\rangle E_{\alpha,1}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})+\langle u_{1},e_{n}\rangle tE_{\alpha,2}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})\big]\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle v,e_{n}\rangle\,.

On the other hand

\langle\sqrt{A}u(t),\sqrt{A}v\rangle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big[\langle u_{0},e_{n}\rangle E_{\alpha,1}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})+\langle u_{1},e_{n}\rangle tE_{\alpha,2}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})\big]\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle v,e_{n}\rangle\,,

and hence for any

we have

\frac{d}{dt}\langle I^{2-\alpha}\big(u^{\prime}-u_{1}\big)(t),v\rangle+\langle\sqrt{A}u(t),\sqrt{A}v\rangle=0\,,

that is (
29
) holds.

In conclusion,
u
given by (
33
) is the weak solution of (
28
) satisfying
the initial conditions (
34
).

(ii) We assume that
u_{0}\in D({A})
and

Repeating similar argumentations to those done before, involving again (

) and (

From (

\frac{d}{dt}I^{2-\alpha}\big(u^{\prime}-u_{1}\big)(t)=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}\big[\langle u_{0},e_{n}\rangle E_{\alpha,1}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})+\langle u_{1},e_{n}\rangle tE_{\alpha,2}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})\big]e_{n}

(43)

and

\lambda_{n}^{2}\big|\langle u_{0},e_{n}\rangle E_{\alpha,1}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})+\langle u_{1},e_{n}\rangle tE_{\alpha,2}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})\big|^{2}\leq C\big(\lambda_{n}^{2}|\langle u_{0},e_{n}\rangle|^{2}+T^{2-\alpha}\lambda_{n}|\langle u_{1},e_{n}\rangle|^{2}\big)\,,

whence

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\big|\lambda_{n}\langle u_{0},e_{n}\rangle E_{\alpha,1}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})+\lambda_{n}\langle u_{1},e_{n}\rangle tE_{\alpha,2}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})\big|^{2}\leq C\big(\|Au_{0}\|^{2}+T^{2-\alpha}\|\sqrt{A}u_{1}\|^{2}\big)\,.

From (

u^{\prime\prime}_{n}(t)=-\lambda_{n}\big[\langle u_{0},e_{n}\rangle t^{\alpha-2}E_{\alpha,\alpha-1}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})+\langle u_{1},e_{n}\rangle t^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})\big].

(44)

Moreover,

\big\|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}u^{\prime\prime}_{n}(t)e_{n}\big\|^{2}\leq C(t^{2\alpha-4}\|Au_{0}\|^{2}+t^{\alpha-2}\|\sqrt{A}u_{1}\|^{2}),

\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}u^{\prime\prime}_{n}(s)e_{n}\ ds=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}u^{\prime\prime}_{n}(s)ds\ e_{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(u^{\prime}_{n}(t)-\langle u_{1},e_{n}\rangle)\ e_{n}=u^{\prime}(t)-u_{1}.

4
Examples

In this section we give an application of our well-posedness results by discussing two examples of concrete models involving well-known partial differential operators. Throughout the section, we denote by
\Omega
a bounded open domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
,
N\geq 1
, with sufficiently smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
.
In both examples we consider the Hilbert space
H=L^{2}(\Omega)
,
endowed with the inner product and norm defined by

\langle u,v\rangle=\int_{\Omega}u(x)v(x)\ dx,\qquad\|u\|=\left(\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{2}\ dx\right)^{1/2}\qquad u,v\in L^{2}(\Omega).

4.1
Time-fractional wave equations

We analyse the fractional boundary value problem

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u=\Delta u\qquad\mbox{in}\ (0,T)\times\Omega,\\
u=0\hskip 45.52458pt\mbox{on}\ (0,T)\times\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(50)

We rewrite (
50
) as an abstract equation of the type (
1
) by introducing the operator
A
as follows:

\begin{split}D(A)&=H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\\
(Au)(x)&=-\Delta u(x),\quad u\in D(A),\ x\in\Omega.\end{split}

It is well known that
A
is a linear self-adjoint positive
operator on
L^{2}(\Omega)
with dense domain.
Moreover, the fractional power
\sqrt{A}
of
A
is well defined and
D(\sqrt{A})=H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

We assume that the eigenvalues
\lambda_{n}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, of the operator
A
are all distinct numbers, whence the eigenspace generated by

\lambda_{n}
has dimension one.

We are in conditions to apply Theorem
3.4
to get a well-posedness result for problem (
50
).

Theorem 4.1

(i)

If
u_{0}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
u_{1}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, then the function

u(t,x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\big[\langle u_{0},e_{n}\rangle E_{\alpha,1}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})+\langle u_{1},e_{n}\rangle tE_{\alpha,2}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})\big]e_{n}(x)

(51)

is the unique weak solution of (
50
) satisfying
the initial conditions

u(0,\cdot)=u_{0},\quad u_{t}(0,\cdot)=u_{1}.

In addition

u_{t}(t,x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\big[-\lambda_{n}\langle u_{0},e_{n}\rangle t^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})+\langle u_{1},e_{n}\rangle E_{\alpha,1}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})\big]e_{n}(x)\,,

and
u_{t}\in C([0,T];D(A^{-\theta}))
for
\theta\in\big(\frac{2-\alpha}{2\alpha},\frac{1}{2}\big)
.

(ii)

For
u_{0}\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
u_{1}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

the weak solution given by (
51
) is a strong one and

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u(t,x)=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\big[\lambda_{n}\langle u_{0},e_{n}\rangle E_{\alpha,1}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})+\lambda_{n}\langle u_{1},e_{n}\rangle tE_{\alpha,2}(-\lambda_{n}t^{\alpha})\big]e_{n}(x)\,.

4.2
Time-fractional Petrovsky systems

We consider the system

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u+\Delta^{2}u=0\qquad\mbox{in}\ (0,T)\times\Omega,\\
u=\Delta u=0\hskip 39.83368pt\mbox{on}\ (0,T)\times\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(52)

We can recast (
52
) as an abstract problem by defining the operator
A
in this way:

\begin{split}D(A)&=H^{4}(\Omega)\cap\{u\in H^{3}(\Omega):u=\Delta u=0\ \mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega\}\\
(Au)(x)&=\Delta^{2}u(x),\quad u\in D(A),\ x\in\Omega.\end{split}

(53)

We assume that the eigenvalues
\lambda_{n}^{\prime}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, of the operator
-\Delta
with domain
H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
are all distinct numbers, and hence the eigenspace generated by

\lambda_{n}^{\prime}
has dimension one.

The biharmonic operator given by (
53
) satisfies:

–

A
is self-adjoint, because,
integrating by parts and taking into account the boundary conditions satisfied by the elements of
D(A)
, we have

\langle Au,v\rangle=\int_{\Omega}\Delta u(x)\Delta v(x)\ dx=\langle u,Av\rangle\qquad u,v\in D(A);

–

the fractional power
\sqrt{A}
of the operator
A
is
-\Delta
with domain
D(\sqrt{A})=H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
;

–

A
is positive, since (
5
) is satisfied. Indeed,

\|\Delta u\|\cdot\|u\|\geq\langle-\Delta u,u\rangle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}\ dx\geq C\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{2}\ dx

and hence

\langle Au,u\rangle=\int_{\Omega}|\Delta u(x)|^{2}\ dx=\|\Delta u\|^{2}\geq C\|u\|^{2}\qquad u\in D(A);

–

the domain
D(A)
is dense in
L^{2}(\Omega)
by the density of
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Lemma 4.2

The spectrum of the operator
A
consists of the sequence tending to
+\infty
of eigenvalues

\lambda_{n}=(\lambda^{\prime}_{n})^{2}
, where
\lambda^{\prime}_{n}
are the eigenvalues of the operator

-\Delta
in
H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

The eigenfunctions
e_{n}
of
-\Delta

(
-\Delta e_{n}=\lambda^{\prime}_{n}e_{n}
)
, which constitutes an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\Omega)
,
are also eigenfunctions of
\Delta^{2}

(
\Delta^{2}e_{n}=\lambda_{n}e_{n}
)
.

Proof.

It is enough to note that

\Delta^{2}e_{n}=-\Delta(-\Delta e_{n})=-\lambda_{n}^{\prime}\Delta(e_{n})=(\lambda_{n}^{\prime})^{2}e_{n}\,.

The operator
\Delta^{2}
cannot have other eigenfunctions, because the sequence
\{e_{n}\}
of the eigenfunctions of
-\Delta
constitutes an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

\square

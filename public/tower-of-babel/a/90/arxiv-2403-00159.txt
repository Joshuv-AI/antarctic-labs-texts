Chow Künneth decomposition for étale motives
Iván Rosas-Soto

Abstract
. In the present article we define an integral analogue of Chow-Künneth decomposition for étale motives. By using families of conservative functors we are able to establish a decomposition of the étale motive of commutative group schemes over a base and we relate to an integral étale Chow-Künneth decomposition of abelian varieties. For a projective variety X X of dimension d d over an algebraically closed field, we construct integral sub-motives h ét 1 ​ ( X ) h^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X) and h ét 2 ​ d − 1 ​ ( X ) h^{2d-1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X) of h ét ​ ( X ) h_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X) .

1.
Introduction

Let
S
be a noetherian finite dimensional scheme and let
G/S
a smooth commutative group scheme of finite type over
S
. According to
[
AHP16
, Theorem 3.7]
in the motivic category
\text{DM}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(S,\mathbb{Q})
we have a decomposition of the relative motive
M_{S}(G)
in the following way

\displaystyle M_{S}(G)\xrightarrow{\simeq}\left(\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}^{\text{kd}(G/S)}\text{Sym}^{n}M_{1}(G/S)\right)\otimes M(\pi_{0}(G/S)),

where
M_{1}(G/S)
is the 1-motive induced by the étale sheaf represented by
\underline{G/S}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
and

is the Kimura dimension, where
g_{s}
is the abelian rank of
G_{s}
and
r_{s}
is the torus rank. The aim of this article is to see if we can lift this isomorphism to integral coefficients in
\text{DM}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(S,\mathbb{Z})

Let us recall some facts concerning the realization of

, denoted by

. As is stated in

, Section 5]

R_{B}(M_{S}(G))=p^{an}_{!}(p^{an})^{!}\mathbb{Q}=p^{an}_{!}\mathbb{Q}[2d]
and
p_{!}^{an}\mathbb{Q}(d)[2d]=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\text{kd}(A)}\left(\bigwedge^{i}\mathcal{H}_{1}(G/S)\right)[i]
where
\mathcal{H}_{1}(G/S):=R_{B}(M_{1}(G/S)[-1])
. On the other hand, for the
\ell-
adic realization of the 1-motive we have the following: consider the realization map

\displaystyle R_{\ell}:\text{DM}_{c}(S,\mathbb{Q})\to D^{b}_{c}(S_{\text{\'{e}t}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})

which is a covariant functor that is compatible with the six functor formalism on both sides. The
\ell
-adic sheaf
\mathcal{H}_{1}(G/S,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
is given by the torsion sheaves
G[\ell^{n}]
, this also holds integrally. Another important fact is related to with the étale cohomology of a connected commutative algebraic group
G
over an algebraically closed field
k

[

, Lemma 4.1]
we have that

\displaystyle H^{*}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(G,\mathbb{Z}/\ell)\simeq\bigwedge^{*}H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(G,\mathbb{Z}/\ell)

for
\ell
a prime number different from
\text{char}(k)
. The same also holds for coefficients
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
and
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
according to
[

.

The aim of this article is to obtain an integral analogue of the decompositon of the étale motive associated to a commutative group scheme over a base

in the category

In order to obtain such decomposition, we use the

2.
Preliminaries

We recall the definition of two models for the category étale of étale motives: the first one
\textbf{DA}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(S,\Lambda)
, uses étale sheaves without transfers, and the second one,
\textbf{DM}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(S,\Lambda)
, uses sheaves with transfers. For the first model we mainly use the references
[
Ayo14
]
and
[
Ayo14a
]
; for the second one we use
[
CD16
]
.

Let
\Lambda
be a commutative ring which in this context is called the ring of coefficients. We are interested in the cases when
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z},\ \mathbb{Q},\ \mathbb{Z}/m
(we will omit
\Lambda
in the notation when
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}
).
We fix a noetherian scheme
S
as our base scheme and we denote
\text{Sch}/S
and
\text{Sm}/S
the categories of schemes of finite type and smooth schemes over
S
respectively. We denote by
\text{Sh}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\text{Sm}/S,\Lambda)
the category of étale sheaves with values in
\Lambda-
modules.

For a given object
X
in
\text{Sm}/S
we denote by
\Lambda^{S}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X)
the étale sheaf associated to the presheaf
U\mapsto\Lambda[\text{Hom}_{\text{Sm}/S}(U,X)]
where
\Lambda[\text{Hom}_{\text{Sm}/S}(U,X)]
is the free
\Lambda-
module generated by
\text{Hom}_{\text{Sm}/S}(U,X)
.

Consider the derived category of étale sheaves
\mathbf{D}(\text{Shv}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\text{Sm}/S,\Lambda))
and denote by
\mathcal{L}
the subcategory of the derived category of étale sheaves that contains the two complexes

\displaystyle\ldots\to 0\to\Lambda^{S}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{U})\to\Lambda_{\text{\'{e}t}}(U)\to 0\to\ldots

and is closed under arbitrary direct sums. Here
\mathbb{A}^{1}:=\text{Spec}(\mathbb{Z}[t])
and
U
is a smooth
S-
scheme the non-zero map is induced by the projection
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{U}\to U
.

Definition 2.1
.

Define
\textbf{DA}^{\text{eff}}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(S,\Lambda)
as the Verdier quotient of
\mathbf{D}(\text{Sh}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\text{Sm}/S,\Lambda))
by
\mathcal{L}
. An object in
\textbf{DA}^{\text{eff}}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(S,\Lambda)
is called an effective motivic sheaf over
S
with coefficients in
\Lambda
. The motivic sheaf
\Lambda^{S}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X)
is called the effective homological motive of
X
and from now on we will denote it by
M^{S}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X)
.

It is necessary to remark that the category
\textbf{DA}^{\text{eff}}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(S,\Lambda)
has the same objects of the category
\mathbf{D}(\text{Sh}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\text{Sm}/S,\Lambda))
, the difference lies in the morphisms of the category since every morphism in
\mathbf{D}(\text{Sh}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\text{Sm}/S,\Lambda))
whose cone is in
\mathcal{L}
gets inverted in
\textbf{DA}^{\text{eff}}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(S,\Lambda)
. In particular there is an isomorphism
M^{S}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{X})\to M^{S}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X)
for all
X\in\text{Sm}/S
induced by
p:\mathbb{A}^{1}_{X}\to X
. Another important observation is that
\textbf{DA}^{\text{eff}}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(S,\Lambda)
inherits the monoidal structure of
\mathbf{D}(\text{Sh}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\text{Sm}/S,\Lambda))
, which at the same time comes from the monoidal structure of
\text{Sh}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\text{Sm}/S,\Lambda)
.

Let
\mathbf{L}
be the Lefschetz motive defined as the cokernel of the inclusion
\Lambda^{S}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\infty_{S})\hookrightarrow\Lambda^{S}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{S})
. The next step in the construction of the triangulated category of motivic étale sheaves is to invert the Lefschetz motive for the monoidal structure. The process used to formally invert the Lefschetz motive in
[
Ayo14a
]
is to consider
\mathbf{L}-
spectra for the tensor product.

An

\displaystyle E=(E_{n},\gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{L}\otimes E_{n}^{\prime}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E_{n+1}}

When

.

Definition 2.3
.

The objects in the category
\textbf{DA}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(S,\Lambda)
are called motivic étale sheaves over
S
. Given a smooth
S-
scheme
X
, then
\Sigma^{\infty}_{\mathbf{L}}\Lambda^{S}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X)
is called the homological motive of
X
and will be denoted by
M^{S}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X)
. We denote
\textbf{DA}_{ct}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(S,\Lambda)
the smallest triangulated subcategory of
\textbf{DA}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(S,\Lambda)
closed under direct summands and containing the motives
M^{S}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X)(-p)[-2p]:=\text{Sus}^{p}_{\mathbf{L}}\Lambda^{S}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X)
for
p\in\mathbb{N}
and
X
an
S-
scheme of finite presentation. Those motivic sheaves are called constructible.

The category
\text{DM}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(S,\Lambda)
is constructed in a similar way as
\textbf{DA}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(S,\Lambda)
, but instead of considering the whole category
\text{Shv}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\text{Sm}/S,\Lambda)
we consider the category of étale sheaves with transfers, i.e. sheaves that come from an étale presheaf that is an additive contravariant functor.

Denote as
\textbf{SmCor}(S,\Lambda)
the category of smooth correspondences over
S
with coefficients in
\Lambda
. The objects are the same ones of
\text{Sm}/S
, and for
U,V\in\text{Sm}/S
the morphisms are finite
\Lambda
-correspondences from
U\to V
. Let
\textbf{Sh}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\textbf{SmCor}(S,\Lambda))
be the category of additive presheaves of commutative groups on
\textbf{SmCor}(S,\Lambda)
whose restriction to
\text{Sm}/S
is an étale sheaf. We call this the category of étale shaves with transfers. According to
[
CD16
, Corollary 2.1.12]
there is an adjoint pair of functors

\displaystyle\gamma^{*}:\text{Sh}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\text{Sm}/S,\Lambda)\rightleftarrows\textbf{Sh}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\textbf{SmCor}(S,\Lambda)):\gamma_{*}.

Let
X
be a smooth
S
-scheme. We denote by
\Lambda^{tr}(X)
the complex of sheaves given by
c(-,X)
the finite correspondences and let
\underline{X}
be the sheaf associated to
X
defined by the presheaf

\displaystyle U\mapsto\underline{X}(U)=\Lambda\text{Hom}_{\text{Sm}/S}(U,X).

of commutative groups. The functor
\gamma_{*}
forgets transfers and
\gamma^{*}(\underline{X})=\mathbb{Z}^{tr}(X)
. We continue by considering the derived category

D(\textbf{Sh}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\textbf{SmCor}(S,\Lambda)))
as
\textbf{Sh}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\textbf{SmCor}(S,\Lambda))
is an abelian category. After that we take the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-localization of the derived category
D(\textbf{Sh}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\textbf{SmCor}(S,\Lambda)))
, giving us the triangulated category of effective étale motives
\text{DM}_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{\text{eff}}(S,\Lambda)
. Finally, to this category we can associate a stable
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-derived category
\text{DM}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(S,\Lambda)
, the category of triangulated étale motives, by
\otimes
-inverting the Tate object
\Lambda^{\text{tr}}_{S}(1):=\Lambda^{\text{tr}}_{S}(\mathbb{P}_{S}^{1},\infty)[-2]
. This can be obtained by applying the functor
\Sigma^{\infty}
.

The functor
\gamma^{*}
is a left Quillen functor, thus we have its derived version

\displaystyle L\gamma^{*}:D(\text{Sh}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\text{Sm}/S,\Lambda))\rightleftarrows D(\textbf{Sh}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\textbf{SmCor}(S,\Lambda))):\gamma_{*}.

which preserves
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-equivalences. With this we obtain an adjunction in the following way

\displaystyle L\gamma^{*}:\textbf{DA}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(S,\Lambda)\rightleftarrows\text{DM}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(S,\Lambda):R\gamma_{*}

If
S
is a noetherian scheme of finite dimension, notice that by
[
Ayo14a
, Théorème B.1]
and
[
CD16
, Remark 5.5.9]
, the categories above mentioned are equivalent. In the context of Voevodsky motives the constructible (compact) objects are called the geometrical motives; the corresponding category is denoted by
\text{DM}^{gm}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(S,\Lambda)
. Also by
[
CD16
, Corollary 5.5.5]
for a quasi-excellent geometrically unibranch noetherian scheme of finite dimension
S
the adjunctions

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}\psi_{!}:\text{DM}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(S,R)\rightleftarrows\text{DM}_{h}(S,R):\mathbf{R}\psi^{*}

give us an equivalence of monoidal triangulated categories.

Among the properties that we have to mention about the different models for the triangulated category of étale motives, is the one concerning conservative functors. Let
\mathfrak{TC}
be the 2-category of triangulated categories. According to
[
Ayo14a
, Théorème 3.9]
, for a commutative ring
\Lambda
the homotopic stable 2-functor
\textbf{DA}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(-,\Lambda):\text{Sch}/S\to\mathfrak{TC}
is separated, this means that for any
S
-morphism
f:X\to Y
, the induced functor
f^{*}
is conservative. Fixing a field
k
, let us consider a field extension
K/k
, and the induced map
p:\text{Spec}(K)\to\text{Spec}(k)
. Since

is a surjective

-morphism, we have that

\displaystyle p^{*}:\textbf{DA}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(k,\Lambda)\to\textbf{DA}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(K,\Lambda)

is conservative. Using the equivalence of categories
\textbf{DA}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(k,\Lambda)\simeq\text{DM}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(k,\Lambda)
(and the same for

), we obtain that

is also conservative. The next examples of a conservative family: according to
[
Ayo14a
, Proposition 3.24]
, for a scheme
S
such that the cohomological dimension of the residue fields is bounded, the family of functors

,for

, is conservative.

Now consider a noetherian scheme

, by

is an isomorphism for any geometric point
\bar{s}
. This theorem can be extended to

, where

.

Definition 2.4

Let

be a scheme. We say that

is good enough for this purposes if it has finite Krull dimension and the punctual p-cohomological dimension is bounded for every prime

.

We can now move on to the following lemma, by mimicking the proof given for

Let

(1)

Let

be a motive. Then

is zero if and only if the pullback
i^{*}_{\bar{s}}M
to any geometric point
\bar{s}\to S
is zero.

(2)

Let

be a morphism in
\textbf{DA}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(S,\mathbb{Z})
. Then
f
is an isomorphism if and only if the pullback
i^{*}_{\bar{s}}(f)
is an isomorphism for any geometric point
\bar{s}\to S
.

[

]
. By

, Proposition 3.24]
we can assume that

with

be a motive such that the pullback

is compactly generated. Therefore we have to prove that all morphism

vanishes, ans according to

, Lemme 3.4]

such that the pullback of

vanishes. By
[
Ayo14a

the functor

3.
Étale decomposition

We present some applications of

, Theorem 1.1]
to the integral decomposition of étale Chow motives. The simplest case is the one described in

, Appendix C]
for varieties without transcendental cohomology classes in degrees different from the dimension. For that, we give the following definition of an integral Chow-Künneth decomposition:

Definition 3.1
.

Let

be a field and let

be a smooth projective variety, of dimension

. We say that

admits an integral Chow-Künneth decomposition in

if:

•

h(X)
admits a rational Chow-Künneth decomposition, see
[

, Definition 6.1.1]
,

\displaystyle h(X)\xrightarrow{\simeq}\bigoplus_{i=0}^{2d}h^{i}(X)\in\text{Chow}(k)_{\mathbb{Q}},

and this map is induced by a morphism

in
\text{Chow}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(k)
.

•

Consider the base change to the algebraic closure

. For every prime number

\displaystyle p_{i}\circ p_{j}=\begin{cases}p_{i}\text{ if }i=j\\
\ 0\text{ if }i\neq j,\end{cases}\quad\rho(\bar{g})^{-1}\circ p_{i}(M_{\bar{k}}/\ell)=R^{i}\bar{f}_{*}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell)\text{ for all }i.

This is nothing but a direct translation of the conservativity properties of the family of functors associated to the change of coefficients in
[

, Proposition 5.4.12]
and combining the results about conservativity
[

, Théorème 3.9]
and continuity

, Proposition 6.3.7]
applied to

Proposition 3.2
.

Consider a field
k
of finite cohomological dimension and let

Proof.

For simplicity, up to tensoring with Lefschetz motive, which is a direct summand of a geometric motive, we may assume that

has an integral Chow-Künneth decomposition then the result is trivial.

Conversely, assume that there exists a Chow-Künneth decomposition for some field extension

[

, Proposition 1.5]
. For the torsion part, let

∎

Remark 4.9

Notice that

In this subsection we aim to obtain an analogue of

, Lemma 1.1]
for the category

Manin principle says that a morphism

between Chow motives is an isomorphism if and only if the associated map
(f\times\text{id}_{Z})_{*}:\text{CH}^{*}(M\otimes h(Z))_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\text{CH}^{*}(N\otimes h(Z))_{\mathbb{Q}}
is an isomorphism for every smooth projective variety

. There are few cases where the structure of the Chow groups are maintained in an easy way, such as projective bundles or blow-ups, but in general it is not an easy task to obtain this property. Recalling that a universal domain
\Omega
over
k
is an algebraically closed field extension of infinite transcendence degree (for example
k=\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
and
\Omega=\mathbb{C}
), the improved Manin priciple states the following:

Theorem 4.10

(
[
Huy18
, Lemma 1.1]
)
.

Consider an algebraically closed field
k
. Let
f:M\to N
be a morphism in the category
\text{Chow}(k)_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Then
f
is an isomorphism of motives in
\text{Chow}(k)_{\mathbb{Q}}
if and only if for
\Omega
a universal domain over
k
, the induced map
(f_{\Omega})_{*}:\text{CH}^{*}(M_{\Omega})_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\text{CH}^{*}(N_{\Omega})_{\mathbb{Q}}
given by the base change

Therefore for an algebraically closed field, is not necessary to test a morphism indexed by the objects in
\text{SmProj}_{k}

By point 1 of Lemma
5.6
,
\rho_{\mathbb{Z}/\ell}
commutes with any additive functor, so we get

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathbb{Z}/\ell}\left(\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\text{kd}(G)}\left(M_{1}(G)^{\otimes i}\right)^{h\mathfrak{S}_{i}}\right)\simeq\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\text{kd}(G)}\rho_{\mathbb{Z}/\ell}\left(\left(M_{1}(G)^{\otimes i}\right)^{h\mathfrak{S}_{i}}\right).

Notice that by definition
\rho_{\mathbb{Z}/\ell}(M)=M\otimes^{L}\mathbb{Z}/\ell
. Since
-\otimes^{L}\mathbb{Z}/\ell
commutes with colimits and is monoidal, see
[
Ayo14a
, Definition 5.6]
, we obtain an isomorphism

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathbb{Z}/\ell}\left(\left(M_{1}(G)^{\otimes i}\right)^{h\mathfrak{S}_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle\simeq\left(\rho_{\mathbb{Z}/\ell}\left(M_{1}(G)^{\otimes i}\right)\right)^{h\mathfrak{S}_{i}}

\displaystyle\simeq\left(\mathcal{H}_{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}/\ell)^{\otimes i}\right)^{h\mathfrak{S}_{i}}.

So in terms of realization we obtain

\rho_{\mathbb{Z}/\ell}(\text{wSym}(M_{1}(G)))\simeq\text{wSym}(\mathcal{H}_{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}/\ell))
. Notice that if
\ell>\text{kd}(G)
, then we get immediately (using the proof in point 3 of
5.6
) that
\text{wSym}(\mathcal{H}_{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}/\ell))\simeq\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\text{kd}(G)}\left(\bigwedge^{i}\mathcal{H}_{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}/\ell)\right)[i]
.

In the following part we will prove that for a prime
\ell\neq\text{char}(k)
and
G
,
H
two smooth commutative algebraic groups we have an isomorphism
\text{wSym}(\mathcal{H}_{1}(G\times H,\mathbb{Z}/\ell))\simeq\text{wSym}(\mathcal{H}_{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}/\ell))\otimes\text{wSym}(\mathcal{H}_{1}(H,\mathbb{Z}/\ell))
, which will allow us to conclude when
\ell\leq\text{kd}(G)
. By definition

\displaystyle\text{wSym}(M_{1}(G\times H))

\displaystyle\simeq\text{wSym}(M_{1}(G)\oplus M_{1}(H))

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\text{kd}(G\times H)}\left(\left(M_{1}(G)\oplus M_{1}(H)\right)^{\otimes i}\right)^{h\mathfrak{S}_{i}}

Since the homotopy fixed points of a motive
M
are defined as a homotopy limit, they commute with finite sums. For simplicity we write
M:=M_{1}(G)
and
N:=M_{1}(H)
, thus we have
\left(M^{\otimes n}\oplus N^{\otimes n}\right)^{h\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\simeq\left(M^{\otimes n}\right)^{h\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\oplus\left(N^{\otimes n}\right)^{h\mathfrak{S}_{n}}
. Moreover we have a canonical morphism

\displaystyle\holim_{B\mathfrak{S}_{n}}(M\oplus N)^{\otimes n}\to(M\oplus N)^{\otimes n}\simeq\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n}\bigoplus^{\binom{n}{i}}M^{\otimes i}\otimes N^{\otimes n-i},

where the last isomorphism is obtained by the distributive and commutative properties. Since
\rho_{\mathbb{Z}/\ell}
commutes with additive functors and limits and is monoidal, we obtain
\rho_{\mathbb{Z}/\ell}\left(\left((M\oplus N)^{\otimes n}\right)^{h\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\right)\simeq\left((M/\ell\oplus N/\ell)^{\otimes n}\right)^{h\mathfrak{S}_{n}}
, passing to the realization
\rho_{\mathbb{Z}/\ell}
and due to the anticommutative structure given by the cup-product, if
n
and
\ell
are coprimes, then

\displaystyle\left((M/\ell\oplus N/\ell)^{\otimes n}\right)^{h\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\simeq\bigwedge^{n}(M/\ell\oplus N/\ell).

Notice that the functor
B\mathfrak{S}_{i}\times B\mathfrak{S}_{j}\to B(\mathfrak{S}_{i}\times\mathfrak{S}_{j})
is an equivalence of categories, then

\displaystyle((M/\ell)^{\otimes i}\otimes(N/\ell)^{\otimes n-i})^{h(\mathfrak{S}_{i}\times\mathfrak{S}_{n-i})}

\displaystyle\simeq\holim_{B\mathfrak{S}_{i}\times B\mathfrak{S}_{n-i}}(M/\ell)^{\otimes i}\otimes(N/\ell)^{\otimes n-i}

\displaystyle\simeq((M/\ell)^{\otimes i})^{h\mathfrak{S}_{i}}\otimes((N/\ell)^{\otimes n-i})^{h\mathfrak{S}_{n-i}}.

Due to the anticommutativity of the weak algebra, we have an isomorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
-vector spaces

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\holim_{B\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\overbrace{M^{\otimes i}\otimes N^{n-i}\oplus\ldots\oplus M^{\otimes i}\otimes N^{n-i}}^{\binom{n}{i}\text{-times}}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus^{\binom{n}{i}}M^{\otimes i}\otimes N^{\otimes n-i}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\holim_{B(\mathfrak{S}_{i}\times\mathfrak{S}_{n-i})}M^{\otimes i}\otimes N^{n-i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

Therefore, we have an isomorphism of graded (anticommutative) algebras
\text{wSym}(\mathcal{H}_{1}(G\times H,\mathbb{Z}/\ell))\simeq\text{wSym}(\mathcal{H}_{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}/\ell))\otimes\text{wSym}(\mathcal{H}_{1}(H,\mathbb{Z}/\ell)).

So the final argument is that the addition map
m:G\times G\to G
induces a morphism of graded algebras over the field
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}

\displaystyle m^{*}:\text{wSym}(M_{1}(G)/\ell)

\displaystyle\to\text{wSym}(M_{1}(G)/\ell)\otimes\text{wSym}(M_{1}(G)/\ell)

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\mapsto x\otimes 1+1\otimes x+\sum x_{i}\otimes y_{i}.

By a lemma about the fundamental structure of such algebras,
[
Mil86
, Lemma 15.2]
, we have that
\text{wSym}(M_{1}(G)/\ell)\simeq\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\text{kd}(G)}\left(\bigwedge^{i}\mathcal{H}_{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}/\ell)\right)[i]
.
∎

H_{1}(A_{\text{\'{e}t}},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})=T_{\ell}(A)
is
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-torsion free since
A[\ell^{n}]\simeq(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n})^{2g}
, so we have an injection
H_{1}(A_{\text{\'{e}t}},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})\hookrightarrow T_{\ell}(A)\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}=H_{1}(A_{\text{\'{e}t}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
, therefore
p_{\mathbb{Q}}
acts as the identity on
H_{1}(A_{\text{\'{e}t}},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
.

Consider the realization
p_{\ell^{n}}\in\text{CH}^{g}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A\times A,\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n})
, where the last group is isomorphic to
H^{2g}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A\times A,\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n})
. As
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
is a flat
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-module and
\varprojlim_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a right exact functor we have that
p_{\ell^{n}}
acts as the identity over
H_{1}(A_{\text{\'{e}t}},\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n})
.
∎

Theorem 5.18
.

Let
k=\bar{k}
be a field and let
A
be an abelian variety. Then there exists a Chow-Künneth decomposition of
A
.

Proof.

Consider an abelian variety
A/k
, then we have that

\displaystyle\text{End}_{\text{Chow}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(k)}(h_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A))\simeq\text{End}_{\text{DM}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(k)}(M_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A)).

Since
M_{1}(A)
is a direct factor of
M_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A)
, then it defines an endomorphism
p
of
M_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A)
, as the endomorphism of the motive
h_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A)
is defined as
\text{CH}^{g}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A\times A)
where
g=\text{dim}(A)
. Since
p\in\text{CH}^{g}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A\times A)
such that
p^{2}=p
, thus we define the motive
h_{1}(A):=(A,p,0)
. The functor
\text{Chow}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(k)^{op}\hookrightarrow\text{DM}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(k)
, sends
h_{1}(A)\mapsto M_{1}(A)
, therefore, as
M_{1}(A)\in\text{Chow}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(k)
we conclude that
h_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A)
admits a Chow-Künneth decomposition.
∎

Theorem 5.19
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
d
over an algebraically closed field
k
. If
\text{Pic}^{0}(X)
is a principally polarized variety, then there exists a decomposition of the motive
h_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X)
as

\displaystyle h_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X)=h_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{0}(X)\oplus h^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X)\oplus h^{+}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X)\oplus h^{2d-1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X)\oplus h_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{2d}(X)

Proof.

An abelian variety
A
admits a principal polarization if and only
\widehat{A}
admits one. So if
A=\text{Pic}^{0}(X)
then the Picard variety is principally polarized if and only if
\text{Alb}_{X}(k)
admits one. Let
k
be an algebraically closed field and let
A
be a principally polarized abelian variety
\lambda:A\xrightarrow{\sim}\widehat{A}
induced by a symmetric ample line bundle. We have an injection

\displaystyle\text{Hom}_{AV}(A,\widehat{A})\hookrightarrow\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}}(T_{\ell}(A),T_{\ell}(\widehat{A}))

Therefore
\lambda
induce an isomorphism of Tate modules. Since we have the following isomorphisms

\displaystyle H^{2g-1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})\simeq T_{\ell}(A)\xrightarrow{\sim}T_{\ell}(\widehat{A})\simeq H^{2g-1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\widehat{A},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})

considering the isomorphism
H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})\simeq H^{2g-1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\widehat{A},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
. Since
\lambda
is induced by a cycle (thanks to the integral étale Fourier transform). Now take an hyperplane
H
in
X
and intersect it with itself
g-1
times, then it induces a Lefschetz operator

\displaystyle L^{g-1}_{A}:H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})\to H^{2g-1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})

which turns out to be an injection. We will see that there exists and étale cycle in
\text{CH}^{g}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(A\times A)
whose multiple by an integer equals the Lefschetz operator. We recall that there exists isomorphisms

\displaystyle\text{Hom}^{0}_{\text{SmProj}_{k}}(X,\text{Pic}^{0}(X))

\displaystyle\simeq\text{Hom}_{AV}(\text{Alb}_{X}(k),\text{Pic}^{0}(X))

\displaystyle\simeq\text{CH}^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X\times X)/\text{CH}^{1}_{\equiv}(X\times X)

where
\text{CH}^{1}_{\equiv}(X\times X)=\text{pr}^{*}_{1}(\text{CH}^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X))\oplus\text{pr}^{*}_{2}(\text{CH}^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X))
and
\text{Hom}^{0}_{\text{SmProj}_{k}}
stands for the pointed morphisms of smooth projective varieties over
k
. Thus the polarization
\lambda:\text{Alb}_{X}(k)\to\text{Pic}^{0}(X)
is induced by a divisor in
X\times X
. Now consider the abelian variety
A=\text{Pic}^{0}(X)
, then there is a morphism
\lambda^{-1}:\text{Pic}^{0}(X)\to\text{Alb}_{X}(k)
, thanks to the existence of a Fourier transform with integral coefficients which is motivic. The cycle
c_{1}(\mathcal{P}_{\text{Pic}^{0}(X)})^{2g-1}/(2g-1)!\in\text{CH}^{2g-1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\text{Pic}^{0}(X)\times\text{Alb}_{X}(k))
where
g=\text{dim}(\text{Pic}^{0}(X))
induce an isomorphism
H^{1}(X,\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n})\to H^{2d-1}(X,\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n})
, since we have isomorphisms
f:\text{Pic}^{0}(X)[\ell^{n}]\simeq H^{1}(X,\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n})
and
g:\text{Alb}_{X}(k)[\ell^{r}]\simeq H^{2d-1}(X,\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n})
, see
[
Mil80
, Chap. III, Cor. 4.18]
, thus we associate the cycle

\displaystyle\lambda^{-1}:=g\circ\frac{c_{1}(\mathcal{P}_{\text{Pic}^{0}(X)})^{2g-1}}{(2g-1)!}\circ f^{-1}\in\text{CH}^{d}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X\times X).

By arguments given in
[
MNP13
, Lemma 6.2.3]
, one has that
L_{X}^{g-1}
defines an isogeny
\alpha:\text{Pic}^{0}(X)\to\text{Alb}_{X}(k)
and another one
\beta:\text{Alb}_{X}(k)\to\text{Pic}^{0}(X)
such that
\alpha\circ\beta=m\cdot\text{id}_{\text{Alb}_{X}(k)}
and
\beta\circ\alpha=m\cdot\text{id}_{\text{Pic}^{0}(X)}
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
. If we take the isogeny
\lambda:\text{Alb}_{X}(k)\to\text{Pic}^{0}(X)
and
\lambda^{-1}:\text{Pic}^{0}(X)\to\text{Alb}_{X}(k)
, with this, we have that
\lambda^{-1}\circ\lambda=\text{id}_{\text{Alb}_{X}(k)}
and
\lambda\circ\lambda^{-1}=\text{id}_{\text{Pic}^{0}(X)}
, since
\alpha\circ\beta
is induced by an algebraic cycle and also
\lambda^{-1}\circ\lambda
(but in this specific case it is induced by an étale cycle). Therefore
m
is invertible in
\text{CH}_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{g}(X\times X)
, thus we obtain the existence of the projector
p_{1}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(X)
and
p_{2d-1}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(X)

Corollary 5.21
.

Let
k=\bar{k}
be a field and let
f:X\to S
be a conic bundle. Then we have an isomorphism

h_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X)\simeq h_{\text{\'{e}t}}(S)\oplus h_{\text{\'{e}t}}(S)(-1)\oplus\text{Pr}(\tilde{C}/C)(-1)
. For a smooth projective surface
S
which admits an integral étale Chow-Künneth decomposition, consider the motive
M=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{6}M^{i}
where each
M^{i}
is defined as

\displaystyle M^{i}:=\begin{cases}h^{3}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(S)\oplus h^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(S)(-1)\oplus\text{Pr}(\tilde{C}/C)(-1)\text{ if }i=3\\
h^{i}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(S)\oplus h^{i-2}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(S)(-1)\text{ if }i\neq 3\end{cases}

then
h_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X)\simeq\bigoplus_{i=0}^{6}M^{i}
.

Proof.

Let us consider the following facts: for
X
we have that the intermediate Jacobian
J^{2}(X)
is an abelian variety isomorphic to
\text{Alb}_{S}(k)\oplus\text{Pic}^{0}(S)\oplus\mathcal{P}_{\tilde{C}}
, see
[
Bel85
, Theorem 3.5]
. Notice that
h^{3}(X)\in\text{Chow}(k)_{\mathbb{Q}}
is a finite dimensional motive isomorphic to the 1-motive given by
M_{1}(J^{2}(X))_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Since we have an isomorphism of cohomology groups

\displaystyle H^{3}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})

\displaystyle\simeq H^{3}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(S,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})\oplus H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(S,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})(-1)\oplus H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(P_{\widetilde{C}},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})(-1)

which is induced by an algebraic cycle since there is an isogeny
\phi:J^{2}(X)\to\text{Alb}_{S}(k)\oplus\text{Pic}^{0}(S)\oplus\mathcal{P}_{\tilde{C}}
, we take the cycle associated cycle
\Gamma_{\phi}^{t}
, this induces an isomorphism of motives
M_{1}(J^{2}(X))_{\mathbb{Q}}\simeq M_{1}(\text{Alb}_{S}(k)\oplus\text{Pic}^{0}(S)\oplus\mathcal{P}_{\tilde{C}})_{\mathbb{Q}}
. This also can be seen from the fact that we have an isomorphism of finite dimensional homological motives

\displaystyle M_{1}(J^{2}(X))_{\mathbb{Q}}\simeq M_{1}(\text{Alb}_{S}(k)\oplus\text{Pic}^{0}(S)\oplus\mathcal{P}_{\tilde{C}})_{\mathbb{Q}}\in\text{Chow}_{\text{hom}}(k)

then the isomorphism can be lifted to
M_{1}(J^{2}(X))_{\mathbb{Q}}\simeq M_{1}(\text{Alb}_{S}(k)\oplus\text{Pic}^{0}(S)\oplus\mathcal{P}_{\tilde{C}})_{\mathbb{Q}}\in\text{Chow}(k)
. As this cycle exists up to rational coefficients, mixing the result
[
RS16
, Theorem 1.3.b]
with the arguments given in
[
Ros22
, Proposition 3.1.7]
, we can conclude similarly that this cycle lifts to an integral étale cycles in different algebraically closed fields, concluding that

\displaystyle h^{i}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X)\simeq\begin{cases}h^{3}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(S)\oplus h^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(S)(-1)\oplus\text{Pr}(\tilde{C}/C)(-1)\text{ if }i=3\\
h^{i}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(S)\oplus h^{i-2}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(S)(-1)\text{ if }i\neq 3.\end{cases}

∎

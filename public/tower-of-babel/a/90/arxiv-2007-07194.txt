Recent rigidity results for graphs with prescribed mean curvature
Bruno Bianchini, Giulio Colombo, Marco Magliaro, Luciano Mari, Patrizia Pucci, Marco Rigoli

Abstract
This survey describes some recent rigidity results obtained by the authors for the prescribed mean curvature problem on graphs u : M → ℝ u:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R} . Emphasis is put on minimal, CMC and capillary graphs, as well as on graphical solitons for the mean curvature flow, in warped product ambient spaces. A detailed analysis of the mean curvature operator is given, focusing on maximum principles at infinity, Liouville properties, gradient estimates. Among the geometric applications, we mention the Bernstein theorem for positive entire minimal graphs on manifolds with non-negative Ricci curvature, and a splitting theorem for capillary graphs over an unbounded domain Ω ⊂ M \Omega\subset M , namely, for CMC graphs satisfying an overdetermined boundary condition. 1 1 1 Keywords: complete Riemannian manifolds; entire solutions; minimal graphs; mean curvature operator; solitons. Introduction

The purpose of this survey is to illustrate some recent rigidity results, obtained by the authors, about the prescribed mean curvature problem on a complete Riemannian manifold
M
. It is a pleasure to dedicate our work to Alberto Farina on the occasion of his birthday, and to have the opportunity to discuss some of his significant contributions to the theory. Hereafter, we shall always restrict to real valued functions
u:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, and their associated entire graphical hypersurfaces
\Sigma\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}\times M
described by the map

\varphi:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\times M,\qquad\varphi(x)=\big(u(x),x\big)

(throughout the paper, the word
entire
will mean defined on all of
M
). Taking into account the huge literature on hypersurfaces with prescribed mean curvature, notably on minimal and constant mean curvature (CMC) ones, some choices have to be made, and we decided to exclusively focus on references considering the graphical case in codimension
1
. Thus, unfortunately, important works concerning embedded (immersed) hypersurfaces have been omitted, unless they provide new results in the graphical case too. Concerning the prescribed mean curvature problem in Euclidean space, that will be briefly recalled in the next section, we recommend the surveys
[
123
,
55
]
for an excellent account. Simon’s
[
123
]
focuses on the minimal surface equation, while Farina’s
[
55
]
includes Liouville theorems for more general operators.

1.1
The prescribed curvature problem in
\mathbb{R}\times M

To put our results into perspective, we briefly recall the main achievements concerning the problem on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. We begin with entire minimal graphs in
\mathbb{R}^{m+1}
, described by functions
u:\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
solving the minimal surface equation

\diver\left(\frac{Du}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}\right)=0,

(1)

that is the Euler-Lagrange equation of the area functional

\Omega\Subset\mathbb{R}^{m}\mapsto\int_{\Omega}\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}\mathrm{d}x

(2)

with respect to compactly supported variations. In 1915, Bernstein
[
8
]
proved that the only solutions of (
1
) on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
are affine functions (his proof, highly nontrivial, has been perfected in
[
78
,
96
]
, where a missing detail was corrected). Since then, various other proofs appeared in the literature, each one exploiting some different peculiarities of the two dimensional setting, especially: the possibility to use holomorphic function theory and the uniformization theorem
[
102
,
76
,
65
,
48
]
, or the quadratic volume growth of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
coupled with the specific form of the Gauss-Bonnet theorem
[
109
,
123
,
97
]
. A presentation of some of them can be found in
[
55
]
. The problem whether Bernstein’s rigidity theorem holds for minimal graphs over
\mathbb{R}^{m}
if
m\geq 3
challenged researchers for long, and the efforts to answer this question gave rise to many new and deep techniques in Geometric Analysis. The first proof of Bernstein theorem that allowed for a possible higher dimensional generalization was given by Fleming
[
67
]
, and since then, in few years, the problem has been completely solved affirmatively in dimension
m\leq 7
by works of De Giorgi (
[
42
,
43
]
,
m=3
), Almgren (
[
1
]
,
m=4
) and Simons (
[
125
]
,
m\leq 7
), while Bombieri, De Giorgi and Giusti
[
12
]
constructed the first counterexample if
m\geq 8
(a large class of further examples was later given by Simon
[
124
]
). Summarizing, the following theorem holds:

\text{each minimal graph over $\mathbb{R}^{m}$ is affine}\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad m\leq 7.

(
\mathscr{B}1
)

If
u
satisfies some a-priori bounds, then more rigidity is expected. Indeed, as a consequence of the local gradient estimate for minimal graphs
u:B_{r}(x)\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
due to Bombieri, De Giorgi and Miranda
[
11
]
:

|Du(x)|\leq c_{1}\exp\left\{c_{2}\frac{u(x)-\inf_{B_{r}}u}{r}\right\},

for some constants
c_{j}(m)
, one deduces both that

positive minimal graphs on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
are constant, for each
m\geq 2

(
\mathscr{B}2
)

and that

\begin{array}[]{c}\text{minimal graphs on $\mathbb{R}^{m}$ with at most linear growth have bounded gradient,}\\
\text{thus, by work of Moser \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{jmoser}{}{}]} and De Giorgi \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{dg1,dg2}{}{}]}, they are affine.}\end{array}

(
\mathscr{B}3
)

Further splitting results in the spirit of (

⁢
B
3

) can be obtained by just assuming the boundedness of some of the directional derivatives of
u
. In particular, improving on work of Bombieri and Giusti
[
13
]
, Farina in
[
53
,
54
]
showed that
u
is affine provided that
n-7
partial derivatives of
u
are bounded on one side.

The possible existence of a similar results for entire minimal graphs defined on more general complete Riemannian manifolds
(M^{m},\sigma)
heavily depends on the geometry of
M
. If
\mathbb{R}\times M
is endowed with the product metric

\langle\,,\,\rangle=\mathrm{d}t^{2}+\sigma

then the (normalized) mean curvature
H
of the image
\varphi(M)
in the upward pointing direction is given by

mH=\diver\left(\frac{Du}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}\right),

formally the same as for
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, where now
D,\diver
are the gradient and divergence in
(M,\sigma)
. In particular, entire minimal graphs solve (
1
) on
M
. However, for instance if
M
is the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{m}
, (
1
) admits uncountably many bounded solutions. Indeed, the Plateau problem at infinity is solvable for every continuous
\phi
on the boundary at infinity
\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{m}
, that is, there exists
u
solving (
1
) on
\mathbb{H}^{m}
and approaching
\phi
at infinity. The result was shown in
[
101
]
for
m=2
, while the higher dimensional case, to our knowledge, first appeared in 2010 as a special case of
[
52
]
. We note in passing that, as a consequence of Theorem
3.2
below, any solution of Plateau’s problem on
\mathbb{H}^{m}
has a bounded gradient.

Recently, various interesting works investigated the Plateau’s problem at infinity on Cartan-Hadamard (CH) manifolds
M
, that is, on complete, simply connected manifolds with non-positive sectional curvature
\mathrm{Sec}
, elucidating the sharp thresholds of
\mathrm{Sec}
to guarantee its solvability or not (cf.
[
112
,
21
,
22
,
18
,
77
,
20
]
). The picture is quite subtle and an updated account can be found in
[
74
]
. In particular, having denoted with
r
the distance from a fixed origin
o\in M
, we mention that the Plateau problem at infinity on such
M
is solvable whenever any of the following pinching conditions hold outside of a compact set
B_{R}(o)
:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle-\frac{e^{2\kappa r}}{r^{2+\varepsilon}}\leq\mathrm{Sec}\leq-\kappa^{2}&\quad\text{(by \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{rite_1}{}{}]}, see also \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{chr}{}{}, Cor 1.7]}),}\\[8.5359pt]
\displaystyle-r^{2(\kappa^{\prime}-2)-\varepsilon}\leq\mathrm{Sec}\leq-\frac{\kappa^{\prime}(\kappa^{\prime}-1)}{r^{2}}&\quad\text{(by \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{chr}{}{}, Thm. 1.5]}),}\end{array}

for constants
\varepsilon,\kappa>0
,
\kappa^{\prime}>1
. Hereafter, an inequality
\mathrm{Sec}\leq G(r)
will shortly mean

\mathrm{Sec}(\pi_{x})\leq G\big(r(x)\big)\qquad\text{for every $x\in M$ and $2$-plane $\pi_{x}\leq T_{x}M$.}

In general, a two-sided bound on
\mathrm{Sec}
is necessary, although a (suitable) upper bound suffices on rotationally symmetric CH manifolds (cf.
[
20
]
). Indeed, in
[
77
]
the authors constructed a
3
-dimensional CH manifold with
\mathrm{Sec}\leq-\kappa^{2}<0
, that admits bounded solutions of the minimal surface equation, but for which the Dirichlet problem at infinity is not solvable for
any
nonconstant boundary datum
\phi
. The dimension restriction is not removable, since for
m=2
the one-sided condition
\mathrm{Sec}\leq-\kappa^{2}<0
suffices for the solvability of the Plateau problem at infinity, see
[
70
]
. We also mention the surprising existence of parabolic, entire minimal graphs in
M^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
, with
M^{2}
a CH surface satisfying
\mathrm{Sec}\leq-\kappa^{2}<0
, obtained in
[
34
,
70
]
.

It is therefore tempting to ask whether results similar to those for
\mathbb{R}^{m}
may hold on manifolds with non-negative curvature, and in view of the structure theory initiated by Cheeger and Colding in
[
24
,
25
,
26
,
27
]
, a particularly intriguing class to investigate in this respect is that of manifolds with
\mathrm{Ric}\geq 0
. This is enforced by the analogy with the behaviour of harmonic functions on
M
. In fact, positive harmonic functions on a manifold with
\mathrm{Ric}\geq 0
are constant
[
131
,
30
]
, and harmonic functions with linear growth provide splitting directions for the tangent cones at infinity of
M
, and for
M
itself provided that
\mathrm{Ric}\geq 0
is strengthened to
\mathrm{Sec}\geq 0

[
28
]
. The parallelism is even more evident by considering that (
1
) rewrites as the harmonicity of
u
on
\Sigma
with its induced metric.

While we are aware of no references considering the full Bernstein theorem (

⁢
B
1

) on spaces different from
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, and in fact the problem seems quite challenging even on manifolds with non-negative sectional curvature, recently some progresses were made on (

⁢
B
2

), (

⁢
B
3

). Below, we comment on the following recent result, independently due to the authors and to Ding
[
44
]
:

Theorem 1.1

(
[
35
,
44
]
)
.

A complete manifold with
\mathrm{Ric}\geq 0
satisfies (

⁢
B
2

): positive solutions of (
1
) on the entire
M
are constant.

Under the further condition
\mathrm{Sec}\geq-\kappa^{2}
for some constant
\kappa>0
, Theorem
1.1
was proved by Rosenberg, Schulze and Spruck in
[
117
]
. If the Ricci and sectional curvatures are allowed to be negative somewhere, then the situation is different: in this respect, we quote the recent
[
20
]
by Casteras, Heinonen and Holopainen, ensuring the constancy of positive minimal graphs with at most linear growth on manifolds with asymptotically non-negative sectional curvature and only one end. Regarding property (

⁢
B
3

), the rigidity of manifolds supporting linearly growing, nonconstant minimal graphs was studied in
[
45
,
46
]
on manifolds satisfying

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\mathrm{Ric}\geq 0,\qquad\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|B_{r}|}{r^{m}}>0\\[8.5359pt]
\text{the curvature tensor decays quadratically}.\end{array}\right.

Together with equation (
1
), that we will investigate in Section
3
, in the present survey we will also consider energies containing a potential term, of the type

\Omega\Subset M\qquad\longmapsto\qquad\int_{\Omega}\Big(\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}+b(x)F(u)\Big)\mathrm{d}x,

with
0<b\in C(M)
and
f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. The corresponding Euler-Lagrange equation becomes

\diver\left(\frac{Du}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}\right)=b(x)f(u),

(3)

with
f=F^{\prime}
. This family of equations includes the capillarity equation
b(x)f(u)=\kappa u
, for some constant
\kappa>0
, that has been thoroughly studied, for instance, in
[
64
]
.

Notation 1.2
.

Hereafter, given two positive functions
f,g
, with
f\gtrsim g
we mean
f\geq Cg
for some positive constant
C
, and similarly for
f\lesssim g
. We write
f\in L^{1}(+\infty)
to denote

\int^{+\infty}f(s)\mathrm{d}s<+\infty,

and similarly for
f\in L^{1}(0^{+})
. When there is no risk of confusion, we write
\infty
instead of
+\infty,-\infty
.

If
f
is non-decreasing, the behaviour of solutions of (
3
) on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
is well understood, thanks to the works of Tkachev
[
128
,
129
]
, Naito and Usami
[

]
and Serrin
[

]
, see also
[

Theorem 1.3

(
[
128
,
100
,
121
]
)
.

If
f
be continuous and non-decreasing on
\mathbb{R}
,
f\not\equiv 0
and suppose

b(x)\gtrsim\big(1+|x|\big)^{-\mu}\qquad\text{on }\,\mathbb{R}^{m},

for some constant
\mu<1
. Then a solution

of (
3
) on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
satisfies
f(u)\equiv 0
on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, and thus it is constant by (

⁢

2

).

Remark 1.4
.

Condition
f\not\equiv 0
, together with
f(u)\equiv 0
, guarantees that
u
is one sided bounded and thus the applicability of (

⁢
B
2

). It is therefore essential, as the example of affine functions show.

Remark 1.5
.

The result establishes that the only graphs with constant mean curvature (CMC) defined on the entire
\mathbb{R}^{m}
must be minimal, a result first proved by Heinz
[
75
]
for
m=2
, and later extended independently by Chern
[
31
]
and Flanders
[
66
]
to all
m\geq 3
. As a matter of fact, Salavessa in
[
118
]
observed that the result is true on each manifold with zero Cheeger constant, in particular, on complete manifolds with subexponential volume growth.

In particular, in the assumptions of Theorem
1.3
the only solution of the capillarity equation on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
is
u\equiv 0
. In Theorem
1.1
below, we shall prove that Theorem
1.3
holds on each manifold with
\mathrm{Ric}\geq 0
; precisely, the implication
f(u)\equiv 0
only requires that
M
has polynomial volume growth of geodesic balls, a condition implied (via Bishop-Gromov comparison), but not equivalent, to
\mathrm{Ric}\geq 0
. On the contrary, as we shall see below, Theorem
1.3
fails on manifolds with faster than polynomial volume growth, cf. Remark
2.23
. We note that the restriction
\mu<1
can be relaxed for the capillarity case, the sharp threshold being
\mu<2
by recent work of Farina and Serrin
[
60
]
, and we will come back on this later, see Theorem
2.29
below.

It is instructive to compare Theorem
1.3
to a corresponding result for the Laplace operator, say for
b\equiv 1
: To make the statement easier, we assume that
f
has only one zero, so up to translation
f(0)=0
,
tf(t)>0
for
t\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
, and set

F(s)=\int_{0}^{s}f(t)\mathrm{d}t.

(4)

Then, by works of Keller
[
80
]
and Osserman
[
105
]
, the only solution of

\Delta u=f(u)\qquad\text{on }\,\mathbb{R}^{m}

(5)

is
u\equiv 0
if and only if the Keller-Osserman conditions

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int^{+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{\sqrt{F(s)}}<\infty,

(6)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{\sqrt{F(s)}}<\infty

(7)

hold. More precisely, (
6
) guarantees the following Liouville property:

any solution of
\Delta u\geq f(u)
is bounded above and satisfies
f(u^{*})\leq 0
,

(8)

where
u^{*}:=\sup_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}u
. Thus,
u\leq 0
on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. The reverse inequality
u\geq 0
follows by noticing that
\bar{u}:=-u
solves
\Delta\bar{u}=\bar{f}(\bar{u})
with
\bar{f}(t)=-f(-t)
, so condition (
7
) is simply a rewriting of (
6
) applied to
\bar{f}
.

Remark 1.6
.

Regarding the case of non-constant weights
b
, let
f>0
on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
, and assume that
f
is increasing for large enough

. If

b(x)\gtrsim\big(1+|x|\big)^{-2}\qquad\text{on }\,\mathbb{R}^{m},

then (

) is necessary and sufficient to guarantee that solutions of

\Delta u\geq b(x)f(u)\qquad\text{on }\,\mathbb{R}^{m}

satisfy

. This result can be obtained, for instance, by combining Theorems 10.3 and Corollary 10.23 in
[

. The quadratic lower bound for

is sharp, since positive entire solutions were constructed in

,

]
when (

b(x)\leq B(|x|),\qquad\text{with }\,\int^{+\infty}sB(s)\mathrm{d}s<\infty.

Remark 1.7
.

We mention that (
6
) is sufficient for the validity of the Liouville property (
8
) on complete manifolds satisfying

\mathrm{Ric}(\nabla r,\nabla r)\geq-\kappa^{2}(1+r^{2})\qquad\text{on }\,M\backslash\mathrm{cut}(o),

for some constant

, where

is the distance from a fixed origin

, and

is the cut-locus of

. This result follows, for example, as a particular case of

\limsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log|B_{r}|}{r^{2}}<\infty.

The absence of a growth requirement on

might be partially justified by considering the Laplacian and the mean curvature operator as members of the the family of quasilinear operators

\Delta_{\varphi}u:=\diver\left(\frac{\varphi(|Du|)}{|Du|}Du\right),

for

with

to

1.2
Further ambient spaces, and MCF solitons

Ambient manifolds of the type
\mathbb{R}\times M
leave aside various cases of interest, notably interesting representations of the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{m+1}
. This and other examples motivated us to consider graphs in more general ambient manifolds
\bar{M}
, that foliate topologically as products
\mathbb{R}\times M
along the flow lines of relevant, in general not parallel, vector fields. A sufficiently large class for our purposes is that of warped products

\bar{M}=\mathbb{R}\times_{h}M,\qquad\text{with metric}\qquad\bar{g}=\mathrm{d}s^{2}+h(s)^{2}g,

(12)

for some positive
h\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
. Note that
X=h(s)\partial_{s}
is a conformal field with geodesic flow lines. Given
v:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, we define the graph

\Sigma^{m}=\Big\{(s,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times M,\ \ s=v(x)\Big\},

that we call a geodesic graph. For instance,
\mathbb{H}^{m+1}
admits the following representations:

(i)

\mathbb{H}^{m+1}=\mathbb{R}\times_{e^{s}}\mathbb{R}^{m}
, with the slices of constant
s
, called horospheres, being flat Euclidean spaces with normalized mean curvature
1
in the direction of
-\partial_{s}
. This follows by changing variables
s=-\log x_{0}
in the upper half-space model

\mathbb{H}^{m+1}=\Big\{(x_{0},x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\ \ :\ \ x_{0}>0\Big\},\qquad\bar{g}=\frac{1}{x_{0}^{2}}\Big(\mathrm{d}x_{0}^{2}+g_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\Big);

(13)

(ii)

\mathbb{H}^{m+1}=\mathbb{R}\times_{\cosh s}\mathbb{H}^{m}
, with the slices
\{s=s_{0}\}
for constant
s_{0}
, called hyperspheres, being totally umbilical hyperbolic spaces of normalized mean curvature
H=\tanh(s_{0})
in the direction of
-\partial_{s}
, and sectional curvature
-(\cosh s_{0})^{-2}
.

In cases (i) and (ii) we say that
\Sigma
is, respectively, a geodesic graph over horospheres and hyperspheres. Do Carmo and Lawson in
[
47
]
investigated geodesic graphs in
\mathbb{H}^{m+1}
with constant mean curvature
H\in[1,1]
, both over horospheres and hyperspheres, and proved the following Bernstein type theorem:

\begin{array}[]{l}\text{if $\Sigma$ is over a horosphere, then $\Sigma$ is a horosphere (in particular, $H=\pm 1$)}\\
\text{if $\Sigma$ is over a hypersphere, then $\Sigma$ is a hypersphere.}\end{array}

Their result is part of a more general statement, that draws the rigidity of a properly embedded CMC hypersurface
\Sigma
from the rigidity of its trace on the boundary at infinity
\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{m+1}
. The proof, relying on the moving plane method, seems difficult to adapt to variable mean curvature, and the search for an alternative approach was one among the geometric motivations that lead us to write the monograph
[
10
]
.

The equations for geodesic graphs with prescribed mean curvature
H
is more transparent when written in terms of the parameter
t
of the flow
\Phi
of
X
, related to
s
by the equation

t=\int_{0}^{s}\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{h(\sigma)},\qquad t:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow t(\mathbb{R}):=I.

(14)

Set

\lambda(t)=h\big(s(t)\big),\qquad u(x)=t\big(v(x)\big),

(15)

so
u:M\rightarrow I
and
\Sigma
writes as
\{t=u(x)\}
. Clearly,
u=v
when
\bar{M}=\mathbb{R}\times M
with the Riemannian product metric. Computing the normalized mean curvature
H
of
\Sigma^{m}
with respect to the upward–pointing unit normal

\nu=\frac{1}{\lambda(u)\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}\Big(\partial_{t}-(\Phi_{u})_{*}Du\Big),

(16)

we obtain the following equation for
u:M\rightarrow I
:

\diver\left(\frac{Du}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}\right)=m\lambda(u)H+m\frac{\lambda_{t}(u)}{\lambda(u)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}\quad\text{on }\,M,

(17)

where
\lambda_{t}
is the derivative of
\lambda
with respect to
t
.
Clearly, if
H=0
this is an equation of type

\diver\left(\frac{Du}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}\right)=b(x)f(u)\ell(|Du|)\quad\text{on }\,M,

(18)

for suitable
b,f,\ell
. Equation (
18
) will be our focus for the first part of this survey. Another prescription of
H
that leads to an equation of the same type arises from the study of mean curvature flow solitons. Briefly, consider a mean curvature flow (MCF) evolving from
\Sigma
, that is, a solution
\varphi:[0,T]\times M\rightarrow\bar{M}
of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\frac{\partial\varphi_{t}}{\partial t}=mH_{t}\qquad\text{on }\,M,\\[5.69046pt]
\varphi(0,x)=(u(x),x)\end{array}\right.

with
H_{t}
the mean curvature of
\Sigma_{t}=\varphi_{t}(M)
in the direction of the normal
\nu_{t}
chosen to match (
16
) at time
t=0
; we call
\varphi
a soliton with respect to a field
Y
on
\bar{M}
, if
\varphi
moves
\Sigma
along the flow
\Psi
of
Y
, that is, if there exists a tangent vector field
T
on
M
with flow
\eta_{t}
, and a time reparametrization
t\mapsto\tau(t)
, such that

\varphi(x,t)=\Psi\big(\tau(t),\eta(t,x)\big).

Differentiating at
t=0
, we see that a MCF soliton satisfies the identity
\bar{g}(Y,\nu)=mH
. Interesting solitons arise when
Y
is a conformal or Killing field, and in our case the choice
Y=\pm h(s)\partial_{s}
allows to rewrite (
17
) as the following equation:

\diver\left(\frac{Du}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}\right)=\left[\frac{m\lambda_{t}(u)\pm\lambda^{3}(u)}{\lambda(u)}\right]\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}=\frac{mh^{\prime}(v)\pm h^{2}(v)}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}

(19)

with
v=t^{-1}(u)
. In particular, a self-translator in
\mathbb{R}^{m+1}
, that is, a soliton for the vertical direction
\partial_{s}
, satisfies

\diver\left(\frac{Du}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}\right)=\frac{\pm 1}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}.

(20)

We mention that the existence problem for equations tightly related to (
19
), describing certain
f
-minimal graphs in
\mathbb{R}\times M
, has recently been considered in
[
19
]
.

Remark 1.8
.

Another relevant class of ambient spaces is that of products
M\times_{h}\mathbb{R}
with metric
\bar{g}=g+h(x)^{2}\mathrm{d}s^{2}
, for some
0<h\in C^{\infty}(M)
. In this case
X=\partial_{s}
is Killing but not parallel, unless
h
is constant. For instance,
\mathbb{H}^{m+1}
admits two such warped product decompositions, according to whether
X
has one or two fixed points at infinity: the first can be obtained by isolating the coordinate
s=x_{m}
in the upper half-space model (
13
), leading to

\mathbb{H}^{m+1}=\mathbb{H}^{m}\times_{h}\mathbb{R}\qquad\text{with }\,h(x_{0},\ldots,x_{m-1})=\frac{1}{x_{0}};

(21)

the second can be written as

\mathbb{H}^{m+1}=\mathbb{H}^{m}\times_{\cosh r}\mathbb{R},\qquad\bar{g}=g_{\mathbb{H}^{m}}+\big(\cosh^{2}r(x)\big)\mathrm{d}s^{2},

(22)

with
r:\mathbb{H}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
the distance from a fixed origin in
\mathbb{H}^{m}
, and corresponds, in the upper half–space model, to the fibration of
\mathbb{H}^{m+1}
via Euclidean lines orthogonal to the totally geodesic hypersphere
\{x_{0}^{2}+|x|^{2}=1\}
. There is no rigidity for graphs in
\mathbb{H}^{m+1}
along these decompositions: the Plateau’s problem at infinity for graphs with constant
H\in(0,1)
is always solvable, by work of Guan and
Spruck
[
73
]
for (
22
), and Ripoll and Telichevesky
[
111
]
for (
21
). Existence for the prescribed mean curvature equation on more general products
M\times_{h}\mathbb{R}
is studied in
[
17
]
.

2
Analytic behaviour of the mean curvature operator

Hereafter, all solutions of differential equations and inequalities will be meant in the weak sense. In our study of the rigidity problem for equations of the type

\diver\left(\frac{Du}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}\right)=\mathscr{F}(x,u,Du)

(23)

on a complete, connected manifold
M
, we assume

\mathscr{F}(x,t,p)\geq\frac{f(t)}{(1+r(x))^{\mu}}\frac{|p|^{1-\chi}}{\sqrt{1+|p|^{2}}},

(24)

for suitable
\chi\in[0,1]
and
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
, reducing the problem to the investigation of solutions of

\diver\left(\frac{Du}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}\right)\geq\frac{f(u)}{(1+r)^{\mu}}\frac{|Du|^{1-\chi}}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}\qquad\text{on }\,M.

(25)

Note that, for
\chi\in(0,1)
, the RHS is allowed to vanish both as
|Du|\rightarrow 0
and as
|Du|\rightarrow\infty
. The form (
24
) is sufficiently general to encompass all of our cases of interest: for instance, (
17
) for
H=0
is included by setting

\mu=0,\qquad f(t)=m\frac{\lambda_{t}(t)}{\lambda(t)}=mh^{\prime}(s(t)),\qquad\chi=1,

while the capillarity equation
implies the next inequality for each
\chi\in[0,1]
:

\diver\left(\frac{Du}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}\right)=\kappa u\geq\frac{\kappa}{C_{\chi}}u\frac{|Du|^{1-\chi}}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}\qquad\text{on }\,\{u>0\},

where we used the fact that
t^{1-\chi}/\sqrt{1+t^{2}}\leq C_{\chi}
on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
, for some constant
C_{\chi}>0
. By the Pasting Lemma (Kato inequality, cf.
[
39
]
)
u_{+}=\max\{u,0\}
solves, in the weak sense,

\diver\left(\frac{Du_{+}}{\sqrt{1+|Du_{+}|^{2}}}\right)\geq\frac{\kappa}{C_{\chi}}u_{+}\frac{|Du_{+}|^{1-\chi}}{\sqrt{1+|Du_{+}|^{2}}}\qquad\text{on }\,M.

(26)

Thus, we can choose

\mu=0,\qquad f(t)=\frac{\kappa}{C_{\chi}}\ \ \text{constant, and any }\,\chi\in[0,1].

As we shall see, the arbitrariness in the choice of
\chi
will allow to reach the optimal thresholds for the rigidity of solutions to (
26
).

Our goal in this section is to identify sharp conditions that force any non-constant solution of (
23
), possibly already matching some a-priori bound, to satisfy
f(u)\equiv 0
on
M
. If
f
has just isolated zeroes, we would directly deduce that
u
must be constant, otherwise the rigidity issue for solutions of

\diver\left(\frac{Du}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}\right)=0

requires further tools and assumptions, described in the next section. The argument to deduce
f(u)\equiv 0
depends on the fact that any non-constant solution of (
25
), for suitable
f
or provided that
u
grows slowly enough, is automatically bounded from above and satisfies

f(u^{*})\leq 0,

where, hereafter in this survey,

u^{*}:=\sup_{M}u,\qquad u_{*}:=\inf_{M}u.

Remark 2.1

(
Constant solutions
)
.

We observe that a constant function solves (
25
) if and only if either

\begin{array}[]{ll}\chi\in[0,1),&\quad\text{independently of }\,f,\ \text{ or}\\[5.69046pt]
\chi=1,&\quad f(u^{*})\leq 0.\end{array}

Therefore, in what follows we concentrate on
non-constant
solutions.

The conclusion
f(u^{*})\leq 0
should be interpreted as a maximum principle at infinity, in the spirit of
[
107
]
. Indeed, if
M
is compact and we assume
u\in C^{2}(M)
and
\chi=1
,
f(u^{*})\leq 0
follows readily by evaluating (
25
) at a point
x_{0}
realizing
u^{*}
, and using that
|Du(x_{0})|=0
,
D^{2}u(x_{0})\leq 0
. We shall be more detailed in the next section. To constrain solutions of (
23
), the idea is to apply the maximum principle at infinity both to
u
and to
\bar{u}:=-u
, which is possible if, for instance,
\mathscr{F}
satisfies conditions like

\mathscr{F}(x,t,p)=-\mathscr{F}(x,-t,-p).

In this case,
\bar{u}
solves

\diver\left(\frac{D\bar{u}}{\sqrt{1+|D\bar{u}|^{2}}}\right)\geq\frac{\bar{f}(\bar{u})}{(1+r)^{\mu}}\frac{|D\bar{u}|^{1-\chi}}{\sqrt{1+|D\bar{u}|^{2}}},\qquad\text{with }\,\bar{f}(t):=-f(-t),

and
\bar{f}(\bar{u}^{*})\leq 0
rewrites as
f(u_{*})\geq 0
. If, for instance,
f
is increasing, we would eventually conclude the identity
f(u)\equiv 0
on
M
.

Taking into account these facts, in the present section we focus on the maximum principle at infinity for solutions of inequality (
25
), and on related Liouville properties. Our purpose is to guide the reader through the results in
[
10
]
, discuss the sharpness of the assumptions and their interplay, and illustrate some geometric applications. For reasons of room, no proofs of the analytic results will be given or sketched, but their applications to geometric problems will be commented in some details.

2.1
Weak and strong maximum principles at infinity

For
u
bounded from above, the necessity to deduce
f(u^{*})\leq 0
even if
u^{*}
is not attained motivated the introduction of the fundamental Omori-Yau maximum principles,
[
104
,
131
,
30
]
, that generated an active area of research related to potential theory on manifolds
[
93
,
94
]
, with applications to a variety of geometric problems. We refer the reader to
[
3
,
10
,
107
]
for a detailed account and an extensive set of references. In its original formulation, the Omori-Yau principle for the Laplace-Beltrami operator is the property that, for every
u\in C^{2}(M)
bounded from above, there exists a sequence
\{x_{j}\}\subset M
satisfying

u(x_{j})\rightarrow u^{*},\qquad|Du(x_{j})|<\frac{1}{j},\qquad\Delta u(x_{j})<\frac{1}{j}.

Clearly, if
u^{*}
is attained at some
x
then the sequence can be chosen to be constantly equal to
x
. The Omori-Yau principle is also called, in
[
3
,
10
]
, the
strong maximum principle at infinity
. The fact that the gradient condition
|Du(x_{j})|<1/j
is unnecessary in many applications motivated the introduction, in
[
107
]
, of the related
weak maximum principle at infinity
, asking for the validity, along a suitable sequence
\{x_{j}\}
, just of bounds

u(x_{j})\rightarrow u^{*},\qquad\Delta u(x_{j})<\frac{1}{j}.

Examples show that the two are indeed different, that is, there exist manifolds satisfying the weak but not the strong maximum principle, cf.
[
15
,
10
]
. The weak principle turns out to have important links with the theory of stochastic processes, explored in
[
107
]
and recalled later in this paper in more detail. In our setting, we shall look at suitable formulations of weak and strong principles, adapted to solutions with low regularity and to mean curvature type operators. Extension to more general operators can be found in
[
10
]
(quasilinear ones) and in
[
93
,
94
]
(fully nonlinear ones).

Hereafter, for
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
and
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
we use the shorthand notations
\{u>\gamma\}
and
\{u>\gamma,\ |Du|<\varepsilon\}
to mean, respectively, the sets

\big\{x\ :\ u(x)>\gamma\big\},\qquad\big\{x\ :\ u(x)>\gamma,\ |Du(x)|<\varepsilon\big\}

Definition 2.2

(
[
10
]
)
.

Given
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
,
\chi\in[0,1]
, we say that

•

the
strong maximum principle at infinity
,
(\mathrm{SMP}_{\infty})
, holds if whenever
u\in C^{1}(M)
is non-constant, bounded from above and solves

\diver\left(\frac{Du}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}\right)\geq\frac{K}{(1+r)^{\mu}}\frac{|Du|^{1-\chi}}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}\qquad\text{on }\,\big\{u>\gamma,\ |Du|<\varepsilon\big\}

(27)

for some constant
\varepsilon>0
and
K,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, then
K\leq 0
.

•

the
weak maximum principle at infinity
,
(\mathrm{WMP}_{\infty})
, holds if whenever
u\in\mathrm{Lip}_{\mathrm{loc}}(M)
is non-constant, bounded from above and solves

\diver\left(\frac{Du}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}\right)\geq\frac{K}{(1+r)^{\mu}}\frac{|Du|^{1-\chi}}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}\qquad\text{on }\,\big\{u>\gamma\big\}

(28)

for some constant
K,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, then
K\leq 0
.

Remark 2.3
.

If
M
is complete, by Ekeland’s principle the set
\{u>\gamma,\ |Du|<\varepsilon\}
is non-empty for each choice of
\gamma<u^{*}
and
\varepsilon>0
. The restriction
u\in C^{1}(M)
is made necessary for such set to be open. Suitable notions of
(\mathrm{SMP}_{\infty})
for viscosity solutions that are less regular than
C^{1}
have been studied in
[
93
,
94
]
in the setting of fully nonlinear potential theory.

Remark 2.4
.

For
\chi=1
and
u\in C^{2}(M)
, then
(\mathrm{SMP}_{\infty})
can equivalently be formulated as the existence of a sequence
\{x_{j}\}\subset M
such that

u(x_{j})\rightarrow u^{*},\qquad|Du(x_{j})|<\frac{1}{j},\qquad\diver\left(\frac{Du}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}\right)(x_{j})<\frac{1}{j},

(29)

making contact with the original formulation of Omori and Yau.

The definition of
(\mathrm{WMP}_{\infty})
is localized on upper level sets
\{u>\gamma\}
of
u
, for
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
. However, with the aid of the Pasting Lemma, it can easily be seen that the following statements are equivalent (cf.
[
10
, Prop. 7.4]
):

(i)

(\mathrm{WMP}_{\infty})
holds;

(ii)

for every
f\in C(\mathbb{R})
, any non-constant solution of

\diver\left(\frac{Du}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}\right)\geq\frac{f(u)}{(1+r)^{\mu}}\frac{|Du|^{1-\chi}}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}\qquad\text{on }\,M

that is bounded from above satisfies
f(u^{*})\leq 0
.

The applications described below will motivate the study of the above maximum principles at infinity. In particular, we mention that although the
(\mathrm{WMP}_{\infty})
can be successfully used to study the rigidity of entire minimal graphs and MCF solitons, to obtain sharp results for hypersurfaces satisfying (
17
) with more general choices of
H
we had to appeal to the full strentgh of the
(\mathrm{SMP}_{\infty})
.

Remark 2.5
.

The above definition suggests that
(\mathrm{WMP}_{\infty})
can be interpreted as a comparison principle with constant functions on open, possibly unbounded sets, namely the upper level sets of
u
. We mention that the development of a comparison theory for solutions of (
23
) on unbounded sets is a rather interesting issue for which many questions are still open, even if the right-hand side does not depend on
Du
. Some sharp results in this respect can be found in
[
106
]
, see also the references therein.

Examples show that some restriction on the geometry of
M
has to be required in order to deduce the validity of
(\mathrm{WMP}_{\infty})
and
(\mathrm{SMP}_{\infty})
. Concerning the weak maximum principle, we prove in
[
10
, Thm. 7.5]
the following sharp

Theorem 2.6

(Thm. 7.5 in
[
10
]
)
.

Let
M
be a complete Riemannian manifold, and let

\chi\in[0,1],\qquad\mu\leq\chi+1.

If

\begin{array}[]{lll}\mu<\chi+1&\text{and}&\displaystyle\qquad\liminf_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log|B_{r}|}{r^{\chi+1-\mu}}<\infty\quad(=0\,\text{ if }\,\chi=0);\\[11.38092pt]
\mu=\chi+1&\text{and}&\displaystyle\qquad\liminf_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log|B_{r}|}{\log r}<\infty\quad(\leq 2\,\text{ if }\,\chi=0),\end{array}

(30)

then
(\mathrm{WMP}_{\infty})
holds. In particular, for
f\in C(\mathbb{R})
, any non-constant solution
u\in\mathrm{Lip}_{\mathrm{loc}}(M)
of

\diver\left(\frac{Du}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}\right)\geq\frac{f(u)}{(1+r)^{\mu}}\frac{|Du|^{1-\chi}}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}\qquad\text{on }\,M

that is bounded from above satisfies
f(u^{*})\leq 0
.

Concerning the strong maximum principle at infinity, it is still an open problem to decide whether the volume growth (
30
) is sufficient. In
[
10
]
, we obtained sharp conditions by imposing the next lower bound on the Ricci curvature:

\mathrm{Ric}(\nabla r,\nabla r)\geq-(m-1)\kappa^{2}\big(1+r^{2}\big)^{\alpha/2}\qquad\text{on }\,M\backslash\mathrm{cut}(o),

(31)

for constants
\kappa\geq 0,\alpha\geq-2
, where
r
is the distance from a fixed origin
o
. Namely, we compare
M
to the radially symmetric model
M_{g}
being
\mathbb{R}^{m}
endowed, in polar coordinates
(r,\theta)
, with the metric

\mathrm{d}s^{2}=\mathrm{d}r^{2}+g(r)^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta^{2},

where
\mathrm{d}\theta^{2}
is the metric on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{m-1}
and
g\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0})
is a solution of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}g^{\prime\prime}=\kappa^{2}(1+r^{2})^{\alpha/2}g\qquad\text{on }\,\mathbb{R}^{+},\\[5.69046pt]

The case
\kappa=0
corresponds to choosing the Euclidean space as model
M_{g}
, described by the function
g(r)=r
, while the case
\alpha=0
corresponds to choosing the Hyperbolic space of curvature
-\kappa^{2}
, for which

g(r)=\frac{\sinh(\kappa r)}{\kappa}.

By
[
108
, Prop. 2.11]
and the Bishop-Gromov comparison theorem, the volume of the geodesic balls

and
\mathbb{B}_{r}
in
M
and
M_{g}
, respectively, relate as follows:

\log|B_{r}|\leq\log|\mathbb{B}_{r}|\sim\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{2\kappa(m-1)}{2+\alpha}r^{1+\alpha/2}&\quad\text{if }\,\alpha>-2,\\[5.69046pt]
\big[(m-1)\bar{\kappa}+1\big]\log r&\quad\text{if }\,\alpha=-2,\end{array}\right.

(32)

as
r\rightarrow\infty
, where
\bar{\kappa}=(1+\sqrt{1+4\kappa^{2}})/2
. Therefore, the inequalities

imply any of the conditions (

\begin{array}[]{lll}\mu<\chi+1&\text{and}&\displaystyle\qquad\liminf_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log|B_{r}|}{r^{\chi+1-\mu}}<\infty\qquad\text{or}\\[11.38092pt]
\mu=\chi+1&\text{and}&\displaystyle\qquad\liminf_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log|B_{r}|}{\log r}<\infty,\end{array}

respectively if either
\alpha>-2
or
\alpha=-2
. Thus, the growth thresholds in the following (sharp) sufficient condition for

in
[

turn out to be the same as for
(\mathrm{WMP}_{\infty})
.

(Thm. 8.5 in
[
10
]
)
.

Let

(

2.6
Application: the capillarity equation

Applying Theorem

to have geodesic balls with growth of their volume faster than polynomial.

Theorem 2.29
.

Let
M
be complete. For a fixed origin
o
, denote with
r(x)=\mathrm{dist}(x,o)
and with

the geodesic ball centered at

. Let

satisfy

\kappa(x)\gtrsim\big(1+r(x)\big)^{-\mu}\qquad\text{for all }\,x\in M,

for some
\mu<2
. If there exists

such that

\liminf_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log|B_{r}|}{r^{2-\varepsilon-\mu}}<\infty,

then the only solution of the capillarity equation

\diver\left(\frac{Du}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}\right)=\kappa(x)u\qquad\text{on }\,M

is

.

Proof: sketch.

Evidently,
u\equiv 0
is the only constant solution. Assume the existence of a non-constant solution

. Up replacing

with

, we can assume that
\{u>0\}\neq\emptyset
. By assumption, there exists
\chi<1
sufficiently close to

The capillarity equation implies

\diver\left(\frac{Du_{+}}{\sqrt{1+|Du_{+}|^{2}}}\right)\gtrsim\frac{u_{+}}{(1+r)^{\mu}}\frac{|Du_{+}|^{1-\chi}}{\sqrt{1+|Du_{+}|^{2}}}\qquad\text{on }\,M,

where we used that

is bounded on

. The inclusion of the “artificial" gradient term enables the function

to satisfy the Keller-Osserman condition

, and therefore we can apply Theorem

3
Gradient estimates and Bernstein theorems in
\mathbb{R}\times M

As we saw above, under general assumptions on
b,f,\ell
solutions of

\diver\left(\frac{Du}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}\right)=b(x)f(u)\ell(|Du|)\qquad\text{on }\,M

indeed satisfy

, in particular

\diver\left(\frac{Du}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}\right)=0\qquad\text{on }\,M.

(72)

However, to infer the constancy of
u
solving (
72
), one needs rather different arguments (and more binding assumptions) than those leading to the results in the previous section; indeed, while the above theorems apply to differential
inequalities
, those in this section are very specific to the equality case, unless in the special situation when

has slow volume growth. This consideration is not surprising, as it parallels the case of harmonic functions: positive solutions of
\Delta u=0
are constant on each complete manifold with
\mathrm{Ric}\geq 0
by
[

,
30
]
, while the constancy of every positive solution of

is equivalent to the parabolicity of

(cf.
[

,

) that, for complete manifolds with

\int^{\infty}\frac{s\mathrm{d}s}{|B_{s}|}=\infty.

(73)

As a matter of fact, (

) is sufficient for the parabolicity of a complete manifold

, regardless to any curvature condition (cf.
[
71
, Thm. 7.5]
). The next example is instructive:

Example 3.1
.

Consider the warped product manifold

Let
\varphi(t)=t/\sqrt{1+t^{2}}
. Then, a direct check shows that

(since

\mathbb{R}
) and solves (

). Denoting with

the distance from an origin

\mathrm{Sec}\geq-\frac{C}{1+r^{2}},\qquad|B_{r}|\leq Cr^{m}.

for some constant

. Furthermore,

is bounded on

if and only if
m\geq 3
. Therefore, if

, assumptions (

) are not sufficient to deduce the constancy of positive solutions of (

, if the first in (

having asymptotically non-negative Sectional curvature, that is, if

and if

has only one end, then positive solutions of (

The situation for manifolds with slow volume growth, including the above example when

is a positive solution of (

, and assume that

satisfies (

\begin{array}[]{l}\text{A complete manifold with slow volume growth, in the sense of \eqref{eq_varopoulos}, satisfies \eqref{bernstein_2}:}\\

4
Splitting for capillary graphs

In the previous sections, we gave conditions to ensure that solutions of (
18

f(u)\equiv 0
, and in some cases that they are constant. In this section, we give conditions to guarantee that the existence of a non-constant
u:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
solving the prescribed mean curvature problem

\diver\left(\frac{Du}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}\right)=f(u)

(97)

forces

to split as a product
N\times\mathbb{R}
, with
u
only depending on the
\mathbb{R}
-variable. The use of solutions of PDEs to prove splitting type results is classical in Geometric Analysis, and dates back at least to the theorem of Cheeger-Gromoll
[
29
]
for manifolds with
\mathrm{Ric}\geq 0
and more than one end. In the same period, Schoen and Yau
[
119
]
investigated the topology of complete, stable minimal hypersurfaces without boundary
\Sigma^{m}\hookrightarrow\bar{M}^{m+1}
in a complete manifold with nonnegative sectional curvature, and discovered the following integral inequality for harmonic functions

:

\frac{1}{m}\int_{\Sigma}\|\mathrm{II}_{\Sigma}\|^{2}\varphi^{2}+\frac{1}{m-1}\int_{\Sigma}\big\|\nabla\|\nabla u\|\big\|^{2}\varphi^{2}\leq\int_{\Sigma}\|\nabla\varphi\|^{2}\|\nabla u\|^{2}\qquad\forall\,\varphi\in\mathrm{Lip}_{c}(\Sigma)

(98)

(see also
[
88
]
for another proof). The formula was later used by Li and Wang
[
88
]
to prove that any such hypersurface must split as a Riemannian product
N\times\mathbb{R}
, provided that it is properly immersed and has more than one end. The elegant techniques were based on the theory of harmonic functions developed in
[
84
]
, and relate to their previous splitting theorems for manifolds with
\mathrm{Ric}\geq-(m-1)\kappa^{2}
and positive spectrum
[
85
,
86
]
.

In
[
56
]
, motivated by De Giorgi and Gibbons’ conjectures for the Allen-Cahn equation

\Delta u=u^{3}-u,

Farina independently discovered an integral formula, tightly related to (
98
), valid for each quasilinear operator. He used the integral inequality to establish the 1D-symmetry of certain stable solutions
u:\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
of

\diver\left(\frac{\varphi(|Du|)}{|Du|}Du\right)=f(u).

Here, stability means the the linearized operator at
u
is non-negative, and
u
is said to have 1D-symmetry if there exists
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{m-1}
such that
u(x)=\bar{u}(\omega\cdot x)
. However, he didn’t publish the results up until 2008, in a joint paper with Sciunzi and Valdinoci
[
59
]
. For the mean curvature operator, the formula reads as follows
[
59

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\left[\frac{|D_{\top}|Du||^{2}}{(1+|Du|^{2})^{3/2}}+\frac{|A|^{2}|Du|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}\right]\varphi^{2}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\frac{|Du|^{2}|D\varphi|^{2}}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}\qquad\forall\,\varphi\in\mathrm{Lip}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{m}),

(99)

where, at a point

and

are the gradient and second fundamental form of the level set

. They deduced the next theorems:

\begin{array}[]{l}\text{if $f\in\mathrm{Lip}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R})$, stable solutions $u\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{m})$ of \eqref{eq_cap_split} have 1D-symmetry if either}\\[5.69046pt]
\begin{array}[]{l}\text{\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{fsv}{}{}, Thm.1.4]} $m=2$ and either $f\geq 0$ or $f\leq 0$ on $\mathbb{R}$;}\\[2.84544pt]

\end{array}\end{array}

For nonconstant

]
, and the survey in
[
133
]
), we could expect more rigidity than in the case of the Laplacian. However, as far as we know there is still no attempt to study the equivalent of the Berestycki-Caffarelli-Nirenberg conjecture for (
97
). We here comment on the very recent
[
36
]
, where we address this conjecture for constant
f
, proving the following splitting theorem for capillary graphs on manifolds with non-negative Ricci curvature. The result also relates to the work in progress
[
57
]
, where the authors study the problem for general
f
, with emphasis on bistable nonlinearities. To state the theorem, we recall that a manifold with boundary
(N,h)
is said to be parabolic if, for each compact subset
K\subset N
, the capacity

\mathrm{cap}(K):=\inf\left\{\int_{N}|D\varphi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\ :\ \varphi\in\mathrm{Lip}_{c}(N),\varphi\geq 1\ \text{on }\,K\right\}

vanishes. It can be checked that the condition corresponds to the recurrency of the minimal Brownian motion on
N
that is normally reflected on
\partial N
. By
[
79
]
, the parabolicity of
(N,h)
is equivalent to the validity of the following principle:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\Delta_{h}u\geq 0\quad\text{on }\,\mathrm{Int}(N)\\[5.69046pt]
\partial_{\eta}u\leq 0\quad\text{on }\,\partial N,\\[5.69046pt]
\sup_{N}u<\infty\end{array}\right.\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\sup_{N}u=\sup_{\partial N}u,

with
\eta
the outward pointing normal to
\partial N
. If
N=\overline{\Omega}
and
\Omega
is an open subset of a complete manifold
M
, by
[
79
]
a sufficient condition for
\overline{\Omega}
to be parabolic is that

\int^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{|\partial B_{s}\cap\Omega|}=\infty,

(103)

with
B_{s}
the metric ball of radius
s
in
M
centered at a fixed origin of
M
. By an application of Hölder inequality (cf.
[
110
, Prop. 1.3]
), (
103
) is implied by

\int^{\infty}\frac{s\,\mathrm{d}s}{|B_{s}\cap\Omega|}=\infty.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
(M,(\,,\,))
be a complete Riemannian manifold of dimension
m\geq 2
, and let
\Omega\subset M
be a connected, parabolic open subset with smooth boundary. Assume that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathrm{Ric}\geq-(m-1)\kappa^{2}&\quad\text{on }\,M,\\[5.69046pt]
\mathrm{Ric}\geq 0&\quad\text{on }\,\Omega.\end{array}\right.

for some constant
\kappa>0
. Split
\partial\Omega
into its connected components
\{\partial_{j}\Omega\}
,
1\leq j\leq j_{0}
, possibly
j_{0}=\infty
. Let
u\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
be a non-constant solution of the capillarity problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\diver\left(\dfrac{Du}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}\right)=mH\qquad\text{on }\,\Omega,\\[14.22636pt]
\inf_{\Omega}u>-\infty,\\[5.69046pt]
u=b_{j},\ \partial_{\eta}u=c_{j}\quad\text{on }\,\partial_{j}\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(104)

for some set of constants
H,c_{j},b_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
, with
\eta
the exterior normal to
\partial\Omega
,
\{c_{j}\}
a bounded sequence if
j_{0}=\infty
, and with the agreement
b_{1}\leq b_{2}\leq\ldots\leq b_{j_{0}}
. Assume that either

\text{$u$ is constant on $\partial\Omega$},

or that

\liminf_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log|\partial\Omega\cap B_{r}|}{r^{2}}<\infty.

(105)

If there exists a Killing vector field
X
on
\overline{\Omega}
with the following properties:

\sup_{\Omega}|X|<\infty,\qquad\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}c_{j}(X,\eta)\geq 0\quad\text{on $\partial_{j}\Omega$, for every $j$,}\\[5.69046pt]
c_{j}(X,\eta)\not\equiv 0\quad\text{on $\partial_{j}\Omega$, for some $j$,}\end{array}\right.

(106)

then:

(i)

\Omega=(0,T)\times N
with the product metric, for some
T\leq\infty
and some complete, boundaryless
N
with
\mathrm{Ric}_{N}\geq 0
.

(ii)

The product
(X,\partial_{t})
is a positive constant on
\Omega
.

(iii)

The solution
u(t,x)
only depends on the variable
t\in(0,T)
. Moreover, setting
\partial_{1}\Omega=\{0\}\times N
,

-

if
H=0
, then
c_{1}<0
and

u(t)=b_{1}-c_{1}t\qquad\text{on }\,\Omega.

(107)

-

if
H>0
, then
c_{1}\leq 0
and

u(t)=b_{1}+\frac{1}{mH}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+c_{1}^{2}}}-\sqrt{1-\left(mHt-\frac{c_{1}}{\sqrt{1+c_{1}^{2}}}\right)^{2}}\right),

(108)

in particular,

\displaystyle T<\frac{1}{mH}\left(1+\frac{c_{1}}{\sqrt{1+c_{1}^{2}}}\right)\ \ \ \text{if }\,H>0,\qquad T\leq\frac{1}{m|H|}\frac{|c_{1}|}{\sqrt{1+c_{1}^{2}}}\ \ \ \text{if }\,H<0.

Furthermore, if
u
is constant on the entire
\partial\Omega
, then the requirement (
105
) can be omitted and the case
H=0
in (iii) occurs.

Remark 4.3
.

Evidently, if
T<\infty
then (
107
), (
108
) relate the constants
b_{2},c_{2}
describing the boundary data on
\partial_{2}\Omega=\{T\}\times N
to
b_{1},c_{1}
.

We stress that
X
is just required to satisfy a mild relation, to make it coherent with the Neumann data: precisely,
X
shall point outwards on components
\partial_{j}\Omega
for which the outward pointing normal derivative of
u
is positive (i.e.,
c_{j}>0
), leaving the possibility that
X
be tangent to
\partial_{j}\Omega
, while
X
shall point inwards if
c_{j}<0
. No condition is imposed on components of
\partial\Omega
where
c_{j}=0
. However, to avoid a degenerate case, we require that
X
is not tangent to the entire portion of
\partial\Omega
where
\partial_{\eta}u\neq 0
.

To conclude, we briefly describe the proof of Theorem
4.2
. The argument follows the lines of
[
58
, Thm.5]
, with some notable improvements depending on the gradient estimate in Theorem
3.4
, and can be divided into the following steps.

-

We prove the monotonicity of
u
along the flow lines of
X
, namely, that
\bar{v}:=(Du,X)
is positive on
\Omega
. To this aim, we first observe that
\mathscr{L}_{g}\bar{v}=0
on
\Omega
, with
\mathscr{L}_{g}
the weighted operator in (
82
). The boundedness of
|X|
and the gradient estimate (
94
), applied with
\kappa=0
, imply
\inf_{\Omega}\bar{v}>-\infty
, while
\bar{v}\geq 0,\not\equiv 0
on
\partial\Omega
because of our boundary condition (
106
). Next, because of the boundedness of
|Du|
and the parabolicity of
(\overline{\Omega},\sigma)
, the weighted operator
\mathscr{L}_{g}
is parabolic on
\Sigma=(\overline{\Omega},g)
(cf.
[
79
]
, and
[
3
,
107
]
), and therefore

\inf_{\Omega}\bar{v}\equiv\inf_{\partial\Omega}\bar{v}\geq 0.

The conclusion
\bar{v}>0
follows from the strong maximum principle. In particular,
\mathrm{d}u\neq 0
on
\Omega
.

-

We show the validity of the following integral formula on
\Sigma
: for every
\varphi\in\mathrm{Lip}_{c}(\overline{\Sigma})
,

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\left[W^{2}\left(\|\nabla_{\top}\|\nabla u\|\|^{2}+\|\nabla u\|^{2}\|A\|^{2}\right)+\frac{\mathrm{Ric}(Du,Du)}{1+|Du|^{2}}\right]\varphi^{2}\\[14.22636pt]
\qquad\displaystyle+\int_{\Sigma}\frac{\bar{v}^{2}}{W^{2}}\left\|\nabla\left(\frac{\varphi\|\nabla u\|W}{\bar{v}}\right)\right\|^{2}\leq\int_{\Sigma}\|\nabla u\|^{2}\|\nabla\varphi\|^{2}\end{array}

(109)

where, at a point
x
such that
\mathrm{d}u(x)\neq 0
,
\nabla_{\top}
and
A
are the gradient and second fundamental form of the level set
\{u=u(x)\}
in
\Sigma
, and integration is with respect to the volume measure of
\Sigma
. Inequality (
109
) is tightly related to (
98
) and (
102
); in particular, the “magic cancellation" of the boundary terms in (
102
) still holds for (
104
). As shown in
[
57
]
, in fact, overdetermined problems associated to a large class of quasilinear operators lead to integral formulas like (
109
).

-

We use again the gradient bound
|Du|\leq C
to deduce that

\int_{\Sigma}\|\nabla u\|^{2}\|\nabla\varphi\|^{2}\leq\int_{\Omega}W|D\varphi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq C^{\prime}\int_{\Omega}|D\varphi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x,

for some constant
C^{\prime}
. Therefore, the parabolicity of
(\overline{\Omega},\sigma)
enables to deduce the existence of a sequence
\varphi_{j}\uparrow 1
such that

\int_{\Omega}|D\varphi_{j}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\rightarrow 0,

so taking limits we deduce

\|\nabla_{\top}\|\nabla u\|\|^{2}+\|\nabla u\|^{2}\|A\|^{2}+\left\|\nabla\left(\frac{\|\nabla u\|W}{\bar{v}}\right)\right\|^{2}\equiv 0\quad\text{on }\,\Omega.

By adapting
[
58
]
, the first two conditions guarantee that
\Omega
splits as a Riemannian product along the flow lines of
\nabla u
(everywhere nonvanishing on
\Omega
), and that
u
only depends on the split direction. Also, the constancy of
\|\nabla u\|W/\bar{v}
rewrites as the constancy of
(X,\partial_{t})
on
\Omega
.

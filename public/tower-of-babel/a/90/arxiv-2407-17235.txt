\displaystyle-2L_{\alpha+\beta}\delta^{AB}-\left(\alpha-\beta\right)T^{AB}.

(34)

Here, we present some of the most notable relations.

•

\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
:

For
\mathcal{N}=1
, the generators
T^{AB}
are no longer available, and one can drop the
\mathfrak{so}(\mathcal{N})
indices. Therefore, the
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
algebra is given by:

\displaystyle[L_{I},L_{J}]

\displaystyle=(I-J)L_{I+J},\quad[L_{I},G_{\alpha}]=\left(\frac{I}{2}-\alpha\right)G_{I+\alpha},\quad\{G_{\alpha},G_{\beta}\}=-2L_{\alpha+\beta}.

(35)

•

\mathfrak{osp}(2|2)
:

For
\mathcal{N}=2
, due to the antisymmetry of the
\mathfrak{so}(2)
generators, we can write
T^{AB}=\epsilon^{AB}{\tt J}
. Therefore, the algebra reduces to the
\mathfrak{osp}(2|2)
superalgebra. We denote the bosonic generators as
{\tt J}
,
{\tt L}_{I}

(I=\pm 1,0)
, and the fermionic ones as
{\tt G}_{\alpha}^{A}

(\alpha=\pm\frac{1}{2},A=1,2)
. Their commutation relations read:

\displaystyle[L_{I},L_{J}]

\displaystyle=(I-J)L_{I+J},\quad[L_{I},G_{\alpha}^{A}]=\left(\frac{I}{2}-\alpha\right)G_{I+\alpha}^{A},

(36)

\displaystyle[G_{\alpha}^{A},{\tt J}]

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{AB}G_{\alpha}^{B},\quad\{G_{\alpha}^{A},G_{\beta}^{B}\}=-2L_{\alpha+\beta}\delta^{AB}-(\alpha-\beta)\epsilon^{AB}{\tt J}.

(37)

•

\mathfrak{osp}(3|2)
:

For
\mathcal{N}=3
, owing to the antisymmetry of the
\mathfrak{so}(3)
generators, we can express
T^{ABC}=\epsilon^{ABC}{\tt J}^{C}
. Consequently, the algebra reduces to the
\mathfrak{osp}(3|2)
superalgebra.
We denote the bosonic generators by
{\tt J}^{A}
(
A=1,2,3
),
{\tt L}_{I}
(
I=\pm 1,0
), and the fermionic generators by
{\tt G}_{\alpha}^{A}
(
\alpha=\pm\frac{1}{2}
,
A=1,2,3
). Their commutation relations are given by

\displaystyle\left[{\tt L}_{I},{\tt L}_{J}\right]

\displaystyle=\left(I-J\right){\tt L}_{I+J},

(38)

\displaystyle\left[{\tt L}_{I},{\tt G}_{\alpha}^{A}\right]

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{I}{2}-\alpha\right){\tt G}_{I+\alpha}^{A},

(39)

\displaystyle\left[{\tt J}^{A},{\tt J}^{B}\right]

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{ABC}{\tt J}^{C},

(40)

\displaystyle\left[{\tt J}^{A},{\tt G}_{\alpha}^{B}\right]

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{ABC}{\tt G}_{\alpha}^{C},

(41)

\displaystyle\left\{{\tt G}_{\alpha}^{A},{\tt G}_{\beta}^{B}\right\}

\displaystyle=2\,\delta^{AB}{\tt L}_{\alpha+\beta}

\displaystyle-\left(\alpha-\beta\right)\delta_{\alpha,-\beta}\epsilon^{ABC}{\tt J}^{C},

(42)

except for zero commutators.

It is important to note that there are variants with different spin contents, referred to as the
\mathfrak{osp}(3|2)
superalgebra in the literature
[
55
]
. Specifically, a copy of the
\mathcal{N}=2
superalgebra arises when the even-graded sector of the superalgebra is decomposed into a spin-2 component, the
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
generators
{\tt L}_{i}
(
i=\pm 1,0
), a spin-1 element
{\tt J}
, a spin-2 multiplet
{\tt A}_{i}
(
i=\pm 1,0
), and a spin-3 multiplet
{\tt W}_{i}
(
i=\pm 2,\pm 1,0
). All these generators together span the bosonic sub-algebra
\mathfrak{sl}(3)\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1)
. Furthermore, the odd-graded elements decompose into two spin-
\frac{3}{2}
multiplets
{\tt G}_{r}^{M}
(
r=\pm\frac{1}{2}
,
M=\pm
) and two spin-
\frac{5}{2}
multiplets
{\tt S}_{r}^{M}
(
r=\pm\frac{3}{2},\pm\frac{1}{2}
,
M=\pm
).

•

\mathfrak{osp}(4|2)
:

For the case
\mathcal{N}=4
where
\mathfrak{so}(4)\simeq\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)
, special treatment is required. We denote the bosonic generators as
{\tt J}^{A}

(A=1,2,3)
,
{\tt L}_{I}

(I=\pm 1,0)
, and the fermionic generators as
{\tt G}_{\alpha}^{a}
,
\bar{{\tt G}}_{\alpha}^{a}

(\alpha=\pm\frac{1}{2},\,a=1,2)
. Their commutation relations are given by

\displaystyle[{\tt L}_{I},{\tt L}_{J}]

\displaystyle=(I-J){\tt L}_{I+J},

(43)

\displaystyle[{\tt L}_{I},{\tt G}_{\alpha}^{a}]

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{I}{2}-\alpha\right){\tt G}_{I+\alpha}^{a},

(44)

\displaystyle[{\tt L}_{I},\bar{{\tt G}}_{\alpha}^{a}]

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{I}{2}-\alpha\right)\bar{{\tt G}}_{I+\alpha}^{a},

(45)

\displaystyle[{\tt J}^{A},{\tt J}^{B}]

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{ABC}{\tt J}^{C},

(46)

\displaystyle[{\tt J}^{A},{\tt G}_{\alpha}^{a}]

\displaystyle=(\sigma^{A})_{ab}{\tt G}_{\alpha}^{b},

(47)

\displaystyle[{\tt J}^{A},\bar{{\tt G}}_{\alpha}^{a}]

\displaystyle=(\sigma^{A})^{*}_{ab}\bar{{\tt G}}_{\alpha}^{b},

(48)

\displaystyle\{{\tt G}_{\alpha}^{a},\bar{{\tt G}}_{\beta}^{b}\}

\displaystyle=2\delta^{ab}{\tt L}_{\alpha+\beta}

\displaystyle-(\alpha-\beta)\delta_{\alpha,-\beta}(\sigma^{c})_{ab}{\tt J}^{c},

(49)

except for zero commutators. Here,
\epsilon^{ijk}
represents the structure constants of
\mathfrak{su}(2)
, and
\sigma^{i}
denotes the Pauli matrices.

The equations of motion for Chern-Simons theory, often referred to as the flatness conditions, correspond to the vanishing of the field strengths, i.e.,
F=\bar{F}=0
, where

F=\mathrm{d}\Gamma+\Gamma\wedge\Gamma=0,\quad\bar{F}=\mathrm{d}\bar{\Gamma}+\bar{\Gamma}\wedge\bar{\Gamma}=0.

(50)

This condition is equivalent to Einstein’s equations. The connection to Einstein’s equations is established by expressing Lie algebra-valued generalizations of the vielbein and spin connection in terms of the gauge connections. Consequently, the metric
g_{\mu\nu}
can be obtained from the vielbein
e=\frac{\ell}{2}(\Gamma-\bar{\Gamma})
as follows:

g_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\,\mathfrak{str}(e_{\mu}e_{\nu}).

(51)

By adopting the radial gauge, asymptotically
AdS_{3}
connections can be represented as

\displaystyle\Gamma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b^{-1}a\left(t,\phi\right)b+b^{-1}\mathrm{d}b,\quad\bar{\Gamma}=b\bar{a}\left(t,\phi\right)b^{-1}+b\mathrm{d}b^{-1},

(52)

where the state-independent group element
b(\rho)
is given by

\displaystyle b(\rho)=e^{{\tt L}_{-1}}e^{\rho{\tt L}_{0}},

(53)

enabling a more general metric that encompasses all
\mathfrak{osp}(\mathcal{N}|2)
charges. It is crucial to note that, as long as
\delta b=0
, the choice of
b
is irrelevant for asymptotic symmetries. This freedom allows for a more general metric, and it is essential to choose boundary conditions that preserve this generality for supergravity.

Furthermore, in the radial gauge, the connections
a\left(t,\phi\right)
and
\bar{a}\left(t,\phi\right)
are
\mathfrak{osp}(3|2)
Lie superalgebra-valued fields that are independent of the radial coordinate:

\displaystyle a\left(t,\varphi\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{t}\left(t,\varphi\right)\mathrm{d}t+a_{\varphi}\left(t,\varphi\right)\mathrm{d}\varphi,

(54)

\displaystyle\bar{a}\left(t,\varphi\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{a}_{t}\left(t,\varphi\right)\mathrm{d}t+\bar{a}_{\varphi}\left(t,\varphi\right)\mathrm{d}\varphi.

(55)

Hereafter, we focus only on the unbarred sector since the analysis of the barred sector works in complete analogy, yielding the same outcomes. The same algorithm can be applied to the barred sector due to the procedure used.

4
\mathcal{N}=(3,3)

\mathfrak{osp}(3|2)\oplus\mathfrak{osp}(3|2)
Higher-Spin Chern-Simons Supergravity

We initiate the study of asymptotically
AdS_{3}
boundary conditions for
\mathfrak{osp}(3|2)\oplus\mathfrak{osp}(3|2)
Chern-Simons theory in the affine case. We illustrate how the procedure outlined in
[
48
]
can be employed to assess the asymptotic symmetry algebra. Based on the obtained results, the most general solution of Einstein’s equations that is asymptotically
AdS_{3}
can be expressed using the following general metric form:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}s^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\rho^{2}+2\left[e^{\rho}N^{(0)}_{i}+N^{(1)}_{i}+e^{-\rho}N^{(2)}_{i}+\mathcal{O}\left(e^{-2\rho}\right)\right]\mathrm{d}\rho\mathrm{d}x^{i}

(56)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left[e^{2\rho}g^{(0)}_{ij}+e^{\rho}g^{(1)}_{ij}+g^{(2)}_{ij}+\mathcal{O}\left(e^{-\rho}\right)\right]\mathrm{d}x^{i}\mathrm{d}x^{j}.

Therefore, it is crucial to define the most general
\mathcal{N}=(3,3)
supergravity boundary conditions that preserve this form of the metric.

4.1
Affine Boundary Conditions

The objective of this section is to construct
\mathcal{N}=(3,3)
extended higher-spin
AdS_{3}
supergravity as
\mathfrak{osp}(3|2)\oplus\mathfrak{osp}(3|2)
Chern-Simons gauge theory on the affine boundary. We proceed with our calculations to elucidate the asymptotic symmetry algebra for the loosest set of boundary conditions. Therefore, we consider the principal embedding of
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
into
\mathfrak{osp}(3|2)
as a sub-algebra, giving rise to the asymptotic symmetry.

We start by proposing the
\mathfrak{osp}(3|2)
Lie superalgebra valued
a_{\varphi}
component of the gauge connection in the form:

\displaystyle a_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{A}\mathcal{J}^{A}{\tt J}^{A}+\gamma_{i}\mathcal{L}^{i}{\tt L}_{i}+\sigma_{M}^{p}\mathcal{G}_{A}^{p}{\tt G}_{p}^{A}

(57)

where
\rho_{A}=\frac{2}{k}
,
\gamma_{0}=-2\gamma_{\pm 1}=-\frac{4}{k}
, and
\sigma_{A}^{-\frac{1}{2}}=-\sigma_{A}^{\frac{1}{2}}=-\frac{1}{k}
are scaling parameters to be identified later. We have twelve state-dependent functions consisting of six bosonic
\left(\mathcal{J}^{A},\mathcal{L}^{i}\right)
and six fermionic
\mathcal{G}_{A}^{p}
, usually called charges. The time component
a_{t}
of the connection
a\left(t,\varphi\right)
can be given as:

\displaystyle a_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta_{A}{\tt J}^{A}+\mu^{i}{\tt L}_{i}+\nu_{A}^{p}{\tt G}_{p}^{A}.

(58)

In this case, we have twelve independent functions
(\eta_{A},\mathcal{\mu}^{i},\nu_{A}^{p})
, as chemical potentials, which are not allowed to vary,
\delta a_{t}=0
.

Using the flatness conditions
(\ref{flatness})
, the equations of motion for fixed chemical potentials impose the following additional conditions on the charges
(\mathcal{J}^{A},\mathcal{L}^{i},\mathcal{G}_{A}^{p})
:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathcal{J}^{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{k}{2}\partial_{\varphi}\eta_{A}+\epsilon^{ABC}(\mathcal{J}^{B}\eta_{C}-\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{G}_{A}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nu_{C}^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\mathcal{G}_{A}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nu_{C}^{\frac{1}{2}})

(59)

\displaystyle 2\partial_{t}\mathcal{L}^{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{k}{4}\partial_{\varphi}\mu^{0}-\mathcal{L}^{+1}\mu^{-1}+\mathcal{L}^{-1}\mu^{+1}-\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{G}_{A}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nu_{A}^{-\frac{1}{2}}-\mathcal{G}_{A}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nu_{A}^{\frac{1}{2}})

(60)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathcal{L}^{\pm 1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{k}{2}\partial_{\varphi}\mu^{\mp 1}+\mathcal{L}^{0}\mu^{\mp 1}\mp\mathcal{L}^{\mp 1}\mu^{0}\mp\mathcal{G}_{A}^{\mp\frac{1}{2}}\nu_{A}^{\mp\frac{1}{2}},

(61)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathcal{G}_{A}^{\mp\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mp k\partial_{\varphi}\nu_{A}^{\mp\frac{1}{2}}\mp\frac{1}{2}\mu^{0}\mathcal{G}_{A}^{\mp\frac{1}{2}}\mp\mu^{-1}\mathcal{G}_{A}^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}+2\mathcal{L}^{0}\nu_{A}^{\mp\frac{1}{2}}+2\mathcal{L}^{\mp}\nu_{A}^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}

(62)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\epsilon^{ABC}(\eta_{{}_{B}}\mathcal{G}_{C}^{\mp\frac{1}{2}}+2\mathcal{J}^{B}\nu_{C}^{\mp\frac{1}{2}})

(63)

representing the temporal evolution of the eight state-dependent source fields.

We aim to derive the asymptotic symmetry algebra for the most general boundary conditions through canonical analysis. To achieve this, we consider all gauge transformations given by

\delta_{\lambda}\Gamma=\mathrm{d}\lambda+\left[\Gamma,\lambda\right],

(64)

which preserve the most general boundary conditions. At this stage, it is appropriate to express the gauge parameter in terms of the
\mathfrak{osp}(3|2)
Lie superalgebra basis:

\lambda=b^{-1}\bigg[\varrho_{A}{\tt J}^{A}+\epsilon^{i}{\tt L}_{i}+\zeta_{A}^{p}{\tt G}_{p}^{A}\bigg]b.

(65)

Note that the gauge parameter includes four bosonic variables
\varrho_{A}
,
\epsilon^{i}
, and four fermionic variables
\zeta_{M}^{p}
, which are arbitrary functions of boundary coordinates.

Now, let’s focus on the gauge parameters satisfying (
64
). The infinitesimal gauge transformations are determined as follows:

\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda}\mathcal{J}^{A}

\displaystyle=\frac{k}{2}\partial_{\varphi}\varrho_{A}+\epsilon^{ABC}(\mathcal{J}^{B}\varrho_{C}-\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{G}_{A}^{\frac{1}{2}}\zeta_{C}^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\mathcal{G}_{A}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\zeta_{C}^{\frac{1}{2}})),

(66)

\displaystyle 2\delta_{\lambda}\mathcal{L}^{0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{k}{4}\partial_{\varphi}\epsilon^{0}-\mathcal{L}^{+1}\epsilon^{-1}+\mathcal{L}^{-1}\epsilon^{+1}-\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{G}_{A}^{\frac{1}{2}}\zeta_{A}^{-\frac{1}{2}}-\mathcal{G}_{A}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\zeta_{A}^{\frac{1}{2}}),

(67)

\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda}\mathcal{L}^{\pm 1}

\displaystyle=\frac{k}{2}\partial_{\varphi}\epsilon^{\mp 1}+\mathcal{L}^{0}\epsilon^{\mp 1}\mp\mathcal{L}^{\mp 1}\epsilon^{0}\mp\mathcal{G}_{A}^{\mp\frac{1}{2}}\zeta_{A}^{\mp\frac{1}{2}},

(68)

\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda}\mathcal{G}_{A}^{\mp\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\mp k\partial_{\varphi}\zeta_{A}^{\mp\frac{1}{2}}\mp\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{0}\mathcal{G}_{A}^{\mp\frac{1}{2}}\mp\epsilon^{-1}\mathcal{G}_{A}^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}+2\mathcal{L}^{0}\zeta_{A}^{\mp\frac{1}{2}}+2\mathcal{L}^{\mp}\zeta_{A}^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle-\epsilon^{ABC}(\varrho_{{}_{B}}\mathcal{G}_{C}^{\mp\frac{1}{2}}+2\mathcal{J}^{B}\zeta_{C}^{\mp\frac{1}{2}}).

(69)

Constraints for the chemical potentials can also be derived analogously:

\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda}\eta^{A}

\displaystyle=\frac{k}{2}\partial_{t}\varrho_{A}+\epsilon^{ABC}(\eta^{B}\varrho_{C}-\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{G}_{A}^{\frac{1}{2}}\zeta_{C}^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\mathcal{G}_{A}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\zeta_{C}^{\frac{1}{2}})),

(70)

\displaystyle 2\delta_{\lambda}\mu^{0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{k}{4}\partial_{t}\epsilon^{0}-\mu^{+1}\epsilon^{-1}+\mu^{-1}\epsilon^{+1}-\frac{1}{2}(\nu_{A}^{\frac{1}{2}}\zeta_{A}^{-\frac{1}{2}}-\nu_{A}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\zeta_{A}^{\frac{1}{2}}),

(71)

\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda}\mu^{\pm 1}

\displaystyle=\frac{k}{2}\partial_{t}\epsilon^{\mp 1}+\mu^{0}\epsilon^{\mp 1}\mp\mu^{\mp 1}\epsilon^{0}\mp\nu_{A}^{\mp\frac{1}{2}}\zeta_{A}^{\mp\frac{1}{2}},

(72)

\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda}\nu_{A}^{\mp\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\mp k\partial_{t}\zeta_{A}^{\mp\frac{1}{2}}\mp\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{0}\nu_{A}^{\mp\frac{1}{2}}\mp\epsilon^{-1}\nu_{A}^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}+2\mu^{0}\zeta_{A}^{\mp\frac{1}{2}}+2\mu^{\mp}\zeta_{A}^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle-\epsilon^{ABC}(\varrho_{{}_{B}}\nu_{C}^{\mp\frac{1}{2}}+2\eta^{B}\zeta_{C}^{\mp\frac{1}{2}}).

(73)

According to the boundary conditions, given in equations (
50
) - (
54
), the left-hand side of these equations
(\ref{chemicalpotvar1})
-
(\ref{chemicalpotvar2})
equals zero.
As a final step, the canonical boundary charge
\mathcal{Q[\lambda]}
that generates the transformations (
66
) - (
69
) can be defined. The variation of the canonical boundary charge
\mathcal{Q[\lambda]}

[
43
]
leading to the asymptotic symmetry algebra is given by

\delta_{\lambda}\mathcal{Q}=\frac{k}{2\pi}\int\mathrm{d}\varphi\;\mathfrak{str}\left(\lambda\delta\Gamma_{\varphi}\right).

(74)

Therefore, the variation of the canonical boundary charge
\mathcal{Q[\lambda]}
can be functionally integrated to yield

\mathcal{Q[\lambda]}=\int\mathrm{d}\varphi\;\left[\mathcal{J}^{A}\varrho_{A}+\mathcal{L}^{i}\epsilon^{-i}+\mathcal{G}_{A}^{p}\zeta_{A}^{-p}\right].

(75)

After determining both the infinitesimal transformations and the canonical boundary charge, we are now positioned to derive the asymptotic symmetry algebra using the standard method
[
65
]
. This can be obtained through the following relation:

\delta_{\lambda}\digamma=\{\digamma,\mathcal{Q}[\lambda]\}

(76)

for any phase space functional
\digamma
. The charges
\mathcal{L}^{i}(z)
,
\mathcal{J}^{A}(z)
, and
\mathcal{G}_{M}^{p}(z)
are identified as the generators of the asymptotic symmetry algebra. Finally, the operator product algebra can be expressed as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{i}(z_{1})\mathcal{L}^{j}(z_{2})

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{k}{2}\eta^{ij}}{z_{12}^{2}}+\frac{(i-j)}{z_{12}}\mathcal{L}^{i+j}(z_{2}),

(77)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{i}(z_{1})\mathcal{G}_{A}^{p}(z_{2})

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{({i\over 2}-p)}{z_{12}}\mathcal{G}_{A}^{i+p}(z_{2}),

(78)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{A}(z_{1})\mathcal{J}^{B}(z_{2})

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{k}{2}\eta}{z_{12}^{2}}\delta^{AB}+\frac{\epsilon^{ABC}}{z_{12}}\mathcal{J}^{C}(z_{2}),

(79)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{A}(z_{1})\mathcal{G}_{B}^{p}(z_{2})

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon^{ABC}}{z_{12}}\mathcal{G}_{C}^{p}(z_{2}),

(80)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{A}^{p}(z_{1})\mathcal{G}_{B}^{q}(z_{2})

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{k}{2}}{z_{12}^{2}}\eta_{AB}^{pq}+\frac{2}{z_{12}}\delta^{AB}\mathcal{L}^{p+q}(z_{2})+\delta_{p,-q}(p-q)\frac{\epsilon^{ABC}}{z_{12}}\mathcal{J}^{C}(z_{2}).

(81)

Here
\eta^{ij}=\mathfrak{str}({\tt L}_{i}{\tt L}_{j})
, and
\eta^{pq}=\mathfrak{str}({\tt G}_{p}^{A}{\tt G}_{q}^{A})
are the bilinear forms in the fundamental representation of the
\mathfrak{osp}(3|2)
Lie superalgebra. The operator product algebra can also be expressed in a more compact form:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{\mathcal{\mathfrak{J}}}^{A}(z_{1})\mathfrak{\mathcal{\mathfrak{J}}}^{B}(z_{2})

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{k}{2}\eta^{AB}}{z_{12}^{2}}+\frac{\mathfrak{\mathcal{\mathfrak{f}}}^{AB}_{~~~C}\mathfrak{\mathcal{\mathfrak{J}}}^{C}(z_{2})}{z_{12}}.

(82)

Note that
\eta^{AB}
is the supertrace matrix, and
\mathfrak{\mathcal{\mathfrak{f}}}^{AB}_{~~C}
are the structure constants of the related algebra with
(A,B=0,\pm 1,\pm\frac{1}{2})
, i.e.,
\eta^{ip}=0
and
\mathfrak{\mathcal{\mathfrak{f}}}^{ij}_{~~i+j}=(i-j)
. Lastly, by repeating the same analysis for the barred sector, the asymptotic symmetry algebra of
\mathcal{N}=(3,3)
supergravity for the loosest set of boundary conditions is given by two copies of the affine
\mathfrak{osp}(3|2)_{k}
algebra.

4.2
Superconformal Boundary Conditions

In this section, our objective is to examine the asymptotic symmetry algebra for the supersymmetric extension of the Brown-Henneaux boundary conditions. We commence by imposing the Drinfeld-Sokolov highest weight gauge condition on the
\mathfrak{osp}(3|2)
Lie superalgebra-valued connection (
57
) to further constrain the coefficients. Thus, the Drinfeld-Sokolov reduction sets the fields as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{0}=\mathcal{G}_{A}^{+\frac{1}{2}}=0,\quad\mathcal{L}^{-1}=\mathcal{L},\quad\mathcal{G}_{A}^{-\frac{1}{2}}=\mathcal{G}_{A},\quad\gamma_{+1}\mathcal{L}^{+1}=1.

(83)

It is noteworthy that the superconformal boundary conditions are the supersymmetric extension of the well-known Brown-Henneaux boundary conditions proposed in
[
25
]
for
AdS_{3}
supergravity. Consequently, the supersymmetric gauge connection takes the form:

\displaystyle a_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\tt L}_{+1}+\gamma_{-1}\mathcal{L}{\tt L}_{-1}+\rho_{A}\mathcal{J}^{A}{\tt J}^{A}+\sigma_{A}^{-{\frac{1}{2}}}\mathcal{G}_{A}{\tt G}_{-{\frac{1}{2}}}^{A}.

(84)

Here,
\gamma_{-1}=-\frac{1}{k}
,
\rho=\frac{1}{2k}
,
\sigma_{+}^{-{\frac{1}{2}}}=\frac{1}{2k}
, and
\sigma_{-}^{-{\frac{1}{2}}}=-\frac{1}{2k}
are scaling parameters. We introduce four functions: two bosonic (
\mathcal{J},\mathcal{L}
) and two fermionic (
\mathcal{G}_{\pm}
) charges. After implementing these steps, we are on the verge of obtaining the superconformal asymptotic symmetry algebra. Following the results implied by the Drinfeld-Sokolov reduction, the gauge parameter
\lambda
has only four independent functions
(\varrho_{A},\mathcal{\epsilon}\equiv\epsilon^{+1},\,\zeta_{A}\equiv\zeta_{A}^{+\frac{1}{2}})
, given by:

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b^{-1}\left[\epsilon{\tt L}_{1}-\epsilon^{\prime}{\tt L}_{0}+\Bigg(\frac{6\mathcal{L}\epsilon}{c}+\frac{3\mathcal{G}_{A}\zeta_{A}}{c}+\frac{\epsilon^{\prime\prime}}{2}\Bigg){\tt L}_{-1}\right.

(85)

\displaystyle\left.+\varrho_{A}{\tt J}^{A}+\zeta_{A}{\tt G}_{\frac{1}{2}}^{A}+\left(\frac{3\epsilon^{ABC}\mathcal{J}^{B}\zeta_{C}}{c}-\frac{3\mathcal{G}_{A}\epsilon}{c}-\zeta_{A}^{\prime}\right){\tt G}_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{A}\right]b.

Substituting this gauge parameter into the expression for the transformation of the fields (
64
), we obtain the infinitesimal gauge transformations:

\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda}\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c}{12}\epsilon^{{}^{\prime\prime\prime}}+\epsilon\mathcal{L}^{\prime}+2\mathcal{L}\epsilon^{\prime}+\frac{3}{2}\mathcal{G}_{A}\zeta_{A}^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\zeta_{A}\mathcal{G}_{A}^{\prime}+\frac{3\epsilon^{ABC}\zeta_{A}\mathcal{J}^{B}\mathcal{G}_{C}}{c}

(86)

\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda}\mathcal{J}^{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{c}{3}\varrho_{A}^{\prime}+\epsilon^{ABC}\bigg(\mathcal{J}^{B}\varrho_{C}+\mathcal{G}_{B}\zeta_{C}\bigg)

(87)

\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda}\mathcal{G}^{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c}{3}\zeta_{A}^{\prime\prime}+2\bigg(\mathcal{L}-\frac{3}{2c}\mathcal{J}^{B}\mathcal{J}^{B}\bigg)\zeta_{A}+\frac{3\mathcal{J}^{A}\mathcal{J}^{B}\zeta_{B}}{c}+\frac{3\mathcal{G}_{A}\epsilon^{\prime}}{2}+\epsilon\mathcal{G}_{A}^{\prime}

(88)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\epsilon^{ABC}\Bigg(\bigg(\varrho_{C}+\frac{3}{c}\epsilon\mathcal{J}^{C}\bigg)\mathcal{G}_{B}+\zeta_{B}{\mathcal{J}^{C}}^{\prime}-2\mathcal{J}^{B}\zeta_{C}^{\prime}\Bigg)

For the given boundary conditions, the boundary charge
\mathcal{Q}[\lambda]
is integrable, and we identify
\mathcal{L}
,
\mathcal{G}^{A}
, and
\mathcal{J}^{A}
as charges. However, these charges do not generate an algebra due to some nonlinear terms
(\mathcal{J}^{B}\mathcal{G}_{C})
in
(\ref{ebc086})
. Moreover, to obtain the correct superconformal algebra
(\ref{qalg11})
-
(\ref{qalg12})
, we need to perform a shift on
\mathcal{L}
and a redefinition of the gauge parameter
\varrho_{A}
as:

\mathcal{L}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}+\frac{3}{2c}\mathcal{J}^{A}\mathcal{J}^{A},\quad\varrho_{A}\rightarrow\varrho_{A}-\frac{3}{c}\epsilon\mathcal{J}^{A}.

(89)

The new gauge parameter
\lambda
is given as:

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle=

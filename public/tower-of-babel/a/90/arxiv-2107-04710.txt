Cartan Subalgebras in von Neumann Algebras Associated with Graph Product Groups
Ionuţ Chifan, Srivatsav Kunnawalkam Elayavalli

Abstract
In this short note we classify the Cartan subalgebras in all von Neumann algebras associated with graph product groups and their free ergodic measure preserving actions on probability spaces.



1
Introduction

Murray and von Neumann found a natural way to associate a crossed-product von Neumann algebra, denoted by
L^{\infty}(X)\rtimes\Gamma
, to any measure preserving action
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
of a countable group
\Gamma
on a probability space
X

X
consists of a singleton this amounts to the group von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{L}(\Gamma)

[
MvN43
]
. The most interesting cases for study is when
L^{\infty}(X)\rtimes\Gamma
and
\mathcal{L}(\Gamma)
are II
1
factors which correspond to the situations when
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
is a free, ergodic action and
\Gamma
is an icc group, respectively.

An important open problem regarding the structure of these algebras is to classify their Cartan subalgebras

To properly introduce our results we briefly recall the graph product group construction. Let
\mathscr{G}=(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{E})
be a finite, simple (no self loops or multiple edges) graph, where
\mathscr{V}
and
\mathscr{E}
denote its vertices and edges sets, respectively. Let
\{\Gamma_{v}\}_{v\in\mathscr{V}}
be a family of groups called vertex groups. The graph product group associated with this data, denoted by
\mathscr{G}\{\Gamma_{v}\}
, is the group generated by
\Gamma_{v}
,
v\in\mathscr{V}
subject to the relations of the groups
\Gamma_{v}
along with the relations
[\Gamma_{u},\Gamma_{v}]=1
, whenever
(u,v)\in\mathscr{E}
. Developing an approach that combines deep results in Popa’s deformation/rigidity theory with various geometric/algebraic aspects of graph product groups we are able to classify the Cartan subalgebras for large classes of von Neumann algebras associated with graph product groups. Specifically, using the classification of normalizers of amenable subalgebras in amalgamated free product von Neumann algebras due to Ioana
[
Io12
]
(see also
[

]
) in cooperation with the transitivity of intertwining virtual Cartan subalgebras from
[

]
and the structure of the parabolic subgroups of a graph product groups
[
AM10
]
we have the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Gamma=\mathscr{G}\{\Gamma_{v}\}
be an icc graph product of groups where
\mathscr{G}
does not admit a de Rham join decomposition of the form
\mathscr{G}=\mathscr{G}_{1}\circ\mathscr{G}_{2}\circ\cdots\circ\mathscr{G}_{k}
so that for all
2\leq i\leq k
we have
\mathscr{G}_{i}=(\{v_{i},w_{i}\},\emptyset)
and
\Gamma_{v_{i}}=\Gamma_{w_{i}}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. Then
\mathcal{L}(\Gamma)
has no Cartan subalgebra.

In particular, when the graph product does not contain elements of order two (i.e. non-Coxeter type) we get a result which complements the results from
[
Ca16
]
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
\mathscr{G}=(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{E})
be any finite simple graph such that there exist
v,w\in\mathscr{V}
satisfying
(v,w)\notin\mathscr{E}
. Assume that
\Gamma=\mathscr{G}(\Gamma_{v})
is any icc graph product group where
|\Gamma_{v}|\geq 3
for all
v\in\mathscr{V}
. Then
\mathcal{L}(\Gamma)
has no Cartan subalgebra.

In their groundbreaking work
[
PV11
]
, Popa and Vaes discovered the first examples of group
\Gamma
that are
\mathcal{C}
-rigid. This means that any free ergodic pmp action
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
gives rise to a von Neumann algebra
L^{\infty}(X)\rtimes\Gamma
whose “functions” subalgebra
L^{\infty}(X)
is the unique Cartan subalgebra, up to unitary conjugacy. Remarkably, their examples include all direct products of free groups
\Gamma=\mathbb{F}_{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{F}_{n_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{F}_{n_{k}}
, for all
n_{i}\geq 2
and
k\geq 1
as well as other examples of right-angled Artin groups. In this paper we are able to provide additional examples of
\mathcal{C}
-rigid general graph products groups thus complementing these results.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Gamma=\mathscr{G}\{\Gamma_{v}\}
be a graph product of groups so that the graph
\mathscr{G}
does not admit a nontrivial join decomposition
\mathscr{G}=\mathscr{H}\circ\mathscr{K}
where either
\mathscr{H}=(\{v\},\emptyset)
, or,
\mathscr{H}=(\{v,w\},\emptyset)

\Gamma_{v}\cong\Gamma_{w}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}

be any free ergodic pmp action and let
\mathcal{M}=L^{\infty}(X)\rtimes\Gamma

\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{M}

\mathcal{A}=uL^{\infty}(X)u^{*}
.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Graph products of groups

In this subsection we briefly recall the notion of graph product of groups introduced by E. Green
[
Gr90
]
also highlighting some of its features that are relevant to this note. Let
\mathscr{G}=(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{E})
be a finite simple graph, where
\mathscr{V}
and
\mathscr{E}
denote its vertices and edges sets, respectively. Let
\{\Gamma_{v}\}_{v\in\mathscr{V}}
be a family of groups called vertex groups. The graph product group associated with this data, denoted by
\mathscr{G}\{\Gamma_{v},v\in\mathscr{V}\}
or simply
\mathscr{G}\{\Gamma_{v}\}
, is the group generated by
\Gamma_{v}
,
v\in\mathscr{V}
with the only relations that
[\Gamma_{u},\Gamma_{v}]=1
, whenever
(u,v)\in\mathscr{E}
.
Given any subset
\mathscr{U}\subset\mathscr{V}
, the subgroup
\Gamma_{\mathscr{U}}=\langle\Gamma_{u}\,:\,u\in\mathscr{U}\rangle
of
\mathscr{G}\{\Gamma_{v},v\in\mathscr{V}\}
is called a full subgroup. This can be identified with the graph product
\mathscr{G}_{\mathscr{U}}\{\Gamma_{u},u\in\mathscr{U}\}
corresponding to the subgraph
\mathscr{G}_{\mathscr{U}}
of
\mathscr{G}
, spanned by the vertices of
\mathscr{U}
. For every
v\in\mathscr{V}
we denote by
{\rm lk}(v)
the subset of vertices
w\neq v
so that
(w,v)\in\mathscr{E}
. Similarly, for every
\mathscr{U}\subseteq\mathscr{V}
we denote by
{\rm lk}(\mathscr{U})=\cap_{u\in\mathscr{U}}{\rm lk}(u)
. Also we make the convention that
{\rm lk}(\emptyset)=\mathscr{V}
. Notice that
\mathscr{U}\cap{\rm lk}(\mathscr{U})=\emptyset
.

Graph product groups admit many amalgamated free product decompositions. One such decomposition which is essential on deriving our main results, involves full subgroups factors in
[
Gr90
, Lemma 3.20]
as follows. For any
w\in\mathscr{V}
we have

\mathscr{G}\{\Gamma_{v}\}=\Gamma_{\mathscr{V}\setminus\{w\}}\ast_{\Gamma_{\rm lk}(w)}\Gamma_{{\rm st}(w)},

where
{\rm st}(w)=\{w\}\cup{\rm lk}(w)
. Notice that always
\Gamma_{{\rm lk}(w)}\lneqq\Gamma_{{\rm st}(w)}
but it could be the case that
\Gamma_{{\rm lk}(w)}=\Gamma_{\mathscr{V}\setminus\{w\}}
, when
\mathscr{V}={\rm st}(w)
. In this case the amalgam decomposition is degenerate.

If a graph
(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{E})=\mathscr{G}
has two proper subgraphs
(\mathscr{W},\mathscr{F})=\mathscr{H},(\mathscr{U},\mathscr{D})=\mathscr{K}\subset\mathscr{G}
whose vertices form a partition
\mathscr{V}=\mathscr{W}\sqcup\mathscr{U}
and
(v,w)\in\mathscr{E}
for all
v\in\mathscr{W}
and
w\in\mathscr{U}
then we say that
\mathscr{G}
admits a non-trivial join product decomposition
\mathscr{G}=\mathscr{H}\circ\mathscr{K}
. A graph
\mathscr{G}=(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{E})
is called irreducible if it does not admit any non-trivial join product decomposition. It is well-known that any graph
\mathscr{G}
admits a (de Rham) join product decomposition
\mathscr{G}=\mathscr{G}_{0}\circ\mathscr{G}_{1}\circ\cdots\circ\mathscr{G}_{k}
for
k\geq 0
, where
\mathscr{G}_{0}
is a clique (i.e. a complete graph) and
\mathscr{G}_{i}\subseteq\mathscr{G}
, for all
i\geq 1
are irreducible subgraphs with at least two vertices.

2.2
Popa’s intertwining-by-bimodules techniques

We next recall from
[
Po03
, Theorem 2.1, Corollary 2.3]
Popa’s
intertwining-by-bimodules
technique.
Let
\mathcal{Q}\subset\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann subalgebra. The basic construction
\langle\mathcal{M},e_{\mathcal{Q}}\rangle
is defined as the von Neumann subalgebra of
\mathbb{B}(L^{2}(\mathcal{M}))
generated by
\mathcal{M}
and the orthogonal projection
e_{\mathcal{Q}}
from
L^{2}(\mathcal{M})
onto
L^{2}(\mathcal{Q})
. There is a semi-finite faithful trace on
\langle\mathcal{M},e_{\mathcal{Q}}\rangle
given by
\text{Tr}(xe_{\mathcal{Q}}y)=\tau(xy)
, for every
x,y\in\mathcal{M}
. We denote by
L^{2}(\langle\mathcal{M},e_{\mathcal{Q}}\rangle)
the associated Hilbert space and endow it with the natural
\mathcal{M}
-bimodule structure.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
Po03
]
)
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
be a tracial von Neumann algebra and
\mathcal{P}\subset p\mathcal{M}p,\mathcal{Q}\subset\mathcal{M}
be von Neumann subalgebras.
Then the following are equivalent:

1.

There exist projections
p_{0}\in\mathcal{P},q_{0}\in\mathcal{Q}
, a
*
-homomorphism
\theta:p_{0}\mathcal{P}p_{0}\rightarrow q_{0}\mathcal{Q}q_{0}
and a non-zero partial isometry
v\in q_{0}\mathcal{M}p_{0}
such that
\theta(x)v=vx
, for all
x\in p_{0}\mathcal{P}p_{0}
.

2.

There exists a non-zero element
a\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\cap p\langle\mathcal{M},e_{\mathcal{Q}}\rangle p
such that
a\geq 0
and
\text{Tr}(a)<\infty
.

3.

There is no sequence
u_{n}\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{P})
satisfying
\|E_{\mathcal{Q}}(x^{*}u_{n}y)\|_{2}\rightarrow 0
, for all
x,y\in p\mathcal{M}
.

If one of these equivalent conditions holds, we write
\mathcal{P}\prec_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{Q}
, and say that
a corner of
\mathcal{P}
embeds into
\mathcal{Q}
inside
\mathcal{M}
.

Moreover, if
\mathcal{P}p^{\prime}\prec_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{Q}
for all projections
0\neq p^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\cap p\mathcal{M}p
, then we write
\mathcal{P}\prec^{s}_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{Q}
.

Next we recall a few intertwining results that will be used in the main proofs. The first is a straightforward generalization of
[
CI17
, Lemma 2.]
, its proof being left to the reader.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
CI17
]
)
.

Let
\Lambda,\Sigma\leqslant\Gamma
be groups, let
\Gamma\curvearrowright\mathcal{P}
be a trace preserving action on a von Neumann algebra and let
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{P}\rtimes\Gamma
be the corresponding crossed product von Neumann algebra. Then
\mathcal{P}\rtimes\Lambda\prec_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{P}\rtimes\Sigma
if an only if there is
g\in\Gamma
such that
[\Lambda:\Lambda\cap g\Sigma g^{-1}]<\infty
.

The second is Popa’s conjugacy criterion for Cartan subalgebras
[
Po01
, Theorem A.1]
.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
Po01
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a II
1
factor and
\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q}\subset\mathcal{M}
be Cartan subalgebras. If
\mathcal{P}\prec_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{Q}
, then there is
u\in\mathscr{U}(\mathcal{M})
such that
u\mathcal{P}u^{*}=\mathcal{Q}
.

2.3
Relative amenability

Recall that the tracial von Neumann algebra
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
is called
amenable
if there exists a sequence
\xi_{n}\in L^{2}(\mathcal{M})\otimes L^{2}(\mathcal{M})
such that
\langle x\xi_{n},\xi_{n}\rangle\rightarrow\tau(x)
and
\|x\xi_{n}-\xi_{n}x\|_{2}\rightarrow 0
, for every
x\in\mathcal{M}
. By Connes’ celebrated classification of injective factors
[
Co76
]
,
\mathcal{M}
is amenable if and only if it is approximately finite dimensional.

Let
\mathcal{P}\subset p\mathcal{M}p
and
\mathcal{Q}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann subalgebras.
Following
[
OP07
, Section 2.2]
we say that
\mathcal{P}
is
amenable relative to
\mathcal{Q}
inside
\mathcal{M}
if there exists a sequence
\xi_{n}\in L^{2}(\langle\mathcal{M},e_{\mathcal{Q}}\rangle)
such that
\langle x\xi_{n},\xi_{n}\rangle\rightarrow\tau(x)
, for every
x\in p\mathcal{M}p
, and
\|y\xi_{n}-\xi_{n}y\|_{2}\rightarrow 0
, for every
y\in\mathcal{P}
. When
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{M}
one simply says that
\mathcal{M}
is
co-amenable relative to

\mathcal{Q}
, paralleling Eymard’s notion of co-amenability in group theory—a subgroup
H
of
G
is co-amenable if there exists a left
G
invariant state on
\ell^{\infty}(G/H)
.

The following lemma is well known, but for convenience of the reader we include a short proof below:

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\Gamma=\Gamma_{1}\ast_{\Sigma}\Gamma_{2}
be a non-degenerate amalgamated free product such that
\Gamma
is co-amenable relative to
\Sigma
. Then
[\Gamma_{i}:\Sigma]=2
for all
i=1,2
.

Proof.

Suppose that
[\Gamma_{1}:\Sigma]\geq 3
, and suppose
\varphi
is a non trivial left
\Gamma
-invariant state on
\ell^{\infty}(\Gamma/\Sigma)
. Let
S_{i}
denote the subset of elements in
\Gamma/\Sigma
whose normal form decomposition begins with
\Gamma_{i}/\Sigma
. Clearly, we have
\phi(\chi_{S_{1}}+\chi_{S_{2}})=1
. Moreover, since
[\Gamma:\Gamma_{1}]\geq 3
, there exists
g_{1}\neq g_{2}\in\Gamma/\Gamma_{1}
. Let
\Gamma_{2}/\Sigma\ni h\neq 1
. Hence
\varphi(g_{1}\chi_{S_{2}}+g_{2}\chi_{S_{2}})\leq\varphi(h\chi_{S_{1}})\leq\varphi(\chi_{S_{2}})
, giving
\varphi(\chi_{S_{2}})=0
. Similarly
\varphi(\chi_{S_{1}})=0
which is a contradiction.
∎

3
Proofs of the main results

The main technical result of the paper is the following intertwining result for
virtual
Cartan subalgebras in crossed products by graph product groups. All other results are derived from it. Its proof relies on the classification of normalizers of amenable subalgebras in amalgamated free products due to Ioana
[
Io12
]
and its more general version by Vaes
[
Va13
]
together with a technique on transitivity of intertwining virtual Cartan subalgebras from
[
Io11
,
CIK13
]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\Gamma=\mathscr{G}\{\Gamma_{v}\}
be a graph product of groups and let
\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright\mathcal{P}
be a trace preserving action on a von Neumann algebra.
Denote by
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{P}\rtimes\Gamma
the corresponding crossed product von Neumann algebra and let
p\in\mathcal{M}
be a projection. Assume that
\mathcal{A}\subset p\mathcal{M}p
is a masa such that its normalizer
\mathscr{N}_{p\mathcal{M}p}(\mathcal{A})^{\prime\prime}\subseteq p\mathcal{M}p
has finite index. Thus one can find a subgraph
\mathscr{G}_{0}\subseteq\mathscr{G}
whose de Rham join product decomposition
\mathscr{G}_{0}=\mathscr{G}_{1}\circ\mathscr{G}_{2}\circ\cdots\circ\mathscr{G}_{k}
satisfies
\mathscr{G}_{i}=(\{v_{i},w_{i}\},\emptyset)
with
\Gamma_{v_{i}}=\Gamma_{w_{i}}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}
for all
2\leq i\leq k
such that
\mathcal{A}\prec_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{P}\rtimes\Gamma_{\mathscr{G}_{0}}
.

Proof.

Let
(\mathscr{V}_{0},\mathscr{E}_{0})=\mathscr{G}_{0}\subseteq\mathscr{G}
be a subgraph such that
|\mathscr{V}_{0}|
is minimal with the property that
\mathcal{A}\prec_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{P}\rtimes\Gamma_{\mathscr{G}_{0}}=:\mathcal{N}
. Let
\mathscr{G}_{0}=\mathscr{G}_{1}\circ\mathscr{G}_{2}\circ\cdots\circ\mathscr{G}_{k}
be its de Rham decomposition where
\mathscr{G}_{1}
is a clique of
\mathscr{G}_{0}
. Next we show all
\mathscr{G}_{i}
with
i\geq 2
have the properties listed in the statement.

Since
\mathcal{A}
is a masa, using
[
CIK13
, Proposition 3.6]
there are projections
0\neq a\in\mathcal{A},0\neq e\in\mathcal{N}
, a masa
\mathcal{B}\subset e\mathcal{N}e
, a projection
0\neq e^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\cap e\mathcal{M}e
and a unitary
u\in\mathcal{M}
such that

\begin{split}&\text{the inclusion }\mathcal{Q}:=\mathscr{N}_{e\mathcal{N}e}(\mathcal{B})^{\prime\prime}\subseteq e\mathcal{N}e\text{ has finite index,}\\
&\text{the support satisfies }s(E_{\mathcal{N}}(e^{\prime}))=e\text{ and }u\mathcal{A}au^{*}=\mathcal{B}e^{\prime}.\end{split}

(3.1)

Fix
i\geq 2
and let
v\in\mathcal{V}_{i}
such that
\Gamma_{\mathscr{G}_{0}}
admits a non-canonical decomposition
\Gamma_{\mathscr{G}_{0}}=\Gamma_{\mathscr{G}_{0}\setminus\{v\}}\ast_{\Gamma_{\text{lk}(v)}}\Gamma_{\text{st}(v)}
; in particular we have that
|\text{lk}(v)|\leq|\mathscr{V}_{0}|-2
. Using
[
Va13
,
Io12
]
one of the following cases must hold:

i)

\mathcal{B}\prec_{\mathcal{N}}\mathcal{P}\rtimes\Gamma_{\text{lk}(v)}
;

ii)

\mathcal{Q}\prec_{\mathcal{N}}\mathcal{P}\rtimes\Gamma_{\mathscr{G}_{0}\setminus\{v\}})
or
\mathcal{P}\rtimes\Gamma_{\text{st}(v)}
;

iii)

\mathcal{Q}
is amenable relative to
\mathcal{P}\rtimes\Gamma_{\text{lk}(v)}
inside
\mathcal{N}
.

Assume i). Thus one can find a partial isometry
0\neq w\in\mathcal{N}
with
w^{*}w\in\mathcal{B}
such that
w\mathcal{B}\subseteq(\mathcal{P}\rtimes\mathcal{G}_{\text{lk}(v)})w
. Now we argue that
we^{\prime}\neq 0
. If
0=we^{\prime}
then
0=w^{*}we^{\prime}
and since
w\in\mathcal{N}
we get
0=w^{*}wE_{\mathcal{N}}(e^{\prime})
. This however further implies that
0=w^{*}ws(E_{\mathcal{N}}(e^{\prime}))=w^{*}we=w^{*}w
, which is contradiction. Therefore we have
we^{\prime}\mathcal{B}=w\mathcal{B}e^{\prime}\subseteq(\mathcal{P}\rtimes\mathcal{G}_{\text{lk}(v)})we^{\prime}
and using the second part in (
3.1
) we get
wua\mathcal{A}\subseteq(\mathcal{P}\rtimes\mathcal{G}_{\text{lk}(v)})we^{\prime}u
. In particular,
\mathcal{A}\prec_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{P}\rtimes\Gamma_{\text{lk}(v)}
and since
|\text{lk}(v)|\leq|\mathscr{V}_{0}|-1
, this contradicts the minimality of
|\mathscr{V}_{0}|
. Thus i) cannot hold.

Now assume
\mathcal{Q}\prec_{\mathcal{N}}\mathcal{P}\rtimes\Gamma_{\mathscr{G}_{0}\setminus\{v\}}
. Using
[
DHI16
, Lemma 2.4(3)]
there is a projection
0\neq q\in\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}\cap e\mathcal{N}e
such that
\mathcal{Q}q\prec^{\text{s}}_{\mathcal{N}}\mathcal{P}\rtimes\Gamma_{\mathscr{G}_{0}\setminus\{v\}}
. Part one in (
3.1
) implies that
\mathcal{Q}q\subseteq q\mathcal{N}q
has finite index. Then combining this with
[
CIK13
, Lemma 2.2]
and
[
DHI16
, Lemma 2.4(1)]
we conclude that
\mathcal{P}\rtimes\Gamma_{\mathscr{G}_{0}}\prec_{\mathcal{N}}\mathcal{P}\rtimes\Gamma_{\mathscr{G}_{0}\setminus\{v\}}
. By Lemma
2.2
it follows that
\Gamma_{\mathscr{G}_{0}\setminus\{v\}}\leqslant\Gamma_{\mathscr{G}_{0}}
has finite index, which is a contradiction. To see this pick
t\in\mathscr{V}_{0}\setminus\{v\}
such that
(t,v)\notin\mathscr{E}
. Now for any
g\in\Gamma_{t}\setminus\{1\}
and
h\in\Gamma_{v}\setminus\{1\}
one can see that
(gh)^{k}\Gamma_{\mathscr{V}_{0}\setminus\{v\}}
for all
k\geq 2
are distinct co-sets in
\Gamma_{\mathscr{V}_{0}}
. If
\mathcal{Q}\prec_{\mathcal{N}}\mathcal{P}\rtimes\Gamma_{\text{st}(v)}
then in a similar manner one gets that
\Gamma_{\text{st}(v)}\leqslant\Gamma_{\mathscr{G}_{0}}
has finite index which is again contradiction. Altogether these show ii) cannot hold either.

Assume iii). Since
\mathcal{Q}\subseteq e\mathcal{N}e
has finite index we have
e\mathcal{N}e
is amenable relative to
\mathcal{Q}
inside
\mathcal{N}
and thus by
[
OP07
, Proposition 2.3]
we get that
e\mathcal{N}e
is amenable relative to
\mathcal{L}(\Gamma_{\text{lk}(v)})
. This implies that
\Gamma_{\mathscr{G}_{0}}
is co-amenable relative to
\Gamma_{\text{lk}(v)}
. Thus by Lemma
2.4
we have
\mathscr{G}_{0}\setminus\text{st}(v)=\{t\}
,
\Gamma_{t},\Gamma_{v}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and also
\text{lk}(v)=\text{lk}_{\mathscr{G}_{0}}(t)
. Thus, a fortiori we have
\mathscr{G}_{i}=(\{v,t\},\emptyset)
which gives the desired conclusion. ∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\Gamma=\mathscr{G}\{\Gamma_{v}\}
be a graph product of groups and let
\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright\mathcal{P}
be a trace preserving action on a von Neumann algebra.
Assume the corresponding crossed product
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{P}\rtimes\Gamma
is a II
1
factor and let
p\in\mathcal{M}
be a projection. Assume that
\mathcal{A}\subset p\mathcal{M}p
is a Cartan subalgebra. Then one can find a join decomposition
\mathscr{G}=\mathscr{G}_{0}\circ\mathscr{K}
where
\mathscr{G}_{0}
has de Rham join product decomposition
\mathscr{G}_{0}=\mathscr{G}_{1}\circ\mathscr{G}_{2}\circ\cdots\circ\mathscr{G}_{k}
so that for all
2\leq i\leq k
we have
\mathscr{G}_{i}=(\{v_{i},w_{i}\},\emptyset)
and
\Gamma_{v_{i}}=\Gamma_{w_{i}}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}
which satisfies
\mathcal{A}\prec_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{P}\rtimes\Gamma_{\mathscr{G}_{0}}
.

Proof.

Consider
\mathscr{G}=\mathscr{H}_{0}\circ\mathscr{H}_{1}\circ\cdots\circ\mathscr{H}_{l}
be the de Rham join product decomposition of
\mathscr{G}
and let
\mathscr{H}_{i}=(\mathscr{V}_{i},\mathscr{E}_{i})
. Form Theorem
3.1
there is a subgraph
\mathscr{G}_{0}\subseteq\mathscr{G}
with de Rham join product decomposition
\mathscr{G}_{0}=\mathscr{G}_{1}\circ\mathscr{G}_{2}\circ\cdots\circ\mathscr{G}_{k}
so that for all
i\geq 2
we have
\mathscr{G}_{i}=(\{v_{i},w_{i}\},\emptyset)
and
\Gamma_{v_{i}}=\Gamma_{w_{i}}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}
which satisfies
\mathcal{A}\prec_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{P}\rtimes\Gamma_{\mathscr{G}_{0}}
. Denote by
\mathscr{W}_{i}=\{v_{i},w_{i}\}
. Using
[
DHI16
, Lemma 2.4(3)]
we have

A\prec^{s}_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{P}\rtimes\Gamma_{\mathscr{G}_{0}}.

(3.2)

Moreover, we can assume
\mathscr{G}_{0}
is a subgraph with all the properties listed above and a minimal number of vertices
|\mathscr{V}_{0}|
. In particular this implies that
\mathscr{G}_{0}
cannot be represented as a link
\mathscr{G}_{0}={\rm lk}(w)
of a vertex such that
\Gamma_{w}
is a finite group.

Next let
I_{1}\sqcup I_{2}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup I_{s}=\{1,\ldots,k\}
be a partition and let
\{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{t}\}\subseteq\{0,\ldots,l\}
be a subset such that
\circ_{j\in I_{r}}\mathscr{G}_{j}\subseteq\mathscr{H}_{i_{r}}
is a subgraph for
1\leq r\leq t
. Here we have
s\leq k
and
t\leq l+1
. Notice that if
i_{1}=0
then necessarily
I_{1}=\{1\}
.

Let
i_{r}\neq 0
and assume
\cup_{j\in I_{r}}\mathscr{W}_{j}\subsetneq\mathscr{V}_{i_{r}}
. Since
\mathscr{G}_{i_{r}}
is irreducible there is
v\in\mathscr{V}_{i_{r}}\setminus\cup_{j\in I_{r}}\mathscr{W}_{j}
such that
{\rm lk}_{\mathscr{G}_{i_{r}}}(v)\cap\circ_{j\in I_{r}}\mathscr{G}_{j}\subsetneq\circ_{j\in I_{r}}\mathscr{G}_{j}
. In particular, we also have
{\rm lk}(v)\cap\mathscr{G}_{0}\subsetneq\mathscr{G}_{0}
. Now we note that
\Gamma_{\mathscr{G}}
admits a non-canonical decomposition
\Gamma_{\mathscr{G}}=\Gamma_{\mathscr{G}\setminus\{v\}}\ast_{\Gamma_{\text{lk}(v)}}\Gamma_{\text{st}(v)}
. Using
[
Va13
,
Io12
]
and
[
DHI16
, Lemma 2.4(3)]
one of the following cases must hold:

i)

\mathcal{A}\prec^{s}_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{P}\rtimes\mathcal{G}_{\text{lk}(v)}
;

ii)

p\mathcal{M}p\prec^{s}_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{P}\rtimes\Gamma_{\mathscr{G}\setminus\{v\}})
or
\mathcal{P}\rtimes\Gamma_{\text{st}(v)}
;

iii)

p\mathcal{M}p
is co-amenable relative to
\mathcal{P}\rtimes\Gamma_{\text{lk}(v)}
.

Assume i) holds. Combining it with (
3.2
) and using
[
DHI16
, Lemma 2.6]
there is
h\in\Gamma
such that
\mathcal{A}\prec^{s}\mathcal{P}\rtimes\left(\Gamma_{{\rm lk}(v)}\cap h\Gamma_{\mathscr{G}_{0}}h^{-1}\right)
. By
[
AM10
, Proposition 3.4]
there exists
\mathscr{S}\subset{\rm lk}(v)\cap\mathscr{G}_{0}\subsetneq\mathscr{G}_{0}
such that
k\Gamma_{\mathscr{S}}k^{-1}=\Gamma_{{\rm lk}(v)}\cap h\Gamma_{\mathscr{G}_{0}}h^{-1}
for some
k\in\Gamma
and hence
\mathcal{A}\prec^{s}\mathcal{P}\rtimes\Gamma_{\mathscr{S}}
. However the graph
\mathscr{S}
has all the properties of
\mathscr{G}_{0}
but but it has a smaller number of vertices thus contradicting the minimality of
|\mathscr{V}_{0}|
. So i) cannot hold.

Assume ii) holds. Then by Lemma
2.2
we have either
[\Gamma:\Gamma_{\mathscr{G}\setminus\{v\}}]<\infty
or
[\Gamma:\Gamma_{{\rm st}(v)}]<\infty
which are impossible.

Assume iii) holds. Therefore
\Gamma
is amenable relative to
\Gamma_{\rm lk(v)}
and by Lemma
2.4
we have
\mathscr{G}\setminus\text{st}(v)=\{t\}\in\circ_{j\in I_{r}}\mathscr{H}_{j}
,
\Gamma_{t},\Gamma_{v}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and thus
\mathscr{G}=\{v,t\}\circ\text{lk}\{v,t\}
. We also have that
\{v,t\}\subseteq\mathscr{V}_{i_{r}}
and since
\mathscr{G}_{i_{r}}
is irreducible we conclude that
\mathscr{G}_{i_{r}}=(\{v,t\},\emptyset)
. But this implies in particular that
(\{t\},\emptyset)=\circ_{j\in I_{r}}\mathscr{H}_{j}
ans since
\Gamma_{t}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}
this again contradicts the minimality of
|\mathscr{V}_{0}|
. So this case is impossible as well.

In conclusion, for all
i_{r}\neq 0
we have
\circ_{j\in I_{r}}\mathscr{H}_{j}=\mathscr{G}_{i_{r}}
which gives the desired conclusion. ∎

Remarks
. Both graph conditions presented in the previous corollary are optimal. In other words, for these graphs there there are choices of the vertex groups for which the result does not hold as stated.

To see this, let first
\mathscr{G}=\{w\}\circ{\rm lk}(w)
be a star-shaped graph. Using a result from
[
OP07
]
, we show there is a graph product group
G=\mathscr{G}\{\Gamma_{v}\}=\Gamma_{w}\times\Gamma_{{\rm lk}(w)}
and a pmp action
G\curvearrowright X
such that
\mathcal{M}=L^{\infty}(X)\rtimes G
has at least two unitary non-conjugate Cartan subalgebras. Indeed, let
\Gamma_{w}=\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rtimes{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
and let
\Gamma_{w}\curvearrowright Y=\underset{\longleftarrow}{\lim}(Y_{n})
be the free ergodic profinite action as in
[
OP08
, Theorem D]
. Also let
\Gamma_{{\rm lk}(w)}\curvearrowright Z
be any free ergodic pmp action. Then the consider the product action
G=\Gamma_{w}\times\Gamma_{{\rm lk}(w)}\curvearrowright Y\times Z=:X
. Then clearly
L^{\infty}(X)
and
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(Z)
are two Cartan subalgebras of
\mathcal{M}
which are not unitarily conjugated.

For the second situation let
\mathscr{G}=(\{w,v\},\emptyset)\circ{\rm lk}(w,v)
and assume
\mathcal{G}_{v}=\mathcal{G}_{w}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. Thus we have a product decomposition of graph product group

G=\mathscr{G}\{\Gamma_{v}\}=(\mathbb{Z}_{2}\ast\mathbb{Z}_{2})\times\Gamma_{{\rm lk}(w)}
. Since the infinite dihedral group
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}_{2}\ast\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is ressidually finite, there is a free ergodic profinite pmp action
\Lambda\curvearrowright Y
. Let
\Gamma_{{\rm lk}(w)}\curvearrowright Z
be any free ergodic pmp action and consider the product action
G=\Gamma_{w}\times\Gamma_{{\rm lk}(w)}\curvearrowright Y\times Z=:X
. Next we view the infinite dihedral group as a semidirect product group
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\langle a,s\,|\,s^{2}=1,sas=a^{-1}\rangle
. Then one can check that
L^{\infty}(X)
and
\mathcal{L}(\langle a\rangle)\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(Z)
are two Cartan subalgebras of
\mathcal{M}
that are not unitarily conjugated. The only noncanonical part of this statement is to show that
\mathcal{L}(\langle a\rangle)\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(Z)\subset\mathcal{M}
is Cartan subalgebra. To see this it suffices to show that
\mathcal{L}(\langle a\rangle)\subset L^{\infty}(Y)\rtimes\Lambda=:\mathcal{N}
is a Cartan subalgebra. Throughout the remainder of the proof let
\Omega=\langle a\rangle
and first we show
\mathcal{L}(\Omega)
is a masa. Let
x\in\mathcal{L}(\Omega)^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{N}
. Hence
xu_{g}=u_{g}x
for all
g\in\Omega
and using the Fourier decomposition
x=\sum_{h\in\Lambda}x_{h}u_{h}
, where
x_{h}\in L^{\infty}(Y)
, we get

x_{ghg^{-1}}=\sigma_{g}(x_{h})\text{ for all }g\in\Omega,h\in\Lambda.

(3.3)

Fix
a^{t}s=h\in\Lambda\setminus\Omega
, where
t\in\mathbb{Z}
. Thus for every
g=a^{r}
, using the presentation of
\Lambda
we have
ghg^{-1}=a^{r+t}sa^{-r}=a^{r+t-1}sa^{-r-1}=\cdots=sa^{-2r-t}
. In particular this implies that
\{ghg^{-1}\,|\,g\in\Omega
is infinite. Using (
3.3
) we get that
x_{h}=0
for all
h\in\Lambda\setminus\Omega
. Now fix
h\in\langle a\rangle
. Since
\Omega
is abelian then (
3.3
) implies that

x_{h}=\sigma_{g}(x_{h})
and since
\sigma
is free we conclude that
x_{h}\in\mathbb{C}1
for all
h\in\Omega
. Altogether, these show that
x=\sum_{h}x_{h}u_{h}\in\mathcal{L}(\Omega)
which give that
\mathcal{L}(\Omega)\subset\mathcal{N}
is a masa. Now we briefly argue it is a Cartan subalgebra. Since
\Lambda\curvearrowright Y:=\underset{\longleftarrow}{\lim}(Y_{n})
is profinite then we have an increasing union of finite dimensional, abelian,
\Lambda
-invariant von Neumann algebras
\cdots\subset\mathcal{B}_{n}:=L^{\infty}(Y_{n})\subset\mathcal{B}_{n+1}:=L^{\infty}(Y_{n+1})\subset\cdots\subset L^{\infty}(Y)
with
\overline{\bigcup_{n}\mathcal{B}_{n}}^{sot}=L^{\infty}(Y)
. Let
\mathcal{B}_{n}=\oplus_{1\leq i\leq k_{n}}\mathbb{C}b^{n}_{i}
for some
\tau
-orthonormal family
\{b^{n}_{i}\}\subset\mathcal{B}_{n}
and observe that for every
h\in\Omega
we have
\sigma_{h}(b^{n}_{i})=\sum_{j}\mu^{n}_{i,j}(h)b^{n}_{j}
where
\mu^{n}_{i,j}(h)=\langle\sigma_{g}(b^{n}_{i}),b^{n}_{j}\rangle\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\mu^{n}_{i,j}(h)|\leq 1
. This clearly implies that
L(\Omega)b_{i}^{n}\subseteq\sum^{k_{n}}_{j=1}b_{j}^{n}L(\Omega)
and
b^{n}_{i}L(\Omega)\subseteq\sum^{k_{n}}_{j=1}L(\Omega)b_{j}^{n}
for all
i
. In particular, for all
i
and all
n
we have
b_{i}^{n}\in\mathscr{QN}_{\mathcal{N}}(L(\Omega))
—the quasinormalizer of
L(\Omega)
in
\mathcal{N}
,
[
Po01
, Section 1.4.2]
. Thus
\bigcup_{n}\mathcal{B}_{n}\subset\mathscr{QN}_{\mathcal{N}}(L(\Omega))
and hence
L^{\infty}(Y)\subseteq\mathscr{QN}_{\mathcal{N}}(L(\Omega))^{\prime\prime}
. Since
\Omega
is normal in
\Lambda
we further get
L^{\infty}(Y)\rtimes\Lambda\subseteq\mathscr{QN}_{\mathcal{N}}(L(\Omega))^{\prime\prime}
and hence
\mathcal{N}=\mathscr{QN}_{\mathcal{N}}(L(\Omega))^{\prime\prime}
. However, since
L(\Omega)\subset\mathcal{N}
is masa, by
[
Po01
, Proposition 1.4.2]
, we have
\mathscr{QN}_{\mathcal{N}}(L(\Omega))^{\prime\prime}=\mathscr{N}_{\mathcal{N}}(\mathcal{L}(\Omega))^{\prime\prime}
and hence
\mathcal{L}(\Omega)\subset\mathcal{N}
is a Cartan subalgebra as claimed.

If one considers the star-shaped graphs excluded in the statement of Theorem
1.3
one can still get a uniqueness of Cartan subalgebra result if stronger conditions are imposed on the vertex groups. To properly introduce the result we recall the following definition.

Following
[
PV11
]
a group
\Gamma
is called
\mathcal{C}
-superrigid if the following property holds. Given any
\Gamma\curvearrowright\mathcal{P}
trace-preserving action and any projection
p\in\mathcal{P}\rtimes\Gamma=\mathcal{M}
then any masa
\mathcal{A}\subseteq p\mathcal{M}p
with finite index normalizer
[p\mathcal{M}p:\mathscr{N}_{p\mathcal{M}p}(\mathcal{A})^{\prime\prime}]<\infty
must satisfy
\mathcal{A}\prec_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{P}
. From
[
PV11
,
PV12
]
it follows that all free groups with at least two generators and, more generally, any non-elementery hyperbolic groups are
\mathcal{C}
-superrigid.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
\Gamma=\mathscr{G}\{\Gamma_{v}\}
be any graph product where the vertex groups
\Gamma_{v}
are non-amenable
\mathcal{C}
-superrigid, for all
v
. Then
\Gamma
is a
\mathcal{C}
-superrigid group.

Proof.

Letting
\mathscr{G}=(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{E})
, we proceed by induction on
|\mathscr{V}|
. When
|\mathscr{V}|=1
the conclusion follows from the definition. Thus it only remains to show the inductive step. If
\mathscr{G}
is not star-shaped the result already follows from Theorem
3.2
. Therefore, since the vertex groups are infinite, we only have to treat the case
\mathscr{G}=\{v\}\circ{\rm lk}(v)
.
Let
\Gamma\curvearrowright\mathcal{P}
be any trace preserving action and let
0\neq p\in\mathcal{P}\rtimes\Gamma=:\mathcal{M}
be any projection. Let
\mathcal{A}\subseteq p\mathcal{M}p
be a masa so that
[p\mathcal{M}p:\mathscr{N}_{p\mathcal{M}p}(\mathcal{A})^{\prime\prime}]<\infty
. Since
\Gamma=\Gamma_{v}\times\Gamma_{{\rm lk}(v)}
we can view
\mathcal{M}=(\mathcal{P}\rtimes\Gamma_{{\rm lk}(v)})\rtimes\Gamma_{v}
and since
\Gamma_{v}
is
\mathcal{C}
-superrigid we further get that
\mathcal{A}\prec_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{P}\rtimes\Gamma_{{\rm lk}(v)}=:\mathcal{N}
. As
\mathcal{A}\subset p\mathcal{M}p
is a masa, using
[
CIK13
, Proposition 3.6]
there are projections
0\neq a\in\mathcal{A},0\neq e\in\mathcal{N}
, a masa
\mathcal{B}\subset e\mathcal{N}e
, a projection
0\neq e^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\cap e\mathcal{M}e
and a unitary
u\in\mathcal{M}
such that the inclusion
\mathcal{Q}:=\mathscr{N}_{e\mathcal{N}e}(\mathcal{B})^{\prime\prime}\subseteq e\mathcal{N}e
has finite index, and the support satisfies
s(E_{\mathcal{N}}(e^{\prime}))=e
and
u\mathcal{A}au^{*}=\mathcal{B}e^{\prime}
.
As
|\rm lk(v)|=|\mathscr{V}|-1
, the inductive hypothesis implies that
\Gamma_{{\rm lk}(v)}
is
\mathcal{C}
-superrigid. Thus the prior relation further implies that
\mathcal{B}\prec_{\mathcal{N}}\mathcal{P}
. Hence one can find a partial isometry

with
w^{*}w\in\mathcal{B}
such that
w\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{P}w
. Same argument from the proof of Theorem

shows that
we^{\prime}\neq 0
. Hence
we^{\prime}\mathcal{B}=w\mathcal{B}e^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{P}we^{\prime}
and combining this with the previous containment it yields
wua\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{P}we^{\prime}u
. In particular,
\mathcal{A}\prec_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{P}
which concludes the proof of the inductive step. ∎

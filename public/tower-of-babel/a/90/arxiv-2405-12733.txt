. In this paper, all results apply only to finite graphs. Let G G be a simple connected finite graph with n n vertices and maximum degree Δ ⁡ ( G ) \Delta(G) . 1.
Introduction

All graphs considered in this paper are simple, finite, and connected, and all our results apply only to finite graphs. In
[
1
]
, Albertson and Collins studied the distinguishing number of a graph, and Collins and Trenk
[
8
]
introduced the distinguishing chromatic number of a graph.
A coloring
h:V_{G}\rightarrow\{1,...,r\}
of the vertices of a graph
G=(V_{G},E_{G})
is
r

provided no nontrivial automorphism of
G
preserves all of the colors of the vertices. The
distinguishing number
of
G

r
such that
G
has an
r
-distinguishing coloring. The coloring
h
is
r
-proper distinguishing
provided
h
is
r
-distinguishing and a proper coloring. The
distinguishing chromatic number
of
G

\chi_{D}(G)
, is the minimum integer
r
such that
G
has an
r
-proper distinguishing coloring.
Collins and Trenk
[
8
]
obtained a Brooks’ Theorem type upper bound for

Theorem 1.1
.

(Collins and Trenk
[
8

Let

, or the cycle graph

Recently, Ferrara et al.
[

The second inequality was stated in

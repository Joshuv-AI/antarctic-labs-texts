On towers of isogeny graphs with full level structures
Antonio Lei, Katharina Müller

Abstract
. Let p p and ℓ \ell be distinct prime numbers, let q q be a power of a prime number r r that is distinct from p p and ℓ \ell , and let M M be a positive integer coprime to q ​ ℓ q\ell . We define the directed graph X ℓ q ​ ( M ) X_{\ell}^{q}(M) whose vertices are given by isomorphism classes of elliptic curves over the finite field of q q elements equipped with a level M M structure. The edges of X ℓ q ​ ( M ) X_{\ell}^{q}(M) are given by ℓ \ell -isogenies. Fix a positive integer N N and write M = p n ​ N M=p^{n}N . We are interested in when the connected components of X ℓ q ​ ( p n ​ N ) X_{\ell}^{q}(p^{n}N) give rise to a tower of Galois coverings as n n varies. We show that only in the supersingular case we do get a tower of Galois coverings. We also study similar towers of isogeny graphs given by oriented supersingular curves, as introduced by Colò–Kohel, enhanced with a level structure.

Introduction

Let
p
and
\ell
be distinct prime numbers. Let
N
be a positive integer that is coprime to
p\ell
. Let
G_{N}^{n}
be the directed graph whose vertices are isomorphism classes
(E,P)
, where

is an ordinary elliptic curve defined over a fixed finite field of characteristic
p
and
P
is a point of order
p^{n}N
on
E
, and the edges of
G_{N}^{n}
are given by
\ell
-isogenies. In
[
LM24a
]
, we studied the tower of graph coverings

G_{N}^{0}\leftarrow G_{N}^{1}\leftarrow G_{N}^{2}\leftarrow\cdots\leftarrow G_{N}^{n}\leftarrow\cdots.

Note that the degree of the covering
G_{N}^{n+1}/G_{N}^{n}
is
p
thanks to the ordinarity condition. We studied the undirected graphs
\tilde{G}_{N}^{n}
obtained from
G_{N}^{n}
by forgetting the directions of the edges of
G_{N}^{n}
. We showed that if

is an elliptic curve representing a non-isolated vertex of
G_{1}^{0}
and
\tilde{{\mathcal{G}}}_{N}^{m}
is a connected component of
{\mathcal{G}}_{N}^{m}
containing a vertex arising from

such that

\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{N}^{m_{0}}\leftarrow\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{N}^{m_{0}+1}\leftarrow\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{N}^{m_{0}+2}\leftarrow\cdots\leftarrow\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{N}^{m_{0}+n}\leftarrow\cdots

is an abelian

-tower in the sense of Vallières and McGown-Vallières
[
Val21
,
MV23
,
MV24
]
, i.e. the covering

is Galois, whose Galois group is isomorphic to the cyclic group
\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}
for all

. Such towers exhibit properties that resemble
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-towers of number fields studied in classical Iwasawa theory
[
Iwa69
,
Iwa73
]
. For example, similar to the class numbers of number fields inside a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower, the number of spanning trees of

can be described explicitly for

[
MV24
,
LV23
]
).

In this article, we study towers of isogeny graphs that result in non-commutative Galois coverings under appropriate hypotheses. Throughout, we fix a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, where
q
is a power of a prime number
r
that is coprime to
p\ell
. We say that two elliptic curves defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
are equivalent if they are isomorphic over
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
. We fix a set of representatives of the equivalence classes of elliptic curves defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, which we denote by
S
.

Given two elliptic curves
E,E^{\prime}\in S
, an isogeny
\phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E\to E^{\prime}
is a non-constant morphism of varieties that sends the point at infinity of
E
to the point at infinity of
E^{\prime}
. Recall that all isogenies are group homomorphisms. We say that
\phi
is an
\ell
-isogeny if its degree is equal to
\ell
. Since
\ell\nmid q
, this is equivalent to
\ker(\phi)
containing
\ell
elements in
E(\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}})
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\ell,r
be distinct prime numbers,
q
a power of
r
and
M
a positive integer coprime to
q\ell
. We define the directed graph
X_{\ell}^{q}(M)
whose vertices are given by triples
(E,Q_{1},Q_{2})
, where
E\in S
and
\{Q_{1},Q_{2}\}
is a basis of
E[M]\cong(\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})^{2}
. The edges of
X_{\ell}^{q}(M)
are given by
\ell
-isogenies, i.e. the set of edges from
(E,Q_{1},Q_{2})
to
(E^{\prime},Q_{1}^{\prime},Q_{2}^{\prime})
is the set of
\ell
-isogenies
\phi\colon E\to E^{\prime}
such that
\phi(Q_{1})=Q_{1}^{\prime}
and
\phi(Q_{2})=Q_{2}^{\prime}
.

Note that we allow isogenies and
M
-torsion points to be defined over
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
, while the elliptic curves are defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We fix one representative
E
for each class in
S
. In particular, the vertices
(E,Q_{1},Q_{2})
and
(E,-Q_{1},-Q_{2})
are considered to be distinct if

. Furthermore,
\phi
and
-\phi
give rise to two distinct edges. If
\phi
induces an edge from
(E,Q_{1},Q_{2})
to
(E^{\prime},Q^{\prime}_{1},Q^{\prime}_{2})
, then
-\phi
induces an edge from
(E,Q_{1},Q_{2})
to
(E,-Q^{\prime}_{1},-Q^{\prime}_{2})
.

The basis
\{Q_{1},Q_{2}\}
is called a
\Gamma(M)
-level structure on the elliptic curve
E
. Fix a positive integer
N
that is coprime to
pq\ell
. We write
M=p^{n}N
for some integer
n\geq 0
.
For

, there exist unique
R_{i}\in E[N]
and
S_{i}\in E[p^{n}]
such that

. Furthermore,
\{R_{1},R_{2}\}
is a
\Gamma(N)
-level structure and
\{S_{1},S_{2}\}
is a
\Gamma(p^{n})
-level structure on
E
. We shall denote a vertex of
X_{\ell}^{q}(p^{n}N)
by
(E,R_{1},R_{2},P,Q)
with
R_{1},R_{2}\in E[N]
and

such that

is a
\Gamma(p^{n}N)
-level structure on

. When

, we can discard

and

, and simply write
(E,R_{1},R_{2})
for a vertex in

.

The map
(E,R_{1},R_{2},P,Q)\mapsto(E,R_{1},R_{2},pP,pQ)
induces a graph covering
X_{\ell}^{q}(p^{n+1}N)/X_{\ell}^{q}(p^{n}N)
(see Corollary
2.9
).
We are interested in when the connected components of
X_{\ell}^{q}(p^{n}N)
give rise to a tower of Galois coverings as
n
varies.
Let
X
be a connected component of
X_{\ell}^{q}(N)
. For

, let
X_{n}
be the pre-image of

under the covering
X_{\ell}^{q}(p^{n}N)/X_{\ell}^{q}(N)
.
Note that if

and

are isogenous. In particular, either both curves are ordinary or both are supersingular. In other words, the ordinary-supersingular dichotomy is constant for all vertices of

as

or

. It turns out that the coverings

exhibit different properties depending on this dichotomy. In particular, we prove:

Theorem A

(Corollary
3.5
, Theorem
3.6
)
.

Let

be an ordinary connected component of

. For

, let

be the pre-image of
X
under the covering
X_{\ell}^{q}(p^{n}N)/X_{\ell}^{q}(N)
.

(i)

The covering

is not Galois when
n
is sufficiently large.

(ii)

Let
X_{0}^{\prime}=X
. For each

, let
X_{n}^{\prime}
be a connected component of the pre-image of
X_{n-1}^{\prime}
in

. Then

is a Galois covering. Furthermore,
\displaystyle\varprojlim_{n}\Gal(X_{n}/X)
is an abelian
p
-adic Lie group of dimension
1
or
2
.

The reason why

is not Galois is that the number of connected components of

grows rapidly in

; see Theorem
3.4
for a precise description.
In the supersingular case, we show:

Theorem B

(Corollaries
4.4
,
4.6
,
4.7
and Remarks
4.5
,
4.8
)
.

Suppose that

. Let

be a supersingular connected component of

. For

, let

be the pre-image of

(i)

The covering

if and only if

. When this occurs, the Galois group of the covering

is isomorphic to

.

(ii)

There exists an integer

such that the number of connected components in

stabilizes. Let

be a connected component of

In particular, in the context of Theorem
B
,

X_{1}\leftarrow X_{2}\leftarrow\cdots\leftarrow X_{n}\leftarrow\cdots

gives an explicit example that fits into the framework of non-commutative Iwasawa of graph coverings developed in
[
KM23
]
. We remark that Roda

]
proved a simple necessary and sufficient condition for the connectivity of

. We shall review this result in the main body of the article (see Theorem

In non-commutative Iwasawa theory of elliptic curves studied in
[
CFK+05
]
, the authors conjectured that the Pontryagin dual of the
p
-primary Selmer group of a
p
-ordinary elliptic curve over a
p
-adic Lie extension
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
of a number field

that contains the cyclotomic

-extension should satisfy the so-called
\mathfrak{M}_{H}(G)
-property. Here,

and

is a subgroup of

such that

is the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
\mathcal{K}_{0}
. The analogue of the
\mathfrak{M}_{H}(G)
-property in the context of graph coverings has recently been studied in
[
KM23
]
. It is therefore natural to seek an appropriate quotient inside the tower given by Theorem

that would play the role of the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of a number field.

Indeed, the graph

admits a natural quotient

obtained by the map

(E,R_{2},R_{2},P,Q)\mapsto(E,R_{1},R_{2},\langle P,Q\rangle),

where

is the Weil pairing (see Definition

). We show that these graphs give rise to an abelian

-tower, similar to the isogeny graphs studied in
[

]
. In particular, we prove:

Theorem C

(Corollary

)
.

There exists an integer

such that if

is a connected component of

and

is the pre-image of

Note that unlike Theorems

and

is independent of whether the chosen connected component is ordinary or supersingular.

In §

, we study isogeny graphs that arise from

(Theorems

,

and

\ell

We conclude this introduction by highlighting the growing interest in isogeny graphs of elliptic curves with level structures in recent years. One such example is Goren–Kassaei’s work
[

]
that delved into the dynamics of Hecke operators on modular curves utilizing such graphs with

[

]
investigated the implications of isogeny graphs for equivalence classes of supersingular elliptic curves with

2.
Voltage assignment and graph covering

In this section, we show that the isogeny graphs with level structures introduced in Definition
1.1
can be naturally realized as voltage graphs. Our discussion can be regarded as a natural extension of
[
LM24a
, Appendix A]
to the non-commutative setting.

2.1.
Generalities of voltage graphs

Let us first recall the definition of a voltage assignment.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a directed graph.

The set of vertices and the set of edges of
X
are denoted by
V(X)
and
\mathbb{E}(X)
, respectively.

Let
(G,\cdot)
a group. A function
\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{E}(X)\rightarrow G
is called a
G
-valued voltage assignment
on
X
.

To each voltage assignment
\alpha
given as in ii), we define the
derived graph

X(G,\alpha)
to be the graph whose vertices and edges are given by
V(X)\times G
and
\mathbb{E}(X)\times G
, respectively. If
(e,\sigma)\in\mathbb{E}(X)\times G
, it links
(s,\sigma)
to
(t,\sigma\cdot\alpha(e))
, where
e
is an edge in
X
that links
s
to
t
.

A graph arises from a voltage assignment is called a
voltage graph
.

Let
G
,
X
and
\alpha
be given as in Definition
2.1
. There is a natural graph covering
X(G,\alpha)/X
given by

V(X(G,\alpha))\ni(v,\sigma)\mapsto v,\quad\mathbb{E}(X(G,\alpha))\ni(e,\sigma)\mapsto e.

There is a natural left action by
G
on
X(G,\alpha)
, given by
g\cdot(v,\sigma)=(v,g\cdot\sigma)
and
g\cdot(e,\sigma)=(e,g\cdot\sigma)
for
g,\sigma\in G
,
v\in V(X)
and
e\in\mathbb{E}(X)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
X
,
G
and
\alpha
be as in Definition
2.1
. Let
H
be a normal subgroup of
G
. We write
\tilde{\alpha}
for the composition of
\alpha
with the projection map
G\to G/H
. Then
X(G/H,\tilde{\alpha})
is an intermediate covering of
X(G,\alpha)/X
.

Proof.

Let
\pi_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X(G,\alpha)\to X(G/H,\tilde{\alpha})
be the map given by

V(X(G,\alpha))\ni(v,\sigma)\mapsto(v,\sigma H),\quad\mathbb{E}(X(G,\alpha))\ni(e,\sigma)\mapsto(e,\sigma H).

Let
\pi_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X(G/H,\tilde{\alpha})\to X
and
\pi_{3}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X(G,\alpha)\to X
be the natural coverings.
It can be verified by direct calculations that
\pi_{1}
is a graph covering and that
\pi_{3}=\pi_{2}\circ\pi_{1}
. Hence, the lemma follows.
∎

Definition 2.3
.

Let
X
be a directed graph.

We write
X^{\prime}
for the undirected graph obtained from
X
by ignoring the directions of the edges of
X
. The natural map from
X
to
X^{\prime}
is called the
forgetful map
.

We say that
X
is
connected
if
X^{\prime}
is connected.

A
connected component
of
X
is the pre-image of a connected component of
X^{\prime}
in
X
under the forgetful map.

If
Y/X
is a covering of directed graph with the projection map
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}Y\to X
, the group of
deck transformations
of
Y/X
, denoted by
\mathrm{Deck}(Y/X)
, is the group of graph automorphisms
\sigma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}Y\to Y
such that
\pi\circ\sigma=\pi
.

We call a covering
Y/X
of directed graphs
d
-sheeted
if
d
is a positive integer such that each element of
V(X)
has
d
pre-images in
V(Y)
.

We say that a covering
Y/X
of directed graphs it is
Galois
if
Y/X
is a
d
-sheeted covering and
|\textup{Deck}(Y/X)|=d
.

The notion of connectedness here is sometimes referred to as ”weakly connected”, as opposed to ”strongly connected”, where one requires the existence of a path between any two given vertices. We shall see in Remark
2.12
that these two notions coincide for the graphs of interest in this paper.

The following result allows us to determine whether a graph covering arising from a voltage assignment is Galois.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
X
be a directed graph,
G
a finite group equipped with a
G
-valued voltage assignment
\alpha
on
X
. Let
Y
denote the derived graph
X(G,\alpha)
.

If
X
is connected, then the natural action of
G
on
Y
permutes transitively the connected components of
Y
.

If
Y
is connected, then
Y/X
is a Galois covering. Furthermore, its Galois group is isomorphic to
G
.

Proof.

This follows from
[
Gon22
, Theorem 2.10]
and
[
Gon21
, §2.3]
.∎

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
X
,
Y
and
G
be as in Proposition
2.4
. Let
d
be the number of connected components of
Y
. If
d!>|G|
, then
Y/X
is not Galois.

Proof.

Let
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
be two connected components of
Y
. Proposition
2.4
i) tells us that there exists
g\in G
such that

V(Y_{1})\ni(v,\sigma)\mapsto(v,g\cdot\sigma),\quad\mathbb{E}(Y_{1})\ni(e,\sigma)\mapsto(e,g\cdot\sigma)

induces an isomorphism of graphs from
Y_{1}
to
Y_{2}
. Thus, any permutation of the connected components of
Y
gives rise to an element of
\mathrm{Deck}(Y/X)
. Since there are
d!
such permutations and the degree of the covering is

, the covering is not Galois if
d!>|G|
.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

Let
G
be a finite group. In the course of this article, we frequently study the number of connected components of

v\in V(X)
and study the set

\left\{(v,g)\in V(X(G,\alpha))\mid(v,g)\textup{ lies in the same connected component as $(v,1)$}\right\}.

Let
d_{v}
be the cardinality of this set. As explained in the proof of Corollary
2.5
, all connected components of
X(G,\alpha)
are isomorphic to each other. Therefore, the number of connected components of
X(G,\alpha)
is given by
\frac{|G|}{d_{v}}
.

2.2.
Realizing isogeny graphs as voltage graphs

The goal of this section is to realize the graph
X_{\ell}^{q}(p^{n}N)
as a voltage graph arising from a
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})
-valued voltage assignment on
X
, similar to the discussion in
[
LM24a
, Appendix A]
.

For each elliptic curve
E\in S
, we fix a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-basis
\{s_{E},t_{E}\}
of the Tate module
T_{p}(E)
.
Let
e\in\mathbb{E}(X_{\ell}^{q}(N))
with source
(E,R_{1},R_{2})
and target
(E^{\prime},R_{1}^{\prime},R_{2}^{\prime})
. It arises from some
\ell
-isogeny
\phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E\rightarrow E^{\prime}
. Since
p
and
\ell
are coprime,
\phi
induces a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-isomorphism

\phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}T_{p}(E)\rightarrow T_{p}(E^{\prime}).

This allows us to define the following voltage assignment:

Definition 2.7
.

For any given
e\in\mathbb{E}(X_{\ell}^{q}(N))
arising from an
\ell
-isogeny
\phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E\rightarrow E^{\prime}
, we define
g_{e}\in\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
to be the transpose of the matrix of
\phi
with respect to our chosen bases
\{s_{E},t_{E}\}
and
\{s_{E^{\prime}},t_{E^{\prime}}\}
so that the following equation holds

\begin{pmatrix}\phi(s_{E})\\
\phi(t_{E})\end{pmatrix}=g_{e}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}s_{E^{\prime}}\\
t_{E^{\prime}}\end{pmatrix}.

For an integer
n\geq 0
, we define
\alpha_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{E}(X_{\ell}^{q}(N))\rightarrow\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})
to be the voltage assignment sending
e
to the image of
g_{e}
under the natural projection
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\rightarrow\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})
.

Let
(E,R_{1},R_{2},P,Q)\in V(X_{\ell}^{q}(p^{n}N))
. Since
\{P,Q\}
is a basis of
E[p^{n}]
, there exists a unique
\sigma\in\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})
such that

(2.1)

\begin{pmatrix}P\\
Q\end{pmatrix}=\sigma\cdot\begin{pmatrix}\overline{s}_{E}\\
\overline{t}_{E}\end{pmatrix},

where
\overline{s}_{E}
and
\overline{t}_{E}
denote the images of
s_{E}
and
t_{E}
in
E[p^{n}]
, respectively. This induces a well-defined injective map

\displaystyle\Phi_{n}\colon V(X_{\ell}^{q}(p^{n}N))

\displaystyle\to V\left(X(\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}),\alpha_{n})\right)

\displaystyle(E,R_{1},R_{2},P,Q)

\displaystyle\mapsto\big((E,R_{1},R_{2}),\sigma\big).

Theorem 2.8
.

For all
n\geq 0
, the map
\Phi_{n}
defines an isomorphism of graphs

\Phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X_{\ell}^{q}(p^{n}N)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}X(\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}),\alpha_{n}).

Proof.

To simplify notation, we write
Y_{n}=X(\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}),\alpha_{n})
,
Z_{n}=X_{\ell}^{q}(p^{n}N)
and
G_{n}=\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})
in this proof.

By definition,
\Phi_{n}
is injective on
V(Z_{n})
. Given any
\left((E,R_{1},R_{2}),\sigma\right)\in V(Y_{n})
, we may define a basis of
E[p^{n}]
via (
2.1
). Hence,
\Phi
is a bijection from
V(Z_{n})
to
V(Y_{n})
.

It remains to show that the bijection
\Phi_{n}
respects the edges of the two graphs. That is, an isogeny
\phi
induces an edge in
Z_{n}
from
v
to
w
if and only if it induces an edge in
Y_{n}
from
\Phi_{n}(v)
to
\Phi_{n}(w)
. Suppose that
e\in\mathbb{E}(Z_{0})
with
(E,R_{1},R_{2})
and
(E^{\prime},R^{\prime}_{1},R^{\prime}_{2})
as the source and target, respectively. Let
\phi
be the corresponding
\ell
-isogeny inducing
e
. The same isogeny gives rise to an edge from
(E,R_{1},R_{2},P,Q)
to
(E^{\prime},R^{\prime}_{1},R^{\prime}_{2},P^{\prime},Q^{\prime})
in
Z_{n}
if and only if

(2.2)

\phi(P)=P^{\prime}\quad\text{and}\quad\phi(Q)=Q^{\prime}.

Let us write

\displaystyle\Phi_{n}((E,R_{1},R_{2},P,Q))

\displaystyle=((E,R_{1},R_{2}),\sigma),

\displaystyle\Phi_{n}((E^{\prime},R^{\prime}_{1},R^{\prime}_{2},P^{\prime},Q^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=((E^{\prime},R^{\prime}_{1},R^{\prime}_{2}),\sigma^{\prime}).

A direct calculation shows that (
2.2
) is equivalent to the equation

\sigma\alpha_{n}(e)=\sigma^{\prime},

which is precisely the condition for there to be an edge going from
((E,R_{1},R_{2}),\sigma)
to
((E^{\prime},R^{\prime}_{1},R^{\prime}_{2}),\sigma^{\prime})
in
Y_{n}
. This shows that
\Phi_{n}
respects the edges of
Y_{n}
and
Z_{n}
, as desired.
∎

Corollary 2.9
.

Let
n\geq 1
be an integer. The map

\displaystyle V(X_{\ell}^{q}(p^{n}N))

\displaystyle\rightarrow V(X_{\ell}^{q}(p^{n-1}N)),

\displaystyle(E,R_{1},R_{2},P,Q)

\displaystyle\mapsto(E,R_{1},R_{2},pP,pQ)

induces a graph covering
X_{n}\rightarrow X_{n-1}
.

Proof.

Let
G_{n}=\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})
and let
\alpha_{n}
be the voltage assignment on
X=X_{\ell}^{q}(N)
given in Definition
2.7
. Let
\pi_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G_{n}\rightarrow G_{n-1}
be the natural projection map and
N_{n}=\ker(\pi_{n})
. Let
\tilde{\alpha_{n}}=\pi_{n}\circ\alpha_{n}
. Then Lemma
2.2
tells us that there is an intermediate graph covering
X(G_{n},\alpha_{n})/X(G_{n}/N_{n},\tilde{\alpha}_{n})
of
X(G_{n},\alpha_{n})/X
.

Let
\Theta_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}V(X(G_{n},\alpha_{n}))\rightarrow V(X_{\ell}^{q}(p^{n}N))
be the inverse of
\Phi_{n}
.
Since
G_{n-1}\cong G_{n}/N_{n}
and
\tilde{\alpha}_{n}=\alpha_{n-1}
, we may identify
X(G_{n}/N_{n},\tilde{\alpha}_{n})
with
X(G_{n-1},\alpha_{n-1})
, and therefore with
X_{\ell}^{q}(p^{n-1}N)
.
Finally, it follows from the definition of the Tate module that

\Theta_{n-1}\circ\pi_{n}=[p]\circ\Theta_{n},

which concludes the proof.
∎

Corollary 2.10
.

Let
X
be a connected component of
X_{\ell}^{q}(N)
(in the sense of Definition
2.3
). For an integer
n\geq 1
, let
X_{n}
denote the pre-image of
X
in

under the natural projection map. If

is connected, the covering

This follows directly from Proposition
2.4
ii).
∎

We conclude this section with a couple of remarks on the connected components of
X_{\ell}^{q}(p^{n}N)
, which will be useful for subsequent sections.

Remark 2.11
.

The graph
X_{\ell}^{q}(p^{n}N)
contains multiple edges if and only if there are two distinct
\ell
-isogenies
\phi,\psi\colon E\to E^{\prime}
, which coincide on
E[p^{n}N]
. Given any two
\ell
-isogenies
\phi
and
\psi
, we have
\deg(\phi-\psi)\leq 4\ell
. Thus,
\ker(\phi-\psi)
does not contain any elements of order
p^{n}N
whenever
p^{n}N>4\ell
. In other words,
X_{\ell}^{q}(p^{n}N)
does not contain multiple edges if
n
is sufficiently large.

Remark 2.12
.

Let
X_{n}
be a connected component of
X_{\ell}^{q}(p^{n}N)
. Let
e\in\mathbb{E}(X_{n})
with
v=(E,R_{1},R_{2},P,Q)
and
w=(E^{\prime},R^{\prime}_{1},R^{\prime}_{2},P^{\prime},Q^{\prime})
as the source and target, respectively. Then
e
arises from an
\ell
-isogeny
\phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E\rightarrow E^{\prime}
such that
\phi(U)=U^{\prime}
for
U\in\{R_{1},R_{2},P,Q\}
. The dual isogeny
\hat{\phi}
gives rise to an edge in
X_{n}
from
w
to
(E,\ell R_{1},\ell R_{2},\ell P,\ell Q)
. Let
d
be the order of
[\ell]
as an automorphism on
E[p^{n}N]
. In particular,
\ell^{d}U=U
for all
U\in\{R_{1},R_{2},P,Q\}
. On repeatedly applying
\phi
and
\hat{\phi}
alternatively, we obtain a path of the form

\displaystyle w\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\hat{\phi}}}{{\to}}

\displaystyle(E,\ell R_{1},\ell R_{2},\ell P,\ell Q)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi}}{{\to}}(E^{\prime},\ell R_{1}^{\prime},\ell R_{2}^{\prime},\ell P^{\prime},\ell Q^{\prime})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\hat{\phi}}}{{\to}}

\displaystyle(E,\ell^{2}R_{1},\ell^{2}R_{2},\ell^{2}P,\ell^{2}Q)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi}}{{\to}}\cdots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\hat{\phi}}}{{\to}}(E,\ell^{d}R_{1},\ell^{d}R_{2},\ell^{d}P,\ell^{d}Q)=v.

In other words, each directed edge in
X_{n}
can be ”reversed” via a path in the opposite direction. Hence,
X_{n}
is a strongly connected directed graph.

Definition 2.13
.

An isogeny of elliptic curves
\phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E\to E^{\prime}
is said to be an
\ell
-power isogeny
if the degree of
\phi
is a power of
\ell
. If
E=E^{\prime}
, we say that
\phi
is an
\ell
-power endomorphism
on
E
.

Remark 2.14
.

Let
v=(E,R_{1},R_{2},P,Q)
and
w=(E^{\prime},R^{\prime}_{1},R^{\prime}_{2},P^{\prime},Q^{\prime})
be two vertices of
X_{\ell}^{q}(p^{n}N)
. Since every
\ell
-power isogeny can be realized as the composition of
\ell
-isogenies, it follows from the definition that there is a path from
v
to
w
if and only if there exists an
\ell
-power isogeny
\phi\colon E\to E^{\prime}
such that
\phi(U)=U^{\prime}
for
U\in\{R_{1},R_{2},P,Q\}
.

3.
The ordinary case

The goal of this section is to prove Theorem
A
stated in the introduction.
We fix an ordinary connected component
X
of
X_{\ell}^{q}(N)
and define
X_{n}
to be the pre-image of
X
in
X_{\ell}^{q}(p^{n}N)
under the natural projection map. As explained in the introduction, all elliptic curves that give rise to a vertex in
X_{n}
are ordinary. Furthermore, since these curves are isogenous to each other, their endomorphism rings are orders in the same imaginary quadratic field, which we denote by
K
. We refer to
K
as the CM field of
X
. Note that the prime
r
is split in
K
.

We begin with the following preliminary lemmas.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
n\geq 1
be an integer. The order of the group
\textup{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})
is
p^{4(n-1)}(p^{2}-1)(p^{2}-p)
.

Proof.

Let
G_{n}=\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})
. Let
\pi_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G_{n}\rightarrow G_{1}
be the group homomorphism induced by the projection
\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
. Then
\pi_{n}
is surjective with

\ker\pi_{n}=I_{2}+pM_{2\times 2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}).

Thus,
|\ker(\pi_{n})|=p^{4(n-1)}
, and
|G_{n}|=p^{4(n-1)}|G_{1}|
. It is a standard fact that
|G_{1}|=(p^{2}-1)(p^{2}-p)
. Hence the lemma follows.
∎

Lemma 3.2

(
[
EIR10
, Theorem 4]
,
[
ECIR11
, Theorems 2 and 3]
)
.

Let
A
be the ring of integers of a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, and let
\varpi
be a uniformizer of
A
. Let
f
and
e
be the degree of inertia and the degree of ramification of the extension, respectively. Let
G_{a}
be the multiplicative group
(A/\varpi^{a})^{\times}
, where
a\geq 1
is an integer.

•

If
e=1
and
p\neq 2
,

G_{a}\cong\mathbb{Z}/(p^{f}-1)\mathbb{Z}\times(\mathbb{Z}/p^{a-1}\mathbb{Z})^{f},

•

If
e=1
,
p=2
and
a\geq 3
,

G_{a}\cong\mathbb{Z}/(2^{f}-1)\mathbb{Z}\times(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\times\mathbb{Z}/2^{a-2}\mathbb{Z}\times(\mathbb{Z}/2^{a-1}\mathbb{Z})^{f-1}.

•

If
e=2
,
p\neq 2
and
a\geq 3
is odd,

G_{a}\cong\mathbb{Z}/(p^{f}-1)\mathbb{Z}\times(\mathbb{Z}/p^{(a-1)/2}\mathbb{Z})^{2f}.

•

If
e=2
,
p\neq 2
and
a\geq 4
is even,

G_{a}\cong\mathbb{Z}/(p^{f}-1)\mathbb{Z}\times(\mathbb{Z}/p^{a/2-1}\mathbb{Z})^{f}\times(\mathbb{Z}/p^{a/2}\mathbb{Z})^{f}.

•

If
e=2
,
p=2
and
a>4
,

G_{a}\cong\mathbb{Z}/(2^{f}-1)\mathbb{Z}\times(\mathbb{Z}/2^{h_{1}}\mathbb{Z})^{f}\times(\mathbb{Z}/2^{h_{2}})^{f},

where
h_{1}=\lceil(a-1)/2\rceil
and
h_{2}=\lceil(a-2)/2\rceil
.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
A
be the ring of integers of a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with ramification index
e\in\{1,2\}
and degree of inertia
f
. Let
n\geq 1
be an integer, and let
\alpha\in A^{\times}
. There exists a constant
d\leq p^{f}-1
such that for
n
sufficiently large, the order of the image of
\alpha
in
(A/p^{n})^{\times}
is equal to
dp^{n-1}
(resp.
dp^{n}
) if
e=1
(resp.
e=2
).

Proof.

We consider the case
p\neq 2
. The topological group
A^{\times}
is isomorphic to
C\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{Z}/(p^{f}-1)\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\oplus ef}
. Let
g_{1},\ldots,g_{ef}\in A^{\times}
be the elements that correspond to the elements
(0,1,0,\dots,0)
,
\dots
,
(0,0,\dots,0,1)
in
C
.

We have
\varpi^{e}A=pA
. By Lemma
3.2
, the order of
g_{i}
in
(A/p^{n})^{\times}
is of the form
p^{t_{n,i}}
, where
t_{n,i}=n-1
(resp.
t_{n,i}\in\{n,n-1\}
) if
e=1
(resp.
e=2
). Let
(\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{ef})
be the image of
\alpha
in
C
. Let
k_{i}=\ord_{{g_{i}}}(\alpha_{i})
for
i=1,\ldots,ef
, and let
d
be the order of
\alpha_{0}
(as an element in
\mathbb{Z}/(p^{f}-1)\mathbb{Z}
). Then, the order of the image of
\alpha
in
(A/\varpi^{n})^{\times}
is given by

\mathrm{\ell cm}(d,p^{t_{n,1}-k_{1}},\dots,p^{t_{n,ef}-k_{ef}}),

from which the corollary follows. The case
p=2
can be shown in a similar way.∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
X
be an ordinary connected component of
X_{\ell}^{q}(N)
, and let
K
be the CM field of
X
. For each integer
n\geq 1
, let
X_{n}
be the pre-image of
X
in
X_{\ell}^{q}(p^{n}N)
. For
n
sufficiently large, the number of connected components in
X_{n}
is given by
cp^{2(n-1)}
(resp.
cp^{3(n-1)}
) if
p
is split (resp. non-split) in
K
, where
c\geq(p^{2}-p)
is a constant independent of
n
.

Proof.

The degree of the covering
X_{\ell}^{q}(p^{n}N)/X_{\ell}^{q}(N)
equals
|\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})|
. As explained in Remark
2.6
, the number of connected components of
X_{n}
is given by
|\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})|
divided by the number of pre-images of a fixed
(E,R_{1},R_{2})\in V(X)
lying in the same connected component of
X_{n}
.

As all curves with complex multiplication by an order in
K
are isogenous, we may assume that
\textup{End}(E)\cong\mathcal{O}
is an order such that
[\mathcal{O}_{K}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{O}]
is not divisible by
\ell
. In particular, the splitting behaviour of
\ell
in
\mathcal{O}
is the same as that in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
.

Let
(E,R_{1},R_{2},P,Q)
and
(E,R_{1},R_{2},P^{\prime},Q^{\prime})
be two pre-images of
(E,R_{1},R_{2})
in
V(X_{n})
. By Remark
2.14
, they lie in the same connected component of
X_{n}
if and only if there is an
\ell
-power isogeny
\phi\colon E\to E
such that
\phi(R_{1})=R_{1}
,
\phi(R_{2})=R_{2}
,
\phi(P)=P^{\prime}
and
\phi(Q)=Q^{\prime}
.

As discussed in
[
LM24a
, Remark 2.4]
, there exists an elliptic curve
\mathbf{E}
defined over some number field
L
with complex multiplication by
\mathcal{O}
such that
\mathbf{E}\pmod{\mathfrak{r}}=E
, where
\mathfrak{r}
is a prime ideal above
r
in
L
. There is a group isomorphism
E[p^{n}]\cong\mathbf{E}[p^{n}]
. As
\mathbf{E}(\mathbb{C})\cong\mathbb{C}/\mathcal{O}
, we have, furthermore
E[p^{n}]\cong\mathcal{O}/(p^{n})
. Therefore, under the isomorphism
\End(E)\cong\mathcal{O}
, the action of the set of aforementioned
\ell
-power isogenies on
E[p^{n}]
can be identified with a subgroup of
(\mathcal{O}/p^{n})^{\times}
.

Let
U
be the (multiplicative) set of elements in
\mathcal{O}
of
\ell
-power norm that act trivially on
E[N]
(since we are only interested in the endomorphisms that fix
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
).
Let
U_{n}
be the image of
U
in
(\mathcal{O}/p^{n})^{\times}
. The number of connected components in
X_{n}
is then given by
|\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})|/|U_{n}|
. Lemma
3.2
tells us that
|(\mathcal{O}/p^{n})^{\times}|
is given by
(p-1)^{2}p^{2(n-1)}
,
(p^{2}-1)p^{2(n-1)}
or
(p-1)p^{2n-1}
, depending on whether
p
is split, inert, or ramified in
\mathcal{O}
. In all three cases, we have
|(\mathcal{O}/p^{n})^{\times}|\leq(p^{2}-1)p^{2(n-1)}
.

If
\ell
is split in
K
, then
U_{n}
is generated by two elements, and
|U_{n}|\leq|(\mathcal{O}/p^{n})^{\times}|\leq(p^{2}-1)p^{2(n-1)}
. If
\ell
is non-split in
K
, then
U_{n}
is cyclic. Thus, it follows from Corollary
3.3
that
|U_{n}|
is bounded above by
(p-1)p^{n-1}
,
(p^{2}-1)p^{n-1}
or
(p-1)p^{n}
, depending on whether
p
is split, inert or ramified in
\mathcal{O}
. In all cases, we have
|U_{n}|\leq(p^{2}-1)p^{n-1}
.
Furthermore, Corollary
3.3
tells us that the order of the generators grows linearly in
p^{n}
.
Hence, the theorem follows from Lemma
3.1
.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
A
(i).

Corollary 3.5
.

The covering
X_{n}/X
is not Galois for
n
sufficiently large.

Proof.

We prove that
X_{n}/X_{n-1}
is not Galois, which in turn implies that
X_{n}/X
is not Galois.

It follows from Theorem
3.4
that the number of connected components of
X_{n}
lying above
X_{n-1}
is at least
p^{2}
when
n
is sufficiently large. Since the degree of the covering
X_{n}/X_{n-1}
is
p^{4}
by Lemma
3.1
, it suffices to show that
(p^{2})!>p^{4}
in light of Corollary
2.5
.

Indeed,

(p^{2}-2)(p^{2}-3)>\frac{4}{3}\geq\frac{1}{1-\frac{1}{p^{2}}}.

This in turn gives

(p^{2})!\geq p^{2}(p^{2}-1)(p^{2}-2)(p^{2}-3)>p^{4},

as required.
∎

We conclude this section with the proof of Theorem
A
(ii):

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
X
be an ordinary connected component of
X_{\ell}^{q}(N)
, and let
K
be the CM field of
X
. Let
X_{0}^{\prime}=X
. For each integer
n\geq 1
, let
X_{n}^{\prime}
be a connected component of the pre-image of
X_{n-1}^{\prime}
in
X_{\ell}^{q}(p^{n}N)
. Then
X_{n}^{\prime}/X
is a Galois covering. Furthermore,
\displaystyle\varprojlim_{n}\Gal(X_{n}^{\prime}/X)
is an abelian
p
-adic Lie group of dimension 2 (resp. dimension 1) if
\ell
is split (resp. non-split) in
K
.

Proof.

It follows from Theorem
2.8
and Proposition
2.4
that
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})
acts transitively on the set of connected components of
X_{n}
.
Let
H_{n}\subset\textup{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})
be the stabilizer of
X^{\prime}_{n}
under this action. From the orbit-stabilizer formula it follows that
X_{n}^{\prime}/X
is a
|H_{n}|
-sheeted covering. Furthermore, the action of
H_{n}
on
X_{n}^{\prime}
gives rise to an injection
\Phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}H_{n}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Deck}(X_{n}^{\prime}/X)
.

Fix a vertex
v=(E,R_{1},R_{2},P,Q)\in V(X_{n}^{\prime})
.
Let
\sigma\in\textup{Deck}(X^{\prime}_{n}/X)
. Then
\sigma(v)
is of the form

. As

is connected, and

are induced by the same isogeny,

is uniquely defined by

. Therefore,

The action of

on

also sends

to a vertex of the form

can be realized as an

-power isogeny from

to
E
. This implies that

is isomorphic to the group

defined in the proof of Theorem

.
Note that

is a subgroup of

-adic Lie group of dimension

. If

is non-split in

, the subgroup

is generated by a non-torsion elements. This implies the assertion of the theorem.

Suppose that

is split in

be the ray class field over

∎

4.
The supersingular case

We now turn our attention to the supersingular case and prove Theorem

. Let

be the supersingular subgraph of

and define

as its pre-image in

for the reduced norm map on

is a maximal order in

Lemma 4.1

(Strong approximation)
.

Let

be the algebraic group such that

. Let

be an element such that

for all

and

for

. There exists an element

that is arbitrarily close to

at all

.

Proof.

See
[
GK21
, proof of Theorem 5.3.3]
.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
C\in\textup{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}N\mathbb{Z})
. There exists
x\in B^{1}
such that

•

x
is integral at all places
u\neq\ell
,

•

x
acts as
C
on

.

Proof.

For each prime
u\mid p^{n}N
, let
C_{u}
be an element of
\textup{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{u})
such that
C_{u}\equiv C\pmod{u^{\ord_{u}(p^{n}N)}}
.
Let
(y_{u})_{u}\in G(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{f})
be an element such that

for all

and that
y_{u}=C_{u}
for all
u\mid p^{n}N
. By Lemma

, there exists

that is arbitrarily close to
y
, except possibly at
\ell
. As

is integral at all places, this implies that
x
is also integral at all places, except possibly at

. As
\ell
is coprime to
p^{n}N
,
x
approximates the matrix
C
at all places

. Thus,
x
acts as

on

.
∎

Theorem 4.3

(Roda)
.

The number of connected components of
X^{\mathrm{ss}}_{n}
is given by the index
[(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\langle\ell\rangle]
. In particular, the number of connected components is uniformly bounded as

varies.

This theorem is the main result of
[
Rod19
, Section 3.3]
. We outline the proof here for the convenience of the reader.

Proof.

Let
(E,R_{1},R_{2},P,Q)
and
(E,R^{\prime}_{1},R^{\prime}_{2},P^{\prime},Q^{\prime})
be two vertices in
X^{\mathrm{ss}}_{n}
. There is a matrix
C
in
\textup{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}N\mathbb{Z})
such that

C\begin{pmatrix}R_{1}+P\\
R_{2}+Q\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}R^{\prime}_{1}+P^{\prime}\\
R^{\prime}_{2}+Q^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}.

In light of Remark
2.14
, we determine when
C
is induced by an
\ell
-power endomorphism.

We claim that
C\in\textup{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}N\mathbb{Z})
induces an
\ell
-power endomorphism on
E
if and only if
\det(C)\in\langle\ell\rangle
as an element in
(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. The only if part is clear. We show the if part below.

First, assume that
C\in\textup{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}N\mathbb{Z})
. By Corollary
4.2
, there exists
x\in B
of norm one that is integral at all places except possibly at
\ell
and such that
x
acts as
C
on
E[p^{n}N]
.
Let
t
be a positive integer such that
\ell^{t}
acts trivially on
E[p^{n}N]
and such that
\ell^{t}x
is integral at all places. Then
y=\ell^{t}x
is an
\ell
-power endomorphism of
E
such that
y(P+R_{1})=P^{\prime}+R^{\prime}_{1}
and
y(Q+R_{2})=Q^{\prime}+R^{\prime}_{2}
.

Assume now that
\det(C)
is a power of
\ell
. By
[
GK21
, proof of Theorem 5.33]
,
E
admits an endomorphism
f
of degree
\ell^{t}
with
t
odd. As
[\ell]
has degree
\ell^{2}
, it follows that there is a matrix
D\in\textup{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}N\mathbb{Z})
induced by an
\ell
-power endomorphism such that
\det(C)=\det(D)
. Thus, to determine whether
C
is induced by an
\ell
-power endomorphism, it suffices to consider
C^{\prime}=CD^{-1}
. As
\det(C^{\prime})=1
, we are reduced to the previous case. This concludes the proof of our claim. Combined with Remark
2.6
, this implies that the number of connected components is given by
[(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\langle\ell\rangle]
.

Suppose that
p\neq 2
. There is a group isomorphism

(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\cong(\mathbb{Z}/Np\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\times(1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p})/(1+p^{n-1}\mathbb{Z}_{p}).

Let
\omega_{p}
be the Teichmüller character and write
\tilde{\ell}=\ell/\omega_{p}(\ell)\in 1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Let
\overline{\langle\tilde{\ell}\rangle}
denote the topological closure of
\langle\tilde{\ell}\rangle
within
1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Note that the multiplicative group
1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is isomorphic to the additive group
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. As
\omega_{p}(\ell)
is a
(p-1)
-st root of unity, it follows that
\tilde{\ell}\neq 1
. In particular, the index
[1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\overline{\langle\tilde{\ell}\rangle}]
is finite. Therefore, we deduce the inequality

[(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\langle\ell\rangle]\leq[(\mathbb{Z}/Np\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\langle\ell\rangle][1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\overline{\langle\tilde{\ell}\rangle}],

where the upper bound is independent of
n
. Thus, the number of connected components in
X_{n}^{\mathrm{ss}}
is bounded independently of
n
. The case
p=2
can be proved in a similar way.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

Assume that
p>2
. Let
T
be the set of primes
\ell
such that

is connected. Let
{\mathcal{T}}
be the set of elements

such that
\langle a\rangle=(\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. Then the Dirichlet density of

is equal to
|{\mathcal{T}}|/\varphi(p^{2}N)
, where

is the Euler totient function.

Proof.

Theorem
4.3
says that the graph

is connected if and only if
\ell
generates

for all

. This is the case if and only if
\ell
generates the cyclic group

, from which the corollary follows.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

Note that
{\mathcal{T}}
is non-empty if and only if

is cyclic. This is the case when

is odd,

is coprime to

and

is cyclic.
If

is odd, for

and

to be coprime, we must have

. In this case, the density given by Corollary

is

.
If

, the group

is not cyclic. In particular,

is not connected for any choice of

.

Corollary 4.6
.

Assume that

is connected for all

. Then
X_{n}^{\mathrm{ss}}/X^{\mathrm{ss}}
is a Galois covering whose Galois group isomorphic to

.

Proof.

This follows immediately from Proposition

ii).
∎

Finally, we show a supersingular analogue of Theorem

are not connected, we may still obtain a tower of Galois coverings.

Corollary 4.7
.

There exists an integer
m_{0}
such that the number of connected components of
X_{n}^{\mathrm{ss}}
stabilizes. Let
Z_{m_{0}}
be a connected component of
X^{\mathrm{ss}}_{m_{0}}
. For
n\geq m\geq m_{0}
, let
Z_{n}
be the inverse image of
Z_{m_{0}}
in
X_{n}
, and let
G_{n,m}\subset\textup{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})
be the subgroup of matrices congruent to
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\pmod{p^{m}}
. Then
Z_{n}/Z_{m}
is Galois with
\Gal(Z_{n}/Z_{m})\cong G_{n,m}
. In particular, the inverse limit
\displaystyle\varprojlim_{n\geq m_{0}}G_{n,m_{0}}
is an open subgroup of
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
.

Proof.

The existence of

is given by Theorem
4.3
.
Let

. Since the number of connected components stabilizes,
Z_{n}
and
Z_{m}
are connected. Furthermore,
Z_{n}/Z_{m}
is a
|G_{n,m}|
-sheeted covering and there is a natural embedding

\psi_{m}\colon G_{n,m}\hookrightarrow\textup{Deck}(Z_{n}/Z_{m}).

It remains to show that this embedding is indeed an isomorphism.

Let
\sigma\in\textup{Deck}(Z_{n}/Z_{m})
and let
v
be a vertex of
Z_{n}
. Let
\pi\colon Z_{n}\to Z_{m}
be the projection map. By definition

\pi(\sigma(v))=\pi(v).

The graph

is a subgraph of
X^{\mathrm{ss}}_{n}
. Therefore, there exists a vertex
v_{0}
of
X^{\mathrm{ss}}
and elements
h,h^{\prime}\in\textup{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})
such that
v=(v_{0},h)
and
\sigma(v)=(v_{0},h^{\prime})
. As
\pi(\sigma(v))=\pi(v)
, the elements
h
and
h^{\prime}
have the same image in
\textup{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}\mathbb{Z})
. In particular, there exists an element
g\in G_{n,m}
such that

, which is equivalent to

. We show in the following that the action of
g
on
Z_{n}
coincides with that of
\sigma
.

Let
w
be a vertex of
Z_{n}
that admits an edge whose source and target are
v
and
w
, respectively. We need to show that
\sigma(w)=g(w)
.
For each edge from
\pi(v)
to
\pi(w)
, we fix an isogeny
\phi
that induces this edge. Each such isogeny induces exactly one edge in
Z_{n}
from
\sigma(v)
to some

such that
\pi(w)=\pi(w^{\prime})
.
As
\sigma
and

are deck transformations of

, we have

. Since

is strongly connected by Remark

, this property propagates to all vertices of

. Thus, the actions of

and

agree as desired, proving that

is indeed an isomorphism.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

The inverse limit
\displaystyle\varprojlim_{n\geq m_{0}}G_{n,m_{0}}
is equal to

if and only if

, which is equivalent to

being odd and

•

.

5.
An abelian

-covering arising from quotients of

Let
\alpha_{n}
be the voltage assignment given in Definition

. Then

is a voltage assignment with values in

. Lemma

tells us that the derived graph

Recall from Section

that we fix a basis

of the Tate module

for each

is a fixed topological generator

of

for all

Let

denote the primitive

Definition 5.1
.

We define a directed graph

as follows.

are as before and

.
The edges of

Proposition 5.2
.

The graph

is isomorphic to

Proof.

By definition, there is a natural bijection

Thus, it remains to show that

respects the edges of the two graphs, as in the proof of Theorem

Let

be the fixed pre-image in

k

to a vertex

(note that the vertex

can depend on the edge

Theorem

allows us to identify

\displaystyle=\left\{(E,R_{1},R_{2},\sigma\tau)\mid\tau\in\textup{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})\right\},

According to the definition of

, which is uniformly bounded in

, as in the proof of Theorem

. Hence, the theorem follows.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem

.

Corollary 5.6
.

Let
m_{0}
be an integer such that the number of connected components of
Y_{\ell}^{q}(p^{n}N)
,
n\geq m_{0}
, stabilizes. Let

. Let
Y_{m}
be a connected component of
Y_{\ell}^{q}(p^{m}N)
and let

be its pre-image in
Y_{\ell}^{q}(p^{n}N)
. Then

is Galois and
\Gal(Y_{n}/Y_{m})
is isomorphic to

{\mathcal{G}}_{n,m}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left\{x\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}x\equiv 1\pmod{p^{m}}\right\}.

Proof.

Clearly,
Y_{n}/Y_{m}
is a
|{\mathcal{G}}_{n,m}|
-sheeted covering and
Y_{n}
is connected. Via the voltage assignment
\beta_{n}
, we see that there is an injective group homomorphism

{\mathcal{G}}_{n,m}\hookrightarrow\textup{Deck}(Y_{n}/Y_{m}).

It suffices to show that this map is surjective.

Let
\tau\in\mathrm{Deck}(Y_{n}/Y_{m})
and
v=(E,R_{1},R_{2},a)\in V(Y_{n})
. Let
v^{\prime}=(E,R_{1},R_{2},\sigma)\in X_{\ell}^{q}(p^{n}N)
be the fixed pre-image under the map
\Psi_{n}
given in Definition
5.1
. Then there exists
g\in{\mathcal{G}}_{n,m}
such that
gv=\tau(v)
. Let
g^{\prime}
be a lift of
g
to
\textup{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})
(so that
\det(g^{\prime})=g
). In particular,

\Psi_{n}(E,R_{1},R_{2},g^{\prime}\sigma)=gv.

Let
\phi
be an
\ell
-isogeny on
E
and let
w=\phi(v)
. Let

w^{\prime}=(\phi(E),\phi(R_{1}),\phi(R_{2}),\sigma\alpha_{n}(e_{\phi})),

where

is the edge induced by
\phi
. By definition,

w=\Psi_{n}(w^{\prime})=(\phi(E),\phi(R_{1}),\phi(R_{2}),a\beta_{n}(e_{\phi})).

By definition, we see that
\tau(w)=\phi(\tau(v))
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\tau(w)

\displaystyle=\phi(\tau(v))=\phi(gv)=\Psi_{n}(\phi(g^{\prime}v^{\prime}))=\Psi_{n}(\phi(E,R_{1},R_{2},g^{\prime}\sigma))

\displaystyle=\Psi_{n}(\phi(E),\phi(R_{1}),\phi(R_{2}),g^{\prime}\sigma\alpha_{n}(e_{\phi}))=(\phi(E),\phi(R_{1}),\phi(R_{2}),ga\beta_{n}(e_{\phi}))

\displaystyle=gw.

Thus,
\tau
is uniquely determined by
\tau(v)
. Hence,
{\mathcal{G}}_{n,m}\cong\textup{Deck}(Y_{n}/Y_{m})
, which concludes the proof.
∎

In particular, we see that

Y_{m_{0}}\leftarrow Y_{m_{0}+1}\leftarrow Y_{m_{0}+2}\leftarrow\cdots\leftarrow Y_{m_{0}+n}\leftarrow\cdots

form an abelian

-tower in the sense of
[

, Definition 4.1]
.

6.
Isogeny graphs of oriented elliptic curves and Volcano graphs

Let
M
be a positive integer coprime to
q\ell
. While the structure of an
\ell
-isogeny graph of ordinary elliptic curves equipped with a
\Gamma_{1}(M)
-level structure is related to the so-called (tectonic) volcano graphs (see
[
LM24a
, §4]
), analogous results for the supersingular counterpart are not available. In
[
CK20
]
, Colò–Kohel introduced the concept of orientations of supersingular elliptic curves. The authors in
[
Onu21
,
ACL+23
,
ACL+24
]
showed that the isogeny graph of supersingular elliptic curves equipped with an orientation (without any level structures) is a volcano graph, analogous to the ordinary counterpart studied in
[
Koh96
]
. In this section, we review the notion of orientations and describe the structure of the corresponding isogeny graphs enhanced with a
\Gamma(M)
-level structure for
M>2
.

Throughout this section, we fix an elliptic curve
E_{0}
and assume that one of the following two conditions holds:

(
H.ss
)

E_{0}/\mathbb{F}_{q}
is supersingular, where
q
is an even power of a prime number
r
(so that
\mathbb{F}_{r^{2}}\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}
) and
r\equiv 1\pmod{12}
.

(
H.ord
)

E_{0}/\mathbb{F}_{q}
is ordinary and
\textup{End}(E_{0})\otimes\mathbb{Q}
does not contain
i=\sqrt{-1}
and
\zeta_{3}
, where
\zeta_{3}
denotes a primitive
3
-rd root of unity.

In both settings, we have
\textup{Aut}(E_{0})=\{\pm 1\}
. Throughout,
B
denotes the quaternion algebra that is ramified only at
r
and
\infty
.

6.1.
The
\ell
-isogeny graph of oriented elliptic curves

We recall the notion of oriented elliptic curves and their isogeny graphs, originally introduced in
[
CK20
]
. We fix once and for all an imaginary quadratic field that admits an embedding

\iota\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}K\hookrightarrow\End(E_{0})\otimes\mathbb{Q}.

If (
(H.ss)
) holds,
r
does not split in
K
and
\iota
can be realized as an embedding of
K
into
B
. If (
(H.ord)
) holds,
\iota
is an isomorphism of fields.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
E/\mathbb{F}_{q}
be an elliptic curve that admits an embedding
\iota\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}K\hookrightarrow\End(E)\otimes\mathbb{Q}
.

We call the pair
(E,\iota)
an
oriented elliptic curve
.

We call an order
\mathcal{O}
of
K

primitive
for
(E,\iota)
, if
\iota(\mathcal{O})=\iota(K)\bigcap\textup{End}(E)
.

We say that
\mathcal{O}
is
\ell
-
primitive
if
\ell
does not divide
[\iota(K)\bigcap\textup{End}(E)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\iota(\mathcal{O})]
.

If
\varphi\colon E\to E^{\prime}
is an isogeny of elliptic curves, we define the orientation
\varphi_{*}\iota
on
E^{\prime}
by

\displaystyle\varphi_{*}\iota\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}K

\displaystyle\rightarrow\End(E^{\prime})\otimes\mathbb{Q},

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle\mapsto\frac{1}{\deg(\varphi)}{\varphi}\circ\iota(\alpha)\circ\widehat{\varphi}.

An
isogeny of oriented elliptic curves

\varphi\colon(E,\iota)\to(E^{\prime},\iota^{\prime})
is an isogeny of elliptic curves
\varphi\colon E^{\prime}\to E
such that
\varphi_{*}\iota=\iota^{\prime}
.

We say that
\ell
-isogenies
\phi,\psi\colon E\to E^{\prime}
are
equivalent isogenies
if they have the same kernel.

We say that
(E,\iota)
and
(E,\iota^{\prime})
are
equivalent oriented elliptic curves
if there exists an
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
-isomorphism of elliptic curves
\phi\colon E\to E^{\prime}
such that
\iota^{\prime}=\phi_{*}\iota
.

Remark 6.2
.

Let
\phi,\psi\colon E\to E^{\prime}
be equivalent isogenies. Then
(\phi(E),\phi_{*}\iota)
and
(\psi(E),\psi_{*}\iota)
are equivalent.

In the ordinary case, there are precisely two orientations on
E
, given by the field automorphisms of
K
. In addition, we can check that
\phi_{*}\iota=\iota
for every endomorphism
\phi
of
E
.

We are now ready to define the
\ell
-isogeny graph of oriented elliptic curves.

Definition 6.3
.

If (
(H.ss)
) (resp. (
(H.ord)
)) holds, we define
\mathcal{X}_{\ell}^{q}
as the directed graph whose vertices are equivalence classes of oriented elliptic curves
(E,\iota)
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
that are supersingular (resp. ordinary with
\End(E)\otimes\mathbb{Q}\cong K
); the edges are given by
\ell
-isogenies up to equivalence. We define
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}^{q}_{\ell}
as the undirected graph obtained from
\mathcal{X}_{\ell}^{q}
after identifying the edges induced by an isogeny and its dual.

The following notions will allow us to describe the structure of
\mathcal{X}_{\ell}^{q}
.

Definition 6.4
.

Let
\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}(E,\iota)\rightarrow(E^{\prime},\iota^{\prime})
be an isogeny of oriented elliptic curves. Assume that
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
are orders of
K
that are primitive for
(E,\iota)
and
(E^{\prime},\iota^{\prime})
, respectively. We say that
\varphi
is

•

horizontal
if
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
,

•

descending
if
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{O}
,

•

ascending
if
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\supset\mathcal{O}
.

The notion of horizontal, ascending, and descending isogenies allows us to define the depth of an oriented elliptic curve.

Definition 6.5
.

Let
(E,\iota)
be an oriented elliptic curve. Assume that
\mathcal{O}
is primitive with respect to
(E,\iota)
. We say that
(E,\iota)
is of depth zero if the conductor of
\mathcal{O}
is not divisible by
\ell
. We say that
(E,\iota)
is of depth
k
if there is a path consisting of
k

\ell
-isogenies that starts at a depth-zero oriented elliptic curve
(E^{\prime},\iota^{\prime})
and ends at
(E,\iota)
.

Finally, we introduce the following notions of graphs, which are the last ingredients in our description of
\mathcal{X}_{\ell}^{q}
.

.

An

•

Theorem 6.7

(

)
.

If (

) holds, the connected components of

Remark 6.8
.

The description given by Theorem

6.2.
The
\ell
-isogeny graph of oriented elliptic curve with level structure

The goal of this section is to study oriented elliptic curves enhanced with a
\Gamma(M)
-level structure. In other words, instead of pairs
(E,\iota)
, we consider tuples of the form
(E,\iota,R_{1},R_{2})
, where
\{R_{1},R_{2}\}
is a basis for
E[M]
.

Definition 6.9
.

Let
M
be a positive integer. An isogeny of oriented elliptic curves with a
\Gamma(M)
-level structure
\varphi\colon(E,\iota,R_{1},R_{2})\to(E^{\prime},\iota^{\prime},R^{\prime}_{1},R^{\prime}_{2})
is an isogeny of oriented elliptic curves
(E,\iota)\to(E^{\prime},\iota^{\prime})
such that
\varphi(R_{1})=R^{\prime}_{1}
and
\varphi(R_{2})=R^{\prime}_{2}
.

Definition 6.10
.

We fix a set of representatives
S^{\prime}
for the equivalence classes of oriented elliptic curves in
V(\mathcal{X}_{\ell}^{q})
.

Let
\mathcal{X}_{\ell}^{q}(M)
be the directed graph whose vertices are quadruples
(E,\iota,R_{1},R_{2})
, where
(E,\iota)\in S^{\prime}
and
\{R_{1},R_{2}\}
is a basis for
E[M]
, and such that the edges are given by
\ell
-isogenies defined as in Definition
6.9
.

Let
e\in\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{X}_{\ell}^{q}(M))
. We say that
e
is horizontal/descending/ascending if the isogeny giving rise to
e
is horizontal/descending/ascending.

Remark 6.11
.

If
\phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E\rightarrow E^{\prime}
is an
\ell
-isogeny of elliptic curves, the composition
\phi\circ[-1]=[-1]\circ\phi
is also an
\ell
-isogeny. Note that
[-1]
acts non-trivially on a basis of

for

. Thus, equivalent isogenies induce edges with different targets if

. For this reason, we have decided to distinguish the edges arising from
\phi
and
\phi\circ[-1]
in our definition of
\mathcal{X}_{\ell}^{q}(M)
for all positive integers

, although these two isogenies are equivalent. In particular, the graphs
\mathcal{X}_{\ell}^{q}(1)
and
\mathcal{X}_{\ell}^{q}
are not isomorphic to each other. They share the same set of vertices, but each edge of
\mathcal{X}_{\ell}^{q}
corresponds to two distinct edges in
\mathcal{X}_{\ell}^{q}(1)
.

In the ordinary case, we studied similar graphs of
\Gamma_{1}(M)
-level structure in
[
LM24a
]
. In contrast to the current setting, the isogenies
\phi\circ[-1]
and
\phi
would always give rise to the same edge in the graphs studied in
loc. cit.

In order to describe the structure of

in more detail, we recall the following notions from
[
ACL+23
]
.

Definition 6.12
.

Let
E/\mathbb{F}_{q}
be an elliptic curve that admits an orientation

and let
\theta\in\textup{End}(E)
. Let

be the minimal polynomial of

, which we assume to be of degree 2. Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
be a root of

and write

\omega_{K}=\begin{cases}\frac{1+\sqrt{\Delta}}{2}\quad&\Delta\equiv 1\pmod{4},\\
\frac{\sqrt{\Delta}}{2}\quad&\Delta\equiv 0\pmod{4},\end{cases}

is the fundamental discriminant of

We say that
\theta
is
\ell
-

if
\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]

\ell

.

We say that

\ell

if

, where

Remark 6.13
.

Given a supersingular elliptic curve

and an orientation

that is

-primitive and

be an order of

\ell
-primitive and

In the ordinary case, suppose

is the order

|\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})|/|U_{n}|
. We can now proceed as before to conclude the proof of the theorem.
∎

As in Corollary

, we have the following corollary, which proves part (i) of Theorem

.

Corollary 6.31
.

The covering
\mathcal{X}_{n}/\mathcal{X}
is not Galois for
n
sufficiently large.

Finally, the proof of Theorem
3.6
is readily extendable to this setting, which proves part (ii) of Theorem
E
.

Theorem 6.32
.

(H.ss)

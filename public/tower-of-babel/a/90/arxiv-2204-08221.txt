Additive divisor problem for multiplicative functions
Yujiao Jiang, Guangshi Lü

Abstract
. Let τ \tau denote the divisor function, and f f be any multiplicative function that satisfies some mild hypotheses. We establish the asymptotic formula or non-trivial upper bound for the shifted convolution sum ∑ n ≤ X f ⁡ ( n ) ​ τ ​ ( n − 1 ) \sum_{n\leq X}f(n)\tau(n-1) . We also derive several applications to multiplicative functions in the automorphic context, including the functions λ π ​ ( n ) , μ ⁡ ( n ) ​ λ π ​ ( n ) \lambda_{\pi}(n),\,\mu(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n) and λ ϕ ​ ( n ) l \lambda_{\phi}(n)^{l} . Here λ π ​ ( n ) \lambda_{\pi}(n) denotes the n n -th Dirichlet coefficient of GL m \text{GL}_{m} automorphic L L -function L ⁡ ( s , π ) L(s,\pi) for an automorphic irreducible cuspidal representation π \pi , λ ϕ ​ ( n ) \lambda_{\phi}(n) denotes the n n -th Fourier coefficient of a holomorphic or Maass cusp form ϕ \phi on SL 2 ​ ( ℤ ) {\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}) , and μ ⁡ ( n ) \mu(n) denotes the Möbius function. We present two different arguments. The first one mainly relies on the uniform estimates for the binary additive divisor problem, while the second is based on the recent estimates of Bettin–Chandee for trilinear forms in Kloosterman fractions. In addition, the Bourgain–Kátai–Sarnak–Ziegler criterion and Linnik’s dispersion method are both employed in these two arguments.

1.
Introduction

Let
f(n)
be a multiplicative function, and let
\tau(n)
be the number of divisors of the integer
n
. We shall consider the problem to find the asymptotic behavior of the sum

\sum_{n\leq X}f(n)\tau(n-1).

One important example is the case of
f(n)=\tau_{k}(n)
, which is the number of representations of
n
as a product of
k
factors. This corresponding problem is well known in number theory and is called the additive divisor problem. Its evaluation has a long and rich history. For
k=2
, the sum was first estimated by Ingham, and the current best estimate that we know is

\sum_{n\leq X}\tau(n)\tau(n-1)=XP_{2}(\log X)+O\big(X^{\frac{2}{3}+\varepsilon}\big),

where
P_{k}(x)
is a polynomial of degree
k
(see
[
32
]
for more details). The case
k=3
was first settled by Hooley
[15]
, whose method depends on the distribution of
\tau(n)
in arithmetic progressions and an identity for
\tau_{3}(n)
. Later, Heath-Brown
[
16
]
gave an asymptotic formula with a power-saving error term. Recently, Topacogullari
[
44
]
subsequently improved their results and showed that

\sum_{n\leq X}\tau_{3}(n)\tau(n-1)=XP_{3}(\log X)+O\big(X^{\frac{8}{9}+\varepsilon}\big).

The first result for general
k
was achieved by Linnik
[
28
]
, who gave the asymptotic formula

\sum_{n\leq X}\tau_{k}(n)\tau(n-1)=XP_{k}(\log X)+E_{k}(X)

with
E_{k}(X)\ll X(\log X)^{k-1}(\log\log X)^{4}
by appealing to his own powerful dispersion method. This result was improved by Motohashi
[
31
]
to obtain
E_{k}(X)\ll X(\log\log X)^{c}(\log X)^{-1}
for some constant
c=c(k)
. Here Motohashi applied a combination of uniform distribution of
\tau_{k}(n)
in arithmetic progressions, Linnik’s dispersion method, and the Hardy–Littlewood circle method. Further, Fouvry–Tenenbaum
[
10
]
, Drappeau
[
7
]
and Topacogullari
[
45
]
investigated the cancellations among the average of Kloosterman sums or Kloosterman fractions and made successive improvements on the error term
E_{k}(X)
.

In addition, various generalizations of the additive divisor problem were also studied extensively by many number theorists. These include some specific multiplicative functions such as
f(n)=\tau_{k}^{m}(n)
, and some classes of multiplicative functions which satisfy specific conditions. In 1973, Wolke
[
46
]
showed the following result: assume the multiplicative function
f
satisfies

\left|f\left(p^{a}\right)\right|\leq D_{1}a^{D_{2}}\quad\text{ for all primes }p\text{ and integers }a\geq 1,

and

\sum_{p\leq X}|f(p)-b|\ll\frac{X}{(\log X)^{A}}\text{ for all }A>0,

where
b
is a complex number and
D_{1},D_{2}
are positive real constants. Then one has

(1.1)

\sum_{n\leq X}f(n)\tau(n-1)=C_{f}X(\log X)^{b}+O\big(X(\log X)^{\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}b-1}(\log\log X)^{c}\big),

where the constants
C_{f}
and
c
depend on
f
. We also recall an interesting result
[
43
]
of Timofeev and Tulyaganov: for any non-negative multiplicative function
f(n)
satisfying (i)
f\left(p^{r}\right)\leq A^{r}
for some
A>0
, (ii)
f(n)\ll n^{\varepsilon}
for any
\varepsilon>0
, (iii)
\sum_{p\leq X}f(p)\ln p\geq\alpha X
with some
\alpha>0
, then one has

(1.2)

\sum_{n\leq X}f(n)\tau(n-1)=(C_{f}+o(1))\sum_{n\leq X}f(n)\log X

with an explicitly given constant
C_{f}
. What is more, Drappeau and Topacogullari
[
8
]
consider another class of multiplicative functions which satisfy (i)
f(p_{1})=f(p_{2})
for any primes
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
with
p_{1}\equiv p_{2}(\text{mod}\,D)
, (ii)
|f(n)|\leq\tau_{k}(n)
for some
k
, where
D
is some fixed integer. They derived an asymptotic formula with a better error term

(1.3)

\sum_{n\leq X}f(n)\tau(n-1)=2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\text{ primitive }\\
\operatorname{cond}(\chi)\mid D\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq\sqrt{X}\\
\operatorname{cond}(\chi)\mid q\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q^{2}\leq n\leq X\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}f(n)\chi(n)+O\Big(\frac{X}{(\log X)^{A}}\Big),

where the implied constant depends only on
k,D
and
A
. They also provided some interesting applications for
f
being the arithmetic functions
\tau_{z}(n),b_{\mathbb{K}}(n),z^{\omega(n)}
, where
z\in\mathbb{C}
,
\omega(n)
is the number of distinct prime divisors of an integer
n
, and
\tau_{z}(n),b_{\mathbb{K}}(n)
are Dirichlet coefficients of
\zeta^{z}(s),\zeta^{1/[\mathbb{K}:\mathbb{Q}]}(s)H(s)
with some Euler product
H(s)
. Finally, it is mentioned that Fouvry and Tenenbaum
[
11
]
derives these applications from an alternative argument.

We find that the previously considered multiplicative functions
f
should at least satisfy the condition
f(n)\ll n^{\varepsilon}
. It is of interest to know whether one can derive an asymptotic formula for sum
\sum_{n\leq X}f(n)\tau(n-1)
without the restriction
f(n)\ll n^{\varepsilon}
. We further expect that our result can apply to coefficients of
\text{GL}_{m}
automorphic
L
-functions for any fixed
m
. Consequently, the purpose of this paper is to extend the previous results for a broader class of multiplicative functions. Let
{\mathcal{F}}
denote the class of all multiplicative functions
f
with the following hypotheses:

(i) The second moment of
f
is bounded by

\sum_{n\leq X}|f(n)|^{2}\ll X(\log X)^{c-1}

for some constant
c\geq 1
.

(ii) Let
\varepsilon>0
denote an arbitrary small constant, and
P
denote the product of primes
p
which belong to the interval
[\exp\big((\log X)^{\varepsilon/2}\big),\,\exp\big((\log X)^{1-\varepsilon/2}\big)]
. Then
f
satisfies the following upper bound estimate condition in sieve theory

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
\left(n,P\right)=1\end{subarray}}|f(n)|^{2}\ll_{\varepsilon}\frac{X}{(\log X)^{1-\varepsilon}}.\\

(iii) The function
f
satisfies the Siegel–Walfisz criterion at primes, which says that for any fixed
A>0
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
p\equiv a({\rm mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}f(p)-\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{p\leq X}f(p)\ll_{A}\frac{X}{(\log X)^{A}}

holds for all
(a,q)=1
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f\in{\mathcal{F}}
. Then we have

\sum_{n\leq X}f(n)\tau(n-1)=\sum_{n\leq X}f(n)\big(c(0,n)(\log n+2\gamma)+2c^{\prime}(0,n)\big)+O\big(X(\log X)^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\big),

where
c(s,n)
is given by

c(s,n)=\prod_{p\mid n}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{s+1}}\right)\prod_{p\nmid n}\left(1+\frac{1}{(p-1)p^{s+1}}\right),

and the implied constant depends on
\varepsilon
and the implied constants in the hypotheses.

Remark 1.1
.

If there exists some constant
k
such that
|f(n)|\leq\tau_{k}(n)
for all integers
n
, then the error term can be improved to
O\big(X(\log X)^{\varepsilon}\big)
. This can be seen from Remark
1.4
or Remark
6.1
.

Remark 1.2
.

The main term in Theorem
1.1
can be evaluated by some standard methods in complex analysis, for instance Perron’s formula. First of all, by direct calculation, one has

c(s,n)=\prod_{p}\left(1+\frac{1}{(p-1)p^{s+1}}\right)\,\prod_{p\mid n}\left(1-\frac{p}{(p-1)p^{s+1}+1}\right):=h(s)g_{s}(n).

It is worth pointing out that
f(n)g_{s}(n)
is multiplicative in variable
n
. Consider the Dirichlet series

D(w,s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f(n)g_{s}(n)n^{-w}

for
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}w>1
and
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}s>-1
. Then we may obtain the asymptotic formula for

\sum_{n\leq X}f(n)g_{s}(n)

when
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}s>-1
. Finally, the main term follows from partial summation and taking the derivative on variable
s
. The detailed process will be seen in the applications.

Remark 1.3
.

This should be compared with the asymptotic formulae (
1.1
), (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) of Wolke, Timofeev–Tulyaganov and Drappeau–Topacogullari, respectively. It is known that the error term in the work of Drappeau–Topacogullari is
\ll X(\log X)^{-A}
, which is stronger than our result. Note that Wolke’s result is better than ours only if
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}b\leq 3/2
. Since the multiplicative functions that they consider are essentially periodic on the set of primes or close to a fixed number at primes on average, it is reasonable that these functions have better asymptotic behaviors. Moreover, our result is noticeably better than that of Timofeev–Tulyaganov.

Finally, we provide a brief overview of the proof of Theorem
1.1
. We will give two different arguments, and they are both based on the generalized Bourgain–Kátai–Sarnak–Ziegler (BKSZ for short) criterion and Linnik’s dispersion method. The BKSZ criterion is actually a finite version of Vinogradov’s bilinear technique, which appeared in the works
[
4
,
24
]
. It is very effective and widely applied to attack Sarnak’s disjointness conjecture. For our purpose, we have to generalize the BKSZ criterion to a greater extent, when compared with the works
[
5
,
18
]
of Cafferata–Perelli–Zaccagnini and Jiang–Lü. It is worth illustrating that these two arguments have completely different starting points and key ingredients. We shall explain them below, respectively.

Our first approach is to use the idea of BKSZ at the beginning. Then the sum that we are concerned with

\sum_{n\leq X}f(n)\tau(n-1)

can be rearranged as a bilinear sum

(1.4)

\sum_{m\leq X/N}\,\sum_{p\sim N}f(m)f(p)\tau(pm-1)

and a sifted sum

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
(n,P)=1\end{subarray}}f(n)\tau(n-1),

where
\exp\big((\log X)^{\varepsilon/2}\big)\ll N\ll\exp\big((\log X)^{1-\varepsilon/2}\big)
. After applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, the bilinear sum is generally reduced to estimate

\sum_{p_{1},p_{2}\sim N}f(p_{1})f(p_{2})\sum_{m\leq X/N}\tau(p_{1}m-1)\tau(p_{2}m-1).

Notice that the divisor function
\tau
is always positive. Though the innermost sum is the binary divisor problem, which has an asymptotic formula uniformly in parameters
p_{1},p_{2}
, we can not precisely calculate the main term of (
1.4
) in this way, due the application of Cauchy’s inequality. In order to overcome the positivity of
\tau,
one may construct the approximation
\widetilde{\tau}
of
\tau
such that the difference of

\sum_{n\leq X}f(n)\tau(n-1)
and
\sum_{n\leq X}f(n)\widetilde{\tau}(n-1)
is admissible, where
\widetilde{\tau}
is also multiplicative. This may work if one refers to the idea in
[
30
, Lemma 3.3]
. Building on the works of Linnik
[
28
, Chapter II(2)]
and Motohashi
[
31
]
, we here choose another approximation of
\tau(n-1)
by
\tau(n+h)
, where
h
is any prime in
[X^{2/3},X(\log X)^{-A}]
. The reason for our choice will be explained in Section 5. If the difference

(1.5)

\sum_{n\leq X}f(n)\big(\tau(n-1)-\tau(n+h)\big)

is admissible for any prime
h\in[X^{2/3},X(\log X)^{-A}]
, then the origin additive divisor problem is reduced to a ternary additive problem

\sum_{h\leq X(\log X)^{-A}}\sum_{n\leq X}\Lambda(h)f(n)\tau(n+h),

which can be solved easily by the Hardy–Litterwood circle method. Notice that the interval of
h
is large enough. So we may study the summation over
h

\sum_{h\leq X(\log X)^{-A}}\Lambda(h)\tau(n+h),

which is just the classical Titchmarsh problem with a large shifted parameter
n\in(0,X]
.

In order to prove that the sum (
1.5
) is admissible, we take the strategy discussed as before. We need to estimate the dispersion of difference

(1.6)

\sum_{m\leq X/N}\Big|\sum_{p\sim N}f(p)\big(\tau(pm-1)-\tau(pm+h)\big)\Big|^{2}

and the sifted sum

(1.7)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
(n,P)=1\end{subarray}}f(n)\tau(n+\ell),

where
\ell=-1
or
\ell=h
as above. The study of sum (
1.6
) then lies on the evaluation of four binary divisor sums

(1.8)

\sum_{m\leq X/N}\tau(p_{1}m+\varpi_{1})\tau(p_{2}m+\varpi_{2}),

where
p_{1},p_{2}\sim N
, and
\left(\varpi_{1},\varpi_{2}\right)
is one of the pairs

(-1,-1),\,(-1,h),\,(h,-1),\,(h,h).

Moreover, the main term of (
1.8
) is required to be carefully handled. We hope that the contribution of four main terms can mutually cancel when summing over prime variables
p_{1},p_{2}
with the weight
f(p_{1})\overline{f(p_{2})}
. At this moment, the Siegel–Walfisz criterion in Hypothesis (iii) plays an important role. The sifted sum (
1.7
) is a standard sieve condition, and is estimated by Hypothesis (ii) and Lemma
3.8
.

Our second approach is based on the hyperbola method, which means that
\tau(n)
is written as a convolution
\sum_{ab=n}1
. Thus, the problem is reduced to investigating the problem of Bombieri–Vinogradov type for
f(n)
with a range of moduli at least
\sqrt{X}
. In fact, we can prove the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
f\in{\mathcal{F}}
. For any
\varepsilon>0
, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq X^{\frac{17}{33}-\varepsilon}\end{subarray}}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
n\equiv 1({\rm mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}f(n)-\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}f(n)\Big|\ll X(\log X)^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon},

where the implied constant depends on
\varepsilon
and the implied constants in the hypotheses.

Remark 1.4
.

Let
k
be a fixed positive integer. Assume
f\in{\mathcal{F}}
satisfies
|f(n)|\leq\tau_{k}(n)
for all integers
n
. By the work
[
9
, Corollary]
of Fouvry and Radziwiłł, one has

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq X^{\frac{17}{33}-\varepsilon}\end{subarray}}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
n\equiv 1({\rm mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}f(n)-\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}f(n)\Big|\ll_{\varepsilon}X(\log X)^{\varepsilon}.

Hence, the restriction
|f(n)|\leq\tau_{k}(n)
is removed at cost of the magnitude
(\log X)^{\frac{1}{2}}
. We also mention that Green
[
14
]
and Granville–Shao
[
13
]
considered the multiplicative functions with stronger restriction
|f(n)|\leq 1
.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
starts with the generalized BKSZ criterion, which can decompose the sum above into two parts. One part need to estimate

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq X^{\frac{17}{33}-\varepsilon}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
(n,P)=1\\
n\equiv 1({\rm mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}|f(n)|.

If the function
f
is divisor-bounded, the Titchmarshi–Brun inequality
[
40
]
can be used directly to estimate the inner sum (see Lemma 9.1 in
[
9
]
). However, it is not suitable for our situation. After exchanging the order of summations, the above double sum is transformed into the sifted sum similar to (
1.7
), and can be treated in the same manner.

The other part can be rewritten as a combination of the sums of type

\sum_{m\sim M}f(m)\Big(\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}q\sim Q\ p\sim N\\
pm\equiv 1({\rm mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}{\sum\ \sum}c_{q}f(p)-\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}q\sim Q\ p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}\\
(pm,q)=1\end{subarray}}{\sum\ \sum}\frac{c_{q}f(p)}{\varphi(q)}\Big),

where
\exp\big((\log X)^{\varepsilon/2}\big)\ll N\ll\exp\big((\log X)^{1-\varepsilon/2}\big)
,
X^{3/4}\ll M\ll X/N
and
Q\ll X^{17/33-\varepsilon}
.
After applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, it reduces to bounding the dispersion

\displaystyle\sum_{m\sim M}\psi\Bigl(\frac{m}{M}\Bigr)\Big|\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}q\sim Q\ p\sim N\\
pm\equiv 1({\rm mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}{\sum\ \sum}c_{q}f(p)-\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}q\sim Q\ p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}\\
(pm,q)=1\end{subarray}}{\sum\ \sum}\frac{c_{q}f(p)}{\varphi(q)}\Big|^{2},

where
\psi
is some compactly supported smooth function. We then follow the usual steps as in Linnik’s dispersion method. Opening the square makes the above sum split into three terms. The most difficult term is to estimate

\sum_{q_{1}\sim Q}\sum_{q_{2}\sim Q}c_{q_{1}}\overline{c_{q_{2}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1},p_{2}\sim N\end{subarray}}f(p_{1})\overline{f(p_{2})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1}m\equiv 1(\text{mod}\,q_{1})\\
p_{2}m\equiv 1(\text{mod}\,q_{2})\end{subarray}}\psi\Bigl(\frac{m}{M}\Bigr).

Next, some familiar techniques and tools are used, including the Poisson summation formula, the Barban–Davenport–Halberstam theorem, Bezout’s identity and sums of Kloosterman fractions. Especially, we benefit from the key estimate of Bettin–Chandee for trilinear forms in Kloosterman fractions (see Lemma
3.5
).

2.
Applications

We now provide some examples, which fit into the framework of our theorem
1.1
.

2.1.
Automorphic forms on
\mathrm{GL}_{m}

Let
m\geq 2
, and let
\mathcal{A}(m)
be the set of all cuspidal automorphic representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{m}
over
\mathbb{Q}
with unitary central character. For each
\pi\in\mathcal{A}(m)
, the corresponding
L
-function is defined by absolutely convergent Dirichlet series as

L(s,\pi)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{\pi}(n)}{n^{s}}

for
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}s>1
. By the Rankin–Selberg theory and the inequality
|\lambda_{\pi}(n)|^{2}\leq\lambda_{\pi\times\tilde{\pi}}(n)
for all positive integers
n
(see
[
21
, Lemma 3.1]
), one has

(2.1)

\sum_{n\leq X}|\lambda_{\pi}(n)|^{2}\leq\sum_{n\leq X}\lambda_{\pi\times\tilde{\pi}}(n)\ll_{\pi}X.

Furthermore, by utilizing the sieve technique, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
\left(n,P(Y,Z)\right)=1\end{subarray}}|\lambda_{\pi}(n)|^{2}\ll_{\pi}X\frac{\log Y}{\log Z}

for any
N_{\pi}<Y<Z\leq X^{\frac{1}{30m^{2}}}
, which has been shown in
[
19
, Lemma 5.3]
. Here
N_{\pi}
is the arithmetic conductor of
\pi
. For any fixed
\pi\in\mathcal{A}(m)
, Hypotheses (i) and (ii) then hold with
c=1
. We further assume that
\pi
is self-dual and
\pi\not\simeq\pi\otimes\chi
for any quadratic primitive character
\chi
. We define the subset
\mathcal{A}^{\flat}(m)
to be those
\pi\in\mathcal{A}(m)
satisfying the above assumptions.
Hypotheses (iii) directly follows from
[
23
, Corollary 4.8]
.

Therefore, Theorem
1.1
can apply the multiplicative functions
\lambda_{\pi}(n)
and
\mu(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)
. Next, using the analytic properties of the twisted automorphic
L
-functions, one may show the quantities of main terms are admissible when compared to
O\big(X(\log X)^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\big)
. However, we want to avoid calculating the main term in Theorem
1.1
for these two cases. We will provide another approach to get the following result.

Theorem 2.1
.

Fixed
\pi\in\mathcal{A}^{\flat}(m)
with
m\geq 2
. Let
\lambda_{\pi}(n)
denote the coefficients of
L(s,\pi)
. For any
\varepsilon>0
, we have

\sum_{n\leq X}\lambda_{\pi}(n)\tau(n-1)\ll X(\log X)^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}

and

\sum_{n\leq X}\mu(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\tau(n-1)\ll X(\log X)^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon},

where the implied constant depends on
\pi,\varepsilon
.

Remark 2.1
.

If
m\geq 5
, this result is completely new. The case of
m\leq 4
has been treated in our previous work joint with Thorner and Wang
[
23
]
through the Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem on higher rank groups.

Proof.

We only prove the second assertion, since it is a little more complicated. From the relation (
7.1
), we get

(2.2)

\sum_{n\leq X}\mu(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\tau(n-1)=\frac{(\log X)^{A}}{X}T_{\mu\lambda_{\pi}}(X)+O\big(X(\log X)^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\big),

where
T_{\mu\lambda_{\pi}}(X)
is a ternary additive problem given by

T_{\mu\lambda_{\pi}}(X)=\sum_{h\leq X(\log X)^{-A}}\sum_{n\leq X}\Lambda(h)\mu(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\tau(n+h).

If
g
is finitely supported, we define the exponential sum
F_{g}:\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
by the formula

F_{g}(\alpha;X)=\sum_{n\leq X}g(n)e(n\alpha).

Then we have

T_{\mu\lambda_{\pi}}(X)=\int_{0}^{1}F_{\Lambda}\big(\alpha;X(\log X)^{-A}\big)F_{\mu\lambda_{\pi}}(\alpha;X)F_{\tau}(-\alpha;2X)\text{d}\alpha.

In order to apply the circle method, we define the major arcs and the minor arcs as follows:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}=\bigcup_{1\leq q\leq R}\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a\leq q\\
(a,q)=1\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{M}(a,q)

with
\mathfrak{M}(a,q)=\big[\frac{a}{q}-\frac{1}{qQ},\ \frac{a}{q}+\frac{1}{qQ}\big]
, and
\mathfrak{m}
is the complement of
\mathfrak{M}
in
\mathbb{T}
:

\mathfrak{m}=\mathbb{T}\setminus\mathfrak{M}.

We take
R=(\log X)^{10}
and
Q=X(\log X)^{-A-10}
.
Then
T_{\mu\lambda_{\pi}}(X)
is divided into two parts as follows:

\displaystyle T_{\mu\lambda_{\pi}}(X)=

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\mathfrak{M}}+\int_{\mathfrak{m}}\Big)\,F_{\Lambda}\big(\alpha;X(\log X)^{-A}\big)F_{\mu\lambda_{\pi}}(\alpha;X)F_{\tau}(-\alpha;2X)\text{d}\alpha

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle T_{\mu\lambda_{\pi}}^{(1)}(X)+T_{\mu\lambda_{\pi}}^{(2)}(X).

On the mirror arcs
\mathfrak{m}
, it follows from the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and the estimate (
2.1
) that

T_{\mu\lambda_{\pi}}^{(2)}(X)\ll\sup_{\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}}|F_{\Lambda}\big(\alpha;X(\log X)^{-A}\big)|X(\log X)^{\frac{3}{2}}.

By the classical estimate of exponential sum over primes
[
17
, Theorme 13.6]
, we have

F_{\Lambda}\big(\alpha;X(\log X)^{-A}\big)\ll\Big(Q^{\frac{1}{2}}X^{\frac{1}{2}}(\log X)^{-\frac{A}{2}}+R^{-\frac{1}{2}}XX(\log X)^{-A}+X^{\frac{3}{5}}\Big)(\log X)^{3}\ll\frac{X}{(\log X)^{A+2}}

for any
\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}
. Hence, the contribution of terms on the mirror arcs
\mathfrak{m}
satisfies

(2.3)

T_{\mu\lambda_{\pi}}^{(2)}(X)\ll\frac{X^{2}}{(\log X)^{A}}.

Now we evaluate the contribution
T_{\mu\lambda_{\pi}}^{(1)}(X)
of terms on the major arcs
\mathfrak{M}
. From now on we assume
\alpha=\beta+a/q\in\mathfrak{M}(a,q)
. It follows from
[
22
, Section 5]
that

(2.4)

\displaystyle F_{\mu\lambda_{\pi}}(\alpha;X)\ll

\displaystyle\Big(1+\frac{X}{qQ}\Big)\max_{1\leq Y\leq X}\Big|F_{\mu\lambda_{\pi}}\Big(\frac{a}{q};Y\Big)\Big|

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle(\log X)^{A+10}X\exp(-c\sqrt{\log X})

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle X\exp\big(-\frac{c}{2}\sqrt{\log X}\big),

where
c>0
is an ineffective constant due to the Siegel-type estimate of twisted automorphic
L
-functions
[
23
, Theorem 4.1]
. Similar to the treatment mirror arcs, we insert the bound (
2.4
) and then obtain that the contribution of terms on
\mathfrak{M}
satisfies

T_{\mu\lambda_{\pi}}^{(1)}(X)\ll X^{2}\exp\big(-\frac{c}{3}\sqrt{\log X}\big).

Combining this with (
2.3
), we get
T_{\mu\lambda_{\pi}}(X)\ll X^{2}(\log X)^{-A}
, which further yields from (
2.2
) that

\sum_{n\leq X}\mu(n)\lambda_{\pi}(n)\tau(n-1)\ll X(\log X)^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}.

This completes the proof of second assertion in Theorem
2.1
.
∎

In order to illustrate that Theorem
2.1
provides a non-trivial upper bound, we need to estimate the correct order of magnitude of

\sum_{n\leq X}|\lambda_{\pi}(n)|\tau(n-1).

We suppose that a cuspidal automorphic representation
\pi
comes from certain symmetric power lift of
{\rm GL}_{2}
, for simplicity.
Let
\phi
be any holomorphic cusp form of weight
k
for
{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, which is a normalized eigenform for the Hecke operators.
Suppose that
\lambda_{{\rm Sym}^{r}\phi}(n)
are the Dirichlet coefficients of its
r
-th symmetric power
L
-function
L_{r}(s,\phi)
, where
r\geq 1.
Thanks to the Sato–Tate conjecture (which is now a theorem of Barnet-Lamb, Geraghty, Harris and Taylor
[
2
]
), it was proved by Lü
[
29
, Lemma 3.4]
and Tang and Wu
[
42
, Theorem 1]

\sum_{n\leq X}|\lambda_{{\rm Sym}^{r}\phi}(n)|\sim c_{r}(\phi)\frac{X}{\log^{\delta_{r}}X},

where
\delta_{r}
can be explicitly determined with
0<\delta_{r}\leq 1-8/\pi^{2}=0.1849...
, and
c_{r}(\phi)
is a positive constant depending on
\phi
and
r.
According to the recent work
[
34
]
of Newton and Thorne, there exists a cuspidal automorphic representation
\pi
on
{\rm GL}_{r+1}
such that

L(s,\pi)=L_{r}(s,\phi)

for all
r\geq 1.
Hence, we have

\sum_{n\leq X}|\lambda_{\pi}(n)|\gg\frac{X}{(\log X)^{1-8/\pi^{2}}},

which further yields from the result (
1.2
) of Timofeev and Tulyaganov that

\sum_{n\leq X}|\lambda_{\pi}(n)|\tau(n-1)\gg X(\log X)^{\frac{8}{\pi^{2}}}.

For this case, since
8/\pi^{2}\geq 4/5>1/2
, it indicates that Theorem
2.1
does give a non-trivial saving.

2.2.
Cusp forms on
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})

Let
k
be an even positive integer, and let
H_{k}
denote the set of arithmetically normalized primitive cusp forms of weight
k
for
{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
which are eigenfunctions of all the Hecke operators. Any
\phi\in H_{k}
has a Fourier expansion at infinity given by

\phi(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{\phi}(n)n^{\frac{k-1}{2}}e(nz),

where
\lambda_{\phi}(1)=1
and the eigenvalues
\lambda_{\phi}(n)\in\mathbb{R}
. Deligne proved the Ramanujan conjecture, which asserts that

(2.5)

|\lambda_{\phi}(n)|\leq\tau(n)

for all
n\geq 1.
Similarly, let
S_{r}
be the set of arithmetically normalized Hecke–Maass cusp forms of eigenvalue
\lambda=1/4+r^{2}
for
{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Then
\phi\in S_{r}
has the Fourier expansion at infinity given by

\phi(z)=\sum_{n\neq 0}\lambda_{\phi}(n)\sqrt{y}K_{ir}(2\pi|n|y)e(nx),

where
K_{ir}
is the
K
-Bessel function and
\lambda_{\phi}(n)
are eigenvalues of
T_{n}
with
\lambda_{\phi}(n)=1
and
\lambda_{\phi}(n)\in\mathbb{R}
. Up to now, the Ramanujan conjecture on Maass cusp forms has not been proved. This is different from holomorphic cusp forms. The current best result is due to Kim and Sarnak, which states that

(2.6)

\left|\lambda_{\phi}(n)\right|\leq n^{\frac{7}{64}}\tau(n).

Moreover, for any
\phi\in H_{k}\cup S_{r}
, the eigenvalues
\lambda_{\phi}(n)
enjoy the multiplicative property

(2.7)

\lambda_{\phi}(m)\lambda_{\phi}(n)=\sum_{d|(m,n)}\lambda_{\phi}\Big(\frac{mn}{d^{2}}\Big)

for all integers
m,n\geq 1.
In particular,
\lambda_{\phi}(n)
are multiplicative. The Hecke
L
-function
L(s,\phi)
associated to
\phi
has the Euler product representation

L(s,{\phi})=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\lambda_{\phi}(n)}{n^{s}}=\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{\lambda_{\phi}(p)}{p^{s}}+\frac{1}{p^{2s}}\right)^{-1}.

We rewrite the Euler product as

L(s,{\phi})=\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{\phi}(p)}{p^{s}}\right)^{-1}\left(1-\frac{\beta_{\phi}(p)}{p^{s}}\right)^{-1},

where
\alpha_{\phi}(p),\beta_{\phi}(p)
are complex numbers with
\alpha_{\phi}(p)=\overline{\beta_{\phi}(p)},\,|\alpha_{\phi}(p)|=|\beta_{\phi}(p)|=1
. For each
m\geq 1
, we
define the
m\mbox{-}
th symmetric power
L
-function by the
degree
m+1
Euler product

L(s,{\rm sym}^{m}{\phi})=\prod_{p}\prod_{0\leq j\leq m}\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{\phi}(p)^{m-j}\beta_{\phi}(p)^{j}}{p^{s}}\right)^{-1}.

Note that
L(s,{\rm sym}^{0}{\phi})=\zeta(s)
and
L(s,{\rm sym}^{1}{\phi})=L(s,{\phi})
.

Recently, Newton and Thorne
[
34
, Theorem B]
proved that if
\phi\in H_{k}
, then the
m
-th symmetric power lift
{\rm sym}^{m}\phi
corresponds to a cuspidal automorphic representation of
{\rm GL}_{m+1}(\mathbb{A})
with trivial central character. This result implies that
{\rm sym}^{m}\phi\in\mathcal{A}^{\flat}(m+1)
for all
m\geq 1
.
If
\phi\in S_{r}
, it is only known that
{\rm sym}^{m}\phi\in\mathcal{A}^{\flat}(m+1)
for
2\leq m\leq 4
, due to the works
[
12
,
26
,
25
]
of Gelbert, Jacquet, Kim and Shahidi.

Another interesting example is the case
f(n)=\lambda_{\phi}(n)^{l}
, where
l
is a positive integer. The problem that we are concerned with is the asymptotic behavior of the sum

M_{\phi,l}(X)=\sum_{n\leq X}\lambda_{\phi}(n)^{l}\tau(n-1).

When
l=1
, the spectral method or
\delta
-symbol method and its variants can give
M_{\phi,l}(X)\ll X^{\frac{2}{3}+\varepsilon}
. Redmond
[
35
,
36
]
considered the case of
l=2
. However, Redmond
[
37
]
then realized that his result for
M_{\phi,2}(X)
was not true, and said that he did not see how to do this so far. This is to say that any non-trivial upper bound of
M_{\phi,l}(X)
is not known if
l\geq 2
. Recently, the authors
[
20
]
investigated the levels of distributions of
\lambda_{\phi}(n)^{2}
and
\lambda_{\phi}(n)^{3}
in arithmetic progressions. When
\phi\in H_{k}
, with the help of Brun–Titchmarsh inequality, we derived

M_{\phi,2}(X)=c_{\phi}X\log X+O\left(X\log\log X\right)

and

M_{\phi,3}(X)\ll X(\log X)^{\frac{64}{15\pi}-1}\log\log X,

where the constant
c_{\phi}
and the implied constants all depend only on
\phi
. When
\phi\in S_{r}
, with the help of Large sieve inequality, we derived

M_{\phi,2}(X)=c_{\phi}X\log X+O\left(X\left(\log X\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\log\log X\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}\right).

In the following, we shall establish the asymptotic formulae of
M_{\phi,l}(X)
for more cases.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\phi\in H_{k}
and
l\geq 2
be any fixed integer. For any
\varepsilon>0
, we have

M_{\phi,l}(X)=XP_{l}(\log X)+O\big(X(\log X)^{\varepsilon}\big),

where
P_{l}(x)
denotes a polynomial in
x
of degree
(2j)!/(j!(j+1)!)
for even
l=2j
, otherwise
P_{l}(x)\equiv 0
.

Remark 2.2
.

The conclusion of Timofeev and Tulyaganov
[
43
]
, with the help of the Sato–Tate distribution, can yield

\sum_{n\leq X}|\lambda_{\phi}(n)|^{l}\tau(n-1)=(d_{l}(\phi)+o(1))X(\log X)^{\eta_{l}},

where
\eta_{l}
can be explicitly computed by

\eta_{l}=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{o}^{\pi}|2\cos\theta|^{l}(\sin\theta)^{2}\,\text{d}\theta=\frac{\Gamma(l+1)}{\Gamma(l/2+2)\Gamma(l/2+1)}.

Note that this result for even
l
has been achieved by Lü
[
29
]
. It is obvious that our theorem
2.2
refines the result above.

Proof.

In order to apply Theorem
1.1
, we need to verify these three hypotheses. By Deligne’s bound (
2.5
), we have

\sum_{n\leq X}|\lambda_{\phi}(n)|^{2l}\leq\sum_{n\leq X}\tau(n)^{2l}\ll X(\log X)^{4^{l}-1}

which implies Hypothesis (i) holds for
c=4^{l}
. Inserting the bound (
2.5
) again and applying the Brun–Titchmarsh inequality
[
40
, Theorem 1]
, we obtain

(2.8)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
(n,P)=1\end{subarray}}|\lambda_{\phi}(n)|^{2l}\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
(n,P)=1\end{subarray}}\tau(n)^{2l}\ll\frac{X}{\log X}\exp\Big(4^{l}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
p\nmid P\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\Big).

Mertens’ theorem can yield

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
p\nmid P\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}=

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{1}{p}-\sum_{p\leq\exp((\log X)^{1-\varepsilon/2})}\frac{1}{p}+\sum_{p\leq\exp((\log X)^{\varepsilon/2})}\frac{1}{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon\log\log X+O(1).

Inserting this into (
2.8
) gives

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
(n,P)=1\end{subarray}}|\lambda_{\phi}(n)|^{2l}\ll\frac{X}{(\log X)^{1-4^{l}\varepsilon}}.

which further yields Hypothesis (ii). For the last hypothesis, it is obvious from (
2.7
) that

\lambda_{\phi}(p)^{l}
is a linear combination of
\lambda_{\mathrm{sym}^{j}\phi}(p)
with
j\geq 0
. Hence, Hypothesis (iii) follows from the estimates of Sielgel–Walfisz type for Dirichlet
L
-functions and twisted symmetric power
L
-functions (see
[
23
, Corollary 4.8]
).

With the notation as in Theorem
1.1
and Remark
1.2
, it follows easily from Remark
1.1
that

(2.9)

\displaystyle M_{\phi,l}(X)=

\displaystyle h(0)\sum_{n\leq X}\lambda_{\phi}(n)^{l}g_{0}(n)\log n+2\big(\gamma h(0)+h^{\prime}(0)\big)\sum_{n\leq X}\lambda_{\phi}(n)^{l}g_{0}(n)

\displaystyle+2h(0)\sum_{n\leq X}\lambda_{\phi}(n)^{l}g_{0}^{\prime}(n)+O\big(X(\log X)^{\varepsilon}\big).

Following the idea in Remark
1.2
, we consider the Dirichlet series

D_{l}(w,s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{\phi}(n)^{l}g_{s}(n)n^{-w},

where
g_{s}(n)
is given by the product

g_{s}(n)=\prod_{p\mid n}\left(1-\frac{p}{(p-1)p^{s+1}+1}\right).

In view of the relation (
2.7
), the series
D_{l}(w,s)
can be decomposed as

D_{l}(w,s)=F_{l}(w)U_{l}(w,s),

where

\displaystyle F_{2j}(w)=\zeta(w)^{A_{j}}L\left(\mathrm{sym}^{2j}\phi,w\right)\prod_{1\leq r\leq j-1}L\left(\operatorname{sym}^{2r}\phi,s\right)^{C_{j}(r)}\quad(l=2j),

\displaystyle F_{2j+1}(w)=L(\phi,w)^{B_{j}}L\left(\operatorname{sym}^{2j+1}\phi,w\right)\prod_{1\leq r\leq j-1}L\left(\operatorname{sym}^{2r+1}\phi,w\right)^{D_{j}(r)}\quad(l=2j+1),

and the constants
A_{j},B_{j},C_{j}(r),D_{j}(r)(1\leq r\leq j-1)
are given by

\displaystyle A_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{(2j)!}{j!(j+1)!},\quad B_{j}=2\frac{(2j+1)!}{j!(j+2)!},

\displaystyle C_{j}(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{(2j)!(2r+1)}{(j-r)!(j+r+1)!},\quad D_{j}(r)=\frac{(2j+1)!(2r+2)}{(j-r)!(j+r+2)!}.

The
L
-function
F_{l}(w)
is of degree
2^{l}
, and for even
l=2j
all coefficients of
F_{2j}(w)
are nonnegative. Moreover,
U_{l}(s,w)
is a double Dirichlet series absolutely convergent in
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}(w+s)>0
. Let us use
v_{l}(n)
and
u_{l,s}(n)
to denote the Dirichlet coefficients of
F_{l}(w)
and
U_{l}(s,w)
, respectively. By Perron’s formula with the analytic properties of symmetric power
L
-functions, a standard procedure yields

(2.10)

\sum_{n\leq X}v_{l}(n)=XQ_{l}(\log X)+O(X^{1-\delta_{l}}),

where
Q_{l}(x)
denotes a polynomial in
x
, and satisfies that
Q_{2j+1}\equiv 0
if
l=2j+1
is odd while
\operatorname{deg}Q_{2j}=(2j)!/(j!(j+1)!)-1
for even
l=2j
, and
\delta_{l}\in(0,1/3)
is a constant depending
l
. Suppose that
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}s>-1/2
, then we have the convolution

\lambda_{\phi}(n)^{l}g_{s}(n)=\sum_{n=ab}v_{l}(a)u_{l,s}(b)

and

\sum_{b=1}^{\infty}|u_{l,s}(b)|b^{-\sigma}\ll 1

for any
\sigma>1/2
. With the help of (
2.10
), we infer that

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq X}\lambda_{\phi}(n)^{l}g_{s}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{b\leq X}u_{l,s}(b)\sum_{a\leq X/b}v_{l}(a)

\displaystyle=X\sum_{b=1}^{\infty}\frac{u_{l,s}(b)}{b}Q_{l}\left(\log\frac{X}{b}\right)+O\left(X^{1+\varepsilon}\sum_{b\geq X}|u_{l,s}(b)|b^{-1}\right)+O(X^{1-\delta_{l}})

\displaystyle=XP_{l,s}(\log X)+O(X^{1-\delta_{l}}),

where
P_{l,s}(x)
is a polynomial in
x
with its coefficients depending on
s
, and satisfies that
P_{2j+1,s}\equiv 0
if
l=2j+1
is odd while
\operatorname{deg}P_{2j,s}=(2j)!/(j!(j+1)!)-1
for even
l=2j
. Subtracting this into (
2.9
), Theorem
2.2
then follows.
∎

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\phi\in S_{r}
. For
l=3,4
, we have

M_{\phi,l}(X)=XP_{l}(\log X)+O\big(X(\log X)^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\big),

where
P_{4}(x)
denotes a polynomial in
x
of degree
2
, and
P_{3}(x)\equiv 0
.

Proof.

As the proof of Theorem
2.2
, we first verify three hypotheses. By the fact
{\rm sym}^{m}\phi\in\mathcal{A}^{\flat}(m+1)
for
2\leq m\leq 4
and the Rankin–Selberg theory, Landau’s lemma gives

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq X}\lambda_{\phi}(n)^{6}=

\displaystyle XQ_{6}(\log X)+O\big(X^{\frac{63}{65}+\varepsilon}\big),

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq X}\lambda_{\phi}(n)^{8}=

\displaystyle XQ_{8}(\log X)+O\big(X^{\frac{255}{257}+\varepsilon}\big),

where
Q_{6},Q_{8}
are polynomials with
\deg Q_{6}=4
and
\deg Q_{8}=14
, respectively (see
[
27
, Remark 1.7]
for example). Thus, Hypothesis (i) holds for
\lambda_{\phi}(n)^{l}
with
l=3,4
. The last hypothesis also holds from the corresponding argument of Theorem
2.2
. Now it remains to check the second one. Since the Ramanujan conjecture is still open for Maass cusp form, the Brun–Titchamrsh inequality can not be used as in (
2.8
). Fortunately, this barrier can be overcome by applying a result
[
15
, Theorem 01]
of Hall and Tenenbaum, which states that if a non-negative multiplicative function
g(n)
satisfies

\sum_{p\leq X}g(p)\log p\ll X\text{ and }\quad\sum_{p\leq X}\sum_{k\geq 2}\frac{g(p^{k})\log p^{k}}{p^{k}}\ll 1,

then one has

(2.11)

\sum_{n\leq X}g(n)\ll\frac{X}{\log X}\sum_{n\leq X}\frac{g(n)}{n}.

We put

g(n)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\lambda_{\phi}(n)^{8}&\text{ if }(n,P)=1,\\
0&\text{ if }(n,P)>1.\end{array}\right.

Clearly,
g(n)
is multiplicative and non-negative. The multiplicative relation (
2.7
) gives

(2.12)

\lambda_{\phi}(p)^{8}=-8+8\lambda_{\phi\times\phi}(p)+7\lambda_{\rm{sym}^{2}\phi\times{\rm sym}^{2}\phi}(p)+6\lambda_{{\rm sym}^{3}\phi\times{\rm sym}^{3}\phi}(p)+\lambda_{{\rm sym}^{4}\phi\times{\rm sym}^{4}\phi}(p).

Using Shahidi’s non-vanishing result
[
39
]
of Rankin–Selberg
L
-functions at
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}s=1
, we get

\sum_{p\leq X}\lambda_{{\rm sym}^{j}\phi\times{\rm sym}^{j}\phi}(p)\log p\ll X.

Thus, it follows from the identity (
2.12
) that

\sum_{p\leq X}\lambda_{\phi}(p)^{8}\log p\ll X.

Moreover, by the bound (
2.6
) of Kim and Sarnak, we find

(2.13)

\sum_{p\leq X}\sum_{k\geq 2}\frac{\lambda_{\phi}(p^{k})^{8}\log p^{k}}{p^{k}}\ll\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{(|\alpha_{\phi}(p)|^{8}+|\beta_{\phi}(p)|^{8})}{p^{1+\frac{1}{8}+\varepsilon}}\ll 1,

where the last step uses the convexity of
L(s,{\rm sym}^{4}\phi\times{\rm sym}^{4}\phi)
. We then use the result (
2.11
) of Hall and Tenenbaum to get

(2.14)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
(n,P)=1\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\phi}(n)^{8}\ll\frac{X}{\log X}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
(n,P)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\lambda_{\phi}(n)^{8}}{n}.

By using the multiplicative property of
\lambda_{\phi}(n)
and the estimate (
2.13
), the logarithmic average of
\lambda_{\phi}(n)^{8}
can be controlled by

(2.15)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
(n,P)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\lambda_{\phi}(n)^{8}}{n}

\displaystyle\ll\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
p\nmid P\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{\lambda_{\phi}(p)^{8}}{p}+\sum_{k\geq 2}\frac{\lambda_{\phi}(p^{k})^{8}}{p^{k}}\right)

\displaystyle\ll\exp\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
p\nmid P\end{subarray}}\Big(\frac{\lambda_{\phi}(p)^{8}}{p}+\sum_{k\geq 2}\frac{\lambda_{\phi}(p^{k})^{8}}{p^{k}}\Big)\bigg)

\displaystyle\ll\exp\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
p\nmid P\end{subarray}}\frac{\lambda_{\phi}(p)^{8}}{p}\bigg).

By
[
38
, Propostion 2.3]
of Rudnick and Sarnak, and summation by parts, we obtain

(2.16)

\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{\lambda_{{\rm sym}^{j}\phi\times{\rm sym}^{j}\phi}(p)}{p}=\log\log X+O(1),

where
1\leq j\leq 4
, and we use the fact that Hypothesis H of Rudnick and Sarnak holds for
\rm{sym}^{j}\phi
with
1\leq j\leq 4
. By (
2.12
) and (
2.16
), we then derive

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
p\nmid P\end{subarray}}\frac{\lambda_{\phi}(p)^{8}}{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{\lambda_{\phi}(p)^{8}}{p}-\sum_{p\leq\exp((\log X)^{1-\varepsilon/2})}\frac{\lambda_{\phi}(p)^{8}}{p}+\sum_{p\leq\exp((\log X)^{\varepsilon/2})}\frac{\lambda_{\phi}(p)^{8}}{p}

\displaystyle=14\varepsilon\log\log X+O(1).

Combining this, (
2.15
) with (
2.14
), we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
(n,P)=1\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\phi}(n)^{8}\ll\frac{X}{(\log X)^{1-14\varepsilon}}.

By the Hölder inequality and Mertens’ theorem, we also have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
(n,P)=1\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\phi}(n)^{6}\ll\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
(n,P)=1\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\phi}(n)^{8}\Big)^{\frac{3}{4}}\;\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
(n,P)=1\end{subarray}}1\Big)^{\frac{1}{4}}\ll\frac{X}{(\log X)^{1-11\varepsilon}}.

These complete the verification of Hypothese (ii) for
\lambda_{\phi}(n)^{l}
with
l=3,4
.

Now we can employ Theorem
1.1
, and then get

\displaystyle M_{\phi,l}(X)=

\displaystyle h(0)\sum_{n\leq X}\lambda_{\phi}(n)^{l}g_{0}(n)\log n+2\big(\gamma h(0)+h^{\prime}(0)\big)\sum_{n\leq X}\lambda_{\phi}(n)^{l}g_{0}(n)

\displaystyle+2h(0)\sum_{n\leq X}\lambda_{\phi}(n)^{l}g_{0}^{\prime}(n)+O\big(X(\log X)^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\big),

where
l=3,4
. The main term can be computed as in the proof of Theorem
2.2
, so we omit the details here.

∎

3.
Some lemmas

3.1.
Classical lemmas

In this section, we will state some results from the literature we need. The first lemma provides an upper bound on short sums of the divisor function in arithmetic progressions.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
b
be arbitrary positive integer and let
q,l
be integers. Suppose that
Y\leq X,q\leq YX^{-\varepsilon}
.
We have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X-Y<n\leq X\\
n\equiv l({\rm mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}\tau(n)^{b}\ll\frac{\tau\big((l,q)\big)^{b}X}{q}\left(\frac{\varphi(q)}{q}\log X\right)^{2^{b}-1}

uniformly in
l,q
, where the implied constant depends only on
\varepsilon
.

Proof.

This lemma immediately follows from Shiu’s classical estimate
[
40
, Theorem 1]
for multiplicative functions and Mertens’ theorem.
∎

The next lemma gives an asymptotic formula for divisor function in arithmetic progression with an explicit main term.

Lemma 3.2
.

Denote

Proof.

3.2.
Sieve condition

We denote by
P(Y,Z)
the product of these primes
p
which belong to the interval
[Y,Z)
,
where
2\leq Y<Z
.
In this section, we shall seek an upper estimate for the sifted sum

(3.3)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
\left(n-a,P(Y,Z)\right)=1\end{subarray}}\tau(n)^{2}

with
0<|a|\leq X
and
\big(a,P(Y,Z)\big)=1
. It is clear that
\tau(n)^{2}\leq\tau_{4}(n)
. Thus, it suffices to estimate the corresponding sum of (
3.3
), in which
\tau_{4}(n)
is instead of
\tau(n)^{2}
. For this purpose, we require the distribution for
\tau_{4}(n)
in arithmetic progressions.
Recall a result given in
[
41
]
, which states that

(3.4)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
n\equiv a({\rm mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}\tau_{4}(n)=\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}XP_{4}(\log X)+O\Big(X^{\frac{3}{5}+\varepsilon}\Big)

holds for any
(a,q)=1
and
q\leq X^{\frac{2}{5}-\varepsilon}
, where
P_{4}(\log X)
is a polynomial in
\log X
of degree
3
with real coefficients that depend on
q
. More precisely, the polynomial
P_{4}(\log X)
is defined by

P_{4}(\log X)=\sum_{j=0}^{3}\frac{1}{j!}B_{3-j}(\log X)\psi_{q}^{(j)}(1)

with
\psi_{q}(s)=\big(\sum\limits_{k|q}k^{-s}\mu(k)\big)^{4}.

Assume that
2\leq Y<Z\leq X^{\frac{1}{10}}
. We first remove the sieve condition
\left(n-a,P(Y,Z)\right)=1
by the Legendre formula

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
\left(n-a,P(Y,Z)\right)=1\end{subarray}}\tau_{4}(n)=\sum_{q|P(Y,Z)}\mu(q)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
n\equiv a({\rm mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}\tau_{4}(n).

We keep the terms for
q|P(Y,Z)
with
q\leq X^{\frac{1}{3}}
and estimate the remaining ones. Applying Rankin’s trick and the trivial bound
\tau_{4}(n)\ll n^{\varepsilon}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q|P(Y,Z)\\
q>X^{1/3}\end{subarray}}|\mu(q)|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
n\equiv a({\rm mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}\tau_{4}(n)\ll

\displaystyle X^{1+\varepsilon}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q|P(Y,Z)\\
q>X^{1/3}\end{subarray}}\frac{|\mu(q)|}{q}\ll X^{1-\varepsilon}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q|P(Y,Z)\\
q>X^{1/3}\end{subarray}}\frac{|\mu(q)|}{q^{1-6\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle X^{1-\varepsilon}\prod_{Y\leq p\leq Z}\Big(1+\frac{1}{p^{1-6\varepsilon}}\Big)\ll X^{1-\frac{2\varepsilon}{5}}.

To estimate the terms with
q\leq X^{1/3}
, we use the asymptotic formula (
3.4
) and get

(3.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q|P(Y,Z)\\
q\leq X^{1/3}\end{subarray}}\mu(q)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
n\equiv a({\rm mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}\tau_{4}(n)=X\sum_{j=0}^{3}\frac{1}{j!}B_{3-j}(\log X)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q|P(Y,Z)\\
q\leq X^{1/3}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(q)\psi_{q}^{(j)}(1)}{\varphi(q)}+O\Big(X^{1-\varepsilon}\Big)

After differentiating
\psi_{q}(s)
, we find that
\psi_{q}^{(j)}(1)/\varphi(q)\ll(\log\log q)^{j}/q
. Then applying Rankin’s trick again, the truncation
q\leq X^{1/3}
in the right-hand side of (
3.5
) can be removed up to error term
X^{1-\frac{2\varepsilon}{5}}
. In addition, an elementary calculation arrives at

\displaystyle\sum_{q|P(Y,Z)}\frac{\mu(q)\psi_{q}^{(j)}(1)}{\varphi(q)}=

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{q|P(Y,Z)}\frac{\mu(q)\psi_{q}(s)}{\varphi(q)}\bigg)^{(j)}\bigg|_{s=1}=\bigg(\prod_{Y\leq p\leq Z}\Big(1-\frac{\psi_{p}(s)}{p-1}\Big)\bigg)^{(j)}\bigg|_{s=1}

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\frac{\log Y}{\log Z}.

Combining all the above estimates, we can derive the following lemma.

Lemma 3.8
.

Suppose that
0<Y<Z\leq X^{\frac{1}{10}}
. Let
P(Y,Z)
denote the product of primes
p
which belong to the interval
[Y,Z)
. Then we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
\left(n-a,P(Y,Z)\right)=1\end{subarray}}\tau(n)^{2}\ll X(\log X)^{3}\frac{\log Y}{\log Z}

for any non-zero
|a|\leq X
and
\big(a,P(Y,Z)\big)=1
.

4.
Reduction of Theorem
1.1

For convenience, we introduce a new notation

S_{f}(X,\ell)=\sum_{n\leq X}f(n)\tau(n+\ell),

where
\ell
is a non-zero integer. First, we make a heuristic observation to illustrate the idea of Motohashi
[
31
]
. By Dirichlet’s hyperbola method, we may write

(4.1)

S_{f}(X,-1)\sim 2\sum_{d\leq\sqrt{X}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
n\equiv 1(\text{mod}\,d)\end{subarray}}f(n).

From now on we assume that
h
is arbitrary prime number in the interval
[X^{2/3},X(\log X)^{-A}]
, where
A
is any sufficiently large positive constant. Let us consider
S_{f}(X,h)
. We may also write

(4.2)

S_{f}(X,h)\sim 2\sum_{d\leq\sqrt{X}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
n\equiv-h(\text{mod}\,d)\end{subarray}}f(n).

The inner sums of (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) can be put into the character sum

(4.3)

\varphi(d)^{-1}\sum_{n\leq X\atop(d,n)=1}f(n)+\varphi(d)^{-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi(\text{mod}\,d)\\
\chi\neq\chi_{0}\end{subarray}}\bar{\chi}(-\ell)\sum_{n\leq X}f(n)\chi(n),

where
\ell=-1
or
\ell=h
as above. Note that
(\ell,d)=1
by the choice of
h
. Thus, they may well be approximately equal to the first term of (
4.3
). In other words, one may expect that
S_{f}(X,-1)
differs little from
S_{f}(X,h)
for each prime
h\in[X^{2/3},X(\log X)^{-A}]
. If so, then
S_{f}(X,-1)
will be approximately equal to

\pi\Big(\frac{X}{(\log X)^{A}}\Big)^{-1}\sum_{X^{2/3}\leq h\leq X(\log X)^{-A}}S_{f}(X,h),

where
\pi\big(X(\log X)^{-A}\big)
denotes the number of primes less than
X(\log X)^{-A}
. This sum obviously belongs to the category of ternary problems, which can be addressed by the Hardy–Littlewood circle method.

In fact, after overcoming certain technical difficulties, it will turn out that
|S_{f}(X,-1)-S_{f}(X,h)|
is relatively small, i.e. less than
X(\log X)^{1/2+\varepsilon}
uniformly for any prime
h\in[X^{2/3},X(\log X)^{-A}]
.

Now we begin to provide the rigorous procedure. Let
\varepsilon
be any sufficiently small positive constant. Let
\Delta\in[(\log X)^{-A},2(\log X)^{-A}]
be a real number such that the number

L:=\frac{(\log X)^{1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}-(\log X)^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}}{\log(1+\Delta)}

is an integer. It is clear that
L\ll(\log X)^{A+1}
. We then can partition the interval
[\exp((\log X)^{\varepsilon/2}),\\
\exp((\log X)^{1-\varepsilon/2})]
into at most
L
intervals

\mathcal{I}_{\nu}:=[\exp((\log x)^{\varepsilon/2})(1+\Delta)^{\nu},\exp((\log x)^{\varepsilon/2})(1+\Delta)^{\ell+1})):=[H_{\nu},H_{\nu+1})

with
0\leq\nu\leq L
. Moreover, we set

\ P_{\nu}=\prod_{H_{0}\leq p<H_{\nu+1}}p,\quad{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}=\{p\in{\mathcal{I}}_{\nu}\},\quad{\mathcal{M}}_{\nu}=\Big\{m\in\big[1,\frac{X}{H_{\nu+1}}\big]:(m,P_{\nu})=1\Big\},

{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}{\mathcal{M}}_{\nu}=\{pm:p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu},m\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\nu}\},

(4.4)

{\mathcal{I}}=\bigcup_{0\leq\nu\leq L}{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}{\mathcal{M}}_{\nu}\ \ \text{and}\ \ {\mathcal{J}}=[1,X]\setminus{\mathcal{I}}.

The intervals above are always meant as subsets of
\mathbb{N}
. Notice that each
n\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}{\mathcal{M}}_{\nu}
can be written in a unique way as
n=pm
with
p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}
and
m\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\nu}
. Thus, we get
|{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}{\mathcal{M}}_{\nu}|=|{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}||{\mathcal{M}}_{\nu}|
and
{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}{\mathcal{M}}_{\nu}\subset[1,X]
. Moreover, the sets
{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}{\mathcal{M}}_{\nu}
are pairwise disjoint for
0\leq\nu\leq L
.

We are first concerned with the behavior of
S_{f}(X,\ell)
. By the decomposition above of
[1,X]
, the sum
S_{f}(X,\ell)
is divided into two parts as follows

\displaystyle S_{f}(X,\ell)=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{I}}}f(n)\tau(n+\ell)+\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{J}}}f(n)\tau(n+\ell)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle S_{f}^{{\mathcal{I}}}(X,\ell)+S_{f}^{{\mathcal{J}}}(X,\ell).

The aim is to turn out that
|S_{f}(X,-1)-S_{f}(X,h)|
is relatively small for any prime
h\in[X^{2/3},X(\log X)^{-A}]
. So we shall estimate the difference of these two sums over
{{\mathcal{I}}}
and
{\mathcal{J}}
, respectively.

5.
Evaluation of
S_{f}^{{\mathcal{I}}}(X,-1)-S_{f}^{{\mathcal{I}}}(X,h)

Since
{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}{\mathcal{M}}_{\nu}
are pairwise disjoint for
0\leq\nu\leq L
, we obtain that

(5.1)

S_{f}^{{\mathcal{I}}}(X,-1)-S_{f}^{{\mathcal{I}}}(X,h)\ll\sum_{0\leq\nu\leq L}\Big|\sum_{pm\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}{\mathcal{M}}_{\nu}}f(pm)\big(\tau(pm-1)-\tau(pm+h)\big)\Big|.

For
pm\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}{\mathcal{M}}_{\nu}
, we have
p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}
and
m\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\nu}
. The
f(pm)
in (
5.1
) can be factored as
f(p)f(m)
by its multiplicativity. Thus, we get

S_{f}^{{\mathcal{I}}}(X,-1)-S_{f}^{{\mathcal{I}}}(X,h)\ll\sum_{0\leq\nu\leq L}\sum_{m\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\nu}}|f(m)|\,\Big|\sum_{p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}}f(p)\big(\tau(pm-1)-\tau(pm+h)\big)\Big|.

Using the
Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and exchanging the order of summations, the inner sum can be performed as follows

(5.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\nu}}|f(m)|\Big|\sum_{p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}}f(p)\big(\tau(pm-1)-\tau(pm+h)\big)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{m\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\nu}}|f(m)|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{m\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\nu}}\Big|\sum_{p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}}f(p)\big(\tau(pm-1)-\tau(pm+h)\big)\Big|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{m\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\nu}}|f(m)|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{m\leq X/H_{\nu+1}}\Big|\sum_{p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}}f(p)\big(\tau(pm-1)-\tau(pm+h)\big)\Big|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{m\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\nu}}|f(m)|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{p_{1},p_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}}f(p_{1})\overline{f(p_{2})}\,\big(V_{1}-V_{2}-V_{3}+V_{4}\big)\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where the four terms
V_{j}\,(j=1,2,3,4)
are given by

\displaystyle V_{1}=\sum_{m\leq X/H_{\nu+1}}\tau(p_{1}m-1)\tau(p_{2}m-1),\quad V_{2}=\sum_{m\leq X/H_{\nu+1}}\tau(p_{1}m-1)\tau(p_{2}m+h),

\displaystyle V_{3}=\sum_{m\leq X/H_{\nu+1}}\tau(p_{1}m+h)\tau(p_{2}m-1),\quad V_{4}=\sum_{m\leq X/H_{\nu+1}}\tau(p_{1}m+h)\tau(p_{2}m+h).

The diagonal contribution in the last line of (
5.2
), that is
p_{1}=p_{2}
for each
\nu
yields at most

\begin{split}&\Big(\sum_{m\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\nu}}|f(m)|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}}|f(p)|^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
n\equiv 1,-h(\text{mod}\,p)\end{subarray}}|\tau(n)|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\ll&\frac{X^{\frac{1}{2}}(\log X)^{2}}{H_{\nu}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\Big(\sum_{p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu},m\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\nu}}|f(pm)|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},\end{split}

by Lemma
3.1
and the multiplicative property of
f(n)
. Hence, by summing over
\nu
and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, the diagonal contribution to
S_{f}^{{\mathcal{I}}}(X,-1)-S_{f}^{{\mathcal{I}}}(X,h)
is less than

(5.3)

\begin{split}&X^{\frac{1}{2}}(\log X)^{2}\Big(\sum_{0\leq\nu\leq L}H_{\nu}^{-1}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{0\leq\nu\leq L}\sum_{p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu},m\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\nu}}|f(pm)|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\ll&X^{\frac{1}{2}}\exp\big(-(\log x)^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\big)\Big(\sum_{n\leq X}|f(n)|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\ll&X\exp\big(-(\log x)^{\frac{\varepsilon}{3}}\big).\end{split}

Now we turn our attention to the off-diagonal terms.
For
p_{1}\neq p_{2}
, the sums
V_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq 4
are shifted convolution sums associated to the divisor function.
Therefore, we have to estimate

V(\varpi_{1},\varpi_{2})=\sum_{m\leq X/H_{\nu+1}}\tau(p_{1}m+\varpi_{1})\tau(p_{2}m+\varpi_{2}),

where
p_{1},p_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}
with
p_{1}\neq p_{2}
, and
\left(\varpi_{1},\varpi_{2}\right)
is one of the pairs

(-1,-1),(-1,h),(h,-1),(h,h).

Putting
X_{1}=p_{1}X/H_{\nu+1}-\varpi_{1}
and using the notation of Lemma
3.2

(5.4)

\displaystyle V(\varpi_{1},\varpi_{2})=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\leq\sqrt{X_{1}}\\
(d,p_{1})=1\end{subarray}}\left(2D\big(p_{2}X/H_{\nu+1}-\varpi_{2};dp_{2},\varpi_{2}-\varpi_{1}\bar{p_{1}}p_{2}\big)\right.

\displaystyle\left.-D\big(d\sqrt{X_{1}}p_{2}/p_{1}+\left(\varpi_{2}p_{1}-\varpi_{1}p_{2}\right)/p_{1};dp_{2},\varpi_{2}-\varpi_{1}\bar{p_{1}}p_{2}\big)\right)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle 2V^{(1)}(\varpi_{1},\varpi_{2})-V^{(2)}(\varpi_{1},\varpi_{2}),

where
p\bar{p}\equiv 1(\bmod d)
. By Lemma

, one has

D\left(p_{2}X/H_{\nu+1}-\varpi_{2};dp_{2},\varpi_{2}-\varpi_{1}\bar{p_{1}}p_{2}\right)\\
=D\big(X;dp_{2},\varpi_{2}-\varpi_{1}\bar{p_{1}}p_{2}\big)+O\Big(\frac{\tau(d)X}{dp_{2}(\log X)^{A-2}}\Big).

Moreover, the terms corresponding to

such that
p_{2}|d
in

contribute by the amount

, in which Lemma

is used again. Noticing that
X_{1}=X\big(1+O\big((\log X)^{-A}\big)\big)
, we further have

V^{(1)}\left(N;v_{1},v_{2}\right)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\leq\sqrt{X}\\

\displaystyle D\big(X;dp_{2},\varpi_{2}-\varpi_{1}\bar{p_{1}}p_{2}\big)=

(5.5)

\displaystyle V^{(1)}(\varpi_{1},\varpi_{2})=

\displaystyle\frac{X}{2p_{2}}\big(\log X+2\gamma-1\big)\big(\log X+2\gamma\big)Y^{(0)}\big(\sqrt{X};\varpi_{2}p_{1}-\varpi_{1}p_{2},p_{1}p_{2}\big)

\displaystyle-\frac{X}{p_{2}}\big(\log X+\log X+4\gamma-1\big)Y^{(1)}\big(\sqrt{X};\varpi_{2}p_{1}-\varpi_{1}p_{2},p_{1}p_{2}\big)

\displaystyle+\frac{2X}{p_{2}}Y^{(2)}\big(\sqrt{X};\varpi_{2}p_{1}-\varpi_{1}p_{2},p_{1}p_{2}\big)

\displaystyle+O\Big(\frac{X\log X}{H_{\nu}^{2}}Y(\varpi_{2}p_{1}-\varpi_{1}p_{2})\Big)+O\Big(\frac{X}{H_{\nu}(\log X)^{A-4}}\Big).

In the same way, we just get

(5.6)

\displaystyle V^{(2)}(\varpi_{1},\varpi_{2})=

\displaystyle\frac{X}{p_{2}}\big(\log X+2\gamma-2\big)Y^{(0)}\big(\sqrt{X};\varpi_{2}p_{1}-\varpi_{1}p_{2},p_{1}p_{2}\big)

\displaystyle-\frac{3X}{p_{2}}Y^{(1)}\big(\sqrt{X};\varpi_{2}p_{1}-\varpi_{1}p_{2},p_{1}p_{2}\big)

\displaystyle+O\Big(\frac{X\log X}{H_{\nu}^{2}}Y(\varpi_{2}p_{1}-\varpi_{1}p_{2})\Big)+O\Big(\frac{X}{H_{\nu}(\log X)^{A-4}}\Big).

Inserting (
5.5
) and (
5.6
) into (
5.4
), we have

\displaystyle V(\varpi_{1},\varpi_{2})=

\displaystyle\frac{X}{p_{2}}\big((\log X+2\gamma-1)(\log X+2\gamma-1)+1\big)Y^{(0)}\big(\sqrt{X};\varpi_{2}p_{1}-\varpi_{1}p_{2},p_{1}p_{2}\big)

\displaystyle-\frac{2X}{p_{2}}\big(\log X+4\gamma-\frac{5}{2}\big)\,Y^{(1)}\big(\sqrt{X};\varpi_{2}p_{1}-\varpi_{1}p_{2},p_{1}p_{2}\big)

\displaystyle+\frac{4X}{p_{2}}Y^{(2)}\big(\sqrt{X};\varpi_{2}p_{1}-\varpi_{1}p_{2},p_{1}p_{2}\big)

\displaystyle+O\Big(\frac{X\log X}{H_{\nu}^{2}}Y(\varpi_{2}p_{1}-\varpi_{1}p_{2})\Big)+O\Big(\frac{X}{H_{\nu}(\log X)^{A-4}}\Big).

Applying Lemma
3.3
, we derive that there exist three polynomials
w_{j}(x)
of degree
j
with numerical coefficients such that

\displaystyle V(\varpi_{1},\varpi_{2})=\frac{X}{H_{\nu}}\sum_{j=0}^{2}\sigma_{-1}^{(2-j)}(\varpi_{2}p_{1}-\varpi_{1}p_{2})w_{j}(\log X)+O\Big(\frac{X}{H_{\nu}(\log X)^{A-4}}\sigma_{-1}^{(0)}(\varpi_{2}p_{1}-\varpi_{1}p_{2})\Big).

We emphasize that the coefficients of
w_{j}
do not depend on
p_{1},p_{2},\varpi_{1},\varpi_{2}
. Then we obtain

(5.7)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1},p_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}\\
p_{1}\neq p_{2}\end{subarray}}f(p_{1})\overline{f(p_{2})}\,\big(V_{1}-V_{2}-V_{3}+V_{4}\big)

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\frac{X(\log X)^{2}}{H_{\nu}}\sum_{j=0}^{2}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1},p_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}\\
p_{1}\neq p_{2}\end{subarray}}f(p_{1})\overline{f(p_{2})}\big(\sigma_{-1}^{(j)}(p_{2}-p_{1})-\sigma_{-1}^{(j)}(hp_{2}-p_{1})-\sigma_{-1}^{(j)}(p_{2}-hp_{1})

\displaystyle+\sigma_{-1}^{(j)}(h(p_{2}-p_{1}))\big)\Big|+\frac{X}{H_{\nu}(\log X)^{A-4}}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varpi_{1}=-1,h\\
\varpi_{2}=-1,h\end{subarray}}\sum_{p_{1}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}}|f(p)|^{2}\sum_{p_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}}\sigma_{-1}^{(0)}(\varpi_{2}p_{1}-\varpi_{1}p_{2}).

By the definition of
\sigma_{-1}^{(j)}
and exchanging the order of summations, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1},p_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}\\
p_{1}\neq p_{2}\end{subarray}}f(p_{1})\overline{f(p_{2})}\sigma_{-1}^{(j)}(p_{2}-p_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p_{1},p_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}}f(p_{1})\overline{f(p_{2})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|(p_{2}-p_{1})\\
d\leq(\log H_{\nu})^{B}\end{subarray}}\frac{(\log d)^{j}}{d}+O\Big(\frac{H_{\nu}}{(\log X)^{A}(\log H_{\nu})^{B-1}}\sum_{p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}}|f(p)|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p_{1}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}}f(p_{1})\sum_{d\leq(\log H_{\nu})^{B}}\frac{(\log d)^{j}}{d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}\\
p_{2}\equiv p_{1}(\text{mod}\,d)\end{subarray}}\overline{f(p_{2})}+O\Big(\frac{H_{\nu}}{(\log X)^{A}(\log H_{\nu})^{B-1}}\sum_{p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}}|f(p)|^{2}\Big).

The contributions of
\sigma_{-1}^{(j)}(hp_{1}+p_{2}),\sigma_{-1}^{(j)}(p_{1}+hp_{2}),\sigma_{-1}^{(j)}(h(p_{1}-p_{2}))
can be computed in the same manner. Moreover, the last term in (
5.7
) is less than
\frac{X}{H_{\nu}(\log X)^{2A-4}}\sum\limits_{p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}}|f(p)|^{2}
. Accordingly, we can obtain from Hypothesis (iii) that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1},p_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}\\
p_{1}\neq p_{2}\end{subarray}}f(p_{1})\overline{f(p_{2})}\,\big(V_{1}-V_{2}-V_{3}+V_{4}\big)\ll\frac{X(\log X)^{2}}{(\log H_{\nu})^{B-1}}\sum_{p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}}\big(1+|f(p)|^{2}\big).

Here the
B
is an arbitrarily large constant. By inserting this into (
5.2
), summing over
\nu
and using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, the off-diagonal contribution to
S_{f}^{{\mathcal{I}}}(X,-1)-S_{f}^{{\mathcal{I}}}(X,h)
is bounded by

(5.8)

\begin{split}&X^{\frac{1}{2}}(\log X)\Big(\sum_{0\leq\nu\leq L}\frac{1}{(\log H_{\nu})^{B-1}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{0\leq\nu\leq L}\sum_{p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu},m\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\nu}}\big(|f(m)|^{2}+|f(pm)|^{2}\big)\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\ll\Big(\frac{X(\log X)^{A+1}}{(\log H)^{B-1}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{n\leq X}|f(n)|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\ll\frac{X(\log X)^{\frac{A+c}{2}}}{(\log H)^{\frac{B-1}{2}}}.\end{split}

Therefore, by taking
B=(3A+c)/\varepsilon+1
for any
A>0
, we obtain the following proposition from (
5.3
) and (
5.8
).

Proposition 5.1
.

Let the notation be as above. Then we have

S_{f}^{{\mathcal{I}}}(X,-1)-S_{f}^{{\mathcal{I}}}(X,h)\ll\frac{X}{(\log X)^{A}}

uniformly for any prime
h\in[X^{2/3},X(\log X)^{-A}]
and any
A>0
.

6.
Evaluation of

In order to estimate the contribution of corresponding sums over the set
{\mathcal{J}}
, we first define the following subsets of

\begin{split}{\mathcal{J}}_{1}^{(\nu)}&=\Big\{n\in[1,X]:n\ \text{has exactly one divisor in ${\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}$ and none in $\bigcup_{0\leq h<\nu}{\mathcal{P}}_{h}$}\Big\},\\
{\mathcal{J}}_{1}&=\bigcup_{0\leq\nu\leq L}{\mathcal{J}}_{1}^{(\nu)},\\
{\mathcal{J}}_{2}&=\Big\{n\in[1,X]:n\ \text{has at least one prime factor in $\bigcup_{0\leq\nu\leq L}{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}$}\Big\},\\
{\mathcal{J}}_{3}&=\Big\{n\in[1,X]:n\ \text{has no prime factors in ${\bigcup_{0\leq\nu\leq L}{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}}$}\Big\}.\end{split}

By the definitions of these subsets of
[1,X]
, it is clear that
P_{\nu}{\mathcal{M}}_{\nu}\subset{\mathcal{J}}_{1}^{(\nu)}
. Thus we have
{\mathcal{I}}\subset{\mathcal{J}}_{1}
. Moreover,
{\mathcal{J}}_{2}\cup{\mathcal{J}}_{3}=[1,X]
and
{\mathcal{J}}_{2}\cap{\mathcal{J}}_{3}=\emptyset
. Thus, we get

{\mathcal{J}}\subset({\mathcal{J}}_{1}\setminus{\mathcal{I}})\cup({\mathcal{J}}_{2}\setminus{\mathcal{J}}_{1})\cup{\mathcal{J}}_{3}.

As a consequence, it follows from Hypothesis (i) that

(6.1)

\displaystyle S_{f}^{{\mathcal{J}}}

\displaystyle(X,\ell)\ll\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{J}}_{1}\setminus{\mathcal{I}}}|f(n)|\tau(n+\ell)+\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{J}}_{2}\setminus{\mathcal{J}}_{1}}|f(n)|\tau(n+\ell)+\Big|\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{J}}_{3}}f(n)\tau(n+\ell)\Big|

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle X(\log X)^{\frac{c-1}{2}}\bigg(\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{J}}_{1}\setminus{\mathcal{I}}}|\tau(n+\ell)|^{2}+\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{J}}_{2}\setminus{\mathcal{J}}_{1}}|\tau(n+\ell)|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\Big|\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{J}}_{3}}f(n)\tau(n+\ell)\Big|.

For each
\nu\in[0,L]
, it is obvious that

{\mathcal{J}}_{1}^{(\nu)}\setminus\\
P_{\nu}{\mathcal{M}}_{\nu}\subset{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}\Big(\frac{X}{H_{\nu+1}},\frac{X}{H_{\nu}}\Big].

Hence, we have

\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{J}}_{1}\setminus{\mathcal{I}}}|\tau(n+\ell)|^{2}\ll\sum_{0\leq\nu\leq L}\sum_{p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|n-X+\ell|\ll X(\log X)^{-A}\\
n\equiv-\ell(\text{mod}\,p)\end{subarray}}|\tau(n)|^{2}.

The innermost sum is actually related to the coefficients

in arithmetic progression over a short interval. By the inequality in Lemma
3.1
, we deduce that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|n-X+\ell|\ll X(\log X)^{-A}\\
n\equiv-\ell(\text{mod}\,p)\end{subarray}}|\tau(n)|^{2}\ll\frac{X}{H_{\nu}(\log X)^{A-3}}.

Further, we obtain

(6.2)

\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{J}}_{1}\setminus{\mathcal{I}}}|\tau(n+\ell)|^{2}\ll\frac{X}{(\log X)^{A-4}}.

Moreover, on account of

{\mathcal{J}}_{2}\setminus{\mathcal{J}}_{1}\subset\bigcup_{0\leq\nu\leq L}\{n\in[1,X]:n\ \text{has at least two prime factors in ${\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}$}\},

we get

\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{J}}_{2}\setminus{\mathcal{J}}_{1}}|\tau(n+\ell)|^{2}\ll\sum_{0\leq\nu\leq L}\sum_{p_{1},p_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X+\ell\\
n\equiv-\ell(\text{mod}\,pq)\end{subarray}}|\tau(n)|^{2}.

Similar to the argument of (
6.2
), we use Lemma
3.1
again and then obtain

(6.3)

\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{J}}_{2}\setminus{\mathcal{J}}_{1}}|\tau(n+\ell)|^{2}\ll X(\log X)^{3}\sum_{0\leq\nu\leq L}\Big(\frac{|{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}|}{H_{\nu}}\Big)^{2}\ll\frac{X}{(\log X)^{A-4}}.

Moreover, it follows from the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality that

\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{J}}_{3}}f(n)\tau(n+\ell)\ll\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
(n,P)=1\end{subarray}}|f(n)|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
(n-\ell,P)=1\end{subarray}}\tau(n)^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
P=P\big(\exp((\log X)^{\varepsilon/2}),\,\exp((\log X)^{1-\varepsilon/2})\big)
.
Note that
\ell=-1
or
\ell=h\in[X^{2/3},X(\log X)^{-A}]
is a prime, so
(\ell,P)=1
. Directly applying Lemma
3.8
with
Y=\exp\big((\log X)^{\varepsilon/2}\big),Z=\exp\big((\log X)^{1-\varepsilon/2}\big),a=\ell
, and inserting Hypothesis (ii), we then get

(6.4)

\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{J}}_{3}}f(n)\tau(n+\ell)\leq X(\log X)^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}.

Combining (
6.2
)–(
6.4
) with (

), we finally conclude the following proposition.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let the notation be as above. For any prime
h\in[X^{2/3},X(\log X)^{-A}]
, we have

S_{f}^{{\mathcal{J}}}(X,-1)-S_{f}^{{\mathcal{J}}}(X,h)\ll X(\log X)^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}.

Remark 6.1
.

Assume the Ramanujan conjecture holds for
f(n)
, which means there exists some positive integer
k
such that
|f(n)|\leq\tau_{k}(n)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}.
Opening the divisor function and using Dirichlet’s hyperbola method, it reduces

\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{J}}_{3}}f(n)\tau(n+\ell)=2\sum_{d\leq\sqrt{X-\ell}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X,\,(n,P)=1\\
n\equiv\ell(\text{mod}\,d)\end{subarray}}f(n).

Note that
\ell=1
or
\ell=h\in[X^{2/3},X(\log X)^{-A}]
is a prime, so
(d,\ell)=1
. The Brun–Titchmarsh inequality
[
40
, Theorem 1]
gives

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X,\,(n,P)=1\\
n\equiv\ell(\text{mod}\,d)\end{subarray}}f(n)\ll\frac{X}{\varphi(d)\log X}\exp\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
p\nmid P\end{subarray}}\frac{|f(p)|}{p}\Big)\ll\frac{X}{\varphi(d)(\log X)^{1-k\varepsilon}}.

A slight estimate of Titchmarsh [42, Equation (3.2)] states that

\sum_{d\leq X}\frac{1}{\varphi(d)}=\frac{\zeta(2)\zeta(3)}{\zeta(6)}\log X+O(1).

Hence, we have, under the Ramanujan conjecture for

,

\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{J}}_{3}}f(n)\tau(n+\ell)\leq X(\log X)^{k\varepsilon}.

This yields that

S_{f}^{{\mathcal{J}}}(X,-1)-S_{f}^{{\mathcal{J}}}(X,h)\ll X(\log X)^{k\varepsilon}.

7.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

By Proposition
5.1
and Proposition
6.1
, we obtain

|S_{f}(X,-1)-S_{f}(X,h)|\ll X(\log X)^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}

uniformly for any prime
h\in[X^{2/3},X(\log X)^{-A}]
. Thus, we have

(7.1)

S_{f}(X,-1)=\frac{(\log X)^{A}}{X}T_{f}(X)+O\big(X(\log X)^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\big),

where
T_{f}(X)
is a ternary additive problem given by

T_{f}(X)=\sum_{h\leq X(\log X)^{-A}}\sum_{n\leq X}\Lambda(h)f(n)\tau(n+h).

Hence, the additive divisor problem that we are considering is reduced to the study of a ternary additive problem
T_{f}(X)
. The Hardy–Littlewood circle method can be applied. Here we provide another direct argument. Notice that the interval of summation over
h
is large enough compared to that over
n
. So we may exchange the order of summations in
T_{f}(X)
. Then it reduces to investigate the original divisor problem of Titchmarsh

T(Y,n)=\sum_{h\leq Y}\Lambda(h)\tau(h+n),

where the shift parameter
n
is in the interval
0<n\ll Y(\log Y)^{A}
.

By the definition of divisor function, we have

(7.2)

\tau(m)=2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\mid m\\
q<\sqrt{m}\end{subarray}}1+\delta_{\square}(m),\quad\delta_{\square}(m):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\text{ if }m\text{ is a perfect square,}\\
0&\text{ otherwise. }\end{array}\right.

Therefore, we deduce that

(7.3)

\displaystyle T(Y,n)=

\displaystyle 2\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq\sqrt{Y+n}\\
(q,n)=1\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h\leq Y\\
h\equiv-n({\rm mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}\Lambda(h)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sqrt{n}<q\leq\sqrt{Y+n}\\
(q,n)=1\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h\leq q^{2}-n\\
h\equiv-n({\rm mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}\Lambda(h)\Big)+O\big(Y^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\big)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle 2\big(S_{1}(Y,n)-S_{2}(Y,n)\big)+O\big(Y^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\big).

It follows from Lemma
3.6
that

(7.4)

S_{1}(Y,n)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq\sqrt{Y+n}\\
(q,n)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{h\leq Y}\Lambda(h)+O\Big(\frac{Y}{(\log Y)^{A}}\Big),

where the ranges of all parameters have been considered. Now we turn to estimate
S_{2}(Y,n)
, and hope that
S_{2}(Y,n)
can be approximated by

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sqrt{n}<q\leq\sqrt{Y+n}\\
(q,n)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{h\leq q^{2}-n}\Lambda(h).

In fact, we shall show that the difference of
S_{2}(Y,n)
and its approximation satisfies

(7.5)

U(Y,n):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sqrt{n}<q\leq\sqrt{Y+n}\\
(q,n)=1\end{subarray}}\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h\leq q^{2}-n\\
h\equiv-n({\rm mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}\Lambda(h)-\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{h\leq q^{2}-n}\Lambda(h)\bigg)\ll\frac{Y}{(\log Y)^{A}}.

Since the condition
h\leq q^{2}-n
makes
q
and
h
interconnected, Lemma
3.6
can not be directly used. To relax this condition, we shall decompose the sums over
q
and
h
into short intervals
\big(Q(1+\Delta_{1})^{-1},Q\big]
and
\big(H(1+\Delta_{1})^{-1},H\big]
, where
\Delta_{1}=(\log Y)^{-B}
with
B>A+3
.
Then we have

\displaystyle U(Y,n)\ll

\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1},j_{2}\geq 0}\bigg|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q(1+\Delta_{1})^{-1}<q\leq Q\\
(q,n)=1\end{subarray}}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}H(1+\Delta_{1})^{-1}<h\leq H\\
h\leq q^{2}-n\\
n\equiv-n({\rm mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}\Lambda(h)-\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}H(1+\Delta_{1})^{-1}<h\leq H\\
h\leq q^{2}-n\\
(h,q)=1\end{subarray}}\Lambda(h)\Big)\bigg|,

where
Q=\sqrt{Y+n}(1+\Delta_{1})^{-j_{1}},H=Y(1+\Delta_{1})^{-j_{2}}
. Note that the condition
n\leq q^{2}
can be dropped as soon as

H+n\leq Q^{2}(1+\Delta_{1})^{-2}.

The contribution of
j_{1},j_{2}
such that
H+n>Q^{2}(1+\Delta_{1})^{-2}

is at most

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sqrt{n}<q\leq\sqrt{Y+n}\\
(q,n)=1\end{subarray}}\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(q^{2}-n)(1+\Delta_{1})^{-3}<h\leq q^{2}-n\\
h\equiv-n({\rm mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}\Lambda(h)\ll

\displaystyle(\log Y)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sqrt{n}<q\leq\sqrt{Y+n}\\
(q,n)=1\end{subarray}}\Big(\frac{\Delta_{1}(q^{2}-n)}{q}+1\Big)

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\Delta_{1}Y(\log Y)^{2}.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle U(Y,n)\ll

\displaystyle\Delta_{1}Y(\log Y)^{2}+\Delta_{1}^{-2}(\log Y)^{2}

\displaystyle\times\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\leq\sqrt{Y+n}\\
H\leq Q^{2}-n\end{subarray}}\bigg|\sum_{Q(1+\Delta_{1})^{-1}<q\leq Q\atop(q,n)=1}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}H(1+\Delta_{1})^{-1}<h\leq H\\
h\equiv-n({\rm mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}\Lambda(h)-\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}H(1+\Delta_{1})^{-1}<h\leq H\\
(h,q)=1\end{subarray}}\Lambda(h)\Big)\bigg|.

Let
\delta>0
be the real number given in Lemma
3.6
. It is clear that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q(1+\Delta_{1})^{-1}<q\leq Q\\
(q,n)=1\end{subarray}}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}H(1+\Delta_{1})^{-1}<h\leq H\\
h\equiv-n({\rm mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}\Lambda(h)-\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}H(1+\Delta_{1})^{-1}<h\leq H\\
(h,q)=1\end{subarray}}\Lambda(h)\Big)\ll\Delta_{1}H(\log H),

which is acceptable if
H\leq\Delta_{1}^{2}\,Y
. Suppose
H>\Delta_{1}^{2}\,Y
, then Lemma
3.6
can be applied and yields

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q(1+\Delta_{1})^{-1}<q\leq Q\\
(q,n)=1\end{subarray}}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}H(1+\Delta_{1})^{-1}<h\leq H\\
n\equiv-n({\rm mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}\Lambda(h)-\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}H(1+\Delta_{1})^{-1}<n\leq H\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}\Lambda(h)\Big)\ll\frac{H}{(\log H)^{3A}}.

In summary, we have the claimed estimate (
7.5
), which means

(7.6)

S_{2}(Y,n)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sqrt{n}<q\leq\sqrt{Y+n}\\
(q,n)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{h\leq q^{2}-n}\Lambda(h)+O\Big(\frac{Y}{(\log Y)^{A}}\Big).

Inserting (
7.4
) and (
7.6
) into (
7.3
), and applying Lemma
3.7
and partial summation, we have

\displaystyle T(Y,n)=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{h\leq Y}\Lambda(h)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq\sqrt{h+n}\\
(q,n)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}+O\Big(\frac{Y}{(\log Y)^{A}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{0}(n)\big((Y+n)\log(Y+n)-n\log n+(2\gamma-1)Y\big)+2c_{0}^{\prime}(n)Y+O\Big(\frac{Y}{(\log Y)^{A}}\Big),

where
Y=X/(\log X)^{A}
. Substituting this asymptotic formula into (

), we then get

\displaystyle S_{f}(X,-1)=\sum_{n\leq X}f(n)\big(c_{0}(n)(\log n+2\gamma)+2c_{0}^{\prime}(n)\big)+E_{f}(X,-1)+O\big(X(\log X)^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\big),

where the term
E_{f}(X,-1)
satisfies

E_{f}(X,-1)\ll\sum_{n\leq X}|f(n)c_{0}(n)|\log\big(1+\frac{Y}{n}\big)+\sum_{n\leq X}|f(n)c_{0}(n)|\,\Big|\frac{n}{Y}\log\big(1+\frac{Y}{n}\big)-1\Big|.

Notice that
c_{0}(n)\ll 1
. It follows from partial summation and Hypothesis (i) that

E_{f}(X,-1)\ll\frac{X}{(\log X)^{A-\frac{c+1}{2}}}.

Hence, taking
A>c/2
gives

S_{f}(X,-1)=\sum_{n\leq X}f(n)\big(c_{0}(n)(\log n+2\gamma)+2c_{0}^{\prime}(n)\big)+O\big(X(\log X)^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\big).

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2

For convenience, we put

E(f,X;q):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
n\equiv 1({\rm mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}f(n)-\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}f(n).

Let
c_{q}
be given by
c_{q}=\text{sgn}\,E(f,X;q)
. With the notation as in (

), we divide
E(f,X;q)
into two parts
E^{{\mathcal{I}}}(f,X;q)
and

, which are the corresponding sums with additional restrictions
n\in{\mathcal{I}}
and

, respectively.

8.1.
Contribution of
E^{{\mathcal{I}}}(f,X;q)

Considering the
q
in dyadic ranges, it suffices to estimate

\sum_{q\sim Q}c_{q}E^{{\mathcal{I}}}(f,X;q)

with
Q\leq X^{17/33-\varepsilon}.
By the definition of interval
{\mathcal{I}}
and using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we have

(8.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{q\sim Q}c_{q}E^{{\mathcal{I}}}(f,X;q)=

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq\nu\leq L}\sum_{m\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\nu}}f(m)\Big(\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}q\sim Q\ p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}\\
pm\equiv 1({\rm mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}{\sum\ \sum}c_{q}f(p)-\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}q\sim Q\ p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}\\
(pm,q)=1\end{subarray}}{\sum\ \sum}\frac{c_{q}f(p)}{\varphi(q)}\Big)

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq\nu\leq L}\Big(\sum_{m\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\nu}}|f(m)|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}T_{\nu}(Q)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
T_{\nu}(Q)
is defined by

T_{\nu}(Q)=\sum_{m\leq X/H_{\nu+1}}\Big|\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}q\sim Q\ p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}\\
pm\equiv 1({\rm mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}{\sum\ \sum}c_{q}f(p)-\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}q\sim Q\ p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}\\
(pm,q)=1\end{subarray}}{\sum\ \sum}\frac{c_{q}f(p)}{\varphi(q)}\Big|^{2}.

By smooth dyadic subdivisions and squaring out
T_{\nu}(Q)
, we then write

(8.2)

\displaystyle T_{\nu}(Q)\ll(\log X)\max_{M\leq X/H_{\nu+1}}\sum_{m}\psi\big(\frac{m}{M}\big)\Big|\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}q\sim Q\ p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}\\
pm\equiv 1({\rm mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}{\sum\ \sum}c_{q}f(p)-\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}q\sim Q\ p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}\\
(pm,q)=1\end{subarray}}{\sum\ \sum}\frac{c_{q}f(p)}{\varphi(q)}\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\log X)\max_{M\in[X^{3/4},X/H_{\nu+1}]}\big(W_{\nu}(Q,M)-2\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}V_{\nu}(Q,M)+U_{\nu}(Q,M)\big)+O\big(X^{\frac{3}{4}+\varepsilon}\big),

where
\psi
is the smooth function as in Lemma
3.4
, and
W_{\nu}(Q,M),V_{\nu}(Q,M),U_{\nu}(Q,M)
are defined by

\displaystyle W_{\nu}(Q,M)=

\displaystyle\sum_{m}\psi\big(\frac{m}{M}\big)\Big|\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}q\sim Q\ p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}\\
pm\equiv 1({\rm mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}{\sum\ \sum}c_{q}f(p)\Big|^{2},

\displaystyle V_{\nu}(Q,M)=

\displaystyle\sum_{m}\psi\big(\frac{m}{M}\big)\,\Big(\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}q\sim Q\ p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}\\
pm\equiv 1({\rm mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}{\sum\ \sum}c_{q}f(p)\Big)\,\Big(\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}q\sim Q\ p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}\\
(pm,q)=1\end{subarray}}{\sum\ \sum}\frac{\overline{c_{q}f(p)}}{\varphi(q)}\Big),

\displaystyle U_{\nu}(Q,M)=

\displaystyle\sum_{m}\psi\big(\frac{m}{M}\big)\Big|\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}q\sim Q\ p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}\\
(pm,q)=1\end{subarray}}{\sum\ \sum}\frac{c_{q}f(p)}{\varphi(q)}\Big|^{2}.

Our goal is to evaluate each term individually.

8.2.
Evaluation of
U_{\nu}(Q,M)

Let us begin with the simplest term

U_{\nu}(Q,M)=\sum_{q_{1}\sim Q}\sum_{q_{2}\sim Q}\frac{c_{q_{1}}\overline{c_{q_{2}}}}{\varphi(q_{1})\varphi(q_{2})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}\\
(p_{1},q_{1})=1\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}\\
(p_{2},q_{2})=1\end{subarray}}f(p_{1})\overline{f(p_{2})}\sum_{(m,q_{1}q_{2})=1}\psi\Big(\frac{m}{M}\Big).

By Poisson’s formula (
3.2
) in Lemma
3.4
, the innermost sum over
m
is

\sum_{(m,q_{1}q_{2})=1}\psi\Big(\frac{m}{M}\Big)=\frac{\varphi(q_{1}q_{2})}{q_{1}q_{2}}\hat{\psi}(0)M+O\big(\tau(q_{1})\tau(q_{2})(\log X)^{4}\big).

This yields

U_{\nu}(Q,M)=\hat{\psi}(0)M\sum_{q_{1}\sim Q}\sum_{q_{2}\sim Q}\frac{c_{q_{1}}\overline{c_{q_{2}}}}{\varphi(q_{1})\varphi(q_{2})}\,\frac{\varphi(q_{1}q_{2})}{q_{1}q_{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}\\
(p_{1},q_{1})=1\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}\\
(p_{2},q_{2})=1\end{subarray}}f(p_{1})\overline{f(p_{2})}+O\Big(\frac{H_{v}^{2}}{(\log X)^{\frac{A}{2}-8}}\Big).

We remove the conditions
(p_{1},q_{1})=1
and
(p_{2},q_{2})=1
at the cost
of the admissible error term
O(MH_{\nu})
. Moreover, we denote
\delta=(q_{1},q_{2})
, and then get

(8.3)

\displaystyle U_{\nu}(Q,M)=

\displaystyle\hat{\psi}(0)M\sum_{\delta}\frac{1}{\delta\varphi(\delta)}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2}\sim Q/\delta\\
(k_{1},k_{2})=1\end{subarray}}{\sum\ \sum}\frac{c_{\delta k_{1}}\overline{c_{\delta k_{2}}}}{k_{1}k_{2}}\Bigl(\sum_{p_{1}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}}f(p_{1})\Bigr)\,\Bigl(\sum_{p_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}}\overline{f(p_{2})}\Bigr)

\displaystyle+O(MH_{\nu})+O\Big(\frac{H_{v}^{2}}{(\log X)^{\frac{A}{2}-8}}\Big).

8.3.
Evaluation of
V_{\nu}(Q,M)

By the definition of
V_{\nu}(Q,M)
and exchanging the order of summations, we have

(8.4)

V_{\nu}(Q,M)=\sum_{q_{1}\sim Q}\sum_{q_{2}\sim Q}\frac{c_{q_{1}}\overline{c_{q_{2}}}}{\varphi(q_{2})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}\\
(p_{1},q_{1})=1\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}\\
(p_{2},q_{2})=1\end{subarray}}f(p_{1})\overline{f(p_{2})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\equiv\overline{p_{1}}(\text{mod}\,q_{1})\\
(m,q_{2})=1\end{subarray}}\psi\Bigl(\frac{m}{M}\Bigr),

where
\overline{p_{1}}
denotes the multiplicative inverse of
p_{1}
modulo
q_{1}
. By applying the Möbius inversion formula and inserting Poisson’s formula (
3.1
) with
H=dq_{1}(\log X)^{4}/M
, the innermost sum is equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\equiv\overline{p_{1}}(\text{mod}\,q_{1})\\
(m,q_{2})=1\end{subarray}}\psi\Bigl(\frac{m}{M}\Bigr)=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|q_{2}\\
(d,q_{1})=1\end{subarray}}\mu(d)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\equiv\eta(\text{mod}\,dq_{1})\end{subarray}}\psi\Bigl(\frac{m}{M}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\hat{\psi}(0)M}{q_{1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|q_{2}\\
(d,q_{1})=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(d)}{d}+O\big(\tau(q_{2})(\log X)^{4}\big),

where
\eta
is a common solution of the congruences
\eta\equiv\overline{p_{1}}(\text{mod}\,q_{1})
and
\eta\equiv 0(\text{mod}\,d)
. Inserting this into (
8.4
), we deduce that

\displaystyle V_{\nu}(Q,M)=

\displaystyle\hat{\psi}(0)M\sum_{\delta}\frac{1}{\delta\varphi(\delta)}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}r_{1},r_{2}\sim Q/\delta\\
(r_{1},r_{2})=1\end{subarray}}{\sum\ \sum}\frac{c_{\delta r_{1}}\overline{c_{\delta r_{2}}}}{r_{1}r_{2}}\Bigl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}\\
(p_{1},q_{1})=1\end{subarray}}f(p_{1})\Bigr)\,\Bigl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}\\
(p_{2},q_{2})=1\end{subarray}}\overline{f(p_{2})}\Bigr)

\displaystyle+O\Big(\frac{QH_{v}^{2}}{(\log X)^{\frac{A}{2}-6}}\Big).

Comparing this with (
8.3
), we obtain the relation

(8.5)

V_{\nu}(Q,M)=U_{\nu}(Q,M)+O(MH_{\nu})+O\Big(\frac{QH_{v}^{2}}{(\log X)^{\frac{A}{2}-8}}\Big).

8.4.
Evaluation of
W_{\nu}(Q,M)

The evaluation of
W_{\nu}(Q,M)
is the most difficult and it involves the key arguments. Before
applying them, in this section we reduce the range of the summation by elementary estimates. By definition, we have

(8.6)

W_{\nu}(Q,M)=\sum_{q_{1}\sim Q}\sum_{q_{2}\sim Q}c_{q_{1}}\overline{c_{q_{2}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1},p_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}\\
p_{1}\neq p_{2}\end{subarray}}f(p_{1})\overline{f(p_{2})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1}m\equiv 1(\text{mod}\,q_{1})\\
p_{2}m\equiv 1(\text{mod}\,q_{2})\end{subarray}}\psi\Bigl(\frac{m}{M}\Bigr)+O(H_{\nu}M\log X),

where the error term comes from the diagonal contribution for
p_{1}=p_{2}
. For the off-diagonal terms, we first factorize each of the variables
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
, to control the contributions of their greatest common divisor in some ranges. For notational conventions, we decompose the variables
q_{1},q_{2}
in a unique way as follows:

\begin{cases}\delta=(q_{1},q_{2}),\\
q_{1}=\delta k_{1},\,q_{2}=\delta k_{2},\\
k_{1}=\delta_{1}k^{\prime}_{1}\text{ with }\delta_{1}|\delta^{\infty}\text{ and }(k^{\prime}_{1},\delta)=1,\\
k_{2}=\delta_{2}k^{\prime}_{2}\text{ with }\delta_{2}|\delta^{\infty}\text{ and }(k^{\prime}_{2},\delta)=1.\end{cases}

Note that the conditions of summation over
m
in (
8.6
) imply that we necessarily have

p_{1}\equiv p_{2}(\text{mod}\,\delta),

Denote
W_{\nu}^{(1)}(Q,M)
to be the sum of the terms in (
8.6
) with
\delta>D:=\exp\big((\log X)^{\varepsilon/4}\big)
. Thus, we obtain from Lemma
3.1
that

(8.7)

\displaystyle W_{\nu}^{(1)}(Q,M)\ll

\displaystyle\sum_{\delta>X^{\varepsilon}}\sum_{k_{2}\sim Q/\delta}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1},p_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}\\
p_{1}\equiv p_{2}(\text{mod}\,\delta)\end{subarray}}|f(p_{1})|^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}M/2<m<5M/2\\
m\equiv\overline{p_{2}}(\text{mod}\,\delta k_{2})\end{subarray}}\tau(|p_{1}m-a|)

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\frac{H_{\nu}^{2}M(\log X)^{4}}{D}.

Denote
W_{\nu}^{(2)}(Q,M)
to be the sum of terms in (
8.6
) with
\delta\leq D
and
\delta_{1}>D
. Then we obtain

(8.8)

\displaystyle W_{\nu}^{(2)}(Q,M)\ll

\displaystyle\sum_{\delta\leq D}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta_{1}|\delta^{\infty}\\
\delta_{1}>X^{\varepsilon}\end{subarray}}\ \sum_{k^{\prime}_{1}\sim Q/(\delta\delta_{1})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1},p_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}\\
p_{1}\equiv p_{2}(\text{mod}\,\delta)\end{subarray}}|f(p_{1})|^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}M/2<m<5M/2\\
m\equiv\overline{p_{2}}(\text{mod}\,\delta\delta_{1}k^{\prime}_{1})\end{subarray}}\tau(|p_{2}m-a|)

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\frac{H_{\nu}^{2}M}{D^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

In the same manner, the sum of of terms in (
8.6
) with
\delta\leq D
and
\delta_{2}>D
is also bounded by
O\big(H_{\nu}^{2}M/D^{1/2}\big)
.

Now, we define
W_{\nu}^{(0)}(Q,M)
to be the partial sum on the left-hand side of (
8.6
) restricted by

\delta\leq D,\delta_{1}\leq D,\delta_{2}\leq D.

Then we get from the discussion before that

W_{\nu}(Q,M)=W_{\nu}^{(0)}(Q,M)+O\Big(\frac{H_{\nu}^{2}M}{D^{\frac{1}{2}}}\Big),

where
W_{\nu}^{(0)}(Q,M)
can be written as

(8.9)

\sum_{\delta,\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\leq D}\sum_{k^{\prime}_{1}\sim Q/(\delta\delta_{1})\ }\sum_{k^{\prime}_{2}\sim Q/(\delta\delta_{2})}c_{\delta\delta_{1}k^{\prime}_{1}}\overline{c_{\delta\delta_{2}k^{\prime}_{2}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1},p_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}\\
p_{1}\neq p_{2}\end{subarray}}f(p_{1})\overline{f(p_{2})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\equiv\overline{p_{1}}(\text{mod}\,\delta\delta_{1}k^{\prime}_{1})\\
m\equiv\overline{p_{2}}(\text{mod}\,\delta\delta_{2}k^{\prime}_{2})\end{subarray}}\psi\Bigl(\frac{m}{M}\Bigr).

The congruence conditions in (
8.9
) are equivalent to the four congruences

\displaystyle m\equiv\overline{p_{1}}(\text{mod}\,\delta\delta_{1}),\quad m\equiv\overline{p_{2}}(\text{mod}\,\delta\delta_{2}),

\displaystyle m\equiv\overline{p_{1}}(\text{mod}\,k^{\prime}_{1}),\quad\;\,m\equiv\overline{p_{2}}(\text{mod}\,k^{\prime}_{2}).

The first two equations are equivalent to
m\equiv a\lambda(\text{mod}\,\delta\delta_{1}\delta_{2})
, where
\lambda(p_{1},p_{2})
is some congruence class modulo
\delta\delta_{1}\delta_{2}
, only depending on the congruence classes of
p_{1}\bmod\delta\delta_{1}
and
p_{2}\bmod\delta\delta_{2}
. Finally, we see that
m
satisfies the single congruence

m\equiv m_{0}(\text{mod}\,\eta k^{\prime}_{1}k^{\prime}_{2}),

with

\eta=\delta\delta_{1}\delta_{2}

and

m_{0}=\lambda\,k^{\prime}_{1}k^{\prime}_{2}\,\overline{k^{\prime}_{1}}\,\overline{k^{\prime}_{2}}+\eta\,\overline{\eta}\,\overline{p_{1}}\,k^{\prime}_{2}\,\overline{k^{\prime}_{2}}+\,\eta\,\overline{\eta}\,\overline{p_{2}}\,k^{\prime}_{1}\overline{k^{\prime}_{1}}(\text{mod}\,\eta k^{\prime}_{1}k^{\prime}_{2}),

where the
\overline{x}
–symbol respectively means the inverse of
x
modulo
\eta
,
k^{\prime}_{1}
and
k^{\prime}_{2}
.

We then apply Poisson’s formula in Lemma
3.4
and deduce

(8.10)

\sum_{m\equiv m_{0}(\text{mod}\,\eta k^{\prime}_{1}k^{\prime}_{2})}\psi\Bigl(\frac{m}{M}\Bigr)=\hat{\psi}(0)\frac{M}{\eta k^{\prime}_{1}k^{\prime}_{2}}+\frac{M}{\eta k^{\prime}_{1}k^{\prime}_{2}}\sum_{1\leq|h|\leq H}e\bigl(\frac{hm_{0}}{\eta k^{\prime}_{1}k^{\prime}_{2}}\bigr)\hat{\psi}\Bigl(\frac{hM}{\eta k^{\prime}_{1}k^{\prime}_{2}}\Bigr)+O(M^{-1})

with

H=M^{-1}Q^{2}(\log X)^{4}.

Inserting (
8.10
) into (
8.9
), we obtain

(8.11)

W_{\nu}^{(0)}(Q,M)=\hat{\psi}(0)M{\mathcal{R}}+M{\mathcal{R}}_{1}+O\Big(\frac{H_{\nu}^{2}Q^{2}}{M}\Big),

where

{\mathcal{R}}=\sum_{\delta,\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\leq D}\sum_{k^{\prime}_{1}\sim Q/(\delta\delta_{1})\ }\sum_{k^{\prime}_{2}\sim Q/(\delta\delta_{2})}\frac{c_{\delta\delta_{1}k^{\prime}_{1}}\overline{c_{\delta\delta_{2}k^{\prime}_{2}}}}{\delta\delta_{1}\delta_{2}k^{\prime}_{1}k^{\prime}_{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1},p_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}\\
p_{1}\equiv p_{2}(\text{mod}\,\delta)\end{subarray}}f(p_{1})\overline{f(p_{2})},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}_{1}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\delta,\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\leq D}\sum_{k^{\prime}_{1}\sim Q/(\delta\delta_{1})\ }\sum_{k^{\prime}_{2}\sim Q/(\delta\delta_{2})}\frac{c_{\delta\delta_{1}k^{\prime}_{1}}\overline{c_{\delta\delta_{2}k^{\prime}_{2}}}}{\eta k^{\prime}_{1}k^{\prime}_{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1},p_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}\\
p_{1}\equiv p_{2}(\text{mod}\,\delta)\end{subarray}}f(p_{1})\overline{f(p_{2})}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{1\leq|h|\leq H}e\bigl(\frac{hm_{0}}{\eta k^{\prime}_{1}k^{\prime}_{2}}\bigr)\hat{\psi}\Bigl(\frac{hM}{\eta k^{\prime}_{1}k^{\prime}_{2}}\Bigr)

and the error term comes from the contribution of the term
O(M^{-1})
in (
8.10
). Now it remains to evaluate
{\mathcal{R}}
and
{\mathcal{R}}_{1}
.

We first treat the term
{\mathcal{R}}
, which can be rewritten as

{\mathcal{R}}=\sum_{\delta,\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\leq D}\sum_{k^{\prime}_{1}\sim Q/(\delta\delta_{1})\ }\sum_{k^{\prime}_{2}\sim Q/(\delta\delta_{2})}\frac{c_{\delta\delta_{1}k^{\prime}_{1}}\overline{c_{\delta\delta_{2}k^{\prime}_{2}}}}{\delta\delta_{1}\delta_{2}k^{\prime}_{1}k^{\prime}_{2}}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\alpha(\text{mod}\,\delta)}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}\\
p_{1}\equiv\alpha(\text{mod}\,\delta)\end{subarray}}f(p_{1})\Big)\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}\\
p_{2}\equiv\alpha(\text{mod}\,\delta)\end{subarray}}\overline{f(p_{2})}\Big).

By Hypotheses (ii) and (iii), the Barban–Davenport–Halberstam theorem (see
[
17
, Theorem 17.5]
) yields

(8.12)

\sum_{k\leq K}\;\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\alpha(\text{mod}\,k)}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}\\
p\equiv\alpha(\text{mod}\,k)\end{subarray}}f(p)-\frac{1}{\varphi(k)}\sum_{p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}}f(p)\Big|^{2}\ll\frac{H_{\nu}^{2}}{(\log X)^{16A}}

provided that
K\leq\exp\big((\log X)^{\varepsilon/2}\big)
. Then it follows from (
8.12
) that

{\mathcal{R}}={\mathcal{R}}_{0}+O\Big(\frac{H_{\nu}^{2}}{(\log X)^{8A}}\Big),

where
{\mathcal{R}}_{0}
is given by

{\mathcal{R}}_{0}=\sum_{\delta,\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\leq D}\sum_{k^{\prime}_{1}\sim Q/(\delta\delta_{1})\ }\sum_{k^{\prime}_{2}\sim Q/(\delta\delta_{2})}\frac{c_{\delta\delta_{1}k^{\prime}_{1}}\overline{c_{\delta\delta_{2}k^{\prime}_{2}}}}{\delta\delta_{1}\delta_{2}k^{\prime}_{1}k^{\prime}_{2}\varphi(\delta)}\Big(\sum_{p_{1}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}}f(p_{1})\Big)\Big(\sum_{p_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}}\overline{f(p_{2})}\Big).

We next extend the summation over all
\delta,\delta_{1},\delta_{2}
to get

{\mathcal{R}}_{0}={\mathcal{R}}^{*}+O\big(D^{-\frac{1}{2}}H_{\nu}^{2}\big)

the error term being estimated by similar arguments as those for (
8.7
) and (
8.8
), where
{\mathcal{R}}^{*}
is given by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}^{*}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\delta,\delta_{1},\delta_{2}}\ \sum_{k^{\prime}_{1}\sim Q/(\delta\delta_{1})\ }\sum_{k^{\prime}_{2}\sim Q/(\delta\delta_{2})}\frac{c_{\delta\delta_{1}k^{\prime}_{1}}\overline{c_{\delta\delta_{2}k^{\prime}_{2}}}}{\delta\delta_{1}\delta_{2}k^{\prime}_{1}k^{\prime}_{2}\varphi(\delta)}\Big(\sum_{p_{1}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}}f(p_{1})\Big)\Big(\sum_{p_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}}\overline{f(p_{2})}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\delta}\frac{1}{\delta\varphi(\delta)}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2}\sim Q/\delta\\
(k_{1},k_{2})=1\end{subarray}}{\sum\sum}\frac{c_{\delta k_{1}}\overline{c_{\delta k_{2}}}}{\delta k_{1}k_{2}}\Big(\sum_{p_{1}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}}f(p_{1})\Big)\Big(\sum_{p_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}}\overline{f(p_{2})}\Big).

So we can conclude that

(8.13)

{\mathcal{R}}={\mathcal{R}}^{*}+O\Big(\frac{H_{\nu}^{2}}{(\log X)^{16A}}\Big).

We are left to estimate
{\mathcal{R}}_{1}.
By definition of
m_{0},
we know

e\bigl(\frac{hm_{0}}{\eta k^{\prime}_{1}k^{\prime}_{2}}\bigr)=e\Bigl(\lambda h\frac{\overline{k^{\prime}_{1}}\,\overline{k^{\prime}_{2}}}{\eta}+h\frac{\overline{\eta}\,\overline{p_{1}}\,\overline{k^{\prime}_{2}}}{k^{\prime}_{1}}+h\frac{\overline{\eta}\,\overline{p_{2}}\,\overline{k^{\prime}_{1}}}{k^{\prime}_{2}}\Bigr).

In order to transform the exponent, we apply Bezout’s relation twice to write

\displaystyle\frac{\overline{\eta}\,\overline{p_{1}}\,\overline{k^{\prime}_{2}}}{k^{\prime}_{1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\eta p_{1}k^{\prime}_{1}k^{\prime}_{2}}-\frac{\overline{k^{\prime}_{1}}}{\eta p_{1}k^{\prime}_{2}}\,(\text{mod}\,1)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\eta p_{1}k^{\prime}_{1}k^{\prime}_{2}}-\frac{\overline{\eta}\,\overline{k^{\prime}_{1}}}{p_{1}k^{\prime}_{2}}-\frac{\overline{p_{1}}\,\overline{k^{\prime}_{1}}\,\overline{k^{\prime}_{2}}}{\eta}\,(\text{mod}\,1).

Moreover, we have

\frac{\overline{\eta}\,\overline{p_{2}}\,\overline{k^{\prime}_{1}}}{k^{\prime}_{2}}-\frac{\overline{\eta}\,\overline{k^{\prime}_{1}}}{p_{1}k^{\prime}_{2}}=\frac{\overline{\eta}(p_{1}-p_{2})\overline{p_{2}k^{\prime}_{1}}}{p_{1}k^{\prime}_{2}}\,(\text{mod}\,1).

Thus, these relations yield

e\bigl(\frac{hm_{0}}{\eta k^{\prime}_{1}k^{\prime}_{2}}\bigr)=e\Bigl(\lambda h\frac{\overline{k^{\prime}_{1}}\,\overline{k^{\prime}_{2}}}{\eta}-h\frac{\overline{p_{1}}\,\overline{k^{\prime}_{1}}\,\overline{k^{\prime}_{2}}}{\eta}+\frac{h}{\eta p_{1}k^{\prime}_{1}k^{\prime}_{2}}+h\frac{\overline{\eta}(p_{1}-p_{2})\overline{p_{2}k^{\prime}_{1}}}{p_{1}k^{\prime}_{2}}\Bigr).

Next we split up the summation over
p_{1},p_{2},h,k^{\prime}_{1},k^{\prime}_{2}
into arithmetic progressions
\alpha_{i}(\text{mod}\,\eta),1\leq i\leq 5
. If we fix the congruence class
\alpha_{i}(\text{mod}\,\eta)
, then the exponents
\lambda h\frac{\overline{k^{\prime}_{1}}\,\overline{k^{\prime}_{2}}}{\eta}
and
h\frac{\overline{p_{1}}\,\overline{k^{\prime}_{1}}\,\overline{k^{\prime}_{2}}}{\eta}
are fixed. Thus, we further have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}_{1}\ll D^{18}\max_{\alpha_{i}\leq D^{3}\atop\delta,\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\leq D}\Big|

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1},p_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}\\
p_{1}\neq p_{2}\end{subarray}}\sum_{1\leq|h|\leq H}\sum_{k^{\prime}_{1}\sim Q/(\delta\delta_{1})\ }\sum_{k^{\prime}_{2}\sim Q/(\delta\delta_{2})}\frac{c_{\delta\delta_{1}k^{\prime}_{1}}\overline{c_{\delta\delta_{2}k^{\prime}_{2}}}}{k^{\prime}_{1}k^{\prime}_{2}}f(p_{1})\overline{f(p_{2})}

\displaystyle\times\hat{\psi}\Bigl(\frac{hM}{\eta k^{\prime}_{1}k^{\prime}_{2}}\Bigr)e\Bigl(\frac{h}{\eta p_{1}k^{\prime}_{1}k^{\prime}_{2}}\Bigr)\,e\Bigl(h\frac{\overline{\eta}(p_{1}-p_{2})\overline{p_{2}k^{\prime}_{1}}}{p_{1}k^{\prime}_{2}}\Bigr)\Big|,

where the variables
p_{1},p_{2}
satisfy the congruence condition
p_{1}\equiv p_{2}(\text{mod}\,\delta)
, and
p_{1},p_{2},h,k^{\prime}_{1},k^{\prime}_{2}
are in congruence classes
\alpha_{i}(\text{mod}\,\eta),1\leq i\leq 5
, respectively.

A straightforward calculation gives

\frac{\partial^{a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}+a_{4}}}{\partial h^{a_{1}}\partial p_{1}^{a_{2}}\partial{k^{\prime}}_{1}^{a_{3}}\partial{k^{\prime}}_{2}^{a_{4}}}\Bigl\{e\Bigl(\frac{h}{\eta p_{1}k^{\prime}_{1}k^{\prime}_{2}}\Bigr)\hat{\psi}\Bigl(\frac{hM}{\gamma k^{\prime}_{1}k^{\prime}_{2}}\Bigr)\Bigr\}\ll(1+|h|)^{-a_{1}}p_{1}^{-a_{2}}{k^{\prime}}_{1}^{-a_{3}}{k^{\prime}}_{2}^{-a_{4}}

for integers
0\leq a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\leq 1
,
p_{1}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu},k_{1}^{\prime},k_{2}^{\prime}\in[Q/D^{2},2Q]
and
h\in[-H,H]
. By partial summations over variables
p_{1},h,k_{1}^{\prime},k_{2}^{\prime}
, we obtain

(8.14)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}_{1}\ll

\displaystyle\frac{D^{22}\log X}{Q^{2}}\max_{\delta,\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\leq D}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1},p_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}\\
p_{1}\neq p_{2}\end{subarray}}\big|f(p_{1})f(p_{2})\big|

\displaystyle\times\Big|\sum_{1\leq|h|\leq H}\sum_{k^{\prime}_{1},k^{\prime}_{2}\leq 2Q}\xi_{1}(h)\xi_{2}(k^{\prime}_{1})\xi_{3}(k^{\prime}_{2})e\Bigl(h\frac{\overline{\eta}(p_{1}-p_{2})\overline{p_{2}k^{\prime}_{1}}}{p_{1}k^{\prime}_{2}}\Bigr)\Big|,

where
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3}
are three sequences of complex numbers with
|\xi_{1}(n)|,|\xi_{2}(n)|,|\xi_{3}(n)|\leq 1
. Notice that the symmetry of variables
p_{1},p_{2}
allows us to without loss of generality replace the condition
1\leq|h|\leq H
by
1\leq h\leq H
.

We shall apply Lemma
3.5
to bound the exponential sum on the second line of (
8.14
). So we localize each of the variables
h,k^{\prime}_{1},k^{\prime}_{2}
dyadically around powers of two that we denote respectively by
H_{1},K_{1},K_{2}
.
For each such dyadic partition and fixed variables
p_{1},p_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu},\eta\leq D^{3}
, we apply Lemma
3.5
with the following choice of variables:

\vartheta\rightarrow p_{1}-p_{2},\;a\rightarrow h,\;m\rightarrow\eta p_{2}k^{\prime}_{1},\;n\rightarrow p_{1}k^{\prime}_{2},

where the left side of
\rightarrow
corresponds to notations of Lemma
3.5
while the right side of
\rightarrow
corresponds to our current notation)
and parameters

|\vartheta|\ll H_{\nu},\ A\rightarrow H_{1},\ M\rightarrow\eta p_{2}K_{1},\ N\rightarrow p_{1}K_{2}.

Note that
\eta p_{2}K_{1}\ll D^{3}H_{\nu}K_{1}
and that
p_{1}K_{2}\ll H_{\nu}K_{2}
. Hence, we appeal to Lemma
3.5
to infer

\displaystyle\sum_{h\sim H_{1}}\sum_{k^{\prime}_{1}\sim K_{1}}

\displaystyle\sum_{k^{\prime}_{2}\sim K_{2}}\xi_{1}(h)\xi_{2}(k^{\prime}_{1})\xi_{3}(k^{\prime}_{2})e\Bigl(h\frac{\overline{\eta}(p_{1}-p_{2})\overline{p_{2}k^{\prime}_{1}}}{p_{1}k^{\prime}_{2}}\Bigr)\ll D^{2}(H_{1}K_{1}K_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigl(1+\frac{H_{1}}{H_{\nu}K_{1}K_{2}}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\times\Big((H_{1}H_{\nu}^{2}K_{1}K_{2})^{\frac{7}{20}+\varepsilon}(H_{\nu}(K_{1}+K_{2}))^{\frac{1}{4}}+(H_{1}H_{\nu}^{2}K_{1}K_{2})^{\frac{3}{8}+\varepsilon}(H_{1}H_{\nu}(K_{1}+K_{2}))^{\frac{1}{8}}\Big).

Summing over all the dyadic partitions and applying Hypothesis (ii), we then get

(8.15)

{\mathcal{R}}_{1}\ll D^{25}\bigl(M^{-\frac{17}{20}}H_{\nu}^{\frac{59}{20}}Q^{\frac{33}{20}}+M^{-1}H_{\nu}^{\frac{23}{8}}Q^{\frac{15}{8}}\bigr).

Finally, inserting the estimates (
8.13
), (
8.15
) of
{\mathcal{R}}
and
{\mathcal{R}}_{1}
into (
8.11
), we have

W_{\nu}(Q,M)=\hat{\psi}(0)M{\mathcal{R}}^{*}+O\Big(M^{\frac{3}{20}+\varepsilon}H_{\nu}^{\frac{59}{20}}Q^{\frac{33}{20}}+M^{\varepsilon}H_{\nu}^{\frac{23}{8}}Q^{\frac{15}{8}}+\frac{MH_{\nu}^{2}}{(\log X)^{8A}}\Big).

Comparing this with (
8.3
), we also have the relation

(8.16)

W_{\nu}(Q,M)=U_{\nu}(Q,M)+O\Big(M^{\frac{3}{20}+\varepsilon}H_{\nu}^{\frac{59}{20}}Q^{\frac{33}{20}}+M^{\varepsilon}H_{\nu}^{\frac{23}{8}}Q^{\frac{15}{8}}+\frac{MH_{\nu}^{2}}{(\log X)^{8A}}\Big).

8.5.
Return Section 8.1

We recall that
Q\leq X^{17/33-\varepsilon}.
Substituting (
8.3
), (
8.5
) and (
8.16
) into (
8.2
), we see

T_{\nu}(Q)\ll\frac{XH_{\nu}}{(\log X)^{4A}},

which further yields from (
8.1
) that

(8.17)

\sum_{q\leq Q}c_{q}E^{{\mathcal{I}}}(f,X;q)\ll\frac{X}{(\log X)^{A-2}}

holds for any
A\geq 2
.

8.6.
Contribution of
E^{{\mathcal{J}}}(f,X;q)

With the notation as in Section 6, we have

(8.18)

\displaystyle\sum_{q\leq Q}c_{q}E^{{\mathcal{J}}}(f,X;q)\ll

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{J}}}|f(n)|\sum_{q\leq Q\atop n\equiv 1({\rm mod}\,q)}|c_{q}|+(\log Q)\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{J}}}|f(n)|

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{J}}}|f(n)|\tau(n-1)+(\log X)\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{J}}}|f(n)|

The first term on the second line of (
8.18
) has been estimated in Section 6, which gives

(8.19)

\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{J}}}f(n)\tau(n-1)\ll X(\log X)^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}.

Now we treat the second term on the second line of (
8.18
). By the decomposition of
{\mathcal{J}}
and Hypothesis (i), we have

(8.20)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{J}}}|f(n)|

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{J}}_{1}\setminus{\mathcal{I}}}|f(n)|+\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{J}}_{2}\setminus{\mathcal{J}}_{1}}|f(n)|+\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{J}}_{3}}|f(n)|

\displaystyle\ll X(\log X)^{\frac{c-1}{2}}(|{\mathcal{J}}_{1}\setminus{\mathcal{I}}|^{\frac{1}{2}}+|{\mathcal{J}}_{2}\setminus{\mathcal{J}}_{1}|^{\frac{1}{2}})+\Big(\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{J}}_{3}}|f(n)|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}|{\mathcal{J}}_{3}|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Moreover, it follows from the definitions of these sets that

|{\mathcal{J}}_{1}\setminus{\mathcal{I}}|\ll\sum_{0\leq\nu\leq L}\sum_{p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}}\frac{X}{H_{\nu}(\log X)^{A}}\ll\frac{X}{(\log X)^{A-1}},

|{\mathcal{J}}_{2}\setminus{\mathcal{J}}_{1}|\ll\sum_{0\leq\nu\leq L}\sum_{p_{1},p_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\nu}}\frac{X}{p_{1}\,p_{2}}\ll\frac{X}{(\log X)^{A-1}}.

and

|{\mathcal{J}}_{3}|=\sum_{n\leq X\atop(n,P)=1}1\ll\frac{X}{(\log X)^{1-\varepsilon}},

where
P=P\big(\exp\big((\log X)^{\varepsilon/2}\big),\,\exp\big((\log X)^{1-\varepsilon/2}\big)\big)
, and
A
is an arbitrarily constant with
A>c+3
. Inserting these estimates and Hypothesis (ii) into (
8.20
), we get

\sum_{n\in{\mathcal{J}}}|f(n)|\ll\frac{X}{(\log X)^{1-\varepsilon}}.

Substituting this and (
8.19
) into (
8.18
), we finally derive

(8.21)

\sum_{q\leq Q}c_{q}E^{{\mathcal{J}}}(f,X;q)\ll X(\log X)^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
, in view of (
8.17
).

9.
Second proof of Theorem
1.1

We shall first deduce the following estimate from Section 8

(9.1)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq\sqrt{X}\end{subarray}}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq q^{2}\\
n\equiv 1({\rm mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}f(n)-\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq q^{2}\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}f(n)\Big)\ll_{\varepsilon}X(\log X)^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}.

With notation
{\mathcal{I}},{\mathcal{J}}
as in Section 8, it is easily seen from the argument in (
8.18
)-(
8.21
) that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq\sqrt{X}\end{subarray}}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq q^{2},\ n\in{\mathcal{J}}\\
n\equiv 1({\rm mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}f(n)-\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq q^{2},\ n\in{\mathcal{J}}\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}f(n)\Big)\ll_{\varepsilon}X(\log X)^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}.

Then it suffices to estimate the contribution of terms from
n\in{\mathcal{I}}
.
Similar to the argument in Section 7, we shall decompose the sums over
q
and
n
into short intervals
\big(Q(1+\Delta_{2})^{-1},Q\big]
and
\big(N(1+\Delta_{2})^{-1},N\big]
. Then we have

\displaystyle T^{{\mathcal{I}}}:=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq\sqrt{X}\end{subarray}}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq q^{2},\ n\in{\mathcal{I}}\\
n\equiv 1({\rm mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}f(n)-\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq q^{2},\ n\in{\mathcal{I}}\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}f(n)\Big)

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1},j_{2}\geq 0}\bigg|\sum_{Q(1+\Delta_{2})^{-1}<q\leq Q}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N(1+\Delta_{2})^{-1}<n\leq N\\
n\leq q^{2},\ n\in{\mathcal{I}}\\
n\equiv 1({\rm mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}f(n)-\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N(1+\Delta_{2})^{-1}<n\leq N\\
n\leq q^{2},\ n\in{\mathcal{I}}\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}f(n)\Big)\bigg|

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1},j_{2}\geq 0}|T^{{\mathcal{I}}}_{j_{1},j_{2}}|,

where
Q=\sqrt{X}(1+\Delta_{2})^{-j_{1}},N=X(1+\Delta_{2})^{-j_{2}}
. Since
n<N
and
q>Q(1+\Delta_{2})^{-1}
in each sum
T^{{\mathcal{I}}}_{j_{1},j_{2}}
, the condition
n\leq q^{2}
can be dropped as soon as

(9.2)

N\leq Q^{2}(1+\Delta_{2})^{-2}.

If the condition (
9.2
) is satisfied, the variables
q,n
are independent. Thus, we can apply the estimate (
8.17
) for
N\geq X^{1-\varepsilon}
and the large sieve inequality for
N<X^{1-\varepsilon}
. These give

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1},j_{2}\geq 0\\
N\leq Q^{2}(1+\Delta_{2})^{-2}\end{subarray}}|T^{{\mathcal{I}}}_{j_{1},j_{2}}|\ll\Delta_{2}^{-2}(\log X)^{2}\frac{X}{(\log X)^{A}}

for any
A>0
. It remains to estimate the contribution of
j_{1},j_{2}
when (
9.2
) is not satisfied. The treatment here is different from the corresponding in Section 7, since there is no good upper bound for the individual
f(n)
. From
n(1+\Delta_{2})>N
and
q\leq Q
, we deduce that
n>q^{2}(1+\Delta_{2})^{-3}
. So the contribution of
j_{1},j_{2}
such that
N>Q^{2}(1+\Delta_{2})^{-2}
is at most

(9.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{q\leq\sqrt{X}}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q^{2}(1+\Delta_{1})^{-3}<n\leq q^{2}\\
n\equiv 1({\rm mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}|f(n)|+\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q^{2}(1+\Delta_{1})^{-3}<n\leq q^{2}\end{subarray}}|f(n)|\Big)

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle X^{\frac{1}{2}}(\log X)^{\frac{c-1}{2}}\bigg(\sum_{n\leq X}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sqrt{n}\leq q\leq\sqrt{n}(1+\Delta_{1})^{3/2}\\
q|(n-1)\end{subarray}}1\Big)^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\Delta_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}X(\log X)^{\frac{c-1}{2}},

where we exchange the order of summations over
q
and
n
, and use the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and Hypothesis (i). We next use the trivial bound
\tau(n-1)
for the sum over
q
once and exchange the order of summations again. This gives

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq X}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sqrt{n}\leq q\leq\sqrt{n}(1+\Delta_{1})^{3/2}\\
q|(n-1)\end{subarray}}1\Big)^{2}\ll

\displaystyle\sum_{q\leq\sqrt{X}(1+\Delta_{1})^{3/2}}\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q^{2}(1+\Delta_{1})^{-3}<n\leq q^{2}\\
n\equiv 1({\rm mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}\tau(n-1)

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\sum_{q\leq\sqrt{X}(1+\Delta_{1})^{3/2}}\tau(q)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q(1+\Delta_{1})^{-3}-1<n\leq q\end{subarray}}\tau(n)

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\Delta_{1}X(\log X)^{2}.

Inserting this into (
9.3
), we obtain that the contribution of
j_{1},j_{2}
such that
N\geq X^{1-\varepsilon}
is bounded by
O\big(\Delta_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}X(\log X)^{\frac{c+1}{2}}\big)
. On taking
\Delta_{1}=(\log X)^{-A/3}
for some sufficiently large
A
, we get

T^{{\mathcal{I}}}\ll\frac{X}{(\log X)^{A/4}},

which further implies the estimate (
9.1
).

By the identity (
7.2
) and Hypothesis (i), we have

\sum_{n\leq X}f(n)\tau(n-1)=2\sum_{q\leq\sqrt{X}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q^{2}<n\leq X\\
n\equiv 1({\rm mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}f(n)+O\big(X^{\frac{3}{4}+\varepsilon}\big).

According to Theorem
1.2
and the estimate (
9.1
), we get

\sum_{n\leq X}f(n)\tau(n-1)=2\sum_{q\leq\sqrt{X}}\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q^{2}<n\leq X\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}f(n)+O\big(X(\log X)^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\big).

Finally, Theorem
1.1
follows from exchanging the order of summations and inserting the estimate in Lemma
3.7
.

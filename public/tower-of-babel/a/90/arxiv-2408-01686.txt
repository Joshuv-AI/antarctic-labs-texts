Standing waves with prescribed mass for Schrödinger equations with competing Van Der Waals type potentials
Shuai Yao, Hichem Hajaiej, Juntao Sun

Abstract
We investigate standing waves with prescribed mass for a class of Schrödinger equations with competing Van Der Waals type potentials, arising in a model of non-relativistic bosonic atoms and molecules. By developing an approach based on a direct minimization of the energy functional on a new constrained manifold, we establish the existence of two normalized solutions for the corresponding stationary problem. One is a local minimizer with positive level and the other one is a global minimizer with negative level. Moreover, we find that the global minimizer is farther away from the origin than the local minimizer. Finally, we explore the relations between the ground state solution and the least action solution, and some dynamical behavior and scattering results are presented as well.



Keywords:
Schrödinger equation; competing nonlinearities;
variational methods; dynamics.

Introduction

In this paper, we are concerned with the following Schrödinger equations
with Van Der Waals type potentials

i\partial_{t}\psi+\Delta\psi+(W_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\ast|\psi|^{2})\psi=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R\times R}^{N},

(1)

where
\psi(t,x)
is the complex valued function in the spacetime
\mathbb{R\times R}^{N}
(
N\geq 3
), and

W_{\alpha,\beta}(x)=\mu_{\alpha}|x|^{-\alpha}+\mu_{\beta}|x|^{-\beta}\quad\text{with }\mu_{\alpha},\mu_{\beta}\in\mathbb{R}.

(
1
) comes from a model of non-relativistic bosonic
atoms and molecules, which has two interacting potentials, and this
interaction is weaker and has a longer range than the Dirac delta type
potential, see
[
10
]
. Physically, if
\alpha>1
and
\beta>1
,
W_{\alpha,\beta}(x)
is the Van Der Waals type potential, also known as
the Van Der Waals interaction, it represents the potential energy between two
neutral molecules and is a consequence of the attractive and repulsive
forces between the molecules. It is a crucial concept in the study of
intermolecular forces in physics and chemistry, see
[
9
,
42
,
46
]
. The
Van Der Waals coefficient
\mu_{6}
,
\mu_{8}
and
\mu_{10}
associated
with alkaline-earth interactions, which were calculated by Porsev and
Derevianko
[
34
]
through the employment of relativistic many-body
perturbation theory, are regarded as having an accuracy reaching up to
1/100
. Moreover, (
1
) also arises as a mean-field limit of a
bosonic system with attractive two-body interactions which can be taken
rigorously in many cases
[
24
]
.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\mu_{\alpha}=1
. According to the distributive law of convolution, this leads to the
study of the following nonlocal equation

i\partial_{t}\psi+\Delta\psi+(|x|^{-\alpha}\ast|\psi|^{2})\psi+\mu_{\beta}(|x|^{-\beta}\ast|\psi|^{2})\psi=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R\times R}^{N}.

(2)

For the Cauchy problem to (
2
), when the initial data
\psi_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we can study the finite Hamiltonian or the finite
energy solutions, which satisfy the energy (Hamiltonian), mass and momentum
conservations:

E[\psi(t)]:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\psi|^{2}dx-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|x|^{-\alpha}\ast|\psi|^{2})|\psi|^{2}dx-\frac{\mu_{\beta}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|x|^{-\beta}\ast|\psi|^{2})|\psi|^{2}dx=E[\psi_{0}],

M[\psi(t)]:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\psi|^{2}dx=M[\psi_{0}],

P[\psi(t)]:=\mathrm{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\bar{\psi}\nabla\psi dx=P[\psi_{0}].

By using standard contraction mapping argument
[
1
,
5
]
, we can get the local
well-posedness result.

Theorem 1.1

(i)
(The energy subcritical case) Let
N\geq 3
,
\mu_{\beta}\in\mathbb{R}
,
0<\alpha<\beta<\min\left\{N,4\right\}
and
the initial data
\psi(0,x)=\psi_{0}(x)\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).
Then
there exists
T_{max}>0
and a unique solution
\psi(t,x)\in C([0,T_{\max});H^{1})
of the Cauchy problem to (
2
).

(ii)
(The energy critical case) Let
N\geq 5
,
\mu_{\beta}\in\mathbb{R}
,
0<\alpha<\beta=4
and
the initial data
\psi(0,x)=\psi_{0}(x)\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).
If
\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{1}}\leq\eta
for some small
\eta>0
, then there exists
T_{max}>0
and a unique solution
\psi(t,x)\in C([0,T_{\max});H^{1})
of the Cauchy problem to (
2
).

An important feature related to the nonlinear time dependent equations such
as (
2
) is to study standing waves. A standing wave of (
2
)
is a solution of the form

\psi(t,x)=e^{i\lambda t}u(x),

where the frequency
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and the real value function
u

satisfies the stationary equation

-\Delta u+\lambda u=(|x|^{-\alpha}\ast\left|u\right|^{2})u+\mu_{\beta}(|x|^{-\beta}\ast\left|u\right|^{2})u\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}.

(3)

Meanwhile, we note that the mass of the solutions to the Cauchy problem
(
2
) is conserved through time, that is, the
L^{2}
-norms of standing
waves are independent of
t
. Therefore it is interesting to study the existence
of standing waves with prescribed mass, which are usually called
normalized solutions
. In this case
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
is unknown and appears as a Lagrange
multiplier. This study seems particularly meaningful from the physical point
of view, since this type of solutions enjoys nice stability properties. In order to
find normalized solutions, we need consider the following problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u+\lambda u=(|x|^{-\alpha}\ast\left|u\right|^{2})u+\mu_{\beta}(|x|^{-\beta}\ast\left|u\right|^{2})u&\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}dx=c,&\end{array}\right.

(4)

where
c>0
given. Solutions of problem (
4
) can be obtained as
critical points of the energy functional
E:H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
given by

E(u):=\frac{1}{2}A\left(u\right)-\frac{1}{4}B_{\alpha}\left(u\right)-\frac{\mu_{\beta}}{4}B_{\beta}\left(u\right)

on the constraint

S(c):=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}dx=c\right\},

where

A\left(u\right):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}dx,\ B_{\alpha}\left(u\right):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(|x|^{-\alpha}\ast\left|u\right|^{2}\right)\left|u\right|^{2}dx\text{ and }B_{\beta}\left(u\right):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(|x|^{-\beta}\ast\left|u\right|^{2}\right)\left|u\right|^{2}dx.

It is easy to show that
E
is a well-defined and
C^{1}
functional on
S(c)
for
3\leq N\leq 4
,
0<\alpha,\beta<\min\left\{N,4\right\}
and
N\geq 5
,
0<\alpha,\beta\leq 4
.

When
\alpha=\beta
, (
3

-\Delta u+\lambda u=\left(|x|^{-\alpha}\ast|u|^{2}\right)u\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}.

(5)

Especially, when

and
\alpha=1
, (

quantum theory of a polaron at rest

32
]

studied as a mass subcritical issue. For example, Lieb

normalized solutions. While for the mass supercritical case

[
29

obtained a normalized solution for (

). Recently, for
the Choquard equation

-\Delta u+\lambda u=\left(|x|^{-\alpha}\ast|u|^{p}\right)|u|^{p-2}u\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},

Li and Ye
[
25
]
showed that if
N\geq 3
,
0<\alpha<N
and
\frac{2N-\alpha+2}{N}<p<\frac{2N-\alpha}{N-2}
, then there exists a
mountain-pass normalized solution for each
c>0.
We refer the reader to
[
7
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
37
,
38
,
43
]
and the references therein, which discuss the normalized solution and stability for similar or more general equations.

When
\alpha\neq\beta
, the situation becomes much more complicated. In
order to better investigate the number and properties of normalized
solutions to problem (
4
), we can present the map to help
to understand the geometry of
E|_{S(c)}
. Such map is known as fibering map and was introduced in
[
35
,
36
,
39
]
. Specifically, for each
u\in S(c)

and
s>0
, we set the dilations

u_{s}\left(x\right):=s^{N/2}u\left(sx\right)\text{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

(6)

Define the fibering map
s\in\left(0,\infty\right)\mapsto g_{u}\left(s\right):=E\left(u_{s}\right)
given by

g_{u}\left(s\right)=\frac{s^{2}}{2}A\left(u\right)-\frac{s^{\alpha}}{4}B_{\alpha}\left(u\right)-\frac{\mu_{\beta}s^{\beta}}{4}B_{\beta}\left(u\right).

(7)

Moreover, we also define the Pohozaev manifold

\mathcal{P}\left(c\right):=\left\{u\in S(c):Q\left(u\right)=0\right\}=\left\{u\in S(c):g_{u}^{\prime}\left(1\right)=0\right\},

(8)

where
Q\left(u\right)=0
is the corresponding Pohozaev type identity of (
3
). It is well-known that any critical point of
E|_{S(c)}
stays
in
\mathcal{P}\left(c\right)
. Notice that the critical points of
g_{u}

allow to project a function on
\mathcal{P}\left(c\right).
Thus,
the monotonicity and the convexity properties of
g_{u}
strongly affect the
structure of
\mathcal{P}\left(c\right)
(and in turn the geometry of

), and also have a strong impact on properties of the
time-dependent equation (
2

In this paper, we focus on the unsolved case, i.e. Case
(iv)
. Our aim is
to develop a method that will enable us to establish more accurate results on the
multiplicity and dynamics of standing waves with a prescribed mass for (
2

solutions to (
4

with prescribed mass of (
2

some essential differences in the geometry of the corresponding energy
functionals

between Case
(ii)
and Case
(iv)
from the variational
point of view, although they have the same number of normalized solutions.
Secondly, we study the relations between the ground state solution and the least action solution based on a rigorous analysis. It is worth emphasizing that one has
to face more challenges, such as previously mentioned aspects. In order to
overcome considerable difficulties, new ideas and techniques have been
explored. More details will be discussed in the next subsection.

1.1
Main results

Definition 1.2

We say that a solution

Let

where

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u+\lambda u=\left(|x|^{-\alpha}\ast u^{2}\right)u&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N},\\

(
HE_{\infty}
)

where

and
2<\alpha<\min\left\{N,4\right\}
.

Theorem 1.3

Assume that
\mu_{\beta}<0
and one of the two following conditions hold:

(i)

(ii)

Then for

problem (

Remark 1.4

(i)
Notice that the solution

with negative level is

(ii)
For

\mu_{\ast}=\frac{2(\alpha-2)^{3}}{\alpha^{3}\mathcal{S}_{4}m_{\infty}}\left(\frac{4-\alpha}{2}\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha-2}}.

According to

(iii)

In Theorem
1.3
, in order to find the solution
u^{-}
with positive

we propose a novel approach based on the direct minimization of the
energy functional
E
on the following new constrained manifold

\mathcal{P}_{D}^{-}\left(c\right):=\left\{u\in\mathcal{V}_{D}:g_{u}^{\prime}\left(1\right)=0,g_{u}^{\prime\prime}\left(1\right)<0\right\},

where

\mathcal{V}_{D}:=\left\{u\in D(c):\Gamma A(u)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}<B_{\alpha}(u)\right\}

with

D(c):=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}dx\leq c\right\}.

(10)

This approach can be viewed as an integration of the approaches in

,
8
]
.
The advantage of the new constrained manifold is that we can guarantee that an arbitrary

can always be
projected onto
\mathcal{P}_{D}^{-}\left(c\right).
Furthermore, we do not
work in the radial functions space

and do not
need to consider Palais-Smale sequences, so that we avoid applying the relatively complex
mini-max approach based on a strong topological argument as in
[

,
8
,
13
,

]
. We also avoid excluding the possibility that the critical
point falls on the boundary of the open subset
[
8
,
44
]
. To recover
the compactness, a novel strategy is to take advantage of the profile
decomposition of the minimizing sequence and the precise parameter control.
It is worth noting that the mountain pass theorem developed by Jeanjean and

[

]
can be also used to find the solution with positive level for

(iv)
. However, the energy functional

need to be restricted in

not in

, and the mass

The second solution
\tilde{u}
with negative level is
obtained as a global minimizer in Theorem

. The method is mainly based on the
concentration-compactness lemma. We firstly establish a new inequality
estimate to prove that the energy functional

is bounded from below on

, and then use the fibering map method to prove that the infimum is
negative, which can help us to rule out the vanishing of the minimizing
sequence. Finally, we introduce a new test function of the dilation
transformation, together with the method in
[

]
, to establish the
strict subadditive inequality to exclude the dichotomy of the minimizing
sequence, so that the compactness can be achieved.

Next, we show the relations between the ground state solution and the least action solution.

Definition 1.5

Given

Let
\lambda(u)
be the Lagrange
multiplier corresponding to an arbitrary minimizer

of problem (

). Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.3
, then the following statements hold.

(i)

of problem (

) with
\lambda=\lambda(u)>0
. Moreover,

(ii)
For given
\lambda\in\left\{\lambda(u):u\in S(c)\text{is a minimizer of problem (\ref{e1-2})}\right\}
, any least action solution

Remark 1.8

Recall the following well-known Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality and
Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality.

Lemma 2.1
(Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality
[
31
,
45
]
)

Let
N\geq 3
. Then there exists a best constant
\mathcal{S}_{\gamma}:=\mathcal{S}_{\gamma}(N,\gamma)>0
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|x|^{-\gamma}\ast|u|^{2})|u|^{2}dx\leq\mathcal{S}_{\gamma}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{\gamma}\|u\|_{2}^{4-\gamma}.

(11)

Here,

\mathcal{S}_{\gamma}=\left(\frac{4-\gamma}{\gamma}\right)^{\gamma/2}\frac{4}{(4-\gamma)\|Q_{\gamma}\|_{2}^{2}},

where
Q_{\gamma}
is a ground state of the following Hartree equation

-\Delta Q+Q=(|x|^{-\gamma}\ast|Q|^{2})Q\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Lemma 2.2
(Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality
[
27
]
)

Assume that
1<a<b<+\infty
is such that
\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{b}+\frac{\gamma}{N}=2.
Then there exists
\mathcal{C}_{HLS}(N,\gamma)>0
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|f(x)||g(y)|}{|x-y|^{\gamma}}\leq\mathcal{C}_{HLS}\|f\|_{a}\|g\|_{b},\quad\forall f\in L^{a}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\text{ and }\forall g\in L^{b}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Remark 2.3

For
\gamma=4
, by
[
11
]
, we know that

\mathcal{S}_{4}^{-\frac{1}{2}}=\inf_{u\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\backslash\left\{0\right\}}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx}{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|x|^{-4}\ast|u|^{2})|u|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}},

where
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\left\{u\in L^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\nabla u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\right\}
. Moreover,
\mathcal{S}_{4}^{-\frac{1}{2}}
is achieved if and only if

U_{\varepsilon,y}(x):=\varepsilon^{\frac{2-N}{2}}U(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon}),\,\forall\varepsilon>0,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

where

U(x):=S^{\frac{4-N}{4}}\mathcal{C}_{HLS}(N,4)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{[N(N-2)]^{\frac{N-2}{4}}}{(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{N-2}{2}}}

with
\mathcal{C}_{HLS}(N,4)=\pi^{2}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N-4}{2})}{\Gamma(N-2)}\left\{\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})}{\Gamma(N)}\right\}^{\frac{4-N}{N}}
and
S
being the best constant for the Sobolev embedding
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Lemma 2.4

Let
u
be a weak solution to the equation

-\Delta u+\lambda u=\left(|x|^{-\alpha}\ast u^{2}\right)u+\mu_{\beta}\left(|x|^{-\beta}\ast u^{2}\right)u.

Then we have following Pohozaev identity

\frac{N-2}{2}A\left(u\right)+\frac{\lambda N}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}dx=\frac{2N-\alpha}{4}B_{\alpha}\left(u\right)+\frac{\mu_{\beta}\left(2N-\beta\right)}{4}B_{\beta}\left(u\right).

(12)

Moreover, there holds

A\left(u\right)-\frac{\alpha}{4}B_{\alpha}\left(u\right)-\frac{\mu_{\beta}\beta}{4}B_{\beta}\left(u\right)=0.

Proof.
Let
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\phi(x)=1
on
B_{1}(0)
,
\phi(x)=0
for
x\not\in B_{2}(0)
and
|\nabla\phi(x)|\leq 2

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Since the function
\phi
has compact support,
we can define
\phi_{\tau}:=\phi(\tau x)x\cdot\nabla u(x)\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for
\tau\in(0,+\infty)
. By testing (
3
) with
the function
\phi_{\tau}
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi_{\tau}dx+\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u\phi_{\tau}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|x|^{-\alpha}\ast|u|^{2})u\phi_{\tau}dx+\mu_{\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|x|^{-\beta}\ast|u|^{2})u\phi_{\tau}dx.

By using an integration by parts, we derive

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi_{\tau}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\phi(\tau x)\left(|\nabla u(x)|^{2}+x\cdot\nabla(|\nabla u|^{2}/2)(x)\right)dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\tau\nabla u(x)\cdot\nabla\phi(\tau x))(x\cdot\nabla u(x))dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left((N-2)\phi(\tau x)+\tau x\cdot\nabla\phi(\tau x)\right)|\nabla u(x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\nabla u(x)\cdot\nabla\phi(\tau x))(\tau x\cdot\nabla u(x))dx.

Since
|(\eta\cdot\zeta)(\eta\cdot\xi)|\leq|\eta|^{2}|\zeta||\xi|

for any
\zeta,\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we have

|(\nabla u(x)\cdot\nabla\phi(\tau x))(\tau x\cdot\nabla u(x))|\leq|\nabla u(x)|^{2}|\tau x||\nabla\phi(\tau x)|\leq|\nabla u(x)|^{2}\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}|z||\nabla\phi(z)|.

Taking a limit as
\tau\rightarrow 0
, by Lebesgue’s dominated convergence
theorem, we obtain

\lim_{\tau\rightarrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi_{\tau}dx=-\frac{N-2}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx.

In light of the definition of
\phi_{\tau}
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u\phi_{\tau}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u(x)\phi(\tau x)x\cdot\nabla u(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\phi(\tau x)x\cdot\nabla(|u|^{2})(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(N\phi(\tau x)+(\tau x)\cdot\nabla\phi(\tau x))|u(x)|^{2}dx.

By Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem again, we observe that

\lim_{\tau\rightarrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u\phi_{\tau}dx=-\frac{N}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}dx.

Let us now compute the nonlocal term, by symmetry and integration by parts,
we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|x|^{-\alpha}\ast|u|^{2})u\phi_{\tau}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x-y|^{-\alpha}|u(y)|^{2}\phi(\tau x)x\cdot\nabla(|u|^{2}/2)(x)dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x-y|^{-\alpha}\left(|u(y)|^{2}\phi(\tau x)x\cdot\nabla(|u|^{2}/2)(x)+|u(x)|^{2}\phi(\tau y)y\cdot\nabla(|u|^{2}/2)(y)\right)dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)|^{2}|u(y)|^{2}}{2|x-y|^{\alpha}}\left(N\phi(\tau x)+(\tau x)\cdot\nabla\phi(\tau x)-\frac{\alpha(x-y)\cdot(x\phi(\tau x)-y\phi(\tau y))}{2|x-y|^{2}}\right)dxdy.

Then we conclude that

\lim_{\tau\rightarrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|x|^{-\alpha}\ast|u|^{2})u\phi_{\tau}dx=-\frac{2N-\alpha}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|x|^{-\alpha}\ast|u|^{2})|u|^{2}dx.

Similarly, we have

\lim_{\tau\rightarrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|x|^{-\beta}\ast|u|^{2})u\phi_{\tau}dx=-\frac{2N-\beta}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|x|^{-\beta}\ast|u|^{2})|u|^{2}dx.

Thus (
12
) holds.

On the other hand, multiplying (
3
) by
u
and integrating on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we get

A(u)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}dx=B_{\alpha}\left(u\right)+\mu_{\beta}B_{\beta}\left(u\right).

(13)

Hence, by combining (
12
) and (
13
), we arrive at the
conclusion.

Asymptotic behavior of solutions and their wave energy essentially depends on the positive coefficient
b=b(t)
in the damping term. According to the categorization by J. Wirth in
[
30

31

, the following types of time-dependent dissipation terms are of special interest: scattering to the free wave equation, non-effective dissipation, effective dissipation and over-damping generating. Briefly speaking, if the solution exhibits an asymptotic behavior similar to that of the wave equation, then as

scattering-generating
case. If the

1.2

non-effective

\begin{cases}v_{t}=-\frac{1}{b(t)}\left(-\Delta\right)^{\sigma}v,&(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad\sigma\geq 1,\\
v(0,x)=v_{0}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{cases}

where a suitable data
v_{0}=v_{0}(x)
depends on
u_{0},u_{1}
, then the damping term is classified as
effective
. Meanwhile, if there are no established decay estimates for the energy of solutions, then the damping term is called
over-damping generating

[
6

have succeeded in deriving the estimates for the solution of linear Cauchy problems (
1.2

\sigma=1

[
10

have derived estimates for
\sigma>1

2.1

Definition 2.1

(Effective dissipation)
.

If the strictly positive function

\mathbf{(B1)}

\mathbf{(B2)}\left|b^{\prime}(t)\right|=\begin{cases}o\left((1+t)^{-\frac{2\delta}{\sigma}}b^{2}(t)\right),&\text{i.e.}\penalty\ \penalty\ t^{\frac{\sigma-2\delta}{\sigma}}b(t)\rightarrow\infty\penalty\ \text{as}\penalty\ t\rightarrow\infty\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{ if}\penalty\ \penalty\ \delta\in(0,\frac{\sigma}{2}),\\
o\left(b^{2}(t)\right),&\text{i.e}\penalty\ tb(t)\rightarrow\infty\penalty\ \text{as}\penalty\ t\rightarrow\infty\penalty\ \text{ if}\penalty\ \delta=0\penalty\ \text{or}\penalty\ \delta=\frac{\sigma}{2};\end{cases}

\mathbf{(B3)}

\frac{\left|b^{(k)}(t)\right|}{b(t)}\lesssim\frac{1}{(1+t)^{k}}

\mathbf{(B4)}

\frac{1}{b(t)}\notin L^{1}([0,\infty))

\mathbf{(B5)}

then the damping term

First we study the corresponding linear Cauchy problem for (

\begin{cases}u_{tt}(t,x)+(-\Delta)^{\sigma}u(t,x)+b(t)(-\Delta)^{\delta}u_{t}(t,x)=0,&(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\\

(3.1)

The Fourier transformation w.r.t.

(3.2)

Putting

\mathaccent 866{u}(t,\xi)=\exp\left(-\frac{|\xi|^{2\delta}}{2}\int\limits_{0}^{t}b(\tau)d\tau\right)\mathaccent 866{v}(t,\xi),

we can rewrite Cauchy problem (

\begin{cases}\mathaccent 866{v}_{tt}+m(t,\xi)\mathaccent 866{v}=0,&(t,\xi)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\\

(3.4)

The following auxiliary weight function has a crucial role in our analysis

\langle\xi\rangle_{b(t)}:=\sqrt{\left||\xi|^{2\sigma}-\frac{|\xi|^{4\delta}b^{2}(t)}{4}\right|}.

(3.5)

\displaystyle=\left\{(t,\xi)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle\xi\rangle_{b(t)}\geq N\frac{|\xi|^{2\delta}b(t)}{2}\right\}\cap\Pi_{\mathrm{hyp}},

\displaystyle Z_{\mathrm{pd}}(N,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\left\{(t,\xi)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}:\varepsilon\frac{|\xi|^{2\delta}b(t)}{2}\leq\langle\xi\rangle_{b(t)}\leq N\frac{|\xi|^{2\delta}b(t)}{2}\right\}\cap\Pi_{\mathrm{hyp}},

from the definition of the elliptic zone and
\frac{b(\tau)^{\prime}}{b(\tau)}\leq\frac{|b(\tau)^{\prime}|}{b(\tau)}\leq\frac{C_{1}}{1+\tau}
from assumption
(B6)
, if
d_{0}>\frac{4C_{1}}{3}
. The second term is dominated the first one, since

\frac{b(s)}{b(t)}\exp\left(-|\xi|^{2\sigma-2\delta}\int_{s}^{t}\frac{1}{b(\tau)}d\tau\right)\frac{\lambda^{2}(t)}{\lambda^{2}(s)}=\exp\left(\int_{s}^{t}(\underbrace{|\xi|^{2\delta}b(\tau)-\frac{|\xi|^{2\sigma-2\delta}}{b(\tau)}-\frac{b^{\prime}(\tau)}{b(\tau)}}_{>0,\text{ if }\tau\geq t_{0}})d\tau\right)

for
t_{0}\leq s\leq t
with
t_{0}
sufficiently large. Thus, we get

\left|\Phi_{t}^{1}(t,s,\xi)\right|\lesssim\frac{|\xi|^{2\sigma-2\delta}}{b(t)}\exp\left(-|\xi|^{2\sigma-2\delta}\int_{s}^{t}\frac{1}{b(\tau)}d\tau\right)
.
Similarly, we can represent
\Psi_{2}
in the following way:

\displaystyle\Psi_{2}(t,s,\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{2}(s)}{\lambda^{2}(t)}-|\xi|^{2\sigma}\int_{s}^{t}\frac{\lambda^{2}(\tau)}{\lambda^{2}(t)}\Phi_{2}(\tau,s,\xi)d\tau,

\displaystyle\left|\Psi_{2}(t,s,\xi)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\lambda^{2}(s)}{\lambda^{2}(t)}+\frac{|\xi|^{2\sigma-2\delta}}{b(s)\lambda^{2}(t)}\int_{s}^{t}\lambda^{2}(\tau)\exp\left(-|\xi|^{2\sigma-2\delta}\int_{s}^{\tau}\frac{1}{b(\theta)}d\theta\right)d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\lambda^{2}(s)}{\lambda^{2}(t)}+\frac{|\xi|^{2\sigma-4\delta}}{b(t)b(s)}\exp\left(-|\xi|^{2\sigma-2\delta}\int_{s}^{t}\frac{1}{b(\tau)}d\tau\right).

Thus, all desired estimates have been verified.
∎

3.0.3
Estimates in the dissipative zone

In the dissipative zone we define the micro-energy
U=U(t,\xi):=\left(\gamma(t)\hat{u},D_{t}\hat{u}\right)^{\mathrm{T}},\;\gamma(t):=\frac{1}{\left((1+t)b(t)\right)^{\frac{\sigma}{2\delta}}}
.
Then, the Fourier transformed Cauchy problem (
3.2
) leads to the system of first order

\displaystyle D_{t}U=\underbrace{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{D_{t}\gamma(t)}{\gamma(t)}&\gamma(t)\\
\frac{|\xi|^{2\sigma}}{\gamma(t)}&i|\xi|^{2\delta}b(t)\end{array}\right)}_{A(t,\xi)}U.

We are interested in the fundamental solution

E_{\text{diss }}=E_{\text{diss }}(t,s,\xi)=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}E_{\text{diss }}^{(11)}&E_{\text{diss }}^{(12)}\\
E_{\text{diss }}^{(21)}&E_{\text{diss }}^{(22)}\end{array}\right)

to system (
3.0.3
), that is, the solution of

D_{t}E_{\text{diss }}(t,s,\xi)=A(t,\xi)E_{\text{diss }}(t,s,\xi),\quad E_{\text{diss }}(s,s,\xi)=I,

for all
0\leq s\leq t
and
(t,\xi),(s,\xi)\in Z_{\text{diss }}\left(d_{0}\right)
. Thus, the solution
U=U(t,\xi)
is represented as

U(t,\xi)=E_{\text{diss }}(t,s,\xi)U(s,\xi).

The entries
E_{\text{diss }}^{(k\ell)}(t,s,\xi),k,\ell=1,2
, of the fundamental solution
E_{\text{diss }}(t,s,\xi)
satisfy the following system of Volterra integral equations for
k=1,2
:

\displaystyle D_{t}E_{\text{diss }}^{(1\ell)}(t,s,\xi)=\frac{D_{t}\gamma(t)}{\gamma(t)}E_{\text{diss }}^{(1\ell)}(t,s,\xi)+\gamma(t)E_{\text{diss }}^{(2\ell)}(t,s,\xi),

\displaystyle D_{t}E_{\text{diss }}^{(2\ell)}(t,s,\xi)=\frac{|\xi|^{2\sigma}}{\gamma(t)}E_{\text{diss }}^{(1\ell)}(t,s,\xi)+i|\xi|^{2\delta}b(t)E_{\text{diss }}^{(2\ell)}(t,s,\xi)

together with their initial conditions

\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}E_{\text{diss }}^{(11)}(s,s,\xi)&E_{\text{diss }}^{(12)}(s,s,\xi)\\
E_{\text{diss }}^{(21)}(s,s,\xi)&E_{\text{diss }}^{(22)}(s,s,\xi)\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}1&0\\
0&1\end{array}\right)
.
By direct calculations, with
\lambda=\lambda(t)
is defined in (
3.7
),
we get

\displaystyle E_{\text{diss }}^{(11)}(t,s,\xi)=\frac{\gamma(t)}{\gamma(s)}+i\gamma(t)\int_{s}^{t}E_{\text{diss }}^{(21)}(\tau,s,\xi)d\tau,\;\;E_{\text{diss }}^{(21)}(t,s,\xi)=\frac{i|\xi|^{2\sigma}}{\lambda^{2}(t)}\int_{s}^{t}\frac{\lambda^{2}(\tau)}{\gamma(\tau)}E_{\text{diss }}^{(11)}(\tau,s,\xi)d\tau,

\displaystyle E_{\text{diss }}^{(12)}(t,s,\xi)=i\gamma(t)\int_{s}^{t}E_{\text{diss }}^{(22)}(\tau,s,\xi)d\tau,\;\;E_{\text{diss }}^{(22)}(t,s,\xi)=\frac{\lambda^{2}(s)}{\lambda^{2}(t)}+\frac{i|\xi|^{2\sigma}}{\lambda^{2}(t)}\int_{s}^{t}\frac{\lambda^{2}(\tau)}{\gamma(\tau)}E_{\text{diss }}^{(12)}(\tau,s,\xi)d\tau.

The next lemma estimates for the entries
E_{\text{diss }}^{(k\ell)}(t,s,\xi)
,
k,\ell=1,2
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Assume effective conditions for
b(t)
. Then in the dissipative zone:

\left(\left|E_{\text{diss }}(t,s,\xi)\right|\right)\lesssim\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{\gamma(t)}{\gamma(s)}\exp\left(-|\xi|^{\sigma}(t-s)\right)&\frac{\gamma(t)}{|\xi|^{2\delta}b(t)}\exp\left(-|\xi|^{\sigma}(t-s)\right)\\
\frac{|\xi|^{2\sigma-2\delta}}{\gamma(s)b(t)}\exp\left(-|\xi|^{\sigma}(t-s)\right)&H\end{array}\right)

with
(s,\xi),(t,\xi)\in Z_{\text{diss }}\left(d_{0}\right),\;0\leq s\leq t
,
\gamma(t):=\frac{1}{\left((1+t)b(t)\right)^{\frac{\sigma}{2\delta}}}
and
H:=\exp\left(-\max\{|\xi|^{\sigma}(t-s),|\xi|^{2\delta}\mathaccent 866{B}(s,t))\}\right)
.

Proof.

First let us consider the first column, that is,

\displaystyle E_{\text{diss }}^{(11)}(t,s,\xi)=\frac{\gamma(t)}{\gamma(s)}+i\gamma(t)\int_{s}^{t}E_{\text{diss }}^{(21)}(\tau,s,\xi)d\tau,\;\;E_{\text{diss }}^{(21)}(t,s,\xi)=\frac{i|\xi|^{2\sigma}}{\lambda^{2}(t)}\int_{s}^{t}\frac{\lambda^{2}(\tau)}{\gamma(\tau)}E_{\text{diss }}^{(11)}(\tau,s,\xi)d\tau.

Plugging the representation for
E_{\text{diss }}^{(21)}(t,s,\xi)
into the integral equation for
E_{\text{diss }}^{(11)}(t,s,\xi)
gives

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{diss}}^{(11)}(t,s,\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma(t)}{\gamma(s)}+i\gamma(t)\int_{s}^{t}\left(\frac{i|\xi|^{2\sigma}}{\lambda^{2}(\tau)}\int_{s}^{\tau}\frac{\lambda^{2}(\theta)}{\gamma(\theta)}E_{\mathrm{diss}}^{(11)}(\theta,s,\xi)d\theta\right)d\tau

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma(t)}{\gamma(s)}-|\xi|^{2\sigma}\gamma(t)\int_{s}^{t}\int_{s}^{\tau}\frac{\lambda^{2}(\theta)}{\lambda^{2}(\tau)}\frac{1}{\gamma(\theta)}E_{\mathrm{diss}}^{(11)}(\theta,s,\xi)d\theta d\tau.

It follows that

\frac{\gamma(s)}{\gamma(t)}E_{\text{diss }}^{(11)}(t,s,\xi)=1-|\xi|^{2\sigma}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{s}^{\tau}\frac{\lambda^{2}(\theta)}{\lambda^{2}(\tau)}\frac{\gamma(s)}{\gamma(\theta)}E_{\text{diss }}^{(11)}(\theta,s,\xi)d\theta d\tau.

By setting
y(t,s,\xi):=\frac{\gamma(s)}{\gamma(t)}E_{\text{diss }}^{(11)}(t,s,\xi)
we obtain

\displaystyle y(t,s,\xi)=1-|\xi|^{2\sigma}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{s}^{\tau}\frac{\lambda^{2}(\theta)}{\lambda^{2}(\tau)}y(\theta,s,\xi)d\theta d\tau.

Thus, we have

|y(t,s,\xi)|\lesssim 1+\int_{s}^{t}\int_{s}^{\tau}|\xi|^{2\sigma}|y(\theta,s,\xi)|d\theta d\tau.

Applying Gronwall’s inequality (cf. Lemma
B.1
) we get the estimate

\displaystyle|y(t,s,\xi)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\exp\left(\int_{s}^{t}\int_{s}^{\tau}|\xi|^{2\sigma}d\theta d\tau\right)\lesssim\exp\left(|\xi|^{2\sigma}(1+t)^{2}\right)\lesssim 1

\displaystyle=ee^{-1}\lesssim e\exp\left(-|\xi|^{\sigma}(t-s)\right)\lesssim\exp\left(-|\xi|^{\sigma}(t-s)\right).

(3.18)

In the above we have used the inequality
|\xi|^{\sigma}\lesssim\frac{1}{1+t}
, which is implied from
|\xi|^{\sigma-2\delta}\lesssim b(t)
(from the definition of the elliptic region), and
|\xi|^{2\delta}\lesssim\frac{1}{(1+t)b(t)}
(from the definition of the dissipative zone), respectively. Hence, we can conclude that

\left|E_{\text{diss }}^{(11)}(t,s,\xi)\right|\lesssim\frac{\gamma(t)}{\gamma(s)}\exp\left(-|\xi|^{\sigma}(t-s)\right).

Now we consider
E_{\text{diss }}^{(21)}(t,s,\xi)
. By using the estimate for
\left|E_{\text{diss }}^{(11)}(t,s,\xi)\right|
we obtain

\displaystyle\left|E_{\text{diss }}^{(21)}(t,s,\xi)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim|\xi|^{2\sigma}\int_{s}^{t}\frac{1}{\gamma(\tau)}\frac{\lambda^{2}(\tau)}{\lambda^{2}(t)}\left|E_{\text{diss }}^{(11)}(\tau,s,\xi)\right|d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\gamma(s)}|\xi|^{2\sigma}\int_{s}^{t}\frac{\lambda^{2}(\tau)}{\lambda^{2}(t)}d\tau\lesssim\frac{1}{\gamma(s)}|\xi|^{2\sigma}(1+t)\lesssim\frac{1}{\gamma(s)}\frac{|\xi|^{2\sigma-2\delta}}{b(t)}.

Here,
|\xi|^{2\delta}\lesssim\frac{1}{(1+t)b(t)}
thanks to the definition of the dissipative zone. Similarly as in (
3.18
) we obtain

\displaystyle\left|E_{\text{diss }}^{(21)}(t,s,\xi)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\gamma(s)}\frac{|\xi|^{2\sigma-2\delta}}{b(t)}\exp\left(-|\xi|^{2\sigma-2\delta}(t-s)\right).

Next, we consider the entries of the second column. We have

\displaystyle E_{\text{diss }}^{(12)}(t,s,\xi)=i\gamma(t)\int_{s}^{t}E_{\text{diss }}^{(22)}(\tau,s,\xi)d\tau,\;\;E_{\text{diss }}^{(22)}(t,s,\xi)=\frac{\lambda^{2}(s)}{\lambda^{2}(t)}+\frac{i|\xi|^{2\sigma}}{\lambda^{2}(t)}\int_{s}^{t}\frac{\lambda^{2}(\tau)}{\gamma(\tau)}E_{\text{diss }}^{(12)}(\tau,s,\xi)d\tau.

Plugging the representation for
E_{\text{diss }}^{(22)}(t,s,\xi)
into the integral equation for
E_{\text{diss }}^{(12)}(t,s,\xi)
gives

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{diss}}^{(12)}(t,s,\xi)

\displaystyle=i\gamma(t)\int_{s}^{t}\left(\frac{\lambda^{2}(s)}{\lambda^{2}(\tau)}+\frac{i|\xi|^{2\sigma}}{\lambda^{2}(\tau)}\int_{s}^{\tau}\frac{\lambda^{2}(\theta)}{\gamma(\theta)}E_{\mathrm{diss}}^{(12)}(\theta,s,\xi)d\theta\right)d\tau

\displaystyle=i\gamma(t)\lambda^{2}(s)\int_{s}^{t}\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}(\tau)}d\tau-|\xi|^{2\sigma}\gamma(t)\int_{s}^{t}\int_{s}^{\tau}\frac{\lambda^{2}(\theta)}{\lambda^{2}(\tau)}\frac{1}{\gamma(\theta)}E_{\mathrm{diss}}^{(12)}(\theta,s,\xi)d\theta d\tau.

By setting
y(t,s,\xi):=\frac{1}{\gamma(t)}E_{\text{diss }}^{(12)}(t,s,\xi)
, it follows that

y(t,s,\xi)=i\lambda^{2}(s)\int_{s}^{t}\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}(\tau)}d\tau-|\xi|^{2\sigma}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{s}^{\tau}\frac{\lambda^{2}(\theta)}{\lambda^{2}(\tau)}y(\theta,s,\xi)d\theta d\tau.

In the same way as it was done for
E_{\text{diss }}^{(11)}(t,s,\xi)
, we obtain

\displaystyle|y(t,s,\xi)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{2}(s)\int_{s}^{t}\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}(\tau)}d\tau+\int_{s}^{t}\int_{s}^{\tau}|\xi|^{2\sigma}|y(\theta,s,\xi)|d\theta d\tau\lesssim(1+t)+\int_{s}^{t}\int_{s}^{\tau}|\xi|^{2\sigma}|y(\theta,s,\xi)|d\theta d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|\xi|^{2\delta}b(t)}+\int_{s}^{t}\int_{s}^{\tau}|\xi|^{2\sigma}|y(\theta,s,\xi)|d\theta d\tau.

Here we use
|\xi|^{2\delta}\lesssim\frac{1}{(1+t)b(t)}
in the dissipative zone. Now by Gronwall’s inequality we get

\displaystyle|y(t,s,\xi)|\lesssim\frac{1}{|\xi|^{2\delta}b(t)}\exp\left(\int_{s}^{t}\int_{s}^{\tau}|\xi|^{2\sigma}|y(\theta,s,\xi)|d\theta d\tau\right)\lesssim\frac{1}{|\xi|^{2\delta}b(t)}\exp\left(|\xi|^{2\sigma}(1+t)^{2}\right)\lesssim\frac{1}{|\xi|^{2\delta}b(t)}.

In the last estimate we have used
|\xi|^{\sigma-2\delta}\lesssim b(t)
from the definition of the elliptic region,
|\xi|^{2\delta}\lesssim\frac{1}{(1+t)b(t)}
from the definition of the dissipative zone, respectively.
Thus, we obtain
\left|E_{\text{diss }}^{(12)}(t,s,\xi)\right|\lesssim\frac{\gamma(t)}{|\xi|^{2\delta}b(t)}
. Similarly as in (
3.18
), we get

\left|E_{\text{diss }}^{(12)}(t,s,\xi)\right|\lesssim\frac{\gamma(t)}{|\xi|^{2\delta}b(t)}\exp\left(-|\xi|^{\sigma}(t-s)\right).

Finally, let us consider
E_{\text{diss }}^{(22)}(t,s,\xi)
by using the representations for
\left|E_{\text{diss }}^{(12)}(t,s,\xi)\right|
. It holds that

\displaystyle\left|E_{\mathrm{diss}}^{(22)}(t,s,\xi)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\lambda^{2}(s)}{\lambda^{2}(t)}+|\xi|^{2\sigma}\int_{s}^{t}\frac{\lambda^{2}(\tau)}{\lambda^{2}(t)}\frac{1}{\gamma(\tau)}\left|E_{\mathrm{diss}}^{(12)}(\tau,s,\xi)\right|d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\lambda^{2}(s)}{\lambda^{2}(t)}+|\xi|^{2\sigma}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{s}^{\tau}E_{\text{diss }}^{(22)}(\theta,s,\xi)d\theta d\tau.

We apply Gronwall’s inequality to obtain

\displaystyle\left|E_{\text{diss }}^{(22)}(t,s,\xi)\right|\lesssim\frac{\lambda^{2}(s)}{\lambda^{2}(t)}\exp\left(\int_{s}^{t}\int_{s}^{\tau}|\xi|^{2\sigma}d\theta d\tau\right)\lesssim\frac{\lambda^{2}(s)}{\lambda^{2}(t)}\exp\left(|\xi|^{2\sigma}(1+t)^{2}\right)\lesssim\frac{\lambda^{2}(s)}{\lambda^{2}(t)}.

Therefore,
\left|E_{\text{diss }}^{(22)}(t,s,\xi)\right|\lesssim\exp\left(-|\xi|^{2\delta}\hat{B}(s,t)\right)
.
In the last estimate we have used
|\xi|^{\sigma-2\delta}\lesssim b(t)
from the definition of the elliptic region and
|\xi|^{2\delta}\lesssim\frac{1}{(1+t)b(t)}
from the definition of the dissipative zone, respectively.

Similarly as in deriving (
3.18
) we can prove that
\left|E_{\text{diss }}^{(22)}(t,s,\xi)\right|\lesssim\exp\left(-|\xi|^{\sigma}(t-s)\right)
. This completes the proof.
∎

4.1
A family of parameter-dependent linear Cauchy problems

Let us consider the following family of parameter-dependent Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}u_{tt}(t,x)+(-\Delta)^{\sigma}u(t,x)+b(t)(-\Delta)^{\delta}u_{t}(t,x)=0,&(t,x)\in[s,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
u(s,x)=f(s,x),\quad u_{t}(s,x)=g(s,x),&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{cases}

(4.1)

where
\sigma\geq 1,\delta\in[0,\frac{\sigma}{2}]
and
n\geq 1
.
The partial Fourier transformation with respect to
x
-variables yields

\begin{cases}\mathaccent 866{u}_{tt}+|\xi|^{2\sigma}\mathaccent 866{u}+b(t)|\xi|^{2\delta}\mathaccent 866{u}_{t}=0,&(t,\xi)\in[s,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
\mathaccent 866{u}(s,\xi)=\mathaccent 866{f}(s,\xi),\quad\mathaccent 866{u}_{t}(s,\xi)=\mathaccent 866{g}(s,\xi),&\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{cases}

(4.2)

Applying the change of variables

\mathaccent 866{y}(t,\xi)=\frac{\lambda(t)}{\lambda(s)}\mathaccent 866{u}(t,\xi)
with
\lambda(t)
is defined in (
3.7
), we obtain the problem

\begin{cases}\mathaccent 866{y}_{tt}+m(t,\xi)\mathaccent 866{y}=0,&(t,\xi)\in[s,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n},s\geq 0,\\

\mathaccent 866{y}_{t}(s,\xi)=\exp\left(\frac{|\xi|^{2\delta}}{2}\int\limits_{0}^{s}b(\tau)d\tau\right)\left(\frac{b(s)}{2}|\xi|^{2\delta}\mathaccent 866{f}(s,\xi)+\mathaccent 866{g}(s,\xi)\right),&\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{cases}

(4.3)

4.1.1
For
\delta\in(0,\frac{\sigma}{2}]

In the same manner as in Section
3
, we divide the phase space
[s,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
into the following zones:

\displaystyle Z_{\mathrm{hyp}}(N)

\displaystyle=\left\{(t,\xi)\in[s,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle\xi\rangle_{b(t)}\geq N\frac{|\xi|^{2\delta}b(t)}{2}\right\}\cap\Pi_{\mathrm{hyp}},

\displaystyle Z_{\mathrm{pd}}(N,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle Z_{\mathrm{red}}(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\left\{(t,\xi)\in[s,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle\xi\rangle_{b(t)}\leq\varepsilon\frac{|\xi|^{2\delta}b(t)}{2}\right\},

\displaystyle=\left\{(t,\xi)\in[s,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle\xi\rangle_{b(t)}\geq\varepsilon\frac{|\xi|^{2\delta}b(t)}{2}\right\}\cap\left\{|\xi|^{2\delta}\geq\frac{d_{0}}{(1+t)b(t)}\right\}\cap\Pi_{\mathrm{ell}},

\displaystyle Z_{\mathrm{diss}}\left(d_{0}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\{(t,\xi)\in[s,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}:|\xi|^{2\delta}\leq\frac{d_{0}}{(1+t)b(t)}\right\}\cap\Pi_{\text{ell }}.

We introduce the limit
b_{\infty}:=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}b(t)\in[0,\infty]
that exists thanks to the monotonic behavior of
b=b(t)
.

Let us introduce the function

h_{1}=h_{1}(t,\xi)=\chi(|\xi|^{2\delta}(1+t)b(t))\frac{1}{\left((1+t)b(t)\right)^{\frac{\sigma}{2\delta}}}+(1-\chi(|\xi|^{2\delta}(1+t)b(t))|\xi|^{\sigma},

(4.4)

and

(4.5)

for our models (
4.2
) and (
4.3
). Here

is a localizing function with
\chi(\zeta)=1
for
0\leq\zeta\leq\frac{1}{2}
and
\chi(\zeta)=0
for
\zeta\geq 1
. We define the energy
U(t,\xi)=\left(h_{1}(t,\xi)\hat{u}(t,\xi),D_{t}\hat{u}(t,\xi)\right)^{T}
.

By (
4.2
),
U=U(t,\xi)
solves the system

\frac{|\xi|^{2\sigma}}{h_{1}(t,\xi)}&i|\xi|^{2\delta}b(t)\end{array}\right)}_{A(t,\xi)}U(t,\xi).

(4.6)

For any
t\geq t_{1}\geq s
, let
E=E\left(t,t_{1},\xi\right)
be the fundamental solution to (
4.6
) which solves

D_{t}E\left(t,t_{1},\xi\right)=A(t,\xi)E\left(t,t_{1},\xi\right),E\left(t_{1},t_{1},\xi\right)=I
. For any

.

On the other hand, we use the dissipative transformed Cauchy problem (

) and define its energy by

Y(t,\xi)=\left(ih_{2}(t,\xi)\mathaccent 866{y}(t,\xi),D_{t}\mathaccent 866{y}(t,\xi)\right)^{\mathrm{T}}
. Then, from (

) we have

\displaystyle I_{R}^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(R^{-2\sigma+\frac{n+2\sigma-2\delta}{p^{\prime}}}+R^{-4\sigma+4\delta+\frac{n+2\sigma-2\delta}{p^{\prime}}}+R^{-4\sigma+2\delta+\frac{n+2\sigma-2\delta}{p^{\prime}}}\right)\lesssim I_{R}^{\frac{1}{p}}R^{-2\sigma+\frac{n+2\sigma-2\delta}{p^{\prime}}}.

(6.26)

Due to assumption (
1.13
), there exists a sufficiently large constant
R_{0}>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}\left(-g^{\prime}(0)u_{0}(x)+g(0)u_{1}(x)+(-\Delta)^{\delta}\left[g^{\prime}(0)u_{0}(x)\right]+(-\Delta)^{\delta}u_{0}(x)\right)\varphi_{R}(x)dx>0,

that is,

\int_{B_{R}}\left(-g^{\prime}(0)u_{0}(x)+g(0)u_{1}(x)+(-\Delta)^{\delta}\left[g^{\prime}(0)u_{0}(x)\right]+(-\Delta)^{\delta}u_{0}(x)\right)\varphi_{R}(x)dx>0,

(6.27)

for any
R>R_{0}
. From (
6.26
) and (
6.27
), one gets

I_{R}^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim R^{-2\sigma+\frac{n+2\sigma-2\delta}{p^{\prime}}}.

(6.28)

It is clear that (
1.14
) is equivalent to
-2\sigma+\frac{n+2\sigma-2\delta}{p^{\prime}}\leq 0
.

Now, by arguments analogous to the case with
b^{\prime}(t)<0
, both subcritical case with
p<1+\frac{2\sigma}{n-2\delta}
, ( i.e.
-2\sigma+\frac{n+2\sigma-2\delta}{p^{\prime}}<0,
) and critical case with
p=1+\frac{2\sigma}{n-2\delta}
, (i.e.
-2\sigma+\frac{n+2\sigma-2\delta}{p^{\prime}}=0
) lead to a contradiction to assumption (
1.13
).

Therefore, there is no global (in time) Sobolev solution to (
1.6
) with
\gamma=0
in these subcritical and critical cases.
Hence, our proof of Theorem
1.3
in the case when
b^{\prime}(t)>0
, the parameter
\sigma
is integer and the parameter
\delta
is fractional from
(0,1)
is completed.

Appendix A
Decay indicator and decay character

The notions of decay character have been introduced by C. Bjorland, M.E. Schonbek, C. J. Niche in
[
1
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
22
]
to measure the algebraic order of the Fourier transform
\hat{u}(\xi)
of the function
u\in L^{2}
near the point
\xi=0
.

Let
\Lambda=(-\Delta)^{1/2}
. We also use
|D|^{s}
to denote
\Lambda^{s}
. First, we recall the notion of decay indicator.

Definition A.1

(Decay indicator)
.

For
u_{0}\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right),s>0
and
r\in(-n/2+s,\infty)
, the decay indicator
P_{r}^{s}\left(u_{0}\right)
corresponding to
\Lambda^{s}u_{0}
is defined by

P_{r}^{s}\left(u_{0}\right)=\lim_{\rho\rightarrow 0}\rho^{-2r-n}\int_{B(\rho)}|\xi|^{2s}\left|\mathaccent 866{u_{0}}(\xi)\right|^{2}d\xi,

where
B(\rho)
is the ball centered at origin with radius
\rho
.

Definition A.2

(Decay character)
.

The decay character of
\Lambda^{s}u_{0}
, denoted by
r_{s}^{*}=r_{s}^{*}\left(u_{0}\right)
is the unique
r\in(-n/2+s,\infty)
such that
0<P_{r}^{s}\left(u_{0}\right)<\infty
, if this number
P_{r}^{s}\left(u_{0}\right)
exists. If such
P_{r}^{s}\left(u_{0}\right)
does not exist, we set
r_{s}^{*}=-n/2+s
when
P_{r}^{s}\left(u_{0}\right)=+\infty
for all
r\in(-n/2+s,\infty)
, or
r_{s}^{*}=+\infty
when
P_{r}^{s}\left(u_{0}\right)=0
for all
r\in(-n/2+s,\infty)
.
For
s=0
, we denote
P_{r}^{0}\left(u_{0}\right)=P_{r}\left(u_{0}\right)
and
r_{0}^{*}=r^{*}
.

Theorem A.1
.

Let
v_{0}\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
with the decay character
r^{*}\left(v_{0}\right)=r^{*}
, where
v_{0}=v(0,x)
,

be a solution to

is positive and continuous with

and
\alpha
is a positive constant. If

\left(1+\int\limits_{0}^{t}a(\tau)d\tau\right)^{-\frac{2r^{*}+n}{2\alpha}}\lesssim\|v(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\left(1+\int\limits_{0}^{t}a(\tau)d\tau\right)^{-\frac{2r^{*}+n}{2\alpha}}.

Proof.

The proof mainly repeats the arguments used in the proof of Theorem 2.10 by Niche and Schonbek in
[
22
]
for the case of coefficient

Appendix B
Some auxiliary inequalities

Lemma B.1

(Gronwall’s inequality)
.

Let
f
and
h
be continuous and nonnegative functions defined on
I=[a,b]
and let
g
be a continuous, positive and nondecreasing function defined on
I
. Then, the inequality

f(t)\leq g(t)+\int_{a}^{t}h(r)f(r)dr,\quad t\in J,

implies that

f(t)\leq g(t)\exp\left(\int_{a}^{t}h(r)dr\right),\quad t\in J.

Lemma B.2
.

The estimate

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(|\xi|^{a}e^{-c|\xi|^{2\kappa}d(t)}\right)(t,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\left(1+d(t)\right)^{-\frac{n}{2\kappa}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)-\frac{a}{2\kappa}}

holds for any
\kappa\in(0,\infty),\quad p\in[1,\infty],\quad t\geq 0,\quad d(t)\geq 0\quad\forall t\geq 0,\quad d(t)\rightarrow\infty
as
t\rightarrow\infty
and for all dimensions
n\geq 1
. The numbers a and
c
are, supposed to be non-negative and positive, respectively.

Proof.

Lemma
B.2
is entirely proved in the same manner as in
[
11
, Theorem 24.2.4]
.

Lemma B.3

(Fractional Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality
[
13
]
)
.

Let

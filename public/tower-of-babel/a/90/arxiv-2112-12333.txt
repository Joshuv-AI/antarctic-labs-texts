Asymptotic normality of least squares estimators to stochastic differential equations driven by fractional Brownian motions
Yasutaka Shimizu, Shohei Nakajima

Abstract




Keywords—
parameter estimation, stochastic differential equation, fractional Brownian motion, least squares estimator, asymptotic normality

1
Introduction

We will consider the following stochastic differential equation (SDE):

X_{t}=X_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}b(X_{s},\theta_{0})ds+\sigma B_{t},~~~t\in(0,T],

(1)

where
\{B_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is a fractional Brownian motion with Hurst index
H\in(1/2,1)
,
\theta_{0}
is a parameter that contains a bounded and open convex subset
\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\{b(\cdot,\theta),\theta\in\Theta\}
is a family of drift coefficients with
b(\cdot,\theta):\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, and
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
is assumed to be the known diffusion coefficient.

This study aims to estimate the unknown parameter
\theta_{0}\in\Theta
. In the case where
H=1/2
—that is,
\{B_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is a Brownian motion—a maximum likelihood estimator (MLE) is the most widely used when observations of the process are continuous (see Prakasa Rao
[
12
]
, Kutoyants
[
6
]
, Liptser, and Shiryaev
[
7
]
). When processes are observed at discrete points, the least squares estimator (LSE) is asymptotically equivalent to the MLE.
For the LSE, the consistency and asymptotic distributions were proved by Kasonaga
[
5
]
and Prakasa Rao
[
11
]
.

Many authors have studied the parametric inference for SDEs driven by fractional Brownian motions based on continuous observation (for example, Brouste and Kleptsyna
[
1
]
, Chiba
[
2
]
, Hu
et al.

[
4
]
, Tudor and Viens
[
13
]
). In particular, Chiba
[
2
]
proposed an
M
-estimator for the equation (
1
) when
1/4<H<1/2
and proved the asymptotic normality and moment convergence.

Neuenkirch and Tindel
[
9
]
studied estimators based on discrete samples from equation (
1
) and showed the consistency of LSE when
1/2<H<1
. However, to the best of our knowledge, asymptotic normality of estimators based on discrete observations has yet to be analyzed. The purpose of this study is to show the asymptotic normality of the LSE. In contrast, Liu
et al.

[
8
]
proved the LAN property for the equation (
1
),
and the optimality of the asymptotic variance of estimator is already known. However, our results do not imply that LSE is asymptotically efficient. Constructing an asymptotic efficient estimator is a task for future research. The main tools in the proof of the asymptotic normality are the central limit theorem of the 2-Hermite power variation and the ergodic theorem to sums of the increments weighted by fractional Brownian motions.

2
Main results

We assume that
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is observed at points
\{t_{k}:~0\leq k\leq n\}
and take equally spaced observations with
t_{k+1}-t_{k}:=h_{n}
.
Define the least squares type procedure,

Q_{n}(\theta):=\frac{1}{nh_{n}^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\left(X_{t_{k}}-X_{t_{k-1}}-h_{n}b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta)\right)^{2}-\sigma^{2}h_{n}^{2H}\right),

and the LSE for the true
\theta_{0}
is defined as

\theta_{n}:=\arg\min_{\theta\in\Theta}|Q_{n}(\theta)|.

(2)

Throughout the paper we will use the following notations:

Notation 2.1
.

For any
a,b\geq 0
, the symbol
a\lesssim b
means that there exists a universal constant
C>0
such that
a\leq Cb
. When
C
depends explicitly on a specific quantity, we indicate it explicitly through the paper.

Let us state the polynomial growth assumptions to drift coefficients
b
, ensuring ergodic properties for process
X
.

Assumption 2.1
.

The function
b
in (
1
) is of
C^{1,3}(\mathbb{R}\times\Theta)
-class
such that, for every
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
and
\theta\in\Theta
,

\left(b(x,\theta)-b(y,\theta)\right)(x-y)\leq-c|x-y|^{2}.

and the following growth conditions hold true

\displaystyle|b(x,\theta)|\leq c(1+|x|^{N}),~|\partial_{x}b(x,\theta)|\leq c(1+|x|^{N}),~|\nabla_{\theta}b(x,\theta)|\leq c(1+|x|^{N})

\displaystyle|\nabla_{\theta}\partial_{x}b(x,\theta)|\leq c(1+|x|^{N}),~|\nabla^{2}_{\theta}b(x,\theta)|\leq c(1+|x|^{N}),

for some constants
c>0,~N\in\mathbb{N}
.

Assumption 2.2
.

There exists a function
U\in C^{2,3}(\mathbb{R}\times\Theta)
such that

\partial_{x}U(x,\theta)=b(x,\theta),

for every
x\in\mathbb{R},~\theta\in\Theta
.

We impose the condition on the size of the sampling step, which is required to control the contribution of fractional Brownian motions.

Assumption 2.3
.

h_{n}=\kappa n^{-\alpha}
with
0<\alpha<\min\{\frac{1}{4(1-H)},1\}
and
\kappa>0
.

Under Assumption
2.1
, the solution to (
1
) converges to a stationary and ergodic stochastic process
(\bar{X_{t}},~t\geq 0)
as
T\to\infty
(see Theorem
3.1
).

Assumption 2.4
.

For every
\theta_{0}\in\Theta
,

E\left|b(\bar{X}_{0},\theta_{0})\right|^{2}=E\left|b(\bar{X}_{0},\theta)\right|^{2},

implies that
\theta=\theta_{0}
.

Assumption 2.5
.

The matrix

I(\theta_{0})=E\left[\left(\nabla_{\theta}{b}(\bar{X},\theta_{0})b(\bar{X},\theta_{0})\right)\right]^{\otimes 2},

is positive definite.

The consistency of LSE (
2
) was given by Neuenkirch and Tindel
[
9
]
.

Theorem 2.1

(Neuenkirch and Tindel
[
9
]
)
.

Under Assumptions
2.1
-
2.4
,
the LSE
\theta_{n}
is strongly consistent with
\theta_{0}
:

\theta_{n}\rightarrow\theta_{0}\hskip 9.24994pta.s.,\hskip 9.24994ptn\to\infty.

To state the main results, we further make some notations: let

\displaystyle\tau_{n}^{H}=\begin{cases}&\sqrt{n}h_{n}^{2-2H},~~~~~~~~~~~H\in(1/2,3/4)\\
&\sqrt{\frac{n}{\log(n)}}h_{n}^{2-2H},~~~~~~H=3/4\\
&(nh_{n})^{2-2H},~~~~~~~~~~H\in(3/4,1).\end{cases}

Theorem 2.2
.

Suppose the same assumptions as in Theorem 1, and that
\frac{1}{2}<\alpha<\min\{\frac{1}{4(1-H)},1\}
, Assumption
2.5
holds true. Then, if
H\in(1/2,3/4]
, there exists a constant
c_{H}>0
such that

\tau_{n}^{H}(\theta_{n}-\theta_{0})\xrightarrow{d}N\left(0,\frac{\sigma^{2}c_{H}^{2}}{4}\left(I(\theta_{0})\right)^{-1}\right),

as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Moreover, if
H\in(3/4,1)
,

\tau_{n}^{H}(\theta_{n}-\theta_{0})\xrightarrow{d}\frac{\sigma\left(I(\theta_{0})\right)^{-1}E\left[\nabla_{\theta}{b}(\bar{X},\theta_{0})b(\bar{X},\theta_{0})\right]}{2}Z,

as
n\rightarrow\infty
where
\bar{X}
is the ergodic limit of the process
X
, and Z is a Hermite random variable that will be defined in the next section.

3
Ergodic theorem

In this section, we shall describe the results of the ergodic theorem in equation (
1
). We will work on the canonical probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
, where
\Omega=C_{0}(\mathbb{R})
is equipped with the topology of the compact convergence,
\mathcal{F}
is the corresponding Borel
\sigma
-algebra, and
P
is the distribution of the fractional Brownian motion. We define the shift operator
\theta_{t}:\Omega\rightarrow\Omega
for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\omega\in\Omega
as

\theta_{t}\omega(\cdot)=\omega(t+\cdot)-\omega(t).

The shifted process
(B_{s}(\theta_{t}\cdot))_{s\in\mathbb{R}}
is a 1–dimensional fractional Brownian motion, and, for any integrable random variable
F:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and any
\omega\in\Omega
, we have

\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{T}F(\theta_{t}(\omega))dt=E[F].

Let us recall the definition of Hölder continuous functions:

C^{\lambda}([a,b];\mathbb{R})=\left\{f:[a,b]\rightarrow\mathbb{R};\|f\|_{\lambda;[a,b]}<\infty,\right\}

where

\|f\|_{\lambda;[a,b]}=\sup_{s,t\in[a,b]}\frac{\left|f(t)-f(s)\right|}{|t-s|^{\lambda}}.

We state the existence and uniqueness of the ergodic limit for
X
and introduce some limit theorems borrowed from Garrido
et al.

[
3
]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Under Assumption
2.1
, for any
\theta\in\Theta
, the equation (
1
) has a unique solution
X\in C^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{R})
for all
\lambda<H
. In addition, there exists a random variable
\bar{X}:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\left|X_{t}(\omega)-\bar{X}(\theta_{t}\omega)\right|=0,

for all
\omega\in\Omega
.

An ergodic theorem for discrete sampling is found in Lemma 3.3 in Neuenkirch and Tindel
[
9
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f\in C^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}\times\Theta)
such that

\displaystyle|f(x,\theta)|\leq C(1+|x|^{N}),~|\partial_{x}f(x,\theta)|\leq C(1+|x|^{N}),~|\nabla_{\theta}f(x,\theta)|\leq C(1+|x|^{N}),

for some
c>0,N\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\left|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}f(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta)-Ef(\bar{X},\theta)\right|\rightarrow 0,~~~a.s.

In addition, assume that there exists a function
U\in C^{2,1}(\mathbb{R}\times\Theta)
such that

\partial_{x}U(x,\theta)=f(x,\theta),~x\in\mathbb{R},~\theta\in\Theta.

Then

\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\left|\frac{1}{nh_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}f\left(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta\right)(B_{t_{k}}-B_{t_{k-1}})+E\left[b(\bar{X},\theta_{0})f(\bar{X},\theta)\right]\right|\rightarrow 0~~~a.s.

as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

We prepare some estimate results for the pth moment of the solution to (
1
). To support these results, we refer to
[
3
]
and
[
9
]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Under Assumption
2.1
, for any
\theta\in\Theta
and
p\geq 1
, there exist constants
c_{p},k_{p}>0
such that

E|X_{t}|^{p}\leq c_{p},~~~~~E|X_{t}-X_{s}|^{p}\leq k_{p}|t-s|^{pH}

for all
s,t\geq 0
.

We need the following convergence results for one–dimensional fractional Brownian motion (cf. Theorem 7.4.1 in
[
10
]
).

Proposition 3.3
.

We set

c_{H}:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(|v+1|^{2H}+|v-1|^{2H}-2|v|^{2H}\right)^{2}<\infty.

Then for
0<H<3/4

\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}c_{H}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}[(B_{k}-B_{k-1})^{2}-1]\xrightarrow{d}N(0,1),

while for
H=3/4
it holds

\frac{1}{\sqrt{n\log(n)}c_{3/4}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}[(B_{k}-B_{k-1})^{2}-1]\xrightarrow{d}N(0,1).

Finally, for
3/4<H<1

\frac{1}{n^{2H-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}[(B_{k}-B_{k-1})^{2}-1]

converges in
L^{2}(\Omega)
to some random variable
Z
, which belongs to the Wiener chaos of
B
with order 2. The random variable
Z
is called a Hermite random variable.

4
Proofs

Let
\mathcal{F}_{t_{k}}:=\sigma(B_{s},~0\leq s\leq t_{k})
and note that

\nabla_{\theta}Q_{n}(\theta)=\frac{1}{nh_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(X_{t_{k}}-X_{t_{k-1}}-h_{n}b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta)\right)\nabla_{\theta}b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta).

Since
\theta_{n}
minimizes to
Q_{n}(\theta)^{2}
, we obtain that

Q_{n}(\theta_{n})\nabla_{\theta}Q_{n}(\theta_{n})=0.

To solve the above equation, we prepare the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Define
\zeta_{n}=\tau_{n}^{H}(\theta_{0}-\theta_{n})
. Then, under assumptions of Theorem
2.2
,

\left\{\begin{aligned} \tau_{n}^{H}Q_{n}(\theta_{n})&=\tau_{n}^{H}\frac{1}{nh_{n}^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left[\sigma^{2}(B_{t_{k}}-B_{t_{k-1}})^{2}-\sigma^{2}h_{n}^{2H}\right]+\frac{2\sigma}{nh_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(B_{t_{k}}-B_{t_{k-1}})\nabla_{\theta}b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{0})\zeta_{n}^{T}\\
&~~~~~+\zeta_{n}o_{p}(1)+o_{p}(1)\\
\nabla_{\theta}Q_{n}(\theta_{n})&=\frac{1}{nh_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sigma(B_{t_{k}}-B_{t_{k-1}})\nabla_{\theta}b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{n})+o_{p}(1).\\
\end{aligned}\right.

Proof.

By using (
1
), we have

\displaystyle Q_{n}(\theta_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{nh_{n}^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\left(X_{t_{k}}-X_{t_{k-1}}-h_{n}b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{n})\right)^{2}-\sigma^{2}h_{n}^{2H}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{nh_{n}^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\{\left(\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\left(b(X_{s},\theta_{0})-b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{n})\right)ds+\sigma(B_{t_{k}}-B_{t_{k-1}})\right)^{2}-\sigma^{2}h_{n}^{2H}\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{nh_{n}^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Biggl\{\left(\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\left(b(X_{s},\theta_{0})-b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{n})\right)ds\right)^{2}+\sigma^{2}(B_{t_{k}}-B_{t_{k-1}})^{2}

\displaystyle+2\sigma(B_{t_{k}}-B_{t_{k-1}})\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\left(b(X_{s},\theta_{0})-b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{n})\right)ds-\sigma^{2}h_{n}^{2H}\Biggl\}.

For the first term, we can calculate that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\left(b(X_{s},\theta_{0})-b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{n})\right)ds\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\left(b(X_{s},\theta_{0})-b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{0})\right)ds+\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\left(b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{0})-b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{n})\right)ds\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\left(b(X_{s},\theta_{0})-b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{0})\right)ds\right)^{2}+\left(\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\left(b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{0})-b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{n})\right)ds\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+2\left(\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\left(b(X_{s},\theta_{0})-b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{0})\right)ds\right)\left(\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\left(b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{0})-b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{n})\right)ds\right)

Through Minkowski’s inequality and Proposition
3.2
we can estimate that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{nh_{n}^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\|\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\left(b(X_{s},\theta_{0})-b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{0})\right)ds\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{nh_{n}^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\left\|b(X_{s},\theta_{0})-b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{0})\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}ds\right)^{2}\lesssim h_{n}^{2H}.

Therefore, under the assumption
nh_{n}^{2}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, we obtain

\tau_{n}^{H}\frac{1}{nh_{n}^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\left(b(X_{s},\theta_{0})-b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{0})\right)ds\right)^{2}\xrightarrow{L^{1}}0,

as
n\rightarrow\infty.
Applying Taylor’s formula, we have

\displaystyle\left(\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\left(b(X_{s},\theta_{0})-b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{0})\right)ds\right)\left(\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\left(b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{0})-b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{n})\right)ds\right)

\displaystyle=h_{n}\left(\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\left(b(X_{s},\theta_{0})-b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{0})\right)ds\right)\nabla_{\theta}b(X_{t_{k-1}},\tilde{\theta}_{n})(\theta_{0}-\theta_{n})^{T},

where
\tilde{\theta}_{n}:=\theta_{0}+\beta_{n}(\theta_{n}-\theta_{0}),~0<\beta_{n}<1
.
With Cauchy–Schwartz and Minkowski’s inequalities, Proposition
3.2
, and (A1), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{nh_{n}^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}E\left|h_{n}\left(\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\left(b(X_{s},\theta_{0})-b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{0})\right)ds\right)\nabla_{\theta}b(X_{t_{k-1}},\tilde{\theta}_{n})\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{nh_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\|\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\left(b(X_{s},\theta_{0})-b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{0})\right)ds\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|1+|X_{t_{k-1}}|^{N}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{nh_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\left\|b(X_{s},\theta_{0})-b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{0})\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}ds\lesssim h_{n}^{H}.

Therefore

\tau_{n}^{H}\frac{1}{nh_{n}^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\left(b(X_{s},\theta_{0})-b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{0})\right)ds\right)\left(\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\left(b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{0})-b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{n})\right)ds\right)\xrightarrow{L^{1}}0,

as
n\rightarrow\infty
. With the Taylor’s formula again, we have

\displaystyle\tau_{n}^{H}\frac{1}{nh_{n}^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\left(b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{0})-b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{n})\right)ds\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau_{n}^{H}}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Biggl(\nabla_{\theta}b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{0})(\theta_{0}-\theta_{n})^{T}

\displaystyle+(\theta_{0}-\theta_{n})\nabla_{\theta}^{2}b(X_{t_{k-1}},\tilde{\theta}_{n})(\theta_{0}-\theta_{n})^{T}\Biggl)^{2}.

Through Theorem
2.1
and Proposition
3.1
, we can obtain

\tau_{n}^{H}\frac{1}{nh_{n}^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\left(b(X_{s},\theta_{0})-b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{n})\right)ds\right)^{2}=o_{P}(1)+\zeta_{n}o_{p}(1).

Let us consider the cross term. At first, we decompose as follows

\displaystyle\tau_{n}^{H}\frac{2\sigma}{nh_{n}^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(B_{t_{k}}-B_{t_{k-1}})\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\left(b(X_{s},\theta_{0})-b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{n})\right)ds

\displaystyle=\tau_{n}^{H}\frac{2\sigma}{nh_{n}^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(B_{t_{k}}-B_{t_{k-1}})\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\left(b(X_{s},\theta_{0})-b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{0})\right)ds

\displaystyle+\tau_{n}^{H}\frac{2\sigma}{nh_{n}^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(B_{t_{k}}-B_{t_{k-1}})\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\left(b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{0})-b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{n})\right)ds.

From Cauchy—Schwartz and Minkowski’s inequalities and Proposition
3.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle E\left|(B_{t_{k}}-B_{t_{k-1}})\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\left(b(X_{s},\theta_{0})-b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{0})\right)ds\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|B_{t_{k}}-B_{t_{k-1}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\left\|b(X_{s},\theta_{0})-b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{0})\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}ds\lesssim h_{n}^{2H+1}.

Thus,

\tau_{n}^{H}\frac{2\sigma}{nh_{n}^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(B_{t_{k}}-B_{t_{k-1}})\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\left(b(X_{s},\theta_{0})-b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{0})\right)ds=o_{p}(1),

,
and we conclude that

\displaystyle\tau_{n}^{H}Q_{n}(\theta_{n})

\displaystyle=\tau_{n}^{H}\frac{1}{nh_{n}^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left[\sigma^{2}(B_{t_{k}}-B_{t_{k-1}})^{2}-\sigma^{2}h_{n}^{2H}\right]+\frac{2\sigma}{nh_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(B_{t_{k}}-B_{t_{k-1}})\nabla_{\theta}b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{0})\zeta_{n}^{T}

\displaystyle+\zeta_{n}o_{p}(1)+o_{p}(1).

We will support the second equality. Using (
1
), we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{\theta}Q_{n}(\theta_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{nh_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(X_{t_{k}}-X_{t_{k-1}}-h_{n}b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{n})\right)\nabla_{\theta}b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{nh_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\left(b(X_{s},\theta_{0})-b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{n})\right)ds+\sigma(B_{t_{k}}-B_{t_{k-1}})\right)\nabla_{\theta}b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{n}).

Let us now apply Taylor’s formula to obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{nh_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}

\displaystyle\left(\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\left(b(X_{s},\theta_{0})-b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{n})\right)ds\right)\nabla_{\theta}b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{nh_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\left(b(X_{s},\theta_{0})-b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{0})\right)ds\right)\nabla_{\theta}b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{n})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{nh_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\left(b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{0})-b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{n})\right)ds\right)\nabla_{\theta}b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\nabla_{\theta}b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{0})(\theta_{0}-\theta_{n})^{T}\nabla_{\theta}b(X_{t_{k-1}},\theta_{0})+o_{p}(1).

,
and we complete the proof.

Five-dimensional non-Abelian supersymmetric Chern-Simons action in Projective Superspace
Ariunzul Davgadorj

Abstract
In this paper, we derive five-dimensional non-Abelian 𝒩 = 1 \mathcal{N}=1 Chern-Simons action from its proposed definition of variation with respect to an infinitesimal deformation of the vector prepotential in a projective superspace setting. It has been a long-standing open problem how to integrate this variation. Now, thanks to the simplicity of a projective superspace technique, we are finally able to obtain this term. Our final result is expressed in terms of the real prepotential multiplets and projective covariant derivatives. The action reduces to the correct formula in the Abelian limit. As a bonus, we also give the supersymmetric Yang-Mills action with a half measure.

Yang-Mills action with a half measure

II.1
5-dimensional superalgebra

Let us start with a comment on spinors in 5-dimensions. Here, one cannot have Majorana spinors like in
d=2,3,4
, because the reality condition that is
\psi=C(\Gamma^{0})^{T}\psi^{*}
has no solution. Therefore, one constructs pairs of Dirac spinors that transform to each other under charge conjugation:
\psi_{1}=C(\Gamma^{0})^{T}\psi^{*}_{2}
and
\psi_{2}=-C(\Gamma^{0})^{T}\psi^{*}_{1}
. Concisely:
\psi_{i}=\epsilon^{ij}C(\Gamma^{0})^{T}\psi^{*}_{j}
This is called the symplectic Majorana spinors / condition.
The antisymmetric 5D
\Gamma
matrices that satisfy a relation
C\Gamma^{a}=-(C\Gamma^{a})^{T}
are:

\displaystyle C\Gamma^{0}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}\\
\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}&0\end{pmatrix}\;\;;\;\;C\Gamma^{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\\
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}&0\end{pmatrix}\;\;;

\displaystyle C\Gamma^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\begin{pmatrix}i&0\\
0&i\end{pmatrix}\\
\begin{pmatrix}-i&0\\
0&-i\end{pmatrix}&0\end{pmatrix}\;\;;\;\;C\Gamma^{3}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}\\
\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}&0\end{pmatrix}\;\;;

\displaystyle C\Gamma^{5}=\begin{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\
i&0\end{pmatrix}&0\\
0&\begin{pmatrix}0&i\\
-i&0\end{pmatrix}\end{pmatrix}\;=\;\begin{pmatrix}-i\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}&0\\
0&i\epsilon^{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}\end{pmatrix}

(1)

Here the antisymmetric
C=\epsilon^{\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}}=\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}&0\\
0&\epsilon^{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}\end{pmatrix}
and the inverse
C^{-1}=\epsilon_{\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}}
serve as charge conjugation matrices.
\epsilon
matrices are used to raise and lower indices.
\epsilon^{\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}}\epsilon_{\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}}=-4
.

Superfields in the superspace
\mathbb{R}^{5|8}
depend on
(x^{\hat{a}},\theta^{\hat{\alpha}}_{i})
coordinates, where
\hat{a}
and
\hat{\alpha}
are the 5-vector and 4-spinor indices of the Lorentz group
SO(4,1)
, while
i
is the index of the
SU(2)
automorphism group. The
SU(2)_{R}
index reflects an internal symmetry and distinguishes components within the doublet. This internal structure allows us to organize the superspace spinorial derivatives as
D^{i}_{\hat{\alpha}}
respecting the
SU(2)_{R}
symmetry. The superalgebra for the ungauged derivatives is:

\{D^{i}_{\hat{\alpha}},D^{j}_{\hat{\beta}}\}=i\epsilon^{ij}\nabla_{\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}}\;,\;\textit{where}\;\;\nabla_{\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}}=(C\Gamma^{\hat{a}})_{\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}}\partial_{\hat{a}}

(2)

While the gauged covariant derivatives (
\mathcal{D}^{i}_{\hat{\alpha}}=D^{i}_{\hat{\alpha}}+\Gamma^{i}_{\hat{\alpha}}\;,\;\Gamma^{i}_{\hat{\alpha}}
is the spinorial gauge connection) satisfy the following algebra:

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{D}^{i}_{\hat{\alpha}},\mathcal{D}^{j}_{\hat{\beta}}\}=i\epsilon^{ij}\left(\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}}+\epsilon_{\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}}\mathbb{W}\right)\;,\;\textit{where}\;\;\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}}=(C\Gamma^{\hat{a}})_{\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}}{\mathcal{D}}_{\hat{a}}

(3)

Above,
\partial_{\hat{a}}
and
\mathcal{D}_{\hat{a}}
denote respectively ungauged and gauged spacetime derivatives. The bold symbols also represent gauged operators.
\mathbb{W}
is the field strength in vector representation.

Now, we define a projective subspace, where our constrained superfields live. This can be achieved by introducing a coordinate on an auxiliary
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
manifold and parameterizing fields and covariant derivatives with this coordinate. The operator that constrains projective superfields is:

\nabla_{\hat{\alpha}}\equiv D^{1}_{\hat{\alpha}}+\zeta D^{2}_{\hat{\alpha}}

(4)

The complex coordinate
\zeta
is a part of an isospinor
\boldsymbol{u}
.

\boldsymbol{u}^{T}=\frac{1}{u^{1}}(u^{1}\;,\;u^{2})\;\;\textit{and}\;\;\zeta\equiv\frac{u^{2}}{u^{1}}

(5)

Then, the nabla operator is written as the product of the projective isospinor and the spinorial covariant derivatives.
\nabla_{\hat{\alpha}}=\boldsymbol{u}^{T}D_{i{\hat{\alpha}}}
. Therefore,
\nabla_{\hat{\alpha}}
transforms covariantly under
SU(2)_{R}
with
1/u^{1}
scaling. From the superalgebra, it is clear that the ungauged spinorial
\nabla
operators anticommute.

\{\nabla_{\hat{\alpha}}(\zeta),\nabla_{\hat{\beta}}(\zeta)\}=0

(6)

The anticommutation rule also applies to the gauge covariantized operator:
\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\hat{\alpha}}\equiv\mathcal{D}_{1\hat{\alpha}}+\zeta\mathcal{D}_{2\hat{\alpha}}
:

\displaystyle\{\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\hat{\alpha}}(\zeta),\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\hat{\beta}}(\zeta)\}=0

(7)

This does not tell us much except that this constraint can be explained as the integrability condition for the existence of the projective superfields
\mathcal{P}
, which are defined to be annihilated by the
\nabla
operators:
\nabla_{\hat{\alpha}}\mathcal{P}=0
, and then the gauged nablas can be related with the ungauged ones through:
\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\hat{\alpha}}=\nabla_{\hat{\alpha}}+\Gamma_{\hat{\alpha}}(\zeta)=e^{U}\nabla_{\hat{\alpha}}e^{-U}=e^{-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muU\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}\nabla_{\hat{\alpha}}e^{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muU\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}
. Here,
e^{V}=e^{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muU\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}e^{U}
.
V
is our prepotential superfield, which in projective superspace we call a tropical multiplet, meaning its
\zeta
-dependance is singular at both north and south pole of the
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
manifold:
V(\zeta)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}v_{n}\zeta^{n}
.
V
and subsequently
e^{V}
are projective superfields,
e^{U}
contains only positive powers of
\zeta
components plus the half of constant terms, hence arctic;
e^{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muU\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}
contains only negative powers of
\zeta
plus the half of constant terms, therefore antarctic multiplet, but
e^{U}
and
e^{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muU\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}
are not annihilated by the nabla operator, so they are not projective superfields.

To produce field strengths, we now take the
\zeta
’s at different points.

\displaystyle\{\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\hat{\alpha}}(\zeta_{1}),\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\hat{\beta}}(\zeta_{2})\}=i(\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2})\left(\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}}+\epsilon_{\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}}\mathbb{W}\right)

(8)

We do the same trick as we did in 4D, take a
\zeta
-derivative with respect to one of the
\zeta
’s and act with
e^{U}
and
e^{-U}
from both sides. However, the difference from the 4-dimensional case is that now we have an additional term
\nabla_{\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}}
, but this will not produce anything problematic, since one can see that
\epsilon^{\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}}(C\Gamma^{\hat{a}})_{\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}}=0
, therefore:

\displaystyle\epsilon^{\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}}\;\;|\;\;\{\nabla_{\hat{\alpha}},[e^{-U}\partial_{\zeta}e^{U},\nabla_{\hat{\beta}}]\}

\displaystyle=ie^{-U}(C\Gamma^{\hat{a}})_{\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}}\partial_{\hat{a}}e^{U}+i\epsilon_{\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}}e^{-U}\mathbb{W}e^{U}

\displaystyle-4i\mathcal{W}

\displaystyle=-\epsilon^{\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}}\nabla_{\hat{\alpha}}\nabla_{\hat{\beta}}A_{\zeta}

(9)

where, as usual, we define the arctic field strength
\mathcal{W}=e^{-U}\mathbb{W}e^{U}
(the field strength in vector representation has no
\zeta
-dependence, while in the arctic representation, contains all positive powers of
\zeta
-expansion plus the constant term) and the covariant
\zeta
-connection
A_{\zeta}=e^{-U}(\partial_{\zeta}e^{U})
(
\mathcal{D}_{\zeta}=\partial_{\zeta}+A_{\zeta}=e^{-U}\partial_{\zeta}e^{U}
). Then the above relation can be written compactly:

\mathcal{W}=\frac{1}{2}i\nabla^{2}A_{\zeta}

(10)

Explicit derivation of the connection
A_{\zeta}
for 4D can be found in
[
31
]
. In 5D, it is exactly the same.

\displaystyle A_{\zeta}(\zeta_{0})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n+1}\oint d\zeta_{1}\ldots\oint d\zeta_{n}\frac{X_{1}\ldots X_{n}}{\zeta_{10}\zeta_{21}\ldots\zeta_{n,n-1}\zeta_{n0}}\;,

\displaystyle\textit{where}\;\;X_{i}=e^{V(\zeta_{i})}-1\;;\;\;\frac{1}{\zeta_{ab}}=\frac{1}{\zeta_{a}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\zeta_{b}}{\zeta_{a}}\right)^{n}

(11)

On the other hand, if we acted with
e^{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muU\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}
and
e^{-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muU\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}
from both sides of the anticommutator above, we would have defined the antarctic covariant
\zeta
connection
\widetilde{A}_{\zeta}
as:
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\zeta}=\partial_{\zeta}+\widetilde{A}_{\zeta}=e^{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muU\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}\partial_{\zeta}e^{-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muU\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}
. Accordingly, in this case we can find the antarctic field strength
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}=e^{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muU\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}\mathbb{W}e^{-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muU\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}
, containing all negative powers of
\zeta
-expansion plus the constant term:

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}=\frac{1}{2}i\nabla^{2}\widetilde{A}_{\zeta}\;\;,\;\;\text{where the antarctic connection is:}

(12)

\displaystyle\widetilde{A}_{\zeta}(\zeta_{0})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n+1}\oint d\zeta_{1}\ldots\oint d\zeta_{n}\frac{X_{1}\ldots X_{n}}{\zeta_{01}\zeta_{21}\ldots\zeta_{n,n-1}\zeta_{0n}}

(13)

Naturally, there is a relation between
A_{\zeta}
and
\widetilde{A}_{\zeta}
involving only
e^{V}
.

\displaystyle e^{-V}(\partial_{\zeta}e^{V})=e^{-U}(\partial_{\zeta}e^{U})+e^{-U}e^{-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muU\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}(\partial_{\zeta}e^{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muU\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu})e^{U}=A_{\zeta}-e^{-V}\widetilde{A}_{\zeta}e^{V}\;\ ,

\displaystyle e^{V}\mathcal{W}e^{-V}=\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}

(14)

II.2
SYM action in terms of the field strength

Zupnik has established long time ago in
[
17
]
, that the 8-supercharge super Yang-Mills theories with full measure have an universal form in terms of the prepotential. That is (in projective superspace description
[
33
,
31
]
):

\displaystyle S_{YM}=\frac{1}{g_{YM}^{2}}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n}\tr\int d^{5}xd^{8}\theta\oint d\zeta_{1}\ldots d\zeta_{n}\frac{X_{1}X_{2}\ldots X_{n}}{\zeta_{21}\ldots\zeta_{n,n-1}\zeta_{1n}}

(15)

Then, one might want to construct the Yang-Mills action with a measure consisting of four supercovariant derivatives in terms of the field strength just like in four-dimensions. In 4D, one can construct a chiral/antichiral action with a chiral /antichiral half-measure. However, in five-dimensions a chiral description does not exist, as it is not Lorentz-covariant; therefore, a projective half-measure is an option. The full measure as projective covariant derivatives and as regular 5D,
\mathcal{N}=1
covariant derivatives respectively: :

\int d^{8}\theta=\Delta^{4}\nabla^{4}=(D^{\hat{\alpha}}_{1}D_{1\hat{\alpha}})^{2}(D^{\hat{\alpha}}_{2}D_{2{\hat{\alpha}}})^{2}
.

\Delta_{\hat{\alpha}}\equiv D_{2{\hat{\alpha}}}-\frac{1}{\zeta}D_{1{\hat{\alpha}}}
is linearly independent from
\nabla_{\hat{\alpha}}
and anticommute among themselves.
\nabla
and
\Delta
operators do not anticommute and we square them with the factor of 1/2:
\nabla^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{\hat{\alpha}}\nabla_{\hat{\alpha}}
. Then,
\Delta^{4}
will serve as the projective half-measure.

We now need to construct a projective covariant superfield
\it{G}
in terms of the field strength
[
18
]
.

G=\nabla^{4}\{A_{\zeta},\mathcal{W}\}\;\;,\;\;\text{where the \lx@text@lbrace\lx@text@rbrace brace indicates anticommutator}

(16)

The idea of applying Sohnius’s prescription to construct a supersymmetric invariant action for an algebra with central charges
[
34
]
to a four-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=2
harmonic superspace in the context of vector-tensor multiplet was developed in
[
35
]
. Later, this was applied to describe five-dimensional super Yang-Mills action in
[
16
]
.

In our case, we take a similar route; first, parametrizing the Grassmann coordinates with the
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
parameter, such that our
\Delta
operator that defines the projective measure is proportional to the partial derivative with respect to this projective Grassmann variable.

Now, let us redefine Grassmann coordinates into projective ones.

\displaystyle\Theta_{\hat{\alpha}}=\theta_{\hat{\alpha}2}-\zeta\theta_{\hat{\alpha}1}\;\;\textit{such that}\;\;\Delta^{\hat{\alpha}}=D^{\hat{\alpha}}_{2}-\frac{1}{\zeta}D^{\hat{\alpha}}_{1}\;\sim\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\Theta_{\hat{\alpha}}}

(17)

\displaystyle\Theta^{\hat{\alpha}}=\theta^{\hat{\alpha}}_{2}-\zeta\theta^{\hat{\alpha}}_{1}\;\;\textit{such that}\;\;\Delta_{\hat{\alpha}}=D_{\hat{\alpha}2}-\frac{1}{\zeta}D_{\hat{\alpha}1}\;\sim\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\Theta^{\hat{\alpha}}}

(18)

Then, we define a projective, constant field
\tau
that couples the projective covariant multiplet
G
, the coupling is gauge-invariant.
The constant field
\tau
is defined by:

\displaystyle\Delta^{2}\tau\equiv\frac{1}{g_{YM}^{2}}\;\implies\;\tau=\frac{1}{g_{YM}^{2}}\Theta^{2};\;\;\;\;\textit{also}\;\;\;\;\nabla\tau=0

(19)

G_{YM}
is a constrained non-Abelian YM multiplet:
\nabla G_{YM}=0
. The SYM action in the Sohnius prescription in projective language will become:

\displaystyle S_{YM}=\int d^{5}x\oint\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta}\Delta^{4}\;\tau\;\rm{tr}\;G_{YM}=\frac{1}{g_{YM}^{2}}\rm{tr}\int d^{5}x\oint\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta}\Delta^{4}\Theta^{2}G,\;\;\textit{where}\;\;G=\nabla^{4}\{A,\mathcal{W}\}

(20)

In the next part, we will show that this particular construction of action reduces to the expected expression in four-dimensional Yang-Mills action formula, proving that this is the correct form.

II.3
Reduction to 4-dimensions

One could want to see how this above-received construction works out if we reduce the dimension, does it produce well-known 4-dimensional chiral actions? For this purpose, let us write down the 5-dimensional symplectic Majorana spinors as a pair of spinors that are constructed from 4-dimensional spinors. The pairs are related to each other with the antisymmetric tensor
\epsilon^{ij}
.
Spinor relations between
\mathbb{R}^{5|8}
and
\mathbb{R}^{4|8}
are:

\displaystyle\theta^{\hat{\alpha}}_{1}=(\theta^{\alpha}_{1}\;,\;\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\theta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{2\dot{\alpha}})\;\;,\;\;\theta^{\hat{\alpha}}_{2}=(\theta^{\alpha}_{2}\;,\;-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\theta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{1\dot{\alpha}})

\displaystyle\theta_{1\hat{\alpha}}=\binom{\theta_{1\alpha}}{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\theta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\dot{\alpha}}_{2}}\;\;,\;\;\theta_{2\hat{\alpha}}=\binom{\theta_{2\alpha}}{-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\theta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\dot{\alpha}}_{1}}

\displaystyle D^{\hat{\alpha}}_{1}=(D^{\alpha}\;,\;\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muQ\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{\dot{\alpha}})\;\;,\;\;D^{\hat{\alpha}}_{2}=(Q^{\alpha}\;,\;-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muD\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{1\dot{\alpha}})

Then, as we defined in (

\displaystyle\Theta^{\hat{\alpha}}=(\theta^{\alpha}_{2}-\zeta\theta^{\alpha}_{1}\;,\;-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\theta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{\dot{\alpha}1}-\zeta\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\theta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{\dot{\alpha}2})\;\;,\;\;\Theta_{\hat{\alpha}}=\binom{\theta_{2\alpha}-\zeta\theta_{1\alpha}}{-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\theta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\dot{\alpha}}_{1}-\zeta\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\theta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\dot{\alpha}}_{2}}

20
). First, the field strength needs to be expressed by

and
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathcal{W}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{4}

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{D}^{i}_{\hat{\alpha}},\mathcal{D}^{j}_{\hat{\beta}}\}=i\epsilon^{ij}\left[\begin{pmatrix}i\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}\mathcal{D}_{5}&\nabla_{\alpha\dot{\beta}}\\

After descending from 4-spinor to 2-spinor representations, the resulting anticommutators are equivalent to the 4D superalgebra:

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{D}^{i}_{\alpha},\mathcal{D}^{j}_{\beta}\}=i\epsilon^{ij}(\nabla_{5}+\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}\mathbb{W}_{5})=i\epsilon^{ij}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{W}_{4}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\;;

\displaystyle\{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathcal{D}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{i}_{\dot{\alpha}},\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathcal{D}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{j}_{\dot{\beta}}\}=i\epsilon^{ij}(-\nabla_{5}+\epsilon_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}\mathbb{W}_{5})=i\epsilon^{ij}\epsilon_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}\mathbb{W}_{4}

\displaystyle(\nabla_{5})_{\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}}=(\partial_{5}+A_{5})_{\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}}=(C\Gamma^{5})_{\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}}\mathcal{D}_{5}=\begin{pmatrix}i\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}&0\\
0&-i\epsilon_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}\end{pmatrix}\mathcal{D}_{5}

Here we used the chiral representation for

\{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathcal{D}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{i}_{\dot{\alpha}},\mathcal{D}^{j}_{\beta}\}&\{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathcal{D}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{i}_{\dot{\alpha}},\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathcal{D}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{j}_{\dot{\beta}}\}\end{pmatrix}

Comparing the 5D and 4D algebras, we find:

\displaystyle i

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{5}+\mathbb{W}_{5}=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{W}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{4}

\displaystyle-i

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{5}+\mathbb{W}_{5}=\mathbb{W}_{4}

Then we have the 5D field strength in terms of 4D ones.

\mathbb{W}_{5}=\frac{1}{2}(\mathbb{W}_{4}+\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{W}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{4})

(32)

It is worth to mention that 5D anticommuting covariant derivatives produce torsion term
\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}}
plus the field strength term
\mathbb{W}_{5}
while in 4D, the anticommutation of only chiral or only antichiral covariant derivatives contain just the field strengths

and
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{W}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{4}
respectively, hence comparing the diagonal part of the 5D anticommutation matrix with the 4D anticommutation, we receive relations between the 5-th spacetime dimension covariant derivate, 5D field strenth and 4D field strengths.

Now that we know 5D field strength is expressed with 4D chiral field strength and its complex conjugation, we reduce the Yang-Mills action in (

II.4
Discussion

In this section, we see that 5-dimensional Yang-Mills action can be described in terms of field strength in projective superspace. We coupled a constant projective field depending on Grassmann coordinates with the projective covariant field which is the descendent derivative of the field strength. Because we defined the derivatives with respect to the projective Grassman coordinates as proportional to the
\Delta
derivatives, it was straightforward to prove that this action reduces to the 4-dimensional chiral action and its complex conjugate part.

III
Chern-Simons action

We take a hint from the description of the variation of the 5-dimensional Chern-Simons action proposed by Zupnik in
[
17
]
and later corrected in the erratum
[

]
. The integrability of Zupnik’s variational form is proven by Kuzenko in
[

. In projective superspace, the action should contain

as the variation of the prepotential. This is because of the way vector multiplets couple to polar/hypermultiplets. By polar multiplets we mean the arctic: analytic around the north pole of the manifold
\mathbb{CP}^{1}

\Upsilon=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Upsilon_{n}\zeta^{n}
and the antarctic: analytic around the south pole

. This form is also consistent with the overall gauge invariance of the action, as we will see below.

\displaystyle\delta S_{CS}=k_{5}\tr\int d^{5}xd^{8}\theta\oint d\zeta e^{-V}\delta e^{V}\{A\;,\;\mathcal{W}\}

(37)

The infinitesimal gauge transformation rule for the non-Abelian vector multiplet has the standard form. Remind you that the gauge parameters

,

are projective polar multiplets.

\displaystyle\delta e^{V}=i\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Lambda\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mue^{V}-e^{V}i\Lambda\;\;,\;\;\delta A=-i\partial_{\zeta}\Lambda+[i\Lambda,A]\;\;,\;\;\delta W=[i\Lambda,\mathcal{W}]

(38)

Additionally, useful to remember (
14

e^{V}Ae^{-V}=\widetilde{A}+\partial_{\zeta}e^{V}e^{-V}
and
e^{V}\mathcal{W}e^{-V}=\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}

contains
\zeta^{-1}

\displaystyle\delta S_{\Lambda}=k_{5}\tr\int d^{5}xd^{8}\theta\oint d\zeta\left(i\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Lambda\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\{\widetilde{A}\;,\;\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}\}+i\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Lambda\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\{\partial_{\zeta}e^{V}e^{-V}\;,\;\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}\}-i\Lambda\{A\;,\;\mathcal{W}\}\right)=0

proven to be gauge-invariant.

d^{8}\theta=(D_{1}D_{1})^{2}(D_{2}D_{2})^{2}
. Using the projectivity of the vector multiplet and the Lindström-Roček gauge, we find:
D_{1}v_{-1}=D_{2}v_{-2}=0,\;D_{2}v_{1}=D_{1}v_{2}=0
. This allows us to write:

(D_{1}D_{1})[v_{-1}v_{1}D_{2}D_{2}v_{-1}]=v_{-1}(D_{2}D_{2}v_{-1})^{2}\;;\;\;(D_{2}D_{2})[v_{-1}v_{1}D_{1}D_{1}v_{1}]=v_{1}(D_{1}D_{1}v_{1})^{2}\;,

which are the first terms of
F
and
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muF\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
parts, respectively in eq.(
87
). Furthermore, for the Abelian case, the second terms of both the
F
and
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muF\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
parts will be zero:
v_{-1}^{2}(D_{2}D_{2})^{2}v_{-1}=v_{-1}^{2}(D_{2}D_{2})D_{1}D_{1}v_{1}=0
and
v_{1}^{2}(D_{1}D_{1})^{2}v_{1}=0
.

Thus, we have proven that the non-Abelian action in (
87
) will reduce to the form equivalent to equation (
48
) in the Abelian limit.

III.3
Discussion

In this example (however it is not a mere example, but it has been an unsolved problem for a long time) we saw how useful it is to use the asymmetric splitting techniques of the projective superspace to decompose the prepotential function
e^{V}
and subsequently express the field strength in terms of the
e^{\check{U}}
(or
e^{\hat{U}}
) projections and the
\zeta
-independent term
D^{\hat{\alpha}}_{1}D_{\hat{\alpha}1}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muF\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
(respectively
D^{\hat{\alpha}}_{2}D_{\hat{\alpha}2}F
). The advantage of doing this was that it is now easier to separate terms, and the contour-integral eliminated unwanted terms and left us with much simpler expressions. Furthermore, analysis on the ordinary covariant derivatives in 5-dimensions and applying partial integrations on them, we can finally pull out the variation and find the long-awaited non-Abelian Chern-Simons action in terms of functionals
F
(
125
) and
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muF\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
(
128
) that depend on unconstrained prepotential superfields
X_{i}=e^{V_{i}}-1
. Our final result is written in terms of the
\zeta
-independent functionals, these are themselves highly nontrivial functionals of the gauge prepotential
V(\zeta_{i})
built through contour-integral projections. Since direct component expansions of F and
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muF\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
are intractable, we only demonstrated the consistency of the non-Abelian action after reducing to the Abelian limit with the earlier obtained Abelian action in
48
. It is only thanks to the contour integrating and splittig techniques on the physical prepotential
e^{V}
and the field strenth
\mathcal{W}
, we have been able to integrate this variational form of action. Unfortunately, after obtaining the integrated lagrangian, it becomes very hard to restore the
\zeta
-dependent multiplets such as field strengths and vector multiplets. However, they are related to the field strengths and the posivitive/negative projections of the prepotential exponential through the eq.(
49
).

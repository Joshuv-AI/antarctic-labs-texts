Relative Higher Homology and representation theory
Rasool Hafezi, Javad asadollahi and Yi Zhang

Abstract
. Higher homological algebra, basically done in the framework of an n n -cluster tilting subcategory ℳ \mathcal{M} of an abelian category 𝒜 \mathcal{A} , has been the topic of several recent researches. In this paper, we study a relative version, in the sense of Auslander-Solberg, of the higher homological algebra. To this end, we consider an additive sub-bifunctor F F of Ext ℳ n ​ ( − , − ) {\rm{Ext}}^{n}_{\mathcal{M}}(-,-) as the basis of our relative theory. This, in turn, specifies a collection of n n -exact sequences in ℳ \mathcal{M} , which allows us to delve into the relative higher homological algebra. Our results include a proof of the relative n n -Auslander-Reiten duality formula, as well as an exploration of relative Grothendieck groups, among other results. As an application, we provide necessary and sufficient conditions for ℳ \mathcal{M} to be of finite type.

1.
Introduction and preliminaries

1.1
.

The importance of relative homological algebra in the representation theory of artin algebras is highlighted in the three influential papers by M. Auslander and Ø. Solberg
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
and
[
15
]
. One way to approach this theory is by examining short exact sequences that fall within a sub-bifunctor
F
of
{\rm{Ext}}^{1}
. Let
\Lambda
be an artin algebra. In
[
13
]
the authors established nice connections between certain additive sub-bifunctors of the extension functor
{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{\Lambda}(-,-)
and homologically finite subcategories of
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda.
For instance, they showed that there is a bijection between the collection of additive sub-bifunctors
F
of
{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{\Lambda}(-,-)
with enough projectives and the collection of contravariantly finite subcategories of
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
containing
\mathcal{P}(\Lambda)
, the subcategory of projective modules in
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
. Dually, there is a bijection between the collection of additive subfunctors
F
of
{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{\Lambda}(-,-)
with enough injectives and the collection of all covariantly finite subcategories of
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
containing
\mathcal{I}(\Lambda)
, where
\mathcal{I}(\Lambda)
denotes the subcategory of injective modules in
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
.

Our initial motivation for this work is to elaborate on such bijections, and other similar results, in the new setting of higher homological algebra. Let us be more precise.

1.2
.

Higher homological algebra, known also as
n
-homological algebra, is a vast generalization of homological algebra, having sequences of length
n+2
playing the role of the short exact sequences in (
1
-)abelian categories. It appeared in a series of papers by Iyama
[
30
,
31
,
32
]
and then axiomatized and studied extensively by Jasso
[
35
]
.

To recall the notion, let
\mathcal{M}
be an additive category and
n\geq 1
be a fixed integer. Let
f^{0}:M^{0}\longrightarrow M^{1}
be a morphism in
\mathcal{M}
. An
n
-cokernel of
f^{0}
is a sequence

M^{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f^{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}M^{2}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow M^{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f^{n}}}{{\longrightarrow}}M^{n+1}

of objects and morphisms in
\mathcal{M}
such that for every
M\in\mathcal{M}
, the induced sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}(M^{n+1},M)\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}(M^{n},M)\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}(M^{2},M)\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}(M^{1},M)\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}(M^{0},M)

of abelian groups is exact. We usually denote an
n
-cokernel of
f^{0}
by
(f^{1},f^{2},\ldots,f^{n})
. Dually we define the notion of an
n
-kernel of a morphism.

An
n
-exact sequence in
\mathcal{M}
is a sequence

0\longrightarrow M^{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f^{0}}}{{\longrightarrow}}M^{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f^{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}M^{2}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow M^{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f^{n}}}{{\longrightarrow}}M^{n+1}\longrightarrow 0

such that
(f^{0},f^{1},\ldots,f^{n-1})
is an
n
-kernel of
f^{n}
and
(f^{1},f^{2},\ldots,f^{n})
is an
n
-cokernel of
f^{0}

[
35
, Definitions 2.2, 2.4]
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an additive category. We say that
\mathcal{M}
is an
n
-abelian category
[
35
, Definition 3.1]
if it is idempotent complete, each morphism in it admits an
n
-cokernel and an
n
-kernel and every monomorphism
f^{0}:M^{0}{\longrightarrow}M^{1}
, resp. every epimorphism
f^{n}:M^{n}{\longrightarrow}M^{n+1}
, can be completed to an
n
-exact sequence

0\longrightarrow M^{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f^{0}}}{{\longrightarrow}}M^{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f^{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}M^{2}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow M^{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f^{n}}}{{\longrightarrow}}M^{n+1}\longrightarrow 0.

1.3
.

The best-known examples of
n
-abelian categories are
n
-cluster tilting subcategories of abelian categories. Cluster tilting subcategories are introduced by Iyama
[
30
, Definition 2.2]
in studying a higher version of Auslander’s correspondence, see also
[
31
]
and
[
32
]
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an abelian category. Recall that a subcategory
\mathscr{B}
of
\mathscr{A}
is called a covariantly finite subcategory if for every
A\in\mathscr{A}
there exists an object
B\in\mathscr{B}
and a morphism
f:A\longrightarrow B
such that for all
B^{\prime}\in\mathscr{B}
the sequence of abelian groups
\mathscr{A}(B,B^{\prime})\longrightarrow\mathscr{A}(A,B^{\prime})\longrightarrow 0
is exact. Such a morphism
f
is called a left
\mathscr{B}
-approximation of
A
. Contravariantly finite subcategories and right
\mathscr{B}
-approximations are defined dually. A functorially finite subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
is a subcategory that is both a covariantly finite and a contravariantly finite subcategory
[
10
, page 113]
.

By definition, an additive subcategory
\mathcal{M}
of
\mathscr{A}
is called an
n
-cluster tilting subcategory if it is a functorially finite and generating-cogenerating subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
and satisfies the equalities
\mathcal{M}^{\perp_{n}}=\mathcal{M}={}^{\perp_{n}}\mathcal{M}
, where

\ \ \ \ \ \ \mathcal{M}^{\perp_{n}}:=\{X\in\mathscr{A}\mid{\rm{Ext}}^{i}_{\mathscr{A}}(\mathcal{M},X)=0,\ \text{ for all}\ 0<i<n\},\ {\rm and}

{}^{\perp_{n}}\mathcal{M}:=\{X\in\mathscr{A}\mid{\rm{Ext}}^{i}_{\mathscr{A}}(X,\mathcal{M})=0,\ \text{ for all}\ 0<i<n\}.

It is known that
\mathcal{M}
has a structure of an
n
-abelian category
[
35
, Theorem 3.16]
.

1.4
.

After introducing the concept of
n
-abelian categories, there have been several attempts to study the various notions of classical homological algebra in these new settings, see for instance,
[
33
]
,
[
37
]
,
[

]
,
[

]
,
[

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an
n
-cluster tilting subcategory of
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
. Let

and

be in
\mathcal{M}

An

-exact sequence

is called an

-extension of
N
by
L
. Two
n
-extensions

and

of

are said to be Yoneda equivalent if there exists a chain of morphisms of
n
-exact sequences

\xi=\xi_{0},\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{l-1},\xi_{l}=\xi^{\prime}

such that for every

, we have either a chain map
\xi_{i}\longrightarrow\xi_{i+1}
or a chain map

starting with

and ending with

. By
[

-extensions of

by

[
21
, Remark 6.44]
, that will be denoted by

. In fact,

is an additive bifunctor on

, which means that, for every object

in
\mathcal{M}
, the functors

are additive
[
13
, Section 1]

\mathcal{A}{\rm b}
denotes the category of abelian groups.

From now on, for simplicity, we refer to additive sub-bifunctors of
{\rm{Ext}}^{n}_{\mathcal{M}}
as subfunctors.
It is known
[
3
, 2.4]
that an additive subfunctor

of
{\rm{Ext}}^{n}_{\mathcal{M}}(-,-):\mathcal{M}^{\rm op}\times\mathcal{M}\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}{\rm b}
determines a collection of

-exact sequences in
\mathcal{M}
which is closed under isomorphisms, direct sums and

-pullbacks and

-pushouts along any other morphism in
\mathcal{M}
. Conversely, a collection of
n
-exact sequences satisfying these properties gives rise to an additive subfunctor of

-exact sequences in

-
F
-exact sequences.

1.5
.

In this paper, we examine the relative homological algebra within an

-cluster tilting subcategory
\mathcal{M}

Section

[

Grothendieck groups in higher homological algebra settings have been studied in

.

We end this introductory section by recalling notions and definitions we need.
Throughout
\Lambda

For an integer

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an

-cluster tilting subcategory of

1.8
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an essentially small additive category. By definition, a (right)

-module is a contravariant additive functor

. The collection of

-modules and natural transformations between them form an abelian category denoted by

with

in

. All finitely presented

We often write

Since an

2.
Subfunctors of
{\rm{Ext}}^{n}_{\mathcal{M}}(-,-)

In this section, we study some basic notions in the context of higher homological algebra. Let
F
be a subfunctor of
{\rm{Ext}}^{n}_{\mathcal{M}}(-,-)
. Elements of

-exact sequences in

Definition 2.1
.

A module

is called

-

-projective if for every

-

-exact sequence

\xi\colon 0\longrightarrow M^{\prime}\longrightarrow M^{1}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow M^{n}\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow 0

is exact. We say that

-

-exact sequence

with

-

The subcategories of

Since, throughout the paper,

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\xi:0\longrightarrow M^{\prime}\longrightarrow M^{1}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow M^{n}\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow 0
be an
F
-exact sequence. For all
X\in\mathcal{M}
, there exist exact sequences

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}(X,M^{\prime})\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}(X,M^{1})\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}(X,M^{n})\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}(X,M)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta}}{{\longrightarrow}}F(X,M^{\prime})

and

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}(M,X)\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}(M^{n},X)\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}(M^{1},X)\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}(M^{\prime},X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta}}{{\longrightarrow}}F(M,X)

of abelian groups.

Proof.

We only prove the existence of the first exact sequence; the proof for the second is omitted as it is dual. By
[
32
, Lemma 3.5]
, we have the following exact sequence

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}(X,M^{\prime})\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}(X,M^{1})\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}(X,M^{n})\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}(X,M)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\rm{Ext}}^{n}_{\mathcal{M}}(X,M^{\prime})

Let

be a morphism in
\mathcal{M}(X,M)
. The connecting map
\delta
maps

to
\xi g
, by taking

-pullback of
\xi
along

\xi
is
F
-exact,
\xi g
is an element of

. This completes the proof.
∎

An

-exact sequence

\xi\colon 0\longrightarrow M^{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f^{0}}}{{\longrightarrow}}M^{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f^{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}M^{2}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow M^{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f^{n}}}{{\longrightarrow}}M^{n+1}\longrightarrow 0

is called split, or contractible, if the identity morphism on

is a split monomorphism if and only if

Corollary 2.4

The following statements hold.

(ii)

We only prove

Corollary 2.7

Let

.

(2)

\mathcal{P}(F)=\tau^{-1}_{n}(\mathcal{I}(F))\cup\mathcal{P}(\Lambda)
.

Remark 2.8
.

We say that a subcategory
\mathscr{X}
of an abelian category
\mathscr{A}
is of finite type if the number of iso-classes of indecomposable objects in
\mathscr{X}
is finite. This is equivalent to saying that it has an additive generator, i.e., there exists
X\in\mathscr{X}
such that
\mathscr{X}={\rm{add}\mbox{-}}X
.
The above corollary, in particular, implies that
\mathcal{P}(F)
is of finite type if and only if
\mathcal{I}(F)
is of finite type.

Remark 2.9
.

It is known that the number of non-isomorphic indecomposable projective modules in
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
is equivalent to the number of non-isomorphic indecomposable injective modules. Suppose that
\mathcal{P}(F)
and
\mathcal{I}(F)
are of finite type. In that case, the above corollary extends this fact to the higher version by establishing equality between the number of non-isomorphic indecomposable modules in
\mathcal{P}(F)
and the number of non-isomorphic indecomposable modules in
\mathcal{I}(F)
.

has enough injectives. Hence the map

∎

As a corollary, we can record the following.

Corollary 2.18

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an
n
-cluster tilting subcategory of

be a subfunctor of

(1)

F
has enough projectives.

(2)

F
has enough injectives.

(3)

has enough projectives and enough injectives.

Proof.

is of finite type, every subcategory is functorially finite. In particular,
\mathcal{P}(F)
and
\mathcal{I}(F)
are functorially finite in

∎

Lemma 4.1

We only prove the validity of the Statement

,
{\rm{mod}}_{F}\mbox{-}\mathcal{M}={\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}

\Sigma_{F}(\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}(-,M))=\widetilde{\Phi^{\prime}}_{F}\circ\widetilde{\Phi}^{-1}_{F}(\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}(-,M))=\widetilde{\Phi^{\prime}}_{F}(\xi).

On the other hand, by an argument similar to

But

, because

. Thus
\widetilde{\Phi^{\prime}}_{F}(\xi)=F(M,-)
. This completes the proof.

-variety, then
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{X}
and
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{X}^{{\rm{op}}}
are abelian categories with enough projectives and injectives.

Since
\mathcal{M}
is an
n
-cluster tilting subcategory of
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
, it is functorially finite. So by

, Theorem 2.3]
, it is a dualizing variety. That is, there exists a duality
D:{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{M}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}\mbox{-}{\rm mod}
, which is defined by using the standard duality
D:{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda\longrightarrow{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda^{\rm op}.
It easily follows from the definition that we have the following restrictions

\textstyle{{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{M}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{D}
\textstyle{\mathcal{M}\mbox{-}{\rm mod}}
\textstyle{{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\overline{\mathcal{M}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

where, by abuse of notation, we use the same notation ‘

and

are dualizing varieties.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
F
be a subfunctor of
{\rm{Ext}}^{n}_{\mathcal{M}}(-,-)
with enough projectives and enough injectives. There exists a, unique up to isomorphism, equivalence
\tau_{F}:\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}\longrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}
such that for every
X,Y\in\mathcal{M}
induces the natural isomorphisms

D\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}(\tau^{-1}_{F}Y,X)\simeq F(X,Y)\simeq D\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}(Y,\tau_{F}X).

Proof.

By Propositions
3.9
and
3.10
, we infer that

{\rm{mod}}_{F}\mbox{-}\mathcal{M}={\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}\ \ \ {\rm and}\ \ \ \mathcal{M}\mbox{-}{\rm mod}_{F}=\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}\mbox{-}{\rm mod}.

Consider the composition

D\circ\Sigma_{F}:{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Sigma_{F}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}\mbox{-}{\rm mod}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle D}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}

of functors. Since
\mathcal{M}
is closed under direct summands,
\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}
and
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}
are idempotent complete. Hence Yoneda functor induces the equivalences

P_{(-)}:\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}\simeq{\rm prj}\mbox{-}{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}\ \ \ {\rm and}\ \ \ P^{(-)}:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}\simeq{\rm prj}\mbox{-}{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}.

Since an equivalence between abelian categories can be restricted to their projective objects, we get the equivalence

(4.1)

\tau_{F}:\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle P_{(-)}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\rm prj}\mbox{-}{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle D\circ\Sigma_{F}|}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\rm prj}\mbox{-}{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle P^{{(-)}^{-1}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}

of functors.

Since a duality sends a projective object to an injective one, the dualities

D:\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}\mbox{-}{\rm mod}\longrightarrow{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}\ \ \ {\rm and}\ \ \ D:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}\mbox{-}{\rm mod}\longrightarrow{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}

give rise to the equivalences

\Theta:\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle P_{(-)}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\rm prj}\mbox{-}\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}\mbox{-}{\rm mod}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle D}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\rm inj}\mbox{-}{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{F},\ X\mapsto D\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}(X,-),

and

\Theta^{\prime}:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle P^{(-)}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\rm prj}\mbox{-}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}\mbox{-}{\rm mod}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle D}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\rm inj}\mbox{-}{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{F},\ X\mapsto D\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}(X,-).

The equivalence
\Sigma^{-1}_{F}\circ D
is restricted to an equivalence between the subcategories of injective functors. This yields the equivalence

(4.2)

\tau^{\prime}_{F}:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Theta^{\prime}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\rm inj}\mbox{-}{\rm mod}\mbox{-}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Sigma^{-1}_{F}\circ D}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\rm inj}\mbox{-}{\rm mod}\mbox{-}\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Theta^{-1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}.

We show that the functors
\tau_{F}
and
\tau^{\prime}_{F}
are mutually inverse equivalences.
To this end, we prove that there are natural equivalences
\tau^{\prime}_{F}\circ\tau_{F}\simeq{\rm id}_{\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}}
and
\tau_{F}\circ\tau^{\prime}_{F}\simeq{\rm id}_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}}.
We only demonstrate the proof for the first natural equivalence, as the proof for the second one is similar. Let
X\in\mathcal{M}
. Applying the natural equivalences (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) imply that

D\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}(\tau^{\prime}_{F}\tau_{F}X,X)\simeq F(X,\tau_{F}X)\simeq D\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}(\tau_{F}X,\tau_{F}X)

By applying the equivalence
\tau_{F}
we get

\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}(\tau^{\prime}_{F}\tau_{F}X,X)\simeq\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}(X,X)

Define the natural isomorphism
\tau^{\prime\prime}_{F}\tau^{\prime}_{F}X\longrightarrow X
as the image of the identity
{\rm id}_{X}
under this isomorphism.

It remains to show that we have the natural isomorphisms

D\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}(\tau^{-1}_{F}Y,X)\simeq F(X,Y)\simeq D\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}(Y,\tau_{F}X).

Let
X\in\mathcal{M}
. The equality
\tau_{F}=P^{{(-)}^{-1}}\circ D\circ\Sigma_{F}\circ P_{(-)}
implies that

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}(-,\tau_{F}X)=D\circ\Sigma_{F}(\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}(-,X)).

This in view of Lemma
4.1
, implies that

(4.3)

D\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}(-,\tau_{F}X)=\Sigma_{F}(\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}(-,X))\simeq F(X,-).

On the other hand, let
Y\in\mathcal{M}
. The equality

\tau^{\prime}_{F}=\Theta^{-1}\circ{\Sigma^{-1}\circ D}\circ\Theta^{\prime}

implies that

\Theta\tau^{\prime}_{F}(Y,-)=\Sigma^{-1}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}(Y,-)),

that, in view of Lemma
4.1
and the fact that
\tau^{\prime}_{F}=\tau^{-1}_{F}
, implies that

(4.4)

D\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}(\tau^{-1}_{F}Y,-)=\Sigma^{-1}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}(Y,-))\simeq F(-,Y).

Therefore the proof can be completed by comparing the equations (
4.3
) and (
4.4
).
∎

Remark 4.6
.

Let

be subfunctors of
{\rm{Ext}}^{n}_{\mathcal{M}}(-,-)

\mathcal{P}(G)\subseteq\mathcal{P}(F)

\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}=\mathcal{M}/\mathcal{P}(F)\simeq\frac{\mathcal{M}/\mathcal{P}(G)}{\mathcal{P}(F)/\mathcal{P}(G)}
, implies that there is a sequence of functors

\mathcal{P}(F)/\mathcal{P}(G)\hookrightarrow\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{G}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}.

The quotient functor
\pi

has enough projectives,
\mathcal{P}(F)
is a contravariantly finite subcategory of
\mathcal{M}
. This implies immediately that
\mathcal{P}(F)/\mathcal{P}(G)
is a contravariantly finite subcategory in the quotient category
\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{G}
. Moreover, it can be seen that there exists a left adjoint
\pi_{!}:{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{G}\longrightarrow{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}
to the embedding

, where

\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{G}(-,X)\longrightarrow\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{G}(-,Y)\longrightarrow H\longrightarrow 0

\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}(-,X)\longrightarrow\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}(-,Y)\longrightarrow\pi_{!}H\longrightarrow 0.

There is a similar treatment for the following sequence of functors

\mathcal{I}(F)/\mathcal{I}(G)\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{G}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi^{\prime}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}.

Therefore, there exists the left adjoint functor

\pi^{\prime}_{!}:{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{G}\longrightarrow{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}.

5.
Relative Grothendieck groups

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a Krull-Schmidt category. The Grothendieck group of
\mathcal{C}
with respect to the trivial split exact structure is denoted by
{\rm K}_{0}(\mathcal{C},0)
. It is defined to be the free abelian group
\oplus_{[X]\in\rm{{ind}\mbox{-}}\mathcal{C}}\mathbb{Z}[X]
generated by the set
\rm{{ind}\mbox{-}}\mathcal{C}
of all isomorphism classes of indecomposable objects in
\mathcal{C}
. We denote by
[X]
the element in
{\rm K}_{0}(\mathcal{C},0)
corresponding to the object
X
of
\mathcal{C}
.

Assume furthermore that
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})
is an exact category. We denote by
{\rm Ex}(\mathcal{C})
the subgroup of
{\rm K}_{0}(\mathcal{C},0)
generated by

\{[X]-[Y]+[Z]\,|\ X{\rightarrowtail}Y{\twoheadrightarrow}Z\ \text{\ is a conflation in}\ \mathcal{C}\}.

The quotient group
{\rm K}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E}):={\rm K}_{0}(\mathcal{C},0)/{\rm Ex}(\mathcal{C})
is called the Grothendieck group of
\mathcal{C}
with respect to the exact structure
\mathcal{E}
. If
\mathcal{C}
is an abelian category, the Grothendieck group of
\mathcal{C}
will be denoted by
{\rm K}_{0}(\mathcal{C})
.

5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an
n
-cluster tilting subcategory of
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
, where
n>0
is a positive integer. Recently, it is shown
[
18
, Proposition 3.1]
that there exists an isomorphism

\mathbb{H}:{\rm K}_{0}(\mathcal{M},0)\longrightarrow{\rm K}_{0}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{M})

of the Grothendieck groups that maps
[M]
to
[(-,M)]
.

Our aim in this section is to provide a relative version of this fact. Throughout, let
\mathcal{M}
be an
n
-cluster tilting subcategory of
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
and
F
be an additive subfunctor of
{\rm{Ext}}^{n}_{\mathcal{M}}(-,-)
.

Definition 5.2
.

The Grothendieck group of
\mathcal{M}
with respect to
F
, denoted by
{\rm K}_{0}(\mathcal{M},F)
, is defined to be the quotient group
{\rm K}_{0}(\mathcal{M},0)/{\rm Ex}^{n}_{F}(\mathcal{M})
, where
{\rm Ex}^{n}_{F}(\mathcal{M})
is the subgroup of
{\rm K}_{0}(\mathcal{M},0)
generated by the set

\{\sum^{n+1}_{i=0}(-1)^{i}[M_{i}]\ |\ 0\longrightarrow M^{0}\longrightarrow M^{1}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow M^{n+1}\longrightarrow 0\ \text{\ is an $F$-exact sequence}\}.

As before, since
n
is fixed, we omit it and write
{\rm Ex}_{F}(\mathcal{M})
for
{\rm Ex}^{n}_{F}(\mathcal{M})
.

Note that if
n=1
and
F={\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{\Lambda}(-,-)
, then
\mathcal{M}={\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
and
{\rm K}_{0}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda,{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{\Lambda})
is the classical Grothendieck group of
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
. See
[
40
]
and
[
18
]
for the case when
n>1
and
F={\rm{Ext}}^{n}_{\Lambda}(-,-)
.

5.3
.

Let

\delta\colon 0\longrightarrow M^{0}\longrightarrow M^{1}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow M^{n}\longrightarrow M^{n+1}\longrightarrow 0

be an
n
-exact sequence in
\mathcal{M}
. By
[
36
, Definition 3.1]
, the contravariant defect of
\delta
, denoted by
\delta^{*}
, is defined by the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow(-,M^{0})\longrightarrow(-,M^{1})\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow(-,M^{n})\longrightarrow(-,M^{n+1})\longrightarrow\delta^{*}\longrightarrow 0

of functors. It follows from the definition that every functor
G
in
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\underline{\mathcal{M}}
is a contravariant defect of an
n
-exact sequence; see
[
18
, Lemma 3.2]
, where they called such functors effaceable. Similarly, one can see that every functor
G
in
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}
is a contravariant defect of an
F
-exact sequence. We use this fact without proof.

The following proposition shows that the relative Grothendieck group of an
n
-cluster tilting subcategory can be obtained as a factor of the Grothendieck group of an abelian category.

Proposition 5.4

There is an isomorphism

{\rm K}_{0}(\mathcal{M},F)\simeq\frac{{\rm K}_{0}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{M})}{{\rm K}_{F}(\mathcal{M})}

denote the subgroup of

of

\textstyle{{\rm K}_{0}(\mathcal{M},0)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathbb{H}\ \ \ }
\textstyle{{\rm K}_{0}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{M})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Let

Using this, we can record the following corollary.

Corollary 5.6

There is an isomorphism

{\rm K}_{0}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda,F^{1}_{\mathcal{X}})\simeq\frac{{\rm K}_{0}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda)}{{\rm K}_{F}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda)}

∎

5.7
.

Consider the restriction functor
\mathcal{R}:{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda\longrightarrow{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{M}
that maps any functor

to the composition of
G
with the inclusion
\mathcal{M}\hookrightarrow{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
, that is,
\mathcal{R}(G)=G|_{\mathcal{M}}
. Since the functor
\mathcal{R}
is exact, it induces a group homomorphism

\overline{\mathcal{R}}:{\rm K}_{0}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda)\longrightarrow{\rm K}_{0}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{M}).

It is also clear that the restriction of
\overline{\mathcal{R}}
to
{{\rm K}_{F}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda)}
induces the homomorphism

Remark 5.10

6.
Representation type of
\mathcal{M}

An artin algebra
\Lambda
is of finite representation type if, up to isomorphism, there are only finitely many indecomposable objects in
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
. Butler
[
17
]
and Auslander
[
7
]
proved that
\Lambda
is of finite representation type if and only if the relations of the Grothendieck group of
\Lambda
are generated by almost split sequences; in their notation languages
{\rm Ex}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda)={\rm AR}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda)
. See
[
20
, Theorem 3.7]
for a version of this result in the context of exact categories and
[
18
]
for a higher version in the context of
n
-cluster tilting subcategories. In this section, we study this result in the context of relative higher homological algebra. Let us begin with the following two facts.

6.1
.

Let
\delta
be the
n
-almost split sequence

0\longrightarrow M^{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f^{0}}}{{\longrightarrow}}M^{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f^{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}M^{2}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow M^{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f^{n}}}{{\longrightarrow}}M^{n+1}\longrightarrow 0

in
\mathcal{M}
. By
1.7
, there exists the sequence

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow{\rm{Hom}}_{\Lambda}(-,M^{0})\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow{\rm{Hom}}_{\Lambda}(-,M^{n+1})\longrightarrow{\rm{Hom}}_{\Lambda}(-,M^{n+1})/\mathcal{J}_{\Lambda}(-,M^{n+1})\longrightarrow 0,

which is exact on
\mathcal{M}
. Hence, by
5.3
,
\delta^{*}={\rm{Hom}}_{\Lambda}(-,M^{n+1})/\mathcal{J}_{\Lambda}(-,M^{n+1})
.

When
M^{n+1}
is an indecomposable module, for any
X\in\rm{{ind}\mbox{-}}\mathcal{M}
,

\delta^{*}(X)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ 0&{\rm if}\ X\not\simeq M^{n+1};\\
\\
\frac{{\rm End}_{\Lambda}(M^{n+1})}{\mathcal{J}_{\Lambda}(M^{n+1},M^{n+1})}&{\rm if}\ X\simeq M^{n+1}.\end{array}\right.

6.2
.

A functor
F
in
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{M}
is said to be of finite length if it has a finite composition series. The support of
F
, denoted by supp
(F)
, is a set of representatives of isomorphism classes of all indecomposable objects
M\in\mathcal{M}
such that
F(M)\not=0
. We know from
[
39
, Lemma 3.4]
that a functor
F
has finite length if and only if the support of
F
is finite.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
F
be an additive subfunctor of
{\rm{Ext}}^{n}_{\mathcal{M}}(-,-)
. Let
{\rm AR}_{F}(\mathcal{M})
denote the subgroup of
{\rm Ex}_{F}(\mathcal{M})
generated by the set

\{\sum^{n+1}_{i=0}(-1)^{i}[M_{i}]\,|\ 0\longrightarrow M^{0}\longrightarrow M^{1}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow M^{n+1}\longrightarrow 0\ \text{\ is an $F$-almost split sequence}\}.

The following statements are equivalent.

(1)

{\rm Ex}_{F}(\mathcal{M})={\rm AR}_{F}(\mathcal{M})
.

(2)

Every functor
G\in{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}
has finite length.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
. Let
G
belong to
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{F}
. Then there is an
F
-exact sequence

\delta\colon 0\longrightarrow M^{0}\longrightarrow M^{1}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow M^{n}\longrightarrow M^{n+1}\longrightarrow 0,

such that
\delta^{*}=G.
Since
{\rm Ex}_{F}(\mathcal{M})={\rm AR}_{F}(\mathcal{M})
, there exist integers
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m}
and
F
-almost split sequences

\delta_{j}\colon 0\longrightarrow X^{0}_{j}\longrightarrow X^{1}_{j}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow X^{n}_{j}\longrightarrow X^{n+1}_{j}\longrightarrow 0,

for
j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
, such that

\sum^{n+1}_{i=0}(-1)^{i}[M^{i}]=\sum^{m}_{j=1}\lambda_{j}(\sum^{n+1}_{i=0}(-1)^{i}[X^{i}_{j}])

in
{\rm K}_{0}(\mathcal{M},0)
.

By applying the group isomorphism
\mathbb{H}
, given in
5.1
, we get the equality

(6.1)

\mathbb{H}(\sum^{n+1}_{i=0}(-1)^{i}[M^{i}])=\mathbb{H}(\sum^{m}_{j=1}\lambda_{j}(\sum^{n+1}_{i=0}(-1)^{i}[X^{i}_{j}]))

in
{\rm K}_{0}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{M})
.
Hence

[G]=\sum^{m}_{j=1}\lambda_{j}[\delta^{*}_{j}].

Lemma 3.6 of
[
39
]
implies that

{\rm supp}(G)\subseteq\bigcup^{m}_{j=1}{\rm supp}(\delta^{*}_{j})

Since
\delta_{j}
’s are corresponded to simple functors in
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{M}
,
|{\rm supp}(\delta^{*}_{j})|=1
. Hence
G
has finite support and therefore by
6.2
is of finite length.

(2)\Rightarrow(1)
. This follows by applying a similar argument as in the proof of Theorem 3.11 of
[
18
]
. For the convenience of the reader, we recall the sketch of the proof. Let

\delta\colon 0\longrightarrow M^{0}\longrightarrow M^{1}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow M^{n}\longrightarrow M^{n+1}\longrightarrow 0

be an
F
-exact sequence in
{\rm Ex}_{F}(\mathcal{M})
. By assumption,
\delta^{*}
has finite length. So

[\delta^{*}]=[S_{1}]+[S_{2}]+\cdots+[S_{t}],

where
S_{1},\ldots,S_{t}
are simple composition factors of
\delta^{*}
. By Proposition 2.3 of
[
6
]
,
S_{i}
, for each
i
, can be written as
{\rm{Hom}}_{\Lambda}(-,X^{i})/\mathcal{J}_{\Lambda}(-,X^{i})
, where
X^{i}\in\mathcal{M}
is an indecomposable module. Moreover, in view of
6.1
, for every indecomposable module
Y\in\mathcal{M}
which is not isomorphic to
X^{i}
, we have
S_{i}(Y)=0.
So for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
,
\delta^{*}(X^{i})\not=0.
This, in particular, implies that
X^{i}
, for all
i
, is not
F
-projective, as otherwise
\delta^{*}(M^{i})=0.
So, by Corollary
4.5
, for each
X^{i}
there exists an
F
-almost split sequence

\delta_{i}\colon 0\longrightarrow N^{0}_{i}\longrightarrow N^{1}_{i}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow N^{n}_{i}\longrightarrow X^{i}\longrightarrow 0.

This follows that
[\delta^{*}_{i}]\in{\rm AR}_{F}(\mathcal{M})
and hence completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 6.11
.

Let

is of finite type. Then the following statements are equivalent.

(1)

{\rm Ex}_{F}(\mathcal{M})={\rm AR}_{F}(\mathcal{M})
.

(2)

Every functor

has finite length.

(3)

\mathcal{M}
is of finite type.

Proof.

The fact that
(1)

(2)
are equivalent follows from Proposition

trivially follows from
(3)

is of finite type, then
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{M}_{F}
is the category of modules over an artin algebra. Now assume that
(2)
is true. Since
\mathcal{P}(F)
is of finite type, we can consider
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{P}(F)
as the module category over the endomorphism algebra of an additive generator of
\mathcal{P}(F).

By composing the group isomorphism
\mathbb{T}:{\rm K}_{0}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda,F^{1}_{\mathcal{X}})\longrightarrow{\rm K}_{0}(\mathcal{M},F)
of
5.7
and Theorem
5.9
with the group isomorphism
\Delta
of Lemma
6.9
we obtain the isomorphism

\mathbb{T}\circ\Delta:{\rm K}_{0}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{P}(F))\longrightarrow{\rm K}_{0}(\mathcal{M},F)

of groups.

In view of
[
11
, Theorem I.1.7]
,
\{[\mathcal{P}(F)(-,X)]-[{\rm rad}(-,X)]\,|\,X\in{\rm ind}\mbox{-}\mathcal{P}(F)\}
is a free basis for
\mathrm{K}_{0}({\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\mathcal{P}(F))
.
For any
X\in{\rm ind}\mbox{-}\mathcal{P}(F)
, we set
\sigma_{X}:=\mathbb{T}\circ\Delta([\mathcal{P}(F)(-,X)]-[{\rm rad}(-,X)])
. Since
\mathbb{T}\circ\Delta
is an isomorphism, the set

\{\sigma_{X}\mid X\in{\rm ind}\mbox{-}\mathcal{P}(F)\}

is a free basis for
{\rm K}_{0}(\mathcal{M},F)
. Moreover, the set

\{\beta_{A}\mid A\in{\rm ind}\mbox{-}\mathcal{M}\setminus{\rm ind}\mbox{-}\mathcal{P}(F)\}

forms a free basis for
{\rm Ex}_{F}(\mathcal{M})
. Hence we get the free basis

\{\sigma_{X}\mid X\in{\rm ind}\mbox{-}\mathcal{P}(F)\}\cup\{\beta_{A}\mid A\in{\rm ind}\mbox{-}\mathcal{M}\setminus{\rm ind}\mbox{-}\mathcal{P}(F)\}.

for
{\rm K}_{0}(\mathcal{M},0)
. Since

, we can write

[D\Lambda]=\displaystyle\sum_{A\in{\rm ind}\mbox{-}\mathcal{M}\setminus{\rm ind}\mbox{-}\mathcal{P}(F)}\lambda_{A}\beta_{A}+\displaystyle\sum_{X\in{\rm ind}\mbox{-}\mathcal{P}(F)}\lambda_{X}\sigma_{X},

where

for only finitely many

. Since every

has injective envelope in
\mathcal{M}
, we have

\langle[Y],[D\Lambda]\rangle=\displaystyle\sum_{A\in{\rm ind}\mbox{-}\mathcal{M}\setminus{\rm ind}\mbox{-}\mathcal{P}(F)}\lambda_{A}\langle[Y],\beta_{A}\rangle+\displaystyle\sum_{X\in{\rm ind}\mbox{-}\mathcal{P}(F)}\lambda_{X}\langle[Y],\sigma_{X}\rangle\neq 0.

By
6.5

\displaystyle\sum_{A\in{\rm ind}\mbox{-}\mathcal{M}\setminus{\rm ind}\mbox{-}\mathcal{P}(F)}\lambda_{A}\langle[Y],\beta_{A}\rangle=\lambda_{Y}l_{Y},

for every
X\in{\rm ind}\mbox{-}\mathcal{M}
.

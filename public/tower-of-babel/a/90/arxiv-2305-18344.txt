The Dirac equation on metrics of Eguchi-Hanson type
Zhuohua Cai †, Xiao Zhang ♭

Abstract
. We investigate parallel spinors on the Eguchi-Hanson metrics and find the space of complex parallel spinors are complex 2-dimensional. For the metrics of Eguchi-Hanson type with the zero scalar curvature, we separate variables for the harmonic spinors and obtain the solutions explicitly. PASC numbers: 03.60.Pm, 04.20.Gz, 04.60.-m Key words: Parallel spinors, Dirac equation, Eguchi-Hanson metric



1.
Introduction

Eguchi-Hanson metrics were constructed in
[
1
,
2
]
, which are complete 4-dimensional Ricci flat, anti-self-dual ALE Riemannian metrics
on
R_{\geq 0}\times P^{3}
. They are one type of gravitational instantons, and play important roles in the Euclidean approach of gravitational quantization
[
3
]
. Since then, gravitational instantons including the Eguchi-Hanson metrics attract much attention both in geometry and in general relativity.

In order to investigate the physical behaviour of spin-
\frac{1}{2}
particles in general relativity, Chandrasekhar solved the Dirac equation
in Kerr spacetimes by separating variables
[
4
]
. We refer to
[
5
,
6
]
and references therein for further developments of the
the Dirac equation in various spacetimes. In
[
7
]
, Sucu and Ünal solved the Dirac equation on Eguchi-Hanson and Bianchi
VII_{0}
gravitational instanton metrics. In particular, the solutions are obtained as the product of two hypergeometric functions for the Eguchi-Hanson gravitational instanton metric.

As it actually relates to manifold’s scalar curvature, it is interesting to study the Dirac equation on much more general metrics of Eguchi-Hanson type with the zero scalar curvature. This type of metrics were constructed by LeBrun using the method of algebraic geometry
[
8
]
and by the second author solving an ordinary differential equation
[
9
]
on
R_{\geq 0}\times S^{3}/Z_{d}

(d>2)
, and they provides the counter-examples of Hawking and Pope’s generalized positive action conjecture.

In this note we first introduce the Eguchi-Hanson metrics, the metrics of Eguchi-Hanson type with the zero scalar curvature as well as the spin connections on them. Then we investigate the parallel spinors and the harmonic spinors. As the existence of a nontrivial parallel spinor on a Riemannian spin manifold implies that the manifold is Ricci-flat, parallel spinors only exist on the Eguchi-Hanson metrics, and we find the space of complex parallel spinors are complex 2-dimensional. For the metrics of Eguchi-Hanson type with the zero scalar curvature, we separate variables for the harmonic spinors and obtain the solutions explicitly.

2.
Metrics of Eguchi-Hanson Type

Metrics of Eguchi-Hanson type are given by

g=f^{-2}\mathrm{d}r^{2}+r^{2}\left(\sigma_{1}^{2}+\sigma_{2}^{2}+f^{2}\sigma_{3}^{2}\right),

(2.1)

where
f
is a real function of
r
,
\sigma_{1}
,
\sigma_{2}
and
\sigma_{3}
are the Cartan-Maurer forms for
SU(2)\cong S^{3}

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\sin\psi\mathrm{d}\theta-\sin\theta\cos\psi\mathrm{d}\phi\right),

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(-\cos\psi\mathrm{d}\theta-\sin\theta\sin\psi\mathrm{d}\phi\right),

\displaystyle\sigma_{3}=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\mathrm{d}\psi+\cos\theta\mathrm{d}\phi\right)

with three Euler angles
\theta
,
\phi
,
\psi
. The frame and coframe are

e^{1}=f^{-1}\mathrm{d}r,\quad e^{2}=r\sigma_{1},\quad e^{3}=r\sigma_{2},\quad e^{4}=rf\sigma_{3},

(2.2)

and

\displaystyle e_{1}=f\partial_{r},\quad e_{2}=\dfrac{2}{r}\left(\sin\psi\partial_{\theta}-\dfrac{\cos\psi}{\sin\theta}\partial_{\phi}+\dfrac{\cos\theta\cos\psi}{\sin\theta}\partial_{\psi}\right),

(2.3)

\displaystyle e_{3}=\dfrac{2}{r}\left(-\cos\psi\partial_{\theta}-\dfrac{\sin\psi}{\sin\theta}\partial_{\phi}+\dfrac{\cos\theta\sin\psi}{\sin\theta}\partial_{\psi}\right),\quad e_{4}=\dfrac{2}{rf}\partial_{\psi}

respectively.

Let
\{\omega^{i}_{j}\}
be the connection 1-forms given by
\mathrm{d}e^{i}=-\omega^{i}_{j}\wedge e^{j}
. Then
[
9
]

\displaystyle\omega^{2}_{1}=\dfrac{f}{r}e^{2},\quad\omega^{3}_{1}=\dfrac{f}{r}e^{3},\quad\omega^{4}_{1}=\left(\dfrac{f}{r}+f^{\prime}\right)e^{4},

(2.4)

\displaystyle\omega^{3}_{4}=\dfrac{f}{r}e^{2},\quad\omega^{4}_{2}=\dfrac{f}{r}e^{3},\quad\omega^{2}_{3}=\left(\dfrac{2}{rf}-\dfrac{f}{r}\right)e^{4}.

Let
B>0
be certain positive constant. Taking

\displaystyle f=\sqrt{1-\dfrac{B}{r^{4}}},

and

\displaystyle r\geq\sqrt[4]{B},\quad 0\leq\theta\leq\pi,\quad 0\leq\phi\leq 2\pi,\quad 0\leq\psi\leq 2\pi,

metric (
2.1
) is the Eguchi-Hanson metric
[
1
,
2
]

g=\left(1-\dfrac{B}{r^{4}}\right)^{-1}\mathrm{d}r^{2}+r^{2}\left(\sigma_{1}^{2}+\sigma_{2}^{2}+\left(1-\dfrac{B}{r^{4}}\right)\sigma_{3}^{2}\right),

(2.5)

which is Ricci-flat and geodesically complete. Topologically, it is

\displaystyle R_{\geq 0}\times SU(2)/Z_{2}\cong R_{\geq 0}\times SO(3)\cong R_{\geq 0}\times P_{3}.

Let
d>2
be a positive integer. Taking

\displaystyle f=\sqrt{1-\dfrac{2A}{r^{2}}-\dfrac{B}{r^{4}}},\quad A=-\dfrac{d-2}{2}\sqrt{\dfrac{B}{d-1}},\quad r_{0}=\sqrt[4]{\dfrac{B}{d-1}}

and

\displaystyle r\geq r_{0},\quad 0\leq\theta\leq\pi,\quad 0\leq\phi\leq 2\pi,\quad 0\leq\psi\leq\frac{4\pi}{d},

metric (
2.1
) is

g=\left(1-\dfrac{2A}{r^{2}}-\dfrac{B}{r^{4}}\right)^{-1}\mathrm{d}r^{2}+r^{2}\left(\sigma_{1}^{2}+\sigma_{2}^{2}+\left(1-\dfrac{2A}{r^{2}}-\dfrac{B}{r^{4}}\right)\sigma_{3}^{2}\right),

(2.6)

which is scalar-flat and geodesically complete, constructed in
[
8
,
9
]
. Topologically, it is

\displaystyle R_{\geq 0}\times SU(2)/Z_{d}\cong R_{\geq 0}\times S^{3}/Z_{d}.

Spin Connection and Parallel Spinors

We refer to
[
10
]
for an introductory knowledge of spin geometry. It is clear that the underline manifolds of both metric (

) and metric (
2.6
) are spin and their complex spinor bundles are complex 4-dimensional equipped with spin connection

\nabla_{e_{k}}\Phi=e_{k}\Phi+\dfrac{1}{4}\sum_{i,j=1}^{4}g\left(\nabla_{e_{k}}e_{i},e_{j}\right)e_{i}\cdot e_{j}\cdot\Phi

\displaystyle\nabla_{e_{1}}\Phi=e_{1}\Phi,

\displaystyle\nabla_{e_{3}}\Phi=e_{3}\Phi+\dfrac{f}{2r}\left(e_{1}\cdot e_{3}+e_{2}\cdot e_{4}\right)\cdot\Phi,

\displaystyle\nabla_{e_{4}}\Phi=e_{4}\Phi+\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\left(\dfrac{f}{r}+f^{\prime}\right)e_{1}\cdot e_{4}+\left(\dfrac{f}{r}-\dfrac{2}{rf}\right)e_{2}\cdot e_{3}\right)\cdot\Phi.

Spinor
\Phi
is parallel if
\nabla_{X}\Phi=0
for any tangent vector

. Any Riemannian metric carried a parallel spinor must be Ricci-flat, see, e.g.
[

]
. Thus it is interesting to investigate the parallel spinors for the Eguchi-Hanson metric.

Theorem 3.1
.

Parallel spinors for the Eguchi-Hanson metric (
2.5
) are complex constant spinors which form a complex 2-dimensional space.

Proof:
Under (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{e_{1}}\Phi=e_{1}\begin{pmatrix}\Phi_{1}\\
\Phi_{2}\\
\Phi_{3}\\
\Phi_{4}\\
\end{pmatrix}=0,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\nabla_{e_{2}}\Phi=e_{2}\begin{pmatrix}\Phi_{1}\\
\Phi_{2}\\
\Phi_{3}\\
\Phi_{4}\\
\end{pmatrix}+\dfrac{f}{2r}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
0\\
-2\mathrm{i}\Phi_{4}\\
-2\mathrm{i}\Phi_{3}\\
\end{pmatrix}=0,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\nabla_{e_{3}}\Phi=e_{3}\begin{pmatrix}\Phi_{1}\\
\Phi_{2}\\
\Phi_{3}\\
\Phi_{4}\\
\end{pmatrix}+\dfrac{f}{2r}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
0\\
2\Phi_{4}\\
-2\Phi_{3}\\
\end{pmatrix}=0,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\nabla_{e_{4}}\Phi=e_{4}\begin{pmatrix}\Phi_{1}\\
\Phi_{2}\\
\Phi_{3}\\
\Phi_{4}\\
\end{pmatrix}+\left(\dfrac{1}{rf}+\dfrac{f^{\prime}}{2}\right)\begin{pmatrix}0\\
0\\
-\mathrm{i}\Phi_{3}\\
\mathrm{i}\Phi_{4}\\
\end{pmatrix}=0.

(3.6)

Firstly, (
3.3
) implies

\displaystyle\partial_{r}\Phi=0.

Secondly, (
3.4
) gives

\displaystyle\left(\sin\psi\partial_{\theta}-\dfrac{\cos\psi}{\sin\theta}\partial_{\phi}+\dfrac{\cos\theta\cos\psi}{\sin\theta}\partial_{\psi}\right)\begin{pmatrix}\Phi_{3}\\
\Phi_{4}\end{pmatrix}=\dfrac{f}{r}\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{i}\Phi_{4}\\
\mathrm{i}\Phi_{3}\end{pmatrix}.

Since the left hand side is independent on
r
, but the right hand side contains function
f(r)
, it must be

\displaystyle\Phi_{3}=\Phi_{4}=0.

Now (
3.6
) gives

\displaystyle\partial_{\psi}\Phi=0.

Thus (
3.4
), (
3.5
) give

\displaystyle\dfrac{2}{r}\left(\sin\psi\partial_{\theta}-\dfrac{\cos\psi}{\sin\theta}\partial_{\phi}\right)\begin{pmatrix}\Phi_{1}\\
\Phi_{2}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\dfrac{2}{r}\left(-\cos\psi\partial_{\theta}-\dfrac{\sin\psi}{\sin\theta}\partial_{\phi}\right)\begin{pmatrix}\Phi_{1}\\
\Phi_{2}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=0.

Therefore

\displaystyle\partial_{\theta}\begin{pmatrix}\Phi_{1}\\
\Phi_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\partial_{\phi}\begin{pmatrix}\Phi_{1}\\
\Phi_{2}\end{pmatrix}=0.

So
\Phi_{1}
and
\Phi_{2}
are arbitrary complex constants and the proof of the theorem is complete. Q.E.D.

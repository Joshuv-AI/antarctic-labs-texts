Galois correspondence for Galois coverings on tropical curves
Song JuAe

Abstract
. We define Galois coverings on tropical curves for which a Galois correspondence and a universal mapping property hold.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 14T15; Secondary 14T20

1.
Introduction

Let
\varphi:\varGamma\to\varGamma^{\prime}
be a map between tropical curves and
G
a finite group.
For an isometric action of
G
on
\varGamma
,
\varphi
is
Galois
if (1)
\varphi
is a finite harmonic morphism, (2) the order of
G
coincides with the degree of
\varphi
, (3) the action of
G
on
\varGamma
induces a transitive action on every fiber, and (4) every stabilizer subgroup of
G
with respect to all but a finite number of points is trivial.
We also say
\varphi
to be
G
-Galois
.
Here, a tropical curve is a metric graph that may have edges of length
\infty
, and a finite harmonic morphism is defined as a morphism of our category of tropical curves (see Section
2
for more details).
When
\varphi
is
G
-Galois, for a finite harmonic morphism
\psi:\varGamma\to\varGamma^{\prime\prime}
, we define
G(\psi)
as the subset of
G
consisting of all elements
g
of
G
satisfying
\psi\circ g=\psi
.
For a fixed
G
-Galois covering
\varphi:\varGamma\to\varGamma^{\prime}
, let
A^{\prime}
be the set of all finite haromonic morphisms
\widetilde{\psi}:\varGamma\to\widetilde{\varGamma^{\prime\prime}}
each of which is
G(\widetilde{\psi})
-Galois.
For two elements
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
of
A^{\prime}
, we write
\psi_{1}\sim\psi_{2}
if there exists a finite harmonic morphism of degree one
\psi_{12}
satisfying
\psi_{1}=\psi_{12}\circ\psi_{2}
.
This
\sim
becomes an equivalence relation.
Let
A:=A^{\prime}/\!\!\sim
.
For
[\psi_{i}]\in A
, where
[\psi_{i}]
denotes the equivalence class of
\psi_{i}
, we write
[\psi_{1}]\leq_{A}[\psi_{2}]
if for any
\psi^{\prime}_{1}\in[\psi_{1}]
and
\psi^{\prime}_{2}\in[\psi_{2}]
, there exists a finite harmonic morphism
\theta
satisfying
\psi^{\prime}_{1}=\theta\circ\psi^{\prime}_{2}
.
This
\leq_{A}
is well-defined and becomes a partial order.
Let
B
be the set of all subgroups of
G
.
For
G_{i}\in B
, we define a partial order
\leq_{B}
such that
G_{1}\leq_{B}G_{2}
if
G_{1}\subset G_{2}
.
Then, we have the following:

Theorem 1.1

(Galois correspondence for Galois coverings)
.

In the above setting, there exists a one-to-one correspondence between
(A,\leq_{A})
and
(B,\leq_{B})
reversing (partial) orders.

For Galois coverings, the following two theorems hold.
Here, for a map between tropical curves
\varphi:\varGamma\to\varGamma^{\prime}
and an isometric action of a finite group
H
on
\varGamma
,
\varphi
is
H
-normal
if
\varphi
satisfies the conditions (1), (3), and (4) above.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\varphi:\varGamma\to\varGamma^{\prime}
be a
G
-Galois covering,
\psi:\varGamma\to\varGamma^{\prime\prime}
in
A^{\prime}
, and
\theta:\varGamma^{\prime\prime}\to\varGamma^{\prime}
the unique continuous map satisfying
\varphi=\theta\circ\psi
.
Then, the following are equivelent:

(1)

G(\psi)
is a normal subgroup of
G
,

(2)
there exists a finite group
H
isometrically acting on
\varGamma^{\prime\prime}
such that
\theta
is
H
-Galois, and

(3)
there exists a finite group
H^{\prime}
isometrically acting on
\varGamma^{\prime\prime}
such that
\theta
is
H^{\prime}
-normal.

Moreover, suppose in addition to
(2)
that for any
g\in G
, there exists
h\in H
satisfying
\psi\circ g=h\circ\psi
, then
H
is isomorphic to the quotient group
G/G(\psi)
.

Theorem 1.3

(Universal mapping property)
.

Let
\varphi:\varGamma\to\varGamma^{\prime}
be
G
-Galois.
Then, for any finite harmonic morphism
\psi:\varGamma\to\varGamma^{\prime\prime}
satisfying for any
g\in G
,
\psi\circ g=\psi
, there exists a unique finite harmonic morphism
\theta
satisfying
\psi=\theta\circ\varphi
.

Our definition of Galois coverings on tropical curves is an enhanced version of the definition in
[
2
]
(the condition (4) is added).
By adding the condition (4), Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
above and Theorem
1.4
below, which will be shown in
[
3
]
, hold; see Remark
3.7
and Examples
3.8
,
3.9
,
3.10
.

Theorem 1.4

(
[
3
]
)
.

Let
\varphi:\varGamma\to\varGamma^{\prime}
be a finite harmonic morphism between tropical curves and
G
a finite group isometrically acting on
\varGamma
.
Then,
\varphi
is
G
-Galois if and only if the action of
G
on
\operatorname{Rat}(\varGamma)
naturally induced by the action of
G
on
\varGamma
is Galois for the semifield extension
\operatorname{Rat}(\varGamma)/\varphi^{\ast}(\operatorname{Rat}(\varGamma^{\prime}))
.

Here,
\operatorname{Rat}(\varGamma)
denotes the semifield of all rational functions on
\varGamma
and
\varphi^{\ast}(\operatorname{Rat}(\varGamma^{\prime}))
the pull-back.
In the setting of Theorem
1.4
, “the action of
G
on
\operatorname{Rat}(\varGamma)
is Galois for
\operatorname{Rat}(\varGamma)/\varphi^{\ast}(\operatorname{Rat}(\varGamma^{\prime}))
” means that the
G
-invariant subsemifield of
\operatorname{Rat}(\varGamma)
is
\varphi^{\ast}(\operatorname{Rat}(\varGamma^{\prime}))
and that subgroups of

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we prepare basic definitions related to tropical curves which we need later.
See, for example,
[
1
]
for details.

In this paper, a
graph
is an unweighted, undirected, finite, connected nonempty multigraph that may have loops.
For a graph
G
, the set of vertices is denoted by
V(G)
and the set of edges by
E(G)
.
The
degree
of a vertex is the number of edges incident to it.
Here, a loop is counted twice.
A
leaf end
is a vertex of degree one.
A
leaf edge
is an edge incident to a leaf end.
A
tropical curve
is the underlying topological space of the pair
(G,l)
of a graph
G
and a function
l:E(G)\to{\boldsymbol{R}}_{>0}\cup\{\infty\}
called a
length function
, where
l
can take the value
\infty
on only leaf edges, together with an identification of each edge
e
of
G
with the closed interval
[0,l(e)]
.
When
l(e)=\infty
, the interval
[0,\infty]
is the one point compactification of the interval
[0,\infty)
and the leaf end of
e
must be identified with
\infty
.
We regard
[0,\infty]
not just as a topological space but as almost a metric space.
The distance between
\infty
and any other point is infinite.
If
E(G)=\{e\}
and
l(e)=\infty
, then we can identify either leaf ends of
e
with
\infty
.
When a tropical curve
\varGamma
is obtained from
(G,l)
, the pair
(G,l)
is called a
model
for
\varGamma
.
There are many possible models for
\varGamma
.
A model
(G,l)
is
loopless
if
G
is loopless.
Let
\varGamma_{\infty}
denote the set of all points of
\varGamma
identified with
\infty
.
An element of
\varGamma_{\infty}
is called a
point at infinity
.
We frequently identify a vertex (resp. an edge) of
G
with the corresponding point (resp. the corresponding closed subset) of
\varGamma
.
The
relative interior

e^{\circ}
of an edge
e
is
e\setminus\{v,w\}
with the endpoint(s)
v,w
of
e
.

Let
\varphi:\varGamma\to\varGamma^{\prime}
be a continuous map between tropical curves.

\varphi
is a
finite morphism
if there exist loopless models
(G,l)
and
(G^{\prime},l^{\prime})
for
\varGamma
and
\varGamma^{\prime}
, respectively, such that
(1)

\varphi(V(G))\subset V(G^{\prime})
holds,
(2)

\varphi(E(G))\subset E(G^{\prime})
holds, and
(3)
for any edge
e
of
G
, there exists a positive integer
\operatorname{deg}_{e}(\varphi)
such that for any points
x,y
of
e
,
\operatorname{dist}(\varphi(x),\varphi(y))=\operatorname{deg}_{e}(\varphi)\cdot\operatorname{dist}(x,y)
holds.
Here,
\operatorname{dist}(x,y)
denotes the distance between
x
and
y
.
Assume that
\varphi
is a finite morphism.

\varphi
is
harmonic
if for every vertex
v
of
G
, the sum
\sum_{e\in E(G):\,e\mapsto e^{\prime},\,v\in e}\operatorname{deg}_{e}(\varphi)
is independent of the choice of
e^{\prime}\in E(G^{\prime})
incident to
\varphi(v)
.
This sum is denoted by
\operatorname{deg}_{v}(\varphi)
.
Then, the sum
\Sigma_{v\in V(G):\,v\mapsto v^{\prime}}\operatorname{deg}_{v}(\varphi)
is independent of the choice of vertex
v^{\prime}
of
G^{\prime}
.
It is said the
degree
of
\varphi
and written by
\operatorname{deg}(\varphi)
.
If both
\varGamma
and
\varGamma^{\prime}
are singletons, we regard
\varphi
as a finite harmonic morphism that can have any number as its degree.
Note that if
\varphi\circ\psi
is a composition of finite harmonic morphisms, then it is also a finite harmonic morphism of degree
\operatorname{deg}(\varphi)\cdot\operatorname{deg}(\psi)
, and thus tropical curves and finite harmonic morphisms between them make a category.

Remark 2.1
.

Let
\varphi:\varGamma\to\varGamma^{\prime}
be a map between tropical curves.
Then
\varphi
is a continuous map whose restriction on
\varGamma\setminus\varGamma_{\infty}
is an isometry if and only if it is a finite harmonic morphism of degree one.
In this paper, we will use the word “a finite group
G
isometrically acts on a tropical curve
\varGamma
” as the meaning that
G
continuously acts on
\varGamma
and it is isometric on
\varGamma\setminus\varGamma_{\infty}
.

Remark 2.2
.

Let
\varGamma
be a tropical curve and
G
a finite group isometrically acting on
\varGamma
.
Let
\varGamma/G
be the quotient space (as a topological space) and
\pi_{G}:\varGamma\to\varGamma/G
be the natural surjection.
Fix a loopless model
(V,E,l)
(
V
is a set of vertices and
E
is a set of edges) for
\varGamma
such that for any
g\in G
and
e\in E
,
g(V)=V
holds and if

, then the restriction of

on

is the identity map of
e
.
Note that by the proof of
[
1
, Lemma 3.4]
, we can choose such a loopless model.
Let
V^{\prime}:=\pi_{G}(V)
,
E^{\prime}:=\pi_{G}(E)
and for any

,
l^{\prime}(\pi_{G}(e)):=|G_{e}|\cdot l(e)
, where
G_{e}
denotes the stabilizer subgroup of

with respect to

and

the order of

.
Then,
(V^{\prime},E^{\prime},l^{\prime})
gives
\varGamma/G

\varGamma/G
.
By loopless models
(V,E,l)
and
(V^{\prime},E^{\prime},l^{\prime})
for
\varGamma
and
\varGamma/G
, respectively,
\pi_{G}
is a finite harmonic morphism of degree
|G|
.

3.
Main results

In this section, we give our definition of Galois coverings on tropical curves and proofs of Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
, and
1.3
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\varGamma
be a tropical curve and
G
a finite group.
An isometric action of
G
on
\varGamma
is
Galois
if there exists a finite subset
U^{\prime}
of
\varGamma/G
such that for any
x^{\prime}\in(\varGamma/G)\setminus U^{\prime}
,
|\pi^{-1}_{G}(x^{\prime})|=|G|
holds.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\varphi:\varGamma\to\varGamma^{\prime}
be a map between tropical curves.

\varphi
is
Galois
if there exists a Galois action of a finite group
G
on
\varGamma
such that there exists a finite haramonic morphism of degree one
\theta:\varGamma/G\to\varGamma^{\prime}
satisfying
\varphi\circ g=\theta\circ\pi_{G}
for any
g\in G
.
Then, we say that
\varphi
is a
G
-Galois covering
on
\varGamma^{\prime}
or just
G
-Galois
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let

\displaystyle\Phi:A\to B;\qquad[\psi]\mapsto G(\psi)

and

\displaystyle\Psi:B\to A;\qquad G^{\prime}\mapsto[\pi_{G^{\prime}}].

We shall show that if
\psi_{1}\sim\psi_{2}
holds, then
G(\psi_{1})=G(\psi_{2})
holds.
For any
f\in G(\psi_{2})
, we have

\displaystyle\psi_{1}\circ f=(\psi_{12}\circ\psi_{2})\circ f=\psi_{12}\circ(\psi_{2}\circ f)=\psi_{12}\circ\psi_{2}=\psi_{1}.

Thus
f\in G(\psi_{1})
holds.
The inverse inclusion can be shown similarly.
Therefore,
\Phi
is well-defined.

We shall show that for any
G^{\prime}\in B
, the action of
G^{\prime}
on
\varGamma
induced by that of
G
on
\varGamma
is Galois.
Let
U^{\prime}
be the finite subset of
\varGamma/G
in Definition
3.1
.
For any
x\in\varGamma\setminus\pi^{-1}_{G}(U^{\prime})
, we have
|G_{x}|=\frac{|G|}{|Gx|}=1
, where we use the orbit-stabilizer theorem (cf.
[
4
, Chapter 6]
) and
Gx
stands for the orbit of
x
by
G
, and thus
|G^{\prime}_{x}|=1
.
Therefore,
\pi_{G^{\prime}}(\pi^{-1}_{G}(U^{\prime}))
is finite and for any
x^{\prime}\in(\varGamma/G^{\prime})\setminus\pi_{G^{\prime}}(\pi^{-1}_{G}(U^{\prime}))
, we have
|\pi^{-1}_{G^{\prime}}(x^{\prime})|=|G^{\prime}|
.

We shall show that for any
G^{\prime}\in B
,
G^{\prime}=G(\pi_{G^{\prime}})
holds.
By the definition of
G(\pi_{G^{\prime}})
,
G^{\prime}\subset G(\pi_{G^{\prime}})
holds.
Let
g\in G(\pi_{G^{\prime}})
.
For any
x\in\varGamma
, since
(\pi_{G^{\prime}}\circ g)(x)=\pi_{G^{\prime}}(x)
, there exists
g^{\prime}\in G^{\prime}
such that
g(x)=g^{\prime}(x)
.
Let us assume that
x
is in
\varGamma\setminus\pi^{-1}_{G}(U^{\prime})
.
Then
g=g^{\prime}
must hold.
In fact, if
g\not=g^{\prime}
, then
g^{-1}g\not=1
and
g^{-1}g^{\prime}(x)=x
.
Thus,
g^{-1}g^{\prime}\in G_{x}\setminus\{1\}
and it contradicts that
|\pi^{-1}_{G}(x)|=|G|
.
By these arguments, we have
[\pi_{G^{\prime}}]\in A
and
\Phi\circ\Psi=\operatorname{id}_{B}
, where
\operatorname{id}_{B}
denotes the identity map of
B
.

We shall show that
\Psi\circ\Phi=\operatorname{id}_{A}
holds.
By the definitions of
A
and Galois coverings, for any
\psi\in A^{\prime}
,
\psi\sim\pi_{G(\psi)}
holds, and hence we have

(\Psi\circ\Phi)([\psi])=[\pi_{G(\psi)}]=[\psi].

We shall show that if
[\psi_{1}]\leq_{A}[\psi_{2}]
holds, then
G(\psi_{1})\geq_{B}G(\psi_{2})
holds.
For
g\in G(\psi_{2})
, we have
\psi_{2}\circ g=\psi_{2}
.
Since
[\psi_{1}]\leq_{A}[\psi_{2}]
, there exists a finite harmonic morphism
\theta
satisfying
\psi_{1}=\theta\circ\psi_{2}
.
Therefore

\displaystyle\psi_{1}\circ g=(\theta\circ\psi_{2})\circ g=\theta\circ(\psi_{2}\circ g)=\theta\circ\psi_{2}=\psi_{1}

hold.
Hence
g\in G(\psi_{1})
.

Finally, we shall show that if
G_{1}\leq_{B}G_{2}
holds, then
[\pi_{G_{1}}]\geq_{A}[\pi_{G_{2}}]
holds.
Since
G_{1}\subset G_{2}
, there exists a unique continuous map
\theta
satisfying
\theta\circ\pi_{G_{1}}=\pi_{G_{2}}
.
We shall show
\theta
is a finite harmonic morphism.
Let
(V,E,l),(V_{1},E_{1},l_{1})
and
(V_{2},E_{2},l_{2})
be loopless models for
\varGamma
,
\varGamma/G_{1}
and
\varGamma/G_{2}
, respectively, compatible with
\pi_{G_{i}}
, i.e.,
\pi_{G_{i}}(V)=V_{i}
for
i=1,2
.
Let
e\in E
.
Since each
\pi_{G_{i}}
is Galois, for any points
x,y
of
e
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{dist}(\pi_{G_{2}}(x),\pi_{G_{2}}(y))=\operatorname{dist}(x,y)=\operatorname{dist}(\pi_{G_{1}}(x),\pi_{G_{1}}(y)).

Hence
\theta
is a finite morphism and
\operatorname{deg}_{\pi_{1}(e)}(\theta)=1
.
Next, we shall show that
\theta
is harmonic.
Fix a vertex
v^{\prime}\in V_{1}
and an edge
e^{\prime\prime}\in E_{2}
incident to
\theta(v^{\prime})
.
Since the action of
G
on
\varGamma
is isometric and
G_{1}\subset G_{2}
, with any
v\in\pi^{-1}_{G_{2}}(\theta(v^{\prime}))
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{e^{\prime}\in E_{1}:\,e^{\prime}\mapsto e^{\prime\prime},\,v^{\prime}\in e^{\prime}}\operatorname{deg}_{e^{\prime}}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\theta^{-1}(\theta(v^{\prime}))|}\cdot\sum_{e^{\prime}\in E_{1}:e^{\prime}\mapsto e^{\prime\prime}}\operatorname{deg}_{e^{\prime}}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\theta^{-1}(\theta(v^{\prime}))|}\cdot|\{e^{\prime}\in E_{1}\,|\,\theta(e^{\prime})=e^{\prime\prime}\}|

and

\displaystyle|\theta^{-1}(\theta(v^{\prime}))|=\frac{|\pi_{G_{2}}^{-1}(\theta(v^{\prime}))|}{|G_{1}v|}=\frac{|G_{2}v|}{|G_{1}v|}.

With any edge
e\in E
such that
\pi_{G_{2}}(e)=e^{\prime\prime}
, the ratio
\frac{|G_{2}e|}{|G_{1}e|}=\frac{|G_{2}|}{|G_{1}|}
coincides with the number of elements of the inverse image of
e^{\prime\prime}
by
\theta
.
Therefore we have

\displaystyle\sum_{e^{\prime}\in E_{1}:\,e^{\prime}\mapsto e^{\prime\prime},\,v^{\prime}\in e^{\prime}}\operatorname{deg}_{e^{\prime}}(\theta)=\frac{|G_{1}v|}{|G_{2}v|}\cdot\frac{|G_{2}|}{|G_{1}|}=\frac{|{G_{2}}_{v}|}{|{G_{1}}_{v}|}\in\boldsymbol{Z}_{>0},

where we use the orbit-stabilizer theorem at the second equality.
Hence the sum is independent of the choice of
e^{\prime\prime}
and thus
\theta
is harmonic.
∎

Next, we will prove Theorem
1.2
.
For this purpose, we define normal coverings on tropical curves:

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\varphi:\varGamma\to\varGamma^{\prime}
be a map between tropical curves.

\varphi
is
normal
if there exists a Galois action of a finite group
H
on
\varGamma
and a finite harmonic morphism
\theta:\varGamma/H\to\varGamma^{\prime}
such that for any
h\in H
,
\varphi\circ h=\theta\circ\pi_{H}
holds.
Then, we say that
\varphi
is a
H
-normal covering
on
\varGamma^{\prime}
or just
H
-normal
.

By definition, if
\varphi
is
G
-Galois, then it is
G
-normal.
Normal coverings have the following property:

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\varphi:\varGamma\to\varGamma^{\prime}
be a
H
-normal covering on
\varGamma^{\prime}
.
Then, for any finite harmonic morphism
\psi:\varGamma^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow\varGamma
, there exists a loopless model
(G^{\prime\prime},l^{\prime\prime})
for
\varGamma^{\prime\prime}
such that for any
e^{\prime\prime}\in E(G^{\prime\prime})
and any automorphim
f
of
\varGamma^{\prime\prime}
, i.e., a finite harmonic morphism of degree one
\varGamma^{\prime\prime}\to\varGamma^{\prime\prime}
, satisfying
\varphi\circ\psi\circ f=\varphi\circ\psi
, there exists
h\in H
such that
(\psi\circ f)|_{e^{\prime\prime}}=(h\circ\psi)|_{e^{\prime\prime}}
, where
|_{e^{\prime\prime}}
denotes the restriction of the map on
e^{\prime\prime}
.

Proof.

By the proof of
[
1
, Lemma 3.4]
, we can choose
(G^{\prime\prime},l^{\prime\prime})
such that

\psi(V(G^{\prime\prime}))
(resp.
(\varphi\circ\psi)(V(G^{\prime\prime}))
) induces a loopless model for
\varGamma
(resp.
\varGamma^{\prime}
) compatible with
\psi
(resp.
\varphi\circ\psi
) and for any
e^{\prime\prime}\in E(G^{\prime\prime})
and
h\in H
, if
h(\psi(e^{\prime\prime}))=\psi(e^{\prime\prime})
, then the restriction
h|_{\psi(e^{\prime\prime})}
is the identity map on
\psi(e^{\prime\prime})
.
For any edge
e^{\prime\prime}\in E(G^{\prime\prime})
and any point
x^{\prime\prime}
of
(e^{\prime\prime})^{\circ}
, since
(\varphi\circ\psi\circ f)(x^{\prime\prime})=(\varphi\circ\psi)(x^{\prime\prime})
, there exists
h\in H
satisfying
(\psi\circ f)(x^{\prime\prime})=(h\circ\psi)(x^{\prime\prime})
.
Since the action of
H
on
\varGamma
is isometric and
h
does not invert any edge,
(\psi\circ f)|_{(e^{\prime\prime})^{\circ}}=(h\circ\psi)|_{(e^{\prime\prime})^{\circ}}
holds.
Since both
\psi\circ f
and
h\circ\psi
are continuous,
(\psi\circ f)|_{e^{\prime\prime}}=(h\circ\psi)|_{e^{\prime\prime}}
holds.
∎

This proposition is an analogue of the fact in the field theory that for an algebraic extension
L/K
of fields, it is normal if and only if all embeddings
\sigma
of
L
to the algebraic closure of
K
containing
L
which is the identity on
K
satisfy
\sigma(L)=L
.
In the tropical curve case, however, we must consider only an edge instead of the whole tropical curve.

Let
\varphi:\varGamma\rightarrow\varGamma^{\prime}
be a
G
-Galois covering on
\varGamma^{\prime}
.
For any
\psi:\varGamma\rightarrow\varGamma^{\prime\prime}
in
A^{\prime}
, since
G(\psi)
is a subgroup of
G
and
\varGamma^{\prime}
and
\varGamma^{\prime\prime}
have quotient topologies, there exists a unique continuous map
\theta:\varGamma^{\prime\prime}\to\varGamma^{\prime}
such that
\varphi=\theta\circ\psi
.

Proposition 3.5
.

In the above setting, the following are equivalent:

(1)

G(\psi)
is a normal subgroup of
G
,

(2)
there exists a finite group
H
isometrically acting on
\varGamma^{\prime\prime}
such that
\theta
is
H
-Galois and for any
g\in G
, there exists
h\in H
satisfying
\psi\circ g=h\circ\psi
, and

(3)
there exists a finite group
H^{\prime}
isometrically acting on
\varGamma^{\prime\prime}
such that
\theta
is
H^{\prime}
-normal and for any
g\in G
, there exists
h^{\prime}\in H^{\prime}
satisfying
\psi\circ g=h^{\prime}\circ\psi
.

Moreover, if the above conditions hold, then
H
in
(2)
is isomorphic to the quotient group
G/G(\psi)
.

For a map between tropical curves, we will use the word “it is a local isometry” as the meaning that it is continuous on the whole domain tropical curve and that it is a local isometry on the domain tropical curve except all points at infinity.

Proof of Proposition
3.5
.

Clearly (2) implies (3).

Assume that (3) holds.
For
g\in G
and
h^{\prime}\in H^{\prime}
, if
\psi\circ g=h^{\prime}\circ\psi
, then
\psi\circ g^{-1}={h^{\prime}}^{-1}\circ\psi
, and thus for any
g^{\prime}\in G(\psi)
, we have

\displaystyle\psi\circ(g^{-1}g^{\prime}g)

\displaystyle=(\psi\circ g^{-1})\circ g^{\prime}g=({h^{\prime}}^{-1}\circ\psi)\circ g^{\prime}g

\displaystyle={h^{\prime}}^{-1}\circ(\psi\circ g^{\prime})\circ g={h^{\prime}}^{-1}\circ\psi\circ g

\displaystyle={h^{\prime}}^{-1}\circ(\psi\circ g)={h^{\prime}}^{-1}h^{\prime}\circ\psi=\psi.

This means that
g^{-1}g^{\prime}g\in G(\psi)
.
Thus (1) holds.

Assume that (1) holds.
By the same argument of the last part of the proof of Theorem
1.1
,
\theta
is a finite harmonic morphism of degree
\frac{|G|}{|G(\psi)|}
.
Here,
G(\psi)
corresponds to
G_{1}
and
G
to
G_{2}
in this case.
We define an isomertic action of
G/G(\psi)
on
\varGamma^{\prime\prime}
as
[g](x^{\prime\prime}):=\psi(g(x))
for
[g]\in G/G(\psi)
,
x^{\prime\prime}\in\varGamma^{\prime\prime}
and any
x\in\psi^{-1}(x^{\prime\prime})
.
It is well-defined.
In fact, if
x_{1}\in\psi^{-1}(x^{\prime\prime})
, then there exists
g_{1}\in G(\psi)
such that
g_{1}(x_{1})=x
.
Since
G(\psi)
is a normal subgroup of
G
, there exists
g_{2}\in G(\psi)
such that
g(x)=gg_{1}(x_{1})=g_{2}g(x_{1})
.
Thus we have
\psi(g(x))=\psi(g_{2}g(x_{1}))=\psi(g(x_{1}))
.
If
[g]=[\widetilde{g}]
, then there exists
g_{1}\in G(\psi)
such that
g=g_{1}\widetilde{g}
.
Hence
\psi(g(x))=\psi(g_{1}\widetilde{g}(x))=\psi(\widetilde{g}(x))
.
Since the action of
G
on
\varGamma
is Galois, there exists a finite subset
U^{\prime}
of
\varGamma/G
such that for any
x^{\prime}\in(\varGamma/G)\setminus U^{\prime}
,
|\pi^{-1}_{G}(x^{\prime})|=|G|
holds.
Let
O
be the finite subset
(\pi_{G/G(\psi)}\circ\psi)(\pi^{-1}_{G}(U^{\prime}))
of
\varGamma^{\prime\prime}/(G/G(\psi))
, where
\pi_{G/G(\psi)}:\varGamma^{\prime\prime}\to\varGamma^{\prime\prime}/(G/G(\psi))
is the natural surjection.
Let
[x^{\prime\prime}]\in(\varGamma^{\prime\prime}/(G/G(\psi)))\setminus O
.
We have
\pi^{-1}_{G/G(\psi)}([x^{\prime\prime}])=(G/G(\psi))x^{\prime\prime}
.
Since
|\varphi^{-1}(\theta(x^{\prime\prime}))|=|G|
and
\psi
is
G(\psi)
-Galois, we have

\displaystyle|(G/G(\psi))x^{\prime\prime}|=|\psi(\varphi^{-1}(\theta(x^{\prime\prime})))|=\frac{|G|}{|G(\psi)|},

and thus the action of
G/G(\psi)
on
\varGamma^{\prime\prime}
is Galois.
Let

\displaystyle\phi:\varGamma^{\prime\prime}/(G/G(\psi))\to\varGamma^{\prime};\qquad\pi_{G/G(\psi)}(x^{\prime\prime})\mapsto\theta(x^{\prime\prime}),

where
x^{\prime\prime}\in\varGamma^{\prime\prime}
.
With any fixed element
x\in\psi^{-1}((G/G(\psi))x^{\prime\prime})
, since

\displaystyle\theta((G/G(\psi))x^{\prime\prime})=\theta(\psi(Gx))=\theta(\psi(x))=\varphi(x),

we have
\theta(x^{\prime\prime})=\varphi(x)
, and thus
\phi
is well-defined.
By definition, for any
[g]\in G/G(\psi)
,
\phi\circ\pi_{G/G(\psi)}=\theta\circ[g]
holds.
Since
\pi_{G/G(\psi)}
and
\theta
are local isometries and
[g]
is an automorphism,
\phi
is a local isometry.
When
\theta(x^{\prime\prime})=\theta(y^{\prime\prime})
, with any
x\in\psi^{-1}((G/G(\psi))x^{\prime\prime})
and
y\in\psi^{-1}((G/G(\psi))y^{\prime\prime})
,
(G/G(\psi))x^{\prime\prime}=\psi(Gx)=\psi(Gy)=(G/G(\psi))y^{\prime\prime}
hold, and thus
\pi_{G/G(\psi)}(x^{\prime\prime})=\pi_{G/G(\psi)}(y^{\prime\prime})
holds.
This means that
\phi
is injective and hence a finite harmonic morphism of degree one.
In conclusion,
\theta
is
G/G(\psi)
-Galois.

We shall show the last assertion.
By (1), the quotient group
G/G(\psi)
naturally acts on
\varGamma^{\prime\prime}
as above.
Since each stabilizer subgroup of
G
with respect to each point of
\varGamma
except a finite number of points is trivial, the natural action is faithful, i.e., for any equivalence class
[g]\in G/G(\psi)
other than
[1]
, there exists a point
x^{\prime\prime}\in\varGamma^{\prime\prime}
such that
[g](x^{\prime\prime})\not=x^{\prime\prime}
.
For any
g\in G
,
\psi\circ g=[g]\circ\psi
and there exists
h\in H
such that
\psi\circ g=h\circ\psi
.
Since the natural action above is faithful, if
[g]\not=[\widetilde{g}]
, then
[g]\circ\psi\not=[\widetilde{g}]\circ\psi
.
Thus, the map

\displaystyle G/G(\psi)\to H;\qquad[g]\mapsto h

is injective.
Since

\displaystyle|H|=\operatorname{deg}(\theta)=\frac{|G|}{|G(\psi)|}=|G/G(\psi)|

holds, this map is surjective.
By definition, it is a group homomorphism.
In conclusion, we have the desired group isomorphism.
∎

To remove the last conditions in (2) and (3) of Proposition
3.5
, we peove the following lemma by using Proposition
3.4
:

Lemma 3.6
.

In the same setting in Proposition
3.5
, the following are equivalent:

(1)
there exists a finite group
H
isometrically acting on
\varGamma^{\prime\prime}
such that
\theta
is
H
-normal, and

(2)
there exists a finite group
H^{\prime}
isometrically acting on
\varGamma^{\prime\prime}
such that
\theta
is
H^{\prime}
-normal and for any
g\in G
, there exists
h^{\prime}\in H^{\prime}
satisfying
\psi\circ g=h^{\prime}\circ\psi
.

Proof.

(2) clearly implies (1).

Assume that (1) holds.
For any
g\in G
, we define
h^{\prime}_{g}:\varGamma^{\prime\prime}\to\varGamma^{\prime\prime};\psi(x)\mapsto(\psi\circ g)(x)
, where
x\in\varGamma
.
Then, by Proposition
3.4
,
h^{\prime}_{g}
is a local isometry.
Since
\psi\circ g
is surjective and continuous,
h^{\prime}_{g}
is an automorphism of
\varGamma^{\prime\prime}
.
Set
H^{\prime}
as the group generated by these
h^{\prime}_{g}
.
Then
H^{\prime}
induces an isometric and faithful action on
\varGamma^{\prime}
.
For any
x\in\varGamma
, we check that
H^{\prime}\psi(x)=H\psi(x)
.
Since for any
g\in G
, there exists
h\in H
such that

\displaystyle(h^{\prime}_{g}\circ\psi)(x)=(\psi\circ g)(x)=(h\circ\psi)(x),

we have
H^{\prime}\psi(x)\subset H\psi(x)
.
Conversely, for any
h\in H
, since

\displaystyle\varphi(\psi^{-1}((h\circ\psi)(x)))=\theta((h\circ\psi)(x))=(\theta\circ\psi)(x)=\varphi(x),

\psi^{-1}((h\circ\psi)(x))
is contained in
Gx
.
Therefore, for any
\widetilde{x}\in\psi^{-1}((h\circ\psi)(x))
, there exists
g\in G
such that
\widetilde{x}=g(x)
.
Thus, we have

\displaystyle(h\circ\psi)(x)=\psi(\widetilde{x})=\psi(g(x))=(\psi\circ g)(x)=(h^{\prime}_{g}\circ\psi)(x),

and hence
H^{\prime}\psi(x)\supset H\psi(x)
.
From this, the action of
H^{\prime}
on
\varGamma^{\prime}
induces a transitive action on each fiber by the transitivity of the action of
H
on each fiber.
In conclusion,
\theta
is
H^{\prime}
-normal and satisfies that
\theta\circ\psi=\varphi
and for any
g\in G
, there exists
h^{\prime}_{g}\in H^{\prime}
such that
\psi\circ g=h^{\prime}_{g}\circ\psi
.
∎

By Proposition
3.5
and Lemma

Finally, we shall prove Theorem
1.3
.
This theorem means that all Galois coverings are categorical quotients.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Since
\varGamma^{\prime}
has the quotient topology, we have a unique continuous map
\theta
satisfying
\psi=\theta\circ\varphi
, and thus it is enough to check that
\theta
is a finite harmonic morphism.
Fix loopless models
(V,E,l),(V^{\prime},E^{\prime},l^{\prime})
and
(V^{\prime\prime},E^{\prime\prime},l^{\prime\prime})
for
\varGamma,\varGamma^{\prime}
and
\varGamma^{\prime\prime}
, respectively, compatible with
\varphi
and
\psi
.
We can choose such loopless models by the assumptions.
Let
e\in E
.
As for any
x,y\in e
,
\operatorname{dist}(x,y)=\operatorname{dist}(\varphi(x),\varphi(y))
holds, we have
\operatorname{deg}_{e}(\psi)=\operatorname{deg}_{\varphi(e)}(\theta)
.
Therefore,
\theta
is a finite morphism.
Let
v
be in
V
and fix an edge
e^{\prime\prime}\in E^{\prime\prime}
incident to
\psi(v)=\theta(\varphi(v))
.
Then, since
\psi
is a finite harmonic morphism, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{deg}_{v}(\psi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in E:\,e\mapsto e^{\prime\prime},\,v\in e}\operatorname{deg}_{e}(\psi)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{e\in E:\,\varphi(e)\mapsto e^{\prime\prime},\,\varphi(v)\in\varphi(e)}\operatorname{deg}_{\varphi(e)}(\theta)}{|Gv|}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{e^{\prime}\in E^{\prime}:\,e^{\prime}\mapsto e^{\prime\prime},\,\varphi(v)\in e^{\prime}}\operatorname{deg}_{e^{\prime}}(\theta)\cdot|\varphi^{-1}(e^{\prime})|}{|Gv|}

\displaystyle=\frac{|G|}{|Gv|}\cdot\left(\sum_{e^{\prime}\in E^{\prime}:\,e^{\prime}\mapsto e^{\prime\prime},\,\varphi(v)\in e^{\prime}}\operatorname{deg}_{e^{\prime}}(\theta)\right).

Thus we have

\displaystyle\sum_{e^{\prime}\in E^{\prime}:\,e^{\prime}\mapsto e^{\prime\prime},\,\varphi(v)\in e^{\prime}}\operatorname{deg}_{e^{\prime}}(\theta)=\operatorname{deg}_{v}(\psi)\cdot\frac{|Gv|}{|G|}=\frac{\operatorname{deg}_{v}(\psi)}{|G_{v}|}.

It is independent of the choice of
e^{\prime\prime}
and since
\operatorname{deg}_{e^{\prime}}(\theta)=\operatorname{deg}_{e}(\psi)
with any
e\in E
such that
\varphi(e)=e^{\prime}
, it is a positive integer.
These mean that
\theta
is harmonic as
v
is an element of
V
.
The uniqueness comes from the universal mapping property of quotient topology.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

In
[
2
]
, the author gave another definition of Galois coverings on tropical curves.
With that definition, for a map between tropical curves
\varphi:\varGamma\to\varGamma^{\prime}
, if there exists an isometric action of a finite group
G
on
\varGamma
such that there exists a finite harmonic morphism of degree one
\theta:\varGamma/G\to\varGamma^{\prime}
satisfying
\varphi\circ g=\theta\circ\pi_{G}
for any
g\in G
, we call
\varphi
a
G
-Galois covering on
\varGamma^{\prime}
.
In this paper, let us call it a
G
-preGalois covering
on
\varGamma^{\prime}
or a
preGalois covering
simply.
There exists a preGalois covering on a tropical curve for which the Galois correspondence and the universal mapping property do not hold.
See Examples
3.8
,
3.9
, and
3.10
.
By Examples
3.9
and
3.10
, we know that for a preGalois covering
\varphi:\varGamma\to\varGamma^{\prime}
, even there exists a model
(G,l)
for
\varGamma
such that the greatest common divisor of
\{\operatorname{deg}_{e}(\varphi)\,|\,e\in E(G)\}
is one,
\varphi
may not be a Galois covering.

Example 3.8

Let
G
be the graph whose set of vertices consists of two vertices and whose set of edges consists of three multiple edges

Let

stand for this quotient tropical curve.

-preGalois covering on

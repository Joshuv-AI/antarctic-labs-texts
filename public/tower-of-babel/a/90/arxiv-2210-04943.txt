Abstract
. The copointed liftings of the Fomin-Kirillov algebra ℱ ​ 𝒦 3 \mathcal{FK}_{3} over the algebra of functions on the symmetric group 𝕊 3 \mathbb{S}_{3} were classified by Andruskiewitsch and the author. We demonstrate here that those associated to a generic parameter are Morita equivalent to the regular blocks of well-known Hopf algebras: the Drinfeld doubles of the Taft algebras and the small quantum groups u q ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 ) u_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}) . The indecomposable modules over these were classified independently by Chen, Chari–Premet and Suter. Consequently, we obtain the indocomposable modules over the generic liftings of ℱ ​ 𝒦 3 \mathcal{FK}_{3} . We decompose the tensor products between them into the direct sum of indecomposable modules. We then deduce a presentation by generators and relations of the Green ring.



1.
Introduction

The distinctive feature of a Hopf algebra, and source of its greatest applications, is that its representations form a tensor category. Therefore it is natural to investigate the structure of the tensor products between its modules. This information is encoded in the Green ring which was first considered in the context of finite groups by J. A. Green
[
18
]

is monoidally equivalent to the regular block of both

Proof.

We recall that

is generated by

subject to the relations

,

,
ca=-ac
,
dc=-cd
,

a^{2}=d^{2}=0

and

In the case of

In consequence, these extensions give rise to monoidal functors from

3.5.
The syzygy modules

We recall that the syzygy

We set

(7)

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\Omega^{k+1}(\varepsilon)\longrightarrow(k+1)P_{\varepsilon}\longrightarrow\Omega^{k}(\varepsilon)\longrightarrow 0\quad\mbox{for $k$ even,}

(8)

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\Omega^{k+1}(\varepsilon)\longrightarrow(k+1)P_{L}\longrightarrow\Omega^{k}(\varepsilon)\longrightarrow 0\quad\mbox{for $k$ odd.}

Equally,

(9)

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\Omega^{k+1}(L)\longrightarrow(k+1)P_{L}\longrightarrow\Omega^{k}(L)\longrightarrow 0\quad\mbox{for $k$ even,}

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\Omega^{k+1}(L)\longrightarrow(k+1)P_{\varepsilon}\longrightarrow\Omega^{k}(L)\longrightarrow 0\quad\mbox{for $k$ odd.}

Notice that

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow k\,L\longrightarrow

4.

4.1.
Preliminaries

We recall some well-known facts which are useful in the calculus of tensor products. We will use them without an explicit mention.

First, tensoring and dualizing are exact functors. The tensor product with a projective module is projective. See for instance
[
13
]

Second, let
M,N,P,Q
be modules with
P
and
Q
projective, and hence injective, forming the exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow M\oplus P\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow N\oplus Q\longrightarrow 0

Then, using the Krull–Schmidt theorem, it follows that
E\simeq\widetilde{E}\oplus P\oplus Q
for some module
\widetilde{E}
fitting in the exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow\widetilde{E}\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow 0.

Lemma 4.1

(
[
9
, Lemma 3.12]
)
.

Let
\{S_{1},S_{2}\}=\{\varepsilon,L\}
and
M
be an indecomposable module such that
M/\operatorname{soc}M\simeq kS_{1}
for some
k\geq 1
. If
f:sP_{S_{1}}\oplus tP_{S_{2}}\longrightarrow M
is an epimorphism with
s,t\geq 1
, then
s\geq k
and
\operatorname{Ker}(f)\simeq\Omega(M)\oplus(s-k)P_{S_{1}}\oplus tP_{S_{2}}
.

Proof.

It is exactly
[
9
, Lemma 3.12]
except for the claim that
s\geq k
which follows using that
kP_{S_{1}}
is the projective cover of
M
.
∎

In Appendix
A
we explain how we implemente our computations in GAP.

4.2.
Simple tensor simple

We have the following isomorphism

(23)

\displaystyle L{\otimes}L\simeq\varepsilon\oplus 2P_{L}.

Indeed, the projective direct summand is generated by
n_{1}:=v_{(13)}{\otimes}v_{(23)}
and
n_{2}:=v_{(12)}{\otimes}v_{(13)}
. To prove this, according to Lemma
3.1
, we must calculate the action of
x_{soc}
on them. By a computation in GAP, we see that:

\displaystyle x_{soc}\cdot n_{1}

\displaystyle=(2a^{2}-a-1)\left(v_{(23)}{\otimes}v_{(12)}-v_{(12)}{\otimes}v_{(13)}-(a+2)v_{(123)}{\otimes}v_{(123)}\right)\quad\mbox{and}

\displaystyle x_{soc}\cdot n_{2}

\displaystyle=(2a^{2}+a-2)\left(v_{(23)}{\otimes}v_{(12)}-v_{(13)}{\otimes}v_{(23)}-(2a+1)v_{(123)}{\otimes}v_{(123)}\right).

These elements are non-zero as
a\neq 1,-\frac{1}{2},-2
. Then
N_{1}:=\mathcal{A}\cdot n_{1}
and
N_{2}:=\mathcal{A}\cdot n_{2}
are direct summands of
L{\otimes}L
isomorphic to
P_{L}
by Lemma
3.1
. Also,
x_{soc}\cdot n_{1}
and
x_{soc}\cdot n_{2}
are clearly linearly independent and hence
N_{1}\cap N_{2}=0
because they generate the socle of
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
. Therefore
L{\otimes}L=N_{1}\oplus N_{2}\oplus N\simeq 2P_{L}\oplus N
for some submodule
N
. Since
\dim(L{\otimes}L)=\dim(2P_{L})+1
,
N
must be isomorphic to
\varepsilon
and (
23
) is proved. We point out that
N
is spanned by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2a+1}v_{(23)}{\otimes}v_{(23)}-\frac{1}{a+2}v_{(12)}{\otimes}v_{(12)}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{a-1}v_{(13)}{\otimes}v_{(13)}-v_{(123)}{\otimes}v_{(132)}+v_{(132)}{\otimes}v_{(123)}.

4.3.
Tensoring by projectives

We will prove that

(24)

\displaystyle P_{L}{\otimes}L\simeq 4P_{L}\oplus P_{\varepsilon}\simeq L{\otimes}P_{L}\quad\mbox{and}\quad P_{\varepsilon}{\otimes}L\simeq 5P_{L}\simeq L{\otimes}P_{\varepsilon}.

We begin by proving the first isomorphism. Let us apply
-{\otimes}L
to the exact sequence (
13
) for
k=1
:

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow L{\otimes}L\overset{\eqref{iso:L ot L}}{\simeq}\varepsilon\oplus 2P_{L}\longrightarrow\Omega(L){\otimes}L\longrightarrow 2\varepsilon{\otimes}L\simeq 2L\longrightarrow 0.

Then
\Omega(L){\otimes}L\simeq 2P_{L}\oplus N
for some module
N
of dimension
11
. On the other hand, we apply
-{\otimes}L
to the exact sequence (
9
) for
k=0
:

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\Omega(L){\otimes}L\simeq 2P_{L}\oplus N\longrightarrow P_{L}{\otimes}L\longrightarrow L{\otimes}L\overset{\eqref{iso:L ot L}}{\simeq}\varepsilon\oplus 2P_{L}\longrightarrow 0.

Then
P_{L}{\otimes}L\simeq 4P_{L}\oplus Q
for some projective module
Q
such that

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow Q\longrightarrow\varepsilon\longrightarrow 0.

Therefore
Q\simeq P_{\varepsilon}
and the first isomorphism of (
24
) follows. Notice that the same proof runs for
L{\otimes}P_{L}
.

The proof of the third isomorphism of (
24
) is similar. We first apply
-{\otimes}L
to the exact sequence (
11
) for
k=1
:

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\varepsilon{\otimes}L\simeq L\longrightarrow\Omega(\varepsilon){\otimes}L\simeq 2L{\otimes}L\overset{\eqref{iso:L ot L}}{\simeq}2\varepsilon\oplus 4P_{L}\longrightarrow 0.

Then
\Omega(\varepsilon){\otimes}L\simeq 4P_{L}\oplus N
for some module
N
of dimension
7
. Second, we apply
-{\otimes}L
to the exact sequence (
7
) for
k=0
:

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\Omega(\varepsilon){\otimes}L\simeq 4P_{L}\oplus N\longrightarrow P_{\varepsilon}{\otimes}L\longrightarrow\longrightarrow\varepsilon{\otimes}L\simeq L\longrightarrow 0.

Then
P_{\varepsilon}{\otimes}L\simeq 4P_{L}\oplus Q
for some projective module
Q
such that

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow Q\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow 0.

Therefore
Q\simeq P_{L}
which proves the third isomorphism of (
24
). Notice that the same proof runs for
L{\otimes}P_{\varepsilon}
.

Remark 4.2
.

Let
[M:L]
and
[M:\varepsilon]
denote the number of composition factors isomorphic to
L
and
\varepsilon
, respectively, of a module
M
. By induction on the length of
M
, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle M{\otimes}P_{L}\simeq P_{L}{\otimes}M

\displaystyle\simeq[M:L]P_{\varepsilon}\oplus(4[M:L]+[M:\varepsilon])P_{L}\quad\mbox{and}

\displaystyle M{\otimes}P_{\varepsilon}\simeq P_{\varepsilon}{\otimes}M

\displaystyle\simeq[M:\varepsilon]P_{\varepsilon}\oplus 5[M:L]P_{L}.

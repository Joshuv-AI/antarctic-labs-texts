Abstract
. We prove that certain sequences of periodic orbits of the diagonal group in the space of lattices equidistribute. As an application we obtain new information regarding the sequence of best approximations to certain vectors with algebraic coordinates. In order to prove these results we generalize the seminal work of Eskin Mozes and Shah about the equidistribution of translates of periodic measures from the real case to the S S -arithmetic case.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Context

Let
G
be a locally compact second countable topological group and
\Gamma<G
a closed subgroup (usually assumed to be
discrete or even a lattice in
G
).
The space
X=\Gamma\backslash G
endowed with
the quotient topology is then called a homogeneous space. The group
G
and its various subgroups act on

X
from the left by
h\cdot\Gamma g=\Gamma gh^{-1}
.
In homogeneous dynamics one studies these actions and tries to understand orbits. Given a closed subgroup
H<G

the most fundamental question is to understand topological properties of
H
-orbits: Which orbits are closed, dense or bounded? What can be said about orbit closures in general? A fundamental role in the discussion is played by periodic
probability measures. Let
\mathcal{P}(X)
denote the space of Borel probability measures on
X
equipped with
the weak
∗
topology and note that the left action of
G
on
X
induces a continuous left action on
\mathcal{P}(X)
by

(g,\mu)\mapsto g\mu
where for
f\in C_{c}(X)
,
\int_{X}f(x)d(g\mu)=\int_{X}f(g^{-1}x)d\mu
.
An orbit
Hx\subset X
is called periodic if there exists
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(X)
which is
H
-invariant (i.e.
h\mu=\mu
for all

h\in H
) and such that
\mu(Hx)=1
. The measure
\mu
is unique in this case
and will be referred to
as the algebraic or periodic measure on the orbit.
It is denoted by

\mu_{Hx}
or
\mu_{\Gamma\backslash\Gamma gH}
if
x=\Gamma g
.

A significant portion of the literature in homogeneous dynamics is devoted to the following:

Problem 1.1
.

Understand the weak
∗
-accumulation points (limits)
of periodic measures. That is, what can be said about possible
limits of sequences
\mu_{H_{n}x_{n}}
where
H_{n}x_{n}
are periodic orbits.

Common phenomena related to Problem
1.1
in particular cases
are:

(1)

There are limit measures in
\mathcal{P}(X)
– this will be referred to as non-escape of mass.

(2)

All limit measures are periodic themselves – this will be referred to as rigidity.

(3)

Often, when non-escape of mass and rigidity occurs, if one imposes some
mild conditions on the sequence
H_{n}x_{n}
(usually of algebraic nature), then
all limit measures must be
G
-invariant. Since there is a unique
G
-invariant measure on
X
, this actually implies that the sequence converges to it – a phenomenon which we refer to as equidistribution.

In the general setting described above Problem
1.1
might be too hard to tackle but under various technical assumptions
on the players
(G,\Gamma,H_{n},x_{n})
quite a bit is known. A standing assumption will be that
\Gamma<G
is a lattice. We mention
two seminal works in this direction:

•

In
[
19
]
Mozes and Shah solve this problem when

are real Lie groups and the periodic

\mu_{H_{n}x_{n}}
is ergodic under the action of a one-parameter unipotent subgroup of

. It
is paramount for their consideration that

will contain unipotent elements and thus when
H_{n}
is a reductive
group - and in particular, a maximal torus -
their results do not apply.

•

When

are reductive algebraic groups defined over
\mathbb{Q}
,
\Gamma<G
is
an arithmetic lattice in

and the centralizer of

-characters,
then in the seminal papers

In applications, it is often required to extend the above results to the

-arithmetic setting. The situation in the literature regarding

1.3.
An application: Best approximations of algebraic vectors

In this subsection we wish to demonstrate the significance of Theorem
1.2
to the theory of Diophantine approximaiton. Readers whose
interest lies solely in the dynamical aspects can skip this subsection altogether.
We briefly recall some basic concepts from the theory of Diophantine approximation. Given an irrational vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
there is a natural correspondence between rational vectors
\frac{1}{q}\textbf{p}
(here
q\in\mathbb{Z},\textbf{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}
and
\gcd(\textbf{p},q)=1
) close to
v
and primitive integral
vectors
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\textbf{p}\\
q\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
approximating the line
\mathbb{R}\cdot\left(\begin{smallmatrix}v\\
1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. One defines
the sequence of best approximations to
v
to be a sequence of primitive integral vectors
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\textbf{p}_{k}\\
q_{k}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}

defined recursively as follows:
q_{1}=1
and
\textbf{p}_{1}

is (one of the potentially finitely many) integral vectors
satisfying
||\textbf{p}_{1}-v||=\min_{\textbf{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}}||\textbf{p}-v||
. For
k>1
,
q_{k}
is the minimal integer
q

for which

\min\left\{||qv-\textbf{p}||:\textbf{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}\right\}<||q_{k-1}v-\textbf{p}_{k-1}||

and
\textbf{p}_{k}
is then chosen so that

||q_{k}v-\textbf{p}_{k}||=\min\left\{||q_{k}v-\textbf{p}||:\textbf{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}\right\}.

A more geometric way to define (and understand) the vectors
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\mathbf{p}_{k}\\
q_{k}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
is as follows: One travels along the ray

\left\{t\cdot\left(\begin{smallmatrix}v\\
1\end{smallmatrix}\right):t\geq 1\right\}
and records the closest integral vectors one sees along the way; that is, one records an integral vector
at a certain (integral) time if its distance to the ray beats the distances recorded thus far. In this description the distance one uses
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is not the euclidean one but
\operatorname{d}(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}w_{1}\\
r_{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right),\left(\begin{smallmatrix}w_{2}\\
r_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right))=\max\left\{||w_{1}-w_{2}||,|r_{1}-r_{2}|\right\}
where

w_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
and
r_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
and the norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
is the euclidean norm (in fact, one can choose any norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
which is
of interest). This way, each irrational vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
yields a sequence of primitive integral vectors
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\mathbf{p}_{k}\\
q_{k}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
which
is referred to as the sequence of best approximations to the ray
\mathbb{R}\cdot\left(\begin{smallmatrix}v\\
1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
or by slight abuse of language, to
v
.

The sequence of best approximations is a central object of study in Diophantine approximation. Recently, in
[
25
]
,
results pertaining to statistical information regarding these sequences in two cases were established: For Lebesgue almost any
v

some information about the behaviour of
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\mathbf{p}_{k}\\
q_{k}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
was established and it was shown that they obey certain
universal laws. In addition, for certain vectors
v
with algebraic coordinates it was shown that the sequence

\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\mathbf{p}_{k}\\
q_{k}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
obeys statistical laws which are different from the universal ones. As an application of Theorem
1.2
we
show that if
\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)
is a totally real field of degree
d
over
\mathbb{Q}
and if
m\neq 0,\pm{1}
is an integer then
the statistical laws corresponding to
v_{n}=(m^{n}\alpha,m^{2n}\alpha^{2},\dots,m^{(d-1)n}\alpha^{d-1})
approach the universal statistical law
as
n\to\infty
. To be more precise we need to define the objects pertaining the random
variables which obey these statistical laws.

To each best approximation vector
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\textbf{p}_{k}\\
q_{k}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
we associate a triple

(\Lambda_{k},w_{k},\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\textbf{p}_{k}\\
q_{k}\end{smallmatrix}\right))\in X_{d-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\hat{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}

,
and

is the pro-finite completion of
\mathbb{Z}
.
This correspondence is defined in the following way: The lattice
\Lambda_{k}
is referred to as the
directional lattice

of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
in direction of the
k
’th best approximation
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\textbf{p}_{k}\\
q_{k}\end{smallmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{k}

\displaystyle\overset{\operatorname{def}}{=}q_{k}^{1/(d-1)}\pi^{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\textbf{p}_{k}\\
q_{k}\end{smallmatrix}\right)}_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n}),

where

The vector

\displaystyle w_{k}

This vector tells us two things: First, its direction

1.5.
About the proof of the main Theorem

We follow closely the structure of

and divide the argument into

(i)

Establish non-escape of mass.

(ii)

Establish algebraicity of limit measures.

We prove non-escape

In order to establish

To establish part (ii) we follow

2.
Growth rates of functions

In this section, we study
(C,\alpha)
-good functions in local fields. The notion of
(C,\alpha)
-good functions is introduced in
[
13
]
. One may read
[
15
,
8
]
for more details. Here we use the definition in
[
14
]
for local fields. The goal in this section is Proposition
2.2
, which shows that certain functions are
(C,\alpha)
-good. At the end of this section, we prove a weak analogue of the intermediate value theorem for
(C,\alpha)
-good functions in local fields (Proposition
2.3
) which will be important in the discussion in §
4
.

Let
k=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
(
p=
prime or
\infty
) with the
p
-adic valuation
|\cdot|_{p}
. Let
F
be a finite extension of
k
. Then there exists a unique extension
|\cdot|_{F}
of
|\cdot|_{p}
on
F
such that for any
x\in F

|x|_{F}=|N_{F/k}(x)|_{p}^{\frac{1}{[F:k]}}

where
N_{F/k}(x)
is the norm of
x
. Equipped with
|\cdot|_{F}
,
F
is a complete field. Note that for any extensions
k\subset F\subset L
, we have
N_{F/k}\circ N_{L/F}=N_{L/k}
and
[L:F][F:k]=[L:k].
Hence one can take the limit over all the finite extensions of
k
and get a well-defined norm
|\cdot|_{\bar{k}}
on the algebraic closure
\overline{k}
of
k
. In the following, we will use the notation
|\cdot|_{F}
for the norm on any finite extension
F
of
k=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
obtained in this manner. We will write
\mathcal{O}_{F}:=\{x\in F:|x|_{F}\leq 1\}.

Let
F
be a complete field with a norm
|\cdot|
,
X
a metric space,
\mu
a Borel measure on
X
and
U
a subset of
X
. Recall
[
14
]
that a continuous function
f:U\to F
is called
(C,\alpha)
-good on
U
with respect to
\mu
if for any open ball
B\subset U
centered in
\operatorname{supp}\mu
, we have

\mu(\{x\in B:|f(x)|<\epsilon\})\leq C\left(\frac{\epsilon}{\|f\|_{\mu,B}}\right)^{\alpha}\mu(B)

for any
\epsilon>0
. Here

\|f\|_{\mu,B}=\sup\{|f(x)|:x\in B\cap\operatorname{supp}(\mu)\}.

In the sequel, we will study the case when
X
is a product of local fields and the measure
\mu
is the Haar measure on
X
.

Lemma 2.1

(C.f.
[
8
, Lemma 2.4]
)
.

Let
F
be a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Then there exists
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}
such that the determinant of the matrix generated by
s_{i}^{k}e^{\lambda_{j}s_{i}}

(i=1,2,...,n^{2},j=1,2,...,n,k=0,1,2,...,n-1)

\left|\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}e^{\lambda_{1}s_{1}}&e^{\lambda_{1}s_{2}}&\cdots&e^{\lambda_{1}s_{n^{2}}}\\
s_{1}e^{\lambda_{1}s_{1}}&s_{2}e^{\lambda_{1}s_{2}}&\cdots&s_{n^{2}}e^{\lambda_{1}s_{n^{2}}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
s_{1}^{n-1}e^{\lambda_{1}s_{1}}&s_{2}^{n-1}e^{\lambda_{1}s_{2}}&\cdots&s_{n^{2}}^{n-1}e^{\lambda_{1}s_{n^{2}}}\\
e^{\lambda_{2}s_{1}}&e^{\lambda_{2}s_{2}}&\cdots&e^{\lambda_{2}s_{n^{2}}}\\
s_{1}e^{\lambda_{2}s_{1}}&s_{2}e^{\lambda_{2}s_{2}}&\cdots&s_{n^{2}}e^{\lambda_{2}s_{n^{2}}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
s_{1}^{n-1}e^{\lambda_{n}s_{1}}&s_{2}^{n-1}e^{\lambda_{n}s_{2}}&\cdots&s_{n^{2}}^{n-1}e^{\lambda_{n}s_{n^{2}}}\end{array}\right|=\prod_{i<j}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{m_{i,j}}\prod_{i<j}(s_{i}-s_{j})\phi(\bm{\lambda},\bm{s})

where
m_{i,j}\in\mathbb{N}
,
\phi(\bm{\lambda},\bm{s})
is an analytic function in
\bm{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{n})\in(p^{\gamma}\mathcal{O}_{F})^{n}
and
\bm{s}=(s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{n^{2}})\in(p^{\gamma}\mathcal{O}_{F})^{n^{2}}
, and for any
\bm{\lambda}\in(p^{\gamma}\mathcal{O}_{F})^{n}
, we have
\phi(\bm{\lambda},\bm{s})\neq 0
.

Proof.

This lemma is proved in
[
8
, Lemma 2.4]
for
F=\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{R}
. Here we give a proof for the
p
-adic case. Indeed, if we expand the determinant and the analytic function
\phi(\bm{\lambda},\bm{s})
as power series of
s_{1},s_{2},...,s_{n^{2}}
and
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},...,\lambda_{n}
over
\mathbb{R}
, the formula proved in
[
8
, Lemma 2.4]
holds as an identity of power series with rational coefficients. By examining the ranges of convergence of power series
s_{i}^{k}e^{\lambda_{j}s_{i}}

(1\leq i\leq n^{2},\;1\leq j\leq n,\;0\leq k\leq n-1)
in the
p
-adic case, one concludes that the formula holds for
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},...,\lambda_{n}
and
s_{1},s_{2},...,s_{n^{2}}
in small neighborhoods of
0
in
\mathcal{O}_{F}
. This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
F
be a finite field extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

(1)

If
p
is Archimedean, then there exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that for any
c_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
, any
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{C}

(1\leq i\leq n)
and any interval
I
in
\mathbb{R}
of length at most
\delta_{0}>0
, the function
f:I\to\mathbb{C}
defined by

f(s)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}c_{i,l}s^{l}e^{\lambda_{i}s},\quad s\in\mathbb{Z}_{p},

is
(C,\alpha)
-good for some
C,\alpha>0
depending only on
\delta_{0}>0
and
n
. The measure on
I
is chosen to be the Lebesgue measure.

(2)

If
p
is non-Archimedean, then there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any
c_{i}\in F
and any distinct
\lambda_{i}\in p^{k}\mathcal{O}_{F}

(1\leq i\leq n)
, the function
f:\mathbb{Z}_{p}\to F
defined by

f(s)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}c_{i,l}s^{l}e^{\lambda_{i}s},\quad s\in\mathbb{Z}_{p},

is
(C,\alpha)
-good for some
C,\alpha>0
depending only on
F
and
n
. Here we choose the measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
to be the Haar measure on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with
\mu(\mathbb{Z}_{p})=1
.

Proof.

The case for the Archimedean place
p=\infty
is essentially proved in
[
8
, Corollary 2.10]
. Here we give a proof for non-Archimedean places. Denote by

d(s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{n^{2}}):=\left|\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}e^{\lambda_{1}s_{1}}&e^{\lambda_{1}s_{2}}&\cdots&e^{\lambda_{1}s_{n^{2}}}\\
s_{1}e^{\lambda_{1}s_{1}}&s_{2}e^{\lambda_{1}s_{2}}&\cdots&s_{n^{2}}e^{\lambda_{1}s_{n^{2}}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
s_{1}^{n-1}e^{\lambda_{1}s_{1}}&s_{2}^{n-1}e^{\lambda_{1}s_{2}}&\cdots&s_{n^{2}}^{n-1}e^{\lambda_{1}s_{n^{2}}}\\
e^{\lambda_{2}s_{1}}&e^{\lambda_{2}s_{2}}&\cdots&e^{\lambda_{2}s_{n^{2}}}\\
s_{1}e^{\lambda_{2}s_{1}}&s_{2}e^{\lambda_{2}s_{2}}&\cdots&s_{n^{2}}e^{\lambda_{2}s_{n^{2}}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
s_{1}^{n-1}e^{\lambda_{n}s_{1}}&s_{2}^{n-1}e^{\lambda_{n}s_{2}}&\cdots&s_{n^{2}}^{n-1}e^{\lambda_{n}s_{n^{2}}}\end{array}\right|.

Let
B
be an open ball in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and define

U_{B,f,\epsilon}:=\{s\in B:|f(s)|_{F}<\epsilon\}.

We may assume
\mu(U_{B,f,\epsilon})>0
. By computing the volume of
p
-adic open balls, one can inductively find
n^{2}
points
s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{n^{2}}\in U_{B,f,\epsilon}
such that for any
1\leq i\neq j\leq n^{2}

|s_{i}-s_{j}|_{p}\geq\frac{\mu(U_{B,f,\epsilon})}{n^{2}}.

Now by interpolation formula, we have

f(s)=\sum_{j=1}^{n^{2}}f(s_{j})\frac{d(s_{1},\dots,s_{j-1},s,s_{j+1}\dots,s_{n^{2}})}{d(s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{n^{2}})},

and by Lemma
2.1

f(s)=\sum_{j=1}^{n^{2}}f(s_{j})\frac{\prod_{i\neq j}(s-s_{i})\phi(\bm{\lambda},s_{1},\dots,s,\dots,s_{n^{2}})}{\prod_{i\neq j}(s_{j}-s_{i})\phi(\bm{\lambda},s_{1},\dots,s_{n^{2}})}

for some analytic function
\phi
. Here
\phi(\bm{\lambda},\bm{s})\neq 0
for
\bm{\lambda}
in a small neighborhood
(p^{l}\mathcal{O}_{F})^{n}
of
\bm{0}
. This implies that for any
s\in B

\displaystyle|f(s)|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{1\leq j\leq n^{2}}\left\{\left|f(s_{j})\frac{\prod_{i\neq j}(s-s_{i})\phi(\bm{\lambda},s_{1},\dots,s,\dots,s_{n^{2}})}{\prod_{i\neq j}(s_{j}-s_{i})\phi(\bm{\lambda},s_{1},\dots,s_{n^{2}})}\right|_{F}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon\frac{\mu(B)^{n^{2}-1}}{(\mu(U_{B,f,\epsilon})/n^{2})^{n^{2}-1}}

for some constant
C>0
depending only on
n
and
\phi
. Hence

\mu(U_{B,f,\epsilon})\leq n^{2}C^{\frac{1}{n^{2}-1}}\left(\frac{\epsilon}{\sup_{s\in B}|f(s)|_{F}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n^{2}-1}}\mu(B).

We finish the proof of the proposition by letting
\alpha=1/(n^{2}-1)
and replacing
n^{2}C^{\frac{1}{n^{2}-1}}
by
C
.
∎

We conclude this section by proving the following proposition, which is a weak analogue of the intermediate value theorem in the
p
-adic case.

Proposition 2.3

(Weak intermediate value theorem)
.

Let
F
be a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with norm
|\cdot|_{F}
and
f:\mathbb{Z}_{p}\to F
be a continuous
(C,\alpha)
-good function. Then there exists a constant
C^{\prime}>0
depending only on
C
,
\alpha
and
p
such that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\sup_{s\in p^{n+1}\mathbb{Z}_{p}}|f(s)|_{F}\geq\sup_{s\in p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}}|f(s)|_{F}/C^{\prime}.

In particular, if
f(0)=0
and
|f(u)|_{F}\geq\rho
for some
u\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and
\rho>0
, then one can find
v\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
such that
\rho/C^{\prime}\leq|f(v)|_{F}<\rho.

Proof.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
S_{n}:=\sup_{s\in p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}}|f(s)|_{F}.
By the
(C,\alpha)
-good property of
f
, for any
\epsilon>0

\mu(\{s\in p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}:|f(s)|_{F}<\epsilon\})\leq C\left(\frac{\epsilon}{S_{n}}\right)^{\alpha}\mu(p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}).

Let

\epsilon_{n}=S_{n}\left(\frac{\mu(p^{n+1}\mathbb{Z}_{p})}{2C\mu(p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p})}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}=\frac{S_{n}}{(2Cp)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}

and we have

\mu(\{s\in p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}:|f(s)|_{F}<\epsilon_{n}\})\leq\frac{1}{2}\mu(p^{n+1}\mathbb{Z}_{p}).

This implies that there exists
v\in p^{n+1}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
such that

|f(v)|_{F}\geq\epsilon_{n}=\frac{S_{n}}{(2Cp)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}.

We finish the proof of the first statement by taking
C^{\prime}=(2Cp)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
.

For the second statement, by continuity of
f
, one can find
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\sup_{s\in p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}}|f(s)|_{p}\geq\rho\text{ and }\sup_{s\in p^{n+1}\mathbb{Z}_{p}}|f(s)|_{p}<\rho.

Now we can apply the first statement and obtain

\sup_{s\in p^{n+1}\mathbb{Z}_{p}}|f(s)|_{F}\geq\sup_{s\in p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}}|f(s)|_{F}/C^{\prime}\geq\rho/C^{\prime}.

This implies that there exists
v\in p^{n+1}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
such that
\rho/C^{\prime}\leq|f(v)|_{F}<\rho.

∎

As a corollary, we deduce the following result.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
F
be a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with norm
|\cdot|_{F}
. Let
C,\alpha>0
and
\{f_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
a sequence of continuous
(C,\alpha)
-good function from
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
to
F
. Suppose
\sup_{s\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}}|f_{i}(s)|_{F}\to\infty
as
i\to\infty.
Then there is a sequence
\{s_{i}\}
in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
such that
s_{i}\to 0\text{ and }f_{i}(s_{i})\to\infty.

Proof.

Let
C^{\prime}
be the constant as in Proposition
2.3
. Since
\lim_{i\to\infty}\sup_{s\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}}|f_{i}(s)|_{F}=\infty,
one can find a sequence
\{k_{i}\}
in
\mathbb{N}
such that

k_{i}\to\infty\text{ and }\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{1}{C^{\prime k_{i}}}\sup_{s\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}}|f_{i}(s)|_{F}=\infty.

By Proposition
2.3
, for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\sup_{s\in p^{k_{i}}\mathbb{Z}_{p}}|f(s)|_{F}\geq\sup_{s\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}}|f(s)|_{F}/C^{\prime k_{i}}.

Hence, for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, there is
s_{i}\in p^{k_{i}}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
such that

|f(s_{i})|_{F}\geq\sup_{s\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}}|f(s)|_{F}/(2C^{\prime k_{i}}).

Then the sequence
\{s_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies our requirement.
∎

3.

In this section, we study the non-escape of mass of
S
-arithmetic periodic orbits. The arguments in this section will rely on the results in
[
13
,
14
,
26
]
. Since
\mathbf{G}
is a reductive
\mathbb{Q}
-group without non-trivial

-characters, we can embed
\mathbf{G}
into
\operatorname{\mathbf{SL}}_{n}
for some

3.1.
(C,\alpha)
-good maps and Nondivergence

Let
X
be a metric space with a locally finite measure
\mu
and
S
a finite set of places of
\mathbb{Q}
. A map

\phi:(X,\mu)\to\operatorname{\mathbf{SL}}_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}),\quad x\in X\mapsto\phi(x)=(\phi_{p}(x))_{p\in S}\in\mathbf{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}),

where
\phi_{p}(x)\in\operatorname{\mathbf{SL}}_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
(
p\in S
), is called
(C,\alpha)
-good if there exist constants
C
and
\alpha>0
such that for every place
p\in S
, each entry of
\phi_{p}
is a
(C,\alpha)
-good function from
X
to
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.

For any
p
in the finite set
S
of places of
\mathbb{Q}
, let
\operatorname{Lie}(\operatorname{\mathbf{SL}}_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}))
denote the Lie algebra of
\operatorname{\mathbf{SL}}_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, and let
\exp_{p}
be the associated exponential map which is defined in a neighborhood of identity in
\operatorname{Lie}(\operatorname{\mathbf{SL}}_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}))
. We write
\exp_{S}
for the product map

(\exp_{p})_{p\in S}:\prod_{p\in S}\operatorname{Lie}(\operatorname{\mathbf{SL}}_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}))\to\prod_{p\in S}\operatorname{\mathbf{SL}}_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})

and
\log_{S}
for the inverse of
\exp_{S}
, whenever these maps are defined. For any set
W
in
\prod_{p\in S}\operatorname{\mathbf{SL}}_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, we denote by
\log_{S}(W)
the image of
W
under the map
\log_{S}
. We write
\operatorname{Lie}(F)
for the Lie algebra of a (
p
-adic) Lie group
F
.

Let
p\in S
and
\{X_{1}^{p},X_{2}^{p},...,X_{l}^{p}\}
a basis of the Lie algebra
\operatorname{Lie}(\mathbf{H}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}))
in a small neighborhood of
0
where the exponential map
\exp_{p}
is defined. By Jordan decomposition, we can write
X_{i}^{p}=Y_{i}^{p}+Z_{i}^{p}
where
Y_{i}^{p}
is semisimple,
Z_{i}^{p}
is nilpotent and
Y_{i}^{p}
and
Z_{i}^{p}
commute. Then by Proposition
2.2
, the map
s\mapsto\exp_{p}(sX_{i}^{p})=\exp_{p}(sY_{i}^{p})\exp_{p}(sZ_{i}^{p})

(s\in\mathbb{Z}_{p})
is
(C,\alpha)
-good for some
C
and
\alpha>0
. Consequently, by
[
14
, Corollary 3.3]
, the map

\Phi_{p}:(s_{1},s_{2},...,s_{l})\mapsto\exp_{p}(s_{1}X_{1}^{p})\exp_{p}(s_{2}X_{2}^{p})\cdots\exp_{p}(s_{l}X_{l}^{p})

is
(C,\alpha)
-good. Since the map
\Phi_{p}
is a local diffeomorphism from a neighborhood of
0
in
\operatorname{Lie}(\mathbf{H}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}))
to
\mathbf{H}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, the Lebesgue measure on a neighborhood of
0
in
\operatorname{Lie}(\mathbf{H}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}))
maps to a measure on
\mathbf{H}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
which is absolutely continuous to the Haar measure on
\mathbf{H}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
.

Now we introduce the notation in
[
14
]
. Let
\mathbb{Q}_{S}^{n}
be the
\mathbb{Q}_{S}
-module
\prod_{p\in S}\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}=\{(x_{p})_{p\in S}:x_{p}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}\}.
Denote by
\mathfrak{M}(\mathbb{Q}_{S},\mathcal{O}_{S},n)
the space of discrete
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-modules in
\mathbb{Q}_{S}^{n}
, which can be identified as

\left\{g^{-1}\Delta:g\in\operatorname{\mathbf{GL}}_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}),\Delta\text{ is a $\mathcal{O}_{S}$-submodule in }\mathcal{O}_{S}^{n}\right\}.

Note that any discrete
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-module in
\mathbb{Q}_{S}^{n}
is a free
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-module. We write
\Omega_{S,n}
for the space of lattices in
\mathbb{Q}_{S}^{n}
defined by

\Omega_{S,n}:=\left\{g^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{S}^{n}:g\in\operatorname{\mathbf{GL}}_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{S})\right\}\cong\operatorname{\mathbf{GL}}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{S})\backslash\operatorname{\mathbf{GL}}_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}).

For any
v\in S
, the valuation
|\cdot|_{v}
induces a norm
\|\cdot\|_{v}
on
\mathbb{Q}_{v}^{n}
. The metric on
\mathbb{Q}_{S}^{n}
, which is induced by the metrics
\|\cdot\|_{v}

(v\in S)
as a product metric, will be denoted by
\|\cdot\|
. The content
c(x)
of
x=(x_{v})_{v\in S}\in\mathbb{Q}_{S}^{n}=\prod_{p\in S}\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{n}
is defined to be the product of
\|x_{v}\|_{v}

(v\in S)
, and will be used in §
3.2
.

Let

\operatorname{\mathbf{GL}}_{n}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{S})=\{x=(x_{p})_{p\in S}\in\operatorname{\mathbf{GL}}_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}):\prod_{p}|\det(x_{p})|_{p}=1\}.

Define the space of unimodular lattices in
\mathbb{Q}_{S}^{n}
by

\Omega_{S,n}^{1}:=\left\{\Delta\in\Omega_{S,n}:\operatorname{cov}(\Delta)=1\right\}\cong\operatorname{\mathbf{GL}}_{n}^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{S})\backslash\operatorname{\mathbf{GL}}_{n}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}).

Here we refer to
[
14
]
for the definition of the covolume
\operatorname{cov}(\Delta)
of a discrete
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-module
\Delta
in
\mathbb{Q}_{S}^{n}
.

As
\mathbf{G}
is embedded into
\operatorname{\mathbf{SL}}_{n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

X_{S}=\mathbf{G}(\mathcal{O}_{S})\backslash\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{S})\subset\operatorname{\mathbf{GL}}_{n}^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{S})\backslash\operatorname{\mathbf{GL}}_{n}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{S})

and
X_{S}
can be identified with a subspace of
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-modules of covolume one in
\mathbb{Q}_{S}^{n}
. Note that the embedding above is a proper map.

In the following, we prove the non-divergence of the orbits

for

, where

is a subgroup of finite index in

. We take a neighborhood of identity

in

(

(

) and

is a subgroup of
\mathbf{H}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
(
\forall p\in S_{f}
). For convenience, in the proof of Theorem

(

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathbf{G}
be a reductive algebraic group defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, which does not admit nontrivial
\mathbb{Q}
-characters. Let
\mathbf{H}
be a reductive
\mathbb{Q}

\mathbb{Q}
-characters. Let

be a finite set of places of
\mathbb{Q}
containing the Archimedean place,
H
a subgroup of finite index in
\mathbf{H}(\mathbb{Q}_{S})
and

. Assume that there exists a compact subset
K_{0}
in

such that for any

\Gamma_{S}\backslash\Gamma_{S}U^{*}h^{-1}\cap K_{0}\neq\emptyset.

Then for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a compact subset

\mu_{\Gamma_{S}\backslash\Gamma_{S}Hg^{-1}}(K)\geq 1-\epsilon.

Proof.

Let
\Omega
be a fundamental domain of
\Gamma_{S}\backslash\Gamma_{S}H
in
H
,
\mu_{H}
the Haar measure on
H
, and let
\delta_{0}
be as in Proposition

We can construct a finite open cover

\{B_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}=\{U^{*}h_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}

Theorem 3.2
.

in

We know that

contains a maximal torus in

of

.

There exists a compact subset

in
\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{S})

In order to prove Theorem

Proposition 3.4
.

There exists a subset

of
\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
such that

(1)

\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})=Y_{p}\mathbf{T}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})

(2)

For any linear representation of
\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
on a vector space
V
over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}

such that

\sup_{t\in\Omega}\|yt.v\|\geq c\|v\|,\quad\forall v\in V,\quad\forall y\in Y_{p}.

Proof.

The case

is essentially proved in
[
8
, Proposition 4.4]
. Here we give a proof for the
p
-adic case. Let
F
be a Galois extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
such that
\mathbf{T}
is
F
-split. By Iwasawa decomposition, there exists a compact subgroup
K_{F}
of
\mathbf{G}(F)
and a unipotent subgroup
U_{F}
of
\mathbf{G}(F)
such that

G(F)=K_{F}U_{F}\mathbf{T}(F).

Let
\{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},...,\sigma_{n}\}=\textup{Gal}(F/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and define

S=\{x\in\mathbf{T}(F):\sigma_{1}(x)\sigma_{2}(x)...\sigma_{n}(x)=e\}.

We prove that
S\mathbf{T}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is a subgroup of finite index in
\mathbf{T}(F)
. We know that
\mathbf{T}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})^{n}
is a subgroup of finite index in
\mathbf{T}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. Let

E=\{x\in\mathbf{T}(F):\sigma_{1}(x)\sigma_{2}(x)\cdots\sigma_{n}(x)\in\mathbf{T}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})^{n}\}.

Then
E
is a subgroup of finite index in
\mathbf{T}(F)
. Now for any

, there exists

such that

\sigma_{1}(x)\sigma_{2}(x)\cdots\sigma_{n}(x)=w^{n}

and hence

,

. This implies that

and
S\mathbf{T}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is a subgroup of finite index in
\mathbf{T}(F)

Let
\Lambda
be a finite set of representatives of
\mathbf{T}(F)/(S\mathbf{T}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}))
in
\mathbf{T}(F)
. Since
\Lambda
normalizes

\mathbf{G}(F)=K_{F}U_{F}\Lambda S\mathbf{T}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})=(K_{F}\Lambda)U_{F}S\mathbf{T}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})=KU_{F}S\mathbf{T}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})

where

is the compact subset
K_{F}\Lambda
in
\mathbf{G}(F)
. Let
Y_{p}=(KU_{F}S)\cap\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. Then
\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})=Y_{p}\mathbf{T}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})

We prove that the subset
Y_{p}
satisfies the second claim in the proposition. Let
\rho
be a representation of
\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
on a space
V
over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, and we extend it to a presentation of
\mathbf{G}(F)
over
F
. Let
y=kus\in Y_{p}
for

,

and

. Then assuming Lemmas
3.5
and
3.6
below, we have for any

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\Omega}\|yt.v\|

\displaystyle=\sup_{t\in\Omega}\|kust.v\|\geq c_{1}\sup_{t\in\Omega}\|ut.(s.v)\|\geq c_{2}\sup_{t\in\Omega}\|(t^{-1}ut).(s.v)\|

\displaystyle\geq c_{2}c_{3}\|s.v\|\geq c_{2}c_{3}c_{4}\|v\|.

Hence we only need to prove the following lemmas.

Lemma 3.5
.

There exists a constant
c_{3}>0
such that for any
v\in V

\sup_{t\in\Omega}\|(t^{-1}ut).v\|\geq c_{3}\|v\|

Proof.

Let
\Phi
be the root system of
(\mathbf{G}(F),\mathbf{T}(F))
and
\Phi_{+}
the set of positive roots. Let
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n}
be the positive simple roots of
\mathbf{T}(F)
in
\Phi_{+}

which maps

to

f_{\Phi}(x):=(\alpha_{1}(x),\alpha_{2}(x),\dots,\alpha_{n}(x)).

Write
u=\exp(\overline{u})
where

\overline{u}=\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi_{+}}u_{\alpha}\in\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi_{+}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}.

Note that for
t\in\Omega
,
t^{-1}ut.v=\exp(\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi_{+}}\alpha(t)u_{\alpha}).
Since the positive roots are generated by simple roots, it follows that the set
\{t^{-1}ut:t\in\Omega\}
is the image of the map
f
defined by

(\alpha_{1}(t),\alpha_{2}(t),\dots,\alpha_{n}(t))\mapsto\exp(\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi_{+}}\alpha(t)u_{\alpha}).

We conclude that
\{t^{-1}ut:t\in\Omega\}
is the image of a polynomial map
f
defined on
f_{\Phi}(\Omega)
which maps
s=(s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{n})\in f_{\Phi}(\Omega)
to

f(s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{n}):=\exp(\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi_{+}}\alpha(s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{n})u_{\alpha})

where
\alpha(s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{n})
is a polynomial in
s_{1},s_{2},...,s_{n}
without constant term.

To finish the proof of the lemma, it is enough to prove that there exists a constant
c_{3}>0
such that

\sup_{s\in f_{\Phi}(\Omega)}\|f(s).v\|\geq c_{3}\|v\|.

Since
f
is a polynomial map on each variable

with a uniformly bounded degree, by
[
14
, Corollary 3.3]
,
f
is
(C,\alpha)
-good for some

and
\alpha
. We also have

, so

Now choose a bounded region
D
in
F^{n}
containing
f_{\Phi}(\Omega)
, and by the
(C,\alpha)
-good property, there are constants
c_{3}>0
depending only on
C
,
\alpha
and

such that

\mu_{F^{n}}(\{s\in D:\|f(s).v\|\leq c_{3}\|v\|\})\leq\frac{1}{2}\mu_{F^{n}}(f_{\Phi}(\Omega))

where

denotes the Haar measure on

. Hence there is

such that
\|f(s).v\|\geq c_{3}\|v\|.
This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

There exists a constant
c_{4}>0
such that for any
v\in V

\|s.v\|\geq c_{4}\|v\|.

Proof.

We write the weight space decomposition for

over
F
as

V=\sum_{\alpha}V_{\alpha}

and for any

we write

. Then

and

\sigma_{i}(s.v)=\sigma_{i}(s).v=\sum_{\alpha}\alpha(\sigma_{i}(s))v_{\alpha}.

We know that
\|\cdot\|
and
\|\sigma_{i}(\cdot)\|
are equivalent norms on the vector space
V_{F}
over
F
, and for any
v\in V_{F}
, there exists a constant
\kappa>0
such that

\frac{1}{\kappa}\|v\|\leq\|\sigma_{i}(v)\|\leq\kappa\|v\|

\frac{1}{\kappa}\max_{\alpha}\|v_{\alpha}\|\leq\|v\|\leq\kappa\max_{\alpha}\|v_{\alpha}\|.

For any

, let
v_{\beta}=\max\|v_{\alpha}\|
. Then

\|s.v\|\geq\frac{1}{\kappa}\|\sigma_{i}(s).v\|\geq\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}}\|\beta(\sigma_{i}(s)).v_{\beta}\|

This implies that
\|s.v\|\geq c_{4}\|v\|
for some constant

.
∎

This completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

with

.

Proof.

Since
\{\operatorname{Ad}\gamma_{i}(H_{p})\}

in

with

. By Proposition

and

f_{i}(s)=\|\gamma_{i}(\exp(sX_{i}))\gamma_{i}^{-1}-e_{p}\|_{p},\quad s\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}

is a

-good function with some
C,\alpha>0
uniformly for any

. Our choices of

’s then imply
f_{i}(1)\to\infty
as
i\to\infty
. By Corollary
2.4
we can find

so that

s_{i}X_{i}\to 0,\quad f_{i}(s_{i})\to\infty.

Therefore, by replacing
X_{i}
with
s_{i}X_{i}
, we may assume
X_{i}\to 0
. Now for any
N>0
, one can find
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
C^{\prime k}\geq N
where
C^{\prime}
is the constant in Proposition
2.3
. Since
f_{i}(0)=0
and
f_{i}(s_{i})\to\infty
as

, we apply Proposition
2.3
with
\rho=C^{\prime k+1}
, and one can find

. Since

.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

A closed subgroup of
\operatorname{\mathbf{SL}}_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
containing arbitrarily large unipotent elements has a one-parameter unipotent subgroup.

Proof.

The exponential map
\exp(X)
can be defined for nilpotent elements
X
in the Lie algebra of
\operatorname{\mathbf{SL}}_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, and the
\log
function
\log(u)
can be defined for unipotent elements
u
in
\operatorname{\mathbf{SL}}_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. Moreover, the sets of nilpotent elements and of unipotent elements are both closed. Now let
\{u_{i}\}
be a sequence of unipotent elements in a closed subgroup
M
with
u_{i}\to\infty
. We take
X_{i}=\log u_{i}
and we have
X_{i}\to\infty
. By passing to a subsequence,
|X_{i}|_{p}X_{i}
converges to a nilpotent element
X
with
|X|_{p}=1
. Now we prove that the one-parameter unipotent subgroup

s\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}\mapsto\exp(sX)

is contained in
M
.

For
s\neq 0
and
i
sufficiently large, since
\overline{\mathbb{Z}}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, one can find
m_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

\left|m_{i}-s|X_{i}|_{p}\right|_{p}\leq\frac{1}{i|X_{i}|_{p}}.

This implies that

m_{i}X_{i}=\left(m_{i}-s|X_{i}|_{p}\right)X_{i}+s|X_{i}|_{p}X_{i}\to sX.

Since
\exp(m_{i}X_{i})=u_{i}^{m_{i}}\in M
and
M
is closed, we have
\exp(sX)\in M
. This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

is closed and of finite volume, by Lemmas
6.6
and
6.7
, we conclude that
\mu_{\Gamma_{\infty}h_{i}\mathbf{T}(\mathbb{R})^{0}g_{i}^{-1}}
converges to the
x_{\infty}^{-1}(\gamma^{-1}\mathbf{M}(\mathbb{R})^{0}\gamma)x_{\infty}
-invariant probability measure
\mu_{\Gamma_{\infty}(\gamma^{-1}\mathbf{M}(\mathbb{R})^{0}\gamma)x_{\infty}}
. This finishes the proof of Theorem
1.8
.
∎

The Fox-Hatcher cycle and a Vassiliev invariant of order three Thanks: The 2nd author is partially supported by JSPS KAKENHI Grant Number 20K03608.
Saki Kanou and Keiichi Sakai

Abstract
. We show that the integration of a 1-cocycle I ⁡ ( X ) I(X) of the space of long knots in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} over the Fox-Hatcher 1-cycles gives rise to a Vassiliev invariant of order exactly three. This result can be seen as a continuation of the previous work of the second named author [ 14 ] , proving that the integration of I ⁡ ( X ) I(X) over the Gramain 1-cycles is the Casson invariant , the unique nontrivial Vassiliev invariant of order two (up to scalar multiplications). The result in the present paper is also analogous to part of Mortier’s result [ 11 ] . Our result differs from, but is motivated by, Mortier’s one in that the 1-cocycle I ⁡ ( X ) I(X) is given by the configuration space integrals associated with graphs while Mortier’s cocycle is obtained in a combinatorial way.



1.
Introduction

The spaces of smooth embeddings of manifolds are receiving a lot of attention in topology, on the ground that various important methods in algebraic and geometric topology are being applied to the spaces.
In this paper we study the space of
(framed) long knots
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

Definition 1.1
.

A
long knot
is an embedding
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{1}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
satisfying
f(x)=(x,0,0)
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{1}
with
\left\lvert{x}\right\rvert\geq 1
. A
framed long knot
is a smooth map
\widetilde{f}=(f,w)\colon\mathbb{R}^{1}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathrm{SO}(3)
such that
f
is a long knot, the first column of
w(x)\in\mathrm{SO}(3)
is equal to
f^{\prime}(x)/\left\lvert{f^{\prime}(x)}\right\rvert
and
w(x)
is the identity matrix for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{1}
with
\left\lvert{x}\right\rvert\geq 1
.
The space of all long knots (respectively framed long knots) is denoted by
\mathcal{K}
(respectively
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}
).

The recent studies of
\mathcal{K}
(and its high dimensional analogues) are revealing relations between the topological nature of
\mathcal{K}
and the
Vassiliev invariants
(see for example
[
9
]
) for knots and links.
In
[
14
]
the second named author has constructed a de Rham 1-cocycle
I(X)
of
\mathcal{K}
(see §
3
), by means of the integrations over configuration spaces associated with graphs, and has shown that the integration of
I(X)
over the
Gramain cycles
of
\mathcal{K}
gives rise to the
Casson invariant

v_{2}
, the Vassiliev invariant of order two uniquely characterized by
v_{2}(\text{trivial knot})=0
and
v_{2}(\text{trefoil knot})=1
.
This may be seen as a real valued version of
[
16
, Theorem 2]
.
After that Mortier has given another 1-cocyle
\alpha^{1}_{3}
of
\mathcal{K}
in a combinatorial way and has shown that its evaluations over the Gramain cycles and the
Fox-Hatcher cycles

\mathit{FH}
are Vassiliev invariants of orders respectively two and three
[
11
, Theorem 4.1]
.
In
[
6
]
1-cocycles on
\mathcal{K}
are also studied in detail from a combinatorial viewpoint.

The main result in the present paper is analogous to the order three part of Mortier’s result.

Theorem 1.2
.

The integration of
I(X)
over the Fox-Hatcher cycles gives rise to a Vassiliev invariant of order three for framed long knots.
More precisely we have

(1.1)

\int_{p_{*}\mathit{FH}_{\widetilde{f}}}I(X)=6v_{3}(f)-\mathrm{lk}(\widetilde{f})v_{2}(f),

where

•

p\colon\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}\to\mathcal{K}
is the first projection and
f=p(\widetilde{f})
,

•

v_{2}
is the Casson invariant, and
v_{3}
is the Vassiliev invariant of order three characterized by the conditions

(1.2)

v_{3}(\text{trivial knot})=0,\quad v_{3}(3_{1}^{+})=1,\quad v_{3}(3_{1}^{-})=-1

(
3_{1}^{+}
and
3_{1}^{-}
are respectively the right-handed and the left-handed trefoil knots),
and

•

\mathrm{lk}(\widetilde{f})\in\mathbb{Z}
is the

of
\widetilde{f}
(see Remark
1.3

Remark 1.3
.

The framing number
\mathrm{lk}(\widetilde{f})
is the linking number of
f=p(\widetilde{f})
and
f^{\prime}
, where
f^{\prime}
is the long knot obtained by moving

slightly into the direction of the second column of

In fact the map

is a homotopy equivalence
[
4

, and the framing number uniquely determines the framing

up to homotopy.
Thus we may regard a framed long knot as a pair
(f,w)
of
f\in\mathcal{K}
and
w\in\mathbb{Z}
.

The
1
-cocycle
I(X)
is constructed by means of the

, that was developed in
[
1
,
3
,
10
]
to describe Vassiliev invariants and was generalized in
[
5
]
to obtain a cochain map from a graph complex to

(up to some correction terms, that vanish in the cases of the spaces of long knots in high dimensional spaces).
Vassiliev invarians (
0
-cocycles of

) are obtained from trivalent graphs, while our

-cocycle

comes from non-trivalent graphs (see Figure

).
It is very interesting, although not strange, that non-trivalent graphs may also have information of Vassiliev invariants.

We note that the right hand side of (
1.1
) coincides with the formula for Mortier’s invariant of order three. We thus expect that the 1-cocycle

is cohomologous to Mortier’s
\alpha^{1}_{3}

2.
The Fox-Hatcher cycle

2.1.
The Fox-Hatcher cycle

The Fox-Hatcher cycle
was introduced in
[
7
]
, and was later studied in
[
8
]
from the viewpoint of the space of knots.
If
f=p(\widetilde{f})
is not trivial, it then gives a non-zero element of
\pi_{1}(\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\widetilde{f}})
, where
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\widetilde{f}}
is the path component of
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}
containing
\widetilde{f}
.

The Fox-Hatcher cycle
is defined as follows.
A framed long knot can be seen as a based embedding
f\colon S^{1}\hookrightarrow S^{3}
(we see
S^{3}
as in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
) together with a framing
w
, with a prescribed behavior near the basepoint.
For
t\in S^{1}
,
w(t)
is an orthonormal basis of
T_{f(t)}S^{3}
whose first vector is
f^{\prime}(t)/\left\lvert{f^{\prime}(t)}\right\rvert
.
There exists an
S^{1}
-action on the space of such embeddings defined by
(\theta\cdot(f,w))(t)\coloneqq(A(\theta)^{-1}f(t-\theta),A(\theta)^{-1}w(t-\theta))
, where
A(\theta)\in\mathrm{SO}(4)
is the matrix given by
A(\theta)=(w(\theta),f(\theta))
.
For any
\widetilde{f}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}
, this action determines a 1-cycle
\mathit{FH}_{\widetilde{f}}\colon S^{1}\to\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\widetilde{f}}
and we call it the
Fox-Hatcher cycle
.
We notice that the
S^{1}
-action looks very similar to the natural
S^{1}
-action on free loop spaces by the reparametrization, and in fact this action defines a
BV-operation
on
H_{*}(\widetilde{\mathcal{K}})

[
15
]
.

Practically it is convenient to describe
\mathit{FH}
on knot diagrams.
In this paper a framed long knot is drawn in a usual knot diagram with so-called
blackboard framing
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
D
be a knot diagram of
\widetilde{f}
with blackboard framing and
c
the “left-most” crossing, namely the crossing that we meet first when traveling from
f(-1)
along the natural orientation of
f
.
We call the transformation shown in Figure
2.1
the
Fox-Hatcher move
(FH-move for short)
on
c
.

Figure 2.1.
The Fox-Hatcher move on
c

The left-most crossing
c
disappears after the FH-move on
c
and the right-most crossing
c^{\prime}
is created.
If the arc that moves in the FH-move is the over-arc (resp. under-arc) at
c
, then after the FH-move it becomes the over-arc (resp. under-arc) at
c^{\prime}
.
We arrive the original diagram
D
after performing the FH-moves for all the other crossings
c
of
D
and the newborn crossings
c^{\prime}
.
The sequence of these FH-moves realizes
\mathit{FH}_{\widetilde{f}}
.

2.2.
FH moves and Gauss diagrams

The configuration of crossings of a knot diagram is encoded by
(linear) Gauss diagrams
.
Here we see how the FH-move on the left-most crossing changes the Gauss diagram.

Definition 2.2
.

A
(linear) Gauss diagram
is a subdivision of
\{1,2,\dots,2n\}
for some natural number
n
into a union
\bigcup_{1\leq k\leq n}\{i_{k},j_{k}\}
of
n
subsets of cardinarity
2
.

A Gauss diagram can be seen as a graph on
\mathbb{R}^{1}
with even number of vertices all of which are on
\mathbb{R}^{1}
and each vertex is joined by exactly one edge with another vertex.
Here segments in
\mathbb{R}^{1}
interposed between two vertices are not regarded as edges.
See Figure
2.2
for example.

A knot diagram and its Gauss diagram

(
[

)
.

Let
c_{1},\dots,c_{n}
be (part of the) crossings of a knot diagram of
f\in\mathcal{K}
such that each
c_{i}
corresponds to
f(p_{i})
and
f(q_{i})
, with
-1<p_{1}<\dots<p_{n}<1
and
p_{i}<q_{i}
for any

.
We say that the crossings
c_{1},\dots,c_{n}

respect

and

.
See Figure

Under the setting of Definition

.
Let

Then the new knot diagram obtained by performing the FH-move on

has crossings

.

We eventually arrive the original Gauss diagram after performing the FH-moves on all the crossings

In this way the set of all the Gauss diagrams is decomposed into the disjoint cycles.

The cocycle
I(X)

In this section we give a quick review of the construction of differential forms on

By a

we mean the oriented real line
\mathbb{R}^{1}
together with two kind of vertices, one is called
interval
and the other

, and
oriented edges
connecting them (see Figure
3.1

Figure 3.1.
An example of graphs; the i-vertices are those labeled by

The interval vertices (or i-vertices for short) are placed on the oriented line while the free vertices (or f-vertices for short) are not on the line.
The i-vertices and the f-vertices of a graph
X
are labeled by respectively the numbers
1,\dots,\mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{i}}
and
\mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{i}}+1,\dots,\mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{i}}+\mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{f}}

\mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{i}}
and
\mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{f}}
are respectively the numbers of the i-vertices and the f-vertices of

We allow graphs to have a
loop
, an edge that has exactly one i-vertex as its endpoint (see Figure
3.1

For a graph
X
, let
E_{X}
be the configuration space

(3.1)

E_{X}\coloneqq\{(f,(y_{1},\dots,y_{\mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{i}}+\mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{f}}}))\in\mathcal{K}\times\mathrm{Conf}_{\mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{i}}+\mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{f}}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\mid y_{i}=f(x_{i})\text{ for some }x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{1}\text{ for }i=1,\dots,\mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{i}}\},

where

(3.2)

\mathrm{Conf}_{k}(M)\coloneqq\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\in M^{\times k}\mid x_{i}\neq x_{j}\text{ if }i\neq j\}

is the space of
k
-point configurations on a space
M
.

To an oriented edge
\alpha
of
X
from the
i
-th vertex to the
j
-th vertex (
i\neq j
), we assign a map

(3.3)

\varphi_{\alpha}\colon E_{X}\to S^{2},\quad\varphi_{\alpha}(f,(y_{1},\dots,y_{v_{i}+v_{f}}))\coloneqq\frac{y_{j}-y_{i}}{\left\lvert{y_{j}-y_{i}}\right\rvert}.

To a loop
\alpha
at
k
-th i-vertex (
1\leq i\leq\mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{i}}
) we assign

(3.4)

\varphi_{\alpha}\colon E_{X}\to S^{2},\quad\varphi_{\alpha}(f,(y_{1},\dots,y_{v_{i}+v_{f}}))\coloneqq\frac{f^{\prime}(x_{k})}{\left\lvert{f^{\prime}(x_{k})}\right\rvert},

where
x_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{1}
satisfies
y_{k}=f(x_{k})
.

Let
\mathrm{vol}\in\Omega_{\mathit{DR}}^{2}(S^{2})
be a unit volume form of
S^{2}
that is anti-symmetric, meaning that
i^{*}\mathrm{vol}=-\mathrm{vol}
for the antipodal map

.
Define
\omega_{X}\in\Omega^{2e}_{\mathit{DR}}(E_{X})
by

(3.5)

\omega_{X}\coloneqq\bigwedge_{\text{edges }\alpha\text{ of }X}\varphi^{*}_{\alpha}(\mathrm{vol}),

where
e
is the number of edges of
X
.
The order of the edges is not important because
\deg\mathrm{vol}=2
is even.

Let
\pi_{X}\colon E_{X}\to\mathcal{K}
be the first projection.
This is a fiber bundle with fiber

(3.6)

\pi_{X}^{-1}(f)=\{y\in\mathrm{Conf}_{\mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{i}}+\mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{f}}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\mid y_{i}=f(x_{i})\text{ for some }x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{1}\text{ for }i=1,\dots,\mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{i}}\}

of dimension
\mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{i}}+3\mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{f}}
.
Integrating
\omega_{X}
along the fiber, we get

(3.7)

I(X)\coloneqq\pi_{X*}(\omega_{X})\in\Omega^{2e-\mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{i}}-3\mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{f}}}_{\mathit{DR}}(\mathcal{K}).

The integration (

dI(X)=\pi_{X*}(d\omega_{X})\pm\pi_{X*}^{\partial}(\omega)=\pm\pi_{X*}^{\partial}(\omega),

4.
Integration of
I(X)
over the Fox-Hatcher cycle

Recall that

is the map forgetting the framing of

.
For any
\widetilde{f}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}

4.1.

.

Corollary 4.2

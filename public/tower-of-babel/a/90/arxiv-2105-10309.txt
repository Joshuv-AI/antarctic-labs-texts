Foliations whose first Chern class is nef
Wenhao Ou

Abstract
. Let ℱ {\mathcal{F}} be a foliation on a projective manifold X X with − K ℱ -K_{\mathcal{F}} nef. Assume that either ℱ {\mathcal{F}} is regular, or it has a compact leaf. 1.
Introduction

From the viewpoint of minimal model program, complex projective manifolds
X
could be classified according to the numerical behavior of their canonical classes
K_{X}
. When
K_{X}\equiv 0
, Yau proved the Calabi conjecture, which confirms the existence of Kähler–Einstein metrics on
X
.
The universal cover of
X
then admits a decomposition, known as Beauville-Bogomolov decomposition (
e.g.

[
Bea83
]
), as the product of an affine space, of holomorphic symplectic manifolds, and of simple Calabi-Yau manifolds.

In a more general setting, if we assume that
-K_{X}
is nef, then Demailly-Peternell-Schneider conjectured an analogue uniformization.
The conjecture was recently settled in
[
Cao19
]
and
[
CH19
]
, based on the positivity of direct images established in
[
PT18
]
.
More precisely, the universal cover of
X
is isomorphic to the product of an affine space, of holomorphic symplectic manifolds, of simple Calabi-Yau manifolds, and of a rationally connected projective manifold with nef anticanonical class.

In
[
Cao19
]
mentioned above, the Albanese morphism
\mathrm{alb}_{X}\colon X\to\mathrm{Alb}(X)
was investigated.
When
-K_{X}
is nef, such a morphism is known to be a fibration after
[
Zha96
]
.
It was also proved that
\mathrm{alb}_{X}
is semistable in codimension one (see
[
Zha05
]
) and equidimensional (see
[
LTZZ10
]
).
In particular, the relative canonical class
K_{X/\mathrm{Alb}(X)}
is the same as
K_{{\mathcal{F}}}
, the canonical class of the foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
induced by
\mathrm{alb}_{X}
.
Since
\mathrm{Alb}(X)
is an abelian variety, the nefness of
-K_{X}
is then transposed to the nefness of
-K_{{\mathcal{F}}}
.

Under such consideration, it is natural to expect similar structural results in the context of foliations on projective manifolds.
Indeed, foliations with zero canonical class have already been broadly studied (
e.g.

[
Tou08
]
,
[
PT13
]
,
[
LPT18
]
,
[
Dru21
]
,
[
DO19
]
and
[
DPPT21
]
).
Foliations with nef anticanonical class have been previously investigated as well (
e.g.

[
Dru17
]
,
[
Dru19
]
,
[
CH19
]
and
[
CCM19
]
). Particularly, for regular foliations with semipositive anticanonical class, Druel proved in
[
Dru19
]
that one can reduce the problem to the case of foliations with zero canonical class.
An analogue statement for foliations with nef anticanonical class was conjectured by Cao and Höring.
The main objective of this paper is to prove this conjecture.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a foliation on a projective manifold
X
with
-K_{\mathcal{F}}
nef. Assume that either
{\mathcal{F}}
is regular, or it has a compact leaf.
Then there is a locally trivial fibration
f\colon X\to Y
with rationally connected fibers.
Moreover, there is a foliation
{\mathcal{G}}
on
Y
with
K_{{\mathcal{G}}}\equiv 0
such that
{\mathcal{F}}=f^{-1}{\mathcal{G}}
.

We also obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a foliation on a projective manifold
X
with
-K_{{\mathcal{F}}}
nef. Assume that
{\mathcal{F}}
has a compact leaf. Then it is regular and there is a regular foliation
{\mathcal{E}}
such that
T_{X}={\mathcal{F}}\oplus{\mathcal{E}}
.

A key ingredient for Theorem
1.1
is the following theorem, which reduces the situation to the case of algebraically integrable foliations.
It follows directly from a series of Druel’s works.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a foliation on a projective manifold
X
with nef anticanonical class
-K_{\mathcal{F}}
. Assume that either
{\mathcal{F}}
is regular or it has a compact leaf. Then the following properties hold.

(1)

The algebraic part
{\mathcal{F}}_{alg}\subseteq{\mathcal{F}}
has a compact leaf, and is induced by a dominant almost holomorphic rational map
\varphi\colon X\dashrightarrow Y
.

(2)

Let
{\mathcal{F}}_{rc}
be the foliation of the relative MRC fibration of
\varphi
. Then
K_{{\mathcal{F}}_{rc}}\equiv K_{{\mathcal{F}}_{alg}}\equiv K_{{\mathcal{F}}}
.

For algebraically integrable foliations, we will prove the following statements, which extend the results in
[
CH19
]
and
[
CCM19
]
.
Together with Theorem
1.3
, they will imply Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be an algebraically integrable foliation on a projective manifold
X
with
-K_{{\mathcal{F}}}
nef. Assume that
{\mathcal{F}}
has a compact leaf. Then there is a foliation
{\mathcal{G}}
on
X
such that
T_{X}={\mathcal{F}}\oplus{\mathcal{G}}
.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be an algebraically integrable foliation on a projective manifold
X
with
-K_{{\mathcal{F}}}
nef. Assume that
{\mathcal{F}}
has a compact leaf. Then it is induced by an equidimensional fibration
f\colon X\to Y
. If moreover
{\mathcal{F}}
is rationally connected, then
f
is a locally trivial family.

The paper is organized as follows. We will work over
\mathbb{C}
, the field of complex numbers, throughout.
In Section
2
, we will recall the basic language on foliations.
In Section
3
, we will prove several elementary results about numerically flat vector bundles.
In Section
4
, we will collect some powerful tools developed in
[
CCM19
]
.
Then we will construct an appropriate semistable reduction for a foliation with nef aniticanonical class in Section
5
.
In the end, we will finish the proofs of the theorems in Section
6
.

2.
Foliations

In this section, we gather some basic properties concerning foliations on complex algebraic varieties.
For more details on the notion of foliations, we refer to
[
AD14
, Section 3]
.

A foliation on a normal variety
X
is a coherent subsheaf
{\mathcal{F}}\subseteq T_{X}
, where
T_{X}=(\Omega_{X}^{1})^{*}
is the reflexive tangent sheaf, such that

(1)

{\mathcal{F}}
is closed under the Lie bracket,

(2)

{\mathcal{F}}
is saturated in
T_{X}
, that is, the quotient
T_{X}/{\mathcal{F}}
is torsion-free.

The canonical class
K_{{\mathcal{F}}}
of
{\mathcal{F}}
is any Weil divisor on
X
such that
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-K_{{\mathcal{F}}})\cong\det{\mathcal{F}}
.
In particular, the first Chern class
c_{1}({\mathcal{F}})
is equivalent to
-K_{\mathcal{F}}
.
Let
X^{\circ}
be the largest open subset of the smooth locus of
X
such that
{\mathcal{F}}|_{X^{\circ}}
is a subbundle of
T_{X^{\circ}}
.
The singular locus of
{\mathcal{F}}
is defined to be
X\setminus X^{\circ}
.
When
X=X^{\circ}
, we say that
{\mathcal{F}}
is a regular foliation of
X
.
A leaf of
{\mathcal{F}}
is a maximal connected and immersed holomorphic submanifold
i\colon L\hookrightarrow X^{\circ}
such that the differential map

\mathrm{d}i\colon T_{L}\to i^{*}{\mathcal{F}}

is an isomorphism.
A leaf is called algebraic if it is open in its Zariski closure in
X
.
The foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
is said to be algebraically integrable if its leaves are algebraic.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a codimension
q
foliation on an
n
-dimensional normal variety

X
.
The normal sheaf
{\mathcal{N}}_{{\mathcal{F}}}
of
{\mathcal{F}}
is the reflexive hull
(T_{X}/{\mathcal{F}})^{**}
.
The
q
-th wedge product of the inclusion
{\mathcal{N}}_{{\mathcal{F}}}^{*}\to(\Omega_{X}^{1})^{**}

gives
rise to a non-zero global section
\omega\in H^{0}(X,(\Omega^{q}_{X}\otimes\mathrm{det}\,{\mathcal{N}}_{{\mathcal{F}}})^{**}),
whose zero locus has codimension at least two in

X
.
Moreover,
\omega
is locally decomposable and integrable.
To say that
\omega
is locally decomposable means that, in a
neighborhood of a general point of
X
,
\omega
decomposes as the wedge product of
q
local
1
-forms

\omega=\omega_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{q}
.
To say that it is integrable means that for this local decomposition one has
\mathrm{d}\omega_{i}\wedge\omega=0
for every
i\in\{1,...,q\}
.

The integrability condition for
\omega
in the previous paragraph is equivalent to the condition that
{\mathcal{F}}
is closed under the Lie bracket.
Conversely, let
{\mathcal{L}}
be a reflexive sheaf of rank one on
X
, and let
\omega\in H^{0}(X,(\Omega_{X}^{q}\otimes{\mathcal{L}})^{**})
be a global section whose
zero locus has codimension at least two in
X
.
Suppose that
\omega
is locally decomposable and integrable.
Then the
kernel of the morphism
T_{X}\to(\Omega_{X}^{q-1}\otimes{\mathcal{L}})^{**}

given by the contraction with
\omega
defines a foliation of codimension
q

on
X
.
These constructions are inverse of each other.

Let
X
and
Y
be normal varieties, and let
\varphi\colon X\dashrightarrow Y
be a dominant rational map that restricts to a morphism
f^{\circ}\colon X^{\circ}\to Y^{\circ}
,
where
X^{\circ}\subseteq X
and
Y^{\circ}\subseteq Y
are smooth open dense subsets.
Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be a foliation on
Y
. Then the pullback foliation
\varphi^{-1}{\mathcal{G}}
on
X
is induced by
(\mathrm{d}f)^{-1}(f^{*}{\mathcal{G}})
on
X^{\circ}
, where

\mathrm{d}f\colon T_{X^{\circ}}\to f^{*}T_{Y^{\circ}}

is the differential map of
f
.

Assume that
{\mathcal{F}}
is an algebraically integrable foliation on a normal variety
X
.
Then there is a unique normal variety
V
in the normalization of the Chow variety of
X
, whose general points parametrize the closures of general leaves of
{\mathcal{F}}
.
Let
U
be the normalization of the universal family over
V
.
Then the natural projective fibration
U\to V
is called the family of leaves of
{\mathcal{F}}
. For more details, see for example
[
AD13
, Lemma 3.2]
.

As a consequence, a foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
is algebraically integrable if and only if there is a dominant rational map
\varphi\colon X\dashrightarrow Y
to a normal variety such that
{\mathcal{F}}=\varphi^{-1}0_{Y}
, where
0_{Y}
is the foliation by points on
Y
.
In this case, we say that
{\mathcal{F}}
is the foliation induced by
\varphi
.

For foliations induced by equidimensional fibrations, we have the following fact.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\varphi\colon X\to Y
be an equidimensional morphism between normal varieties, and
{\mathcal{F}}
the foliation induced.
Let
Y^{\prime}\to Y
be a finite dominant morphism with
Y^{\prime}
normal.
Let
X^{\prime}
be the normalization of
X\times_{Y}Y^{\prime}
. If
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}
is the foliation induced by the natural projection
X^{\prime}\to Y^{\prime}
, and if
p\colon X^{\prime}\to X
is the natural finite morphism, then
K_{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}}=p^{*}K_{\mathcal{F}}
.

Proof.

Let
D
be any prime divisor in
X
.
We only need to show that
K_{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}}=p^{*}K_{{\mathcal{F}}}
around a general point of
p^{-1}(D)
.

If
D
is not contained in the branched locus of
p
, then
p
is locally a diffeomorphism
around a general point of
p^{-1}(D)
.
Thus
K_{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}}=p^{*}K_{{\mathcal{F}}}
around a general point of
p^{-1}(D)
.

Assume that
D
is contained in the branched locus of
p
.
In this case,
D
must be
{\mathcal{F}}
-invariant by the construction of
X^{\prime}
.
We can then apply
[
Dru21
, Lemma 3.4]
to show that
K_{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}}=p^{*}K_{{\mathcal{F}}}
around a general point of
p^{-1}(D)
.
This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a foliation on a normal variety
X
. There exist a normal variety
Y
, unique up to birational equivalence, a dominant rational map with connected fibers
\varphi\colon X\dashrightarrow Y
, and a foliation
{\mathcal{G}}
on
Y
, such that the following properties hold.

(1)

{\mathcal{G}}
is purely transcendental, that is, there is no positive-dimensional algebraic subvariety through a general point of
Y
which is tangent to
{\mathcal{G}}
.

(2)

{\mathcal{F}}=\varphi^{-1}{\mathcal{G}}
.

The foliation induced by
\varphi
is called the algebraic part of
{\mathcal{F}}
.
For more details, see
[
LPT18
, Section 2.3]
.

We will need the following lemma for the proofs of the main theorems.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
{f}\colon{X}\to{Y}
be a smooth dominant morphism between complex manifolds.
Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a foliation on
{X}
whose rank is equal to
\dim{Y}
.
Let
q\colon{Y}^{\prime}\to{Y}
be a finite surjective morphism with
Y^{\prime}
smooth, and let
{f}^{\prime}\colon{X}^{\prime}\to{Y}^{\prime}
be the base change of
{f}
over
{Y}^{\prime}
.
Let
{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}={f}^{-1}{\mathcal{H}}
be the pullback foliation.
If
{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}
is regular and transverse to every fiber of
{f}^{\prime}
, then
{\mathcal{H}}
is regular and transverse to every fiber of
{f}
.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{Y}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{Y}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{f}^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{p}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{f}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{q}}

Proof.

Set
n=\dim X
and
d=\dim{Y}
.
We first notice that it suffices to prove the assertion in codimension one of
X
.
Indeed, let
U\subseteq X
be an open subset whose complement has codimension at least two in
X
.
If we can prove that
{\mathcal{H}}|_{U}
is transverse to
T_{X/Y}|_{U}
, that is
T_{U}=T_{X/Y}|_{U}\oplus{\mathcal{H}}|_{U}
,
then we must have
T_{X}=T_{X/Y}\oplus{\mathcal{H}}
, for
{\mathcal{H}}
,
T_{X/Y}
and
T_{X}
are all reflexive sheaves (see
[
Har80
, Proposition 1.6]
).
Since
f
is a smooth morphism,
T_{X/Y}
is locally free.
Thus this decomposition of
T_{X}
will imply that
{\mathcal{H}}
is regular and transverse to all fibers of
f
.

Hence by shrinking
X
, we may assume that
{\mathcal{H}}
is a subbundle of
T_{X}
.
Let
D\subseteq X
be the branched locus of
p
.
Then by assumption, we see that none of the components of
D
is
{\mathcal{H}}
-invariant.
Therefore, thanks to
[
Dru21
, Lemma 3.4]
, we get
p^{*}(\mathrm{det}\,{\mathcal{N}}_{{\mathcal{H}}})\cong\mathrm{det}\,{\mathcal{N}}_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}}
,
where
{\mathcal{N}}_{{\mathcal{H}}}
and
{\mathcal{N}}_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}}
are the normal bundles of
{\mathcal{H}}
and
{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}
respectively.

Let
x\in X
be a point. We will show that
T_{X/Y}
and
{\mathcal{H}}
are transverse at
x
.
The problem is now local around
x
.
By shrinking
X
around
x
, we may assume that there is a nowhere vanishing holomorphic
(n-d)
-form
\omega
such that
{\mathcal{H}}
is the kernel of the contraction by
\omega
.
Then the conclusion of the previous paragraph implies that
\omega^{\prime}=p^{*}\omega
is nowhere vanishing.

It remains to show that, for every local smooth vector field
v
on
X
, nowhere vanishing and with values in
T_{X/Y}
, the contraction
v\lrcorner\omega
is nowhere vanishing.
Since
f^{\prime}
is the base change of
f
, it follows that there is a smooth vector field
v^{\prime}
on
X^{\prime}
, nowhere vanishing and with values in
T_{X^{\prime}/Y^{\prime}}
, such that
\mathrm{d}p(v^{\prime})=v
.
Moreover, by assumption,
v^{\prime}\lrcorner\omega^{\prime}
is nowhere vanishing.
Since
v^{\prime}\lrcorner\omega^{\prime}=p^{*}(v\lrcorner\omega)
, it follows that

v\lrcorner\omega
is nowhere vanishing.
This completes the proof.
∎

3.
Numerically flat vector bundles on complex manifolds

The notion of numerically flat vector bundles was introduced in
[
DPS94
]
.
It plays an important role in the proof of locally trivial fibrations.
A vector bundle
{\mathcal{E}}
on a projective manifold is numerically flat if both
{\mathcal{E}}
and
{\mathcal{E}}^{*}
are nef.
We recall the following criterion of numerically flat vector bundles for reflexive sheaves (see
[
BS94
, Corollary 3]
and
[
Nak04
, Theorem IV.4.1]
)

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X
be a projective manifold of dimension
n
and
{\mathcal{E}}
a reflexive sheaf on
X
. Let
H
be an ample divisor in
X
. Then
{\mathcal{E}}
is a numerically flat vector bundle if and only if the following conditions hold.

(1)

{\mathcal{E}}
is
H
-semistable.

(2)

c_{1}({\mathcal{E}})\cdot H^{n-1}=c_{1}({\mathcal{E}})^{2}\cdot H^{n-2}=c_{2}({\mathcal{E}})\cdot H^{n-2}=0
.

A torsion free (coherent) sheaf
{\mathcal{E}}
on a complex Kähler manifold
(Y,\omega)
is called weakly positively curved if the following statement holds.
Let
Y^{\circ}
be the locally free locus of
{\mathcal{E}}
. For every
\epsilon>0
, there is a possibly singular Hermitian metric
h_{\epsilon}
of
{\mathcal{E}}|_{Y^{\circ}}
such that

\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h_{\epsilon}}({\mathcal{E}})\succeq-\epsilon\omega\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{{\mathcal{E}}}\mbox{ on }Y^{\circ}.

For more details, we refer to
[
CH19
, Section 2]
and
[
PT18
, Section 2]
.
The following lemma was essentially proved in
[
PT18
, Theorem 2.5.2]
,
[
Pău16
, Theorem 2.21]
and
[
CH19
, Lemma 2.10]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a torsion free weakly positively curved sheaf on a projective manifold
X
.
Let
A
be an ample divisor and
a>0
an integer.
Then there exists an integer
m>0
, such that the natural evaluation map

H^{0}(X,{(\Sym}^{am}{\mathcal{E}})^{**}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(mA))\to({\Sym}^{am}{\mathcal{E}})^{**}_{x}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(mA)_{x}

is surjective for general
x\in X
.

Proof.

We follow the lines of
[
Pău16
, Theorem 2.21]
.
We denote by
X_{\mathcal{E}}
the locally free locus of
{\mathcal{E}}
.
Let
\mu\colon Y\to\mathbb{P}({\mathcal{E}})
be a desingularization of the main component of
\mathbb{P}({\mathcal{E}})
,
which is an isomorphism over
X_{\mathcal{E}}
.
We set
P
the pullback of the tautological line bundle
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}({\mathcal{E}})}(1)
to
Y
.
Let
f\colon Y\to X
be the natural morphism and
Y_{1}=f^{-1}(X_{\mathcal{E}})
.
Then
Y_{1}\cong\mathbb{P}({\mathcal{E}}|_{X_{\mathcal{E}}})
.
We consider the line bundle
L=P^{\otimes 2a}\otimes f^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(A)
.

Since
{\mathcal{E}}
is weakly positively curved,
L|_{Y_{1}}
admits a possibly singular metric
h_{L}
which has semipositive curvature.
Moreover,
h_{L}
is bounded along a general fiber
Y_{x}
of
f
.
In particular, the multiplier ideal sheaf of
h_{L}^{\otimes k}|_{Y_{x}}
is trivial for any integer
k
large enough.
Arguing as in
[
Pău16
, Theorem 2.21]
, we obtain that for some integer
k
large enough, the reflexive sheaf

({(\Sym}^{2ak}{\mathcal{E}})^{**}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(kA))\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(kA)={(\Sym}^{2ak}{\mathcal{E}})^{**}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(2kA)

is generated by global sections over some open dense subset of
X
.
We can then deduce the lemma by letting
m=2k
.
∎

The following assertion is an extension of
[
CH19
, Proposition 2.11]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
X
be a projective manifold and
{\mathcal{E}}
a reflexive sheaf on
X
with
c_{1}({\mathcal{E}})=0
.
Let
X_{0}\subseteq X
be an open subset whose complement has codimension at least
2
.
Assume that
{\mathcal{E}}
is locally free over
X_{0}
.
Let
Y=\mathbb{P}({\mathcal{E}}|_{X_{0}})
and
L
the tautological line bundle.
If the diminished base locus
\mathbb{B}_{-}(L)
is not surjective onto
X_{0}
, then
{\mathcal{E}}
is a numerically flat vector bundle.

Remark 3.4
.

We note that, in the previous lemma, even though
Y
is only quasi-projective, we can define
\mathbb{B}_{-}(L)
in the following way.
Let
A
be an ample divisor in
X
and
f\colon Y\to X
the natural morphism.
Then we set

\mathbb{B}_{-}(L)=\bigcup_{a>0}\bigcap_{m>0}\mathrm{Bs}(L^{\otimes am}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}(f^{*}mA)).

Here, for any line bundle
M
on
Y
,
\mathrm{Bs}(M)
is the base locus of
H^{0}(Y,M)
, regarded as a closed subset of
Y
.
Then we see that, the locus
\mathbb{B}_{-}(L)
is not surjective onto
X_{0}
if and only if for any integer
a>0
, there exists an integer
m>0
, such that the natural evaluation map

H^{0}(X,{(\Sym}^{am}{\mathcal{E}})^{**}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(mA))\to({\Sym}^{am}{\mathcal{E}})^{**}_{x}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(mA)_{x}

is surjective for general
x\in X
.

Proof of Lemma
3.3
.

We may assume that
\dim X\geqslant 2
. We may also assume that
X_{0}
is the locally free locus of
{\mathcal{E}}
.
Since
{\mathcal{E}}
is reflexive, the complement of
X_{0}
has codimension at least 3.
Let
H
be an ample enough divisor in
X
and
S
the complete intersection surface of very general elements of the linear system
|H|
.
Then
S\subseteq X_{0}
and
{\mathcal{E}}|_{S}
is a vector bundle.
By Theorem
3.1
and Mehta-Ramanathan theorem, it is enough to show that
{\mathcal{E}}|_{S}
is numerically flat.
The assumptions in the lemma imply that the diminished base locus of
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}({\mathcal{E}}|_{S})}(1)
is not surjective onto
S
.
Hence
{\mathcal{E}}|_{S}
is numerically flat by
[
CH19
, Proposition 2.11]
.
∎

As pointed out in the paragraph after
[
Sim92
, Corollary 3.10]
, Simpson proved that a numerically flat vector bundle is indeed flat.
In general, there may exist non isomorphic flat structures on a holomorphic vector bundle (see Example
3.7
below).
In the following Theorem
3.5
, we underline a “canonical” flat structure on a numerically flat vector bundle.
In the remainder of this paper, we will work with it for any numerically flat vector bundle.
We note that, such a flat structure is exactly the one obtained from the correspondence in
[
Sim92
, Section 3]
.

We recall some basic results about connections on complex vector bundles.
Let
\nabla
be a smooth connection on a smooth complex vector bundle
{\mathcal{Q}}
on a complex manifold.
If the curvature of
\nabla^{0,1}
is zero,
then
\nabla
determines a holomorphic bundle structure
{\mathcal{F}}
on
{\mathcal{Q}}
such that
\nabla^{0,1}=\bar{\partial}_{{\mathcal{F}}}
.
Conversely, every holomorphic vector bundle
{\mathcal{F}}
admits a smooth connection such that
\nabla^{0,1}=\bar{\partial}_{{\mathcal{F}}}
.
For more details, see
[
Kob14
, Section 1.3]
.

A holomorphic vector bundle on a compact Kähler manifold is called Hermitian flat if it admits a Hermitian metric whose Chern connection is flat.
In this case, this connection is also the Hermitian-Yang-Mills connection, which is unique.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a numerically flat vector bundle on a projective manifold
X
.
Thanks to
[
DPS94
, Theorem 1.18]
, there is a sequence of holomorphic subbundles

0={\mathcal{E}}_{0}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq{\mathcal{E}}_{k}={\mathcal{E}}

such that each
{\mathcal{G}}_{i}={\mathcal{E}}_{i}/{\mathcal{E}}_{i-1}
is a Hermitian flat vector bundle with the unique Hermitian flat connection
\nabla_{i}
.
Such a filtration always splits as smooth complex vector bundle.
Hence if we set
{\mathcal{Q}}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}{\mathcal{G}}_{i}
, then
{\mathcal{Q}}
is isomorphic to
{\mathcal{E}}
as smooth vector bundles.
Thus in the following theorem, to construct a connection on
{\mathcal{E}}
, we work with
{\mathcal{Q}}
.

Theorem 3.5
.

With the notation above, there is a unique flat connection
\nabla_{\mathcal{F}}
on the complex vector bundle
{\mathcal{Q}}
such that

(1)

\nabla_{\mathcal{F}}
induces a holomorphic structure
{\mathcal{F}}
on
{\mathcal{Q}}
, which is isomorphic to
{\mathcal{E}}
as holomorphic vector bundles;

(2)

if
\{{\mathcal{F}}_{i}\}
is the filtration of holomorphic subbundles on
{\mathcal{F}}
, induced by
\{{\mathcal{E}}_{i}\}
and the isomorphism of item (1), then
\{{\mathcal{F}}_{i}\}
is compatible with
\nabla_{\mathcal{F}}
;

(3)

the
(1,0)
-part
\nabla_{\mathcal{F}}^{1,0}
is
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}\nabla_{i}^{1,0}
.

Proof.

The existence of
\nabla_{\mathcal{F}}
goes back to
[
Sim92
, Section 3]
.
We also refer to
[
Den21
, Section 3]
for a more explicit construction.
For the uniqueness, we will prove by induction on the length
k
of the filtration.
If
k=1
, then the connection is the unique Hermitian flat connection.

Now we assume the uniqueness for lengths smaller than
k
. Let
\nabla_{a}
and
\nabla_{b}
be two connections on
{\mathcal{Q}}
which satisfy the conditions.
By assumption,
\nabla_{a}
and
\nabla_{b}
induce isomorphic holomorphic structures.
Such an isomorphism corresponds to some smooth automorphism
\varphi
of
{\mathcal{Q}}
.
By induction, its restrictions on
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k-1}{\mathcal{G}}_{i}
and on
\bigoplus_{i=2}^{k}{\mathcal{G}}_{i}
are the identity maps.
Therefore, item (2) of the theorem implies that there is some smooth function
\eta
with values in
{\mathcal{G}}_{k}^{*}\otimes{\mathcal{G}}_{1}
such that
\varphi
is of the shape

\varphi=\mathrm{Id}_{{\mathcal{Q}}}+\eta,

where we view
\eta
as a smooth endomorphism of
{\mathcal{Q}}.

Hence we have

\nabla_{b}^{0,1}=\nabla_{a}^{0,1}+\nabla_{k\to 1}^{0,1}\eta,

where
\nabla_{k\to 1}
is the Hermitian flat connection on
{\mathcal{G}}_{k}^{*}\otimes{\mathcal{G}}_{1}
induced by
\nabla_{1}
and
\nabla_{k}
.
Since
\nabla_{a}
and
\nabla_{b}
have the same
(1,0)
-part, it follows that

\nabla_{b}=\nabla_{a}+\nabla_{k\to 1}^{0,1}\eta
.
We write
\delta=\nabla_{k\to 1}^{0,1}\eta
.
The conditions on flatness imply that

\nabla_{k\to 1}\delta=0.

Since
\nabla_{k\to 1}
is a Hermitian flat connection, the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma implies that
\delta=0
.
Hence
\nabla_{a}=\nabla_{b}
.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

We note that Theorem
3.5
holds for any filtration

0={\mathcal{E}}_{0}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq{\mathcal{E}}_{k}={\mathcal{E}}

on
{\mathcal{E}}
such that each graded piece
{\mathcal{E}}_{i}/{\mathcal{E}}_{i-1}
is Hermitian flat.
Indeed, we will prove later that the connection in the theorem is independent of the choice of the filtration, see Corollary
3.10
and Remark
3.11
below.

Example 3.7
.

In general, a holomorphic vector bundle may have non isomorphic flat structures, all compatible with the holomorphic structure.
Let
X
be an elliptic curve and
{\mathcal{E}}={\mathcal{O}}_{X}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
. We write
\mathbf{e}_{1}
and
\mathbf{e}_{2}
for the generators of the two summand of
{\mathcal{E}}
.
We note that
\Omega_{X}^{1}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
. For any constant holomorphic 1-form
s\in H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{1})
, we can define a flat connection
\nabla_{s}
on
{\mathcal{E}}
as follows,

\nabla_{s}(f\mathbf{e}_{1}+g\mathbf{e}_{2})=\mathrm{d}f\mathbf{e}_{1}+\mathrm{d}g\mathbf{e}_{2}+gs\mathbf{e}_{1},

where
f,g
are arbitrary smooth functions on
X
.
Then
({\mathcal{E}},\nabla_{s})
is isomorphic to
({\mathcal{E}},\nabla_{0})
as flat vector bundles if and only if
s=0
.
Furthermore, the unique connection satisfying the conditions of Theorem
3.5
is
\nabla_{0}
.

Until the end of this paper, a connection on a holomorphic vector bundle is always assumed to be compatible with the holomorphic structure.
We say that a flat connection on a numerically flat vector bundle
{\mathcal{E}}
satisfies the conditions of Theorem
3.5
if it is induced by the isomorphism
{\mathcal{E}}\cong{\mathcal{F}}
of Theorem
3.5
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a numerically flat vector bundle on a projective manifold
X
.
Let
\nabla_{\mathcal{E}}
be a flat connection on
{\mathcal{E}}
which satisfies the conditions of Theorem
3.5
.
Then any section
s\in H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{E}})
is parallel with respect to
\nabla_{\mathcal{E}}
.

Proof.

We first note that
\nabla_{\mathcal{E}}^{0,1}s=0
as
s
is holomorphic. It remains to show that
\nabla_{\mathcal{E}}^{1,0}s=0
.
There is a sequence of subbundles

0={\mathcal{E}}_{0}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq{\mathcal{E}}_{k}={\mathcal{E}},

such that each
{\mathcal{G}}_{i}={\mathcal{E}}_{i}/{\mathcal{E}}_{i-1}
is an irreducible Hermitian flat vector bundle with connection
\nabla_{i}
.
We will prove by induction on the length
k
.
If
{\mathcal{E}}
is irreducible Hermitian flat, then it is slope stable. Hence either
{\mathcal{E}}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
or
s=0
. In both cases,
s
is constant and
\nabla_{\mathcal{E}}s=0
.

Now we assume the statement is true for lengths smaller than
k
. We have the exact sequence

0\to{\mathcal{E}}_{k-1}\to{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{G}}_{k}\to 0.

If
s\in H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{E}}_{k-1})
then
\nabla_{\mathcal{E}}^{1,0}s=0
by induction.
Assume that
s\notin H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{E}}_{k-1})
.
Then
{\mathcal{G}}_{k}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
as it is an irreducible Hermitian flat vector bundle which admits a nonzero global holomorphic section.
In this case, the exact sequence above splits and
{\mathcal{E}}\cong{\mathcal{E}}_{k-1}\oplus{\mathcal{G}}_{k}
as holomorphic vector bundles.
We can decompose
s=s^{\prime}+s^{\prime\prime}
according to this direct sum.
From the item (3) of Theorem
3.5
, we see that

\nabla_{\mathcal{E}}^{1,0}=(\nabla_{\mathcal{E}}|_{{\mathcal{E}}_{k-1}})^{1,0}\oplus\nabla_{k}^{1,0},

where
\nabla_{k}
is the unique Hermitian flat connection on
{\mathcal{G}}_{k}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
.
Then we have
\nabla_{k}^{1,0}s^{\prime\prime}=0
, and
(\nabla_{{\mathcal{E}}}|_{{\mathcal{E}}_{k-1}})^{1,0}(s^{\prime})=0
by induction. Hence
\nabla_{\mathcal{E}}^{1,0}s=0
.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

It is indispensable to specific the connection in Lemma
3.8
.
For instance, we consider a flat vector bundle
({\mathcal{E}},\nabla_{s})
on an elliptic curve
X
defined in Example
3.7
, such that
s
is a nonzero constant holomorphic 1-form.
The generator
\mathbf{e}_{2}
can be view as a section
\mathbf{e}_{2}\in H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{E}})
.
Then
\nabla_{s}\mathbf{e}_{2}=s\mathbf{e}_{1}
is not zero. That is,
\mathbf{e}_{2}
is not parallel with respect to
\nabla_{s}
.

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
\varphi\colon{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{G}}
be a generically surjective morphism between numerically flat vector bundles on a projective manifold
X
.
Then
\varphi
is surjective.

Assume further that
{\mathcal{E}}
and
{\mathcal{G}}
are equipped with flat connections
\nabla_{\mathcal{E}}
and
\nabla_{\mathcal{G}}
respectively, which satisfy the conditions of Theorem
3.5
.
Then
\varphi
is a morphism of flat vector bundles.

Proof.

To prove that
\varphi
is surjective, it is enough to show that the induced morphism

{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\to(\det{\mathcal{G}})\otimes(\bigwedge^{l}{\mathcal{E}}^{*})

does not vanish at any point of
X
.
Here
l
is the rank of
{\mathcal{G}}
.
Since
(\det{\mathcal{G}}^{*})\otimes(\bigwedge^{l}{\mathcal{E}})
is numerically flat, hence nef, the statement follows from
[
DPS94
, Lemma 1.16]
.

For the second part of the corollary, we may identify
\varphi
as an element of
H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{E}}^{*}\otimes{\mathcal{G}})
.
If

0={\mathcal{E}}_{0}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq{\mathcal{E}}_{k}={\mathcal{E}}^{*}\mbox{ and }0={\mathcal{F}}_{0}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq{\mathcal{F}}_{l}={\mathcal{F}}

are filtrations whose graded pieces are Hermitian flat vector bundles,
then we have a filtration

0={\mathcal{G}}_{0}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq{\mathcal{G}}_{t}={\mathcal{E}}^{*}\otimes{\mathcal{F}}

such that

{\mathcal{G}}_{s}=\bigoplus_{i+j=s}({\mathcal{E}}_{i}\otimes{\mathcal{F}}_{j}).

Furthermore, for each
s
we have,

{\mathcal{G}}_{s}/{\mathcal{G}}_{s-1}\cong\bigoplus_{i+j=s}({\mathcal{E}}_{i}/{\mathcal{E}}_{i-1}\otimes{\mathcal{F}}_{j}/{\mathcal{F}}_{j-1}).

As a result, the graded pieces of the filtration
\{{\mathcal{G}}_{s}\}
are Hermitian flat vector bundles.
We can then verify that the tensor connection
\nabla_{{\mathcal{E}}^{*}\otimes{\mathcal{G}}}
on
{\mathcal{E}}^{*}\otimes{\mathcal{G}}
satisfies the conditions of
Theorem
3.5
as well.
Hence Lemma
3.8
implies that

\varphi
is parallel with respect to
\nabla_{{\mathcal{E}}^{*}\otimes{\mathcal{G}}}
.
It follows that, for any local smooth section
\sigma
of
{\mathcal{E}}
, we have

\nabla_{\mathcal{G}}(\varphi(\sigma))=\varphi(\nabla_{\mathcal{E}}\sigma)+(\nabla_{{\mathcal{E}}^{*}\otimes{\mathcal{G}}})\varphi(\sigma)=\varphi(\nabla_{\mathcal{E}}\sigma).

In another word,
\varphi
is a morphism of flat vector bundles.
∎

Remark 3.11
.

If we take
\varphi
as the identity endomorphism of
{\mathcal{E}}
in the previous corollary, then we may deduce that a connection satisfying the conditions of Theorem
3.5
is unique, independent of the choice of the filtration
\{{\mathcal{E}}_{i}\}
.

The lemma below reveals a relation between locally trivial families and flat holomorphic vector bundles.

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
({\mathcal{E}},\nabla_{\mathcal{E}})
be a flat vector bundle on a complex manifold
X
.
Assume that there is a surjective morphism of graded commutative
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-algebras

\bigoplus_{p\geqslant 0}{\Sym}^{p}{\mathcal{E}}\to\bigoplus_{p\geqslant 0}{\mathcal{Q}}_{p},

such that
{\mathcal{Q}}_{0}={\mathcal{O}}_{X}
and that each graded piece
{\Sym}^{p}{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{Q}}_{p}
is a morphism of flat vector bundles.
Then

f\colon Z=\mathrm{Proj}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}\bigoplus_{p\geqslant 0}{\mathcal{Q}}_{p}\to X

is a locally trivial family over
X
.
Moreover, the connection
\nabla_{{\mathcal{E}}}
induces a foliation
{\mathcal{G}}
on
Z
such that
T_{Z}={\mathcal{G}}\oplus{\mathcal{F}}
, where
{\mathcal{F}}
is the foliation induced by
f
.

Proof.

We consider the following commutative diagram, where the vertical arrows are product maps

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Sym}^{i}{\mathcal{E}}\otimes{\Sym}^{j}{\mathcal{E}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{Q}}_{i}\otimes{\mathcal{Q}}_{j}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Sym}^{i+j}{\mathcal{E}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{Q}}_{i+j}}

Since the horizontal maps and the first vertical map are morphisms of flat vector bundles, and since the horizontal maps are surjective, we see that
{\mathcal{Q}}_{i}\otimes{\mathcal{Q}}_{j}\to{\mathcal{Q}}_{i+j}

is a morphism of flat vector bundles as well.

Let
\pi\colon\widetilde{X}\to X
be the universal cover of
X
.
Then the connection
\nabla_{{\mathcal{E}}}
determines isomorphisms

\pi^{*}{\mathcal{E}}\cong E\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{\widetilde{X}}\mbox{ and }\pi^{*}{\mathcal{Q}}_{p}\cong Q_{p}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{\widetilde{X}},

where
E
and
Q_{p}
are complex vector spaces equipped with
\pi_{1}(X)
-actions.
Moreover, under these isomorphisms, the natural morphism

\bigoplus_{p\geqslant 0}{\Sym}^{p}\pi^{*}{\mathcal{E}}\to\bigoplus_{p\geqslant 0}\pi^{*}{\mathcal{Q}}_{p}

is induced by a surjective
\pi_{1}(X)
-equivariant morphism of graded commutative
\mathbb{C}
-algebras

\bigoplus_{p\geqslant 0}{\Sym}^{p}E\to\bigoplus_{p\geqslant 0}Q_{p}.

Let
I
be the kernel of the previous morphism and

F=\mathrm{Proj}\bigoplus_{p\geqslant 0}Q_{p}.

Then
F
is the subvariety of
\mathbb{P}(E)
with graded ideal
I
.
Since
I
is stable under the
\pi_{1}(X)
-action, we see that
F
is stable under the natural
\pi_{1}(X)
-action on
\mathbb{P}(E)
.
Hence we obtain an induced polarized
\pi_{1}(X)
-action on
F
.
Moreover, there is a
\pi_{1}(X)
-equivariant isomorphism
Z\times_{X}\widetilde{X}\cong F\times\widetilde{X}
.
This shows that
Z
is locally trivial.
The relative tangent bundle
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}
of the natural projection
F\times\widetilde{X}\to F
is
\pi_{1}(X)
-equivariant.
Hence it descends to a foliation
{\mathcal{G}}
on
Z
. Furthermore, we have
T_{Z}={\mathcal{G}}\oplus{\mathcal{F}}
.
∎

Remark 3.13
.

The foliation
{\mathcal{G}}
in the previous lemma has the following alternative description.
The flat connection
\nabla_{\mathcal{E}}
induces a flat Ehresmann connection on
\mathbb{P}({\mathcal{E}})
, which can be viewed as a foliation
{\mathcal{H}}
on
\mathbb{P}({\mathcal{E}})
.
By construction, we have a natural embedding
Z\to\mathbb{P}({\mathcal{E}})
of fiber bundles over
X
.
Then
{\mathcal{H}}
induces an Ehresmann connection on

, which is exactly
{\mathcal{G}}
.

The following proposition on representations of fundamental groups is an application of
[
Kol93
, Theorem 4.8]
.

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a proper fibration between smooth complex algebraic varieties.
Assume that for every prime divisor
B
in
Y
, there is an irreducible component of
f^{*}B
which is reduced and dominates
B
.
Let
\xi
be a linear representation of the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(X)
.
If
\xi
induces trivial representation on general fibers of
f
, then
\xi
factors through
\pi_{1}(Y)
.

Proof.

By removing some closed subset of codimension at least
2
in
Y
, we may assume that
f
is equidimensional.
Let
F
be a general fiber of
f
, and
H\subseteq\pi_{1}(X)
the intersection of all normal subgroups of finite index which contain the image of
\pi_{1}(F)
.
Since a linear group is residually finite, and since
\mathrm{Ker}(\xi)
contains the image of
\pi_{1}(F)
, we obtain that

H\subseteq\mathrm{Ker}(\xi)
.

We write
K
the kernel of the surjective morphism
\theta\colon\pi_{1}(X)\to\pi_{1}(Y)
. The proposition is equivalent to show that

K\subseteq\mathrm{Ker}(\xi).

From the previous discussion, it is enough to show that
K\subseteq H
.
Let
G\subseteq\pi_{1}(X)
be a normal subgroup of finite index which contains
H
.
We need to show that it contains
K
.
Let
p\colon X(G)\to X
be the finite étale cover corresponding to
G
, and
\pi\colon Y(G)\to Y
the normalization of
Y
in function field of
X(G)
.
Since
G
contains the image of
\pi_{1}(F)
, the morphism
p
is a trivial cover over
F
.

By assumption, for any prime divisor
B
in
Y
, the pullback
f^{*}B
has a reduced component.
It follows from
[
Kol93
, Lemma 4.8.4]
that
\pi
is étale (we note that the proof of
[
Kol93
, Lemma 4.8.4]
does not require
Y
to be proper).
Then
\pi
corresponds to a subgroup
M\subseteq\pi_{1}(Y)
of finite index .
Since
p
is a trivial cover over
F
, it follows that
X(G)\cong X\times_{Y}Y(G)
.
Therefore
G=\theta^{-1}(M)
, and hence it contains
K
.
This completes the proof.
∎

We also need the following lemma for the proofs of the main theorems.

Lemma 3.15
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a surjective morphism between smooth quasi-projective varieties. Assume that its general fibers are proper connected.
Let
{\mathcal{V}}
and
{\mathcal{W}}
be two vector bundles on
Y
. If there is an isomorphism
\varphi\colon f^{*}{\mathcal{V}}\cong f^{*}{\mathcal{W}}
on
X
, then
\varphi
descends to an isomorphism
\eta\colon{\mathcal{V}}\cong{\mathcal{W}}
on
Y
.

Proof.

We only need to show that
\varphi
descends to a morphism
\eta\colon{\mathcal{V}}\to{\mathcal{W}}
on
Y
.
Indeed, since
f\colon X\to Y
is surjective, if
\eta
exists, then it must be an isomorphism.

The morphism
\varphi
can be viewed as an element in
H^{0}(X,f^{*}({\mathcal{V}}^{*}\otimes{\mathcal{W}}))
.
By the projection formula, we have

f_{*}f^{*}({\mathcal{V}}^{*}\otimes{\mathcal{W}})\cong{\mathcal{V}}^{*}\otimes{\mathcal{W}}\otimes f_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}.

Hence it is enough to show that
f_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}
.

Let
V\subseteq Y
be any non empty open subset and
U=f^{-1}(V)
.
We consider a holomorphic function
\sigma
on
U
.
By assumption, there is an open dense subset
V_{0}\subseteq V
such that
f
has proper connected fibers over
V_{0}
.
Hence
\sigma|_{U_{0}}
descends to a holomorphic function
\mu_{0}
on
V_{0}
, where
U_{0}=f^{-1}(V_{0}).

We have to show that
\mu_{0}
can be extended to a holomorphic function
\mu
on
V
.
By Riemann’s Removable Singularity theorem, it is enough to show that
\mu_{0}
is locally bounded over
V
.
We also note that, by Hartogs’ theorem, we may shrink
V
and assume that
f
is equidimensional.

Let
y\in V
be a point. Let
Z\subseteq X
be the complete intersection of general very ample divisors such that
\dim Z=\dim Y
.
By shrinking
V
around
y
if necessary, we may assume that
Z\cap U
is quasi-finite over
V
.
Then the morphism

f|_{Z\cap U}\colon Z\cap U\to V

is an open morphism, with respect to the analytic Euclidean topology.
Let
x\in Z\cap U
be a point lying over
y
, and
U^{\prime}\subseteq Z\cap U
an Euclidean open neighborhood of
x
.
Then
V^{\prime}=f(U^{\prime})
is an Euclidean open subset of
V
containing
y
.
Since
\sigma
is holomorphic, we may assume that
\sigma|_{U^{\prime}}
is bounded.
Hence
\mu_{0}|_{V_{0}\cap V^{\prime}}
is bounded. That is,
\mu_{0}
is bounded around
y
.
This completes the proof.
∎

4.
Algebraically integrable foliations with semistable leaves and nef anticanonical classes

Algebraically integrable foliations with nef anticanonical classes induced by rational maps which are semistable in codimension one are well studied in
[
Cao19
]
,
[
CH19
]
and
[
CCM19
]
.
Their results are crucial for the proofs of the main theorems in the present paper.
For the reader’s convenience, we summarize some of them here.

Throughout this section, we consider the following situation. The morphism
\varphi\colon\Gamma\to Y
is a fibration between projective manifold, and
\pi
is a birational morphism onto a normal projective variety
X
.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

We make the following assumptions.

(1)

The
\pi
-exceptional locus
E
is pure of codimension one, and it does not dominate the base
Y
.

(2)

Every
\varphi
-exceptional divisor is
\pi
-exceptional.

(3)

There is a
\pi
-exceptional
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
E^{\prime\prime}
such that

-K_{\Gamma/Y}+E^{\prime\prime}

is nef.

(4)

For every prime divisor
B\subseteq Y
, any non reduced component of
\varphi^{*}B
is
\pi
-exceptional.

(5)

There is a finite group
G
acting on the varieties
\Gamma
,
Y
and
X
, such that the morphisms
\varphi
and
\pi
are
G
-equivariant.
Furthermore, the quotient
X/G
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial.

We recall that a prime divisor in
\Gamma
is
\varphi
-exceptional if its image has codimension at least two in
Y
.
If
E^{\prime\prime}=\sum a_{i}E_{i}
is the decomposition into prime divisors, then we set
|E^{\prime\prime}|=\sum|a_{i}|E_{i}
.
The main objective of this section is to prove the following proposition.

Proposition 4.1
.

With the assumption above, for every prime divisor
B\subseteq Y
, there is at most one irreducible component of
\varphi^{-1}(B)
which is not contained in the
\pi
-exceptional locus.

The proof of the proposition will be postponed to the end of the section.
We fix a sufficiently ample,
G
-invariant divisor
A
in
\Gamma
, so that it is
\varphi
-relatively very ample, and that for each
p\geqslant 1
, the natural morphism

\mathrm{Sym}^{p}\varphi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{\Gamma}(A)\to\varphi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{\Gamma}(pA)

is surjective.
Let
Y_{0}\subseteq Y
be a Zariski open subset such that the following properties hold.

\bullet

The fibration
\varphi
is equidimensional over
Y_{0}
.

\bullet

For any prime divisor
B\subseteq Y
, the preimage
\varphi^{-1}(B)
is contained in the
\pi
-exceptional locus if and only if
B\subseteq Y\setminus Y_{0}
.

Remark 4.2
.

In
[
CCM19
, Section 3]
,
Y_{0}
is defined as the largest open subset which satisfies the properties. However, for later use in this paper, we need to remove from
Y_{0}
some closed subset of codimension at least two. Therefore we define
Y_{0}
in this way.

Lemma 4.3

(
[
CCM19
, Lemma 3.5]
)
.

Let
L
be a
\varphi
-relatively big Cartier divisor in
\Gamma
and
P
a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor in
Y
. Assume that
L-\varphi^{*}P
is pseudoeffective. Then for any large enough integer
c
,

\varphi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{\Gamma}(L+cE^{\prime\prime})-P

is weakly positively curved if it is not zero.
That is, for a fixed Kähler form
\omega_{Y}
on Y, for a real number
\epsilon>0
, there is a possibly singular Hermitian metric
h_{\epsilon}
on
\varphi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{\Gamma}(L+cE^{\prime\prime})

and
{\mathcal{V}}_{p}^{**}|_{Y_{0}}
.

Proof.

By assumption
\rho^{*}{\mathcal{E}}_{p}
is equipped with the flat connection
\nabla_{\rho^{*}{\mathcal{E}}_{p}}
of Theorem
3.5
.
Since
\Gamma^{\circ}
contains general fibers of
\varphi
, by the definition of
{\mathcal{E}}_{p}
(see Lemma
6.2
), the restriction of
\rho^{*}{\mathcal{E}}_{p}
to a general fiber of
\varphi
is isomorphic to a trivial bundle as holomorphic vector bundles.
From the uniqueness of Theorem
3.5
, we obtain that
\nabla_{\rho^{*}{\mathcal{E}}_{p}}
induces the trivial connection on the restriction of
\rho^{*}{\mathcal{E}}_{p}
to a general fiber of
\varphi
.
Therefore, the representation of the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(\Gamma)
corresponding to
(\rho^{*}{\mathcal{E}}_{p},\nabla_{\rho^{*}{\mathcal{E}}_{p}})
induces trivial representation on the fundamental group of a general fiber of
\varphi
.
Then Proposition
3.14
implies that there is a flat vector bundle
{\mathcal{W}}_{p}
on
Y
such that
\rho^{*}{\mathcal{E}}_{p}=\varphi^{*}{\mathcal{W}}_{p}
.

Finally, we remark that
Y_{0}\setminus\varphi(\Gamma^{\circ})
has codimension at least
2
in
Y_{0}
. Hence Lemma
3.15
implies that there is a natural isomorphism between
{\mathcal{W}}_{p}|_{Y_{0}}
and
{\mathcal{V}}_{p}^{**}|_{Y_{0}}
.
∎

.
Then it satisfies the condition of Theorem

, and is

Lemma 6.5
.

There is a surjective morphism of commutative graded

\bigoplus_{p\geqslant 0}{\Sym}^{p}{\mathcal{W}}_{1}\to\bigoplus_{p\geqslant 0}{\mathcal{W}}_{p}.

Proof.

For any integers

{\mathcal{V}}_{i}\otimes{\mathcal{V}}_{j}\to{\mathcal{V}}_{i+j}

is sufficiently ample on a general fiber of

.
Furthermore, these morphisms are all

We can then obtain generically surjective morphisms

{\Sym}^{p}{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\to{\mathcal{E}}_{p}\mbox{ and }{\mathcal{E}}_{i}\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{j}\to{\mathcal{E}}_{i+j}.

By Corollary

, they are surjective morphisms of flat vector bundles.

Proof of Theorem

.

By Lemma

and by Lemma

, the variety

is a locally trivial family over

.
Furthermore the connection
\nabla_{{\mathcal{W}}_{1}}
induces a foliation
{\mathcal{G}}_{Z}
on
Z
,
which is transverse to the fibers of

.

By removing some closed subset of codimension at least two, we assume that
{\mathcal{V}}_{1}
is locally free on

.
By Lemma

, there is a natural isomorphism between
{\mathcal{W}}_{p}|_{Y_{0}}
and
{\mathcal{V}}_{p}^{**}|_{Y_{0}}
.
Since
{\Sym}^{p}{\mathcal{W}}_{1}\to{\mathcal{W}}_{p}
is surjective, we conclude that the natural morphism

{\Sym}^{p}{\mathcal{V}}_{1}|_{Y_{0}}\to{\mathcal{V}}_{p}|_{Y_{0}}

is surjective,

{\mathcal{V}}_{p}|_{Y_{0}}\cong{\mathcal{W}}_{p}|_{Y_{0}}
is locally free for any

.

We recall that
{\mathcal{V}}_{p}=\varphi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{\Gamma}(L_{p})
, where

In particular, the morphism
{\Sym}^{p}{\mathcal{V}}_{1}\to{\mathcal{V}}_{p}

\varphi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{\Gamma}(pL_{1})

\varphi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{\Gamma}(pL_{1})|_{Y_{0}}\cong{\mathcal{V}}_{p}|_{Y_{0}}\cong{\mathcal{W}}_{p}|_{Y_{0}}.

\varphi
-relative linear system

is the sum of a
\varphi
-relatively sufficiently ample divisor and an effective

-exceptional divisor,

only contracts
\rho
-exceptional divisors.
In particular, up to a closed subset of codimension at least two, the restriction

We recall that
\Gamma^{\circ}\subseteq\Gamma
is the largest open subset such that

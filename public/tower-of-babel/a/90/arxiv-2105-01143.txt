Abstract
. We construct a circle-invariant trace from the factorization homology over the circle 𝗍𝗋𝖺𝖼𝖾 : ∫ 𝕊 1 α 𝖤𝗇𝖽 ¯ ​ ( 𝖵 ) ⟶ 𝟙 {\sf trace}\colon\int^{\alpha}_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\underline{\End}(V)\longrightarrow\uno associated to a dualizable object V ∈ 𝖃 V\in\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{X}} in a symmetric monoidal ( ∞ , 1 ) (\oo,1) -category. This proves a conjecture of Toën–Vezzosi [ TV1 ] on existence of circle-invariant traces. Underlying our construction is a calculation of the factorization homology over the circle of the walking adjunction in terms of the paracyclic category of Getzler–Jones [ GJ ] : ∫ 𝕊 1 𝖠𝖽𝗃 ≃ 𝚫 ↺ ⊲ ⁡ ⊳ . \int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\Adj~\simeq~\para^{\tl\!\tr}~. This calculation exhibits a form of Poincaré duality for 1-dimensional factorization homology.

Cobordism hypothesis.

Recall, after Baez–Dolan
[
BaDo

and Hopkins–Lurie
[

\mathsf{Z}_{V}

Identifying the values of
\mathsf{Z}_{V}
on cobordisms that are disjoint unions of disks is a simple matter using dualizability data of

(e.g., its dual
V^{\vee}
, the unit
\uno\xrightarrow{\eta}V^{\vee}\otimes V

V\otimes V^{\vee}\xrightarrow{\epsilon}\uno

Furthermore, identifying the value

as a composition (in
\Bord

For instance, witnessing

(\uno\xrightarrow{\mathsf{Z}_{V}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\uno)~\simeq~(\uno\xrightarrow{\eta}V^{\vee}\otimes V\simeq V\otimes V^{\vee}\xrightarrow{\epsilon}\uno)~.

In this way, one can easily identify all values of the sought TQFT

combinatorial presentations
of 1-manifolds as gluings of disjoint unions of 1-disks along boundary 0-spheres.
The

This can be summarized as, for instance, the problem of constructing the requisite action

While a choice of a combinatorial presentation of
\mathbb{S}^{1}

A.2.
The walking monad and comonad

We review some basic categories of ordered sets and their relations to algebra.

Definition A.10
.

•

The
walking monad
is the monoidal category
\mathsf{O}
in which an object is a linearly ordered finite sets, a morphism is an order preserving map, and whose monoidal structure is join of linearly ordered sets.
The
walking comonad
is the monoidal category
\mathsf{O}^{\op}
.

•

\mathsf{O}_{+}
is the left
\mathsf{O}
-module category of linearly ordered finite sets with a maximum, whose morphisms are those order preserving maps that preserve maxima, and whose left
\mathsf{O}
-module structure is join of linearly ordered sets.

•

\mathsf{O}_{-}
is the right
\mathsf{O}
-module category of linearly ordered finite sets with a minimum, whose morphisms are those order preserving maps that preserve minima, and whose right
\mathsf{O}
-module structure is join of linearly ordered sets.

•

\mathsf{O}_{\pm}
is the category of linearly ordered finite sets with a distinct minimum and maximum, whose morphisms are those order preserving maps that preserve extrema.

Observation A.11
.

The canonical inclusion of the non-empty finite linearly ordered sets extends as an equivalence between
(\infty,1)
-categories:

\mathbf{\Delta}^{\tl}~\simeq~\mathsf{O}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad(\mathbf{\Delta}^{\op})^{\tr}~\simeq~\mathsf{O}^{\op}~.

Reversing linear orders defines an equivalence between
\mathsf{O}
-module categories,

\mathsf{O}_{+}~\simeq~\mathsf{O}_{-}^{\op}~.

Observation A.12
.

The representable simplicial set
\Hom_{\mathbf{\Delta}}\bigl(-,[1]\bigr)\colon\mathbf{\Delta}^{\op}\to{\sf Sets}
canonically factors through the forgetful functor
\mathsf{O}_{\pm}\to{\sf Sets}
as an equivalence between categories:

\mathbf{\Delta}^{\op}\xrightarrow{~\simeq~}\mathsf{O}_{\pm}~,\qquad[p]\mapsto\Hom_{\mathbf{\Delta}}\bigl([p],[1]\bigr)~.

Observation A.13
.

For each
I\in\mathsf{O}
, taking joins defines a functor:

\mathsf{O}^{\times I}\longrightarrow\mathsf{O}_{/I}~,\qquad(J_{i})_{i\in I}\longmapsto\left(\underset{i\in I}{\bigstar}J_{i}\to\underset{i\in I}{\bigstar}\ast=I\right)~.

This functor is an equivalence, with inverse given by
(J\xrightarrow{f}I)\mapsto\left(f^{-1}(i)\right)_{i\in I}
.

The next result records the sense in which
\mathsf{O}
and
\mathsf{O}^{\op}
corepresent associative algebras and associative coalgebras, respectively.

Proposition A.14
.

In the monoidal category
\mathsf{O}
, the final object
\ast\in\mathsf{O}
has a unique associative algebra structure; in the monoidal category
\mathsf{O}^{\op}
, the initial object
\ast\in\mathsf{O}^{\op}
has a unique associative coalgebra structure.
For each monoidal
(\infty,1)
-category
\mathcal{Y}
, the resulting functors

\Fun^{\mathcal{E}_{1}}(\mathsf{O},\mathcal{Y})\xrightarrow{~\simeq~}\Alg(\mathcal{Y})\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\Fun^{\mathcal{E}_{1}}(\mathsf{O}^{\op},\mathcal{Y})\xrightarrow{~\simeq~}{\sf cAlg}(\mathcal{Y})~,\qquad F\mapsto F(\ast)~,

are equivalences between
(\infty,1)
-categories.

Proof.

By taking categorical opposites, the first statement implies the second. So we are reduced to proving the first statement.

Consider the associative
\infty
-operad
{\sf Assoc}^{\otimes}\to\mathbf{\Delta}^{\op}
associated to the associative operad
{\sf Assoc}
.
The monoidal envelope
{\sf Env}({\sf Assoc})

where

In particular, the underlying category of

\mathsf{O}

In other words, there is an equivalence between monoidal categories:

The result then follows from the universal property of monoidal envelopes.

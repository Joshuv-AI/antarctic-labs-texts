1.
Introduction

We consider the following system of Schrödinger equations

(1.1)

\left.\begin{cases}-\Delta U+\lambda U=\alpha_{0}U^{3}+\beta UV^{2}\\
-\Delta V+\mu(y)V=\alpha_{1}V^{3}+\beta U^{2}V\end{cases}\right.\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},\ N=2,3,

where
\lambda
,
\alpha_{0}
,
\alpha_{1}>0
are positive constants,
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
is the coupling constant, and
\mu:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a potential function. Our objective is to construct infinitely many solutions of (
1.1
) where the radial symmetry is broken to exhibit many peaks over a circle and at the origin.

This study answers a question among many problems from exploring the radial symmetry, or breaking it, of the solutions of nonlinear Schrödinger equations. By the work of Gidas et. al.
[
4
]
via the moving plane method, the following radial symmetry result is well-known, i.e., for the scalar Schrödinger equation

(1.2)

-\Delta U+\mu(|y|)U=|U|^{p-1}U\quad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$}

if the potential function
\mu
is radial and non-decreasing in the radial variable, then the solution of (
1.2
) must be radial. Thus, the solutions with interesting patterns breaking the radial symmetry, such as solutions with many peaks, can only exist after violating the assumptions of this problem. Further, it is an interesting question if the cross interactions between species, where one considers the system of coupled Schrödinger equations, provide different circumstances for the existence of non-radial solutions.

For the scalar Schrödinger equation, by omitting the non-decreasing condition, i.e.,
\mu
is merely radial, Wei and Yan
[
11
]
presented such an example. They considered a
\mu
that possesses the prescribed asymptotic behavior

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mu(|y|)=\mu_{0}+\frac{a}{|y|^{m}}+{O}\left(\frac{1}{|y|^{m+\theta}}\right)\quad\text{for some $\mu_{0},a,\theta>0$ and $m>1$ as $|y|\rightarrow\infty$}.

The constructed solution
u
has segregated
k
peaks over a circle. More specifically,
u
peaks about
k
points
x_{i}=(z_{i},\mathbf{0})
,
i=1,\cdots,k
where

z_{i}=\left(R\cos\left(\frac{2(i-1)\pi}{k}\right),~R\sin\left(\frac{2(i-1)\pi}{k}\right)\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\quad\text{for $i=1,\cdots,k$}

and the radius
R
is sufficiently large, which dependes on the number of peaks
k
. They showed that there are infinitely many such solutions for every
k
larger than a certain
k_{0}
. Cerami, et al.
[
3
]
showed that such a result holds when the radial symmetry of
\mu
is also broken.

Also, it has been actively studied if radial symmetry result of the scalar case continues for the problems of coupled Schrödinger systems. If
n
-species interact, we employ
n
potential functions. We adopt a naive classification by behaviors of potentials. We will speak of a potential that is a constant function, an important case among non-decreasing potentials, or a potential that is an increasing function, and so on. In regard with this, for the prescribed asymptotic behaviors similar to (
1.3
), asymptotically,
a\leq 0
and
a>0
correspond to the non-decreasing and decreasing potentials, respectively.

Having those said, we remark on a few relevant works. Peng and Wang
[
9
]
considered two species Schrödinger system

\begin{cases}-\Delta U+P(|x|)U=\alpha_{0}U^{3}+\beta V^{2}U,\\
-\Delta V+Q(|x|)V=\beta V^{3}+\alpha_{0}U^{2}V,\end{cases}

and constructed various interesting solutions. They generated solutions of patterns where two species peak in a synchronized manner at shared sites or in a segregated manner at respective sites over a circle. The assumption on the potential function were such that one of
P(\cdot)
and
Q(\cdot)
violates the non-decreasing condition, or some other sophisticated conditions were supposed. See Theorem 1.1 and 1.2 in
[
9
]
. Wang et al.
[
10
]
has improved the results of
[
9
]
, allowing larger class of potentials. The second leading order in (
1.3
) with

has been included. Long, et. al.
[
7
]
considered a problem where two potentials are exponentially decreasing and obtained the result. The problems with severer violations, further omitting the radial symmetry of potentials, have been investigated by Ao and Wei
[
2
]
and Ao, et. al.
[
1
]
. The case with nonlinearity other than

have been studied by Zheng
[
12
]
. Recently, Peng, et. al.
[
8
]
constructed non-radial solutions in the three species problem, where the potentials are all positive constant; thus non-decreasing. Their results clearly show that it is possible to have non-radial patterns in the system case even with radial and non-increasing potentials. The results can be compared to those of Wei and Yan
[
11
]
. Not infinitely many solutions are constructed and results follow under a certain condition on the constants.

Lin and Peng
[
6
]
advanced a question in another direction. They sought a different pattern. In their solution, while one species peaks at many points over a circle as before, the other species peaks at the origin. They constructed solutions of these patterns in a model where the coupling terms are linear.

Our work in this paper can be summarized as follows. Continuing the work of Lin and Peng
[
6
]

1.1
) where the coupling terms are nonlinear. We also consider one potential to be a constant function, while the other potential

violates the non-decreasing condition. We though mention that we have made a small improvement from the work of Wei and Yan
[

]
and others that the asymptotic behavior of

\displaystyle 1.\quad\mu(y)=\mu_{0}+\frac{a}{|y|^{m}}+O\left(\frac{1}{|y|^{m+\theta}}\right)\quad\text{for some $\mu_{0},a,\theta>0$ and $m>\frac{1}{2}$ as $|y|\rightarrow\infty$.}

\displaystyle 2.\quad\text{$\mu$ is bounded and $\mu(y)\geq\tilde{\mu}_{0}>0$ for all $y$.}

3.
Results

Our solution scheme is based on the following formal observations. Suppose
\Big(U_{0}+u,\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}V_{i}+v\Big)
is a solution of (
1.1
), or
(u,v)
formally solves

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u+\lambda u-3\alpha_{0}U_{0}^{2}u=g_{0}(u,v),\\
&-\Delta v+\mu(y)v-3\alpha_{1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}V_{i}\right)^{2}v=g_{1}(u,v),\end{aligned}\right.

where

\left\{\begin{aligned} &g_{0}(u,v):=3\alpha_{0}U_{{0}}u^{2}+\alpha_{0}u^{3}+\beta(U_{0}+u)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}V_{i}+v\right)^{2},\\
&g_{1}(u,v):=3\alpha_{1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}V_{i}\right)v^{2}+\alpha_{1}v^{3}+\beta\left(U_{0}+u\right)^{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}V_{i}+v\right)\\
&\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad-(\mu-1)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}V_{i}\right)+\alpha_{1}\left\{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}V_{i}\right)^{3}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}V_{i}^{3}\right\}.\end{aligned}\right.

Based on these observations, for a fixed

by the defining relation

(3.1)

\left<L_{0}(u),\varphi\right>_{0}=\ell_{u}[\varphi].

For a fixed

\tilde{\ell}_{v}[\phi]:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\nabla v\cdot\nabla\phi+\mu(y)v\phi-3\alpha_{1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}V_{i}\right)^{2}v\phi\right)dy,

and the linear operator
L_{1}:\hat{H}_{s}\rightarrow\hat{H}_{s}
is defined by the relation

(3.2)

\left<L_{1}(v),\phi\right>_{1}=\tilde{\ell}_{v}[\phi].

On the other hand,
g_{0}(u,v)
and
g_{1}(u,v)
defines the linear functionals

and

and

\displaystyle G_{0}(u,v)[\varphi]:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g_{0}(u,v)\varphi\quad\text{for $\varphi\in H_{s}$},\quad G_{1}(u,v)[\phi]:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g_{1}(u,v)\phi\quad\text{for $\phi\in\hat{H}_{s}$}.

Applying Riesz representation theorem, there exist

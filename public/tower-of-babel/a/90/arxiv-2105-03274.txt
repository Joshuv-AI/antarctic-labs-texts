Abstract
. Lovász (1967) showed that two finite relational structures A A and B B are isomorphic if, and only if, the number of homomorphisms from C C to A A is the same as the number of homomorphisms from C C to B B for any finite structure C C . Soon after, Pultr (1973) proved a categorical generalisation of this fact. We propose a new categorical formulation, which applies to any locally finite category with pushouts and a proper factorisation system. As special cases of this general theorem, we obtain two variants of Lovász’ theorem: the result by Dvořák (2010) that characterises equivalence of graphs in the k k -dimensional Weisfeiler–Leman equivalence by homomorphism counts from graphs of tree-width at most k k , and the result of Grohe (2020) characterising equivalence with respect to first-order logic with counting and quantifier depth k k in terms of homomorphism counts from graphs of tree-depth at most k k . The connection of our categorical formulation with these results is obtained by means of the game comonads of Abramsky et al. We also present a novel application to homomorphism counts in modal logic.



1.
Introduction

Over fifty years ago, Lovász
[
20
]
proved that two finite graphs (or, more generally, any two finite relational structures)
A
and
B
are isomorphic if, and only if, for every finite graph
C
, the number of homomorphisms from
C
to
A
is the same as the number of homomorphisms from
C
to
B
. While one direction of this equivalence is obvious, the other direction establishes that the isomorphism type of a finite graph
A
is characterised by an infinite integer vector
V
indexed by
\mathcal{F}
, the collection of (isomorphism classes of) finite graphs, where for
C\in\mathcal{F}
,
V_{C}
is the number of homomorphisms from
C
to
A
. This seminal result has led to extensions and investigations in many different directions. One that concerns us is that by restricting the collection
\mathcal{F}
to a natural sub-collection, we can often obtain characterisations of natural coarsenings of the relation of isomorphism. For any subclass
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{F}
, say that a pair of graphs
G
and
H
is
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
-homomorphism equivalent if for any
K
in
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
the number of homomorphisms from
K
to
G
is the same as the number of homomorphisms from
K
to
H
.

For instance, Dvořák
[
11
]
shows that for any
k
, if we consider the collection
\mathcal{T}_{k}
of all graphs of tree-width at most
k
, then a pair of graphs are
\mathcal{T}_{k}
-homomorphism equivalent if, and only if, they are equivalent with respect to the
k
-dimensional Weisfeiler–Leman (
k
-WL) equivalence. The
k
-WL
equivalence relations are a widely studied family of approximations of the graph isomorphism relation (see
[
17
]
for a recent exposition) with many equivalent characterisations in terms of combinatorics, logic, algebra and linear optimisation. From our point of view, one important such characterisation is the fact that two graphs
G
and
H
are
k
-WL equivalent if, and only if, they are not distinguished by any sentence of
\mathcal{C}^{k+1}
—first-order logic with counting quantifiers restricted to no more than
k+1
distinct variables
[
9
]
. This logical formulation of the equivalence extends naturally to finite structures over any relational vocabulary.

Dvořák’s result offers yet another characterisation of
k
-WL equivalence, this time in terms of homomorphism counts from the equally widely studied class of graphs
\mathcal{T}_{k}
. An important special case is that of
k=1
.
Since
\mathcal{T}_{1}
is the class of finite forests, and
1
-WL equivalence is the same as
fractional isomorphism
(see
[
27
]
), the result also provides an elegant characterisation of equivalence of graphs with respect to the number of homomorphisms from trees. It is also known that two graphs are co-spectral if, and only if, they are
\mathrm{Cyc}
-homomorphism equivalent, where
\mathrm{Cyc}
is the class of simple cycles, and that two graphs are
quantum isomorphic
if, and only if, they are
\mathcal{P}
-homomorphism equivalent for the class
\mathcal{P}
of planar graphs
[
23
]
. Another recent addition to this collection of results is that of Grohe
[
14
]
. He shows that, if
\mathcal{D}_{n}
is the collection of graphs of
tree-depth
at most
n
, then two graphs are
\mathcal{D}_{n}
-homomorphism equivalent if, and only if, they are not distinguished by any sentence of
\mathcal{C}_{n}
—first-order logic with counting with quantifier depth at most
n
.

This variety of results relating homomorphism counts over restricted classes to approximations of isomorphism, often proved with very different methods, calls for the development of a more general theory. In the present paper we develop a general categorical result which yields, as example applications, the results of Lovász, Dvořák and Grohe mentioned above. The connection between our categorical result and the results of Dvořák and Grohe is established using the game comonads of Abramsky et al.
[
1
,
3
]
. Specifically, in
[
1
]
the graded pebbling comonad
\mathbb{P}_{k}
on the category of
\sigma
-structures (for a finite relational signature
\sigma
) is introduced and it is shown that the coalgebras for this comonad correspond in a natural way with certain tree decompositions of width at most
k-1
. In particular, a finite
\sigma
-structure admits a coalgebra structure for this comonad if, and only if, it has tree-width at most
k-1
. At the same time, it is shown that isomorphism in the Kleisli category corresponding to the comonad (or equivalently in the Eilenberg–Moore category) is exactly indistinguishability in
\mathcal{C}^{k}
. This brings together in one categorical construction the essential elements of Dvořák’s theorem. In an exactly analogous fashion, the Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé comonad
\mathbb{E}_{n}
of
[
3
]
captures, at the level of coalgebras, the structures of tree-depth at most
n
and yields a notion of isomorphism corresponding to equivalence in
\mathcal{C}_{n}
. These categorical formulations of the combinatorial parameters tree-width and tree-depth on the one hand, and the logical equivalences with respect to
\mathcal{C}^{k}
and
\mathcal{C}_{n}
on the other, are what enable us to give a single result generalising the three theorems of Lovász, Dvořák and Grohe.

Our general result states that in any locally finite category with pushouts and a proper factorisation system, two objects
m
and
n
are isomorphic if, and only if, the number of morphisms from
k
to
m
is the same as that from
k
to
n
for any object
k
. This is proved in Section
3
. In Section
4
, we use the game comonads to derive the theorems of
Dvořák and Grohe from this general result and also a combination of the two characterising equivalence in
\mathcal{C}^{k}_{n}
—first-order logic with counting with quantifier depth
n
and
k
variables. In Section
5
, we apply the machinery we have developed to another game comonad: the graded modal comonad
\mathbb{M}_{k}
. This gives a new Lovász-style result for pointed Kripke structures, relating homomorphism counts from synchronization trees of bounded height to equivalence in a modal logic with counting. Finally, in Section
6
we draw some conclusions from this about certain normal forms for first-order logic with counting.

Related work

Other categorical generalisations of Lovász’ theorem have been obtained by Pultr
[
26
]
and Isbell
[
15
]
. Cf. also Lovász’ paper
[
21
]
. In modern terms, Pultr works with a finitely well-powered, locally finite category with (extremal epi, mono) factorisations and Isbell requires the category to be locally finite with a special type of factorisation system (which he calls “bicategory”). Unlike the results in op. cit., our Theorem
5
does not hinge on a combinatorial counting argument, but rather on an application of the inclusion-exclusion principle, and appears to be better suited for applications to game comonads.

2.2.
Comonads

A
comonad
(
in Kleisli form
) on a category
\mathcal{A}
is given by:

•

an object map

•

a morphism
\varepsilon_{A}\colon\mathbb{C}(A)\to A
for every
A\in\mathcal{A}

•

a
coextension operation
associating with any morphism

f^{*}\colon\mathbb{C}(A)\to\mathbb{C}(B)

These data must satisfy the following equations for all morphisms

and

\varepsilon_{A}^{*}={\rm id}_{\mathbb{C}(A)},\ \ \varepsilon_{B}\circ f^{*}=f,\ \ (g\circ f^{*})^{*}=g^{*}\circ f^{*}.

In particular, we can extend

to a functor

by setting

for every morphism
f\colon A\to B
.

3.
A Categorical Lovász-Type Theorem

A category is
locally finite
if there are only finitely many morphisms between any two of its objects. A locally finite category
\mathcal{A}
is said to be
combinatorial
if, for all
m,n\in\mathcal{A}
,

m\cong n\ \Longleftrightarrow\ |\hom_{\mathcal{A}}(k,m)|=|\hom_{\mathcal{A}}(k,n)|\ \ \forall k\in\mathcal{A}.

Thus, Lovász’ theorem
[
20
]
states that, for any finite relational signature
\sigma
, the category
\Sigma_{f}
of finite
\sigma
-structures with homomorphisms is combinatorial.
The aim of this section is to prove the following generalisation of Lovász’ result.

Theorem 5
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a locally finite category. If
\mathcal{A}
has pushouts and a proper factorisation system, then it is combinatorial.

Definition 6

A pair of classes of morphisms

(2)

A

is a weak factorisation system

\mathcal{E}
are epimorphisms and all morphisms in

It is an easy observation that any proper factorisation system is an
orthogonal
factorisation system, meaning that the diagonal fillers are unique.

We describe two different proper factorisation systems in the category
\Sigma
of
\sigma
-structures, for a fixed relational signature
\sigma
. Each homomorphism of
\sigma
-structures

There are two natural ways to turn

and, in the second, we set

These two ways of turning
\widetilde{A}
into a
\sigma

.

For example, the first one yields the weak factorisation system where

Both factorisation systems are proper. Moreover, since the image of a finite

\sigma

The category

Generic and degenerate elements

The key step in the proof of the classical Lovász’ theorem consists in showing that, if
|\hom_{\Sigma_{f}}(C,A)|=|\hom_{\Sigma_{f}}(C,B)|
for all
C
in
\Sigma_{f}
, then also
|\operatorname{inj}_{\Sigma_{f}}(C,A)|=|\operatorname{inj}_{\Sigma_{f}}(C,B)|
for all
C
in
\Sigma_{f}
, where
\operatorname{inj}_{\Sigma_{f}}(C,A)
is the set of all injective homomorphisms
C\hookrightarrow A
, and similarly for
\operatorname{inj}_{\Sigma_{f}}(C,B)
. We aim to show the analogous fact in our setting, where injective homomorphisms are replaced by
\mathcal{M}
-morphisms.

The intuition behind the following definition is that a
\sigma
-homomorphism
f\colon C\to A
is non-injective precisely when there exist an onto non-injective
\sigma
-homomorphism
e\colon C\twoheadrightarrow C^{\prime}
and a
\sigma
-homomorphism
g\colon C^{\prime}\to A
such that
f=g\circ e
. In terms of the hom-functor
E\coloneqq\hom_{\Sigma_{f}}(-,A)
,
f\in E(C)
is non-injective if, and only if,
f=E(e)(g)
for some onto non-injective
\sigma
-homomorphism
e\colon C\twoheadrightarrow C^{\prime}
and
g\in E(C^{\prime})
.

Definition 10
.

A
strict quotient
in
\mathcal{A}
is an
\mathcal{E}
-morphism which is not an isomorphism. (Equivalently, by Lemma
7
(b)
, a strict quotient is an arrow in
\mathcal{E}\setminus\mathcal{M}
.)

Further, consider a functor
E\colon\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{FinSet}
. For every
k\in\mathcal{A}
, we say that
s\in E(k)
is
degenerate
if there exist a strict quotient
f\colon k\twoheadrightarrow l
and
t\in E(l)
such that
{E(f)(t)=s}
. Otherwise,
s
is called
generic
. The subset of
E(k)
consisting of the generic elements is denoted by
{E}\llbracket k\rrbracket
.

The next lemma shows that this definition matches our intuition for hom-functors on
\mathcal{A}
.

Let

for some

-morphisms

.

Proof.

Let
f
be an arbitrary element of

. Assume

3.3.
Specialising to finite coalgebras

To conclude this section, we show that Theorem

\Sigma_{f}
to categories of finite coalgebras for comonads on
\Sigma
. This result is then applied in Sections

Given a comonad
\mathbb{C}
on
\Sigma
, we say that a coalgebra
(A,\alpha)
for
\mathbb{C}
is
finite
if
A
is a finite
\sigma
-structure. The full subcategory of
\mathrm{EM}(\mathbb{C})
defined by the finite coalgebras is denoted

Corollary 14
.

Let
\mathbb{C}
be any comonad on
\Sigma
. Then
\mathrm{EM}_{f}(\mathbb{C})
, the category of finite coalgebras for
\mathbb{C}
, is combinatorial.

In order to prove the previous result, we need the following notions.
Recall that, for any category
\mathcal{A}
and object
A\in\mathcal{A}
, two epimorphisms
f\colon A\to B
and
g\colon A\to C
are
equivalent
, written

, if there exists an isomorphism
h\colon B\to C
such that
h\circ f=g
. It is not difficult to see that
\sim
is an equivalence relation on the collection of epimorphisms with domain
A
. The category
\mathcal{A}
is said to be
well-copowered
if, for each
A\in\mathcal{A}
, the collection of all
\sim
-equivalence classes of epimorphisms with domain
A
is a set (as opposed to a proper class). Any cocomplete category that is well-copowered admits a proper factorisation system, see e.g.
[
8
, Propositions 4.4.2, 4.4.3]
and
[
5
, Proposition 14.7]
.

Moreover, recall that a functor
F\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}

creates colimits

4.
Applications to Finite Model Theory

In this section, we apply Corollary
14
to the game comonads introduced in Section

to give new proofs of results of Grohe and Dvořák connecting homomorphism counts and indistinguishability in appropriate logic fragments.
We start by establishing a general result (Theorem
16
) and then proceed to show how Grohe’s and Dvořák’s theorems follow from it.

Before stating this general result, we illustrate the underlying idea for a generic game comonad
\mathbb{C}
.
In Section
2
we have recalled how game comonads capture combinatorial parameters of
\sigma
-structures. On the other hand, they can also express equivalence with respect to appropriate logic fragments. For this, we need to extend the signature to account for equality in the logic. Let
\sigma^{+}\coloneqq\sigma\cup\{I\}
be the relational signature obtained by adding a binary relation symbol

to
\sigma
, and let
\Sigma^{+}
be the category of
\sigma^{+}
-structures with homomorphisms. There is an

, i.e. a full and faithful functor,

which sends a
\sigma
-structure

to the
\sigma^{+}
-structure

where

is interpreted as the identity.

As the comonad
\mathbb{C}
on
\Sigma
is defined for arbitrary relational signatures, we have a corresponding comonad
\mathbb{C}^{+}
on
\Sigma^{+}
. Typically, it turns out that, for an appropriate logic fragment
\mathcal{L}
,

A\equiv_{\mathcal{L}}B\ \ \Longleftrightarrow\ \ F^{\mathbb{C}^{+}}(J(A))\cong F^{\mathbb{C}^{+}}(J(B))

for all

, where

is the adjunction associated with

, cf. Section
2.3
.

Provided that
\mathbb{C}^{+}
sends finite structures to finite structures, we can apply Corollary

to translate the isomorphism

into a statement about homomorphism counts for
J(A)
and
J(B)
in
\Sigma_{f}^{+}
. It then remains to go back to
\sigma
-structures to obtain a statement about homomorphism counts for
A
and
B
in
\Sigma_{f}
. To this end, note that the functor
J\colon\Sigma\to\Sigma^{+}
has a left adjoint

H\colon\Sigma^{+}\to\Sigma

sending a
\sigma^{+}
-structure
D
to
D^{-}/{\sim}
, where
D^{-}
is the
\sigma
-reduct of
D
and
\sim
is the equivalence relation generated by

(for a proof of this fact, see Lemma

).

We now state the general result (whose proof is deferred to Section

) from which Grohe’s and Dvořák’s theorems will be derived in Sections

and
4.2
, respectively. Let us write
\mathrm{im}(U^{\mathbb{C}})
for the full subcategory of

consisting of the objects of the form

for

, and similarly for

.

Theorem 16
.

Let
\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{C}^{+}
be comonads on
\Sigma
and
\Sigma^{+}
, respectively, and
\mathcal{L}
a logic fragment. Suppose the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

for all
A,B\in\Sigma_{f}
,
A\equiv_{\mathcal{L}}B
if, and only if,
F^{\mathbb{C}^{+}}(J(A))\cong F^{\mathbb{C}^{+}}(J(B))
;

(2)

\mathbb{C}^{+}
sends finite
\sigma^{+}
-structures to finite
\sigma^{+}
-structures;

(3)

the embedding

and its left adjoint

restrict to
\Sigma_{f}\cap\mathrm{im}(U^{\mathbb{C}})
and
\Sigma_{f}^{+}\cap\mathrm{im}(U^{\mathbb{C}^{+}})
.

Then, for any finite
\sigma
-structures
A
and

,

A\equiv_{\mathcal{L}}B\ \text{ if, and only if, }\ |\hom_{\Sigma_{f}}(C,A)|=|\hom_{\Sigma_{f}}(C,B)|

for every finite
\sigma
-structure

in
\mathrm{im}(U^{\mathbb{C}})
.

Remark 17
.

The proofs of Grohe’s and Dvořák’s theorems essentially reduce to showing that the assumptions of Theorem
16
are satisfied for the appropriate comonads. The “combinatorial core” of these results, which requires a specific argument for each comonad, corresponds to verifying that the functor
H
restricts to
\Sigma_{f}^{+}\cap\mathrm{im}(U^{\mathbb{C}^{+}})\to\Sigma_{f}\cap\mathrm{im}(U^{\mathbb{C}})
. This amounts to saying that the operation

does not increase the tree-depth or tree-width of

, and can be understood as an equality elimination result (cf. Section

).

4.1.
Bounded tree-depth

Let
\mathcal{C}
be the extension of first-order logic obtained by adding, for each natural number

The aim of this section is to use Theorem

to give a new proof of a recent result of Grohe
[

5
5

5

Grohe proved this result for undirected vertex-coloured graphs, but pointed out that his proof can be extended to arbitrary

Theorem 18

For any

\sigma

Let

be the Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé comonad on

By Proposition

are satisfied for

,

, and

Item

is a consequence of

:

Proposition 19
.

For any two
\sigma
-structures

A\equiv_{\mathcal{C}_{n}}B
if, and only if,

Item

Proposition 20
.

The embedding
J\colon\Sigma\to\Sigma^{+}
and its left adjoint

restrict to the full subcategories consisting of finite structures with tree-depth at most

Proof.

Clearly, if
A
is a (finite)

-structure with tree-depth at most

has tree-depth at most
n

.
To improve readability, set
C\coloneqq H(D)

Suppose

{\leq\,}n

d_{1}{\,\frown\,}d_{2}
in the Gaifman graph

. Recall from the definition of

that, at the level of sets,

is the equivalence relation generated by

. If

denote its

This minimum exists because

Clearly,

It remains to prove that, for all

If

(u,v)\in I^{A}
, we know that
u{\,\frown\,}v
and so
u\bowtie v
in
\mathbb{A}
. Because
p^{\prime}(z)=p(z)
whenever
v\not\leq z
, to show that
u\bowtie w
in
\mathbb{A}/_{u\sim v}
we only need to check that

(1)

\forall z\in A,\ \ v<z\leq w\ \Longrightarrow\ p^{\prime}(z)\not=p^{\prime}(u).

Observe that
p^{\prime}(u)=p(u)
. Also, by definition of
p^{\prime}
, for any
z
such that
v<z\leq w
the equality
p^{\prime}(z)=p(u)
is satisfied if, and only if, either
p(z)=p(v)
and
u\not\bowtie z_{\min}
, or
p(z)=p(u)
and
v\not\bowtie z_{\min}
. However, none of these two scenarios can occur because
v\bowtie w
implies
p(z)\neq p(v)
and
v\bowtie z_{\min}
. Hence, the condition in (
1
) is satisfied.

On the other hand, assume that
w<v
. Then
u{\,\uparrow\,}w
. If
u<w
then
u\bowtie v
implies
u\bowtie w
in
\mathbb{A}
. Similarly, if
w\leq u
then
v\bowtie w
entails
u\bowtie w
in
\mathbb{A}
. Therefore,
u\bowtie w
in
\mathbb{A}/_{u\sim v}
because
p(z)=p^{\prime}(z)
for every
z
such that
v\not\leq z
.

(2) Let
w,w^{\prime}\in A\setminus\{v\}
be such that
w\bowtie w^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{A}
. We can assume without loss of generality that
w\leq w^{\prime}
. If
v\not\leq w^{\prime}
then
p(z)=p^{\prime}(z)
for every
z
such that
w\leq z\leq w^{\prime}
, and so
w\bowtie w^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{A}/_{u\sim v}
.
Thus, suppose
v\leq w^{\prime}
. It follows that
v{\,\uparrow\,}w
and so either
v<w
or
w<v
.

First we assume
v<w\leq w^{\prime}
. Note that, for any
z
such that
w<z\leq w^{\prime}
, if
p^{\prime}(z)=p^{\prime}(w)
and
p(z)\notin\{p(u),p(v)\}
, then by definition of
p^{\prime}
we must have
p(z)=p(w)
, contradicting the fact that
w\bowtie w^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{A}
. So, we only have to take care of the case where either
p(z)=p(u)
or
p(z)=p(v)
. Set

\displaystyle U\coloneqq\{x\in A\mid v<x\leq w^{\prime}\text{ and }p(x)=p(u)\}

\displaystyle V\coloneqq\{x\in A\mid v<x\leq w^{\prime}\text{ and }p(x)=p(v)\}.

Suppose
U
and
V
are non-empty and so they admit minimal elements
\overline{u}
and
\overline{v}
, respectively. Then, either
\overline{u}<\overline{v}
or
\overline{v}<\overline{u}
. If
\overline{u}<\overline{v}
then any
z\geq\overline{u}
such that
p(z)=p(u)
satisfies
v\bowtie z_{\min}
(because
z_{\min}=\overline{u}
) and so
p^{\prime}(z)=p(v)
, and similarly any
z\geq\overline{u}
such that
p(z)=p(v)
satisfies
u\not\bowtie z_{\min}
(because
z_{\min}=\overline{v}
) and so
p^{\prime}(z)=p(u)
.
On the other hand, if
\overline{v}<\overline{u}
then
p^{\prime}(z)=p(z)
for any
z\geq\overline{v}
. Either way, for any
z
between
w
and
w^{\prime}
,
p^{\prime}(z)=p^{\prime}(w)
implies
p(z)=p(w)
. Hence,
w\bowtie w^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{A}
entails
w\bowtie w^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{A}/_{u\sim v}
. A similar reasoning applies, mutatis mutandis, when
U=\emptyset
or
V=\emptyset
.

Suppose next that
w<v<w^{\prime}
. Then
p^{\prime}(w)=p(w)
and, as
w\bowtie w^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{A}
, it follows that
p(w)\not=p(v)
. If
p(w)\not=p(u)
then we conclude that
w\bowtie w^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{A}/_{u\sim v}
because
p^{\prime}(z)=p^{\prime}(w)
entails
p(z)=p(w)
for any element
z
between
w
and
w^{\prime}
.
To conclude the proof, suppose towards a contradiction that
p(w)=p(u)
and there exists a
z\in A\setminus\{v\}
such that
w<z\leq w^{\prime}
and
p^{\prime}(z)=p^{\prime}(w)
. Then,
p^{\prime}(z)=p(u)
. Since
w\bowtie w^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{A}
, we get
p(z)\not=p(u)
and so
v<z
,
p(z)=p(v)
and
u\not\bowtie z_{\min}
in
\mathbb{A}
.
As
u\bowtie v
in
\mathbb{A}
, there must exist an
x
such that

w<v<x\leq z_{\min}\leq z\leq w^{\prime}

and
p(x)=p(u)
, contradicting the fact that
w\bowtie w^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{A}
.
∎

Applying this construction iteratively, we obtain the following analogue of Proposition

, which concludes the proof of Dvořák’s theorem.

5.
Homomorphism Counting in Modal Logic

In this section we prove a new homomorphism counting result for (multi-)modal logic. This is derived by applying the categorical framework from Section
3
to a game comonad for modal logic defined on the category of pointed Kripke structures. Since there is no equality in the logic, this time we can dispense with the extra relation
I
. This considerably simplifies the proofs, compared to the previous section.

Let
\sigma
be a signature consisting of relation symbols of arity at most
2
. Each unary relation symbol
P
yields a propositional variable
p
, and each binary relation symbol
R_{\alpha}
yields modalities
\Box_{\alpha}
and
\Diamond_{\alpha}
.
We can think of a
\sigma
-structure
A
as a Kripke structure for this multi-modal logic, where
P^{A}
gives the valuation for the propositional variable
p
, and
R_{\alpha}^{A}
gives the accessibility relation for the modalities
\Box_{\alpha}
and
\Diamond_{\alpha}
.

Each formula
\varphi
in this modal logic admits a translation into a first-order formula
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{x}
in one free variable
x
; this is known as the
standard translation
, see e.g.
[
7
, §2.4]
. We let
\llbracket p\rrbracket_{x}\coloneqq P(x)
and let
\llbracket\ \rrbracket_{x}
commute with Boolean connectives. Further, set
\llbracket\Box_{\alpha}\varphi\rrbracket_{x}\coloneqq\forall y.\,R_{\alpha}(x,y)\to\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{y}
and
\llbracket\Diamond_{\alpha}\varphi\rrbracket_{x}\coloneqq\exists y.\,R_{\alpha}(x,y)\wedge\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{y}
where
y
is a fresh variable. Consider a
pointed Kripke structure
, that is a pair
(A,a)
where
A
is a
\sigma
-structure and
a\in A
. Then
A,a\models\varphi
according to Kripke semantics if, and only if,
A\models\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{x}[a/x]
in the standard model-theoretic sense.

Henceforth, we assume all Kripke structures under consideration are pointed. Let
\mathsf{Kri}
be the category of Kripke structures and
\sigma
-homomorphisms preserving the distinguished element.
For every natural number
k
, there is a
modal comonad

\mathbb{M}_{k}
on
\mathsf{Kri}
, see
[
3
]
. For any Kripke structure
(A,a)
, the carrier of
\mathbb{M}_{k}(A,a)
is the set of all paths of length
{\leq\,}k
starting from
a
:

a\xrightarrow{R_{1}}a_{1}\xrightarrow{R_{2}}a_{2}\to\cdots\xrightarrow{R_{n}}a_{n}

where
R_{1},\dots,R_{n}
are binary relations from
\sigma
.
The distinguished element of the Kripke structure
\mathbb{M}_{k}(A,a)
is the trivial path
(a)
of length
0
. If
P\in\sigma
is unary then
P^{\mathbb{M}_{k}(A,a)}
is defined as the set of paths
p
such that the last element
a_{n}
of
p
belongs to
P^{A}
. For a binary relation symbol
R\in\sigma
, let
R^{\mathbb{M}_{k}(A,a)}
be the set of pairs of paths
(p,p^{\prime})
such that
p^{\prime}
is obtained by extending
p
by one step along
R
. The morphism
\varepsilon_{(A,a)}\colon\mathbb{M}_{k}(A,a)\to(A,a)
sends a path to its last element; for the definition of the coextension operation, see
[
3
, §2.3]
.

The comonad
\mathbb{M}_{k}
captures a well known combinatorial parameter of Kripke structures, as we now recall. Let us say that a Kripke structure
(A,a)
is
rooted
if, for any
a^{\prime}\in A
, there is a path from
a
to
a^{\prime}
. If we further require that this path be unique, then
(A,a)
is a
synchronization tree
. A synchronization tree
(A,a)
has
height
{\leq\,}k
if, for each
a^{\prime}\in A
, the length of the unique path from
a
to
a^{\prime}
is at most
k
. For a proof of the following result, see
[
4
, Proposition 6.6]
.

Proposition 28
.

A rooted Kripke structure
(A,a)
admits a coalgebra structure
(A,a)\to\mathbb{M}_{k}(A,a)
if, and only if,
(A,a)
is a synchronization tree of height
{\leq\,}k
.

On the other hand, the modal comonad
\mathbb{M}_{k}
characterises logical equivalence with respect to an appropriate modal logic. Let us extend the multi-modal logic introduced above by adding, for every positive integer
n
and binary relation symbol
R_{\alpha}\in\sigma
,
graded modalities

\Box_{\alpha}^{n}
and
\Diamond_{\alpha}^{n}
defining
A,a\models\Diamond_{\alpha}^{n}\varphi
if there are at least
n
distinct
R_{\alpha}
-successors of
a
that satisfy
\varphi
(and
\Box_{\alpha}^{n}\varphi=\neg\Diamond_{\alpha}^{n}\neg\varphi
). A formula in this graded modal logic has
modal depth
{\leq\,}k
if it contains at most
k
nested modalities. Let
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{ML}}_{k}
be the image of the standard translation of formulas with graded modalities that have modal depth
{\leq\,}k
.
The following result is a special case of
[
3
, Theorem 16]
.

Proposition 29
.

For finite Kripke structures
(A,a)
and
(B,b)
,

(A,a)\equiv_{\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{ML}}_{k}}(B,b)\quad\text{iff}\quad F^{\mathbb{M}_{k}}(A,a)\,\cong\,F^{\mathbb{M}_{k}}(B,b).

We aim to exploit the two preceding propositions to obtain a characterisation of the equivalence relation
\equiv_{\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{ML}}_{k}}
over finite Kripke structures in terms of homomorphism counts from finite synchronization trees of height
{\leq\,}k
(Theorem
31
below).

To this end, let
\mathsf{Kri}_{f}
and
\mathsf{RKri}_{f}
be the full subcategories of
\mathsf{Kri}
consisting, respectively, of the finite Kripke structures, and finite rooted Kripke structures. Note that
\mathbb{M}_{k}
restricts to a comonad on
\mathsf{Kri}_{f}
, that we denote again by
\mathbb{M}_{k}
.
Further, as
\mathbb{M}_{k}(A,a)
is a finite synchronization tree whenever
(A,a)
is a finite Kripke structure,
\mathbb{M}_{k}
restricts to a comonad
\mathbb{M}_{k}^{*}
on the category
\mathsf{RKri}_{f}
.

Lemma 30
.

The following statements hold:

(1)

the inclusion
\mathsf{RKri}_{f}\hookrightarrow\mathsf{Kri}_{f}
has a right adjoint
R
;

(2)

R
lifts to a functor
\overline{R}\colon\mathrm{EM}(\mathbb{M}_{k})\to\mathrm{EM}(\mathbb{M}_{k}^{*})
making the following diagram commute.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{EM}(\mathbb{M}_{k})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{EM}(\mathbb{M}_{k}^{*})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{Kri}_{f}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{RKri}_{f}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{R}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F^{\mathbb{M}_{k}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F^{\mathbb{M}_{k}^{*}}}

Proof.

(1) For any finite Kripke structure
(A,a)
, let
R(A,a)
be the substructure of
(A,a)
consisting of the elements accessible from
a
. To show that this assignment extends to a functor
R
that is a right adjoint to the inclusion
\mathsf{RKri}_{f}\hookrightarrow\mathsf{Kri}_{f}
, it is enough to observe that, for any
(A,a)\in\mathsf{Kri}_{f}
,
(B,b)\in\mathsf{RKri}_{f}
, and homomorphism
h\colon(B,b)\to(A,a)
, there exists a unique homomorphism
h^{\prime}\colon(B,b)\to R(A,a)
such that
h=e\circ h^{\prime}
, where
e\colon R(A,a)\to(A,a)
is the substructure embedding.

(2) Let
\overline{R}
be the functor sending a coalgebra
\alpha\colon(A,a)\to\mathbb{M}_{k}(A,a)
to its restriction
\alpha_{\restriction R(A,a)}\colon R(A,a)\to\mathbb{M}_{k}^{*}R(A,a)
. Then
\overline{R}F^{\mathbb{M}_{k}}(A,a)
coincides with
F^{\mathbb{M}_{k}^{*}}R(A,a)
, for any
(A,a)
in
\mathsf{Kri}_{f}
, because the carrier of
F^{\mathbb{M}_{k}}(A,a)=(\mathbb{M}_{k}(A,a),\delta_{A})
is rooted and consists only of paths of length
{\leq\,}k
that start at
a
. The equality on morphisms holds for the same reason.
∎

We are now ready to prove a Lovász-type theorem for graded modal logic.

Theorem 31
.

Let
(A,a)
and
(B,b)
be finite (pointed) Kripke structures. Then
(A,a)\equiv_{\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{ML}}_{k}}(B,b)
if, and only if,

|\hom_{\mathsf{Kri}_{f}}((C,c),(A,a))|\,=\,|\hom_{\mathsf{Kri}_{f}}((C,c),(B,b))|

for every finite synchronization tree
(C,c)
of height
{\leq\,}k
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{A}\coloneqq(A,a)
and
\mathbb{B}\coloneqq(B,b)
. For the purpose of this proof, write
V\colon\mathsf{RKri}_{f}\hookrightarrow\mathsf{Kri}_{f}
for the inclusion functor and let
\mathrm{EM}^{*}\coloneqq\mathrm{EM}(\mathbb{M}_{k}^{*})
. By Proposition
28
, the statement about homomorphism counts is equivalent to

|\hom_{\mathsf{Kri}_{f}}(V(U^{\mathbb{M}_{k}^{*}}(\mathbb{D})),\mathbb{A})|\,=\,|\hom_{\mathsf{Kri}_{f}}(V(U^{\mathbb{M}_{k}^{*}}(\mathbb{D})),\mathbb{B})|

for all coalgebras
\mathbb{D}
in
\mathrm{EM}^{*}
. In turn, by virtue of the adjunctions
V\dashv R
and
U^{\mathbb{M}_{k}^{*}}\dashv F^{\mathbb{M}_{k}^{*}}
, this is equivalent to

|\hom_{\mathrm{EM}^{*}}(\mathbb{D},F^{\mathbb{M}_{k}^{*}}(R(\mathbb{A})))|\,=\,|\hom_{\mathrm{EM}^{*}}(\mathbb{D},F^{\mathbb{M}_{k}^{*}}(R(\mathbb{B})))|

for all
\mathbb{D}\in\mathrm{EM}^{*}
. Since
\mathrm{EM}^{*}
is combinatorial by Corollary
14
and Remark
15
, the last statement is equivalent to
F^{\mathbb{M}_{k}^{*}}(R(\mathbb{A}))\cong F^{\mathbb{M}_{k}^{*}}(R(\mathbb{B}))
and thus, by Lemma
30
, to
\overline{R}(F^{\mathbb{M}_{k}}(\mathbb{A}))\cong\overline{R}(F^{\mathbb{M}_{k}}(\mathbb{B}))
. Note that the restriction of
\overline{R}
to the image of
F^{\mathbb{M}_{k}}
is full and faithful. Hence,
\overline{R}(F^{\mathbb{M}_{k}}(\mathbb{A}))\cong\overline{R}(F^{\mathbb{M}_{k}}(\mathbb{B}))
if, and only if,
F^{\mathbb{M}_{k}}(\mathbb{A})\cong F^{\mathbb{M}_{k}}(\mathbb{B})
which, by Proposition
29
, is equivalent to
\mathbb{A}\equiv_{\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{ML}}_{k}}\mathbb{B}
.
∎

6.
Logical Normal Forms

Let
\mathcal{C}^{k}_{\infty}
denote the closure of
\mathcal{C}^{k}
under infinitary conjunctions and disjunctions. This infinitary logic has been much studied in finite model theory as a means of delimiting the expressive power of fixed-point logics with counting. In this logic we can express all, and only, the properties of finite structures that are invariant under the equivalence relation
\equiv_{\mathcal{C}^{k}}
. In fact, since it is known that each finite structure is characterised up to
\equiv_{\mathcal{C}^{k}}
by a single sentence of
\mathcal{C}^{k}
, it follows that every class of finite structures that is invariant under
\equiv_{\mathcal{C}^{k}}
is defined by a single infinitary disjunction of
\mathcal{C}^{k}
sentences (see
[
24
]
for details). An entirely analogous normal form holds for
\mathcal{C}_{n,\infty}
—the closure of
\mathcal{C}_{n}
under infinitary conjunctions and disjunctions (see
[
19
, Chapter 8]
).

The theorems of Dvořák and Grohe provide a route to alternative normal forms for these infinitary logics. To see this, note that for any finite structure
A
, we can write a primitive positive sentence
\gamma_{A}
of first-order logic such that for any
B
,
B\models\gamma_{A}
if, and only if, there is a homomorphism from
A
to
B
. Here,
primitive positive
means that the sentence is built up from atomic formulas using only conjunctions and existential quantification. The sentence
\gamma_{A}
is called the
canonical conjunctive query of
A
(see
[
13
, Chapter 6]
). It is known that if
A
has tree-width strictly less than
k
, then
\gamma_{A}
can be written using no more than
k
variables
[
18
, Lemma 5.2]
. Similarly, if
A
has tree-depth at most
n
, then
\gamma_{A}
can be chosen to have quantifier depth at most
n

[
29
, Lemma 2.14]
.

Given a positive integer
t
, we can transform
\gamma_{A}
by standard means into a sentence
\gamma_{A}^{t}
of
\mathcal{C}
which asserts that there are at least
t
distinct homomorphisms from
A
. That is, for any
B
,
B\models\gamma^{t}_{A}
if, and only if,
|\hom(A,B)|\geq t
. To show this, we establish something more general.

Let
\gamma(\bar{x})
be a primitive positive formula with free variables among
\bar{x}
.
For any structure
B
and any interpretation
\iota
taking the variables in
\bar{x}
to elements of
B
, we write
B\models\gamma[\iota]
to indicate that the formula
\gamma
is satisfied in
B
when the free variables
\bar{x}
are interpreted according to
\iota
. Since
\gamma
is primitive positive, we have
B\models\gamma[\iota]
precisely if there is a function
\kappa
mapping the existential quantifiers in
\gamma
to elements of
B
so that every atomic formula
R(\bar{y})
occurring in
\gamma
is satisfied in
B
when each variable
y
in
\bar{y}
is interpreted by
\iota(y)
, if this occurrence of
y
is free in
\gamma
, and by
\kappa
applied to the quantifier binding
y
otherwise. We write
|B\models\gamma[\iota]|
to denote the number of distinct functions
\kappa
witnessing
B\models\gamma[\iota]
and observe that
|B\models\gamma_{A}|=|\hom(A,B)|
.

We now define by induction on the structure of
\gamma
a formula
\gamma^{t}(\bar{x})
of
\mathcal{C}
with the property that for any structure
B
and interpretation
\iota
of
\bar{x}
in
B
, we have
B\models\gamma^{t}[\iota]
if, and only if,
|B\models\gamma[\iota]|\geq t
. For atomic
\gamma
,
\gamma^{1}
is just
\gamma
and
\gamma^{t}
is
false
if
t>1
. For
\gamma
a conjunction
\gamma_{1}\land\gamma_{2}
, let
T\coloneqq\{(t_{1},t_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}\mid t_{1}\cdot t_{2}\geq t\}
. Then

\gamma^{t}\coloneqq\bigvee_{(t_{1},t_{2})\in T}(\gamma^{t_{1}}_{1}\land\gamma^{t_{2}}_{2}).

When
\gamma
is
\exists x.\gamma_{1}
, let
F
be the collection of all finite partial functions
f
on
\mathbb{N}
such that
\sum_{s\in\mathrm{dom}(f)}sf(s)\geq t
. Then

\gamma^{t}\coloneqq\bigvee_{f\in F}\bigwedge_{s\in\mathrm{dom}(f)}\exists_{\geq f(s)}x.\gamma_{1}^{s}.

Note that both the quantifier rank and the total number of variables that appear in
\gamma^{t}_{A}
are the same as for
\gamma_{A}
, and
\gamma^{t}_{A}
still has no negation symbols. It then follows from Dvořák’s theorem that any sentence
\varphi
of
\mathcal{C}^{k}_{\infty}
is equivalent in the finite (i.e., over finite structures) to an infinite Boolean combination of sentences of
\mathcal{C}^{k}
of the form
\gamma^{t}_{A}
. Indeed,
\varphi
is equivalent to

\bigvee_{B\in\mathcal{M}}\ \bigwedge_{A\in\mathcal{T}}\left(\gamma^{|\hom(A,B)|}_{A}\land\neg\gamma^{|\hom(A,B)|+1}_{A}\right)

where
\mathcal{M}
consists of one representative for each
\equiv_{\mathcal{C}^{k}}
-class of structures in
\{B\mid B\models\varphi\}
and
\mathcal{T}
consists of one representative for each isomorphism class of finite structures that have tree-width at most
k-1
.

Likewise, it is a consequence of Grohe’s theorem that any sentence of
\mathcal{C}_{n,\infty}
is equivalent (in the finite) to an infinite Boolean combination of sentences of
\mathcal{C}_{n}
of the form
\gamma^{t}_{A}
; a similar normal form was also exhibited in
[
14
, §5]
. This yields an interesting normal form for these counting logics. In particular, there is no need of universal quantifiers or of equality, as neither of these is used in the sentences
\gamma^{t}_{A}
. Further, if we allow dual counting quantifiers
\exists_{{\leq}i}
with the semantics that
A\models\exists_{{\leq}i}x.\,\varphi
if, and only if,
A\models\varphi[a/x]
holds for at most
i
distinct elements
a\in A
, then we do not need negation at all in our formulas.

From our point of view, the most interesting aspect of this is the elimination of equality symbols. Consider for example the simple first-order sentence in the language of graphs:
\exists x\exists y.(x\neq y\land E(x,y))
asserting the existence of an edge between two distinct vertices. At first sight, it does not seem possible to express this property without using the equality symbol. However, it is possible to do so with infinitary Boolean connectives and counting quantifiers as we illustrate.

First, consider the formula
\exists_{\geq t}xy.E(x,y)
asserting that there exist at least
t
pairs
x,y
for which
E(x,y)
. Note this is just
\gamma^{t}
when
\gamma
is the sentence
\exists x\exists y.E(x,y)
. Now, the sentence
\exists x\exists y.(x\neq y\land E(x,y))
is equivalent to

\bigvee_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\exists_{\geq i}xy.E(x,y)\land\exists_{\leq i-1}x.E(x,x)\right).

We now give a direct proof, in our categorical framework, that we can eliminate equality from
\mathcal{C}^{k}_{\infty}
and
\mathcal{C}_{n,\infty}
.

Theorem 32
.

Every sentence of
\mathcal{C}^{k}_{\infty}
is equivalent in the finite to one without equality, and every sentence of
\mathcal{C}_{n,\infty}
is equivalent in the finite to one without equality.

Proof.

We give the proof only for
\mathcal{C}_{n,\infty}
as the other is entirely analogous. It suffices to prove that if two finite structures
A,B
are not distinguished by any sentence of
\mathcal{C}_{n}
without equality, then they are not distinguished by any sentence of
\mathcal{C}_{n}
.

Suppose then that
A
and
B
are not distinguished by any sentence of
\mathcal{C}_{n}
without equality. We thus have
F^{\mathbb{E}_{n}}(A)\cong F^{\mathbb{E}_{n}}(B)
in
\mathrm{EM}(\mathbb{E}_{n})
and so
\hom(D,F^{\mathbb{E}_{n}}(A))\cong\hom(D,F^{\mathbb{E}_{n}}(B))
for every
D
in
\mathrm{EM}_{f}(\mathbb{E}_{n})
. By the adjunction
U^{\mathbb{E}_{n}}\dashv F^{\mathbb{E}_{n}}
, we have
\hom(U^{\mathbb{E}_{n}}(D),A)\cong\hom(U^{\mathbb{E}_{n}}(D),B)
for every
D
in
\mathrm{EM}_{f}(\mathbb{E}_{n})
. Since a finite structure has tree-depth at most
n
if, and only if, it admits an
\mathbb{E}_{n}
-coalgebra structure, this implies
\hom(C,A)\cong\hom(C,B)
for every finite structure
C
with tree-depth at most
n
and hence, by Theorem
18
,
A\equiv_{\mathcal{C}_{n}}B
.
∎

7.
Conclusion

The ideas developed in this paper connect homomorphism counting results in finite model theory with the framework of game comonads recently put forward by Abramsky et al.

Our categorical generalisation of Lovász’ theorem allows us to give uniform proofs of results by Dvořák and Grohe. The only part of the proof specific to each of these results is the combinatorial argument underlying the fact that the adjunction between
\sigma
-structures and
\sigma^{+}
-structures restricts to structures of bounded tree-width and bounded tree-depth, respectively. We also establish a new homomorphism counting result for modal logic which characterises the equivalence relation
\equiv_{\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{ML}}_{k}}
over finite Kripke structures in terms of homomorphism counts.

As a by-product of the modularity of this general framework, we are immediately able to deduce: (i) the characterisation of the equivalence relation
\equiv_{\mathcal{C}^{k}_{n}}
in terms of homomorphism counts from structures admitting a
k
-pebble forest cover of height
{\leq\,}n
, (ii) the specialisation of our results to undirected graphs without loops (cf. Remark
24
), and (iii) equality elimination results for counting logics (Theorem
32
).

We believe that our method lays a pathway to discovering more Lovász-type theorems. In particular, any comonad on the category of
\sigma
-structures that satisfies the conditions of Theorem
16
will yield a Lovász-type theorem. The natural next step to test this theory is to apply our results to the game comonads introduced in
[
2
]
and
[
10
]
.

On the other hand, there are many Lovász-type theorems that do not immediately fit into our framework. For example, quantum isomorphism
[
23
]
and co-spectrality are characterised by restricting the homomorphism counts to planar graphs and cycles, respectively. Are there suitable comonads (or monads) that could allow us to recover these characterisations in a uniform manner? Another line for future investigation consists in fine-tuning the general theory introduced in this paper. For example, can we refine Theorem
31
to yield a homomorphism counting result for p-morphisms, the natural concept of morphism between Kripke structures?

The ideas that we have described in this paper contribute to advancing the theory of game comonads, which connects two distinct areas of logic in computer science: on the one hand finite and algorithmic model theory, and on the other hand categorical and structural methods in semantics. Game comonads have been successfully applied, e.g., to investigate equirank-variable homomorphism preservation theorems in finite model theory
[
25
]
. We broaden the applicability of this theory by bringing these techniques to the area of Lovász-type results in finite model theory.

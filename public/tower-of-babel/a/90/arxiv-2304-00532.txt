From actions of an abelian group on itself to left braces
A. Ballester-Bolinches, R. Esteban-Romero, L. A. Kurdachenko, V. Pérez-Calabuig

Abstract
We present a construction of left braces of right nilpotency class at most two based on suitable actions of an abelian group on itself with an invariance condition. This construction allows us to recover the construction of a free right nilpotent one-generated left brace of class two. Mathematics Subject Classification (2020): 16T25, 16N40, 81R50 Keywords: left brace, right nilpotent left brace, one-generated left brace, Smoktunowicz-nilpotent left brace.

Introduction

A
skew left brace

(A,{+},{\cdot})
consists of a set
A
with two binary operations such that
(A,{+})
and
(A,{\cdot})
are groups related by the modified distributive law
a(b+c)=ab-a+ac
for all
a
,
b
,
c\in A
. If, in addition, the group
(A,{+})
is abelian, we speak of a
skew left brace of abelian type
or, simply, a
left brace
. When the operations
+
and
\cdot
are clear from the context, we will refer to the left brace
(A,{+},{\cdot})
simply as
A
. The structure of left brace was introduced by Rump as a generalisation of Jacobson radical rings in
[
11
]
. Guarnieri and Vendramin
[
8
]
introduced skew left braces by removing the commutativity of the addition in Rump’s left braces. An interesting survey on left braces can be found in the paper of Cedó
[
5
]
, where most of the definitions and results presented in this section can be found.

The structure of a left brace depends critically on the lambda action of the multiplicative group on the additive group. Let
a
,
b
be elements of a left brace
A
. We define
\lambda_{a}(b)=-a+ab
. It is well known that
\lambda\colon(A,{\cdot})\longrightarrow\Aut(A,{+})
given by
\lambda(a)=\lambda_{a}
for
a\in A
is a group homomorphism.

Another interesting operation that gives structural information on a left brace is the star operation. We define it as
a*b=-a+ab-b=\lambda_{a}(b)-b
for
a
,
b\in B
. Given two subsets
L
and
M
of
A
, we define
L*M
as the subgroup of the additive group generated by
\{l*m\mid l\in L,m\in M\}
. We set
A^{(1)}=A
,
A^{(r+1)}=A^{(r)}*A
for a natural
r
, and
A^{1}=A
,
A^{r+1}=A*A^{r}
for a natural
r
.

These constructions allow us to define two different nilpotencies for left braces: we say that a left brace
A
is
left nilpotent
if there exists a natural
m
such that
A^{m+1}=\{0\}
, and that
A
is
right nilpotent
if there exists a natural
m
such that
A^{(m+1)}=\{0\}
. If
A
is right nilpotent (respectively, left nilpotent), the smallest
m
satisfying
A^{(m+1)}=\{0\}
, but
A^{(m)}\neq\{0\}
(respectively,
A^{m+1}=\{0\}
, but
A^{m}\neq\{0\}
) is called the
right
(respectively,
left
)
nilpotency class
of
A
. A left brace that is both left nilpotent and right nilpotent is said to be
nilpotent in the sense of Smoktunowicz
or, simply,
Smoktunowicz-nilpotent
. These left braces were studied by Smoktunowicz in
[
13
]
. For left braces, this concept coincides with the central nilpotency introduced in
[
2
]
for skew left braces (see also
[
9
]
).

We say that a subset
S
of a left brace
(A,{+},{\cdot})
is a
subbrace
of
(A,{+},{\cdot})
if
S
is a subgroup of
(A,{+})
and of
(A,{\cdot})
. The intersection of a family of subbraces of a given left brace
(A,{+},{\cdot})
is again a subbrace of
(A,{+},{\cdot})
. If
X
is a subset of
(A,{+},{\cdot})
, the intersection of all subbraces of
(A,{+},{\cdot})
is a subbrace of
(A,{+},{\cdot})
called the
subbrace generated by

X
. When
X=\{a\}
is a singleton, we simply speak of the
subbrace generated by
a
. The description of the subbrace generated by a set or the free object on a set
X
in the category of left braces seems to be a difficult problem in general. In fact, Agata and Alicja Smoktunowicz ask about such description in the class of left braces of right nilpotency class at most two
[
14
, Question 5.5]
.

Stefanello and Trappeniers
[
15
, Theorem 3.13]
have given some interesting characterisations of left braces of right nilpotency class at most two. We present here their results in the form we need in the sequel.

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
A
be a left brace. The following statements are equivalent in pairs:

1.

A^{(3)}=\{0\}
.

2.

If
z\in A^{(2)}
and
a\in A
, then
z*a=0
.

3.

A^{(2)}\subseteq\Ker\lambda
.

4.

\lambda
is a group homomorphism between
(A,{+})
and
\Aut(A,{+})
.

5.

\lambda_{\lambda_{a}(b)}=\lambda_{b}
for every
a
,
b\in A
.

The left braces satisfying Condition
4
of Proposition
1.1
have been studied by Bardakov, Neshchadim, and Yadav in
[
1
]
, who called them
\lambda
-homomorphic
. They turn out to be examples of
bi-skew left braces
or
symmetric skew left braces
, that is, skew left braces
(A,{+},{\cdot})
for which
(A,{\cdot},{+})
is again a skew left brace, studied for the first time by Childs in
[
6
]
and later by Caranti
[
3
]
, Koch
[
10
]
, and Caranti and Stefanello
[
4
]
. We will prove Proposition
1.1
in Section
2
for the sake of completeness.

A subbrace
S
of a left brace
A
is called a
left ideal
of
A
if
A*S\subseteq S
. This is equivalent to saying that
\lambda_{a}(S)\subseteq S
for each
a\in S
. A subbrace
S
of a left brace
A
is called an
ideal
of
A
if
A*S\subseteq S
and
S*A\subseteq A
. This is equivalent to saying that
S
is a left ideal of
A
and
S
is a normal subgroup of the multiplicative group of
S
. We obtain that
A^{(r)}
is an ideal of
A
for all naturals

and that
A^{r}
is a left ideal of
A
for all naturals
r
. The
socle
of
A
, defined as the kernel of
\lambda
in the case of left braces, is another example of an ideal of
A
.

The first result of this paper is a general construction of a left brace from an abelian group
(A,{+})
and an action of
(A,{+})
on itself that satisfies an invariance condition from which the universal constructions related to one-generated subbraces of right nilpotent left braces of class at most two directly follow.

Theorem A
.

Suppose that
(B,{+})
is an abelian group acting on itself by means of an action
\lambda\colon(B,{+})\longrightarrow\Aut(B,{+})
given by
\lambda(x)=\lambda_{x}
for
x\in B
. Suppose also that if
x
,
y\in B
, then
\lambda_{\lambda_{y}(x)}=\lambda_{x}
. We can define a product
\cdot
on
B
by means of
xy=x+\lambda_{x}(y)
for
x
,
y\in B
. Then
(B,{+},{\cdot})
is a left brace with
B^{(3)}=\{0\}
.

As a consequence of Proposition
1.1
, all left braces of right nilpotency class two can be described in this way.

Rump
[
12
]
, answering the above-mentioned question of Smoktunowicz and Smoktunowicz, has given a description of the free one-generated right nilpotent left brace of class two and a description of subbrace generated by an element of a right nilpotent left brace of class at most two.

Theorem B

(Rump
[
12
]
)
.

Let
(A,{+},{\cdot})
be a left brace with
A^{(3)}=\{0\}
. Let
a\in A
. For
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, we define
a_{i}=\lambda_{a^{i}}(a)=-a^{i}+a^{i+1}
. In particular,
a_{0}=-a^{0}+a^{1}=-0+a=a
. We observe that
\lambda_{a^{j}}(a_{i})=\lambda_{a^{j}}(\lambda_{a^{i}}(a))=\lambda_{a^{j}a^{i}}(a)=\lambda_{a^{i+j}}(a)=a_{i+j}
.
The set

B=\Bigl\{\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}x_{i}a_{i}\mid x_{i}\in\mathbb{Z},i\in\mathbb{Z},\text{ and $\{i\in\mathbb{Z}\mid x_{i}\neq 0\}$ is finite}\Bigr\}.

coincides with the subbrace of
A
generated by
a\in A
.
Furthermore, if
D=\{\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}x_{i}a_{i}\in B\mid\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}x_{i}=0\}
, then
B*B=D
.

Theorem C

(see Rump
[
12
]
)
.

Let
C
be a free abelian group with basis
\{c_{i}\mid i\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. Given
x\in C
, then
x=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}x_{i}c_{i}
with
x_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
and
x_{i}\neq 0
for a finite number of

. We define an action of

on

by means of
\lambda_{x}(y)=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}y_{i}c_{i+\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}x_{j}}=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}y_{i-\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}x_{j}}c_{i}
for

(C,{+},{\cdot})
with the multiplication given by

for

is a left brace generated by

with

and

for all

. Moreover, if

is a left brace with

and

from

The lambda map of a skew left brace is always an action from the multiplicative group of the left brace on the additive group. Our construction of left braces in Theorem
A
is based on an action from the additive group on itself. The following result shows the relation between both actions.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(A,{+})
be an abelian group,
\lambda\colon A\longrightarrow\Aut(A,{+})
and define a binary operation
\cdot
on
A
by means of
ab=a+\lambda_{a}(b)
(this happens when
\lambda
is the map defined on a left brace
(A,{+},{\cdot})
by
\lambda_{a}(b)=-a+ab
for
a
,
b\in A
). If two of the following three statements hold, then so does the other one:

1.

\lambda_{a+b}=\lambda_{a}\circ\lambda_{b}
for every
a
,
b\in A
(that is,
\lambda\colon(A,{+})\longrightarrow\Aut(A,{+})
is a group homomorphism).

2.

\lambda_{ab}=\lambda_{a}\circ\lambda_{b}
for every
a
,
b\in A
.

3.

\lambda_{\lambda_{a}(b)}=\lambda_{b}
for every
a
,
b\in A
.

Proof.

1
and
2
imply
3
. Since
\lambda_{a}\circ\lambda_{b}=\lambda_{ab}=\lambda_{a+\lambda_{a}(b)}=\lambda_{a}\circ\lambda_{\lambda_{a}(b)}
, we conclude that
\lambda_{b}=\lambda_{\lambda_{a}(b)}
.

2
and
3
imply
1
. We have that

\lambda_{a+b}=\lambda_{a\lambda_{a}^{-1}(b)}=\lambda_{a}\circ\lambda_{\lambda_{a}^{-1}(b)}=\lambda_{a}\circ\lambda_{\lambda_{a}(\lambda_{a}^{-1}(b))}=\lambda_{a}\circ\lambda_{b}
.

3

2

\lambda_{ab}=\lambda_{a+\lambda_{a}(b)}=\lambda_{a}\circ\lambda_{\lambda_{a}(b)}=\lambda_{a}\circ\lambda_{b}
.

Some of the results of Section

\binom{n}{0}=1,\qquad\binom{n}{k}=\frac{n(n-1)\dotsm(n-k+1)}{k!}\quad\text{if $k>0$}.

Some of the well-known identities of the usual binomial coefficients are also satisfied by the generalised binomial coefficients. The next lemma summarises some of these identities (see, for example,
[

Lemma 2.2
.

1.

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and

\binom{n}{k}+\binom{n}{k+1}=\binom{n+1}{k+1}.

2.

(Chu-Vandermonde’s Theorem) Let
n
and

be integer numbers and

\binom{n+t}{r}=\sum_{k=0}^{r}\binom{n}{k}\binom{t}{r-k}.

The following properties of the star operation can be found, for instance, in

Proposition 2.6
.

Let

We prove this proposition by induction on

Then, by using Lemma

\displaystyle a^{n+1}

\displaystyle=a+a*a^{n}+a^{n}

\displaystyle=a+a*\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}a_{k}\right)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}a_{k}

\displaystyle=a+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(a*a_{k})+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}a_{k}

\displaystyle=a+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}a_{k+1}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}a_{k}

\displaystyle=a+\sum_{k=2}^{n+1}\binom{n}{k-1}a_{k}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}a_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\binom{n}{k-1}a_{k}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}a_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\binom{n+1}{k}a_{k}.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
A
be a left brace such that
A^{(3)}=\{0\}
. Let
a
be an element of

. Write

,

(na)*a_{j}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}a_{k+j}=\binom{n}{1}a_{j+1}+\binom{n}{2}a_{j+2}+\dots+\binom{n}{n-1}a_{j+n-1}+a_{j+n}.

By Proposition

\displaystyle(a^{n+1})*a_{j}

\displaystyle=(aa^{n})*a_{j}

\displaystyle=a*(a^{n}*a_{j})+a^{n}*a_{j}+a*a_{j}

\displaystyle=a*\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}a_{k+j}\right)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}a_{k+j}+a_{j+1}

Proof of Theorem

We show first that
(B,{\cdot})
is a group. Clearly,

\displaystyle(xy)z

\displaystyle=xy+\lambda_{xy}(z)=(x+\lambda_{x}(y))+\lambda_{x+\lambda_{x}(y)}(z)=x+\lambda_{x}(y)+\lambda_{x}(\lambda_{\lambda_{x}(y)}(z))

\displaystyle=x+\lambda_{x}(y)+\lambda_{x}(\lambda_{y}(z))=x+\lambda_{x}(y+\lambda_{y}(z))=x+\lambda_{x}(yz)=x(yz).

It follows that
\cdot
is associative. Furthermore,
0x=0+\lambda_{0}(x)=0+x=x
and

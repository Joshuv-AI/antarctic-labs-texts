Endomorphisms of Equivariant Algebraic K K -theory
K Arun Kumar and Girja S Tripathi

Abstract
. In this paper we study equivariant algebraic K K -theory for an action of a finite constant group scheme in the equivariant motivic homotopy theory. We prove that the equivariant algebraic K K -theory is represented by an equivariant ind-scheme defined by Grassmannians. Using this result we show that in the category of equivariant motivic spaces the set of endomorphisms of the motivic space defined by K 0 ​ ( G , − ) K_{0}(G,-) coincides with the set of endomorphisms of infinite Grassmannians in the equivariant motivic homotopy category. To this end we explicitly recall the folklore computation of equivariant K K -theory of Grassmannians.

Theorem 5.2

(Projective bundle theorem)
.

Let

be a
G
-equivariant
S
-scheme and let

be a

We can go further and describe

-algebra along the same lines as the non-equivariant case.

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
(X,\sigma)
and
(\mathcal{E},\tau)
be as above. The homotopy equivalence
K(G,X,\sigma)^{n}\xrightarrow{\sim}K(G,\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}),p(\tau))
given in Theorem

induces an isomorphism

of

-algebras.

Proof.

First by Theorem
5.2
we have a commutative diagram of
K_{0}(G,X,\sigma)
-modules

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{0}(G,X,\sigma)^{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{*}(G,X,\sigma)^{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{0}(G,\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E},p(\tau))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{*}(G,\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E},p(\tau))}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

where the vertical maps are isomorphisms and the bottom map is a ring homomorphism. Using the extension-restriction adjunction with respect to the ring homomorphism

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{*}(G,X,\sigma)\otimes_{K_{0}(G,X,\sigma)}K_{0}(G,X,\sigma)^{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{*}(G,X,\sigma)^{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{*}(G,X,\sigma)\otimes_{K_{0}(G,X,\sigma)}K_{0}(G,\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E},p(\tau)))}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

of

Along the lines of the non-equivariant case we can give an explicit description of the ring

using
\lambda
-operations. For any
(X,\sigma)
let the

[(\mathcal{E},\tau)]\mapsto[(\wedge^{n}\mathcal{E},\wedge^{n}\tau)]

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
(X,\sigma)
be a
G
-equivariant
S
-scheme and let
(\mathcal{E},\tau)
be a
G
-equivariant vector bundle over
(X,\sigma)
of constant rank
n
. Let
f:\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})\to X
be the corresponding projective bundle. The
K_{0}(G,X,\sigma)
-algebra homomorphism
c:K_{0}(G,X,\sigma)[t]\to K_{0}(G,\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}),p(\tau))
given by
t\mapsto[\mathcal{O}(-1)]
induces an isomorphism of rings,

\displaystyle K_{0}(G,X,\sigma)[t]/(\theta)\cong K_{0}(G,\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}),p(\tau)),

\displaystyle\mathrm{where}\>\>\theta=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}\lambda^{n-i}[(\mathcal{E},\tau)]t^{i}

and hence by Corollary
5.3
we get an isomorphism of graded
K_{*}(G,X,\sigma)
-algebras,

K_{*}(G,X,\sigma)[t]/(\theta)\cong K_{*}(G,\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}),p(\tau)).

Proof.

Let
c(\mathcal{E},\tau)=[(f^{*}\mathcal{E},f^{*}\tau)]
and
t=[\mathcal{O}(-1)]
in
K_{0}(G,\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}),p(\tau))
. Consider the element
(c(\mathcal{E},\tau)-t\in K_{0}(G,\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}),p(\tau))
. We have
(c(\mathcal{E},\tau)-t)=[\mathcal{F},\hat{\tau}]
where
\mathcal{F}
is the quotient,

\mathcal{O}(-1)\rightarrowtail f^{*}\mathcal{E}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{F}

over
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})
and hence
\lambda^{n}(c(\mathcal{E},\tau)-t)=[(\wedge^{n}\mathcal{F},\wedge^{n}\hat{\tau})]=0

0=\lambda^{n}(c(\mathcal{E},\tau)-t)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\lambda^{i}(c(\mathcal{E},\tau))\lambda^{n-i}(-t).

in
K_{0}(G,\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}))

\lambda^{1}(t)=t
and
\lambda^{i}(t)=0
for

\lambda

is a free

-module with basis

. Hence we are done.

(X,\sigma)

G

S

be a
G

We can use this result to compute the K-theory ring of any Grassmannian.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
f:Gr(r,\mathcal{E})\to X
be a Grassmannian over any scheme
X
with canonical subbundle
\mathcal{U}_{r}\hookrightarrow f^{*}\mathcal{E}
. The
K_{*}(G,X)
-algebra

is faithfuly flat and generated by
\lambda^{i}=[\Lambda^{i}\mathcal{U}_{r}]
, for
1\leq i\leq r
, in
K_{0}(G,Gr(r,\mathcal{E}))

Proof.

Given a Grassmannian
Gr(r,\mathcal{E})
, the projective bundle
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{U}_{r})\to Gr(r,\mathcal{E})\to X
is the flag bundle
Flag((1,r,n-r-1),\mathcal{E})\to X
. We can than recursively construct a sequence

X\leftarrow Gr(r,\mathcal{E})\leftarrow Flag((1,r,n-r-1),\mathcal{E})\leftarrow Flag((1,1,r,n-r-2),\mathcal{E})\leftarrow\ldots Flag((1^{r},\mathcal{E}))

of flag bundles. Let the canonical flag over
Flag((1^{r},\mathcal{E}))
be given by

F_{1}\hookrightarrow F_{2}\hookrightarrow\ldots\hookrightarrow F_{r}\hookrightarrow f^{*}\mathcal{E}

with
F_{r}=\mathcal{U}_{r}
. By Corollary
5.5
the morphism
K_{*}(G,Gr(r,\mathcal{E}))\to K_{*}(G,Flag(1^{r},\mathcal{E}))
is an inclusion and
K_{*}(G,Flag(1^{r},\mathcal{E}))
is generated as a
K_{*}(G,Gr(r,\mathcal{E}))
-algebra by
t_{i}=[F_{i}]-[F_{i-1}]
for
0<i\leq r
. Also, the image of
\lambda^{i}=[\Lambda^{i}\mathcal{U}_{r}]
in
K_{*}(G,Flag(1^{r},\mathcal{E}))
is the
i^{th}
elementary symmetric polynomial
e_{i}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})
. We therefore get a morphism
K_{*}(G,X)[e_{1},\ldots,e_{r}]\to K_{*}(G,Gr(r,\mathcal{E}))
such that the following diagram commutes

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{*}(G,X)[e_{1},\ldots,e_{r}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{*}(G,X)[t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{*}(G,Gr(r,\mathcal{E}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{*}(G,Flag(1^{r},\mathcal{E}))}

where the right vertical map is a quotient map. For any ring
R
the polynomial ring

is a free module over the ring of symmetric polynomials

with basis

\{t_{1}^{a_{1}}t_{2}^{a_{2}}\ldots t_{r}^{a_{r}}|0\leq a_{i}\leq r-i,i\leq r\}

which is precisely the basis of
K_{*}(G,Flag(1^{r},\mathcal{E}))
as a

-module. Hence the left vertical map is a surjection. Faithfully flatness follows from the two out of three property.

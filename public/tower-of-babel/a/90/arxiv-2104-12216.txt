Integrals of incomplete beta functions, with applications to order statistics, random walks and string enumeration
Stephen B. Connor, Christopher J. Fewster

Abstract
We study the probability that one beta-distributed random variable exceeds the maximum of two others, allowing all three to have general parameters. This amounts to studying Euler transforms of products of two incomplete beta functions. We provide a closed form for the general problem in terms of Kampé de Fériet functions and a variety of simpler closed forms in special cases. The results are applied to derive the moments of the maximum of two independent beta-distributed random variables and to find inner products of incomplete beta functions. Restricted to positive integer parameters, our results are applied to determine an expected exit time for a conditioned random walk and also to a combinatorial problem of enumerating strings comprised of three different letters, subject to constraints. Keywords: incomplete beta function, Kampé de Fériet functions, Euler transform, order statistics, string enumeration, generalized Whipple identity, generalized Dixon identity, exit time. Mathematics Subject Classification (2020): Primary 33C20; Secondary 05A15, 05A10

Introduction

In this note we investigate and document various integrals of incomplete beta functions,
of interest in their own right, which have applications to probability theory and combinatorics. Among other things, we will provide formulae for: (a) all moments of the maximum of two independent random variables following beta distributions with arbitrary parameters, (b) inner products of incomplete beta functions, and (c) solutions to a combinatorial problem involving the enumeration of strings of certain type.

For parameters
\mu,\mu^{\prime}>0
, we write
X_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}}\sim{\sf Beta}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
for the random variable with density proportional to
s^{\mu-1}(1-s)^{\mu^{\prime}-1}
for
s\in[0,1]
. The cumulative distribution function of
X_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}}
is given for
t\in[0,1]
by the regularized incomplete beta function

I(\mu,\mu^{\prime};t)=\mathbb{P}(X_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}}\leq t)=\frac{1}{B(\mu,\mu^{\prime})}\int_{0}^{t}s^{\mu-1}(1-s)^{\mu^{\prime}-1}\,ds\,,

where
B(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
is the beta function

B(\mu,\mu^{\prime})=\frac{\Gamma(\mu)\Gamma(\mu^{\prime})}{\Gamma(\mu+\mu^{\prime})}=\int_{0}^{1}s^{\mu-1}(1-s)^{\mu^{\prime}-1}\,ds\,.

We are interested in calculating integrals of the following type:

\mathcal{B}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},\mu,\mu^{\prime},\nu,\nu^{\prime}):=\frac{1}{B(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})}\int_{0}^{1}t^{\lambda-1}(1-t)^{\lambda^{\prime}-1}I(\mu,\mu^{\prime};t)I(\nu,\nu^{\prime};t)\,dt\,,

(1)

and will provide a closed form for the general case, in terms of Kampé de Fériet functions
(
Kampé de
Fériet 1921a
;
Kampé de
Fériet 1921b
)
, and a variety of special cases in terms of
hypergeometric and elementary functions.
When the six parameters are all positive reals, the integrals converge and have the probabilistic interpretation

\mathcal{B}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},\mu,\mu^{\prime},\nu,\nu^{\prime})=\mathbb{P}(X_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}>\max\{Y_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}},Z_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}\})\,,

(2)

where
X_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}},Y_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}},Z_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}
are independent beta random variables, as is clear on noting that
I(\mu,\mu^{\prime};t)I(\nu,\nu^{\prime};t)
is the cumulative distribution function for
\max\{Y_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}},Z_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}\}
. In consequence the following identities are obvious:

\mathcal{B}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},\mu,\mu^{\prime},\nu,\nu^{\prime})=\mathcal{B}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},\nu,\nu^{\prime},\mu,\mu^{\prime})

(3)

and

\mathcal{B}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},\mu,\mu^{\prime},\nu,\nu^{\prime})+\mathcal{B}(\mu,\mu^{\prime},\nu,\nu^{\prime},\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})+\mathcal{B}(\nu,\nu^{\prime},\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},\mu,\mu^{\prime})=1\,.

(4)

Furthermore, setting
\lambda^{\prime}=1
yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(\lambda,1,\mu,\mu^{\prime},\nu,\nu^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\lambda\int_{0}^{1}t^{\lambda-1}I(\mu,\mu^{\prime};t)I(\nu,\nu^{\prime};t)\,dt

\displaystyle=1-\int_{0}^{1}t^{\lambda}\,\frac{d}{dt}\left(I(\mu,\mu^{\prime};t)I(\nu,\nu^{\prime};t)\right)\,dt

\displaystyle=1-\mathbb{E}[\max\{Y_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}},Z_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}\}^{\lambda}]\,,

(5)

and so all moments of the random variable
\max\{Y_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}},Z_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}\}
may be deduced from the family of integrals in (
1
). Thanks to the invariance of the beta distribution under the transformation
(s,\mu)\leftrightarrow(1-s,\mu^{\prime})
,
\mathcal{B}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},\mu,\mu^{\prime},\nu,\nu^{\prime})
may equivalently be expressed as

\mathcal{B}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},\mu,\mu^{\prime},\nu,\nu^{\prime})=\mathbb{P}(X_{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda}<\min\{Y_{\mu^{\prime},\mu},Z_{\nu^{\prime},\nu}\})\,;

(6)

hence our results can just as well be used to determine properties of the minimum of two independent beta random variables.

A closed form for
\mathcal{B}(\lambda,1,\mu,\mu^{\prime},\nu,\nu^{\prime})

{}_{3}F_{2}

Proposition 1
.

Let
Y_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}}
and
Z_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}
be independent beta random variables as defined above. Then for any
\lambda\geq 0
the
\lambda

\max\{Y_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}},Z_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}\}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\max\{Y_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}},Z_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}\}^{\lambda}]

\displaystyle=\frac{B(\lambda+\mu+\nu,\mu^{\prime})}{\nu B(\mu,\mu^{\prime})B(\nu,\nu^{\prime})}\,{}_{3}F_{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1-\nu^{\prime},\nu,\lambda+\mu+\nu\\
\nu+1,\mu^{\prime}+\lambda+\mu+\nu\end{array};1\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{B(\lambda+\mu+\nu,\nu^{\prime})}{\mu B(\mu,\mu^{\prime})B(\nu,\nu^{\prime})}\,{}_{3}F_{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1-\mu^{\prime},\mu,\lambda+\mu+\nu\\
\mu+1,\nu^{\prime}+\lambda+\mu+\nu\end{array};1\right)\,.

The random variable
\max\{Y_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}},Z_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}}\}
does not follow a standard distribution, yet particular cases of the integral in (
1

For example, the Program Evaluation and Review Technique (PERT) is a widely-used method for analysing the time-to-completion of a project involving multiple subtasks. Independent beta random variables are commonly used to model the time taken for each individual subtask to be completed; the maximum of two such distributions is then sometimes approximated by another beta distribution with (numerically approximated) matching first and second moments
(
Sculli and
Wong 1985
)

Calculating properties of the maximum of a set of independent random variables falls, of course, under the more general umbrella of determining their order statistics. In the case of beta random variables, methods are provided in
Thomas and
Samuel 2008
and
Abdelkader 2010
for computing moments of order statistics in the case where the parameters of the beta distribution(s) are all integer-valued. However, the resulting equations are either recursive
(
Thomas and
Samuel 2008
)
or quite complicated nested sums of ‘inclusion exclusion’ type
(
Abdelkader 2010
)

Samuel 2008

{\sf Beta}(m,n)

The work of

In that setting it is shown that the moments of beta order statistics may be expressed as a sum of Kampé de Fériet functions.

By contrast, in Section
2.2

\mathbb{E}[\max\{Y_{\mu,\nu},Z_{\mu,\nu}\}]=1-\mathcal{B}(1,1,\mu,\nu,\mu,\nu)=\frac{\mu}{\mu+\nu}+\frac{2B(2\mu,2\nu)}{(\mu+\nu)B(\mu,\nu)^{2}}

(12)

Cordeiro

beta beta distribution

\frac{t^{\mu-1}(1-t)^{\mu^{\prime}-1}}{B(\mu,\mu^{\prime})B(a,b)}I(\mu,\mu^{\prime};t)^{a-1}(1-I(\mu,\mu^{\prime};t))^{b-1}\,,\quad t\in[0,1].

\displaystyle\frac{1}{6}\mathbb{E}[W^{\lambda}]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{\lambda+\mu-1}(1-t)^{\mu^{\prime}-1}}{B(\mu,\mu^{\prime})}I(\mu,\mu^{\prime};t)(1-I(\mu,\mu^{\prime};t))\,dt

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(Y_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}}<X_{\lambda+\mu,\mu^{\prime}}<Z_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(Y_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}}<X_{\lambda+\mu,\mu^{\prime}})-\mathcal{B}(\lambda+\mu,\mu^{\prime},\mu,\mu^{\prime},\mu,\mu^{\prime})\,,

and these two terms may be computed using results derived below (Remark

For any set of positive real-valued arguments, the integral

Main results

2.1
General formulae

We begin by recalling some basic facts concerning generalised hypergeometric functions, defined as usual by

{}_{p}F_{q}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}a_{1},\dots,a_{p}\\
b_{1},\dots,b_{q}\end{array};t\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a_{1})_{k}\dots(a_{p})_{k}}{(b_{1})_{k}\dots(b_{q})_{k}}\,\frac{t^{k}}{k!}\,,

(19)

where the Pochhammer symbol
(a)_{k}=a(a+1)\cdots(a+k-1)
denotes the
k^{th}
rising factorial of
a
. The Euler transform (
NIST
, Eq.16.5.2
) states that

\int_{0}^{1}u^{c-1}(1-u)^{d-c-1}\,{}_{p}F_{q}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}a_{1},\dots,a_{p}\\
b_{1},\dots,b_{q}\end{array};tu\right)\,du=\frac{\Gamma(c)\Gamma(d-c)}{\Gamma(d)}\,{}_{p+1}F_{q+1}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}a_{1},\dots,a_{p},c\\
b_{1},\dots,b_{q},d\end{array};t\right)\,.

(20)

Starting with
{}_{1}F_{0}(-a;t)=(1-t)^{a}
the Euler transform immediately yields one of the hypergeometric representations of the regularized incomplete beta function:

\displaystyle I(\mu,\mu^{\prime};t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{B(\mu,\mu^{\prime})}\int_{0}^{t}s^{\mu-1}(1-s)^{\mu^{\prime}-1}\,ds=\frac{t^{\mu}}{B(\mu,\mu^{\prime})}\int_{0}^{1}u^{\mu-1}\,{}_{1}F_{0}(1-\mu^{\prime};tu)\,du

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{\mu}}{\mu B(\mu,\mu^{\prime})}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1-\mu^{\prime},\mu\\
\mu+1\end{array};t\right)\,.

Accordingly, our integral
\mathcal{B}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},\mu,\mu^{\prime},\nu,\nu^{\prime})
may be expressed in terms of an Euler transform of a product of hypergeometric functions

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},\mu,\mu^{\prime},\nu,\nu^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu\nu B(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})B(\mu,\mu^{\prime})B(\nu,\nu^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\int_{0}^{1}t^{\lambda+\mu+\nu-1}(1-t)^{\lambda^{\prime}-1}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1-\mu^{\prime},\mu\\
\mu+1\end{array};t\right){}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1-\nu^{\prime},\nu\\
\nu+1\end{array};t\right)\,dt\,.

A product of
{}_{2}F_{1}
hypergeometric functions of
t
can be represented as a series in
t
with coefficients involving values of
{}_{4}F_{3}
hypergeometric functions evaluated at
1

(

{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1-\mu^{\prime},\penalty\ \mu\\
\mu+1\end{array};t\right){}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1-\nu^{\prime},\penalty\ \nu\\
\nu+1\end{array};t\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(1-\mu^{\prime})_{n}t^{n}}{(\mu+n)n!}{}_{4}F_{3}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1-\nu^{\prime},\penalty\ \nu,\penalty\ -\mu-n,\penalty\ -n\\
1+\nu,\penalty\ \mu^{\prime}-n,\penalty\ 1-\mu-n\end{array};1\right)\,.

The two

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\nu B(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})B(\mu,\mu^{\prime})B(\nu,\nu^{\prime})}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1-\mu^{\prime})_{n}}{(\mu+n)n!}B(\lambda+\mu+\nu+n,\lambda^{\prime})

\displaystyle\qquad\times{}_{4}F_{3}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1-\nu^{\prime},\penalty\ \nu,\penalty\ -\mu-n,\penalty\ -n\\

which terminates if

{}_{3}F_{2}
hypergeometric functions:

\mathcal{B}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},\mu,\mu^{\prime},\nu,\nu^{\prime})=1-\frac{\mathcal{C}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},\mu,\mu^{\prime},\nu,\nu^{\prime})+\mathcal{C}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},\nu,\nu^{\prime},\mu,\mu^{\prime})}{B(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})B(\mu,\mu^{\prime})B(\nu,\nu^{\prime})}

where
\mathcal{C}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},\mu,\mu^{\prime},\nu,\nu^{\prime})
is as defined by the series given in (
13
),
which obviously terminates in the case
\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
.

For general (real positive) parameters, the series (
13
) may be evaluated in terms of a Kampé de Fériet function
(
Kampé de
Fériet 1921a
;
Kampé de
Fériet 1921b
)
, on noting that

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda\nu\mathcal{C}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},\mu,\mu^{\prime},\nu,\nu^{\prime})}{B(\lambda+\mu+\nu,\mu^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1-\lambda^{\prime})_{k}\,(\lambda)_{k}\,(\lambda+\mu+\nu)_{k}}{k!(\lambda+1)_{k}\,(\lambda+\mu+\nu+\mu^{\prime})_{k}}\,{}_{3}F_{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1-\nu^{\prime},\nu,k+\lambda+\mu+\nu\\
\nu+1,k+\mu^{\prime}+\lambda+\mu+\nu\end{array};1\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1-\lambda^{\prime})_{k}\,(\lambda)_{k}\,(\lambda+\mu+\nu)_{k}\,(\lambda+\mu+\nu+k)_{j}\,(1-\nu^{\prime})_{j}\,(\nu)_{j}}{(\lambda+1)_{k}\,(\lambda+\mu+\nu+\mu^{\prime})_{k}\,(\lambda+\mu+\nu+\mu^{\prime}+k)_{j}\,(\nu+1)_{j}\,k!j!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\lambda+\mu+\nu)_{j+k}\,(1-\lambda^{\prime})_{k}\,(\lambda)_{k}\,(1-\nu^{\prime})_{j}\,(\nu)_{j}}{(\lambda+\mu+\nu+\mu^{\prime})_{j+k}\,(\lambda+1)_{k}\,(\nu+1)_{j}\,k!j!}

from which

\mathcal{C}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},\mu,\mu^{\prime},\nu,\nu^{\prime})=\frac{B(\lambda+\mu+\nu,\mu^{\prime})}{\lambda\nu}F^{1:2;2}_{1:1;1}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\lambda+\mu+\nu:\lambda,1-\lambda^{\prime}\mathbin{;}\nu,1-\nu^{\prime}\\
\lambda+\mu+\nu+\mu^{\prime}:\lambda+1\mathbin{;}\nu+1\end{array};1,1\right)

follows by the definition of the Kampé de Fériet function. Here, we recall that the general Kampé de Fériet function in
2
variables is given by

F^{A:C;F}_{B:D;G}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}(a):(c)\mathbin{;}(f)\\
(b):(d)\mathbin{;}(g)\end{array};x,y\right)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{((a))_{m+n}((c))_{m}((f))_{n}}{((b))_{m+n}((d))_{m}((g))_{n}}\,\frac{x^{m}y^{n}}{m!n!}\,,

(39)

where
(a)
,
(c)
etc are sequences of length
A
,
C
etc, and the notation
((c))_{m}=\prod_{i=1}^{C}(c_{i})_{m}
denotes the product of Pochhammer symbols.
Thus the proof of Proposition
2
is complete and we have evaluated the full family of integrals
\mathcal{B}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},\mu,\mu^{\prime},\nu,\nu^{\prime})
in terms of two Kampé de Fériet functions.

Remark 3
.

At this point, it is useful to note the simpler problem of calculating the probability that one beta random variable exceeds another:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(X_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}\geq Y_{\mu,\mu^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{B(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})}\int_{0}^{1}t^{\lambda-1}(1-t)^{\lambda^{\prime}-1}I(\mu,\mu^{\prime};t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu B(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})B(\mu,\mu^{\prime})}\int_{0}^{1}t^{\lambda+\mu-1}(1-t)^{\lambda^{\prime}-1}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1-\mu^{\prime},\mu\\
\mu+1\end{array};t\right)\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{B(\lambda+\mu,\lambda^{\prime})}{\mu B(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})B(\mu,\mu^{\prime})}\,{}_{3}F_{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1-\mu^{\prime},\mu,\lambda+\mu\\
\mu+1,\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}+\mu\end{array};1\right)\,,

which follows by a simple application of first (
2.1
) and then (
20
). As
\nu\to 0+
the random variable
Z_{\nu,1}
converges in probability to 0, and (
3
) agrees as expected with the corresponding limit of
\mathcal{B}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},\mu,\mu^{\prime},\nu,1)
in (
2.1
).

To conclude our general discussion, we note that (
6
) implies that

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},\mu,\mu^{\prime},\nu,\nu^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(X_{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda}<\min\{Y_{\mu^{\prime},\mu},Z_{\nu^{\prime},\nu}\})

\displaystyle=1-\mathbb{P}(X_{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda}\geq Y_{\mu^{\prime},\mu})-\mathbb{P}(X_{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda}\geq Z_{\nu^{\prime},\nu})+\mathbb{P}(X_{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda}\geq\max\{Y_{\mu^{\prime},\mu},Z_{\nu^{\prime},\nu}\})

\displaystyle=1-\mathbb{P}(X_{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda}\geq Y_{\mu^{\prime},\mu})-\mathbb{P}(X_{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda}\geq Z_{\nu^{\prime},\nu})+\mathcal{B}(\lambda^{\prime},\lambda,\mu^{\prime},\mu,\nu^{\prime},\nu)\,,

and, combined with Remark
3
, one may obtain from Proposition
2
an expression for
\mathcal{B}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},\mu,\mu^{\prime},\nu,\nu^{\prime})
involving a series of
{}_{3}F_{2}
’s that terminates if
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}
. Combining these observations with the identity in (
4
) shows that any
\mathcal{B}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},\mu,\mu^{\prime},\nu,\nu^{\prime})
in which both
\{\mu,\mu^{\prime}\}
and
\{\nu,\nu^{\prime}\}
contain at least one natural number may also be expressed as a finite sum of
{}_{3}F_{2}
’s, as claimed in Section
1
. We refrain from writing these expressions explicitly.

2.2
Some special cases

We conclude this section by considering some particular sets of parameter values for which the integral
\mathcal{B}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},\mu,\mu^{\prime},\nu,\nu^{\prime})
can be expressed in terms of a small number of hypergeometric or simpler functions.

Case 1:

\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}=1
,
\mu^{\prime}=\mu
,
\nu^{\prime}=\nu
.

We first consider the situation in which the random variables
Y_{\mu,\mu}
and
Z_{\nu,\nu}
both follow symmetric beta distributions. Here the hypergeometric functions in (
2.1
) take the form

{}_{3}F_{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}a,b,c\\
e,f\end{array};1\right)

where
a+b=1+i+j
,
e+f=2c+1+i
,
i=-1
,
j=1
. Consequently, they may be evaluated using a contiguous generalization of Whipple’s theorem proved in
Lavoie
et al. 1996
: i.e., for
a+b=1
,
e+f=2c

\displaystyle{}_{3}F_{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}a,b,c\\
e,f\end{array};1\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(e)\Gamma(f)}{4^{a}(c-1)\Gamma(e-a)\Gamma(f-a)}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left(\frac{\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}(e-a+1))\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}(f-a))}{\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}(e+a+1))\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}(f+a))}+\frac{\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}(f-a+1))\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}(e-a))}{\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}(f+a+1))\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}(e+a))}\right)\,.

After rearrangement in (
2.1
), this gives the result leading to (
12
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(1,1,\mu,\mu,\nu,\nu)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\Gamma(\mu+\tfrac{1}{2})\Gamma(\nu+\tfrac{1}{2})\Gamma(\mu+\nu+1)}{4\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(1+\mu)\Gamma(1+\nu)\Gamma(\mu+\nu+\tfrac{1}{2})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{(\mu+\nu)B(\mu+\nu,\mu+\nu+1)}{4\mu\nu B(\mu,\mu+1)B(\nu,\nu+1)}\,.

Case 2:

\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}=1
,
\mu^{\prime}=\mu+1
,
\nu^{\prime}=\nu+1
.

Similarly to Case 1, this can be evaluated using the
i=0
,
j=-1

Lavoie
et al. 1996

i.e., for
a+b=0

e+f=2c+1

\displaystyle{}_{3}F_{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}a,b,c\\
e,f\end{array};1\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(e)\Gamma(f)}{2^{2a+1}\Gamma(e-a)\Gamma(f-a)}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left(\frac{\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}(e-a))\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}(f-a))}{\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}(e+a))\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}(f+a))}+\frac{\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}(e-a+1))\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}(f-a+1))}{\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}(e+a+1))\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}(f+a+1))}\right)\,.

After rearrangement, this gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(1,1,\mu,\mu+1,\nu,\nu+1)

which may be written for integer parameters as

\mathcal{B}(1,1,m,m+1,n,n+1)=\frac{4mn+3(m+n)+2}{2(2m+1)(2n+1)}-\frac{m+n}{4(m+n)+2}\frac{\binom{m+n}{m}^{2}}{\binom{2m+2n}{2m}}.

(44)

\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}=1
,

,

Here we consider the situation in which the random variables

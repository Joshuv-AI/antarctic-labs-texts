Universal polynomials for multi-singularity loci of maps
Toru Ohmoto

Abstract
. In the present paper, we prove the existence of universal polynomials which express multi-singularity loci classes of prescribed types for proper morphisms f : X → Y f:X\to Y between smooth schemes over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero – we call them Thom polynomials for multi-singularity types of maps . It has been referred to as the Thom-Kazarian principle and unsolved for a long time. This result solidifies the foundation for a general enumerative theory of singularities of maps which is applicable to a broad range of problems in classical and modern algebraic geometry. For instance, it extends curvilinear multiple-point theory to be adapted to maps with arbitrary singularities; it guarantees universal expressions for counting divisors with prescribed isolated singular points in a given family of divisors; various counting problems of intersection and contact are formulated in a united way, and so on. In particular, it would contribute to a satisfactory answer to the rest of (an advanced form of) Hilbert’s 15th problem and connect such classics to recent new interests in enumerations inspired by mathematical physics and other fields. First, we introduce the multi-singularity loci class for an arbitrary proper morphism via Intersection Theory on Hilbert schemes of points combined with the Thom-Mather Theory of singularities of maps. A main feature of our proof is a striking use of algebro-geometric cohomology operations . Somewhat surprisingly, when trying to grasp a full perspective of classical enumerative geometry, we will inevitably encounter algebraic cobordism and motivic cohomology.



1.
Introduction

From classical to modern enumerative geometry, numerous problems are related to counting multiple-points of singular maps associated to given geometric situations. For instance, counting hyperplanes tangent at
r
points to a hypersurface in the
r
-dimensional projective space
\mathbb{P}^{r}
is a typically classical problem, while a modern question posed in string theory,
instanton counting
, is concerning enumeration of
r
-nodal curves in a sub-linear system on a projective surface.
Although these two questions seem quite different at first glance, they are actually very close to each other; it is because they can be interpreted as counting
r
multiple-points of certain singular maps (§
4.6
).
A little surprisingly, however, an adequate general theory for this kind of enumerative problems has not yet been well established so far – that is the main theme of the present paper.

Roughly saying, a
multi-singularity
of a map
f:X\to Y
between manifolds means the germ of
f
at distinct points
p_{1},\cdots,p_{r}
of
X
satisfying
f(p_{1})=\cdots=f(p_{r})\in Y
, while to distinguish, a singular map-germ at a single point (
r=1
) is referred to as a
mono-singularity
or
local singularity
.
Put

\kappa:=\dim Y-\dim X

and we call it the
codimension of
f
.
A
singularity type
is an equivalence class of map-germs up to local isomorphisms, and
multi-singularity loci
of prescribed type for
f:X\to Y
are defined in a certain way as subvarieties of
X
and
Y
. Precise definitions of those terms are provided in the later sections.
Of our particular interest is to universally express the cohomology classes represented by those multi-singularity loci in terms of intrinsic invariants of
X
,
Y
and
f
, and also to reveal the whole hidden combinatorial structure among those cohomological expressions.
A solid foundation for solving such problems has been intensively pursued over the past decades within the framework of Intersection Theory developed by Fulton, Kleiman and MacPherson
[
Fu84
,
Kl87
]
– in §
2.1.2
, we will briefly summarize the
multiple-point theory
and its limitation as our motivational background.

Meanwhile, at the beginning of this century, a remarkably novel approach was proposed from topology, that suggests a significant breakthrough in this field.
Based on his topological argument using complex cobordism theory
MU^{*}
, M. Kazarian
[
Ka03
,
Ka06
]
has stated the existence of a unique universal polynomial which represents the (source) multi-singularity locus of prescribed type for any generic proper holomorphic map
f:X\to Y
between complex manifolds – it is called the
Thom polynomial for a multi-singularity type of maps
. We will use this terminology to mean a universal polynomial in variables
c_{i}\;(i\geq 1)
and
s_{I}
with rational coefficients such that its shape depends only on the singularity type, where the
quotient Chern class

c_{i}=c_{i}(f)
means the
i
-th Chern class of the difference bundle
f^{*}TY-TX
, i.e.,

c(f):=1+c_{1}(f)+c_{2}(f)+\cdots=\frac{1+f^{*}c_{1}(TY)+f^{*}c_{2}(TY)+\cdots}{1+c_{1}(TX)+c_{2}(TX)+\cdots}

and the
Landweber-Novikov class

s_{I}=s_{I}(f)
with index
I=(i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{k})
is given by

s_{I}(f):=f_{*}(c^{I}(f))=f_{*}(c_{1}(f)^{i_{1}}c_{2}(f)^{i_{2}}\cdots c_{k}(f)^{i_{k}}).

The information of the map
f
is only encoded in the substitution for variables
c_{i}=c_{i}(f)
and
f^{*}s_{I}
of the polynomial, that infers the principle that

local symmetry of singularities governs the global appearance of the singularity locus for a given map
f
.

\kappa=1

f:X^{2}\to Y^{3}

3

Y=\mathbb{P}^{3}

A_{1}

A_{0}^{3}
) and the degree of the double-point curve (
A_{0}^{2}

c_{i}(f)
and
f^{*}s_{I}(f)
for
c_{i}
and
s_{I}
. Similarly, for a map
f:X^{3}\to\mathbb{P}^{4}
of a
3
-fold into
4
-space, not only the degree of the curve loci formed by singularities of types
A_{1}
,
A_{0}^{2}
and
A_{0}^{3}
but also the number of singular points of each type of
A_{1}A_{0}
,
A_{0}A_{1}
and
A_{0}^{4}
is counted in entirely the same way (the meaning of symbols will be explained in the latter section).
This picture is drastically extended by Kazarian – many new Thom polynomials are systematically provided and applied to classical/modern enumerative problems, that leads to rediscover many known formulas and to make hundreds of generalizations possible. A key observation, not apparent at all, indicates a well-organized combinatorial structure among all multi-singularity Thom polynomials. It would be regarded as a taxonomy or systematics for ‘counting formulas’ in enumerative geometry.

Actually, the simplest case is treated in a well-established theory,
originated by René Thom
[
Th55
]
, which shows the existence of a universal polynomial of Chern classes
c_{i}(f)
that represents the locus of a given
mono-singularity type

[
Po71
,
Da72
,
Ro73
,
Ri01
,
FR02
,
FR12
,
Ka03
,
Ka06
,
Oh06
,
Oh08
,
BS12
]
.
A typical example is the famous
Thom-Porteous formula
;
a certain Schur polynomial in Chern classes expresses the degeneracy locus of a vector bundle morphism or the
\Sigma^{i}
-locus of a map, see §
2.1.1
.
In this theory, most essential is the existence of the
classifying stack for mono-singularity types
, which is given by the quotient stack
[V/G]
for a certain algebraic group action of
G
on some affine space
V

(for the Thom-Porteous case, we consider the natural action of
G=GL(l)\times GL(m)
on the affine space
V
of
l\times m
matrices).
Then the Thom polynomial of a mono-singularity type is given by the
G
-equivariant characteristic class representing the orbit closure of that type. That is fully established in the framework of Equivariant Intersection Theory based on the algebraic Borel construction
[
To99
,
ED98
]
.

In contrast, for counting multiple-points of singular maps (
r\geq 2)
,
certain moduli spaces of
r
-tuples of points on
X
(e.g., Hilbert schemes, Fulton-MacPherson configuration spaces etc.) must be involved, that makes the problem much deeper and harder than the mono-singularity case.
Critical is, at least for now, the lack of the
classifying stack for multi-singularity types
.
In fact, there has not yet been found any full proof of the existence of multi-singularity Thom polynomials, so we call it
Kazarian’s conjecture
or the
Thom-Kazarian principle
throughout the present paper.
Here is an impressive phrase from an article of S. Kleiman on Hilbert’s 15th problem
[
Kl76
]
:

“
… In the offing, there is the exciting hope of the development in algebraic geometry of a general enumerative theory of singularities of mappings, a theory of Thom polynomials, which will, among other things, unify and justify the classical work dealing with prescribed conditions of intersection and contact imposed on linear spaces.
”

Nearly half a century later, the story is not complete yet – from our viewpoint, Kazarian’s conjecture is exactly the missing piece of the expected theory !

In the present paper, we prove the conjecture with a sufficient generality
in the context of algebraic geometry over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic zero.
We deal with
arbitrary
proper morphisms
f:X\to Y
between smooth schemes, i.e., without any restriction of singularities of
f
and dimensions.
A key ingredient of our argument is Intersection Theory on relative Hilbert schemes of points associated to
f
and its relation with the Thom-Mather theory of singularities of mappings under (formal) algebraic geometry setting.
Hilbert schemes are highly singular in general, that has long been a major obstacle to establishing a general multiple-point theory (§
2.1.2
).
In order to overcome it, we employ an entirely new approach – it heavily relies on algebraic cobordism theory
\Omega^{*}
constructed by Levine-Morel
[
LM07
]
and further simplified by Levine-Pandharipande
[
LP09
]
.
The theory
\Omega^{*}
requires
resolution of singularities
, and therefore (just for this), we put the hypothesis of characteristic zero.
Most notable in our approach is that we find a ‘shortcut proof’ of the conjecture by making use of
cohomology operations
in algebraic geometry, which has recently been established by A. Vishik
[
Vi19
]
as an algebraic counterpart to cohomology operations in complex cobordism
MU^{*}
.

We will state precisely our main results in §
4.5
later (Theorems
4.17
and
4.18
).
Instead, in the steps (i) to (vi) below, we outline a very clear flow of our arguments and results. Each step requires many technical details and ideas, which will be fully discussed in the main body from §2. There are two parts: the first part (i–iii) is devoted to defining multi-singularity loci classes for
arbitrary
proper morphisms, and the second part (iv–vi) is devoted to our proof of the existence of universal polynomial expressions for those classes. A major prototype is
degeneracy loci class
in the Thom-Porteous formula and
double-point loci class
in the double-point formula (see §
2.1
).

Let
\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}
be fixed. Assume that
X
and
Y
are smooth schemes with

m=\dim X\;\;\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\;\;l=m+\kappa=\dim Y

(letters
l,m,\kappa
are used with this meaning throughout the present paper).
Let
\mathcal{O}_{X,p}
denote the stalk at a closed point
p\in X
of the structure sheaf of
X
and
\mathfrak{m}_{X,p}
the maximal ideal. A minimal basics of the Thom-Mather Theory for singularities of maps will be reviewed in §
2.2
.

(i)

Given a finitely determined mono-germ
f:(\mathbb{A}^{m},0)\to(\mathbb{A}^{l},0)
with the determinacy degree
k_{f}<\infty
,
put

I_{f}:=f^{*}(\mathfrak{m}_{\mathbb{A}^{l},0})\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{m},0}+\mathfrak{m}_{\mathbb{A}^{m},0}^{k_{f}+1}

(see also §
4.1
). Let
\eta
denote the
\mathcal{K}
-equivalence class of
f
,
\mathcal{K}
-type of
f
for short, i.e., the isomorphism class of the quotient algebra
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{m},0}/I_{f}
(§
2.2.1
).
Abusing the notation, we often write
I_{\eta}
to mean the ideal
I_{f}
.
We denote the colength by

n(\eta):=\dim_{k}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{m},0}/I_{\eta}<\infty

and also set

\ell(\eta):=\mbox{$\mathcal{K}_{e}$-codimension of $\eta$}<\infty

which is equal to the minimal dimension
l^{\prime}
of the target space of a stable mono-germ
(\mathbb{A}^{m^{\prime}},0)\to(\mathbb{A}^{l^{\prime}},0)
with
\kappa=l^{\prime}-m^{\prime}
whose quotient algebra is isomorphic to
\eta
(§
2.2.4
).
We may also use the letter
\eta
to mean an
equisingularty type
, a certain moduli family of
\mathcal{K}
-types
(then the definition of
\ell(\eta)
is modified by taking account of the modality).

(ii)

Throughout this paper,
we let a
multi-singularity type

\underline{\eta}=(\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\cdots,\eta_{r})

mean an
ordered

r
-tuple of
\mathcal{K}
-types
\eta_{i}

of finitely determined mono-germs
(\mathbb{A}^{m},0)\to(\mathbb{A}^{l},0)

(1\leq i\leq r)
.
Types of entries
\eta_{i}
are allowed to be duplicate, e.g., in case of
\kappa>0
, we denote by
A_{0}^{r}=(A_{0},\cdots,A_{0})
(
r
times) the
r
-tuple of immersion-germs, which will be used for expressing the
r
multiple-point locus.
In case of
\kappa\leq 0
, we always assume that every entry
\eta_{i}
is
not submersive
, namely,
\underline{\eta}
is a combination of types of
isolated complete intersection singularities
(ICIS).
In any case, we put

n=n(\underline{\eta}):=\sum_{i=1}^{r}n(\eta_{i}),\qquad\ell=\ell(\underline{\eta}):=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\ell(\eta_{i})

where
n
is the total multiplicity and

\ell
is equal to the expected codimension of the multi-singularity locus of type
\underline{\eta}
in the target space.
For a multi-singularity type
\underline{\eta}
,
we define the
geometric subset
of the (ordered) Hilbert scheme of
n
points in
X

\Xi(X;\underline{\eta})\subset X^{[[n]]}

to be the Zariski closure of the subset parametrizing ideal sheaves supported at
r
distinct points of
X
with stalks being isomorphic to
I_{\eta_{i}}
’s (
1\leq i\leq r
) (Definition
4.1
).

(iii)

Given an
arbitrary
morphism
f:X\to Y
from an
m
-fold
X
to an
l
-fold
Y
,
we introduce the
Hilbert extension map

f^{[[n]]}:X^{[[n]]}\to(X\times Y)^{[[n]]}

(cf.
[
Ia87
,
Gaf93
,
DL96
]
).
This can be thought of as a “compactification” of the multi-jet extension map introduced by J. Mather
[
Mat70
]
.
Although the Hilbert schemes may be highly singular and have many components of different dimension, the map
f^{[[n]]}
itself is always a regular embedding and the normal bundle is isomorphic to the tautological bundle
(f^{*}TY)^{[[n]]}
(Proposition
3.2
). Then, Fulton-MacPherson’s
refined Gysin map

[
Fu84
, §6]
is well adapted; it produces the intersection product of
f^{[[n]]}
with the closed subscheme
\Xi(X;\underline{\eta})\times Y\hookrightarrow(X\times Y)^{[[n]]}
.
Projecting it to
X
and
Y
provided
f
is proper,
we define
multi-singularity loci classes

as rational equivalence classes of algebraic cycles

m_{\underline{\eta}}(f)\in{\rm CH}^{\ell-\kappa}(X),\quad n_{\underline{\eta}}(f)\in{\rm CH}^{\ell}(Y),

which are supported on the multi-singularity loci
M_{\underline{\eta}}(f)\subset X
and
N_{\underline{\eta}}(f)\subset Y
, respectively (Definitions
4.4
and
4.9
). It holds that

f_{*}m_{\underline{\eta}}(f)=n_{\underline{\eta}}(f).

In case that
f
is appropriately generic, the loci
M_{\underline{\eta}}(f)
and
N_{\underline{\eta}}(f)
have the right dimension (
=\dim Y-\ell
) and represent exactly the classes
m_{\underline{\eta}}(f)
and
n_{\underline{\eta}}(f)
, respectively, up to certain multiplicities (Proposition
4.15
). For instance, if
f
is generic and the target locus
N_{\underline{\eta}}(f)
is of dimension
0
, then we have the formula counting
\underline{\eta}
-singular points in the target space

\deg N_{\underline{\eta}}(f)=\frac{1}{\#{\rm Aut}(\underline{\eta})}\int_{Y}n_{\underline{\eta}}

where
{\rm Aut}(\underline{\eta})\subset\mathfrak{S}_{r}
is the subgroup of permutations preserving the collection
\underline{\eta}
.

(iv)

After a brief review of algebraic cobordism theory in §
5
,
we show that the class
n_{\underline{\eta}}(f)
depends only on the algebraic cobordism class

[f:X\to Y]\in\Omega^{\kappa}(Y)

(Proposition
6.16
).
The proof is based on careful consideration of refined intersection caused by a double-point degeneration
(Proposition
6.1
).
Furthermore, we prove that the resulting map

n_{\underline{\eta}}:\Omega^{\kappa}(Y)\to{\rm CH}^{\ell}(Y)

is a (unstable) cohomology operation in an analogous sense as Quillen’s in topology (Proposition
6.17
and Corollary
6.18
).

(v)

In §
6.3
, we complete the proof of our main theorem (Theorem
4.17
).
Thanks to a recent result of Vishik
[
Vi19
]
,
it turns out that the operation
n_{\underline{\eta}}
tensored by
\mathbb{Q}
is uniquely written as a polynomial with
\mathbb{Q}
-coefficients of the Landweber-Novikov operations with indices
I=(i_{1},i_{2},\cdots)

s_{I}:\Omega^{\kappa}(Y)\to{\rm CH}^{\ell}(Y),\;\;[f]\mapsto s_{I}(f)=f_{*}(c^{I}(f)).

That gives the desired multi-singularity Thom polynomial expression in the target space
Y
.
A
K
-theoretic refinement of the theorem is also obtained in entirely the same way (§
5.5
).

(vi)

In §
6.4
, for an appropriately nice map (later called an
admissible map
)
f:X\to Y
of codimension
\kappa\geq 0
,
we identify the class
m_{\underline{\eta}}(f)
in terms of
c_{i}(f)
and
f^{*}s_{I}(f)
,
that leads to the existence of the source multi-singularity Thom polynomial (Theorem
4.18
).
The proof uses the result of (v) above but also heavily relies on another aspect coming from local symmetry of singularity types, that is based on the so-called
restriction method
due to R. Rimányi in topology
[
Ri01
]
(Remark
6.25
).

To be precise, what we prove in the step (v) is the existence of
cobordism Thom polynomials in Landweber-Novikov classes
defined in
\Omega^{\ell}(Y)
(Theorem
6.19
).
Since algebraic cobordism
\Omega^{*}
is the universal oriented cohomology theory
[
LM07
]
,
the Chow theory version in
{\rm CH}^{*}
and the
K
-theory version in
K^{0}[\beta,\beta^{-1}]
immediately follow. Notice that in another oriented cohomology theory
A^{*}
, there is no longer
a priori
‘fundamental classes’ for singular subvarieties in general. In particular, finding an
\underline{\eta}
-singularity loci class
n^{\Omega}_{\underline{\eta}}(f)\in\Omega^{\ell}(Y)
is more or less equivalent to that one picks up a ‘universal’ desingularization of the
\underline{\eta}
-locus. It is not unique, so
n^{\Omega}_{\underline{\eta}}(f)
depends on the choice. For instance, there has been studied the Thom-Porteous formula in algebraic cobordism
\Omega^{*}
by using specific resolutions of orbit closures of
\Sigma^{i}

[
Hu12
]
.
This remark is important for properly employing Vishik’s result on cohomology operations
[
Vi19
, Thm.3.18]
.
In fact, we will show that given a certain distinguished class of the geometric subset
\Xi(X;\underline{\eta})
in
\Omega_{*}(X^{[[n]]})
, called an
\Omega
-assignment
later (Definition
5.6
), there exists a unique universal polynomial in
s_{I}
’s expressing the corresponding
\underline{\eta}
-singularity loci class
n^{\Omega}_{\underline{\eta}}(f)
in
\Omega^{\ell}(Y)_{\mathbb{Q}}
. It should be emphasized that in our approach, the existence of multi-singularity Thom polynomials is implicitly indebted on resolution of singularities of
\Xi(X;\underline{\eta})
.

Notice that the existence of the target Thom polynomial for
n_{\underline{\eta}}(f)
does not directly imply that of the source Thom polynomial for
m_{\underline{\eta}}(f)
.
In the step (v), our main formula of the target class
n_{\underline{\eta}}(f)
is valid for
arbitrary
proper maps
f
,
while in the step (vi) for the source class
m_{\underline{\eta}}(f)
,
we consider only appropriately nice maps with
\kappa\geq 0
because of some technical reason.
It is highly expected that this restriction can be dropped, but it is surely related to some essential issues, and therefore this part of the conjecture still remains open (see §
7.1.4
, §
7.1.5
and §
7.1.8
).

We consider separately the two cases of positive and negative codimension
\kappa
of maps (cf. §
2.1.2
and §
2.1.3
).
From the above construction, the source and target multi-singularity loci classes are essentially determined by the geometric subset
\Xi(X;\underline{\eta})
in
X^{[[n]]}
. Namely, the roll of
Y
is somehow auxiliary, and it infers that the form of Thom polynomial should depend only on the isomorphism class of the quotient algebra associated to
\underline{\eta}

with
\kappa
as a parameter
(see §
7.1.1
). That is very related to tautological integrals over Hilbert schemes
[
BS12
,
Ka17
,
Re17
,
Be17
,
BS21
,
Be23
]
. In fact, along this line, Bérczi-Szenes
[
BS21
]
recently announced that they have proven
target multiple-point formulas
of
n_{A_{0}^{r}}(f)
for generic maps (see §
2.1.2
), i.e., in our side, it corresponds to Theorem
4.17
for
\underline{\eta}=A_{0}^{r}
for generic maps with
\kappa>0
.

Our proof using cohomology operations is not constructive, and therefore it does not directly yield any explicit forms of universal polynomials.
Nevertheless, once we assume the existence of universal polynomials, there is an effective method for computing their explicit forms based on equivariant cohomology using local symmetries of singularity types, that is called the
interpolation
or
restriction method
invented by R. Rimányi
[
Ri01
,
FR02
,
FR12
]
.
That reduces the problem to solving a system of linear equations of unknown coefficients in the universal polynomial of a given type.
Indeed, by this method, explicit computations have already been given for several mono and multi-singularity types
[
Ri01
,
Ri02
,
FR02
,
Ka03
,
Ka06
,
Oh16
]
– for instance, Marangell-Rimányi
[
MR10
]
calculated the
general quadruple-point formula of
m_{A_{0}^{4}}(f)
which should be valid even for non-curvilinear maps (§
2.1.2
).
Notice that our existence theorems ensure all of those computational results for multi-singularities of maps.
We would not repeat such computations here.
Rather, at the last step (vi), we essentially employ the restriction method to identify the source multi-singularity Thom polynomials.

We end this introduction by briefly mentioning a more general framework.
Besides singularity theory of maps, the whole perspective envisioned by Kazarian in
[
Ka03
]
involves the Thom-Pontryagin construction, Thom spectra, classifying spaces, infinite loop spaces, cohomology operations, and even more, Gromov’s h-principle, all of which are deep concepts in differential topology and homotopy theory.
However, the complex cobordism theory
MU^{*}
, which is described in
C^{\infty}
category, does not fit the classification theory of singularities in complex analytic category.
Therefore, as suggested in
[
Ka03
]
for finding an entire proof of the conjecture, we should unite and integrate those topological ideas suitably in algebraic geometry side (possibly, including positive characteristic case as well); especially, most desirable is to find an algebro-geometric
classifying stack for multi-singularity types of maps
.
That is very challenging – in such an attempt, one would encounter Voevodsky-Morel’s motivic cohomology and Artin stacks for classifying multi-singularities of maps (see §
7.1.8
and §
7.1.9
).
As mentioned before, however, our strategy in the present paper avoids this deeper part of the problem and instead takes a shortcut by directly using Vishik’s theory of cohomology operations for
\Omega^{*}
over a field of characteristic
0
.
So, in this sense, the full scope of the Thom-Kazarian principle is still left for future work, see §
7
for more comments.

will denote the fixed base field of characteristic

,

Also we denote by

For

The rest of the present paper is organized as follows.
In the first part, §2, §3 and §4, we prepare necessary materials, and especially we introduce multi-singularity loci classes in
{\rm CH}^{*}
and state main results precisely. The second part, §5 and §6, is devoted to the proofs of theorems using algebraic cobordism

and cohomology operations. In the final section §7 we discuss future perspectives.

2.1.2.
Multiple-point formulas

Another important prototype is the so-called
multiple-point formulas
,
which have been explored in depth around 80’s

[
Co87
,
DL96
,
Kl81
,
Kl90
,
Le79
,
Pi78
,
Ra85
,
Ro73
,
Ro84
,
Va81
]
; readers are referred to excellent surveys
[
Kl77
,
Kl87
]
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a proper morphism in
{\rm\bf Sm}_{k}

with positive codimension
\kappa=l-m>0
(
l=\dim Y
,
m=\dim X
).

The double-point locus of
f
is the Zariski closure of the set of points
p_{1}\in X
such that there exist another points
p_{2}\;(\not=p_{1})\in X
with
f(p_{1})=f(p_{2})
.
The
double-point loci class

m_{A_{0}^{2}}(f)\in{\rm CH}^{*}(X)
represents the locus if it has the right dimension, and there is a universal expression of the class in terms of invariants of
X
,
Y
and
f
– that is the
double-point formula

[
Ro73
,
FL77
]
(see
[
Fu84
, §9.3]
):

m_{A_{0}^{2}}(f)=f^{*}f_{*}(1)-c_{\kappa}(f)

where
c_{\kappa}=c_{\kappa}(f)=c_{\kappa}(f^{*}TY-TX)
.
The next is the
triple-point formula
which expresses
the closure of the set of
p_{1}\in X
such that
there are distinct
p_{2},p_{3}\,(\not=p_{1})\in X
mapped to
f(p_{1})
.
That is given by the following formula with a nested structure
[
Kl81
,
Kl90
,
Ro84
]
:

m_{A_{0}^{3}}(f)=f^{*}f_{*}(m_{A_{0}^{2}}(f))-2c_{\kappa}f^{*}f_{*}(1)+2\left(c_{\kappa}^{2}+\sum_{i=0}^{\kappa-1}2^{i}c_{\kappa-i-1}c_{\kappa+i+1}\right).

Those formulas are obtained by explicitly constructing desingularizations of the double and the triple-point loci by blow-ups. One may expect that the same method would work inductively for finding higher multiple-point formulas.
However, it turns out to be very hard in general – for instance, the general
quadruple-point formula
of
m_{A_{0}^{4}}(f)
is already problematic
[
DL96
]
.
The reason will be mentioned briefly in the end of this subsection.

On the other hand, by the projection formula

f_{*}(\alpha\cdot f^{*}\beta)=f_{*}(\alpha)\cdot\beta,

the right hand sides of the double and triple-point formulas are written in terms of Chern classes
c_{i}(f)
and Landweber-Novikov classes

f^{*}s_{0}=f^{*}f_{*}(1),\;\;f^{*}s_{0\cdots 01}=f^{*}f_{*}(c_{\kappa}),\;\;f^{*}s_{0}^{2}=f^{*}f_{*}f^{*}f_{*}(1).

A far-extension of this observation finally arrives at Kazarian’s conjecture
[
Ka03
]
mentioned in Introduction.

The conjecture is strongly supported by
curvilinear multiple-point theory
– it was successfully established by Kleiman, Colley and Ran
[
Co87
,
Kl81
,
Kl90
,
Ra85
]

for an important particular class of maps, called
curvilinear maps
,
which are morphisms
f:X\to Y
in
{\rm\bf Sm}_{k}
with
\kappa\geq 0
and corank at most one (i.e.,
\dim\ker df_{p}\leq 1
for any
p\in X
).
A curvilinear singularity type is denoted by
A_{\mu}
, i.e., its local ring is isomorphic to
k[x]/\langle x^{\mu+1}\rangle
, and a curvilinear multi-singularity type is denoted by
A_{\bullet}=(A_{\mu_{1}},\cdots,A_{\mu_{r}})
.
For generic curvilinear maps, the classes
m_{A_{\bullet}}(f)
with some specific types
A_{\bullet}
are explicitly computed by an algorithm in
[
Co87
]
and closed recursive formulas on
m_{A_{0}^{r}}(f)
have also been studied
[
Kl81
,
Ra85
,
Kl90
,
Ka08
]
.
The multiple-point theory covers many important applications in classical enumerative geometry, while those formulas may return wrong answers when applying to non-curvilinear maps, i.e., one needs some correction terms supported on singularities of corank greater than one.
Note that non-curvilinear maps map appear even in low dimensional cases, so an uncovered area has still remained largely, see, e.g.,
[
DL96
,
MR10
,
Ne23
]
.

Another remarkable evidence of the conjecture is recently brought about from a new approach of complex analytic geometry with
non-reductive geometric invariant theory
by Bérczi-Szenes
[
BS12
,
BS21
]
and Bérczi
[
Be17
,
Be23
]
.
They have been developing advanced residue techniques for computing
m_{A_{\mu}}(f)
(mono-singularity of type
A_{\mu}
) and the target multiple-point formula of
n_{A_{0}^{r}}(f)
for generic maps
f
with
\kappa>0
via tautological integrals on Hilbert schemes (cf. §
4.6.3
, §
7.1.10
).
The latter result also strongly supports part of the conjecture. A particular advantage of their approach is that it is constructive via direct computations, that is in clear contrast to our approach.

For proving the double-point formula, a key observation is that the scheme-theoretic preimage
(f\times f)^{-1}(\Delta_{Y})
of the diagonal
\Delta_{Y}\subset Y\times Y
contains not only the double-point locus of
f
but also the diagonal
\Delta_{X}\subset X\times X
.
To remove the unnecessary contribution from
\Delta_{X}
, we may use the blow-up along
\Delta_{X}

\pi:{\rm Bl}(X\times X)\to X\times X.

The embedding
X\to X\times Y
of the graph of
f
naturally induces an embedding

f^{\rm Bl}:{\rm Bl}(X\times X)\to{\rm Bl}((X\times Y)\times(X\times Y))

where the target is the blow-up along the diagonal
\Delta_{X\times Y}
.
Then we have the intersection product of the embedding
f^{\rm Bl}
with the subscheme

\mbox{Bl}(X\times X)\times\Delta_{Y}\hookrightarrow\mbox{Bl}((X\times Y)\times(X\times Y))

in the sense of Fulton-MacPherson, and pushing the product to the first factor
X
of
\pi
, we define the double-point loci class
m_{A_{0}^{2}}(f)\in{\rm CH}^{\kappa}(X)
.
The contribution from
\Delta_{X}
is just the excess intersection along the exceptional divisor, and computing it explicitly, the desired formula is obtained
[
Fu84
, §9.2]
.
Emphasized is that the formula is valid for
arbitrary
morphisms
f
in
{\rm\bf Sm}_{k}
(
\kappa>0
).
Taking a further blow-up, the triple-point formula is derived in a similar but more involved way.

A key issue for us is how to generalize this picture of the proof.
To do this, we employ
ordered
Hilbert schemes
X^{[[n]]}
with the projection

\pi:X^{[[n]]}\to X^{n}=X\times\cdots\times X.

Associated to the graph map of
f
, we introduce the
n
-th Hilbert extension map

f^{[[n]]}:X^{[[n]]}\to(X\times Y)^{[[n]]}

(cf.
[
Gaf93
,
DL96
]
).
Note that
f^{[[2]]}=f^{\rm Bl}
with

X^{[[2]]}=\mbox{Bl}(X\times X)
and
(X\times Y)^{[[2]]}=\mbox{Bl}((X\times Y)\times(X\times Y))
, thus
f^{[[n]]}
must be a suitable generalization.
In fact,
f^{[[n]]}
is a regular embedding (Corollary
3.5
), and thus we have
the intersection product of
f^{[[n]]}
with the subscheme

X^{[[n]]}\times\Delta_{Y}\hookrightarrow(X\times Y)^{[[n]]},

which leads to higher order multiple-point loci classes
m_{A_{0}^{n}}(f)
(Definition
4.9
).

In case of
n\leq 3
, the Hilbert scheme
X^{[[n]]}
is smooth for any dimension of
X
.
In particular, the preimage of
X^{[[3]]}\times\Delta_{Y}
via
f^{[[3]]}
provides a desingularization of the triple-point locus of generic maps
f
, which coincides with the desingularization by blow-ups used for proving the triple-point formula
[
Ro84
,
Gaf93
]
(see Remark
3.10
).
In case that
f
is curvilinear, for any
n
,
it suffices to consider open subsets of
X^{[[n]]}
consisting of
0
-dimensional subschemes lying on smooth curve-germs, called curvilinear Hilbert schemes; they are actually smooth and used for desingularizing curvilinear multiple-point loci
[
Kl90
]
.
In such nice cases, in principle, everything can be handled by traditional methods within
{\rm\bf Sm}_{k}
.

It turn out, however, that the general case including non-curvilinear maps
f
with
n\geq 4
and
\dim X\geq 3
is very problematic.
It is because the Hilbert scheme
X^{[[n]]}
itself becomes to be highly singular and we can not ignore the effect caused by its complicated singularities and various dimensional components.
For instance, singularities of
X^{[[4]]}
cause fatal difficulties in constructing a desingularization of the quadruple-point locus for
non-curvilinear maps

f
, as demonstrated by Dias-Le Barz
[
DL96
]
.

Instead of Hilbert schemes of points, we could have begun with the Fulton-MacPherson configuration space, which is also a compactification of the configuration space of distinct
n
points and is always smooth
[
FM94
]
. Nevertheless, there is a trade-off; the FM configuration space has also its own complicated structure depending on the choice of centers for consecutive blow-ups in its construction, and especially it does not have a functorial property which the Hilbert scheme possesses.
Anyway, we conclude that it is very hard to reach the final goal, solving the conjecture, by explicit calculations through piling up step-by-step construction of desingularizations of multi-singularity loci.
In the present paper we explore a new
non-constructive
approach in detail.

2.1.3.
Discriminant of ICIS and Legendre maps

The case of maps with non-positive codimension (
\kappa\leq 0
) belongs to
non-curvilinear
enumerative geometry – known results are quite limited (see, e.g.,
[
Va81
,
Va07
]
).
In his second paper
[
Ka06
]
, Kazarian extended his conjectures to maps of
\kappa\leq 0
.
Our approach deals with this case as well; indeed, the above Hilbert extension maps are defined without any change.

In this case, we work with
isolated complete intersection singularities
(ICIS),
e.g.,
A_{\mu}
-singularity type defined by
x_{1}^{\mu+1}+x_{2}^{2}+\cdots+x_{|\kappa|+1}^{2}=0
.
Namely, given a proper morphism
f:X\to Y
with
\kappa\leq 0
,
we are concerned with counting singular fibers which have a prescribed combination of several ICIS’s.
In other words, we consider the critical scheme
C(f)=M_{A_{1}}(f)
in
X
, where
{\rm coker}\,df
has positive dimension, and want to count multiple-points of prescribed type for the singular map

f_{C}:C(f)\to Y

obtained by restricting
f
to
C(f)
(note that
C(f)
may not be smooth in general).
The image of
f_{C}
is usually called the
discriminant set
of
f
, denoted by
D(f)\subset Y
.
Of particular interest is the case where
\dim{\rm coker}\,df
is at most one and
df:X\to{\rm Hom}(TX,f^{*}TY)
is transverse to the 1st Thom-Boardman stratum
\Sigma^{1}
. Then

C(f)\,(=df^{-1}(\Sigma^{1}))
is smooth and
D(f)
is locally the discriminant of
isolated hypersurface singularities
; then, the map
f_{C}
is often referred to as a
Legendre map

[
AGV85
]
.
We will come back to this case in §
4.6.2
and §
7.1.6
.

2.1.4.
Hilbert’s 15th problem and beyond

In classical literature, a traditional way to study a non-singular projective variety
X
is to first map it into some projective space
\mathbb{P}^{l}
and count singular points of the image variety – elementary numerical characters of
X
, named
rank, class, class of immersions
, and so on, have been introduced in this way, see, e.g.,
[
Sa82
,
SR49
,
Kl77
,
Pi78
]
. For instance, Semple-Roth
[
SR49
]
noted that for a projective surface there are exactly four basic characters which generate all other characters of Salmon-Cayley-Zeuthen-Baker, often referred to as Enriques’ formulas, and thereafter, Roth claimed an analogous statement for a projective
3
-fold that there are seven basic characters which recover dozens of others. That is directly related to the nature of cobordism theory from a modern viewpoint.
In our language, the target Thom polynomials
n_{\underline{\eta}}
express those numerical characters in terms of Landweber-Novikov classes
s_{I}
of maps
f:X\to\mathbb{P}^{l}
, and it turns out that the set of basic characters exactly correspond to the set of
s_{I}
’s – in case of surfaces, there are four Chern monomials
1,c_{1},c_{1}^{2},c_{2}
, and for
3
-folds, there are seven
1,c_{1},c_{1}^{2},c_{2},c_{1}^{3},c_{1}c_{2},c_{3}
, and their pushforward via
f
generate cobordism invariants
[
SO17
]
.

As well-known, eminent Hilbert’s 15th problem was asking for a rigorous justification of the enumerative geometry of H. Schubert and other pioneers in their era. This is surely more than the quest about the ring structure of cohomology of Grassmannians and flag varieties, because most chapters of Schubert’s book
[
Sc79
]
are devoted to developing and deepening his methods for solving various kinds of enumerative problems in projective algebraic geometry. After almost a hundred years, Fulton-MacPherson’s Intersection Theory
[
Fu84
]
successfully provides a firm foundation of enumerative geometry, that is widely recognized nowadays. However, indeed less known, even in a classical context, a number of questions still remains unexplored well, which is consolidated to Kazarian’s conjecture.
Solving the conjecture revives this 19th century enumerative geometry in a modern way with new flavors
[
Ka03
,
Ka06
,
MR10
,
Oh16
,
SO17
,
Ne23
,
NO24
]
. For instance, as an application of Thom polynomials, Nekarda
[
Ne23
]
has expanded a classical work of H. Schubert on contact problems of lines with a projective surface in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
and
\mathbb{P}^{4}
with some interests from computer vision. Also one can apply Thom polynomial theory to some questions from mathematical physics such as instanton counting problem, see §
4.6.3
.

2.2.
A digest of the Thom-Mather Theory

Singularity theory of differentiable mappings has been established in both complex analytic category and real
C^{\infty}
category (cf.
[
Mat69
,
Mat70
,
AGV85
,
MN20
]
).
Here we briefly present a minimal digest of the local theory for later use.
In particular, we describe it in algebraic category over an algebraic closed field
k
of characteristic zero, according to Mather
[
Mat71
]
.
In this context, classification of singularities of maps is essentially considered at the level of
formal schemes
.

2.2.1.
Map-germs and equivalence relations

Let
X
be a scheme and
p\in X
a closed point.
Let
\mathcal{O}_{X,p}
denote the stalk at
p
of the structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{X}
and
\mathfrak{m}_{X,p}
its maximal ideal.
Also let
\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,p}
denote the
\mathfrak{m}_{X,p}
-adic completion of
\mathcal{O}_{X,p}
,
and
\hat{\mathfrak{m}}_{X,p}
the maximal ideal generated by
\mathfrak{m}_{X,p}
.
For a finite set
S
of closed points in
X
,
{\mathcal{O}}_{X,S}=\bigoplus_{p\in S}{\mathcal{O}}_{X,p}
(
\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,S}
as well).

Let
f:X\to Y
be a morphism in
{\rm\bf Sm}_{k}
and
q\in Y
a closed point and
S\subset f^{-1}(q)
finite closed points.
The
map-germ
f:(X,S)\to(Y,q)
of
f
at
S
means the ring homomorphism
f^{*}:{\mathcal{O}}_{Y,q}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{X,S}

(it is called a
multi-germ
if
|S|>1
,
mono-germ
if
|S|=1
).

Two mono-germs
f,g:(X,p)\to(Y,q)
are
formally
\mathcal{K}
-equivalent
if their formal quotient algebras are isomorphic:

\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,p}/f^{*}(\hat{\mathfrak{m}}_{Y,q})\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,p}\simeq\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,p}/g^{*}(\hat{\mathfrak{m}}_{Y,q})\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,p}.

The equivalence class of
f
is called the (formal)
\mathcal{K}
-type of
f
throughout the present paper.
The letter
\mathcal{K}
refers to the
contact equivalence
of map-germs – we often use the
stably
\mathcal{K}
-equivalence
which is the equivalence among formal quotient algebras associated to map-germs with a fixed codimension
\kappa

[
Mat69
,
AGV85
,
MN20
]
.

We say that
f:(X,p)\to(Y,q)
is
k
-
\mathcal{K}
-determined
if any mono-germ
g
satisfying that

g^{*}({\mathfrak{m}}_{Y,q}){\mathcal{O}}_{X,p}\equiv f^{*}({\mathfrak{m}}_{Y,q}){\mathcal{O}}_{X,p}\;\;\mbox{modulo}\;\;{\mathfrak{m}}_{X,p}^{k+1}

is formally
\mathcal{K}
-equivalent to
f
.
Namely, the
\mathcal{K}
-type of
f
is determined by the Taylor expansion of
f
at
p
up to order
k
in some affine local coordinates of
X
and
Y
centered at
p,q
, respectively. Also
f
is
finitely
\mathcal{K}
-determined
if it is
k
-
\mathcal{K}
-determined for some
k
.
The minimal of such
k
is called the
determinacy degree
of
f
at
p
; denote it by
k_{f}(p)
.
In case of non-positive codimension
\kappa\leq 0
(i.e.,
\dim X\geq\dim Y
),

f
is a finitely determined map-germ if and only if
f=0
defines an isolated complete intersection singularity (ICIS) at
p
.

There is another natural equivalence among map-germs: we say mono-germs
f,g:(X,p)\to(Y,q)
are
formally
\mathcal{A}
-equivalent
if
formal ring homomorphisms
f^{*},g^{*}:\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{Y,q}\to\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,p}
commute with each other via formal local isomorphisms of the source and the target spaces. It is known that formal
\mathcal{A}
-equivalence implies formal
\mathcal{K}
-equivalence, but the converse does not hold (cf. §
2.2.3
).

2.2.2.
Sheaf of vector-fields along
f

Let
f:X\to Y
be a morphism in
{\rm\bf Sm}_{k}
.
Let
\theta(f)
denote the sheaf of regular rational vector-fields along
f
, i.e., sections of
f^{*}TY
.
Precisely, for open affine sets
U\subset X
and
V\subset Y
with
f(U)\subset V
,
it assigns a
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)
-module

\theta(f)(U)=\{\,\mbox{derivations $D:\mathcal{O}_{Y}(V)\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)$}\,\}

i.e.,
D
is
k
-linear and satisfies

D(\varphi\psi)=(f^{*}\varphi)D\psi+(f^{*}\psi)D\varphi\quad(\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{O}_{Y}(V)).

In particular,
\theta(id_{X})
is the sheaf of regular rational vector-fields (derivation) on
X
, denoted also by
\theta_{X}(=\mathcal{D}er_{X})
.

We associate two sheaf homomorphisms

tf:\theta_{X}\to\theta(f),\quad\omega f:\theta_{Y}\to\theta(f)

in a natural way as follows. For any open affine sets
U,V
as above, for
v\in\theta_{X}(U)
, we define

tf(v):\mathcal{O}_{Y}(V)\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(U),\;\;\varphi\mapsto v(\varphi\circ f).

Also for
w\in\theta_{Y}(V)
, we define

\omega f(w):\mathcal{O}_{Y}(V)\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(U),\;\;\varphi\mapsto w(\varphi)\circ f.

We denote the stalk of
\theta(f)
at
p
by
{\theta}(f)_{p}
,
and the
\mathfrak{m}_{X,p}
-adic completion by
\hat{\theta}(f)_{p}
.
Put also
\hat{\theta}(f)_{S}=\bigoplus_{p\in S}\hat{\theta}(f)_{p}
and so on.
In particular, we associate

tf:\hat{\theta}_{X,S}\to\hat{\theta}(f)_{S},\quad\omega f:\hat{\theta}_{Y,q}\to\hat{\theta}(f)_{S}.

2.2.3.
Locally stable maps

A morphism
f:X\to Y
in
{\rm\bf Sm}_{k}
is
locally stable at
S
in the sense of Thom-Mather
(or say, the germ of
f
at
S
is locally stable) if it holds that

\hat{\theta}(f)_{S}=tf(\hat{\theta}_{X,S})+\omega f(\hat{\theta}_{Y,q})

at the formal level
[
Mat71
,
Mat69
]
.
Namely, any formal infinitesimal deformation of
f
is naturally recovered by formal vector-fields of the source and the target spaces.
The local stability of
f
at
S
implies

{\theta}(f)_{S}=tf({\theta}_{X,S})+\omega f({\theta}_{Y,q})+\mathfrak{m}_{X,S}^{c}\theta(f)_{S}

for any power
c
.

In
[
Mat69
, Thm.A]
,
Mather also showed that if two locally stable germs are
\mathcal{K}
-equivalent (or more stronger,
\mathcal{K}
-equivalent up to
(\dim Y+1)
-jets), then they are
\mathcal{A}
-equivalent.
Thus, to classify locally stable map-germs up to
\mathcal{A}
-equivalence, it suffices to classify quotient formal algebras.
There is a translation of this theorem in algebraic geometry setting, see
[
Mat71
, §5]
.

We call
f:X\to Y
a
locally stable map
if for every closed point
q\in Y
and every finite set of closed points
S\subset f^{-1}(q)
, the germ
f:(X,S)\to(Y,q)
is locally stable.

Remark 2.1
.

(Generic linear projection)

Locally stable maps form a sufficiently large class of maps in projective algebraic geometry.
Actually, the locally stability of maps is an
open condition
, provided a certain projectivity condition is assumed
[
Mat71
, §2]
.
A typical case is generic linear projection of a smooth scheme
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{r}
(
m=\dim X
).
Let
W
be an open subset of the moduli of linear projections
\pi
from
\mathbb{P}^{r}
to
Y=\mathbb{P}^{l}
(
r>l
)
such that the center
C_{\pi}
intersects
X
transversely (if
m\leq l
, then
C_{\pi}\cap X=\emptyset
).
In
[
Mat71
, §3]
, it is shown that if
(m,l)
is a certain pair of dimensions (i.e., it is in the so-called
nice range of dimensions
), then
there is an non-empty Zariski open subset of
W
consisting of
\pi
for which
\pi:X-C_{\pi}\to Y
is locally stable.

2.2.4.
\mathcal{K}_{e}
-codimension

By using the Malgrange preparation theorem, the local stability of map-germs is characterized in a more convenient form as follows
[
Mat69
,
AGV85
,
MN20
]
.

The
\mathcal{K}_{e}
-codimension
of
f:(X,p)\to(Y,q)
is defined by

\ell(f_{p}):=\dim_{k}{\theta}(f)_{p}/(tf({\theta}_{X,p})+f^{*}({\mathfrak{m}}_{Y,q}){\theta}(f)_{p})

(often written by
d_{e}(f,\mathcal{K})
in literature).
This is invariant under (stably)
\mathcal{K}
-equivalence.
It is known that
f
is finitely determined if and only if
\ell(f_{p})
is finite
[
Mat69
,
AGV85
]
.
In particular, it is shown
[
Mat69
, Prop.1.6]
that the local stability of a multi-germ
f:(X,S)\to(Y,q)
is equivalent to the sujectivity of the linear map induced by
\omega f
(the
Kodaira-Spencer map
):

\bar{\omega}f:T_{q}Y\to\bigoplus_{p\in S}{\theta}(f)_{p}/(tf({\theta}_{X,p})+f^{*}({\mathfrak{m}}_{Y,q}){\theta}(f)_{p}).

For each
p\in S
, let
A_{p}
be the kernel of the linear map
\bar{\omega}f_{p}
from
T_{q}Y
to the
p
-component of the direct sum.
The surjectivity of the above
\bar{\omega}f
means that
f
is locally stable at each
p\in S
(i.e.,
\bar{\omega}f_{p}
is surjective) and all
A_{p}
(
p\in S)
have
regular intersections
in
T_{q}Y
, i.e.,

{\rm codim}(\bigcap_{p\in S}A_{p})=\sum_{p\in S}{\rm codim}A_{p}=\sum_{p\in S}\ell(f_{p})=:\ell(f_{S}).

In particular, if
f:(X,S)\to(Y,q)
is locally stable, then

\ell(f_{S})\leq l\;(=\dim Y).

Namely,
\ell(f_{S})
is the minimal dimension of the target space of a locally stable multi-germ which is stably
\mathcal{K}
-equivalent to
f
.
Hence,
\ell(f_{S})
is exactly equal to the codimension of the corresponding multi-singularity stratum in
Y
of the locally stable map
f

[
Mat69
, p.227]
. Later, we will denote by
N_{\underline{\eta}}^{\circ}(f)
the stratum in
Y
, where
\underline{\eta}
is the multi-singularity type of the germ of
f
at
S
as defined in Introduction (ii).

Example 2.2
.

A multi-germ
f_{S}
of
r
-tuple of immersions (
\kappa>0
) with regular intersections
is locally stable, and then,
\ell(f_{S})(=\ell(A_{0}^{r}))=r\kappa
, that is the codimension of the
r
-th multiple-point locus
N_{A_{0}^{r}}^{\circ}
in the target space
Y
.

3.
Hilbert schemes

As seen above, the
\mathcal{K}
-classification of finitely determined map-germs
(\mathbb{A}^{m},0)\to(\mathbb{A}^{l},0)

is nothing but classifying ideals in
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{m},0}
of finite colength having
l
generators up to isomorphisms of the source space. It is very related to a certain invariant stratification of the Hilbert schemes of
0
-dimensional subschemes.

3.1.
Hilbert scheme of points

Let
p\in X
and
I_{p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X,p}
an ideal. The Hilbert-Samuel function of
\mathcal{O}_{X,p}/I_{p}
is defined to be the sequence
T(I):=(t_{0},t_{1},\cdots)
where

t_{j}=\dim_{k}\frac{\mathfrak{m}_{X,p}^{j}}{(I_{p}\cap\mathfrak{m}_{X,p}^{j})+\mathfrak{m}_{X,p}^{j+1}}

and
\dim_{k}\mathcal{O}_{X,p}/I_{p}=\sum t_{j}
is called the colength of
I_{p}
. For an ideal sheaf
I
on
X
supported at closed points
p_{1},\cdots,p_{r}
, the colength of
I
means the sum of colength of the stalk
I_{p_{i}}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X,p_{i}}
.
Let
X^{[n]}
denote the Hilbert scheme parameterizing all ideal sheaves on
X

of colength
n
, and
{\rm Hilb}^{n}(\mathcal{O}_{X,p})
the punctual Hilbert scheme which parameterizes the ideals of
\mathcal{O}_{X,p}
of colength
n

[
Ia87
,
BGS81
,
Gaf93
]
.

According to Grothendieck’s general framework,
X^{[n]}
is the projective scheme which represents the contravariant functor
{\rm\bf Sch}_{k}\to\mbox{\bf Sets}
which assigns to every
U\in{\rm\bf Sch}_{k}
the set of closed subschemes
Z\subset U\times X
so that the first factor projection
Z\to U
is a flat morphism whose fiber are
0
-dimensional subschemes of
X
of length
n
. In fact, there exists the universal scheme
\mathcal{Z}_{X}^{n}
on
X^{[n]}
, i.e., it is a closed subscheme of
X^{[n]}\times X
such that the first factor projection

\pi:\mathcal{Z}_{X}^{n}\to X^{[n]}

is a flat family of subschemes of
X
of length
n
which possesses the following universal property:
if
Z\subset U\times X
is a flat family parametrized by
U
, then there exists a unique morphism
\varphi_{Z}:U\to X^{[n]}
such that
Z=\varphi_{Z}^{*}\mathcal{Z}_{X}^{n}\,(=U\times_{X^{[n]}}\mathcal{Z}_{X}^{n})
.

We denote by

an element of

corresponding to the ideal sheaf

At

At

Suppose that we are given a vector bundle

Let

3.2.

3.4.
Differential of Hilbert extension map

Due to Grothendieck, the differential of a morphism in

[

Let

\varphi\in{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{X,p}}(I_{p},\mathcal{O}_{X,p}/I_{p})

,
and

In the above setting, a vector field-germ (derivation)

defines a particular element of

modulo

That yields a canonical inclusion

T_{p}X\hookrightarrow T_{z_{I}}X^{[n]}.

Now, take local coordinates

This is immediately extended to multi-singularity case. In other words, via the tautological construction,
we have the vector bundle map

at

.

The restriction of the differential

3.5.
Ordered Hilbert scheme

For our purpose, more suitable is the
ordered Hilbert scheme
defined as follows (cf. Rennemo
[
Re17
]
).

Let
S^{n}X
be the
n
-th symmetric product of
X
, i.e.,

S^{n}X:=X^{n}/\mathfrak{S}_{n},\quad X^{n}=\overbrace{X\times\cdots\times X}^{n}

where
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is the
n
-th symmetric group.
It is also the moduli scheme parameterizing effective
0
-dimensional cycles of degree
n
in
X
; every element of
S^{n}X
is written as
\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}[p_{i}]
, where
p_{i}\in X
,
n_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}=n
.
The Hilbert scheme
X^{[n]}
is regarded as a ‘partial desingularization’ of
S^{n}X
;
there exists a morphism

\pi^{HC}:X^{[n]}\to S^{n}X,\;\;z_{I}\mapsto\sum_{p\in X}(\dim\mathcal{O}_{X,p}/I_{p})\,[p],

called the
Hilbert-Chow morphism
.
We see that the fiber of
\pi^{HC}
at
n[p]\in S^{n}X
is the punctual Hilbert scheme
{\rm Hilb}^{n}(\mathcal{O}_{X,p})
, and the fiber at
\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}[p_{i}]
is isomorphic to the Cartesian product of
{\rm Hilb}^{n_{i}}(\mathcal{O}_{X,p_{i}})
.
Especially, the general fiber consists of one point, so
\pi^{HC}
is birational.
It is known that for a smooth surface
X
(
m=\dim X=2
),
\pi
is a crepant resolution of
S^{n}X
for any
n
.

The
ordered
Hilbert scheme

is defined by the fiber product of the Hilbert-Chow morphism and the canonical projection
X^{n}\to S^{n}X
:

\textstyle{X^{[[n]]}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{X^{[n]}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{X^{n}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{S^{n}X}

We denote a point of

by
(x,z_{I})
with

x=(p_{1},\cdots,p_{n})\in X^{n},\quad z_{I}\in X^{[n]}

where
p_{i}
may be duplicated and
\pi^{HC}(z_{I})=\sum[p_{i}]
.
Note that
X^{[[n]]}
is a compactification of the configuration space, i.e.,
it has an open dense subset isomorphic to the configuration space of ordered
n
-tuples of points on

F(X,n):=\{\,(p_{1},\cdots,p_{n})\in X^{n}\,|\,p_{i}\not=p_{j}\;(i\not=j)\,\}.

In particular, when

,

coincide with the blow-up

of

along the diagonal (cf.
[
FM94
]
).

Basic properties of non-ordered Hilbert schemes are naturally transported to ordered ones as follows.

•

(Regular embedding)

Given a regular embedding
\iota:X\hookrightarrow M
,
we set

\iota^{[[n]]}:X^{[[n]]}\to M^{[[n]]},\;\;\;\;\iota^{[[n]]}(p_{1},\cdots,p_{n},z_{I}):=(\iota(p_{1}),\cdots,\iota(p_{n}),\iota^{[n]}(z_{I})).

3.2
. In fact, the sheaf homomorphism over
M^{[n]}
in the proof of Lemma

is pulled back via the projection
M^{[[n]]}\to M^{[n]}
and the zero locus in

exactly coincides with
X^{[[n]]}
.

•

(Hilbert extension map)

For a morphism
f:X\to Y
in
{\rm\bf Sm}_{k}
, we have the ordered Hilbert extension map

f^{[[n]]}:X^{[[n]]}\to(X\times Y)^{[[n]]}

and the ordered relative Hilbert scheme

as well.
The normal bundle of the embedding

is isomorphic to
the pullback of that of non-ordered extension map

, and thus
the normal at

is identified with

(notations are the same as above).
Also there is a canonical inclusion

In particular, Lemma

can be read off as for the differential of

,
i.e.,

with the restriction to

and the projection to the normal space

•

(Disjoint union)

Note that for the disjoint union, there is a canonical decomposition of the non-ordered Hilbert scheme:

(X_{1}\sqcup X_{2})^{[n]}=\bigsqcup_{i+j=n}X_{1}^{[i]}\times X_{2}^{[j]}.

For the ordered version, we have the decomposition

where the summand runs over all partitions

.

Proof

In relation with the above example (ii), a modern typical question raises as

5.1.
Oriented cohomology theory

As an algebro-geometric counterpart to Quillen’s program on complex cobordism theory, Levine-Morel
[
LM07
]
have introduced the notion of an oriented cohomology theory on
{\rm\bf Sm}_{k}
, or more generally an oriented Borel-Moore homology theory on
{\rm\bf Sch}_{k}
.
Let
R^{*}
be the category of graded commutative rings.
An
oriented cohomology theory
on
{\rm\bf Sm}_{k}
is an additive (pull-back) functor
A^{*}:{\rm\bf Sm}_{k}^{op}\to R^{*}
which enjoys
the following axioms:

-

pushforward
is associated to every proper morphism
6
6

6

In the original definition in
[
LM07
]
, morphisms are assumed to be projective, but this assumption is weakened by Gonzalez-Karu
[
GK15
]
.

so that it satisfies expected properties (covariant functoriality, projection formula, compatibility for transverse cartesian square);

-

for every vector bundle
E\to X
of rank
n
, let
P:=\mathbb{P}(E^{*})
the scheme of hyperplanes of
E
and let
\mathcal{O}(1)
be the tautological quotient line bundle over
P
; then
A^{*}(P)
is a free
A^{*}(X)
-module with basis
1,\xi,\cdots,\xi^{n-1}
,
where we put

\xi=c_{1}(\mathcal{O}(1)):=s^{*}s_{*}(1)

with the zero-section
s:P\to\mathcal{O}(1)
;

-

for an
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}
-bundle

, the pullback

p^{*}:A^{*}(X)\to A^{*}(V)

is an isomorphism.

Note that
{\rm CH}^{*}
and
K^{0}
are typical examples.
Given an oriented cohomology theory

, one may use Grothendieck’s construction of Chern classes of a vector bundle

of rank
n
over

: for each

there exists a unique element

such that

c_{0}(E)=1\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}c_{i}(E)\xi^{n-i}=0.

One can easily check all the axioms of Chern classes.

5.2.
Algebraic cobordism

A successful theory of
algebraic cobordism

\Omega_{*}
on
{\rm\bf Sch}_{k}
has been established by Levine-Morel
[
LM07
]
following Quillen’s axiomatic approach.
For irreducible
Y\in{\rm\bf Sm}_{k}
, we set

\Omega^{*}(Y)=\Omega_{\dim Y-*}(Y).

Here we always assume the ground field
k
to be of characteristic zero.

Theorem 5.1
.

(
[
LM07
, Thm.7.1.1, Thm.7.1.3]
)

(1) The theory
\Omega_{*}(-)
is the universal oriented Borel-Moore homology theory on
{\rm\bf Sch}_{k}
.

(2) The theory
\Omega^{*}(-)
is the universal oriented cohomology theory on
{\rm\bf Sm}_{k}
.

The universality (2) means that for any oriented cohomology theory
A^{*}
on
{\rm\bf Sm}_{k}
,
there is a unique natural transformation
\Omega^{*}\to A^{*}
.
In particular, it is known that

\Omega^{*}\otimes_{\mathbb{L}^{*}}\mathbb{Z}\simeq{\rm CH}^{*},\quad\Omega^{*}\otimes_{\mathbb{L}^{*}}\mathbb{Z}[\beta,\beta^{-1}]\simeq K^{0}[\beta,\beta^{-1}],

where
\mathbb{L}
denotes the Lazard ring, which is isomorphic to the absolute ring,
\Omega^{*}(k)
for
{\rm Spec}(k)

[
LM07
]
.

A remarkable approach to re-interpreting
\Omega_{*}
geometrically was later found by Levine-Pandharipande
[
LP09
]
.
Briefly, let us review the geometric construction.

Let
Y\in{\rm\bf Sch}_{k}
. Two morphisms
f:X\to Y
and
f^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to Y
are said to be
isomorphic
if there exists an isomorphism
\varphi:X\to X^{\prime}
so that
f=f^{\prime}\circ\varphi
.
Let
\mathcal{M}(Y)
denote the set of isomorphism classes over
Y

of proper morphisms
f:X\to Y
with
X\in{\rm\bf Sm}_{k}
which is graded by the dimension of
X
.
The set
\mathcal{M}(Y)
is a monoid where the addition of isomorphism classes of
f:X_{1}\to Y
and
g:X_{2}\to Y
is defined by the disjoint union

f+g:X_{1}\sqcup X_{2}\to Y.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{*}^{+}(Y)
be the graded group completion of
\mathcal{M}_{*}(Y)
.

Now, consider a family of morphisms to
Y
; let

F:M\to Y\times\mathbb{P}^{1}

be a proper morphism with
M\in{\rm\bf Sm}_{k}
of pure dimension. Put
pr_{1}
and
pr_{2}
the projections to the first and second factors of
Y\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
, respectively.
Assume that

\pi:=pr_{2}\circ F:M\to\mathbb{P}^{1}

is a
double-point degeneration over
0\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
,
that means that

\pi^{-1}(0)=A\cup B

where
A
and
B
are smooth Cartier divisors intersecting transversely in
M
.
By restricting the map
pr_{1}\circ F
, we obtain maps
A\to Y
and
B\to Y
.
Let

D=A\cap B

and
N_{A/D}
the normal bundles of
D
in
A
. Set

\pi_{P}:P:=\mathbb{P}(N_{A/D}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{D})\to D.

Here it does not matter to distinguish
A
and
B
,
because it holds that

N_{A/D}\otimes N_{B/D}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{D}

and thus the resulting
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundles are isomorphic.
Composing
\pi_{P}
with
pr_{1}\circ F|_{D}
, we have a map
P\to Y
.
Let
p\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
be an arbitrary regular value of
\pi
and
X_{1}
the regular fiber at
p
,
and it yields a map
pr_{1}\circ F:X_{1}\to Y
.
Then an associated
double-point relation over
Y
is defined by

[A\to Y]+[B\to Y]-[P\to Y]-[X_{1}\to Y]\in\mathcal{M}_{*}^{+}(Y).

Let
\mathcal{R}_{*}(Y)
be the subgroup generated by
all
double-point relations over
Y
.
As a special case, if
\pi
is smooth over
0,\infty\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
, we may view
\pi
as a double-point degeneration with
\pi^{-1}(0)=A\cup\emptyset
and
D=\emptyset
, then naïve cobordism is also a double-point relation:

[\pi^{-1}(0)\to Y]-[\pi^{-1}(\infty)\to Y]\in\mathcal{R}_{*}(Y).

.

(
[

5.3.
Formal group law

Let
A^{*}
be an oriented cohomology theory on
{\rm\bf Sm}_{k}
.
For a line bundle
L
on
X
in
{\rm\bf Sm}_{k}
, we have defined
c_{1}(L):=s^{*}s_{*}(1)\in A^{*}(X)
.
In case of
A^{*}={\rm CH}^{*}
, the first Chern class is additive, i.e.,

c_{1}(L\otimes L^{\prime})=c_{1}(L)+c_{1}(L^{\prime})
, but it is not so for other cases.
Namely, there is a unique power series

F_{A}(u,v)=u+v+\sum_{i,j\geq 1}a_{ij}u^{i}v^{j}\in A^{*}(k)[[u,v]]

with
a_{ij}\in A^{1-i-j}(k)
such that

c_{1}(L\otimes M)=F_{A}(c_{1}(L),c_{1}(M))

for any line bundles
L
and
M
on
X
.
The series should satisfy
F_{A}(u,v)=F_{A}(v,u)
and
F_{A}(u,F(v,w))=F_{A}(F(u,v),w)
(thus
a_{ij}
satisfy that
a_{ij}=a_{ji}
and some additional relations caused by the latter property), and then

F
is called a
commutative formal group law
(of rank one).

In particular, for
A^{*}={\rm CH}^{*}
(additive) and
K^{0}[\beta,\beta^{-1}]
(multiplicative), respectively,

F_{ad}(u,v)=u+v,\qquad F_{ml}(u,v)=u+v-\beta uv.

The universal theory
A^{*}=\Omega^{*}
corresponds to the universal formal group law
F_{\mathbb{L}}
.
A computation shows that
a_{11}=-[\mathbb{P}^{1}]
,
a_{12}=a_{21}=[\mathbb{P}^{1}]^{2}-[\mathbb{P}^{2}]
and so on
[
LM07
, §2.5]
. The double-point relation measures the difference of
F_{\mathbb{L}}
from the additive one
F_{ad}=u+v
; for divisors
A
,
B
and
X_{1}
in the above setting,
X_{1}
is linearly equivalent to
A+B
, and thus the double-point relation means

[P\to D]=A+B-F_{\mathbb{L}}(A,B)=-\sum a_{ij}A^{i}B^{j}.

5.4.
Cohomology operations

We refer to a recent work of A. Vishik
[
Vi19
]
on cohomology operations in algebraic geometry;
results which we will later make use of are algebro-geometric counterpart to well-known facts/theorems in complex cobordism theory.

Let
A^{*}
and

be oriented cohomology theories on
{\rm\bf Sm}_{k}

\kappa,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}

A

\textstyle{A^{\kappa}(Y^{\prime})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{B^{\ell}(Y^{\prime})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

(

-

.

is obtained as follows

5.5.
Multi-singularity loci cobordism classes

Let
\underline{\eta}=(\eta_{1},\cdots,\eta_{r})
be a multi-singularity type.
To define
m_{\underline{\eta}}(f)\in{\rm CH}^{*}(X)
and
n_{\underline{\eta}}(f)\in{\rm CH}^{*}(Y)
(Definition

), we have used the fundamental class of the geometric subset in
the Chow group of
X^{[[n]]}\in{\rm\bf Sch}_{k}
:

[\Xi(X;\underline{\eta})]\in{\rm CH}_{*}(X^{[[n]]}).

Instead, fixing a scheme structure, we may take the class of the structure sheaf

[\mathcal{O}_{\Xi(X;\underline{\eta})}]\cdot\beta^{d}\in G_{0}(X^{[[n]]})[\beta,\beta^{-1}]

with
d=\dim\Xi(X;\underline{\eta})
) in the Grothendieck group of coherent sheaves on
X^{[[n]]}
with parameter
\beta
.
It is then straightforward to define
K

m^{K}_{\underline{\eta}}(f)\in K^{0}(X)[\beta,\beta^{-1}],\;\;\;n^{K}_{\underline{\eta}}(f)\in K^{0}(Y)[\beta,\beta^{-1}]

in entirely the same way as in
{\rm CH}^{*}

(e.g., see
[
KS04
, §2.2]
for a summary of Intersection Theory in
K
-theory).

More generally, we are concerned with algebraic cobordism multi-singularity loci classes.

Definition 5.6
.

(
\Omega
-assignment)

Suppose that to every smooth scheme
X
, one assigns a distinguished bordism class

\mu_{\Xi}(X;\underline{\eta})\in\Omega_{*}(X^{[[n]]}).

We call it an
\Omega
-assignment
\mu
for the type
\underline{\eta}
, if the following properties are satisfied:

(1)

the image of
\mu_{\Xi}(X;\underline{\eta})
in
{\rm CH}_{*}(X^{[[n]]})
via the natural transformation is the fundamental class
[\Xi(X;\underline{\eta})]
;

(2)

For any closed embedding
\iota:X^{\prime}\hookrightarrow X
in
{\rm\bf Sm}_{k}
, with
X^{\prime}
and
X
connected, it holds that

\mu_{\Xi}(X^{\prime};\underline{\eta})=(\iota^{[[n]]})^{!}(\mu_{\Xi}(X;\underline{\eta}))\in\Omega_{*}(X^{\prime[[n]]})

where
\iota^{[[n]]}:X^{\prime[[n]]}\to X^{[[n]]}
is the regular embedding obtained in Proposition
3.2

and
(\iota^{[[n]]})^{!}
is the refined Gysin map;

(3)

The assignment to the disjoint union of
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
is of the form

\mu_{\Xi}(X_{1}\sqcup X_{2};\underline{\eta})=\sum_{I\sqcup J}\;\mu_{\Xi}(X_{1};\underline{\eta}_{I})\times\mu_{\Xi}(X_{2};\underline{\eta}_{J})

in certain components of the algebraic cobordism group of the disjoint union

\bigoplus_{I\sqcup J}\;\Omega_{*}(X_{1}^{[[I]]}\times X_{2}^{[[J]]})\subset\Omega_{*}((X_{1}\sqcup X_{2})^{[[n]]})

where the summand runs over all partition
I\sqcup J=\{1,\cdots,r\}
(
I
or
J
can be empty),

[[I]]:=\sum_{i\in I}n(\eta_{i})
, and
\times
is the cross product in
\Omega_{*}
.

To see the

of such an
\Omega
-assignment, we first need to give
\mu_{\Xi}(X;\underline{\eta}_{J})
(for any
J
) for every
connected
smooth scheme
X
so that (1) and (2) are satisfied. That is achieved by Hironaka’s resolution of singularities (see Remark
5.8
below). Then, we take the equality in (3) as the definition of
\mu_{\Xi}(X_{1}\sqcup X_{2};\underline{\eta})
for the disjoint union of connected
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
, and iteratively use this to define
\mu_{\Xi}(X;\underline{\eta})
for
X
having multiple connected components.

Definition 5.7
.

(Multi-singularity loci cobordism classes)

Suppose that we are given an
\Omega
-assignment

\underline{\eta}
.

LM07

For short, we will often write

Remark 5.8
.

(Resolution of singularities)

Such an
\Omega
-assignment can be constructed by equivariant resolution of singularities as briefly explained below. Let
X
be a connected smooth scheme.
Note that
\Xi^{\circ}(X;\underline{\eta})
is a locally closed smooth subscheme of
X^{[[n]]}
given as an orbit of the action of local isomorphisms of
X
at ordered
r
points.
Thus singularities of the Zariski closure
\Xi(X;\underline{\eta})
are located along
some adjacent strata in
\Xi(X;\underline{\eta})-\Xi^{\circ}(X;\underline{\eta})
, and they are determined by the dimension of
X
and local data of more complicated multi-singularity types than
\underline{\eta}
. Namely, any desingularization of
\Xi(X;\underline{\eta})
are essentially indebted on those local data only. For an open set
U\subset\mathbb{A}^{m}
,
choose an embedded resolution of the locus
\Xi(U;\underline{\eta})\subset U^{[[n]]}
so that it is equivariant with respect to the groupoid action of local isomorphisms at
r
points. By glueing such finitely many patches for
X=\bigcup U_{\alpha}
, we obtain an assignment
\mu_{\Xi}(X;\underline{\eta})\in\Omega_{*}(X^{[[n]]})
. When the dimension
m
goes up, additional desingularizations are created along new adjacent strata. In particular, we may first construct a desingularizaton of
\Xi(\mathbb{P}^{N};\underline{\eta})
and go to higher
N
step by step, that produces a projective limit system
\{\mu_{\Xi}(\mathbb{P}^{N};\underline{\eta})\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
. From this system, we obtain
\mu_{\Xi}(X;\underline{\eta})
via the Gysin homomorphism of the inclusion of any smooth
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
, that yields an
\Omega
-assignment which we seek for (at least in case that
X
is quasi-projective).

The
\Omega
-assignment itself is just a bit hard technical matter in theory. Truly interesting is the case that we are given a nice resolution of the geometric subset of a specific singularity type. For instance, there are at least two interesting results on Thom polynomials valued in
\Omega^{*}
as far as the author knows.

Example 5.9
.

(Degeneracy loci cobordism class)
Hudson
[
Hu12
]
has established the
algebraic cobordism Thom-Porteous formula
by using the Bott-Samelson resolution of the closures of Schubert cells (also see
[
HPM20
]
). Those satisfy the above properties, i.e., they define an
\Omega
-assignment.
Note that since the theory
\Omega^{*}
established in
[
LM07
,
LP09
]
, there have appeared many successful works by several authors on
Schubert Calculus
in
\Omega^{*}
and also in other oriented cohomology theory, e.g.,
[
HV11
,
HPM20
]
.

Example 5.10
.

(Double-point loci cobordism class)
Based on
[
Mc76
,
Ro73
]
, Audin
[
Au80
]
presented the
double-point formula in complex cobordism
MU
, which should also be valid in
\Omega^{*}
. In this case, we consider
\underline{\eta}=A_{0}^{2}
(
n=2
) and the geometric subset
\Xi(A_{0}^{2})=X^{[[2]]}={\rm Bl}(X\times X)
which is smooth, thus we have an
\Omega
-assignment for the type

The relative Hilbert scheme provides a desingularization of the double-point locus for a generic map (Remark
3.10
).

\bar{\psi}(\partial_{z^{\prime}})=\alpha^{-1}s_{0}

6.4.4.
Proof of Theorem
4.18

Let
f:X\to Y
be an admissible map of codimension
\kappa(\geq 0)
in
{\rm\bf Sm}_{k}
.
Take the stratification as above, and
put

\hat{m}_{\underline{\eta}}(f):=\sum R_{J_{1}}(f)f^{*}n_{J_{2}}(f)\cdots f^{*}n_{J_{s}}(f)

in
{\rm CH}^{\ell-\kappa}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}
, where the summand runs over all possible partitions

\{1,\cdots,r\}=J_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup J_{s}
with
1\in J_{1}
.
By Lemma
4.12
(1) and a remark after Theorem
4.17
, we see

f_{*}m_{\underline{\eta}}(f)=n_{\underline{\eta}}(f)=f_{*}\hat{m}_{\underline{\eta}}(f)\in{\rm CH}^{\ell}(Y)_{\mathbb{Q}}.

In general
f_{*}
may not be injective, so this formula is insufficient to imply the theorem.

First, we claim that for any types
\underline{\xi}
it holds that

[formula]

i_{\underline{\xi}}^{*}{m}_{\underline{\eta}}(f)=i_{\underline{\xi}}^{*}\hat{m}_{\underline{\eta}}(f)\;\in{\rm CH}^{\ell-\kappa}(M_{\underline{\xi}}^{\circ})_{\mathbb{Q}}.

In fact, applying the above relation to the universal singular map
f_{\underline{\xi}}
, we have

f_{\underline{\xi}*}m_{\underline{\eta}}(f_{\underline{\xi}})=n_{\underline{\eta}}(f_{\underline{\xi}})=f_{\underline{\xi}*}\hat{m}_{\underline{\eta}}(f_{\underline{\xi}})\in{\rm CH}^{\ell}(Y_{\underline{\xi}})_{\mathbb{Q}}.

Since
f_{\underline{\xi}*}
is injective by Lemma
6.22
, we see

m_{\underline{\eta}}(f_{\underline{\xi}})=\hat{m}_{\underline{\eta}}(f_{\underline{\xi}})\in{\rm CH}^{\ell-\kappa}(X_{\underline{\xi}})_{\mathbb{Q}}.

Pulling back the both sides via
\rho_{0}\circ s_{0}
,

i_{\underline{\xi}}^{*}{m}_{\underline{\eta}}(f)=s_{0}^{*}\rho_{0}^{*}m_{\underline{\eta}}(f_{\underline{\xi}})=s_{0}^{*}\rho_{0}^{*}\hat{m}_{\underline{\eta}}(f_{\underline{\xi}})=i_{\underline{\xi}}^{*}\hat{m}_{\underline{\eta}}(f)

where the first equality follows from Lemma
6.24
and the third one from Lemma
6.23
.
So the claim
(*)
is proven.

Recall the excision property
[
Fu84
, §1.8]
: for a closed subscheme
W
of
X
, we have the exact sequence

\textstyle{{\rm CH}_{q}(W)_{\mathbb{Q}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{i_{*}}
\textstyle{{\rm CH}_{q}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{j^{*}}
\textstyle{{\rm CH}_{q}(X-W)_{\mathbb{Q}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}

where
i_{*}
is the pushforward of the inclusion and
j^{*}
is the flat pullback.
Initially we take
W
to be the complement to the open stratum
M_{A_{0}}^{\circ}
, and then replace
X
and
W
, respectively, by
M_{\underline{\xi}}
and
\partial M_{\underline{\xi}}:=M_{\underline{\xi}}-M_{\underline{\xi}}^{\circ}
(the union of boundary strata) according to the adjacency of strata.

Inductively using the excision, it turns out that the class
\hat{m}_{\underline{\eta}}(f)\in{\rm CH}^{\ell-\kappa}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}={\rm CH}_{m-\ell+\kappa}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}
is supported on
{\rm CH}_{m-\ell+\kappa}(M_{\underline{\eta}})_{\mathbb{Q}}
, i.e., for any other type
\underline{\xi}
than
\underline{\eta}
, the class vanishes in
{\rm CH}_{m-\ell+\kappa}(M_{\underline{\xi}}^{\circ})_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Indeed, if
\hat{m}_{\underline{\eta}}(f)
is localized to a class
c\in{\rm CH}_{m-\ell+\kappa}(M_{\underline{\xi}})_{\mathbb{Q}}
, then it is pulled back to
j^{*}c\in{\rm CH}_{m-\ell+\kappa}(M_{\underline{\xi}}^{\circ})_{\mathbb{Q}}
and

s_{0}^{*}s_{0*}j^{*}c=i_{\underline{\xi}}^{*}\hat{m}_{\underline{\eta}}(f)=0

by
(r)
and
(*)
, where
s_{0}:M_{\underline{\xi}}^{\circ}\hookrightarrow{\rm N}_{0}(\underline{\xi})
is the zero section. Then
j^{*}c=0
by our assumption on
f
that
c_{top}({\rm N}_{0}(\underline{\xi}))
is a non-zero divisor
(indeed,
s_{0}^{*}s_{0*}
is just the multiplication by the top Chern class).
Hence, by the excision,
c\in{\rm CH}_{m-\ell+\kappa}(\partial M_{\underline{\xi}})_{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Since
\partial M_{\underline{\xi}}
consists of several strata of codimension higher than that of
\underline{\xi}
, the induction proceeds.

Finally,
(r)
and
(*)
in the case of
\underline{\xi}=\underline{\eta}
shows that
i_{\underline{\eta}}^{*}\hat{m}_{\underline{\eta}}(f)
equals
\#{\rm Aut}(\underline{\eta}/\eta_{1})\cdot[M_{\underline{\eta}}^{\circ}]
in
{\rm CH}_{m-\ell+\kappa}(M_{\underline{\eta}})_{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Thus we conclude that
m_{\underline{\eta}}(f)
and
\hat{m}_{\underline{\eta}}(f)
coincide.

\Box

Remark 6.25
.

(Restriction method)

In case of
\kappa\geq 0
, we have seen that
m_{\underline{\eta}}(f)
is expressed by a unique polynomial of variables
c_{i}(f)
and
s_{I}(f)
, that is the source multi-singularity Thom polynomial of type
\underline{\eta}
.
This fact makes it possible to determine the precise form of the Thom polynomial practically.
Namely, the above formula
(r)
taken for all
\underline{\xi}
gives an overdetermined system of linear equations of unknown coefficients of the polynomial for
\underline{\eta}
, and all that remains is to solve it.
That is the strategy of the restriction method, and there have already been done many explicit computations of Thom polynomials, see e.g.,
[
Ri01
,
FR02
,
Ka03
,
Ka06
,
Oh16
]
.
This method brought a striking breakthrough in this field, because people had previously thought that to get the Thom polynomial for a mono/multi-singularity type is more or less equivalent to explicitly find a nice desingularization of the singularity locus of that type (in general, the latter problem is hard).
Indeed, the problem is now understood separately in two aspects, the hidden combinatorics in computation and the existence of universal expression.
The computation strategy well performs with equivariant geometry, while our proof of the existence of multi-singularity Thom polynomials heavily relies on the existence of desingularizations of geometric subsets (
\Omega
-assignments) that is guaranteed by Hironaka’s resolution of singularities.

7.
Perspectives

7.1.
Further problems

Finally, we collect some of further research directions and explain each of them briefly.

7.1.1.
Thom series and geometric subsets

For mono-singularity types, Thom polynomials have been considered so that
\kappa=\dim Y-\dim X
is involved as a
parameter
– those are often called
Thom series

[
FR12
,
BS12
,
Ka17
]
.
Also for multi-singularity types, it is very natural to expect that there is a universal series containing
\kappa
as a parameter, say
multi-singularity Thom series
(cf. §
2.1.2
). Indeed, by our definition, all multi-singularity loci classes of type
\underline{\eta}
for any
\kappa
are determined at once by the invariant stratum
\Xi(X;\underline{\eta})\subset X^{[[n]]}
which depends on the type of local algebra. Here
Y
and
\kappa
are sort of auxiliary data.
In this sense, the expected theory of multi-singularity Thom series is tied with the geometry of the invariant stratification of Hilbert schemes.

7.1.2.
Universal Segre clsses

Let
\underline{\eta}
be a multi-singularity type of map-germs.
Suppose that
f:X\to Y
(in
{\rm\bf Sm}_{k}
) is a proper locally stable map.
Set
V:=N_{\underline{\eta}}(f)
for short and
\iota:V\hookrightarrow Y
the inclusion.
If
V
is smooth, we may consider the Chern classes
c(TV)
of the tangent bundle and its pushforward into
Y
. In general, however, the locus
V
is singular along adjacent strata, and
then, an alternative to
c(TV)
is the
Chern-Schwartz-MacPherson class
(CSM class) for singular varieties
[
Mac74
]
, denoted by
c^{\rm SM}(V)\in{\rm CH}_{*}(V)
.
More suitably, we make use of the
Segre-Schwartz-MacPherson class
(SSM class) for
\iota:V\hookrightarrow Y
;

s^{{\rm SM}}(V,Y):=\iota^{*}c(TY)^{-1}\frown c^{\rm SM}(V)\;\in{\rm CH}_{*}(V)

(in case that
V
is smooth, then
s^{{\rm SM}}(V,Y)=c(TV-\iota^{*}TY)
).
Consider the SSM class in
{\rm CH}^{*}(Y)
via the pushforward
\iota_{*}
(for
Y
is smooth),
the leading term is nothing but the class
[V]
, thus it is expressed by the Thom polynomial of type
\underline{\eta}
in
Y
. Namely, taking account of the multiplicity (Proposition
4.15
), we have

\#{\rm Aut}(\underline{\eta})\cdot\iota_{*}s^{{\rm SM}}(V,Y)=n_{\underline{\eta}}(f)+h.o.t.\in{\rm CH}^{*}(Y).

In particular, the Euler characteristic of the locus is given by

\chi(V)=\frac{1}{\#{\rm Aut}(\underline{\eta})}\int_{Y}c(TY)\cdot s^{SM}(V,Y).

A general theory of universal SSM classes for singularity loci has been proposed in the author’s previous work
[
Oh16
,
Oh06
,
Oh08
]
.
It was well established for mono-singularity case in
[
Oh06
]
. Afterwards, the theory has been featured in variants of
Schubert Calculus
, e.g., equivalriant conormal Schubert Calculus, by several authors in relation with the theory of stable envelopes due to Okounkov and others, see e.g.,
[
AMS17
,
FRW21
]
.

Also for multi-singularity case, it is natural to expect that each higher order term of the SSM class (in
Y
) is universally expressed in terms of Landweber-Novikov classes, which we call
target higher Thom polynomial
, provisionally.
That would possibly be verified in a similar way as in the present paper, that will be discussed somewhere else. In fact, assuming such universal expressions, some higher Thom polynomials have been computed by the restriction method
[
Oh16
,
PP21
,
NO25
]
and applied successfully to the study on vanishing topology of quasi-homogeneous map-germs
[
Oh16
,
PP21
]
. Also this is related to Chern class of the sheaf of logarithmic vector fields for a free divisor (discriminant)
[
Li18
,
Oh16
]
.

7.1.3.
Generating series

In
[
Ka06
]
Kazarian presented a generating series for target multi-singularity Thom polynomials, that comes from the combinatorial structure of residual polynomials:

1+\sum_{\underline{\eta}}n_{\underline{\eta}}\cdot\frac{t^{\underline{\eta}}}{|{\rm Aut}(\underline{\eta})|}=\exp\left(\sum_{\underline{\eta}}f_{*}(R_{\underline{\eta}})\cdot\frac{t^{\underline{\eta}}}{|{\rm Aut}(\underline{\eta})|}\right).

Also, in a similar way, the author made a conjectural generating series for target higher Thom polynomials (universal SSM classes) in
[
Oh16
, §6.4]
.
It would be very natural to expect that those generating series are directly related to an invariant stratification of
X^{[n]}
mentioned in §
7.1.1
.
For example, in
[
Oh08
,
Ca10
]
, a generating series (zeta function) of the CSM classes of
X^{[[n]]}
is presented in relation with the symmetric product
S^{n}X
.
Its relative version might give some clue for future work.

7.1.4.
Bivariant theory

As mentioned before,
we expect that Theorem
4.18
on the existence of source multi-singularity Thom polynomials should be true for any codimension
\kappa
and for arbitrary maps.
An interesting approach is related to Grothendieck’s
bivariant theory
.
From our approach using cohomology operations, an optimistic hope is that there is a suitable
bivariant algebraic cobordism theory
which admits a bivariant analogue to Vishik’s theorem so that there exists a certain cohomology operation

m_{\underline{\eta},\mu}^{\Omega}:\Omega^{0}(X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\to}}Y)\to\Omega^{\ell-\kappa}(X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\to}}Y)

expressed in terms of Chern classes and Landweber-Novikov classes which satisfies
that the class
[id:X\to X]
is sent to the source loci class
m_{\underline{\eta},\mu}^{\Omega}(f)\in\Omega^{\ell-\kappa}(X)
.
That leads to the desired solution for the existence of the source multi-singularity Thom polynomial in the same principle as in the proof of Theorem
4.17
.
This picture may be discussed better in a wider perspecitve of classifying stacks or derived algebraic geometry (§
7.1.8
, §
7.1.9
).

7.1.5.
Critical cobordism

We have seen in Remark
6.25
that in case of non-negative codimension
\kappa\geq 0
, the restriction method is available for computing certain muti-singularity Thom polynomials.
However, in case of
\kappa<0
, Theorem
4.17
is not enough for fully supporting the use of this method – indeed, in this case, the universal singular map
f_{\underline{\xi}}
is always improper, so Theorem
4.17
can not be applied to it.

This trouble was noticed by Kazarian in
[
Ka06
]
and he introduced
localized Thom polynomials
(still its existence is a conjecture).
Indeed, our true object is the
restriction map on the critical scheme of
f

f_{C}:C(f)\to Y

and it should be enough to assume the properness of
f_{C}
, not that of
f
.
Note that
C(f)
is not always smooth, thus it does not directly yield a cobordism class in
Y
.
Then it is natural to pick up a specified cobordism class
[\tilde{f}_{C}:\tilde{C}(f)\to Y]
by using a canonical desingularization of the first Thom-Boardman singularity, i.e., we take the simplest degeneracy loci class in
\Omega^{*}(X)
for the bundle map
df:TX\to f^{*}TY
in the sense of
[
Hu12
]
and push it out to
\Omega^{*}(Y)
.
We need to characterize this cobordism class and connect it with
m_{\underline{\eta}}(f)
and
n_{\underline{\eta}}(f)
, and seek for a new variant of algebraic cobordism theory, say it provisionally,
critical cobordism
, for this restricted class of proper maps
\tilde{f}_{C}:\tilde{C}(f)\to Y
without starting with
f:X\to Y
.

7.1.6.
Legendre singularities

A particular case of the expected critical cobordism is an algebraic version of
Legendre cobordism theory
(cobordism theory for Legendre maps due to Arnol’d and Vassiliev
[
Va88
]
).
Let
Y
and
L
be in
{\rm\bf Sm}_{k}
such that
\dim Y=\dim L+1
.
The projectived cotangent bundle
\pi:\mathbb{P}(T^{*}Y)\to Y
possesses a canonically defined
contact structure
(i.e., maximally non-integrable hyperplane field).
An immersion
\bar{\varphi}:L\to\mathbb{P}(T^{*}Y)
is said to be
Legendrian
if it is integrable (i.e.,
the image
d\varphi(T_{x}N)
at every point
x\in N
is included in the contact hyperplane), and
the composed map
\varphi:=\pi\circ\bar{\varphi}:L\to Y
is called a
Legendre map

[
AGV85
]
.
Legendre maps form a special class of maps.
For instance, if
f:X\to Y
(
\kappa<0
) is appropriately generic and
\dim{\rm coker}\,df_{x}\leq 1
, then the critical locus
L:=C(f)
is smooth and admits a Legendre immersion
L\to\mathbb{P}(T^{*}Y)
in a canonical way so that its Legendre map, i.e., the composition with
\pi
, coincides with the restriction
f_{C}:L\to Y
.
Legendre singularities has been well explored in Arnol’d’s school in the context of the classification of hypersurface singularities (note that it differs from the classification of ICIS). Legendrian Thom polynomials for mono-singularities, that live in the Chow ring of Lagrange Grasmannian, have been well studied, see
[
Va88
,
Ka03
,
MPW11
]
.
So we have enough material to build new theories of
algebraic Legendre cobordism
and muti-singularity Legendrian Thom polynomials.

7.1.7.
Multi-singularities in
\mathcal{A}
-classification

There are many different versions of classification theories of map-germs. Legendre singularities mentioned above is one of them. Also
\mathcal{A}
-classification is quite important in singularity theory of maps. For locally stable germs,
\mathcal{A}
-classification and
\mathcal{K}
-classification are equivalent
[
Mat69
]
, but for unstable germs,
\mathcal{A}
-classification gets to be much finer. Such unstable singularities appear in families of mappings, e.g., morphisms between total spaces of manifold bundles over the common base, and one seeks for universal expression of the cohomology class represented by the
bifurcation locus
of prescribed type defined as a subscheme of the base.
Thom polynomials in
\mathcal{A}
-classification of mono-germs has been studied in
[
SO18
]
, while there is a large room for developing multi-singularity Thom polynomials in
\mathcal{A}
-classification.
For instance, bifurcation loci of a map from a universal family of curves
[
Mu83
]
to another manifold are quite common objects in algebraic geometry (epsecially the theory of Gromov-Witten invariants).

7.1.8.
Classifying stacks for multi-singularities

The set of all (semi-ordered) multi-singularity types of map-germs of codimension
\kappa
admits a poset-structure via the adjacency relation:
\underline{\xi}<\underline{\tau}
if
\underline{\xi}
is adjacent to
\underline{\tau}
, i.e.,
M_{\underline{\xi}}^{\circ}\subset{\rm Closure}(M_{\underline{\tau}}^{\circ})-M_{\underline{\tau}}^{\circ}
for a stable unfolding of type
\underline{\xi}
. Let
\kappa>0
be fixed.
With respect to this partial order, suppose that we are given a poset
\mathcal{T}
consiting of several multi-singularity types. A
\mathcal{T}
-map

f:X\to Y
is defined to be a proper map in
{\rm\bf Sm}_{k}
having only singularities of type in
\mathcal{T}
.
We may expect that there is a ‘universal
\mathcal{T}
-map’

F_{\mathcal{T}}:\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{T}}\to\mathcal{Y}_{\mathcal{T}}

between certain Artin stacks with global stratifications of quotient stacks

\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{T}}:=\bigsqcup_{\underline{\xi}\in\mathcal{T}}\,X_{\underline{\xi}},\;\;\mathcal{Y}_{\mathcal{T}}:=\bigsqcup_{\underline{\xi}\in\mathcal{T}}\,Y_{\underline{\xi}}

such that for any
\mathcal{T}
-map
f:X\to Y
there exists a morphism (classifying map)
\rho:Y\to\mathcal{Y}_{\mathcal{T}}

whose fiber product with
F_{\mathcal{T}}
recovers
f
in a certain sense (perhaps, up to cobordism):

\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\bar{\rho}}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{T}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{F_{\mathcal{T}}}
\textstyle{Y\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\rho}
\textstyle{\mathcal{Y}_{\mathcal{T}}}

In differential topology, it has been made up as the
Thom-Pontrjagin construction for singular maps
due to Rimányi-Szücs
[
RS98
]
.
In algebraic geometry, the counterpart has not yet been found so far – building blocks, i.e., quotient stacks
X_{\underline{\xi}}
,
Y_{\underline{\xi}}
and the universal singular map
f_{\underline{\xi}}
, have been obtained, but it is totally unclear how to glue them (perhaps, for this glueing, the Hilbert schemes should take a key role).
As for Intersection Theory on Artin stacks, see e.g.,
[
Kr99
]
.

In this picture, one may assign to the cobordism class of
f:X\to Y
the pullback
\rho^{*}[BG_{\underline{\eta}}]\in{\rm CH}^{\ell}(Y)
, that should define our cohomology operation
n_{\underline{\eta}}
.
It is also interesting to relate the universal map
F_{\mathcal{T}}
with an expected bivariant theory (cf. §
7.1.4
).

7.1.9.
Motivic homotopy theory

There is a ‘bigger’ theory of algebraic cobordism due to Morel and Voevodsky, denoted by
{\rm MGL}^{p,q}(-)
with bi-degree, as an algebraic counterpart to stable homotopy theory in topology.
If the base field is of characteristic
0
, there is a natural transformation

\Omega^{\kappa}(Y)\to{\rm MGL}^{2\kappa,\kappa}(Y)

(for
Y\in{\rm\bf Sm}_{k}
) and it is known to be isomorphic (M. Levine).
Beyond this background, it would be very meaningful to
pursue a direct analogy of Thom’s original idea
[
Th71
]
on the Thom-Pontrjagin construction in algebraic geometry side, as Kazarian suggested in
[
Ka03
]
.
In fact, as the classifying space of algebraic cobordism, the
infinite loop space of motivic Thom spectrum

\Omega_{\rm T}^{\infty}\Sigma^{\kappa}_{\rm T}{\rm MGL}_{k}
is recently argued in the context of derived algebraic geometry, e.g., see §1.4 of
[
EHK20
]
.
The cohomology of the loop space must contain all the information of universal polynomials associated to multi-singularity types.
The space should also be related to
\mathcal{Y}_{\mathcal{T}}
mentioned in §
7.1.8
; it approximates the loop space as an Artin stack presentation.

7.1.10.
Direct approach via tautological integrals

There is still room for thinking about a constructive approach which directly generalizes the proof of double-point formula (cf. §
2.1.2
). Consider inductively the class

R_{\underline{\eta}}(f):=m_{\underline{\eta}}(f)-\sum R_{J_{1}}(f)f^{*}n_{J_{2}}(f)\cdots f^{*}n_{J_{s}}(f)

in
{\rm CH}^{\ell-\kappa}(X)
, where the sum runs over the partition with
J_{1},J_{2}\not=\emptyset
.
This class should be understood as the ‘unnecessary contribution from the smallest diagonal’, which is given by the excess intersection of
f^{[[n]]}
along fibers over the diagonal of the Hilbert-Chow morphism, and the problem is to show that
R_{\underline{\eta}}
is indeed uniquely written as a polynomial of Chern classes
c_{i}=c_{i}(f)
.
That should be directly related to tautological integrals on Hilbert schemes
[
Re17
,
Be17
,
BS12
,
Be23
]

and also non-associative Hilbert schemes
[
Ka17
]
.

7.1.11.
Real singularities

Enumerative theory of multi-singularities for real algebraic, real analytic or
C^{\infty}
maps is somewhat mysterious. First we apply the classical Borel-Heafliger theorem (cf. Matszangosz
[
Mts19
]
). That is, taking fixed point sets of the involution for a complex algebraic variety
X(\mathbb{C})
,
we may obtain mod.2 enumerative formulas for multi-singularities of real maps
f:X(\mathbb{R})\to Y(\mathbb{R})
after replacing Chern classes
c_{i}=c_{i}(f)
by the Stiefel-Whitney classes
w_{i}=w_{i}(f)
in
H_{*}(X(\mathbb{R});\mathbb{Z}_{2})
.
Also it is quite meaningful to seek for formulas with integer coefficients using Pontryagin classes, but known results are less, comparing with the case of complex singularities.
Moreover, as a very new direction, it sounds interesting to seek for ‘multiple-point formulas’ in the Chow-Witt cohomology
[
Le19
]
.

7.1.12.
Complex links and quantum invariants

For the link of a complex plane curve singularity, the so-called
Hilbert scheme invariants
comes up in relation with quantum polynomial invariants of the link in the
3
-sphere
S^{3}
. There has not been found any higher dimensional analogue so far. That may be related to the Euler product-type expression of the generating series of weighted Euler characteristics of strata of Hilbert schemes, or of the CSM classes of those strata
[
Oh08
,
Ca10
]
(see §
7.1.3
).
A hope is that for a quasi-homogeneous isolated complete intersection singularity (or hypersurface singularity), we may find Hilbert scheme invariants for the complex link via torus-localization of higher Thom polynomials (universal SSM classes for multi-singularities) mentioned in §
7.1.2
.

7.2.
Concluding remark

We have developed a general enumerative theory of singularities of maps by solving affirmatively Kazarian’s conjecture
[
Ka03
,
Ka06
]
in the context of algebraic geometry over an algebraically closed filed of characteristic
0
. We have shown the existence of unique universal polynomials which express the source and the target multi-singularity loci classes for prescribed type (Theorems
4.17
and
4.18
). Our result also holds suitably for any oriented cohomology theory
A^{*}
(Theorem
6.19
). Main technical ingredients are Hilbert extension maps, desingularization of geometric subsets (
\Omega
-assignments), cohomology operations on algebraic cobordism
\Omega^{*}
, and equivariant geometry with symmetries of singularities.
We remark that in Theorem
4.18
about the source multi-singularity loci classes, we only consider a certainly nice case with
\kappa\geq 0
, thus the full scope of the conjecture, including the setup over a ground field of positive characteristic, still remains open.

Thom polynomial theory connects local and global natures of singularities of maps.
Local classification theory of singularities of maps is essentially the matter of commutative algebra and
has been thoroughly studied for several decades, while the global homotopical nature in algebraic geometry has been developed much later – it was quite recent that key materials have become available, such as algebraic Borel construction, algebraic version of Thom spectra, algebraic coboridisms and algebraic cohomology operations, and so on.
Now, on the basis of these recent achievements in algebraic geometry, we have viewed a new horizon in enumerative geometry of singularities.

Twisted Koszul algebras of nonisolated hypersurface singularities
Sangwook Lee

Abstract
. Given a hypersurface singularity (not necessarily isolated) with a finite abelian group action, we develop a method to define an explicit product structure on the twisted Koszul algebra (whose invariant subalgebra is the orbifold Koszul algebra).



1.
Introduction

Hypersurface singularities seem elementary but are still quite interesting objects due to abundant structures behind them. They have been vigorously studied in various areas such as topology, algebraic geometry, symplectic geometry and representation theory etc.

We can generalize usual hypersurface singularity slightly, by considering a finite group action by
G
on the affine space together with a polynomial
W
, which is invariant under the action. Such a pair
(W,G)
is called an
orbifold Landau-Ginzburg model
. It is interesting in its own right, and it also arises naturally in mirror symmetry in the following manner. Suppose that a symplectic manifold
X
has a LG mirror
W:\Bbbk^{n}\to\Bbbk
. If
\tilde{X}
is a normal covering space of
X
such that its deck transformation group
G
is finite abelian, then it is expected that a mirror of
\tilde{X}
is given by the orbifold LG model
(W,{\widehat{G}})
. See
[
CL20
,
HJL22
]
for example.

We will focus on the computation of Hochschild type invariants among several approaches toward understanding singularities of orbifold LG models. In this aspect, isolated singularities have been investigated more than nonisolated ones so far. When the singularity is isolated, we come up with so-called
orbifold Jacobian algebra
as desired Hochschild invariant. There are works on orbifold Jacobian algebras as
[
CT13
,
BTW23
,
HLL20
,
Shk20
]
. On the other hand, when the orbifold singularity is nonisolated its Hochschild invariant (namely orbifold Koszul algebra) is less understood. Nevertheless, nonisolated orbifold singularities also naturally arise when we study mirror symmetry of noncompact manifolds, e.g.
[
HJL22
]
. In such a case, the orbifold Koszul algebra is expected to be isomorphic to the symplectic cohomology of given noncompact manifold. Since symplectic cohomology is quite difficult to compute in general, it is desirable to know the structure of orbifold Koszul algebras as explicit as possible.

In this work, we investigate orbifold Koszul algebras in terms of matrix factorizations. The original reason for the author to use matrix factorizations (other than curved algebras as in previous works) is as follows: in earlier works
[
CHL17
,
CHL19
]
Cho-Hong-Lau discovered symplectic manifolds with prescribed Lagrangian submanifolds so that we can find their mirrors and prove (homological) mirror symmetry in geometric way. In this context we are naturally led to the category of matrix factorizations of the mirror orbifold LG model, and Hochschild invariants of the category of MFs are encoded in terms of Floer theory. (The relation between Floer theory and category of MFs was crucially used also in
[
HJL22
]
.)

Now we briefly recall the setting and main problem.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
W\in\Bbbk[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]
, and a finite group
G
act on
\Bbbk[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]
such that
W
is invariant under
G
-action. Then
(W,G)
is called an
orbifold Landau-Ginzburg model
.

If an orbifold LG model
(W,G)
further satisfies that

•

G
is abelian,

•

for
h\in G
,
h\cdot x_{i}=h_{i}x_{i}
for some
h_{i}\in\Bbbk^{*}
,

then we call
(W,G)
a
diagonal orbifold LG model
.

In this paper we will only deal with diagonal orbifold LG models. Now we want to define an analogue of Chen-Ruan cohomology for a diagonal orbifold LG model.

(1.1)

K^{\bullet}(W,G):=\bigoplus_{g\in G}K^{\bullet}(\partial W^{g})\cdot\xi_{g}.

Here,
K^{*}(\partial W^{g})
is the Koszul complex of the Jacobian ideal of the function
W|_{{\textrm{Fix}}(g)}
, and
\xi_{g}
is a formal generator which will be precisely defined later. It is rather easy to compute the module structure of
K^{\bullet}(W,G)
, but it is never clear what is the product on it. That is why we are led to consider Hochschild cohomology which has a natural associative algebra structure. We can consider a bimodule over a (curved) algebra or a category of matrix factorizations, whose Hochschild cohomology is isomorphic to
K^{\bullet}(W,G)
as a module. As we mentioned above, we will use matrix factorizations. The following is the starting point:

Theorem A
.

Let
(W,G)
be a diagonal orbifold LG model. Let
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{h}
be Koszul matrix factorizations of the diagonal ideal and the
h
-twisted diagonal ideal respectively (for
h\in G
), of a polynomial

(1.2)

W\boxminus W:=W(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n})-W(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})\in\Bbbk[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}].

Then we have a quasi-isomorphism of chain complexes

(-)_{kos}:\bigoplus_{h\in G}hom_{MF(W\boxminus W)}(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{h})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}K^{\bullet}(W,G).

It is known that
\bigoplus_{h\in G}hom_{MF(W\boxminus W)}(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{h})
is isomorphic to an endomorphism algebra of a
(1\times G)
-equivariant matrix factorization of
W\boxminus W
, so we can define an intrinsic product structure on
K^{\bullet}(W,G)
via the quasi-isomorphism. The remaining problem is therefore to construct a quasi-inverse of
(-)_{kos}
, and it is the main advance of this work.

Theorem B
.

Let
W\in R=\Bbbk[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]
,
S=\Bbbk[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}]
and
(W,G)
be a diagonal orbifold LG model. For
i,j\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
and
h\in G
, there exist polynomials
g_{ji}^{h}\in S
and
f_{ji}^{h}\in R
such that for

\displaystyle\eta_{h}:S[\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{n},\partial_{1},\cdots,\partial_{n}]

\displaystyle\to S[\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{n},\partial_{1},\cdots,\partial_{n}],

\displaystyle\theta_{I}\partial_{J}

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum(-1)^{|I|}g^{h}_{ji}\frac{\partial\theta_{I}}{\partial\theta_{i}}\partial_{j}\partial_{J}+\sum f^{h}_{ji}\frac{\partial^{2}\theta_{I}}{\partial\theta_{j}\partial\theta_{i}}\partial_{J},

the map
\exp(\eta_{h})
is a quasi-inverse of
(-)_{kos}
.

Finally, we will illustrate an example which is relevant in mirror symmetry. This will not only be helpful in understanding the recipe, but also reflect an interesting geometric feature of our result, in the sense that we can observe a similarity between an orbifold Koszul algebra and a symplectic cohomology ring by looking at the explicit algebra structure.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\theta_{i}
and
\partial_{i}
(for
i=1,\cdots,n
) be formal variables with
|\theta_{i}|=-1
,
|\partial_{i}|=1
and

(2.1)

\theta_{i}\theta_{j}=-\theta_{j}\theta_{i},\;\;\partial_{i}\partial_{j}=-\partial_{j}\partial_{i},\;\;\partial_{i}\theta_{j}=-\theta_{j}\partial_{i}+\delta_{ij}

Let
I=\{i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}\}
be an ordered subset of
\{1,\cdots,n\}
. We will frequently use following multi-index notations:

\theta_{I}:=\theta_{i_{1}}\cdots\theta_{i_{k}},\quad\partial_{I}:=\partial_{i_{1}}\cdots\partial_{i_{k}}.

We recall an elementary definition in terms of above variables.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
A
be a commutative ring. For a sequence
(r_{1},\cdots,r_{n})
of elements in
A
,

K^{\bullet}(r_{1},\cdots,r_{n}):=(A\langle\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{n}\rangle,\sum_{i}r_{i}\partial_{i})

is called the
Koszul complex
of
(r_{1},\cdots,r_{n})
.

The cochain complex
K^{\bullet}(r_{1},\cdots,r_{n})
is explicitly spelled out as

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{A\cdot\theta_{1}\cdots\theta_{n}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sum_{i}r_{i}\partial_{i}}
\textstyle{\bigoplus\limits_{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{n-1}}A\cdot\theta_{i_{1}}\cdots\theta_{i_{n-1}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\cdots\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\bigoplus\limits_{i}A\cdot\theta_{i}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sum_{i}r_{i}\partial_{i}}
\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}

with
\bigoplus\limits_{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m}}A\cdot\theta_{i_{1}}\cdots\theta_{i_{m}}=K^{-m}(r_{1},\cdots,r_{n})
.
Koszul complexes can be used to define invariants of singularities as follows.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(W,G)
be a diagonal orbifold LG model. For
h\in G
define

x_{i}^{h}:=\begin{cases}x_{i}&{\textrm{ if }}h\cdot x_{i}=x_{i}\\
0&{\textrm{ if }}h\cdot x_{i}\neq x_{i}\end{cases},\quad W^{h}:=W(x_{1}^{h},\cdots,x_{n}^{h})\in\Bbbk[x_{1}^{h},\cdots,x_{n}^{h}].

Let

I_{h}:=\{i\mid h\cdot x_{i}\neq x_{i}\},\quad I^{h}:=I_{h}^{c}

be ordered subsets of
\{1,\cdots,n\}
and define a sequence
\partial W^{h}:=\big(\frac{\partial W^{h}}{\partial x_{i}}:i\in I^{h}\big)
in
\Bbbk[x_{1}^{h},\cdots,x_{n}^{h}]
. The
twisted Koszul complex
of
(W,G)
is defined as

K^{\bullet}(W,G):=\bigoplus_{h\in G}K^{\bullet}(\partial W^{h})\cdot\theta_{I_{h}}.

Defining
G
-action on variables
\{\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{n},\partial_{1},\cdots,\partial_{n}\}
by

h\cdot\theta_{i}:=h_{i}^{-1}\theta_{i},\quad h\cdot\partial_{i}:=h_{i}\partial_{i}

when
h\cdot x_{i}=h_{i}x_{i}
, we call

K^{\bullet}(W,G)^{G}=\bigoplus_{h\in G}\big(K^{\bullet}(\partial W^{h})\cdot\theta_{I_{h}}\big)^{G}

the
orbifold Koszul complex
of
(W,G)
, where
\big(K^{\bullet}(\partial W^{h})\cdot\theta_{I_{h}}\big)^{G}
is the subcomplex of
G
-invariants of
K^{\bullet}(\partial W^{h})\cdot\theta_{I_{h}}
.

Observe that as each
h
-summand we did not just take the Koszul complex of Jacobian ideal of
W^{h}
, but shifted it by
\theta_{I_{h}}
. Such a modification will appear meaningful later.

We recall another important invariant of
W
.

Definition 2.3
.

A
matrix factorization
of
W\in R
is a
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded projective
R
-module
P=P_{0}\oplus P_{1}
together with a morphism
d=(d_{0},d_{1})
of degree 1 such that

d^{2}=W\cdot\mathrm{id}.

Let
\phi:(P,d)\to(Q,d^{\prime})
be a morphism of degree
j\in\mathbb{Z}/2
. Define

D\phi:=d^{\prime}\circ\phi-(-1)^{|\phi|}\phi\circ d

and define the composition of morphisms in the usual sense. This defines a dg-category of matrix factorizations
(MF(W),D,\circ)
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
(W,G)
be a diagonal orbifold LG model and
S=\Bbbk[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}]
. Let

\nabla_{j}W:=\frac{W(y_{1},\cdots,y_{j},x_{j+1},\cdots,x_{n})-W(y_{1},\cdots,y_{j-1},x_{j},\cdots,x_{n})}{y_{j}-x_{j}},

and let

\Delta_{h}:=\Big(S\langle\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{n}\rangle,\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big((y_{i}-hx_{i})\theta_{i}+\nabla_{i}W(h\cdot x_{1},\cdots,h\cdot x_{n},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n})\cdot\partial_{i}\big)\Big)

be a matrix factorization of
W\boxminus W
(in (
1.2
)). Here
S\langle\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{n}\rangle
is
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded, by
division into even/odd degrees.

Now we introduce the first key step towards our main result.

Proposition 2.5
.

(
[
HJL22
, Proposition 2.6]
)
We have a quasi-isomorphism of chain complexes

\bigoplus_{h\in G}hom_{MF(W\boxminus W)}(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{h})\simeq K^{\bullet}(W,G).

We will need the following part of the proof of Proposition
2.5
. Since
\Delta_{h}
is constructed by the resolution of a shifted MCM module
S/(y_{1}-hx_{1},\cdots,y_{n}-hx_{n})[-n]
over the hypersurface ring
S/(W\boxminus W)
, we have quasi-isomorphisms

(2.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}hom_{MF(W\boxminus W)}(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{h})&\simeq hom_{S}\big(\Delta_{1},S/(y_{1}-hx_{1},\cdots,y_{n}-hx_{n})[-n]\big)\\
&\simeq hom_{S}\big(\Delta_{1}[n],S/(y_{1}-hx_{1},\cdots,y_{n}-hx_{n})\big)\\
&\simeq\Delta_{1}^{\vee}[-n]\otimes_{S}\big(S/(y_{1}-hx_{1},\cdots,y_{n}-hx_{n})\big).\end{split}

The latter chain complex in (
2.2
) is a double complex, and the proof involves typical spectral sequence argument.

We can spell out a quasi-isomorphism explicitly as follows.
Let
\phi\in hom_{MF(W(y)-W(x))}(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{h})
be a closed morphism. By (
2.2
) its cohomology class
[\phi]
is completely determined by
pr\circ\phi
, where
pr:\Delta_{h}\to S\cdot\theta_{1}\cdots\theta_{n}
is the projection map. We have a unique expression

(2.3)

pr\circ\phi=\sum_{J,K}b_{JK}\theta_{J}\theta_{K}\partial_{K^{op}}

such that
J
and
K
are ordered subsets of
\{1,\cdots,n\}
,
b_{JK}\in S
, and
\partial_{K^{op}}:=\partial_{k_{m}}\cdots\partial_{k_{1}}
whereas
\theta_{K}=\theta_{k_{1}}\cdots\theta_{k_{m}}
. We point out that
K
may be empty. We also define

(pr\circ\phi)_{+}:=\sum_{J:I_{h}\subset J}\sum_{K}b_{JK}\theta_{J}\theta_{K}\partial_{K^{op}}.

For an
S
-module homomorphism

\phi=\sum f_{IJ}(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n},x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})\theta_{I}\partial_{J}
we define

(2.4)

\phi|_{{\textrm{Fix}}(h)}:=\sum f_{IJ}(x_{1}^{h},\cdots,x_{n}^{h},x_{1}^{h},\cdots,x_{n}^{h})\theta_{I}\partial_{J}.

With respect to the expression (
2.3
) and notation (
2.4
), the quasi-isomorphism

(-)_{kos}:hom(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{h})\to K^{\bullet}(\partial_{x_{i}}W^{h}:i\in I^{h})\cdot\theta_{I_{h}}

can be realized as

\phi_{kos}=\big(\sum_{J:I_{h}\subset J}\sum_{K}b_{JK}\theta_{J}\big)|_{{\textrm{Fix}}(h)}.

Observe that
\phi_{kos}
is determined only by
(pr\circ\phi)_{+}
.

3.
Classification of closed morphisms of matrix factorizations

This section is devoted to the construction of a quasi-inverse of
(-)_{kos}
.
First, for
0\leq j<i\leq n
and
h\in G
, define

{\overline{W}}^{h}_{j,i}:=W(y_{1},\cdots,y_{j},x_{j+1},\cdots,x_{i},hx_{i+1},\cdots,hx_{n})\in S,

{\widetilde{W}}^{h}_{j,i}:=W(x_{1}^{h},\cdots,x_{j}^{h},x_{j+1},\cdots,x_{i},hx_{i+1},\cdots,hx_{n})\in R.

(Recall that
x_{i}^{h}=x_{i}
if
hx_{i}=x_{i}
and
x_{i}^{h}=0
otherwise.) We also define

(3.1)

{\overline{W}}^{h}_{i,i}:=W(y_{1},\cdots,y_{i},hx_{i+1},\cdots,hx_{n}),

(3.2)

{\widetilde{W}}^{h}_{i,i}:=W(x_{1}^{h},\cdots,x_{i}^{h},hx_{i+1},\cdots,hx_{n}).

For
i,j\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
with
j<i
, define

(3.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}g^{h}_{ji}&:=\begin{cases}\frac{({\overline{W}}^{h}_{j,i}-{\overline{W}}^{h}_{j-1,i})-({\overline{W}}^{h}_{j,i-1}-{\overline{W}}^{h}_{j-1,i-1})}{(y_{j}-x_{j})(x_{i}-hx_{i})}&{\rm if\;}i\in I_{h},\\
\frac{\partial_{i}{\overline{W}}^{h}_{j,i}-\partial_{i}{\overline{W}}^{h}_{j-1,i}}{y_{j}-x_{j}}&{\rm if\;}i\in I^{h},\end{cases}\\
f^{h}_{ji}&:=\begin{cases}\frac{({\widetilde{W}}^{h}_{j,i}-{\widetilde{W}}^{h}_{j-1,i})-({\widetilde{W}}^{h}_{j,i-1}-{\widetilde{W}}^{h}_{j-1,i-1})}{(x_{j}-hx_{j})(x_{i}-hx_{i})}&{\rm if\;}i,j\in I_{h},\\
\frac{\partial_{i}{\widetilde{W}}^{h}_{j,i}-\partial_{i}{\widetilde{W}}^{h}_{j-1,i}}{x_{j}-hx_{j}}&{\rm if\;}j\in I_{h}{\rm\;and\;}i\in I^{h},\\
\frac{(\partial_{j}{\widetilde{W}}^{h}_{j,i}-\partial_{i}{\widetilde{W}}^{h}_{j,i-1})-({\partial_{j}{\widetilde{W}}^{h}_{i-1,i-1}-\partial_{j}{\widetilde{W}}^{h}_{i,i}})}{x_{i}-hx_{i}}&{\rm if\;}j\in I^{h}{\rm\;and\;}\;i\in I_{h},\\
0&{\rm otherwise}\end{cases}\end{split}

and

(3.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}g^{h}_{ii}&:=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{y_{i}-x_{i}}\cdot\Big(\frac{{\overline{W}}^{h}_{i,i}-{\overline{W}}^{h}_{i-1,i-1}}{y_{i}-hx_{i}}-\frac{{\overline{W}}^{h}_{i-1,i}-{\overline{W}}^{h}_{i-1,i-1}}{x_{i}-hx_{i}}\Big)&{\rm if\;}i\in I_{h},\\
\frac{1}{y_{i}-x_{i}}\cdot\Big(\frac{{\overline{W}}^{h}_{i,i}-{\overline{W}}^{h}_{i-1,i-1}}{y_{i}-hx_{i}}-\partial_{i}{\overline{W}}^{h}_{i-1,i}\Big)&{\rm if\;}i\in I^{h}.\end{cases}\end{split}

Observe that
g^{h}_{ji}=0
if
j>i
, and
f^{h}_{ji}=0
if
j\geq i
. Also, if
i\in I^{h}
then
\frac{{\overline{W}}^{h}_{i,i}-{\overline{W}}^{h}_{i-1,i-1}}{y_{i}-hx_{i}}-\partial_{i}{\overline{W}}^{h}_{i-1,i}
is indeed divisible by
y_{i}-x_{i}
because it vanishes after we evaluate
y_{i}=x_{i}
, so
g_{ii}^{h}
is also well-defined.

For
S=k[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}]
, define an
S
-linear map

\displaystyle\eta_{h}:S[\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{n},\partial_{1},\cdots,\partial_{n}]

\displaystyle\to S[\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{n},\partial_{1},\cdots,\partial_{n}],

(3.5)

\displaystyle\theta_{I}\partial_{J}

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum(-1)^{|I|}g^{h}_{ji}\frac{\partial\theta_{I}}{\partial\theta_{i}}\partial_{j}\partial_{J}+\sum f^{h}_{ji}\frac{\partial^{2}\theta_{I}}{\partial\theta_{j}\partial\theta_{i}}\partial_{J}.

We also define a map

(3.6)

\exp(\eta_{h}):=1+\eta_{h}+\frac{\eta_{h}^{2}}{2!}+\cdots\;\;:S[\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{n},\partial_{1},\cdots,\partial_{n}]\to S[\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{n},\partial_{1},\cdots,\partial_{n}].

(
3.6
) is a finite sum due to graded commutativity of
\theta
and
\partial
, hence well-defined.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
h\in G
, and let
\displaystyle\sum_{|I|=r}a_{I}\theta_{I}\theta_{I_{h}}
be a cocycle of the Koszul complex of
(\partial_{i}W^{h}:i\in I^{h})
where
a_{I}\in k[x_{1}^{h},\cdots,x_{n}^{h}]
is identified as an element of
S
by natural inclusion. Then

\exp(\eta_{h})\big(\sum a_{I}\theta_{I}\theta_{I_{h}}\big)
is a closed element in
hom_{MF(W(y)-W(x))}(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{h})
.

Proof.

Throughout the proof, we omit all upper indices
h
, i.e.

g_{ji}=g^{h}_{ji},\;\;f_{ji}=f^{h}_{ji},\;\;{\overline{W}}={\overline{W}}^{h},\;\;{\widetilde{W}}={\widetilde{W}}^{h}.

Let
f\theta_{I}\partial_{J}\in hom_{MF(W(y)-W(x))}(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{h})
. For the differential
D
of the chain complex
hom_{MF(W(y)-W(x))}(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{h})
, decompose
D={D^{\uparrow}}+{D^{\downarrow}}
as follows:

\displaystyle{D^{\uparrow}}(f\theta_{I}\partial_{J}):=

\displaystyle\sum(y_{i}-hx_{i})\theta_{i}\cdot f\theta_{I}\partial_{J}+(-1)^{|I|+|J|+1}\sum f\theta_{I}\partial_{J}\cdot(y_{i}-x_{i})\theta_{i},

\displaystyle{D^{\downarrow}}(f\theta_{I}\partial_{J}):=

\displaystyle\sum\nabla_{i}W(hx,y)\partial_{i}\cdot f\theta_{I}\partial_{J}+(-1)^{|I|+|J|+1}\sum f\theta_{I}\partial_{J}\cdot\nabla_{i}W(x,y)\partial_{i}.

Step 1
.

We have

\displaystyle{D^{\uparrow}}\big(\sum_{I}a_{I}\theta_{I}\theta_{I_{h}}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}\sum_{I}(y_{i}-hx_{i})\theta_{i}\cdot a_{I}\theta_{I}\theta_{I_{h}}+\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}\sum_{I}(-1)^{|I|+|I_{h}|+1}a_{I}\theta_{I}\theta_{I_{h}}\cdot(y_{i}-x_{i})\theta_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I^{h}}\sum_{I}(y_{i}-hx_{i})\theta_{i}\cdot a_{I}\theta_{I}\theta_{I_{h}}+\sum_{i\in I^{h}}\sum_{I}(-1)^{|I|+|I_{h}|+1}a_{I}\theta_{I}\theta_{I_{h}}\cdot(y_{i}-x_{i})\theta_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I^{h}}\sum_{I}(y_{i}-x_{i})\theta_{i}\cdot a_{I}\theta_{I}\theta_{I_{h}}-\sum_{i\in I^{h}}\sum_{I}(y_{i}-x_{i})\theta_{i}\cdot a_{I}\theta_{I}\theta_{I_{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Step 2
.

Now we spell out
{D^{\downarrow}}\Big(\frac{\eta_{h}^{k}}{k!}\big(a_{I}\theta_{I}\theta_{I_{h}}\big)\Big)
and
{D^{\uparrow}}\Big(\frac{\eta_{h}^{k+1}}{(k+1)!}\big(a_{I}\theta_{I}\theta_{I_{h}}\big)\Big)
for each
I\subset I^{h}
.

By graded commutativity (see
[
Lee22
]
for more detail), we have

(3.7)

\frac{\eta_{h}^{k}}{k!}(a_{I}\theta_{I}\theta_{I_{h}})=\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle l+m=k}}{{1\leq i_{\bullet},j_{\bullet}\leq n}}}(-1)^{\epsilon_{l}}g_{j_{1}i_{1}}\cdots g_{j_{l}i_{l}}f_{i_{l+1}i_{l+2}}\cdots f_{i_{l+2m-1}i_{l+2m}}\cdot a_{I}\frac{\partial^{l+2m}(\theta_{I}\theta_{I_{h}})}{\partial\theta_{i_{1}}\cdots\partial\theta_{i_{l+2m}}}\partial_{j_{1}}\cdots\partial_{j_{l}},

with the sign given by

\epsilon_{l}:=l(|I|+|I_{h}|)-\frac{l(l-1)}{2}.

The following identities will be frequently (and implicitly) used in the sequel:

(-1)^{|I|+|I_{h}|+\epsilon_{l}+l}=(-1)^{\epsilon_{l+1}},\quad(-1)^{\epsilon_{l+2}}=(-1)^{\epsilon_{l}+1}.

We introduce a notation

\Phi_{(j_{1},i_{1}),\cdots,(j_{l},i_{l})}^{(i_{l+1},i_{l+2}),\cdots,(i_{l+2m-1},i_{l+2m})}:=g_{j_{1}i_{1}}\cdots g_{j_{l}i_{l}}f_{i_{l+1}i_{l+2}}\cdots f_{i_{l+2m-1}i_{l+2m}}.

Then we can write

\displaystyle{D^{\downarrow}}\Big(\frac{\eta_{h}^{k}}{k!}\big(a_{I}\theta_{I}\theta_{I_{h}}\big)\Big)

Theorem 4.1
.

H^{\bullet}(K^{\bullet}(W,\mathbb{Z}/2))^{\mathbb{Z}/2}\cong SH^{\bullet}(T^{*}S^{1})
.

Proof.

Recall that
SH^{\bullet}(T^{*}S^{1})\cong\mathbb{C}[x,x^{-1}][\theta]
where
\theta^{2}=0
. Observe that
\mathbb{C}[x,x^{-1}][\theta]
is isomorphic to a quotient of free commutative algebra:

\mathbb{C}[x,x^{-1}][\theta]\cong\mathbb{C}[\alpha,\beta,\gamma]/(\alpha\beta-1,\gamma^{2}).

Define an algebra homomorphism from the free commutative algebra as

\displaystyle\mu:\mathbb{C}[\alpha,\beta,\gamma]

\displaystyle\to H^{\bullet}(K^{\bullet}(W,\mathbb{Z}/2))^{\mathbb{Z}/2},

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle\mapsto\lambda_{+}:=\big(\frac{x_{3}}{2}\big)^{1}+(\theta_{1}\theta_{2})^{\rho},

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle\mapsto\lambda_{-}:=\big(\frac{x_{3}}{2}\big)^{1}-(\theta_{1}\theta_{2})^{\rho},

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle\mapsto\tau:=(\theta_{1}\theta_{2}\theta_{3})^{\rho}.

\mu
is surjective due to

\mu(\alpha+\beta)=(x_{3})^{1},\quad\mu\big(\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}\big)=(\theta_{1}\theta_{2})^{\rho},\quad\mu(\gamma)=(\theta_{1}\theta_{2}\theta_{3})^{\rho},

\quad\mu(\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}\cdot\gamma)=(\theta_{1}\theta_{2})^{\rho}\cup_{kos}(\theta_{1}\theta_{2}\theta_{3})^{\rho}=\Big(\frac{x_{2}}{2}\theta_{1}-\frac{x_{2}x_{3}}{4}\theta_{2}+\big(\frac{x_{3}^{2}}{4}-1\big)\theta_{3}\Big)^{1},

meaning that we recover all generators of
H^{\bullet}(K^{\bullet}(W,\mathbb{Z}/2))^{\mathbb{Z}/2}
by
\mu
. We also check that

\lambda_{+}\cup_{kos}\lambda_{-}=1,\quad\tau\cup_{kos}\tau=0

completely determine the relation of multiplications between them, hence

\ker\mu=(\alpha\beta-1,\gamma^{2}).\qed

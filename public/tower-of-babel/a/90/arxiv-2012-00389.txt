Nonlocal characterizations of variable exponent Sobolev spaces Thanks: The second author is member of Gruppo Nazionale per l’Analisi Matematica, la Probabilità e le loro Applicazioni (GNAMPA) of the Istituto Nazionale di Alta Matematica (INdAM)
Gianluca Ferrari, Marco Squassina

Abstract
. We obtain some nonlocal characterizations for a class of variable exponent Sobolev spaces arising in nonlinear elasticity theory and in the theory of electrorheological fluids. We also get a singular limit formula extending Nguyen results to the anisotropic case.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
35J92, 35P30, 34L16

1.
Introduction

In the last twenty years, starting from the work by Bourgain, Brezis and Mironescu
[
5
]
,
there has been a considerable effort in the literature to provide some useful nonlocal characterizations
of functions in Sobolev spaces. The results in
[
5
]
are mainly for
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
on a bounded domain
\Omega
, but they can be
extended to the whole space setting. In particular, if
p\in(1,+\infty)
and
u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
u\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if

\sup_{s\in(0,1)}(1-s)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+ps}}\,dx\,dy<+\infty,

in which case

\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(1-s)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+ps}}\,dx\,dy=K_{n,p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{p}dx,

where

(1.1)

K_{n,p}=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left|\,\omega\cdot\boldsymbol{e}\,\right|^{p}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega),\quad\boldsymbol{e}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

Another nonlocal characterization was obtained by Nguyen in
[
10
,
11
,
12
]

involving a nonhomogenous functional. More precisely,
if
p\in(1,+\infty)
and
u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
u\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if

\sup_{\delta\in(0,1)}\underset{|u(x)-u(y)|>\delta}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\frac{\delta^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+p}}\,dx\,dy<+\infty,

in which case

\lim_{\delta\searrow 0}\underset{|u(x)-u(y)|>\delta}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\frac{\delta^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+p}}\,dx\,dy=K_{n,p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx.

Nonhomogeneous quantities like these appear in some new estimates for the topological degree investigated in
[
4
]
. The limiting
case
p=1
is related to BV functions but it is actually more delicate, see
[
10
,
11
]
.
On the other hand, differential equations and variational problems involving variable
p(x)
-growth conditions, and hence variable exponent Sobolev spaces
W^{1,p(\cdot)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, arise
from nonlinear elasticity theory and electrorheological fluids, and have been the target of various investigations, especially in regularity theory and in nonlocal problems
(see e.g.
[
1
,
2
,
14
,
7
]
).

Let
p:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[1,+\infty)
be a measurable function. Let’s define

p^{-}:=\operatornamewithlimits{ess\,inf}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}p\qquad\text{and}\qquad p^{+}:=\operatornamewithlimits{ess\,sup}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}p.

For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we set

(1.2)

K_{n,p(x)}:=\frac{1}{p(x)}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left|\omega\cdot\boldsymbol{e}\right|^{p(x)}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega),\quad\boldsymbol{e}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

We also set

W^{1,p^{\pm}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right):=W^{1,p^{+}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\cap W^{1,p^{-}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right).

In this framework, in the spirit of the results of
[
10
]
, we have the following

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
1<p^{-}\leq p^{+}<+\infty
and let
u\in W^{1,p^{\pm}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
. Then

(a)

there exists
C>0
, depending only on
n
and
p^{\pm}
, such that for every
\delta>0

(1.3)

\underset{|u(x)-u(y)|>\delta}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\frac{\delta^{p(x)}}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy\leq C\left(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p^{+}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}^{p^{+}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p^{-}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}^{p^{-}}\right);

(b)

we have

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\underset{|u(x)-u(y)|>\delta}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\frac{\delta^{p(x)}}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K_{n,p(x)}|\nabla u(x)|^{p(x)}\,dx;

(c)

if
u\in L^{p(\cdot)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
and

\sup_{0<\delta<1}\underset{|u(x)-u(y)|>\delta}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\frac{\delta^{p(x)}}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy<+\infty,

then
u\in W^{1,p(\cdot)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
.

Unfortunately, it has not been possible to provide a limit formula in the more general context of the Sobolev space
W^{1,p(\cdot)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
,
the basic problem being that the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function in one direction on
L^{p(\cdot)}
fails to be bounded (for the modular)
unless
p(\cdot)
is a constant
[
6
]
, see also
[
9
]
.
In the limit case
p^{-}=1
, let
u\in L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

E=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:p(x)=1\}.
Assume
{\rm int}(E)\neq\emptyset
and

\sup_{0<\delta<1}\underset{|u(x)-u(y)|>\delta}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\frac{\delta^{p(x)}}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy<+\infty.

Then, if
B
denotes any ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, it holds

\sup_{B\subset{\rm int}(E)}|B|^{-\frac{n+1}{n}}\int_{B}\int_{B}|u(x)-u(y)|\,dx\,dy<+\infty,

which, for
n=1
, reads as

\sup_{B\subset{\rm int}(E)}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B}|u(x)-u(y)|\,dx\,dy<+\infty,

namely
u\in BMO(E)
, the space of bounded mean oscillation functions on
E
. See Remark
4.6
.

2.
Preliminary stuff

In this section we recall some basic properties of the variable exponent spaces, see
[
3
,
7
,
8
]
.

2.1.
Variable exponents spaces

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a measurable set and let
p:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[1,+\infty)
be a measurable function. We define
L^{p(\cdot)}\left(\Omega\right)
as the space of measurable functions
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
with

\rho_{p(\cdot)}(u):=\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p(x)}\,dx<+\infty,

so, denoting by
M\left(\Omega\right)
the space of measurable functions on the domain
\Omega
, we set

L^{p\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\Omega\right):=\left\{u\in M\left(\Omega\right):\rho_{p(\cdot)}(u)<+\infty\right\}.

The function
p
is called exponent of
L^{p(\cdot)}\left(\Omega\right)
, while
\rho_{p(\cdot)}(u)
is the modular of
u
.
If
u\in L^{p(\cdot)}\left(\Omega\right)
,

\|u\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}\left(\Omega\right)}:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\rho_{p(\cdot)}\left(\frac{u}{\lambda}\right)\leq 1\right\}

is a norm for
L^{p(\cdot)}\left(\Omega\right)
, called Luxemburg norm, which makes the space complete.
In other words,
L^{p(\cdot)}\left(\Omega\right)
is a Banach space with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}\left(\Omega\right)}
.
Taken a locally integrable function
\omega:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(0,+\infty)
, we can introduce a weighed version of variable exponent Lebesgue spaces. We define
L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega,\omega)
as the space of measurable functions
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\rho_{p(\cdot),\,\omega}(u):=\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p(x)}\,\omega(x)\,dx<+\infty,

so we can set

L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega,\omega):=\left\{u\in M\left(\Omega\right):\rho_{p(\cdot),\,\omega}(u)<+\infty\right\}.

The function
\omega
is called weight of the space. Moreover,
L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega,\omega)
is a Banach space with norm

\|u\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}\left(\Omega,\omega\right)}:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\rho_{p(\cdot),\,\omega}\left(\frac{u}{\lambda}\right)\leq 1\right\}.

The following relationship between the norm and the modular holds:

(2.1)

\min\left\{\rho_{p(\cdot),\,\omega}(u)^{\frac{1}{p^{-}}},\rho_{p(\cdot),\,\omega}(u)^{\frac{1}{p^{+}}}\right\}\leq\|u\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}\left(\Omega,\,\omega\right)}\leq\max\left\{\rho_{p(\cdot),\,\omega}(u)^{\frac{1}{p^{-}}},\rho_{p(\cdot),\,\omega}(u)^{\frac{1}{p^{+}}}\right\}.

We denote by
W^{1,p(\cdot)}\left(\Omega\right)
the space of

u\in L^{p(\cdot)}\left(\Omega\right)
such that their gradient
\nabla u\in L^{p(\cdot)}\left(\Omega\right)
, so that

W^{1,p(\cdot)}\left(\Omega\right):=\left\{u\in L^{p(\cdot)}\left(\Omega\right):\exists\nabla u\in L^{p(\cdot)}\left(\Omega\right)\right\}.

If
u\in W^{1,p(\cdot)}\left(\Omega\right)
, the object

\|u\|_{W^{1,p(\cdot)}\left(\Omega\right)}:=\|u\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}\left(\Omega\right)}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}\left(\Omega\right)}

is a norm for
W^{1,p(\cdot)}\left(\Omega\right)
. Moreover,
W^{1,p(\cdot)}\left(\Omega\right)
is a Banach space
with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{W^{1,p(\cdot)}\left(\Omega\right)}
.

2.2.
Fractional Sobolev spaces

If
s\in(0,1)
, we can also extend the concept of fractional Sobolev space to the variable exponent case, as follows.
Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a measurable set and let
p:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[1,+\infty)
and
q:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[1,+\infty)
be two measurable functions. If we suppose that
p
and
q
are two bounded exponents, then there exist
p^{+},p^{-},q^{+},q^{-}\in[1,+\infty)
such that

\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}:\qquad p^{-}\leq p(x,y)\leq p^{+},\qquad q^{-}\leq q(x)\leq q^{+}.

Taken
s\in(0,1)
, we denote by
W=W^{s,q(\cdot),p(\cdot\,,\,\cdot)}\left(\Omega\right)
the functions space

W:=\left\{u\in L^{q(\cdot)}\left(\Omega\right):\exists\lambda>0:\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p(x,y)}}{\lambda^{p(x,y)}|x-y|^{n+sp(x,y)}}\,dx\,dy<+\infty\right\}.

If we set the variable exponent seminorm as

[u]_{s,p(\cdot\,,\,\cdot)}:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p(x,y)}}{\lambda^{p(x,y)}|x-y|^{n+sp(x,y)}}\,dx\,dy\leq 1\right\},

it is possible to prove that
W
is a Banach space with respect to the norm

\|u\|_{W}:=\|u\|_{L^{q(\cdot)}\left(\Omega\right)}+[u]_{s,p(\cdot\,,\,\cdot)}.

The concepts introduced are consistent with the classical definitions of the spaces
L^{p}\left(\Omega\right)
,
W^{1,p}\left(\Omega\right)
and
W^{s,p}\left(\Omega\right)
when the functions
p
and
q
are equal and constant.

2.3.
Maximal functions

Let
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
be a local summable function. We define its maximal function
\mathcal{M}(u)
by setting

\mathcal{M}(u)(x):=\sup_{r>0}\,\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B(x,r)}|u(y)|\,dy=\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}^{n}\left(B(x,r)\right)}\int_{B(x,r)}|u(y)|\,dy,

where
\mathcal{L}^{n}\left(A\right)
represents the
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure of
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Moreover, we introduce the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator as the function

\mathcal{M}:\left\{u\mapsto\mathcal{M}(u)\right\}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
p\in(1,+\infty]
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
, depending only on the dimension
n
of the space and on the index
p
, such that

\forall u\in L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right):\quad\|\mathcal{M}(u)\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}\leq C\,\|u\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}.

In other words, the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator
\mathcal{M}:L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\to L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
is bounded.

Proof.

See
[
15
, Chapter 1, Theorem 1]
.
∎

For our purposes, taken any
\omega\in{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
, we also define

\mathcal{M}_{\omega}(u)(x):=\sup_{h>0}\frac{1}{h}\int_{0}^{h}|u(x+s\omega)|\,ds\,

as the maximal function of
u
along the considered direction
\omega
. Arguing as in
[
13
, Lemma 3.1]
, it is possible to prove the existence of a universal constant
C>0
such that, for all
\omega\in{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\mathcal{M}_{\omega}(u)(x)|^{p}\,dx\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(x)|^{p}\,dx,\qquad\forall u\in L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right).

For variable exponents the inequality fails unless
p(\cdot)
is constant
[
6
,
9
]
. For instance, if

p(x)=2
on
(-\infty,-2)
and
p(x)\geq 4
on
[2,+\infty)
, then
\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u|^{p(x)}dx<+\infty
,
but
\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathcal{M}(u)|^{p(x)}dx=+\infty
for the function
u(x)=|x|^{-1/3}\chi_{[2,\infty)}(x)
.

Definition 2.2
.

A function
\alpha:\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is called
\log
-Hölder continuous on
\Omega
if there exists a constant
c>0
such that

(2.2)

\forall x,y\in\Omega:\quad|\alpha(x)-\alpha(y)|\leq\frac{c}{\log\left(e+|x-y|^{-1}\right)}.

Moreover,
\alpha
satisfies the
\log
-Hölder decay condition if there exist
\alpha_{\infty}\in\mathbb{R}
and
c>0
such that

(2.3)

\forall x\in\Omega:\quad|\alpha(x)-\alpha_{\infty}|\leq\frac{c}{\log\left(e+|x|\right)}.

The function
\alpha
is called globally
\log
-Hölder continuous on the domain
\Omega
if it is
\log
-Hölder continuous on
\Omega
and it satisfies the decay condition just introduced. In this case, the constant
c
satisfying both the equations (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) is called
\log
-Hölder constant of
\alpha
.

Now let’s introduce the following class of variable exponents:

\mathcal{P}^{\log}\left(\Omega\right):=\left\{p\in M\left(\Omega\right):\frac{1}{p}\text{ is globally $\log$-H\"{o}lder continuous}\right\}.

We denote by
c_{\log}\left(p\right)
, or
c_{\log}
, the
\log
-Hölder constant of
1/\penalty p
and, if
\Omega
is a bounded domain, we are able to introduce the index
p_{\infty}
by setting

p_{\infty}:=\left(\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}\frac{1}{p(x)}\right)^{-1},

with the usual convention
1/\penalty+\infty=0
.

Let us introduce an important theorem about the boundness of the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator
\mathcal{M}:\left\{u\mapsto\mathcal{M}\left(u\right)\right\}
on the Lebesgue space
L^{p(\cdot)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
p\in\mathcal{P}^{\log}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
be a bounded variable exponent, with
p^{-}>1
. Then there exists a constant
K_{p^{-}}>0
, depending only on the dimension
n
of the space and on the
\log
-Hölder constant
c_{\log}(p)
of
1/\penalty p
, such that

\forall u\in L^{p(\cdot)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right):\quad\|\mathcal{M}(u)\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}\leq K_{p^{-}}\|u\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}.

In other words, the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator
\mathcal{M}:L^{p(\cdot)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\to L^{p(\cdot)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
is bounded.

Proof.

See
[
7
, Theorem 4.3.8]
.
∎

As shown in
[
7
, Corollary 4.3.11]
, the previous theorem holds also in the case of exponents
p\in\mathcal{P}^{\log}\left(\Omega\right)
and functions
u\in L^{p\left(\cdot\right)}\left(\Omega\right)
, with
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

3.
Anisotropic formulas

We are now ready to study the behavior of the singular limit in the anisotropic case.
In the following, unless otherwise stated, we will assume
1<p^{-}\leq p^{+}<+\infty
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
u\in W^{1,p^{\pm}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
.
Then there exists a positive constant
C
, dependent only on
n
and
p^{\pm},
such that for all
\delta>0

\underset{|u(x)-u(y)|>\delta}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\frac{\delta^{p(x)}}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy\leq C\left(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p^{+}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}^{p^{+}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p^{-}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}^{p^{-}}\right).

In particular, the integral at the left-hand side is finite.

Proof.

By using polar coordinates, we have

\displaystyle\underset{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|>\delta}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\frac{\delta^{p(x)}}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle=\underset{\left|u(x+h\omega)-u(x)\right|>\delta}{\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}}\frac{\delta^{p(x)}}{h^{n+p(x)}}h^{n-1}\,dh\,dx\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\underset{\left|u(x+h\omega)-u(x)\right|>\delta}{\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}}\frac{\delta^{p(x)}}{h^{p(x)+1}}\,dh\,dx\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega).

Thanks to this equation, it is sufficient to prove the existence of a constant
C>0
, dependent only on
p^{\pm}
, such that for all
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
we have

\underset{|u(x+h\omega)-u(x)|>\delta}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}}\frac{\delta^{p(x)}}{h^{p(x)+1}}\,dh\,dx\leq C\left(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p^{+}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}^{p^{+}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p^{-}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}^{p^{-}}\right).

From the fundamental theorem of calculus,

\displaystyle\left|u\right(x+h\omega\left)-u(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{h}\left|\frac{d}{ds}u(x+s\omega)\right|\,ds=\int_{0}^{h}\left|\nabla u(x+s\omega)\cdot\omega\right|\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{h}\left|\nabla u(x+s\omega)\right|\,ds\leq h\,\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(\nabla u\right)(x),

for a.e.
(x,h)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,+\infty)
, where

\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(u\right)\left(x\right)=\sup_{h>0}\,\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{0}^{h}\left|u\left(x+s\omega\right)\right|\,ds

is the maximal function of
u
with respect to the direction
\omega\in{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
previously introduced. From this inequality, it follows the set inclusion

\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\left|u\left(x+h\omega\right)-u(x)\right|>\delta\right\}\subset\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:h\,\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(\nabla u\right)(x)>\delta\right\},

from which we have

\displaystyle\underset{\left|u\left(x+h\omega\right)-u(x)\right|>\delta}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}}\frac{\delta^{p(x)}}{h^{p(x)+1}}\,dh\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\underset{h\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(\nabla u\right)(x)>\delta}{\int_{0}^{+\infty}}\frac{\delta^{p(x)}}{h^{p(x)+1}}\,dh\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left[-\frac{1}{p(x)}\frac{\delta^{p(x)}}{h^{p(x)}}\right]_{{\delta}/\penalty{\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(\nabla u\right)(x)}}^{+\infty}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{p(x)}\left|\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(\nabla u\right)(x)\right|^{p(x)}\,dx.

Recalling that
p^{-}\leq p(x)\leq p^{+}
, we can increase the multiplicative inverse of
p(x)
to
1/\penalty p^{-}
, getting

(3.1)

\underset{\left|u\left(x+h\boldsymbol{e}_{n}\right)-u(x)\right|>\delta}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}}\frac{\delta^{p(x)}}{h^{p(x)+1}}\,dh\,dx\leq\frac{1}{p^{-}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(\nabla u\right)(x)\right|^{p(x)}\,dx.

At this point, we split the integral at the right-hand side, over the sets of
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with

\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(\nabla u\right)(x)\leq 1\qquad\text{or}\qquad\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(\nabla u\right)(x)>1,

so that we are able to increase the integrand function by using, respectively, the exponents
p^{-}
or
p^{+}
and then by extending both the integrals over the entire space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In this way, we get

\frac{1}{p^{-}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(\nabla u\right)(x)\right|^{p(x)}\,dx\leq\frac{1}{p^{-}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(\nabla u\right)(x)\right|^{p^{-}}\,dx+\frac{1}{p^{-}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(\nabla u\right)(x)\right|^{p^{+}}\,dx.

As a direct conseguence of the theory of maximal funcrions, there exist positive constants
C_{p^{\pm}}
, depending on
p^{\pm}
, such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p^{-}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(\nabla u\right)(x)\right|^{p(x)}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{p^{-}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(\nabla u\right)(x)\right|^{p^{-}}\,dx+\frac{1}{p^{-}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(\nabla u\right)(x)\right|^{p^{+}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{p^{-}}}{p^{-}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p^{-}}\,dx+\frac{C_{p^{+}}}{p^{-}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p^{+}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p^{+}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}^{p^{+}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p^{-}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}^{p^{-}}\right),

where
C:=\max\left\{C_{p^{+}},C_{p^{-}}\right\}/p^{-}
. The assertion follows.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Observe that, if
p:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[1,+\infty)
is bounded and measurable, then we have

W^{1,p^{\pm}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\subset W^{1,p(\cdot)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right).

In fact, taken
u\in W^{1,p^{\pm}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, first of all

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|u(x)\right|^{p(x)}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{u(x)\leq 1}\left|u(x)\right|^{p(x)}dx+\int_{u(x)>1}\left|u(x)\right|^{p(x)}dx

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{L^{p^{-}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}^{p^{-}}+\|u\|_{L^{p^{+}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}^{p^{+}}

and, at the same time,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p(x)}dx\leq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p^{-}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}^{p^{-}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p^{+}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}^{p^{+}},

so
u\in W^{1,p(\cdot)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
.

Theorem 3.3

(Anisotropic limit I)
.

For all
u\in W^{1,p^{\pm}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, we have the limit formula

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0}\underset{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|>\delta}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\frac{\delta^{p(x)}}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}

\displaystyle dx\,dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K_{n,p(x)}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p(x)}\,dx,

where
K_{n,p(x)}
is defined as in (
1.2
). In particular, the limit exists and is finite.

Proof.

First of all, taken
h>0
, let’s prove that, for all
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, we have

(3.2)

\sup_{\delta\in(0,1)}\underset{\left|\frac{u(x+\delta h\omega)-u(x)}{\delta h}\right|h>1}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}}\frac{1}{h^{p(x)+1}}\,dh\,dx<+\infty,

and

(3.3)

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\underset{\left|\frac{u(x+\delta h\omega)-u(x)}{\delta h}\right|h>1}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}}\frac{1}{h^{p(x)+1}}\,dh\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{p(x)}\left|\nabla u(x)\cdot\omega\right|^{p(x)}\,dx.

Since
u\in W^{1,p^{\pm}}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
,

u(x+h\omega)-u(x)=\int_{0}^{h}\frac{d}{ds}u(x+s\omega)\,ds=\int_{0}^{h}\left(\nabla u(x+s\omega)\cdot\omega\right)\,ds

for all
(x,h)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,+\infty)
.
Let’s set

\displaystyle A(\delta)

\displaystyle:=\left\{(x,h)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,+\infty):\left|\frac{u(x+\delta h\omega)-u(x)}{\delta h}\right|h>1\right\},

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle:=\left\{(x,h)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,+\infty):\Biggl|\left.\frac{d}{ds}u(x+s\omega)\right|_{s=0}\Biggr|h>1\right\},

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle:=\Bigl\{(x,h)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,+\infty):\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(\nabla u\right)(x)\,h>1\Bigr\},

and let
\chi_{K}\left(x,h\right)
be the characteristic function of a set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,+\infty)
.
By the definition of maximal function, we have the inequalities chain

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(\nabla u\right)(x)

\displaystyle=\sup_{h>0}\,\frac{1}{h}\int_{0}^{h}\left|\nabla u(x+s\omega)\right|\,ds

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{h>0}\,\frac{1}{h}\left|\int_{0}^{h}\frac{d}{ds}u(x+s\omega)\,ds\right|

\displaystyle=\sup_{h>0}\,\frac{1}{h}\left|u(x+h\omega)-u(x)\right|\geq\left|\frac{u(x+\delta h\omega)-u(x)}{\delta h}\right|,\quad\forall\delta>0,

so that

A(\delta)\subset B\Longrightarrow\chi_{A(\delta)}\left(x,h\right)\leq\chi_{B}\left(x,h\right),\qquad\forall\left(x,h\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,+\infty).

Since

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{h^{p(x)+1}}\chi_{B}\left(x,h\right)\,dh\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{p(x)}\left|\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(\nabla u\right)(x)\right|^{p(x)}\,dx,

from what observed in the proof of the previous Lemma, the right hand side is finite, so we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{h^{p(x)+1}}\chi_{A\left(\delta\right)}\left(x,h\right)\,dh\,dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{h^{p(x)+1}}\chi_{B}\left(x,h\right)\,dh\,dx<+\infty,

as well as (
3.2
).
The function
\dfrac{\chi_{A\left(\delta\right)}\left(x,h\right)}{h^{p(x)+1}}
is dominated by
\dfrac{\chi_{B}\left(x,h\right)}{h^{p(x)+1}}
, that is summable. Since

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\chi_{A(\delta)}\left(x,h\right)=\chi_{A}\left(x,h\right)

for a.e.
\left(x,h\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\times(0,+\infty)
, from dominated convergence, it follows

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0}

\displaystyle\underset{\left|\frac{u(x+\delta h\omega)-u(x)}{\delta h}\right|h>1}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}}\frac{1}{h^{p(x)+1}}\,dh\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{h^{p(x)+1}}\chi_{A}\left(x,h\right)\,dh\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{p(x)}\Biggl|\left.\frac{d}{ds}u(x+s\omega)\right|_{s=0}\Biggr|^{p(x)}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{p(x)}\left|\nabla u(x)\cdot\omega\right|^{p(x)}\,dx.

So, since (
3.3
) holds, we are ready to prove the Lemma.
Making the change of variable
y-x=\delta h\omega
, with
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, we have

\displaystyle\underset{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|>\delta}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\frac{\delta^{p(x)}}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle=\underset{\left|\frac{u(x+\delta h\omega)-u(x)}{\delta h}\right|h>1}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}}\frac{\delta^{p(x)}}{|\delta h|^{n+p(x)}}\,\delta^{n}h^{n-1}\,dh\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)\,dx

\displaystyle=\underset{\left|\frac{u(x+\delta h\omega)-u(x)}{\delta h}\right|h>1}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}}\frac{1}{|h|^{p(x)+1}}\,dh\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)\,dx,

so, by using dominated convergence theorem and equation (
3.3
), it follows

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0}\underset{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|>\delta}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\frac{\delta^{p(x)}}{|x-y|^{n+p}}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\underset{\left|\frac{u(x+\delta h\omega)-u(x)}{\delta h}\right|h>1}{\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}}\frac{1}{h^{p(x)+1}}\,dh\,dx\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{p(x)}\left|\nabla u(x)\cdot\omega\right|^{p(x)}\,dx\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega).

Since, for every
V\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and for all
p\geq 1
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left|V\cdot\omega\right|^{p}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)=p\,K_{n,p}\,|V|^{p},

where
K_{n,p}
is defined as in (
1.1
), then, for every
V\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left|V\cdot\omega\right|^{p(x)}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)=p(x)\,K_{n,p(x)}\,|V|^{p(x)}.

As a consequence, we are able to obtain the Nguyen type limit formula

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\underset{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|>\delta}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\frac{\delta^{p(x)}}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K_{n,p(x)}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p(x)}\,dx.

Observe that, by the definition of
K_{n,p(x)}
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0}\underset{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|>\delta}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\frac{\delta^{p(x)}}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{p(x)}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}{\left|\omega\cdot\boldsymbol{e}\right|^{p(x)}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)\right)\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p(x)}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{p^{-}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}{\left|\omega\cdot\boldsymbol{e}\right|^{p^{-}}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p(x)}\,dx

\displaystyle=K_{n,p^{-}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p(x)}\,dx,

so that the limit is finite.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

The function
\left\{x\mapsto K_{n,p(x)}\right\}
is also bounded, since
|K_{n,p(x)}|\leq K_{n,p^{-}}
.
Moreover
\left\{s\mapsto K_{n,s}\right\}
, for
s\in[1,+\infty)
, is a monotonically decreasing function, that tends to
0
as
s\to+\infty
.
In fact, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}\left[\frac{1}{s}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\omega\cdot\boldsymbol{e}|^{s}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{s^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\omega\cdot\boldsymbol{e}|^{s}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{s}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\omega\cdot\boldsymbol{e}|^{s}\log{|\omega\cdot\boldsymbol{e}|}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)<0,\qquad s\geq 1,

and the assertions follow. Hence, roughy speaking, very large anisotropic exponents
p(x)
will produce,
in some sense, a small measure
\mu=K_{n,p(x)}{\mathcal{L}}^{n}
in the limit formula.

In the classical case, where
p
is a constant exponent, if we have

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\underset{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|>\delta}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\frac{\delta^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+p}}\,dx\,dy=K_{n,p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx,

then we have also

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\underset{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|>\delta}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\frac{p\,\delta^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+p}}\,dx\,dy=p\,K_{n,p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}\,dx.

This fact is not obvious in the variable exponent case.

Theorem 3.5

(Anisotropic limit II)
.

For all
u\in W^{1,p^{\pm}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, we have the limit formula

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0}\underset{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|>\delta}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\frac{p(x)\,\delta^{p(x)}}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}

\displaystyle dx\,dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}p(x)\,K_{n,p(x)}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p(x)}\,dx,

where
K_{n,p(x)}
has been introduced previously. In particular, the limit exists and is finite.

Proof.

In analogy to the previous case, taken
h>0
, we prove that, for all
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
,

(3.4)

\sup_{\delta\in(0,1)}\underset{\left|\frac{u(x+\delta h\omega)-u(x)}{\delta h}\right|h>1}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}}\frac{p(x)}{h^{p(x)+1}}\,dh\,dx<+\infty

and

(3.5)

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\underset{\left|\frac{u(x+\delta h\omega)-u(x)}{\delta h}\right|h>1}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}}\frac{p(x)}{h^{p(x)+1}}\,dh\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla u(x)\cdot\omega\right|^{p(x)}\,dx.

Of course, we have the inequality

\underset{\left|\frac{u(x+\delta h\omega)-u(x)}{\delta h}\right|h>1}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}}\frac{p(x)}{h^{p(x)+1}}\,dh\,dx\leq p^{+}\underset{\left|\frac{u(x+\delta h\omega)-u(x)}{\delta h}\right|h>1}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}}\frac{1}{h^{p(x)+1}}\,dh\,dx

and, by what previously proved, (
3.4
) holds.
Now, taking into account the equations obtained for the first anisotropic limit formula, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{p(x)}{h^{p(x)+1}}\chi_{B}\left(x,h\right)\,dh\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\mathcal{M}_{\omega}\left(\nabla u\right)(x)\right|^{p(x)}\,dx,

where the right-hand side is convergent, as proved in Lemma
3.1
.
From
A(\delta)\subset B
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{p(x)}{h^{p(x)+1}}\chi_{A\left(\delta\right)}\left(x,h\right)\leq\frac{p(x)}{h^{p(x)+1}}\chi_{B}\left(x,h\right),\qquad\forall(x,h)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,+\infty),\quad\forall\delta>0,

where

p(x)h^{-p(x)-1}\chi_{B}\left(x,h\right)
is summable.
Then, by the dominated convergence theorem,

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0}\underset{\left|\frac{u(x+\delta h\omega)-u(x)}{\delta h}\right|h>1}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}}\frac{p(x)}{h^{p(x)+1}}\,dh\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{p(x)}{h^{p(x)+1}}\chi_{A}\left(x,h\right)\,dh\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla u(x)\cdot\omega\right|^{p(x)}\,dx,

proving (
3.5
). Now we are ready to prove the theorem.
Setting
y-x=\delta h\omega
, with
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
,

\underset{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|>\delta}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\frac{p(x)\,\delta^{p(x)}}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy=\underset{\left|\frac{u(x+\delta h\omega)-u(x)}{\delta h}\right|h>1}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}}\frac{p(x)}{h^{p(x)+1}}\,dh\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)\,dx,

so, making the limit as
\delta\to 0
, it results

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0}\underset{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|>\delta}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\frac{p(x)\,\delta^{p(x)}}{|x-y|^{n+p}}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\underset{\left|\frac{u(x+\delta h\omega)-u(x)}{\delta h}\right|h>1}{\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}}\frac{p(x)}{h^{p(x)+1}}\,dh\,dx\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla u(x)\cdot\omega\right|^{p(x)}\,dx\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega).

In conclusion, applying Fubini-Tonelli’s theorem and recalling that, for any
V\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left|V\cdot\omega\right|^{p(x)}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)=p(x)\,K_{n,p(x)}\,|V|^{p(x)},

we get

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\underset{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|>\delta}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\frac{p(x)\,\delta^{p(x)}}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}p(x)\,K_{n,p(x)}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p(x)}\,dx.

Of course the right-hand side is finite,
so that the limit is also finite. The proof is complete.
∎

4.
Sufficient conditions

First we state the following

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
u\in L^{p(\cdot)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\cap C^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
. Then we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}p(x)\,K_{n,p(x)}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p(x)}\,dx\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\varepsilon\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{p(x)+\varepsilon}}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy.

Moreover, if
u
satisfies

C(u):=\sup_{0<\varepsilon<1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\varepsilon\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{p(x)+\varepsilon}}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy<+\infty,

then
u\in W^{1,p(\cdot)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
.

Proof.

By using polar coordinates, we get

C(u)=\sup_{0<\varepsilon<1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{\varepsilon\left|u(x+r\omega)-u(x)\right|^{p(x)+\varepsilon}}{r^{p(x)+1}}\,dr\,dx\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega).

Consider the restriction to the open balls
B_{A}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
at the origin with radius
A>0
,

\sup_{0<\varepsilon<1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{B_{A}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{\varepsilon\left|u(x+r\omega)-u(x)\right|^{p(x)+\varepsilon}}{r^{p(x)+1}}\,dr\,dx\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)\leq C(u).

Since
u\in C^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, arguing as in the proof of
[
10
, Lemma 4]
,
we have

\left|Du(x)\cdot r\omega\right|^{p(x)+\varepsilon}\leq|u(x+r\omega)-u(x)|^{p(x)+\varepsilon}+Cr^{p(x)+\varepsilon+1},\qquad\forall\,(\omega,x,r)\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times B_{A}\times(0,1).

Multiplying by
\varepsilon
and dividing by
r^{p(x)+1}
, after integrating, we get

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{B_{A}}\int_{0}^{1}

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon\left|Du(x)\cdot r\omega\right|^{p(x)+\varepsilon}}{r^{p(x)+1}}\,dr\,dx\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{B_{A}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\varepsilon\,|u(x+r\omega)-u(x)|^{p(x)+\varepsilon}}{r^{p(x)+1}}\,dr\,dx\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)

\displaystyle+C\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{B_{A}}\int_{0}^{1}\varepsilon\,r^{\varepsilon}\,dr\,dx\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{B_{A}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\varepsilon\,|u(x+r\omega)-u(x)|^{p(x)+\varepsilon}}{r^{p(x)+1}}\,dr\,dx\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega).

After some computation, we are able to apply Fatou’s Lemma as shown in the following equation

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{B_{A}}\int_{0}^{1}

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon\left|Du(x)\cdot r\omega\right|^{p(x)+\varepsilon}}{r^{p(x)+1}}\,dr\,dx\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{B_{A}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\varepsilon\,r^{\varepsilon-1}\,dr\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left|Du(x)\cdot\omega\right|^{p(x)+\varepsilon}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)\right)dx

\displaystyle=\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{B_{A}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left|Du(x)\cdot\omega\right|^{p(x)+\varepsilon}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)\right)dx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{B_{A}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left|Du(x)\cdot\omega\right|^{p(x)+\varepsilon}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)\right)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{A}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left|Du(x)\cdot\omega\right|^{p(x)}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega)\right)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{A}}p(x)\,K_{n,p(x)}\left|Du(x)\right|^{p(x)}\,dx,

hence the inequality

\int_{B_{A}}p(x)\,K_{n,p(x)}\left|Du(x)\right|^{p(x)}\,dx\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{B_{A}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\varepsilon\,|u(x+r\omega)-u(x)|^{p(x)+\varepsilon}}{r^{p(x)+1}}\,dr\,dx\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\omega).

By the arbitrariness of
A>0
, we conclude that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}p(x)\,K_{n,p(x)}\left|Du(x)\right|^{p(x)}\,dx\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\varepsilon\,|u(x)-u(y)|^{p(x)+\varepsilon}}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy\leq C(u).

Since
p^{+}\,K_{n,p^{+}}\leq p(x)\,K_{n,p(x)}
, if
C(u)
is finite,
we get
u\in W^{1,p(\cdot)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, concluding the proof.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
u\in L^{p(\cdot)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\cap C^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
and set
\omega(x)=p(x)\,K_{n,p(x)}
. Then

(4.1)

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n},\,\omega\right)}\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\max_{\pm}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\varepsilon\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{p(x)+\varepsilon}}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy\right)^{1/p^{\pm}}

and, if the right-hand side is finite,
u\in W^{1,p(\cdot)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
. In addition, if
p\in\mathcal{P}^{\log}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, we have
\mathcal{M}\left(\nabla u\right)\in L^{p(\cdot)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.1
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p(x)}\omega(x)\,dx\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\varepsilon\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{p(x)+\varepsilon}}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy,

while, remembering property (
2.1
), we have also

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n},\,\omega\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{\pm}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{p(x)}\omega(x)\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\pm}}}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{\pm}\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\varepsilon\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{p(x)+\varepsilon}}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\pm}}}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\max_{\pm}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\varepsilon\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{p(x)+\varepsilon}}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\pm}}},

so the first assertion follows. Since
p^{+}\,K_{n,p^{+}}\leq p(x)\,K_{n,p(x)}
, if the last term is finite,
we get
u\in W^{1,p(\cdot)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
.
Moreover, if
p\in\mathcal{P}^{\log}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, by applying Theorem
2.3
, there exists a constant
K_{p^{-}}>0
such that

\left\|\mathcal{M}\left(\nabla u\right)\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}\leq K_{p^{-}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)},

and the proof is complete.
∎

We will need the following lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a measurable set and let
\psi
and
\phi
be two non-negative measurable functions on the domain
\Omega\times\Omega
. If we take a measurable function
\alpha:\Omega\to(-1,+\infty)
, then

\int_{0}^{1}\underset{\phi(x,y)>\delta}{\int\int}\delta^{\alpha(x)}\psi(x,y)\,dx\,dy\,d\delta=\underset{\phi(x,y)\leq 1}{\int\int}\frac{\phi^{\alpha(x)+1}(x,y)}{\alpha(x)+1}\psi(x,y)\,dx\,dy+\underset{\phi(x,y)>1}{\int\int}\frac{\psi(x,y)}{\alpha(x)+1}\,dx\,dy.

Proof.

From a direct computation, by using Fubini-Tonelli’s theorem, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\underset{\phi(x,y)>\delta}{\int\int}\delta^{\alpha(x)}\psi(x,y)\,dx\,dy\,d\delta=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\psi(x,y)\underset{\phi(x,y)>\delta}{\int_{0}^{1}}\delta^{\alpha(x)}\,d\delta\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\psi(x,y)\underset{\delta<\phi(x,y)\leq 1}{\int_{0}^{1}}\delta^{\alpha(x)}\,d\delta\,dx\,dy+\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\psi(x,y)\underset{\phi(x,y)>1}{\int_{0}^{1}}\delta^{\alpha(x)}\,d\delta\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle=\underset{\phi(x,y)\leq 1}{\int\int}\psi(x,y)\left[\frac{\delta^{\alpha(x)+1}}{\alpha(x)+1}\right]_{0}^{\phi(x,y)}\,dx\,dy+\underset{\phi(x,y)>1}{\int\int}\psi(x,y)\left[\frac{\delta^{\alpha(x)+1}}{\alpha(x)+1}\right]_{0}^{1}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle=\underset{\phi(x,y)\leq 1}{\int\int}\psi(x,y)\frac{\phi^{\alpha(x)+1}(x,y)}{\alpha(x)+1}\,dx\,dy+\underset{\phi(x,y)>1}{\int\int}\frac{1}{\alpha(x)+1}\psi(x,y)\,dx\,dy,

and the assertion follows.
∎

\displaystyle\sup_{0<\varepsilon<1}

\displaystyle+

,
p^{\pm}
and the first assertion follows.

Theorem 4.5
.

The following facts hold:

(a)

if
u\in L^{p(\cdot)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\cap C^{2}_{b}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
is a bounded
C^{2}
function and

\sup_{0<\varepsilon<1}\underset{|u(x)-u(y)|\leq 1}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\frac{\varepsilon\,|u(x)-u(y)|^{p(x)+\varepsilon}}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy+\underset{|u(x)-u(y)|>1}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy<+\infty,

then
u\in W^{1,p(\cdot)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
;

(b)

if
u\in L^{p(\cdot)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
and

\sup_{0<\delta<1}\underset{|u(x)-u(y)|>\delta}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\frac{\delta^{p(x)}}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy<+\infty,

then
u\in W^{1,p(\cdot)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
.

Proof.

Let
u\in L^{p(\cdot)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
be a bounded

function satisfying (a). Then

\displaystyle\sup_{0<\varepsilon<1}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon\,|u(x)-u(y)|^{p(x)+\varepsilon}}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{0<\varepsilon<1}\underset{|u(x)-u(y)|\leq 1}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\frac{\varepsilon\,|u(x)-u(y)|^{p(x)+\varepsilon}}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle+2^{p^{+}+1}\max\left\{1,\|u\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}^{p^{+}+1}\right\}\underset{|u(x)-u(y)|>1}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy<+\infty.

The assertion follows from Lemma
4.1
.

Let
u\in L^{p(\cdot)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\cap C^{2}_{b}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
such that

(4.3)

\sup_{0<\delta<1}\underset{|u(x)-u(y)|>\delta}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\frac{\delta^{p(x)}}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy\leq C<+\infty,

for some constant
C>0
.
Multiplying the inequality by
\varepsilon\delta^{\varepsilon-1}
, with
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, and integrating with respect to
\delta
over the interval
(0,1)
, we have

\int_{0}^{1}\underset{|u(x)-u(y)|>\delta}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\frac{\varepsilon\delta^{p(x)+\varepsilon-1}}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy\,d\delta\leq C.

Applying Lemma
4.3
with

\alpha(x)=p(x)+\varepsilon-1,\qquad\phi(x,y)=|u(x)-u(y)|\qquad\text{and}\qquad\psi(x,y)=\dfrac{\varepsilon}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}},

the left-hand side of the last inequality becomes

\underset{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|\leq 1}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\frac{\varepsilon\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{p(x)+\varepsilon}}{(p(x)+\varepsilon)|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy+\underset{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|>1}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\frac{\varepsilon}{(p(x)+\varepsilon)|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy\leq C,

\displaystyle\sup_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}\underset{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|\leq 1}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\frac{\varepsilon\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{p(x)+\varepsilon}}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\leq C(p^{+}+1).

Recalling that, by (

assumption (a) of the Theorem is satisfied, so

In the general case, one can argue as in

Remark 4.6
.

Let
u\in L^{p(\cdot)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
with
1=p^{-}\leq p^{+}<+\infty

and set
E=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:p(x)=1\right\}
. Assume
{\rm int}(E)\neq\emptyset
and

\Lambda=\sup_{0<\delta<1}\underset{|u(x)-u(y)|>\delta}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\frac{\delta^{p(x)}}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy<+\infty.

Then

\sup_{B\subset{\rm int}(E)}|B|^{-\frac{n+1}{n}}\int_{B}\int_{B}|u(x)-u(y)|\,dx\,dy<+\infty.

In particular, for
n=1
, it reads as

\sup_{B\subset{\rm int}(E)}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B}|u(x)-u(y)|\,dx\,dy<+\infty,

so
u\in BMO(E)
, the space of bounded mean oscillation functions on
E
.
In fact, let
x_{0}\in{\rm int}(E)
and
B\subset{\rm int}(E)

a ball centered at
x_{0}
.
We have, for all
\delta\in(0,1)
,

\underset{|u(x)-u(y)|>\delta}{\int_{B}\int_{B}}\frac{\delta}{|x-y|^{n+1}}\,dx\,dy\leq\underset{|u(x)-u(y)|>\delta}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\frac{\delta^{p(x)}}{|x-y|^{n+p(x)}}\,dx\,dy\leq\Lambda.

Then, taking into account
[
12
, (a) of Theorem 1]
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B}\int_{B}|u(x)-u(y)|\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle\leq C\left(|B|^{\frac{n+1}{n}}\!\underset{|u(x)-u(y)|>\delta}{\int_{B}\int_{B}}\frac{\delta}{|x-y|^{n+1}}\,dx\,dy+\delta|B|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\Lambda|B|^{\frac{n+1}{n}}+\delta|B|^{2}\right),

for some constant

depending on
n
. Then the above assertions follow by the arbitrariness of
\delta\in(0,1)
– making the limit as
\delta\to 0^{+}
– and

.

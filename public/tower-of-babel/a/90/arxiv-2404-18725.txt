Abstract

. Its proof follows the strategy from
[

and
[

, Theorem 1.2]

, we build two forms

Lattices, coverings and extraordinary forms

3.1
Lattices

By a
lattice
, we mean an additive subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with rank
2
. A lattice is
proper
when it is different from
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. If
\Lambda
is a lattice generated by linearly independent vectors
\vec{u},\vec{v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, the
index
of
\Lambda
is the positive integer

[\mathbb{Z}^{2}:\Lambda]=|\mathbb{Z}^{2}/\Lambda|=|\det(\vec{u},\vec{v})|.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\gamma\in{\rm GL}(2,\mathbb{Q})
. We define the lattice
L(\gamma)
through

L(\gamma):=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:\gamma\begin{pmatrix}x\\
y\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\right\}.

We will frequently use the obvious remarks

L(\gamma)=L(-\gamma)

(3.1)

and

L(\gamma)=\mathbb{Z}^{2}\iff\gamma\text{ has integer coefficients}.

(3.2)

We recall the following property, which is proved by a direct calculation (see
[
1
, Lemma 6.9]
).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\gamma\in{\rm GL}(2,\mathbb{Q})
. Then
[\mathbb{Z}^{2}:L(\gamma)]
is an integer multiple of
|\det\gamma|^{-1}
.

The following lemma provides a unique decomposition of matrices in
{\rm GL}(2,\mathbb{Q})
(see
[
1
, Lemma 6.8]
).

Lemma 3.3
.

Every matrix
f\in{\rm GL}(2,\mathbb{Q})
can be uniquely written under the form

f=\frac{N}{D}\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&a_{2}\\
a_{3}&a_{4}\end{pmatrix},

where the integers
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
,
a_{3}
,
a_{4}
,
D
and
N
satisfy the conditions

D,\,N\geq 1,\ \gcd(D,N)=1,\ \gcd(a_{1},\,a_{2},\,a_{3},\,a_{4})=1.

Let
p\geq 2
be a prime and let
v_{p}(n)
be the
p
–adic valuation of the integer
n
with the convention
v_{p}(0)=+\infty
. Several times we will use the following easy lemma without further reference.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
p
be a prime. Let
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}
, with
\gamma\neq 0
. Then the lattice defined by the equation

\alpha x_{1}+\beta x_{2}\equiv 0\bmod\gamma

•

is included in the lattice

\{(x_{1},x_{2}):x_{1}\equiv 0\bmod p\}

as soon as the following inequality holds

v_{p}(\alpha)<\min\left(v_{p}(\beta),v_{p}(\gamma)\right),

•

has its index divisible by
p
as soon as the following inequality holds

\min\left(v_{p}(\alpha),v_{p}(\beta)\right)<v_{p}(\gamma).

3.2
Coverings

Definition 3.5
.

Let
k\geq 1
be an integer and let
(\Lambda_{i})_{1\leq i\leq k}
be
k
lattices. We say that

\mathcal{C}=\{\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{k}\}

is a covering of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
(or a covering) if and only if we have the equality

\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq k}\Lambda_{i}=\mathbb{Z}^{2}.

The lattices
\Lambda_{j}
are the component lattices of the covering
\mathcal{C}
.

Here is a first property of coverings that we will use to assert that some given sets of lattices can not be coverings.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
k\geq 2
and
\mathcal{C}=\{\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{k}\}
be a covering. We then have the inequality

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{[\mathbb{Z}^{2}:\Lambda_{i}]}>1.

Proof.

We state a general combinatorial inequality: let
k\geq 2
be an integer and let
\mathcal{E}_{i}
(for
1\leq i\leq k
) be subsets of some set
\mathcal{E}
. Let
\chi_{\mathcal{F}}
be the characteristic function of a subset
\mathcal{F}
of
\mathcal{E}
. Then for every
x\in\mathcal{E}
one has the inequality

\chi_{\mathcal{E}_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\mathcal{E}_{k}}(x)\leq\chi_{\mathcal{E}_{1}}(x)+\cdots+\chi_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}(x)-\chi_{\mathcal{E}_{1}\cap\mathcal{E}_{2}}(x).

(3.3)

In the euclidean plane, let
D(R)
be the disk centered at the origin and with radius
R
. By hypothesis we have
\left(\Lambda_{1}\cup\dots\cup\Lambda_{k}\right)\cap D(R)=\mathbb{Z}^{2}\cap D(R)
. As
R
tends to infinity, one has

\sum_{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\chi_{\Lambda_{i}\cap D(R)}(\boldsymbol{x})\sim\frac{\pi R^{2}}{[\mathbb{Z}^{2}:\Lambda_{i}]}.

(3.4)

We apply the above formula (
3.3
) with the choice

\mathcal{E}_{i}=\Lambda_{i}\cap D(R).

We sum over all
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, apply (
3.4
) and let
R
tend to infinity to obtain the inequality

\pi\leq\pi\left(\frac{1}{[\mathbb{Z}^{2}:\Lambda_{1}]}+\cdots+\frac{1}{[\mathbb{Z}^{2}:\Lambda_{k}]}-\frac{1}{[\mathbb{Z}^{2}:\Lambda_{1}\cap\Lambda_{2}]}\right).

This gives Lemma
3.6
, since
\Lambda_{1}\cap\Lambda_{2}
is also a lattice.
∎

Definition 3.7

(Minimal covering)
.

Let
k\geq 1
, let
\Lambda_{i}
be lattices and let
\mathcal{C}=\{\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{k}\}
be a covering. We say that
\mathcal{C}
is a minimal covering of length
k
if and only if replacing any
\Lambda_{i}
by some proper sublattice
\Lambda^{\prime}_{i}\varsubsetneq\Lambda_{i}
, the set

\{\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{i-1},\Lambda^{\prime}_{i},\Lambda_{i+1},\ldots,\Lambda_{k}\}

is not a covering.

If
\mathcal{C}
is a minimal covering of length
k\geq 2
and if
1\leq i\neq j\leq k
, we never have
\Lambda_{i}\subseteq\Lambda_{j}
. In particular, every
\Lambda_{i}
is a proper lattice. The following lemma asserts that, from any covering, one can extract a minimal covering (see
[
2
, Lemma 3.3]
).

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
k\geq 1
and let

\mathcal{C}:=\{\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{k}\}

be a covering. Then there exists an integer
1\leq k^{\prime}\leq k
, an injection
\phi:\{1,\ldots,k^{\prime}\}\rightarrow\{1,\ldots,k\}
and lattices
\Lambda^{\prime}_{j}
(
1\leq j\leq k^{\prime}
) such that

\mathcal{C}^{\prime}:=\{\Lambda^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\Lambda^{\prime}_{k^{\prime}}\}

is a minimal covering, and for all
1\leq j\leq k^{\prime}
one has
\Lambda^{\prime}_{j}\subseteq\Lambda_{\phi(j)}
. In particular, for all
1\leq j\leq k^{\prime}
, one has

[\mathbb{Z}^{2}:\Lambda_{\phi(j)}]\,\Bigl|\,[\mathbb{Z}^{2}:\Lambda^{\prime}_{j}].

(3.5)

Definition 3.9
.

With the conventions from Lemma
3.8
we say that
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is a minimal covering extracted from the covering
\mathcal{C}
. Note that,
\mathcal{C}
being given, the minimal extracted covering
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is not necessarily unique.

The divisibility condition (
3.5
) will prove to be an efficient tool for eliminating a given set of
k
lattices as a covering of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, as soon as one has at their disposal a catalog of minimal coverings with length
\leq k
. This remark is a crucial tool in the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

3.4
Links with extraordinary forms

We recall the definition and some straightforward properties of an isomorphism between two forms.

Definition 3.15
.

Let
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
be two forms in
{\rm Bin}(d,\mathbb{Q})
. An isomorphism from
F_{1}
to
F_{2}
is an element
\rho\in{\rm GL}(2,\mathbb{Q})
such that
F_{1}\circ\rho=F_{2}
. The set of all such isomorphisms is denoted by
{\rm Isom}(F_{1}\rightarrow F_{2},\mathbb{Q})
. Suppose
{\rm Isom}(F_{1}\rightarrow F_{2},\mathbb{Q})
is not empty and let
\rho
be one of its elements. Then we have the equalities

\displaystyle{\rm Isom}(F_{1}\rightarrow F_{2},\mathbb{Q})

\displaystyle=\rho\cdot{\rm Aut}(F_{2},\mathbb{Q})={\rm Aut}(F_{1},\mathbb{Q})\cdot\rho

\displaystyle{\rm Isom}(F_{2}\rightarrow F_{1},\mathbb{Q})

\displaystyle=\rho^{-1}\cdot{\rm Aut}(F_{1},\mathbb{Q})={\rm Aut}(F_{2},\mathbb{Q})\cdot\rho^{-1}.

The following key proposition is
[
2
, Prop. 2.5]
(see also the remarks
[
2
, Comments 2.6]
). The equality (
3.9
) exhibits two coverings of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
that we will deeply investigate in the context of the hypothesis
(C3)

.

d\geq 3
and let

be a pair of linked extraordinary forms. Then there exists

, a pair of linked extraordinary forms
(G_{1},G_{2})
and a pair

of positive integers such that

1.

we have
F_{1}=F_{2}\circ\rho
,

2.

\Bigl(G_{1}\sim_{{\rm GL}(2,\mathbb{Z})}F_{1}\textup{ and }G_{2}\sim_{{\rm GL}(2,\mathbb{Z})}F_{2}\Bigr)\textup{ or }\Bigl(G_{1}\sim_{{\rm GL}(2,\mathbb{Z})}F_{2}\textup{ and }G_{2}\sim_{{\rm GL}(2,\mathbb{Z})}F_{1}\Bigr),

Lemma 4.15

Consider the set

of four polynomials in two variables

,

. Let

and

\left|\{P\in\mathcal{S}:P(u,v)\equiv 0\bmod 9\}\right|\leq 1.

(4.33)

Proof.

We make a succession of simple observations:

•

For any integers
(u,v)
, we have
A(u,v)\equiv 0\bmod 3
if and only if
u\equiv v\bmod 3
. If

, then

. We deduce that

implies

.

•

Since

, in order to prove the inequality (

), it is sufficient to show that the polynomials

and

do not simultaneously vanish modulo

, on some

with

.

•

Let
(u,v)
be such that

. By taking the difference, we obtain that
(u-v)(u+v)\equiv 0\bmod 9
. We analyze this congruence in three subcases

–

if

, then
C(u,v)\equiv 3u^{2}\bmod 9
, and the condition

, so

,

–

if

, then

,

–

if

Appendix A
Algorithms

Consider the set

\mathcal{S}=\{(L_{1},\dots,L_{6}):L_{1}\cup\dots\cup L_{6}=\mathbb{Z}^{2},L_{i}\text{ is a subgroup of }\mathbb{Z}^{2}\text{ for all }i\}.

It will be most convenient to consider general subgroups of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, i.e. we allow the rank of
L_{i}
to be smaller than
2
. We turn the set of
6
-tuples
(L_{1},\dots,L_{6})
in a partial order by defining

(L_{1},\dots,L_{6})\preccurlyeq(K_{1},\dots,K_{6})

if there exists a permutation
\sigma\in S_{6}
such that

L_{i}\subseteq K_{\sigma(i)}.

In particular, this gives a partial order on the set
\mathcal{S}
by restriction. Recall that
\mathcal{S}
consists of infinitely many elements. A good way to describe them is as follows.

Theorem A.1
.

There are finitely many minimal elements in
\mathcal{S}
under the ordering
\preccurlyeq
.

Write
\mathcal{T}
for this finite set. Then one may reconstruct
\mathcal{S}
as

\mathcal{S}=\{(L_{1},\dots,L_{6}):t\preccurlyeq(L_{1},\dots,L_{6})\text{ for some }t\in\mathcal{T}\}.

The goal of this section is to give a constructive proof of the above theorem. In fact, we will use an algorithm to compute a finite superset of
\mathcal{T}
. We will also show the following result in the process.

Theorem A.2
.

There exists a finite set of points
P\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with the following property. Suppose that
(L_{1},\dots,L_{6})
satisfies

P\subseteq L_{1}\cup\dots\cup L_{6}.

Then we have
L_{1}\cup\dots\cup L_{6}=\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

The list
P
will be completely explicit.

A.1

The goal of this subsection is to give and explain the algorithm that computed Table

. We will first give an important subroutine. All our algorithms are implemented with SageMath.

A.1.1
Subroutine for covering

We will now give an algorithm that accomplishes the following task.

Routine isCover(L).

Input: a list
(L_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}
, where each
L_{i}
is a subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

Output: return True if and only if
\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq n}L_{i}=\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

V = FreeModule(ZZ, 2)
e1 = vector((1, 0))
e2 = vector((0, 1))

def isCover(L):
boolean = True
for i in range(len(L)):
if L[i].rank() == 2:
boolean = False

if boolean:
return False

W = V.span([e1, e2])
for i in range(len(L)):
if L[i].rank() == 2:
W = W.intersection(L[i])

genList = W.gens()
gen1 = genList[0]
gen2 = genList[1]
a = gen1[0]
b = gen2[1]

for i in range(a):
for j in range(b):
boolean = False
for k in range(len(L)):
v = vector((i, j))
if L[k].rank() == 2 and v in L[k]:
boolean = True
if not boolean:
return False

return True

Lemma A.3
.

The subroutine isCover(L) outputs true if and only if
\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq n}L_{i}=\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

Proof.

We have

\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq n}L_{i}\neq\mathbb{Z}^{2},

if the rank of
L_{i}
is smaller than
2
for all
i
. This is checked in the first seven lines of the algorithm. Write
S
for the subset of
\{1,\dots,n\}
such that the rank of
L_{i}
equals
2
. Then we observe that

\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq n}L_{i}=\mathbb{Z}^{2}\Longleftrightarrow\bigcup_{i\in S}L_{i}=\mathbb{Z}^{2}.

So it suffices to show that isCover(L) outputs true if and only if
\bigcup_{i\in S}L_{i}=\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Now we check this last condition as follows. Define

W:=\bigcap_{i\in S}L_{i}.

Since all the
L_{i}
are of rank
2
, we see that
W
is of finite index in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. The generators of
W
are given by SageMath in row echelon form with the first generator being
(a,0)
for some
a
, and the second generator being
(\ast,b)
for some
b
. From this, we see that a fundamental domain for
\mathbb{Z}^{2}/W
is

\mathcal{F}=\{(i,j):0\leq i<a,0\leq j<b\}.

By this we mean that

\bigcup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}f+W=\mathbb{Z}^{2},

and
(f+W)\cap(g+W)=\{0\}
for all distinct
f,g\in\mathcal{F}
. By construction of
W
, we have that

\bigcup_{i\in S}L_{i}=\mathbb{Z}^{2}\Longleftrightarrow\mathcal{F}\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in S}L_{i}.

This last condition is checked at the end of the algorithm.
∎

Lemma A.4
.

Suppose that
n\leq 6
. Then the algorithm FindLattice(L, index) terminates and gives the claimed output.

Proof.

Let us first remark that the algorithm FindLattice(L, index) in principle may crash. This can only happen in the assignment

v = PointList[index]

in the first while loop, because the variable index may be out of bounds, or in other words the variable index might be at least the length of the list
P
. We will first show that this does not happen for
n\leq 6
, and here we will of course use the specific shape of
P
in equation (
A.1
).

We run the algorithm with
L=(\{0\})_{1\leq i\leq 6}
on the computer, and we find that the algorithm terminates without crashing. Now suppose that

\displaystyle P\subseteq\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq 6}L_{i}

(A.2)

for some subgroups
L_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Then we claim that

\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq 6}L_{i}=\mathbb{Z}^{2}.

Suppose that the claim is false. We will show that then the algorithm crashes. Indeed, take such a list
(L_{i})_{1\leq i\leq 6}
. By equation (
A.2
) we may ensure that we recursively call the routine FindLattice(K, index) with a list
(K_{i})_{1\leq i\leq 6}
satisfying

(K_{i})_{1\leq i\leq 6}\preccurlyeq(L_{i})_{1\leq i\leq 6}.

But then the algorithm must crash.

From the claim we deduce that the algorithm does not crash on all valid inputs for
n\leq 6
. Furthermore, it is also clear that the algorithm terminates if it does not crash, since at each recursive call we increase the variable index by
1
.

It remains to show that the algorithm gives the claimed output. Take a tuple
(L_{i}^{\prime})_{1\leq i\leq 6}
satisfying

\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq 6}L_{i}^{\prime}=\mathbb{Z}^{2},\quad(L_{i})_{1\leq i\leq 6}\preccurlyeq(L_{i}^{\prime})_{1\leq i\leq 6},\quad L_{i}^{\prime}\neq\mathbb{Z}^{2}\text{ for all }i.

To find our tuple
(K_{i})_{1\leq i\leq 6}
, we simply enforce that
(K_{i})_{1\leq i\leq 6}\preccurlyeq(L_{i}^{\prime})_{1\leq i\leq 6}
at each recursive step.
∎

Breakdown of superconductivity in a magnetic field with self-intersecting zero set
Kamel Attar

Abstract
. We prove that the lowest eigenvalue of the Laplace operator with a magnetic field having a self-intersecting zero set is a monotone function of the parameter defining the strength of the magnetic field, in a neighborhood of infinity. We apply this monotonicity result on the study of the transition from superconducting to normal states for the Ginzburg-Landau model, and prove that the transition occurs at a unique threshold value of the applied magnetic field.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Breakdown of superconductivity.

Superconductivity is a state of metals and alloys that appear below a certain critical temperature
\displaystyle T_{c}
. When the temperature is below
\displaystyle T_{c}
the body must be in the superconducting state; if an applied magnetic field (with small intensity) attempts to destroy the superconductivity, an induced magnetic field appears and repels the applied magnetic field. If the intensity of the applied magnetic field is increased gradually past a critical value, the superconductor can no longer resist the magnetic field and the appearance of superconductivity in the metal decreases gradually past critical conducting states (superconducting state, mixed conducting state and normal conducting state). Eventually, a large magnetic field destroys superconductivity from the sample (see Figure
1
).

\displaystyle H_{C_{1}}
is the intensity of the magnetic field where a superconductor switches from the superconducting state to the mixed conducting state (vortex state). In this case the superconductor allows the applied magnetic field to pass through small regions of the sample
[

.

•

\displaystyle H_{C_{2}}
is the intensity of the magnetic field that corresponds to the transition from superconductivity located in regions far from the boundary to surface superconductivity

•

\displaystyle H_{C_{3}}
is the intensity of the magnetic field that corresponds to the transition from surface superconductivity to the normal conducting state; in this case the superconductivity can no longer exist in the superconductor

1.2.
Magnetic field with self-intersecting zeros

Suppose that
\displaystyle\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is bounded, open, simply connected with smooth boundary. Consider a vertical magnetic field of the form

B_{0}\vec{z}

where the function
\displaystyle B_{0}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
has a non-trivial zero set

(1.1)

\Gamma=\{x\in\overline{\Omega}:B_{0}(x)=0\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Sigma=\{x\in\Gamma:\nabla B_{0}(x)=0\}

and satisfies the following properties (see
[
8
]
)

\Gamma\neq\varnothing,\quad\Sigma\neq\varnothing{\rm\penalty\ is\penalty\ finite,}\quad\Sigma\cap\partial\Omega=\varnothing

and

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rll}|B_{0}|+|\nabla B_{0}|>0&\mbox{ in }\overline{\Omega}\setminus\Sigma&(a)\\
\nabla B_{0}\times\vec{n}\neq 0&\mbox{ on }\Gamma\cap\partial\Omega&(b)\\
|\nabla B_{0}|+|{\rm Hess}B_{0}|>0&\mbox{ on }\Gamma&(c)\\
\vec{\tau_{1}}\times\vec{\tau_{2}}\neq 0&\mbox{ on }\Sigma&(d)\,.\end{array}\right.

Here
\displaystyle\vec{n}
is the unit normal vector to the boundary,
\displaystyle\vec{\tau}_{1}
and
\displaystyle\vec{\tau}_{2}
are the unit tangent vector on the intersection point
\displaystyle x\in\Sigma
, and
\displaystyle{\rm Hess}B_{0}
is the Hessian matrix of the magnetic field at the point
\displaystyle x
which has two non-zero eigenvalues
\displaystyle\lambda^{\rm Hess}_{1}(x)
and
\displaystyle\lambda^{\rm Hess}_{2}(x)
with opposite signs and labeled as follows

(1.3)

|\lambda^{\rm Hess}_{1}(x)|\leq|\lambda^{\rm Hess}_{2}(x)|\quad(x\in\Sigma)\,.

Assumption
\displaystyle\eqref{B(x)}_{(a)}
implies that for any open set
\displaystyle\omega
relatively compact in
\displaystyle\Omega
,
\displaystyle\Gamma\cap\omega
is either empty, or consists of a union of smooth curves and the quantity
\displaystyle|\nabla B_{0}|
does not vanish on
\displaystyle\Gamma
. We see from
\displaystyle\eqref{B(x)}_{(b)}
that the
\displaystyle B_{0}
is allowed to vanish on a finite number of boundary points and that the curve
\displaystyle\Gamma
cannot intersect tangentially
\displaystyle\partial\Omega
. Assumptions
\displaystyle\eqref{B(x)}_{(c)}
and
\displaystyle\eqref{B(x)}_{(d)}
tell us that the smooth curves can intersect on isolated points where
\displaystyle|\nabla B_{0}|
vanishes.

The case where the magnetic field vanishes non-degenerately along a smooth non self intersecting curve, is the subject of numerous works in the contexts of shape optimization
[

[

1.3.
Magnetic Laplacian and strong diamagnetism

Consider the unique vector field
\displaystyle\mathbf{F}\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
satisfying the following properties

(1.4)

\nu\cdot\mathbf{F}=0\penalty\ {\rm on\penalty\ }\partial\Omega,\quad{\rm div}\mathbf{F}=0\penalty\ \&\penalty\ \curl\mathbf{F}=B_{0}{\penalty\ \rm in\penalty\ }\Omega\,,

where
\displaystyle\nu
is the unit outward normal of the boundary of
\displaystyle\Omega
.

Let us introduce the magnetic Schrödinger operator

(1.5)

P_{b\mathbf{F}}^{\Omega}=-(\nabla-ib\mathbf{F})^{2}\,,

with domain

D(P_{b\mathbf{F}}^{\Omega}):=\{u\in\mathcal{V}(\Omega)\penalty\ :\penalty\ P_{b\mathbf{F}}^{\Omega}u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\penalty\ \&\penalty\ \nu\cdot(\nabla-ib\mathbf{F})u=0\penalty\ {\rm on}\penalty\ \partial\Omega\}\,.

The lowest eigenvalue (ground state energy) of this operator is

(1.6)

\mu_{1}(b\mathbf{F})=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi\in H^{1}(\Omega)\\
\phi\neq 0\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{\mathcal{Q}_{b\mathbf{F}}^{\Omega}(\phi)}{\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\right)\,,

where

(1.7)

\mathcal{Q}_{b\mathbf{F}}^{\Omega}(\phi)=\int_{\Omega}|(\nabla-ib\mathbf{F})\phi|^{2}\,dx\,.

The large field limit
\displaystyle b\to+\infty
can be transformed to a semi-classical limit by introducing the small parameter
\displaystyle h=\frac{1}{b}
. The full asymptotic expansion of the lowest eigenvalue
\displaystyle P_{b\mathbf{F}}^{\Omega}
is derived by Dauge, Miqueu and Raymond
[

]
.
Based on it, we prove the monotonicity of
\displaystyle\mu_{1}(b\mathbf{F})
with respect to
\displaystyle b
in a neighborhood of
\displaystyle+\infty
. This property is named

in the literature
[

,

,

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists
\displaystyle b_{0}>0
, such that the function
\displaystyle b\longmapsto\mu_{1}(b\mathbf{F})
is monotone increasing on
\displaystyle[b_{0},+\infty[

1.4.
Ginzburg-Landau model

Consider the Ginzburg-Landau functional

(1.8)

\mathcal{E}_{\kappa,H,B_{0}}(\psi,\mathbf{A})=\int_{\Omega}\left(|(\nabla-i\kappa H\mathbf{A})\psi|^{2}+\frac{\kappa^{2}}{2}(1-|\psi|^{2})^{2}\right)\,dx+\kappa^{2}H^{2}\int_{\Omega}|\curl\mathbf{A}-B_{0}|^{2}\,dx\,.

The vector field
\displaystyle\mathbf{A}
is called the magnetic potential which describes the induced magnetic field in the sample via

\curl\mathbf{A}=\overrightarrow{\nabla}\times\mathbf{A}\,.

The positive number
\displaystyle\kappa
is the characteristic scale of the superconductor that distinguishes the material of the sample; we will study the case of materials of type II where
\displaystyle\kappa
is sufficiently large (
\displaystyle\kappa\to\infty
). The complex-valued function
\displaystyle\psi
is the order parameter that rises in the superconductor phase, where the modulus squared
\displaystyle|\psi(x)|^{2}
can be interpreted as the local density of the superconducting electron Cooper pairs as follows:

•

If
\displaystyle|\psi(x)|\neq 0
, then at location
\displaystyle x
, the material is in the superconducting state.

•

If
\displaystyle|\psi(x)|=0
, then at location
\displaystyle x
, there exists no Cooper pairs, and the material is in the normal state.

•

If
\displaystyle|\psi(x)|
has zeros but does not vanish identically, the material is in the
mixed state
.

Since the functional is invariant under gauge transformations
\displaystyle(\psi,\mathbf{A})\to(e^{i\kappa H\chi}\psi,\mathbf{A}-\nabla\chi)
, it is enough to consider configurations
\displaystyle(\psi,\mathbf{A})
in the space
\displaystyle H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{C})\times H^{1}_{\Div}(\Omega)
of sobolev functions, where

(1.9)

H^{1}_{\Div}(\Omega)=\Big\{\mathbf{A}=(\mathbf{A}_{1},\mathbf{A}_{2})\in H^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)\penalty\ :\penalty\ \Div\mathbf{A}=0\penalty\ {\rm in}\penalty\ \Omega\,,\,\mathbf{A}\cdot\nu=0\penalty\ {\rm on}\,\partial\Omega\,\Big\}\,,

with
\displaystyle\nu
being the unit interior normal vector of

\displaystyle\partial\Omega
.

The equilibrium state of the system will be where the total energy
\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\kappa,H,B_{0}}
is minimal, we denote by
\displaystyle(\psi,\mathbf{A})
the minimizing configuration of
\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\kappa,H,B_{0}}
. When
\displaystyle\psi=0
, the minimizing configuration is in the form
\displaystyle(0,\mathbf{F})
with
\displaystyle\mathbf{F}
in
\displaystyle H^{1}_{\Div}(\Omega)
such that
\displaystyle\curl\mathbf{F}=B_{0}
. Notice that this solution is unique (up to
a gauge transformation) and we call the pair
\displaystyle(0,\mathbf{F})
the normal state.

Ginzburg-Landau equations

Minimizers,
\displaystyle(\psi,\mathbf{A})\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{C})\times H^{1}_{\Div}(\Omega)
of (
1.8
) have to satisfy the Ginzburg-Landau equations,

(1.10)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}-(\nabla-i\kappa H\mathbf{A})^{2}\psi=\kappa^{2}\,(1-|\psi|^{2})\,\psi&{\rm in}&\Omega&(a)\\
-\nabla^{\bot}\curl(\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{F})=\displaystyle\frac{1}{\kappa H}\IM(\overline{\psi}\,(\nabla-i\kappa H\mathbf{A})\psi)&{\rm in}&\Omega&(b)\\
\nu\cdot(\nabla-i\kappa H\mathbf{A})\psi=0&{\rm on}&\partial\Omega&(c)\\
\curl\mathbf{A}=\curl\mathbf{F}&{\rm on}&\partial\Omega&(d)\,.\end{array}\right.

Here,

\displaystyle\nabla^{\bot}\curl\mathbf{A}=\Big(\partial_{x_{2}}(\curl\mathbf{A}),-\partial_{x_{1}}(\curl\mathbf{A})\Big)
. The boundary conditions state that the supperconducting current can not flow outside the sample (the sample is surrounded by vacuum).

A solution
\displaystyle(\psi,\mathbf{A})
of (
1.10
) is said to be trivial if
\displaystyle\psi=0
everywhere (consequently
\displaystyle\curl\mathbf{A}=b
everywhere). Let us recall a simple criterion
[
25
, Lemma 2.1]
for the existence of a non-trivial solution of the Ginzburg-Landau equations in (
1.10
).

Proposition 1.2

(Sufficient condition for existence of non-trivial solutions)
.

For all
\displaystyle\kappa
,
\displaystyle H>0
, if
\displaystyle\mu_{1}(\kappa H\mathbf{F})<\kappa^{2}
, then every minimizer of
\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\kappa,H,B_{0}}
is non-trivial.

Our main result proves that the condition
\displaystyle\mu_{1}(\kappa H\mathbf{F})<\kappa^{2}
is also necessary, provided
\displaystyle\kappa
is sufficiently large.

Theorem 1.3

(Necessary condition for existence of non-trivial solutions)
.

There exists
\displaystyle\kappa_{0}>0
such that, for all
\displaystyle\kappa\geq\kappa_{0}
and
\displaystyle H>0
, the following two properties are equivalent:

A.

There exists a solution
\displaystyle(\psi,\mathbf{A})
of (
1.10
) such that
\displaystyle\psi\not\equiv 0
.

B.

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(\kappa H\mathbf{F})<\kappa^{2}
.

For non sign-changing smooth magnetic fields and smooth domains, Theorem
1.3
was proved by Fournais-Helffer
[
13
]
. For uniform magnetic fields and non-smooth domains, it was proved by
[
5
]
.

Discussion

Combining Theorems
1.1
and Theorem
1.3
, we know that the transition from trivial to non-trivial solution occurs at a unique threshold
\displaystyle H_{C_{3}}(\kappa)
. More precisely,

\exists\,\kappa_{0}>0,\penalty\ \forall\,\kappa\geq\kappa_{0},\penalty\ \exists\,H_{C_{3}}(\kappa)>0

such that

•

\displaystyle H=H_{C_{3}}(\kappa)
is the unique solution of
\displaystyle\mu_{1}(\kappa H)=\kappa^{2}
;

•

For
\displaystyle H<H_{C_{3}}(\kappa)
, every minimizer of
\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\kappa,H,B_{0}}
is non-trivial (i.e. the order parameter does not vanish everywhere) ;

•

For
\displaystyle H\geq H_{C_{3}}(\kappa)
, every solution
\displaystyle(\psi,\mathbf{A})
of (
1.10
) is trivial (i.e.
\displaystyle\psi\equiv 0
) .

The existing spectral asymptotics
[
8
]
for the eigenvalue
\displaystyle\mu_{1}(b\mathbf{F})
also yield a complete asymptotic expansion of
\displaystyle H_{C_{3}}(\kappa)
; in fact (see Theorem
2.1
)

H_{C_{3}}(\kappa)\sim\kappa^{3}\sup_{x\in\Sigma}\left(\frac{1}{|\lambda^{\rm Hess}_{2}(x)|\left[\mathcal{X}_{1}\Big(\varepsilon(x)\Big)\right]^{2}}\right)+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{+\infty}c_{j}\kappa^{3-j}\qquad(\kappa\to+\infty)\,.

Here

(1.11)

\varepsilon(x)=\sqrt{\frac{|\lambda^{\rm Hess}_{1}(x)|}{|\lambda^{\rm Hess}_{2}(x)|}},

and for
\displaystyle\varepsilon>0
,
\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{1}(\varepsilon)
denotes the first eigenvalue of the following operator

(1.12)

\mathcal{J}^{\varepsilon}:=D_{t}^{2}+\left(D_{s}-\left(\frac{t^{3}}{3}-\varepsilon^{2}s^{2}t\right)\right)^{2}\,,\qquad\text{in}\penalty\ L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\,.

Furthermore, for
\displaystyle\kappa\geq\kappa_{0}
and
\displaystyle H<H_{C_{3}}(\kappa)
, the local minimizers of the functional
\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\kappa,H,B_{0}}
are necessarily non-trivial. In fact,
\displaystyle(0,\mathbf{F})
is a local minimizer (locally stable) provided that
the second variation of the
\displaystyle\mathcal{E}
is positive,

{\rm Hess}_{(0,\mathbf{F})}>0\,,

which is equivalent to the property
\displaystyle\mu_{1}(\kappa H\mathbf{F})>\kappa^{2}
.

Theorem 2.1

(Dauge-Miqueu-Raymond

Let

Here,

\lambda(\beta)=\mu_{1}(\beta^{2}\mathbf{F})\,.

.

Let

There exist positive constants
\displaystyle C_{1}
and
\displaystyle C_{2}
such that, if
\displaystyle\kappa>0
,
\displaystyle H>0

\displaystyle(\psi,\mathbf{A})
is a solution of (

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|(\nabla-i\kappa H\mathbf{A})\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|(\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{F})\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

.

We denote by

\displaystyle P_{b\mathbf{F}}^{\Omega}
with Neumann condition in

4.
Criterion for the breakdown of superconductivity

The purpose of this section is to prove Theorem
1.3
.
One ingredient in the proof is

4.1
below on the localization of the order parameter, which is an adaptation of an analogous result obtained in
[

]
for the case where the magnetic field has a non-empty zero set but not self-intersecting. It says that, if
\displaystyle(\psi,\mathbf{A})
is a critical point of the functional in (
1.8

\displaystyle H
is of order
\displaystyle\kappa^{3}
, then
\displaystyle|\psi|
is concentrated near the set
\displaystyle\Sigma=\{x\in\Omega\penalty\ :\penalty\ B_{0}(x)=0,\&\,\nabla B_{0}(x)=0\}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\displaystyle\varepsilon>0
and
\displaystyle\alpha\in\left(0,1\right)
. There exist two positive constants
\displaystyle C
and
\displaystyle\kappa_{0}
such that, if

(4.1)

\kappa\geq\kappa_{0}\,,\quad H\geq\varepsilon\,\kappa^{3}\,,

and
\displaystyle(\psi,\mathbf{A})
is a solution of (
1.10
), then

(4.2)

\|\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\,\kappa^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\|\psi\|^{2}_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\,.

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle\ell=\kappa^{-\alpha}
with

Implicit ZCA Whitening Effects of Linear Autoencoders for Recommendation
Katsuhiko Hayashi, Kazuma Onishi

Abstract
Recently, in the field of recommendation systems, linear regression (autoencoder) models have been investigated as a way to learn item similarity. In this paper, we show a connection between a linear autoencoder model and ZCA whitening for recommendation data. In particular, we show that the dual form solution of a linear autoencoder model actually has ZCA whitening effects on feature vectors of items, while items are considered as input features in the primal problem of the autoencoder/regression model. We also show the correctness of applying a linear autoencoder to low-dimensional item vectors obtained using embedding methods such as Item2vec to estimate item-item similarities. Our experiments provide preliminary results indicating the effectiveness of whitening low-dimensional item embeddings.

Introduction

E-commerce has become an indispensable part of our daily lives. Recommender systems help users find products they want to buy on e-commerce sites and have a wide range of applications, such as in movie recommendations and product recommendations. Collaborative filtering (CF) is one of the most widely used approaches in recommender systems.
Nearest neighbor CF approaches are divided into user-based and item-based ones.
In item-based collaborative filtering (ICF), recommendations to users are made by finding items that are similar to other items that a given user has already had an interaction with. Therefore, the similarity measure between two items plays an important role in the ICF approach.
While early ICF models used such statistical measures as Pearson correlation and cosine similarity
[
21
,
11
,
5
]
,
model-based methods
[
15
,
9
,
18
]
have recently been investigated as a way to learn item similarity.
In particular, ICF models based on linear regressions (autoencoders)
[
15
,
18
,
8
,
14
]
have achieved current state-of-the-art performances on several benchmark datasets for implicit recommendation.

As to the reason for the empirical success of linear autoencoders, in this study,
we reveal that they actually have ZCA whitening
[
10
]
effects on recommendation data.
The whitening transformation removes correlations between the feature dimensions of the item vectors, and the item-item similarity estimated from the transformed vectors improves
the quality of the recommendations.
Our finding also has the following empirical contribution:

•

Whitening Item Embeddings
: In the field of natural language processing, it is known that whitening word embeddings improves the performance of various retrieval and similarity tasks
[
19
,
7
]
. Analogously, embedding methods such as Item2vec
[
1
]
, which learns a latent semantic feature vector representation of items, are useful in the field of information retrieval.
Thus, whitening item embeddings is expected to improve recommendation performance. Our experiments provide preliminary results that show the effectiveness of whitening low-dimensional item embeddings.

3.1
Linear Autoencoder with L2 Regularization

Linear autoencoders can be trained with the multivariate least squares fitting approach.
The linear algebraic formulation can be represented as a ridge linear regression problem:

\widehat{\textbf{B}}=\argmin_{\textbf{B}}\Bigl\{||\textbf{X}-\textbf{X}\textbf{B}||_{F}^{2}+\lambda||\textbf{B}||_{F}^{2}\Bigr\}

(1)

where
\lambda>0
is a regularization parameter.
The closed form solution of the above equation is given as

\widehat{\textbf{B}}=(\textbf{X}^{\mathrm{T}}\textbf{X}+\lambda\textbf{I}_{|I|})^{-1}\textbf{X}^{\mathrm{T}}\textbf{X}

(2)

or, equivalently
[
17
]
,

\widehat{\textbf{B}}=\textbf{X}^{\mathrm{T}}(\textbf{X}\textbf{X}^{\mathrm{T}}+\lambda\textbf{I}_{|U|})^{-1}\textbf{X}.

(3)

For a proof of the equivalence between Eqs.(
2
) and (
3
), we refer the read to the Appendix A of the paper
[
3
]
(in the case of
|I|>|U|
) and our Appendix
A
(in the case of
|U|>|I|
).
Note that though the minimization of Eq.(
1
) is achieved in an obvious way (
\textbf{B}=\textbf{I}_{|I|}
),
\mathop{\text{diag}}{(\textbf{B})}=\textbf{0}_{|I|}
is imposed as a constraint condition in practical linear autoencoder models like SLIM
[
15
]
and EASE
[

]
.

3.2
EASE: Linear Autoencoder with

Diagonal Constraints
[
18
]

Steck
[
18
]
introduced a linear autoencoder model, called EASE.
The objective function of EASE for learning the item-item similarity matrix
B
is:

\displaystyle\widehat{\textbf{B}}_{\text{EASE}}=\argmin_{\textbf{B}}\Bigl\{||\textbf{X}-\textbf{X}\textbf{B}||_{F}^{2}+\lambda||\textbf{B}||_{F}^{2}\Bigr\}

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}\ \ \ \mathop{\text{diag}}{(\textbf{B})}=\textbf{0}_{|I|}.

The optimization problem can be solved with the following Lagrangian:

L=||\textbf{X}-\textbf{X}\textbf{B}||_{F}^{2}+\lambda||\textbf{B}||_{F}^{2}+2\bm{\alpha}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathop{\text{diag}}{(\textbf{B})}

where
\bm{\alpha}=[\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{|I|}]^{\mathrm{T}}
is the vector of Lagrange multipliers.
By setting the derivative to zero,
we derive the estimate of the similarity matrix
B
:

\widehat{\textbf{B}}_{\text{EASE}}=(\textbf{X}^{\mathrm{T}}\textbf{X}+\lambda\textbf{I}_{|I|})^{-1}(\textbf{X}^{\mathrm{T}}\textbf{X}-\mathop{\text{diagMat}}{(\bm{\alpha})}).

By imposing the constraint
\mathop{\text{diag}}(\widehat{\textbf{B}}_{\text{EASE}})=0
and defining
\widehat{\textbf{P}}=(\textbf{X}^{\mathrm{T}}\textbf{X}+\lambda\textbf{I}_{|I|})^{-1}
,
\bm{\alpha}
is determined as

\bm{\alpha}=\textbf{1}_{|I|}\oslash\mathop{\text{diag}}(\widehat{\textbf{P}})-\lambda\textbf{1}_{|I|}.

Steck
[
18
]
showed that the solution can be derived in the following closed form:

\widehat{\textbf{B}}_{\text{EASE}}=\textbf{I}_{|I|}-\widehat{\textbf{P}}\mathop{\text{diagMat}}{(\textbf{1}_{|I|}\oslash\mathop{\text{diag}}(\widehat{\textbf{P}}))}

where
\oslash
denotes elementwise division.

4
Relationship between Linear Autoencoder

and ZCA Whitening

4.1
ZCA Whitening (Zero-phase Component Analysis)

Whitening is an operation that eliminates correlations between features in a data sample. That is, correlation between any two components
x_{i}
and
x_{j}
of a sample vector
\textbf{x}=[x_{1},\dots,x_{D}]
in the
D
-dimensional feature space is reduced to zero. In the following, we assume that a feature vector of each sample is centered; i.e.,
\frac{1}{D}\sum_{i=1}^{D}x_{i}=0
.
Given
N
data samples
\textbf{X}=[\textbf{x}_{1},\dots,\textbf{x}_{N}]\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times N}
,
the correlation coefficients between features are represented by the following covariance matrix:

\textbf{$\Phi$}_{\textbf{X}}=\frac{1}{N}\textbf{X}\textbf{X}^{\mathrm{T}}.

We now consider a linear transformation
\textbf{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times D}
of feature vectors:

\textbf{w}_{n}=\textbf{P}\textbf{x}_{n}\ \ \ (n=1,\dots,N).

Accordingly, the covariance matrix of the transformed vectors
\textbf{W}=[\textbf{w}_{1},\dots,\textbf{w}_{N}]
is defined as:

\textbf{$\Phi$}_{\textbf{W}}=\frac{1}{N}\textbf{W}\textbf{W}^{\mathrm{T}}.

The purpose of whitening is to find a projection matrix
P
that makes the covariance matrix
\textbf{$\Phi$}_{\textbf{W}}
be the identity matrix
\textbf{I}_{D}
.
If
\textbf{$\Phi$}_{\textbf{W}}=\textbf{I}_{D}
,
P
must satisfy the following equation:

\textbf{P}^{\mathrm{T}}\textbf{P}=\textbf{$\Phi$}_{\textbf{X}}^{-1}.

(4)

P
that satisfies Eq.(
4
) can be represented by using the eigenvectors
U
of
\textbf{X}\textbf{X}^{\mathrm{T}}
.

\textbf{X}\textbf{X}^{\mathrm{T}}=\textbf{U}\bm{\Sigma}\textbf{U}^{\mathrm{T}}

where
U
is a square
D\times D
matrix whose
i
-th column is the
i
-th eigen vector of
\textbf{X}\textbf{X}^{\mathrm{T}}
, and
\bm{\Sigma}
is a diagonal matrix whose diagonal elements are the corresponding eigenvalues,
d_{ii}=\lambda_{i}
.
Since
\bm{\Sigma}
is an orthogonal matrix (
\textbf{U}^{\mathrm{T}}\textbf{U}=\textbf{U}\textbf{U}^{\mathrm{T}}=\textbf{I}_{D}
),
\textbf{$\Phi$}_{\textbf{X}}^{-1}
can be denoted by
\textbf{U}\bm{\Sigma}^{-1}\textbf{U}^{\mathrm{T}}
.
In zero-phase component Analysis (ZCA) or ZCA whitening
[
10
]
, we use

\textbf{P}_{\text{ZCA}}=\textbf{U}\bm{\Sigma}^{-1/2}\textbf{U}^{\mathrm{T}}

as a transformation matrix
P
.
In practice, noise
\epsilon
is often added to the diagonal of
\bm{\Sigma}
:

\textbf{P}_{\text{ZCA}}=\textbf{U}(\bm{\Sigma}+\epsilon\textbf{I}_{D})^{-1/2}\textbf{U}^{\mathrm{T}}.

4.2
Whitening Effects of Linear Autoencoders

Linear Autoencoder with L2 Regularization

First, we consider the ZCA whitening transformation of the user-item interaction matrix
\textbf{X}\in\{0,1\}^{|U|\times|I|}
(here, we assume
|U|>|I|
):

\textbf{W}=\textbf{P}_{\text{ZCA}}\textbf{X}

where
\textbf{P}_{\text{ZCA}}=\textbf{U}(\bm{\Sigma}+\epsilon\textbf{I}_{|U|})^{-1/2}\textbf{U}^{\mathrm{T}}
.
Note that
U
and
\bm{\Sigma}
are the eigen vector and eigenvalue matrices of
\textbf{X}\textbf{X}^{\mathrm{T}}
.
After this whitening transformation,
we simply consider

\widehat{\textbf{B}}_{\text{ZCA}}=\textbf{W}^{\mathrm{T}}\textbf{W}

as an item-item similarity matrix for ICF-based recommender systems.

Now let us rewrite
\widehat{\textbf{B}}_{\text{ZCA}}
:

\displaystyle\widehat{\textbf{B}}_{\text{ZCA}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textbf{W}^{\mathrm{T}}\textbf{W}=(\textbf{P}_{\text{ZCA}}\textbf{X})^{\mathrm{T}}(\textbf{P}_{\text{ZCA}}\textbf{X})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textbf{X}^{\mathrm{T}}\bigl(\textbf{U}(\bm{\Sigma}+\epsilon\textbf{I}_{|U|})^{-1/2}\textbf{U}^{\mathrm{T}}\bigr)\bigl(\textbf{U}(\bm{\Sigma}+\epsilon\textbf{I}_{|U|})^{-1/2}\textbf{U}^{\mathrm{T}}\bigr)\textbf{X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textbf{X}^{\mathrm{T}}\bigl(\textbf{U}(\bm{\Sigma}+\epsilon\textbf{I}_{|U|})^{-1}\textbf{U}^{\mathrm{T}}\bigr)\textbf{X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textbf{X}^{\mathrm{T}}\bigl(\textbf{U}\bm{\Sigma}\textbf{U}^{\mathrm{T}}+\epsilon\textbf{U}\textbf{U}^{\mathrm{T}}\textbf{U}\textbf{U}^{\mathrm{T}}\bigr)^{-1}\textbf{X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textbf{X}^{\mathrm{T}}\bigl(\textbf{X}\textbf{X}^{\mathrm{T}}+\epsilon\textbf{I}_{|U|}\bigr)^{-1}\textbf{X}\ \ \ \ \ \ (=\text{Eq.(\ref{eq:solution2})})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigl(\textbf{X}^{\mathrm{T}}\textbf{X}+\epsilon\textbf{I}_{|I|}\bigr)^{-1}\textbf{X}^{\mathrm{T}}\textbf{X}\ \ \ \ \ \ (=\text{Eq.(\ref{eq:solution1})}).

Refer to our Appendix
A
for details on the final transformation.
This result clearly shows a connection between the shallow linear autoencoder and ZCA whitening.
We can see that linear autoencoders actually have implicit ZCA whitening-like effects on feature vectors of items

Linear Autoencoder with Diagonal Constraints

As pointed out in the paper
[
14
]
, the solution of EASE can be divided into two terms: regularization and diagonal constraints. The former regularization part is equivalent to the
solution of linear autoencoder with L2 regularization:

\widehat{\textbf{B}}_{\text{EASE}}=\underbrace{\bigl(\textbf{X}^{\mathrm{T}}\textbf{X}+\lambda\textbf{I}_{|I|}\bigr)^{-1}\textbf{X}^{\mathrm{T}}\textbf{X}}_{\widehat{\textbf{B}}_{\text{ZCA}}}-\bigl(\textbf{X}^{\mathrm{T}}\textbf{X}+\lambda\textbf{I}_{|I|}\bigr)^{-1}\mathop{\text{diagMat}}(\bm{\alpha}).

This result shows that EASE also has implicit whitening effects on item vectors.
From
[
14
]
, it is known that the latter diagonal constraint part plays a role that penalizes the impact of unpopular items.
In future work, we will further investigate the role of the diagonal constraint part from the view point of the data preprocessing stage.

Whitening Item Embeddings

Several studies
[
1
,
6
,
8
]
have shown that a latent semantic representation of items is useful for estimating item similarity.
We consider item embeddings
\textbf{E}\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times|I|}
, where
D<|I|
is the dimension size of the item embedding.
Our finding on a relationship between the linear autoencoder and ZCA whitening shows the correctness of using a linear autoencoder on item embeddings:

\widehat{\textbf{B}}=\argmin_{\textbf{B}}\Bigl\{||\textbf{E}-\textbf{E}\textbf{B}||_{F}^{2}+\lambda||\textbf{B}||_{F}^{2}\Bigr\}.

The final solution is

\widehat{\textbf{B}}=(\textbf{E}^{\mathrm{T}}\textbf{E}+\lambda\textbf{I}_{|I|})^{-1}\textbf{E}^{\mathrm{T}}\textbf{E}.

In the case of
D<|I|
, by using Lemma 9 of the paper
[
3
]
, we can transform the above equation into

\widehat{\textbf{B}}=\textbf{E}^{\mathrm{T}}(\textbf{E}\textbf{E}^{\mathrm{T}}+\lambda\textbf{I}_{D})^{-1}\textbf{E}.

Moreover, we can further transform this equation as follows:

\displaystyle\widehat{\textbf{B}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textbf{E}^{\mathrm{T}}(\textbf{E}\textbf{E}^{\mathrm{T}}+\lambda\textbf{I}_{D})^{-1}\textbf{E}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textbf{E}^{\mathrm{T}}(\textbf{U}\bm{\Sigma}\textbf{U}^{\mathrm{T}}+\lambda\textbf{U}\textbf{I}_{D}\textbf{U}^{\mathrm{T}})^{-1}\textbf{E}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textbf{E}^{\mathrm{T}}\textbf{U}(\bm{\Sigma}+\lambda\textbf{I}_{D})^{-1}\textbf{U}^{\mathrm{T}}\textbf{E}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textbf{E}^{\mathrm{T}}\textbf{U}(\bm{\Sigma}+\lambda\textbf{I}_{D})^{-1/2}\textbf{U}^{\mathrm{T}}\textbf{U}(\bm{\Sigma}+\lambda\textbf{I}_{D})^{-1/2}\textbf{U}^{\mathrm{T}}\textbf{E}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\textbf{P}_{\text{ZCA}}\textbf{E})^{\mathrm{T}}(\textbf{P}_{\text{ZCA}}\textbf{E})

where
\textbf{U}\bm{\Sigma}\textbf{U}^{\mathrm{T}}
is the eigenvalue decomposition of
\textbf{E}\textbf{E}^{\mathrm{T}}
and
\textbf{P}_{\text{ZCA}}
denotes
\textbf{U}(\bm{\Sigma}+\lambda\textbf{I}_{D})^{-1/2}\textbf{U}^{\mathrm{T}}
.
The result shows that the linear autoencoder implicitly decorrelates latent features of item embeddings through the ZCA whitening process.

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we have shown that linear autoencoder models have ZCA whitening-like effects on recommendation data.
This finding ensures the correctness of applying a linear autoencoder model to low-dimensional item embedding vectors. Our initial experiments also reveal the effectiveness of whitening low-dimensional item embeddings.

In future work, we will try other methods to make item embeddings, such as Item2vec
[
1
]
and GloVe
[
16
]
.

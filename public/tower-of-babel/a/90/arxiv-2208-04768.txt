Abstract
. The Heegaard Floer knot complex C ​ F ​ K ∞ ​ ( K ) \mathit{C\!F\!K}^{\infty}(K) defined by Ozsváth-Szabó and the related complex 𝒞 ​ ℱ ​ 𝒦 ∞ ​ ( K ) \mathcal{C\!F\!K}^{\infty}(K) developed by Zemke have rich algebraic structures. Among the invariants that are derived from these is the knot concordance invariant τ ⁡ ( K ) \tau(K) and the Oszváth-Stipsicz-Szabó Upsilon function Υ K ​ ( t ) \Upsilon_{K}(t) , which has derivative τ ⁡ ( K ) \tau(K) near 0. Recent work by a number of authors has found that torsion of a certain type in the homology of these complexes provides constraints on the number of maxima and minima in cobordisms between knots and, in particular, on the number of minima in slice disks for knots. We let

The basic idea of the defintion of
{\Upsilon^{\sf{Tor}}}_{K}(t)
is straightforward. For any
t\in[0,2]

\mathcal{F}^{t}

\mathcal{C\!F\!K}^{\infty}(K)

{\mathbb{F}}

\mathcal{F}^{t}_{s}

\mathcal{F}^{t}_{s}
is spanned by elements of the from

for which
\frac{t}{2}j+(1-\frac{t}{2})i\geq-s
.
We define the
Upsilon torsion function
,
{\Upsilon^{\sf{Tor}}}_{K}(t)

2\epsilon

\epsilon

\epsilon
such that for all

has image isomorphic to
{\mathbb{F}}[u,v]
. (Intuitively, shifting by
\epsilon

H(\mathcal{F}^{t}_{s})

The basic properties of
{\Upsilon^{\sf{Tor}}}_{K}(t)
are provided by the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

For every knot
K
,

(1)

{\Upsilon^{\sf{Tor}}}_{K}(t)
is a piecewise linear nowhere negative function on
[0,2]
.

(2)

{\Upsilon^{\sf{Tor}}}_{K}(0)=0
and
{\Upsilon^{\sf{Tor}}}_{K}(t)={\Upsilon^{\sf{Tor}}}_{K}(2-t)
.

(3)

{\Upsilon^{\sf{Tor}}}_{K\mathbin{\#}J}(t)=\max\{{\Upsilon^{\sf{Tor}}}_{K}(t),{\Upsilon^{\sf{Tor}}}_{J}(t)\}
.

(4)

{\Upsilon^{\sf{Tor}}}_{-K}(t)={\Upsilon^{\sf{Tor}}}_{K}(t)
.

Formally, the relationship between
{\Upsilon^{\sf{Tor}}}_{K}(t)
and
\text{Ord}_{v}(K)
is given by the next theorem.

Theorem 2

There exists an
\eta>0
such that if
0<\alpha<\eta

\frac{d}{dt}{\Upsilon^{\sf{Tor}}}_{K}(t)\big|_{t=\alpha}={\Upsilon^{\sf{Tor}}}_{K}(\alpha)/\alpha=\text{Ord}_{v}(K)

A key result of

Theorem 3

If
S

K

Note that by turning the concordance upside down, one gets a similar statement for local minima. We have the following immediate corollary.

Corollary 4

If
K
is a slice knot in
S^{3}

K
is greater than
\Upsilon^{\sf Tor}_{K}(\tau)/\tau
for all

2.
A brief review of the Heegaard Floer complexes
\mathcal{C\!F\!K}^{\infty}(K)
and
\mathit{C\!F\!K}^{\infty}(K)

In this section we include only the necessary background; more details can be found in, for example,
[
26
]
. A Heegaard diagram for
S^{3}

determines a set of intersection points
\mathcal{I}
. A base point
z
yields a function, the
absolute grading
,
{\rm{gr}}_{z}\colon\thinspace\mathcal{I}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
. An ordered pair of basepoints
(z,w)
determines a knot
K\subset S^{3}
and a function
A\colon\thinspace\mathcal{I}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
defined by
A({\mathbf{x}})=({\rm{gr}}_{w}({\mathbf{x}})-{\rm{gr}}_{z}({\mathbf{x}}))/2
.

The complex
\mathcal{C\!F\!K}^{-}(K)
is an
{\mathbb{F}}[u,v]
–chain complex, freely generated over
{\mathbb{F}}[u,v]
by the set
\mathcal{I}
. We do not write down the boundary map here. The
{\mathbb{F}}[u,v]
–chain complex
\mathcal{C\!F\!K}^{\infty}(K)
is defined by
\mathcal{C\!F\!K}^{\infty}(K)=\mathcal{C\!F\!K}^{-}(K)\otimes{\mathbb{F}}[u,u^{-1},v,v^{-1}]
. Elements are represented by finite sums of terms of the form
u^{i}v^{j}{\mathbf{x}}
with
i,j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathcal{I}
.

We let
U=uv
and write
\mathcal{C\!F\!K}_{U}^{\infty}(K)
for
\mathcal{C\!F\!K}^{\infty}(K)
viewed as an
{\mathbb{F}}[U]
–chain complex. Then we have the direct sum decomposition

\mathcal{C\!F\!K}_{U}^{\infty}(K)=\oplus_{k}\ \mathcal{C\!F\!K}_{U}^{\infty}(K)_{\{k\}}

where
\mathcal{C\!F\!K}_{U}^{\infty}(K)_{\{k\}}
is the
{\mathbb{F}}[U]
–subcomplex generated by terms
u^{i}v^{j}{\mathbf{x}}
satisfying
A({\mathbf{x}})+j-i=k
.
The variable
v
acts on the collection of these
{\mathbb{F}}[U]
–complexes, giving (ungraded) isomorphisms

v\mathcal{C\!F\!K}_{U}^{\infty}(K)_{\{k\}}=\mathcal{C\!F\!K}_{U}^{\infty}(K)_{\{k+1\}}
.

There is an identification of the Ozsváth-Szabó complex
\mathit{C\!F\!K}^{\infty}(K)
, generated over
{\mathbb{F}}
by terms
[{\mathbf{x}},i,j]
satisfying
A({\mathbf{x}})-j+i=0
, with

\mathcal{C\!F\!K}(K)_{\{0\}}
. The identification is induced by the map
[{\mathbf{x}},i,j]\to u^{-i}v^{-j}{\mathbf{x}}
.

There are two gradings on
\mathcal{C\!F\!K}^{\infty}(K)
determined by the following functions on
{\mathbb{F}}
–generators:
{\rm{gr}}_{z}(u^{i}v^{j}{\mathbf{x}})={\rm{gr}}_{z}({\mathbf{x}})-2j
and
{\rm{gr}}_{w}(u^{i}v^{j}{\mathbf{x}})={\rm{gr}}_{w}({\mathbf{x}})-2i
. These agree on
\mathcal{C\!F\!K}_{\{0\}}(K)
and differ by
k
on
\mathcal{C\!F\!K}_{U}^{\infty}(K)_{\{k\}}
. Corresponding to this, there is a single grading,
{\rm{gr}}
, on
\mathit{C\!F\!K}^{\infty}(K)
given by
{\rm{gr}}([{\mathbf{x}},i,j])={\rm{gr}}_{z}({\mathbf{x}})+2i={\rm{gr}}_{w}({\mathbf{x}})+2j
. The action of
U
on
\mathit{C\!F\!K}^{\infty}(K)
lowers gradings by
2
.

3.
The filtration
\mathcal{F}^{t}_{s}
and Upsilon torsion.

For the moment we restrict to working with rational numbers. For any
t\in[0,2]\cap{\mathbb{Q}}
we define an increasing filtration
{\mathcal{F}}^{t}_{s}
on
\mathcal{C\!F\!K}^{\infty}(K)
parametrized by real numbers
s
, where
{\mathcal{F}}^{t}_{s}
is generated over
{\mathbb{F}}[u,v]
by elements
u^{i}v^{j}{\mathbf{x}}
satisfying
\frac{t}{2}j+\big(1-\frac{t}{2}\big)i\geq-s
.

The intersections
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}^{t}_{s}={\mathcal{F}}^{t}_{s}\cap\mathit{C\!F\!K}^{\infty}(K)
defines an increasing filtration of the
{\mathbb{F}}[U]
–complex
\mathit{C\!F\!K}^{\infty}(K)
:
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}^{t}_{s}
is generated by
[{\mathbf{x}},i,j]
where
\frac{t}{2}j+\big(1-\frac{t}{2}\big)i\leq s
(as well as
A({\mathbf{x}})-j+i=0
). (In
[
15
]
the notation was slightly different, with
\mathcal{F}^{t}_{s}
there being the same as what we denote
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}^{t}_{s}
here.)

We now present the basic definitions. By restricting to rational values of
t
, it should be evident that the use of
minimum
instead of
infimum
is justified. The fact that everything mentioned is well-defined follows most easily from Theorem
11
.

Definition 6
.

For an element
a\neq 0\in\mathcal{C\!F\!K}^{\infty}(K)
, the
filtration level
of
a
is the minimum
s
such that
a\in\mathcal{F}^{t}_{s}
. If
t=\frac{m}{n}
, then the filtration level will be
\frac{r}{2n}
for some
r\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Definition 7
.

For each
t
, an element
a\in\mathcal{F}^{t}_{s}
is called
Upsilon torsion
in
\mathcal{C\!F\!K}^{\infty}(K)
if
[a]\neq 0\in H(\mathcal{F}^{t}_{s})
and
[a]=0\in H(\mathcal{F}^{t}_{s+\epsilon})
for some
\epsilon>0
. Here
H(\mathcal{F}^{t}_{s})
denotes the (unfiltered) homology of the complex.

Definition 8
.

The
Upsilon order
of an Upsilon torsion element
a\in\mathcal{C\!F\!K}^{\infty}(K)
at filtration level
s
is
the minimum
\epsilon
such
that
[a]=0\in H(\mathcal{F}^{t}_{s+\epsilon})
.

Definition 9
.

For each
t\in[0,2]\cap{\mathbb{Q}}
, the
Upsilon torsion function

\Upsilon^{\sf Tor}_{K}(t)
is defined to be
twice
the maximum order among all Upsilon torsion elements. (That the maximum exists is a consequence of Lemma
10
, the proof of which is presented in the appendix.)

Comments.
The map
v\colon\thinspace\mathcal{C\!F\!K}^{\infty}(K)_{\{k\}}\to\mathcal{C\!F\!K}^{\infty}(K)_{\{k+1\}}
preserves the differences between filtration levels. Thus,
{\Upsilon^{\sf{Tor}}}_{K}(t)
could have been defined in terms of the induced filtration on
\mathcal{C\!F\!K}^{\infty}(K)_{0}
. That is, in defining
{\Upsilon^{\sf{Tor}}}_{K}(t)
we could have used
\mathit{C\!F\!K}^{\infty}(K)
and its filtration
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}^{t}_{s}
. However, maps induced by cobordisms do not preserve
\mathit{C\!F\!K}^{\infty}(K)
and this makes the definition using
\mathcal{C\!F\!K}^{\infty}(K)
more practical.

.

Let
U=uv
. There is an
{\mathbb{F}}[U]
–equivariant filtered change of basis that expresses the filtered

–chain complex

3.1.
Viewing
{\Upsilon^{\sf{Tor}}}_{K}(t)
as a function of
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

In Section
8
we will prove that
{\Upsilon^{\sf{Tor}}}_{K}(t)
is piecewise linear. Since we have defined
{\Upsilon^{\sf{Tor}}}_{K}(t)
only in the case that

, that proof applies only for

4.
Torsion order

We would like to interpret
{\Upsilon^{\sf{Tor}}}_{K}(t)
in terms of the action of
v
on
\mathcal{C\!F\!K}^{t}(K)
. Let
t=\frac{m}{n}
. In this case, all filtration levels and Upsilon orders of Upsilon torsion elements are rational with values
\frac{r}{2n}
for some
r
. The action of
v
lowers filtration levels by
\frac{m}{2n}
.

We form the complex
\mathcal{C\!F\!K}^{\infty}(K)\otimes_{{\mathbb{F}}[v]}{\mathbb{F}}[v^{1/m}]
which we abbreviate
\mathcal{C\!F\!K}^{\infty}(K,1/m)
. The
\mathcal{F}^{t}
filtration extends to
\mathcal{C\!F\!K}^{\infty}(K,1/m)
, where now in the formula
\frac{t}{2}j+\big(1-\frac{t}{2}\big)i\geq-s
the values of
i
and
j
can be rational numbers with denominator
m
. We continue to denote this filtration as
{\mathcal{F}^{t}}
. The maximum order of a torsion element is the same as for
\mathcal{C\!F\!K}^{\infty}(K)
, that is,
{\Upsilon^{\sf{Tor}}}_{K}(t)/2
.

We have the subcomplex
\mathcal{C\!F\!K}^{-}(K,1/m)
generated by elements of filtration level nonpositive. This is a
{\mathbb{F}}[v^{1/m}]
–module. Since
v^{1/m}
lowers filtration levels by
(t/2)/m=1/2n
, if a nontrivial torsion element exists, then there is such an element in
\mathcal{C\!F\!K}^{-}(K,1/m)
of filtration level
0
and of Upsilon order
{\Upsilon^{\sf{Tor}}}_{K}(t)/2
.

Theorem 12
.

The
{\mathbb{F}}[v^{1/m}]
-torsion submodule of the homology group
H(\mathcal{C\!F\!K}^{-}(K,1/m))
is annihilated by
v^{M}
if and only if
M\geq{\Upsilon^{\sf{Tor}}}_{K}(t)/t
.

Proof.

Consider a cycle
c\in\mathcal{C\!F\!K}^{-}(K,1/m)
of filtration level 0 and of Upsilon order
{\Upsilon^{\sf{Tor}}}_{K}(t)/2
. Since
v^{1/m}
lowers filtration levels by
1/2n
, to be annihilated by
(v^{1/m})^{k}
we need to have
k(1/2n)\geq{\Upsilon^{\sf{Tor}}}_{K}(t)/2
; that is
k\geq n{\Upsilon^{\sf{Tor}}}_{K}(t)
.

If
v^{M}
annihilates
H(\mathcal{C\!F\!K}^{-}(K,1/m))
, then writing
v^{M}=\big(v^{1/m}\big)^{mM}
we require
mM\geq n{\Upsilon^{\sf{Tor}}}_{K}(t)
. This can be rewritten as
M\geq\frac{n}{m}{\Upsilon^{\sf{Tor}}}_{K}(t)={\Upsilon^{\sf{Tor}}}_{K}(t)/t
.
∎

Definition 13
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{M}
is a
{\mathbb{F}}[x]
–module, that
x^{m}\cdot c=0
for all
{\mathbb{F}}[x]
–torsion elements
c\in\mathcal{M}
, and that
m
is the least exponent for which
x^{m}\cdot c=0
for all such

. Then we will write
\text{Ord}_{{\mathbb{F}}[x]}(\mathcal{M})=m
and refer to it as the
torsion order
of
\mathcal{M}
.

With this, Theorem
12
can be restated as the following.

Corollary 14
.

For
t=m/n
, the torsion order of the homology group
H(\mathcal{C\!F\!K}^{-}(K,1/m))
under the action of
{\mathbb{F}}[v^{1/m}]
is
{\Upsilon^{\sf{Tor}}}_{K}(t)/t
.

4.1.
General torsion results

Suppose that
\mathcal{M}
is a
{\mathbb{F}}[x]
–module. Then clearly

\text{Ord}_{{\mathbb{F}}[x]}(x^{k}\mathcal{M})=\max\{0,\text{Ord}_{{\mathbb{F}}[x]}(\mathcal{M})-k\}.

Thus, for modules
\mathcal{M}_{1}
and
\mathcal{M}_{0}
, if we have

\text{Ord}_{{\mathbb{F}}[x]}(x^{k}\mathcal{M}_{1})=\text{Ord}_{{\mathbb{F}}[x]}(x^{j}\mathcal{M}_{0}),

then

\ \max\{0,\text{Ord}_{{\mathbb{F}}[x]}(\mathcal{M}_{1})-k\}=\max\{0,\text{Ord}_{{\mathbb{F}}[x]}(\mathcal{M}_{0})-j\}.

We will use two special cases.

Theorem 15
.

Let
\text{Ord}_{{\mathbb{F}}[x]}(\mathcal{M}_{1})=\mathcal{O}_{1}
and
\text{Ord}_{{\mathbb{F}}[x]}(\mathcal{M}_{0})=\mathcal{O}_{0}
.

(1)

If
x^{M}\mathcal{M}_{1}=x^{M}\mathcal{M}_{0}
and
\mathcal{O}_{1}\leq\mathcal{O}_{0}
then
M\geq\mathcal{O}_{0}
.

(2)

If
\mathcal{M}_{1}=x^{2g}\mathcal{M}_{0}
, then
\mathcal{O}_{1}\leq\mathcal{O}_{0}
and
2g\geq\mathcal{O}_{0}-\mathcal{O}_{1}
.

5.
Bordism maps

Let

to a knot

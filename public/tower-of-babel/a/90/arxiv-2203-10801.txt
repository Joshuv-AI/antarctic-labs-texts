Towards the standard Majorana representations of 3-transposition groups Journal: Journal of L a T e X Templates
Albert Gevorgyan

Abstract
In this paper, we discuss 3 3 -transposition groups. In particular, we find sizes of maximal symmetric subgroups of the groups, which are in Fischer list. In addition, we build faithful representations of symmetric groups in orthogonal, symplectic, and unitary spaces, which fully verify the main result. Thus, we find candidate groups, for which one may be able to build standard Majorana representations.

Auxiliary results

In this section, we prove several lemmas, which later help us to prove the results of the main part.

Definition 3
.

Let
(G,D)
be a
3
-transposition group. We define a chain of transpositions as a sequence of pairwise distinct transpositions
g_{1},...,g_{m}
, for which
[g_{i},g_{j}]=1
(with
i\neq j
) if and only if
|i-j|\neq 1
. We call
m
the length of the chain.

Recall that, when
G
is an orthogonal, symplectic or unitary group, then the transpositions are of form
t(v)
for some vectors
v
of the corresponding vector space, and the transpositions commute if and only if the corresponding vectors are orthogonal. Thus, while working with such groups, if the transpositions
g_{1}=t(v_{1}),...,g_{m}=t(v_{m})
form a chain, we say that the vectors
v_{1},...,v_{m}
form a chain of length
m
. Note that if
v_{1},...,v_{m}
form a chain, then no pair of vectors may be collinear.

Lemma 1
.

(big) Let
(G,D)
be a
3
-transposition group. Let
m
be the length of the maximal chain of transpositions of
G
. Then,
\varphi(G)=m+1
.

Proof.

First, let us note that if there exists a monomorphism
\psi:S_{n}\rightarrow G
, then the transpositions
d_{1}=\psi(1,2)
,
d_{2}=\psi(2,3)
, …,
d_{n-1}=\psi(n-1,n)
form a chain. Hence,
m\geq\varphi(G)-1
, so
\varphi(G)\leq m+1
.

On the other hand, suppose there exist transpositions
d_{1},...,d_{m}
forming a chain. Then, if we create the non-commuting graph of
\{d_{1},...,d_{m}\}
, it is a Coxeter graph of form
A_{m}
. It is well-known fact that the corresponding Coxeter group is isomorphic to
S_{m+1}
. Thus, there exists a homomorphism
\psi:S_{m+1}\rightarrow<d_{1},...,d_{m}>
with
\psi(i,i+1)=d_{i}
.

Note that
ker(f)\unlhd S_{m+1}
. Moreover, the normal subgroups of
S_{m+1}
are
\{1\}
,
A_{m+1}
and
S_{m+1}
and possibly the Klein
4
-group (if
m+1=4
). In addition, if
ker(f)\neq\{1\}
, then the factor group
S_{m+1}/ker(f)
does not contain
m
involutions. Therefore,
ker(f)
is trivial and
f
is an isomorphism. Thus
\varphi(G)\geq m+1
.
∎

In addition, we use the following classical result, which can be found in
[
1
]
.

Lemma 2
.

(Witt):
Let
V
be an orthogonal, symplectic or unitary space. Let
U
and
W
be subspaces of
V
and suppose that
\alpha:U\rightarrow W
is an isometry of subspaces. Then,
\alpha
extends to an isometry of
V
.

Lemma 3
.

(small):
Let
V
be an orthogonal, symplectic or unitary space. Let the vectors
v_{1},...,v_{n+1}\in V
form a chain. Then the vectors
v_{1},...,v_{n}
are linearly independent.

Proof.

Suppose, for a contradiction, there exists a non-trivial linear combination
c_{1}v_{1}+...+c_{n}v_{n}=0
. Let
m
be the maximal number with
c_{m}\neq 0
. Then,
c_{1}v_{1}+...+c_{m}v_{m}=0
. Therefore,
0=(0,v_{m+1})=(c_{1}v_{1}+...+c_{m}v_{m},v_{m+1})=c_{1}(v_{1},v_{m+1})+...+c_{m}(v_{m},v_{m+1})=c_{m}(v_{m},v_{m+1})\neq 0
, which is a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 4
.

(main):
Let
V
be an orthogonal, symplectic or unitary space. Let
v_{1},...,v_{m}
be linearly independent vectors in
V
, which form a chain. Then,
v_{1},...,v_{m}
can be continued to a maximal chain of
V
.

Proof.

Let
w_{1},...,w_{n}
be a maximal chain of
V
. If
n=m
, then the statement is true. Otherwise,
m\leq n-1
, so by the small lemma the vectors
w_{1},...,w_{m}
are linearly independent. Furthermore, let
w_{1}^{\prime}=w_{1}
. Then, we can substitute
w_{2}
with
w_{2}^{\prime}=c_{2}w_{2}
for some constant
c_{2}\neq 0
so that
(v_{1},v_{2})=(w_{1}^{\prime},w_{2}^{\prime})
. Similarly, we can substitute
w_{3},...,w_{m}
with their scalar multiples so that
(v_{i},v_{i+1})=(w_{i}^{\prime},w_{i+1}^{\prime})
for
i=1,...,m-1
. Let
U=span(v_{1},...,v_{m})
and
W=span(w_{1},...,w_{m})
. Then,
v_{1},...,v_{m}
form a basis for
U
and
w_{1}^{\prime},...,w_{m}^{\prime}
form a basis for
W
. Hence, we can define a linear map
L:W\rightarrow U
with
L(w_{i}^{\prime})=v_{i}
for
i=1,...,m
. Then, by the choice of
w^{\prime}_{1},...,w^{\prime}_{m}
,
L
is an isometry. Therefore, by the Witt’s lemma,
L
can be extended to an isometry
T
of
V
. Denote,
v_{m+1}=T(w_{m+1})
, …,
v_{n}=T(w_{n})
. From the fact that
T
is isometry, one may deduce that
v_{1}=T(w_{1}^{\prime})
, …,
v_{n}=T(w_{n})
is a chain as well.
∎

It is well known that if
V
is a symplectic, unitary or orthogonal space,
v\in V
and

is a constant, then
t(v)=t(cv)
. Therefore, if we have a chain of vectors
v_{1},...,v_{n}
and multiply some of these vectors by non-zero constants, then it will also be a chain.

Main results

Now, after stating and proving important lemmas in the previous sections, we are ready for proving the main results of this paper. We compute the value of

Proposition 1
.

If

, then
\varphi(G)=n

The statement is elementary.

Proposition 2
.

If
G\cong Sp_{2n}(2)
and
n\geq 2
, then
\varphi(G)=2n+2
.

Proof.

Assume that
V
is a symplectic space of dimension
2n
over the
2
-element field. Let
v_{1},...,v_{2n}
be a basis for
V
with
(v_{i},v_{j})=1
if
\{i,j\}\in\{\{1,2\},\{3,4\},...,

\{2n-1,2n\}\}

(v_{i},v_{j})=0

Note that the following vectors are linearly independent and form a chain:

v_{1}+v_{2},v_{2}+v_{3},...,v_{2n-1}+v_{2n},(v_{1}+v_{2}+...+v_{2n-2})+v_{2n}.

Thus, by the main lemma, this may be continued to a maximal chain. Let
c_{1}v_{1}+...+c_{2n}v_{2n}
be the next vector in the chain. Then, it is orthogonal to each of the vectors

v_{1}+v_{2},v_{2}+v_{3},...,v_{2n-1}+v_{2n}.

Therefore,
c_{1}=c_{2}=...=c_{2n}
. Moreover, the vector is non-zero, therefore
c_{1}=1
. Hence, the next vector in this chain is
v_{1}+...+v_{2n}
. Note that this chain cannot be continued anymore, as there are no other non-zero vectors orthogonal to vectors
v_{1}+v_{2},v_{2}+v_{3},...,v_{2n-1}+v_{2n}
. Hence, the maximal chain has length
2n+1
, so by the big lemma,
\varphi(G)=(2n+1)+1=2n+2
.
∎

Proposition 3

Let

\varphi(G)=n+2

Assume that
V

Now, assume that

According to the main lemma, this chain can be continued to a maximal chain. Let
c_{1}v_{1}+...c_{n}v_{n}
be the next vector in this chain. Then, it is orthogonal to the vectors
v_{1}+v_{2},...,v_{n-2}+v_{n-1}
. Therefore,

. Moreover, this is a singular vector, so

5
Geometrical interpretation of the main results

In this section, we discuss the inverse of the problem in the previous section for the classical groups. We build representations of symmetric groups
S_{n}
in the spaces with symplectic, unitary, or orthogonal forms. From the previous part, one may easily deduce, that if
G
is a symplectic, unitary or orthogonal group of dimension
n
, then
n\leq\varphi(G)\leq n+2
. Therefore, we anticipate that
S_{n}
should act on a space of dimension
m
, with
n-2\leq m\leq n
. For that purpose, we build a symplectic, unitary, or orthogonal space
V
with basis
v_{1},...,v_{n}
and allow
S_{n}
to permute the basis. Then, we take the subspace, which is orthogonal to some vector, and, if possible, factorize it over some vector. If needed, for fixing the sign of an orthogonal space, we take a direct sum with a one-dimensional space.

In this section we look for a representation of
S_{n}
with
n\geq 5
. Actually, in this case, the action of
S_{n}
on the corresponding space is faithful, because the kernel of the action is a normal subgroup of
S_{n}
, that is
\{1\},A_{n}
or
S_{n}
. And the image of the representation has more than two elements, so the kernel is the trivial subgroup.

5.1
Symplectic spaces over the 2-element field

Let
n
be an even integer. Suppose that
W
is an
n
-dimensional symplectic space over the
2
-element field. Let
w_{1},...,w_{n}
be a basis for
W
with
(w_{i},w_{j})=1
, if
i\neq j
. Let

. Then,

Abstract
It is shown that generalized trigonometric functions and generalized hyperbolic functions can be transformed from each other. As an application of this transformation, a number of properties for one immediately lead to the corresponding properties for the other. In this way, Mitrinović-Adamović-type inequalities, multiple-angle formulas, and double-angle formulas for both can be produced.



Keywords:

Generalized trigonometric functions,
Generalized hyperbolic functions,
Multiple-angle formulas,
Double-angle formulas,

p
-Laplacian.

1
Introduction

In almost all literature dealing with generalized trigonometric functions and generalized hyperbolic functions, e.g.
[
Dosly2005
,
Klen
,
Neuman2015
,
YHWL2019
]
, both are currently studied independently. In this paper, by expanding the range of parameters contained in these functions beyond normal, we show that a duality between the two can be seen and one can be represented using the other, and we apply the duality to investigate formulas of generalized trigonometric and hyperbolic functions.

Before stating our results, we introduce the definitions and
some properties of generalized trigonometric and hyperbolic functions.

Let
q/(q+1)<p<\infty,\ 1<q<\infty
and

F_{p,q}(y):=\int_{0}^{y}\frac{dt}{(1-t^{q})^{1/p}},\quad y\in[0,1).

We will denote by
\sin_{p,q}
the inverse function of
F_{p,q}
, i.e.,

\sin_{p,q}{x}:=F_{p,q}^{-1}(x).

Clearly,
\sin_{p,q}{x}
is monotone increasing on

[0,\pi_{p,q}/2)
onto
[0,1)
,
where

\pi_{p,q}:=2F_{p,q}(1)=2\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dt}{(1-t^{q})^{1/p}}=\begin{cases}(2/q)B(1/p^{*},1/q),&1<p<\infty,\\
\infty,&q/(q+1)<p\leq 1,\end{cases}

and
p^{*}:=p/(p-1)
and
B
is the beta function.
In almost literature dealing with generalized trigonometric functions,
the parameter
p
is assumed to be
p>1
,
but we here allow it to be
p\leq 1
.
Note that the condition
q/(q+1)<p\leq 1
implies that
\sin_{p,q}{x}
is monotone
increasing on
[0,\infty)
and no longer similar to
\sin{x}
, but to
\tanh{x}
.
Since
\sin_{p,q}{x}\in C^{1}(0,\pi_{p,q}/2)
,
we also define
\cos_{p,q}{x}
by

\cos_{p,q}{x}:=\frac{d}{dx}(\sin_{p,q}{x}).

Then, it follows that

\cos_{p,q}^{p}{x}+\sin_{p,q}^{q}{x}=1.

(1.1)

In case
(p,q)=(2,2)
, it is obvious that
\sin_{p,q}{x},\ \cos_{p,q}{x}

and
\pi_{p,q}
are reduced to the ordinary
\sin{x},\ \cos{x}
and
\pi
,
respectively.
This is a reason why these functions and the constant are called

generalized trigonometric functions
(with parameter
(p,q)
)
and the
generalized
\pi
, respectively.

The generalized trigonometric functions are well studied in the context of
nonlinear differential equations (see
[
Dosly2005
,
Lang2016
,
Kobayashi-Takeuchi
,
Takeuchi2016
]
and the references given there).
Suppose that
u
is a solution of
the initial value problem of
p
-Laplacian

(|u^{\prime}|^{p-2}u^{\prime})^{\prime}+\frac{(p-1)q}{p}|u|^{q-2}u=0,\quad u(0)=0,\ u^{\prime}(0)=1,

(1.2)

which is reduced to the equation
u^{\prime\prime}+u=0
of simple harmonic motion for

u=\sin{x}
in case
(p,q)=(2,2)
.
Then,

\frac{d}{dx}(|u^{\prime}|^{p}+|u|^{q})=\left(\frac{p}{p-1}(|u^{\prime}|^{p-2}u^{\prime})^{\prime}+q|u|^{q-2}u\right)u^{\prime}=0.

Therefore,
|u^{\prime}|^{p}+|u|^{q}=1
, hence it is reasonable to define
u
as
a generalized sine function and
u^{\prime}
as a generalized cosine function.
Indeed, it is possible to show that
u
coincides with
\sin_{p,q}
defined as above.
The generalized trigonometric functions are often applied to
the eigenvalue problem of
p
-Laplacian.

In a similar way,

G_{p,q}(y):=\int_{0}^{y}\frac{dt}{(1+t^{q})^{1/p}},\quad y\in[0,\infty).

We will denote by
\sinh_{p,q}
the inverse function of
G_{p,q}
, i.e.,

\sinh_{p,q}{x}:=G_{p,q}^{-1}(x).

Clearly,
\sinh_{p,q}{x}
is monotone increasing
on
[0,\pi_{r,q}/2)
onto
[0,\infty)
,
where
r
is the constant determined by

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{r}=1+\frac{1}{q},\quad\mbox{i.e.},\quad r=\frac{pq}{pq+p-q}.

(1.3)

Indeed, by
1+t^{q}=1/(1-s^{q})
,

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}G_{p,q}(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{(1+t^{q})^{1/p}}=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{ds}{(1-s^{q})^{1/r}}=\frac{\pi_{r,q}}{2}.

The important point to note here that for a fixed
q\in(1,\infty)
,
if
r=r(p)
is regarded as a function of
p
, then

\displaystyle p>\frac{q}{q+1}\ \Leftrightarrow\ r(p)>\frac{q}{q+1},

(1.4)

\displaystyle r(r(p))=p.

(1.5)

In particular,
\pi_{r,q}
has been defined when
p>q/(q+1)
.
Note that the condition
r>1
, i.e.,
p<q
, implies that
\sinh_{p,q}{x}
is defined in
the
bounded
interval
[0,\pi_{r,q}/2)
with

\lim_{x\to\pi_{r,q}/2}\sinh_{p,q}{x}=\infty
and
no longer similar to
\sinh{x}
, but to
\tan{x}
.
Since
\sinh_{p,q}{x}\in C^{1}(0,\pi_{r,q}/2)
,
we also define
\cosh_{p,q}{x}
by

\cosh_{p,q}{x}:=\frac{d}{dx}(\sinh_{p,q}{x}).

Then, it follows that

\cosh_{p,q}^{p}{x}-\sinh_{p,q}^{q}{x}=1.

(1.6)

In case
(p,q)=(2,2)
, it is obvious that
\sinh_{p,q}{x},\ \cosh_{p,q}{x}

and the interval
[0,\pi_{r,q}/2)
are reduced to

\sinh{x},\ \cosh{x}
and
[0,\infty)
, respectively.
This is a reason why these functions are called

generalized hyperbolic functions
(with parameter
(p,q)
).
Just as
\sin_{p,q}{x}
satisfies (
1.2
),

\sinh_{p,q}{x}
is a solution of
the initial value problem of
p
-Laplacian

(|u^{\prime}|^{p-2}u^{\prime})^{\prime}-\frac{(p-1)q}{p}|u|^{q-2}u=0,\quad u(0)=0,\ u^{\prime}(0)=1.

We are interested in finding a duality between
generalized trigonometric and hyperbolic functions.

Let us start with a brief observation.
Since
F_{1,2}(x)=\tanh^{-1}{x}
, it is easily seen that

\sin_{1,2}{x}=\tanh{x},\quad\cos_{1,2}{x}=\frac{1}{\cosh^{2}{x}}.

This yields that the hyperbolic functions can be written in terms of
the generalized trigonometric functions as

\sinh{x}=\frac{\sin_{1,2}{x}}{\sqrt{\cos_{1,2}{x}}},\quad\cosh{x}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\cos_{1,2}{x}}}.

From this observation, it is expected that any generalized hyperbolic function can be translated in terms of generalized trigonometric functions. In fact,
we can prove the following crucial lemmas that play an important role in this paper.

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
q/(q+1)<p<\infty,\ 1<q<\infty
and
r
be the number defined in (
1.3
).
Then, for
x\in[0,\pi_{r,q}/2)
,

\sinh_{p,q}{x}=\frac{\sin_{r,q}{x}}{\cos_{r,q}^{r/q}{x}},\quad\cosh_{p,q}{x}=\frac{1}{\cos_{r,q}^{r/p}{x}}.

Lemma 1.2
.

Let
q/(q+1)<p<\infty,\ 1<q<\infty
and
r
be the number defined in (
1.3
).
Then, for
x\in[0,\pi_{p,q}/2)
,

\sin_{p,q}{x}=\frac{\sinh_{r,q}{x}}{\cosh_{r,q}^{r/q}{x}},\quad\cos_{p,q}{x}=\frac{1}{\cosh_{r,q}^{r/p}{x}}.

Lemmas
1.1
and
1.2
tell us the counterparts to generalized hyperbolic functions of the properties already known for generalized trigonometric functions, and vice versa.
For example, Lemma
1.2
immediately converts
(
1.1
) into (
1.6
) (with
p
replaced by
r
), that is,

\cos_{p,q}^{p}{x}+\sin_{p,q}^{q}{x}=1

into

\cosh_{r,q}^{r}{x}-\sinh_{r,q}^{q}{x}=1;

and Lemma
1.1
(with (
1.5
)) does vice versa.

Using this idea with our lemmas, we will show the following generalization
of Mitrinović-Adamović inequality.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
q/(q+1)<p<\infty
and
1<q<\infty
. Then,
for
x\in(0,\pi_{p,q}/2)
,

\cos_{p,q}^{1/(q+1)}{x}<\frac{\sin_{p,q}{x}}{x}<1.

(1.7)

Moreover, for
x\in(0,\pi_{r,q}/2)
,
where
r
is the number defined in (
1.3
),

\cosh_{p,q}^{1/(q+1)}{x}<\frac{\sinh_{p,q}{x}}{x}<\cosh_{p,q}^{p/q}{x}.

(1.8)

Inequalities (
1.7
) and (
1.8
) were proved by Klén
et al.
[
Klen
, Theorems 3.6 and 3.8]

when
p=q>1
; and Neuman
[
Neuman2015
, Theorem 6.1 and (6.9)]

when
p,\ q>1
(with better upper bounds).
In our approach,
Theorem
1.3
allows us to obtain the same inequalities
over the wider range of parameters
q/(q+1)<p<\infty
and
1<q<\infty
,
and (
1.8
) immediately follows from (
1.7
)
by Lemma
1.2
.

As a further application of our lemmas, the following theorem is
obtained by deriving the identities (
1.11
) and (
1.12
) below
of the generalized hyperbolic
functions corresponding to the multiple-angle formulas
(
1.9
) and (
1.10
) of the
generalized trigonometric functions in
[
Takeuchi2016
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
1<q<\infty
. Then, for
x\in[0,\pi_{2,q}/2^{2/q+1})=[0,\pi_{q^{*},q}/4)
,

\displaystyle\sin_{2,q}{(2^{2/q}x)}=2^{2/q}\sin_{q^{*},q}{x}\cos_{q^{*},q}^{q^{*}-1}{x},

(1.9)

\displaystyle\cos_{2,q}{(2^{2/q}x)}=\cos_{q^{*},q}^{q^{*}}{x}-\sin_{q^{*},q}^{q}{x}.

(1.10)

Moreover, for the same
x
,

\displaystyle\sinh_{2q/(q+2),q}{(2^{2/q}x)}=\frac{2^{2/q}\sinh_{q/2,q}{x}}{(1-\sinh_{q/2,q}^{q}{x})^{2/q}},

(1.11)

\displaystyle\cosh_{2q/(q+2),q}{(2^{2/q}x)}=\left(\frac{1+\sinh_{q/2,q}^{q}{x}}{1-\sinh_{q/2,q}^{q}{x}}\right)^{2/q+1}.

(1.12)

Remark 1.5
.

In case of
q=2
, they are reduced to the double-angle formula
of
\sin,\ \cos,\ \tan
and
\sec^{2}
.

The multiple-angle formulas of the same type as (
1.9
) and (
1.10
) for generalized hyperbolic functions have not been found
so far. In parallel with Theorem
1.4
, we
establish them and furthermore deduce the corresponding formulas
of the same type as (
1.11
) and (
1.12
)
for generalized trigonometric identities.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
1<q<\infty
. Then, for
x\in[0,\pi_{2q/(q+2),q}/2^{2/q+1})=[0,\pi_{q/2,q}/2)
,

\displaystyle\sinh_{2,q}{(2^{2/q}x)}=2^{2/q}\sinh_{q^{*},q}{x}\cosh_{q^{*},q}^{q^{*}-1}{x},

\displaystyle\cosh_{2,q}{(2^{2/q}x)}=\cosh_{q^{*},q}^{q^{*}}{x}+\sinh_{q^{*},q}^{q}{x}.

Moreover, for the same
x
,

\displaystyle\sin_{2q/(q+2),q}{(2^{2/q}x)}=\frac{2^{2/q}\sin_{q/2,q}{x}}{(1+\sin_{q/2,q}^{q}{x})^{2/q}},

\displaystyle\cos_{2q/(q+2),q}{(2^{2/q}x)}=\left(\frac{1-\sin_{q/2,q}^{q}{x}}{1+\sin_{q/2,q}^{q}{x}}\right)^{2/q+1}.

Remark 1.7
.

In case of
q=2
, they are reduced to the double-angle formula
of
\sinh,\ \cosh,\ \tanh
and
\operatorname{sech}^{2}
.

Proofs of Results

In this section, we will prove the lemmas and theorems introduced in Section 1.
Properties (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) are used frequently.

Proof of Lemma
1.1
.

Let
p>q/(q+1)
.
Then,
r>q/(q+1)
by (
1.4
) and
the integration

\sin_{r,q}^{-1}{y}=\int_{0}^{y}\frac{dt}{(1-t^{q})^{1/r}},\quad y\in[0,1),

with
1-t^{q}=1/(1+s^{q})
gives

\displaystyle\sin_{r,q}^{-1}{y}=\int_{0}^{y/(1-y^{q})^{1/q}}\frac{ds}{(1+s^{q})^{1/p}}=\sinh_{p,q}^{-1}{\left(\frac{y}{(1-y^{q})^{1/q}}\right)}.

Hence, we obtain

\sinh_{p,q}{x}=\frac{\sin_{r,q}{x}}{\cos_{r,q}^{r/q}{x}},\quad x\in\left[0,\frac{\pi_{r,q}}{2}\right).

Also, by (
1.6
),

\cosh_{p,q}{x}=\frac{1}{\cos_{r,q}^{r/p}{x}},

and the proof is complete.
∎

Proof of Lemma
1.2
.

Let
p>q/(q+1)
.
Then,
r>q/(q+1)
by (
1.4
).
Lemma
1.1
with
p
replaced by
r
and (
1.5
) show

\sinh_{r,q}{x}=\frac{\sin_{p,q}{x}}{\cos_{p,q}^{p/q}{x}},\quad\cosh_{r,q}{x}=\frac{1}{\cos_{p,q}^{p/r}{x}},\quad x\in\left[0,\frac{\pi_{p,q}}{2}\right).

Therefore,

\sin_{p,q}{x}=\frac{\sinh_{r,q}{x}}{\cosh_{r,q}^{r/q}{x}},\quad\cos_{p,q}{x}=\frac{1}{\cosh_{r,q}^{r/p}{x}},

and the proof is complete.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

We prove (
1.7
).
Since
\sin_{p,q}{x}<x
in
(0,\pi_{p,q}/2)
, the second inequality holds;
hence we show the first inequality.

Let
f(x):=x-\sin_{p,q}{x}\cos_{p,q}^{-1/(q+1)}{x}
. Then,

f^{\prime}(x)=1-\cos_{p,q}^{q/(q+1)}{x}-\frac{q}{p(q+1)}\sin_{p,q}^{q}{x}\cos_{p,q}^{q/(q+1)-p}{x},

f^{\prime\prime}(x)=-\frac{q^{2}(pq+p-q)}{p^{2}(q+1)^{2}}\sin_{p,q}^{2q-1}{x}\cos_{p,q}^{(2q+1)/(q+1)-2p}{x}.

Since
pq+p-q>0
, we see
f^{\prime\prime}(x)<0
.
Therefore,
f^{\prime}(x)<\lim_{x\to+0}f^{\prime}(x)=0
.
Moreover,
f(x)<\lim_{x\to+0}f(x)=0
, which means (
1.7
).

Next we show (
1.8
).
Let
p>q/(q+1)
.
Then,
r>q/(q+1)
by (
1.4
).
From (
1.7
) with
p
replaced by
r
,

\cos_{r,q}^{1/(q+1)}{x}<\frac{\sin_{r,q}{x}}{x}<1.

By Lemma
1.2
with
p
replaced by
r
and (
1.5
),

\left(\frac{1}{\cosh_{p,q}^{p/r}{x}}\right)^{1/(q+1)}<\frac{\sinh_{p,q}{x}}{x\cosh_{p,q}^{p/q}{x}}<1.

Multiplying both sides by
\cosh_{p,q}^{p/q}{x}
, we conclude (
1.8
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

The former half is the multiple-angle formulas of generalized trigonometric

[
Takeuchi2016
]
.

1.1
with

\sinh_{2q/(q+2),q}{(2^{2/q}x)}=\frac{\sin_{2,q}{(2^{2/q}x)}}{\cos_{2,q}^{2/q}{(2^{2/q}x)}}=\frac{2^{2/q}\sin_{q^{*},q}{x}\cos_{q^{*},q}^{q^{*}-1}{x}}{(\cos_{q^{*},q}^{q^{*}}{x}-\sin_{q^{*},q}^{q}{x})^{2/q}}.

with

shows

\frac{2^{2/q}\sinh_{q/2,q}{x}}{(1-\sinh_{q/2,q}^{q}{x})^{2/q}}.

The formula of
\cosh_{2q/(q+2),q}
immediately follows from
(
1.6
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

The former half is shown as follows.
Let
y\in[0,\infty)
. Setting
t^{q}=((1+s^{q})^{1/2}-1)/2
in

\sinh_{q^{*},q}^{-1}{y}=\int_{0}^{y}\frac{dt}{(1+t^{q})^{1/q^{*}}},

we have

\displaystyle\sinh_{q^{*},q}^{-1}{y}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{y(4(1+y^{q}))^{1/q}}\dfrac{\dfrac{2^{-1-1/q}s^{q-1}}{(1+s^{q})^{1/2}((1+s^{q})^{1/2}-1)^{1-1/q}}\,ds}{2^{-1+1/q}((1+s^{q})^{1/2}+1)^{1-1/q}}

\displaystyle=2^{-2/q}\int_{0}^{y(4(1+y^{q}))^{1/q}}\frac{ds}{(1+s^{q})^{1/2}};

that is,

\sinh_{q^{*},q}^{-1}{y}=2^{-2/q}\sinh_{2,q}^{-1}{(y(4(1+y^{q}))^{1/q})}.

(2.1)

Hence we obtain

\sinh_{2,q}{(2^{2/q}x)}=2^{2/q}\sinh_{q^{*},q}{x}\cosh_{q^{*},q}^{q^{*}-1}{x}.

In particular, letting
y\to\infty
in (
2.1
) and using
r(q^{*})=q/2,\ r(2)=2q/(q+2)
,
we get

\frac{\pi_{q/2,q}}{2}=\frac{\pi_{2q/(q+2),q}}{2^{1+2/q}}.

The formula of
\cosh_{q^{*},q}
immediately follows from
(
1.6
).

The latter half is proved as follows.
By Lemma
1.2
with
r(2q/(q+2))=2
and the former half,

\sin_{2q/(q+2),q}{(2^{2/q}x)}=\frac{\sinh_{2,q}{(2^{2/q}x)}}{\cosh_{2,q}^{2/q}{(2^{2/q}x)}}=\frac{2^{2/q}\sinh_{q^{*},q}{x}\cosh_{q^{*},q}^{q^{*}-1}{x}}{(\cosh_{q^{*},q}^{q^{*}}{x}+\sinh_{q^{*},q}^{q}{x})^{2/q}}.

Lemma
1.1
with
r(q^{*})=q/2
shows
that the right-hand side becomes

\frac{2^{2/q}\sin_{q/2,q}{x}}{(1+\sin_{q/2,q}^{q}{x})^{2/q}}.

The formula of
\cos_{2q/(q+2),q}
immediately follows from
(
1.1
).
∎

Lemma 3.1
.

For
x\in[0,\pi_{3/2,6}/4)

\sinh_{2,6}(2x)=\frac{2\sinh_{2,6}x\cosh_{2,6}x}{\sqrt{1-8\sinh_{2,6}^{6}x}},

(3.1)

\cosh_{2,6}(2x)=\frac{1+20\sinh_{2,6}^{6}x-8\sinh_{2,6}^{12}x}{(1-8\sinh_{2,6}^{6}x)^{3/2}}.

Proof.

The change of variable
s=t^{2}

\sinh_{2,6}^{-1}y=\int^{y}_{0}\frac{dt}{\sqrt{1+t^{6}}},\quad y\in[0,\infty),

leads to the representation

\sinh_{2,6}^{-1}y=\frac{1}{2}\int^{y^{2}}_{0}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{s(1+s^{3})}}.

The furthermore change of variable

\operatorname{cn}u=\frac{1-(\sqrt{3}-1)s}{1+(\sqrt{3}+1)s},\quad k^{2}=\frac{2+\sqrt{3}}{4},

gives

\displaystyle\sinh_{2,6}^{-1}y

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int^{\operatorname{cn}^{-1}\phi(y)}_{0}\frac{((\sqrt{3}+1)\operatorname{cn}u+(\sqrt{3}-1))^{2}}{2\cdot 3^{3/4}\operatorname{sn}u\operatorname{dn}u}\frac{2\sqrt{3}\operatorname{sn}u\operatorname{dn}u}{((\sqrt{3}+1)\operatorname{cn}u+(\sqrt{3}-1))^{2}}du

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\cdot 3^{1/4}}\operatorname{cn}^{-1}\phi(x),

where
\operatorname{sn}{u}=\operatorname{sn}{(u,k)},\ \operatorname{cn}{u}=\operatorname{cn}{(u,k)}
and
\operatorname{dn}{u}=\operatorname{dn}{(u,k)}
are the
Jacobian elliptic functions, and

\phi(y)=\frac{1-(\sqrt{3}-1)y^{2}}{1+(\sqrt{3}+1)y^{2}}.

Thus,

\sinh_{2,6}x=\phi^{-1}(\operatorname{cn}(2\cdot 3^{1/4}x)),\quad x\in\left[0,\frac{\pi_{3/2,6}}{2}\right),

and

\phi^{-1}(x)=\sqrt{\frac{1-x}{(\sqrt{3}-1)+(\sqrt{3}+1)x}}.

For
0\leq x<\pi_{3/2,6}/4
,

\displaystyle\sinh_{2,6}(2x)=

\displaystyle\phi^{-1}(\operatorname{cn}(2\tilde{x}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi^{-1}\left(\frac{\operatorname{cn}^{2}\tilde{x}-\operatorname{sn}^{2}\tilde{x}\operatorname{dn}^{2}\tilde{x}}{1-k^{2}\operatorname{sn}^{4}\tilde{x}}\right)

where
\tilde{x}:=2\cdot 3^{1/3}x
. Recall that
\operatorname{sn}^{2}z=1-\operatorname{cn}^{2}z,\ \operatorname{dn}^{2}z=1-k^{2}\operatorname{sn}^{2}{z}
; then the last equality gives

\displaystyle\sinh_{2,6}(2x)=\phi^{-1}\left(\frac{\phi(X)^{2}-(1-\phi(X)^{2})(1-k^{2}(1-\phi(X)^{2})}{1-k^{2}(1-\phi(X)^{2})^{2}}\right),

where
X:=\sinh_{2,6}x
.
With the observation

\displaystyle 1-\phi(X)^{2}=\frac{4\sqrt{3}X^{2}(1+X^{2})}{(1+(\sqrt{3}+1)X^{2})^{2}},

\displaystyle 1-k^{2}(1-\phi(X)^{2})=\frac{1-X^{2}+X^{4}}{(1+(\sqrt{3}+1)X^{2})^{2}},

\displaystyle 1-k^{2}(1-\phi(X)^{2})^{2}=\frac{1+4(\sqrt{3}+1)X^{2}-8X^{6}+4(\sqrt{3}+1)X^{8}}{(1+(\sqrt{3}+1)X^{2})^{4}},

implies that

\displaystyle\sinh_{2,6}(2x)=

\displaystyle\phi^{-1}\left(\frac{\phi(X)^{2}-(1-\phi(X)^{2})(1-k^{2}(1-\phi(X)^{2})}{1-k^{2}(1-\phi(X)^{2})^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi^{-1}\left(\frac{1-4(\sqrt{3}-1)X^{2}-8X^{6}-4(\sqrt{3}-1)X^{8}}{1+4(\sqrt{3}+1)X^{2}-8X^{6}+4(\sqrt{3}+1)X^{8}}\right).

Routine simplification now results in the formula

\sinh_{2,6}(2x)=\frac{2X\sqrt{1+X^{6}}}{\sqrt{1-8X^{6}}}=\frac{2\sinh_{2,6}x\cosh_{2,6}x}{\sqrt{1-8\sinh_{2,6}^{6}x}}.

The formula of
\cosh_{2,6}
immediately follows from
(
1.6
).
∎

4
Generalization of Tangent Function

Let
x\in[0,\pi_{p,q}/2)
for simplicity.
Generalized tangent function is usually defined as

\tan_{p,q}{x}:=\frac{\sin_{p,q}{x}}{\cos_{p,q}{x}}.

Now, we propose another generalization of the tangent function:

\tau_{p,q}(x)=\frac{\sin_{p,q}{x}}{\cos_{p,q}^{p/q}{x}},

which coincides with
\tan_{p,q}{x}
if
p=q
.
Then,
\tau_{p,q}(x)
has properties very similar to
\tan{x}
more than
\tan_{p,q}{x}
:

\displaystyle 1+\tau_{p,q}^{q}(x)=\frac{1}{\cos_{p,q}^{p}{x}},

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}(\tau_{p,q}(x))=\frac{1}{\cos_{p,q}^{p/q+p-1}{x}}=(1+\tau_{p,q}^{q}(x))^{1/q+1-1/p},

\displaystyle\tau_{2,q}(2^{2/q}x)=\frac{2^{2/q}\tau_{q^{*},q}(x)}{(1-\tau_{q^{*},q}^{q}(x))^{2/q}}.

Moreover, Lemma
1.1
gives

\sinh_{p,q}{x}=\tau_{r,q}(x),\quad\cosh_{p,q}{x}=(1+\tau_{r,q}^{q}(x))^{1/p},

(4.1)

where
r=pq/(pq+p-q)
.
In particular, Lemma
1.2
and (
4.1
) with (
1.5
)
yield

\tan_{p,q}{x}=\tau_{p,q}(x)(1+\tau_{p,q}^{q}(x))^{1/p-1/q}.

Let us apply this function to integration.
Setting
\tau_{q^{*},q}(x/2^{2/q})=t
, we have

\displaystyle\sin_{2,q}{x}=\frac{2^{2/q}\sin_{q^{*},q}{(x/2^{2/q})}\cos_{q^{*},q}^{q^{*}-1}{(x/2^{2/q})}}{(\cos_{q^{*},q}^{q^{*}}{(x/2^{2/q})}+\sin_{q^{*},q}^{q}{(x/2^{2/q}))^{2/q}}}=\frac{2^{2/q}t}{(1+t^{q})^{2/q}},

\displaystyle\cos_{2,q}{x}=\frac{\cos_{q^{*},q}^{q^{*}}{(x/2^{2/q})}-\sin_{q^{*},q}^{q}{(x/2^{2/q})}}{\cos_{q^{*},q}^{q^{*}}{(x/2^{2/q})}+\sin_{q^{*},q}^{q}{(x/2^{2/q})}}=\frac{1-t^{q}}{1+t^{q}},

\displaystyle\tan_{2,q}{x}=\frac{\sin_{2,q}{x}}{\cos_{2,q}{x}}=\frac{2^{2/q}t}{(1+t^{q})^{2/q-1}(1-t^{q})},

\displaystyle\tau_{2,q}{(x)}=\frac{2^{2/q}\tau_{q^{*},q}{(x/2^{2/p})}}{(1-\tau_{q^{*},q}{(x/2^{2/q})})^{2/q}}=\frac{2^{2/q}t}{(1-t^{q})^{2/q}},

\displaystyle\frac{dt}{dx}=\frac{1}{2^{2/q}\cos_{q^{*},q}^{2/(q-1)}{(x/2^{2/p})}}=\frac{(1+t^{q})^{2/q}}{2^{2/q}}.

For example,

\displaystyle\int\frac{dx}{\sin_{2,q}{x}}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{dt}{t}=\log{|t|}+C=\log{|\tau_{q^{*},q}{(2^{2/q}x)}|}+C,

\displaystyle\int\frac{dx}{\cos_{2,q}^{2/q}{x}}

\displaystyle=2^{2/q}\int\frac{dt}{(1-t^{q})^{2/q}}=2^{2/q}\sin_{q/2,q}^{-1}{t}+C

\displaystyle=2^{2/q}\sin_{q/2,q}^{-1}{(\tau_{q^{*},q}{(2^{2/q}x)})}+C.

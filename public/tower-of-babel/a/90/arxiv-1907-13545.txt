Abstract
We present possible extensions of the quantum statistical mechanical formulation of class field theory to the non-abelian case, based on the action of the absolute Galois group on Grothendieck’s dessins d’enfant, the embedding in the Grothendieck–Teichmüller group, and the Drinfeld–Ihara involution.

1
Introduction

1.1
Abelian Galois theory via quantum statistical mechanics

The Bost–Connes system
[
11
]
provides the original model for
the recasting of explicit class field theory problems in the setting of classical or
quantum statistical mechanics.

Basically, it starts with classical Mellin transforms
of Dirichlet series of various arithmetic origins, and represents them as
calculations of statistical averages of certain observables
as functions of inverse temperature
1/kT
(classical systems)
or imaginary time
it
(quantum systems).
Here is a brief description of such systems relevant in the contexts of abelian extensions
of number fields
(see
[
11
]
and
[
21
, Chapter 3]
).

One constructs the arithmetic noncommutative algebra
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}}={\mathbb{Q}}[{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}]\rtimes{\mathbb{N}}
given by
a semigroup crossed product. One then passes to the associated
C^{*}
-algebra of observables with
the time evolution
({\mathcal{A}},\sigma_{t})
. The algebra is
{\mathcal{A}}=C^{*}({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})\rtimes{\mathbb{N}}=C\big(\hat{\mathbb{Z}}\big)\rtimes{\mathbb{N}}
. The time evolution acts trivially on the abelian subalgebra,
\sigma_{t}|_{C^{*}({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})}={\rm Id}
, and nontrivially on the semigroup
{\mathbb{N}}
:
\sigma_{t}(\mu_{n})=n^{{\rm i}t}\mu_{n}
, where
\mu_{n}
are the isometries in
{\mathcal{A}}
corresponding to
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. The group
G=\hat{\mathbb{Z}}^{*}
acts as symmetries of the quantum statistical mechanical system
({\mathcal{A}},\sigma_{t})
. There are covariant representations
\pi\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
on the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}=\ell^{2}({\mathbb{N}})
, with
{\rm e}^{{\rm i}tH}\pi(a){\rm e}^{-{\rm i}tH}=\pi(\sigma_{t}(a))
, for
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
and
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
, with Hamiltonian
H
, for which the partition function
Z(\beta)={\rm Tr}\big({\rm e}^{-\beta H}\big)=\zeta(\beta)
is the Riemann zeta function. The extremal KMS equilibrium states
\varphi\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\infty}
at zero temperature, when evaluated on elements of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
, take values in the maximal abelian extension
{\mathbb{Q}}^{ab}
(generated by roots of unity) and intertwine the symmetries and the Galois action,
{\rm\theta}(\gamma)\varphi(a)=\varphi(\gamma a)
, for
\gamma\in\hat{\mathbb{Z}}^{*}={\rm GL}_{1}\big(\hat{\mathbb{Z}}\big)
and
\varphi\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\infty}
, with
\theta\colon\hat{\mathbb{Z}}^{*}\to{\rm Gal}\big({\mathbb{Q}}^{ab}/{\mathbb{Q}}\big)
the class field theory isomorphism.

An interested reader will find much more details and basic references in
[
21
, Chapter 3, Section 2]
.

This construction of the Bost–Connes system was then generalized in
[
22
,
36
,
46
,
63
]
, to the case of abelian extensions of arbitrary number fields. Given a number field
{\mathbb{K}}
, there is an associated quantum statistical mechanical system
{\rm BC}_{\mathbb{K}}
with the properties that its partition function is the Dedekind zeta function of
{\mathbb{K}}
, and its symmetry group is the Galois group of the maximal abelian extension
{\rm Gal}\big({\mathbb{K}}^{ab}/{\mathbb{K}}\big)
. Moreover, there is an arithmetic subalgebra
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{K}}
with the property that evaluations of zero-temperature KMS states on elements of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{K}}
take values in
{\mathbb{K}}^{ab}
, and
intertwine the action of
{\rm Gal}({\mathbb{K}}^{ab}/{\mathbb{K}})
by symmetries of the quantum statistical
mechanical system with the Galois action on
{\mathbb{K}}^{ab}
.

Later, it was shown in
[
24
,
25
]
that the quantum statistical mechanical system
{\rm BC}_{\mathbb{K}}
completely determines the number field
{\mathbb{K}}
. Other generalizations of the Bost–Connes system were developed for the abelian varieties related to 2-lattices and the Shimura variety of
{\rm GL}_{2}

(see
[
20
]
), and for more general Shimura varieties in
[
36
]
and abelian varieties in
[
62
]
. See also
[
21
, Chapter 3]
for an overview of these arithmetic quantum statistical mechanical models.

1.2
Non-abelian Galois theory via quantum statistical mechanics

A natural further generalization of this program is a development of quantum statistical mechanical models for the action of the non-abelian absolute Galois group
{\rm Gal}\big(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Q}}\big)
instead of its abelianization
{\rm Gal}\big(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Q}}\big)^{ab}={\rm Gal}\big({\mathbb{Q}}^{ab}/{\mathbb{Q}}\big)
as in the original Bost–Connes system. This development is the main focus of the present paper. We will
approach the problem from the point of view of Belyi’s theorem and Grothendieck’s theory of

dessins d’enfant
, see
[
33
]
and further articles in the same collection. See also the
recent work
[
8
,
32
]
.

More concretely, whereas
{\rm Gal}\big(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Q}}\big)^{ab}
can be presented in terms of the profinite
completion of the fundamental group of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\setminus\{0,\infty\}
, according to Grothedieck’s prophetic vision,
{\rm Gal}\big(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Q}}\big)
might be similarly presented in terms the profinite completion of the fundamental group (or rather groupoid) of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}
. A decisive step in this direction was made by G. Belyi
[
6
]
. The semigroup
{\mathbb{N}}
of the classical Bost–Connes system is replaced here by the semigroup of Belyi-extending maps of
[
61
]
.

The relation to the profinite fundamental groups mentioned above can be
seen more explicitly in the following way. The abelianization
{\rm Gal}\big(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Q}}\big)^{ab}
is isomorphic by the class field theory isomorphism to
\hat{\mathbb{Z}}^{*}
and acts by
automorphisms on the profinite completion
\hat{\mathbb{Z}}
of the fundamental group
of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\setminus\{0,\infty\}
. On the other hand, the absolute Galois
group
{\rm Gal}\big(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Q}}\big)
has a faithful action by automorphisms on
the profinite completion
\hat{F}_{2}
of the fundamental group of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}
, as in of
[
41
, Proposition 1.6]
and Theorem 1 in the Appendix to the same paper. This action is given
on a set of generators
x
,
y
of
\hat{F}_{2}
by
x\mapsto x^{\chi(\gamma)}
and
y\mapsto f_{\gamma}^{-1}(x,y)y^{\chi(\gamma)}f_{\gamma}(x,y)
, with
\chi\colon{\rm Gal}\big(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Q}}\big)\to\hat{\mathbb{Z}}^{*}
the cyclotomic character and
f_{\gamma}(x,y)
are elements in the commutator subgroup of
\hat{F}_{2}

[
41
, Proposition 1.5]
. These two actions are clearly compatible through the cyclotomic character, since the action
\alpha\in\hat{\mathbb{Z}}^{*}
on the profinite fundamental group of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\setminus\{0,\infty\}
is just given by
x\mapsto x^{\alpha}
.

Belyi’s theorem, Grothendieck’s program, and a construction of the respective versions of Bost–Connes system will be described in the Section
2
of this paper. This section, however, is mainly concerned with the combinatorics and enumeration of dessins d’enfant, rather than Galois action upon the fundamental groupoid of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}
. However, see Section
2.5
and the following discussions, making explicit the fact that not all our constructions are purely combinatorial ones that avoid an explicit use of Galois action upon dessins.

Our main motivation here is the fact that various Bost–Connes systems
are practically equivalent to the relevant enumerations of dessins d’enfant, and their
behaviour formally is determined by asymptotic properties of the relevant enumerations and
their generating functions.

The broad idea behind our generalization of the Bost–Connes system from the
abelianized to the absolute Galois group can be summarized as obtained by replacing
the Hopf algebra
{\mathbb{Q}}[{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}]
on roots of unity by a suitable Hopf algebra on dessins,
and the action of the power maps separating different orbits of roots of unity by the
action of a semigroup of Belyi-extending maps. However, expecting that the resulting
quantum statistical mechanical system obtained in this way will suffice to
separate the different Galois orbits of dessins is probably too strong an expectation,
at least in its present form. We will show that one can see some of the new Galois
invariants of dessins introduced in
[
61
]
occurring in the low temperature KMS
states of the system, but some technical restrictions on the choice of the Belyi-extending
maps, which will be specified in Section
2
, limit the effectiveness of
these invariants at separating Galois orbits. It is possible that
using constructions from
[
8
,
32
]
, one may be able to obtain
a more refined version of the quantum statistical mechanical system presented here
that bypasses this limitation.

The Hopf algebra formalism of dessins we discuss in Section
2
makes it possible,
in principle, to obtain new Galois invariants of dessins from known ones, by applying
the formalism of Birkhoff factorization, so that the new invariants depend not only
on a dessin and its sub-dessins, but also on associated quotient dessins. We illustrate
this principle in Proposition
2.16

in the case of Tutte and Bollobás–Riordan–Tutte polynomials.

Section
3
is dedicated to the absolute Galois action and its (partial) descriptions.
Such descriptions are centred around various versions of the
Grothendieck–Teichmüller group
.
We start with its formal definition in the Section
3.1
, where this group
appears together with its action upon the Bost–Connes algebra and upon the relevant
quantum statistical mechanical system. In this environment, the Bost–Connes statistical mechanics
becomes much more sensitive to the arithmetics of the absolute Galois group, through its
relation to the Grothendieck–Teichmüller group, rather than through its action on dessins
as in the choices surveyed in Section
2
.

The last Section
3.10
presents, following
[
16
]
, one of the avatars
{\bf mGT}
of the
Grothendieck–Teichmüller group as the symmetry group of the genus zero modular operad introduced in
[
45
]
and much studied afterwards.

Section
4
discusses the relation between the algebras of our quantum statistical mechanical systems and Drinfeld’s quasi-triangular quasi-Hopf algebras
[
30
,
31
]
. In particular, we show that both the Bost–Connes algebra and our Hopf algebra of dessins d’enfant have an associated direct system of quasi-triangular quasi-Hopf algebras obtained using Drinfeld’s twisted quantum double construction
[
29
]
.

2
Dessins and dynamics

Below we will use (with certain laxity) the language of graphs as it was described in
[
10
]
.

A finite graph
\tau
is a quadruple of data
(V_{\tau},F_{\tau},\delta_{\tau},j_{\tau})
of finite sets
V_{\tau}
of
vertices
and
F_{\tau}
of
flags
, a map
\partial_{\tau}\colon F_{\tau}\to V_{\tau}
, and an involution
j_{\tau}\colon F_{\tau}\to F_{\tau}
:
j_{\tau}^{2}={\rm id}
. For each vertex
v\in V_{\tau}
, the graph with flags
\partial_{\tau}^{-1}(v)
and trivial involution
j
is called
the corolla
of
v
in
\tau
. If a graph has just one vertex, it is called a corolla itself.

A
geometric realisation
of graph
\tau
is a topological space of the following structure.
If
\tau
is a non-empty corolla with vertex
v
, then its geometric realisation is the disjoint union
of segments whose components bijectively correspond to elements of
F_{\tau}

modulo identification of all points
0
, which becomes the geometric
realisation of
v
. Generally, consider the disjoint union of geometric realisations of corollas
of all vertices of
\tau
, and then identify the endpoints of two different flags,
if these two flags are connected by the involution
j_{\tau}
. Sometimes one considers the endpoint removed in the case of geometric realisations of all flags that are stationary points of the involution.

The pairs of flags, connected by involution, resp. their geometric realisations, are called
edges
of
\tau
, resp. their geometric realisations.

In order not to mix free flags with halves of edges, one may call a free flag
a leaf
, or
a tail
as in
[
45
]
.

The description of graphs in terms of flags with an involution is common especially in contexts originating from physics where corollas represent possible interactions and one expects graph to have both internal edges, namely pairs of different flags glued by the involution, and external edges (leaves or tails), namely flags that are fixed points of the involution.

Among various types of labelings or markings of the sets
V_{\tau}
,
F_{\tau}
forming a graph,
an important role is played by
orientations of flags.
If two flags form an edge, their orientations
must agree. We often use oriented trees, in which one free flag is chosen as a
root
,
and all other leaves are oriented in such a way, that from each leaf there exists a unique
oriented path to the root.

Below we will often pass in inverse direction: from the geometric realization of a graph
to its set-theoretic description. It should not present any difficulties for the reader.

2.1
Belyi maps and dessins d’enfant

Let
\Sigma
be a smooth compact Riemann surface. We recall the following definition
(see
[
6
,
33
]
).

Definition 2.1
.

(i)

A Belyi map
f\colon\Sigma\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{1}({\mathbb{C}})
is a holomorphic map that is unramified outside of the set
\{0,1,\infty\}
.

(ii)

Dessin d’enfant
D=D_{f}
of
f
is the preimage
f^{-1}([0,1])
embedded in
\Sigma
and
considered as a marked graph in the following way.

Its set of vertices
V(D)
is the union
f^{-1}(0)\cup f^{-1}(1)
; each point in the preimage
V_{1}(D)=f^{-1}(1)
, resp.
V_{0}(D)=f^{-1}(0)
, is marked white, resp. black. Such graphs are called bipartite ones.

Its set of (open) edges
E(D)
consists of connected components of
f^{-1}(0,1)
of the preimage of the unit interval
{\mathcal{I}}
.

Each
\Sigma
is the Riemann surface of a smooth complex algebraic curve.
The role of Belyi maps in this context is determined by Belyi’s converse theorem
[
6
]
:
each smooth complex algebraic curve defined over subfield of algebraic numbers admits
a Belyi map.

Grothendieck’s intuition behind introduction of dessins of such maps was the hope,
that action of the absolute Galois group upon the category of such algebraic curves
would admit an explicit description after the transfer of this action to dessins.

More precisely, in terms of branched coverings and Belyi maps
f\colon\Sigma\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, we can view the Galois action of
G={\rm Gal}\big(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Q}}\big)
in the following way. In the connected case, consider the finite extension
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(\Sigma)
of the function field
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(t)
. The field
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(\Sigma)
is given by
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(t)[z]/(P)
where
P\in\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(t)[z]
is an irreducible polynomial. An element
\gamma\in G
maps
P\mapsto P_{\gamma}
where
P_{\gamma}
is the polynomial obtained by action of
\gamma
on the coefficients of
P
(extending the action of
\gamma
to
{\mathbb{Q}}(t)
by the identity on
t
). Thus the action of
G
maps the extension
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(t)[z]/(P)
to
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(\Sigma_{\gamma}):=\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(t)[z]/(P_{\gamma})
. The action extends to the case where
\Sigma
is not necessarily connected, by decomposing the étale algebra of the covering into a direct sum of field extensions as above. Correspondingly, the element
\gamma\in G
maps the dessin
D
determined by the branched covering
f\colon\Sigma\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, or equivalently by the extension
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(\Sigma)
to the dessin
\gamma D
determined by
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(\Sigma_{\gamma})
.

Therefore we must first trace how the combinatorics of dessins encodes the geometry of their Belyi maps.

2.2
Geometry of Belyi maps vs combinatorics of dessins

(a)

The number of edges
d=\#E(D)
equals the degree of
f\colon\Sigma\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}({\mathbb{C}})

(b)

The numbers of black and white
vertices
m=\#V_{0}(D)
and
n=\#V_{1}(D)
correspond to the orders of ramification of
f
at
0
and
1
.

(c)

The numbers
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{m}
and
\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{n}
of edges in the corollas around the
black, resp. white vertices, give the ramification profiles of
f
at
0
and
1
. According to the
degree sum formula for bipartite graphs,
\sum_{i}\mu_{i}=d=\sum_{j}\nu_{j}
.

(d)

In order to keep track of additional data of topology of embedding
D=D_{f}
into
\Sigma
we must endow
D

with cyclic ordering of flags at each corolla coming from the canonical orientation of
\Sigma
.
In other words,
D
must be considered as a bipartite
ribbon
graphs.

(e)

The graph
D
is connected iff the covering is connected, or equivalently iff the étale algebra of the covering is a field. The
degree
d
is the dimension of the étale algebra of the covering as a vector space over
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(t)
,
in the connected case the degree of the field extension. The group of symmetries of a connected
dessin is the automorphism group of the extension, which is a finite group of order at most equal to
the degree
d
.

(f)

Finally, for each bipartite ribbon graph
D
one can construct a Belyi map

f
such that
D=D_{f}
.

In order to see that, one can first construct a map of topological surfaces
\Sigma^{\prime}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}({\mathbb{C}})

with desired properties, by working locally in a suitable covering of
\Sigma^{\prime}
,
and then endow
\Sigma^{\prime}
with the complex structure lifted from
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}({\mathbb{C}})
.

2.3
Regular and clean dessins

For studying action of the Galois group, two subclasses of dessins d’enfant are especially interesting.

(a)

Regular dessins.
A dessin is called regular one, if it is connected and if its symmetry
group is as large as possible: its cardinality equals to the degree
d
.

Regular dessins correspond to Galois field extensions. Geometrically, they correspond to regular branched
coverings where the deck group acts transitively on any fiber. Every connected dessin admits a
regular closure, which corresponds to the smallest extension that is Galois.

(b)

Clean dessins
. A dessin is called clean, if all its white vertices have valence two.

The clean dessins can be re-encoded to ribbon graphs that are not bipartite, by colouring all its vertices
black and then inserting valence two white vertices in the middle of each edge. This was the class
of dessins originally considered by Grothendieck. In terms of branched coverings of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}({\mathbb{C}})
and
Belyi maps, the clean dessins correspond to coverings whose ramification profile over the point
1

is only of type
(2,2,\ldots,2,1,\ldots,1)
, that is, they have either simple ramification or no ramification.

Any dessin, not necessarily connected, whose all connected components are clean,
will be called
locally clean
.

2.4
Hopf algebras of dessins

(a)

Locally clean dessins.
We consider first the class
{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{D}}
of locally clean dessins re-encoded as in Section
2.3
(b). Isomorphism classes of such graphs can be used in order to construct a Hopf algebra, which will serve as a generalization to ribbon graphs
[
52
]
of the Connes–Kreimer Hopf algebra of renormalization in perturbative quantum field theory
[
19
]
.

We start by constructing a Hopf algebra
{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{D}}}
of locally clean dessins.
As an algebra
{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{D}}}
is the free commutative
{\mathbb{Q}}
-algebra freely
generated by isomorphism classes of clean dessins. Locally clean dessins
are identified with the monomials given by the products of their connected
components.
More generally, if
D
is a clean design considered as ribbon graph on the black vertices
and
\delta\subset D
is its proper subgraph, we can construct the quotient
D/\delta
.

Combining these two constructions, we can construct the map

\displaystyle\Delta\colon\ {\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{D}}}\to{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{D}}}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{D}}},\qquad\Delta(D):=D\otimes 1+1\otimes D+\sum_{\delta\subset D}\delta\otimes D/\delta.

An equivalent description of the coproduct is given in
[
52
]
in terms of surfaces.
Let
\Sigma
denote the Riemann surface determined by the dessin
D
. Consider open not
necessarily connected subsurfaces (Riemann surfaces with boundary)
\sigma\subset\Sigma
.
To each such subsurface
\sigma
one can associate a quotient
\Sigma/\sigma
and a closure
\bar{\sigma}
.
The quotient is the closed surface obtained by replacing
\sigma
in
\Sigma
with a sphere with
the same number of holes glued back into
\Sigma
in place of
\sigma
. The closure
\bar{\sigma}
is
obtained by adding boundary edges so that every sphere with holes in
\bar{\sigma}
has the same
number of boundary edges as it would have in
\Sigma
. The coproduct is then equivalently
written in terms of surfaces as
[
52
]

\displaystyle\Delta(\Sigma)=\Sigma\otimes 1+1\otimes\Sigma+\sum_{\sigma\subset\Sigma}\bar{\sigma}\otimes\Sigma/\sigma.

This coproduct is shown in
[
52
]
to be coassociative.

The number of edges (which is equal to the degree of the Belyi map) is
additive,
\#E(D)=\#E(\delta)+\#E(D/\delta)
. One can consider the Hopf algebra as graded by
this degree. The antipode on a connected graded Hopf algebra is defined inductively by the formula

S(X)=-X+\sum S(X^{\prime})X^{\prime\prime}
for
\Delta(X)=X\otimes 1+1\otimes X+\sum X^{\prime}\otimes X^{\prime\prime}
with terms

X^{\prime}
and
X^{\prime\prime}
of lower degree.

(b)

General dessins.
This construction can be extended from clean dessins to all dessins in the following way.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
D
be a connected dessin. A
(
possibly non-connected
)
sub-dessin
\delta

consists of a
(
possibly non-connected
)
subgraph of
D
which is bipartite.

We consider
\delta
as endowed with internal and external edges: the internal edges
are the edges between vertices of the subgraph and the external edges
(
flags
)
are half-edges
for each edge in
D
with one end on the subgraph
\delta
and the other on some
vertex in
D\smallsetminus\delta
. We endow
\delta
with a ribbon structure where
the corolla of each vertex
(
including both internal and external edges
)
has a cyclic
orientation induced from the ribbon structure of
D
.

In the following, with a slight abuse of notation, we will use the notation
\delta\subset D

for the sub-dessin with or without the external edges included, depending on context. In
each case the graph
\delta
is bipartite and with a ribbon structure induced by
D
.
We describe how to obtain a quotient dessin in this more general case, and we then
discuss the corresponding possible choices of grading of the resulting Hopf algebra.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
D
be a connected dessin and
\delta\subset D
a sub-dessin as in Definition
2.2
. There is a quotient bipartite graph
D/\delta
, obtained by shrinking each component of
\delta
to a bipartite graph with one white and one black vertex and a single edge, for which any choice of a cyclic ordering of the boundary components of a tubular neighborhood of
\delta
in
\Sigma

determines a ribbon structure, making
D/\delta
a dessin.

Proof.

We need to check that the graph
D/\delta
is indeed still a dessin, namely that
it is bipartite and has a ribbon structure, and that these are compatible with those of
the initial dessin
D
and of the subdessin
\delta
.

The dessin structure on the
quotient
D/\delta
is obtained by the following procedure.
First label all the external half edges of
\delta
with black/white labels according to whether
they are attached to a black or white vertex of
\delta
.

Forgetting temporarily the bipartite structure, consider then the quotient graph, which we denote by
D//\delta
,
obtained by shrinking each connected component of the subgraph
\delta
to a single vertex,
but with the edges of
D//\delta
out of these quotient vertices retaining the black/white labels coming
from the bipartite structure.

Consider a small tubular neighborhood of the subgraph
\delta
on the surface
\Sigma

where
D
is embedded and consider its boundary components: endow the corollas of the
quotient vertices in
D//\delta
with a cyclic structure obtained by listing the half edges in the order
in which they are met while circling around each of the boundary components (in an assigned
cyclic order) in the direction induced by the orientation of
\Sigma
.

Because of the black/white labels of the external edges of
\delta
, the cyclic ordering of half-edges
around each quotient vertex in
D//\delta
can be seen as a shuffle of two cyclically ordered sets
of black and white labelled half-edges: separate out each quotient vertex into a black and a white vertex
connected by one edge, with the cyclically ordered black half-edges attached to the black vertex
and the cyclically ordered white half-edges attached to the white vertex.
The resulting graph
D/\delta
obtained in this way is bipartite and has a ribbon structure,
hence it defines a quotient dessin. The ribbon structure defined in this way depends on the
assignment of a cyclic ordering to the boundary components of a tubular neighborhood
of
\delta
in
\Sigma
.
∎

We can again reformulate the construction of the quotient dessins
D/\delta
in terms of
the surfaces
\sigma
and
\Sigma/\sigma
as above
[

]
.
Although the formulation we gave above in terms of graphs is more explicit, an advantage of
the reformulation in terms of surfaces we discuss below is that it explains
the definition of the ribbon structure on
D/\delta
used in Lemma

in a more natural way as follows.

Corollary 2.4
.

The ribbon structure on
D/\delta
can be equivalently described by
considering
D/\delta
embedded in the surface
\Sigma/\sigma
, for
\sigma

the sub-surface with boundary
\sigma\subset\Sigma
containing
\delta
,
with the external edges of
\delta
cutting through the components
of
\partial\sigma
. Each cyclic orientation of the components of
\partial\sigma

determines a ribbon structure on
D/\delta
.

Proof.

An open subsurface
\sigma
of
\Sigma
containing
\delta
,
with the property that the external edges of
\delta
pass through
the boundary curves
\partial\sigma
is equivalent, up to homotopy, to
a tubular neighborhood of
\delta
in
\Sigma
with its boundary curves.
We can assume for simplicity that
\delta
, hence
\sigma
, are connected.
Thus, a choice of a cyclic ordering of the boundary curves of such a
tubular neighborhood is equivalent to the choice of a cyclic ordering
of the components of
\partial\sigma
. The quotient bipartite graph
D/\delta

is contained in the surface
\Sigma/\sigma
obtained by gluing a sphere
with the same number of punctures to the boundary

\partial(\Sigma\smallsetminus\sigma)=\partial\sigma
, with the bipartite graph
with one black and one white vertex and the same external edges
of
\delta
contained in this sphere, with the same external edges
cutting through the same boundary components as in the case
of
\delta
in
\sigma
. If
\delta
has multiple connected components,
and
\sigma
has correspondingly multiple components, then the same

∎

As the result, we obtain another commutative connected Hopf algebra
{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{D}}}
with involution,
with a grading that is again expressible in terms of the number of edges (the degree of the
respective Belyi map), as follows.

Lemma 2.5
.

Any of the functions defined on connected dessins as follows defines a good grading
of the Hopf algebra
{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{D}}}
,

\displaystyle\deg(D)=\begin{cases}b_{1}(D)&\text{or}\\
\#E(D)-1&\text{or}\\
\#V(D)-2.\end{cases}

(2.1)

Proof.

The degree defined with any of the options in (
2.1
) for connected dessins
extends additively to monomials in
{\mathcal{H}}_{D}
(multi-connected dessins) so that one
obtains, respectively,
b_{1}(D)
,
\#E(D)-b_{0}(D)
, and

\#V(D)-2b_{0}(D)
.
With the construction of the quotient dessin
D/\delta
of a subdessin
\delta\subset D

as in Lemma
2.3
, we have
\#V(D)=\#V(\delta)+\#V(D/\delta)-2b_{0}(\delta)

and
\#E(D)=\#E(\delta)+\#E(D/\delta)-b_{0}(\delta)
, hence
b_{1}(D)=1-\#V(D)+\#E(D)=1-\#V(\delta)-\#V(D/\delta)+2b_{0}(\delta)+\#E(\delta)+\#E(D/\delta)-b_{0}(\delta)=b_{0}(\delta)+\#E(\delta)-\#V(\delta)+1+\#E(D/\delta)-\#V(D/\delta)=b_{1}(\delta)+b_{1}(D/\delta)
.
Thus, in all three cases of (
2.1
) we obtain

\deg(D)=\deg(\delta)+\deg(D/\delta)
.
∎

The gradings of Lemma

are the analogs for dessins of the
choices of gradings for the original Connes–Kreimer Hopf algebra of graphs
by either loops
b_{1}(\Gamma)
, or internal edges
\#E(\Gamma)
or by

\#V(\Gamma)-1
(for connected graphs, extending to

for monomials). Here all the choices of grading in Lemma

have a direct
interpretation in terms of Belyi maps, since

is the degree of the Belyi

is the sum of the orders of ramification at

and

.

The coassociativity of the coproduct (

) is the only property that needs to
be verified to ensure that indeed we obtain a Hopf algebra, as all the other properties are
clearly verified. The argument for the coassociativity is similar to the case of the Conner–Kreimer
Hopf algebra, with some modifications due to the different definition of the quotient graph
that we used in Lemma
2.3
. We present the explicit argument for the reader’s
convenience.

Proposition 2.6
.

As an algebra
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}}
is the commutative polynomial
{\mathbb{Q}}
-algebra in the connected dessins.
The coproduct on
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}}
is given by

\displaystyle\Delta(D)=D\otimes 1+1\otimes D+\sum_{\delta\subset D}\delta\otimes D/\delta,

(2.2)

where now the sum is over the sub-dessins defined as in Definition
2.2
and also
over all the possible ribbon structures on the corresponding quotients, defined as in
Lemma
2.3
. Then
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}}
is a graded connected Hopf algebra, with the grading by
\#E(D)-b_{0}(D)
, where
\#E(D)
is the number of edges
(
the degree of the Belyi map
)
.

Proof.

We just need to show that
\Delta
is coassociative. The antipode is then constructed inductively as before. It suffices to check the identity
(\Delta\otimes{\rm id})\Delta=({\rm id}\otimes\Delta)\Delta
on a single connected dessin
D
. We have
(\Delta\otimes{\rm id})\Delta(D)=\sum\limits_{\delta\subseteq D}\Delta(\delta)\otimes D/\delta
. If
\delta
is multiconnected,
\Delta(\delta)
is the product of
\Delta(\delta_{j})

over the connected components
\delta_{j}
, since
\Delta
is an algebra homomorphism. We have

\Delta(\delta)=\sum\limits_{\delta^{\prime}\subseteq\delta}\delta^{\prime}\otimes\delta/\delta^{\prime}
, where in the case where
\delta
has multiple connected components
the subdessin
\delta^{\prime}
of
\delta
consists of a collection
of a subdessin of each component. Similarly, in the quotient dessin
\delta/\delta^{\prime}
we can perform the
quotient operation of Lemma
2.3
separately on the components of
\delta
, so that the
resulting
\delta/\delta^{\prime}
is a product of the quotients over each component. The bipartite and ribbon
structure of
\delta^{\prime}
are the same with respect to
\delta
as they are with respect to
D
, since the
set of external edges of
\delta^{\prime}
in
\delta
are either external lines in
\delta
or internal edges of
\delta
that define external lines in
\delta^{\prime}
and in each case these would also occur as external
lines of
\delta^{\prime}
in
D
. Thus we have
(\Delta\otimes{\rm id})\Delta(D)=\sum\limits_{\delta^{\prime}\subseteq\delta\subseteq D}\delta^{\prime}\otimes\delta/\delta^{\prime}\otimes D/\delta
. We also have
({\rm id}\otimes\Delta)\Delta(D)=\sum\limits_{\delta^{\prime}\subseteq D}\delta^{\prime}\otimes\Delta(D/\delta^{\prime})
. As subgraphs and quotient graphs we have
\Delta(D/\delta^{\prime})=\sum\limits_{\delta^{\prime}\subseteq\delta\subseteq D}\delta/\delta^{\prime}\otimes D/\delta
(see
[
21
, Theorem 1.27]
). Thus, we need to check that this identification is also compatible with the bipartite and the ribbon structures. The identification above depends on identifying subgraphs
\tilde{\delta}\subseteq D/\delta^{\prime}

with subgraphs
\delta\subseteq D
with
\delta^{\prime}\subseteq\delta
. Given such a
\delta
with

\delta^{\prime}\subseteq\delta\subseteq D
one obtains
\tilde{\delta}
from
\delta
with the quotient procedure
described in Lemma
2.3
, which determines the bipartite and ribbon structure on
\tilde{\delta}

and every
\tilde{\delta}\subseteq D/\delta^{\prime}
is obtained in this way from a unique
\delta\subseteq D
with

\delta^{\prime}\subseteq\delta
, with
\tilde{\delta}=\delta/\delta^{\prime}
.
∎

Each of the Hopf algebras
{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{D}}}
or
{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{D}}}
determines the respective
affine group scheme
{\mathcal{G}}={\mathcal{G}}_{{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{D}}}
or
{\mathcal{G}}_{{\mathcal{D}}}
whose
\bar{{\mathbb{Q}}}
-points
are morphisms of
{\mathbb{Q}}
-algebras
{\mathcal{H}}\to\bar{{\mathbb{Q}}}

and multiplication is defined by
\phi_{1}*\phi_{2}(X)=(\phi_{1}\otimes\phi_{2})\Delta(X)
,
with inverse
\phi^{-1}=\phi\circ S
given by composition with the antipode.

2.5
Hopf algebra and Galois action

We want to make the construction described above of the Hopf algebra
{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{D}}}
of dessins
compatible with the Galois action. This would mean requiring that, for all
\gamma\in{\rm Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Q}})
we have
\Delta(\gamma D)=\sum\gamma\delta\otimes\gamma D/\delta
. The condition is satisfied
if we have an identification of the class of images
\gamma\delta
of subdessins
\delta\subset D

with the class of subdessins of
\gamma D
and an identification of the images of the quotient
dessins
\gamma D/\delta
with the quotients
\gamma D/\gamma\delta
.
When we consider in the construction of the coproduct
\Delta(D)

of
{\mathcal{H}}_{D}
all the possible choices of subdessins
\delta
and quotient dessins
D/\delta
, defined
as in Lemma
2.6
, we are also including pairs
\{\delta,D/\delta\}
that are not necessarily
compatible with the Belyi map and the Galois action on the Belyi maps, for which the required identifications need not hold. We can see this by considering
the following example, based on of
[
47
, Example 2.2.15]
.

Lemma 2.7
.

The dessins
A
and
B
shown in the figure are in the same Galois orbit. The subdessins
\delta_{A}
and
\delta_{B}
circled in the figure are also in the same Galois orbit
(
which in this case consists of a single element
)
. However, the quotients
A/\delta_{A}
and
B/\delta_{B}
do
not belong to the same Galois orbit.

Proof.

The circled subgraph
\delta_{B}
of
B
is the only subdessin in the same Galois orbit
of the subgraph
\delta_{A}
of
A
since the action of the Galois group is trivial on trees with
less than five edges. The quotient dessins
A/\delta_{A}
and
B/\delta_{B}
, however,
are not in the same Galois orbit since they have different vertex degrees (a Galois invariant).
∎

This problem can be corrected by only considering, in the
construction of the coproduct
\Delta(D)
of the Hopf algebra,
certain pairs
\{\delta,D/\delta\}
of subdessins and quotient
dessins that behave well with respect to the Galois orbit.

Definition 2.8
.

A balanced pair
\{\delta,D/\delta\}
for a dessin
D
is a pair of subdessin
and quotient dessin with the property that for
\gamma\in{\rm Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Q}})

the pair
\{\gamma\delta,\gamma D/\delta\}
is a pair of a subdessin and
a quotient dessin for
\gamma D
. A subdessin
\delta\subset D
is strongly
balanced if for all subdessins
\delta^{\prime}\subseteq\delta
the pair
\{\delta^{\prime},D/\delta^{\prime}\}

is balanced. Let
{\mathcal{B}}(D)
denote the set of strongly balanced subsessins.

Example 2.9
.

The subdessins circled in the figure are an example of strongly balanced subdessins. Both have quotient dessin the bipartite line with three white and three black vertices.
All their subdessins consist of either a single edge with a white and a black vertex, or
two edges with a common black vertex and two white vertices. In the first case the
quotient dessin is just
A
or
B
itself and in the second case the quotients are given
by two tree dessins on six edges that are in the same Galois orbit.

Note that the balanced property depends on the entire Galois orbit of
D
not just on
D
itself,
hence the set
{\mathcal{B}}(D)
of strongly balanced pairs is associated to all dessins
D
in the same orbit.
The coproduct of the Hopf algebra
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}}
is then modified by summing in (
2.2
)
only over the strongly balanced subdessins
\delta
, with each appearing once in the coproduct,

\displaystyle\Delta(D)=D\otimes 1+1\otimes D+\sum_{\delta\in{\mathcal{B}}(D)}\delta\otimes D/\delta.

(2.3)

In the following we just write for simplicity of notation
\Delta(D)=\sum\limits_{{\mathcal{B}}(D)}\delta\otimes D/\delta
with the primitive part
D\otimes 1+1\otimes D
implicitly included in the sum.

Lemma 2.10
.

The restriction (
2.3
) of the coproduct to the strongly balanced subdessins maintains the coassociativity property.

Proof.

We have
(\Delta\otimes{\rm id})\Delta(D)=\sum\limits_{\delta\in{\mathcal{B}}(D)}\Delta(\delta)\otimes D/\delta

and
\Delta(\delta)=\sum\limits_{\delta^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{B}}(\delta)}\delta^{\prime}\otimes\delta/\delta^{\prime}
. Because of the strongly
balanced conditions
{\mathcal{B}}(D)
and
{\mathcal{B}}(\delta)
, for
\delta^{\prime}\subset\delta
we have both
\gamma D/\delta^{\prime}=\gamma D/\gamma\delta^{\prime}
and
\gamma\delta/\delta^{\prime}=\gamma\delta/\gamma\delta^{\prime}
. Thus, the pair

(\delta/\delta^{\prime},D/\delta^{\prime})
with
D/\delta^{\prime}=(D/\delta)/(\delta/\delta^{\prime})
is a balanced pair for
D/\delta
, and in fact strongly balanced, since any subdessin of
\delta/\delta^{\prime}
can be identified with a
\delta/\delta^{\prime\prime}
for some subdessin
\delta^{\prime\prime}\subset\delta
. Thus, the rest of the argument of Proposition
2.6
continues to hold.
∎

We still denote by
{\mathcal{G}}
the affine group scheme dual to the Hopf algebra
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}}
endowed with the coproduct (
2.3
).

Proposition 2.11
.

The action of the absolute Galois group
G={\rm Gal}\big(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Q}}\big)
passes through the automorphism group scheme
{\rm Aut}({\mathcal{G}})
of the affine group scheme
{\mathcal{G}}
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that the
G
-action on dessins induces an action on
{\mathcal{H}}
by
bialgebra homomorphisms. The compatibility with the multiplication is clear since on
non-connected dessins the action of
G
is defined componentwise, so
\gamma(D_{1}\cdot D_{2})=\gamma D_{1}\cdot\gamma D_{2}
. The compatibility with comultiplication
\Delta(\gamma D)=\sum\limits_{\delta\subseteq D}\gamma\delta\otimes\gamma D/\delta
requires the identification of
the class of images
\gamma\delta
of subdessins
\delta\subset D
with the class of subdessins
of
\gamma D
and the identification of the images of the quotient dessins
\gamma D/\delta
with
the quotients
\gamma D/\gamma\delta
. This is ensured by the balanced condition on the
pairs
\{\delta,D/\delta\}
introduced in the coproduct (
2.3
).
Thus, elements
\gamma\in G
act as bialgebra homomorphisms on
{\mathcal{H}}
. The compatibility

S\circ\gamma=\gamma\circ S
with the antipode
S
then follows for a general bialgebra homomorphism,
hence they are automorphisms of the Hopf algebra
{\mathcal{H}}
and dually of the affine group scheme
{\mathcal{G}}
.
∎

2.6
Rota–Baxter algebras and Birkhoff factorization

Let
{\mathcal{R}}
be a commutative algebra.
A structure of Rota–Baxter algebra of weight
-1
on it is given by a linear operator
T\colon{\mathcal{R}}\to{\mathcal{R}}
satisfying the Rota–Baxter relation of weight
-1
:

\displaystyle T(xy)+T(x)T(y)=T(xT(y))+T(T(x)y).

(2.4)

The ring of Laurent series with
T
given by the projection onto the polar part is the prototype
example of a Rota–Baxter algebra of weight
-1
. The Rota–Baxter relation (
2.4
)
ensures that the range
{\mathcal{R}}_{+}=(1-T){\mathcal{R}}
is a subalgebra of
{\mathcal{R}}
, not just a linear subspace,
and
{\mathcal{R}}_{-}=T{\mathcal{R}}
as well.

Consider now
{\mathcal{R}}
-valued characters of, say,
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}}
.

The Birkhoff factorization
\phi=(\phi_{-}\circ S)\star\phi_{+}
of a
character
\phi\in{\rm Hom}_{{\rm Alg}}({\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}},{\mathcal{R}})
is defined inductively by the formula

\displaystyle\phi_{-}(x)=-T(\phi(x)+\sum\phi_{-}(x^{\prime})\phi(x^{\prime\prime})),

\displaystyle\phi_{+}(x)=(1-T)(\phi(x)+\sum\phi_{-}(x^{\prime})\phi(x^{\prime\prime})),

where
\Delta(x)=\sum x^{\prime}\otimes x^{\prime\prime}
in Sweedler notation.
The factors
\phi_{\pm}
are algebra homomorphisms

\phi_{\pm}\in{\rm Hom}_{{\rm Alg}}({\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}},{\mathcal{R}}_{\pm})
.

In the case where the Rota–Baxter algebra is an algebra of Laurent power
series with the operator
T
given by projection onto the polar part, the
evaluation at
0
of the positive part
\phi_{+}
of the Birkhoff factorization
has the effect of killing the polar part, hence it achieves renormalization,
see
[
19
]
. Such a systematic deletion of “divergences” (at least, formal ones) is used
in the Connes–Kreimer algebraic theory of renormalization in quantum field theory
[
19
]
. For a general introduction to Rota–Baxter algebras and their properties see
[
35
]
. In our context,
formal sums over dessins d’enfant with weights defining partition functions
are only formal series because “there are too many” such dessins, so that
divergences should be deleted by renormalization. This will be achieved through
a deformation of our quantum statistical mechanical system, which we introduce
in Section
2.14
, rather than through a Birkhoff factorization procedure.
Birkhoff factorizations, however, have other interesting applications besides the elimination
of divergences in quantum field theory. As shown in
[
56
]
in the
context of computation, Birkhoff factorization can be used to enrich invariants of a graph by
compatibly combining invariants of subgraphs and quotient graphs.
The reason why we are considering them here is similar to the applications
considered in
[
56
]
, namely as a method for constructing new Galois
invariants of dessins, as we show in the following.

Lemma 2.12
.

The action
\phi\mapsto\gamma\phi
of
G={\rm Gal}\big(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Q}}\big)
on the group-scheme
{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{D}}
is compatible with the Birkhoff factorization, in the sense that
(\gamma\phi)_{\pm}=\gamma\cdot\phi_{\pm}
.

Proof.

The action of
G
on
{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{D}}
is determined by the action of
G
on
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}}
by
Hopf algebra homomorphisms. We have
\Delta(\gamma x)=\sum\gamma x^{\prime}\otimes\gamma x^{\prime\prime}
.
Thus we obtain
\phi_{\pm}(\gamma x)
as either
-T
or
1-T
applied to
\phi(\gamma x)+\sum\phi_{-}(\gamma x^{\prime})\phi(\gamma x^{\prime\prime})
. This consistently defines
\gamma\phi_{\pm}(x)
as
\phi_{\pm}(\gamma x)
compatibly with the action of
G
on
{\rm Hom}({\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}},{\mathcal{R}}_{\pm})
.

Lemma 2.13
.

The action of the Galois group
G={\rm Gal}\big(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Q}}\big)
by Hopf algebra homomorphisms of
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}}
determines a Hopf ideal and a quotient Hopf algebra
{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{D}},G}
.

Proof.

Consider the ideal
{\mathcal{I}}_{{\mathcal{D}},G}
of the algebra
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}}
generated by elements of the form

D-\gamma D
for
D\in{\mathcal{D}}
and
\gamma\in G
. To see that
{\mathcal{I}}_{{\mathcal{D}},G}
is a Hopf ideal notice
that we have
\Delta(D)-\Delta(\gamma D)=\sum\delta^{\prime}\otimes\delta^{\prime\prime}-\sum\gamma\delta^{\prime}\otimes\gamma\delta^{\prime\prime}=\sum(\delta^{\prime}-\gamma\delta^{\prime})\otimes\delta^{\prime\prime}+\sum\gamma\delta^{\prime}\otimes(\delta^{\prime\prime}-\gamma\delta^{\prime\prime})
hence
\Delta({\mathcal{I}}_{{\mathcal{D}},G})\subset{\mathcal{I}}_{{\mathcal{D}},G}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}}+{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}}\otimes{\mathcal{I}}_{{\mathcal{D}},G}
. Since
{\mathcal{I}}_{{\mathcal{D}},G}
is a Hopf ideal, it defines a quotient Hopf algebra
{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{D}},G}={\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}}/{\mathcal{I}}_{{\mathcal{D}},G}
.
∎

Characters in
{\rm Hom}_{{\rm Alg}}({\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{D}},G},{\mathcal{R}})
with
{\mathcal{R}}
a commutative algebra over
{\mathbb{Q}}

are
{\mathcal{R}}
-valued Galois invariants of dessins that satisfy the multiplicative property

\phi(D\cdot D^{\prime})=\phi(D)\phi(D^{\prime})
over connected components. The following is then an immediate
consequence of the previous statements.

Proposition 2.14
.

Given a Galois invariant of dessins
\phi\in{\rm Hom}_{{\rm Alg}}({\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{D}},G},{\mathcal{R}})
, where
({\mathcal{R}},T)
is a Rota–Baxter algebra of weight
-1
, the Birkhoff factorization
\phi=(\phi_{-}\circ S)\star\phi_{+}
determines new Galois invariants
\phi_{\pm}\in{\rm Hom}_{{\rm Alg}}({\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{D}},G},{\mathcal{R}}_{\pm})
.

One should regard the Galois invariants
\phi_{\pm}
constructed in this way as a refinement
of the Galois invariant
\phi
in the sense that they do not depend only on the value of
\phi

on
D
, but also inductively on the values on all the subdessins
\delta
and the quotient
dessins
D/\delta
, hence for example they can potentially distinguish between non-Galois
conjugate dessins with the same value of
\phi
, but for which the invariant
\phi
differs
on subdessins or quotient dessins.

2.7
New Galois invariants of dessins from Birkhoff factorization

We discuss here a simple explicit example, to show how the formalism
of Rota–Baxter algebras and Bikrhoff factorizations recalled in the
previous subsection can be used as a method to construct new
Galois invariants of dessins.

We start by considering a known invariant of dessins with values in a
polynomial algebra. This is given by the
2
-variable Tutte polynomial,
as well as its
3
-variable generalization, the Bollobás–Riordan–Tutte polynomial
of
[
9
]
defined for ribbon graphs. Both can be applied to dessins
D

and we will show in Lemma
2.15
that both are Galois invariants of dessins.
We also consider some possible specializations of these polynomials,
related to the Jones polynomial, as discussed in
[
26
]
.
As already discussed in
[
2
,
3
]
in the context of the
Connes–Kreimer Hopf algebra of Feynman graphs, such invariants derived from
the Tutte polynomial can be regarded as “algebraic Feynman rules”, which
simply means characters of the Hopf algebra
{\mathcal{H}}
: homomorphisms of
commutative algebras from
{\mathcal{H}}
to a commutative algebra
{\mathcal{R}}
of polynomials
(Tutte and Bollobás–Riordan case) or of Laurent polynomials (Jones case).

We then consider
a Rota–Baxter structure of weight
-1
on the target algebra of polynomials
or of Laurent polynomials
and use the Birkhoff factorization formula to obtain new Galois invariants
of dessins.

The main point of this construction in our environment is not
just subtraction of divergences: the intended effect is to
introduce new invariants that are sensitive not only to
D
itself but
that encode in a consistent way (where consistency is determined by the Rota–Baxter operator)
the information carried by all the sub-dessins
\delta
and the
quotient dessins
D/\delta
as well.

The Tutte polynomial of a graph is determined uniquely by a deletion-contraction
relation and the value on graphs consisting of a set of vertices with
no edges, to which the computation reduces by repeated application
of the deletion-contraction relation. It can also be defined by a closed
“sum over states” formula

\displaystyle P_{D}(x,y)=\sum_{\delta\subset D}(x-1)^{b_{0}(\delta)-b_{0}(D)}(y-1)^{b_{1}(\delta)},

(2.5)

where here the sum is over all subgraphs
\delta\subset D
with the same
set of vertices
V(\delta)=V(D)
but with fewer edges
E(\delta)\subset E(D)
.

The Bollobás–Riordan–Tutte polynomial is defined for an oriented
ribbon graph. It can also be characterised in terms of a deletion-contraction
relation or in terms of a state-sum formula, which generalizes (
2.5
) in the form

\displaystyle BR_{D}(x,y,z)=\sum_{\delta\subset D}(x-1)^{b_{0}(\delta)-b_{0}(D)}(y-1)^{b_{1}(\delta)}z^{b_{0}(\delta)+b_{1}(\delta)-f(\delta)},

(2.6)

with
f(\delta)
the number of faces of the surface embedding of
\delta
.
Note that here, for consistency with (
2.5
), we used
y-1
instead of
y
in
the analogous formula of
[
9
]
: switching to the
y-1
variable is also advocated
in the last section of
[
9
]
for symmetry reasons.

As shown in
[
26
, Theorem 5.4]
, the specialization of the Bollobás–Riordan–Tutte polynomial

\displaystyle t^{2-2\#V(D)+\#E(D)}BR_{D}\big({-}t^{4},1-t^{-2}\big(t^{2}+t^{-2}\big),\big(t^{2}+t^{-2}\big)^{2}\big)

gives the Kauffman bracket (for a link projection whose associated connected ribbon graphs is
D
). It is similarly known that the Jones polynomial is obtained from the
2
-variable Tutte polynomial by specializing to
P_{D}(-t,-1/t)
. The specialization to the diagonal
P_{D}(t,t)
of the Tutte polynomial, on the other hand, gives the Martin polynomial
[
59
]
. Such
1
-variable specializations, with values in polynomials or Laurent polynomials, are also invariants of dessins
D
.

Lemma 2.15
.

The Tutte polynomial
P_{D}(x,y)
is a Galois invariant of dessins
D
,
namely
P_{\gamma D}(x,y)=P_{D}(x,y)
for all
\gamma\in G={\rm Gal}\big(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Q}}\big)
.
Similarly, the Bollobás–Riordan–Tutte polynomial
BR_{D}(x,y,z)
is a Galois invariant of dessins
D
.

Proof.

Note that if
D
is a dessin, then the quantities
b_{0}(D)
,
\#E(D)=d

and
\#V(D)=m+n
are Galois invariants and so is
b_{1}(D)
by the Euler
characteristic formula. For
\gamma\in G={\rm Gal}\big(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Q}}\big)
we then have

\displaystyle P_{\gamma D}(x,y)=\sum_{\delta^{\prime}\subset\gamma D}(x-1)^{b_{0}(\delta^{\prime})-b_{0}(D)}(y-1)^{b_{1}(\delta^{\prime})}.

As in Proposition
2.11
, we can argue that the set of subgraphs
\delta^{\prime}\subset\gamma D
with
V(\delta^{\prime})=V(\gamma D)
and
E(\delta^{\prime})\subset E(\gamma D)
can be identified with the set of subgraphs
\gamma\delta
where
\delta
ranges over the
subgraphs of
D
with
V(\delta)=V(D)
and
E(\delta)\subset E(D)
. Thus, we get

\displaystyle P_{\gamma D}(x,y)=\sum_{\gamma\delta\subset\gamma D}(x-1)^{b_{0}(\gamma\delta)-b_{0}(D)}(y-1)^{b_{1}(\gamma\delta)}=P_{D}(x,y),

since
b_{0}(\gamma\delta)=b_{0}(\delta)
and
b_{1}(\gamma\delta)=b_{1}(\delta)
. The case of the
Bollobás–Riordan–Tutte polynomial
BR_{D}(x,y,z)
is similar: we only need to verify that the number of
faces
f(D)
of the embedding of
D
in
\Sigma
is also a Galois invariant. This is the case (see also
Lemma
2.48
below)
since by the Riemann–Hurwitz formula
\chi(\Sigma)=-d+m+n+r
, where the degree
d
and
the ramification indices
m
,
n
,
r
at
0
,
1
,
\infty
are all Galois invariants. Writing
\chi(\Sigma)=\#V(D)-\#E(D)+f(D)
then shows
f(D)
is also a Galois invariant.
∎

Note that, as shown explicitly by the state-sum formulae (
2.5
) and (
2.6
),
these invariants are only sensitive to simple Galois invariants such as
b_{0}
,
b_{1}
, for
subdessins
\delta\subset D
. However, when we apply to this invariant the Birkhoff factorization procedure we obtain more refined invariants that also depend on the quotient dessins
D/\delta
.

We now discuss Rota–Baxter structures. In the case of specializations to
Kauffman brackets and Jones polynomials, which take values in the algebra
{\mathcal{R}}

of one-variable Laurent polynomials, we can use the Rota–Baxter operator
T
of
weight
-1
given by projection onto the polar part. In the case of Tutte and
Bollobás–Riordan–Tutte polynomials with values in a multivariable polynomial
algebra, we can consider a Rota–Baxter operator built out of the following
Rota–Baxter structure of weight
-1
on the algebra of polynomials, applied
in each variable. As shown in
[
34
]
, the algebra
{\mathcal{R}}={\mathbb{Q}}[t]
with the vector space basis given by
the polynomials

\displaystyle\pi_{n}(t)=\frac{t(t+1)\cdots(t+n-1)}{n!},

(2.7)

for
n\geq 1
and
\pi_{0}(t)=1
, is a Rota–Baxter algebra of weight
-1
with
the Rota–Baxter operator
T
given by the linear operator defined on this basis by

\displaystyle T(\pi_{n})=\pi_{n+1}.

In the case of multivariable polynomials, Rota–Baxter structures of
weight
-1
can be constructed using the tensor product in the category of
commutative Rota–Baxter algebras described in
[
13
]
.

Proposition 2.16
.

Let
\phi\in{\rm Hom}_{{\rm Alg}_{\mathbb{Q}}}({\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}},{\mathcal{R}})
be either the Tutte or
Bollobás–Riordan–Tutte polynomial or one of its specializations to
Kauffman bracket or Jones polynomial or Martin polynomial. Let
{\mathcal{R}}
be endowed with
the Rota–Baxter operator
T
of weight
-1
as above.
Then the Birkhoff factorization
\phi=(\phi_{-}\circ S)\star\phi_{+}

determines new Galois invariants
\phi_{\pm}\in{\rm Hom}_{{\rm Alg}_{\mathbb{Q}}}({\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{D}},G},{\mathcal{R}}_{\pm})

which depend on both the strongly balanced subdessins
\delta\subset D
and their
quotient dessins
D/\delta
.

Proof.

We discuss explicitly only the one-variable cases of the Martin polynomial
specialization
PD(t,t)
and the Jones polynomial specialization
P_{D}(-t,-1/t)

of the Tutte polynomial. The other cases are not conceptually different but computationally
more complicated.
For the Jones specialization
P_{D}(-t,-1/t)
is a Laurent polynomial and we
use the Rota–Baxter operator
T
of projection onto the polar part. We obtain
two new invariants
P_{D,\pm}
with
P_{D,+}(t)
a polynomial in
t
and
P_{D,-}(u)

a polynomial in
u=1/t
given by

\displaystyle P_{D,-}(1/t):=-T(P_{D}(-t,-1/t)+\sum_{\delta\in{\mathcal{B}}(D)}P_{\delta,-}(1/t)P_{D/\delta}(-t,-1/t)),

\displaystyle P_{D,+}(t):=(1-T)(P_{D}(-t,-1/t)+\sum_{\delta\in{\mathcal{B}}(D)}P_{\delta,-}(1/t)P_{D/\delta}(-t,-1/t)).

In the case of the Martin specialization
P_{D}(t,t)
of the Tutte polynomial, let
\pi_{n}
be
the linear basis of
{\mathbb{Q}}[t]
as in (
2.7
) and let

\displaystyle P_{D}(t,t)=\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}(D)\pi_{n}(t)

be the expansion in this basis of the Martin polynomial. Let
u_{m,n,k}\in{\mathbb{Q}}
be the combinatorial
coefficients satisfying
\pi_{n}\pi_{m}=\sum_{k}u(m,n,k)\pi_{k}
. The Rota–Baxter operator in this case is the linear map
T(\pi_{n})=\pi_{n+1}
so that
T(P_{D}(t,t))=\sum\limits_{n\geq 1}a_{n-1}(D)\pi_{n}
. In this case, the new invariants
P_{D,\pm}(t)
obtained from the Birkhoff factorization are polynomial
invariants of the form

\displaystyle P_{D,\pm}(t):=\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}^{\pm}(D)\pi_{k}(t),

\displaystyle a_{k}^{-}(D):=a_{k-1}(D)+\sum_{\delta}\sum_{m,n}a_{m}^{-}(\delta)a_{n}(D/\delta)u_{m,n,k-1},

\displaystyle a_{k}^{+}(D):=(a_{k}(D)-a_{k-1}(D))+\sum_{\delta}\sum_{m,n}a_{m}^{-}(\delta)a_{n}(D/\delta)(u_{m,n,k}-u_{m,n,k-1}).

This shows explicitly how passing from the coefficients
a_{n}(D)
to the coefficients
a_{n}^{\pm}(D)

incorporates the information on the Galois invariants of all the quotient dessins
D/\delta
as
well as sub-dessins
\delta
in a combination dictated by the specific form of the Rota–Baxter operator.
∎

2.8
The semigroup of Belyi-extending maps

Belyi-extending maps were introduced in
[
61
]
as a way to obtain new Galois invariants for
the action of the absolute Galois group on dessins d’enfant. They consist of maps
h\colon{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
,
defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
, ramified only at
\{0,1,\infty\}
, and mapping
\{0,1,\infty\}
to itself. In particular, they have the property that, if
f\colon\Sigma\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
is a Belyi map, then the composition
h\circ f
is still a Belyi map. This defines an action of the semigroup of Belyi-extending maps on dessins, which commutes with the action of
G={\rm Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Q}})
.

In the following, we will denote by
{\mathcal{E}}
the semigroup of Belyi-extending maps with the operation of
composition. We define the product in
{\mathcal{E}}
as
\eta_{1}\cdot\eta_{2}=\eta_{2}\circ\eta_{1}
, for the
convenience of writing semigroup homomorphisms rather than anti-homomorphism later in this
section, since the action of
{\mathcal{E}}
on Belyi maps will be by composition on the right.

Notice that the semigroup
{\mathbb{N}}
of self maps of
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
(extended to self maps of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}

ramified at
0
and
\infty
), used in
[
17
]
for the construction of the Bost–Connes endomotive
can be seen as a subsemigroup of the semigroup
{\mathcal{E}}
of Belyi-extending maps.

We can in principle consider two different versions of the semigroup
{\mathcal{E}}
, one
as in
[
61
]
, where we consider all Belyi-extending maps (mapping the set

\{0,1,\infty\}
into itself), and one where we consider only those Belyi-extending maps
that map it to itself. The first choice has the advantage of giving rise to a larger set of
new Galois invariants of dessins. In particular, the
only known example of a Belyi-extending map separating Galois-orbits, as shown in
[
61
]
,
does not satisfy the more restrictive condition. In the more restrictive class
one can always assume, up to a change of coordinates on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, that a Belyi-extending map
sends the points
0
,
1
,
\infty
to themselves. In each isomorphism class there is a unique
representative with this property. This assumption is convenient when one
considers dynamical properties under compositions, see for instance
[
5
]
.
The main construction we discuss works in both settings, but some statements will
depend on choosing the more restrictive class of Belyi-extending maps, as we will see
in the following subsections. We will state explicitly when this choice is needed.

Note that, in principle, one could also consider a larger semigroup
{\mathcal{E}}_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}}

consisting of all Belyi maps of genus zero mapping
\{0,1,\infty\}
into itself. The
construction of the crossed product system we outline below would also work in
this case, but this choice would have the inconvenient property that the absolute
Galois group
{\rm Gal}\big(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Q}}\big)
action would involve both the part

{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}}
of the algebra and the semigroup
{\mathcal{E}}_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}}
,
unlike the Bost–Connes case. However, we will include the case of

{\mathcal{E}}_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}}
and subsemigroups in our discussion, because it will
be useful in presenting in an explicit way a method of removal of certain catastrophic
everywhere-divergence problems that can occur in the partition function
of the quantum statistical mechanical system. We will discuss this in
Section
2.14
below.

Definition 2.17
.

Given an element
\eta\in{\mathcal{E}}
we denote by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}_{\eta}=\{h\in{\mathcal{E}}\,|\,\exists\,h^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}},\,h=h^{\prime}\circ\eta\},

the range of precomposition by
\eta
. We also denote by
{\mathcal{B}}_{\eta}

the subset of Belyi maps that are in the range of composition with a given Belyi-extending map

\eta\in{\mathcal{E}}
, that is,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}_{\eta}=\big\{f\colon\Sigma\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\text{ Belyi }\,|\,\exists\,f^{\prime}\colon\Sigma\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1},\text{ Belyi}\colon f=\eta\circ f^{\prime}\big\}.

We consider the Hilbert space
\ell^{2}({\mathcal{E}})
with the standard orthonormal basis
\{\epsilon_{\eta}\}

for
\eta\in{\mathcal{E}}
ranging over the Belyi extending maps. The semigroup
{\mathcal{E}}
acts on
\ell^{2}({\mathcal{E}})

by precomposition,
\mu_{\eta}\epsilon_{\eta^{\prime}}=\epsilon_{\eta^{\prime}\circ\eta}
. With the product in
{\mathcal{E}}

defined as above, this gives a semigroup homomorphism
\mu\colon{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{B}}(\ell^{2}({\mathcal{E}}))
.
We denote by
\Pi_{\eta}
the orthogonal projection of
\ell^{2}({\mathcal{E}})
onto the subspace generated
by the elements of
{\mathcal{E}}_{\eta}
.

2.9
Developing maps

We review here some general facts about orbifold uniformization and developing maps and apply them to
the studies of multivalued inverses of Belyi maps.

In general (see
[
64
]
), an orbifold datum
(X,D)
consists of a complex manifold
X
, a
divisor
D=\sum_{i}m_{i}Y_{i}
on
X
with integer multiplicities
m_{i}>2
, and hypersurfaces
Y_{i}

such that near every point of
X
there is a neighborhood
{\mathcal{U}}
with a branched
cover that ramifies along the loci
{\mathcal{U}}\cap Y_{i}
with branching indices
m_{i}
.

A complex manifold
\tilde{X}
with a branched covering map
f\colon\tilde{X}\to X
that
ramifies exactly along
Y=\cup_{i}Y_{i}
with indices
m_{i}
is called an
uniformization of
the orbifold datum
(X,D)
.

One might consider the multivalued inverse
\phi\colon X\to\tilde{X}
of the
uniformization map
f\colon\tilde{X}\to X
. This is usually referred to
as the
developing map
in the cases where
\tilde{X}
is simply connected, though
we will be using the term “developing map” more generally here for multivalued inverse maps.
Some concrete developing maps are related to certain classes of differential equations, such
as hypergeometric, Lamé, Heun, etc.
In particular, the Belyi maps
f\colon\Sigma\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
can be
seen as special cases of uniformization of orbifold data
\big({\mathbb{P}}^{1},D\big)
with
D

supported on
\{0,1,\infty\}
, and we can consider the associated multivalued
developing map
\phi=\phi_{f}
.

In the case where
\tilde{X}={\mathbb{H}}
is the upper half-plane and the uniformization map
is the quotient map that realizes an orbifold datum
\big({\mathbb{P}}^{1},D\big)
as the quotient of

{\mathbb{H}}
by a Fuchsian group, if we denote by
z
the coordinate on
{\mathbb{H}}
and by
t
the (affine) coordinate on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}({\mathbb{C}})
, a developing
map
z=\phi(t)
(period map) is obtained as the ratio
\phi(t)=u_{1}(t)/u_{2}(t)
of two independent non-trivial
solutions of the differential equation (orbifold uniformization equation, see
[
64
, Proposition 4.2]
)

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}^{2}u}{{\rm d}t^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\{z,t\}u=0,

(2.8)

where
\{z,t\}=\{\phi(t),t\}={\mathcal{S}}(\phi)(t)
is the Schwarzian derivative

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}(\phi)(t):=\left(\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}}{\phi^{\prime}}\right)^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}}{\phi^{\prime}}\right)^{2}=\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime\prime}}{\phi^{\prime}}-\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}}{\phi^{\prime}}\right)^{2}.

More generally, given a Belyi map
f\colon\Sigma\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, we can precompose it with a Fuchsian uniformization
\pi\colon{\mathbb{H}}\to{\mathbb{H}}/\Gamma=\Sigma
and consider the multivalued inverse of the Belyi map
f
in terms of a developing map for
f\circ\pi
.

Riemann surfaces
\Sigma
that are obtained from algebraic curves defined over number fields admit
uniformizations
\Sigma={\mathbb{H}}/\Gamma
by Fuchsian groups that are finite index subrgroups of a Fuchsian
triangle group
\Delta
, hence one can view Belyi maps as maps
f\colon{\mathbb{H}}/\Gamma=\Sigma\to{\mathbb{H}}/\Delta={\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, see
[
14
]
.

In the case of a triangle group
\Delta
the orbifold uniformization equation
reduces to a hypergeometric equation, hence the resulting
\phi(t)
can be described in terms of hypergeometric functions.

The classical hypergeometric equation, depending on three parameters
a
,
b
,
c

\displaystyle t(t-1)F^{\prime\prime}+((a+b-1)t-c)F^{\prime}+abF=0

(2.9)

has the hypergeometric function

\displaystyle F(a,b,c|t)=\sum\frac{(a)_{n}(b)_{n}}{(c)_{n}}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

as a solution, with
(x)_{n}=x(x+1)\cdots(x+n-1)
the Pochhammer symbol.

Two linearly independent solutions
f_{1}
,
f_{2}
, locally defined in sectors near
t=0
,
can be obtained as
{\mathbb{C}}
-linear combinations of
F(a,b,c|t)
and
t^{1-c}F(a-c+1,b-c+1,2-c|t)
.
The Schwarz map
\phi(t)=f_{1}(t)/f_{2}(t)
given by the ratio of two independent
solutions maps holomorphically the upper half plane
{\mathbb{H}}
to the curvilinear triangle
T
with
vertices
\phi(0)
,
\phi(1)
,
\phi(\infty)
and angles
|1-c|\pi
,
|c-a-b|\pi
, and

|a-b|\pi
. The equation (
2.9
) is equivalent to (
2.8
), for the case of branch
points at
0
,
1
,
\infty
and these angles. Extending the map by Schwarz reflection, one obtains in this way the developing map, for the uniformization of the orbifold datum
\big({\mathbb{P}}^{1}({\mathbb{C}}),D\big)
, with set of orbifold points
\{0,1,\infty\}
and angles as above, with a tessellation of
{\mathbb{H}}
(in the hyperbolic case) by copies of
T
and the group generated by reflections about the edges of
T
.

In the case where
|1-c|=m_{0}^{-1}
,
|c-a-b|=m_{1}^{-1}
,
|a-b|=m_{2}^{-1}
are
inverses of positive integers, the Schwarz function
\phi(t)
can be inverted and
the inverse function
t=t(z)
, the uniformization map, is an automorphic
function for the Fuchsian triangle group
\Delta(m_{0},m_{1},m_{2})
, which is
related to the problem of computing the Hauptmodul for triangle groups
[
14
,
28
]
.
For a Belyi map
f\colon\Sigma={\mathbb{H}}/\Gamma\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}={\mathbb{H}}/\Delta
the uniformization
equation can be seen as the pullback of the equation for
{\mathbb{H}}/\Delta
.
A formulation of multivalued inverse developing maps of Belyi maps in terms of
log-Riemann surfaces is discussed in
[
7
]
.

The properties of developing maps for Belyi maps recalled here give us the following
consequence, which we will be using in our construction of the quantum statistical
mechanical system based on Belyi-extending maps in the next subsections.

Lemma 2.18
.

Consider an element
\eta\in{\mathcal{E}}
, that is, a Belyi map
\eta\colon{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
, with
\eta(\{0,1,\infty\})\subseteq\{0,1,\infty\}
. Let
\phi_{\eta}
be its developing map as above.

If
{\mathcal{B}}_{\eta}
is the subset of Belyi maps introduced in Definition
2.17
, then composition
\phi_{\eta}\circ f
of any
f\in{\mathcal{B}}_{\eta}
with the developing map
\phi_{\eta}
gives a map
f^{\prime}
satisfying
\eta\circ f^{\prime}=f
.

Also, for any
\eta^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\eta}
, with
{\mathcal{E}}_{\eta}
as in Definition
2.17
, precomposition of
\eta^{\prime}
with
\phi_{\eta}
gives a map
\eta^{\prime\prime}
with
\eta^{\prime\prime}\circ\eta=\eta^{\prime}
. We put
\rho_{\eta}(f):=\phi_{\eta}\circ f
for
f\in{\mathcal{B}}_{\eta}
.

For more general Belyi maps
f
we define as above the compositions
\eta^{\prime}\circ\rho_{\eta}(f)
, for any
\eta^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\eta}
.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma
be the uniformizing group of the source orbifold datum

\big({\mathbb{P}}^{1},\eta^{-1}(D)\big)
with
D=\{0,1,\infty\}
and let
\bar{\phi}_{\eta}(t)\colon\big({\mathbb{P}}^{1},D\big)\to\big({\mathbb{P}}^{1},\eta^{-1}(D)\big)
, and let
\pi\colon{\mathbb{H}}\to{\mathbb{H}}/\Gamma={\mathbb{P}}^{1}
be the uniformization map for
\big({\mathbb{P}}^{1},\eta^{-1}(D)\big)
, with
\phi_{\pi}
the corresponding developing map.

The multivalued inverse
\phi_{\eta}
for the Belyi-extending map
\eta\colon{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
can then be regarded as the composition
\pi\circ\tilde{\phi}_{\eta}
of the developing map
\tilde{\phi}_{\eta}=\phi_{\pi}\circ\phi_{\eta}
for
\eta\circ\pi
and the uniformization
\pi\colon{\mathbb{H}}\to{\mathbb{H}}/\Gamma
of
({\mathbb{P}}^{1},\eta^{-1}(D))
. Given
f\in{\mathcal{B}}_{\eta}
, the composition
\phi_{\eta}\circ f
is defined and gives
a Belyi map
f^{\prime}\colon\Sigma\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
such that
\eta\circ f^{\prime}=f
. Thus, the transformation

\rho_{\eta}(f)=\phi_{\eta}\circ f
is well defined on
f\in{\mathcal{B}}_{\eta}
.

For
\eta^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\eta}
similarly we have
\eta^{\prime}=h^{\prime}\circ\eta
for some
h^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}
. Thus, the composition
\eta^{\prime}\circ\phi_{\eta}
is defined and gives an element in
{\mathcal{E}}
. Given a Belyi map
f\colon\Sigma\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, a Belyi-extending map
\eta
, and a Belyi-extending map
\eta^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\eta}
, with
\eta^{\prime}=h^{\prime}\circ\eta
for some
h^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}
, we have
\eta^{\prime}\circ\rho_{\eta}(f)
given by the composition
\eta^{\prime}\circ\phi_{\eta}\circ f=h^{\prime}\circ f
.
∎

2.10
Belyi-extending semigroup and Belyi functions operators

Returning to the framework of the end of Section
2.5
, recall that isometries
\mu_{\eta}
of
\ell^{2}({\mathcal{E}})
act upon the basis elements
\epsilon_{\eta^{\prime}}
as
\mu_{\eta}\epsilon_{\eta^{\prime}}=\epsilon_{\eta^{\prime}\circ\eta}
. We have
\mu_{\eta}^{*}\mu_{\eta}={\rm id}
.

Clearly,
e_{\eta}:=\mu_{\eta}\mu_{\eta}^{*}
is the projector acting on
\ell^{2}({\mathcal{E}})
as the orthogonal projector
\Pi_{\eta}
onto the subspace generated by the basis elements
\epsilon_{\eta^{\prime}}
with
\eta^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\eta}
.

Given a Belyi map
f\colon\Sigma\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, let
D=D_{f}
be the associated dessin.
We include here the case where
D
may have multiple connected components.
This case corresponds to branched coverings where
\Sigma
can have multiple
components. Given a Belyi-extending map
\beta\in{\mathcal{E}}
, we consider the dessin

\eta(D)
corresponding to the Belyi map
\eta\circ f
as in
[
61
]
.

Definition 2.19
.

Let
\varphi\in{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{D}}\big(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\big)={\rm Hom}_{\rm Alg_{\mathbb{Q}}}\big({\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}},\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\big)
be a character of the Hopf algebra of dessins. Given a Belyi map
f\colon\Sigma\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, we define a linear operator
\pi_{\varphi}(f)
by setting

\displaystyle\pi_{\varphi}(f)\epsilon_{\eta}=\varphi(\eta(D_{f}))\epsilon_{\eta},

(2.10)

for all
\eta\in{\mathcal{E}}
and with
\eta(D_{f})=D_{\eta\circ f}
. For simplicity of notation we shall write
\varphi(\eta\circ f):=\varphi(\eta(D_{f}))
in the above.

The operator
\pi_{\varphi}(f)
is bounded if

\displaystyle\sup_{\eta\in{\mathcal{E}}}|\varphi(\eta(D_{f}))|\leq C_{\varphi,f},

(2.11)

for some constant
C_{\varphi,f}>0
.

2.11
Invariant characters and balanced characters

We will now discuss simple examples of characters
\varphi
that satisfy this boundedness
condition.

Let
\iota\colon\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
be a fixed embedding.
Let
\lambda\in\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
be an algebraic number of absolute value
|\iota(\lambda)|\leq 1
,
with the property that all its Galois conjugates are also contained in the unit disk. Note that
this condition can be easily achieved, for instance by dividing by a large integer, unlike
the more delicate conditions (such as for Pisot and Salem numbers) where one requires
one of the points in the Galois orbit to remain outside of the unit disk.

Proposition 2.20
.

Let
\lambda\in\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
be chosen as above, so that

Proof.

Consider
\lambda\in\bar{\mathbb{Q}}

\varphi\colon{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}}\to\bar{\mathbb{Q}}

{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{D}},G}
. Given a Belyi function

\displaystyle\varphi(\eta(D_{f}))=\iota(\lambda)^{\#E(\eta(D_{f}))}=\big(\iota(\lambda)^{\#E(D_{f})}\big)^{\#E(D_{\eta})}.

In Proposition

\varphi\in{\rm Hom}_{{\rm Alg}_{\mathbb{Q}}}\big({\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}},\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\big)

Definition 2.21
.

A character

\displaystyle\varphi(\gamma D)=\gamma\varphi(D),

\gamma\in G={\rm Gal}\big(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Q}}\big)

The following example shows that the set of balanced characters is non-empty, although

-orbits on the set
{\mathcal{D}}
of dessins, which
in itself requires an explicit knowledge of these orbits. The question of constructing
an explicit map

of dessins to
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}

Lemma 2.22
.

Let
{\mathcal{R}}=\{D\}
be a set of representatives of the
G
-orbits on the set of dessins. Let
\{\lambda_{D}\}
be a set of algebraic numbers with
|\iota(\gamma\lambda_{D})|\leq 1

for all
\gamma\in G
and
\deg(\lambda_{D})=\#{\rm Orb}(D)

\displaystyle\varphi(\gamma D)=(\gamma\lambda_{D})^{\#E(D)}

{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}}
, with the property that the linear operators
\pi_{\varphi}(\gamma f)
on
\ell^{2}({\mathcal{E}})
are bounded for any Belyi function
f
and all
\gamma\in G

Proof.

Let
d_{D}=\#{\rm Orb}(D)
be the length of the corresponding orbit. By the orbit-stabilizer theorem this is also the cardinality of the set of cosets
d_{D}=\#G/{\rm Stab}(D)
.
For each

, choose an algebraic number
\lambda_{D}\in\bar{\mathbb{Q}}

\displaystyle\varphi(\gamma D)=\gamma(\lambda_{D})^{\#E(D)}.

Since

and we can identify the action of

on
{\rm Orb}(D)
with the left multiplication on the cosets

\varphi
bijectively maps
{\rm Orb}(D)
to

in such a way

is a balanced character.

Possibly after dividing by a sufficiently large integer, we can assume
that
\lambda_{D}
and all its Galois conjugates lie inside the unit disk, so that the sequence

\big\{\gamma(\lambda_{D})^{n}\big\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is bounded in the

\ell^{\infty}
-norm, for all
\gamma\in G
.
Consider then the operators
\pi_{\varphi}(f)
associated to the character
\varphi
,
for Belyi maps
f

by

, where

is the

Since all the Belyi-extending maps

are defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}

\gamma\eta(D)=\eta(\gamma D)
for all
\gamma\in G

\eta(D)
as the representative for

\displaystyle\pi_{\varphi}(f)\epsilon_{\eta}=\lambda_{\eta(D)}^{\#E(\eta(D))}\epsilon_{\eta}.

Since the algebraic numbers

for all

and all

∎

We will now proceed to the construction of a quantum statistical mechanical

Let

Remark 2.26

Note that for any given choice of a subsemigroup

of the semigroup of Belyi-expanding maps, one can adapt the construction above and obtain an

Proposition 2.27

Let
{\mathcal{E}}_{BC}\subset{\mathcal{E}}

\eta

Choose a balanced character
\varphi\colon{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}}\to{\mathbb{Q}}^{ab}\subset\bar{\mathbb{Q}}

is the dessin of a Belyi function ramified only at

,

to themselves. By the Riemann–Hurwitz formula, the ramification

and

, so

while

. Similarly, for a Belyi-extending map with

of Belyi-extending maps, the multiplicity of

\epsilon_{\eta}
of
\ell^{2}({\mathcal{E}})
with the basis

of

for
\{e(r)\}_{r\in{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}}
the generators of the Bost–Connes algebra
and

in
{\mathbb{C}}
.
∎

In the statement of Lemma
2.25
above we have regarded the partition function purely as a formal Dirichlet series. In order to proceed to the consideration of Gibbs states and limiting zero-temperature states, however, we need a setting where this Dirichlet series converges for
sufficiently large
\beta>0
. As we discuss in detail in the next subsection, due to the
particular nature of semigroup homomorphisms
\Upsilon\colon{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathbb{N}}
from the
semigroup of Belyi-expanding maps to the integers, the Dirichlet series (

)
tends to be everywhere divergent, except in the case where the system is
constructed using the subsemigroup
{\mathcal{E}}_{BC}
of

that recovers the

After discussing semigroup homomorphisms from the semigroup of Belyi-expanding maps, we return to the construction of the quantum statistical mechanical system and we show how Definition
2.24

and Proposition
2.25
can be modified to avoid this divergence problem.

2.13
Semigroup homomorphisms of Belyi-extending maps

We discuss here semigroup homomorphisms with source the semigroup of
Belyi-extending maps with the operation of composition. In particular, we calculate the
behaviour of the multiplicities associated to these semigroup homomorphisms,
namely the size of the level sets.

As a simple illustration of the most severe type of divergence one
expects to encounter in the partition functions, we consider first the case of the larger
semigroup
{\mathcal{E}}_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}}
of Belyi maps of genus zero mapping
\{0,1,\infty\}
into
itself, and the subsemigroup
{\mathcal{E}}_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}},0}
of Belyi polynomials, which have
bipartite planar trees as dessins.

Lemma 2.28
.

The map
\Upsilon\colon{\mathcal{E}}_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}}\to{\mathbb{N}}
given by the degree
\Upsilon(\eta)=\deg(\eta)

is a semigroup homomorphism. The level sets of its restriction to
{\mathcal{E}}_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}},0}
are given by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}_{\Upsilon,d}:=\{\eta\in{\mathcal{E}}\,|\,\deg(\eta)=d\}=\{\text{bipartite trees }T\,|\,\#E(T)=d\},

\displaystyle\#{\mathcal{E}}_{\Upsilon,d}=2(d+1)^{d-1}.

Proof.

The degree of a Belyi map
\eta\in{\mathcal{E}}
is
\deg(\eta)=\#E(D_{\eta})
,
the number of edges of its dessin. For Belyi maps
\eta\colon{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
in
{\mathcal{E}}_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}},0}
the
dessin
D_{\eta}
is a bipartite tree. Since the bipartite structure on a tree is uniquely
determined by assigning the color of a single vertex, the number of elements in
{\mathcal{E}}_{\Upsilon,d}

can be identified with twice the number of trees on
d
edges, which is equal to
(d+1)^{d-1}
.
∎

Remark 2.29
.

For
\Upsilon\colon{\mathcal{E}}_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}},0}\to{\mathbb{N}}
given by the degree
\Upsilon(\eta)=\deg(\eta)
, the formal Dirichlet series (
2.13
)

\displaystyle 2\sum_{d\geq 1}(d+1)^{d-1}d^{-\beta}

is everywhere divergent.

When we restrict ourselves to the subsemigroup
{\mathcal{E}}\subset{\mathcal{E}}_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}}
of Belyi-extending maps
and to the corresponding
{\mathcal{E}}_{0}\subset{\mathcal{E}}_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}},0}
, the multiplicities decrease, since
many bipartite trees do not correspond to Belyi maps defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
. One can see
that there are choices of subsemigroups
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
of the semigroup
{\mathcal{E}}
of Belyi-extending maps
for which the partition function does not have this dramatic everywhere-divergence problem.
We give an example of such a subsemigroup in Lemma
2.31
below. In such cases,
we can just use the quantum statistical mechanical system constructed in Section
2.12

above as the correct generalization of the Bost–Connes system. However, subsemigroups
of
{\mathcal{E}}
with this property tend to be too small to achieve separation of Galois orbits. For this
reason, we want to allow larger semigroups of Belyi maps and we need to introduce an
appropriate method that will cure possible everywhere-divergence phenomena in the
partition function.

In the example above we have considered the degree homomorphism.
One can obtain other semigroup homomorphisms
\Upsilon\colon{\mathcal{E}}_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}}\to{\mathbb{N}}
(with restrictions

\Upsilon\colon{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathbb{N}}
) by composing
the degree homomorphism with an arbitrary semigroup homomorphism
\Psi\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}
.
Since
{\mathbb{N}}
is the free abelian semigroup generated by primes, a homomorphism

\Phi
can be obtained by assigning to each prime
p
a number
\Psi(p)\in{\mathbb{N}}
. It is easy
to check, however, that any homomorphism
\Psi\circ\Upsilon
obtained in this way
will not compensate for the everywhere divergence problem of Remark
2.29
. This
leads to the natural question of whether there are other interesting semigroup
homomorphism with source
{\mathcal{E}}
that do not factor through the degree map.
If one restricts to the subsemigroup
{\mathcal{E}}_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}},0}
as in Lemma
2.28
,
for which the dessins are trees, then the length of the unique path in the tree
from
0
to
1
is an example of such a morphism. We focus here below on
a semigroup homomorphism to a non-abelian semigroup of matrices.

In particular, we will be observing more closely how other combinatorial data of dessins
behave under the composition of maps, such as the number of W/B vertices in the
bipartition (the ramification indices
m
and
n
of the map at the points
0
and
1
).
This will identify some interesting (non-abelian) semigroup laws, and resulting
semigroup homomorphisms.

Proposition 2.30
.

Consider the subsemigroup
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}}

We will discuss the map (

In restricting to the subsemigroup

For a composition

points while the second set consists of

d^{\prime}(m-1)
points. Thus, we have
\#(\eta^{\prime}\circ\eta)^{-1}(0)=d^{\prime}(m-1)+m^{\prime}
.
It remains to check that the product in the semigroup
M_{2}^{+}({\mathbb{Z}})

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}d^{\prime}&m^{\prime}-1\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}d&m-1\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}d^{\prime}d&d^{\prime}(m-1)+m^{\prime}-1\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}

is equal to
\Upsilon(\eta^{\prime}\circ\eta)
. When restricting to the subsemigroup
{\mathcal{E}}_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}},0}
,
the counting of the size of the level sets is based on the number of bipartite trees with
m

white and
n
black vertices, which is equal to
m^{n-1}n^{m-1}
and the fact that the
total number of vertices is
m+n=d+1
.
∎

The semigroup homomorphism (
2.14
) therefore captures the
semigroup law satisfied by the ramification indices
m
and
n
. Note
that the determinant homomorphism of semigroups
\det\colon M_{2}^{+}({\mathbb{Z}})\to{\mathbb{N}}

produces the semigroup homomorphism given by the degree

\displaystyle\det\circ\Upsilon(\eta)=\deg(\eta).

We can use the semigroup homomorphism constructed above to
identify choices of sufficiently small subsemigroups
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
of

{\mathcal{E}}
that do not have everywhere-divergence phenomena in the
partition function. The following shows that the genus zero single cycle
normalized Belyi maps considered in
[
5
]
provide an
example of such a semigroup.

Lemma 2.31
.

Consider genus zero Belyi maps
\eta\colon{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, normalized so
that they fix the points
0
,
1
,
\infty
,
such that the corresponding dessin
D_{\eta}
has a single cycle.
They form a subsemigroup
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}\subset{\mathcal{E}}
of the semigroup
of Belyi-extending maps with the property that the partition function (
2.13
)

\displaystyle Z(\beta)=\sum_{\eta\in{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}}\Upsilon(\eta)^{-\beta}

of the associated quantum statistical mechanical system for the
degree homomophism
\Upsilon(\eta)=\deg(\eta)
is convergent for
\beta>2
.

Proof.

The normalized genus zero single cycle Belyi maps are a particular case of the
more general class of conservative (or critically fixed) rational maps
[
23
]
, where
one assumes that the critical points are also fixed points. The genus zero and single
cycle condition correspond to requiring that the ramification indices
m
,
n
,
r
at
0
,
1
,
\infty

satisfy
2d+1=m+n+r
. One can see that these maps form a semigroup using the
semigroup law of Proposition
2.30
for the ramification indices. Indeed, for a
composition
\eta\circ\eta^{\prime}
of two such maps we have

2dd^{\prime}+1=d(m^{\prime}-1)+m+d(n^{\prime}-1)+n+d(r^{\prime}-1)+r=2dd^{\prime}+d-3d+2d+1
. As observed in
[
5
]

it is known that all the normalized genus zero single cycle Belyi maps are defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
,
so this is a subsemigroup of the semigroup
{\mathcal{E}}
of Belyi-extending maps. The number of
normalized genus zero single cycle Belyi maps of a given degree
d
is computed in

[
5
, Corollary 2.8 and Remark 2.9]
and is of the form

\displaystyle N(d)=\frac{1}{12}\big(d^{2}+4d-c\big),

where the constant
c
takes one of the following values

\displaystyle c=\begin{cases}5,&d\equiv 1\mod 6,\\
8,&d\equiv 4\mod 6,\\
9,&d\equiv 3,5\mod 6,\\
12,&d\equiv 0,1\mod 6.\end{cases}

Thus, the partition function (
2.13
) for the semigroup
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
for the
degree homomorphism
\Upsilon(\eta)=\deg(\eta)

\displaystyle Z(\beta)=\sum_{\eta\in{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}}\Upsilon(\eta)^{-\beta}=\sum_{d\geq 1}\#\{\eta\in{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}\,|\,\deg(\eta)=d\}d^{-\beta}=\sum_{d\geq 1}N(d)d^{-\beta}

is convergent for
\beta>2
.
∎

As in
[
61
]
, given a dessin
D
with Belyi function

and a Belyi-extending map
\eta\in{\mathcal{E}}
, we denote by
\eta(D)
the dessin
of the composite function
\eta\circ f
.

Corollary 2.32
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}\subset{\mathcal{E}}
be a subsemigroup of Belyi-extending maps for which
the partition function
Z(\beta)=\sum_{\eta\in{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}}\Upsilon(\eta)^{-\beta}
is
convergent for sufficiently large

. Consider the quantum statistical
mechanical system of Section
2.12
with

\varphi\in{\rm Hom}_{{\rm Alg}_{\mathbb{Q}}}\big({\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}},\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\big)
a character
satisfying the boundedness condition. Then all the invariants
\varphi(\eta(D))
,
for
\eta\in{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
and dessins
D
, occur as values of zero-temperature KMS states.

Proof.

If
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
is a subsemigroup of Belyi-extending maps with partition function
that converges for large
\beta
, as in the case of Lemma
2.31
, then
the quantum statistical mechanical system of Section
2.12
has low temperature KMS
states of the form

\displaystyle\psi_{\beta,\varphi}(X)=Z(\beta)^{-1}\sum_{\eta\in{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}}\big\langle\epsilon_{\eta},X{\rm e}^{-\beta H}\epsilon_{\eta}\big\rangle,

for elements
X
of the crossed product algebra
{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}},{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}}
and in particular

\displaystyle\psi_{\beta,\varphi}(D)=Z(\beta)^{-1}\sum_{\eta\in{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}}\big\langle\epsilon_{\eta},\pi_{\varphi}(f){\rm e}^{-\beta H}\epsilon_{\eta}\big\rangle=Z(\beta)^{-1}\sum_{\eta\in{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}}\varphi(\eta(D))\Upsilon(\eta)^{-\beta},

where

is the Belyi map with dessin

∎

In
[
61
]
the Belyi-extending maps are used to construct new Galois invariants of dessins,
in the form of invariants of the form
\varphi(\eta(D))
, where
D
is a given dessin,
\varphi
is
a Galois invariant, and
\eta\in{\mathcal{E}}
ranges over the Belyi-extending maps. In our setting, these
invariants occur as values of zero-temperature KMS states. However,
if the subsemigroup
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
is too small (as in the case of Lemma
2.31
) one
does not expect that the invariants
\varphi(\eta(D))
would have good properties with
respect to separating different Galois orbits of dessins. Thus, it is preferable to develop
a way to extend the construction of Section
2.12
to obtain a
quantum statistical mechanical system that can be used in cases of larger semigroups
of Belyi-extending maps, for which the partition function (
2.13
) may have the
type of everywhere-divergence problem encountered in the case of Remark
2.29
.

This observation was suggested to us by Lieven Le Bruyn:
examples include the case where one restricts to the trees of dynamical Belyi polynomials. This
semigroup contains several free subsemigroups, such as all Belyi polynomials of fixed degree
d>2
that
form a free semigroup. It is possible to take
1
as a leaf-vertex, and consider the subsemigroup
of such dynamical Belyi polynomials having the same Julia set. (If two polynomials have different
Julia sets, then their forward orbit is dense in the plane by
[
60
]
.)
Then this subsemigroup acts on the
inverse images of
1
like the action of the power maps on the roots of unity. However, even this
subsemigroup is likely to be too large.

To the purpose of analyzing how to treat the everywhere divergent cases, we return to a consideration of the model
case of the semigroup
{\mathcal{E}}_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}},0}
of Belyi maps with composition considered in Lemma
2.28

and Remark
2.29
, for which we know that everywhere-divergence occurs. This semigroup
can be equivalently seen as a semigroup of bipartite trees with a product
operation that reflects the composition of maps, see
[
1
]
.

2.14
Extended system, partial isometries, and partition function

In this subsection we present a method for curing the type of everywhere-divergence
problems in the partition function (
2.13
) that occur in the example of the
semigroup
{\mathcal{E}}_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}},0}
in Remark
2.29
. For the purpose of clarity,
we illustrate how the method works in the case of this semigroup.
A similar method, mutatis mutandis, can be applied to other semigroups with
similar divergence phenomena in the partition function.

We use the semigroup homomorphism (
2.14
) in order to modify the
quantum statistical mechanical system of Proposition
2.25
in such
a way that the Dirichlet series of the resulting partition function becomes convergent
for large
\beta>0
.

In the process, we will have to slightly modify our algebra:
the isometries
\mu_{\eta}
with
\mu_{\eta}^{*}\mu_{\eta}=1

and
\mu_{\eta}\mu_{\eta}^{*}=e_{\eta}
will be replaced by partial isometries (which we will still
call
\mu_{\eta}
) with
\mu_{\eta}\mu_{\eta}^{*}=e_{\eta}
and
\mu_{\eta}^{*}\mu_{\eta}=\tilde{e}_{\eta}

where both
e_{\eta}
and
\tilde{e}_{\eta}
are projectors.

Actually, we will construct
the one-parameter family of such systems, depending on a parameter
\theta>0
with

\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}\smallsetminus{\mathbb{Q}}
. We can think of this additional parameter as
a regularization parameter for the original system
({\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}},{\mathcal{E}}},\sigma_{t})

that eliminates the divergence of the partition function.

Definition 2.33
.

Given a
\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{*}_{+}\smallsetminus{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{+}
, let

denote the

Lemma 2.34
.

Let the semigroup be chosen as
{\mathcal{E}}={\mathcal{E}}_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}},0}
.
Consider the Hilbert space
\ell^{2}({\mathcal{E}}\times\Omega_{\theta})
with the standard
orthonormal basis

.
Let
\mu_{\eta}
be the operators

\displaystyle\mu_{\eta}\epsilon_{\eta^{\prime},\lambda}=\begin{cases}\epsilon_{\eta^{\prime}\circ\eta,\alpha_{\eta}^{-1}(\lambda)},&\text{if}\ \alpha_{\eta}^{-1}(\lambda)\in\Omega_{\theta},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\alpha_{\eta}=\begin{pmatrix}d&m-1\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}

2.14

and

The
\mu_{\eta}
are partial isometries with
\mu_{\eta}\mu_{\eta}^{*}=e_{\eta}

as before), and
\mu_{\eta}^{*}\mu_{\eta}=\tilde{e}_{\eta}

\displaystyle\tilde{e}_{\eta}\epsilon_{\eta^{\prime},\lambda}=\begin{cases}\epsilon_{\eta^{\prime},\lambda},&\text{if}\ \alpha_{\eta}^{-1}(\lambda)\in\Omega_{\theta},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

The matrix
\alpha_{\eta}
maps
\Omega_{\theta}
to itself by the action
by fractional linear transformations,
\lambda\mapsto\alpha_{\eta}(\lambda)=d\lambda+m-1
.
The inverse
\alpha_{\eta}^{-1}

\displaystyle\alpha_{\eta}^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}d^{-1}&d^{-1}(1-m)\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

The condition that for a given

\alpha_{\eta}^{-1}(\lambda)=d^{-1}a\theta+d^{-1}(b+1-m)
also in
\Omega_{\theta}

is satisfied if

,

, and

that the operation
\alpha_{\eta}^{-1}(\theta)

is defined with values in
\Omega_{\theta}
. The projector
\tilde{e}_{\eta}
is given by the
characteristic function of this subset. The adjoint
\mu_{\eta}^{*}
of the operator
\mu_{\eta}
of
(
2.18
) is given by
\mu_{\eta}^{*}\epsilon_{\eta^{\prime},\lambda}=\epsilon_{\eta^{\prime}\circ\phi_{\eta},\alpha_{\eta}(\lambda)}

if
\eta^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\eta}
and zero otherwise. Thus,
\mu_{\eta}
and
\mu^{*}_{\eta}
satisfy the relations as stated.
∎

Now, given a Belyi function
f\colon\Sigma\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
and a character
\varphi\in{\rm Hom}\big({\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}},\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\big)

\pi_{\varphi}(f)
defined as before, acting as

\pi_{\varphi}(f)\epsilon_{\eta,\lambda}=\varphi(\eta(D))\epsilon_{\eta,\lambda}
.

Lemma 2.35
.

Let
\sigma_{\eta}(f)=f\circ\eta
as before, for all Belyi maps

and all
\eta\in{\mathcal{E}}
. Also let

for

\mu_{\eta}
and
\mu_{\eta}^{*}
as in Lemma

\displaystyle\mu_{\eta}^{*}\pi_{\varphi}(f)\mu_{\eta}=\pi_{\varphi}(\sigma_{\eta}(f))e_{\eta}\tilde{e}_{\eta},\qquad\mu_{\eta}\pi_{\varphi}(f)\mu_{\eta}^{*}=\pi_{\varphi}(\rho_{\eta}(f))e_{\eta}\tilde{e}_{\eta}.

(2.19)

Proof.

We have
\mu_{\eta}^{*}\pi_{\varphi}(f)\mu_{\eta}\epsilon_{\eta^{\prime},\lambda}=\varphi(\eta^{\prime}\eta(D))e_{\eta}\tilde{e}_{\eta}\epsilon_{\eta^{\prime},\lambda}=\pi_{\varphi}(\sigma_{\eta}(f))e_{\eta}\tilde{e}_{\eta}\epsilon_{\eta^{\prime},\lambda}

∎

Definition 2.36

{\mathcal{E}}={\mathcal{E}}_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}},0}

, with the relations
\mu_{\eta}\mu_{\eta}^{*}=e_{\eta}

\mu_{\eta}^{*}\mu_{\eta}=\tilde{e}_{\eta}

Modifying the isometries

Remark 2.37

Unlike what happens in the original Bost–Connes system, in our case the partial

Definition 2.38
.

Consider a family of densely defined unbounded linear operators

on

\displaystyle H\epsilon_{\eta,\lambda}=\begin{cases}(F(\alpha_{\eta}(\lambda)-F(\theta))\log(\deg(\eta))\epsilon_{\eta,\lambda}&\text{if}\ \eta\neq{\rm id},\\

Proposition 2.39
.

Let the semigroup be chosen as
{\mathcal{E}}={\mathcal{E}}_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}},0}
.
We choose
\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{*}_{+}\smallsetminus{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{+}
to be an algebraic number such that

\big\{1,\theta,\theta^{2}\big\}
are linearly independent over
{\mathbb{Q}}
. Let
F(\lambda)=\lambda^{2}
.
Then, for
d\neq 1
, the multiplicity
M(d,m,\lambda)
of an eigenvalue

(F(d\lambda+m-1)-F(\theta))\log(d)
of the operator
H
is equal to

\displaystyle M(d,m,\lambda)=2+\#T_{d,m},

(2.21)

where
\#T_{d,m}
is the number of bipartite trees with
d
edges and
m
white vertices, given by (
2.17
). For
d=1
, each eigenvalue
F(\lambda)-F(\theta)
of
H
has multiplicity one.

Proof.

Suppose that
\epsilon_{\eta,\lambda}
and
\epsilon_{\eta^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}
, with
\eta,\eta^{\prime}\neq{\rm id}
, are in the same eigenspace of
H
. We have, for
d=\deg(\eta)
,
d^{\prime}=\deg(\eta^{\prime})
,

m=\#\eta^{-1}(0)
, and
m^{\prime}=\#(\eta^{\prime})^{-1}(0)
,

\displaystyle(F(d\lambda+m-1)-F(\theta))\log(d)=(F(d^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}+m^{\prime}-1)-F(\theta))\log(d^{\prime}),

where
\lambda=a\theta+b,\lambda^{\prime}=a^{\prime}\theta+b^{\prime}\in\Omega_{\theta}
.
By our choice of
F
, it takes values
F(\lambda)\in\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
for
all
\lambda\in\Omega_{\theta}
.

Recall that if we have algebraic numbers
\alpha_{1}
,
\alpha_{2}
,
\beta_{1}
,
\beta_{2}

such that

and
\log(\alpha_{2})
are linearly independent over
{\mathbb{Q}}

\beta_{1}\log(\alpha_{1})+\beta_{2}\log(\alpha_{2})\neq 0
.
This shows that, for
d
,
d^{\prime}
with
\log(d)
,
\log(d^{\prime})
linearly independent over
{\mathbb{Q}}

only need to check the dependent case.

Two logarithms of integers
\log(d)
,
\log(d^{\prime})

are linearly dependent over
{\mathbb{Q}}

for some

\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{+}
(hence

and

have the same prime factors).
Thus we can write

,

for
\delta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{*}_{+}
and

.
We are then looking at the relation

\displaystyle k(F(d\lambda+m-1)-F(\theta))=\ell(F(d^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}+m^{\prime}-1)-F(\theta)).

(2.22)

By our choice of
\theta
and of the function
F

k(F(u\theta+v)-F(\theta))=\ell(F(u^{\prime}\theta+v^{\prime})-F(\theta))
for some

, and some

gives

\displaystyle k\big((u\theta+v)^{2}-\theta^{2}\big)=\ell\big((u^{\prime}\theta+v^{\prime})^{2}-\theta^{2}\big),

that is,

\displaystyle k\big(u^{2}\theta^{2}+2uv\theta+v^{2}-\theta^{2}\big)=\ell\big((u^{\prime})^{2}\theta^{2}+2u^{\prime}v^{\prime}\theta+(v^{\prime})^{2}-\theta^{2}\big).

The independence of

implies relations

\displaystyle k\big(u^{2}-1\big)=\ell\big((u^{\prime})^{2}-1\big),\qquad kuv=\ell u^{\prime}v^{\prime},\qquad kv^{2}=\ell(v^{\prime})^{2}.

The last one gives

, which substituted in the second one gives

.

The first one then gives
k\big(u^{2}-1\big)=\ell\big((k/\ell)u^{2}-1\big)
,
hence

, so that
d=d^{\prime}
,

and

.
Thus, we obtain that the relation (

(hence

and

.
The latter equality gives

.

We have both
1\leq m\leq d
and
1\leq m^{\prime}\leq d
so that

0\leq|m^{\prime}-m|\leq d-1
and
(m^{\prime}-m)/(d-1)\in{\mathbb{Z}}
for either

(so

or

and

H

\ell^{2}({\mathcal{E}}\times\Omega_{\theta})

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{{\rm i}tH}X{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}tH}\epsilon_{\eta,\lambda}=\sigma_{t}(X)\epsilon_{\eta,\lambda},

Proposition 2.41
.

Let

(2.23)

Proof.

By Proposition

\displaystyle Z(\beta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{d,a\geq 1,b\geq 0}\sum_{1\leq m\leq d}M(d,m,\lambda){\rm e}^{-\beta(F(d(a\theta+b)+m-1)-F(\theta))}d^{-\beta}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\geq 1,b\geq 0}{\rm e}^{-\beta(F(a\theta+b)-F(\theta))}+\sum_{d>1,a\geq 1,b\geq 0}\sum_{1\leq m\leq d}(2+\#T_{d,m}){\rm e}^{-\beta F(d(a\theta+b)+m-1)}d^{-\beta}.

The first series, which corresponds to the case

2.16
Enumeration problems for dessins d’enfant

The enumeration problem for Grothendieck dessins d’enfant was considered in
[
43
,
65
]
. In
[
65
]
it is shown that the generating function for the number of dessins with
assigned ramification profile at
\infty
and given number of preimages of

satisfy the infinite system of PDEs given by the Kadomtsev–Petviashvili (KP)
hierarchy. In
[
43
]
it is shown that this generating function satisfies the Eynard–Orantin
topological recursion. Moreover in
[
4
]
it is shown that the generating function
of the enumeration of dessins d’enfant with fixed genus, degree, and ramification profile
at
\infty
is the partition function of a matrix model, which in the case of clean dessins
agrees with the Kontsevich–Penner model of
[
12
]
. We see here that it is also,
in an immediate and direct way, the partition function of a quantum statistical mechanical
system with algebra of observables given by the (Hopf) algebra
{\mathcal{H}}
constructed above.

Another interesting aspect of the enumeration problem for dessins d’enfant is addressed in
[
38
]
, namely a piecewise polynomiality result and a wall crossing phenomenon. More precisely, the enumeration of (not necessarily connected) dessins d’enfant corresponds to the case of “double strictly monotone Hurwitz numbers” considered in
[
38
]
. In that case, the counting is given by

\displaystyle h_{g;\mu,\nu}=\sum_{\phi}\frac{1}{\#{\rm Aut}(\phi)}

(2.25)

of all isomorphism classes of branched coverings
\phi\colon\Sigma\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
of genus
g
, branched over

\{0,1,\infty\}
with assigned ramification profiles
\mu=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{m})
and

and
\infty
, with degree
\sum_{i}\mu_{i}=d=\sum_{j}\nu_{j}
.
Let

2.17
Additive invariants and partition function

Consider the Hilbert space
\ell^{2}({\mathcal{D}})
generated by the set
{\mathcal{D}}
of all
(not necessarily connected) dessins d’enfant, with the standard orthonormal
basis
\{\epsilon_{D}\}_{D\in{\mathcal{D}}}
and with the action of the algebra
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}}

by
D\cdot\epsilon_{D^{\prime}}=\epsilon_{D\cdot D^{\prime}}
. Since
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}}
is also a Hopf algebra,
we have also an adjoint action of the Hopf algebra on itself, of the form
D\colon D^{\prime}\mapsto\sum\delta^{\prime}D^{\prime}S(\delta^{\prime\prime})

where in Sweedler notation
\Delta(D)=\sum\delta^{\prime}\otimes\delta^{\prime\prime}
, with
S
the antipode,
given by the recursive formula
S(\delta^{\prime\prime})=-\delta^{\prime\prime}+\sum S(\delta_{1}^{\prime\prime})\cdot\delta_{2}^{\prime\prime}
for

\Delta(\delta^{\prime\prime})=\sum\delta_{1}^{\prime\prime}\otimes\delta_{2}^{\prime\prime}
. Given an element
X=\sum_{i}a_{i}D_{i}
with

a_{i}\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
D_{i}\in{\mathcal{D}}
, we write
\epsilon_{X}
for the vector
\epsilon_{X}=\sum_{i}a_{i}\epsilon_{D_{i}}

in
\ell^{2}({\mathcal{D}})
. We can then set
D\cdot_{\Delta}\epsilon_{D^{\prime}}:=\epsilon_{\sum\delta^{\prime}D^{\prime}S(\delta^{\prime\prime})}
.

Let
{\mathcal{N}}
be an additive invariant of dessins d’enfant, that is, an invariant of isomorphism
classes of dessins with the property that it is additive on connected components,

{\mathcal{N}}(D\cdot D^{\prime})={\mathcal{N}}(D)+{\mathcal{N}}(D^{\prime})
. This is the case for invariants such as the genus, the
degree, the ramification indices.

Lemma 2.44
.

An
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
-valued additive invariant
{\mathcal{N}}
of dessins d’enfant determines a time evolution
of the algebra
{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{D}},{\mathbb{C}}}:={\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}{\mathbb{C}}
of the form
\sigma_{t}(D)={\rm e}^{{\rm i}t{\mathcal{N}}(D)}D
,
implemented on
\ell^{2}({\mathcal{D}})
by the Hamiltonian

the generating function of dessins with assigned invariant
{\mathcal{N}}(D)

We have
{\rm e}^{{\rm i}tH}D{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}tH}\epsilon_{D^{\prime}}={\rm e}^{-{\rm i}t{\mathcal{N}}(D^{\prime})}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}t{\mathcal{N}}(D\cdot D^{\prime})}\epsilon_{D\cdot D^{\prime}}=\sigma_{t}(D)\epsilon_{D^{\prime}}
, since
{\mathcal{N}}(D\cdot D^{\prime})={\mathcal{N}}(D)+{\mathcal{N}}(D^{\prime})
. The partition function is given by

\displaystyle Z(\beta)={\rm Tr}\big({\rm e}^{-\beta H}\big)=\sum_{\lambda\in{\mathcal{N}}({\mathcal{D}})}\#\{D\in{\mathcal{D}}\,|\,{\mathcal{N}}(D)=\lambda\}{\rm e}^{-\beta\lambda}.

(2.26)

With the change of variables

, this can be identified with the generating function for
the counting of dessins
D\in{\mathcal{D}}
with assigned value of
{\mathcal{N}}(D)
.
∎

Note that in (

quantum statistical mechanical system, one assumes that the invariant
{\mathcal{N}}
is such that
the series is convergent for sufficiently large
\beta>0
, while the generating function can
be regarded more generally as a formal power series.

Corollary 2.45
.

If the additive invariant
{\mathcal{N}}
of dessins d’enfant also satisfies

It suffices to check that

, using again the property that

for all the terms in the coproduct.
∎

Suppose given a finite set of integer-valued additive invariants

. Consider the
{\mathbb{R}}^{*}_{+}
-valued
additive invariant
{\mathcal{N}}_{\lambda}(D)=\lambda_{1}{\mathcal{N}}_{1}(D)+\cdots+\lambda_{k}{\mathcal{N}}_{k}(D)
, and the time
evolution
\sigma_{t}
determined by
{\mathcal{N}}_{\lambda}
on
{\mathcal{H}}_{D,{\mathbb{C}}}
as in Lemma
2.44
.
If each
{\mathcal{N}}_{i}
also satisfies
{\mathcal{N}}_{i}(D)={\mathcal{N}}_{i}(\delta)+{\mathcal{N}}_{i}(D/\delta)
for all sub-dessins then
\sigma_{t}

is also a Hopf algebra homomorphism as in Corollary
2.45
.

Lemma 2.46
.

For a given set of additive invariants

{\mathcal{N}}=({\mathcal{N}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{N}}_{k})
and a choice of coefficients
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k})

as above, the partition function of the time evolution
\sigma_{t}
computes the generating functions of
dessins d’enfant with fixed invariants
{\mathcal{N}}_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,k
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle Z(\beta)={\rm Tr}\big({\rm e}^{-\beta H}\big)=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathcal{N}}_{\lambda}({\mathcal{D}})}\#\{D\in{\mathcal{D}}\,|\,{\mathcal{N}}_{\lambda}(D)=\alpha\}{\rm e}^{-\beta\alpha},

where
\alpha\in{\mathcal{N}}_{\lambda}({\mathcal{D}})
means that
\alpha=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}n_{i}
with
n_{i}\in{\mathcal{N}}_{i}({\mathcal{D}})
.
Since the
\lambda_{i}
are linearly independent over
{\mathbb{Q}}
, this determines the
n_{i}
and we can write
the sum above as

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}}\#\{D\in{\mathcal{D}}\,|\,{\mathcal{N}}_{i}(D)=n_{i}\}{\rm e}^{-\beta\lambda_{1}n_{1}}\cdots{\rm e}^{-\beta\lambda_{k}n_{k}}.

Upon setting
t_{i}={\rm e}^{-\lambda_{i}}
, we identify this series with the generating function for
dessins with fixed values of the invariants
{\mathcal{N}}_{i}(D)
for
i=1,\ldots,k
.
∎

In particular, we see from this simple general fact that we can reinterpret as partition functions
the generating functions of
[
43
]
and
[
65
]
for the number of dessins with assigned
ramification profile at
\infty
and given number of preimages of
0
and
1
, as well as the
generating function of
[
38
]
of dessins of genus
g

with assigned ramification profiles over
0
and
\infty
.

While this system recovers the correct partition function that encodes the counting
problem for dessins, since the representation of the algebra
D\cdot\epsilon_{D^{\prime}}=\epsilon_{D\cdot D^{\prime}}

on the Hilbert space is a simple translation of the basis elements, we do not have an interesting
class of low temperature KMS states, unlike the case we discussed in the previous subsections.

2.18
Fibered product structure on dessins

This subsection together with the subsequent Sections
2.19

and
2.20
contain a short digression on a construction
of dynamics and partition functions based on different product
structures on dessins.

We describe here a possible variant of the construction presented in the previous
subsection, where instead of considering the (Hopf) algebra
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}}
of not
necessarily connected dessins, where the multiplication is given by the disjoint
union, we consider a commutative algebra of dessins based on a different
product structure built using the fibered product of the Belyi maps. The resulting
construction of an associated quantum statistical mechanical system is similar
to the previous case, but the associated partition function will have here the
structure of a Dirichlet series rather than the usual power series generating
function for the counting of dessins.

A product operation on dessins induced by the fibered product of the Belyi maps
was discussed in
[
58
]
.
Let
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
be two dessins with
f_{i}\colon X_{i}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}({\mathbb{C}})
the associated Belyi maps.
Let
Y
denote the desingularization of the fibered product
\tilde{Y}=X_{1}\times_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}({\mathbb{C}})}X_{2}
,
fibered along the Belyi maps
f_{i}
, and let
f\colon Y\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}({\mathbb{C}})
denote the resulting branched covering map. Consider the graph
D
given by the preimage
f^{-1}({\mathcal{I}})
in
Y
. The graph
D
can be combinatorially
described in terms of
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
, with bipartite set of vertices

V_{0}(D)=V_{0}(D_{1})\times V_{0}(D_{2})
and
V_{1}(D)=V_{1}(D_{1})\times V_{1}(D_{2})
and with set of edges
given by all pairs of edges
(e_{1},e_{2})\in E(D_{1})\times E(D_{2})
with endpoints in
V(D)
. We denote
the fibered product of dessins by
D=D_{1}\star D_{2}
. We correspondingly write
f=f_{1}\star f_{2}

for the fibered product of the Belyi maps. Under this fibered product operations, the degree is multiplicative

d=d_{1}d_{2}
, and so are the ramifications
m=\#V_{0}(D)=m_{1}m_{2}
and
n=\#V_{1}(D)=n_{1}n_{2}

and the ramification profiles
\mu_{i}=\mu_{i,1}\mu_{i,2}
and
\nu_{j}=\nu_{j,1}\nu_{j,2}
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
be the algebra over
{\mathbb{Q}}
generated by the dessins
D
with the fibered product as above.
Equivalently we think of elements of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
as functions with
finite support
a\colon{\mathcal{D}}\to{\mathbb{Q}}
from the set
{\mathcal{D}}
of dessins, with the convolution product

a_{1}\star a_{2}(D)=\sum\limits_{D=D_{1}\star D_{2}}a_{1}(D_{1})a_{2}(D_{2})
.
The resulting convolution algebra
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
is commutative.
We let
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{C}}={\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}{\mathbb{C}}
be the complex algebra obtained by change of coefficients.
The generators of the algebra
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
are those dessins
D
that admit no non-trivial
fibered product decomposition
D=D_{1}\star D_{2}
, which we refer to as
“indecomposible dessins”.

2.19
Arrangements and semigroup laws

We now consider the commutative semigroups
{\mathcal{S}}:=\oplus_{n\geq 1}{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
and
{\mathcal{S}}\oplus{\mathcal{S}}
,
with the product of
(\mu,\nu)\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m}\oplus{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
and
(\mu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m^{\prime}}\oplus{\mathbb{N}}^{n^{\prime}}
given
by
(\mu\mu^{\prime},\nu\nu^{\prime})
with
(\mu\mu^{\prime}=(\mu_{i}\mu^{\prime}_{i^{\prime}})_{(i,i^{\prime})},\nu\nu^{\prime}=(\nu_{j}\nu^{\prime}_{j^{\prime}}))\in{\mathbb{N}}^{mm^{\prime}}\oplus{\mathbb{N}}^{nn^{\prime}}
.
We restrict this semigroup law to the arrangements
{\mathcal{H}}(m,n)
considered in
[
38
]
.

Lemma 2.47
.

Given
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
let
{\mathcal{H}}(m,n)=\Big\{(\mu,\nu)\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m}\oplus{\mathbb{N}}^{n}\,|\,\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\mu_{i}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\mu_{j}\Big\}
. The arrangements
{\mathcal{H}}(m,n)

determine a subsemigroup
{\mathcal{H}}_{deg}\subset{\mathcal{S}}\otimes{\mathcal{S}}
.

Proof.

With
{\mathcal{H}}_{deg}=\oplus_{m,n}{\mathcal{H}}(m,n)\subset{\mathcal{S}}\otimes{\mathcal{S}}
we see that
for
(\mu,\nu)\in{\mathcal{H}}(m,n)
and
(\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{H}}(m^{\prime},n^{\prime})
we have

(\mu\mu^{\prime},\nu\nu^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{H}}(mm^{\prime},nn^{\prime})
with
\sum_{(i,i^{\prime})}\mu_{i}\mu^{\prime}_{i^{\prime}}=d\cdot d^{\prime}=\sum_{(j,j^{\prime})}\nu_{j}\nu^{\prime}_{j"}
, where
\sum_{i}\mu_{i}=d=\sum_{j}\nu_{j}
and

\sum_{i^{\prime}}\mu^{\prime}_{i^{\prime}}=d^{\prime}=\sum_{j^{\prime}}\nu^{\prime}_{j^{\prime}}
.
∎

This simple fact shows that the data of the ramification profiles at two of the three
ramification points, say at
0
and
\infty
, of the dessins can be arranged as a
multiplicative semigroup structure. This semigroup operation is consistent with the
algebra operation in
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
given by the fibered product.

Lemma 2.48
.

The degree, the ramification profiles and the ramification
indices over the points
\{0,1,\infty\}
are multiplicative with respect to the fibered product operation of Belyi functions
f\colon\Sigma\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
. If
\mu=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{m})

and
\nu=(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{n})
are the ramification profiles over
0
and
\infty
,
then the ramification index
r
at
1
satisfies
r=\chi(\Sigma)-\chi(D)
, where

\chi
is the topological Euler characteristic.

Proof.

We have already seen in the construction of the fibered product
dessin
D=D_{1}\star D_{2}
in the previous subsection that the ramification
indices and the ramification profiles behave multiplicatively. By the Riemann–Hurwitz
formula the Euler characteristic
\chi(\Sigma)
satisfies

\displaystyle\chi(\Sigma)=d\cdot\chi\big({\mathbb{P}}^{1}\big)-\sum_{P\in\Sigma}(e_{P}-1),

where the sum is taken over the ramification points with the corresponding
ramification index, so we have

\displaystyle\chi(\Sigma)=2d+\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\mu_{i}-1)+\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\nu_{j}-1)+\sum_{k=1}^{r}(\rho_{k}-1)=-d+m+n+r,

where
\rho=(\rho_{k})_{k=1}^{r}
is the ramification profile over the point
1
.
Since
m+n=\#V(D)
and
d=\#E(D)
we have
-d+m+n=\chi(D)
.
∎

The relation between the ramification index
r
and the Euler characteristics

\chi(\Sigma)
and
\chi(D)
shows that the enumeration of dessins with fixed

\mu
,
\nu
,
g
as in
[
38
]
can be reformulated as the enumeration of
dessins with fixed
\mu
,
\nu
,
r
. The advantage of this formulation is that the
assignment of the data
(\mu,\nu,r)
is multiplicative with respect to the
fibered product operation on Belyi functions.

2.20
Multiplicative invariants and partition function

Let
\Upsilon
be a multiplicative invariant of dessins d’enfant, that is, an
invariant with the property that
\Upsilon(D\star D^{\prime})=\Upsilon(D)\star\Upsilon(D^{\prime})
,
where
D\star D^{\prime}
is the fibered product. We assume that
\Upsilon
takes values in
a group, a semigroup, or an algebra.

As in the previous subsections, we consider the Hilbert space
\ell^{2}({\mathcal{D}})
.
The algebra
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
acts by bounded operators with

D\cdot\epsilon_{D^{\prime}}=\epsilon_{D\star D^{\prime}}
.

Lemma 2.49
.

An
{\mathbb{N}}
-valued multiplicative invariant
\Upsilon
of dessins d’enfant determines
a time evolution on the algebra
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}{\mathbb{C}}
of the form

\sigma_{t}(D)=\Upsilon(D)^{{\rm i}t}D
, implemented by the Hamiltonian

H\epsilon_{D}=\log(\Upsilon(D))\epsilon_{D}
. The partition function of this
quantum statistical mechanical system is a Dirichlet series with coefficients
enumerating dessins with assigned invariant
\Upsilon(D)
.

Proof.

We have
{\rm e}^{{\rm i}tH}D{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}tH}\epsilon_{D^{\prime}}=\Upsilon(D^{\prime})^{-{\rm i}t}\Upsilon(D\star D^{\prime})^{{\rm i}t}\epsilon_{D\star D^{\prime}}=\sigma_{t}(D)\epsilon_{D^{\prime}}
, by the multiplicative property. The partition function is given by the formal
Dirichlet series

\displaystyle Z(\beta)={\rm Tr}\big({\rm e}^{-\beta H}\big)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\#\{D\in{\mathcal{D}}\,|\,\Upsilon(D)=n\}n^{-\beta}.\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qed

(2.27)

In particular, since the ramification profiles, ramification indices and degree behave
multiplicatively with respect to the fibered product of dessins, we obtain a partition
function as above, associated to the counting problem of
[
38
]
, where the
multiplicative invariants take values in the semigroup
{\mathcal{H}}_{\deg}
of Lemma
2.47
.

Proposition 2.50
.

The partition function (
2.27
) associated to the counting problem of
[
38
]
is
given by

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n,r}\sum_{(\mu,\nu)\in{\mathcal{H}}(m,n)}h_{g;\mu,\nu}r^{-s}\mu_{1}^{-s_{1}}\cdots\mu_{m}^{-s_{m}}\nu_{1}^{-\sigma_{1}}\cdots\nu_{n}^{-\sigma_{n}}

(2.28)

with the
h_{g;\mu,\nu}
as in (
2.25
) and with

{\mathcal{H}}(m,n)=\big\{(\mu,\nu)\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m}\times{\mathbb{N}}^{n}\,|\,\sum_{i}\mu_{i}=\sum_{j}\nu_{j}\big\}
.

Proof.

First observe that by Lemma
2.48
we can express the genus
g
as a function
of the multiplicative invariants
d
,
r
,
m
,
n
, hence we can identify the counting
function
h_{g;\mu,\nu}
of (
2.25
) with a function
h_{r;\mu,\nu}
. The piecewise
polynomiality result of
[
38
]
shows that the
h_{r;\mu,\nu}
are polynomials

P_{d,r,C}(\mu,\nu)
in the variables
(\mu,\nu)
of degree
2d+1-2r-m-n
,
for all
(\mu,\nu)
in a fixed chamber
C
of the arrangement
{\mathcal{H}}(m,n)
.
Let
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}
and
\kappa_{1},\ldots,\kappa_{n}
and
\lambda
be coefficients
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{*}_{+}
such that, for all integers
r,a_{i},b_{j}>1
with
\sum_{i}a_{i}=\sum_{j}b_{j}
,
the products

r^{\lambda}a_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots a_{m}^{\alpha_{m}}b_{1}^{\kappa_{1}}\cdots b_{n}^{\kappa_{n}}\neq 1
.
Then given multiplicative invariants
(\mu,\nu)\in{\mathcal{H}}_{deg}
we can form
a single multiplicative invariant

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{m,n}(D):=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\mu_{i}(D)^{\alpha_{i}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\nu_{j}(D)^{\kappa_{j}},

such that
\big\{D\,|\,\Upsilon_{m,n}(D)=r^{\lambda}a_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots a_{m}^{\alpha_{m}}b_{1}^{\kappa_{1}}\cdots b_{n}^{\kappa_{n}}\big\}=\{D\,|\,\mu_{i}(D)=a_{i},\,\nu_{j}(D)=b_{j}\}
.
Given a sequence
\{\alpha_{i},\kappa_{j}\}
that satisfies the property above for
given

,

,
n
we obtain a time evolution
\sigma_{t}(D)=\Upsilon_{m(D),n(D)}(D)^{{\rm i}t}D

on
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{C}}={\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}{\mathbb{C}}
with partition function given by the formal Dirichlet series

\displaystyle Z(\beta)=\sum_{D\in{\mathcal{D}}}\Upsilon_{m(D),n(D)}(D)^{-\beta}=\sum_{r,m,n}h_{r,m,n;\mu,\nu}r^{-\beta\lambda}\mu_{1}^{-\beta\alpha_{1}}\cdots\mu_{m}^{-\beta\alpha_{m}}\nu_{1}^{-\beta\kappa_{1}}\cdots\nu_{n}^{-\beta\kappa_{n}}.

This agrees with (
2.28
) for
s=\beta\lambda
,
s_{i}=\beta\alpha_{i}
and
\sigma_{j}=\beta\kappa_{j}
.
∎

3
Bost–Connes crossed product algebras

and the Grothendieck–Teichmüller groupoid

The basic object of combinatorial topology related to the set of Belyi maps

f\colon\Sigma\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, is the fundamental groupoid of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}

classifying (modulo homotopy) oriented paths between pairs of, say,
algebraic points of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}({\mathbb{C}})\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}
.

For studying its interactions with the absolute Galois group, it is convenient
to restrict the set of base points to Deligne’s base points at
0
,
1
and
\infty
that is,
real tangent directions to these points, as explained in
[
40
,
41
]
, and earlier,
although in a less explicit form, in
[
31
]
.

This groupoid can be visualized via the
Grothendieck–Teichmüller
group: product of two cyclic sugroups
generated by loops around
0
and
1
respectively, and connecting them involution
generated by a path from
0
to
1
. We will sometimes refer to this involution
as
hidden symmetry
, or else
Drinfeld–Ihara
involution.

This section is dedicated to the constructions of quantum statistical mechanical
systems associated to the absolute Galois group
G={\rm Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Q}})
.
They transfer to the Grothendieck–Teichmüller environment
versions of the Bost–Connes algebras considered in
[
55
]
.

We will start with a description of the
combinatorial version of the (profinite) Grothendieck–Teichmüller group
{\bf mGT}

as the automorphism group of the genus zero modular operad, as was done in
[
16
]
.

3.1
Bost–Connes algebra with Drinfeld–Ihara involution

The group
{\bf mGT}
was defined in
[
16
]
in the following way.
One starts with the same projective limit as in the Bost–Connes endomotive (
[
17
, Section 3.3]
,
and
[
55
, Section 2.1]
).
Put
X_{n}={\rm Spec}({\mathbb{Q}}[{\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}}])
with projections
X_{n}\to X_{m}
when
m|n
ordered
by divisibility, and consider first the limit
X=\varprojlim_{n}X_{s}={\rm Spec}({\mathbb{Q}}[{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}])
.

We then enrich the action of the automorphism group
\hat{\mathbb{Z}}^{*}
on
X
and on the Bost–Connes
algebra
{\mathbb{Q}}[{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}]\rtimes{\mathbb{N}}
corresponding to the
actions of the groups
{\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}}^{*}
of the
X_{n}
, with
the further symmetries
\theta_{n}
of
X_{n}
. Concretely,
the map
\theta_{n}
is the involution of
X_{n}=\{0,\ldots,n-1\}

given by
k\mapsto n-k+1
.

The non-abelian group
{\bf mGT}_{n}
is defined as the subgroup
of the symmetric group
S_{n}
generated by
{\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}}^{*}
and
\theta_{n}
. One defines the group

{\bf mGT}=\varprojlim_{n}{\bf mGT}_{n}

Lemma 3.1
.

The involutions
\theta_{n}\colon X_{n}\to X_{n}
are compatible with the
maps
\sigma_{m}\colon X_{nm}\to X_{n}
of the projective system of the Bost–Connes endomotive, namely
\sigma_{m}\circ\theta_{nm}=\theta_{n}\circ\sigma_{m}
.
They determine an involution
\theta\colon X\to X
on the projective limit.

Proof.

If we identify
X_{n}
with the set of roots of unity of
order
n
, this involution can be equivalently written as
\theta_{n}(\zeta)=\zeta_{n}\cdot\zeta^{-1}
,
where
\zeta_{n}=\exp(2\pi{\rm i}/n)
. Also,
identifying the set
X_{n}
with roots of unity of order
n
, the maps

\sigma_{m}\colon X_{nm}\to X_{n}
are given by raising to the
m
-th power,

\sigma_{m}(\zeta)=\zeta^{m}
. Thus, we have
\sigma_{m}(\theta_{nm}(\zeta))=\zeta_{nm}^{m}\cdot\big(\zeta^{-1}\big)^{m}=\theta_{n}(\zeta^{m})=\theta_{n}(\sigma_{m}(\zeta))
.
∎

We refer to the map
\theta\colon X\to X
as the Drinfeld–Ihara involution of the
Bost–Connes endomotive mentioned above. The following reformulation of this involution in terms of its action on the Bost–Connes algebra then follows directly.

Proposition 3.2
.

The involution
\theta\colon X\to X
described above induces a self-map of the
arithmetic Bost–Connes algebra
{\mathbb{Q}}[{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}]\rtimes{\mathbb{N}}
,
which acts by
\theta(e(r))=e\big(\frac{1-a}{b}\big)
on generators

e(r)
with
r\in{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
,
r=a/b
for integers with
(a,b)=1
.

The group
{\bf mGT}
acts on the Bost–Connes quantum statistical mechanical
system compatibly with the time evolution and preserving the arithmetic subalgebra.

Proof.

The first statement follows directly from Lemma
3.1
, which determines
the action of
\theta
on the generators
e(r)
of the abelian subalgebra
{\mathbb{Q}}[{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}]
.
The action on the arithmetic Bost–Connes algebra
{\mathbb{Q}}[{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}]\rtimes{\mathbb{N}}
is then
determined by letting
\theta
act as the identity on the remaining generators
\mu_{n}
.

This action extends to an action on the Bost–Connes
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})\rtimes{\mathbb{N}}
,
which preserves the arithmetic subalgebra. This action
is compatible with the time evolution since the action is trivial on the
generators
\mu_{n}
so that
\theta\circ\sigma_{t}=\sigma_{t}\circ\theta
.

The group
{\bf mGT}
is generated by elements of
\hat{\mathbb{Z}}^{*}
and
the Ihara involution
\theta
. In fact, we can write arbitrary elements in
{\bf mGT}

as sequences
\theta^{\epsilon_{0}}\gamma_{1}\theta\gamma_{2}\theta\cdots\theta\gamma_{n}\theta^{\epsilon_{1}}

for
\epsilon_{0},\epsilon_{1}\in\{0,1\}
and with the
\gamma_{k}\in\hat{\mathbb{Z}}^{*}
.
The group
\hat{\mathbb{Z}}^{*}
acts by automorphisms of the Bost–Connes quantum statistical
mechanical system with
\gamma\circ\sigma_{t}=\sigma_{t}\circ\gamma
, preserving
the abelian subalgebra. Since the action of
\theta
also has these properties, we
obtain an action of
{\bf mGT}
as symmetries of the Bost–Connes quantum statistical mechanical system.
∎

To be more precise, the effect of enlarging the group of symmetries from
\hat{\mathbb{Z}}^{*}
to
{\bf mGT}

implies that we can consider a larger family of covariant representations of
the Bost–Connes system on the same Hilbert space
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{N}})
, parameterized
by elements of
{\bf mGT}
, by setting, for
\alpha\in{\bf mGT}
,

\displaystyle\pi_{\alpha}(e(r))\epsilon_{n}=\alpha(\zeta_{r})^{n}\epsilon_{n},

where
\zeta_{r}
is the image of
r\in{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
under a fixed embedding of
the abstract roots of unity
{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
in
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
. The isometries
\mu_{n}
from
[
55
]
act in the
usual way,
\mu_{m}\epsilon_{n}=\epsilon_{nm}
. This change does not affect the
generating Hamiltonian of the time evolution, nor the corresponding partition
function given by the Riemann zeta function. The low temperature Gibbs
states are still given by polylogarithm functions evaluated at roots of unity,
normalized by the Riemann zeta function. However, when evaluating
zero temperature KMS states on elements of the arithmetic subalgebra,
we now have an action of the larger group
{\bf mGT}
on the values
in
{\mathbb{Q}}^{ab}
. This action by a non-abelian group no longer has a Galois
interpretation as an action on roots of unity, hence it does not directly give
us a way to extend the Bost–Connes system to non-abelian Galois theory.

In the remaining part of this section, we will show that there are other possible
ways of incorporating the Drinfeld–Ihara involution in a construction generalizing the
Bost–Connes algebra that lead to more interesting changes to the
structure of the resulting quantum statistical mechanical system.

3.2
Crossed product algebras and field extensions

The variant of the Bost–Connes quantum statistical mechanical system
constructed in
[
55
]
was aimed at merging two different aspects of
{\mathbb{F}}_{1}
-geometry: the
relation described in
[
18
]
of the integral Bost–Connes algebra to the
extensions
{\mathbb{F}}_{1^{m}}
of
[
42
]
, and the analytic functions over
{\mathbb{F}}_{1}

constructed in
[
53
]
. The modified Bost–Connes algebra considered in
[
55
]
involves an action of endomorphisms on the Habiro ring of
[
37
]
.

We consider here a simpler variant of the algebra of
[
55
]
, which will enable us
to present naturally the transition from abelian to non-abelian Galois groups
in a form that recovers the embedding of the absolute Galois group in the
Grothendieck–Teichmüller group as described by Ihara in
[
41
]
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathbb{Q}}={\mathbb{Q}}[t^{r};r\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{+}]
be the polynomial algebra in rational powers of a variable
t
.
For
s\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{+}^{*}
let
\sigma_{s}
denote the action
\sigma_{s}(f)(t)=f(t^{s})
for
f\in{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Thus, we can form the group crossed product algebra
{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rtimes{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{+}
.

The subsemigroup
{\mathbb{N}}
of the group
{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{+}
induces an action by endomorphisms on
the subalgebra
{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathbb{Q}}={\mathbb{Q}}[t]
by
\sigma_{n}(f)(t)=f(t^{n})
. Note that, unlike
the morphisms
\sigma_{n}
of the Bost–Connes system, acting by endomorphisms of

{\mathbb{Q}}[{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}]
, which are surjective but not injective, in this case the morphisms
\sigma_{n}

acting on
{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
are injective.

Consider, as in the case of the original
Bost–Connes algebra, generators
\mu_{n}
and
\mu_{n}^{*}
, for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, satisfying
the relations
\mu_{n}\mu_{m}=\mu_{nm}
,
\mu_{n}^{*}\mu_{m}^{*}=\mu^{*}_{nm}
,
\mu_{n}^{*}\mu_{n}=1
,
and in the case where
(n,m)=1
also
\mu_{n}\mu_{m}^{*}=\mu_{m}^{*}\mu_{n}
.
Consider the algebra generated by
{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
and the
\mu_{n}
,
\mu_{n}^{*}
with the relations

\mu_{n}^{*}f=\sigma_{n}(f)\mu_{n}^{*}
and
f\mu_{n}=\mu_{n}\sigma_{n}(f)
. The elements

\pi_{n}=\mu_{n}\mu_{n}^{*}
satisfy
\pi_{n}^{2}=\pi_{n}=\pi_{n}^{*}
and
\pi_{n}f=f\pi_{n}
for all
f\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathbb{Q}}

and all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Unlike the Bost–Connes case, the
\pi_{n}
are not elements of the algebra
{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathbb{Q}}

on which the semigroup is acting.

Denote by
\rho_{n}
the endomorphisms
\rho_{n}(f):=\mu_{n}f\mu_{n}^{*}
.
They satisfy the relations
\sigma_{n}(\rho_{n}(f))=f
and

\rho_{n}(\sigma_{n}(f))=\pi_{n}f
. The map that sends
\mu_{n}f\mu_{n}^{*}
to
f(t^{1/n})
identifies
the direct limit of the injective maps
\sigma_{n}\colon{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathbb{Q}}\to{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
with
{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
on which
the action of the
\sigma_{n}
becomes an action by automorphisms. This leads to the
crossed product algebra
{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rtimes{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{+}
.

Furthermore, in
[
55
]
there was considered the
inverse limit
\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathbb{Q}}=\varprojlim_{N}{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathcal{J}}_{N}
with respect to the ideals
{\mathcal{J}}_{N}

generated by
(t^{r})_{N}=(1-t^{r})\cdots\big(1-t^{rN}\big)
for
r\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{+}
, which is modelled on the Habiro
ring construction of
[
37
]
. Here instead we replace
{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
and
{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
with the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-algebras

{\mathcal{A}}_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}}=\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\{\{t\}\}
, given by the algebraically closed
field of formal Puiseux series
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\{\{t\}\}=\cup_{N\geq 1}\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\big(\big(t^{1/N}\big)\big)
, and
the field of rational functions
{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathbb{Q}}=\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(t)
, respectively.

3.3
The Drinfeld–Ihara hidden symmetry involution

The involution
t\mapsto 1-t
maps isomorphically
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\{\{t\}\}\to\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\{\{1-t\}\}
,
and as in
[
41
]
we denote by
M
and
M^{\prime}
, the maximal Galois extensions of

\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(t)
unramified outside of
\{0,1,\infty\}
inside
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\{\{t\}\}
and in
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\{\{1-t\}\}
, respectively, with the involution mapping
M\to M^{\prime}
. The absolute Galois group
G_{\mathbb{Q}}={\rm Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Q}})
acts on
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\{\{t\}\}
and
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\{\{1-t\}\}
by acting on the Puiseux coefficients and induces actions on
M
and
M^{\prime}
. Moreover, as discussed in
[
41
]
, the Galois group
{\rm Gal}\big(M/\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(t)\big)
can be identified with the profinite fundamental group
\hat{F}_{2}=\hat{\pi}_{1}\big({\mathbb{P}}^{1}\smallsetminus\{0,1,\infty\},(0,1)\big)
,
where
F_{2}=\pi_{1}\big({\mathbb{P}}^{1}\smallsetminus\{0,1,\infty\},(0,1)\big)
is the free group on two generators.

As observed in
[
41
]
, the maximal abelian subextension of
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(t)
in
M
is generated by
all the elements
t^{1/N}
and
(1-t)^{1/N}
. Thus, we can view a slightly modified version of the
construction of the crossed product algebra
{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rtimes{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{+}
mentioned above as a
construction of this maximal abelian extension, just like the original Bost–Connes system can be
regarded as a noncommutative geometry construction of the maximal abelian extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}
.

3.4
Semigroup action and maximal abelian extension

More precisely, we need to modify the above crossed product algebra construction to account
for the missing involution
t\leftrightarrow 1-t
. This can be done by replacing the polynomial algebra

{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathbb{Q}}={\mathbb{Q}}[t]
with
{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathbb{Q}}=\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(t)
and, in addition to the endomorphisms
\sigma_{n}(f)(t)=f(t^{n})

considering an extra generator
\tau(f)(t)=f(1-t)
.

Definition 3.3
.

Denote by
{\mathcal{S}}
the non-abelian semigroup given by the free product
{\mathcal{S}}={\mathbb{N}}\star{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
. Its elements
can be written in the form

\displaystyle\mu_{s}=F^{\epsilon_{0}}\mu_{n_{1}}F\mu_{n_{2}}F\cdots F\mu_{n_{k}}F^{\epsilon_{1}},

with
s=s(\epsilon_{0},\epsilon_{1},\underline{n})
for

\epsilon_{0},\epsilon_{1}\in\{0,1\}
and
\underline{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})
.

Denote by
{\mathcal{S}}_{0}
and
{\mathcal{S}}_{1}
the two abelian sub-semigroups given, respectively, by
{\mathcal{S}}_{0}={\mathbb{N}}

and
{\mathcal{S}}_{1}=F{\mathbb{N}}F=\{\tilde{\mu}_{n}:=F\mu_{n}F\,|\,n\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
.

The following statement is obtained by directly adapting the original argument of
[
55
]
as
discussed in the subsection
3.2
above.

Lemma 3.4
.

The sub-semigroups
{\mathcal{S}}_{0}
and
{\mathcal{S}}_{1}
define the action of endomorphisms
\sigma_{n}
and
\tilde{\sigma}_{n}
upon
{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathbb{Q}}=\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(t)
, with partial inverses
\rho_{n}
and
\tilde{\rho}_{n}
respectively.

The direct limits with respect to
injective endomorphisms
\sigma_{n}
and
\tilde{\sigma}_{n}
can be identified, respectively, with the crossed
product algebras
{\mathcal{B}}_{{\mathbb{Q}},0}\rtimes{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{+}
and
{\mathcal{B}}_{{\mathbb{Q}},1}\rtimes{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{+}
, where

{\mathcal{B}}_{{\mathbb{Q}},0}=\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(t^{r};r\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{+})

and
{\mathcal{B}}_{{\mathbb{Q}},1}=\bar{\mathbb{Q}}((1-t)^{r};r\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{+})
.

Proof.

The elements
\tilde{\mu}_{n}=F\mu_{n}F
, with
\tilde{\mu}_{n}^{*}=F\mu_{n}^{*}F
, satisfy

\tilde{\mu}_{n}^{*}f\tilde{\mu}_{n}=\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(f)
. Here
\tilde{\sigma}_{n}=\tau\circ\sigma_{n}\circ\tau

is the semigroup action
\tilde{\sigma}_{nm}=\tilde{\sigma}_{n}\circ\tilde{\sigma}_{m}
given by

\tilde{\sigma}_{n}(f)(t)=f\big(1-(1-t)^{n}\big)
. Correspondingly we have
\tilde{\mu}_{n}f\tilde{\mu}_{n}^{*}=\tilde{\rho}_{n}(f)
with
\tilde{\rho}_{n}(f)(t)=f\big(1-(1-t)^{1/n}\big)
.
∎

Together with the description of
[
41
]
of the maximal abelian subextension of
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(t)
in
M

as generated by all the elements
t^{1/N}
and
(1-t)^{1/N}
(see also
[
51
]
), we then obtain
this maximal abelian subextension in the following way.

Proposition 3.5
.

Consider the algebras
{\mathcal{B}}_{{\mathbb{Q}},0}
and
{\mathcal{B}}_{{\mathbb{Q}},1}
of Lemma
3.4
as
subalgebras of
M
. They together generate the maximal abelian subextension of
M
.

3.5
Semigroup action and non-abelian extensions

We now consider the semigroup action of the full
{\mathcal{S}}={\mathbb{N}}\star{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
and the endomorphisms
\sigma_{s}
of
{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathbb{Q}}=\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(t)
, given by
\sigma_{s}(f)=\mu_{s}^{*}f\mu_{s}
with partial inverses
\rho_{s}(f)=\mu_{s}f\mu_{s}^{*}
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Consider the
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
-algebra generated by the field of rational functions
{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathbb{Q}}=\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(t)

and an additional generator
F
satisfying relations
F=F^{*}
,
F^{2}={\rm Id}
, and

FfF=\tau(f)
, for all
f\in\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(t)
.

The resulting algebra is a group crossed product
\tilde{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathbb{Q}}={\mathcal{B}}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rtimes{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
,
where the
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
action is the Drinfeld–Ihara involution
t\mapsto 1-t
.

Proof.

The generator
F
is a sign operator since it satisfies
F=F^{*}
,
F^{2}={\rm Id}
,
and it implements the Drinfeld–Ihara involution by the relation
FfF=\tau(f)
, hence we obtain
the action of
{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rtimes{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
as superalgebra.
∎

Consider now the
\bar{{\mathbb{Q}}}
-algebra generated by the field of rational functions
{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathbb{Q}}=\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(t)

and a set of generators
\mu_{n}
,
\mu_{n}^{*}
with relations

\displaystyle\mu_{n}\mu_{m}=\mu_{nm},\qquad\mu_{n}^{*}\mu_{m}^{*}=\mu^{*}_{nm},\qquad\mu_{n}^{*}\mu_{n}=1

for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, as well as

\displaystyle\mu_{n}\mu_{m}^{*}=\mu_{m}^{*}\mu_{n}

for
(n,m)=1
, and

\displaystyle\mu_{n}^{*}f=\sigma_{n}(f)\mu_{n}^{*},\qquad f\mu_{n}=\mu_{n}\sigma_{n}(f)

for all
f\in\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(t)
and all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Moreover, include an additional generator
F
satisfying

\displaystyle F=F^{*},\qquad F^{2}={\rm Id},\qquad FfF=\tau(f)

for all
f\in\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(t)
.

This
\bar{{\mathbb{Q}}}
-algebra contains the group crossed product
{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rtimes{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
of Lemma
3.6
and the full semigroup
{\mathcal{S}}={\mathbb{N}}\star{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
. In fact the same argument as in Section
3.2
shows that we can describe this algebra in terms of a direct limit over the endomorphisms
\sigma_{s}
acting on
{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
, where the action in the limit becomes implemented by automorphisms, so that the resulting algebra is a group crossed product by
\Upsilon={\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{+}\star{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Lemma 3.7
.

For an element
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
and for
f(t)=t
consider the function
\rho_{s}(t)
. This determines a curve
\Sigma_{s}
which is a branched covering of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
branched at

\{0,1,\infty\}
, with field of functions
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(\Sigma_{s})
given by the smallest finite
Galois extension of
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(t))
unramified outside of
\{0,1,\infty\}
that contains
\rho_{s}(t)
, with Galois group
G_{s}:={\rm Gal}\big(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(\Sigma_{s})/\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(t)\big)
.

Proof.

Using notation of the Definition
3.3
, we can write
s
in the form

s=s(\epsilon_{0},\epsilon_{1},\underline{n})
for some
\epsilon_{0},\epsilon_{1}\in\{0,1\}
and some
\underline{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})
. Then the element
z=\rho_{s}(t)
is given by
\rho_{s}(t)=\tau^{\epsilon_{0}}\rho_{n_{1}}\tau\rho_{n_{2}}\cdots\tau\rho_{n_{k}}\tau^{\epsilon_{1}}
. It satisfies a polynomial equation of the form
\tau^{\epsilon_{0}}\sigma_{n_{1}}\tau\cdots\tau\sigma_{n_{k}}\tau^{\epsilon_{1}}(z)=t
. For example, for
s=\mu_{n}F\mu_{m}F
we have
z=\rho_{s}(t)=1-\big(1-t^{1/n}\big)^{1/m}
satisfying
\big(1-(1-z)^{m}\big)^{n}=t
, while for
s=\mu_{n}F\mu_{m}
we have
z=\rho_{s}(t)=\big(1-t^{1/n}\big)^{1/m}
with
\big(z^{m}-1\big)^{n}=t
. Thus, the element
\rho_{s}(t)
detemines a branched covering of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
with branch locus in the set
\{0,1,\infty\}
.

Any finite field extension of
{\mathbb{C}}(t)
unramified outside of
\{0,1,\infty\}
determines a compact complex Riemann surface
\Sigma_{s}
with a branched covering of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
unramified outside of these three points. Such
\Sigma_{s}
is defined over a number field and descends to an algebraically closed fields
(see the descent technique described in
[
51

).
This gives the required result for the field of functions
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(\Sigma_{s})
.
∎

Proposition 3.8
.

The fields
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(\Sigma_{s})
form a direct system over the semigroup
{\mathcal{S}}

ordered by divisibility. The direct limit
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(\Sigma_{\mathcal{S}}):=\varinjlim_{s\in{\mathcal{S}}}\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(\Sigma_{s})
,
with symmetries
G_{\mathcal{S}}=\varprojlim_{s\in{\mathcal{S}}}G_{s}
, contains all functions of the form
(1-t^{r})^{r^{\prime}}
for
r,r^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{+}
. The resulting action of
{\mathcal{S}}

on the direct limit
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(\Sigma_{\mathcal{S}})
becomes invertible and extends to an action of the
enveloping group
\Upsilon={\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{+}\star{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Proof.

For all
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
, the morphisms
\sigma_{s}
acting on
{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
are injective.
This can be seen by explicitly writing

\displaystyle\sigma_{s}(f)=\tau^{\epsilon_{0}}\sigma_{n_{1}}\tau\sigma_{n_{2}}\cdots\tau\sigma_{n_{k}}\tau^{\epsilon_{1}}(f),

for an element
s=s(\epsilon_{0},\epsilon_{1},\underline{n})\in{\mathcal{S}}
, where both the involution
\tau

and the endomorphisms
\sigma_{n_{i}}
are injective. The map
f\mapsto\sigma_{s}(f)
gives an
embedding of
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(t)
inside
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(\Sigma_{s})
. Thus, on the limit
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(\Sigma_{\mathcal{S}})
the
morphisms
\sigma_{s}
become invertible and the field
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(\Sigma_{\mathcal{S}})
contains all the

\rho_{s}(f)
for all
f\in\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(t)
. In particular, it then contains all the functions of the form

(1-t^{r})^{r^{\prime}}
for
r,r^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{+}
.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

By viewing
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(\Sigma_{s})
as a subfield of

\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\{\{t\}\}
(or of

with

(0,1)_{\mathbb{C}}={\mathbb{C}}\smallsetminus((-\infty,0]\cup[1,\infty))
considered in
[

, with a convergent Puiseux series expansion on
(0,1)
, where all
the
(1-t^{r})^{r^{\prime}}

3.6
Quantum statistical mechanics of
\boldsymbol{{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{+}}

The definition of a quantum statistical mechanical system associated to this construction, relies on
the general setting for a quantum statistical mechanics for the group
{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{+}
described in
[
57
]
, which we recall briefly here, adapted to our environment.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}=\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{+})
be the Hilbert space with canonical orthonormal basis
\{\epsilon_{r}\}_{r\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{+}}
. For
r=p_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots p_{k}^{n_{k}}
the prime decomposition of
r\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{+}
with
p_{i}

distinct primes and
n_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, consider the densely defined unbounded linear operator

\displaystyle H\epsilon_{r}=(|n_{1}|\log(p_{1})+\cdots+|n_{k}|\log(p_{k}))\epsilon_{r}.

(3.1)

The operator
{\rm e}^{-\beta H}
is trace class for
\beta>1
with

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\big({\rm e}^{-\beta H}\big)=\frac{\zeta(\beta)^{2}}{\zeta(2\beta)}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{2^{\omega(n)}}{n^{\beta}}.

Here
\zeta(\beta)
is the Riemann zeta function, and
\omega(n)
denotes the number of pairwise distinct
prime factors of
n
.

Consider the algebra
C^{*}_{r}({\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{+})

acting via the left regular representation on
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{+})
. The smallest
sub-
C^{*}
-algebra
{\mathcal{A}}({\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{+})
of
{\mathcal{B}}\big(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{+})\big)
that contains
C^{*}_{r}({\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{+})
and is
preserved by the time evolution
\sigma_{t}(X)={\rm e}^{-{\rm i}tH}X{\rm e}^{{\rm i}tH}
, with
H
as
above, is generated by
C^{*}_{r}({\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{+})
and the projections
\Pi_{k,\ell}

with
\Pi_{k,\ell}\epsilon_{a/b}=\epsilon_{a/b}
when
k|a
and
\ell|b
, for
(a,b)=1
,
and zero otherwise. The spectral projections of
H
belong to this
C^{*}
-algebra, hence
the time evolution acts by inner automorphisms, see
[
57
]
for a more detailed
discussion.

3.7
Quantum statistical mechanics of
\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{S}}}
and
\boldsymbol{\Upsilon}

In our setting, we consider first the semigroup
{\mathcal{S}}={\mathbb{N}}\star{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
. It acts upon the
Hilbert space
\ell^{2}({\mathcal{S}})
via the left regular representation. Thus, for
\{\epsilon_{s}\}_{s\in{\mathcal{S}}}
the standard orthonormal basis of
\ell^{2}({\mathcal{S}})
and for a given
s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}
we have
\mu_{s^{\prime}}\epsilon_{s}=\epsilon_{s^{\prime}s}
.

Lemma 3.10
.

Consider the semigroup
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}_{r}({\mathcal{S}})
acting on
\ell^{2}({\mathcal{S}})
by the left regular representation. The transformations
\sigma_{t}(\mu_{s})=n^{{\rm i}t}\mu_{s}
, where

n=n_{1}\cdots n_{k}
for
s=s(\epsilon_{0},\epsilon_{1},\underline{n})
with
\underline{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})
,
define a time evolution
\sigma\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\rm Aut}(C^{*}_{r}({\mathcal{S}}))
. Consider then the densely defined unbounded linear operator on the Hilbert space
\ell^{2}({\mathcal{S}})
given by

\displaystyle H\epsilon_{s}=\log(n_{1}\cdots n_{k})\epsilon_{s},

for
s=s(\epsilon_{0},\epsilon_{1},\underline{n})
with
\epsilon_{0},\epsilon_{1}\in\{0,1\}
and
\underline{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})
, with
n_{i}\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
n_{i}>1
.
The operator
H
is the Hamiltonian that generates the time evolution
\sigma_{t}

on the
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}_{r}({\mathcal{S}})
. The partition function of the Hamiltonian
H
is given by

\displaystyle Z(\beta)={\rm Tr}\big({\rm e}^{-\beta H}\big)=\frac{4}{2-\zeta(\beta)},

where
\zeta(\beta)
is the Riemann zeta function.

Proof.

In order to check that
\sigma_{t}(\mu_{s})=n^{{\rm i}t}\mu_{s}
defines a time evolution as above,
we first notice that the assignment
n\colon s\mapsto n(s)=n_{1}\cdots n_{k}
for
s=s(\epsilon_{0},\epsilon_{1},\underline{n})
with
\underline{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})
is a semigroup homomorphism
{\mathcal{S}}\to{\mathbb{N}}
.

In order to see that the operator
H
is the infinitesimal generator of this time evolution we check that, for all
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
and for all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we have
\sigma_{t}(\mu_{s})={\rm e}^{{\rm i}tH}\mu_{s}{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}tH}
as operators in
{\mathcal{B}}(\ell^{2}({\mathcal{S}}))
.

Indeed, both sides act on a basis element
\epsilon_{s^{\prime}}
as
n(ss^{\prime})^{{\rm i}t}n(s^{\prime})^{-{\rm i}t}\epsilon_{ss^{\prime}}=n(s)^{{\rm i}t}\epsilon_{ss^{\prime}}
. The multiplicity of an eigenvalue
\log n
of the Hamiltonian
H
is
4P_{n}
, where the factor
4
accounts for the
four choices of
\epsilon_{0},\epsilon_{1}\in\{0,1\}
and
P_{n}
is the total number of ordered factorizations
of
n
into nontrivial positive integer factors.

Thus, the partition function equals

\displaystyle Z(\beta)

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}\big({\rm e}^{-\beta H}\big)=4\sum_{n}P_{n}n^{-\beta}=4\left(1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}n^{-\beta}\sum_{n=n_{1}\cdots n_{k}}1\right)

\displaystyle=4\left(1+\sum_{k\geq 1}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{n_{i}\geq 2}n_{i}^{-\beta}\right)=4\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(\zeta(\beta)-1)^{k}=\frac{4}{2-\zeta(\beta)}.

∎

We now consider the enveloping group
\Upsilon={\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{+}\star{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
. We proceed as in the
quantum statistical mechanics of
{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{+}
described in
[
57
]
and recalled above.
For a positive rational number
r\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{+}
with prime decomposition
r=p_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots p_{\ell}^{a_{\ell}}
where
p_{i}
a pairwise distinct primes and
a_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
a_{i}\neq 0
, let

\displaystyle n(r)=p_{1}^{|a_{1}|}\cdots p_{\ell}^{|a_{\ell}|}.

(3.2)

Lemma 3.11
.

Consider on
\ell^{2}(\Upsilon)
the densely defined unbounded linear operator

\displaystyle H\epsilon_{\upsilon}=\log(n(r_{1})\cdots n(r_{k}))\epsilon_{\upsilon},

(3.3)

for an element
\upsilon=\upsilon(\epsilon_{0},\epsilon_{1},\underline{r})
with

\epsilon_{0},\epsilon_{1}\in\{0,1\}
and
\underline{r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{k})
with
r_{i}\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{+}
,

r_{i}\neq 1
. The operator
{\rm e}^{-\beta H}
satisfies

\displaystyle Z(\beta)={\rm Tr}\big({\rm e}^{-\beta H}\big)=\frac{4\zeta(2\beta)}{2\zeta(2\beta)-\zeta(\beta)^{2}}.

Proof.

The eigenvalue
\log n
of
H
has multiplicity
4\sum\limits_{n=n_{1}\cdots n_{k}}2^{\omega(n_{1})+\cdots+\omega(n_{k})}
where the factor of
4
accounts for the choices of
\epsilon_{0},\epsilon_{1}\in\{0,1\}
and
each factor
2^{\omega(n_{i})}
, with
\omega(n_{i})
the number of distinct prime factors of
n_{i}
, accounts
for all the
r_{i}\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{+}
with
n(r_{i})=n_{i}
.

Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\big({\rm e}^{-\beta H}\big)

\displaystyle=4\left(1+\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{n=n_{1}\cdots n_{k}}2^{\omega(n_{1})+\cdots+\omega(n_{k})}n^{-\beta}\right)

\displaystyle=4\left(1+\sum_{k\geq 1}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{n_{i}\geq 2}\frac{2^{\omega(n_{i})}}{n_{i}^{\beta}}\right)=4\left(1+\sum_{k}\frac{\zeta(\beta)^{2}}{\zeta(2\beta)}-1\right)^{k}=\frac{4}{2-\frac{\zeta(\beta)^{2}}{\zeta(2\beta)}}.

∎

As in the case of the quantum statistical mechanics of
{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{+}
, the time evolution on

3.9
Gibbs states

We discuss here some functions associated to the evaluation of low temperature KMS
states (Gibbs states) of the time evolutions defined earlier in this section. We start with
a simpler case based on the quantum statistical mechanics of
{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{+}
of
[
57
]
,
and then we move to the dynamics considered in Lemma
3.16
.

Lemma 3.18
.

Consider the Hilbert space
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{N}})
with the Hamiltonian
H\epsilon_{n}=\log(n)\epsilon_{n}
. Let
h_{\mathbb{C}}
be an analytic function in the region
(0,1)_{\mathbb{C}}
, which is bounded on the unit interval and with convergent Puiseux series
h_{\mathbb{C}}(\tau)=\sum_{k}a_{k}\tau^{k/N}
for some

N
, at all
\tau\in(0,1)
. Let
\pi_{\tau}(h)
be the linear operator on
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{N}})

defined by
\pi_{\tau}(h)\epsilon_{n}=\sigma_{n}(h_{\mathbb{C}})(\tau)\epsilon_{n}=h_{\mathbb{C}}(\tau^{n})\epsilon_{n}
. The
corresponding Gibbs state is given by

\displaystyle\varphi_{\tau,\beta}(h):=\frac{{\rm Tr}\big(\pi_{\tau}(h){\rm e}^{-\beta H}\big)}{{\rm Tr}\big({\rm e}^{-\beta H}\big)}=\frac{1}{\zeta(\beta)}\sum_{k}a_{k}{\rm Li}_{\beta}\big(\tau^{k/N}\big).

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\varphi_{\tau,\beta}(h):=\frac{{\rm Tr}\big(\pi_{\tau}(h){\rm e}^{-\beta H}\big)}{{\rm Tr}\big({\rm e}^{-\beta H}\big)}=\frac{1}{\zeta(\beta)}\sum_{n\geq 1}h_{\mathbb{C}}(\tau^{n})n^{-\beta}.

Under the assumption on
h_{\mathbb{C}}
, the series
\sum\limits_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}},\,k\geq-M}a_{k}\tau^{kn/N}
is absolutely
convergent at
\tau
and we can write the above as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\zeta(\beta)}\sum_{k}a_{k}\sum_{n}\tau^{kn/N}n^{-\beta}=\frac{1}{\zeta(\beta)}\sum_{k}a_{k}{\rm Li}_{\beta}\big(\tau^{k/N}\big).

We are considering here the polylogarithm function
{\rm Li}_{s}(z)
in the range where
z\in(0,1)
and
s>1
, hence we have its integral description of the form

\displaystyle{\rm Li}_{s}(z)=\frac{1}{2}z+z\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sin(s\arctan t-t\log z)}{\big(1+t^{2}\big)^{s/2}\big({\rm e}^{2\pi t}-1\big)}\,{\rm d}t.

We can estimate the integral by

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\sin(s\arctan t-t\log z)}{\big(1+t^{2}\big)^{s/2}\big({\rm e}^{2\pi t}-1\big)}\,{\rm d}t\leq\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(s-\log z)}{\big(1+t^{2}\big)^{s/2}2\pi}\,{\rm d}t,

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\sin(s\arctan t-t\log z)}{\big(1+t^{2}\big)^{s/2}\big({\rm e}^{2\pi t}-1\big)}\,{\rm d}t\leq\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{{\rm d}t}{\big(1+t^{2}\big)^{s/2}\big({\rm e}^{2\pi t}-1\big)}.

Thus, for some positive
A_{\beta},B_{\beta}>0
, we obtain, in our range
\beta>1
and
z\in(0,1)
, the estimate

{\rm Li}_{\beta}\big(\tau^{k/N}\big)\leq A_{\beta}\tau^{k/N}-B_{\beta}\tau^{k/N}\log\tau^{k/N}
. For
\tau\in(0,1)
,
the entropy function
-\tau\log\tau
is maximal at
\tau=1/e
with value
1/e
. Thus, we further estimate

\displaystyle-B_{\beta}\tau^{k/N}\log\tau^{k/N}=\frac{-B_{\beta}}{(1-\eta)}\tau^{k\eta/N}\tau^{k(1-\eta)/N}\log\tau^{k(1-\eta)/N}\leq\frac{B_{\beta}}{e(1-\eta)}\tau^{k\eta/N}.

If
0<\eta<1
is chosen close to
1
and so that
\tau^{\eta/N}
is still within the domain of
convergence of
\sum_{k}\!a_{k}\tau^{\eta k/N}
, then we obtain from this estimate the convergence of
the series
\sum_{k}\!a_{k}{\rm Li}_{\beta}\big(\tau^{k/N}\big)
.
∎

We now consider the effect of extending the previous setting from
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{N}})
to
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{+})
,
with the Hamiltonian of (
3.1
).

Lemma 3.19
.

Consider the Hilbert space
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{+})
with the Hamiltonian
H
of (
3.1
). Let
h_{\mathbb{C}}
be as in Lemma
3.18
and let
\pi_{\tau}(h)
denote the linear operator on
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{+})
defined by
\pi_{\tau}(h)\epsilon_{r}=h_{\mathbb{C}}(\tau^{r})\epsilon_{r}
. The corresponding
Gibbs state is given by

\displaystyle\varphi_{\tau,\beta}(h):=\frac{{\rm Tr}\big(\pi_{\tau}(h){\rm e}^{-\beta H}\big)}{{\rm Tr}\big({\rm e}^{-\beta H}\big)}=\frac{\zeta(2\beta)}{\zeta(\beta)^{2}}\sum_{k}a_{k}{\rm Li}_{\beta}\big(\tau^{k/N}\big).

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\varphi_{\tau,\beta}(h):=\frac{{\rm Tr}\big(\pi_{\tau}(h){\rm e}^{-\beta H}\big)}{{\rm Tr}\big({\rm e}^{-\beta H}\big)}=\frac{\zeta(2\beta)}{\zeta(\beta)^{2}}\sum_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{2^{\omega}(n)}h_{\mathbb{C}}(\tau^{r_{j}})n^{-\beta},

where
\omega(n)
is the number of pairwise distinct prime factors of
n=p_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots p_{\omega(n)}^{k_{\omega(n)}}
and the
r_{j}
run over all the
2^{\omega(n)}
possible choices of
\pm
signs in
p_{1}^{\pm k_{1}}\cdots p_{\omega(n)}^{\pm k_{\omega(n)}}
.
By the assumptions on
h_{\mathbb{C}}
, the Puiseux series
\sum_{k}a_{k}\tau^{rk/N}
is absolutely convergent at
\tau
,
hence we can write the summation in the expression above as

\displaystyle\sum_{k}a_{k}\sum_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{2^{\omega}(n)}\tau^{r_{j}k/N}n^{-\beta}.

We have
\min\{r_{j}\,|\,j=1,\ldots,\omega(n)\}=1/n
, and
\tau^{r_{j}k/N}\leq\tau^{\frac{k}{nN}}
for
all
j=1,\ldots,\omega(n)
, hence we can give an upper bound for this

\displaystyle\sum_{k}a_{k}\sum_{n}\frac{2^{\omega(n)}\tau^{\frac{k}{nN}}}{n^{\beta}}.

Where the absolute convergence holds we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n}\frac{2^{\omega(n)}\tau^{k/(nN)}}{n^{\beta}}=\sum_{n\geq 1}2^{\omega(n)}\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\frac{\log^{\ell}\big(\tau^{k/N}\big)}{\ell!n^{\beta+\ell}}=\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\frac{\log^{\ell}\big(\tau^{k/N}\big)}{\ell!}\frac{\zeta(\beta+\ell)^{2}}{\zeta(2(\beta+\ell))}.

Since
\zeta(s)^{2}/\zeta(2s)
is decreasing for a real variable
s>1
, the convergence of the series above
is controlled by the convergence of the Puiseux series
\sum_{k}a_{k}\tau^{k/N}
.
∎

The more general case is less explicit, but the case above serves as a model for the expected
behavior of the Gibbs states.
On the Hilbert space
\ell^{2}(\Upsilon)
with the Hamiltonian
H
of (
3.3
) and
the operators
\pi_{\tau}(h)
of (
3.4
), the Gibbs states have the form

\displaystyle\varphi_{\tau,\beta}(h)=\frac{{\rm Tr}\big(\pi_{\tau}(h){\rm e}^{-\beta H}\big)}{{\rm Tr}\big({\rm e}^{-\beta H}\big)}=\sum_{\upsilon\in\Upsilon}\sigma_{\upsilon}(h)(\tau)n(\upsilon)^{-\beta},

where for
\upsilon=\upsilon(\epsilon_{0},\epsilon_{1},\underline{(}r))
with

\underline{r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{k})
we write
n(\upsilon):=n(r_{1})\cdots n(r_{k})
with

n(r_{j})
as in (
3.2
). As before, for a fixed positive integer

, we have

\min\{r\,|\,n(r)=n\}=1/n
and
\max\{r\,|\,n(r)=n\}=n
. We have

\sigma_{\upsilon}(h)=\tau^{\epsilon_{0}}\sigma_{r_{1}}\tau\cdots\tau\sigma_{r_{k}}\tau^{\epsilon_{1}}(h)
,
where
\sigma_{r_{j}}(t)=t^{r_{j}}
. Using estimates of terms of the form
(1-\tau^{r})^{r^{\prime}}
by terms

\big(1-\tau^{n(r)}\big)^{1/n(r^{\prime})}
we can estimate the convergence of the series above
in terms of a series

\displaystyle h(\tau)+\sum_{k}\sum_{n=n_{1}\cdots n_{k}}2^{\omega(n_{1})+\cdots+\omega(n_{k})}h(P_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}}(\tau)),n^{-\beta},

where

with the
\sigma_{r_{j}}

\sigma_{n_{j}}
or

\sigma_{1/n_{j}}

Quasi-triangular quasi-Hopf algebras

In this section we consider a different point of view, based on quasi-triangular quasi-Hopf algebras.
The results of Drinfeld
[
31
]
showed that the Grothendieck–Teichmüller group
GT
acts
by tranformations of the structure (associator and
R
-matrix) of quasi-triangular quasi-Hopf algebras,
hence the absolute Galois group also acts via its embedding into
GT
. We show here that there
are systems of quasi-triangular quasi-Hopf algebras naturally associated to the constructions we
discussed in the previous section. In particular, we show that the Bost–Connes endomorphisms

\sigma_{n}
,
\rho_{n}
on the group algebra
{\mathbb{C}}[{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}]
determine a system of quasi-triangular
quasi-Hopf algebras. We also show that there is a system of quasi-triangular
quasi-Hopf algebras associated to our previous construction of the Hopf algebra of dessins d’enfant.

4.1
Twisted quantum double and quasi-triangular quasi-Hopf algebras

Recall the following notions from
[
30
]
. A quasi-Hopf algebra
{\mathcal{H}}
over a field
{\mathbb{K}}
is a unital associative algebra over
{\mathbb{K}}
with multiplication
m\colon{\mathcal{H}}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{H}}
, endowed with a counit
\epsilon\colon{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathbb{K}}
, a comultiplication
\Delta\colon{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{H}}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}
, an invertible element
\Phi\in{\mathcal{H}}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}
(the associator), as well as an antihomomorphism

S\colon{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{H}}
and two distinguished elements
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathcal{H}}
such that

1.

\Phi
satisfies the pentagon identity

\displaystyle(\Delta\otimes 1\otimes 1)\Phi(1\otimes 1\otimes\Delta)\Phi=(\Phi\otimes 1)(1\otimes\Delta\otimes 1)\Phi(1\otimes\Phi).

2.

\Delta
is quasi-associative

\displaystyle(1\otimes\Delta)\Delta(x)=\Phi^{-1}(\Delta\otimes 1)\Delta(x)\Phi,\ \ \forall x\in{\mathcal{H}}.

3.

\Phi
and the counit
\epsilon
satisfy

\displaystyle(1\otimes\epsilon\otimes 1)\Phi=1.

4.

The antipode
S
satisfies the relations

•

m(S\otimes\alpha)\Delta(x)=\epsilon(x)\alpha
and
m(1\otimes\beta S)\Delta(x)=\epsilon(x)\beta
, for all
c\in{\mathcal{H}}
,

•

m(m\otimes 1)(S\otimes\alpha\otimes\beta S)\Phi=1
and
m(m\otimes 1)(1\otimes\beta S\otimes\alpha)\Phi^{-1}=1
.

The structure of quasi-triangular quasi-Hopf algebra upon
{\mathcal{H}}
is given by an invertible element
{\mathcal{R}}\in{\mathcal{H}}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}
such that
\Delta^{t}(x)={\mathcal{R}}\Delta(x){\mathcal{R}}^{-1}
, where
\Delta^{t}
is the coproduct
\Delta
with swapped order of the two factors in
{\mathcal{H}}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}
.

The Drinfeld quantum double construction of
[
29
]
assigns to a finite dimensional Hopf algebra
{\mathcal{H}}

a quasi-triangular Hopf algebra
{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{H}})={\mathcal{H}}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}^{*}
, where
{\mathcal{H}}^{*}
is the linear dual of
{\mathcal{H}}

with the Hopf algebra structure with coproduct corresponding to the product of
{\mathcal{H}}
and viceversa. The
quasi-triangular structure is determined by the element
{\mathcal{R}}
obtained by considering the image of the
identity in
{\rm Hom}({\mathcal{H}},{\mathcal{H}})
under the identification of the latter with
{\mathcal{H}}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}^{*}
,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}=\sum_{a}e^{a}\otimes 1\otimes 1\otimes e_{a},

where
\{e_{a}\}
is a basis of
{\mathcal{H}}
and
\{e^{a}\}
is the dual basis.

The construction of the Drinfeld quantum double can be extended from Hopf algebras
to quasi-Hopf algebras, in such a way that it assigns to a quasi-Hopf algebra a
quasi-triangular quasi-Hopf algebra. This can be done in a categorical way, as shown
in
[
50
]
. Namely, the Drinfeld quantum double construction has a categorical
formulation (see
[
29
]
) by considering the rigid monoidal category
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{H}}
of
modules over a Hopf algebra
{\mathcal{H}}
and associating to it a braided tensor category

{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{H}})
which can be identified with the category of modules
{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{H}})}

over the Drinfeld quantum double
{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{H}})
quasi-triangular Hopf algebra.
Since by the Tannaka reconstruction theorem for Hopf algebras, there is a bijection
between Hopf algebras and rigid monoidal categories with a forgetful functor to
finite dimensional vector spaces, the categorical formulation of the Drinfeld quantum
double can be seen as a construction that assigns to a rigid monoidal category
{\mathcal{C}}

a braided tensor category
{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{C}})
called the Drinfeld double or the Drinfeld center.
Using Tannaka reconstruction,
{\mathcal{C}}
determines a Hopf algebra and
{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{H}})

determines its double quasi-triangular Hopf algebras. It is shown in
[
50
]
that the
same kind of categorical argument can be applied starting with a quasi-Hopf algebra
{\mathcal{H}}
,
by considering its category of modules and then obtaining from the Drinfeld center
construction
{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{H}}})
a quasi-triangular quasi-Hopf algebra,
{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{H}})
, the twisted Drinfeld double, by applying the Tannaka reconstruction theorem for quasi-Hopf algebras proven in
[
49
]
.

The categorical formulation of Drinfeld quantum doubles can also be used to show functoriality
properties. Indeed, the functoriality of the Drinfeld center was proved in
[
44
]
, as a functor
with source a category whose objects are tensor categories and whose
morphisms are bimodules and target a category whose objects are braid tensor
categories with morphisms given by monoidal categories obtained from a suitable tensoring
operation of the source and target braid tensor categories, see
[
44
]
for more details.

In particular, we are interested here in the twisted Drinfeld double
{\mathcal{D}}^{\omega}(G)

of a finite group
G
. It is a quasi-triangular
quasi-Hopf algebra, obtained by applying the Drinfeld quantum double construction of

[
29
]
to the quasi-Hopf algebra obtained by twisting the Hopf algebra
associated to
G
by a
3
-cocycle
\omega\in H^{3}(G,{\mathbb{G}}_{m})
.
More precisely, the group algebra
{\mathbb{C}}[G]
of
G
has a Hopf algebra
structure with the coproduct of group-like elements given by

\Delta(g)=g\otimes g
, and product given by the convolution product of the group algebra,
while
{\mathbb{C}}^{G}
has coproduct given by the convolution product
\Delta(e^{g})=\sum\limits_{g=ab}e^{a}\otimes e^{b}

and product given by the pointwise product of functions,
e^{g}e^{h}=\delta_{g,h}e^{g}
.
One considers then the product
{\mathbb{C}}^{G}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}[G]
with basis
e_{g}
of
{\mathbb{C}}[G]
and
e^{g}
of
{\mathbb{C}}^{G}
.
A
3
-cocycle
\omega\in Z^{3}(G,U(1)
for a finite group
G
satisfies the cocycle identity

\displaystyle\omega(y,s,t)\omega(x,ys,t)\omega(x,y,s)=\omega(s,y,st)\omega(xy,s,t)

and
\omega(x,e,y)=1
for
e
the identity element in
G
. The choice of a

3
-cocycle
\omega
has the effect of twisting the Hopf algebra
{\mathbb{C}}^{G}
into
a quasi-Hopf algebra
{\mathbb{C}}^{G}_{\omega}
, with associator
\Phi
given by

\Phi=\sum\limits_{a,b,c\in G}\omega(a,b,c)^{-1}e^{a}\otimes e^{b}\otimes e^{c}
.
The twisted quantum double
{\mathcal{D}}^{\omega}(G)={\mathbb{C}}^{G}_{\omega}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}[G]
is
the extension
{\mathbb{C}}^{G}_{\omega}\to{\mathcal{D}}^{\omega}(G)\to{\mathbb{C}}[G]
with the first map
given by
e^{g}\mapsto e^{g}\otimes 1
and the second by
e^{g}\otimes e_{g^{\prime}}\mapsto\delta_{g,1}e_{g^{\prime}}
.
The quasi-Hopf algebra structure of
{\mathcal{D}}^{\omega}(G)
is determined by the product and coproduct

\displaystyle(e^{g}\otimes e_{h})({\rm e}^{g^{\prime}}\otimes e_{h^{\prime}})=\theta_{g}(h,h^{\prime})\delta_{h^{-1}gh,g^{\prime}}e^{g}\otimes hh^{\prime},

\displaystyle\Delta(e^{g}\otimes e_{h})=\sum_{g=ab}\gamma_{h}(a,b)e^{a}\otimes e_{h}\otimes e^{b}\otimes e_{h}

where
\theta_{g}(h,h^{\prime})
and
\gamma_{h}(a,b)
are given by

\displaystyle\theta_{g}(h,h^{\prime})=\frac{\omega(g,h,h^{\prime})\omega\big(h,h^{\prime},(hh^{\prime})^{-1}ghh^{\prime}\big)}{\omega\big(h,h^{-1}gh,h^{\prime}\big)},

\displaystyle\gamma_{h}(a,b)=\frac{\omega(a,b,h)\omega\big(h,h^{-1}ah,h^{-1}bh\big)}{\omega\big(a,h,h^{-1}bh\big)}.

The quasi-triangular structure on
{\mathcal{D}}^{\omega}(G)
is given by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}=\sum_{a\in G}(e^{a}\otimes 1)\otimes(1\otimes e_{a}).

This construction of a quasi-triangular quasi-Hopf algebra associated to a finite group
was introduced in
[
27
]
in the context of RCFT orbifold models and

3
-dimensional topological field theory. The compatibility between the direct
construction described in
[
27
]
and the general categorical formulation of the
twisted Drinfeld double mentioned above is discussed in detail in
[
50
]
.

4.2
A Bost–Connes system of quasi-triangular quasi-Hopf algebras

We show here that there is a direct system of quasi-triangular quasi-Hopf algebras
naturally associated to the Bost–Connes datum, by which we mean the datum
of the algebra
{\mathbb{C}}[{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}]
together with the endomorphisms
\sigma_{n}(e(r))=e(nr)

and
\rho_{n}(e(r))=n^{-1}\sum\limits_{s\colon ns=r}e(s)
. We also write
\tilde{\rho}_{n}(e(r))=\sum\limits_{s\colon ns=r}e(s)

for the correspondences considered in the integral Bost–Connes system of
[
18
]
.

Consider the tensor product
{\mathbb{C}}[{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}]\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}}
.
Using the Bost–Connes notation we write
e(r)
for the basis of
{\mathbb{C}}[{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}]
for
r\in{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}

and we write
e_{r}
for the dual basis of
{\mathbb{C}}^{{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}}
. In particular, for a fixed level
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
we consider the tensor product
{\mathbb{C}}[{\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}}]\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{{\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}}}
and we still use the same
notation
e(r)
and
e_{r}
for the dual bases.

The choice of a
3
-cocycle
\omega
for
{\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}}
is given by the choice of a representative in

H^{3}({\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}},U(1))\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}}.

An explicit description of the representatives is given
in
[
39
, Proposition 2.3]
as

\displaystyle\omega_{a}(g_{i_{1}},g_{i_{2}},g_{i_{3}})=\zeta_{m}^{ai_{1}[(i_{2}+i_{3})/3]},\qquad 0\leq a\leq m,

for
\zeta_{m}
a primitive root of unity of order
m
. The twisted Drinfeld double
{\mathcal{D}}^{\omega}({\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}})
of a finite cyclic group, with
\omega
chosen as one of the representatives of
H^{3}({\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}},U(1))\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}}
above, is then obtained as recalled in the previous subsection.

Proposition 4.1
.

The Bost–Connes endomorphisms
\sigma_{n}
and
\rho_{n}
determine a direct system of quasi-triangular quasi-Hopf algebras
{\mathcal{D}}^{\omega_{n}}({\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}})
indexed by the positive integers
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
ordered by divisibility, with associators related by
\omega_{nm}=\sigma_{n}(\omega_{m})

and
R
-matrices related by
{\mathcal{R}}_{nm}=\tilde{\rho}_{n}({\mathcal{R}}_{m})
.

Proof.

The Bost–Connes maps
\sigma_{n}\colon{\mathbb{Z}}/nm{\mathbb{Z}}\twoheadrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}
map roots of unity of order
nm
to their
n
-th power. They organize the roots of unity into a projective system with
\varprojlim_{n}{\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}}=\hat{\mathbb{Z}}
. By identifying
X_{n}={\rm Spec}({\mathbb{C}}[{\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}}])
with
{\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}}
, the maps above induce morphisms
\sigma_{n}\colon{\mathbb{C}}[{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}]\to{\mathbb{C}}[{\mathbb{Z}}/nm{\mathbb{Z}}]
that determine the limit algebra
{\mathbb{C}}[{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}]
as a direct limit of the
{\mathbb{C}}[{\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}}]
. On
{\mathbb{C}}^{{\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}}}
the morphisms
\sigma_{n}\colon{\mathbb{Z}}/nm{\mathbb{Z}}\twoheadrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}
act by precomposition,
\sigma_{n}\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{{\mathbb{Z}}/nm{\mathbb{Z}}}
with
f\mapsto\sigma_{n}(f)=f\circ\sigma_{n}
. The group cohomology is a contravariant functor with respect to group homomorphisms, with the restriction map
\sigma_{n}\colon H^{3}({\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}},U(1))\to H^{3}({\mathbb{Z}}/nm{\mathbb{Z}},U(1))
.
Consider then the
R
-matrix of the quasi-triangular structure, given by the element
{\mathcal{R}}_{m}=\sum\limits_{a\in{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}}(e_{a}\otimes 1)\otimes(1\otimes e(a))
. The image of
{\mathcal{R}}_{m}
under the map
\tilde{\rho}_{n}
is given by

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}_{n}({\mathcal{R}}_{m})=\sum_{a\in{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{b\colon nb=a}(e_{b}\otimes 1)\otimes(1\otimes e(b))=\sum_{b\in{\mathbb{Z}}/nm{\mathbb{Z}}}(e_{b}\otimes 1)\otimes(1\otimes e(b))={\mathcal{R}}_{nm}.

∎

4.3
Dessins d’enfant and quasi-triangular quasi-Hopf algebras

We now consider a similar construction of a system of quasi-triangular quasi-Hopf algebras associated to the Hopf algebra of dessins d’enfant of Proposition
2.6
.

Proposition 4.2
.

The Hopf algebra of dessins d’enfant of Proposition
2.6
determines an associated system of quasi-triangular quasi-Hopf algebras
{\mathcal{D}}^{\omega_{d}}({\mathcal{G}}_{d})
.

Proof.

The construction of the quasi-triangular quasi-Hopf algebras
{\mathcal{D}}^{\omega_{d}}({\mathcal{G}}_{d})

is similar to the one we have seen in Proposition
4.1
associated to the Bost–Connes
datum, and relies in a similar way on the twisted Drinfeld quantum double. By construction, the Hopf algebra
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}}
of dessins d’enfant of Proposition
2.6
is a connected commutative Hopf algebra, with grading as in Lemma
2.5
related to the degree of the Belyi maps,
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}}=\oplus_{d\geq 0}{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{D}},d}
with
{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{D}},0}={\mathbb{Q}}
. Thus, the dual affine group scheme
{\mathcal{G}}
is a pro-unipotent affine group scheme,
{\mathcal{G}}=\varprojlim_{d}{\mathcal{G}}_{d}
. We work here with complex coefficients, with
{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{D}},{\mathbb{C}}}={\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}{\mathbb{C}}
. For a fixed
d\in{\mathbb{N}}

consider the product
{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{D}},{\mathbb{C}},d}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}[{\mathcal{G}}_{d}]
. We can use this to construct a twisted Drinfeld quantum double
{\mathcal{D}}^{\omega_{d}}({\mathcal{G}}_{d})
, given the choice of a
3
-cocycle

\omega\in H^{3}({\mathcal{G}}_{d},U(1))
. The functoriality of the Drinfeld quantum double via the functoriality of the Drinfeld center
[
44
]
, together with the functoriality of the group cohomology and the categorical construction of the twisted Drinfeld quantum double of
[
50
]
then show that the projective system of group homomorphisms between the
{\mathcal{G}}_{d}
and corresponding dual direct system of Hopf algebras
{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{D}},{\mathbb{C}},d}
induce a system of quasi-triangular quasi-Hopf algebras
{\mathcal{D}}^{\omega_{d}}({\mathcal{G}}_{d})
.
∎

We then have two actions of the absolute Galois group
G={\rm Gal}\big(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Q}}\big)
on the
quasi-triangular quasi-Hopf algebras
{\mathcal{D}}^{\omega_{d}}({\mathcal{G}}_{d})
. On the one hand,
the action of
G
by Hopf algebra automorphisms of
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{D}}
restricts to an action
on the
{\mathcal{H}}_{{\mathcal{D}},d}
since the degree is a Galois invariant, hence
G
acts by
automorphisms of
{\mathcal{G}}_{d}
. On the other hand we also have the embedding of
G

into the Grothendieck–Teichmüller group
GT
, which acts on the quasi-triangulated
quasi-Hopf structure of the
{\mathcal{D}}^{\omega_{d}}({\mathcal{G}}_{d})
, by transforming the pair
(\Phi,{\mathcal{R}})

of the associator and the
R
-matrix as in
[
31
]
.

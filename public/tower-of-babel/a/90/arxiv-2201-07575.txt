Partial impulse observability of linear descriptor systems Journal: System & Control Letters
Juhi Jaiswal, Thomas Berger, Nutan Kumar Tomar

Abstract
A research paper in this journal vol. 61, no. 3, pp. 427–434, 2012, by M. Darouach, provides a functional observer design for linear descriptor systems under the partial impulse observability condition. The observer design is correct, but there was a flaw in the algebraic criterion characterizing partial impulse observability. In the present paper, we derive a novel characterization of partial impulse observability in terms of a simple rank condition involving the system coefficient matrices and an alternative characterization in terms of the Wong sequences.



1
Introduction

We consider linear time-invariant multivariable descriptor systems of the form

\displaystyle E\dot{x}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Ax(t),

(1a)

\displaystyle y(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Cx(t),

(1b)

\displaystyle z(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Lx(t),

(1c)

where
E,A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n},~C\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times n}
, and

are known constant matrices. We call

the (unknown) semistate vector,
y(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
the measured output vector, and
z(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{r}
the unknown output vector. The unknown output
z
contains those variables which cannot be measured and, therefore, observers are required to estimate them.

In
darouach2012functional
, Darouach derived an algebraic test for partial impulse observability of (
1
) with respect to
L
and used this concept in designing a functional observer to estimate

. Under the same algebraic assumption, the observer designing approach of
darouach2012functional
has been improved by using a linear matrix inequality (LMI) formulation in
darouach2017functional
. The concept of partial impulse observability was first introduced in
darouach2012functional
as follows.

Definition 1

[
darouach2012functional
, Def. 1]

The descriptor system (
1
), or the triplet
(E,A,C)
, is said to be partially impulse observable with respect to
L
, if

is impulse free for

only if

is impulse free for
t\geq 0
.

In the papers
darouach2012functional
and

, the following characterization of partial impulse observability of (
1

Theorem 1

(
[

,
(1)
&
(5)

]

[

,
(1)

The triplet

\rank\begin{bmatrix}E&A\\
0&E\\
0&C\\
0&L\end{bmatrix}=\rank\begin{bmatrix}E&A\\
0&E\\
0&C\end{bmatrix}.

(2)

Roughly speaking, partial observability of (
1
) is related to the reconstruction of
z(t)
from the knowledge of
y(t)
. Since, corresponding to an inconsistent initial value,
z
may exhibit impulses, the concept of partial impulse observability is an important aspect for any descriptor system. Thus it is evident that to study partial impulse observability, we have to consider a proper framework of distributional solutions of (
1
). Here, we consider the class of piecewise-smooth distributions
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}_{pw\mathscr{C}^{\infty}}
as introduced in
trenn2009distributional
; for a thorough discussion of this class of distributions, we also refer to
trenn2013solution
. Motivated by
berger2017observability
, we denote the set of all distributional solutions of (
1
) on
[0,\infty)
by

\mathscr{B}:=\left\{\,(x,y,z)\in(\mathscr{D}^{\prime}_{pw\mathscr{C}^{\infty}})^{n+p+r}\left|\,\vphantom{(x,y,z)\in(\mathscr{D}^{\prime}_{pw\mathscr{C}^{\infty}})^{n+p+r}}(x,y,z)\text{ satisfies \eqref{dls1} on }[0,\infty)\right.\right\}.

\mathscr{B}
is called ITP-behavior in

. We stress that it is important that the system (

[0,\infty)
, and the solution is free on

, which is different from considering solutions of (
1
) on
{\mathbb{R}}
restricted to

, because of possible impulsive terms due to inconsistent initial values. Furthermore, it is important to note that the distributional restriction to any interval

is well defined for

, see

can be uniquely represented as a combination of a distribution induced by a locally integrable piecewise-smooth function

and their distributional derivatives
\delta_{t_{j}}^{(i)}
, see
trenn2009distributional
. The part of
\mathcal{D}\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}_{pw\mathscr{C}^{\infty}}
corresponding to

and its derivatives is called the

and denoted by

, see also the definition in
[

. Since the class

also allows to perform point evaluation of any element, throughout the article,

, and

stand for the impulsive part of the respective variables at time

We exploit the behavior
\mathscr{B}
to reformulate the definition of partial impulse observability as follows.

Definition 2

The descriptor system (
1
) or the triplet
(E,A,C)
is partially impulse observable with respect to

\forall\,(x,y,z)\in\mathscr{B}:\ \big(\forall\,t\geq 0:\ y[t]=0\big)\ \implies\ \big(\forall\,t\geq 0:\ z[t]=0\big).

2
Preliminaries

First we collect some standard results for the characterization of solutions to the following homogeneous system:

\mathscr{E}\dot{x}=\mathscr{A}x,

(3)

where
\mathscr{E},\mathscr{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
. By a solution we mean a piecewise-smooth distribution
x\in(\mathscr{D}^{\prime}_{pw\mathscr{C}^{\infty}})^{n}
which satisfies (
3
) on
[0,\infty)
. The first order matrix polynomial
(\lambda\mathscr{E}-\mathscr{A})

\lambda

for (
3

(\lambda\mathscr{E}-\mathscr{A})
is called

, if

and
\det(\lambda\mathscr{E}-\mathscr{A})\neq 0
. If a matrix pencil is not regular, it is called

. For any singular matrix pencil, the Kronecker canonical form (KCF) is the simplest decomposition which provides many useful theoretical tools for analyzing (

Lemma 1

gantmacher1959theory

The Kronecker Canonical Form (KCF): For every matrix pencil
(\lambda\mathscr{E}-\mathscr{A})
there exist nonsingular matrices
P\in\mathbb{C}^{m\times m}
and
Q\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
such that, for multi-indices
\epsilon,~f,~\sigma
, and
\eta
,

\displaystyle P(\lambda\mathscr{E}-\mathscr{A})Q=\begin{bmatrix}\lambda E_{\epsilon}-A_{\epsilon}&&&\\
&\lambda I_{f}-J_{f}&&\\
&&\lambda J_{\sigma}-I_{\sigma}&\\
&&&\lambda E_{\eta}-A_{\eta}\end{bmatrix},

(4)

where
\lambda E_{\epsilon}-A_{\epsilon}
and
\lambda E_{\eta}-A_{\eta}
have block diagonal structure; each block takes the form
\lambda E_{{\epsilon}_{i}}-A_{{\epsilon}_{i}}=\lambda\begin{bmatrix}I_{{\epsilon}_{i}}&0_{{\epsilon}_{i}\times 1}\end{bmatrix}-\begin{bmatrix}0_{{\epsilon}_{i}\times 1}&I_{{\epsilon}_{i}}\end{bmatrix}
and
\lambda E_{{\eta}_{i}}-A_{{\eta}_{i}}=\lambda\begin{bmatrix}I_{{\eta}_{i}}\\
0_{1\times{\eta}_{i}}\end{bmatrix}-\begin{bmatrix}0_{1\times{\eta}_{i}}\\
I_{{\eta}_{i}}\end{bmatrix}
, respectively; both
J_{f}
and
J_{\sigma}
are in Jordan canonical form;
J_{\sigma}
has zeros on its diagonal and thus is a nilpotent matrix;
J_{f}
contains, on its diagonal, all finite eigenvalues of
(\lambda\mathscr{E}-\mathscr{A})
.

Remark 1

The blocks in (
4
) appear only in pairs. For example, if
E_{\epsilon}
vanishes, then
A_{\epsilon}
also vanishes. Moreover,
\epsilon-
blocks with
\epsilon_{i}=0
and/or
\eta-
blocks with
\eta_{i}=0
are possible, which results in zero columns (for
\epsilon_{i}=0
) and/or zero rows (for
\eta_{i}=0
) in the KCF (

). The KCF structure (
4
) is unique up to the reordering of the diagonal blocks. The KCF (
4
) without
\epsilon-
and
\eta-
blocks is also called the Weierstrass canonical form (WCF). In case of a regular matrix pencil
(\lambda\mathscr{E}-\mathscr{A})
, the KCF (
4

Remark 2

In this paper, we use the KCF (
4
) to simplify the proof of some theoretical results. But, the determination of the KCF is not recommended because the computation is numerically ill-posed
van1979computation
. Furthermore, since
J_{f}
and
J_{\sigma}
are in Jordan canonical form, in general, the matrices
P
and
Q
in (
4
) are complex-valued matrices. This is computationally undesirable, because if the system matrices are real-valued, one would like to get real
P
and
Q
. To remove such difficulties in the computation of the KCF, based on the Wong sequences, a numerically stable quasi-Kronecker decomposition, which also reveals the KCF structure, can be found in
berger2012quasi
,
berger2013addition
.

Definition 3

For matrices

Counterexample

6
Conclusion

This paper has established necessary and sufficient conditions for the partial impulse observability of linear descriptor systems. The developed conditions in terms of a rank criterion involving the original system coefficient matrices and the Wong sequences, respectively, are very easy to implement.

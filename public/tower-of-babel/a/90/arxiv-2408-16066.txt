Linear preservers of parallel matrix pairs with respect to the k k -numerical radius
Bojan Kuzma, Chi-Kwong Li, Edward Poon, Sushil Singla

Abstract
. Let 1 ≤ k < n 1\leq k<n be integers. Two n × n n\times n matrices A A and B B form a parallel pair with respect to the k k -numerical radius w k w_{k} if w k ​ ( A + μ ​ B ) = w k ​ ( A ) + w k ​ ( B ) w_{k}(A+\mu B)=w_{k}(A)+w_{k}(B) for some scalar μ \mu with | μ | = 1 |\mu|=1 ; they form a TEA (triangle equality attaining) pair if the preceding equation holds for μ = 1 \mu=1 . We classify linear bijections on 𝕄 n {\mathbb{M}}_{n} and on ℍ n {\mathbb{H}}_{n} which preserve parallel pairs or TEA pairs. Such preservers are scalar multiples of w k w_{k} -isometries, except for some exceptional maps on ℍ n {\mathbb{H}}_{n} when n = 2 ​ k n=2k .

1.
Introduction

Let
{\bf V}
be a normed space equipped with a norm
\|\cdot\|
over
{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{C}}
or
{\mathbb{R}}
.
Suppose
x,y\in{\bf V}
. Then
x
is parallel to
y
, denoted by
x\|y
,
if
\|x+\mu y\|=\|x\|+\|y\|
for some
\mu\in{\mathbb{F}}
with
|\mu|=1
.
If the equality holds with
\mu=1
, we say that
x
and
y
form a triangle equality attaining (TEA) pair.
We are interested in linear maps
T\colon{\bf V}\rightarrow{\bf V}
preserving parallel pairs
i.e.,
T(x)\|T(y)
whenever
x\|y
. We are also interested in linear maps preserving TEA pairs, i.e.,

(T(x),T(y))
is a TEA pair whenever
(x,y)
is a TEA pair.

Note that the zero map, and a map of the form
x\mapsto f(x)y
for a fixed
y\in{\bf V}

and a fixed linear functional
f
on
V
always preserves parallel pairs. Thus, the semigroup of
linear preservers of parallel pairs contains degenerate maps. Furthermore, it also contains all scalar multiples of isometries.
We are interested if it contains additional invertible linear maps or not. We remark that the answer
relies on the properties of the given norm. For example, if the norm is strictly convex, i.e.,
\|x+y\|<\|x\|+\|y\|

whenever
x,y
are linearly independent,
then
x
and
y
are parallel if and only if they are linearly dependent, in which case every
linear map will preserve parallel pairs.
Nevertheless, there are results showing that bijective linear maps
preserving parallel pairs or TEA pairs must be nonzero multiples of isometries; see
[
3
,
9
,
10
]
.
In this paper, we
consider parallel pair preservers and TEA preservers on
{\mathbb{M}}_{n}
, the space of complex
n\times n
matrices, when
the norm is the
k
-numerical radius for
1\leq k<n
.
Recall that the
k
-numerical range and the
k
-numerical radius of
A\in{\mathbb{M}}_{n}
are defined as

W_{k}(A)=\{\mathop{\rm tr}AP:P^{*}=P=P^{2},\mathop{\rm tr}P=k\},

and

w_{k}(A)=\max\{|\mu|:\mu\in W_{k}(A)\}.

It is known that
w_{k}
is a norm on
{\mathbb{M}}_{n}
and is invariant under unitary similarities, i.e.,

w_{k}(U^{*}AU)=W_{k}(A)
for any
A\in{\mathbb{M}}_{n}
and unitary
U\in{\mathbb{M}}_{n}
.
It follows from
[
5
]
(see also
[
11
]
for more general results) that a linear map

T\colon{\mathbb{M}}_{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{M}}_{n}
is an isometry for
w_{k}
if and only if there is
\mu\in{\mathbb{C}}
with

|\mu|=1
and a unitary
U
such that one of the following holds.

(1)
\mu T
has the form
A\mapsto U^{*}AU
or
A\mapsto U^{*}A^{t}U,

(2)

n=2k>2
and
\mu T
has the form

A\mapsto\dfrac{1}{k}(\mathop{\rm tr}A)I-U^{*}AU
or
A\mapsto\dfrac{1}{k}(\mathop{\rm tr}A)I-U^{*}A^{t}U.

We will confirm that invertible linear maps on
{\mathbb{M}}_{n}
preserving parallel pairs or TEA pairs with respect
to the
k
-numerical radius norm are scalar multiplies of isometries.
In particular, we have the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
1\leq k<n
, and
T\colon{\mathbb{M}}_{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{M}}_{n}
be a bijective linear map.
The following conditions are equivalent.

(a)

T
preserves
parallel pairs with respect to the
k
-numerical radius.

(b)

T
preserves TEA pairs with respect to the
k
-numerical radius.

(c)

There
is a nonzero
\mu\in{\mathbb{C}}
and a unitary
U\in{\mathbb{M}}_{n}
such that

(c.1)

\mu T
has the form
A\mapsto U^{*}AU
or
A\mapsto U^{*}A^{t}U,
or

(c.2)

n=2k>2
and
\mu T
has the form

A\mapsto\dfrac{1}{k}(\mathop{\rm tr}A)I-U^{*}AU
or
A\mapsto\dfrac{1}{k}(\mathop{\rm tr}A)I-U^{*}A^{t}U.

Let
{\mathbb{H}}_{n}
be the real linear space of Hermitian matrices in
{\mathbb{M}}_{n}
.
We will show that a similar result holds for the real space of
Hermitian matrices except when
n=2k
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
1\leq k<n
. Suppose
T\colon{\mathbb{H}}_{n}\to{\mathbb{H}}_{n}
is
a bijective real linear map. The following conditions are equivalent.

(a)

T

preserves parallel pairs with respect to the
k
-numerical radius.

(b)

T

preserves TEA pairs with respect to the
k
-numerical radius.

(c)

There exist a nonzero
\mu\in{\mathbb{R}}
and a unitary
U\in{\mathbb{M}}_{n}
such that one of the following holds.

(c.1)

\mu T
has the form
A\mapsto U^{*}AU
or
A\mapsto U^{*}A^{t}U.

(c.2)

n=2k>2
and
\mu T
is a unital map such that

T(A)=cU^{*}AU
for all
A\in{\mathbb{H}}_{n}^{0}
, or

T(A)=cU^{*}A^{t}U
for all
A\in{\mathbb{H}}_{n}^{0}
,

where
{\mathbb{H}}_{n}^{0}
denotes the set of trace zero matrices in
{\mathbb{H}}_{n}
,
and
c\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}
.

(c.3)

n=2
and
\mu T
is a bijective unital trace preserving map.

The proofs of Theorem 1.2 and Theorem 1.3 will be given in Section 2 and Section 3, respectively.
We will always assume that
1\leq k<n
in our discussion. Let us collect some basic properties of the
k
-numerical range
and
k
-numerical radius for easy reference in the next proposition. We will denote by

\lambda_{1}(G)\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}(G)
the eigenvalues of
G\in{\mathbb{H}}_{n}
.

Proposition 1.3
.

The following properties of
W_{k}(A)
and
w_{k}(A)
hold.

(1)

W_{k}(\mu A+\nu I)=\mu W_{k}(A)+k\nu
for any
\mu,\nu\in{\mathbb{C}}
.

(2)

W_{k}(A)=\bigcap_{\theta\in[0,2\pi)}\{\mu:e^{i\theta}\mu+e^{-i\theta}\bar{\mu}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}(e^{i\theta}A+e^{-i\theta}A^{*})\}.

If
A\in{\mathbb{H}}_{n}
, then
W_{k}(H)
is the real interval
[\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{n+1-j}(A),\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}(A)]
.

(3)

w_{k}(A)=\max_{\theta\in[0,2\pi)}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}(e^{i\theta}A+e^{-i\theta}A^{*})/2
.

(4)

Suppose
A=A_{1}\oplus A_{2}\in{\mathbb{M}}_{\ell}\oplus{\mathbb{M}}_{n-\ell}
. Then

W_{k}(A)={\rm conv}\{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}:\mu_{1}\in W_{r}(A_{1}),\mu_{2}\in W_{k-r}(A_{2}),0\leq r\leq\ell,0\leq k-r\leq n-\ell\}.

Consequently,

w_{k}(A)\leq\max\{w_{r}(A_{1})+w_{k-r}(A_{2}):0\leq r\leq\ell,0\leq k-r\leq n-\ell\},

where
W_{0}(X)=\{0\}
and
w_{0}(X)=0
for any square matrix
X
.

(5)

The set
W_{k}(A)
is a nondegenerate line segment with endpoints
a_{1},a_{2}
if and only if

A=cI+dH
for some
c,d\in{\mathbb{C}}
with
|d|=1
and
H=H^{*}
with eigenvalues
\mu_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\mu_{n}

such that
a_{1}=ck+d(\mu_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{k})
and

a_{2}=ck+d(\mu_{n}+\cdots+\mu_{n-k+1})
. Also,
W_{k}(A)=\{\xi\}
if and only if
A=\xi I/k
.

Note that (1) appears in
[
6
, (2.1)]
; (2) follows from
[
6
, (5.1)]
and the convexity of
W_{k}(A)

[
6
, (3.1)]
.
For (4) we refer to
[
6
, (4.3)]
(see also
[
8
, Theorem 4.4]
for a generalization) while (5) follows from property (1) and
[
6
, (5.1a)]
.

Our study concerns parallel pairs of matrices with respect to the
k
-numerical radius.
We refer to a recent paper
[
14
]
for the study of parallel pairs with respect to the numerical radius in
C^{*}
algebras
and
[
2
]
for the study of parallel pairs with respect to the joint numerical radius for
m
-tuples of matrices.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We say that the
k
-numerical radius of
A
is attained at a rank-
k
projection
P
if
|\mathop{\rm tr}AP|=w_{k}(A)
.
We begin with a few results concerning the properties of parallel pairs,
which are useful for our proofs and may be of independent interest.
We shall use the following lemma repeatedly without comment.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose
A,B\in{\mathbb{M}}_{n}
.
Then
A\|B
if and only if any one of the following
equivalent conditions holds.

(a)

There is a rank-
k
orthogonal projection

P
such that
w_{k}(A)=|\mathop{\rm tr}(AP)|
and
w_{k}(B)=|\mathop{\rm tr}(BP)|
.

(b)

There is a unitary
U
such that the leading
k\times k
blocks
A_{1},B_{2}
of
U^{*}AU
and
U^{*}BU

satisfy
w_{k}(A)=|\mathop{\rm tr}A_{1}|
and
w_{k}(B)=|\mathop{\rm tr}B_{1}|
.

Proof.

The statements follow readily from the definitions.

\Box

For
A\in{\mathbb{M}}_{n}
, let

{\mathcal{P}}(A)=\{B\in{\mathbb{M}}_{n}:\;B\hbox{ is parallel to }A\},

and for a rank-
k
orthogonal projection
P\in{\mathbb{M}}_{n}
, let us define

S(P)=\{B\in{\mathbb{M}}_{n}:\mathop{\rm tr}(BP)=w_{k}(B)\}.

By Lemma
2.1
, we have the following.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
1\leq k<n
,
A\in{\mathbb{M}}_{n}
.
Then

{\mathcal{P}}(A)=\bigcup\{e^{i\theta}S(P):\theta\in[0,2\pi),P^{2}=P=P^{*},\mathop{\rm tr}P=k,|\mathop{\rm tr}AP|=w_{k}(A)\}.

Recall that a set
{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq{\mathbb{M}}_{n}
is a convex cone if

rA+sB\in{\mathcal{S}}
whenever
A,B\in{\mathcal{C}}
and
r,s\in[0,\infty)
.
The real affine dimension of
{\mathcal{C}}
is the (real) dimension of

\mathop{\rm Span}_{\mathbb{R}}({\mathcal{C}})
, the linear span of
{\mathcal{C}}
over
{\mathbb{R}}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose
P\in{\mathbb{M}}_{n}
is a rank-
k
orthogonal projection. Then
S(P)
is a convex cone with real affine dimension

\gamma(n,k)=k^{2}+(n-k)^{2}+n^{2}-1.

Moreover, with respect to the direct sum decomposition
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}=\mathop{\rm Ran}P\oplus\mathop{\rm Ker}P
,

(2.1)

S(P)=\left\{B=\begin{pmatrix}B_{11}&B_{12}\\
-B_{12}^{*}&B_{22}\end{pmatrix}:B_{11}\in{\mathbb{M}}_{k},B_{22}\in{\mathbb{M}}_{n-k},B_{12}\in{\mathbb{M}}_{k\times(n-k)},\mathop{\rm tr}B_{11}=w_{k}(B)\right\}

and

\mathop{\mathop{\rm Span}}\nolimits_{{\mathbb{R}}}(S(P))=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}B_{11}&B_{12}\\
-B_{12}^{*}&B_{22}\end{pmatrix}:B_{11}\in{\mathbb{M}}_{k},B_{22}\in{\mathbb{M}}_{n-k},B_{12}\in{\mathbb{M}}_{k\times(n-k)},\mathop{\rm tr}B_{11}\in{\mathbb{R}}\right\}.

Proof.

Note that
B=H+iG\in S(P)
, with
H,G
hermitian, if and only
\mathop{\rm tr}(PHP)+i\mathop{\rm tr}(PGP)=w_{k}(B)
. Thus,
\mathop{\rm tr}(PGP)=0
. Moreover,
w_{k}(B)\geq w_{k}(H)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}(H)\geq\mathop{\rm tr}(PHP)=w_{k}(B)
shows that
\mathop{\rm tr}(PHP)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}(H)
.
By
[
4
, Lemma 4.1]
,
H=H_{1}\oplus H_{2}\in{\mathbb{H}}_{k}\oplus{\mathbb{H}}_{n-k}
is block diagonal with respect to the direct sum
decomposition
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}=\mathop{\rm Ran}P\oplus\mathop{\rm Ker}P
, and
\lambda_{k}(H_{1})\geq\lambda_{1}(H_{2})
. Thus
B\in S(P)
if and only if it has the form
in (
2.1
).

Let
B,C\in S(P)
and
b,c\geq 0
. Then
G=bB+cC
has the form

\begin{pmatrix}bB_{11}+cC_{11}&G_{12}\cr-G_{12}^{*}&G_{22}\cr\end{pmatrix}
and

w_{k}(G)\geq\mathop{\rm tr}GP=b\mathop{\rm tr}B_{11}+c\mathop{\rm tr}C_{11}=bw_{k}(B)+cw_{k}(C)\geq w_{k}(G),

whence
G\in S(P)
. Thus
S(P)
is a convex cone.

The affine dimension of
S(P)
is the dimension of the real linear subspace spanned by
S(P)
. By (
2.1
),
S(P)\subseteq S_{1}+iS_{2}
, where

S_{1}=\mathop{\mathop{\rm Span}}\nolimits_{{\mathbb{R}}}\{H_{1}\oplus H_{2}:H_{1}=H_{1}^{*}\in{\mathbb{M}}_{k},H_{2}=H_{2}^{*}\in{\mathbb{M}}_{n-k}\}
has dimension
k^{2}+(n-k)^{2}
, and

S_{2}=\mathop{\mathop{\rm Span}}\nolimits_{{\mathbb{R}}}\{G\in{\mathbb{M}}_{n}:G=G^{*},\mathop{\rm tr}PGP=\sum_{j=1}^{k}G_{jj}=0\}
has dimension
n^{2}-1
.
So,
\dim\mathop{\mathop{\rm Span}}\nolimits_{{\mathbb{R}}}S(P)\leq n^{2}+k^{2}+(n-k)^{2}-1=\gamma(n,k)
.
It suffices to show that
S_{1}+iS_{2}\subseteq\mathop{\mathop{\rm Span}}\nolimits_{{\mathbb{R}}}S(P)
.

Case 1.
Let
A=H_{1}\oplus A_{2}
such that

H_{1}^{*}=H_{1}\in{\mathbb{M}}_{k}
is positive-definite and

A_{2}\in{\mathbb{M}}_{n-k}

satisfies
\lambda_{k}(H_{1})\geq\|A_{2}\|
.
Then by property (4) in Proposition
1.3
, we see that

w_{k}(A)=\mathop{\rm tr}H_{1}
so that

A\in S(P)
. Consequently,
{\mathbb{H}}_{k}\oplus{\mathbb{M}}_{n-k}\subseteq\mathop{\mathop{\rm Span}}\nolimits_{{\mathbb{R}}}S(P)
.

Case 2.
Suppose
1\leq i\leq k<j\leq n
and

A=(I_{k}\oplus 0_{n-k})+i(\mu E_{ij}+\bar{\mu}E_{ji})
for
\mu\in{\mathbb{C}}
with
|\mu|=1/2
. Then
A
is permutationally similar to
A_{0}\oplus I_{k-1}\oplus 0_{n-k-1}
, where
A_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}1&i\mu\cr i\bar{\mu}&0\cr\end{pmatrix}
. Note that
W_{1}(A_{0})
is a circular disk of radius
1/2
centered at
1/2
. Then by property (4) in Proposition
1.3
,

w_{k}(A)\leq w_{1}(A_{0})+w_{k-1}(I_{k-1}\oplus 0_{n-k-1})=1+(k-1)=\mathop{\rm tr}(AP)\leq w_{k}(A),

whence
A\in S(P)
.
Combining this result with Case 1 shows that
iG\in\mathop{\mathop{\rm Span}}\nolimits_{{\mathbb{R}}}S(P)
for every Hermitian
G
of the form
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&G_{12}\\
G_{12}^{\ast}&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
with
G_{12}\in{\mathbb{M}}_{k\times(n-k)}.

Case 3.
Suppose
k>1
. Let
0\neq C\in{\mathbb{M}}_{k}
satisfy
\mathop{\rm tr}C=0
. Let
\epsilon=1/(2k\|C\|)
and let
A=(I_{k}+\epsilon C)\oplus 0_{n-k}
. Note that for any rank-
r
orthogonal projection
Q\in{\mathbb{M}}_{k}
with
r<k
,

|\mathop{\rm tr}((I_{k}+\epsilon C)Q)|\leq|\mathop{\rm tr}Q|+\epsilon|\mathop{\rm tr}(CQ)|\leq r+\epsilon r\|C\|<r+1/2<k=\mathop{\rm tr}(AP).

Then by property (4) in Proposition
1.3
,

\displaystyle w_{k}(A)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq r\leq k}w_{r}(I_{k}+\epsilon C)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\{|\mathop{\rm tr}(Q+\epsilon CQ)|:0\leq r\leq k,Q^{2}=Q^{*}=Q\in{\mathbb{M}}_{k},\mathop{\rm tr}Q=r\}\leq\mathop{\rm tr}(AP),

so
A\in S(P)
. Combining this result with Case 1 shows that
C\oplus 0_{n-k}\in\mathop{\mathop{\rm Span}}\nolimits_{{\mathbb{R}}}S(P)
for every
C\in{\mathbb{M}}_{k}
with
\mathop{\rm tr}C=0
.

Finally, cases 1 - 3 together show that
S_{1}+iS_{2}\subseteq\mathop{\mathop{\rm Span}}\nolimits_{{\mathbb{R}}}S(P)
, as desired.

\Box

In the following discussion, we always let

\gamma(n,k)=k^{2}+(n-k)^{2}+n^{2}-1.

Notice that two rank-
k
projections
P_{1},P_{2}
coincide if and only if
S(P_{1})=S(P_{2})
.
We shall show more in the next lemma.

Lemma 2.4
.

Given two rank-
k
orthogonal projections
P,Q
and real numbers
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}
, we have

\mathop{\mathop{\rm Span}}\nolimits_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{i\theta_{1}}S(P)=\mathop{\mathop{\rm Span}}\nolimits_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{i\theta_{2}}S(Q)

if and only if
P=Q
and
\theta_{1}=\theta_{2}
modulo
\pi
.

Proof.

If
P\neq Q
, then there exists a unit vector
x\in\mathop{\rm Ker}P
with
x\notin\mathop{\rm Ker}Q
. Let
G=xx^{*}
. By Lemma
2.3
,
e^{i\theta}G\in\mathop{\mathop{\rm Span}}\nolimits_{{\mathbb{R}}}S(P)
for all
\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}
, so
e^{i\theta}G\in\mathop{\mathop{\rm Span}}\nolimits_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{i\theta_{1}}S(P)
for all
\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}
. On the other hand,
x\notin\mathop{\rm Ker}Q
implies
Qx\neq 0
, and hence
\mathop{\rm tr}(QGQ)=\|Qx\|^{2}\neq 0
.
Take
\theta=\pi/4+\theta_{2}
. Then

e^{i(\theta-\theta_{2})}G=\cos(\pi/4)G+i\sin(\pi/4)G\notin\mathop{\mathop{\rm Span}}\nolimits_{{\mathbb{R}}}S(Q)

by Lemma
2.3
because
\mathop{\rm tr}Q(e^{i(\theta-\theta_{2})}G)Q=e^{i(\theta-\theta_{2})}\|Qx\|^{2}\notin{\mathbb{R}}
.
Hence
e^{i\theta}G\notin\mathop{\mathop{\rm Span}}\nolimits_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{i\theta_{2}}S(Q)
, so the spans are different.

Lastly, assume
P=Q
but
\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\notin{\mathbb{Z}}\pi
. Consider any unit vector

x\in\mathop{\rm Ran}P
.
By Lemma
2.3

H:=xx^{\ast}
belongs to
\mathop{\mathop{\rm Span}}\nolimits_{{\mathbb{R}}}S(P)
, so
e^{i\theta_{1}}H\in\mathop{\mathop{\rm Span}}\nolimits_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{i\theta_{1}}S(P)
. However,

e^{i(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})}H=\cos(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})H+i\sin(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})H

has nonzero imaginary part with
\mathop{\rm tr}(P\sin(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})HP)=\sin(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})\|x\|^{2}\neq 0
, and as such does not belong to
\mathop{\mathop{\rm Span}}\nolimits_{{\mathbb{R}}}S(P)
. Hence,
e^{i\theta_{1}}H\notin\mathop{\mathop{\rm Span}}\nolimits_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{i\theta_{2}}S(P)
.

\Box

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
T\colon{\mathbb{M}}_{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{M}}_{n}
be a bijective
{\mathbb{R}}
-linear map.
Suppose
P_{1}
,
P_{2}
are distinct rank-
k
orthogonal projections.
Then there do not exist a rank-
k
orthogonal projection
Q
and
\theta_{3}\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that
T(e^{i\theta_{1}}S(P_{1}))\cup T(e^{i\theta_{2}}S(P_{2}))\subseteq e^{i\theta_{3}}S(Q)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.3
,
e^{i\theta}S(P)
contains
\gamma(n,k)

{\mathbb{R}}
-linearly independent matrices for any rank-
k
projection
P
and any choice of
\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Since
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are distinct, Lemma
2.4
implies that

e^{i\theta_{1}}S(P_{1})\cup e^{i\theta_{2}}S(P_{2})
contains at least
\gamma(n,k)+1
real linearly independent matrices, and hence, by the bijectivity of
T
, so does
T(e^{i\theta_{1}}S(P_{1}))\cup T(e^{i\theta_{2}}S(P_{2}))
. By Lemma
2.3
, this exceeds the real affine dimension of
\mathop{\mathop{\rm Span}}\nolimits_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{i\theta_{3}}S(Q)
, so the result follows.

\Box

Let
{\mathbb{T}}=\{\mu\in{\mathbb{C}}:|\mu|=1\}\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}
denote the unimodular group.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
A\in{\mathbb{M}}_{n}
. Suppose
w_{k}(A)
is attained at a unique (rank-
k
) projection
P
,
w_{k}(A)=\mathop{\rm tr}(AP)
, and
\mathop{\rm Ran}P
is a reducing subspace for
A
.
Suppose
{\mathcal{C}}
is a cone with affine dimension greater or equal to
\gamma(n,k)=k^{2}+(n-k)^{2}+n^{2}-1
satisfying
A\in{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(A)
. Then
{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq S(P)
, so its affine dimension equals
\gamma(n,k)
.

Proof.

With respect to the decomposition
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}=\mathop{\rm Ran}P\oplus\mathop{\rm Ker}P
, we may write
A=\begin{pmatrix}A_{11}&0\\
0&A_{22}\end{pmatrix}
with
A_{11}\in{\mathbb{M}}_{k}
,
A_{22}\in{\mathbb{M}}_{n-k}
, and
\mathop{\rm tr}A_{11}=w_{k}(A)>0
. Note that, by Lemma
2.1
,
\mathcal{P}(A)={\mathbb{T}}S(P)
.
By Lemma
2.3
, every nonzero element of
{\mathcal{C}}
has the form
\mu B=\mu\begin{pmatrix}B_{11}&B_{12}\\
-B_{12}^{*}&B_{22}\end{pmatrix}
with
\mathop{\rm tr}B_{11}=w_{k}(B)>0
and
\mu\in{\mathbb{T}}
.

We first show that if
B_{12}\neq 0
, then
\mu=1
.

Since
{\mathcal{C}}
is a cone, for every
r>0

A+r\mu B\in{\mathcal{C}}\subset{\mathbb{T}}S(P)
, so

(2.2)

\begin{pmatrix}A_{11}+r\mu B_{11}&r\mu B_{12}\\
-r\mu B_{12}^{*}&A_{22}+r\mu B_{22}\end{pmatrix}=\nu\begin{pmatrix}C_{11}&C_{12}\\
-C_{12}^{*}&C_{22}\end{pmatrix}=\nu C

for some
\nu\in{\mathbb{T}}
and some
k\times(n-k)
matrix
C_{12}
(both of which may depend on
r
). Equating the
(2,1)
-block entries and taking the conjugate transpose gives
-r\bar{\mu}B_{12}=-\bar{\nu}C_{12}
, so

\nu^{2}r\bar{\mu}B_{12}=\nu C_{12}=r\mu B_{12}

(by equating the
(1,2)
-block entries in (
2.2
)), whence
\nu=\pm\mu
.

Equating the
(1,1)
-block entries in (
2.2
) and taking traces gives

\mathop{\rm tr}A_{11}+r\mu\mathop{\rm tr}B_{11}=\pm\mu\mathop{\rm tr}C_{11}
. Because
\mathop{\rm tr}A_{11}
,
\mathop{\rm tr}B_{11}
,
\mathop{\rm tr}C_{11}
are all positive,
\mu=\pm 1
. If
\mu=-1
we can choose
r=\mathop{\rm tr}A_{11}/\mathop{\rm tr}B_{11}
to get
0=\pm\mathop{\rm tr}C_{11}=\pm w_{k}(C)\neq 0
, a contradiction. So
\mu=1
as claimed.

If
0\neq D:=D_{11}\oplus D_{22}\in{\mathcal{C}}
and
{\mathcal{C}}
contains a matrix
B
with
B_{12}=PB(I-P)\neq 0
, then the above arguments applied to
D+rB\in{\mathcal{C}}
(
r>0
), give that
\mathop{\rm tr}(D_{11}+rB_{11})>0
for every
r>0
, and hence
\mathop{\rm tr}D_{11}\geq 0
; thus
D\in S(P)
by Lemma
2.3
.

Consequently
{\mathcal{C}}\subset S(P)
, unless one never has
B_{12}\neq 0
, that is, if
{\mathcal{C}}\subset{\mathbb{M}}_{k}\oplus{\mathbb{M}}_{n-k}
. But the latter is impossible: since

\dim_{{\mathbb{R}}}{\mathbb{M}}_{k}\oplus{\mathbb{M}}_{n-k}=2k^{2}+2(n-k)^{2}<k^{2}+(n-k)^{2}+n^{2}-1=\gamma(n,k),

{\mathcal{C}}
is not contained in
{\mathbb{M}}_{k}\oplus{\mathbb{M}}_{n-k}
.

\Box

We have established basic properties for parallel pairs of matrices with respect to the
k
-numerical radius.
Now, we focus on bijective linear maps preserving parallel pairs. The following lemma shows that

is a scalar multiple of

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
A\in{\mathbb{M}}_{n}
. The following are equivalent.

(a)

A=\gamma I
for some
\gamma\in{\mathbb{C}}
.

(b)

A\|B
for every
B\in{\mathbb{M}}_{n}
.

(c)

A\|P
for every rank-
k
orthogonal projection
P\in{\mathbb{M}}_{n}
.

Proof.

Suppose (a) holds and
A=\gamma I
with

\gamma\in{\mathbb{C}}
. Note for any
B\in{\mathbb{M}}_{n}
, there is a rank-
k
projection

P
such that
|\mathop{\rm tr}(BP)|=w_{k}(B)
. So there is
a complex unit
\xi
such that

w_{k}(A+\xi B)\geq|\mathop{\rm tr}(\gamma I+\xi B)P|=|\gamma\mathop{\rm tr}P|+|\mathop{\rm tr}(BP)|=w_{k}(A)+w_{k}(B).

Thus,
B\in{\mathcal{P}}(A)
. Hence (b) holds.
The implication (b)
\Rightarrow
(c) is clear.

Suppose (c) holds. Let
P
be a rank-
k
orthogonal projection.
By Lemma
2.1
, there exists a rank-
k
orthogonal projection
Q
satisfying
w_{k}(A)=|\mathop{\rm tr}AQ|
and
k=w_{k}(P)=|\mathop{\rm tr}PQ|=\mathop{\rm tr}PQ

(see
[
4
, Lemma 4.1]
). Thus
|\mathop{\rm tr}AP|=w_{k}(A)
for every rank-
k
orthogonal projection
P
, so
W_{k}(A)
lies on a circle. Since
W_{k}(A)
is convex (see
[
6
]
or
[
13
]
),

must be a singleton and the result follows by property (5) of Proposition
1.3
.

\Box

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
T\colon{\mathbb{M}}_{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{M}}_{n}
be a bijective linear map preserving parallel pairs with respect to the
k
-numerical radius. Suppose
A
attains its
k
-numerical radius at a unique projection
Q
, and suppose
\mathop{\rm Ran}Q
is reducing for
A
. Then
X=T^{-1}(A)
also attains its
k
-numerical radius at a unique projection.

Proof.

Let a unimodular
\mu\in{\mathbb{C}}
be such that
\mathop{\rm tr}[(\mu A)Q]=w_{k}(A)=w_{k}(\mu A)
; clearly,
\mathop{\rm Ran}Q
is reducing also for
\mu A
. Suppose, to reach a contradiction, that
P_{1}
,
P_{2}
are distinct rank-
k
orthogonal projections such that
e^{-i\theta_{j}}\mathop{\rm tr}XP_{j}=w_{k}(X)
for some
\theta_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}
with

.
By the definition of

and Lemma

, we have
X\in e^{i\theta_{j}}S(P_{j})\subseteq{\mathcal{P}}(X)
. Applying
T
gives

A\in T(e^{i\theta_{j}}S(P_{j}))\subseteq T({\mathcal{P}}(X))\subseteq\mathcal{P}(T(X))={\mathcal{P}}(A).

By Lemma
2.3
, the cone
e^{i\theta}S(P)
contains
\gamma(n,k)

{\mathbb{R}}
-linearly independent matrices for any rank-
k
projection
P
and any choice of
\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Thus, by bijectivity of
T
,
T(e^{i\theta_{j}}S(P_{j}))
is a cone of affine dimension
\gamma(n,k)
contained in
{\mathcal{P}}(A)
. As such,
\mu T(e^{i\theta_{j}}S(P_{j}))
is also a cone of dimension
\gamma(n,k)
contained in
\mu{\mathcal{P}}(A)={\mathcal{P}}(\mu A)

\mu A
.
Hence, by Lemma
2.6
, we have

\mu T(e^{i\theta_{j}}S(P_{j}))\subseteq S(Q).

This contradicts Lemma
2.5
.

\Box

Lemma 2.9
.

Suppose
T\colon{\mathbb{M}}_{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{M}}_{n}
is a bijective unital linear map
preserving parallel pairs with respect to the
k
-numerical radius.
If
A\in{\mathbb{M}}_{n}
satisfies
W_{k}(A)=[-1,1]
and
T(X)=A
, then
W_{k}(X)=[-1,1]
.

Proof.

Since
W_{k}(A)=[-1,1]
, we see that
A=A^{*}
by Proposition
1.3
(5).
Assume that
A
has eigenvalues
a_{1}\geq\cdots\geq a_{n}
with corresponding
orthonormal eigenvectors
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
, and
T(X)=A
.
We consider two cases.

Case 1
Suppose
a_{k}>a_{k+1}
,
a_{n-k}>a_{n-k+1}
.

We have
\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}=1=-\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{n+1-j}
.
Let
P_{+}
be the rank-
k
projection whose range is spanned by
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\}
.
Let
\gamma
be an arbitrary complex number with
\mathrm{Re}\,\gamma>0
.
By Proposition
1.3
(1),
W_{k}(A+\gamma I)=W_{k}(A)+k\gamma=[-1+k\gamma,1+k\gamma]
.
Thus
w_{k}(A+\gamma I)
is attained at precisely those rank-
k
projections
P
satisfying
\mathop{\rm tr}AP=1
.
By
[
4
, Lemma 4.1]
and Schur’s result that the sum of the
k
largest diagonal entries of a hermitian matrix is bounded above
by the sum of its
k
largest eigenvalues
[
12
, Theorem B.1, p. 300]
(or by the interlacing property for the eigenvalues
of a compression of hermitian matrix) we see that
P=P_{+}
is the unique rank-
k
projection satisfying
|\mathop{\rm tr}(P(A+\gamma I)P)|=w_{k}(A+\gamma I)=|1+k\gamma|
. We may write

w_{k}(A+\gamma I)=e^{i\theta_{\gamma}}\mathop{\rm tr}(A+\gamma I)P_{+}
for some
\theta_{\gamma}\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Suppose
w_{k}(X+\gamma I)
is attained at a rank-
k
projection
Q_{\gamma}
. Then, by Lemma
2.2
,

(2.3)

X+\gamma I\in e^{i\phi_{\gamma}}S(Q_{\gamma})\subseteq\mathcal{P}(X+\gamma I)

for some
\phi_{\gamma}\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Applying
T
we have

A+\gamma I=T(X+\gamma I)\in T(e^{i\phi_{\gamma}}S(Q_{\gamma}))\subseteq T(\mathcal{P}(X+\gamma I))\subseteq\mathcal{P}(T(X+\gamma I))=\mathcal{P}(A+\gamma I).

Multiplying by
e^{i\theta_{\gamma}}
gives

e^{i\theta_{\gamma}}(A+\gamma I)\in e^{i(\theta_{\gamma}+\phi_{\gamma})}T(S(Q_{\gamma}))\subseteq\mathcal{P}(e^{i\theta_{\gamma}}(A+\gamma I))=\mathcal{P}(A+\gamma I).

Since
w_{k}(e^{i\theta_{\gamma}}(A+\gamma I))=\mathop{\rm tr}(e^{i\theta_{\gamma}}(A+\gamma I)P_{+})
is attained uniquely at
P_{+}
,
and since
\mathop{\rm Ran}P_{+}
reduces
e^{i\theta_{\gamma}}(A+\gamma I)
, and
e^{i(\theta_{\gamma}+\phi_{\gamma})}T(S(Q_{\gamma}))

is a cone of dimension
\gamma(n,k)
, we can apply Lemma
2.6
to conclude that

e^{i(\theta_{\gamma}+\phi_{\gamma})}T(S(Q_{\gamma}))=T(e^{i(\theta_{\gamma}+\phi_{\gamma})}S(Q_{\gamma}))\subseteq S(P_{+})
.

But this holds for all
\gamma
with positive real part. Hence, by Lemma
2.5
,
there is a single projection
Q_{+}
such that
Q_{\gamma}=Q_{+}
for all
\gamma
with
\text{Re}\,\gamma>0
.
Let
z_{0}=\mathop{\rm tr}XQ_{+}\in W_{k}(X)
. Then

w_{k}(X+\gamma I)=|\mathop{\rm tr}(X+\gamma I)Q_{+}|=|\gamma k+\mathop{\rm tr}XQ_{+}|=|\gamma k+z_{0}|

for all
\gamma
with positive real part; by continuity, this holds for all
\gamma
with nonnegative real part. In particular, if

w=w_{1}+iw_{2}
is the Cartesian decomposition of
w\in W_{k}(X)
(and likewise for
\gamma=\gamma_{1}+i\gamma_{2}
and
z_{0}=x_{0}+iy_{0}
), we have

(w_{1}+k\gamma_{1})^{2}+(w_{2}+k\gamma_{2})^{2}\leq(x_{0}+k\gamma_{1})^{2}+(y_{0}+k\gamma_{2})^{2};\qquad\gamma_{1}\geq 0.

When
\gamma_{1}=0
this simplifies into the inequality

w_{1}^{2}-x_{0}^{2}\leq(y_{0}+k\gamma_{2})^{2}-(w_{2}+k\gamma_{2})^{2}=(y_{0}-w_{2})(2k\gamma_{2}+w_{2}+y_{0}),

valid for every
\gamma_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, which is possible only if
w_{2}=y_{0}
. Thus
W_{k}(X)
lies in the
horizontal line segment
[-x_{0}+iy_{0}\,,\,x_{0}+iy_{0}]
. It follows that the
k
-numerical range of
\hat{X}:=X-\frac{y_{0}}{k}iI_{n}

lies in a real line, so
\hat{X}
is Hermitian by
[
6
, 5.1(a)]
. Moreover, by the uniqueness of the rank-
k
projection

Q_{+}=Q_{\gamma}
in (
2.3
),
we must have
\lambda_{k}(\hat{X})>\lambda_{k+1}(\hat{X})
, where as usual we organize the eigenvalues of
\hat{X}
in descending
order
\lambda_{1}(\hat{X})\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{n}(\hat{X})
, and
Q_{+}
is the projection onto the subspace spanned by the
eigenvectors of
\hat{X}
corresponding to
\lambda_{1}(\hat{X}),\dots,\lambda_{k}(\hat{X})
.

Now let
P_{-}
be the rank-
k
projection whose range is spanned by
\{v_{n-k+1},\dots,v_{n}\}
.
Then for all complex
\gamma
with
\mathrm{Re}\,\gamma<0
,
P=P_{-}
is the only rank-
k
projection satisfying

|\mathop{\rm tr}(P(A+\gamma I)P)|=w_{k}(A+\gamma I)=|1+k\gamma|
.
Hence, we may repeat the preceding arguments to conclude that there exists a unique projection
Q_{-}
for which (
2.3
) holds
true when
\mathrm{Re}\,\gamma<0
. Consequently
\lambda_{n-k}(\hat{X})>\lambda_{n-k+1}(\hat{X})
and
Q_{-}
is the projection onto the
subspace spanned by the eigenvectors of
\hat{X}
corresponding to
\lambda_{n-k+1}(\hat{X}),\dots,\lambda_{n}(\hat{X})
.

To summarize,
|\mathop{\rm tr}(\hat{X}\pm rI)Q_{\pm}|=w_{k}(\hat{X}\pm rI)
for all
r>0
, so by continuity,

w_{k}(\hat{X})=|\mathop{\rm tr}\hat{X}Q_{+}|=|\mathop{\rm tr}\hat{X}Q_{-}|
is attained at both
Q_{+}
and
Q_{-}
.
Since
\lambda_{k}(\hat{X})>\lambda_{k+1}(\hat{X})
,

\mathop{\rm tr}\hat{X}Q_{+}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}(\hat{X})>\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{n+1-j}(\hat{X})=\mathop{\rm tr}\hat{X}Q_{-},

so
\mathop{\rm tr}\hat{X}Q_{-}=-\mathop{\rm tr}\hat{X}Q_{+}
. This shows that
W_{k}(X)=[-b+iy_{0},b+iy_{0}]
, for some
b>0
, is symmetric
relative to imaginary axis.

Finally, let
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
satisfy Re

. Then
X+zI\in(b+iy_{0}+kz)S(Q_{+})
. Applying
T
, and noting that

T(S(Q_{+}))\subseteq\mu S(P_{+})
for some
\mu\in{\mathbb{T}}
, gives

A+zI\in(b+iy_{0}+kz)T(S(Q_{+}))\subset(b+iy_{0}+kz)\mu S(P_{+}).

Divide by
1+kz
to get

\frac{A+zI}{1+kz}\in\frac{b+iy_{0}+kz}{1+kz}T(S(Q_{+}))\subseteq\frac{b+iy_{0}+kz}{1+kz}\mu S(P_{+})\subseteq\mathcal{P}\left(\frac{A+zI}{1+kz}\right).

Since
w_{k}((A+zI)/(1+kz))=\mathop{\rm tr}P_{+}(A+zI)/(1+kz)
is attained uniquely at
P_{+}
, we can apply Lemma 2.5 to
conclude that the cone
{\mathcal{C}}=\frac{b+iy_{0}+kz}{1+kz}\mu S(P_{+})\subseteq S(P_{+})
. Thus
f(z)=\frac{b+iy_{0}+kz}{1+kz}

has constant argument for Re
z>0
. In other words, the Möbius transformation
f

maps the open right-half plane into a ray, so it must be a constant function, whence
b+iy_{0}=1
.
Thus,
W_{k}(X)=[-1,1]
as asserted.

Case 2.

Suppose

a_{k}=a_{k+1}
or
a_{n-k}=a_{n-k+1}
.
For
m=1,2,\dots
, let

, where

with
\beta_{1}>\cdots>\beta_{n}

Then
\lambda_{k}(A_{m})>\lambda_{k+1}(A_{m})
,
\lambda_{n-k}(A_{m})>\lambda_{n-k+1}(A_{m})

and

. Then

Since

.
Hence,

.

Lemma 3.13

T(X)=Y=\frac{1}{c}UXU^{\ast}-\frac{1}{c}f(UXU^{\ast})I.

To see this, start with
X=T^{-1}(Y)=cU^{\ast}YU+f(Y)
, then
Y=\frac{1}{c}UXU^{\ast}-\frac{1}{c}f(Y)I
; applying
f(\cdot)
and using
f(I)=1-c

we deduce that
T(X)=Y=\frac{1}{c}UXU^{\ast}-\frac{1}{c}f(UXU^{\ast})I
. Define
\tilde{T}
by
\tilde{T}(X)=cU^{\ast}T(X)U
, so
\tilde{T}
preserves parallel pairs and

\tilde{T}(X)=X+g(X)I,

where
g(X):=-f(UXU^{\ast})
.

Suppose, by way of contradiction, that
g
is not a scalar multiple of
the trace functional. Then there exists a Hermitian
X\in\mathop{\rm Ker}\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\setminus\mathop{\rm Ker}g
with all its eigenvalues simple (since the set of trace-zero Hermitian matrices with all eigenvalues simple is dense in
\mathop{\rm Ker}\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\cap{\mathbb{H}}_{n}
). We can assume that
X=X_{1}\oplus X_{2}
,
X_{1}={\rm diag}\,(\lambda_{1}(X),\dots,\lambda_{k}(X))
,
X_{2}={\rm diag}\,(\lambda_{k+1}(X),\dots,\lambda_{n}(X))
, and
\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}(X_{1})=-\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}(X_{2})=w_{k}(X)
. Since all eigenvalues of
X
are simple, it attains its
k
-numerical radius at exactly two rank-
k
projections,
P=E_{11}+\dots+E_{kk}
and
I-P
. As such,
{\mathcal{P}}(X)=(-S(P))\cup S(P)\cup(-S(I-P))\cup S(I-P)
contains a two-dimensional
{\mathbb{R}}
-subspace, namely
{\mathbb{R}}I_{k}\oplus{\mathbb{R}}I_{k}={\mathbb{R}}(I_{k}\oplus 0_{k})+{\mathbb{R}}(0_{k}\oplus I_{k})
.
This is mapped into
\tilde{T}(X)=X+g(X)I
which achieves its
k
-numerical radius at exactly one rank-
k
projection (if

g(X)>0
this is
P
; if
g(X)<0
this would be
I-P
). But then
(-S(P))\cup S(P)={\mathcal{P}}(\tilde{T}(X))\supseteq\tilde{T}({\mathcal{P}}(X))
would contain a two-dimensional subspace, contradicting Lemma
3.13
.
Thus
g
is a scalar multiple of the trace functional, and hence so is
f
.

\Box

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

The implication (b)
\Rightarrow
(a) is clear.
The implication (a)
\Rightarrow
(c) follows from Lemma
3.14
if
n\geq 3
, so suppose (a) holds and
n=2
. We may assume that
T
is unital by Lemma
3.7
.
Suppose a nonzero
X\in{\mathbb{H}}_{2}
has trace zero. Then
X
attains its numerical radius at exactly two rank-1 projections
P
and
I-P
. Thus
\mathcal{P}(X)=S(P)\cup(-S(P))\cup(-S(I-P))\cup S(I-P)
contains a two-dimensional subspace
{\mathbb{R}}P+{\mathbb{R}}(I-P)
.
Since
T(\mathcal{P}(X))\subseteq\mathcal{P}(T(X))
and
T
is bijective,
\mathcal{P}(T(X))
contains a two-dimensional subspace. It follows that
\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}T(X)=0
. (Reason: If
\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}T(X)\neq 0
, then
T(X)
(which can’t be a scalar multiple of
I
due to the bijectivity of
T
) must attain its numerical radius at a unique projection
Q
, so
\mathcal{P}(T(X))=S(Q)\cup-S(Q)
contains a two-dimensional subspace, contradicting Lemma
3.13
.) It follows that
T
preserves the set of trace-zero matrices; since
T
is also unital, it preserves the trace and (c.3) holds.

It remains to prove (c)
\Rightarrow
(b).
If (c.1) holds, then clearly (b) holds.

Suppose (c.2) holds. We may replace
T
by
A\mapsto\gamma UT(A^{+})U^{*}
for a suitable nonzero
\gamma
and unitary
U
(here
A^{+}
denotes
A
or
A^{t}
, depending on the case in (c.2)) and assume that
T
is unital and
T(X)=cX
for all
X\in{\mathbb{H}}_{n}^{0}
. Now suppose
(A_{1},A_{2})
is a nontrivial TEA pair (that is,
A_{1}
,
A_{2}
are nonzero). If
A_{1}+A_{2}
attains its
k
-numerical radius at a rank-
k
projection
P
then

|\mathop{\rm tr}A_{1}P|+|\mathop{\rm tr}A_{2}P|\leq w_{k}(A_{1})+w_{k}(A_{2})=w_{k}(A_{1}+A_{2})=|\mathop{\rm tr}(A_{1}+A_{2})P|\leq|\mathop{\rm tr}A_{1}P|+|\mathop{\rm tr}A_{2}P|,

so
w_{k}(A_{j})=|\mathop{\rm tr}A_{j}P|
and

\mathop{\rm tr}A_{1}P
,
\mathop{\rm tr}A_{2}P
have the same sign. Moreover,
\mathop{\rm Ran}P
is a reducing subspace for both
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
. If needed, we may replace
A_{j}
by
-A_{j}
and assume
\mathop{\rm tr}A_{j}P>0
.

We may write
A_{j}=a_{j}I+Z_{j}
where
\mathop{\rm tr}Z_{j}=0
. With respect to the decomposition
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}=\mathop{\rm Ran}P\oplus\mathop{\rm Ker}P
, we may further write
Z_{j}=X_{j}\oplus Y_{j}
with
\mathop{\rm tr}Y_{j}=-\mathop{\rm tr}X_{j}
. Then
w_{k}(A_{j})=a_{j}k+\mathop{\rm tr}X_{j}
and
a_{j},\mathop{\rm tr}X_{j}\geq 0
(if they had opposite signs,
|\mathop{\rm tr}A_{j}(I-P)|=|a_{j}k-\mathop{\rm tr}X_{1}|>|a_{j}k+\mathop{\rm tr}X_{1}|=\mathop{\rm tr}A_{j}P=w_{k}(A_{j})
, a contradiction).

By Proposition
1.3
(2) it follows that
\mathop{\rm tr}X_{j}
is the sum of the
k
largest eigenvalues of
Z_{j}
. Thus
T(A_{j})=a_{j}I+cZ_{j}
attains its
k
-numerical radius at
P
if
c>0
and at
I-P
if
c<0
, so
T(A_{1}),T(A_{2})
form a TEA pair. Thus (b) holds.

Finally, suppose
n=2
and (c.3) holds. We may scale
T
and assume
T
is unital. Suppose
(A_{1},A_{2})
is a nontrivial TEA pair. As above when we assumed (c.2), we may write
A_{j}=a_{j}I+x_{j}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
with respect to a suitable basis. Write
X_{0}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. Since
T
preserves trace, we may replace
T
by
A\mapsto UAU^{*}
for a suitable unitary and assume that
T(X_{0})=cX_{0}
for some nonzero
c
. Then the argument for (c.2) applies and we conclude that
T(A_{1}),T(A_{2})
form a TEA pair, so (b) holds.

\Box

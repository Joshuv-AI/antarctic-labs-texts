Non-principal Euclidean ideal class in a family of biquadratic fields with the class number two
Srilakshmi Krishnamoorthy, Sunil Kumar Pasupulati

Abstract
. Lenstra introduced the notion of a Euclidean ideal class, which is a generalization of the Euclidean domain. Lenstra also proved that the Euclidean ideal in a number field K K implies that the class group of K K is cyclic. We construct a family of biquadratic fields that have a Euclidean ideal whenever the class number is 2. This extends the families given by Graves, Hsu, Chattopadhyay and Muthukrishnan.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary:11A05, Secondary 11R29.

1.
Introduction

For a number field
K
, let
\mathcal{O}_{K},Cl_{K},h_{K},\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times},H(K)
, and
f(K)
denote the ring of integers of
K
, the ideal class group of
K
,
the class number of
K
, the multiplicative group of units of
K
, the Hilbert class field of
K
, and the conductor of
K
, respectively. If
L/K
is an extension of number fields and a prime
\mathfrak{P}
of
L
lies above a prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
K
, then the residue degree
f(\mathfrak{P}/\mathfrak{p})
is equal to the dimension of
\mathcal{O}_{L}/\mathfrak{P}
as
\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{p}
vector space.

In 1979, Lenstra
[
11
]
introduced Euclidean ideals, which are generalization of Euclidean domains.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
R
be a Dedekind domain and
\mathbb{I}
be the set of all non-zero integral ideals of
R
. The ideal
C\in\mathbb{I}
is called a
Euclidean ideal
if there exists a function
\Psi:\mathbb{I}\to W
, where
W
is a well-ordered set, such that for all
I\in\mathbb{I}
and every
x\in I^{-1}C\setminus C
, there exists a
y\in C
such that

\Psi\left((x-y)IC^{-1}\right)<\Psi(I).

We say
\Psi
is a Euclidean function for
C
.
If
C
is a Euclidean ideal, then every ideal in the ideal class
[C]
is also a Euclidean ideal, and the ideal class
[C]
is called a
Euclidean ideal class.

Lenstra
[
11
]
proved that if
C
is a Euclidean ideal, then the ideal class
[C]
generates
Cl_{K}
, hence
Cl_{K}
is cyclic. Nevertheless the converse need not be true. For example, the imaginary quadratic fields
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})
, where
d=19,43,67,163,
have trivial class groups but do not contain a Euclidean ideal.
Lenstra
[
11
]
proved that suppose
K
is a number field with
\rank(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times})\geq 1
, under the assumption of the generalized Riemann hypothesis
(GRH),
Cl_{K}
is cyclic if and only if
K
has a Euclidean ideal class.
More precisely, assuming the GRH, a number field, not an imaginary quadratic field, has a cyclic class group if and only if it has a Euclidean ideal class.

Graves
[
5
]
constructed an explicit biquadratic field
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2},\sqrt{35})
which has a non-principal Euclidean ideal class by using a growth result (
Theorem

2.7
). This was generalized by Hsu

[
8
]
, in which the author explicitly constructed a family of real biquadratic fields
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q},\sqrt{kr})
that have a non-principal Euclidean ideal class, whenever
h_{K}=2
and
q,k,r
are all primes congruent to 1 modulo 4 which are greater than
29
.
Chattopadhyay and Muthukrishnan
[
1
]
extended Hsu’s family of biquadratic fields by removing the condition
q\equiv 1\mod 4
.

In this paper, we provide the following new family of real biquadratic fields
K
that have a non-principal Euclidean ideal class whenever
h_{K}=2
by assuming only
one of
r,k
is congruent to
1
modulo
4
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q},\sqrt{kr})
, either
k
or
r

is congruent to
1
modulo
4
. Suppose that
q,r
, and
k
are prime numbers, none of which is
2,3,5,7
or
17
and
h_{K}=2
, then
K
has a non-principal Euclidean ideal class.

The real biquadratic field
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q},\sqrt{kr})
has unit rank
3
but we cannot apply
[
3
, Theorem 2]
and conclude that K has a Euclidean ideal, as

{\mathrm{Gal}}\left(\mathbb{Q}\left(\zeta_{f\left(H(K)\right)}\right)/K\right)
is not necessarily cyclic, however we may apply
Theorem

1.2
. For example,
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{13},\sqrt{19\times 37})
, then some simple SageMath calculations and
Lemma

3.1
imply that the Hilbert class field of
K
is
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{13},\sqrt{19},\sqrt{37})
and its conductor is equal to 36556. The Galois group

{\mathrm{Gal}}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{f})/K)\cong\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/9\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/36\mathbb{Z}
is not cyclic. But we can conclude that
K
has a non-principal Euclidean ideal class by
Theorem

1.2
.

The organization of the paper is as follows. In
Section

2
, we write some useful preliminaries for proving the main result. In
Section

3
, we prove
Theorem

1.2
. In
Section

4
, we provide some examples of real biquadratic fields satisfying the assumptions of
Theorem

1.2
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Definitions and results

Definition 2.1
.

Let
K/\mathbb{Q}
be a finite abelian extension. The
conductor
of
K
, which is denoted by
f(K)
is the smallest even number such that
K\subset\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{f(K)})
.

Definition 2.2
.

The
Hilbert class field

H(K)
of a number field
K
is the maximal unramified abelian extension of
K
.

For example, If
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{11},\sqrt{43\times 13})
, then
H(K)=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{11},\sqrt{43},\sqrt{13})
.

.

Let
K
be a number field and
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime of
K
lies over a rational prime
p
. The prime
\mathfrak{p}
is called a prime of first degree if
\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{p}\cong\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
L_{1},L_{2}
be finite extensions of number field
K
. Suppose the prime
p
in
K
is unramified in
L_{1}
. If
\mathfrak{P}_{2}
is a prime of
L_{2}
lying above
p
, then
\mathfrak{P}_{2}
is unramified in
L_{1}L_{2}
over
L_{2}
.

Proof.

The proof is evident from
[
9
, Lemma 2.2]
∎

.

.

2.2.
Few results on Euclidean ideals

Graves
[
6
]
proved a useful growth result, which gives a condition for the existence of a Euclidean ideal in number fields, without the assumption of the GRH. We state the result below, which is one of the main ingredients for proving our theorem.

Theorem 2.7
.

(Graves
[
6
]
)
Suppose that K is a number field such that
\rank(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times})\geq 1

and that
C
is a non-zero ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. If
[C]
generates the class group of
K
and

\Biggr|\{\mathfrak{P}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}:\mathfrak{P}\text{\ is \ prime },\ \mathbb{N}(\mathfrak{P})\leq x,\ [\mathfrak{P}]=[C],\ \pi_{\mathfrak{P}}\text{\ is \ onto}\}\Biggr|\gg\frac{x}{(\log x)^{2}},

where
\pi_{\mathfrak{P}}
is the canonical map from
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}\to(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{P})^{\times}
, then
[C]
is a Euclidean ideal class.

Using the above growth result, Graves and Murty
[
7
]
proved the existence of a Euclidean ideal class in Galois number field
K
with abelian Hilbert class field
H(K)
and
\rank(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times})\geq 4
.

Theorem 2.8
.

(Graves
[
5
]
)
Let K be a totally real number field with conductor
f(K)
and let

\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
be a multiplicatively independent set contained
in
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
. If
l=\lcm(16,f(K)),
and if

\gcd(u,l)=\gcd(\frac{u-1}{2},l)=1
for some integer u, then

\displaystyle\left|\begin{Bmatrix}\mathfrak{P}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K},\quad\mathfrak{P}\ \text{is a prime }&\Bigm|&N(\mathfrak{P})\equiv u\pmod{l},\ N(\mathfrak{P})\leq x,\\
\text{ of first degree }&\Bigm|&\langle-1,e_{i}\rangle\twoheadrightarrow(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{P})^{\times}\end{Bmatrix}\right|\gg\frac{x}{(\log x)^{2}},

for at least one
i
, where
\langle-1,e_{i}\rangle
is multiplicative group generated by
-1
and
e_{i}
.

2.3.
The Artin symbol and its applications

Let us state a lemma, which is essential to define the Artin symbol.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
K\subset L
be a Galois extension of number fields and let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime
in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
which is unramified in L. If
\mathfrak{P}
is prime of
\mathcal{O}_{L}
containing

\mathfrak{p}
, then there exists a unique element
\sigma\in Gal(L/K)
such that for
all
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{L}
,

\displaystyle\sigma(\alpha)\equiv\alpha^{Nm(\mathfrak{p})}\pmod{\mathfrak{P}},

where
Nm(\mathfrak{p})=|\frac{\mathcal{O}_{K}}{\mathfrak{p}}|
is the norm of
\mathfrak{p}
.

Proof.

Refer to
[
2
, Lemma 5.19]
.
∎

Definition 2.10
.

The unique element
\sigma
in the above lemma is called the
Artin symbol
and it is denoted by
\left(\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak{P}}\right)
.

In the next lemma, we state some useful properties of the Artin symbol which will be used in later part of this paper.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
K\subset L
be a Galois extension and the prime
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
be unramified in
L
. Given a prime
\mathfrak{P}
of
L
containing
\mathfrak{p}
,

(1)

If
\sigma\in Gal(L/K)
, then

\left(\frac{L/K}{\sigma(\mathfrak{P})}\right)=\sigma\left(\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak{P}}\right)\sigma^{-1}.

(2)

The prime
\mathfrak{p}
splits completely in
L
if and only if
\left(\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak{P}}\right)=1
.

Proof.

Refer to
[
2
, Lemma 5.21]
.
∎

Remark 2.12
.

Let
L/K
be an abelian extension and the prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
K
is unramified in
L
. For any two primes
\mathfrak{P}_{1},\mathfrak{P}_{2}
of
L
which lie above a prime
\mathfrak{p}
, we have
\left(\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak{P}_{1}}\right)=\left(\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak{P}_{2}}\right)
. Therefore for any prime
\mathfrak{P}
in
L
, which lies above
\mathfrak{p}
, the Artin symbol is also denoted by
\left(\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak{p}}\right)
.

Definition 2.13
.

Let
K
be a number field and let
\mathbb{P}_{K}
be the set of all prime ideals of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. If
\mathcal{S}
is a subset of
\mathbb{P}_{K}
, then the
Dirichlet density
of the set
\mathcal{S}
is defined to be

\delta(\mathcal{S})=\text{lim}_{s\to 1^{+}}\frac{\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{S}}Nm(\mathfrak{p})^{-s}}{-\log(s-1)},

provided the limit exists, where
Nm(\mathfrak{p})=|\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{p}|
.

Now we recall the Chebotarev density theorem
[
2
, Theorem 8.17]
, which helps to calculate the Dirichlet density of a set.

Theorem 2.14
.

Let
L/K
be a finite Galois extension of global fields and let
\mathcal{C}
be a conjugacy class in
{\mathrm{Gal}}(L/K)
. The Dirichlet density of the set

\Big\{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{K}\Bigm|\ \mathfrak{p}\ \text{ is unramified in $L$ and }\left(\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak{p}}\right)=\mathcal{C}\Big\}

exists and it is equal to
\frac{|\mathcal{C}|}{[L:K]}
.

We end this section by stating a lemma whose proof is evident from
[
2
, Corollary 5.21, Corollary 5.25]
.

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
K
be a number field. Suppose
\mathfrak{P}
and
\mathfrak{p}
are primes of
H(K)
and
K
respectively, which lies above the rational prime p. If
\mathfrak{P}/p
has residue degree greater than or equal to
2
and
\mathfrak{p}/p
has residue degree
1
, then
\mathfrak{p}
is a non-principal ideal.

3.
Proof of the Main Theorem

We start this section by explicitly calculating the Hilbert class field of
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q},\sqrt{kr}),
which plays a crucial role in the proof of our main theorem.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q},\sqrt{kr})
and at least one of
k,r

is congruent to
1
modulo
4
, then
H(K)=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q},\sqrt{k},\sqrt{r})
.

Proof.

Suppose
L=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q},\sqrt{k},\sqrt{r})
, then the conductor
f(L)=4qkr
. Therefore only primes that can ramify in
L/K
are those primes that lie above
2,q,k,
and
r
.
We have at least one of
k,r
is congruent to
1
modulo
{4}
, so without loss of generality, we assume that
r\equiv 1\pmod{4}
.
Since
r\equiv 1\pmod{4}
, the prime
2
does not ramify in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{r})
. Thus by
Lemma

2.4
, the primes which lie above
2
in
K
are unramified in
L
.
If
p
is one of
k,q,r,
then
p
is unramified in at least
one of the quadratic fields

\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{r})
,
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{k})
. By
Lemma

2.4
, the
primes which lie above
p
in
K
are unramified in
L
. Therefore all the primes of
K
are unramified in
L
. As a consequence of the fact that the Hilbert class field
H(K)
of
K
is the maximal abelian unramified extension of
K
, thus
L\subset H(K)
. Moreover, since
Gal(H(K)/K)\cong Cl_{K}
, we obtain
H(K)=L
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

The primes which split completely in
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q},\sqrt{kr})
but not in its Hilbert class field
H(K)=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q},\sqrt{k},\sqrt{r})
have density
\frac{1}{8}
.

Proof.

Let
X_{K},X_{H(K)}
denote the sets of prime numbers which split completely in
K
and
H(K)
respectively. Then

\displaystyle X_{K}

\displaystyle=\left\{p\ \Bigm|\ \text{ $p$ is prime and}\ \left(\frac{K/\mathbb{Q}}{p}\right)=1\right\},

\displaystyle X_{H(K)}

\displaystyle=\left\{p\ \Bigm|\ \text{ $p$ is prime and}\ \left(\frac{H(K)/\mathbb{Q}}{p}\right)=1\right\}.

Since
K\subset H(K)
, it follows that
X_{H(K)}\subset X_{K}
.
The identity elements in
{\mathrm{Gal}}(K/\mathbb{Q})
, and
{\mathrm{Gal}}(H(K)/K)
each form their own conjugacy class. By
Theorem

2.14
, the Dirichlet densities of
X_{K}
and
X_{H(K)}
are

\frac{1}{4}
and
\frac{1}{8}
respectively. Since
X_{H(K)}\subset X_{K},
we conclude that the Dirichlet
density of the set
X_{K}\setminus X_{H(K)}
is
\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{8}=\frac{1}{8}
, therefore
X_{K}\setminus X_{H(K)}
is infinite. ∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2

Since
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q},\sqrt{kr})
is a totally real number field of degree
4
,
\rank(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times})=3
, and there exists a multiplicatively independent set
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
in
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
. Let
l=16qkr
.
Next we will find an integer
u
satisfying the following conditions :

(1)

(u,l)=1,

(2)

(\frac{u-1}{2},l)=1,

(3)

If a prime
p
satisfies
p\equiv u\pmod{4qkr}
, then
f(\mathfrak{p}/p)=1
and
f(\mathfrak{P}/p)=2
.

By
Lemma

3.2
, there are infinitely many primes satisfying condition
3
.
Observe that a prime
p
splits completely in
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q},\sqrt{kr})
if and only if
p
splits completely in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q})
and
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{kr})
. This translates to

\displaystyle\left(\frac{q}{p}\right)=1\ \text{and}\ \left(\frac{kr}{p}\right)=1.

Similarly, a prime
p
splits completely in
H(K)=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q},\sqrt{k},\sqrt{r})
if and only if

\displaystyle\left(\frac{q}{p}\right)=1,\left(\frac{k}{p}\right)=1,\ \text{and}\ \left(\frac{r}{p}\right)=1.

Thus a prime
p\notin X_{H(K)}
if and only if one among
\left(\frac{q}{p}\right),\left(\frac{k}{p}\right),\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)
is equal to
-1
.
Hence

\displaystyle{}X_{K}\setminus X_{H(K)}=\Biggr\{p:\ \text{ $p$ is prime and}\ \left(\frac{q}{p}\right)=1\ \text{and}\ \left(\frac{k}{p}\right)=\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)=-1\Biggr\}.

We demonstrate how
u
can be found by using the Chinese remainder theorem.
For that, we translate the conditions(1)-(3) into simultaneous congruences.

Since either
k
or
r
is congruent to
1
modulo
4
, without loss of generality, we can assume that
k\equiv 3\pmod{4},r\equiv 1\pmod{4}
.
By
Theorem

2.5
and
Theorem

2.6
, there exist prime numbers
p_{1}<q,p_{2}<k,p_{3}<r
that are congruent to
3
modulo
4

\left(\frac{p_{1}}{q}\right)=(-1)^{\frac{q-1}{2}},\left(\frac{p_{2}}{k}\right)=1\ \text{and}\ \left(\frac{p_{3}}{r}\right)=-1.

Now consider the following system of simultaneous congruences.

(3.1)

x\equiv p_{1}\pmod{q},

(3.2)

x\equiv p_{2}\pmod{k},

(3.3)

x\equiv p_{3}\pmod{r},

(3.4)

x\equiv 3\pmod{4}.

The Chinese remainder theorem promises a unique solution modulo
4qkr
to the above system of congruences, say
x_{0}
.
Since
\gcd(x_{0},4qkr)=1
, by Dirichlet’s theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions, there exist infinitely many prime numbers
p
satisfying
p\equiv x_{0}\pmod{4qkr}
. We pick one such prime and call it
u
. It is easy to observe that
u
satisfies condition (
1

\gcd(\frac{u-1}{2},l)\neq 1
, then either there exists an odd prime
p\mid l
such that
u\equiv 1\pmod{p}
or
u\equiv 1\pmod{4}
. This contradicts that
u
is a solution of the above simultaneous congruences (
3.1
)-(
3.4
). Therefore
u
satisfies condition (
2
).

Now by quadratic reciprocity, we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{q}{u}\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\frac{u-1}{2}\frac{q-1}{2}}\left(\frac{u}{q}\right)=(-1)^{\frac{q-1}{2}}\left(\frac{p_{1}}{q}\right)=(-1)^{\frac{q-1}{2}}(-1)^{\frac{q-1}{2}}=1,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{k}{u}\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\frac{k-1}{2}\frac{u-1}{2}}\left(\frac{u}{k}\right)=(-1)\left(\frac{p_{2}}{k}\right)=-1,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{r}{u}\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\frac{r-1}{2}\frac{u-1}{2}}\left(\frac{u}{r}\right)=\left(\frac{p_{3}}{r}\right)=-1.

Hence
u\in X_{K}\setminus X_{H(K)}
and also satisfies condition (
3
).

Let
u
satisfy conditions (
1
)-(
3
). If
p\equiv u\pmod{4qkr}
, then every prime
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
which lies above
p
does not
split in
H(K)
and
f(\mathfrak{P}/\mathfrak{p})=2
, where
\mathfrak{P}
is any prime of
H(K)
which lies above
\mathfrak{p}
. Hence by
Lemma

2.15
,

\mathfrak{p}
is not a principal ideal, and since
h_{K}=2
,
[\mathfrak{p}]
is the generator of
Cl_{K}
. For each
i\in\{1,2,3\},

\left|\begin{Bmatrix}&\Bigm|&N(\mathfrak{P})\leq x,\\
\mathfrak{P}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}&\Bigm|&[\mathfrak{P}]=[C],\\
\text{prime }&\Bigm|&\pi_{p}\text{\ is \ onto}\end{Bmatrix}\right|>\left|\begin{Bmatrix}\text{prime }&\Bigm|&N(\mathfrak{P})\equiv u\pmod{l},\\
\text{ ideals of }&\Bigm|&N(\mathfrak{P})\leq x,\\
\text{ first degree}&\Bigm|&\langle-1,e_{i}\rangle\twoheadrightarrow(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{P})^{\times}\end{Bmatrix}\right|.

Now using

, we obtain

\left|\begin{Bmatrix}\mathfrak{P}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}\ \text{prime ideal }&\Bigm|&N(\mathfrak{P})\leq x,[\mathfrak{P}]=[C],\pi_{p}\text{\ is \ onto}\\
\end{Bmatrix}\right|\gg\frac{x}{(\log x)^{2}}.

Applying

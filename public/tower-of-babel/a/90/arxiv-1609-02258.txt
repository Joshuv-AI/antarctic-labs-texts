Tighter bound of Sketched Generalized Matrix Approximation
\name Haishan Ye, Qiaoming Ye, \name Zhihua Zhang

Abstract
Generalized matrix approximation plays a fundamental role in many machine learning problems, such as CUR decomposition, kernel approximation, and matrix low rank approximation. Especially with Today’s applications involved in larger and larger dataset, more and more efficient generalized matrix approximation algorithems become a crucially important research issue. In this paper, we find new sketching techniques to reduce the size of the original data matrix to develop new matrix approximation algorithms. Our results derive a much tighter bound for the approximation than previous works: we obtain a ( 1 + ϵ ) (1+\epsilon) approximation ratio with small sketched dimensions which implies a more efficient generalized matrix approximation.

Introduction

Matrix manipulations are the basis of modern data analysis. As the datasets becomes larger and larger, it is much more difficult to perform exact matrix multiplication, inversion, and decomposition. Consequently, matrix approximation techniques have been extensively studied, including approximate matrix multiplication
[
5
,
6
,
10
]
and low-rank matrix approximation
[
1
,
2
,
4
,
13
]
.

In this paper we are concerned with the generalized matrix approximation problem
[
16
,
8
,
15
]
:

\min_{{\bf X}}\|{\bf A}-{\bf M}{\bf X}{\bf N}\|_{F},

where
{\bf A}\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n}}
,
{\bf M}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times c}
and
{\bf N}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{r\times n}
. It is well known that the solution is
{\bf X}^{*}={\bf M}^{\dagger}{\bf A}{\bf N}^{\dagger}
. It costs
{\mathcal{O}}(\mathrm{nnz}({\bf A})\cdot\min(c,r)+mc^{2}+nr^{2})
time to solve the generalized matrix approximation exactly to get
{\bf X}^{*}
. Generalized matrix approximation takes an important role in solving some machine learning problems such as the CUR decomposition
[
17
,
9
,
18
]
, modified Nyström method
[
17
]
, and distributed PCA
[
12
,
3
]
. It is also a key research topic in numerical linear algebra
[
16
,
8
]
.

When
{\bf N}
is the identity matrix, the generalized matrix approximation degenerates to the ordinary least squares regression. To solve the least squares regression
\tilde{{\bf X}}=\mathop{\rm argmin}_{\bf X}\|{\bf M}{\bf X}-{\bf A}\|_{F}
more efficiently when
c\ll m
, recent studies suggest multiplying a sketching matrix
{\bf S}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{s_{c}\times m}
, where
s_{c}={\mathcal{O}}(c/\epsilon)
, to get a sketched least squares regression
\hat{{\bf X}}=\mathop{\rm argmin}_{\bf X}\|{\bf S}({\bf M}{\bf X}-{\bf A})\|_{F}
. The studies
[
4
,
7
,
14
]
also prove that the reduced least squares regression obtains a
(1+\epsilon)
relative error bound, which is,
\|{\bf M}\hat{{\bf X}}-{\bf A}\|_{F}\leq(1+\epsilon)\|{\bf M}\tilde{{\bf X}}-{\bf A}\|_{F}
.

Since the ordinary least squares regression is a special case of the generalized matrix approximation, it is reasonable to use the sketching technique to accelerate solving the generalized matrix approximation. Recently,
Wang et al. 2015
proposed to use leverage-score sketching matrices
{\bf S}_{\bf M}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{s_{c}\times m}
and
{\bf S}_{\bf N}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{s_{r}\times n}
to efficiently solve the generalized matrix approximation via

\hat{{\bf X}}=\mathop{\rm argmin}_{\bf X}\|{\bf S}_{\bf M}({\bf A}-{\bf M}{\bf X}{\bf N}){\bf S}_{\bf N}^{T}\|_{F}.

However, it needs
s_{c}={\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{\min(m,n)}c/\sqrt{\epsilon})
and
s_{r}={\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{\min(m,n)}r/\sqrt{\epsilon})
to get a
(1+\epsilon)
relative error bound in
[
18
]
. The result of
[
18
]
can be easily extended to other sketching matrices with the same reduced dimensions, i.e., the same
s_{c}
and
s_{r}
.
Boutsidis et al. 2016
proposed to solve the generalized rank-constrained matrix approximation problem
\min_{\text{rank}({\bf X})\leq k}\|{\bf A}-{\bf M}{\bf X}{\bf N}\|_{F}
by multiplying sketching matrices
{\bf S}_{\bf M}
and
{\bf S}_{\bf N}
with the affine embedding property. It needs
s_{c}={\mathcal{O}}(c/\epsilon^{2})
and
s_{r}={\mathcal{O}}(r/\epsilon^{2})
to achieve a
(1+\epsilon)
relative error bound.

In this paper, we prove that
s_{c}={\mathcal{O}}(c/\epsilon)
and
s_{r}={\mathcal{O}}(r/\epsilon)
are enough to achieve a
(1+\epsilon)
relative error bound for the generalized matrix approximation problem. We compare our result with previous work in Table
1
in detail. As we can see, our bound is much tighter than previous work. Besides, a tigher bound leads to faster sketched generalized matrix approximation. Using leverage-score sketching, the running time in Theorem
7
is much less than that of
Boutsidis et al. 2016
since,
c
and
r
is much less than
n
and
m
, especially when
{\bf A}
is dense. Comparing with
[
18
]
, though the first part of compuation cost both is
{\mathcal{O}}(\mathrm{nnz}({\bf M}))
, the running time is
m
times less than
[
18
]
as to the second part of computation cost. Hence, our result is better, especially when
\mathrm{nnz}({\bf M})
is small, because the leading cost will be the second part of computation cost.

Notation and Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce some notation and preliminaries
that will be used in this paper.

2.1
Notation

Let
{\bf I}_{m}
be the
m{\times}m
identity matrix.
Given a matrix
{\bf A}=[a_{ij}]\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n}
of rank
\rho
, its SVD is given as

{\bf A}={\bf U}\mbox{\boldmath$\Sigma$\unboldmath}{\bf V}^{T}={\bf U}_{k}\mbox{\boldmath$\Sigma$\unboldmath}_{k}{\bf V}_{k}^{T}+{\bf U}_{\rho\setminus k}\mbox{\boldmath$\Sigma$\unboldmath}_{\rho{\setminus}k}{\bf V}_{\rho{\setminus}k}^{T}
,
where
{\bf U}_{k}
and
{\bf U}_{\rho{\setminus}k}
contain the left singular vectors of
{\bf A}
,
{\bf V}_{k}
and
{\bf V}_{\rho{\setminus}k}
contain the right
singular vectors of
{\bf A}
, and
\mbox{\boldmath$\Sigma$\unboldmath}=\mathrm{diag}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{\rho})
with
\sigma_{1}\geq\sigma_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\sigma_{\rho}>0
are
the nonzero singular values of
{\bf A}
. Accordingly,
\|{\bf A}\|_{F}\triangleq(\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}^{2})^{1/2}=(\sum_{i}\sigma_{i}^{2})^{1/2}

is the Frobenius norm of
{\bf A}
and

\|{\bf A}\|_{2}\triangleq\sigma_{1}
is the spectral norm.

Additionally,
{\bf A}^{\dagger}\triangleq{\bf V}\mbox{\boldmath$\Sigma$\unboldmath}^{-1}{\bf U}^{T}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times m}
is
the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse of
{\bf A}
, which is unique. It is easy to verify that
{\mathrm{rank}}({\bf A}^{\dagger})={\mathrm{rank}}({\bf A})=\rho
.
Moreover, for all
i=1,\dots,\rho
,

\sigma_{i}({\bf A}^{\dagger})=1/\sigma_{\rho-i+1}({\bf A})
. If
{\bf A}
is of full row rank, then
{\bf A}{\bf A}^{\dagger}={\bf I}_{m}
.
Also, if
{\bf A}
is of full column rank, then
{\bf A}^{\dagger}{\bf A}={\bf I}_{n}
. When
m=n
,
\text{tr}({\bf A})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{ii}
is the trace of
{\bf A}
.

It is well known that
{\bf A}_{k}\triangleq{\bf U}_{k}\mbox{\boldmath$\Sigma$\unboldmath}_{k}{\bf V}_{k}^{T}
is the minimizer of both
\min\|{\bf A}-{\bf X}\|_{F}
and
\min\|{\bf A}-{\bf X}\|_{2}
over all matrices
{\bf X}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n}
of rank at most
k\leq\rho
. Thus,
{\bf A}_{k}
is called the best rank-
k
approximation of
{\bf A}
.
Let
\mathrm{nnz}({\bf A})
denote the number of nonzero entries of
{\bf A}
.

2.2
Subspace Embedding

Oblivious subspace embedding is an important sketching tool in randomized numerical linear algebra.
By oblivious subspace embedding, a matrix can be projected to a much lower dimensional subspace,
which leads to much faster matrix operations.

Definition 1
(
[
19
]
)

Given
\varepsilon>0
and
\delta>0
, let
\Pi
be a distribution on
s\times m
real matrices, where
s
relies on
m
,
d
,
\varepsilon
and
\delta
. Suppose that
with probability at least
1-\delta
, for any fixed
m\times d
matrix
{\bf A}
, a matrix
{\bf S}
drawn from distribution
\Pi

is a
(1+\epsilon)

\ell_{2}
-subspace embedding for
{\bf A}
, that is, for all
{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\|{\bf S}{\bf A}{\bf x}\|_{2}^{2}=(1\pm\epsilon)\|{\bf A}{\bf x}\|_{2}^{2}
with probability

1-\delta
. Then we call
\Pi
an
(\epsilon,\delta)
-oblivious
\ell_{2}
-subspace
embedding.

For the sake of conciseness, the
(\epsilon,\delta)
-oblivious
\ell_{2}
-subspace embedding is referred as an
\epsilon
-subspace embedding. Now we list some important subspace embedding matrices and their properties which will be used in this paper.

Definition 2
(leverage-score sketching matrix)

Let
{\bf V}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times k}
be column orthonormal basis for
{\bf A}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times k}
with
n>k
, and
{\bf v}_{i,*}
denote the
i
-th row of
{\bf V}
. Let
\ell_{i}=\|{\bf v}_{i,*}\|_{F}^{2}/k
and
r
be an integer with
1\leq r\leq n
. Then the
\ell_{i}
’s are leverage scores for
{\bf A}
. Construct a sampling matrix
\mbox{\boldmath$\Omega$\unboldmath}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times r}
and a rescaling matrix
{\bf D}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{r\times r}
as follows. For every
j=1,\dots,r
, independently and with replacement, pick an index
i
from the set
\{1,2\dots,n\}
with probability
\ell_{i}
and set
\mbox{\boldmath$\Omega$\unboldmath}_{ij}=1
and
{\bf D}_{jj}=1/\sqrt{\ell_{i}r}
. The leverage-score sketching matrix
{\bf S}
for
{\bf A}
is then defined as
{\bf S}=\mbox{\boldmath$\Omega$\unboldmath}{\bf D}
.

Theorem 3
(
[
19
]
)

Given
{\bf A}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times d}
of full column rank, assume
{\bf S}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{s\times m}
is an
\epsilon
-subspace embedding matrix for
{\bf A}
. If
{\bf S}
is a sparse subspace embedding matrix, then
s={\mathcal{O}}(d^{2}\epsilon^{-2})
is sufficient. If
{\bf S}
is an
s\times m
matrix of i.i.d. normal random variables with variance
1/s
, then
s=\Theta(d\epsilon^{-2})
is sufficient. For a leverage-score sketching matrix
{\bf S}
,
s={\mathcal{O}}(d\log d\epsilon^{-2})
is needed.

Theorem 4
(
[
4
,
3
]
)

For
{\bf A}\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n}}
and
{\bf B}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times k}
, there is an
s=\Theta(\epsilon^{-2})
, so that for an
s\times m
sparse embedding matrix
{\bf S}
or an
s\times m
matrix
{\bf S}
of i.i.d. normal random variables with variance
1/s
, or an
s\times m
leverage-score sketching matrix for
{\bf A}
under the condition that
{\bf A}
has orthonormal columns, then it holds that

\|{\bf A}^{T}{\bf S}^{T}{\bf S}{\bf B}-{\bf A}^{T}{\bf B}\|_{F}^{2}<\epsilon^{2}\|{\bf A}\|_{F}^{2}\|{\bf B}\|_{F}^{2}

(1)

with probability at least
1-\delta
for any fixed
\delta>0
.

Other types of sketching matrices like Subsampled Randomized Hadamard Transformation and detailed properties of sketching matrices and subspace embedding matrices can be
found in the survey
[
19
]
.

Main Result

We first give the conditions that subspace embedding matrices should satisfy for a sketched generalized matrix approximation to achieve a
(1+\epsilon)
error bound. The detailed conditions are depicted in Theorem

Theorem 5

Given that
{\bf A}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n}
,
{\bf M}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times c}
and
{\bf N}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{r\times n}
, assume that
{\bf S}_{\bf M}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{s_{c}\times m}
is a subspace embedding matrix for
{\bf M}
with error parameter
\epsilon_{0}=1/2
, and
{\bf S}_{\bf M}
also makes Eqn. (
1
) hold with error parameter
\sqrt{\epsilon/c}
. Similarly,
{\bf S}_{\bf N}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{{\bf s}_{r}\times n}
is a subspace embedding matrix for
{\bf N}^{T}
with error parameter
\epsilon_{0}=1/2
and
{\bf S}_{\bf N}
also makes Eqn. (
1
) hold with error parameter
\sqrt{\epsilon/r}

(2)

\hat{{\bf X}}=({\bf S}_{{\bf M}}{\bf M})^{\dagger}{\bf S}_{{\bf M}}{\bf A}{\bf S}_{{\bf N}}^{T}({\bf N}{\bf S}_{{\bf N}}^{T})^{\dagger}.

(3)

The conditions required in Theorem

The linear SL 2 ⁡ ( ℤ ) \operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}) -action on 𝕋 n \mathbb{T}^{n} : ergodic and von Neumann algebraic aspects
PAUL JOLISSAINT, ALAIN VALETTE

Abstract
The linear action of SL 2 ⁡ ( ℝ ) \operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R}) on ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} corresponding to its unique irreducible representation induces an action SL 2 ⁡ ( ℤ ) ↷ 𝕋 n \operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\curvearrowright\mathbb{T}^{n} for every n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 that factors through PSL 2 ⁡ ( ℤ ) \operatorname{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}) for n n odd. Thus, setting G n = SL 2 ⁡ ( ℤ ) G_{n}=\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}) (resp. G n = PSL 2 ⁡ ( ℤ ) G_{n}=\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}) ) for n n even (resp. n n odd), G n ↷ 𝕋 n G_{n}\curvearrowright\mathbb{T}^{n} is free and ergodic, every ergodic sub-equivalence relation of the orbital equivalence relation is either amenable or rigid, and the fundamental group of the II 1 factor N n ≔ L ∞ ​ ( 𝕋 n ) ⋊ G n N_{n}\coloneqq L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})\rtimes G_{n} is trivial. For n n even, L ∞ ​ ( 𝕋 n ) ⋊ H L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})\rtimes H is a maximal Haagerup subalgebra of N n N_{n} for every suitable maximal amenable subgroup H H of SL 2 ⁡ ( ℤ ) \operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}) .

INTRODUCTION

It is a classical fact that, for
n\geq 1
, the Lie group
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
admits a unique irreducible representation on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Indeed, let
P_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})
be the space of homogeneous polynomials of degree
n-1
in two variables
X,Y
with real coefficients: we have a standard representation
\rho_{n-1}
of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
on
P_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})
defined by

\Big(\rho_{n-1}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\Big)(P)(X,Y)=P(aX+cY,bX+dY)

for
P\in P_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})
and

\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
Now we may identify
P_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})
with
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by means of the canonical basis
X^{n-1},X^{n-2}Y,\ldots,XY^{n-2},Y^{n-1}
. It is clear that the subgroup
P_{n-1}(\mathbb{Z})
of polynomials with integer coefficients is invariant under
\rho_{n-1}(\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
, so we get an
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
-action on the
n
-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{n}=P_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})/P_{n-1}(\mathbb{Z})
. If
n
is odd, the representation
\rho_{n-1}
factors through
\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, so the action of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
factors through
\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Define
G_{n}
as
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
for
n
even and as
\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
for
n
odd. In this paper, by
linear action
on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
we mean the action of
G_{n}
on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
. Observe that the linear action preserves the normalized Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, so it is a p.m.p. (probability measure-preserving) action. For
n=2
, we recover the well-studied action of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
on the 2-torus. Our first result generalizes a well-known property of this case:

Theorem 1.1

Fix
n\geq 2
. Then the following statements hold true:

(i)

The linear action on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
is ergodic and essentially free.

(ii)

If
h\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
is hyperbolic and
n
is even, then the subgroup
\langle h\rangle
generated by

h
acts ergodically on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
.

We observe that the second part of Theorem
1.1
only holds for
n
even. Indeed we show:

Proposition 1.2

For
n
odd, no amenable subgroup of
\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
acts ergodically on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
.

A countable, ergodic, measure preserving equivalence relation
\mathcal{R}
on a standard probability space
(X,\mu)
is said to be
rigid
if the inclusion of
L^{\infty}(X,\mu)
into the von Neumann algebra
L(\mathcal{R})
associated with
\mathcal{R}
(see
[
FM77II
]
), is rigid in the sense of Popa
[
Po06
]
. In Theorem 0.1 of
[
Io10
]
, Ioana discovered a remarkable property of the orbital equivalence relation
\mathcal{S}_{2}
induced by the linear action of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
, namely: every ergodic sub-equivalence relation of
\mathcal{S}_{2}
is either amenable or rigid. We will refer to this phenomenon as
Ioana’s dichotomy
, and we observe that it is a vast generalization of a result by Burger
[
Bu91
]
that says that, for a subgroup
H
of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, either
H
is amenable (and so
\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rtimes H
is amenable), or the pair
(\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rtimes H,\mathbb{Z}^{2})
has the relative property (T). More examples of this dichotomy, for homogeneous spaces of certain Lie groups, were provided by Ioana and Shalom (Theorem C in
[
IS13
]
). We contribute to the list by showing:

Theorem 1.3

For
n\geq 2
, let
\mathcal{S}_{n}
be the orbital equivalence relation on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
induced by the linear action. Then Ioana’s dichotomy holds for
\mathcal{S}_{n}
.

In the final part of the paper, we consider von Neumann algebraic aspects, more precisely we deal with the von Neumann algebra
N_{n}\coloneqq L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})\rtimes G_{n}
obtained via the group measure space construction. It is a type II
1
factor as a consequence of Theorem
1.1
. Denote by
L(G)
the group von Neumann algebra of a countable group
G
; using Fourier series we may identify
N_{n}
with
L(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rtimes G_{n})
. Now
G_{n}
has the Haagerup property and the pair
(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rtimes G_{n},\mathbb{Z}^{n})
has the relative property (T) (by Proposition 3.1 in
[
Va23
]
), so the factors
N_{n}
belong to Popa’s
\mathcal{HT}
-class
[
Po06
]
; hence by Section 6 and Corollary 8.2 of
[
Po06
]
they inherit
\ell^{2}
-Betti numbers
\beta_{k}^{HT}(N_{n})
that coincide with those of the acting group
G_{n}
, and we have:

Theorem 1.4

For every
n\geq 2
, the
\ell^{2}
-Betti numbers of the factor
N_{n}
satisfy
\beta_{k}^{HT}(N_{n})=0
for every
k\not=1
and

\beta_{1}^{HT}(N_{n})=\begin{cases}1/12&\textrm{for\ even\ }n\\
1/6&\textrm{for\ odd\ }n.\end{cases}

In particular, if
n
is odd and
m
is even, then
N_{n}
and
N_{m}
are non-isomorphic, and, for every
n
, the fundamental group
\mathcal{F}(N_{n})
is trivial.

In Theorem 3.1 of
[
JS21
]
, Jiang and Skalski proved that, if
H
is a maximal amenable subgroup of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
containing some hyperbolic matrix (hence by Proposition 2.6 in
[
Va23
]
, the subgroup
H
is isomorphic either to
\mathbb{Z}\times C_{2}
or to
\mathbb{Z}\rtimes C_{4}
), then
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\rtimes H
is a maximal Haagerup von Neumann subalgebra of
N_{2}
, where the Haagerup property for finite von Neumann algebras has been studied first in
[
Ch83
]
and then systematically by the first author in
[
Jo02
]
. Building on Theorem
1.3
, we extend that result as follows:

Theorem 1.5

Fix
n\geq 2
. Then the following statements hold true:

(1)

For any hyperfinite subfactor
P
of
N_{n}
which contains
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})\cong L(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
as a Cartan subalgebra, every maximal Haagerup von Neumann subalgebra
P\subset Q\subset N_{n}
is a hyperfinite subfactor of
N_{n}
. Moreover:

(2)

If
n
is even, let
R=L(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rtimes H)
where
H
is a maximal amenable subgroup of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
which contains a hyperbolic element. Then
R
is a maximal Haagerup von Neumann subalgebra of
N_{n}
.

(3)

If
n
is odd, then
P
and
Q
in (1) are not of the form
L(K)
for any subgroup
K
of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rtimes\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.

The existence of a hyperfinite subfactor
P
as in the preceeding theorem is a consequence of Proposition 3.6 of
[
Po81
]
.

Moreover, in a finite von Neumann algebra, every von Neumann subalgebra with the Haagerup property is contained in a maximal one: it is stated in Corollary 1.12 of
[
JS21
]
as a consequence of Lemma 1.11 of the same article. However, the latter only treats increasing unions of countably many subalgebras, which is not sufficient to apply Zorn’s lemma, even if their arguments would work for nets instead of sequences; as the proof of the above mentioned lemma does not contain much details, we think that it is worth providing a proof of the existence of maximal Haagerup subalgebras in Proposition
4.7
below.

By a celebrated result of Connes-Feldman-Weiss
[
CFW81
]
, the sub-equi-valence relation
\mathcal{R}
of
\mathcal{S}_{n}
such that
P=L(\mathcal{R})
is induced by a
\mathbb{Z}
-action. Part 3 of Theorem
1.5
above shows that
\mathcal{R}
will not be produced by any subgroup of
G_{n}
.

Section 2 contains the proofs of Theorem
1.1
and Proposition
1.2
. Section 3 is about Ioana’s dichotomy: the main new ingredient is the fact that the
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-action induced on the projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(\mathbb{R})
by the representation
\rho_{n-1}
, is amenable (see Proposition
3.5
): for
n=2
, i.e. the case treated in
[
Io10
]
, it is clear that the group
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
acts amenably on
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, as
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
identifies with
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})/B
, where
B
is the Borel subgroup, and
B
is amenable (see Proposition 4.3.2 in
[
Zi84
]
). The final section 4 contains the proofs of Theorems
1.4
and
1.5
.

2
ERGODICITY

Proof of Theorem
1.1
:

i.

We start with essential freeness. Observe that
\rho_{n-1}(\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R}))\subset\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
(otherwise, composing
\rho_{n-1}
with the determinant we would get a non-trivial homomorphism from
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
to an abelian group); hence we have
\rho_{n-1}(\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))\subset\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})\cap N_{n}(\mathbb{Z})=\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
, so that the linear action on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
is the restriction to
\rho_{n-1}(\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
of the standard action of
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
. The latter is known to be essentially free, see e.g. Lemma 5.2.4 in
[
Zi84
]
and the comment following it.

Concerning ergodicity, view
\mathbb{T}^{n}
as a compact abelian group on which
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
acts by automorphisms. View
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
as the Pontryagin dual of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
. By Proposition 1.5 in
[
BM00
]
, the linear action is ergodic if and only if every non-zero
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
-orbit on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is infinite. So assume by contradiction that some non-zero
v\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
has a finite orbit. Then the stabilizer
\Gamma
of
v
has finite index in
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, in particular
\Gamma
is non-amenable. By Proposition 3.1 in
[
Va23
]
, the restriction of
\rho_{n-1}
to
\Gamma
is irreducible, so it cannot have non-zero invariant vectors, and we reached a contradiction.

ii.

By Example 1.4.(iii) in
[
BM00
]
, it is enough to check that none of the complex eigenvalues of
\rho_{n-1}(h)
is a root of unit. But for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
with
|\lambda|>1
, the matrix
h
is conjugate in
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
to

h_{\lambda}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda&0\\
0&1/\lambda\end{pmatrix}
.
Now
\rho_{n-1}(h_{\lambda})

is diagonal in the basis
X^{n-1},X^{n-2}Y,\ldots,XY^{n-2},Y^{n-1}
of
P_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})
, with eigenvalues
\lambda^{n-1},\lambda^{n-3},\ldots,\lambda^{-(n-3)},\lambda^{-(n-1)}
. Since
n
is even, none of those eigenvalues is a root of 1.
\blacksquare

Proof of Proposition
1.2
:
Fix some odd
n
. Let
H
be an amenable subgroup of
\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, we must show that
H
has a finite orbit in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
. Clearly we may assume that
H
is maximal amenable, so we may appeal to the classification in Lemma 2.5 of
[
Va23
]
, giving three cases to consider:

•

H
is cyclic of order
3
, and there is nothing to prove.

•

H
is infinite cyclic consisting of parabolic elements: then
H
is conjugate to the subgroup generated by

u=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}
; but
\rho_{n-1}(u)
fixes the monomial
X^{n-1}
.

•

H
is either infinite cyclic or infinite dihedral, with torsion-free part being generated by some hyperbolic matrix
h
, conjugate in
\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
to a diagonal matrix

h_{\lambda}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda&0\\
0&1/\lambda\end{pmatrix}

with
\lambda>1
. As in the proof of the second part of Theorem
1.1
, the eigenvalues of
\rho_{n-1}(h_{\lambda})
(hence also of
\rho_{n-1}(h)
) are
\lambda^{n-1},\lambda^{n-3},\ldots,\lambda^{-(n-3)},\lambda^{-(n-1)}
, but now
1
appears in the list, as
n
is odd. So
\rho_{n-1}(h)
admits in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
an eigenvector
v
with eigenvalue
1
. As
\rho_{n-1}(h)
has integer coefficients, by Gaussian elimination we may assume
v
to have rational coordinates, and by chasing denominators we may assume
v\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Then the orbit of
v
under
\rho_{n-1}(H)
has at most two elements.

\blacksquare

IOANA’S DICHOTOMY

3.1
EQUIVALENCE RELATIONS ON STANDARD SPACES

Let
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu)
be a standard probability space. A
(countable) standard equivalence relation
on
X
is a Borel subset
\mathcal{R}\subset X\times X
which is an equivalence relation whose equivalence classes
[x]_{\mathcal{R}}\coloneqq\{y\in X\colon x\sim y\}
are all at most countable. We denote by
p^{i}:X\times X\rightarrow X
the projection defined by
p^{i}(x_{1},x_{2})=x_{i}
for
i=1,2
; as they are countable-to-one maps, they imply the existence of
\sigma
-finite measures
\nu_{i}
,
i=1,2
, on the Borel subsets of
\mathcal{R}
which are defined by

\nu_{i}(C)\coloneqq\int_{X}|(p^{i})^{-1}(x)\cap C|d\mu(x)

for every such Borel set
C
. We assume henceforth that
\mu
is
invariant
under
\mathcal{R}
, which means that
\nu\coloneqq\nu_{1}=\nu_{2}
. Thus, for every integrable function
f
on
\mathcal{R}
, one has

\int_{\mathcal{R}}f(x,y)d\nu(x,y)=\int_{X}\Big(\sum_{x\sim y}f(x,y)\Big)d\mu(x)=\int_{X}\Big(\sum_{y\sim x}f(x,y)\Big)d\mu(y).

Finally, the
full group
of
\mathcal{R}
, denoted by
[\mathcal{R}]
, consists in all automorphisms
\theta
of
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu)
such that
\theta(x)\in[x]_{\mathcal{R}}
for almost every
x\in X
, and
\mathcal{R}
is
ergodic
if any
\mathcal{R}
-invariant Borel subset of
X
is either null or co-null.

Natural examples of countable equivalence relations are given by p.m.p. actions of countable groups: if
\Gamma\curvearrowright(X,\mu)
is such an action, then the
orbital equivalence relation

\mathcal{R}_{\Gamma}\coloneqq\{(x,\gamma x)\colon x\in X,\gamma\in\Gamma\}
is a countable equivalence relation which is ergodic if and only if the action of
\Gamma
is. It turns out that, by Theorem 3 of
[
FM77I
]
, every equivalence relation is of this form, but the acting group is far from being unique.

Recall that
\mathcal{R}
is
hyperfinite
if there exists a Borel automorphism
T
of
(X,\mu)
such that, for (almost) every
x\in X
, the equivalence class
[x]_{\mathcal{R}}
coincides with the orbit
\{T^{k}x\colon k\in\mathbb{Z}\}
of
x
. It turns out that, by the main result of
[
CFW81
]
, hyperfiniteness of
\mathcal{R}
is equivalent to its amenability, whose definition we recall from
[
CFW81
]
:

Definition 3.1

With
X,\mathcal{R}
as above: say that
\mathcal{R}
is an amenable equivalence relation if there is an assignment
x\mapsto\nu_{x}
from
X
to the state space of

\ell^{\infty}([x]_{\mathcal{R}})
such that:

(1)

the family
(\nu_{x})_{x\in X}
is
\mathcal{R}
-invariant, i.e.
\gamma_{*}(\nu_{x})=\nu_{y}
for every
\gamma=(y,x)\in\mathcal{R}
;

(2)

for every
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{R},\nu)
, the function
x\mapsto\nu_{x}(f)
is measurable.

3.2
AMENABILITY OF THE LINEAR ACTION OF
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
ON
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(\mathbb{R})

The proof of Ioana’s main theorem in
[
Io10
]
uses in a crucial way the topological amenability of the action of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
(in the sense of
[
An02
]
). Analogously, the proof of Theorem
1.3
will rest on the topological amenability of the action of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
on
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(\mathbb{R})
.
Let us first recall the relevant definition.

Let
\Gamma
be a countable group and
X
be a locally compact space equipped with a left action of
\Gamma
by homeomorphisms. We denote by
\mathrm{Prob}(\Gamma)
the set of probability measures on
\Gamma
, equipped with the weak
∗
-topology, and with the natural action of
\Gamma
inherited by the left translation action of
\Gamma
on itself.

Definition 3.2

(
[
An02
]
, Definition 2.1) Let
(\Gamma,X)
be as above. We say that the action is topologically amenable if there exists a net
(m_{i})_{i\in I}
of continuous maps
x\mapsto m_{i}^{x}
from
X
to
\mathrm{Prob}(\Gamma)
such that

\lim_{i}\|sm_{i}^{x}-m_{i}^{sx}\|_{1}=0

uniformly on compact subsets of
\Gamma\times X
.

We need the following stability properties of the class of topologically amen-able actions.

Lemma 3.3

Let
(\Gamma,X)
be a topologically amenable action on a locally compact space
X
.

i.

If
Z
is a closed
\Gamma
-invariant subspace of
X
, then the action of
\Gamma
on
Z
is topologically amenable.

ii.

Let
Y
be any locally compact space endowed with a
\Gamma
-action. Then the diagonal action of
\Gamma
on
X\times Y
is topologically amenable.

iii.

Let
F
be a finite group acting on
X
, such that the
F
-action commutes with the
\Gamma
-action, then the
\Gamma
-action on the orbit space
X/F
is topologically amenable.

iv.

For every
n\geq 2
, the diagonal action of
\Gamma
on the
n
-fold symmetric product
\Sigma^{n}X\coloneqq X^{n}/Sym(n)
is topologically amenable.

Proof
: Let
(m_{i})_{i\in I}
be a net of continuous maps
X\rightarrow\mathrm{Prob}(\Gamma)
as in Definition
3.2
.

i.

Restrict the maps
m_{i}
to
Z
.

ii.

For
i\in I
, define
n_{i}:X\times Y\rightarrow\mathrm{Prob}(\Gamma)
by
n_{i}^{(x,y)}\coloneqq m_{i}^{x}
(for
(x,y)\in X\times Y
). Using the
\Gamma
-equivariance of the projection
X\times Y\rightarrow X
, the net
(n_{i})_{i\in I}
realizes the topological amenability of the action
(\Gamma,X\times Y)
.

iii.

Say that
F
acts on
X
on the right. For
i\in I
and
f\in F
, define
m_{i,f}:X\rightarrow\mathrm{Prob}(\Gamma)
by
m_{i,f}^{x}\coloneqq m_{i}^{xf}
(for
x\in X
), and set
n_{i}=:\frac{1}{|F|}\sum_{f\in F}m_{i,f}
. Then the net
(n_{i})_{i\in I}
factors through
X/F
and realizes the topological amenability of the action
(\Gamma,X/F)
.

iv.

Follows immediately from the two previous points.

\blacksquare

It is apparently classical that
\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
is homeomorphic to
\Sigma^{n}\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
, see e.g. Example 1.2 in
[
Mo97
]
. Since we need an
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-equivariant version of this homeomorphism, we elaborate on this. Denote by
P_{n}(\mathbb{C})
the space of homogeneous polynomials of degree
n
in two variables
X,Y
with complex coefficients: complexifying
\rho_{n}
we get a representation of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
on
P_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, identified with
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
through the canonical basis of monomials. Passing to the complex projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
, we get the linear action of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
.

Lemma 3.4

For every
n\geq 1
, there exists an
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-equivariant homeomorphism
F:\Sigma^{n}\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
.

Proof
: Consider the continuous,
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-equivariant map:

\begin{array}[]{cl}(\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\})^{n}&\rightarrow P_{n}(\mathbb{C})\\
((a_{1},b_{1}),\ldots,(a_{n},b_{n}))&\mapsto P(X,Y)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(a_{i}X+b_{i}Y).\end{array}

It clearly descends to a continuous,
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-equivariant map
F:\Sigma^{n}\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
, and it remains to check that
F
is bijective. Let
P(X,Y)
be a non-zero polynomial in
P_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, write
P(X,Y)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}c_{k}X^{n-k}Y^{k}
, and let
\ell
be the smallest index
k
such that
c_{k}\neq 0
, so that

P(X,Y)=\sum_{k=\ell}^{n}c_{k}X^{n-k}Y^{k}=Y^{n}\sum_{k=\ell}^{n}c_{k}\Big(\frac{X}{Y}\Big)^{n-k}.

Set
Z=\frac{X}{Y}
and
Q(Z)=\sum_{k=\ell}^{n}c_{k}Z^{n-k}
; view
Q
as a polynomial of degree
n-\ell
in the polynomial ring
\mathbb{C}[Z]
; factoring
Q
uniquely as a product of
n-\ell
factors of degree 1, we get
Q(Z)=c_{\ell}\prod_{i=1}^{n-\ell}(Z-z_{i})
, hence

\tau

be a von Neumann subalgebra of

of the Hilbert space

We will consider below the following particular class of von Neumann subalgebras of

Important von Neumann algebras that will be considered here are the associated von Neumann algebras of groups: let

). As its commutant
JBJ
is a finite von Neumann algebra,
\langle N,e_{B}\rangle
is semifinite; we then denote by
\mathcal{J}(\langle N,e_{B}\rangle)
the norm-closed, two-sided ideal in

\langle N,e_{B}\rangle
generated by its finite projections. If
B=\mathbb{C}1
, then
\langle N,e_{B}\rangle=JB^{\prime}J
coincides with the algebra of all bounded operators on
L^{2}(N,\tau)
and the ideal
\mathcal{J}(\langle N,e_{B}\rangle)
is the ideal of all compact operators
\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(N,\tau))
.

The following definition is due to Popa
[
Po06
]
; notice that the Haagerup property considered in
[
Jo02
]
corresponds to the case
B=\mathbb{C}1
.

Definition 4.1

Let
B\subset N
be von Neumann algebras as above. Then
N
has property H relative to
B
if there exists a sequence
(\phi_{n})
of normal, completely positive
B
-bimodular maps on
N
satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

\phi_{n}(1)\leq 1
and
\tau\circ\phi_{n}\leq\tau
for every
n
;

(2)

(T_{\phi_{n}})\subset\mathcal{J}(\langle N,e_{B}\rangle)
;

(3)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\phi_{n}(x)-x\|_{2}=0
for every
x\in N
.

As for rigidity, relation property H is independent of the chosen trace.

Typical and important examples of inclusions of finite von Neumann algebras with property H as above come from crossed products: assume that a countable group
G
acts on some finite von Neumann algebra
B
and that the action, denoted by
\sigma
, preserves some trace
\tau
.

Then one has:

Proposition 4.2

(
[
Po06
]
, Proposition 3.1) Let
G
,
(B,\tau)
and
\sigma
be as above. Then
N=B\rtimes_{\sigma}G
has property H relative to
B
if and only if the group
G
has the Haagerup property.
\blacksquare

Now, let us consider the following situation: let
\Gamma
be a countable group that acts by automorphisms on some discrete group
H
. Then the von Neumann algebra
L(H\rtimes\Gamma)
associated to the semidirect product group
G=H\rtimes\Gamma
is naturally isomorphic to the crossed product algebra
N=L(H)\rtimes\Gamma
. Assume that
\Gamma
has the Haagerup property and that the pair
(G,H)
has the relative property (T). Then
L(H)
is an HT subalgebra of
N
in the sense of the following definition:

Definition 4.3

(Definition 6.1,
[
Po06
]
)
Let
N
be a finite von Neumann algebra and let
B\subset N
be a von Neumann subalgebra. Then
B
is an HT subalgebra of
N
if the following two conditions are met:

(a)

N
has property H relative to
B
;

(b)

there exists a von Neumann subalgebra
B_{0}\subset B
such that
B^{\prime}_{0}\cap N\subset B
and the inclusion
B_{0}\subset N
is rigid.

If
A\subset N
is a Cartan subalgebra that satisfies both conditions, then it is called an HT Cartan subalgebra.

The importance of existence of HT Cartan subalgebras is illustrated by the following results:

Theorem 4.4

(Theorem 6.2,
[
Po06
]
) Let
N
be a type
\mathrm{II}_{1}
factor with two HT Cartan subalgebras
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
. Then there exists a unitary element
u\in N
such that
uA_{1}u^{*}=A_{2}
.
\blacksquare

Corollary 4.5

(Corollaries 6.5 and 8.2,
[
Po06
]
)
Let
A_{i}\subset N_{i}
,
i=1,2
, be HT Cartan subalgebras of type
\mathrm{II}_{1}
factors, and let
\theta:N_{1}\rightarrow N_{2}
be an isomorphism. Then there exists
u\in U(N_{2})
such that
u\theta(A_{1})u^{*}=A_{2}
. Thus, if
A\subset N
is an HT Cartan subalgebra of a type
\mathrm{II}_{1}
factor
N
,
there exists a unique (up to isomorphism) standard equivalence relation
\mathcal{R}_{N}^{HT}
on the standard probability space
(X,\mu)
such that the inclusion
A\subset N
is isomorphic to the inclusion
L^{\infty}(X,\mu)\subset L(\mathcal{R}_{N}^{HT},s)
for some cocycle
s
. Thus the factor
N
inherits
\ell^{2}
-Betti numbers
\beta_{k}^{HT}(N)\coloneqq\beta_{k}(\mathcal{R}_{N}^{HT})
for every integer
k\geq 0
, which are isomorphism invariants of
N
.
\blacksquare

Following Notation 6.4 of
[
Po06
]
, we denote by
\mathcal{HT}
the class of all type II
1
factors with separable predual with HT Cartan subalgebras.

Another interesting property of the
\ell^{2}
-Betti numbers
(\beta_{k}^{HT}(N))_{k\geq 0}
for
N\in\mathcal{HT}
is that, for every real number
t>0
, we have
\beta_{k}^{HT}(N^{t})=\beta_{k}^{HT}(N)/t
(Corollary 8.2,
[
Po06
]
), where
N^{t}
denotes the
amplification of
N
by
t
, which is the factor
pM_{n}(N)p
, where
n\geq t
is a positive integer and
p\in M_{n}(N)
is a projection with (non-normalized) trace
\operatorname{Tr}\otimes\tau(p)=t
. (We recall that the isomorphism class of
N^{t}
depends neither on
p
nor on
n\geq t
.)

Recall also that if
N
is a type II
1
factor, its
fundamental group

\mathcal{F}(N)
is the set of all
t>0
such that
N^{t}
is isomorphic to
N
. Hence, if
N\in\mathcal{HT}
is such that
0<\beta_{k}(N)<+\infty
for some
k
, then
\mathcal{F}(N)
is trivial.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
:

Set
A_{n}=L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
which is a Cartan subalgebra of
N_{n}
. Then, by Proposition 3.1 of
[
Va23
]
, the inclusion
A_{n}\subset N_{n}
is rigid, and as
G_{n}
has the Haagerup property,
A_{n}
is an HT Cartan subalgebra of
N_{n}
. Furthermore, it follows from Corollary 3.16 in
[
Ga00
]
, that
\beta_{k}(\mathcal{R}_{N}^{HT})=\beta_{k}(G_{n})
for all
k\geq 0
and
n\geq 2
. The rest is a consequence of the above discussion and Corollary
4.5
.

\blacksquare

Remark 4.6

Theorem
1.4
raises a natural question: are
N_{m}
and
N_{n}
isomorphic or not for
m\neq n
? It was pointed out to us by A. Ioana that, by Theorem 1 in his paper
[
Io12
]
, the factor
N_{n}
has a unique group measure space Cartan subalgebra, up to unitary conjugation. As a consequence,
N_{m}
is isomorphic to
N_{n}
if and only if the equivalence relation
\mathcal{S}_{m}
on
\mathbb{T}^{m}
is isomorphic to the equivalence relation
\mathcal{S}_{n}
on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, or, as both are orbital with respect to free and ergodic actions, if and only if the latter are orbit equivalent.

4.2
PROOF OF THEOREM
1.5

First, we need to prove the existence of maximal Haagerup von Neumann algebras in finite ones.

In fact, we will prove a more general result which not only yields the above statement, but which implies the following result of independent interest: given a finite von Neumann algebra
(N,\tau)
with separable predual, the subset
\operatorname{HAP}(N)
of all von Neumann subalgebras of
N
that have the Haagerup property is closed with respect to the
Effros-Maréchal topology
that we recall now (see for instance
[
HW98
]
or
[
AHO23
]
). Denote by
\operatorname{SA}(N)
the set of all von Neumann subalgebras of
N
. As the latter is finite, the topology on
\operatorname{SA}(N)
can be described as follows: a net
(M_{i})_{i\in I}\subset\operatorname{SA}(N)
converges to
M\subset N
if and only

\liminf_{i\in I}M_{i}=\limsup_{i\in I}M_{i}=M,

where

\liminf_{i\in I}M_{i}\coloneqq\{x\in N\colon\exists(x_{i})_{i\in I}\in\ell^{\infty}(I,M_{i})\ :\ \lim_{i\in I}\|x_{i}-x\|_{2}=0\}

and

\limsup_{i\in I}M_{i}\coloneqq\langle\{x\in N\colon\exists(x_{i})_{i\in I}\in\ell^{2}(I,M_{i})\ :\ \mathrm{wo}-\lim_{i\in I}x_{i}=x\}\rangle.

Notation
\langle E\rangle
means the von Neumann algebra generated by the subset
E\subset N
, wo-
\lim
refers to the limit with respect to the weak operator topology on
N
and
\ell^{\infty}(I,M_{i})
denotes the C
∗
-algebra of bounded nets
(x_{i})_{i\in I}\in\prod_{i}M_{i}
.

Statement (1) of the next result was inspired by Proposition 4.7 in
[
AHO23
]
that states that the subset of amenable von Neumann subalgebras of
N
is closed; it is a von Neumann algebraic analogue of the fact that, for a countable group
G
, the set of Haagerup subgroups is closed in the Chabauty topology on the set of all subgroups of
G
(which follows easily from Proposition 6.1.1 in
[
CCJJV01
]
).

Proposition 4.7

Let
(N,\tau)
be a finite von Neumann algebra as above and let
(M_{i})_{i\in I}\subset\operatorname{HAP}(N)
be a net such that
M\coloneqq\liminf_{i\in I}M_{i}
exists. Then
M\in\operatorname{HAP}(N)
. In particular,

(1)

the subset
\operatorname{HAP}(N)
is closed in
\operatorname{SA}(N)
with respect to the Effros-Maréchal topology and,

(2)

for every von Neumann subalgebra
P\in\operatorname{HAP}(N)
, there exists a maximal Haagerup von Neumann subalgebra
Q\subset N
which contains
P
.

Proof
: Denoting by
(N)_{1}
the unit ball of
N
for the operator norm, and setting
(E)_{1}=E\cap(N)_{1}
for every subset
E
of
N
, let
(M_{i})_{i\in I}\in\operatorname{HAP}(N)
and
M\in\operatorname{SA}(N)
be such that
M=\liminf_{i\in I}M_{i}
.

To show that
M\in\operatorname{HAP}(N)
, it suffices to prove that, for every finite set
F\subset(M)_{1}
and every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a completely positive map
\Phi=\Phi_{(F,\varepsilon)}:M\rightarrow M
with the following properties:

(a)

\Phi(1)\leq 1
and
\tau\circ\Phi\leq\tau
;

(b)

the extension
T_{\Phi}
of
\Phi
on
L^{2}(M,\tau)
is a compact operator;

(c)

\max_{x\in F}\|\Phi(x)-x\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon
.

Thus, let
F\subset(M)_{1}
and
\varepsilon>0
as above. Let us number the elements of
F
so that
F=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}\}
. For each
1\leq k\leq r
, there exists a net
(x_{k}^{(i)})_{i\in I}\in\ell^{\infty}(I,M_{i})
such that
x_{k}^{(i)}\in(M_{i})_{1}
for every
i
and that

\lim_{i\in I}\|x_{k}^{(i)}-x_{k}\|_{2}=0.

Fix henceforth an element
j\in I
such that

\max_{1\leq k\leq r}\|x_{k}^{(j)}-x_{k}\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon/3.

Since
M_{j}\in\operatorname{HAP}(N)
, there exists a completely positive map
\phi_{j}:M_{j}\rightarrow M_{j}
which has properties (a) and (b), and such that

\max_{1\leq k\leq r}\|\phi_{j}(x_{k}^{(j)})-x_{k}^{(j)}\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon/3.

Set
\Phi\coloneqq E_{M}\circ\phi_{j}\circ E_{M_{j}}|_{M}:M\rightarrow M
.
It obviously satisfies condition (a) above. Moreover,
T_{\Phi}=e_{M}T_{\phi_{j}}e_{M_{j}}|_{L^{2}(M,\tau)}
is a compact operator on
L^{2}(M,\tau)
, and finally we have for every
1\leq k\leq r
, since
x_{k}\in M
,

\displaystyle\|\Phi(x_{k})-x_{k}\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\|E_{M}[\phi_{j}(E_{M_{j}}(x_{k}))-x_{k}]\|_{2}\leq\|\phi_{j}(E_{M_{j}}(x_{k}))-x_{k}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\phi_{j}[E_{M_{j}}(x_{k})-x_{k}^{(j)}]\|_{2}+\|\phi_{j}(x_{k}^{(j)})-x_{k}^{(j)}\|_{2}+\|x_{k}^{(j)}-x_{k}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|x_{k}^{(j)}-x_{k}\|_{2}+\|\phi_{i}(x_{k}^{(j)})-x_{k}^{(j)}\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon.

(We have used the inequality
\|\phi_{j}(y)\|_{2}\leq\|y\|_{2}
for every
y\in M_{j}
which follows from
\tau\circ\phi_{j}\leq\tau
.)

Statement (1) follows then trivially, and, in order to prove Statement (2),
by Zorn’s lemma, it suffices to prove that, if
(Q_{i})_{i\in I}
is an increasing net in
\operatorname{HAP}(N)
such that
P\subset Q_{i}
for every
i
, then
Q\coloneqq(\bigcup_{i\in I}Q_{i})^{\prime\prime}\in\operatorname{HAP}(N)
. But obviously
Q=\liminf_{i}Q_{i}
, and
Q\in\operatorname{HAP}(N)
by the main part of the proof.

\blacksquare

Proof of Theorem
1.5
:

Fix
n\geq 2
. The abelian subalgebra
A_{n}=L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
being a Cartan subalgebra of
N_{n}
, by Proposition 3.6 of
[
Po81
]
, it is contained in an intermediate hyperfinite subfactor
P
of
N_{n}
in which it is still a Cartan subalgebra. Let then
P\subset Q\subset N_{n}
be a maximal Haagerup von Neumann subalgebra whose existence is guaranteed by Proposition
4.7
. By the considerations following Definition
3.12
, we see that
Q
is a factor, hence
Q=L(\mathcal{R},s)
, where
\mathcal{R}
is an ergodic subequivalence relation of
\mathcal{S}_{n}
and
s
is a Borel 2-cocycle on
\mathbb{R}
. By Corollary
3.16
, either
\mathcal{R}
is amenable or the inclusion
L^{\infty}(X,\mu)\subset L(\mathcal{R},s)=Q
is rigid. The second case is precluded by the assumption that
Q
has the Haagerup property, so
\mathcal{R}
is amenable, hence
s
is trivial and
Q
is a hyperfinite von Neumann subalgebra.

Next, fix
n\geq 2
even. Let
H
be a maximal amenable subgroup of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
containing some hyperbolic matrix. Set
P=A_{n}\rtimes H
; view
P
as a von Neumann subalgebra of
N_{n}
. Then
P
is a subfactor, by the second part of Theorem
1.1
. Moreover
P
is maximal amenable in
N_{n}
, by a result of Boutonnet and Carderi (Theorem A and Corollary B in
[
BC15
]
). The above argument shows that
P
is maximal Haagerup in
N_{n}
.

Finally, if
n\geq 3
is odd, and if
P
and
Q
are as in the first part of the proof, being factors, they cannot be of the form
L(K)
for any subgroup
K
of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rtimes\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
by Proposition
1.2
.

\blacksquare

Absence of gravitational polarization mechanism in the canonical bimetric theory Preprint: DESY-23-010
Luc Blanchet, Lavinia Heisenberg, François Larrouturou

Abstract
Motivated by a version of the “Dipolar Dark Matter” model, that aims at a relativistic completion of the phenomenology of MOND, we investigate the gravitational polarization mechanism in the canonical bimetric theory with an effective matter coupling. We explicitly show the fundamental obstacle why such theories cannot achieve a consistent gravitational polarization, and thus fail to recover the MONDian phenomenology at low energies.

I
Introduction

Despite passing all current tests with flying colors (the last in date being the discovery of gravitational waves
Abbott et al. 2016
;
Abbott et al. 2017
), our theory of General Relativity (GR) suffers from some “darkness”,
i.e.
, yet ambiguous gravitational phenomena that can be explained either by invoking non-gravitational ingredients, or by “beyond-GR” completions
Heisenberg 2019
. The paramount instance is that stars orbiting in periphery of galaxies do not move accordingly to GR predictions
Bosma 1981
;
Rubin et al. 1982
, but instead behave as if the visible mass of the galaxy was only a small fraction of the total mass. The usual way to address this “missing mass” problem is to postulate the existence of some unknown non-relativistic form of matter, insensitive to electromagnetic forces (otherwise it would have already been seen), coined “cold dark matter”
Bertone et al. 2005
. If this paradigm is a cornerstone of current physics, there is still no evidence of this new particle, despite decades of experimental efforts
Aprile et al. 2020
;
Albert et al. 2020
;
Aad et al. 2021
.

Another way to explain the missing mass problem could be to consider that in some regime, the laws of gravity deviate from GR. This path has been initiated by the 40-years old seminal work of Milgrom
Milgrom 1983
, setting a very simple modification of Newton’s law that accounts for the motion of stars in galaxies and provides a natural explanation for the Tully-Fischer relation. Nevertheless, the MOND (for MOdified Newtonian Dynamics) approach is yet only phenomenological, despite great efforts to embed it within a viable fundamental theory (see
e.g.

Bekenstein 2004
;
Bruneton and Esposito-Farèse 2007
;
Deffayet et al. 2014
, and
Famaey and McGaugh 2012
for a comprehensive review on the MONDian approach). An alternative possibility to build such a relativistic completion is to exploit the dielectric analogy of MOND
Blanchet 2007
, which has led to several “Dipolar Dark Matter” (DDM) models
Blanchet and Le Tiec 2008
;
Blanchet and Le Tiec 2009
;
Bernard and Blanchet 2015
;
Blanchet and Heisenberg 2015a
;
Blanchet and Heisenberg 2015b
;
Blanchet and Heisenberg 2017
. In the present work, we focus on a subclass of such models
Blanchet and Heisenberg 2015a
;
Blanchet and Heisenberg 2015b
;
Blanchet and Heisenberg 2017
that are based on a bimetric extension of GR in which the two metrics are supposed to host opposite “gravitational charges” (see also
Milgrom 2009
for a different type of MONDian bimetric theory).

Thus the DDM phenomenology relies on a mechanism of gravitational polarization associated with opposite gravitational charges. It was indeed claimed in
Blanchet and Heisenberg 2015b
, that in the non-relativistic limit, using the canonical bimetric theory, the matter present in each sector feel forces with opposite signs, leading to the desired polarization. Nevertheless, as shown in the present work, due to a missing term in
Blanchet and Heisenberg 2015b
, unfortunately such polarisation mechanism cannot happen in this bimetric framework, which ruins this DDM model. However previously proposed DDM models
Blanchet and Le Tiec 2008
;
Blanchet and Le Tiec 2009
in standard GR are still viable.

The plan of this paper is as follows. The DDM model used, which is an extension of the one studied in
Blanchet and Heisenberg 2015b
;
Blanchet and Heisenberg 2017
, is presented in Sec.
II
. The proof of the impossibility of a gravitational polarization mechanism in this general set up is then exposed in Sec.
III
. Concluding remarks are presented in Sec.
IV
.

III
Proof of the absence of polarization mechanism

Let us now turn to the study of the weak-field (non-relativistic) limit of the theory (
1
), investigated along the lines of
Blanchet and Heisenberg 2015b
. In order to reach a consistent weak-field limit, we assume that the Universe is empty in average,
i.e.
that the matter only enters at perturbative order in the action. Moreover, seeking for a viable gravitational polarization mechanism requires to perturb both metrics around a common background metric, say
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}
. Note that, as we will see later on, an empty background is not necessarily a flat one, as the interaction term will play the role of an effective cosmological constant. Thus
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}
is not the Minkowskian metric, but rather a de Sitter (or anti-de Sitter) one. We then perturb each metric as

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=(\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu})^{2}\equiv(\bar{g}_{\mu\rho}+h_{\mu\rho})\bar{g}^{\rho\lambda}(\bar{g}_{\lambda\nu}+h_{\lambda\nu})\,,

(8)

\displaystyle f_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=(\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}+\ell_{\mu\nu})^{2}\equiv(\bar{g}_{\mu\rho}+\ell_{\mu\rho})\bar{g}^{\rho\lambda}(\bar{g}_{\lambda\nu}+\ell_{\lambda\nu})\,.

Up to quadratic order in the metric perturbations, the Lagrangian becomes

\Lambda\equiv\Lambda_{g}=\Lambda_{f}

together with the mirror relation obtained by replacing
h^{\rho\sigma}\rightarrow\ell^{\rho\sigma}
.
Therefore, using the conservation laws (
12
) of the stress-energy tensors, we find that the divergence of both field equations (
17

16

\Gamma_{1}\overline{\nabla}_{\rho}\big[h^{\rho\mu}-\ell^{\rho\mu}-h\,\bar{g}^{\rho\mu}+\ell\,\bar{g}^{\rho\mu}\big]=0\,.

(19)

Imposing
\Gamma_{1}=0
, the two sectors would decorrelate at first order, as clear from Eq. (
17

\overline{\nabla}_{\rho}\big[h^{\rho\mu}-\ell^{\rho\mu}\big]=\overline{\nabla}^{\mu}\big[h-\ell\big]\,.

(20)

We now investigate the phenomenological consequences of this constraint in the non-relativistic limit
c\to\infty
. In this regime, we consider Newtonian-like systems, with sizes significantly smaller that the effects of a reasonable cosmological constant. We can thus discard the effect of the effective cosmological constant
\Lambda
, and take a Minkowskian ansatz for
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}
, which allows a proper 3+1 decomposition of the components. On this flat background, we parametrize the metric perturbations by single Newtonian potentials
U_{g}
and
U_{f}
and two “PPN” constants
\gamma_{g}
and
\gamma_{f}
as

\displaystyle h^{00}=\frac{U_{g}}{c^{2}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{c^{4}}\right)\,,\qquad h^{0i}=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{c^{3}}\right)\,,\qquad h^{ij}=\gamma_{g}\,\frac{U_{g}}{c^{2}}\,\delta_{ij}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{c^{4}}\right)\,,

(21)

\displaystyle\ell^{00}=\frac{U_{f}}{c^{2}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{c^{4}}\right)\,,\qquad\ell^{0i}=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{c^{3}}\right)\,,\qquad\ell^{ij}=\gamma_{f}\,\frac{U_{f}}{c^{2}}\,\delta_{ij}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{c^{4}}\right)\,.

The
ij
components of the field equations (
17
) set
\gamma_{g}=\gamma_{f}=1
, and the constraint equation (
20
) then gives

\partial_{i}\big[U_{g}-U_{f}\big]=0\,.

(22)

However, the gravitational polarization mechanism discussed in
Blanchet and Heisenberg 2015b
required that the gradients of
U_{g}
and
U_{f}
have opposite signs: as the particles described by
J_{g}^{\mu}
and
J_{f}^{\mu}
feel the force respectively due to the potentials
U_{g}
and
U_{f}
, they will be displaced in the opposite direction. The
U(1)

A_{\mu}
coupling both sectors would then create an effective polarization of the spacetime medium, reproducing the main feature of the model
Blanchet 2007
. It is thus clear that the constraint (
22
) is incompatible with such a mechanism.

IV
Conclusion

This study has proven that, contrarily to what was claimed in the work
Blanchet and Heisenberg 2015b
, the framework based on canonical bimetric theory is unable to produce a consistent gravitational polarization mechanism. This shortage unfortunately spoils its capacity to account for an effective MONDian phenomenology, and thus, to be a proper relativistic completion of the MOND paradigm. Note that another DDM model, based on standard GR
Blanchet and Le Tiec 2008
;
Blanchet and Le Tiec 2009
, is still viable in this respect; this model has recently been simulated numerically on galactic scales
Stahl et al. 2022
.
Nevertheless, there is still hope that a viable mechanism could be achieved in a ghost-free bigravity framework, as DDM was based on the Hassan-Rosen model, but other bimetric theories exist, for instance the newly proposed minimal bigravity
De Felice et al. 2021
, which has the advantage of having stable cosmological solutions as well as escaping the Higuchi ghost present in usual bigravity scenarii.

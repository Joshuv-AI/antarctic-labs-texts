Abstract
. This paper deals with a second order dynamical system with a Tikhonov regularization term in connection to the minimization problem of a convex Fréchet differentiable function. The fact that beside the asymptotically vanishing damping we also consider an implicit Hessian driven damping in the dynamical system under study allows us, via straightforward explicit discretization, to obtain inertial algorithms of gradient type. We show that the value of the objective function in a generated trajectory converges rapidly to the global minimum of the objective function and depending the Tikhonov regularization parameter the generated trajectory converges weakly to a minimizer of the objective function or the generated trajectory converges strongly to the element of minimal norm from the argmin \argmin set of the objective function. We also obtain the fast convergence of the velocities towards zero and some integral estimates. Our analysis reveals that the Tikhonov regularization parameter and the damping parameters are strongly correlated, there is a setting of the parameters that separates the cases when weak convergence of the trajectories to a minimizer and strong convergence of the trajectories to the minimal norm minimizer can be obtained.



Key Words.
convex optimization; continuous second order dynamical system; Hessian driven damping; Tikhonov regularization; convergence rate; strong convergence.

AMS subject classification.
34G20, 47J25, 90C25, 90C30, 65K10.

Introduction

1.1.
The problem formulation and a model result

In this paper we study the asymptotical behaviour of the trajectories generated by the second order dynamical system

(DS)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\ddot{x}(t)+\frac{\alpha}{t^{q}}\dot{x}(t)+\nabla g\left(x(t)+\left(\gamma+\frac{\beta}{t^{q}}\right)\dot{x}(t)\right)+\epsilon(t)x(t)=0,\\
x(t_{0})=u_{0}\in\mathcal{H},\,\dot{x}(t_{0})=v_{0}\in\mathcal{H},\end{cases}

in connection to the minimization problem

(P)

\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\mathcal{H}}g(x),

where
\mathcal{H}
is a real Hilbert space endowed with the scalar product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and norm
\|\cdot\|
and

g:\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a convex Fréchet differentiable function. Throughout the paper we assume that
{\nabla}g
is Lipschitz continuous on bounded sets and the solution set of
g
, namely
\argmin g,
is non-empty.

The starting time in (
DS
) is
t_{0}>0
and we assume that
\alpha>0,\,q\in]0,1[
and
\gamma>0,\,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
or
\gamma=0,\,\beta>0
. In case
\beta<0
we also assume that
t_{0}>\left|\frac{\beta}{\gamma}\right|^{\frac{1}{q}}.
Further,
\epsilon:[t_{0},+\infty[\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a non-increasing function of class
C^{1}
, such that
\lim\limits_{t\to+\infty}\epsilon(t)=0.

Note that
\epsilon(t)x(t)
in (
DS
) is a Tikhonov regularization term that allows us to obtain the strong convergence of a trajectory generated by (
DS
) to the minimal norm minimizer of
g,
see
[
14
,
6
,
26
,
21
]
. Further, we do not allow
q=1
since in that case (
DS
) becomes the dynamical system studied in
[
2
]
. However, we show that by considering
q\in]0,1[
the results obtained in
[
2
]
can be improved. Further we avoid the case
\gamma=\beta=0
since then (
DS
) becomes the dynamical system studied in
[
26
]
. Our analysis shows that the damping parameters and the Tikhonov regularization parameter are strongly correlated, there is a setting of the parameters which assure weak convergence of the trajectories generated by (
DS
) to a minimizer of
g
and rapid convergence of the function values to the global minimum of
g
, meanwhile for another constellation of the parameters involved, rapid convergence of the function values and strong convergence of the generated trajectories to the minimal norm minimizer is obtained. In order to visualize the facts emphasized above, let us specify
\epsilon(t)=\frac{a}{t^{p}},\,a,p>0
and let us denote
\beta(t)=\gamma+\frac{\beta}{t^{q}}.
It is easy to see that
\lim\limits_{t\to+\infty}\epsilon(t)=0
,
\epsilon(t)
is a non-increasing function of class
C^{1}
and satisfies
\epsilon(t)\geq 0

(ii)

If

1.2.
Motivation and related works

Starting with the seminal work of Su, Boyd and Candès
[
33
]
, second order dynamical systems have been intensively studied in the literature in connection to the minimization problem having in its objective a (convex) smooth function, see
[
3
,
10
,
5
,
8
,
9
,
13
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
20
,
19
,
22
,
23
,
28
,
32
]
. These dynamical systems lead via implicit/explicit discretizations to inertial algorithms, hence the asymptotical behaviour of the trajectories generated by these dynamical systems give an insight into the behaviour of the sequences generated by the algorithms obtained via discretization see
[
4
,
29
,
30
,
31
,
25
]
.

Indeed, according to
[
33
]
the dynamical system

(1)

\ddot{x}(t)+\frac{\alpha}{t}\dot{x}(t)+{\nabla}g(x(t))=0,\,\,x(t_{0})=u_{0},\,\dot{x}(t_{0})=v_{0},\,t_{0}>0,\,u_{0},v_{0}\in\mathcal{H}

with
\alpha=3
is the exact limit of Nesterov’s accelerated convex gradient method
[
30
]
and that is the reason why
(
1
) has been intensively studied in the literature in connection to the minimization problem

\inf_{x\in\mathcal{H}}g(x).
In
[
33
]
the authors proved that
g(x(t))-\min g=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{t^{2}}\right)

for every
\alpha\geq 3
as
t\longrightarrow+\infty
, however they did not show the convergence of a generated trajectory to a minimum of the objective function
g.

In
[
10
]
, Attouch, Chbani, Peypouquet and Redont considered the case
\alpha>3
in (
1
), and showed that the generated trajectory
x(t)
converges weakly to a minimizer of
g
as
t\longrightarrow+\infty
and also that
g(x(t))-\min g=o\left(\frac{1}{t^{2}}\right)
as
t\longrightarrow+\infty
. A perturbed version of (
1
) was studied by Attouch, Peypouquet and Redont in
[
15
]
, see also
[
5
,
9
,
16
,
32
]
. They assumed that the objective
g
is twice continuously differentiable and the perturbation of their system is made at the damping term. More precisely, they studied the dynamical system with Hessian driven damping

(2)

\ddot{x}(t)+\frac{\alpha}{t}\dot{x}(t)+\beta{\nabla}^{2}g(x(t))\dot{x}(t)+{\nabla}g(x(t))=0,\,\,x(t_{0})=u_{0},\,\dot{x}(t_{0})=v_{0},\,t_{0}>0,\,u_{0},v_{0}\in\mathcal{H},

where
\alpha>0
and
\beta>0.

In case
\alpha>3,\,\beta>0
they showed the weak convergence of a generated trajectory to a minimizer of
g
. Moreover, they obtained that in this case the convergence rate of the objective function along the trajectory, that is
g(x(t))-\min g,
is of order
{o}\left(\frac{1}{t^{2}}\right)
.

What one can notice concerning the dynamical systems (
1
) and (
2
) is that these systems will never give through the natural implicit/explicit discretization Nesterov type inertial algorithms, since the gradient of
g
is evaluated in
x(t),
and this term via implicit/explicit discretization will become
{\nabla}g(x_{n})
or
{\nabla}g(x_{n+1})
and never of the form
{\nabla}g(x_{n}+\alpha_{n}(x_{n}-x_{n-1}))
as Nesterov’s gradient method requires. That was the reason why in
[
3
]
, (see also
[
28
]
), Alecsa, László and Pinţa introduced the dynamical system

(3)

\ddot{x}(t)+\frac{\alpha}{t}\dot{x}(t)+\nabla g\left(x(t)+\left(\gamma+\frac{\beta}{t}\right)\dot{x}(t)\right)=0,\,x(t_{0})=u_{0},\,\dot{x}(t_{0})=v_{0},\,t_{0}>0,\,u_{0},v_{0}\in\mathcal{H}

where
\alpha>0,\,\beta\in\mathbb{R},\,\gamma\geq 0.
The term
\nabla g\left(x(t)+\left(\gamma+\frac{\beta}{t}\right)\dot{x}(t)\right)
in (
3
) is called implicit Hessian damping due to the fact that by using the Taylor expansion for
{\nabla}g(\cdot)
we get

{\nabla}g\left(x(t)+\left(\gamma+\frac{\beta}{t}\right)\dot{x}(t)\right)\approx{\nabla}g(x(t))+\left(\gamma+\frac{\beta}{t}\right){\nabla}^{2}g(x(t))\dot{x}(t).

This fact also explains why the trajectories of (
2
) and (
3
) share a similar behaviour. Indeed, the objective function value in a trajectory generated by (
3
) converges in order
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{t^{2}}\right)
to the global minimum of the objective function and the generated trajectory converges weakly to a minimizer of the objective function
g,
see
[
3
]
. Further, according to
[
2
]
, the dynamical system (
3
) can be thought as an intermediate system between the dynamical system (
1
) and the dynamical system (
2
), which inherits the best properties of the latter systems. Note that explicit Euler discretization of (
3
) leads to Nesterov type inertial algorithms, where the gradient is evaluated at an extrapolated term of the form
x_{n}+\frac{\gamma n+\beta}{n+\alpha}(x_{n}-x_{n-1}),
see
[
3
]
.

Tikhonov regularized versions of (
1
) and (
2
) were studied in
[
11
]
and
[
18
]
. In these papers, for a constellation of the parameters the authors obtained fast convergence for the function values in a generated trajectory and weak convergence of the generated trajectory to a minimizer of the objective function, meanwhile for another setting of the parameters the strong convergence result
\liminf_{t\to+\infty}\|x(t)-x^{*}\|=0
have been obtained, where
x(t)
is the generated trajectory and
x^{*}
is the minimal norm minimizer of the objective function. For a while it was an open question whether one can obtain fast convergence of the function values and strong convergence results in a generated trajectory for the same constellation of the parameters involved. The first breakthrough has been obtained in
[
14
]
where the authors introduced the dynamical system

(4)

\displaystyle\ddot{x}(t)+\delta\sqrt{\epsilon(t)}\dot{x}(t)+\nabla g\left(x(t)\right)+\epsilon(t)x(t)=0,\,x(t_{0})=u_{0},\,\dot{x}(t_{0})=v_{0},

with
\delta>0
and succeeded to obtain both fast convergence towards the minimal value of the objective function and the strong convergence result
\liminf_{t\to+\infty}\|x(t)-x^{*}\|=0
. Similar results have been obtained in
[
2
]
for the Tikhonov regularized second order dynamical system (
3
), where the Tikhonov regularization parameter has the form
\epsilon(t)=\frac{a}{t^{2}}
and
\alpha>3.
In contrast, in this paper we assume that
0<q<1
instead of
q=1
considered in
[
2
]
and beside fast convergence rates we are able to obtain the strong convergence result
\lim_{t\to+\infty}\|x(t)-x^{*}\|=0
. Moreover these results hold for every
\alpha>0
and every Tikhonov regularization parameter
\epsilon(t)=\frac{a}{t^{p}},\,q\leq p<q+1.

The results from
[
14
]
have been improved in
[
6
]
where the authors studied the dynamical system (
4
) and succeeded to obtain the strong convergence result
\lim_{t\to+\infty}\|x(t)-x^{*}\|=0.
These results then have been extended and further improved in
[
26
]
, where it is also shown that the best choice for the damping parameter is not
\sqrt{\epsilon(t)}.
We emphasize that the results of this paper are in concordance to the results obtained in
[
26
]
. Further, the results from
[
18
]
have been improved in
[
7
]
where the authors succeeded to obtain fast convergence rates and the strong convergence result
\lim_{t\to+\infty}\|x(t)-x^{*}\|=0.
Since, as we emphasized before, (
DS
) and the Tikhonov regularized system with Hessian driven damping considered in
[
7
]
are strongly related, let us compare the results from this paper and the results from
[
7
]
. The second order dynamical system with Hessian driven damping and Tikhonov regularization studied in
[
7
]
is

(5)

\displaystyle\ddot{x}(t)+\delta\sqrt{\epsilon(t)}\dot{x}(t)+\beta\nabla^{2}g\left(x(t)\right)\dot{x}(t)+\nabla g\left(x(t)\right)+\epsilon(t)x(t)=0,\,x(t_{0})=u_{0},\,\dot{x}(t_{0})=v_{0},

where
g
is a convex function of class
C^{2}
,
\delta,\beta>0
and
\epsilon(t)
is a Tikhonov regularization parameter. According to the model result from
[
7
]
, when one specify
\epsilon(t)=\frac{1}{t^{2q}},\,0<q<1
then the trajectories generated by the dynamical system (
5
) converge strongly to the minimal norm minimizer of
g
,
g(x(t))-\min g=\mathcal{O}(t^{-2q})\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty
and
\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}t^{3q-1}\|{\nabla}g(x(t))\|^{2}dt<+\infty.
In contrast, we assume that our objective function is of class
C^{1}
and according to Theorem
1
the trajectories generated by the dynamical system (
5
) converge strongly to the minimal norm minimizer of
g
whenever
q\leq p<q+1
, and if
2q<p<q+1
then

g(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t))-\min g=\mathcal{O}(t^{-2q})\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty,
but we also have the pointwise estimate for the velocity
\|\dot{x}(t)\|=\mathcal{O}(t^{-q})
. Moreover, we have the integral estimates
\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}t^{q}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}dt<+\infty,\,\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}t^{q}\left(g\left(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t)\right)-\min g\right)dt<+\infty
and

\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}t^{2q}\|{\nabla}g(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t))\|^{2}dt<+\infty,\mbox{ if }\gamma>0,\,\beta\in\mathbb{R}\mbox{ and }p>\max\left(\frac{2q+1}{2},2q\right).

Observe that in this paper we are able to obtain extra and improved pointwise and integral estimates, which shows again that, though the damping parameters and the Tikhonov regularization parameter are strongly correlated, the best choice of the damping is not
\delta\sqrt{\epsilon(t)},\,\delta>0.

Finally, note that explicit Euler discretization of the dynamical system (
DS
) yields to inertial gradient methods. Indeed, by discretizing (
DS
) with the constant stepsize
h
,
t_{n}=nh,\,\,x_{n}=x(t_{n}),\,\epsilon(t_{n})=\epsilon_{n}
we get

\frac{x_{n+1}-2x_{n}+x_{n-1}}{h^{2}}+\frac{\alpha}{h^{q+1}n^{q}}(x_{n}-x_{n-1})+{\nabla}g\left(x_{n}+\left(\frac{\gamma}{h}+\frac{\beta}{h^{q+1}n^{q}}\right)(x_{n}-x_{n-1})\right)+\epsilon_{n}x_{n}=0.

Equivalently, by denoting
h^{2q}
with
s
,
h^{1-q}\alpha
still with
\alpha
,
\frac{\gamma}{h}
still with
\gamma,

\frac{\beta}{h^{q+1}}
still with
\beta
and
h^{2q}\epsilon_{n}
still with
\epsilon_{n}
, the latter equation can be written as a gradient type inertial algorithm with a Tikhonov regularization term, that is,

(6)

x_{n+1}=x_{n}+\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{n^{q}}\right)(x_{n}-x_{n-1})-s{\nabla}g\left(x_{n}+\left(\gamma+\frac{\beta}{n^{q}}\right)(x_{n}-x_{n-1})\right)-\epsilon_{n}x_{n}.

We emphasize that algorithm (
6
) has a similar form as the Nesterov type algorithm with double Tikhonov regularization studied in
[
24
]
, hence the results from this paper opens the gate for the study of the strong convergence of inertial gradient type algorithms with a Tikhonov regularization term.

1.3.
The organization of the paper

The outline of the paper is the following. After a short section concerning the existence and uniqueness of the
trajectories generated by the dynamical system (
DS
), in section 3 we deal with the setting of the parameters that assure weak convergence of the generated trajectories to a minimizer of the objective function. To this purpose we introduce a general energy functional, which will play the role of a Lyapunov function associated to the dynamical system (
DS
) and beside the weak convergence of the generated trajectories to a minimizer of the objective function we obtain fast convergence rates for the function values and velocity but also some integral estimates involving the gradient of the objective function. In section 4 for another constellation of the parameters, by constructing a powerful Lyapunov function we obtain strong convergence of the generated trajectories to the minimal norm minimizer of the objective function, but also fast convergence of the function values in a generated trajectory and fast convergence of the velocity. Some integral estimates are also provided.
In section 5 we conclude our paper and we present some possible related future researches.

2.
Existence and uniqueness of the trajectories generated by the dynamical system (
DS
)

In what follows we show the existence and uniqueness of a classical
C^{2}
solution
x
of the dynamical system (
DS
). To this purpose we rewrite (
DS
) as a first order system relevant for the Cauchy-Lipschitz-Picard theorem.

Theorem 2
.

Let
(u_{0},v_{0})\in\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}
. Then, the dynamical system (
DS
) admits a unique global
C^{2}(]t_{0},+\infty[,\mathcal{H})
solution.

Proof
.

Indeed, by using the notation
X(t):=(x(t),\dot{x}(t))
, the dynamical system (
DS
) can be put in the form

(7)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dot{X}(t)=F(t,X(t))\\
X(t_{0})=(u_{0},v_{0}),\end{cases}

where
F:[t_{0},+\infty[\times\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}
,

F(t,u,v)=\left(v,-\frac{\alpha}{t^{q}}v-\epsilon(t)u-\nabla g\left(u+\beta(t)v\right)\right),

and we denote
\beta(t)=\gamma+\frac{\beta}{t^{q}}.
Note that due to our assumptions
\beta(t)>0
on the interval
[t_{0},+\infty[.

Our proof is inspired from
[
8
]
. Since
{\nabla}g
is Lipschitz on bounded sets, it is obvious that for (
7
) the classical Cauchy-Picard theorem can be applied, hence, there exist a unique
C^{1}
local solution
X.
Consequently, (
DS
) has a unique
C^{2}
local solution. Let
x
be a maximal solution of (
DS
), defined on an interval
[t_{0},T_{\max}[,\,T_{\max}\leq+\infty.

In order to prove that
\dot{x}
is bounded we consider the energy functional
W:[t_{0},+\infty[\to\mathbb{R},

(8)

\displaystyle W(t)=g(x(t))+\frac{1}{2}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}+\frac{\epsilon(t)}{2}\|x(t)\|^{2}.

The time derivative of (
8
) reads as

\displaystyle\dot{W}(t)=\langle\nabla g(x(t)),\dot{x}(t)\rangle+\langle\dot{x}(t),\ddot{x}(t)\rangle+\frac{\dot{\epsilon}(t)}{2}\|x(t)\|^{2}+\epsilon(t)\langle x(t),\dot{x}(t)\rangle.

From (
DS
) we have
\ddot{x}(t)=-\frac{\alpha}{t^{q}}\dot{x}(t)-\epsilon(t)x(t)-\nabla g(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t))
, and we obtain

\displaystyle\dot{W}(t)

\displaystyle=\langle{\nabla}g(x(t))-\nabla g(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t)),\dot{x}(t)\rangle+\frac{\dot{\epsilon}(t)}{2}\|x(t)\|^{2}-\frac{\alpha}{t^{q}}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}.

Now, by using the fact that
{\nabla}g
is monotone and
\dot{\epsilon}(t)\leq 0
we get

\displaystyle\dot{W}(t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\beta(t)}\langle{\nabla}g(x(t))-\nabla g(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t)),x(t)-(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t))\rangle+\frac{\dot{\epsilon}(t)}{2}\|x(t)\|^{2}-\frac{\alpha}{t^{q}}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 0.

Consequently,
W
is non-increasing on
[t_{0},+\infty[
, hence by the fact that
g
is bounded from below we get
\|\dot{x}(t)\|<+\infty
for all
t\geq t_{0}.

Let
\|\dot{x}_{\infty}\|=\sup_{t\in[t_{0},T_{\max}[}\|\dot{x}(t)\|

and assume that
T_{\max}<+\infty.
Since
\|x(t)-x(t^{\prime})\|\leq\|\dot{x}_{\infty}\||t-t^{\prime}|
, we get that
\lim_{t\to T_{\max}}x(t):=x_{\infty}\in\mathcal{H}
. By (
DS
) the map
\ddot{x}
is also bounded on the interval
[t_{0},T_{\max}[
and under the same argument as before

\lim_{t\to T_{\max}}\dot{x}(t):=\dot{x}_{\infty}
exists. Applying the local existence theorem with initial data
(x_{\infty},\dot{x}_{\infty})
, we can extend the maximal solution to a strictly larger interval, a clear contradiction. Hence
T_{\max}=+\infty,
which completes the proof.

\blacksquare

3.
Weak convergence for the trajectories of the regularized dynamical system (
DS
) and fast convergence for the function values in a generated trajectory

In this section we show that the function values in a trajectory generated by the dynamical system (
DS
) converge to the global minimum of the objective function. Further, we obtain some pointwise and integral estimates concerning the function values in a trajectory generated by the dynamical system (
DS
), but also for the gradient and velocity.
Finally, we give sufficient conditions on the Tikhonov regularization parameter in order to show that the trajectory
x(t)
generated by the dynamical system (
DS
) converges weakly to a minimizer of our objective function
g.

In this section we consider the following weak assumption:

(C0)

\displaystyle\text{There exist }0<K\text{ such that }\frac{q(1-q)}{t^{2}}\leq\epsilon(t)\leq\frac{K}{t^{q}},\mbox{ for }t\mbox{ large enough.}

Our first general result is the following.

Theorem 3
.

For some starting points
u_{0},\,v_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
let
x:[t_{0},\infty)\to\mathcal{H}
be the unique global solution of (

\mathcal{E}
we obtain

g(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t))-g^{*}=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{t^{2q}}\right),\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty,

\sup_{t\geq t_{0}}\|b(x(t)-x^{\ast})+t^{q}\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}<+\infty,
and
\sup_{t\geq t_{0}}\|x(t)-x^{\ast}\|^{2}<+\infty.

The latter two relations give us at once
\|\dot{x}(t)\|=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{t^{2q}}\right),\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty
and that the trajectory
x(t)
is bounded.

Further, (
23
) yields the following integral estimates.

(24)

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}t^{q}\left(g(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t))-g^{\ast}\right)dt<+\infty,

(25)

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}t^{q}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}dt<+\infty,

(26)

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}t^{2q}\left\|{\nabla}g(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t))\right\|^{2}dt<+\infty,\mbox{ whenever }\ \gamma>0,\,\beta\in\mathbb{R},

(27)

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}t^{q}\left\|{\nabla}g(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t))\right\|^{2}dt<+\infty,\mbox{ whenever }\ \gamma=0,\,\beta>0,

(28)

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}t^{q}\epsilon(t)\|x(t)\|^{2}dt<+\infty

In order to show that
g(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t))-g^{*}=o\left(\frac{1}{t^{2q}}\right),\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty,
and
\|\dot{x}(t)\|=o\left(\dfrac{1}{t^{q}}\right),\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty
assume for now that the limit
\lim\limits_{t\to+\infty}\|x(t)-x^{\ast}\|
exists, which shall be proved in Theorem
6
. Then, (
21
) provides that

\dot{\mathcal{E}}(t)\leq\frac{r_{3}\|x^{\ast}\|^{2}}{2}t^{q}\epsilon(t),\mbox{ for all }t\geq t_{2}.

Obviously, by the hypotheses we have
\dfrac{r_{3}\|x^{\ast}\|^{2}}{2}t^{q}\epsilon(t)\in L^{1}([t_{2},+\infty[)
, hence, according to Lemma (
14
) there exists the limit

\lim\limits_{t\to+\infty}\mathcal{E}(t).

Since the limit
\lim\limits_{t\to+\infty}\|x(t)-x^{\ast}\|
exists we get that the limit

(29)

\lim\limits_{t\to+\infty}t^{2q}\left(g(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t))-g^{\ast}\right)+\frac{t^{2q}\epsilon(t)}{2}\|x(t)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|t^{q}\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}

also exists.

By combining (
24
), (
25
) and (
28
) we find that

(30)

\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{t^{q}}\left(t^{2q}\left(g(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t))-g^{\ast}\right)+\frac{t^{2q}\epsilon(t)}{2}\|x(t)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|t^{q}\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}\right)dt<+\infty.

Since
q<1
, the function
t\longrightarrow\frac{1}{t^{q}}\not\in L^{1}([t_{0},+\infty[)
, therefore (
29
) and (
30
) lead to

(31)

\lim\limits_{t\to+\infty}t^{2q}\left(g(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t))-g^{\ast}\right)+\frac{t^{2q}\epsilon(t)}{2}\|x(t)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|t^{q}\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}=0.

Consequently,

g(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t))-\min g=o\left(\frac{1}{t^{2q}}\right)\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty,

\|x(t)\|=o\left(\frac{1}{t^{q}\sqrt{\epsilon(t)}}\right)\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty,

and

\|\dot{x}(t)\|=o\left(\frac{1}{t^{q}}\right)\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty.

\blacksquare

In what follows we show that under the hypotheses of Theorem
5
, the trajectories generated by the dynamical system (
DS
) converge weakly to a minimizer of the objective function
g.

Theorem 6
.

For some starting points
u_{0},\,v_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
let
x:[t_{0},\infty)\to\mathcal{H}
be the unique global solution of (
DS
). Suppose that the condition (
C0
) holds and the Tikhonov regularization parameter satisfies
\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}t^{q}\epsilon(t)dt<+\infty
.

Then the trajectory
x(t)
converges weakly, as
t\to+\infty
, to an element of
\argmin g
.

Proof
.

We will use Opial’s lemma (see Lemma
15
from Appendix).
In order to prove the existence of the weak limit of
x(t)
, we will use Lemma 16 from
[
26
]
(see Lemma
16
from Appendix) and Lemma 6 from
[
26
]
. For
x^{\ast}\in\argmin g
let us introduce the anchor function

w(t)=\frac{1}{2}\|x(t)-x^{\ast}\|^{2}.

The classical derivation chain rule leads to

\ddot{w}(t)+\frac{\alpha}{t^{q}}\dot{w}(t)=\left\langle\ddot{x}(t)+\frac{\alpha}{t^{q}}\dot{x}(t),x(t)-x^{\ast}\right\rangle+\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}=\left\langle-{\nabla}g(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t))-\epsilon(t){x}(t),x(t)-x^{\ast}\right\rangle+\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}.

We consider

\langle-{\nabla}g(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t)),x(t)-x^{\ast}\rangle=\langle-{\nabla}g(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t)),x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t)-x^{\ast}\rangle+\beta(t)\langle{\nabla}g(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t)),\dot{x}(t)\rangle.

From the monotonicity of
{\nabla}g
we have that

\langle-{\nabla}g(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t)),x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t)-x^{\ast}\rangle\leq 0,

from which it follows that

\langle-{\nabla}g(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t)),x(t)-x^{\ast}\rangle\leq\beta(t)\langle{\nabla}g(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t)),\dot{x}(t)\rangle\leq\frac{|\beta(t)|}{2}\left(\|{\nabla}g(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t))\|^{2}+\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}\right).

Using that
|\beta(t)|\leq\gamma+\dfrac{|\beta|}{t}\leq\tilde{K}=\gamma+\dfrac{|\beta|}{t_{0}}
, it follows that

\langle-{\nabla}g(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t)),x(t)-x^{\ast}\rangle\leq\frac{\tilde{K}}{2}\left(\|{\nabla}g(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t))\|^{2}+\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}\right).

By employing that

\left\langle-\epsilon(t)x(t),x(t)-x^{\ast}\right\rangle=\frac{\epsilon(t)}{2}(\|x^{\ast}\|^{2}-\|x(t)\|^{2}-\|x(t)-x^{\ast}\|^{2}),

and using that
\epsilon(t)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, it follows that

(32)

\ddot{w}(t)+\frac{\alpha}{t^{q}}\dot{w}(t)\leq\frac{\epsilon(t)}{2}\|x^{\ast}\|^{2}+\left(1+\frac{\tilde{K}}{2}\right)\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}+\dfrac{|\beta(t)|}{2}\|{\nabla}g(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t))\|^{2}.

In what follows, let us consider
k(t)=\frac{\epsilon(t)}{2}\|x^{\ast}\|^{2}+\left(1+\frac{\tilde{K}}{2}\right)\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}+\dfrac{\tilde{K}}{2}\|{\nabla}g(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t))\|^{2}
. Note that by the hypotheses of the theorem we have
t^{q}\frac{\epsilon(t)}{2}\|x^{\ast}\|^{2}\in L^{1}[t_{0},+\infty[
.
Further, using the integral estimate (
25
) it follows that
t^{q}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}\in L^{1}[t_{0},+\infty[
, hence
\left(1+\frac{\tilde{K}}{2}\right)t^{q}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}\in L^{1}[t_{0},+\infty[
.
Moreover, in one hand for the case when
\gamma=0,\beta>0
, the integral estimate (
27
) leads to
\beta t^{q}\|{\nabla}g(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t))\|^{2}\in L^{1}[t_{0},+\infty[
. By taking into account that in this case
t^{q}|\beta(t)|=\beta
we find that
\frac{|\beta(t)|}{2}t^{q}\|{\nabla}g(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t))\|^{2}\in L^{1}[t_{0},+\infty[
.
On the other hand, when
\gamma>0,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, from (
26
) we have that
t^{2q}\|{\nabla}g(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t))\|^{2}\in L^{1}[t_{0},+\infty[
, which evidently implies that
t^{q}\|{\nabla}g(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t))\|^{2}\in L^{1}[t_{0},+\infty[
, and we finally obtain
\dfrac{|\beta(t)|}{2}t^{q}\|{\nabla}g(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t))\|^{2}\in L^{1}[t_{0},+\infty[
.

Hence, we get that
t^{q}k(t)\in L^{1}[t_{0},+\infty[
. Now, by applying the same reasoning as in Lemma 6 from
[
26
]
, we obtain the existence of the limit
\lim\limits_{t\to+\infty}\|x(t)-x^{\ast}\|
. Now, if
\overline{x}\in\mathcal{H}
is a weak sequential limit point of
x(t)
then there exists a
sequence
(t_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq[t_{0},+\infty[
such that
\lim_{n\to+\infty}t_{n}=+\infty
and
x(t_{n})
converges weakly
to
\overline{x}
as
n\to+\infty.

Obviously the function
g
is weakly lower semicontinuous, since it is convex
and continuous, consequently
g(\overline{x})\leq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}g(x(t_{n}))=\lim_{n\to+\infty}g(x(t_{n}))=g^{*}=\min g
, which shows that
\overline{x}\in\argmin g.

According to the Opial lemma it follows that

w-\lim_{t\to+\infty}x(t)\in\argmin g.

\blacksquare

4.
On the strong convergence of the trajectories generated by the dynamical system (
DS
)

In the present section we will recall the idea of Tikhonov regularization, which is linked to the strong convergence results to a minimizer of the objective function of minimal norm regarding our dynamical system (
DS
). Let us denote
x_{t}
the unique solution of the strongly convex minimization problem
\min_{x\in\mathcal{H}}g_{t}(x),

where
g_{t}(x)=g(x)+\frac{\epsilon(t)}{2}\|x\|^{2}
. From
[
12
]
we know that the Tikhonov approximation curve
t\to x_{t}
satisfies
x^{\ast}=\lim\limits_{t\to+\infty}x_{t}
, where
x^{\ast}=\argmin\limits_{x\in\argmin g}\|x\|
is the minimal norm element from the set
\argmin g.
Therefore,
x^{\ast}=\proj_{\argmin g}0
and
\|x_{t}\|\leq\|x^{\ast}\|
(see
[
18
]
). By employing the definition of the strongly convex function
g_{t}(x)
we get that

(37)

{\nabla}g_{t}(y)={\nabla}g(y)+\epsilon(t)y,

and by taking into account the unique minimum denoted as
x_{t}
, it follows that

(38)

{\nabla}g_{t}(x_{t})={\nabla}g(x_{t})+\epsilon(t)x_{t}=0.

As claimed in Lemma 2 from
[
6
]
the derivable almost everywhere function
t\longrightarrow x_{t}
is endowed with the property

(39)

\left\|\frac{d}{dt}x_{t}\right\|\leq-\frac{\dot{\epsilon}(t)}{\epsilon(t)}\|x_{t}\|\mbox{ for almost every }t\geq t_{0}.

Because
g_{t}
is strongly convex, the gradient inequality leads to

(40)

g_{t}(y)-g_{t}(x)\geq\langle{\nabla}g_{t}(x),y-x\rangle+\frac{\epsilon(t)}{2}\|x-y\|^{2},\mbox{ for all }x,y\in\mathcal{H}.

In particular

(41)

g_{t}(x)-g_{t}(x_{t})\geq\frac{\epsilon(t)}{2}\|x-x_{t}\|^{2},\mbox{ for all }x\in\mathcal{H}.

Let
y:[t_{0},+\infty[\to\mathcal{H},s\longrightarrow y(s)
derivable at a point
t\in]t_{0},+\infty[.
Then, one has that

(42)

\frac{d}{dt}g_{t}(y(t))=\langle{\nabla}g_{t}(y(t)),\dot{y}(t)\rangle+\frac{\dot{\epsilon}(t)}{2}\|y(t)\|^{2}.

Finally, for all
x,y\in\mathcal{H}
, one has

(43)

g(x)-g(y)=(g_{t}(x)-g_{t}(x_{t}))+(g_{t}(x_{t})-g_{t}(y))+\frac{\epsilon(t)}{2}(\|y\|^{2}-\|x\|^{2})\leq g_{t}(x)-g_{t}(x_{t})+\frac{\epsilon(t)}{2}\|y\|^{2}.

Now we are ready to prove the main result of the present section.

Theorem 10
.

Consider
0<q<1
and assume that the Tikhonov parametrization function satisfies the following conditions:

(C1)

\displaystyle\text{There exists }K_{1}>0\text{ and }r\in(q,1)\text{ such that }\epsilon(t)\geq\dfrac{K_{1}}{t^{r+q}}\mbox{ for }t\mbox{ big enough}.

(C2)

\displaystyle\text{If }\gamma\neq 0\text{ then }\epsilon(t)\leq\dfrac{\alpha}{2\gamma t^{q}}\mbox{ for }t\mbox{ big enough.}

(C3)

\displaystyle\text{There exists }K_{3}>0\text{ such that }\left(\dfrac{\dot{\epsilon}(t)}{\epsilon(t)}\right)^{2}\leq\dfrac{K_{3}}{t^{2}}\mbox{ for }t\mbox{ big enough}.

For some starting points
u_{0},\,v_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
let
x:[t_{0},\infty)\to\mathcal{H}
be the unique global solution of (
DS
). Then, the following statements are true.

(i) (convergence of the trajectory)
x(t)
converges strongly, as
t\to+\infty
, to
x^{*}
, the element of minimum norm of
\argmin g
;

(ii) (fast convergence rates)
g_{t}(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t))-g_{t}(x_{t})=\mathcal{O}(t^{2r-2q-2}+\epsilon(t)t^{r-1}),

g(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t))-\min g=\mathcal{O}(t^{2r-2q-2}+\epsilon(t)),

\|\dot{x}(t)\|=\mathcal{O}(t^{r-q-1}+\sqrt{\epsilon(t)}t^{\frac{r-1}{2}})

and

\|x(t)-x_{t}\|=\mathcal{O}(t^{r-1}+\sqrt{\epsilon(t)}t^{\frac{2q+r-1}{2}})\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty;

(iii) (integral estimates) If
t^{2q-1}\epsilon(t)\in L^{1}[t_{0},+\infty[
then

\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}t^{q}\left(g_{t}(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t))-g_{t}(x_{t})\right)dt<+\infty
and

\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}t^{q}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}dt<+\infty.
Further,

\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}t^{2q}\|{\nabla}g(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t))+\epsilon(t)x(t)\|^{2}dt<+\infty,\mbox{ if }\gamma>0

and

\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}t^{2q-1}\|{\nabla}g(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t))\|^{2}dt<+\infty,\mbox{ if }\gamma=0.

Proof
.

Let us define, for every
t\geq t_{0}
, the following energy functional

(44)

\displaystyle E(t)

\displaystyle=a(t)(g_{t}(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t))-g_{t}(x_{t}))+\frac{1}{2}\|b(x(t)-x_{t})+c(t)\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}+\frac{d(t)}{2}\|x(t)-x_{t}\|^{2},

where
b\in(0,\alpha)
,
c(t)=t^{q}
,
d(t)=b(\alpha-b-qt^{q-1})
and

a(t)=\dfrac{c^{2}(t)-bc(t)\beta(t)}{1+\beta^{\prime}(t)-\dfrac{\alpha\beta(t)}{t^{q}}+\beta^{2}(t)\epsilon(t)}.

Obviously there exists
t_{1}\geq t_{0}
such that
a(t)\geq 0
and

Remark 11
.

Natural candidates for the Tikhonov regularization parameter are
\epsilon(t)=\frac{a}{t^{p}},\,a,p>0.
In this case condition (C1) is nothing else that
p<q+1.
Indeed, for
p<q+1
one can choose
r\in]\max(q,p-q),1[
and
K_{1}=a
and one has
\epsilon(t)\geq\frac{K_{1}}{t^{r+q}}.
Note that condition (C2), in which we assume that
\gamma>0
, holds whenever
p>q
or
p=q
and
a\leq\dfrac{\alpha}{2\gamma}.
Finally, (C3) always holds.

Consequently the following result is valid.

Corollary 12
.

Consider
0<q<1
and
\epsilon(t)=\frac{a}{t^{p}},\,a>0
and
q\leq p<q+1.
Further, when
q=p
assume that
a\leq\dfrac{\alpha}{2\gamma}.
Let
x
be a trajectory generated by the dynamical system (
DS
). Then the following statements hold true.

(i)
x(t)
converges strongly, as
t\to+\infty
, to
x^{*}
, the element of minimum norm of
\argmin g
;

(ii)

g(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t))-\min g=\mathcal{O}(t^{-\min(2q,p)})

and
\|\dot{x}(t)\|=\mathcal{O}(t^{-\frac{\min(2q,p)}{2}})\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty,

further for every
r\in]\max(q,p-q),1[
one has

\|x(t)-x_{t}\|=\mathcal{O}(t^{r-1}+t^{\frac{2q+r-p-1}{2}})\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty;

(iii) If
p>2q
then

\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}t^{q}\left(g_{t}(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t))-g_{t}(x_{t})\right)dt<+\infty
and

\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}t^{q}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}dt<+\infty.
Further,

\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}t^{2q}\|{\nabla}g(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t))+t^{-p}x(t)\|^{2}dt<+\infty,\mbox{ if }\gamma>0

and

\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}t^{2q-1}\|{\nabla}g(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t))\|^{2}dt<+\infty,\mbox{ if }\gamma=0.

(iv) Moreover, if
p>\max\left(\frac{2q+1}{2},2q\right),\,\gamma>0
then

\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}t^{2q}\|{\nabla}g(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t))\|^{2}dt<+\infty.

Proof
.

Note that (i) and (iii) are direct applications of Theorem
10
, hence let us show (ii). According to Theorem
10
the fast convergence rates in this special case are

g(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t))-\min g=\mathcal{O}(t^{2r-2q-2}+t^{-p}),

\|\dot{x}(t)\|=\mathcal{O}(t^{r-q-1}+t^{\frac{r-p-1}{2}})

and

\|x(t)-x_{t}\|=\mathcal{O}(t^{r-1}+t^{\frac{2q+r-p-1}{2}})\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty.

Now, taking into account that
r<1
we get
t^{2r-2q-2}+t^{-p}\leq t^{-2q}+t^{-p}\leq 2t^{-\min(2q,p)}
the claim follows.

Concerning (iv), observe that in this case

t^{2q}\|{\nabla}g(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t))\|^{2}\leq 2t^{2q}\|{\nabla}g(x(t)+\beta(t)\dot{x}(t))+t^{-p}x(t)\|^{2}+2t^{2q-2p}\|x(t)\|^{2}

and
t^{2q-2p}\|x(t)\|^{2}\in L^{1}[t_{0},+\infty[,
hence the claim is a consequence of (iii).

\blacksquare

5.
Conclusion, perspectives

In this paper we studied a second order dynamical system with a Tikhonov regularization term and an implicit Hessian driven damping in connection to a minimization problem of a smooth convex function. Natural explicit discretization of our dynamical system leads to inertial algorithm of gradient type with a Tikhonov regularization term, hence the results of the present paper opens the gate for study the strong convergence properties of these algorithms. We emphasize that the literature is very poor yet in such results, see
[
24
,
27
]
. Depending on the setting of the parameters involved we obtained both weak and strong convergence of the generated trajectories to a minimizer of the objective function and also fast convergence rates and integral estimates involving the function values in a generated trajectory, velocity and gradient. Our results not only extend but also improve the results from the literature concerning second order dynamical systems with a Tikhonov regularization term.

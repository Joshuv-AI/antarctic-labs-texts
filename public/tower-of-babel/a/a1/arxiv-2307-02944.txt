The General Apple Property and Boolean terms in Integral Bounded Residuated Lattice-ordered Commutative Monoids.
by Antoni Torrens

Abstract
In this paper we give equational presentations of the varieties of integral bounded residuated lattice-ordered commutative monoids (bounded residuated lattices for short) satisfying the General Apple Property (GAP), that is, varieties in which all of its directly indecomposable members are local. This characterization is given by means of Boolean terms: A variety 𝖵 \mathsf{V} of bounded residuated lattices has GAP iff there is an unary term b ⁡ ( x ) b(x) such that 𝖵 \mathsf{V} satisfies the equations b ( x ) ∨ ¬ b ( x ) ≈ ⊤ b(x)\lor\neg b(x)\approx\top and ( x k → b ( x ) ) ⋅ ( b ( x ) → k . x ) ≈ ⊤ (x^{k}\to b(x))\cdot(b(x)\to k.x)\approx\top , for some k > 0 k>0 . Using this characterization, we show that for any variety 𝖵 \mathsf{V} of bounded residuated lattice satisfying GAP there is k > 0 k>0 such that the equation k . x ∨ k . ¬ x ≈ ⊤ k.x\lor k.\neg x\approx\top holds in 𝖵 \mathsf{V} , that is, 𝖵 ⊆ 𝖶𝖫 𝗄 \mathsf{V}\subseteq\mathsf{WL_{\mathsf{k}}} . As a consequence we improve Theorem 5.7 of [ CT12 ] , showing in theorem that a variety of bounded residuated lattices has Boolean retraction term if and only if there is k > 0 k>0 such that it satisfies the equation k . x k ∨ k . ( ¬ x ) k ≈ ⊤ k.x^{k}\lor k.(\neg x)^{k}\approx\top . We also see that in Bounded residuated lattices GAP is equivalent to Boolean lifting property (BLP) and so, it is equivalent to quasi-local property (in the sense of [ GLM12 ] ). Finally, we prove that a variety of bounded residuated lattices has GAP and its semisimple members form a variety if and only if there exists an unary term which is simultaneously Boolean and radical for this variety.



Keywords:
Bounded residuated lattice, direct decomposability, Boolean term, radical term, semisimplicity, local algebras, General Apple property.

Introduction

The Apple property (AP) was considered in
[
BB87
]
to study free algebras in locally finite varieties having Fraser-Horn property (FHP).
Following
[
BB87
]
a finite algebra
\boldsymbol{A}
has the
Apple Property
(AP) if for any proper factor congruence relation
\theta
on
\boldsymbol{A}
,
\theta\not=A\times A
, such that

\boldsymbol{A}/\theta
is directly indecomposable, then the interval lattice
[\theta,A\times A]
has exactly one coatom of
\langle Con(\boldsymbol{A}),\subseteq\rangle
, that is, the algebra
\boldsymbol{A}/\theta
is
local
, i.e., it has a unique maximal proper congruence relation.
A variety
\mathsf{V}
has AP if any
\boldsymbol{A}\in\mathsf{V}
finite and directly indecomposable is local,
or equivalently, if any finite algebra in
\mathsf{V}
has AP.

In this paper we consider a more general condition.
We say that an algebra
\boldsymbol{A}
has the
General Apple Property
(GAP) if for
any proper congruence relation
\theta
on
\boldsymbol{A}
such that

\boldsymbol{A}/\theta
is directly indecomposable, then
\boldsymbol{A}/\theta
is local, and a variety
\mathsf{V}
has GAP when any algebra in
\mathsf{V}
has GAP; i.e, (see Lemma
3.4
),
\mathsf{V}
has GAP if and only if any directly indecomposable in
\mathsf{V}
is local.

Our study is focused in integral bounded residuated lattice-ordered commutative monoids (bounded residuated lattices for short). They form an arithmetical variety
\mathsf{BRL}
, i.e., its members are congruence distributive and congruence permutable, and so they have FHP.

For a bounded residuated lattice
\boldsymbol{A}
, GAP can be stated in the next form:

“If
F
is a proper implicative filter such that
\boldsymbol{A}/F
is directly indecomposable, then
F
is contained in a unique maximal proper implicative filter”.

By definition, a
factor congruence relation
on an algebra is a congruence relation on this algebra complemented in the congruence lattice, and which permutes with one of its complements. Algebras whose factor congruences form a Boolean algebra are said to have the
Boolean factor congruence property
(BFP). Varieties with FHP have BFP (see
[
BiBu90
]
), and so, varieties of bounded residuated lattices have BFP.
Since in bounded residuated lattices factor congruences correspond with Boolean (complemented) elements, we deduce (see Lemma
3.6
) that in any bounded residuated lattice
\boldsymbol{A}
GAP is equivalent to:

“Every Stone ultrafilter of
\boldsymbol{A}
is contained in a unique maximal implicative filter of
\boldsymbol{A}
”,

where by Stone ultrafilter we mean an implicative filter generated by an ultrafilter of the Boolean algebra of Boolean elements of
\boldsymbol{A}
.

Using this fact, we give in Theorem
3.8
a characterization of GAP for bounded residuated lattices in similar way as it is done in
[
CT96
, Theorem 3.3]
for MV-algebras. From this result we deduce that GAP is equivalent to Boolean Lifting Property (BLP), in the sense of
[
GM14
]
.
In Theorem
3.10
we give equational presentations of the varieties of bounded residuated lattices satisfying GAP, by means of
Boolean terms
defined in Section 2. As a consequence, we obtain that each variety of bounded residuated lattices satisfying GAP is contained in
\mathsf{WL_{\mathsf{k}}}
for some
k>0
, that is, it satisfies the equation
k.x\lor k.\neg x\approx\top
. We also characterize the varieties of bounded residuated lattices satisfying GAP whose semisimple members form a variety, by means of the existence of radical and Boolean terms. As a consequence, in Theorem

3.19
, we improve Theorem 5.7. of
[
CT12
]
, showing that a variety of bounded residuated lattices has
Boolean retraction term
if and only if there is
n>0
such that it satisfies the equation
n.x^{n}\lor n.(\neg x)^{n}\approx\top
. These results are described in Section 3, which is the main section of the paper.

To obtain these results it was necessary to study the Boolean algebra formed by complemented elements in bounded residuated lattices, the implicative filters generated by its Boolean filters (ultrafilters), called Stone filters (Stone ultrafilters), and its relation with direct decomposability. This is done in Section 2, in which we also introduce Boolean terms, needed in section 3, giving some properties of them.

The paper also contains a preliminary section in which we give the notation and some results on bounded residuated lattices needed in the paper. We explain them in some detail so that the paper is as self-contained as possible; however, more information on these results can be found in
[
GJKO07
,
CT12
,
T16
]
and in the papers and books referenced therein.
This section has two subsection, namely 1.1 and 1.2.
In subsection 1.1, we recall some arithmetical properties, the family of varieties
(\mathsf{WL_{\mathsf{k}}})_{k>0}
introduced in
[
CT12
]
and the family
(\mathsf{E_{m}})_{m>0}
of
m
-contractive varieties
m>0
, which play an important role in the paper.
In subsection 1.2, we recall the relation between implicative filters and congruence relations, the most common properties on prime filters, maximal filters, and their relation with directly indecomposable, simple and semisimple members of
\mathsf{BRL}
.
We also recall some properties on semisimplicity and its relation with radical term.

Finally, at the end of the paper, we present two examples of varieties of bounded residuated lattices admitting boolean term satisfying the condition (
3.1
) and so, by Theorem
3.10
, they are GAP. Furthermore, both admit a Boolean term, which is also a radical term, and thus the classes of their semisimple members are varieties.

The reader familiar with bounded residuated lattices only need a quick reading of section 1, paying special attention to the nomenclature and notation used in the paper. We assume that the reader is also familiar with the basic results on Universal algebra which can be found in the books

Let

The properties listed in following lemma are needed for further results.

Lemma 2.1

Let

be a bounded residuated lattice. Then for any

(c)

]

.

Property

An algebra
\boldsymbol{A}
is called

provided that it has a unique proper maximal congruence, i.e,
\langle Con(\boldsymbol{A}),\subseteq\rangle
has only one coatom.

Lemma 3.1

If
\boldsymbol{A}
is local algebra, then
\boldsymbol{A}
is directly indecomposable.

Proof:

Assume
\boldsymbol{A}
is a local. If
\boldsymbol{A}
is not directly indecomposable, then there is a non-trivial pair factor of congruences
\{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\}
. For
i\in\{1,2\}
,
\theta_{i}\not=\nabla_{A}
, then, since
\langle Con(\boldsymbol{A}),\subseteq\rangle
is closed under the union of upward directed families, by Zorn’s Lemma, there is a proper maximal congruence
\varphi_{i}
containing
\theta_{i}
. Moreover, since
\theta_{1}\vee\theta_{2}=A
, we have
\varphi_{1}\not=\varphi_{2}
. This contradicts the assumption, and so
\boldsymbol{A}
is directly indecomposable.
\Box

The following lemma is easily provable.

Lemma 3.2

Let

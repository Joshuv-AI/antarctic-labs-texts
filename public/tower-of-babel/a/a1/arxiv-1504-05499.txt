Some identities of symmetry for q q -Bernoulli polynomials under symmetric group of degree n n
Dae San Kim, Taekyun Kim

Abstract
. In this paper, we give some new identities of symmetry for q q -Bernoulli polynomials under the symmetric group of degree n n arising from p p -adic q q -integrals on ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} .

1.
Introduction

Let
p
be a fixed prime number. Throughout this paper
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,
\mathbb{Q}_{p}

and
\mathbb{C}_{p}
will denote the ring of
p
-adic integers,
the field of
p
-adic rational numbers and the completion of the
algebraic closure of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. The
p
-adic norm is normalized
as
\left|p\right|_{p}=\frac{1}{p}
. Let
q
be an indeterminate
in
\mathbb{C}_{p}
such that
\left|1-q\right|_{p}<p^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}
.
The
q
-analogue of the number
x
is defined as
\left[x\right]_{q}=\frac{1-q^{x}}{1-q}
.
Note that
\lim_{q\rightarrow 1}\left[x\right]_{q}=x
. Let
UD\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right)

be the space of uniformly differentiable functions on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. For

f\in UD\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right),
the
p
-adic
q
-integral on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}

is defined by Kim as

(1.1)

I_{q}\left(f\right)=\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}f\left(x\right)d\mu_{q}\left(x\right)=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\left[p^{N}\right]_{q}}\sum_{x=0}^{p^{N}-1}f\left(x\right)q^{x},\quad\left(\text{see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{Number}{key-7}{}{}]}}\right).

From (
1.1
), we have

(1.2)

qI_{q}\left(f_{1}\right)-I_{q}\left(f\right)=\left(q-1\right)f\left(0\right)+\frac{q-1}{\log q}f^{\prime}\left(0\right),\quad\text{where }f_{1}\left(x\right)=f\left(x+1\right).

As is well known, the Bernoulli numbers are defined by

B_{0}=1,\quad\left(B+1\right)^{n}-B_{n}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }n=1,\\
0&\text{if }n>1,\end{cases}

with the usual convention about replacing
B^{n}
by
B_{n}
(see

[
6
,
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
7
,
9
,
8
,
10
,
12
,
11
]
).
The Bernoulli polynomials are given by

(1.3)

B_{n}\left(x\right)=\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}B_{l}x^{n-l},\quad\left(n\geq 0\right),\quad\left(\text{see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{Number}{key-11}{}{}]}}\right).

In
[
3
]
, L. Carlitz considered the
q
-analogue of Bernoulli
numbers as follows:

(1.4)

\beta_{0,q}=1,\quad q\left(q\beta_{q}+1\right)^{n}-\beta_{n,q}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }n=1,\\
0&\text{if }n>1,\end{cases}

with the usual convention about replacing
\beta_{q}^{n}
by
\beta_{n,q}
.

He also defined
q
-Bernoulli polynomials as follows:

(1.5)

\beta_{n,q}\left(x\right)=\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}q^{lx}\left[x\right]_{q}^{n-l}\beta_{l,q}\quad\left(\text{see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{Number}{key-2,key-3,key-4,key-8}{}{}]}}\right).

In
[
7
]
, Kim proved the following integral representation
related to Carlitz
q
-Bernoulli polynomials:

(1.6)

\beta_{n,q}\left(x\right)=\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\left[x+y\right]_{q}^{n}d\mu_{q}\left(x\right),\quad\left(n\geq 0\right).

From (
1.2
), we note that

(1.7)

q\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\left[x+1\right]_{q}^{n}d\mu_{q}\left(x\right)-\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\left[x\right]_{q}^{n}d\mu_{q}\left(x\right)=\begin{cases}q-1&\text{if }n=0\\
1&\text{if }n=1\\
0&\text{if }n>1\end{cases}.

By (
1.7
), we get

\beta_{0,q}=1,\quad q\beta_{n,q}\left(1\right)-\beta_{n,q}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }n=1\\
0&\text{if }n>1\end{cases}.

The purpose of this paper is to give identities of symmetry for Carlitz’s

q
-Bernoulli polynomials under the symmetric group of degree
n

arising from
p
-adic
q
-integrals on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

2.
Symmetric identiites of
\beta_{n,q}\left(x\right)
under
S_{n}

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
w_{1},w_{2},\dots,w_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then, we have

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}e^{\left[\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{j}\right)y+\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\right)x+w_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{i}\right)k_{j}\right]_{q}t}d\mu_{q^{w_{1}w_{2}\cdots w_{n-1}}}\left(y\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\left[p^{N}\right]_{q^{w_{1}w_{2}\cdots w_{n-1}}}}\sum_{y=0}^{p^{N}-1}e^{\left[\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{j}\right)y+\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\right)x+w_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{i}\right)k_{j}\right]_{q}t}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}\times q^{\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{j}\right)y}.

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\left[w_{n}p^{N}\right]_{q^{w_{1}w_{2}\cdots w_{n-1}}}}\sum_{m=0}^{w_{n}-1}\sum_{y=0}^{p^{N}-1}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}\times e^{\left[\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{j}\right)\left(m+w_{n}y\right)+\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\right)x+w_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{i}\right)k_{j}\right]_{q}t}q^{w_{1}w_{2}\cdots w_{n-1}\left(m+w_{n}y\right)}.

Thus, by
2.1
, we get

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}\frac{1}{\left[\prod_{l=1}^{n-1}w_{l}\right]_{q}}\prod_{l=1}^{n-1}\sum_{k_{l}=0}^{w_{l}-1}q^{w_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{i}\right)k_{j}}

\displaystyle\times\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}e^{\left[\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{j}\right)y+\prod_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}x+w_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{i}\right)k_{j}\right]_{q}t}d\mu_{q^{w_{1}w_{2}\cdots w_{n-1}}}\left(y\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\left[\prod_{l=1}^{n}w_{l}p^{N}\right]_{q}}\prod_{l=1}^{n-1}\sum_{k_{l}=0}^{w_{l}-1}\sum_{m=0}^{w_{n}-1}\sum_{y=0}^{p^{N}-1}q^{\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{j}\right)\left(m+w_{n}y\right)+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{i}\right)k_{j}w_{n}}

\displaystyle\times e^{\left[\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{j}\right)\left(m+w_{n}y\right)+\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\right)x+w_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{i}\right)k_{j}\right]_{q}t}.

We note that (
2.2
) is invariant under any permutation
\sigma\in S_{n}
.
Therefore, by (
2.2
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

For
w_{1},w_{2},\dots,w_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
, the following
expressions

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left[\prod_{l=1}^{n-1}w_{\sigma\left(l\right)}\right]_{q}}\prod_{l=1}^{n-1}\sum_{k_{l}=0}^{w_{\sigma\left(l\right)}-1}q^{w_{\sigma\left(n\right)}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{\sigma\left(i\right)}\right)k_{j}}

\displaystyle\times\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}e^{\left[\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{\sigma\left(j\right)}\right)y+\prod_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}x+w_{\sigma\left(n\right)}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{\sigma\left(i\right)}\right)k_{j}\right]_{q}t}d\mu_{q^{w_{\sigma\left(1\right)}\cdots w_{\sigma\left(n-1\right)}}}\left(y\right)

are the same for any
\sigma\in S_{n}
.

We observe that

(2.3)

\displaystyle\left[\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{j}\right)y+\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\right)x+w_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{i}\right)k_{j}\right]_{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{j}\right]_{q}\left[y+w_{n}x+\frac{w_{n}}{w_{1}}k_{1}+\cdots+\frac{w_{n}}{w_{n-1}}k_{n-1}\right]_{q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{j}\right]_{q}\left[y+w_{n}x+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{w_{n}}{w_{j}}k_{j}\right]_{q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}.

Thus, by (
2.3
), we get

(2.4)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}e^{\left[\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{j}\right)y+\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\right)x+w_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{i}\right)k_{j}\right]_{q}t}d\mu_{q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}\left(y\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{j}\right]_{q}^{m}\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\left[y+w_{n}x+w_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{k_{j}}{w_{j}}\right]_{q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}^{m}d\mu_{q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}\left(y\right)\frac{t^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{j}\right]_{q}^{m}\beta_{m,q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}\left(w_{n}x+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{w_{n}}{w_{j}}k_{j}\right)\frac{t^{m}}{m!}.

For
m\geq 0
, from (
2.4
), we have

(2.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\left[\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{j}\right)y+\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\right)x+w_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{i}\right)k_{j}\right]_{q}^{m}d\mu_{q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}\left(y\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{j}\right]_{q}^{m}\beta_{m,q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}\left(w_{n}x+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{w_{n}}{w_{j}}k_{j}\right).

Therefore, by Theorem
2.1
and (
2.5
), we obtain the
following theorem.

Theorem 2.2
.

For
m\geq 0
,
w_{1},\dots,w_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
, the
following expressions

\displaystyle\left[\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{\sigma\left(j\right)}\right]_{q}^{m-1}\prod_{l=1}^{n-1}\sum_{k_{l}=0}^{w_{\sigma\left(l\right)}-1}q^{\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{\sigma\left(i\right)}\right)k_{j}w_{\sigma\left(n\right)}}

\displaystyle\times\beta_{m,q^{w_{\sigma\left(1\right)}\cdots w_{\sigma\left(n-1\right)}}}\left(w_{\sigma\left(n\right)}x+w_{\sigma\left(n\right)}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{k_{j}}{w_{\sigma\left(j\right)}}\right)

are the same for any
\sigma\in S_{n}
.

It is easy to show that

(2.6)

\displaystyle\left[y+w_{n}x+w_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{k_{j}}{w_{j}}\right]_{q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left[w_{n}\right]_{q}}{\left[\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{j}\right]_{q}}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{i}\right)k_{j}\right]_{q^{w_{n}}}

\displaystyle+q^{w_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{i}\right)k_{j}}\left[y+w_{n}x\right]_{q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}.

From (
2.6
), we can derive the following equation:

(2.7)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\left[y+w_{n}x+w_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{k_{j}}{w_{j}}\right]_{q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}^{m}d\mu_{q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}\left(y\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{m}\binom{m}{l}\left(\frac{\left[w_{n}\right]_{q}}{\left[\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{j}\right]_{q}}\right)^{m-l}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{i}\right)k_{j}\right]_{q^{w_{n}}}^{m-l}q^{lw_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{i}\right)k_{j}}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}\times\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\left[y+w_{n}x\right]_{q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}^{l}d\mu_{q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}\left(y\right)

(2.8)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{m}\binom{m}{l}\left(\frac{\left[w_{n}\right]_{q}}{\left[\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{j}\right]_{q}}\right)^{m-l}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{i}\right)k_{j}\right]_{q^{w_{n}}}^{m-l}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}\times q^{lw_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{i}\right)k_{j}}\beta_{l,q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}\left(w_{n}x\right).

Thus, by (
2.7
), we get

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{j}\right]_{q}^{m-1}\prod_{l=1}^{n-1}\sum_{k_{l}=0}^{w_{l}-1}q^{w_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{i}\right)k_{j}}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}\times\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\left[y+w_{n}x+w_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{k_{j}}{w_{j}}\right]_{q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}^{m}d\mu_{q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}\left(y\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{m}\binom{m}{l}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{j}\right]_{q}^{l-1}\left[w_{n}\right]_{q}^{m-l}\beta_{l,q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}\left(w_{n}x\right)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}\times\prod_{s=1}^{n-1}\sum_{k_{s}=0}^{w_{s}-1}q^{\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{i}\right)k_{j}w_{n}\left(l+1\right)}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{i}\right)k_{j}\right]_{q^{w_{n}}}^{m-l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{m}\binom{m}{l}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{j}\right]_{q}^{l-1}\left[w_{n}\right]_{q}^{m-l}\beta_{l,q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}\left(w_{n}x\right)T_{m,q^{w_{n}}}\left(w_{1},w_{2},\dots,w_{n-1}\mid l\right),

where

\displaystyle T_{m,q}\left(w_{1},w_{2},\dots,w_{n-1}\mid l\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{s=1}^{n-1}\sum_{k_{s}=0}^{w_{s}-1}q^{\left(l+1\right)\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{i}\right)k_{j}}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{i}\right)k_{j}\right]_{q}^{m-l}.

As this expression is invariant under any permutation in
S_{n}
, we have the following
theorem.

Theorem 2.3
.

For
m\geq 0
,
n,w_{1},\dots,w_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
,
the following expressions

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{m}\binom{m}{l}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{\sigma\left(j\right)}\right]_{q}^{l-1}\left[w_{\sigma\left(n\right)}\right]_{q}^{m-l}\beta_{l,q^{w_{\sigma\left(1\right)}\cdots w_{\sigma\left(n-1\right)}}}\left(w_{\sigma\left(n\right)}x\right)

\displaystyle\times T_{m,q^{w_{\sigma\left(n\right)}}}\left(w_{\sigma\left(1\right)},\dots,w_{\sigma\left(n-1\right)}\mid l\right)

are all the same for
\sigma\in S_{n}
.

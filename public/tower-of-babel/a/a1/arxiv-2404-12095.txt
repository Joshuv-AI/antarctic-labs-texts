Introduction

Throughout this paper
\mathbb{N}
,
\mathbb{R}
, and
\mathbb{R}_{+}

\mathbb{R}^{2}

A sequence
\big<u_{i}\big>_{i=0}^{\infty}
is said to be convex if it satisfies the following inequality

\displaystyle 2u_{i}\leq u_{i-1}+u_{i+1}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad i\in\mathbb{N}.

In other words,
\big<u_{i}\big>_{i=0}^{\infty}

\big<u_{i}-u_{i-1}\big>_{i=1}^{\infty}
is increasing. If the converse of the above inequality holds, we will term
\big<u_{i}\big>_{i=0}^{\infty}
as a concave sequence.

The purpose of this paper is to investigate a necessary and sufficient condition that
n

\displaystyle\dfrac{y_{i}-y_{i-1}}{x_{i}-x_{i-1}}\leq\dfrac{y_{i+1}-y_{i}}{x_{i+1}-x_{i}}\quad\quad\mbox{for all}\quad\quad i\in\{2,\cdots,n-1\}

Based on this result, we derived some other interesting findings. We assume some sequential convexity properties on

\big<y_{i}\big>_{i=1}^{n}

Having prior knowledge of the vertices of a convex polygon often reduces lot of mathematical and computational tasks. In linear programming problems, the optimized value of the cost function always lies in one of the vertices of the constrains formulated convex polygon. In computational geometry, efficient algorithms are highly dependent upon the extreme points of convex
n

In the way of proving our results, we establish several lemmas, and propositions related to fractional inequality, convex sequence, and convex function theory.

1.
Main Results

Our first result shows a very important fractional inequality. Later this inequality is going to be used extensively to establish some of the results. This inequality is also mentioned in one of our recently submitted papers. However, for readability purpose, we mentioned the statement along with the proof.

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be arbitrary. Then for any
a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
and
b_{1},\cdots,b_{n}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
; the following inequalities hold

(1)

\displaystyle\min\bigg\{\dfrac{a_{1}}{b_{1}},\cdots,\dfrac{a_{n}}{b_{n}}\bigg\}\leq\dfrac{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}}{b_{1}+\cdots+b_{n}}\leq\max\bigg\{\dfrac{a_{1}}{b_{1}},\cdots,\dfrac{a_{n}}{b_{n}}\bigg\}.

Proof.

We will prove the theorem by using mathematical induction. For
n=1
, there is nothing to prove. For
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
and
b_{1},b_{2}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, without loss of generality, we can assume that
\dfrac{a_{1}}{b_{1}}\leq\dfrac{a_{2}}{b_{2}}
; which is equivalent to

\displaystyle a_{1}(b_{1}+b_{2})\leq(a_{1}+a_{2})b_{1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad(a_{1}+a_{2})b_{2}\leq(b_{1}+b_{2})a_{2}.

The two inequalities above together yield the following

(2)

\displaystyle\dfrac{a_{1}}{b_{1}}\leq\dfrac{a_{1}+a_{2}}{b_{1}+b_{2}}\leq\dfrac{a_{2}}{b_{2}}

which validates (
1
) for
n=2.
Now we assume that the statement is true for a
n\in\mathbb{N}.

Let
a_{1},\cdots,a_{n},a_{n+1}\in\mathbb{R}
and
b_{1},\cdots,b_{n},b_{n+1}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Utilizing (
2
) and our induction assumption (
1
), we can compute the following two inequalities

\displaystyle\min\bigg\{\dfrac{a_{1}}{b_{1}},\cdots,\dfrac{a_{n}}{b_{n}},\dfrac{a_{n+1}}{b_{n+1}}\bigg\}\leq\min\bigg\{\dfrac{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}}{b_{1}+\cdots+b_{n}},\dfrac{a_{n+1}}{a_{n+1}}\bigg\}\leq\dfrac{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}+a_{n+1}}{b_{1}+\cdots+b_{n}+b_{n+1}}

\displaystyle\mbox{and}

\displaystyle\dfrac{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}+a_{n+1}}{b_{1}+\cdots+b_{n}+b_{n+1}}\leq\max\bigg\{\dfrac{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}}{b_{1}+\cdots+b_{n}},\dfrac{a_{n+1}}{a_{n+1}}\bigg\}\leq\max\bigg\{\dfrac{a_{1}}{b_{1}},\cdots,\dfrac{a_{n}}{b_{n}},\dfrac{a_{n+1}}{b_{n+1}}\bigg\}.

This justifies (
1
) for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and completes our proof.
∎

We can obtain several mean inequalities as a consequence of the above result.

If
b_{1}=\cdots=b_{n}=1
; then (
1
) turns into the standard arithmetic mean inequality which is represented by as follows

\displaystyle\min\{a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}\}\leq\dfrac{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}}{n}\leq\max\{a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}\}.

On the other hand, we can consider
b_{i}=1/x_{i}
and
a_{i}=1
for all
i\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
. Upon substituting these in (
1
), we get the Harmonic mean inequality for the positive numbers
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}
that can be formulated as

\displaystyle\min\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\}\leq\dfrac{n}{\dfrac{1}{x_{1}}+\cdots+\dfrac{1}{x_{n}}}\leq\max\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\}.

Before moving to the main result of this section, there are some notions and terminologies that we need to recall.
There are several ways to represent a convex function. Besides the standard definition of convexity; for any given function, the monotonic property of the associated slope function can also be used to determine convexity. In other words,
A function
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be convex if for any
x^{\prime},

x
and
x"\in I
with
x^{\prime}<x<x^{\prime\prime}
, the below mentioned inequality holds

(3)

\displaystyle\dfrac{f(x)-f(x^{\prime})}{x-x^{\prime}}\leq\dfrac{f(x")-f(x)}{x"-x}.

The other concept we are going to utilize is the epigraph of a function. For a function
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
; the notion of epigraph can be formulated as follows

\displaystyle epi(f)=\Big\{(x,y):\,\,f(x)\leq y,\,\,x\in I\Big\}.

One of the basic characterizations of a convex function can be stated as "A function
f
is convex if and only if
epi(f)
is a convex set."

Now we have all the required tools to proceed to state the first theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
P_{1}(x_{1},y_{1}),\cdots,P_{n}(x_{n},y_{n})
are
n
points(
n\geq 3
) with
x_{1}<\cdots<x_{n}
. Then
\overline{P_{1}P_{2}},\cdots\overline{P_{n}P_{1}}
form a convex
n
-gon in the below mentioned half-space

(4)

\displaystyle\underline{\mathscr{H}}=\bigg\{(x,y)\big|\quad x\in\mathbb{R}\quad\mbox{and}\quad y\leq y_{1}+\bigg(\dfrac{x-x_{1}}{x_{n}-x_{1}}\bigg)(y_{n}-y_{1})\bigg\}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{2}}

if and only if the following inequality holds

(5)

\displaystyle\dfrac{y_{i}-y_{i-1}}{x_{i}-x_{i-1}}\leq\dfrac{y_{i+1}-y_{i}}{x_{i+1}-x_{i}}\quad\quad\mbox{for all}\quad\quad i\in\{2,\cdots,n-1\}.

Proof.

There are several steps involve in the proof.
To prove the theorem, we assume that (
5
) is valid. We define the function
f:[x_{1},x_{n}]\to\mathbb{R}
as follows

(6)

\displaystyle f(x):=ty_{i}+(1-t)y_{i+1}\quad\mbox{where}\quad x:=tx_{i}+(1-t)x_{i+1}\quad(t\in[0,1]\,\mbox{ and }\,i\in\{1,\cdots,n-1\}.

From the construction, it is clearly visible that
f
is formulated by joining total
n-1
consecutive line segments that are defined in between the points
x_{i}
and
x_{i+1}
for all
i\in\{1,\cdots,n-1\}
. we term these as functional line segments of
f
. For the proof, first we are going to establish that the function
f
is convex. But before that we need to validate the statement below

(7)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\mbox{{Slope of the functional line segment(s) of $f$ that contains $(x^{\prime},f(x^{\prime}))$}}

\displaystyle\leq\mbox{{Slope of the line joining the points $(x^{\prime},f(x^{\prime}))$ and $(x^{\prime\prime},f(x^{\prime\prime}))$}}

\displaystyle\leq\mbox{{Slope of the functional line segment(s) of $f$ that contains $(x^{\prime\prime},f(x^{\prime\prime}))$}.}

If both the points
(x^{\prime},f(x^{\prime}))
and
(x^{\prime\prime},f(x^{\prime\prime}))
lie in the same functional line segment, the statement is obvious.

Next we consider the case when
x^{\prime}\in[x_{i-1},

x_{i}[
and
x^{\prime\prime}\in]x_{i},x_{i+1}]
, (
i\in\{2,\cdots,n-1\}
) that is
x^{\prime}
and
x^{\prime\prime}
; lie in two consecutive intervals.
Utilizing (
5
) and basic geometry of slopes in straight lines, we can compute the inequality below

(8)

\displaystyle\dfrac{f(x_{i})-f(x^{\prime})}{x_{i}-x^{\prime}}=\dfrac{f(x_{i})-f(x_{i-1})}{x_{i}-x{i-1}}\leq\dfrac{f(x_{i+1})-f(x_{i})}{x_{i+1}-x_{i}}=\dfrac{f(x")-f(x_{i})}{x"-x_{i}}.

The expression
\dfrac{f(x")-f(x^{\prime})}{x"-x^{\prime}}
can also be written as
\dfrac{f(x")-f(x_{i})+f(x_{i})-f(x^{\prime})}{(x"-x_{i})+(x_{i}-x^{\prime})}
.
This along with (
2
) and (
8
) yields

\displaystyle\dfrac{f(x_{i})-f(x^{\prime})}{x_{i}-x^{\prime}}\leq\dfrac{f(x")-f(x^{\prime})}{x"-x^{\prime}}\leq\dfrac{f(x")-f(x_{i})}{x"-x_{i}}.

and validates the statement (
7
) for this particular case.

Finally we assume
x^{\prime}\in[x_{j},x_{j+1}]
,
x"\in[x_{k},x_{k+1}]
where
j\in\{1,\cdots,n-2\}
and
k\in\{3,\cdots,n\}

First using (
1

\displaystyle\dfrac{f(x")-f(x^{\prime})}{x"-x^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\Big(f(x")-f(x_{k})\Big)+\Big(f(x_{k})-f(x_{k-1})\Big)+\cdots+\Big(f(x_{j+1})-f(x^{\prime})\Big)}{\Big(x"-x_{k}\Big)+\Big(x_{k}-x_{k-1}\Big)+\cdots+\Big(x_{j+1}-x^{\prime}\Big)}

\displaystyle\leq\max\bigg\{\dfrac{f(x")-f(x_{k})}{x"-x_{k}},\dfrac{f(x_{k})-f(x_{k-1})}{x_{k}-x_{k-1}},\cdots,\dfrac{f(x_{j+1})-f(x_{j})}{x_{j+1}-x_{j}}\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{f(x")-f(x_{k})}{x"-x_{k}}=\dfrac{f(x_{k+1})-f(x_{k})}{x_{k+1}-x_{k}}.

Similarly, we can compute the following inequality as well

\displaystyle\dfrac{f(x_{j+1})-f(x_{j})}{x_{j+1}-x_{j}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{f(x_{j+1})-f(x^{\prime})}{x_{j+1}-x^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\min\bigg\{\dfrac{f(x_{j+1})-f(x^{\prime})}{x_{j+1}-x^{\prime}},\dfrac{f(x_{j+2})-f(x_{j+1})}{x_{j+2}-x_{j+1}},\cdots,\dfrac{f(x")-f(x_{k})}{x"-x_{k}}\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{\Big(f(x_{j+1})-f(x^{\prime})\Big)+\Big(f(x_{j+2})-f(x_{j+1})\Big)+\cdots+\Big(f(x")-f(x_{i})\Big)}{\Big(x_{j+1}-x^{\prime}\Big)+\Big(x_{j+2}-x_{j+1})\Big)+\cdots+\Big(x^{\prime}"-x_{k}\Big)}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{f(x")-f(x^{\prime})}{x"-x^{\prime}}.

The above two inequalities establishes the statement (
7

We are now ready to show that

with

\displaystyle\dfrac{f(x)-f(x^{\prime})}{x-x^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{f(x_{i+1})-f(x_{i})}{x_{i+1}-x_{i}}=\dfrac{y_{i+1}-y_{i}}{x_{i+1}-x_{i}}

\displaystyle\mbox{and}

\displaystyle\dfrac{y_{i+1}-y_{i}}{x_{i+1}-x_{i}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{f(x_{i+1})-f(x_{i})}{x_{i+1}-x_{i}}\leq\dfrac{f(x")-f(x)}{x"-x}.

Combining the above two inequalities, we arrive at (
3

Proposition 2.1
.

Suppose
\big<x_{i}\big>_{i=0}^{\infty}
is a strictly increasing and concave sequence. While
\big<y_{i}\big>_{i=1}^{\infty}
is a increasing and convex sequence. Then for any

(9)

\displaystyle\dfrac{y_{i}-y_{i-1}}{x_{i}-x_{i-1}}\leq\dfrac{y_{i+1}-y_{i}}{x_{i+1}-x_{i}}.

By our assumptions

\big<y_{i}\big>_{i=1}^{\infty}

is satisfied.

Proposition 2.5

Let
\big<u_{i}\big>_{i=1}^{n}
be a convex sequence. Then there exists an element

\displaystyle u_{i}\leq u_{m}\quad\mbox{for all $i<m$}\quad\quad\quad\mbox{or}\quad\quad\quad u_{m}\leq u_{i}\quad\mbox{for all $m<i$}.

\big<u_{i}\big>_{i=1}^{n}
implies increasingness of the sequence
\big<u_{i+1}-u_{i}\big>_{i=1}^{n-1}.

If all the terms in this monotone sequence are either non-negative or non-positive, then one can easily validates (
10
). If not, then there exists a
m\in\mathbb{N}\cap]1,n[
such that the following inequalities holds

\displaystyle u_{m+1}-u_{m}\geq 0\quad\quad\quad\quad\mbox{or}\quad\quad\quad u_{m}-u_{m-1}\leq 0.

This together with non-decreasingness property of the sequence
\big<u_{i+1}-u_{i-1}\big>_{i=1}^{n-1}
yields (
10
) and completes the proof of the statement.
∎

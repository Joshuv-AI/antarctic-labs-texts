Gravitational waves induced by the local-type non-Gaussian curvature perturbations
Chen Yuan, Qing-Guo Huang

Abstract
The current observational constraints still leave a substantial mass window ∼ [ 10 − 16 , 10 − 14 ] ∪ [ 10 − 13 , 10 − 12 ] ​ M ⊙ \sim[10^{-16},10^{-14}]\cup[10^{-13},10^{-12}]M_{\odot} for primordial black holes (PBHs) representing all of dark matter (DM) in our Universe. The gravitational waves (GWs) induced by the curvature perturbations are inevitably generated during the formation of these PBHs, and fall in the frequency band of LISA. Such scalar induced gravitational waves (SIGWs) are supposed to be definitely detected by LISA even when the second-order local-type non-Gaussianity characterized by the parameter F NL F_{\rm NL} is taken into account. In this letter, we give a comprehensive analysis of the GWs induced by the local-type non-Gaussian curvature perturbations up to the third-order denoted by the non-linear parameter G NL G_{\rm NL} , and we find that a log-dependent slope of SIGWs in the infrared region is generically predicted and the amplitude of SIGWs can be further suppressed by several orders of magnitude. As a result, the null-detection of SIGWs by LISA cannot rule out the possibility of PBHs making up all of DM.



Introduction.
The nature of dark matter (DM) remains one of the most important problems in fundamental physics. The detection of gravitational waves (GWs) generated by the coalescence of two
\sim 30M_{\odot}
black holes

PBHs are supposed to form from the collapse of over-dense regions in the radiation dominated (RD) era. An over-dense region is generated when very large curvature perturbation (for example,

Usually, the probability density function (PDF) of curvature perturbation is assumed to be Gaussian. However, PBHs are significantly generated by the enhanced scalar curvature perturbation over that measured by the cosmic microwave background on the very large scales, and the non-negligible non-Gaussianity is also expected to be generated. Since the formation of PBHs takes place at the tail of the PDF, the non-Gaussianity would drastically alter the PBH abundance
Byrnes et al. 2012
, and can suppress the SIGWs by several orders of magnitude
Nakama et al. 2017
. Recently, the GWs induced by scalar curvature perturbation with a second-order (
e.g.
F_{\mathrm{NL}}
-order) local-type non-Gaussianity were studied in
Cai et al. 2019d
;
Unal 2019
. However,
F_{\mathrm{NL}}
is just the coefficient of the even-order curvature perturbation, and it is necessary to extend to the odd-order curvature perturbation (
e.g.
G_{\mathrm{NL}}
-order) for achieving a general analysis of GWs induced by the local-type non-Gaussian scalar curvature perturbations.

In this letter, we provide a comprehensive analysis of GWs induced by the local-type non-Gaussian curvature perturbations up to the third-order (
e.g.
G_{\mathrm{NL}}
-order). We find a universal
k^{3}\ln^{2}(4k_{*}^{2}/3k^{2})
behavior of non-Gaussian contribution to SIGWs in the infrared region and the SIGWs can be undetectable by LISA once the
G_{\mathrm{NL}}
-order non-Gaussianity is taken into account.
The goal of this work is to explore whether the null detection of GWs induced by non-Gaussian perturbations can rule out PBH DM or not. As a benchmark example, we consider a local-type model and treat the non-Gaussian parameters as free parameters.

SIGWs induced by local-type non-Gaussian scalar curvature perturbations.
Let’s start with the perturbed metric in Newton gauge

ds^{2}=a^{2}\left\{-(1+2\phi)d\eta^{2}+[(1-2\phi)\delta_{ij}+{1\over 2}h_{ij}]dx^{i}dx^{j}\right\},

(2)

where
\phi
is the linear scalar perturbation and
h_{ij}
is the second-order tensor perturbation. The equation of motion for
h_{ij}
is obtained by the second-order Einstein equation

h_{ij}^{\prime\prime}+2\mathcal{H}h_{ij}^{\prime}-\nabla^{2}h_{ij}=-4\mathcal{T}_{ij}^{\ell m}S_{\ell m},

(3)

where
\mathcal{H}\equiv a^{\prime}/a
is the conformal Hubble parameter and the prime denotes derivative with respect to the conformal time
\eta
. The transverse and traceless projection operator in Fourier space is defined by

\mathcal{T}_{ij}^{\ell m}(\bm{k})\equiv e_{ij}^{(+)}(\bm{k})e^{(+)lm}(\bm{k})+e_{ij}^{(\times)}(\bm{k})e^{(\times)lm}(\bm{k}),

where the polarization tensors are
(e_{i}e_{j}-\bar{e}_{i}\bar{e}_{j})/\sqrt{2}
and
(e_{i}\bar{e}_{j}+\bar{e}_{i}e_{j})/\sqrt{2}

for
+
and
\times
modes respectively. From now on, we choose
e=(1,0,0)
and
\bar{e}=(0,1,0)
.
During the RD era, the source term reads

\displaystyle S_{ij}\!\!=\!3\phi\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\phi\!-\!{1\over\mathcal{H}}(\partial_{i}\phi^{\prime}\partial_{j}\phi+\partial_{i}\phi\partial_{j}\phi^{\prime})\!-\!{1\over\mathcal{H}^{2}}\partial_{i}\phi^{\prime}\partial_{j}\phi^{\prime}\!.\quad

(4)

The observational quantity,
\Omega_{\mathrm{GW}}(k,\eta)
, is defined as the energy density of GWs per logarithm wavelength normalized by the critical energy,
\rho_{c}(\eta)
, namely

\Omega_{\mathrm{GW}}(k,\eta)\equiv{1\over\rho_{c}(\eta)}{\mathrm{d}\rho_{\mathrm{GW}}(k,\eta)\over\mathrm{d}\ln k}={k^{3}\over 48\pi^{2}}\left(k\over\mathcal{H}\right)^{2}\overline{\left\langle|h_{\bm{k}}(\eta)|^{2}\right\rangle},

(5)

where
\mathrm{d}\rho_{\mathrm{GW}}
is the energy density of GWs in the frequency range
(k,k+\mathrm{d}k)
.
The GWs will be induced by curvature perturbations throughout the RD era, and the density parameter at the matter-radiation equality is
\Omega_{\mathrm{GW}}(k)=\Omega_{\mathrm{GW}}(k,\eta\rightarrow\infty)
.
After solving Eq. (
3
) by Green’s function in Fourier space,
\Omega_{\mathrm{GW}}(k)
can be expressed by,
Kohri and Terada 2018
,

\Omega_{\mathrm{GW}}(k)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}u\int_{|1-u|}^{1+u}\mathrm{d}v~P_{\zeta}(uk)P_{\zeta}(vk)I(u,v),

(6)

where the dimensionless curvature power spectrum
P_{\zeta}(k)
is defined as

\displaystyle\left\langle\zeta(\bm{k})\zeta(\bm{k^{\prime}})\right\rangle

\displaystyle\equiv{2\pi^{2}\over k^{3}}P_{\zeta}(k)\delta(\bm{k}+\bm{k^{\prime}}).

(7)

\Omega_{\mathrm{GW}}(k)
in Eq. (
6
) is valid during the RD era and the nowadays density parameter of SIGWs is given by
\Omega_{\mathrm{GW},0}(k)=\Omega_{r}\times\Omega_{\mathrm{GW}}(k)
where
\Omega_{r}
is the density parameter for radiation.
Here we use the the comoving curvature perturbation
\zeta
which is related to
\phi
by
\phi=(2/3)\zeta
, and
I(u,v)
takes the form,
Kohri and Terada 2018
,

\displaystyle I(u,v)

\displaystyle={1\over 12}\left({4v^{2}-(1+v^{2}-u^{2})^{2}\over 4uv}\right)^{2}\left(3(u^{2}+v^{2}-3)\over 4u^{3}v^{3}\right)^{2}

(8)

\displaystyle\times\Bigg(\Big(-4uv+(u^{2}+v^{2}-3)\ln\Big|{3-(u+v)^{2}\over 3-(u-v)^{2}}\Big|\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle+\pi^{2}\left(u^{2}+v^{2}-3\right)^{2}\Theta(u+v-\sqrt{3})\Bigg).

For the local-type non-Gaussian curvature perturbation, in real space, the curvature curvature perturbation
\zeta
can be expanded in terms of the Gaussian part,
\zeta_{g}
, as follows

\zeta(\zeta_{g})=\zeta_{g}+F_{\mathrm{NL}}\left(\zeta_{g}^{2}-\left\langle\zeta_{g}^{2}\right\rangle\right)+G_{\mathrm{NL}}\zeta_{g}^{3},

(9)

where
\left\langle\zeta_{g}^{2}\right\rangle=\int P_{g}(k)\mathrm{d}\ln k
is the variance of the Gaussian part. Here, the non-Gaussian parameters
F_{\mathrm{NL}}
and
G_{\mathrm{NL}}
are related to
f_{\mathrm{NL}}
and
g_{\mathrm{NL}}
in literature by
F_{\mathrm{NL}}=3/5f_{\mathrm{NL}}
and
G_{\mathrm{NL}}=9/25g_{\mathrm{NL}}
respectively. For convenience, we define
P_{g}(k)\equiv A_{g}\tilde{P}_{g}(k)
with
A_{g}
standing for the dimensionless amplitude of the Gaussian part and
\tilde{P}_{g}(k)
being normalized by
\int\tilde{P}_{g}(k)\mathrm{d}\ln k=1
.
Therefore, including the corrections from the non-Gaussian parts, the effective curvature power spectrum reads

\displaystyle{P}_{\zeta}(k)=\alpha_{1}{\tilde{P}}_{1}(k)+\alpha_{2}{\tilde{P}}_{2}(k)+\alpha_{3}{\tilde{P}}_{3}(k),

(10)

where
\alpha_{1}\equiv A_{g}\left(1+3G_{\mathrm{NL}}A_{g}\right)^{2}
,
\alpha_{2}\equiv F_{\mathrm{NL}}^{2}A_{g}^{2}
,
\alpha_{3}\equiv 3G_{\mathrm{NL}}^{2}A_{g}^{3}
,
{\tilde{P}}_{1}\equiv{\tilde{P}}_{g}
and the non-Gaussian power spectra
\tilde{P}_{2}(k)
and
\tilde{P}_{3}(k)
read

\displaystyle{\tilde{P}}_{2}(k)

\displaystyle\equiv\int\mathrm{d}^{3}p{k^{3}\tilde{P}_{g}(p)\tilde{P}_{g}(|\bm{k}-\bm{p}|)\over 2\pi p^{3}|\bm{k}-\bm{p}|^{3}}

(11)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\!\!\!\mathrm{d}v\!\int_{|1-v|}^{1+v}\mathrm{d}u~\frac{\tilde{P}_{g}(uk)\tilde{P}_{g}(vk)}{u^{2}v^{2}},

\displaystyle{\tilde{P}}_{3}(k)

\displaystyle\equiv\int\mathrm{d}^{3}p\mathrm{d}^{3}q{k^{3}\tilde{P}_{g}(p)\tilde{P}_{g}(q)\tilde{P}_{g}(|\bm{k}-\bm{p}-\bm{q}|)\over 8\pi^{2}p^{3}q^{3}|\bm{k}-\bm{p}-\bm{q}|^{3}}

(12)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\!\!\!\mathrm{d}v\!\int_{|1-v|}^{1+v}\mathrm{d}u~\frac{\tilde{P}_{g}(uk)\tilde{P}_{2}(vk)}{2u^{2}v^{2}}.

Here we change the variables to
|\bm{k}-\bm{p}|/k=u
and
p/k=v
. And then the density parameter
\Omega_{\mathrm{GW}}(k)
can be written by

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathrm{GW}}(k)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}{\tilde{\Omega}}_{ij}(k),

(13)

where

\displaystyle{\tilde{\Omega}}_{ij}(k)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}u\int_{|1-u|}^{1+u}\!\!\mathrm{d}v{\tilde{P}}_{i}(uk){\tilde{P}}_{j}(vk)I(u,v).

(14)

By our definition,
{\tilde{\Omega}}_{ij}={\tilde{\Omega}}_{ji}
.
Roughly speaking, the amplitudes of shape functions
{\tilde{\Omega}}_{ij}
are
{\cal O}(1)
because
{\tilde{P}}_{g}
is normalized to be unity, and thus the amplitude of
\Omega_{\mathrm{GW}}(k)
can be estimated by the dominated coefficient among
\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}
.

It is difficult to obtain an analytic result of
{\tilde{\Omega}}_{ij}
for a general curvature power spectrum. Here we consider a log-normal power spectrum for the Gaussian part, namely

{\tilde{P}}_{1}(k)={A_{g}\over\sqrt{2\pi\sigma_{*}^{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{\ln(k/k_{*})^{2}}{2\sigma_{*}^{2}}\right),

(15)

to illustrate the main features of SIGWs. In the limit of
\sigma_{*}\rightarrow 0
,
\tilde{P}_{1}(k)
approaches to a
\delta
power spectrum, namely
k_{*}\delta(k-k_{*})
. In such limit, the higher order power spectra become

\displaystyle\tilde{P}_{2}(k)

\displaystyle=\tilde{k}^{2}\Theta(2-\tilde{k})

(16)

\displaystyle\tilde{P}_{3}(k)

\displaystyle=M(\tilde{k})\Theta(3-\tilde{k}),

(17)

where
\tilde{k}\equiv k/k_{*}
,
M(\tilde{k})\equiv\min(2\tilde{k}^{3},(3-\tilde{k})\tilde{k}^{2})
and
\Theta
is the Heaviside theta function. Then
{\tilde{\Omega}}_{ij}
becomes

\displaystyle\tilde{\Omega}_{11}

\displaystyle={\tilde{k}^{-2}}I\left({1\over\tilde{k}},{1\over\tilde{k}}\right)\Theta(2-\tilde{k}),

\displaystyle\tilde{\Omega}_{12}

\displaystyle=\tilde{k}\int_{\big|1-{1\over\tilde{k}}\big|}^{\min({2\over\tilde{k}},1+{1\over\tilde{k}})}\mathrm{d}v~v^{2}I\left({1\over\tilde{k}},v\right)\Theta(3-\tilde{k}),

\displaystyle\tilde{\Omega}_{22}

\displaystyle=\tilde{k}^{4}\int_{0}^{{2\over\tilde{k}}}\mathrm{d}v\int_{|1-v|}^{1+v}\mathrm{d}u~u^{2}v^{2}I(u,v)\Theta(2-u\tilde{k}),

\displaystyle\tilde{\Omega}_{13}

\displaystyle=\tilde{k}^{-1}\int_{\big|1-{1\over\tilde{k}}\big|}^{\min({3\over\tilde{k}},1+{1\over\tilde{k}})}\mathrm{d}v~M(v\tilde{k})I\left({1\over\tilde{k}},v\right)\Theta(4-\tilde{k}),

\displaystyle\tilde{\Omega}_{23}

\displaystyle=\tilde{k}^{2}\int_{0}^{3\over\tilde{k}}\mathrm{d}v\int_{|1-v|}^{1+v}\mathrm{d}u~u^{2}I(u,v)M(v\tilde{k})\Theta(2-u\tilde{k}),

\displaystyle\tilde{\Omega}_{33}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{{3\over\tilde{k}}}\mathrm{d}v\int_{|1-v|}^{1+v}\mathrm{d}u~M(u\tilde{k})M(v\tilde{k})I(u,v)\Theta(3-u\tilde{k}).

Testing the postulation of PBH DM by LISA.
Similar to
Cai et al. 2019d
, we also consider a narrow curvature power spectrum, and then the PBHs are roughly monochromatic. According to
Byrnes et al. 2012
, the formation probability
\beta
of PBHs can be evaluated by

\displaystyle\beta=\int_{\zeta(\zeta_{g})>\zeta_{c}}{1\over\sqrt{2\pi A_{g}}}\exp\left(-{\zeta_{g}^{2}\over 2A_{g}}\right)\mathrm{d}\zeta_{g}.

(18)

Here, the integrating regions include all of the regions satisfying
\zeta(\zeta_{g})>\zeta_{c}
.
And the formation probability

\displaystyle f_{\mathrm{pbh}}\simeq 2.5\times 10^{8}\beta\left(\frac{g_{*}^{\mathrm{form}}}{10.75}\right)^{-\frac{1}{4}}\left(\frac{m_{\mathrm{pbh}}}{M_{\odot}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

(19)

e.g.
the
G_{\mathrm{NL}}
order). We find that in general the SIGWs can be written in the form of Eq. (
13
) where the amplitude of
\Omega_{\mathrm{GW},0}(f)
is mainly determined by the dominated coefficient among
\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}
, and the shape functions from the non-Gaussian parts have a universal behavior with slope
n_{\rm GW}=3-2/\ln(2f_{*}/\sqrt{3}f)
in the infrared region. Even though a log-dependent slope of SIGW is proposed for a Gaussian curvature perturbation in
Yuan et al. 2019b
, we find such a log-dependent slope is quite generic even for the non-Gaussian curvature perturbations and can be taken as a generic feature for the SIGWs accompanying formation of PBHs.

Because the effects on the SIGWs from non-Gaussianity are completely encoded in the modification of scalar power spectrum (
e.g.
Eq. (
10
)), one can not identify any features in SIGWs as smoking-guns for non-Gaussianity. However, even though different models with or without non-Gaussianity in the very early Universe can produce the same SIGWs if they generate the same effective scalar power spectrum, the PDFs of the curvature perturbations are different, and then lead to different abundances of PBHs due to the different integration region in Eq. (
18
). In this sense, we can identify the non-Gaussianity by considering both the SIGWs and the abundance of PBHs, not SIGWs only.

In addition, we also explore the detectability of the GWs induced by the local-type non-Gaussian curvature perturbation for PBHs serving as all of DM by LISA in detail. Since the third-order non-Gaussianity can further suppress the amplitude of
\Omega_{\mathrm{GW},0}(f)
, the SIGWs may not be detected by LISA even for PBHs in the mass range
\sim[10^{-16},10^{-14}]\cup[10^{-13},10^{-12}]M_{\odot}
making up all of the DM in our Universe. In this sense, a more sensitive GW detector than LISA is needed for testing the postulation of PBH DM.

Although higher-order non-Gaussianities could either enhance or suppress the SIGWs, the
g_{NL}
-order has already made the SIGWs low enough to escape the detection of LISA, and thus a null detection by LISA cannot rule out the PBH DM hypothesis. As a result, we don’t consider the higher-order non-Gaussianities in this letter.

To include the entire non-Gaussian effects instead of taking a local-type model, one can calculate the full probability density function to evaluate the formation of PBHs and the SIGWs based on
Celoria et al. 2021
. Moreover, it has been proposed recently that the quantum diffusion effects during the formation of PBHs are not negligible
Pattison et al. 2017
;
Ezquiaga et al. 2020
;
Ando and Vennin 2021
;
Pattison et al. 2021
, and its correction to the formation of PBHs and SIGWs are worth considering. In addition, the connected diagrams also make contributions to SIGWs
Unal 2019
;
Adshead et al. 2021
, and should be taken into account for a full analysis. We will leave these for future work.

Qualitative analysis on the critical points of the Robin function
Francesca Gladiali, Massimo Grossi, Peng Luo, Shusen Yan

Abstract
. Let Ω ⊂ ℝ N \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N} be a smooth bounded domain with N ≥ 2 N\geq 2 and Ω ε = Ω \ B ⁡ ( P , ε ) \Omega_{\varepsilon}=\Omega\backslash B(P,\varepsilon) where B ⁡ ( P , ε ) B(P,\varepsilon) is the ball centered at P ∈ Ω P\in\Omega and radius ε \varepsilon . In this paper, we establish the number, location and non-degeneracy of critical points of the Robin function in Ω ε \Omega_{\varepsilon} for ε \varepsilon small enough. We will show that the location of P P plays a crucial role on the existence and multiplicity of the critical points. The proof of our result is a consequence of delicate estimates on the Green function near to ∂ B ⁡ ( P , ε ) \partial B(P,\varepsilon) . Some applications to compute the exact number of solutions of related well-studied nonlinear elliptic problems will be showed.

Introduction and main results

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 2
be a smooth domain. For
(x,y)\in D\times D
,
x\neq y
, denote by
G_{D}(x,y)
the Green function in
D
. It verifies

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{x}G_{D}(x,y)=\delta_{x}(y)&\hbox{in }D,\\[5.69054pt]
G_{D}(x,y)=0&\hbox{on }\partial D,\end{cases}

in the sense of distribution. We have the classical representation formula

(1.1)

G_{D}(x,y)=S(x,y)-H_{D}(x,y),

where
S(x,y)
is the
fundamental solution
given by

S(x,y)=\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\big|x-y\big|&~\mbox{if}~N=2,\\[5.69054pt]
\frac{C_{N}}{|x-y|^{N-2}}&~\mbox{if}~N\geq 3,\end{cases}

where
C_{N}:=\frac{1}{N(N-2)\omega_{N}}
, with
\omega_{N}
the volume of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
H_{D}(x,y)
is the
regular part of the Green function
which is harmonic in both variables
x
and
y
. The
Robin
function is defined as

\mathcal{R}_{D}(x):=H_{D}(x,x)~\,~\mbox{in}~D.

Note that our definition differs, up to a multiplicative constant, from that of some other authors (see for example
[
2
]
and
[
6
]
where
\mathcal{R}_{D}(x)=2\pi H_{D}(x,x)
for
N=2
). However, since we are interested in the critical points of the Robin function, this difference plays no role in our results.

The
Robin
function plays a fundamental role in a great number of problems (see
[
2
,
12
]
and the references therein). It also plays a role in the theory of conformal mappings and is closely related to the inner radius function (see
[
19
]
) and to some
geometric quantities such as the capacity and transfinite diameter of sets (see
[
2
,
12
]
).
For elliptic problems involving critical Sobolev exponent
[
16
,
23
]
, the number of solutions is linked to
the the number of non-degenerate critical points of the Robin function.
Despite the great interest on the Robin function, many questions are still unanswered and we are far from a complete understanding of its properties.

The only bounded domain where the Robin function is explicitly known is the ball centered at a point
Q\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
; in this case the Robin function is
radial
and
Q
is the only critical point (it turns out to be non-degenerate). The computation of the number of critical points as well as other geometric properties (for example the shape of level sets) in general domains of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
interested a great number of experts in PDEs, but anyway very few results are known.
One additional difficulty is that it is not known if the Robin function satisfies some differential equation. This is known only for planar simply-connected domains (
[
2
]
) where it solves the Liouville equation.

To our knowledge, one of the first result in general domains is
[
6
]
, in which Caffarelli and Friedman
proved that the Robin function admits only one non-degenerate critical point in convex and bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Note that here a crucial role is played by the Liouville equation.
Later, the existence and uniqueness of critical points of Robin function for a convex and bounded domain in higher dimension was proved in
[
10
]
. However, the non-degeneracy of this critical point is still open. Also some results on the non-degeneracy of the critical points of the Robin function for some symmetric domain can be found in
[
17
]
.
For non-convex domains, for example domains with “rich” topology, we cannot expect the uniqueness of the critical point of the Robin function. But how the topology of the domain impacts the number of critical points of Robin function is still unclear and seems to be a very difficult issue.

In this paper we study what happens when we remove a small hole to a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
. More precisely, denoting by
B(x_{0},r)
the ball centered at
x_{0}
of radius
r
, set

(1.2)

\Omega_{\varepsilon}=\Omega\backslash B(P,\varepsilon)\,~\mbox{with}\,~P\in\Omega.

Robin function in
\Omega

Robin function in
\Omega_{\varepsilon}

Note that the Robin function
\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}
blows up at
\partial B(P,\varepsilon)
. So, for
\varepsilon
small enough,
\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}
and
\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}
look like very differently near

.

Our aim is to study the number of critical points of the Robin function
\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}
as well as their non-degeneracy.
Observe that the regular part
H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)
satisfies

\begin{cases}\Delta H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)=0&\mbox{in}\ \Omega_{\varepsilon},\\
H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)=S(x,y)&\mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}.\end{cases}

Hence, by the standard regularity theory, we have that

(1.3)

H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)\to H_{\Omega}(x,y)

in any compact set
K\subset\overline{\Omega}\setminus B(P,r)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
for some small
fixed

. Setting

in (

) we get that
\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x)\to\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(x)
for any

, which is a good information on the behavior of
\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}

far away
from

. The behavior of
\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}
close to
\partial B_{\varepsilon}
is much more complicated and is the most delicate problem to be addressed in this paper. Here a careful use of the maximum principle for harmonic functions will be crucial. Actually, sharp estimates up to
\partial B(P,\varepsilon)
will allow us to prove the existence of critical points for
\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}
which converge to
P
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Our first result emphasizes the role of the center of the hole
B(P,\varepsilon)
. Indeed the scenario is very different depending on whether
P
is a critical point of
\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}
or not.

In all the paper we denote, for
x\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}
, by
O\big(f(\varepsilon,x)\big)
a quantity such that

O\big(f(\varepsilon,x)\big)\leq C|f(\varepsilon,x)|,

where

is a constant independent of
\varepsilon
and

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
\Omega
is a bounded smooth domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 2
with

If

\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(P)\neq 0,

then for
\varepsilon
small enough,

\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\sharp\Big\{\mbox{critical points of $\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}$ in $B(P,r)\setminus B(P,\varepsilon)$}\Big\}=1,

where
B(P,r)\subset\Omega
is chosen not containing any critical point of
\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}
.
Moreover the critical point
x_{\varepsilon}\in B(P,r)
of
\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}
verifies, for
\varepsilon
small enough,

(P_{1})

(1.4)

x_{\varepsilon}=P+\begin{cases}\varepsilon^{\frac{N-2}{2N-3}}\left(\Big(\frac{2}{N\omega_{N}|\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(P)|^{2N-2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2N-3}}\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(P)+o(1)\right)&~\mbox{for}~N\geq 3,\\[11.38109pt]
r_{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(P)}{\pi^{2}|\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(P)|^{2}}+o(1)\right)&~\mbox{for}~N=2,\end{cases}

where
r_{\varepsilon}
is the unique solution of the equation

(1.5)

r-\frac{\ln r}{\ln\varepsilon}=0~~\mbox{in}~(0,\infty).

(P_{2})

x_{\varepsilon}
is a non-degenerate critical point with

\mbox{index}_{x_{\varepsilon}}\big(\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\big)=(-1)^{N+1}
.

(P_{3})

\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x_{\varepsilon})\to\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(P)
.

Remark 1.2
.

The condition
\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(P)\neq 0
cannot be removed. Indeed, if
\Omega=B(0,R)
we know that
0
is the unique critical point of
\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}
and in the annulus
\Omega_{\varepsilon}=B(0,R)\backslash B(0,\varepsilon)
we have that
\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}
is radial with respect to the origin. Since
\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\Big|_{\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}}=+\infty
we have that the set of minima of
\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}
is a sphere and then
\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}
admits infinitely many minima.

Remark 1.3
.

Although
\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\Big|_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}=+\infty
, we have by
(P_{3})
that
\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x_{\varepsilon})\leq C
. Roughly speaking this means that
x_{\varepsilon}
is not “so close” to
\partial B(P,\varepsilon)
(actually by
(P_{1})
we have that
\frac{|x_{\varepsilon}-P|}{\varepsilon}\to+\infty
).

Remark 1.4
.

Let us give the idea of the proof of the theorem. Our starting point is the following basic representation formula for the gradient of the Robin function (see
[
24
]
, and
[
13
]
for
N=2,3
and
[
4
]
for any
N\geq 2
),

(1.6)

\begin{split}\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x)=&\int_{\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\nu_{\varepsilon}(y)\left(\frac{\partial G_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}\right)^{2}d\sigma_{y}\\
=&\int_{\partial\Omega}\nu(y)\left(\frac{\partial G_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}\right)^{2}d\sigma_{y}-\int_{\partial B(P,\varepsilon)}\frac{y-P}{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{\partial G_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}\right)^{2}d\sigma_{y},\end{split}

where
\nu_{\varepsilon}(y)
and
\nu(y)
are the outer unit normal to
\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}
and
\partial\Omega
respectively. By (
1.6
) we will derive some

N\geq 3
, where we get,
uniformly

(1.7)

\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x)=\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(x)+\!\!\!\!\!\underbrace{\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B(P,\varepsilon)}(x)}_{=-\frac{2\varepsilon^{N-2}}{N\omega_{N}}\frac{x-P}{\left(|x-P|^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}\right)^{N-1}}}\!\!\!\!\!\!+O\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{N-2}}{|x-P|^{N-1}}\right)+O(\varepsilon).

The previous estimate is a
second order
expansion of the Robin function in
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
.

It turns out that
\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(x)
and
\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B(P,\varepsilon)}(x)
are the leading terms of the expansion of
\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x)
.

Note that from (
1.7
) we get that
\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x)\to\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(x)
uniformly on the compact sets of
\Omega

P

\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(x)

\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x)

is the same as
\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(x)
.
Next let us study what happens when
x\to P
. Here the analysis is very delicate but
formally, using the uniform convergence in (
1.7
), we get

\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left(P+\varepsilon^{\frac{N-2}{2N-3}}y\right)\sim\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(P)-\frac{2}{N\omega_{N}}\frac{y}{|y|^{2N-2}}=\nabla\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\partial\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(P)}{\partial x_{j}}y_{j}-\frac{2}{N\omega_{N}(4-2N)}\frac{1}{|y|^{2N-4}}\right).

Since the function
F(y)=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\partial\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(P)}{\partial x_{j}}y_{j}-\frac{2}{N\omega_{N}(4-2N)}\frac{1}{|y|^{2N-4}}
admits a unique non-degenerate critical point with index

we get that the same holds for
\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}
, which proves
(P_{1})
and
(P_{2})
for
N\geq 3
.

The case

is a little bit more complicated because

does not goes to

as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Actually, an additional term appears in the expansion,

(1.8)

\!\!\!\!\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x)=\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(x)+\underbrace{\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B(P,\varepsilon)}(x)}_{=-\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{x-P}{|x-P|^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}}}+\frac{1}{\pi}\left(1-\frac{\ln|x-P|}{\ln\varepsilon}\right)\frac{x-P}{|x-P|^{2}}+O\left(\frac{1}{|x-P||\ln\varepsilon|}\right).

However, up to some technicalities, the proof follows the same line as in the case
N\geq 3
.

Finally let us point out that the maximum principle plays a crucial role to get uniform estimates up to
\partial\Omega
in (
1.7
) and (

Remark 1.12

(1.13)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=u^{p}&{\text{in}~\Omega_{\varepsilon}},\\

2.
Uniform estimates on the regular part of the Green function

Set

\Omega_{\varepsilon}=\Omega\backslash B_{\varepsilon}~\mbox{where}~B_{\varepsilon}=B(P,\varepsilon)~\mbox{with}~P\in\Omega.

Observe that
B_{\varepsilon}\subset\Omega
for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small and we will always assume this is the case. We denote by

B^{c}_{\varepsilon}:=\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{\varepsilon}

and without loss of generality, we take

.

In this section we will prove two crucial lemmas that will be repeatedly used in the proof of our expansion of the Robin function and its derivatives. In order to clarify their role, let us write down the following representation formula for the gradient of the Robin function proved in
[
4
]
, p.170:
for
x\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}
and letting
\nu_{y}
be the outer unit normal to the boundary of the domain,

(2.1)

\begin{split}\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x)=&\int_{\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\nu(y)\left(\frac{\partial G_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(y,x)}{\partial\nu_{x}}\right)^{2}d\sigma_{y}=\int_{\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\nu(y)\left(\frac{\partial G_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}\right)^{2}d\sigma_{y}\\
=&\int_{\partial\Omega}\nu(y)\left(\frac{\partial G_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}\right)^{2}d\sigma-\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{\partial G_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}\right)^{2}d\sigma_{y}\end{split}

=
(
using the identities
G_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}=G_{\Omega}+H_{\Omega}-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}
and
G_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}=G_{B^{c}_{\varepsilon}}+H_{B^{c}_{\varepsilon}}-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}
)

\displaystyle\underbrace{\int_{\partial\Omega}\nu(y)\left(\frac{\partial G_{\Omega}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}\right)^{2}d\sigma_{y}}_{=\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(x)}+\int_{\partial\Omega}\nu(y)\left(\frac{\partial\big(H_{\Omega}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)\big)}{\partial\nu_{y}}\right)^{2}d\sigma_{y}

\displaystyle+2\int_{\partial\Omega}\nu(y)\frac{\partial G_{\Omega}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}\frac{\partial\big(H_{\Omega}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)\big)}{\partial\nu_{y}}d\sigma_{y}

+

\displaystyle\underbrace{-\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{\partial G_{B^{c}_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}\right)^{2}d\sigma_{y}}_{=\nabla\mathcal{R}_{B^{c}_{\varepsilon}}(x)\hbox{ by Lemma \ref{Lap}}}-\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{\partial\big(H_{B^{c}_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)\big)}{\partial\nu_{y}}\right)^{2}d\sigma_{y}

\displaystyle-2\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\frac{\partial G_{B^{c}_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}\frac{\partial\big(H_{B^{c}_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)\big)}{\partial\nu_{y}}d\sigma_{y}.

Hence in order to estimate the integrals in the boxes we need the behavior of
H_{\Omega}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)
on
\partial\Omega
and
H_{B^{c}_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)
on
\partial B_{\varepsilon}
respectively. It will be done in the next lemmas.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
x\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}
and
y\in\bar{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}
, with
|y|\geq C_{0}>0
and
i=1,\cdots,N
, it holds

(2.4)

\frac{\partial\big(H_{\Omega}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)\big)}{\partial y_{i}}=\begin{cases}O\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{N-2}}{|x|^{N-2}}\right)+O(\varepsilon)\,\,\,~&\mbox{for}~N\geq 3,\\[8.53581pt]
O\Big(\left|\frac{\ln|x|}{\ln\varepsilon}\right|\Big)+O\Big(\frac{1}{|\ln\varepsilon|}\Big)\,\,\,~&\mbox{for}~N=2.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let us point out that the functions
H_{\Omega}(x,y)
and
H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)
are well defined if
x\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}
.

For any
y\in\bar{\Omega}
with
|y|\geq C_{0}>0
, we have that

\begin{cases}\Delta_{x}\big(H_{\Omega}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)\big)=0~&\mbox{for}~x\in\Omega\backslash B_{\varepsilon},\\[5.69054pt]
H_{\Omega}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)=0~&\mbox{for}~x\in\partial\Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
H_{\Omega}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)=-G_{\Omega}(x,y)~&\mbox{for}~x\in\partial B_{\varepsilon}.\end{cases}

By the representation formula for harmonic function, we obtain

H_{\Omega}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)=\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\partial G_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,t)}{\partial\nu_{t}}G_{\Omega}(y,t)d\sigma_{t}.

Since
G_{\Omega}(y,t)
has no singularity if
t\in\partial B_{\varepsilon}
and
|y|\geq C_{0}>0
, we see that

\nabla_{y}\big(H_{\Omega}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)\big)
is well defined for
y\in\bar{\Omega}
with
|y|\geq C_{0}>0
.

Now fix
y\in\Omega
with
|y|\geq C_{0}>0
and observe that

\begin{cases}\Delta_{x}\frac{\partial\big(H_{\Omega}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)\big)}{\partial y_{i}}=0~&\mbox{for}~x\in\Omega\backslash B_{\varepsilon},\\[2.84526pt]
\frac{\partial\big(H_{\Omega}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)\big)}{\partial y_{i}}=0~&\mbox{for}~x\in\partial\Omega,\\[2.84526pt]
\frac{\partial\big(H_{\Omega}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)\big)}{\partial y_{i}}=-\frac{\partial G_{\Omega}(x,y)}{\partial y_{i}}~&\mbox{for}~x\in\partial B_{\varepsilon}.\end{cases}

For
N\geq 3
we consider, in
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
, the function
b_{\varepsilon}(x,y):=\frac{\partial\big(H_{\Omega}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)\big)}{\partial y_{i}}+\frac{\varepsilon^{N-2}}{|x|^{N-2}}\frac{\partial G_{\Omega}(0,y)}{\partial y_{i}}
. We
have

\begin{cases}\Delta_{x}b_{\varepsilon}(x,y)=0~&\mbox{for}~x\in\Omega\backslash B_{\varepsilon},\\[8.53581pt]
b_{\varepsilon}(x,y)=\frac{\varepsilon^{N-2}}{|x|^{N-2}}\frac{\partial G_{\Omega}(0,y)}{\partial y_{i}}=O\left(\varepsilon^{N-2}\right)~&\mbox{for}~x\in\partial\Omega,\\[8.53581pt]
b_{\varepsilon}(x,y)=\underbrace{-\frac{\partial G_{\Omega}(x,y)}{\partial y_{i}}+\frac{\partial G_{\Omega}(0,y)}{\partial y_{i}}}_{=O(\varepsilon)\text{ uniformly for $|y|\geq C_{0}>0$}}~&\mbox{for}~x\in\partial B_{\varepsilon}.\end{cases}

So, by the maximum principle, we get that

\frac{\partial\big(H_{\Omega}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)\big)}{\partial y_{i}}+\frac{\varepsilon^{N-2}}{|x|^{N-2}}\frac{\partial G_{\Omega}(0,y)}{\partial y_{i}}=O\big(\varepsilon\big),

for any
x\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}
which implies (
2.4
) for
N\geq 3
.

For
N=2
we consider
b_{\varepsilon}(x,y):=\frac{\partial\big(H_{\Omega}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)\big)}{\partial y_{i}}+\frac{\ln|x|}{\ln\varepsilon}\frac{\partial G_{\Omega}(0,y)}{\partial y_{i}}
, so that, for any
|y|\geq C_{0}>0
,

\begin{cases}\Delta_{x}b_{\varepsilon}(x,y)=0~&\mbox{for}~x\in\Omega\backslash B_{\varepsilon},\\[8.53581pt]
b_{\varepsilon}(x,y)=\frac{\ln|x|}{\ln\varepsilon}\frac{\partial G_{\Omega}(0,y)}{\partial y_{i}}=O\left(\frac{1}{|\ln\varepsilon|}\right)~&\mbox{for}~x\in\partial\Omega,\\[8.53581pt]
b_{\varepsilon}(x,y)=-\frac{\partial G_{\Omega}(x,y)}{\partial y_{i}}+\frac{\partial G_{\Omega}(0,y)}{\partial y_{i}}=O\big(\varepsilon\big)~&\mbox{for}~x\in\partial B_{\varepsilon},\end{cases}

and exactly as for
N\geq 3
we get
b_{\varepsilon}(x,y)=O\left(\frac{1}{|\ln\varepsilon|}\right)

for any
x\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}
,
|y|\geq C_{0}>0
. So the claim follows.
∎

Remark 3.2

If

(see (

3.1
) can be written in this way,

\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x)=\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(x)+\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\frac{|x|^{2}}{|x|^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}}+O\left(\left|\frac{\ln|x|}{\ln\varepsilon}\right|\right).

Proof.

Starting by (
1.1
) and the representation formula for harmonic function we have

H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,t)=-\int_{\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\partial G_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}S(y,t)d\sigma_{y}

and so

(3.2)

\begin{split}\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x)=&-\int_{\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\partial G_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}S(x,y)d\sigma_{y}\\
=&-\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial G_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}S(x,y)d\sigma_{y}-\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\partial G_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}S(x,y)d\sigma_{y}\\
=&\underbrace{-\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial G_{\Omega}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}S(x,y)d\sigma_{y}}_{=\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(x)}-\underbrace{\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial\big(H_{\Omega}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)\big)}{\partial\nu_{y}}S(x,y)d\sigma_{y}}_{K_{1,\varepsilon}}\\
&-\underbrace{\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\partial G_{B_{\varepsilon}^{c}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}S(x,y)d\sigma_{y}}_{=\hbox{ see \eqref{lap2}}}-\underbrace{\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\partial\big(H_{B_{\varepsilon}^{c}}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)\big)}{\partial\nu_{y}}S(x,y)d\sigma_{y}}_{K_{2,\varepsilon}}\\
=&\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(x)-K_{1,\varepsilon}-K_{2,\varepsilon}+\begin{cases}\mathcal{R}_{B_{\varepsilon}^{c}}(x)&\hbox{ if }N\geq 3,\\[8.53581pt]
\mathcal{R}_{B_{\varepsilon}^{c}}(x)+\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\frac{|x|}{\varepsilon}&\hbox{ if }N=2.\end{cases}\end{split}

Computation of
K_{1,\varepsilon}

By (
2.4
) we get that

\begin{split}K_{1,\varepsilon}=&\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial\big(H_{\Omega}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)\big)}{\partial\nu_{y}}S(x,y)d\sigma_{y}\\
=&O\Big(\big|\nabla\big(H_{\Omega}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)\big)\big|\Big)\underbrace{\int_{\partial\Omega}S(x,y)d\sigma_{y}}_{=O(1)}\\
=&\begin{cases}O\Big(\frac{\varepsilon^{N-2}}{|x|^{N-2}}\Big)+O(\varepsilon)\,\,\,~&\mbox{for}~N\geq 3,\\[8.53581pt]
O\Big(\left|\frac{\ln|x|}{\ln\varepsilon}\right|\Big)+O\Big(\frac{1}{|\ln\varepsilon|}\Big)\,\,\,~&\mbox{for}~N=2.\end{cases}\end{split}

Computation of
K_{2,\varepsilon}

First we observe that

(3.3)

\begin{split}&\frac{\partial\big(H_{B^{c}_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)\big)}{\partial\nu_{y}}\Big|_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}=\phi_{\varepsilon}(x,y)\cdot\frac{-y}{\varepsilon}+\begin{cases}O(1)&\mbox{if}\ N\geq 3,\\[5.69054pt]
O\Big(\frac{1}{|\ln\varepsilon|}\Big)&\mbox{if}\ N=2\end{cases}\\
=&\begin{cases}\frac{N-2}{\varepsilon}\left(H_{\Omega}(x,0)-H_{\Omega}(0,0)\frac{\varepsilon^{N-2}}{|x|^{N-2}}\right)+O(1)&\mbox{if}\ N\geq 3,\\[5.69054pt]
-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left[\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\frac{G_{\Omega}(x,0)}{-\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\varepsilon-H_{\Omega}(0,0)}-1\right)-2\left(\nabla_{y}H_{\Omega}(x,0)\cdot y-\nabla_{y}H_{\Omega}(0,0)\cdot y\frac{\ln|x|}{\ln\varepsilon}\right)\right]+O\Big(\frac{1}{|\ln\varepsilon|}\Big)&\mbox{if}\ N=2.\end{cases}\end{split}

Case
N\geq 3
.
By (
3.3
) we get that

\begin{split}K_{2,\varepsilon}=&\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\partial\big(H_{B_{\varepsilon}^{c}}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)\big)}{\partial\nu_{y}}\frac{C_{N}}{|x-y|^{N-2}}d\sigma_{y}\\
=&\left[\frac{N-2}{\varepsilon}\left(H_{\Omega}(x,0)-H_{\Omega}(0,0)\frac{\varepsilon^{N-2}}{|x|^{N-2}}\right)+O(1)\right]\underbrace{\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{C_{N}}{|x-y|^{N-2}}d\sigma_{y}}_{\hbox{by \eqref{ap5}}=\frac{1}{N-2}\frac{\varepsilon^{N-1}}{|x|^{N-2}}}\\
=&O\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{N-2}}{|x|^{N-2}}\right),\end{split}

and so the claim follows for
N\geq 3
.

Case
N=2
.
In the same way we get by (
3.3
),

\begin{split}K_{2,\varepsilon}=&-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\partial\big(H_{B_{\varepsilon}^{c}}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)\big)}{\partial\nu_{y}}\ln|x-y|d\sigma_{y}\\
=&\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}\varepsilon}\underbrace{\left(\frac{G_{\Omega}(x,0)}{-\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\varepsilon-H_{\Omega}(0,0)}-1\right)}_{=-1+O\left(\big|\frac{\ln|x|}{\ln\varepsilon}\big|\right)}\underbrace{\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\ln|x-y|d\sigma_{y}}_{=2\pi\varepsilon\ln|x|\hbox{ by \eqref{ap6}}}\\
&-\frac{1}{\pi\varepsilon}\left(\nabla_{y}H_{\Omega}(x,0)-\nabla_{y}H_{\Omega}(0,0)\frac{\ln|x|}{\ln\varepsilon}\right)\cdot\underbrace{\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}y\ln|x-y|d\sigma_{y}}_{=O\left(\varepsilon^{2}\big|\ln|x|\big|\right)\hbox{ by \eqref{ap7}}}\\
&+O\left(\frac{1}{|\ln\varepsilon|}\right)\underbrace{\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\big|\ln|x-y|\big|d\sigma_{y}}_{=O\left(\varepsilon\big|\ln|x|\big|\right)\hbox{ by \eqref{ap7}}}=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln|x|+O\left(\Big|\frac{\ln|x|}{\ln\varepsilon}\Big|\right).\end{split}

By (
3.2
) and the estimates for
K_{1,\varepsilon}
and
K_{2,\varepsilon}
the claim follows.
∎

3.2.
Estimate of
\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}

The main result of this section is the following.

Proposition 3.3
.

We have that for any
x\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}
,

(3.4)

\begin{split}&\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x)=\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(x)+\nabla\mathcal{R}_{B^{c}_{\varepsilon}}(x)+\begin{cases}O\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{N-2}}{|x|^{N-1}}\right)+O(\varepsilon)\,\,\,~&\mbox{for}~N\geq 3,\\[8.53581pt]
\frac{1}{\pi}\left(1-\frac{\ln|x|}{\ln\varepsilon}\right)\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}+O\left(\frac{1}{|x||\ln\varepsilon|}\right)~&\mbox{for}~N=2.\end{cases}\end{split}

Proof.

Recalling (
2.1
) we have that

(3.5)

\begin{split}&\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x)=\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(x)+\nabla\mathcal{R}_{B^{c}_{\varepsilon}}(x)\\
&+\underbrace{\int_{\partial\Omega}\nu(y)\left(\frac{\partial\big(H_{\Omega}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)\big)}{\partial\nu_{y}}\right)^{2}d\sigma_{y}}_{I_{1,\varepsilon}}+\underbrace{2\int_{\partial\Omega}\nu(y)\frac{\partial G_{\Omega}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}\frac{\partial\big(H_{\Omega}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)\big)}{\partial\nu_{y}}d\sigma_{y}}_{I_{2,\varepsilon}}\\
&-\underbrace{\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{\partial\big(H_{B^{c}_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)\big)}{\partial\nu_{y}}\right)^{2}d\sigma_{y}}_{I_{3,\varepsilon}}-\underbrace{2\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\frac{\partial G_{B^{c}_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}\frac{\partial\big(H_{B^{c}_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)\big)}{\partial\nu_{y}}d\sigma_{y}}_{I_{4,\varepsilon}}.\end{split}

We will show that, for
N\geq 3
, the integrals
I_{1,\varepsilon},\cdots,I_{4,\varepsilon}
are lower order terms with respect to
\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}
and
\nabla\mathcal{R}_{B^{c}_{\varepsilon}}
. If
N=2
the situation is more complicated, because both integrals
I_{3,\varepsilon}
and
I_{4,\varepsilon}
give a contribution.

Computation of
I_{1,\varepsilon}

By (
2.4
) we get that

I_{1,\varepsilon}=\begin{cases}O\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{2(N-2)}}{|x|^{2(N-2)}}\right)+O\big(\varepsilon^{2}\big)\,\,\,~&\mbox{for}~N\geq 3,\\[8.53581pt]
O\left(\left|\frac{\ln^{2}|x|}{\ln^{2}\varepsilon}\right|\right)+O\Big(\frac{1}{|\ln\varepsilon|^{2}}\Big)\,\,\,~&\mbox{for}~N=2.\end{cases}

Computation of
I_{2,\varepsilon}

By (
2.4
) we get that

\begin{split}|I_{2,\varepsilon}|=&O\Big(\big|\nabla\big(H_{\Omega}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)\big)\big|\Big)\underbrace{\int_{\partial\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial G_{\Omega}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}\right|d\sigma_{y}}_{=1}\\
=&\begin{cases}O\Big(\frac{\varepsilon^{N-2}}{|x|^{N-2}}\Big)+O\big(\varepsilon\big)\,\,\,~&\mbox{for}~N\geq 3,\\[8.53581pt]
O\Big(\left|\frac{\ln|x|}{\ln\varepsilon}\right|\Big)+O\Big(\frac{1}{|\ln\varepsilon|}\Big)\,\,\,~&\mbox{for}~N=2.\end{cases}\end{split}

Computation of
I_{3,\varepsilon}

Then we look at the cases
N\geq 3
and
N=2
separately.

Case
N\geq 3

By (
3.3
) we get

\begin{split}I_{3,\varepsilon}=&\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{\partial\big(H_{B^{c}_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)\big)}{\partial\nu_{y}}\right)^{2}d\sigma_{y}\\
=&(N-2)^{2}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\left[\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left(H_{\Omega}(x,0)-H_{\Omega}(0,0)\frac{\varepsilon^{N-2}}{|x|^{N-2}}\right)+O(1)\right]^{2}d\sigma_{y}\\
\end{split}

\begin{split}=&\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(H_{\Omega}(x,0)-H_{\Omega}(0,0)\frac{\varepsilon^{N-2}}{|x|^{N-2}}\right)^{2}\underbrace{\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{y}{\varepsilon}d\sigma_{y}}_{=0}+O\Big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big)\underbrace{\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\left|\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\right|d\sigma_{y}}_{=O(\varepsilon^{N-1})}\\
&+O(1)\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\left|\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\right|d\sigma_{y}=O\Big(\varepsilon^{N-2}\Big).\end{split}

Case
N=2

Again by (
3.3
) we have that

\begin{split}I_{3,\varepsilon}=&\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{\partial\big(H_{B^{c}_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)\big)}{\partial\nu_{y}}\right)^{2}d\sigma_{y}=\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\left(\phi_{\varepsilon}(x,y)\cdot\nu_{y}+O\Big(\frac{1}{|\ln\varepsilon|}\Big)\right)^{2}d\sigma_{y}\\
=&\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\left(\phi_{\varepsilon}(x,y)\cdot\nu_{y}\right)^{2}d\sigma_{y}+O\Big(\frac{1}{|\ln\varepsilon|}\Big)\underbrace{\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}|\phi_{\varepsilon}(x,y)|d\sigma_{y}}_{=O(1)}+O\Big(\frac{\varepsilon}{(\ln\varepsilon)^{2}}\Big)\\
=&\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\left(\phi_{\varepsilon}(x,y)\cdot\nu_{y}\right)^{2}d\sigma_{y}+O\Big(\frac{1}{|\ln\varepsilon|}\Big)=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}\left(\frac{G_{\Omega}(x,0)}{-\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\varepsilon-H_{\Omega}(0,0)}-1\right)^{2}\underbrace{\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{y}{\varepsilon}d\sigma_{y}}_{=0}\\
&+\frac{2}{\pi\varepsilon^{2}}\left(1-\frac{\ln|x|}{\ln\varepsilon}+O\Big(\frac{1}{|\ln\varepsilon|}\Big)\right)\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\left[\nabla_{y}H_{\Omega}(x,0)\cdot y-\nabla_{y}H_{\Omega}(0,0)\cdot y\frac{\ln|x|}{\ln\varepsilon}\right]d\sigma_{y}\\
&+\underbrace{\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{|y|}{\varepsilon}O(1)d\sigma_{y}}_{=O(\varepsilon)}+O\Big(\frac{1}{|\ln\varepsilon|}\Big)\\
=&\frac{2}{\pi\varepsilon^{3}}\left(1-\frac{\ln|x|}{\ln\varepsilon}+O\Big(\frac{1}{|\ln\varepsilon|}\Big)\right)\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left(\frac{\partial H_{\Omega}(x,0)}{\partial y_{j}}-\frac{\partial H_{\Omega}(0,0)}{\partial y_{j}}\frac{\ln|x|}{\ln\varepsilon}\right)\underbrace{\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}yy_{j}d\sigma_{y}}_{=\pi\varepsilon^{3}\delta_{j}^{i}}+O\Big(\frac{1}{|\ln\varepsilon|}\Big)\\
=&2\left(1-\frac{\ln|x|}{\ln\varepsilon}\right)\left(\nabla_{y}H_{\Omega}(x,0)-\nabla_{y}H_{\Omega}(0,0)\frac{\ln|x|}{\ln\varepsilon}\right)+O\Big(\frac{1}{|\ln\varepsilon|}\Big)\\
=&2\nabla_{y}H_{\Omega}(x,0)+O\left(\left|\frac{\ln|x|}{\ln\varepsilon}\right|\right)+O\Big(\frac{1}{|\ln\varepsilon|}\Big).\end{split}

Computation of
I_{4,\varepsilon}

As in the previous step let us consider the case
N\geq 3
firstly.

Case
N\geq 3

Recalling (
3.3
) and using (
A.4
) and (
A.5
) we have

\begin{split}I_{4,\varepsilon}=&2\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\frac{\partial G_{B^{c}_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}\frac{\partial\big(H_{B^{c}_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)\big)}{\partial\nu_{y}}d\sigma_{y}\\
=&2\frac{N-2}{\varepsilon}\underbrace{\left(H_{\Omega}(x,0)-H_{\Omega}(0,0)\frac{\varepsilon^{N-2}}{|x|^{N-2}}\right)}_{=O(1)}\underbrace{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}y\frac{\partial G_{B^{c}_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}d\sigma_{y}}_{=O\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{N-1}}{|x|^{N-1}}\right)\hbox{ by }\eqref{ap2}}\\
&+O(1)\underbrace{\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\partial G_{B^{c}_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}d\sigma_{y}}_{=O\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{N-2}}{|x|^{N-2}}\right)\hbox{ by }\eqref{ap1}}=O\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{N-2}}{|x|^{N-1}}\right).\end{split}

Case
N=2

Using (
3.3
) we have

(3.6)

\begin{split}I_{4,\varepsilon}=&2\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\frac{\partial G_{B^{c}_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}\frac{\partial\big(H_{B^{c}_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)\big)}{\partial\nu_{y}}d\sigma_{y}\\
=&2\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\frac{\partial G_{B^{c}_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}\left(\phi_{\varepsilon}(x,y)\cdot\nu_{y}+O\Big(\frac{1}{|\ln\varepsilon|}\Big)\right)d\sigma_{y}\\
=&\frac{1}{\pi\varepsilon}\left(1-\frac{\ln|x|}{\ln\varepsilon}+O\Big(\frac{1}{|\ln\varepsilon|}\Big)\right)\underbrace{\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\frac{\partial G_{B^{c}_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}d\sigma_{y}}_{=-\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\varepsilon\hbox{ by }\eqref{ap2}}\\
+&\underbrace{\frac{4}{\varepsilon}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\frac{\partial G_{B^{c}_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}\left(\nabla_{y}H_{\Omega}(x,0)\cdot y-\nabla_{y}H_{\Omega}(0,0)\cdot y\frac{\ln|x|}{\ln\varepsilon}\right)d\sigma_{y}}_{:=\widetilde{K}_{4,\varepsilon}}\\
&+O\Big(\frac{1}{|\ln\varepsilon|}\Big)\underbrace{\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{|y|}{\varepsilon}\left|\frac{\partial G_{B^{c}_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}\right|d\sigma_{y}}_{=O(1)\hbox{ by }\eqref{ap1}}.\end{split}

Also, by (
A.6
), we have
\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}yy_{j}\frac{\partial G_{B^{c}_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}d\sigma_{y}=-\varepsilon^{4}\frac{xx_{j}}{|x|^{4}}-\delta_{j}^{i}\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\right)

and then

(3.7)

\begin{split}\widetilde{K}_{4,\varepsilon}=&-2\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\right)\left(\nabla_{y}H_{\Omega}(x,0)-\nabla_{y}H_{\Omega}(0,0)\frac{\ln|x|}{\ln\varepsilon}\right)\\
&-4\varepsilon^{2}\frac{x}{|x|^{4}}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left(\frac{\partial H_{\Omega}(x,0)}{\partial y_{j}}-\frac{\partial H_{\Omega}(0,0)}{\partial y_{j}}\frac{\ln|x|}{\ln\varepsilon}\right)x_{j}\\
=&-2\left(\nabla_{y}H_{\Omega}(x,0)-\nabla_{y}H_{\Omega}(0,0)\frac{\ln|x|}{\ln\varepsilon}\right)+O\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\right).\end{split}

Hence from (
3.6
) and (
3.7
), we find

\begin{split}I_{4,\varepsilon}&=-\frac{1}{\pi}\left(1-\frac{\ln|x|}{\ln\varepsilon}\right)\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}-2\nabla_{y}H_{\Omega}(x,0)+O\left(\frac{1}{|x|\cdot|\ln\varepsilon|}\right).\end{split}

Collecting the estimates for
I_{1,\varepsilon},\cdots,I_{4,\varepsilon}
we have the expansion for
\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}
.
∎

3.3.
Estimate of
\nabla^{2}\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}

These estimates will be crucial to prove the uniqueness of the critical point of
\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}
close to
\partial B(0,\varepsilon)
.
The basic result of this section is the following.

Proposition 3.4
.

For any
i,j=1,\cdots,N
and for
x\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}
such that
dist(x,\partial\Omega)\geq C>0
, we have

(3.8)

\begin{split}&\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x)}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}=\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(x)}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}+\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{R}_{B_{\varepsilon}^{c}}(x)}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}+\\
&\begin{cases}O\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{N-2}}{dist(x,\partial B_{\varepsilon})|x|^{N-2}}+\frac{\varepsilon}{dist(x,\partial B_{\varepsilon})}+\frac{\varepsilon^{N-2}}{|x|^{N}}+\frac{\varepsilon^{N-2}|x|}{dist(x,\partial B_{\varepsilon})^{N}}+\frac{\varepsilon^{N-2}|x|^{2}}{dist(x,\partial B_{\varepsilon})^{N+1}}\right)&\mbox{for}~~N\geq 3,\\[8.53581pt]
\frac{1}{\pi}\left(1-\frac{\ln|x|}{\ln\varepsilon}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\left(\frac{x_{i}}{|x|^{2}}\right)+O\left(\frac{1}{|x|^{2}|\ln\varepsilon|}+\left|\frac{\ln|x|}{dist(x,\partial B_{\varepsilon})\ln\varepsilon}\right|\right)+\\
O\left(\frac{|x|}{dist(x,\partial B_{\varepsilon})^{2}|\ln\varepsilon|}+\frac{|x|^{2}}{dist(x,\partial B_{\varepsilon})^{3}|\ln\varepsilon|}\right)&\mbox{for}~~N=2.\end{cases}\end{split}

Proof.

Differentiating formula (
3.5
) for
i,j=1,\cdots,N
we have

\begin{split}&\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x)}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}=\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(x)}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}+\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{R}_{B_{\varepsilon}^{c}}(x)}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\\
&+2\underbrace{\int_{\partial\Omega}\nu_{i}(y)\left(\frac{\partial^{2}\big(H_{\Omega}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)\big)}{\partial x_{j}\partial\nu_{y}}\right)\left(\frac{\partial\big(H_{\Omega}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)\big)}{\partial\nu_{y}}\right)d\sigma_{y}}_{J_{1,\varepsilon}}\\
&+2\underbrace{\int_{\partial\Omega}\nu_{i}(y)\frac{\partial^{2}G_{\Omega}(x,y)}{\partial x_{j}\partial\nu_{y}}\frac{\partial\big(H_{\Omega}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)\big)}{\partial\nu_{y}}d\sigma_{y}}_{J_{2,\varepsilon}}+2\underbrace{\int_{\partial\Omega}\nu_{i}(y)\frac{\partial G_{\Omega}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}\frac{\partial^{2}\big(H_{\Omega}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)\big)}{\partial x_{j}\partial\nu_{y}}d\sigma_{y}}_{J_{3,\varepsilon}}\\
&-2\underbrace{\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{y_{i}}{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}\big(H_{B_{\varepsilon}^{c}}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)\big)}{\partial x_{j}\partial\nu_{y}}\right)\left(\frac{\partial\big(H_{B_{\varepsilon}^{c}}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)\big)}{\partial\nu_{y}}\right)d\sigma_{y}}_{J_{4,\varepsilon}}\\
&-2\underbrace{\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{y_{i}}{\varepsilon}\frac{\partial^{2}G_{B_{\varepsilon}^{c}}(x,y)}{\partial x_{j}\partial\nu_{y}}\frac{\partial\big(H_{B_{\varepsilon}^{c}}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)\big)}{\partial\nu_{y}}d\sigma_{y}}_{J_{5,\varepsilon}}-2\underbrace{\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{y_{i}}{\varepsilon}\frac{\partial G_{B_{\varepsilon}^{c}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}\frac{\partial^{2}\big(H_{B_{\varepsilon}^{c}}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)\big)}{\partial x_{j}\partial\nu_{y}}d\sigma_{y}}_{J_{6,\varepsilon}}.\end{split}

We have to estimate the integrals
J_{1,\varepsilon},\cdots,J_{6,\varepsilon}
. The computations are very similar to those of Proposition
3.3
.

Computation of
J_{1,\varepsilon}

By Lemma
2.1
and (
2.6
) we get

\begin{split}&J_{1,\varepsilon}=\begin{cases}O\Big(\frac{\varepsilon^{2N-4}}{dist(x,\partial B_{\varepsilon})|x|^{2N-4}}\Big)+O\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{dist(x,\partial B_{\varepsilon})}\right)&\mbox{for}~N\geq 3,\\[8.53581pt]
O\left(\frac{\ln^{2}|x|}{dist(x,\partial B_{\varepsilon})\ln^{2}\varepsilon}\right)&\mbox{for}~N=2.\end{cases}\end{split}

Computation of
J_{2,\varepsilon}

Here we use the assumption that
x
satisfies
dist(x,\partial\Omega)\geq C>0
. We need it to have

\frac{\partial^{2}G_{\Omega}(x,y)}{\partial x_{j}\partial\nu_{y}}=O(1)\,\mbox{ for}~~\,~y\in\partial\Omega.

Using Lemma
2.1
we immedialtely have

\begin{split}&\left|J_{2,\varepsilon}\right|\leq\int_{\partial\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial^{2}G_{\Omega}(x,y)}{\partial x_{j}\partial\nu_{y}}\right|\left|\frac{\partial\big(H_{\Omega}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)\big)}{\partial\nu_{y}}\right|d\sigma_{y}=\begin{cases}O\Big(\frac{\varepsilon^{N-2}}{|x|^{N-2}}\Big)+(\varepsilon)~&\mbox{for}~N\geq 3,\\[8.53581pt]
O\left(\left|\frac{\ln|x|}{\ln\varepsilon}\right|\right)~&\mbox{for}~N=2.\end{cases}\end{split}

Computation of
J_{3,\varepsilon}

Recalling that
\int_{\partial\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial G_{\Omega}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}\right|d\sigma_{y}=1
we have

\begin{split}J_{3,\varepsilon}=&\int_{\partial\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial G_{\Omega}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}\right|\left|\frac{\partial^{2}\big(H_{\Omega}(x,y)-H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)\big)}{\partial x_{j}\partial\nu_{y}}\right|d\sigma_{y}\\
=&\begin{cases}O\Big(\frac{\varepsilon^{N-2}}{dist(x,\partial B_{\varepsilon})|x|^{N-2}}\Big)+O\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{dist(x,\partial B_{\varepsilon})}\right)\,\,\,~&\mbox{for}~N\geq 3,\\[8.53581pt]
O\left(\left|\frac{\ln|x|}{dist(x,\partial B_{\varepsilon})\ln\varepsilon}\right|\right)~&\mbox{for}~N=2.\end{cases}\end{split}

Computation of
J_{4,\varepsilon}

We will show that

(3.9)

\begin{split}J_{4,\varepsilon}=\begin{cases}O\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{N-2}}{dist(x,\partial B_{\varepsilon})}\right)+O\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{2N-4}}{|x|^{N-1}}\right)~&\mbox{for}~N\geq 3,\\[8.53581pt]
\frac{\partial^{2}H_{\Omega}(x,0)}{\partial x_{j}\partial y_{i}}+O\left(\frac{1}{dist(x,\partial B_{\varepsilon})|\ln\varepsilon|}\right)+O\left(\big|\frac{\ln|x|}{\ln\varepsilon}\big|\right)~&\mbox{for}~N=2.\end{cases}\end{split}

By (
2.7
) we derive that

(3.10)

\begin{split}&\frac{\partial^{2}\big(H_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)-H_{B^{c}_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)\big)}{\partial x_{j}\partial\nu_{y}}\Big|_{y\in\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\\
=&\begin{cases}\frac{N-2}{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{\partial H(x,0)}{\partial x_{j}}-(2-N)H_{\Omega}(0,0)\frac{\varepsilon^{N-2}x_{j}}{|x|^{N}}\right)+O\left(\frac{1}{dist(x,\partial B_{\varepsilon})}\right)&\mbox{for}~N\geq 3,\\[8.53581pt]

\begin{split}J_{4,\varepsilon}=&\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{y_{i}}{\varepsilon}\left(H_{\Omega}(x,0)-H_{\Omega}(0,0)\frac{\varepsilon^{N-2}}{|x|^{N-2}}+O(\varepsilon)\right)\\

\begin{split}&\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(0)+o(1)=\frac{2\varepsilon^{N-2}}{N\omega_{N}|x_{\varepsilon}|^{2N-3}}\left[\frac{x_{\varepsilon}}{|x_{\varepsilon}|}+o(1)\right]\Rightarrow\\
&\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\varepsilon^{N-2}}{|x_{\varepsilon}|^{2N-3}}=\frac{N\omega_{N}}{2}|\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(0)|,\end{split}

which gives the claim.

Next we consider the case
N=2
. As for
N\geq 3
we get that, if
x_{\varepsilon}\to x_{0}\neq 0
by (
3.4
) we derive that (
4.2
) holds. So assume that
x_{\varepsilon}\to 0
. By (
3.4
) we have

(4.6)

\begin{split}\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(0)+o(1)&=\frac{x_{\varepsilon}}{\pi|x_{\varepsilon}|^{2}}\left(\frac{\ln|x_{\varepsilon}|}{\ln\varepsilon}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{|x_{\varepsilon}|^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}}+O\left(\frac{1}{|\ln\varepsilon|}\right)\right).\end{split}

Since
\frac{1}{|x_{\varepsilon}|}\to+\infty
and at least one among
\frac{(x_{\varepsilon})_{i}}{|x_{\varepsilon}|}\neq 0
, using that the two terms
\frac{\ln|x_{\varepsilon}|}{\ln\varepsilon}
,
\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{|x_{\varepsilon}|^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}}
have the same sign, we get that

(4.7)

\frac{\ln|x_{\varepsilon}|}{\ln\varepsilon}=o(1)\hbox{ and }\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{|x_{\varepsilon}|^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}}=o(1),

which implies also that

(4.8)

\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{|x_{\varepsilon}|^{3}}=o(1)\hbox{ and then }\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{|x_{\varepsilon}|\big(|x_{\varepsilon}|^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}\big)}=o(1),

(indeed, if by contradiction
\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{|x_{\varepsilon}|^{3}}\geq k>0
then
2-3\frac{\ln|x_{\varepsilon}|}{\ln\varepsilon}\leq\frac{\ln k}{\ln\varepsilon}
, a contradiction as
\varepsilon\to 0
.)
So that (
4.6
) becomes

(4.9)

\begin{split}\pi|\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(0)|(1+o(1))&=\frac{1}{|x_{\varepsilon}|}\frac{\ln|x_{\varepsilon}|}{\ln\varepsilon}\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{|\ln|x_{\varepsilon}||}\right)\right)\\
&=\frac{r_{\varepsilon}}{|x_{\varepsilon}|}\frac{\ln|x_{\varepsilon}|}{\ln r_{\varepsilon}}\left(1+o(1)\right)=\left[\frac{\ln\frac{|x_{\varepsilon}|}{r_{\varepsilon}}}{\frac{|x_{\varepsilon}|}{r_{\varepsilon}}\ln r_{\varepsilon}}+\frac{1}{\frac{|x_{\varepsilon}|}{r_{\varepsilon}}}\right]\big(1+o(1)\big).\end{split}

Since the both terms
\frac{\ln\frac{|x_{\varepsilon}|}{r_{\varepsilon}}}{\frac{|x_{\varepsilon}|}{r_{\varepsilon}}\ln r_{\varepsilon}}
and
\frac{|x_{\varepsilon}|}{r_{\varepsilon}}
are positive and
r_{\varepsilon}\to 0
, it cannot happen that
\frac{|x_{\varepsilon}|}{r_{\varepsilon}}\to 0
. Moreover, since
\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(0)\neq 0
it is not possible that
\frac{|x_{\varepsilon}|}{r_{\varepsilon}}\to+\infty.

Then
\frac{|x_{\varepsilon}|}{r_{\varepsilon}}\to A\in(0,+\infty)
and by (
4.9
) we have that
A=\frac{1}{\pi|\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(0)|}
which proves the claim.

∎

Remark 4.2
.

Let us point out that

(4.10)

r_{\varepsilon}\in\left(\frac{1}{|\ln\varepsilon|},\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\ln\varepsilon|}}\right).

In fact, taking

, then

g^{\prime}(r)=1-\frac{1}{r\ln\varepsilon}>0,~\mbox{for any}~r\in(0,\infty),

.

Proposition

implies that the critical points of

In the next lemma we introduce a function

Lemma 4.4
.

Let us consider the function
F:\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{R}
as

F(y)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\partial\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(0)}{\partial x_{j}}y_{j}-\frac{D_{N}}{4-2N}\frac{1}{|y|^{2N-4}}~&\mbox{if}~N\geq 3,\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{2}\frac{\partial\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(0)}{\partial x_{j}}y_{j}~-\frac{1}{\pi}\log|y|&\mbox{if}~N=2,\end{cases}

with

(4.11)

D_{N}=\frac{2}{N\omega_{N}}\quad\hbox{for }N\geq 2.

Then, the function
F
has a unique critical point
y_{0}
(see (

)) which is non-degenerate and satisfies

(4.12)

\det\big(Hess\big(F(y_{0})\big)\big)=(-1)^{N}\frac{|\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(0)|^{\frac{N(2N-2)}{2N-3}}}{D_{N}^{\frac{N}{2N-3}}}(3-2N)\neq 0.

Remark 4.5
.

The non-degeneracy of
y_{0}
, together with (

), implies that there exists
\bar{\delta}>0
such that
\overline{B(y_{0},\bar{\delta})}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
and
det\big(Hess\big(F(y)\big)\big)\neq 0
in
B(y_{0},\bar{\delta})
. If necessary, choosing a smaller

we can assume that

for every

. From now we fix

in the definition of
\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}
.

Proof.

By a straightforward computation we have that
y_{0}
is the unique critical point of
F(y)
. Next we have that, for
N\geq 2
, the Hessian matrix of
F(y)
computed at
y_{0}=(y_{1,0},..,y_{N,0})
is given by

Hess\big(F(y_{0})\big)=-D_{N}\frac{1}{|y_{0}|^{2N-2}}\underbrace{\left(\delta^{i}_{j}+(2-2N)\frac{y_{i,0}y_{j,0}}{|y_{0}|^{2}}\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq N}}_{A_{ij}}.

Note that
\lambda_{1}=3-2N
is the first eigenvalue of the matrix

with associated eigenvector
v_{1}=y_{0}
. Next, we observe that any vector of the space
X:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},x\bot y_{0}\}
is an eigenvector of the matrix

corresponding to the eigenvalue

, so that

. Hence we have that

\begin{split}\det Hess\big(F(y_{0})\big)=&det\left[-\frac{|\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(0)|^{\frac{2N-2}{2N-3}}}{D_{N}^{\frac{1}{2N-3}}}\left(\delta^{i}_{j}-(2N-2)\frac{y_{i,0}y_{j,0}}{|y_{0}|^{2}}\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\right]\\
=&(-1)^{N}\frac{|\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(0)|^{\frac{N(2N-2)}{2N-3}}}{D_{N}^{\frac{N}{2N-3}}}\displaystyle\Pi^{N}_{i=1}\lambda_{i}=(-1)^{N}\frac{|\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(0)|^{\frac{N(2N-2)}{2N-3}}}{D_{N}^{\frac{N}{2N-3}}}(3-2N)\neq 0,\end{split}

which proves (
4.12
).
∎

Finally, the

We end the proof showing that

Taking
Z=\textbf{P}^{T}(y)
we get that the system
\nabla\widehat{F}(y)=0
becomes

\lambda_{i}Z_{i}-D_{N}\frac{Z_{i}}{|Z|^{2N-2}}=0,~~\mbox{for}~N\geq 2\hbox{ and }i=1,\cdots,N.

Note that these zeros are critical point of the function

(5.8)

T(y)=\frac{1}{2}\sum^{N}_{j=1}\lambda_{j}y_{j}^{2}-\frac{D_{N}}{(4-2N)|y|^{2N-4}}\,\,\,~\mbox{for}~N\geq 3.

Next step is the computation of the determinant of the hessian matrix of
\widehat{F}
. We know

\begin{split}\det Hess\left(\widehat{F}\big(y^{(l)}\big)\right)=&\det\left(\textbf{P}^{T}Hess\Big(T\big(y^{(l)}\big)\Big)\textbf{P}\right)=\det\left(Hess\Big(T\big(y^{(l)}\big)\Big)\right)\\
=&\det\left(diag\big(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{N}\big)+\Big(-\frac{D_{N}}{|y^{(l)}|^{2N-2}}\delta_{ik}+(2N-2)\frac{D_{N}y^{(l)}_{i}y^{(l)}_{k}}{|y^{(l)}|^{2N}}\Big)_{1\leq i,k\leq N}\right)\\
=&\det\left(\underbrace{diag\big(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{l},\cdots,\lambda_{N}-\lambda_{l}\big)+(2N-2)\lambda_{l}\Big(\frac{y^{(l)}_{i}y^{(l)}_{k}}{|y^{(l)}|^{2}}\Big)_{1\leq i,k\leq N}}_{:=\bf{M_{0}}}\right).\end{split}

By the basic theory of linear algebra, we can find that the eigenvalues of

\bf{M_{0}}
are
\lambda_{s}-\lambda_{l}
for
s=1,\cdots,l-1,l+1,\cdots,N
and
(2N-2)\lambda_{l}
.
Hence we have

\det Hess\left(\widehat{F}\big(y^{(l)}\big)\right)=(2N-2)\lambda_{l}\prod_{s=1\atop s\neq l}^{N}(\lambda_{s}-\lambda_{l}).

∎

Assume that
\lambda_{l}
is a
simple
eigenvalue. Then, following the notations of Remark
5.5
,
analogously to the previous section we define

\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon,\pm}^{(l)}=\begin{cases}B\left(y_{\pm}^{(l)}\varepsilon^{\frac{N-2}{2N-2}},\delta^{(l)}\varepsilon^{\frac{N-2}{2N-2}}\right)&\mbox{if}\ N\geq 3,\\[5.69054pt]
B(y_{\pm}^{(l)}\widehat{r}_{\varepsilon},\delta^{(l)}\widehat{r}_{\varepsilon})&\mbox{if}\ N=2,\end{cases}

and

\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}}=\underset{l}{\cup}\ \mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon,\pm}^{(l)}.

Proposition
5.1
implies that, under the assumption that all the eigenvalues of
\left(\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(0)}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\right)_{i,j=1,..N}
are positive, the critical points
x_{\varepsilon}
verify either

x_{\varepsilon}\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}\setminus\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}}
and then
x_{\varepsilon}
converge to a critical point of
\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}

or

x_{\varepsilon}\to 0
and then
x_{\varepsilon}\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.8
.

Recall that we assume that
P=0\in\Omega
. As in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, if

is a critical point of
\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}

different from

we necessarily have that

. Following the notation of Remark
5.5
, denote by
\bar{y}_{+}^{(l)}
the critical point of
\widehat{F}
associated to

and let us show the existence of

in

which by (

) verifies (

.

Let us introduce the function

Furthermore, using Corollary

Since

is a non-degenerate critical point of

then

in

.

Finally, (
1.10

) and (

)–(

) in Theorem
1.1
.

∎

Remark 5.6

(What about multiple eigenvalues of
Hess\big(\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(0)\big)
?)
.

In this case we are not able to give a complete description of the critical points of

\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}
.
Suppose that we have a multiple eigenvalue satisfying

\lambda_{j}=\lambda_{j+1}=\cdots=\lambda_{j+k},\quad j,k\geq 1.

In this case, the function
T(y)
defined in (
5.8
) admits a manifold of critical points given by

S^{k}=\left\{x_{j}^{2}+\cdots+x_{j+k}^{2}=\left(\frac{D_{N}}{\lambda_{j}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2N-2}}\right\}.

By (
5.6
) we have that the Hessian matrix is non degenerate in the directions different from
x_{j},\cdots,x_{j+k}
and so it is a
non-degenerate
manifold of critical points for
N
in the sense of Morse-Bott theory. However, even in this explicit case, it seems hard to get existence results of critical points for
T(y)
under non radial small perturbations. Of course no possible information can be deduced about the non-degeneracy.

Without additional assumptions, as pointed out in the introduction, it is even possible to have infinitely many solutions (the radial case). For these reasons the case of multiple eigenvalues is unclear.

Before the close of this section, we give a partial result when
\Omega
is a symmetric domain.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be convex and symmetric with respect
x_{1},\cdots,x_{N}
for
N\geq 2
.
Then we have that
\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x)

has at least
2N
critical points which are located on the coordinate axis, i.e.

x_{i,\varepsilon}^{\pm}=\Big(\underbrace{0,\cdots,0}_{i-1},\pm\big(\frac{2}{N\omega_{N}\lambda_{i}}+o(1)\big)^{\frac{1}{N-2}}\varepsilon^{\frac{N-2}{2N-2}},\underbrace{0,\cdots,0}_{N-i}\Big)~~~~\mbox{for}~~~~i=1,\cdots,N.

Moreover we have
\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x_{i,\varepsilon}^{\pm})}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}\neq 0
for
i=1,\cdots,N
.

Proof.

We prove the claim by constructing
2N
zeros for
\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x)
.
For the case
N\geq 3
, as in the previous theorem we study the equation

0=\big(\lambda_{i}+o(1)\big)Z_{i}-\Big(\frac{2}{N\omega_{N}}+o(1)\Big)\frac{Z_{i}}{|Z|^{2N-2}}\varepsilon^{N-2},~~\mbox{for}~N\geq 3.

Here we introduce the points

Y_{1,\varepsilon}=\big((1-a)\alpha_{1}\varepsilon^{\frac{N-2}{2N-2}},0,\cdots,0\big)~\mbox{and}~Y_{2,\varepsilon}=\big((1+a)\alpha_{1}\varepsilon^{\frac{N-2}{2N-2}},0,\cdots,0\big)

for any
a\in(0,1)
and
\alpha_{1}=\left(\frac{D_{N}}{\lambda_{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2N-2}}
.
We have that
\Omega_{\varepsilon}=\Omega\setminus B(0,\varepsilon)
is symmetric and then

\frac{\partial\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(Y_{1,\varepsilon})}{\partial y_{i}}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(Y_{2,\varepsilon})}{\partial y_{i}}=0\,\,~\mbox{for any}~i=2,\cdots,N.

Also we have

\begin{split}\frac{\partial\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(Y_{1,\varepsilon})}{\partial y_{1}}=&\textbf{P}^{T}\frac{\partial\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(Y_{1,\varepsilon})}{\partial x_{1}}=\big(\lambda_{1}+o(1)\big)Y_{1,\varepsilon}-\big(\frac{2}{N\omega_{N}}+o(1)\big)\frac{Y_{1,\varepsilon}}{|Y_{1,\varepsilon}|^{2N-2}}\varepsilon^{N-2}\\
=&(1-a)\alpha_{1}\lambda_{1}\varepsilon^{\frac{N-2}{2N-2}}\left[1-\frac{1}{(1-a)^{2N-2}}+o(1)\right]>0,\end{split}

and similarly,

\frac{\partial\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(Y_{2,\varepsilon})}{\partial y_{1}}=(1+a)\alpha_{1}\lambda_{1}\varepsilon^{\frac{N-2}{2N-2}}\left[1-\frac{1}{(1+a)^{2N-2}}+o(1)\right]<0.

This implies that there exists
Z_{1,\varepsilon}=\big(b\varepsilon^{\frac{N-2}{2N-2}},0,\cdots,0\big)
with
b\in\big((1-a)\alpha_{1},(1+a)\alpha_{1}\big)
satisfying
\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(Z_{1,\varepsilon})=0
.
Taking
a
small, we can write
b=\alpha_{1}+o(1)
.
Hence there exists
Z_{1,\varepsilon}=\Big(\big(\alpha_{1}+o(1)\big)\varepsilon^{\frac{N-2}{2N-2}},0,\cdots,0\Big)
satisfying
\nabla\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(Z_{1,\varepsilon})=0
.

The same computation holds if we replace

Y_{1,\varepsilon},Y_{2,\varepsilon}
by
-Y_{1,\varepsilon},-Y_{2,\varepsilon}
getting the existence of a
second
critical point
-Z_{1,\varepsilon}
.
Since
Hess\big(\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(0)\big)
has
N
positive eigenvalues

0<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{N}
, then repeating the argument above for any positive eigenvalue,
we have that
\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x)

has at least
2N
critical points
x_{i,\varepsilon}^{\pm}(i=1,\cdots,N)
in
B(0,r)\backslash B(0,\varepsilon)
, where

B(0,r)\backslash\{0\}\subset\Omega
does not contain any critical point of
\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(x)
.
Moreover we can write

x_{i,\varepsilon}^{\pm}=\pm Z_{i,\varepsilon}=\Big(\underbrace{0,\cdots,0}_{i-1},\pm\big(\frac{2}{N\omega_{N}\lambda_{i}}+o(1)\big)^{\frac{1}{N-2}}\varepsilon^{\frac{N-2}{2N-2}},\underbrace{0,\cdots,0}_{N-i}\Big),~~~~\mbox{for}~~~~i=1,\cdots,N.

Also using (
3.13
) for
N\geq 3
, we compute

\begin{split}\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x_{i,\varepsilon}^{\pm})}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}=&\lambda_{i}+\left(\frac{2}{N\omega_{N}}+o(1)\right)\Big(2N-3\Big)\frac{1}{|x_{i,\varepsilon}^{\pm}|^{2N-2}}\varepsilon^{N-2}+o\big(1\big)\\
=&\Big(2N-2\Big)\lambda_{i}+o\big(1\big)\neq 0,~\,~\mbox{for}~\,~i=1,\cdots,N.\end{split}

For the case
N=2
, as in the previous theorem we study the equation

0=\big(\lambda_{i}+o(1)\big)Z_{i}-\Big(\frac{1}{\pi}+o(1)\Big)\frac{Z_{i}\ln|Z|}{|Z|^{2}\ln\varepsilon}.

Then similar the idea in proving the case
N\geq 3
, we can write

x_{1,\varepsilon}^{\pm}=\Big(\big(1+o(1)\big)\widehat{r}_{\varepsilon,1},0\Big)~~~~\mbox{and}~~~~x_{2,\varepsilon}^{\pm}=\Big(0,\big(1+o(1)\big)\widehat{r}_{\varepsilon,2}\Big).

Finally using (
3.13
) for
N=2
, we compute

\begin{split}\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x_{i,\varepsilon}^{\pm})}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}=\lambda_{i}+\frac{1}{\pi|x_{i,\varepsilon}^{\pm}|^{2N-2}}\frac{\ln\widehat{r}_{\varepsilon,i}}{\ln\varepsilon}+o\big(1\big)=2\lambda_{i}+o\big(1\big)\neq 0,~\,~\mbox{for}~\,~i=1,2.\end{split}

∎

Remark 5.8
.

Here we point out that the positive eigenvalues may be multiple in Theorem
5.7
.

6.
Examples on which the conditions Theorem
1.8
hold

In Theorem
1.8
we proved that if all positive eigenvalues of the Hessian matrix of
\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}
are simple, then we can give the precise number and non-degeneracy of the critical points of Robin function
\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x)
.
In this section, we exhibit some domains on which this assumption is verified.

We recall that the regular part of Green’s function in
B_{1}=B_{1}(0)
is

H_{B_{1}}(x,y)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\frac{1}{|y|\cdot\big|x-\frac{y}{|y|^{2}}\big|}&~~\mbox{for}~~y\in B_{1}(0)~~\mbox{and}~~N=2,\\
\frac{1}{N(N-2)\omega_{N}}\frac{1}{|y|^{N-2}\cdot\big|x-\frac{y}{|y|^{2}}\big|^{N-2}}&~~\mbox{for}~~y\in B_{1}(0)~~\mbox{and}~~N\geq 3.\end{cases}

Then we have following result,

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
N\geq 2
and

\Omega_{\delta}=\Big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},~\displaystyle\sum^{N}_{i=1}x_{i}^{2}\Big(1+\alpha_{i}\delta\Big)^{2}<1~~\mbox{with}~~\delta>0\hbox{ and }0<\alpha_{1}\leq\alpha_{2}\leq\cdots\leq\alpha_{N}\Big\}.

Then the Robin function
\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\delta}}
has a unique critical point
P=0
and is even with respect to
x_{1},\cdots,x_{N}
.

Moreover if
\alpha_{1}\leq\alpha_{2}\leq\cdots<\alpha_{l_{0}+1}=\alpha_{l_{0}+2}=\cdots=\alpha_{l_{0}+k}<\alpha_{l_{0}+k+1}\leq\cdots\leq\alpha_{N}
, then

\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\delta}}(x)
is radial function with respect to
x_{l_{0}+1}\cdots x_{l_{0}+k}
, i.e.

\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\delta}}(x)=\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\delta}}\Big(x_{1},\cdots,x_{l_{0}},\sqrt{x^{2}_{l_{0}+1}+\cdots+x^{2}_{l_{0}+k}},x_{l_{0}+k+1},\cdots,x_{N}\Big),

and we have that
\lambda_{l_{0}+1}=\lambda_{l_{0}+2}=\cdots=\lambda_{l_{0}+k}
for any
\delta>0
.

Moreover if there exists some
k
such that
\alpha_{k}\neq\alpha_{j}
for any
j\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
with
j\neq k
,
then the
k
-th eigenvalue of
Hess\big(\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\delta}}(0)\big)
is simple for
\delta>0
small.

Finally if
\alpha_{i}\neq\alpha_{j}
for any
i,j\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
with
i\neq j
, then all the eigenvalues of
Hess\big(\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\delta}}(0)\big)
are simple.

Proof.

Firstly, suppose that
A
is a reflection or a rotation such that
A\Omega=\Omega
. We have

(i)
H(y,x)
and
H(Ay,Ax)
are harmonic in
y
.

(ii) For any
y\in\partial\Omega
and
x\in\Omega
it holds
H(y,x)=H(Ay,Ax)
.

Hence we have
H(y,x)=H(Ay,Ax)
for any
x,y\in\Omega
, which gives

\mathcal{R}(x)=\mathcal{R}(Ax).

Now since
\Omega_{\delta}
is symmetric with respect to
x_{1},\cdots,x_{N}
, then
the Robin function
\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\delta}}
is even function with respect to
x_{1},\cdots,x_{N}
. Also from the fact that
\Omega_{\delta}
is convex and
[
6
,
10
]
, we have that
\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\delta}}
has a unique critical point
P=0
.
Moreover by
[
17
]

Hess\big(\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\delta}}(0)\big)
is a diagonal matrix with
i
-th eigenvalue
\lambda_{i}=\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\delta}}(0)}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}
for
i=1,\cdots,N
.
If
\alpha_{1}\leq\alpha_{2}\cdots<\alpha_{l_{0}+1}=\alpha_{l_{0}+2}=\cdots=\alpha_{l_{0}+k}<\alpha_{l_{0}+k+1}\leq\cdots\leq\alpha_{N}
, then
\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\delta}}(x)
is a radial function with respect to
x_{l_{0}+1}\cdots x_{l_{0}+k}
and
\lambda_{l_{0}+1}=\lambda_{l_{0}+2}=\cdots=\lambda_{l_{0}+k}
for any
\delta>0
.

Set
\alpha_{1}\leq\alpha_{2}\cdots\leq\alpha_{N}
and consider the general case for
\delta>0
small.
Let

g_{\delta}(x,y)=H_{\delta}(x,y)-{H}_{B(0,1)}(x,y)
, then we have

(6.1)

\begin{cases}\Delta_{x}g_{\delta}(x,y)=0,~~&\mbox{in}~~\Omega_{\delta},\\[2.84526pt]
g_{\delta}(x,y)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{N(N-2)\omega_{N}}\Big(\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N-2}}-\frac{1}{|y|^{N-2}\cdot\big|x-\frac{y}{|y|^{2}}\big|^{N-2}}\Big)&\mbox{for}~~{N\geq 3},\\[2.84526pt]
\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\frac{1}{|x-y|}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\frac{1}{|y|\cdot\big|x-\frac{y}{|y|^{2}}\big|}&\mbox{for}~~{N=2},\end{cases}~~&\mbox{on}~~\partial\Omega_{\delta}.\end{cases}

Next, on
\partial\Omega_{\delta}
, we have

(6.2)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N-2}}&-\frac{1}{|y|^{N-2}\cdot\big|x-\frac{y}{|y|^{2}}\big|^{N-2}}=\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N-2}}\Big(1-\frac{|x-y|^{N-2}}{|y|^{N-2}\cdot\big|x-\frac{y}{|y|^{2}}\big|^{N-2}}\Big)\\
=&\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N-2}}\Big(1-\frac{1}{\big(1-\alpha(x,y)\big)^{\frac{N-2}{2}}}\Big)=\frac{(N-2)\alpha(x,y)}{2|x-y|^{N-2}}+O\big(\alpha^{2}(x,y)\big),~~\mbox{for}~~N\geq 3,\end{split}

and

(6.3)

\begin{split}\ln\frac{1}{|x-y|}&-\ln\frac{1}{|y|\cdot\big|x-\frac{y}{|y|^{2}}\big|}=\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{|y|^{2}\cdot\big|x-\frac{y}{|y|^{2}}\big|^{2}}{|x-y|^{2}}\\
=&\frac{1}{2}\ln\big(1-\alpha(x,y)\big)=-\frac{1}{2}\alpha(x,y)+O\big(\alpha(x,y)^{2}\big),\end{split}

where

\alpha(x,y):=\frac{|x-y|^{2}-|y|^{2}\cdot\big|x-\frac{y}{|y|^{2}}\big|^{2}}{|x-y|^{2}}.

Also for
x\in\partial\Omega_{\delta}
, it holds

(6.4)

\begin{split}|y|^{2}\cdot\big|x-\frac{y}{|y|^{2}}\big|^{2}=&|y|^{2}\cdot|x|^{2}-2\langle x,y\rangle+1\\
=&-2\delta(|y|^{2}-1)\sum^{N}_{i=1}\alpha_{i}x^{2}_{i}+|x-y|^{2}+O\big(\delta^{2}\big).\end{split}

Hence for
x\in\partial\Omega_{\delta}
, from (
6.2
), (
6.3
) and (

(6.5)

\begin{split}g_{\delta}(x,y)=&-\frac{\delta(|y|^{2}-1)}{N\omega_{N}|x-y|^{N}}\sum^{N}_{i=1}\alpha_{i}x_{i}^{2}+O\big(\delta^{2}\big),~~\mbox{for}~~N\geq 2.\end{split}

Now using (
6.1
) and (

\begin{split}H_{\delta}(x,y)={H}_{B(0,1)}(x,y)-\frac{\delta(|y|^{2}-1)}{N\omega_{N}}v_{\delta}(x,y)+O\big(\delta^{2}\big),~~\mbox{for}~~N\geq 2,\end{split}

where

is the solution of

(6.6)

\begin{cases}\Delta_{x}v_{\delta}(x,y)=0~~&\mbox{in}~~\Omega_{\delta},\\[2.84526pt]

Then it follows

(6.7)

\mathcal{R}_{\delta}(x)=\mathcal{R}(x)-\frac{\delta(|x|^{2}-1)}{N\omega_{N}}v_{\delta}(x,x)+O\big(\delta^{2}\big),~~\mbox{for}~~N\geq 2.

Also for

and

\begin{split}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N}}=&\Big(\frac{1}{|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}-2\langle x,y\rangle}\Big)^{\frac{N}{2}}\\

=&\frac{1}{|x|^{N}}\Big(1+\frac{N\langle x,y\rangle}{|x|^{2}}-\frac{N}{2}\frac{|y|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}+\frac{N(N+2)}{2}\frac{\langle x,y\rangle^{2}}{|x|^{4}}+O\big(|y|^{3}\big)\Big).\end{split}

From (

) and (

), we get

v_{\delta}(x,y)=v^{(1)}_{\delta}(x,y)+Nv^{(2)}_{\delta}(x,y)-\frac{N}{2}v^{(3)}_{\delta}(x,y)+\frac{N(N+2)}{2}v^{(4)}_{\delta}(x,y)+v^{(5)}_{\delta}(x,y),

where

\begin{cases}\Delta_{x}v^{(1)}_{\delta}(x,y)=0&~~\mbox{in}~~\Omega_{\delta},\\[5.69054pt]
v^{(1)}_{\delta}(x,y)=\frac{1}{|x|^{N}}\displaystyle\sum^{N}_{i=1}\alpha_{i}x_{i}^{2}&~~\mbox{on}~~\partial\Omega_{\delta},\end{cases}~~~~~\begin{cases}\Delta_{x}v^{(2)}_{\delta}(x,y)=0&~~\mbox{in}~~\Omega_{\delta},\\[5.69054pt]

\begin{cases}\Delta_{x}v^{(3)}_{\delta}(x,y)=0&~~\mbox{in}~~\Omega_{\delta},\\[5.69054pt]
v^{(3)}_{\delta}(x,y)=\frac{|y|^{2}}{|x|^{N+2}}\displaystyle\sum^{N}_{i=1}\alpha_{i}x_{i}^{2}&~~\mbox{on}~~\partial\Omega_{\delta},\end{cases}~~~~~\begin{cases}\Delta_{x}v^{(4)}_{\delta}(x,y)=0&~~\mbox{in}~~\Omega_{\delta},\\[5.69054pt]

and

\begin{cases}\Delta_{x}v^{(5)}_{\delta}(x,y)=0&~~\mbox{in}~~\Omega_{\delta},\\[5.69054pt]

We know that as

,
v_{\delta}^{(1)}(x,y)\rightarrow v^{(1)}(x,y)
, where

(6.9)

\Delta v^{(1)}(x,y)=0~~\mbox{in}~~B_{1}(0),~~~\,v^{(1)}(x,y)=\displaystyle\sum^{N}_{i=1}\alpha_{i}x_{i}^{2}~~\mbox{on}~~\partial B_{1}(0).

Solving (
6.9
), we can get

v^{(1)}(x,y)=-\frac{(N-1)}{2}\big(|x|^{2}-1\big)+\displaystyle\sum^{N}_{i=1}\alpha_{i}x_{i}^{2}.

Then it holds

\begin{split}(|x|^{2}-1)v^{(1)}(x,x)=&-\frac{(N-1)}{2}\big(|x|^{2}-1\big)^{2}+\big(|x|^{2}-1\big)\displaystyle\sum^{N}_{i=1}\alpha_{i}x_{i}^{2},\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\nabla^{2}&\Big((|x|^{2}-1)v^{(1)}(x,x)\Big)\Big|_{x=0}=\nabla^{2}\Big(\big(N-1\big)|x|^{2}-\displaystyle\sum^{N}_{i=1}\alpha_{i}x_{i}^{2}\Big)\Big|_{x=0}\\
=&2(N-1)\textbf{E}_{N}-2~\mbox{diag}\big(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdots,\alpha_{N}\big),\end{split}

where
\textbf{E}_{N}
is the unit matrix. In the same way we have that, for
\delta\to 0
,

v_{\delta}^{(i)}(x,y)\rightarrow v^{(i)}(x,y)
, for
i=2,3,4
where

\begin{split}v^{(2)}(x,y)=&\langle x,y\rangle\displaystyle\sum^{N}_{i=1}\alpha_{i}x_{i}^{2}-\frac{N(N-1)}{2(N+2)}\big(|x|^{2}-1\big)\langle x,y\rangle-\frac{2(|x|^{2}-1)}{N+2}\sum^{N}_{i=1}\alpha_{i}x_{i}y_{i},\end{split}

v^{(3)}(x,y)=|y|^{2}v^{(1)}(x,y)=|y|^{2}\left(-\frac{(N-1)}{2}\big(|x|^{2}-1\big)+\displaystyle\sum^{N}_{i=1}\alpha_{i}x_{i}^{2}\right).

and

\begin{split}{v}^{(4)}(x,y)=&-2|y|^{2}\Big(\displaystyle\sum^{N}_{i=1}\alpha_{i}g_{i}(x)\Big)-\sum^{N}_{i=1}\Big(N(N-1)+8\alpha_{i}\Big)g_{i}(x)y_{i}^{2}\\
&-\sum^{N}_{i=1}\sum_{j\neq i}\Big(N(N-1)+8\alpha_{i}\Big)f_{ij}(x)y_{i}y_{j}+\Big(\displaystyle\sum^{N}_{i=1}\alpha_{i}x_{i}^{2}\Big)\langle x,y\rangle^{2},\end{split}

with

f_{ij}(x)=\frac{x_{i}x_{j}(|x|^{2}-1)}{12}~~\mbox{and}~~g_{i}(x)=\frac{(|x|^{2}-1)x_{i}^{2}}{2(N+4)}-\frac{(|x|^{4}-1)}{4(N+2)(N+4)}+\frac{(|x|^{2}-1)}{2N(N+4)}.

For future aims it will be useful to remark that

\begin{split}v^{(4)}(x,x)=&\frac{N(N-1)|x|^{2}+4\displaystyle\sum^{N}_{i=1}\alpha_{i}x_{i}^{2}}{2N(N+2)}+O\big(|x|^{4}\big).\end{split}

Finally since
v_{\delta}^{(5)}(x,y)=O\big(|y|^{3}\big)
as
\delta\to 0
we get

\nabla^{2}\Big((|x|^{2}-1)v^{(5)}_{\delta}(x,x)\Big)\Big|_{x=0}\to\textbf{O}_{N},

where
\textbf{O}_{N}
is the zero matrix in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Hence by the explicit form of
v_{\delta}^{(1)},..,v_{\delta}^{(4)}
, a straightforward (and tedious) computation gives that

(6.10)

\begin{split}\nabla^{2}&\Big((|x|^{2}-1)v_{\delta}(x,x)\Big)\Big|_{x=0}\\
&\to-\frac{\big(N-1\big)\big(N^{2}-2N-4\big)}{N+2}\textbf{E}_{N}-\frac{8\big(N+1\big)}{N+2}~\mbox{diag}\big(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{N}\big).\end{split}

Observe that

(6.11)

\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{R}_{B_{1}}(x)}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}=\frac{2}{N\omega_{N}}\delta^{i}_{j},~~\mbox{for}~~1\leq i,j\leq N.

Finally, let
\lambda_{i}
for
i=1,\cdots,N
be the eigenvalues of
Hess\big(\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\delta}}(0)\big)
; for
N\geq 2
, from (
6.7
), (
6.10
) and (
6.11
), we find

\lambda_{i}=\frac{2}{N\omega_{N}}+\frac{(N-1)(N^{2}-2N-4)+8(N+1)\alpha_{i}}{N(N+2)\omega_{N}}\delta+o\big(\delta\big),~~\mbox{for}~~i=1,\cdots,N.

Hence we deduce that if there exists some
k
such that
\alpha_{k}\neq\alpha_{j}
for any
j\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
with
j\neq k
,
then the corresponding
k
-th eigenvalue of
Hess\big(\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\delta}}(0)\big)
is simple for
\delta>0
small.

Furthermore, if
\alpha_{i}\neq\alpha_{j}
for any
i,j\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
with
i\neq j
, then all the eigenvalues of
Hess\big(\mathcal{R}_{\Omega_{\delta}}(0)\big)
are simple.
∎

Appendix A
Some useful computations on the Green function in the exterior of the ball

We recall that Green function
G_{B_{\varepsilon}^{c}}(x,y)
of
\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{\varepsilon}
is given by (see
[
2
]
for example)

G_{B_{\varepsilon}^{c}}(x,y)=\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\ln\big|{x-y}\big|-\ln\sqrt{\frac{|x|^{2}|y|^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}+\varepsilon^{2}-2x\cdot y}\right)&~\mbox{if}~N=2,\\[5.69054pt]
C_{N}\left(\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N-2}}-\frac{\varepsilon^{N-2}}{\big||x|y-\varepsilon^{2}\frac{x}{|x|}\big|^{N-2}}\right)&~\mbox{if}~N\geq 3,\end{cases}

and, for
i=1,\cdots,N
,

(A.1)

\frac{\partial G_{B_{\varepsilon}^{c}}(x,y)}{\partial y_{i}}=\frac{1}{N\omega_{N}}\left(\frac{x_{i}-y_{i}}{|x-y|^{N}}+\varepsilon^{N-2}\frac{|x|^{2}y_{i}-\varepsilon^{2}x_{i}}{\big(|x|^{2}|y|^{2}-2\varepsilon^{2}x\cdot y+\varepsilon^{4}\big)^{\frac{N}{2}}}\right).

The
Robin function
of
\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{\varepsilon}
is given by (see
[
2
]
)

(A.2)

\mathcal{R}_{B_{\varepsilon}^{c}}(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\frac{\varepsilon}{|x|^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}}&~\mbox{if}~N=2,\\[5.69054pt]
C_{N}\frac{\varepsilon^{N-2}}{\big(|x|^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}\big)^{N-2}}&~\mbox{if}~N\geq 3.\end{cases}

It will be also useful the well-known representation formula for harmonic function in the ball
B_{\varepsilon}
: if
u
is harmonic in
B_{\varepsilon}
we have that

(A.3)

u(x)=-\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\partial G_{B_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}u(y)ds_{y}=\frac{1}{N\omega_{N}}\frac{\varepsilon^{2}-|x|^{2}}{\varepsilon}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{u(y)}{|x-y|^{N}}ds_{y}.

In next lemma we prove some identities which use in our computations.

Lemma A.1
.

We have that, for any
x\in B^{c}_{\varepsilon}
and
N\geq 2
the following equalities hold:

(A.4)

\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\partial G_{B^{c}_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}d\sigma_{y}=-\frac{\varepsilon^{N-2}}{|x|^{N-2}},

(A.5)

\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}y_{j}\frac{\partial G_{B^{c}_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}d\sigma_{y}=-\frac{x_{j}}{|x|^{N}}\varepsilon^{N}

and

(A.6)

\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}y_{i}y_{j}\frac{\partial G_{B^{c}_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}d\sigma_{y}=-\frac{\varepsilon^{N}}{|x|^{N}}\left[\varepsilon^{2}\frac{x_{i}x_{j}}{|x|^{2}}+\frac{\delta^{i}_{j}}{N}\big(|x|^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}\big)\right].

Proof.

In order to prove (
A.4
) and (
A.5
) we will use the well known facts that, for
x\in B_{\varepsilon}
,

(A.7)

\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\partial G_{B_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}d\sigma_{y}=-1\quad\hbox{and }\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}y_{j}\frac{\partial G_{B_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}d\sigma_{y}=-x_{j},

(these can be deduced by (
A.3
)).
Let us prove (
A.4
) for
N\geq 3
. We have that, for for
x\in B_{\varepsilon}^{c}
,

\begin{split}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}&\frac{\partial G_{B^{c}_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}d\sigma_{y}=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}-|x|^{2}}{N\omega_{N}\varepsilon}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N}}d\sigma_{y}=(\hbox{setting $x=\varepsilon^{2}\frac{t}{|t|^{2}}$, and then $|t|\leq\varepsilon$})\\
&\varepsilon\frac{|t|^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}}{N\omega_{N}|t|^{2}}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{|t|^{2N}}{|\varepsilon^{2}t-|t|^{2}y|^{N}}d\sigma_{y}=\frac{(|t|^{2}-\varepsilon^{2})|t|^{N-2}}{N\omega_{N}\varepsilon^{N-1}}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{|t-y|^{N}}d\sigma_{y}\\
&\hbox{ (using \eqref{ap3} for }t\in B_{\varepsilon})=\frac{|t|^{N-2}}{\varepsilon^{N-2}}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\partial G_{B_{\varepsilon}}(t,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}=-\frac{\varepsilon^{N-2}}{|x|^{N-2}}.\end{split}

In the same way we get (
A.5
),

\begin{split}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}&y_{j}\frac{\partial G_{B^{c}_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}d\sigma_{y}=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}-|x|^{2}}{N\omega_{N}\varepsilon}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{y_{j}}{|x-y|^{N}}d\sigma_{y}=(\hbox{setting $x=\varepsilon^{2}\frac{t}{|t|^{2}}$, and then $|t|\leq\varepsilon$})\\
&=\varepsilon\frac{|t|^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}}{N\omega_{N}|t|^{2}}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{|t|^{2N}y_{j}}{|\varepsilon^{2}t-|t|^{2}y|^{N}}d\sigma_{y}=\frac{(|t|^{2}-\varepsilon^{2})|t|^{N-2}}{N\omega_{N}\varepsilon^{N-1}}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{y_{j}}{|t-y|^{N}}d\sigma_{y}\\
&\hbox{ (using \eqref{ap3} for }t\in B_{\varepsilon})=\frac{|t|^{N-2}}{\varepsilon^{N-2}}t_{j}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\partial G_{B_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}=-\frac{x_{j}}{|x|^{N}}\varepsilon^{N}.\end{split}

Let us prove (
A.6
). We have that the function

a(x)=x_{i}x_{j}+\frac{\delta^{i}_{j}}{N}(\varepsilon^{2}-|x|^{2})

is harmonic and
a\big|_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}=x_{i}x_{j}
. So

(A.8)

\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}y_{j}y_{j}\frac{\partial G_{B_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}=-\left(x_{i}x_{j}+\frac{\delta^{i}_{j}}{N}(\varepsilon^{2}-|x|^{2})\right)

and arguing as before we that

\begin{split}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}&y_{i}y_{j}\frac{\partial G_{B^{c}_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}d\sigma_{y}=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}-|x|^{2}}{N\omega_{N}\varepsilon}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{y_{i}y_{j}}{|x-y|^{N}}d\sigma_{y}=(\hbox{setting $x=\varepsilon^{2}\frac{t}{|t|^{2}}$, and then $|t|\leq\varepsilon$})\\
&=\varepsilon\frac{|t|^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}}{N\omega_{N}|t|^{2}}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{|t|^{2N}y_{i}y_{j}}{|\varepsilon^{2}t-|t|^{2}y|^{N}}d\sigma_{y}=\frac{(|t|^{2}-\varepsilon^{2})|t|^{N-2}}{N\omega_{N}\varepsilon^{N-1}}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{y_{i}y_{j}}{|t-y|^{N}}d\sigma_{y}\\
&\hbox{ (using \eqref{ap14} for }t\in B_{\varepsilon})=-\frac{|t|^{N-2}}{\varepsilon^{N-2}}\left(t_{i}t_{j}+\frac{\delta^{i}_{j}}{N}(\varepsilon^{2}-|t|^{2})\right)\\
&=-\frac{\varepsilon^{N-2}}{|x|^{N-2}}\left[\varepsilon^{4}\frac{x_{i}x_{j}}{|x|^{4}}+\frac{\delta^{i}_{j}}{N}\varepsilon^{2}\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\right)\right]=-\frac{\varepsilon^{N}}{|x|^{N}}\left[\varepsilon^{2}\frac{x_{i}x_{j}}{|x|^{2}}+\frac{\delta^{i}_{j}}{N}\big(|x|^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}\big)\right],\end{split}

which proves the claim.
∎

Next lemma concerns some identities on the Robin function.

Lemma A.2
.

We have that, for any
x\in B^{c}_{\varepsilon}
and
N\geq 2
the following identities hold:

(A.9)

\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{\partial G_{B^{c}_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}\right)^{2}d\sigma_{y}=-\nabla\mathcal{R}_{B_{\varepsilon}^{c}}(x)=\frac{2}{N\omega_{N}}\varepsilon^{N-2}\frac{x}{(|x|^{2}-\varepsilon^{2})^{N-1}}

and

(A.10)

-\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\partial G_{B_{\varepsilon}^{c}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}S(x,y)d\sigma_{y}=\begin{cases}C_{N}\frac{\varepsilon^{N-2}}{\left(|x|^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}\right)^{N-2}}=\mathcal{R}_{B_{\varepsilon}^{c}}(x)&\mbox{ if }N\geq 3,\\[8.53581pt]
\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(-\ln\frac{|x|^{2}}{|x|^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}}+\ln|x|\right)=\mathcal{R}_{B_{\varepsilon}^{c}}(x)+\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\frac{|x|}{\varepsilon}&\mbox{ if }N=2.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let us prove (
A.9
). For
t\in B_{\varepsilon}
, using the expression of the Green and the Robin function in
B_{\varepsilon}
and recalling
[
4
]
, p. 170, we have

\begin{split}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\nu(y)\left(\frac{\partial G_{B_{\varepsilon}}(t,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}\right)^{2}d\sigma_{y}=\nabla\mathcal{R}_{B_{\varepsilon}}(t)=\frac{2}{N\omega_{N}}\varepsilon^{N-2}\frac{t}{(\varepsilon^{2}-|t|^{2})^{N-1}}\end{split}

and we deduce the following formula,

(A.11)

\frac{1}{(N\omega_{N})^{2}}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\frac{1}{|t-y|^{2N}}d\sigma_{y}=\frac{2}{N\omega_{N}}\varepsilon^{N-2}\frac{t}{(\varepsilon^{2}-|t|^{2})^{N-1}}\quad\hbox{for any }t\in B_{\varepsilon}.

Now, let
x\in B^{c}_{\varepsilon}
, then using (
A.1
)

\begin{split}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}&\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{\partial G_{B^{c}_{\varepsilon}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}\right)^{2}d\sigma_{y}=\frac{1}{(N\omega_{N})^{2}}\frac{\left(\varepsilon^{2}-|x|^{2}\right)^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{2N}}d\sigma_{y}\\
&(\hbox{setting $x=\varepsilon^{2}\frac{t}{|t|^{2}}$ and so $|t|\leq\varepsilon$})=\frac{1}{(N\omega_{N})^{2}}\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{|t|^{4}}(|t|^{2}-\varepsilon^{2})^{2}\int_{|y|=\varepsilon}\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\frac{1}{\left|\frac{\varepsilon^{2}t-|t|^{2}y}{|t|^{2}}\right|^{2N}}d\sigma_{y}\\
=&\frac{1}{(N\omega_{N})^{2}}(|t|^{2}-\varepsilon^{2})^{2}\frac{|t|^{2N-4}}{\varepsilon^{2N-2}}\int_{|y|=\varepsilon}\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\frac{1}{|t-y|^{2N}}d\sigma_{y}\\
&(\hbox{using \eqref{Res} for $|t|\leq\varepsilon$})=\frac{2}{N\omega_{N}}\frac{|t|^{2N-4}}{\varepsilon^{N-2}}\frac{t}{\left(\varepsilon^{2}-|t|^{2}\right)^{N-1}}\\
=&\frac{2}{N\omega_{N}}\varepsilon^{N-2}\frac{x}{(|x|^{2}-\varepsilon^{2})^{N-1}}=-\nabla\mathcal{R}_{B_{\varepsilon}^{c}}(x),\end{split}

which gives the claim.

Next let us prove (
A.10
). If
N\geq 3
we have

\begin{split}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}&\frac{\partial G_{B_{\varepsilon}^{c}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}S(x,y)d\sigma_{y}=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}-|x|^{2}}{N\omega_{N}\varepsilon}C_{N}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{2N-2}}d\sigma_{y}\\
&(\hbox{setting $x=\varepsilon^{2}\frac{t}{|t|^{2}}$})=\frac{|t|^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}}{N\omega_{N}\varepsilon}C_{N}\frac{|t|^{2N-4}}{\varepsilon^{2N-4}}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{|t-y|^{2N-2}}d\sigma_{y}\\
=&\frac{|t|^{2N-4}}{\varepsilon^{2N-4}}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\partial G_{B_{\varepsilon}}(t,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}S(t,y)d\sigma_{y}=-C_{N}\frac{|t|^{2N-4}}{\varepsilon^{2N-4}}\frac{\varepsilon^{N-2}}{\left(\varepsilon^{2}-|t|^{2}\right)^{N-2}}=-C_{N}\frac{\varepsilon^{N-2}}{\left(|x|^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}\right)^{N-2}}.\end{split}

If
N=2
we have, arguing as above,

\begin{split}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}&\frac{\partial G_{B_{\varepsilon}^{c}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}S(x,y)d\sigma_{y}=-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}-|x|^{2}}{4\pi^{2}\varepsilon}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\ln|x-y|}{|x-y|^{2}}d\sigma_{y}\\
=&-\varepsilon^{2}\frac{|t|^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}}{4\pi^{2}\varepsilon|t|^{2}}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\ln|\varepsilon^{2}\frac{t}{|t|^{2}}-y|}{|\varepsilon^{2}\frac{t}{|t|^{2}}-y|^{2}}d\sigma_{y}=-\frac{|t|^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}}{4\pi^{2}\varepsilon}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\ln|t-y|+\ln\varepsilon-\ln|t|}{|t-y|^{2}}d\sigma_{y}\\
=&\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\partial G_{B_{\varepsilon}}(t,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}S(t,y)d\sigma_{y}-\frac{|t|^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}}{4\pi^{2}\varepsilon}\ln\frac{\varepsilon}{|t|}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{|t-y|^{2}}d\sigma_{y}\\
=&-\mathcal{R}_{B_{\varepsilon}}(t)+\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\frac{\varepsilon}{|t|}=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\frac{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon^{2}-|t|^{2}}+\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\frac{\varepsilon}{|t|}\\
=&-\mathcal{R}_{B_{\varepsilon}^{c}}(x)-\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\frac{|x|}{\varepsilon},\end{split}

which ends the proof.
∎

The final lemma computes some useful integrals.

Lemma A.3
.

We have that, for any
x\in B^{c}_{\varepsilon}
and
N\geq 2
the following identities hold,

(A.12)

\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{C_{N}}{|x-y|^{N-2}}d\sigma_{y}=\frac{1}{N-2}\frac{\varepsilon^{N-1}}{|x|^{N-2}},

(A.13)

\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\ln|x-y|d\sigma_{y}=2\pi\varepsilon\ln|x|,

and

(A.14)

\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\big|\ln|x-y|\big|d\sigma_{y}=O\left(\varepsilon\big|\ln|x|\big|\right).

Proof.

For
x\in B_{\varepsilon}^{c}
, using (
A.4
) and (
A.5
), we have

\begin{split}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}&\frac{C_{N}}{|x-y|^{N-2}}d\sigma_{y}=C_{N}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{|x|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}-2x\cdot y}{|x-y|^{N}}d\sigma_{y}\\
&=\frac{\varepsilon}{N-2}\frac{|x|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}-|x|^{2}}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\partial G_{B_{\varepsilon}^{c}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}d\sigma_{y}-\frac{2\varepsilon}{N-2}\frac{x}{\varepsilon^{2}-|x|^{2}}\cdot\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}y\frac{\partial G_{B_{\varepsilon}^{c}}(x,y)}{\partial\nu_{y}}d\sigma_{y}\\
&=\frac{\varepsilon^{N-1}}{N-2}\frac{1}{|x|^{N-2}}.\end{split}

Let us prove (
A.13
). Denoting by
F(x)=\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\ln|x-y|d\sigma_{y}
, by (
A.3
) we get, for any
x\in B_{\varepsilon}
,

\begin{split}\frac{\partial F(x)}{\partial x_{i}}=&x_{i}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{2}}d\sigma_{y}-\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\frac{y_{i}}{|x-y|^{2}}d\sigma_{y}=0,\end{split}

and then
F(x)=F(0)=\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\ln|y|d\sigma_{y}=2\pi\varepsilon\ln\varepsilon.
Next, if
x\in B_{\varepsilon}^{c}
,

\begin{split}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\ln|x-y|d\sigma_{y}&\left(\hbox{setting $x=\varepsilon^{2}\frac{t}{|t|^{2}}$}\right)=\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\big(\ln|x-y|+\ln\frac{\varepsilon}{|t|}\big)d\sigma_{y}\\
=&2\pi\varepsilon\ln\varepsilon+2\pi\varepsilon\ln\frac{|x|}{\varepsilon}=2\pi\varepsilon\ln|x|,\end{split}

which proves (
A.13
).

Finally to estimate
\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\big|\ln|x-y|\big|d\sigma_{y}
we consider the alternative either
\varepsilon<|x|\leq\frac{1}{2}
or
|x|\geq\frac{1}{2}
.

If
|x|\geq\frac{1}{2}
then

\begin{split}\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\big|\ln|x-y|\big|d\sigma_{y}&\leq\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\left[\big|\ln|x|\big|+\frac{1}{2}\Big|\underbrace{\ln\left(1-2\frac{x\cdot y}{|x|^{2}}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\right)}_{=o(1)}\Big|\right]d\sigma_{y}\\
&=\big(2\pi\big|\ln|x|\big|+o(1)\big)\varepsilon.\end{split}

On the other hand, if
\varepsilon<|x|\leq\frac{1}{2}
, for
y\in\partial B_{\varepsilon}
we have
|x-y|<1
. So

\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\big|\ln|x-y|\big|d\sigma_{y}=-\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}\ln|x-y|d\sigma_{y}=-2\pi\varepsilon\ln|x|,

which ends the proof.

∎

Non-Hermitian chiral anomalies
Sharareh Sayyad, Julia D. Hannukainen, Adolfo G. Grushin

Abstract
The chiral anomaly underlies a broad number of phenomena, from enhanced electronic transport in topological metals to anomalous currents in the quark-gluon plasma. The discovery of topological states of matter in non-Hermitian systems raises the question of whether there are anomalous conservation laws that remain unaccounted for. To answer this question, we consider both two and four space-time dimensions, presenting a unified formulation to calculate anomalous responses in Hermitianized, anti-Hermitianized and non-Hermitian systems of massless electrons with complex Fermi velocities coupled to non-Hermitian gauge fields. Our results indicate that the quantum conservation laws of chiral currents of non-Hermitian systems are not related to those in Hermitianized and anti-Hermitianized systems, as would be expected classically, due to novel anomalous terms that we derive. We further present some physical consequences of our non-Hermitian anomaly that may have implications for a broad class of emerging experimental systems that realize non-Hermitian Hamiltonians.

) will change the induced electric charge created by a magnetic monopole from
e/2
of the Hermitian system
Rosenberg and Franz 2010
, to an arbitrary value (see SM) that depends on the phase of the complex Fermi velocity.

One-loop diagrammatic calculation of the
1+1
dimensional anomaly-.

We also derive the chiral anomaly for the (anti)-Hermitianized, and non-Hermitian actions in
1+1
dimensions using the diagrammatic method, see the SM
SuppMat
.
Within this approach, we integrate out the fermionic degrees of freedom from the underlying partition function, which results in an effective action for the gauge fields.
The functional integration of an Hermitian action, with a Lagrangian density
\mathcal{L}=\Psi^{\dagger}(\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\gamma^{0}\not{\zc D})\Psi
, requires an orthogonal eigenbasis of the self-adjoint operator
(\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\gamma^{0}\not{\zc D})
to yield the effective action
\Gamma[V,W]=-\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\ln[\det(\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\gamma^{0}\not{\zc D})]
.
By using the product rules of determinants and logarithms, and omitting any constant vacuum contributions
\Gamma[V,W]
is rewritten as
\Gamma[V,W]=-\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\ln[\det(\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\not{\zc D})]
.
When the operator
(\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\gamma^{0}\not{\zc D})
is not self-adjoint, one should instead use the corresponding biorthogonal eigenbasis to evaluate the functional integral.
To take advantage of the well-developed Hermitian field theory and to avoid the complexities of finding functional determinants from a biorthogonal basis, we construct the self-adjoint operator
\not{\zc D}\not{\zc D}_{\mathcal{L}}^{\dagger}
, where
\not{\zc D}_{\mathcal{L}}^{\dagger}\equiv\gamma^{\mu}{\zc D}_{\mu}^{\dagger}
.
Formally, this is done by considering the sum of the effective actions with respect to the operators
\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\gamma^{0}\not{\zc D}
and
\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\gamma^{0}\not{\zc D}_{\mathcal{L}}^{\dagger}
and using properties of the determinant and the logarithm to obtain
\Gamma[V,W]=-\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{i}}}{2}\ln[\det(\not{\zc D}\not{\zc D}_{\mathcal{L}}^{\dagger})]
(see SM
SuppMat
).
For the various actions given in Eqs. (
2
,
5
), we expand this effective action up to the second-order in the gauge fields. These calculations give rise to divergent momentum integrals which are treated with gauge-invariant regularization methods
Peskin and Schroeder 1995
.

The vector (chiral) currents are defined as the sum of the functional derivatives of the effective action with respect to
V
and
V^{\dagger}
(
W
and
W^{\dagger}
), from which the expressions for divergence of the vector and chiral currents (terms first-order in the gauge fields), e.g., in Eq. (
23
), follow.

Summary-.

We have found non-Hermitian anomalies in massless Dirac fermions with complex velocities coupled to non-Hermitian gauge fields. We have presented a unified non-Hermitian formulation to bring the Hermitianized, anti-Hermitianized, and non-Hermitian cases under one umbrella.
Our results show that non-Hermiticity allows new anomalous terms in the conservation laws for the chiral current in both in
2
and
4
dimensions. Interestingly, these anomalous terms could not be inferred by simply adding the Hermitianized and anti-Hermitianized results, as would be expected classically, see Fig.
1
. In this sense, these anomalies are different and richer than those that occur in Hermitian systems.
We further demonstrate this point by presenting non-Hermitian anomalous Hall effect and non-Hermitian chiral magnetic effect.

Finally, we note that novel parity-like anomalies may exist in non-Hermitian systems. Exploring them might give a different interpretation to the non-universality of the Hall response in non-Hermitian Chern insulators
Hirsbrunner et al. 2019
.

Non-Hermitian chiral anomaly: Fujikawa’s path integral formalism

In this section, we provide a detailed derivation of the chiral anomaly in
2
and
4

The massless fermions with complex Fermi velocities coupled to non-Hermitian gauge fields

in Euclidean space are described by the Dirac action

\displaystyle{\cal S}_{\rm{nh}}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\int{\rm d}^{d}x\Big[\bar{\Psi}\gamma^{\mu}({\zc D}_{\rm{nh},\mu}\Psi)\Big],

\displaystyle=\gamma^{\mu}{\zc D}_{\rm{nh},\mu}=\gamma^{\mu}M_{\mu}^{\nu}\partial_{\nu}-\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\gamma^{\mu}M_{\mu}^{\nu}\left(V_{\nu}+\gamma^{5}W_{\nu}\right),

B.1
Conserved classical currents

Let us first present the classical vector and chiral currents for the Dirac action in Eq. (

\Psi

\displaystyle-\bar{\Psi}\gamma^{\mu}(\tilde{V}_{\mu}+\gamma^{5}\tilde{W}_{\mu})+\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\tilde{d}_{\mu}\bar{\Psi}\gamma^{\mu}=0,

(61)

\displaystyle\gamma^{0}\gamma^{\mu}(\tilde{V}_{\mu}+\gamma^{5}\tilde{W}_{\mu})\Psi+\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\gamma^{0}\gamma^{\mu}\tilde{d}_{\mu}\bar{\Psi}=0.

(62)

B.2
Non-conservation of quantum currents

Performing chiral and vector rotations, with rotating angles
\beta
and
\kappa

\displaystyle\bar{\Psi}_{\rm rot}=\bar{\Psi}e^{-\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\kappa-\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\gamma^{5}\beta},\qquad\Psi_{\rm rot}=e^{\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\kappa-\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\gamma^{5}\beta}{\Psi},

(65)

keeps the rotated action

invariant under this transformation
if one imposes

\displaystyle\gamma^{\mu}\tilde{V}_{\mu}+\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{5}\tilde{W}_{\mu}=\gamma^{\mu}M_{\mu}^{\nu}\partial_{\nu}\kappa-\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{5}M_{\mu}^{\nu}\partial_{\nu}\beta.

(66)

While the above relation ensures the chiral symmetry of the action (
\tilde{\cal S}
), the measure in the generating function (
\tilde{\cal Z}
)
exhibits the chiral anomaly after applying the chiral transformations in Eq. (
65
).
To evaluate the chiral anomaly, we employ Fujikawa’s method
Fujikawa and Suzuki 2004
and calculate the changes of the path-integral measure under the chiral and vector transformations.

To begin with, we note that
\tilde{\zc D}
is non-Hermitian,

\tilde{\not{\zc D}}^{\dagger}=(\tilde{d}^{\dagger}_{\mu}+\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\tilde{V}^{\dagger}_{\mu}+\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\gamma^{5\dagger}\tilde{W}_{\mu}^{\dagger})\gamma^{\mu\dagger}=-\gamma^{\mu}(\tilde{d}^{\dagger}_{\mu}+\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\tilde{V}^{\dagger}_{\mu}-\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\gamma^{5}\tilde{W}_{\mu}^{\dagger})=-\gamma^{\mu}\tilde{\zc D}_{\mu}^{\dagger}\neq\tilde{\not{\zc D}},

(67)

which is obtained using the self-adjoint properties of gamma matrices (
\gamma^{\mu\dagger}=-\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{5\dagger}=\gamma^{5}
). Note that by placing
\gamma^{\mu}
on the left hand side of
\tilde{\zc D}_{\mu}^{\dagger}
in Eq. (
67

\tilde{\zc D}_{\mu}^{\dagger}
is no longer equal to the complex conjugate of
\tilde{\zc D}_{\mu}
, i.e.,
\tilde{\zc D}_{\mu}^{\dagger}\neq[\tilde{\zc D}_{\mu}]^{\dagger}
.
To avoid dealing with the unnecessary complexities of the non-Hermitian eigenvalue problem, we consider the Hermitian Laplacian operators
\tilde{\not{\zc D}}\tilde{\not{\zc D}}^{\dagger}
and
\tilde{\not{\zc D}}^{\dagger}\tilde{\not{\zc D}}
, with eigenvalues
\{\lambda^{2}\}
, such that

\displaystyle\tilde{\not{\zc D}}\tilde{\not{\zc D}}^{\dagger}|\eta_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}_{n}|\eta_{n}\rangle,\quad\tilde{\not{\zc D}}^{\dagger}\tilde{\not{\zc D}}|\xi_{n}\rangle=\lambda^{2}_{n}|\xi_{n}\rangle,

(68)

\displaystyle\tilde{\not{\zc D}}^{\dagger}|\eta_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=\lambda^{*}_{n}|\xi_{n}\rangle,\quad\tilde{\not{\zc D}}|\xi_{n}\rangle=\lambda_{n}|\eta_{n}\rangle.

(69)

where
\{|\xi_{n}\rangle\}
and
\{|\eta_{n}\rangle\}
are eigenvectors. We further assume that these sets of eigenbases are normalizable and complete

\displaystyle\forall\{|\phi\rangle\}\in\Big\{\{|\xi\rangle\},\{|\eta\rangle\}\Big\},\quad\int{\rm d}^{2}x\phi^{\dagger}_{n}(x)\phi_{m}(x)=\delta_{nm},\quad\sum_{n}\phi^{\dagger}_{n}(y)\phi_{n}(x)=\delta(x-y),

(70)

where

2

\displaystyle\Psi(x)=\sum\limits_{n}a_{n}\xi_{n}(x),\qquad\bar{\Psi}(x)=\sum\limits_{n}\eta^{\dagger}_{n}(x)\bar{b}_{n},

where

\displaystyle\Gamma[V,W]

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{i}}}{4}\ln[\det(\not{\zc D}\not{\zc D}_{\mathcal{L}}^{\dagger})]-\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{i}}}{4}\ln[\det(\not{\zc D}_{\mathcal{L}}^{\dagger}\not{\zc D})]

(171)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{i}}}{2}\ln[\det(\not{\zc D}\not{\zc D}_{\mathcal{L}}^{\dagger})]

(172)

which is Hermitian and subsequently well-defined.

D.2
The chiral anomaly from the Hermitianized action
\cal S_{\rm{h}}

In this subsection, we calculate the Hermitian chiral anomaly where the starting point is the Hermitianized action,

\displaystyle{\cal Z}_{\rm{h}}

\displaystyle\propto\int{\cal D}\Psi{\cal D}\bar{\Psi}e^{\mathop{\mathrm{i}}{\cal S}_{\rm{h}}},

(173)

\displaystyle{\cal S}_{\rm{h}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{i}}}{2}\int{\rm d}^{d}x\Big[\bar{\Psi}\gamma^{\mu}({\zc D}_{\rm{nh},\mu}\Psi)-\overline{({\zc D}_{\rm{nh},\mu}\Psi)}\gamma^{\mu}\Psi\Big]=\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\int{\rm d}^{2}x\bar{\Psi}\gamma^{\mu}{\zc D}_{\rm{h},\mu}\Psi,

(174)

\displaystyle{\zc D}_{\rm{h},\mu}

\displaystyle=\Big[\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}[M_{\mu}^{\nu}]\partial_{\nu}-\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}[M_{\mu}^{\nu}V_{\nu}]-\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\gamma^{5}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}[M_{\mu}^{\nu}W_{\nu}]\Big],

(175)

where the Dirac operator,
{\zc D}_{\rm{nh},\mu}
, is

\not{\zc D}_{\rm{nh}}=\gamma^{\mu}{\zc D}_{\rm{nh},\mu}=\gamma^{\mu}M_{\mu}^{\nu}\partial_{\nu}-\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\gamma^{\mu}M_{\mu}^{\nu}\left(V_{\nu}+\gamma^{5}W_{\nu}\right),

(176)

Using the unified notation described in Table.
2
, the Hermitian Dirac operator,
{\zc D}_{\rm{h},\mu}
, is written as

\displaystyle{\zc D}_{\rm{h},\mu}

\displaystyle=\tilde{d}_{\mu}-\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\tilde{V}_{\mu}-\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\gamma^{5}\tilde{W}_{\mu}.

(177)

{\zc D}_{\rm{h}}
has the same form as the associated Dirac operator for the well-known Schwinger model with external vector- and chiral fields.
By integrating out the fermionic degrees of freedom from the action, the effective theory of the gauge fields reads

\displaystyle\Gamma[\tilde{V},\tilde{W}]=-\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\ln[\det(\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\not{\zc D}_{\rm{h}})].

(178)

We expand the effective action to second order in the fields, to extract the linear response terms:

\displaystyle\Gamma^{(2)}[\tilde{V},\tilde{W}]

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{i}}}{2}\Big[\text{Tr}[G(x)\not{\tilde{V}}G(y)\not{\tilde{V}}]+\text{Tr}[G(x)\gamma^{5}\not{\tilde{W}}G(y)\gamma^{5}\not{\tilde{W}}]-\text{Tr}[G(x)\not{\tilde{V}}G(y)\gamma^{5}\not{\tilde{W}}]-\text{Tr}[G(x)\gamma^{5}\not{\tilde{W}}G(y)\not{\tilde{V}}]\Big],

(179)

where the Green’s function is

\displaystyle G(x)=\frac{1}{\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\tilde{\not{d}}},

(180)

and where the trace is considered over both position and spin space.
After calculating the trace and integrating over the loop momentum, the effective action becomes

\displaystyle\Gamma^{(2)}[\tilde{V},\tilde{W}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\det(\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}[M])^{2}}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{2}\tilde{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\big[\tilde{V}_{\mu}(\tilde{k})\tilde{V}_{\nu}(-\tilde{k})\Pi_{\rm{h}}^{\mu\nu}+\tilde{W}_{\mu}(\tilde{k})\tilde{W}_{\nu}(-\tilde{k})\Pi_{\rm{h}}^{\mu\nu}+2\tilde{V}_{\mu}(\tilde{k})\tilde{W}_{\nu}(-\tilde{k})\Pi_{5,\rm{h}}^{\mu\nu}\big],

(181)

where the vacuum polarization tensors are

\displaystyle\Pi_{\rm{h}}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{\tilde{k}^{\mu}\tilde{k}^{\nu}-\tilde{k}^{2}g^{\mu\nu}}{\tilde{k}^{2}},

(182)

\displaystyle\Pi_{5,\rm{h}}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{\varepsilon^{\nu\beta}(\tilde{k}^{\mu}\tilde{k}_{\beta}-\delta_{\beta}^{\mu}\tilde{k}^{2})}{\tilde{k}^{2}}.

(183)

The factor
\det(\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}[M])^{2}
is the product of the Jacobians obtained from the variable transforms of both the loop momentum (
p
) as
\tilde{p}_{\mu}=\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}[M^{\nu}_{\mu}]p_{\nu}
, and the external momentum (
k
) such that
\tilde{k}_{\mu}=\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}[M^{\nu}_{\mu}]k_{\nu}
.
By inserting Eqs. (
182
,
183
) in Eq. (
181
), the effective action in terms of the bare momenta and fields reads

\displaystyle\Gamma^{(2)}[V,W]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\det(\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}[M])}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{2}}\big[

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}(M_{\mu}^{\alpha}M_{\nu}^{\beta}V_{\alpha}V_{\beta}+M_{\mu}^{*\alpha}M_{\nu}^{*\beta}V^{\dagger}_{\alpha}V^{\dagger}_{\beta}+M_{\mu}^{\alpha}M_{\nu}^{*\beta}V_{\alpha}V^{\dagger}_{\beta}+M_{\mu}^{*\alpha}M_{\nu}^{\beta}V^{\dagger}_{\alpha}V_{\beta})\Pi_{\rm{h}}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}(M_{\mu}^{\alpha}M_{\nu}^{\beta}W_{\alpha}W_{\beta}+M_{\mu}^{*\alpha}M_{\nu}^{*\beta}W^{\dagger}_{\alpha}W^{\dagger}_{\beta}+M_{\mu}^{\alpha}M_{\nu}^{*\beta}W_{\alpha}W^{\dagger}_{\beta}+M_{\mu}^{*\alpha}M_{\nu}^{\beta}W^{\dagger}_{\alpha}W_{\beta})\Pi_{\rm{h}}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}(M_{\mu}^{\alpha}M_{\nu}^{\beta}V_{\alpha}W_{\beta}+M_{\mu}^{*\alpha}M_{\nu}^{*\beta}V^{\dagger}_{\alpha}W^{\dagger}_{\beta}+M_{\mu}^{\alpha}M_{\nu}^{*\beta}V_{\alpha}W^{\dagger}_{\beta}+M_{\mu}^{*\alpha}M_{\nu}^{\beta}V^{\dagger}_{\alpha}W_{\beta})\Pi_{5,\rm{h}}^{\mu\nu}\big].

(184)

The corresponding rescaled vector current is given by the functional derivative of the effective action with respect to the gauge field and its Hermitian conjugate,

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}[M_{\mu}^{\sigma}]j^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta\Gamma^{(2)}}{\delta V_{\sigma}}+\frac{\delta\Gamma^{(2)}}{\delta V^{\dagger}_{\sigma}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}[M_{\mu}^{\sigma}](M_{\nu}^{\beta}V_{\beta}+M_{\nu}^{*\beta}V^{\dagger}_{\beta})\frac{\Pi_{\rm{h}}^{\mu\nu}}{\det(\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}[M])}+\frac{1}{2}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}[M^{\sigma}_{\mu}](M_{\nu}^{\beta}W_{\beta}+M_{\nu}^{*\beta}W^{\dagger}_{\beta})\frac{\Pi^{\mu\nu}_{5,\rm{h}}}{\det(\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}[M])}.

(185)

Similarly, the rescaled chiral current is obtained by summing the functional derivatives of the effective action with respect to the chiral gauge field and its Hermitian conjugate:

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}[M^{\sigma}_{\mu}]j^{5,\mu}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta\Gamma^{(2)}}{\delta W_{\sigma}}+\frac{\delta\Gamma^{(2)}}{\delta W^{\dagger}_{\sigma}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}[M^{\sigma}_{\mu}](M_{\nu}^{\beta}W_{\beta}+M_{\nu}^{*\beta}W^{\dagger}_{\beta})\frac{\Pi^{\mu\nu}_{\rm{h}}}{\det(\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}[M])}+\frac{1}{2}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}[M^{\sigma}_{\nu}](M_{\mu}^{\beta}V_{\beta}+M_{\mu}^{*\beta}V^{\dagger}_{\beta})\frac{\Pi^{\mu\nu}_{5,\rm{h}}}{\det(\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}[M])}.

(186)

The divergence of the rescaled vector and chiral currents is

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}[M_{\mu}^{\alpha}]\partial_{\alpha}j^{\mu}=0,

(187)

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}[M_{\mu}^{\alpha}]\partial_{\alpha}j^{5,\mu}=\frac{1}{\det(\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}[M])\pi}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}[M_{\mu}^{\alpha}]\partial_{\alpha}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}[M_{\nu}^{\beta}V_{\beta}].

(188)

D.3
The chiral anomaly from the anti-Hermitianized action
\cal S_{\rm{ah}}

In this subsection, we calculate the chiral anomaly from the anti-Hermitian action Eq. (
56
), defined in terms of a complex velocity matrix and complex gauge fields as

\displaystyle{\cal Z}_{\rm{ah}}

\displaystyle\propto\int{\cal D}\Psi{\cal D}\bar{\Psi}e^{\mathop{\mathrm{i}}{\cal S}_{\rm{ah}}},

(189)

\displaystyle{\cal S}_{\rm{ah}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{i}}}{2}\int{\rm d}^{d}x\Big[\bar{\Psi}\gamma^{\mu}({\zc D}_{\rm{nh},\mu}\Psi)+\overline{({\zc D}_{\rm{nh},\mu}\Psi)}\gamma^{\mu}\Psi\Big]=\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\int{\rm d}^{2}x\bar{\Psi}\gamma^{\mu}{\zc D}_{\rm{ah},\mu}\Psi,

(190)

\displaystyle{\zc D}_{\rm{ah},\mu}

\displaystyle=\Big[\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[M_{\mu}^{\nu}]\partial_{\nu}+\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[M_{\mu}^{\nu}V_{\nu}]+\gamma^{5}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[M_{\mu}^{\nu}W_{\nu}]\Big],

(191)

where the operator,
{\zc D}_{\rm{nh},\mu}
, is defined in Eq. (
176
).
Using the unified notation described in Table.
2
, the anti-Hermitian Dirac operator,

\displaystyle{\zc D}_{\rm{ah},\mu}

\displaystyle=\tilde{d}_{\mu}-\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\tilde{V}_{\mu}-\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\gamma^{5}\tilde{W}_{\mu}.

(192)

The operator
\gamma^{0}\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\not{\zc D}_{\rm{ah}}
is anti-Hermitian and therefore diagonalizable with a complete set of orthogonal eigenvectors. Hence, the functional determinant of
\gamma^{0}\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\not{\zc D}_{\rm{ah}}
is well-defined and the subsequent effective action is

\displaystyle\Gamma[V,W]=-\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{i}}}{2}\ln[\det(\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\not{\zc D}_{\rm{ah}})].

(193)

\Gamma[V,W]
is expanded analogously to the effective theory of the Hermitianized action in Sec

and results in the effective action, up to second order in the fields

\displaystyle\Gamma^{(2)}[V,W]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\det(\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[M])}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{2}}\big[

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}(M_{\mu}^{\alpha}M_{\nu}^{\beta}V_{\alpha}V_{\beta}+M_{\mu}^{*\alpha}M_{\nu}^{*\beta}V^{\dagger}_{\alpha}V^{\dagger}_{\beta}-M_{\mu}^{\alpha}M_{\nu}^{*\beta}V_{\alpha}V^{\dagger}_{\beta}-M_{\mu}^{*\alpha}M_{\nu}^{\beta}V^{\dagger}_{\alpha}V_{\beta})\Pi^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}(M_{\mu}^{\alpha}M_{\nu}^{\beta}W_{\alpha}W_{\beta}+M_{\mu}^{*\alpha}M_{\nu}^{*\beta}W^{\dagger}_{\alpha}W^{\dagger}_{\beta}-M_{\mu}^{\alpha}M_{\nu}^{*\beta}W_{\alpha}W^{\dagger}_{\beta}-M_{\mu}^{*\alpha}M_{\nu}^{\beta}W^{\dagger}_{\alpha}W_{\beta})\Pi^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}(M_{\mu}^{\alpha}M_{\nu}^{\beta}V_{\alpha}W_{\beta}+M_{\mu}^{*\alpha}M_{\nu}^{*\beta}V^{\dagger}_{\alpha}W^{\dagger}_{\beta}-M_{\mu}^{\alpha}M_{\nu}^{*\beta}V_{\alpha}W^{\dagger}_{\beta}-M_{\mu}^{*\alpha}M_{\nu}^{\beta}V^{\dagger}_{\alpha}W_{\beta})\Pi_{5}^{\mu\nu}\big],

(194)

\displaystyle\Pi_{\rm{ah}}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{\tilde{M}_{\alpha}^{\mu}\tilde{M}_{\beta}^{\nu}k^{\alpha}k^{\beta}-\bar{M}_{\alpha}^{\sigma}\tilde{M}_{\sigma}^{\beta}k^{\alpha}k_{\beta}g^{\mu\nu}}{\bar{M}_{\kappa}^{\rho}\tilde{M}_{\rho}^{\delta}k^{\kappa}k_{\delta}},

(195)

\displaystyle\Pi_{\rm{ah},5}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{\varepsilon^{\nu\beta}(\tilde{M}_{\alpha}^{\mu}\tilde{M}_{\beta}^{\sigma}k^{\alpha}k_{\sigma}-\delta_{\beta}^{\mu}\tilde{M}_{\alpha}^{\sigma}\tilde{M}_{\sigma}^{\lambda}k^{\alpha}k_{\lambda})}{\tilde{M}_{\kappa}^{\rho}\tilde{M}_{\rho}^{\delta}k^{\kappa}k_{\delta}},

(196)

where
\tilde{M}_{\nu}^{\mu}=\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[M_{\nu}^{\mu}]
.
The associated vector current is defined as the sum of the functional derivatives of the effective action with respect to both
V
and
V^{\dagger}

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[M^{\sigma}_{\mu}]j^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta\Gamma^{(2)}}{\delta V_{\sigma}}+\frac{\delta\Gamma^{(2)}}{\delta V^{\dagger}_{\sigma}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{i}}}{2}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[M^{\sigma}_{\mu}](M_{\nu}^{\beta}V_{\beta}-M_{\nu}^{*\beta}V^{\dagger}_{\beta})\frac{\Pi^{\mu\nu}}{\det(\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[M])}+\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{i}}}{2}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[M^{\sigma}_{\mu}](M_{\nu}^{\beta}W_{\beta}-M_{\nu}^{*\beta}W^{\dagger}_{\beta})\frac{\Pi^{\mu\nu}_{5}}{\det(\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[M])}

(197)

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[M^{\sigma}_{\mu}]j^{5,\mu}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta\Gamma^{(2)}}{\delta W_{\sigma}}+\frac{\delta\Gamma^{(2)}}{\delta W^{\dagger}_{\sigma}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{i}}}{2}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[M^{\sigma}_{\mu}](M_{\nu}^{\beta}W_{\beta}-M_{\nu}^{*\beta}W^{\dagger}_{\beta})\frac{\Pi^{\mu\nu}}{\det(\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[M])}+\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{i}}}{2}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[M^{\sigma}_{\nu}](M_{\mu}^{\beta}V_{\beta}-M_{\mu}^{*\beta}V^{\dagger}_{\beta})\frac{\Pi^{\mu\nu}_{5}}{\det(\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[M])}.

(198)

The divergence of both currents are

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[M_{\mu}^{\alpha}]\partial_{\alpha}j^{\mu}=0,

(199)

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[M_{\mu}^{\alpha}]\partial_{\alpha}j^{5,\mu}=\frac{1}{\det(\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[M])\pi}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[M_{\mu}^{\alpha}]\partial_{\alpha}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}[M_{\nu}^{\beta}V_{\beta}].

(200)

D.4
The chiral anomaly from the non-Hermitian action
\cal S_{\rm{nh}}

The generic non-Hermitian action introduced in Eq.
50
is in Minkowski space given by

\displaystyle{\cal Z}_{\rm{nh}}

\displaystyle\propto\int{\cal D}\Psi{\cal D}\bar{\Psi}e^{\mathop{\mathrm{i}}{\cal S}_{\rm{nh}}},

(201)

\displaystyle{\cal S}_{\rm nh}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\int{\rm d}^{2}x\Big[\bar{\Psi}\gamma^{\mu}({\zc D}_{{\rm nh},\mu}\Psi)\Big],

(202)

\displaystyle\not{\zc D}_{\rm nh}

\displaystyle=\gamma^{\mu}{\zc D}_{{\rm nh},\mu}=\gamma^{\mu}M_{\mu}^{\nu}\partial_{\nu}-\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\gamma^{\mu}M_{\mu}^{\nu}\left(V_{\nu}+\gamma^{5}W_{\nu}\right),

(203)

where we have kept the gauge fields non-Hermitian, but considered a real Fermi velocity matrix (
M
), due to the technical challenges arising from complex momentum integrals.
For notational convenience, we define
\tilde{V}_{\mu}=M_{\nu}^{\nu}V_{\nu}
,
\tilde{W}_{\mu}=M_{\nu}^{\nu}W_{\nu}
, and
\tilde{d}_{\mu}=M_{\mu}^{\nu}\partial_{\nu}
, such that the non Hermitian Dirac operator reads

\displaystyle\zc D_{{\rm nh},\mu}

\displaystyle={\zc D}_{\mu}=\tilde{d}_{\mu}-\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\tilde{V}_{\mu}-\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\tilde{W}_{\mu}.

(204)

The effective action of the fields, obtained by integrating out the fermionic degrees of freedom takes the form of Eq. (
172
) as

\displaystyle\Gamma[V,W]=-\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{i}}}{2}\ln[\det(\not{\zc D}\not{\zc D}_{\mathcal{L}}^{\dagger})],

(205)

since the underlying action is non-Hermitian.
Here

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\not{\zc D}

\displaystyle=i\tilde{\not{d}}+\not{\tilde{V}}+\varepsilon^{\mu\nu}\gamma_{\nu}\tilde{W}_{\mu}

(206)

\displaystyle(\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\not{\zc D}_{\mathcal{L}})^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=i\tilde{\not{d}}+\not{\tilde{V}}^{\dagger}+\varepsilon^{\mu\nu}\gamma_{\nu}\tilde{W}^{\dagger}_{\mu},

(207)

where
\varepsilon^{\mu\nu}\gamma_{\nu}=\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{5}
.
The product between
\not{\zc D}
and
\not{\zc D}_{\mathcal{L}}^{\dagger}
is

\displaystyle\not{\zc D}\not{\zc D}_{\mathcal{L}}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=-\tilde{d}^{2}+\tilde{V}_{\mu}\tilde{V}^{\dagger\mu}+\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\tilde{d}_{\mu}\tilde{V}^{\dagger\mu}+\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\tilde{V}^{\dagger\mu}\tilde{d}_{\mu}+\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\tilde{V}^{\mu}\tilde{d}_{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}[\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}](\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\tilde{d}\tilde{V}_{\nu}^{\dagger}+\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\tilde{V}^{\dagger}_{\nu}d\tilde{d}_{\mu}+\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\tilde{V}_{\mu}\tilde{d}_{\nu})

\displaystyle+\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\alpha}(\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\tilde{d}_{\mu}+\tilde{V}_{\mu})\varepsilon^{\nu\beta}g_{\beta\alpha}\gamma^{\alpha}\tilde{W}^{\dagger}_{\nu}+\gamma^{\sigma}\gamma^{\nu}\varepsilon^{\mu\rho}g_{\rho\sigma}\gamma^{\sigma}\tilde{W}_{\mu}(\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\tilde{d}_{\nu}+\tilde{V}^{\dagger}_{\nu})+\gamma^{\sigma}\gamma^{\alpha}\varepsilon^{\mu\rho}g_{\rho\sigma}\tilde{W}_{\mu}\varepsilon^{\nu\beta}g_{\beta\alpha}\tilde{W}^{\dagger}_{\nu},

(208)

where, since we are only interested in the terms contributing to the linear response, all terms that are zero in the expansion to the second-order in the fields are omitted; an example is the term proportional to
[\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}]\tilde{V}_{\mu}\tilde{V}^{\dagger}_{\nu}
which is zero when the trace over the spin is taken.
By simplifying Eq. (
D.4
) further, and only keeping the relevant terms, it is rewritten as

\displaystyle\not{\zc D}\not{\zc D}_{\mathcal{L}}^{\dagger}=-\tilde{d}^{2}+\Delta^{(1)}+\Delta^{(2)}+\Delta^{(s)},

(209)

where

\displaystyle\Delta^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\tilde{d}_{\mu}\tilde{V}^{\dagger\mu}+\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\tilde{V}^{\dagger\mu}\tilde{d}_{\mu}+\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\tilde{V}^{\mu}\tilde{d}_{\mu}+\varepsilon^{\mu\nu}(\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\tilde{d}_{\nu}\tilde{W}^{\dagger}_{\mu}+\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\tilde{W}^{\dagger}_{\mu}\tilde{d}_{\nu}+\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\tilde{W}_{\mu}\tilde{d}_{\nu})

(210)

\displaystyle\Delta^{(2)}

\displaystyle=g^{\mu\nu}(\tilde{V}_{\mu}\tilde{V}^{\dagger}_{\nu}-\tilde{W}_{\mu}\tilde{W}^{\dagger}_{\nu})+\varepsilon^{\mu\nu}(\tilde{V}_{\nu}\tilde{W}^{\dagger}_{\mu}+\tilde{W}_{\mu}\tilde{V}^{\dagger}_{\nu})

(211)

\displaystyle\Delta^{(s)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}[\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}](\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\tilde{d}_{\mu}\tilde{V}_{\nu}^{\dagger}+\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\tilde{V}^{\dagger}_{\nu}\tilde{d}_{\mu}+\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\tilde{V}_{\mu}\tilde{d}_{\nu})+\frac{1}{2}[\gamma^{\nu},\gamma^{\alpha}]\varepsilon^{\mu\beta}g_{\beta\alpha}(\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\tilde{d}_{\nu}\tilde{W}^{\dagger}_{\mu}+\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\tilde{W}^{\dagger}_{\mu}\tilde{d}_{\nu}-\mathop{\mathrm{i}}\tilde{W}_{\mu}\tilde{d}_{\nu}),

(212)

Expanding the effective action in Eq.(
205
) up to the second-order casts

\displaystyle\Gamma^{(2)}[V,W]

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{i}}}{2}\ln\det(\tilde{\not{\zc D}}\tilde{\not{\zc D}}_{\mathcal{L}}^{\dagger})

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{i}}}{2}\big(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}[(-\tilde{d}^{2})^{-1}\Delta^{(2)}]-\frac{1}{2}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}[(-\tilde{d}^{2})^{-1}\Delta^{(1)}(-\tilde{d}^{2})^{-1}\Delta^{(1)}]-\frac{1}{2}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}[(-\tilde{d}^{2})^{-1}\Delta^{(s)}(-\tilde{d}^{2})^{-1}\Delta^{(s)}]\big).

(213)

By defining the internal and external momenta in terms of the velocities as
\tilde{p}_{\mu}=M_{\mu}^{\nu}p
and
\tilde{k}_{\mu}=M_{\mu}^{\nu}k
, the traces in Eq. (
D.4
) yield

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}[(-\tilde{d}^{2})^{-1}\Delta^{(2)}]

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\det[M]^{2}}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{2}\tilde{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{2}\tilde{p}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{g^{\mu\nu}(\tilde{V}_{\mu}\tilde{V}^{\dagger}_{\nu}-\tilde{W}_{\mu}\tilde{W}^{\dagger}_{\nu})+\varepsilon^{\mu\nu}(\tilde{V}_{\nu}\tilde{W}^{\dagger}_{\mu}+\tilde{W}_{\mu}\tilde{V}^{\dagger}_{\nu})}{\tilde{p}^{2}(\tilde{p}+\tilde{k})^{2}}(\tilde{p}+\tilde{k})^{2},

(214)

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}[(-\tilde{d}^{2})^{-1}\Delta^{(1)}(-\tilde{d}^{2})^{-1}\Delta^{(1)}]

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\det[M]^{2}}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{2}\tilde{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{2}\tilde{p}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{1}{\tilde{p}^{2}(\tilde{p}+\tilde{k})^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\Big[\big[\tilde{V}^{\dagger}_{\mu}\tilde{V}^{\dagger}_{\nu}+\tilde{V}_{\mu}\tilde{V}_{\nu}\big]\big[\tilde{p}^{\mu}(\tilde{p}+\tilde{k})^{\nu}\big]+\tilde{V}^{\dagger}_{\mu}\tilde{V}_{\nu}(\tilde{k}^{\mu}\tilde{k}^{\nu}+2\tilde{p}^{\mu}\tilde{p}^{\nu}+\tilde{p}^{\mu}\tilde{k}^{\nu}+\tilde{k}^{\mu}\tilde{p}^{\nu})

\displaystyle+\tilde{W}^{\dagger}_{\mu}\tilde{W}_{\nu}\big[-(\tilde{k}^{2}+2\tilde{p}^{2}+2\tilde{p}^{\alpha}\tilde{k}_{\alpha})g^{\mu\nu}+\tilde{k}^{\mu}\tilde{k}^{\nu}+2\tilde{p}^{\mu}\tilde{p}^{\nu}+\tilde{p}^{\mu}\tilde{k}^{\nu}+\tilde{k}^{\mu}\tilde{p}^{\nu}\big]

\displaystyle+\big[\tilde{V}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}\tilde{W}^{\dagger}_{\mu}+\tilde{V}_{\alpha}\tilde{W}^{\dagger}_{\mu}\big]\big[\varepsilon^{\mu\nu}(2\tilde{p}_{\nu}\tilde{p}^{\alpha}+\tilde{p}_{\nu}\tilde{k}^{\alpha}+\tilde{k}_{\nu}\tilde{p}^{\alpha})\big]

\displaystyle+\big[\tilde{V}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}\tilde{W}_{\mu}+\tilde{V}_{\alpha}\tilde{W}^{\dagger}_{\mu}\big]\big[\varepsilon^{\mu\nu}(2\tilde{p}_{\nu}\tilde{p}^{\alpha}+\tilde{p}_{\nu}\tilde{k}^{\alpha}+\tilde{k}_{\nu}\tilde{p}^{\alpha}+\tilde{k}_{\nu}\tilde{k}^{\alpha})\big]\Big],

(215)

and

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}[(-\tilde{d}^{2})^{-1}\Delta^{(s)}(-\tilde{d}^{2})^{-1}\Delta^{(s)}]

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\det[M]^{2}}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{2}\tilde{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{2}\tilde{p}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{1}{\tilde{p}^{2}(\tilde{p}+\tilde{k})^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\Big[\big[\tilde{V}^{\dagger}_{\nu}\tilde{V}^{\dagger}_{\mu}+\tilde{V}_{\nu}\tilde{V}_{\mu}\big]\big[-\tilde{p}^{\alpha}(\tilde{p}+\tilde{k})_{\alpha}g^{\mu\nu}+\tilde{p}^{\mu}(\tilde{p}+\tilde{k})^{\nu}\big]

\displaystyle+\tilde{V}^{\dagger}_{\nu}\tilde{V}_{\mu}\big[(\tilde{k}^{2}+2\tilde{p}^{2}+2\tilde{k}_{\mu}\tilde{p}^{\mu})g^{\nu\mu}-(\tilde{k}^{\nu}\tilde{k}^{\mu}+2\tilde{p}^{\nu}\tilde{p}^{\mu}+\tilde{p}^{\nu}\tilde{k}^{\mu}+\tilde{k}^{\nu}\tilde{p}^{\mu})\big]

\displaystyle+\big[\tilde{W}^{\dagger}_{\mu}\tilde{W}^{\dagger}_{\nu}+\tilde{W}_{\nu}\tilde{W}_{\mu}\big]\big[\tilde{p}^{\mu}(\tilde{p}+k)^{\nu}\big]+\tilde{W}^{\dagger}_{\mu}\tilde{W}_{\nu}\big[-(\tilde{k}^{\mu}\tilde{k}^{\nu}+2\tilde{p}^{\mu}\tilde{p}^{\nu})+\tilde{p}^{\mu}\tilde{k}^{\nu}+\tilde{k}^{\mu}\tilde{p}^{\nu}\big]

\displaystyle+\big[\tilde{V}^{\dagger}_{\nu}\tilde{W}^{\dagger}_{\mu}+\tilde{V}_{\nu}\tilde{W}_{\mu}\big]\big[-\varepsilon^{\mu\nu}2\tilde{p}^{\alpha}(\tilde{p}+\tilde{k})_{\alpha}+\varepsilon^{\mu\alpha}2\tilde{p}_{\alpha}(\tilde{p}+\tilde{k})^{\nu}\big]

\displaystyle+\big[\tilde{V}^{\dagger}_{\nu}\tilde{W}_{\mu}+\tilde{V}_{\nu}\tilde{W}^{\dagger}_{\mu}\big]\big[\varepsilon^{\mu\nu}(2\tilde{p}^{2}+\tilde{k}^{2}+2\tilde{p}_{\alpha}\tilde{k}^{\alpha})-\varepsilon^{\mu\alpha}(\tilde{k}_{\alpha}\tilde{k}^{\nu}+2\tilde{p}_{\alpha}\tilde{p}^{\nu}+2\tilde{k}_{\alpha}\tilde{p}^{\nu})\big]\Big].

(216)

Here each factor of

is the Jacobian of the transformation of the two momentum variables.
Collecting the expressions for the traces results in

\displaystyle\Gamma^{(2)}[V,W]

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{i}}}{\det[M]^{2}}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{2}\tilde{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{2}\tilde{p}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{1}{\tilde{p}^{2}(\tilde{p}+\tilde{k})^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\Big[\big[\tilde{V}_{\mu}\tilde{V}_{\nu}^{\dagger}-\tilde{W}_{\mu}\tilde{W}_{\nu}^{\dagger})\big]g^{\mu\nu}\big[(\tilde{p}+\tilde{k})^{2}-\frac{1}{2}(\tilde{k}^{2}+2\tilde{p}^{2}+2\tilde{p}\tilde{k})\big]

Local dynamics and thermal activation in the transverse-field Ising chain
Jiahao Yang, Weishi Yuan, Takashi Imai, Qimiao Si, Jianda Wu, Márton Kormos

Abstract
There has been considerable recent progress in identifying candidate materials for the transverse-field Ising chain (TFIC), a paradigmatic model for quantum criticality. Here, we study the local spin dynamical structure factor of different spin components in the quantum disordered region of the TFIC. We show that the low-frequency local dynamics of the spins in the Ising- and transverse-field directions have strikingly distinctive temperature dependencies. This leads to the thermal-activation gap for the secular term of the NMR 1 / T 2 ′ 1/T_{2}^{\prime} relaxation rate to be half of that for the 1 / T 1 1/T_{1} relaxation rate. Our findings reveal a new surprise in the nonzero-temperature dynamics of the venerable TFIC model and uncover a means to evince the material realization of the TFIC universality.



Introduction.

While classical
matter freezes at zero temperature, quantum
many-body systems often display
multiple ground states due to the competition between different couplings.
Upon a continuous transformation from one ground state to another,
quantum criticality develops.
It is anchored by a quantum critical point (QCP) at zero temperature,
in contrast to a classical critical point that appears at
a thermally-induced phase transition.
Quantum criticality has emerged as a general organizing principle to understand
many of the richest phenomena that have been observed in quantum materials

Special Issue: Quantum Criticality and Novel
Phases 2013
;
Paschen and Si 2021
.
These include the cuprates
Keimer et al. 2015
;
Ramshaw et al. 2015
, heavy fermion metals
Kirchner et al. 2020
;
Coleman and Schofield 2005
;
Löhneysen et al. 2007
and iron pnictides
Dai et al. 2009
;
Licciardello et al. 2019
.
One of the prominent features of quantum criticality is that it mixes spatial and temporal fluctuations.
While this intermixing complicates the description of quantum criticality, it also implies that
dynamical properties can be used to characterize the nature of quantum criticality.
As another outstanding feature of quantum criticality,
approaching the QCP by a non-thermal control parameter (
g
)
and by temperature (
T
) represent two independent ways to examine its universal behavior.
As such, it is instructive to probe quantum criticality by analyzing dynamical properties as a function of temperature,
which can be conveniently studied experimentally.

A paradigmatic model for continuous quantum phase transitions is the transverse field Ising model in one spatial dimension

;

Barouch and McCoy 1971
;
Suzuki 1971
;
Suzuki 1976
;
Kopp and Chakravarty 2005

It represents a prototype setting to explore the properties of quantum criticality.
Yet, in spite of its venerable status, there is much about it that remains to be understood.
Suitable materials to study this model have only been emerging recently

;
Wang et al. 2018

Cui et al. 2019

The model.

The Hamiltonian of the transverse field Ising chain is given by
Pfeuty 1970

H_{I}=-J\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left({{\sigma_{i}^{z}\sigma_{i+1}^{z}}+g{\sigma_{i}^{x}}}\right)\,,

(1)

where
\sigma_{i}^{x}

associated with the spin components
S^{\mu}=\sigma^{\mu}/2,({\mu=x,y,z})\;
on site

Below we shall refer to the

At zero temperature, the system undergoes a quantum phase transition
when the transverse field is tuned across its QCP

The Hamiltonian can be conveniently converted to fermionic operators,

c_{i}
and
c_{i}^{\dagger}
through Jordan-Wigner transformation.
After introducing a Bogoliubov rotation, as described in Appendix
A

the Hamiltonian takes the canonical form
H_{\text{I}}=\sum_{k}\epsilon_{k}(\gamma_{k}^{\dagger}\gamma_{k}-\frac{1}{2})

with single-particle energy dispersion

\epsilon_{k}=2J\sqrt{1+g^{2}-2g\cos k}\,,

(2)

where momentum

is dimensionless in all calculations.
At zero momentum, the gap
\Delta=2J|g-g_{c}|
which vanishes at

In the vicinity of the QCP, the low energy effective description of the system is given by
an Ising field theory obtained
as the scaling limit of the lattice Hamiltonian Eq. (
18

In this limit the lattice spacing goes to zero,
a\to 0,
while

g\to 1

2J(1-g)=\Delta,

The resulting Hamiltonian describes a relativistic field theory of free Majorana fermions with mass
m=\Delta/c^{2}

{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{I}}=\int\mathrm{d}x\left[\hbar c\frac{i}{2}(\psi\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x}-\bar{\psi}\frac{\partial\bar{\psi}}{\partial x})\pm\Delta\frac{i}{2}(\bar{\psi}\psi-\psi\bar{\psi})\right]\,,

(3)

where the sign of second term is

(

) for the paramagnetic (ferromagnetic) regime, corresponding to

(

) in the lattice model.
The field operators are related to the lattice operators as

\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle{\,(-)}}}{{\psi}}\!\!\!\!\!(ja)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2a}}\left(e^{\mp i\pi/4}c_{j}+e^{\pm i\pi/4}c_{j}^{\dagger}\right).

The spectrum of the Ising field theory is built from multi-particle free fermion states with relativistic dispersion
for a single particle

\varepsilon(p)=\sqrt{\Delta^{2}+p^{2}c^{2}}=\Delta\cosh\theta\,,

(4)

where

\theta
is the relativistic rapidity parameter.
The scaling limit of the transverse magnetization operator can be written as

\sigma_{j}^{x}=-2a\,\varepsilon(x=ja)\equiv-2a\,i\,\bar{\psi}(x)\psi(x)\,.

(5)

The field theory operator
\sigma(x)
corresponding to the order parameter is non-local with respect to the fermions
and cannot be expressed in a simple way in terms of the latter.
Assuming the standard field theory normalization, it is related to the lattice magnetization as (
\hbar=c=1
)
Pfeuty 1970

\sigma(x=ja)=2^{-1/24}e^{-1/8}\mathcal{A}^{3/2}a^{-1/8}\sigma^{z}_{j}=\bar{s}J^{1/8}\sigma^{z}_{j}\,,

where
\mathcal{A}=1.2824271291\dots

Dynamic structure factor.

We compute the
local spin dynamical
structure factor (DSF) for frequencies
\omega\ll k_{\text{B}}T

which are relevant to the NMR relaxation rates.
The DSF of the spin component
\alpha
is given by

S^{\alpha\alpha}(\omega,q)=\frac{-2}{1-e^{-\beta\omega}}\mathrm{Im}\,\chi^{\alpha\alpha}(\omega,q)=\\
\sum_{l}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}t\,e^{i\omega t-iqla}\langle\sigma^{\alpha}_{l+1}(t)\sigma^{\alpha}_{1}(0)\rangle_{T}\,,

(7)

where
\beta=(k_{\text{B}}T)^{-1}
, and

\chi^{\alpha\alpha}(\omega,q)
is the dynamical
spin susceptibility at the
transferred energy

\omega
and momentum
q
.
In the field theory, we consider the continuum operators Eqs. (
5
), (
6
)
and the summation over lattice sites is replaced by an integral over
x
.
The local DSF is

S^{\alpha\alpha}(\omega)=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}q}{2\pi}S^{\alpha\alpha}(\omega,q)
.
Both on the lattice and in the field theory,
we proceed with a Lehmann spectral representation.
Using the field theory language,
it reads

S^{\alpha\alpha}(\omega,q)=\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}}\sum_{r,s=0}^{\infty}C^{\alpha\alpha}_{r,s}(\omega,q)

(8)

with
\alpha=x,y,z

sig
and

C^{\alpha\alpha}_{r,s}(\omega,q)=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta_{1}\dots\mathrm{d}\theta_{r}}{(2\pi)^{r}r!}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime}_{1}\dots\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime}_{s}}{(2\pi)^{s}s!}e^{-\beta E_{r}}(2\pi)^{2}\\
\delta(\omega+E_{r}-E_{s})\delta(q+P_{r}-P_{s})\,|\langle\theta_{1}\dots\theta_{r}|\sigma^{\alpha}(0,0)|\theta^{\prime}_{1}\dots\theta^{\prime}_{s}\rangle|^{2}\,,

(9)

where the energy and momentum eigenvalues of the
multiparticle states
|\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{n}\rangle

are

E_{n}=\Delta\sum_{i}^{n}\cosh\theta_{i}
and
P_{n}=(\Delta/c)\sum_{i}^{n}\sinh\theta_{i}

(
c
is re-introduced here and following).
As described in Appendix
B
,
this series is a low-temperature expansion. It has term-by-term divergences that can be
regularized in a linked cluster expansion
Essler and Konik 2009
;
Pozsgay and Takács 2010
,

{S}^{\alpha\alpha}(t,x)=\sum_{r=0,s=0}^{\infty}D^{\alpha\alpha}_{r,s}(t,x),

where the finite terms
D^{\alpha\alpha}_{r,s}
are certain linear combinations
of
C^{\alpha\alpha}_{r,s}

and terms appearing in the expansion of the partition function.

Local transverse DSF in the scaling limit.

We now turn to truncated form factor series results of
S^{xx}(\omega)

in the quantum disordered region with
\omega\ll k_{\text{B}}T\ll\Delta
.
The field theory operator corresponding to the transverse magnetization,
\varepsilon=i\,\bar{\psi}(x)\psi(x)
[cf. Eq. (
5
)],
is quadratic in the fermionic operators, so it only has nonzero form factors between states that
either have an equal number of particles or the particle number difference is 2. In particular,

F_{2}^{\varepsilon}(\theta|\theta^{\prime})\equiv\langle\theta|\varepsilon(0)|\theta^{\prime}\rangle=-i\frac{\Delta}{c}\cosh\left(\frac{\theta-\theta^{\prime}}{2}\right)\,,

(10)

as can be obtained from the plane wave expansion of the fields [cf. Eq. (
19
)] in a straightforward way.

The first term in the series is the vacuum contribution
C_{00}^{xx}=|\langle 0|\varepsilon|0\rangle|^{2}\delta(\omega)\delta(k)
,
i.e. 0 particle - 0 particle (0p - 0p) contribution.
In the field theory this is a divergent contribution
and requires renormalization (e.g. by normal ordering).
However, since we are interested in the finite (but small)
\omega
domain, we ignore this term.
The terms
D_{0,s}^{xx}
and
D_{s,0}^{xx}
contribute to frequencies
\omega\geq s\Delta
outside of our domain of interest,

0<\omega\ll\Delta.

The first contributing term is thus

C_{11}^{xx}(\omega,q)=\int\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta}{2\pi}\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime}}{2\pi}|F_{2}^{\varepsilon}(\theta|\theta^{\prime})|^{2}e^{-\beta\Delta\cosh\theta}(2\pi)^{2}\\
\delta\left[q+\frac{\Delta}{c}(\sinh\theta-\sinh\theta^{\prime})\right]\delta[\omega+\Delta(\cosh\theta-\cosh\theta^{\prime})]\,.

(11)

Then after performing two integrals, the leading behavior of local transverse DSF
at
\omega\ll k_{\text{B}}T\ll\Delta
region is (cf. Appendix
C
)

C_{11}^{xx}(\omega)\approx-\frac{\Delta}{\pi c^{2}}e^{-\frac{\Delta}{k_{\text{B}}T}}\left[\ln\left(\frac{\omega}{4k_{\text{B}}T}\right)-\frac{k_{\text{B}}T}{2\Delta}+\gamma_{\text{E}}\right]\,,

(12)

where
\gamma_{E}
is Euler’s constant.
The main features of the result are the
\sim e^{-\Delta/k_{\text{B}}T}
temperature dependence and the
\sim\ln(\omega/k_{\text{B}}T)
logarithmic frequency dependence.

We have computed
C_{11}^{xx}
, but since in
D_{11}^{xx}=C_{11}^{xx}-\mathcal{Z}_{1}C_{00}^{xx}

the second term contributes to the static
\sim\delta(\omega)
response only,
the obtained result is equal to
D_{11}^{xx}
for any nonzero
\omega
.
The higher particle number contributions
D_{r,s}^{xx}

with
\mathrm{max}(r,s)\geq 2
contain the factor
e^{-\beta E_{r}}\delta(E_{r}-E_{s}+\omega)
,
so they are exponentially suppressed at low temperature
\beta\Delta\gg 1

for frequencies
\omega\ll\Delta
, in particular,

D_{r,s}^{xx}\sim e^{-\mathrm{max}(r,s)\beta\Delta}
.
So we find that the leading order contribution to the local transverse DSF
S^{xx}(\omega)

in the quantum disordered region with
\omega\ll k_{\text{B}}T\ll\Delta
is given by Eq. (
12
).
An alternative derivation of the same result is given in Appendix
C.1
.

Contrasting thermal activations of
S^{xx}
and
S^{zz}
.

Our key result is the different exponential temperature dependence,

e^{-\Delta/(k_{\text{B}}T)}
vs.
e^{-2\Delta/(k_{\text{B}}T)}
,
for
S^{xx}
and
S^{zz}
, respectively.
This is eventually a consequence of the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}:\sigma^{z}\to-\sigma^{z}
symmetry of the model.
For large transverse fields
g\gg 1,
the ground state corresponds to spins pointing in the
x
direction,
so intuitively, excitations in the paramagnetic phase can be thought of as spin flips in the transverse direction generated by
\sigma^{z}
.
This means that
\sigma^{z}
creates and destroys particles that carry the same quantum numbers as the operator itself,
i.e. they are odd under spin reversal in the
z
direction.
As a consequence, the nonzero matrix elements of
\sigma^{z}
in the paramagnetic phase
are between states with different particle number parity
Berg et al. 1979
;
Iorgov et al. 2011
.
For the transverse magnetization
\sigma^{x}
it is the opposite:
its only nonvanishing matrix elements are between states of the same parity because it is quadratic in the fermionic operators.

As discussed in the introduction, at low frequencies
\omega\ll\Delta

only those matrix elements can contribute in the Lehmann-representation where the energies of the two states are close to each other due to the Dirac-delta expressing energy conservation.
This implies that matrix elements between the ground state and
the excited states do not contribute.
Moreover, independently of which state carries the Boltzmann factor, the contribution of the matrix element will be
\sim e^{-n\Delta/(k_{\text{B}}T)}
where
n
is the
larger
of the particle numbers in the two states.
Together with the parity property of
\sigma^{z}
this implies that the leading temperature dependence of the longitudinal DSF is
e^{-2\Delta/(k_{\text{B}}T)}
coming from 1p - 2p contributions
while that of the transverse DSF is
e^{-\Delta/(k_{\text{B}}T)}
coming from 1p - 1p matrix elements.

We note in passing that in the quantum
E_{8}
integrable field theory,
the scaling limit of the Ising spin chain perturbed by a
longitudinal
magnetic field at its QCP (
g=g_{c}=1
),
even the longitudinal DSF features a
e^{-\Delta/(k_{\text{B}}T)}
decay
Wu et al. 2014
.
This is a consequence of the fact that
\sigma^{z}
has nonzero 1p - 1p matrix element
Delfino and Mussardo 1995
as the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry is explicitly broken by the perturbing field.

applied along the

Conclusion.

To conclude, we determined
the leading behavior of the local
transverse

DSF in the quantum disordered region of the TFIC at
small transfer energy
with temperature much smaller than the gap.
It is shown that when the transfer energy is
much smaller than the
temperature the local transverse DSF exhibits
a logarithmic singularity.
We found that the extracted thermal activation gap
from the local
transverse
DSF is half of that for the

longitudinal
one, which can be attributed to the different
parities of
\sigma^{x}
and
\sigma^{z}
in the quantum disordered
region of the TFIC. This sharp contrast can be directly
tested in a proper NMR setup through

1/T_{1}
and
1/T_{2}
relaxation rate measurements.
It is known that the thermodynamical quantity, the Grüneisen
ratio of the TFIC exhibits unique quantum critical behavior
Wu et al. 2018
.
Our work reveals a
sharp contrast of
the temperature dependence of the

transverse
and
longitudinal
local DSFs of the TFIC,
which represents
a unique
and surprising
dynamical feature for the TFIC.
Accessing the universality of the TFIC is a crucial step
toward a realization of the exotic quantum
phenomena such as the new particles in the
quantum

E_{8}
integrable model
Wu et al. 2014
;
Zhang et al. 2020
;
Wang et al. 2021
;
Zou et al. 2021
.
Our work implies that combined measurements of
the dynamical and thermodynamic quantities
provide telltale experimental signs for
the class of TFIC universality.

Appendix A
Diagonalization of the lattice and field theory Hamiltonians

After Jordan–Wigner transformation,
\sigma^{x}_{i}=1-2c^{\dagger}_{i}c_{i}
and

\sigma_{i}^{z}=-\prod\limits_{j<i}{\left({1-2c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j}}\right)}\left({c_{i}+c_{i}^{\dagger}}\right)
,
the TFIC Hamiltonian Eq. (
1
) becomes

H_{\text{I}}=-J\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left[(c_{i}^{\dagger}c_{i+1}+c_{i}^{\dagger}c_{i+1}^{\dagger}+h.c.)+g(1-2c_{i}^{\dagger}c_{i})\right]

(17)

in terms of the fermionic operators
c_{i},c_{i}^{\dagger}.

After Fourier transformation,
c_{j}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{k}c_{k}e^{ikj},

the Hamiltonian is diagonalized by a Bogoliubov rotation

\gamma_{k}=u_{k}c_{k}-iv_{k}c_{-k}^{\dagger}\,,

where

u_{k}=\cos(\theta_{k}/2)\,,v_{k}=\sin(\theta_{k}/2)

with the Bogoliubov angle

After these steps, we arrive at

The field theory Hamiltonian Eq. (

\displaystyle\psi(x,t)

\displaystyle=\phantom{i}\sqrt{\frac{mc}{2}}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta}{2\pi}e^{-\theta/2}\left[\alpha a(\theta)e^{ip_{\theta}x-i\varepsilon_{\theta}t}+\alpha^{*}a^{\dagger}(\theta)e^{-ip_{\theta}x+i\varepsilon_{\theta}t}\right]\,,

(19a)

\displaystyle\bar{\psi}(x,t)

(19b)

where
\alpha=e^{-i\pi/4},
and

\{a(\theta),a^{\dagger}(\theta^{\prime})\}=2\pi\delta(\theta-\theta^{\prime})

Appendix B
Form-factor method and linked cluster expansion

Exploiting the local nature of the transverse magnetization in terms of the fermions, the transverse DSF
S^{xx}(\omega,q)
can be obtained exactly. This is however not true for the longitudinal DSF
S^{zz}(\omega,q).

Still, in both cases one can give a systematic low temperature expansion
Essler and Konik 2009
;
Pozsgay and Takács 2010
. As we are mainly interested in the low-temperature NMR relaxation rates, we first discuss this more general approach, applicable both in the spin chain and in the field theory. We shall use the field theory notations but everything can be translated to the spin chain in a straightforward manner.

Our starting point is the Lehmann spectral representation,

S^{\alpha\alpha}(\omega,q)=\sum_{l}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}t\,e^{i\omega t-iql}\langle\sigma_{l+1}^{\alpha}(t)\sigma^{\alpha}_{1}(0)\rangle_{T}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}}\sum_{n,m}e^{-\beta E_{n}}(2\pi)^{2}\delta(\hbar\omega+E_{n}-E_{m})\delta(q+P_{n}-P_{m})|\langle n|\sigma^{\alpha}_{1}(0)|m\rangle|^{2}\,.

(22)

Using the multiparticle energy eigenstates
1
1

1

More generally, in interacting field theories the basis of asymptotic scattering states is used.

|\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{n}\rangle
in the Lehmann representation (
22
) leads to

S^{\alpha\alpha}(\omega,q)=\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}}\sum_{r,s=0}^{\infty}C^{\alpha\alpha}_{r,s}(\omega,q)

(23)

with
\alpha=x,y,z
and

C^{\alpha\alpha}_{r,s}(\omega,q)=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta_{1}\dots\mathrm{d}\theta_{r}}{(2\pi)^{r}r!}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime}_{1}\dots\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime}_{s}}{(2\pi)^{s}s!}e^{-\beta E_{r}}\\
(2\pi)^{2}\delta(\hbar\omega+E_{r}-E_{s})\delta(q+P_{r}-P_{s})\,|\langle\theta_{1}\dots\theta_{r}|\sigma^{\alpha}(0,0)|\theta^{\prime}_{1}\dots\theta^{\prime}_{s}\rangle|^{2}\,,

(24)

where the energy and momentum eigenvalues are

E_{n}=mc^{2}\sum_{i}^{n}\cosh\theta_{i}
and
P_{n}=mc\sum_{i}^{n}\sinh\theta_{i}.

This series is a low-temperature expansion in the following sense. The term
C_{r,s}
(we omit the
αα
superscript)
carries a factor
e^{-\beta E_{r}}<e^{-r\beta m}
, thus the small expansion parameter is
e^{-\beta m}.
Its dependence on
s
is
less obvious but note that thanks to the energy conserving Dirac-delta,
the energies of the two states with
r
and
s
particles are related. For a fixed frequency
\omega
and at low temperature one can truncate the double sum in both
r
and
s
. The partition function can also be written as

\mathcal{Z}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{Z}_{n}
where
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
has a factor of

e^{-\beta E_{n}}
.

Now the main problem to be solved is the
regularization of the singularities present in the partition function
and in the matrix elements (form factors) of the operators in question.
In infinite volume all
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
are singular due to
the fact that they contain a scalar product of two momentum
eigenstates. Similarly,
C_{r,s}
inherits the
kinematical poles of the form factors whenever two rapidities in the
two sets coincide, i.e.
\theta_{i}=\theta^{\prime}_{j}
for some
i,j
. Since the structure factor is a well-defined physical quantity, these singularities must cancel each other. In order to make this manifest we reshuffle the infinite series in a linked cluster expansion
Essler and Konik 2009
;
Pozsgay and Takács 2010

\chi^{\alpha\alpha}(t,x)=\sum_{r=0,s=0}^{\infty}D^{\alpha\alpha}_{r,s}(t,x)\,,

(25)

where the terms

\displaystyle D_{0,s}

\displaystyle=C_{0,s}\,,

(26)

\displaystyle D_{1,s}

\displaystyle=C_{1,s}-\mathcal{Z}_{1}C_{0,s-1}\,,

(27)

\displaystyle D_{2,s}

\displaystyle=C_{2,s}-\mathcal{Z}_{1}C_{1,s-1}+(\mathcal{Z}_{1}^{2}-\mathcal{Z}_{2})C_{0,s-2}+\dots

(28)

are supposed to be finite, and equivalent relations hold with the
indices interchanged. In order to obtain a finite result one needs to
regularize the divergencies either in a continuum scheme by adding
infinitesimal imaginary parts to the rapidities
Essler and Konik 2009
, or by
going to a large but finite volume
L
that satisfies
1\ll mL\ll e^{m\beta}
, so that the density of thermally excited particles is
small
Essler and Konik 2009
;
Pozsgay and Takács 2010
. The singularities manifest themselves as
positive powers of
L
, while the final result for the
D_{r,s}
should be
\sim\mathcal{O}(L^{0})
.
All the
D_{1n}(t,x)
terms are given for any massive relativistic
diagonal scattering theory in Ref.
Pozsgay and Takács 2010
, while the general
expression for
D_{22}(t,x)
can be found in Ref.
Szécsényi and Takács 2012
.

The resulting series still can have diverging terms, signalling that the
zero temperature quantity is already singular. This happens around the single particle
dispersion relation
\hbar\omega\sim\epsilon(k)
, where the zero temperature DSF is
proportional to an on-shell Dirac-delta which broadens at non-zero
temperatures
Essler and Konik 2009
.
In these cases a resummation of infinitely many terms is necessary in order to obtain the finite result.
However, if we are interested in the small-
\omega
behavior in
the disordered phase of the Ising model then due to
\hbar\omega\ll mc^{2}
we are far from the mass shell.
In this case the individual terms are not singular and the truncated series should give
a good approximation.

Appendix C
Detailed field theory calculation of local transverse DSF

In this appendix we provide the details of the calculations leading to Eq. (
12
).
Our starting expression is Eq. (
11
) from the main text,

C_{11}(\omega,q)=\int\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta}{2\pi}\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime}}{2\pi}|F_{2}^{\varepsilon}(\theta|\theta^{\prime})|^{2}e^{-\beta m\cosh\theta}(2\pi)^{2}\delta[q+m(\sinh\theta-\sinh\theta^{\prime})]\delta[\omega+m(\cosh\theta-\cosh\theta^{\prime})]\,.

(29)

Both integrals can be performed using the Dirac-delta constraints. The system of equations can be brought to the following form in terms of
x=e^{\theta^{\prime}}
and
y=e^{\theta}

\displaystyle x-y

\displaystyle=\tilde{\omega}+\tilde{p}\equiv A\,,

(30a)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{x}-\frac{1}{y}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\omega}-\tilde{p}\equiv B\,,

(30b)

where
\tilde{\omega}=\omega/m,\tilde{q}=q/m.
This leads to the two solutions
\{x_{+},y_{+}\}
and
\{x_{-},y_{-}\}

\displaystyle x_{\pm}

\displaystyle=\frac{AB\pm\sqrt{AB(AB-4)}}{2B}\,,

(31a)

\displaystyle y_{\pm}

\displaystyle=\frac{-AB\pm\sqrt{AB(AB-4)}}{2B}\,.

(31b)

These roots must be real and positive, so their product must be positive, implying

x_{+}y_{+}=x_{-}y_{-}=-x_{+}x_{-}=-\frac{A}{B}=\frac{\tilde{q}+\tilde{w}}{\tilde{q}-\tilde{w}}>0\,,

(32)

so
\tilde{q}^{2}-\tilde{w}^{2}>0.
Their sum is also positive, leading to

x_{\pm}+y_{\pm}=\frac{\pm\sqrt{AB(AB-4)}}{B}\,,

(33)

so only the
``+"
solution is valid for
B=\tilde{\omega}-\tilde{q}>0
and only the
``-"
solution is valid for
B=\tilde{\omega}-\tilde{q}<0.
Note that since
AB<0,
the expression under the square root is always positive so the reality condition is automatically satisfied. Summarizing,

\displaystyle e^{\theta_{\pm}}

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{2}-\omega^{2}\pm\sqrt{(q^{2}-\omega^{2})(q^{2}-\omega^{2}+4m^{2})}}{2m(\omega-q)}\,,

(34)

\displaystyle e^{\theta^{\prime}_{\pm}}

\displaystyle=\frac{-q^{2}+\omega^{2}\pm\sqrt{(q^{2}-\omega^{2})(q^{2}-\omega^{2}+4m^{2})}}{2m(\omega-q)}\,,

(35)

where the
"+"
roots are valid for
q<-|\omega|
and the
"-"
roots are valid for
q>|\omega|.

The Jacobian of the change of variables

\{m(\cosh\theta^{\prime}-\cosh\theta),m(\sinh\theta^{\prime}-\sinh\theta)\}\to\{\theta,\theta^{\prime}\}

(36)

is
m^{2}\sinh(\theta-\theta^{\prime}).
Conveniently,

\cosh(\theta-\theta^{\prime})=1+(q^{2}-\omega^{2})/(2m^{2})\,,\qquad\cosh\theta_{\pm}=\left(-\omega\mp q\sqrt{1+4m^{2}/(q^{2}-\omega^{2})}\right)/(2m)\,.

(37)

Using
|F^{(\varepsilon)}(\theta|\theta^{\prime})|^{2}=m^{2}[1+\cosh(\theta-\theta^{\prime})]/2

we obtain

C_{11}(\omega,q)=e^{-m\beta\cosh\theta_{\pm}}\frac{q^{2}-\omega^{2}+4m^{2}}{2\sqrt{(q^{2}-\omega^{2})(q^{2}-\omega^{2}+4m^{2})}}=\frac{1}{2}e^{\beta\omega/2}\sqrt{1+\frac{4m^{2}}{q^{2}-\omega^{2}}}e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}|q|\sqrt{1+\frac{4m^{2}}{q^{2}-\omega^{2}}}}\,,

(38)

where we used
\pm q=-|q|.

The local dynamic structure factor is given by

C_{11}(\omega)=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}q}{2\pi}C_{11}(\omega,q)=2\int_{|\omega|}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}q}{2\pi}C_{11}(\omega,q)\,.

(39)

Note that as long as
\omega
is non-zero, the exponential cuts off
the diverging prefactor and the integrand remains finite. This is no longer true for
\omega=0
which signals a logarithmic singularity.
We can extract the leading behavior in the small-
\omega
(and low-
T
) limit as

\begin{split}&C_{11}(\omega)\approx\frac{1}{2}e^{\beta\omega/2}\int_{|\omega|}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}q}{\pi}\left(\frac{2m}{q}+\frac{q}{4m}\right)e^{-\frac{\beta m}{2}\left(\frac{q^{2}}{4m^{2}}+2+\frac{\omega^{2}}{q^{2}}\right)}\\
&\approx\frac{1}{2}e^{\beta\omega/2}\int_{\omega}^{p_{m}}\frac{\mathrm{d}q}{\pi}\left(\frac{2m}{q}+\frac{q}{4m}\right)e^{-\frac{\beta m}{2}\left(2+\frac{\omega^{2}}{q^{2}}\right)}+\frac{1}{2}e^{\beta\omega/2}\int_{p_{m}}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}q}{\pi}\left(\frac{2m}{q}+\frac{q}{4m}\right)e^{-\frac{\beta m}{2}\left(\frac{q^{2}}{4m^{2}}+2\right)}\\
&=\frac{e^{\beta\omega/2}e^{-m\beta}}{32\pi}\left\{\frac{2\omega}{m}e^{-\frac{\beta m(m+\omega)}{2m+\omega}}\left(e^{\frac{\beta m}{2}}(2m+\omega)-\omega e^{\frac{\beta m\omega}{4m+2\omega}}\right)+\left(16m-\beta\omega^{2}\right)\left[\Gamma\left(0,\frac{m\beta\omega}{4m+2\omega}\right)-\Gamma\left(0,\frac{m\beta}{2}\right)\right]\right\}\\
&\approx-\frac{m}{\pi}e^{\beta\omega/2}e^{-m\beta}\left[\ln\left(\frac{\beta\omega}{4}\right)-\frac{1}{2m\beta}+\gamma_{\text{E}}\right]\,,\end{split}

(40)

where
the incomplete gamma function
\Gamma(a,z)=\int_{z}^{\infty}{{t^{a-1}}{e^{-t}}dt}
,
Euler’s constant
\gamma_{\text{E}}\approx 0.57712
, and
p_{m}=\sqrt{\omega(2m+\omega)}
is the extreme point of the exponent.

C.1
Alternative derivation of the local transverse DSF

Another way to obtain the result in Eq. (

) is to focus on the

DSF from the start, defined as

(41)

This contains a disconnected piece proportional to
\delta(\omega).
The first nontrivial term in the expansion of the connected part is

C_{11}(\omega)=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta}{2\pi}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime}}{2\pi}|F_{2}^{\varepsilon}(\theta|\theta^{\prime})|^{2}e^{-\beta m\cosh\theta}2\pi\delta[\omega+m(\cosh\theta-\cosh\theta^{\prime})]\,.

Let us assume that
\omega>0
so the energy conservation condition has two real solutions
\theta_{+}
and
\theta_{-}=-\theta_{+}
for all
\theta,
where we denote the positive solution by

\theta_{+}=\mathrm{arccosh}(\cosh\theta+\omega/m)>0\,.

(43)

C_{11}(\omega)=m\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta}{2\pi}\frac{e^{-\beta m\cosh\theta}}{\sqrt{(\cosh\theta+\omega/m)^{2}-1}}[1+\cosh\theta(\cosh\theta+\omega/m)]\,,

To extract the small-

\begin{split}C_{11}(\omega)&\approx\frac{m}{\pi}\int_{1}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}u\,e^{-m\beta u}\frac{u^{2}+1}{(u+1)\sqrt{(u-1)(u-1+\tilde{w})}}\approx\frac{m}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}v\,e^{-m\beta(1+v)}\frac{1+v/2}{\sqrt{v(v+\tilde{w})}}\\

In this appendix we present the form factor calculation, analogous to that in App.

The transverse magnetization on the lattice is given in terms of the Jordan–Wigner fermions as

\sigma_{j}^{x}=1-2c^{\dagger}_{j}c_{j}\,.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}\sum_{j=1}^{L}c_{j}e^{ikj}=c(k)

\displaystyle=\cos(\vartheta_{k}/2)\alpha(k)+i\sin(\vartheta_{k}/2)\alpha^{\dagger}(-k)\,,

(49a)

(49b)

with

(50)

\sigma^{x}_{j}

(51)

We can also compute the matrix elements

\displaystyle\langle 0|\sigma^{x}_{j}|k,k^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle\equiv\langle 0|\sigma^{x}_{j}\,\alpha^{\dagger}_{k}\alpha^{\dagger}_{k^{\prime}}|0\rangle=\phantom{-}\frac{2i}{L}e^{-i(k+k^{\prime})j}\sin\left(\frac{\vartheta_{k}-\vartheta_{k^{\prime}}}{2}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\equiv\langle 0|\alpha_{k}\,\sigma^{x}_{j}\,\alpha^{\dagger}_{k^{\prime}}|0\rangle=-\frac{2}{L}e^{i(k-k^{\prime})j}\cos\left(\frac{\vartheta_{k}+\vartheta_{k^{\prime}}}{2}\right)+\delta_{k,k^{\prime}}\left\langle\sigma^{x}\right\rangle\,.

(53)

The first contributions to the local DSF in the Lehmann representation are

\displaystyle C_{00}(\omega)

\displaystyle=2\pi\delta(\omega)\left\langle\sigma^{x}\right\rangle^{2}\,,

(54)

\displaystyle C_{11}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,m}e^{-\beta\varepsilon_{n}}2\pi\delta(\omega+\varepsilon_{n}-\varepsilon_{m})\left[\delta(\omega)\left\langle\sigma^{x}\right\rangle^{2}\delta_{n,m}+\left(\frac{2}{L}\right)^{2}\cos^{2}\left(\frac{\vartheta_{k_{n}}-\vartheta_{k_{m}}}{2}\right)-\delta_{n,m}\frac{4}{L}\cos\left(\frac{\vartheta_{k_{n}}-\vartheta_{k_{m}}}{2}\right)\right]\,,

Transverse DSF

The transverse DSF follows by

\begin{split}S^{xx}(\omega,q)&=\sum_{l=1}^{N}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}\mathrm{d}te^{i\omega t-iqla}\cdot\left[\left\langle\sigma^{x}_{l}(t)\sigma^{x}_{0}\right\rangle_{T}-\langle\sigma^{x}_{l}(t)\rangle_{T}\langle\sigma^{x}_{0}\rangle_{T}\right]\,,\end{split}

In the following we set the lattice spacing as

Starting from

\begin{split}S^{xx}(\omega,q)&=\frac{4}{N}\sum_{k}\left[2\pi\delta(\omega-\epsilon_{k+q/2}+\epsilon_{k-q/2})\cdot(u_{k+q/2}^{2}u_{k-q/2}^{2}-u_{k+q/2}v_{k+q/2}u_{k-q/2}v_{k-q/2})\cdot(1-n_{k+q/2})n_{k-q/2}\right.\\
&\left.\quad+2\pi\delta(\omega+\epsilon_{k+q/2}+\epsilon_{k-q/2})\cdot(u_{k-q/2}^{2}v_{k+q/2}^{2}+u_{k+q/2}v_{k+q/2}u_{k-q/2}v_{k-q/2})\cdot n_{k+q/2}n_{k-q/2}\right.\\
&\left.\quad+2\pi\delta(\omega-\epsilon_{k+q/2}-\epsilon_{k-q/2})\cdot(u_{k+q/2}^{2}v_{k-q/2}^{2}+u_{k+q/2}v_{k+q/2}u_{k-q/2}v_{k-q/2})\cdot(1-n_{k+q/2})(1-n_{k-q/2})\right.\\
&\left.\quad+2\pi\delta(\omega+\epsilon_{k+q/2}-\epsilon_{k-q/2})\cdot(v_{k+q/2}^{2}v_{k-q/2}^{2}-u_{k+q/2}v_{k+q/2}u_{k-q/2}v_{k-q/2})\cdot n_{k+q/2}(1-n_{k-q/2})\right]\\
&=\int^{\pi}_{-\pi}\mathrm{d}k\left[2({1+f(k,q)})\cdot\delta(\omega-\epsilon_{k-q/2}+\epsilon_{k+q/2})\cdot(1-n_{k-q/2})n_{k+q/2}+({1-f(k,q)})\right.\\

(65)

with Fermi distribution function

n_{k}=\left[e^{\epsilon_{k}/(k_{\text{B}}T)}+1\right]^{-1}
and

\begin{split}f(k,q)&=4J^{2}\left({[g-\cos(k-q/2)][g-\cos(k+q/2)]}-{\sin(k-q/2)\sin(k+q/2)}\right)/(\epsilon_{k-q/2}\epsilon_{k+q/2})\,.\end{split}

Here we have taken advantage of particle and energy conservation during the derivation,
for example

\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}\mathrm{d}te^{i\omega t}\langle e^{iHt}\gamma_{k-q/2}\gamma_{k+q/2}^{\dagger}e^{-iHt}\gamma_{k+q/2}\gamma_{k-q/2}^{\dagger}\rangle_{T}=2\pi\delta(\omega+\epsilon_{k+q/2}-\epsilon_{k-q/2})(1-n_{{k-q/2}})n_{{k+q/2}}.

\omega>0

Appendix F
Detailed field theory calculation of longitudinal DSF

In this section we turn to the DSF of the order parameter
field.
This operator is highly nonlocal in terms of the Jordan–Wigner fermions prohibiting an exact calculation based on free fermion techniques. However, one can still use the truncated form factor series approach.
This approach has been used in the study of local spin DSF and NMR relaxation rate
1/T_{1}
in Refs.
Wu et al. 2014
;
Steinberg et al. 2019
.
The calculation of the form factors of
\sigma^{z}
are far from being trivial, but are known exactly even on the finite spin chain
Iorgov et al. 2011
.
Here we perform
the
calculation in the paramagnetic phase
in the scaling limit, focusing on the DSF of the continuum spin operator
\sigma(x)
in Eq. (
6
).
In the disordered phase, the
\sigma(x)
operator creates and destroys particles, so its only non-zero matrix elements are between states with particle numbers of different parity, that is, the total number of particles in the two states must be odd.
The vacuum form factors are given by
Berg et al. 1979

F^{\sigma}_{n}(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{n})=\langle 0|\sigma|\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{n}\rangle=\bar{\sigma}\sum_{i<j}\tanh\left(\frac{\theta_{i}-\theta_{j}}{2}\right)

(79)

with
\bar{\sigma}=\bar{s}m^{1/8}
where
\bar{s}
is defined in Eq. (
6
)
and we work with
\hbar=c=1
for the following field theory calculation.
All other matrix elements can be obtained by the crossing relation.
For example,

\langle\theta|\sigma|\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{n}\rangle=\langle 0|\sigma|\theta+i\pi,\theta_{2},\dots,\theta_{n}\rangle\,,

(80)

whenever
\theta\neq\theta_{j}
which will be the case in
our calculations.

Thus the first contributions to the DSF come from
D_{01}(t,x)=C_{01}(t,x)
and
D_{10}(t,x)=C_{10}(t,x)
, yielding

S^{zz}_{01}(\omega,q)=\bar{\sigma}^{2}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta}{2\pi}(2\pi)^{2}\delta(\omega-m\cosh\theta)\delta(q-m\sinh\theta)\,,

(81)

and
S_{10}(\omega,q)=e^{\beta\omega}S_{01}(-\omega,-q).

Due to the energy conserving Dirac-delta, both
S_{10}
and
S_{01}
are zero for
\omega<m
. It is clear that all

D_{0,s}
and
D_{r,0}
will also give zero contribution, which reflects the fact that
the zero temperature result is identically zero.

Energy conservation at small frequencies also leads to a great simplification in higher orders, similarly to the case of the transverse magnetization in the previous sections.
Because of the Dirac-delta and
\omega\approx 0
the two states in each
matrix element must have almost equal energies, so

S_{r,s}(\omega\approx 0,q)\sim e^{-\mathrm{max}(r,s)\cdot\beta m}
. This
implies that the classification in terms of orders of
e^{-\beta m}
is simplified, because in every order there is only a finite number of terms. For instance, in the second order one has
S_{12}+S_{21}
, in the third order
S_{23}+S_{32}
, in the fourth
S_{14}+S_{41}+S_{34}+S_{43}
, and so on.

Thus up to the second order one needs only two terms,
S_{12}
and
S_{21}
. We use the expression for
D_{12}
given in Ref.
Essler and Konik 2009
that can be shown to be equivalent to the more general formula in Ref.
Pozsgay and Takács 2010
,

\begin{split}D_{12}&=\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta}{2\pi}\int_{C_{+}}\int_{C_{-}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta_{1}}{2\pi}\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta_{2}}{2\pi}|F_{3}^{\sigma}(\theta+i\pi,\theta_{1},\theta_{2})|^{2}e^{-\beta m\cosh\theta}\\
&\times 2\pi\delta[q+m(\sinh\theta-\sinh\theta_{1}-\sinh\theta_{2})]2\pi\delta[\omega+m(\cosh\theta-\cosh\theta_{1}-\cosh\theta_{2})]\\
&-\bar{\sigma}^{2}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta}{2\pi}e^{-m\beta\cosh\theta}2\pi\delta(\omega-m\cosh\theta)2\pi\delta(q-m\sinh\theta)\,,\end{split}

(82)

where the contours
C_{\pm}
are running above and below the real axis, respectively, to avoid the kinematical poles of the form factors. But
\theta=\theta_{i}

(i=1,2)
is impossible for
\omega<m
, so the integrals avoid the poles even for real rapidities and there is no need to shift the contours off the real axis.
The last term is proportional to
D_{01}
so it does not contribute for
\omega<m
and
we are left with

\begin{split}S^{zz}_{12}(\omega,q)&=\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta}{2\pi}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta_{1}}{2\pi}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta_{2}}{2\pi}|F_{3}^{\sigma^{z}}(\theta+i\pi,\theta_{1},\theta_{2})|^{2}e^{-\beta m\cosh\theta}\\
&\times 2\pi\delta[q-m(\sinh\theta_{1}+\sinh\theta_{2}-\sinh\theta)]2\pi\delta[\omega-m(\cosh\theta_{1}+\cosh\theta_{2}-\cosh\theta)]\,.\end{split}

(83)

Exploiting the Dirac-deltas we perform the integrals over rapidities
\theta_{1,2}.
The Jacobian of the transformation is
m^{2}\sinh(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}).
The set of two constraint equations coming from the Dirac-deltas has two solutions,
\{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\}=\{\theta_{+},\theta_{-}\}
and
\{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\}=\{\theta_{-},\theta_{+}\}
, where

\theta_{\pm}=\log\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(\omega_{\theta}+q_{\theta}\pm\frac{\sqrt{(\omega_{\theta}^{2}-p_{\theta}^{2})(\omega_{\theta}^{2}-q_{\theta}^{2}-4)}}{\omega_{\theta}-q_{\theta}}\right)\right]

(84)

with
q_{\theta}\equiv q/m+\sinh\theta,\omega_{\theta}\equiv\omega/m+\cosh\theta.
The rapidities
\theta_{1,2}
must be real which gives restrictions on the remaining rapidity
\theta.
The reality condition of
\theta_{1,2}
is equivalent to the condition that
e^{\theta_{1}}+e^{\theta_{2}}
and
e^{\theta_{1}}\,e^{\theta_{2}}
must be positive, which gives
\omega_{\theta}+q_{\theta}>0,\,\omega_{\theta}-q_{\theta}>0.

Moreover, the combination under the square root must also be positive,
\omega_{\theta}^{2}-q_{\theta}^{2}>4.
One of the first two conditions, e.g.
\omega_{\theta}-q_{\theta}>0
can then be dropped which leaves us with two conditions. The solution of
\omega_{\theta}^{2}-q_{\theta}^{2}>4
for
|\omega|<m
is the following:

\displaystyle\theta

\displaystyle<\theta^{(-)}

\displaystyle\qquad|\omega|

\displaystyle<q\,,

(85a)

\displaystyle\theta

\displaystyle>\theta^{(+)}

\displaystyle\qquad-|\omega|

\displaystyle>q\,,

(85b)

\displaystyle\theta

\displaystyle<\theta^{(-)}\;\text{ or }\;\theta>\theta^{(+)}

\displaystyle\qquad-\omega

\displaystyle<q<\omega\,,

(85c)

and for
\omega<q<-\omega
(
\omega<0
) there is no solution. Here

\theta^{(\pm)}=\log\left[\frac{q^{2}-\omega^{2}+3m^{2}\pm\sqrt{(q^{2}-\omega^{2}+m^{2})(q^{2}-\omega^{2}+9m^{2})}}{2m(\omega-q)}\right].

(86)

It turns out that the other condition,
\omega_{\theta}+q_{\theta}>0
, is automatically satisfied, so Eqs. (
85
) give the integration domain of
\theta
in the various cases depending on
\omega
and
q.

Thus we find

S^{zz}_{12}(\omega,q)=2\int_{D}\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta}{2\pi}\frac{e^{-\beta m\cosh\theta}|F_{3}^{\sigma}[\theta+i\pi,\theta_{+}(\theta),\theta_{-}(\theta)]|^{2}}{m^{2}\sqrt{(\omega_{\theta}^{2}-q_{\theta}^{2})(\omega_{\theta}^{2}-q_{\theta}^{2}-4)}}\,,

(87)

where
D
denotes the domain given in Eqs. (
85
) and we used that
\sinh(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})=\sqrt{(\omega_{\theta}^{2}-q_{\theta}^{2})(\omega_{\theta}^{2}-q_{\theta}^{2}-4)}/2.

It is easy to see that
S_{21}(\omega,q)=e^{\beta\omega}S_{12}(-\omega,q),
so we have the total leading
\mathcal{O}(e^{-2m\beta})
contribution to the DSF.
The result

is plotted in Fig.

.

Appendix G
Exact transverse DSF in the field theory

Using the plane wave expansion Eq. (
19
) and
\varepsilon=i\bar{\psi}\psi,
the connected correlation function

C^{\varepsilon}(x,t)=\langle\varepsilon(x,t)\varepsilon(0,0)\rangle_{T}-\langle\varepsilon(x,t)\rangle\langle\varepsilon(0,0)\rangle_{T}

(90)

can be written as a four-fold rapidity integral of a linear combination of thermal expectation values of products of four creation/annihilation operators. Using the thermal Wick’s theorem,

\langle a^{\pm}(\theta)b_{1}b_{2}b_{3}\rangle_{T}=f^{\pm}(\theta)\Big(\{a,b_{1}\}\langle b_{2}b_{3}\rangle_{T}-\{a,b_{2}\}\langle b_{1}b_{3}\rangle_{T}+\{a,b_{3}\}\langle b_{1}b_{2}\rangle_{T}\Big)\,,

(91)

where
a^{+}=a^{\dagger},a^{-}=a,
and
f^{+}(\theta)=(1+e^{\beta\varepsilon_{\theta}})^{-1}=f(\theta),

f^{-}(\theta)=(1+e^{-\beta\varepsilon_{\theta}})^{-1}=1-f(\theta),
one arrives at

\begin{split}C^{\varepsilon}(x,t)&=\frac{m^{2}}{4}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta}{2\pi}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime}}{2\pi}\left[f(\theta)f(\theta^{\prime})(e^{\theta-\theta^{\prime}}-1)e^{i(p+p^{\prime})\cdot x}+(1-f(\theta))(1-f(\theta^{\prime}))(e^{\theta-\theta^{\prime}}-1)e^{-i(p+p^{\prime})\cdot x}\right.\\
&\left.+f(\theta)(1-f(\theta^{\prime}))(e^{\theta-\theta^{\prime}}+1)e^{i(p-p^{\prime})\cdot x}+(1-f(\theta))f(\theta^{\prime})(e^{\theta-\theta^{\prime}}+1)e^{-i(p-p^{\prime})\cdot x}\right]\,,\end{split}

(92)

where we used the Lorentz product notation,
p\cdot x=\varepsilon t-px.

At zero temperature
T=0,

f(\theta)=0
and we obtain the closed form result

\begin{split}C(x,t)&=\frac{m^{2}}{4}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta}{2\pi}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime}}{2\pi}(e^{\theta-\theta^{\prime}}-1)e^{-i(p+p^{\prime})\cdot x}=\frac{m^{2}}{4}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta}{2\pi}e^{\theta}e^{-ip\cdot x}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime}}{2\pi}e^{-\theta}e^{-ip^{\prime}\cdot x}-\frac{m^{2}}{4}\left(\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta}{2\pi}e^{-ip\cdot x}\right)^{2}\\
&=m^{2}K_{0}^{2}\left(m\sqrt{x^{2}-t^{2}}\right)-m^{2}K_{1}^{2}\left(m\sqrt{x^{2}-t^{2}}\right)\,.\end{split}

(93)

Note that since
(p+p^{\prime})\cdot x=(\varepsilon_{\theta}+\varepsilon_{\theta^{\prime}})t-(p_{\theta}+p_{\theta^{\prime}})x
and
\varepsilon_{\theta}+\varepsilon_{\theta^{\prime}}\geq 2\Delta,
after Fourier transformation
S(\omega,q)|_{T=0}=0
for
0<\omega<2\Delta.

At low temperature, the leading order can be obtained by approximating
f(\theta)\approx e^{-\beta\varepsilon(\theta)}
and keeping only first powers of
f(\theta):

\begin{split}C(x,t)&\approx\frac{m^{2}}{4}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta}{2\pi}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime}}{2\pi}\left[(1-f(\theta)-f(\theta^{\prime}))(e^{\theta-\theta^{\prime}}-1)e^{-i(p+p^{\prime})\cdot x}\right.\\
&\left.+f(\theta)(e^{\theta-\theta^{\prime}}+1)e^{i(p-p^{\prime})\cdot x}+f(\theta^{\prime})(e^{\theta-\theta^{\prime}}+1)e^{-i(p-p^{\prime})x}\right]\,.\end{split}

(94)

Taking the Fourier transform, for frequencies
|\omega|<m
only the second line gives nonzero contribution and it recovers the expression Eq. (
11
).

Appendix H

In this appendix we report the details of the asymptotic analysis of Eq. (

\begin{split}S^{xx}(\omega)&\approx\int_{q_{c}}^{\pi}\frac{dq}{\pi}\ \left|\frac{\sqrt{1+g^{2}-2g\cos\frac{q}{2}}}{gJ\sin\frac{q}{2}}\right|\,e^{-\epsilon_{k_{-}+q/2}/(k_{\text{B}}T)}\\

Foldy-Wouthuysen transformation for gapped Dirac fermions in two-dimensional semiconducting materials and valley excitons under external fields
Yao-Wen Chang, Yia-Chung Chang

Abstract
In this work, we provide a detailed derivation of Foldy-Wouthuysen (FW) transformation for two-dimensional (2D) gapped Dirac fermions under external fields and apply the formalism to study valley excitons in 2D semiconducting materials. Similar to relativistic quantum few-body problem, the gapped Dirac equation can be transformed into a Schrödinger equation with ”relativistic” correction terms. In this 2D materials system, the correction terms can be interpreted as the Berry-curvature effect. The Hamiltonian for a valley exciton in external fields can be written based on the FW transformed Dirac Hamiltonian. Various valley-dependent effects on excitons, such as fine-structure splittings of exciton energy levels, valley-selected exciton transitions, and exciton valley Zeeman effect are discussed within this framework.

While there are a lot of theoretical works using different methods to study different
issues of valley excitons, the connection among different theories and interpretations is
not manifest.

One of theoretical issues to study valley excitons is to include external-field
interactions to the exciton model. For a Wannier exciton, which consists of an
electron and a hole bound by the Coulomb interaction, the system is
similar to a hydrogen atom in external fields. The electric-field interaction
can be included by the dipole-field interaction and the magnetic-field interaction can be
included as the vector fields in the kinetic momentums of the electron and hole. For
the valley excitons in 2D materials, the exciton Hamiltonian is constructed by the
Bloch-electron wavefunctions solved from diagonalizing a Dirac Hamiltonian or a few-band
tight-binding Hamiltonian
berkelbach2015bright
;
berkelbach2017optical
. However, the
Dirac Hamiltonian or tight-binding Hamiltonian with including external-field interactions
is much more difficult to solve, such that the exciton Hamiltonian under external field
also becomes more difficult to derive. To simplify the problem,
approximations are necessary. In fact, a similar problem had been intensively studied in the fields of relativistic few-body
physics
bethe2012quantum
;
reiher2014relativistic
. A relativistic particle which is
described by a three-dimensional Dirac fermion under external fields can be reduced to a
nonrelativistic particle with relativistic corrections in low-energy limit by the
Foldy-Wouthuysen (FW) transformation
reiher2014relativistic
;
foldy1950dirac
;
eriksen1958foldy
;
silenko2008foldy
;
silenko2016general
. A relativistic few-body problem,
such as a real hydrogen atom in external fields, can be studied by solving the
nonrelativistic Hamiltonian with including the relativistic
corrections
close1970relativistic
;
krajcik1974relativistic
;
anthony1994relativistic
.
The approximation scheme can achieve extreme accuracy in prediction of energy spectra and
fine structures. Following the same strategy, the 2D Dirac fermions can also be reduced to nonrelativistic form
by the FW transformation, and the valley-exciton Hamiltonian can be approximated as the
Wannier-exciton Hamiltonian with band-geometry corrections. The valley excitons in
external fields can then be studied by the corrected exciton Hamiltonian.

Semiclassical methods have been used to study the band-structure correction to the
properties of excitons

;
yao2008valley
;
yao2008berry
;
chang2008berry
;
gradhand2012first

;

;

;
zhou2015berry
;
trushin2018model
;
van2019spectrum
;
yong2019valley
and anomalous selection rule

;

Hamiltonian can be derived from a FW transformation of 2D gapped Dirac
fermions

;
trushin2018model
. However, the joint effect of the
Berry-curvature effect and external fields has yet to be discussed. We intend to fill
this gap by extending the derivation of FW transformation to incorporate both effects for 2D gapped Dirac
fermions in external fields.

In the present work, the Bloch electron in 2D materials under external fields is described
by a 2D gapped Dirac model, and the FW transformation is used to derive the electron-hole
representation of the Dirac fermion. The FW transformed single-particle Hamiltonian,
two-particle interaction, and interband transition are derived. Hamiltonians for valley
excitons in external fields, including an in-plane electric field, an in-plane
electromagnetic field, and an out-of-plane magnetic field can be written. The formalism
is applied to the study of physical properties of valley excitons. Within this theoretical
framework, several known valley-dependent excitonic effects including exciton energy-level
splittings, valley-selected exciton transitions, and exciton valley Zeeman effect are
studied and discussed.

II.2
Electromagnetic-field interaction

The electromagnetic-field interaction with the Dirac fermion can be included by extending
the kinetic momentum

\displaystyle\bm{\pi}^{\star}(t)=\bm{\pi}-q\int\tilde{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}(\mathbf{k},t)e^{\mathtt{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}}\frac{\text{d}^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{2}},

(4)

where

\mathbf{k}

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}(\mathbf{k},t)e^{\mathtt{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}}\simeq\tilde{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}(\mathbf{k},t)\left(1+\mathtt{i}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}+\cdots\right)\simeq\tilde{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}(\mathbf{k},t).

(5)

Including the electromagnetic-field interaction, the electronic Hamiltonian can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{\star}(t)=\hat{\mathcal{H}}-\hat{\bm{j}}\cdot\bm{\mathcal{A}}(t),

(6)

where

(7)

It can be shown that the polarization operator can be related to the
current operator by

\displaystyle\frac{\text{d}\hat{\bm{\xi}}}{\text{d}t}=\mathtt{i}\left[\hat{\mathcal{H}},\;\hat{\bm{\xi}}\right]=\hat{\bm{j}},

(8)

where the interaction picture is used for the time-dependent fermion field operators. By

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{\star}_{\text{L}}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-\mathtt{i}\hat{\bm{\xi}}\cdot\bm{\mathcal{A}}(t)}\left[\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{\star}(t)-\mathtt{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial{t}}\right]e^{\mathtt{i}\hat{\bm{\xi}}\cdot\bm{\mathcal{A}}(t)}+\mathtt{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial{t}}

(9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{H}}-\hat{\bm{\xi}}\cdot\bm{\mathcal{F}}(t),

with
\bm{\mathcal{F}}=-{\partial{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}}/{\partial{t}}
being the
electromagnetic field. The formula of electromagnetic-field interaction in

9

8

\displaystyle\langle N|\hat{\bm{j}}|M\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle N|\frac{\text{d}\hat{\bm{\xi}}}{\text{d}t}|M\rangle=\mathtt{i}(E_{N}-E_{M})\langle N|\hat{\bm{\xi}}|M\rangle,

(10)

with
|N\langle

N

N

Therefore, the transition probabilities can be related to the current matrix element via the relation

III
FW transformation

Based on the derivation in Sec.
II.3
, we find that the key to connect
the Dirac equation and the exciton Hamiltonian is the unitary transformation matrix

{\mathcal{U}}(\mathbf{k})
. We need to solve the diagonalization problem in
Eq. (
13
) first to find the electron and hole energies. However, it
will be difficult if external fields are included in the Dirac Hamiltonian matrix. One
way to solve this problem is to replace the wavevector
\mathbf{k}
by

-\mathtt{i}\bm{\nabla}
in Eq. (
13
). Namely,

\tilde{\mathtt{h}}_{\tau}(-\mathtt{i}\bm{\nabla})\equiv\mathtt{h}_{\tau}(\mathbf{r})
and

\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\tau}(-\mathtt{i}\bm{\nabla})\equiv{\varepsilon}_{\tau}(\mathbf{r})
.
We get

\displaystyle{\mathcal{U}}_{\tau}(-\mathtt{i}\bm{\nabla})\mathtt{h}_{\tau}(\mathbf{r}){\mathcal{U}}^{\dagger}_{\tau}(-\mathtt{i}\bm{\nabla})={\varepsilon}_{\tau}(\mathbf{r})\beta.

(30)

Eq. (
30
) is the basic idea of FW transformation. By a proper
choice of the unitary transformation, the analytic formulation of the single-particle
Hamiltonian
\varepsilon_{\tau}(\mathbf{r})
can be derived. Since

\varepsilon_{\tau}(\mathbf{r})\beta
is still a functional of
-\mathtt{i}\bm{\nabla}
,

\varepsilon_{\tau}(\mathbf{r})
is actually the electron
Hamiltonian and
-\varepsilon_{\tau}(\mathbf{r})
is the hole Hamiltonian.

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\text{FW}}=\mathcal{U}_{\text{FW}}\left(\mathcal{H}-\mathtt{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial{t}}\right)\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}_{\text{FW}}+\mathtt{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial{t}}.

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}={\beta}mv^{2}_{\text{F}}+\mathcal{E}+\mathcal{O}+\mathcal{O}^{\prime},

(32)

with
{\beta}\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}{\beta}
,
{\beta}\mathcal{O}=-\mathcal{O}{\beta}
,

{\beta}\mathcal{O}^{\prime}=-\mathcal{O}^{\prime}{\beta}
, where
\mathcal{E}
is an even operator and

\mathcal{O}
is an odd operator in the Hamiltonian,
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
is an odd operator of
the external interaction of the Hamiltonian. Accordingly, the FW transformation operator
up to the order of
1/m^{2}
has the expression
silenko2008foldy
;
silenko2016general

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{\text{FW}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\left[\mathcal{S},\;\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\right]\right)\exp\left(\mathtt{i}\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\right)\exp\left(\mathtt{i}\mathcal{S}\right),

(33)

where
\mathcal{S}=-\mathtt{i}\beta\mathcal{O}/(2mv^{2}_{\text{F}})
,
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=-\mathtt{i}\left[\mathcal{O},\mathcal{G}\right]/(4m^{2}v^{4}_{\text{F}})
,
with

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{E}+\mathcal{O}^{\prime}-\mathtt{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial{t}}.

(34)

The FW Hamiltonian up to the order of
1/m^{2}
can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\text{FW}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\beta}mv^{2}_{\text{F}}+\mathcal{E}+\mathcal{O}^{\prime}+\frac{{\beta}\mathcal{O}^{2}}{2mv^{2}_{\text{F}}}-\frac{\left[\mathcal{O},\left[\mathcal{O},\mathcal{G}\right]\right]}{8m^{2}v^{4}_{\text{F}}}.

(35)

Note that the odd operator
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
is not discussed by cited
literatures
eriksen1958foldy
;
silenko2008foldy
;
silenko2016general
. As we will show
lately in Sec.
III.4
, the operator contributes mainly to the
interband transition, which is not considered in atomic systems.

III.2
Single-particle Hamiltonian

In the present case, the correspondences between the single-particle Hamiltonian and the
Eriksen’s Hamiltonian are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}={\mathtt{h}}-q\mathbf{r}\cdot\bm{\mathcal{F}},\hskip 8.61108pt\mathcal{E}=-q\mathbf{r}\cdot\left(\mathbf{F}+\bm{\mathcal{F}}\right),

(36)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}=v_{\text{F}}\bm{\pi}\cdot{\bm{\alpha}_{\tau}},\hskip 8.61108pt\mathcal{O}^{\prime}=0.

(37)

By using
\bm{\pi}\times\bm{\pi}=\mathtt{i}qB\mathbf{e}_{\perp}
and

\left(\mathbf{a}\cdot\bm{\alpha}_{\tau}\right)\left(\mathbf{b}\cdot\bm{\alpha}_{\tau}\right)=\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}+\mathtt{i}\tau\mathbf{e}_{\perp}\cdot\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}\beta
, we find

\mathcal{O}^{2}={v^{2}_{\text{F}}}\left(|\bm{\pi}|^{2}-\beta\tau qB\right)
,

\left[\mathcal{O},\mathcal{G}\right]=\mathtt{i}qv_{\text{F}}\left(\mathbf{F}+\bm{\mathcal{F}}\right)\cdot{\bm{\alpha}_{\tau}}
, and

\left[\mathcal{O},\left[\mathcal{O},\mathcal{G}\right]\right]=2\tau qv^{2}_{\text{F}}\beta\mathbf{e}_{\perp}\cdot\left(\mathbf{F}+\bm{\mathcal{F}}\right)\times\bm{\pi}
. The FW Hamiltonian is
given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\text{FW}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta\left[mv^{2}_{\text{F}}+\frac{|\bm{\pi}|^{2}}{2m}-\tau\frac{q\mathbf{e}_{\perp}\cdot\left(\mathbf{F}+\bm{\mathcal{F}}\right)\times\bm{\pi}}{4m^{2}v^{2}_{\text{F}}}\right]

(38)

\displaystyle-q\left(\mathbf{F}+\bm{\mathcal{F}}\right)\cdot\mathbf{r}-\tau\frac{qB}{2m}.

Based on the FW single-particle Hamiltonian, the effective single-particle energy is given
by

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\text{FW},i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle mv^{2}_{\text{F}}+\frac{|\bm{\pi}_{i}|^{2}}{2m}-\tau_{i}\frac{q_{i}\mathbf{e}_{\perp}\cdot\left(\mathbf{F}+\bm{\mathcal{F}}\right)\times\bm{\pi}_{i}}{4m^{2}v^{2}_{\text{F}}}

(39)

\displaystyle-q_{i}\left(\mathbf{F}+\bm{\mathcal{F}}\right)\cdot\mathbf{r}_{i}-\tau_{i}\frac{q_{i}B}{2m},

where
i\in\{\text{e},\text{h}\}
,
q=-e
for electrons and
q=e
for holes,

\tau_{\text{e}}
and
\tau_{\text{h}}
are valley indices of the effective electron and
hole.

III.3
Two-particle interaction

Now we consider a two-particle Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\mathtt{h}_{1}+\mathtt{h}_{2}+\mathcal{V}_{12},

(40)

where
\mathcal{V}_{12}
is a two-particle interaction, and
{\mathtt{h}}_{i}=v_{\text{F}}\bm{\pi}_{i}\cdot{\bm{\alpha}_{\tau}}_{i}+mv^{2}_{\text{F}}\beta_{i}+q_{i}\Phi_{i}

is the single-particle Hamiltonian matrix. The Hamiltonian can be divided as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\beta_{1}mv^{2}_{\text{F}}+\beta_{2}mv^{2}_{\text{F}}+\mathcal{E}+\mathcal{O}_{1}+\mathcal{O}_{2},

(41)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}=q_{1}\Phi_{1}+q_{2}\Phi_{2}+\mathcal{V}_{12},

(42)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{1}=v_{\text{F}}\bm{\pi}_{1}\cdot{\bm{\alpha}_{\tau_{1}}},\hskip 8.61108pt\mathcal{O}_{2}=v_{\text{F}}\bm{\pi}_{2}\cdot{\bm{\alpha}_{\tau_{2}}}.

(43)

The FW transformed Hamiltonian is then given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\text{FW}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathtt{h}_{1}+\mathtt{h}_{2}-\frac{\left[\mathcal{O}_{1},\left[\mathcal{O}_{1},\mathcal{G}\right]\right]}{8m^{2}v^{4}_{\text{F}}}-\frac{\left[\mathcal{O}_{2},\left[\mathcal{O}_{2},\mathcal{G}\right]\right]}{8m^{2}v^{4}_{\text{F}}}

(44)

\displaystyle-\frac{\left[\mathcal{O}_{1},\left[\mathcal{O}_{2},\mathcal{G}\right]\right]+\left[\mathcal{O}_{2},\left[\mathcal{O}_{1},\mathcal{G}\right]\right]}{8m^{2}v^{4}_{\text{F}}},

with
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{V}_{12}-\mathtt{i}\partial/\partial{t}
. It can shown
\left[\mathcal{O}_{i},\mathcal{G}\right]=-\mathtt{i}v_{\text{F}}\bm{\alpha}_{\tau_{i}}\cdot\bm{\nabla}_{i}\mathcal{V}_{12}
,

\left[\mathcal{O}_{i},\left[\mathcal{O}_{i},\mathcal{G}\right]\right]=-v^{2}_{\text{F}}\nabla^{2}_{i}\mathcal{V}_{12}-2v^{2}_{\text{F}}\beta_{i}\tau_{i}\mathbf{e}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{\nabla}_{i}\mathcal{V}_{12}\times\bm{\pi}_{i}
, and

\left[\mathcal{O}_{i},\left[\mathcal{O}_{j},\mathcal{G}\right]\right]=-v^{2}_{\text{F}}(\bm{\alpha}_{\tau_{i}}\cdot\bm{\nabla}_{i})(\bm{\alpha}_{\tau_{j}}\cdot\bm{\nabla}_{j})\mathcal{V}_{12}
for
i\neq{j}
. The FW
transformed two-particle interaction is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\text{FW},12}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{12}+\beta_{1}\tau_{1}\frac{\mathbf{e}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{\nabla}_{1}\mathcal{V}_{12}\times\bm{\pi}_{1}}{4m^{2}v^{2}_{\text{F}}}+\frac{\nabla^{2}_{1}\mathcal{V}_{12}}{8m^{2}v^{2}_{\text{F}}}

(45)

\displaystyle+\beta_{2}\tau_{2}\frac{\mathbf{e}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{\nabla}_{2}\mathcal{V}_{12}\times\bm{\pi}_{2}}{4m^{2}v^{2}_{\text{F}}}+\frac{\nabla^{2}_{2}\mathcal{V}_{12}}{8m^{2}v^{2}_{\text{F}}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\left(\bm{\alpha}_{\tau_{1}}\cdot\bm{\nabla}_{1}\right)\left(\bm{\alpha}_{\tau_{2}}\cdot\bm{\nabla}_{2}\right)\mathcal{V}_{12}}{4m^{2}v^{2}_{\text{F}}}.

For 2D materials, the two-particle interaction is assumed to be given by the screened
Coulomb interaction

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{ij}=\frac{q_{i}q_{j}}{e^{2}}V({r}_{ij}).

(46)

The FW transformed screened Coulomb potential is then given by

\displaystyle V_{\text{FW},ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V({r}_{ij})+\tau_{i}\frac{\mathbf{e}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{\nabla}_{i}V({r}_{ij})\times\bm{\pi}_{i}}{4m^{2}v^{2}_{\text{F}}}+\frac{\nabla^{2}_{i}V({r}_{ij})}{8m^{2}v^{2}_{\text{F}}}

(47)

\displaystyle+\tau_{j}\frac{\mathbf{e}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{\nabla}_{j}V({r}_{ij})\times\bm{\pi}_{j}}{4m^{2}v^{2}_{\text{F}}}+\frac{\nabla^{2}_{j}V({r}_{ij})}{8m^{2}v^{2}_{\text{F}}}

for
i,j\in\{\text{e},\text{h}\}
. The FW transformed screened Coulomb potential can be
used to describe the electron-electron interaction, the electron-hole interaction, and the
hole-hole interaction in 2D materials.

III.4
Interband transition

Assuming that the contributions from the static electric and magnetic fields to the
interband transition can be neglected, the correspondences between electromagnetic-field
interaction and Eriksen’s Hamiltonian are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\mathtt{h}^{(0)}-qv_{\text{F}}\bm{\alpha}_{\tau}\cdot\bm{\mathcal{A}},\hskip 8.61108pt\mathcal{E}=0,\hskip 8.61108pt\mathcal{O}=v_{\text{F}}\mathbf{p}\cdot{\bm{\alpha}_{\tau}},

(48)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}^{\prime}=-qv_{\text{F}}\bm{\alpha}_{\tau}\cdot\bm{\mathcal{A}}.

(49)

We find
\left[\mathcal{O},\mathcal{G}\right]=2\mathtt{i}qv^{2}_{\text{F}}\tau\mathbf{e}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{\mathcal{A}}\times\mathbf{p}\beta
,
\left[\mathcal{O},\left[\mathcal{O},\mathcal{G}\right]\right]=-4qv^{3}_{\text{F}}\left(\mathbf{e}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{\mathcal{A}}\times\mathbf{p}\right)(\mathbf{e}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{\alpha}_{\tau}\times\mathbf{p})
. The FW transformed Hamitonian with
electromagnetic-field interaction is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\text{FW}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathtt{h}^{(0)}_{\text{FW}}-q\bm{u}_{\text{FW}}\cdot\bm{\mathcal{A}},

(50)

where the FW effective velocity matrix is

\displaystyle\bm{u}_{\text{FW}}\cdot\bm{\mathcal{A}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v_{\text{F}}\bm{\alpha}_{\tau}\cdot\bm{\mathcal{A}}-\frac{v_{\text{F}}\left(\mathbf{e}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{\mathcal{A}}\times\mathbf{p}\right)(\mathbf{e}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{\alpha}_{\tau}\times\mathbf{p})}{2m^{2}v^{2}_{\text{F}}}.

By defining the circular-polarized components of variables

\mathcal{A}^{\pm}=(\mathcal{A}^{x}\pm\mathtt{i}\mathcal{A}^{y})/\sqrt{2}
,

p^{\pm}=(p^{x}\pm\mathtt{i}p^{y})/\sqrt{2}
, and

\displaystyle\alpha^{\pm}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\alpha^{x}\pm\mathtt{i}\alpha^{y}\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}0&{\tau\pm{1}}\\
{\tau\mp{1}}&0\end{pmatrix},

(52)

we can find
\bm{\alpha}_{\tau}\cdot\bm{\mathcal{A}}=\alpha^{+}\mathcal{A}^{-}+\alpha^{-}\mathcal{A}^{+}
and

\displaystyle\left(\mathbf{e}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{\mathcal{A}}\times\mathbf{p}\right)(\mathbf{e}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{\alpha}_{\tau}\times\mathbf{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\alpha^{-}p^{2}/2-\alpha^{+}(p^{-})^{2}\right]\mathcal{A}^{+}

\displaystyle+\left[\alpha^{+}p^{2}/2-\alpha^{-}(p^{+})^{2}\right]\mathcal{A}^{-}.

The FW velocity matrix,
{u}^{\pm}_{\text{FW}}=({u}^{x}_{\text{FW}}\pm\mathtt{i}{u}^{y}_{\text{FW}})/\sqrt{2}
, is given by

\displaystyle{u}^{\pm}_{\text{FW}}=\left(1-\frac{p^{2}}{4m^{2}v^{2}_{\text{F}}}\right)v_{\text{F}}\alpha^{\pm}+\frac{(p^{\pm})^{2}}{2m^{2}v^{2}_{\text{F}}}v_{\text{F}}\alpha^{\mp}.

(54)

The velocity matrix will be used to derive the selection rule and transition amplitude in
Sec.
IV.3
.

IV
Applications to valley excitons

In this section, we will use the formulation derived from the FW transformation of a
gapped Dirac Hamiltonian to study valley excitons in 2D materials. Based on the
formulations, the Hamiltonian for a valley exciton in external fields can be written. The
valley-exciton Hamiltonian is similar to the Wannier-exciton Hamiltonian, which is written
as an electron and a hole bound by an attractive Coulomb interaction, except that the
band-geometry correction terms are added. The effects of external-field interactions,
including electric-field interaction, electromagnetic-field interaction, and
magnetic-field interaction, on valley excitons can then be studied.

IV.1
Valley excitons in electric fields

An exciton in an electric field (
|\mathbf{F}|\neq{0}
) and an electromagnetic field
(
|\bm{\mathcal{A}}|\neq{0}
) without magnetic field (
{B}={0}
) is considered in this
section. The effective exciton Hamiltonian derived from the FW transformed Dirac
Hamiltonian is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\text{X}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta\Delta+\left(\varepsilon_{\text{FW},\text{e}}+\delta\varepsilon_{\text{e}}\right)+\left(\varepsilon_{\text{FW},\text{h}}+\delta\varepsilon_{\text{h}}\right)-V_{\text{FW},\text{eh}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta+\frac{|\mathbf{p}_{\text{e}}|^{2}}{2m_{\text{e}}}+\frac{|\mathbf{p}_{\text{h}}|^{2}}{2m_{\text{h}}}-V({r}_{\text{eh}})

\displaystyle+\tau_{\text{e}}\frac{\mathbf{e}_{\perp}\cdot\left[e\left(\mathbf{F}+\bm{\mathcal{F}}\right)-\bm{\nabla}_{\text{e}}V({r}_{\text{eh}})\right]\times\mathbf{p}_{\text{e}}}{4{m}^{2}v^{2}_{\text{F}}}

\displaystyle-\tau_{\text{h}}\frac{\mathbf{e}_{\perp}\cdot\left[e\left(\mathbf{F}+\bm{\mathcal{F}}\right)+\bm{\nabla}_{\text{h}}V({r}_{\text{eh}})\right]\times\mathbf{p}_{\text{h}}}{4{m}^{2}v^{2}_{\text{F}}}

\displaystyle+e(\mathbf{r}_{\text{e}}-\mathbf{r}_{\text{h}})\cdot\left(\mathbf{F}+\bm{\mathcal{F}}\right)-\frac{\nabla^{2}_{\text{e}}V({r}_{\text{eh}})}{8{m}^{2}v^{2}_{\text{F}}}-\frac{\nabla^{2}_{\text{h}}V({r}_{\text{eh}})}{8{m}^{2}v^{2}_{\text{F}}},

where
\delta\Delta
is the electron-correlation modification to the band-gap energy,

\Delta=\delta\Delta+2mv^{2}_{\text{F}}
is the band-gap energy,

\delta\varepsilon_{\text{e}}
and
\delta\varepsilon_{\text{h}}
are band-asymmetry
modifications to the electron and hole energies,
m_{\text{e}}
and
m_{\text{h}}
are
effective electron mass and effective hole mass, which are defined by

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathbf{p}|^{2}}{2m_{\text{e}}}=\frac{|\mathbf{p}|^{2}}{2m}+\delta\varepsilon_{\text{e}},\hskip 8.61108pt\frac{|\mathbf{p}|^{2}}{2m_{\text{h}}}=\frac{|\mathbf{p}|^{2}}{2m}+\delta\varepsilon_{\text{h}}.

(56)

Assuming
\delta\varepsilon_{\text{e}}=-\delta\varepsilon_{\text{h}}
, we can find

m=2\mu
, with
\mu=m_{\text{e}}m_{\text{h}}/(m_{\text{e}}+m_{\text{h}})
the reduced
mass. A coordinate transformation to the relative coordinate system is applied

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{m_{\text{e}}\mathbf{r}_{\text{e}}+m_{\text{h}}\mathbf{r}_{\text{h}}}{m_{\text{X}}},\hskip 8.61108pt\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{r}_{\text{e}}-\mathbf{r}_{\text{h}},

(57)

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}_{\text{e}}=\frac{m_{\text{e}}}{m_{\text{X}}}\mathbf{P}+\mathbf{p},\hskip 4.30554pt\mathbf{p}_{\text{h}}=\frac{m_{\text{h}}}{m_{\text{X}}}\mathbf{P}-\mathbf{p},

(58)

where
m_{\text{X}}=m_{\text{e}}+m_{\text{h}}
is the exciton mass,
\mathbf{R}
and

\mathbf{P}=-\mathtt{i}\bm{\nabla}_{\mathbf{R}}
are the center-of-mass position and
momentum,
\mathbf{r}
and
\mathbf{p}=-\mathtt{i}\bm{\nabla}
are internal-coordinate
position and momentum. The exciton Hamiltonian becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\text{X}}=\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{\text{X}}+\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}_{\text{X}}+\mathcal{J}_{\text{X}},

(59)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{\text{X}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{P^{2}}{2m_{\text{X}}}+\frac{e\Omega(m_{\text{e}}\tau_{\text{e}}-m_{\text{h}}\tau_{\text{h}})}{4m_{\text{X}}}\mathbf{e}_{\perp}\cdot\left(\mathbf{F}+\bm{\mathcal{F}}\right)\times\mathbf{P}

(60)

is the exciton translational Hamiltonian,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}_{\text{X}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta+\frac{p^{2}}{2\mu}-V({r})+e\mathbf{r}\cdot\left(\mathbf{F}+\bm{\mathcal{F}}\right)-\frac{\Omega}{4}\nabla^{2}V({r})

(61)

\displaystyle+\frac{\Omega(\tau_{\text{e}}+\tau_{\text{h}})}{4}\mathbf{e}_{\perp}\cdot\left[e\left(\mathbf{F}+\bm{\mathcal{F}}\right)-\bm{\nabla}V({r})\right]\times\mathbf{p},

is the exciton internal Hamiltonian with

\displaystyle\Omega=\frac{1}{m^{2}v^{2}_{\text{F}}}=\frac{1}{4\mu^{2}v^{2}_{\text{F}}}

(62)

the Berry curvature, and

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\text{X}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{e\Omega(m_{\text{e}}\tau_{\text{e}}-m_{\text{h}}\tau_{\text{h}})}{4m_{\text{X}}}\mathbf{e}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{\nabla}V({r})\times\mathbf{P}

(63)

is the exciton translational-internal coupling. For the exciton internal Hamiltonian, the
first four terms describe a Wannier exciton in an electric field, the fifth term is the
Darwin interaction, and the last term is the exciton valley-orbit coupling, which
resembles 2D version of spin-orbit coupling for atomistic systems. Both the exciton
valley-orbit coupling and the Darwin interaction can be considered as the contributions
from the Berry-curvature effect to the exciton energy levels of intravalley excitons
(
\tau_{\text{e}}=\tau_{\text{h}}
).

Based on the translational Hamiltonian in Eq. (
60
), it is
also shown that the Berry-curvature effect contributes to the valley-dependent exciton
transport and the exciton translational-internal coupling in the electric field for
intervalley excitons (
\tau_{\text{e}}=-\tau_{\text{h}}
). By using the Heisenberg
equation of motion and omitting the translational-internal coupling, the exciton mobility
in an electric field can be obtained by

\displaystyle\Big\langle\frac{\text{d}\mathbf{R}}{\text{d}t}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathtt{i}\langle\left[\mathbf{R},\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{\text{X}}\right]\rangle

(64)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\langle\mathbf{P}\rangle}{m_{\text{X}}}+\frac{e\Omega(m_{\text{e}}\tau_{\text{e}}-m_{\text{h}}\tau_{\text{h}})}{4m_{\text{X}}}\mathbf{e}_{\perp}\times\mathbf{F},

where
\langle\mathbf{P}\rangle=0
for the ensemble of excitons. The second term in
Eq. (
64
) can be seen as a contribution to the exciton valley Hall
effect, which has been observed experimentally
onga2017exciton
. This is effect is believed to be
driven by the side-jump together with the skew-scattering mechanism
glazov2020skew
.
While the contribution in Eq. (
64
) to the exciton valley Hall effect
is infinitesimal for intravalley excitons, it may be measurable if intervalley excitons
also contribute to the exciton mobility. Due to the simplicity of the present
model, the contribution of intervalley excitons to the exciton Hall
conductivity will be studied by an improved model in future research.

\tau_{\text{e}}=\tau_{\text{h}}=\tau
, the exciton internal Hamiltonian
for the intravalley exciton can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}_{\text{intravalley-X},\tau}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta+\frac{p^{2}}{2\mu}-V({r})-\frac{\Omega}{4}\nabla^{2}V({r})

(65)

\displaystyle-\frac{\tau\Omega}{2}\mathbf{e}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{\nabla}V({r})\times\mathbf{p}.

On the other hand, by assuming that
\tau_{\text{e}}=-\tau_{\text{h}}=\tau

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}_{\text{intervalley-X},\tau}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta+\frac{p^{2}}{2\mu}-V({r})-\frac{\Omega}{4}\nabla^{2}V({r}).

(66)

The Darwin interaction is found in both Hamiltonians, but only the intravalley-exciton

wavefunction can be calculated by the eigenvalue equation

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}_{\text{X},\tau}(\mathbf{r})\Psi_{\text{X},I\tau}(\mathbf{r})=E_{\text{X},I\tau}\Psi_{\text{X},I\tau}(\mathbf{r}),

(67)

for both intravalley excitons and intervalley excitons. Since the exciton internal
Hamiltonian and the angular momentum operator commutes,

\left[\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}_{\text{X},\tau}(\mathbf{r}),\;\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{r})\right]=0
,
with the angular momentum operator being given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{r})=-\mathtt{i}\mathbf{e}_{\perp}\cdot\mathbf{r}\times\bm{\nabla}=-\mathtt{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\varphi},

(68)

the exciton wavefunction is also an eigenfunction of the angular momentum operator,

\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{r})\Psi_{\text{X},I\tau}(\mathbf{r})=l\Psi_{\text{X},I\tau}(\mathbf{r})
, with
l
the angular momentum of the exciton. The
exciton wavefunction can be written as
\Psi_{\text{X},I\tau}(\mathbf{r})=e^{\mathtt{i}l\varphi}\mathcal{R}_{n}(r)
, where
\mathcal{R}_{n}(r)
is the radial
wavefunction of the exciton, with
n
the principal quantum number. By using

\mathbf{p}=-\mathtt{i}\bm{\nabla}
and

\displaystyle\mathbf{e}_{\perp}\cdot\bm{\nabla}V(r)\times\bm{\nabla}=\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial V(r)}{\partial{r}}\mathbf{e}_{\perp}\cdot\mathbf{r}\times\bm{\nabla},

(69)

it can be shown that the exciton vally-orbit coupling (
\mathcal{V}_{\text{X,VOC}}
), the
fifth term in Eq. (
65
) and
Eq. (
66
) is proportional to the angular momentum
operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\text{X,VOC}}(\mathbf{r})\propto-\frac{\Omega}{2}\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial V(r)}{\partial{r}}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{r}),

(70)

such that
\langle\mathcal{V}_{\text{X,VOC}}\rangle_{I\tau}\propto{l}
with

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{O}\rangle_{I\tau}\equiv\int\Psi^{*}_{\text{X},I\tau}(\mathbf{r})\mathcal{O}_{\tau}(\mathbf{r})\Psi_{\text{X},I\tau}(\mathbf{r})\text{d}^{2}r

V
Summary and perspectives

In the present work, the FW transformation is applied to 2D gapped Dirac fermions under
external fields. The single-particle Hamiltonian, two-particle interaction, and interband
transition obtained from the FW transformation are used to study the valley-dependent
physical properties of 2D materials. Exciton Hamiltonians for intravalley and intervalley
excitons in an in-plane electromagnetic field and in an out-of-plane magnetic field are
derived. Exciton energy-level splittings, valley-selected exciton transitions, and exciton
valley Zeeman effect are formulated analytically and studied. The variational method is
used to solve the exciton energy levels and optical spectra. The calculated results are
quantitatively coincident with literatures. Even though these effects have been discussed
by different theoretical methods in literatures, we believe that the present theoretical
framework still provides a new viewpoint on these topics and a straightforward derivation
procedure to study related problems.

While the Berry-curvature effects on valley excitons have been studied in literatures,
many related topics still demand investigation. One
topics of particular importance is the Berry-curvature effect on valley trions and other valley-dependent exciton
complexes. For problems involving more than two particles, a semiclassical derivation
of Berry-curvature effect becomes much more difficult. Therefore, the present FW
transformation method becomes quite useful for deriving effective Hamiltonians for
valley-dependent exciton complexes. For trions, some theoretical studies addressing the
Berry-curvature effect have been published
hichri2019charged
;
hichri2020trion
, but
joint effects of valley degree of freedom and external fields on trions have yet to be
studied. Another possible extension of the present framework is to apply the
FW transformation to 2D gapped Dirac fermions with additional interactions or
band-structure modifications. For example, the trigonal warping in the band structures of
2D materials can be included by the FW transformation as a nonlocal kinetic correction to
the exciton Hamiltonian and interband transition. Such extensibility shows the potential
and versatility of the present theoretical framework. These topics will be considered in
future studies.

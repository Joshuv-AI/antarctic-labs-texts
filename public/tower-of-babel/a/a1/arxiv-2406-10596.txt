Twisting of Lie triple systems, L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebras, and (generalized) matched pairs
Jia Zhao, Haobo Xia*

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce notions of (proto-, quasi-)twilled Lie triple systems and give their equivalent descriptions using the controlling algebra and bidegree convention. Then we construct an L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebra via a twilled Lie triple system. Besides, we establish the twisting theory of Lie triple systems and then characterize the twisting as a Maurer-Cartan element in the constructed L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebra. Finally, we clarify the relationship between twilled Lie triple systems and matched pairs and clarify the relationship between twilled Lie triple systems and relative Rota-Baxter operators respectively so that we obtain the relationship between matched pairs of Lie triple systems and relative Rota-Baxter operators.



1.
Introduction

Lie triple systems can be traced back to Cartan’s study on symmetric spaces
[
4
]
. It was abstracted as an algebraic object and was named as Lie triple systems by Jacobson
[
10
]
, and then representation of Lie triple systems was defined by Hodge and Parshall
[
8
]
. Indeed, Leibniz algebras and Nambu algebras are related with Lie triple systems closely and Lie triple systems play an important role in Lie theory. The relationship between Lie triple systems and other algebraic structures is reflected in the following two aspects: on the one hand, a Lie triple system is a special Nambu algebra; on the other hand, the space of fundamental objects of Lie triple systems is endowed with a Leibniz algebraic structure. Due to the importance of Lie triple systems, Lister established a structure theory of Lie triple systems
[
16
]
. Moreover, applications of Lie triple systems on numerical analysis of differential equations were studied in
[
17
]
and that
T^{*}
-extension of Lie triple systems is compatible with nilpotency and solvability was examined in
[
15
]
.

Motivated by studies on (quasi-)Lie bialgebras and (quasi-)Hopf algebras, Drinfeld introduced an operation called the twisting in
[
7
]
. One provides a method to study Manin triples via the twisting operations. Twisting operations play an important role in the context of bialgebra theory and Poisson geometry, see
[
11
,
12
,
19
,
20
]
for more details. It is well known that a graded commutative algebra
\wedge^{\bullet}(V\oplus V^{*})
is equipped with a Poisson bracket
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
defined to be
\{v,v^{\prime}\}=\{\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}\}=0
and
\{v,\epsilon\}=\langle v,\epsilon\rangle
for any
v,v^{\prime}\in V
and
\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}\in V^{*}
. Then a Lie algebra structure on
V\oplus V^{*}
is an element
\Theta
in
\wedge^{3}(V\oplus V^{*})
such that
\{\Theta,\Theta\}=0
. Moreover, the structure
\Theta
has a close connection with Lie bialgebra structures. A Lie bialgebra structure is pair
(\mu,\nu)
, such that
\Theta:=\mu+\nu
is a Lie algebra structure on
V\oplus V^{*}
, where
\mu\in(\wedge^{2}V^{*})\otimes V
and
\nu\in V^{*}\otimes(\wedge^{2}V)
. If
(\mu,\nu)
forms a Lie bialgebra structure on
V
, then
(V\oplus V^{*},\mu+\nu)
is called a Drinfeld double. Suppose that
r\in\wedge^{2}V
, then by definition, the twisting of a structure
\Theta
by
r
is a transformation

\Theta^{r}:=\exp(X_{r})(\Theta),

where
X_{r}
is a Hamiltonian vector field
X_{r}:=\{\cdot,r\}
. The relationship between twisting and the classical Yang-Baxter equation is reinforced in the sequel. Let
(\mu,\nu=0)
be a Lie bialgebra on the vector space
V
, then the Drinfeld double is the space
V\oplus V^{*}
with the structure
\Theta:=\mu
. If
r
is a solution to the classical Yang-Baxter equation

[r,r]=0,

then a pair
(\mu,\{\mu,r\})
is a Lie bialgebra structure and the double
\mu+\{\mu,r\}=\Theta^{r}
, where
[r,r]:=\{\{\mu,r\},r\}
. Generally, Lie 2-(co-, bi-)algebra structures are characterized as Maurer-Cartan elements in a graded Poisson algebra
(S^{\bullet}(V[2]\oplus V^{*}[1]),\{\cdot,\cdot\})
in
[
5
]
.

Thanks to our study on Lie-Yamaguti bialgebra theory in
[
28
]
, we obtain the bialgebra theory of Lie triple systems naturally. In order to establish a comprehensive theoretical system for Lie triple system bialgebra, the aim of this paper is to build the twisting theoty of Lie triple systems. We introduce the notions of (proto-, quasi-)twilled Lie triple systems and then we construct an
L_{\infty}
-algebra from a twilled Lie triple system. Consequently, we characterize the twisting as a Maurer-Cartan element of the constructed
L_{\infty}
-algebra. Finally, we introduce notions of matched pairs and generalized matched pairs of Lie triple systems to clarify the relationship between (generalized) matched pairs and relative Rota-Baxter operators. Similar to the case of
3
-Lie algebras, the reason why a solution to the classical Yang-Baxter equation (treated as a special relative Rota-Baxter operator) can
not
give rise to a double Lie triple system bialgebra is that a relative Rota-Baxter operator does
not
corresponds to a usual matched pair of Lie triple systems, but corresponds to a

2.
Preliminaries: Lie triple systems and their controlling algebras

In this section, we first recall some basic notions such as Lie triple systems, representations and their controlling algebras.

Definition 2.1
.

[
10
]

A
Lie triple system
is a vector space
\mathfrak{g}
, together with a trilinear bracket
\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
such that the following equations are satisfied for all
x,y,z,w,t\in\mathfrak{g}
,

(1)

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket y,z,x\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket z,x,y\right\rrbracket=0,

(2)

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,y,\left\llbracket z,w,t\right\rrbracket\right\rrbracket=\left\llbracket\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket,w,t\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket z,\left\llbracket x,y,w\right\rrbracket,t\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket z,w,\left\llbracket x,y,t\right\rrbracket\right\rrbracket.

Here, Eq. (
2
) is called the
fundamental identity
. We denote a Lie triple system by a pair
(\mathfrak{g},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
.

Remark 2.2
.

A Lie triple system structure induces a Leibniz algebra structure on its tensor space. Let
(\mathfrak{g},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
be a Lie triple system. Define an operation
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{F}}
on
\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}
to be

[\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}]_{\mathsf{F}}:=\left\llbracket x_{1},x_{2},y_{1}\right\rrbracket\otimes y_{2}+y_{1}\otimes\left\llbracket x_{1},x_{2},y_{2}\right\rrbracket,\quad\forall\mathcal{X}=x_{1}\otimes x_{2},~\mathcal{Y}=y_{1}\otimes y_{2}\in\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g},

then
(\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{F}})
is a Leibniz algebra. Elements in
\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}
are called the
fundamental objects
.

Example 2.3
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
be a Lie algebra. We define
\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
by

\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket:=[[x,y],z],\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Then
(\mathfrak{g},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
becomes a Lie triple system naturally.

Definition 2.4
.

[
27
]

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
be a Lie triple system and
V
a vector space. A
representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on
V
consists of a bilinear map
\rho:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)
such that for all
x,y,z,w\in\mathfrak{g}
,

(3)

\displaystyle\rho(z,w)\rho(x,y)-\rho(y,w)\rho(x,z)-\rho(x,\left\llbracket y,z,w\right\rrbracket)+D_{\rho}(y,z)\rho(x,w)=0,

(4)

\displaystyle\rho(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket,w)+\rho(z,\left\llbracket x,y,w\right\rrbracket)=[D_{\rho}(x,y),\rho(z,w)],

where the bilinear map
D_{\rho}:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)
is given by

(5)

\displaystyle D_{\rho}(x,y):=\rho(y,x)-\rho(x,y),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

It is obvious that
D_{\rho}
is skew-symmetric and we write
D
instead of
D_{\rho}
without ambiguities in the sequel. We denote a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on
V
by
(V;\rho)
.

Example 2.5
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
be a Lie triple system. We define
\mathfrak{R}:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
by
(x,y)\mapsto\mathfrak{R}_{x,y}
, where
\mathfrak{R}_{x,y}z=\left\llbracket z,x,y\right\rrbracket
for all
z\in\mathfrak{g}
. Then
(\mathfrak{g};\mathfrak{R})
forms a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on itself, called the
adjoint representation
. By (
1
),
\mathfrak{L}\triangleq D_{\mathfrak{R}}=\mathfrak{R}_{y,x}-\mathfrak{R}_{x,y}
is given by for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{L}_{x,y}z=\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket,\quad\forall z\in\mathfrak{g}.

By a direct calculation, we have the following

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
be a Lie triple system and
V
a vector space. Let
\rho:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)
be a linear map. Then
(V;\rho)
is a representation of
(\mathfrak{g},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
if and only if there is a Lie triple system structure
\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\rho}
on the direct sum
\mathfrak{g}\oplus V
which is defined by for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g},u,v,w\in V
,

\displaystyle~\left\llbracket x+u,y+v,z+w\right\rrbracket_{\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket+D(x,y)w+\rho(y,z)u-\rho(x,z)v,

where
D
is given by (
5
).
This Lie triple system
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mu})
is called the
semidirect product Lie triple system
, denoted by
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\rho}V
, or simply by
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes V
.

In the following, we recall the controlling algebras of Lie triple systems. Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a vector space and
C^{*}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})=\oplus_{p\geqslant 0}C^{p}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
, where
C^{p}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})=\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{2p+1}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
and degrees of elements in
C^{p}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
are assumed to be
p
. For all
P\in C^{p}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
and
Q\in C^{q}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
, define a graded bracket (called the
Nambu bracket
)
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{N}}

to be

[P,Q]_{\mathsf{N}}=P\circ Q-(-1)^{pq}Q\circ P,

where
P\circ Q\in C^{p+q}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
is defined by

(6)

\displaystyle P\circ Q(\mathcal{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{X}_{p+q},x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{p}(-1)^{(k-1)q}(-1)^{\sigma}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}(k-1,q)}P\Big(\mathcal{X}_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,\mathcal{X}_{\sigma(k-1)},Q\big(\mathcal{X}_{\sigma(k)},\cdots,\mathcal{X}_{\sigma(k+q-1)},x_{k+q}\big)\otimes y_{k+q},\mathcal{X}_{k+q+1},\cdots,\mathcal{X}_{p+q},x\Big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{p}(-1)^{(k-1)q}(-1)^{\sigma}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}(k-1,q)}P\Big(\mathcal{X}_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,\mathcal{X}_{\sigma(k-1)},x_{k+q}\otimes Q\big(\mathcal{X}_{\sigma(k)},\cdots,\mathcal{X}_{\sigma(k+q-1)},y_{k+q}\big),\mathcal{X}_{k+q+1},\cdots,\mathcal{X}_{p+q},x\Big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}(p,q)}(-1)^{\sigma}P\Big(\mathcal{X}_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,\mathcal{X}_{\sigma(p)},Q\big(\mathcal{X}_{\sigma(p+1)},\cdots,\mathcal{X}_{\sigma(p+q)},x\big)\Big),

for all
\mathcal{X}_{i}\in\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g},~i=1,2,\cdots,p+q
and
x\in\mathfrak{g}
.

Then
(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{N}})
is a graded Lie algebra and its Maurer-Cartan elements corresponds to Nambu algebra structures
[
18
]
. Let
C^{*}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})=\oplus_{p\geqslant 0}C_{\mathsf{LTS}}^{p}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})=\oplus_{p\geqslant 0}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathsf{LTS}}^{p}(\otimes^{2p+1}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
be a graded subspace of
C^{*}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
such that for any
P\in C^{p}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
,
P
satisfies

\displaystyle P(\mathcal{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{X}_{p-1},x,x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle~P(\mathcal{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{X}_{p-1},x,y,z)+P(\mathcal{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{X}_{p-1},y,z,x)+P(\mathcal{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{X}_{p-1},z,x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

The corresponding Nambu bracket is denoted by
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{LTS}}
, when the graded vector space is restricted to
C^{*}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})=\oplus_{p\geqslant 0}C_{\mathsf{LTS}}^{p}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
. Then
(C^{*}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})=\oplus_{p\geqslant 0}C_{\mathsf{LTS}}^{p}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{LTS}})
is a graded subalgebra and its Maurer-Cartan elements corresponds to Lie triple system structures
[
26
]
. In the present paper, we always consider the graded Lie algebra
(C^{*}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{LTS}})
.

3.
(Proto-, quasi-)twilled Lie triple systems

In this section, we introduce notions of (proto-, quasi-)twilled Lie triple systems, and use the graded Lie algebra bracket in the controlling algebra to give structures of (proto-, quasi-)twilled Lie triple systems. For this purpose, we have to examine the lift of a given linear map and its bidegree first.

3.1.
Lift and bidegree

Let
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
be vector spaces, and elements in
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
are denoted by
x,y,z,x_{i}
and those in
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
by
u,v,w,u_{i}
. We denote by
\mathfrak{g}^{l,k}
the direct sum of all
(l+k)
-tensor power of
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
:
\otimes^{n}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2})
, where
n=l+k
, and
l
(resp.
k
) means the quantities of
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
(resp.
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
). For example,
\mathfrak{g}^{2,1}\subset\otimes^{3}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2})
can be written as

\mathfrak{g}^{2,1}=(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{1}\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{2})\oplus(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{2}\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{1})\oplus(\mathfrak{g}_{2}\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{1}\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{1}).

Then
\displaystyle{\otimes^{n}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2})=\oplus_{l+k=n}\mathfrak{g}^{l,k}}
. For example,

\otimes^{3}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2})=\mathfrak{g}^{3,0}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{2,1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{1,2}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{0,3}.

By
\mathrm{Hom}
-functor, we have

(7)

\displaystyle C^{p}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2})=\bigoplus_{l+k=2p}C^{p}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathfrak{g}^{l,k},\mathfrak{g}_{1})\oplus\bigoplus_{l+k=2p}C^{p}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathfrak{g}^{l,k},\mathfrak{g}_{2}).

For a linear map
f\in C^{p}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathfrak{g}^{l,k},\mathfrak{g}_{1})
(resp.
f\in C^{p}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathfrak{g}^{l,k},\mathfrak{g}_{2})
),
f
naturally induces a linear map
\hat{f}\in C^{p}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2})
defined to be

\displaystyle\hat{f}:=\begin{cases}f,\quad\text{on }~~\mathfrak{g}^{l,k},\\
0,\quad\text{all other cases.}\end{cases}

The linear map
\hat{f}
is called a
lift
of
f
. For example, the lifts of linear maps
\alpha:\otimes^{3}\mathfrak{g}_{1}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{1}
,
\beta:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}_{1}\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{2}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{2}
and
\gamma:\mathfrak{g}_{2}\otimes(\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}_{1})\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{2}
are defined to be

\displaystyle\hat{\alpha}((x,u),(y,v),(z,w))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha(x,y,z),0),

\displaystyle\hat{\beta}((x,u),(y,v),(z,w))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(0,\beta(x,y,w)),

\displaystyle\hat{\gamma}((x,u),(y,v),(z,w))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(0,\gamma(u,y,z))

respectively. A linear map
H:\mathfrak{g}_{2}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{1}
induces its lift given by
\hat{H}(x,u)=(H(u),0)
. It is straightforward to see that
\hat{H}\circ\hat{H}=0
.

Now we give the notion of bidegree of linear maps in
C^{p}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2})
in the sequel.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
f\in C^{p}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2})
be a linear map and
l,k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Suppose that
l
and
k
satisfies the following conditions:

(i)

l+k=2p
;

(ii)

If
X\in\mathfrak{g}^{l+1,k}
, then
f(X)\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}
;

(iii)

If
X\in\mathfrak{g}^{l,k+1}
, then
f(X)\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}
;

(iv)

All the other cases,
f(X)=0
,

then we say that the
bidegree
of
f
is
l|k
, denoted by
||f||=l|k
. A linear map
f
is said to be
homogeneous
if
f
has a bidegree.

Notice that
l+k\geqslant 0,~k,l\geqslant-1
since
p\geqslant 0
and
l+1,k+1\geqslant 0
. For example,
||\hat{H}||=-1|1
, where
H:\mathfrak{g}_{2}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{1}
is a linear map;
||\hat{\alpha}||=||\hat{\beta}||=||\hat{\gamma}||=2|0
. Consequently, we obtain a homogeneous element
\hat{\mu}:=\hat{\alpha}+\hat{\beta}+\hat{\gamma}
whose bidegree is
2|0

\hat{\mu}((x,u),(y,v),(z,w))=(\alpha(x,y,z),\beta(x,y,w)+\gamma(u,y,z)-\gamma(v,x,z)).

However, there does not exist any linear map whose lift is
\hat{\mu}
, but we focus on
\hat{\mu}
since it is a multiplication of the semidirect product type.

We give some lemmas in the following.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
f_{1},\cdots,f_{n}\in C^{p}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2})
be homogeneous linear maps and the bidegrees of

are different. Then
\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}=0
if and only if

Lemma 3.3
.

If
||f||=-1|l
(resp.
l|-1
) and
||g||=-1|k
(resp.
||g||=k|-1
), then
[f,g]_{\mathsf{LTS}}=0

Suppose that
||f||=-1|l
and

. Then both
f
and

are lifts of some linear maps in

. Hence, we have

, which leads to

.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
f\in C^{p}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2})
and

Lemma 3.5

Suppose that

and

||[f,g]_{\mathsf{LTS}}||=l_{f}+l_{g}|k_{f}+k_{g}

Since

thus by Lemma

Proto-twilled and quasi-twilled Lie triple systems

The operation
\left\llbracket(x,u),(y,v),(z,w)\right\rrbracket_{\mathcal{G}}

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathcal{G}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{1},\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{2}),\quad\left\llbracket x,y,w\right\rrbracket_{\mathcal{G}}=(\left\llbracket x,y,w\right\rrbracket_{1},\left\llbracket x,y,w\right\rrbracket_{2}),

\displaystyle~\left\llbracket x,v,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathcal{G}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\left\llbracket x,v,z\right\rrbracket_{1},\left\llbracket x,v,z\right\rrbracket_{2}),\quad\left\llbracket x,v,w\right\rrbracket_{\mathcal{G}}=(\left\llbracket x,v,w\right\rrbracket_{1},\left\llbracket x,v,w\right\rrbracket_{2}),

\displaystyle~\left\llbracket u,v,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathcal{G}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\left\llbracket u,v,z\right\rrbracket_{1},\left\llbracket u,v,z\right\rrbracket_{2}),\quad\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{\mathcal{G}}=(\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{1},\left\llbracket u,v,z\right\rrbracket_{2}).

Here the operation
\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{1}
(resp.

) denotes the projection of
\mathcal{G}
onto
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
(resp.
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
).

In the following, we use the notation
\Theta
to denote the operation

on

, i.e.,

\Theta((x,u),(y,v),(z,w))=\left\llbracket(x,u),(y,v),(z,w)\right\rrbracket_{\mathcal{G}}.

Write
\Theta=\hat{\phi}_{1}+\hat{\mu}_{1}+\hat{\psi}+\hat{\mu}_{2}+\hat{\phi}_{2}
as in Lemma

, then we obtain that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}~~\hat{\phi}_{1}((x,u),(y,v),(z,w))&=&(0,\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{2}),\\
~~\hat{\mu}_{1}((x,u),(y,v),(z,w))&=&(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{1},\left\llbracket x,y,w\right\rrbracket_{2}+\left\llbracket u,y,z\right\rrbracket_{2}-\left\llbracket v,x,z\right\rrbracket_{2}),\\
~~\hat{\psi}((x,u),(y,v),(z,w))&=&(\left\llbracket x,y,w\right\rrbracket_{1}+\left\llbracket u,y,z\right\rrbracket_{1}-\left\llbracket v,x,z\right\rrbracket_{1},\left\llbracket u,v,z\right\rrbracket_{2}+\left\llbracket x,v,w\right\rrbracket_{2}-\left\llbracket y,u,w\right\rrbracket_{2}),\\
~~\hat{\mu}_{2}((x,u),(y,v),(z,w))&=&(\left\llbracket u,v,z\right\rrbracket_{1}+\left\llbracket x,v,w\right\rrbracket_{1}-\left\llbracket y,u,w\right\rrbracket_{1},\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{2}),\\
~~\hat{\phi}_{2}((x,u),(y,v),(z,w))&=&(\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{1},0).\end{array}\right.

It is easy to see that

and

Proposition 3.8
.

The Maurer-Cartan equation
[\Theta,\Theta]_{\mathsf{LTS}}=0
is equivalent to the following conditions:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}[\hat{\phi}_{1},\hat{\mu}_{1}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}&=&0,\\
~[\hat{\psi},\hat{\phi}_{1}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}+\frac{1}{2}[\hat{\mu}_{1},\hat{\mu}_{1}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}&=&0,\\
~[\hat{\phi}_{1},\hat{\mu}_{2}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}+[\hat{\psi},\hat{\mu}_{1}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}&=&0,\\
~[\hat{\psi},\hat{\phi}_{2}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}+[\hat{\mu}_{1},\hat{\mu}_{2}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}+\frac{1}{2}[\hat{\psi},\hat{\psi}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}&=&0,\\
~[\hat{\mu}_{1},\hat{\phi}_{2}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}+[\hat{\psi},\hat{\mu}_{2}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}&=&0,\\
~[\hat{\psi},\hat{\phi}_{2}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}+\frac{1}{2}[\hat{\mu}_{2},\hat{\mu}_{2}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}&=&0,\\
~[\hat{\mu}_{2},\hat{\phi}_{2}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}&=&0.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Since
\Theta=\hat{\phi}_{1}+\hat{\mu}_{1}+\hat{\psi}+\hat{\mu}_{2}+\hat{\phi}_{2}\in C^{1}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{G})
, then expanding the expression
[\Theta,\Theta]_{\mathsf{LTS}}\in C^{2}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{G})
yields the following nonzero terms by bidegree classification:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}[\hat{\phi}_{1},\hat{\mu}_{1}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}&\in&(5|-1),\\
~2[\hat{\psi},\hat{\phi}_{1}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}+[\hat{\mu}_{1},\hat{\mu}_{1}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}&\in&(4|0),\\
~2[\hat{\phi}_{1},\hat{\mu}_{2}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}+2[\hat{\psi},\hat{\mu}_{1}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}&\in&(3|1),\\
~2[\hat{\psi},\hat{\phi}_{2}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}+2[\hat{\mu}_{1},\hat{\mu}_{2}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}+[\hat{\psi},\hat{\psi}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}&\in&(2|2),\\
~2[\hat{\mu}_{1},\hat{\phi}_{2}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}+2[\hat{\psi},\hat{\mu}_{2}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}&\in&(1|3),\\
~2[\hat{\psi},\hat{\phi}_{2}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}+[\hat{\mu}_{2},\hat{\mu}_{2}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}&\in&(0|4)\\
~[\hat{\mu}_{2},\hat{\phi}_{2}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}&\in&(-1|5).\end{array}\right.

Thus the Maurer-Cartan equation
[\Theta,\Theta]_{\mathsf{LTS}}=0
holds if and only if all of its expanding parts vanish.
∎

.

Remark 3.10
.

Since

, thus the condition in Definition

. Hence the condition making the proto-twilled Lie triple system

It is direct to obtain the following proposition.

Proposition 3.11
.

The triple
(\mathcal{G},\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2})
is a quasi-twilled Lie triple system if and only if the following conditions are satisfied:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}[\hat{\phi}_{1},\hat{\mu}_{1}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}&=&0,\\

3.3.
Twilled Lie triple systems and
L_{\infty}
-algebras

In this subsection, we introduce the notion of twilled Lie triple systems, which is our main object in the present paper, and then we construct an
L_{\infty}
-algebra from a twilled Lie triple system via the higher derived brackets.

Definition 3.12
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2})
be a proto-twilled Lie triple system and
\Theta=\hat{\phi}_{1}+\hat{\mu}_{1}+\hat{\psi}+\hat{\mu}_{2}+\hat{\phi}_{2}\in C^{1}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{G})
its structure. If both
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
all vanish, or equivalently, both
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
are subalgebras of
\mathcal{G}
, then the proto-twilled Lie triple system
(\mathcal{G},\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2})
is called a
twilled Lie triple system
.

Remark 3.13
.

In
[
22
]
, the first author et al. explored product structures on Lie-Yamaguti algebras, and we obtain the corresponding product structure on Lie triple system if we restrict the binary operation on Lie-Yamaguti algebra to zero. By Definition
3.12
, we see that
\mathcal{G}
is a twilled Lie triple system if and only if there exists a product structure on
\mathcal{G}
.

It is easy to deduce the following proposition.

Proposition 3.14
.

The triple
(\mathcal{G},\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2})
is a twilled Lie triple system if and only if the following conditions are satisfied:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}~\frac{1}{2}[\hat{\mu}_{1},\hat{\mu}_{1}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}&=&0,\\
~[\hat{\psi},\hat{\mu}_{1}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}&=&0,\\
~[\hat{\mu}_{1},\hat{\mu}_{2}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}+\frac{1}{2}[\hat{\psi},\hat{\psi}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}&=&0,\\
~[\hat{\psi},\hat{\mu}_{2}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}&=&0,\\
~\frac{1}{2}[\hat{\mu}_{2},\hat{\mu}_{2}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}&=&0.\end{array}\right.

Remark 3.15
.

From Proposition
3.14
, we obtain that
\hat{\mu}_{1}\in C^{1}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{G})
is a Lie triple system structure on
\mathcal{G}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}
. Moreover, by (
3.2
), we see that
\hat{\mu}_{1}|_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{1}}
is a Lie triple system structure on
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
, and we denote this Lie triple system by
(\mathfrak{g}_{1},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}})
. Set
\rho_{1}(x,y)u:=\hat{\mu}_{1}(u,x,y)
, then
D_{1}(x,y)u=D_{\rho_{1}}(x,y)u=\hat{\mu}_{1}(x,y,u)
since
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
is a Lie triple system. Consequently,
(\mathfrak{g}_{2};\rho_{1})
is a representation of
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
. Similarly, we have
\hat{\mu}_{2}|_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{2}}
is a Lie triple system structure on
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
, and
(\mathfrak{g}_{1};\rho_{2})
is a representation of
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
, where
\rho_{2}(u,v)x:=\hat{\mu}_{2}(x,u,v)
and
D_{2}(u,v)x=D_{\rho_{2}}(u,v)x=\hat{\mu}_{2}(u,v,x)
.

In the sequel, we construct an
L_{\infty}
-algebra via a given twilled Lie triple system. Before this, let us recall the method of higher derived brackets of a differential graded Lie algebra. Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],d)
be a differential graded Lie algebra and
d_{t}:=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}d_{i}t^{i}
a formal deformation of
d
with

. Then

is a differential on
\mathfrak{g}[[t]]
, which is a Lie triple system of formal series with coefficients in
\mathfrak{g}
. The square zero condition

is equivalent to

\sum_{i+j=n,\atop i,j\geqslant 0}d_{i}\circ d_{j}=0,\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}.

Then set
l_{k}:\otimes^{k}\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
to be

(18)

\displaystyle l_{k}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k})=[\cdots[[d_{k-1}(x_{1}),x_{2}],x_{3}],\cdots,x_{k}]

for all homogeneous elements
x_{1},\cdots,x_{k}\in\mathfrak{g}
. The collection of graded linear maps
\{l_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is called the
higher derived brackets
.

A suitable
L_{\infty}
-algebra can be obtained from a differential graded Lie algebra via the method of higher derived brackets. The notion of
L_{\infty}
-algebras was introduced in
[
23
]
. See
[
13
,
14
]
for more details.

Proposition 3.16
.

(

Let

to be

Theorem 3.17
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2})
be a twilled Lie triple system. Then with the above notations,
(C^{*}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{g}_{1}),l_{1},l_{2},l_{3})
is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra.

Proof.

We define derivations
d_{i}
to be

d_{0}:=[\hat{\mu}_{2},\cdot]_{\mathsf{LTS}},\quad d_{1}:=[\hat{\psi},\cdot]_{\mathsf{LTS}},\quad d_{2}:=[\hat{\mu}_{1},\cdot]_{\mathsf{LTS}},\quad d_{k}=0,\quad\forall k\geqslant 3.

For all
f\in C^{*}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{G})
, since
2[[\hat{\mu}_{2},\hat{\mu}_{2}]_{\mathsf{LTS}},f]_{\mathsf{LTS}}=[[\hat{\mu}_{2},\hat{\mu}_{2}]_{\mathsf{LTS}},f]_{\mathsf{LTS}}
, we deduce that

d_{0}\circ d_{0}=0,

which implies that
(C^{*}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{G}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{LTS}},d_{0})
is a differential graded Lie algebra.
Moreover, for all
f\in C^{*}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{G})
, by Proposition
3.14
, we have

\displaystyle(d_{0}\circ d_{1}+d_{1}\circ d_{0})(f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\hat{\mu}_{2},[\hat{\psi},f]_{\mathsf{LTS}}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}+[\hat{\psi},[\hat{\mu}_{2},f]_{\mathsf{LTS}}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[[\hat{\mu}_{2},\hat{\psi}]_{\mathsf{LTS}},f]_{\mathsf{LTS}}=0,

\displaystyle~(d_{0}\circ d_{2}+d_{1}\circ d_{1}+d_{2}\circ d_{0})(f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\hat{\mu}_{2},[\hat{\mu}_{1},f]_{\mathsf{LTS}}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}+[\hat{\psi},[\hat{\psi},f]_{\mathsf{LTS}}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}+[\hat{\mu}_{1},[\hat{\mu}_{2},f]_{\mathsf{LTS}}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[[\hat{\mu}_{1},\hat{\mu}_{2}]_{\mathsf{LTS}},f]_{\mathsf{LTS}}+\frac{1}{2}[[\hat{\psi},\hat{\psi}]_{\mathsf{LTS}},f]_{\mathsf{LTS}}=0,

\displaystyle~(d_{1}\circ d_{2}+d_{2}\circ d_{1})(f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\hat{\psi},[\hat{\mu}_{1},f]_{\mathsf{LTS}}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}+[\hat{\mu}_{1},[\hat{\psi},f]_{\mathsf{LTS}}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[[\hat{\psi},\hat{\mu}_{1}]_{\mathsf{LTS}},f]_{\mathsf{LTS}}=0,

\displaystyle~(d_{2}\circ d_{2})(f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\hat{\mu}_{1},[\hat{\mu}_{1},f]_{\mathsf{LTS}}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}=\frac{1}{2}[[\hat{\mu}_{1},\hat{\mu}_{1}]_{\mathsf{LTS}},f]_{\mathsf{LTS}}=0.

Thus we obtain that
\displaystyle{\sum_{i+j=n,\atop n\geqslant 0}d_{i}\circ d_{j}=0}
and we can construct the higher derived brackets on
C^{*}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{G})
as follows:

\displaystyle l_{1}(f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\hat{\mu}_{2},f]_{\mathsf{LTS}},

\displaystyle~l_{2}(f,g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[[\hat{\psi},f]_{\mathsf{LTS}},g]_{\mathsf{LTS}},

\displaystyle~l_{3}(f,g,h)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[[[\hat{\mu}_{1},f]_{\mathsf{LTS}},g]_{\mathsf{LTS}},h]_{\mathsf{LTS}},

\displaystyle~l_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,\quad k\geqslant 4,

for all
f\in C^{p}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{G}),~g\in C^{q}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{G}),~h\in C^{r}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{G})
. It is direct to see that
C^{*}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{g}_{1})
is an abelian subalgebra of
(C^{*}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{G}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{LTS}})
, and we show that

are closed on
C^{*}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{g}_{1})
in the sequel. For all

and

and thus

, which implies that

and

are closed on

. Consequently, by Proposition

, we obtain that
(C^{*}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{g}_{1}),l_{1},l_{2},l_{3})
is an

-algebra.
∎

4.
Twisting of twilled Lie triple systems

Let
f\in C^{0}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{G})
be a
0
-cochain. Set

\exp(X_{f})(\cdot):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}X_{f}^{k},

where
X_{f}^{k}:=\underbrace{[\cdots[[}_{k}\cdot,f]_{\mathsf{LTS}},f]_{\mathsf{LTS}},\cdots,f]_{\mathsf{LTS}}
. Note that
\exp(X_{f})(\cdot)
is not well defined in general.

Let
(\mathcal{G},\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2})
be a proto-twilled Lie triple system with the structure
\Theta:=\hat{\phi}_{1}+\hat{\mu}_{1}+\hat{\psi}+\hat{\mu}_{2}+\hat{\phi}_{2}
and
\hat{H}\in C^{0}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{G})
be the lift of a linear map
H:\mathfrak{g}_{2}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{1}
. Then
\exp(X_{\hat{H}})(\cdot)
is a well-defined operator since
\hat{H}\circ\hat{H}=0
.

Definition 4.1
.

With the above notations, the transformation
\exp(X_{\hat{H}})(\Theta)
is called a
twisting
of
\Theta
by
H
, which is denoted by
\Theta^{H}
.

We need to show that the twisting of the structure is again a Lie triple system structure. Before this, we show a property of the twisting.

Lemma 4.2
.

We have the following equation:

\Theta^{H}=\exp(-\hat{H})\circ\Theta\circ\Big(\exp(\hat{H})\otimes\exp(\hat{H})\otimes\exp(\hat{H})\Big).

Proof.

For all
(x_{1},u_{1}),(x_{2},u_{2}),(x_{3},u_{3})\in\mathcal{G}
, we have

\displaystyle[\Theta,\hat{H}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}\Big((x_{1},u_{1}),(x_{2},u_{2}),(x_{3},u_{3})\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\Theta\circ\hat{H}-\hat{H}\circ\Theta)\Big((x_{1},u_{1}),(x_{2},u_{2}),(x_{3},u_{3})\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Theta\bigg(H(x_{1},u_{1}),(x_{2},u_{2}),(x_{3},u_{3})\bigg)+\Theta\bigg((x_{1},u_{1}),H(x_{2},u_{2}),(x_{3},u_{3})\bigg)+\Theta\bigg((x_{1},u_{1}),(x_{2},u_{2}),H(x_{3},u_{3})\bigg)

\displaystyle-\hat{H}\bigg(\Theta\Big((x_{1},u_{1}),(x_{2},u_{2}),(x_{3},u_{3})\Big)\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Theta\bigg((H(u_{1}),0),(x_{2},u_{2}),(x_{3},u_{3})\bigg)+\Theta\bigg((x_{1},u_{1}),(H(u_{2}),0),(x_{3},u_{3})\bigg)+\Theta\bigg((x_{1},u_{1}),(x_{2},u_{2}),(H(u_{3}),0)\bigg)

\displaystyle-\hat{H}\bigg(\Theta\Big((x_{1},u_{1}),(x_{2},u_{2}),(x_{3},u_{3})\Big)\bigg).

Thus we obtain that

(19)

\displaystyle X_{\hat{H}}(\Theta)=\Theta\circ(\hat{H}\otimes{\rm{Id}}\otimes{\rm{Id}})+\Theta\circ({\rm{Id}}\otimes\hat{H}\otimes{\rm{Id}})+\Theta\circ({\rm{Id}}\otimes{\rm{Id}}\otimes\hat{H})-\hat{H}\circ\Theta.

By Eq. (
19
) and the fact that
\hat{H}\circ\hat{H}=0
, we compute that

\displaystyle[[\Theta,\hat{H}]_{\mathsf{LTS}},\hat{H}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}\Big((x_{1},u_{1}),(x_{2},u_{2}),(x_{3},u_{3})\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\Theta,\hat{H}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}\Big((H(u_{1}),0),(x_{2},u_{2}),(x_{3},u_{3})\Big)+[\Theta,\hat{H}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}\Big((x_{1},u_{1}),(H(u_{2}),0),(x_{3},u_{3})\Big)

\displaystyle+[\Theta,\hat{H}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}\Big((x_{1},u_{1}),(x_{2},u_{2}),(H(u_{3}),0)\Big)-\hat{H}\circ[\Theta,\hat{H}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}\Big((x_{1},u_{1}),(x_{2},u_{2}),(x_{3},u_{3})\Big)

\displaystyle=

Corollary 4.4
.

The linear map

Theorem 4.5
.

With the above notations, we have

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\hat{\phi}_{1}^{H}&=&\hat{\phi}_{1},\\

4.2
, we have

5.
Matched pairs and relative Rota-Baxter operators

In this section, we examine the relationship between twilled Lie triple systems and matched pairs and relationship between twilled Lie triple systems and relative Rota-Baxter operators respectively in order to obtain the relationship between matched pairs of Lie triple systems and relative Rota-Baxter operators. This work help us achieve bialgebra theory for Lie triple systems.

5.1.
Matched pairs and twilled Lie triple systems

The notion of matched pairs of Lie-Yamaguti algebras was introduced in
[
28
]
, and here we restrict the Lie-Yamaguti algebras to the context of Lie triple systems to give the definition of matched pairs of Lie triple systems.

Definition 5.1
.

A
matched pair
of Lie triple systems consists of two Lie triple systems
(\mathfrak{g}_{1},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}})
and
(\mathfrak{g}_{2},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}_{2}})
, and two linear maps
\rho_{1}:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}_{1}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g}_{2})
and
\rho_{2}:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}_{2}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g}_{1})
, such that the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)

(\mathfrak{g}_{2};\rho_{1})
is a representation of
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
;

(ii)

(\mathfrak{g}_{1};\rho_{2})
is a representation of
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
;

(iii)

For all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
u,v,w\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}
, the following equalities hold:

\displaystyle~\rho_{2}(u,v)\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,y,\rho_{2}(u,v)z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}-\rho_{2}(D_{1}(x,y)u,v)z-\rho_{2}(u,D_{1}(x,y)v)z,

\displaystyle~\left\llbracket x,y,\rho_{2}(u,v)z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{2}(u,D_{1}(x,y)v)z+\rho_{2}(\rho_{1}(z,y)u,v)x-\rho_{2}(\rho_{1}(z,x)u,v)y,

\displaystyle~\left\llbracket\rho_{2}(u,v)x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{2}(u,\rho_{1}(y,z)v)x+D_{2}(v,\rho_{1}(x,y)u)z-\rho_{2}(v,\rho_{1}(x,z)u)y,

\displaystyle~\rho_{1}(x,y)\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket u,v,\rho_{1}(x,y)w\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}_{2}}-\rho_{1}(D_{2}(u,v)x,y)w-\rho_{1}(x,D_{2}(u,v)y)w

\displaystyle~\left\llbracket u,v,\rho_{1}(x,y)w\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{1}(x,D_{2}(u,v)y)w+\rho_{1}(\rho_{2}(w,v)x,y)u-\rho_{1}(\rho_{2}(w,u)x,y)v,

\displaystyle~\left\llbracket\rho_{1}(x,y)u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{1}(x,\rho_{2}(v,w)y)u+D_{1}(y,\rho_{2}(u,v)x)y-\rho_{1}(y,\rho_{2}(u,w)x)v,

where
D_{1}(x,y)=D_{\rho_{1}}(x,y)=\rho_{1}(y,x)-\rho_{1}(x,y)
and
D_{2}(u,v)=D_{\rho_{2}}(u,v)=\rho_{2}(v,u)-\rho_{2}(u,v)
. We denote a matched pair of Lie triple systems by a quadruple
(\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2};\rho_{1},\rho_{2})
.

The following proposition demonstrates that a matched pair of Lie triple systems gives rise to a Lie triple systems structure on its direct sum.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2};\rho_{1},\rho_{2})
be a matched pair of Lie triple systems, where
\rho_{1}:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}_{1}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g}_{2})
and
\rho_{2}:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}_{2}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g}_{1})
. Define an operation
\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\bowtie}
on
\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}
to be

(28)

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x+u,y+v,z+w\right\rrbracket_{\bowtie}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}+D_{2}(u,v)z+\rho_{2}(v,w)x-\rho_{2}(u,w)y

\displaystyle+\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}_{2}}+D_{1}(x,y)w+\rho_{1}(y,z)u-\rho_{1}(x,z)v.

Then
(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\bowtie})
is a Lie triple system, which is called the
double
of
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
.

Proof.

Let the quadruple
(\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2};\rho_{1},\rho_{2})
be a matched pair of Lie triple systems, then we have the following equations:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}[\hat{\pi}_{1},\hat{\rho}_{1}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}+\frac{1}{2}[\hat{\rho}_{1},\hat{\rho}_{1}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}&=&0,\\
~[\hat{\pi}_{2},\hat{\rho}_{2}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}+\frac{1}{2}[\hat{\rho}_{2},\hat{\rho}_{2}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}&=&0,\\
~[\hat{\pi}_{2},\hat{\rho}_{1}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}+[\hat{\rho}_{2},\hat{\pi}_{1}+\hat{\rho}_{1}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}&=&0.\end{array}\right.

Here,
\hat{\pi}_{1},~\hat{\rho}_{1},~\hat{\pi}_{2},~\hat{\rho}_{2}\in C^{1}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{G})
are given by

\displaystyle\hat{\pi}_{1}((x,u),(y,v),(z,w))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}},

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}_{1}((x,u),(y,v),(z,w))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{1}(x,y)w+\rho_{1}(y,z)u-\rho_{1}(x,z)v,

\displaystyle~\hat{\pi}_{2}((x,u),(y,v),(z,w))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}_{2}},

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}_{2}((x,u),(y,v),(z,w))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{2}(u,v)z+\rho_{2}(v,w)x-\rho_{2}(u,w)y,\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}_{1},~u,v,w\in\mathfrak{g}_{2},

respectively.
It is straightforward to see that the equation

[\hat{\pi}_{1},\hat{\rho}_{1}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}+\frac{1}{2}[\hat{\rho}_{1},\hat{\rho}_{1}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}=0~~(\text{resp.}~~[\hat{\pi}_{2},\hat{\rho}_{2}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}+\frac{1}{2}[\hat{\rho}_{2},\hat{\rho}_{2}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}=0)

is equivalent to
(\mathfrak{g}_{2};\rho_{1})
(resp.
(\mathfrak{g}_{1};\rho_{2})
) is a representation of
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
(resp.
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
), and that the equation

[\hat{\pi}_{1},\hat{\rho}_{2}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}+[\hat{\rho}_{1},\hat{\pi}_{2}+\hat{\rho}_{2}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}=0

is equivalent to Condition (iii).
Since
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
are Lie triple systems, i.e.,

[\pi_{1},\pi_{1}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}=[\pi_{2},\pi_{2}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}=0,

then we have

[\hat{\pi}_{1}+\hat{\rho}_{1}+\hat{\pi}_{2}+\hat{\rho}_{2},\hat{\pi}_{1}+\hat{\rho}_{1}+\hat{\pi}_{2}+\hat{\rho}_{2}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}=0,

which implies that
(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\bowtie})
is a Lie triple system. This finishes the proof.
∎

To demonstrate the relationship between matched pairs and twilled Lie triple systems, we introduce the notion of strict twilled Lie tripe systems as follows.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2})
be a twilled Lie triple system equipped with the structure
\Theta=\hat{\mu}_{1}+\hat{\psi}+\hat{\mu}_{2}
. Then
(\mathcal{G},\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2})
is called
strict
if
\psi=0
, or equivalently, the following conditions are satisfied:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}~\frac{1}{2}[\hat{\mu}_{1},\hat{\mu}_{1}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}&=&0,\\
~[\hat{\mu}_{1},\hat{\mu}_{2}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}&=&0,\\
~\frac{1}{2}[\hat{\mu}_{2},\hat{\mu}_{2}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}&=&0.\end{array}\right.

Theorem 5.4
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between matched pairs and strict twilled Lie triple systems.

Proof.

Let the quadruple
(\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2};\rho_{1},\rho_{2})
be a matched pair of Lie triple systems, then by Proposition
5.2
, the double operation
\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\bowtie}
is a Lie triple system structure on the direct sum of vector space
\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}
. The letter is equivalent to that
\hat{\mu}_{1}+\hat{\mu}_{2}\in C^{1}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{G})
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the graded Lie algebra
(C^{*}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{G}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{LTS}})
, i.e.,

[\hat{\mu}_{1}+\hat{\mu}_{2},\hat{\mu}_{1}+\hat{\mu}_{2}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}=0,

where
\hat{\mu}_{1}
and
\hat{\mu}_{2}
are given by

\displaystyle\hat{\mu}_{1}(x+u,y+v,z+w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}},D_{1}(x,y)w+\rho_{1}(y,z)u-\rho_{1}(x,z)v\Big),

\displaystyle\hat{\mu}_{2}(x+u,y+v,z+w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(D_{2}(u,v)z+\rho_{2}(v,w)x-\rho_{2}(u,w)y,\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}_{2}}\Big),

for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}_{1},~u,v,w\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}
. By the convention in (
3.2
), define

\rho_{1}(x,y)u:=\left\llbracket u,x,y\right\rrbracket_{2},\quad\rho_{2}(u,v)x:=\left\llbracket x,u,v\right\rrbracket_{1}.

Consequently we obtain

D_{1}(x,y)u=\rho_{1}(y,x)u-\rho_{1}(x,y)u=\left\llbracket x,y,u\right\rrbracket_{2},

and similarly

D_{2}(u,v)x=\rho_{2}(v,u)x-\rho_{2}(u,v)x=\left\llbracket u,v,x\right\rrbracket_{1}.

Then the structure
\Theta=\hat{\mu}_{1}+\hat{\mu}_{2}
, which demonstrates that
\mathcal{G}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}
is a strict twilled Lie triple system. This finishes the proof.
∎

It is natural to ask what object a usual twilled Lie triple system corresponds to. To answer this question, we shall introduce the notion of generalized matched pairs of Lie triple systems. Before this, we give the definition of generalized representation of Lie triple systems first.

For a linear map
\tau:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{2}V,V)
, there induces a linear map
\mathcal{D}_{\tau}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{2}V,V)
defined to be

(31)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\tau}(x)(u,v)=\tau(x)(v,u)-\tau(x)(u,v),\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g},~u,v\in V.

We denote
\mathcal{D}_{\tau}
by
\mathcal{D}
without ambiguities.

Definition 5.5
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
be a Lie triple system and
V
a vector space. A
generalized representation
of
\mathfrak{g}
on
V
consists of linear maps
\rho:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V)
and
\tau:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{2}V,V)
, such that
\hat{\pi}+\hat{\rho}+\hat{\tau}\in C^{1}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,\mathfrak{g}\oplus V)
is a Maurer-Cartan element of
(C^{*}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,\mathfrak{g}\oplus V),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{LTS}})
, i.e.,

[\hat{\pi}+\hat{\rho}+\hat{\tau},\hat{\pi}+\hat{\rho}+\hat{\tau}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}=0.

Here
\hat{\pi},~\hat{\rho},~\hat{\tau}
are given by

\displaystyle\hat{\pi}(x+u,y+v,z+w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket,

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}(x+u,y+v,z+w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D(x,y)w+\rho(y,z)u-\rho(x,z)v,

\displaystyle\hat{\tau}(x+u,y+v,z+w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(z)(u,v)+\tau(x)(v,w)-\tau(y)(u,w),\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g},~~u,v,w\in V,

respectively, where
\mathcal{D}
is defined by (
31
). We denote a generalized representation of a Lie triple system
\mathfrak{g}
by a pair
(V;(\rho,\tau))
.

Example 5.6
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
be a Lie triple system. Define

\mathcal{R}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})

to be

\mathcal{R}(x)(y,z):=\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket,\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Then
(\mathfrak{g};(\mathfrak{R},\mathcal{R}))
is a generalized representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on itself, where
(\mathfrak{g};\mathfrak{R})
is the adjoint representation of
\mathfrak{g}
. Obviously,
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{R}}=\mathcal{L}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
is given by

\mathcal{L}(x)(y,z)=\left\llbracket y,z,x\right\rrbracket,\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Generalized representation can be characterized by the notion of generalized semidirect product Lie triple systems.

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
be a Lie triple system,
V
a vector space, and
\rho:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V)
and
\tau:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{2}V,V)
be linear maps. Then
(V;(\rho,\tau))
is a generalized representation of
\mathfrak{g}
if and only if there exists a Lie triple system structure
\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{(\rho,\tau)}
on the direct sum
\mathfrak{g}\oplus V
defined to be for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
and
u,v,w\in V
,

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x+u,y+v,z+w\right\rrbracket_{(\rho,\tau)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket+D(x,y)w+\rho(y,z)u-\rho(x,z)v

\displaystyle+\mathcal{D}(z)(u,v)+\tau(x)(v,w)-\tau(y)(u,w).

The Lie triple system
(\mathfrak{g},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{(\rho,\tau)})
is called the
generalized product Lie triple system
.

Proof.

It follows from

\left\llbracket x+u,y+v,z+w\right\rrbracket_{(\rho,\tau)}=(\hat{\pi}+\hat{\rho}+\hat{\tau})(x+u,y+v,z+w)

and involves a direct computation.
∎

Remark 5.8
.

The notion of generalized representation of Lie triple systems given, it is natural to establish a new cohomology for Lie triple systems and then deformations and extensions can be explored consequently. We will examine this problem in the future and we are also looking forward to new studies in this direction.

Next, we introduce the notion of generalized matched pairs of Lie triple systems in the sequel.

Definition 5.9
.

A
generalized matched pair
of Lie triple systems consists of two Lie triple systems
(\mathfrak{g}_{1},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}})
and
(\mathfrak{g}_{2},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}_{2}})
, and two pairs of linear maps
\rho_{1}:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}_{1}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g}_{2}),~\tau_{1}:\mathfrak{g}_{1}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{g}_{2})
and
\rho_{2}:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}_{2}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}),~\tau_{2}:\mathfrak{g}_{2}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{1})
, such that the following equalities hold:

(32)

\displaystyle[\hat{\pi}_{1},\hat{\rho}_{1}+\hat{\tau}_{1}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}+\frac{1}{2}[\hat{\rho}_{1}+\hat{\tau}_{1},\hat{\rho}_{1}+\hat{\tau}_{1}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(33)

\displaystyle~[\hat{\pi}_{2},\hat{\rho}_{2}+\hat{\tau}_{2}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}+\frac{1}{2}[\hat{\rho}_{2}+\hat{\tau}_{2},\hat{\rho}_{2}+\hat{\tau}_{2}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(34)

\displaystyle~[\hat{\pi}_{1},\hat{\rho}_{2}+\hat{\tau}_{2}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}+[\hat{\rho}_{1}+\hat{\tau}_{1},\hat{\pi}_{2}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}+[\hat{\rho}_{1}+\hat{\tau}_{1},\hat{\rho}_{2}+\hat{\tau}_{2}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

where

\displaystyle\hat{\pi}_{1}((x,u),(y,v),(z,w))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}},\quad\hat{\rho}_{1}((x,u),(y,v),(z,w))=D_{1}(x,y)w+\rho_{1}(y,z)u-\rho_{1}(x,z)v,

\displaystyle\hat{\tau}_{1}((x,u),(y,v),(z,w))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{1}(z)(u,v)+\tau_{1}(x)(v,w)-\tau_{1}(y)(u,w),

\displaystyle\hat{\pi}_{2}((x,u),(y,v),(z,w))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}_{2}},\quad\hat{\rho}_{2}((x,u),(y,v),(z,w))=D_{2}(u,v)z+\rho_{2}(v,w)x-\rho_{2}(u,w)y,

\displaystyle\hat{\tau}_{2}((x,u),(y,v),(z,w))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{2}(w)(x,y)+\tau_{2}(u)(y,z)-\tau(v)(x,z),\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}_{1},~u,v,w\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}.

We denote a generalized matched pair of Lie triple systems by a quadruple
\Big(\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2};(\rho_{1},\tau_{1}),(\rho_{2},\tau_{2})\Big)
.

If the quadruple
\Big(\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2};(\rho_{1},\tau_{1}),(\rho_{2},\tau_{2})\Big)
is a generalized matched pair of Lie triple systems, then it is straightforward to see that Eq. (
32
) (resp. Eq. (
33
)) means that
(\mathfrak{g}_{2};(\rho_{1},\tau_{1}))
(resp.
(\mathfrak{g}_{1};(\rho_{2},\tau_{2}))
) is a generalized representation of
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
(resp.
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
), and Eq. (
34
) means certain compatibility conditions.

Proposition 5.10
.

Suppose that the quadruple
\Big(\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2};(\rho_{1},\tau_{1}),(\rho_{2},\tau_{2})\Big)
is a generalized matched pair of Lie triple systems. For all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
u,v,w\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}
, define a new operation
\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}}
on the direct sum
\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}
to be

(35)

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x+u,y+v,z+w\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}+D_{2}(u,v)z+\rho_{2}(v,w)x-\rho_{2}(u,w)y+\mathcal{D}_{2}(w)(x,y)+\tau_{2}(u)(y,z)-\tau_{2}(v)(x,z)

\displaystyle+\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}_{2}}+D_{1}(x,y)w+\rho_{1}(y,z)u-\rho_{1}(x,z)v+\mathcal{D}_{1}(z)(u,v)+\tau_{1}(x)(v,w)-\tau_{1}(y)(u,w),

then
(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}})
is a Lie triple system.

Proof.

It follows from

\left\llbracket x+u,y+v,z+w\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}}=[\hat{\pi}_{1}+\hat{\rho}_{1}+\hat{\tau}_{1}+\hat{\pi}_{2}+\hat{\rho}_{2}+\hat{\tau}_{2},\hat{\pi}_{1}+\hat{\rho}_{1}+\hat{\tau}_{1}+\hat{\pi}_{2}+\hat{\rho}_{2}+\hat{\tau}_{2}]_{\mathsf{LYS}}(x+u,y+v,z+w).

This completes the proof.
∎

Now, we are ready to give our another key conclusion in this section.

Theorem 5.11
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between generalized matched pairs and twilled Lie triple systems.

Proof.

The quadruple
(\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2};(\rho_{1},\tau_{1}),(\rho_{2},\tau_{2}))
is a generalized matched pair of Lie triple systems, then the operation
\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}}
is a Lie triple system on the direct sum of vector space
\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}
. The letter is equivalent to that
\hat{\mu}_{1}+\hat{\psi}+\hat{\mu}_{2}\in C^{1}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{G})
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the graded Lie algebra
(C^{*}_{\mathsf{LTS}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{G}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathsf{LTS}})
, i.e.,

[\hat{\mu}_{1}+\hat{\psi}+\hat{\mu}_{2},\hat{\mu}_{1}+\hat{\psi}+\hat{\mu}_{2}]_{\mathsf{LTS}}=0,

where
\hat{\mu}_{1},\hat{\psi},\hat{\mu}_{2}
are given by

\displaystyle\hat{\mu}_{1}(x+u,y+v,z+w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}},D_{1}(x,y)w+\rho_{1}(y,z)u-\rho_{1}(x,z)v\Big),

\displaystyle\hat{\psi}(x+u,y+v,z+w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\mathcal{D}_{2}(w)(x,y)+\tau_{2}(u)(x,y)-\tau_{2}(v)(x,z),\mathcal{D}_{1}(z)(u,v)+\tau_{1}(x)(v,w)-\tau_{1}(y)(u,w)\Big),

\displaystyle\hat{\mu}_{2}(x+u,y+v,z+w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(D_{2}(u,v)z+\rho_{2}(v,w)x-\rho_{2}(u,w)y,\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}_{2}}\Big),

for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}_{1},~u,v,w\in\mathfrak{g}_{2}
. By the convention in (
3.2
), define

\displaystyle\rho_{1}(x,y)u:=\left\llbracket u,x,y\right\rrbracket_{2},\quad\rho_{2}(u,v)x:=\left\llbracket x,u,v\right\rrbracket_{1},

\displaystyle\tau_{1}(x)(u,v):=\left\llbracket x,u,v\right\rrbracket_{2},\quad\tau_{2}(u)(x,y):=\left\llbracket u,x,y\right\rrbracket_{1}.

Consequently we obtain

\mathcal{D}_{1}(x)(u,v)=\left\llbracket u,v,x\right\rrbracket_{2},\quad\mathcal{D}_{2}(u)(x,y)=\left\llbracket x,y,u\right\rrbracket_{1}.

Then the structure
\Theta=\hat{\mu}_{1}+\hat{\psi}+\hat{\mu}_{2}
, which demonstrates that
\mathcal{G}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}
is a twilled Lie triple system. This finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.12
.

If we constructed the dual representation of a generalized representation, then we could establish the generalized bialgebra of Lie triple systems and even, the generalized Lie-Yamaguti bialgebra theory. We will examine this projection in the future.

5.2.
Relative Rota-Baxter operators and twilled Lie triple systems

In this subsection, we construct a twilled Lie triple system via a relative Rota-Baxter operator and give some examples to end up with this section.

Definition 5.13
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
be a Lie triple system and
(V;\rho)
a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
. Then a linear map
T:V\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is called a
relative Rota-Baxter operator
on
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to
(V;\rho)
if
T
satisfies

\displaystyle\left\llbracket Tu,Tv,Tw\right\rrbracket=T\Big(D(Tu,Tv)w+\rho(Tv,Tw)u-\rho(Tu,Tw)v\Big),\quad\forall u,v,w\in V.

Besides, a relative Rota-Baxter operator
T:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
with respect to the adjoint representation
(\mathfrak{g};\mathfrak{R})
is called a
Rota-Baxter operator
on
\mathfrak{g}
, i.e.,
T
satisfies

\left\llbracket Tx,Ty,Tz\right\rrbracket=T\Big(\left\llbracket Tx,Ty,z\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket x,Ty,Tz\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket Tx,y,Tz\right\rrbracket\Big),\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

In
[
6
]
, relative Rota-Baxter operators are realized as Maurer-Cartan elements in a suitable
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(C^{*}(V,\mathfrak{g}),l_{3})
(This
L_{\infty}
-algebra was called the Lie 3-algebra in the literature). When the (strict) twilled Lie triple system is just the semidirect product
((\mathfrak{g}\ltimes V,\Theta=\hat{\mu}_{1}),\mathfrak{g},V)
, the
L_{\infty}
-algebra constructed in Theorem
3.17
is just the controlling algebra of relative Rota-Baxter operators. Thus based on Theorem
4.6
, we have the following result to relate relative Rota-Baxter operators with twilled Lie triple systems.

Proposition 5.14
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
be a Lie triple system,
(V;\rho)
a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
, and
T:V\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
a linear map. Then
((\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,\Theta^{T}),\mathfrak{g},V)
is a twilled Lie triple system if and only if
T
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator.

Proof.

Since the semidirect product
((\mathfrak{g}\ltimes V,\Theta),\mathfrak{g},V)
is a strict Lie triple system, by Theorem
4.5
, the twisting
\Theta^{T}
has the structures as follows:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\hat{\mu}_{1}^{T}&=&\hat{\mu}_{1},\\
\hat{\psi}^{T}&=&X_{\hat{T}}(\hat{\mu}_{1}),\\
\hat{\mu}_{2}^{T}&=&\frac{1}{2}X_{\hat{T}}^{2}(\hat{\mu}_{1}),\\
\hat{\phi}_{2}^{T}&=&\frac{1}{6}X_{\hat{T}}^{3}(\hat{\mu}_{1}).\end{array}\right.

Thus
((\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,\Theta^{T}),\mathfrak{g},V)
is a twilled Lie triple system if and only if
\hat{\phi}_{2}^{T}=0
, which is equivalent to that
T
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator.
∎

Corollary 5.15
.

Let
T:V\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie triple system
(\mathfrak{g},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho)
. Then
\hat{\mu}_{2}^{T}
defines a Lie triple system structure
\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{T}
on
V
and a representation
\varrho:\otimes^{2}V\longrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
as follows:

\displaystyle\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D(Tu,Tv)w+\rho(Tv,Tw)u-\rho(Tu,Tw)v,

\displaystyle\varrho(u,v)x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,Tu,Tv\right\rrbracket-T\Big(D(x,Tu)v-\rho(x,Tv)u\Big),\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g},~u,v,w\in V.

Proof.

By Proposition
5.14
,
((\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,\Theta^{T}),\mathfrak{g},V)
is a twilled Lie triple system, which implies that
\hat{\mu}_{2}^{T}
defines a Lie triple system structure on
V
. Moreover, for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}
and
u,v,w\in V
, define

\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{T}:=\hat{\mu}_{2}^{T}(u,v,w),\quad\varrho(u,v)x:=\hat{\mu}_{2}^{T}(u,v,x),

which give a Lie triple system structure on
V
and a representation of
(V,\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{T})
on
\mathfrak{g}
.
∎

.

.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)

\displaystyle T=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0&0\\

0&0&0&1\end{pmatrix},

then
T:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}

has a basis
\{\bm{e}_{1},\bm{e}_{2}\cdots,\bm{e}_{8}\}

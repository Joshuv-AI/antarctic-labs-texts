On isolated singularities for fractional Lane-Emden equation
in the Serrin’s critical case

Abstract
A challenging problem for nonlocal semilinear elliptic equation is the fractional Lane-Emden equation in the Serrin’s critical case. This is due to the various challenges and the lack of tools to analyze the isolated singularities. These difficulties come from the nonlocal setting where the ODE’s tools fail to provide useful information. Additionally, the computation of the fractional Laplacian for the involved logarithmic functions is tricky. Finally, it is not an easy task to show that the solutions are in the appropriate function space. In this paper, we solve the fractional Lane-Emden equation in the Serrin’s critical case for the fractional Laplacian by developing an innovative and self-contained approach that also applies to the classical setting ( Laplacian). We give a classification of the isolated singularities of positive solutions to the semilinear fractional elliptic equations ( E ) ( − Δ ) s ​ u = u N N − 2 ​ s in Ω ∖ { 0 } , u ≥ 0 in ℝ N ∖ Ω , (E)\qquad\qquad(-\Delta)^{s}u=u^{\frac{N}{N-2s}}\quad{\rm in}\ \ \Omega\setminus\{0\},\qquad u\geq 0\quad{\rm in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega,\qquad\qquad\quad where s ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) s\in(0,1) , Ω \Omega is a bounded domain containing the origin in ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} with N > 2 ​ s N>2s and N N − 2 ​ s \frac{N}{N-2s} is the Serrin’s critical exponent. We use an initial asymptotic at infinity to transform the critical case into a subcritical case where the underlying equation involves the fractional Hardy operator. The construction of singular solutions is based on the fact that some special functions are subsolutions of the original problem near the origin. We also classify the non-removable singularities of the solutions of ( E ) (E) and show the existence of a sequence of isolated singular solutions parameterizing the coefficients of the second order blow up term. To the best of our knowledge, this idea has also been first used in this work.

It is known that the isolated singularity of solutions to (

Our aim in this paper is two-fold: the first is to classify the positive singular solutions of the fractional problem (

-\frac{2s}{p^{*}-1}=2s-N

(1.6)

\mathcal{K}_{s}=\Big(-\mathcal{C}_{s}^{\prime}(0)\frac{N-2s}{2s}\Big)^{\frac{N-2s}{2s}},

(1.7)

\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}\mathcal{K}_{s}=\Big(\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{2}\Big)^{\frac{N-2}{2}}=\mathcal{K}_{1}.

(1.8)

See

has no positive solution.

Note that the mapping
\mu\in[\mu_{0},+\infty)\mapsto p^{*}_{\mu}
is strictly decreasing. Particularly,
p^{*}_{\mu}=\frac{N}{N-2s}
for
\mu=0
and

p^{*}_{\mu}=\frac{N+2s}{N-2s}
for
\mu=\mu_{0}
.

C.1
Basic estimates

Lemma C.1

Let
\mu\geq\mu_{0}
,
O
is a bounded domain containing the origin and nonnegative function
f\in C^{\beta}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\bar{O})
for some
\beta\in(0,1)
.
The homogeneous problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}{\cal L}^{s}_{\mu}u\geq f&{\rm in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\bar{O},\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{{\cal L}_{\mu}^{s}}u\geq 0&{\rm in}\ \ \,\bar{O}\end{array}\right.

(C.2)

has no positive solution if

\lim_{r\to+\infty}\int_{B_{r}(0)\setminus B_{R_{0}}(0)}f(x)|x|^{2s-\tau_{+}-N}dx=+\infty.

Particularly, the above assumption could be replaced by

\liminf_{|x|\to+\infty}f(x)|x|^{2s-\tau_{+}}>0.

Proof.

Let
u_{f}
be positive solution of (
C.2
) and denote by

\displaystyle O^{\sharp}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:\ \frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\in O\right\}\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm and}\hskip 9.24994ptu^{\sharp}(x)=|x|^{2s-N}u_{f}(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}})\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm for}\ x\in O^{\sharp}.

(C.3)

Clearly,
(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})^{\sharp}=\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
and the Kelvin transformation is the following

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}u^{\sharp}(x)=|x|^{-2s-N}\big((-\Delta)^{s}u_{f}\big)\left(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\right)\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm for}\ x\in O^{\sharp}.

(C.4)

Therefore,
u^{\sharp}
is a super solution of

{\cal L}_{\mu}^{s}u^{\sharp}(x)\geq|x|^{-2s-N}\tilde{f}(x)\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm in}\ \ O^{\sharp},

(C.5)

where
\tilde{f}(x)=f(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}})
.

Note that there exists
r_{1}>0
such that

B_{r_{1}}(0)\subset O^{\sharp}\cup\{0\}.

Note that for
r>\frac{1}{r_{1}}>0

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r_{1}}(0)\setminus B_{\frac{1}{r}}(0)}f(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}})|x|^{-2s-N+\tau_{+}}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(0)\setminus B_{\frac{1}{r_{1}}}(0)}f(y)|y|^{2s-\tau_{+}-N}dy

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle+\infty\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm as}\ \,r\to+\infty.

The contradiction follows by
[
15
, Theorem 1.3]
. We complete the proof.

\Box

Lemma C.2

Let
\mu\geq\mu_{0}
,
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
and
u_{0}
be a positive solution of (
1.9
) in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega
,
then

\liminf_{|x|\to+\infty}u_{0}(x)|x|^{N-2s}>0.

Proof.
Let
f=Qu^{p}
, then there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and
r_{1}>r_{2}>0
such that

f\geq\varepsilon_{0}\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm in}\ \ B_{r_{1}}\setminus B_{r_{2}}.

Our problem reduces to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}(-\Delta)^{s}u\geq f&{\rm in}\ \,\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\bar{\Omega},\\[5.69054pt]
\hskip 18.49988pt\ \ u\geq 0&{\rm in}\ \ \bar{\Omega}.\end{array}\right.

(C.6)

Let

v^{\sharp}(x)=|x|^{2s-N}u(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}})\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm for}\ x\in O^{\sharp},

and direct computation shows that

(-\Delta)^{s}v^{\sharp}(x)=|x|^{-2s-N}f(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}})\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm in}\ \ B_{1}\setminus\{0\}.

Then Maximum principle shows that

v^{\sharp}(x)\geq c_{32}\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm in}\ \ B_{\frac{1}{2}}\setminus\{0\},

which implies that

u_{0}(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}})\geq c_{32}|x|^{N-2s}\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm in}\ \ B_{\frac{1}{2}}\setminus\{0\}

and then

u_{0}(x)\geq c_{32}|x|^{2s-N}\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{2},

where
\tau_{+}+\tau_{-}=2s-N
.

\Box

Lemma C.3

Let
2s+\theta>0
,
\tau_{0}<0

p\in\left[1,\ 1+\frac{2s+\theta}{-\tau_{0}}\right)

(C.7)

and
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j}
be the sequence generated by

\tau_{j}=2s+\theta+p\tau_{j-1}\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm for}\hskip 9.24994ptj=1,2,3\cdots.

Then
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j}
is an increasing sequence of numbers and for any
\bar{\tau}>\tau_{0}

there exists
j_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\tau_{j_{0}}\geq\bar{\tau}\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm and}\hskip 9.24994pt\tau_{j_{0}-1}<\bar{\tau}.

Proof.

For
p\in(0,1+\frac{2s+\theta}{-\tau_{0}})
, we have that

\tau_{1}-\tau_{0}=2\alpha+\tau_{0}(p-1)>0

and

\tau_{j}-\tau_{j-1}=p(\tau_{j-1}-\tau_{j-2})=p^{j-1}(\tau_{1}-\tau_{0}),

which imply that the sequence
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j}
is increasing and our conclusions are obvious.

\Box

Lemma C.4

Assume that
\mu>\mu_{0}
,

O_{r}=\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\bar{B}_{\frac{1}{r}}
, the nonnegative function
g\in C^{\beta}_{loc}(O_{r})
for some
\beta\in(0,1)
and there exist
\tau\in(\tau_{-},\tau_{+})
,
c_{33}>0
and
r_{3}>0
such that

g(x)\geq c_{33}|x|^{\tau-2s}\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm in}\ \,O_{r_{3}}.

Let
u_{g}
be a positive solution of problem

{\cal L}_{\mu}^{s}u\geq g\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm in}\ \ O_{r},\hskip 18.49988ptu\geq 0\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus O_{r},

(C.8)

then there exists
c_{34}>0
such that

u_{g}(x)\geq c_{34}|x|^{\tau}\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm in}\ \ O_{r_{3}}.

Proof.
For
\tau\in(\tau_{-},\tau_{+})
, we have that

{\cal L}^{s}_{\mu}|x|^{\tau}=b_{s}(\tau)|x|^{\tau-2s}\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\},

where
b_{s}(\tau)>0
.

In the case
O=B_{1}\setminus\{0\}
, we use the function

w(x)=|x|^{\tau}-|x|^{\tau_{+}}\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}

as a sub solution

{\cal L}^{s}_{\mu}u=c_{s}(\tau)|x|^{\tau-2s}\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm in}\ B_{1}\setminus\{0\},\hskip 18.49988ptu\leq 0\ \ {\rm in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1},

where
c_{s}(\tau)>0
.
Then our argument follows by comparison principle.

When
O=\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}
, we use the function

w(x)=|x|^{\tau}-|x|^{\tau_{-}}\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}

as a sub solution

{\cal L}^{s}_{\mu}u=c_{s}(\tau)|x|^{\tau-2s}\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\bar{B}_{1},\hskip 18.49988ptu\leq 0\ \ {\rm in}\ \ \bar{B}_{1}

and the left is standard.
\Box

C.2
Nonexistence

Proof of Theorem
C.1
.
By contradiction,
we assume that (
C.1
) has a positive solution
u_{0}
.
From Lemma
C.3
, we have that

u_{0}(x)\geq d_{0}|x|^{2s-N}\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm in}\ \,\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{2}.

Step 1:
We first show the nonexistence of

(-\Delta)^{s}u+\frac{\mu}{|x|^{2s}}=u^{p}\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm in}\ \,\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\},

(C.9)

under the assumption that
\mu\in[0,\mu_{0})
and
p\in(0,p^{*}_{\mu})
,
where

p^{*}_{\mu}=1+\frac{2s}{-\tau_{-}}.

(C.10)

Let
\tau_{0}=\tau_{-}
,
which verifies that for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\bar{B}_{r}

{\cal L}^{s}_{\mu}u_{0}(x)\geq d_{0}^{p}|x|^{p\tau_{-}+\theta}=d_{0}^{p}|x|^{\tau_{1}-2s}\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm in}\ \,\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\bar{B}_{r},

where

\tau_{1}:=p\tau_{0}+2s.

If
p\tau_{0}\geq\tau_{+}-2s
, then

u_{0}^{p}\geq d_{0}^{p}|x|^{\tau_{+}-2s}

and a contradiction follows by Lemma
C.1
. We are done.

If not, by Lemma
C.4
, we have that

u_{0}(x)\geq d_{1}|x|^{\tau_{1}}\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm in}\ \,\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\bar{B}_{r}.

Iteratively, we recall that

\tau_{j}:=p\tau_{j-1}+\theta+2s,\hskip 9.24994ptj=1,2,\cdots.

Note that for
p\in(0,p^{*}_{\theta,\mu})

\tau_{1}-\tau_{0}=(p-1)\tau_{0}+\theta+2s>0.

If
\tau_{j+1}=\tau_{j}p+\theta+2s\in(\tau_{-},\tau_{+})
,
it following by Theorem
C.4
that

u_{0}(x)\geq d_{j+1}|x|^{\tau_{j+1}},

where

\tau_{j+1}=p\tau_{j}+2s+\theta>\tau_{j}.

If
p\tau_{j+1}+\theta\geq\tau_{-}
, we are done by lemma
C.4
. In fact, this iteration could stop by finite times since
\tau_{j}\to+\infty
as
j\to+\infty
if
p\geq 1
.

Step 2.
Nonexistence in the critical case
\mu>\mu_{0}

p=p^{*}_{\theta,\mu}>1
.
Note that for some
\sigma_{0}>0

\frac{1}{2}u_{0}^{p-1}(x)\geq\sigma_{0}|x|^{-2s}\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm in}\ \,\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}.

Here we can assume that
\mu-\sigma_{0}\geq\mu_{0}
, otherwise, we only take a smaller value for
\sigma_{0}
.
So we can write problem (
1.1
) as following

{\cal L}_{\mu-\sigma_{0}}^{s}u_{0}\geq\frac{1}{2}u_{0}^{p}\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm in}\ \ R^{N}\setminus B_{r},

(C.11)

which the critical exponent

p^{*}_{\mu-\sigma_{0}}=1+\frac{2s}{-\tau_{-}(s,\mu-\sigma_{0})}>1+\frac{2s}{-\tau_{+}(s,\mu)}=p^{*}_{\mu},

where
\mu\in(\mu_{0},+\infty)\mapsto\tau_{+}(s,\mu)
is strictly increasing.
Thus a contradiction comes from
step 1
for (
C.11
).

\Box

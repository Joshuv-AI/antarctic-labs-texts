Abstract




Abstract
Let
\Omega
be a function of homogeneous of degree zero and vanish on the unit sphere
{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
. In this paper, we investigate the limiting weak-type behavior for singular integral operator
T_{\Omega}
associated with rough kernel
\Omega
. We show that, if
\Omega\in L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
, then

\lim_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}\lambda|\{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}:|T_{\Omega}(f)(x)|>\lambda\}|=n^{-1}\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\|f\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})},\hskip 9.24994pt0\leq f\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}}).

Moreover,
n^{-1}\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}
is a lower bound of weak-type norm of
T_{\Omega}
when
\Omega\in L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
. Corresponding results for rough bilinear singular integral operators defined in the form
T_{\vec{\Omega}}(f_{1},f_{2})=T_{\Omega_{1}}(f_{1})\cdot T_{\Omega_{2}}(f_{2})
have also been established.

1
Introduction

It is well-known that the existence and boundedness of singular integral operator were first investigated by Calderón and Zygmund
[
1
,
2
]
. Let
\Omega\in L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
and satisfy the following vanishing condition:

\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}\Omega(\theta)d\sigma(\theta)=0,

(1.1)

where
{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
denotes the unit sphere on the Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
,
d\sigma(\cdot)
is the induced Lebesgue measure on
{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
. It was shown in
[
2
]
that the rough singular integral operator
T_{\Omega}
defined by

T_{\Omega}(f)(x)=\text{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\Omega(y/|y|)}{|y|^{n}}f(x-y)dy

is bounded on
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
for
1<p<\infty
, provided that
\Omega\in L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
, where

L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}):=\{\Omega\in L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}):\,\|\Omega\|_{L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}:=\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}|\Omega(\theta)|\log(e+|\Omega(\theta)|)d\sigma(\theta)<\infty\}.

Moreover, if
\Omega
is an odd function on
{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
,
\Omega\in L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
is sufficient to guarantee the
L^{p}
-boundedness of
T_{\Omega}
.

In 1979, Connett
[
9
]
, Ricci and Weiss
[
30
]
independently proved that
T_{\Omega}
is still
L^{p}
bounded when
\Omega
belongs to a larger space
H^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
. Here
H^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
denotes the Hardy space on
{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
which contains
L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
as a proper subspace. The result in
[
9
,
30
]
can be formulated in the following way.

Theorem A

(
[
9
,
30
]
)
.

Let
\Omega
be an integrable function on
{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
with mean value zero which satisfies
\Omega\in H^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
. Then
T_{\Omega}
can be extended to be a bounded operator from
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
into itself for
1<p<\infty
. More precisely, there exists a positive constant
C
, independent of
\Omega
and
f
, such that

\|T_{\Omega}(f)\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}\leq C\|\Omega\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}.

(1.2)

Remark 1.1
.

The precise constant on the right side of inequality (
1.2
) was not given explicitly in
[
9
]
and
[
30
]
. However, one can prove inequality (
1.2
) by following the main steps in
[
19
,
30
]
. On the other hand, inequality (
1.2
) is a special case of Theorem 1 of
[
28
]
, where the singular integral operators related to homogeneous mappings were studied.

Since then, great achievements have been made in this area. Among them are the celebrated works of Christ
[
3
]
, Christ and Rubio de Francia
[
4
]
, Hofmann
[
20
]
, Seeger
[
31
]
, Tao
[
32
]
, etc. In
[
3
]
, Christ established the weak type
(1,1)
boundedness of Bochner-Riesz means and rough Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator. The weak type
(1,1)
boundedness of
T_{\Omega}
was given by Christ and Rubio de Francia
[
4
]
when
\Omega\in L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
for
n\leq 5
, and independently obtained by Hofmann
[
20
]
when
\Omega\in L^{q}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
for
n=2
. It was eventually proved by Seeger
[
31
]
and Tao
[
32
]
that
T_{\Omega}
is of weak type
(1,1)
when
\Omega\in L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
for all dimensions. More related works, in particular, weighted version of the above results, can be found in references
[
16
,
17
,
21
,
27
,
33
,
34
]
.

Here we will focus on the best constants problem of weak endpoints estimates for some important operators in Harmonic analysis. This belongs to less fine problems and has attracted lots of attentions. For example, for
n=1
, Davis
[
10
]
obtained the best constant of weak-type
(1,1)
for Hilbert transform, and Melas
[
29
]
proved that
\|M\|_{L^{1}\to L^{1,\infty}}=\frac{11+\sqrt{61}}{11}
for Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator
M
. However, for
n\geq 2
, things become more subtle. Even for the well-known Riesz transform, there is no such information.

In 2004, Janakiraman
[
22
]
considered the Riesz transform and the singular integral operator
T_{\Omega}
. It was shown that the constant
\|T_{\Omega}\|_{L^{1}\to L^{1,\infty}}
is at worst
C(\log n\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}}+\|T_{\Omega}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}^{2})
, if
\Omega
satisfies (
1.1
) and the new regularity condition that

\sup_{\xi\in{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\Omega(\theta)-\Omega(\theta+\delta\xi)|d\sigma(\theta)\leq Cn\delta\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\Omega(\theta)|d\sigma(\theta),\quad\text{for }0<\delta<\frac{1}{n}.

(1.3)

To explore the lower bounds of
\|T_{\Omega}\|_{L^{1}\to L^{1,\infty}}
, in 2006, under the same kernel conditions, Janakiraman
[
23
]
gave the following limiting weak-type behavior for
T_{\Omega}
:

\lim_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}\lambda|\{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}:|T_{\Omega}(f)(x)|>\lambda\}|=\frac{\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}}{n}\|f\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})},\quad\text{for }0\leq f\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}}).

Similar result have also been obtained for Hardy-Littlewood maximal function
M
.

Subsequently, Janakiraman’s results were essentially improved by Ding and Lai
[
11
]
in the way that the kernel condition (
1.3
) was replaced by
a weaker
L^{1}
-Dini condition, that is,
\Omega\in L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
and satisfies

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\omega_{1}(\delta)}{\delta}d\delta<\infty,

where
\omega_{1}(\delta)=\sup_{|h|\leq\delta}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\Omega(\theta+h)-\Omega(\theta)|d\sigma(\theta)
for
\delta>0
.
They also proved the similar results for maximal type operators with Dini type kernels, see
[
12
]
.

As we mentioned before
[
31
,
32
]
,
\Omega\in L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
is a sufficient condition to guarantee the weak-type
(1,1)
boundedness of
T_{\Omega}
. However, both the results of Janakiraman
[
23
]
, Ding and Lai
[
11
]
were obtained under certain smoothness conditions. Therefore, one may ask, whether these smoothness conditions can be removed. Note that, the following space inclusion relationships are true:

L^{1}-\text{Dini}\subsetneq L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})\subsetneq H^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})\subsetneq L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}).

Thus it is quite natural to ask the following question:

Questions 1:
Does
T_{\Omega}
still enjoy the limiting weak-type behaviors when
\Omega\in L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
?

In this paper, we give a positive answer to this question. Our first main result is as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\in L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
and satisfy the vanishing condition
(\ref{eq1.1})
. Then for all
0\leq f\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, it holds that

(i)

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}\lambda|\{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}:|T_{\Omega}(f)(x)|>\lambda\}|=\frac{1}{n}\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\|f\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})};

(ii)

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}\lambda\big|\big\{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}:\big||T_{\Omega}(f)(x)|-\|f\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}|\Omega(x)||x|^{-n}\big|>\lambda\big\}\big|=0.

Before stating our second main result, let’s recall some background for multilinear operators. It was Coifman and Grafakos
[
5
,
6
]
who first studied the bilinear singular integral operators given by inner products of finite vectors of Calderón-Zygmund operators as follows:

T_{\vec{\Omega}}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)=T_{\Omega_{1}}(f_{1})(x)T_{\Omega_{2}}(f_{2})(x).

It was originated from investigating the boundedness of the determinant of the Jacobian
[
7
]
. They
[
5
,
6
]
proved that
T_{\vec{\Omega}}
is bounded from the product spaces of the Hardy spaces to the Hardy space
H^{r}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
and the weak Hardy space
H^{r,\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, and from the product spaces of the Lebesgue spaces to the Hardy space
H^{r}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
and the weak Hardy space
H^{r,\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
for different
p
and
q
when
\Omega
satisfies a certain number of moments vanishing and a strong smoothness condition.

Inspired by these works, Ding and Lu
[
14
]
essentially improved the result in
[
6
]
by replacing their smoothness kernel condition to a weaker
L^{s}
-Dini condition for
s>1
. Later on, Ding and Lu
[
15
]
extended the result in
[
14
]
to multilinear fractional integral operators.

On the other hand, when
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}\in L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
, it is easy to see that the
L^{1}\times L^{1}\to L^{1/2,\infty}
boundedness of
T_{\vec{\Omega}}
holds (see Lemma
2.3
). Therefore the general question along these lines is the following:

Question 2:
What kinds of limiting weak-type behaviors does
T_{\vec{\Omega}}
enjoy when
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}\in L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
?

This question will be addressed by our next theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}\in L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
and both of them satisfy the vanishing condition
(\ref{eq1.1})
. Then for all
0\leq f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, we have

(i)

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}\lambda^{1/2}|\{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}:|T_{\vec{\Omega}}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)|>\lambda\}|=\frac{1}{n}\|\Omega_{1}\Omega_{2}\|_{L^{1/2}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}^{1/2}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{2}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}^{1/2};

(ii)

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}\lambda^{1/2}\big|\big\{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}:\big||T_{\vec{\Omega}}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)|-\prod\limits_{i=1}^{2}\big(\|f_{i}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}|\Omega_{i}(x)|\big)|x|^{-2n}\big|>\lambda\big\}\big|=0.

An immediate application of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
yields the lower bounds of weak norms of
T_{\Omega}
and
T_{\vec{\Omega}}
as follows.

Corollary
.

Let
\Omega,\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}\in L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
and all of them satisfy the vanishing condition
(\ref{eq1.1})
. Then, it holds that

\|T_{\Omega}\|_{L^{1}\to L^{1,\infty}}\geq\frac{1}{n}\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\quad\text{and}\quad\|T_{\vec{\Omega}}\|_{L^{1}\times L^{1}\to L^{1/2,\infty}}\geq\frac{1}{n}{\|\Omega_{1}\Omega_{2}\|_{L^{1/2}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}^{1/2}}.

Remark 1.2
.

For simplicity, we only consider the bilinear case. The same reasoning as in this paper shows that Theorem
1.2
and this corollary can be extended easily to
m
-linear operators for
m\geq 2.

One of our main ingredients is to give the fine bounds of
\|T_{\Omega}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
for
\Omega\in L\log L(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
(see Lemma 3.2 below), which is the key of our proofs. To demonstrate our novelty,
we now explain a little bit of the main ideas in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. We first split the the function
f
into two terms:
f_{1}
has a compact support and
f_{2}
has a small norm. To prove Theorem
1.1
, the first difficulty lies in dealing with the rough
L\log L
kernel. We need to decompose
\Omega
into
\widetilde{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}
and
\Omega-\widetilde{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}
: one has the property of continuity on
{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
and another has a small norm. Unfortunately, this decomposition may break the vanishing property, therefore we reconstruct the kernels into
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
and
\Omega-\Omega_{\varepsilon}
to make sure that both of them enjoy the vanishing condition. Continue to the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we need to focus on the contributions of
T_{\Omega-\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(f_{1})
and
T_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(f_{1})
. For
T_{\Omega-\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(f_{1})
, the difficulty lies in that we need to prove that the weak
(1,1)
norm of
T_{\Omega-\Omega_{\varepsilon}}
tends to
0
if
\|\Omega-\Omega_{\varepsilon}\|_{L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}
tends to
0
. We note that, Seeger and Tao
[
31
,
32
]
showed that, the weak
(1,1)
norm of
T_{\Omega-\Omega_{\varepsilon}}
can be controlled by
C(\|\Omega-\Omega_{\varepsilon}\|_{L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}+1)
, this does not fit our needs. Fortunately, by the recent work of Ding and Lai
[
13
]
, together with (
1.2
), we can make the two ends meet. As for
T_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(f_{1})
, we take a viewpoint far away from origin. Then the contribution of
T_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(f_{1})
is clear when we obtain an upper and a lower control of it. It is worth pointing out that we use a delicate localization method when dealing with the lower control of
T_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(f_{1})
.

The organization of this paper is as follows. In Section
2
, we will present some notations and basic lemmas, which will be used later. Section
3
will be devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.1
, and the proof of Theorem
1.2
will be given in Section
4
. Throughout this paper, the letter
C
will stand for positive constants, not necessarily the same one at each occurrence, but always independent of
\Omega
and
f
.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we will recall and establish some auxiliary lemmas. First, we present the following lemma, which will play a fundamental role in our arguments.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\lambda,\gamma>0
,
\Phi\in L^{n/r}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
,
S\subset{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
be a measurable set. Then we have

\bigg|\bigg\{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}:\frac{|\Phi(\frac{x}{|x|})|}{|x|^{\gamma}}>\lambda,\frac{x}{|x|}\in S\bigg\}\bigg|=\frac{\|\Phi\|_{L^{n/\gamma}(S)}^{n/\gamma}}{n\lambda^{n/\gamma}}.

Proof.

Note that

\bigcup_{\theta\in S}\bigg\{r\theta:0<r<\left|\frac{\Phi(\theta)}{\lambda}\right|^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}\bigg\}=\bigg\{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}:\frac{|\Phi(\frac{x}{|x|})|}{|x|^{\gamma}}>\lambda,\frac{x}{|x|}\in S\bigg\},

therefore

\bigg|\bigg\{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}:\frac{|\Phi(\frac{x}{|x|})|}{|x|^{\gamma}}>\lambda,\frac{x}{|x|}\in S\bigg\}\bigg|=\int_{S}\int_{0}^{\big|\frac{\Phi(\theta)}{\lambda}\big|^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}}r^{n-1}drd\sigma(\theta)=\frac{\|\Phi\|_{L^{n/\gamma}(S)}^{n/\gamma}}{n\lambda^{n/\gamma}}.

∎

Before stating the next lemmas, we need to introduce a more general singular integral operator defined by

T_{\Omega}^{K}(f)(x)=\text{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Omega(x-y)K(x,y)f(y)dy,

where
\Omega
is homogeneous of degree
0
and satisfies the vanishing condition (
1.1
),
K
satisfies the size condition

|K(x,y)|\leq\frac{C}{|x-y|^{n}},

and the regularity condition that for a fixed
\delta\in(0,1]
,

\displaystyle|K(x_{1},y)-K(x_{2},y)|\leq C\frac{|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\delta}}{|x_{1}-y|^{n+\delta}},\quad|x_{1}-y|>2|x_{1}-x_{2}|,

\displaystyle|K(x,y_{1})-K(x,y_{2})|\leq C\frac{|y_{1}-y_{2}|^{\delta}}{|x-y_{1}|^{n+\delta}},\quad|x-y_{1}|>2|y_{1}-y_{2}|.

The following weak type
(1,1)
bound criterion for
T_{\Omega}^{K}
was given by Ding and Lai.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
13
]
)
.

Suppose
\Omega\in L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
. In addition, suppose
\Omega
and
K
satisfy some appropriate cancellation conditions such that
T_{\Omega}^{K}
is well defined for
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
and extends to a bounded operator on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
with bound
C\|\Omega\|_{L\log L}
. Then for any
\lambda>0
, we have

\lambda|\{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}:|T_{\Omega}^{K}(f)(x)|>\lambda\}\lesssim\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}\|f\|_{L^{1}},

where

\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}=\|\Omega\|_{L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\Omega(\theta)|\left(1+\log^{+}\frac{|\Omega(\theta)|}{\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}}\right)d\sigma(\theta).

With Theorem A in hand, together with Lemma
2.2
, we have the following key lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\Omega,\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}\in L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
and all of them satisfy the vanishing condition
(\ref{eq1.1})
. Then
T_{\Omega}
is bounded from
L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
to
L^{1,\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
and
T_{\vec{\Omega}}
is bounded from
L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\times L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
to
L^{1/2,\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. They enjoy the following norm inequalities:

\|T_{\Omega}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\to L^{1,\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}\leq C\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}\leq C(\|\Omega\|_{L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}+1),

and

\|T_{\vec{\Omega}}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\times L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\to L^{1/2,\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}\leq C\prod_{i=1}^{2}\mathcal{C}_{\Omega_{i}}\leq C\prod_{i=1}^{2}(\|\Omega_{i}\|_{L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}+1).

Proof.

Note that
H^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
is the Hardy space on the unit sphere and contains
L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
as a proper subspace. It is natrual to ask whether
\|\Omega\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\leq C\|\Omega\|_{L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}
holds or not. We have no answer at present time. However, we can show that

\|\Omega\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\leq C\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}.

(2.1)

To prove inequality (
2.1
), without losing generality, we may assume that
\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}>0
, otherwise nothing needs to be proved. Let
a_{0}=\frac{1}{\sigma({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}
. For any
\theta\in{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
, the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function on the unit sphere is defined by

M(\Omega)(\theta)=\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{\sigma(\widetilde{B}(\theta,r))}\int_{\widetilde{B}(\theta,r)}|\Omega(\theta^{\prime})|d\sigma(\theta^{\prime}),

where
\widetilde{B}(\theta,r)=\{\theta\in{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}:\sigma(\theta,\theta^{\prime})<r\}
. Then for
\theta\in{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
, it holds that

M(\Omega)(\theta)\geq\frac{1}{\sigma({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\Omega(\theta^{\prime})|d\sigma(\theta^{\prime})=a_{0}.

It was known from
[
8
]
that
\|\Omega\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\leq\|M(\Omega)\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}
. Therefore, to prove inequality (
2.1
), it is enough to show that

\|M(\Omega)\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\leq C\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}.

(2.2)

To see inequality (
2.2
) is true, we first notice that

\displaystyle\|M(\Omega)\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\sigma(\{\theta\in{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}:M(\Omega)(\theta)>\alpha\})d\alpha

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\sigma(\{\theta\in{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}:M(\Omega\chi_{\{|\Omega|>\alpha/2\}})(\theta)>\frac{\alpha}{2}\})d\alpha

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\infty}\sigma(\{\theta\in{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}:M(\Omega\chi_{\{|\Omega|\leq\alpha/2\}})(\theta)>\frac{\alpha}{2}\})d\alpha

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{2a_{0}}^{\infty}\sigma(\{\theta\in{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}:M(\Omega\chi_{\{|\Omega|>\alpha/2\}})(\theta)>\frac{\alpha}{2}\})d\alpha+\int_{0}^{2a_{0}}\sigma({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})d\alpha

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{2a_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{C}{\alpha}\int_{\{|\Omega|>\alpha/2\}}|\Omega(\theta)|d\sigma(\theta)d\alpha+2\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})},

where the last inequality follows from the weak type
(1,1)
boundedness of Hardy-Littlewood maximal function on the unit sphere give by Knopf
[
24
,
25
]
and Li
[
26
]
.

Therefore

\displaystyle\|M(\Omega)\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{a_{0}2^{n}}^{a_{0}2^{n+1}}\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{\{|\Omega|>\alpha\}}|\Omega(\theta)|d\sigma(\theta)d\alpha+2\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{\{|\Omega|>a_{0}2^{n}\}}|\Omega(\theta)|d\sigma(\theta)+2\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(n+1)\int_{\{a_{0}2^{n}<|\Omega|<a_{0}2^{n+1}\}}|\Omega(\theta)|d\sigma(\theta)+2\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{\{a_{0}2^{n}<|\Omega|<a_{0}2^{n+1}\}}|\Omega(\theta)|\log^{+}\frac{|\Omega(\theta)|}{a_{0}}d\sigma(\theta)+(C+2)\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{\{a_{0}2^{n}<|\Omega|<a_{0}2^{n+1}\}}|\Omega(\theta)|\log^{+}\frac{|\Omega(\theta)|}{\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}}d\sigma(\theta)+\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}.

Then we prove inequality (
2.2
), and thus inequality (
2.1
) is true.

Combining inequality (
2.1
) with inequality (
1.2
), we get

\|T_{\Omega}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\to L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}\leq C\|\Omega\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\leq C\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}.

(2.3)

By Lemma
2.2
, the condition
\|T_{\Omega}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\to L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}\leq C\|\Omega\|_{L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}
is a sufficient condition to obtain the weak type
(1,1)
bound criterion of
T_{\Omega}
. However, we observe that, Lemma
2.2
still holds when this sufficient condition is relaxed to
\|T_{\Omega}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\to L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}\leq C\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}
. The main reason is that it was only used to estimate the good function
g
, which comes from the Calderón-Zygmund decomposition of
f
. Hence, inequality (
2.3
) and Lemma
2.2
yield that

\|T_{\Omega}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\to L^{1,\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}\leq C\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}.

By the definition of
\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}
, it is obvious that

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}\leq

\displaystyle\|\Omega\|_{L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\Omega(\theta)|d\sigma(\theta)+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\Omega(\theta)|\log^{+}|\Omega(\theta)|d\sigma(\theta)

\displaystyle+\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\log^{+}\frac{1}{\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 3\|\Omega\|_{L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}+\frac{1}{\text{e}}\leq 3(\|\Omega\|_{L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}+1),

where the second inequality follows from two basic facts that

\log^{+}|\Omega(\theta)|\leq\log(e+|\Omega(\theta)|)\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha\log^{+}\frac{1}{\alpha}\leq\frac{1}{\text{e}}\quad\text{for }\alpha\in(0,1].

As for
T_{\vec{\Omega}}
, by the Hölder’s inequality for weak spaces
[
18
, p.16]
: for
p_{j}\in(0,\infty)
,
j=1,\cdots,k
, it holds that

\|f_{1}\cdots f_{k}\|_{L^{p,\infty}}\leq p^{-\frac{1}{p}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}^{\frac{1}{p_{j}}}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p_{j},\infty}},\quad\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{p_{k}}.

This inequality, together with the pointwise control
|T_{\vec{\Omega}}(f_{1},f_{2})|\leq|T_{\Omega_{1}}(f_{1})||T_{\Omega_{2}}(f_{2})|
gives that

\displaystyle\|T_{\vec{\Omega}}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{1/2,\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\big\||T_{\Omega_{1}}(f_{1})||T_{\Omega_{2}}(f_{2})|\big\|_{L^{1/2,\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2}\|T_{\Omega_{1}}(f_{1})\|_{L^{1,\infty}}\|T_{\Omega_{2}}(f_{2})\|_{L^{1,\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C\prod_{i=1}^{2}\mathcal{C}_{\Omega_{i}}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})},

which finishes the proof of Lemma
2.3
.
∎

L\log L

Lemma 2.4
.

If
\Phi_{1}(\theta),\Phi_{2}(\theta)\in L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})
, then they enjoy the following properties:

(i)

\Phi_{1}(\theta),\Phi_{2}(\theta)\in L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})

(ii)

The quasi-triangle inequality is true in

On the other hand, since for all

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\Omega-\Omega_{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\leq 3\|\Omega-\Omega_{\varepsilon}\|_{L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}+\|\Omega-\Omega_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\log^{+}\frac{1}{\|\Omega-\Omega_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}}

Finally, from (

,

Part 2: Upper estimate for

2.1

\displaystyle|G_{(1-\sqrt{\varepsilon})\lambda}^{1,1}|

4.2

\displaystyle+C\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{2}(\|\Omega_{i}\|_{L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}+1)^{1/2}+\sum_{i=1}^{2}(\|\Omega_{i}\|_{L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}+1)^{1/2}\Big)\varepsilon^{1/8}.

Thus it remains to prove

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\Big\|\prod\limits_{i=1}^{2}(|\Omega_{i,\varepsilon}|+\varepsilon)\Big\|_{L^{1/2}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}^{1/2}=\|\Omega_{1}\Omega_{2}\|_{L^{1/2}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}^{1/2}.

But there is no triangle inequality on the space

\displaystyle\big|\|(|\Omega_{1,\varepsilon}|+\varepsilon)(|\Omega_{2,\varepsilon}|+\varepsilon)\|_{L^{1/2}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}^{1/2}-\|\Omega_{1}\Omega_{2}\|_{L^{1/2}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}^{1/2}\big|

\displaystyle\big\|(|\Omega_{1,\varepsilon}|+\varepsilon)(|\Omega_{2,\varepsilon}|+\varepsilon)-|\Omega_{1}\Omega_{2}|\big\|_{L^{1/2}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\big|\|(|\Omega_{1,\varepsilon}|+\varepsilon)(|\Omega_{2,\varepsilon}|+\varepsilon)\|_{L^{1/2}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}^{1/2}-\|\Omega_{1}\Omega_{2}\|_{L^{1/2}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}^{1/2}\big|

The right side of this inequality converges to

as

, which implies that inequality (

) holds.

Hence, by the arbitrariness of

, it follows that

\varlimsup_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}\lambda^{1/2}|G_{\lambda}|\leq\frac{\|\Omega_{1}\Omega_{2}\|_{L^{1/2}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}^{1/2}}{n},

which gives the desired upper estimate.

Then

|H_{(1-2\sqrt{\varepsilon})\lambda}^{1}\cap V_{\varepsilon}|\leq C\prod_{i=1}^{2}(\|\Omega_{i}\|_{L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}+1)^{1/2}\frac{\varepsilon^{1/8}}{\lambda^{1/2}}.

(4.13)

Part 3: Upper estimate for
|H_{(1-2\sqrt{\varepsilon})\lambda}^{1}\cap\widetilde{V}_{\varepsilon}^{c}|
.

For all
|x|>\widetilde{R}_{\varepsilon}
, it follows from the inequality (
4.5
) that

\Big||T_{\vec{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}}(g_{1},g_{2})(x)|-\frac{|\Omega_{1,\varepsilon}(\frac{x}{|x|})\Omega_{2,\varepsilon}(\frac{x}{|x|})|}{|x|^{2n}}\Big|\leq 2\frac{(|\Omega_{1,\varepsilon}(\frac{x}{|x|})|+\varepsilon)(|\Omega_{2,\varepsilon}(\frac{x}{|x|})|+\varepsilon)}{(1-2\sqrt{\varepsilon})^{2n}|x|^{2n}}.

Then by Lemma
2.1
,
|H_{(1-2\sqrt{\varepsilon})\lambda}^{1}\cap\widetilde{V}_{\varepsilon}^{c}|
is dominated by

|H_{(1-2\sqrt{\varepsilon})\lambda}^{1}\cap\widetilde{V}_{\varepsilon}^{c}|\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}\Big\|\prod\limits_{i=1}^{2}(|\Omega_{i,\varepsilon}|+\varepsilon)\Big\|_{L^{1/2}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\backslash S_{\varepsilon})}^{1/2}}{n(1-2\sqrt{\varepsilon})^{n+1/2}\lambda^{1/2}}.

Let
\theta\in{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\backslash S_{\varepsilon}
, then at least one of
|\Omega_{1,\varepsilon}(\theta)|
and
|\Omega_{2,\varepsilon}(\theta)|
should be not more than
\varepsilon
. So we let

\widetilde{S}_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}=\{\theta\in{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}:|\Omega_{1,\varepsilon}(\theta)|\leq\varepsilon\}\quad\text{ and }\quad\widetilde{S}_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}=\{\theta\in{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}:|\Omega_{2,\varepsilon}(\theta)|\leq\varepsilon\}.

It is obvious that
{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\backslash\widetilde{S}_{\varepsilon}\subset\widetilde{S}_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\cup\widetilde{S}_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}
, and

\displaystyle\Big\|\prod\limits_{i=1}^{2}(|\Omega_{i,\varepsilon}|+\varepsilon)\Big\|_{L^{1/2}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\backslash\widetilde{S}_{\varepsilon})}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\prod\limits_{i=1}^{2}(|\Omega_{i,\varepsilon}|+\varepsilon)\Big\|_{L^{1/2}(\widetilde{S}_{\varepsilon}^{\prime})}^{1/2}+\Big\|\prod\limits_{i=1}^{2}(|\Omega_{i,\varepsilon}|+\varepsilon)\Big\|_{L^{1/2}(\widetilde{S}_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime})}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left(\|2\varepsilon\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\||\Omega_{i,\varepsilon}|+\varepsilon\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{2}(\|\Omega_{i}\|_{L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}+\sigma({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})\varepsilon)^{1/2}\varepsilon^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{2}(\|\Omega_{i}\|_{L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}+1)^{1/2}\varepsilon^{1/8}.

Then we have the upper estimate for
|F_{(1-2\sqrt{\varepsilon})\lambda}^{1}\cap V_{\varepsilon}^{c}|
:

|H_{(1-2\sqrt{\varepsilon})\lambda}^{1}\cap\widetilde{V}_{\varepsilon}^{c}|\leq C\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2}(|\Omega_{i}\|_{L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}+1)^{1/2}\frac{\varepsilon^{1/8}}{\lambda^{1/2}}.

(4.14)

Finally, by (
4.2
), (
4.3
), (
4.11
), (
4.12
), (
4.13
) and (
4.14
), it holds that

\displaystyle|H_{\lambda}|\leq C\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{2}(\|\Omega_{i,\varepsilon}\|_{L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}+1)^{1/2}+\sum_{i=1}^{2}(\|\Omega_{i,\varepsilon}\|_{L\log L({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}})}+1)^{1/2}\Big)\frac{\varepsilon^{1/8}}{\lambda^{1/2}}+|\overline{B(0,\widetilde{R}_{\varepsilon})}|.

Multiplying
\lambda^{1/2}
on both sides, and by the arbitrariness of
\varepsilon
, we finally get

\lim_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}\lambda^{1/2}|H_{\lambda}|=0.

This finishes the proof of Theorem
1.2
(ii) and also completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Freeness of Hecke modules at non-minimal levels
Srikanth B. Iyengar, Chandrashekhar B. Khare, Jeffrey Manning

Abstract
. 1.
Introduction

This work concerns the action of Hecke algebras and Galois deformation rings on homology groups of certain arithmetic manifolds. Let
X_{0}(N)
be a modular curve,
\mathcal{O}
the ring of integers of a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
, and
\mathfrak{m}
a non-Eisenstein maximal ideal of the Hecke algebra
\mathbb{T}
acting on
\operatorname{H}_{1}(X_{0}(N),\mathcal{O})_{\mathfrak{m}}
. Using the modularity lifting results of Wiles
[
9
]
, and Taylor and Wiles
[
8
]
, Diamond
[
3
]
proved that a certain deformation ring
R
acts freely on
\operatorname{H}_{1}(X_{0}(N),\mathcal{O})_{\mathfrak{m}}
, so the map
R\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{T}
, through which the
R
action on Hecke module factors, is an isomorphism. In particular, the
\mathbb{T}
-module
\operatorname{H}_{1}(X_{0}(N),\mathcal{O})_{\mathfrak{m}}
is free. Freeness of this Hecke module was proved earlier by Mazur
[
7
]
using geometric arguments, and even without the assumption that
\mathfrak{m}
is non-Eisenstein.

Diamond uses patching to deduce freeness of the Hecke module over
R
when
N
is the minimal level for the residual representation
\overline{\rho}_{\mathfrak{m}}
attached to
\mathfrak{m}
. For non-minimal levels
N
, he uses a numerical criterion,
[
3
, Theorem 2.4]
, for a finitely generated
R
-module to be free and for
R
to be a complete intersection. The fact that the criterion is applicable is verified by level raising arguments a la Ribet, that had already been used in similar contexts in
[
9
]
. Use of classical multiplicity one results for modular forms which prove such freeness results after tensoring with the fraction field of
\mathcal{O}
were another important ingredient in being able to apply the numerical criterion.

In
[
6
]
we developed a higher codimensional version of the Wiles–Lenstra–Diamond numerical criterion for finitely generated modules
M
of sufficient depth over certain families of rings
R
to have a free direct summand of positive rank, and for
R
to be complete intersection. We applied this to the ring
R_{\infty}
and module
M_{\infty}
produced by the patching method to deduce that
M_{\infty}
has a free direct summand of some positive rank. From this it follows that the lowest degree non-vanishing homology group
\operatorname{H}_{d}(Y,\mathcal{O})_{\mathfrak{m}}
of a certain arithmetic manifold
Y
associated to
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
over an arbitrary number field
F
also has a free direct summand of positive rank as a module over the Hecke algebra
\mathbb{T}
.

On the other hand our methods did not yield freeness of the Hecke module, in contrast to the result of
[
3
]
. The reason for this is that patching gives no direct control over the generic rank of the module
M_{\infty}
. Typically the only way of determining this generic rank is to use classical multiplicity one theorems for automorphic forms to determine the rank of
M_{\infty}
at all
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-points of
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{T}\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}R_{\infty}
. This approach only works if
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{T}
has enough
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-points — to directly deduce freeness from the main result of
[
6
]
one would need to have a
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-point of
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{T}
on each irreducible component of
\operatorname{Spec}R_{\infty}
. This issue also arises in
[
2
, Theorem 1.3]
.

In the modular curve case considered by Diamond, this is not an issue as the Hecke algebra is flat over
\mathcal{O}
and so has enough
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-points. For arithmetic manifolds associated to arbitrary number fields, the Hecke algebra
\mathbb{T}
is, in general, not flat over
\mathcal{O}
and may not have enough
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-points to determine the generic rank of
M_{\infty}
. In fact,
\mathbb{T}
may well have no
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-points at all! Thus the requirement that
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{T}
have a
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-point on each irreducible component of
\operatorname{Spec}R_{\infty}
is extremely restrictive.

In this paper we develop new commutative algebra arguments, building on those of
[
6
]
, to show freeness of patched modules under far less restrictive conditions. The main result is Theorem
4.1
which allows us to use information about the rank of
M_{\infty}
over one component of
\operatorname{Spec}R_{\infty}
to determine its rank over the other components.

When the number field
F
is totally complex (considered in Theorem
5.5
), this allows us to deduce freeness assuming only the existence of a
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-point of
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{T}
lying on one particular component of
\operatorname{Spec}R_{\infty}
(the “highest level” component). This is equivalent to assuming the existence of a particular geometric characteristic 0 lift of
\overline{\rho}_{\mathfrak{m}}
(ramified at a specified set of primes
\Sigma
, as well as some other auxiliary primes). One might expect that such a lift always exists (although this is far from known in general), and so our work represents a plausible approach for proving freeness of Hecke modules associated to totally complex fields.

For an arbitrary number field
F
(considered in Theorem
5.6
) the situation is somewhat more complicated. In order to deduce freeness from our method, one needs to prove an appropriate lower bound on the generic rank of
M_{\infty}
on the “minimal level” component in addition to having a characteristic
0
lift of
\overline{\rho}_{\mathfrak{m}}
corresponding to a point on the “highest level” component. In the case when
F
is totally complex, the required lower bound is
1
and so one gets this for free. However in the case of a general
F
, we are only able to prove this in the case when
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{T}
has an additional
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-point on the “minimal level” component of
\operatorname{Spec}R_{\infty}
.

2.
Congruence modules and Wiles defect

In this section we recall the construction and basic properties of congruence modules and Wiles defect, introduced in
[
6
, Section 2]
, for modules over local rings.

2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a discrete valuation ring, with valuation
\operatorname{ord}(-)
and uniformizer
\varpi
. Throughout we fix a complete local
\mathcal{O}
-algebra
A
and a finitely generated
A
-module
M
. Given a map
\lambda\colon A\to\mathcal{O}
of
\mathcal{O}
-algebras, set
\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Ker}(\lambda)
and

\Phi_{\lambda}(A)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{tors}(\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}/\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}^{2})\,,

namely, the torsion part of the cotangent module
\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}/\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}^{2}
of
\lambda
. This is a torsion
\mathcal{O}
-module. For any finitely generated
A
-module
M
, set

\operatorname{F}^{i}_{\lambda}(M)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}{\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A}(\mathcal{O},M)}^{\operatorname{tf}}

the torsion-free quotient of the
\mathcal{O}
-module
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A}(\mathcal{O},M)
. Here
\mathcal{O}
is viewed as an
A
-module via
\lambda
. The
congruence module
of
M
at
\lambda
is the
\mathcal{O}
-module

\Psi_{\lambda}(M)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Coker}\left(\operatorname{F}^{c}_{\lambda}(M)\xrightarrow{\ \operatorname{F}^{c}(\varepsilon)\ }\operatorname{F}^{c}_{\lambda}(M/\mathfrak{p}M)\right)

where
c\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}0pt{\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}}
and
\varepsilon\colon M\to M/\mathfrak{p}M
is the natural surjection.

2.2
.

Let
A
be an
\mathcal{O}
-algebra and
\lambda\colon A\to\mathcal{O}
a map of
\mathcal{O}
-algebras. The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

The local ring
A_{\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}}
is regular.

(2)

The rank of the
\mathcal{O}
-module
\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}/\mathfrak{p}^{2}_{\lambda}
is
0pt\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}
.

(3)

The
\mathcal{O}
-module
\Psi_{\lambda}(A)
is torsion.

(4)

The
\mathcal{O}
-module
\Psi_{\lambda}(M)
is torsion for each finitely generated
A
-module
M
.

Moreover, when these conditions hold the
\mathcal{O}
-module
\Psi_{\lambda}(A)
is cyclic.

Condition (2) is that the embedding dimension of the ring
A_{\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}}
is equal to its Krull dimension, so the equivalence of (1) and (2) is one definition of regularity; see
[
1
, Definition 2.2.1]
. For the other equivalences see

[
6
, Theorem 2.5 and Lemma 2.6]
.

The pairs
(A,\lambda)
satisfying the equivalent conditions above are the objects of a category
\operatorname{C}_{\mathcal{O}}
. A morphism
\varphi\colon(A,\lambda)\to(A^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})
in
\operatorname{C}_{\mathcal{O}}
is a map of
\mathcal{O}
-algebras
\varphi\colon A\to A^{\prime}
over
\mathcal{O}
; that is to say, with
\lambda^{\prime}\circ\varphi=\lambda
. For a natural number
c
, the subcategory
\operatorname{C}_{\mathcal{O}}(c)
of
\operatorname{C}_{\mathcal{O}}
consists of pairs
(A,\lambda)
such that
0pt\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}=c
.

2.3
.

Fix a pair
(A,\lambda)
in
\operatorname{C}_{\mathcal{O}}
and a finitely generated
A
-module
M
. Since
A
is regular at
\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}
, and in particular a domain, the
A_{\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}}
-module
M_{\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}}
has a rank. The
Wiles defect
of
M
at
\lambda
is the integer

\delta_{\lambda}(M)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{rank}_{A_{\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}})\cdot\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Phi_{\lambda}(A)-\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Psi_{\lambda}(M)\,.

In particular the Wiles defect of
A
at
\lambda
is

\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Phi_{\lambda}(A)-\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Psi_{\lambda}(A)\,.

We refer to
[
6
, Introduction]
for a discussion on precedents to this definition. Here are some salient properties of the Wiles defect.

(1)

If
A
is complete intersection, then
\delta_{\lambda}(A)=0
; the converse holds when in addition
0ptA\geq c+1
.

(2)

If
0pt_{A}M\geq c+1
, then
\delta_{\lambda}(M)\geq 0
.

(3)

If
\varphi\colon A^{\prime}\to A
is a surjective map in
\operatorname{C}_{\mathcal{O}}(c)
and
0pt_{A}M\geq c
, then

\delta_{\lambda\varphi}(M)=\delta_{\lambda}(M)\,.

We refer to this property as the invariance of domain for congruence modules.

(4)

Assume
A
is Gorenstein and
M
is maximal Cohen–Macaulay. If
\delta_{\lambda}(M)=\mu\cdot\delta_{\lambda}(A)
, for
\mu\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{rank}_{A_{\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}})
, then there is an isomorphism of
A
-modules

M\cong A^{\mu}\oplus W\quad\text{and $W_{\mathfrak{p}_{\lambda}}=0$.}

These results are established in
[
6
]
: For (1) and (2), see Theorem A; for (3), see Theorem E, and for (4) see Theorem B.

3.
The setup

Fix a finite set
T
. In the number theoretic applications,
T
will be a finite set of primes in the ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{F}
of a number field
F
. However, for ease of notation, in this section we take
T\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{1,\cdots,n\}
for some integer
n\geq 1
.

3.1
.

Fix an integer
g\geq 1
and the
\mathcal{O}
-algebra

A\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{\mathcal{O}[\![{x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{n},t_{1},\dots,t_{g}}]\!]}{(x_{1}y_{1},\dots,x_{n}y_{n})}\,.

For each subset
\Sigma\subseteq T
, set

A_{\Sigma}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}A/(x_{i}\mid i\notin\Sigma)\,.

Evidently each
A_{\Sigma}
is a reduced complete intersection, of dimension
n+g
. Moreover the ring
A_{\varnothing}\cong A/(\boldsymbol{x})
is regular. Given subsets
\Sigma^{\prime}\subseteq\Sigma
there is an induced surjection

A_{\Sigma}\twoheadrightarrow A_{\Sigma^{\prime}}\,,

of
\mathcal{O}
-algebras. In particular, the family
\{A_{\Sigma}\}
, as
\Sigma
various over subsets of
T
, has an initial object,
A_{T}=A
, and final object
A_{\varnothing}
.

For each
\Sigma\subseteq T
, consider the ideal

I_{\Sigma}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}A(x_{i}\mid i\notin\Sigma)+A(y_{j}\mid j\in\Sigma)\,.

Then the Zariski closed subsets
V(I_{\Sigma})
, as
\Sigma
varies over the subsets of
T
, are the irreducible components of
\operatorname{Spec}A
. Moreover

\operatorname{Spec}A_{\Sigma}=\bigcup_{\Sigma^{\prime}\subseteq\Sigma}V(I_{\Sigma^{\prime}})

viewed as subsets of
\operatorname{Spec}A
. Thus, the irreducible components of
\operatorname{Spec}A_{\Sigma}
are a subset of the irreducible components of
\operatorname{Spec}A
.

3.2
.

We focus on the
\mathcal{O}
-points in
\operatorname{Spec}A
, namely, the subset

\mathcal{V}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{(a_{1},\dots,a_{n},b_{1},\dots,b_{n},c_{1},\dots,c_{g})\in\varpi(\mathcal{O})^{2n+g}\mid a_{i}b_{i}=0\quad\text{for $1\leq i\leq n$}.\}

Since
\mathcal{O}
is a domain
a_{i}b_{i}=0
if and only if
a_{i}=0
or
b_{i}=0
. Each point
\boldsymbol{v}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b},\boldsymbol{c})
in
\mathcal{V}
corresponds to the prime ideal

\mathfrak{p}_{\boldsymbol{v}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(x_{i}-a_{i},y_{j}-b_{j},t_{k}-c_{k}\mid 1\leq i,j\leq n\text{ and }1\leq k\leq g)\,.

This is the kernel of the map of local
\mathcal{O}
-algebras

\lambda_{\boldsymbol{v}}\colon A\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}\quad\text{where $\lambda_{\boldsymbol{v}}(x_{i})=a_{i},\lambda_{\boldsymbol{v}}(y_{j})=b_{i},\text{ and }\lambda_{\boldsymbol{v}}(t_{k})=c_{k}$.}

For
\Sigma\subseteq T
consider the following subsets of
\mathcal{V}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\Sigma}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{(\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b},\boldsymbol{c})\in\mathcal{V}\mid\text{$a_{i}=0$ for $i\not\in\Sigma$}\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{\Sigma}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{(\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b},\boldsymbol{c})\in\mathcal{V}\mid\text{$a_{i}=0$ for $i\not\in\Sigma$ and $b_{j}=0$ for $j\in\Sigma$.}\}

Thus
\mathcal{V}_{\Sigma}
are the
\mathcal{O}
-valued points in
\operatorname{Spec}A_{\Sigma}
viewed as a subset
\operatorname{Spec}A
. Furthermore,
\mathcal{Z}_{\Sigma}
, as
\Sigma
varies over the subsets of
T
, are the
\mathcal{O}
-valued point of the irreducible components of
\operatorname{Spec}A
; see
3.1
. Set

(3.3)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Z}}^{\circ}_{\Sigma}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathcal{Z}_{\Sigma}\setminus\bigcup_{\Sigma^{\prime}\neq\Sigma}\mathcal{Z}_{\Sigma}

\displaystyle=\{(\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b},\boldsymbol{c})\in\mathcal{V}\mid\text{$a_{i}\neq 0$ for $i\in\Sigma$ and $b_{j}\neq 0$ for $j\notin\Sigma$.}\}

These are the
\mathcal{O}
-valued points that lie in a single irreducible component of
\operatorname{Spec}A
. Observe that in
{\mathcal{Z}}^{\circ}_{\Sigma}
there are points
\boldsymbol{v}
where a given subset of the components
\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b}
is fixed, and the other components are of any specified order. This fact is the key in the proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Each
\boldsymbol{v}
in
\mathcal{Z}^{\circ}_{\Sigma}
is a regular point of
A
, in that the local ring
A_{\mathfrak{p}_{\boldsymbol{v}}}
is regular. Set

(3.4)

{\mathcal{V}}^{\circ}_{\Sigma}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\bigsqcup_{\Sigma^{\prime}\subseteq\Sigma}{\mathcal{Z}}^{\circ}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}\,;

these are the
\mathcal{O}
-valued points on
\operatorname{Spec}A_{\Sigma}
that lie in a single component. Observe that the surjection
A\to A_{\Sigma}
is an isomorphism at each
\boldsymbol{v}
in
\mathcal{V}^{\circ}_{\Sigma}
, in that localization at
\boldsymbol{v}
is an isomorphism

A_{\mathfrak{p}_{\boldsymbol{v}}}\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }(A_{\Sigma})_{\mathfrak{p}_{\boldsymbol{v}}}\,.

Thus
\boldsymbol{v}
is regular also as a point in
\operatorname{Spec}A_{\Sigma}
; said otherwise,
(A_{\Sigma},\lambda_{\boldsymbol{v}})
is in
\operatorname{C}_{\mathcal{O}}
.

3.5
.

We compute the cotangent modules of the rings
A_{\Sigma}
at various points in
\mathcal{V}^{\circ}_{\Sigma}
. To ease up notation, we set

\Phi_{\boldsymbol{v}}(-)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Phi_{\lambda_{\boldsymbol{v}}}(-)\,.

Here is a simple computation.

Lemma 3.6
.

For
\Sigma^{\prime}\subseteq\Sigma
and
\boldsymbol{v}
is in
\mathcal{Z}^{\circ}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}
one gets

\Phi_{\boldsymbol{v}}(A_{\Sigma})=\bigoplus_{i\in\Sigma^{\prime}}\frac{\mathcal{O}[y_{i}]}{a_{i}[y_{i}]}\bigoplus_{j\in\Sigma\setminus\Sigma^{\prime}}\frac{\mathcal{O}[x_{j}]}{b_{j}[x_{j}]}\,,

and hence
\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Phi_{\boldsymbol{v}}(A_{\Sigma})=\sum_{i\in\Sigma^{\prime}}\operatorname{ord}(a_{i})+\sum_{j\in\Sigma\setminus\Sigma^{\prime}}\operatorname{ord}(b_{j})
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we can assume
\Sigma=T
. For
\boldsymbol{v}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b},\boldsymbol{c})
in
\mathcal{V}_{\Sigma}
one has

x_{i}y_{i}=b_{i}(x_{i}-a_{i})+a_{i}(y_{i}-b_{i})+(x_{i}-a_{i})(y_{i}-b_{i})

so it is immediate from the description of
\mathfrak{p}_{\boldsymbol{v}}
, see
3.2
, that the cotangent module
\mathfrak{p}_{\boldsymbol{v}}/\mathfrak{p}_{\boldsymbol{v}}^{2}
is generated as an
\mathcal{O}
-module by classes
[x_{i}-a_{i}],[y_{i}-b_{i}]
, for
i\in T
, and
[t_{k}-c_{k}]
for
1\leq k\leq g
, subject to the relations

b_{i}[x_{i}-a_{i}]+a_{i}[y_{i}-b_{i}]=0\quad\text{for $i\in T$}\,.

That is to say, there is an isomorphism of
\mathcal{O}
-modules

\mathfrak{p}_{\boldsymbol{v}}/\mathfrak{p}_{\boldsymbol{v}}^{2}=\bigoplus_{i\in T}\frac{\mathcal{O}[x_{i}-a_{i}]\oplus\mathcal{O}[y_{i}-b_{i}]}{b_{i}[x_{i}-a_{i}]+a_{i}[y_{i}-b_{i}]}\bigoplus_{k=1}^{g}\mathcal{O}[t_{k}-c_{k}]

Thus when
\boldsymbol{v}
is in
\mathcal{Z}^{\circ}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}
, it is clear from (
3.3
) that

\Phi_{\boldsymbol{v}}(A)=\bigoplus_{i\in\Sigma^{\prime}}\frac{\mathcal{O}[y_{i}]}{a_{i}[y_{i}]}\bigoplus_{j\not\in\Sigma^{\prime}}\frac{\mathcal{O}[x_{j}]}{b_{j}[x_{j}]}

This is the desired result.
∎

We also need to compute the cotangent module of the ring

B\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}A_{\Sigma}/I\quad\text{where}\quad I\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\bigcap_{\Sigma^{\prime}\subsetneq\Sigma}I_{\Sigma^{\prime}}\,.

This ring is not complete intersections when
|\Sigma|\geq 2
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Fix a subset
\Sigma\subseteq T
and let
B
be the quotient ring defined above. Fix
s\in\Sigma
and set
\Sigma^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Sigma\setminus\{s\}
. For any
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathcal{Z}^{\circ}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}
one has

\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Phi_{\boldsymbol{v}}(B)=\sum_{i\in\Sigma^{\prime}}\operatorname{ord}(a_{i})+\min\{\operatorname{ord}(b_{s}),\sum_{i\in\Sigma^{\prime}}\operatorname{ord}(a_{i})\}\,.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can assume
\Sigma=T
and
s=1
. Then

B=A/I\quad\text{where $I=(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})$.}

For
\boldsymbol{v}=(\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b},\boldsymbol{c})
in
\mathcal{Z}^{\circ}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}
one has
a_{1}=0
and
a_{i}\neq 0
for
i\geq 2
. Then

x_{1}\cdots x_{n}=x_{1}(x_{2}-a_{2}+a_{2})\cdots(x_{n}-a_{n}+a_{n})=(a_{2}\dots a_{n})x_{1}+z

where
z
is a sum of monomials quadratic or higher in the
(x_{i}-a_{i})
. It follows that
\Phi_{\boldsymbol{v}}(B)
is the quotient of
\Phi_{\boldsymbol{v}}(A)
by the term
(a_{2}\dots a_{n})[x_{1}]
. Thus the stated inequality is immediate from the description of
\Phi_{\boldsymbol{v}}(A)
in Lemma
3.6
.
∎

3.8
.

We keep the notation from
3.1
and
3.2
. Let
M_{\Sigma}
be a maximal Cohen–Macaulay
A_{\Sigma}
-module; it is also maximal Cohen–Macaulay as an
A
-module.

For each point
\boldsymbol{v}
in
\mathcal{V}^{\circ}_{\Sigma}
, described in (
3.4
), the ring
A_{\Sigma}
is regular at
\boldsymbol{v}
, so
M_{\Sigma}
is free at
\boldsymbol{v}
, that is to say,
(M_{\Sigma})_{\mathfrak{p}_{\boldsymbol{v}}}
is free over
(A_{\Sigma})_{\mathfrak{p}_{\boldsymbol{v}}}\cong A_{\mathfrak{p}_{\boldsymbol{v}}}
; this is by the Auslander-Buchsbaum equality; see
[
1
, Theorem 1.3.3]
. Moreover, the rank of this free module is the same at any
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathcal{Z}^{\circ}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}
, for any
\Sigma^{\prime}\subseteq\Sigma
, for these lie in the same irreducible component of
\operatorname{Spec}A_{\Sigma}
. We denote this number
\operatorname{rank}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}(M_{\Sigma})
; thus

\operatorname{rank}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}(M_{\Sigma})=\operatorname{rank}_{A_{\mathfrak{p}_{\boldsymbol{v}}}}(M_{\Sigma})_{\mathfrak{p}_{\boldsymbol{v}}}\,.

We extend this to all
\Sigma^{\prime}\subseteq T
by setting
\operatorname{rank}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}(M_{\Sigma})=0
when
\Sigma^{\prime}\not\subseteq\Sigma
, because
(M_{\Sigma})_{\mathfrak{p}_{\boldsymbol{v}}}=0

for
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathcal{Z}^{\circ}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}
.

3.9
.

We fix a family of modules
M_{\Sigma}
, where
M_{\Sigma}
is an
A_{\Sigma}
-module, equipped with
A_{\Sigma}
-linear surjections

\pi_{\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}}\colon M_{\Sigma}\twoheadrightarrow M_{\Sigma^{\prime}}\,,

whenever
\Sigma^{\prime}\subseteq\Sigma
, satisfying the following properties:

(1)

M_{\Sigma}
is a self-dual, maximal Cohen–Macaulay,
A_{\Sigma}
-module;

(2)

\pi_{\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}}
is an isomorphism at
\mathfrak{p}_{\boldsymbol{v}}
for each
\boldsymbol{v}
in
\mathcal{V}^{\circ}_{\Sigma}
.

(3)

For any integer
s\not\in\Sigma^{\prime}
, and
\Sigma\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Sigma^{\prime}\cup\{s\}
, the composition

M_{\Sigma^{\prime}}\cong(M_{\Sigma^{\prime}})^{\vee}\xrightarrow{\ (\pi_{\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}})^{\vee}\ }(M_{\Sigma})^{\vee}\cong M_{\Sigma}\xrightarrow{\ \pi_{\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}}\ }M_{\Sigma^{\prime}}\,,

is multiplication by
y_{s}
.

Following the discussion in
3.8
, for
\Sigma\subseteq T
set

\mu_{\Sigma}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{rank}_{\Sigma}M_{T}\,.

Given the isomorphism in condition (2) above, for subsets
\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}\subseteq T
one has

\operatorname{rank}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}M_{\Sigma}=\begin{cases}\mu_{\Sigma^{\prime}}&\text{when $\Sigma^{\prime}\subseteq\Sigma$}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

To ease up notation, we set

\Psi_{\boldsymbol{v}}(M)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Psi_{\lambda_{\boldsymbol{v}}}(M)\,.

Here is a computation of congruence modules.

Lemma 3.10
.

Fix
\Sigma^{\prime}\subseteq\Sigma\subseteq T
and
\boldsymbol{v}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b},\boldsymbol{c})
in
\mathcal{Z}^{\circ}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}
one has

\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Psi_{\boldsymbol{v}}(M_{\Sigma})=\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Psi_{\boldsymbol{v}}(M_{\Sigma^{\prime}})+\mu_{\Sigma^{\prime}}(\sum_{i\in\Sigma\setminus\Sigma^{\prime}}\operatorname{ord}(b_{i}))\,.

Proof.

We verify that by an induction on the cardinality of
\Sigma\setminus\Sigma^{\prime}
. The base case is when
\Sigma=\Sigma^{\prime}
, and then the stated equality is clear.

For the induction step it suffices to note that if
\Sigma=\Sigma^{\prime}\cup\{s\}
for
s\notin\Sigma^{\prime}
, then

\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Psi_{\boldsymbol{v}}(M_{\Sigma})=\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Psi_{\boldsymbol{v}}(M_{\Sigma^{\prime}})+\operatorname{rank}_{\boldsymbol{v}}(M_{\Sigma})\operatorname{ord}(b_{s})\,.

This is immediate by property
3.9
(3) and
[
6
, Proposition 4.4]
.
∎

Proposition 3.11
.

For each
\Sigma\subseteq T
the
A_{\Sigma}
-module
M_{\Sigma}
has a free summand of rank
\mu_{\varnothing}
. In particular, one gets an inequality
\mu_{\Sigma}\geq\mu_{\varnothing}
.

Proof.

Pick a
\boldsymbol{v}
in
\mathcal{Z}^{\circ}_{\varnothing}
. Since the local ring
A_{\varnothing}
is regular and
M_{\varnothing}
is maximal Cohen–Macaulay, it is free, and of rank equal to
\mu_{\varnothing}
. Thus
\Psi_{\boldsymbol{v}}(M_{\varnothing})=0
and hence from Lemma
3.10
applied with
\Sigma^{\prime}=\varnothing
one gets the first equality below:

\displaystyle\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Psi_{\boldsymbol{v}}(M_{\Sigma})

\displaystyle=\mu_{\varnothing}\cdot\big(\sum_{i\in\Sigma}\operatorname{ord}(b_{i})\big)

\displaystyle=\mu_{\varnothing}\cdot\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Phi_{\boldsymbol{v}}(A_{\Sigma})\,.

The second equality is by Lemma
3.10
, applied with
\Sigma^{\prime}=\varnothing
. Since
A_{\Sigma}
is complete intersection, and hence Gorenstein, it remains to apply
2.3
(4).
∎

4.
A criterion for freeness

The theorem below builds on and strengthens Proposition
3.11
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\{A_{\Sigma}\}
and
\{M_{\Sigma}\}
be the families of rings and modules described in
3.1
and
3.9
, respectively. If
\mu_{T}\leq\mu_{\varnothing}
, then
M_{\Sigma}\cong A_{\Sigma}^{\mu_{\varnothing}}
for each
\Sigma\subseteq T
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.11
, for each
\Sigma\subseteq T
there is an
A_{\Sigma}
-module
W_{\Sigma}
and an isomorphism of
A_{\Sigma}
-modules

(4.2)

M_{\Sigma}\cong A_{\Sigma}^{\mu_{\varnothing}}\oplus W_{\Sigma}\,.

We prove that
W_{\Sigma}
is zero. To that end it suffices to prove
\mu_{\Sigma}\leq\mu_{\varnothing}
for each
\Sigma\subseteq T
.

Indeed, then
\operatorname{rank}_{\boldsymbol{v}}(M_{\Sigma})\leq\mu_{\varnothing}
for
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathcal{Z}^{\circ}_{\Sigma}
, as described in (
3.4
). Thus the isomorphism in (
4.2
) implies that
\operatorname{rank}_{\boldsymbol{v}}(W_{\Sigma})=0
for each such
\boldsymbol{v}
. This implies that the support of
W_{\Sigma}
does not contain any component of
\operatorname{Spec}A_{\Sigma}
. Since
W_{\Sigma}
is a maximal Cohen–Macaulay
A_{\Sigma}
-module, we conclude that it is zero, as desired.

It thus remains to verify that

\mu_{\Sigma}\leq\mu_{\varnothing}\quad\text{for each $\Sigma\subseteq T$.}

We verify this by computing the congruence module of
W_{\Sigma}
at suitable points
\boldsymbol{v}
in
\mathcal{V}^{\circ}_{\Sigma}
, and proving that, if the desired inequality does not hold, then there are points
\boldsymbol{v}
at which the Wiles defect of
W_{\Sigma}
would be negative, violating
2.3
(
2
).

Since the inequality above holds when
\Sigma=T
, it suffices to prove that if the inequality holds for a given
\Sigma
, it holds also for
\Sigma^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Sigma\setminus\{s\}
for any
s\in\Sigma
.

With
\Sigma
and
\Sigma^{\prime}
as above, fix
\boldsymbol{v}
in
\mathcal{Z}^{\circ}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}
. The isomorphisms in (
4.2
), applied to
\Sigma
and
\Sigma^{\prime}
, yield the first equality and the third inequality, respectively, below:

(4.3)

\displaystyle\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Psi_{\boldsymbol{v}}(W_{\Sigma})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Psi_{\boldsymbol{v}}(M_{\Sigma})-\mu_{\varnothing}\cdot\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Psi_{\boldsymbol{v}}(A_{\Sigma})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Psi_{\boldsymbol{v}}(M_{\Sigma^{\prime}})+\mu_{\Sigma^{\prime}}\cdot\operatorname{ord}(b_{s})-\mu_{\varnothing}\cdot\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Psi_{\boldsymbol{v}}(A_{\Sigma})

\displaystyle\geq\mu_{\varnothing}\cdot\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Psi_{\boldsymbol{v}}(A_{\Sigma^{\prime}})+\mu_{\Sigma^{\prime}}\cdot\operatorname{ord}(b_{s})-\mu_{\varnothing}\cdot\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Psi_{\boldsymbol{v}}(A_{\Sigma})

\displaystyle=\mu_{\varnothing}\cdot\left[\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Psi_{\boldsymbol{v}}(A_{\Sigma^{\prime}})-\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Psi_{\boldsymbol{v}}(A_{\Sigma})\right]+\mu_{\Sigma^{\prime}}\cdot\operatorname{ord}(b_{s})

\displaystyle=(\mu_{\Sigma^{\prime}}-\mu_{\varnothing})\cdot\operatorname{ord}(b_{s})\,.

The second equality is by Lemma
3.10
while the last one is by Lemma
3.6
.

Since
\mu_{\Sigma}\leq\mu_{\varnothing}
, the
A_{\Sigma}
-module
W_{\Sigma}
is not supported on
\mathcal{Z}^{\circ}_{\Sigma}
. Since
W_{\Sigma}
is also maximal Cohen–Macaulay, it follows that the
A_{\Sigma}
action on it factors through the quotient ring
B\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}A_{\Sigma}/I
, where

I\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\bigcap_{\Sigma^{\prime\prime}\subsetneq\Sigma}I^{\prime\prime}_{\Sigma}

considered in Lemma
3.7
. Observe that the surjection
A_{\Sigma}\to A_{\Sigma^{\prime}}
factors through the map
A_{\Sigma}\to B
. In particular, the latter is also an isomorphism at
\boldsymbol{v}
, and hence the pair
(B,\lambda_{\boldsymbol{v}}\circ\varepsilon)
, where
\varepsilon\colon B\to A_{\Sigma^{\prime}}
is the canonical surjection, is also in
\operatorname{C}_{\mathcal{O}}
, and the results in
2.3
apply to this pair as well.

Since
W_{\Sigma}
is maximal Cohen–Macaulay, the invariance of domain property of congruence modules
2.3
(
3
) gives the equality below:

(4.4)

\displaystyle(\mu_{\Sigma^{\prime}}-\mu_{\varnothing})\cdot\operatorname{length}\Phi_{\boldsymbol{v}}(B)

\displaystyle\geq\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Psi_{\boldsymbol{v}}^{B}(W_{\Sigma})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Psi_{\boldsymbol{v}}(W_{\Sigma})

\displaystyle\geq(\mu_{\Sigma^{\prime}}-\mu_{\varnothing})\cdot\operatorname{ord}(b_{s})\,,

where
\Psi_{\boldsymbol{v}}^{B}(W_{\Sigma})
is the congruence module of
W_{\Sigma}
, treated as a
B
-module. The first inequality holds because the Wiles defect of the
B
-module
W_{\Sigma}
at
\boldsymbol{v}
is non-negative; see
2.3
(
2
). The last inequality is from (
4.3
).

Suppose, contrary to the desired result,
\mu_{\Sigma^{\prime}}>\mu_{\varnothing}
. Then (
4.4
) implies

\operatorname{length}\Phi_{\boldsymbol{v}}(B)\geq\operatorname{ord}(b_{s})\quad\text{for each $\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathcal{Z}^{\circ}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}$.}

On the other hand, from Lemma
3.7
we get that

\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Phi_{\boldsymbol{v}}(B)=\sum_{i\in\Sigma}\operatorname{ord}(a_{i})+\min\{\operatorname{ord}(b_{s}),\sum_{i\in\Sigma}\operatorname{ord}(a_{i})\}\,.

In particular, when
b_{s}\geq\sum_{i\in\Sigma}\operatorname{ord}(a_{i})
combining the (in)equalities above yields

2\sum_{i\in\Sigma}\operatorname{ord}(a_{i})\geq\operatorname{ord}(b_{s})\quad\text{for each $\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathcal{Z}^{\circ}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}$.}

It is clear from (
3.3
) that there are points
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathcal{Z}^{\circ}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}
with
\operatorname{ord}(b_{s})
arbitrarily large and
a_{i}
fixed. This violates the inequality above, yielding the contradiction we seek.
∎

4.5
.

Theorem
4.1
addresses only rings of the form considered in (
3.1
). This is primarily for ease of exposition, as this is sufficient to deal with the rings considered in
[
6
]
. One could prove analogues of Theorem
4.1
with the rings
A_{\Sigma}
replaced by more general classes of Gorenstein rings.

Indeed, there are more general families of rings
A_{\Sigma}
and modules
M_{\Sigma}
for which the argument of Proposition
3.11
applies. Besides Proposition
3.11
, the primary input into the proof of Theorem
4.1
is Lemma
3.7
, which allows one to pick points
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathcal{Z}^{\circ}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}
making
\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Psi_{\boldsymbol{v}}(M_{\Sigma})
arbitrarily large, while keeping
\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}}\Phi_{\boldsymbol{v}}(B)
bounded. But one can find such
\boldsymbol{v}
in more general cases than we have considered here (roughly, one merely needs to find a point
\boldsymbol{v}_{0}\in\mathcal{Z}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}\cap\mathcal{Z}_{\Sigma}
at which
\operatorname{Spec}B
is smooth, and pick
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathcal{Z}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}^{\circ}
to be sufficiently close to
\boldsymbol{v}_{0}
).

This could potentially allow one to prove freeness of Hecke modules at more general levels than we consider here and in
[
6
]
.

In this section we apply our results from Section

to the number theoretic context explored in

Let

From now on assume that

For any

]
. Let

]
.

By expanding

, and so in particular

Now assume that

\displaystyle\mu_{\Sigma^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\dim_{E}(M_{\Sigma^{\prime},\infty}\otimes_{\lambda}E)

\operatorname{H}_{r_{2}}(Y_{0}(\mathcal{N}_{\Sigma}),\mathcal{O})_{\mathfrak{m}_{\Sigma}}\cong R_{\Sigma}\cong\mathbb{T}_{\Sigma}

Theorem 5.5
.

Let

be a totally complex number field in which

hold for

.

and

is absolutely irreducible. Let
\Sigma
be a finite set of primes of

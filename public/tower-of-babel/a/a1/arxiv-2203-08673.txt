How to construct Gorenstein projective modules relative to complete duality pairs over Morita rings
Yajun Ma, Jiafeng L

Abstract




\bf Abstract

Let
\Delta=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&{{}_{A}N_{B}}\\
{{}_{B}M_{A}}&B\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)
be a Morita ring with
M\otimes_{A}N=0=N\otimes_{B}M
.
We first study how to construct
(complete) duality pairs of
\Delta
-modules using (complete) duality pairs of
A
-modules and
B
-modules, generalizing the result of Mao (Comm. Algebra, 2020, 12: 5296–5310) about the duality pairs over a triangular matrix ring. Moreover, we construct Gorenstein projective modules relative to complete duality pairs of
\Delta
-modules. Finally, we give an application to Ding projective modules.

Keywords:
Duality pair; Morita ring; Gorenstein projective object.

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification:
18G80, 18G25, 16E30.

1.
Introduction

The notion of duality pairs was introduced by Holm and Jøgensen in
[
15
]
, which is very useful in relative homological algebra because duality pairs are very closely related to purity, existences of covers and envelopes, and to complete cotorsion pairs. Recently, in order to show how Gorenstein homological algebra can be done with respect to a
duality pair, the notion of a complete duality pair was introduced by Gillespie in
[
10
]
. For a given complete duality pair
(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{A})
, he introduced the notion of Gorenstein
(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{A})
-projective modules (see
[
10
, Definition 4.2]
or Definition
4.1
below). Indeed, if
R
is a commutative Noetherian ring of finite Krull dimension, then this definition, applied to the flat-injective duality pair, agrees with the usual definition of Gorenstein projective modules studied by Enochs and many other authors. See
[
6
]
for the basic theory of Gorenstein projective modules.

One of interesting topics in Gorenstein homological algebra is to determine Gorenstein projective modules over the triangular matrix ring

\Delta=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&{{}_{A}N_{B}}\\
0&B\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)
under some conditions on the bimodule
{}_{A}N_{B}
. See
[
20
,
28
,
30
,
19
]
for instance. A natural extension of triangular matrix rings is the class of Morita rings. Recall that
Morita rings are
2\times 2
matrix rings associated to Morita contexts
[
1
,
3
]
. For more recent results on Morita rings, we refer to
[
14
,
9
,
8
,
29
]
. A particular case of interest is the Morita ring
\Delta=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&{{}_{A}}N_{B}\\
{{}_{B}}M_{A}&B\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)
with
M\otimes_{A}N=0=N\otimes_{B}M
. The reason is that the natural functors
\mathrm{T}_{A}
and
\mathrm{H}_{A}
(resp.,
\mathrm{T}_{B}
and
\mathrm{H}_{B}
) defined by Green-Psaroudakis in
[
14
]
preserve the left half of duality pairs from the category of left
A
-modules (resp., left
B
-modules) to the category of left
\Delta
-modules (see Lemma
3.3
and Theorem
3.4
).
The main problem considered in this paper is:

Problem.
Construct Gorenstein projective modules relative to complete duality pairs over the Morita ring
\Delta=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&{{}_{A}}N_{B}\\
{{}_{B}}M_{A}&B\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)
with
M\otimes_{A}N=0=N\otimes_{B}M
.

In order to solve this problem, we first give a method to construct duality pairs over the Morita ring
\Delta
. To this end, for any class
\mathcal{C}_{1}
(resp.,
\mathcal{C}_{2}
) of left (resp., right)
A
-modules and any class
\mathcal{D}_{1}
(resp.,
\mathcal{D}_{2}
) of left (resp., right)
B
-modules, we denote by
\mathfrak{B}^{\mathcal{C}_{1}}_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}
the class of left
\Delta
-modules
(X,Y,f,g)

such that
f:M\otimes_{A}X\rightarrow Y
and
g:N\otimes_{B}Y\rightarrow X
are monomorphisms,
X/\mbox{\rm im}(g)\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
Y/\mbox{\rm im}(f)\in\mathcal{D}_{1},
and by
\mathfrak{J}_{\mathcal{C}_{2},\mathcal{D}_{2}}
the class of right
\Delta
-modules
(X,Y,f,g)
such that
\tilde{f}:X\rightarrow\mbox{\rm Hom}_{B}(N,Y)
and
\tilde{g}:Y\rightarrow\mbox{\rm Hom}_{A}(M,X)
are epimorphisms,
\mbox{\rm ker}\tilde{f}\in\mathcal{C}_{2}
and
\mbox{\rm ker}\tilde{g}\in\mathcal{D}_{2},
where
\tilde{f}(x)(n)=f(x\otimes n)
for
f\in\mbox{\rm Hom}_{B}(X\otimes_{A}N,Y),x\in X,n\in N
and
\tilde{g}(y)(m)=g(y\otimes m)
for
g\in\mbox{\rm Hom}_{A}(Y\otimes_{B}M,X),y\in Y,m\in M.

We have the following theorem which is contained in Theorem
3.4
and Corollary
3.8
below.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
\Delta=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&{{}_{A}}N_{B}\\
{{}_{B}}M_{A}&B\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)
is a Morita ring with
M\otimes_{A}N=0=N\otimes_{B}M
. Let
\mathcal{C}_{1}
(resp.,
\mathcal{C}_{2}
) be a class of left (resp., right)
A
-modules and
\mathcal{D}_{1}
(resp.,
\mathcal{D}_{2}
) be a class of left (resp., right)
B
-modules.

(1)

(\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2})
and
(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})
are duality pairs if and only if
(\mathfrak{B}^{\mathcal{C}_{1}}_{\mathcal{D}_{1}},\mathfrak{J}_{{\mathcal{C}_{2}},{\mathcal{D}_{2}}})
is a duality pair.

(2)

If
N_{B}
and
M_{A}
are finitely generated projective modules, then
(\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2})
and
(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})
are complete duality pairs if and only if
(\mathfrak{B}^{\mathcal{C}_{1}}_{\mathcal{D}_{1}},\mathfrak{J}_{{\mathcal{C}_{2}},{\mathcal{D}_{2}}})
is a complete duality pair.

We note that Theorem
1.1
(1) generalizes the equivalence of (1) and (2) in
[
24
, Theorem 2.1]
to a more general case, where
the author assumes the ring
\Delta
to be a triangular matrix ring.

Assume that
\mathcal{L}
(resp.,
\mathcal{A}
) is a class of left (resp., right)
R
-modules over a ring
R
such that
(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{A})
is a complete duality pair of
R
-modules. Recall from
[
10
, Definition 4.2]
that a left
R
-module
X
is called Gorenstein
(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{A})
-projective if there exists an exact complex of projective left
R
-modules

\mathbb{P}:\cdots\rightarrow P^{-1}\xrightarrow{d^{-1}}P^{0}\xrightarrow{d^{0}}P^{1}\rightarrow\cdots

such that
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(\mathbb{P},L)
is exact for any
L\in\mathcal{L}
and that
X\cong\ker d^{0}.
As a consequence of Theorem
1.1
, we have the following theorem which provides a solution to the above problem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
\Delta=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&{{}_{A}}N_{B}\\
{{}_{B}}M_{A}&B\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)
is a Morita ring with
M\otimes_{A}N=0=N\otimes_{B}M.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{1}
(resp.,
\mathcal{C}_{2}
) be a class of left (resp., right)
A
-modules and
\mathcal{D}_{1}
(resp.,
\mathcal{D}_{2}
) be a class of left (resp., right)
B
-modules such that
(\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2})
and
(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})
are complete duality pairs over
A
and
B
respectively, and let
N_{B}
and
M_{A}
be finitely generated projective.

(1)

Assume that
N\otimes_{B}D\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
for any
D\in\mathcal{D}_{1}.

If
X
is a Gorenstein
(\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2})
-projective left
A
-module, then
\mathrm{T}_{A}(X)
is a Gorenstein
(\mathfrak{B}^{\mathcal{C}_{1}}_{\mathcal{D}_{1}},\mathfrak{J}_{{\mathcal{C}_{2}},{\mathcal{D}_{2}}})
-projective left
\Delta
-module.

(2)

Assume that
M\otimes_{A}C\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
for any
C\in\mathcal{C}_{1}.

If
Y
is a Gorenstein
(\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2})
-projective left
B
-module, then
\mathrm{T}_{B}(Y)
is a Gorenstein
(\mathfrak{B}^{\mathcal{C}_{1}}_{\mathcal{D}_{1}},\mathfrak{J}_{{\mathcal{C}_{2}},{\mathcal{D}_{2}}})
-projective left
\Delta
-module.

As a consequence of Theorem
1.2
, we provide a method to construct Ding projecive modules over the Morita ring
\Delta
under some conditions (see Corollary
4.8
).

The structure of this paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we fix notations, recall some
definitions and properties of Morita rings for later
proofs. In Section 3, we first provide a method to construct
duality pairs of
\Delta
-modules using duality pairs of
A
-modules and
B
-modules (see Theorem
3.4
), and then demonstrate that the above constructions still stand whenever the duality pairs of
A
-modules and
B
-modules are perfect or complete (see Theorem
3.7
and Corollary
3.8
.
In Section 4, we construct Gorenstein projective modules relative to the complete duality pair over the Morita ring
\Delta
(see Theorem
4.3
). This is based on the above construcuton of duality pairs over the Morita ring
\Delta
established in Section 3. Moreover, we give an application to the construction of Ding projective modules.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout this paper, all rings are nonzero associative ring with identity and all modules are unitary. All classes of modules are assumed to be closed under isomorphisms and contain 0.
For a ring
R
, we write
R\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}
(resp.,
\mathrm{Mod}\text{-}R
) for the category of left (resp., right)
R
-modules, and we use
{}_{R}X
(resp.,
X_{R}
) to denote a left (resp., right)
R
-module
X
, and the injective dimension of
{}_{R}X
is denoted by
\mathrm{id}_{R}X.

The character module
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
of a
R
-module
M
is denoted by
M^{+}.

Let
A
and
B
be two rings,
{}_{B}M_{A}
and
{}_{A}N_{B}
bimodules with
M\otimes_{A}N=0=N\otimes_{B}M.
Then
\Delta=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&{{}_{A}}N_{B}\\
{{}_{B}}M_{A}&B\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)
has a natural ring structure, where the addition is obvious and the mutiplication is given by

\left(\begin{matrix}a&n\\
m&b\\
\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}a^{\prime}&n^{\prime}\\
m^{\prime}&b^{\prime}\\
\end{matrix}\right)=\left(\begin{matrix}aa^{\prime}&an^{\prime}+nb^{\prime}\\
ma^{\prime}+bm^{\prime}&bb^{\prime}\\
\end{matrix}\right).

This is the special case of the general version of Morita rings in the sense of H. Bass
[
1
]
.

In the rest of this paper, we always assume that
\Delta=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&{{}_{A}}N_{B}\\
{{}_{B}}M_{A}&B\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)
is a Morita ring with
M\otimes_{A}N=0=N\otimes_{B}M
.

.

\Delta

is a right
\Delta
-module.
Let

Then
G^{\prime}((X\oplus Y)^{+})=((X\oplus Y)^{+}e,(X\oplus Y)^{+}(1-e),g^{\prime},f^{\prime}),

\end{smallmatrix}\right),g^{\prime}:(X\oplus Y)^{+}e\otimes_{A}N\rightarrow(X\oplus Y)^{+}(1-e)

We claim that
((X\oplus Y)^{+}e,(X\oplus Y)^{+}(1-e),g^{\prime},f^{\prime})\cong(X^{+},Y^{+},g_{+},f_{+})
, where

and

,

Indeed, it is easy to check that

3.
Duality pairs over Morita rings

In this section, we study duality pairs over the Morita ring
\Delta
. First, we recall some definitions and facts.

Definition 3.1
.

[
15
, Definition 2.1]

A duality pair of
R
-modules is a pair
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
, where
\mathcal{X}
is a class of left (resp., right)
R
-modules and
\mathcal{Y}
is a class of right (resp., left)
R
-modules, subject to the following conditions:

(1)

For an
R
-module
X
, one has
X\in\mathcal{X}
if and only if
X^{+}\in\mathcal{Y}.

(2)

\mathcal{Y}
is closed under direct summands and finite direct sums.

Recall from
[
15
]
that a duality pair
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
of
R
-modules is called perfect if
\mathcal{X}
contains
R
and is closed under direct sums and extensions.

Remark 3.2
.

Let
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
be a duality pair of
R
-modules. Then
\mathcal{X}
is closed under direct summands and finite direct sums.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a class of left
A
-modules and
\mathcal{D}
be a class of left
B
-modules.
We introduce the following classes of left
\Delta
-modules.

(1)
\mathfrak{A}^{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{D}}=

\{(X,Y,f,g):X\in\mathcal{C},Y\in\mathcal{D}\}
.

(2)
\mathfrak{B}^{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{D}}=

\{(X,Y,f,g):f~\text{and}~g\text{~are~monomorphisms,}~X/\mbox{\rm im}(g)\in\mathcal{C}~\text{and}~Y/\mbox{\rm im}(f)\in\mathcal{D}\}
.

(3)
\mathfrak{J}^{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{D}}=

\{(X,Y,f,g):\widetilde{f}~\text{and}~\widetilde{g}\text{~are~epimorphisms,}~\mbox{\rm ker}(\widetilde{f})\in\mathcal{C}~\text{and}~\mbox{\rm ker}(\widetilde{g})\in\mathcal{D}\}.

If
\mathcal{C}
is a class of right
A
-modules and
\mathcal{D}
is a class of right
B
-modules, then
there are similar symbols
\mathfrak{A}_{{\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{D}}}
,
\mathfrak{B}_{{\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{D}}}
,

\mathfrak{J}_{{\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{D}}}
for the cases of right
\Delta
-modules.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{1}
(resp.,
\mathcal{C}_{2}
) be a class of left (resp., right)
A
-modules and
\mathcal{D}_{1}
(resp.,
\mathcal{D}_{2}
) be a class of left (resp., right)
B
-modules.
Then

(1)

X\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
if and only if
\mathrm{T}_{A}(X)\in\mathfrak{B}^{\mathcal{C}_{1}}_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}
.

(1’)

Y\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
if and only if
\mathrm{T}_{B}(Y)\in\mathfrak{B}^{\mathcal{C}_{1}}_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}
.

(2)

Let
X
be a left
A
-module. Then
X^{+}\in\mathcal{C}_{2}
if and only if
\mathrm{T}_{A}(X)^{+}\in\mathfrak{J}_{{\mathcal{C}_{2}},{\mathcal{D}_{2}}}.

(2’)

Let
Y
be a left
B
-module. Then
Y^{+}\in\mathcal{D}_{2}
if and only if
\mathrm{T}_{B}(Y)^{+}\in\mathfrak{J}_{{\mathcal{C}_{2}},{\mathcal{D}_{2}}}.

(3)

X\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
if and only if
\mathrm{H}_{A}(X)\in\mathfrak{J}^{\mathcal{C}_{1}}_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}.

(3’)

Y\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
if and only if
\mathrm{H}_{B}(Y)\in\mathfrak{J}^{\mathcal{C}_{1}}_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}.

(4)

Let
X
be a left
A
-module. If
{}_{A}N
is finite presented, then
X^{+}\in\mathcal{C}_{2}
if and only if
\mathrm{H}_{A}(X)^{+}\in\mathfrak{B}_{{\mathcal{C}_{2}},{\mathcal{D}_{2}}}.

(4’)

Let
Y
be a left
B
-module. If
{}_{B}M
is finitely generated, then
Y^{+}\in\mathcal{D}_{2}
if and only if
\mathrm{H}_{B}(Y)^{+}\in\mathfrak{B}_{{\mathcal{C}_{2}},{\mathcal{D}_{2}}}.

Proof.

We only prove (1)-(4), and the proofs of (1’)-(4’) are similar.

(1) Since
N\otimes_{B}M=0
by hypothesis, it is easy to check that
X\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
if and only if
\mathrm{T}_{A}(X)\in\mathfrak{B}^{\mathcal{C}_{1}}_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}
.

(2) Since
N\otimes_{B}M=0
by hypothesis,
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{B}(N,(M\otimes_{A}X)^{+})\cong(N\otimes_{B}M\otimes_{A}X)^{+}=0.

Then it follows that
X^{+}\in\mathcal{C}_{2}
if and only if
\mathrm{T}_{A}(X)^{+}\in\mathfrak{J}_{{\mathcal{C}_{2}},{\mathcal{D}_{2}}}.

(3) Since
N\otimes_{B}M=0
by hypothesis,
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{B}(M,\mbox{\rm Hom}_{A}(N,X))\cong\mbox{\rm Hom}_{A}(N\otimes_{B}M,X)=0.

Then it follows that
X\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
if and only if
\mathrm{H}_{A}(X)\in\mathfrak{J}^{\mathcal{C}_{1}}_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}.

(4) Since
{}_{A}N
is finitely presented,
X^{+}\otimes_{A}N\cong\mbox{\rm Hom}_{A}(N,X)^{+}
by
[
6
, Theorem 3.2.11]
.
Then
(e_{X})_{+}:X^{+}\otimes_{A}N\rightarrow\mbox{\rm Hom}_{A}(N,X)^{+}
is an isomorphism,
where
e_{X}:N\otimes_{B}\mbox{\rm Hom}_{A}(N,X)\rightarrow X
is the evaluation map.
By
[
6
, Theorem 3.2.11]
again,
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{A}(N,X)^{+}\otimes_{A}M\cong X^{+}\otimes_{A}N\otimes_{B}M=0
since
N\otimes_{B}M=0
by hypothesis.
Then it is easy to see that
X^{+}\in\mathcal{C}_{2}
if and only if
\mathrm{H}_{A}(X)^{+}\in\mathfrak{B}_{{\mathcal{C}_{2}},{\mathcal{D}_{2}}}.

∎

]
renamed these modules Ding projective modules.
For a ring
R
, a left
R
-module
M
is called
Ding projective
if there is an exact sequence of projective left
R
-modules

\mathbf{P}=\cdots\rightarrow P_{1}\rightarrow P_{0}\rightarrow P^{0}\rightarrow P^{1}\rightarrow\cdots

with
M\cong\mathrm{Ker}(P_{0}\rightarrow P^{0})
such that
\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(\mathbf{P},F)
is exact for any flat left
R
-module
F
.
For more details on Ding projective modules, we refer to
[
4
,
11
,
21
,
25
,
16
]
.

Example 4.2
.

If
R
is a right coherent ring, then
(\mathrm{Flat}(R),\mathrm{FP}\text{-}\mathrm{Inj}(R^{op}))
is a complete duality pair by
[
2
, Theorem 1]
, where
\mathrm{Flat}(R)
denotes the class of flat left
R
-modules and

\mathrm{FP}\text{-}\mathrm{Inj}(R^{op})
denotes the class of
\mathrm{FP}
-injective right
R
-modules. Note that Gorenstein
(\mathrm{Flat}(R),\mathrm{FP}\text{-}\mathrm{Inj}(R^{op}))
-projective modules is exactly the Ding projective modules.

The following is the main result of this section which contains Theorem
1.2
in the introduction.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
N_{B}
and
M_{A}
be finitely generated projective.

(1)

Assume that
N\otimes_{B}D\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
for any
D\in\mathcal{D}_{1}.

If
X
is a Gorenstein
(\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2})
-projective left
A
-module, then
\mathrm{T}_{A}(X)
is a Gorenstein
(\mathfrak{B}^{\mathcal{C}_{1}}_{\mathcal{D}_{1}},\mathfrak{J}_{{\mathcal{C}_{2}},{\mathcal{D}_{2}}})
-projective left
\Delta
-module.

(2)

Assume that
{}_{A}N
and
{}_{B}M
are projective modules,
M\otimes_{A}C\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathrm{id}_{B}\mbox{\rm Hom}_{A}(N,C)<\infty
for any
C\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
.
If
(X,Y,f,g)
is a Gorenstein
(\mathfrak{B}^{\mathcal{C}_{1}}_{\mathcal{D}_{1}},\mathfrak{J}_{{\mathcal{C}_{2}},{\mathcal{D}_{2}}})
-projective left
\Delta
-module, then
X
is a Gorenstein
(\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2})
-projective left
A
-module.

(3)

Assume that
M\otimes_{A}C\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
for any
C\in\mathcal{C}_{1}.

If
Y
is a Gorenstein
(\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2})
-projective left
B
-module, then
\mathrm{T}_{B}(Y)
is a Gorenstein
(\mathfrak{B}^{\mathcal{C}_{1}}_{\mathcal{D}_{1}},\mathfrak{J}_{{\mathcal{C}_{2}},{\mathcal{D}_{2}}})
-projective left
\Delta
-module.

(4)

Assume that
{}_{A}N
and
{}_{B}M
are projective modules,
N\otimes_{B}D\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathrm{id}_{A}\mbox{\rm Hom}_{B}(M,D)<\infty
for any
D\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
.
If
(X,Y,f,g)
is a Gorenstein
(\mathfrak{B}^{\mathcal{C}_{1}}_{\mathcal{D}_{1}},\mathfrak{J}_{{\mathcal{C}_{2}},{\mathcal{D}_{2}}})
-projective left
\Delta
-module, then
Y
is a Gorenstein
(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2})
-projective left
B
-module.

Proof.

We only need prove (1) and (2), and the proofs of (3) and (4) are similar.

(1). Let
{}_{A}X
be a Gorenstein
(\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2})
-projective module.
Then there exists an exact sequence of projective left
A
-modules

\mathbb{P}:\cdots\rightarrow P^{-1}\xrightarrow{d^{-1}}P^{0}\xrightarrow{d^{0}}P^{1}\rightarrow\cdots

such that
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(\mathbb{P},C)
is exact for any
C\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
and that
X\cong\ker d^{0}.

Since
M_{A}
is projective by hypothesis,
we have the exact sequence of projective
\Delta
-modules

\mathrm{T}_{A}(\mathbb{P}):\cdots\rightarrow\mathrm{T}_{A}(P^{-1})\xrightarrow{\mathrm{T}_{A}(d^{-1})}\mathrm{T}_{A}(P^{0})\xrightarrow{\mathrm{T}_{A}(d^{0})}\mathrm{T}_{A}(P^{1})\rightarrow\cdots

such that
\mathrm{T}_{A}(X)\cong\ker(\mathrm{T}_{A}(d^{0}))
by Lemma
2.2
.
For any
(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime},f^{\prime},g^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{B}^{\mathcal{C}_{1}}_{\mathcal{D}_{1}},

we have exact sequences

0\rightarrow N\otimes_{B}Y^{\prime}\xrightarrow{g^{\prime}}X^{\prime}\rightarrow X^{\prime}/\mbox{\rm im}(g^{\prime})\rightarrow 0

of
A
-modules with
X^{\prime}/\mbox{\rm im}(g^{\prime})\in\mathcal{C}_{1}

and

0\rightarrow M\otimes_{A}X^{\prime}\xrightarrow{f^{\prime}}Y^{\prime}\rightarrow Y^{\prime}/\mbox{\rm im}(f^{\prime})\rightarrow 0

of
B
-modules with
Y^{\prime}/\mbox{\rm im}(f^{\prime})\in\mathcal{D}_{1}.

Note that
N\otimes_{B}M=0
.
It follows that
N\otimes_{B}Y^{\prime}\cong N\otimes_{B}Y^{\prime}/\mbox{\rm im}(f).

So
N\otimes_{B}Y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
by hypothesis.
Since
\mathbb{P}
is an exact complex of projective
A
-modules,
we get an exact sequence of complexes

0\rightarrow\mbox{\rm Hom}_{A}(\mathbb{P},N\otimes_{B}Y^{\prime})\rightarrow\mbox{\rm Hom}_{A}(\mathbb{P},X^{\prime})\rightarrow\mbox{\rm Hom}_{A}(\mathbb{P},X^{\prime}/\mbox{\rm im}(g^{\prime}))\rightarrow 0.

Since
X
is a Gorenstein
(\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2})
-projective module, it follows that
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{A}(\mathbb{P},N\otimes_{B}Y^{\prime})
and
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{A}(\mathbb{P},X^{\prime}/\mbox{\rm im}(g^{\prime}))

are exact.
Hence
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{A}(\mathbb{P},X^{\prime})
is exact.
Note that

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm Hom}_{\Delta}(T_{A}(\mathbb{P}),(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime},f^{\prime},g^{\prime}))\cong\mbox{\rm Hom}_{A}(\mathbb{P},X^{\prime})

by Lemma
2.1
.
So
\mathrm{T}_{A}(X)
is a Gorenstein
(\mathfrak{B}^{\mathcal{C}_{1}}_{\mathcal{D}_{1}},\mathfrak{J}^{\mathcal{C}_{2}}_{\mathcal{D}_{2}})
-projective left
\Delta
-module, as desired.

(2). Let
(X,Y,f,g)
be a Gorenstein
(\mathfrak{B}^{\mathcal{C}_{1}}_{\mathcal{D}_{1}},\mathfrak{J}_{{\mathcal{C}_{2}},{\mathcal{D}_{2}}})
-projective left
\Delta
-module.
Then we have an exact sequence of projective left
\Delta
-modules

\mathbb{T}:\cdots\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{T}_{A}(P^{-1})\oplus\mathrm{T}_{B}(Q^{-1})\xrightarrow{d^{-1}}\mathrm{T}_{A}(P^{0})\oplus\mathrm{T}_{B}(Q^{0})\xrightarrow{d^{0}}\mathrm{T}_{A}(P^{1})\oplus\mathrm{T}_{B}(Q^{1})\rightarrow\cdots

such that
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T},(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime},f^{\prime},g^{\prime}))
is exact for any
(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime},f^{\prime},g^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{B}^{\mathcal{C}_{1}}_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}
and
\mbox{\rm ker}d^{0}\cong(X,Y,f,g),

where each
P^{i}
is a projective left
A
-module and each
Q^{i}
is a projective left
B
-module.
Then we have the following exact sequence

\mathrm{U}_{A}(\mathbb{T}):\cdots\rightarrow P^{-1}\oplus(N\otimes_{B}Q^{-1})\xrightarrow{\mathrm{U}_{A}(d^{-1})}P^{0}\oplus(N\otimes_{B}Q^{0})\xrightarrow{\mathrm{U}_{A}(d^{0})}P^{1}\oplus(N\otimes_{B}Q^{1})\rightarrow\cdots

such that
\mbox{\rm ker}(\mathrm{U}_{A}(d^{0}))\cong X.

Since
{}_{A}N
is projective by hypothesis, each
N\otimes_{B}Q^{i}
is a projective left
A
-module.
Thus each
P^{i}\oplus(N\otimes_{B}Q^{i})
is a projective left
A
-module.
For any
C\in\mathcal{C}_{1},

consider the following exact sequence of left
\Delta
-modules

0\rightarrow(0,M\otimes_{A}C,0,0)\rightarrow(C,M\otimes_{A}C,1,0)\rightarrow(C,0,0,0)\rightarrow 0.

Since
\mathbb{T}
is an exact complex of projective left
\Delta
-modules,
we get an exact sequence of complexes

\textstyle{0\rightarrow\mbox{\rm Hom}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T},(0,M\otimes_{A}C,0,0))\rightarrow\mbox{\rm Hom}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T},(C,M\otimes_{A}C,1,0))\rightarrow\mbox{\rm Hom}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T},(C,0,0,0))\rightarrow 0.}

Note that
M\otimes_{A}C\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
N\otimes_{B}M=0
by hypothesis, it follows that

(0,M\otimes_{A}C,0,0)
and
(C,M\otimes_{A}C,1,0)
are in
\mathfrak{B}^{\mathcal{C}_{1}}_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}.

Thus
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T},(0,M\otimes_{A}C,0,0))
and
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T},(C,M\otimes_{A}C,1,0))
are exact.
So
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T},(C,0,0,0))
is exact.
Since
\mathrm{id}_{B}\mbox{\rm Hom}_{A}(N,C)<\infty
by hypothesis,
there is an exact sequence of left
B
-modules

0\rightarrow\mbox{\rm Hom}_{A}(N,C)\rightarrow J^{0}\rightarrow J^{1}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow J^{n-1}\rightarrow J^{n}\rightarrow 0

for some nonnegative integer
n
with each
J^{i}
injective.
Then we have an exact sequence of left
\Delta
-modules

0\rightarrow\mathrm{H}_{B}(\mbox{\rm Hom}_{A}(N,C))\rightarrow\mathrm{H}_{B}(J^{0})\rightarrow\mathrm{H}_{B}(J^{1})\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow\mathrm{H}_{B}(J^{n-1})\rightarrow\mathrm{H}_{B}(J^{n})\rightarrow 0

as
{}_{B}M
is projective.
Note from the adjunction isomorphism that
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{B}(M,\mbox{\rm Hom}_{A}(N,C))\cong\mbox{\rm Hom}_{A}(N\otimes_{B}M,C)
. Then from
N\otimes_{B}M=0
we have
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{B}(M,\mbox{\rm Hom}_{A}(N,C))=0.

Thus
\mathrm{H}_{B}(\mbox{\rm Hom}_{A}(N,C))=(0,\mbox{\rm Hom}_{A}(N,C),0,0).

Note from Lemma
2.2
(2) that each
\mathrm{H}_{B}(J^{i})
is an injective left
\Delta
-module.
This implies that
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T},\mathrm{H}_{B}(J^{i}))
is exact for
0\leq i\leq n
.
It follows that
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T},(0,\mbox{\rm Hom}_{A}(N,C),0,0))
is exact by induction.
One sees that the following sequence of left
\Delta
-modules

0\rightarrow(C,0,0,0)\rightarrow(C,\mbox{\rm Hom}_{A}(N,C),0,e_{C})\rightarrow(0,\mbox{\rm Hom}_{A}(N,C),0,0)\rightarrow 0

is exact in
\Delta\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}.

Thus we have the following exact sequence of complexes

0\longrightarrow\mbox{\rm Hom}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T},(C,0,0,0))\longrightarrow\mbox{\rm Hom}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T},(C,\mbox{\rm Hom}_{A}(N,C),0,e_{C}))\longrightarrow\\
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T},(0,\mbox{\rm Hom}_{A}(N,C),0,0))\longrightarrow 0

as
\mathbb{T}
is an exact complex of projective left
\Delta
-modules.
Note that
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T},(C,0,0,0))
and
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T},(0,\mbox{\rm Hom}_{A}(N,C),0,0))
are exact in
\Delta\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}
.
So
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T},(C,\mbox{\rm Hom}_{A}(N,C),0,e_{C}))
is exact in
\Delta\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}.

By Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm Hom}_{A}(\mathrm{U}_{A}(\mathbb{T}),C)

\displaystyle\cong\mbox{\rm Hom}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T},\mathrm{H}_{A}(C))=\mbox{\rm Hom}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{T},(C,\mbox{\rm Hom}_{A}(N,C),0,e_{C})).

Thus
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{A}(\mathrm{U}_{A}(\mathbb{T}),C)
is exact in
\Delta\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}.

So
{}_{A}X
is a Gorenstein
(\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2})
-projective left
A
-module. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

A left
\Delta
-module
(X,Y,f,g)
is flat if and only if
f
and
g
are monomorphisms,
X/\mbox{\rm im}(g)
and
Y/\mbox{\rm im}(f)
are flat.

Proof.

Let
(X,Y,f,g)
be a left
\Delta
-module.
Then we have the following exact sequences

M\otimes_{A}X\xrightarrow{f}Y\rightarrow Y/\mbox{\rm im}(f)\rightarrow 0

of
B
-modules and

N\otimes_{B}Y\xrightarrow{g}X\rightarrow X/\mbox{\rm im}(g)\rightarrow 0

of
A
-modules.
Since
M\otimes_{A}N=0=N\otimes_{B}M
, we have
N\otimes_{B}Y\cong N\otimes_{B}Y/\mbox{\rm im}(f)
and

M\otimes_{B}X\cong M\otimes_{B}X/\mbox{\rm im}(g).

Assume that
(X,Y,f,g)
is a flat left
\Delta
-module, it follows from
[
17
, Theorem 3.6.5]
that
X/\mbox{\rm im}(g)
and
Y/\mbox{\rm im}(f)
are flat modules,
M\otimes_{A}X/\mbox{\rm im}(g)\cong\mbox{\rm im}(f)
and
N\otimes_{B}Y/\mbox{\rm im}(f)\cong\mbox{\rm im}(g).

Thus
M\otimes_{B}X\cong\mbox{\rm im}(f)
and
N\otimes_{B}Y\cong\mbox{\rm im}(g).

So
f
and
g
are monomorphism.

Conversely, if
f
and
g
are monomorphisms,
X/\mbox{\rm im}(g)
and
Y/\mbox{\rm im}(f)
are flat modules, then
M\otimes_{B}X\cong\mbox{\rm im}(f)
and
N\otimes_{B}Y\cong\mbox{\rm im}(g).

So
N\otimes_{B}Y/\mbox{\rm im}(f)\cong\mbox{\rm im}(g)
and
M\otimes_{B}X/\mbox{\rm im}(g)\cong\mbox{\rm im}(f).

Thus
(X,Y,f,g)
is a flat left
\Delta
-module by
[
17
, Theorem 3.6.5]
.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{A})
and
(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
be complete duality pairs over a ring
R
.
Then
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Let
X\in\mathcal{A}
.
Then
X^{+}\in\mathcal{L}
. If
X
is not in
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
, then
X^{+}
is not in
\mathcal{L}
as
(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
is a complete duality pairs, which is a contradiction. So
X\in\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
, that is,
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
.
On the other hand, we can show that
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
.
Hence
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
.
∎

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
\Delta
be a right coherent ring with
N_{B}
and
M_{A}
finitely generated projective.
Then a right
\Delta
-module
(X,Y,f,g)
is an
FP
-injective right
\Delta
-module if and only if
\tilde{g}:Y\rightarrow\mbox{\rm Hom}_{A}(M,X)
is a
B
-epimorphism,
\tilde{f}:X\rightarrow\mbox{\rm Hom}_{B}(N,Y)
is an
A
-epimorphism,
\ker\tilde{f}
is an
FP
-injective right
B
-module and
\ker\tilde{g}
is an
FP
-injective right
A
-module.

Proof.

Since
\Delta
is a right coherent ring, it follows from
[
29
, Theorem 5.4]
that
A
and
B
are right coherent rings.
Thus this assertion follows from Corollary
3.8
, Example
4.2
,
Lemmas
4.5
and
4.6
.
∎

Almost Gibbsian Measures on a Cayley Tree
Matteo D’Achille, Arnaud Le Ny

Abstract




Abstract:

We consider the ferromagnetic n.n. Ising model on Cayley trees submitted to a modified majority rule transformation with overlapping cells already known to lead to non-Gibbsian measures. We describe the renormalized measures within the generalized Gibbs framework and prove that they are almost Gibbs at any temperature.

AMS 2000 subject classification
: Primary- 60K35; secondary- 82B20.

Keywords and phrases
: Renormalized Gibbs measures, dependent percolation, Cayley trees.

Introduction

In mathematical statistical mechanics,
Gibbs measures
have been rigorously designed to represent equilibrium states and to model phase transitions, following the pioneering works of Dobrushin, Lanford and Ruelle who described them as an extension of Markov chains, both dynamically and spatially, in terms of the specification of their conditional probabilities w.r.t. the outside of finite sets
[
9
,
34
]
. This
DLR approach
has been fully put on rigorous grounds in the eighties by Georgii
[
21
]
. In the mean time, some pathologies of the formal definition of Gibbs measures arose within numerical studies of critical phenomena
[
22
,
30
]
, identified afterwards to be a manifestation of possible non-Gibbsianness of the renormalized measures
[
11
]
. This non-Gibbsianness has been since then mainly coined by the exhibition of bad configurations, which are points of essential discontinuity of the renormalized measures. This led to a Dobrushin program of restoration of Gibbsianness, launched by Dobrushin himself in a talk in Renkum in 1995
[
10
]
. Within this program, two main restoration notions have been proposed,
weak Gibbsianness
and
almost Gibbsianness
, the latter being stronger than the former
1

1

See
[

]
or
[
32
]
, where one can learn that the weakly Gibbsian representation is indeed too weak due to a possible failure of the variational principle in a weak but non almost Gibbsian example.
. On
d
-dimensional integer lattices
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, most initial efforts of restoration have been adressed to the decimation transformation, leading to an almost Gibbsian description at any temperature in a series of papers
[
11
,
15
,
36
]
, while positive results concerning preservation of Gibbsianness near the critical point and in uniqueness regions have been proposed in
[
2
,

,

,
41
]
.

As it is usually difficult to evaluate the measure of the set of discontinuity points (so-called
bad configurations
), the approach leading to almost Gibbsianness on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
heavily relies on a specification-dependent variational principle (
zero relative entropy
characterization) which is known to fail on trees
[

]
. Due to this difficulty, while considering trees as lattices, most efforts have been addressed to the detection of non-Gibbsian measures, by renormalization
[

]
or

, see
e.g.

[

), with the notable exception of the failure of almost Gibbsianness for the random-cluster measures proved in

2.1
Gibbs measures

In this paper we consider Ising spins with single-spin state-space

the set of all the finite subsets of

and for

, we consider the finite-volume configuration space

, equipped with the product measurable structure
(\mathcal{F}_{V},\rho_{V})
, and denote

the canonical projection of
\omega\in\Omega
on
\Omega_{V}
. Similarly, for any

such that

the element of

which agrees with

in
{V}
and with

in
{V}^{\prime}

,

denotes the cardinality of

In this work, the lattices we consider are Cayley trees

Ising Potential:
we consider nearest-neighbor (

\forall\omega\in\Omega,\Psi_{A}(\omega)=\begin{cases}-J(i,j)\;\omega_{i}\omega_{j}&{\rm if}\;A=\{i,j\}\\

(1)

Here,

Definition 2.1
.

[UAC potential]
A potential

{\mathcal{H}^{\Phi}_{V}(\sigma\mid\omega)}\coloneqq\sum_{A\in\mathcal{S},A\cap{V}\neq\emptyset}\Phi_{A}(\sigma_{V}\omega_{{V}^{c}}).

In the particular case of free boundary conditions at finite-volume

Associated to (

(3)

is the

V
.

Definition 2.2
.

[Gibbs specifications]

For an UAC potential

In the case of free boundary conditions, we write uniformly in

More generally, a specification

This

Definition 2.3
.

[Gibbs Measures]

A Gibbs measure
\mu
is a probability measure on
\Omega

5

2.4
Almost Gibbsian and weakly Gibbsian measures

For a given specification
\gamma
, we denote by
\Omega_{\gamma}
its set of points of essential continuity (
good configurations
). Of course, for a Gibbs specification,
\Omega_{\gamma}=\Omega
.

Definition 2.6
.

[Almost Gibbsian measure]
A probability measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(\Omega)
is
almost Gibbsian
if there exists a specification
\gamma
such that

\mu\in\mathcal{G}({{\gamma}})\quad{\rm and}\quad\mu(\Omega_{\gamma})=1.

This is equivalent to the statement that there exist regular versions of finite-volume conditional probabilities of
\mu
which are continuous as functions of the boundary conditions, possibly except on a zero-measure set.

Definition 2.7
.

[Weakly Gibbsian measure]
A probability measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(\Omega)
is
weakly Gibbsian
if
\mu\in\mathcal{G}({\gamma^{\beta\Phi}})
with a potential
\Phi
converging on a full set (
\mu(\Omega_{\Phi})=1
),
i.e.
s.t.

\forall V\in\mathcal{S},\;\sum_{A\cap{V}\neq\emptyset}\Phi_{A}(\omega)<\infty,\;{\rm for}\;\mu-{\rm a.e.}(\omega).

Note that an almost-sure version of the Kozlov-Sullivan representation (see
[
40
]
) allows to reconstruct an almost surely convergent potential consistent with an almost Gibbsian measure, so that
almost Gibbs implies weakly Gibbs.

4
Main result: almost Gibbsianness at any temperature

The main contribution of this paper is the following

Theorem 4.1
.

The renormalized measures
\nu=T\mu

\mu

\mathcal{T}_{0}^{2}

Proof.

In the non-Gibbsian result of the previous section, the essential discontinuity of the bad null configuration
\omega^{\prime 0}
defined above is due to the massive dissemination of information possible through a neutral (filled of zero) cell, for which no specific discrimination between plus or minus is made. In turns, it appears that the crucial point to get essential discontinuity

transmission of far away information is that the configuration possesses enough

We proceed as follows: first, we recall some necessary percolation notions (paths and percolations of zeros) and describe the essential continuity of the magnetization in absence of such a percolation (Lemma

C(\eta^{\prime})\coloneqq e^{n_{0}(\eta^{\prime})(\beta)}\mid x_{n_{0}(\eta^{\prime})}-y_{n_{0}(\eta^{\prime})}\mid

so that

C\coloneqq\sup_{\eta^{\prime}\neq{\eta^{\prime 1}_{\rm alt},\eta^{\prime 2}_{\rm alt}}}C(\eta^{\prime})<\infty

is well defined. This proves that (
13
) converges to zero as
R\to\infty
, proving also (
7
).

Proceeding similarly in the case
\eta^{\prime}_{0}=-
, and conditioning on the events
\{X_{R+2}=+\}
and
\{X_{R+2}=-\}
, we get continuity of the magnetization for all the configurations having only one path of zeros so that
n_{0}(\eta^{\prime})<\infty
,
i.e.
except the so-called exceptional
alternating configurations
described below. This proves Lemma 4.3.

Remark
(Peculiar alternating configurations) Assume again
\eta^{\prime}_{0}=+
and define
\eta^{\prime 1}_{\rm alt}
and
\eta^{\prime 2}_{\rm alt}
to be such that
N(\eta^{\prime})=1
and

\forall i=1,2,\;\forall R\in\mathbb{N},\;\forall n=0\dots R-1,\quad h^{i}_{R}=(-1)^{i},\;h^{i}_{n}=-h^{i}_{n+1}

(14)

where
h^{1}
(resp.
h^{2}
) is the environment under
\eta^{\prime 1}_{\rm alt}
(resp.

). For such configurations, elementary analysis of the products of matrices considered in this section show that the limits considered do not exist, because sequences alternate between two accumulation points. Nevertheless, we prove in Lemma

that such configurations form a

∎

4.3
Sufficient condition for essential continuity

Recall that, for a configuration
\eta^{\prime}
,
N_{R}(\eta^{\prime})
denotes the number of paths of zeros up to level
R
in
\eta^{\prime}
and
N=N(\eta^{\prime})=\lim_{R\to\infty}N_{R}(\eta^{\prime})
exists by monotonicity. Use Lemma
4.3
to show

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\eta^{\prime}\in\Omega^{\prime}
with
N(\eta^{\prime})
arbitrary, different from the null configuration or the alternate configurations defined above in (
14
). Then

\forall R\geq 0,\sup_{\omega^{\prime}_{1},\omega^{\prime}_{2}\in\mathcal{N}^{R}(\eta^{\prime})}\mid\langle\sigma^{\prime}_{r}\rangle^{\omega^{\prime}_{1},R}-\langle\sigma^{\prime}_{r}\rangle^{\omega^{\prime}_{2},R}\mid\leq C\cdot N_{R}(\eta^{\prime})\cdot(e^{-\beta})^{R}.

(15)

Proof.

The proof consists in associating the recursive tree structure and the Markov property of the measure
\mu
to the estimate (
7
) of Lemma
4.3
for each path of zeros of length
R
.

First of all, let
\Pi(\eta^{\prime})=\left(\pi_{k}\right)_{k=1}^{N_{R}(\eta^{\prime})}
be the set of path of zeros in
\eta^{\prime}
according to some (inessential) ordering, and let
\overline{\eta}^{\prime k}
be the configuration coinciding with
\eta^{\prime}
everywhere, except at the
N_{R}(\eta^{\prime})-1
path of zeros different from the
k
-th one, that is,
\overline{\eta}^{\prime k}=\eta^{\prime}_{\pi_{k}\cup\Pi^{c}}+_{\pi_{k}^{c}\cup{\Pi}}
. Let
n_{k}(\eta^{\prime})
be the smallest integer such that no entry of the matrix
P_{n}
along
\pi_{k}
is zero; that is, accordance with our notations in Lemma
4.3
,
n_{k}(\eta^{\prime})=n_{0}(\overline{\eta}^{\prime k})
.

If
N_{R}(\eta^{\prime})=1
the claim just coincides with Lemma
4.3
. Let us now prove it for
N_{R}(\eta^{\prime})=2
. First, observe that, for any
\omega_{1}^{\prime},\omega_{2}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}^{R}(\eta^{\prime})
, by Markov property and splitting on the tree,

\forall R>0,\left|\langle\sigma^{\prime}_{r}\rangle^{\omega^{\prime}_{1},R}-\langle\sigma^{\prime}_{r}\rangle^{\omega^{\prime}_{2},R}\right|\leq p(\beta)\left|\langle\sigma^{\prime}_{r1}\rangle^{\omega^{\prime}_{1},R}\langle\sigma^{\prime}_{r0}\rangle^{\omega^{\prime}_{1},R}-\langle\sigma^{\prime}_{r1}\rangle^{\omega^{\prime}_{2},R}\langle\sigma^{\prime}_{r0}\rangle^{\omega^{\prime}_{2},R}\right|

where
p(\beta)\coloneqq\nu[\eta^{\prime}_{0}=0]=\frac{2+e^{-2\beta}}{\left(e^{-\beta}+e^{\beta}\right)^{2}}
(see Section 4.4).

Second, remark that any four numbers
x,y,z,w\in[0,1]
we have

\begin{split}|xy-zw|&=|xy-yz+yz-zw|=|y(x-z)+z(y-w)|\underset{\rm tr.ineq.}{\leq}y|x-z|+z|y-w|\\
&\underset{y,z\in[0,1]}{\leq}|x-z|+|y-w|\;.\end{split}

(16)

Putting

\begin{cases}x&=\langle\sigma^{\prime}_{r0}\rangle^{\omega^{\prime}_{1},R}\\
y&=\langle\sigma^{\prime}_{r1}\rangle^{\omega^{\prime}_{1},R}\\
z&=\langle\sigma^{\prime}_{r0}\rangle^{\omega^{\prime}_{2},R}\\
w&=\langle\sigma^{\prime}_{r1}\rangle^{\omega^{\prime}_{2},R}\\
\end{cases}

gives

\left|\langle\sigma^{\prime}_{r1}\rangle^{\omega^{\prime}_{1},R}\langle\sigma^{\prime}_{r0}\rangle^{\omega^{\prime}_{1},R}-\langle\sigma^{\prime}_{r1}\rangle^{\omega^{\prime}_{2},R}\langle\sigma^{\prime}_{r0}\rangle^{\omega^{\prime}_{2},R}\right|\leq\left|\langle\sigma^{\prime}_{r0}\rangle^{\omega^{\prime}_{1},R}-\langle\sigma^{\prime}_{r0}\rangle^{\omega^{\prime}_{2},R}\right|+\left|\langle\sigma^{\prime}_{r1}\rangle^{\omega^{\prime}_{1},R}-\langle\sigma^{\prime}_{r1}\rangle^{\omega^{\prime}_{2},R}\right|.

(17)

Now, apply Lemma
4.3
to each of the quantities of the rhs, we get

\forall R\geq 0,\sup_{\omega^{\prime}_{1},\omega^{\prime}_{2}}\mid\langle\sigma^{\prime}_{r}\rangle^{\omega^{\prime}_{1},R}-\langle\sigma^{\prime}_{r}\rangle^{\omega^{\prime}_{2},R}\mid\leq 2\,C\,(e^{-\beta})^{R},

which is the claim for
N_{R}(\eta^{\prime})=2
.
The result for general (finite)
N_{R}(\eta^{\prime})
is obtained analogously by iteration.
∎

4.4
Percolation model and almost Gibbsianness

In the previous sections we have seen that the number of infinite paths of zeros is a good discriminant for detecting failures of quasilocality. Let us consider now the marginal probability of being
0
for the image configuration
\eta^{\prime}
. Due to the overlap among neighboring cells involved in the majority rule transformation
T
, clearly the random variables
(\eta^{\prime}_{j})_{j\in\mathcal{T}_{0}^{2}}
are not independent. Nevertheless, we can obtain a few informations about
\eta^{\prime}_{j}
’s due to the Markovianness
10
10

10

Strictly speaking, the standard (one-sided) Markov chain property is valid only for the Gibbs measures which are indeed Markov chains, see
[
21
,
26
,
48
,
51
]
. Nevertheless, this is true for any extremal Gibbs measures, and once our result is proven for any extremal
\mu
, it is straightforward to extend to all Gibbs measures.
of the original measure. For a site
j\in\mathcal{T}_{0}^{2}
, denote
j1
and
j0
its descendants using the binary representation introduced in Section 3. When the conditioning is possible, for
\eta^{\prime}\in\Omega^{\prime}
, from Bayes’s formula we get

\begin{split}\nu\big[\eta^{\prime}_{j1}=0\big]=&\nu\big[\eta^{\prime}_{j1}=0\mid\eta^{\prime}_{j}=+\big]\nu\big[\eta^{\prime}_{j}=+\big]+\nu\big[\eta^{\prime}_{j1}=0\mid\eta^{\prime}_{j}=-\big]\nu\big[\eta^{\prime}_{j}=-\big]+\\
+&\nu\big[\eta^{\prime}_{j1}=0\mid\eta^{\prime}_{j}=0\big]\nu\big[\eta^{\prime}_{j}=0\big]\end{split}

(18)

(if a conditioning is not possible, an analogous formula holds without their contributions). Let us now compute separately the three different conditional probabilities appearing in (
18
). In order to do so, let us introduce the shortcut

\mu_{\pm\pm\mid\pm}\coloneqq\mu\big[\sigma_{j0}=\pm,\sigma_{j1}=\pm\mid\sigma_{j}=\pm\big].

First, for the event
\{\eta^{\prime}_{j}=+\}
,

\begin{split}\nu\big[\eta^{\prime}_{j1}=0\mid\eta^{\prime}_{j}=+\big]=\;&\;(\mu_{+-\mid+}+\mu_{-+\mid+})+\mu_{--\mid+}=\frac{2}{\left(e^{-\beta}+e^{\beta}\right)^{2}}+\frac{e^{-2\beta}}{\left(e^{-\beta}+e^{\beta}\right)^{2}}\\
=\;&\frac{2+e^{-2\beta}}{\left(e^{-\beta}+e^{\beta}\right)^{2}}.\end{split}

(19)

Analogously for the event
\{\eta^{\prime}_{j}=-\}
we get

\begin{split}\nu\big[\eta^{\prime}_{j1}=0\mid\eta^{\prime}_{j}=-\big]=\;&2\mu_{+-\mid-}+\mu_{++\mid-}=\;\frac{2+e^{-2\beta}}{\left(e^{-\beta}+e^{\beta}\right)^{2}}.\\
\end{split}

(20)

Lastly, the contribution due to conditioning on
\{\eta^{\prime}_{j}=0\}
becomes

\displaystyle\nu\big[\eta^{\prime}_{j1}=0\mid\eta^{\prime}_{j}=0\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2+e^{-2\beta}}{\left(e^{-\beta}+e^{\beta}\right)^{2}}\left(\mu[\sigma_{j}=+]+\mu[\sigma_{j}=-]\right)=\frac{2+e^{-2\beta}}{\left(e^{-\beta}+e^{\beta}\right)^{2}},

where the factors
2
come from
+/-

\nu[\eta^{\prime}_{j}=0]\coloneqq p(\beta)=\frac{2+e^{-2\beta}}{\left(e^{-\beta}+e^{\beta}\right)^{2}}\quad\forall j\in\mathcal{T}_{0}^{2}.

Therefore, the problem becomes one of bond percolation on a Cayley tree
with a
\beta
-dependent probability of open bond
(see
e.g.

[
23
]
, Chapter 10). In the case of our binary tree
\mathcal{T}^{2}_{0}
,
N(\eta^{\prime})
being the number of infinite clusters of zeros in the configuration
\eta^{\prime}
, we thus have

\nu[N(\eta^{\prime})>0]=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lllll}\;0&\textrm{iff}&p(\beta)\leq p_{\rm c}=\frac{1}{2}\\
\\
\;>0&\textrm{iff}&p(\beta)>p_{\rm c}=\frac{1}{2}.\\
\end{array}\right.

In particular, we get almost Gibbsianness at high temperatures
\beta\leq\beta_{1}
with
\beta_{1}^{2}=\ln{\left(1+\sqrt{2}\right)}
.

Let us now study almost Gibbsianness in the low temperature regime
\beta>\beta_{1}
. By means of the sufficient condition of Lemma
4.4
, our aim is first to investigate the
\nu
-measure of the set
\Omega_{g}
of configurations for which
N_{R}(\eta^{\prime})
(the number of paths of zeros between the root and generation
R
in configuration
\eta^{\prime}
) grows slower than
e^{\beta R}
:

\Omega_{g}\coloneqq\left\{\eta^{\prime}\in\Omega^{\prime}\;:\;\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{N_{R}}{e^{\beta R}}=0\right\}.

(21)

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\mu
be any Gibbs measure for the Ising model on
\mathcal{T}_{0}^{2}
and
\nu=T\mu
. Then

\nu(\Omega_{g})=1.

Proof.

To proceed, we prove
\lim_{R\to\infty}\nu\big[\{N_{R}(\eta^{\prime})>e^{\beta R}\}\big]]=0
so that typically
\lim_{R}\frac{N_{R}}{e^{\beta R}}=0
.

To this aim, let us consider the sequence
(N_{R})_{R}
of random variables on
(\Omega^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
. As
R
grows, there is a probability
p(\beta)^{2}
of increasing
N_{R}
by
1
(
i.e.
opening two new bonds), a probability
(1-p(\beta))^{2}
of closing two bonds, etc. Therefore, for
\nu
-a.e.
\eta^{\prime}
,

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{\nu}\left[N_{R}\mid\mathcal{F}_{V_{R-1}}\right](\eta^{\prime})=\;&p^{2}\left(N_{R-1}+1\right)+2p(1-p)N_{R-1}+(1-p)^{2}\left(N_{R-1}-1\right)\\
=\;&N_{R-1}(\eta^{\prime})+2p-1.\end{split}

(22)

By induction we simply get
\mathbb{E}_{\nu}\left[N_{R}\right]=p+\left(R-1\right)\left(2p-1\right)

and thus

\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\nu}\left[N_{R}\right]}{e^{\beta R}}=0.

(23)

∎

To go beyond expectations, let us now bound the probability of a deviation larger than
e^{\beta R}
.
In the context of percolation on trees, at this point one usually exploits the special tree topology to obtain recursive relations. An example is the determination of the critical temperature of the ferromagnetic Ising model with constant interaction strength (see
[
39
]
, Theorem 2.1). Instead, here we obtain a (tight) bound on the probability of an exponential deviation following a slightly different route based on a combination of an exponential Chebyshev’s inequality and a uniform bound on the cumulant generating function of
N_{R}
:

Lemma 4.6

(Exponential deviation bound for
N_{R}
)
.

For any
\theta\geq 0

\nu[N_{R}(\eta^{\prime})>e^{\beta R}]\leq e^{\theta\left(R-e^{R\beta}\right)}

so that only sub-exponential deviations are allowed at any finite temperature
\frac{1}{\beta}
as
R\to\infty
.

Proof.

For
R>0
, exponential Chebyshev’s inequality implies,
\forall\theta\in\mathbb{R}
,

\nu[N_{R}(\eta^{\prime})>e^{\beta R}]\leq\mathbb{E}_{\nu}\left[e^{\theta N_{R}(\eta^{\prime})}\right]e^{-\theta e^{R\beta}}.

(24)

In analogy with (
22
) we just have

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{\nu}\left[e^{\theta N_{R}(\eta^{\prime})}\mid\mathcal{F}_{R-1}\right]&=p^{2}e^{\theta(N_{R-1}+1)}+2p(1-p)e^{\theta N_{R-1}}+(1-p)^{2}e^{\theta(N_{R-1}-1)}\\
&=\left(p^{2}e^{\theta}+2p(1-p)+(1-p)^{2}e^{-\theta}\right)e^{\theta N_{R-1}},\\
\end{split}

(25)

and hence by recurrence the full
moment generating function
(MGF) is given by

\mathbb{E}_{\nu}\left[e^{\theta N_{R}(\eta^{\prime})}\right]\coloneqq M_{R}(\theta,p)=\left(p^{2}e^{\theta}+2p(1-p)+(1-p)^{2}e^{-\theta}\right)^{R}.

A straightforward calculation shows that, for
\theta\geq 0
, the
Cumulant

K_{R}(\theta,p)\coloneqq\ln{M_{R}(\theta,p)}

satisfies, uniformly in
\theta
,

K_{R}(\theta,0)=-R\theta\leq K_{R}(\theta,p)\leq R\theta=K_{R}(\theta,1),

from which the assertion follows directly (recall that
p=p(\beta)\in[0,1]
).
∎

From Lemma
4.6
one gets that
\nu[N_{R}(\eta^{\prime})>e^{\beta R}]
converges to
0
more than exponentially in the depth
R
, namely as
\sim e^{-e^{R}}
, which together with (
23
) implies Lemma
4.5
by elementary probabilistic arguments. Indeed, for
l=e^{\beta}
and
\epsilon=\epsilon_{n}=\frac{1}{2^{n}}>0
, we have

\displaystyle\nu(\Omega_{g}^{c})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu\left[\cup_{n}\left\{\liminf_{R}\frac{N_{R}(\eta^{\prime})}{l^{R}}>\epsilon_{n}\right\}\right]\leq\liminf_{R}\sup_{\epsilon}\epsilon\,e^{-l^{R}}\underset{R\to\infty}{\to}0\;.

Now, we consider the integers
n_{k}
defined in Section 4.3 and introduce the sets

\Omega_{k}\coloneqq\left\{\eta^{\prime}:n_{k}(\eta^{\prime})<\infty\right\}.

Consider the subset of
\Omega_{g}

\Omega_{f}\coloneqq\left\{\,\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{N_{R}(\eta^{\prime})}{e^{\beta R}}=0\;{\rm and}\;n_{k}(\eta^{\prime})<\infty,k=1,\ldots,N_{R}(\eta^{\prime})\right\}=\Omega_{g}\cap\big(\cap_{k}\Omega_{k}\big).

We prove now that it is also
\nu

Lemma 4.7
.

\nu[\Omega_{f}]=1.

Proof.

Consider the event that, up to level
R-1
, the configuration
\eta^{\prime}
contains a path of zeros along sites
\pi
and alternating field

up to level
R-1

\pi

\mathcal{A}_{R-1}(\eta^{\prime})=\left\{h_{n}=(-1)^{n},\;\forall n=1,\ldots,R-1{\rm\;at\;neighboring\;sites\;of\;a\;path\;of\;zeros}\;\pi\right\}.

Then

\mathbb{P}\left[h_{R}\cdot h_{R-1}=-1|\mathcal{A}_{R-1}(\eta^{\prime})\right]_{\beta}\leq\mathbb{P}\left[h_{R}\cdot h_{R-1}=-1|\mathcal{A}_{R-1}(\eta^{\prime})\right]_{\beta=0}=\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^{2}<p_{c},

where we have used the infinite temperature limit gives an upper bound by attractivity, and
p_{c}=p_{c}(2)=\frac{1}{2}
is critical threshold for Bernoulli percolation on a Cayley tree order
2
.
Thus, if
h_{R-1}=+1
with
\eta^{\prime}_{\pi_{R}}=0
then there are
3

3

\Omega_{c}=\{\eta^{\prime}\in\Omega^{\prime}:\langle\sigma^{\prime}_{r}\rangle^{\cdot}\;{\rm is\;essentially\;continuous}\}.

.

\Omega_{f}\subset\Omega_{c}.

5
Possible extensions and research perspectives

It would be interesting to understand if our almost Gibbsianness result holds for Cayley trees of general order
k\geq 2
.
Concerning the crucial step in Theorem
4.6
, here we only observe that the MGF of the number of zeros reaching level
R
in primed configuration
\eta^{\prime}
,
N^{(k)}_{R}(\eta^{\prime})
, is

M^{(k)}_{R}(\theta,p^{(k)})=\left[e^{-\theta}\left(p^{(k)}\left(e^{\theta}-1\right)+1\right)^{k}\right]^{R},

where now
p^{(k)}\equiv p^{(k)}(\beta)
is the probability that a primed
0
percolates at inverse temperature
\beta
for the Cayley tree
\mathcal{T}^{k}
(
i.e.
generalizing (
4.4
)), and a
k

vs

\beta
tradeoff becomes possible. Recently, ferromagnetic Ising models on Cayley trees subjects to inhomogeneous external fields have attracted some interest that could be useful for our purposes (see
e.g.

[
3
]
for a recent work concerning spatially dependent external fields that are “small perturbations” of the critical external field value).

Moreover, the model considered in this paper can be naturally generalized
to non-rooted Cayley trees and to non-uniform
c_{j}
possibly different from two, or with other sizes of cells. The majority rule could also be generalized as a stochastic transformation. For example, let
\epsilon\in[0,1]
and
\xi
be a Bernoulli random
variable with parameter
\epsilon
and values
0
or
1
. Define the
deterministic map
t_{\epsilon}\colon\Omega\longrightarrow\Omega^{\prime};\omega\longmapsto\omega^{\prime}

where
\omega^{\prime}
is defined by

\omega^{\prime}_{j}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lllll}\;+1&\textrm{iff}&\frac{1}{c}\sum_{i\in C_{j}}\omega_{i}=+1\;\textrm{and}\;\xi=0\\
\;-1&\textrm{iff}&\frac{1}{c}\sum_{i\in C_{j}}\omega_{i}=-1\;\textrm{and}\;\xi=0\\
\;0&\textrm{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Its action is described by a probabilistic kernel
T_{\epsilon}
defined by:

\forall A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime},\forall\omega\in\Omega,\quad T_{\epsilon}(\omega,A^{\prime})=(1-\xi)\delta_{t_{\epsilon}(\omega)}(A^{\prime})+\xi\delta_{0}(A^{\prime}).

It could be interesting to study the difference between the deterministic transformation and the stochastic one, as this could play a role on the degree of non-Gibbsianness of the image measure, similar to the van den Berg example on the integers (see
[
38
]
).

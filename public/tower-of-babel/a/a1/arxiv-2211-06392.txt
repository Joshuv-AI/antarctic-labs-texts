Frankel Property, Maximum Principle at Infinity and Splitting Theorems for complete minimal hypersurfaces


Abstract
In this paper, we study complete minimal hypersurfaces in Riemannian n n -manifolds ℳ n \mathcal{M}^{n} for dimensions 4 ≤ n ≤ 7 4\leq n\leq 7 , and we obtain some results in the spirit of known work for n = 3 n=3 . Key contributions include extending the work of Anderson and Rodríguez to higher dimensions. Specifically, we show that in four dimensional manifolds with nonnegative sectional curvature and positive scalar curvature, two disjoint properly embedded minimal hypersurfaces bound a slab isometric to the product of one hypersurface with an interval. Our results are grounded in a maximum principle at infinity for two-sided, parabolic, properly embedded minimal hypersurfaces in complete Riemannian manifolds of bounded geometry, generalizing the work of Mazet in dimension three to higher dimensions. We also leverage the recent classification of complete two-sided stable minimal hypersurfaces by Chodosh, Li, and Stryker.

Introduction

In 1897, J. Hadamard
[
26
]
proved that two complete and embedded geodesics on a strictly convex
2-
sphere must intersect if one of them is compact. The closure of an embedded complete geodesic is a geodesic lamination. If a geodesic lamination of a strictly convex sphere were not one compact geodesic, then it would contain a stable geodesic. Using the formula for the second variation of arc length and a log-cut-off trick, one sees there is no such stable geodesic. Hence an embedded complete geodesic on such a
2-
sphere (e.g., an ellipsoid) is a compact (circle) geodesic. In the Appendix, we provide another proof of this using the Gauss-Bonnet Theorem. When the ambient dimension is three, we have:

Theorem
[
50
]
.

Let
\mathcal{M}^{3}
be a closed orientable manifold with positive Ricci curvature. If
\Sigma
is an injectively immersed complete orientable minimal surface of bounded curvature in
\mathcal{M}^{3}
, then
\Sigma
is compact.

The idea behind these theorems is that when the closure of a minimal hypersurface
\Sigma
is a minimal lamination
\mathcal{L}
, then
\Sigma
is properly embedded or there is a limit leaf
L\in\mathcal{L}
(
L
maybe
\Sigma
) that is stable. To exclude
L
, we might ask ourself three different questions:

•

When do two minimal hypersurfaces necessarily intersect?

•

When do two minimal hypersurfaces satisfy a Maximum Principle at Infinity: if
\Sigma_{1}\cap\Sigma_{2}=\emptyset
then the distance between
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
is bounded away from zero?

•

When does the ambient geometry determine the non-existence of stable two-sided minimal hypersurfaces?

T. Frankel
[
24
]
considered a complete
n-
manifold
\mathcal{M}^{n}
with positive Ricci curvature and two minimal hypersurfaces
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
in
\mathcal{M}^{n}
,
\Sigma_{1}
closed (compact with no boundary),
\Sigma_{2}
immersed and
\Sigma_{2}
proper (
\overline{\Sigma}_{2}=\Sigma_{2}
). He proved
\Sigma_{1}\cap\Sigma_{2}\neq\emptyset
. C. Croke and B. Kleiner
[
18
]
considered two closed orientable embedded minimal hypersurfaces in an orientable complete
\mathcal{M}^{n}
with non-negative Ricci curvature. They proved that if
\Sigma_{1}
does not intersect
\Sigma_{2}
then they are both totally geodesic and either (i) or (ii) holds:

(i)

\Sigma_{1}\cup\Sigma_{2}
bounds a domain in
\mathcal{M}
isometric to
\Sigma_{1}\times[0,{\bf d}]
, with the product metric,
{\bf d}:={\rm dist}_{\mathcal{M}}(\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2})
;

(ii)

\mathcal{M}^{n}
is a mapping torus; i.e.,
\mathcal{M}
is isometric to
\Sigma_{1}\times[0,{\bf d}]
, with the product metric, where
\Sigma_{1}\times\left\{0\right\}
is identified with
\Sigma_{1}\times\left\{{\bf d}\right\}
by an isometry.

A property that obliges minimal submanifolds to intersect is now known as a Frankel property. Thus the space of closed minimal hypersurfaces in
\mathcal{M}^{n}
with positive Ricci curvature satisfies the Frankel property and, nowadays, this is well understood if the ambient manifold has non-negative Ricci curvature and one of the minimal hypersurfaces is compact. When the minimal hypersurfaces are complete and non-compact we refer to
[
17
]
for a detailed exposition and references therein

Hoffman and Meeks
[
28
]
proved that a connected, proper, possibly branched, nonplanar minimal surface
\hbox{\bb R}^{3}
is not contained in a halfspace, their
Half-space Theorem
. They also proved that if
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
are two properly immersed minimal surfaces in the Euclidean three-space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that
\partial\Sigma_{1}=\partial\Sigma_{2}=\emptyset
, then they are parallel planes; their
Strong Half-space Theorem
. To prove, they construct an orientable complete least area surface
L
between
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
;
L
is stable hence a plane and they conclude with their Half-space Theorem. Also, Meeks-Rosenberg proved the following Maximum Principle at Infinity:

Theorem
[
44
]
.

Let
\Sigma_{1}
be a properly immersed minimal surface in
\hbox{\bb R}^{3}
. Then, if
\Sigma_{2}
is a properly immersed minimal surface in
\hbox{\bb R}^{3}\setminus\Sigma_{1}
, then
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
are parallel planes

Many people have proved such Maximum Principles (see
[
32
,
37
,
44
,
48
,
52
]
and references therein), and the most general in ambient dimension three for us was done by L. Mazet
[
37
]
; that we will explain in detail and generalize later for higher ambient dimension.

Understanding stable minimal hypersurfaces is foundational on the subject. A complete two-sided stable minimal surface in
\hbox{\bb R}^{3}
is a flat plane
[
7
,
23
,
47
]
. R. Schoen
[
54
]
used this to prove a two-sided stable complete minimal surface
\Sigma
in a complete
3-
manifold
\mathcal{M}^{3}
of bounded geometry has bounded curvature, i.e., the sectional curvatures are bounded in absolute value and the injectivity radius has a positive lower bound. That is, there exists a constant
\Lambda>0
, depending on
\mathcal{M}^{3}
, such that if
\Sigma
is as above, then

|A(x)|{\rm dist}_{\Sigma}(x,\partial\Sigma)\leq\Lambda,\text{ for all }x\in\Sigma,

here

is the norm of the second fundamental form of
\Sigma
.

Remark 1.1
.

Schoen’s curvature estimate depends also on an upper bound on the covariant derivate of the curvature tensor. H. Rosenberg, R. Souam and E. Toubiana
[
53

obtained such a curvature estimate assuming only that the
3-
manifold
\mathcal{M}^{3}
is of bounded geometry.

These results are, we believe, of the upmost importance for the study of complete minimal surfaces in
3-
manifolds. Recently, O. Chodosh and C. Li
[
13
]
proved that an orientable, complete, stable minimal hypersurface in
\hbox{\bb R}^{4}
is a flat (
\hbox{\bb R}^{3}
) hyperplane (see also
[
8
,
14
,
16
,
29
,
38
]
for alternative proofs and extensions).

Until recently, curvature estimates for stable minimal hypersurfaces in dimensions greater than three depend not only on the distance from the boundary but also on a volume growth hypothesis
[
55
,
57
]
. Thanks to recent results
[
15
,
16
,
38
]
, where they proved that two-sided stable minimal hypersurfaces in
\hbox{\bb R}^{n}
,
n=4,5,6
, are hyperplanes, and a blow-up argument, we now have curvature estimates that only depend on the intrinsic distance to the boundary for twos-sided stable minimal hypersurfaces when the ambient manifold has bounded geometry and dimension
n=4,5,6
. It is expected that two-sided stable minimal hypersurfaces in
\hbox{\bb R}^{7}
are hyperplanes, which would imply curvature estimates for stable hypersurfaces that only depend on the intrinsic distance to the boundary in ambient manifolds of bounded geometry.

M. Anderson and L. Rodríguez
[
4
]
proved that the existence of an area-minimizing surface in a complete three-manifold of non-negative Ricci curvature splits the manifold. The idea behind
[
4
]
is to construct a sequence of area-minimizing surfaces with two boundary components, one boundary on the original area-minimizing surface and the other boundary small and on a close equidistant surface, using the Douglas Criterion for the Plateau Problem. Then, taking limits when the boundary component on the original area-minimizing goes to infinity and the other component shrinks to a point on the equidistant, they are able to construct another area-minimizing smooth surface, passing through this point. Finally, they prove that this gives a local, and then global, foliation of the manifold. O. Chodosh, M. Eichmair and V. Moraru extended the above result under the weaker condition of non-negative scalar curvature of the ambient three-manifold (see
[
12
]
for a state of the art and references therein). In higher dimension, see also
[
45
]
when the area-minimizer is compact.

Structure of the paper

In Section

, we include the basic notation and definitions we will use along the paper. An important tool in our proofs is to prove that area-minimizing
{\rm mod}(2)
hypersurfaces have curvature estimates only depending on the ambient manifold and distance to the boundary when

. This is done in detail in Section
3
.

In Section
4

[
37
]
can be extended up to dimension seven with the same techniques, thanks to the Schoen-Simon-Yau’s curvature estimates
[
55
,
57
]
and the regularity theory for area-minimizers developed by the combined works of De Giorgi, Federer, Fleming, Hardt and Simon (cf.

, even in Euclidean space, as the example of the catenoid shows. In fact, there are no parabolic, complete, minimal hypersurfaces by the monotonicity formula in

,

. Also, inspired by the Tubular Neighborhood Theorem of Meeks-Rosenberg in Euclidean three-space
[

,

]
, we focus on the existence of an embedded

tube when the ambient manifold has non-negative Ricci curvature.

In Section
5
we establish the two main new contributions of this paper. We restrict to
4-
manifolds of non-negative sectional curvatures and scalar curvature bounded below by a positive constant. Under these conditions, complete stable two-sided minimal hypersurfaces are totally geodesic and parabolic (cf.
[
15
]
and
[
46

) which is a fundamental tool in the proof. The existence of a non-separating, properly embedded minimal hypersurface in such manifolds implies a local splitting.

Theorem
5.1

Let
(\mathcal{M}^{4},g)
be a complete orientable
4-
manifold of bounded geometry, nonnegative sectional curvature, and scalar curvature bounded below by a positive constant. If
\Sigma\subset\mathcal{M}
is a properly embedded orientable minimal hypersurface that is not separating, then
\Sigma
is embedded and
\mathcal{M}
is a mapping torus over
\Sigma
.

This enables us to prove a Frankel-type property in these manifolds:

Theorem
5.2

Let
(\mathcal{M}^{4},g)
be a complete orientable
4-
manifold of bounded geometry, nonnegative sectional curvature, and scalar curvature bounded below by a positive constant. Let
\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2}\subset\mathcal{M}
be two properly embedded orientable minimal hypersurfaces. Then either
\Sigma_{1}
intersects
\Sigma_{2}
, or
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
bound a region with a product metric.

.

The previous theorems can be extended to quasi-embedded minimal hypersurfaces, which will be defined later. In Section
5
, we provide more detailed information about the splitting in the previous results. However, for the sake of clarity in exposition, we have chosen to present a simpler version here.

In Section

we focus on area-minimizing hypersurfaces extending the Anderson-Rodríguez Splitting Theorem
[
4
]
up to dimension seven. We shall clarify that Anderson
[
3
]
obtained a result like our Theorem
6.1
under the stronger assumption that the volume growth of geodesic balls of
\mathcal{M}^{n}
is cubic.

1.2
Concluding remarks

An interesting subject for future research is the study of properly embedded minimal and constant mean curvature (CMC) hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
with the product metric, where
\mathbb{S}^{2}
is the standard 2-sphere of curvature one, and
\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
with the product metric, where
\mathbb{T}^{2}
is the standard flat 2-torus. There are many examples of minimal hypersurfaces in these spaces. We know a great deal about properly embedded minimal surfaces
\Sigma
in
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}

[
41
,
43
,
51
]
. If
\Sigma
has bounded curvature, then
\Sigma
has linear area growth. When
\Sigma
has finite topology,
\Sigma
has bounded curvature. Hence, when
\Sigma
has finite topology,
\Sigma\times\mathbb{R}
has quadratic area growth in
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
or
\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
, thus it is parabolic.

Let
l\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a line. Thus,
\Sigma=\mathbb{S}^{2}\times l
or
\Sigma=\mathbb{T}^{2}\times l
is a properly embedded parabolic minimal hypersurface to which the techniques of our theorems apply. For example, suppose
\Sigma
is a properly embedded minimal hypersurface of bounded curvature in
\mathcal{M}^{4}:=\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
. If
\Sigma
does not separate
\mathcal{M}
, then
\mathcal{M}
splits. This is proved in the spirit of our Theorem
5.1
. Cutting open
\mathcal{M}
along
\Sigma
results in a manifold with two boundary components, each a copy of
\Sigma
, which we call this new manifold
\mathcal{N}
with boundary
\partial\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{S}_{1}\cup\mathcal{S}_{2}
. In
\mathcal{N}
, we find a least-area minimal hypersurface
F
that is orientable and separates
\mathcal{N}
into two components, each containing a boundary component of
\mathcal{N}
. Then, the universal cover of
F
is a flat hyperplane by Chodosh-Li’s Theorem
[
13
]
, therefore
F
is flat as well. Proceeding as in the proof of Theorem
5.1
, we conclude that
\mathcal{N}
is isometric to
\Sigma\times[0,{\bf d}]
, where
{\bf d}:=\text{dist}_{\mathcal{N}}(\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{2})
. The Half-space theorem applies to
\Sigma
. This convinced us that the study of properly embedded minimal (and CMC) hypersurfaces in these 4-manifolds is an interesting subject.

We also hope that the proof of Hadamard’s Theorem given in the Appendix will generalize to dimension 3, to prove that an embedded complete geodesic in a closed
3-
manifold
\mathcal{M}^{3}
of strictly positive sectional curvature must intersect any closed totally geodesic surface in
\mathcal{M}^{3}
.

To our knowledge, the Chodosh-Li-Stryker Theorem
[
15
]
on complete stable minimal hypersurfaces in positively curved
4-
manifolds has not been extended to either five or six-dimensional positively curved manifolds. Such a generalization would be essential to extend the results of this paper. Additionally, the techniques in this article should be adaptable to CMC hypersurfaces under appropriate conditions in the ambient manifold. Finally, the results that contain Section
5
can be used to obtain topological obstructions to the existence of manifolds with non-negative Ricci curvature (see
[
58
]
and references therein).

To conclude, regularity issues appear in Section
6
in dimensions higher than seven and, for us, it is not clear that our results extend straightforward.

2.1
Parabolicity and quasi-isometries

An important notion in this paper is parabolicity. A Riemannian manifold
\mathcal{M}
without boundary
\partial\mathcal{M}=\emptyset
(resp. with boundary
\partial\mathcal{M}\neq\emptyset
) is
parabolic
(resp.
parabolic with boundary
) if every positive superharmonic function
u
on
\mathcal{M}
(resp. with
u=0
on
\partial\mathcal{M}
) must be constant. A crucial property of parabolicity is that it is preserved by quasi-isometries between Riemannian manifolds.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},g)
and
(\mathcal{N},h)
be two
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifolds and

\Phi:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
. Given
C\geq 1
, we say that
\Phi
is a (local) quasi-isometry, or
C
-isometry, if for any
p\in\mathcal{M}
and
v\in T_{p}\mathcal{M}
it holds

C^{-1}\,g(v,v)\leq h(d\Phi_{p}(v),d\Phi_{p}(v))\leq C\,g(v,v).

If
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
have no boundary and
\Phi:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
is a quasi-isometry diffeomorphism,
\mathcal{M}
is parabolic if and only if
\mathcal{N}
is parabolic. Also, if
\partial\mathcal{M}\neq\emptyset
,
\mathcal{N}
parabolic,
\partial\mathcal{N}=\emptyset
and
\Phi:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
is a
k
-isometry injection, then
\mathcal{M}
is parabolic (with boundary). See
[
37
, Section 3]
for details on parabolic manifolds and quasi-isometries.

2.2
Hypersurfaces in manifolds

Let
\Sigma^{n-1}\subset\mathcal{M}^{n}
be a two-sided (possibly with boundary
\partial\Sigma
) hypersurface immersed in a Riemannian manifold
\mathcal{M}
. We still denote by
g
, or
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, the Riemannian metric induced on
\Sigma
. Let
\overline{\nabla}
and
\nabla
be the Levi-Civita connections in
\mathcal{M}
and
\Sigma
respectively. Let us denote by
\mathcal{N}\Sigma
the unit normal bundle; that is,

\mathcal{N}\Sigma=\left\{(p,v)\,:\,v\in\mathcal{N}_{p}\Sigma=T_{p}\Sigma^{\perp}\subset T_{p}\mathcal{M},\,|v|=1\right\}.

Since we are assuming that
\Sigma
is two-sided,
\mathcal{N}\Sigma
is trivial, and set
N:\Sigma\to\mathcal{N}\Sigma
as a fixed unit normal. Therefore, by the Gauss Formula, we obtain

\overline{\nabla}_{X}Y=\nabla_{X}Y+\langle A(X),Y\rangle N\,\text{ for all }\,X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\Sigma),

where
A:T\Sigma\to T\Sigma
is the Weingarten (or shape) operator, given by
A(X):=-\overline{\nabla}_{X}N
. We denote the
mean curvature
and
extrinsic curvature
, respectively, as

(n-1)H={\rm Trace}(A)\,\,\,\text{ and }\,\,\,K_{e}={\rm det}(A).

Let
\overline{R}
and
R
denote the Riemann Curvature tensors of
\mathcal{M}
and
\Sigma
, respectively. Then, the Gauss Equation states that for all
X,Y,Z,W\in\mathfrak{X}(\Sigma)
we have

\langle R(X,Y)Z,W\rangle=\langle\overline{R}(X,Y)Z,W\rangle+\langle A(X),W\rangle\langle A(Y),Z\rangle-\langle A(X),Z\rangle\langle A(Y),W\rangle.

In particular, if
K(X,Y)=\langle R(X,Y)Y,X\rangle
and
\overline{K}(X,Y)=\langle\overline{R}(X,Y)Y,X\rangle
denote the sectional curvatures in
\Sigma
and
\mathcal{M}
, respectively, of the plane generated by the orthonormal vectors
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\Sigma)
, the Gauss Equation becomes

K(X,Y)=\overline{K}(X,Y)+\langle A(X),X\rangle\langle A(Y),Y\rangle-\langle A(X),Y\rangle^{2}.

We also denote by
Ric
and
Sc
the Ricci tensor and scalar curvature of
\Sigma
with the induced metric. For
p\in\Sigma
and
r>0
, let
\mathcal{D}_{p}(r)
be the metric ball in
\Sigma
centered at the point

with radius

,
\mathcal{D}_{p}(r):=\left\{q\in\Sigma\,:\,\,{\rm dist}_{\Sigma}(p,q)<r\right\}
.

A special class of hypersurfaces we will use in this paper is the class of properly quasi-embedded, which we shall define next:

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\mathcal{M}
be a properly immersed, two-sided hypersurface in a complete manifold
\mathcal{M}
.

is said to be properly quasi-embedded if there exists a compact set
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{M}
such that

The above definition leads us to:

Definition 2.3
.

Let

be a properly quasi-embedded hypersurface. We say that

.

2.3
Tubes

Let
\mathcal{M}

{\rm Tub}_{\epsilon}(\Sigma)=\left\{p\in\mathcal{M}\,:\,{\rm dist}_{\mathcal{M}}(p,\Sigma)\leq\epsilon\right\}.

Moreover,

Definition 2.4
.

We say that

is:

(a)

\epsilon
-Embedded:
The map

.

(b)

Well-oriented:
Each equidistant hypersurface

at distance
\epsilon’\in[-\epsilon,\epsilon]\setminus\left\{0\right\}
, is mean convex (non-negative mean curvature) with the orientation that points away from
\Sigma
.

(c)

Bounded extrinsic geometry:
There exists a constant
C\geq 1
so that
\Sigma(\epsilon’)
is
C
-isometric to
\Sigma
with second fundamental form uniformly bounded by

for all
\epsilon’\in[-\epsilon,\epsilon]
.

2.4
Minimal hypersurfaces

Let
\Sigma\subset\mathcal{M}
be a compact (two-sided) hypersurface with boundary

(possibly empty),

\Sigma
, and

\Psi:\Sigma\times\mathcal{I}\to\mathcal{M}

\Sigma(0)=\Psi\left(\Sigma\times\left\{0\right\}\right)
. Let
X(p)=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\Psi(p,t)
denote the variational vector field of this variation, then:

Theorem 2.1

(First variation Formula)
.

In the above conditions, if
\eta
denotes the inward unit conormal along
\partial\Sigma
and
\vec{H}
the mean curvature vector along
\Sigma
, then

\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}{\rm Area}\left(\Sigma(t)\right)=\int_{\partial\Sigma}\langle X,\eta\rangle ds-2\int_{\Sigma}\langle X,\vec{H}\rangle dv_{g}.

(2.1)

From the First Variation Formula (
2.1

\Sigma
is minimal if, and only if, it is a

of the area functional for any compactly supported variation fixing the boundary.

We continue this section by recalling the second variation formula of the area for minimal hypersurfaces. We focus on especially interesting variational vector fields
X
, those which are normal, i.e.,
X(p)=f(p)N(p)
and
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Sigma)
, where
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Sigma)
denotes the linear space of piecewise smooth functions compactly supported on
\Sigma
that vanish at the boundary
\partial\Sigma
, i.e.,
f_{|\partial\Sigma}\equiv 0
.

Remark 2.1
.

In fact, we only need
f\in H^{1,2}_{0}(\Sigma)
, where
H^{1,2}_{0}(\Sigma)
is the closure of
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Sigma)
in the topology associated to the Sobolev norm. If
\Sigma
is not compact, we will consider
f
as a compactly supported function.

A minimal hypersurface is said
stable
if for any relatively compact domain
\Omega\subset\Sigma
, the area of
\Omega
cannot be decreased up to second order by a variation
\Omega(t)
of the domain leaving the boundary
\partial\Omega(t)
fixed. In other words, if

\left.\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\right|_{t=0}{\rm Area}(\Sigma(t))\geq 0,

for any variation of the domain leaving the boundary fixed. We can write the Second Variation Formula as

\left.\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\right|_{t=0}{\rm Area}(\Sigma(t))=-\int_{\Sigma}fLf\,dv_{g},

(2.2)

where
L:C_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma)\to C_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma)
is the linearized operator of mean curvature or
Jacobi operator
(see
[
49
]
), that is,

Lf:=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}H(t)=\Delta f+(|A|^{2}+\overline{Ric}(N,N))f,

(2.3)

where
H(t)
is the mean curvature of the hypersurface
\Sigma(t)
and
|A|^{2}
denotes the square of the length of the second fundamental form of
\Sigma
. The above formula (
2.3
) is also referred to as the
First Variation Formula for the Mean Curvature
and it holds whether
\Sigma
is minimal or not. It is well-known (cf.
[
35
]
) that:

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume that
(\mathcal{M}^{n},g)
has nonnegative Ricci curvature
\overline{Ric}\geq 0
and
\Sigma\subset\mathcal{M}
is a complete, two-sided, parabolic, stable minimal hypersurface. Then,
\Sigma
is totally geodesic and the Ricci curvature of the ambient manifold vanishes along
\Sigma
in the normal direction, i.e.,
\overline{Ric}(N,N)\equiv 0
along
\Sigma
.

2.5
Local foliations

The following lemma is well known in this subject and we welcome a reader to tell us to whom it should be attributed.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Sigma^{n-1}
be a complete, embedded, two-sided minimal hypersurface in a complete manifold
\mathcal{M}^{n}
of non-negative Ricci curvature. Suppose that for some
\delta>0
the map
\Psi:\Sigma\times[0,\delta]\to\mathcal{M}
,
\Psi(p,t):={\rm exp}_{p}(tN(p))
, where
N
is a unitary normal to
\Sigma=\Psi(\Sigma\times\left\{0\right\})
, is an embedding.

If there is a
\bar{t}\in(0,\delta]
such that the equidistant
\Sigma(\bar{t})=\Psi(\Sigma\times\left\{\bar{t}\right\})
is a minimal hypersurface, then
\Psi:\Sigma\times[0,\bar{t}]\to\mathcal{M}
is an isometry onto its image, where
\Sigma\times[0,\bar{t}]
has the product metric
\sigma+dt^{2}
;
\sigma
the induced metric on
\Sigma
.

Proof.

The geodesics
\gamma_{p}(t)=F(p,t)
,
t\in[0,\delta]
, are minimizing,
\Psi
has no critical points so there are no conjugate points on
\gamma_{p}
and no two geodesics
\gamma_{p}
,
\gamma_{q}
intersect if
p\neq q
.

Let
d={\rm dist}_{\mathcal{M}}\left(\Psi(p,t),\Psi(\Sigma\times\left\{0\right\})\right)=t
denote the distance function on
\Psi(\Sigma\times[0,\delta])\subset\mathcal{M}
to
\Sigma=\Psi(\Sigma\times\left\{0\right\})
; so
\overline{\nabla}d=N_{t}
on
\Psi(\Sigma\times[0,\delta])
where
N_{t}
is the unitary normal along the equidistant
\Sigma(t)=\Psi(\Sigma\times\left\{t\right\})
,
t\in[0,\delta]
, such that
N_{0}=N
.

The First Variation Formula for the Mean Curvature (
2.3
) of the equidistant hypersurfaces
\Sigma(t)
,
t\in[0,\delta]
, is:

H^{\prime}(p,t)=|A_{t}(p)|^{2}+\overline{Ric}(\overline{\nabla}d_{\Psi(p,t)},\overline{\nabla}d_{\Psi(p,t)})\geq 0,

where
A_{t}(p)
is the second fundamental of
\Sigma(t)
at
\gamma_{p}(t)
; and the sign of
(n-1)H(p,t)={\rm Trace}\left(A_{t}(p)\right)
is such that

A_{t}(p)(u)=-\overline{\nabla}_{u}\left(\overline{\nabla}d_{\Psi(p,t)}\right),\,\,u\in T_{\Psi(p,t)}\Sigma(t).

Since
H(p,0)=H(p,\bar{t})=0
for all
p\in\Sigma
and
H
is non-decreasing on
t
, then
H(p,t)=0
for all
(p,t)\in\Sigma\times[0,\bar{t}]
, each equidistant
\Sigma(t)
,
t\in[0,\bar{t}]
is totally geodesic (
A_{t}\equiv 0
for all
t\in[0,\bar{t}]
), and
\overline{Ric}(\overline{\nabla}d,\overline{\nabla}d)\equiv 0
as well in this region
\Psi(\Sigma\times[0,\bar{t}])
.

Observe that
\overline{\nabla}d
is parallel in
\mathcal{M}
;
\overline{\nabla}_{v}\overline{\nabla}d=0
for all
v\in T_{\Psi(p,t)}\mathcal{M}
. To see this, decompose a tangent vector
v\in T_{\Psi(p,t)}\mathcal{M}
into its part tangent to the equidistant
\Sigma(t)
and normal to
\Sigma(t)
; i.e., parallel to
\overline{\nabla}d
. Since
A_{t}\equiv 0
,
\overline{\nabla}_{v}\overline{\nabla}d=0
for all
v
tangent to
\Sigma(t)
and
\overline{\nabla}_{\overline{\nabla}d}\overline{\nabla}d=0
, so
\overline{\nabla}_{v}\overline{\nabla}d=0
for all
v\in T_{\Psi(p,t)}\mathcal{M}
.

Next, notice that
\overline{\nabla}d
is a Killing field in
\mathcal{M}
:

\langle\overline{\nabla}_{X}\overline{\nabla}d,Y\rangle+\langle\overline{\nabla}_{Y}\overline{\nabla}d,X\rangle=0\text{ for any }X,Y\in T_{\Psi(p,t)}\mathcal{M}.

To check the above equation, again decompose
X
and
Y
into their tangent and normal parts. Hence, the map
\Sigma(t)\to\Sigma(s)
given by the integral curves
\gamma_{p}(t)
of
\overline{\nabla}d
is an isometry for
t,s\in[0,\bar{t}]
. Thus
\Psi
preserves the scalar product of the metric
\sigma+dt^{2}
, which proves the lemma.
∎

3
Area-minimizing hypersurfaces

In this section, we use several advanced results in Geometric Measure Theory (GMT) to address the problems we consider. We will not delve into the techniques involved in proving these theorems but will provide exact references when necessary. We are particularly grateful to Brian White for explaining several important subtleties of GMT and for patiently answering our questions. His lectures in 2012 on GMT
[
11
]
, written by Otis Chodosh, are an inspiring presentation of GMT, and we will frequently reference this text (cf. also
[
19
,
40
,
61
]
). In this section, we assume that the ambient manifold
\mathcal{M}^{n}
,
3\leq n\leq 7
, is connected (possibly with boundary), orientable, and of bounded geometry.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{M}
be an orientable properly embedded minimal hypersurface. We say that
\mathcal{F}
is area-minimizing with
\hbox{\bb Z}_{2}
-coefficients, in short, area-minimizing
{\rm mod}(2)
, if for any compact domain
C\subset\mathcal{F}
with
C^{1}
boundary and
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{M}
a compact hypersurface with
\partial\mathcal{S}=\partial C
(not as oriented hypersurfaces) and homologous to
C
,
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(C)\leq\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{S})
.

We establish here the result from GMT we will use:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{M}^{n},g)
,
3\leq n\leq 7
, be a complete, orientable Riemannian manifold and
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{M}
be a relatively compact domain with piecewise, mean convex,
C^{1}
boundary. Let
W\subset\partial\mathcal{C}
be a compact domain such that
\partial W:=\Gamma
is a disjoint union of embedded hypersurfaces in
\partial\mathcal{C}
. Then, there exists a compact area-minimizing
{\rm mod}(2)
hypersurface
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{C}
,
\partial\mathcal{F}=\Gamma
and
\mathcal{F}
homologous to
W
, i.e.,
\mathcal{F}
is area-minimizing
{\rm mod}(2)
relative to
(\mathcal{C},W,\Gamma)
.

The strategy to prove Theorem
3.1
is as follows. First, observe that the Compactness Theorem
[
11
, Section 3.4]
guarantees a convergent subsequence
F_{i}
bounding
\Gamma
whose masses approach the infimum of the mass of integral currents spanning
\Gamma
. Such a subsequence converges to a minimizer
\mathcal{F}

[
11
, Theorem 8.1]
being an integral current. Regularity theory
{\rm mod}(2)
(cf. Theorem
[
11
, Theorem 9.14]
) implies that
\mathcal{F}
is smooth, embedded, and two-sided for
3\leq n\leq 7
.

3.1
Construction of area-minimizers

Let
(\mathcal{M}^{n},g)
be an oriented complete manifold with boundary. Moreover, its boundary
\partial\mathcal{M}
is said to be
a good barrier
if it is the disjoint union of smooth hypersurfaces meeting at interior angles less than or equal to
\pi
along their boundaries and such that the mean curvature of these hypersurfaces with respect to the inward orientation is non-negative.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{M}^{n},g)
,
3\leq n\leq 7
, be a connected, orientable, complete manifold of bounded geometry with boundary

which is a good barrier and has two connected components,

and

. Let

p_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{2}

contains a properly embedded, orientable, stable minimal hypersurface

. In fact,

is area-minimizing

, intersects

, and is homologous to one of the boundary components of
\mathcal{M}
, say
\mathcal{S}_{1}
.

Lemma
3.1
essentially follows from the arguments in the proof of
[

, Lemma 2.2]
, with the modification that the ambient manifold is complete without boundary, while the boundary of the ambient manifold acts as a

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
(\mathcal{M}^{4},g)
be a complete orientable manifold of bounded geometry, non-negative sectional curvatures,
\overline{K}_{ij}\geq 0
, and scalar curvature bounded away from zero,
\overline{Sc}\geq c>0

be two properly quasi-embedded, two-sided, minimal hypersurfaces. Then, either
\Sigma_{1}

We shall explain first the meaning of parallel in a Riemannian manifold. Usually,

means that the distance between the two hypersurfaces is constant. For us, to be parallel is a stronger condition, we also ask that between the hypersurfaces the manifold has a product structure.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{M}^{n}
be a complete Riemannian manifold. We say that two disjoint properly embedded hypersurfaces
\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2}\subset\mathcal{M}
are parallel if there exists a domain
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathcal{M}\setminus\left(\Sigma_{1}\cup\Sigma_{2}\right)
such that
\partial\mathcal{U}=\Sigma_{1}\cup\Sigma_{2}
and
\overline{\mathcal{U}}
is a product manifold over
\Sigma_{1}
(or
\Sigma_{2}
); i.e,
\overline{\mathcal{U}}=\Sigma_{1}\times[0,{\bf d}]
endowed with the product metric. Here
{\bf d}:={\rm dist}_{\mathcal{M}}(\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2})>0
and
\Sigma_{2}=\Sigma_{1}\times\left\{{\bf d}\right\}
.

\partial S_{i}^{\epsilon,t}=\tilde{C}(\epsilon,t)\cup\partial K_{i}

\tilde{D}(\epsilon,t)
an area-minimizing hypersurface with boundary
\tilde{C}(\epsilon,t)
. Now, we need to prove:

Lemma 6.1
.

Fix
\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0})
and
t\in[-\epsilon_{0},\epsilon_{0}]
. Then, there exists
i_{0}\in\hbox{\bb N}
, depending on
\epsilon
,

and the upper bound for the sectional curvature of
\mathcal{M}
, so that

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{i}^{\epsilon,t})<\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(K_{i})+\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\tilde{D}(\epsilon,t))\text{ for all }i\geq i_{0}.

Proof.

Consider the smooth map
\Psi_{i}:\mathcal{A}_{i}(\epsilon)\to\mathcal{M}
given by
\Psi_{i}(q)={\rm exp}_{q}(f_{i}^{\epsilon,t}(q)N(q))
. Then,

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{i}^{\epsilon,t})=\int_{\mathcal{A}_{i}(\epsilon)}\left|{\rm Jac}\,\Psi_{i}\right|d\Sigma,

where
d\Sigma
is the volume element in
\Sigma
. Hence, we need to estimate the Jacobian
\left|{\rm Jac}\,\Psi_{i}\right|
. Let
q\in\mathcal{A}_{i}(\epsilon)
and
\left\{e_{j}\right\}_{j=1,\ldots,n-1}\in T_{q}\mathcal{A}_{i}(\epsilon)
be an orthonormal basis so that

e_{1}=\frac{\nabla f_{i}^{\epsilon,t}}{|\nabla f_{i}^{\epsilon,t}|}\text{ and }g\left(e_{j},\nabla f_{i}^{\epsilon,t}\right)=0\text{ for }j=2,\ldots,n-1;

we can do this choice for all
q\in\mathcal{A}_{i}(\epsilon)\setminus\mathcal{C}
, where
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{C})=0
. For
i=1,\ldots,n-1
, consider the geodesic
\alpha_{i}:(-\tau,\tau)\to\mathcal{A}_{i}(\epsilon)
with initial conditions
\alpha_{j}(0)=q
and
\alpha_{j}^{\prime}(0)=e_{j}
. Consider the variation by normal geodesics
g_{j}(s,r):={\rm exp}_{\alpha_{j}(s)}(rf_{i}^{\epsilon,t}(\alpha_{j}(s))N(\alpha_{j}(s)))
,
(s,r)\in(-\tau,\tau)\times[0,1]
, and set

\tilde{J}_{j}(r):=\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right|_{s=0}g_{j}(s,r),\quad r\in[0,1],

the Jacobi field along the geodesic
r\to g_{j}(0,r)
with initial condition
\tilde{J}_{j}(0)=e_{j}
. Consider also the Jacobi field along the geodesic
r\to g^{0}_{j}(0,r)
associated to the variation
g^{0}_{j}(s,r):={\rm exp}_{\alpha_{j}(s)}(rN(\alpha_{j}(s)))
, that is,

J_{j}(r):=\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right|_{s=0}g^{0}_{j}(s,r),\quad r\in[0,1],

with, also, initial condition
J_{j}(0)=e_{j}
. Observe that, since
\Sigma
is totally geodesic and
\left\{e_{j}\right\}_{j=1,\ldots,n-1}
is orthonormal, then

\left\{\gamma_{q}^{\prime}(r),J_{1}(r),\ldots,J_{n-1}(r)\right\}

is orthogonal at
q_{r}:=\gamma_{q}(r)={\rm exp}_{q}(rN(q))
for all
r>0
; in particular, it is a tangent basis along
\gamma_{q}
(cf.
[
31
, page 179]
).

Set
\bar{r}:=(f_{i}^{\epsilon,t})(q)
and
\bar{q}:=\gamma_{q}(\bar{r})\in S_{i}^{\epsilon,t}
. Then,
\left\{\tilde{J}_{j}(1)\right\}_{j=1}^{n-1}
is a basis of
T_{\bar{q}}S_{i}^{\epsilon,t}
and it can be orthogonally decomposed as

\tilde{J}_{j}(1)=J_{j}(\bar{r})+e_{j}(f_{i}^{\epsilon,t})\gamma_{q}^{\prime}(\bar{r})

(6.1)

and hence, by (
6.1
), we get

\tilde{J}_{1}(1)\wedge\ldots\wedge\tilde{J}_{n-1}(1)=J_{1}(\bar{r})\wedge\ldots\wedge J_{n-1}(\bar{r})+e_{1}(f_{i}^{\epsilon,t})\gamma_{q}^{\prime}(\bar{r})\wedge J_{2}(\bar{r})\wedge\ldots\wedge J_{n-1}(\bar{r}),

and now, since
J_{1}(\bar{r})\wedge J_{2}(\bar{r})\wedge\ldots\wedge J_{n-1}(\bar{r})
and
\gamma_{q}^{\prime}(\bar{r})\wedge J_{2}(\bar{r})\wedge\ldots\wedge J_{n-1}(\bar{r})
are orthogonal, we obtain

\left|\tilde{J}_{1}(1)\wedge\ldots\wedge\tilde{J}_{n-1}(1)\right|^{2}=\left|J_{1}(\bar{r})\wedge\ldots\wedge J_{n-1}(\bar{r})\right|^{2}\left(1+\frac{\left|\nabla f_{i}^{\epsilon,t}\right|^{2}}{\left|J_{1}(\bar{r})\right|^{2}}\right).

(6.2)

Using now that
\overline{Ric}\geq 0
, it is well-known (see
[
27
]
) that

\left|J_{1}(\bar{r})\wedge\ldots\wedge J_{n-1}(\bar{r})\right|\leq\left|e_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge e_{n-1}\right|=1.

(6.3)

Thus, since
\left|{\rm Jac}\,\Psi_{i}\right|=\left|\tilde{J}_{1}(1)\wedge\ldots\wedge\tilde{J}_{n-1}(1)\right|
and using (
6.2
) and (
6.3
), we obtain

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{i}^{\epsilon,t})\leq\int_{\mathcal{A}_{i}(\epsilon)}\sqrt{1+\frac{\left|\nabla f_{i}^{\epsilon,t}\right|^{2}}{\left|J_{1}(\bar{r})\right|^{2}}}d\Sigma.

(6.4)

Finally, we need to estimate the right-hand side of (
6.4
). First, since
|J_{1}(0)|=1
and any normal geodesic starting at any point
q\in\overline{D_{p}(\epsilon_{0})}
does not develop focal points in
[-\epsilon_{0},\epsilon_{0}]
, then
\left|J_{1}\right|\geq\lambda>0
, for some
\lambda
depending only on the upper bound of the sectional curvature. Second, using the inequality
\sqrt{1+x}\leq 1+x
for all
x\geq 0
applied to (
6.4
), we obtain

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{i}^{\epsilon,t})&\leq\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{A}_{i}(\epsilon))+\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{A}_{i}(\epsilon)}\left|\nabla f_{i}^{\epsilon,t}\right|^{2}\,d\Sigma\\
&=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(K_{i})+\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{A}_{i}(\epsilon)}\left|\nabla f_{i}^{\epsilon,t}\right|^{2}\,d\Sigma-\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(D_{p}(\epsilon)).\end{split}

(6.5)

Thus, since
\Sigma
is parabolic, there exists
i_{0}\in\hbox{\bb N}
(cf.
[
34
, Theorem 10.1]
) so that

\int_{\mathcal{A}_{i}(\epsilon)}\left|\nabla f_{i}^{\epsilon,t}\right|^{2}\,d\Sigma<\lambda^{2}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(D_{p}(\epsilon))\text{ for all }i\geq i_{0},

that applied to (
6.5
) implies
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{i}^{\epsilon,t})<\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(K_{i})
for
i\geq i_{0}
. The proof is completed.
∎

Remark 6.1

(Douglas Criterion for the Plateau Problem)
.

When we want find area-minimizing hypersurfaces (currents) in
\mathcal{M}
with two (disjoint) boundary components
\Gamma_{1}\cup\Gamma_{2}
that is connected; we must ensure that there exists a comparison hypersurface
S\subset\mathcal{M}
,
\partial S=\Gamma_{1}\cup\Gamma_{2}
, of area less than
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\Sigma_{1})+\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\Sigma_{2})
, where
\Sigma_{i}
are ay least-area hypersurfaces bounding
\Gamma_{i}
,
i=1,2
. In such case, any area-minimizing hypersurface
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{M}
with
\partial\mathcal{A}=\Gamma_{1}\cup\Gamma_{2}
must be connected.

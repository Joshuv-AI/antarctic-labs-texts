Regression with Label Permutation in Generalized Linear Model


Abstract
1 1 1 The initial version of this paper was submitted in May 2021. The assumption that response and predictor belong to the same statistical unit may be violated in practice. Unbiased estimation and recovery of true label ordering based on unlabeled data are challenging tasks and have attracted increasing attentions in the recent literature. In this paper, we present a relatively complete analysis of label permutation problem for the generalized linear model with multivariate responses. The theory is established under different scenarios, with knowledge of true parameters, with partial knowledge of underlying label permutation matrix and without any knowledge. Our results remove the stringent conditions required by the current literature and are further extended to the missing observation setting which has never been considered in the field of label permutation problem. On computational side, we propose two methods, “maximum likelihood estimation” algorithm and “two-step estimation” algorithm, to accommodate for different settings. When the proportion of permuted labels is moderate, both methods work effectively. Multiple numerical experiments are provided and corroborate our theoretical findings.

Introduction

A key assumption in regression problems is that response-predictor pairs correspond to the same statistical unit. In practice, this assumption may be violated when different subsets of variables are collected asynchronously and are merged together with certain label disagreements.
That is, responses and predictors may not be perfectly paired together so that the statistical inferences based on such label-contaminated data sets could be inaccurate and biased.
Research on the unlabeled problem has a long history and can be traced back to 1970s under the
name “broken sample problem”
(
DeGroot et al. 1971
;
Goel 1975
;
DeGroot and Goel 1976
;
DeGroot and Goel 1980
;
Chan and Loh 2001
;
Bai and Hsing 2005
;
Slawski et al. 2020
)
.
In recent years, we
have witnessed a renaissance of this problem due to its wide applications, such as data integration,
privacy protection, computer vision, robotics, sensor networks, etc.
See
Unnikrishnan et al. 2015
;
Pananjady et al. 2018
;
Pananjady et al. 2017
;
Slawski and Ben-David 2019
;
Zhang et al. 2019
;
Slawski et al. 2020
;
Zhang et al. 2022

and the references therein for more explanations.

Important applications of label permutation problem include linkage record, data de-anonymization, and
header-free communication. In linkage record
(
Newcombe and Kennedy 1962
;
Fellegi and Sunter 1969
)
, people would like to integrate multiple databases,
where each contains different pieces of information about the same identity, into one comprehensive
database. In this process, the biggest challenge is how to find the matching across different databases.
For data de-anonymization
(
Nazarov et al. 2018
)
, the task is to identify
the labels, which aims to preserve privacy, with public data. It can be seen as the inverse problem of privacy protection.
For the header-free communication
(
Pananjady et al. 2017
;
Shi and Shi 2019
)
, we have a sensor network where the sensor identity is omitted during communication to reduce the transmission cost and latency. In this scenario, reconstruction of signal involves recovering the unknown correspondence.

In the literature, for the sake of simplicity, linear models are often considered for studying the label permutation problem.
However, the linear model assumption may be violated when the error distribution is skewed or heavy tailed.
Additionally, linear models cannot capture the structure of count data which is another popular data type and is becoming increasingly ubiquitous (e.g. survival analysis
(
Fleming and Harrington 2011
)
, online streaming services
(
Cugola and Margara 2012
)
, educational testing
(
Templin and Henson 2010
)
, etc).
With these in mind, in this paper, we specifically adopt the formulation of generalized linear model (GLM) to take care of multivariate non-Gaussian responses.
The problem is formulated as follows,

\displaystyle Y=\Pi^{\sharp}Y^{\sharp},

(1)

where
Y^{\sharp}
is the response matrix when the data are labeled correctly. For each entry of
Y^{\sharp}
,
Y^{\sharp}[i,l]
admits the density function

f_{il}(y)=\exp\{y\lambda_{il}-\psi(\lambda_{il})+c(y)\}

for
i\in[n]
and
l\in[m]
with
\lambda_{il}=\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b}_{l}^{\sharp}
.
Here,
n
is the number of units/individuals,
m
is the number of observations for each unit/individual,
\lambda_{il}
refers to the natural parameter.
c(y)
is a nuisance function free of parameter.
Unobserved
\Pi^{\sharp}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
denotes an underlying row permutation matrix.
In other words, we only observe the response matrix up to certain label permutations.

X:=(\mathbf{x}_{i})\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
represents the covariate/design matrix which is fully observed;
B^{\sharp}:=(\mathbf{b}_{l}^{\sharp})\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times m}
is the underlying true parameter coefficient matrix, which may be unknown. Our task is to recover the label permutation matrix
\Pi^{\sharp}
based on permuted data
Y
and design matrix
X
.

Contributions.
In this paper, we study the label permutation problem under generalized linear model framework which is different from the classical linear model in the following ways. The response could be discrete instead of continuous such that the resulting estimator does not admit a nice closed form. In the linear model, the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) plays an important role in recovering the underlying labels. In contrast, there is no such unified criteria in the generalized linear model.
The existing work of
Wang et al. 2020
for generalized linear model only considers the case of
m=1
. When the regression parameter
B
is assumed to be known, the sufficient condition for label permutation recovery requires

\min_{1\leq i_{1}\neq i_{2}\leq n}|\psi^{\prime}(\lambda_{i_{1}})-\psi^{\prime}(\lambda_{i_{2}})|
grows as
n
grows.
There are no theoretical guarantees under the situation
m>1
or the situation when regression parameter
B
is unknown.
In this paper, we bridge this gap and show the perfect label recovery results under different scenarios.
Moreover, we also consider the situation of missing observations, i.e., each entry in response
Y
may be missing completely at random with certain probability.
The corresponding theory has also been established in this paper.
Such missing observation case has not been studied yet in the literature for unlabeled regression problems.
On the technical side, we also want to point out the analysis of generalized linear models is harder than that of linear models due to the existence of exponential function whose second order derivative may not be bounded. Additionally, we need to take special care of analyzing the maximum likelihood estimator which admits no closed form and involves
n!
different possible permutations.
The results in current paper add theoretical values in many applications, e.g., data integration, privacy protection, etc.

2.2
Model

In this paper, we specifically study the generalized linear model with label permutation. The problem can be written in the following matrix form,

\displaystyle Y=\Pi^{\sharp}Y^{\sharp},

(2)

where
Y^{\sharp}\sim f(XB^{\sharp})
, i.e.,
Y^{\sharp}[i,l]\sim f_{il}(y)
with

f_{il}(y)=\exp\{y\lambda_{il}-\psi(\lambda_{il})+c(y)\},~\lambda_{il}=\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b}_{l}^{\sharp}

for
i\in[n]
and
l\in[m]
.
Unobserved
\Pi^{\sharp}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
denotes an underlying row permutation matrix (i.e. a binary matrix with each row/column containing one and only one non-zero entry),
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
represents the covariate/design matrix, and
B^{\sharp}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times m}
is the underlying true parameter coefficient matrix.
Here
\psi(\cdot)
is a smooth uni-variate convex function over
\mathbb{R}
.
When we take function
\psi(x)=x^{2}
, then the density is a normal density.
When we take
\psi(x)=\exp\{x\}
, then it becomes a Poisson distribution.
Our goal is to recover the underlying permutation matrix
\Pi^{\sharp}
given mislabeled observations
Y
and
X
.

For a fixed permutation
\Pi
, the log-likelihood function after removing the nuisance parts (the term not related to the parameters) is given by

\displaystyle L(\Pi,B)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{l=1}^{m}\bigg\{Y[i,l](\mathbf{x}_{\Pi(i)}^{T}\mathbf{b}_{l})-\psi(\mathbf{x}_{\Pi(i)}^{T}\mathbf{b}_{l})\bigg\}=\langle-\psi(\Pi XB)+Y\circ\Pi XB\rangle.

(3)

In the rest of paper, we consider to recover
\Pi^{\sharp}
by maximizing the above log-likelihood function. When the true parameter matrix
B^{\sharp}
is known, the estimator will be

\hat{\Pi}:=\arg\max_{\Pi}L(\Pi,B^{\sharp})
.
When the parameter matrix
B
is unknown, the estimator will be

\hat{\Pi}:=\arg\max_{\Pi}\max_{B}L(\Pi,B).

We use
\sharp
to denote the true value and use
a\gtrsim b~(a\lesssim b)
to represent
a\geq Kb~(a\leq b/K)
for some sufficiently large constant
K
.

\|\mathbf{a}\|
and
\|A\|
represent
\ell_{2}
norm of vector
\mathbf{a}
and spectral norm of matrix
A
, respectively.
For any
\mathcal{S}
, its cardinality is denoted by
|\mathcal{S}|
.
For two positive real numbers
a
and
b
,
b=O(a),b=\Omega(a)
and
b=\Theta(a)
indicate the relations,
b\leq C_{2}a
,
b\geq a/C_{1}
and
a/C_{1}\leq b\leq C_{2}a
, correspondingly, where
C_{1},C_{2}
are some constants.
For random sequences,
x_{n}=o_{p}(1)
means
x_{n}
converges to
0
in probability and
x_{n}=O_{p}(y_{n})
means
x_{n}/y_{n}
is stochastically bounded as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
For an arbitrary univariate function
f
,
f(\mathbf{a})
and
f(A)
are obtained via applying

to vector
\mathbf{a}
and matrix

elementwisely.
We let
\psi^{\prime}(\lambda)
and
\psi^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)
be the first and second order derivative of

.

3
Recovery Analysis when
B^{\sharp}
is known

3.1
Permutation Recovery

When
B^{\sharp}
is known, we only need to estimate
\Pi
by maximizing the likelihood function without estimating
B
.
In other words, the best estimator should be

\displaystyle\hat{\Pi}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\arg\max_{\Pi}L(\Pi,B^{\sharp})=\arg\max_{\Pi}\langle-\psi(\Pi XB^{\sharp})+Y\circ\Pi XB^{\sharp}\rangle.

Successful recovery of label permutation matrix (i.e.
\hat{\Pi}=\Pi^{\sharp}
) means that

\displaystyle\langle-\psi(\Pi XB^{\sharp})+Y\circ\Pi XB^{\sharp}\rangle\leq\langle-\psi(\Pi^{\sharp}XB^{\sharp})+Y\circ\Pi^{\sharp}XB^{\sharp}\rangle

holds for any
\Pi\neq\Pi^{\sharp}
.
In other words, for any fixed
\Pi\neq\Pi^{\sharp}
, we need to identify certain sufficient conditions to ensure that the following probability

\displaystyle P\big(\sup_{\Pi\neq\Pi^{\sharp}}\langle-\psi(\Pi XB)+Y\circ\Pi XB\rangle\geq\langle-\psi(\Pi^{\sharp}XB)+Y\circ\Pi^{\sharp}XB\rangle\big)

is vanishing as both
n
and
m
go to infinity in a suitable asymptotic regime.

Before moving to our main results, we first introduce the following row-wise quantities.

Information Gap
For each pair of individuals
i
and
j
, we define

\displaystyle\Delta_{ij}:=\langle\psi^{\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i})\circ\bm{\lambda}_{i}-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{i})\rangle-\langle\psi^{\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i})\circ\bm{\lambda}_{j}-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{j})\rangle,

(4)

where
\bm{\lambda}_{i}=(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b}_{1}^{\sharp},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b}_{m}^{\sharp})
for
i\in[n]
.

Variance
For each pair of individuals
i
and
j
, we define

\displaystyle v_{ij}:=\langle\psi^{\prime\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i})\circ(\bm{\lambda}_{i}-\bm{\lambda}_{j})^{2}\rangle=\sum_{l=1}^{m}\psi^{\prime\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l])(\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l]-\bm{\lambda}_{j}[l])^{2},

(5)

where
\psi^{\prime}
and
\psi^{\prime\prime}
are the first and second order derivative of function
\psi
.

It can be checked that
\Delta_{ij}
and
v_{ij}
are the expectation and variance of
\langle\mathbf{y}_{i}\circ\bm{\lambda}_{i}-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{i})\rangle-\langle\mathbf{y}_{i}\circ\bm{\lambda}_{j}-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{j})\rangle
, respectively.
We can show that all labels are distinguishable when
\Delta_{ij}
is relatively large compared with
v_{ij}
for all pairs of
i
and
j
.
In other words,
\min_{i,j\in[n]}\Delta_{ij}^{2}/v_{ij}
can be viewed as the counterpart of
signal-to-noise ratio
(SNR,
Pananjady et al. 2018
) in the linear regression setting.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume
B^{\sharp}
is known and suppose
X
,
B^{\sharp}
and
\Pi^{\sharp}
satisfy that

\displaystyle\Delta_{ij}\gtrsim\sqrt{(\log n)v_{ij}},~~\forall i,j\in[n].

(6)

We have the perfect label recovery with high probability,

\displaystyle P(\hat{\Pi}\neq\Pi^{\sharp})\leq n^{2}\max_{i\neq j}\exp\{-\frac{\Delta_{ij}^{2}}{16v_{ij}}\}\rightarrow 0.

By comparing the orders of two sides in (
6
), it is easier for larger value of
m
to satisfy (
6
).

3.2
Examples

In this section, multiple examples along with intuitive explanations are given to illustrate the relationship between
m
and
n
for perfect permutation recovery.
Examples
3.1
-
3.4
given below describe four different data generation mechanisms. We can observe that, in those cases, it is impossible to recover
\Pi^{\sharp}
when
m=1
.
Hence, the sufficient conditions,

\displaystyle\min_{i_{1}\neq i_{2}}|\psi^{\prime}(\lambda_{i_{1}})-\psi^{\prime}(\lambda_{i_{2}})|\gtrsim\sqrt{\log n}~(\textrm{linear model}),

\displaystyle\min_{i_{1}\neq i_{2}}|\sqrt{\psi^{\prime}(\lambda_{i_{1}})}-\sqrt{\psi^{\prime}(\lambda_{i_{2}})}|\gtrsim\sqrt{\log n}~(\textrm{poisson model})

given by
Wang et al. 2020

are too restrictive.

Example 3.1
.

Consider the scenario
p=1
, then
X=\mathbf{x}
is a vector. Assume
\mathbf{x}[i]\sim\textrm{Uniform}[a_{1},a_{2}]
for all
i
and assume each entry of
B^{\sharp}
is bounded between
b_{1}
and
b_{2}
(
0<b_{1}<b_{2}
).
Without loss of generality,
a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2}
are all positive.
We define
x_{gap}:=\min_{i,j}x_{gap,ij}:=\min_{i,j}|\mathbf{x}[i]-\mathbf{x}[j]|
, which is the minimum difference between any pair,
\mathbf{x}[i]
and
\mathbf{x}[j]
.
It is not hard to see that
x_{gap}=\Theta_{p}(\frac{1}{n^{2}})
. Moreover, when
m\gtrsim n^{4}\log n
, it is sufficient for recovery of
\Pi^{\sharp}
.

Example 3.2
.

Consider the scenario
p=log_{2}(n)+1
and
n=2^{n_{1}}
(
n_{1}
is a positive integer) and the design matrix
X
is complete in the sense that it satisfies

X=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0&\cdots&0\\
1&1&0&\cdots&0\\
1&0&1&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
1&1&1&\cdots&1\\
\end{pmatrix}.

For instance, in the educational testing
(
Templin and Henson 2010
)
,
each of
n
rows corresponds to a student with certain skill sets. The first column represents the intercept and rest of columns represent
p

Without loss of generality, we assume that each entry of
B^{\sharp}
is generated from the standard normal distribution. In this case, it suffices to require
m\geq\log n
for correctly estimating
\Pi^{\sharp}
for any strictly convex
\psi
with bounded second derivative.

Example 3.3
.

Consider the scenario

and

with
C_{p}^{s}\geq n
. (

is the

) with

\psi

3.3
On the lower bound of
m

In this section, we discuss the minimum required number of
m
for permutation recovery.
In particular, we consider the case that
B^{\sharp}
is known, which is an easier task compared with the problem when
B^{\sharp}
is unknown.
To start with, we first recall the following Fano’s lemma
(
Assouad 1996
)
.

Lemma 3.2

(Fano’s Lemma)
.

Let
X
be a random variable following probability distribution
f
, where
f
is from set
\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{r+1}\}
which satisfies that

KL(f_{i}\|f_{j})\leq\beta~~\textrm{for all}~~i\neq j.

Let
\psi(X)\in\{1,\ldots,r+1\}
be an estimate of index of distribution. Then

\displaystyle\inf_{\psi}\sup_{i}P(\psi(X)\neq i)\geq 1-\frac{\beta+\log 2}{\log r}.

(7)

We consider the following
n!
models. For any permutation matrix
\Pi_{k}

(k=1,\ldots,n!)
, we define
f_{k}
as the probability distribution of
Y=\Pi_{k}Y^{\sharp}
.
Therefore, the KL divergence between
f_{k_{1}}
and
f_{k_{2}}
is

\displaystyle KL(f_{k_{1}}\|f_{k_{2}})=

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{f_{k_{1}}}\langle Y\circ\bm{\Lambda}_{k_{1}}-\psi(\bm{\Lambda}_{k_{1}})\rangle-\mathbf{E}_{f_{k_{1}}}\langle Y\circ\bm{\Lambda}_{k_{2}}-\psi(\bm{\Lambda}_{k_{2}})\rangle

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\Lambda_{k_{1}}(\Pi_{k_{1}},B^{\sharp})-\Lambda_{k_{1}}(\Pi_{k_{2}},B^{\sharp}),

(8)

where
\bm{\Lambda}_{k}=\Pi_{k}XB^{\sharp}
.
We let
\Delta(X,B^{\sharp}):=\max_{k_{1}\neq k_{2}}\Lambda_{k_{1}}(\Pi_{k_{1}},B^{\sharp})-\Lambda_{k_{1}}(\Pi_{k_{2}},B^{\sharp})
.
Therefore, by Fano’s lemma, we have

\inf_{\hat{\Pi}}\sup_{\Pi_{k}}P(\hat{\Pi}\neq\Pi_{k})\geq 1-\frac{\Delta(X,B^{\sharp})+\log 2}{\log(n!)}
.
In particular, if
\Delta(X,B^{\sharp})\leq Cmn
, we consequently have

\displaystyle\inf_{\hat{\Pi}}\sup_{\Pi_{k}}P(\hat{\Pi}\neq\Pi_{k})\geq 1-\frac{Cmn+\log 2}{n\log n}\geq 1/2,

(9)

when
m\lesssim\log n
.
In other words,
m=\log n
is the minimal number for perfect permutation recovery up to a multiplicative constant in Example
3.4
.

On the other hand,
m\gtrsim\log n
may not be tight under some situations.
For instance, in Example 1, we can see that there exist
i_{1}
and
i_{2}
such that

|\mathbf{x}_{i_{1}}-\mathbf{x}_{i_{2}}|
is
\Theta(1/n^{2})
.
We let
\Pi_{1}=\mathbf{I}
and set
\Pi_{2}(i)=i
for
i\neq i_{1},i_{2}
and
\Pi_{2}(i_{1})=i_{2}
and
\Pi_{2}(i_{2})=i_{1}
.
Under such case, we have the following result.

Corollary 3.3
.

By the constructions of
\Pi_{1}
and
\Pi_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\inf_{\hat{\Pi}}\sup_{\Pi_{k}\in\{\Pi_{1},\Pi_{2}\}}P(\hat{\Pi}\neq\Pi_{k})\geq 1-\frac{cm/n^{4}+\log 2}{\log 2}.

(10)

Thus, the minimum requirement of
m
is at least of order
n^{4}
(
\gg 1
) in Example
3.1
.

4
Recovery Analysis when
B^{\sharp}
is unknown

However, in practice, we have no prior knowledge of
B
and need to estimate the parameter matrix
B
and permutation matrix
\Pi
simultaneously.
For a fixed
\Pi
, we define
\hat{B}(\Pi)
to be the best estimator maximizing the likelihood function, that is,

\displaystyle\hat{B}(\Pi)=\arg\max_{B}\langle-\psi(\Pi XB)+Y\circ\Pi XB\rangle.

(11)

On the computational side, this is a concave optimization and
\hat{B}(\Pi)
can be solved efficiently.
On theoretical side,
\hat{B}(\Pi)
does not admit explicit form which makes analysis harder.
In the following, we discuss situations under which the labels can be recovered perfectly.

First of all, we note that the model is not identifiable when
p\geq n
and
X
has full row rank.
This is because, there exists a
p
by
n
matrix
P_{x}
such that
I=XP_{x}
.
We can find that

\Pi^{\sharp}XB^{\sharp}=\Pi^{\sharp}\Pi\Pi XB^{\sharp}=(\Pi^{\sharp}\Pi)X(P_{x}\Pi XB^{\sharp}).

Thus, the underlying
\Pi^{\sharp}
and
B^{\sharp}
are again not identifiable.
Such non-identifiability means that we have no chance to recover the true label permutation matrix, since there exist multiple global optimal values. In
Unnikrishnan et al. 2015
, it is further shown that
n\geq 2p
is a necessary condition for perfect permutation recovery under the linear model setting with
m=1
and zero noise.

In the rest of paper, we only consider the case that
p<n
for the generalized linear model. Moreover, we only focus on the situation that
p
is
n^{a}
with
a<1/2
so that the estimator has nice asymptotic properties even if we do not know the true
\Pi^{\sharp}
.

We recall the definition of log-likelihood function

L(\Pi,B)=\langle-\psi(\Pi XB)+Y\circ(\Pi XB)\rangle

and introduce its corresponding population version

\displaystyle\Lambda(\Pi,B):=\mathbb{E}\langle-\psi(\Pi XB)+Y\circ(\Pi XB)\rangle=\langle-\psi(\Pi XB)+\psi^{\prime}(\Pi^{\sharp}XB^{\sharp})\circ(\Pi XB)\rangle,

(12)

where the expectation is taken with respect to
Y
.

4.1
Scenario 1:
d(\mathbf{I},\Pi^{\sharp})
is small

If we have the prior knowledge that the underlying permutation matrix
\Pi^{\sharp}
is close to
\mathbf{I}
(i.e.
d(\mathbf{I},\Pi^{\sharp})
is small), we then
consider a “two-step estimation” computational method for dealing such case.
In the first step, “two-step” algorithm aims to find a reasonable estimator of
B^{\sharp}
by treating
\Pi=\mathbf{I}
. In the second step, we plug in this estimator to the objective and obtain the permutation matrix by maximizing the log-likelihood function.
The implementation details are given in Algorithm
1
.

Algorithm 1

Two-step Estimation.

Input:

Observation matrix
Y
and design matrix
X
.

Output:

Estimated permutation matrix
\hat{\Pi}
and estimated coefficient matrix

\hat{B}
.

1. Solve
\hat{B}:=\arg\max_{B}\{\langle-\psi(XB)+Y\circ XB\rangle\}
.

2. Solve
\hat{\Pi}:=\arg\max_{\Pi}\{\langle-\psi(\Pi X\hat{B})+Y\circ\Pi X\hat{B}\rangle\}
.

In below, we provide a theoretical analysis of the proposed two-step estimator.
Under mild conditions, we show that
\hat{\Pi}
returned by Algorithm
1
perfectly matches
\Pi^{\sharp}
with high probability.
To start with, we first assume the following assumptions on function
\psi
and design matrix,
X
.

A0
We assume that
\psi^{\prime\prime}(\cdot)
is either monotonic or bounded.

A1
Each entry of
X
is bounded (i.e.
|X[i,j]|\leq C_{0}
for universal constant
C_{0}
).

A2
There exist constants
c_{1}>0
, and
\gamma_{1p}
(which may depend on
p
) such that

\sharp\{i:X[i,:]\mathbf{b}\geq c_{1}\}\geq n/\gamma_{1p}

and
\sharp\{i:X[i,:]\mathbf{b}\leq-c_{1}\}\geq n/\gamma_{1p}
hold for any
\mathbf{b}
with
\|\mathbf{b}\|=1
.

Remark 4.1
.

Assumption A0 is satisfied by most generalized linear models.
For examples,
\psi^{\prime\prime}
is bounded for Gaussian or Bernoulli distribution;

\psi^{\prime\prime}
is monotonic for Poisson or Gamma distribution.

Remark 4.2
.

For a general
n
by
p
matrix
X
, its largest singular value is bounded by
\sqrt{n}\sqrt{p}\max_{i,j}|X[i,j]|=O(\sqrt{np})
.
For an
n\times p
matrix
X
with each entry being sampled from sub-Gaussian distribution, then its largest singular value is
O_{p}(\sqrt{n}+\sqrt{p})
.
(Here we say
Z
is a sub-Gaussian random variable if
\mathbb{E}\exp\{tZ\}\leq\exp\{t^{2}\sigma^{2}/2\}
holds for all
t>0
and fixed constant
\sigma
.)

Remark 4.3
.

It can be checked that Assumption A2 is satisfied with high probability when
X
is a matrix with i.i.d sub-Gaussian random variables as its entries. Under such case,
\gamma_{1p}
is reduced to some constant. Furthermore, A2 tells us that the smallest singular value of
X
is bounded from below. In fact,
\sigma_{min}(X)\geq\sqrt{c_{1}^{2}n/\gamma_{1p}}=c_{1}\sqrt{n}/\sqrt{\gamma_{1p}}
.

We further introduce the following notations:

x_{max}:=\max_{i}\|\mathbf{x}_{i}\|
;

\psi_{max}^{{}^{\prime}\sharp}:=\max_{i,j}\psi(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b}_{j}^{\sharp})
,
\psi_{max}^{{}^{\prime\prime}\sharp}:=\max_{i,j}\exp\{\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b}_{j}^{\sharp}\}
,
and

\psi_{min}^{{}^{\prime\prime}\sharp}:=\min_{i,j}\exp\{\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b}_{j}^{\sharp}\}

which represent the maximum expected value of
Y_{ij}
’s, maximum variance/minimum variance of
Y_{ij}
’s respectively.
We also write
\psi_{cb}^{\sharp}=\psi_{max}^{{}^{\prime}\sharp}+\psi_{max}^{{}^{\prime\prime}\sharp}

and define permutation-wise variance term

v_{\Pi,partial}=\sum_{i:\Pi^{\sharp}(i)\neq\Pi(i)}\sum_{l=1}^{m}\psi^{\prime\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{\Pi^{\sharp}(i)}^{\sharp}[l])(\bm{\lambda}_{\Pi(i)}^{\sharp}[l]-\bm{\lambda}_{\Pi^{\sharp}(i)}^{\sharp}[l])^{2}

and minimum pairwise variance term

v_{min}=\min_{i,j}\sum_{l=1}^{m}\psi^{\prime\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{\sharp}[l])(\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{\sharp}[l]-\bm{\lambda}_{j}^{\sharp}[l])^{2}

to quantify the differences between
X
’s rows.

Theorem 4.1
.

With the knowledge that
d(\mathbf{I},\Pi^{\sharp})\leq h_{max}
and assumptions A0 - A2, we also assume that
p=O(n^{a})
(
a<\frac{1}{2}
) and
h_{max}\lesssim n/(p\gamma_{1p}\log n)
.
Then it holds that

\displaystyle\|\hat{\mathbf{b}}_{l}-\mathbf{b}_{l}^{\sharp}\|=:O_{p}(\delta^{\ast})=O_{p}(\frac{\sqrt{p}(\sqrt{\psi_{cb}^{\sharp}}\sqrt{n-h_{max}}+\psi_{cb}^{\sharp}h_{max}\log n)}{n\psi_{min}^{{}^{\prime\prime}\sharp}}\gamma_{1p})

(13)

for
l\in[m]
.
Furthermore, if

\displaystyle\Lambda(\Pi^{\sharp},B^{\sharp})-\Lambda(\Pi,B^{\sharp})\gtrsim v_{\Pi,partial}\cdot\sqrt{\log n/v_{min}}

(14)

and

\displaystyle\Lambda(\Pi^{\sharp},B^{\sharp})-\Lambda(\Pi,B^{\sharp})\gtrsim md(\Pi,\Pi^{\sharp})\psi_{cb}^{\sharp}x_{max}\delta^{\ast},

(15)

then it holds that

P(\hat{\Pi}\neq\Pi^{\sharp})\rightarrow 0
.

Here,
\delta^{\ast}
defined in (
13
) is the estimation error for regression parameter. The first term can be viewed as the variance term and the second term is the bias term. Conditions (
14
) and (
15
) require that the information gap should dominate the errors caused by variance and bias, respectively.
The requirement
h_{max}\leq n/(p\gamma_{1p}\log n)
restricts the number of permuted labels.
When
p
is fixed, then
n/(\log n)
is similar to the condition
h_{max}\leq cn/(\log(n/h_{max}))
required in
Slawski et al. 2020
for linear models. The additional
p
in the denominator appears since that we do not have the access to the explicit form of
\hat{B}(\Pi)
in generalized linear model setting.

Especially, we can find that
\Lambda(\Pi^{\sharp},B^{\sharp})-\Lambda(\Pi,B^{\sharp})
is
\Omega(md(\Pi,\Pi^{\sharp}))
,

v_{\Pi,partial}
is
O(md(\Pi,\Pi^{\sharp}))
and
v_{min}=\Omega(m)
when
\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{\sharp}[l]
is bounded and
\min_{i,j}\|\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{\sharp}-\bm{\lambda}_{j}^{\sharp}\|_{1}
is
\Omega(m)
.
Then condition (
14
) can be simplified to

md(\Pi,\Pi^{\sharp})\gtrsim md(\Pi,\Pi^{\sharp})\sqrt{\log n/m},

which requires
m\geq K\log n
for some large constant
K
. This leads to the following corollary.

Corollary 4.2
.

Under the sub-Gaussian design matrix
X
with knowledge that
d(\mathbf{I},\Pi^{\sharp})\leq h_{max}
, we assume
h_{max}p=n/\log n
,
p=O(n^{a})
(
a<\frac{1}{2}
), and
m\gtrsim\log n
.
We have

P(\hat{\Pi}\neq\Pi^{\sharp})\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as}~n\rightarrow\infty.

4.2
Scenario 2: no knowledge of
d(\mathbf{I},\Pi^{\sharp})

For general
\Pi^{\sharp}
, we also aim to recover the underlying permutation matrix without any knowledge of
d(\mathbf{I},\Pi^{\sharp})
and
B^{\sharp}
.
To facilitate the theoretical analysis, we first define
B(\Pi):=\arg\max_{B}\Lambda(\Pi,B)
.
Then it is straightforward to check that
B(\Pi^{\sharp})=B^{\sharp}
.
We also define

\displaystyle\Lambda(\Pi):=\max_{B}\Lambda(\Pi,B)=\Lambda(\Pi,B(\Pi)).

(16)

We additionally introduce the following permutation-wise quantities.

Information Gap
For each permutation
\Pi
, we define

\displaystyle\Delta(X,B^{\sharp},\Pi^{\sharp},\Pi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Lambda(\Pi^{\sharp})-\Lambda(\Pi).

(17)

This quantity can be interpreted as the information gap between permutation
\Pi^{\sharp}
and
\Pi
. It can be seen that
\Delta(X,B^{\sharp},\Pi^{\sharp},\Pi)
is always non-negative.

Variance
For each fixed permutation
\Pi
, we define

\displaystyle v_{\Pi,B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{l=1}^{m}\psi^{\prime\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{\Pi^{\sharp}(i)}[l])(\bm{\lambda}_{\Pi(i)}[l])^{2}.

(18)

It can be easily checked that
v_{\Pi,B}
is the variance of
L(\Pi,B)
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Under assumptions A0 - A2 and with no knowledge of
B^{\sharp}
and
\Pi^{\sharp}
, we assume that
p=O(n^{a})
(
a<\frac{1}{2}
) and there exists a constant
c_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\Delta(X,B^{\sharp},\Pi^{\sharp},\Pi)\gtrsim\max\{\sqrt{(n+mp)mn\psi_{max}^{{}^{\prime\prime}\sharp}x_{max}^{2}\log n},(n\log n+mp)x_{max}\}

(19)

for any
\Pi
satisfying
d(\Pi,\Pi^{\sharp})>c_{0}\frac{n}{p\gamma_{1p}\log n}
, and

\displaystyle\Lambda(\Pi^{\sharp},B^{\sharp})-\Lambda(\Pi,B^{\sharp})\gtrsim\max\{v_{\Pi,partial}\cdot\sqrt{\log n/v_{min}},md(\Pi,\Pi^{\sharp})\psi_{cb}^{\sharp}x_{max}\delta^{\ast}\}

(20)

for any
\Pi
satisfying
d(\Pi,\Pi^{\sharp})\leq c_{0}\frac{n}{p\gamma_{1p}\log n}
.
Then it holds that

\displaystyle P(\hat{\Pi}\neq\Pi^{\sharp})\rightarrow 0

(21)

as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
Furthermore,

\|\hat{\mathbf{b}}_{l}-\mathbf{b}_{l}^{\sharp}\|=O_{p}(\frac{\gamma_{1p}\sqrt{p\psi_{cb}^{\sharp}}}{\sqrt{n}\psi_{min}^{\sharp}})

for all
l\in[m]
.

Based on Theorem
4.3
, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 4.4
.

Without any knowledge of
B^{\sharp}
and
\Pi^{\sharp}
, we assume that

\Delta(X,B^{\sharp},\Pi^{\sharp},\Pi)\geq c_{1}md(\Pi,\Pi^{\sharp})
for
d(\Pi,\Pi^{\sharp})>\frac{c_{0}n}{p\log n}

and

\Lambda(\Pi^{\sharp},B^{\sharp})-\Lambda(\Pi,B^{\sharp})\geq c_{2}md(\Pi,\Pi^{\sharp})
for
d(\Pi,\Pi^{\sharp})\leq\frac{c_{0}n}{p\log n}

(
c_{0}
,
c_{1}
,
c_{2}
are universal constants).
Then it holds that

P(\hat{\Pi}\neq\Pi^{\sharp})\rightarrow 0,

as long as
m/(x_{max}^{2}\log n)\rightarrow\infty
,
\gamma_{1p}=O(1)
and
p=n^{a}
(
0<a<\frac{1}{2}
).

4.3
On Computation of Maximum Likelihood Estimator

We consider to compute the maximum likelihood estimator, which is

\displaystyle(\hat{\Pi},\hat{B})=\arg\max_{\Pi,B}\langle-\psi(\Pi XB)+Y\circ\Pi XB\rangle.

(22)

Unfortunately, when
p>1
, the above optimization problem (even for the linear models) is NP-hard.
We consider a coordinate ascent method, i.e., alternatively maximizing
\Pi
given
B
and maximizing
B
given
\Pi
. The method will always converge to some critical point but not necessarily to the global optimal solution.

For the choice of a good initial permutation matrix
\Pi_{ini}
,
we consider the following heuristic objective,

\displaystyle\Pi_{ini}=\arg\max_{\Pi}\langle\Pi,Y_{\psi}Y_{\psi}^{T}XX^{T}\rangle,

(23)

where
Y_{\psi}=(\psi^{\prime})^{-1}(Y+1)
be the inverse transformation of the original data through link function
\psi^{\prime}
.
The intuition is that
Y_{\psi}\approx\Pi^{\sharp}XB^{\sharp}
.
It can be calculated that

\displaystyle\langle\Pi,Y_{\psi}Y_{\psi}^{T}XX^{T}\rangle\approx\langle\Pi,\Pi^{\sharp}XB^{\sharp}(\Pi^{\sharp}XB^{\sharp})^{T}XX^{T}\rangle=\|B^{\sharp}\|^{2}\langle X,\Pi^{\sharp}X\rangle\langle\Pi X,\Pi^{\sharp}X\rangle,

(24)

when
m=1
and
p=1
. If term
\langle X,\Pi^{\sharp}X\rangle
is positive, then the maximum value of (
24
) is achieved when
\Pi=\Pi^{\sharp}
.
Such warm start computational scheme is presented in Algorithm
2

and maximum likelihood estimation scheme is given in Algorithm
3
.

Algorithm 2

Warm start for maximum likelihood estimation.

Input:

Response matrix
Y
and design matrix
X

Output:

A good initial permutation matrix
\Pi_{ini}
.

1. Compute the matrix
Y_{\psi}=(\psi^{\prime})^{-1}(Y+1)
.

2. Compute the matrix
C=Y_{\psi}Y_{\psi}^{T}XX^{T}
.

3. Solve
\Pi_{ini}:=\arg\max_{\Pi}\langle\Pi,C\rangle
.

4. Return
\Pi_{ini}
as the initial
\Pi
.

Algorithm 3

Maximum likelihood (ML) estimation.

Input:
Response matrix
Y
, design matrix
X
and initial permutation matrix
\Pi_{ini}
.

Output:
Estimated permutation matrix
\hat{\Pi}
and estimated coefficient matrix
\hat{B}
.

Let
\hat{\Pi}=\Pi_{ini}
.

while
the likelihood is not converged
do

1. Solve
\hat{B}:=\arg\max_{B}\{\langle-\psi(\hat{\Pi}XB)+Y\circ\hat{\Pi}XB\rangle\}
.

2. Solve
\hat{\Pi}:=\arg\max_{\Pi}\{\langle-\psi(\Pi X\hat{B})+Y\circ\Pi X\hat{B}\rangle\}
.

end

while

Return
\hat{B}
and
\hat{\Pi}
.

4.4
Remarks

The technical challenge in the generalized linear model setting lies in the facts that the second derivative of likelihood function is associated with exponential functions which are not bounded. To be more specific, the Hessian matrix can be written as

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}L(\mathbf{I},\mathbf{b})=-X^{T}DX,

with
D=\textrm{diag}(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
and
d_{i}=\psi^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b})
when
m=1
.
For example,
\psi^{\prime\prime}(x)=\exp\{x\}
is obviously unbounded for Poisson model.
Moreover, for any fixed
\Pi
, the maximizer,

\hat{\mathbf{b}}(\Pi)=\arg\max_{\mathbf{b}}L(\Pi,\mathbf{b}),

does not admit the closed form. This brings additional difficulty while there is no such issue in the standard linear models.

In
Wang et al. 2020
, their analysis only considers the case when
d(\mathbf{I},\Pi^{\sharp})
is small and
B^{\sharp}
is known. Our results include the most general cases, i.e., we have no prior knowledge of
\Pi
and
B^{\sharp}
. Therefore, we need to take special care of uniform bound over all possible permutations in our analyses.

From the computational perspective, note that both "two-step estimation" and "ML" methods require computing

\hat{\Pi}:=\arg\max_{\Pi}\{\langle-\psi(\Pi X\hat{B})+Y\circ\Pi X\hat{B}\rangle\}
. Since the number of candidates for the permutation matrix is n!, we cannot directly solve this optimization problem.
However, we can reformulate this problem into a linear assignment problem which can be solved efficiently by specialized techniques such as Hungarian algorithm
(
Kuhn 1955
)
or the Auction algorithm
(
Bertsekas and Castañón 1992
)
.
To be more specific, we define an
n
by
n
cost matrix
C=(C[i,j])
, with

C[i,j]=\langle\psi(\mathbf{x}_{i}B)-\mathbf{y}_{j}\circ(\mathbf{x}_{i}B)\rangle.

Note that permutation matrix
\Pi
has only one non-zero element "1" in each row and column. It is then equivalent to solve the assignment problem,

\min_{\tau}\sum_{i\in[n]}C[\tau(i),i],

where
\tau
is an one-to-one mapping from
[n]
to
[n]
.
Hence
\hat{\Pi}
can be solved via using Hungarian algorithm or Auction algorithm.

Compared with
Wang et al. 2020
, they adopt an
\ell_{1}
regularization framework for computing
\hat{\Pi}
and
\hat{B}
, while our method does not.
This is due to the fact that
Wang et al. 2020
assume a sparsely mismatch regime that
d(\Pi^{\sharp},\mathbf{I})
is small. That is the reason they introduce

\ell_{1}
regularizer to encourage recovering a sparse permutation. By contrast, we do not require
d(\Pi^{\sharp},\mathbf{I})
to be small in our theory.

5
Extension to Missing Observation Case

In this section, we generalize our results to the situations when data are not fully observed.
To be specific, we consider the following model

\displaystyle Y_{miss}=E\circ Y=E\circ(\Pi^{\sharp}Y^{\sharp}),

(25)

where
E
is a binary matrix such that “1” means the entry is observed and “0” means the entry is missing.
The elements in
E
are independent Bernoulli(
q
) random variables and
q

(0<q<1)
is the observation rate.

The likelihood function with missing observations can be written as

\displaystyle L(\Pi,B,E)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\sum_{i,l:E[i,l]=1}Y[i,l](\mathbf{x}_{\Pi(i)}^{T}\mathbf{b}_{l})-\psi(\mathbf{x}_{\Pi(i)}^{T}\mathbf{b}_{l})\}

(26)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle E\circ(Y\circ(\Pi XB)-\psi(\Pi XB))\rangle

and its expectation can be computed as

\displaystyle\Lambda(\Pi,B,q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\{\sum_{i,l:E[i,l]=1}Y[i,l](\mathbf{x}_{\Pi(i)}^{T}\mathbf{b}_{l})-\psi(\mathbf{x}_{\Pi(i)}^{T}\mathbf{b}_{l})\}

(27)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q\cdot\Lambda(\Pi,B),

where the expectation is taken over both
E
and
Y
. We also define

\Lambda(\Pi,q):=\max_{B}\Lambda(\Pi,B,q)=q\cdot\Lambda(\Pi).

5.1
When
B^{\sharp}
is known

In this scenario, we can similarly define the following terms, the row-wise information gap,

\displaystyle\Delta_{ij}(q)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sum_{l}\mathbf{1}\{E[\Pi^{\sharp}(i),l]=1\}\{(\psi^{\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{\sharp}[l])\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{\sharp}[l]-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{\sharp}[l]))-(\psi^{\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{\sharp}[l])\bm{\lambda}_{j}^{\sharp}[l]-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{j}^{\sharp}[l]))\}

(28)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q\sum_{l}\{(\psi^{\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{\sharp}[l])\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{\sharp}[l]-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{\sharp}[l]))-(\psi^{\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{\sharp}[l])\bm{\lambda}_{j}^{\sharp}[l]-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{j}^{\sharp}[l]))\}=q\Delta_{ij}

and the row-wise variance,

\displaystyle v_{ij}(q)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle q\sum_{l=1}^{m}\psi^{\prime\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{\sharp}[l])(\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{\sharp}[l]-\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{\sharp}[l])^{2}

(29)

\displaystyle+~q(1-q)\sum_{l=1}^{m}(\psi^{\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{\sharp}[l])(\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{\sharp}[l]-\bm{\lambda}_{j}^{\sharp}[l])-(\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{\sharp}[l])-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{j}^{\sharp}[l])))^{2},

which is the variance of
\mathrm{var}(\langle E[\Pi^{\sharp}(i),:]\circ(\mathbf{y}_{i}\circ\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{\sharp}-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{\sharp}))\rangle-\langle E[\Pi^{\sharp}(i),:]\circ(\mathbf{y}_{i}\circ\bm{\lambda}_{j}^{\sharp}-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{j}^{\sharp}))\rangle)
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Assume
B^{\sharp}
is known and suppose
X
,
B^{\sharp}
,
\Pi^{\sharp}
satisfy that

\displaystyle\Delta_{ij}(q)\gtrsim\sqrt{(\log n)v_{ij}(q)}~~\forall i,j\in[n],

(30)

then it holds that

\displaystyle P(\hat{\Pi}\neq\Pi^{\sharp})\leq n^{2}\max_{i\neq j}\exp\{-\frac{\Delta_{ij}^{2}(q)}{16v_{ij}(q)}\}.

(31)

Remark 5.1
.

Especially when
\lambda_{il}^{\sharp}
’s are bounded and
\min_{i,j}\sum_{l\in[m]}(\lambda_{il}^{\sharp}-\lambda_{jl}^{\sharp})^{2}=\Omega(m)
, then
q\geq\frac{\log n}{m}
is required for the perfect permutation recovery. In other words, the number of required observations (
m
) for each individual is reciprocal to observation rate (
q
).

5.2
When
B
is unknown and
d(\mathbf{I},\Pi^{\sharp})
is small

Under this scenario, we further define the partial variance term

\displaystyle v_{\Pi,partial,q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q\sum_{i:\Pi(i)\neq\Pi^{\sharp}(i)}\sum_{l=1}^{m}\bigg\{\psi^{\prime\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{\sharp}[l])(\bm{\lambda}_{\Pi(i)}[l]^{\sharp}-\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l]^{\sharp})^{2}

\displaystyle+q(1-q)(\psi^{\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l]^{\sharp})(\bm{\lambda}_{\Pi(i)}[l]^{\sharp}-\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l]^{\sharp})-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{\Pi(i)}^{\sharp}[l])+\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l]^{\sharp}))^{2}\bigg\}

and minimum variance gap

v_{min,q}=\min_{i,j}\{q\sum_{l=1}^{m}\psi^{\prime\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{\sharp}[l])(\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l]^{\sharp}-\bm{\lambda}_{j}[l]^{\sharp})^{2}+q(1-q)\sum_{l=1}^{m}(\psi^{\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{\sharp}[l])(\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l]^{\sharp}-\bm{\lambda}_{j}[l]^{\sharp})-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l]^{\sharp})+\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{j}[l]^{\sharp}))^{2}\}.

We also assume the following assumptions on design matrix.

E1
Entries of
X
are bounded by some constant
C_{0}
.

E2
Let
\mathcal{S}_{l}=\{i:E[i,l]=1\}
(
l=1,\ldots,m
). There exist constants
c_{2}>0
and
\gamma_{2p}
such that

\sharp\{i:\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b}\geq c_{2},i\in\mathcal{S}_{l}\}\geq|\mathcal{S}_{l}|/\gamma_{2p}

and

\sharp\{i:\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b}\leq-c_{2},i\in\mathcal{S}_{l}\}\geq|\mathcal{S}_{l}|/\gamma_{2p}
hold for any
\mathbf{b}
with
\|\mathbf{b}\|=1
.

Remark 5.2
.

Assumptions E1 and E2 are parallel to A1 and A2.
They put the restrictions on sub-design matrices
X[\mathcal{S}_{l},:]
’s.
In particular, E2 holds by taking
\gamma_{2p}=\Theta(1)
with high probability, when each entry of
X
follows i.i.d. standard Normal distribution and
qn\gtrsim p
.

Theorem 5.2
.

With the knowledge that
d(\mathbf{I},\Pi^{\sharp})\leq h_{max}
and assumptions A0, E1 and E2, we assume
p=O((qn)^{a})
(
a<\frac{1}{2}
) and

5.4
ML Estimator with Missing Observations

The warm-start stage of ML estimation algorithm is modified in the missing observation setting. In particular, we use SoftImpute
(
Hastie et al. 2015
)
method to impute the missing entries of the data matrix. The procedure is given as below in Algorithm
4
.

Algorithm 4

ML estimation with warm start for missing observations.

Input:

Observations with missing entries
Y_{miss}
, design matrix
X

Output:

A good initial permutation matrix
\Pi_{ini}
.

1. Compute the matrix
Y_{\psi,miss}=(\psi^{\prime})^{-1}(Y_{miss}+1)
.

2. Use SoftImpute method to complete the matrix,
Y_{\psi,miss}
, to get
Y_{\psi,comp}
.

3. Compute the matrix
C=Y_{\psi,comp}Y_{\psi,comp}^{T}XX^{T}
.

4. Solve
\Pi_{ini}:=\arg\max_{\Pi}\langle\Pi,C\rangle
.

5. Set
\hat{\Pi}=\Pi_{ini}
as the initial permutation matrix.

while
The likelihood not converged
do

6.a. Solve
\hat{B}:=\arg\max_{B}\{\langle-E\circ\psi(\hat{\Pi}XB)+E\circ Y_{miss}\circ\hat{\Pi}XB\rangle\}
.

6.b. Solve
\hat{\Pi}:=\arg\max_{\Pi}\{\langle-E\circ\psi(\Pi X\hat{B})+E\circ Y_{miss}\circ\Pi X\hat{B}\rangle\}
.

end

while

7. Return
\hat{B}
and
\hat{\Pi}
.

Algorithm 5

7
Conclusion

In this paper, we provide theoretical analyses of label permutation problem for the generalized linear model.
The theory takes multivariate responses into account and is established under three different scenarios, with knowledge of
B^{\sharp}
, with knowledge of
d(\mathbf{I},\Pi^{\sharp})
and without any knowledge.
Our results are more general and remove the stringent conditions which are required by the case when
m=1
.
A detailed comparison with existing literature are also provided to emphasize the technical challenges of considered setting.
We further extend our results to the missing observation setting which has never been considered in the literature of label permutation problem.
We also propose two computational methods, “maximum likelihood estimation” algorithm with warm start and “two-step estimation” algorithm.
When the proportion of permuted labels is not too large, both methods work effectively under different settings of generating design matrix
X
.
Experimental results match our theoretical findings.
In practice,
our computational methods sometimes may fail to find the global optimum when the proportion of permuted labels is large. Developing more efficient estimation methods constitutes a further promising direction.

\bm{\lambda}_{i}
, we thus have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\langle\mathbf{y}_{i}\circ\bm{\lambda}_{i}-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{i})\rangle

(42)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\psi^{\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i})\circ\bm{\lambda}_{i}-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{i})\rangle.

It can be checked that

\displaystyle\Delta_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\psi^{\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i})\circ\bm{\lambda}_{i}-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{i})\rangle-\langle\psi^{\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i})\circ\bm{\lambda}_{j}-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{j})\rangle\geq 0

(43)

for any convex function
\psi
.

For any
i\neq j
, we next calculate the variance of
\langle\mathbf{y}_{i}\circ\bm{\lambda}_{i}-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{i})\rangle-\langle\mathbf{y}_{i}\circ\bm{\lambda}_{j}-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{j})\rangle
.

\displaystyle\mathrm{var}(\langle\mathbf{y}_{i}\circ\bm{\lambda}_{i}-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{i})\rangle-\langle\mathbf{y}_{i}\circ\bm{\lambda}_{j}-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{j})\rangle)

(44)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{m}\mathrm{var}(Y[i,l](\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l]-\bm{\lambda}_{j}[l]))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{m}\psi^{\prime\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l])(\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l]-\bm{\lambda}_{j}[l])^{2}=:v_{ij}.

To characterize the difference between
\Delta_{ij}
and
\langle Y[i,:]\circ\bm{\lambda}_{i}-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{i})\rangle-\langle Y[i,:]\circ\bm{\lambda}_{j}-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{j})\rangle
, we can show that

\displaystyle P(|\langle\mathbf{y}_{i}\circ\bm{\lambda}_{i}-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{i})\rangle-\langle\mathbf{y}_{i}\circ\bm{\lambda}_{j}-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{j})\rangle-\Delta_{ij}|\geq v_{ij}x)

(45)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P(|\langle(\mathbf{y}_{i}-\psi^{\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i}))\circ(\bm{\lambda}_{i}-\bm{\lambda}_{j})\rangle|\geq v_{ij}x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\{-tv_{ij}x\}\exp\{v_{ij}t^{2}\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\{-v_{ij}x^{2}/4\},

(46)

where (
45
) utilizes the property of moment generating function of generalized linear model. That is,
it is well known that

\mathbb{E}[\exp\{tY\}]=\exp\{\psi(\lambda+t)-\psi(\lambda)\}
,
where the density of
Y
is proportional to

\exp\{y\lambda-\psi(\lambda)\}
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\exp\{t\langle(\mathbf{y}_{i}-\psi^{\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i}))\circ(\bm{\lambda}_{i}-\bm{\lambda}_{j})\rangle\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{l=1}^{m}\exp\{\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l]+t(\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l]-\bm{\lambda}_{j}[l]))-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l])-t\psi^{\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l])(\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l]-\bm{\lambda}_{j}[l])\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\prod_{l=1}^{m}\exp\{\psi^{\prime\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l])(\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l]-\bm{\lambda}_{j}[l])^{2}t^{2}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\{t^{2}\sum_{l=1}^{m}\psi^{\prime\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l])(\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l]-\bm{\lambda}_{j}[l])^{2}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\{v_{ij}t^{2}\}

for any small
t
satisfying

\frac{1}{2}\psi^{\prime\prime}(\lambda_{i}[l])>|\psi^{\prime\prime\prime}(\lambda_{i}[l])(\lambda_{i}[l]-\lambda_{j}[l])t|
.

By taking
x=\Delta_{ij}/2v_{ij}
, yhis gives

\displaystyle P(\langle-\psi((XB)[j,:])+(Y[i,:]\circ(XB)[j,:])\rangle\geq\langle-\psi((XB)[i,:])+(Y\circ XB)[i,:]\rangle)

(47)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle P(\langle-\psi((XB)[j,:])+(Y[i,:]\circ(XB)[j,:])\rangle\geq\langle-\psi((XB)[i,:])+(Y\circ XB)[i,:]\rangle-\frac{\Delta_{ij}}{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\{-\Delta_{ij}^{2}/(16v_{ij})\}.

Finally, by union bound over all possible pairs of
i
and
j
, this completes the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Appendix D
Proof of Results with Knowledge that
d(\mathbf{I},\Pi^{\sharp})
is Small: Theorem
4.1

In this section, we prove the results when we have the prior knowledge that

d(\mathbf{I},\Pi^{\sharp})
is small.
In order to prove the recovery consistency, we need to control the following quantities,

\|B-B^{\sharp}\|_{F}

and

(L(\mathbf{I},B)-L(\Pi,B))-(\Lambda(\mathbf{I},B)-\Lambda(\Pi,B))
.

Suppose we have already known that the estimator
\hat{B}
which is close to the truth
B^{\sharp}
, i.e., in the
\delta
-neighborhood of
B^{\sharp}
.
Then we can show that

\displaystyle P(\sup_{B\in B_{\delta}(B^{\sharp})}|(L(\mathbf{I},B)-L(\Pi,B))-(\Lambda(\mathbf{I},B)-\Lambda(\Pi,B))|\geq 2v_{\Pi,partial}x)

(48)

vanishes for any fixed
\Pi
, where
B_{\delta}(B^{\sharp}):=\{B:\|\mathbf{b}_{l}-\mathbf{b}_{l}^{\sharp}\|_{2}\leq\delta,~l\in[m]\}
and
\delta
is a sufficiently small constant which is determined later.
Therefore,

\displaystyle P(\sup_{B\in B_{\delta}(B^{\sharp})}|(L(\mathbf{I},B)-L(\Pi,B))-(\Lambda(\mathbf{I},B)-\Lambda(\Pi,B))|\geq 2v_{\Pi,partial}x)

(49)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle P(|(L(\mathbf{I},B^{\sharp})-L(\Pi,B^{\sharp}))-(\Lambda(\mathbf{I},B^{\sharp})-\Lambda(\Pi,B^{\sharp}))|\geq v_{\Pi,partial}x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\{-\frac{1}{4}v_{\Pi,partial}x^{2}\}.

Hence, we get

\displaystyle P(\max_{\Pi}\sup_{B\in B_{\delta}(B^{\sharp})}|(L(\mathbf{I},B)-L(\Pi,B))-(\Lambda(\mathbf{I},B)-\Lambda(\Pi,B))|\geq 2v_{\Pi,partial}x)

(50)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\Pi}P(|(L(\mathbf{I},B^{\sharp})-L(\Pi,B^{\sharp}))-(\Lambda(\mathbf{I},B^{\sharp})-\Lambda(\Pi,B^{\sharp}))|\geq v_{\Pi,partial}x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{h}\sum_{\Pi:d(\Pi,\mathbf{I})=h}\cdot\exp\{-v_{\Pi,partial}x^{2}/4\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{h}n!/(n-h)!\cdot\exp\{-v_{\Pi,partial}x^{2}/4\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{h}n^{h}\cdot\exp\{-hv_{min}x^{2}/4\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{h}\exp\{-h(v_{min}x^{2}-\log n)\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\exp\{-2(v_{min}x^{2}-\log n)\}}{1-\exp\{-(v_{min}x^{2}-\log n)\}},

where
v_{min}:=\min_{i,j}\sum_{l}\bm{\lambda}_{il}^{\sharp}\log^{2}(\bm{\lambda}_{jl}^{\sharp}/\bm{\lambda}_{il}^{\sharp})
and we use the fact that
\sum_{\Pi}=\sum_{h}\sum_{\Pi:d(\Pi,\mathbf{I})=h}

and
|\{\Pi:d(\Pi,\mathbf{I})=h\}|\leq n!/(n-h)!\leq n^{h}
.

By (
50
), we know that
L(\Pi,\hat{B})\leq L(\mathbf{I},\hat{B})-\Lambda(\mathbf{I},\hat{B})+\Lambda(\Pi,\hat{B})+O_{p}(xv_{\Pi,partial})
with
x=\sqrt{\log n/v_{min}}
.
This tells us that if we can show that

\displaystyle\Lambda(\mathbf{I},\hat{B})-\Lambda(\Pi,\hat{B})-O_{p}(xv_{\Pi,partial})>0

(51)

for any
\Pi
with
d(\mathbf{I},\Pi^{\sharp})\leq h_{max}
.
Then we can conclude that
\hat{\Pi}=\mathbf{I}
which gives the desired result.

D.1
First bound of
\|B-B^{\sharp}\|_{F}

Since the likelihood function can be written as the sum of separate functions of
\mathbf{b}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{b}_{m}
, we only need to focus on single
\mathbf{b}_{l}
for
l\in[m]
.
Without loss of generality, we can assume
m=1
. Then the estimator
\hat{\mathbf{b}}
is

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{b}}=\arg\max_{\mathbf{b}}\{\langle-\psi(X\mathbf{b})+Y\circ X\mathbf{b}\rangle\}.

(52)

When
d(\mathbf{I},\Pi^{\sharp})\leq h_{max}
, we aim to show
\hat{\mathbf{b}}
is a consistent estimator of
\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}
.

For simplicity, we can assume
\Pi^{\sharp}(i)=i
for
i>h_{max}

and let
L(\mathbf{b})=\langle-\psi(X\mathbf{b})+Y\circ X\mathbf{b}\rangle
.
In the following, we aim to find a
\delta_{n}
such that
for any
\mathbf{b}
with
\|\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}\|\geq\delta_{n}
, it holds

L(\mathbf{b})<L(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})
.
By the definition that

L(\hat{\mathbf{b}})\geq L(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})
, we will arrive at
\|\hat{\mathbf{b}}-\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}\|\leq\delta_{n}
.

By computation, we can see

\displaystyle L(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})-L(\mathbf{b})

(53)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(L(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})-\Lambda(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}))-(L(\mathbf{b})-\Lambda(\mathbf{b}))+\Lambda(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})-\Lambda(\mathbf{b})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(L(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})-\Lambda(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}))-(L(\mathbf{b})-\Lambda(\mathbf{b}))+\Lambda_{1}(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})-\Lambda_{1}(\mathbf{b})+\Lambda_{2}(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})-\Lambda_{2}(\mathbf{b})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-O_{p}(\sqrt{v_{b^{\sharp}}})-O_{p}(\sqrt{v_{b}})-h_{max}C_{max}+\sigma_{min}(H)\delta_{n}^{2}.

(54)

For the last inequality, we use the following facts,
L(\mathbf{b})-\Lambda(\mathbf{b})=O_{p}(\sqrt{v_{b}})
with
v_{b}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\psi^{\prime\prime}(\lambda_{i}^{\sharp})(\lambda_{i}(\mathbf{b}))^{2}
for any
\mathbf{b}
. In addition,

\displaystyle|\Lambda_{1}(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})-\Lambda_{1}(\mathbf{b})|

(55)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\sum_{i\leq h_{max}}\{-\psi(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})+\psi^{\prime}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}-(-\psi(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b})+\psi^{\prime}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b})\}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i\leq h_{max}}|\lambda_{max}(\delta_{n})|+\psi^{\prime}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})|\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}-\mathbf{b})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2h_{max}\psi_{max}(\delta_{n})+h_{max}\psi_{max}^{{}^{\prime}\sharp}\max_{i,\mathbf{b}}|\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}-\mathbf{b})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{max}C_{max},

where
C_{max}:=2\psi_{max}(\delta_{n})+\psi_{max}^{{}^{\prime}\sharp}\max_{i,\mathbf{b}}|\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}-\mathbf{b})|
,
\psi_{max}(\delta)=\max_{i,\mathbf{b}:\|\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}\|\leq\delta_{n}}\psi(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b})
and

\psi_{max}^{{}^{\prime}\sharp}=\max_{i}\psi^{\prime}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})
.
Moreover, via using the fact that
f(y)=f(x)+f^{\prime}(x)(y-x)+\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime\prime}(z)(y-z)^{2}
(
z
is some point between
x
and
y
)for any smooth function
f
, we have

\displaystyle\Lambda_{2}(\mathbf{b})-\Lambda_{2}(\mathbf{b}^{{}^{\prime}})

(56)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i>h_{max}}\{-\psi(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b})+\psi^{\prime}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b})\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b}-(-\psi(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b}^{{}^{\prime}})+\psi^{\prime}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b})\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b}^{{}^{\prime}})\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i>h_{max}}(\psi^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\tilde{\mathbf{b}})(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b}^{{}^{\prime}}))^{2}/2

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{b}^{{}^{\prime}})^{T}\sum_{i\geq h_{max}}\psi^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\tilde{\mathbf{b}})\{\mathbf{x}_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\}(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{b}^{{}^{\prime}})/2

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{b}^{{}^{\prime}})^{T}H(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{b}^{{}^{\prime}})/2,

where
\tilde{\mathbf{b}}
is between
\mathbf{b}
and
\mathbf{b}^{{}^{\prime}}
, and

\displaystyle H=\sum_{i>h_{max}}\psi^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\tilde{\mathbf{b}})\mathbf{x}_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}.

(57)

Again we know that

(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})^{T}H(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{b}^{{}^{\prime}})\geq c_{1}(n-h_{max})\psi_{min}^{\sharp}/{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\gamma_{1p}}\|\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}\|^{2}

holds for some constant
c_{1}
by using the curvature technique (see (
87
)).
Therefore, we can specifically take

\delta_{n}^{2}=C{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\gamma_{1p}}\frac{\sqrt{v_{b^{\sharp}}}+\psi_{max}^{\sharp}h_{max}}{(n-h_{max})\psi_{min}^{\sharp}}

with some large constant
C
. With this choice of
\delta_{n}
, from (
54
), we can check that

L(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})-L(\mathbf{b})>0

for any
\mathbf{b}
with
\|\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}\|=\delta_{n}
when
p=O(n^{a})
(
a<\frac{1}{2}
). By the concavity of likelihood function, we then know that the two-step estimator
\hat{\mathbf{b}}
must lie in the ball
\{\mathbf{b}:\|{\mathbf{b}}-\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}\|\leq\delta_{n}\}
.

D.2
Second bound of
\|B-B^{\sharp}\|_{F}

We consider the Taylor expansion of
L(\mathbf{b})
at value
\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}
. Then, it can be computed that

\displaystyle\mathbf{0}=\nabla L(\hat{\mathbf{b}})=\nabla L(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})+\nabla^{2}L(\bar{\mathbf{b}})(\hat{\mathbf{b}}-\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})

(58)

where
\bar{\mathbf{b}}
is some point between
\hat{\mathbf{b}}
and
\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}
.
By the formula
\nabla^{2}L(\mathbf{b})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\psi^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b})\mathbf{x}_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}
, we then know that

\displaystyle|\nabla^{2}L(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})-\nabla^{2}L(\bar{\mathbf{b}})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\psi^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})\mathbf{x}_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}(1-\psi^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\bar{\mathbf{b}})/\psi^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}))|

(59)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\psi^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})\cdot o_{p}(1)\cdot\mathbf{x}_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}|,

since
\|\bar{\mathbf{b}}-\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}\|\leq\|\hat{\mathbf{b}}-\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}\|=o_{p}(1)
.
Then we know that
\sigma_{min}(\nabla^{2}L(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}))\geq\sigma_{min}(\partial^{2}L(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}))/2
.
We thus have

\displaystyle\|\hat{\mathbf{b}}-\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}\|=\|(\nabla^{2}L(\bar{\mathbf{b}}))^{-1}\partial L(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})\|\leq\frac{2}{\sigma_{min}(\partial^{2}L(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}))}\|\partial L(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})\|.

(60)

For
l
-th (
l=1,\ldots,p
) element of
\nabla L(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})
, we can find that
\nabla L(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})[l]=\sum_{i\leq h_{max}}\nabla L_{i}(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})[l]+\sum_{i>h_{max}}\nabla L_{i}(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})[l]
. The first term is bounded by

b_{s}:=|\sum_{i\leq h_{max}}(Y[i]-\psi^{\prime}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}))X[i,l]\}|,

which is order of
\log n\sum_{i\leq h_{max}}(\psi_{max}^{{}^{\prime\prime}\sharp}\log n+\psi_{max}^{{}^{\prime}\sharp})|X[i,l]|
.
Here we use the observation that

\displaystyle Y[i,j]=O_{p}(\psi_{max}^{{}^{\prime\prime}\sharp}\log n+\psi_{max}^{{}^{\prime}\sharp})

(61)

for all
i\in[n],j\in[m]
.
The second term is bounded by
C\sqrt{\textrm{var}(\sum_{i>h_{max}}\nabla L_{i}(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})[l])}
and the upper bound of
\textrm{var}(\sum_{i>h_{max}}\nabla L_{i}(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})[l])
can be computed explicitly, i.e.,

v_{s}:=\max_{l\in[p]}\sum_{i>h_{max}}\psi^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})(X[i,l])^{2}.

Putting all above together, we get

\displaystyle\|\hat{\mathbf{b}}-\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{p}(\sqrt{v_{s}}+b_{s})/\sigma_{min}(\partial^{2}L(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}))=O_{p}(\frac{\sqrt{p}(\sqrt{\psi_{max}^{{}^{\prime\prime}\sharp}}\|X\|_{2,\infty}+(\psi_{max}^{{}^{\prime\prime}\sharp}\log n+\psi_{max}^{{}^{\prime}\sharp})h_{max})}{\sigma_{min}^{2}(X)})

(62)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O_{p}(\frac{\sqrt{p}(\sqrt{\psi_{max}^{{}^{\prime\prime}\sharp}}\sqrt{n-h_{max}}+(\psi_{max}^{{}^{\prime\prime}\sharp}\log n+\psi_{max}^{{}^{\prime}\sharp})h_{max})}{n\psi_{min}^{{}^{\prime\prime}\sharp}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\gamma_{1p}}):=\delta^{\ast}.

It is clear that the bound
\delta^{\ast}
is tighter than
\delta_{n}
.
Especially, when
\psi_{max}^{{}^{\prime\prime}\sharp},\psi_{min}^{{}^{\prime\prime}\sharp}
and
\psi_{max}^{{}^{\prime}\sharp}
are bounded and
\gamma_{1p}
is
O(1)
, then
\delta^{\ast}
can be simplified as
\frac{\sqrt{p}(\sqrt{n-h_{max}}+(\log n)h_{max})}{n}
.

D.3
Difference of
\Lambda(\mathbf{I},\hat{\mathbf{b}})-\Lambda(\Pi,\hat{\mathbf{b}})

By straightforward calculation, we get

\displaystyle\Lambda(\mathbf{I},\hat{\mathbf{b}})-\Lambda(\Pi,\hat{\mathbf{b}})

(63)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\Lambda(\mathbf{I},\hat{\mathbf{b}})-\Lambda(\mathbf{I},\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}))-(\Lambda(\Pi,\hat{\mathbf{b}})-\Lambda(\Pi,\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}))+(\Lambda(\mathbf{I},\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})-\Lambda(\Pi,\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}))

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-4h_{max}\lambda_{max}^{\sharp}x_{max}\delta^{\ast}+\Lambda(\mathbf{I},\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})-\Lambda(\Pi,\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}),

where the last inequality depends on the following fact

\displaystyle(\Lambda(\mathbf{I},\hat{\mathbf{b}})-\Lambda(\mathbf{I},\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}))-(\Lambda(\Pi,\hat{\mathbf{b}})-\Lambda(\Pi,\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}))

(64)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\Lambda(\mathbf{I},\hat{\mathbf{b}})-\Lambda(\Pi,\hat{\mathbf{b}}))-(\Lambda(\mathbf{I},\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})-\Lambda(\Pi,\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i\leq d(\mathbf{I},\Pi)}\bigg\{-\psi(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\hat{\mathbf{b}})+\psi^{\prime}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}{\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}})\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\hat{\mathbf{b}}+\psi(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}{\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}})-\psi^{\prime}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}{\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}})\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}{\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}}

\displaystyle+\psi(\mathbf{x}_{\Pi(i)}^{T}\hat{\mathbf{b}})-\psi^{\prime}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}{\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}})\mathbf{x}_{\Pi(i)}^{T}\hat{\mathbf{b}}-\psi(\mathbf{x}_{\Pi(i)}^{T}{\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}})+\psi^{\prime}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}{\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}})\mathbf{x}_{\Pi(i)}^{T}{\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}}\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i\leq d(\mathbf{I},\Pi)}4\psi_{max}^{{}^{\prime}\sharp}\cdot x_{max}\cdot\|\hat{\mathbf{b}}-\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}\|\leq 4d(\mathbf{I},\Pi)\psi_{max}^{{}^{\prime}\sharp}x_{max}\delta^{\ast},

where
\psi_{max}^{{}^{\prime}\sharp}=\max_{i}\psi^{\prime}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})
and
x_{max}=\max_{i}\|\mathbf{x}_{i}\|
.

By (
63
) and summing over
l\in[m]
, we have that

\displaystyle\Lambda(\mathbf{I},\hat{B})-\Lambda(\Pi,\hat{B})

(65)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-4md(\mathbf{I},\Pi)\psi_{max}^{{}^{\prime}\sharp}x_{max}\delta^{\ast}+\Lambda(\mathbf{I},B^{\sharp})-\Lambda(\Pi,B^{\sharp}),

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle xv_{\Pi,partial},

when

\displaystyle\Lambda(\mathbf{I},B^{\sharp})-\Lambda(\Pi,B^{\sharp})\gtrsim xv_{\Pi,partial}

(66)

and

\displaystyle\Lambda(\mathbf{I},B^{\sharp})-\Lambda(\Pi,B^{\sharp})\gtrsim md(\mathbf{I},\Pi)\psi_{max}^{{}^{\prime}\sharp}x_{max}\delta^{\ast}.

(67)

This completes the proof of Theorem
4.1
.

D.4
On
\Lambda(\mathbf{I},B^{\sharp})-\Lambda(\Pi,B^{\sharp})

We investigate the lower bound of
\Lambda(\mathbf{I},B^{\sharp})-\Lambda(\Pi,B^{\sharp})
.

\displaystyle\Lambda(\mathbf{I},B^{\sharp})-\Lambda(\Pi,B^{\sharp})

(68)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l\in[m]}\sum_{i\leq d(\mathbf{I},\Pi)}\bigg\{-\psi(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}{\mathbf{b}_{l}^{\sharp}})+\psi^{\prime}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}{\mathbf{b}_{l}^{\sharp}})\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}{\mathbf{b}_{l}^{\sharp}}

\displaystyle-(\psi(\mathbf{x}_{\Pi(i)}^{T}{\mathbf{b}_{l}^{\sharp}})+\psi^{\prime}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}{\mathbf{b}_{l}^{\sharp}})\mathbf{x}_{\Pi(i)}^{T}{\mathbf{b}_{l}^{\sharp}})\bigg\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{l\in[m]}\sum_{i\leq d(\mathbf{I},\Pi)}\frac{1}{2}\psi_{min}^{{}^{\prime\prime}\sharp}\big((\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}-\mathbf{x}_{\Pi(i)}^{T})\mathbf{b}_{l}^{\sharp}\big)^{2}.

Under sub-Gaussian design,
\sum_{l\in[m]}\big((\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}-\mathbf{x}_{j}^{T})\mathbf{b}_{l}^{\sharp}\big)^{2}
is
\Omega(m)
for any pair of
i,j\in[m]
when
m\gtrsim\log n
and
p\gtrsim\log n
.
Thus,
\Lambda(\mathbf{I},B^{\sharp})-\Lambda(\Pi,B^{\sharp})
is
\Omega(md(\mathbf{I},\Pi))
.

Appendix E
Proof of Results without any knowledge of
B^{\sharp}
and
\Pi^{\sharp}
: Theorem
4.3

In this section, we provide the proof when we do not have any knowledge of
B^{\sharp}
and
\Pi^{\sharp}
.
For each fixed permutation
\Pi
, we define

\displaystyle v_{\Pi,B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{l=1}^{m}\psi^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{x}_{\Pi^{\sharp}(i)}^{T}\mathbf{b}_{l}^{\sharp})(\mathbf{x}_{\Pi(i)}^{T}\mathbf{b}_{l})^{2}.

(69)

It can be easily checked that
v_{\Pi,B}
is the variance of
L(\Pi,B)
.

We compute the deviation
|\langle L(\Pi,B)-\Lambda(\Pi,B)\rangle|
.
The moment generating function of
\langle L(\Pi,B)-\Lambda(\Pi,B)\rangle
is

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\exp\{t\langle L(\Pi,B)-\Lambda(\Pi,B)\rangle\}

(70)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\exp\{t\sum_{i}\langle(Y[i,:]-\psi^{\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i}))\circ\bm{\lambda}_{\pi_{i}}\rangle\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{l=1}^{m}\exp\{\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{\sharp}[l]+t\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l])-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{\sharp}[l])-t\psi^{\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{\sharp}[l])\bm{\lambda}_{\pi_{i}}[l]\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{l=1}^{m}\exp\{\psi^{\prime\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{\sharp}[l])(\bm{\lambda}_{\pi_{i}}[l])^{2}t^{2}\}

(71)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\{t^{2}(\sum_{i,l}\psi^{\prime\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{\sharp}[l])(\bm{\lambda}_{\pi_{i}}[l])^{2})\}=\exp\{v_{\Pi,B}t^{2}\}

(72)

for any small
t
satisfying

\frac{1}{2}\psi^{\prime\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{\sharp}[l])>|t\psi^{\prime\prime\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{\sharp}[l])\bm{\lambda}_{\pi_{i}}[l]|
.
Thus, we have

\sharp\{|\mathbf{x}_{i}\delta b|\leq C_{1}\epsilon\}\geq n-\frac{n}{C_{1}},

for any
\|\delta b\|\leq\epsilon
.
The size of
\epsilon
-cover is bounded by
(2/\epsilon+1)^{p}

We then have that

\displaystyle P(\sharp\{\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\bm{\beta}>c_{0}-C_{1}\epsilon\}\geq p_{0}n-nt-n/C_{1};~~\forall\bm{\beta})\leq(2/\epsilon+1)^{p}2\exp\{-\frac{2nt^{2}}{p_{0}(1-p_{0})}\}.

(110)

We than can choose
t
,
p_{0}
,
C_{1}
and
\epsilon
such that

\displaystyle p\log(2/\epsilon+1)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle 2t^{2}/(p_{0}(1-p_{0}))n

(111)

\displaystyle p_{0}n-nt-n/C_{1}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle p

(112)

\displaystyle C_{1}\epsilon

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle o(1).

(113)

Then we have that

\sharp\{i||x_{\pi_{i}}^{T}b|\geq c_{0}/2\}\geq(p_{0}-t-1/C_{1})n.

Thus, we can see that assumption

is satisfied by letting

\gamma_{1p}=p_{0}-t-1/C_{1}=\Theta(1)
.

Appendix F
Proof of Results in the Missing Case

For the purpose of completeness, we provide proof under missing observation cases.
The proof strategy is similar to that of the previous setting.

F.1
Size of
\mathcal{S}_{l}

By the definition, we know that
\mathcal{S}_{l}=\{i:E[i,l]=1\}
. Next, we give the upper and lower bound of
\mathcal{S}_{l}
.
By Bernstein inequality, we have that

\displaystyle P(|\sum E[i,l]-qn|\geq nx)\leq\exp\{-\frac{n^{2}x^{2}}{2(nq(1-q)+nx/3)}\}.

(114)

Thus

\displaystyle P(|\sum E[i,l]-qn|\geq nq/2)\leq\exp\{-\frac{3nq}{7}\}.

(115)

In other words,

\displaystyle P(qn/2\leq\min_{l}|\mathcal{S}_{l}|\leq\max_{l}|\mathcal{S}_{l}|\leq 3qn/2)\leq m\exp\{-\frac{3nq}{7}\},

(116)

which means the sizes of
S_{l}
’s are around
qn
with high probability.

F.2
When
B^{\sharp}
is known

We next calculate the variance of
\langle E[i,:]\circ(\mathbf{y}_{i}\circ\bm{\lambda}_{i}-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{i}))\rangle-\langle E[i,:]\circ(\mathbf{y}_{i}\circ\bm{\lambda}_{j}-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{j}))\rangle
.

\displaystyle\mathrm{var}(\langle E[i,:]\circ(\mathbf{y}_{i}\circ\bm{\lambda}_{i}-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{i}))\rangle-\langle E[i,:]\circ(\mathbf{y}_{i}\circ\bm{\lambda}_{j}-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{j}))\rangle)

(117)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q\sum_{l=1}^{m}\mathrm{var}(Y[i,l](\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l]-\bm{\lambda}_{j}[l])-(\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l])-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{j}[l])))

\displaystyle+q(1-q)(\sum_{l=1}^{m}\mathbb{E}[Y[i,l]](\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l]-\bm{\lambda}_{j}[l])-(\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l])-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{j}[l])))^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q\sum_{l=1}^{m}\psi^{\prime\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l])(\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l]-\bm{\lambda}_{j}[l])^{2}+q(1-q)\sum_{l=1}^{m}(\psi^{\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l])(\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l]-\bm{\lambda}_{j}[l])-(\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l])-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{j}[l])))^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l}\{qx_{ij,2}[l]+q(1-q)(x_{ij,1}[l])^{2}\}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle v_{ij}(q),

(118)

where, for simplicity, we let
x_{ij,1}[l]=\psi^{\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l])(\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l]-\bm{\lambda}_{j}[l])-(\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l])-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{j}[l]))
and
x_{ij,2}[l]=\psi^{\prime\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l])(\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l]-\bm{\lambda}_{j}[l])^{2}
.
We may also suppress subscript
i,j
in
x_{ij,1}
or
x_{ij,2}
in the following calculations.

The moment generating function of
\langle E[i,:]\circ(\mathbf{y}_{i}\circ\bm{\lambda}_{i}-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{i}))\rangle-\langle E[i,:]\circ(\mathbf{y}_{i}\circ\bm{\lambda}_{j}-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{j}))\rangle-\Delta_{ij}(q)
is

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\exp\{t(\langle E[i,:]\circ(\mathbf{y}_{i}\circ\bm{\lambda}_{i}-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{i}))\rangle-\langle E[i,:]\circ(\mathbf{y}_{i}\circ\bm{\lambda}_{j}-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{j}))\rangle-\Delta_{ij}(q))\}

(119)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{l=1}^{m}\mathbb{E}\exp\bigg\{t\bigg(E[i,l](Y[i,l](\bm{\lambda}_{j}[l]-\bm{\lambda}_{j}[l])-(\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l])-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{j}[l])))-\Delta_{ij}(q)[l]\bigg)\bigg\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{l=1}^{m}\bigg\{\big((1-q)+q\mathbb{E}\exp\{t(Y[i,l](\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l]-\bm{\lambda}_{j}[l])-(\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l])-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{j}[l])))\}\big)\exp\{-t\Delta_{ij}(q)[l]\}\bigg\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{l=1}^{m}\bigg\{\big((1-q)+q\exp\{\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l]+t(\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l]-\bm{\lambda}_{j}[l]))-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l])-t(\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l])-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{j}[l]))\}\big)\exp\{-t\Delta_{ij}(q)[l]\}\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\prod_{l=1}^{m}(1+qx_{1}[l]t+aqx_{2}[l]t^{2}+bq(x_{1}[l]t+ax_{2}[l]t^{2})^{2})(1-qx_{1}[l]t+c(qx_{1}[l]t)^{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\prod_{l=1}^{m}(1+2(aqx_{2}[l]t^{2}+bqx_{1}^{2}[l]t^{2}+cq^{2}x_{1}^{2}[l]t^{2}-q^{2}x_{1}^{2}[l]t^{2}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\prod_{l=1}^{m}(1+2(aqx_{2}[l]t^{2}+bq(1-q)x_{1}^{2}[l]t^{2}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\prod_{l=1}^{m}\exp\{2(qx_{2}[l]+q(1-q)x_{1}^{2}[l])t^{2}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\{2v_{ij}(q)t^{2}\},

where we suppress symbols
x_{ij,1},x_{ij,2}
to
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
respectively. We also use the Taylor expansion for multiple times in the above inequalities which depend on the following fact,

\exp\{x\}\leq 1+x+(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{5}x)x^{2}

for any
|x|<0.5
.
In (
119
), we specifically take
a=1
,
c=1-b
,
b=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{10}(qx_{1}t+qax_{2}t^{2})<1
.
This choice is possible and (
119
) holds for any
t\lesssim\min\{1/\bm{\lambda}_{j}[l],1/(qx_{1}[l]),1/\sqrt{qx_{2}[l]}\}
for any
l
.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle P(|\langle E[i,:]\circ(\mathbf{y}_{i}\circ\bm{\lambda}_{i}-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{i}))\rangle-\langle E[i,:]\circ(\mathbf{y}_{i}\circ\bm{\lambda}_{j}-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{j}))\rangle-\Delta_{ij}(q)|\geq v_{ij}(q)x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\inf_{t}\exp\{2v_{ij}(q)t^{2}\}\exp\{-v_{ij}(q)xt\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\{-\frac{1}{8}v_{ij}(q)x^{2}\}

as long as
x\lesssim 4\min_{l}\{1/\bm{\lambda}_{j}[l],1/(qx_{1}[l]),1/\sqrt{qx_{2}[l]}\}
.

Similar to no missing observation case, we can obtain that the requirement for permutation recovery is

\displaystyle\Delta_{ij}(q)\gtrsim\sqrt{(\log n)v_{ij}(q)}.

That is,

\Delta_{ij}(q)^{2}\gtrsim q\sum_{l=1}^{m}\psi^{\prime\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l])(\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l]-\bm{\lambda}_{j}[l])^{2}+q(1-q)\sum_{l=1}^{m}(\psi^{\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l])(\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l]-\bm{\lambda}_{j}[l])-(\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{i}[l])-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{j}[l])))^{2}.

Especially, when
\lambda_{il}^{\sharp}
’s are bounded and
\min_{i,j}\sum_{l\in[m]}(\lambda_{il}^{\sharp}-\lambda_{jl}^{\sharp})^{2}=\Omega(m)
,
the above inequality becomes

q^{2}m^{2}\gtrsim\log n(qm+q(1-q)m).

Thus
q\geq\frac{\log n}{m}
is required for the perfect permutation recovery.

F.3
With knowledge that
d(\mathbf{I},\Pi^{\sharp})
is small

Again, in order to prove the recovery consistency, we need to control the following quantities,

\|B-B^{\sharp}\|

and

(L(\mathbf{I},B,E)-L(\Pi,B,E))-(\Lambda(\mathbf{I},B,q)-\Lambda(\Pi,B,q))
.

Suppose we have already known that the estimator
\hat{B}
which is close to the truth
B^{\sharp}
, i.e., in the
\delta
-neighborhood of
B^{\sharp}
.
Then we let
B_{\delta}(B^{\sharp}):=\{B:\|B-B^{\sharp}\|_{2}\leq\delta\}
and
\delta
is a sufficiently small constant which will be determined later.
For any fixed
\Pi
, we have

\displaystyle P(\sup_{\mathbf{B}\in B_{\delta}(B^{\sharp})}|(L(\mathbf{I},B,E)-L(\Pi,B,E))-(\Lambda(\mathbf{I},B,q)-\Lambda(\Pi,B,q))|\geq 2v_{\Pi,partial,q}x)

(120)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle P(|(L(\mathbf{I},B^{\sharp},E)-L(\Pi,B^{\sharp},E))-(\Lambda(\mathbf{I},B^{\sharp},q)-\Lambda(\Pi,B^{\sharp},q))|\geq v_{\Pi,partial,q}x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\{-\frac{1}{4}v_{\Pi,partial,q}x^{2}\}.

Hence, we get

\displaystyle P(\max_{\Pi}\sup_{B\in B_{\delta}(B^{\sharp})}|(L(\mathbf{I},B,E)-L(\Pi,B,E))-(\Lambda(\mathbf{I},B,q)-\Lambda(\Pi,B,q))|\geq 2v_{\Pi,partial,q}x)

(121)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\Pi}P(|(L(\mathbf{I},B^{\sharp})-L(\Pi,B^{\sharp}))-(\Lambda(\mathbf{I},B^{\sharp})-\Lambda(\Pi,B^{\sharp}))|\geq v_{\Pi,partial,q}x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{h}\sum_{\Pi:d(\Pi,\mathbf{I})=h}\cdot\exp\{-v_{\Pi,partial,q}x^{2}/4\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{h}n!/(n-h)!\cdot\exp\{-v_{\Pi,partial,q}x^{2}/4\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{h}n^{h}\cdot\exp\{-hv_{min,q}x^{2}/4\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{h}\exp\{-h(v_{min,q}x^{2}-\log n)\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\exp\{-2(v_{min,q}x^{2}-\log n)\}}{1-\exp\{-(v_{min,q}x^{2}-\log n)\}},

where we recall that
v_{min,q}=\min_{i,j}\sum_{l}\bigg\{q\psi^{\prime\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{\sharp}[l])(\bm{\lambda}_{j}^{\sharp}[l]-\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{\sharp}[l])^{2}+q(1-q)(\psi^{\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{\sharp}[l])(\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{\sharp}[l]-\bm{\lambda}_{j}^{\sharp}[l])-(\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{\sharp}[l])-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{k}^{\sharp}[l])))^{2}\bigg\}
.

By (
121
), we know that
L(\Pi,\hat{B},E)\leq L(\mathbf{I},\hat{B},E)-\Lambda(\mathbf{I},\hat{B},q)+\Lambda(\Pi,\hat{B},q)+O_{p}(xv_{\Pi,B^{\sharp},q})
with
x=\sqrt{\log n/v_{min}}
.
This tells us that if we can show that

\displaystyle\Lambda(\mathbf{I},\hat{B},q)-\Lambda(\Pi,\hat{B},q)-O_{p}(xv_{\Pi,B^{\sharp},q})>0.

(122)

Then we can conclude that
\hat{\Pi}=\mathbf{I}
which leads to the desired result.

F.3.1
First bound of
\|B-B^{\sharp}\|_{F}

Again, without loss of generality, we first assume
m=1
. Then the estimator
\hat{\mathbf{b}}
is

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{b}}=\arg\max_{\mathbf{b}}\{\langle E\circ(-\psi(X\mathbf{b})+Y\circ X\mathbf{b})\rangle\}.

(123)

When
d(\mathbf{I},\Pi^{\sharp})\leq h_{max}
, we aim to show
\hat{\mathbf{b}}
is a consistent estimator of
\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}
.
For simplicity, we assume
\Pi^{\sharp}(i)=i
for
i>h_{max}
and let
L(\mathbf{b})=\langle E\circ(-\psi(X\mathbf{b})+Y\circ X\mathbf{b})\rangle
.
In the following, we construct a
\delta_{n}
such that
for any
\mathbf{b}
with
\|\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}\|\geq\delta_{n}
, it holds

L(\mathbf{b})<L(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})
.
By the definition that

L(\hat{\mathbf{b}})\geq L(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})
, we will arrive at
\|\hat{\mathbf{b}}-\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}\|\leq\delta_{n}
.

We compute that

\displaystyle L(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})-L(\mathbf{b})

(124)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(L(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})-\Lambda(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp},q))-(L(\mathbf{b})-\Lambda(\mathbf{b},q))+\Lambda(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp},q)-\Lambda(\mathbf{b},q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(L(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})-\Lambda(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp},q))-(L(\mathbf{b})-\Lambda(\mathbf{b},q))+\Lambda_{1}(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp},q)-\Lambda_{1}(\mathbf{b},q)+\Lambda_{2}(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp},q)-\Lambda_{2}(\mathbf{b},q)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-O_{p}(\sqrt{v_{b^{\sharp},q}})-O_{p}(\sqrt{v_{b,q}})-qh_{max}C_{max}+\sigma_{min}(H_{q})\delta_{n}^{2}.

For the last inequality, we use the following facts. Note that
L(\mathbf{b})-\Lambda(\mathbf{b},q)=O_{p}(\sqrt{v_{b,q}})
with

v_{b,q}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\{q\psi^{\prime\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{\sharp})(\bm{\lambda}_{i}(\mathbf{b}))^{2}+q(1-q)\big(\psi^{\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{\sharp})\bm{\lambda}_{i}(\mathbf{b})-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{i}(\mathbf{b}))\big)\}

for any
\mathbf{b}
. Additionally note that

\displaystyle|\Lambda_{1}(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp},q)-\Lambda_{1}(\mathbf{b},q)|

(125)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q|\sum_{i\leq h_{max}}\{-\psi(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})+\psi^{\prime}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}-(-\psi(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b})+\psi^{\prime}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b})\}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle q\sum_{i\leq h_{max}}|\psi_{max}(\delta_{n})|+q\psi^{\prime}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})|\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}-\mathbf{b})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle qh_{max}\psi_{max}(\delta_{n})+qh_{max}\psi_{max}^{{}^{\prime}\sharp}\max_{i,\mathbf{b}}|\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}-\mathbf{b})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle qh_{max}C_{max},

where
C_{max}=\psi_{max}(\delta_{n})+\psi_{max}^{{}^{\prime}\sharp}\max_{i,\mathbf{b}}|\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}-\mathbf{b})|
.
Moreover, for any
\mathbf{b}
and
\mathbf{b}^{{}^{\prime}}
, we compute

\displaystyle\Lambda_{2}(\mathbf{b},q)-\Lambda_{2}(\mathbf{b}^{{}^{\prime}},q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q\sum_{i>h_{max}}\{-\psi(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b})+\psi^{\prime}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b})\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b}-(-\psi(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b}^{{}^{\prime}})+\psi^{\prime}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b})\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b}^{{}^{\prime}})\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q\sum_{i>h_{max}}\psi^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\tilde{\mathbf{b}})(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b}^{{}^{\prime}})^{2}/2

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{b}^{{}^{\prime}})^{T}\sum_{i\geq h_{max}}\psi^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\tilde{\mathbf{b}})\mathbf{x}_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{b}^{{}^{\prime}})/2

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{b}^{{}^{\prime}})^{T}H_{q}(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{b}^{{}^{\prime}})/2,

where
\tilde{\mathbf{b}}
is between
\mathbf{b}
and
\mathbf{b}^{{}^{\prime}}
, and

\displaystyle H_{q}=q\sum_{i>h_{max}}\psi^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\tilde{\mathbf{b}})\mathbf{x}_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}.

Again by curvature technique, we know that

(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})^{T}H_{q}(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})\geq c_{1}q(n-h_{max})\psi_{min}^{\sharp}/{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\gamma_{2p}}\|\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}\|^{2}

for some constant
c_{1}
.
Specifically, we can take

\delta_{n}^{2}=C\frac{\sqrt{v_{b^{\sharp},q}}+q\lambda_{max}^{\sharp}h_{max}}{q(n-h_{max})\lambda_{min}^{\sharp}/{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\gamma_{2p}}}

with some large constant
C
. With this choice of
\delta_{n}
, from (
54
), we can check that

L(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})-L(\mathbf{b})>0

holds for any
\mathbf{b}
with
\|\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}\|=\delta_{n}
when

and
ph_{max}<n/\log n
. By the concavity of likelihood function, we then know that
\|\hat{\mathbf{b}}-\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}\|\leq\delta_{n}
.

F.3.2
Second bound of
\|B-B^{\sharp}\|_{F}

We do the Taylor expansion of
L(\mathbf{b})
at
\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}
. Then, it can be computed that

\displaystyle\mathbf{0}=\nabla L(\hat{\mathbf{b}})=\nabla L(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})+\nabla^{2}L(\bar{\mathbf{b}})(\hat{\mathbf{b}}-\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})

where
\bar{\mathbf{b}}
is some point between
\hat{\mathbf{b}}
and
\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}

Again, by curvature technique under assumption
E2
, we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}L(\bar{\mathbf{b}})(\hat{\mathbf{b}}-\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})\|

(126)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle cqn\psi_{min}^{{}^{\prime\prime}\sharp}/{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\gamma_{2p}}\|\hat{\mathbf{b}}-\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}\|.

We thus have

\displaystyle\|\hat{\mathbf{b}}-\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}\|=\|(\nabla^{2}L(\bar{\mathbf{b}}))^{-1}\nabla L(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})\|\leq\frac{1}{cqn\psi_{min}^{{}^{\prime\prime}\sharp}/{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\gamma_{2p}}}\|\nabla L(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})\|.

(127)

For

-th element of
\nabla L(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})

\nabla L(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})[l]=\sum_{i\leq h_{max}:E[i]=1}\nabla L_{i}(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})[l]+\sum_{i>h_{max}:E[i]=1}\nabla L_{i}(\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})[l].

The first term is bounded by

b_{s}:=|\sum_{i\leq h_{max}}(Y[i]-\psi^{\prime}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}))X[i,l]\}|,

which is order of
(\log n)\sum_{i\leq h_{max}}\psi_{max}^{{}^{\prime\prime}\sharp}|X[i,l]|
. The second term is bounded by

and the upper bound of

v_{s,q}:=\max_{l\in[p]}\sum_{i>h_{max}}q\psi^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})(X[i,l])^{2}+q(1-q)(\psi^{\prime}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})X[i,l]-\psi(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}))^{2}.

Thus

\displaystyle\|\hat{\mathbf{b}}-\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{p}(\sqrt{v_{s,q}}+b_{s})/(cqn\psi_{min}^{{}^{\prime\prime}\sharp}/{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\gamma_{2p}})=O_{p}(\frac{\sqrt{p}(\sqrt{v_{s,q}}+\psi_{max}^{{}^{\prime\prime}\sharp}h_{max}\log n)}{qn})

(128)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O_{p}(\frac{\sqrt{p}(\sqrt{q\psi^{{}^{\prime\prime}\sharp}_{max}+q(1-q)\psi^{\sharp 2}_{cb}}\sqrt{n-h_{max}}+\psi_{max}^{{}^{\prime\prime}\sharp}h_{max}\log n)}{qn\psi_{min}^{{}^{\prime\prime}\sharp}/{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\gamma_{2p}}}):=\delta_{q}^{\ast}.

Thus the bound
\delta^{\ast}
is tighter than
\delta_{n}
.
Especially, when
\mathbf{x}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}
is bounded for all
i
and
\gamma_{2p}=O(1)
, then
\delta^{\ast}
can be simplified as
\frac{\sqrt{p}(\sqrt{q(n-h_{max})}+h_{max}\log n)}{qn}
.

F.3.3
Difference of
\Lambda(\mathbf{I},\hat{\mathbf{b}},q)-\Lambda(\Pi,\hat{\mathbf{b}},q)

By straightforward calculation, we get

\displaystyle\Lambda(\mathbf{I},\hat{\mathbf{b}},q)-\Lambda(\Pi,\hat{\mathbf{b}},q)

(129)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q\bigg\{(\Lambda(\mathbf{I},\hat{\mathbf{b}})-\Lambda(\mathbf{I},\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}))-(\Lambda(\Pi,\hat{\mathbf{b}})-\Lambda(\Pi,\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}))+(\Lambda(\mathbf{I},\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})-\Lambda(\Pi,\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}))\bigg\}

\displaystyle-4qh_{max}\psi_{cb}^{\sharp}x_{max}\delta^{\ast}+q(\Lambda(\mathbf{I},\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})-\Lambda(\Pi,\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})),

By (

) and summing over

, we have that

\displaystyle\Lambda(\mathbf{I},\hat{B},q)-\Lambda(\Pi,\hat{B},q)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-4qmd(\mathbf{I},\Pi)\psi_{cb}^{\sharp}x_{max}\delta^{\ast}+q(\Lambda(\mathbf{I},\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})-\Lambda(\Pi,\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})),

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle xv_{\Pi,partial,q},

when

\displaystyle q(\Lambda(\mathbf{I},\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})-\Lambda(\Pi,\mathbf{b}^{\sharp}))\gtrsim xv_{\Pi,partial,q}

and

\displaystyle\Lambda(\mathbf{I},\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})-\Lambda(\Pi,\mathbf{b}^{\sharp})\gtrsim md(\mathbf{I},\Pi)\psi_{cb}^{\sharp}x_{max}\delta^{\ast}.

This completes the proof if we take

.

F.4
With no knowledge of
B^{\sharp}
and
\Pi^{\sharp}

We consider to compute the moment generating function of
\langle L(\Pi,B,E)-\mathbb{E}\Lambda(\Pi,B,E)\rangle

By similar procedure, we can obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\exp\{t\langle L(\Pi,B,E)-\mathbb{E}\Lambda(\Pi,B,E)\rangle\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{l=1}^{m}\mathbb{E}\exp\bigg\{t\bigg(E[i,l](Y[i,l]\bm{\lambda}_{\Pi(i)}[l]-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{\Pi(i)}[l]))-q(\psi^{\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{\sharp}[l])\bm{\lambda}_{\Pi(i)}[l]-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{\Pi(i)}[l]))\bigg)\bigg\}

\displaystyle\exp\{2v_{\Pi,B,q}t^{2}\}

for

(

is around of order

where

\displaystyle v_{\Pi,B,q}=q\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{l=1}^{m}\psi^{\prime\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{\sharp}[l])(\bm{\lambda}_{\Pi(i)}[l])^{2}+q(1-q)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{l=1}^{m}(\psi^{\prime}(\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{\sharp}[l])\bm{\lambda}_{\Pi}[l]-\psi(\bm{\lambda}_{\Pi(i)}[l]))^{2}.

Appendix G
ADMM Computational Approach

For completeness,
in this section, we discuss the computational aspects of the problem in linear models.
We relax the ML estimation problem to a bi-convex problem and solve it via an ADMM algorithm
proposed in
Zhang et al. 2019
.
A detailed description is given in the sequel.

ADMM formulation

First, we are trying to solve

\min_{\Pi,B}~\|Y-\Pi XB\|_{F}^{2}=\|P_{\Pi X}^{\perp}Y\|_{F}^{2}

(146)

where projection matrix
P_{\Pi X}^{\perp}
is defined as

I-\Pi X\left(X^{T}X\right)^{-1}X^{T}\Pi^{T}
.
Note that we can decompose
Y
as

P_{\Pi X}^{\perp}Y+P_{\Pi X}Y
. Since
\|Y\|_{F}^{2}=\|P_{\Pi X}^{\perp}Y\|^{2}+\|P_{\Pi X}Y\|^{2}
can be treated as a constant,
minimizing
\|P_{\Pi X}^{\perp}Y\|^{2}
is equivalent
to maximizing
\|P_{\Pi X}Y\|^{2}
.

By introducing two redundant variables
\Pi_{1}
and
\Pi_{2}
, we formulate (
146
) as

\min_{\Pi_{1},~\Pi_{2}}-\text{trace}\left(\Pi_{1}P_{X}\Pi_{2}^{T}YY^{T}\right),~s.t.\Pi_{1}=\Pi_{2},

(147)

where
P_{X}:=X(X^{T}X)^{-1}X^{T}
.
We propose to solve (
147
) with
the ADMM Algorithm
[
Boyd et al. 2011
]
and present the
details of the algorithm in Algorithm
6
.

Algorithm 6

ADMM algorithm for the recovery of
\Pi
.

1:

Input:

Initial estimate for the permutation matrix

\Pi^{(0)}
and create an
n\times n
matrix
\mu^{(0)}=\mathbf{0}
.

2:

For time
t+1
:

Update
\Pi_{1}^{(t+1)},\Pi_{2}^{(t+1)}
as

\displaystyle\Pi_{1}^{(t+1)}=

\displaystyle\argmin_{\Pi_{1}}\langle\Pi_{1},-YY^{T}\Pi^{(t)}_{2}P_{X}^{T}+\mu^{(t)}-\rho\Pi^{(t)}_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle\Pi_{2}^{(t+1)}=

\displaystyle\argmin_{\Pi_{2}}\langle\Pi_{2},~YY^{T}\Pi^{(t+1)}_{1}P_{X}-\mu^{(t)}-\rho\Pi^{(t+1)}_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle\mu^{(t+1)}=

\displaystyle~\mu^{(t)}+\rho\left(\Pi^{(t+1)}_{1}-\Pi_{2}^{(t+1)}\right).

3:

Termination
: Stop the ADMM algorithm once
\Pi_{1}^{(t+1)}

is identical to
\Pi_{2}^{(t+1)}
.

Since ADMM may exhibit slow convergence
[
Boyd et al. 2011
]
, we adopt a warm start strategy to accelerate the algorithm, which consists of two steps:

•

Compute the average values
\bar{X}=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{p}X[:,i]
.

•

Obtain a rough estimate
\Pi^{(0)}
by using Algorithm
7
or
8
with
X=\bar{X}
.

Algorithm 7

Averaging estimator.

1:

Compute the average
\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}Y[:,i]
.

2:

Compute
\hat{\Pi}
by maximizing

\left(\langle m^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{m}Y[:,i],\Pi X\rangle\right)^{2}
.

Algorithm 8

Eigenvalue estimator.

1:

Compute the principal eigenvector
\mathbf{u}
of

m^{-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}Y[:,i]Y[:,i]^{T}\right)
.

2:

Recover
\hat{\Pi}
by maximizing

\left(\langle\mathbf{u},~\Pi X\rangle\right)^{2}
.

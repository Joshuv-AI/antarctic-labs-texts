5
Defect of semismallness

In this section, we review the defect of semismallness of a morphism, an invariant introduced by de Cataldo and Migliorini that plays a crucial role in the decomposition theorem and Lefschetz’s theorem. It appears in Fact

.

5.1
Stratifications and constructible sheaves

We refer to
[

, Sec. 2.1]
for the definitions of

and

of a complex analytic space.

Theorem

Theorem 5.1.1

(Analytic Chevalley theorem)
.

Let

be a proper morphism of reduced complex analytic spaces. For every integer

, let

. Then

is an analytic subset of

is a constructible stratification of

Proof.

Let

[

, Thm. 3.6, p.137]
,

is an analytic subset of

, p.94]

. By Remmert theorem (see,

,
[

, Thm. 4A, p.150]

is analytic in

.
∎

Definition 5.1.2

(
[

, p.125]

(

5.2
Equivalent definitions

The defect of semismallness measures how far a morphism of complex manifolds is from being semismall (see,
e.g.
,
[
KW01
, Def. 7.3, p.156]
). However, in the literature there exist multiple seemingly different definitions. We review some of them and show that they are equivalent.

Definition 5.2.1
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a proper morphism of complex manifolds with
\dim X=n
.

•

(
[
EV89
, Definition 1.1]
) Define

r_{1}(f)=\max_{Z}(\dim Z-\dim f(Z)-\mathrm{codim}_{X}(Z)),

(22)

where
Z
runs through all irreducible analytic subsets of
X
.

•

(
[
Max19
, Definition 9.3.7]
) For a Whitney stratification
(X=\sqcup S_{\alpha},Y=\sqcup T_{\lambda})
of
f
, we choose a point
y_{\lambda}\in T_{\lambda}
in each stratum, and define

r_{2}(f)=\max_{\lambda}\{2\dim f^{-1}(y_{\lambda})+\dim T_{\lambda}-n\}.

(23)

(By convention, the empty space has dimension
-\infty
.)

•

(
[
dCM05
, Definition 4.7.2]
) For each integer
i\geq 0
, let
Y_{i}=\{y\in Y:\dim f^{-1}(y)=i\}
. Define

r_{3}(f)=\max_{i\geq 0}(2i+\dim Y_{i}-n).

•

(
[
PS13
, Definition 2.8]
) For each integer
i\geq 0
, let
Y_{\geq i}=\{y\in Y:\dim f^{-1}(y)\geq i\}
for each
i\geq 0
. Define

r_{4}(f)=\max_{i\geq 0}(2i+\dim Y_{\geq i}-n).

•

(
[
dCM09
, Sec. 3.3.2, part 2]
) Define

r_{5}(f)=\dim X\times_{Y}X-n.

(24)

•

(
[
Wil16
, Sec 3.2]
) Define

r_{6}(f)=\max\{i\in\mathbb{Z}:{}^{p}\mathcal{H}^{i}(Rf_{*}\mathbb{C}_{X}[n])\neq 0\}.

Proposition 5.2.2
.

The first five numbers in Definition
5.2.1
are all equal.

This common integer is called the
defect of semismallness
of
f
and denoted by
r(f)
. We shall show
r(f)=r_{6}(f)
in Proposition
5.3.2

2
.

Proof.

•

r_{3}(f)=r_{4}(f)
: As each
Y_{i}
is a subset of
Y_{\geq i}
, one has
r_{3}(f)\leq r_{4}(f)
. There are only finitely many integers
i\geq 0
with
Y_{\geq i}
nonempty, so the maximum defining
r_{4}(f)
is attained at some
i_{0}(\geq 0)
. Then

2(i_{0}+1)+\dim Y_{\geq i_{0}+1}\leq 2i_{0}+\dim Y_{\geq i_{0}}.

Since
Y_{\geq i_{0}}=Y_{\geq i_{0}+1}\cup Y_{i_{0}}
, one has
\dim Y_{\geq i_{0}}=\dim Y_{i_{0}}
. Then

r_{4}(f)=2i_{0}+\dim Y_{\geq i_{0}}-n\leq r_{3}(f).

Therefore,
r_{3}(f)=r_{4}(f)
.

•

r_{2}(f)=r_{5}(f)
: By Thom’s first isotopy lemma (see,
e.g.
,
[
Mat12
, Prop. 11.1]
), for every
\lambda
, the restriction
f|_{f^{-1}(T_{\lambda})}:f^{-1}(T_{\lambda})\to T_{\lambda}
is a topologically locally trivial fibration. Therefore,
\dim f^{-1}(y_{\lambda})
is independent of
y_{\lambda}\in T_{\lambda}
and

\dim f^{-1}(T_{\lambda})\times_{T_{\lambda}}f^{-1}(T_{\lambda})=\dim T_{\lambda}+2\dim f^{-1}(y_{\lambda}).

(25)

As
\{f^{-1}(T_{\lambda})\times_{T_{\lambda}}f^{-1}(T_{\lambda})\}_{\lambda}
is a locally finite partition of
X\times_{Y}X
into
locally closed subsets (in the analytic Zariski topology), one has

\dim X\times_{Y}X=\max_{\lambda}[\dim f^{-1}(T_{\lambda})\times_{T_{\lambda}}f^{-1}(T_{\lambda})].

(26)

Plugging (
25
)
into (
26
) we get
r_{5}(f)=r_{2}(f)
. In particular,
r_{2}(f)
is independent of the choice of the stratifications.

•

r_{1}(f)\leq r_{2}(f)
: For every irreducible analytic subset

,

is an irreducible analytic subset of
Y
. Then
\{Y\setminus f(Z),f(Z)\}
is a constructible stratification of

. Fact

yields a Whitney stratification

of

. There exists

such that

is an open subset of

, hence

. Then

\dim Z=\dim(f^{-1}(T_{\lambda_{0}})\cap Z)\leq\dim f^{-1}(T_{\lambda_{0}}).

Then

This shows

. In particular, the maximum in (

•

r_{2}(f)\leq r_{1}(f)
: Fix a Whitney stratification

.

•

r_{2}(f)\leq r_{3}(f)
: By Theorem

•

r_{3}(f)\leq r_{2}(f)

from Kähler manifolds to Fujiki class
\mathcal{C}
. As
\dim X-r(\alpha)\leq\dim\alpha(X)
, the numerical hypothesis in Theorem
7.1.3
is more restrictive than that in Fact
1.1.1
. An example from
[
GL87
, Remark, p.401]
is reconsidered in the last paragraph of
[
KW15
, Sec. 3]
, to show that the bound
p+q<\dim X-r(\alpha)
is optimal for Fact
1.1.6
.

7.2
Moishezon manifolds

Moishezon manifolds are examples of manifolds in Fujiki class
\mathcal{C}
.

Definition 7.2.1

(Moishezon manifold,
[
MM07
, Def. 2.2.12]
)
.

A connected compact complex manifold
X
is called Moishezon if it has
\dim X
algebraically independent meromorphic functions.

In fact, according to
[
MM07
, Thm. 2.2.16]
, for every Moishezon manifold
X
, there is a proper modification
\pi:X^{\prime}\to X
with
X^{\prime}
a projective manifold. In particular,
X
is the meromorphic image of a projective manifold, hence in Fujiki class
\mathcal{C}
. Conversely, a connected compact complex manifold that is the meromorphic image of a projective manifold is Moishezon by the proof of
[
Voi02
, Cor. 12.12]
. For more references, see
[
JM22
, Sec. 1]
.

The intersection of the two subclasses, Kähler and Moishezon, is exactly the class of projective manifolds. More precisely, Moishezon’s theorem (see,
e.g.
,
[
Voi02
, Thm. 12.13]
) asserts that a Moishezon manifold is Kähler if and only if it is projective. A Moishezon manifold may not be homotopy equivalent to a Kähler manifold (
[
Ogu94
, Thm. 1]
). Kodaira-Spencer stability theorem (see,
e.g.
,
[
Voi02
, Thm. 9.1]
) shows that small deformations of a Kähler manifold are Kähler. Similarly, small deformations of a regular manifold are regular (
[
AT13
, Cor. 3.7]
). By contrast, there is a small deformation of a Moishezon manifold that is not in Fujiki class
\mathcal{C}
(
[
Cam91
, Sec. 0]
). In particular, there exists a regular manifold that is not in Fujiki class
\mathcal{C}
.

Moishezon manifolds are abundant. For example, for every smooth proper algebraic variety
X/\mathbb{C}
, its analytification
X^{\mathrm{an}}
is a Moishezon manifold (
[
Har77
, p.442]
). Hironaka (
[
Hir60
]
, see also
[
Har77
, p.443]
) gives examples of Moishezon manifolds that are not algebraic,
and smooth proper algebraic varieties that are not projective. The situation is depicted below. Every inclusion in this graph is strict.

Proposition 7.2.2
.

If
X
is a Moishezon manifold, then
\mathrm{Alb}(X)
and
\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(X)
are complex abelian varieties dual to each other.

Proof.

By
[
MM07
, Thm. 2.2.16]
,
X
admits a proper modification
\pi:X^{\prime}\to X
with
X^{\prime}
a projective manifold. By
[
Voi02
, Prop. 7.16]
, the Jacobian
\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(X^{\prime})
is projective. From Proposition
4.1.2

5
, the torus
\mathrm{Alb}(X^{\prime})
is dual to
\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(X^{\prime})
, so
\mathrm{Alb}(X^{\prime})
is algebraic. By
[
Uen75
, Prop. 9.12, p.107]
, the morphism
\pi_{*}:\mathrm{Alb}(X^{\prime})\to\mathrm{Alb}(X)
given by Proposition
4.1.2

1
is an isomorphism.
∎

Remark 7.2.3
.

By
[
BL04
, p.70]
, the analytic dual torus of a complex abelian variety is an abelian variety. Moreover, by
[
MRM74
, p.86]
, the (algebraic) dual abelian variety (defined in
[
MRM74
, p.78]
) of a complex abelian variety coincides with its analytic dual torus, so we do not distinguish the two duals in this case.

From now on, let
X/\mathbb{C}
be a smooth proper algebraic variety of dimension
n
with a base point
x_{0}\in X(\mathbb{C})
, and let
\mathrm{Sch}/\mathbb{C}
(resp.
\mathrm{Sets}
) be the category of
\mathbb{C}
-schemes (resp. sets). The fppf-sheaf associated to the functor

P_{X/\mathbb{C}}:(\mathrm{Sch}/S)^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Sets},\quad T\mapsto\mathrm{Pic}(X\times_{\mathbb{C}}T)

is called the
relative Picard functor
of
X
(
[
BLR90
, Def. 2, p.201]
). From
[
BLR90
, p.211, p.231 and p.233]
, the relative Picard functor of
X
is represented by a smooth group scheme
\mathrm{Pic}_{X/\mathbb{C}}
over
\mathbb{C}
. In particular, the group
\mathrm{Pic}_{X/\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{C})=\mathrm{Pic}(X)
. By
[
BLR90
, Thm. 3, p.232]
, the identity component
\mathrm{Pic}^{0}_{X/\mathbb{C}}
of
\mathrm{Pic}_{X/\mathbb{C}}
is proper over
\mathbb{C}
, hence a complex abelian variety called the
Picard variety
of
X
.

From
[
Ser58
, Thm. 5]
, there is an abelian variety
\mathrm{Alb}(X)/\mathbb{C}
with a
\mathbb{C}
-morphism
\alpha_{X,x_{0}}:(X,x_{0})\to(\mathrm{Alb}(X),0)
of pointed varieties satisfying the following universal property:

11

similar to that stated in Proposition
4.1.2

3
every
\mathbb{C}
-morphism of pointed varieties
(X,x_{0})\to(A,0)
with
A/\mathbb{C}
an abelian variety factors uniquely through a morphism of abelian varieties
\mathrm{Alb}(X)\to A
. Such morphism
\alpha_{x_{0}}
is unique up to a unique isomorphism. We call
\mathrm{Alb}(X)
the
algebraic Albanese variety
of
X
and
\alpha_{X,x_{0}}:(X,x_{0})\to(\mathrm{Alb}(X),0)
the
algebraic Albanese morphism
corresponding to
x_{0}
.

For every
O_{X}
-module
F
, let
F^{\mathrm{an}}
be the corresponding
O_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}
-module defined in

, Exp. XII, 1.3]
. Hence a functor

\mathrm{Mod}(O_{X})\to\mathrm{Mod}(O_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}),\quad F\mapsto F^{\mathrm{an}}.

By Serre’s GAGA

GR71
, Exp. XII, Thm. 4.4]
, the natural group morphism

\mathrm{Pic}(X)\to\mathrm{Pic}(X^{\mathrm{an}}),\quad L\mapsto L^{\mathrm{an}}

is an isomorphism. Corollary

of GAGA type compares the algebraic Picard variety and the analytic Jacobian. Once again, it is well-known, but a proof is given for the lack of reference.

Corollary 7.2.4
.

1.

The analytification of
\mathrm{Alb}(X)
(resp.
\alpha_{X,x_{0}}:X\to\mathrm{Alb}(X)
) is
\mathrm{Alb}(X^{\mathrm{an}})
(resp.
\alpha_{X^{\mathrm{an}},x_{0}}:X^{\mathrm{an}}\to\mathrm{Alb}(X^{\mathrm{an}})
).

2.

The analytification of
\mathrm{Pic}^{0}_{X/\mathbb{C}}
is
\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(X^{\mathrm{an}})
.

Proof.

1.

Since
X^{\mathrm{an}}
is a Moishezon manifold, by Proposition
7.2.2
, its Albanese torus
\mathrm{Alb}(X^{\mathrm{an}})
is projective. By Chow’s theorem
[
BL04
, Cor. A.4]
, the map
\alpha_{X^{\mathrm{an}},x_{0}}
is algebraic. By Proposition
4.1.2

3
, every algebraic morphism
(X,x_{0})\to(A,0)
to an abelian variety
A/\mathbb{C}
factors uniquely through an analytic (hence algebraic by Chow’s theorem again) morphism of complex tori
\mathrm{Alb}(X^{\mathrm{an}})\to A^{\mathrm{an}}
. The result follows.

2.

By
[
Moc12
, Prop. A.6]
, the (algebraic) dual abelian variety of
\mathrm{Pic}^{0}_{X/\mathbb{C}}
is
\mathrm{Alb}(X)
. By Proposition
4.1.2

5
,
\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(X^{\mathrm{an}})
is the (analytic) dual torus of
\mathrm{Alb}(X^{\mathrm{an}})=\mathrm{Alb}(X)^{\mathrm{an}}
, so
\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(X^{\mathrm{an}})
is the analytification of
\mathrm{Pic}^{0}_{X/\mathbb{C}}
.

∎

Identifying
\mathrm{Pic}^{0}_{X/\mathbb{C}}
with
\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(X^{\mathrm{an}})

via
Corollary
7.2.4

2
, one can define thin subsets of
\mathrm{Pic}^{0}_{X/\mathbb{C}}
. Define the defect of semismallness of a proper morphism
f:M\to N
between complex algebraic varieties by
r(f)=r(f^{\mathrm{an}})
. With this terminology, we get the following generic vanishing result for smooth proper algebraic varieties.

Corollary 7.2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a
unitary
local system on
X^{\mathrm{an}}
, and let
E=\mathcal{E}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}O_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}
be the corresponding holomorphic vector bundle. Then for any integers
p,q\geq 0
with
p+q>n+r(\alpha)
or
p+q<n-r(\alpha)
, the locus
S^{p,q}(X^{\mathrm{an}},E)
is contained in a thin (and arithmetic when
\mathcal{E}
is semisimple of geometric origin in
D_{c}^{b}(X^{\mathrm{an}})
) subset of
\mathrm{Pic}^{0}_{X/\mathbb{C}}
.

Proof.

By Corollary
7.2.4

1
, the analytification
\alpha^{\mathrm{an}}_{X,x_{0}}:X^{\mathrm{an}}\to\mathrm{Alb}(X)^{\mathrm{an}}
coincides with
\alpha_{X^{\mathrm{an}},x_{0}}:X^{\mathrm{an}}\to\mathrm{Alb}(X^{\mathrm{an}})
, and by definition,
r(\alpha)=r(\alpha^{\mathrm{an}})
. From Theorem
7.1.3

3
, the locus
S^{p,q}(X^{\mathrm{an}},E)
is contained in a thin subset of
\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(X)
.

What remains to show is the assertion in the parentheses. Assume that
\mathcal{E}
is semisimple of geometric origin. By the decomposition theorem
[
BBDG82
, Thm. 6.2.5]
,
\mathcal{K}:=R\alpha_{*}\mathcal{E}[n+r(\alpha)]
is semisimple of geometric origin in
D_{c}^{b}(\mathrm{Alb}(X^{\mathrm{an}}))
. By Theorem
7.1.3

2
, one may assume that
p+q>n+r(\alpha)
, so that

S^{p,q}(X^{\mathrm{an}},E)\subset\Sigma^{p+q}(X^{\mathrm{an}},\mathcal{E})\cap T(X)\subset\Sigma^{>0}(\mathrm{Alb}(X),\mathcal{K}),

where the first inclusion follows from (
33
) and the second from (
32

6.2.2

3
,
\Sigma^{>0}(\mathrm{Alb}(X),\mathcal{K})
is contained in an arithmetic thin subset of
\mathrm{Pic}^{0}_{X/\mathbb{C}}
.
∎

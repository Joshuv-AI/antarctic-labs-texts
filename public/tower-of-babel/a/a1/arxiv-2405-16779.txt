1.
Introduction

For a closed immersion between schemes
f\colon X\to Y
, we have
the normal sheaf
N_{f}
and normal cone
C_{f}
defined by

N_{f}=\mathrm{Spec}_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\sym I/I^{2},C_{f}=\mathrm{Spec}_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\oplus_{d\geq 0}I^{d}/I^{d+1},

where
I
denotes the ideal sheaf of
X
in
Y
.
As functors of closed immersions between schemes locally of finite type over a base field
k
, the functors

N
and
C
are extended to morphisms between algebraic stacks (
[
Kr1
,
Kr2
,
KKP
,
AP
]
), and are
involved in the construction of virtual fundamental classes
[
LT
,
BeFa
]
. See
[
BH
]
and the references therein for extensions beyond higher stacks.

For a DM stack
X
locally of finite type over
k
, the normal sheaf and normal cone of the
map
X\to\mathrm{Spec}k
are the intrinsic normal sheaf and intrinsic normal cone
N_{X}
and
C_{X}
introduced in

[
BeFa
]
. And it is proved that they carry information about deformations of maps from
\mathrm{Spec}k
to
X
.
More precisely, for any
k
-point

\xi\colon\mathrm{Spec}k\to X,

the coarse moduli space of the pullback of
N_{X}

along
\xi
is
\ext^{1}_{k}(\xi^{*}\mathbb{L}_{X},k)
, where
\mathbb{L}_{X}
denotes the cotangent complex of
X
.
The vector space

\ext^{1}_{k}(\xi^{*}\mathbb{L}_{X},k)
is the universal obstruction space for
\xi
(
[
BeFa
, Lemma 4.6]
),
and its subspace of
curvilinear obstructions is
the coarse moduli space of the pullback of
C_{X}

along
\xi
(
[
BeFa
, Lemma 4.7]
). Globally, universality of
N_{X}
builds into the definition of an obstruction theory for
X
and it is equivalent to a closed embedding of

N_{X}
into a cone stack (
[
BeFa
, Theorem 4.5]
), while curvilinearity of
C_{X}

lacks transparent description.

When
\Char k=0
, curvilinear extensions are
simple extensions
.(See Section
2.3
.)
The key observation in this paper is that,
simple obstructions
, i.e., obstructions for simple extensions,
are contained in the subspace

\ext^{1}_{k}(\mathbf{L}\xi^{*}\Omega^{1}_{X},k)

of
\ext^{1}(\xi^{*}\mathbb{L}_{X},k)
, and we are led to consider the subcone

\mathbb{V}(\Omega_{X}^{1}[-1]),\text{or}\ h^{1}/h^{0}((\Omega^{1}_{X})^{\vee}),

of
N_{X}
.(See Section
2.2
for notation.)
If
k
is also algebraically closed, then we show
C_{X}
is supported in
\mathbb{V}(\Omega_{X}^{1}[-1])

(Theorem
3.2
). It is proved using
Lemma
2.4
which deduce global information about support of closed substacks from their pointwise coarse sheaves. Here the support of a closed substack
C
is the reduced substack
C_{\mathrm{red}}
associated to it.

Cone reduction for a subcone of
N_{X}
concerns its support.
It is easy to see cone reduction by cosections for
\mathbb{V}(\Omega_{X}^{1}[-1])
.
A cosection

\sigma\colon N_{X}\to A^{1}_{k}

is a map between cones and is induced by a map

\mathcal{O}_{X}[1]\to\mathbb{L}_{X}^{\geq-1}

from
\mathcal{O}_{X}[1]
to the truncated cotangent complex of
X

in
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qcoh}}(X)
.
Since there is no nonzero map from
\mathcal{O}_{X}[1]
to
\Omega^{1}_{X}
[0], we conclude

\mathbb{V}(\Omega_{X}^{1}[-1])
is supported in the kernel
\sigma^{-1}\{0\}
. This observation improves

[
KL
, Corollary 4.5]
. Note that we consider maps
\mathcal{O}_{X}[1]\to\mathbb{L}_{X}^{\geq-1}
without
additional obstruction theories for
X
.
More generally, for any
\mathcal{G}[1]\to\mathbb{L}_{X}^{\geq-1}
, where
\mathcal{G}
is a coherent sheaf on
X
, the
obvious vanishing
of
\mathcal{G}[1]\to\Omega_{X}^{1}[0]
can be viewed as a global version of
[
BuFl
, Proposition 6.13(1)]
.
Semiregularity can also be treated globally from this perspective as in
[
Ch
, Section 5]
and
[
Sc
, Section 3]
.

We also deduce a relative version.
For a map
f\colon X\to Y
between DM stacks locally of finite type over
k
, and
Y
smooth,
we look for subcones of
N_{f}
that contain
C_{f}
.
The cone
\mathbb{V}(\Omega_{f}[-1])
is too small for closed immersions.
As
C_{X}
is given by
[C_{f}/f^{*}T_{Y}]
, it follows from Theorem
3.2
that

C_{f}
is supported in

\mathbb{V}(\{f^{*}{\Omega_{Y}^{1}}\to\Omega_{X}^{1}\}[-1])

(Proposition
3.4
) .
Here

\{f^{*}\Omega_{Y}^{1}\to\Omega_{X}^{1}\}
is the two-term complex with
f^{*}\Omega_{Y}^{1}
in degree
-1
and
\Omega_{X}^{1}
degree 0.

Cone reduction by cosections can also be treated in the framework of derived algebraic geometry and
derived differential geometry. See

[
AKLPR
, Section 6]
,
[
BKP
, Appendix A.1]
, and
[
Sa
, Proposition 5.1]
.

Recent interests in cone reduction come from the work of
Oh-Thomas (
[
OT
]
).
In their approach to construct algebraic virtual classes on moduli spaces of sheaves on complex Calabi-Yau 4 folds, it is of interest to show
a normal cone inside a quadratic bundle is isotropic or supported in some maximal isotropic subbundle.
The isotropic condition in
[
OT
]
is established using
the Darboux theorem (
[
BBJ
]
) for shifted symplectic structures (
[
PTVV
]
).
The local counterparts to these global questions
concern the subspace of curvilinear obstructions
inside the standard
obstruction space with its quadratic form induced by Serre Duality, and imply global results for the reduced normal cone. However, these local questions are not as trivial to establish as those in this paper and seems out of reach for us.

The paper is organized as follows.
Preliminary results are collected in Section 2, from which the
the main results in Section 3 follow rather easily. In Section 4, we globalize
the semiregularity map(
[
BuFl
]
) for sheaves
to a map in the derived category, and show vanishing of obstructions for simple extensions.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Coarse sheaf and support

2.1.1.
\pi_{0}

Let
\pi_{0}\colon\mathbf{Grpd}\to\mathbf{Sets}
be the functor that sends a groupoid to its isomorphic classes of objects.
It is the
left adjoint of the inclusion
\mathbf{Sets}\to\mathbf{Grpd}
.

We view
\mathbf{Sch}_{k}
as a site with the big étale topology.
For a stack

Y\colon\mathbf{Sch}_{k}\to\mathbf{Grpd},

denote
\pi_{0}(Y)
the sheafification of the
composition

\pi_{0}\circ Y\colon\mathbf{Sch}_{k}\to\mathbf{Sets}.

Clearly,
\pi_{0}(Y)
is functorial in
Y
and there is a natural transformation

Y\to\pi_{0}(Y).

Recall a map
M\to N
between stacks over
\mathbf{Sch}_{k}
is fully faithful if the relative diagonal

M\to M\times_{N}M
is an isomorphism. Equivalently, for any
T\in\mathbf{Sch}_{k}
,

M(T)\to N(T)
is fully faithful in
\mathbf{Grpd}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
M\to N
be a fully faithful map of stacks, then

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{0}(M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{0}(N)}

is a cartesian diagram in the
(2,1)
-category of stacks over
\mathbf{Sch}_{k}
.

Proof.

If

G\to H

is a fully faithful map in
\mathbf{Grpd}
,
then

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{0}(G)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{0}(H)}

is (homotopy) cartesian in
\mathbf{Grpd}
.
It follows that

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{0}\circ M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{0}\circ N}

is cartesian, and we see the lemma holds by taking stackification.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

If
g\colon Z\to Y
is a closed immersions between algebraic stacks, then

is fully faithful.
From the lemma we see that
\pi_{0}(Z)\to\pi_{0}(Y)
is representable by closed immersions.
In particular,
\pi_{0}(Z)\to\pi_{0}(Y)
is fully faithful, and we view
\pi_{0}(Z)
as a subsheaf of
\pi_{0}(Y)
.

Remark 2.3

For the closed immersion

2.1.2.
Support of closed substacks

Let

be an algebraic stack over

, and

closed substacks of
N
.
For any point
f\colon T\to X
in
\mathbf{DM}_{k}
, we have subsheaves

\pi_{0}(f^{*}C)
and
\pi_{0}(f^{*}D)
of
\pi_{0}(f^{*}N)
. Here

are the fiber products

The following lemma is a variation on
[

, Proposition 2.1]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let

be an algebraic closed field. If for any
k
- point

\xi\colon\mathrm{Spec}k\to X,

the sheaf
\pi_{0}(\xi^{*}C)
is contained in
\pi_{0}(\xi^{*}D)
as subsheaves of
\pi_{0}(\xi^{*}N)
,
then

is supported in
D
. In other words, the inclusion map
C_{\mathrm{red}}\to N
factors through the inclusion

.

.

By passing to smooth covers, we can assume

and

are in
\mathbf{Sch}_{k}
.
For schemes in
\mathbf{Sch}_{k}
, closed points are dense, and
C
is supported in
D

if
closed points of
C
belongs to those of
D
.
As
k
is algebraically closed, closed points are
k
-points, and we only need to show

C(k)\subset D(k)
.

Any
k
-point of
C
lies over some closed point
\xi\colon\mathrm{Spec}k\to X
.
By Lemma

, we have cartesian diagrams

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\xi^{*}C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\xi^{*}N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{0}(\xi^{*}C)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{0}(\xi^{*}N),}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\xi^{*}D}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\xi^{*}N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{0}(\xi^{*}D)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{0}(\xi^{*}N).}

From the assumption
\pi_{0}(\xi^{*}C)\subset\pi_{0}(\xi^{*}D)
,
we see
\xi^{*}C\subset\xi^{*}D
as closed subschemes of
\xi^{*}N
, and

2.2.
The functor
h^{1}/h^{0}((-)^{\vee})

Let
X\in\mathbf{DM}_{k}
. Associated to
E\in\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{coh}}^{\leq 0}(X)
is the cone stack

h^{1}/h^{0}(E^{\vee}).

(See
[
BeFa
, Proposition 2.4]
.)
If
\tau^{[-1,0]}(E)
is quasi-isomorphic to a two-term complex of coherent sheaves

\mathcal{Q}\to\mathcal{F}

with
\mathcal{F}
locally free, then
h^{1}/h^{0}(E^{\vee})
is the quotient stack

[\mathrm{C}(\mathcal{Q})/\mathrm{C}(\mathcal{F})],

where
\mathrm{C}(-)=\mathrm{Spec}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\sym(-)
.
The map
\mathcal{Q}\to\mathcal{F}
induces a map between abelian cones
\mathrm{C}(\mathcal{F})\to\mathrm{C}(\mathcal{Q})
,
and as a morphism of abelian group schemes over
X
, we have a Picard stack
[\mathrm{C}(\mathcal{Q})/\mathrm{C}(\mathcal{F})]
.
It can also be described as the stack which associates to
a
T
-point
f\colon T\to X
the mapping space between
f^{*}(E[-1])
and
\mathcal{O}_{T}
, and
is denoted
\mathbb{V}(E[-1])
in
[
AP
]
.
We will also use the
\mathbb{V}
notation.

Remark 2.5
.

The
\mathbb{V}
construction using mapping spaces is more general than
h^{1}/h^{0}
. For a 3-term complex

F

in degree
[0,2]
on an algebraic stack,
\mathbb{V}(F)
is in general
a Picard 2-stack. The
\mathbb{V}
notation depends on the convention of
authors, it is either
\mathbb{V}(E)
or
\mathbb{V}(E^{\vee})
.

When
E
is perfect,
\pi_{0}(h^{1}/h^{0}(E^{\vee}))
is give by
h^{1}(E^{\vee})
. Here

denotes its extension to the big étale site. (See e.g.,
[
BeFa
, Section 2, p. 60]
for extensions from
a small site to a big site.)

From its description, it is clear the pullback of

along

is
h^{1}/h^{0}((g^{*}E)^{\vee})
.
In particular, for any point
\xi\in X(k)
, the pullback is
h^{1}/h^{0}((\xi^{*}E)^{\vee})
.
As
\tau^{[-1,0]}(\xi^{*}E)
is quasi-isomorphic to
h^{-1}(\xi^{*}E)[1]\oplus h^{0}(\xi^{*}E)

in the derived category of
k
-modules, it is easy to see

\pi_{0}(h^{1}/h^{0}((g^{*}E)^{\vee}))
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{C}(h^{-1}(\xi^{*}E))
, or the
k
-vector space

\ext^{1}_{k}(\xi^{*}E,k)
.

2.3.
Simple extensions and obstructions

A closed immersion
S\to S^{\prime}
between schemes with ideal
I
is
a square zero extension if
I^{2}=0
, in which case the ideal sheaf
I

has an induced
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-module structure.
In the affine case,
S\to S^{\prime}
corresponds to a surjection of commutative rings
A^{\prime}\to A
with
square zero kernel.

Isomorphic classes of square zero extensions of
S
by an
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-module
J

can be identified with

\ext^{1}_{S}(\mathbb{L}_{S},J)
(
[
Il
, III.1.2.3]
).
For a square zero extension
S\to S^{\prime}
, the element it determines in

\ext^{1}_{S}(\mathbb{L}_{S},J)

is the map

\mathbb{L}_{S}\to\mathbb{L}_{S/S^{\prime}}\to\tau^{\geq-1}\mathbb{L}_{S/S^{\prime}}\simeq J[1].

The natural map
\mathbb{L}_{S}\to\Omega_{S}^{1}
induces an inclusion

\ext^{1}_{S}(\Omega_{S}^{1},J)\to\ext^{1}_{S}(\mathbb{L}_{S},J).

Elements of the subspace

\ext^{1}_{S}(\Omega_{S}^{1},J)
correspond to simple extensions.
Alternatively, a square zero extension
S\to S^{\prime}
is simple, if

I/I^{2}\to{\Omega_{S^{\prime}}^{1}}_{|_{S}}\to\Omega_{S}\to 0

is exact on the left. (See e.g.,
[
BuFl
, Theorem 9.2 (2)]
.)

It is easy to see curvilinear extensions

k\to k[t]/(t^{n+1})\to k[t]/(t^{n}),n\geq 2

are simple when
\Char k=0
.

Let
S\to S^{\prime}
be a square zero extension in
\mathbf{Sch}_{k}

classified by some map
e\colon\mathbb{L}_{S}\to J[1]
,
and
f\colon S\to X
a map in
\mathbf{DM}_{k}
.
The obstruction to lift
f
from
S
to
S^{\prime}

lies in
\ext^{1}_{S}(f^{*}\mathbb{L}_{X},J)
, and
is given by
the map

(1)

f^{*}\mathbb{L}_{X}\to\mathbb{L}_{S}\xrightarrow{e}J[1].

(
[
Il
, III.2.2.4]
)

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
e
be a simple extension of
S
by
J
, then the obstruction
to lift
f\colon S\to X
along
e
to
S^{\prime}

lies in the subspace

\ext^{1}_{S}(\mathbf{L}f^{*}\Omega^{1}_{X},J)

of
\ext^{1}_{S}(f^{*}\mathbb{L}_{X},J)
.

Proof.

The lemma holds because the map
f^{*}\mathbb{L}_{X}\to\mathbb{L}_{S}\to\Omega_{S}^{1}[0]
factors through

\mathbf{L}f^{*}\Omega^{1}_{X}
.
∎

In this section, we assume the field

For a map

.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{L}f^{*}{\Omega_{Y}^{1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{L}_{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{L}_{f}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{*}{\Omega_{Y}^{1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega_{X}^{1}}

.
Now we apply

, Proposition 2.6]
to finish the proof.

Unfortunately, we don’t know how to prove this inside

4.
semiregularity map for sheaves

4.1.

Let
M
be a DM stack in
\mathbf{DM}_{k}
with an obstruction theory

F\to\mathbb{L}_{M}
, i.e., a map in
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{coh}}(M)
whose cone lies in
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{coh}}^{\leq-2}(M)
.
Let
\rho\colon G\to F
be a map in
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{coh}}(M)
,
it functions as a global semiregularity map.
For any map
f\colon S\to M
and square zero extension
\mathbb{L}_{S}\to J[1]

we obtain the map

\ext^{1}_{S}(f^{*}F,J)\xrightarrow{f^{*}\rho\circ}\ext^{1}_{S}(f^{*}G,J),

which we view as a semiregularity type map.

4.2.

Now we move on to the semiregularity map for sheaves in
[
BuFl
]
, we will globalize the map as above.
For a discussion of works related to semiregular maps, see
[
Le
, Chapter 3]
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to B
be a flat family of smooth projective varieties over an algebraically closed
field
k
with
\Char k=0
, and

\mu\colon M\to B
a moduli
scheme of stable sheaves on the fibers of
\pi
. (See e.g.,
[
HL
, Chapter 4]
.)
Consider the cartesian digram,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\times_{B}X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu}

where we used
p,q
to denote projection maps.

We have a map

(2)

(p_{*}\mathop{\mathcal{H}om}\nolimits(E,E)[1])^{\vee}\to\mathbb{L}_{\mu}

obtained from
the Atiyah class of a twisted universal sheaf
E

on
M\times_{B}X
relative to
q
.
(See e.g.,
[
Ku
, Section 1.4]
.) And an obstruction theory can be constructed from this map (
[
HT
, Section 4]
).

Next, we will construct the map in
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{coh}}(M)
with target
(p_{*}\mathop{\mathcal{H}om}\nolimits(E,E)[1])^{\vee}
,
or dually, a map with source
p_{*}\mathop{\mathcal{H}om}\nolimits(E,E)[1]
, that induces the local semiregularity map
up to
\pm
signs.
For each integer
n\geq 0
,
consider the composition

E\otimes\wedge^{n}\mathbb{L}_{p}[n]\xrightarrow{\mathrm{At}(E)\otimes\mathrm{id}}E\otimes\mathbb{L}_{p}[1]\otimes\wedge^{n}\mathbb{L}_{p}[n]\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}\otimes\wedge}E\otimes\wedge^{n+1}\mathbb{L}_{p}[n+1],

where

\mathrm{At}(E)\colon E\to E\otimes\mathbb{L}_{p}[1]
denotes the Atiyah class of
E
relative to
p
.
It induces a map

\mathrm{At}(E)\circ\colon\mathop{\mathcal{H}om}\nolimits(E,E\otimes\wedge^{n}\mathbb{L}_{p}[n])\to\mathop{\mathcal{H}om}\nolimits(E,E\otimes\wedge^{n+1}\mathbb{L}_{p}[n+1]).

These maps determine

(\mathrm{At}(E)\circ)^{n}[1]\colon\mathop{\mathcal{H}om}\nolimits(E,E)[1]\to\mathop{\mathcal{H}om}\nolimits(E,E\otimes\wedge^{n}\mathbb{L}_{p}[n])[1].

Composing with the trace map

\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits\colon\mathop{\mathcal{H}om}\nolimits(E,E\otimes\wedge^{n}\mathbb{L}_{p}[n])[1]\simeq\mathop{\mathcal{H}om}\nolimits(E,E)\otimes\wedge^{n}\mathbb{L}_{p}[n+1]\xrightarrow{\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits_{E}\otimes\mathrm{id}}\wedge^{n}\mathbb{L}_{p}[n+1]

and pushforward alone p,
we obtain the map

(3)

p_{*}\mathop{\mathcal{H}om}\nolimits(E,E)[1]\to p_{*}\wedge^{n}\mathbb{L}_{p}[n+1].

The product of these maps over
n

p_{*}\mathop{\mathcal{H}om}\nolimits(E,E)[1]\to\prod_{n\geq 1}p_{*}\wedge^{n}\mathbb{L}_{p}[n+1]

in
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{coh}}(M)

is dual to the desired global semiregularity map up to
\pm
signs on each factor indexed by
n
.

As

\mathbb{L}_{p}\simeq q^{*}\mathbb{L}_{\pi}\simeq q^{*}\Omega_{\pi}^{1}
,
we see

p_{*}\wedge^{n}\mathbb{L}_{p}[n+1]\simeq p_{*}q^{*}\Omega^{n}_{\pi}[n+1]\simeq\mu^{*}\mathbf{R}\pi_{*}\Omega_{\pi}^{n}[n+1].

Note that

\mathbf{R}\pi_{*}\Omega_{\pi}^{n}\simeq\bigoplus_{j\geq 0}\mathbf{R}^{j}\pi_{*}\Omega_{\pi}^{n}[-j]

is a direct sum of shifted
locally free coherent sheaves by
[
De
, Theorem 6.1, Proposition 5.5(i)]
.

Vanishing of simple extensions under the semiregularity map was proved
in
[
BuFl
]
, an alternative proof for smooth projective families
follows from the lemma below.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
M
be a DM stack,
m
an integer,
\mathcal{G}
a locally free coherent sheaf on
M
with a
map
\phi\colon\mathcal{G}[m]\to\mathbb{L}_{M}
.
Let

f\colon\mathrm{Spec}A\to M
be a map.
If
m\neq 0
,
then the composition

f^{*}\mathcal{G}[m]\xrightarrow{f^{*}\phi}f^{*}\mathbb{L}_{M}\to\mathbb{L}_{A}\to\Omega_{A}^{1}

is zero.
It follows that for any
A
-module
J
and map
e\colon\Omega_{A}^{1}\to J[1]
in
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qcoh}}(\mathrm{Spec}A)

the composition

f^{*}\mathcal{G}[m]\xrightarrow{f^{*}\phi}f^{*}\mathbb{L}_{M}\to\mathbb{L}_{A}\to\Omega_{A}^{1}\xrightarrow{e}J[1]

is zero.

Proof.

As
f^{*}\mathcal{G}
is a locally free coherent sheaf on
\mathrm{Spec}A
,

\hom_{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qcoh}}(\mathrm{Spec}A)}(f^{*}\mathcal{G}[m],\Omega_{A}^{1})=\ext^{-m}_{A}(f^{*}\mathcal{G},\Omega_{A}^{1})=0

if
m\neq 0
. For
m=0
,

\ext^{-m}_{A}(f^{*}\mathcal{G},J[1])=\ext^{1}_{A}(f^{*}\mathcal{G},J)=0.

∎

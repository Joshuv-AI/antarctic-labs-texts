Blocks of profinite groups with cyclic defect group
Ricardo J. Franquiz Flores, John W. MacQuarrie

Abstract
We demonstrate that the blocks of a profinite group whose defect groups are cyclic have a Brauer tree algebra structure analogous to the case of finite groups. We show further that the Brauer tree of a block with defect group ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} is of star type.



1
Introduction

The modular representation theory of a finite group
G
may loosely be described as the study of the category of
kG
-modules and their relationship with the group
G
, where
k
for us will be an algebraically closed field whose characteristic
p
divides the order of
G
. A simple but effective approach to modular representation theory is to write
kG
as a direct product of indecomposable algebras, known in the theory as the blocks of
G
, and study the representation theory of each block separately. The difficulty of a given block
B
may be measured by a
p
-subgroup of
G
, called the defect group of
B
. The only general family of defect groups whose corresponding blocks are completely understood, is the class of cyclic
p
-groups. Blocks with cyclic defect group have an explicit description as so-called “Brauer tree algebras”. For a clear and encyclopedic discussion of the block theory of finite groups we recommend
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
.

The study of the modular representation theory of profinite groups was begun in
[
13
]
,
[
12
]
, while the study of blocks and defect groups has been initiated recently in
[
5
]
. In this article we classify, in Theorem
4.3
, the blocks of an arbitrary profinite group whose defect groups are cyclic (meaning either a finite cyclic
p
-group or the
p
-adic integers
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
): they are Brauer tree algebras in strict analogy with the finite case. Our approach is to use limit arguments and invoke the corresponding theory for finite groups, thus avoiding explicit mention of the most technical arguments required for finite groups. The results are quite striking: a block of the profinite group
G
with finite cyclic defect group has finite dimension (Theorem
4.4
) and is thus a block of a finite quotient of
G
– the blocks of profinite groups with finite cyclic defect group are thus precisely the blocks of finite groups with cyclic defect group. Meanwhile, a block with defect group
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
has a very simple Brauer tree, of so-called “star type” (Theorem
5.2
) – the blocks with defect group
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
are thus well behaved as algebras, even by the standard of blocks of cyclic defect group (Corollary
5.3
).

Preliminaries

Let
k
be a field of characteristic

, regarded as a discrete topological ring. In what follows, when the coefficient ring is unspecified it is assumed to be

Definition 2.1
.

A

is an associative, unital, Hausdorff topological

-algebra

Equivalently, a pseudocompact algebra is an inverse limit of discrete finite dimensional algebras in the category of topological algebras. For a general introduction to pseudocompact objects, see

. If

is a profinite group, an inverse system of finite continuous quotients

Definition 2.2
.

If

-module

The category of pseudocompact modules for a pseudocompact algebra is abelian and has exact inverse limits.

2.2
Radicals and socles of pseudocompact
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]

Definition 2.6
.

Let
A
be a pseudocompact
k

be a pseudocompact

-module. The

, is the intersection of the maximal open submodules of

. For each

recursively to be

, where

. For convenience, we also define

.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let

be pseudocompact
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
-module with

. Then

1.

For each

,
\textnormal{Rad}^{i}(U)=\varprojlim_{N}\textnormal{Rad}^{i}(U_{N})
and the maps of the inverse system are surjective.

2.

For each

.

The case

of the first part follows from routine checks, using that
\varphi_{MN}(\textnormal{Rad}(U_{N}))=\textnormal{Rad}(U_{M})
whenever

by

, Proposition 9.15]
, while the general case follows by induction on

. The second part follows from Lemma

.
∎

Definition 2.8
.

Let

be a pseudocompact algebra and
U
a pseudocompact

-module. We say that

is

if every closed submodule

of

has a closed complement – that is, there is a closed submodule

of

such that

.

Lemma 2.9

(
[
8
, Lemma 3.9]
)
.

Let
A
be a pseudocompact algebra and
U
a pseudocompact
A
-module. The following are equivalent:

1.

is semisimple.

2.

Every open submodule of

has a complement.

3.

is a direct product of simple modules.

Recall that the

\textnormal{Soc}(V)
of a finite dimensional module

is the largest semisimple submodule of

. If
N\leq M
are open normal subgroups of
G
and

is a simple submodule of

, then

\varphi_{MN}(X)
is simple or zero, so that
\varphi_{MN}(\textnormal{Soc}(U_{N}))\subseteq\textnormal{Soc}(U_{M})
.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
G
be a profinite group and
U
a pseudocompact
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
-module. The restriction of the inverse system
\{U_{N},\varphi_{MN}\}
to the socles yields an inverse system, whose limit is the maximal closed semisimple submodule of

.

Proof.

Denote by
L=\varprojlim_{N}\{\textnormal{Soc}(U_{N}),\varphi_{MN}|_{\textnormal{Soc}(U_{N})}\}
. We first confirm that
L
is a semisimple submodule of
U
. Let
W
be an open submodule of
L
. By Lemma

, it is sufficient to confirm that the canonical projection
\pi:L\to L/W
splits. But since

is finite dimensional,

factors through
\textnormal{Soc}(U_{N})
for some cofinal subset of

, as
\pi_{N}\varphi_{N}
. Since
\textnormal{Soc}(U_{N})
is semisimple and

is surjective, there is a non-empty linear variety
I_{N}=\{\iota_{N}:L/W\to\textnormal{Soc}(U_{N})\,|\,\pi_{N}\iota_{N}=\textnormal{id}_{L/W}\}\subseteq\textnormal{Hom}_{k[\hskip-0.60275pt[{G}]\hskip-0.60275pt]}(L/W,\textnormal{Soc}(U_{N}))
, which is necessarily closed since the latter is finite dimensional. The

form an inverse system in the obvious way, the limit is non-empty by

, and an element of the limit is a splitting of

To prove the lemma, it remains to check that every closed semisimple submodule

,

and so

.
∎

Definition 2.11
.

The

of

, denoted by

, is the maximal closed semisimple submodule of

.

Observe that the inverse system of socles need not be surjective, and it can happen that

Lemma 2.12
.

Let

be a profinite group and

a pseudocompact

-module. Then
U/\textnormal{Soc}(U)\cong\varprojlim_{N}U_{N}/\textnormal{Soc}(U_{N})

Proof.

The result follows by applying Lemma

and

.
∎

2.3
Blocks and defect groups

Let
k
be a field of characteristic
p
and
A
a pseudocompact
k
-algebra. An element
e\in A
is
idempotent
if
e^{2}=e
. Two idempotents
e,f
of
A
are
orthogonal
if
ef=fe=0
. A non-zero idempotent is
primitive
if it cannot be written as the sum of two non-zero orthogonal idempotents. We denote by
Z(A)
the center of
A
. An idempotent
e\in A
is called
centrally primitive
if
e
is a primitive idempotent of
Z(A)
.

By
[
6
, IV. §3, Corollaries 1,2]
, there is a set of pairwise orthogonal centrally primitive idempotents
E=\{e_{i}\mbox{ : }i\in I\}
in
A
such that

\displaystyle A=\prod\limits_{i\in I}Ae_{i}=\prod\limits_{i\in I}B_{i}.

(1)

Each
B_{i}
is an algebra with unity
e_{i}
. We call
B_{i}
a
block of

and
e_{i}
a
block idempotent
.

We give a brief reminder of the defect group of a block. For more details and the results mentioned in this paragraph, see
[
5
, §5]
. The group
G
acts continuously on
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
by conjugation (notation
g\cdot x={}^{g}x
). If
H
is an open subgroup of
G
, denote by
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]^{H}
the set of
H
-fixed points of
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
under this action. We have a linear map
\textnormal{Tr}_{H}^{G}:k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]^{H}\to k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]^{G}
given by
\textnormal{Tr}_{H}^{G}(a):=\sum_{g\in G/H}{}^{g}a
, where “
G/H
” denotes a set of left coset representatives of
H
in
G
. If
B
is a block of
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
with block idempotent
e
, a
defect group
of
B
is a closed subgroup
D
of
G
, minimal with the property that

e\in\bigcap_{N\trianglelefteq_{O}G}\textnormal{Tr}_{DN}^{G}(k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]^{DN}).

The defect groups of a block are pro-
p
subgroups of
G
, unique up to conjugation in
G
.

A block of
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
is precisely an indecomposable summand of
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
, treated as a
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G\times G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
-module with action
(g_{1},g_{2})\cdot x:=g_{1}xg_{2}^{-1}
. We may thus define the coinvariants of a block

: if
N
is an open normal subgroup of
G
, we define
B_{N}:=B_{N\times N}
. For any given
N
,
B_{N}
is a direct product of blocks of
k[G/N]
, though not necessarily a block. As with
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
, the natural projections
\varphi_{MN},\varphi_{N}
are algebra homomorphisms. We recall some further results from
[
5
]
:

Lemma 2.13

(
[
5
, Proposition 4.7 and Corollary 5.10]
)
.

If the block
B
of
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{G}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
has defect group
D
, then
B
can be expressed as the inverse limit of a surjective inverse system of blocks
Z_{N}
of
k[G/N]
having defect group

, as

runs through some cofinal inverse system of open normal subgroups of

.

In
[
5
]
the blocks

of Lemma

were called “

”, but in this article we will use the notation

for something else.

3
Blocks with cyclic defect groups

From this point forward we suppose that
k
is algebraically closed. Recall that a profinite group is
cyclic
if it possesses a dense cyclic abstract subgroup. The cyclic pro-
p
groups are the finite cyclic groups
\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}
(
n\in\mathbb{N}
) and the
p
-adic integers
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. We fix some notation. Let
B
be block of the profinite group
G
having non-trivial cyclic defect group
D
. Denote by
\mathcal{S}
a set of representatives of the isomorphism classes of simple
B
-modules. By
[
15
, Proposition 3.3]
, there is a natural bijection between the indecomposable projective
B
-modules and the simple
B
-modules, given by sending the projective indecomposable
P
to the simple module
P/\textnormal{Rad}(P)
.

Denote by
\mathcal{P}=\{P_{S}\,:\,S\in\mathcal{S}\}
a set of representatives of the isomorphism classes of indecomposable projective
B
-modules, indexed in such a way that
P_{S}
is the projective cover of
S
. Write
B=\varprojlim_{N\in\mathcal{N}}Z_{N}
as in Lemma
2.13
, with the cofinal set
\mathcal{N}
chosen so that the block
Z_{N}
has (cyclic) defect group
DN/N
. For
N\in\mathcal{N}
, we denote by
\mathcal{S}_{N},\mathcal{P}_{N}
corresponding sets of representatives of simple modules and indecomposable projective modules for
Z_{N}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The set
\mathcal{S}
(hence also
\mathcal{P}
) is finite.

Proof.

It follows from
[
11
, Theorem 11.1.1 and Theorem 11.1.3]
that
|\mathcal{S}_{N}|
divides
p-1
.

Assume for contradiction that
|\mathcal{S}|\geq p
and fix a set
\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{p}\}
of distinct simples in
\mathcal{S}
. By
[
5
, Corollary 4.9]
, there is
N\in\mathcal{N}
such that
\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{p}\}
are distinct simple modules in
Z_{N}
. But this contradicts the first sentence.
∎

Using Lemma
3.1
, we restrict
\mathcal{N}
further, assuming from now on that each
N\in\mathcal{N}
acts trivially on every simple module in
B
.

Lemma 3.2
.

For each
P=P_{S}\in\mathcal{P}
and
N\in\mathcal{N}
, the module
P_{N}
is non-zero and indecomposable.

Proof.

Let
\pi:P\to S
be the canonical projection. The functor
(-)_{N}
is right exact, and so we obtain a surjective map
\pi_{N}:P_{N}\to S_{N}=S
, and so
P_{N}\neq 0
. By Lemma
2.7
,
P/\textnormal{Rad}(P)
surjects onto
P_{N}/\textnormal{Rad}(P_{N})
. But the former is simple by the first paragraph of this section, and so
P_{N}/\textnormal{Rad}(P_{N})
is simple. It follows that
P_{N}
is indecomposable.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
B
be a block of
G
with cyclic defect group
D
. There is
N_{0}\trianglelefteq_{O}G
acting trivially on each
S\in\mathcal{S}
and such that
B_{N}
is a block for each
N\leq N_{0}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
,
\mathcal{S}
is finite, and hence by
[
5
, Corollary 4.9]
there is
N_{0}\in\mathcal{N}
such that each simple module lies in
Z_{N_{0}}
. But each block of
B_{N_{0}}
contains at least one simple module, and hence
Z_{N_{0}}=B_{N_{0}}
. The same holds for any
N\leqslant N_{0}
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Fix
P=P_{S}\in\mathcal{P}
. The multiplicity of
P
as a factor of
B
is finite.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.2
,
P_{N}
is the projective cover of
S_{N}=S
for each
N\in\mathcal{N}
. The multiplicity of
P_{N}
as a summand of
B_{N}
is
\textnormal{dim}_{k}(S)
by
[
17
, Theorem 7.3.9]
,

and so
P
has the same multiplicity as a factor of
B
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

For each
P\in\mathcal{P}
,

\textnormal{Rad}(P)/\textnormal{Soc}(P)\cong\varprojlim_{N}\textnormal{Rad}(P_{N})/\textnormal{Soc}(P_{N}).

Proof.

Note that by
[
2
, Corollary 6.3.4]
and our hypotheses on
N\in\mathcal{N}
, the indecomposable projective

-modules are not simple, so that in particular
\textnormal{Soc}(P_{N})\leqslant\textnormal{Rad}(P_{N})
. The result now follows by applying Lemma
2.5
with
U=\textnormal{Rad}(P),\ V=\textnormal{Soc}(P)
and
W_{N}=\textnormal{Soc}(P_{N})
.
∎

3.1
Indecomposable projective modules of blocks with cyclic defect group

We maintain the notation fixed in the previous section. We denote by
\mathcal{N}
a cofinal set of open normal subgroups such that each

acts trivially on every simple
B
-module, and such that

is a block with defect group

. Observe that under these conditions,

can be canonically identified with

, via the map sending

Recall that a finite dimensional module is

if it has a unique composition series. We call a pseudocompact

-module

Lemma 3.6

Fix

are non-isomorphic simple modules or zero.

Proof.

are not simple for every
N
in some cofinal system inside

\displaystyle\textnormal{Rad}(P_{N})/\textnormal{Rad}^{2}(P_{N})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle=

\Gamma(B)

Proof.

Let

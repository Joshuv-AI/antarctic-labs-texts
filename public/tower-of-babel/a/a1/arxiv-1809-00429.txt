Abstract
. We establish existence, uniqueness and higher order weighted L p L_{p} -Sobolev regularity for the stochastic heat equation with zero Dirichlet boundary condition on angular domains and on polygonal domains in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} . We use a system of mixed weights consisting of appropriate powers of the distance to the vertexes and of the distance to the boundary to measure the regularity with respect to the space variable. In this way we can capture the influence of both main sources for singularities: the incompatibility between noise and boundary condition on the one hand and the singularities of the boundary on the other hand. The range of admissible powers of the distance to the vertexes is described in terms of the maximal interior angle and is sharp.

1.
Introduction

In this article we continue the analysis started in
[
2
]
towards a refined
L_{p}
-theory for second order stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs, for short) on non-smooth domains.
The main challenges in the construction of such a theory come from two effects that are known to influence the regularity of the solution:
On the one hand, the incompatibility between noise and boundary condition results in blow-ups of the higher order derivatives near the boundary—even if the boundary is smooth.
On the other hand, the singularities of the boundary cause a similar effect in their vicinity—even if the forcing terms are deterministic.
We refer to the introduction of
[
2
]
and the literature therein for details.

The well developed
L_{p}
-theory for second order SPDEs on smooth domains,
carried out within the analytic approach initiated by N.V. Krylov,
shows that the incompatibility between noise and boundary condition can be captured very accurately by using a system of weights based on the distance to the boundary, see, for instance,
[
4
,
6
,
14
,
18
,
19
]
.
Moreover, the results in
[
2
]
indicate that a system of weights based on the distance to a corner of the underlying domain is suitable to describe the impact of this boundary singularity on the solution.
Thus, in order to capture both effects, a system based on a combination of appropriate powers of the distance to the boundary and of the distance to the boundary singularities suggests itself.

Our primary goal in this article is to show how such a system of mixed weights can be used in order to provide higher order spatial weighted
L_{p}
-Sobolev regularity for second order SPDEs with zero Dirichlet boundary condition on angular domains and on polygonal domains in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
For the moment we restrict ourselves to the stochastic heat equation, since already the analysis of this equation involves many non-trivial steps and has been a persisting problem for a long time.
At the same time, we believe that in this way we can shade some light on the general strategy without getting lost in details.
Our general setting is as follows:
Let
(w^{k}_{t})
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, be a sequence of independent real-valued standard Brownian motions on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
and let
T\in(0,\infty)
be a finite time horizon.
We consider the stochastic heat equation

\left.\begin{aligned} du&=\big(\Delta&&u+f^{0}+f^{i}_{x_{i}}\big)\,dt+g^{k}\,dw^{k}_{t}\quad\text{on }\Omega\times(0,T]\times\mathcal{O},\\
u&=0&&\quad\text{on }\Omega\times(0,T]\times\partial\mathcal{O},\\
u(0)&=0&&\quad\text{on }\Omega\times\mathcal{O},\end{aligned}\right\}

(1.1)

on various types of domains
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Our focus lies in particular on polygonal domains and on angular domains
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Note that, as usual, here and in the sequel we use the Einstein summation convention on the repeated indexes
i
and
k
.

Our main results address the existence, uniqueness and higher order spatial regularity of the solution to Equation (
1.1
) on angular domains and on polygonal domains
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
By using a weight system based solely on the distance to the set of vertexes of
\mathcal{O}
, we establish existence and uniqueness of a solution to Equation (
1.1
) with suitable weighted
L_{p}
-Sobolev regularity of order one with respect to the space variable; see Theorem
2.8
(angular domains) and Theorem
5.4
(polygonal domains).
The lower bound of the range (
2.6
) for the weight parameter
\theta
, which corresponds to the best integrability property of the solution near the vertex, is sharp; see also the introduction of
[
2
]
.
Moreover, by using, in addition, appropriate powers of the distance to the boundary
\partial\mathcal{O}
we describe the behavior of higher order spatial derivatives of the solution; see Corollary
2.11
(angular domains) and Theorem
5.6
(polygonal domains).

The key estimate, which paves the way for all the results mentioned above, is presented in Theorem
2.5
.
Roughly speaking, it shows which system of weights is suitable in order to be able to lift the spatial regularity of the solution of the stochastic heat equation (
1.1
) on an angular domain with the regularity of the forcing terms. In short, it can be stated as follows:
Let

\mathcal{D}:=\mathcal{D}_{\kappa_{0}}:=\big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:x=(r\cos\vartheta,r\sin\vartheta),\;r>0,\;\vartheta\in(0,\kappa_{0})\big\},

(1.2)

be an angular domain with vertex at the origin and angle
\kappa_{0}\in(0,2\pi)
.
Moreover, let
\rho(x):=\rho_{\mathcal{D}}(x):=\mathrm{dist}(x,\partial\mathcal{D})
be the distance of a point
x\in\mathcal{D}
to the boundary
\partial\mathcal{D}
of
\mathcal{D}
. If
u
is the solution to Equation (
1.1
) on
\mathcal{D}
,
then, for arbitrary
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
1<\Theta<p+1
and
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
,
we can estimate

{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T}\bigg(\sum_{\lvert\alpha\rvert\leq m}\int_{\mathcal{D}}\big\lvert\rho(x)^{\lvert\alpha\rvert-1}D^{\alpha}u(t,x)\big\rvert^{p}\lvert x\rvert^{\theta-2}\bigg(\frac{\rho(x)}{\lvert x\rvert}\bigg)^{\Theta-2}\,dx\bigg)\,dt

by the weighted
L_{p}
-norm

{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{D}}\big\lvert\lvert x\rvert^{-1}u(t,x)\big\rvert^{p}\lvert x\rvert^{\theta-2}\bigg(\frac{\rho(x)}{\lvert x\rvert}\bigg)^{\Theta-2}\,dx\,dt

of the solution plus appropriate weighted
L_{p}
-Sobolev norms of the forcing terms
f^{0}
,
f^{i}
and
g
, of order
(m-2)\lor 0
,

, and
m-1
, respectively.
It is worth mentioning that the range for the parameter
\Theta
in this estimate is natural and sharp, see Remark
2.6
for details.

As already mentioned above, there already exists a comprehensive
L_{p}
-regularity theory for second order SPDEs in weighted Sobolev spaces with weights based solely on the distance to the boundary, see also
[
5
]
in addition to the reference given above. Typically, the solution
u
to Equation (
1.1
) on a domain
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
fulfills

{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T}\bigg(\sum_{\lvert\alpha\rvert\leq m}\int_{\mathcal{O}}\big\lvert\rho^{\lvert\alpha\rvert-1}D^{\alpha}u\big\rvert^{p}\rho^{\Theta-d}\,dx\bigg)\,dt<\infty,

provided that the domain
\mathcal{O}
is sufficiently smooth and the free terms
f^{0}
,
f^{i}
and
g
are in corresponding weighted Sobolev spaces. More precisely,
on smooth domains, i.e., at least
\mathcal{C}^{1}
, such a theory is possible with

d-1<\Theta<d+p-1,

see, e.g.,
[
4
, Remark 2.7]
.
However, on non-smooth domains, only
\Theta\in(d+p-2-\varepsilon,d+p-2+\varepsilon)
is possible with a small
\varepsilon>0
that depends on the roughness of the boundary of the domain and is not explicitly given
[
5
]
. In particular, for large
p>2
,
\Theta=d
is not admissible, see
[
5
, Example 2.17]
for a typical counterexample.
Our results show that, on polygonal domains, if we use an appropriate power of the distance to the set of vertexes to control the behavior of the solution in their proximate vicinity, then
\Theta=d=2
is possible away from the vertexes.

Our analysis takes place within the framework of the analytic approach.
The proofs of the main results rely on a mixture of Green function estimates on angular domains, suitable localization techniques and some delicate estimates for the stochastic heat equation on
\mathcal{C}^{1}
domains.
Alternatively, one could think of Equation (
1.1
) as an abstract Banach space valued stochastic evolution equation and try to obtain a similar theory by using the extension of the semigroup approach for SPDEs to Banach spaces developed by J.M.A.M. van Neerven, M.C. Veraar and L. Weis
[
24
,
25
,
26
]
. However, for this to succeed, one would have to (at least!) check whether the (properly defined) Dirichlet Laplacian on weighted Sobolev spaces has an appropriate functional calculus.
Moreover, one would need a description of the domain of the square root of this operator in terms of suitable weighted Sobolev spaces.
To the best of our knowledge, both questions are not trivial and yet to be answered.
In this context it is worth mentioning that the recently developed Calderón-Zygmund theory for singular stochastic integrals from
[
21
]
together with the
L_{p}
-theory developed in
[
2
]
lead to an
L_{q}(L_{p})
-theory with
q\neq p
without making use of precise descriptions of the domains of fractional powers of the Laplacian nor of the existence of a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus, see
[
21
, Example 8.12]
.

2.
The stochastic heat equation on angular domains

In this section we present our analysis for the stochastic heat equation

\displaystyle du=(\Delta u+f^{0}+f^{i}_{x^{i}})\,dt+g^{k}\,dw^{k}_{t},\quad t\in(0,T],

(2.1)

on angular domains
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
with zero Dirichlet boundary condition and vanishing initial value.
We establish existence and uniqueness (Theorem
2.8
) as well as higher order spatial regularity of the solution (Corollary
2.11
) within a framework of weighted Sobolev spaces.
The weights are products of appropriate powers of the distance to the vertex and of the distance to the boundary (two infinite edges and the vertex).
The key estimate, which enables us to describe the behavior of the higher order derivatives of
u
near the boundary even if the forcing terms behave badly near the boundary but are sufficiently smooth inside the domain, is presented in Theorem
2.5
, see also Remark
2.6
.

To state our results, we first introduce appropriate function spaces.
The notation is mainly borrowed from
[
2
]
. Throughout,
\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{D}_{\kappa_{0}}
is as defined in (
1.2
) with
\kappa_{0}\in(0,2\pi)
and
\rho_{\circ}(x):=\lvert x\rvert
denotes the distance of a point
x\in\mathcal{D}
to the origin (the only vertex of
\mathcal{D}
).
Let
p>1
and
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
.
We write

L^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D}):=L_{p}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{D}),\rho_{\circ}^{\theta-2}dx;{\mathbb{R}})\quad\text{and}\quad L^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D};\ell_{2}):=L_{p}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{D}),\rho_{\circ}^{\theta-2}dx;\ell_{2})

for the weighted
L_{p}
-spaces of real-valued and
\ell_{2}
-valued functions with weight
\rho_{\circ}^{\theta-2}
. For
n\in\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
let

K^{n}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D})=\bigg\{f:\lVert f\rVert_{K^{n}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D})}:=\bigg(\sum_{\lvert\alpha\rvert\leq n}\big\lVert\rho_{\circ}^{\lvert\alpha\rvert}D^{\alpha}f\big\rVert_{L_{p,\theta}^{[\circ]}(\mathcal{D})}^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}<\infty\bigg\},

and define
K^{n}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D};\ell_{2})
accordingly. Note that

K^{0}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D})=L^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D})\quad\text{and}\quad K^{0}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D};\ell_{2})=L^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D};\ell_{2}).

Moreover, we write
\mathring{K}^{1}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D})
for the closure in
K^{1}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D})
of the space
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathcal{D})
of test functions.

The weighted Sobolev spaces introduced above are classical examples of Kondratiev spaces. For their basic properties as well as their relevance in the analysis of elliptic partial differential equations on domains with conical singularities we refer to
[
11

, see also the pioneering works
[

In the sequel, we will frequently use the following basic properties.
They are mainly a consequence of the fact that
for any multi-index
\alpha

\sup_{\mathcal{D}}\left(\rho^{|\alpha|-1}_{\circ}|D^{\alpha}\rho_{\circ}|\right)\leq N(\alpha)<\infty;

the proof is left to the reader.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
p>1,\theta\in\mathbb{R}
and
n\geq 1
. If
\alpha
is a multi-index with

|\alpha|\leq n

\|\rho_{\circ}^{|\alpha|}D^{\alpha}f\|_{K^{n-|\alpha|}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D})}+\|D^{\alpha}(\rho^{|\alpha|}_{\circ}f)\|_{K^{n-|\alpha|}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D})}\leq N\|f\|_{K^{n}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D})},

and

\|f_{x}\|_{K^{n-1}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D})}\leq N\|f\|_{K^{n}_{p,\theta-p}(\mathcal{D})},

with
N
independent of

.

To formulate our conditions on the different parts of the equations, we will use the
L_{p}
-spaces of predictable stochastic processes on
\Omega_{T}:=\Omega\times(0,T]
taking values in the weighted Sobolev spaces introduced above.
For

,
\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and

\mathbb{K}^{n}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D},T):=L_{p}(\Omega_{T},\mathcal{P},\mathbb{P}\otimes dx;K^{n}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D})),

\mathbb{K}^{n}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D},T;\ell_{2}):=L_{p}(\Omega_{T},\mathcal{P},\mathbb{P}\otimes dx;K^{n}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D};\ell_{2})),

\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D},T):=\mathbb{K}^{0}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D},T),\quad\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D},T;\ell_{2}):=\mathbb{K}^{0}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D},T;\ell_{2}),

Using these spaces we introduce the following classes of stochastic processes that are tailor-made for the analysis of Equation (

Definition 2.2
.

For

in the sense of distributions with

Remark 2.3
.

The spaces

However, this is not really a difference, since

Remark 2.6

In the proof of Lemma

For this step to succeed, in particular to make sure that we maintain equality ‘for all

It also plays a crucial role in the proof of the existence result for the stochastic heat equation on polygonal domains (Theorem

Lemma 2.7
.

Let

(2.5)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i}\|f^{i}\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D},T)}+\|g\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D},T;\ell_{2})}\Big),

We have now all ingredients needed to state and prove our main existence and uniqueness result for Equation (

Theorem 2.8

(Existence and uniqueness/angular domains)

Let

p\left(1-\frac{\pi}{\kappa_{0}}\right)<\theta<p\left(1+\frac{\pi}{\kappa_{0}}\right).

\displaystyle u(t,x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{D}}\Gamma(t-s,x,y)f^{0}(s,y)\,dy\,ds-\sum_{i}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{D}}\Gamma_{y^{i}}(t-s,x,y)f^{i}(s,y)\,dy\,ds

is the unique solution to Equation (

) on
\mathcal{D}

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathbb{K}^{1}_{p,\theta-p}(\mathcal{D},T)}\leq N\Big(\|f^{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta+p}(\mathcal{D},T)}+\sum_{i}\|f^{i}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D},T)}+\|g\|_{\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D},T;\ell_{2})}\Big),

does not depend on

.

If

for

.
The extension presented here is essential to treat Equation (

) on polygons in Section

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
p\geq 2
and let
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
fulfill (
2.6
). Assume
f^{i}\in\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D},T)
for

and define

v(t,x):=-\sum_{i}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{D}}\Gamma_{y^{i}}(t-s,x,y)f^{i}(s,y)\,dy\,ds.

Then

is the unique solution in the class
\mathcal{K}^{1}_{p,\theta,0}(\mathcal{D},T)
to the equation

dv=(\Delta v+f^{i}_{x^{i}})\,dt,\quad t\in(0,T],

(2.8)

on
\mathcal{D}
.
Moreover, there exists a constant
N=N(p,\theta,\kappa_{0})
such that

\|v\|_{\mathbb{K}^{1}_{p,\theta-p}(\mathcal{D},T)}\leq N\sum_{i}\lVert f^{i}\rVert_{\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D},T)}.

(2.9)

In particular,
N
does not depend on
T
.

Step 1.
Let
f^{i}\in\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D},T)
,
i=1,2
. By
[
12
, Theorem 3.10]
, for any
0<\lambda<\frac{\pi}{\kappa_{0}}

(
=:\lambda^{\pm}_{c}
in
[
12
]
),

|\Gamma_{y}(t-s,x,y)|\leq N\bigg(\frac{\lvert x\rvert}{\lvert x\rvert+\sqrt{t-s}}\bigg)^{\lambda}\bigg(\frac{\lvert y\rvert}{\lvert y\rvert+\sqrt{t-s}}\bigg)^{\lambda-1}(t-s)^{-\frac{3}{2}}e^{-\frac{\sigma|x-y|^{2}}{t-s}},

where the constants
\sigma,N>0
depend only on
\kappa_{0}
and
\lambda
.
Since
\theta
satisfies (
2.6
), we can take
\lambda
sufficiently large such that
1-\lambda<\theta/p<1+\lambda
. Then the kernel

\mathcal{T}_{1}(t,s,x,y):=\mathds{1}_{x\in\mathcal{D}}\mathds{1}_{y\in\mathcal{D}}\mathds{1}_{t>s}|x|^{-1}\frac{|x|^{(\theta-2)/p}}{|y|^{(\theta-2)/p}}\Gamma_{y}(t-s,x,y)

satisfies the algebraic conditions in
[

, Proposition A.5]
with

,
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\lambda-1
and

. Hence by this proposition,

\|v\|_{\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta-p}(\mathcal{D},T)}=\|\rho_{\circ}^{-1}v\|_{\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D},T)}\leq N(p,\theta,\kappa_{0})\sum_{i}\|f^{i}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D},T)}.

Step 2.
Assume the
f^{i}
s are sufficiently nice, say,
f^{i}\in L_{p}(\Omega_{T},\mathcal{P};\mathcal{C}^{2}_{c}(\mathcal{D}))
. Then by

, Theorem 3.7]

v:=\sum_{i}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{D}}\Gamma(t-s,x,y)f^{i}_{x^{i}}(s,y)\,dy\,ds=-\sum_{i}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{D}}\Gamma_{y^{i}}(t-s,x,y)f^{i}(s,y)\,dy\,ds

is the unique solution to Equation (

) in the class
\mathcal{K}^{1}_{p,\theta,0}(\mathcal{D},T)
, see also
[
23

28

.
This, together with Step 1 and Theorem
2.5
with

and
\Theta=2
lead to (
2.9
) for

.

Step 3.
General
f^{i}\in\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D},T)
,
i=1,2
. Uniqueness follows from the case
f^{i}=0
. Take a sequence
(f^{i}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L_{p}(\Omega_{T},\mathcal{P};\mathcal{C}^{2}_{c}(\mathcal{D}))
such that

f^{i}_{n}\to f^{i}
in
\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D},T)
for each

. Let
v_{n}\in\mathcal{K}^{1}_{p,\theta,0}(\mathcal{D},T)
be the solution to Equation (
2.8
)
with

(v_{n})

\mathring{\mathbb{K}}^{1}_{p,\theta-p}(\mathcal{D},T)

Let

in
\mathring{\mathbb{K}}^{1}_{p,\theta-p}(\mathcal{D},T)
. Fix
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathcal{D})

Then taking the limit in

(v_{n}(t,\cdot),\varphi)=-\sum_{i}\int^{t}_{0}((v_{n}(s,\cdot))_{x^{i}}+f^{i}_{n}(s,\cdot),\varphi_{x^{i}})ds,\quad\forall\;t\leq T,\quad(\mathbb{P}\textup{-a.s.})

and using the continuity of

in

∎

Remark 2.13
.

To keep the presentation short, the results in this section are formulated only for angular domains
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
with vertex at the origin and with one of the edges being the positive
x^{1}
-axis.
However, since every angular domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
can be seen as a translation of a rotation of such a domain, all results can be extended accordingly, as the Laplace operator is invariant under translations and rotations.
More precisely, fix
a\in(-\pi,\pi)
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Let

\tilde{\mathcal{D}}:=\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\kappa_{0}}(x_{0},a):=\big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:x=x_{0}+(r\cos\vartheta,r\sin\vartheta),\;r>0,\;\vartheta\in(a,a+\kappa_{0})\big\}.

Replacing
\mathcal{D}
and
\rho_{\circ}
by
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\kappa_{0}}(x_{0},a)
and
\tilde{\rho}_{\circ}(x):=|x-x_{0}|
, respectively, in the definitions of the weighted Sobolev spaces from above, we can define analogous spaces, such as
K^{n}_{p,\theta}(\tilde{\mathcal{D}})
,
\mathbb{K}^{n}_{p,\theta}(\tilde{\mathcal{D}},T)
and

\mathcal{K}^{1}_{p,\theta,0}(\tilde{\mathcal{D}},T)
, on
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}
.
Then, the results in this section hold with
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}
in place of
\mathcal{D}
. Indeed,
let
Q=(q_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq 2}
be the orthogonal matrix such that
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{\kappa_{0}}(x_{0},a)=x_{0}+Q\mathcal{D}_{\kappa_{0}}
. Then, since the Laplacian is invariant under the rotations and translations, the statement that
u\in\mathcal{K}^{1}_{p,\theta,0}(\tilde{\mathcal{D}},T)
satisfies

du=(\Delta u+f^{0}+f^{i}_{x^{i}})\,dt+g^{k}dw^{k}_{t},

(2.10)

in the sense of distribution (analogous meaning to Definition
2.2
) is the same as the statement that
v(t,x):=u(t,x_{0}+Qx)\in\mathcal{K}^{1}_{p,\theta,0}(\mathcal{D},T)
satisfies

dv=(\Delta v+\tilde{f}^{0}+\tilde{f}^{i}_{x^{i}})\,dt+\tilde{g}^{k}\,dw^{k}_{t},

where

\tilde{f}^{0}(t,x)=f^{0}(t,x_{0}+Qx)
,
\tilde{f}^{i}(t,x)=q_{1i}f^{1}(t,x_{0}+Qx)+q_{2i}f^{2}(t,x_{0}+Qx)
,
i=1,2
,
and
\tilde{g}(t,x)=g(t,x_{0}+Qx)
.
Hence, all existence and uniqueness results as well as all estimates can be extended to general angular domains, since, obviously,

\|h(x)\|_{K^{n}_{p,\theta}(\tilde{\mathcal{D}})}\sim\|h(x_{0}+Qx)\|_{K^{n}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D})}

for any
h\in K^{n}_{p,\theta}(\tilde{\mathcal{D}})
.
To extend Lemma
2.7
, formally set
\Delta u=0
in (
2.10
).

3.
Proof of Theorem
2.5

In this section we give a detailed proof of the key estimate from Theorem
2.5
.
Our proof is based on a suitable a-priori estimate for the stochastic heat equation on
\mathcal{C}^{1}
domains, as presented in Lemma
3.6
below.
We use this result to establish an estimate for the solution on a subdomain of
\mathcal{D}
which is bounded away from the vertex and from infinity (see Lemma
3.7
below).
Then we can prove Theorem
2.5
by using a dilation argument, as
\mathcal{D}
is invariant under positive dilation.
For this strategy to succeed, it is crucial that the constant in Lemma
3.6
does not depend on the time horizon
T
.

We start with the definition of the weighted Sobolev spaces
H^{n}_{p,\Theta}(G)
on
\mathcal{C}^{1}
domains
G\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d\geq 1
), which we need for the statement of Lemma
3.6
. First we recall the definition of a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
domain.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
G
be a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 1
.
We write
\partial G\in\mathcal{C}^{1}_{u}
and say that
G
is a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
domain if there exist constants
r_{0},K_{0}\in(0,\infty)
such that
for any
x_{0}\in\partial G
there exists
a one-to-one continuously differentiable mapping
\Psi
of

B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})
onto a domain
J\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

(i)

J_{+}:=\Psi(B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})\cap G)\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
and

\Psi(x_{0})=0
;

(ii)

\Psi(B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})\cap\partial G)=J\cap\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:y^{1}=0\}
;

(iii)

\|\Psi\|_{\mathcal{C}^{1}(B_{r_{0}}(x_{0}))}\leq K_{0}
and

|\Psi^{-1}(y_{1})-\Psi^{-1}(y_{2})|\leq K_{0}|y_{1}-y_{2}|
for any
y_{i}\in J
;

(iv)

\Psi_{x}
is uniformly continuous in
B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})
.

Throughout this article, we assume that
G
is either
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon x^{1}>0\}
or a bounded
\mathcal{C}^{1}
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d\geq 1
). Note that in both cases,
G
is of class
\mathcal{C}^{1}_{u}
in the sense of
[
4
, Assumption 2.1]
.
Recall that
\rho(x)=\rho_{G}(x)=\mathrm{dist}(x,\partial G)
for
x\in G
;

\rho(x)=x^{1}
if
G=\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
.
For
p>1
and
\Theta\in\mathbb{R}
, we write

L_{p,\Theta}(G):=L_{p}(G,\rho^{\Theta-d}dx;{\mathbb{R}})\quad\text{and}\quad L_{p,\Theta}(G;\ell_{2}):=L_{p}(G,\rho^{\Theta-d}dx;\ell_{2})

for the weighted

-spaces of real-valued/
\ell_{2}
-valued functions with weight
\rho^{\Theta-d}
.
For

, by

we denote the space of all

such that

\|f\|^{p}_{H^{n}_{p,\Theta}(G)}:=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq n}\|\rho^{\lvert\alpha\rvert}D^{\alpha}f\|^{p}_{L_{p,\Theta}(G)}<\infty.

(3.1)

Moreover, we define the dual spaces

H^{-n}_{p,\Theta}(G):=\big(H^{n}_{p^{\prime},\Theta^{\prime}}(G)\big)^{*},\qquad\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1,\quad\frac{\Theta}{p}+\frac{\Theta^{\prime}}{p^{\prime}}=d.

The space
H^{n}_{p,\Theta}(G;\ell_{2})
is defined analogously for

.

To state the main properties of these spaces, we introduce some additional notation.
For
k\in\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
, let

|f|^{(0)}_{k}:=|f|^{(0)}_{k,G}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in G\\
|\beta|\leq k\end{subarray}}\rho^{|\beta|}(x)|D^{\beta}f(x)|.

If
G
is bounded, let
\psi
be a bounded
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
function defined in
G
with
|\psi|^{(0)}_{k}+|\psi_{x}|^{(0)}_{k}<\infty
for any

, which is comparable to
\rho

N^{-1}\rho(x)\leq\psi(x)\leq N\rho(x)
for some constant

; see, e.g.,
[
6

.
It is known that, if
G
is bounded, then the map
\Psi
in Definition
3.1
can be chosen in such a way that
\Psi
is infinitely differentiable in

and for any multi-index
\alpha

(3.2)

see, e.g.,
[
6
]
or the proof of
[
7
, Lemma 4.9]
.
Actually, after appropriate rotation and translation, one can take
\Psi(x^{1},x^{\prime})=(\psi(x),x^{\prime})
. By
[
22
, Theorem 3.2]
and (
3.2
) above, if
\text{supp}\,u\subset B_{r}(x_{0})\cap\overline{G}
and
r<r_{0}/{K_{0}}
, then for any
\nu,\Theta\in\mathbb{R}
and

, we have

\|\psi^{\nu}u\|_{H^{n}_{p,\Theta}(G)}\sim\|(x^{1})^{\nu}(u\circ\Psi^{-1})\|_{H^{n}_{p,\Theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}.

(3.3)

Here are some other properties of the spaces
H^{n}_{p,\Theta}(G)
taken from
[
22
]
(see also
[
6
,
16
]
). If

Lemma 3.2
.

(i)

\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(G)
is dense in

(ii)

For any

(iii)

For any

,

(3.4)

(iv)

For

and

(vi)

and

N^{-1}\|u\|_{H^{n}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\|u\|_{H^{n}_{p,\Theta}(G)}\leq N\|u\|_{H^{n}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

where

if

, and otherwise it is the dual space of

Note that, by Lemma

and the properties of

For the corresponding spaces of predictable

\mathbb{H}^{n}_{p,\Theta}(G,T):=L_{p}(\Omega_{T},\mathcal{P};H^{n}_{p,\Theta}(G))\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{L}_{p,\Theta}(G,T):=\mathbb{H}^{0}_{p,\Theta}(G,T),

as well as

\mathbb{H}^{n}_{p,\Theta}(G,T;\ell_{2}):=L_{p}(\Omega_{T},\mathcal{P};H^{n}_{p,\Theta}(G;\ell_{2}))\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{L}_{p,\Theta}(G,T;\ell_{2}):=\mathbb{H}^{0}_{p,\Theta}(G,T;\ell_{2}).

The following classes of stochastic processes are tailor-made for the analysis of Equation (
2.1
) on
G
.

Definition 3.3
.

For
p\geq 2
and
\Theta\in\mathbb{R}
we write
u\in\mathfrak{H}^{n}_{p,\Theta,0}(G,T)
if

u\in\mathbb{H}^{n}_{p,\Theta-p}(G,T)

and
there exist

f\in\mathbb{H}^{n-2}_{p,\Theta+p}(G,T)
and

g\in\mathbb{H}^{n-1}_{p,\Theta}(G,T;\ell_{2})

such that

du=f\,dt+g^{k}\,dw^{k}_{t},\quad t\in(0,T],

on
G
in the sense of distributions with
u(0,\cdot)=0
; see Definition
2.2
accordingly. We denote

\mathbb{D}u:=f\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathbb{S}u:=g.

In this article, Equation (
2.1
) has the following meaning on
G
.

Definition 3.4
.

We say that
u\in\mathbb{H}^{n}_{p,\Theta-p}(G,T)
is
a solution to Equation (
2.1
) on
G

in the class
\mathfrak{H}^{n}_{p,\Theta,0}(G,T)

if

u\in\mathfrak{H}^{n}_{p,\Theta,0}(G,T)
with

\mathbb{D}u=\Delta u+f^{0}+f^{i}_{x^{i}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathbb{S}u=g.

Remark 3.5
.

All definitions above are given only for
\mathcal{C}^{1}
domains, as we say from the beginning that in this article
G
is either a bounded
\mathcal{C}^{1}
domain or the half plane. However, all the spaces defined above as well as the solution concept make sense on any domain
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with non-empty boundary.

Now we have all notions we need in order to state and prove the a-priori estimate for Equation (
2.1
) on bounded
\mathcal{C}^{1}
domains that we use to prove Lemma
3.7
and therefore Theorem
2.5
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded
\mathcal{C}^{1}
domain,
p\geq 2
,
n\in\{-1,0,1,\ldots\}
, and
d-1<\Theta<d-1+p
. Moreover, let
f^{0}\in\mathbb{H}^{n}_{p,\Theta+p}(G,T)
,
f^{i}\in\mathbb{H}^{n+1}_{p,\Theta}(G,T)
,
i=1,2
, and
g\in\mathbb{H}^{n+1}_{p,\Theta}(G,T;\ell_{2})
.
Assume
u
is a solution to Equation (
2.1
) on
G

in the class
\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{p,\Theta_{1},0}(G,T)
for some
\Theta_{1}\in[\Theta,d+p-1)
. Then

u\in\mathfrak{H}^{n+2}_{p,\Theta,0}(G,T)
and

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{n+2}_{p,\Theta-p}(G,T)}

\displaystyle\leq N\Big(\|u\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p,\Theta}(G,T)}+\|f^{0}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{p,\Theta+p}(G,T)}

(3.5)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i}\|f^{i}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}_{p,\Theta}(G,T)}+\|g\|_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}_{p,\Theta}(G,T;\ell_{2})}\Big),

where
N=N(p,d,\Theta,n,G)
. In particular,
N
does not depend on
T
.

Step 1.
First we prove that
u\in\mathfrak{H}^{n+2}_{p,\Theta}(G,T)
.
By

[
4
, Theorem 2.9]
, under the given assumptions, there exists a solution
v\in\mathfrak{H}^{n+2}_{p,\Theta}(G,T)
. Since
G
is bounded, by Lemma
3.2
(v)
,

\mathbb{H}^{n+2}_{p,\Theta-p}(G,T)\subset\mathbb{H}^{1}_{p,\Theta-p}(G,T)\subset\mathbb{H}^{1}_{p,\Theta_{1}-p}(G,T),

and therefore
v\in\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{p,\Theta_{1}}(G,T)
.
By the uniqueness part of
[
4
, Theorem 2.9]
we get
u=v
(in

\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{p,\Theta_{1}}(G,T)
).

Step 2.
We prove Estimate (
3.5
).
In fact, by
[
4
, Theorem 2.9]
, this estimate holds even without the term
\|u\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p,\Theta}(G,T)}
on the right hand side if we allow the constant
N
to depend on
T
.
However, a close look at the proof of
[
4
, Theorem 2.9]
reveals that, indeed, if we leave this term on the right hand side, the constant can be kept independent of
T
, since the dependence on
T
comes in only in the very last step of the relevant part of the proof of
[
4
, Theorem 2.9]
, when a Gronwall argument is used in order to get rid of the terms that depend on
u
on the right hand side.

Instead of reproving
[
4
, Theorem 2.9]
, we illustrate the relevant steps in the proof and the changes required to obtain independence of
T
.
The key estimate is (5.6) of
[
4
]
, which says that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{n+2}_{p,\Theta-p}(G,T)}

\displaystyle\leq N\Big(\|\psi u_{x}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{p,\Theta}(G,T)}+\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{p,\Theta}(G,T)}

(3.6)

\displaystyle+\|\psi(f^{0}+f^{i}_{x^{i}})\|_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{p,\Theta}(G,T)}+\|g\|_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}_{p,\Theta}(G,T;\ell_{2})}\Big).

In our setting, i.e., for the stochastic heat equation, the constant
N
in this estimate does not depend on
T
.
Indeed, as explained in detail in the proof of
[
4
, Theorem 2.9]
, by using a suitable partition of unity, Estimate (
3.6
) is obtained through a combination of an a-priori estimate on the half space (
[
4
, Theorem 2.10]
) and its analogue on the entire space (
[
15
, Theorem 5.1]
).
The former theorem does not add any dependence on
T
as the constant therein is explicitly proven to be independent of
T
.
This is different for
[
15
, Theorem 5.1]
: The constant therein may indeed depend on
T
.
However, this dependence only occurs if we consider equations with variable coefficients.
For the stochastic heat equation we may use the a-priori estimate from
[
15
, Theorem 4.2]
instead, which holds with a constant that does not depend on
T
.
Note that on the left hand side of the estimate in
[
15
, Theorem 4.2]
, we have the
L_{p}
-norm of the second order derivatives of the solution.
However, this is not a problem since in the proof of (
3.6
) we only use this estimate for the solution of a stochastic heat equation with compact support in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and, due to Poincaré’s inequality, for a function
v
with compact support in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the norms
\|v\|_{H^{n+2}_{p}}
,
\sum_{i}\|v_{x^{i}}\|_{H^{n+1}_{p}}
and
\sum_{i,j}\|v_{x^{i}x^{j}}\|_{H^{n}_{p}}
are all equivalent.

To derive (
3.5
) from (
3.6
) we argue as follows: Using (
3.6
) and the basic properties of the weighted Sobolev spaces
H^{n}_{p,\Theta}(G)
from Lemma
3.2
, we easily obtain

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{n+2}_{p,\Theta-p}(G,T)}

\displaystyle\leq N\Big(\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}_{p,\Theta}(G,T)}+\|f^{0}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{p,\Theta+p}(G,T)}

(3.7)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i}\|f^{i}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}_{p,\Theta}(G,T)}+\|g\|_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}_{p,\Theta}(G,T;\ell_{2})}\Big).

Thus if
n=-1
, then (
3.5
) is proved. If
n\geq 0
, then another application of Lemma
3.2
(v)
shows that (
3.7
) implies

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{n+2}_{p,\Theta-p}(G,T)}

\displaystyle\leq N\Big(\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}_{p,\Theta-p}(G,T)}+\|f^{0}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{p,\Theta+p}(G,T)}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i}\|f^{i}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}_{p,\Theta}(G,T)}+\|g\|_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}_{p,\Theta}(G,T;\ell_{2})}\Big),

which means that we can control
\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{n+2}_{p,\Theta-p}(G,T)}
by
\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}_{p,\Theta-p}(G,T)}
and suitable norms of the free terms.
After repeating this step for
n
more times, we arrive at

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{n+2}_{p,\Theta-p}(G,T)}

\displaystyle\leq N\Big(\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}_{p,\Theta-p}(G,T)}+\|f^{0}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{p,\Theta+p}(G,T)}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i}\|f^{i}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}_{p,\Theta}(G,T)}+\|g\|_{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}_{p,\Theta}(G,T;\ell_{2})}\Big).

Estimate (
3.5
) follows by applying (
3.7
) with
n=-1
. Note that all constants in the estimates above are independent of
T
.
∎

Now we go back to
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
and proceed to prove Theorem
2.5
. The key step is presented in Lemma
3.7
below. It provides an estimate of suitable weighted
L_{p}
-norms of the derivatives of the solution in

U_{1}:=\{x\in\mathcal{D}\,\colon\,1<\lvert x\rvert<4\}

by appropriate weighted
L_{p}
-norms of
u
and of the derivatives of the free terms on the slightly bigger domain

V_{1}:=\{x\in\mathcal{D}\,\colon\,1/2<\lvert x\rvert<8\}.

As these domains are bounded away from the vertex, the estimate involves only the distance
\rho
to the boundary.
It is crucial that the constant in Lemma
3.7
below does not depend on
T
. In the proof we use Lemma
3.6
with
d=2
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
p\geq 2
,
1<\Theta<p+1
, and
m\in\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
. Moreover, let
u\in\mathcal{K}^{1}_{p,\theta,0}(\mathcal{D},T)
be a solution to Equation (
2.1
) on
\mathcal{D}
for some
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{\lvert\alpha\rvert\leq m+1}\int_{U_{1}}\big\lvert\rho^{\lvert\alpha\rvert-1}D^{\alpha}u\big\rvert^{p}\rho^{\Theta-2}\,dx\,dt

(3.8)

\displaystyle\leq N\,{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{V_{1}}\bigg(\lvert u\rvert^{p}+\sum_{\lvert\alpha\rvert\leq(m-1)\vee 0}\big\lvert\rho^{\lvert\alpha\rvert+1}D^{\alpha}f^{0}\big\rvert^{p}+\sum_{\lvert\alpha\rvert\leq m}\sum_{i}\big\lvert\rho^{\lvert\alpha\rvert}D^{\alpha}f^{i}\big\rvert^{p}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\lvert\alpha\rvert\leq m}\big\lvert\rho^{\lvert\alpha\rvert}D^{\alpha}g\big\rvert_{\ell_{2}}^{p}\bigg)\rho^{\Theta-2}\,dx\,dt,

where
N=N(p,\Theta,\kappa_{0},m)
. In particular,
N
does not depend on
T
.

Assume that the integrals on the right hand side of (
3.8
) are finite (if not, the statement is trivial).
Fix a constant
\varepsilon\in(0,1/4)
, and for
k=1,2,3
, let

U^{k}_{1}:=\{x\in\mathcal{D}:2^{-k\varepsilon}<|x|<2^{2+k\varepsilon}\}.

Choose a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
radial non-negative function
\eta=\eta(|x|)
such that
\eta(t)=1
for
t\in[1,4]
and
\eta(t)=0
if
t\not\in[2^{-\varepsilon},2^{2+\varepsilon}]
. Also choose a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
domain
G\subset\mathcal{D}
such that

U^{2}_{1}\subset G\subset U^{3}_{1}\subset V_{1}.

By the choice of
\eta
and
G
,
u\eta
vanishes on the boundary of
G
and there exists
N=N(\varepsilon)
such that for all
x\in\mathcal{D}\cap\text{supp}\,\eta
,

N^{-1}\rho_{\mathcal{D}}(x)\leq\rho_{G}(x)\leq N\rho_{\mathcal{D}}(x).

(3.9)

Let
l:=2m+3
and set
\zeta:=\eta^{l}
.
Then

d(\zeta u)=\big(\Delta(\zeta u)+u\Delta\zeta-f^{i}\zeta_{x^{i}}+f^{0}\zeta+(-2u\zeta_{x^{i}}+f^{i}\zeta)_{x^{i}}\big)dt+\zeta g^{k}dw^{k}_{t},\quad t\in(0,T],

on
G
in the sense of distributions.
Moreover,
\zeta u\in\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{p,2,0}(G,T)
since
u\in\mathcal{K}^{1}_{p,\theta,0}(\mathcal{D},T)
solves Equation (
2.1
) on
\mathcal{D}
and since, by Hardy’s inequality,

\displaystyle\lVert\zeta u\rVert_{L_{p,2-p}(G)}\leq N\,\lVert(\zeta u)_{x}\rVert_{L_{p,2}(G)}\leq N\,\lVert\zeta u\rVert_{K^{1}_{p,\theta-p}(\mathcal{D})}\leq N(p,\theta,G,\zeta)\,\lVert u\rVert_{K^{1}_{p,\theta-p}(\mathcal{D})}.

The second inequality above is due to
\rho_{0}\sim 1
on the support of
\zeta
.
Thus, by an application of Lemma
3.6
with
n=m-1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\zeta u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{m+1}_{p,\Theta-p}(G,T)}

\displaystyle\leq N\Big(\sum_{i}\|\zeta f^{i}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{m}_{p,\Theta}(G,T)}+\sum_{i}\|u\zeta_{x^{i}}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{m}_{p,\Theta}(G,T)}

\displaystyle+\|\zeta f^{0}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{m-1}_{p,\Theta+p}(G,T)}+\|u\Delta\zeta\|_{\mathbb{H}^{m-1}_{p,\Theta+p}(G,T)}

\displaystyle+\|\zeta g\|_{\mathbb{H}^{m}_{p,\Theta}(G,T;\ell_{2})}+\|\zeta u\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p,\Theta}(G,T)}\Big),

once we can prove that all norms on the right hand side are finite.
The norms that do not involve
u
together with
\|\zeta u\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p,\Theta}(G,T)}
can be estimated by

\displaystyle\|\eta u\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p,\Theta}(G,T)}+\|\eta f^{0}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{(m-1)\vee 0}_{p,\Theta+p}(G,T)}+\|\eta g\|_{\mathbb{H}^{m}_{p,\Theta}(G,T;\ell_{2})}+\sum_{i}\|\eta f^{i}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{m}_{p,\Theta}(G,T)},

which is finite since the right hand side of (
3.8
) is finite (use (
3.9
), Lemma
3.2
(in particular, part
(iv)
) and the properties of
\eta
to estimate the norms above by the right hand side of (
3.8
)).
Moreover, since

\|u\zeta_{x^{i}}\|_{H^{m}_{p,\Theta}(G)}=l\,\|u\eta^{l-1}\eta_{x^{i}}\|_{H^{m}_{p,\Theta}(G)}\leq N\|u\eta^{l-2}\|_{H^{m}_{p,\Theta}(G)},

and

\|u\Delta\zeta\|_{H^{m-1}_{p,\Theta+p}(G)}\leq N\|u\eta^{l-2}\|_{H^{m}_{p,\Theta}(G)},

the condition

\|\eta^{l-2}u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{m}_{p,\Theta}(G,T)}<\infty

(3.10)

is sufficient in order be able to apply Lemma
3.6
and obtain

\displaystyle\|\eta^{l}u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{m+1}_{p,\Theta-p}(G,T)}

\displaystyle\leq N\Big(\|\eta^{l-2}u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{m}_{p,\Theta}(G,T)}+\|\eta u\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p,\Theta}(G,T)}

\displaystyle\quad\,\,+\sum_{i}\|\eta f^{i}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{m}_{p,\Theta}(G,T)}+\|\eta f^{0}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{m-1}_{p,\Theta+p}(G,T)}+\|\eta g\|_{\mathbb{H}^{m}_{p,\Theta}(G,T;\ell_{2})}\Big).

In particular,
this shows that

\lVert\eta^{l-2}u\rVert_{\mathbb{H}^{m}_{p,\Theta}(G,T)}<\infty\quad\Rightarrow\quad\lVert\eta^{l}u\rVert_{\mathbb{H}^{m+1}_{p,\Theta-p}(G,T)}<\infty.

In order to prove that (
3.10
) holds, we argue as follows:
Since
\|\eta^{l-2}u\|_{H^{m}_{p,\Theta}(G)}\leq N\|\eta^{l-2}u\|_{H^{m}_{p,\Theta-p}(G)}
,
we can iterate the arguments above with
m
replaced by
m-j
and
l
replaced by
l-2j
successively for
j=1,\ldots,m
. After finitely many steps we arrive at the statement that if

\|\eta u\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p,\Theta}(G,T)}<\infty
, then

\displaystyle\|\eta^{2m+3}u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{m+1}_{p,\Theta-p}(G,T)}

\displaystyle\leq N\Big(\|\eta u\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p,\Theta}(G,T)}+\sum_{i}\|\eta f^{i}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{m}_{p,\Theta}(G,T)}

(3.11)

\displaystyle+\|\eta f^{0}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{(m-1)\vee 0}_{p,\Theta+p}(G,T)}+\|\eta g\|_{\mathbb{H}^{m}_{p,\Theta}(G,T;\ell_{2})}\Big).

But, as already explained above,
\|\eta u\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p,\Theta}(G,T)}
is indeed finite since the right hand side of (
3.8
) is assumed to be finite.
Therefore, Estimate (
3.11
) holds.
Moreover, it proves (
3.8
), since due to (
3.9
) and that fact that
\eta=1
on
U_{1}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m+1}\int_{U_{1}}\big\lvert\rho^{\lvert\alpha\rvert-1}D^{\alpha}u\big\rvert^{p}\rho^{\Theta-2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m+1}\int_{G}\big\lvert\rho^{\lvert\alpha\rvert-1}D^{\alpha}(u\eta^{2m+3})\big\rvert^{p}\rho^{\Theta-2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq N\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m+1}\int_{G}\big\lvert\rho^{\lvert\alpha\rvert-1}_{G}D^{\alpha}(u\eta^{2m+3})\big\rvert^{p}\rho^{\Theta-2}_{G}\,dx

\displaystyle=N\lVert\eta^{2m+3}u\rVert_{H^{m+1}_{p,\Theta-p}(G)}^{p},

and, as already mentioned above, the right hand side of (
3.11
) can be estimated from above by the right hand side of (
3.8
).
Note that all constants in the estimates above are independent of
T
.
∎

Now we can prove Theorem
2.5
by applying Lemma
3.7
to
u_{n}(t,x):=u(2^{2n}t,2^{n}x)
for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and summing up the resulting estimates.

For every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
u_{n}(t,x):=u(2^{2n}t,2^{n}x)
,
x\in\mathcal{D}
,
t\leq 2^{-2n}T
. Since

u\in\mathcal{K}^{1}_{p,\theta,0}(\mathcal{D},T)
solves Equation (
2.1
) on
\mathcal{D}
, for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
u_{n}\in\mathcal{K}^{1}_{p,\theta,0}(\mathcal{D},2^{-2n}T)
and

\displaystyle du_{n}=\big(\Delta u_{n}+2^{2n}f^{0}_{n}+2^{n}(f^{i}_{n})_{x^{i}}\big)\,dt+2^{n}g^{k}_{n}\,d(2^{-n}w^{k}_{2^{2n}t}),\qquad t\in(0,2^{-2n}T],

on
\mathcal{D}
in the sense of distributions with
u_{n}(0,\cdot)=0
and

f^{0}_{n}(t,x)=f^{0}(2^{2n}t,2^{n}x),\,\,f^{i}_{n}(t,x)=f^{i}(2^{2n}t,2^{n}x),\,\,\text{ and }\,\,g_{n}(t,x)=g(2^{2n}t,2^{n}x).

Note that
(2^{-n}w^{k}_{2^{2n}t})_{t\geq 0}
,
k=1,2,\ldots
, is a sequence of independent one-dimensional Wiener processes.
By Lemma
3.7
applied to
u_{n}
for
t\leq 2^{-2n}T
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}

\displaystyle\int^{2^{-2n}T}_{0}\;\sum_{k=0}^{m+1}\int_{U_{1}}|\rho^{k-1}(x)2^{nk}D^{k}u(2^{2n}t,2^{n}x)|^{p}\rho^{\Theta-2}(x)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq N\;{\mathbb{E}}\int^{2^{-2n}T}_{0}\int_{V_{1}}\bigg(\;|u(2^{2n}t,2^{n}x)|^{p}

\displaystyle\hskip 96.73918pt+\sum_{k=0}^{(m-1)\vee 0}|\rho^{k+1}(x)2^{2n}2^{nk}D^{k}f^{0}(2^{2n}t,2^{n}x)|^{p}

\displaystyle\hskip 96.73918pt+\sum_{k=0}^{m}\sum_{i}|\rho^{k}(x)2^{n}2^{nk}D^{k}f^{i}(2^{2n}t,2^{n}x)|^{p}

\displaystyle\hskip 96.73918pt+\sum_{k=0}^{m}|\rho^{k}(x)2^{n}2^{nk}D^{k}g(2^{2n}t,2^{n}x)|^{p}_{\ell_{2}}\;\bigg)\rho^{\Theta-2}(x)\,dx\,dt.

Thus, if for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,

U_{n}:=\{x\in\mathcal{D}:2^{n-1}<|x|<2^{n+1}\}\quad\text{and}\quad V_{n}:=\{x\in\mathcal{D}:2^{n-2}<|x|<2^{n+2}\},

then, multiplying both sides by
2^{n(\theta-2-p)}
, changing variables
(2^{2n}t,2^{n}x)\to(t,x)
, and using the relations
\rho(2^{-n}x)=2^{-n}\rho(x)
and
|x|\sim 2^{n}
on
U_{n-1}
and on
V_{n-1}
, we get

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\int^{T}_{0}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m+1}\int_{U_{n-1}}|\rho^{k-1}D^{k}u|^{p}\rho_{\circ}^{\theta-2}\left(\frac{\rho}{\rho_{\circ}}\right)^{\Theta-2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq N\;{\mathbb{E}}\int^{T}_{0}\int_{V_{n-1}}\bigg(|\,\rho_{\circ}^{-1}u|^{p}+\sum_{k=0}^{(m-1)\vee 0}|\rho^{k+1}D^{k}f^{0}|^{p}

\displaystyle\hskip 91.04872pt+\sum_{k=0}^{m}\sum_{i}|\rho^{k}D^{k}f^{i}|^{p}+|\rho^{k}D^{k}g|^{p}_{\ell_{2}}\bigg)\rho_{\circ}^{\theta-2}\bigg(\frac{\rho}{\rho_{\circ}}\bigg)^{\Theta-2}\,dx\,dt.

By summing up with respect to
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we obtain the desired result.
∎

The following uniqueness result on bounded
\mathcal{C}^{1}
domains will be used in Section
5
to treat the stochastic heat equation on polygons.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
G
be a bounded
\mathcal{C}^{1}
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For
j=1,2
, let
p_{j}\geq 2
and
\Theta_{j}\in(d-1,d-1+p_{j})
, and assume that
u\in\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{p_{1},\Theta_{1},0}(G,T)
is a solution to Equation (
2.1
) on
G
with
f^{0}
,
f^{i}
and
g
satisfying

f^{0}\in\mathbb{L}_{p_{j},\Theta_{j}+p_{j}}(G,T)\cap\mathbb{L}_{p_{j},d+p_{j}}(G,T),\quad f^{i}\in\mathbb{L}_{p_{j},\Theta_{j}}(G,T)\cap\mathbb{L}_{p_{j},d}(G,T),\;i=1,2,

g\in\mathbb{L}_{p_{j},\Theta_{j}}(G,T;\ell_{2})\cap\mathbb{L}_{p_{j},d}(G,T;\ell_{2}),

for

By
[
4
, Theorem 2.9]
, we can define
v_{1}
and

and it belongs to
\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{q_{1},d,0}(G,T)

(v) and the uniqueness in
\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{p_{1},\Theta_{1}\vee d,0}(G,T)

. Now let
v_{3}
be the solution to the problem in
\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{p_{2},\Theta_{2}.0}(G,T)
. The same argument as above shows

.
∎

4.
Proof of Lemma
2.7

In this section we prove the second key auxiliary result of this article, Lemma
2.7
.
Throughout, we take and fix a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
radial function
\eta
and a corresponding
\mathcal{C}^{1}
domain
G\subseteq\mathcal{D}
as in the proof of Lemma
3.7
. Recall that
\eta(t)=1
for
1\leq t\leq 4
. As a consequence, there exists a constant
c>0
such that

\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\eta(e^{n+t})>c>0,\quad\forall\,t\in\mathbb{R}.

(4.1)

Our proof of Lemma
2.7
relies on the the following characterization of the
L_{p,\theta}^{[\circ]}(\mathcal{D})
-norm.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
p>1
and
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
u\colon\mathcal{D}\to\mathbb{R}
be a measurable function.

(i)

If
\eta
and
G
are as above, then

\|u\|^{p}_{L^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D})}\sim\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{n\theta}\|\eta(|x|)u(e^{n}x)\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathcal{D})}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{n\theta}\|\eta(|x|)u(e^{n}x)\|^{p}_{L_{p}(G)}.

(ii)

For any function
\xi\in C^{\infty}_{0}((0,\infty))
we have

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{n\theta}\|\xi(|x|)u(e^{n}x)\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathcal{D})}\leq N(\xi,\eta,p,\theta)\|u\|^{p}_{L^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D})}.

To see
(i)
, it is enough to repeat the proof of
[
16
, Remark 1.3]
. Indeed, by the change of variables
e^{n}x\to x
,

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{n\theta}\|\eta(|x|)u(e^{n}x)\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathcal{D})}=\int_{\mathcal{D}}\zeta(x)|u(x)|^{p}dx,

where

\zeta(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{n(\theta-2)}\eta^{p}(e^{-n}|x|)\sim|x|^{\theta-2},

see
[
16
, Remark 1.3]
. Moreover, since
\mathrm{supp}\,\eta\cap\mathcal{D}\subset G
, the equality in
(i)
is also satisfied. Part
(ii)

holds since

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{n(\theta-2)}\xi^{p}(e^{-n}|x|)\leq N(\xi,\eta,\theta,p)|x|^{\theta-2};

see
[
16
, Lemma 1.4]
for details.
∎

In addition to Lemma
4.1
, we also need the following counterpart of Lemma
2.7
for the stochastic heat equation on bounded
\mathcal{C}^{1}
domains.
In the proof, we are going to use the common abbreviations

\mathbb{H}^{n}_{p}(T):=L_{p}(\Omega_{T},\mathcal{P};H^{n}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{L}_{p}(T;\ell_{2}):=L_{p}(\Omega_{T},\mathcal{P};L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\ell_{2})),

for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
G
be a bounded
\mathcal{C}^{1}
domain,
\Theta\in\mathbb{R}
,
p\geq 2
, and
u\in\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{p,\Theta,0}(G,T)
with
du=fdt+g\,dw^{k}_{t}
. Then
u\in L_{p}(\Omega;\mathcal{C}([0,T];L_{p,\Theta}(G))
, and for any
c>0
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{t\leq T}\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{p}_{L_{p,\Theta}(G)}\leq N\Big(c\|u\|^{p}_{\mathbb{H}^{1}_{p,\Theta-p}(G,T)}+c^{-1}\|f\|^{p}_{\mathbb{H}^{-1}_{p,\Theta+p}(G,T)}+\|g\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}_{p,\Theta}(G,T;\ell_{2})}\Big),

where
N=N(d,p,\theta,G,T)
. In particular, if
f=f^{0}+f^{i}_{x^{i}}
, then the right hand side above is bounded by a constant multiple of

c\|u\|^{p}_{\mathbb{H}^{1}_{p,\Theta-p}(G,T)}+c^{-1}\|f^{0}\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}_{p,\Theta+p}(G,T)}+c^{-1}\|f^{i}\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}_{p,\Theta}(G,T)}+\|g\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}_{p,\Theta}(G,T;\ell_{2})}.

Introduce a partition of unity
\zeta_{0},\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2},\cdots,\zeta_{M}

of
G
such that
\zeta_{0}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(G)
and

\zeta_{j}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(B_{r}(x_{j}))

(j=1,2,\cdots,M)
, where

x_{j}\in\partial G
and
r<r_{0}/K_{0}
.
For any
\Theta\in\mathbb{R}
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
v\in H^{m}_{p,\Theta}(G)
, since
\zeta_{0}
has compact support in
G
, we can consider
\zeta_{0}v
as a function defined on the entire space, so that by Lemma
3.2
(vi)
,

\|\zeta_{0}v\|_{H^{m}_{p,\Theta}(G)}\sim\|\zeta_{0}v\|_{H^{m}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(4.2)

Also, by (
3.3
), for
j\geq 1
and any
\Theta,\nu\in\mathbb{R}
,

\|\psi^{\nu}\zeta_{j}v\|^{p}_{H^{m}_{p,\Theta}(G)}\sim\|(x^{1})^{\nu}(\zeta_{j}v)(\Psi^{-1}_{j})\|^{p}_{H^{m}_{p,\Theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})},

(4.3)

where
\Psi_{j}
is the corresponding mapping from Definition
3.1
related to
x_{j}\in\partial G
.
Thus

\displaystyle\|v\|^{p}_{H^{m}_{p,\Theta}(G)}

\displaystyle=\Big\lVert\sum_{j=0}^{M}\zeta_{j}v\Big\rVert_{{H^{m}_{p,\Theta}(G)}}^{p}\sim\sum_{j=0}^{M}\|\zeta_{j}v\|^{p}_{H^{m}_{p,\Theta}(G)}

(4.4)

\displaystyle\sim\|\zeta_{0}v\|^{p}_{H^{m}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\sum_{j=1}^{M}\|(\zeta_{j}v)(\Psi^{-1})\|^{p}_{H^{m}_{p,\Theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}.

As a consequence,

{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{t\leq T}\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{p}_{L_{p,\Theta}(G)}\leq N\bigg({\mathbb{E}}\sup_{t\leq T}\|\zeta_{0}u\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\sum_{j=1}^{M}{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{t\leq T}\|(\zeta_{j}u)(\Psi^{-1}_{j})\|^{p}_{L_{p,\Theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}\bigg).

Therefore, in order to obtain the desired estimate and the continuity assertion, it is enough to estimate the terms on the right hand side appropriately and to prove continuity of
\zeta_{j}u
,
j=0,\ldots,M
.
For the first term, note that

d(\zeta_{0}u)=\zeta_{0}f\,dt+\zeta_{0}g\,dw^{k}_{t},\quad\,t\in(0,T],

on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\zeta_{0}u\in\mathbb{H}^{1}_{p}(T)
due to (
4.2
).
Therefore, by
[
17
, Corollary 4.12]
(for
p>2
) and
[
17
, Remark 4.14]
(for
p=2
),

\zeta_{0}u\in L_{p}(\Omega;\mathcal{C}([0,T];L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})),

(4.5)

and there exists a constant
N
, such that, for any
c>0
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{t\leq T}\|\zeta_{0}u\|^{p}_{L_{p}}

\displaystyle\leq Nc\|\zeta_{0}u\|^{p}_{\mathbb{H}^{1}_{p}(T)}+Nc^{-1}\|\zeta_{0}f\|^{p}_{\mathbb{H}^{-1}_{p}(T)}+N\|\zeta_{0}g\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}_{p}(T;\ell_{2})}

\displaystyle\leq Nc\|\zeta_{0}u\|^{p}_{\mathbb{H}^{1}_{p,\Theta-p}(G,T)}+Nc^{-1}\|\zeta_{0}f\|^{p}_{\mathbb{H}^{-1}_{p,\Theta}(G,T)}+N\|\zeta_{0}g\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}_{p,\Theta}(G,T;\ell_{2})}.

Moreover, for every
j\in\{1,\ldots,M\}
,

d((\zeta_{j}u)(\Psi^{-1}_{j}))=(\zeta_{j}f)(\Psi^{-1}_{j})\,dt+(\zeta_{j}g^{k})(\Psi^{-1}_{j})\,dw^{k}_{t}=:F_{j}\,dt+G^{k}_{j}\,dw^{k}_{t},\quad t\in(0,T],

on
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
and due to (
4.3
),
(\zeta_{j}u)(\Psi^{-1}_{j})\in\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{p,\Theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},T)
(see Section
3
for notation). Therefore,
by
[
17
, Theorem 4.1]
(for
p>2
) and
[
17
, Remark 4.5]
(for
p=2
),

(\zeta_{j}u)(\Psi^{-1}_{j})\in L_{p}(\Omega;\mathcal{C}([0,T];L_{p,\Theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})),

(4.6)

and

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}

\displaystyle\sup_{t\leq T}\|(\zeta_{j}u)(\Psi^{-1}_{j})\|^{p}_{L_{p,\Theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq Nc\|(\zeta_{j}u)(\Psi^{-1}_{j})\|^{p}_{\mathbb{H}^{1}_{p,\Theta-p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},T)}+Nc^{-1}\|F_{j}\|^{p}_{\mathbb{H}^{-1}_{p,\Theta+p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},T)}+N\|G_{j}\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}_{p,\Theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},T;\ell_{2})}

\displaystyle\leq Nc\|\zeta_{j}u\|^{p}_{\mathbb{H}^{1}_{p,\Theta-p}(G,T)}+Nc^{-1}\|\zeta_{j}f\|^{p}_{\mathbb{H}^{-1}_{p,\Theta+p}(G,T)}+N\|\zeta_{j}g\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}_{p,\Theta}(G,T;\ell_{2})}.

Summing up gives the desired estimate and
u\in L_{p}(\Omega;\mathcal{C}([0,T];L_{p,\Theta}(G)))
follows from (
4.5
) and (
4.6
), together with (
4.4
).
The second assertion is due to the fact that, by Lemma
3.2
,

\|f^{i}_{x^{i}}\|_{H^{-1}_{p,\Theta+p}(G)}\leq N\|\psi f^{i}_{x^{i}}\|_{H^{-1}_{p,\Theta}(G)}\leq N\|f^{i}\|_{L_{p,\Theta}(G)}.\qed

We have now all ingredients we need in order to prove Lemma
2.7
.

We first prove Estimate (
2.5
). By Lemma
4.1
,

{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{t\leq T}\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{p}_{L^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D})}\leq N\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{n\theta}\,{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{t\leq T}\|u(t,e^{n}x)\eta(x)\|^{p}_{L_{p}(G)}.

(4.7)

For
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
v_{n}(t,x):=u(t,e^{n}x)\eta(x)
. Then

dv_{n}=[e^{-n}(f^{i}(t,e^{n}x))_{x^{i}}\eta(x)+f^{0}(t,e^{n}x)\eta(x)]dt+g^{k}(t,e^{n}x)\eta(x)dw^{k}_{t},\quad t\in(0,T],

on
G
.
Note that

e^{-n}(f^{i}(t,e^{n}x))_{x^{i}}\eta(x)=e^{-n}[f^{i}(t,e^{n}x)\eta(x)]_{x^{i}}-e^{-n}f^{i}(t,e^{n}x)\eta_{x^{i}}(x),

and

(v_{n})_{x^{i}}=e^{n}u_{x^{i}}(t,e^{n}x)\eta(x)-u(t,e^{n}x)\eta_{x^{i}}(x).

(4.8)

Obviously,
L_{p,2}(G)=L_{p}(G)
and by Hardy’s inequality,

\|v_{n}\|_{H^{1}_{p,2-p}(G)}\leq N\Big(\|\rho^{-1}_{G}v_{n}\|_{L_{p}(G)}+\sum_{i}\|(v_{n})_{x^{i}}\|_{L_{p}(G)}\Big)\leq N\|(v_{n})_{x}\|_{L_{p}(G)}.

(4.9)

By Lemma
4.2
with
\Theta=d=2
, (
4.9
), and (
4.8
), for any
c>0

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{t\leq T}\|v_{n}(t,\cdot)\|^{p}_{L_{p}(G)}

\displaystyle\leq N\Big(ce^{np}\sum_{i}\|u_{x^{i}}(\cdot,e^{n}\cdot)\eta\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}_{p,d}(G,T)}+c\sum_{i}\|u(\cdot,e^{n}\cdot)\eta_{x^{i}}\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}_{p,d}(G,T)}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+e^{-np}c^{-1}\|f^{i}(\cdot,e^{n}\cdot)\eta\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}_{p,d}(G,T)}+e^{-np}c^{-1}\sum_{i}\|\rho f^{i}(\cdot,e^{n}\cdot)\eta_{x^{i}}\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}_{p,d}(G,T)}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+c^{-1}\|\rho f^{0}(\cdot,e^{n}\cdot)\eta\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}_{p,d}(G,T)}+\|\eta g(\cdot,e^{n}\cdot)\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}_{p,d}(G,T;\ell_{2})}\Big).

Since
\rho
is bounded in
G
, we can drop
\rho
above, so that, if we choose

c:=e^{-np}
and use (
4.7
), we get

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}

\displaystyle\sup_{t\leq T}\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{p}_{L^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D})}

\displaystyle\leq N\bigg(\sum_{n}e^{n\theta}\|u_{x}(\cdot,e^{n}\cdot)\eta\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}_{p,d}(\mathcal{D},T)}+\sum_{n}e^{n(\theta-p)}\sum_{i}\|u(\cdot,e^{n}\cdot)\eta_{x^{i}}\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}_{p,d}(\mathcal{D},T)}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\sum_{n,i}e^{n\theta}\|f^{i}(\cdot,e^{n}\cdot)\eta\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}_{p,d}(\mathcal{D},T)}+\sum_{n,i}e^{n\theta}\|f^{i}(\cdot,e^{n}\cdot)\eta_{x^{i}}\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}_{p}(\mathcal{D},T)}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\sum_{n}e^{n(\theta+p)}\|f^{0}(\cdot,e^{n}\cdot)\eta\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}_{p,d}(\mathcal{D},T)}+N\sum_{n}e^{n\theta}\|g(\cdot,e^{n}\cdot)\eta\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}_{p,d}(\mathcal{D},T;\ell_{2})}\bigg).

Therefore, due to Lemma
4.1
(ii)
, Estimate (
2.5
) holds.

To prove the continuity assertion, we take a sequence of smooth functions
\xi_{n}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that
\xi_{n}=1
if
3/n<|x|<n
,
\xi_{n}(x)=0
if
|x|\leq 2/n
or
|x|\geq 2n
, and

\sup_{i}\sup_{n}\sup_{x}|x|\,|(\xi_{n})_{x^{i}}|<\infty.

(4.10)

Moreover, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
G_{n}\subset\mathcal{D}
be a bounded
\mathcal{C}^{1}
domain such that

\mathcal{D}\cap\{x:2/n<|x|<2n\}\subset G_{n}\subset\mathcal{D}\cap\{x:1/n<|x|<3n\}.

Then

d(\xi_{n}u)=\left[(\xi_{n}f^{i})_{x^{i}}-(\xi_{n})_{x^{i}}f^{i}+\xi_{n}f^{0}\right]dt+\xi_{n}g^{k}dw^{k}_{t},\quad t\in(0,T],

on
G_{n}
and, by the choice of
\xi_{n}
, we also know that
\xi_{n}u\in\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{p,2}(G_{n},T)
since
\xi_{n}u\in\mathcal{K}^{1}_{p,\theta,0}(\mathcal{D},T)\subseteq\mathring{K}^{1}_{p,\theta-p}(\mathcal{D},T)
, see also
[
2
, Remark 3.2]
. By Lemma
4.2
with
\Theta=2
,
\xi_{n}u\in L_{p}(\Omega;\mathcal{C}([0,T];L_{p}(G_{n}))
.
Since
\xi_{n}
vanishes near the origin and toward infinity, we conclude

\xi_{n}u\in L_{p}(\Omega;\mathcal{C}([0,T];L_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D})).

Applying (
2.5
) to
\xi_{n}u-\xi_{m}u
and using (
4.10
), we find that
\xi_{n}u
is a Cauchy sequence in

L_{p}(\Omega;\mathcal{C}([0,T];L_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D}))
, which converges in this space to a limit
v
. Moreover, applying (
2.5
) to
\xi_{n}u-u
, we find that
u=v
in
L_{p}(\Omega;L_{\infty}([0,T];L_{p,\Theta}(\mathcal{D}))
. Therefore,
u(t)=v(t)
for all
t\leq T
(
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.). Thus
u
has the desired version with continuous paths.
∎

5.
The stochastic heat equation on polygons

In this section we present our analysis for the stochastic heat equation (
2.1
) on polygons in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. We fix the following setting: Throughout,
let
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a polygon with vertexes
\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{M}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
. For
x\in\mathcal{O}
, put

\tilde{\rho}(x):=\min_{1\leq j\leq M}|x-v_{j}|,\quad\quad\rho(x):=\rho_{\mathcal{O}}(x):=\text{dist}(x,\partial\mathcal{O}),

and for
j=1,\ldots,M
define

\kappa_{j}:=\text{interior angle at}\,v_{j},\quad\quad\kappa_{0}:=\max_{1\leq j\leq M}\kappa_{j}.

Motivated by the analysis of the stochastic heat equation on angular domains from Section
2
, we are going to use weighted Sobolev spaces with weights based on the distance
\tilde{\rho}
to the set of vertexes in order to establish existence and uniqueness of a solution. More precisely, for
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
,
p>1
and
n\in\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
, we define the spaces
K^{n}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O})
,
K^{n}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O};\ell_{2})
,
L^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O})
, and

L^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O};\ell_{2})
in the same way as the corresponding spaces on
\mathcal{D}
from Section
2
with
\rho_{\circ}
replaced by
\tilde{\rho}
, i.e., for instance,

\|u\|^{p}_{K^{n}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O})}=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq n}\int_{\mathcal{O}}|\tilde{\rho}^{|\alpha|}D^{\alpha}u|^{p}\tilde{\rho}^{\theta-2}dx.

The space
\mathring{K}^{1}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O})
is the closure of the space
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathcal{O})
of test functions in
K^{1}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O})
.
In analogy to Section
2
, for the
L_{p}
-spaces of predictable stochastic processes with values in the weighted Sobolev spaces introduced above we use the abbreviations

\mathbb{K}^{n}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O},T):=L_{p}(\Omega_{T},\mathcal{P};K^{n}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O})),\qquad\mathbb{K}^{n}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O},T;\ell_{2}):=L_{p}(\Omega_{T},\mathcal{P};K^{n}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O};\ell_{2})),

\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O},T):=\mathbb{K}^{0}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O},T),\quad\quad\quad\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O},T,\ell_{2}):=\mathbb{K}^{0}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O},T;\ell_{2}),

and

\mathring{\mathbb{K}}^{1}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O},T):=L_{p}(\Omega_{T},\mathcal{P};\mathring{K}^{1}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O})).

Moreover,
\mathcal{K}^{1}_{p,\theta,0}(\mathcal{O},T)
is defined the following way.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
p\geq 2
. We write
u\in\mathcal{K}^{1}_{p,\theta,0}(\mathcal{O},T)
if

u\in\mathring{\mathbb{K}}^{1}_{p,\theta-p}(\mathcal{O},T)

and
there exist
f^{0}\in\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta+p}(\mathcal{O},T)
,
f^{i}\in\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O},T)
,
i=1,2
, and

g\in\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O},T;\ell_{2})

such that

du=(f^{0}+f^{i}_{x^{i}})\,dt+g^{k}\,dw^{k}_{t},\quad t\in(0,T],

on
\mathcal{O}
in the sense of distributions with
u(0,\cdot)=0
; see Definition
2.2
accordingly. In this situation
we also write

\mathbb{D}u:=f^{0}+f^{i}_{x^{i}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathbb{S}u:=g.

In this article, Equation (
2.1
) has the following meaning on
\mathcal{O}
.

Definition 5.2
.

We say that
u
is a solution to Equation (
2.1
) on
\mathcal{O}
in the class
\mathcal{K}^{1}_{p,\theta,0}(\mathcal{O},T)

if

u\in\mathcal{K}^{1}_{p,\theta,0}(\mathcal{O},T)
with

\mathbb{D}u=\Delta u+f^{0}+f^{i}_{x^{i}}=f^{0}+(f^{i}+u_{x^{i}})_{x^{i}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathbb{S}u=g.

Before we look at Equation (
2.1
) in detail, we first prove the following version of Lemma
2.7
for polygons.
It is a key ingredient in our existence and uniqueness proof below.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
p\geq 2
and
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. Assume that
u\in\mathcal{K}^{1}_{p,\theta,0}(\mathcal{O},T)
, such that
du=(f^{0}+f^{i}_{x^{i}})dt+g^{k}dw^{k}_{t}
with

f^{0}\in\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta+p}(\mathcal{O},T),\quad f^{i}\in\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O},T),\,i=1,2,\quad\text{and }g\in\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O},T;\ell_{2}).

Then
u\in L_{p}(\Omega;\mathcal{C}([0,T];L^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O})))
and

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{t\leq T}

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{p}_{L^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O})}

\displaystyle\leq N\Big(\|u\|^{p}_{\mathbb{K}^{1}_{p,\theta-p}(\mathcal{O},T)}+\|f^{0}\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta+p}(\mathcal{O},T)}+\sum_{i}\|f^{i}\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O},T)}+\|g\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O},T;\ell_{2})}\Big)

\displaystyle=:N\,C(u,f^{0},f^{i},g,T),

where
N=N(d,p,\theta,T)
is a non-decreasing function of
T
.
In particular, for any
t\leq T
,

\|u\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O},t)}\leq\int^{t}_{0}{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{r\leq s}\|u(r)\|^{p}_{L^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D})}ds\leq N(d,p,\theta,T)\int^{t}_{0}C(u,f^{0},f^{i},g,s)\,ds.

We combine Lemma
2.7
(see also Remark
2.13
) and Lemma
4.2
as follows.

Fix a sufficiently small
r>0
such that
B_{3r}(v_{j})
contains only one vertex
v_{j}
and intersects with only two edges for each
j\leq M
.
Choose a function
\xi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that
0<\xi(x)\leq 1
for
|x|<2r
,
\xi(x)=1
for
|x|<r
, and
\xi(x)=0
for
|x|\geq 2r
.
Let
\xi_{j}(x):=\xi(x-v_{j})
and put
\xi_{0}(x):=1-\sum_{j=1}^{M}\xi_{j}(x)
,
x\in\mathcal{O}
.
Note that by the choice of
r
and
\xi
, the supports of the
\xi_{j}
’s (
j\geq 1
) do not overlap, and therefore
0\leq\sum_{j=1}^{M}\xi^{j}\leq 1
. Moreover,
\xi_{0}(x)=1
if
x
is not close to vertexes, that is, if
\tilde{\rho}(x)=\min_{1\leq j\leq M}|x-v_{j}|\geq r
.
For
j=1,2,\ldots,M
, let
\mathcal{D}_{j}
be the angular domain centered at
v_{j}
with interior angle
\kappa_{j}
such that

\mathcal{D}_{j}\cap B_{3r}(v_{j})=\mathcal{O}\cap B_{3r}(v_{j}).

(5.1)

Moreover, let
G
be a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
domain in
\mathcal{O}
such that

\xi_{0}(x)=0\quad\text{for }\,x\in\mathcal{O}\setminus G\quad\text{and}\quad\inf_{x\in G}\tilde{\rho}(x)>c>0.

(5.2)

Note that by the choice of
\xi_{j}
,
\mathcal{D}_{j}
,
j=0,1,\ldots,M
, and
G
, for any
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
and
v\in\mathring{K}{}^{1}_{p,\theta-p}(\mathcal{O})
,

\|\xi_{0}v\|_{L^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O})}\sim\|\xi_{0}v\|_{L_{p}(G)},\quad\quad\|\xi_{0}v\|_{K^{1}_{p,\theta-p}(\mathcal{O})}\sim\|\xi_{0}v\|_{H^{1}_{p,2-p}(G)},

(5.3)

\|\xi_{j}v\|_{K^{1}_{p,\theta-p}(\mathcal{O})}=\|\xi_{j}v\|_{K^{1}_{p,\theta-p}(\mathcal{D}_{j})}\quad(j\geq 1).

(5.4)

The first and the third relation are trivial and hold actually for arbitrary measurable
v\colon\mathcal{O}\to\mathbb{R}
, provided the expressions make sense. The second one is due to (
5.2
) and Hardy’s inequality as

\displaystyle\|\xi_{0}v\|_{K^{1}_{p,\theta-p}(\mathcal{O})}

\displaystyle\leq N(\|\xi_{0}v\|_{L_{p}(G)}+\sum_{i}\|(\xi_{0}v)_{x^{i}}\|_{L_{p}(G)})

\displaystyle\leq N\|\xi_{0}v\|_{H^{1}_{p,2-p}(G)}\leq N\sum_{i}\|(\xi_{0}v)_{x^{i}}\|_{L_{p}(G)}\leq N\|\xi_{0}v\|_{K^{1}_{p,\theta-p}(\mathcal{O})}.

The three relations from (
5.3
) and (
5.4
) together imply, in particular, that

\|v\|_{L^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O})}\sim\sum_{j=0}^{M}\|\xi_{j}v\|_{L^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O})}\sim\|\xi_{0}v\|_{L_{p}(G)}+\sum_{j=1}^{M}\|\xi_{j}v\|_{L^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D}_{j})},

\displaystyle\|v\|_{K^{1}_{p,\theta-p}(\mathcal{O})}\sim\sum_{j=0}^{M}\|\xi_{j}v\|_{K^{1}_{p,\theta-p}(\mathcal{O})}\sim\|\xi_{0}v\|_{H^{1}_{p,2-p}(G)}+\sum_{j=1}^{M}\|\xi_{j}v\|_{K^{1}_{p,\theta-p}(\mathcal{D}_{j})}.

(5.5)

Also note that for any multi-index
\alpha
,

\sum_{j=0}^{M}\|vD^{\alpha}\xi_{j}\|_{L^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O})}+\sum_{j=0}^{M}\|v\tilde{\rho}D^{\alpha}\xi_{j}\|_{L^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O})}\leq N\|v\|_{L^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O})}.

(5.6)

Using the preparations above, we can verify the assertion the following way.
For each
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,M\}
,
u^{j}:=\xi_{j}u\in\mathcal{K}^{1}_{p,\theta,0}(\mathcal{D}_{j},T)
with

du^{j}=\big((\xi_{j}f^{i})_{x^{i}}+\xi_{j}f^{0}-(\xi_{j})_{x^{i}}f^{i}\big)\,dt+\xi_{j}g^{k}\,dw^{k}_{t},\quad t\in(0,T],

(5.7)

on
\mathcal{D}_{j}
in the sense of distributions.
Thus, by Lemma
2.7
(see also Remark
2.13
) and (
5.1
),

u^{j}\in L_{p}(\Omega;\mathcal{C}([0,T];L^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O})))
, and

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{t\leq T}\|\xi_{j}u\|^{p}_{L^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O})}\leq

\displaystyle N\Big(\|\xi_{j}f^{0}\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta+p}(\mathcal{O},T)}+\sum_{i}\|\xi_{j}f^{i}\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O},T)}

(5.8)

\displaystyle+\|\xi_{j}u\|^{p}_{\mathbb{K}^{1}_{p,\theta-p}(\mathcal{O},T)}+\|(\xi_{j})_{x^{i}}\tilde{\rho}f^{i}\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O},T)}+\|\xi_{j}g\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O},T;\ell_{2})}\Big).

Also,
u^{0}:=\xi_{0}u\in\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{p,2,0}(G,T)
and (
5.7
) holds with
j=0
. Thus, by Lemma
4.2
and (
5.2
),
u^{0}\in L_{p}(\Omega;\mathcal{C}([0,T];L^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O})))
, and (
5.8
) holds with
j=0
. Therefore, by summing up all these estimates and using above relations, we get the desired result.
∎

Our main existence and uniqueness result for the stochastic heat equation on polygons reads as follows.
Recall that in this section
\kappa_{0}
denotes the maximum over all interior angles of the polygon
\mathcal{O}
.

Theorem 5.4

(Existence and uniqueness/polygons)
.

Let
p\geq 2
and assume that
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
satisfies (
2.6
). Then for any

f^{0}\in\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta+p}(\mathcal{O},T),\quad f^{i}\in\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O},T),\,i=1,2,\quad\text{and}\quad g\in\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O},T;\ell_{2}),

Equation (
2.1
) on
\mathcal{O}

has a unique solution
u\in\mathcal{K}^{1}_{p,\theta,0}(\mathcal{O},T)
.
Moreover,

\|u\|_{\mathbb{K}^{1}_{p,\theta-p}(\mathcal{O},T)}\leq N\,\Big(\|f^{0}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta+p}(\mathcal{O},T)}+\sum_{i}\|f^{i}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O},T)}+\|g\|_{\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O},T;\ell_{2})}\Big),

(5.9)

where
N=N(p,\theta,\kappa_{0},T)
.

Step 1.

We first prove that (
5.9
) holds given that a solution
u\in\mathcal{K}^{1}_{p,\theta,0}(\mathcal{O},T)
already exists, by using corresponding results for the stochastic heat equation on angular domains and on
\mathcal{C}^{1}
domains. This will, in particular, take care of the uniqueness.

Let
r>0
,
\xi_{j}
,
\mathcal{D}_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,M
, as well as
\xi_{0}
and
G
be as in the proof of Lemma
5.3
.
A very similar reasoning as therein can be used to verify that
\xi_{0}u\in\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{p,\theta,0}(G,T)
,
\xi_{j}u\in\mathcal{K}^{1}_{p,\theta,0}(\mathcal{D}_{j},T)
for
j\geq 1
, and that for all
j\in\{0,1,\ldots,M\}
,

\displaystyle d(\xi_{j}u)

\displaystyle=\big(\Delta(\xi_{j}u)+(-2u(\xi_{j})_{x^{i}}+\xi_{j}f^{i})_{x^{i}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+u\Delta\xi_{j}-(\xi_{j})_{x^{i}}f^{i}+\xi_{j}f^{0}\big)\,dt+\xi_{j}g^{k}\,dw^{k}_{t},\quad t\in(0,T].

Thus, by Theorem
2.8
for
j\geq 1
and by Lemma
3.6
for
j=0
(see also
[
4
, Theorem 2.9]
), we obtain the estimate for

\|\xi_{j}u\|^{p}_{\mathbb{K}^{1}_{p,\theta-p}(\mathcal{O},t)}
for each
t\leq T
. Then summing up over all
j
and using (
5.5
) and (
5.6
), yields that for each
t\leq T
,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathbb{K}^{1}_{p,\theta-p}(\mathcal{O},t)}

\displaystyle\leq N\Big(\|u\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O},t)}+\|f^{0}\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta+p}(\mathcal{O},T)}

(5.10)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\sum_{i}\|f^{i}\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O},T)}+\|g\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O},T;\ell_{2})}\Big).

Recall that

du=(f^{0}+(f^{i}+u_{x^{i}})_{x^{i}})\,dt+g^{k}\,dw^{k}_{t},\quad t\leq T,

and
\|u_{x}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O},s)}\leq N\|u\|_{\mathbb{K}^{1}_{p,\theta-p}(\mathcal{O},s)}
.
Thus, by Lemma
5.3
and (
5.10
), for each
t\leq T
,

\displaystyle\|u\|^{p}_{\mathbb{K}^{1}_{p,\theta-p}(\mathcal{O},t)}

\displaystyle\leq N\int^{t}_{0}\|u\|^{p}_{\mathbb{K}^{1}_{p,\theta-p}(\mathcal{O},s)}\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad+N\Big(\|f^{0}\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta+p}(\mathcal{O},T)}+\sum_{i}\|f^{i}\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O},T)}+\|g\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{O},T;\ell_{2})}\Big).

Hence the desired estimate follows by Gronwall’s inequality.

Step 2.
We prove existence as follows.
Due to Lemma
5.3

and the a-priori estimate obtained in Step 1, we may assume
f^{0}
,
f^{i}
,
i=1,2
, and
g
are very nice in the sense that they vanish near the boundary and

f^{i},f^{i}_{x^{i}},f^{0}\in L_{2}(\Omega_{T},\mathcal{P};L_{2}(\mathcal{O})),\quad\text{and}\quad g\in L_{2}(\Omega_{T},\mathcal{P};L_{2}(\mathcal{O};\ell_{2})).

Then, by classical results (see, for instance,
[
27
]
or
[
5
, Theorem 2.12]
), there exists a unique solution
u
in
\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{2,2,0}(\mathcal{O},T)
, which satisfies, in particular,

\rho^{-1}u,u_{x^{i}}\in L_{2}(\Omega_{T},\mathcal{P};L_{2}(\mathcal{O})),\;i=1,2,\;\text{ and }\;\sup_{x}|u|\leq N\|u_{x}\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{O})}.

(5.11)

Note that for each
j\geq 1
,

d(\xi_{j}u)=\big(\Delta(\xi_{j}u)+f^{j,i}_{x^{i}}+f^{j,0}\big)\,dt+\xi_{j}g^{k}\,dw^{k}_{t},\quad t\in(0,T],

(5.12)

on
\mathcal{D}_{j}
, where, due to (
5.11
) and the fact that
(\xi_{j})_{x^{i}}=0
near the vertex
v_{j}
,

f^{j,i}:=-2(\xi_{j})_{x^{i}}u+\xi_{j}f^{i}\in\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D}_{j},T)\cap\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{2,2}(\mathcal{D}_{j},T),

(5.13)

f^{j,0}:=u\Delta\xi_{j}+f^{0}\xi_{j}+\sum_{i}(\xi_{j})_{x_{i}}f^{i}\in\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta+p}(\mathcal{D}_{j},T)\cap\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{2,2+2}(\mathcal{D}_{j},T),

(5.14)

and

\xi_{j}g\in\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta}(\mathcal{D}_{j},T;\ell_{2})\cap\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{2,2}(\mathcal{D}_{j},T;\ell_{2}).

Since
\tilde{\rho}(x)\geq\rho(x)
, it follows that for each for
j\geq 1
we have
\xi_{j}u\in\mathcal{K}^{1}_{2,2,0}(\mathcal{O},T)
. Thus, by Lemma
2.12
, we conclude
\xi_{j}u\in\mathcal{K}^{1}_{p,\theta,0}(\mathcal{O},T)
if
j\geq 1
. Similar arguments based on Lemma

3.8
yield that
\xi_{0}u\in\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{p,2,0}(G,T)
. Therefore,
\xi_{0}u\in\mathcal{K}^{1}_{p,\theta,0}(\mathcal{O},T)
(see (
5.3
)), and consequently

u\in\mathcal{K}^{1}_{p,\theta,0}(\mathcal{O},T)
.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

Note that even if we were to consider Equation (
2.1
) on
\mathcal{O}
with
f^{i}=0
,
i=1,2
, our proof strategy for Theorem
5.4
(and Theorem
5.6
below) requires that we are able to handle the localized equation on
\mathcal{D}_{j}
with forcing term
((\xi_{j})_{x^{i}}u)_{x^{i}}
, which means that we have to be able to treat Equation (
2.1
) on angular domains with
f^{i}\neq 0
.
This is why we need the extension of
[
2

presented in Theorem
2.8
even for the proof of Theorem
5.4
with
f^{i}=0
,
i=1,2
.

We conclude with our main higher order regularity result for the stochastic heat equation on polygons.

Theorem 5.6

(Higher order regularity/polygons)
.

Given the setting of Theorem
5.4
, let
u
be the unique solution in the class
\mathcal{K}^{1}_{p,\theta,0}(\mathcal{O},T)
to Equation (
2.1
) on
\mathcal{O}
.
Assume that

\displaystyle C(m,\theta,f^{i},f^{0},g)

\displaystyle:={\mathbb{E}}\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\mathcal{O}}\bigg(\sum_{|\alpha|\leq(m-1)\vee 0\color[rgb]{0,0,0}}|\rho^{\lvert\alpha\rvert+1}D^{\alpha}f^{0}|^{p}+\sum_{i}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}|\rho^{|\alpha|}D^{\alpha}f^{i}|^{p}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+|\tilde{\rho}f^{0}|^{p}+\sum_{\lvert\alpha\rvert\leq m}|\rho^{\lvert\alpha\rvert}D^{\alpha}g|_{\ell_{2}}^{p}\bigg)\tilde{\rho}^{\theta-2}\,dx\,dt<\infty,

for some
m\in\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
.
Then

{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{\lvert\alpha\rvert\leq m+1}\int_{\mathcal{O}}\big\lvert\rho^{\lvert\alpha\rvert-1}D^{\alpha}u\big\rvert^{p}\tilde{\rho}^{\theta-2}\,dx\,dt\leq N\,C(m,\theta,f^{i},f^{0},g),

(5.15)

where
N=N(p,\theta,\kappa_{0},m,T)
.

We prove the statement by induction over
m
. As in the proof of the results above, we use a partition of unity and apply corresponding results for the stochastic heat equation on angular domains (Corollary
2.11
) and on
\mathcal{C}^{1}
domains (
[
4
, Theorem 2.9]
) to estimate the solutions of the localized equations.

Let
r>0
,
\xi_{j}
,
\mathcal{D}_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,M
, as well as
\xi_{0}
and
G
be as in the proof of Lemma
5.3
.
In addition, assume that
G\subset\mathcal{O}
is chosen in such a way that

\mathcal{O}\setminus\bigcup_{j}B_{2r/3}(v_{j})\subseteq G\subseteq\mathcal{O}\setminus\bigcup_{j}B_{r/3}(v_{j}).

As a consequence,

\rho_{G}\sim\rho_{\mathcal{O}}\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{\rho}\sim 1\quad\text{on}\quad\textup{supp}\,\xi_{0}\cap\mathcal{O}.

(5.16)

Step 1. The base case.
Let
m=0
.
Note that in this case, the only difference in Estimate (
5.15
) compared to (
5.9
) is the weight we put on
u
on the left hand side of the inequality:
\rho^{-p}\tilde{\rho}^{\theta-2}
in (
5.15
) instead of the smaller
\tilde{\rho}^{\theta-p-2}
from (
5.9
).
But to obtain this sharper estimate we argue in a very similar fashion as in the proof of the latter with two changes:
We use Corollary
2.11
instead of Theorem
2.8
to estimate the solution in the vicinity of vertexes and we use the slightly modified choice of
G
and (
5.16
) to replace
\rho_{G}
by
\rho_{\mathcal{O}}
after applying
[
4
, Theorem 2.9]
to estimate the solution away from the vertexes.
In detail, we argue as follows: The same reasoning as in the proof of Theorem
5.4
shows that
\xi_{0}u\in\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{p,\theta,0}(G,T)
and
\xi_{j}u\in\mathcal{K}^{1}_{p,\theta,0}(\mathcal{D}_{j},T)
for
j\geq 1
satisfy (
5.12
) on
G
and on
\mathcal{D}_{j}
,
j\geq 1
, respectively.
In particular, if
1\leq j\leq M
, then
by Corollary
2.11
(see also Remark
2.13
), Estimate (
5.15
) holds with
\xi_{j}u
and
C(0,\theta,f^{j,i},f^{j,0},\xi_{j}g)
in place of
u
and
C(0,\theta,f^{i},f^{0},g)
, respectively. Here
f^{j,i}
and
f^{j,0}
are taken from (
5.13
) and (
5.14
).
Moreover, by the corresponding result on
\mathcal{C}^{1}
domains (see
[
4
, Theorem 2.9]
) and (
5.16
), Estimate (
5.15
) also holds for
\xi_{0}u
and
C(0,\theta,f^{0,i},f^{0,0},\xi_{0}g)
in place of
u
and
C(0,\theta,f^{i},f^{0},g)
, respectively.
Summing up all these estimates and using the second relationship in (
5.16
) yields

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{\lvert\alpha\rvert\leq 1}\int_{\mathcal{O}}\big\lvert\rho^{\lvert\alpha\rvert-1}D^{\alpha}u\big\rvert^{p}\tilde{\rho}^{\theta-2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq N\sum_{j=0}^{M}C(0,\theta,f^{j,i},f^{j,0},\xi_{j}g)

\displaystyle\leq N\,\|u\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}^{[\circ]}_{p,\theta-p}(\mathcal{O})}+NC(0,\theta,f^{i},f^{0},g)

\displaystyle\leq N\,C(0,\theta,f^{i},f^{0},g);

the last inequality above is due to (
5.9
). The base case is proved.

Step 2. The induction step.
Suppose that (
5.15
) holds for some
m\in\{0,1,\ldots\}
and
C(m+1,\theta,f^{i},f^{0},g)<\infty
. Then, by assumption,

{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{\lvert\alpha\rvert\leq m+1}\int_{\mathcal{O}}\big\lvert\rho^{\lvert\alpha\rvert-1}D^{\alpha}u\big\rvert^{p}\tilde{\rho}^{\theta-2}\,dx\,dt\leq NC(m,\theta,f^{i},f^{0},g).

(5.17)

Using (
5.17
), one can easily check that

\sum_{j=0}^{M}C(m+1,\theta,f^{j,i},f^{j,0},\xi_{j}g)\leq N\,C(m+1,\theta,f^{i},f^{0},g).

Therefore, appropriate applications of Corollary
2.11
(see also Remark
2.13
) and
[
4
, Theorem 2.9]
yield suitable estimates of
\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m+2}{\mathbb{E}}\int^{T}_{0}|\rho^{|\alpha|-1}D^{\alpha}(\xi_{j}u)|^{p}\tilde{\rho}^{\theta-2}dxdt
for
j=0
and
1\leq j\leq M
, respectively, which, summed up, yield

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{\lvert\alpha\rvert\leq m+2}\int_{\mathcal{O}}

\displaystyle\big\lvert\rho^{\lvert\alpha\rvert-1}D^{\alpha}u\big\rvert^{p}\tilde{\rho}^{\theta-2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq N\sum_{j=0}^{M}C(m+1,\theta,f^{j,i},f^{j,0},\xi_{j}g)\leq N\,C(m+1,\theta,f^{i},f^{0},g).

Thus the induction goes through and the theorem is proved.
∎

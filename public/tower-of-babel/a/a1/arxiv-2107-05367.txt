Conditions forcing the existence of relative complements in lattices and posets
Ivan Chajda, Helmut Länger

Abstract
It is elementary and well-known that if an element x x of a bounded modular lattice 𝐋 \mathbf{L} has a complement in 𝐋 \mathbf{L} then x x has a relative complement in every interval [ a , b ] [a,b] containing x x . We show that the relatively strong assumption of modularity of 𝐋 \mathbf{L} can be replaced by a weaker one formulated in the language of so-called modular triples. We further show that, in general, we need not suppose that x x has a complement in 𝐋 \mathbf{L} . By introducing the concept of modular triples in posets, we extend our results obtained for lattices to posets. It should be remarked that the notion of a complement can be introduced also in posets that are not bounded.

2
Relative complements in lattices

As promised in the introduction, we show that there are conditions weaker than modularity ensuring that an element
x
of
[a,b]
has a relative complement. We are going to state two such results. The following result is a special case of Proposition
3.3
which was proved in
[
5
]
. For the sake of completeness we provide a proof.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge)
be a lattice,
a,b\in L
with
a\leq b
,
x\in[a,b]
,
y\in L
,
e:=(a\vee y)\wedge b
and
f:=a\vee(y\wedge b)
. Then
f\leq e
and the following are equivalent:

(i)

e,f\in R(a,b,x)
,

(ii)

(a\vee y)\wedge x=a
and
x\vee(y\wedge b)=b
.

Proof.

Because of
a\leq a\vee y
,
a\leq b
,
y\wedge b\leq a\vee y
and
y\wedge b\leq b
we have
f\leq e
. Moreover, we have

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle\leq f\leq e\leq b,

\displaystyle e\wedge x

\displaystyle=\big((a\vee y)\wedge b\big)\wedge x=(a\vee y)\wedge x,

\displaystyle x\vee f

\displaystyle=x\vee\big(a\vee(y\wedge b)\big)=x\vee(y\wedge b).

Since
a\leq x
and
a\leq f\leq e
, we have that
x\wedge e=a
implies
x\wedge f=a
, and since
x\leq b
and
f\leq e\leq b
, we have that
x\vee f=b
implies
x\vee e=b
. Hence (i) and (ii) are both equivalent to
e\wedge x=a
and
x\vee f=b
.
∎

It is worth noticing that we do not assume
y
to be a complement of
x
.

(ii)

Proof.

Since
a\leq a\vee y
,
a\leq b
,
y\wedge b\leq a\vee y

, we have

. Because of Theorem
2.1
, (i) is equivalent to

(a\vee y)\wedge x=a\text{ and }b=x\vee(y\wedge b).

(1)

Since

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=a\vee 0=a\vee(y\wedge x),

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle=1\wedge b=(x\vee y)\wedge b,

(
1
) is equivalent to

(a\vee y)\wedge x=a\vee(y\wedge x)\text{ and }(x\vee y)\wedge b=x\vee(y\wedge b).

(2)

Finally, due to the definition of modular triples, (

\big(a\vee(y\wedge b)\big)\wedge x=a\text{ and }b=x\vee\big((a\vee y)\wedge b\big).

(3)

Remark 2.8
.

Consider the following well-known result mentioned in the introduction: If
\mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge,0,1)
is a bounded modular lattice,
a,b\in L
with
a\leq b
,
x\in[a,b]
and
y

x
then

. In the proof of this result only modularity of the triples
(a,y,b)
,
(a,y,x)
and

is used. According to Theorem

, modularity of these three triples must imply modularity of the triples

which can be easily shown:

\displaystyle\big(a\vee(y\wedge b)\big)\wedge x

\displaystyle=\big((a\vee y)\wedge b\big)\wedge x=(a\vee y)\wedge x=a\vee(y\wedge x)=a\vee\big((y\wedge b)\wedge x\big),

\displaystyle\big(x\vee(a\vee y)\big)\wedge b

\displaystyle=(x\vee y)\wedge b=x\vee(y\wedge b)=x\vee\big(a\vee(y\wedge b)\big)=x\vee\big((a\vee y)\wedge b\big).

3
Relative complements in posets

Now we turn our attention to complements and relative complements in posets. For this purpose, we must define again distributive and modular triples.

Let
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq)
be a poset and
a,b,c\in P
. Then the poset
\mathbf{P}
is called
distributive
if

L\big(U(x,y),z\big)=LU\big(L(x,z),L(y,z)\big)

for all
x,y,z\in P
. We call
(a,b,c)
a
distributive
triple of
\mathbf{P}
if

L\big(U(a,b),c\big)=LU\big(L(a,c),L(b,c)\big)

and we call
(a,b,c)
a
modular triple
of
\mathbf{P}
if
a\leq c
and

L\big(U(a,b),c\big)=LU\big(a,L(b,c)\big).

The following result is a straightforward generalization of the corresponding result for lattices.

Proposition 3.1
.

(
[
3
]
)
Let
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq)
be a distributive poset and
x\in P
. Then
x
has at most one complement.

Analogously as in Lemma
1.1
, distributivity of
(P,\leq)
can be replaced by a weaker condition. The following lemma extends Lemma
1.1
to posets.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq)
be a poset and
x\in L
. Assume that
y
and
z
are complements of
x
and that
(x,y,z)
and
(x,z,y)
are distributive triples. Then
y=z
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle L(y)

\displaystyle=P\cap L(y)=LU(x,z)\cap L(y)=L\big(U(x,z),y\big)=LU\big(L(x,y),L(z,y)\big)=

\displaystyle=L\big(UL(x,y)\cap UL(z,y)\big)=L\big(P\cap UL(z,y)\big)=LUL(z,y)=L(z,y)=L(y,z)=

\displaystyle=LUL(y,z)=L(P\cap UL(y,z)\big)=L\big(UL(x,z)\cap UL(y,z)\big)=

\displaystyle=LU\big(L(x,z),L(y,z)\big)=L\big(U(x,y),z\big)=LU(x,y)\cap L(z)=P\cap L(z)=L(z)

and hence
y=z
.
∎

(

Let
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq)

with

\displaystyle L\big(U(a,y),x\big)

\displaystyle=L(a),

\displaystyle U\big(x,L(y,b)\big)

\displaystyle=U(b)

Let us note that

(ii)

L\big(U(a,y),x\big)=L(a)

U\big(x,L(y,b)\big)=U(b)

We have

\displaystyle L(e,x)

\displaystyle=L(e)\cap L(x)=L\big(U(a,y),b\big)\cap L(x)=LU(a,y)\cap L(b)\cap L(x)=

\displaystyle=LU(a,y)\cap L(x)=L\big(U(a,y),x\big),

\displaystyle U(x,f)

\displaystyle=U(x)\cap U(f)=U(x)\cap U\big(a,L(y,b)\big)=U(x)\cap U(a)\cap UL(y,b)=

\displaystyle=U(x)\cap UL(y,b)=U\big(x,L(y,b)\big).

Since

and
a\leq f\leq e
, we have that

implies
L(c,f)=L(a)

,

(ii)

(a,y,x)
and
(x,y,b)
are modular triples.

Proof.

Since
U(a,y)\cup\{b\}\subseteq U\big(a,L(y,b)\big)=U(f)
we have

f\in L(f)=LU(f)\subseteq L\big(U(a,y),b\big)=L(e)

and hence
a\leq f\leq e\leq b
. Because of Theorem
3.4
, (i) is equivalent to

L\big(U(a,y),x\big)=L(a)\text{ and }U(b)=U\big(x,L(y,b)\big).

(5)

Clearly, (
5
) is equivalent to

L\big(U(a,y),x\big)=L(a)\text{ and }L(b)=LU\big(x,L(y,b)\big).

(6)

Since

\displaystyle L(a)

\displaystyle=LU(a)=L\big(U(a)\cap P\big)=L\big(U(a)\cap UL(y,x)\big)=LU\big(a,L(y,x)\big),

\displaystyle L(b)

\displaystyle=P\cap L(b)=LU(x,y)\cap L(b)=L\big(U(x,y),b\big),

(

L\big(U(a,y),x\big)=LU\big(a,L(y,x)\big)\text{ and }L\big(U(x,y),b\big)=LU\big(x,L(y,b)\big).

(7)

7
) is equivalent to (ii).
∎

y

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq)
be a poset,
a,b\in L
with
a\leq b
,
x\in[a,b]
and
y\in P
and assume
(a,y,b)
to be a modular triple and
e
to be the greatest element of
L\big(U(a,y),b\big)=LU\big(a,L(y,b)\big)
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

e\in R(a,b,x)
,

(ii)

L\Big(U\big(a,L(y,b)\big),x\Big)=L(a)
and
U\Big(x,L\big(U(a,y),b\big)\Big)=U(b)
.

Proof.

Since

\displaystyle L(e)

\displaystyle=L\big(U(a,y),b\big)=LU\big(a,L(y,b)\big),

\displaystyle U(e)

\displaystyle=UL\big(U(a,y),b\big)

and

\displaystyle L(e,x)

\displaystyle=L(e)\cap L(x)=LU\big(a,L(y,b)\big)\cap L(x)=L\Big(U\big(a,L(y,b)\big),x\Big),

\displaystyle U(x,e)

\displaystyle=U(x)\cap U(e)=U(x)\cap UL\big(U(a,y),b\big)=U\Big(x,L\big(U(a,y),b\big)\Big),

(i) and (ii) are both equivalent to
L(e,x)=L(a)
and
U(x,e)=U(b)
.
∎

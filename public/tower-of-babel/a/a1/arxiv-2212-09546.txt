Solutions of the Sinh-Gordon and sine-Gordon equations and applications
G. Polychrou

Abstract
. We study the elliptic sinh-Gordon and sine-Gordon equations on the real plane and we introduce new families of solutions. We use a Bäcklund transformation that connects the elliptic versions of sine-Gordon and sinh-Gordon equations. As an application, we construct new harmonic maps between surfaces, when the target is of constant curvature − 1 -1 .

Introduction and Statement of the Results

Our aim is to find new solutions of the elliptic sinh-Gordon equation

(1)

\Delta w=k\sinh(2w),k\in\mathbb{R},

and also to find new solutions of the elliptic sine-Gordon equation

(2)

\Delta\theta=-l\sin(2\theta),l\in\mathbb{R},

and apply them to the harmonic map problem.
For simplicity we shall only consider the case
k=2
and
l=-2
.

Firstly, we consider solutions
w
of the sinh-Gordon equation (
1

(3)

\sinh w(x,y)=\tan(A(x)+B(y)),

for some real functions
A(x)
and
B(y)

[
2
]
. Next we apply these solutions to the harmonic map problem, as in
[
4
]
and
[
13

Next, we describe solutions of (
2

(4)

\tan(\frac{\theta(x,y)}{2})=F(x)G(y),

or of the form

(5)

\sin(\theta(x,y))=\tanh(C(x)+D(y)).

In
[

]
, the authors introduce a Bäcklund transformation between the solutions of the elliptic sinh-Gordon equation and the elliptic sine-Gordon. There is a

that connects solutions of equation (

(6)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}w-\partial_{y}\theta

\displaystyle=-2\sinh w\sin\theta,

Therefore, given a solution

of the sine-Gorodn equation and vice versa.

Using the Bäcklund transformation we construct new families of solutions of (

Preliminaries

In this section we present some known results.
Let

Riemann surfaces
(M,g)
,
(N,h)

u=u(z,\bar{z})=R+iS

\partial_{z}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}-i\partial_{y}),\quad\partial_{\bar{z}}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}+i\partial_{y}).

Also, we introduce isothermal coordinates
(x,y)

\partial_{z\bar{z}}u+\partial_{u}F(u,\bar{u})\partial_{z}u\partial_{\bar{z}}u=0;

see
[
6

We define the Hopf differential of

by

\Lambda(z)dz^{2}=\left(e^{F(u,\bar{u})}\partial_{z}u\partial_{z}\bar{u}\right)dz^{2}.

The following proposition is well-known.

Proposition 1

(
[
6
]
)
.

A necessary and sufficient condition for

(8)

where
\mu(z)
is a holomorphic function.

On the domain surface
M

Z=\int e^{-\mu(z)/2}\,dz.

In this specific system, the above equations simplify by considering
\mu(z)=0
.
In particular, the harmonic map condition (
8
) becomes

(9)

e^{F(u,\bar{u})}\partial_{z}u\partial_{z}\bar{u}=1.

We shall say that the harmonic map corresponds to a solution

of (

\frac{\partial_{\bar{z}}u}{\partial_{z}u}=e^{-2w}.

We now recall a main result of

Consider now

3.
The sinh-Gordon equation

In this section, we study a new family of solutions of the sinh-Gordon equation (
1
) that are of the form

(12)

\sinh\omega(x,y)=\tan(A(x)+B(y)).

We shall find a family of such solutions and then we shall give an example of a corresponding harmonic map.

Theorem 4
.

If
w
is a solution of the sinh-Gordon equation (
1
) of the form

\sinh\omega(x,y)=\tan(A(x)+B(y))=\tan(A_{0}+B_{0}+\int_{0}^{x}a(t)dt+\int_{0}^{y}b(s)ds),

then the functions
a(x)
and
b(y)
satisfy the differential equations

(13)

(a^{\prime}(x))^{2}=-(a(x))^{4}+c_{1}(a(x))^{2}+c_{2},

(14)

(b^{\prime}(y))^{2}=-(b(y))^{4}+(8-c_{1})(b(y))^{2}+c_{3}.

where
a(x)=A^{\prime}(x)
,
b(y)=B^{\prime}(y)
and
c_{1},c_{2}
and
c_{3}
are constants such that
4c_{1}=16+c_{3}-c_{2}
.

Proof.

Differentiating
w(x,y)
and replacing into (
1
) we have

(A^{\prime\prime}(x)+B^{\prime\prime}(y))\cosh w+((A^{\prime}(x))^{2}+(B^{\prime}(y))^{2})\sinh w\cosh w=2\sinh 2w.

Using the fact that
\sinh\omega(x,y)=\tan(A(x)+B(y))
and dividing with
\cosh w
we get

\tan(A(x)+B(y))=\frac{A^{\prime\prime}(x)+B^{\prime\prime}(y)}{4-(A^{\prime}(x))^{2}-(B^{\prime}(y))^{2}}.

Differentiating with respect to
x

and using the identity
1/\cos^{2}\phi=1+\tan^{2}\phi
we obtain

(15)

A^{\prime}(x)(4-(A^{\prime}(x))^{2}-(B^{\prime}(y))^{2})^{2}+A^{\prime}(x)(A^{\prime\prime}(x)+B^{\prime\prime}(y))^{2}

=A^{\prime\prime\prime}(x)(4-(A^{\prime}(x))^{2}-(B^{\prime}(y))^{2})+2A^{\prime}(x)A^{\prime\prime}(x)(A^{\prime\prime}(x)+B^{\prime\prime}(y)).

Differentiating with respect to
y
we have

-4A^{\prime}(x)B^{\prime}(y)B^{\prime\prime}(y)(4-(A^{\prime}(x))^{2}-(B^{\prime}(y))^{2})+2A^{\prime}(x)B^{\prime\prime\prime}(y)(A^{\prime\prime}(x)+B^{\prime\prime}(y))

=-2B^{\prime}(y)B^{\prime\prime}(y)A^{\prime\prime\prime}(x)+2A^{\prime}(x)A^{\prime\prime}(x)B^{\prime\prime\prime}(y).

Therefore we get that

\frac{A^{\prime\prime\prime}(x)}{A^{\prime}(x)}+2(A^{\prime}(x))^{2}=8-\frac{B^{\prime\prime\prime}(y)}{B^{\prime}(y)}-2(B^{\prime}(y))^{2}=c_{1}.

So we derive the equations

(16)

A^{\prime\prime\prime}(x)+2(A^{\prime}(x))^{3}=c_{1}A^{\prime}(x),

(17)

B^{\prime\prime\prime}(y)+2(B^{\prime}(y))^{3}=(8-c_{1})B^{\prime}(y).

Starting with equation (
16
), we have

2A^{\prime\prime}(x)A^{\prime\prime\prime}(x)+4(A^{\prime}(x))^{3}A^{\prime\prime}(x)=c_{1}2A^{\prime}(x)A^{\prime\prime}(x)

\Rightarrow(A^{\prime\prime}(x))^{2}+(A^{\prime}(x))^{4}=c_{1}(A^{\prime}(x))^{2}+c_{2}.

By letting
A(x)=A_{0}+\int_{0}^{x}a(t)dt
, we obtain

(18)

(a^{\prime}(x))^{2}=-(a(x))^{4}+c_{1}(a(x))^{2}+c_{2}.

In a similar manner, we get

(19)

(b^{\prime}(y))^{2}=-(b(y))^{4}+(8-c_{1})(b(y))^{2}+c_{3},

where
B(y)=B_{0}+\int_{0}^{y}b(s)ds
. Finally, using (
15
) we derive the connection

4c_{1}=c_{3}-c_{2}+16
.
∎

Corollary 5

By using the initial conditions

In

Example 6

Proposition 7

Proof.

Set

Differentiating with respect to

and

\frac{1}{F^{\prime}(x)F(x)}(\frac{F^{\prime\prime}(x)}{F(x)})^{\prime}=\frac{1}{H^{\prime}(y)H(y)}(\frac{H^{\prime\prime}(y)}{H(y)})^{\prime}=4A.

Therefore,

(\frac{F^{\prime\prime}(x)}{F(x)})^{\prime}=4AF^{\prime}(x)F(x),(\frac{H^{\prime\prime}(y)}{H(y)})^{\prime}=4AH^{\prime}(y)H(y).

Integrating twice, we obtain

(33)

F^{\prime}(x)^{2}=AF^{4}(x)+BF^{2}(x)+C,

(34)

H^{\prime}(y)^{2}=A^{\prime}H^{4}(y)+B^{\prime}H^{2}(y)+C^{\prime}.

Taking into account (
32

∎

If

Letting

Conclusions.

We have found new solutions of the sinh-Gordon equation and the sine-Gordon equation, motivated by the Bäcklund transformation which relates solutions of these equations. Next in each such class of solutions we have provided an example of a corresponding harmonic map. Our aim next is to study sine-Gordon type equations, not necessarily elliptic, and their symmetries in order to extend our results. This project is currently under investigation and it is motivated by the study of wave maps between Lorentz spaces.

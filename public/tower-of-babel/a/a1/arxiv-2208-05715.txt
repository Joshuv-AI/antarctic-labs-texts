Analytical validation of the helicity conservation for the compressible Euler equations
Yanqing Wang, Wei Wei, Yulin Ye

Abstract
In [ 25 ] , Moffatt introduced the concept of helicity in an inviscid fluid and examined the helicity preservation of smooth solution to barotropic compressible flow. In this paper, it is shown that the weak solutions of the above system in Onsager type spaces B ˙ p , c ⁡ ( ℕ ) 1 / 3 \dot{B}^{1/3}_{p,c(\mathbb{N})} guarantee the conservation of the helicity. The parallel results of homogeneous incompressible Euler equations and the surface quasi-geostrophic equation are also obtained.

Introduction

The
Euler equations describing incompressible inviscid fluids with constant density in
\Omega\times(0,\,T)
read

\left\{\begin{aligned} &v_{t}+v\cdot\nabla v+\nabla\Pi=0,\\
&\text{div}\,v=0,\\
&v|_{t=0}=v_{0}(x),\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where the unknown vector
v
represents the flow velocity field, and the scalar function
\Pi=-\Delta^{-1}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}(v_{i}v_{j})
stands for the pressure. The
initial velocity
v_{0}
satisfies
\text{div}\,v_{0}=0
. Let
\Omega
be the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
or the periodic domain
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, where
d\geq 2
is the spatial dimension. We denote
\omega=\text{curl\,}v
by the vorticity of the flow velocity in (
1.1
), whose equations read

\omega_{t}+v\cdot\nabla\omega-\omega\cdot\nabla v=0,~~\text{div\,}\omega=0.

(1.2)

It is well known that there exist two physically conserved quantities for the regular solutions of the Euler system (
1.1
):

•

energy conservation

\int_{\Omega}|v(x,t)|^{2}dx=\int_{\Omega}|v(x,0)|^{2}dx.

(1.3)

•

helicity preservation

\int_{\Omega}\omega(x,t)\cdot v(x,t)dx=\int_{\Omega}\omega(x,0)\cdot v(x,0)dx.

(1.4)

Besides the above two conserved laws of the Euler equations (
1.1
), one can find others in
[
24
, p.24]
.
In a seminal paper
[
27
]
,
Onsager conjectured that weak solutions of the Euler equation with
Hölder continuity exponent
\alpha>\frac{1}{3}
do conserve energy and that turbulent
or anomalous dissipation occurs when
\alpha\leq\frac{1}{3}
. The first attempt to the energy conservation issue for weak solutions of the Euler equations (
1.1
) was given by Eyink
[
18
]
in a stronger Hölder space
C_{*}^{\alpha}
with
\alpha>\frac{1}{3}
,
which is included in
C^{\alpha}
. Subsequently Constantin-E-Titi
[
11
]
successfully solved the positive part of the
Onsager’s conjecture and proved that the energy is conserved if a weak solution
v
is in the Besov space
L^{3}(0,T;B^{\alpha}_{3,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))
with
\alpha>1/3
. Later on, by deriving a local energy equation which contains a term
D(v)
representing the dissipation or production of energy caused by the lack of smoothness of solution, Duchon and Robert gave a weaker condition on the solutions to conserve energy in
[
17
]
, that is, if
v
satisfies
\int|v(t,x+\xi)-v(t,x)|^{3}dx\leq C(t)|\xi|\sigma(|\xi|)
, where
C(t)\in L^{1}(0,T)
and
\sigma(a)\rightarrow 0
as
a\rightarrow 0
, then energy is conserved. Recently,
Cheskidov-Constantin-Friedlander-Shvydkoy
[
8
]
refined the above spaces to the
critical space
L^{3}(0,T;B^{1/3}_{3,c(\mathbb{N})})
, where

B^{1/3}_{3,c(\mathbb{N})}=\{v\in B^{1/3}_{3,\infty}:\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}2^{q}\|\Delta_{q}v\|^{3}_{L^{3}}=0\}
and
\Delta_{q}
represents a smooth restriction of
v
into Fourier modes of order
2^{q}
. In the follow-up paper, Shvydkoy
[
28
,
29
]
stated that this condition of energy conservation can be equivalent to

\lim_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|v(x+z,t)-v(x,t)|^{3}dxdt}{|z|}=0.

It is worth remarking that
B^{1/3}_{3,q}
for
1\leq q<\infty
are included in
B^{1/3}_{3,c(\mathbb{N})}
, which is called as the Onsager-critical space.
In this direction, the recent progress can be found in

[
20
]
.
The proof of the other part of
Onsager’s conjecture
in three-dimensional space is given by Isett
[
21
]
, which adopted the approach developed
by De Lellis and Szekelyhidi
[
13
,
14
,
12
,
15
]
.

Next, we turn our attention to the helicity conservation (
1.4
) in the Euler equations.
The concept of the
helicity in an inviscid fluid was introduced by Moffatt in
[
25
]
. As pointed in
[
25
,
26
]
,
helicity is important at a fundamental level in relation to flow kinematics
because it admits topological interpretation in relation to the linkage or
linkages of vortex lines of the flow.
Indeed,
Moffatt
[
25
]
examined that the helicity of smooth solutions to
the following compressible Euler equations (
1.5
) is invariant in time,

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\rho_{t}+\nabla\cdot(\rho v)=0,\\
&(\rho v)_{t}+\mathrm{div}\,(\rho v\otimes v)+\nabla\pi(\rho)=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.5)

The proof in
[
25
]
relies on the transport formula involving moving region. To the best of our knowledge, there has been little literature concerning the helicity preservation of weak solutions to
the compressible Euler equations (
1.5
). In this direction, most previous works focus on
the homogeneous incompressible Euler equations (
1.1
). In particular,
the study of helicity conservation for the incompressible Euler equations (
1.1
)
was originated from
Chae’s interesting works
[
5
,
4
]
.
In
[
4
]
, Chae considered the preservation of the helicity of weak solutions to the Euler equations via
\omega

in spaces
L^{3}(0,T;B^{\alpha}_{\frac{9}{5},\infty})
with
\alpha>\frac{1}{3}
.
Subsequently,
it is shown that
v\in L^{r_{1}}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{\frac{9}{2},q})
and

\omega\in L^{r_{2}}(0,T;\in\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{\frac{9}{5},q})
, with
\alpha>\frac{1}{3}
,
q\in[2,\infty]
,
r_{1}\in[2,\infty]
,
r_{2}\in[1,\infty]
and
\frac{2}{r_{1}}+\frac{1}{r_{2}}=1
, ensure the helicity conservation of weak solutions in
[
5
]
. After that, Cheskidov-Constantin-Friedlander-Shvydkoy
[
8
]
established the helicity conservation class based on the velocity
v
in Onsager critical space

L^{3}(0,T;B^{\frac{2}{3}}_{3,c(\mathbb{N})}).

(1.6)

Very recently, De Rosa
[
16
]
proved that the helicity is a constant provided that

v\in L^{2r}(0,T;W^{\theta,2p})
and
\omega\in L^{\kappa}(0,T;W^{\alpha,q})
with
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{\kappa}
and
2\theta+\alpha\geq 1
.
Notice that there holds the embedding relation
W^{\theta,p}\hookrightarrow B^{\theta}_{p,c(\mathbb{N})}
, which can be found in
[
16
,
8
]
.
The first objective of this paper is to address the question
how much regularity is needed for a weak solution of the compressible Euler equations (
1.5
) to conserve the helicity.
To this end, we shall need to give the equation for the vorticity
\omega
from (
1.5
). Our observation is that for any smooth solutions
(\rho,v)
of compressible Euler equations (
1.5
) with
0<c_{1}\leq\rho\leq c_{2}<\infty
, dividing the both sides of the momentum equation
\eqref{CEuler}_{2}
by
\rho
, we can reformulate the system (
1.5
) as

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\rho_{t}+\nabla\cdot(\rho v)=0,\\
&v_{t}+v\cdot\nabla v+\nabla\Pi(\rho)=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.7)

where
\Pi(\rho)=\int_{1}^{\rho}\frac{\pi^{\prime}(s)}{s}ds
.
Then the momentum equation in (
1.7
) is the same as that in
the incompressible case. However, this is not valid for weak solutions
(\rho,v)
even with
0<c_{1}\leq\rho\leq c_{2}<\infty
. To use the equations (
1.7
), we shall apply the techniques due to
Feireisl, Gwiazda, Swierczewska-Gwiazda and Wiedemann in
[
19
]
to show that the weak solutions of compressible Euler equations (
1.5
) in Onsager type spaces
B^{1/3}_{p,c(\mathbb{N})}
are also the weak solutions of equations (
1.7
).
Now we state our first main result on the helicity conservation of weak solutions for the compressible Euler equations (
1.7
) as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(\rho,v)
be a solution of (
1.5
) in the sense of distributions and
\text{div\,}v,\text{curl\,}v\in C([0,T];L^{\frac{2d}{d+1}}(\Omega))
. Assume that there exist two positive constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
such that

\displaystyle 0<c_{1}\leq\rho\leq c_{2}<\infty,~~\rho\in L^{3}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{3}}_{3,c(\mathbb{N})}),~\rho v\in L^{3}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{3}}_{3,\infty}),

(1.8)

\displaystyle v\in L^{3}(0,T;B^{\frac{1}{3}}_{3,c(\mathbb{N})}),\pi\in C^{2}[c_{1},c_{2}],

and one of the following four conditions is satisfied

(1)

\omega\in L^{3}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{3}}_{3,\infty});

(2)

\omega\in L^{3}(0,T;L^{3}(\Omega));

(3)

v\in L^{\frac{p}{p-2}}(0,T;L^{\frac{q}{q-2}}(\Omega)),\omega\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\Omega)),
with
2<p,q<\infty
;

(4)

\text{div\,}v,\omega\in L^{3}(0,T;L^{\frac{9}{4}}(\Omega)),
with
d=3
.

Then the helicity of the compressible Euler equation (
1.5
) is conserved, that is, for any
0<t<T
,

\int_{\Omega}\omega(x,t)\cdot v(x,t)dx=\int_{\Omega}\omega(x,0)\cdot v(x,0)dx.

Remark 1.1
.

The helicity conservation of weak solutions for the compressible Euler equations (
1.5
)
in Onsager’s critical space
B^{\theta}_{p,c(\mathbb{N})}
and
B^{\theta}_{p,\infty}
in terms of the velocity and the vorticity is studied in
Theorem
1.2
and Corollary
1.3
. This is consistent with Moffatt’s work
[
25
]
. The regularity condition that
\text{div\,}v,\text{curl\,}v\in C([0,T];L^{\frac{2d}{d+1}}(\Omega))
is necessary to ensure that the helicity is well defined.

Remark 1.2
.

The constraint that
\rho v\in L^{3}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{3}}_{3,\infty})
can be removed, if the condition that
\rho\in L^{3}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{3}}_{3,c(\mathbb{N})})
is replaced by
\rho\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{3}}_{\infty,c(\mathbb{N})})
due to Lemma
2.5
.

Remark 1.3
.

This theorem is valid for the compressible isentropic Euler system. Indeed, for the isentropic pressure law
\pi(\rho)=\kappa\rho^{\gamma}
with
\gamma>1
and
\kappa=\frac{(\gamma-1)^{2}}{4\gamma},
the pressure condition that
\pi\in C^{2}[c_{1},c_{2}]
is obviously satisfied if
0<c_{1}\leq\rho\leq c_{2}<\infty
.

Remark 1.4
.

The sufficient conditions (3) in this theorem and (4)-(5) in Theorem
1.2
below are partially motivated by the recent works
[
31
]
on energy balance of the Navier-Stokes equations based on the combination of the velocity and its gradient.

Remark 1.5
.

Since the following embedding relations in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is valid

W^{1,\frac{9}{4}}\hookrightarrow H^{\frac{7}{6}}\hookrightarrow B^{\frac{2}{3}}_{3,c(\mathbb{N})},

the fourth criterion of this theorem is also in Onsager’s critical space.
It is an interesting question to show
the velocity
v
in Onsager’s critical space

L^{3}(0,T;B^{\frac{2}{3}}_{3,c(\mathbb{N})})
keeping the helicity conservation for the compressible Euler equations.

Remark 1.9

In dimension two, the criterion (1) in Corollary

The starting point
of (1) in Theorem
1.1

is the study of the cross-helicity in the ideal MHD equations.
Precisely, for any vector functions

\displaystyle\nabla(A\cdot B)=A\cdot\nabla B+B\cdot\nabla A+A\times\text{curl}B+B\times\text{curl}A,

\displaystyle\text{curl}(A\times B)=A\text{div}B-B\text{div}A+B\cdot\nabla A-A\cdot\nabla B,

which together with the condition that
\mathrm{div}\,\omega=0
lead to

\displaystyle v\cdot\nabla v=\frac{1}{2}\nabla|v|^{2}+\omega\times v,

(1.10)

\displaystyle\text{curl}(\omega\times v)=\omega\text{div}v+v\cdot\nabla\omega-\omega\cdot\nabla v.

Then we can use (

\left\{\begin{aligned} &v_{t}+\omega\times v+\nabla(\Pi(\rho)+\frac{1}{2}|v|^{2})=0,\\

&\text{div\,}\omega=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.11)

which is very closed to the inviscid ideal Magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) equations

\left\{\begin{aligned} &v_{t}+\omega\times v-b\cdot\nabla b+\nabla(p+\frac{1}{2}|v|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|b|^{2})=0,\\
&b_{t}-\text{curl}(v\times b)=0,\\
&\mathrm{div}\,v=\mathrm{div}\,b=0,\end{aligned}\right.

where

describes the magnetic field.

To define Besov spaces, we need the following dyadic unity partition

\Delta_{j}u:=0~~\text{if}~~j\leq-2,~~\Delta_{-1}u:=\varrho(D)u=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{h}(y)u(x-y)dy,

\text{and}~~\Delta_{j}u:=\varphi\left(2^{-j}D\right)u=2^{jd}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h(2^{j}y)u(x-y)dy~~\text{if}~~j\geq 0.

The nonhomogeneous low-frequency cut-off operator
S_{j}
is defined by

S_{j}u:=\sum_{k\leq j-1}\Delta_{k}u.

The homogeneous dyadic blocks
\dot{\Delta}_{j}
and homogeneous low-frequency cut-off operators
\dot{S}_{j}
are defined for every
j\in\mathbb{Z}
by

\dot{\Delta}_{j}u:=\varphi(2^{-j}D)u=2^{jd}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h(2^{j}y)u(x-y)dy,

\text{ and }~~\dot{S}_{j}u:=\varrho(2^{-j}D)u=2^{jd}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{h}(2^{j}y)u(x-y)dy.

Then for
-\infty<s<\infty
and
1\leq p,q\leq\infty,
the homogeneous Besov semi-norm
\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}}
of
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}/\mathcal{P}
is given by

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{jqs}\left\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}f\right\|_{L^{p}}^{q}\right)^{1/q},~~\text{if}\ q\in[1,\infty),\\
\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{js}\left\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}f\right\|_{L^{p}},~~~~~~~~~\text{if}~q=\infty.\end{array}\right.

Moreover, for
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
, we define the inhomogeneous Besov norm
\left\|f\right\|_{B^{s}_{p,q}}
of
f\in\mathcal{S}^{{}^{\prime}}
as

\left\|f\right\|_{B^{s}_{p,q}}=\left\|f\right\|_{{L^{p}}}+\left\|f\right\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}}.

Motivated by
[
8
]
, we denote
\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{p,c(\mathbb{N})}
with
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
as the class of all tempered distributions
f
satisfying

\left\|f\right\|_{\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{p,\infty}}<\infty~\text{and}~\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}2^{j\alpha}\left\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}f\right\|_{L^{p}}=0.

(2.1)

It is clear that the Besov
spaces
\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{p,q}
are included in
\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{p,c(\mathbb{N})}
for any
1\leq q<\infty
. For more background on harmonic analysis in the context of fluids, we refer the readers to
[
6
]
.

Furthermore, we let
\eta_{\varepsilon}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a standard mollifier, i.e.
\eta(x)=C_{0}e^{-\frac{1}{1-|x|^{2}}}
for
|x|<1
and
\eta(x)=0
for
|x|\geq 1
, where
C_{0}
is a constant such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\eta(x)dx=1
. For
\varepsilon>0
, the rescaled mollifier
\eta_{\varepsilon}(x)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\eta(\frac{x}{\varepsilon})
, and for any function
f\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, its mollified version is defined by

f^{\varepsilon}(x)=(f*\eta_{\varepsilon})(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x-y)\eta_{\varepsilon}(y)dy,\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Subsequently, we collect some Lemmas which will be used in the present paper.

Lemma 2.1

(Bernstein inequality)
.

For any
b\geq a\geq 1
, there holds that

\left\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}f\right\|_{L^{b}}\leq 2^{jd(\frac{1}{a}-\frac{1}{b})}\left\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}f\right\|_{L^{a}}.

(2.2)

The following lemma gives a characterization of Besov spaces in terms of finite differences in the same spirit of
[
28
,
34
,
17
]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
p\in[1,\infty]
, then for any
f\in\mathcal{S}^{{}^{\prime}}_{h}
, there holds

f\in{\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{p,\infty}}\Longleftrightarrow ess\sup_{|y|>0}\frac{\|f(\cdot-y)-f(\cdot)\|_{L^{p}}}{|y|^{\alpha}}<\infty;

(2.3)

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}2^{j\alpha}\left\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}f\right\|_{L^{p}}=0\Longleftrightarrow\lim\limits_{|y|\rightarrow 0}\frac{\|f(\cdot-y)-f(\cdot)\|_{L^{p}}}{|y|^{\alpha}}=0.

(2.4)

Proof.

(1) “LHS
\Rightarrow
RHS” in (
2.3
). First, by the mean value theorem, the Bernstein inequality and the Minkowski inequality, we have for any tempered distribution
f
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\|f(\cdot-y)-f(\cdot)\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\left(\sum_{j\leq N}2^{j}|y|\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L^{p}}+\sum_{j>N}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L^{p}}\right).

(2.5)

Before going further, just as
[
8
]
, we set the following localized kernel

K(j)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &2^{j\alpha},~~~~~~~~\text{if}~j\leq 0,\\
&2^{-(1-\alpha)j},~\text{if}~j>0,\end{aligned}\right.

and denote
d_{j}=2^{j\alpha}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L^{p}}
.

As a consequence, using the mean value theorem, the Bernstein inequality and the Minkowski inequality again, we rewrite (
2.5
) as

\displaystyle\|f(\cdot-y)-f(\cdot)\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(2^{N(1-\alpha)}|y|\sum_{j\leq N}2^{-(N-j)(1-\alpha)}2^{j\alpha}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L^{p}}+2^{-\alpha N}\sum_{j>N}2^{(N-j)\alpha}2^{j\alpha}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L^{p}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(2^{N(1-\alpha)}|y|+2^{-\alpha N}\right)(K\ast d_{j})(N).

Thanks to the localized kernel
K(\cdot)\in l^{1}
, by choosing
N
such that

2^{N(1-\alpha)}|y|\approx 2^{-N\alpha},

we arrive at

\|f(\cdot-y)-f(\cdot)\|_{L^{p}}\leq C|y|^{\alpha}(K\ast d_{j})(N)\leq C|y|^{\alpha}\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}d_{j}.

(2.6)

This implies that
ess\sup_{|y|>0}\frac{\|f(\cdot-y)-f(\cdot)\|_{L^{p}}}{|y|^{\alpha}}\leq C\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{p,\infty}}
.

Now we will prove the reverse inequality “LHS
\Longleftarrow
RHS” in (
2.3
). As the mean value of the function
h
is
0
, we can write

\displaystyle\dot{\Delta}_{j}f(x)

\displaystyle=2^{jd}\int h(2^{j}y)f(x-y)dy

(2.7)

\displaystyle=2^{jd}\int h(2^{j}y)\left(f(x-y)-f(x)\right)dy,

which together with Minkowski inequality yields that

\displaystyle 2^{j\alpha}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}f\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{jd}\int 2^{j\alpha}|h(2^{j}y)|\|f(\cdot-y)-f(\cdot)\|_{L^{p}}dy

(2.8)

\displaystyle\leq ess\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\|f(\cdot-y)-f(\cdot)\|_{L^{p}}}{|y|^{\alpha}}\int 2^{j(\alpha+d)}|y|^{\alpha}|h(2^{j}y)|dy

\displaystyle\leq C\,ess\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\|f(\cdot-y)-f(\cdot)\|_{L^{p}}}{|y|^{\alpha}}.

Then we conclude the result (
2.3
).

(2) The proof of (
2.4
) is similar to (
2.3
). Due to (
2.6
) and (
2.8
), we immediately obtain the result (
2.4
) after taking the limits.
We complete the proof of this lemma.
∎

As a corollary of Lemma
2.2
, we have the following properties.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
0<\alpha,\beta<1
and
1\leq q_{1},q_{2}\leq\infty
. Assume that
f\in\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{q_{1},\infty}
and
g\in\dot{B}^{\beta}_{q_{2},c(\mathbb{N})}
, then we have

\displaystyle f\in\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{q_{1},\infty}\Leftrightarrow ess\sup_{|y|>0}\frac{\|f(\cdot-y)-f(\cdot)\|_{L^{q_{1}}}}{|y|^{\alpha}}<\infty,

(2.9)

\displaystyle g\in\dot{B}^{\beta}_{q_{2},c(\mathbb{N})}\Leftrightarrow ess\sup_{|y|>0}\frac{\|g(\cdot-y)-g(\cdot)\|_{L^{q_{2}}}}{|y|^{\beta}}<\infty~\text{and}~\lim\limits_{|y|\rightarrow 0}\frac{\|g(\cdot-y)-g(\cdot)\|_{L^{q_{2}}}}{|y|^{\beta}}=0.

Moreover, there hold

\displaystyle\|f(\cdot-y)-f(\cdot)\|_{L^{q_{1}}}=\text{O}(|y|^{\alpha})\left\|f\right\|_{\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{q_{1},\infty}},

(2.10)

\displaystyle\|g(\cdot-y)-g(\cdot)\|_{L^{q_{2}}}=\text{o}(|y|^{\beta})\left\|g\right\|_{\dot{B}^{\beta}_{q_{2},c(\mathbb{N})}}.

Combining the properties of mollifier with Corollary
2.3
, we derive the following Lemma.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in(0,1)
,
p,q\in[1,\infty]
, and
k\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
. Assume that
f\in L^{p}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{q,\infty})
,
g\in L^{p}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\beta}_{q,c(\mathbb{N})})
, then there hold

(1)

\|f^{\varepsilon}-f\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q})}\leq\text{O}(\varepsilon^{\alpha})\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{q,\infty})}
;

(2)

\|\nabla^{k}f^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q})}\leq\text{O}(\varepsilon^{\alpha-k})\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{q,\infty})}
;

(3)

\|g^{\varepsilon}-g\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q})}\leq\text{o}(\varepsilon^{\beta})\|g\|_{L^{p}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\beta}_{q,c(\mathbb{N})})}
;

(4)

\|\nabla^{k}g^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q})}\leq\text{o}(\varepsilon^{\beta-k})\|g\|_{L^{p}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\beta}_{q,c(\mathbb{N})})}.

Proof.

(1) Since
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\eta_{\varepsilon}(y)dy=1
, we deduce from direct calculations that

\displaystyle f^{\varepsilon}(x)-f(x)=

\displaystyle\int f(x-y)\eta_{\varepsilon}(y)dy-f(x)\int\eta_{\varepsilon}(y)dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int[f(x-y)-f(x)]\eta_{\varepsilon}(y)dy.

According to the Minkowski inequality and Corollary
2.3
, we see that

\displaystyle\|f^{\varepsilon}-f\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q})}\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}\|f(x-y)-f(x)\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q})}\eta_{\varepsilon}(y)dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}O(|y|^{\alpha})\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{q,\infty})}\eta_{\varepsilon}(y)dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}O(\varepsilon^{\alpha})\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{q,\infty})}\eta_{\varepsilon}(y)dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle O(\varepsilon^{\alpha})\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{q,\infty})},

which concludes (1).

(2) Some straightforward computations yield that

\displaystyle\nabla^{k}f^{\varepsilon}(x)=\nabla^{k}(f\ast\eta_{\varepsilon})(x)=

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}\nabla^{k}_{x}f(x-y)\eta_{\varepsilon}(y)dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{k}\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}\nabla^{k}_{y}f(x-y)\eta_{\varepsilon}(y)dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\varepsilon}^{-k}\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}f(x-y)\nabla^{k}\eta_{\varepsilon}(y)dy.

Using the fact
\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}\nabla^{k}\eta(y)dy=0
, we infer that

{\varepsilon}^{-k}\int\nabla^{k}\eta_{\varepsilon}(y)dy=0.

(2.11)

Hence, we arrive at

\nabla f^{\varepsilon}(x)={\varepsilon}^{-k}\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}[f(x-y)-f(x)]\nabla^{k}\eta_{\varepsilon}(y)dy.

Then making use of the Minkowski inequality and Corollary
2.3
once again,
we know that

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{k}f^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q})}\leq

\displaystyle C{\varepsilon}^{-k}\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}\|f(x-y)-f(x)\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q})}|\nabla^{k}\eta_{\varepsilon}(y)|dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\varepsilon}^{-k}\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}O(|y|^{\alpha})\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{q,\infty})}|\nabla^{k}\eta_{\varepsilon}(y)|dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\varepsilon}^{-k}\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}O(\varepsilon^{\alpha})\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{q,\infty})}|\nabla^{k}\eta_{\varepsilon}(y)|dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle O(\varepsilon^{\alpha-k})\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{q,\infty})}.

This verifies the second part of this lemma. Exactly as the above derivation, we can finish the proof of the rest part of this lemma.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
p\in[1,\infty]
, then there hold

\|fg\|_{{\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{p,\infty}}}\leq C\left(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|g\|_{{\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{p,\infty}}}+\|f\|_{{\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{\infty,\infty}}}\|g\|_{L^{p}}\right),~\text{for any}~f\in B^{\alpha}_{\infty,\infty},g\in B^{\alpha}_{p,\infty},

(2.12)

and

\|fg\|_{{\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{p,c(\mathbb{N})}}}\leq C\left(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|g\|_{{\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{p,c(\mathbb{N})}}}+\|f\|_{{\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{\infty,c(\mathbb{N})}}}\|g\|_{L^{p}}\right),\text{for any}~f\in B^{\alpha}_{\infty,c(\mathbb{N})},g\in B^{\alpha}_{p,c(\mathbb{N})}.

(2.13)

Proof.

Using the triangle inequality ,we have

\displaystyle|f(x-y)g(x-y)-f(x)g(x)|

(2.14)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\displaystyle|f(x-y)\Big(g(x-y)-g(x)\Big)|+|\Big(f(x-y)-f(x)\Big)g(x)|},

which together with the Minkowski inequality implies that

\displaystyle\frac{\|f(\cdot-y)g(\cdot-y)-fg(\cdot)\|_{L^{p}}}{|y|^{\alpha}}

(2.15)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\frac{\|f(\cdot-y)\Big(g(\cdot-y)-g(\cdot)\Big)\|_{L^{p}}}{|y|^{\alpha}}+\frac{\|\Big(f(\cdot-y)-f(\cdot)\Big)g(\cdot)\|_{L^{p}}}{|y|^{\alpha}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\frac{\|g(\cdot-y)-g(\cdot)\|_{L^{p}}}{|y|^{\alpha}}+\frac{\|f(\cdot-y)-f(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}}{|y|^{\alpha}}\|g\|_{L^{p}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|g\|_{{\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{p,\infty}}}+\|f\|_{{\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{\infty,\infty}}}\|g\|_{L^{p}}\right),

where we have used the Corollary
2.3
.
Moreover, taking the limits on (
2.15
) and using the Corollary
2.3
again, we have

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{|y|\rightarrow 0}\frac{\|f(\cdot-y)g(\cdot-y)-fg(\cdot)\|_{L^{p}}}{|y|^{\alpha}}

(2.16)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\lim\limits_{|y|\rightarrow 0}\frac{\|g(\cdot-y)-g(\cdot)\|_{L^{p}}}{|y|^{\alpha}}+\lim\limits_{|y|\rightarrow 0}\frac{\|f(\cdot-y)-f(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}}{|y|^{\alpha}}\|g\|_{L^{p}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|g\|_{{\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{p,c(\mathbb{N})}}}+\|f\|_{{\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{\infty,c(\mathbb{N})}}}\|g\|_{L^{p}}\right).

∎

Next, we will state a new Constantin-E-Titi type commutator estimate as follows.

Lemma 2.6
.

Assume that
0<\alpha,\beta<1
,
1\leq p,q,p_{1},p_{2}\leq\infty
and
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}
.
Then, there holds

\displaystyle\|(fg)^{\varepsilon}-f^{\varepsilon}g^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q})}\leq\text{o}(\varepsilon^{\alpha+\beta}),

(2.17)

provided that one of the following three conditions is satisfied,

(1)

f\in L^{p_{1}}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{q_{1},c(\mathbb{N})})
,
g\in L^{p_{2}}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\beta}_{q_{2},\infty})
,
1\leq q_{1},q_{2}\leq\infty
,
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}
;

(2)

\nabla f\in L^{p_{1}}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{q_{1},c(\mathbb{N})})
,
\nabla g\in L^{p_{2}}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\beta}_{q_{2},\infty})
,
\frac{2}{d}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}
,
1\leq q_{1},q_{2}<d
;

(3)

f\in L^{p_{1}}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{q_{1},c(\mathbb{N})})
,
\nabla g\in L^{p_{2}}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\beta}_{q_{2},\infty})
,
\frac{1}{d}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}
,
1\leq q_{2}<d
,
1\leq q_{1}\leq\infty
.

Remark 2.1
.

The estimate that
\int_{0}^{T}\int|(fg)^{\varepsilon}-f^{\varepsilon}g^{\varepsilon}||\nabla h|dxdt
frequently appears in the study of energy (helicity) conservation of fluid equations
(see
[
11
,
32
,
33
,
7
,
16
,
31
]
).
Therefore, it seems that this lemma is very helpful in this research of weak solutions in critical Besov spaces.

Remark 2.2
.

We would like to point out that
o(\cdot)
should be replaced by
O(\cdot)
if the space
\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{q_{1},c(\mathbb{N})}
in the condition of this lemma is replaced by the space
\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{q_{1},\infty}
.

Remark 2.3
.

It is worth remarking that
\nabla
may be replaced by curl for incompressible flow if

.

Remark 2.4
.

The results of Lemma
2.2
-
2.6
still hold for the nonhomogeneous Besov spaces.

Proof.

First, we recall the following identity observed by Constantin-E-Titi in
[
11
]
that

\displaystyle(fg)^{\varepsilon}(x)-f^{\varepsilon}g^{\varepsilon}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\eta_{\varepsilon}(y)[f(x-y)-f(x)][g(x-y)-g(x)]dy-(f-f^{\varepsilon})(g-g^{\varepsilon})(x).

(1) Using the Hölder’s inequality and Minkowski inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\|(fg)^{\varepsilon}-f^{\varepsilon}g^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{|y|\leq\varepsilon}\eta_{\varepsilon}(y)\|f(\cdot-y)-f(\cdot)\|_{L^{p_{1}}(0,T;L^{q_{1}})}\|g(\cdot-y)-g(\cdot)\|_{L^{p_{2}}(0,T;L^{q_{2}})}dy

\displaystyle+C\|f-f^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p_{1}}(0,T;L^{q_{1}})}\|g-g^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p_{2}}(0,T;L^{q_{2}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle o(\varepsilon^{\alpha+\beta})\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{q_{1},c(\mathbb{N})})}\|g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\beta}_{q_{2},\infty})},

where Corollary
2.3
and Lemma
2.4
are used.

(2) Let
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{q_{1}^{\natural}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}^{\natural}}
and
\frac{1}{q_{i}^{\natural}}=\frac{1}{q_{i}}-\frac{1}{d}
,
i=1,2.
A slight variant of the above proof implies that

\displaystyle\|(fg)^{\varepsilon}-f^{\varepsilon}g^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{|y|\leq\varepsilon}\eta_{\varepsilon}(y)\|f(\cdot-y)-f(\cdot)\|_{L^{p_{1}}(0,T;L^{q_{1}^{\natural}})}\|g(\cdot-y)-g(\cdot)\|_{L^{p_{2}}(0,T;L^{q_{2}^{\natural}})}dy

\displaystyle+C\|f-f^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p_{1}}(0,T;L^{q_{1}^{\natural}})}\|g-g^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p_{2}}(0,T;L^{q_{2}^{\natural}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{|y|\leq\varepsilon}\eta_{\varepsilon}(y)\|\nabla f(\cdot-y)-\nabla f(\cdot)\|_{L^{p_{1}}(0,T;L^{q_{1}})}\|\nabla g(\cdot-y)-\nabla g(\cdot)\|_{L^{p_{2}}(0,T;L^{q_{2}})}dy

\displaystyle+C\|\nabla f-\nabla f^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p_{1}}(0,T;L^{q_{1}})}\|\nabla g-\nabla g^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p_{2}}(0,T;L^{q_{2}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle o(\varepsilon^{\alpha+\beta})\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{q_{1},c(\mathbb{N})})}\|\nabla g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\beta}_{q_{2},\infty})},

where we have used the Hölder inequality and Sobolev embedding theorem.

(3) Arguing in the same manner as above, we know that

\displaystyle\|(fg)^{\varepsilon}-f^{\varepsilon}g^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{|y|\leq\varepsilon}\eta_{\varepsilon}(y)\|f(\cdot-y)-f(\cdot)\|_{L^{p_{1}}(0,T;L^{q_{1}})}\|g(\cdot-y)-g(\cdot)\|_{L^{p_{2}}(0,T;L^{q_{2}^{\natural}})}dy

\displaystyle+C\|f-f^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p_{1}}(0,T;L^{q_{1}})}\|g-g^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p_{2}}(0,T;L^{q_{2}^{\natural}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{|y|\leq\varepsilon}\eta_{\varepsilon}(y)\|f(\cdot-y)-f(\cdot)\|_{L^{p_{1}}(0,T;L^{q_{1}})}\|\nabla g(\cdot-y)-\nabla g(\cdot)\|_{L^{p_{2}}(0,T;L^{q_{2}})}dy

\displaystyle+C\|f-f^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p_{1}}(0,T;L^{q_{1}})}\|\nabla g-\nabla g^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p_{2}}(0,T;L^{q_{2}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle o(\varepsilon^{\alpha+\beta})\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{q_{1},c(\mathbb{N})})}\|\nabla g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\beta}_{q_{2},\infty})},

where the indices are required to satisfy
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}^{\natural}}
and
\frac{1}{q_{2}^{\natural}}=\frac{1}{q_{2}}-\frac{1}{d}
with
1\leq q_{2}<d
.

Then the proof of this lemma is completed.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
p,q,p_{1},q_{1},p_{2},q_{2}\in[1,\infty)
with
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}},\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}
. Assume that
f\in L^{p_{1}}(0,T;L^{q_{1}}(\Omega))
and
g\in L^{p_{2}}(0,T;L^{q_{2}}(\Omega))
. Then, as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, there hold

\|(fg)^{\varepsilon}-f^{\varepsilon}g^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\Omega))}\rightarrow 0,

(2.18)

and

\|(f\times g)^{\varepsilon}-f^{\varepsilon}\times g^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\Omega))}\rightarrow 0.

(2.19)

Proof.

First, it follows from the triangle inequality that

\displaystyle\|(fg)^{\varepsilon}-f^{\varepsilon}g^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|(fg)^{\varepsilon}-(fg)\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q})}+\|fg-f^{\varepsilon}g\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q})}+\|f^{\varepsilon}g-f^{\varepsilon}g^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(\|(fg)^{\varepsilon}-fg\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q})}+\|f-f^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p_{1}}(0,T;L^{q_{1}})}\|g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(0,T;L^{q_{2}})}

\displaystyle+\|f^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p_{1}}(0,T;L^{q_{1}})}\|g-g^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p_{2}}(0,T;L^{q_{2}})}\Big),

which together with the properties of the standard mollifiers yields (
2.18
).

Furthermore, to conclude (
2.19
), we only consider the case that the spatial dimension
d=3
, since the proof for the case
d=2
is similar. Without loss of generality, we assume that
f=(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})
and
g=(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3})
.
Then by a direct computation, we have

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\|(f\times g)^{\varepsilon}-f^{\varepsilon}\times g^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q})}=\Big\|\left(\left|\begin{matrix}\overrightarrow{i}&\overrightarrow{j}&\overrightarrow{k}\\
f_{1}&f_{2}&f_{3}\\
g_{1}&g_{2}&g_{3}\\
\end{matrix}\right|\right)^{\varepsilon}-\left|\begin{matrix}\overrightarrow{i}&\overrightarrow{j}&\overrightarrow{k}\\
f_{1}^{\varepsilon}&f_{2}^{\varepsilon}&f_{3}^{\varepsilon}\\
g_{1}^{\varepsilon}&g_{2}^{\varepsilon}&g_{3}^{\varepsilon}\\
\end{matrix}\right|\Big\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q})}

(2.20)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\|\left((f_{2}g_{3}-f_{3}g_{2})\overrightarrow{i}-(f_{1}g_{3}-f_{3}g_{1})\overrightarrow{j}+(f_{1}g_{2}-f_{2}g_{1})\overrightarrow{k}\right)^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle-\left((f_{2}^{\varepsilon}g_{3}^{\varepsilon}-f_{3}^{\varepsilon}g_{2}^{\varepsilon})\overrightarrow{i}-(f_{1}^{\varepsilon}g_{3}^{\varepsilon}-f_{3}^{\varepsilon}g_{1}^{\varepsilon})\overrightarrow{j}+(f_{1}^{\varepsilon}g_{2}^{\varepsilon}-f_{2}^{\varepsilon}g_{1}^{\varepsilon})\overrightarrow{k}\right)\Big\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\|\left[\Big((f_{2}g_{3})^{\varepsilon}-f_{2}^{\varepsilon}g_{3}^{\varepsilon}\Big)-\Big((f_{3}g_{2})^{\varepsilon}-f_{3}^{\varepsilon}g_{2}^{\varepsilon}\Big)\right]\overrightarrow{i}-\left[\Big((f_{1}g_{3})^{\varepsilon}-f_{1}^{\varepsilon}g_{3}^{\varepsilon}\Big)-\Big((f_{3}g_{1})^{\varepsilon}-f_{3}^{\varepsilon}g_{1}^{\varepsilon}\Big)\right]\overrightarrow{j}

\displaystyle+\left[\Big((f_{1}g_{2})^{\varepsilon}-f_{1}^{\varepsilon}g_{2}^{\varepsilon}\Big)-\Big((f_{2}g_{1})^{\varepsilon}-f_{2}^{\varepsilon}g_{1}^{\varepsilon}\Big)\right]\overrightarrow{k}\Big\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q})},

which together with the triangle inequality and (
2.18
) leads to (
2.19
).
∎

For the convenience of readers, we end this section by presenting the definition of weak solutions to the compressible Euler equations (

), incompressible Euler equations (
1.1
) and the surface quasi-geostrophic equation
(

Definition 2.1
.

A pair (
\rho,v
) is called a weak solution to the compressible Euler equations (

), if (

) satisfies

(i)

for any test function

, there holds

\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}\rho(x,t)\partial_{t}\phi(x,t)+\rho(x,t)v(x,t)\nabla\phi(x,t)dxdt=0.

(ii)

for any test vector field
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega)
, there holds

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\rho(x,t)v(x,t)\partial_{t}\varphi(x,t)

\displaystyle+(\rho(x,t)v(x,t)\otimes v(x,t))\nabla\varphi(x,t)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\pi(\rho)(x,t)\text{div}\varphi(x,t)dxdt=0.

(iii)

the energy inequality holds

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(t)\leq\mathcal{E}(0),

(2.21)

where
\mathcal{E}(t)=\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\rho|v|^{2}+\kappa\frac{\rho^{\gamma}}{\gamma-1}\right)dx
.

Definition 2.2
.

(i)

(ii)

v

.

3
Helicity conservation for the compressible Euler equations

This section contains the proof of helicity conservation of weak solutions in Onsager type spaces
\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{3}}_{p,c(\mathbb{N})}
for the compressible Euler equations.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(\rho,v)
be a weak solution of compressible Euler equations (
1.5
) in the sense of Definition
2.1
, and
\text{div\,}v,\text{curl\,}v\in C([0,T];L^{\frac{2d}{d+1}}(\Omega))
. If
(\rho,v)
additionally satisfies

\displaystyle 0<c_{1}\leq\rho\leq c_{2}<\infty,~\rho\in L^{3}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{3}}_{3,c(\mathbb{N})}),~\rho v\in L^{3}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{3}}_{3,\infty}),

\displaystyle v\in L^{3}(0,T;B^{\frac{1}{3}}_{3,c(\mathbb{N})}),

then
(\rho,v)
is also a weak solution of system (
1.7
) in the sense of distributions.

Proof.

First, we mollify the momentum equation
\eqref{CEuler}_{2}
in space (with the kernel and notation as in Section 2):

\displaystyle(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}_{t}+\text{div}(\rho v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}+\nabla\pi(\rho)^{\varepsilon}=0.

(3.1)

Then, in virtue of appropriate commutators, the above equation can be rewritten as

\displaystyle(\rho^{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon})_{t}

\displaystyle+\text{div}((\rho v)^{\varepsilon}\otimes v^{\varepsilon})+\nabla\pi(\rho^{\varepsilon})

(3.2)

\displaystyle=-[(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}-(\rho^{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon})]_{t}-\text{div}[(\rho v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}-(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}\otimes v^{\varepsilon}]-\nabla[\pi(\rho^{\varepsilon})-\pi(\rho)^{\varepsilon}].

Taking into account of mollified version of the continuity
equation

\rho^{\varepsilon}_{t}+\text{div}(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}=0,

(3.3)

a direct computation shows

\displaystyle\rho^{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon}_{t}+\rho^{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla v^{\varepsilon}+\nabla\pi(\rho^{\varepsilon})=

\displaystyle-[(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}-\rho^{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon}]\cdot\nabla v^{\varepsilon}-[(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}-(\rho^{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon})]_{t}

(3.4)

\displaystyle-\text{div}[(\rho v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}-(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}\otimes v^{\varepsilon}]-\nabla[\pi(\rho^{\varepsilon})-\pi(\rho)^{\varepsilon}].

Since
0<c_{1}\leq\rho\leq c_{2}<\infty
, setting
\Pi(\rho^{\varepsilon})=\int_{1}^{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\frac{\pi^{\prime}(s)}{s}ds
and dividing both sides of equation (
3.4
) by
\rho^{\varepsilon}
, we arrive at

\displaystyle v^{\varepsilon}_{t}+\omega^{\varepsilon}\times v^{\varepsilon}+\nabla\left(\Pi(\rho^{\varepsilon})+\frac{1}{2}|v^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right)

(3.5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}[(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}-\rho^{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon}]\cdot\nabla v^{\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}[(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}-(\rho^{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon})]_{t}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\text{div}[(\rho v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}-(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}\otimes v^{\varepsilon}]-\frac{1}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\nabla[\pi(\rho^{\varepsilon})-\pi(\rho)^{\varepsilon}],

where we have used the identity that
v^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla v^{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{2}\nabla|v^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\omega^{\varepsilon}\times v^{\varepsilon}
with
\omega=\text{curl\,}v
.

Let
\varphi(x,t)\in C_{0}^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega)
be a test function. Multiplication with
\varphi
and then integration yield that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\left(v^{\varepsilon}_{\tau}+\omega^{\varepsilon}\times v^{\varepsilon}+\nabla\left(\Pi(\rho^{\varepsilon})+\frac{1}{2}|v^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right)\right)\varphi dxd\tau

(3.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\Big(-\frac{1}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}[(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}-\rho^{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon}]\cdot\nabla v^{\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}[(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}-(\rho^{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon})]_{\tau}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\text{div}[(\rho v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}-(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}\otimes v^{\varepsilon}]-\frac{1}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\nabla[\pi(\rho^{\varepsilon})-\pi(\rho)^{\varepsilon}]\Big)\varphi dxd\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}.

Next we will show that the four terms
I_{1}-I_{4}
on the RHS of (
3.6
) tend to zero as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.

Indeed, note that

\rho\in L^{3}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{3}}_{3,c(\mathbb{N})}),\rho v\in L^{3}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{3}}_{3,\infty}),v\in L^{3}(0,T;{B}^{\frac{1}{3}}_{3,c(\mathbb{N})}).

Using Corollary
2.3
, Lemma
2.4
and the Hölder inequality, the first term
I_{1}
on the RHS of (
3.6
) can be handled as

\displaystyle|I_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq C\|(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}-\rho^{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T;L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega))}\|\nabla v^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{3}(0,T;L^{3}(\Omega))}\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega))}

(3.7)

\displaystyle\leq o(1)\|\rho\|_{L^{3}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{3}}_{3,c(\mathbb{N})})}\|v\|_{L^{3}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{3}}_{3,c(\mathbb{N})})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq o(1).

For second term
I_{2}
, using integration by parts, conbination the Corollary
2.3
with Lemma
2.4
, we have

\displaystyle|I_{2}|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}[(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}-(\rho^{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon})]\left(-\frac{\partial_{\tau}\rho^{\varepsilon}}{(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{2}}\varphi+\frac{1}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\partial_{\tau}\varphi\right)dxd\tau\right|

(3.8)

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}[(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}-(\rho^{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon})]\left(\frac{\text{div}(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}}{(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{2}}\varphi+\frac{1}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\partial_{\tau}\varphi\right)dxd\tau\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\|(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}-\rho^{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T;L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega))}\left(\|\nabla(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{3}(0,T;L^{3}(\Omega))}+\|\partial_{\tau}\varphi\|_{L^{3}(0,T;L^{3}(\Omega))}\right)

\displaystyle\leq o(\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}})\|\rho\|_{L^{3}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{3}}_{3,c(\mathbb{N})})}\|v\|_{L^{3}(0,T;{\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{3}}_{3,c(\mathbb{N})}})}\big(O(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}-1})\|\rho v\|_{L^{3}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{3}}_{3,\infty})}+1\big)

\displaystyle\leq o(1)+o(\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}).

The third term
I_{3}
can be estimated similarly as
I_{2}
:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |I_{3}|&=\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}[(\rho v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}-(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}\otimes v^{\varepsilon}]\left(-\frac{\nabla\rho^{\varepsilon}}{(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{2}}\varphi+\frac{\nabla\varphi}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\right)dxd\tau\right|\\
&\leq C\|(\rho v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}-(\rho v)^{\varepsilon}\otimes v^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T;L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega))}\left(\|\nabla\rho^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{3}(0,T;L^{3}(\Omega))}+\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{3}(0,T;L^{3}(\Omega))}\right)\\
&\leq o(\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}})\|\rho v\|_{L^{3}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{3}}_{3,\infty})}\|v\|_{L^{3}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{3}}_{3,c(\mathbb{N})})}\left(o(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}-1})\|\rho\|_{L^{3}({\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{3}}_{3,c(\mathbb{N})}})}+1\right)\\
&\leq o(1)+o(\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}).\end{aligned}

(3.9)

For the last term
I_{4}
, notice that if
\pi(s)\in C^{2}([a,b])
, then for any
s,s_{0}\in[a,b]
there holds

|\pi(s)-\pi(s_{0})-\pi^{{}^{\prime}}(s_{0})(s-s_{0})|\leq C(s-s_{0})^{2},

where the constant
C
can be independent of
s
and
s_{0}
.
This yields that

|\pi(\rho^{\varepsilon}(t,x))-\pi(\rho(t,x))-\pi^{{}^{\prime}}(\rho(t,x))(\rho^{\varepsilon}(t,x)-\rho(t,x))|\leq C|\rho^{\varepsilon}(t,x)-\rho(t,x)|^{2}.

(3.10)

Similarly, we also have

|\pi(\rho(t,y))-\pi(\rho(t,x))-\pi^{{}^{\prime}}(\rho(t,x))(\rho(t,y)-\rho(t,x))|\leq C|\rho(t,y)-\rho(t,x)|^{2}.

(3.11)

Applying convolution with respect to
y
to the last inequality and invoking Jensen’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle|\pi^{\varepsilon}(\rho(t,x))-\pi(\rho(t,x))-\pi^{{}^{\prime}}(\rho(t,x))(\rho^{\varepsilon}(t,x)-\rho(t,x))|\leq C(\rho(t,\cdot)-\rho(t,x))^{2}*\eta_{\varepsilon}(x),

(3.12)

which together with (
3.10
) gives

\displaystyle|\pi(\rho^{\varepsilon}(t,x))-\pi^{\varepsilon}(\rho(t,x))|

\displaystyle\leq C|\rho^{\varepsilon}(t,x)-\rho(t,x)|^{2}+C(\rho(t,\cdot)-\rho(t,x))^{2}*\eta_{\varepsilon}(x).

(3.13)

Then it follows from integration by parts that
I_{4}
can be handled as

\displaystyle|I_{4}|=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}[\pi(\rho^{\varepsilon})-\pi^{\varepsilon}(\rho)]\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\text{div}\varphi-\varphi\cdot\frac{\nabla\rho^{\varepsilon}}{(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{2}}\right)dxd\tau\right|

(3.14)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\rho^{\varepsilon}(t,x)-\rho(t,x)|^{2}+(\rho(t,\cdot)-\rho(t,x))^{2}*\eta_{\varepsilon}(x)\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T;L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\times\left(\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{3}(0,T;L^{3}(\Omega))}+\|\nabla\rho^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{3}(0,T;L^{3}(\Omega))}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle o(\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}})\|\rho\|_{L^{3}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{3}}_{3,c(\mathbb{N})})}^{2}\left(1+o(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}-1})\|\rho\|_{L^{3}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{3}}_{3,c(\mathbb{N})})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle o(\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}})+o(1).

Hence, letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
and using the Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem for LHS of (
3.6
), we have

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\left(v_{\tau}+\omega\times v+\nabla\left(\Pi(\rho)+\frac{1}{2}|v|^{2}\right)\right)\varphi dxd\tau=0,

(3.15)

which means that
(\rho,v)
is also a weak solution of equations

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\rho_{t}+\nabla\cdot(\rho v)=0,\\
&v_{t}+\omega\times v+\nabla\left(\Pi(\rho)+\frac{1}{2}|v|^{2}\right)=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.16)

This concludes the proof of this proposition.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
(\rho,v)
be a weak solution to the compressible Euler equations (
1.5
). Combining (
1.8
) and Proposition
3.1
, we know that
(\rho,v)
is also a weak solution to the system (
3.16
). Then one can mollify the equation (
3.16
) in space direction to deduce that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &v^{\varepsilon}_{t}+(\omega\times v)^{\varepsilon}+\nabla(\Pi(\rho)+\frac{1}{2}|v|^{2})^{\varepsilon}=0,\\
&\omega^{\varepsilon}_{t}-\text{curl}(v\times\omega)^{\varepsilon}=0,\\
&\text{div}\omega^{\varepsilon}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Consequently, by a straightforward computation, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}(v^{\varepsilon}\cdot\omega^{\varepsilon})dx=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}v_{t}^{\varepsilon}\cdot\omega^{\varepsilon}+\omega_{t}^{\varepsilon}\cdot v^{\varepsilon}dx

(3.17)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}-\left((\omega\times v)^{\varepsilon}+\nabla(\Pi(\rho)+\frac{1}{2}|v|^{2})^{\varepsilon}\right)\cdot\omega^{\varepsilon}+\left(\text{curl}\left(v\times\omega\right)\right)^{\varepsilon}\cdot v^{\varepsilon}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}-\left(\omega\times v\right)^{\varepsilon}\cdot\omega^{\varepsilon}+\left(v\times\omega\right)^{\varepsilon}\cdot\omega^{\varepsilon}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}-2\Big((\omega\times v)^{\varepsilon}-\omega^{\varepsilon}\times v^{\varepsilon}\Big)\cdot\omega^{\varepsilon}-2(\omega^{\varepsilon}\times v^{\varepsilon})\cdot\omega^{\varepsilon}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}-2\Big((\omega\times v)^{\varepsilon}-\omega^{\varepsilon}\times v^{\varepsilon}\Big)\cdot\omega^{\varepsilon}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I,

where we have used the identity that
\omega^{\varepsilon}\times v^{\varepsilon}\cdot v^{\varepsilon}=\omega^{\varepsilon}\times\omega^{\varepsilon}\cdot v^{\varepsilon}=0
.

Next we will show that
I
tends to zero as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.

(1) The Hölder inequality ensures that

\displaystyle|I|\leq

\displaystyle C\|(\omega\times v)^{\varepsilon}-\omega^{\varepsilon}\times v^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T;L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega))}\|\omega^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{3}(0,T;L^{3}(\Omega))}

(3.18)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|(\omega\times v)^{\varepsilon}-\omega^{\varepsilon}\times v^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T;L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega))}\|\nabla v^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{3}(0,T;L^{3}(\Omega))}.

By
v\in L^{3}(0,T;{B}^{\frac{1}{3}}_{3,c(\mathbb{N})})
,

\omega\in L^{3}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{3}}_{3,\infty})
and Lemma
2.6
, we deduce that

\|(\omega\times v)^{\varepsilon}-\omega^{\varepsilon}\times v^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T;L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega))}\leq o(\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}).

Combining
v\in L^{3}(0,T;{B}^{\frac{1}{3}}_{3,c(\mathbb{N})})

and (2) in Lemma
2.4
, we observe that

\|\nabla v^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{3}(0,T;L^{3}(\Omega))}\leq o(\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{3}}).

Hence there holds

|I|\leq o(1).

(2) By the Hölder inequality and Lemma
2.7
, we get

|I|\leq C\|(\omega\times v)^{\varepsilon}-\omega^{\varepsilon}\times v^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T;L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega))}\|\omega^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{3}(0,T;L^{3}(\Omega))}\rightarrow 0,

(3.19)

due to the condition that
v\in L^{3}(0,T;B^{\frac{1}{3}}_{3,c(\mathbb{N})}(\Omega))
and
\omega\in L^{3}(0,T;L^{3}(\Omega))
.

(3) It follows from the Hölder inequality that

\displaystyle|I|\leq

\displaystyle C\|(\omega\times v)^{\varepsilon}-\omega^{\varepsilon}\times v^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{p}{p-1}}(0,T;L^{\frac{q}{q-1}})}\|\omega^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q})}

(3.20)

and

\|\omega\times v\|_{L^{\frac{p}{p-1}}(0,T;L^{\frac{q}{q-1}}(\Omega))}\leq C\|v\|_{L^{\frac{p}{p-2}}(0,T;L^{\frac{q}{q-2}}(\Omega))}\|\omega\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\Omega))}.

Thus, by using the properties of the standard mollifiers, we know that

\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}|I|=0.

(4) Recall the identical equation

-\Delta A=\text{curl\,}\text{curl\,}A-\nabla\text{div\,}A.

The classical elliptic estimate yields that

\|\nabla A\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C(\|\text{curl\,}A\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|\text{div\,}A\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}).

Therefore, there holds

\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\frac{9}{4}}(\Omega)}\leq C(\|\text{curl\,}v\|_{L^{\frac{9}{4}}(\Omega)}+\|\text{div\,}v\|_{L^{\frac{9}{4}}(\Omega)}).

When dimemsion
d=3
, the Sobolev inequality implies that

\|v\|_{L^{9}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\frac{9}{4}}(\Omega)}.

Integrating this estimate in time direction, we derive

\|v\|_{L^{3}(0,T;L^{9}(\Omega))}\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{3}(0,T;L^{\frac{9}{4}}(\Omega))}.

Finally, we get
v\in L^{3}(0,T;L^{9}(\Omega))
and
\text{div\,}u,\omega\in L^{3}(0,T;L^{\frac{9}{4}}(\Omega))
. From the previous case, we complete the proof of this case.
This concludes Theorem
1.1
.
∎

4
Helicity conservation for the homogeneous incompressible Euler equations

This section is devoted to the proof of helicity conservation of weak solutions in Onsager type spaces
\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{3}}_{p,c(\mathbb{N})}
for the homogeneous incompressible Euler equations.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
v
be a weak solution to the homogeneous incompressible Euler equations (
1.1
). Then one can mollify the equation (
1.11
) in space direction to deduce that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &v^{\varepsilon}_{t}+(\omega\times v)^{\varepsilon}+\nabla(\Pi+\frac{1}{2}|v|^{2})^{\varepsilon}=0,\\
&\omega^{\varepsilon}_{t}-\text{curl}(v\times\omega)^{\varepsilon}=0,\\
&\text{div}\omega^{\varepsilon}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Consequently, by a straightforward computation, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}(v^{\varepsilon}\cdot\omega^{\varepsilon})dx=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}v_{t}^{\varepsilon}\cdot\omega^{\varepsilon}+\omega_{t}^{\varepsilon}\cdot v^{\varepsilon}dx

(4.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}-\left((\omega\times v)^{\varepsilon}+\nabla(\Pi+\frac{1}{2}|v|^{2})^{\varepsilon}\right)\cdot\omega^{\varepsilon}+\left(\text{curl}\left(v\times\omega\right)\right)^{\varepsilon}\cdot v^{\varepsilon}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}-\left(\omega\times v\right)^{\varepsilon}\cdot\omega^{\varepsilon}+\left(v\times\omega\right)^{\varepsilon}\cdot\omega^{\varepsilon}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}-2\Big((\omega\times v)^{\varepsilon}-\omega^{\varepsilon}\times v^{\varepsilon}\Big)\cdot\omega^{\varepsilon}-2(\omega^{\varepsilon}\times v^{\varepsilon})\cdot\omega^{\varepsilon}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}-2\Big((\omega\times v)^{\varepsilon}-\omega^{\varepsilon}\times v^{\varepsilon}\Big)\cdot\omega^{\varepsilon}dx,

where integration by parts and the fact that

(v^{\varepsilon}\times\omega^{\varepsilon})\cdot\omega^{\varepsilon}=v^{\varepsilon}\cdot(\omega^{\varepsilon}\times\omega^{\varepsilon})=0
were utilized.

From
\eqref{identity}_{1}
and the divergence-free condition that div
\,v=0
, we infer that

\omega\times v=v\cdot\nabla v-\frac{1}{2}\nabla|v|^{2}=\text{div}(v\otimes v)-\frac{1}{2}\nabla|v|^{2},

which entails that

(\omega\times v)^{\varepsilon}=(v\cdot\nabla v)^{\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{2}\nabla(|v|^{2})^{\varepsilon}=\text{div}(v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{2}\nabla(|v|^{2})^{\varepsilon}

and

\omega^{\varepsilon}\times v^{\varepsilon}=v^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla v^{\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{2}\nabla|v^{\varepsilon}|^{2}=\text{div}(v^{\varepsilon}\otimes v^{\varepsilon})-\frac{1}{2}\nabla|v^{\varepsilon}|^{2}.

Plugging the latter two equations into (
4.1
), we use integration by parts and observe that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}v^{\varepsilon}\cdot\omega^{\varepsilon}dx=

\displaystyle-2\int_{\Omega}\text{div}\big((v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}-(v^{\varepsilon}\otimes v^{\varepsilon})\big)\cdot\omega^{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{2}\nabla\left(|v^{\varepsilon}|^{2}-(|v|^{2})^{\varepsilon}\right)\cdot\omega^{\varepsilon}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{\Omega}\big((v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}-v^{\varepsilon}\otimes v^{\varepsilon}\big)\nabla\omega^{\varepsilon}dx.

Then integrating the latter relation in time over
(0,t)
, we see that

\int_{\Omega}v^{\varepsilon}(x,t)\cdot\omega^{\varepsilon}(x,t)dx-\int_{\Omega}v^{\varepsilon}(x,0)\cdot\omega^{\varepsilon}(x,0)dx=2\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\big(v^{\varepsilon}\otimes v^{\varepsilon}-(v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}\big)\nabla\omega^{\varepsilon}dxds.

(4.2)

Now we are in a position to apply Lemma
2.6
to complete the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

(1) Note that
v\in L^{k}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{p,c(\mathbb{N})})
, we apply Lemma
2.6
to obtain

\|(v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}-v^{\varepsilon}\otimes v^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{k}{2}}(0,T;L^{\frac{p}{2}}(\Omega))}\leq o(\varepsilon^{2\alpha}).

Since

\omega\in L^{\ell}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\beta}_{q,\infty}),
we adopt Lemma

2.4
to get

\|\nabla\omega^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\ell}(0,T;L^{q}(\Omega))}\leq O(\varepsilon^{\beta-1}).

In light of the Hölder inequality, we see that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\big((v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}-v^{\varepsilon}\otimes v^{\varepsilon}\big)\nabla\omega^{\varepsilon}dxds\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|(v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}-v^{\varepsilon}\otimes v^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{k}{2}}(0,T;L^{\frac{p}{2}}(\Omega))}\|\nabla\omega^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\ell}(0,T;L^{q}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle o(\varepsilon^{2\alpha})O(\varepsilon^{\beta-1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle o(\varepsilon^{\beta+2\alpha-1}).

Then letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
in (
4.2
), this completes the proof of the first part.

(2) In the same manner as above, making use of Remark
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\big((v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}-v^{\varepsilon}\otimes v^{\varepsilon}\big)\nabla\omega^{\varepsilon}dxds\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|(v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}-v^{\varepsilon}\otimes v^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{k}{2}}(0,T;L^{\frac{p}{2}}(\Omega))}\|\nabla\omega^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\ell}(0,T;L^{q}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle O(\varepsilon^{2\alpha})o(\varepsilon^{\beta-1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle o(\varepsilon^{\beta+2\alpha-1}).

Then letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, this together with (
4.2
) yields the second part of Theorem
1.2
.

(3) Choose
q_{1}=q_{2}=\frac{3d}{d+2}
. Then there holds
\frac{2}{d}+\frac{2d-2}{3d}=\frac{d+2}{3d}+\frac{d+2}{3d}
. Hence, we apply (2) in Lemma
2.6
and Remark
2.3
to obtain

\|(v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}-v^{\varepsilon}\otimes v^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T;L^{\frac{3d}{2d-2}}(\Omega))}\leq o(\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}),

where we have used the condition that

\omega\in L^{3}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{3}}_{\frac{3d}{d+2},c(\mathbb{N})}).
Following the same path as above, we get

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\big((v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}-v^{\varepsilon}\otimes v^{\varepsilon}\big)\nabla\omega^{\varepsilon}dxds\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|(v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}-v^{\varepsilon}\otimes v^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T;L^{\frac{3d}{2d-2}}(\Omega))}\|\nabla\omega^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{3}(0,T;L^{\frac{3d}{d+2}}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle O(\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}})o(\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{3}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle o(1),

which together with (
4.2
) concludes the third part of this theorem.

(An alternative approach to (3) and (
1.6
)):

Next we provide an alternative approach to proving this part.
According to the boundedness of Riesz Transform in
homogeneous Besov spaces and the condition that
\omega\in L^{3}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{3}}_{\frac{3d}{d+2},c(\mathbb{N})}),
we have
\nabla v\in L^{3}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{3}}_{\frac{3d}{d+2},c(\mathbb{N})}).

The Bernstein inequality further helps us to get
v\in L^{3}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\frac{4}{3}}_{\frac{3d}{d+2},c(\mathbb{N})}).

Recall that
\dot{B}^{\frac{4}{3}}_{\frac{3d}{d+2},c(\mathbb{N})}\hookrightarrow\dot{B}^{\frac{2}{3}}_{3,c(\mathbb{N})}
, we may achieve the desired proof of (3) from (
1.6
).

Subsequently, to make the paper more readable and more self-contained, we also present the proof of (
1.6
).
Notice that

\|\nabla\omega^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\nabla^{2}v^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}~\text{for}~1<q<\infty,

which together with the fact that
v\in L^{3}(0,T;B^{\frac{2}{3}}_{3,c(\mathbb{N})}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
and Lemma
2.4
means that

\|\nabla\omega^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\nabla^{2}v^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}\leq o(\varepsilon^{-\frac{4}{3}}).

Using the Hölder inequality and (1) in Lemma
2.6
, we arrive at

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\big((v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}-v^{\varepsilon}\otimes v^{\varepsilon}\big)\nabla\omega^{\varepsilon}dxds\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|(v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}-v^{\varepsilon}\otimes v^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T;L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega))}\|\nabla\omega^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{3}(0,T;L^{3}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle o(\varepsilon^{\frac{4}{3}})o(\varepsilon^{-\frac{4}{3}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle o(1).

This together with (

) gives (
1.6
).

5
Helicity conservation for 2-D surface quasi-geostrophic equations

In this section, we are concerned with the helicity conservation of weak solutions to 2-D surface quasi-geostrophic equations.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

First, due to the divergence-free condition of velocity
v(x,t)
, we rewrite the equation (
1.12
) as

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\theta_{t}+\mathrm{div}\,(v\otimes\theta)=0,\\
&v(x,t)=(-\mathcal{R}_{2}\theta,\mathcal{R}_{1}\theta),\\
&\theta|_{t=0}=\theta_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

(5.1)

where
\mathcal{R}_{i}
represents the Riesz transform for
i=1,2
.
Next, we regularize the equation (
5.1
) to obtain

\theta^{\varepsilon}_{t}+\partial_{j}(v_{j}\theta)^{\varepsilon}=0,

and

\partial_{i}\theta^{\varepsilon}_{t}+\partial_{j}(\partial_{i}v_{j}\theta)^{\varepsilon}+\partial_{j}(v_{j}\partial_{i}\theta)^{\varepsilon}=0.

Thus, it follows from a direct computation that for
i=1,2
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\theta^{\varepsilon}\partial_{i}\theta^{\varepsilon}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\theta^{\varepsilon}\partial_{i}\partial_{t}\theta^{\varepsilon}dx+\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\theta^{\varepsilon}\partial_{i}\theta^{\varepsilon}dx

(5.2)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\theta^{\varepsilon}[\partial_{j}(\partial_{i}v_{j}\theta)^{\varepsilon}+\partial_{j}(v_{j}\partial_{i}\theta)^{\varepsilon}]dx-\int_{\Omega}\partial_{j}(v_{j}\theta)^{\varepsilon}\partial_{i}\theta^{\varepsilon}dx.

Using the divergence-free condition of
v(x,t)
again, we have the fact that
\int\partial_{j}(\partial_{i}v_{j}^{\varepsilon}\theta^{\varepsilon})\theta^{\varepsilon}dx=0
. Then we can reformulate (
5.2
) as

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\theta^{\varepsilon}\partial_{i}\theta^{\varepsilon}dx=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\theta^{\varepsilon}\big[\partial_{j}(\partial_{i}v_{j}\theta)^{\varepsilon}-\partial_{j}(\partial_{i}v_{j}^{\varepsilon}\theta^{\varepsilon})\big]dx-\int_{\Omega}\theta^{\varepsilon}\big[\partial_{j}(v_{j}\partial_{i}\theta)^{\varepsilon}-\partial_{j}(v_{j}^{\varepsilon}\partial_{i}\theta^{\varepsilon})\big]dx

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\theta^{\varepsilon}\partial_{j}(v_{j}^{\varepsilon}\partial_{i}\theta^{\varepsilon})dx-\int_{\Omega}\partial_{j}(v_{j}\theta)^{\varepsilon}\partial_{i}\theta^{\varepsilon}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\partial_{j}\theta^{\varepsilon}\big[(\partial_{i}v_{j}\theta)^{\varepsilon}-(\partial_{i}v_{j}^{\varepsilon}\theta^{\varepsilon})\big]dx+\int_{\Omega}\big[(v_{j}\partial_{i}\theta)^{\varepsilon}-(v_{j}^{\varepsilon}\partial_{i}\theta^{\varepsilon})\big]\partial_{j}\theta^{\varepsilon}dx

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\theta^{\varepsilon}v_{j}^{\varepsilon}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\theta^{\varepsilon}dx+\int_{\Omega}(v_{j}\theta)^{\varepsilon}\partial_{j}\partial_{i}\theta^{\varepsilon}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\partial_{j}\theta^{\varepsilon}\big[(\partial_{i}v_{j}\theta)^{\varepsilon}-(\partial_{i}v_{j}^{\varepsilon}\theta^{\varepsilon})\big]dx+\int_{\Omega}\big[(v_{j}\partial_{i}\theta)^{\varepsilon}-(v_{j}^{\varepsilon}\partial_{i}\theta^{\varepsilon})\big]\partial_{j}\theta^{\varepsilon}dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\big[(v_{j}\theta)^{\varepsilon}-\theta^{\varepsilon}v_{j}^{\varepsilon}\big]\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\theta^{\varepsilon}dx,

which leads to

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\theta^{\varepsilon}(x,t)\partial_{i}\theta^{\varepsilon}(x,t)dx-\int_{\Omega}\theta^{\varepsilon}(x,0)\partial_{i}\theta^{\varepsilon}(x,0)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{j}\theta^{\varepsilon}\big[(\partial_{i}v_{j}\theta)^{\varepsilon}-(\partial_{i}v_{j}^{\varepsilon}\theta^{\varepsilon})\big]dxds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\big[(v_{j}\partial_{i}\theta)^{\varepsilon}-(v_{j}^{\varepsilon}\partial_{i}\theta^{\varepsilon})\big]\partial_{j}\theta^{\varepsilon}dxds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\big[(v_{j}\theta)^{\varepsilon}-\theta^{\varepsilon}v_{j}^{\varepsilon}\big]\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\theta^{\varepsilon}dxds

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle I+II+III.

For the first term
I
, we conclude by the Hölder inequality that

|I|\leq\|(\partial_{i}v_{j}\theta)^{\varepsilon}-(\partial_{i}v_{j}^{\varepsilon}\theta^{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T;L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\Omega))}\|\partial_{j}\theta^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{3}(0,T;L^{6}(\Omega))}.

Take

q=\frac{6}{5},d=2,q_{1}=\frac{3}{2},q_{2}=\frac{3}{2}<2.
Note that
\frac{1}{2}+\frac{5}{6}=\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}
and

\nabla\theta\in L^{3}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{3}}_{\frac{3}{2},c(\mathbb{N})})
, we can invoke (3) in Lemma
2.6
to get

\|(\partial_{i}v_{j}\theta)^{\varepsilon}-(\partial_{i}v_{j}^{\varepsilon}\theta^{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T;L^{\frac{6}{5}}(\Omega))}\leq o(\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}).

Combining
Sobolev’s inequality and Lemma
2.4
, we deduce from
\nabla\theta\in L^{3}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{3}}_{\frac{3}{2},c(\mathbb{N})})
that

\|\partial_{j}\theta^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{3}(0,T;L^{6}(\Omega))}\leq C\|\nabla\partial_{j}\theta^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{3}(0,T;L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega))}\leq o(\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{3}}).

Collecting the above estimates, we get

|I|\leq o(1).

Likewise, we have

|II|\leq o(1).

It remains to estimate the third term
III
. Using the Hölder inequality, we find

|III|\leq\|(v_{j}\theta)^{\varepsilon}-\theta^{\varepsilon}v_{j}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T;L^{3}(\Omega))}\|\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\theta^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{3}(0,T;L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega))}.

Choosing

q=3,d=2,q_{1}=\frac{3}{2}=q_{2}=\frac{3}{2}<2,
(2) in Lemma
2.6
enables us
to get

\|(v_{j}\theta)^{\varepsilon}-\theta^{\varepsilon}v_{j}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(0,T;L^{3}(\Omega))}\leq o(\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}),

where we have used the condition that
\nabla\theta\in L^{3}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\frac{1}{3}}_{\frac{3}{2},c(\mathbb{N})})
.
Besides, Lemma
2.4
allows us to conclude that

\|\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\theta^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{3}(0,T;L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega))}\leq o(\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{3}}).

In summary, we know that

|III|\leq o(1).

We achieve the proof of this theorem.
∎

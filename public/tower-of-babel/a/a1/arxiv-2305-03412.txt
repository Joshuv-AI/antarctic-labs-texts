Partial reflections and globally linked pairs in rigid graphs
Dániel Garamvölgyi, Tibor Jordán

Abstract
A d d -dimensional framework is a pair ( G , p ) (G,p) , where G G is a graph and p p maps the vertices of G G to points in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . The edges of G G are mapped to the corresponding line segments. A graph G G is said to be globally rigid in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} if every generic d d -dimensional framework ( G , p ) (G,p) is determined, up to congruence, by its edge lengths. A finer property is global linkedness: we say that a vertex pair { u , v } \{u,v\} of G G is globally linked in G G in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} if in every generic d d -dimensional framework ( G , p ) (G,p) the distance between u u and v v is uniquely determined by the edge lengths. In this paper we investigate globally linked pairs in graphs in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . We give several characterizations of those rigid graphs G G in which a pair { u , v } \{u,v\} is globally linked if and only if there exist d + 1 d+1 internally disjoint paths from u u to v v in G G . We call these graphs d d -joined. Among others, we show that G G is d d -joined if and only if for each pair of generic frameworks of G G with the same edge lengths, one can be obtained from the other by a sequence of partial reflections along hyperplanes determined by d d -separators of G G . We also show that the family of d d -joined graphs is closed under edge addition, as well as under gluing along d d or more vertices. As a key ingredient to our main results, we prove that rigid graphs in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} contain no crossing d d -separators. Our results give rise to new families of graphs for which global linkedness (and global rigidity) in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} can be tested in polynomial time.

A promising avenue is the investigation of globally linked pairs. Following

2.2
Rational functions and rational maps

We shall need some definitions from algebraic geometry, which we introduce adapted to the affine space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For a thorough introduction to the notion of rational maps, see, e.g.,
[
4
, Chapter 5]
.

A
rational function
(over
\mathbb{R}
, in the variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
) is an element of the field of fractions of the polynomial ring
\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
. A rational function
\varphi
can be represented as a quotient
f/g
of polynomials
f,g\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, where
g
is not the zero polynomial. Two pairs
f_{1}/g_{1}
and
f_{2}/g_{2}
represent the same rational function if and only if
f_{1}g_{2}-f_{2}g_{1}
is the zero polynomial. The
domain of definition
of a rational function
\varphi
is the set

\dom(\varphi)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\varphi\text{ has a representative }f/g\text{ with }g(x)\neq 0\}.

The
value

\varphi(x)
of
\varphi
at a point
x\in\dom(\varphi)
is
f(x)/g(x)
, where
f/g
is a representative of
\varphi
with
g(x)\neq 0
. It is easy to see that this is well-defined.

A
rational map
from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, denoted by
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
, is an
m
-tuple of rational functions
(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{m})
in
n
variables. The
domain of definition
of
F
is
\dom(F)=\cap_{i=1}^{m}\dom(\varphi_{i})
; this is a dense open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The
value
of
F
at a point
x\in\dom(F)
is
F(x)=(\varphi_{1}(x),\ldots,\varphi_{m}(x)).
Thus, we may view
F
as a function from
\dom(F)
to
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
We say that
F
has
rational coefficients
if each
\varphi_{i}
has a representative
f/g
with
f
and
g
having rational coefficients.

We say that a rational map
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
is
dominant
if there is no nonzero polynomial
f\in\mathbb{R}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]
such that
F(\dom(F))\subseteq\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:f(y)=0\}
. In particular, this holds if
F(\dom(F))
is a dense subset of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
If
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
G:\mathbb{R}^{m}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{k}
are dominant rational maps with
F=(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{m})
and
G=(\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{k})
, then we can define their composition
G\circ F
in the natural way by taking representatives
\varphi_{i}=f_{i}/g_{i},i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
and
\psi_{j}=f^{\prime}_{j}/g^{\prime}_{j},j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
and letting the
j
-th component of
G\circ F
be represented by

\Psi_{j}=\frac{f^{\prime}_{j}(f_{1}/g_{1},\ldots,f_{m}/g_{m})}{g^{\prime}_{j}(f_{1}/g_{1},\ldots,f_{m}/g_{m})},

which is a rational function in the variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
. This is well-defined, and the condition that
F
is dominant ensures that the denominator in the above expression is not identically zero. The rational map
G\circ F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{k}
defined by
(\Psi_{1},\ldots,\Psi_{k})
is again dominant, and if
F
and
G
have rational coefficients, then so does
G\circ F
. The domain of definition of
G\circ F
includes (but may be larger than)
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x\in\dom(F)\text{ and }F(x)\in\dom(G)\}
, and at a member
x
of the latter set we have
(G\circ F)(x)=G(F(x))
.

Let us say that a point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
generic
if its
n
coordinates are algebraically independent over
\mathbb{Q}
; that is, if there is no nonzero polynomial
f\in\mathbb{Q}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
such that
f(x)=0
. The following lemma captures the easy but useful observation that a rational map with rational coefficients is completely determined by its value at any generic point.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
F,G:\mathbb{R}^{n}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
be rational maps with rational coefficients and let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a generic point. Then we have
x\in\dom(F)\cap\dom(G)
. Furthermore, if
F(x)=G(x)
, then
F
and
G
are equal as rational maps.

Proof.

Let
(f_{1}/g_{1},\ldots,f_{m}/g_{m})
and
(f^{\prime}_{1}/g^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,f^{\prime}_{m}/g^{\prime}_{m})
be representations of
F
and
G
, respectively, with rational coefficients. Since
x
is generic, we have that
g_{i}(x)\neq 0
and
g^{\prime}_{i}(x)\neq 0
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
. It follows that
x\in\dom(F)\cap\dom(G)
. If
F(x)=G(x)
, then for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
we have

\frac{f_{i}(x)}{g_{i}(x)}=\frac{f^{\prime}_{i}(x)}{g^{\prime}_{i}(x)}.

By rearranging we get
f_{i}(x)g^{\prime}_{i}(x)-f^{\prime}_{i}(x)g_{i}(x)=0
. Since
f_{i}g^{\prime}_{i}-f^{\prime}_{i}g_{i}
is a polynomial with rational coefficients and
x
is generic, this means that
f_{i}g^{\prime}_{i}-f^{\prime}_{i}g_{i}
is the zero polynomial, so
f_{i}/g_{i}
and
f^{\prime}_{i}/g^{\prime}_{i}
represent the same rational function. Since this holds for each index
i
, we have
F=G
, as claimed.

2.3
Congruences and reflections

A function

has the form

, where

is an orthogonal matrix and

is a vector.
We shall use the following standard facts.

Lemma 2.8
.

[
20

Let
\alpha:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a congruence. If

, then
\alpha
is either the identity map, or the orthogonal reflection in the affine hyperplane spanned by
p_{1},\ldots,p_{d}
.

Lemma 2.9
.

[
20
, (the proof of) Theorem 2]

Let
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a set of
d+1
affinely independent points and let
p,p^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a pair of points. If
\left\lVert p-s\right\rVert=\left\lVert p^{\prime}-s\right\rVert
for all
s\in S
, then
p=p^{\prime}
.

Let
X\subseteq(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{d+1}
denote the set of vector tuples
\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{d},x\right)
such that
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}\}
is affinely independent, and let
F:X\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be the map that sends each tuple
\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{d},x\right)
to the orthogonal reflection of
x
in the affine hyperplane spanned by
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}\}
. This turns out to be a rational map with rational coefficients, a fact that we shall repeatedly exploit in
Section

3
.

Lemma 2.10
.

The map
F
defined above is (the function induced by) a rational map with rational coefficients.

Proof.

It is well-known that given a matrix
M\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times(d-1)}
of full column rank, the orthogonal projection onto the linear subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
spanned by the columns of
M
is given by
M(M^{T}M)^{-1}M^{T}
, see
[
19
, Section 5.13]
. From this it is not difficult to derive that the orthogonal reflection of
x
in the affine hyperplane generated by
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}\}
is given by

(I-2M(M^{T}M)^{-1}M^{T})(x-a_{1})+a_{1},

where
M
is the matrix having
a_{2}-a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}-a_{1}
as its columns. Note that
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}\}
being affinely independent implies that
M^{T}M
is invertible.
It is well-known (and follows from, e.g., Cramer’s rule) that the map
X\mapsto X^{-1}
corresponds to a rational map
\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
with rational coefficients whose domain is the set of invertible matrices. It follows that the above expression for orthogonal reflections corresponds to a rational map with rational coefficients whose domain includes the set of vector tuples
\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{d},x\right)
with
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}\}
affinely independent. ∎

2.4
Rigid and globally rigid graphs

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and let
d\geq 1
be an integer. As defined in the introduction, a
realization
of
G
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a pair
(G,p)
, where
p
is a map from
V
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We also say that the pair
(G,p)
is a
d
-dimensional framework
. The
length
of an edge
uv\in E
in
(G,p)
is
\left\lVert p(u)-p(v)\right\rVert
.
Two frameworks
(G,p)
and
(G,q)
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
are
equivalent
if for every edge
uv\in E
, the length of
uv
is the same in
(G,p)
and
(G,q)
. The frameworks are
congruent
if there is a congruence
\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
that maps
p(v)
to
q(v)
for all
v\in V
. This is well-known to be equivalent to the condition that
\left\lVert p(u)-p(v)\right\rVert=\left\lVert q(u)-q(v)\right\rVert
holds for every pair of vertices
u,v\in V
.

A
d
-dimensional framework
(G,p)
is
rigid
if there is some
\varepsilon>0
such that every equivalent framework
(G,q)
with
\left\lVert p(v)-q(v)\right\rVert<\varepsilon
for all
v\in V
is congruent to
(G,p)
. If this holds globally, that is, if every equivalent framework is congruent to
(G,p)
, then we say that
(G,p)
is
globally rigid
.

It turns out that the combinatorial structure of
G
determines the (global) rigidity of
(G,p)
for “almost all”
p
. To be more precise, we say that a
d
-dimensional framework
(G,p)
is
generic
if the multiset of the
d\cdot|V|
coordinates of
\{p(v),v\in V\}
is algebraically independent over
\mathbb{Q}
; in other words, if
p
is generic as a point in
\mathbb{R}^{d|V|}
. It is known that if some generic
d
-dimensional realization of a graph is rigid (resp. globally rigid), then every generic
d
-dimensional realization of the graph is rigid (resp. globally rigid), see
[
2
,
3
,
10
]
.
Thus, we say that a graph
G
is
rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
if its generic realizations in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
are rigid, and
globally rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
if its generic realizations in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
are globally rigid.

It is well-known that if a graph on at least
d+1
vertices is rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then it is
d
-connected. The next lemma describes some basic gluing results regarding graph rigidity. The first part can be found in, e.g.,
[
23
, Lemma 11.1.9(a)]
, while the second is easy to deduce from the definitions.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be the union of the graphs
G_{1}=(V_{1},E_{1}),G_{2}=(V_{2},E_{2})
. If
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
|V_{1}\cap V_{2}|\geq d
, then
G
is rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Conversely, if
G
is rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
V_{1}\cap V_{2}
is a clique in both
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, then
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Given a graph
G
, its
cone graph
is obtained from
G
by adding a new
vertex
v
and new edges from
v
to each vertex of
G
.

Theorem 2.12
.

[
22
]

A graph is rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
if and only if its cone graph is rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
.

2.5
The rigidity matrix and the rigidity matroid

The rigidity matroid of a graph
G
is a matroid defined on the edge set
of
G
which reflects the rigidity properties of all generic realizations of

G
.
Let
(G,p)
be a realization of a graph
G=(V,E)
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
The
rigidity matrix
of the framework
(G,p)

is the matrix
R(G,p)
of size

|E|\times d|V|
, where, for each edge
v_{i}v_{j}\in E
, in the row
corresponding to
v_{i}v_{j}
,
the entries in the
d
columns corresponding to vertices
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
contain
the
d
coordinates of

(p(v_{i})-p(v_{j}))
and
(p(v_{j})-p(v_{i}))
, respectively,
and the remaining entries
are zeros.
The rigidity matrix of
(G,p)
defines
the
rigidity matroid
of
(G,p)
on the ground set
E

by linear independence of rows. It is known that any pair of generic frameworks

(G,p)
and
(G,q)
have the same rigidity matroid.
We call this the
d
-dimensional
rigidity matroid

{\cal R}_{d}(G)=(E,r_{d})
of the graph
G
.

We denote the rank of
{\cal R}_{d}(G)
by
r_{d}(G)
.
A graph
G=(V,E)
is
\mathcal{R}_{d}
-independent
if
r_{d}(G)=|E|
, and it is an
\mathcal{R}_{d}
-circuit
if it is not
\mathcal{R}_{d}
-independent, but every proper
subgraph of
G
is
\mathcal{R}_{d}
-independent. We note that in the literature such graphs are sometimes called
M
-independent in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
M
-circuits in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, respectively.
A pair
\{u,v\}
of nonadjacent vertices in
G
is
linked in
G
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
if
r_{d}(G)=r_{d}(G+uv)
. Equivalently,
\{u,v\}
is linked in
G
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
if there is an
\mathcal{R}_{d}
-circuit in
G+uv
that contains
uv
.

We shall use the following characterization of rigid graphs.

Theorem 2.13
.

[
9
]

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph on at least
d
vertices. Then
G
is rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
if and only if
r_{d}(G)=d|V|-\binom{d+1}{2}
.

Let
{\cal M}
be a matroid on ground set
E
with rank function
r
.
We can define a relation on the pairs of elements of
E
by
saying that
e,f\in E
are
equivalent if
e=f
or there is a circuit
C
of
{\cal M}

with
\{e,f\}\subseteq C
.
This defines an equivalence relation. The equivalence classes are
the
connected components
of
{\cal M}
.
The matroid is said to be
connected
if there is only one equivalence class.
Given a graph
G=(V,E)
, the subgraphs induced by the edge sets of the connected components
of
{\cal R}_{d}(G)
are the

\mathcal{R}_{d}
-connected components
of
G
.
The graph is said to be
\mathcal{R}_{d}
-connected
if
{\cal R}_{d}(G)
is connected.

2.6
Globally linked pairs

Let
(G,p)
be a framework in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. As a “granular” counterpart to global rigidity, we define a pair of vertices
\{u,v\}
to be
globally linked
in
(G,p)
if for every equivalent framework
(G,q)
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have
\left\lVert p(u)-p(v)\right\rVert=\left\lVert q(u)-q(v)\right\rVert
. Thus,
(G,p)
is globally rigid if and only if every pair of vertices is globally linked in
(G,p)
. In contrast with global rigidity, this is not, in general, a generic property: it may happen that (in a given dimension)
\{u,v\}
is globally linked in some generic realizations and not globally linked in others, see
[
15
, Figs. 1 and 2]
. Nevertheless, we define the pair
\{u,v\}
to be
globally linked in
G
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
if
\{u,v\}
is globally linked in every generic realization of
G
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

We shall need the following “trilateration” result concerning globally linked pairs.

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and let
S\subseteq V
be a clique of size
d+1
in
G
. Let
u
and
v
be a pair of vertices in
G
. If
\{u,s\}
and
\{v,s\}
are globally linked in
G
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for every
s\in S
, then
\{u,v\}
is globally linked in
G
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Proof.

Let
(G,p)
be a generic
d
-dimensional realization of
G
and
(G,q)
an equivalent realization. Since
G[S]
is complete, it is globally rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, so there is a congruence that maps
(G[S],q)
to
(G[S],p)
. By applying this congruence to all of
(G,q)
, we may suppose that
p(s)=q(s)
for every
s\in S
.

Using the assumption that
\{u,s\}
is globally linked in
G
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have
\left\lVert p(u)-p(s)\right\rVert=\left\lVert q(u)-q(s)\right\rVert=\left\lVert q(u)-p(s)\right\rVert
for every
s\in S
. Since
(G,p)
is generic,
\{p(s),s\in S\}
is affinely independent, so
Lemma

2.9
implies
p(u)=q(u)
. By the same reasoning we also have
p(v)=q(v)
, so in particular
\left\lVert p(u)-p(v)\right\rVert=\left\lVert q(u)-q(v)\right\rVert,
as desired.
∎

Let
G
be a graph. The
globally linked closure of
G
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, denoted by
\text{cl}^{*}_{d}(G)
, is the graph obtained from
G
by adding the edge
uv
for every pair
\{u,v\}
that is globally linked in
G
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The following lemma, which is easy to deduce from the definitions, shows that
\text{cl}^{*}_{d}
is indeed a closure operator.

Lemma 2.15
.

We have the following for all
d\geq 1
and all graphs
G
.

•

E(G)\subseteq E(\text{cl}^{*}_{d}(G))
,

•

\text{cl}^{*}_{d}(\text{cl}^{*}_{d}(G))=\text{cl}^{*}_{d}(G)
, and

•

for all graphs
G^{\prime}
with
E(G^{\prime})\subseteq E(G)
,
E(\text{cl}^{*}_{d}(G^{\prime}))\subseteq E(\text{cl}^{*}_{d}(G))
.

The following observation captures a key property of taking the globally linked closure of a graph.

Lemma 2.16
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and let
(G,p)
and
(G,q)
be
d
-dimensional realizations of
G
with
(G,p)
generic. Then
(G,p)
and
(G,q)
are equivalent if and only if
(\text{cl}^{*}_{d}(G),p)
and
(\text{cl}^{*}_{d}(G),q)
are equivalent. Consequently,
G
is rigid (resp. globally rigid) in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
if and only if
\text{cl}^{*}_{d}(G)
is rigid (resp. globally rigid) in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Proof.

Clearly if
(\text{cl}^{*}_{d}(G),p)
and
(\text{cl}^{*}_{d}(G),q)
are equivalent, then so are
(G,p)
and
(G,q)
. On the other hand, if
(G,p)
and
(G,q)
are equivalent, then by the definition of being globally linked we have that
\left\lVert p(u)-p(v)\right\rVert=\left\lVert q(u)-q(v)\right\rVert
for any pair
u,v\in V
for which
\{u,v\}
is globally linked in
G
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Since
uv
is an edge of
\text{cl}^{*}_{d}(G)
if and only if
\{u,v\}
is globally linked in
G
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it follows that
(\text{cl}^{*}_{d}(G),p)
and
(\text{cl}^{*}_{d}(G),q)
are equivalent.

From this and the relevant definitions it is immediate that
(G,p)
is rigid (resp. globally rigid) if and only if
(\text{cl}^{*}_{d}(G),p)
is rigid (resp. globally rigid). Since
(G,p)
is generic, this means that
G
is rigid (resp. globally rigid) in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
if and only if
\text{cl}^{*}_{d}(G)
is rigid (resp. globally rigid) in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
∎

Following
[
15
]
, we define a
d
-dimensional globally rigid cluster

of
G
to be a maximal vertex set of
G
in which each vertex pair is globally linked in
G
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. In other words, the
d
-dimensional globally rigid clusters of
G
are the maximal cliques of
\text{cl}^{*}_{d}(G)
.

The following simple observation is central to the paper.

Lemma 2.17
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and let
S\subseteq V
be a set of at most
d
vertices. If
S
is a separator in
G
, then it is also a separator in
\text{cl}^{*}_{d}(G)
, and moreover if
S
is
(u,v)
-separating in
G
, then it is also
(u,v)
-separating in
\text{cl}^{*}_{d}(G)
, for any pair of vertices
u,v\in V
. In particular, if
u,v\in V
are nonadjacent and
\kappa(G,u,v)\leq d
, then
\{u,v\}
is not globally linked in
G
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Proof.

Let
u,v\in V
be a pair of vertices such that
S
is
(u,v)
-separating in
G
, and let
X
be a
(u,v)
-separating fragment such that
N_{G}(X)=S
. Let
(G,p)
be a generic realization of
G
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
For any generic realization
(G,p)
, we can reflect the points
p(x),x\in\overline{X}
in a hyperplane
H
containing
\{p(s),s\in S\}
and leave the rest of the points in place to obtain an equivalent noncongruent realization
(G,q)
. Since
(G,p)
is generic, we can choose
H
in such a way that it avoids the points
\{p(v),v\in V-S\}
. With this choice we have
\left\lVert p(u^{\prime})-p(v^{\prime})\right\rVert\neq\left\lVert q(u^{\prime})-q(v^{\prime})\right\rVert
for all pairs
u^{\prime}\in X
and
v^{\prime}\in\overline{X}
. This shows that all such pairs
\{u^{\prime},v^{\prime}\}
are not globally linked in
G
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and thus there are no edges in
\text{cl}^{*}_{d}(G)
going between
X
and
\overline{X}
. It follows that
S
is still
(u,v)
-separating in
\text{cl}^{*}_{d}(G)
, as required.
∎

Corollary 2.18
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph. The
d
-separators, the
d
-fragments and the
(d+1)
-blocks of
G
and
\text{cl}^{*}_{d}(G)
coincide.

In the preceding proof if
|S|=d
, then the hyperplane along which we reflect is just the affine span of
\{p(x),x\in S\}
. Intuitively,
(G,q)
is a “partial reflection” of
(G,p)
through this hyperplane. Our first goal is to gain a deeper understanding of the operation of taking such partial reflections.

3
Sequences of partial reflections

In this section we carefully define and analyze the operation of taking partial reflections. Although the notion of (sequences of) partial reflections is fairly intuitive, their analysis turns out to be rather technical and notation-heavy. Apart from the definition of a reduced sequence of partial reflections, which is central to our investigations, we shall only use the results of this section at a few key points in the next section. Nonetheless, we believe that this analysis may also be useful in the context of other problems involving the global rigidity of frameworks.

3.1
Basic definitions

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph,
X
a
d
-fragment of
G
and
S=N_{G}(X)
. We say that a
d
-dimensional framework
(G,p)
is
R_{X}
-admissible
if
p(S)=\{p(v):v\in S\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is affinely independent. Given an
R_{X}
-admissible framework
(G,p)
, we define the
partial reflection of
(G,p)
corresponding to
X
to be the framework
(G,R_{X}(p))
given by

R_{X}(p)(v)=\begin{cases}p(v)&v\in X\cup N_{G}(X),\\
\mathcal{R}_{p(S)}(p(v))&v\in\overline{X},\end{cases}

where
\mathcal{R}_{p(S)}
denotes the orthogonal reflection in the affine hyperplane spanned by
p(S)
.
The following two lemmas record basic properties of partial reflections which we shall use without reference throughout the paper.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X
be a
d
-fragment of
G
and let
(G,p)
be an
R_{X}
-admissible framework in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then
(G,p)
is equivalent to
(G,R_{X}(p))
. Moreover, if there are vertices
u\in X
and
v\in\overline{X}
such that
p(u)
and
p(v)
are not in the affine hyperplane spanned by
p(S)
, then
(G,p)
and
(G,R_{X}(p))
are not congruent.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
X
be a
d
-fragment of
G
. Consider an
R_{X}
-admissible framework
(G,p)
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
(G,q)
be a congruent framework. Then
(G,q)
is also
R_{X}
-admissible and
(G,R_{X}(p))
and
(G,R_{X}(q))
are congruent.

It follows from
Lemma

2.10
that the mapping
p\mapsto R_{X}(p)
corresponds to a rational map
\mathbb{R}^{d|V|}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d|V|}
with rational coefficients whose domain of definition is the set

\dom(R_{X})=\{p\in\mathbb{R}^{d|V|}:(G,p)\text{ is }R_{X}\text{-admissible}\}.

We call this rational map the
partial
d
-reflection corresponding to
X
, and by a slight abuse of notation we also denote it by
R_{X}
. This map is dominant: indeed, its image is also
\dom(R_{X})
, a dense open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d|V|}
.
For convenience, we also define
R_{X}
to be the identity map of
\mathbb{R}^{d|V|}
whenever
X\subseteq V
is not a
d
-fragment of
G
. In particular, we shall use this notation when
X=\varnothing
and when
X=V-S
for some
d
-separator
S
of
G
.

A
sequence of partial
d
-reflections
of
G
is a tuple
F=(R_{X_{1}},\ldots,R_{X_{k}})
, where
X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}
are
d
-fragments of
G
.
A framework
(G,p)
is
F
-admissible
if
(G,p)
is
X_{1}
-admissible and
(G,(R_{X_{i-1}}\circ\ldots\circ R_{X_{1}})(p))
is
R_{X_{i}}
-admissible for each
i\in\{2,\ldots,k\}
.
For an
F
-admissible framework
(G,p)
we define
F(p)=R_{X_{k}}\circ\ldots\circ R_{X_{1}}(p)
. In this way, we associate to each sequence of partial
d
-reflections
F
a dominant rational map with rational coefficients whose domain of definition includes (but may be larger than) the set of
F
-admissible frameworks. Again, for convenience we shall use a slight abuse of notation and say that
F
itself is a rational map.

We also consider the empty sequence to be a sequence of partial
d
-reflections, consisting of zero reflections, and corresponding to the identity map on
\mathbb{R}^{d|V|}
. We refer to this as the
trivial
sequence of partial
d
-reflections. When defining a sequence of partial
d
-reflections
F=(R_{X_{1}},\ldots,R_{X_{k}})
, we shall usually allow the possibility that
k=0
with the understanding that
F
is the trivial sequence of partial
d
-reflections in this case. We also consider a single partial
d
-reflection
R_{X}
to be a sequence of partial reflections, and we shall continue to write
R_{X}
instead of
(R_{X})
in this case.
For a pair of sequences of partial
d
-reflections
F=(R_{X_{1}},\ldots,R_{X_{k}}),F^{\prime}=(R_{X_{k+1}},\ldots,R_{X_{l}})
, we let
F^{\prime}\circ F
denote the sequence
(R_{X_{1}},\ldots,R_{X_{k}},R_{X_{k+1}},\ldots,R_{X_{l}})
of partial
d
-reflections.

A generic framework in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
on at least
d+1
vertices is in general position, and in particular
R_{X}
-admissible for any partial
d
-reflection
R_{X}
. The following lemma shows that partial reflections preserve genericity.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
X
be a
d
-fragment of
G=(V,E)
and let
(G,p)
be an
R_{X}
-admissible framework in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then
(G,p)
is generic if and only if
(G,R_{X}(p))
is generic.

Proof.

(G,p)
is generic if and only if the field
\mathbb{Q}(p)
generated by the coordinates of
p
over
\mathbb{Q}
has transcendence degree
d|V|
over
\mathbb{Q}
. Since
R_{X}
is a rational map with rational coefficients, we can take representatives
f_{1}/g_{1},\ldots,f_{d|V|}/g_{d|V|}
for its coordinate functions, where each
f_{i}
and
g_{i}
is a polynomial in
d|V|
variables and with rational coefficients. Note that since
p
is generic, we have
g_{i}(p)\neq 0
for
i=1,\ldots,d|V|

It follows that generic frameworks are

-reflections

Our goal in this section is to understand how the sequences of partial

We start by highlighting a notational issue. As we noted before, globally rigid graphs in

. Let

Concluding remarks

5.1
Partial reflections and noncrossing separators

In
Section

3
we introduced sequences of partial reflections and analyzed them in some detail. We showed that in
d
-connected graphs with no crossing
d
-separators, partial reflections have a simple structure: every sequence of partial
d
-reflections is equivalent to a reduced sequence of partial
d
-reflections (
Corollary

3.9
) and the reduced sequences are pairwise nonequivalent, apart from trivialities (
Corollary

3.11
).

If we allow crossing
d
-separators, the situation becomes more complicated. Although we did not pursue this direction, we believe that the following is true: if
X
and
Y
are crossing
d
-fragments in a
d
-connected graph, then the sequences of partial reflections
\mathcal{R}_{X},\mathcal{R}_{X}\circ\mathcal{R}_{Y},\mathcal{R}_{X}\circ\mathcal{R}_{Y}\circ\mathcal{R}_{X},\ldots,
are pairwise nonequivalent. Note that this would lead to an alternative, geometric proof of
Theorem

4.2
, since given any generic realization of a rigid graph, the maximum number of pairwise noncongruent, equivalent realizations is finite.

In fact, this is known in the
d=2
case.
Partial
2
-reflections of the four-cycle
C_{4}
have been studied (under a different name) in the context of
Darboux’ porism
. As a consequence, for each reduced sequence of partial
2
-reflections

of
C_{4}
, a characterization is available for the space of realizations
(C_{4},p)
that are mapped to a congruent copy by
F
. See
[
12
]
and references therein.

be the three vertices of
H_{1}
that are disjoint from the hinges and
let
w
be a vertex in
H_{4}
that is
also disjoint from the hinges.
The key observation is the following. It can be shown using standard techniques from differential topology and the observation that the graph
H^{\prime}=H+xw+yw+zw
is rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
(and in particular
r_{3}(H^{\prime})=r_{3}(H)+3
).

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
(H,p)
be a generic realization in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
of the graph
H
described above. There is a positive number
\varepsilon
(depending on
(H,p)
) such that for every point
q_{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
\left\lVert p(w)-q_{w}\right\rVert<\varepsilon
there is a realization
(H,q)
, equivalent to
(H,p)
, with
q(w)=q_{w}
and
q(x)=p(x),q(y)=p(y),q(z)=p(z)
.

Let
K
be obtained from
G
and
H
by identifying the vertices
a_{1},a_{2},s_{1}
and
b_{1}
with
x,y,z
and
w
, respectively. We shall label the identified vertices according to the labeling in
G
. See
Figure

8
.
Since
G
and
H
are both
\mathcal{R}_{3}
-connected, so is
K
.
We shall construct two generic realizations
(K,p)
and
(K,p^{\prime})
such that
\{a_{1},b_{1}\}
is globally linked in
(K,p)
, but not in
(K,p^{\prime})
.

Let us first consider an arbitrary generic realization
(H,p_{H})
of
H
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and fix a positive number
\varepsilon
with the properties described by
Lemma

5.3
. Let us extend
(H,p_{H})
to a generic realization
(K,p)
in such a way that the distance of
p(b_{1})
from the plane determined by
\{p(s_{1}),p(s_{2}),p(s_{3})\}
is less than
1/2\varepsilon
, and let
p_{G}
denote the restriction of
p
to the vertex set of
G
. We claim that
\{a_{1},b_{1}\}
is not globally linked in
(K,p)
. Indeed, we can take the framework
(G,q_{G})
obtained by the partial
reflection in the plane spanned by
\{p(s_{1}),p(s_{2}),p(s_{3})\}
. By construction, we have
\left\lVert p_{G}(b_{1})-q_{G}(b_{1})\right\rVert<\varepsilon
, so
Lemma

5.3
guarantees the existence of a framework
(H,q_{H})
that is equivalent to
(H,p_{H})
and agrees with
(G,q_{G})
on the set of identified vertices. By identifying the common vertices of
(G,q_{G})
and
(H,q_{H})
, we obtain a framework
(K,q)
that is equivalent to
(K,p)
but
\left\lVert q(a_{1})-q(b_{1})\right\rVert\neq\left\lVert p(a_{1})-p(b_{1})\right\rVert
, as claimed.

Now let us fix an
a_{1},b_{1}
-path
P
in
K
whose vertices are contained in
V(H)-\{s_{1}\}
. Consider a generic framework
(K,p^{\prime})
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Let
\delta
denote the sum of the Euclidean lengths in
(K,p^{\prime})
of the edges in
P
, and let
\nu
denote the distance of
p^{\prime}(b_{1})
from the plane determined by
\{p^{\prime}(s_{1}),p^{\prime}(s_{2}),p^{\prime}(s_{3})\}
. Note that by (repeated applications of) the triangle inequality, in every framework
(K,q^{\prime})
that is equivalent to
(K,p^{\prime})
and for which the two frameworks agree on
\{a_{1},a_{2},s_{1}\}
, we have
\left\lVert p^{\prime}(b_{1})-q^{\prime}(b_{1})\right\rVert\leq 2\delta
. On the other hand, if
\left\lVert q^{\prime}(a_{1})-q^{\prime}(b_{1})\right\rVert\neq\left\lVert p^{\prime}(a_{1})-p^{\prime}(b_{1})\right\rVert
for such a realization
(K,q^{\prime})
, then the restriction of
(K,q^{\prime})
to
G
must necessarily be the framework obtained from the restriction of
(K,p^{\prime})
to
G
by a partial reflection in the plane spanned by
\{p^{\prime}(s_{1}),p^{\prime}(s_{2}),p^{\prime}(s_{3})\}
. In particular,
\left\lVert p^{\prime}(b_{1})-q^{\prime}(b_{1})\right\rVert=2\nu
. It follows that if we choose
(K,p^{\prime})
in such a way that
\delta<\nu
(which is clearly possible),
then
\{a_{1},b_{1}\}
will be globally linked in
(K,p^{\prime})
, as desired.

We note that this example also shows that a graph obtained by gluing two graphs

need not satisfy

.

5.3
Globally linked pairs and gluing

A key step in the proof of
Theorem

4.13
in
[
15
]
is the following result, which implies
Theorem

5.5
below.

Theorem 5.4
.

[
15
, Corollary 5.5]
Let
G
be an
\mathcal{R}_{2}
-circuit and let
\{u,v\}
be a
2
-separator of
G
. Then
\{u,v\}
is globally linked in
G
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be the union of the graphs
G_{1}=(V_{1},E_{1}),G_{2}=(V_{2},E_{2})
with
V_{1}\cap V_{2}=\{u,v\}
. If
\{u,v\}
is linked in
G_{i}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
for
i\in\{1,2\}
, then
\{u,v\}
is globally linked in
G
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Proof.

If
u
and
v
are adjacent in
G
, then the conclusion is immediate, so we may suppose that
u
and
v
are nonadjacent. Since
\{u,v\}
is linked in
G_{i}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
G_{i}+uv
contains an
\mathcal{R}_{2}
-circuit
C_{i}
that spans
u
and
v
, for
i\in\{1,2\}
. It is well-known that in this case
C=C_{1}\cup C_{2}-uv
(the
2-sum
of
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
) is also an
\mathcal{R}_{2}
-circuit (see, e.g.,
[
15
, Lemma 5.2]
). Since
\{u,v\}
is a
2
-separator in
C
,
Theorem

5.4
implies that
\{u,v\}
is globally linked in
C
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and thus it is also globally linked in
G
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
∎

There is a similarly short proof of
Theorem

5.4
that uses
Theorem

5.5
; in this sense, the two theorems are equivalent.
We conjecture that this “gluing theorem” can be extended in two directions: one, to higher dimensions, and two, to graphs that are glued along larger vertex sets.

Conjecture 5.6
.

Let
G
be the union of the graphs
G_{1}=(V_{1},E_{1}),G_{2}=(V_{2},E_{2})
with
|V_{1}\cap V_{2}|\leq d+1
, and let
u,v\in V_{1}\cap V_{2}
. If
\{u,v\}
is linked in
G_{i}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
i\in\{1,2\}
, then
\{u,v\}
is globally linked in
G
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Theorem

4.8
implies that
5.6
is true if
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are both
d
-joined. The
d=1
case is also true: this follows from the fact that a pair of vertices
\{u,v\}
is linked in
G
in
\mathbb{R}^{1}
if and only if
\kappa(G,u,v)\geq 1
, and globally linked in
G
in
\mathbb{R}^{1}
if and only if
\kappa(G,u,v)\geq 2
.
We can also show that the conjecture holds when
d=2
. Finally, in
[
7
]
the first author recently gave an affirmative answer for
5.6
in the special case when every vertex pair in
V_{1}\cap V_{2}
is linked in both
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We note that if we allow intersections of size larger than
d+1
, then the conclusion in
5.6
is not true in general. For the simplest counterexample, let
G=G_{1}=G_{2}
be a path of length
2
. In this case, the endvertices are linked, but not globally linked, in
G
in
\mathbb{R}^{1}
.

5.4
Algorithmic aspects

As we noted before, giving a combinatorial characterization (and a polynomial-time recognition algorithm) for (globally) rigid graphs in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a major open problem for
d\geq 3
, although probabilistic polynomial-time algorithms are known both in the case of rigidity
[
2
]
and global rigidity
[
10
]
. The situation is even worse in the case of globally linked pairs: no polynomial-time algorithm (probabilistic or otherwise) is known for testing whether a pair of vertices is globally linked in a graph in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, even for
d=2
.

For
d
-joined graphs, many questions related to global rigidity and globally linked pairs reduce to vertex connectivity problems, which are generally well-understood. In particular, we can check whether such a graph is globally rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by checking
(d+1)
-connectivity, and whether a pair of vertices is globally linked in the graph
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by checking local connectivity. We can also find an optimal augmentation into a globally rigid graph (i.e., a minimum cardinality set of edges whose addition makes the graph globally rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
) by finding an optimal augmentation into a
(d+1)
-connected graph, a problem for which there is an efficient algorithm, see
[
21
]
. We note that the optimal augmentation of a rigid graph into a globally rigid graph is known to be polynomial-time solvable in the
d=1
and
d=2
cases, see
[
18
]
.

However, testing whether a graph is
d
-joined might be difficult even in the
d=2
case. (Recall that a graph on at least
2
vertices is
1
-joined if and only if it is connected.) In this direction, we pose the following counterpart of
Theorem

5.5
as a conjecture and describe how it leads to a polynomial-time algorithm for recognizing
2
-joined graphs.

Conjecture 5.7
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be the union of the graphs
G_{1}=(V_{1},E_{1}),G_{2}=(V_{2},E_{2})
with
V_{1}\cap V_{2}=\{u,v\}
. If
\{u,v\}
is not linked in
G_{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then
\{u,v\}
is globally linked in
G
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
if and only if it is globally linked in
G_{1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

If
5.7
is true, then we can decide in polynomial time whether a graph is
2
-joined, as follows. Since the following discussion only concerns the
2
-dimensional case, we shall drop the suffix “in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
” throughout the paragraph.
Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph on at least
3
vertices. If
G
is not rigid, then it is not
2
-joined, while if it is
3
-connected, then it is
2
-joined if and only if it is globally rigid; each of these cases can be checked in polynomial time. If
G
is rigid but not
3
-connected, then consider an inclusion-wise minimal
2
-fragment
X
, which can be found recursively using the fact that there are no crossing
2
-separators in
G
(
Theorem

4.2
). Let us label the vertices in
N_{G}(X)
as
\{u,v\}
and consider
G_{1}=G[X\cup N_{G}(X)]+uv
and
G_{2}=G[V-X]+uv
.
Now
Theorem

4.3
and the minimal choice of
X
imply that
G
is
2
-joined if and only if
G_{1}
is globally rigid (which we can check in polynomial time),
G_{2}
is
2
-joined (which we can check recursively), and
\{u,v\}
is globally linked in
G
.
Thus we only need to decide whether
\{u,v\}
is globally linked in
G
, given that
G_{1}
is globally rigid. If
G_{1}-uv
is also globally rigid, then
\{u,v\}
is globally linked in
G_{1}-uv,
and thus also in
G
. Let us thus assume that
G_{1}-uv
is not globally rigid. It is still rigid by Hendrickson’s Theorem
[
11
]
, so
\{u,v\}
is linked, but not globally linked in
G
. It follows from
Theorem

5.5
and (if true) the
2
-dimensional case of
5.7
that
\{u,v\}
is globally linked in
G
if and only if it is linked in
G_{2}-uv
, which we can check in polynomial time.

In closing, we remark that given a polynomial-time algorithm for deciding whether a graph is globally rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it is also possible to check in polynomial time whether a graph has a globally rigid gluing construction in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, using a similar (but simpler) recursive approach as in the previous paragraph.

Topological computation of the first Milnor fiber cohomology of hyperplane arrangements
Morihiko Saito

Abstract
. We study a topological method to calculate the first Milnor fiber cohomology of a defining polynomial of a reduced projective hyperplane arrangement X X of degree d d . We can show the vanishing of a monodromy eigenspace of the first Milnor fiber cohomology with eigenvalue of order m ≥ 2 m\geq 2 if X ∖ ( X [ ( m ) ] ∪ X ⟨ 3 ⟩ ) X\setminus(X^{[(m)]}\cup X^{\langle 3\rangle}) or more generally X ∖ ( X [ ( m ) ] ∪ X ⟨ 3 ⟩ ∪ X d ) X\setminus(X^{[(m)]}\cup X^{\langle 3\rangle}\cup X_{d}) is connected. Here X [ ( m ) ] X^{[(m)]} is the set of points of X X with multiplicity divisible by m m , and X ⟨ 3 ⟩ := ⋃ i , j , k X i ∩ X j ∩ X k X^{\langle 3\rangle}:=\bigcup_{i,j,k}X_{i}\cap X_{j}\cap X_{k} with X i X_{i} the irreducible components of X X , where the union is taken over i , j , k i,j,k with codim ​ X i ∩ X j ∩ X k = 3 {\rm codim}\,X_{i}\cap X_{j}\cap X_{k}=3 . This hypothesis can be relaxed to some extent. The assertion is reduced to the case of a line arrangement in 𝐏 2 {\bf P}^{2} by Artin’s vanishing theorem (where X ⟨ 3 ⟩ = ∅ X^{\langle 3\rangle}=\emptyset ), and we use a projection from 𝐏 2 {\bf P}^{2} to 𝐏 1 {\bf P}^{1} with center a sufficiently general point of X d X_{d} . It may be expected that the assumption of an improved assertion is always satisfied for m ≥ 5 m\geq 5 (and also for m = 4 m=4 except the Hessian arrangement). The resulting vanishing of eigenspaces has been conjectured for m ≥ 5 m\geq 5 .

[

[

[

]

]

\displaystyle:=\{P\in X\mid{\rm mult}_{P}X\in m\hskip 1.0pt{\mathbb{Z}}\},

\displaystyle X^{\langle 3\rangle}

\displaystyle:=\hbox{$\bigcup$}_{i,j,k}\,X_{i}\cap X_{j}\cap X_{k},

\displaystyle X^{\prime}_{\langle m\rangle}

\displaystyle:=X\,{\setminus}\,(X^{[(m)]}\cup X^{\langle 3\rangle}\cup X_{d}).

1. Milnor fiber cohomology of line arrangements

In this section we study a topological method to describe the Milnor fiber cohomology of line arrangements.

1.1. Calculation of the Milnor fiber cohomology.
Let
L\subset Y:={\mathbb{P}}^{2}
be a reduced line arrangement with
f
a defining polynomial of degree
d
. It is well-known that there are local systems
{\mathcal{L}}^{k,}
of rank 1 on
U:=Y\,{\setminus}\,L\,
(
k\in[0,d{-}1]
) such that

[formula]

H^{j}(F_{\!f},{\mathbb{C}})_{\lambda}\cong H^{j}(U,{\mathcal{L}}^{k,})\quad\quad\bigl(\lambda=\exp(-2\pi\sqrt{-1}\hskip 1.0ptk/d)\bigr),

and the monodromy around any irreducible component of
L
is given by multiplication by
\lambda^{-1}=\exp(2\pi\sqrt{-1}\hskip 1.0ptk/d)
. This is constructed by taking the direct image of the constant sheaf under the natural morphism
f^{-1}(1)\to U
which is an unramified covering of degree
d
, see
[
Di1
]
,
[
CS
]
,
[
BS
]
, etc. In particular, we have
{\mathcal{L}}^{(0)}={\mathbb{C}}_{U}
, and hence
H^{j}(F_{\!f},{\mathbb{C}})_{1}=H^{j}(U,{\mathbb{C}})
.

Let
\pi:{\mathcal{Y}}\to Y:={\mathbb{P}}^{2}
be the blow-up along a sufficiently general point
P\in L_{d}
with
j_{{\mathcal{Y}}}:U\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{Y}}
a natural inclusion. Here the
L_{i}
(
i\in[1,d]
) are the irreducible components of
L
. Set

{\mathcal{F}}:={\mathbf{R}}(j_{{\mathcal{Y}}})_{*}{\mathcal{L}}^{(k^{\prime})}[2]\quad\,\,\,\hbox{with}\quad\,\,\,k^{\prime}\,{=}\,d/m\,\,\,\,\,\hbox{or}\,\,\,\,\,d-d/m.

(Here we use the irreducibility of cyclotomic polynomials implicitly, see also
[
Sa4
]
.)
This is a direct factor of the underlying
{\mathbb{C}}
-complex of a mixed Hodge module, and has the weight filtration
W
, see
[
Sa2
]
,
[
BS
]
.

Lemma 1.1.

We have the isomorphisms

[formula]

{\rm Gr}^{W}_{2}{\mathcal{F}}={\rm IC}_{{\mathcal{Y}}}{\mathcal{L}}^{(k^{\prime})},\quad\quad{\rm Gr}^{W}_{i}{\mathcal{F}}=0\,\,\,\,(i\,{\neq}\,2,3).

Moreover
\,\dim_{{\mathbb{C}}}{\mathcal{H}}^{j}{\rm Gr}^{W}_{i}\!{\mathcal{F}}_{y}=km\hskip 1.0pt{-}\hskip 1.0pt2
if
\,\pi(y)\in L^{[km]}

(k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0})\,
with
\,j\,{=}\,i\hskip 1.0pt{-}\hskip 1.0pt3\,{=}\,{-}1\,
or
\,0
, and it vanishes otherwise. Here
L^{[r]}:=\{P\in L\mid{\rm mult}_{P}L\,{=}\,r\hskip 1.0pt\}
.

Proof.
Let
\sigma:\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}\to{\mathcal{Y}}
be the blow-up along all the points of
\pi^{-1}({\rm Sing}\,L)
with
j_{\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}}:U\hookrightarrow\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}
the inclusion. Then (1.1.2) holds with
{\mathcal{Y}}
,
{\mathcal{F}}
replaced by
\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}
,
{\mathbf{R}}(j_{\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}})_{*}{\mathcal{L}}^{(k^{\prime})}[2]
, and we have the isomorphism

[formula]

{\rm IC}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}}{\mathcal{L}}^{(k^{\prime})}[-2]=(j_{\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}})_{*}{\mathcal{L}}^{(k^{\prime})}.

Moreover the restriction of
R^{j}(j_{\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}})_{*}{\mathcal{L}}^{(k^{\prime})}
to
\sigma^{-1}(y)\cong{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
with
\pi(y)\in{\rm Sing}\,L
is the direct image of a local system of rank 1 on the complement of
km
points in
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
(whose local monodromies are
non-trivial
)
if
\pi(y)\in L^{[km]}
with
j\,{=}\,0
or 1, and it vanishes otherwise. Since the Euler number of the complement of
km
points in
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
is
2\hskip 1.0pt{-}\hskip 1.0ptkm
, we then get the last assertions of Lemma 1.1 together with the isomorphism

[formula]

{\mathbf{R}}\sigma_{*}{\rm IC}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}}{\mathcal{L}}^{(k^{\prime})}={\rm IC}_{{\mathcal{Y}}}{\mathcal{L}}^{(k^{\prime})},

using the decomposition theorem
[
BBD
]
(see also
[
Sa1
]
). This finishes the proof of Lemma 1.1.

1.2. Generic projection.
There is a natural projection
\rho:{\mathcal{Y}}\to C:={\mathbb{P}}^{1}
so that
{\mathcal{Y}}
is a
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
-bundle over
C
, and the exceptional divisor of the blow-up is the zero-section. (Here
C
is identified with the set of lines on
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
passing through
P
.) There is
c_{\infty}\in C
such that

\pi\bigl(\rho^{-1}(c_{\infty})\bigr)=L_{d}.

There are isomorphisms

Y^{\prime}:=Y\,{\setminus}\,L_{d}\cong{\mathbb{C}}^{2},\quad\quad C^{\prime}:=C\,{\setminus}\,\{c_{\infty}\}\cong{\mathbb{C}}.

Let
\rho^{\prime}:Y^{\prime}\to C^{\prime}
be the restriction of
\rho
to
Y^{\prime}\,\bigl(={\mathcal{Y}}\,{\setminus}\,\pi^{-1}(L_{d})\bigr)\cong{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
. Then
\rho^{\prime}
is identified with a linear function with a constant term by the above isomorphisms.

Since
P\in L_{d}
is sufficiently general, we may assume the following:

[formula]

The restriction of
\rho^{\prime}
to
{\rm Sing}\,L\,{\setminus}\,L_{d}
is
injective.

Set

C^{\prime\prime}:=C^{\prime}\,{\setminus}\,\rho^{\prime}({\rm Sing}\,L\,{\setminus}\,L_{d}).

Lemma 1.2.

We have the vanishing

[formula]

R^{j}\rho_{*}{\rm Gr}^{W}_{i}\!{\mathcal{F}}=0\,\,\,\,\,\hbox{unless $\,(i,j)\,{=}\,(2,-1)\,$ or $\,(3,0)$}.

For
c\in C^{\prime}\cong{\mathbb{C}}
, there are equalities

[formula]

\dim_{{\mathbb{C}}}(R^{-1}\rho_{*}{\rm Gr}^{W}_{2}\!{\mathcal{F}})_{c}=\begin{cases}d\hskip 1.0pt{-}\hskip 1.0pt2&\hbox{\it if}\,\,\,\,c\in C^{\prime\prime}\\
d\hskip 1.0pt{-}\hskip 1.0pt3&\hbox{\it if}\,\,\,\,c\in\rho^{\prime}(L^{[k]}),\,\,k/m\in{\mathbb{Z}},\\
d\hskip 1.0pt{-}\hskip 1.0ptk\hskip 1.0pt{-}\hskip 1.0pt1&\hbox{\it if}\,\,\,\,c\in\rho^{\prime}(L^{[k]}),\,\,k/m\notin{\mathbb{Z}},\end{cases}

Proof.
The assertion for
{\rm Gr}^{W}_{3}\!{\mathcal{F}}
follows from Lemma 1.1. For
{\rm Gr}^{W}_{2}\!{\mathcal{F}}
, it is enough to calculate

[formula]

H^{j}({\mathcal{Y}}_{c},{\rm IC}_{{\mathcal{Y}}}{\mathcal{L}}^{(k^{\prime})}|_{{\mathcal{Y}}_{c}})=H^{j}(\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}_{c},{\rm IC}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}}{\mathcal{L}}^{(k^{\prime})}|_{\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}_{c}}),

using (1.1.4) (since
\rho
,
\sigma
are proper), where
{\mathcal{Y}}_{c}:=\rho^{-1}(c)
,
\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}_{c}:=\sigma^{-1}({\mathcal{Y}}_{c})
. We see that (1.2.4) vanishes for
j\,{\neq}\,1
, and its dimension is as in (1.2.3) by an argument similar to the proof of Lemma 1.1. This finishes the proof of Lemma 1.2.

1.3. Description of the first Milnor fiber cohomology.
Let
j_{C^{\prime}}:C^{\prime}\hookrightarrow C
be a natural inclusion. Set

{\mathcal{G}}:={\mathbf{R}}\rho_{*}{\mathcal{F}}={\mathbf{R}}(j_{C^{\prime}})_{*}{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}\quad\hbox{with}\quad{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}:={\mathcal{G}}|_{C^{\prime}}.

(This is equal to
{}^{\bf p}{\mathcal{H}}^{0}{\mathbf{R}}\rho_{*}{\mathcal{F}}\,
in the notation of
[
BBD
]
.) We have the weight filtration
W
on
{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}
with

[formula]

{\rm Gr}^{W}_{i}{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}=({\mathbf{R}}\rho_{*}{\rm Gr}^{W}_{i}\!{\mathcal{F}})|_{C^{\prime}}\quad\quad(i\,{=}\,2,3).

We have

[formula]

{\rm Supp}\,{\rm Gr}^{W}_{3}{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}\subset C^{\prime}\,{\setminus}\,C^{\prime\prime},\quad{\mathcal{H}}^{j}{\rm Gr}^{W}_{3}{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}=0\,\,\,\,(j\,{\neq}\,0),

and
{\rm Gr}^{W}_{2}{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}
is an intersection complex with coefficients in a local system. More precisely,

{\rm Gr}^{W}_{2}{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}[-1]
is a sheaf in the usual sense on
C^{\prime}
, and is the open direct image of a local system
{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime\prime}
of rank
d{-}2
on
C^{\prime\prime}
, where
{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime\prime}:={\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}[-1]|_{C^{\prime\prime}}
. (This can be shown using the decomposition theorem
[
BBD
]
or more directly by Lemma 1.4 below.)

We thus get the following.

Proposition 1.3.

There are isomorphisms

[formula]

(R^{-1}\rho_{*}{\rm Gr}^{W}_{2}\!{\mathcal{F}})|_{C^{\prime}}={\mathcal{H}}^{-1}{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime},

[formula]

H^{1}(F_{\!f},{\mathbb{C}})_{\lambda}\cong H^{-1}(C^{\prime},{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime})=\Gamma(C^{\prime\prime},{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime\prime}),

where
\lambda=\exp(\pm 2\pi\sqrt{-1}/m)
.
(
Note that the monodromy is defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}.)

1.4. Description of the vanishing cycles.
We have the following.

Lemma 1.4.

Let
c\in\rho^{\prime}(L^{[k]})\,{\setminus}\,\{c_{\infty}\}\,\subset\,C^{\prime}\cong{\mathbb{C}}
. Then

[formula]

\dim_{{\mathbb{C}}}\varphi_{t-c,\,1}\,{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime\prime}=1,\,\,\,\,\dim_{{\mathbb{C}}}\varphi_{t-c,\,\neq 1}\,{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime\prime}=0\quad\hbox{if}\quad k/m\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

[formula]

\dim_{{\mathbb{C}}}\varphi_{t-c,\,1}\,{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime\prime}=0,\,\,\,\,\dim_{{\mathbb{C}}}\varphi_{t-c,\,\neq 1}\,{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime\prime}=k{-}1\quad\hbox{if}\quad k/m\notin{\mathbb{Z}}.

Here
\,\varphi_{t-c,\,1}
and
\,\varphi_{t-c,\,\neq 1}\,
denote respectively the unipotent and non-unipotent monodromy part of the vanishing cycle functor
\varphi_{t-c}
with
t
the coordinate of
C^{\prime}\cong{\mathbb{C}}
.

Proof.
This is shown by calculating the vanishing cycles
\varphi_{\widetilde{\rho}^{*}t-c}(j_{\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}})_{*}{\mathcal{L}}^{(k^{\prime})}
on
\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}
using (1.1.3–4) together with the commutativity of the nearby and vanishing cycle functors with the direct image under a proper morphism, where
\widetilde{\rho}:=\rho\,\raise 0.6458pt\hbox{${\scriptstyle\circ}$}\,\sigma
.

In the case
k/m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, the direct image sheaf
(j_{\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}})_{*}{\mathcal{L}}^{(k^{\prime})}
is a local system on the complement of the proper transform of
L
in an open neighborhood of
\,\sigma^{-1}(y)
with
\pi(y)\in L^{[k]}
. So (1.4.1) follows.

In the case
k/m\notin{\mathbb{Z}}
, the restriction of
\varphi_{\widetilde{\rho}^{*}t-c,\,\neq 1}(j_{\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}})_{*}{\mathcal{L}}^{(k^{\prime})}
to
\,\sigma^{-1}(y)
with
\pi(y)\in L^{[k]}
is the direct image of a local system of rank 1 on the complements of
k{+}1
points in
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
(where the local monodromies are non-trivial), and we have the vanishing

\varphi_{\widetilde{\rho}^{*}t-c,\,1}(j_{\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}})_{*}{\mathcal{L}}^{(k^{\prime})}=0,

since we can verify the isomorphism

(j_{\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}})_{*}{\mathcal{L}}^{(k^{\prime})}|_{\{\widetilde{\rho}^{*}t=c\}}\,\,\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 4.2679pt\raise 3.98337pt\hbox{$\sim$}\hss}\hbox{$\longrightarrow$}\,\,\psi_{\widetilde{\rho}^{*}t-c,\,1}(j_{\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}})_{*}{\mathcal{L}}^{(k^{\prime})}.

So we get (1.4.2). This finishes the proof of Lemma 1.4.

1.5. Description of a general stalk of
{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime\prime}
.
Let
c\in C^{\prime\prime}
. Set

Z_{c}:=\pi\bigl(\rho^{-1}(c)\bigr)\subset Y,\quad Z^{\prime}_{c}:=Z_{c}\,{\setminus}\,L,\quad{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}_{c}:={\mathcal{L}}^{(k^{\prime})}|_{Z^{\prime}_{c}},

[formula]

{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime\prime}_{c}=H^{1}(Z^{\prime}_{c},{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}_{c}).

Since
Z_{c}\cong{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
and

, we have a ramified covering

p_{c}:\widetilde{Z}_{c}\to Z_{c}

of degree

, which is unramified over

, and is

over

that is,
|p_{c}^{-1}(z)|=1
if

. Here

is normal, and hence smooth. Let

. We then get an isomorphism

[formula]

{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}_{c}\cong\bigl((p^{\prime}_{c})_{*}{\mathbb{C}}_{\widetilde{Z}^{\prime}_{c}}\bigr)^{\gamma^{*}=\lambda^{\prime}},

with right-hand side the direct factor of
(p^{\prime}_{c})_{*}{\mathbb{C}}_{\widetilde{Z}^{\prime}_{c}}
on which the action of a generator

coincides with multiplication by

(replacing

if necessary). Here

is semi-simple, since it has finite order. (Note that a local system of rank 1 on a Zariski-open subset of

Since

is totally ramified at

Proposition 1.5.

We have the isomorphism

[formula]

{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime\prime}_{c}\cong H^{1}(\widetilde{Z}_{c},{\mathbb{C}})^{\gamma^{*}=\lambda^{\prime}}.

(The right-hand side denotes the direct factor of

\gamma^{*}
coincides with multiplication by

. Note that the isomorphisms in (1.5.2–3) are canonical up to constant multiplication by

2. Proof of Theorems 1 and 2

In this section we prove the main theorems.

2.1. Description of the right-hand side of
(1.5.3)
.
We can describe the right-hand side of (1.5.3) using the isomorphism by Poincaré duality:

[formula]

H^{1}(\widetilde{Z}_{c},{\mathbb{C}})=H^{1}(\widetilde{Z}_{c},{\mathbb{C}})^{\vee}=H_{1}(\widetilde{Z}_{c},{\mathbb{C}}).

Note that for
\tau,\tau^{\prime}\in H^{1}(\widetilde{Z}_{c},{\mathbb{C}})
,
\sigma\in H_{1}(\widetilde{Z}_{c},{\mathbb{C}})
, we have

[formula]

\langle\gamma^{*}\tau,\gamma^{*}\tau^{\prime}\rangle=\langle\tau,\tau^{\prime}\rangle,\quad\langle\gamma^{*}\tau,\sigma\rangle=\langle\tau,\gamma_{*}\sigma\rangle.

Let
z_{i}\in Z_{c}
with
\{z_{i}\}=Z_{c}\cap L_{i}
for each line
L_{i}\subset L
(where
z_{d}=P
). Note that
\Xi_{c}=\{z_{1},\dots,z_{d}\}
. Choose a path
\xi_{i}
between
z_{d}
and
z_{i}
, which is contained in
Z^{\prime}_{c}
except the both ends. Here we may assume
\xi_{i}\cap\xi_{j}=\{z_{d}\}
(
i\,{\neq}\,j
). We have the following.

Lemma 2.1.

Choosing
z_{\infty}\in Z_{c}
, its complement
Z_{c}\,{\setminus}\,\{z_{\infty}\}
is identified with
{\mathbb{C}}
, and the
\xi_{i}
can be defined as the closed line segment with endpoints
z_{i}
,
z_{d}
in the
2
-dimensional real affine space
{\mathbb{C}}
, if

are sufficiently general.

Proof.
Let
\ell_{i}
be the half line in the real 2-dimensional affine space
Z_{c}\,{\setminus}\,\{z_{\infty}\}\cong{\mathbb{C}}
with endpoint
z_{d}
and containing
z_{i}
. (Recall that any algebraic automorphism of the complex algebraic variety
{\mathbb{C}}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
is given by
\alpha\hskip 1.0ptx\hskip 1.0pt{+}\hskip 1.0pt\beta
with
\alpha\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*},\beta\in{\mathbb{C}}
, where

is an algebraic coordinate of
{\mathbb{C}}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
. This says also that the ambiguity of algebraic coordinate of
{\mathbb{C}}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
is given by it.) We have to show that
\ell_{i}\,{\neq}\,\ell_{j}
(
i\,{\neq}\,j
) if
P,c
are sufficiently general.

Take coordinates

of
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}\cong{\mathbb{P}}^{2}\,{\setminus}\,L_{d}
such that the equation of
L^{\prime}_{i}:=L_{i}\,{\setminus}\,L_{d}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
(
i\in[1,d{-}1]
) can be written as

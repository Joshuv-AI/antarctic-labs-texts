Abstract
. The finitistic dimension conjecture is closely connected to the symmetry of the finitistic dimension. Recent work indicates that such connection extends to one of its upper bounds, the delooping level. In this paper, we show that the same holds for the derived delooping level, which is an improvement of the delooping level. This reduces the finitistic dimension conjecture to considering algebras whose opposite algebra has (derived) delooping level zero. We thereby demonstrate ways to utilize the new concept of derived delooping level to obtain new results and present additional work involving tensor product of algebras.

16G10, 16E05

1.
Introduction

The finitistic dimension conjecture, which states that the little finitistic dimension (findim) of an Artin algebra
\Lambda
is always finite, carries significant homological implications. It is a sufficient condition for numerous other conjectures for Artin algebras, the most important of which includes the Wakamatsu tilting conjecture
[
18
]
, the Gorenstein symmetry conjecture (a consequence of the Wakamatsu tilting conjecture), the Auslander-Reiten conjecture
[
3
,
13
]
, and the Nakayama conjecture (a consequence of the Auslander-Reiten conjecture). Investigations of the findim conjecture go hand in hand with a better understanding of the representation theory of Artin algebras. Since the conjecture specifically asks about the projective dimension of
\Lambda
-modules, it is proved in special cases where the module category or the syzygy category is well understood.

In addition to solving the findim conjecture through a thorough understanding of the module category
\mathrm{mod}\,\Lambda
, other techniques rely on various upper and lower bounds of findim. There is too much work done on the subject to be comprehensive, so we only mention some invariants we are most interested in. One such lower bound is called the depth, defined as the supremum of
\mathrm{grade}\,S
over all simple
\Lambda
-modules
S
, where

\mathrm{grade}\,S=\inf\{n\in\mathbb{N}\mid\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{n}(S,\Lambda)\neq 0\}.

These definitions come from the study of stable module category
[
1
]
, and when
\Lambda
is a commutative Noetherian local ring, the Auslander-Buchsbaum formula
[
2
]
implies
0pt\Lambda=\mathrm{findim}\,\Lambda
. Popular upper bounds include the
\varphi
-dimension
\varphi\dim\Lambda

[
14
]
and the delooping level
\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda

[
9
]
. The author and Igusa
[
11
]
recently improved the delooping level to the derived delooping level
\mathrm{ddell}\,\!
. Precisely, these upper bounds satisfy

\mathrm{findim}\,\Lambda\leq\varphi\dim\Lambda,

\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}\leq\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda\leq\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda.

One natural question related to these upper bounds is whether they are always finite, and a positive answer to that question would solve the findim conjecture for Artin algebras. For the rest of the paper, when we refer to the little findim conjecture, the big findim conjecture, the
\varphi
-dimension conjecture, the delooping level conjecture, and the derived delooping level conjecture, we mean their corresponding invariants
\mathrm{findim}\,\Lambda
,
\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda
,
\varphi\dim\Lambda
,
\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda
,
\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda
are finite for all Artin algebras
\Lambda
, respectively. The
\varphi
-dimension conjecture is false by the counterexamples in
[
5
,
12
]
, but the delooping level is zero in those cases. The delooping level conjecture is false by the counterexample in
[
16
]
. However, the derived delooping level is shown to be finite and equal to the big finitistic dimension of the opposite algebra in that case (Example 3.8 in
[
11
]
), providing evidence that this new concept deserves future attention.

Another interesting aspect of the findim conjecture involves its symmetry. Let
\Lambda
be an Artin algebra. Cummings
[
6
, Theorem A]
proves the equivalence between the big finitistic dimension conjecture and the statement that
\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda<\infty
implies
\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}<\infty
for all
\Lambda
. She also proves the stronger result
[
6
, Theorem B]
that if
\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda=\infty
, then there is a related algebra
\tilde{\Lambda}
(using Construction
2.4
later) such that
\mathrm{Findim}\,\tilde{\Lambda}=\infty
and
\mathrm{Findim}\,\tilde{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{op}}=0
. The analogous result for the delooping level is the equivalence between the delooping level conjecture and the statement that
\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda<\infty
implies
\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}<\infty
for all
\Lambda
and is proved in
[
20
]
. Due to the counterexample in
[
16
]
, we know that the delooping level conjecture does not hold. However, there is no known example where
\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda=\infty
. In this paper, we prove the corresponding symmetry statement for the derived delooping level, thus providing another sufficient condition for the findim conjecture.

Theorem 1.1
.

The derived delooping level conjecture holds if and only if
\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda=0
implies
\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}<\infty
for all Artin algebras
\Lambda
.

In our formulation, algebras satisfying
\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda=0
have many useful properties such as every embedding from one projective module to another splits. These properties may make the findim conjecture easier to work with, compared to the case where we consider all
\Lambda
with
\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda<\infty
.

Preliminaries

We start with the necessary definitions and notations for the rest of the paper. Let
\Lambda

\Lambda

\mathrm{mod}\,\Lambda

\Lambda

\mathrm{mod}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
is the category of finitely generated left
\Lambda
-modules. Similarly, let
\mathrm{Mod}\,\Lambda

\Lambda
-modules, respectively. If we use the word module without specifying left or right, we always mean
right

\prescript{}{A}{M}

\Sigma M

(resp. cokernel of the injection

\mathrm{findim}\,\Lambda=\sup\{\mathrm{pd}\,M\mid\mathrm{pd}\,M<\infty,M\in\mathrm{mod}\,\Lambda\},

\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda=\sup\{\mathrm{pd}\,M\mid\mathrm{pd}\,M<\infty,M\in\mathrm{Mod}\,\Lambda\},

\mathrm{findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}\,(\text{resp. }\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}})=\sup\{\mathrm{id}\,M\mid\mathrm{id}\,M<\infty,M\in\mathrm{mod}\,\Lambda\,(\text{resp. }M\in\mathrm{Mod}\,\Lambda)\}

\mathrm{id}\,M=\inf\{n\in\mathbb{N}\mid\Sigma^{n}M\text{ is injective}\}.

The two upper bounds that we investigate are the delooping level and derived delooping level. Let

Definition 2.1
.

[

Let

(1)

The

\displaystyle\mathrm{dell}\,M

\displaystyle=\inf\{n\in\mathbb{N}\mid\Omega^{n}M\text{ is a stable retract of }\Omega^{n+1}\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\Omega$}}^{n+1}\Omega^{n}M\}

\displaystyle=\inf\{n\in\mathbb{N}\mid\Omega^{n}M\text{ is a stable retract of }\Omega^{n+1}N\text{ for some $N$}\}.

(2)

The
k
-delooping level
of

is

\displaystyle k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,M

\displaystyle=\inf\{n\in\mathbb{N}\mid\Omega^{n}M\text{ is a stable retract of }\Omega^{n+k}\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\Omega$}}^{n+k}\Omega^{n}M\}

\displaystyle=\inf\{n\in\mathbb{N}\mid\Omega^{n}M\text{ is a stable retract of }\Omega^{n+k}N\text{ for some $N$}\}.

In the special case that
k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,M=0
for all

, we say

is
infinitely deloopable
.

(3)

The
derived delooping level
of

\displaystyle\mathrm{ddell}\,M=\inf\{m\in\mathbb{N}\mid

\displaystyle\,\exists n\leq m\text{ and an exact sequence in $\mathrm{mod}\,\Lambda$ of the form}

\displaystyle\,\,0\to C_{n}\to C_{n-1}\to\cdots\to C_{1}\to C_{0}\to M\to 0,

\displaystyle\text{ where $(i+1)$-$\mathrm{dell}\,C_{i}\leq m-i$, }i=0,1,\dots,n\},

where we say
\mathrm{ddell}\,M
is equal to
m
using
n
and the exact sequence

0\to C_{n}\to\cdots\to C_{0}\to M\to 0
.

For the three invariants above that are defined on modules, we can define them on the algebra

Theorem 2.2
.

[
9
,
11
]

For Artin algebras
\Lambda
,

(1)

\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}\leq\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda\leq\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda\leq k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda.

In many cases, the upper bounds
\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda
and
\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda
are equal to
\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
, such as the radical square zero case
[
8

. While
\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda=\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
is not true in general (in fact, arbitrarily different as in
[
16
]
), there is no conclusive example where
\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}=\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda
does not hold. So, it is interesting to ask to what extent the equalities
\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}=\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda

\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}=\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda

In the special case where
\mathrm{gldim}\,\Lambda<\infty

and
\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}

and
\mathrm{ddell}\,M\leq\mathrm{pd}\,M

(2)

\mathrm{gldim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}\geq k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}\geq\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}\geq\mathrm{gldim}\,\Lambda\geq k\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda\geq\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda\geq\mathrm{gldim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}.

\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda

Observation 2.3

If

\mathrm{gldim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}=k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}=\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}=\mathrm{gldim}\,\Lambda=k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda=\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta}

3.

,

, and
f:M\otimes_{A}\bar{S}\to N
be a
B
-morphism so that
(M,N,f)
is a
\Lambda
-module. Then for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, we have

(1)

k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,\!_{A}M\leq k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,\!_{\Lambda}(M,N,f)\leq k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,\!_{A}M+1
.

(2)

k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,A\leq k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda\leq k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,A+1
.

Proof.

(1)

By (2) of Lemma
3.1
, we get that
\Omega_{\Lambda}(M,N,f)=(\Omega_{A}M,0,0)\oplus(0,Z,0)
. Since
B
is isomorphic to the direct sum of copies of
\mathbb{K}[X]/(X^{2})
, every finitely generated
B
-module is the direct sum of copies of
\mathbb{K}
and
\mathbb{K}[X]/(X^{2})
. As
B
-modules,
\mathbb{K}
is infinitely deloopable as its own syzygy, and
\mathbb{K}[X]/(X^{2})
is projective. Thus, every
\Lambda
-module of the form
(0,Z,0)
is infinitely deloopable. For higher syzygies, we see

\Omega_{\Lambda}^{i}(M,N,f)=(\Omega_{A}^{i}M,0,0)\oplus(0,Z^{\prime},0),

where
(0,Z^{\prime},0)
is infinitely deloopable and therefore has
k
-delooping level 0 for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.

We first show
k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,\!_{A}M\leq k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,\!_{\Lambda}(M,N,f)
. Suppose
k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,\!_{\Lambda}(M,N,f)=m
is finite. Then there exists
(M^{\prime},N^{\prime},f^{\prime})\in\mathrm{mod}\,\Lambda
such that

\Omega_{\Lambda}^{m}(M,N,f)=(\Omega_{A}^{m}M,0,0)\oplus(0,Z,0)\xhookrightarrow{\!\oplus}\Omega_{\Lambda}^{m+k}(M^{\prime},N^{\prime},f^{\prime})\oplus U=(\Omega_{A}^{m+k}M^{\prime},0,0)\oplus(0,Z^{\prime},0)\oplus U,

where
U
is some projective
\Lambda
-module and both
(0,Z,0)
and
(0,Z^{\prime},0)
are infinitely deloopable. By the definition of morphisms in
\mathrm{mod}\,\Lambda
, there is no nonzero map from
(\Omega_{A}^{m}M,0,0)
to
(0,Z^{\prime},0)
. Also, the
\Lambda
-module
(\Omega_{A}^{m}M,0,0)
does not have projective summands, so
(\Omega_{A}^{m}M,0,0)
must be a direct summand of
(\Omega_{A}^{m+k}M^{\prime},0,0)
. In other words,
\Omega_{A}^{m}M
is a direct summand of
\Omega_{A}^{m+k}M^{\prime}
in
\mathrm{mod}\,A
, showing
k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,\!_{A}M\leq m
.

Now we prove
k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,\!_{\Lambda}(M,N,f)\leq k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,\!_{A}M+1
. Suppose
k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,\!_{A}M=m
is finite. Then there exists
M^{\prime}\in\mathrm{mod}\,A
such that
\Omega_{A}^{m}M
is a stable retract of
\Omega_{A}^{m+k}M^{\prime}
. In particular, non-projective summands of
\Omega_{A}^{m}M
are also summands of
\Omega_{A}^{m+k}M^{\prime}
, and projective summands of
\Omega_{A}^{m}M
are not necessarily summands of
\Omega_{A}^{m+k}M^{\prime}
. We know
\Omega_{\Lambda}^{m}(M,N,f)=(\Omega_{A}^{m}M,0,0)\oplus(0,Z,0)
, where
(0,Z,0)
is infinitely deloopable. If
\Omega_{A}^{m}M
has no projective summand, then
(\Omega_{A}^{m}M,0,0)
is a direct summand of
\Omega_{\Lambda}^{m+k}(M^{\prime},0,0)=(\Omega_{A}^{m+k}M^{\prime},0,0)
, implying
k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,\!_{\Lambda}(M,N,f)\leq m
. However, if
\Omega_{A}^{m}M
has projective summands,
(\Omega_{A}^{m}M,0,0)
has a summand of the form
(P,0,0)
where
P
is a projective
A
-module. Since
P
may not be a summand of
\Omega_{A}^{m+k}M^{\prime}
,
(P,0,0)
is not necessarily a stable retract of
(\Omega_{A}^{m+k}M^{\prime},0,0)
. Moreover,
(P,0,0)
does not map to
(0,Z^{\prime},0)
nontrivially for any
Z^{\prime}\in\mathrm{mod}\,B
and is not a projective
\Lambda
-module. So,
k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,\!_{\Lambda}(M,N,f)
may not be equal to
m
.

We solve this problem by taking another syzygy. Since the non-projective summands of
\Omega_{A}^{m}M
are summands of
\Omega_{A}^{m+k}M^{\prime}
,
\Omega_{A}^{m+1}M
is a direct summand of
\Omega_{A}^{m+k+1}M^{\prime}
. This shows that the only possibly non-infinitely deloopable summand
(\Omega_{A}^{m+1}M,0,0)
of
\Omega_{\Lambda}^{m+1}(M,N,f)
is a summand of
(\Omega_{A}^{m+k+1}M^{\prime},0,0)\xhookrightarrow{\!\oplus}\Omega_{\Lambda}^{m+k+1}(M^{\prime},0,0)
. Therefore,
k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,\!_{\Lambda}(M,N,f)\leq m+1
.

(2)

From the previous part, we know
k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,\!_{\Lambda}(0,S^{\prime},0)=0
for every simple
B
-module
S^{\prime}
, so we only need to consider simple
\Lambda
-modules of the form
(S,0,0)
for every simple
A
-module
S
. Then it follows immediately from the previous part that

\sup\{k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,\!_{A}S\mid S_{A}\text{ is simple}\}\leq\sup\{k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,\!_{\Lambda}(S,0,0)\mid S_{A}\text{ is simple}\}\leq\sup\{k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,\!_{A}S\mid S_{A}\text{ is simple}\}+1,

which implies
k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,A\leq k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda\leq k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,A+1
.

∎

Now we can show that transforming from
A
to
\Lambda
only changes the derived delooping level by at most 1.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
A
be an Artin algebra and
\Lambda=(\widetilde{A^{\mathrm{op}}})^{\mathrm{op}}
be as above. Then
\mathrm{ddell}\,A\leq\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda\leq\mathrm{ddell}\,A+1
.

Proof.

We prove
\mathrm{ddell}\,A\leq\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda
first. Recall that
\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda
is always achieved by simple
\Lambda
-modules of the form
(S,0,0)
, where
S
is a simple
A
-module, since
(0,Z,0)
is infinitely deloopable for all
Z\in\mathrm{mod}\,B
.

Suppose
\mathrm{ddell}\,\!_{\Lambda}(S,0,0)=m<\infty
using
n
and the exact sequence

(4)

0\to(C_{n},D_{n},f_{n})\to\cdots\to(C_{0},D_{0},f_{0})\to(S,0,0)\to 0,

where
(i+1)
-
\mathrm{dell}\,\!_{\Lambda}(C_{i},D_{i},f_{i})\leq m-i
.

By (1) of Lemma
3.1
, we get the exact sequence in
\mathrm{mod}\,A

(5)

0\to C_{n}\to\cdots\to C_{0}\to S\to 0.

By Lemma
3.2
(1), we know
(i+1)
-
\mathrm{dell}\,\!_{A}C_{i}\leq(i+1)
-
\mathrm{dell}\,\!_{\Lambda}(C_{i},D_{i},f_{i})\leq m-i
for
i=1,\dots,n
. Therefore,
\mathrm{ddell}\,\!_{A}S\leq m
. Repeating the argument for all simple
A
-modules
S
shows
\mathrm{ddell}\,A\leq\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda
.

On the other hand, if
\mathrm{ddell}\,A=m
using
n
and the exact sequence

(6)

0\to C_{n}\to\cdots\to C_{0}\to S\to 0

where
(i+1)
-
\mathrm{dell}\,\!_{A}C_{i}\leq m-i
for
i=0,\dots,n
, then we can easily induce the following corresponding exact sequence in
\mathrm{mod}\,\Lambda

(7)

0\to(C_{n},0,0)\to\cdots\to(C_{0},0,0)\to(S,0,0)\to 0,

where
(i+1)
-
\mathrm{dell}\,\!_{\Lambda}(C_{i},0,0)\leq{(m+1)}-i
for
i=1,\dots,n
. Iterating over all simple
A
-modules
S
, we get
\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda\leq\mathrm{ddell}\,A+1
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Proposition
3.3
is still true if we replace all
\mathrm{ddell}\,\!
with the more general
k\text{-}\mathrm{ddell}\,\!
due to the same relation between
k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,\!_{A}M
and

in Lemma

. We include the simpler statement in the proposition for better readability.

We can now prove the main theorem of this section.

Theorem 3.5
.

The derived delooping level conjecture holds if and only if
\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda=0
implies
\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}<\infty
for all Artin algebras
\Lambda
.

Proof.

The forward direction is trivial, so we prove the reverse direction.

Let
A
be an Artin algebra. Construct the algebra
\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}=\widetilde{A^{\mathrm{op}}}
as before. We know
\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}=0
by construction, so by assumption,
\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda<\infty
. By Proposition
3.3
,
\mathrm{ddell}\,A<\infty
.
∎

Another consequence of Proposition
3.3
is that we can bound
\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
above in terms of
\mathrm{ddell}\,A
. Note that the inequality
\mathrm{Findim}\,A^{\mathrm{op}}\leq\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
in Corollary
3.6
appeared first in
[
7
]
.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
A
be an Artin algebra and
\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}=\widetilde{A^{\mathrm{op}}}
be as before. Then
\mathrm{Findim}\,A^{\mathrm{op}}\leq\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}\leq\mathrm{ddell}\,A+1
. In particular, if
\mathrm{ddell}\,A=\mathrm{Findim}\,A^{\mathrm{op}}
, then
\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}\in\{\mathrm{ddell}\,A,\mathrm{ddell}\,A+1\}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
(1), if
\mathrm{Findim}\,A^{\mathrm{op}}=\mathrm{id}\,M=n
with the minimal injective resolution

0\to M\to I_{0}\to\cdots I_{n}\to 0,

then we get a corresponding injective resolution of
(M,0,0)
in
\mathrm{mod}\,\Lambda

0\to(M,0,0)\to(I_{0},0,0)\to\cdots(I_{n},0,0)\to 0,

so
\mathrm{Findim}\,A^{\mathrm{op}}\leq\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
.

Therefore, the corollary follows from

\mathrm{Findim}\,A^{\mathrm{op}}\leq\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}\leq\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda\leq\mathrm{ddell}\,A+1.

∎

We conclude this section with an example where
\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda=\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda\neq\mathrm{dell}\,A=\mathrm{ddell}\,A
. As we saw in the proof of Proposition

, the inequality can only occur if there is a simple

-module

with

4.
Tensor product of algebras

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field and
\Lambda
be a finite dimensional
\mathbb{K}
-algebra. In the context of the finitistic dimension conjecture, we will assume
\mathbb{K}
is algebraically closed since the finitistic dimension is invariant under field extensions
[
15
]
. We also assume
\Lambda
is basic since every finite dimensional algebra over
\mathbb{K}
is Morita equivalent to a basic finite dimensional algebra over
\mathbb{K}
, and the finitistic dimension is invariant under Morita equivalence. In Construction
2.4
, if we choose
A=B
and
\bar{S}=A
considered as an
A
-bimodule, the triangular matrix algebra
\begin{pmatrix}A&A\\
0&A\end{pmatrix}
is the tensor product
A\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}\mathbb{K}A_{2}
, where
A_{2}
is
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 2}
, the Dynkin quiver of type
A_{2}
, and the path algebra
\mathbb{K}A_{2}
is isomorphic to the
2\times 2
upper triangular matrix algebra with coefficients in
\mathbb{K}
. In that case, we know the global dimension of the tensor product behaves additively
[
19
, Lemma 3.4]
. That is,
\mathrm{gldim}\,\left(\begin{pmatrix}A&A\\
0&A\end{pmatrix}\right)=\mathrm{gldim}\,A+\mathrm{gldim}\,\mathbb{K}A_{2}=\mathrm{gldim}\,A+1
. In general, we are interested in understanding how the derived delooping level behaves under taking tensor product with other finite dimensional algebras over the base field
\mathbb{K}
. We present the first step in proving such results involving the derived delooping level, and more general cases will be the topic of a future paper.

For the rest of this section, let
\Lambda_{1}
and
\Lambda_{2}
be basic finite dimensional algebras over the algebraically closed field
\mathbb{K}
. Suppose that
\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda_{1}^{\mathrm{op}}=m<\infty
and that
\mathrm{gldim}\,\Lambda_{2}=n<\infty
so that
\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda_{2}
is also
n
by Observation
2.3
. We identify
(\Lambda_{1}\otimes\Lambda_{2})^{\mathrm{op}}
with
\Lambda_{1}^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes\Lambda_{2}^{\mathrm{op}}
. If
S_{i}
and
T_{j}
are simple modules of
\Lambda_{1}
and
\Lambda_{2}
, respectively, then they are 1-dimensional over
\mathbb{K}
. Their tensor product
S_{i}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}T_{j}
is also 1-dimensional as a
(\Lambda_{1}\otimes\Lambda_{2})
-module, hence simple. If
P_{i}
and
Q_{j}
are projective modules of
\Lambda_{1}
and
\Lambda_{2}
, respectively, then
P_{i}\otimes Q_{i}
is a projective
(\Lambda_{1}\otimes\Lambda_{2})
-module because it is the direct sum of direct summands of
\Lambda_{1}\otimes\Lambda_{2}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose
M\in\mathrm{mod}\,\Lambda_{1}
and
N\in\mathrm{mod}\,\Lambda_{2}
. Modules denoted with letters
P
or
Q
are projective unless stated otherwise.

(1)

Given exact sequences
0\to C_{m}\to\cdots\to C_{1}\to C_{0}\to M\to 0
in
\mathrm{mod}\,\Lambda_{1}
and
0\to D_{n}\to\cdots\to D_{1}\to D_{0}\to N\to 0
in
\mathrm{mod}\,\Lambda_{2}
, there is an exact sequence in
\mathrm{mod}\,\Lambda_{1}\otimes\Lambda_{2}

0\to C_{m}\otimes D_{n}\to\cdots\to\bigoplus_{i+j=k}C_{i}\otimes D_{j}\to\cdots\to C_{0}\otimes D_{0}\to M\otimes N\to 0.

In particular, when the two exact sequences are projective resolutions, this shows

\mathrm{Findim}\,(\Lambda_{1}^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes\Lambda_{2}^{\mathrm{op}})\geq\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda_{1}^{\mathrm{op}}+\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda_{2}^{\mathrm{op}}.

(2)

If
M
is a direct summand of
M^{\prime}
, then
M\otimes N
is a direct summand of
M^{\prime}\otimes N
.

(3)

If
k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,M=m
and
Q\in\mathrm{mod}\,\Lambda_{2}
is projective, then
k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,(M\otimes Q)\leq m
in
\mathrm{mod}\,(\Lambda_{1}\otimes\Lambda_{2})
.

Proof.

(1)

By definition.

(2)

Tensor the split monomorphism
M\to M^{\prime}
with the identity map on
N
to get a split monomorphism
M\otimes N\to M^{\prime}\otimes N
.

(3)

Suppose
\Omega^{m}M\xhookrightarrow{\!\oplus}\Omega^{m+k}M^{\prime}
for some
M^{\prime}
. Applying
-\otimes Q
to
0\to\Omega^{m}M\to P_{m-1}\to\cdots\to P_{0}\to M\to 0
, we get a truncated projective resolution of
M\otimes Q
whose
m
-syzygy is
\Omega^{m}M\otimes Q
. By the previous part, it is a direct summand of the
(m+k)
-syzygy
\Omega^{m+k}M^{\prime}\otimes Q
.

∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
S
and
T
be simple modules over
\Lambda_{1}
and
\Lambda_{2}
, respectively, and
\mathrm{pd}\,T=t
. If the
s
-syzygy
\Omega^{s}S
can be delooped
t+1
more times, i.e.,
(s+t+1)
-
\mathrm{dell}\,\Omega^{s}S=0
, then
\mathrm{ddell}\,(S\otimes T)\leq s+t
.

Proof.

We have exact sequences

0\to\Omega^{s}S\to P_{s-1}\to\cdots\to P_{1}\to P_{0}\to S\to 0,

0\to\Omega^{t}T=Q_{t}\to Q_{t-1}\to\cdots\to Q_{1}\to Q_{0}\to T\to 0,

where all
P^{\prime}s
and
Q^{\prime}s
are projective.

By taking their tensor product as shown in Lemma
4.1
(1), we get an exact sequence

(9)

{0\to\Omega^{s}S\otimes Q_{t}\to(P_{s-1}\otimes Q_{t})\oplus(\Omega^{s}S\otimes Q_{t-1})\to\cdots\to Q_{0}\otimes P_{0}\to S\otimes T\to 0},

where the first non-projective term counting from the right is
\Omega^{s}S\otimes Q_{0}
if
\Omega^{s}S
is not projective. If
\Omega^{s}S
is projective, then every term in (
9
) is projective, and the result follows.

So assume
\Omega^{s}S
is not projective. All non-projective summands of the terms in (
9
) are at positions
s+t
to
s
(
Q_{0}\otimes P_{0}
is at position 0), and they are
\Omega^{s}S\otimes Q_{t}
,
\Omega^{s}S\otimes Q_{t-1}
,
\dots
,
\Omega^{s}S\otimes Q_{0}
. Since
(s+t+1)
-
\mathrm{dell}\,\Omega^{s}S=0
, we have
k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,\Omega^{s}S=0
for
k=s+1,\dots,s+t+1
. By Lemma
4.1
(3),
k
-
\mathrm{dell}\,(\Omega^{s}S\otimes Q)=0
for any projective
\Lambda_{2}
-module
Q
for
k=s+1,\dots,s+t+1
. Therefore, we get

•

(s+t+1)
-
\mathrm{dell}\,(\Omega^{s}S\otimes Q_{t})=0\leq{s+t-(s+t)=0,}

•

(s+t)
-
\mathrm{dell}\,(\Omega^{s}S\otimes Q_{t-1})=0\leq{s+t-(s+t-1)=1,}

⋮

•

(s+1)
-
\mathrm{dell}\,(\Omega^{s}S\otimes Q_{0})=0\leq{s+t-s=t.}

The terms at other positions of the exact sequence are all projective, so we naturally have their
k
-delooping level zero for any
k
. Therefore, by the definition of
\mathrm{ddell}\,\!
, we get
\mathrm{ddell}\,(S\otimes T)\leq s+t
.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

If
\mathrm{gldim}\,\Lambda_{2}=n<\infty
and
\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda_{1}^{\mathrm{op}}=k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda_{1}=m
for any
k\geq n+1
, then

\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda_{1}+\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda_{2}=\mathrm{ddell}\,(\Lambda_{1}\otimes\Lambda_{2})=\mathrm{Findim}\,(\Lambda_{1}\otimes\Lambda_{2})^{\mathrm{op}}.

Proof.

The corollary follows from

\displaystyle m+n

\displaystyle\geq\mathrm{ddell}\,(\Lambda_{1}\otimes\Lambda_{2})\geq\mathrm{Findim}\,(\Lambda_{1}\otimes\Lambda_{2})^{\mathrm{op}}=\mathrm{Findim}\,(\Lambda_{1}^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes\Lambda_{2}^{\mathrm{op}})\geq\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda_{1}^{\mathrm{op}}+\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda_{2}^{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda_{1}+\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda_{2}=m+n,

where in particular the first inequality
\mathrm{ddell}\,(\Lambda_{1}\otimes\Lambda_{2})\leq m+n
is a consequence of Proposition
4.2
since the argument works for any pair of simple modules
S
and
T
.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

If
\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda_{1}^{\mathrm{op}}=k\text{-}\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda_{1}=m<\infty
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, then

\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda_{1}+\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda_{2}=\mathrm{ddell}\,(\Lambda_{1}\otimes\Lambda_{2})=\mathrm{Findim}\,(\Lambda_{1}\otimes\Lambda_{2})^{\mathrm{op}}.

for any
\Lambda_{2}
with finite global dimension.

5.
Future Directions

We hope the new definition of derived delooping level may rekindle more interest in studying the finitistic dimensions. As we saw in Proposition
3.3
, the derived delooping level does not change under the construction of
\Lambda
from
A
. It is important to consider under what other constructions and operations does the derived delooping level stay unchanged. We formulate some future questions below.

Question 5.1
.

(1)

Can we extend Proposition
3.3
to other constructions of
\Lambda
from
A
that are related to triangular matrix algebras?

(2)

Can we loosen the condition of
\Lambda_{2}
in Section
4
to be more general algebras, such as algebras
\Lambda
whose
\mathrm{Findim}\,\Lambda^{\mathrm{op}}
is
\mathrm{dell}\,\Lambda
or
\mathrm{ddell}\,\Lambda

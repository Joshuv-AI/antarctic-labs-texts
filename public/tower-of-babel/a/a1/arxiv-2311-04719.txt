Abstract
. Let ( R , 𝔪 ) (R,\mathfrak{m}) be a Noetherian local ring and M M a finitely generated R R -module. In this paper, we study the relation among the Hilbert function, Hilbert multiplicity, index of reducibility of an 𝔪 \mathfrak{m} -primary ideal, and the irreducible multiplicity under idealization R ⋉ M R\ltimes M . Some applications to Cohen-Macaulayness and Cohen-Macaulay type of idealization are given.



1.
Introduction

Let
(A,\mathfrak{n})
be a Noetherian local ring and
I
an
\mathfrak{n}
-primary ideal of
A
. We denote by
\ell_{A}(*)
the length of an
A
-module. Let
L
be a finitely generated
R
-module of dimension
t
. It is well known that
\ell_{A}(L/I^{n+1}L)
agrees with a polynomial function of degree
t
for all
n\gg 0
. That is, there exist integers
\mathrm{e}_{I}^{0}(L),\dots,\mathrm{e}_{I}^{t}(L)
such that

\ell_{A}(L/I^{n+1}L)=\mathrm{e}_{I}^{0}(M)\binom{n+t}{t}-\mathrm{e}_{I}^{1}(L)\binom{n+t-1}{n-1}+\cdots+(-1)^{t}\mathrm{e}_{I}^{t}(L)

for
n\gg 0
. We call

\mathrm{e}_{I}^{0}(L),\dots,\mathrm{e}_{I}^{t}(L)
the
Hilbert coefficients
of
L
with respect to
I
. The leading coefficient
\mathrm{e}_{I}^{0}(L)
is called the
(Hilbert) multiplicity
of
L
with respect to
I
. The notions of Hilbert function, Hilbert multiplicity, and Hilbert coefficients are well known and have attracted the interest of several researchers
[
7
, Chapter 4]
.

Next we consider another function
\ell_{A}(I^{n+1}L:_{L}\mathfrak{n}/I^{n+1}L)
, the socle dimension of
L/I^{n+1}L
. Since
I
is
\mathfrak{n}
-primary, we also have

\ell_{A}(I^{n+1}L:_{L}\mathfrak{n}/I^{n+1}L)=\operatorname{ir}_{L}(I^{n+1}L),

where
\operatorname{ir}_{L}(I^{n+1}L)
is the index of reducibility of
I^{n+1}L
. Recall that a submodule
N
of
L
is called an
irreducible submodule
if
N
cannot be written as an intersection of two properly larger submodules of
L
. The number of irreducible components of an irredundant irreducible decomposition of
N
, which is independent of the choice of the decomposition by Noether
[
13
]
, is called the
index of reducibility
of
N
and denoted by
\mathrm{ir}_{L}(N)
. In 2015, N. T. Cuong, P. H. Quy, and H. L. Truong proved that the function
\mathrm{ir}_{L}(I^{n+1}L)
agrees with a polynomial function of degree
t-1
for
n\gg 0
(
[
8
, Theorem 4.1]
). That is, there exist integers
\mathrm{f}_{I}^{0}(L),\dots,\mathrm{f}_{I}^{t-1}(L)
such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{ir}_{L}(I^{n+1}L)=&\ell_{A}(I^{n+1}L:_{L}\mathfrak{n}/I^{n+1}L)\\
=&\mathrm{f}_{I}^{0}(L)\binom{n+t-1}{t-1}-\mathrm{f}_{I}^{1}(L)\binom{n+t-2}{n-2}+\cdots+(-1)^{t-1}\mathrm{f}_{I}^{t-1}(L)\end{split}

for
n\gg 0
.
The numbers
\mathrm{f}_{I}^{0}(L),\dots,\mathrm{f}_{I}^{t-1}(L)
are called the
irreducibility coefficients
of
L
with respect to
I
and the leading coefficient
\mathrm{f}_{I}^{0}(L)
is called the
irreducible multiplicity
of
L
with respect to
I
(see
[
17
]
,
[
3
]
). The index of reducibility and irreducible multiplicity have a strong connection with the structure of rings (see
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
15
]
-
[
18
]
). Some relations between (Hilbert) multiplicity and irreducible multiplicity are given (see
[
3
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
18
]
).

From now on, let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring of dimension
d
and
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module of dimension
t
.

The purpose of this note is to study the Hilbert function, Hilbert multiplicity, index of reducibility of powers of an ideal, and the irreducible multiplicity under idealization
R\ltimes M
. Recall that the Cartesian product
R\times M
is a commutative ring concerning componentwise addition and multiplication defined by

(r_{1},m_{1})(r_{2},m_{2})=(r_{1}r_{2},r_{1}m_{2}+r_{2}m_{1}).

and is called the
idealization
of
M
or the
trivial extension
of
R
by
M
, denoted by
R\ltimes M
. Idealization of a module was introduced by Nagata
[
12
]
. One can find many properties of idealizations in
[
5
]
and
[
12
]
. Idealization of a module is usually used to reduce results concerning submodules to the ideal case, generalizing results from ring to modules and constructing examples of commutative rings with zero divisors.

The first main result gives a relation among the Hilbert function, Hilbert coefficients of idealization
R\ltimes M
, and those of
R
and
M
as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
I
be an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal of
R
. Set
J=I\times IM
. Then

\ell_{R\ltimes M}(R\ltimes M/J^{n+1})=\ell_{R}(R/I^{n+1})+\ell_{R}(M/I^{n+1}M).

Furthermore, if
d=t
, then
\e_{J}^{i}(R\ltimes M)=\e_{I}^{i}(R)+\e_{I}^{i}(M)
for
i=0,\dots,d
. If
d>t
, then
\e_{J}^{i}(R\ltimes M)=\e_{I}^{i}(R)
for
i=0,\dots,d-t-1,
and
\e_{J}^{i}(R\ltimes M)=\e_{I}^{i}(R)+\e_{I}^{i}(M)
for
i=d-t,\dots,d.

In the above theorem we assume that
J
is a certain primary ideal of
R\ltimes M
. We will also consider the multiplicity
\e_{J}^{0}(R\ltimes M)
of
R\ltimes M
for arbitrary primary ideal
J
of
R\ltimes M
in Corollary
2.1
.

The second main result gives a relation among the index of reducibility of powers of an ideal, and the irreducible multiplicity of idealization
R\ltimes M
and those of
R
of
M
. The second main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
I
be an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal of
R
. Set
J=I\times IM
. Then

\operatorname{ir}_{M}(I^{n+1}M)\leq\operatorname{ir}_{R\ltimes M}(J^{n+1})\leq\operatorname{ir}_{R}(I^{n+1})+\operatorname{ir}_{M}(I^{n+1}M)

for all
n>0
and

\mathrm{f}_{I}^{0}(M)\leq\mathrm{f}_{J}^{0}(R\ltimes M)\leq\mathrm{f}_{I}^{0}(R)+\mathrm{f}_{I}^{0}(M).

Furthermore for
n>0
, the following hold.

(i)

\operatorname{ir}_{M}(I^{n+1}M)=\operatorname{ir}_{R\ltimes M}(J^{n+1})
if and only if
M/I^{n+1}M
is faithful.

(ii)

\operatorname{ir}_{R\ltimes M}(J^{n+1})=\operatorname{ir}_{R}(I^{n+1})+\operatorname{ir}_{M}(I^{n+1}M)
if and only if
(I^{n+1}:_{R}\mathfrak{m})M=I^{n+1}M
.

In
[
10
]
, S. Goto, S. Kumashiro, and N. T. H. Loan explored the Cohen-Macaulay type of idealization. Suppose
M
is a Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module and
Q
is a parameter ideal of
M
, then
\mathrm{f}_{Q}^{0}(M)
is the Cohen-Macaulay type of
M
(see Lemma
2.4
). With this observation, we can regard Theorem
1.2
as a generalization of
[
10
, Theorem 2.2]
.
As shown in Example
2.6
, the inequalities in Theorem
1.2
can be strict. It seems to us that the behavior of the irreducibility function and irreducible multiplicity are more mysterious than those of Hilbert function and Hilbert multiplicity. Example
2.7
shows that the irreducible multiplicity is not compatible with the reduction ideal.

In the next section we prove the main results.

2.
Proof of main results

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring. Then
R\ltimes M
is also a Noetherian local ring with unique maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}\times M
, and
\dim R\ltimes M=\dim R
. Let
\rho:R\ltimes M\rightarrow R
,
\rho((a,m))=a
be the canonical projection and
\sigma:R\rightarrow R\ltimes M
,
\sigma(a)=(a,0)
be the canonical inclusion. Then
\rho
and
\sigma
are local ring homomorphisms. Let
L
be an
R
-module (resp. an
R\ltimes M
-module). Then
L
is an
R\ltimes M
-module (resp. an
R
-module). Furthermore a submodule of
L
as an
R
-module (resp. an
R\ltimes M
-module) is also a submodule of
L
as an
R\ltimes M
-module (resp. an
R
-module). This follows that
\ell_{R}(L)=\ell_{R\ltimes M}(L).

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
I
be an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal of
R
. Set
J=I\times IM
. Then
J
is an
\mathfrak{m}\times M
-primary ideal of
R\ltimes M
by
[
5
, Theorem 3.6]
. By
[
5
, Theorem 3.3 (2)]
and by induction we have
J^{n+1}=(I\times IM)^{n+1}=I^{n+1}\times I^{n+1}M
. Hence

\displaystyle R\ltimes M/J^{n+1}

\displaystyle\cong(R\ltimes M)/(I^{n+1}\ltimes I^{n+1}M)

\displaystyle\cong\big(R/I^{n+1}\big)\ltimes\big(M/I^{n+1}M\big).

These implies that

\ell_{R\ltimes M}(R\ltimes M/J^{n+1})=\ell_{R}(R/I^{n+1})+\ell_{R}(M/I^{n+1}M).

So,

{\e}_{J}^{0}(R\ltimes M)\binom{n+d}{d}-\e_{J}^{1}(R\ltimes M)\binom{n+d-1}{d-1}+\cdots+(-1)^{d}{\e}_{J}^{d}(R\ltimes M)

={\e}_{I}^{0}(R)\binom{n+d}{d}-\e_{I}^{1}(R)\binom{n+d-1}{d-1}+\cdots+(-1)^{d}{\e}_{I}^{d}(R)+

{\e}_{I}^{0}(M)\binom{n+t}{t}-\e_{I}^{1}(M)\binom{n+t-1}{t-1}+\cdots+(-1)^{t}{\e}_{I}^{t}(M).

By comparing the coefficients, we have the second statement on multiplicity in the theorem.

∎

Theorem
1.1
considers Hilbert function and Hilbert coefficients of
R\ltimes M
with respect to a homogeneous
\mathfrak{m}\times M
-primary ideal
J=I\times IM
. Recall that the homogeneous ideals of
R\ltimes M
have the form
I\times N
, where
I
is an ideal of
R
,
N
is a submodule of
M
and
IM\subseteq N
. Prime ideals of
R\ltimes M
have the form
\p\times M
, where
\p\in\operatorname{Spec}(R)
. So they are homogeneous. However, a primary ideal need not be homogeneous. For example, in local ring
\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}\ltimes\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
ideal
J
generated by
(\overline{2},\overline{1})
is a primary ideal but
J
is not homogeneous. The following corollary consider the multiplicity of
J
on
R\ltimes M
for arbitrary
\mathfrak{m}\times M
-primary ideal
J
of
R\ltimes M
.

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
J
be an
\mathfrak{m}\times M
-primary ideal of
R\ltimes M
. Set
I=\sigma^{-1}(J)
, where
\sigma:R\rightarrow R\ltimes M
,
\sigma(a)=(a,0)
is the canonical inclusion. Then the following statements are true.

(i) If
d=t
, then
\e_{J}^{0}(R\ltimes M)=\e_{I}^{0}(R)+\e_{I}^{0}(M)
.

(ii) If
d>t
, then
\e_{J}^{0}(R\ltimes M)=\e_{I}^{0}(R)
.

Proof.

Since
I=\sigma^{-1}(J)
,
I
is an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal of
R
. Set
J^{\prime}=J+(0)\times M
. Then
J^{\prime}=I\times M
. We have
J\subseteq J^{\prime}
. Since
((0)\times M)^{2}=0
, we get that

(J^{\prime})^{n+1}=(J+(0))^{n+1}=J(J^{\prime})^{n},

for all
n\geq 1
. Hence
J
is a reduction of
J^{\prime}
. This implies that

e_{J}^{0}(R\ltimes M)=e_{J^{\prime}}^{0}(R\ltimes M)=e_{I\times M}^{0}(R\ltimes M)=e_{I\times IM}^{0}(R\ltimes M).

The conclusion (i) and (ii) follow from Theorem
1.1
.
∎

Cohen-Macaulayness of idealizations was considered in
[
5
]
by considering the depth and the dimension of idealizations. Using the above result on the multiplicity of idealizations, we can give another proof.

Corollary 2.2
.

The following statements hold true.

(i)

Assume
d=t
. Then
R\ltimes M
is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
R
is Cohen-Macaulay and
M
are Cohen-Macaulay.

(ii)

Assume
d>t
. Then
R\ltimes M
is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
R
is Cohen-Macaulay and
M=0
.

Proof.

Let
Q=(r_{1},\dots,r_{d})
be a parameter ideal of
R
. Then
\ell_{R}(M/QM)<\infty.
Let
\overline{Q}
be an ideal generated by elements
(r_{1},0),\dots,(r_{d},0)
. It is easy to check that

\overline{Q}=Q\times QM
. Since

R\ltimes M/\overline{Q}\cong R/Q\ltimes M/QM,

we get that

\ell_{R\ltimes M}(R\ltimes M/\overline{Q})=\ell_{R}(R/Q)+\ell_{R}(M/QM)<\infty
. Hence
\overline{Q}
is a parameter ideal of
R\ltimes M
.

(i) Assume
d=t
.
By Theorem
1.1
,

\e(\overline{Q},R\ltimes M)=\e(Q\times QM,R\ltimes M)=\e(Q,R)+\e(Q,M).

Then

\ell(R\ltimes M/\overline{Q})-\e(\overline{Q},R\ltimes M)=\ell(R/Q)-\e(Q,R)+\ell(M/QM)-e(Q,M).

This implies that
R\ltimes M
is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
R
is Cohen-Macaulay and
M
are Cohen-Macaulay.

(ii) Assume that
d>t
. Then

\ell(R\ltimes M/\overline{Q})-\e(\overline{Q},R\ltimes M)=\ell(R/Q)-\e(Q,R)+\ell(M/QM).

It follows that
R\ltimes M
is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
R
is Cohen-Macaulay and
M=0
.

∎

An elementary property of colon ideal in idealization is given in the following lemma (see
[
1
]
). It is a preliminary to prove Theorem
1.2
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
I\times N
and
I^{\prime}\times N^{\prime}
be homogeneous ideals of
R\ltimes M
. Then

(I\times N):_{R\ltimes M}(I^{\prime}\times N^{\prime})=((I:_{R}I^{\prime})\cap(N:_{R}N^{\prime}))\times(N:_{M}I^{\prime}).

Furthermore
(I\times N):_{R\ltimes M}(I^{\prime}\times N^{\prime})
is a homogeneous ideal of
R\ltimes M
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

By
[
5
, Theorem 3.3 (2)]
and by induction on
n
we have

J^{n+1}=(I\times IM)^{n+1}=I^{n+1}\times I^{n+1}M.

By Lemma
2.3
,

J^{n+1}:_{R\ltimes M}(\mathfrak{m}\times M)=((I^{n+1}:_{R}\mathfrak{m})\cap(I^{n+1}M:_{R}M))\times(I^{n+1}M:_{M}\mathfrak{m}).

Then

\displaystyle\Big(J^{n+1}

\displaystyle:_{R\ltimes M}(\mathfrak{m}\times M)\Big)/J^{n+1}

\displaystyle=\Big(((I^{n+1}:_{R}\mathfrak{m})\cap(I^{n+1}M:_{R}M))\times(I^{n+1}M:_{M}\mathfrak{m})\Big)/\Big(I^{n+1}\times I^{n+1}M\Big)

\displaystyle\cong\Big(\big((I^{n+1}:_{R}\mathfrak{m})\cap(I^{n+1}M:_{R}M)\big)/I^{n+1}\Big)\times\Big((I^{n+1}M:_{M}\mathfrak{m})/I^{n+1}M\Big).

So,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\ell_{R}((I^{n+1}M&:_{M}\mathfrak{m})/I^{n+1}M)\leq\ell_{R\ltimes M}(J^{n+1}:_{R\ltimes M}(\mathfrak{m}\times M)/J^{n+1})\\
&=\ell_{R}((I^{n+1}:_{R}\mathfrak{m})\cap(I^{n+1}M:_{R}M)/I^{n+1})+\ell_{R}((I^{n+1}M:_{M}\mathfrak{m})/I^{n+1}M)\\
&\leq\ell_{R}(I^{n+1}:_{R}\mathfrak{m}/I^{n+1})+\ell_{R}((I^{n+1}M:_{M}\mathfrak{m})/I^{n+1}M).\end{split}

This proves the first statement.
Dividing by
n^{t-1}
and taking
n\rightarrow\infty
, we get that

\mathrm{f}_{I}^{0}(M)\leq\mathrm{f}_{J}^{0}(R\ltimes M)\leq\mathrm{f}_{I}^{0}(R)+\mathrm{f}_{I}^{0}(M).

(i)
\operatorname{ir}_{M}(I^{n+1}M)=\operatorname{ir}_{R\ltimes M}(J^{n+1})
if and only if
(I^{n+1}:_{R}\mathfrak{m})\cap(I^{n+1}M:_{R}M)=I^{n+1}.
This is equivalent to
(0:_{R/I^{n+1}}\mathfrak{m})\cap(0:_{R/I^{n+1}}M/I^{n+1}M)=0
. Since
R/I^{n+1}
is Artinian local ring, the above condition means
\operatorname{Soc}(M/I^{n+1}M)=0
or
M/I^{n+1}M
is faithful.

(ii)
\operatorname{ir}_{R\ltimes M}(J^{n+1})=\operatorname{ir}_{R}(I^{n+1})+\operatorname{ir}_{M}(I^{n+1}M)
if and only if
I^{n+1}:_{R}\mathfrak{m}\subseteq I^{n+1}M:_{R}M
. This is equivalent to
(I^{n+1}:_{R}\mathfrak{m})M\subseteq I^{n+1}M
or
(I^{n+1}:_{R}\mathfrak{m})M=I^{n+1}M
.

∎

The type
[
7
, Definition 1.2.15]
of a Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module
M
is called the Cohen-Macaulay type of
M
, defined by
r_{R}(M)=\dim_{R/\mathfrak{m}}\big(\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{t}(R/\mathfrak{m},M)\big)
. The Cohen-Macaulay type, the index of reducibility of
QM
, and the dimension of socle
\operatorname{Soc}(M/QM)
, where
Q
is a parameter of a Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module
M
coincide as in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose that
M
is a Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module and
Q
is a parameter ideal of
M
. Then

r_{R}(M)=\ell_{R}((Q:_{M}\mathfrak{m})/QM)=\operatorname{ir}_{M}(QM)=\dim_{R/\mathfrak{m}}(\operatorname{Soc}(M/QM))=\mathrm{f}_{Q}^{0}(M).

Proof.

Since
Q
is an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal,

\operatorname{ir}_{M}(QM)=\ell_{R}((Q:_{M}\mathfrak{m})/QM)=\dim_{R/\mathfrak{m}}(\operatorname{Soc}(M/QM)).

Since
M
is Cohen-Macaulay,
r_{R}(M)=\ell_{R}((Q:_{M}\mathfrak{m})/QM)
. Furthermore, we also have that
Q
is generated by
M
-regular sequence of
t
element. This follows that
Q^{n}M/Q^{n+1}M\cong\big(M/QM\big)^{\binom{n+t-1}{t-1}}.
Since
Q
is generated by
M
-regular sequence,

Q^{n+1}M:\mathfrak{m}\subseteq Q^{n+1}M:Q=Q^{n}M.

We have

\displaystyle\operatorname{ir}_{M}(Q^{n+1}M)

\displaystyle=\ell_{R}\big((Q^{n+1}M:\mathfrak{m})/Q^{n+1}M\big)

\displaystyle=\ell_{R}\big(0:_{Q^{n}M/Q^{n+1}M}\mathfrak{m}\big)

\displaystyle=\ell_{R}((Q:_{M}\mathfrak{m})/QM)\binom{n+t-1}{t-1}.

So,
\mathrm{f}_{Q}^{0}(M)=\ell_{R}((Q:_{M}\mathfrak{m})/QM)
. Furthermore, we get that

(1)

\displaystyle\operatorname{ir}_{M}(Q^{n+1}M)=\mathrm{f}_{Q}^{0}(M)\binom{n+t-1}{t-1}.

∎

Lemma
2.4
is not true if
Q
is not a parameter ideal of
R
(See Example
2.7
).

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
M
be a maximal Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module. Then

\mathrm{r}_{R}(M)\leq\mathrm{r}(R\ltimes M)\leq\mathrm{r}(R)+\mathrm{r}_{R}(M).

Let
Q
be a parameter ideal of
R
and set
\overline{R}=R/Q,\overline{M}=M/QM
. We then have the following.

(i)
\mathrm{r}(R\ltimes M)=\mathrm{r}_{R}(M)
if and only if
\overline{M}
is a faithful
\overline{R}
-module.

(ii)
\mathrm{r}(R\ltimes M)=\mathrm{r}(R)+\mathrm{r}_{R}(M)
if and only if
\left(Q:_{R}\mathfrak{m}\right)M=QM
.

Proof.

Assume
Q=(r_{1},\dots,r_{d})
be a parameter ideal of
R
. Then
Q
is a parameter ideal of
M
. Let
\overline{Q}
be an ideal of
R\ltimes M
generated by
(r_{1},0),\dots,(r_{d},0)
. Then
\overline{Q}=Q\times QM
is a parameter ideal of
R\ltimes M
. Then (i) and (ii) follow from Theorem
1.2
and Lemma
2.4
.

Example 2.6
.

(
[
10
, Example 2.3]
)
Let
k
be a field and
\ell\geq 2
be an integer. Set
S=k[[X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{\ell}]]
the formal power series ring in variables
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{\ell}
. Let
\mathfrak{a}=\mathbb{I}_{2}(\mathbb{M})
denote the ideal of
S
generated by the maximal minors of the matrix

\mathbb{M}=\begin{pmatrix}X_{1}&X_{2}&\ldots&X_{\ell-1}&X_{\ell}\\
X_{2}&X_{3}&\ldots&X_{\ell}&X_{1}^{q}\end{pmatrix},

where
\mathfrak{q}\geq 2
. We set
R=S/\mathfrak{a}
. Then
R
is a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension one. For each integer
2\leq p\leq\ell
, we consider the ideal
I_{p}=(x_{1})+(x_{p},x_{p+1},\ldots,x_{\ell})
of
R
, where
x_{i}
denotes the image of
X_{i}
in
R
. Then by
[
10
, Example 2.3]
,
r(R)=\ell-1,

r(R\ltimes I_{p})=(l-p)+1
and

r_{R}(I_{p})=\begin{cases}\ell&\mbox{if}\,\,p=2\\
\ell-1&\mbox{if}\,\,p\geq 3\end{cases}

for each
2\leq p\leq\ell.
Let
\ell=p=3
and set
M=I_{3}
. we have

r(M)<r(R\ltimes M)<r(R)+r(M).

Note that
M
is Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module of dimension 1. Let
Q=(r)
be a parameter ideal of
R
. Then it is a parameter ideal of
M
. Set
\overline{Q}=(r,0)
. Then
\overline{Q}
is a parameter ideal of
R\ltimes M
and
\overline{Q}=Q\times QM
(see Corollary
2.2
). By Lemma
2.4
,
\mathrm{f}_{Q}^{0}(M)=r_{R}(M)
. So,

\mathrm{f}_{Q}^{0}(M)<\mathrm{f}_{\overline{Q}}^{0}(R\ltimes M)<\mathrm{f}_{Q}^{0}(R)+\mathrm{f}_{Q}^{0}(M).

Since
R
,
M
and
R\ltimes M

Example 2.7
.

Consider the ring
R
in Example
2.6
, where
p=\ell=q=2.
Then

R=k[[x_{1},x_{2}]]/(x_{1}^{3}-x_{2}^{2})\cong k[[t^{2},t^{3}]],

where
x_{1}\mapsto t^{2},x_{2}\mapsto t^{3}.
Set
I=I_{2}
and
J=(x_{1})
. Then
I
is the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
R
. Since
I^{2}=JI
, we get that
J
is a reduction ideal of
I
. By Example
2.6
,
r(R)=\ell-1=1.
Since
J
is a parameter ideal of the Cohen-Macaulay ring
R
,
\mathrm{f}_{J}^{0}(R)=1
by Lemma
2.4
. Now, we compute
\ell_{R}\big((I^{n+1}:\mathfrak{m})/I^{n+1}\big)
. Since
I^{n+1}=x_{1}^{n}I
,

I^{n+1}:\mathfrak{m}=(t^{2n+2},t^{2n+3}):(t^{2},t^{3})=(t^{2n},t^{2n+1}).

\displaystyle\ell_{R}\big((I^{n+1}:\mathfrak{m})/I^{n+1}\big)

\displaystyle=\ell_{R}\big((t^{2n},t^{2n+1})/(t^{2n+2},t^{2n+3})\big)

\displaystyle=\dim_{k}(kt^{2n}+kt^{2n+1})

\displaystyle=2.

So,
\mathrm{f}_{I}^{0}(R)=2

\mathrm{f}_{I}^{0}(R)\neq\mathrm{f}_{J}^{0}(R).

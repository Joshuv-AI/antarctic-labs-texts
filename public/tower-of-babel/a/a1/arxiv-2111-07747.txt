Ribet’s conjecture for Eisenstein maximal ideals
Debargha Banerjee, Narasimha Kumar, Dipramit Majumdar

Abstract
. According to Ogg’s conjecture (Mazur’s Theorem), cuspidal subgroup coincides with rational torsion points of the Jacobian variety of modular curves of the form X 0 ​ ( N ) X_{0}(N) for a prime number N N . There is a recent interest to generalize the conjecture for arbitrary N N by Ribet, Ohta and Yoo. In this direction, Ribet conjectured [ 28 , p. 360] that all the Eisenstein maximal ideals are “cuspidal". Hwajong Yoo proved the conjecture ( under certain hypothesis) provided that those ideals are rational . In this article, we show that ( under certain hypothesis), Ribet’s conjecture is true for non-rational Eisenstein maximal ideals.

Proof.

Recall that

for any modular form

\gamma_{d}=\begin{pmatrix}d&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}
. This essentially follows from the fact if the Fourier expansion of

then the Fourier expansion of

, where

0\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

We obtain,

L([l]^{+}E_{\varphi},\chi,s)=L(E_{\varphi},\chi,s)-\frac{\varphi(l)}{l}L(E_{\varphi\mid_{\gamma_{l}}},\chi,s)=(1-\chi(l)\varphi(l)l^{-s})L(E_{\varphi},\chi,s),

and

L([q]^{-}E_{\varphi},\chi,s)=L(E_{\varphi},\chi,s)-\varphi^{-1}(q)L(E_{\varphi\mid_{\gamma_{q}}},\chi,s)=(1-\chi(q)\varphi^{-1}(q)q^{1-s})L(E_{\varphi},\chi,s).

Using the above two formulas we get,

L(E_{\varphi,T_{1},T_{2}},\chi,s)=\Big(\prod_{l\mid T_{1}}(1-\chi(l)\varphi(l)l^{-s})\Big)\Big(\prod_{q\mid T_{2}}(1-\chi(q)\varphi^{-1}(q)q^{1-s})\Big)L(E_{\varphi},\chi,s).

Result follows from the fact that,
E_{\varphi,M,L}=E_{\varphi,T_{1},T_{2}}\mid_{\gamma_{\frac{ML}{T_{1}T_{2}}}}
and

.

Theorem 17

Let

-module. Given a cohomology class

Proof.

Choose a prime ideal

. Consider now the cohomology class

We define

\Phi_{\mathfrak{P}}(\gamma)=\Phi_{\mathfrak{P}}(\{x,\gamma x\})

We show
\Phi_{\mathfrak{P}}=0
in the following 4 steps.

Step 1
:
If

and
\mathfrak{P}\nmid\frac{m-1}{2}

\Phi_{\mathfrak{P}}(\{0,\frac{Na}{m}\}_{\Gamma_{0}(N)})=0

We remark that the proof of this step is essentially same as in Step
1
of
[
22
, Theorem 2.1]
.

Step 2
:

In this step, we show that

\Phi_{\mathfrak{P}}
vanishes on the subgroup:

\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(N)=\Big\{\begin{pmatrix}a&bN\\
cN&d\end{pmatrix}\in SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})|a,d\equiv\pm 1\pmod{N^{2}}\Big\}.

The proof of this step is same as in Step
2
of
[
22
, Theorem 2.1]
.

Step 3
:

Let

\Gamma_{0}^{{}^{\prime}}(N)=\Big\{\begin{pmatrix}a&bN\\
cN&d\end{pmatrix}\Big\}\subset\Gamma_{0}(N),

then
\Phi_{\mathfrak{P}}
vanishes on
\Gamma_{0}^{{}^{\prime}}(N)
.

Since we have
(a,N)=(d,N)=1
, which is equivalent to saying
(a,N^{2})=(d,N^{2})=1
, we see that,

a^{\frac{\phi(N^{2})}{2}}=d^{\frac{\phi(N^{2})}{2}}=\pm 1\pmod{N^{2}},

here
\phi
denotes the Euler’s totient function. Let
\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&bN\\
cN&d\end{pmatrix}\equiv\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\
0&d\end{pmatrix}\pmod{N}\in\Gamma_{0}^{{}^{\prime}}(N)
.
Then

\gamma^{\frac{\phi(N^{2})}{2}}\equiv\begin{pmatrix}a^{\frac{\phi(N^{2})}{2}}&0\\
0&d^{\frac{\phi(N^{2})}{2}}\end{pmatrix}\pmod{N}.

Since
a^{\frac{\phi(N^{2})}{2}}=d^{\frac{\phi(N^{2})}{2}}=\pm 1\pmod{N^{2}}
, we see that
\gamma^{\frac{\phi(N^{2})}{2}}\in\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}(N)
.
Thus
\Phi_{\mathfrak{P}}(\gamma^{\frac{\phi(N^{2})}{2}})=0
by Step 2. Since
\Phi_{\mathfrak{P}}
is a homomorphism, we have,

\Phi_{\mathfrak{P}}(\gamma^{\frac{\phi(N^{2})}{2}})=\frac{\phi(N^{2})}{2}\Phi_{\mathfrak{P}}(\gamma)=0.

Since

(and hence
A_{\mathfrak{P}}
) is torsionfree, we have
\Phi_{\mathfrak{P}}(\gamma)=0
.

Step 4
:

In this step, we show that
\Phi_{\mathfrak{P}}=0
.

Let

be
the matrix

Nc&d\\
\end{array}\right)
, now consider a parabolic matrix

0&1\\
\end{array}\right)

Clearly, we then have

.

Since the homomorphism
\Gamma_{0}(N)\to H_{1}(X_{0}(N),\mathbb{Z})
is surjective, we see
\Phi_{\mathfrak{P}}=0
.

Since
\Phi_{\mathfrak{P}}=0
for all primes
\mathfrak{P}
, we get
\Phi=0
.

\displaystyle\tilde{\beta}_{\Gamma_{0}(N),\varphi,M,L}

\displaystyle=\frac{f^{4}\phi(T_{2,\varphi})}{24\prod_{p\mid f}p^{2}}\Big(\prod_{p\mid f}p^{\nu_{p}(M)+\delta_{p}}\Big)\tau(\varphi^{-1})\prod_{p\mid fT_{1}}(p^{2}-1)

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{f^{2}}{\prod_{p\mid f}p^{2}}\Big)\Big(\frac{f}{\prod_{p\mid f}p}\cdot\frac{1}{24}\prod_{p\mid f}(p-1)p(p+1)\Big)\Big(f\phi(T_{2,\varphi})\tau(\varphi^{-1})\prod_{p\mid f}p^{\nu_{p}(M)+\delta_{p}}\prod_{p\mid T_{1}}(p^{2}-1)\Big)

Since
f>2
, it follows that
\frac{f}{\prod_{p\mid f}p}\cdot\frac{1}{24}\prod_{p\mid f}(p-1)p(p+1)
is an integer. As a consequence, we see that
\tilde{\beta}_{\Gamma_{0}(N),\varphi,M,L}\in\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{f},\varphi]
.

Case II:

Now we consider the case
\varphi^{2}\neq\mathbbm{1}
( this implies
f\geq 5
). In this situation
\xi
is an primitive Dirichlet character of conductor
n>1
. Note that

B_{2}(\xi^{-1})=n\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\xi^{-1}(i)\big((\frac{i}{n})^{2}-\frac{i}{n}+\frac{1}{6}\big)=\frac{1}{6n}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\xi^{-1}(i)(6i^{2}-6in+n^{2}).

As a consequence, we obtain

\displaystyle\tilde{\beta}_{\Gamma_{0}(N),\varphi,M,L}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{24}\frac{f^{3}}{n^{3}\prod_{p\mid f,p\nmid n}p^{2}}\Big(f\phi(T_{2,\varphi})\big(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\xi^{-1}(i)(6i^{2}-6in+n^{2})\big)\tau(\varphi^{-1})\tau(\xi)\prod_{p\mid f}p^{\nu_{p}(M)+\delta_{p}}\prod_{p\mid fT_{1},p\nmid n}(p^{2}-\xi(p))\Big).

Then it follows that
12\tilde{\beta}_{\Gamma_{0}(N),\varphi,M,L}\in\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{f},\varphi]
(since either
f
is even, or if
f
is odd, then

\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\xi^{-1}(i)(6i^{2}-6in+n^{2})=2\sum_{i=1}^{(n-1)/2}\xi^{-1}(i)(6i^{2}-6in+n^{2})).

∎

5.
Classification of non-rational Eisenstein maximal ideals

Let
p
be an odd prime and
N=p^{2}N^{\prime}
, where
N^{\prime}
is a square-free integer which is co-prime to
p
. Recall that, if all the prime divisors of
N^{\prime}
are congruent to
\pm 1
modulo
p
, then
N
is
p
-good.

Denote by
M_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\mathbb{C})
the complex vector space of classical modular forms as in
[
8
]
.
Let
M_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\mathbb{Z})
denote the set of elements
f\in M_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\mathbb{C})
whose Fourier coefficients at the cusp
\infty
are in
\mathbb{Z}
.
By
[
7
, Cor. 12.3.12, Proposition 12.4.1]
,
M_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\mathbb{Z})
is stable under the action of Hecke operators and contains a basis of
M_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\mathbb{C})
.
For any
\mathbb{Z}
algebra
S
, we define,
M_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N),S)=M_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\mathbb{Z})\otimes S
and we denote by
S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N),S)
the corresponding space of cusp forms (the forms for which constant terms are zero).

For any integer
n
, the Hecke operators
T_{n}
act on the the space
S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\mathbb{Z})
. Let
\mathbb{T}(N)\subseteq\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{Z}}(S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\mathbb{Z})
be Hecke algebra, i.e.,
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra generated by
T_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. It follows that
\mathbb{T}:=\mathbb{T}(N)
is a free
\mathbb{Z}
-module of rank
d
, where
d
is the dimension of the complex vector space
S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\mathbb{C})
.

Note that for a maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
\mathbb{T}
, the contraction
\mathfrak{m}\cap\mathbb{Z}=l\mathbb{Z}
is a non zero prime ideal of
\mathbb{Z}
. Hence,
l\in\mathfrak{m}
and
\mathbb{T}/\mathfrak{m}
can be identified with a finite field of characteristic
l
, say
\kappa(\mathfrak{m})
. By abuse of notation, we call
l
the characteristic of
\mathfrak{m}
.

Following
[
17
, Proposition 5.1]
, we associate Galois representation to any arbitrary
maximal ideals
\mathfrak{m}
of
\mathbb{T}
. According to loc. cit., there exist an unique continuous semi-simple Galois representation

\rho_{\mathfrak{m}}:{\mathrm{Gal}}(\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{\mathbb{Q}})\rightarrow{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(\mathbb{T}/\mathfrak{m})

unramified outside
lN
such that
\mathrm{Tr}(\rho_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathrm{Frob}_{p}))\equiv T_{p}\pmod{\mathfrak{m}}
and
\mathrm{det}(\rho_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathrm{Frob}_{p}))\equiv p\pmod{\mathfrak{m}}

for all primes
p\nmid lN
.

Recall that, this proposition in turn follows from
[
6
, Theorem 6.7]
. Using Cheboterov density theorem, we see that

\mathrm{det}(\rho_{\mathfrak{m}})=\overline{\chi_{l}}
, here
\overline{\chi_{l}}
denotes the mod
l
cyclotomic character.

We choose an isomorphism
\psi^{\prime}:\mathbb{T}/\mathfrak{m}\to\kappa(\mathfrak{m})
. Using the isomophism
\psi^{\prime}
, we can view
\rho_{\mathfrak{m}}
as a representation
\rho_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime}:{\mathrm{Gal}}({\bar{\mathbb{Q}}}/\mathbb{Q})\to{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(\kappa(\mathfrak{m}))
. Note that for a different choice of isomorphism
\psi^{\prime\prime}:\mathbb{T}/\mathfrak{m}\to\kappa(\mathfrak{m})
, we obtain a different representation
\rho_{m}^{\prime\prime}:{\mathrm{Gal}}({\bar{\mathbb{Q}}}/\mathbb{Q})\to{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(\kappa(\mathfrak{m}))
. Observe that the representations
\rho_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime}
and
\rho_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime\prime}
are Galois
{\mathrm{Gal}}(\kappa(\mathfrak{m})/\mathbb{F}_{l})
conjugate. Thus the representation
\rho_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime}
is unique upto Galois
{\mathrm{Gal}}(\kappa(\mathfrak{m})/\mathbb{F}_{l})
conjugate.

Definition 21
.

(Cuspform associated to maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
)
We have a natural ring homomorphism

T_{\mathfrak{m}}:\mathbb{T}\rightarrow\frac{\mathbb{T}}{\mathfrak{m}}\cong\kappa(\mathfrak{m}).

and there is a non-degenarate bilinear pairing
[
17
, Eqn. (10), Pg. 465]
:

S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\kappa(\mathfrak{m}))\times{\mathrm{Hom}}(\mathbb{T},\kappa(\mathfrak{m}))\rightarrow\kappa(\mathfrak{m}).

Hence the ring homomorphism
T_{\mathfrak{m}}
give rise to an
unique
cuspform (depending on the chosen isomorphism between
\mathbb{T}/\mathfrak{m}
and
\kappa(\mathfrak{m})
)
f_{\mathfrak{m}}:=\sum_{n\geq 1}T_{\mathfrak{m}}(T_{n})q^{n}\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\kappa(\mathfrak{m}))
. We call
f_{\mathfrak{m}}
the cusp form associated to the Eisenstein maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
. Different choice of isomorphisms between
\mathbb{T}/\mathfrak{m}
and
\kappa(\mathfrak{m})
will give rise to
{\mathrm{Gal}}(\kappa(\mathfrak{m})/\mathbb{F}_{l})
-conjugate cuspforms in
S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\kappa(\mathfrak{m}))
.
The mod
l
modular form
f_{\mathfrak{m}}
may lift to several modular forms of different weights, levels and nebentypus
[
3
, Theorem 2.1]
.

Let us fix an isomorphism
\psi^{\prime}:\mathbb{T}/\mathfrak{m}\to\kappa(m)
and by abuse of notation, let us denote
\rho_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime}
by
\rho_{\mathfrak{m}}
. Recall that a maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
in
\mathbb{T}
is called Eisenstein maximal ideal if the Galois representation
\rho_{\mathfrak{m}}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(\kappa(\mathfrak{m}))
associated to
\mathfrak{m}
is reducible. Since it is semi-simple,
there exist continuous characters

\bar{\alpha}_{\mathfrak{m}},\bar{\beta}_{\mathfrak{m}}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\kappa(\mathfrak{m})^{\times}

such that

\rho_{\mathfrak{m}}\simeq\bar{\alpha}_{\mathfrak{m}}\bigoplus\bar{\beta}_{\mathfrak{m}}.

We classify the Eisenstein maximal ideals of
\mathbb{T}
in the following proposition. This is a generalisation of
[
19
, Lemma 0.1]
. For any prime
p
, let
G_{p}
be the decomposition group at the prime
p
.

Proposition 22
.

Let
p
be an odd prime and
N=p^{2}N^{\prime}
, where
N^{\prime}
is a square-free integer which is coprime to
p
. Let
\mathfrak{m}
be a maximal ideal of
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{T}(N)
. Assume that
l\nmid 6p
, here
l
denotes the residual characteristic of
\mathfrak{m}
.
Then
\mathfrak{m}
is Eisentstein if and only if there exist there exists a unique character
\overline{\epsilon}_{\mathfrak{m}}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\kappa(\mathfrak{m})^{\times}
unramified outside
p

such that

\rho_{\mathfrak{m}}\simeq\overline{\epsilon}_{\mathfrak{m}}\oplus(\overline{\epsilon}_{\mathfrak{m}})^{-1}\overline{\chi}_{l}.

Proof.

If
\overline{\chi}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\kappa(\mathfrak{m})^{\times}
is a continuous character, by
[
20
, Prop 21.6.3, p.223]
, then
\overline{\chi}=\overline{\theta}\overline{\chi}_{l}^{i}
for some integer
i
and some character
\overline{\theta}
unramified at
l
. Since
\rho_{\mathfrak{m}}=\bar{\alpha}_{\mathfrak{m}}\oplus\bar{\beta}_{\mathfrak{m}}
,
for some continuous character
\bar{\alpha}_{\mathfrak{m}},\bar{\beta}_{\mathfrak{m}}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\kappa(\mathfrak{m})^{\times}
, it follows that there are unramified (at
l
) characters
\overline{\epsilon}_{1},\overline{\epsilon}_{2}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\kappa(\mathfrak{m})^{\times}
and some integer
i
and
j
such that

\rho_{\mathfrak{m}}\simeq\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\overline{\epsilon}_{1}\overline{\chi}_{l}^{i}&0\\
0&\overline{\epsilon}_{2}\overline{\chi}_{l}^{j}\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right).

Since
\overline{\chi}_{l}=\det(\rho_{\mathfrak{m}})=\overline{\epsilon}_{1}\overline{\epsilon}_{2}\overline{\chi}_{l}^{i+j}
, it follows that
i+j\equiv 1\pmod{l-1}
and
\overline{\epsilon}_{1}=(\overline{\epsilon}_{2})^{-1}
. Let
\overline{\epsilon}_{\mathfrak{m}}:=\overline{\epsilon}_{2}
. Note that
\overline{\epsilon}_{\mathfrak{m}}
is unramified at
l
.

Let
f_{\mathfrak{m}}\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\kappa(\mathfrak{m}))
be the cusp form associated to the Eisenstein maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
(Definition
21
). Let
\rho_{f_{\mathfrak{m}}}
be the unique two dimensional semisimple odd Galois representation (continuous)
\rho_{f_{\mathfrak{m}}}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{l})
associated to
f_{\mathfrak{m}}
.
Observe that
\rho_{f_{\mathfrak{m}}}
and
\rho_{\mathfrak{m}}
have same characteristic polynomial at
\mathrm{Frob}_{r}
for all prime
r\nmid lN=lp^{2}N^{\prime}
. By Brauer-Nesbitt theorem, for any prime
q
, we have
(\rho_{f_{\mathfrak{m}}})^{s.s.}\mid_{G_{q}}\simeq\rho_{\mathfrak{m}}\mid_{G_{q}}
.
Recall that
N^{\prime}
is squarefree, it follows that
\rho_{f_{\mathfrak{m}}}
is semistable outside
lp

[
12
, Proposition 14.1, p. 113]
. It follows that
\overline{\epsilon}_{\mathfrak{m}}\overline{\chi}_{l}^{j}
and
(\overline{\epsilon}_{\mathfrak{m}})^{-1}\overline{\chi}_{l}^{i}
are unramified outside
pl
. By applying
[
9
, Theorem 2.5]
and noting that

(\rho_{f_{\mathfrak{m}}})^{s.s.}\mid_{I_{l}}\simeq\rho_{\mathfrak{m}}\mid_{I_{l}}
, we deduce that
\overline{\epsilon}_{\mathfrak{m}}
is unramified outside
p
as
p\neq l
.

Let
a_{l}
denotes the
T_{l}
eigenvalue of
f_{\mathfrak{m}}
. By our assumption,
l\geq 5
and
l\nmid 6p
. If
l\nmid N^{\prime}
, then as
\rho_{f_{\mathfrak{m}}\mid_{G_{q}}}
is reducible, we get that
a_{l}\neq 0
, as otherwise it will contradict Fontaine’s theorem
[
9
, Theorem 2.6]
. Hence from Deligne’s theorem
[
9
, Theorem 2.5]
we get

\rho_{f_{\mathfrak{m}}\mid_{G_{l}}}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\lambda(a_{l}^{-1})\overline{\chi}_{l}&\star\\
0&\lambda(a_{l})\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right),

here
\lambda(x)
denotes the unramified character which sends
\mathrm{Frob}_{l}
to
x
.
On the other hand if
l\mid N^{\prime}
, we can not directly apply
[
9
, Theorem 2.5, Theorem 2.6]
. We use the following trick of Ribet as described in
[
19
]
. By
[
18
, Theorem 2.1, Step 4]
we can replace mod
l
cusp form
f_{\mathfrak{m}}\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\kappa(\mathfrak{m}))
by some cusp form
g_{\mathfrak{m}}\in S_{l+1}(\Gamma_{0}(N/l),\kappa(\mathfrak{m}))
as
f_{\mathfrak{m}}
and
g_{\mathfrak{m}}
have same mod
l
Galois representation. Now as
l\nmid N/l
, using
[
9
, Theorem 2.5, Theorem 2.6]
, we obtain

\rho_{g_{\mathfrak{m}}\mid_{G_{l}}}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\lambda(a_{l}^{-1})\overline{\chi}_{l}^{l}&\star\\
0&\lambda(a_{l})\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right).

Note that
\overline{\chi}_{l}^{l}=\overline{\chi}_{l}
. As a consequence, in either case we may assume that
\overline{\alpha}_{\mathfrak{m}}=\overline{\epsilon}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{-1}\overline{\chi}_{l}
and
\overline{\beta}_{\mathfrak{m}}=\overline{\epsilon}_{\mathfrak{m}}
.
∎

.

Corollary 24

∎

Corollary 26
.

Let
\mathfrak{m}
be a non-rational Eisenstein maximal ideal of
N=p^{2}N^{\prime}
(
N^{\prime}
is square-free, co-prime to
p
) and residual characteristic of
\mathfrak{m}
is
l\nmid 6p
. Let

I_{\mathfrak{m}}(N)=(f_{r}(T_{r}),r\text{ primes such that }r\nmid N).

Then
\mathfrak{m}\supset(l,I_{\mathfrak{m}}(N))
.

Proof.

The ideal
(l,I_{\mathfrak{m}}(N))
is independent of the choice of
f_{r}(X)
as any lift of
\overline{f_{r}(X)}
satisfies
f_{r}(X)\equiv\overline{f_{r}(X)}\pmod{l}
. We have a map,

\mathbb{T}(N)\to\mathbb{T}(N)/\mathfrak{m}\cong\kappa(\mathfrak{m})\text{ sending }T_{r}\mapsto\overline{\epsilon}_{\mathfrak{m}}(r)+r(\overline{\epsilon}_{\mathfrak{m}})^{-1}(r)\text{ for primes }r\nmid N.

It is obvious that
(l,I_{\mathfrak{m}}(N))
is in the kernel of the map (for any choice isomorphism between
\mathbb{T}/\mathfrak{m}
and
\kappa(\mathfrak{m})
) and hence
\mathfrak{m}\supset(l,I_{\mathfrak{m}}(N))
.
∎

For rest of the section we assume that
N
is
p
-good, that is, there exists an odd prime
p
and a square-free integer
N^{\prime}
whose all the prime divisors of are congruent to
\pm 1
modulo
p
and
N=p^{2}N^{\prime}

whose residual characteristic

is co-prime to
6p
.

First we recall definition of
q
-old and
q
-new Eisenstein ideal
[
30
, Section 2]
. For any prime

, there are two degeneracy coverings
\alpha_{q}(N/q),\beta_{q}(N/q):X_{0}(N)\to X_{0}(N/q)

and we identify

(resp.

\Gamma_{0}(N/q)\backslash\mathbb{H}\cup\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})

This two degeneracy maps in turn define two maps
\alpha_{q}(N/q)^{*},\beta_{q}(N/q)^{*}:J_{0}(N/q)\to J_{0}(N)

Consider the map
\gamma_{q}(N)^{*}:J_{0}(N/q)\times J_{0}(N/q)\to J_{0}(N)

-old subvariety of

J_{0}(N)
by

-new subvariety of

of

N
be a
p
-good integer for an odd prime

. For a prime

(resp.

N
(resp.
N/q

U_{p}=0\in\mathbb{T}^{p-new},U_{p}^{2}-\tau_{p}U_{p}\in\mathbb{T}^{p-old}\text{ and }U_{p_{i}}+w_{p_{i}}=0\in\mathbb{T}^{p-new},U_{p_{i}}^{2}-\tau_{p_{i}}U_{p_{i}}+p_{i}=0\in\mathbb{T}^{p-old},

Observe that as

Lemma 28
.

Let

be

-good and let

Given a
p
-good integer
N
, from Theorem 1 it follows that residual characteristic a non-rational Eisenstein ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
\mathbb{T}(N)
must divide

. Given a
p
good integer
N
, we determie which primes (other than

) may appear as residual characteristic of some non-rational Eisenstein ideal of
\mathbb{T}(N)
. To to do this we define the following two subsets of rational primes as follows.

Let

denote the set of rational primes such that

, here the union is over the set of non-trivial Dirichlet characters of conductor

and

Proposition 36
.

Let
N
be an integer which is
p
-good for an odd prime
p
and let
\mathfrak{m}
be a non-rational Eisenstein maximal ideal of
\mathbb{T}(N)
. The residual characteristic of
\mathfrak{m}
belongs in the set
\{2,3,p\}\cup S_{1}(N)\cup S_{2}(N)
.

Proof.

If the residual characteristic divides
6p
, then nothing to prove. From now we assume that
\mathfrak{m}
is a non-rational Eisenstein ideal of
\mathbb{T}(N)
whose residual characteristic
l
is co-prime to
6p
.
From the proof of Theorem 1 it follows that
E_{\mathfrak{m}}=E_{\varphi,M,\frac{N^{\prime}}{M}}
for some non-trivial Dirichlet character
\varphi
of conductor
p
and some integer
M\mid N^{\prime}
. Moreover, the residual characteristic
l
of
\mathfrak{m}
divides
|C_{\Gamma_{0}(N)}(E_{\mathfrak{m}})|=|\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{p},\varphi]/(Num(\tilde{\beta}_{\Gamma_{0}(N),\varphi,M,\frac{N^{\prime}}{M}})|
. Recall that if
\varphi
is the quadratic character, then

\tilde{\beta}_{\Gamma_{0}(N),\varphi,M,\frac{N^{\prime}}{M}}=\frac{p^{2}\phi(\frac{N^{\prime}}{M})}{24}\sqrt{\pm p}\prod_{l\mid pM}(l^{2}-1)=\frac{p^{2}}{24}\sqrt{\pm p}\Big(\phi(\frac{N^{\prime}}{M})\prod_{l\mid pM}(l^{2}-1)\Big)

otherwise,

\tilde{\beta}_{\Gamma_{0}(N),\varphi,M,\frac{N^{\prime}}{M}}=\frac{p^{2}\phi(\frac{N^{\prime}}{M})}{4}B_{2}(\xi^{-1})\tau(\varphi^{-1})\tau(\xi)\prod_{l\mid M}(l^{2}-1)=\frac{p}{24}\tau(\varphi^{-1})\tau(\xi)\Big(6pB_{2}(\xi^{-1})\phi(\frac{N^{\prime}}{M})\prod_{l\mid M}(l^{2}-1)\Big).

Since
l\nmid 6p
, in the case
\varphi
is quadratic character,
l\mid|\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{p},\varphi]/(Num(\tilde{\beta}_{\Gamma_{0}(N),\varphi,M,\frac{N^{\prime}}{M}})|

if and only if

l\mid|\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{p},\varphi]/(6pB_{2}(\xi^{-1})\phi(\frac{N^{\prime}}{M})\prod_{p\mid M}(p^{2}-1))|.

The last statement implies that

∎

(1)

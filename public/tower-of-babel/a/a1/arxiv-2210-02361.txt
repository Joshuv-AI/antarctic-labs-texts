The Power of Duality: Response Time Analysis meets Integer Programming
Max A. Deppert, Klaus Jansen

Abstract
We study a mutually enriching connection between response time analysis in real-time systems and the mixing set problem. Thereby generalizing over known results we present a new approach to the computation of response times in fixed-priority uniprocessor real-time scheduling. We even allow that the tasks are delayed by some period-constrained release jitter. By studying a dual problem formulation of the decision problem as an integer linear program we show that worst-case response times can be computed by algorithmically exploiting a conditional reduction to an instance of the mixing set problem. In the important case of harmonic periods our new technique admits a near-quadratic algorithm to the exact computation of worst-case response times. We show that generally, a smaller utilization leads to more efficient algorithms even in fixed-priority scheduling. Worst-case response times can be understood as least fixed points to non-trivial fixed point equations and as such, our approach may also be used to solve suitable fixed point problems. Furthermore, we show that our technique can be reversed to solve the mixing set problem by computing worst-case response times to associated real-time scheduling task systems. Finally, we also apply our optimization technique to solve 4-block integer programs with simple objective functions.

Introduction

We consider fixed-priority uniprocessor real-time task scheduling with constrained deadlines and task release jitters in the
sporadic task model

[
1
]
which is a common model to analyze real-time task systems.

Model

A sporadic task system
\mathscr{T}=\set{\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n}}
is a set of sporadic tasks
\tau_{i}
which are given as a quadruple of non-negative integers
\tau_{i}=(c_{i},d_{i},p_{i},\eta_{i})
where
c_{i}
represents the task’s worst-case execution time,
d_{i}
its relative deadline,
p_{i}
its period, and
\eta_{i}
its release jitter. The task
\tau_{i}
generates an infinite sequence of jobs. Each job has an execution time of at most
c_{i}\geq 1
time units and a deadline exactly
d_{i}
time units after its arrival time, where
c_{i}\leq d_{i}\leq p_{i}
. The jobs of the task are considered to arrive separated in time by at least
p_{i}\geq 1
time units. However, a job is not ready for execution until it is
released
. The time difference between the arrival time and the release time is called
release jitter
. Hence,
in jitter-free systems the jobs are released in the moment they arrive, i.e.
arrival time equals release time.
The release jitter
\eta_{i}
of the task is the maximum time difference between the arrival times and the release times over all jobs of
\tau_{i}
. Naturally, one can assume that
\eta_{i}\leq d_{i}-c_{i}\leq p_{i}-c_{i}
.

The task system
\mathscr{T}
has
harmonic periods
iff
p_{i}\geq p_{j}
implies that
p_{i}/p_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
for all tasks
\tau_{i},\tau_{j}
.
The
utilization
of a task
\tau_{i}
is the quantity
c_{i}/p_{i}
and the utilization of the task set
\mathscr{T}
is
\sum_{i\leq n}c_{i}/p_{i}
. The common assumption is that
\sum_{i\leq n}c_{i}/p_{i}\leq 1
as otherwise there are job sequences of
\mathscr{T}
which can not be scheduled by any algorithm. We refer to this utilization bound as the
schedulability utilization bound
. However, a necessary utilization bound is
\sum_{i<n}c_{i}/p_{i}<1
as otherwise there is no solution for worst-case response times at all. We refer to this bound as the
general utilization bound
.

Response Time Analysis and Schedulability Testing

The
response time
of a task is the maximum time difference between the release of a job and its completion. In a worst-case scenario we refer to them as
worst-case response times
and these may be used to decide the schedulability of a system. Since task release jitters intensify the complexity of worst-case response times and schedulability testing as well as other types of analysis, they are often assumed to be zero, i.e.
\eta_{i}=0
for all
i\leq n
.
As pointed out by Liu and Layland
[
5
]
, in such
jitter-free
systems the
critical instant
occurs when the jobs of all tasks
\tau_{i}
are released simultaneously. See also
SAS
(
SAS
)
[
7
,
8
]
.
Therefore, without release jitters the worst-case response time of task
\tau_{j}
is the earliest point in time
r_{j}
where

\textstyle r_{j}\geq c_{j}+\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}c_{i}\lceil r_{j}/p_{i}\rceil.

This inequality models the situation that all jobs are released at time
0
.
If
r_{j}\leq d_{j}
, then task
t_{j}
completes its job on time which means that task
\tau_{j}
is schedulable. If
r_{j}\leq d_{j}
for all tasks
t_{j}
, then the task system
\mathscr{T}
is schedulable using the given priorities.

In this paper we consider systems with release jitters. Audsley et al.
[
9
]
and Tindell et al.
[
10
]
have discussed the release jitter problem in detail (see also
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
). Their work leads to the following understanding of worst-case response times in systems with task release jitters.
The worst-case response time of task
\tau_{j}
is the earliest point in time
r_{j}
where

r_{j}\geq c_{j}+\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}c_{i}\lceil(r_{j}+\eta_{i})/p_{i}\rceil.

The task is schedulable if
r_{j}\leq d_{j}-\eta_{j}
and the task set
\mathscr{T}
is schedulable if
r_{j}\leq d_{j}-\eta_{j}
for all tasks
\tau_{j}
.
Without loss of generality we can assume that the execution requirement of each job of a task
\tau_{i}
always equals the task’s worst-case execution time
c_{i}
. Also we can assume that the
i
-th job of a task
\tau_{j}
is released in the time interval
[(i-1)p_{j},(i-1)p_{j}+\eta_{j}]
and its due time is
(i-1)p_{j}+d_{j}
.
Furthermore, we may restrict our interest to the response time
r_{n}
of the last task
\tau_{n}
:

r_{n}=\min\set{t}{t\geq c_{n}+\sum_{i<n}c_{i}\Big\lceil\frac{t+\eta_{i}}{p_{i}}\Big\rceil,t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}.

We refer to the problem of the computation of
r_{n}
as
response time computation
(RTC).

For
FP
scheduling, RTC is NP-hard in general due to Eisenbrand and Rothvoß
[
16
]
even for
\eta=0
and it is even hard to approximate within a constant factor. However, for jitter-free task systems with harmonic periods it is polynomial which is a great result by Bonifaci et al.
[
17
]
and it may even be computed in near-linear time, cf. Nguyen et al.
[
18
]
.

The computation of response times in real-time systems is often treated as a fixed point problem, where one aims for the least fixed point to the fixed point equation
\Phi(t)=t
where
\Phi(t)=c_{n}+\sum_{i<n}c_{i}\lceil(t+\eta_{i})/p_{i}\rceil
. The computation of least fixed points (e.g.
[
19
,
20
]
) is an important topic in order theory and logic with many applications, for example in denotational semantics. As such, the new approach which we present in this paper may also be used to find least fixed points of suitable fixed point problems .

Mixing Set

In the
Mixing Set
problem one is given capacities
a\in\mathbb{Q}^{n}
and a right-hand side
b\in\mathbb{Q}^{n}
and the goal is to find a solution
(s,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n}
which optimizes a linear objective function while satisfying the following system of inequalities:

s+a_{i}x_{i}\geq b_{i}\quad\forall i\in\set{1,\dots,n}

The problem is NP-hard due to Eisenbrand and Rothvoß
[
21
]
. However, it can be solved in polynomial time in the case of unit capacities
[
22
,
23
]
or harmonic capacities
[
24
]
(see also
[
25
,
26
]
for simpler approaches). The
Mixing Set
problem plays an important role in production planning (in particular lot-sizing
[
27
]
).

Here we introduce a dualization technique for optimization problems. Also we show first insights about the

2.1
A Dualization Technique

We present a simple optimization technique by studying a dual formulation of the associated decision problem. Consider a general optimization problem as follows.
Let
X
be a set and let
f:X\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
and
g:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be two computable functions and we seek to minimize
f(x)
over all
x\in X
while
g(x)\geq b
for some right-hand side
b\in\mathbb{R}
and other constraints
G(x)
have to be satisfied, i.e. we aim to compute

\sigma=\min\set{f(x)}{g(x)\geq b,\;G(x),\;x\in X}.

For an integer
k
it holds that
\sigma\leq k
if and only if

\max\set{g(x)}{f(x)\leq k,\;G(x),\;x\in X}\geq b.

Hence, if we can bound
\sigma
to an interval
[\ell,u]
and have an algorithm to decide (
2.1
) in at most time
T
for each
k\in[\ell,u]
, then we can compute
\sigma
in time
\Oh(T\log(u-\ell))
by using a binary search for the smallest feasible
k
in
[\ell,u]
.
At first glance this does not help a lot, as we have replaced one optimization problem by another. However, if
f
is a function of low complexity, then in order to decide (
2.1
) we can try to utilize an algorithm for
\max\set{g(x)}{G(x),x\in X}
with respect to the simple additional constraint
f(x)\leq\kappa
(which is of low complexity). Remark that the approach may be adapted to approximate
\sigma
for a function
f:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as well. However, in this paper we only care about integer-valued objectives. We apply this technique in
\cref
response-time-computation,application-to-4-block.

2.2
Mixing Set

Although
Mixing Set
can be solved in more general settings, for the scope of this paper we will always assume that
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{n}
and
b\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. In more detail, given capacities
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{n}
, a right-hand side
b\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, and weights
w\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
(and
w_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
) we consider the following minimization problem:

\min\set{w_{0}s+\textstyle\sum_{i\leq n}w_{i}x_{i}}{s+a_{i}x_{i}\geq b_{i}\;\forall i\in[n],\;s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\;x\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}

Formally, this is not a
Mixing Set
problem, since
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
is required to be an integer instead of a real number. However, due to the integrality of all other numbers the relaxation to
s\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
admits just the same set of
optimal
solutions. From
[
26
]
(or
[
24
,
25
]
) we can derive the following theorem:

Theorem 2

(
[
26
]
)
.

For harmonic capacities, (
2.2
) can be computed in time
\Oh(n^{2})
.

Given a value for
s
it is obvious that
x_{i}=\lceil(b_{i}-s)/a_{i}\rceil
for all
i\leq n
leads to an optimal completion of
s
to a solution
(s,x)
to (
2.2
). The solution
(s,x)
might not be optimal overall but the choice of
x
is optimal with respect to
s
. Therefore, given a solution
(s,x)
we will assume that
x_{i}=\lceil(b_{i}-s)/a_{i}\rceil
. Furthermore, we will write
x_{i}(s)=\lceil(b_{i}-s)/a_{i}\rceil
(and
x(s)=(x_{1}(s),\dots,x_{n}(s))
) to denote the dependency of variable
x_{i}
on
s
. Remark that by adding/subtracting the least common multiple
m=\lcm_{j\leq n}a_{j}
to/from
s
the value
x_{i}(s)
is shifted by
m/a_{i}
in the sense that

x_{i}(s\pm m)=\Big\lceil\frac{b_{i}-s\mp m}{a_{i}}\Big\rceil=\Big\lceil\frac{b_{i}-s}{a_{i}}\Big\rceil\mp\frac{m}{a_{i}}=x_{i}(s)\mp\frac{m}{a_{i}}

for all
i\leq n
.
We will depend on an upper bound on the value of
s
in optimal solutions to (
2.2
). Therefore, the following observations will be important.

Observation 3
.

If
\sum_{i\leq n}\frac{w_{i}}{a_{i}}>w_{0}
, then (
2.2
) is unbounded.

Proof.

Let
m=\lcm_{i\leq n}a_{i}
. For any feasible solution
(s,x)
we get another feasible solution by adding a multiple of
m
to
s
. In particular it holds that

\displaystyle w_{0}(s+m)+\sum_{i\leq n}w_{i}x_{i}(s+m)

\displaystyle\;=\;w_{0}(s+m)+\sum_{i\leq n}w_{i}x_{i}(s)-\sum_{i\leq n}w_{i}\frac{m}{a_{i}}

\displaystyle\;=\;w_{0}s+\sum_{i\leq n}w_{i}x_{i}(s)+m\Big(w_{0}-\sum_{i\leq n}\frac{w_{i}}{a_{i}}\Big)

and that means that solution
(s^{\prime},x(s^{\prime}))
with
s^{\prime}=s+m>s
will be a truly better solution than
(s,x)
if
\sum_{i\leq n}\frac{w_{i}}{a_{i}}>w_{0}
.
∎

Hence, we assume that
\sum_{i\leq n}\frac{w_{i}}{a_{i}}\leq w_{0}
in general.

Observation 4
.

There is an optimal solution to (
2.2
) with
s<\lcm_{i\leq n}a_{i}
.

Proof.

Let
m=\lcm_{i\leq n}a_{i}
and let
(s,x)
be an optimal solution with
s\geq m
. We find

w_{0}(s-m)+\!\sum_{i\leq n}w_{i}x_{i}(s-m)=w_{0}s+\!\sum_{i\leq n}w_{i}x_{i}(s)-m\Big(\!\underbrace{w_{0}-\!\!\sum_{i\leq n}\frac{w_{i}}{a_{i}}}_{\geq\,0}\!\Big)\leq w_{0}s+\!\sum_{i\leq n}w_{i}x_{i}(s).

Hence,
(s^{\prime}=s-m,x^{\prime}=x(s^{\prime}))
is an optimal solution too. Iterate this argument to find an optimal solution with
s
smaller than
m
.
∎

By using an analog contradiction proof it is easy to see that
any
optimal solution will hold
s<\lcm_{i\leq n}a_{i}
if
\sum_{i\leq n}\frac{w_{i}}{a_{i}}
is truly smaller than
w_{0}
. We will use this strengthening:

Observation 5
.

If
\sum_{i\leq n}\frac{w_{i}}{a_{i}}<w_{0}
, then any optimal solution to (
2.2
) holds
s<\lcm_{i\leq n}a_{i}
.

In
\cref
lcm-bound-for-S-is-tight we show that this bound on
s
is tight in general.

2.3
Response Time Bounds

First of all, we refine the utilization bound
\sum_{i<n}c_{i}/p_{i}<1
. In general it holds that

\sum_{i<n}\frac{c_{i}}{p_{i}}\leq 1-\frac{1}{\lcm_{i<n}p_{i}}.

To see this, let
m=\lcm_{j<n}p_{j}
. From
\sum_{i<n}c_{i}/p_{i}<1
it follows that
m\cdot\sum_{i<n}c_{i}/p_{i}<m
and both sides of this inequality are integers which implies that

Lemma 6

It holds that
\ell\leq r_{n}\leq u=\min\set{u_{1},u_{2}}

\displaystyle\ell=\frac{c_{n}+\sum_{i<n}\frac{\eta_{i}}{p_{i}}c_{i}}{1-\sum_{i<n}\frac{c_{i}}{p_{i}}},\;u_{1}=\ell+\frac{\sum_{i<n}c_{i}}{1-\sum_{i<n}\frac{c_{i}}{p_{i}}},\;u_{2}=\left\lceil\frac{\sum_{i\leq n}c_{i}}{(1-\sum_{i<n}\frac{c_{i}}{p_{i}})\lcm_{i<n}p_{i}}\right\rceil\lcm_{i<n}p_{i}.

By dropping the roundings it is easy to see that

To prove the former upper bound

r_{n}\;=\;c_{n}+\sum_{i<n}\Big\lceil\frac{r_{n}+\eta_{i}}{p_{i}}\Big\rceil c_{i}\;\leq\;c_{n}+\sum_{i<n}\Big(\frac{r_{n}+\eta_{i}}{p_{i}}+1\Big)c_{i}

To confirm the latter upper bound

u_{2}\geq(\sum_{i\leq n}c_{i})/(1-\sum_{i<n}c_{i}/p_{i})

\sum_{i\leq n}c_{i}\leq u_{2}(1-\sum_{i<n}c_{i}/p_{i})
and we observe

\displaystyle c_{n}+\sum_{i<n}\Big\lceil\frac{u_{2}+\eta_{i}}{p_{i}}\Big\rceil c_{i}\;

\displaystyle=\;c_{n}+\sum_{i<n}\Big(\frac{u_{2}}{p_{i}}+\Big\lceil\frac{\eta_{i}}{p_{i}}\Big\rceil\Big)c_{i}

\lcm
property

\displaystyle\leq\;c_{n}+\sum_{i<n}\Big(\frac{u_{2}}{p_{i}}+1\Big)c_{i}

0\leq\eta_{i}\leq p_{i}

\displaystyle=\;u_{2}\sum_{i<n}\frac{c_{i}}{p_{i}}+\sum_{i\leq n}c_{i}

which proves the claim.

Lemma 7

3
Response Time Computation

We introduce generalized problem formulations as follows. For an integer
\gamma\geq 1
we aim to compute the general response time

\mathscr{R}(\gamma)=\min\set{t}{t\geq\gamma+\textstyle\sum_{i<n}\displaystyle c_{i}\Big\lceil\frac{t+\eta_{i}}{p_{i}}\Big\rceil,\;t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}

or the response time for the simplified instance without release jitters

\mathscr{R}_{0}(\gamma)=\min\set{t}{t\geq\gamma+\textstyle\sum_{i<n}\displaystyle c_{i}\Big\lceil\frac{t}{p_{i}}\Big\rceil,\;t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}.

Obviously,
r_{n}=\mathscr{R}(c_{n})
is the worst-case response time of task
\tau_{n}
and
r_{n}^{\prime}=\mathscr{R}_{0}(c_{n})
is the worst-case response time for the simplified instance without release jitters.

Before presenting our algorithms we want to mention a last ingredient, which simply says that task release jitters make worst-case response times worse. The argument is simple:

\gamma+\sum_{i<n}c_{i}\Big\lceil\frac{\mathscr{R}(\gamma)}{p_{i}}\Big\rceil\leq c_{n}+\sum_{i<n}c_{i}\Big\lceil\frac{\mathscr{R}(\gamma)+\eta_{i}}{p_{i}}\Big\rceil\leq\mathscr{R}(\gamma)

\mathscr{R}(\gamma)

\mathscr{R}(\gamma)
is a solution for the jitter-free case as well. Thus, we find that

\mathscr{R}_{0}(\gamma)\leq\mathscr{R}(\gamma).

The following theorem reveals a first simple algorithm to compute worst-case response times for general periods in exponential time.

.

Given capacities
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{n}
, a right-hand side
b\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
which holds that
\lcm_{j\leq n}a_{j}\leq b_{i}\leq b_{\min}+a_{i}
for all
i\leq n
, and weights
w\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
, one can compute

\textstyle\min\,\{\,s+\sum_{i\leq n}w_{i}x_{i}\;|\;s+a_{i}x_{i}\geq b_{i}\;\forall i\leq n,\;s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\;x\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\,\}

in time
\Oh(T_{\mathscr{R}}\log b_{\max})
where
T_{\mathscr{R}}
is a global bound on the time required to compute (
3.1
).

Proof.

Choose
\beta=b_{\min}
and
\eta_{i}=b_{i}-b_{\min}
for all
i\in[n]
. We get
\beta\geq\lcm_{i\leq n}a_{i}\geq S
and
0\leq\eta_{i}\leq a_{i}
for all
i\leq n
. This implies
b_{i}=\beta+\eta_{i}
and thus, we can write (
17
) as

\min\,\{\,s+\textstyle\sum_{i\leq n}w_{i}x_{i}\;|\;s+a_{i}x_{i}\geq\beta+\eta_{i}\;\forall i\leq n,\;s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\;x\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\,\}.

Now, we use
\cref
reverse-reduction to start a binary search for the optimal
k
. We know that
\mathscr{R}(\beta-k)
can be computed in time
T_{\mathscr{R}}
and the objective value of our
Mixing Set
instance is upper bounded by
b_{\max}
(since
(s=b_{\max},x=0)
is always a solution with objective value
b_{\max}
) which implies a total running time of
\Oh(T_{\mathscr{R}}\log b_{\max})
.
∎

In fact,
\cref
mixing-set-with-large-crowded-right-hand-side implicitly solves the general case. We use it to prove the following.

Theorem 18
.

Given capacities
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{n}
, a right-hand side
b\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, and weights
w\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
, one can compute

\textstyle\min\,\{\,s+\sum_{i\leq n}w_{i}x_{i}\;|\;s+a_{i}x_{i}\geq b_{i}\;\forall i\leq n,\;s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\;x\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\,\}

in time
\Oh(n\!+\!T_{\mathscr{R}}\log b_{\max})
where
T_{\mathscr{R}}
is a global bound on the time required to compute (
3.1
).

Proof.

Let
m=\lcm_{j\leq n}a_{j}
. We define a new right-hand side
b^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
by

b_{i}^{\prime}=b_{i}+\lceil(m-b_{i})/a_{i}\rceil a_{i}

for each
i\leq n
. It is easy to see that
m\leq b_{i}^{\prime}\leq m+a_{i}\leq b^{\prime}_{\min}+a_{i}
for all
i\leq n
. Also, we have

\displaystyle\min\set{s+\textstyle\sum_{i\leq n}w_{i}x_{i}}{s+a_{i}x_{i}\geq b^{\prime}_{i}\forall i\leq n,s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},x\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}

\displaystyle=\min\set{s+\textstyle\sum_{i\leq n}w_{i}\left\lceil\frac{b^{\prime}_{i}-s}{a_{i}}\right\rceil}{s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}

\displaystyle=\textstyle\sum_{i\leq n}w_{i}\left\lceil\frac{m-b_{i}}{a_{i}}\right\rceil+\min\set{s+\textstyle\sum_{i\leq n}w_{i}\left\lceil\frac{b_{i}-s}{a_{i}}\right\rceil}{s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}

\displaystyle=\textstyle\sum_{i\leq n}w_{i}\left\lceil\frac{m-b_{i}}{a_{i}}\right\rceil+\eqref{large-crowded-ip}

where we applied

\left\lceil\frac{b_{i}^{\prime}-s}{a_{i}}\right\rceil=\left\lceil\frac{b_{i}+\big\lceil\frac{m-b_{i}}{a_{i}}\big\rceil a_{i}-s}{a_{i}}\right\rceil=\left\lceil\frac{m-b_{i}}{a_{i}}\right\rceil+\left\lceil\frac{b_{i}-s}{a_{i}}\right\rceil.

Hence, we can solve the system for
b^{\prime}
with
\cref
mixing-set-with-large-crowded-right-hand-side and subtract
\sum_{i\leq n}w_{i}\lceil(m-b_{i})/a_{i}\rceil
to obtain the optimal value. This gives the desired running time.
∎

Now, we aim to identify the instances of
Mixing Set
which may be solved by using our reversed reduction in combination with efficient algorithms for the computation of worst-case response times. By turning to harmonic periods
p_{i}
and using
\cref
reverse-reduction again, we can apply Nguyen et al.
[
18
]
to compute

\textstyle\min\set{s+\sum_{i<n}c_{i}x_{i}}{s+p_{i}x_{i}\geq\beta\;\forall i<n,\;s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\;x\in\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}}

in time
\Oh(n\log n+n\log\beta)
for any
\beta\geq\max_{i<n}p_{i}
by computing the response times

\min\set{t}{t\geq\beta-k+\textstyle\sum_{i<n}\big\lceil\frac{t}{p_{i}}\big\rceil c_{i},\;t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}

in a binary search for the smallest feasible
k
. It suffices to search in the interval
[0,\beta]
since
(s=\beta,x=0)
is always a solution with value
\beta
. Remark that the usual running time to solve instances of
Mixing Set
with harmonic capacities is
\Oh(n^{2})
. In a more classical formulation this yields the following theorem.

Theorem 19
.

Given harmonic capacities
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{n}
, a right-hand side value
\beta\geq a_{\max}
, and weights
w\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
, one can compute

\textstyle\min\,\{\,s+\sum_{i\leq n}w_{i}x_{i}\;|\;s+a_{i}x_{i}\geq\beta\;\forall i\leq n,\;s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\;x\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\,\}

in time
\Oh(n\log n+n\log\beta)\leq\Oh(n\log(n+\beta))
.

The algorithm of Nguyen et al. runs in linear time
\Oh(n)
after an initial sorting step in time
\Oh(n\log n)
to sort the tasks by their periods. As we seek to apply the algorithm multiple times, we can save the time to sort by only doing it once.

However, for arbitrary capacities and the special case
b_{i}=\beta
for all
i\leq n
and
\beta\geq\lcm_{j\leq n}a_{j}
we can also derive the following corollary from
\cref
mixing-set-with-large-crowded-right-hand-side.

Corollary 20
.

Given capacities
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{n}
, a right-hand side value
\beta\geq\lcm_{i\leq n}a_{i}
, and weights
w\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
, one can compute
(
19
)
in time
\Oh(T_{\mathscr{R}}\log\beta)
where
T_{\mathscr{R}}
is a global upper bound on the time required to compute (
3.1
).

4-Block Integer Programming

Block-structured integer programming has received major interest in the recent past. Namely, the three most important lines of research are
n
-fold integer programming,
2
-stage stochastic integer programming, and
4
-block integer programming
[
35
,
36
,
37
,
38
]
. The last one is the most general but also the least understood flavor of block-structured integer programs. In this section we will consider 4-block integer programs with only one coupling constraint inequality. Furthermore, we consider a simpler objective function which addresses at most two
bricks
. From
\cref
4-block-hardness it follows that this problem is still hard to approximate to any constant factor.
In fact, the literature on
4
-block integer programs is not unified in its formal presentation. Here we consider
4
-block integer programs as follows.

Consider matrices

B_{1},\dots,B_{n}

B_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{r\times s}

for all

and

. We aim to compute

\min\set{w^{\intercal}x}{\mathcal{B}x=b,\;0\leq x\leq u,\;x\in\mathbb{Z}^{s+nt}}

where

\mathcal{B}=\begin{pmatrix}D&C_{1}&\cdots&C_{n}\\
B_{1}&A_{1}\\
\vdots&&\ddots\\
B_{n}&&&A_{n}\end{pmatrix}.

We use the notion of
bricks

to refer to different areas of
w
and

. For each

let

and
x^{(i)}=(x_{(i-1)t+1},\dots,x_{it})
denote the
i

w
and
x
. Furthermore, let
w^{(0)}=(w_{1},\dots,w_{s})
and
x^{(0)}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{s})
denote the
0

w
and

In the following we assume that there is exactly one coupling constraint

and that there is a

such that

for all

. In other words, the objective function addresses exactly two bricks of the solution and at least one of them is the first brick.

denote the first row of
\mathcal{B}
. We aim to compute

\tau=\min\set{w^{\intercal}x}{a^{\intercal}x\geq b_{0},\;\mathcal{B}^{\prime}x=b,\;0\leq x\leq u,\;x\in\mathbb{Z}^{s+nt}}

\mathcal{B}^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}C_{1}&B_{1}\\
\vdots&&\ddots\\
C_{n}&&&B_{n}\end{pmatrix}.

For the decision problem

“

6
Conclusion

To the best of our knowledge the presented connection between RTC and
Mixing Set
was unknown before. In the case of harmonic periods we derived a simple and efficient algorithm to solve RTC in polynomial time even in the presence of task release jitters.

There are several avenues for future work. Although our results generalize over existing work (e.g.
[
17
,
18
]
) there is more space for generalization. For example, the relaxation to arbitrary deadlines (e.g.
[
41
,
42
,
12
,
14
]
), which potentially exceed their task’s period, is an exciting direction.

Various authors of previous results where concerned about the investigation of fixed point iterations for which they proved convergence (e.g.
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
). However, to the best of our knowledge they do not give worst-case bounds for the actual running time, i.e. the number of steps to arrive at the smallest fixed point. Therefore, it remains as an interesting question how our algorithms compete in practice.

Another avenue is to ask for new complexity results. Ekberg
[
4
]
did not consider task release jitters. Therefore, a natural question is: Where are the utilization borders between polynomial and NP-hard schedulability testing in the presence of task release jitters?

Appendix A
Hardness of Approximation of 4-Block IPs

Eisenbrand and Rothvoß
[
16
]
proved that worst-case response times cannot be approximated to a constant factor, unless
\operatorname{P}=\textsc{{NP}}
, even if the task system is jitter-free and has a total utilization truly smaller than
1
. Here we reduce RTC to a 4-block integer linear program. The worst-case response time of a task system without jitters is

r_{n}=\min\set{t}{t\geq c_{n}+\textstyle\sum_{i<n}\big\lceil\frac{t}{p_{i}}\big\rceil c_{i},\;t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}

which may be restated as an integer linear program:

r_{n}=\min\set{t}{t\geq c_{n}+\textstyle\sum_{i<n}x_{i}c_{i},\;p_{i}x_{i}\geq t\;\forall i\in[n-1],\;t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\;x\in\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}}.

Thus, we can introduce a 4-block matrix
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n\times n}
and a right-hand side
b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
leading to

r_{n}=\min\set{t}{\mathcal{A}\begin{pmatrix}t\\
x\end{pmatrix}\geq b,\;t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\;x\in\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}}

where

\mathcal{A}=\begin{pmatrix}\begin{array}[]{c|ccccc}1&-c_{1}&\cdots&-c_{n-1}\\
\hline\cr-1&p_{1}&&\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\\
-1&&&p_{n-1}\end{array}\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad b=\begin{pmatrix}c_{n}\\
\hline\cr 0\\
\vdots\\
0\end{pmatrix}.

As
p_{i}x_{i}\geq t\geq 0
and
p_{i}\geq 1
imply that
x_{i}\geq 0
this leads to the following formulation as a 4-block integer program:

r_{n}=\min\set{x_{1}}{\mathcal{A}x\geq b,\;x\geq 0,\;x\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}

Therefore, it is NP-hard to approximate 4-block ILPs to a constant factor, even if all block matrices have size
1\times 1
, all constraints are inequalities, the objective function addresses the first variable only, the variables are unbounded, and the right-hand side has at most one non-zero entry.

Appendix B
The Bound of Observation
4
is Tight for General Instances

Here we prove that for optimal solutions to general instances of
Mixing Set
the value
\lcm_{i\leq n}a_{i}-1
is indeed the best upper bound on
s
.
Given an arbitrary number
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
we define an instance of
Mixing Set
by
w_{i}=2^{i}
,
a_{i}=n\cdot 2^{i}
, and
b_{i}=n\cdot 2^{n}-1
for all
i\in[n]
. Remark that
\lcm_{i\leq n}a_{i}=a_{n}=n\cdot 2^{n}
and
\sum_{i\leq n}w_{i}/a_{i}=\sum_{i\leq n}1/n=1
. We get the objective function

f(s)=s+\sum_{i\leq n}w_{i}\left\lceil\frac{b_{i}-s}{a_{i}}\right\rceil=s+\sum_{i\leq n}2^{i}\left\lceil\frac{n\cdot 2^{n}-1-s}{n\cdot 2^{i}}\right\rceil.

First consider
0\leq s\leq n\cdot 2^{n}-2
. We have
1\leq n\cdot 2^{n}-1-s\leq n\cdot 2^{n}-1
and at least for
i=n
this yields

\left\lceil\frac{n\cdot 2^{n}-1-s}{n\cdot 2^{i}}\right\rceil=1>\frac{n\cdot 2^{n}-1-s}{n\cdot 2^{i}}

which implies that

f(s)=s+\sum_{i\leq n}2^{i}\left\lceil\frac{n\cdot 2^{n}-1-s}{n\cdot 2^{i}}\right\rceil>s+\sum_{i\leq n}\frac{n\cdot 2^{n}-1-s}{n}=\sum_{i\leq n}\frac{n\cdot 2^{n}-1}{n}=n\cdot 2^{n}-1

and from the integrality of both sides it follows that
f(s)\geq n\cdot 2^{n}
.
However, for
s=n\cdot 2^{n}-1
we have

f(s)=s+\sum_{i\leq n}2^{i}\left\lceil\frac{n\cdot 2^{n}-1-s}{n\cdot 2^{i}}\right\rceil=n\cdot 2^{n}-1<n\cdot 2^{n}

which reveals
\lcm_{i\leq n}a_{i}-1
as the smallest optimal solution.

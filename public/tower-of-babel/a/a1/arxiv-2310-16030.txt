Weak well-posedness of stochastic Volterra equations with completely monotone kernels and non-degenerate noise
Yushi Hamaguchi

Abstract
We establish weak existence and uniqueness in law for stochastic Volterra equations (SVEs for short) with completely monotone kernels and non-degenerate noise under mild regularity assumptions. In particular, our results reveal the regularization-by-noise effect for SVEs with singular kernels, allowing for multiplicative noise with Hölder diffusion coefficients. In order to prove our results, we reformulate the SVE into an equivalent stochastic evolution equation (SEE for short) defined on a Gelfand triplet of Hilbert spaces. We prove weak well-posedness of the SEE using stochastic control arguments, and then translate it into the original SVE.



Keywords:
Stochastic Volterra equation; stochastic evolution equation; uniqueness in law; regularization by noise.

: 60H20; 60H15; 60G22; 60H50.

Introduction

We consider the following stochastic Volterra equation (SVE for short):

X_{t}=x(t)+\int^{t}_{0}K(t-s)b(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{t}_{0}K(t-s)\sigma(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}W_{s},\ \ t>0,

(1.1)

associated with a
d
-dimensional Brownian motion
W
. Here, the drift coefficient
b:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the diffusion coefficient
\sigma:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
, the kernel
K:(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
and the forcing term
x:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
are given. If the kernel
K
and the forcing term
x
are constants, say
K(t)=1
and
x(t)=x_{0}
for some
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then the SVE (
1.1
) becomes a standard stochastic differential equation (SDE for short):

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}X_{t}=b(X_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\sigma(X_{t})\,\mathrm{d}W_{t},\ \ t>0,\\
X_{0}=x_{0}.\end{cases}

(1.2)

More generally, if
K
is the fractional kernel, i.e.
K(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}t^{\alpha-1}
with exponent
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1]
, and if the forcing term
x
is of the form
x(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}t^{a-1}x_{0}
for some
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then the SVE (
1.1
) corresponds to a kind of time-fractional SDE (cf.
[
27
]
). Here and after,
\Gamma(\alpha)
denotes the Gamma function. SVEs provide suitable models of dynamics with hereditary properties, memory effects and roughness of the path (cf.
[
5
,
15
,
18
]
), which cannot be described by standard SDEs. In particular, a rough volatility model in mathematical finance can be modelled by using an SVE of the form (
1.1
) with the fractional kernel
K(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}t^{\alpha-1}
with exponent
\alpha
strictly less than
1
and a square-root type diffusion coefficient
\sigma
(cf.
[
13
,
18
]
). This motivates us to study SVEs with singular kernels and non-Lipschitz coefficients; we say that the kernel
K
is singular if
\lim_{t\downarrow 0}K(t)=\infty
, and regular if
\lim_{t\downarrow 0}K(t)<\infty
. The analysis of SVEs with singular kernels is much more difficult than that of the regular kernels case and the standard SDEs case since the solutions are no longer Markovian or semimartingales in general. For these reasons, we cannot apply fundamental tools established in the literature of Markov or semimartingale theory to SVEs directly. There are still many important open problems on well-posedness in the setting of singular kernels and non-Lipschitz coefficients.

In this paper, we are interested in
weak well-posedness
, that is,
existence of a weak solution
and
uniqueness in law
for SVEs with completely monotone kernels and non-Lipschitz coefficients. By a weak solution of the SVE (
1.1
), we mean a tuple
(X,W,\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{F},\mathbb{P})
consisting of a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{F}=(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
, a
d
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}
-Brownian motion
W
and an
\mathbb{F}
-predictable process
X
which satisfies (
1.1
) in a suitable sense. Uniqueness in law means that the first components
X
of any weak solutions have the same probability law.

In the context of SDEs, general theory of weak well-posedness has been extensively studied. For example, it is well-known that if the drift coefficient
b
is measurable and bounded, and if the diffusion coefficient
\sigma
is continuous, bounded and non-degenerate (in the sense that
\sigma(x)\sigma(x)^{\top}
is uniformly elliptic), then the SDE (
1.2
) is weakly well-posed, and the weak solution is a time-homogeneous Markov process satisfying the (strong) Feller property; see
[
29
, Chapter 7]
. This means that, although the deterministic ordinary differential equation which corresponds to (
1.2
) with
\sigma=0
may have multiple solutions, adding a non-degenerate noise by a Brownian motion makes the equation well-posed in the sense of uniqueness in law. This phenomenon is called the
regularization-by-noise
effect for SDEs in terms of the Brownian noise; see
[
14
]
. From this well-known fact about SDEs, it is natural to ask whether such a regularization-by-noise effect appears for the SVE (
1.1
) as well. This question is quite non-trivial since, unlike the SDEs case, many standard tools in stochastic calculus cannot be applied directly to SVEs due to the lack of Markov and semimartingale properties. Nevertheless, in this paper, we provide a positive answer in this direction. Our main result is
3.1
which can be roughly stated as follows:

Main result

(Rough statement of
3.1
)
.

Assume that the kernel
K
is completely monotone and satisfies
\int^{1}_{0}t^{-1/2}K(t)\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty
. Suppose that the drift coefficient
b
is uniformly continuous and that the diffusion coefficient
\sigma
is non-degenerate and uniformly continuous. Then, under some
“balance condition”
between the singularity of
K
and the modulus of continuity of
\sigma
, the SVE (
1.1
) is weakly well-posed.

The “balance condition” in the above statement means that the more singular the kernel
K
is, the more regular the diffusion coefficient
\sigma
must be, and vice versa. On the one hand, if the diffusion coefficient
\sigma
is Lipschitz continuous, then the “balance condition” in the above statement automatically holds; see
3.4
. On the other hand, if
K
is the fractional kernel
K(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}t^{\alpha-1}
with exponent
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1]
and if
\sigma
is
\gamma
-Hölder continuous with exponent
\gamma\in(\frac{1}{2},1]
, then the “balance condition” becomes
\alpha\gamma>\frac{1}{2}
; see
3.5
. We note that no additional conditions are assumed on the drift coefficient
b
other than the uniform continuity. For example, although the (one-dimensional) deterministic fractional Volterra equation
X_{t}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}|X_{s}|^{\beta}\mathrm{sign}(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s
with
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1]
and
\beta\in(0,1)
has infinitely many solutions, the corresponding SVE with non-degenerate “Volterra noise” is weakly well-posed; see
3.7
. This seems to be the first explicit example of the regularization-by-noise effect for Volterra equations.

Well-posedness of SVEs with (locally) Lipschitz continuous coefficients has been investigated in many papers; see for example
[
6
,
7
]
for the classical results of SVEs with sufficiently regular kernels and
[
4
,
16
,
33
]
for the case of singular kernels. Here, let us briefly review some existing results on existence and/or uniqueness of solutins of SVEs with non-Lipschitz coefficients investigated in the literature so far.

•

A slight extension beyond the Lipschitz case.

Wang
[
31
]
considered SVEs with (non-convolution type) singular kernels and coefficients which slightly beyond the Lipschitz case. This is the first paper for well-posedness of SVEs with non-Lipschitz coefficients. However, the assumptions in
[
31
]
exclude the case where the coefficient
b
or
\sigma
is only Hölder continuous with the exponent strictly less than
1
.

•

Yamada–Watanabe type results for one-dimensional SVEs with the fractional kernel.

Mytnik and Salisbury
[
23
]
considered a one-dimensional (drift-less) SVE with the fractional kernel
K(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}t^{\alpha-1}
with exponent
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1]
and a
\gamma
-Hölder continuous diffusion coefficient
\sigma
with exponent
\gamma\in(\frac{1}{2},1]
. Relying on the special structure of the fractional kernel, they first reformulated the SVE into a stochastic partial differential equation (SPDE for short), and then applied the method of the famous Yamada–Watanabe pathwise uniqueness result for one-dimensional SDEs
[
32
]
to the SPDE. They showed that, under the balance condition
\alpha\gamma>\frac{1}{2}
, pathwise uniqueness holds for the SVE (with the forcing term
x
of a special form). Prömel and Scheffels
[
25
]
generalized this result to the time-inhomogeneous setting, allowing for a non-zero (but Lipschitz continuous) drift coefficient
b
. The pathwise uniqueness issue without the balance condition (i.e. the case of
\alpha\gamma\leq\frac{1}{2}
) is an open problem at the moment.

•

Yamada–Watanabe type results for one-dimensional SVEs with regular kernels.
In the case of sufficiently smooth kernels (which excludes any singular kernels such as the fractional kernel), it has been shown that pathwise uniqueness holds for one-dimensional SVEs with Lipschitz drift coefficients and
\gamma
-Hölder diffusion coefficients with exponent
\gamma\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
; see
[
23
, Section 8]
,
[
3
, Proposition B.3]
and
[
26
]
.

•

Weak existence for SVEs with continuous coefficients.

Abi Jaber et al.
[
2
]
showed general weak existence and stability results for SVEs (with jumps) of convolution type under mild regularity assumptions, allowing for non-Lipschitz coefficients and singular kernels. See
[
24
]
for the study in this direction with time-inhomogeneous coefficients and non-convolution type kernels.

•

Uniqueness in law for SVEs with affine coefficients.
Abi Jaber, Larsson and Pulido
[
4
]
introduced the so-called affine Volterra process which is defined as the solution of the SVE (
1.1
) where both
b(x)
and
a(x):=\sigma(x)\sigma(x)^{\top}
are affine with respect to
x
. By means of the explicit exponential-affine representation formula for the Fourier–Laplace transform, they proved uniqueness in law (in certain state spaces) for some specific examples. A unified theory for SVEs with affine coefficients was introduced by Cuchiero and Teichmann
[
10
,
11
]
in terms of lifts of affine Volterra processes to the so-called infinite dimensional generalized Feller processes. See also
[
1
]
for the study of weak well-posedness of affine SVEs with
L^{1}
kernels and jumps.

Compared with the aforementioned works, this paper is the first step showing uniqueness in law for SVEs under much mild regularity assumptions, beyond the affine case. It is interesting that, although the derivations are completely different, the “balance condition”
\alpha\gamma>\frac{1}{2}
between the exponent
\alpha
of the fractional kernel
K
and the Hölder exponent
\gamma
of the diffusion coefficient
\sigma
appearing in our special case (
3.5
) coincides with that of
[
23
]
where pathwise uniqueness for one-dimensional SVEs was investigated. In view of general theory on weak well-posedness of SDEs (cf.
[
29
]
), one may expect that some of assumptions in our results, especially the “balance condition”, can be somewhat weakened or dropped. However, the “balance condition” turns out to be necessary at least in our strategy for proving weak well-posedness. We will discuss about this condition after completing the proof; see
Section

4.4.2
.

Now let us explain our idea to show weak well-posedness of SVEs. As we mentioned before, the analysis of SVEs with singular kernels is very difficult due to the lack of Markov and semimartingale properties. Then, instead of analysing SVEs directly, we consider the associated
Hilbert-valued Markovian lifts
introduced in our previous work
[
16
]
. Namely, noting that every completely monotone kernel
K
can be written as
K(t)=\int_{[0,\infty)}e^{-\theta t}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)
by a unique Radon measure
\mu
on
[0,\infty)
(cf.
[
28
, Theorem 1.4]
), we reformulate the SVE (
1.1
) into the following equation:

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}Y_{t}(\theta)=-\theta Y_{t}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}t+b(\mu[Y_{t}])\,\mathrm{d}t+\sigma(\mu[Y_{t}])\,\mathrm{d}W_{t},\ \ \theta\in{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu},\ t>0,\\
Y_{0}(\theta)=y(\theta),\ \ \theta\in{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}.\end{cases}

(1.3)

Here,
{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\subset[0,\infty)
is the support of the measure
\mu
, and
\mu[y]:=\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}y(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)
for suitable functions
y:{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The above equation can be seen as a stochastic evolution equation (SEE for short) on a Gelfand triplet
\mathcal{V}_{\mu}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\mu}
of Hilbert spaces consisting of functions
y:{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
; see
[
16
, Section 2]
and
Section

2
below. Roughly speaking, there is an equivalence between the original SVE (
1.1
) and the lifted SEE (
1.3
), and we can apply well-known tools of stochastic calculus in Hilbert spaces to the latter. Our idea is to show weak well-posedness of the lifted SEE (
1.3
), and then to translate it into the original SVE (
1.1
).

Note that, however, there are at least three difficulties for proving weak well-posedness of the lifted SEE (
1.3
). First, the lifted SEE is
highly degenerate
in the sense that the state space
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
is (typically) infinite dimensional, but the Brownian noise is finite dimensional; even if the diffusion coefficient
\sigma
is non-degenerate in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, it would be degenerate in the infinite dimensional state space
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
. This indicates that the regularization-by-noise effect for the lifted SEE is highly non-trivial. Second, in the case of singular kernels, the integral map
y\mapsto\mu[y]
is continuous only in the smaller space
\mathcal{V}_{\mu}
(with stronger topology) and not continuous in the state space
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
, hence the coefficients
y\mapsto b(\mu[y])
or
y\mapsto\sigma(\mu[y])
are not continuous in
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
even if the maps
x\mapsto b(x)
and
x\mapsto\sigma(x)
are continuous in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Third, at this moment, there are no useful criteria of compactness in the space of paths on the Gelfand triplet
\mathcal{V}_{\mu}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{V}^{*}_{\mu}
. For these reasons, it is not realistic to use the analytic approach relying on the results of the associated Kolmogorov equations which has been commonly adopted in the study of finite dimensional SDEs (cf.
[
29
]
). Instead, inspired by the probabilistic approach of Kulik and Scheutzow
[
21
]
for the study of SDEs with delay, we demonstrate the so-called
Control-and-Reimburse (C-n-R)
strategy to prove weak well-posedness of the lifted SEE (
1.3
). The idea of the C-n-R strategy is “
to apply a stochastic control in order to improve the system, and then to take into account the impact of the control
”
[
21
]
. We will explain more detailed ideas concerning the C-n-R strategy in the introductory part of
Section

4
. Using such stochastic control arguments, we overcome the difficulties caused by the non-degeneracy and low regularity of the coefficients of the lifted SEE. Furthermore, besides the weak well-posedness, we show that the weak solution of the lifted SEE becomes a Markov process on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
and satisfies the Feller property. This result indicates that the solution
Y
of the SEE (
1.3
) is a “Markovian lift” of the solution
X
of the original SVE (
1.1
). The same general ideas in the spirit of the C-n-R strategy were also used in the studies of ergodicity of various stochastic systems; see
[
8
,
20
]
and references cited therein. It is worth to remark that a related idea was used to establish the asymptotic log-Harnack inequality for the lifted SEE in our previous work
[
16
]
.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows: In
Section

2
, we briefly review the framework of Hilbert-valued Markovian lifts of SVEs introduced in
[
16
]
and then describe the associated solution concepts. In
Section

3
, we state our main result (
3.1
) and show some applications.
Section

4
is devoted to the proof of
3.1
. In
Appendix
, we show an auxiliary result needed in the proof of the main result.

2
Solution concepts for SVEs and SEEs

First, we describe an assumption on the kernel
K
appearing in the SVE (
1.1
). Recall that a kernel
K:(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is said to be completely monotone if
K
is infinitely differentiable and satisfies
(-1)^{k}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{k}}{\mathrm{d}t^{k}}K(t)\geq 0
for any
t\in(0,\infty)
and any nonnegative integers
k
. By Bernstein’s theorem (cf.
[
28
, Theorem 1.4]
), there exists a unique Radon measure
\mu
on
[0,\infty)
such that

K(t)=\int_{[0,\infty)}e^{-\theta t}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)=\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}e^{-\theta t}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta),\ \ t>0,

where
{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\subset[0,\infty)
denotes the support of
\mu
. We say that a completely monotone kernel
K
is
regular
if
\lim_{t\downarrow 0}K(t)<\infty
, and
singular
if
\lim_{t\downarrow 0}K(t)=\infty
. It is easy to see that
K
is regular if and only if
\mu([0,\infty))<\infty
. Concerning the singularity, for each fixed
\alpha\in(0,1]
,
\int^{1}_{0}t^{\alpha-1}K(t)\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty
if and only if
\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}1\wedge(\theta^{-\alpha})\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)<\infty
, and these equivalent conditions imply
\int^{1}_{0}K(t)^{1/\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty
; see
[
16
, Lemma 2.1]
. Namely, the regularity/singularity of the kernel
K
(at
t=0
) is characterized by the lightness/heaviness of the tail of the measure
\mu
.

Assumption 1
.

K:(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is a completely monotone kernel with the corresponding Radon measure
\mu
on
[0,\infty)
. Furthermore, there exists a non-increasing Borel measurable function
r
on
[0,\infty)
satisfying
1\wedge(\theta^{-1/2})\leq r(\theta)\leq 1
for any
\theta\in[0,\infty)
such that

\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)<\infty.

For a completely monotone kernel
K
, the above assumption is equivalent to
\int^{1}_{0}t^{-1/2}K(t)\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty
. The function
r
characterizes the regularity/singularity of the kernel
K
. In this paper, if the kernel
K
is regular, we will take
r\equiv 1
. The following are examples of kernels which satisfy
1
:

•

A finite combination of exponential kernel:
K(t)=\sum^{k}_{i=1}c_{i}e^{-\theta_{i}t}
with
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
c_{i}>0
and
\theta_{i}\in[0,\infty)
for
i=1,\dots,k
. This kernel is regular, and the corresponding Radon measure is
\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)=\sum^{k}_{i=1}c_{i}\delta_{\theta_{i}}(\mathrm{d}\theta)
with
{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}=\{\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{k}\}
. Here,
\delta_{\theta_{i}}
denotes the Dirac measure at point
\theta_{i}
.

•

The fractional kernel:
K(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}t^{\alpha-1}
with exponent
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
. This kernel is singular. The corresponding Radon measure is as follows:

\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\theta^{-\alpha}\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{(0,\infty)}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta,\ \ {\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}=[0,\infty).

We can take
r(\theta)=1\wedge(\theta^{-\eta})
for any
\eta\in(1-\alpha,\frac{1}{2}]
.

•

The Gamma kernel:
K(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}e^{-\beta t}t^{\alpha-1}
with exponents
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
and
\beta>0
. This kernel is singular. The corresponding Radon measure is as follows:

\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(1-\alpha)}(\theta-\beta)^{-\alpha}\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{(\beta,\infty)}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta,\ \ {\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}=[\beta,\infty).

we can take
r(\theta)=1\wedge(\theta^{-\eta})
for any
\eta\in(1-\alpha,\frac{1}{2}]
.

Definition 2.1
.

•

, we have, for
1\leq m\leq M<\infty
,

1\wedge(\theta^{-1/2})\leq r(\theta)=r_{1}(\theta)\leq r_{m}(\theta)\leq r_{M}(\theta)\leq 1

for any
\theta\in[0,\infty)
, and

R_{m}\leq R_{M}\leq\frac{1}{r(M)}\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)<\infty,\ \varepsilon_{M}\leq\varepsilon_{m}\leq r(m)\int_{{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\cap(m,\infty)}r(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta).

In particular, by the dominated convergence theorem, we have

\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\varepsilon_{m}}{r(m)}=0\ \text{and}\ \lim_{m\to\infty}R_{m}\varepsilon_{m}=0.

(3.3)

On the one hand, if the kernel
K
is regular (or equivalently if
\mu([0,\infty))<\infty
), then we take
r\equiv 1
, and we see that
R_{m}=\mu([0,\infty))<\infty
and
\varepsilon_{m}=0
for any
m\in[1,\infty)
. On the other hand, if the kernel
K
is singular (or equivalently if
\mu([0,\infty))=\infty
), then
\lim_{m\to\infty}R_{m}=\infty
and
\varepsilon_{m}>0
for any
m\in[1,\infty)
.

Next, for each uniformly continuous map
\varphi
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
which takes values in either
\mathbb{R}^{n}
or
\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
, we define

\rho_{\varphi}(t):=\inf_{(a,b)\in\mathcal{A}_{\varphi}}(at+b),\ t\geq 0,

where

\mathcal{A}_{\varphi}:=\left\{(a,b)\in[0,\infty)\times[0,\infty)\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}|\varphi(x)-\varphi(\bar{x})|^{2}\leq a|x-\bar{x}|^{2}+b\ \text{for any $x,\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$}\right\}.

By the uniform continuity of
\varphi
, it can be easily shown that, for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
a_{\varepsilon}>0
such that
(a_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon)\in\mathcal{A}_{\varphi}
. This implies that
\rho_{\varphi}(0)=0
. Also, we see that
\rho_{\varphi}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is non-decreasing, concave and satisfies

|\varphi(x)-\varphi(\bar{x})|^{2}\leq\rho_{\varphi}(|x-\bar{x}|^{2})

for any
x,\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The function
\rho_{\varphi}
can be seen as a modulus of continuity of
\varphi
, which is the minimal choice among all functions satisfying the same properties. That is, if
\rho:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is a non-decreasing concave function with
\rho(0)=0
such that
|\varphi(x)-\varphi(\bar{x})|^{2}\leq\rho(|x-\bar{x}|^{2})
for any
x,\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then it holds that
\rho_{\varphi}(t)\leq\rho(t)
for any
t\geq 0
. The concavity and
\rho_{\varphi}(0)=0
imply that
\rho_{\varphi}
is sub-additive in the sense that
\rho_{\varphi}(s+t)\leq\rho_{\varphi}(s)+\rho_{\varphi}(t)
for any
s,t\geq 0
, which also implies that
\rho_{\varphi}(st)\leq(1+s)\rho_{\varphi}(t)
for any
s,t\geq 0
.

Let
(E,d)
be a Polish space. We denote the space of probability measures on
E
(endowed with the Borel
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{B}(E)
) by
\mathcal{P}(E)
. The
Lévy–Prokhorov metric
between two probability measures
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\in\mathcal{P}(E)
is defined by

d_{\mathrm{LP}}(\nu_{1},\nu_{2}):=\inf\left\{\varepsilon>0\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\nu_{1}(A)\leq\nu_{2}(A^{\varepsilon})+\varepsilon\ \text{and}\ \nu_{2}(A)\leq\nu_{1}(A^{\varepsilon})+\varepsilon\ \text{for all}\ A\in\mathcal{B}(E)\right\},

where
A^{\varepsilon}:=\left\{x\in E\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\text{there exists $y\in A$ such that}\ d(x,y)<\varepsilon\right\}
. It is well-known that
(\mathcal{P}(E),d_{\mathrm{LP}})
is a Polish space and that the convergence with respect to
d_{\mathrm{LP}}
is equivalent to the weak convergence of probability measures.

Now we are ready to state our main result.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that
1
holds and that
b:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\sigma:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
are uniformly continuous. Assume that
\sigma
satisfies the uniform ellipticity condition; there exists a constant
c_{\mathrm{UE}}>0
such that
\sigma(x)\sigma(x)^{\top}\geq c_{\mathrm{UE}}I_{n\times n}
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Furthermore, assume either the following (i) or (ii) holds:

(i)

The kernel
K
is singular, and it holds that
\displaystyle\liminf_{m\to\infty}R_{m}\frac{\rho_{\sigma}(\varepsilon^{2}_{m})}{\varepsilon_{m}}=0
.

(ii)

The kernel
K
is regular, and it holds that
\displaystyle\liminf_{\delta\downarrow 0}\frac{\rho_{\sigma}(\delta^{2})}{\delta}=0
.

Then, for the SEE (
1.3
):

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}Y_{t}(\theta)=-\theta Y_{t}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}t+b(\mu[Y_{t}])\,\mathrm{d}t+\sigma(\mu[Y_{t}])\,\mathrm{d}W_{t},\ \ \theta\in{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu},\ t>0,\\
Y_{0}(\theta)=y(\theta),\ \ \theta\in{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu},\end{cases}

the following hold:

•

Weak existence and uniqueness in law hold for any initial condition
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
.

•

The map
y\mapsto\mathrm{Law}_{\mathbb{P}^{y}}(Y^{y})
from
(\mathcal{H}_{\mu},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}})
to
(\mathcal{P}(\Lambda),d_{\mathrm{LP}})
is continuous, where
(Y^{y},W^{y},\Omega^{y},\mathcal{F}^{y},\mathbb{F}^{y},\mathbb{P}^{y})
is a weak solution with the initial condition
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
.

•

For any weak solution
(Y,W,\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{F},\mathbb{P})
, the process
Y
is a time-homogeneous Markov process on
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
, which satisfies the Feller property.

In particular, for the SVE (
1.1
):

X_{t}=x(t)+\int^{t}_{0}K(t-s)b(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{t}_{0}K(t-s)\sigma(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}W_{s},\ \ t>0,

the following hold:

•

Weak existence and uniqueness in law hold for any forcing term
x
of the form
x=\mathcal{K}y
for some
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
.

•

The map
y\mapsto\mathrm{Law}_{\mathbb{P}^{y}}(X^{y})
from
(\mathcal{H}_{\mu},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}})
to
(\mathcal{P}(L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{R}^{n})),d_{\mathrm{LP}})
is continuous, where, for each
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
,
(X^{y},W^{y},\Omega^{y},\mathcal{F}^{y},\mathbb{F}^{y},\mathbb{P}^{y})
denotes a weak solution of the SVE with the forcing term
x=\mathcal{K}y
.

Remark 3.2
.

The pair of conditions (i) and (ii) in the above theorem stands for the “balance condition” between the singularity of the kernel
K
and the modulus of continuity of the diffusion coefficient
\sigma
. This means that the more singular the kernel
K
is, the more regular the diffusion coefficient
\sigma
must be, and vice versa; see
3.3
below. In particular,
\sigma
is at least
1/2
-Hölder continuous in the sense that
\displaystyle\liminf_{\delta\downarrow 0}\frac{\rho_{\sigma}(\delta^{2})}{\delta}=0
. In contrast, no additional conditions are assumed on the drift coefficient
b
other than the uniform continuity.

As a special case of
3.1
, we shall consider the case of Hölder continuous diffusion coefficients. We say that a map
\sigma:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
satisfies the
finite range Hölder condition
with exponent
\gamma\in(0,1]
if there exists a constant
c_{\mathrm{Hol}}\in(0,\infty)
such that

|\sigma(x)-\sigma(\bar{x})|\leq c_{\mathrm{Hol}}|x-\bar{x}|^{\gamma}

for any
x,\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying
|x-\bar{x}|\leq 1
. This condition implies that

|\sigma(x)-\sigma(\bar{x})|\leq c_{\mathrm{Hol}}\big(|x-\bar{x}|^{\gamma}+|x-\bar{x}|\big)

for any
x,\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying
|x-\bar{x}|>1
. Indeed, taking
p\in\mathbb{N}
such that
|x-\bar{x}|\in(p,p+1]
, we have

\displaystyle|\sigma(x)-\sigma(\bar{x})|

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{p+1}_{q=1}\Big|\sigma\Big(\bar{x}+\frac{q}{p+1}(x-\bar{x})\Big)-\sigma\Big(\bar{x}+\frac{q-1}{p+1}(x-\bar{x})\Big)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq c_{\mathrm{Hol}}(p+1)\Big(\frac{|x-\bar{x}|}{p+1}\Big)^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq c_{\mathrm{Hol}}\big(|x-\bar{x}|^{\gamma}+|x-\bar{x}|\big).

Therefore,
|\sigma(x)-\sigma(\bar{x})|^{2}\leq\rho\big(|x-\bar{x}|^{2}\big)
for any
x,\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
\rho(t):=2c^{2}_{\mathrm{Hol}}\big(t^{\gamma}+t\big)
,
t\geq 0
, which is a concave function on
[0,\infty)
. This implies that
\rho_{\sigma}\leq\rho
, and hence
\limsup_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{\rho_{\sigma}(t)}{t^{\gamma}}<\infty
.

Corollary 3.3
.

Suppose that the kernel
K
is completely monotone and satisfies
\int^{1}_{0}t^{\eta-1}K(t)\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty
for some
\eta\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
. Let
b:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a uniformly continuous map. Suppose that
\sigma:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
satisfies the uniform ellipticity condition and the finite range Hölder condition with exponent
\gamma=\frac{1}{2(1-\eta)}\in(\frac{1}{2},1]
. Then, for any free term
x
of the form
x(t)=K(t)x_{0}
for any
t>0
for some
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, weak existence and uniqueness in law hold for the SVE (
1.1
).

Proof.

We check the conditions (i) and (ii) in
3.1
. If the kernel
K
is regular, then we have
\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0}\frac{\rho_{\sigma}(\delta^{2})}{\delta}=0
since
\limsup_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{\rho_{\sigma}(t)}{t^{\gamma}}<\infty
and
\gamma>\frac{1}{2}
, and thus the condition (ii) in
3.1
holds. Assume that
K
is singular. By the assumption and
[
16
, Lemma 2.1]
, we can and will take
r(\theta)=1\wedge(\theta^{-\eta})
as the function
r
appearing in
1
. Let
m\in[1,\infty)
be fixed. Observe that
\theta^{-1}r_{m}(\theta)^{-1}=m^{-\eta}\theta^{\eta-1}\leq r(m)^{\frac{1}{\eta}-1}r(\theta)
for any
\theta\in(m,\infty)
. Thus, we have

\varepsilon_{m}=\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}(1-r_{m}(\theta))^{2}\theta^{-1}r_{m}(\theta)^{-1}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\leq r(m)^{\frac{1}{\eta}-1}\int_{{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\cap(m,\infty)}r(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)

and

\displaystyle R_{m}\varepsilon^{2\gamma-1}_{m}

\displaystyle\leq r(m)^{(\frac{1}{\eta}-1)(2\gamma-1)-1}\Big(\int_{{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\cap(m,\infty)}r(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\Big)^{2\gamma-1}\Big(\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\Big)

\displaystyle=\Big(\int_{{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\cap(m,\infty)}r(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\Big)^{2\gamma-1}\Big(\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\Big).

Noting that
\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)<\infty
, by the dominated convergence theorem, we get
\lim_{m\to\infty}R_{m}\varepsilon^{2\gamma-1}_{m}=0
. Since
\limsup_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{\rho_{\sigma}(t)}{t^{\gamma}}<\infty
, the condition (i) in
3.1
holds.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Suppose that the kernel
K
is completely monotone and satisfies
\int^{1}_{0}t^{-1/2}K(t)\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty
. Let
b:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a uniformly continuous map. Assume that
\sigma:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
is globally Lipschitz continuous and satisfies the uniform ellipticity condition. Then, for any free term
x
of the form
x(t)=K(t)x_{0}
for any
t>0
for some
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, weak existence and uniqueness in law hold for the SVE (
1.1
).

Corollary 3.5
.

Consider the fractional kernel
K(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}t^{\alpha-1}
with exponent
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1]
. Let
b:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a uniformly continuous map. Assume that
\sigma:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
satisfies the uniform ellipticity condition and the finite range Hölder condition with exponent
\gamma\in(\frac{1}{2},1]
. Furthermore, assume that the
balance condition

\alpha\gamma>\frac{1}{2}
holds. Then, for any free term
x
of the form
x(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}t^{\alpha-1}x_{0}
for any

for some

).

Remark 3.6
.

Consider the fractional kernel
K(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}t^{\alpha-1}
with exponent
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
. Note that the corresponding Radon measure is given by
\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\theta^{-\alpha}\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{(0,\infty)}\,\mathrm{d}\theta
. As the function
r
in
1
we can take
r(\theta)=1\wedge(\theta^{-\eta})
for any
\eta\in(1-\alpha,\frac{1}{2}]
. In this case, for each
m\in[1,\infty)
, the numbers
R_{m}
and
\varepsilon_{m}
can be calculated explicitly as follows:

\displaystyle R_{m}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r_{m}(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int^{m}_{0}\theta^{-\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}\theta+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(1-\alpha)}m^{\eta}\int^{\infty}_{m}\theta^{-\eta-\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\cdot\frac{\eta}{(1-\alpha)(\alpha+\eta-1)}m^{1-\alpha}

and

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{m}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}(1-r_{m}(\theta))^{2}\theta^{-1}r_{m}(\theta)^{-1}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(1-\alpha)}m^{-\eta}\int^{\infty}_{m}(1-m^{\eta}\theta^{-\eta})^{2}\theta^{-1+\eta-\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\cdot\frac{2\eta^{2}}{\alpha(\alpha-\eta)(\alpha+\eta)}m^{-\alpha}.

Example 3.7
.

Consider the following (one-dimensional) deterministic Volterra equation with the fractional kernel:

X_{t}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}|X_{s}|^{\beta}\mathrm{sign}(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s,\ t>0,

(3.4)

with
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
\beta\in(0,1)
. Obviously, the function
X_{t}=0
,
t>0
, is a solution of the above Volterra equation. Besides, for any
t_{0}\geq 0
, the functions

X_{t}=\pm C_{\alpha,\beta}(t-t_{0})^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\beta}}\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{(t_{0},\infty)}(t),\ t>0,

with the constant
C_{\alpha,\beta}=\Big(\beta\frac{\Gamma(\frac{\alpha\beta}{1-\beta})}{\Gamma(\frac{\alpha}{1-\beta})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{1-\beta}}
, are also solutions. Indeed, for
t>t_{0}
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}|X_{s}|^{\beta}\mathrm{sign}(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\pm\frac{C^{\beta}_{\alpha,\beta}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int^{t}_{t_{0}}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}(s-t_{0})^{\frac{\alpha\beta}{1-\beta}}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\pm\frac{C^{\beta}_{\alpha,\beta}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int^{t-t_{0}}_{0}(t-t_{0}-s)^{\alpha-1}s^{\frac{\alpha\beta}{1-\beta}}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\pm\frac{C^{\beta}_{\alpha,\beta}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}B\Big(\alpha,\frac{\alpha\beta}{1-\beta}+1\Big)(t-t_{0})^{\alpha+\frac{\alpha\beta}{1-\beta}}

\displaystyle=\pm C_{\alpha,\beta}(t-t_{0})^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\beta}}

\displaystyle=X_{t},

where
B(x,y)=\int^{1}_{0}(1-s)^{x-1}s^{y-1}\,\mathrm{d}s
,
x,y>0
, denotes the Beta function; we used the relations
B(x,y)=\frac{\Gamma(x)\Gamma(y)}{\Gamma(x+y)}
and
\Gamma(x+1)=x\Gamma(x)
for
x,y>0
. Thus, the deterministic Volterra equation (
3.4
) has infinitely many solutions. In contrast, if
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1]
,
3.5
shows that weak existence and uniqueness in law hold for the corresponding SVE

X_{t}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}|X_{s}|^{\beta}\mathrm{sign}(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\sigma(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}W_{s},\ \ t>0,

whenever the diffusion coefficient
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is non-degenerate and satisfies the finite range Hölder condition with exponent
\gamma\in(\frac{1}{2\alpha},1]
. This reveals the regularization-by-noise effect for SVEs, allowing for multiplicative noise with Hölder coefficients.

Remark 3.8
.

Abi Jaber et al.
[
2
]
proved weak existence of SVEs (with jumps) under some mild assumptions. In their main result
[
2
, Theorem 1.2]
, they assumed that the kernel
K
satisfies

\int^{T}_{0}\frac{K(t)^{p}}{t^{\eta p}}\,\mathrm{d}t+\int^{T}_{0}\int^{T}_{0}\frac{|K(t)-K(s)|^{p}}{|t-s|^{1+\eta p}}\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty

(3.5)

for any
T>0
for some
p\in[2,\infty)
and
\eta\in(0,1)
. This assumption was used to show a priori estimates on Sobolev–Slobodeckij norms of weak solutions of SVEs, which was crucial for the tightness argument; see
[
2
, Theorem 1.4 and Corollary 1.5]
. Observe that if
\int^{1}_{0}\frac{K(t)^{p}}{t^{\eta p}}\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty
, by the Hölder inequality one has

\int^{1}_{0}t^{\alpha-1}K(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\Big(\int^{1}_{0}t^{\frac{p}{p-1}(\alpha+\eta-1)}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\Big(\int^{1}_{0}\frac{K(t)^{p}}{t^{\eta p}}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty

for any
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{p}-\eta,\frac{1}{2})
. If the kernel
K
is completely monotone with the corresponding Radon measure
\mu
, then
\int^{1}_{0}t^{\alpha-1}K(t)\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty
is equivalent to
\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}1\wedge(\theta^{-\alpha})\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)<\infty
; see
[
16
, Lemma 2.1]
. Thus, we cannot apply
[
2
, Theorem 1.2]
to show weak existence for an SVE under
1
. Indeed, for example, the completely monotone kernel
K(t)=\int^{\infty}_{2}\frac{e^{-\theta t}}{\theta^{1/2}(\log\theta)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\theta
with the corresponding Radon measure
\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)=\frac{1}{\theta^{1/2}(\log\theta)^{2}}\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{[2,\infty)}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta
satisfies
1
with
r(\theta)=1\wedge(\theta^{-1/2})
:

\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)=\int^{\infty}_{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta}{\theta(\log\theta)^{2}}=\frac{1}{\log 2}<\infty,

but does not satisfy (
3.5
) since, for any
\alpha<\frac{1}{2}
,

\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}1\wedge(\theta^{-\alpha})\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)=\int^{\infty}_{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta}{\theta^{\frac{1}{2}+\alpha}(\log\theta)^{2}}=\infty.

4
Proof of the main theorem

In this section, we prove
3.1
. Our aim is to construct a sequence
\{Y^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of mild solutions of some (strongly) well-posed SEEs and show that:

•

the sequence of the laws of
\{Y^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathcal{P}(\Lambda)
with respect to the weak convergence topology, which implies weak existence of the original SEE (
1.3
), and that

•

for an arbitrarily given weak solution
(Y,W,\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{F},\mathbb{P})
of the original SEE (
1.3
), the law of
Y
is specified as the weak limit of the laws of
\{Y^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, which implies uniqueness in law.

We will construct such a sequence in
Section

4.3
. In order to show the above assertions, we need to estimate the Lévy–Prokhorov metric between
\mathrm{Law}_{\mathbb{P}}(Y)
and
\mathrm{Law}_{\mathbb{P}}(\bar{Y})
, where
(Y,W,\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{F},\mathbb{P})
is a given weak solution of an SEE, and
\bar{Y}
is a mild solution of a (strongly) well-posed SEE approximating the original one. In the Lipschitz coefficients case, we can estimate the difference
Y-\bar{Y}
in the strong sense by a standard method; see
[
16
, Theorem 2.17]
. However, this kind of estimate is very difficult in the general non-Lipschitz (and even non-Hölder) setting, and we need to compare their probability laws in a more sophisticated way. Inspired by the work
[
21
]
about SDEs with delay, we will perform the so-called
Control-and-Reimburse (C-n-R)
strategy. Namely, instead of estimating
d_{\mathrm{LP}}(\mathrm{Law}_{\mathbb{P}}(Y),\mathrm{Law}_{\mathbb{P}}(\bar{Y}))
directly, we divide the estimate into two steps:

d_{\mathrm{LP}}\big(\mathrm{Law}_{\mathbb{P}}(Y),\mathrm{Law}_{\mathbb{P}}(\bar{Y})\big)\leq\underbrace{d_{\mathrm{LP}}\big(\mathrm{Law}_{\mathbb{P}}(Y),\mathrm{Law}_{\mathbb{P}}(\hat{Y})\big)}_{\text{control}}+\underbrace{d_{\mathrm{LP}}\big(\mathrm{Law}_{\mathbb{P}}(\hat{Y}),\mathrm{Law}_{\mathbb{P}}(\bar{Y})\big)}_{\text{reimbursement}},

(4.1)

where
\hat{Y}
is a “controlled version” of
\bar{Y}
. The first term of the right-hand side of (
4.1
) is about the “control-step” where we aim to improve
\bar{Y}
by an appropriately controlled version
\hat{Y}
so that the difference
Y-\hat{Y}
is easier to estimate in the strong sense; we will estimate its modified (but equivalent) norm in terms of the topology of the convergence in probability on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
; see (
4.7
) below. The second term of the right-hand side of (
4.1
) is about the “reimbursement-step” where we have to reimburse the impact of the control; we will estimate the total variation distance between the law of the controlled version
\hat{Y}
and the law of
\bar{Y}
; see (
4.8
) below.

The rest of this section is organized as follows: In
Section

4.1
, we summarize some preliminary results. In
Section

4.2
, we provide key estimates for the C-n-R strategy. Then, in
Section

4.3
, we prove
3.1
. After that, in
Section

4.4
, we make some remarks on the proof of our main theorem.

4.1
Preliminaries

Suppose that
1
holds. Recall the definitions (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) of
r_{m}
,
R_{m}
and
\varepsilon_{m}
. For each
m\in[1,\infty)
, define

\mu_{m}[y]:=\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r_{m}(\theta)y(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta),\ \|y\|_{m}:=\Big(\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r_{m}(\theta)|y(\theta)|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\Big)^{1/2},\ \ y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}.

For
m=\infty
, we set
r_{\infty}(\theta):=1
for any

and
\mu_{\infty}[\cdot]:=\mu[\cdot]
; see (

).

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose that
1
holds.

(i)

Let

. Then, it holds that

\|y\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}\leq\|y\|_{m}\leq r(m)^{-1/2}\|y\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}

for any
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
. In particular, the norm
\|\cdot\|_{m}
is equivalent to the original norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}
in
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
.

(ii)

Let
m\in[1,\infty)
. Then, it holds that

\big|\mu_{m}[y]\big|^{2}\leq R_{m}\|y\|^{2}_{m}

for any
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
. In particular, the map
\mu_{m}[\cdot]
is a bounded linear operator from
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

(iii)

Let
m\in[1,\infty)
and
M\in[m,\infty]
. Then, it holds that

\big|\mu_{M}[y]-\mu_{m}[y]\big|^{2}\leq\varepsilon_{m}\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\theta r_{m}(\theta)|y(\theta)|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{m}}{r(m)}\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\theta r(\theta)|y(\theta)|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)

for any
y\in\mathcal{V}_{\mu}
. In particular,
\lim_{m\to\infty}\mu_{m}[\cdot]|_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}=\mu[\cdot]|_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}
with respect to the operator norm of bounded linear operators from
\mathcal{V}_{\mu}
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

The assertion (i) follows from the estimates
r(\theta)\leq r_{m}(\theta)\leq\frac{r(\theta)}{r(m)}
. Noting the definitions of
R_{m}
,
\mu_{m}[\cdot]
and
\|\cdot\|_{m}
, by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

\big|\mu_{m}[y]\big|^{2}=\Big|\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r_{m}(\theta)y(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\Big|^{2}\leq\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r_{m}(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\,\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r_{m}(\theta)|y(\theta)|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)=R_{m}\|y\|^{2}_{m}

for any
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
. Thus, the assertion (ii) holds. Concerning the assertion (iii), note that
0\leq r_{m}(\theta)\leq r_{M}(\theta)\leq 1
. Then, by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we have

\displaystyle\big|\mu_{M}[y]-\mu_{m}[y]\big|^{2}

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}(r_{M}(\theta)-r_{m}(\theta))y(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}(1-r_{m}(\theta))^{2}\theta^{-1}r_{m}(\theta)^{-1}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\,\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\theta r_{m}(\theta)|y(\theta)|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{m}\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\theta r_{m}(\theta)|y(\theta)|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)

for any
y\in\mathcal{V}_{\mu}
. Thus, the first inequality holds. The second inequality follows from the inequality
r_{m}(\theta)\leq\frac{r(\theta)}{r(m)}
. By (
3.3
), we see that
\mu_{m}[\cdot]|_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}
converges to
\mu_{\infty}[\cdot]|_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}=\mu[\cdot]|_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}
as
m\to\infty
in the operator norm of bounded linear operators from
\mathcal{V}_{\mu}
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose that
1
holds. Assume that
b
and
\sigma
satisfy the linear growth condition and the local Lipschitz condition; for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a constant
L_{k}>0
such that, for
\varphi=b,\sigma
,

|\varphi(x)-\varphi(\bar{x})|\leq L_{k}|x-\bar{x}|

for any
x,\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying
|x|\vee|\bar{x}|\leq k
. Let
M\in[1,\infty)
, that is,
M
is assumed to be finite. Then, for any initial condition
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
, weak existence and pathwise uniqueness hold for the SEE (
4.2
). In particular, uniqueness in law and strong existence hold.

Under the above assumptions, let a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{F},\mathbb{P})
and a
d
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}
-Brownian motion
W
be given. For each
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
, denote by
Y^{y}
the mild solution of the SEE (
4.2
) with the initial condition
y
. Then, the following hold:

•

The map
y\mapsto Y^{y}
is continuous in probability as a map from
(\mathcal{H}_{\mu},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}})
to the space of
\Lambda
-valued random variables on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
. In particular, the map
y\mapsto\mathrm{Law}_{\mathbb{P}}(Y^{y})
from
(\mathcal{H}_{\mu},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}})
to
(\mathcal{P}(\Lambda),d_{\mathrm{LP}})
is continuous.

•

The process
Y^{y}
is a time-homogeneous Markov process on
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
, which has the Feller property.

Proof.

Since
M<\infty
, by
4.1
(ii), the map
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\ni y\mapsto\mu_{M}[y]\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded linear operator. Thus, by the assumption, the maps
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\ni y\mapsto b(\mu_{M}[y])\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\ni y\mapsto\sigma(\mu_{M}[y])\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
are locally Lipschitz continuous. Thus, by
[
16
, Theorem 2.18]
, we see that weak existence and pathwise uniqueness hold for the SEE (
4.2
). In particular, by the Yamada–Watanabe theorem (see
[
22
]
for a general version of the Yamada–Watanabe theorem), uniqueness in law and strong existence hold.

Let a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{F},\mathbb{P})
and a
d
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}
-Brownian motion
W
be fixed. For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
, let
Y=Y^{k,y}
be the mild solution of the SEE

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}Y_{t}(\theta)=-\theta Y_{t}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}t+b_{k}(\mu_{M}[Y_{t}])\,\mathrm{d}t+\sigma_{k}(\mu_{M}[Y_{t}])\,\mathrm{d}W_{t},\ \ \theta\in{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu},\ t>0,\\
Y_{0}(\theta)=y(\theta),\ \ \theta\in{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu},\end{cases}

(4.4)

where
b_{k}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\sigma_{k}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
are defined by

b_{k}(x):=\begin{cases}b(x)\ &\text{if $|x|\leq k$},\\
b\Big(\frac{k}{|x|}x\Big)\ &\text{if $|x|>k$}\end{cases}\ \text{and}\ \sigma_{k}(x):=\begin{cases}\sigma(x)\ &\text{if $|x|\leq k$},\\
\sigma\Big(\frac{k}{|x|}x\Big)\ &\text{if $|x|>k$}.\end{cases}

Note that the maps
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\ni y\mapsto b_{k}(\mu_{M}[y])\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\ni y\mapsto\sigma_{k}(\mu_{M}[y])\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
are globally Lipschitz continuous. Thus, by
[
16
, Theorem 2.17]
, there exists a constant
C_{k}>0
such that, for any
T>0
and any
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
,

\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Y^{k,y_{1}}_{t}-Y^{k,y_{2}}_{t}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}+\int^{T}_{0}\|Y^{k,y_{1}}_{t}-Y^{k,y_{2}}_{t}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big]\leq C_{k}e^{C_{k}T}\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}.

In particular,
y\mapsto Y^{k,y}
is continuous in probability as a map from
(\mathcal{H}_{\mu},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}})
to the space of
\Lambda
-valued random variables on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
. Furthermore, by
[
16
, Theorem 2.19]
, we see that
Y^{k,y}
is a Markov process on
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
for any
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
.

Let
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
be fixed, and denote by
Y^{y}
the strong solution of the SEE (
4.2
) with the initial condition
y
. Define

\zeta^{k,y}:=\inf\{t\geq 0\,|\,|\mu_{M}[Y^{y}_{t}]|\geq k\}.

Again by
[
16
, Theorem 2.17]
, there exists a constant
C_{k}>0
such that, for any
T>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Y^{k,y}_{t\wedge\zeta^{k,y}}-Y^{y}_{t\wedge\zeta^{k,y}}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}+\int^{T\wedge\zeta^{k,y}}_{0}\|Y^{k,y}_{t}-Y^{y}_{t}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big]

\displaystyle\leq C_{k}e^{C_{k}T}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int^{T\wedge\zeta^{k,y}}_{0}\Big\{|b_{k}(\mu_{M}[Y^{y}_{t}])-b(\mu_{M}[Y^{y}_{t}])|^{2}+|\sigma_{k}(\mu_{M}[Y^{y}_{t}])-\sigma(\mu_{M}[Y^{y}_{t}])|^{2}\Big\}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big].

By the definition of the maps
b_{k}
,
\sigma_{k}
and the stopping time
\zeta^{k,y}
, we see that the right-hand side is zero. Thus, by using
4.1
(i), (ii) and
4.2
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(Y^{y}|_{[0,T]}\neq Y^{k,y}|_{[0,T]}\ \text{in $\Lambda_{T}$}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}(\zeta^{k,y}<T)=\mathbb{P}\Big(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\mu_{M}[Y^{y}_{t}]|\geq k\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{k^{2}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\mu_{M}[Y^{y}_{t}]|^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{R_{M}}{r(M)k^{2}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Y^{y}_{t}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{R_{M}}{r(M)k^{2}}C_{0}e^{C_{0}T}(1+\|y\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}),

where
C_{0}=C_{0}(c_{\mathrm{LG}},\mu)>0
is the constant appearing in
4.2
. In particular,
Y^{k,y}\to Y^{y}
in
\Lambda
as
k\to\infty
in probability on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
uniformly in
y
on each bounded subset of
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
. By the continuity of the map
y\mapsto Y^{k,y}
, we see that
y\mapsto Y^{y}
is continuous in probability as a map from
(\mathcal{H}_{\mu},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}})
to the space of
\Lambda
-valued random variables on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
, and in particular weakly continuous.

The Markov property of
Y=Y^{y}
follows from that of
Y^{k}=Y^{k,y}
and a standard approximation argument. Denote by
\{P^{k}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
the Markov semigroup (defined on
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})
) associated with the approximating SEE (
4.4
), and define

P_{t}f(y):=\mathbb{E}^{y}\big[f(Y^{y}_{t})\big],\ \ t\geq 0,\ y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu},\ f\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu}),

where
(Y^{y},W^{y},\Omega^{y},\mathcal{F}^{y},\mathbb{F}^{y},\mathbb{P}^{y})
is the weak solution (which is unique in law) of the SEE (
4.2
) with the initial condition
y
. By the above discussions, for any
f\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})
, we have
P_{t}f\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})
and
\lim_{k\to\infty}P^{k}_{t}f(y)=P_{t}f(y)
uniformly in
y
on each bounded subset of
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
. By the Markov property of
Y^{k}
, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
0\leq s_{1}<\cdots<s_{p}=s<t
,
f\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu}^{p})
and

, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[f(Y^{k}_{s_{1}},\dots,Y^{k}_{s_{p}})g(Y^{k}_{t})\Big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big[f(Y^{k}_{s_{1}},\dots,Y^{k}_{s_{p}})P^{k}_{t-s}g(Y^{k}_{s})\Big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big[f(Y^{k}_{s_{1}},\dots,Y^{k}_{s_{p}})P_{t-s}g(Y^{k}_{s})\Big]+\mathbb{E}\Big[f(Y^{k}_{s_{1}},\dots,Y^{k}_{s_{p}})\big(P^{k}_{t-s}g(Y^{k}_{s})-P_{t-s}g(Y^{k}_{s})\big)\Big].

On the one hand, by the facts that
\lim_{k\to\infty}Y^{k}=Y
weakly on
\Lambda
and that
P_{t-s}g\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})
, we see that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big[f(Y^{k}_{s_{1}},\dots,Y^{k}_{s_{p}})g(Y^{k}_{t})\Big]=\mathbb{E}\Big[f(Y_{s_{1}},\dots,Y_{s_{p}})g(Y_{t})\Big]

and

\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big[f(Y^{k}_{s_{1}},\dots,Y^{k}_{s_{p}})P_{t-s}g(Y^{k}_{s})\Big]=\mathbb{E}\Big[f(Y_{s_{1}},\dots,Y_{s_{p}})P_{t-s}g(Y_{s})\Big].

On the other hand, by the tightness of
\{Y^{k}_{s}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
on
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
, for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a compact set
\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}\subset\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
such that
\mathbb{P}(Y^{k}_{s}\notin\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon})<\varepsilon
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and hence

\displaystyle\Big|\mathbb{E}\Big[f(Y^{k}_{s_{1}},\dots,Y^{k}_{s_{p}})\big(P^{k}_{t-s}g(Y^{k}_{s})-P_{t-s}g(Y^{k}_{s})\big)\Big]\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big[\big|P^{k}_{t-s}g(Y^{k}_{s})-P_{t-s}g(Y^{k}_{s})\big|\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{\{Y^{k}_{s}\in\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}\}}\Big]+2\|f\|_{\infty}\|g\|_{\infty}\mathbb{P}\big(Y^{k}_{s}\notin\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big[\big|P^{k}_{t-s}g(Y^{k}_{s})-P_{t-s}g(Y^{k}_{s})\big|\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{\{Y^{k}_{s}\in\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}\}}\Big]+2\|f\|_{\infty}\|g\|_{\infty}\varepsilon.

Noting that
\lim_{k\to\infty}P^{k}_{t-s}g(y)=P_{t-s}g(y)
uniformly in
y\in\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon}
, the dominated convergence theorem yields that the first term of the last line above tends to zero as

. Since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, we see that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big[f(Y^{k}_{s_{1}},\dots,Y^{k}_{s_{p}})\big(P^{k}_{t-s}g(Y^{k}_{s})-P_{t-s}g(Y^{k}_{s})\big)\Big]=0.

Consequently, we get

\mathbb{E}\Big[f(Y_{s_{1}},\dots,Y_{s_{p}})g(Y_{t})\Big]=\mathbb{E}\Big[f(Y_{s_{1}},\dots,Y_{s_{p}})P_{t-s}g(Y_{s})\Big].

Thus, the process
Y
is a time-homogeneous Markov process on
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
with the Markov semigroup
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
. The Feller property has been already proved;
P_{t}f\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})
for any
f\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})
. This completes the proof.

, that
(b_{k},\sigma_{k})\to(b,\sigma)

uniformly on any compact subsets of

and any

with a common constant

, and assume that, for each

, there exists a weak solution

Furthermore, assume that
\{Y^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is tight on
\Lambda
. Then, weak existence holds for the SEE (
1.3
) with the initial condition

.

Proof.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, define
K_{k}(t):=\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}e^{-\theta t}r_{M_{k}}(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)
,
x_{k}(t):=\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}e^{-\theta t}y(\theta)r_{M_{k}}(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)
,

, and

Z^{k}_{t}:=\int^{t}_{0}b_{k}(X^{k}_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{t}_{0}\sigma_{k}(X^{k}_{s})\,\mathrm{d}W^{k}_{s}.

By
2.5
(with
\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)

X^{k}_{t}=x_{k}(t)+\int^{t}_{0}K_{k}(t-s)\,\mathrm{d}Z^{k}_{s},\ t>0.

Let
x(t):=(\mathcal{K}y)(t)=\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}e^{-\theta t}y(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)
. By
4.1
(iii) and Tonelli’s theorem, we have

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{0}|x(t)-x_{k}(t)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int^{\infty}_{0}\big|\mu[e^{-\cdot t}y]-\mu_{M_{k}}[e^{-\cdot t}y]\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{M_{k}}}{r(M_{k})}\int^{\infty}_{0}\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\theta r(\theta)e^{-2\theta t}|y(\theta)|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{\varepsilon_{M_{k}}}{r(M_{k})}\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\theta r(\theta)\frac{1}{2\theta}\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{(0,\infty)}(\theta)|y(\theta)|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)

3.3
), we see that

Since

is tight on the Polish space

\Lambda

\Lambda

and
\tilde{Y}^{k}

\tilde{\mathbb{P}}

for any

\tilde{X}^{k}:=\mu_{M_{k}}[\tilde{Y}^{k}]
for each

. Let

be fixed. Note that, by

as

to

for any

. Thus, we see that
\lim_{k\to\infty}\int^{T}_{0}|\tilde{X}^{k}_{t}-\tilde{X}_{t}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t=0

\tilde{\mathbb{P}}
-a.s. for any
T>0
. In particular, noting that
\mathrm{Law}_{\tilde{\mathbb{P}}}(\tilde{X}^{k})=\mathrm{Law}_{\mathbb{P}^{k}}(X^{k})
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we see that
\{X^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is tight on
L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Next, observe that the increasing process
\int^{\cdot}_{0}\big\{|b_{k}(X^{k}_{t})|^{2}+|\sigma_{k}(X^{k}_{t})|^{2}\big\}\,\mathrm{d}t
is strongly majorized by the increasing process
c^{2}_{\mathrm{LG}}\int^{\cdot}_{0}(1+|X^{k}_{t}|)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t
in the sense that the difference of the two is increasing; see
[
17
, DefinitionVI.3.34]
. Furthermore, the tightness of
\{X^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
on
L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{R}^{n})
and the continuity of the map
x\mapsto c^{2}_{\mathrm{LG}}\int^{\cdot}_{0}(1+|x_{t}|)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t
from
L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
C(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{R})
imply that
\{c^{2}_{\mathrm{LG}}\int^{\cdot}_{0}(1+|X^{k}_{t}|)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is tight on
C(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{R})
. Therefore, by
[
17
, Propositions VI.3.35 and VI.3.36 and Theorem VI.4.18]
, the continuous semimartingale
\{Z^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is tight on
C(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and hence
\{(X^{k},Z^{k})\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is tight on the Polish space
L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{R}^{n})\times C(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{R}^{n})
. By Prokhorov’s theorem, there exists a subsequence (again denoted by
\{(X^{k},Z^{k})\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
) such that
(X^{k},Z^{k})
weakly converges to an
L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{R}^{n})\times C(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{R}^{n})
-valued random variable
(X,Z)
defined on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be the augmentation of the filtration generated by
(\int^{\cdot}_{0}X_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s,Z)
. Observe that the process
\bar{X}
defined by

\bar{X}_{t}(\omega):=\begin{cases}\lim_{h\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{h}\int^{t}_{(t-h)\vee 0}X_{s}(\omega)\,\mathrm{d}s\ &\text{if the limit exists},\\
0\ &\text{otherwise},\end{cases}\ \ t\geq 0,\ \omega\in\Omega,

is
\mathbb{F}
-predictable. Furthermore, Lebesgue’s differentiation theorem yields that
X_{t}(\omega)=\bar{X}_{t}(\omega)
for a.e.
t>0
for every
\omega\in\Omega
. Thus, replacing
X
by
\bar{X}
, if necessary, we may assume that
X
is
\mathbb{F}
-predictable. Then, by
[
2
, Theorem 1.6]
, it holds that

X_{t}=x(t)+\int^{t}_{0}K(t-s)\,\mathrm{d}Z_{s}

\mathbb{P}
-a.s. for a.e.
t>0
. Besides,
Z
is an
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued continuous semimartingale with the canonical decomposition given by

Z_{t}=\int^{t}_{0}b(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s+M_{t},\ t\geq 0,

where
M
is an
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued continuous local martingale with
M_{0}=0
such that

\langle M^{i},M^{j}\rangle_{t}=\int^{t}_{0}a(X_{s})_{i,j}\,\mathrm{d}s,\ t\geq 0,\ i,j\in\{1,\dots,n\},

with the notation
a(x):=\sigma(x)\sigma(x)^{\top}
. Extending the filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{F},\mathbb{P})
, if necessary, we can find a
d
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}
-Brownian motion
W
such that
M_{t}=\int^{t}_{0}\sigma(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}W_{s}
for any
t\geq 0

\mathbb{P}
-a.s.; see
[
19
, Theorem 3.4.2 and the arguments in the proof of Theorem 5.4.6]
. Then, we see that the tuple
(X,W,\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{F},\mathbb{P})
is a weak solution of the SVE (
1.1
) with the forcing term
x=\mathcal{K}y
. By
2.6
(i), weak existence holds for the SEE (
1.3
) with the initial condition
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
. This completes the proof of
4.4
.
∎

4.2
Key estimates for the C-n-R strategy

The following proposition provides key estimates for the C-n-R strategy (

Y
and
\bar{Y}
, and the process
\hat{Y}
corresponds to a controlled version of
\bar{Y}

4.8

and
\nu_{2}

d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\nu_{1},\nu_{2}):=\sup_{A\in\mathcal{B}(E)}|\nu_{1}(A)-\nu_{2}(A)|,

d_{\mathrm{LP}}(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})\leq d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})

Proposition 4.5

1

and a

\mathbb{F}

Y
and
\bar{Y}
are mild solutions of the following SEEs:

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}Y_{t}(\theta)=-\theta Y_{t}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}t+b(\mu_{M}[Y_{t}])\,\mathrm{d}t+\sigma(\mu_{M}[Y_{t}])\,\mathrm{d}W_{t},\ \ \theta\in{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu},\ t>0,\\

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}\bar{Y}_{t}(\theta)=-\theta\bar{Y}_{t}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}t+\bar{b}(\mu_{\bar{M}}[\bar{Y}_{t}])\,\mathrm{d}t+\bar{\sigma}(\mu_{\bar{M}}[\bar{Y}_{t}])\,\mathrm{d}W_{t},\ \ \theta\in{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu},\ t>0,\\

(4.5)

respectively. Also, suppose that
\hat{Y}

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}\hat{Y}_{t}(\theta)=-\theta\hat{Y}_{t}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}t+\bar{b}(\mu_{\bar{M}}[\hat{Y}_{t}])\,\mathrm{d}t+\bar{\sigma}(\mu_{\bar{M}}[\hat{Y}_{t}])\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}+\lambda\mu_{m}[Y_{t}-\hat{Y}_{t}]\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{[0,\tau]}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t,\ \ \theta\in{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu},\ t>0,\\

(4.6)

with the stopping time
\tau

\tau=\inf\left\{t\geq 0\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\begin{aligned} \int^{t}_{0}\big|\mu_{m}[Y_{s}-\hat{Y}_{s}]\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\geq\Delta_{1},\ \int^{t}_{0}\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\theta r_{m}(\theta)|Y_{s}(\theta)-\hat{Y}_{s}(\theta)|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\,\mathrm{d}s\geq\Delta_{2}\\

\mathbb{P}(\Omega\setminus\hat{\Omega})\leq 324JR_{m}\Delta^{-1}_{3}\Big\{\Delta_{0}+\rho_{\bar{\sigma}}\big(\Delta_{1}+\varepsilon_{m}\Delta_{2}\big)\Big\}.

(4.7)

c_{\mathrm{UE}}>0

4.5

d_{\mathrm{TV}}\big(\mathrm{Law}_{\mathbb{P}}(\hat{Y}),\mathrm{Law}_{\mathbb{P}}(\bar{Y})\big)\leq\frac{1}{2c^{1/2}_{\mathrm{UE}}}\lambda\Delta_{1}^{1/2}.

(4.8)

Proof.

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big\{\|Y_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}+\|\hat{Y}_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}\Big\}+\int^{T}_{0}\Big\{\|Y_{t}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}+\|\hat{Y}_{t}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}\Big\}<\infty\ \text{a.s.}

for any

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}|Y_{t}(\theta)-\hat{Y}_{t}(\theta)|^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+2\Big\langle Y_{t}(\theta)-\hat{Y}_{t}(\theta),b(\mu_{M}[Y_{t}])-\bar{b}(\mu_{\bar{M}}[\hat{Y}_{t}])\Big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t+\big|\sigma(\mu_{M}[Y_{t}])-\bar{\sigma}(\mu_{\bar{M}}[\hat{Y}_{t}])\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+2\Big\langle Y_{t}(\theta)-\hat{Y}_{t}(\theta),\Big(\sigma(\mu_{M}[Y_{t}])-\bar{\sigma}(\mu_{\bar{M}}[\hat{Y}_{t}])\Big)\mathrm{d}W_{t}\Big\rangle.

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\Big(\int^{T}_{0}\big|Y_{t}(\theta)-\hat{Y}_{t}(\theta)\big|^{2}\,\big|\sigma(\mu_{M}[Y_{t}])-\bar{\sigma}(\mu_{\bar{M}}[\hat{Y}_{t}])\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/2}r_{m}(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)

for any

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r_{m}(\theta)|Y_{t}(\theta)-\hat{Y}_{t}(\theta)|^{2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt-2\lambda\Big\langle\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r_{m}(\theta)(Y_{t}(\theta)-\hat{Y}_{t}(\theta))\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta),\mu_{m}[Y_{t}-\hat{Y}_{t}]\Big\rangle\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{[0,\tau]}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r_{m}(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)\big|\sigma(\mu_{M}[Y_{t}])-\bar{\sigma}(\mu_{\bar{M}}[\hat{Y}_{t}])\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+2\Big\langle\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r_{m}(\theta)(Y_{t}(\theta)-\hat{Y}_{t}(\theta))\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta),\Big(\sigma(\mu_{M}[Y_{t}])-\bar{\sigma}(\mu_{\bar{M}}[\hat{Y}_{t}])\Big)\mathrm{d}W_{t}\Big\rangle.

. First, we estimate

\displaystyle I^{b}_{t}

\displaystyle=\int^{t\wedge\tau\wedge\zeta}_{0}\Big\langle\mu_{m}[Y_{s}-\hat{Y}_{s}],b(\mu_{M}[Y_{s}])-\bar{b}(\mu_{M}[Y_{s}])\Big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{t\wedge\tau\wedge\zeta}_{0}\Big\langle\mu_{m}[Y_{s}-\hat{Y}_{s}],\bar{b}(\mu_{M}[Y_{s}])-\bar{b}(\mu_{\bar{M}}[Y_{s}])\Big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s

4.4

4.4.1
The regular case

Now we consider the “regular case” (ii) of
3.1
. Namely, assume that the kernel
K
is regular (i.e.,
\lim_{t\downarrow 0}K(t)<\infty
, or equivalently
\mu([0,\infty))<\infty
) and that the following holds:

\liminf_{\delta\downarrow 0}\frac{\rho_{\sigma}(\delta^{2})}{\delta}=0.

By the regularity of
K
, we can take
r\equiv 1
in
1
. In this case, for any
m\in[1,\infty)
, we have
r_{m}(\theta)=1
for any
\theta\in[0,\infty)
, and thus

\displaystyle R_{m}=\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}r_{m}(\theta)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)=\mu([0,\infty))<\infty,

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{m}=\int_{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}(1-r_{m}(\theta))^{2}\theta^{-1}r_{m}(\theta)^{-1}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}\theta)=0.

Take a decreasing sequence
\{\delta_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,1]
such that
\lim_{k\to\infty}\delta_{k}=0
and
\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\rho_{\sigma}(\delta^{2}_{k})}{\delta_{k}}=0
. For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
m_{k}=M_{k}=1
, and define
\Delta_{0,k},\Delta_{1,k},\Delta_{2,k},\Delta_{3,k}\in(0,\infty)
,
\lambda_{k}\in(1,\infty)
and
J_{k}\in[1,\infty)
by

\Delta_{0,k}=\rho_{b}(\delta^{2}_{k})^{1/2}\delta_{k}+\rho_{\sigma}(\delta^{2}_{k}),\ \Delta_{1,k}=\delta^{2}_{k},\ \Delta_{2,k}=1,\ \Delta_{3,k}=\Big(\rho_{b}(\delta^{2}_{k})^{1/2}+\frac{\rho_{\sigma}(\delta^{2}_{k})}{\delta_{k}}\Big)^{1/2}\delta_{k},

and

\lambda_{k}=1+\Big(\rho_{b}(\delta^{2}_{k})^{1/2}+\frac{\rho_{\sigma}(\delta^{2}_{k})}{\delta_{k}}\Big)^{1/2}\delta^{-1}_{k},\ J_{k}=\Big(\rho_{b}(\delta^{2}_{k})^{1/2}+\frac{\rho_{\sigma}(\delta^{2}_{k})}{\delta_{k}}\Big)^{-1/4}.

Construct suitable maps
b_{k}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\sigma_{k}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
by taking convolutions of
b
and
\sigma
with smooth mollifiers. Then, by the same arguments as in
Section

4.3
, we can show that the conclusions of

hold in the regular case (ii).

4.4.2
The balance condition

Let us remark on the “balance condition”

\liminf_{m\to\infty}R_{m}\frac{\rho_{\sigma}(\varepsilon^{2}_{m})}{\varepsilon_{m}}=0

(4.21)

appearing in the singular case (i) of
3.1
. Note that, in the singular case,
\varepsilon_{m}>0
for any
m\in[1,\infty)
,
\lim_{m\to\infty}R_{m}=\infty
and
\lim_{m\to\infty}R_{m}\varepsilon_{m}=0
. By means of
4.5
, the key step in
Section

4.3
is to construct sequences
\{m_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset[1,\infty)
,
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(1,\infty)
and
\{\Delta_{i,k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,\infty)
,

, such that
\lim_{k\to\infty}m_{k}=\infty
and that

\begin{cases}\Delta_{3,k}\leq C(\lambda_{k}-1)\Delta_{1,k}\ \text{for any $k\in\mathbb{N}$ for some constant $C\in(0,\infty)$},\\
\Delta_{3,k}\leq C\Delta_{2,k}\ \text{for any $k\in\mathbb{N}$ for some constant $C\in(0,\infty)$},\\
\lim_{k\to\infty}\Delta_{3,k}=0,\\
\lim_{k\to\infty}R_{m_{k}}\Delta^{-1}_{3,k}\rho_{\sigma}(\Delta_{1,k}+\varepsilon_{m_{k}}\Delta_{2,k})=0,\\
\lim_{k\to\infty}\lambda_{k}\Delta^{1/2}_{1,k}=0.\end{cases}

(4.22)

Here, in view of the C-n-R strategy (
4.1
), the first four lines of (
4.22
) correspond to the “control-step” (
4.7
), and the last line corresponds to the “reimbursement-step” (
4.8
). The first two lines are required in order to ensure that the stopping time

4.18

We have shown in
Section

that the balance condition (

4.22

\sigma
is of “Hölder-type”, i.e.,

for some constant
\gamma\in(0,1]
, then the balance condition (
4.21
) is necessary for the existence of such sequences. Indeed, by easy calculations, we can show that (
4.22
) implies that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\Delta_{1,k}=0,\ \lim_{k\to\infty}\varepsilon_{m_{k}}\Delta_{2,k}=0,\ \lim_{k\to\infty}\Delta_{3,k}\Delta^{-1/2}_{1,k}=0,

and that

\lim_{k\to\infty}R_{m_{k}}\Delta^{\gamma-1/2}_{1,k}=0,\ \lim_{k\to\infty}R_{m_{k}}\varepsilon^{\gamma}_{m_{k}}\Delta^{\gamma-1}_{2,k}=0,\ \lim_{k\to\infty}R_{m_{k}}\Delta^{\gamma}_{1,k}\Delta^{-1}_{2,k}=0,\ \lim_{k\to\infty}R_{m_{k}}\varepsilon^{\gamma}_{m_{k}}\Delta^{\gamma}_{2,k}\Delta^{-1/2}_{1,k}=0.

(4.23)

Since
\lim_{k\to\infty}R_{m_{k}}=\infty
and
\lim_{k\to\infty}\Delta_{1,k}=0
, the first equality in (
4.23
) implies that
\gamma>\frac{1}{2}
. Now assume that the balance condition (
4.21
) does not hold;
\liminf_{m\to\infty}R_{m}\varepsilon^{2\gamma-1}_{m}>0
. Then, noting that
\gamma\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
, the first, second and fourth equalities in (
4.23
) imply that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\Delta_{1,k}}{\varepsilon^{2}_{m_{k}}}=0,\ \lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\varepsilon_{m_{k}}}{\Delta_{2,k}}=0\ \text{and}\ \lim_{k\to\infty}\Big(\frac{\Delta_{2,k}}{\varepsilon_{m_{k}}}\Big)^{\gamma}\Big(\frac{\varepsilon^{2}_{m_{k}}}{\Delta_{1,k}}\Big)^{1/2}=0,

respectively, and we get a contradiction.

The above discussions indicate that the balance condition (
4.21
) is essential for the C-n-R strategy to prove
3.1
based on the estimates appearing in
4.5
.

Appendix

In this appendix, we prove existence and uniqueness of the mild solutions of SEEs of the form (
4.6
) involving stopping times which depend on the solutions.

Proposition A.1
.

Suppose that
1
holds. Let a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{F},\mathbb{P})
and a
d
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}
-Brownian motion
W
be fixed. Let
b_{1},b_{2}:\Omega\times[0,\infty)\times\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}:\Omega\times[0,\infty)\times\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
be measurable maps satisfying the following conditions:

•

For
\varphi=b_{1},b_{2},\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
, the process
(\varphi(t,y))_{t\geq 0}
is predictable for any
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
.

•

There exist a constant
c_{\mathrm{LG}}>0
and a nonnegative predictable process
\xi
satisfying
\mathbb{E}[\int^{T}_{0}\xi^{2}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t]<\infty
for any
T>0
such that, for
\varphi=b_{1},b_{2},\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
,

|\varphi(t,y)|\leq c_{\mathrm{LG}}(\xi_{t}+\|y\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}})

for any
t\geq 0
and any
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
a.s.

•

There exist
L_{k}>0
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, such that, for
\varphi=b_{1},b_{2},\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
,

|\varphi(t,y)-\varphi(t,\bar{y})|\leq L_{k}\|y-\bar{y}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}

for any
t\geq 0
and any
y,\bar{y}\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
satisfying
\|y\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}\vee\|\bar{y}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}\leq k
a.s., for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Also, let
f:\Omega\times[0,\infty)\times\mathcal{V}_{\mu}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{f}}
and
g:\Omega\times[0,\infty)\times\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{g}}
with
d_{f},d_{g}\in\mathbb{N}
be measurable maps satisfying the following conditions:

•

The process
(f(t,y))_{t\geq 0}
is predictable for any
y\in\mathcal{V}_{\mu}
, and the process
(g(t,y))_{t\geq 0}
is predictable for any
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
.

•

There exist a constant
c_{f}>0
and a nonnegative predictable process
\eta
satisfying
\int^{T}_{0}\eta_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty
a.s. for any
T>0
such that

|f(t,y)|\leq c_{f}(\eta_{t}+\|y\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}})

for any
t\geq 0
and any
y\in\mathcal{V}_{\mu}
a.s.

•

For
\mathbb{P}
-almost every
\omega\in\Omega
, the map
[0,\infty)\times\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\ni(t,y)\mapsto g(\omega,t,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{g}}
is continuous.

Let
O_{f}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d_{f}}
and
O_{g}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d_{g}}
be open sets. Then, for any initial condition
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
, there exists a unique mild solution
Y
to the following SEE:

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}Y_{t}(\theta)=-\theta Y_{t}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}t+\big\{b_{1}(t,Y_{t})+b_{2}(t,Y_{t})\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{[0,\tau]}(t)\big\}\,\mathrm{d}t+\big\{\sigma_{1}(t,Y_{t})+\sigma_{2}(t,Y_{t})\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{[0,\tau]}(t)\big\}\,\mathrm{d}W_{t},\\
\hskip 199.16928pt\theta\in{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu},\ t>0,\\
Y_{0}(\theta)=y(\theta),\ \ \theta\in{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}.\end{cases}

(A.1)

with the stopping time
\tau=\tau(Y)
given by

\tau=\inf\left\{t\geq 0\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\int^{t}_{0}f(s,Y_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s\notin O_{f}\ \text{or}\ g(t,Y_{t})\notin O_{g}\right\}.

Proof.

By
[
16
, Theorem 2.18]
, there exists a unique mild solution
\hat{Y}
to the SEE

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}\hat{Y}_{t}(\theta)=-\theta\hat{Y}_{t}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}t+\big\{b_{1}(t,\hat{Y}_{t})+b_{2}(t,\hat{Y}_{t})\big\}\,\mathrm{d}t+\big\{\sigma_{1}(t,\hat{Y}_{t})+\sigma_{2}(t,\hat{Y}_{t})\big\}\,\mathrm{d}W_{t},\ \ \theta\in{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu},\ t>0,\\
\hat{Y}_{0}(\theta)=y(\theta),\ \ \theta\in{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}.\end{cases}

Define a stopping time
\hat{\tau}=\hat{\tau}(\hat{Y})
by

\hat{\tau}:=\inf\left\{t\geq 0\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\int^{t}_{0}f(s,\hat{Y}_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s\notin O_{f}\ \text{or}\ g(t,\hat{Y}_{t})\notin O_{g}\right\}.

Again by
[
16
, Theorem 2.18]
, there exists a unique mild solution
Y
to the SEE

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}Y_{t}(\theta)=-\theta Y_{t}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}t+\big\{b_{1}(t,Y_{t})+b_{2}(t,Y_{t})\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{[0,\hat{\tau}]}(t)\big\}\,\mathrm{d}t+\big\{\sigma_{1}(t,Y_{t})+\sigma_{2}(t,Y_{t})\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{[0,\hat{\tau}]}(t)\big\}\,\mathrm{d}W_{t},\\
\hskip 199.16928pt\theta\in{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu},\ t>0,\\
Y_{0}(\theta)=y(\theta),\ \ \theta\in{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}.\end{cases}

Also, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a unique mild solution
Y^{k}
to the SEE

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}Y^{k}_{t}(\theta)=-\theta Y^{k}_{t}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}t+\big\{b^{k}_{1}(t,Y^{k}_{t})+b^{k}_{2}(t,Y^{k}_{t})\big\}\,\mathrm{d}t+\big\{\sigma^{k}_{1}(t,Y^{k}_{t})+\sigma^{k}_{2}(t,Y_{t})\big\}\,\mathrm{d}W_{t},\ \ \theta\in{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu},\ t>0,\\
Y^{k}_{0}(\theta)=y(\theta),\ \ \theta\in{\mathrm{supp}\,\mu},\end{cases}

where
b^{k}_{1},b^{k}_{2}:\Omega\times[0,\infty)\times\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\sigma^{k}_{1},\sigma^{k}_{2}:\Omega\times[0,\infty)\times\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
are defined by

\varphi^{k}(t,y):=\begin{cases}\varphi(t,y)\ &\text{if $\|y\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}<k$},\\
\varphi\Big(t,\frac{k}{\|y\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}}y\Big)\ &\text{if $\|y\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}\geq k$},\end{cases}

for
\varphi=b_{1},b_{2},\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
. Note that, for
\varphi=b_{1},b_{2},\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
,

|\varphi^{k}(t,y)-\varphi^{k}(t,\bar{y})|\leq L_{k}\|y-\bar{y}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}

for any
t\geq 0
and any
y,\bar{y}\in\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
a.s. Thus, by
[
16
, Theorem 2.17]
, there exists a constant
C_{k}>0
such that, for any
T>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Y^{k}_{t\wedge\hat{\tau}\wedge\zeta_{k}}-Y_{t\wedge\hat{\tau}\wedge\zeta_{k}}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}+\int^{T\wedge\hat{\tau}\wedge\zeta_{k}}_{0}\|Y^{k}_{t}-Y_{t}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big]

\displaystyle\leq C_{k}e^{C_{k}T}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int^{T\wedge\hat{\tau}\wedge\zeta_{k}}_{0}\Big\{|b^{k}_{1}(t,Y_{t})+b^{k}_{2}(t,Y_{t})-b_{1}(t,Y_{t})-b_{2}(t,Y_{t})|^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt+|\sigma^{k}_{1}(t,Y_{t})+\sigma^{k}_{2}(t,Y_{t})-\sigma_{1}(t,Y_{t})-\sigma_{2}(t,Y_{t})|^{2}\Big\}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big]

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Y^{k}_{t\wedge\hat{\zeta}_{k}}-\hat{Y}_{t\wedge\hat{\zeta}_{k}}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}+\int^{T\wedge\hat{\zeta}_{k}}_{0}\|Y^{k}_{t}-\hat{Y}_{t}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mu}}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big]

\displaystyle\leq C_{k}e^{C_{k}T}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int^{T\wedge\hat{\zeta}_{k}}_{0}\Big\{|b^{k}_{1}(t,\hat{Y}_{t})+b^{k}_{2}(t,\hat{Y}_{t})-b_{1}(t,\hat{Y}_{t})-b_{2}(t,\hat{Y}_{t})|^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt+|\sigma^{k}_{1}(t,\hat{Y}_{t})+\sigma^{k}_{2}(t,\hat{Y}_{t})-\sigma(t,\hat{Y}_{t})-\sigma_{2}(t,\hat{Y}_{t})|^{2}\Big\}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big],

where
\zeta_{k}
and
\hat{\zeta}_{k}
are stopping times defined by

\zeta_{k}:=\inf\{t\geq 0\,|\,\|Y_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}\geq k\}\ \text{and}\ \hat{\zeta}_{k}:=\inf\{t\geq 0\,|\,\|\hat{Y}_{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}\geq k\}.

By the definitions of
b^{k}_{1},b^{k}_{2}
,
\sigma^{k}_{1},\sigma^{k}_{2}
,
\zeta_{k}
and
\hat{\zeta}_{k}
, we see that, for any
T>0
,

Y^{k}_{t}=Y_{t}\ \ \text{for any $t\in[0,T\wedge\hat{\tau}\wedge\zeta_{k}]$ a.s.}

and

Y^{k}_{t}=\hat{Y}_{t}\ \ \text{for any $t\in[0,T\wedge\hat{\zeta}_{k}]$ a.s.}

This implies that

\mathbb{P}\big(Y_{t}=\hat{Y}_{t}\ \text{for any $t\in[0,T\wedge\hat{\tau}]$}\big)\geq\mathbb{P}\big(\zeta_{k}>T\ \text{and}\ \hat{\zeta}_{k}>T\big)

for any
T>0
and any
k\in\mathbb{N}
. By the
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
-continuity of the processes
Y
and
\hat{Y}
, the right-hand side tends to
1
as
k\to\infty
. Thus, we see that
Y_{t}=\hat{Y}_{t}
for any
t\in[0,T\wedge\hat{\tau}]
a.s. for any
T>0
, which implies that
Y_{t}=\hat{Y}_{t}
for any
t\in[0,\hat{\tau}]
a.s. Now we define

\tau:=\inf\left\{t\geq 0\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\int^{t}_{0}f(s,Y_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s\notin O_{f}\ \text{or}\ g(t,Y_{t})\notin O_{g}\right\}.

Noting that the processes
\int^{\cdot}_{0}f(s,Y_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s
,
g(\cdot,Y_{\cdot})
,
\int^{\cdot}_{0}f(s,\hat{Y}_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s
and
g(\cdot,\hat{Y}_{\cdot})
are continuous and that
O_{f}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d_{f}}
and
O_{g}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d_{g}}
are open sets, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(\hat{\tau}<\tau\big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\Big(\int^{\hat{\tau}}_{0}f(s,Y_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s\in O_{f},\ g(\hat{\tau},Y_{\hat{\tau}})\in O_{g}\ \text{and}\ \hat{\tau}<\tau\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\Big(\int^{\hat{\tau}}_{0}f(s,\hat{Y}_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s\in O_{f},\ g(\hat{\tau},\hat{Y}_{\hat{\tau}})\in O_{g}\Big)=0

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(\tau<\hat{\tau}\big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\Big(\int^{\tau}_{0}f(s,\hat{Y}_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s\in O_{f},\ g(\tau,\hat{Y}_{\tau})\in O_{g}\ \text{and}\ \tau<\hat{\tau}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\Big(\int^{\tau}_{0}f(s,Y_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s\in O_{f},\ g(\tau,Y_{\tau})\in O_{g}\Big)=0.

Thus, we see that
\tau=\hat{\tau}
a.s. By the construction, the process
Y
is a mild solution of the SEE (
A.1
).

Now we prove the uniqueness. Assume that there is another mild solution
\bar{Y}
to the SEE (
A.1
) with the stopping time
\tau
replaced by

\bar{\tau}=\inf\left\{t\geq 0\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\int^{t}_{0}f(s,\bar{Y}_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s\notin O_{f}\ \text{or}\ g(t,\bar{Y}_{t})\notin O_{g}\right\}.

By the same arguments as above, we see that
Y_{t}=\bar{Y}_{t}
for any
t\in[0,\tau\wedge\bar{\tau}]
a.s. This implies that
\tau=\bar{\tau}
a.s. Therefore,
Y
and
\bar{Y}
solve the same SEE. By the uniqueness of the mild solution, we get
Y_{t}=\bar{Y}_{t}
for any
t\geq 0
a.s. This completes the proof.

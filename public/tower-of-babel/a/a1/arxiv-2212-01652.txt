Tangent groupoid and tangent cones in sub-Riemannian geometry
Omar Mohsen

Abstract
. Let X 1 , ⋯ , X m X_{1},\cdots,X_{m} be vector fields satisfying Hörmander’s Lie bracket generating condition on a smooth manifold M M . We generalise Connes’s tangent groupoid, by constructing a completion of the space M × M × R + × M\times M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times} using the sub-Riemannian metric. We use our space to calculate all the tangent cones of the sub-Riemannian metric in the sense of the Gromov-Hausdorff distance. This generalises a result of Bellaïche.



Introduction

Let
M
be a smooth manifold. In
[
Con94a
, Chapter 2.5]
, Connes introduced the tangent groupoid

\mathbb{T}M:=M\times M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}\sqcup TM\times\{0\}.

The space
\mathbb{T}M
is equipped with a smooth manifold structure by the deformation to the normal cone construction, see
[
Ful84a
, Chapter 5]
.
The space
\mathbb{T}M
is also a Lie groupoid, in particular the space
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{T}M)
is equipped with a convolution law making it a
*
-algebra. The convolution law is a deformation of the usual convolution law on
M\times M
which is used in Schwartz kernels of operators acting on
L^{2}M
and the convolution law on
T_{x}M
for
x\in M
which comes from the commutative group structure. The tangent groupoid has been studied and generalised by many authors, see
[
Erp10a
,
HY19a
,
Car+99a
,
Car08a
,
LMN00a
,
AS11c
,
MP97a
,
DL09a
,
HS87a
,
Hig10a
,
HH18a
,
Moh21a
,
EY17a
,
CP19a
,
LR01a
,
Ewe21a
]
.
1
1

1

This list is certainly not exhaustive.

Following Gromov
[
Gro96a
]
, if
(X,d)
is a metric space, then the limit (if it exists)
\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}(X,t^{-1}d,x)
in the sense of pointed Gromov-Hausdorff distance is called the tangent cone of
X
at
x
. The tangent cone of a Riemannian manifold
(M,d_{M})
at
x
is
T_{x}M
. Connes tangent groupoid can be seen as an improvement of this simple fact, because the function

(1)

\mathbb{T}M\to\mathbb{R}_{+},\quad(y,x,t)\mapsto t^{-1}d_{M}(x,y),\quad(\xi,x,0)\mapsto d_{T_{x}M}(\xi,0)

is continuous, where
d_{T_{x}M}
is the constant distance associated to the Riemannian metric.

In this article, we present a generalization of Connes’s tangent groupoid in sub-Riemannian geometry. This construction is a special case of the author’s construction
[
Moh22b
]
, and has been used in
[
AMY22a
,
Moh22c
]
. The construction given in
[
Moh22b
]
is based on ideas and techniques due to Androulidakis and Skandalis
[
AS09a
,
AS11b
]
and Debord
[
Deb01a
,
Deb13a
]
. The goal here is to give a self-contained presentation of our construction which does not rely on singular foliations. We then use our construction to calculate the tangent cones in sub-Riemannian geometry.

So let
X_{1},\cdots,X_{d}
be vector fields which satisfy Hörmander’s Lie bracket generating condition of depth
N\in\mathbb{N}
, i.e., for every
x\in M
, the tangent space
T_{x}M
is spanned by
X_{1}(x),\cdots,X_{d}(x)
together with their iterated brackets
[X_{i},X_{j}](x),[[X_{i},X_{j}],X_{k}](x),\cdots
of length
\leq N
. A theorem of Chow
[
Cho39a
]
implies that any two points of
M
can be connected by a piecewise smooth path
\gamma
such that
\gamma^{\prime}(t)\in\mathrm{span}(X_{1}(\gamma(t)),\cdots,X_{d}(\gamma(t)))
for almost all
t\in[0,1]
. One then defines the Carnot-Carathéodory metric
d_{CC}(x,y)
to be the minimum length of all such paths, see
[
Bel96a
]
.

Generalizing a result of Mitchell
[
Mit85a
]
, Bellaïche
[
Bel96a
]
computed the tangent cone of
(M,d_{CC})
as follows. Let
G
be the free
N
-step nilpotent Lie group with
d
generators, one for each
X_{1},\cdots,X_{d}
. For each
x\in M
,
Bellaïche identifies a simply connected Lie subgroup
\mathfrak{r}_{x}\subseteq G
of codimension
\dim(M)
. He shows that

(2)

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}(M,t^{-1}d_{CC},x)=(G/\mathfrak{r}_{x},d_{G/\mathfrak{r}_{x}},\mathfrak{r}_{x})

where
d_{G/\mathfrak{r}_{x}}
is a Carnot-Carathéodory metric on the homogeneous space
G/\mathfrak{r}_{x}
.

A naive guess of the analogue of Connes’s tangent groupoid is
M\times M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}\bigsqcup_{x\in M}G/\mathfrak{r}_{x}\times\{0\}
. There are two problems with this definition. First it is not a groupoid because
\mathfrak{r}_{x}
in general is not normal in
G
.
Second it is not locally compact (with respect to some natural topology one can define). These issues are ultimately due to the fact that the limit (
2
) is not uniform in
x
. That is, if
x_{n}\in M
is a sequence converging to
x
and
t_{n}\to 0^{+}
, then
\lim_{n\to+\infty}(M,t_{n}^{-1}d_{CC},x_{n})
exists (up to taking a subsequence) but the limit is of the form
(G/H,d_{G/H},H)
where
H\subseteq G
is a simply connected Lie group and in general
H\neq\mathfrak{r}_{y}
for all
y\in M
.

This leads to our construction. For each
x\in M
, we define a subset
\mathcal{G}^{0}_{x}
of simply connected Lie subgroups of
G
of codimension
\dim(M)
, which satisfies the following conditions

(1)

\mathfrak{r}_{x}\in\mathcal{G}^{0}_{x}
.

(2)

If
g\in G
,
H\in\mathcal{G}^{0}_{x}
, then
gHg^{-1}\in\mathcal{G}^{0}_{x}
.

(3)

The set
\mathcal{G}^{0}_{x}
is closed when identified (using Lie algebras) with a subset of the Grassmannian manifold of subspaces of
\mathfrak{g}
of codimension
\dim(M)
.

The set
\mathcal{G}^{0}_{x}
comes from looking at linear relations between the vector fields
X_{1},\cdots,X_{d}
and their commutators which we now define.
Let
\mathfrak{g}
be the Lie algebra of
G
. There is a natural linear map

\natural:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathcal{X}(M),

where
\mathcal{X}(M)
is the space of vector fields on
M
. The map
\natural
is the unique linear map which sends
X_{i}
to
X_{i}
and which sends iterated Lie brackets
of
X_{i}
in
\mathfrak{g}
of length
\leq N
to the corresponding Lie brackets in
\mathcal{X}(M)
. Notice that
\natural
isn’t a Lie algebra homomorphism because iterated Lie brackets in
\mathcal{X}(M)
of length
>N
do not necessarily vanish.
For
x\in M
, let
\natural_{x}:\mathfrak{g}\to T_{x}M
be the composition of
\natural
with the evaluation map at
x\in M
. The map
\natural_{x}
is surjective by Hörmander’s condition.
Therefore,
\ker(\natural_{x})
is a subspace of
\mathfrak{g}
of codimension equal to
\dim(M)
. Let
\mathrm{Grass}(\mathfrak{g})
be the Grassmannian manifold of subspaces of codimension
\dim(M)
.
We also make use of the natural graded dilation
\alpha_{t}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{g}
which is defined by
\alpha_{t}(X_{i})=tX_{i}
,
\alpha_{t}([X_{i},X_{j}])=t^{2}[X_{i},X_{j}]
, etc.
The main point of our construction is that the convergence in the Gromov-Hausdorff distance of
(M,t_{n}^{-1}d_{CC},x_{n})
can be reduced to the linear algebra problem of computing the limit of
\alpha_{t_{n}^{-1}}(\ker(\natural_{x_{n}}))
in the Grassmannian manifold
\mathrm{Grass}(\mathfrak{g})
.
We thus define

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{0}_{x}:=\{\mathfrak{h}\in\mathrm{Grass}(\mathfrak{g}):\exists(t_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times},(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq M\text{ such that }

\displaystyle t_{n}\to 0,x_{n}\to x,\alpha_{t_{n}^{-1}}(\ker(\natural_{x_{n}}))\to\mathfrak{h}\}

Even though
\natural
is not a Lie algebra homomorphism and therefore
\ker(\natural_{x})
is not a Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
in general. We prove that the limit
\alpha_{t_{n}^{-1}}(\ker(\natural_{x_{n}}))
, if it exists, is always a Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
.
Therefore,
\mathcal{G}^{0}_{x}
can be considered as a set of Lie subgroups of
G
.

We go further by creating a space that includes all the spaces
\mathcal{G}^{0}_{x}
together as
x
varies. To this end, we consider the inclusion

\displaystyle M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}\to\mathrm{Grass}(\mathfrak{g})\times M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}

\displaystyle(x,t)\mapsto(\alpha_{\frac{1}{t}}(\ker(\natural_{x})),x,t)

We take the space

\mathbb{G}^{0}:=M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}\bigsqcup_{x\in M}\mathcal{G}^{0}_{x}\times\{(x,0)\}

to be the closure of
M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}
inside
\mathrm{Grass}(\mathfrak{g})\times M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}
equipped with the subspace topology. This topology is a second countable locally compact metrizable topology
which makes the natural projection
\mathbb{G}^{0}\to M\times\mathbb{R}
a proper continuous map. Our main theorem is the following

Theorem A
.

If
(x_{n},t_{n})\in M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}
converges in
\mathbb{G}^{0}
to
(H,x,0)
where
H\in\mathcal{G}^{0}_{x}
, i.e., if
t_{n}\to 0
,
x_{n}\to x
and
\alpha_{t_{n}^{-1}}(\ker(\natural_{x_{n}}))\to\mathfrak{h}
, then

\lim_{n\to+\infty}(M,t_{n}^{-1}d_{CC},x_{n})=(G/H,d_{G/H},H),

where
d_{G/H}
is a Carnot-Carathéodory metric on the homogeneous space
G/H
.

Since the projection map
\mathbb{G}^{0}\to M\times\mathbb{R}
is proper, it follows that Theorem
A
gives all possible tangent cones.

We prove Theorem
A
by constructing the analogue of Connes’s tangent groupoid which as a set is equal to

\mathbb{G}:=M\times M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}\bigsqcup_{x\in M,H\in\mathcal{G}^{0}_{x}}G/H\times\{0\}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{G}^{0}.

We equip
\mathbb{G}
with a second countable locally compact metrizable topology. We show that
\mathbb{G}
is a continuous family groupoid in the sense of Paterson
[
Pat00a
]
.
Finally, to deduce Theorem
A
, we prove that the following function

\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{R}_{+},\quad(y,x,t)\mapsto t^{-1}d_{CC}(y,x),\quad(gH,x,0)\mapsto d_{G/H}(gH,H)

is continuous. We end the introduction with a few remarks

(1)

If
X_{1},\cdots,X_{d}
have constant rank and
X_{1},\cdots,X_{d},[X_{i},X_{j}]
have constant rank, and
X_{1},\cdots,X_{d},[X_{i},X_{j}],[[X_{i},X_{j}],X_{k}]
have constant rank, etc., then the limit (
2
) is uniform in
x
and
\mathcal{G}^{0}_{x}=\{\mathfrak{r}_{x}\}
.
In this case our tangent groupoid coincides with previous constructions by Choi and Ponge
[
CP19a
]
and van Erp and Yuncken
[
EY17a
]
, see also
[
Moh21a
,
HH18a
]
for different constructions of the tangent groupoid in this case.

(2)

The above strategy of computing tangent cones by defining a deformation groupoid appears in Pansu’s work
[
Pan83a
]
, where he computes the tangent cones at infinity of simply connected nilpotent Lie groups.

(3)

The space
\mathbb{G}^{0}
is a closed subset of
\mathbb{G}
. There is a natural
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}
-action on
\mathbb{G}
which leaves the set
\mathbb{G}^{0}
invariant. We show that the
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}
-action is proper on
\mathbb{G}\backslash\mathbb{G}^{0}
and that the quotient
\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{G}):=(\mathbb{G}\backslash\mathbb{G}^{0})/\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}
is second countable metrizable space which is compact if
M
is compact. As a set
\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{G})
is equal to the union of the dense open subset
(M\times M)\backslash\Delta_{M}
and some other set coming from the tangent cones. Hence, we can view
\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{G})
as a blowup of
M\times M
along the diagonal using the sub-Riemannian metric. In a future paper, we will show the connection between this space and the heat kernel and Weyl law of maximally hypoelliptic differential operators, see Remark
1.12
.

If

right

G
. This differs by a sign from the convention usually used in Lie group theory but agrees with the convention usually used in Lie groupoid theory. In particular, for the sake of completeness, the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula is

(3)

\log(e^{v}\cdot e^{w})=v+w-\frac{1}{2}[v,w]+\frac{1}{12}[v,[v,w]+\cdots,\quad v,w\in\mathfrak{g}.

If
\mathfrak{g}
is a nilpotent Lie algebra, then the BCH formula is a finite sum, and hence equips
\mathfrak{g}
with the structure of a Lie group. We will use
\mathfrak{g}
to denote both the Lie algebra and the Lie group, usually denoting the product by
v\cdot w
for
v,w\in\mathfrak{g}
. We will usually use
v^{-1}
instead of
-v
to denote the inverse of
v
.

If
L\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie subalgebra, then it is also a Lie subgroup. We will use
\mathfrak{g}/L
to denote the set of left cosets of
L
and
\frac{\mathfrak{g}}{L}
for the (rarely used) quotient of
\mathfrak{g}
by
L
as vector spaces.

•

If
X,Y
are topological spaces, then a partially defined function
f:X\to Y
will always mean a function
f
defined on an open subset of

.

1.
Tangent cones

Definition 1.1

(
[
AMY22a
]
)
.

Let
M
be a smooth manifold. We recall that
\mathcal{X}(M)
denotes the Lie algebra of vector fields on
M
. A weighted sub-Riemannian structure of depth
N\in\mathbb{N}
is a family

0=\mathcal{F}^{0}\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{N}=\mathcal{X}(M)

of finitely generated
C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
-modules of vector fields such that for all
i,j\in\mathbb{N}

(4)

[\mathcal{F}^{i},\mathcal{F}^{j}]\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{i+j}.

We will use the convention
\mathcal{F}^{i}=\mathcal{F}^{N}
for all
i\geq N
throughout the article.

Remark 1.2
.

The results of this article can be extended to the more general situation where
\mathcal{F}^{i}
are only locally finitely generated. We will restrict to the finitely generated case for simplicity.

Example 1.3
.

Let
X_{1},\cdots,X_{d}\in\mathcal{X}(M)
be vector fields satisfying Hörmander’s Lie bracket generating condition of depth
N\in\mathbb{N}
. We can define
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
inductively by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{1}=\langle X_{1},\cdots,X_{d}\rangle,\quad\mathcal{F}^{k}=\mathcal{F}^{k-1}+[\mathcal{F}^{1},\mathcal{F}^{k-1}].

More generally if
v_{1},\cdots,v_{d}\in\mathbb{N}
are natural numbers, thought of as weights of
X_{1},\cdots,X_{d}
. Then we can define
\mathcal{F}^{k}
to be the module generated by the iterated Lie brackets

[X_{a_{1}},[\cdots,[X_{a_{n-1}},X_{a_{n}}]\cdots]

such that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{a_{i}}\leq k
. Hörmander’s condition implies that
\mathcal{F}^{N\max(v_{1},\cdots,v_{d})}=\mathcal{X}(M)
.

If

(5)

\mathcal{F}^{i+1}=\mathcal{F}^{i}+[\mathcal{F}^{i},\mathcal{F}^{1}]

for all
i\geq 1
, then a weighted sub-Riemannian structure is just a sub-Riemannian structure. By this, we mean that
\mathcal{F}^{1}
is a module generated by some vector fields which satisfy Hörmander’s condition of depth
N
, and
\mathcal{F}^{i}
is the module generated by their iterated Lie brackets of length
\leq i
.

Osculating groups.
Let
x\in M
. We define

(6)

\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\mathcal{F}^{i}}{\mathcal{F}^{i-1}+I_{x}\mathcal{F}^{i}},

where
I_{x}\subseteq C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
is the ideal of smooth functions vanishing at
x
. If
X\in\mathcal{F}^{i}
, then
[X]_{i,x}
denotes the class of
X
in
\frac{\mathcal{F}^{i}}{\mathcal{F}^{i-1}+I_{x}\mathcal{F}^{i}}\subseteq\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}
. By (
4
), the Lie bracket of vector fields descends to a bilinear map

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{F}^{i}}{\mathcal{F}^{i-1}+I_{x}\mathcal{F}^{i}}\times\frac{\mathcal{F}^{j}}{\mathcal{F}^{j-1}+I_{x}\mathcal{F}^{j}}

\displaystyle\to\frac{\mathcal{F}^{i+j}}{\mathcal{F}^{i+j-1}+I_{x}\mathcal{F}^{i+j}}.

The space
\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}
is thus equipped with the structure of a graded nilpotent Lie algebra given by

\displaystyle\forall X\in\mathcal{F}^{i},Y\in\mathcal{F}^{j},\quad[[X]_{i,x},[Y]_{j,x}]:=\begin{cases}\Big[[X,Y]\Big]_{i+j,x}\quad&\text{if}\ i+j\leq N\\
0\quad&\text{if}\ i+j>N\end{cases}

Hence
\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}
is a Lie group by the BCH formula (
3
).

Space of tangent cones.

For
x\in M
, we denote by
\mathrm{Grass}(\mathcal{F})_{x}
the Grassmannian manifold of linear subspaces of
\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}
of codimension equal to
\dim(M)
. Let

(7)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Grass}(\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F}):=M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}\bigsqcup_{x\in M}\mathrm{Grass}(\mathcal{F})_{x}\times\{(x,0)\}.

We will equip
\mathrm{Grass}(\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F})
with a topology. To do so we need to choose a set of generators for each module
\mathcal{F}^{i}
. We formalise this as follows

Definition 1.4
.

A graded basis is a pair
(V,\natural)
where
V=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{N}V^{i}
is a graded
\mathbb{R}
-vector space,

(8)

\natural:V\to\mathcal{X}(M)

an
\mathbb{R}
-linear map
such that

(1)

for all
k\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
,
\natural(V^{k})\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{k}
.

(2)

for all
k\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
, the
C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
module generated by
\natural(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}V^{i})
is equal to
\mathcal{F}^{k}
.

Let
(V,\natural)
be a graded basis. For
(x,t)\in M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}
, we define the linear maps

(9)

\displaystyle\natural_{t}:V\to\mathcal{X}(M),\quad\natural_{t}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}v_{i}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}t^{i}\natural(v_{i}),\quad v_{i}\in V^{i}

\displaystyle\natural_{x,t}:V\to T_{x}M,\quad\natural_{x,t}(v)=\natural_{t}(v)(x),\quad v\in V.

The map
\natural_{x,t}
is surjective because
\natural(V)
generates
\mathcal{X}(M)
. We define a grading preserving linear map

\natural_{x,0}:V\to\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x},\quad\sum_{i=1}^{N}v_{i}\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{N}[\natural(v_{i})]_{i,x},\quad v_{i}\in V^{i}.

The map
\natural_{x,0}
is also surjective, see
[
AMY22a
, Proposition 1.1]
.

Let
\mathrm{Grass}(V)
be the Grassmannian manifold of linear subspaces of
V
of codimension equal to
\dim(M)
. We define

(10)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Grass}(\natural):\mathrm{Grass}(\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F})

\displaystyle\to\mathrm{Grass}(V)\times M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}

\displaystyle(x,t)

\displaystyle\mapsto(\ker(\natural_{(x,t)}),x,t),\quad t\neq 0

\displaystyle(L,x,0)

\displaystyle\mapsto(\natural_{x,0}^{-1}(L),x,0),\quad L\in\mathrm{Grass}(\mathcal{F})_{x}

The map
\mathrm{Grass}(\natural)
is clearly injective. Thus, we are lead to the following definition.

Definition 1.5
.

The topology on
\mathrm{Grass}(\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F})
is the topology which makes
\mathrm{Grass}(\natural)
an embedding.

We have used the graded basis
(V,\natural)
to define the topology on
\mathrm{Grass}(\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F})
. The following proposition which will be proved in Section
3.2
says that if we pick a different graded basis, then we would obtain the same topology.

Proposition 1.6
.

(1)

The image of the map
\mathrm{Grass}(\natural)
is a closed subset of
\mathrm{Grass}(V)\times M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

(2)

The space
\mathrm{Grass}(\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F})
is second countable locally compact metrizable, and the natural projection
\mathrm{Grass}(\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F})\to M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a continuous proper map.

(3)

The topology on
\mathrm{Grass}(\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F})
does not depend on the choice of the graded basis.

In general
M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}
is not dense in
\mathrm{Grass}(\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F})
. This leads to the following

Definition 1.7
.

We define

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{0}_{x}:

\displaystyle=\{L\in\mathrm{Grass}(\mathcal{F})_{x}:(L,x,0)\in\overline{M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}}\subseteq\mathrm{Grass}(\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F})\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{0}:

\displaystyle=\{(L,x):x\in M,L\in\mathrm{Grass}(\mathcal{F})_{x}\}

\displaystyle\mathbb{G}^{0}:

\displaystyle=\overline{M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}}=M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}\sqcup\mathcal{G}^{0}\times\{0\}\subseteq\mathrm{Grass}(\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F}).

We equip these spaces with the subspace topology from
\mathrm{Grass}(\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F})
. We stress that for the rest of the article, we will not use the space
\mathrm{Grass}(\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F})
. Only the subspace
\mathbb{G}^{0}
is of interest to us.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
x\in M
. The following holds

(1)

If
L\in\mathcal{G}^{0}_{x}
, then
L
is a Lie subalgebra and a Lie subgroup of

For Part 1, it is enough to show that if
x_{n}\to x
and
t_{n}\to 0
and
\ker(\natural_{x_{n},t_{n}})\to L
, where
L\in\mathrm{Grass}(V)
, then
\ker(\natural_{x,0})\subseteq L
. The map
\natural_{x,0}
preserves the grading on
V
and
\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}
. Hence, it is enough to consider
v\in\ker(\natural_{x,0})\cap V^{k}
for some
k
. Hence,
\natural(v)\in\mathcal{F}^{k-1}+I_{x}\mathcal{F}^{k}
. By Definition
1.4
, we get that

\natural(v)=\sum_{i\in I}f_{i}\natural(v_{i})+\sum_{j\in J}g_{j}\natural(w_{j}),

where
I,J
are some finite index sets,
v_{i}\in V^{\omega(i)}
with
\omega(i)<k
,
w_{j}\in V^{k}
,
f_{i},g_{j}\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
and
g_{j}(x)=0
. Since

v-\sum_{i\in I}f_{i}(x_{n})t_{n}^{k-\omega(i)}v_{i}-\sum_{j\in J}g_{j}(x_{n})w_{j}\in\ker(\natural_{x_{n},t_{n}})

and

v-\sum_{i\in I}f_{i}(x_{n})t_{n}^{k-\omega(i)}v_{i}-\sum_{j\in J}g_{j}(x_{n})w_{j}\xrightarrow{n\to+\infty}v,

it follows from Proposition
3.5
that
v\in L
.

Part 2 follows immediately from Part 1. For Part 3, let
(V,\natural)
and
(V^{\prime},\natural^{\prime})
be graded basis,
(x_{n},t_{n})\in M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}
such that
(x_{n},t_{n})\to(x,0)
and
\ker(\natural_{x_{n},t_{n}})\to\natural_{x,0}^{-1}(L)
. We need to show that
\ker(\natural^{\prime}_{x_{n},t_{n}})\to\natural_{x,0}^{\prime-1}(L)
. Since Grassmannian manifolds are compact, by passing to a subsequence we can suppose that
\ker(\natural^{\prime}_{x_{n},t_{n}})
converges, and by Part
1
that we just proved, the limit has to be of the form
\natural_{x,0}^{\prime-1}(L^{\prime})
for some
L^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Grass}(\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x})
. We need to show that
L=L^{\prime}
. Let
\phi
be as in Theorem
3.3
,

\psi:V\times M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\to V^{\prime},\quad\psi(v,y,t)=\frac{d}{ds}\Big\rvert_{s=0}\phi(sv,y,t).

Since
\phi
is smooth,
\psi
is smooth and linear in
v
. Let
v\in\ker(\natural_{y,t})
for
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}
. By Theorem
3.3
.2,

\exp(\natural_{y,t}^{\prime}(\phi(sv,y,t)))\cdot y=\exp(\natural_{y,t}(sv))\cdot y=y,\quad\text{for }s\text{ small enough}.

Hence, by Theorem
3.4
,
\psi(v,y,t)\in\ker(\natural_{y,t}^{\prime})
. Now let
l\in\natural_{x,0}^{-1}(L)
. By Proposition
3.5
, there exists
l_{n}\in\ker(\natural_{x_{n},t_{n}})
such that
l_{n}\to l
. Hence,
\psi(l_{n},x_{n},t_{n})\to\psi(l,x,0)
. We just proved that
\psi(l_{n},x_{n},t_{n})\in\ker(\natural^{\prime}_{x_{n},t_{n}})
. Hence, by Proposition
3.5
,
\psi(l,x,0)\in\natural_{x,0}^{\prime-1}(L^{\prime})
. By Theorem
3.3
, it follows that
\natural_{x,0}(l)=\natural^{\prime}_{x,0}(\psi(l,x,0))
. Hence,
\natural_{x,0}(l)\in L^{\prime}
. Since
\natural_{x,0}
is surjective, we proved that
L\subseteq L^{\prime}
. Since both have the same dimension, we get that
L=L^{\prime}
.

3.3.
Proof of Theorem
1.8

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\natural)
be a graded basis. The map
\natural_{x,0}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}
is a Lie algebra homomorphism because of (
20
). Hence, it is also a Lie group homomorphism. Let
x_{n}\in M
,
t_{n}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}
, such that
x_{n}\to x
,
t_{n}\to 0
,
\ker(\natural_{x_{n},t_{n}})\to\natural_{x,0}^{-1}(L)

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
g_{n}\in\mathfrak{g}
converge to
g\in\mathfrak{g}
,
y_{n}=\exp(\natural_{t_{n}}(g_{n}))\cdot x_{n}
. Then
y_{n}\to x
and

\ker(\natural_{y_{n},t_{n}})\to g\natural_{x,0}^{-1}(L)g^{-1}=\natural_{x,0}^{-1}\left(\natural_{x,0}(g)L\natural_{x,0}(g)^{-1}\right).

Proof.

The last equality follows from the fact that
\natural_{x,0}
is a Lie group homomorphism. Let
\phi
as in Theorem
3.2
. Consider the map
\psi:\dom(\psi)\subseteq\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\times M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathfrak{g}
defined by

\psi(g,v,y,t)=\frac{d}{ds}\Big\rvert_{s=0}\phi\Big(\phi(g,sv,y,t),-g,\exp(\natural_{t}(g))\cdot y,t\Big).

The domain of
\psi
is the set of points for which the right-hand side is well-defined. Let
v\in\ker(\natural_{y,t})
. In the next equation

z=\exp(\natural_{t}(g))\cdot y
, then

\displaystyle\exp\Big(\natural_{t}\Big(\phi(\phi(g,sv,y,t),-g,z,t)\Big)\Big)\cdot z

\displaystyle=\exp(\natural_{t}(\phi(g,sv,y,t)))\cdot\Big(\exp(\natural_{t}(-g))\cdot z\Big)

\displaystyle=\exp(\natural_{t}(\phi(g,sv,y,t)))\cdot y

\displaystyle=\exp(\natural_{t}(g))\cdot\Big(\exp(\natural_{t}(sv))\cdot y\Big)

\displaystyle=\exp(\natural_{t}(g))\cdot y=z.

It follows from Theorem
3.4
, that

\psi(g,v,y,t)\in\ker(\natural_{\exp(\natural_{t}(g))\cdot y,t}),\quad t\neq 0.

Now we can prove the lemma. It is clear that
y_{n}\to x
. By compactness of Grassmannian manifolds, we can by passing to a subsequence, suppose that
\ker(\natural_{y_{n},t_{n}})
converges, which by Proposition
1.6
.1 must be of the form
\natural_{x,0}^{-1}(L^{\prime})
for some
L^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}^{0}_{x}
. Let

3.5

v_{n}\in\ker(\natural_{x_{n},t_{n}})
such that
v_{n}\to v
. Notice that as

(g_{n},v_{n},x_{n},t_{n})\to(g,v,x,0)\in\dom(\psi)

∎

Lemma

.2. For Theorem

\natural_{x,0}

In this section, we prove that the groupoid
\mathbb{G}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{G}^{0}
is naturally a continuous family groupoid. Continuous family groupoids were introduced by Paterson
[
Pat00a
]
. We recall here the definition. We first need the following terminology

Definition 4.1
.

Let

be a topological space and

More generally let

and

. We say that

is
C^{\infty,0}
if
g
is
C^{\infty,0}
.

A continuous family groupoid with

(1)

The set

(2)

(3)

of pairs

, where
\Omega\subseteq G
is an open subset, and
\phi:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}\times G^{0}

and the following diagram commutes

(24)

(g_{n}L_{n},x_{n},0)\to(\natural_{x,0}(v)L,x,0)
by Proposition
2.2
. Furthermore,
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\natural_{x,0}(v)L,x,0)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}=1
by Theorem
6.4
. Hence,
\natural_{x,0}(v)L\neq L
and so
(\natural_{x,0}(v)L,x,0)
defines an element of
\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{G})
.∎

8.
Carnot-Carathéodory metric and Gromov-Hausdorff convergence

In this section, we suppose that we have a sub-Riemannian structure on
M
, i.e.,

(31)

\mathcal{F}^{i+1}=\mathcal{F}^{i}+[\mathcal{F}^{i},\mathcal{F}^{1}]

for all
i\geq 1
.

We recall the following notion. If
V,W
are finite dimensional vector spaces,
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
is a norm on
V
,
f:V\to W
is a surjective linear map, then the following formula

\left\lVert w\right\rVert:=\inf_{v\in f^{-1}(w)}\left\lVert v\right\rVert

defines a norm on
W
which we call the quotient norm by
f
.

Let
(V,\natural)
be a graded basis. We equip
V^{1}
with a Euclidean norm
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
. For
x\in M,t\neq 0
, we denote by
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{x,t}
the quotient norm on
\ev_{x}(\mathcal{F}^{1})
by

\natural_{x,t|V^{1}}:V^{1}\to\ev_{x}(\mathcal{F}^{1})=\{X(x):X\in\mathcal{F}^{1}\}.

It is clear that if
v\in\ev_{x}(\mathcal{F}^{1})
, then
\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{x,t}=\frac{1}{t}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{x,1}
.

If
c:I\to M
is an absolutely continuous path such that
c^{\prime}(s)\in\ev_{c(s)}(\mathcal{F}^{1})
for almost all
s\in I
, then we define

\mathrm{length}_{t}(c):=\int_{0}^{1}\left\lVert c^{\prime}(s)\right\rVert_{c(s),t}ds.

We also define a distance by

d_{t}(x,y)=\inf\mathrm{length}_{t}(c)

where the infimum is taken over all absolutely continuous paths from
x
to
y
. It is clear that

(32)

d_{t}(x,y)=\frac{d_{1}(x,y)}{t}.

Let
(L,x,0)\in\mathbb{G}
. Notice that (
31
) implies that

\mathcal{F}^{i+1}/L=\mathcal{F}^{i}/L+[\mathcal{F}^{i}/L,\mathcal{F}^{1}/L].

Hence,
\mathcal{F}^{i}/L
also defines a sub-Riemannian structure on
\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/L
. The derivative of the left
\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}
-action on
\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/L
, gives a linear map
\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}\to\mathcal{X}(\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/L)
. By composing this map with
\natural_{x,0}:V\to\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}
, we get a map
V\to\mathcal{X}(\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/L)
. The image of
V^{1}
is a family of generators for
\mathcal{F}^{1}/L
. Hence, for any
gL\in\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/L
, we have a surjective linear map

(33)

V^{1}\to\mathcal{F}^{1}/L\to\ev_{gL}(\mathcal{F}^{1}/L)=\{X(gL)\in T_{gL}\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/L:X\in\mathcal{F}^{1}/L\}.

The norm
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
on
V^{1}
, by (
33
), defines a quotient norm on
\ev_{gL}(\mathcal{F}^{1}/L)
, denoted by
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/L}
. If
c:I\to\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/L
is an absolutely continuous path with
c^{\prime}(s)\in\ev_{c(s)}(\mathcal{F}^{1}/L)
for almost all
s\in I
, then we define

\mathrm{length}_{t}(c):=\int_{0}^{1}\left\lVert c^{\prime}(s)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/L}ds.

We also define a distance by

d_{\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/L}(gL,hL)=\inf\mathrm{length}_{t}(c),

where the infimum is taken over all absolutely continuous paths from
gL
to
hL
. We remark that the way we defined the distance
d_{\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/L}
implies immediately that if
hL\in\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/L
, then right multiplication by
Lh^{-1}
is an isometry

\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/L\to\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/hLh^{-1}
. Hence,

(34)

d_{\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/L}(gL,hL)=d_{\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/hLh^{-1}}(gLh^{-1},hLh^{-1}),

where
L\in\mathcal{G}^{0}_{x},gL,hL\in\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/L
.

Theorem 8.1
.

The function

\displaystyle d_{\mathbb{G}}

\displaystyle:\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}

\displaystyle d_{\mathbb{G}}(y,x,t)

\displaystyle=d_{t}(y,x)\quad(y,x,t)\in M\times M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}

\displaystyle d_{\mathbb{G}}(gL,x,0)

\displaystyle=d_{\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/L}(gL,L),\quad(gL,x,0)\in\mathcal{G}\times\{0\}

is continuous, and homogeneous of degree
1
with respect to the Debord-Skandalis action, i.e.,

d{G}(\alpha_{\lambda}(\gamma))=\lambda d{G}(\gamma),\quad\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times},\gamma\in\mathbb{G}.

Proof.

Homogeneity follows from (
32
) and the trivial to check fact that
\alpha_{\lambda}
is an isometry from
(\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/L,d_{\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/L})
to
(\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/\alpha_{\lambda}(L),\lambda d_{\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/\alpha_{\lambda}(L)})
. Continuity of
d_{\mathbb{G}}
on
M\times M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}
follows from
[
Bel96a
, Corollary 2.6]
. By homogeneity, it is enough to prove continuity in a neighbourhood of
(L,x,0)
. The result follows from Theorem
4.4
and Proposition
5.2
and
[
ABB20a
, Theorem 3.56]
.
∎

Let
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
be a quasi-norm on
V
. Then
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}:\mathbb{G}^{0}\to[0,+\infty]
and
d_{\mathbb{G}}
are
1
-homogeneous which vanish only on
\mathbb{G}^{0}
. Hence, the quotient
\frac{{\left|\kern-0.75346pt\left|\kern-0.75346pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-0.75346pt\right|\kern-0.75346pt\right|}}{d_{\mathbb{G}}}
descends to a map

\frac{{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}}{d_{\mathbb{G}}}:\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{G})\to]0,+\infty].

Let
\mathbb{U}
be an open set like in Proposition
2.3
. Let
\mathbb{P}:\mathbb{G}\backslash\mathbb{G}^{0}\to\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{G}^{0})
be the quotient map. It is open because it is the quotient by a group action. Hence,
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{Q}_{V}(\mathbb{U})\backslash\mathbb{G}^{0})
is open. By Theorem
6.4
, we obtained an open neighbourhood of
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{G})
on which
\frac{{\left|\kern-0.75346pt\left|\kern-0.75346pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-0.75346pt\right|\kern-0.75346pt\right|}}{d_{\mathbb{G}}}
is continuous. Since
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{G})=\pi^{-1}(\Delta_{M})
, where
\pi:\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{G})\to M\times M
the map from Theorem
7.1
, we thus obtain the following

Theorem 8.2
.

Let
x\in M
. Then there exists a compact neighbourhood
K\subseteq M\times M
of
(x,x)
and
C\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}
such that on
\pi^{-1}(K)
, one has

C^{-1}\leq\frac{{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}}{d_{\mathbb{G}}}\leq C

So if
x_{0}
is fixed, and
y
and
x
are close enough to
x_{0}
, then one has the inequality

C^{-1}d_{1}(y,x)\leq\inf\{{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\in V:y=\exp(\natural(v))\cdot x\}\leq Cd_{1}(y,x).

Theorem 8.3
.

Let
(x_{n},t_{n})\in M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}
be a sequence which converges in
\mathbb{G}^{0}
to
(L,x,0)
. Then the pointed metric spaces
(M,d_{t_{n}},x_{n})=(M,\frac{d_{1}}{t_{n}},x_{n})
converge in the pointed Gromov-Hausdorff sense to
(\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/L,d_{\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/L},L)
.

Proof.

In the proof, if
(X,d)
is a metric space,
x\in X
,
r\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, then
B_{X}(x,r)
or
B_{d}(x,r)
denote the closed ball of radius
r
and center
x
.

Lemma 8.4
.

Let
(X_{n},d_{n})
,
(X,d)
be metric spaces,
x_{n}\in X_{n}
. Suppose that
(X_{n},d_{n})
are length metric spaces, and for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have a map
\phi_{n}:X\to X_{n}
such that

(1)

\phi_{n}
is injective and

\sup_{x,y\in X}|d(x,y)-d_{n}(\phi_{n}(x),\phi_{n}(y))|\xrightarrow{n\to+\infty}0

(2)

There exists a sequence
R_{n}>0
such that
\phi_{n}(X)\subseteq B_{X_{n}}(x_{n},R_{n})
and
R_{n}\to R
for some
R>0
.

(3)

For each
R^{\prime}<R
, there exists
m
such that for
n>m
,
B_{X_{n}}(x_{n},R^{\prime})\subseteq\phi_{n}(X)
.

Then
(B_{X_{n}}(x_{n},R),d_{n})
converges in the Gromov-Hausdorff distance to
(X,d)
.

Recall that a length metric space
[
Gro99a
, Definition 1.7 on Page 6]
is a metric space
(X,d)
such that for any
x,y\in X
and
\epsilon>0
, there exists a continuous path
l:[0,1]\to X
such that
l(0)=x
and
l(1)=y
and the length of
l
is less than or equal to
d(x,y)+\epsilon
.

Proof.

By replacing
R_{n}
with
\max(R_{n},R)
we can suppose that
R_{n}\geq R
for all
n
. Let
d_{GH}
and
d_{H}
denote the Gromov-Hausdorff and Hausdorff distance respectively. Condition
1
implies that

\lim_{n\to+\infty}d_{GH}((X,d),(\im(\phi_{n}),d_{n}))=0.

Let
R^{\prime}<R
. Take
n
big enough so that Condition
3
is satisfied. One has

d_{H}(\im(\phi_{n}),B_{X_{n}}(x_{n},R_{n}))\leq d_{H}(B_{X_{n}}(x_{n},R^{\prime}),B_{X_{n}}(x_{n},R_{n}))\leq R_{n}-R^{\prime}

where the last inequality follows from the fact that
X_{n}
is a length space.
Hence,

\lim_{n\to+\infty}d_{GH}((\im(\phi_{n}),d_{n}),(B_{X_{n}}(x_{n},R_{n}),d_{n}))=0.

Since
X_{n}
is a length space,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}d_{GH}((B_{X_{n}}(x_{n},R_{n}),d_{n}),(B_{X_{n}}(x_{n},R),d_{n}))=0.

The result follows from triangle inequality.
∎

Let
(\Omega,\psi)
be a chart of
\mathbb{G}
such that
(L,x,0)\in\Omega
. Without loss of generality, we suppose that
\psi(\Omega)=U\times V
where
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{\dim(M)}
is an open subset and
V\subseteq\mathbb{G}^{0}
is an open neighbourhood of
(L,x,0)\in\mathbb{G}^{0}
. We can further suppose that
(x_{n},t_{n})\in V
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We denote by
p:U\times V\to U
the projection onto
U
.

Now choose any
R>0
small enough such that
B_{\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/L}(L,R)\times\{(x,0)\}
is a compact subset of
\Omega
. Let
\phi_{n}:B_{\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/L}(L,R)\to M
defined by

(\phi_{n}(gL),x_{n},t_{n})=\psi^{-1}\Big(p(\psi(gL,x,0)),x_{n},t_{n}\Big).

By construction
(\phi_{n}(gL),x_{n},t_{n})\to(gL,x,0)
for all
gL\in B_{\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/L}(L,R)
. By Theorem
2.5
,

(\phi_{n}(gL),\phi_{n}(hL),t_{n})\to(gLh^{-1},x,0),\quad\forall gL,hL\in B_{\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/L}(L,R).

By Theorem
8.1
and (
34
), it follows that

(35)

d_{t_{n}}(\phi_{n}(gL),\phi_{n}(hL))\to d_{\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/L}(gL,hL),\quad\forall gL,hL\in B_{\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/L}(L,R).

The limit (
35
) is uniform in
gL,hL
.
To see this, suppose that it is not. Since
B_{\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/L}(L,R)
is compact, we can construct subsequences
(g_{n}L)_{n\in\mathbb{N}},(h_{n}L)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq B_{\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/L}(L,R)
such that
g_{n}L\to gL
and
h_{n}L\to hL
yet
d_{t_{n}}(\phi_{n}(g_{n}L),\phi_{n}(h_{n}L))

does not converge to
d_{\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/L}(gL,hL)
. It is clear from the definition of
\phi_{n}
that
(\phi_{n}(g_{n}L),x_{n},t_{n})\to(gL,x,0)
. The same holds for
hL
. Hence, by Theorem
2.5
, Theorem
8.1
and (
34
)
d_{t_{n}}(\phi_{n}(g_{n}L),\phi_{n}(h_{n}L))
converges to
d_{\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/L}(gL,hL)
which is a contradiction.

Let
R_{n}=\sup\{d_{t_{n}}(\phi_{n}(gL),x_{n}):gL\in B_{\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/L}(L,R)\}
. We have

d_{t_{n}}(\phi_{n}(gL),x_{n})\to d_{\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/L}(gL,L),\quad\forall gL\in B_{\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/L}(L,R).

The limit is also uniform by an argument similar to before. Hence,
R_{n}\to R
. We now check the last condition of Lemma
8.4
. Let
R^{\prime}<R
. Suppose that the last condition is not satisfied. Hence, there exists
y_{n}\in B_{d_{t_{n}}}(x_{n},R^{\prime})
such that
y_{n}\notin\mathrm{Im}(\phi_{n})
. Clearly
y_{n}\to x
. By Theorem
8.2
and Proposition
2.2
, there exists a subsequence of
(y_{n},x_{n},t_{n})
which converges to
(gL,x,0)\in\mathbb{G}
for some
gL
. By Theorem
8.1
,
gL\in B_{\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/L}(L,R^{\prime})
. Hence,
(gL,x,0)\in\Omega
. So
(y_{n},x_{n},t_{n})\in\Omega
for
n
big enough. Then
\psi(y_{n},x_{n},t_{n})=(a_{n},x_{n},t_{n})
and
a_{n}\to a
with
\psi(gL,x,0)=(a,L,x,0)
. Since
R^{\prime}<R
, it follows that
\psi^{-1}(a_{n},L,x,0)=(h_{n}L,x,0)
with
h_{n}L\in B_{\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/L}(L,R)
for
n
big enough. Hence,
y_{n}=\phi_{n}(h_{n}L)
, which is a contradiction.

By Lemma
8.4
, we obtain that
B_{d_{t_{n}}}(x_{n},R)
converges to
B_{\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/L}(L,R)
. In fact all the above works with
R^{\prime}<R
. Since
\mathcal{G}^{0}_{x}
is compact, we deduce that there exists
R>0
such that any sequence

(y_{n},s_{n})\in M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}
converging to an element
(K,x,0)\in\mathbb{G}^{0}
and for any
R^{\prime}\leq R
, one has

\lim_{n\to+\infty}B_{d_{s_{n}}}(y_{n},R^{\prime})=B_{\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/K}(K,R^{\prime}).

By using the
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}
action on
\mathbb{G}^{0}
, we deduce that for all
R\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}
,

\lim_{n\to+\infty}B_{d_{s_{n}}}(y_{n},R)=B_{\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/K}(K,R).

Hence,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}(M,d_{s_{n}},y_{n})=(\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/K,d_{\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}/K},K).

This finishes the proof.
∎

Drawing Diestel-Leader graphs in 3D
Amandine Escalier

Abstract
In this short note we give some code to represent Diestel-Leader graphs in 3D. The code is written in TikZ.



Figure 1
:
Two views of the Diestel-Leader graph
DL(3,2)

The history of Diestel-Leader graphs takes its root in the following question asked
by Woess
[
Woe91
]
: is every connected locally finite vertex-transitive graph
quasi-isometric to some Cayley graph? In the hope of answering no to this
question, Diestel and Leader
[
DL01
]
defined
what we now call
Diestel-Leader graphs
. However it was only later, in the
famous papers of Eskin, Fisher an Whyte
[
EFW07
,
EFW12
]
that it was showed that
some of the aforementioned graphs are not quasi-isometric to any Cayley graph.

In this note we give a code to draw these graphs in 3D, using TikZ. Readers only
interested by producing an illustration of some
DL(p,q)
can jump to the last
pages of this article (or the end of the .tex file) and copy-paste the
code given in
section

2.2
, write the wanted values of
p
and
q
(line 29) and
then compile. Readers wishing to change the code can rely on the description
made in
section

2.1
. We start this note with a short reminder of the
definition of Diestel-Leader graphs.

1
Diestel-Leader graphs

We recall here the definition of Diestel-Leader graphs.
We refer to
[
Woe91
, Section 2]

for more details.

1.1
Tree and horocycles

Let
q\geq 2
and denote by
\mathbf{T}=T_{q}
the homogenous tree of degree
q+1
. Denote by

d
the usual graph distance on
\mathbf{T}
fixing to
1
the length of an edge.

A
geodesic ray
is an infinite sequence
(v_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of vertices
of
\mathbf{T}
such that
d(v_{i},v_{j})=|i-j|
, for all
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
. We say that two
rays are equivalent if ther symmetric difference
1
1

1

Recall that the
symmetric difference of two sets
A
and
B
is defined by
|A\triangle B|=(A\backslash B)\cup(B\backslash A)
is finite.
We call
end
of
T_{q}
an equivalence class of rays in
\mathbf{T}
and denote
by
\partial\mathbf{T}
the space of ends of
\mathbf{T}
.

Let
\hat{\mathbf{T}}:=\partial\mathbf{T}\cup\mathbf{T}
. For any elements
x,y\in\hat{\mathbf{T}}
there is a unique geodesic in
\hat{T}
, denoted by
\overline{xy}
,
that connects
x
and
y
.

Now fix an end
w\in\partial\mathbf{T}
, the
confluent
of two elements

x,y\in\hat{\mathbf{T}}\backslash{w}
with respect to
w
, denoted by
x\curlywedge y
, is
defined as the element
c=x\curlywedge y
such that
\overline{xw}\cap\overline{yw}=\overline{cw}
, that is to say: the confluent is
the point where the two geodesics
\overline{xw}
and
\overline{yw}
towards

w
meet (see
fig.

2
).

Figure 2
:
Example of the confluent of two points in
T_{2}

Now, fix a root vertex
o\in\mathbf{T}
. We define below a
Busemann
function
, which will allow us to endow our tree with some height notion.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
w\in\partial T_{q}
. The
Busemann function
with respect to
w

is the map
\mathfrak{h}:\mathbf{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
defined by

\mathfrak{h}(x)=d(x,x\curlywedge o)-d(o,x\curlywedge o).

Example 1.2
.

Let’s turn back to
fig.

2
and let
o=y
be the root. Then for
x

represented in the figure, we have
x\curlywedge o=c
and thus

d(x,x\curlywedge o)=2
and
d(o,x\curlywedge o)=1
. Therefore
\mathfrak{h}(x)=1
.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
w\in\partial T_{q}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. The
horocycle
with respect
to
w
, denoted by
H_{k}
, is the set
H_{k}=\left\{x\in\mathbf{T}:\mathfrak{h}(x)=k\right\}
.

We refer to
fig.

3
for an illustration.
Note that every horocycle in
\hat{T}
is infinite. Every vertex
x
in a
horocycle
H_{k}
has exactly one neighbour
x^{-}
(called the

predecessor
) in
H_{k-1}
and exactly
q
neighbours
(called
successors
) in
H_{k+1}
(see

fig.

3
for an illustration).

Figure 3
:
Horocycles, predecessor, successors

1.2
Diestel-Leader graphs

Now fix
p,q\geq 2
and consider
T_{p}
and
T_{q}
with respective roots
o_{p}

and
o_{q}
, and respective reference ends
w_{p}
and
w_{q}
.

Definition 1.4
.

The Diestel-Leader graph
DL(p,q)
is the graph whith set of vertices

V\left(DL(p,q)\right)=\big\{(x,y)\in T_{p}\times T_{q}:\mathfrak{h}(x)=-\mathfrak{h}(y)\big\}.

and where there is an edge between two elements
(x_{1},y_{1})
and
(x_{2},y_{2})
,
if and only if
(x_{1},x_{2})
is an edge in
T_{p}
and
(y_{1},y_{2})
is an edge in

T_{q}
.

If there is an edge between two vertices
(x_{1},y_{1})
and

(x_{2},y_{2})
in
DL(p,q)
, then remark that either

•

x_{2}
is one of the
p
childs of
x_{1}
in
\mathbf{T}_{p}
and in this case

y_{2}
is the only predecessor of
y_{1}
in
\mathbf{T}_{q}
, namely
y_{2}=y^{-}_{1}
(see
fig.

4(a)
);

•

or
x_{2}=x^{-}_{1}
is the unique predecessor of
x_{1}
in
\mathbf{T}_{p}

and in this case
y_{2}
is one of the
q
childs of
y_{1}
in
\mathbf{T}_{q}
(see

fig.

4(b)
).

We represent these two cases in
fig.

4
. The edge is drawn in

blue
, the
p
-regular tree in

orange
and the
q
-regular tree in

brown
. In light blue is represented the Diestel-Leader
graph, we refer to
fig.

5
for a
drawing of
DL(p,q)
itself. Note that in
fig.

5
, the
corresponding
T_{p}
and
T_{q}
are drawn on the planes
x=0
and
y=0

respectively.

(a)

When
x_{2}
is a child of
x_{1}
and
y_{2}=y^{-}_{1}

(b)

When
y_{2}
is a child of
y_{1}
and
x_{2}=x^{-}_{1}

Remark 1.5
.

When
p=q
the graph
DL(p,q)
is a Cayley graph of the Lamplighter group

\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\wr\mathbb{Z}
.

Structure of the code

The code is composed of one main loop cut in two parts: the first one that draws
the tree on the plane
y=0
, and the second one that draws simultaneously the
tree located on the plane
x=0
and the Diestel-Leader graph.

In this description “height” will stand for the
z
-coordinates in the picture.

Lines 58 to 72

We start by drawing the regular tree of degree
p

represented on the plane
y=0
, in orange in the pictures. This tree grows from
the bottom to the top.

At height
h
there are

p^{{\color[rgb]{0.4,0.6448,0.727}h}}
vertices to draw and the
space between two consecutive nodes at this height is

{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}\mathrm{pspace}}=p^{\text{layers-{\color[rgb]{0.4,0.6448,0.727}h}}}

(in particular at the top, when
{\color[rgb]{0.4,0.6448,0.727}h}=layers
, the
space between two nodes is equal to
1
).

Moreover the first horizontal node at height
{\color[rgb]{0.4,0.6448,0.727}h}
has for
horizontal coordinates the middle of

[0,p^{\mathrm{layers}-{\color[rgb]{0.4,0.6448,0.727}h}}-1]
that is to say

p^{\mathrm{layers}-{\color[rgb]{0.4,0.6448,0.727}h}}/2-0.5={\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}\mathrm{pspace}}/2-0.5,

and therefore, if
{\color[rgb]{0.4,0.6448,0.727}h}\neq layers
, the first of its
child (at height
{\color[rgb]{0,0.408,0.545}n}={\color[rgb]{0.4,0.6448,0.727}h}+1
)
has coordinates the middle of

[0,p^{\mathrm{layers}-{\color[rgb]{0,0.408,0.545}n}}-1]
, namely

p^{\mathrm{layers}-{\color[rgb]{0.4,0.6448,0.727}h}}/(2p)-0.5={\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}\mathrm{pspace}}/(2p)-0.5
.

Figure 6
:
Ranges of the different loop variables for the first tree

Now fix
n\in\{1,\ldots,\text{layers}\}
, the loop
starting at line
58
goes over all the nodes at height

{\color[rgb]{0.4,0.6448,0.727}h}={\color[rgb]{0,0.408,0.545}n}-1
and draws the
edges between a node at height
n-1
and its
q
childs at height
n
. To do so, it
fixes the horizontal position
k\in\{1,\ldots,p^{n-1}-1\}

of the chosen node at height
n-1
. This node thus has to be shifted to the
right by
{\color[rgb]{0.4167,0.245,0.075}pshift}={\color[rgb]{0.8334,0.49,0.15}k}*{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}\mathrm{pspace}}

and hence drawn at the coordinates

({\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}\mathrm{pspace}}-0.5+{\color[rgb]{0.8334,0.49,0.15}k}*{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}\mathrm{pspace}},0,n-1)=({\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}\mathrm{pspace}}-0.5+{\color[rgb]{0.4167,0.245,0.075}pshift},0,n-1).

The childs are then located at height
{\color[rgb]{0,0.408,0.545}n}
. Similarly
as above, the first child to be drawn has for horizontal coordinates the middle of

[0,q^{\mathrm{layers}-{\color[rgb]{0,0.408,0.545}n}-1}-1]
and the
“
\backslash\mathrm{child}
-th” is shifted
to right by
\mathrm{child}*{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}\mathrm{pspace}}
. Hence the
coordinates of the “
\backslash\mathrm{child}
-th” child is given by

({\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}\mathrm{pspace}}/q-0.5+{\color[rgb]{0.4167,0.245,0.075}pshift}+\mathrm{child}*{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}\mathrm{pspace}},0,n-1).

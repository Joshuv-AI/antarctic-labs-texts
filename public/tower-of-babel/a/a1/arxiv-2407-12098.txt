Boundary fractional Hardy’s inequality in dimension one: The critical case
Adimurthi, Purbita Jana, Prosenjit Roy

Abstract
. We prove fractional boundary Hardy’s inequality in dimension one for the critical case s ​ p = 1 sp=1 . Optimality of the inequality is obtained for any p p . The extra logarithmic correction term appears in usual fashion. We also provide a concrete (workable) example of a sequence of smooth functions that converges to constant function in W s , p ​ ( ( , , , ) ) W^{s,p}((0,1)) for s ​ p = 1 sp=1 and p = 2 p=2 .

Poincaré Inequality
Let
a>0
,
p\geq 1
,
s\in(0,1)
then for each
u\in C^{1}{([0,a])}
, one has

\int_{0}^{a}|u(s)-(u)_{(0,a)}|^{p}ds\leq C(s,p,a)[u]_{s,p,(0,a)}^{p}.

The following lemma is Lemma 2.2 in
[
27
]
with choice of
a=1=d
and
sp=1
.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
Sobolev Inequality for the critical case] Let
sp=1
and
D=(\lambda r,\lambda R)
. For any
\tau>0
and
\lambda,r,R>0
, there exist a constant
C=C(r,R,s,p)>0
such that

\left(\fint_{r\lambda}^{R\lambda}|u(x)-(u)_{D}|^{\tau}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\tau}}\leq C[u]_{s,p,D}.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
E=(a,b),F=(b,c)\subset\mathbb{R}
be disjoint. Then for any
\tau\geq 1
one has for some constant
C>0
,
\forall u\in C^{1}([a,c]),

|(u)_{F}-(u)_{E}|\leq\frac{C(c-a)}{\min\{(b-a),(c-b)\}}[u]_{s,p,E\cup F}.

Proof.

Let us start with the expression
|(u)_{F}-(u)_{E}|^{\tau}
.

|(u)_{F}-(u)_{E}|^{\tau}=\left|\fint_{F}\{u(x)-(u)_{E\cup F}\}dx-\fint_{E}\{u(x)-(u)_{E\cup F}\}dx\right|^{\tau}\\
\leq C\left|\fint_{F}\{u(x)-(u)_{E\cup F}\}dx\right|^{\tau}+C\left|\fint_{E}\{u(x)-(u)_{E\cup F}dx\}\right|^{\tau}.

Now applying Jensen’s inequality (this is the step where the assumption
\tau\geq 1
is required) we have

|(u)_{F}-(u)_{E}|^{\tau}\leq C\fint_{F}\left|u(x)-(u)_{E\cup F}\right|^{\tau}dx+C\fint_{E}\left|u(x)-(u)_{E\cup F}\right|^{\tau}dx.

This implies

|(u)_{F}-(u)_{E}|^{\tau}\leq\frac{C}{\min\{|E|,|F|\}}\int_{E\cup F}\left|u(x)-(u)_{E\cup F}\right|^{\tau}dx\\
\leq\frac{C|E\cup F|}{\min\{|E|,|F|\}}\fint_{E\cup F}\left|u(x)-(u)_{E\cup F}\right|^{\tau}dx.

Applying lemma
2.1
, with
\lambda=1,r=a
and
R=c,
we have

\left(\fint_{E\cup F}|u(x)-(u)_{E\cup F}|^{\tau}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\tau}}\leq C[u]_{s,p,E\cup F},\hskip 8.53581pt\forall u\in C^{1}([a,c]).

Finally combining (
2
) and (
2
) the result follows.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

[An Inequality]
Let
\Lambda,\tau>1
. There exist
C(\Lambda,\tau)>0,
such that for all
1<D_{\tau}<\Lambda
, one has

\left(|a|+|b|\right)^{\tau}\leq D_{\tau}|a|^{\tau}+\frac{C}{(D_{\tau}-1)^{\tau-1}}|b|^{\tau}.

The proof of the above inequality is done in lemma 2 of
[
28
]
, but we give a short here for the completeness of this article.

Proof.

Putting
x=|a|/|b|
, it is sufficient to prove that

(1+x)^{\tau}\leq D_{\tau}x^{\tau}+\frac{C}{(D_{\tau}-1)^{\tau-1}},\hskip 8.53581ptx>0.

Consider the function
f(x)=(1+x)^{\tau}-D_{\tau}x^{\tau}-\frac{C}{(D_{\tau}-1)^{\tau-1}}
. The above inequality is trivially true for
x\in(0,1]
for choice of
C
large enough. Notice that
f(1)<0
and
\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}f(t)=-\infty
. Also there exist unique
x_{0}=(D_{\tau}^{\frac{1}{\tau-1}}-1)^{-1}\in(1,\infty)
, such that
f^{\prime}(x_{0})=0
and
f(x_{0})<0
. All this together proves the inequality.
∎

Now we state and give a sketch of the proof of another elementary lemma that will be used several times later on.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
m_{i}\geq 0
and
\lambda\in(0,1]
, then

\left(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}m_{i}\right)^{\lambda}\leq\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}m_{i}^{\lambda}.

Proof.

The proof is by induction argument. For
i=2
, due to homogeneity, it is sufficient to prove that the function
f(x)=(x+1)^{\lambda}-x^{\lambda}-1
is non-positive on the set
x\geq 0
. But this follows easily after observing
f(0)=0
and
f^{\prime}(x)<0
for
x>0
. Then assuming the inequality to be true for
i=n
, it is easy to prove that it is true for
i=n+1
by writing

\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}m_{i}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}\right)+m_{n+1}

and using the first case. Finally the proof follows by taking limit
n
to infinity.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

The following theorem will be used to proof our main theorem
1.3
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.3
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{|\delta_{x}|^{\gamma}u}{\log(2/\delta_{x})}\right\|_{L^{\tau}((0,1/2))}\leq C\left([u]_{W^{s,p}((0,1))}^{p}+|u|_{p,(0,1)}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\ \ \forall\,u\in C^{1}([0,1]),

where
\delta_{x}=dist(x,(0,1)^{c}).

Let us finish the proof of our main theorem using the Theorem
3.1
, then we will provide the proof of it.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
First observe that it is sufficient to proof the required inequality for functions in
C^{1}([0,1])
as it is a dense subset of
W^{s,p}((0,1)).

Applying Theorem
3.1
on
u-(u)_{(0,1)}\in C^{1}{([0,1])}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{|\delta_{x}|^{\gamma}(u-(u)_{(0,1)}}{\log(2/\delta_{x})}\right\|_{L^{\tau}((0,1/2))}\leq C\left([u-(u)_{(0,1)}]_{W^{s,p}((0,1))}^{p}+|u-(u)_{(0,1)}|_{p,(0,1)}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Applying Poincaré inequality and using

[u-(u)_{(0,1)}]_{W^{s,p}((0,1))}=[u]_{W^{s,p}((0,1))},

we get

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{|\delta_{x}|^{\gamma}(u-(u)_{(0,1)})}{\log(2/\delta_{x})}\right\|_{L^{\tau}((0,1/2))}\leq C[u-(u)_{(0,1)}]_{W^{s,p}((0,1))},\ \ \forall u\in C^{1}([0,1]).

Now independently, notice that using the change of variable
1-x=t
, one has

\left\|\frac{|\delta_{x}|^{\gamma}(u-(u)_{(0,1)})}{\log(2/\delta_{x})}\right\|_{L^{\tau}((1/2,1))}=\left\|\frac{|\delta_{t}|^{\gamma}(u-(u)_{(0,1)})}{\log(2/\delta_{t})}\right\|_{L^{\tau}((0,1/2))}.

We write

\left\|\frac{|\delta_{x}|^{\gamma}(u-(u)_{(0,1)})}{\log(2/\delta_{x})}\right\|_{L^{\tau}((0,1))}^{\tau}=\left\|\frac{|\delta_{x}|^{\gamma}(u-(u)_{(0,1)})}{\log(2/\delta_{x})}\right\|_{L^{\tau}((0,1/2))}^{\tau}+\left\|\frac{|\delta_{x}|^{\gamma}(u-(u)_{(0,1)})}{\log(2/\delta_{x})}\right\|_{L^{\tau}((1/2,1))}^{\tau}:=A+B.

We can apply directly the previous theorem (Theorem
3.1
) on the term
A
to get the required right hand, while an analogous version of Theorem
3.1
can also be proved for interval
(1/2,1)
to estimate the term
B
.. Combining them the proof is completed.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
Define the set
A_{k}:=\left\{x\ \big|\ 2^{k}\leq x<2^{k+1}\right\}
for
k\in\{-1,-2,\cdots\}:=\mathbb{Z}^{-}
. Notice that

(0,1)=\underset{k=-\infty}{\overset{-1}{\cup}}A_{k}.

Applying Sobolev inequality for the critical case with

r=1,R=2,\lambda=2^{k}
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}
and
D:=A_{k}
, we have for some constant
C>0
(independent of
k
),

(3.1)

\left(\frac{1}{2^{k}}\int_{A_{k}}|u-(u)_{A_{k}}|^{\tau}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\tau}}\leq C[u]_{s,p,A_{k}}.

Independently, using convexity we have for some constant
C>0
,

(3.2)

|u|^{\tau}\leq C\left\{|u-(u)_{A_{k}}|^{\tau}+|(u)_{A_{k}}|^{\tau}\right\}.

Using the condition
\tau\gamma=-1
, we obtain

\int_{A_{k}}|x|^{\tau\gamma}|u|^{\tau}dx\leq 2^{\tau\gamma(k+1)}|A_{K}|\fint_{A_{k}}|u|^{\tau}dx=C\fint_{A_{k}}|u|^{\tau}dx.

Now applying (
3.1
) and (
3.2
),

\int_{A_{k}}|x|^{\tau\gamma}|u|^{\tau}dx\leq\fint_{A_{k}}|(u)_{A_{k}}|^{\tau}dx+C[u]_{s,p,A_{k}}^{\tau},\hskip 8.53581pt\forall k\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}.

Let
k\leq-2
. For each
x\in A_{k}
, we have
2^{k}<(\delta_{x}=)x<2^{k+1}.
This implies that
2/x>2^{-k}
and hence
\log(2/x)>(-k)\log(2)
. Therefore, we have

\int_{A_{k}}\frac{|\delta_{x}|^{\tau\gamma}|u|^{\tau}}{\log^{\tau}(2/\delta_{x})}dx=\int_{A_{k}}\frac{|x|^{\tau\gamma}|u|^{\tau}}{\log^{\tau}(2/x)}dx\leq C\left\{\frac{|(u)_{A_{k}}|^{\tau}}{(-k)^{\tau}}+\frac{[u]_{s,p,A_{k}}^{\tau}}{(-k)^{\tau}}\right\}.

Since
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}
, we have trivially

\int_{A_{k}}\frac{|\delta_{x}|^{\tau\gamma}|u|^{\tau}}{\log^{\tau}(2/\delta_{x})}dx\leq C\left\{\frac{|(u)_{A_{k}}|^{\tau}}{(-k)^{\tau}}+[u]_{s,p,A_{k}}^{\tau}\right\}.

Summing the above inequality from
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}
to
-2
, we obtain after using Lemma
2.4
with
\lambda=p/\tau\leq 1
,

(3.3)

\int_{2^{m}}^{1/2}\frac{|\delta_{x}|^{\tau\gamma}|u|^{\tau}}{\log^{\tau}(2/\delta_{x})}dx=\sum_{k=m}^{-2}\int_{A_{k}}\frac{|x|^{\tau\gamma}|u|^{\tau}}{\log^{\tau}(2/x)}dx\leq C\left\{\sum_{k=m}^{-2}\frac{|(u)_{A_{k}}|^{\tau}}{(-k)^{\tau}}+\sum_{k=m}^{-2}[u]_{s,p,A_{k}}^{\tau}\right\}\\
\leq C\sum_{k=m}^{-2}\frac{|(u)_{A_{k}}|^{\tau}}{(-k)^{\tau}}+C[u]_{s,p,(0,1)}^{\tau}.

Now independently, applying lemma
2.2
with
E=A_{k}
and
F=A_{k+1},
we get for some constant
C>0
(independent of
k
)

|(u)_{A_{k}}|^{\tau}\leq\left|(u)_{A_{k+1}}+C[u]_{s,p,A_{k}\cup A_{k+1}}\right|^{\tau}.

For
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}\setminus\{-1\}
, consider the numbers
D_{\tau}^{k}:=\left(\frac{-k}{-k-(1/2)}\right)^{\tau-1}>1.
Observe that
D_{\tau}^{k}\leq 2.
Now applying lemma
2.3
with
D_{\tau}=D_{\tau}^{k},\Lambda=2^{\tau-1}
we obtain

(3.4)

|(u)_{A_{k}}|^{\tau}\leq\left(\frac{-k}{-k-(1/2)}\right)^{\tau-1}\left|(u)_{A_{k+1}}\right|^{\tau}+C(-k)^{\tau-1}[u]_{s,p,A_{k}\cup A_{k+1}}^{\tau}.

In the above estimate we have used the fact that

D_{\tau}^{k}-1\sim\frac{1}{-k}.

Dividing both sides of (
3.4
) by
(-k)^{\tau-1}
, we have

\frac{|(u)_{A_{k}}|^{\tau}}{(-k)^{\tau-1}}\leq\frac{(u)_{A_{k+1}}^{\tau}}{(-k-(1/2))^{\tau-1}}+C[u]_{s,p,A_{k}\cup A_{k+1}}^{\tau}.

Summing the above inequality from

k=m
to
k=-2
, we have

\sum_{k=m}^{-2}\frac{|(u)_{A_{k}}|^{\tau}}{(-k)^{\tau-1}}\leq\sum_{k=m}^{-2}\frac{(u)_{A_{k+1}}^{\tau}}{(-k-(1/2))^{\tau-1}}+C\sum_{k=m}^{-2}[u]_{s,p,A_{k}\cup A_{k+1}}^{\tau}.

Changing sides, rearranging and re-indexing, we get

(3.5)

\frac{|(u)_{A_{m}}|^{\tau}}{(-m)^{\tau-1}}+\sum_{k=m+1}^{-2}\left\{\frac{1}{(-k)^{\tau-1}}-\frac{1}{(-k+(1/2))^{\tau-1}}\right\}|(u)_{A_{k}}|^{\tau}\leq C(\tau)|(u)_{A_{-1}}|^{\tau}+C\sum_{k=m}^{-2}[u]_{s,p,A_{k}\cup A_{k+1}}^{\tau}.

Now using the asymptotics,

\frac{1}{(-k)^{\tau-1}}-\frac{1}{(-k+(1/2))^{\tau-1}}\sim\frac{1}{(-k)^{\tau-1}}.

Putting this in (
3.5
),

(3.6)

\sum_{k=m}^{-2}\frac{|(u)_{A_{k}}|^{\tau}}{(-k)^{\tau-1}}\leq C(\tau)|(u)_{A_{-1}}|^{\tau}+C\sum_{k=m}^{-2}[u]_{s,p,A_{k}\cup A_{k+1}}^{\tau}\leq C(\tau)|(u)_{A_{-1}}|^{\tau}+C[u]_{s,p,(0,1)}^{\tau}.

In the last inequality we have used Lemma
2.4
with
\lambda=p/\tau\leq 1,m_{i}=[u]_{s,p,A_{i}\cup A_{i+1}}^{\tau}
for
i=m,m+1,\cdots,-2
and remaining
m_{i}=0
.
Independently we have after using Hölder’s inequality,

(3.7)

(u)_{A_{-1}}=2\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}u(s)ds\leq 2\int_{0}^{1}|u(s)|ds\leq 2|u|_{p,(0,1)}.

Combining (
3.6
), (
3.3
) together with (

), we get

(3.8)

\left(\int_{2^{m}}^{1/2}\frac{|\delta_{x}|^{\tau\gamma}|u|^{\tau}}{\log^{\tau}(2/\delta_{x})}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\tau}}\leq\left(C^{\prime}(\tau)|u|_{p,(0,1)}^{\tau}+C[u]_{s,p,(0,1)}^{\tau}\right)^{\frac{1}{\tau}}\leq\left(C^{\prime\prime}(\tau)|u|_{p,(0,1)}^{p}+C^{\prime}[u]_{s,p,(0,1)}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

In the last inequality we have again used Lemma

(with only two non zero values of

) with

Finally taking limit

4.
Density Theorem

First we prove the density theorem as mentioned in the introduction section, as some part of the calculation with the sequence of test functions
u_{\epsilon}
(see below) will be required for us in the proof of optimality part in Theorem
1.3
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
sp=1,p=2
then the following sequence of function converges to constant function
1
in
W^{s,p}(0,1)
:

(4.1)

u_{\epsilon}(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{|\log\epsilon|}{|\log x|},\hskip 22.76219ptx\in(0,\epsilon)\\
1,\hskip 36.98857ptx\in(\epsilon,1-\epsilon),\\
\frac{|\log(1-\epsilon)|}{|\log(1-x)|},\hskip 8.53581ptx\in(1-\epsilon,1).\end{cases}

Proof.

We want to show that

[u_{\epsilon}-1]_{s,p,(0,1)}+|u_{\epsilon}-1|_{p,(0,1)}\rightarrow 0\ \textrm{as}\ \epsilon\rightarrow 0.

|u_{\epsilon}-1|_{p,(0,1)}\rightarrow 0
follows from application Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem. Since
[u_{\epsilon}-1]_{s,p,(0,1)}=[u_{\epsilon}]_{s,p,(0,1)}
, it is sufficient to show
[u_{\epsilon}]_{s,p,(0,1)}\rightarrow 0
. Using change of variable, definition and symmetry of the
u_{\epsilon}
, we can write

[u_{\epsilon}]_{s,p,(0,1)}=2[u_{\epsilon}]_{s,p,(0,\epsilon)}+2\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\int_{\epsilon}^{1-\epsilon}\frac{|u_{\epsilon}(x)-u_{\epsilon}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{2}}dxdy:=I_{\epsilon}+K_{\epsilon}.

Let us first estimate the term
I_{\epsilon}.

Change the variable as
\log x=-s,\ \log y=-t
, to get

(4.2)

I_{\epsilon}=(\log\epsilon)^{2}\int_{|log\epsilon|}^{\infty}\int_{|\log\epsilon|}^{\infty}\frac{(s-t)^{2}}{(st)^{2}(e^{-s}-e^{-t})^{2}}e^{-s}e^{-t}dsdt\\
=(\log\epsilon)^{2}\int_{|log\epsilon|}^{\infty}\int_{|\log\epsilon|}^{\infty}\frac{(s-t)^{2}}{(st)^{2}(e^{s}-e^{t})^{2}}e^{s}e^{t}dsdt=(\log\epsilon)^{2}\int_{|log\epsilon|}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{2}}\int_{|log\epsilon|}^{\infty}\frac{1}{s^{2}}\frac{(s-t)^{2}}{(e^{s-t}+e^{t-s}-2)}dsdt\\
=(\log\epsilon)^{2}\int_{|log\epsilon|}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{2}}\int_{|log\epsilon|}^{t}\frac{1}{s^{2}}\frac{(s-t)^{2}}{(e^{s-t}+e^{t-s}-2)}dsdt+(\log\epsilon)^{2}\int_{|log\epsilon|}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{2}}\int_{t}^{|log\epsilon|}\frac{1}{s^{2}}\frac{(s-t)^{2}}{(e^{s-t}+e^{t-s}-2)}dsdt\\
:=I_{1}^{\epsilon}+I_{2}^{\epsilon}.

Now introducing the change of variable
s-t=x
for a fixed
t
in
I_{2}
first to get

I_{2}^{\epsilon}=(\log\epsilon)^{2}\int_{|log\epsilon|}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{g(x)}{(t+x)^{2}}dx\right)ds,

where
g(x)=\frac{x^{2}}{e^{x}+e^{-x}-2}.
Note that
g(x)\rightarrow 1
as
x\rightarrow 0
and
g(x)\leq Cx^{2}e^{-x}
. This implies that

\int_{0}^{\infty}g(x)dx<\infty.

Therefore

I_{2}^{\epsilon}\leq C(\log\epsilon)^{2}\int_{|log\epsilon|}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{4}}=\frac{C}{|\log\epsilon|}\rightarrow 0.

Using the same change of variable for
I_{1}^{\epsilon}
and
t+x\geq|\log\epsilon|
we obtain

I_{1}^{\epsilon}=(\log\epsilon)^{2}\int_{|log\epsilon|}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{2}}\left(\int_{|\log\epsilon|-t}^{0}\frac{g(x)}{(t+x)^{2}}dx\right)dt\leq\int_{|log\epsilon|}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{2}}\left(\int_{|\log\epsilon|-t}^{0}g(x)dx\right)dt.

Since
g
is an even function and we already know that
\int_{0}^{\infty}g(x)dx<\infty
, we have finally

I_{1}^{\epsilon}\leq C\int_{|log\epsilon|}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{2}}=\frac{C}{|\log\epsilon|}\rightarrow 0,\ \textrm{as}\ \epsilon\rightarrow 0.

Now let us turn to the estimate of
K_{\epsilon}.

(4.3)

K_{\epsilon}=\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\int_{\epsilon}^{1-\epsilon}\frac{(\log\epsilon-\log x)^{2}}{\log^{2}x(x-y)^{2}}dydx=\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\frac{(\log\epsilon-\log x)^{2}}{\log^{2}x}\left[\frac{1}{x-y}\right]_{\epsilon}^{1-\epsilon}dx\\
=\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\frac{(\log\epsilon-\log x)^{2}}{\log^{2}x}\left\{\frac{1}{x-1+\epsilon}-\frac{1}{x-\epsilon}\right\}dx=(1-2\epsilon)\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\frac{(\log\epsilon-\log x)^{2}}{(\log^{2}x)(\epsilon-x)(1-x-\epsilon)}dx.

Note that
1-x-\epsilon\geq 1-2\epsilon
, this implies

K_{\epsilon}\leq\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\frac{(\log\epsilon-\log x)^{2}}{(\log^{2}x)(\epsilon-x)}dx.

Change the variable as
\log x=-s
to get

\displaystyle K_{\epsilon}\leq\int_{|\log\epsilon|}^{\infty}\frac{(\log\epsilon+s)^{2}}{s^{2}(\epsilon-e^{-s})}e^{-s}ds=\int_{|\log\epsilon|}^{\infty}\frac{(\log\epsilon+s)^{2}}{s^{2}(\epsilon e^{s}-1)}ds.

Now again change the variable as
t=s+\log\epsilon
, to get

K_{\epsilon}\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{2}}{(t-\log\epsilon)^{2}(e^{t}-1)}dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{2}}{(t+|\log\epsilon|)^{2}(e^{t}-1)}dt\leq\frac{1}{\log^{2}\epsilon}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{2}}{(e^{t}-1)}dt\rightarrow 0.

The proof of the theorem is completed after noticing that the last integral is finite.
∎

Optimality of the weight function:
We just treat the case
p=2
, the case of general
p
can be treated with usual estimates and considering the same sequence of test function
u_{\epsilon}
. Consider the sequence of function
u_{\epsilon}
as in (
4.1
). We have already proved in Theorem
4.1
(after combining all the estimates therein), that

\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(u_{\epsilon}(x)-u_{\epsilon}(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{2}}dxdy\leq\frac{C}{|\log\epsilon|}+o\left(\frac{1}{|\log\epsilon|}\right).

For our notation
o(\delta)
is a term such that
\frac{o(\delta)}{\delta}\rightarrow 0.

Now we claim that the inequality in the above step is actually an equality. To prove this it is sufficient to prove that

I_{1}^{\epsilon},\ I_{2}^{\epsilon}\geq\frac{C}{|\log\epsilon|}.

But this is true because

I_{2}^{\epsilon}\geq(\log\epsilon)^{2}\int_{|log\epsilon|}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\frac{g(x)}{(t+x)^{2}}dx\right)dt\geq(\log\epsilon)^{2}/4\int_{|log\epsilon|}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{4}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}g(x)dx\right)dt.

In the last step we have used
t+x\leq 2t
first and then
g(x)\geq 0
and
t\geq|\log(\epsilon)|\geq 1
. Using similar estimates the other inequality also follows. Now let us calculate the following expression:

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(u_{\epsilon}-\bar{u_{\epsilon}})^{2}}{\delta_{x}(\log\delta_{x})^{2}}dx=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(u_{\epsilon}-\bar{u_{\epsilon}})^{2}}{\min\{x,1-x\}\log^{2}(\min\{x,1-x\})}dx,

where

\bar{u_{\epsilon}}=(u_{\epsilon})_{(0,1)}.

First let us calculate the term

\bar{u_{\epsilon}}=2\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\frac{|\log\epsilon|}{|\log x|}dx+\int_{\epsilon}^{1-\epsilon}dx=2|\log\epsilon|\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\frac{dx}{|\log x|}+1-2\epsilon=1+C_{\epsilon}.

Also note that the first term
C_{\epsilon}
goes faster to zero than
\epsilon
, after making an estimate
|\log x|\geq|\log\epsilon|.
Now let us first estimate the term

(4.4)

\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\frac{(u_{\epsilon}-\bar{u_{\epsilon}})^{2}}{x\log^{2}x}dx=\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\frac{u_{\epsilon}^{2}}{x\log^{2}x}dx+\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\frac{\bar{u_{\epsilon}}^{2}}{x\log^{2}x}dx-2\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\frac{u_{\epsilon}\bar{u_{\epsilon}}}{x\log^{2}x}dx\\
=\log^{2}\epsilon\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\frac{dx}{x\log^{4}x}+\bar{u_{\epsilon}}^{2}\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\frac{dx}{x\log^{2}x}-2\bar{u_{\epsilon}}\log\epsilon\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\frac{dx}{x\log^{3}x}.

After making change of the variable as
\log x=z
, we get finally after “actual intergration”,

\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\frac{(u_{\epsilon}-\bar{u_{\epsilon}})^{2}}{x\log^{2}x}dx=\frac{1}{3|\log\epsilon|}+\frac{\bar{u_{\epsilon}}^{2}}{|\log\epsilon|}-\frac{\bar{u_{\epsilon}}}{|\log\epsilon|}=\frac{1}{3|\log\epsilon|}+o\left(\frac{1}{|\log\epsilon|}\right).

Now using (
4
) we will estimate the following term (we estimate until
\frac{1}{2}
as the other half can be estimated in similar way):

\int_{\epsilon}^{.5}\frac{(u_{\epsilon}-\bar{u_{\epsilon}})^{2}}{x\log^{2}x}dx=C_{\epsilon}^{2}\int_{\epsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{dx}{x\log^{2}x}=KC_{\epsilon}^{2}.

Note from
C_{\epsilon}^{2}\rightarrow 0
faster than
\epsilon^{2}
(faster than
\frac{1}{|log\epsilon|}
), hence this term is of no importance. Therefore we have

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(u_{\epsilon}-\bar{u_{\epsilon}})^{2}}{\delta_{x}\log^{2}\delta_{x}}dx=\frac{1}{3|\log\epsilon|}+o\left(\frac{1}{|\log\epsilon|}\right).

Assume
\tau=p=2
. Now suppose that there exist a function,
f
with the property that
f(x)\rightarrow\infty
as
x\rightarrow 0+
and there is a further improvement of the inequality in Theorem
1.3
in the sense that the following holds:

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{f(x)|\delta_{x}|^{\gamma}(u-(u)_{(0,1)})}{\log(2/\delta_{x})}\right\|_{L^{\tau}((0,1))}\leq C[u]_{W^{s,p}((0,1))},\ \ \forall\,u\in W^{s,p}((0,1)).

Consider the sequence of function
u_{\epsilon}
as given in theorem
4.1
. Using similar type of computation as done above, it is easy to show that

(4.5)

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(u_{\epsilon}-\bar{u_{\epsilon}})^{2}}{\delta_{x}\log^{2}\delta_{x}}dx\geq\frac{f(\epsilon)}{3|\log\epsilon|}+o\left(\frac{f(\epsilon)}{|\log\epsilon|}\right).

This implies as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
,

[u_{\epsilon}]_{1/2,2,(0,1)}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(u_{\epsilon}-\bar{u_{\epsilon}})^{2}}{\delta_{x}\log^{2}\delta_{x}}dx\right)^{-1}\leq\frac{C}{f(\epsilon)}\rightarrow 0,

and therefore clearly (
4.5
) cannot be true. Similar argument will hold if
f\rightarrow\infty
as
x\rightarrow 1-
.

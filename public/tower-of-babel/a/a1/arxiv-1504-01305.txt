Guanlong Bao, Hasi Wulan and Fangqin Ye

Abstract
. Using the Cauchy-Riemann operator, we characterize 𝒬 K \mathcal{Q}_{K} spaces, Besov spaces and analytic Morrey spaces in terms of pseudoanalytic extensions of primitive functions. Our results are also true on some classical Banach spaces, such as the Bloch space, B ​ M ​ O ​ A BMOA and the Dirichlet space. Keywords : the Cauchy-Riemann operator; pseudoanalytic extension; primitive functions; 𝒬 K \mathcal{Q}_{K} spaces; Besov spaces; Morrey spaces.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{D}
be the unit disk in the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
.
Denote by
H(\mathbb{D})
the space of functions analytic in
\mathbb{D}
. The Green function
in the unit disk with singularity at
a\in\mathbb{D}
is given by

g(a,z)=\log\frac{1}{|\sigma_{a}(z)|},\ z\in\mathbb{D}.

Here

\sigma_{a}(z)=\frac{a-z}{1-\overline{a}z},

is a Möbius transformation of
\mathbb{D}
.

Throughout this paper, we
assume that
K:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is a
right-continuous and increasing function. A function

f\in H(\mathbb{D})
belongs to the space
\mathcal{Q}_{K}
if

\|f\|_{\mathcal{Q}_{K}}^{2}=\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\,\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}K\left(g(a,z)\right)dA(z)<\infty,

where
dA(z)
is the Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{D}
.
By
[
14
, Theorem 2.1]
,
f\in\mathcal{Q}_{K}
if and only if

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}K\left(1-|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2}\right)dA(z)<\infty.

See
[
14
]
and

[
15
]
for more results of
\mathcal{Q}_{K}
spaces. If

K(t)=t^{p}
,
0\leq p<\infty
, then the space
\mathcal{Q}_{K}
gives the space

\mathcal{Q}_{p}
(see
[
5
,
29
,
30
]
). In particular,
\mathcal{Q}_{0}
is the Dirichlet space
\mathcal{D}
;

\mathcal{Q}_{1}=BMOA
, the space of bounded mean oscillation (see
[
6
,
17
]
); by
[
3
]
, for all
p\in(1,\infty)
, the spaces
\mathcal{Q}_{p}
are the same and equal to the Bloch space
\mathcal{B}
which consists of all functions
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
with

\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}}=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})|f^{\prime}(z)|<\infty.

For
0<p<\infty
, the Hardy space
H^{p}
consists of all functions
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
with

\|f\|_{H^{p}}^{p}=\sup_{0<r<1}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})|^{p}d\theta<\infty.

It is well-known that if
f\in H^{p}
, then its nontangential limit
f(e^{i\theta})
exists almost everywhere (see
[
9
,
16
]
).

In this paper, we need the Cauchy-Riemann operator

\overline{\partial}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+i\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right),\ z=x+iy.

Dyn’kin
[
13
]
said that the so-called pseudoanalytic extension is a method of extension with a given estimate of the Cauchy-Riemann operator.
Dyn’kin
[
13
]
characterized many classical smoothness spaces applying the pseudoanalytic extension method. Note that

\bigcup_{0<p<1}\mathcal{Q}_{p}\subseteq BMOA\subseteq\bigcap_{0<q<\infty}H^{q}.

Dyakonov and Girela
[
12
]
obtained an interesting characterization of
\mathcal{Q}_{p}
spaces in terms of pseudoanalytic extension as follows.

Theorem A
.

If
0<p<1
and
f\in\bigcap_{0<q<\infty}H^{q}
, then the following conditions are equivalent.

(i)

f\in\mathcal{Q}_{p}
.

(ii)

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}\left(\frac{1}{|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2}}-1\right)^{p}dA(z)<\infty.

(iii)

There exists a function
\widetilde{f}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}})
satisfying

\widetilde{f}(z)=O(1),\ \text{as}\ \ z\rightarrow\infty,

\lim_{r\rightarrow 1^{+}}\widetilde{f}(re^{i\theta})=f(e^{i\theta}),\ \text{a.e. and in}\ L^{q}([-\pi,\ \pi])\ \text{for all}\ \ q\in[1,\infty),

and

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}}|\overline{\partial}\widetilde{f}(z)|^{2}\left(|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2}-1\right)^{p}dA(z)<\infty.

The proof of the above theorem in
[
12
]
involved the Calderón-Zygmund operators and the Muckenhoupt weights (see
[
19
]
).
For
a\in\mathbb{D}
and
0<p<1
, let

U_{a}(z)=\left|1-\frac{1}{|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2}}\right|^{p}=\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{p}||z|^{2}-1|^{p}}{|z-a|^{2p}},\ z\in\mathbb{C}.

Dyakonov and Girela
[
12
]
showed that
U_{a}
is a
Muckenhoupt weight with

\sup_{Q}\left[\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}U_{a}(z)dA(z)\right]\left[\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}(U_{a}(z))^{-1}dA(z)\right]<\infty

for all
a\in\mathbb{D}
. Here
Q
ranges over the disks in
\mathbb{C}
and
|Q|
denotes the area of
Q
. The above estimate of
U_{a}
with
a=0
was also used to establish the
\mathcal{Q}_{p}
corona theorem (see

[
28
, Theorem 3.1]
). Applying Theorem A, Dyakonov and Girela
[
12
]
also gave some nice properties of
\mathcal{Q}_{p}
spaces.

Motivated by Theorem A, it is nature to ask some questions as follows.

Question 1.
Can we obtain pseudoanalytic extensions for all
\mathcal{Q}_{p}
,
0\leq p<\infty
?

Question 2.
Let
n
be a positive integer. Does there exist a pseudoanalytic extension characterization of
\mathcal{Q}_{p}
spaces such that the extension function is
O(z^{n})
as
z\rightarrow\infty
?

Question 3.
Can we give characterizations for some spaces of analytic functions such as
\mathcal{Q}_{K}
spaces, Besov spaces and analytic Morrey spaces in terms of pseudoanalytic extensions?

Throughout this article, denote by
F
the primitive function of
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
; that is

F(z)=\int_{0}^{z}f(w)dw,\ z\in\mathbb{D}.

Related to the above questions, in this paper, we characterize some analytic function spaces on
\mathbb{D}
by pseudoanalytic extension of primitive functions. Our method, without using
Calderón-Zygmund operators and Muckenhoupt weights, can be applied to some spaces, such as
\mathcal{Q}_{K}
spaces, Besov spaces, analytic Morrey spaces and so on. Our results are also new for
\mathcal{Q}_{p}
spaces.

In this paper, the symbol
A\approx B
means that
A\lesssim B\lesssim A
. We say that
A\lesssim B
if there exists a constant
C
such that
A\leq CB
.

2.
\mathcal{Q}_{K}
spaces and pseudoanalytic extension

In this section, we need two more constraints on
K
as follows.

[formula]

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\varphi_{K}(s)}{s}ds<\infty

and

[formula]

\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi_{K}(s)}{s^{1+q}}ds<\infty,\ \ \ \ 0<q<2,

where

\varphi_{K}(s)=\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}\frac{K(st)}{K(t)},\ \ \ \ \ 0<s<\infty.

Under conditions (2.1) and (2.2),
\mathcal{Q}_{K}
spaces have been studied extensively (see
[
24
,
25
,
26
]
). From now on we always assume the function
K
satisfying the double condition, namely
K(2t)\approx K(t)
for all
t\in(0,1)
.

A very useful tool in the study of
\mathcal{Q}_{K}
spaces is
K
-Carleson
measure. Let
\ell(I)
be the length of an arc
I
of the unit circle
\partial\mathbb{D}
. Define the
Carleson box by

S_{G}(I)=\begin{cases}\{r\zeta\in G:1-\frac{\ell(I)}{2\pi}<r<1,\zeta\in I\},&G=\mathbb{D},\\
\{r\zeta\in G:1<r<1+\ell(I),\zeta\in I\},&\kern 5.0ptG=\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}.\end{cases}

Following
[
15
]
and
[
24
]
, a positive Borel measure
\mu
on
G=\mathbb{D}
or
G=\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
is said to be a

K
-Carleson measure if

\sup_{I\subset\partial\mathbb{D}}\int_{S_{G}(I)}K\left(\frac{|1-|z||}{\ell(I)}\right)d\mu(z)<\infty.

By
[
14
]
, we know that all
\mathcal{Q}_{K}
spaces are subsets of the Bloch space. Then the primitive function
F
of a
\mathcal{Q}_{K}
function
f
must be in the Hardy space
H^{2}
since the Bloch functions’ Taylor coefficients is bounded (see
[
1
]
). Therefore, the primitive function
F
has its nontangential limit
F(e^{i\theta})
almost everywhere on the unit circle.

The following is the main result of this section.

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose that
K
satisfies (2.1) and (2.2). Let
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
with its primitive function
F\in H^{2}
and let
n\geq 2
be an integer. Then the following conditions are equivalent.

(i)

f\in\mathcal{Q}_{K}
.

(ii)

There exists a function
\widetilde{F_{n}}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}})
satisfying

[formula]

\lim_{r\rightarrow 1^{+}}\widetilde{F_{n}}(re^{i\theta})=F(e^{i\theta})\ a.e.\ \theta\in[0,2\pi],

[formula]

\widetilde{F_{n}}(z)=O(z^{n}),\ \text{as}\ \ z\rightarrow\infty,

[formula]

\overline{\partial}\widetilde{F_{n}}(z)=O(z^{n-2}),\ \text{as}\ \ z\rightarrow\infty,

and

[formula]

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}}\frac{|\overline{\partial}\widetilde{F_{n}}(z)|^{2}}{(|z|^{n}-1)^{2}}K\left(1-\frac{1}{|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2}}\right)dA(z)<\infty.

(iii)

There exists a function
\widetilde{F_{n}}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}})
satisfying (2.3), (2.4), (2.5) and
|\overline{\partial}\widetilde{F_{n}}(z)|^{2}/(|z|^{n}-1)^{2}
is a
K
-Carleson measure on
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
.

Before embarking into the proof of Theorem 2.1, we state some lemmas below.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that
K
satisfies (2.1). Let
\mu
be a positive Borel measure on
\mathbb{D}
. Then the following conditions are equivalent.

(i)

\mu
is a
K
-Carleson measure on
\mathbb{D}
.

(ii)

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}K(1-|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2})d\mu(z)<\infty.

(iii)

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}K\left(\frac{1}{|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2}}-1\right)d\mu(z)<\infty.

Proof.

Note that
(i)\Leftrightarrow(ii)
was proved in
[
15
]
. To show the equivalence of
(ii)
and
(iii)
, let

K_{1}(t)=K\left(\frac{t}{1-t}\right),\ 0<t<\frac{1}{2}.

By the monotonicity of
K
,
K_{1}(s)
is also increasing for
s\in(0,1)
. Moreover,

K(t)\leq K\left(\frac{t}{1-t}\right)\leq K(2t),\ 0<t<\frac{1}{2}.

Since
K(2t)\approx K(t)
for
t\in(0,1)
,
K_{1}(t)\approx K(t)
for
t\in(0,1/2)
. Thus, the proof is complete. ∎

Wulan and Zhu
[
27
]
proved that if
K
satisfies (2.1) and
n
is a positive integer, then
f\in\mathcal{Q}_{K}
if and only if

[formula]

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{(n)}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{2n-2}K\left(1-|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2}\right)dA(z)<\infty.

Combining this with Lemma 2.2, we obtain immediately a characterization of
\mathcal{Q}_{K}
spaces as follows. If
K
satisfies (2.1) and
n
is a positive integer, then
f\in\mathcal{Q}_{K}
if and only if

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{(n)}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{2n-2}K\left(\frac{1}{|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2}}-1\right)dA(z)<\infty.

For the case of
\mu
on
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
, we give a similar description of
K
-Carleson measure as follows.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that
K
satisfies (2.1). Let
\mu
be a positive Borel measure on
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. Then the following conditions are equivalent.

(i)

\mu
is a
K
-Carleson measure on
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
.

(ii)

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{1<|z|<1+2\pi}K\left(|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2}-1\right)d\mu(z)<\infty.

(iii)

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{1<|z|<1+2\pi}K\left(1-\frac{1}{|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2}}\right)d\mu(z)<\infty.

Proof.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
. Fix
a=re^{i\theta}\in\mathbb{D}
. Let
I
be the arc of center
e^{i\theta}
and
\ell(I)=\frac{2\pi(1-|a|)}{2\pi|a|+1}
. Then for any
z\in S_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}}(I)
, one gets

|1-\bar{a}z|\geq 1-(1+\ell(I))|a|=\frac{1}{2\pi}\ell(I)

and

\displaystyle|1-\bar{a}z|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|a|\left|e^{i\left(\theta+\frac{\ell(I)}{2}\right)}-\frac{1}{\bar{a}}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r\left|e^{i\frac{\ell(I)}{2}}-\frac{1}{r}\right|=\left[(1-r)^{2}+4r\left(\sin\frac{\ell(I)}{4}\right)^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left[\left(\frac{2\pi r+1}{2\pi}\ell(I)\right)^{2}+4r\left(\frac{\ell(I)}{4}\right)^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq 2\ell(I).

Hence

|1-\bar{a}z|\approx 1-|a|\approx\ell(I),\ \ z\in S_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}}(I).

Set

S_{n}=\left\{r\zeta\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}:1<r<1+\ell(2^{n}I),\zeta\in 2^{n}I\right\},

where
2^{n}I
denotes the arc with the same center as
I
and with length
2^{n}\ell(I)
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{1<|z|<1+2\pi}K\left(|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2}-1\right)d\mu(z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{S_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}}(I)}K\left(|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2}-1\right)d\mu(z)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{S_{n}\backslash S_{n-1}}K\left(|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2}-1\right)d\mu(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{S_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}}(I)}K\left(\frac{|z|-1}{\ell(I)}\right)d\mu(z)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{S_{n}\backslash S_{n-1}}K\left(\frac{|z|-1}{2^{2n}\ell(I)}\right)d\mu(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{S_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}}(I)}K\left(\frac{|z|-1}{\ell(I)}\right)d\mu(z)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sup_{z\in S_{n}}\frac{K\left(\frac{|z|-1}{2^{2n}\ell(I)}\right)}{K\left(\frac{|z|-1}{2^{n}\ell(I)}\right)}\int_{S_{n}}K\left(\frac{|z|-1}{\ell(2^{n}I)}\right)d\mu(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{S_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}}(I)}K\left(\frac{|z|-1}{\ell(I)}\right)d\mu(z)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{K}(2^{-n})\int_{S_{n}}K\left(\frac{|z|-1}{\ell(2^{n}I)}\right)d\mu(z).

Since
d\mu
is a
K
-Carleson measure and

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{K}(2^{-n})\thickapprox\int_{0}^{1}\varphi_{K}(s)\frac{ds}{s}<\infty,

we obtain that

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{1<|z|<1+2\pi}K\left(|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2}-1\right)d\mu(z)<\infty.

(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)
. It follows from that

K\left(1-\frac{1}{|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2}}\right)\leq K\left(|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2}-1\right)

holds for all
a\in\mathbb{D}
and
1<|z|<1+2\pi
.

(iii)\Rightarrow(i)
. For given a subarc
I\in\partial\mathbb{D}
, set
e^{i\theta}
the midpoint of
I
and set

a=\frac{2\pi-\ell(I)}{2\pi(\ell(I)+1)}e^{i\theta}.

Then for any
z\in S_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}}(I)
, we have

0<|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2}-1\leq\frac{\frac{1}{\pi}(2\pi|a|+1)(2+\ell(I))\left(\ell(I)\right)^{2}}{\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\ell(I)\right)^{2}}\leq 8\pi(2\pi+1)(\pi+1).

Let

K_{2}(t)=K\left(\frac{t}{1+t}\right),\ 0<t<8\pi(2\pi+1)(\pi+1).

By the monotonicity of
K
,
K_{2}(s)
is also increasing for
s\in(0,1)
. Moreover,

K\left(\frac{t}{8\pi(2\pi+1)(\pi+1)+1}\right)\leq K_{2}(t)\leq K(t),\ 0<t<8\pi(2\pi+1)(\pi+1).

Since
K(2t)\approx K(t)
for
t\in(0,1)
,

K_{2}(t)\approx K(t)
for
t\in(0,8\pi(2\pi+1)(\pi+1))
. Thus,

\displaystyle\int_{S_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}}(I)}K\left(\frac{|z|-1}{\ell(I)}\right)d\mu(z)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\int_{S_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}}(I)}K\left(|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2}-1\right)d\mu(z)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\int_{S_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}}(I)}K\left(\frac{|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2}-1}{|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2}}\right)d\mu(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{1<|z|<1+2\pi}K\left(1-\frac{1}{|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2}}\right)d\mu(z).

Hence, (i) follows.
∎

From
[
15
]
and
[
25
]
, if
K
satisfies (2.1) and (2.2), then there exists a small enough constant
c
,
0<c<q/2
, such that
K(t)/t^{c}
is increasing and
K(t)/t^{q-c}
is decreasing in (0, 1). To prove Theorem 2.1, we also need the following estimate.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let (2.1) and (2.2) hold for
K
. If
s<\min(1+c,2-q+c)
and
2s+r-4\geq 0
, then

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{K\left(1-|\sigma_{a}(w)|^{2}\right)}{(1-|w|^{2})^{s}|1-\overline{w}z|^{r}}dA(w)\lesssim\frac{K\left(1-|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2}\right)}{(1-|z|^{2})^{s+r-2}}

for all
a,z\in\mathbb{D}
. Here
c
is a small enough positive constant depending only on (2.1) and (2.2).

Proof.

For fixed
a
,
z\in\mathbb{D}
, let
\lambda=\sigma_{z}(a)
. Then

|\sigma_{a}(w)|=|\sigma_{\lambda}\circ\sigma_{z}(w)|.

Note that
2s+r-4\geq 0
. Checking the proof of Lemma 2.1 in
[
7
]
, one gets

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{K\left(1-|\sigma_{a}(w)|^{2}\right)}{(1-|w|^{2})^{s}|1-\overline{w}z|^{r}}dA(w)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{K\left(1-|\lambda|^{2}\right)}{(1-|z|^{2})^{s+r-2}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\varphi_{K}\left(\frac{1-|u|^{2}}{|1-\overline{\lambda}u|^{2}}\right)\frac{1}{(1-|u|^{2})^{s}}dA(u).

Since
K
satisfies (2.1) and (2.2), there exists a small enough positive constant
c
depending only on (2.1) and (2.2) (see
[
15
,
25
]
), such that

\varphi_{K}(t)\lesssim t^{c},\ 0<t\leq 1

and

\varphi_{K}(t)\lesssim t^{q-c},\ t\geq 1.

Thus,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\varphi_{K}\left(\frac{1-|u|^{2}}{|1-\overline{\lambda}u|^{2}}\right)\frac{1}{(1-|u|^{2})^{s}}dA(u)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|u|^{2})^{c-s}}{|1-\overline{\lambda}u|^{2c}}dA(u)+\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|u|^{2})^{q-c-s}}{|1-\overline{\lambda}u|^{2q-2c}}dA(u).

For
s<1+c<q+1-c
and
s<2-q+c
, using Lemma 3.10 in
[
35
]
, we get

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|u|^{2})^{c-s}}{|1-\overline{\lambda}u|^{2c}}dA(u)+\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|u|^{2})^{q-c-s}}{|1-\overline{\lambda}u|^{2q-2c}}dA(u)\lesssim 1.

Thus,

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{K\left(1-|\sigma_{a}(w)|^{2}\right)}{(1-|w|^{2})^{s}|1-\overline{w}z|^{r}}dA(w)\lesssim\frac{K\left(1-|\lambda|^{2}\right)}{(1-|z|^{2})^{s+r-2}}\approx\frac{K\left(1-|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2}\right)}{(1-|z|^{2})^{s+r-2}}.

∎

Now we are ready to finish the proof of Theorem 2.1.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
. From now on, we write

w^{*}=1/\overline{w},\ w\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}.

Let
f\in\mathcal{Q}_{K}
and

F(z)=\int_{0}^{z}f(\zeta)d\zeta,\ z\in\mathbb{D}.

Set

\widetilde{F_{n}}(z)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{i}}{i!}(z^{*}-z)^{i}F^{(i)}(z^{*}),\ z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}.

Clearly,
\widetilde{F_{n}}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}})
satisfying

\lim_{r\rightarrow 1^{+}}\widetilde{F_{n}}(re^{i\theta})=F(e^{i\theta})\ \ a.e.\ \theta\in[0,2\pi],

and

\widetilde{F_{n}}(z)=O(z^{n}),\ \text{as}\ \ z\rightarrow\infty.

Note that

\overline{\partial}\widetilde{F_{n}}(z)=\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n!}(z^{*}-z)^{n}(z^{*})^{2}F^{(n+1)}(z^{*}).

Then

\overline{\partial}\widetilde{F_{n}}(z)=O(z^{n-2}),\ \text{as}\ \ z\rightarrow\infty.

Making the change of variable
z=\zeta^{*}
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}}\frac{\left|\overline{\partial}\widetilde{F_{n}}(z)\right|^{2}}{(|z|^{n}-1)^{2}}K\left(1-\frac{1}{|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2}}\right)dA(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(n!)^{2}}\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}}\frac{|z^{*}-z|^{2n}|z^{*}|^{4}\left|f^{(n)}(z^{*})\right|^{2}}{(|z|^{n}-1)^{2}}K\left(1-\frac{1}{|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2}}\right)dA(z)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left|f^{(n)}(\zeta)\right|^{2}(1-|\zeta|)^{2n-2}K\left(1-|\sigma_{a}(\zeta)|^{2}\right)dA(\zeta).

Combining this with (2.7), we get the desired result.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
. Assume that (ii) holds. Let
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
R>1
. Following a technique in
[
10
]
or
[
12
]
and in view of (2.3), we employ the Cauchy-Green formula to the function that equals
F
in
\mathbb{D}
and
\widetilde{F_{n}}
in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. Then

[formula]

F(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|\zeta|=1}\frac{F(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}d\zeta=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|\zeta|=R}\frac{\widetilde{F_{n}}(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}d\zeta-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{1<|\zeta|<R}\frac{\overline{\partial}\widetilde{F_{n}}(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}dA(\zeta).

By (2.4),

\int_{|\zeta|=R}\frac{\widetilde{F_{n}}(\zeta)}{(\zeta-z)^{n+2}}d\zeta\rightarrow 0,\ \text{as}\ R\rightarrow\infty.

By (2.5), we know

\left|\int_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}}\frac{\overline{\partial}\widetilde{F_{n}}(\zeta)}{(\zeta-z)^{n+2}}dA(\zeta)\right|<\infty.

These together with (2.8) give

F^{(n+1)}(z)=-\frac{(n+1)!}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}}\frac{\overline{\partial}\widetilde{F_{n}}(\zeta)}{(\zeta-z)^{n+2}}dA(\zeta).

By the Hölder inequality, the change of variable and the well known estimates in Zhu’s book
[
35
, Lemma 3.10]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|F^{(n+1)}(z)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}}\frac{1}{|\zeta-z|^{4}}dA(\zeta)\int_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}}\frac{|\overline{\partial}\widetilde{F_{n}}(\zeta)|^{2}}{|\zeta-z|^{2n}}dA(\zeta)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{1}{|1-\overline{w}z|^{4}}dA(w)\int_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}}\frac{|\overline{\partial}\widetilde{F_{n}}(\zeta)|^{2}}{|\zeta-z|^{2n}}dA(\zeta)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}}\frac{|\overline{\partial}\widetilde{F_{n}}(\zeta)|^{2}}{|\zeta-z|^{2n}}dA(\zeta).

Using Lemma 2.4,
n\geq 2
and (2.6), we see that

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})^{2n-2}\left|F^{(n+1)}(z)\right|^{2}K\left(1-|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2}\right)dA(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}}\frac{|\overline{\partial}\widetilde{F_{n}}(\zeta)|^{2}}{|\zeta-z|^{2n}}dA(\zeta)(1-|z|^{2})^{2n-4}K\left(1-|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2}\right)dA(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{K\left(1-|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2}\right)}{|1-\overline{w}z|^{4}}dA(z)|\overline{\partial}\widetilde{F_{n}}(w^{*})|^{2}|w|^{2n-4}dA(w)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{|\overline{\partial}\widetilde{F_{n}}(w^{*})|^{2}|w|^{2n-4}}{(1-|w|^{2})^{2}}K\left(1-|\sigma_{a}(w)|^{2}\right)dA(w)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}}\frac{|\overline{\partial}\widetilde{F_{n}}(\zeta)|^{2}}{(|\zeta|^{n}-1)^{2}}K\left(1-\frac{1}{|\sigma_{a}(\zeta)|^{2}}\right)dA(\zeta)<\infty.

Note that
F^{\prime}(z)=f(z)
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
. By (2.7), we get
f\in\mathcal{Q}_{K}
.

(ii)\Leftrightarrow(iii)
. If

\overline{\partial}\widetilde{F_{n}}(z)=O(z^{n-2}),\ \text{as}\ \ z\rightarrow\infty,

then

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{|z|\geq 1+2\pi}\frac{|\overline{\partial}\widetilde{F_{n}}(z)|^{2}}{(|z|^{n}-1)^{2}}K\left(1-\frac{1}{|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2}}\right)dA(z)<\infty.

This together with Lemma 2.3, we see that condition (ii) is equivalent to condition (iii).
We finish the proof.

\square

Remark.
In Theorem 2.1, by the particular choice of
K
, we obtain the corresponding characterizations of
\mathcal{Q}_{p}
for
0<p<1
,
BMOA
and the Bloch space.

3.
Besov spaces, Morrey spaces and pseudoanalytic extension

In this section, we describe Besov spaces and Morrey spaces by pseudoanalytic extension of primitive functions.

For
1<p<\infty
, the Besov space
B_{p}
is the space of analytic functions
f
in
\mathbb{D}
such that

[formula]

\|f\|_{B_{p}}^{p}=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{p-2}dA(z)<\infty.

In particular, if
p=2
, then
B_{p}
is the Dirichlet space
\mathcal{D}
.
Note that (3.1) does not hold for
p=1
. The Besov space
B_{1}
consists of all
analytic functions
f
on
\mathbb{D}
which have a representation as

f(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c_{k}\sigma_{a_{k}}(z),\,\,a_{k}\in\mathbb{D}\,\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|c_{k}|<\infty.

Form
[
2
]
,
f\in B_{1}
if and only if

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime\prime}(z)|dA(z)<\infty.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
n\geq 2
be an integer. It is well known that
f\in B_{p}
if and only if

[formula]

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{(n)}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{np-2}dA(z)<\infty.

See
[
11
,
34
,
35
]
for more results of Besov spaces.

Note that all Besov spaces are subsets of the Bloch space. Thus, if
f\in B_{p}
, then its primitive function
F
belongs to the Hardy space
H^{2}
.
Now we give a pseudoanalytic extension characterization of Besov spaces as follows.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
with its primitive function
F\in H^{2}
and let
n\geq 2
be an integer. If
1\leq p<\infty
, then the following conditions are equivalent.

(i)

f\in B_{p}
.

(ii)

There exists a function
\widetilde{F_{n}}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}})
satisfying

[formula]

\lim_{r\rightarrow 1^{+}}\widetilde{F_{n}}(re^{i\theta})=F(e^{i\theta})\ a.e.\ \theta\in[0,2\pi],

[formula]

\widetilde{F_{n}}(z)=O(z^{n}),\ \text{as}\ \ z\rightarrow\infty,

[formula]

\overline{\partial}\widetilde{F_{n}}(z)=O(z^{n-2}),\ \text{as}\ \ z\rightarrow\infty,

and

[formula]

\int_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}}\frac{|\overline{\partial}\widetilde{F_{n}}(z)|^{p}}{\left(|z|^{\frac{np}{2}-p+2}-1\right)^{2}}dA(z)<\infty.

Proof.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
. Let
f\in B_{p}
. Set

\widetilde{F_{n}}(z)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{i}}{i!}(z^{*}-z)^{i}F^{(i)}(z^{*}),\ z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}.

It is easy to check that
\widetilde{F_{n}}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}})
and
\widetilde{F_{n}}
satisfies (3.3), (3.4) and (3.5). Furthermore, by the following estimates

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}}\frac{|\overline{\partial}\widetilde{F_{n}}(z)|^{p}}{\left(|z|^{\frac{np}{2}-p+2}-1\right)^{2}}dA(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(n!)^{p}}\int_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}}\frac{|z^{*}-z|^{np}|z^{*}|^{2p}\left|f^{(n)}(z^{*})\right|^{p}}{\left(|z|^{\frac{np}{2}-p+2}-1\right)^{2}}dA(z)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|w|^{2})^{np}\left|f^{(n)}(w)\right|^{p}}{\left(1-|w|^{\frac{np}{2}-p+2}\right)^{2}}dA(w)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}(1-|w|)^{np-2}|f^{(n)}(w)|^{p}dA(w)

and (3.2), we know that
\widetilde{F_{n}}
also satisfies (3.6).

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
. Checking the proof of Theorem 2.1, one gets

F^{(n+1)}(z)=-\frac{(n+1)!}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}}\frac{\overline{\partial}\widetilde{F_{n}}(\zeta)}{(\zeta-z)^{n+2}}dA(\zeta),\,z\in\mathbb{D}.

Applying the Hölder inequality and
[
35
, Lemma 3.10]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|F^{(n+1)}(z)\right|^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}}\frac{1}{|\zeta-z|^{4}}dA(\zeta)\right)^{p-1}\int_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}}\frac{|\overline{\partial}\widetilde{F_{n}}(\zeta)|^{p}}{|\zeta-z|^{np-2p+4}}dA(\zeta)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{1}{|1-\overline{w}z|^{4}}dA(w)\right)^{p-1}\int_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}}\frac{|\overline{\partial}\widetilde{F_{n}}(\zeta)|^{p}}{|\zeta-z|^{np-2p+4}}dA(\zeta)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2p-2}}\int_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}}\frac{|\overline{\partial}\widetilde{F_{n}}(\zeta)|^{p}}{|\zeta-z|^{np-2p+4}}dA(\zeta).

Note that
n\geq 2
. Using
[
35
, Lemma 3.10]
again, we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})^{np-2}\left|F^{(n+1)}(z)\right|^{p}dA(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}}\frac{|\overline{\partial}\widetilde{F_{n}}(\zeta)|^{p}}{|\zeta-z|^{np-2p+4}}dA(\zeta)(1-|z|^{2})^{np-2p}dA(z)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{|\overline{\partial}\widetilde{F_{n}}(w^{*})|^{p}|w|^{np-2p}}{|1-\overline{w}z|^{np-2p+4}}dA(w)(1-|z|^{2})^{np-2p}dA(z)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{np-2p}}{|1-\overline{w}z|^{np-2p+4}}dA(z)|\overline{\partial}\widetilde{F_{n}}(w^{*})|^{p}|w|^{np-2p}dA(w)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left(1-|w|^{2}\right)^{-2}|\overline{\partial}\widetilde{F_{n}}(w^{*})|^{p}|w|^{np-2p}dA(w)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left(1-|w|^{\frac{np}{2}-p+2}\right)^{-2}|\overline{\partial}\widetilde{F_{n}}(w^{*})|^{p}|w|^{np-2p}dA(w)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}}\frac{|\overline{\partial}\widetilde{F_{n}}(\zeta)|^{p}}{(|\zeta|^{\frac{np}{2}-p+2}-1)^{2}}dA(\zeta).

Since
F^{\prime}(z)=f(z)
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
, by (3.2) and (3.6), we obtain
f\in B_{p}
. This finishes the proof.
∎

For
0<\lambda\leq 1
, the analytic Morrey space
\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}
is the set of all functions
f\in H^{2}
satisfying

\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}}=\sup_{I\subseteq\partial\mathbb{D}}\left(\frac{1}{\ell(I)^{\lambda}}\int_{I}|f(\zeta)-f_{I}|^{2}|d\zeta|\right)^{1/2}<\infty,

where
\ell(I)
denotes the length of
I
and

f_{I}=\frac{1}{\ell(I)}\int_{I}f(\zeta)|d\zeta|.

Clearly,
\mathcal{L}^{2,1}
is the space
BMOA
. By
[
30
, p. 54]
,
f\in\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}
if and only if

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|a|^{2})^{1-\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}(1-|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2})dA(z)<\infty.

Let
n\geq 1
be an integer. Using a similar statement in
[
4
]
, we see that
f\in\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}
if and only if

[formula]

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|a|^{2})^{1-\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{(n)}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{2n-2}(1-|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2})dA(z)<\infty.

Recently, the interest in analytic Morrey space has grown rapidly. See
[
8
,
18
,
20
,
21
,
23
,
31
,
32
]
for more results of
\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}
spaces.

Note that if
f\in\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}
, then its primitive function
F
belongs to
H^{2}
. Applying (3.7), we get the following characterization of analytic Morrey space. The proof is similar to Theorem 2.1, we omit it.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
with its primitive function
F\in H^{2}
and let
n\geq 2
be an integer. If
0<\lambda\leq 1
, then the following conditions are equivalent.

(i)

f\in\mathcal{L}^{2,\lambda}
.

(ii)

There exists a function
\widetilde{F_{n}}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}})
satisfying

\lim_{r\rightarrow 1^{+}}\widetilde{F_{n}}(re^{i\theta})=F(e^{i\theta})\ a.e.\ \theta\in[0,2\pi],

\widetilde{F_{n}}(z)=O(z^{n}),\ \text{as}\ \ z\rightarrow\infty,

\overline{\partial}\widetilde{F_{n}}(z)=O(z^{n-2}),\ \text{as}\ \ z\rightarrow\infty,

and

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|a|^{2})^{1-\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}}\frac{|\overline{\partial}\widetilde{F_{n}}(z)|^{2}}{(|z|^{n}-1)^{2}}\left(1-\frac{1}{|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2}}\right)dA(z)<\infty.

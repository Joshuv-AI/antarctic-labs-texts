Algebraic properties of paraorthomodular posets
Ivan Chajda, Davide Fazio, Helmut Länger, Antonio Ledda and

Abstract
Paraorthomodular posets are bounded partially ordered set with an antitone involution induced by quantum structures arising from the logico-algebraic approach to quantum mechanics. The aim of the present work is starting a systematic inquiry into paraorthomodular posets theory both from an algebraic and order-theoretic perspective. On the one hand, we show that paraorthomodular posets are amenable of an algebraic treatment by means of a smooth representation in terms of bounded directoids with antitone involution. On the other, we investigate their order-theoretical features in terms of forbidden configurations. Moreover, sufficient and necessary conditions characterizing bounded posets with an antitone involution whose Dedekind-MacNeille completion is paraorthomodular are provided.

06A11, 06C15, 06B23, 06B75, 08B99, 03G12, 03G25.

1
Introduction

It is well known (see e.g.
[
23
]
) that any physical theory determines a class of event-state systems
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{S})
, where
\mathcal{E}
contains the events that may occur relatively to a given system and
\mathcal{S}
contains the states that such a physical system may assume. In quantum theory, it is customary to identify
\mathcal{E}
with the set
\Pi(\mathcal{H})
of projection operators of a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, which is in bijective correspondence with the set
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{H})
of closed subspaces of
\mathcal{H}
. Moreover, if we order
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{H})
by set inclusion, then it can be made into the universe of an
orthomodular lattice
(OML). Orthomodular lattices were introduced in 1936 by G. Birkhoff and J. von Neumann
[
3
]
, and independently by K. Husimi
[
25
]
, as a suitable algebraic tool for investigating the logical structure underlying physical theories which, like quantum mechanics, do not conform to classical logic.
However, later on it was recognized that this framework needed to be revised. In fact, joins (i.e. disjunctions in the corresponding logic) need not exist provided the elements in question are not orthogonal. For this reason,
orthomodular posets
were defined with the aim of faithfully formalising event structures of quantum mechanical systems (see e.g.
[
2
,
27
]
for a detailed account). Since the early forties, a large amount of investigations on the algebraic and order theoretical properties of orthomodular posets and, later,
orthoalgebras
(see below and e.g.
[
20
]
), has become the core subject of investigations on
sharp quantum structures

[
14
]
. However, in spite of the importance and depth of results in this field, some doubts concerning the relevance of OML’s for the foundation of quantum mechanics arose when it was discovered that the lattices of projection operators on Hilbert spaces do not generate the whole variety of orthomodular lattices (see e.g.
[
14
]
). This shows that there are equational properties of event-state systems that cannot be captured by the proposed mathematical abstraction. Hence, alternative approaches recently appeared. Let us mention e.g.
weakly orthomodular
and
dually weakly orthomodular
lattices
[
9
]
, and
pseudo-orthomodular
posets
[
10
,
11
]
.

A different abstract counterpart of the set of quantum events has been suggested within the
unsharp
approach to quantum theory by effect algebras
[
19
,
22
]
and quantum MV algebras
[
21
]
. These structures ensure the algebraic treatment of quantum effects, namely bounded self-adjoint linear operators on a Hilbert space satisfying the Born’s rule. Effect algebras framework is indeed very general: any orthoalgebra, and so any orthomodular poset (lattice) is an example thereof. However, since the
canonical
order (CO) induced by the Born’s rule (see
[
14
, Chapter 4]
) on the set
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{H})
of effects on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
does not ensure that meets and joins of elements always exist (see e.g.
[
15
, Exercise 17, p.124]
), effect algebras do not lend themselves to easy logico-algebraic investigations. Therefore, in
[
23
,
24
]

paraorthomodular lattices
, i.e. regular bounded lattices with an antitone involution
′
satisfying the paraorthomodular condition

x\leq y\quad\text{and}\quad x^{\prime}\land y=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad x=y

were introduced. They abstract a natural generalization of the lattice ordering on closed subspaces of a Hilbert space to the whole class of effects by means of the Olson
spectral
order
\leq_{s}

[
30
]
. This ordering properly extends (CO), since e.g. the condition

E\leq_{s}F\ \mbox{iff}\ F-E\geq_{s}\mathbb{O}

need not be satisfied (see
[
30
]
).
Although their “concrete” representatives enjoy several properties which are not shared by effect algebras, paraorthomodular lattices are nevertheless capable of capturing an essential cross-cutting trait of quantum structures. Indeed, as it will be clear in the sequel, if the notion of paraorthomodular lattice is relaxed by not requiring that the underlying bounded poset with antitone involution is either a lattice or regular, then the derived concept of
paraorthomodular poset
frames quantum structures into a common unifying picture. Any effect algebra determines a bounded poset with antitone involution, which is paraorthomodular in the above sense.

The aim of the present work is starting a systematic inquiry into paraorthomodular posets from a dual perspective. On the one hand, we show that paraorthomodular posets are amenable of an algebraic tratment by means of a smooth representation in terms of bounded directoids with antitone involution. On the other, we investigate their order-theoretical features in terms of forbidden configurations. Moreover, sufficient and necessary conditions characterizing bounded posets with an antitone involution whose Dedekind-MacNeille completion is paraorthomodular are provided. These results generalize well known achievements in the framework of completions of quantum structures
[
18
,
17
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, after dispatching all the notions needed in the sequel, we characterize paraorthomodular posets in terms of forbidden configurations. Moreover, we show that any effect algebra induces a bounded poset with antitone involution which is paraorthomodular. In Section
3
, we introduce the class of paraorthomodular directoids as the algebraic counterpart of paraorthomodular posets. It will turn out that paraorthomodular directoids form a proper super quasi-variety of paraorthomodular lattices. Furthermore, we show that sharp quantum structures, in particular orthoalgebras, can be represented as a proper subvariety of paraorthomodular directoids. This result extends the application of techniques developed in
[
6
]
. Finally, in Section
4
we investigate bounded posets with an antitone involution whose Dedekind-MacNeille completion are paraorthomodular lattices.

2
Paraorthomodular posets

In this section we firstly recall several known but useful concepts.

A
paraorthomodular lattice
is a bounded lattice
\mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{\prime},0,1)
with an antitone involution
′
satisfying for any
x,y\in L
the condition

(P)

x\leq y
and
x^{\prime}\wedge y=0
imply
x=y
.

Of course, every orthomodular lattice is paraorthomodular. In fact, if the antitone involution
′
is a complementation, i.e. if
\mathbf{L}
is an ortholattice, then
\mathbf{L}
is orthomodular if and only if it satisfies condition (P). The matter is that in general
′
need not be a complementation.

Let
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq)
be a poset,
a,b\in P
and
A,B\subseteq P
. We define the lower and upper cone of
A
as follows:

\displaystyle L(A)

\displaystyle:=\{x\in P\mid x\leq A\},

\displaystyle U(A)

\displaystyle:=\{x\in P\mid x\geq A\}.

Here
x\leq A
means
x\leq y
for all
y\in A
and, similarly,
x\geq A
means
x\geq y
for all
y\in A
. The expression
A\leq B
means
x\leq y
for all
x\in A
and
y\in B
. Instead of
L(\{a\})
,
L(\{a,b\})
and
L(U(A))
we simply write
L(a)
,
L(a,b)
and
LU(A)
, respectively. Analogously, we proceed in similar cases.

The
poset

\mathbf{P}
is called
distributive
if it satisfies the LU-identity

L(U(x,y),z)\approx LU(L(x,z),L(y,z))

and
modular
if

L(U(x,y),z)=LU(x,L(y,z))

for all
x,y,z\in P
with
x\leq z
.

Now we define our main concept.

Definition 2.1
.

A paraorthomodular poset is a bounded poset
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
with an antitone involution
′
satisfying for all
x,y\in P
the condition

(P*)

x\leq y
and
L(x^{\prime},y)=\{0\}
imply
x=y
.

In the sequel, we will denote by
\mathcal{P}
the class of paraorthomodular posets.

Apparently, if
\mathbf{P}
is a lattice then (P) is equivalent to (P*), thus both concepts coincide. On the other hand, there exist paraorthomodular posets which are not lattices.

It is easy to see that for a poset
(P,\leq,0)
with bottom element
0
and for
a,b\in P
the assertions
L(a,b)=\{0\}
and
a\wedge b=0
are equivalent and that (P*) is equivalent to

x<y\text{ implies }L(x^{\prime},y)\neq\{0\}.

Moreover, it is clear that if a poset
\mathbf{P}
with an antitone involution is constructed from a bounded poset with an antitone involution by adding a new bottom element and a new top element, then
\mathbf{P}
is paraorthomodular.

, but

\displaystyle=L(1,c^{\prime})=L(c^{\prime})=\{0,a,d,c^{\prime}\}\neq\{0,a,d\}=L(b^{\prime},c^{\prime},1)=LU(a,0,d)=

A forbidden configurations characterization of paraorthomodular posets comes next.

The concept of a strong subposet was introduced by the first author and J. Rachůnek in
[
12
]
. Now we extend it for bounded posets with involution as follows.

Definition 2.3
.

A strong subset of a bounded poset
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
with an antitone involution is a subset
A
of
P
containing
0
and
1
such that
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
and for all
x,y\in A
the following hold:

•

U_{\mathbf{P}}L_{\mathbf{A}}(x,y)=U_{\mathbf{P}}L_{\mathbf{P}}(x,y)
,

•

L_{\mathbf{P}}U_{\mathbf{A}}(x,y)=L_{\mathbf{P}}U_{\mathbf{P}}(x,y)
.

That this concept is not curious is witnessed by the following result. It shows that strong subsets of a bounded poset with an antitone involution play a similar role as subsets do for lattices.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
be a bounded poset with an antitone involution
′
. If
\mathbf{M}
is a bounded subposet of
\mathbf{P}
closed under
′
and
\mathbf{M}
is a lattice, then
\mathbf{M}
is a strong subset of
\mathbf{P}
.

Proof.

Clearly, for any
x,y\in M
,

\displaystyle x\lor^{\mathbf{M}}y

\displaystyle=x\lor^{\mathbf{P}}y,

\displaystyle x\land^{\mathbf{M}}y

\displaystyle=x\land^{\mathbf{P}}y.

Therefore,

\displaystyle L_{\mathbf{P}}U_{\mathbf{M}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=L_{\mathbf{P}}U_{\mathbf{M}}(x\lor y)=L_{\mathbf{P}}(x\lor y)=L_{\mathbf{P}}U_{\mathbf{P}}(x\lor y)=L_{\mathbf{P}}U_{\mathbf{P}}(x,y),

\displaystyle U_{\mathbf{P}}L_{\mathbf{M}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=U_{\mathbf{P}}L_{\mathbf{M}}(x\land y)=U_{\mathbf{P}}(x\land y)=U_{\mathbf{P}}L_{\mathbf{P}}(x\land y)=U_{\mathbf{P}}L_{\mathbf{P}}(x,y).

∎

\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)

but

. As regards the left-to-right direction, suppose that
\mathbf{P}
is not paraorthomodular. Then there exist

such that

and

, but

. Consider the subposet

In the last part of this section, we show that paraorthomodular posets framework is indeed capable of setting quantum structures arising in the sharp and unsharp approach to quantum theory into a common unified picture.

Recall that an
orthomodular poset
is a bounded poset
(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
with an antitone involution
′
satisfying the following conditions for all
x,y\in P
:

(1)

The unary operation
′
is a complementation,

(2)

x\leq y^{\prime}
implies that
x\vee y
exists,

(3)

x\leq y
implies
y=x\vee(y\wedge x^{\prime})
(
orthomodular law
).

Using (2) one can easily see that the expressions in (3) are well-defined, i.e.
y\wedge x^{\prime}
as well as
x\vee(y\wedge x^{\prime})
exist.

Lemma 2.6
.

Every orthomodular poset is paraorthomodular.

Proof.

If
(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
is an orthomodular poset,
a,b\in P
,
a\leq b
and
L(a^{\prime},b)=\{0\}
then
a^{\prime}\wedge b
exists and
a^{\prime}\wedge b=0
and using the orthomodular law we conclude

a=a\vee 0=a\vee(b\wedge a^{\prime})=b.

∎

Another interesting class of paraorthomodular posets are the so-called pseudo-orthomodular ones, see
[
10
]
.

A
pseudo-orthomodular poset
(
[
10
]
) is a bounded poset
(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
with an antitone involution
′
which is a complementation satisfying one of the following equivalent conditions:

\displaystyle L(U(L(x,y),y^{\prime}),y)

\displaystyle\approx L(x,y),

\displaystyle U(L(U(x,y),y^{\prime}),y)

\displaystyle\approx U(x,y).

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\mathbf{P}
be a pseudo-orthomodular poset. Then
\mathbf{P}
is paraorthomodular.

Proof.

If
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
,
a,b\in P
,
a\leq b
and
L(a^{\prime},b)=\{0\}
then

U(a)=U(0,a)=U(L(b,a^{\prime}),a)=U(L(U(b),a^{\prime}),a)=U(L(U(b,a),a^{\prime}),a)=U(b,a)=U(b)

and hence
a=b
.
∎

We continue with the definition of an effect algebra (see
[
19
]
and
[
28
]
). For an extensive account, the reader is referred to Dvurečenskij and Pulmannová’s monograph
[
15
]
.

Definition 2.8
.

An effect algebra is a partial algebra
\mathbf{E}=(E,\oplus,0,1)
of type
(2,0,0)
satisfying conditions
(E1)
–
(E4)
for all
x,y,z\in E
:

(E1)

If
x\oplus y
exists, so does
y\oplus x
and
x\oplus y=y\oplus x
,

(E2)

if
x\oplus y
and
(x\oplus y)\oplus z
exist, so do
y\oplus z
and
x\oplus(y\oplus z)
and
(x\oplus y)\oplus z=x\oplus(y\oplus z)
,

(E3)

there exists a unique
x^{\prime}\in E
such that
x\oplus x^{\prime}
is defined and
x\oplus x^{\prime}=1
,

(E4)

If
x\oplus 1
exists then
x=0
.

Since
′
is a unary operation on
E
it can be regarded as a further fundamental operation. Hence in the following we will write
\mathbf{E}=(E,\oplus,{}^{\prime},0,1)
instead of
\mathbf{E}=(E,\oplus,0,1)
.

Let
\mathbf{E}=(E,\oplus,{}^{\prime},0,1)
be an effect algebra and
a,b\in E
. The following facts are well-known:

(F1)

By defining
a\leq b
if there exists some
c\in E
such that
a\oplus c
exists and
a\oplus c=b
,
(E,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
becomes a bounded poset with an antitone involution. We call
\leq
the
induced order
of
\mathbf{E}
. Recall that the element
c
is unique, if it exists. Then
c
is equal to
(a\oplus b^{\prime})^{\prime}
and it is denoted by
b\ominus a
.

(F2)

a\oplus b
exists if and only if
a\leq b^{\prime}
.

(F3)

a\oplus 0
and
0\oplus a
exist and
a\oplus 0=0\oplus a=a
.

(F4)

(a^{\prime})^{\prime}=a
.

Proposition 2.9
.

Every effect algebra
\mathbf{P}=(P,\oplus,0,1)
is a paraorthomodular poset.

Proof.

It is enough to check condition (P*). Let
x,y\in P
such that
x\leq y
and
L(x^{\prime},y)=\{0\}
. Then
L(y\ominus x)=L(x^{\prime},y\ominus x)\subseteq L(x^{\prime},y)
. Hence
y\ominus x=0
, i.e.
x=y
.
∎

3
A representation by commutative directoids

The aim of this section is to show that to every paraorthomodular poset
\mathbf{P}
one can assign (in a non-unique way) a certain algebra
\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{P})
with everywhere defined operations such that the original poset
\mathbf{P}
can be completely recovered from
\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{P})
. Moreover, properties of
\mathbf{P}
can often be expressed by means of identities and quasi-identities satisfied by
\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{P})
.

The following concept was introduced by J. Ježek and R. Quackenbush, see
[
26
]
and also
[
8
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

A commutative directoid is a groupoid
(D,\sqcap)
satisfying the following identities

\displaystyle x\sqcap x

\displaystyle\approx x\text{ {\rm(}idempotence{\rm)}},

\displaystyle x\sqcap y

\displaystyle\approx y\sqcap x\text{ {\rm(}commutativity{\rm)}},

\displaystyle(x\sqcap(y\sqcap z))\sqcap z

\displaystyle\approx x\sqcap(y\sqcap z)\text{ {\rm(}weak associativity{\rm)}}.

Let
(P,\leq)
be a downward directed poset (for example, a poset with
0
). Define
x\sqcap y:=x\wedge y
if
x\wedge y
exists and let
x\sqcap y=y\sqcap x
be an arbitrary element of
L(x,y)
otherwise (
x,y\in P
). It is elementary that
(P,\sqcap)
is a commutative directoid. It will be called a
commutative directoid assigned
to
(P,\leq)
. Conversely, let
(D,\sqcap)
be a commutative directoid. For all
x,y\in D
define

x\leq y\text{ if and only if }x\sqcap y=x.

Then
(D,\leq)
is a downward directed poset, the so-called
poset induced
by
(D,\sqcap)
. If
\mathbf{D}=(D,\sqcap)
is obtained from a poset
\mathbf{P}
as above, i.e. it is assigned to
\mathbf{D}
, we will express this fact also by
\mathbf{D}=\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{P})
. Conversely, the fact that
\mathbf{P}
is induced by
\mathbf{D}
will be expressed also by
\mathbf{P}=\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{D})
. If
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
is a class of posets and directoids, respectively, we will say that
\mathcal{C}
is induced by
\mathcal{D}
(
\mathcal{D}
is assigned to
\mathcal{C}
) if

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle=\{\mathbf{P}\mid\text{there exists some }\mathbf{D}\in\mathcal{D}\text{ with }\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{D})=\mathbf{P}\}\text{ and}

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}

\displaystyle=\{\mathbf{D}\mid\text{there exists some }\mathbf{P}\in\mathcal{C}\text{ with }\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{P})=\mathbf{D}\}.

Although the assignment
(P,\leq)\mapsto(P,\sqcap)
is not unique, the poset induced by
(P,\sqcap)
coincides with the original one. Hence, every commutative directoid assigned to a poset contains the whole information concerning this poset.

Now let
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,{}^{\prime})
be a poset with an antitone involution and
\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{P})
an assigned commutative directoid. Then we can define

x\sqcup y:=(x^{\prime}\sqcap y^{\prime})^{\prime}

for all
x,y\in P
. It is easy to show (by De Morgan’s laws) that
(P,\sqcup)
is a commutative directoid, too. Moreover,

x\leq y\text{ if and only if }x\sqcup y=y.

Recall from
[
33
]
(see also
[
8
]
) that the algebra
(P,\sqcup,\sqcap)
is called a
\lambda
-lattice
.

3.1
Paraorthomodular directoids

If
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
is an orthomodular poset then the assigned commutative directoids
\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{P})=(P,\sqcap,{}^{\prime},0,1)
can be characterized by identities. Hence the class of these commutative directoids forms a variety, see
[
6
]
. A similar result holds for orthoposets. Analogously, to the class of complemented posets (satisfying additional properties) a variety of commutative directoids can be assigned, see
[
7
]
. This motivates us to derive a similar result for the class of paraorthomodular posets. First we recall the following results from
[
6
]
and
[
7
]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,{}^{\prime})
be a downward directed poset with a unary operation
′
and
\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{P})=(P,\sqcap,{}^{\prime})
an assigned commutative directoid. Then
′
is an antitone involution on
(P,\leq)
if and only if
\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{P})
satisfies the following identities:

(4)

x^{\prime\prime}\approx x
,

(5)

(x\sqcap y)^{\prime}\sqcap y^{\prime}\approx y^{\prime}
.

For the reader’s convenience, we repeat the short proof.

Proof.

Let
a,b\in P
. The operation
′
is an involution if and only if (4) holds. If
′
is antitone then from
a\sqcap b\leq b
we obtain
b^{\prime}\leq(a\sqcap b)^{\prime}
, thus

(a\sqcap b)^{\prime}\sqcap b^{\prime}=b^{\prime},

i.e. (5) holds. If, conversely, (5) holds and
a\leq b
then

b^{\prime}=(a\sqcap b)^{\prime}\sqcap b^{\prime}=a^{\prime}\sqcap b^{\prime}\leq a^{\prime},

i.e.
′
is antitone.
∎

The following remark is (almost) immediate.

Remark 3.3
.

Let
\mathbf{D}:=(D,\sqcap,{}^{\prime})
be a commutative directoid with a unary operation
′
. Then
\mathbf{D}
satisfies conditions
(4) and (5) from Proposition
3.2
if and only if it satisfies

((x\sqcap y)^{\prime}\sqcap y^{\prime})^{\prime}\approx y.

It is also possible to describe the lower cone
L(a,b)
in terms of an assigned commutative directoid as follows.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq)
be a downward directed poset,
\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{P})=(P,\sqcap)
an assigned commutative directoid and
a,b\in P
. Then
L(a,b)=\{(x\sqcap a)\sqcap(x\sqcap b)\mid x\in P\}
.

Proof.

If
c\in L(a,b)
then
c=c\sqcap c=(c\sqcap a)\sqcap(c\sqcap b)
. Conversely, if
d\in P
then

\displaystyle(d\sqcap a)\sqcap(d\sqcap b)\leq d\sqcap a\leq a,

\displaystyle(d\sqcap a)\sqcap(d\sqcap b)\leq d\sqcap b\leq b,

i.e.
(d\sqcap a)\sqcap(d\sqcap b)\in L(a,b)
.
∎

Now we are ready to prove our first result.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
be a bounded poset with a unary operation
′
and

If a bounded directoid
\mathbf{D}
with an antitone involution is such that

the class of

paraorthomodular directoids. Clearly,

is assigned to
\mathcal{P}
. Now, since

is an elementary class (by Theorem

), one might wonder if
\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{P}}
is also a universal class. The next results show that, indeed, there are sufficient conditions for answering the above question in the positive. Let
\mathcal{C}
be a class of directoids. Let us denote by
\mathcal{C}_{0}
the class of bounded involution posets induced by

.

Consider a universal formula
\eta
in the language
(\leq,0,1)
. Let us define its translation

as follows: For any literal

\lnot(x\sqcap y\approx x)\text{, otherwise.}\end{cases}

Let us remark that

in the language

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\eta
be a universal formula in the language
(\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
. Then, for any involutive directoid

if and only if

Proof.

For general reasons, we can safely assume that

\eta
corresponds to

\forall\vec{x}\bigvee_{i\in I}\bigwedge_{j\in J}\phi_{i,j},

where
\phi_{i,j}
is either of the form
x\leq y
, or of the form
\lnot(x\leq y)
, with

. Consider

t(\forall\vec{x}\bigvee_{i\in I}\bigwedge_{j\in J}\phi_{i,j})=\forall\vec{x}\bigvee_{i\in I}\bigwedge_{j\in J}t(\phi_{i,j}).

It is evident that

is also in disjunctive normal form. Let us suppose that

\mathbf{D}\not\models t(\eta)[\vec{x},\vec{a}]\equiv\bigvee_{i\in I}\bigwedge_{j\in J}t(\phi_{i,j})[\vec{x},\vec{a}],

with
\vec{a}
elements of

. Then, for any

there is

such that
\mathbf{D}\not\models t(\phi_{i,j})[\vec{x},\vec{a}]
. So, without loss of generality, for some

, either

\displaystyle a_{p}\sqcap a_{q}\neq a_{p}\text{ if }\phi_{i,j}\text{ is of the form }x\leq y\text{, or}

\displaystyle a_{p}\sqcap a_{q}=a_{p}\text{ if }\phi_{i,j}\text{ is of the form }\lnot(x\leq y).

Then, for any
i\in I
there is

such that,
\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{D})\not\models\phi_{i,j}
. The converse direction is analogous.
∎

The next result shows that if a class

of bounded posets with antitone involution induced by a class

If

is a universal class, then

is universal.

Proof.

Let us assume that

is a universal class. Then there exists a family of universal formulae

. Consider

. By assumption, for all

∎

Actually, Theorem

suggests that, in order to assign (at least) a quasivariety of directoids to the class

Let
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)

x\sqcap y:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\min\{x,y\}&\text{if }x\not\parallel y,\\
\text{an arbitrary element of }L(x,y)\setminus\{0\}&\text{if }x^{\prime}<y\text{ and }L(x,y)\neq\{0\},\\

Clearly,

\mathbf{D}
with an antitone involution obtained from a bounded poset
\mathbf{P}

assigned to
\mathbf{P}

\mathbf{D}
with an antitone involution is canonically assigned to a bounded poset

with an antitone involution, we will write

\mathcal{C}
of bounded posets with an antitone involution, and a class

of bounded directoids with an antitone involution, we will say that

if

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle=\{\mathbf{P}\mid\text{there exists some }\mathbf{D}\in\mathcal{D}\text{ with }\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{D})=\mathbf{P}\}\text{ and}

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}

\displaystyle=\{\mathbf{D}\mid\text{there exists some }\mathbf{P}\in\mathcal{C}\text{ with }\mathbb{D}^{c}(\mathbf{P})=\mathbf{D}\}.

From now on, we will denote by

the class of bounded directoids with an antitone involution canonically assigned to

.

.

Proposition 3.12
.

The variety generated by

Proof.

In order to prove the statement, let us show that any bounded directoid

In what follows we present another and stronger generalization of paraorthomodular lattices.

Definition 3.14
.

A sharply paraorthomodular poset is a bounded poset
(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
with an antitone involution
′
satisfying for all
x,y\in P
the conditions

(10)

x\leq y^{\prime}
implies that
x\vee y
exists,

(11)

x\leq y
and
x^{\prime}\wedge y=0
imply
x=y
.

Let us note that (10) and (11) coincide with (2) and (P), respectively.

Of course, every sharply paraorthomodular poset is paraorthomodular.

It is easy to see that in every poset
(P,\leq,{}^{\prime})
with an antitone involution the following conditions are equivalent:

(12)

x\leq y^{\prime}
implies that
x\vee y
exists,

(13)

x\leq y
implies that
x^{\prime}\wedge y
exists.

Examples of sharply paraorthomodular posets abound in quantum structures literature. In fact, any orthomodular poset and any
\{\leq,^{\prime},0,1\}
-reduct
\mathcal{P}(\mathbf{A})
of a lattice effect algebra
\mathbf{A}
are obviously sharply paraorthomodular, by virtue of Lemma
2.6
and Proposition
2.9
, respectively. Furthermore, any
\{\leq,^{\prime},0,1\}
-reduct
\mathcal{P}(\mathbf{A})
of a
4
-loop pasting
\mathbf{A}
(of an admissible system
\mathcal{F}
) of MV-algebras
[
13
, Definition 12]
is an example of a proper sharply paraorthomodular poset. Indeed, by
[
13
, pp. 356–357]
,
\mathbf{A}
is an effect algebra. So, by Proposition
2.9
,
\mathcal{P}(\mathbf{A})
is paraorthomodular. Moreover, by
[
13
, Theorem 16]
, although
\mathbf{A}
is not lattice-ordered, joins of orthogonal elements always exist in
A
. Hence,
\mathcal{P}(\mathbf{A})
is sharply paraorthomodular.

We are going to characterize sharply paraorthomodular posets by means of assigned commutative directoids.

Lemma 3.15
.

Let
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
be a bounded poset with an antitone involution
′
and
\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{P})=(P,\sqcap,{}^{\prime},0,1)
an assigned commutative directoid. Then
\mathbf{P}
satisfies
(10)
if and only if
\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{P})
satisfies the identity

(14)

z\leq((x\sqcup z)\sqcup(y\sqcup z)^{\prime})\sqcap(y\sqcup z)
.

Proof.

Let
a,b,c\in P
. First assume that
\mathbf{P}
to satisfy condition (10) which is equivalent to (13). Put

\displaystyle d

\displaystyle:=(a\sqcup c)\sqcup(b\sqcup c)^{\prime},

\displaystyle e

\displaystyle:=b\sqcup c.

Then
e^{\prime}\leq d
, i.e.
d^{\prime}\leq e
and hence by (13)
d\wedge e=d^{\prime\prime}\wedge e
exists, thus
d\sqcap e=d\wedge e
, and because of
c\leq d,e
we have

c\leq d\wedge e=d\sqcap e=((a\sqcup c)\sqcup(b\sqcup c)^{\prime})\sqcap(b\sqcup c),

i.e. (14) holds. Conversely, assume (14) holds. If
a\leq b
and
c\leq a^{\prime},b
then

c\leq((a^{\prime}\sqcup c)\sqcup(b\sqcup c)^{\prime})\sqcap(b\sqcup c)=(a^{\prime}\sqcup b^{\prime})\sqcap b=a^{\prime}\sqcap b

which means
a^{\prime}\sqcap b=a^{\prime}\wedge b
and hence
a^{\prime}\wedge b
exists, i.e. (13) and therefore also (10) holds.
∎

Now, we characterize sharply paraorthomodular posets by an identity and a quasi-identity of an assigned commutative directoid as follows.

Theorem 3.16
.

Let
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
be a bounded poset with an antitone involution
′
and
\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{P})=(P,\sqcap,{}^{\prime},0,1)
an assigned commutative directoid. Then
\mathbf{P}
is sharply paraorthomodular if and only if
\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{P})
satisfies identity
(14)
and quasi-identity
(8)
.

Proof.

Let
a,b\in P
. First assume
\mathbf{P}
to be sharply paraorthomodular. Then, because of Lemma
3.15
, (14) holds. If
(a\sqcap b)^{\prime}\sqcap b=0
then, since
a\sqcap b\leq b
and (13) is equivalent to (10), we have that
(a\sqcap b)^{\prime}\wedge b
exists and hence
(a\sqcap b)^{\prime}\wedge b=(a\sqcap b)^{\prime}\sqcap b=0
whence
a\sqcap b=b
according to (11), i.e. (8) holds. Conversely, assume
\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{P})
satisfies (14) and (8). Then, because of Lemma

, (10) holds. If
a\leq b
and
a^{\prime}\wedge b=0
then
(a\sqcap b)^{\prime}\sqcap b=a^{\prime}\sqcap b=a^{\prime}\wedge b=0
whence by (8)

, i.e. (11) holds.
∎

Corollary 3.17
.

The class of commutative directoids assigned to the class of sharply paraorthomodular posets forms a quasivariety of algebras.

In what follows, we will call any member from the quasivariety of commutative directoids assigned to sharply paraorthomodular posets a
paraorthomodular directoid
.

3.2
Sharp quantum structures as subvarieties of
\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{P}}_{c}

As it has been recalled in Section

\mathbf{A}=(A,\oplus,0,1)
and

,

need not exist provided that

. Therefore,

is a partial algebra. In

in terms of

and

. Furthermore, if
\mathbf{A}
is an orthomodular poset, then one can do more. In fact, any orthomodular poset
\mathbf{P}
can be arranged into a total algebra by assigning a bounded commutative directoid
\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{P})
with antitone involution to
\mathbf{P}
satisfying further equational conditions.

Definition 3.18

(cf.
[
6
]
)
.

An
orthomodular
directoid is a bounded commutative directoid with antitone involution

An ortho-directoid is an algebra

and so the desired result follows trivially. Otherwise, we have

\displaystyle(x\sqcap y^{\prime})\sqcup(y\sqcup(((x\sqcap y^{\prime})\sqcup y)\sqcup z)^{\prime})^{\prime}

\displaystyle\approx\max\{x\sqcap y^{\prime},(y\sqcup(((x\sqcap y^{\prime})\sqcup y)\sqcup z)^{\prime})^{\prime}\}\approx

\displaystyle\approx(y\sqcup(((x\sqcap y^{\prime})\sqcup y)\sqcup z)^{\prime})^{\prime}.

The equality
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{A}))=\mathbf{A}
is straightforward.
∎

The next corollary is immediate. It fully characterizes orthoposets induced by orthoalgebras in terms of their assigned directoids.

Corollary 3.26
.

Let
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
be a bounded poset with an antitone involution. Then
\mathbf{P}
is the bounded poset with antitone involution induced by an orthoalgebra if and only if there exists an assigned directoid
\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{P})
which is an ortho-directoid.

Moreover, since an orthoalgebra
\mathbf{A}
is an orthomodular poset if and only if
x\oplus y=x\lor y
, for any pair of orthogonal elements
x,y\in A
(see e.g.
[
19
]
), by Lemma
3.15
one has also the following

Corollary 3.27
.

Let
\mathbf{A}=(A,\oplus,^{\prime},0,1)
be an orthoalgebra. Then
\mathbf{A}
is an orthomodular poset if and only if
\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{A})
satisfies
2.
of Definition
3.18
.

In other words, we have a full equational characterization of the class of orthomodular posets within the class of orthoalgebras by means of their assigned varieties of bounded directoids with antitone involution which, in turn, are subvarieties of
\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{P}}_{c}
. The results of this section, as far as we know, establish novel connections between orthomodular posets (lattices), orthoalgebras and paraorthomodular posets.

4
Dedekind-MacNeille completions

In this section we investigate bounded posets with an antitone involution whose Dedekind-MacNeille completion are paraorthomodular lattices.

It is well-known that every poset
(P,\leq)
can be embedded into a complete lattice
\mathbf{L}
. We frequently take the so-called Dedekind-MacNeille completion
\BDM(P,\leq)
for this
\mathbf{L}
.

Hence, let
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq)
be a poset. Put
\DM(\mathbf{P}):=\{L(A)\mid A\subseteq P\}
. Then
\BDM(\mathbf{P}):=(\DM(\mathbf{P}),\subseteq)
is a complete lattice and
x\mapsto L(x)
an embedding from
\mathbf{P}
to
\BDM(\mathbf{P})
preserving all existing joins and meets, and an order isomorphism between the posets
\mathbf{P}
and
(\{L(x)\mid x\in P\},\subseteq)
. We usually identify
P
with
\{L(x)\mid x\in P\}
.

It is easy to see that if
B,C\subseteq P
such that
B\leq C
then
\bigvee_{\BDM(\mathbf{P})}B=\bigwedge_{\BDM(\mathbf{P})}C
if and only if
L(C)\leq U(B)
.

By Banaschewski (
[
1
]
) and Schmidt (
[
32
]
) the Dedekind-MacNeille completion of a poset
\mathbf{P}
is (up to isomorphism) any complete lattice
\mathbf{L}
into which
\mathbf{P}
can be supremum-densely and infimum-densely embedded (i.e.  for every element
x\in L
there exist
M,Q\subseteq P
such that
x=\bigvee\varphi(M)=\bigwedge\varphi(Q)
, where
\varphi\colon{}P\to L
is the embedding).

In what follows, if
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
is a bounded poset with an antitone involution
′
and
X,Y\subseteq P
, we define:

•

X^{\prime}:=\{x^{\prime}\in P\mid x\in X\}
,

•

X^{\bot}:=\{a\in P\mid a\leq x^{\prime}\text{ for all }x\in X\}=L(X^{\prime})
,

•

X\vee_{\bot\bot}Y:=(X\cup Y)^{\bot\bot}
.

A well-known result by Riečanová (
[
31
]
) characterizes those effect algebras admitting an effect algebraic Dedekind-MacNeille completion in terms of strong
D
-continuity. Since we intend to characterize those involutive posets admitting a paraorthomodular Dedekind-MacNeille completion we will need a slightly weaker notion of weak
D
-continuity.

Definition 4.1
.

A bounded poset
(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
with an antitone involution
′
is called weakly
D
-continuous if and only if for all
B,C\subseteq P
with
B\leq C
the following condition is satisfied:

(WDC)

If
\bigwedge\nolimits_{\mathbf{P}}(C\cup B^{\prime})=0
then
L(C)\leq U(B)
.

In the following, we establish a characterization of involutive bounded posets with paraorthomodular Dedekind-MacNeille completion.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
be a bounded poset with an antitone involution
′
. Then
\BDM(\mathbf{P})
is paraorthomodular if and only if
\mathbf{P}
is weakly
D
-continuous.

Proof.

First assume
\BDM(\mathbf{P})
to be paraorthomodular,
B,C\subseteq P
,
B\leq C
and
\bigwedge\nolimits_{\mathbf{P}}(C\cup B^{\prime})=0
. Let
X:=\bigvee_{\BDM(\mathbf{P})}B
and
Y:=\bigwedge_{\BDM(\mathbf{P})}C
. Then
X\subseteq Y
and
X^{\bot}\wedge Y=0
. Since
\BDM(\mathbf{P})
is paraorthomodular we obtain
X=Y
. We conclude that
L(C)\leq U(B)
, i.e.
\mathbf{P}
is weakly
D
-continuous. Conversely, assume
\mathbf{P}
to be weakly continuous. Since
\BDM(\mathbf{P})
is a complete lattice with antitone involution
⊥
it is enough to check the following condition:

If
X,Y\in\DM(\mathbf{P})
,
X\subseteq Y
and
X^{\bot}\wedge Y=0
then
X=Y
.

We put
B:=X
and
C:=U(Y)
. Then
B\leq C
and
\bigwedge\nolimits_{\mathbf{P}}(C\cup B^{\prime})=0
. We conclude from (WDC) that
X=\bigvee_{\BDM(\mathbf{P})}B=\bigwedge_{\BDM(\mathbf{P})}C=Y
. Hence
\BDM(\mathbf{P})
is paraorthomodular.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Every weakly
D
-continuous involutive bounded poset is paraorthomodular.

The next definition and theorem are suggested by a similar result of Niederle for Boolean posets (
[
29
, Theorem 17]
).

Definition 4.4
.

Let
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
be a bounded poset with an antitone involution
′
. A subset
X
of
P
is called involution-closed and doubly dense in
\mathbf{P}
if the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)

a=\bigvee_{\mathbf{P}}(L(a)\cap X)=\bigwedge_{\mathbf{P}}(U(a)\cap X)
for all
a\in P
,

(ii)

X^{\prime}\subseteq X
,

(iii)

0,1\in X
.

Remark 4.5
.

Recall that any involution-closed and doubly dense subset
X
in
\mathbf{P}
is a bounded poset with induced order and involution. Moreover, if
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
is a bounded poset with an antitone involution
′
then
P
is an involution-closed and doubly dense subset in its Dedekind-MacNeille completion
\BDM(\mathbf{P})
. This can be shown by the same arguments as in
(
[
29
, Theorem 16]
)
, so we omit it.

Theorem 4.6
.

Embedding theorem for weakly
D
-continuous paraorthomodular posets.

Let
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
be a bounded poset with an antitone involution
′
. Then
\mathbf{P}
is a weakly
D
-continuous paraorthomodular poset if and only if there exists a complete paraorthomodular lattice
\mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{\prime},0,1)
such that
P
is an involution-closed and doubly dense subset of
L
.

Proof.

From Theorem
4.2
we know that every weakly
D
-continuous paraorthomodular poset
\mathbf{P}
is an involution-closed and doubly dense subset in
\BDM(\mathbf{P})
. Conversely, let
\mathbf{P}
be an involution-closed and doubly dense subset of a complete paraorthomodular lattice
\mathbf{L}=(L,\wedge,\vee,{}^{\prime},0,1)
. Then
\mathbf{P}
is an involutive bounded poset. Assume
B,C\subseteq P
,
B\leq C
and
\bigwedge\nolimits_{\mathbf{P}}(C\cup B^{\prime})=0
. Put
X:=\bigvee_{\mathbf{L}}B
and
Y:=\bigwedge_{\mathbf{L}}C
. Then
X\leq Y
and
X^{\prime}\wedge Y=0
(since
u\in P
,
u\leq X^{\prime}\wedge Y
implies
u\in L(C\cup B^{\prime})
, i.e.
u=0
). Since
\mathbf{L}
is paraorthomodular we obtain
X=Y
, i.e.
\mathbf{P}
is weakly
D
-continuous and from Corollary
4.3
we have that it is also paraorthomodular.
∎

In
[
18
]
Finch proved that an orthomodular poset admits an orthomodular DM-completion if and only if it satisfies the so-called
B
-property. Fazio, Ledda and Paoli gave in
[
16
, Proposition 2]
a new characterization of the
B
-property for orthoposets as follows.

Proposition 4.7
.

[
16
, Proposition 2]
An orthoposet
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
has the
B
-property if and only if it satisfies for all
X,Y\subseteq P

(FLP)

L(X)\subsetneqq L(Y)
implies
L(Y)\cap LU(X^{\prime})\neq\{0\}
,

Using this concept we can prove

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
be a bounded poset with an antitone involution
′
. Then
\BDM(\mathbf{P})
is paraorthomodular if and only if
\mathbf{P}
satisfies
(FLP)
for all
X,Y\subseteq P
.

Proof.

If
\BDM(\mathbf{P})
is paraorthomodular,
X,Y\subseteq P
and
L(X)\subsetneqq L(Y)
then

LU(X^{\prime})\cap L(Y)=L(L(X)^{\prime})\cap L(Y)=L(X)^{\bot}\cap L(Y)\neq\{0\}.

If
\mathbf{P}
satisfies (FLP) for all
X,Y\subseteq P
,
A,B\in\DM(\mathbf{P})
,
A\subseteq B
and
A^{\bot}\cap B=\{0\}
then

B\cap LU(U(A)^{\prime})=B\cap LUL(A^{\prime})=B\cap L(A^{\prime})=B\cap A^{\bot}=\{0\}

and hence
A=B
proving paraorthomodularity of
\BDM(\mathbf{P})
.
∎

Summarizing the previous results we obtain

Corollary 4.9
.

For a bounded poset
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
with an antitone involution
′
the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

\BDM(\mathbf{P})
is paraorthomodular,

(ii)

\mathbf{P}
is weakly
D
-continuous,

(iii)

\mathbf{P}
satisfies
(FLP)
for all
X,Y\subseteq P
.

Uniform approximation of Betti numbers
Denis-Charles Cisinski

Abstract
. We prove that Lefschetz’s principle of approximating the cohomology of a possibly singular affine scheme of finite type over a field by the cohomology of a suitable (thickening of a) hyperplane section can be made uniform: in the affine case, we can choose the hyperplane section independently of the cohomology. Using Jouanolou’s trick, this gives a new way to bound the Betti numbers of quasi-projective schemes over a field, independently of the cohomology. This is achieved through a motivic version of Deligne’s generic base change formula and an axiomatic presentation of the theory of perverse sheaves. These methods produce generating families of Voevodsky motivic sheaves that are realized in perverse sheaves over any base of equal characteristic.



Introduction

Since Grothendieck introduced étale cohomology,
proving that the Betti numbers of
\ell
-adic cohomology
of algebraic varieties over a separably closed field
are independent of
\ell

remains a deep open problem in positive characteristic.
The case of smooth and proper algeraic varieties
is reached by weight techniques, using Deligne’s proof of the Weil conjectures,
but there is not any other general result in this direction.
The next best thing consists in providing uniform bounds of Betti
numbers. This can be done by dévissage from the case of
smooth and proper algebraic varieties,
and then applying descent techniques together with de Jong’s alteration theorem,
as explained by Katz
[
Kat01
, Theorem 5]

who credits the result to de Jong and Berthelot.
There are results for non-constant coefficients as well, working with
compatible families
[
KL85
,
Kat01
,
Org19
]
.

The present notes give new proofs of existence of such uniform bound, using
motivic techniques (in particular,
we do not only prove independence of
\ell
results
but rather independence against a large class of Weil cohomologies).
We apply Lefschetz’s principle of approximating
a quasi-projective scheme
X
by a suitable hyperplane section.
Of course, this needs adjustments in order to take into account the
singularities of

as well as the fact that we want the choice of
the hyperplane section to be independent of the cohomology we work
with (e.g.

or Berthelot’s rigid cohomology if

j_{!}\,j^{*}A_{X}\cong j_{!}\,j^{*}\,v_{!}\,v^{*}A_{X})
).

Definition 3.2
.

Let
X
be an affine scheme of finite type over a field
k
of dimension
d
.
A
Lefschetz subscheme of

is a closed subscheme
Z
of
X
of the form

Z=W\cup H
, where
w\colon W\hookrightarrow X
is a closed subscheme
of dimension
<d
, with affine complement,
that contains all irreducible components of

of dimension
<d
,
such that

is regular,
and

j_{!}\,j^{*}\mathbf{Z}_{X}

in the sense of

,
where

denotes the complement of

in
X
.
The

(X,Z)
is the trace of the identity of

in
\mathit{DM}(\mathrm{Spec}(k))
; we will denote it

\mathit{rk}(X,Z)=(-1)^{d}\mathit{Tr}\big(1_{M(X,Z)}\big)\in\mathbf{Z}

(this is always an integer because it coincides with the Euler characteristic
of its

-adic realizations

, Cor. 2.2.7]
).
A

is a pair
(X,Z)
that consists of an affine
scheme

.

A

Lefschetz filtrations exist in practice thanks to the following proposition.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let

be an infinite field.
Then, for any affine

-scheme

in

of dimension
<\dim X
, there is a Lefschetz subscheme

of

containing

.

along a finite extension of

.

Define

to be the union of

with the singular locus of

as well as with the irreducible components of

of dimension
<d
.
We may enlarge

so that its complement if affine, and
then apply Corollary

to

,

in

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
X
be an affine scheme of dimension
d\geq 0
,

Z
a Lefschetz subscheme of
X
, and
j\colon X-Z\hookrightarrow X

the corresponding open immersion. Then
\mathit{rk}(X,Z)

is a non-negative integer. Furthermore,
for any Artin-Verdier six-functors formalism
D
defined on
k
-schemes
of finite type, the induced realization functor

r_{D}\colon\mathit{DM}(\mathrm{Spec}(k))\to D(\mathrm{Spec}(k))

yields

H^{q}(X,j_{!}\mathbf{1}_{X-Z})\cong\begin{cases}0&\text{if $q\neq d$,}\\
r_{D}(M(X,Z)[-d])^{\wedge}&\text{if $q=d$.}\end{cases}

(where the cohomology is defined using the ordinary
t
-structure
as in Remark
2.21
).
The rank of
H^{d}(X,j_{!}\mathbf{1}_{X-Z})
(defined as the trace of its identity)
is equal to the class of
\mathit{rk}(X,Z)

in
\pi_{0}\operatorname{Map}_{D(\mathrm{Spec}(k))}(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1})
.

In particular, if
k
is separably closed,
for any prime
\ell
distinct from the characteristic of
k
,
we can compute the
\ell
-adic cohomology of the pair
(X,Z)
motivically:

H^{q}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,j_{!}\mathbf{Z}_{\ell})\cong\begin{cases}0&\text{if $q\neq d$,}\\
\mathbf{Z}_{\ell}^{\mathit{rk}(X,Z)}&\text{if $q=d$.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
D
be an Artin-Verdier six-functors formalism defined on
k
-schemes
of finite type. We then have colimit preserving functors

r_{D}\colon\mathit{DM}(X)\to D(X)

that commute with the six operations.
With the notations of Remark
2.21
,
if
M(X,Z)^{\wedge}\cong a_{*}j_{!}j^{*}\mathbf{Z}_{X}

denotes the dual of
M(X,Z)
in
\mathit{DM}(\mathrm{Spec}(k))
, we thus
have

r_{D}(M(X,Z)^{\wedge})\cong\Gamma(X,j_{!}\,j^{*}\mathbf{1}_{X})\,.

Therefore,

r_{D}(M(X,Z)[-d])\cong\Gamma(X,j_{!}\,j^{*}\mathbf{1}_{X}[d])^{\wedge}

is concentrated in degree
0
by virtue of Theorem
2.22

applied to
M=\mathbf{Z}_{W}
and
U=W
(with the notations of
Definition
3.2
).
Since
r_{D}
is symmetric monoidal, it preserves the formation of traces
of endomorphisms of dualizable objects.
For any
D
such that the constructible
heart
D^{\heartsuit}_{c}(\mathrm{Spec}(k))
is tannakian over
\mathbf{Q}
,
we get that
\mathit{rk}(X,Z)
is the Euler
characteristic of the image of
a_{*}j_{!}j^{*}\mathbf{Z}_{X}[d]
through
r_{D}
,
and thus a non-negative integer. For instance, if
\ell
is a prime
not equal to the characteristic of
k
, and if
\bar{k}
is a
separable closure of
k
, we can associate to each
k
-scheme
X
the

\bar{k}
-scheme
\bar{X}
obtained by pulling back
X
to
\mathrm{Spec}(\bar{k})
.
The assignment

X\mapsto D(\bar{X},\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})

then defines an Artin-Verdier six functors formalism
D

such that
D^{\heartsuit}_{c}(\mathrm{Spec}(k))
is the category of
finite dimensional
\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}
-vector
spaces. This proves that
\mathit{rk}(X,Z)
is a non-negative
integer.

Let us assume that
k=\bar{k}
is separably closed
(in order to simplify the notations). We can
analyze the situation a little bit further as follows.
The morphisms of rings
\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}\leftarrow\mathbf{Z}_{\ell}\to\mathbf{F}_{\ell}

induce isomorphisms on their Grothendieck rings

\mathbf{Z}=K_{0}(\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})\cong K_{0}(\mathbf{Z}_{\ell})\cong K_{0}(\mathbf{F}_{\ell})=\mathbf{Z}\,.

Therefore, for any perfect complex of
\mathbf{Z}_{\ell}
-modules
C
, we always have:

\sum_{q}(-1)^{q}\dim_{\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}}H^{q}(C)\otimes\mathbf{Q}=\sum_{q}(-1)^{q}\dim_{\mathbf{F}_{\ell}}H^{q}(C\otimes^{L}_{\mathbf{Z}_{\ell}}\mathbf{F}_{\ell})\,.

If ever both
C\otimes\mathbf{Q}
and
C\otimes^{L}_{\mathbf{Z}_{\ell}}\mathbf{F}_{\ell}
are concentrated in
cohomological degree
d
, this means that the dimensions of

H^{d}(C\otimes^{L}_{\mathbf{Z}_{\ell}}\mathbf{F}_{\ell})
and of

H^{d}(C)\otimes\mathbf{Q}
are the same.
A direct consequence can be seen in the exact sequence

0\to H^{q}(C)\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}_{\ell}}\mathbf{F}_{\ell}\to H^{q}(C\otimes^{L}_{\mathbf{Z}_{\ell}}\mathbf{F}_{\ell})\to\mathrm{Tor}_{1}(H^{q+1}(C),\mathbf{F}_{\ell})\to 0\,.

This yields an isomorphism (an injective linear map from a space that has at least
the dimension of the codomain)

H^{q}(C)\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}_{\ell}}\mathbf{F}_{\ell}\cong H^{q}(C\otimes^{L}_{\mathbf{Z}_{\ell}}\mathbf{F}_{\ell})

for all
q
(for
q\neq d
this is even easier)
and tells us that
H^{q+1}(C)
is free for all
q
.
Finally, we must have
H^{q}(C)\cong 0
for any
q\neq d

and
H^{d}(C)\cong\mathbf{Z}^{r}_{\ell}
with
r
the dimension of

H^{d}(C)\otimes\mathbf{Q}
as a
\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}
-vector space.

On constructible objects, the
\ell
-adic realization functor

r_{\ell}\colon\mathit{DM}_{c}(Y)\to D^{b}_{c}(Y,\mathbf{Z}_{\ell})

induces the realization functors

\mathit{DM}_{c}(Y)\to D^{b}_{c}(Y,\mathbf{F}_{\ell})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathit{DM}_{c}(Y)\to D^{b}_{c}(Y,\mathbf{Z}_{\ell})\otimes\mathbf{Q}=D^{b}_{c}(Y,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})

by the formulæ

M\mapsto r_{\ell}(M)\otimes^{L}_{\mathbf{Z}_{\ell}}\mathbf{F}_{\ell}\quad\text{and}\quad M\mapsto r_{\ell}(M)\otimes\mathbf{Q}

respectively. Therefore, by virtue of Theorem
2.22

for
D^{b}_{c}(-,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})

and
D^{b}_{c}(-,\mathbf{F}_{\ell})
, we see that we can
apply the discussion above with
C
be the
\ell
-adic
realization of the motive
a_{*}j_{!}j^{*}\mathbf{Z}_{X}[d]
in
the stable
\infty
-category

D^{b}_{c}(\mathrm{Spec}(k),\mathbf{Z}_{\ell})\cong\mathit{Perf}(\mathbf{Z}_{\ell})
.
This proves the last part
of the lemma.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Let
(X,Z)
be a Lefschetz pair, with
X
of dimension
d

over a separably closed field.
Let
j\colon X-Z\to X
be the corresponding embedding.
Given any prime number
\ell
distinct from the
characteristic of the ground field, we have
a long exact sequence

\cdots\to H^{q}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,j_{!}\mathbf{Z}_{\ell})\to H^{q}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbf{Z}_{\ell})\to H^{q}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(Z,\mathbf{Z}_{\ell})\to H^{q+1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,j_{!}\mathbf{Z}_{\ell})\to\cdots

By virtue of the preceding lemma, this yields isomorphisms

H^{q}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbf{Z}_{\ell})\cong H^{q}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(Z,\mathbf{Z}_{\ell})\quad\text{for $q\neq d,d-1$,}

and an exact sequence of the form below.

0\to H^{d-1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbf{Z}_{\ell})\to H^{d-1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(Z,\mathbf{Z}_{\ell})\to\mathbf{Z}_{\ell}^{\mathit{rk}(X,Z)}\to H^{d}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbf{Z}_{\ell})\to 0

The map
H^{d-1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbf{Z}_{\ell})\to H^{d-1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(Z,\mathbf{Z}_{\ell})
is thus a split monomorphism inducing an isomorphism
on torsion subgroups

H^{d-1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbf{Z}_{\ell})_{\mathit{tors}}\cong H^{d-1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(Z,\mathbf{Z}_{\ell})_{\mathit{tors}}
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let

be an affine scheme of Krull dimension

,
of finite type over a separably closed

, and

.

3.8
.

We can interpret the existence of Lefschetz filtrations as an approximation
of the motivic
t
-structure on
\mathit{DM}(X)
(whose existence would imply
Grothendieck’s standard conjectures
[
Bei10
]
). In order to simplify our task,
we will work with
\mathbf{Q}
-linear coefficients (it is straightforward to produce an
integral version from any
\mathbf{Q}
-linear one using the rigidity theorem).

We observe that objects of the form
M(X)(n)
, with
X
affine of finite type over
the base field
k
and

are compact and generate
\mathit{DM}(\mathrm{Spec}(k))
as
a compactly generated stable
\infty
-category.
If
k
is infinite, this implies that the objects of the form
M(X,Z)(n)

with
(X,Z)
a Lefschetz pair and

also form a generating
family. In order to allow any field, we proceed as follows.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
k
be any field.
We define the family
\mathcal{G}_{k}
as follows:
this is the family of motives of the form
f_{*}M(X,Z)[-\dim X](n)
,
where
f\colon\mathrm{Spec}(K)\to\mathrm{Spec}(k)
is a Galois extension of the base field,
with
(X,Z)
a Lefschetz pair defined over
K
, and
n\in\mathbf{Z}
.
Then
\mathcal{G}_{k}
is a family of compact generators of
\mathit{DM}(k,\mathbf{Q})

as a cocomplete stable
\infty
-category.

Proof.

The case where
k
is infinite is obvious from
Proposition
3.3
:
any affine scheme has a Lefschetz filtration
and given a Lefschetz filtration by subschemes
X_{i}
, one can
reconstruct the motive of
X
from the
M(X_{i},X_{i-1})
’s.
Since the motives of the form
M(X)(n)
with
X
affine form a generating
family, this proves the claim if

is infinite,
in particular for
k
separably closed.
In general,
we know that, if
\bar{k}
is a separable closure of

, then the pullback
functor

\mathit{DM}(\mathrm{Spec}(k),\mathbf{Q})\to\mathit{DM}(\mathrm{Spec}(\bar{k}),\mathbf{Q})

is conservative and preserve the formation of internal Hom’s of the form

as long as

is compact; see
[
CD19
, Lemma 4.3.7]
. By a continuity argument
(as in
[
CD19
, Prop. 4.3.1]
), it is now easy to see that
it suffices to prove that

generates
\mathit{DM}(\mathrm{Spec}(k),\mathbf{Q})

if

generates
\mathit{DM}(\mathrm{Spec}(\bar{k}),\mathbf{Q})

which we already know.
∎

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
M
be any constructible object in
\mathit{DM}(\mathrm{Spec}(k),\mathbf{Q})
.
There exists a constant

and integer

For any

\mathbf{Q}

.

The preceding proposition means that

(such as extracting a direct factor in a finite coproducts, taking cofibers, shifting

, and

.

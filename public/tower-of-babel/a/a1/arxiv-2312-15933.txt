On the trace formulas and completeness property of root vectors systems for 2 × 2 2\times 2 Dirac type operators Journal: Journal of Functional Analysis
Anton A. Lunyov, Mark M. Malamud

Abstract
The paper is concerned with the completeness property of the system of root vectors of a boundary value problem for the following 2 × 2 2\times 2 Dirac type equation L ​ y = − i ​ B − 1 ​ y ′ + Q ⁡ ( x ) ​ y = λ ​ y , y = col ⁡ ( y 1 , y 2 ) , x ∈ [ 0 , 1 ] , \displaystyle Ly=-iB^{-1}y^{\prime}+Q(x)y=\lambda y,\quad y=\col(y_{1},y_{2}),\quad x\in[0,1], B = diag ( b 1 , b 2 ) , b 1 < 0 < b 2 , and Q ∈ W 1 n [ 0 , 1 ] ⊗ ℂ 2 × 2 , \displaystyle B=\diag(b_{1},b_{2}),\quad b_{1}<0<b_{2},\quad\text{and}\quad Q\in W_{1}^{n}[0,1]\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2}, subject to general non-regular two-point boundary conditions C ​ y ​ ( 0 ) + D ​ y ​ ( 1 ) = 0 Cy(0)+Dy(1)=0 . If b 2 = − b 1 = 1 b_{2}=-b_{1}=1 this equation is equivalent to the one dimensional Dirac equation. We establish asymptotic expansion of the characteristic determinant of this boundary value problem. This expansion directly yields new completeness result for the system of root vectors of such boundary value problem with non-regular and even degenerate boundary conditions. We also present several explicit completeness results in terms of the values Q ( j ) ​ ( 0 ) Q^{(j)}(0) and Q ( j ) ​ ( 1 ) Q^{(j)}(1) . In the case of degenerate boundary conditions and analytic Q ⁡ ( ⋅ ) Q(\cdot) , the criterion of completeness property is established.

Introduction

In this paper we continue investigations from the cycle of our previous papers
[
32
,
15
,
17
,
18
,
20
]
on spectral properties of first order
n\times n
systems of ODE.
Here we restrict ourselves to the case of
2\times 2
Dirac type system of the form

-iB^{-1}y^{\prime}+Q(x)y=\lambda y,\qquad y=\col(y_{1},y_{2}),\qquad x\in[0,1],

(1.1)

where

B=\diag(b_{1},b_{2}),\quad b_{1}<0<b_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad Q=\begin{pmatrix}0&Q_{12}\\
Q_{21}&0\end{pmatrix}\in L^{1}([0,1];\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2}).

(1.2)

In the case
-b_{1}=b_{2}=1
system (
1.1
) is equivalent to the classical
2\times 2
Dirac system.

With system (
1.1
) one associates, in a natural way,
the maximal operator
L=L(Q)
acting in
L^{2}([0,1];\mathbb{C}^{2})
on
the domain

\dom(L)=\{y\in W_{1}^{1}([0,1];\mathbb{C}^{2}):Ly\in L^{2}([0,1];\mathbb{C}^{2})\}.

(1.3)

Here
W_{p}^{n}[a,b]
denotes the Sobolev space of functions
f
having
n-1
absolutely continuous derivatives on
[a,b]
and satisfying
f^{(n)}\in L^{p}[a,b]
, in particular
W_{p}^{0}[a,b]=L^{p}[a,b]

To obtain a boundary value problem (BVP), equation (
1.1
) is subject to the following boundary conditions

U_{j}(y):=a_{j1}y_{1}(0)+a_{j2}y_{2}(0)+a_{j3}y_{1}(1)+a_{j4}y_{2}(1)=0,\quad j\in\{1,2\}.

(1.4)

Going forward we set

A_{jk}:=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1j}&a_{1k}\\
a_{2j}&a_{2k}\end{pmatrix}\qquad\text{and}\qquad J_{jk}:=\det(A_{jk}),\qquad j,k\in\{1,\ldots,{4}\}.

(1.5)

Denote by
L_{U}:=L_{U}(Q)
the operator associated in

L^{2}([0,1];\mathbb{C}^{n})
with the
BVP (
1.1
)–(
1.4
). It is defined as the
restriction of the maximal operator
L=L(Q)
to the domain

\dom(L_{U})=\{y\in\dom(L):U_{1}(y)=U_{2}(y)=0\}.

(1.6)

The general spectral problem for
n\times n
first order system of the form (
1.1
)
for the first was investigated by G. Birkhoff and R. Langer
[
2
]
. More precisely, they introduced the concepts of
regular and strictly regular boundary conditions
, investigated the asymptotic behavior of eigenvalues and eigenfunctions and proved
a pointwise convergence result
on spectral decompositions for the corresponding differential operator.

The first completeness result for such systems was established by V.P. Ginzburg
[
6
]
in the case
B=I_{n}
,
Q(\cdot)=0
.

Recall that boundary conditions (
1.4
) are called
regular
, if

J_{32}=\det(A_{12}P_{+}+A_{34}P_{-})\neq 0\quad\text{and}\quad J_{14}=\det(A_{12}P_{-}+A_{34}P_{+})\neq 0,

(1.7)

where
P_{+}
(resp.
P_{-}
) is the spectral projection
onto positive (resp. negative) part of the spectrum of the matrix
B=B^{*}
.

V.A. Marchenko
[
33
]
was the first to establish
completeness property for the system of root functions of the Dirac operator
L_{U}
(
-b_{1}=b_{2}
) with regular boundary conditions and continuous potential matrix
Q
. The last restriction occurs because the transformation operators used for the proof was constructed in
[
33
]
only for continuous
Q
.

Later, L.L. Oridoroga and one of the authors
[
32
]
established completeness property for
B
-weakly regular
boundary value problems for arbitrary
n\times n
first order systems of ODE with integrable matrix potential
Q\in L^{1}([0,1];\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
(originally this result was announced in
[
30
]
much earlier).
In particular, for
2\times 2
Dirac type system (with
Q\in L^{1}([0,1];\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
)

the condition of
B
-weakly regularity turns into (
1.7
)
and hence ensures the completeness property for such BVP with regular boundary conditions.

Let us also briefly mention that the Riesz basis property in
L^{2}([0,1];\mathbb{C}^{2})
of BVP (
1.1
)–(
1.4
)
for
2\times 2
Dirac system with various smoothness
assumptions on the potential matrix
Q
was investigated in numerous papers
(see
[
3
,
5
,
4
,
16
,
37
,
18
,
36
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
8
]
and the references therein).
The first general result for non-smooth potentials was obtained by P. Djakov and B. Mityagin
[
3
,
5
]

who proved under the assumption
Q\in L^{2}([0,1];\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
that the system of root vectors of the BVP (
1.1
)–(
1.4
)
with strictly regular boundary conditions
forms a Riesz basis and forms
a block Riesz basis
whenever boundary conditions are only regular.
Note however that the methods of these papers substantially rely on
L^{2}
-techniques (such as Parseval equality, Hilbert-Schmidt operators, etc.) and that cannot be applied to
L^{1}
-potentials.
The most complete result on the Riesz basis property for
2\times 2
Dirac and Dirac-type systems, respectively, with
Q\in L^{1}([0,1];\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
and strictly regular boundary conditions was obtained independently, by different methods and at the same time by A.M. Savchuk and A.A. Shkalikov
[
37
]
and by the authors
[
16
,
18
]
.
The case of regular boundary conditions is treated in
[
37
]
for the first time.

Let us denote by
\Lambda_{Q}:=\Lambda_{U,Q}=\{\lambda_{Q,n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}:=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
the spectrum of the operator
L_{U}(Q)
, counting multiplicity.
Namely, if
m_{a}(\lambda_{n})
is the algebraic multiplicity of the eigenvalue
\lambda_{n}
of the operator
L_{U}(Q)
, then the number
\lambda_{n}
appears in the sequence
\Lambda_{Q}
exactly
m_{a}(\lambda_{n})
times.
It is worth mentioning, that the algebraic multiplicity
m_{a}(\lambda_{n})
of the eigenvalue
\lambda_{n}
coincides with the multiplicity of the zero
\lambda_{n}
of the characteristic determinant
\Delta_{Q}(\cdot)=\Delta_{Q,U}(\cdot)
, an entire function given by the following formula:

\Delta_{Q}(\lambda)=J_{12}+J_{34}e^{i(b_{1}+b_{2})\lambda}\\
+J_{32}\varphi_{11}(1,\lambda)+J_{13}\varphi_{12}(1,\lambda)+J_{42}\varphi_{21}(1,\lambda)+J_{14}\varphi_{22}(1,\lambda).

(1.8)

where
(\varphi_{jk}(x,\lambda))_{j,k=1}^{2}:=\Phi(x,\lambda)
is the fundamental matrix of the system (
1.1
) (uniquely) determined by the initial condition
\Phi(0,\lambda)=I_{2}
.
Recall also that

\Delta_{0}(\lambda)=J_{12}+J_{34}e^{i(b_{1}+b_{2})\lambda}+J_{32}e^{ib_{1}\lambda}+J_{14}e^{ib_{2}\lambda}

(1.9)

is the characteristic determinant of problem (
1.1
)–(
1.4
) with
Q=0
.

Note that
boundary conditions (
1.7
) are regular if and only if the entire function
\Delta_{0}(\cdot)
is of maximal growth in both half-planes
\mathbb{C}_{\pm}
. In this connection we recall one of the key results of our previous work
[
18
]
which states
that the characteristic determinant
\Delta_{Q}(\cdot)
of the problem (
1.1
)–(
1.4
) with
Q(\cdot)\in L^{1}([0,1];\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
admits the following representation:

\displaystyle\Delta_{Q}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{0}(\lambda)+\int^{1}_{0}g_{1}(t)e^{ib_{1}\lambda t}dt+\int^{1}_{0}g_{2}(t)e^{ib_{2}\lambda t}dt,

(1.10)

with certain functions
g_{1},g_{2}\in L^{1}[0,1]
expressed via kernels of the transformation operators. In turn, this formula ensures that the
determinant
\Delta_{Q}(\cdot)
is also an entire sine-type function with the indicator diagram
[-ib_{2},i|b_{1}|]\subset i\mathbb{R}
,
i.e. has the exponential type
|b_{1}|(b_{2})
in
\mathbb{C}_{+}(\mathbb{C}_{-})
.
However, the converse is not true:
certain non-regular boundary conditions
also lead to the characteristic
determinant
\Delta_{Q}(\cdot)
with the same indicator diagram
[-ib_{2},i|b_{1}|]
(see
[
32
]
and
[

]
)

A stronger effect (of the maximal growth of

[

.

Emphasize that representation (
1.10
) plays a crucial role in several recent investigations. For instance, A.S. Makin
[
23
,
24

applied representation (

Dirac system if and only if this function is of Paley-Wiener class.
In
[
25

Makin used this representation to find explicit algebraic conditions on a potential matrix

that guarantee the (non-block!) Riesz basis property for arbitrary regular but not necessary strictly regular

Dirac operator.
Recently the authors
[

applied this representation to evaluate Lipschitz dependance of the spectral
data on the potential matrix

running through compact sets of
L^{p}([0,\ell];\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
,
p\in[1,2]
.
Moreover, in
[
19

we generalized representation (
1.10

If the functions

\displaystyle\Delta_{Q}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=e^{ib_{1}\lambda}\cdot\Bigl(J_{32}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{g_{1}^{(k-1)}(1)}{(-ib_{1}\lambda)^{k}}+o(\lambda^{-n})\Bigr),\qquad\Im\lambda\to+\infty,

(1.11)

\displaystyle\Delta_{Q}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=e^{ib_{2}\lambda}\cdot\Bigl(J_{14}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{g_{2}^{(k-1)}(1)}{(-ib_{2}\lambda)^{k}}+o(\lambda^{-n})\Bigr),\qquad\Im\lambda\to-\infty,

(see Remark

)

)

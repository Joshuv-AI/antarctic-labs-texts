Random Schrödinger operators with complex decaying potentials
Jean-Claude Cuenin, Konstantin Merz

Abstract
. We prove that the eigenvalues of a continuum random Schrödinger operator − Δ + V ω -\Delta+V_{\omega} of Anderson type, with complex decaying potential, can be bounded (with high probability) in terms of an L q L^{q} norm of the potential for all q ≤ d + 1 q\leq d+1 . This shows that in the random setting, the exponent q q can be essentially doubled compared to the deterministic bounds of Frank (Bull. Lond. Math. Soc., 2011). This improvement is based on ideas of Bourgain (Discrete Contin. Dyn. Syst., 2002) related to almost sure scattering for lattice Schrödinger operators.

2.
Born series

The proof of the eigenvalue estimates starts with the standard observation that
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,\infty)
is an eigenvalue if and only if
I+R_{0}(z)V
fails to be invertible as a bounded operator. This follows from the identity

\displaystyle-\Delta+V-z=(-\Delta-z)(I+R_{0}(z)V).

(10)

Here we denoted the free resolvent operator
(-\Delta-z)^{-1}
by
R_{0}(z)
and we omitted the subscript
\omega
on
V
. Similarly, we will denote the perturbed resolvent operator
(-\Delta+V-z)^{-1}
by
R(z)
.
To avoid confusion between the deterministic and the random potential we focus our attention on the Anderson type potentials (
2
).
In this case, the assumption that
V\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
already implies that
V
is bounded (this follows from (
4
) and the fact that the
\ell^{p}
spaces are nested). In particular,
R_{0}(z)V
is a bounded operator. In the general case (
3
), one truncates the potential at some fixed large level. Since the estimates of Theorems
1
–
3
are independent of the
L^{\infty}
norm of
V
and the truncated Schrödinger operator converges to the untruncated one in the norm resolvent sense, there is no loss of generality in assuming that the deterministic potential is bounded. In the following, we assume that
V
is supported on a ball of radius
R
, i.e. the setting of Theorem
1
.
The case where
V
is not compactly supported (Theorems
2
and
3
) will be considered in Section
7
.

Returning to (
10
), we see that
z
cannot be an eigenvalue if the Born series

\displaystyle R(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(-1)^{n}[R_{0}(z)V]^{n}R_{0}(z)

(11)

converges, which is the case if the spectral radius of
R_{0}V
is less than
1
. Consider the following multilinear expansion (omitting
z
),

\displaystyle[R_{0}V]^{n}=\sum_{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n}}R_{0}^{\sigma_{1}}VR_{0}^{\sigma_{2}}V\ldots R_{0}^{\sigma_{n}}V

(12)

where
\sigma_{j}\in\{\rm{low},\rm{high}\}
. Here,
R_{0}^{\rm low}
is the resolvent (smoothly) localized to frequencies in
B(0,2)
and
R_{0}^{\rm high}=R_{0}-R_{0}^{\rm low}
.
Since we are dealing with scale-invariant estimates, we may assume without loss of generality that
\lambda=1
, hence
z=(1+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon)^{2}
.
Then each summand is a composition of operators of the form

C^{(\delta_{2})}VC^{(\delta_{1})}
, where
C^{(\delta)}
denotes a function satisfying a bound

\displaystyle|C^{(\delta)}(\xi)|\leq(||2\pi\xi|^{2}-1|+\delta)^{-1/2}

(13)

and the corresponding Fourier multiplier is denoted by the same symbol.
Clearly, the bound
(
13
) holds with
\delta=1
for
C^{(\delta)}=(R_{0}^{\rm high})^{1/2}
or
C^{(\delta)}=|R_{0}^{\rm high}|^{1/2}
.
In Section
6.2
we will show that (
13
) holds with
\delta=1/R
if
C^{(\delta)}
is a mollification of
(R_{0}^{\rm low})^{1/2}
or
|R_{0}^{\rm low}|^{1/2}
at scale
1/R
. Such a mollification can always be performed (except for the first resolvent in the Born series, but this does not affect convergence), due to the localizing effect of the potential, which we assumed to be supported in a ball of radius
R
.
The spectral radius is given by Gelfand’s formula,
\operatorname{spr}(R_{0}V)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|[R_{0}V]^{n}\|^{1/n}
. Thus, in view of the previous discussion, we have

\operatorname{spr}(R_{0}V)\leq\sup\|C^{(\delta_{2})}VC^{(\delta_{1})}\|
, where the supremum is taken over all functions satisfying (
13
). We will ignore the high frequency part of the resolvent
R_{0}^{\rm high}

from now on since there are obvious elliptic estimates available for this part. We may thus restrict our attention to functions as in (
13
) that are compactly supported in
B(0,2)
. We summarize the observations of this Section in the following proposition.

Proposition 5
.

Let
z=(1+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon)^{2}
, with
|\varepsilon|\ll 1
. Let
V
be supported in a ball of radius
R
. If (for a given realization of
\omega
)

\displaystyle\|C^{(\delta_{2})}VC^{(\delta_{1})}\|\leq c<1

(14)

for all functions
C^{(\delta_{i})}
,
i=1,2
, satisfying (
13
) with
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}=1/R
and supported in
B(0,2)
, then
z
is not an eigenvalue of
-\Delta+V
.

We refer to operators of the form (
14
) as “elementary operators” since they form the building blocks of the Born series. We prove norm estimates on these and related operators in Section
6
. These estimates are the key technical elements in this work.

Remark 6
.

Strictly speaking, the previous argument is only valid for
\varepsilon\neq 0
, but there are techniques to extend this to embedded eigenvalues (

[

3.
Localization and discretization

3.1.
Localization in momentum space

Denote by
\mathcal{Q}_{h}
the collection of all cubes
Q_{h}
of sidelength
h
.
Define the weight function

\displaystyle w_{Q_{h}}(x)=(1+h^{-1}\operatorname{dist}(x,Q_{h}))^{-100d},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad Q_{h}\in\mathcal{Q}_{h}.

(15)

Lemma 6
.

Let
v\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and assume that
\hat{v}
is supported in
B(0,1/h)
. Then
v
is locally constant on cubes
Q_{h}
of sidelength
h
in the sense that

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{h})}\lesssim|Q_{h}|^{-1}\|v\|_{L^{1}(w_{Q_{h}})}.

Proof.

By scaling, it suffices to prove this for
h=1
.
Choose
\eta\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\eta=1
on
B(0,1)
. Then we have
\hat{v}=\eta\hat{v}
and hence
v=(\eta)^{\vee}\ast v
. Since
(\eta)^{\vee}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, it follows that

\displaystyle\kappa_{w}=\sup_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{1}}\sup_{(x,y)\in Q\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|(\eta)^{\vee}(x-y)|w_{Q}(y)^{-1}<\infty,

where the first supremum is taken over all cubes of sidelength one. Thus, for any cube
Q
of sidelength one and for
x\in Q
, we have

\displaystyle|v(x)|\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|(\eta)^{\vee}(x-y)||v(y)|\mathrm{d}y\leq\kappa_{w}\|v\|_{L^{1}(w_{Q})}.

Taking the supremum over
x\in Q
proves the claim.
∎

Lemma 7
.

Let
v\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and assume that
\hat{v}
is supported in
B(0,1/h)
.
Let
\Lambda_{h}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a set of
h
-separated points. Then for any
p\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\|v\|_{\ell^{p}(\Lambda_{h})}\lesssim h^{-d/p}\|v\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Proof.

Again by scaling, we can assume
h=1
. Thus, let

\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a set of
1
-separated points. Pick a collection of cubes
Q
of sidelength one that cover
\Lambda
. By Lemma
6
,

\displaystyle\|v\|_{\ell^{p}(\Lambda)}^{p}=\sum_{\nu\in\Lambda}|v(\nu)|^{p}\lesssim\sum_{Q}\|v\|_{L^{1}(w_{Q})}^{p},

Write
v=\sum_{Q^{\prime}}v_{Q^{\prime}}
, where
v_{Q^{\prime}}
is supported on
Q^{\prime}
. Then

\displaystyle\|v_{Q^{\prime}}\|_{L^{1}(w_{Q})}\leq(1+\operatorname{dist}(Q,Q^{\prime}))^{-100d}\|v_{Q^{\prime}}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

By Hölder,
\|v_{Q^{\prime}}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\|v_{Q^{\prime}}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\sum_{Q}\|v\|_{L^{1}(w_{Q})}^{p}\lesssim\sum_{Q,Q^{\prime}}(1+\operatorname{dist}(Q,Q^{\prime}))^{-100dp}\|v_{Q^{\prime}}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}\lesssim\|v\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p},

where we summed a geometric series in
Q
.
∎

Lemma 8

There exists a bump function

\operatorname{supp}\phi\subset B(0,1)
and with non-negative Fourier transform satisfying
\mathbf{1}_{B(0,1)}\leq\hat{\phi}
. Moreover,
\hat{\phi}
is an even function.

It is clear that the rescaled function
\phi_{R}(\xi)=R^{d}\phi(R\xi)
satisfies

\displaystyle\operatorname{supp}\phi_{R}\subset B(0,R^{-1}),\quad\mathbf{1}_{B(0,R)}\leq\hat{\phi}_{R}.

Let
M_{\lambda}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\,|\xi|=\lambda\}
, and consider the extension operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}:L^{2}(M_{\lambda},\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\lambda})\to L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}g)(x)=(g\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\lambda})^{\vee}(x),

where
\sigma_{\lambda}
is surface measure on
M_{\lambda}
. We write
\mathcal{E}\equiv\mathcal{E}_{1}
and
M\equiv M_{1}
,
\sigma\equiv\sigma_{1}
.

3.3.
Discrete Fourier extension operator

Definition 1
.

Let
{\rm Discres}(M,p,2)
be the best constant such that the following hold for each
R\geq 2
, each collection
\Lambda^{*}_{R}
consisting of
1/R
-separated points on
M
, each sequence
a_{\nu}\subset\mathbb{C}
, each ball
B_{R}
and each collection
\Lambda_{1}
of
1
-separated points in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

\displaystyle\|\sum_{\nu\in\Lambda^{*}_{R}}a_{\nu}\mathrm{e}(\nu\cdot x)\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}(\Lambda_{1}\cap B_{R})}\leq{\rm Discres}(M,p,2)R^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\|a_{\nu}\|_{\ell^{2}(\Lambda^{*}_{R})}.

(16)

Proposition 9
.

If
1\leq p\leq\infty
, then

\displaystyle{\rm Discres}(M,p,2)\lesssim\|\mathcal{E}\|_{L^{2}(M,\mathrm{d}\sigma)\to L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(17)

Moreover, if
p\geq 2
, then the reverse inequality also holds.

Proof.

The claim is a special case of
[
6
, Prop. 1.29]
, with one small difference. There,
{\rm Discres}(M,p,2)
is defined with the
L^{p^{\prime}}(B_{R})
norm in the left hand side of (
16
). Thus, let
{\rm Discres}^{\prime}(M,p,2)
be the best constant in the inequality

\displaystyle\|\sum_{\nu\in\Lambda^{*}_{R}}a_{\nu}\mathrm{e}(\nu\cdot x)\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(B_{R})}\leq{\rm Discres}^{\prime}(M,p,2)R^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\|a_{\nu}\|_{\ell^{2}(\Lambda^{*}_{R})}.

(18)

Then
[
6
, Prop. 1.29]
asserts that the proposition holds with
{\rm Discres}^{\prime}(M,p,2)
in place of
{\rm Discres}(M,p,2)
.
Thus, (
17
) follows once we show that

\displaystyle{\rm Discres}^{\prime}(M,p,2)\gtrsim{\rm Discres}(M,p,2).

(19)

Without loss of generality we may assume that
B_{R}=B(0,R)
. If we set

\displaystyle f(x)=\sum_{\nu\in\Lambda^{*}_{R}}a_{\nu}\mathrm{e}(\nu\cdot x),\quad\mbox{then}\quad\mathcal{F}(f\hat{\phi}_{R})(\xi)=\sum_{\nu\in\Lambda^{*}_{R}}a_{\nu}\phi_{R}(\xi+\nu),

where
\phi_{R}
is as before and
\mathcal{F}
denotes the Fourier transform. Note that
\mathcal{F}(f\hat{\phi}_{R})=\hat{f}\ast\phi_{R}
is supported in an
1/R
-neighborhood of
M
.
In particular, it is supported on the ball
B(0,2)
. Thus, for any collection
\Lambda_{1}
of
1
-separated points in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}(\Lambda_{1}\cap B_{R})}\leq\|f\hat{\phi}_{R}\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}(\Lambda_{1})}\lesssim\|f\hat{\phi}_{R}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

where we used
\hat{\phi}_{R}\geq\mathbf{1}_{B_{R}}
in the first inequality and Lemma
7
in the second.
By a partition of unity and a sparsification argument we may assume that
f
is supported on a disjoint union of balls of radius
R
. By the rapid decay of
\hat{\phi}_{R}
and by the definition of
{\rm Discres}^{\prime}(M,p,2)
,

\displaystyle\|\hat{\phi}_{R}f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim_{N}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j^{-N}\|f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(B(x_{j},R))}\lesssim{\rm Discres}^{\prime}(M,p,2)R^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\|a_{\nu}\|_{\ell^{2}(\Lambda^{*}_{R})},

where we used that (
18
) holds uniformly in the centers of the balls. Combining the last two estimates yields (
19
).

To prove the reverse inequality to (
17
), we may assume that
B_{R}=B(0,R)
. By
[
6
, Prop. 1.29]
it suffices to prove the reverse inequality to (
19
). Let
\Lambda_{1}
be a
1
-net of points
x_{j}\in B_{R}
. Let
f(x)
be defined as above. Without loss of generality we may assume that
f
is supported on a disjoint collection of balls
B(x_{j},10)
. Then

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(B_{R})}

\displaystyle=(\sum_{j}\|f\|^{p^{\prime}}_{L^{p^{\prime}}(B(x_{j},10))})^{1/p^{\prime}}=(\int_{B(0,10)}\sum_{j}|f(x_{j}+y)|^{p^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}y)^{1/p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\rm Discres}(M,p,2)R^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\|a_{\nu}\|_{\ell^{2}(\Lambda^{*}_{R})},

where we used that (
16
) holds for each collection
x_{j}+y
of
1
-separated points, uniformly in
y
.
∎

4.
Randomization

4.1.
Sub-gaussian random variables

We recall that a (complex) scalar random variable
X
is called
sub-gaussian
if it has finite sub-gaussian norm,

\displaystyle\|X\|_{\psi_{2}}=\inf\{t>0:\,\mathbf{E}\exp(|X|^{2}/t^{2})\leq 2\}<\infty.

We will need the following elementary properties of sub-gaussian (e.g. Gaussian or symmetric Bernoulli) random variables. (see e.g.
[
26
, Proposition 2.6.1 and Exercise 2.5.10]
).

Proposition 11
.

Assume that
(X_{j})_{j=1}^{N}
,
N\geq 2
, is a finite collection of i.i.d. mean-zero sub-gaussian random variables.

i)

Then
\sum_{j=1}^{N}X_{j}
is also sub-gaussian, and

\displaystyle\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}X_{j}\|_{\psi_{2}}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|X_{j}\|_{\psi_{2}}^{2}.

ii)

We have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\max_{j\leq N}|X_{j}|\lesssim\sqrt{\log N}\max_{j\leq N}\|X_{j}\|_{\psi_{2}}.

Proof.

The claim follows by applying
[
26
, Prop. 2.6.1 and Ex. 2.5.10]
to
\operatorname{Re}X_{j}
and
\operatorname{Im}X_{j}
separately.
∎

4.2.
Tail bounds

We now consider tail bounds for

vector-valued
Gaussian or Bernoulli random variables
X
. We have
(\mathbf{E}\|X\|^{p})^{1/p}\asymp(\mathbf{E}\|X\|^{q})^{1/q}
for all
p,q>0

(cf.
[
15
, Corollary 3.2 and Theorem 4.7]
),
which, combined with
[
15
, (3.5), (4.12)]
implies

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(\|X\|>t)\leq\exp\left(-\frac{ct^{2}}{(\mathbf{E}\|X\|)^{2}}\right)

for some
c>0
. Thus the following lemma is obvious.

Lemma 12
.

If
\mathbf{E}\|X\|\leq C
, then

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(\|X\|>MC)\leq\exp(-cM^{2})

for any
M>0
.

5.
Entropy bound

Consider a linear operator
S:\mathcal{H}\to\ell^{\infty}_{m}
, where
\mathcal{H}
is a finite-dimensional Hilbert space and
\ell^{\infty}_{m}=\ell^{\infty}(\{1,\ldots,m\})
. For
\varepsilon>0
let
\mathcal{N}(\varepsilon)
be the minimal number of balls in
\ell^{\infty}_{m}
of radius
\varepsilon
needed to cover the set
\{Sx:\,x\in\mathcal{H},\,\|x\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq 1\}
. Here we use the convention that the centers of the balls are contained in the set they cover (i.e.
\mathcal{N}(t)
is the
covering number
as opposed to the
exterior covering number
, see e.g.
[
26
, Sect. 4.2]
). Using an entropy bound known as the “dual Sudakov inequality”, which is attributed to Pajor and Tomczak-Jaegermann
[
17
]
, Bourgain
[
2
, (4.2)]
shows that

\displaystyle\log\mathcal{N}(\varepsilon)\lesssim(\log m)\varepsilon^{-2}\|S\|_{\mathcal{H}\to\ell^{\infty}_{m}}^{2}.

(20)

The quantity
\log\mathcal{N}(\varepsilon)
is called the
entropy number
of the image of the unit ball in
\mathcal{H}
under the map
S
. The crucial observation is that (
20
) is independent of
\dim\mathcal{H}
.
We apply this bound to the operator featuring in (
16
), i.e.

\displaystyle S:\ell^{2}_{\rm av}(\Lambda^{*}_{R})\to\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda_{1}\cap B_{R}),\quad\{a_{\nu}\}\mapsto\{\sum_{\nu\in\Lambda^{*}_{R}}a_{\nu}\mathrm{e}(\nu\cdot x)\}_{x}

(21)

In this case, we have
\mathcal{H}=\ell^{2}_{\rm av}(\Lambda^{*}_{R})
and
\ell^{\infty}_{m}=\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda_{1}\cap B_{R})
. In particular, we have
m\asymp R^{d}
. Here and in the following we always assume
R\geq 2
. Proposition
10
gives

\displaystyle\|S\|_{\ell^{2}_{\rm av}(\Lambda^{*}_{R})\to\ell^{p^{\prime}}(\Lambda_{1}\cap B_{R})}\lesssim 1\quad\mbox{for}\quad p^{\prime}\geq 2(d+1)/(d-1).

(22)

In particular, we have the trivial bound (
p^{\prime}=\infty
)

\displaystyle\|S\|_{\ell^{2}_{\rm av}(\Lambda^{*}_{R})\to\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda_{1}\cap B_{R})}\lesssim 1.

(23)

Combining the latter with (
20
) yields the following entropy bound.

Proposition 13
.

Let
S
be given by (
21
). The entropy number satisfies the bound

\displaystyle\log\mathcal{N}(\varepsilon)\lesssim(\log R)\varepsilon^{-2}.

Corollary 14
.

Let
p^{\prime}\geq 2(d+1)/(d-1)
. For every
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
there exist sets
\mathcal{F}_{k}\subset\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda_{1}\cap B_{R})
with the following properties.

(a)

\log|\mathcal{F}_{k}|\lesssim\log(R)4^{k}
(here
|\cdot|
denotes counting measure).

(b)

For
\xi\in\mathcal{F}_{k}
,

\displaystyle\|\xi\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda_{1})}\lesssim 2^{-k},\quad\|\xi\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}(\Lambda_{1})}\lesssim 1.

(24)

(c)

For each
a\in\ell^{2}_{\rm av}(\Lambda^{*}_{R})
with
\|a\|_{\ell^{2}_{\rm av}(\Lambda^{*}_{R})}\leq 1
there is a representation

\displaystyle Sa=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\xi^{(k)}\quad\mbox{for some}\quad\xi^{(k)}\in\mathcal{F}_{k}.

Proof.

We follow Bourgain
[
3
, page 75-76]
, but provide more details (note also that there is a misprint in (3.13) there; it should be
4^{r}
, not
4^{-r}
). This is a standard chaining argument.

We start by noting that, in view of (
20
) and (
23
), we have
\mathcal{N}(C)=1
for
C
sufficiently large. In the following (and only in this proof) denote the unit ball in
\ell^{2}_{\rm av}(\Lambda^{*}_{R})
by
B_{1}
. Similarly,
B(\xi,\varepsilon)
denotes a ball centered at
\xi
and with radius
\varepsilon
in
\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda_{1}\cap B_{R})
. We also write
\|\cdot\|_{p}=\|\cdot\|_{\ell^{p}(\Lambda_{1})}
here. By possibly rescaling
SB_{1}
by a constant, we may assume that
C=1
. Thus, we have
\mathcal{N}(1)=1
. We get, by Proposition
13
,

\displaystyle\log\mathcal{N}(2^{-k})\lesssim\log(R)4^{k}.

(25)

Thus, for each
k\geq 0
, there exist subsets
\mathcal{E}_{k}\subset\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda_{1}\cap B_{R})
of cardinality
\mathcal{N}(2^{-k})
satisfying

\displaystyle SB_{1}\subset\bigcup_{\xi\in\mathcal{E}_{k}}B(\xi,2^{-k}).

Applying these nets for each
k
, we can assign to each element
Sa\in SB_{1}
a chain
\{\xi_{k}\}

converging to
Sa
, with
\xi_{k}\in\mathcal{E}_{k}
and

\displaystyle\|\xi_{k}-\xi_{k-1}\|_{\infty}\leq 2^{-k}+2^{1-k}

(26)

for all
k
.
By telescoping, we have

\displaystyle Sa=\xi_{0}+\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\xi_{k}-\xi_{k-1})

Thus, we may choose
\mathcal{F}_{0}=\mathcal{E}_{0}
and
\mathcal{F}_{k}\subset\mathcal{E}_{k}-\mathcal{E}_{k-1}
,
k>0
, as the collection of all vectors
\xi^{(k)}=\xi_{k}-\xi_{k-1}
for which (
26
) holds. Since the difference set
\mathcal{E}_{k}-\mathcal{E}_{k-1}
has cardinality
|\mathcal{E}_{k}||\mathcal{E}_{k-1}|
the claimed properties hold by construction.
∎

6.
Local bounds on elementary operators

6.1.
Local extension bound

Let
h,R>0
. Consider
V_{\omega}
of the form (
3
), where
V
is a given deterministic potential supported in
B_{R}
.
Also fix
p^{\prime}\geq 2(d+1)/(d-1)
and define
q
by
1/q=1/p-1/p^{\prime}
. Note that this convention differs from that in the main theorems by a change of variables
q\to 2q
.

Lemma 15
.

Under the above assumptions,
we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\|\mathcal{E}^{*}V_{\omega}\mathcal{E}\|_{L^{2}(M,\mathrm{d}\sigma)\to L^{2}(M,\mathrm{d}\sigma)}\lesssim\langle h\rangle^{d/2}(\log\langle R\rangle)^{1/2}(\log\langle h\rangle+\log\langle R\rangle)^{2}\|V\|_{L^{2q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Proof.

Since the right hand side only gets larger if we replace
R
and
h
by
R+2
and
h+2
, respectively, we may assume
R,h\geq 2
.
We first observe that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{*}V_{\omega}\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}^{*}(V_{\omega}\ast\varphi)\mathcal{E}

(27)

for any Schwartz function
\varphi
satisfying
\hat{\varphi}=1
on
B(0,2)
. We can thus assume without loss of generality that
V
is smooth on the unit scale.
Let
g,g^{\prime}
be unit vectors in
L^{2}(M,\mathrm{d}\sigma)
. Then

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{E}^{*}V_{\omega}\mathcal{E}g,g^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in h\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\omega_{j}\int_{Q_{h}+j}\overline{V(x)(\mathcal{E}g)(x)}(\mathcal{E}g^{\prime})(x)\mathrm{d}x,

where
Q_{h}=[0,h)^{d}
. Let
\Lambda^{*}_{R}=\{\eta_{\nu}\}
be a
1/R
-net in
M
. By working with a partition of unity, we may assume that
g
is supported on a collection of disjoint balls
B(\eta_{\nu},10/R)
. After a change of variables
g(\eta)=g(\eta_{\nu}+\tau)
, we may write

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}g(x)=\sum_{\nu}\int_{M\cap B(0,10/R)}\mathrm{e}(x\cdot(\eta_{\nu}+\tau))g(\eta_{\nu}+\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau,

where
\mathrm{d}\tau
denotes surface measure, and similarly (summing over a possibly different index set)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}g^{\prime}(x)=\sum_{\nu^{\prime}}\int_{M\cap B(0,10/R)}\mathrm{e}(x\cdot(\eta_{\nu^{\prime}}+\tau^{\prime}))g^{\prime}(\eta_{\nu^{\prime}}+\tau^{\prime})\mathrm{d}\tau^{\prime}.

Similarly to the change of variables
\eta=\eta_{\nu}+\tau
in the domain, we change variables
x=x_{i}+y
in the target. Here,
\Lambda_{1}=\{x_{i}\}
is a
1
-net in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Hence, for any integrable function
F:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
, supported on a disjoint collection of balls
B(x_{i},10)
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}F(x)\mathrm{d}x=\sum_{i}\int_{B(0,10)}F(x_{i}+y)\mathrm{d}y.

Using a partition of unity we may sparsify the potential, so that the above holds for

\displaystyle F_{j}(x)=\overline{V(x)(\mathcal{E}g)(x)}(\mathcal{E}g^{\prime})(x)\mathbf{1}_{Q_{h}+j}(x).

Note that in this case the sum is restricted to those
i
satisfying
x_{i}\in B(j,10+h)
. For fixed
\tau\in B(0,10/R)
and
y\in B(0,10)
we consider the discrete extension operator

\displaystyle S:\ell^{2}_{\rm av}(\Lambda^{*}_{R})\to\ell^{\infty}(\Lambda_{1}\cap B_{R}),\quad\!\!\!\!\!\!\{g(\eta_{\nu}+\tau)\}_{\nu}\mapsto\{\sum_{\nu}\mathrm{e}((x_{i}+y)\cdot(\eta_{\nu}+\tau))g(\eta_{\nu}+\tau)\}_{i}.

Note that the points
\mu_{\nu}=\eta_{\nu}+\tau
and
z_{i}=x_{i}+y
form a
1/R
-separated set in
M
and a
1
-separated set in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, respectively, so that (
22
), (
23
) hold.
Using Corollary
14
, we can find a representation (note that the vectors
\xi^{(k)}
depend on
\tau,y
)

\displaystyle\sum_{\nu}\mathrm{e}((x_{i}+y)\cdot(\eta_{\nu}+\tau))g(\eta_{\nu}+\tau)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\xi_{i}^{(k)},\quad\xi^{(k)}\in\mathcal{F}_{k},

with bounds

\displaystyle\|\xi^{(k)}\|_{\infty}\lesssim 2^{-k}\|g(\eta_{\nu}+\tau)\|_{\ell^{2}_{\nu,{\rm av}}},\quad\|\xi^{(k)}\|_{p^{\prime}}\lesssim\|g(\eta_{\nu}+\tau)\|_{\ell^{2}_{\nu,{\rm av}}}

(28)

for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
y\in B(0,10)
. Similarly, there is a representation

\displaystyle\sum_{\nu^{\prime}}\mathrm{e}((x_{i}+y)\cdot(\eta_{\nu^{\prime}}+\tau^{\prime}))g^{\prime}(\eta_{\nu^{\prime}}+\tau^{\prime})=\sum_{k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\xi_{i}^{(k^{\prime})},\quad\xi^{(k^{\prime})}\in\mathcal{F}_{k^{\prime}},

with bounds

\displaystyle\|\xi^{(k^{\prime})}\|_{\infty}\lesssim 2^{-k^{\prime}}\|g^{\prime}(\eta_{\nu^{\prime}}+\tau^{\prime})\|_{\ell^{2}_{\nu^{\prime},{\rm av}}},\quad\|\xi^{(k^{\prime})}\|_{p^{\prime}}\lesssim\|g^{\prime}(\eta_{\nu^{\prime}}+\tau^{\prime})\|_{\ell^{2}_{\nu^{\prime},{\rm av}}}.

(29)

The above observations lead to the estimate

\displaystyle|\langle\mathcal{E}^{*}V_{\omega}\mathcal{E}g,g^{\prime}\rangle|\leq\sum_{k,k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\int\max_{(\xi,\xi^{\prime})\in\mathcal{F}_{k}\times\mathcal{F}_{k^{\prime}}}|\sum_{j\in h\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{i}\omega_{j}\overline{V(x_{i}+y)\xi_{i}}\xi_{i}^{\prime}|\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{d}\tau^{\prime},

where the integral is taken over
(y,\tau,\tau^{\prime})\in B(0,10)\times(M\cap B(0,10/R))^{2}
and the sum over
i
is restricted to
x_{i}+y\in Q_{h}+j
(we recall that
y
is fixed). By monotonicity of the expectation,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}|\langle\mathcal{E}^{*}V_{\omega}\mathcal{E}g,g^{\prime}\rangle|\leq\sum_{k,k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\int\mathbf{E}\max_{\mathcal{F}_{k}\times\mathcal{F}_{k^{\prime}}}|X_{\xi,\xi^{\prime}}|\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{d}\tau^{\prime},

where (suppressing the dependence on
y,\tau,\tau^{\prime}
)

\displaystyle X_{\xi,\xi^{\prime}}=\sum_{j\in h\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\omega_{j}\sum_{i}\overline{V(x_{i}+y)\xi_{i}}\xi_{i}^{\prime}.

The conclusion follows by Lemma
16
and
25
(details of the calculation are provided in the appendix).
∎

Lemma 16
.

Let
R,h\geq 2
. Then we have the following bounds,

\displaystyle\int\mathbf{E}\max_{\mathcal{F}_{k}\times\mathcal{F}_{k^{\prime}}}|X_{\xi,\xi^{\prime}}|\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{d}\tau^{\prime}\lesssim(\log R)^{1/2}h^{d/2}\|V\|_{L^{2q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

\displaystyle\int\max_{\mathcal{F}_{k}\times\mathcal{F}_{k^{\prime}}}|X_{\xi,\xi^{\prime}}|\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{d}\tau^{\prime}\lesssim R^{d-d/2q}2^{-k-k^{\prime}}\|V\|_{L^{2q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Proof.

Note first that the index set of
X_{\xi,\xi^{\prime}}
is finite and has cardinality
N
, satisfying

\displaystyle\log N=\log|\mathcal{F}_{k}\times\mathcal{F}_{k^{\prime}}|\lesssim\log R\max(4^{k},4^{k^{\prime}}),

(30)

by Corollary
14
(a).
Proposition
11
implies that
X_{\xi,\xi^{\prime}}
are (scalar) subgaussian random variables, and

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\max_{\mathcal{F}_{k}\times\mathcal{F}_{k^{\prime}}}|X_{\xi,\xi^{\prime}}|\lesssim\sqrt{\log N}(\sum_{j\in h\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\sum_{i}\overline{V(x_{i}+y)\xi_{i}}\xi_{i}^{\prime}|^{2})^{1/2},

where we recall that we are assuming
\|\omega_{j}\|_{\psi_{2}}\lesssim 1
. Using Hölder’s inequality twice, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\max_{\mathcal{F}_{k}\times\mathcal{F}_{k^{\prime}}}|X_{\xi,\xi^{\prime}}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{\log N}\left\|\|V(x_{i}+y)\|_{\ell^{q}_{i}}\|\xi_{i}\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}_{i}}\|\xi_{i}^{\prime}\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}_{i}}\right\|_{\ell^{2}_{j}}

(31)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{\log N}\left\|\|V(x_{i}+y)\|_{\ell^{q}_{i}}\right\|_{\ell^{2q}_{j}}\left\|\|\xi_{i}\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}_{i}}\|\xi_{i}^{\prime}\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}_{i}}\right\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}_{j}},

(32)

where we recall that
i
is restricted to
x_{i}+y\in Q_{h}+j
and
y
is fixed. In particular, we have

\displaystyle|\{j\in h\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\,x_{i}+y\in Q_{h}+j\}|

\displaystyle=1\quad\mbox{for each }i

(33)

and

\displaystyle|\{i:\,x_{i}+y\in Q_{h}+j\}|

\displaystyle\leq h^{d}\quad\mbox{for each }j\in h\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

(34)

We will show that

\displaystyle\left\|\|\xi_{i}\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}_{i}}\|\xi_{i}^{\prime}\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}_{i}}\right\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}_{j}}\lesssim h^{d/p^{\prime}}\min(2^{-k},2^{-k^{\prime}})\|g(\eta_{\nu}+\tau)\|_{\ell^{2}_{\nu,{\rm av}}}\|g^{\prime}(\eta_{\nu^{\prime}}+\tau^{\prime})\|_{\ell^{2}_{\nu^{\prime},{\rm av}}}

(35)

By symmetry in
\xi,\xi^{\prime}
, it suffices to prove this in the case
k\geq k^{\prime}
. Using Hölder once more, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\|\xi_{i}\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}_{i}}\|\xi_{i}^{\prime}\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}_{i}}\right\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}_{j}}\leq\left\|\|\xi_{i}\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}_{i}}\right\|_{\ell^{\infty}_{j}}\left\|\|\xi_{i}^{\prime}\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}_{i}}\right\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}_{j}}.

By Fubini’s theorem and (
33
),

\displaystyle\left\|\|\xi_{i}^{\prime}\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}_{i}}\right\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}_{j}}

\displaystyle=(\sum_{i}\sum_{j\in h\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\xi_{i}^{\prime}|^{p^{\prime}})^{1/p^{\prime}}=(\sum_{i}|\xi_{i}^{\prime}|^{p^{\prime}})^{1/p^{\prime}}=\|\xi^{\prime}\|_{p^{\prime}}.

Similarly, by (
34
) we have

\displaystyle\left\|\|\xi_{i}\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}_{i}}\right\|_{\ell^{\infty}_{j}}\leq h^{d/p^{\prime}}\|\xi\|_{\infty}.

Combining these estimates with (
28
), (
29
) yields (
35
). Next, we have (again by Hölder, Fubini and (
34
))

\displaystyle\left\|\|V(x_{i}+y)\|_{\ell^{q}_{i}}\right\|_{\ell^{2q}_{j}}

\displaystyle\leq h^{d/2q}\left\|\|V(x_{i}+y)\|_{\ell^{2q}_{i}}\right\|_{\ell^{2q}_{j}}

(36)

\displaystyle=h^{d/2q}\left\|\|V(x_{i}+y)\|_{\ell^{2q}_{j}}\right\|_{\ell^{2q}_{i}}

(37)

\displaystyle=h^{d/2q}\|V(x_{i}+y)\|_{\ell^{2q}_{i}}.

(38)

Integrating (
31
) over
y,\tau,\tau^{\prime}
and using (
35
), (

We recall from Section

Lemma 17

For

Lemma 25
.

Let
A>0
. Then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k,k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\min(2^{-k-k^{\prime}},A)\lesssim\begin{cases}A(1+(\log A)^{2})\quad&\mbox{if }A<1,\\
1\quad&\mbox{if }A\geq 1.\end{cases}

Proof.

The case
A\geq 1
is trivial. Assume
A<1
. We split the double sum into the obvious regions

\Sigma_{1}=\{(k,k^{\prime}):2^{-k-k^{\prime}}\leq A\}
and
\Sigma_{2}=\{(k,k^{\prime}):2^{-k-k^{\prime}}>A\}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{(k,k^{\prime})\in\Sigma_{1}}\min(2^{-k-k^{\prime}},A)=\sum_{k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}2^{-k^{\prime}}\sum_{k:2^{-k}\leq 2^{k^{\prime}}A}2^{-k}\lesssim\sum_{k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}2^{-k^{\prime}}\min(1,2^{k^{\prime}}A).

Splitting the last sum again in the obvious way yields

\displaystyle\sum_{(k,k^{\prime})\in\Sigma_{1}}\min(2^{-k-k^{\prime}},A)\lesssim A(1+\log A^{-1}).

Turning to the contribution of
\Sigma_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{(k,k^{\prime})\in\Sigma_{2}}\min(2^{-k-k^{\prime}},A)

\displaystyle=A\sum_{k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}|\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}:\,2^{-k}>2^{k^{\prime}}A\}|

\displaystyle\leq A\sum_{k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}(\log A-k^{\prime})_{+}\leq A(\log A)^{2}.

The claim follows since
\log A^{-1}\leq 1+(\log A)^{2}
.
∎

We now provide details of the calculation at the end of the proof of Lemma
15
. Without loss of generality we may assume that
\|V\|_{2q}=1
. By Lemma
16
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k,k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\int\mathbf{E}\max_{\mathcal{F}_{k}\times\mathcal{F}_{k^{\prime}}}|X_{\xi,\xi^{\prime}}|\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{d}\tau^{\prime}\lesssim R^{d-d/2q}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\min(2^{-k-k^{\prime}},A)

with

, where we recall that we are assuming that

.
Since we may always assume that

and

(otherwise there is no randomization), we have

, and thus

\displaystyle R^{d-d/2q}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\min(2^{-k-k^{\prime}},A)\lesssim(\log R)^{1/2}h^{d/2}(\log h+\log R)^{2}

Appendix B
Knapp example

As mentioned in the introduction, we prove optimality (up to logarithms) of the key bounds of Lemmas
15
and
19
. In view of the foliation (
46
) it is sufficient to prove optimality of Lemma
15
. To this end, let
V
be the indicator function of the tube

T_{R}=\{(x_{1},x^{\prime}):\,|x_{1}|<R,|x^{\prime}|<R^{1/2}\},

normalized in
L^{q}
, i.e.
V=R^{-\frac{d+1}{2q}}\mathbf{1}_{T_{R}}
(we will mollify this later). Here
R>1
is a large parameter.
We consider the randomization
V_{\omega}
(as in (
3
)) of this potential. We assume in the following that
\lambda=1
in Lemma
15
and that
h
is sufficiently small (to be fixed later). It is easy to see that we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\|\mathcal{E}^{*}V_{\omega}\mathcal{E}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}\|\mathcal{E}^{*}\overline{V_{\omega}}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{E}^{*}V_{\omega}\mathcal{E}\|=\mathbf{E}\sup_{\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)}=1}|\langle\mathcal{E}\mathcal{E}^{*}V_{\omega}\mathcal{E}f,V_{\omega}\mathcal{E}f\rangle|

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)}=1}|\mathbf{E}\langle\mathcal{E}\mathcal{E}^{*}V_{\omega}\mathcal{E}f,V_{\omega}\mathcal{E}f\rangle|

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)}=1}|\operatorname{Re}\mathbf{E}\langle\mathcal{E}\mathcal{E}^{*}V_{\omega}\mathcal{E}f,V_{\omega}\mathcal{E}f\rangle|,

where we recall that
M
is the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. In order to estimate the last expression from below,
we consider a Knapp example (see e.g.
[
6
, Example 1.8]
)

\displaystyle\hat{f}_{R}(\xi):=R^{\frac{d-1}{4}}\eta(R\xi_{1},R^{1/2}\xi^{\prime}),

\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)}=1
. Assuming, as we may, that
\mathbf{E}\,\omega_{i}\omega_{j}=\delta_{ij}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\langle\mathcal{E}\mathcal{E}^{*}V_{\omega}\mathcal{E}f,V_{\omega}\mathcal{E}f\rangle=\sum_{j\in h\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\mathcal{E}\mathcal{E}^{*})(x-y)\overline{V_{j}(y)(\mathcal{E}f)(y)}V_{j}(x)(\mathcal{E}f)(x)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}x,

Since

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\|\mathcal{E}^{*}V_{\omega}\mathcal{E}\|^{2}\gtrsim\operatorname{Re}\sum_{j\in h\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{F_{j}(x-u)}F_{j}(x)\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}x,\quad(F_{j}=V_{j}\mathcal{E}f_{R})

(which is small as we will see).
At this point we consider a smooth (at the scale of

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in h\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{F_{j}(x-u)}F_{j}(x)=(2h)^{d}(1-\mathcal{O}(R^{-1/2}))\sum_{j\in h\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|F_{j}(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

Computing the integral, this shows that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\|\mathcal{E}^{*}V_{\omega}\mathcal{E}\|^{2}\gtrsim R^{1-\frac{d+1}{q}}\|V\|_{q},

which implies that

Self-Covering, Finiteness, And Fibering Over A Circle
Lizhen Qin, Yang Su, Botong Wang

Abstract
. A topological space is called self-covering if it is a nontrivial cover of itself. We prove that a closed self-covering manifold M M with free abelian fundamental group fibers over a circle under mild assumptions. In particular, we give a complete answer to the question whether a self-covering manifold with fundamental group ℤ \mathbb{Z} is a fiber bundle over S 1 S^{1} , except for the 4 4 -dimensional smooth case. As an algebraic Hilfssatz, we develop a criterion for finite generation of modules over a commutative Noetherian ring. We also construct examples of self-covering manifolds with non-free abelian fundamental group, which are not fiber bundles over S 1 S^{1} .



Dedicated to Francis Thomas Farrell on the occasion of his 80th birthday.

1.
Introduction

A topological space is called self-covering if it is homeomorphic, or more generally homotopy equivalent, to a nontrivial covering space of itself. Self-covering spaces, especially self-covering manifolds, are well-studied in the literature (see e.g.
[
3
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
42
]
and
[
48
]
). Many works about self-covering spaces including this paper are motivated by the following questions.

Which closed manifolds admit a self-covering structure? And what geometric structures does a self-covering manifold possess?

The simplest self-covering closed manifold is a circle. In this paper, we study when a closed self-covering manifold is a fiber bundle over a circle.

We work in the category of smooth (DIFF), piecewise-linear (PL) and topological (TOP) manifolds simultaneously. Throughout this paper, the abbreviation CAT stands for any of these categories. In particular, a CAT manifold is a smooth, piecewise-linear or topological manifold, and a CAT isomorphism is a diffeomorphism, a piecewise-linear homeomorphism or a homeomorphism, etc.

We start with a simple observation. Let
F
be a closed CAT manifold and
f
be an automorphism of
F
. Then the quotient space
M=F\times[0,1]/\sim
with
(x,0)\sim(f(x),1)
is a CAT fiber bundle over
S^{1}
with fiber
F
and monodromy
f
. For each integer
k>0
, let
M_{k}=F\times[0,1]/\sim
with
(x,0)\sim(f^{k}(x),1)
. Then
M_{k}
is a
k
-fold cover of
M
. If there is an automorphism
g\colon F\to F
such that
f^{k}g
is CAT pseudo-isotopic to
gf
or
gf^{-1}
, then
M
is isomorphic to
M_{k}
and we obtain a self-covering manifold
M
, which is a fiber bundle over
S^{1}
. The motivation of this paper is to study whether every self-covering manifold can be constructed in this way. In other words, we study the following question.

Question 1.1
.

Suppose
M
is a connected closed CAT
n
-manifold, which is isomorphic to a
k
-fold cover
M_{k}
of itself for some
k>1
. Is
M
a CAT fiber bundle over
S^{1}
with monodromy
f
such that
f^{k}g
is CAT pseudo-isotopic to
gf
or
gf^{-1}
for some automorphism
g
of
F
?

The answer to Question
1.1
is negative in general as we construct two types of counterexamples in Theorems
C
and
D
. However, under restrictions of the fundamental group, one may obtain a positive answer. A special case of Question
1.1
is the following.

Question 1.2
.

Is a connected closed self-covering CAT
n
-manifold
M
with
\pi_{1}(M)=\mathbb{Z}
a CAT fiber bundle over
S^{1}
?

Though depending on both the dimension and the category, the answer to Question
1.2
is almost always positive. The question is trivial for
n\leq 2
. For
n=3
, an affirmative answer is given by the classical theory of
3
-manifolds. We will give an answer to Question
1.2
in dimension
\geq 4
in this paper. The main result is a fibering theorem for self-covering manifolds with free abelian fundamental group in dimension
\geq 5
(Theorem
B
).

To fiber a manifold
M
over a circle, one could apply the classical fibering theorem of Browder-Levine
[
4
]
, or more generally, the theorems of Farrell
[
13
]
and Siebenmann
[
41
]
. We would like to emphasize that all these theorems rely on the assumption that the infinite cyclic cover
M_{\infty}
of
M
induced by certain epimorphism
\pi_{1}(M)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
is homotopy equivalent to a finite CW complex. This finiteness condition is indeed necessary for
M
being fibered over a circle. In fact, for a fiber bundle
M
over
S^{1}
, the infinite cyclic cover corresponding to the epimorphism
\pi_{1}(M)\to\pi_{1}(S^{1})
is homotopy equivalent to the fiber
F
, which is a closed manifold. Therefore our key step toward the fibering theorem of self-covering manifolds is to deduce the homotopy finiteness from the self-covering assumption. This is a problem in homotopy theory. So we first study the infinite cyclic coverings of CW complexes, which itself has a long history and many applications (see e.g.
[
9
]
,
[
21
]
,
[
31
]
and
[
40
]
). In this paper, we prove that certain finiteness and periodicity (Theorems
A
,
3.2
and
4.1
) of the infinite cyclic covers can be derived from the self-covering assumption of the bases.

To begin with, we state our finiteness theorem for CW complexes. Let
X
be a connected CW complex with fundamental group
\pi_{1}(X)=G\times\mathbb{Z}
, where
G
is a finitely generated abelian group. Let
X_{k}
be the finite cyclic cover of
X
with
\pi_{1}(X_{k})=G\times(k\mathbb{Z})
, and let
X_{\infty}
be the infinite cyclic cover with
\pi_{1}(X_{\infty})=G\times 0
. Recall that a CW complex is of finite type if there are only finitely many cells in each dimension, whereas the dimension of the complex may be infinite; a space is finitely dominated if it is dominated by a finite CW complex.

Theorem A
.

Let
X
be defined as above. Suppose there exists a homotopy equivalence
h:X\rightarrow X_{k}
with
k>1
and
h_{\sharp}(G\times 0)=G\times 0
, where
h_{\sharp}:\pi_{1}(X)\rightarrow\pi_{1}(X_{k})
is the isomorphism induced by
h
. If
X
is homotopy equivalent to a CW complex of finite type (resp. is finitely dominated), then so is
X_{\infty}
.

Remark 1.3
.

The assumption
h_{\sharp}(G\times 0)=G\times 0
in Theorem
A
is reflected in the following geometric picture. Suppose
X
is a fiber bundle over
S^{1}
with fiber
F
and
\pi_{1}(F)=G\times 0
. Then
X_{k}
is also a bundle over
S^{1}
with the same fiber. If the map
h:X\rightarrow X_{k}
preserves the bundle structure, then
h_{\sharp}(G\times 0)=G\times 0
.

Remark 1.4
.

In Theorem
A
, even if
X
is homotopy equivalent to a finite CW complex, the conclusion can not be strengthened to “
X_{\infty}
is homotopy equivalent to a finite CW complex”. For example, if
Y
is finitely dominated but not homotopy equivalent to a finite CW complex, then
X=Y\times S^{1}
is homotopy equivalent to a finite CW complex with
X_{\infty}\simeq Y
. In Theorem
C
, we present a closed smooth manifold as an additional counterexample.

Now we consider self-covering manifolds under the same assumption. Namely, let
M
be a connected closed CAT manifold with fundamental group
\pi_{1}(M)=G\times\mathbb{Z}
, where
G
is an abelian group. Let
M_{k}
be the finite cyclic cover of
M
with
\pi_{1}(M_{k})=G\times(k\mathbb{Z})
. Assume that there exists a homotopy equivalence
h:M\rightarrow M_{k}
for some
k>1
such that the induced isomorphism
h_{\sharp}\colon\pi_{1}(M)\to\pi_{1}(M_{k})
satisfies
h_{\sharp}(G\times 0)=G\times 0
. In this case, the composition

(1.1)

\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow G\times\mathbb{Z}\overset{h_{\sharp}}{\longrightarrow}G\times(k\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow k\mathbb{Z}.

is an isomorphism, and hence the image of
1\in\mathbb{Z}
is either
k
or
-k
.

Definition 1.5
.

If the composition of (
1.1
) maps 1 to
k
, we say that
h

preserves the orientation of the deck transformations
. Otherwise, we say that
h

reverses the orientation of the deck transformations
.

Theorem B
.

Let
M
,
G
and
h:M\rightarrow M_{k}
be defined as above. Suppose that
G=\mathbb{Z}^{r}
for some
r\geq 0
, and
\dim M\geq 5
. When
\dim M=5
, we further assume that
G=0
and the category is TOP. Then the following statements hold.

(1)

The manifold
M
is a CAT fiber bundle over
S^{1}
with connected fiber
F
and
\pi_{1}(F)=G\times 0
.

(2)

There is a homotopy equivalence
g:F\rightarrow F
such that
f^{k}g
is homotopic to
gf
(resp.
gf^{-1}
) when
h
preserves (resp. reverses) the orientation of the deck transformations, where
f:F\to F
is the monodromy action.

(3)

Furthermore, if
h
is a CAT isomorphism, then there is a CAT automorphism
g\colon F\to F
such that
f^{k}g
is CAT pseudo-isotopic to
gf
(resp.
gf^{-1}
) when
h
preserves (resp. reverses) the orientation of the deck transformations.

Theorem
B
gives a positive answer to Question
1.1
in dimension
>5
under the assumption that
\pi_{1}(M)=\mathbb{Z}^{r}\times\mathbb{Z}
and
h_{\sharp}(\mathbb{Z}^{r}\times 0)=\mathbb{Z}^{r}\times 0
, and in dimension
5
as well for the TOP category when
\pi_{1}(M)=\mathbb{Z}
.

Remark 1.6
.

When
\dim M=5
, even if
G=0
, the conclusion of Theorem

is no longer true for DIFF and PL, see Theorem
F
.

Furthermore, the monodromy

in Theorem

has certain homological periodicity (Corollary
1.7
, which is a consequence of Theorem

), and for special classes of manifolds,

is indeed periodic up to isotopy (Corollary

).

Corollary 1.7
.

Let

,

, and

be as in Theorem

, where

, and

where

and

Corollary 1.8

Let

Recall that a smooth manifold is almost parallelizable if the tangent bundle is trivial outside an arbitrary point.

Corollary 1.9
.

Let

Remark 1.10
.

In Corollary

To show the finiteness properties of the infinite cyclic cover, we are led to questions in commutative algebra. In fact, we need Theorem
G
as the algebraic foundation of this paper, which may be of independent interest. This theorem helps us to reduce a problem of modules to a problem of vector spaces.

Before stating the theorem, we set the notations and recall some basic facts from commutative algebra. See Section
2
for more details. Let
R
be a Noetherian commutative ring and
S
be a commutative
R
-algebra with generators
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
. Here by an
R
-algebra, we mean
S
is equipped with a ring homomorphism
\iota:R\rightarrow S
. In particular,
S
is also a Noetherian commutative ring, and any
S
-module is naturally an
R
-module. Let
\mathrm{Spec}(R)
be the prime spectrum of
R
, i.e., the set of all prime ideals of
R
. For each
P\in\mathrm{Spec}(R)
, let
\kappa(P)
be the residue field of
R
at
P
, i.e., the quotient field of
R/P
.

Let
\mathfrak{L}
be an
S
-module and
P\in\mathrm{Spec}(R)
, we consider the following property for a pair
(\mathfrak{L},P)
.

Property 1.11
.

As a
\kappa(P)
-vector space,
\kappa(P)\otimes_{R}\mathfrak{L}
is finite-dimensional and the eigenvalues of the induced action of
t_{i}
on
\kappa(P)\otimes_{R}\mathfrak{L}
are integral over
R/P
for every
1\leq i\leq n
.

Let
\mathfrak{M}
be a nonzero finitely generated
S
-module. By definition, a prime ideal
P\in\mathrm{Spec}(R)
is an associated prime ideal of
\mathfrak{M}
if
P=\mathrm{Ann}_{R}(x)
for some
x\in\mathfrak{M}
, i.e.,
P
is the annihilator ideal of
x
. Let
\mathrm{Ass}_{R}(\mathfrak{M})
be the set of associated prime ideals of
\mathfrak{M}
, then
\mathrm{Ass}_{R}(\mathfrak{M})
is a nonempty finite subset of
\mathrm{Spec}(R)
(cf. Section
2
).

Theorem G
.

Let
R
,
S
and
\mathfrak{M}
be defined as the above. The following statements are equivalent.

(1)

The pair
(\mathfrak{M},P)
satisfies Property
1.11
for any
P\in\mathrm{Spec}(R)
.

(2)

The pair
(\mathfrak{M},P)
satisfies Property
1.11
for any
P\in\mathrm{Ass}_{R}(\mathfrak{M})
.

(3)

As an
R
-module,
\mathfrak{M}
is finitely generated.

2.
Algebraic Foundation

In this section, we prove Theorem
G
which is the algebraic foundation of this paper. The proof uses extensively results in commutative algebra.

By our assumption, both
R
and
S
are Noetherian commutative rings, and
S
is an
R
-algebra via the ring homomorphism
\iota:R\rightarrow S
and
S
is generated by
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
as an
R
-algebra. Since
\mathfrak{M}
is a nonzero finitely generated
S
-module, the set
\mathrm{Ass}_{S}(\mathfrak{M})
is nonempty and finite (cf.
[
28
, Theorems 6.1 & 6.5]
). By
[
27
, (9.A)]
(or
[
28
, Exercise 6.7]
),

\mathrm{Ass}_{R}(\mathfrak{M})=\{\iota^{-1}(Q)\mid Q\in\mathrm{Ass}_{S}(\mathfrak{M})\}.

Thus
\mathrm{Ass}_{R}(\mathfrak{M})
is also nonempty and finite.

The difficult part of Theorem
G
is the implication
(2)\Rightarrow(3)
. To prove it, we shall reduce
\mathfrak{M}
to be a module with one generator over
S
. So we first discuss some quotient rings of
S
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose
Q\in\mathrm{Spec}(S)
and
P=\iota^{-1}(Q)
. If
(S/Q,P)
satisfies Property
1.11
, then
S/Q
is a finitely generated
R
-module.

Proof.

Since
\kappa(P)\otimes_{R}(S/Q)
is finite-dimensional over
\kappa(P)
and the eigenvalues of
t_{i}
on it are integral over
R/P
, there is a monic polynomial
f_{i}(u)\in(R/P)[u]
such that
f_{i}(t_{i})=0
on this vector space. Thus we have the following commutative diagram.

\textstyle{S/Q\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\times f_{i}(t_{i})}
\textstyle{S/Q\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\kappa(P)\otimes_{R}(S/Q)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{0}
\textstyle{\kappa(P)\otimes_{R}(S/Q)}

Here the vertical maps are the map
x\mapsto 1\otimes x
. Since
Q
is prime,
S/Q
is a domain and hence a torsion free module over
R/P
. Notice that

\kappa(P)\otimes_{R}(S/Q)=\kappa(P)\otimes_{R/P}(S/Q).

Thus these vertical maps are injective, which implies that the action of
f_{i}(t_{i})
on
S/Q
is
0
. Let
\bar{t}_{i}
be the image of
t_{i}
in
S/Q
. We see
f_{i}(\bar{t}_{i})=0
in
S/Q
. Thus
\bar{t}_{i}
is integral over
R/P
. Since
S/Q
is an

3.
CW Complex: Homological Finiteness

In this section, we prove a homological finiteness theorem which is the algebraic topological foundation of this work. Let us first explain its assumption, which is more general than that of Theorem
A
.

Let
X
be a connected CW complex. Suppose there is an epimorphism

(3.1)

\varphi:\ \pi_{1}(X)\rightarrow G\times\mathbb{Z},

where
G
is a finitely generated abelian group. Let
X_{k}
be the
k
-cover of
X
with
\pi_{1}(X_{k})=\varphi^{-1}(G\times k\mathbb{Z})
, and
X_{\infty}
be the infinite cyclic cover of
X
with
\pi_{1}(X_{\infty})=\varphi^{-1}(G\times 0)
. Assume that there is a homotopy equivalence
h:X\rightarrow X_{k}
for some
k>1
, such that the induced isomorphism
h_{\sharp}:\pi_{1}(X)\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}\pi_{1}(X_{k})

satisfies

(3.2)

h_{\sharp}(\ker\varphi)=\ker\varphi\quad\text{and}\quad h_{\sharp}(\varphi^{-1}(G\times 0))=\varphi^{-1}(G\times 0).

In the special case that
\varphi=\mathrm{id}
, the space
X
satisfies the assumption of Theorem
A
.

By (
3.2
), the above
h_{\sharp}
induces an isomorphism

(3.3)

[h_{\sharp}]\colon G\times\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow G\times(k\mathbb{Z})\quad\text{with}\quad[h_{\sharp}](G\times 0)=G\times 0.

The epimorphism
\varphi\colon\pi_{1}(X_{\infty})\to G
gives rise to an action

(3.4)

\pi_{1}(X_{\infty})\times G\rightarrow G,\ \ (\xi,g)\mapsto\varphi(\xi)\cdot g.

Let
R_{0}
be a commutative Noetherian ring, and let
R=R_{0}[G]
be the group ring. We identify the group ring
R_{0}[G\times\mathbb{Z}]
with the Laurent polynomial ring
R[t,t^{-1}]
, where
t
corresponds to
1\in\mathbb{Z}
. Both
R
and
R[t,t^{-1}]
are Noetherian commutative rings. Under the action of (
3.4
),
R
is a
\pi_{1}(X_{\infty})
-module, and
t
acts on
X_{\infty}
via deck transformation. The cellular chain complex
C_{\bullet}(X_{\infty};R)
with local coefficient system
R
is a chain complex of free
R[t,t^{-1}]
-modules, with a basis corresponding to the cells of
X
.

Proposition 3.1
.

If
X
is of finite type, then the homology group
H_{j}(X_{\infty};R)
is a finitely generated
R[t,t^{-1}]
-module for each
j
.

Proof.

Since
X
is of finite type,
C_{j}(X_{\infty};R)
is a free
R[t,t^{-1}]
-module of finite rank for each
j
. The conclusion follows from the Noetherianity of
R[t,t^{-1}]
.
∎

The main result of this section is the following stronger finiteness theorem.

Theorem 3.2
.

If
X
is of finite type, then the homology group
H_{j}(X_{\infty};R)
is a finitely generated
R
-module for each
j
.

We prove this theorem in the rest of this section.

For each
P\in\mathrm{Spec}(R)
, let
\kappa(P)
be the residual field of
R
at
P
, i.e. the quotient field of
R/P
. We first study the eigenvalues of the action of the deck transformation
t
on
\kappa(P)\otimes_{R}H_{j}(X_{\infty};R)
.

Lifting the homotopy equivalence
h\colon X\rightarrow X_{k}
to the covering space
X_{\infty}
, one gets a homotopy equivalence
\overline{h}\colon X_{\infty}\rightarrow X_{\infty}
. By assumption (
3.3
),
\overline{h}_{\sharp}\colon\pi_{1}(X_{\infty})\rightarrow\pi_{1}(X_{\infty})
induces an automorphism
[h_{\sharp}]|_{G\times 0}\colon\ G\rightarrow G
. Extend the isomorphism
[h_{\sharp}]\colon G\times 0\to G\times 0
in (
3.3
) to an automorphism
\sigma
of the group ring
R=R_{0}[G]
:

\sigma\left(\sum_{i}a_{i}g_{i}\right)=\sum_{i}a_{i}[h_{\sharp}](g_{i}),

where
a_{i}\in R_{0}
and
g_{i}\in G
. Then the homotopy equivalence
\overline{h}
induces a
\sigma
-twisted
R
-module isomorphism
\overline{h}_{*}\colon H_{j}(X_{\infty};R)\rightarrow H_{j}(X_{\infty};R)
. Here
\sigma

, the following equality always holds

(3.5)

\overline{h}_{*}(rx)=\sigma(r)\overline{h}_{*}(x).

Next we examine the commutativity between
\overline{h}_{*}
and the action of
t
on
H_{j}(X_{\infty};R)
. The isomorphism
[h_{\sharp}]
in (
3.3
) has the form

[h_{\sharp}]\colon\ G\times\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow G\times\mathbb{Z},\ \ \ (g,n)\mapsto(\sigma(g)+ng_{0},\pm kn),

where
[h_{\sharp}](0,1)=(g_{0},\pm k)
. Generalizing Definition
1.5
, we say
h

preserves the orientation of deck transformations
if
[h_{\sharp}](0,1)=(g_{0},k)
and
h

reverses the orientation of deck transformations
if otherwise.
From (

), we see that the chain map
\overline{h}_{*}\colon C_{\bullet}(X_{\infty};R)\to C_{\bullet}(X_{\infty};R)
satisfies

(3.7)

\overline{h}_{*}\cdot t=g_{0}\cdot t^{\pm k}\cdot\overline{h}_{*},

and hence the same equality holds for
\overline{h}_{*}\colon H_{\bullet}(X_{\infty};R)\to H_{\bullet}(X_{\infty};R)

By Proposition
3.1
and
[

,
\mathrm{Ass}_{R[t,t^{-1}]}(H_{j}(X_{\infty};R))
is a nonempty finite set provided that

is nonzero. Also by
[

\mathrm{Ass}_{R}(H_{j}(X_{\infty};R))=\{Q\cap R\mid Q\in\mathrm{Ass}_{R[t,t^{-1}]}(H_{j}(X_{\infty};R))\},

which is also finite.

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose
P\in\mathrm{Ass}_{R}(H_{j}(X_{\infty};R))
, and
\kappa(P)\otimes_{R}H_{j}(X_{\infty};R)
is a nonzero finite-dimensional

-vector space. Then the eigenvalues of

and

actions on this vector space are integral over

Proof.

Lifting to covering spaces, the homotopy equivalence
h\colon X\to X_{k}
induces a family of homotopy equivalences
X_{k^{i-1}}\to X_{k^{i}}
for all
i>0
. Composing these maps we obtain homotopy equivalences
h_{i}\colon X\to X_{k^{i}}
for all

. Note that

which is exactly
\overline{h}^{i}
, the

\overline{h}
.

By (

), we have
\overline{h}_{*}^{2}\cdot t=g_{1}\cdot t^{k^{2}}\cdot\overline{h}_{*}^{2}
for some

. Replacing
\overline{h}_{*}
with
\overline{h}_{*}^{2}
if necessary, we may assume

preserves the orientation of deck transformation.

We know
\overline{h}_{*}\colon H_{j}(X_{\infty};R)\rightarrow H_{j}(X_{\infty};R)
is a
\sigma
-twisted isomorphism of

-modules (c.f. (

)) and
\sigma
is an automorphism of
R
. It follows from the definition of the associated prime ideals that if

, then so are
\sigma(P^{\prime})
and
\sigma^{-1}(P^{\prime})
. Thus,
\sigma
acts on the finite set
\mathrm{Ass}_{R}(H_{j}(X_{\infty};R))
. Then
\sigma^{i}
acts trivially on
\mathrm{Ass}_{R}(H_{j}(X_{\infty};R))
for some

. Note that
\overline{h}_{*}^{i}
is
\sigma^{i}
-twisted. Replacing
\overline{h}_{*}
with
\overline{h}_{*}^{i}
if necessary, we may assume
\sigma(P)=P
. In this case,
\sigma
induces automorphisms of
R/P
and
\kappa(P)
.

The
\sigma
-twisted
R
-module isomorphism
\overline{h}_{*}\colon H_{j}(X_{\infty};R)\rightarrow H_{j}(X_{\infty};R)
extends to a well-defined map
\sigma\otimes\overline{h}_{*}\colon\kappa(P)\otimes_{R}H_{j}(X_{\infty};R)\rightarrow\kappa(P)\otimes_{R}H_{j}(X_{\infty};R)

t=(\sigma\otimes\overline{h}_{*})^{-1}\cdot g_{0}\cdot t^{k}\cdot(\sigma\otimes\overline{h}_{*}),

of actions on
\kappa(P)\otimes_{R}H_{j}(X_{\infty};R)
, where

and

stand for
\kappa(P)
-linear maps

and

respectively. Since

is

-twisted, we have

Lemma 3.6
.

By Proposition

(2)

Proposition 4.2
.

Suppose

and

are in

such that

for some

. Then
A^{m}=1
for some positive

prime to
k
.

Proof.

The assumption implies
B^{2}A^{k^{2}}B^{-2}=A
. And
m
is prime to
k
if it is prime to

. Therefore by replacing
B
with

if necessary, we may assume

.

Let
\lambda
be an eigenvalue of
A
, then the equation

implies that
\lambda^{k}
is an eignvalue of
A
as well. We claim
\lambda
is a root of unity. Otherwise,
A
would have infinitely many distinct eigenvalues
\lambda^{k^{u}}
for all
u\in\mathbb{N}
, which is impossible. Let
\Lambda(A)
be the set of the eigenvalues of
A
. Let
H
be the subgroup of the multiplicative group
\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
generated by
\Lambda(A)
, then

is finite, say of order
m
. Since
\Lambda(A^{k})=\Lambda(A)
, the map

is an automorphism of

. Therefore,

is prime to
k
.

Let
D=A^{m}
, then all eigenvalues of

are
1
. Thus
N=D-1
is nilpotent. We have to prove

is indeed

. Suppose

for some

. When

, we have

D^{k^{v}}-1=(1+N)^{k^{v}}-1=\sum_{i=1}^{u-1}\frac{k^{v}!}{(k^{v}-i)!i!}N^{i}.

Thus for each

, we have

divides all entries of

when

is large enough. On the other hand,

B^{v}\left(D^{k^{v}}-1\right)B^{-v}=D-1=N.

So
k^{r}
divides all entries of

for all

, which implies

.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let

or

are annihilated by some integer

∎

Proof of Theorem

Let

Now we turn to the proof of Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem

.

(1). Let

be the covering of

is finitely dominated. Since

The idea is to interpret the fibering problem as a splitting problem following
[

Let

be the deck transformation corresponding

,

By the fact

Cutting

becomes

, and

becomes

and

is isomorphic to

is a fiber bundle over

with fiber

.

(2). From (1), we see

is isomorphic to

, and the deck transformation

,

is the monodromy of the fiber bundle. The homotopy equivalence

induces a homotopy equivalence

such that

(resp.

) if

preserves (resp. reverses) the orientation of deck transformations. Let

,

(3). Both

are fiber bundles over

with fiber

. Fix a fiber

Proof of Theorem

The construction of such a manifold follows from the theory of

[

). Let

be the positive definite unimodular symmetric quadratic form over

of rank

(c. f.

is the Kirby-Siebenmann class

. Therefore

is smoothable.

Equip

with a smooth structure. For each integer

N_{2^{u}}

is diffeomorphic to

and

.

It remains to show that

is not a DIFF (PL) bundle over

for all

were such a bundle, then its fiber

\Gamma
be a group, and let
\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma]=\{\sum_{i}n_{i}g_{i}\ |\ n_{i}\in\mathbb{Z},\ g_{i}\in\Gamma\}
be the integral group ring, with conjugation
\sum_{i}n_{i}g_{i}\mapsto\sum_{i}n_{i}g_{i}^{-1}
. The group
K_{0}(\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma])
is the Grothendieck group of finitely generated projective
\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma]
-modules, i. e., elements in
K_{0}(\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma])
are of the form
[P]-[Q]
, where
P
and
Q
are finitely generated projective left
\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma]
-modules.
There is a canonical involution (or conjugation) on
K_{0}(\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma])
, defined as follows. For a finitely generated projective left
\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma]
-module
P
, its dual module
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma]}(P,\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma])
is a right
\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma]
-module. We convert this right module to a left module via the conjugation of
\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma]
and denote it by
P^{*}
. Equivalently, if
P
is represented by an idempotent matrix
A
over
\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma]
, then
P^{*}
is represented by the conjugate transpose of
A
. The map
[P]\mapsto[P^{*}]
extends to an involution of
K_{0}(\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma])
, denoted by
y\mapsto y^{*}
. A caveat: for an abelian group
\Gamma
, the group ring
\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma]
is a commutative ring, and hence the dual module
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma]}(P,\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma])
is automatically a left module, but this is
not
the conjugate
P^{*}
in the definition.

Let
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}/p
be a cyclic group of prime order

. A nice reference for the basic facts about

[

\lx@sectionsign
1-3]

, which, by Rim’s Theorem, induces an isomorphism between the reduced

-groups

\widetilde{K}_{0}(\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}/p])\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}\widetilde{K}_{0}(\mathbb{Z}[\xi_{p}]).

The group

is isomorphic to the ideal class group of
\mathbb{Z}[\xi_{p}]
, whose elements are represented by the fractional ideals

(c. f.
[

By
[

, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 6.1
.

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}[\xi_{p}]}(L,\mathbb{Z}[\xi_{p}])=L^{-1}
.

The conjugation of
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}/p]
corresponds to the complex conjugation of
\mathbb{Z}[\xi_{p}]
. By Lemma

, for

(6.1)

[L]^{*}=\left[\overline{L^{-1}}^{\mathbb{C}}\right]=-\left[\overline{L}^{\mathbb{C}}\right],

where

There are classical results on the

-eigenspace of

under the complex conjugation. More precisely,

and

There is also an involution on

represented by an

.

Lemma 6.5
.

For each
y\in\widetilde{K}_{0}(\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}/p])
,
\phi(y)^{*}=-\phi\left(y^{*}\right)
.

Proof.

Suppose

. We have

\phi([P])^{*}=[tP+(I-P)]^{*}=[t^{-1}P^{*}+I-P^{*}]=-[tP^{*}+I-P^{*}]=-\phi\left([P]^{*}\right).

Here the third equality is because
(t^{-1}P^{*}+I-P^{*})(tP^{*}+I-P^{*})=I
. Similarly,
\phi([Q])^{*}=-\phi\left([Q]^{*}\right)
. The conclusion follows.
∎

7.
Further Questions

We conclude this paper with some questions.

This paper only deals with the fibering problem with abelian fundamental groups. A generalization to arbitrary fundamental groups can be formulated as follows. Let
M
be a closed manifold with fundamental groups
G\rtimes\mathbb{Z}
, where
G
is finitely presented (and not necessarily abelian). Let
M_{k}
be the
k
-cover of
M
with fundamental group
G\rtimes(k\mathbb{Z})
. Suppose there is a homotopy equivalence
h:M\rightarrow M_{k}
such that
h_{\sharp}(G)=G
for some
k>1
, where
h_{\sharp}:\pi_{1}(M)\rightarrow\pi_{1}(M_{k})
is induced by
h
. Does
M
always fiber over
S^{1}
? To answer this question, we need to know if
M_{\infty}
is homotopy equivalent to a finite CW complex, where
M_{\infty}
is the infinite cyclic cover of
M
with
\pi_{1}(M_{\infty})=G
. This leads to the following question about generalizations of Theorem
A
.

Question 7.1
.

Suppose
X
is a CW complex with
\pi_{1}(X)=G\rtimes\mathbb{Z}
, where
G
is finitely presented. Let
X_{k}
and
X_{\infty}
be covers of
X
with
\pi_{1}(X_{k})=G\rtimes(k\mathbb{Z})
and
\pi_{1}(X_{\infty})=G
. Suppose
h:X\rightarrow X_{k}
is a homotopy equivalence for some
k>1
such that
h_{\sharp}(G)=G
. If
X
is homotopy equivalent to a CW complex of finite type (resp. finitely dominated), then does this imply
X_{\infty}
is as well?

Because our arguments heavily rely on the theory of commutative algebra, they do not apply if
\pi_{1}(X)
is not abelian.

Question 7.2
.

Is there a proof of Theorem
A
without using extensively commutative algebra?

We have answered Question
1.2
almost completely with one exception:
n=4
and CAT is DIFF (=PL). As pointed out in the introduction, to answer this missing case is equivalent to answer the following question, which is open to the best of the authors’ knowledge.

Question 7.3
.

Do
S^{1}\times S^{3}
and
S^{1}\tilde{\times}S^{3}
have a unique smooth structure?

Finally, we are interested in the following question.

Question 7.4
.

Is there an
n
-manifold
M
satisfying the conclusion of Theorem
C
with
n<9
, especially
n\geq 5
?

Forbidden paths and cycles in the undirected underlying graph of a 2-quasi best match graph
Annachiara Korchmaros

Abstract
The undirected underlying graph of a 2-quasi best match graph (2-qBMG) is proven not to contain any induced graph isomorphic to P 6 P_{6} or C 6 C_{6} . This new feature allows for the investigation of 2-BMGs further by exploiting the numerous known results on P 6 P_{6} and C 6 C_{6} free graphs together with the available polynomial algorithms developed for their studies. In this direction, there are also some new contributions about dominating bicliques and certain vertex decompositions of the undirected underlying graph of a 2-qBMG.

Phylogenetic combinatorics, quasi best match graphs,
P_{6}
- and
C_{6}
-free graphs, dominating set, vertex decomposition.

Introduction

Quasi-best match graphs are directed vertex-colored graphs explained by phylogenetic trees.
A tree-free characterization is known so far for the case of two colors
Korchmaros et al. (2023)
:
A
two-color quasi best match graph
(2-qBMG) is a bipartite directed graph
\overrightarrow{G}
without loops and parallel edges which has the following three properties:

(N1)

if
u
and
v
are two independent vertices then there exist no vertices
w,t
such that
ut,vw,tw
are edges;

(N2)

bi-transitive, i.e., if
uv,vw,wt
are edges then
ut
is also an edge;

(N3)

if
u
and
v
have common out-neighbor then either all out-neighbors of
u
are also out-neighbors of
v
or
all out-neighbors of
v
are also out-neighbors of
u
.

A 2-qBMG is a
two-colored best match graph
(2-BMG) if it is sink-free, i.e., no vertex has empty out-neighborhood, and it is a
reciprocal two-best match graph
(reciprocal 2-BMG) if all its edges are symmetric.
Moreover, 2-qBMGs but not 2-BMGs form a hereditary class
Korchmaros et al. (2023)
.

2-BMGs, 2-qBMGs, and more generally best match graphs
have been the subjects of intensive studies, also motivated by their relevant roles in the current investigation of orthology predictions from sequence similarity in the case that the gene families histories are free from gene transfers; see
Ramírez-Rafael et al. (2024)
;
Schaller et al. (2021)
;
Korchmaros (2021)
;
Korchmaros et al. (2023)
;
Schaller et al. (2021a)
;
Schaller et al. (2021b)
;
Schaller et al. (2021c)
;
Hellmuth and Stadler (2024)
. A major contribution
(
Schaller et al., 2021b
, Theorem 3.4)
is a characterization of 2-qBMGs by three types of forbidden induced subgraphs, called of types
(F_{1}),(F_{2})
and
(F_{3})
; see Section
2
.

This investigation focuses on specific properties of the underlying undirected graph of a 2-qBMG. Actually, such undirected graphs have been touched so far only marginally in the study of 2-qBMGs except in the very special case of reciprocal 2-BMGs; see
Manuela et al. (2020)
;
Hellmuth et al. (2020)
.
The main motivation for this study is the richness of the literature on undirected graphs. It is indeed much richer than on directed graphs, with plenty of fundamental works on forbidden configurations, decompositions, and vertex-colorings, as well as on algorithms and valuations on computational complexity. The aim is to exploit some of these deeper results on undirected graphs to gain new insights into 2-qBMGs. It may happen, however, that going back to a digraph
\overrightarrow{G}
from its underlying undirected graph
G
, a deep result on
G
only has a very limited, or even trivial impact on
\overrightarrow{G}
. Thus, the structural relationship between
\overrightarrow{G}
and
G
is far to be immediate.
For instance,
(
Schaller et al., 2021b
, Theorem 3.4)
does not yield an analog characterization in terms of forbidden induced subgraphs of the underlying undirected graph.
This depends on the fact that the underlying undirected graph of a digraph of type
(F_{i})
,
1\leq i\leq 3
with only required edges is either a path of length
4
or
5
but it is not necessarily a forbidden subgraph of the underlying undirected graph of a 2-qBMG.

Therefore, the challenge is to find appropriate results and knowledge on undirected graphs that may produce relevant contributions to the study of 2-qBMGs via their underlying undirected graphs.

The main results in this direction are Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
3.9
, which state that the underlying undirected graph of every 2-qBMG is
P_{6}
-free and
C_{6}
-free, that is, both
P_{6}
and
C_{6}
are forbidden induced subgraphs. This result is sharp as the underlying undirected graph of a 2-qBMG may have an induced path
P_{5}
(and hence induced
P_{4}
as well); see Theorem
3.2
. Therefore, the underlying undirected graphs of a type
(F_{i})
digraph,
1\leq i\leq 3
, are not forbidden subgraphs for the underlying undirected graph of 2-qBMGs.

In their seminal paper
Bacsó and Tuza (1993)
appeared in 1993, Bacsó and Tuza pointed out the strong relationship between
P_{k}
-freeness and dominating subsets in undirected graphs. Relevant contributions on such a relationship were given in several papers, especially in
Bacsó et al. (2005)
;
Brandstädt et al. (2006)
;
van’t Hof and Paulusma (2010)
;
Liu and Zhou (1994)
;
Liu et al. (2007)
. In particular, for any bipartite graph
G
, Liu and Zhou proved that
G
is both
P_{6}
-free and
C_{6}
-free if and only if every connected induced subgraph of
G
has a dominating biclique; see
(
Liu and Zhou, 1994
, Theorem 1)
. An independent constructive proof of the Liu-Zhou theorem is due to P. van’t Hof and D. Paulusma
van’t Hof and Paulusma (2010)
where the authors also provide an algorithm that finds such a dominating biclique of a connected
P_{6}
-free graph in polynomial time. For a discussion on the computational complexity of the problem of finding (not necessarily dominating) bicliques in undirected bipartite graphs, see
Peeters (2003)
. An upper bound on the number of bicliques of
C_{6}
-free undirected bipartite graphs in terms of the sizes of their color classes
U,W
is given in
(
Prisner, 2000
, Theorem 3.3)
where it is shown that such number does not exceed
|U|^{2}|W|^{2}
.

Recognition and optimization problems for both
P_{6}
and
C_{6}
-free bipartite undirected graphs and certain decompositions involving
K\oplus S
graphs have been studied by several authors, following the paper
Fouquet et al. (1999)
. In particular, it is shown that the class of both
P_{6}
and
C_{6}
-free bipartite graphs can be recognized in linear time. Also, efficient solutions for two NP-hard problems are presented in this class of graphs: the maximum balanced biclique problem and the maximum independent set problem. For more details, see the recent paper
Quaddoura and Al-Qerem (2024)
.

A contribution to the study of the vertex decomposition problem for 2-qBMGs in smaller connected 2-qBMGs of type (A) is given in Theorem
4.2
, where a connected 2-qBMG is of type (A) if its underlying undirected graph is a
K\oplus S
graph, that is, it has a vertex decomposition into a (dominating) biclique
K
and a stable set
S
, that is, any two vertices in
S
are independent.

Background

The notation and terminology for undirected graphs are standard. Let
G
be an undirected graph with vertex-set
V(G)
and edge-set
E(G)
.
In particular, a path
P_{n}
of an undirected graph
G
is a sequence
v_{1}v_{2}\cdots v_{n}
of pairwise distinct vertices of
G
such that
v_{i}v_{i+1}\in E(G)
for
i=1,\ldots,n-1
. A
P_{n}

path-graph
is an undirected graph on
n
vertices with a path
v_{1}v_{2}\cdots v_{n}
containing no chord, i.e. any edge in
E(G)
is
v_{i}v_{i+1}\in E(G)
for some
1\leq i<n
. A
P_{n}
path-graph with a path
v_{1}v_{2}\cdots v_{n}
containing no chord is a bipartite graph with (uniquely determined) color sets
\{v_{1},v_{3},\ldots\}
and
\{v_{2},v_{4},\ldots\}
. An undirected graph is
P_{n}
-
free
if it has no induced subgraph isomorphic to a
P_{n}
path-graph. A
cograph
is a
P_{4}
-free graph.

A cycle
C_{n}
of an undirected graph
G
is a sequence
v_{1}v_{2}\cdots v_{n}v_{1}
of pairwise distinct vertices of
G
such that
v_{i}v_{i+1}\in E(G)
for
i=1,\ldots,n-1
, and
v_{n}v_{1}\in E(G)
.
A
C_{n}

cycle-graph
is an undirected graph on
n
vertices with a cycle
v_{1}v_{2}\cdots v_{n}v_{1}
containing no chord, i.e. any edge in
E(G)
is either
v_{i}v_{i+1}\in E(G)
for some
1\leq i\leq n-1
, or
v_{n}v_{1}
. For
n
even, a
C_{n}
cycle-graph with a cycle
v_{1}v_{2}\cdots v_{n}v_{1}
containing no chord is a bipartite graph with (uniquely determined) color classes
\{v_{1},v_{3},\ldots\}
and
\{v_{2},v_{4},\ldots\}
. Bipartite cycle-graphs can only exist for even
n
. An undirected graph is
C_{n}
-
free
if it has no induced subgraph isomorphic to an
C_{n}
cycle-graph.

A set
D\subseteq V(G)
is a
dominating set
of an undirected graph
G
if, for any vertex
u\in V(G)\setminus D
, there is a vertex
v\in D
such that
uv\in E(G)
. We also say that
D
dominates
G
. A subgraph
H
of
G
is a
dominating subgraph
of
G
if the vertex set of
H
dominates
G
.

An undirected graph
G
is of type
K\oplus S
if either
G
is degenerate, i.e. if it has an isolated vertex, or there is a partition of
V(G)
into two sets: a biclique set
K
and a stable set
S
; see
Fouquet et al. (1999)
;
Quaddoura and Al-Qerem (2024)
.

For directed graphs
\overrightarrow{G}
with vertex-set
V(\overrightarrow{G})
and edge-set
E(\overrightarrow{G})
, in particular for 2-qBMGs, notation and terminology come from
Korchmaros et al. (2023)
. In particular,
N^{+}(v)
and
N^{-}(v)
stand for the set of out-neighbours and in-neighbours of
v
in
\overrightarrow{G}
,
and
v
is a
sink
when
N^{+}(v)=\emptyset
, and
v
is a source when
N^{-}(v)=\emptyset
.
A digraph
\overrightarrow{G}
is
oriented
if
uv\in E(\overrightarrow{G})
implies
vu\notin E(\overrightarrow{G})
for any
u,v\in V(\overrightarrow{G})
. An oriented digraph has a
topological vertex ordering
if its vertices can be labeled with
v_{1},v_{2},\ldots
such that for any
v_{i}v_{j}\in E(\overrightarrow{G})
we have
i<j
. A sufficient condition for an oriented digraph to have a topological ordering is to be
acyclic
, that is, there is no directed cycle in the digraph. An
orientation
of a digraph
\overrightarrow{G}
is a digraph obtained from
\overrightarrow{G}
by keeping the same vertex set but retaining exactly one edge from each symmetric edge. From
(
Korchmaros, 2021
, Lemma 2.2)
and
(
Korchmaros, 2021
, Theorem 3.8)
, any orientation of a 2-qBMG is acyclic whenever at least one of the following conditions are satisfied:

(
*
)

no two (or more than two) symmetric edges of
\overrightarrow{G}
have a common endpoint;

(
**
)

no two (or more than two) vertices of
\overrightarrow{G}
are equivalent, i.e. no two vertices have the same in- and out-neighbors.

Acyclic-oriented digraphs are odd–even graphs. For a pair
(\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{O})
where
\mathfrak{A}
is a finite set of non-negative even integers and
\mathfrak{O}
is a set of positive odd integers, the associated odd–even oriented digraph
\overrightarrow{G}
has vertex-set
\mathfrak{A}
and edge-set with

and
\frac{1}{2}(b-a)

and

.
An oriented bipartite digraph is a
bitournament
if for any two vertices

with different colors, either

, or
uv\in E(\overrightarrow{G})
. A bi-transitive bitournament is an odd-even digraph; see
(
Korchmaros, 2021
, Proposition 3.9)
.

In addition, for four vertices

of
\overrightarrow{G}

is an (N1)-configuration if

A 2-qBMG is

Forbidden induced path graphs of underlying undirected 2-qBMGs

The main result in the paper which establishes a new property of 2-qBMGs is given in the following theorem.

.

The underlying undirected graph of a 2-qBMG is

Theorem 3.2
.

There exist 2-qBMGs on five vertices which are not
P_{5}
-free. More precisely,
there are exactly six non-isomorphic 2-qBMGs on five vertices
\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4},v_{5}\}
whose underlying undirected graph is a
P_{5}

4
Dominating sets in 2-qBMGs

As pointed out in the introduction, Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
3.9
, together with previous results, give new contributions to the current studies on the structure of 2-qBMGs. This section is focused on dominating biclique sets.

Recall that a connected 2-qBMG is of type (A) if its undirected underlying graph has a vertex decomposition into a biclique and a stable set. It may be observed that such a biclique is necessarily a dominating set.

Example 4.1
.

The digraph on
10
vertices
\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{10}\}
with edge-set

\displaystyle E(\overrightarrow{G})=\{

\displaystyle v_{1}v_{5},v_{1}v_{6},v_{1}v_{7},v_{1}v_{8},v_{5}v_{2},v_{6}v_{2},v_{2}v_{7},v_{2}v_{8},

\displaystyle v_{5}v_{3},v_{6}v_{3},v_{3}v_{7},v_{3}v_{8},v_{5}v_{4},v_{6}v_{4},v_{7}v_{4},v_{4}v_{8},

\displaystyle v_{5}v_{9},v_{1}v_{10},v_{2}v_{10}\}

is a 2-qBMG of type (A) whose underlying undirected graph

has an induced dominating subgraph with vertex set
\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4},v_{5},v_{6},v_{7},v_{8}\}
and stable set
\{v_{9},v_{10}\}
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Every connected 2-qBMG has a vertex decomposition into connected 2-qBMGs of type (A).

Proof.

Let
\overrightarrow{G}\,
be a connected 2-qBMG not of type (A).
Let
G
be the underlying undirected graph of
\overrightarrow{G}
, where
U
and

W
are its color classes.
As mentioned in the introduction, from
(
Liu and Zhou, 1994
, Theorem 1)
,
G
contains a dominating biclique
\Delta
with vertex set
T\cup Z
where
T\subseteq U
and
Z\subseteq W
. Let
\overrightarrow{\Delta}
be the directed subgraph of
\overrightarrow{G}
induced by
\Delta
.
Let
S
be the subset of
V(G)
consisting of all vertices
v\in V(G)
for which
N^{+}(v)\cup N^{-}(v)
is contained in
T\cup Z
. Let
\overrightarrow{\Sigma}
be the induced subgraph of
\overrightarrow{G}
on
T\cup Z\cup S
. Since
\Delta
is a dominating set of

\overrightarrow{\Sigma}
is connected without isolated vertex and is a connected 2-qBMG. The underlying undirected graph
\Sigma
of

is a

.

Furthermore, let
\overrightarrow{G}_{1}
be the induced subgraph of
\overrightarrow{G}
on

\overrightarrow{G}_{1}
is isolated, although some vertex of
\overrightarrow{G}
may become a sink in
\overrightarrow{G}_{1}
, and non-equivalent vertices
\overrightarrow{G}
may become equivalent in
\overrightarrow{G}_{1}
. If

is not a connected 2-qBMG of type (A), we can repeat the above construction to define a finite sequence

such that
\overrightarrow{\Sigma},\overrightarrow{\Sigma}_{1},\overrightarrow{\Sigma}_{2},\ldots,\overrightarrow{\Sigma}_{k}

If some of the members of the subsequence

gives the claimed partition.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\overrightarrow{G}
be a 2-qBMG satisfying at least one of conditions (
*
) and (
**

Fix an orientation
\overrightarrow{\Gamma}
of
\overrightarrow{G}

Let
\overrightarrow{\Delta}
be an induced subgraph of
\,\,\overrightarrow{\Gamma}
such that

Let

\overrightarrow{G}

has a topological ordering. Therefore, the vertices of
\overrightarrow{\Gamma}
can be labelled with

In particular, for

5
Future directions

The
P_{6}
-and
C_{6}
-freeness in the undirected underlying 2-qBMGs raises the problem of finding more such forbidden subgraphs on six (or eight) vertices. In this direction, the domino graph on six vertices (i.e. the Cartesian product
P_{2}\times P_{3}
) and its generalization on eight vertices, the long-domino or ladder graph, appear worth investigating.

In
Quaddoura and Al-Qerem (2024)
, bipartite
P_{6}
and
C_{6}
-free graphs are characterized as those graphs where every connected subgraph is of type
K\oplus S
. This gives rise to a linear time algorithm for recognizing if an undirected graph is
P_{6}
and
C_{6}
-free by tracing all
K\oplus S
decompositions. It would be interesting to investigate whether a similar approach can be used to perform a linear time algorithm to recognize if a digraph is a 2-qBMG. The first step is to check whether the converse of
(
Quaddoura and Al-Qerem, 2024
, Theorem 2)
holds.

The s-dim of a graph
G
is the minimum number of bicliques needed to cover the edge-set of
G
. Computing the s-dim (biclique cover problem) is NP-complete for bipartite graphs
Dawande et al. (2001)
; however, this problem is linear for some classes of graphs, such as bipartite domino-free graphs
Amilhastre et al. (1998)
. Investigating the complexity of the biclique cover problem for the family of underlying undirected graphs of 2-qBMGs is also an interesting topic for future investigation.

For two disjoint subsets
W
and
T
of vertices,
W

2-dominates

T
if every vertex of
T
is
adjacent to at least two vertices of
W
. A partition
\{V_{1},V_{2},\ldots,V_{n}\}
of vertices in
V(G)
into
n
parts is a
2-transitive partition
of size
n
if
V_{i}
2-dominates for all
1\leq i<j\leq n
. Finding a 2-transitivity partition of maximum order is NP-complete for bipartite graphs
Paul and Santra (2023)
. Studying the complexity of this problem for the family of underlying undirected graphs of 2-qBMGs is also an interesting topic for future investigation.

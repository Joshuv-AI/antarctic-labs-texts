Deformed Quantum Phase Spaces, Realizations, Star Products and Twists
Stjepan MELJANAC a and Rina ŠTRAJN b

Abstract
We review deformed quantum phase spaces and their realizations in terms of undeformed phase space. In particular, methods of calculation for the star product, coproduct of momenta and twist from realizations are presented, as well as their properties and the relations between them. Lie deformed quantum phase spaces and Snyder type spaces are considered. Examples of linear realizations of the κ \kappa -Minkowski spacetime are elaborated. Finally, some new results on quadratic deformations of quantum phase spaces and a generalization of Yang and triply special relativity models are presented.

2
Lie deformed quantum phase space and realizations

The undeformed quantum phase space is defined with coordinates
x_{\mu}
and momenta
p_{\mu}

\displaystyle[x_{\mu},x_{\nu}]=0,\quad[p_{\mu},p_{\nu}]=0,\qquad[p_{\mu},x_{\nu}]=-{\rm i}\eta_{\mu\nu},\qquad\mu,\nu=0,1,\dots,n-1,

where
\eta_{\mu\nu}=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,\dots,1)
. The generalization of
\eta_{\mu\nu}
to a metric with arbitrary Lorentz signature is straightforward.

We consider the deformed quantum phase space defined with noncommutative (NC) coordinates
\hat{x}_{\mu}
and momenta
p_{\mu}
of the type

\displaystyle[\hat{x}_{\mu},\hat{x}_{\nu}]={\rm i}\hat{x}_{\alpha}C_{\mu\nu\alpha}(l,p)+{\rm i}d_{\mu\nu}(l,p),\qquad[p_{\mu},\hat{x}_{\nu}]=-{\rm i}\varphi_{\mu\nu}(l,p),\qquad[p_{\mu},p_{\nu}]=0,

(2.1)

where
l
is a parameter of order of the Planck length and summation over repeated indices is assumed. For
l=0
,
C_{\mu\nu\alpha}(0,p)=0
and
d_{\mu\nu}(0,p)=0
. Functions
C_{\mu\nu\alpha}
and
\varphi_{\mu\nu}
depend on momenta
p_{\alpha}
, where
C_{\mu\nu\alpha}
are a generalization of the structure constants.

For example, the original Snyder space is defined with

\displaystyle[\hat{x}_{\mu},\hat{x}_{\nu}]={\rm i}l^{2}M_{\mu\nu},

(2.2)

where
M_{\mu\nu}
are Lorentz generators. The realization Snyder proposed
[
116
]
is given by

\displaystyle\hat{x}_{\mu}=x_{\mu}+l^{2}(x\cdot p)p_{\mu},\qquad M_{\mu\nu}=x_{\mu}p_{\nu}-x_{\nu}p_{\mu}=\hat{x}_{\mu}p_{\nu}-\hat{x}_{\nu}p_{\mu}.

Hence

\displaystyle C_{\mu\nu\alpha}(l,p)=l^{2}(\eta_{\mu\alpha}p_{\nu}-\eta_{\nu\alpha}p_{\mu}).

If
C_{\mu\nu\lambda}
are structure constants, then NC coordinates
\hat{x}_{\mu}
close a Lie algebra. A perturbative construction of
\varphi_{\mu\nu}(l,p)
corresponding to the symmetric ordering can be found in
[
34
]
. From
[p_{\mu},\hat{x}_{\nu}]=-{\rm i}\varphi_{\mu\nu}(l,p)
, (
2.1
), it follows that the realization of NC coordinates
\hat{x}_{\mu}
can be written as

\displaystyle\hat{x}_{\mu}=x_{\alpha}\varphi_{\alpha\mu}(l,p)+\chi_{\mu}(l,p),

(2.3)

and the inverse is given by

\displaystyle x_{\nu}=(\hat{x}_{\mu}-\chi_{\mu}(l,p))\big(\varphi^{-1}\big)_{\mu\nu},

and

\displaystyle p_{\mu}=-{\rm i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{\mu}}.

(2.4)

If
l=0
,
\varphi_{\mu\nu}(0,p)=\eta_{\mu\nu}
. All Jacobi relations for the class of deformed quantum phase spaces/deformed Weyl–Heisenberg algebras (
2.1
) (obtained from (

Realizations of the type given in equation (

is at most linear in

\displaystyle\hat{x}_{\mu}=x_{\mu}+lK_{\beta\mu\alpha}x_{\alpha}p_{\beta}+\chi_{\mu}(l,p),\qquad K_{\beta\mu\alpha}\in\mathbb{R},

(3.4)

K^{-1}

According to the PBW theorem, if coordinates

,

3.1
Snyder space and an extension with tensorial coordinates

Examples of non associative star products are related to the following realizations of coordinates
\hat{x}_{\mu}

\displaystyle\hat{x}_{\mu}=x_{\mu}\varphi_{1}\big(l^{2}p^{2}\big)+l^{2}(x\cdot p)p_{\mu}\varphi_{2}\big(l^{2}p^{2}\big).

Then the commutation relations
[\hat{x}_{\mu},\hat{x}_{\nu}]
lead to a generalized Snyder algebra
[
86
]

\displaystyle[\hat{x}_{\mu},\hat{x}_{\nu}]={\rm i}l^{2}M_{\mu\nu}\psi\big(l^{2}p^{2}\big),

where
M_{\mu\nu}=x_{\mu}p_{\nu}-x_{\nu}p_{\mu}
are Lorentz generators. Specially, for
\psi\big(l^{2}p^{2}\big)=1
, it becomes the Snyder algebra originally proposed in
[
116
]
.

In the original Snyder model NC coordinates
\hat{x}_{\mu}
do not close a Lie algebra and the corresponding star products are non associative. For example, the star product corresponding to the realization

\displaystyle\hat{x}_{\mu}=x_{\mu}+l^{2}(x\cdot p)p_{\mu}

(3.6)

leads to

\displaystyle x_{\mu}*x_{\nu}=x_{\mu}x_{\nu},

and

\displaystyle x_{\mu}*(x_{\nu}*x_{\rho})=x_{\mu}x_{\nu}x_{\rho}-l^{2}(\eta_{\mu\nu}x_{\rho}+\eta_{\mu\rho}x_{\nu}),

\displaystyle(x_{\mu}*x_{\nu})*x_{\rho}=x_{\mu}x_{\nu}x_{\rho}-\frac{l^{2}}{2}(\eta_{\mu\rho}x_{\nu}+\eta_{\nu\rho}x_{\mu}+2\eta_{\mu\nu}x_{\rho}).

Hence, the star product is non associative

\displaystyle(x_{\mu}*x_{\nu})*x_{\rho}-x_{\mu}*(x_{\nu}*x_{\rho})=\frac{l^{2}}{2}(\eta_{\mu\rho}x_{\nu}-\eta_{\nu\rho}x_{\mu}).

Using the realization in equation (
3.6

for which equation (
3.5

\displaystyle\Delta p_{\mu}=\frac{1}{1-l^{2}p_{\alpha}\otimes p^{\alpha}}\left(p_{\mu}\otimes 1-\frac{l^{2}}{1+\sqrt{1+l^{2}p^{2}}}p_{\mu}p_{\alpha}\otimes p^{\alpha}+\sqrt{1+l^{2}p^{2}}\otimes p_{\mu}\right)

4
Coproduct of momenta and twist from star product

and realizations

The relation between the star product and the twist operator is given by

\displaystyle(f*g)(x)=m\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\triangleright\otimes 1)(f(x)\otimes g(x)),

where
m
is the multiplication map,
A\otimes B\rightarrow AB
.

Using the star product (
3.2
) and the above relation for
f*g
, a family of twist operators can be writen as
[
85
,
86
,
87
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{-1}={:}\exp(({\rm i}(1-u)x_{\alpha}\otimes 1+u1\otimes x_{\alpha})(\Delta-\Delta_{0})p_{\alpha}){:}\,\exp({\rm i}\mathcal{G}(p\otimes 1,1\otimes p)),

(4.1)

where
{:}\,{:}
denotes normal ordering, in which the
x
s are to the left of the
p
’s,
u
is a real parameter and

\displaystyle\Delta p_{\alpha}=\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}(p\otimes 1,1\otimes p),\qquad\Delta_{0}p_{\alpha}=p_{\alpha}\otimes 1+1\otimes p_{\alpha},

where
\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}(l,k,q)
is given in (
3.3
) and
\mathcal{G}
in (
3.4
). Applying
[
101
, Theorem 1]
, we can find the twist in the form without normal ordering, see for example
[
100
, Section 4]
.

It is important to note that
\Delta p_{\alpha}
is the coproduct of momenta and it holds

\displaystyle\Delta p_{\alpha}=\mathcal{F}\Delta_{0}p_{\alpha}\mathcal{F}^{-1}.

This can be proved using the identity

\displaystyle\sum_{k,l=0}^{n}(-1)^{n}\frac{x^{k}px^{l}}{k!l!}=0,\qquad\text{for}\ n\geq 2.

Alternatively, we can obtain the twist in the following way. The coproduct
\Delta p_{\mu}
is obtained from
\Delta p_{\alpha}=\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}(p\otimes 1,1\otimes p)
and (
3.3
)

\displaystyle\Delta p_{\mu}=\left(\exp\left(K^{-1}_{\beta}(p)\otimes\varphi_{\alpha\beta}(p)\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{\alpha}}\right)\right)(1\otimes p_{\mu}).

The coproduct is coassociative if and only if the star product is associative, i.e., if
\hat{x}_{\mu}
close a Lie algebra.

We can define new momenta
p^{W}_{\mu}
corresponding to the Weyl realization

\displaystyle p^{W}_{\mu}=K_{\mu}^{-1}(p),

with the property

\displaystyle p_{\mu}^{W}\triangleright\text{e}^{{\rm i}K(k)\cdot x}=k_{\mu}\text{e}^{{\rm i}K(k)\cdot x},

and

\displaystyle\big[p^{W}_{\mu},\text{e}^{{\rm i}k\cdot\hat{x}}\big]=k_{\mu}\text{e}^{{\rm i}k\cdot\hat{x}}.

Hence, the coproduct of momenta is

\displaystyle\Delta p_{\mu}=\text{e}^{-{\rm i}p^{W}_{\alpha}\otimes\hat{x}_{\alpha}}(1\otimes p_{\mu})\text{e}^{{\rm i}p^{W}_{\beta}\otimes\hat{x}_{\beta}}.

Then we obtain the relation between
\Delta p_{\mu}
and
\Delta_{0}p_{\mu}

\displaystyle\Delta p_{\mu}=\mathcal{F}\Delta_{0}p_{\mu}\mathcal{F}^{-1}=\text{e}^{-{\rm i}p_{\alpha}^{W}\otimes\hat{x}_{\alpha}}\text{e}^{{\rm i}p_{\beta}\otimes x_{\beta}}(\Delta_{0}p_{\mu})\text{e}^{-{\rm i}p_{\alpha}\otimes x_{\alpha}}\text{e}^{{\rm i}p^{W}_{\beta}\otimes\hat{x}_{\beta}}.

If
\chi_{\mu}(l,p)=0
, then the twist operator for the realization
\hat{x}_{\mu}=x_{\alpha}\varphi_{\alpha\beta}(l,p)
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{-1}=\text{e}^{-{\rm i}p_{\alpha}\otimes x_{\alpha}}\text{e}^{{\rm i}p^{W}_{\beta}\otimes x_{\gamma}\varphi_{\gamma\beta}(l,p)}.

(4.2)

If
\chi_{\mu}(l,p)\neq 0
, then the twist
\mathcal{F}^{-1}
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{-1}=\text{e}^{-{\rm i}p_{\alpha}\otimes x_{\alpha}}\text{e}^{{\rm i}p^{W}_{\beta}\otimes x_{\gamma}\varphi_{\gamma\beta}(l,p)}\text{e}^{{\rm i}\mathcal{G}(p\otimes 1,1\otimes p)},

(4.3)

and the consistency check is

\displaystyle\hat{x}_{\mu}=m\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\triangleright\otimes 1)(x_{\mu}\otimes 1)=x_{\alpha}\varphi_{\alpha\mu}(l,p)+\chi_{\mu}(l,p).

Note that the following identity holds

\displaystyle{:}\text{e}^{{\rm i}(1\otimes x_{\alpha})(\Delta-\Delta_{0})p_{\alpha}}{:}=\text{e}^{-{\rm i}p_{\alpha}\otimes x_{\alpha}}\text{e}^{{\rm i}p_{\beta}^{W}\otimes x_{\gamma}\varphi_{\gamma\beta}(l,p)}.

The twists in this section, specially (
4.1
) and (
4.3
), are given in the Hopf algebroid approach
[

and generally do not satisfy the cocycle condition in the Hopf algebra sense

\hat{x}_{\mu}
close a Lie algebra
[

5.1
Right covariant realization of
\boldsymbol{\kappa}
-Minkowski space time

The right covariant realization of
\kappa
-Minkowski space is given by

\displaystyle\hat{x}_{\mu}=x_{\mu}-a_{\mu}x\cdot p,\qquad[\hat{x}_{\mu},\hat{x}_{\nu}]={\rm i}(a_{\mu}\hat{x}_{\nu}-a_{\nu}\hat{x}_{\mu}).

J_{\mu}(k,q)
for the right covariant realization satisfies the following differential equation

\displaystyle\frac{\text{d}J_{\mu}(tk,q)}{\text{d}t}=k_{\mu}-a\cdot kJ_{\mu}(tk,q),

with initial condition
J_{\mu}(0,q)=q_{\mu}
.
J_{\mu}^{(0)}(tk,q)=tk_{\mu}+q_{\mu}
for
a_{\mu}=0
. The solution of the above differential equation is

\displaystyle J_{\mu}(tk,q)=k_{\mu}\frac{1-\text{e}^{-ta\cdot k}}{a\cdot k}+q_{\mu}\text{e}^{-ta\cdot k}.

For
t=1
,

\displaystyle J_{\mu}(k,q)=k_{\mu}\frac{1-\text{e}^{-a\cdot k}}{a\cdot k}+q_{\mu}\text{e}^{-a\cdot k}.

For
k_{\mu}=0
,
J_{\mu}(0,q)=q_{\mu}
. For
q_{\mu}=0
,

\displaystyle J_{\mu}(k,0)=k_{\mu}\frac{1-\text{e}^{-a\cdot k}}{a\cdot k}\equiv K_{\mu}(k).

From here it follows that

\displaystyle a\cdot K=1-\text{e}^{-a\cdot k},\qquad a\cdot k=-\ln(1-a\cdot K(k)),\qquad\text{e}^{-a\cdot k}=1-a\cdot K.

Hence, the inverse of
K_{\mu}(k)
is given by

\displaystyle K_{\mu}^{-1}(k)=-k_{\mu}\frac{\ln(1-a\cdot k)}{a\cdot k}=k_{\mu}^{W}.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\mu}(k,q)=J_{\mu}\big(K^{-1}(k),q\big)=k_{\mu}+q_{\mu}(1-a\cdot k),

\displaystyle\Delta p_{\mu}=p_{\mu}\otimes 1+(1-a\cdot p)\otimes p_{\mu}.

In the Hopf algebroid approach, the twist corresponding to the right covariant realization is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\text{e}^{-{\rm i}p_{\beta}^{W}\otimes\hat{x}_{\beta}}\text{e}^{{\rm i}p_{\alpha}\otimes x_{\alpha}}=\text{e}^{\mathcal{A}}\text{e}^{\mathcal{B}},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=-{\rm i}p_{\alpha}^{W}\otimes x_{\alpha}+{\rm i}a\cdot p^{W}\otimes x\cdot p,\qquad\mathcal{B}={\rm i}p_{\alpha}\otimes x_{\alpha},\qquad[\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}]=\big(a\cdot p^{W}\otimes 1\big)\mathcal{B}.

Using a special case of the BCH formula, we get (see
[
48
, Appendix C]
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\exp\left(\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}\left(\frac{a\cdot p^{W}\otimes 1}{1\otimes 1-\text{e}^{-a\cdot p^{W}\otimes 1}}\right)\right)

Using the relation

\displaystyle p_{\mu}^{W}=-p_{\mu}\frac{\ln(1-a\cdot p)}{a\cdot p},

we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\text{e}^{-{\rm i}\ln(1-a\cdot p)\otimes D},\qquad D=x_{\alpha}p_{\alpha},\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Delta p_{\mu}=p_{\mu}\otimes 1+(1-a\cdot p)\otimes p_{\mu}.

(5.9)

We have shown that the twist in the Hopf algebroid approach corresponding to the right covariant realization of
\kappa
-Minkowski is identical to the Jordanian twist leading to the same right covariant realization and satisfying the cocycle condition in the Hopf algebra sense.

5.2
Left covariant realization of
\boldsymbol{\kappa}
-Minkowski space time

The left covariant realization of
\kappa
-Minkowski spacetime is given by

\displaystyle\hat{x}_{\mu}=x_{\mu}(1+a\cdot p),\qquad[\hat{x}_{\mu},\hat{x}_{\nu}]={\rm i}(a_{\mu}\hat{x}_{\nu}-a_{\nu}\hat{x}_{\mu}).

J_{\mu}(k,q)
for the left covariant realization satisfies the following differential equation

\displaystyle\frac{\text{d}J_{\mu}(tk,q)}{\text{d}t}=k_{\mu}(1+a\cdot J(tk,q)),

with initial condition
J_{\mu}(0,q)=q_{\mu}
.
J_{\mu}^{(0)}(tk,q)=tk_{\mu}+q_{\mu}
for
a_{\mu}=0
. The solution of the above differential equation is

\displaystyle J_{\mu}(tk,q)=k_{\mu}\frac{\text{e}^{ta\cdot k}-1}{a\cdot k}(1+a\cdot q)+q_{\mu}.

For
t=1
,

\displaystyle J_{\mu}(k,q)=k_{\mu}\frac{\text{e}^{a\cdot k}-1}{a\cdot k}(1+a\cdot q)+q_{\mu}.

For
q_{\mu}=0

\displaystyle J_{\mu}(k,0)=k_{\mu}\frac{\text{e}^{a\cdot k}-1}{a\cdot k}\equiv K_{\mu}(k).

It follows that
a\cdot K=\text{e}^{a\cdot k}-1
,
a\cdot k=\ln(1+a\cdot K(k))
, and the inverse of
K_{\mu}(k)
is given by

\displaystyle K^{-1}_{\mu}(k)=k_{\mu}\frac{\ln(1+a\cdot k)}{a\cdot k}=k_{\mu}^{W}.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\mu}(k,q)=J_{\mu}\big(K^{-1}(k),q\big)=k_{\mu}(1+a\cdot q)+q_{\mu},\qquad\Delta p_{\mu}=p_{\mu}\otimes(1+a\cdot p)+1\otimes p_{\mu}.

The twist in the Hopf algebroid approach corresponding to the left covariant realization is

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\text{e}^{-{\rm i}p_{\beta}^{W}\otimes\hat{x}_{\beta}}\text{e}^{{\rm i}p_{\alpha}\otimes x_{\alpha}}=\text{e}^{\mathcal{A}}\text{e}^{\mathcal{B}},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=-{\rm i}p_{\beta}^{W}\otimes x_{\beta}-{\rm i}p_{\beta}^{W}\otimes x_{\beta}a\cdot p,\qquad\mathcal{B}={\rm i}p_{\alpha}\otimes x_{\alpha},

\displaystyle[\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}]=-(\ln(1+a\cdot p)\otimes 1)\mathcal{B}=-\big(a\cdot p^{W}\otimes 1\big)\mathcal{B}.

Using a special case of the BCH formula we get
\mathcal{F}
(see
[
48
, Appendix C]
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\exp\left(\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}\left(\frac{-a\cdot p^{W}\otimes 1}{1\otimes 1-\text{e}^{a\cdot p^{W}\otimes 1}}\right)\right).

Using

\displaystyle p^{W}_{\mu}=p_{\mu}\frac{\ln(1+a\cdot p)}{a\cdot p},\qquad a\cdot p^{W}=\ln(1+a\cdot p),

we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\text{e}^{-{\rm i}p_{\beta}^{W}\otimes x_{\beta}a\cdot p}=\text{e}^{-{\rm i}a_{\alpha}p_{\beta}^{W}\otimes L_{\beta\alpha}},\qquad L_{\beta\alpha}=x_{\beta}p_{\alpha},\qquad\Delta p_{\mu}=p_{\mu}\otimes(1+a\cdot p)+1\otimes p_{\mu}.

We have shown that the twist in the Hopf algebroid approach corresponding to the left covariant realization of the
\kappa
-Minkowski spacetime is different from the Jordanian twist leading to the same left covariant realization which satisfies the cocycle condition in the Hopf algebra sense,
\mathcal{F}=\exp(-{\rm i}D\otimes\ln(1+a\cdot p))

\mathcal{F}=\exp(-{\rm i}\ln(1-a\cdot p)\otimes D)

6
Quadratic deformations of quantum phase space

In
[
117
]
, deformed Heisenberg algebras were constructed as examples of NC structures and the framework for higher dimensional NC spaces based on quantum groups was studied. Furthermore, quadratic deformations of the Minkowski space from twisted Poincaré symmetries were constructed in
[
67
]
. The construction was based on the twist

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\exp\left(\frac{{\rm i}}{2}\Theta^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}M_{\alpha\beta}\wedge M_{\gamma\delta}\right),

where
M_{\alpha\beta}
are Lorentz generators and the
r
-matrix is given with
r=\frac{1}{2}\Theta_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}M_{\mu\nu}\wedge M_{\rho\sigma}
. Quadratic deformations of the Galilei group and the Newton equation for classical space were considered in
[
31
]
.

Generally, quadratic algebras, i.e., quadratic deformations, can be defined with the following commutation relations

\displaystyle[\hat{x}_{\mu},\hat{x}_{\nu}]=\Theta^{\mu\nu\gamma\delta}\hat{x}_{\gamma}\hat{x}_{\delta},

where all Jacobi relations have to be satisfied and multiplication in the enveloping algebra
\mathcal{U}(\hat{x})
is associative. In this case the general realization of the NC coordinates is

\displaystyle\hat{x}_{\mu}=x_{\alpha}\varphi_{\alpha\mu}({\rm i}L_{\gamma\delta}),

where
L_{\gamma\delta}=x_{\gamma}p_{\delta}
generate the
\mathfrak{gl}(n)
algebra

\displaystyle[L_{\mu\nu},L_{\rho\sigma}]={\rm i}(\eta_{\mu\sigma}L_{\rho\nu}-\eta_{\rho\nu}L_{\mu\sigma}).

In the lowest order in
K_{\mu\nu\gamma\delta}
we get

\displaystyle\hat{x}_{\mu}=x_{\mu}+{\rm i}x_{\alpha}L_{\beta\gamma}K_{\mu\gamma\beta\alpha}+O\big(K^{2}\big)=x_{\mu}+{\rm i}K_{\mu\gamma\beta\alpha}x_{\alpha}x_{\beta}p_{\gamma}+O\big(K^{2}\big),

with
K_{\mu\nu\gamma\delta}-K_{\nu\mu\gamma\delta}=\Theta_{\mu\nu\gamma\delta}
.

We point out that Sections
3
and
4
cannot be applied to these quadratic deformations. The construction of such quadratic algebras can be performed using twist operators that, besides Lorentz generators, include dilatation operators
D_{\mu}
and
D

\displaystyle D=\sum_{\mu}D_{\mu}=\sum_{\mu}x_{\mu}p_{\mu},\qquad D_{\mu}=x_{\mu}p_{\mu}\quad\text{(no summation)},

and more generally
L_{\mu\nu}=x_{\mu}p_{\nu}
generating the
\mathfrak{gl}(n)
algebra. Here we consider a simple case.

6.1
Quadratic deformations of Minkowski space from dilatation

We consider the twist

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\exp\bigg(\sum_{\alpha,\beta}a_{\alpha\beta}D_{\alpha}\otimes D_{\beta}\bigg),\qquad a_{\beta\alpha}=-a_{\alpha\beta},

(6.1)

where

\displaystyle[D_{\alpha},D_{\beta}]=0,\qquad[D_{\alpha},p_{\beta}]={\rm i}p_{\alpha}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\quad\text{(no summation)},\qquad[D_{\alpha},x_{\beta}]=-{\rm i}x_{\alpha}\eta_{\alpha\beta},

and
p_{\alpha}
are momenta.

The action of
D_{\alpha}
in undeformed quantum phase space is defined as

\displaystyle D_{\alpha}\triangleright f(x)=-{\rm i}x_{\alpha}\frac{\partial f(x)}{\partial x_{\alpha}}.

The deformed quantum phase space is defined with

\displaystyle\hat{x}_{\alpha}\hat{x}_{\beta}=q_{\alpha\beta}\hat{x}_{\beta}\hat{x}_{\alpha},\qquad q_{\alpha\beta}=\exp(a_{\alpha\beta}-a_{\beta\alpha})=\exp(2a_{\alpha\beta}),\qquad[D_{\alpha},\hat{x}_{\beta}]=-{\rm i}\hat{x}_{\alpha}\eta_{\alpha\beta},

\displaystyle p_{\alpha}\hat{x}_{\beta}-\text{e}^{a_{\beta\alpha}}\hat{x}_{\beta}p_{\alpha}=-{\rm i}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\exp\bigg(\sum_{\gamma}{\rm i}a_{\beta\gamma}D_{\gamma}\bigg),\qquad c_{\alpha\beta}=\text{e}^{a_{\alpha\beta}}.

The realization for
\hat{x}_{\alpha}
is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\hat{x}_{\alpha}=x_{\alpha}\exp\bigg(\sum_{\beta}{\rm i}a_{\alpha\beta}D_{\beta}\bigg)=x_{\alpha}\phi_{\alpha},\qquad\phi_{\alpha}=\exp\bigg(\sum_{\beta}{\rm i}a_{\alpha\beta}D_{\beta}\bigg),\\
&\phi_{\alpha}\triangleright 1=1,\qquad\hat{x}_{\alpha}\triangleright 1=x_{\alpha}.\end{split}

Note that

\displaystyle x_{\alpha}\phi_{\alpha}=\exp(-a_{\alpha\alpha}\phi_{\beta}x_{\alpha}),\qquad x_{\alpha}\phi_{\beta}=\exp(-a_{\beta\alpha}\phi_{\beta}x_{\alpha}),

\displaystyle x_{\alpha}f({\rm i}D_{\alpha})=f({\rm i}D_{\alpha}-1)x_{\alpha},\qquad p_{\alpha}f({\rm i}D_{\alpha})=f({\rm i}D_{\alpha}+1)p_{\alpha}.

The coproducts are given by

\displaystyle\Delta D_{\alpha}=\mathcal{F}\Delta_{0}D_{\alpha}\mathcal{F}^{-1}=D_{\alpha}\otimes 1+1\otimes D_{\alpha}=\Delta_{0}D_{\alpha},\qquad\Delta\phi_{\alpha}=\phi_{\alpha}\otimes\phi_{\alpha}\!\quad\text{(no summation)},

\displaystyle\Delta p_{\alpha}=\mathcal{F}\Delta_{0}p_{\alpha}\mathcal{F}^{-1}=p_{\alpha}\otimes\phi_{\alpha}+\tilde{\phi}_{\alpha}\otimes p_{\alpha},

\displaystyle\tilde{\phi}_{\alpha}=\exp\bigg(\sum_{\alpha}{\rm i}a_{\beta\alpha}D_{\beta}\bigg),\qquad\phi_{\alpha}=\exp\bigg(\sum_{\beta}{\rm i}a_{\alpha\beta}D_{\beta}\bigg),

\displaystyle\Delta\tilde{\phi}_{\alpha}=\tilde{\phi}_{\alpha}\otimes\tilde{\phi}_{\alpha},\qquad\phi_{\alpha}\hat{x}_{\beta}=\text{e}^{a_{\alpha\beta}}\hat{x}_{\beta}\phi_{\alpha},\qquad[\phi_{\alpha},\hat{x}_{\beta}]=\big(\text{e}^{a_{\alpha\beta}}-1\big)\hat{x}_{\beta}\phi_{\alpha}.

The twist
\mathcal{F}
given in equation (
6.1
) is abelian and satisfies the cocycle condition. The star product is associative

\displaystyle x_{\alpha}*x_{\beta}=\hat{x}_{\alpha}\hat{x}_{\beta}\triangleright 1=\hat{x}_{\alpha}\triangleright x_{\beta}=\text{e}^{a_{\alpha\beta}}x_{\alpha}x_{\beta},

\displaystyle x_{\beta}*x_{\alpha}=\text{e}^{a_{\beta\alpha}}x_{\alpha}x_{\beta},\qquad x_{\alpha}*x_{\beta}=q_{\alpha\beta}x_{\beta}*x_{\alpha},

\displaystyle\hat{x}_{\alpha}=m\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\triangleright\otimes 1)(x_{\alpha}\otimes 1)=x_{\alpha}\exp\bigg(\sum_{\beta}a_{\alpha\beta}D_{\beta}\bigg),

\displaystyle(f*g)(x)=m\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\triangleright\otimes\triangleright)(f(x)\otimes g(x)).

For
a_{\beta\alpha}=-a_{\alpha\beta}
,
q_{\alpha\beta}=\exp(2a_{\alpha\beta})=(c_{\alpha\beta})^{2}
,
c_{\alpha\alpha}=q_{\alpha\alpha}=1
,
\tilde{\phi}_{\alpha}=(\phi_{\alpha})^{-1}
.

The

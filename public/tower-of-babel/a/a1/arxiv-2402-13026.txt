Dispersed Dyck paths revisited
Helmut Prodinger

Abstract
. Dispersed Dyck paths are Dyck paths, with possible flat steps on level 0. We revisit and augment questions about them from the Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences, in a systematic way that uses generating functions and the kernel method.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
05A15

1.
Introduction

Dispersed Dyck paths consist of up-steps
U=(1,1)
and down-steps
D=(1,-1)
, never go below the
x
-axis, and can have horizontal steps on the
x
-axis,

(n,0)\to(n+1,0)
. According to the OEIS
[
7
]
, it seems that they were invented/suggested by Emeric Deutsch.
All sequences that are explicitly cited are from
[
7
]
, using the local identifiers
A\#\#\#\#\#\#
.

As in various examples discussed in the past
[
3
,
4
,
6
]
we will use the kernel method to set up appropriate
generating functions. There are three (or four) bivariate (or trivariate) generating functions
F(u),G(u),H(u)
(also depending on
z
) related to the nature of the last step of the
prefix of a dispersed Dyck path. To solve the system, a ‘bad’ factor
u-r_{2}
must be divided out from numerator and denominator, after which one can plug in
u=0

and identify the unknown quantities
f_{1}=F(0)
, etc.

Various questions (from the encyclopedia of integer sequences
[
7
]
) about such paths will be revisited; our method is fairly automatic and purely refers to generating functions. To be more specific, these include 1-ascents, 1-descents, valleys on level 0, occurrences of
UUDD
.
Also, we obtain expression for paths that do not have to go back to the
x
-axis, rather finish at a level
j
, or, more generally, on
any
level. This is either achieved by looking at the coefficient of
u^{j}
, say, or, setting
u=1
. The generating functions of interest usually have 3 variables:
z
for the length (number of steps),
u
for the final height, and
t
for an additional parameter of interest.

2.
Counting 1-ascents

A 1-ascent is an ascent consisting of exactly 1 up step. The paper
[
2
]
contains some analysis, but without generating functions. The paper
[
1
]
discusses

d
-ascents for
d\geq 2
as well, but the quadratic equations that are so common in the context of Dyck paths, are then of higher order, and the results are consequently of an
asymptotic nature.

We distinguish 3 states, together with the current level. Down-steps are unproblematic, but when after them an up-step arrives, it might be the only one or further up-steps follow.
A graph describes all the possible scenarios, as in
[
4
]
. It has 3 layers of states, and
sequences
f_{i}
,
i\geq 1
,
g_{i}
,
h_{i}
,
i\geq 0
, in that order. These quantities all depend on the variable
z
and
describe generating functions of paths leading to a particular state. Since on level 0, flat (horizontal) steps are also allowed, the quantities
f_{0}
,
g_{0}
, and
h_{0}
are somewhat special and will be treated as parameters. Compare
[
5
]
for this technique. We will deal with trivariate generating functions

F(u)=\sum_{i\geq 1}f_{i}u^{i-1},\ G(u)=\sum_{i\geq 1}g_{i}u^{i-1},\ H(u)=\sum_{i\geq 1}h_{i}u^{i-1},

the other variables
z
(counting the length) and
t
(counting the 1-ascents) are not explicitly mentioned.

Figure 1.
Three layers of states, labelled
f,g,h
, in that order. The state
f_{0}
is responsible for dispersed Dyck paths, and all others to prefixes of them.

The recursions can be read off, by considering the last step made,

\displaystyle f_{i}

\displaystyle=zf_{i+1}+ztg_{i+1}+zh_{i+1},\ i\geq 1,\ f_{0}=1+zf_{0}+zf_{1}+ztg_{1},

\displaystyle g_{i+1}

\displaystyle=zf_{i},\ i\geq 1,\ g_{1}=zf_{0},

\displaystyle h_{i+1}

\displaystyle=zg_{i}+zh_{i},\ i\geq 0.

Translating these into the trivariate generating functions, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 1}u^{i}f_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 1}u^{i}zf_{i+1}+\sum_{i\geq 1}u^{i}ztg_{i+1}+\sum_{i\geq 1}u^{i}zh_{i+1},

\displaystyle uF(u)

\displaystyle=zF(u)-zf_{1}+ztG(u)-ztg_{1}+zH(u),

G(u)-g_{1}=zuF(u),\quad H(u)=zuG(u)+zuH(u).

The system can be solved, but still depends on the initial values
f_{1},g_{1},h_{1}
:

\displaystyle F(u)

\displaystyle={\frac{z(-f_{1}+zuf_{1}+zug_{1})}{-{z}^{3}{u}^{2}+u-z{u}^{2}-z+{z}^{2}u-{z}^{2}tu+{z}^{3}t{u}^{2}}},

\displaystyle G(u)

\displaystyle={\frac{-{z}^{2}uf_{1}-{z}^{2}tug_{1}-zg_{1}+ug_{1}+{z}^{3}{u}^{2}f_{1}+{z}^{3}t{u}^{2}g_{1}+{z}^{2}ug_{1}-z{u}^{2}g_{1}}{-{z}^{3}{u}^{2}+u-z{u}^{2}-z+{z}^{2}u-{z}^{2}tu+{z}^{3}t{u}^{2}}},

\displaystyle H(u)

\displaystyle=-{\frac{zu({z}^{2}uf_{1}+{z}^{2}tug_{1}+zg_{1}-ug_{1})}{-{z}^{3}{u}^{2}+u-z{u}^{2}-z+{z}^{2}u-{z}^{2}tu+{z}^{3}t{u}^{2}}}.

The naive approach would be to plug in
u=0
and identity the initial values, but this doesn’t work, and requires some preparations.
The denominator will be factored,

-{z}^{3}{u}^{2}+u-z{u}^{2}-z+{z}^{2}u-{z}^{2}tu+{z}^{3}t{u}^{2}=z(-z^{2}-1+z^{2}t)(u-r_{1})(u-r_{2})

with

r_{1}=\frac{1+z^{2}(1-t)+W}{2z(1+z^{2}(1-t))},\quad r_{2}=\frac{1+z^{2}(1-t)-W}{2z(1+z^{2}(1-t))}

and the abbreviation

W:=\sqrt{1-2(t+1)z^{2}-(t+3)(1-t)z^{4}}.

Dividing out the factor
u-r_{2}
from numerator and denominator (‘kernel method’), we find

\displaystyle F(u)

\displaystyle={\frac{{z}^{2}(f_{1}+g_{1})}{(-{z}^{2}-1+{z}^{2}t)(r_{2}z+zu-1)}},

\displaystyle G(u)

\displaystyle={\frac{r_{2}{z}^{3}f_{1}+r_{2}{z}^{3}tg_{1}-r_{2}zg_{1}-{z}^{2}f_{1}-{z}^{2}tg_{1}+g_{1}+{z}^{2}g_{1}+u{z}^{3}f_{1}+u{z}^{3}tg_{1}-zug_{1}}{(-{z}^{2}-1+{z}^{2}t)(r_{2}z+zu-1)}},

\displaystyle H(u)

\displaystyle=-{\frac{z(r_{2}{z}^{2}f_{1}+r_{2}{z}^{2}tg_{1}-r_{2}g_{1}+{z}^{2}uf_{1}+{z}^{2}tug_{1}+zg_{1}-ug_{1})}{(-{z}^{2}-1+{z}^{2}t)(r_{2}z+zu-1)}},

and now
u=0
is possible, with

\displaystyle f_{1}

\displaystyle={\frac{{z}^{2}(f_{1}+g_{1})}{(-{z}^{2}-1+{z}^{2}t)(r_{2}z-1)}},\quad g_{1}=zf_{0},\quad h_{1}=0.

But we have

f_{0}=1+zf_{0}+zf_{1}+ztg_{1},

and both,
f_{1}
and
g_{1}
can be expressed in terms of
f_{0}
:

f_{1}={\frac{{z}^{2}g_{1}}{1-{z}^{2}t-r_{2}z-r_{2}{z}^{3}+r_{2}{z}^{3}t}}.

The ultimate solution is now

f_{0}={\frac{-1+2z-{z}^{2}+{z}^{2}t+W}{2z({z}^{2}+1-2z-{z}^{3}+{z}^{3}t-{z}^{2}t)}},

with the series expansion

f_{0}=1+z+(t+1){z}^{2}+(2t+1){z}^{3}+({t}^{2}+3t+2){z}^{4}+(3{t}^{2}+4t+3){z}^{5}+({t}^{3}+6{t}^{2}+8t+5){z}^{6}+\cdots.

For
t=0
we find the enumeration of dispersed Dyck paths without 1-ascents (A191385)

\displaystyle f_{0}\big|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{-1+2z-z^{2}+\sqrt{1-2z^{2}-3z^{4}}}{2z(1-2z+z^{2}-z^{3})}

\displaystyle=1+z+z^{2}+z^{3}+2z^{4}+3z^{5}+5z^{6}+7z^{7}+12z^{8}+18z^{9}+31z^{10}+47z^{11}+81z^{12}+\cdots.

For
t=1
, we find

f_{0}\big|_{t=1}=-\frac{1}{2z}+\frac{\sqrt{1-4z^{2}}}{2z(1-2z)}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\binom{2n}{n}z^{2n}+\sum_{n\geq 0}\binom{2n+1}{n}z^{2n+1},

which is the enumeration of dispersed Dyck paths of length
n
:
\binom{n}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}
.
The number of 1-ascents in paths of length 5 can be deduced from
(3{t}^{2}+4t+3)
: it is
3\cdot 2+4
, and in general, we must differentate

f_{0}
w.r.t.
t
, followed by
t=1
.

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial z}\bigg|_{t=1}

\displaystyle=\frac{z^{2}(1-4z^{2}+\sqrt{1-4z^{2}})}{2(1-2z)(1-4z^{2})}

\displaystyle=\frac{z^{2}}{2(1-2z)}+\frac{z^{2}}{2(1-4z^{2})^{3/2}}+\frac{z^{3}}{(1-4z^{2})^{3/2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 2}2^{n-3}z^{n}+\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(2n-1)!}{2(n-1)!(n-1)!}z^{2n}+\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(2n-1)!}{(n-1)!(n-1)!}z^{2n+1}.

The coefficients of
z^{n}
form the sequene A045621.
Furthermore, we get

\displaystyle F(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{z^{2}r_{2}}{1-zr_{2}-zu}f_{0},

\displaystyle G(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{z(1-zu)(1-zr_{2})}{1-zr_{2}-zu}f_{0},

\displaystyle H(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{uz^{2}(1-zr_{2})}{1-zr_{2}-zu}f_{0}.

From this, it is easy to find
f_{j},g_{j},h_{j}
since we only have to expand
1/(1-zr_{2}-zu)
in powers of
u
, which is basically a geometric series. We will not write this out.
However, we will sum
all
these quantities, as it describes all dispersed Dyck paths with open end (partial paths, sometimes called meander).
We have to be careful and, when a path ends in a state from the second layer (‘g’), the last up step is a 1-ascent, and we need to attach an extra factor
t
.
The resulting formula is surprisingly simple:

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Big(f_{0}+F(1)+tG(1)+H(1)\Big)\bigg|_{t=1}=\frac{z(1-z)^{2}}{(1-2z)^{2}}=z+\sum_{n\geq 2}(n+2)2^{n-3}z^{n}.

3.
Counting 1-descents

In this section, the number of 1-descents will be counted. The definition is similar; a down-step rendered by up-steps or standing at the end of the dispersed Dyck path.
The same letters as in the previous section will be used, but now with a different meaning. The figure is again self-explanatory:

Figure 2.
Three layers of states, labelled
f,g,h
, in that order.

Here are the relevant recursions:

\displaystyle f_{i+1}

\displaystyle=zf_{i}+ztg_{i}+zh_{i},\ i\geq 1,\ f_{0}=\frac{1}{1-z},

\displaystyle g_{i}

\displaystyle=zf_{i+1},\ i\geq 1,\ g_{0}=zg_{0}+zf_{1},\ g_{0}=\frac{z}{1-z}f_{1}

\displaystyle h_{i}

\displaystyle=zg_{i+1}+zh_{i+1},\ i\geq 1,\ h_{0}=zh_{0}+zg_{1}+zh_{1},\ h_{0}=\frac{z}{1-z}(g_{1}+h_{1}),

and

\displaystyle f_{1}

\displaystyle=zf_{0}+ztg_{0}+zh_{0}=\frac{z}{1-z}+\frac{z^{2}t}{1-z}f_{1}+\frac{z^{2}}{1-z}(g_{1}+h_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{z(1+zg_{1}+zh_{1})}{1-z-z^{2}t}.

So the kernel method will give us
g_{1}
and
h_{1}
, and
f_{1}
follows. The recursions will be
translated into trivariate generating functions, as before:

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 1}u^{i}f_{i+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 1}u^{i}zf_{i}+\sum_{i\geq 1}u^{i}ztg_{i}+\sum_{i\geq 1}u^{i}zh_{i},

\displaystyle F(u)-f_{1}

\displaystyle=uzF(u)+uztG(u)+uzH(u);

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 1}u^{i}g_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 1}u^{i}zf_{i+1},

\displaystyle uG(u)

\displaystyle=zF(u)-zf_{1};

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 1}u^{i}h_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 1}u^{i}zg_{i+1}+\sum_{i\geq 1}u^{i}zh_{i+1},

\displaystyle uH(u)

\displaystyle=zG(u)-zg_{1}+zH(u)-zh_{1}.

We do not write the solutions for
F(u),G(u),H(u)
, only the relevant denominator

z^{3}-u+u^{2}z+uz^{2}t+z-uz^{2}-z^{3}t=z(u-r_{1})(u-r_{2})

with

r_{1}=\frac{1+z^{2}-z^{2}t+W}{2z},\quad r_{2}=\frac{1+z^{2}-z^{2}t-W}{2z},

and

W=\sqrt{1-2z^{2}t-2z^{2}+z^{4}t^{2}+2z^{4}t-3z^{4}}.

Then we divide out the factor
u-r_{2}
in the usual way, and get, after setting
u=0
,

g_{1}=\frac{zr_{2}}{1+z^{2}-z^{2}t}f_{1},\quad g_{1}+h_{1}=\frac{r_{2}-z}{z}f_{1},

f_{1}=\frac{z}{1-z+z^{2}-z^{2}t-zr_{2}}.

This provides also the values
f_{0},g_{0},h_{0}
, and

f_{0}+tg_{0}+h_{0}=\frac{1-r_{2}}{1-2z+z^{2}-z^{3}-z^{2}t+z^{3}t},

which is the generating function of dispersed Dyck paths returning to the 0-level. Again, to count the contributions of the 1-descents, we compute

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial z}(f_{0}+tg_{0}+h_{0})\Big|_{t=1}

\displaystyle=\frac{z^{2}(1-4z^{2}+\sqrt{1-4z^{2}})}{2(1-2z)(1-4z^{2})}

\displaystyle=\frac{z^{2}}{2(1-2z)}+\frac{z^{2}}{2(1-4z^{2})^{3/2}}+\frac{z^{3}}{(1-4z^{2})^{3/2}}

as before. This is natural, as reading from right to left turns 1-upsteps to 1-downsteps, and vice versa. Considering
all
paths, regardless where
they end,

\frac{\partial}{\partial z}(f_{0}+F(1)+tg_{0}+tG(1)+h_{0}+H(1))\Big|_{t=1}=\frac{z^{2}}{2(1-2z)^{2}}+\frac{z^{2}\sqrt{1-4z^{2}}}{2(1-2z)^{2}},

which is surprisingly simple, with a simple series expansion,

\sum_{n\geq 2}(n-1)2^{n-3}z^{n}+\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{2(2n-2)!}{(n-1)!(n-1)!}z^{2n}+\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(2n-2)!(4n-3)}{2(n-1)!(n-1)!}z^{2n+1}.

4.
Counting valleys on level 0

We do now exactly what the title of the section says;

Figure 3.
Two layers of states, labelled
f,g
, in that order.

Here are the usual recursions,

f_{0}=1+zf_{0}+zg_{0},\ g_{0}=zf_{1},\ f_{1}=ztg_{0}+zf_{0}+zf_{2},\ f_{i}=zf_{i-1}+zf_{i+1},\ i\geq 2.

f_{0}=\frac{1}{1-z}+\frac{z}{1-z}g_{0}=\frac{1}{1-z}+\frac{z^{2}}{1-z}f_{1},\quad tf_{0}-t-ztf_{0}=ztg_{0}

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 2}u^{i-1}f_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 2}u^{i-1}zf_{i-1}+\sum_{i\geq 2}u^{i-1}zf_{i+1}

\displaystyle F(u)-f_{1}

\displaystyle=uzF(u)+\frac{z}{u}(F(u)-f_{1})-zf_{2},

\displaystyle F(u)

\displaystyle=uzF(u)+\frac{z}{u}(F(u)-f_{1})+ztg_{0}+zf_{0},

\displaystyle F(u)

\displaystyle=uzF(u)+\frac{z}{u}(F(u)-f_{1})+z^{2}tf_{1}+zf_{0}.

We have
f_{0}=\frac{1+z^{2}f_{1}}{1-z}
, and from the kernel method, after dividing out

u-r_{2},\quad\text{with}\quad r_{2}:=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4z^{2}}}{2z},

f_{1}=\frac{z}{1-z-{z}^{2}t+{z}^{3}(t-1)+z(z-1)r_{2}}.

Then,

\displaystyle f_{1}\big|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{2-3z+z\sqrt{1-z^{2}}}

\displaystyle=1+z+z^{2}+2z^{3}+3z^{4}+5z^{5}+8z^{6}+14z^{7}+23z^{8}+41z^{9}+69z^{10}+125z^{11}+\cdots

which is sequence A191388 (no valleys on level 0). Furthermore

F(u)=\frac{f_{1}}{1-ur_{2}}\Longrightarrow[u^{j-1}]F(u)=f_{j}=f_{1}r_{2}^{j-1}.

Finally,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial t}\bigg|_{t=1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-3z^{2}+(z^{2}-1)\sqrt{1-4z^{2}}}{2z(1-2z)}

\displaystyle=z^{5}+2z^{6}+7z^{7}+14z^{8}+37z^{9}+74z^{10}+176z^{11}+352z^{12}+\cdots,

which is sequence A191389 (number of valleys on level 0). By setting
u=1
in
g_{0}+F(u)
, we can address the number of such
partial dispersed Dyck paths according to no valleys resp. number of valleys. Since these formulæ are easy to obtain and not
too attractive, we do not display them here.

5.
Counting occurrences of
UUDD

Figure 4.
Four layers of states, labelled
f,g,h,k
, in that order.

The recursions are

\displaystyle f_{i}

\displaystyle=zf_{i+1}+zg_{i+1}+ztk_{i+1},\ i\geq 1,\ f_{0}=1+zf_{0}+zf_{1}+zg_{1}+ztk_{1},

\displaystyle g_{i+1}

\displaystyle=zf_{i}+zk_{i},\ i\geq 1,\ g_{1}=zf_{0}

\displaystyle h_{i+1}

\displaystyle=zg_{i}+zh_{i},\ i\geq 2,\ h_{2}=zg_{1},

\displaystyle k_{i}

\displaystyle=zh_{i+1},\ i\geq 1,\ k_{1}=zh_{2}=z^{2}g_{1}.

The following generating functions will be used:

\displaystyle F(u)=\sum_{i\geq 1}f_{i}u^{i-1},\ G(u)=\sum_{i\geq 1}g_{i}u^{i-1},\ H(u)=\sum_{i\geq 2}h_{i}u^{i-2},\ K(u)=\sum_{i\geq 1}k_{i}u^{i-1}.

Summing and simplifying,

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 1}u^{i}f_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 1}u^{i}zf_{i+1}+\sum_{i\geq 1}u^{i}zg_{i+1}+\sum_{i\geq 1}u^{i}ztk_{i+1},

\displaystyle uF(u)

\displaystyle=zF(u)-zf_{1}+zG(u)-zg_{1}+ztK(u)-ztk_{1},

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 1}u^{i}g_{i+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 1}u^{i}zf_{i}+\sum_{i\geq 1}u^{i}zk_{i},

\displaystyle G(u)-g_{1}

\displaystyle=zuF(u)+zuK(u),

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 2}u^{i-1}h_{i+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 2}u^{i-1}zg_{i}+\sum_{i\geq 2}u^{i-1}zh_{i},

\displaystyle H(u)-h_{2}

\displaystyle=zG(u)-zg_{1}+zuH(u),\ H(u)=\frac{z}{1-zu}G(u),

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 1}u^{i-1}k_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 1}u^{i-1}zh_{i+1},

\displaystyle K(u)

\displaystyle=zH(u).

It is beneficial to reduce the system to just one equation, for
F(u)
. Note also that

f_{1}=-\frac{1+zf_{0}+z^{2}f_{0}+z^{4}tf_{0}-f_{0}}{z},\ g_{1}=zf_{0},\ h_{2}=z^{2}f_{0},\ k_{1}=z^{3}f_{0}.

Then

F(u)=-{\frac{-{z}^{3}u-{z}^{4}f_{0}u-zu-{z}^{2}f_{0}u+zuf_{0}+1+zf_{0}+{z}^{2}f_{0}+{z}^{4}tf_{0}-f_{0}}{-{z}^{4}u+z+{z}^{4}tu+z{u}^{2}-u}}.

Dividing out, as part of the kernel method,

u-r_{2},\quad\text{with}\quad r_{2}={\frac{1+{z}^{4}-{z}^{4}t-\sqrt{{z}^{8}-2{z}^{8}t+2{z}^{4}+{z}^{8}{t}^{2}-2{z}^{4}t+1-4{z}^{2}}}{2z}}

and setting
u=0
leads to

-\frac{1+zf_{0}+z^{2}f_{0}+z^{4}tf_{0}-f_{0}}{z}=f_{1}={\frac{z\left({z}^{2}+{z}^{3}f_{0}+1+zf_{0}-f_{0}\right)}{-{z}^{4}+{z}^{4}t-1+r_{2}z}}

from which
f_{0}
can be computed:

\displaystyle f_{0}

\displaystyle={\frac{-{z}^{4}-1+{z}^{4}t+2z+\sqrt{z^{8}-2z^{8}t+2z^{4}+z^{8}t^{2}-2z^{4}t+1-4z^{2}}}{2z\left(-{z}^{4}t+1-2z+{z}^{4}\right)}}

\displaystyle=1+z+2z^{2}+3z^{3}+(t+5)z^{4}+(8+2t)z^{5}+(14+6t)z^{6}+(23+12t)z^{7}+\cdots

The special cases are as follows:

\displaystyle f_{0}\big|_{t=0}

\displaystyle={\frac{2z+\sqrt{z^{8}+2z^{4}+1-4z^{2}}-{z}^{4}-1}{2z\left(+1-2z+{z}^{4}\right)}}

\displaystyle=1+z+2z^{2}+3z^{3}+5z^{4}+8z^{5}+14z^{6}+23z^{7}+41z^{8}+69z^{9}+124z^{10}+\cdots

which enumerates dispersed Dyck paths without
UUDD
(sequence A191794) and

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial t}\bigg|_{t=1}

\displaystyle=\frac{z^{4}}{(1-2z)\sqrt{1-4z^{2}}}

\displaystyle=z^{4}+2z^{5}+6z^{6}+12z^{7}+30z^{8}+60z^{9}+140z^{10}+280z^{11}+630z^{12}+\cdots,

which enumerates the number of occurrences of
UUDD
in dispersed Dyck paths (sequence A100071).

Of course, dispersed Dyck paths with arbitrary endpoint can also be discussed using

f_{0}+F(1)+G(1)+H(1)+K(1)
, but we are not going to display any formula; they are easy to obtain.

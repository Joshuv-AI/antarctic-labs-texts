Topology of real algebraic curves near the non-singular tropical limit
Cédric Le Texier

Abstract
. In the 1990’s, Itenberg and Haas studied the relations between combinatorial data in Viro’s patchworking and the topology of the resulting non-singular real algebraic curves in the projective plane. Using recent results from Renaudineau and Shaw on real algebraic curves near the non-singular tropical limit, we continue the study of Itenberg and Haas inside any non-singular projective toric surface. We give new Haas’ like criteria for patchworking non-singular real algebraic curves with prescribed number of connected components, in terms of twisted edges on a non-singular tropical curve. We then obtain several sufficient conditions for patchworking non-singular real algebraic curves with only ovals in their real part. One of these sufficient conditions does not depend on the ambient toric surface. In that case, we count the number of even and odd ovals of those curves in terms of the dual subdivision, and construct some new counter-examples to Ragsdale’s conjecture.

1.
Introduction

The aim of this article is to study the topology of real algebraic curves near the non-singular tropical limit, in terms of the induced combinatorial data on the tropical limit, continuing the work started by Itenberg
[
Ite95
]
and Haas
[
Haa97
]
.
A
non-singular real algebraic curve

\mathcal{C}
in a non-singular projective toric surface
\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}
is a non-singular algebraic curve with set of real points
\mathcal{C}({\mathbb{R}})=\mathcal{C}\cap\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}({\mathbb{R}})
, for
\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}({\mathbb{R}})
non-empty defined by the construction from Gelfand, Kapranov and Zelevinsky
[
GKZ14
, Theorem 11.5.4]
.

One of the first results motivating the study of the topology of non-singular real algebraic curves is the
Harnack-Klein inequality

[
Har76
]
,
[
Kle73
]
, which states that the number of connected components of the real part
\mathcal{C}({\mathbb{R}})
of a non-singular real algebraic curve
\mathcal{C}
of genus
g
is less than or equal to
g+1
.
Moreover, Harnack gave a construction of non-singular real algebraic curves in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
reaching this bound for any degree
d

[
Har76
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a non-singular real algebraic curve of genus
g
.
We say that
\mathcal{C}
is a
maximal
curve (or
M
-curve
) if its real part
\mathcal{C}({\mathbb{R}})
has
g+1
connected components.
More generally, we say that
\mathcal{C}
is a
(M-r)
-curve
if its real part
\mathcal{C}({\mathbb{R}})
has
g+1-r
connected components.

Haas (
[
Haa97
]
) gave a necessary and sufficient criterion for a real algebraic curve near the non-singular tropical limit to be maximal, using Viro’s combinatorial patchworking
[
Vir80
]
,
[
Vir84
]
,
[
Vir01
]
.
This criterion can be stated in terms of
twisted edges
on a tropical curve (see
Definition

3.9
and
Figure

3(c)
).
Renaudineau and Shaw (
[
RS21
]
) introduced a tool allowing to compute the number of connected components of a real algebraic curve near the non-singular tropical limit in terms of twisted edges on the tropical limit, and recovered Haas result.
Using these results, we introduce a new criterion for a real algebraic curve near the non-singular tropical limit to be a
(M-1)
-curve.
In the following, a
primitive cycle
is a cycle on a tropical curve bounding a connected component of the complement, and an
exposed edge
is an edge of a tropical curve lying in the boundary of an unbounded connected component of the complement.

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
4.16
)
.

Let
C
be a non-singular tropical curve in a non-singular projective tropical toric surface
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}
, and let
T
be an admissible set of twisted edges on
C
.
A real algebraic curve
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}
near the non-singular tropical limit
C
and inducing the set of twisted edges
T
is a
(M-1)
-curve if and only if the dual subdivision
\Delta_{C}
of
C
contains a complete subgraph
K_{n}
on
1\leq n\leq 4
vertices satisfying the following conditions.

(1)

The non-exposed twisted edges are those dual to the edges of
K_{n}
.

(2)

Every primitive cycle
\gamma
in
C
dual to a vertex of
K_{n}
has an odd number of twisted edges.

(3)

Every primitive cycle
\gamma
in
C
not dual to a vertex of
K_{n}
has an even number of twisted edges.

We can also use
Theorem

1.2
in order to construct many examples of
(M-r)
curves for any
r\leq g
, see
Corollary

4.21
.
Indeed, let
C
be a non-singular tropical curve given as the gluing of
r
non-singular tropical curves
C_{1},\ldots,C_{r}
at some unbounded edges, such that the set of twisted edges
T_{i}
on each
C_{i}
satisfy
Theorem

1.2
.
Then a real algebraic curve
\mathcal{C}
near the non-singular tropical limit
C
with set of twisted edges
T_{1}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup T_{r}
will be a
(M-r)
curve.
However, we would not be able to construct
dividing curves
by this process.

Definition 1.3
.

A non-singular real algebraic curve
\mathcal{C}
is said to be
dividing
if
\mathcal{C}({\mathbb{C}})\backslash\mathcal{C}({\mathbb{R}})
is disconnected.

Since the genus of a Riemann surface
S
is given as the maximal number of non-intersecting loops on
S
which does not disconnect
S
, every maximal curve is dividing.
Furthermore, every dividing curve of genus
g
is a
(M-2s)
-curve, for
0\leq s\leq\left\lfloor\frac{g}{2}\right\rfloor
.
In the process of proving the criterion for maximal curves, Haas obtained a necessary and sufficient condition to construct dividing curves near the non-singular tropical limit
[
Haa97
]
, again via Viro’s combinatorial patchworking.
Using Haas dividing criterion in addition to the results of
[
RS21
]
, we give a new criterion for a real algebraic curve near the non-singular tropical limit to be a
(M-2)
-dividing curve, in terms of twisted edges on the tropical limit.

Theorem 1.4

(Theorem
4.22
)
.

Let
C
be a non-singular tropical curve in a non-singular projective tropical toric surface
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}
, and let
T
be an admissible dividing set of twisted edges on
C
.
A real algebraic curve
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}
near the non-singular tropical limit
C
and inducing the set of twisted edges
T
is a dividing
(M-2)
curve if and only if the subgraph of the dual subdivision
\Delta_{C}
dual to the set of non-exposed twisted edges is either a complete planar bipartite or a complete planar tripartite graph.

We can also use
Theorem

1.4
in order to construct many examples of dividing
(M-2s)
curves for any
s\leq\left\lfloor\frac{g}{2}\right\rfloor
, see
Corollary

4.25
, in a similar way as we construct
(M-r)
curves via
Theorem

1.2
.
The next natural question to ask would be about the possible arrangements of connected components in the real part of real algebraic curves near the non-singular tropical limit.

In its 16th problem, Hilbert
[
Hil00
]
asked what are the realisable
isotopy types
for a non-singular real algebraic curve
\mathcal{C}
of degree 6 in the projective plane
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, or said otherwise the realisable homeomorphism types for the pair
(\mathbb{P}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}),\mathcal{C}({\mathbb{R}}))
.
This problem and its generalisation to higher degrees has been extensively studied during the 20th century
[
Hil91
]
,
[
Rag06
]
,
[
Pet38
]
,
[
Gud69
]
,
[
Rok78
]
,
[
Vir80
]
,
[
Ite93
]
,
[
Ore02
]
.
In the projective plane case, the classification problem reduces to the realisable arrangements of
ovals
and
pseudo-lines
.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a non-singular real algebraic curve in a non-singular projective toric surface
\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}
.
A connected component
C_{0}
of
\mathcal{C}({\mathbb{R}})
is called an
oval
if
C_{0}
divides
\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}({\mathbb{R}})
into two connected components.
We say that an oval
C_{0}
of
\mathcal{C}({\mathbb{R}})
is
even
if
C_{0}
lies inside an even number of ovals of
\mathcal{C}({\mathbb{R}})
, and we say that
C_{0}
is
odd
otherwise.

For
\mathcal{C}
a non-singular real algebraic curve of even degree in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, the connected components of the real part
\mathcal{C}({\mathbb{R}})
are all ovals.
In general, for
\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}
a non-singular projective toric surface distinct from
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, we need stronger restrictions in order to have only ovals in the real part of a non-singular real algebraic curve.
For
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}
a real algebraic curve near the non-singular tropical limit
C
with set of twisted edges
T
, we obtain that if every twisted edge in
T
is non-exposed, the real part
\mathcal{C}({\mathbb{R}})
consists only of ovals, see
Proposition

5.10
.
In particular, that condition does not depend on the ambient non-singular projective toric surface
\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}
.

In the dividing case, we can partition a set of twisted edges into
multi-bridges
, see
Definition

5.1
and
Proposition

5.2
.
A multi-bridge
B
on a non-singular tropical curve
C
is a twist-admissible set of bounded edges of
C
which are parallel modulo 2, such that the graph
C\backslash B
is disconnected and for every proper subset
B^{\prime}\subset B
, the graph
C\backslash B
is connected.
These multi-bridges form a spanning family of the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-vector space
\Div(C)
of dividing configuration of twists,
Proposition

5.2
.
If we restrict to configuration of twists with only non-exposed twisted edges, we obtain a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-vector subspace
\Circuit(C)\subset\Div(C)
generated by
circuits
(see
Definition

5.1
), which are multi-bridges with only non-exposed edges.
An element
T
of
\Circuit(C)
satisfies
Proposition

5.10
, hence we want to count the number of even and odd ovals of real algebraic curves near the non-singular tropical limit
C
with set of twisted edges belonging to
\Circuit(C)
.

Whenever a partition of a set of twisted edges
T
contains a circuit
T^{\prime}
such that its dual
(T^{\prime})^{\vee}\subset\Delta_{C}
contains an integer point of the dual subdivision
\Delta_{C}
of
C
with both coordinates even, then untwisting the edges of
T^{\prime}
cannot decrease the number of even and odd ovals of the real part, see
Proposition

6.6
.
A similar statement in the maximal case was shown by Haas
[
Haa97
, Theorem 10.6.0.5]
.
In order to construct non-singular real algebraic curves with many even or many odd ovals, we can then restrict to even-free configurations of twists.
A configuration of twists
T\in\Circuit(C)
is
even-free
if its the dual set of edges
T^{\vee}
does not contain any integer point with both coordinates even in
\Delta_{C}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
, for
\Delta
the Newton polygon of
C
.
Note that the even-free circuits generate again a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-vector subspace
\EvFreeCirc(C)\subset\Circuit(C)
.

Itenberg
[
Ite95
]
introduced a way to count the number of even and odd ovals of maximal curves of even degree in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
near the non-singular tropical limit, whenever the induced set of twisted edges on the tropical limit
C
can be partitioned into even-free
bridges
, that is multi-bridges with a single edge of
C
.
Haas
[
Haa97
]
extended this result to all maximal curves near the non-singular tropical limit, hence whenever the induced set of twisted edges can be partitioned into even-free bridges and even-free
double-bridges
, that is multi-bridges with exactly two edges of
C
.
Note that Itenberg and Haas’ counts are expressed in terms of
zone decomposition
of a Newton polygon (see
Definition

6.7
), that is a decomposition of the Newton polygon induced by the set of edges dual to the set of twisted edges.
In the maximal case, these zone decompositions are induced by set of edges dual to bridges and double-bridges.
We extend those counts first to a partial count of even and odd empty ovals in the case when the tropical limit has set of twisted edges
T
belonging to
\EvFreeCirc(C)
, see
Proposition

6.11
.

In order to control easily the number of connected components, we want to use a configuration of twists in
\EvFreeCirc(C)
satisfying
Corollary

4.25
.
Such a configuration of twists
T
is dual to a disjoint union of complete bipartite graphs of the form
K_{2,2l}
and complete tripartite graphs
K_{2,2,2}
, see
Lemma

6.13
.
We restrict to disjoint unions of complete bipartite graphs of the form
K_{2,2l}
and obtain the following count of even and odd ovals.

Theorem 1.6

(
Theorem

6.15
)
.

Let
C
be a non-singular tropical curve in
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}
such that its Newton polygon
\Delta
(dual to
\Sigma
) has every edge of even lattice length.
Let
T:=T_{1}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup T_{s}\in\EvFreeCirc(C)
be such that all
(T_{i})^{\vee}\subset\Delta_{C}
are disjoints and each
(T_{i})^{\vee}
is a complete bipartite graph of the form
K_{2,2l_{i}}
.
Let
Y_{T}:=Y_{1}^{T}\sqcup Y_{0}^{T}
be the zone partition (see
Definition

6.7
) of
\Delta
induced by
T
such that
Y_{1}^{T}
contains the unique zone
Z^{T}
meeting the boundary of
\Delta
.
Let
p_{j}
and
n_{j}
be the number of even and odd interior integer points in
Y_{j}^{T}
.
Then for
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}
a real algebraic curve near the non-singular tropical limit
C
with set of twisted edges
T
, the numbers
p
of even ovals and
n
of odd ovals of
\mathcal{C}({\mathbb{R}})
are

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle=n_{1}+p_{0}+1+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s}(l_{i}+1),

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle=p_{1}+n_{0}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s}(l_{i}-1).

Let
R(k)=\frac{3k^{2}-3k}{2}
.
Ragsdale
[
Rag06
]
made the conjecture that a non-singular real algebraic curve
\mathcal{C}
of degree
2k
in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
has
p\leq R(k)+1
even ovals and
n\leq R(k)
odd ovals.
Petrowsky
[
Pet38
]
proved that

|p-n|\leq R(k)+1

and that there exist some curves reaching this bound for any degree
2k
.
He then conjectured that
p\leq R(k)+1
and
n\leq R(k)+1
.
Viro disproved Ragsdale’s conjecture (but not Petrowsky’s one) by constructing maximal curves of degree 8 with
n=R(k)+1

[
Vir80
]
, and Itenberg disproved both Ragsdale and Petrowsky’s conjectures by constructing via combinatorial patchworking curves with

p=R(k)+1+\left\lfloor\frac{(k-3)^{2}+4}{8}\right\rfloor\text{ and }n=R(k)+\left\lfloor\frac{(k-3)^{2}+4}{8}\right\rfloor.

Haas
[
Haa95
]
gave another construction of counter-examples, again via combinatorial patchworking, giving rise to

p=R(k)+1+\left\lfloor\frac{k^{2}-7k+16}{6}\right\rfloor\text{ and }n=R(k)+\left\lfloor\frac{k^{2}-7k+16}{6}\right\rfloor.

Brugallé
[
B+06
]
constructed a family of non-singular real algebraic curves, with increasing degree
2k
, such that the family has asymptotically maximal number of even ovals with respect to the Harnack-Klein and Petrowsky inequalities, that is

\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{p}{k^{2}}=\frac{7}{4}.

Moreover, Renaudineau gave a tropical construction of a family with asymptotically maximal number of even ovals
[
Ren17
]
.
Hence one of the remaining open problem is to determine the maximal number of even ovals constructible in low degree.
Using
Theorem

1.6
, we construct some new counter-examples to Ragsdale conjecture.

Theorem 1.7

(Theorem
6.19
)
.

There exists a dividing
(M-2\left\lfloor\frac{k-3}{2}\right\rfloor)
non-singular real algebraic curve of degree
2k\geq 10
in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
with

R(k)+1+\frac{k^{2}-5k+s(k)}{6}

even ovals, with
s(k)
determined by the value of
k
modulo 6 as follows.

•

If
k=0\mod 6
, then
s(k)=0
.

•

If
k=1\mod 6
, then
s(k)=10
.

•

If
k=2\mod 6
, then
s(k)=8
.

•

If
k=3,5\mod 6
, then
s(k)=6
.

•

If
k=4\mod 6
, then
s(k)=4
.

For instance, we construct in
Example

6.20
a non-singular real algebraic curve of degree 14 in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
with 68 even ovals, hence 4 even ovals more than Ragsdale’s conjecture (see also
Figure

11(a)
for the construction and
Figure

11(b)
for the isotopy type of the real part).
As an intermediate step for the general construction, we construct in
Lemma

6.16
a dividing
(M-2)
non-singular real algebraic curve of degree
2k\geq 10
in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
with more even ovals than Ragsdale’s conjecture.
As we see in
Example

6.18
, we obtain in degree 14 a dividing
(M-2)
non-singular real algebraic curve with 67 even ovals, hence 3 even ovals more than Ragsdale’s conjecture (see also
Figure

10(b)
for the construction).

2.
Tropical curves

We recall in this section several definitions in tropical geometry.
For more details and examples, one can read
[
BIMS15
, Section 2]
,
[
IMS09
, Chapter 1]
,
[
MS09
]
.

2.1.
Tropical curves in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

A
tropical polynomial in two variables
is

P(x,y)=\max\limits_{(i,j)\in A}(a_{i,j}+ix+jy),

where
A
is a finite subset of
({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})^{2}
and the coefficients
a_{i,j}
are in the tropical semi-ring
{\mathbb{T}}=({\mathbb{R}}\cup\{-\infty\},\max,+)
.
Note that we use the
``\max"
operation, while some references in the literature use the
``\min"
operation instead.
Thus, a tropical polynomial is a convex piecewise affine function, with corner locus

V_{P}=\{(x_{0},y_{0})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}~|~\exists(i,j)\neq(k,l),~a_{i,j}+ix_{0}+jy_{0}=a_{k,l}+kx_{0}+ly_{0}\}.

The set
V_{P}
is a 1-dimensional polyhedral complex in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.

Definition 2.1
.

The
weight function
is defined on the edges
e
of
V_{P}
as

w(e):=\max\limits_{(i,j),(k,l)}(\gcd(|i-k|,|j-l|))

for all pairs
(i,j)
and
(k,l)
such that the value of
P(x,y)
on
e
is given by the corresponding monomials.
The
tropical curve

C\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
defined by
P(x,y)
is the 1-dimensional polyhedral complex
V_{P}
equipped with the weight function
w
on the edges.

2.2.
Dual Subdivision

Let
P(x,y)=\max\limits_{i,j\in A}(a_{i,j}+ix+jy)
be a tropical polynomial defining a tropical curve
C
.
The
Newton polygon
of
P(x,y)
, denoted by
\Delta(P)
, is the convex lattice polygon given by

\Delta(P):=\Conv\{(i,j)\in({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})^{2}~|~a_{i,j}\neq-\infty\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}.

The tropical polynomial
P
also determines a subdivision of
\Delta(P)
, as follows.
Given
(x_{0},y_{0})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, let

\Delta(x_{0},y_{0}):=\Conv\{(i,j)\in({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})^{2}~|~P(x_{0},y_{0})=a_{i,j}+ix_{0}+jy_{0}\}\subset\Delta(P).

The tropical curve
C
induces a polyhedral decomposition of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, and the polytope
\Delta(x_{0},y_{0})
only depends on the cell
F\ni(x_{0},y_{0})
of the decomposition given by
C
.
Thus, we define the face
F^{\vee}:=\Delta(x_{0},y_{0})
dual to the face
F
of the polyhedral subdivision of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
induced by
C
, for
(x_{0},y_{0})\in F
.
The
dual subdivision

\Delta_{C}
of a non-singular tropical curve
C
is the union

\bigcup_{F}F^{\vee},

for
F
running through the faces of the decomposition of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
induced by
C
.

Definition 2.2
.

We say that a tropical curve
C
is
non-singular
if its dual subdivision
\Delta_{C}
is a triangulation with each 2-dimensional cell of Euclidean area
1/2
.

Equivalently, a tropical curve
C\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
with Newton polytope
\Delta
is non-singular if and only if
C
has
2\Area(\Delta)
vertices.
In particular, if a tropical curve
C\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
is non-singular, every vertex of
C
is 3-valent and every edge
e
of
C
has weight
w(e)=1
, and all integer points of
\Delta_{C}
are vertices in the subdivision.

2.3.
Tropical curves as limit of amoebas

We want to see that any tropical curve can be approximated by algebraic curves in
({\mathbb{C}}^{\times})^{2}
, via the logarithm map.
Consider the following map, where
t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
:

\displaystyle\Log_{t}:({\mathbb{C}}^{\times})^{2}

\displaystyle\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

\displaystyle(z,w)

\displaystyle\mapsto(\log_{t}|z|,\log_{t}|w|).

We call the image of a subset
V\subset({\mathbb{C}}^{\times})^{2}
via
\Log_{t}
the
amoeba
of
V
in base
t
(see for instance
[
GKZ14
]
).

Theorem 2.3

(
[
Kap00
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{t}(z,w)=\sum_{i,j}\alpha_{i,j}(t)z^{i}w^{j}
be a polynomial whose coefficients are functions
\alpha_{i,j}:{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
, and suppose that
\alpha_{i,j}(t)\sim\beta_{i,j}t^{-a_{i,j}}
when
t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
tends to
0
, with
\beta_{i,j}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
and
a_{i,j}\in{\mathbb{T}}
.
If
\mathcal{C}_{t}
denotes the curve in
({\mathbb{C}}^{\times})^{2}
defined by the polynomial
\mathcal{P}_{t}
, then the amoebas
\Log_{t}(\mathcal{C}_{t})
converge to the tropical curve defined by the tropical polynomial
P(x,y)=\max_{i,j}(a_{i,j}+ix+jy)
when
t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
tends to
0
.

2.4.
Tropical curve in a tropical toric surface

We refer to
[
MS09
]
and
[
MR09
]
for more details on tropical toric varieties.
Let
\Sigma
be a fan in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, and let
\sigma
be a cone of
\Sigma
.
We denote
U_{\sigma}:={\mathbb{T}}^{\dim\sigma}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2-\dim\sigma}
.
For
\tau

induces a basis of

Proof.

Let
\rho
be a twisted cycle in
(C,\mathcal{E})
.
By definition of a twisted cycle, the underlying graph of
\rho
is connected, hence by construction its real part
{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{\mathcal{E}}
is a connected graph.
Again by construction, every vertex of
{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{\mathcal{E}}
is in the boundary of exactly two edges, hence for
[{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{\mathcal{E}}]
the 1-dimensional chain in

induced by the real part
{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{\mathcal{E}}
, the boundary chain
\partial[{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{\mathcal{E}}]
is trivial in

.
Then the chain
[{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{\mathcal{E}}]
induces a class (also called
[{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{\mathcal{E}}]
) of the homology group
H_{1}({\mathbb{R}}C_{\mathcal{E}},{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
.
Since
{\mathbb{R}}C_{\mathcal{E}}
is 1-dimensional, the class
[{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{\mathcal{E}}]
is non-trivial in

Then since
{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{\mathcal{E}}
is connected, it is a connected component of the real part

.

Now let

be a connected component of
{\mathbb{R}}C_{\mathcal{E}}
.
Let

be the absolute value map.
The map

sends any two consecutive edges of

to two (not necessarily distinct) adjacent edges of

Therefore the map

sends

to a cycle

on

(in the sense of equivalence class of a closed walk under cyclic permutation and reversal).
If

in

Similarly, if

Therefore

5.

5.1.
Partition of dividing configuration of twists

We want to describe a set of generators of the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-vector space
\Div(C)
, in order to formulate relative topology criteria for dividing real tropical curves in the next sections.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
C
be a non-singular tropical curve in a non-singular projective tropical toric surface
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}
.
A
multi-bridge

T
on
C
is a dividing set of twisted edges on
C
with common direction modulo 2, such that
C\backslash T
is disconnected and for any proper subset
T^{\prime}\subsetneq T
, the graph
C\backslash T^{\prime}
is connected.
A
circuit

T
on
C
is a multi-bridge on
C
such that every edge of
T
is non-exposed.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
C
be a non-singular tropical curve in a non-singular projective tropical toric surface
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}
.
Then the set of all multi-bridges on
C
spans the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-vector space
\Div(C)
.
Moreover, if every primitive cycle of
C
has at most two edges of each possible direction modulo 2, then the set of all multi-bridges form a basis of
\Div(C)
.

Proof.

Let
T\in\Div(C)
.
Assume
e_{1}
is an edge of
T
that does not belong to a primitive cycle of
C
, for
T
seen as a subset of bounded edges of
C
.
The edge
e_{1}
is then a multi-bridge, and the identification
\Edge^{0}(C)\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{|\Edge^{0}(C)|}
sends
e_{1}
to the configuration of twists
T_{1}\in\Div(C)
.
Hence the sum
T+T_{1}
is a configuration of twists
\widehat{T_{1}}\in\Div(C)
, since
\Div(C)
is a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-space, such that
T_{1}
and
\widehat{T_{1}}
are disjoint seen as subsets of bounded edges of
C
.
Therefore, we can restrict to the case where all the edges of
T
lie on primitive cycles of
C
.

Assume
e_{2}
is an edge of
T
lying on a primitive cycle
\gamma
of
C
, for
T
seen as a subset of bounded edges of
C
.
By
Equation

1
and
Equation

2
, there exists an edge
e_{3}
distinct from
e_{2}
with
\overrightarrow{e_{2}}=\overrightarrow{e_{3}}\mod 2
in the set
\gamma\cap T
, for
\gamma
and
T
seen as subsets of bounded edges of
C
.
If both
e_{2}
and
e_{3}
are exposed, the set
\{e_{2},e_{3}\}
is a multi-bridge on
C
, and for
T_{2}:=e_{2}+e_{3}\in\Div(C)
the associated configuration of twists, the sum
T+T_{2}
is a configuration of twists
\widehat{T_{2}}\in\Div(C)
such that
T_{2}
and
\widehat{T_{2}}
are disjoint seen as subsets of bounded edges of
C
.
If at least one of the two edges
e_{2},e_{3}
is non-exposed (say
e_{3}
), we use again
Equation

1
and
Equation

2
on the unique primitive cycle
\gamma^{\prime}
distinct from
\gamma
containing
e_{3}
, and so on, until we cannot add a new edge in this manner (if
e_{2}
is non-exposed, one has to add edges in the same way as for
e_{3}
).
The resulting subset of bounded edges

is a multi-bridge, and for
T_{3}:=\sum_{i=2}^{k}e_{i}\in\Div(C)
the corresponding configuration of twists, the sum
T+T_{3}
is a configuration of twists
\widehat{T_{3}}\in\Div(C)
such that
T_{3}
and
\widehat{T_{3}}
are disjoint seen as subsets of bounded edges of
C
.
Therefore, any configuration of twists

is given as a sum of configuration of twists associated to multi-bridges on
C
.
Moreover, if every primitive cycle of
C
has at most two edges of each possible direction modulo 2, any two distinct multi-bridges on
C
are disjoint, hence the configurations of twists associated to the multi-bridges on

form a basis of
\Div(C)

∎

5.2.
Real tropical curves with only ovals

For
(C,\mathcal{E})
a non-singular real tropical curve in the non-singular tropical toric surface
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}
, we say that a connected component
{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{\mathcal{E}}
of the real part
{\mathbb{R}}C_{\mathcal{E}}
is an
oval
if
{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{\mathcal{E}}
divides
{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}
into two connected components.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
(C,\mathcal{E})
be a non-singular real tropical curve in a non-singular tropical projective toric surface
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}
.
Let
\rho
be a twisted cycle on
(C,\mathcal{E})
which does not contain any 1-valent vertex of
C
.
Then the real part
{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{\mathcal{E}}
is an oval.

Proof.

Since
\rho
does not contain any 1-valent vertex of
C
, the real part
{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{\mathcal{E}}
of
\rho
is strictly contained in an orthant of the real algebraic torus
({\mathbb{R}}^{\times})^{2}\subset{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}
. Since
{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{\mathcal{E}}
is homeomorphic to the circle
S^{1}
, it must then divide that orthant into two connected components.
Therefore
{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{\mathcal{E}}
is an oval.
∎

By definition, the homology class
[{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{\mathcal{E}}]
of an oval is trivial in the homology group of the ambient space
H_{1}({\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma};{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
.
Then if all the connected components of the real part
{\mathbb{R}}C_{\mathcal{E}}
of a non-singular real tropical curve
(C,\mathcal{E})
are ovals, we obtain that the homology class
[{\mathbb{R}}C_{\mathcal{E}}]
, given as the sum of homology class of the connected components, is trivial in
H_{1}({\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma};{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
.

If we only assume that the class
[{\mathbb{R}}C_{\mathcal{E}}]
is trivial in
H_{1}({\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma};{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
, we have the following equivalent condition on the Newton polygon of
C
.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
(C,\mathcal{E})
be a non-singular real tropical curve in
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}
, and let
\Delta
be the Newton polygon of
C
dual to the fan
\Sigma
.
The homology class
[{\mathbb{R}}C_{\mathcal{E}}]
is trivial in
H_{1}({\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma};{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
if and only if every edge of
\Delta
has even lattice length.

Definition 5.7
.

A non-singular real tropical curve
(C,\mathcal{E})
in
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}
is said to be of
strict even degree
if every edge of the associated Newton polygon
\Delta
has even lattice length (in particular, the non-singular real tropical curve
(C,\mathcal{E})
satisfies
Proposition

5.6
).

Proof of
Proposition

5.6
.

The homology class
[{\mathbb{R}}C_{\mathcal{E}}]
is trivial in
H_{1}({\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma};{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
if and only if
{\mathbb{R}}C_{\mathcal{E}}
intersects each 1-dimensional face of the stratification of
{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}
an even number of times.
Now there is a one-to-one correspondence between the 1-valent vertices of
C
on a 1-dimensional stratum of
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}
and the intersection points of
{\mathbb{R}}C_{\mathcal{E}}
with the corresponding 1-dimensional stratum of
{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}
.
Therefore the homology class
[{\mathbb{R}}C_{\mathcal{E}}]
is trivial in
H_{1}({\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma};{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
if and only if the non-singular tropical curve
C
intersect each 1-dimensional stratum of
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}
in an even number of points.
The latter condition can be translated into the desired Newton polygon condition.
∎

Corollary 5.8
.

Let
(C,\mathcal{E})
be a non-singular real tropical curve in
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}
.
If all the connected components of the real part
{\mathbb{R}}C_{\mathcal{E}}
are ovals, then
(C,\mathcal{E})
is of strict even degree in
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}
.

We want to study the sufficient conditions for
{\mathbb{R}}C_{\mathcal{E}}
to contain only ovals, in two distinct directions.
The first idea comes from the well known fact that a non-singular real algebraic curve of even degree in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
has real part consisting only of ovals.

We denote by
\Delta_{d}
the 2-dimensional lattice polytope given as the convex hull of the set
\{(0,0),(d,0),(0,d)\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{\geq 0}
.
We will say that a lattice polytope
\Delta
is a
lattice transformation of
a lattice polytope
\Delta^{\prime}
if
\Delta
is given as the image of
\Delta^{\prime}
via the composition of a lattice translation with a linear map in
\operatorname{{\mathbf{G}L}}(2,{\mathbb{Z}})
.

Proposition 5.9
.

Let
(C,\mathcal{E})
be a non-singular real tropical curve of strict even degree in
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}
.
Assume that the Newton polygon
\Delta
of
C
, dual to the fan
\Sigma
, is a lattice transformation of some
\Delta_{2k}
, for
k\geq 1
.
Then all the connected components of the real part
{\mathbb{R}}C_{\mathcal{E}}
are ovals.

Proof.

Assume first that
C
is of even degree in
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}^{2}
, so that the Newton polygon of
C
is the lattice polytope
\Delta_{2k}
for some
k\geq 1
.
The homology group
H_{1}({\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}^{2};{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
, thus the real part
{\mathbb{R}}C_{\mathcal{E}}
contains at most one pseudo-line, as they would otherwise intersect.
By assumption on the Newton polygon, for each 1-dimensional stratum
\tau
of
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}^{2}
, we have
|C\cap\tau|=0\mod 2
.
Now there is a one-to-one correspondence between the 1-valent vertices of
C
on a 1-dimensional stratum of
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}
and the intersection points of
{\mathbb{R}}C_{\mathcal{E}}
with the corresponding 1-dimensional stratum of
{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}
.
Therefore the homology class
[{\mathbb{R}}C_{\mathcal{E}}]
is trivial in
H_{1}({\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}^{2};{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
. Thus, the real part
{\mathbb{R}}C_{\mathcal{E}}
does not contain a pseudo-line, hence every connected component of
{\mathbb{R}}C_{\mathcal{E}}
must be an oval.

Assume now that
\Delta
is a lattice transformation of some
\Delta_{2k}
.
The lattice transformation induces a homeomorphism between
({\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma},{\mathbb{R}}C_{\mathcal{E}})
and
({\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}^{2},{\mathbb{R}}C_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}^{\prime})
, where
C^{\prime}
is a non-singular tropical curve of even degree in
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}^{2}
.
As we saw above, the connected components of
{\mathbb{R}}C_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}^{\prime}
are all ovals.
Therefore all the connected components of
{\mathbb{R}}C_{\mathcal{E}}
are ovals.
∎

The second idea we want to explore is to consider the configurations of twists preserving the “all ovals” property no matter the ambient tropical toric surface.

Proposition 5.10
.

Let
(C,\mathcal{E})
be a non-singular real tropical curve of strict even degree in
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}
.
Assume that every edge in the set of twisted edges
T
on
C
is non-exposed.
Then all the connected components of the real part
{\mathbb{R}}C_{\mathcal{E}}
are ovals.

Proof.

By assumption, there exists a unique twisted cycle
\rho
on
(C,\mathcal{E})
intersecting the 1-dimensional strata of
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}
.
Since every edge of the Newton polygon
\Delta
has even lattice length, the homology class
[{\mathbb{R}}C_{\mathcal{E}}]
is trivial in
H_{1}({\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma};{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
by
Proposition

5.6
.
By
Lemma

5.5
, every twisted cycle in
(C,\mathcal{E})
distinct from
\rho
has real part an oval.
We can write the homology class
[{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{\mathcal{E}}]
as the sum of homology classes (with
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-coefficients)
[{\mathbb{R}}C_{\mathcal{E}}]+\sum_{i=1}^{r}[{\mathbb{R}}(\rho_{i})_{\mathcal{E}}]
, with each

being a twisted cycle on
(C,\mathcal{E})
distinct from

.
Since all the summands are trivial in

[{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{\mathcal{E}}]
is trivial in

, hence

∎

6.
Even and odd ovals

Let
(C,\mathcal{E})
be a non-singular real tropical curve in
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}
with real part consisting only of ovals.
Let
{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{\mathcal{E}}
be such an oval.
We say that
{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{\mathcal{E}}
is
even
if
{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{\mathcal{E}}
is contained in an even number of discs in
{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}
bounded by another oval of
{\mathbb{R}}C_{\mathcal{E}}

{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{\mathcal{E}}
is
odd
.

6.1.
Counting in terms of distribution of signs

We want to count the number of even and odd ovals in
{\mathbb{R}}C_{\mathcal{E}}
based on a distribution of signs
\delta
associated to
\mathcal{E}
on the dual subdivision
\Delta_{C}
of
C
.
Recall that a non-singular tropical curve
C
is of
strict even degree
in
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}
if all the edges of the Newton polygon
\Delta
of
C
(dual to the fan
\Sigma
) are of even lattice length.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
C
be a non-singular tropical curve of strict even degree in
{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}
, and let
\delta
be a distribution of signs on
\Delta_{C}
.
Assume that the connected components of
{\mathbb{R}}C_{\delta}
are all ovals.
Any two connected components
v^{\vee},(v^{\prime})^{\vee}\subset({\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}\backslash C)^{*}
are contained in the same number modulo 2 of ovals of
{\mathbb{R}}C_{\delta}
if and only if the integer points
v,v^{\prime}\in\Delta_{C}^{*}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
dual to
v^{\vee},(v^{\prime})^{\vee}
satisfy
\delta(v)=\delta(v^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Let
(\mathcal{C}_{t})
be a family of non-singular real algebraic curves converging to the tropical limit
(C,\delta)
.
For

small enough, the curve
\mathcal{C}_{t}
is a non-singular real algebraic curve such that the pair
(\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}({\mathbb{R}}),\mathcal{C}_{t}({\mathbb{R}}))
is homeomorphic to
({\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma},{\mathbb{R}}C_{\delta})
by
Theorem

3.3
.
In particular, we have a one-to-one correspondence between the connected components of
\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}({\mathbb{R}})\backslash\mathcal{C}_{t}({\mathbb{R}})
and the connected components of
{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}\backslash{\mathbb{R}}C_{\delta}
.
Let
f_{t}
be a defining polynomial of
\mathcal{C}_{t}
, such that the signs of the monomials of

induces the distribution of signs
\delta
on
\Delta_{C}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
.
Let

be a connected component of
\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}({\mathbb{R}})\backslash\mathcal{C}_{t}({\mathbb{R}})
and

the corresponding connected component of

.
Let

be the integer point dual to

.
By definition of
\delta
, we have

if and only if
\delta(v)>0
, and similarly
f_{t}|_{D_{t}}<0
if and only if
\delta(v)<0
.
The value of

along a path in
\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}({\mathbb{R}})
intersecting
\mathcal{C}_{t}({\mathbb{R}})
transversely changes of sign at each intersection of the path with an oval.
Therefore, any two connected components
v^{\vee},(v^{\prime})^{\vee}\subset({\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{P}_{\Sigma}\backslash C)^{*}
are contained in the same number of ovals modulo 2 of
{\mathbb{R}}C_{\delta}
if and only if the dual integer points

satisfy
\delta(v)=\delta(v^{\prime})
.
∎

.

.

containing

, a connected component
v^{\vee}
is contained in

Lemma 6.14

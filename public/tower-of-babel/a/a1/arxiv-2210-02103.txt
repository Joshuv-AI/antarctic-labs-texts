Abstract
. We study differential splitting fields of quaternion algebras with derivations. A quaternion algebra over a field k k is always split by a quadratic extension of k k . However, a differential quaternion algebra need not be split over any algebraic extension of k k . We use solutions of certain Riccati equations to provide bounds on the transcendence degree of splitting fields of a differential quaternion algebra.

Introduction

In this paper, we study differential splitting fields of quaternion algebras with derivations. Quaternion algebras are 4-dimensional central simple algebras. Derivations on central simple algebras are studied by Hochschild
[
4
]
, Hoechsmann
[
5
]
, Juan and Magid
[
6
]
, Kulshrestha and Srinivasan
[
8
]
, Gupta, Kaur and Singh
[
3
]
.

A notion of splitting fields for a differential central simple algebra was introduced in
[
6
]
. Authors also proved the existence of a finitely generated differential field extensions that split the given differential algebra.
It is clear from
[
6
, Proposition 2]
that every
m^{2}
-dimensional differential central simple algebra is split by a differential field extension of transcendence degree at most
m^{2}
. In particular, every differential quaternion algebra is split by a differential field extension of transcendence degree at most
4
(also see
[
8
, Theorem 4.1]
). This raises a question:

Question 1.1
.

What is the optimal bound on transcendence degree of a splitting field?

We answer the above question for quaternion algebras over a field
k
with
\mathrm{char}(k)\neq 2
. In
Proposition

4.1
, we provide a construction of differential splitting fields of a differential quaternion algebra using differential fields containing solutions of a certain Riccati equation. We then use this proposition to show the transcendence degree of such a splitting field is at most
3
(see
Theorem

4.3
). Moreover, for
0\leq r\leq 3
, we give examples of differential quaternion algebras whose splitting fields have transcendence degree at least
r
over
k
(see
Example

5.2
and
Theorem

6.5
). Thus, the bound
3
is optimal.
In
Section

6
, we consider a particular class of differential quaternion algebras that are split over a transcendence degree at most one extension of
k
.

Let
k
be a field containing a primitive
m
th root of unity, say
\omega
.
An
m^{2}
-dimensional central simple algebra over
k
is a symbol algebra if it is generated by two elements
u,v
such that
u^{m},v^{m}\in{k}^{\times}
and
vu=\omega uv
.
For generators
u,v
of a symbol algebra
A
, there is a derivation on
A
that restricts to a given derivation on
k
such that
d(k(u))\subseteq k(u)
and
d(k(v))\subseteq k(v)
; such a derivation is named as standard derivation.
It was shown in
[
3
, Theorem 5.4]
that a
m^{2}
-dimensional symbol algebra with standard derivation is split by a finite field extension of degree at most
m^{2}
if
m
is odd and
2m^{2}
if
m
is even.
It is natural to ask the following questions.

Question 1.2
.

(i)

Can one classify all differential symbol algebras over a differential field that are split by finite field extensions?

(ii)

Can one classify all standard derivations on a given symbol algebra?

If
\mathrm{char}(k)\neq 2
then any quaternion algebra is a symbol algebra. Thus, in particular, a quaternion algebra with standard derivation is split by a finite field extension of degree at most
8
(also see
[
8
, Theorem 4.1]
).
We show that its converse is not true (see
Example

7.1
)
In the case of quaternion algebras, we show that the above questions are inter-related.
In
Theorem

7.3
, we prove that a differential quaternion algebra is split by a finite differential field extension if and only if the derivation on the differential split algebra is standard. We hope to extend these results to an arbitrary symbol algebra in future.

Notation

Throughout this paper we fix a field

\mathrm{char}(k)\neq 2

Let

For

2.
Differential splitting of central simple algebras

A ring (resp. algebra or field)
R
together with an additive map
d\colon R\rightarrow R
satisfying
d(xy)=xd(y)+d(x)y
for all
x,y\in R
, is called differential ring (resp. algebra or field) and is denoted by
(R,d)
. The map
d
on
R
is called a
derivation on
R
. The set
C_{(R,d)}=\{x\in R\mid d(x)=0\}
is called the set of
constants of
(R,d)
.

For any element
\vartheta\in R
,
there is an
inner derivation

\partial_{\vartheta}
on
R
given by
\partial_{\vartheta}(x)=x\vartheta-\vartheta x
for
x\in R
. Note that
C_{(R,\partial_{\vartheta})}
is equal to the centralizer of
\vartheta
in
R
.

A
central simple algebra over
k
is a finite dimensional
k
-algebra with center
k
and no non-trivial two-sided ideals.
A central simple
k
-algebra
A

splits over a field extension
L/k
if
A\otimes_{k}L\simeq M_{m}(L)
as
L
-algebras and
L
is called a
splitting field
of
A
.

Let
(k,\,^{\prime})
be a differential field. For a field extension
L/k
, there is a derivation on
L
that restricts to
′
on
k
(see
[
9
, Section 3]
). Moreover, when
L/k
is a finite field extension, there is a unique extension of
′
on
L
. For convenience, we denote the derivations on field extensions by
′
whenever the derivation is clear from the context.

A central simple
k
-algebra
A
together with a derivation
d
that restricts to
′
on k is called differential central simple algebra over
k
, and is denoted by
(A,d)
. The set of all derivations on
A
that are extensions of
′
is denoted by
Der(A/(k,\,^{\prime}))
.

We say that
(A_{1},d_{1})
and
(A_{2},d_{2})
are isomorphic as differential
k
-algebras if there exists a
k
-algebra isomorphism
\phi:(A_{1},d_{1})\rightarrow(A_{2},d_{2})
such that
\phi\circ d_{1}=d_{2}\circ\phi
; and
\phi
is called
differential isomorphism
.

The derivation
′
on the matrix algebra
M_{m}(k)
defined by
(a_{ij})^{\prime}=(a^{\prime}_{ij})
is called the
coordinate-wise derivation on
M_{m}(k)
. For the matrix algebra
M_{m}(k)
, we have
Der(M_{m}(k)/(k,\,^{\prime}))=\{d_{P}=\,^{\prime}+\partial_{P}\mid\ P\in M_{m}(k)\}
(see
[
1
, Theorem 2]
).

In
[
6
]
, the authors introduced the notion of differential splitting fields.
A differential central simple algebra
(A,d)
over
(k,\,^{\prime})

splits over a differential field
(L,\,^{\prime})\supseteq(k,\,^{\prime})
if
(A\otimes_{k}L,d^{\ast})\simeq(M_{m}(L),\,^{\prime})
as differential
L
-algebras, where
d^{\ast}:=d\otimes\,^{\prime}=d\otimes id_{L}+id_{A}\otimes\,^{\prime}
. In
[
6
]
, the authors proved the existence of differential splitting fields using the theory of Picard-Vessiot extensions. The following characterization of isomorphic differential matrix algebras was crucial in constructing differential splitting fields.

Proposition 2.1
.

[
6
, Proposition 2]

Let
P\in M_{m}(k)\setminus\{0\}
. Then
(M_{m}(k),d_{P})
and
(M_{m}(k),\,^{\prime})
are isomorphic as differential
k
-algebras if and only if there exists
F\in GL_{m}(k)
such that
F^{\prime}=PF
.

For a quaternion algebra
Q
over
k
,
a derivation
d\in Der(Q/(k,\,^{\prime}))
is called a
standard derivation
if there exists
u,v\in Q^{0}
such that
u^{2},v^{2}\in{k}^{\times},\ vu=-uv,\ d(u)\in k(u)
and
d(v)\in k(v)
, and we denote this derivation by
d_{(u,v)}
.
In
[
8
, Proposition 3.2]
, it was shown that for every derivation
d\in Der(Q/(k,\,^{\prime}))
, there exists a unique
\vartheta\in Q^{0}
such that
d=d_{(u,v)}+\partial_{\vartheta}
. Furthermore, this characterization was used to construct splitting fields of quaternion algebras with derivations.
We recall results related to differential splitting of quaternion algebras.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
(k,\,^{\prime})
be a differential field with
\mathrm{char}(k)=0
.
Let
Q=(\alpha,\beta)_{k}
be a quaternion algebra.

(1)

[
8
, Theorem 4.1]
The differential algebra
(Q,d)
is split by a finitely
generated differential field extension of
(k,\,^{\prime})
. The algebra
(Q,d_{(u,v)})
is split by a finite extension of
(k,\,^{\prime})
.

(2)

[
8
, Theorem 4.5]
If
\,{}^{\prime}
is the zero derivation and
Q
is a division algebra with non-zero derivation
d
, then
(Q,d)
is split by a field extension of transcendence degree
1
and is not split by any algebraic extension of
k
.

We are now interested in constructing differential splitting fields for an arbitrary differential quaternion algebra
(Q,d)
.

3.
Solutions of Riccati equations

In the process of finding the splitting fields of a differential quaternion algebra, we come across a certain types of first order differential equations with variable coefficients.
In this section, we will note some essential results concerning homogeneous linear differential equations and Riccati equations.

A first order differential equation over
k
of the form

X^{\prime}=\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}X+\alpha_{2}X^{2},

where
\alpha_{0},\alpha_{2}\neq 0
, is called
Riccati equation
. If
\alpha_{0}=\alpha_{2}=0
then equation becomes a homogenous linear differential equation. In general, solutions of a Riccati equation are not known, but there are methods to solve particular cases. We will use Riccati equations whose solutions are known to construct examples of differential quaternion algebras that split over fields with fixed transcendence degrees. For this purpose, in the following results, we relate solutions of Riccati equations to that of a certain linear differential equation.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
a\in k
and let
\theta\in\overline{k}
be a solution of
Y^{\prime}=aY
such that
n:=[k[\theta]:k]
is minimal. Then
\theta^{n}\in k
.

Proof.

Consider the polynomial ring
k[X]
with derivation
D
such that
D(X)=aX
.
Let
p(X)=X^{n}+a_{n-1}X^{n-1}+\dots+a_{1}X+a_{0}
be the minimal polynomial of
\theta
over
k
.
Consider the differential homomorphism
\phi:k[X]\to k[\theta]
defined by
\phi(X)=\theta
. Then the kernel of
\phi
is the prime ideal generated by
p(X)
. Since
D(p)(\theta)=0
,
p
divides
D(p)
and whereby
D(p)=nap
. On comparing the coefficients, we obtain
D(a_{i})=(n-i)aa_{i}
for
0\leq i\leq n-1
. This implies
\sqrt[n-i]{a_{i}}
is a solution of
Y^{\prime}=aY
. Since
n
is the minimal degree for such a solution, we obtain
a_{i}=0
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
. Therefore,
p(X)=X^{n}+a_{0}
. ∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
k(t)
be the rational function field with derivation
t^{\prime}=\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}t+\alpha_{2}t^{2}
where
\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in k
and
\alpha_{2}\neq 0
.
Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
f\in k(t)
be a solution of the differential equation
Y^{\prime}=n\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2}+\alpha_{2}t\right)Y
.
Then each zero and pole of
f
is a solution of
X^{\prime}=\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}X+\alpha_{2}X^{2}
.

Proof.

Let
p=\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2}+\alpha_{2}t
.
Since
p\notin k
, the solution
f\notin k
.
Let
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{r}\in\overline{k}
,
\eta\in k
and
m_{1},\ldots,m_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}
be such that
f=\eta\prod_{i=1}^{r}(t-\gamma_{i})^{m_{i}}
.
Then

f^{\prime}=\eta\prod_{i=1}^{r}(t-\gamma_{i})^{m_{i}-1}\cdot\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\Big((t^{\prime}-\gamma_{i}^{\prime})\prod_{j\neq i}(t-\gamma_{j})\Big)\right)+\frac{\eta^{\prime}}{\eta}f.

Since
f^{\prime}=npf
, for
1\leq i\leq r
, we have
(t-\gamma_{i})
divides
(t^{\prime}-\gamma_{i}^{\prime})=\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}t+\alpha_{2}t^{2}-\gamma_{i}^{\prime}
,
which implies that each
\gamma_{i}
is a solution of
X^{\prime}=\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}X+\alpha_{2}X^{2}
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume that the non-linear differential equation

(3.1)

X^{\prime}=\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}X+\alpha_{2}X^{2}

over
k
has no algebraic solution and
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
be its distinct transcendental solutions.
Then the differential equation
Y^{\prime}=\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2}+\alpha_{2}\lambda_{1}\right)Y
has no algebraic solution over
k(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
.

Proof.

Set
L=k(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
and
p=\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2}+\alpha_{2}\lambda_{1}
.
Suppose that
Y^{\prime}=pY
has a solution in
\overline{L}
.

First assume that
\lambda_{2}\in\overline{k(\lambda_{1})}
.
Then
\overline{L}=\overline{k(\lambda_{1})}
.
Let
\theta\in\overline{L}
be a solution of
Y^{\prime}=pY
such that
n=[k(\lambda_{1},\theta):k(\lambda_{1})]
is minimal.
By
Proposition

3.1
, it follows that
\theta^{n}=f\in k(\lambda_{1})
.
Then
f^{\prime}=npf
.
Since
Equation

3.1
has no solution in
\overline{k}
, by
Lemma

3.2

f\in k
.
This implies
p\in k
, which is a contradiction.

Now assume that
\lambda_{2}\notin\overline{k(\lambda_{1})}
.
Set
K=k(\lambda_{2})
.
Note that
\lambda_{2}
is the only solution of
Equation

3.1
in
\overline{K}
.
Let
\theta\in\overline{L}
be a solution of
Y^{\prime}=pY
such that
n=[K(\lambda_{1},\theta):K(\lambda_{1})]
is minimal.
Then, by
Proposition

3.1
,
\theta^{n}=f\in K(\lambda_{1})
and
f^{\prime}=npf
.
By
Lemma

3.2
, we have that
f=\eta(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})^{m}
for some
m\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\eta\in K
.

Substituting
f=\eta(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})^{m}
in
f^{\prime}=npf
, we further get

\frac{\eta^{\prime}}{n\eta}+\frac{m}{n}\big(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})\big)=\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2}+\alpha_{2}\lambda_{1}\,.

On comparing the coefficients, we obtain
m=n
and
(\eta^{-1})^{\prime}=n\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2}+\alpha_{2}\lambda_{2}\right)\eta^{-1}
.
Since
Equation

3.1
has no solution in
\overline{k}
, using
Lemma

3.2
, we arrive at a contradiction as above .
∎

4.
Splitting of differential quaternion algebras

Let
Q=(\alpha,\beta)_{k}
be a quaternion algebra with generators
u,v
.
For
\xi\in\overline{k}
such that
\xi^{2}=\alpha
, the homomorphism
\mathbf{\Phi_{Q,\xi}}:Q\otimes_{k}k(\xi)\rightarrow M_{2}(k(\xi))

determined by

u\otimes 1\mapsto A=\left(\begin{matrix}\xi&\\
&-\xi\\
\end{matrix}\right);\,\,\,\,v\otimes 1\mapsto B=\left(\begin{matrix}&\beta\\
1&\\
\end{matrix}\right),

and
\lambda\otimes r\mapsto\lambda rI_{2}
where
\lambda\in k
and
r\in k(\xi)
is an isomorphism of
k(\xi)
-algebras. Let
d=d_{(u,v)}+\partial_{\vartheta}
, where
\vartheta=a_{1}u+a_{2}v+a_{3}uv\in Q^{0}
, be a derivation in
Der(Q/(k,\,^{\prime}))
. The isomorphism
\Phi_{Q,\xi}
induces a differential isomorphism
(Q\otimes_{k}k(\xi),d^{\ast})\rightarrow(M_{2}(k(\xi)),d_{P})
where

(4.1)

P=\left(\begin{matrix}a_{1}\xi+\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{4\beta}&(a_{2}+a_{3}\xi)\beta\\
a_{2}-a_{3}\xi&-a_{1}\xi-\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{4\beta}\\
\end{matrix}\right),

see
[
8
, Theorem 4.1]
.
By
Proposition

2.1
, the differential algebra
(M_{2}(k(\xi)),d_{P})
is split over
(L,\,^{\prime})
if and only if there exists
F\in GL_{2}(L)
such that

F^{\prime}=PF
. Write
F=(f_{ij})
with
f_{ij}\in L
. By comparing the matrix entries in the equation
F^{\prime}=PF
we obtain

(4.2)

\displaystyle f_{11}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\left(a_{1}\xi+\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{4\beta}\right)f_{11}+(a_{2}+a_{3}\xi)\beta f_{21},

(4.3)

\displaystyle f_{21}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=(a_{2}-a_{3}\xi)f_{11}-\left(a_{1}\xi+\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{4\beta}\right)f_{21},

(4.4)

\displaystyle f_{12}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\left(a_{1}\xi+\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{4\beta}\right)f_{12}+(a_{2}+a_{3}\xi)\beta f_{22},

(4.5)

\displaystyle f_{22}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=(a_{2}-a_{3}\xi)f_{12}-\left(a_{1}\xi+\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{4\beta}\right)f_{22}.

Taking
L=k(\xi)(f_{11},f_{12},f_{21},f_{22})
, it is clear that
(Q,d)
is always split by a differential field extension of transcendence degree at most
4
.

Here, we will show that a differential quaternion algebra is split by a differential field extension of transcendence degree at most
3
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\xi\in\overline{k}
be such that
\xi^{2}=\alpha
.
Let
(L,\,^{\prime})
be a differential field extension of
(k(\xi),\,^{\prime})
.

(a)

If
(Q,d)
is split by
(L,\,^{\prime})
, then the differential equation

(4.6)

X^{\prime}=(a_{2}+a_{3}\xi)\beta+2\left(a_{1}\xi+\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{4\beta}\right)X-(a_{2}-a_{3}\xi)X^{2}

has at least two distinct solutions in
L
.

(b)

If
Equation

4.6

has at least two distinct solutions (say
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
) in
L
, then

(Q,d)
is split by a simple extension
(L(\mu),\,^{\prime})
where
\mu
is a solution of the differential equation

(4.7)

\frac{Y^{\prime}}{Y}=(a_{2}-a_{3}\xi)\lambda_{1}-\left(a_{1}\xi+\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{4\beta}\right).

Proof.

(a)

Assume that
(Q,d)
is split by
(L,\,^{\prime})
.
Then the algebras
(Q\otimes k(\xi),d^{\ast})\simeq(M_{2}(k(\xi)),d_{P}))
are also split by
(L,\,^{\prime})
. From
Proposition

2.1
, there exists
F=(f_{ij})\in GL_{2}(L)
such that
F^{\prime}=PF
.
First suppose that
a_{2}=a_{3}=0
. From Equations (
4.2
) to (
4.5
), we get that
f_{11},f_{12},f_{21}^{-1}
and
f_{22}^{-1}
are solutions of the
Equation

4.6
,
X^{\prime}=\left(a_{1}\xi+\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{4\beta}\right)X
.

Now suppose
a_{2}\neq 0
or
a_{3}\neq 0
. Since
F\in GL_{2}(L)
, from Equations (
4.2
) to (
4.5
), we conclude that
f_{ij}\neq 0
for all
i,j
.
Considering the equations
\frac{(\ref{f11})}{f_{11}}-\frac{(\ref{f21})}{f_{21}}
and
\frac{(\ref{f12})}{f_{12}}-\frac{(\ref{f22})}{f_{22}}
, we obtain that
\frac{f_{11}}{f_{21}}
and
\frac{f_{12}}{f_{22}}
are solutions of
Equation

4.6
.
Since
F\in GL_{2}(L)
, we have that
\frac{f_{11}}{f_{21}}\neq\frac{f_{12}}{f_{22}}
.

(b)

Set

F=\left(\begin{matrix}\lambda_{1}\mu&\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\mu(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})}\\
\mu&\frac{1}{\mu(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})}\\
\end{matrix}\right).

Note that
\det(F)=1
and
F^{\prime}=PF
. Thus, by
Proposition

2.1

(M_{2}(L(\mu)),d_{P})\simeq(M_{2}(L(\mu)),\,^{\prime})
. Hence,
(Q,d)
splits over
(L(\mu),\,^{\prime})
.

∎

Using this proposition, we can easily construct an example where differential splitting field of a quaternion algebra is of transcendence degree at most
1
.

Example 4.2
.

For
a\in k
, let

be a derivation on the quaternion algebra

. Then the

. Let

(

),

is split by

where

.

Since the generators

and

, where

,

or

Now we prove the main theorem of this section.

Theorem 4.3
.

(Q,d)
is split by a differential field extension of

of transcendence degree at most

Proof.

It follows from

(

is split by the differential field extension

where

are solutions of

.

5.
Examples of splitting fields with transcendence degree
2
and
3

In this section, we construct examples of differential splitting fields with transcendence degree at least
2
and
3
. We first observe the following result, which is crucial in the construction of such splitting fields.

Proposition 5.1
.

The differential quaternion algebra
(Q,d)
is split by a field extension of transcendence degree at least
2
if
the non-linear differential equation (
4.6
)

X^{\prime}=(a_{2}+a_{3}\xi)\beta+2\left(a_{1}\xi+\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{4\beta}\right)X-(a_{2}-a_{3}\xi)X^{2}

has no algebraic solutions over
k
.
Moreover, if any two solutions of
Equation

4.6
are algebraically independent over
k
, then
(Q,d)
is split by a field extension of transcendence degree at least
3
.

Proof.

Let
(L,\,^{\prime})
be a differential field extension of
(k(\xi),\,^{\prime})
containing two distinct roots of
Equation

4.6
.
Let
\lambda\in L
be a solution of
Equation

4.6
.
By
Proposition

4.1
(
b
),
(Q,d)
is split by an extension
L(\mu)
, where

\mu^{\prime}=(a_{2}-a_{3}\xi)\lambda-\left(a_{1}\xi+\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{4\beta}\right)\mu
.
Since

4.6
has no algebraic solution over

\mathrm{tr.deg}_{k}{L(\mu)}=\mathrm{tr.deg}_{k}{L}+1

∎

Example 5.2
.

Let
(k,\,^{\prime})
be the differential field where
k=\mathbb{Q}(t)
and
t^{\prime}=1
.
Let
Q=(1,t)_{k}
,
u,v\in Q^{0}
such that
u^{2}=1
,
v^{2}=t
and
vu=-uv
.

(i)
Consider the derivation
d=d_{(u,v)}+\partial_{\vartheta}
on
Q
with
\vartheta=\frac{1}{4t}(-u-2v+2uv)
.
Then the
Equation

4.6
becomes
X^{\prime}=\frac{1}{t}X^{2}
. The general solution of this differential equation is given by
\frac{-1}{\log(t)+c}
, where

is an arbitrary constant. This implies,
X^{\prime}=\frac{1}{t}X^{2}
has no solution in
\overline{k}
and infinitely many solutions in

5.1

.

(ii)
Consider the derivation
d=d_{(u,v)}+\partial_{\vartheta}
on
Q
with
\vartheta=-\frac{1}{4t}u-v

Then the
Equation

4.6
becomes
X^{\prime}=X^{2}-t
, which is a Riccati differential equation that is not solvable by any algebraic function over
k
(see
[
7
, Example p.42-43]
).

Let
K=k(\lambda)
be a rational function field where
\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda^{2}-t
and
\lambda+\frac{1}{\theta}
be another solution of
X^{\prime}=X^{2}-t
in
\overline{K}
, i.e
\theta\in\overline{K}
. Then it is easy to observe that
\theta^{\prime}=-2\lambda\theta-1
.
Let
p(X)=X^{m}+a_{m-1}X^{m-1}+\dots+a_{1}X+a_{0}
be the minimal polynomial of
\theta
over
K
. Consider a differential homomorphism
\phi:K[X]\to K[\theta]
defined by
\phi(X)=\theta
. Then the kernel of
\phi
is the prime ideal generated by
p(X)
. Since
p^{\prime}(\theta)=0
,
p
divides
p^{\prime}
and whereby
p(X)^{\prime}=-2m\lambda p(X)
.
On comparing the coefficients of
X^{m-1}
, we obtain
a_{m-1}
is a solution of the differential equation

(5.1)

Y^{\prime}=-2\lambda Y+c

over
K
, when
c=m
.
Let
m\neq 0
then
a_{m-1}\neq 0
. Write
a_{m-1}=\frac{f}{g}
where
f,g
are coprime polynomials in
k[\lambda]
and
g
is monic. Then
f^{\prime}g-fg^{\prime}=-2\lambda fg+mg^{2}
implies
g
divides
g^{\prime}
.

Let
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{r}\in\overline{k}
and
m_{1},\ldots,m_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}
be such that
g=\prod_{i=1}^{r}(\lambda-\gamma_{i})^{m_{i}}
.
Then

g^{\prime}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}(\lambda-\gamma_{i})^{m_{i}-1}\cdot\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\Big((\lambda^{\prime}-\gamma_{i}^{\prime})\prod_{j\neq i}(\lambda-\gamma_{j})\Big)\right).

.

Write

, where

. Then

, which is a contradiction. Therefore,

and

is a solution of
Equation

5.1
for

containing two distinct solutions of

. By

5.1
, splitting fields of

over

.

6.
Splitting fields with transcendence degree
1

In
Example

4.2
, we have seen that for derivations of the form
d=d_{(u,v)}+\partial_{au}
where
a\in k
, the transcendence degree of splitting field is at most one. We will further analyze these derivations to have the transcendence degree equal to
1
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
(Q,d)
be a differential quaternion algebra where
d=d_{(u,v)}+\partial_{au}
for
a\in k
.
Then
(Q,d)
is split by a finite extension of
(k,\,^{\prime})
, if and only if

a\in\left\{\frac{\theta^{\prime}}{n\xi\theta}\mid\theta\in k(\xi),n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

Proof.

By
Proposition

4.1
,
(Q,d)
is split by a finite extension
(L,\,^{\prime})
of
(k,\,^{\prime})
if and only if

L
contains a solution of the equation

(6.1)

X^{\prime}=\left(a\xi+\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{4\beta}\right)X,

that is, if and only if

L(\sqrt[4]{\beta})
contains a solution of the equation
X^{\prime}=a\xi X
.
Let
\lambda\in\overline{k(\xi)}
be a solution of
Equation

6.1
and
\theta=N_{k(\xi)(\lambda)/k(\xi)}(\lambda)
.
For
n=[k(\xi)(\lambda):k(\xi)]
, we have
\theta^{\prime}=na\xi\theta
.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

For
\theta\in k(\xi)
,
\frac{\theta^{\prime}}{\theta}\in\xi k
if and only if
N_{k(\xi)/k}(\theta)\in C_{k}
.

Proof.

Let
\overline{\theta}
be the conjugate of
\theta
.
Note that
\overline{\theta}^{\prime}=\overline{\theta^{\prime}}
.
Then
\frac{\theta^{\prime}}{\theta}\in\xi k
if and only if

\frac{\theta^{\prime}}{\theta}=-\overline{\frac{\theta^{\prime}}{\theta}}
,
that is,
(N_{k(\xi)/k}(\theta))^{\prime}=\theta^{\prime}\cdot\overline{\theta}+\theta\cdot(\overline{\theta})^{\prime}=0
.
∎

Remark 6.3
.

To describe the set of all
a\in k
such that
(Q,d)
is split by a finite extension of
(k,\,^{\prime})
we need to find the set
S=\{\theta\in k(\xi)\setminus k\mid N_{k(\xi)/k}(\theta)\in C_{k}\}
.
Consider the non-empty subset
\tilde{S}=\{\theta\in k(\xi)\setminus k\mid N_{k(\xi)/k}(\theta)\in{C}^{\times 2}_{k}\}
of
S
.
By Hilbert’s Theorem 90
[
2
, Example 2.3.4]
, we have

\tilde{S}=C_{k}\cdot\left\{\frac{\gamma}{\overline{\gamma}}\mid\gamma\in{k(\xi)}^{\times}\right\}.

Example 6.4
.

Let
(k,\,^{\prime})
be the differential field where
k=\mathbb{Q}(t)
and
t^{\prime}=1
.
Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
be an odd degree polynomial.
Then

S=\tilde{S}
as in
Remark

6.3
.
For the derivation
d=d_{(u,v)}+\partial_{au}
where

,
(Q,d)

if and only if

a\in\left\{\frac{\theta^{\prime}}{n\xi\theta}\mid\theta\in\tilde{S},n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
(k,\,^{\prime})
be the differential field where
k=\mathbb{Q}(t)
and
t^{\prime}=1
.
Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
be an odd degree polynomial.
Consider the quaternion algebra
Q=(\alpha,\beta)
with derivation
d=d_{(u,v)}+\partial_{au}
where
a=\frac{f}{g}\in\mathbb{Q}(t)
.
Then
(Q,d)
is not split by any algebraic extension of
k
, if

(a)

deg(g)-deg(f)<\frac{deg(\alpha)+3}{2}
.

(b)

for a factor
h
of
\alpha
,
h^{2}
divides
g
.

Proof.

Suppose
(Q,d)
is split by an algebraic extension of
k
.
Then, by
Example

6.4
, there exists
\theta\in\tilde{S}
such that
a=\frac{\theta^{\prime}}{n\xi\theta}
.
It follows from
Remark

6.3
that

\theta=c\frac{\gamma_{0}+\xi\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{0}-\xi\gamma_{1}}

where
c\in\mathbb{Q}
and
\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
are coprime polynomials.
Observe that

\frac{\theta^{\prime}}{n\xi\theta}=\frac{2\alpha(\gamma_{0}\gamma_{1}^{\prime}-\gamma_{0}^{\prime}\gamma_{1})+\alpha^{\prime}\gamma_{0}\gamma_{1}}{n\alpha(\gamma_{0}^{2}-\alpha\gamma_{1}^{2})}\in\mathbb{Q}(t).

Set
p=2\alpha(\gamma_{0}\gamma_{1}^{\prime}-\gamma_{0}^{\prime}\gamma_{1})+\alpha^{\prime}\gamma_{0}\gamma_{1}
and
q=n\alpha(\gamma_{0}^{2}-\alpha\gamma_{1}^{2})
.

(a)

Let
m=deg(\alpha),m_{0}=deg(\gamma_{0})
and
m_{1}=deg(\gamma_{1})
.
We have
deg(p)\leq m+m_{0}+m_{1}-1
and
deg(q)=m+\max(2m_{0},2m_{1}+m)

and calculating further, we get that

deg(q)-deg(p)\geq\frac{m+3}{2}
.

(b)

Let
h\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
be an irreducible factor of
\alpha
.
Write
\alpha=h\tilde{\alpha}
with
\tilde{\alpha}\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
.
If
h\nmid\gamma_{0}
then
h^{2}\nmid q
, whereby
h^{2}\nmid g
.
If
h\mid\gamma_{0}
then
h^{2}|q
,
h^{3}\nmid q
and
h|p
, whereby
h^{2}\nmid g
.

∎

7.
Standard derivations

Let
(k,\,^{\prime})
be a differential field and let
(Q,d)
be a differential quaternion algebra over
(k,\,^{\prime})
.
Recall that
d
is a standard derivation on
Q
if there exist
u,v\in Q^{0}
such that
u^{2},v^{2}\in{k}^{\times},uv=-vu,d(u)\in k(u)
and
d(v)\in k(v)
.
For a differential field extension
(L,\,^{\prime})
over
(k,\,^{\prime})
, we say that
d
becomes standard over
L
if
d^{\ast}=d\otimes\,^{\prime}
is standard derivation on
Q\otimes_{k}L
.
A quaternion algebra with standard derivation is split by a finite field extension of degree at most
8
(see
[
8
, Theorem 4.1]
), but the following example shows that the converse need not be true.

Example 7.1
.

Let
(k,\,^{\prime})
be the differential field where
k=\mathbb{Q}(t)
and
t^{\prime}=1
.
Let
Q=(1,t)_{k}
with
u,v\in Q
such that
u^{2}=1
,
v^{2}=t
and
vu=-uv
.
Consider the derivation
d=d_{(u,v)}+\partial_{-\frac{1}{8t}u}
on
Q
.
Then Equations (
4.6
) and (
4.7
) will become
X^{\prime}=\frac{1}{4t}X
and
Y^{\prime}=-\frac{1}{8t}Y
, respectively, and it follows
from
Proposition

4.1
that a field extension
(L,\,^{\prime})
splits
(Q,d)
if and only if
L
contains solutions of these equations, that is, if and only if
L
contains
k(\sqrt[8]{t})
.
Thus,
[L:k]\geq 8
.

We now show that
d
is not a standard derivation on
Q
.
Let
\tilde{u}=a_{1}u+a_{2}v+a_{3}uv
such that
{\tilde{u}}^{2}\in{k}^{\times}
and
d(\tilde{u})\in k\tilde{u}
.
Then
d(\tilde{u})\in k\tilde{u}
implies that
d(\tilde{u})=\frac{N(\tilde{u})^{\prime}}{2N(\tilde{u})}\tilde{u}
and we get the following equations:

(7.1)

\displaystyle\frac{N(\tilde{u})^{\prime}}{2N(\tilde{u})}a_{1}

\displaystyle=a_{1}^{\prime}

(7.2)

\displaystyle\frac{N(\tilde{u})^{\prime}}{2N(\tilde{u})}a_{2}

\displaystyle=a_{2}^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2t}a_{2}+\frac{a_{3}}{4t}

(7.3)

\displaystyle\frac{N(\tilde{u})^{\prime}}{2N(\tilde{u})}a_{3}

\displaystyle=a_{3}^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2t}a_{3}+\frac{a_{2}}{4t}

Note that
a_{2}=0
if and only if
a_{3}=0
.
Suppose that
a_{2},a_{3}\in{k}^{\times}
.
From Equations (
7.2
) and (
7.3
), we obtain that
\frac{a_{2}}{a_{3}}
is a solution of the Riccati Equation
X^{\prime}=\frac{1}{4t}(X^{2}-1)
.
Note that
\pm 1
are solutions of this equation and using separation of variables method, we observe that the general solution other than
\pm 1
is
X=\frac{1+c\sqrt{t}}{1-c\sqrt{t}}
with
c^{\prime}=0
.
Since
a_{2},a_{3}\in k
, we get that
\frac{a_{2}}{a_{3}}=\pm 1
.
Thus
N(\tilde{u})=a_{1}^{2}\neq 0
, whereby
a_{1}\in{k}^{\times}
.
If
a_{2}=a_{3}
, it follows from Equations (
7.1
) and (
7.2
) that
\frac{a_{1}}{a_{2}}
is a solution of the linear equation
Y^{\prime}=\frac{3}{4t}Y
and hence
\frac{a_{1}}{a_{2}}
is a constant multiple of
t^{\frac{3}{4}}
, which is a contradiction.
Similarly, if
a_{2}=-a_{3}
, we get that
\frac{a_{1}}{a_{2}}
is a solution of
Y^{\prime}=\frac{1}{4t}Y
, whereby
\frac{a_{1}}{a_{2}}
is a constant multiple of
t^{\frac{1}{4}}
, which is again a contradiction.
Thus
a_{2}=a_{3}=0
, and
\tilde{u}\in ku
.

Thus any
\tilde{u}\in Q^{0}
such that
d(\tilde{u})\in k\tilde{u}
is an element of
ku
.
Since any pair of such elements cannot generate
Q
over
k
,

d
is not a standard derivation on
Q
.

Now, we show that a derivation on a quaternion algebra becomes standard over its differential splitting fields.

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
(Q,d)
be a differential quaternion algebra over
(k,\,^{\prime})
and let
(L,\,^{\prime})
be a differential splitting field. Then
d
becomes standard over
L
.

Proof.

Since
(Q\otimes_{k}L,d^{*})\simeq(M_{2}(L),d_{P})
for a trace zero matrix
P
, we have
(M_{2}(L),d_{P})\simeq(M_{2}(L),\,^{\prime})
.
By
Proposition

2.1
, there exists
F\in GL_{2}(L)
such that
F^{\prime}=PF
.
By
Proposition

4.1
,
L
contains a solution of
Equation

4.7
, say
\theta
.
Set

U=F\left(\begin{matrix}\theta&0\\
0&-\theta\end{matrix}\right)F^{-1}\mbox{and }V=F\left(\begin{matrix}0&\theta\\
\theta&0\end{matrix}\right)F^{-1}.

Then
U^{2}=V^{2}=\theta^{2}
,
VU=-UV
,
d_{P}(U)=\frac{\theta^{\prime}}{\theta}U
and
d_{P}(V)=\frac{\theta^{\prime}}{\theta}V
. Let
\tilde{u}
and
\tilde{v}
be pre-images of
U
and
V
respectively, in
Q\otimes_{k}L
. Then
\tilde{u}
and
\tilde{v}
are generators of
Q\otimes_{k}L
with
d^{*}(\tilde{u})=\frac{\theta^{\prime}}{\theta}\tilde{u}
and
d^{*}(\tilde{v})=\frac{\theta^{\prime}}{\theta}\tilde{v}
. Thus,
(Q\otimes_{k}L,d^{*})=(Q\otimes_{k}L,d_{(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})})
. ∎

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
(Q,d)
be a differential quaternion algebra over
(k,\,^{\prime})
.
Then
d
becomes standard over a finite extension of
k
if and only if
(Q,d)
is split by a finite extension of
k
.

Proof.

Suppose
d
becomes standard over a finite extension of
k
, then using
Theorem

2.2
(
1
) we get that
(Q,d)
is split by a finite extension of
k
.

Conversely, let
(Q,d)
be split by a finite extension
(L,\,^{\prime})
of
(k,\,^{\prime})
. Then by
Proposition

7.2
,
d
becomes standard over
L
.
∎

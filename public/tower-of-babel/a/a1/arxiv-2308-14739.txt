Sharper dimension-free bounds on the Frobenius distance between sample covariance and its expectation
Nikita Puchkin, Fedor Noskov, Vladimir Spokoiny

Abstract
We study properties of a sample covariance estimate Σ ^ \widehat{\Sigma} given a finite sample of n n i.i.d. centered random elements in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} with the covariance matrix Σ \Sigma . We derive dimension-free bounds on the squared Frobenius norm of ( Σ ^ − Σ ) (\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma) under reasonable assumptions. For instance, we show that ‖ Σ ^ − Σ ‖ F 2 \smash{\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{\rm F}^{2}} differs from its expectation by at most 𝒪 ⁡ ( Tr ⁡ ( Σ 2 ) / n ) \smash{\mathcal{O}({\rm{Tr}}(\Sigma^{2})/n)} with overwhelming probability, which is a significant improvement over the existing results. This allows us to establish the concentration phenomenon for the squared Frobenius distance between the covariance and its empirical counterpart in the case of moderately large effective rank of Σ \Sigma .



1
Introduction

Covariance estimation is one of classical topics in multivariate statistics with applications in plenty of areas, including signal processing
(
Krim and Viberg 1996
;
Haghighatshoar and Caire 2018
)
, bioinformatics
(
Xie and Bentler 2003
;
Schäfer and Strimmer 2005
;
Hero and Rajaratnam 2012
)
, image analysis
(
Dahmen et al. 2000
;
Zhang and Schneider 2010
)
, and finance
(
Ledoit and Wolf 2003
;
Holtz 2010
;
Bai and Shi 2011
;
Fan et al. 2015
)
. Let
{\bf X},{\bf X}_{1},\dots,{\bf X}_{n}
be i.i.d. centered random vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with a covariance matrix
\mathbb{E}{\bf X}{\bf X}^{\top}=\Sigma
. In the present paper, we study properties of the most natural estimator of
\Sigma
, namely, the sample covariance, which is defined by the formula

\widehat{\Sigma}=\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{\bf X}_{i}{\bf X}_{i}^{\top}.

The question of how well
\widehat{\Sigma}
approximates
\Sigma
was extensively studied in the last decades. For instance, it arose in the paper of
Kannan et al. 1997
in the context of volume estimation. The authors used the sample covariance to bring a convex body
{\mathcal{K}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
into a nearly isotropic position in an intermediate step of their algorithm. Assuming that
{\bf X}_{1},\dots,{\bf X}_{n}
are uniformly distributed on
{\mathcal{K}}
,
Kannan et al. 1997
showed that, for any
\delta\in(0,1)
, the operator norm of
(\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma)
is of order
{\mathcal{O}}(d/(\delta\sqrt{n}))
with probability at least
(1-\delta)
(see their proof of Theorem 5.11). This result was improved in a series of works
(
Bourgain 1999
;
Rudelson 1999
;
Giannopoulos et al. 2005
;
Paouris 2006
;
Adamczak et al. 2010
)
until
Adamczak et al. 2011
obtained that, if the sample size
n
is large enough, then

\left\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\right\|\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}

with overwhelming probability
(1-\exp\{-{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{d})\})
for a large class of distributions, including the log-concave ones and the measures satisfying the Poincaré inequality. Here and further in this paper, the relation
\lesssim
stands for inequality up to a positive multiplicative constant (see the notation section below). A bit sharper bound can be derived for random vectors with lighter tails. For example, in
(
Vershynin 2012b
, Corollary 5.50)
, the author showed that, if
{\bf X}_{1},\dots,{\bf X}_{n}
are sub-Gaussian, then

\left\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\right\|\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{d+\log(1/\delta)}{n}}\vee\frac{d+\log(1/\delta)}{n}

(1)

with probability at least
(1-\delta)
. Here and further in this paper,
(a\vee b)
stands for
\max\{a,b\}
. The inequality (
1
), in particular, yields that the Frobenius norm of
(\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma)
is of order
{\mathcal{O}}(d/\sqrt{n})
with high probability.

The bound (
1
) cannot be improved in the worst-case scenario. If the population covariance
\Sigma
has
d
large eigenvalues, then the number of parameters to be estimated is
\Omega(d^{2})
. This means that the performance of
\widehat{\Sigma}
suffers from the curse of dimensionality. In
Tropp 2012
and
Vershynin 2012b
, the authors noticed that if the distribution of
{\bf X}_{1},\dots,{\bf X}_{n}
is supported on a Euclidean ball of radius
R
centered at the origin, then the following inequality holds with high probability:

\left\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\right\|\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{R\|\Sigma\|\log(d/\delta)}{n}}\vee\frac{R\log(d/\delta)}{n}.

(2)

We see that the finite support assumption almost eliminates the dependence on
d
. However, we still face problems when the dimension becomes exponentially large compared to the sample size
n
. Fortunately,
Vershynin 2012b
noticed that if the data lies near a low-dimensional subspace (which is often the case in high-dimensional tasks), then the rate of convergence of the sample covariance matrix depends on the intrinsic dimension, characterized by the effective rank

\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)=\frac{{\rm Tr}(\Sigma)}{\|\Sigma\|}.

In
Adamczak 2015
and
Koltchinskii and Lounici 2017
, the authors proved a dimension-free version of (
1
) in the Gaussian setup:

\left\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\right\|\lesssim\|\Sigma\|\left(\sqrt{\frac{\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)+\log(1/\delta)}{n}}\vee\frac{\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)+\log(1/\delta)}{n}\right).

(3)

As mentioned in
(
Koltchinskii and Lounici 2017
, Theorem 9)
, a similar inequality holds for a broad class of sub-Gaussian distributions
satisfying the
\psi_{2}
-
L_{2}
-equivalence condition.
The word “dimension-free” means that the right-hand side of (
3
) depends only on the operator norm and effective rank of
\Sigma
. Hence, the bound (
3
) still makes sense even if the ambient dimension
d
is huge. Later, this result was extended in the papers of
Vershynin 2018
,
Zhivotovskiy 2024
, and
Han 2022
. In
(
Zhivotovskiy 2024
, Theorem 3)
, the author also obtained a dimension-free version of
(
Adamczak et al. 2010
, Theorem 4.1)
for the log-concave case. A bit earlier,
Bunea and Xiao 2015
studied the behaviour of the Frobenius norm of
(\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma)
under the same
\psi_{2}
-
L_{2}
-equivalence assumption. They proved that (see
(
Bunea and Xiao 2015
, Proposition A.3)
)

\left\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}\lesssim\|\Sigma\|\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log(2/\delta)}{n}}\vee\frac{\log(2/\delta)}{n}\right)

(4)

with probability at least
(1-\delta)
. We would like to note that the low effective rank assumption is not the only way to escape the curse of dimensionality. There are a lot of papers devoted to the problem of covariance estimation under
structural assumptions (for example, sparsity
(
Bickel and Levina 2008a
;
Cai and Zhou 2012
;
Fan et al. 2015
)
, Kronecker product structure
(
Tsiligkaridis and Hero 2013
;
Leng and Pan 2018
)
, bandable
(
Bickel and Levina 2008b
;
Cai et al. 2010
)
or Toeplitz
(
Xiao and Wu 2012
;
Cai et al. 2013
)
matrix
\Sigma
to mention a few). This goes beyond the scope of the present paper. A reader is referred to the comprehensive survey
(
Cai et al. 2016
)
on this subject. We consider the situation when
\Sigma
has no other properties but a small effective rank.

The aforementioned works illustrate how rapidly the topic of covariance estimation evolved in the last decade. Recently developed advanced techniques allowed statisticians to examine more challenging setups, like those with missing observations
(
Lounici 2014
;
Lounici and Pacreau 2023
;
Abdalla 2023
)
, heavy tails
(
Vershynin 2012a
;
Srivastava and Vershynin 2013
;
Youssef 2013
;
Tikhomirov 2018
;
Mendelson and Zhivotovskiy 2020
;
Abdalla and Zhivotovskiy 2022
)
and adversarial contamination
(
Abdalla and Zhivotovskiy 2022
;
Minasyan and Zhivotovskiy 2023
)
. We do not pursue the goal of pushing these results even further. In contrast, we use our technical findings to discover subtle effects that have not been noticed in the classical sub-Gaussian setting so far.

Contribution.
In the present paper, we study properties of the squared Frobenuis distance between the sample covariance and its expectation, which received less attention compared to the operator norm of
(\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma)
. The state-of-the-art result (
4
) of
Bunea and Xiao 2015
implies that

\left\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}\lesssim\frac{\|\Sigma\|^{2}\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)^{2}\log(2/\delta)}{n}

with high probability in the case of moderate confidence level
\delta
(that is,
\log(1/\delta)\lesssim n
). This bound exhibits the correct dependence on the operator norm and on the effective rank of
\Sigma
. For instance, if
{\bf X}_{1},\dots,{\bf X}_{n}
have the Gaussian distribution
{\mathcal{N}}({\bf 0},\Sigma)
, then

\mathbb{E}\left\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}=\frac{({\rm Tr}(\Sigma))^{2}+{\rm Tr}(\Sigma^{2})}{n},

and, according to the Paley-Zygmund inequality,
\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}
is not smaller than
\big[({\rm Tr}(\Sigma))^{2}+{\rm Tr}(\Sigma^{2})\big]/(2n)
on an event of positive probability.
However, the upper bound
{\mathcal{O}}(({\rm Tr}(\Sigma))^{2}/n)
becomes suboptimal for some distributions, if we are speaking of the difference

\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}-\mathbb{E}\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}
. A thorough analysis of higher-order derivatives of the cumulant generating function

\varphi(U)=\log\mathbb{E}e^{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}U\boldsymbol{\xi}},\quad\text{where}\quad\boldsymbol{\xi}=\Sigma^{-1/2}{\bf X},

(5)

allows us to establish a dimension-free high-probability upper bound

\left|\left\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}-\mathbb{E}\left\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}\right|\lesssim\frac{\|\Sigma\|^{2}}{n}\max\left\{\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{2})\sqrt{\log(8/\delta)},\log(8/\delta)\right\},\quad\text{where}\quad\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{2})=\frac{{\rm Tr}(\Sigma^{2})}{\|\Sigma\|^{2}},

which holds for a large subclass of sub-Gaussian distributions (see Assumption
2.5
and Proposition
2.2
below), arbitrary
\delta\in(0,1)
, such that
\log(1/\delta)\lesssim n/\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)^{2}
, and
n\gtrsim\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)^{6}
(see Theorem
2.4
and Theorem
2.6
). We would like to note that
\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{2})
, also referred to as stable rank of
\Sigma
, is always not greater than
\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)^{2}
and sometimes it can be significantly smaller than the squared effective rank. As a consequence, we obtain the following bound on
\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}
with a sharp constant in a high-dimensional setting (Corollary
2.7
):

\left|\frac{\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}}{\mathbb{E}\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}}-1\right|\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{\log(8/\delta)}}{\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)-1}={\mathcal{O}}\left(\frac{1}{\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)}\right),\quad\mathtt{r}(\Sigma),\,n/\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)^{6}\rightarrow\infty.

Motivation.
Our results on concentration of
\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}
around its expectation have direct implications in hypothesis testing and construction of confidence bands. The squared Frobenius norm is a popular tool for construction of statistical criterions for testing a simple hypothesis or two-sample tests (see, for instance,
(
Ledoit and Wolf 2002
;
Song Xi Chen and Zhong 2010
;
Li and Chen 2012
;
Han and Wu 2020
)
). The reason for that can be easily explained by Theorems
2.4
and
2.6
.
Assume that our goal is to check whether
\Sigma
is equal to a given matrix
\Sigma_{0}
. Suppose that there is
\omega>0
, such that the inequality

\left\|{\bf X}^{\top}V{\bf X}-{\rm Tr}(V\Sigma)\right\|_{\psi_{1}}\leqslant\omega\left\|\Sigma^{1/2}V\Sigma^{1/2}\right\|_{{\rm F}}

(6)

holds for all matrices
V\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
. Here
\|\cdot\|_{\psi_{1}}
stands for the Orlicz norm (see the definition (
7
) below). In Section
2
, we discuss examples of probability distributions satisfying (
6
). Under this condition,

\widehat{T}=\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|{\bf X}\|^{4}+\frac{1}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\big({\bf X}_{2i-1}^{\top}{\bf X}_{2i}\big)^{2}

is a consistent estimate
\widehat{T}
of
n\,\mathbb{E}\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}=\mathbb{E}\|{\bf X}\|^{4}-{\rm Tr}(\Sigma^{2})
. Then, under the null hypothesis
\mathcal{H}_{0}:\Sigma=\Sigma_{0}
, the statistic
\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma_{0}\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}-\widehat{T}/n
will be as large as
{\mathcal{O}}({\rm Tr}(\Sigma^{2})/n)
. On the other hand, if
\Sigma\neq\Sigma_{0}
, then we have

\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma_{0}\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}-\widehat{T}/n=\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}-\widehat{T}/n+2{\rm Tr}\left[(\Sigma-\Sigma_{0})(\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma)\right]+\|\Sigma-\Sigma_{0}\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}.

Due to (
6
), it holds that
\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}-\widehat{T}/n={\mathcal{O}}({\rm Tr}(\Sigma^{2})/n)
and

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\left[(\Sigma-\Sigma_{0})(\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left({\bf X}_{i}^{\top}(\Sigma-\Sigma_{0}){\bf X}_{i}-{\rm Tr}\left[(\Sigma-\Sigma_{0})\Sigma\right]\right)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{O}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\left\|{\bf X}^{\top}(\Sigma-\Sigma_{0}){\bf X}-{\rm Tr}\left[(\Sigma-\Sigma_{0})\Sigma\right]\right\|_{\psi_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{O}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\left\|\Sigma^{1/2}(\Sigma-\Sigma_{0})\Sigma^{1/2}\right\|_{{\rm F}}\right)

with high probability. Hence, the test statistic

\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma_{0}\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}-\widehat{T}/n
will be of order

\|\Sigma-\Sigma_{0}\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}-{\mathcal{O}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\left\|\Sigma^{1/2}(\Sigma-\Sigma_{0})\Sigma^{1/2}\right\|_{{\rm F}}\right)-{\mathcal{O}}\left(\frac{{\rm Tr}(\Sigma^{2})}{n}\right).

This leads to a detection boundary
\|\Sigma-\Sigma_{0}\|_{{\rm F}}\gtrsim\|\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}/\sqrt{n}
. This does not follow from the result of
Bunea and Xiao 2015
.

Notation.
Throughout the paper,
\|A\|_{{\rm F}}
and
\|A\|
stand for the Frobenius and the operator norm of
A
, respectively. If the matrix
A
is symmetric positive semidefinite and
A\neq O
, where
O
is the matrix with zero entries, we denote its effective rank by

\mathtt{r}(A)={\rm Tr}(A)/\|A\|.

We use the standard notation
(A\otimes B)
for the Kronecker product of matrices
A
and
B
. Its definition and some useful properties are listed in Appendix
A
. For any matrix
U\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times q}
with columns
{\bf u}_{1},\dots,{\bf u}_{q}
, the vectorization operator is given by
{\rm\bf vec}(U)=({\bf u}_{1}^{\top},\dots,{\bf u}_{q}^{\top})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{pq}
. Here and further in the paper, the bold font is reserved for vectors, while matrices and scalars are displayed in regular font. For a random variable
\eta
, its Orlicz
\psi_{s}
-norm,
s\geqslant 1
, is defined as

\|\eta\|_{\psi_{s}}=\inf\left\{t>0:\mathbb{E}e^{|\eta|^{s}/t^{s}}\leqslant 2\right\}.

(7)

The expressions
(a\vee b)
and
(a\wedge b)
denote
\max\{a,b\}
and
\min\{a,b\}
, respectively.
Sometimes, instead of the standard
{\mathcal{O}}
notation, we use
f\lesssim g
or
g\gtrsim f
, which means that there is a universal constant
c>0
, such that
f\leqslant cg
.
Finally,
{\mathcal{B}}({\bf x},R)
is the Euclidean ball of radius
R
centered at
{\bf x}
.

Paper structure.
The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we present our main results and discuss their implications. After that, we illustrate them with some numerical simulations in Section
3
. Section
4
is devoted to the proofs of the statements from Section
2
, some technical details are moved to Appendix.

2
Main results

In this section, we present rigorous statements of the main results of the paper. Let us start with a couple of auxiliary definitions. Throughout the paper,
\boldsymbol{\xi}=\Sigma^{-1/2}{\bf X}
stands for the whitened vector. We assume that the covariance matrix
\Sigma
is invertible for simplicity. In a general situation, one can always reduce the problem of covariance estimation to the non-degenerate case, adding tiny isotropic Gaussian noise to the observations. For a matrix
U\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, such that
\mathbb{E}e^{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}U\boldsymbol{\xi}}<\infty
, let
\mathsf{P}_{U}
denote a probability measure, defined as

{\rm d}\mathsf{P}_{U}({\bf x})=\frac{e^{{\bf x}^{\top}U{\bf x}}}{\mathbb{E}e^{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}U\boldsymbol{\xi}}}{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}({\bf x}),\quad\text{where}\quad\boldsymbol{\xi}=\Sigma^{-1/2}{\bf X}.

(8)

For any Borel function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times d}\rightarrow\mathbb{\mathbb{R}}
, the expression
\mathsf{P}_{U}f(\boldsymbol{\xi},U)
stands for the expectation of
f(\boldsymbol{\xi},U)
with respect to the measure
\mathsf{P}_{U}
:

\mathsf{P}_{U}f(\boldsymbol{\xi},U)=\frac{\mathbb{E}f(\boldsymbol{\xi},U)e^{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}U\boldsymbol{\xi}}}{\mathbb{E}e^{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}U\boldsymbol{\xi}}}.

(9)

As we mentioned in the introduction, our proof is based on the study of derivatives of the cumulant generating function
\varphi(U)
, defined in (
5
). They admit a nice representation in terms of the introduced functional
\mathsf{P}_{U}
. The only property we require is the regularity of the third and the fourth derivatives of
\varphi(U)
, which is guaranteed by the following assumption.

Assumption 2.1
.

There exist positive numbers
\tau
and
\rho_{\max}
such that the vector
\boldsymbol{\xi}=\Sigma^{-1/2}{\bf X}
satisfies the inequality

\mathsf{P}_{U}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-\mathsf{P}_{U}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}\right)^{4}\leqslant\tau^{2}\;\|V\|_{{\rm F}}^{4}

for all
U\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
such that
\|U\|_{{\rm F}}\leqslant\rho_{\max}
and all
V\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
.

To our knowledge, Assumption
2.1
has not been used in the literature on covariance estimation, so the natural question is what are the examples of random vectors
\boldsymbol{\xi}
satisfying this condition. We claim that Assumption
2.1
holds for a broad class of distributions and support our assertion with the next proposition.

Proposition 2.2
.

Assume that there exists
\omega>0
such that

\left\|\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-{\rm Tr}(V)\right\|_{\psi_{1}}\leqslant\omega\|V\|_{{\rm F}}\quad\text{for all $V\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$}.

(10)

Then the random vector
\boldsymbol{\xi}
satisfies Assumption
2.1
with
\tau=64\omega^{2}
and
\rho_{\max}=(6\omega)^{-1}
.

Proposition
2.2
immediately yields that Assumption
2.1
is fulfilled for all random vectors
\boldsymbol{\xi}
satisfying the Hanson-Wright inequality. For instance, this is the case for sub-Gaussian random vectors with independent entries (see, e.g.,
(
Hanson and Wright 1971
;
Rudelson and Vershynin 2013
)
). Less trivial examples are the distributions with the convex concentration property
(
Adamczak 2015
)
. The random vector
\boldsymbol{\xi}
is said to have the convex concentration property if there exists a positive constant
K
such that

\mathbb{P}\left(\left|g(\boldsymbol{\xi})-\mathbb{E}g(\boldsymbol{\xi})\right|\geqslant t\right)\leqslant 2e^{-t^{2}/K^{2}}

for any convex
1
-Lipschitz function
g
and any
t>0
. We would like to note that random vectors with strongly log-concave density or satisfying the log-Sobolev inequality have such property. It is also easy to verify that the distributions satisfying the Hanson-Wright inequality have the
\psi_{2}
-
L_{2}
-equivalence property. However, we would like to note that Assumption
2.1
(and, consequently, (
10
))
is a stronger condition than the one considered in
(
Bunea and Xiao 2015
)
. We support our claim with the following proposition.

Proposition 2.3
.

There exists a random vector
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying the following properties:

•

\mathbb{E}\boldsymbol{\xi}={\bf 0}
and
\mathbb{E}\boldsymbol{\xi}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}=I_{d}
;

•

for any
{\bf u}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
it holds that
\left\|\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}{\bf u}\right\|_{\psi_{2}}^{2}\leqslant 16\,\mathbb{E}\|\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}{\bf u}\|^{2}/3
;

•

there exists a matrix
V\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
such that

\mathbb{E}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-\mathbb{E}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}\right)^{4}=d^{2}\;\left\|V\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{4}.

The counterexample in Proposition
2.3
is quite straightforward. We take
\boldsymbol{\xi}
equal to a random vector with i.i.d. Rademacher entries multiplied by a bounded random variable. We postpone the proof of Proposition
2.3
to Section
4.2
and proceed with a high-probability upper bound on the Frobenius norm of
(\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma)
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Suppose that Assumption
2.1
holds with
\tau\geqslant 2
. Fix any
\delta\in(0,1)
and let

\mathtt{R}(\Sigma,\delta)=\frac{3\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)}{2\sqrt{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{2})}+2\sqrt{e\log(3/\delta)}.

Assume that the sample size
n
satisfies the inequalities

\|\Sigma\|\,\mathtt{R}(\Sigma,\delta)\leqslant\rho_{\max}\sqrt{n}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)^{2}\,\mathtt{R}(\Sigma,\delta)^{2}\left(\frac{\mathtt{R}(\Sigma,\delta)^{2}}{4}+3\right)\leqslant 36n.

(11)

Then, with probability at least
(1-\delta)
, it holds that

\left\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}-\mathbb{E}\left\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}<\frac{4\|\Sigma\|^{2}}{n}\max\left\{\sqrt{2\big(\tau\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{2})^{2}+\tau^{2}\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{4})\big)\log(3/\delta)},\;4e\tau\log(3/\delta)\right\}.

(12)

According to Lemma
4.5
below, under Assumption
2.1
, it holds that

n\;\mathbb{E}\left\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}\leqslant\big({\rm Tr}(\Sigma)\big)^{2}+(\tau-1)\;{\rm Tr}(\Sigma^{2}).

Hence, Theorem
2.4
yields that

\left\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}\lesssim\frac{\|\Sigma\|^{2}\left(\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)^{2}+\log(3/\delta)\right)}{n}

with probability at least
(1-\delta)
, resembling the result of
Bunea and Xiao 2015
with slightly better dependence on
\delta
(we have
\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)^{2}+\log(2/\delta)
, instead of
\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)^{2}\log(1/\delta)
, cf. (
4
)). However, as we show in Corollary
2.7
a bit later, the expression in the right-hand side of (
12
) may be much smaller than the expectation of
\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}
.

The result of Theorem
2.4
is also related to concentration of quadratic forms, because the squared Frobenius norm of
(\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma)
can be naturally represented as

n\left\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}={\rm\bf vec}(H)^{\top}\left(\Sigma\otimes\Sigma\right){\rm\bf vec}(H),

where
\otimes
stands for the Kronecker product of matrices and
{\rm\bf vec}(H)
is a random vector, obtained by reshaping of

H=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{i}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{i}^{\top}-I_{d}\right).

It is easy to observe that the matrix
H
may have sub-exponential dependent entries under Assumption
2.1
. At the same time, most of the papers studying the quadratic forms work with sub-Gaussian random vectors (see, for instance,
(
Laurent and Massart 2000
;
Hsu et al. 2012
;
Klochkov and Zhivotovskiy 2020
;
Spokoiny 2023
)
and the previously mentioned
(
Hanson and Wright 1971
;
Rudelson and Vershynin 2013
;
Adamczak 2015
)
).
The only exception we are aware of is the paper of
Sambale 2023
, where the author considers quadratic forms of sub-exponential random vectors with independent components. One may also consider
\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}
as a polynomial of a sub-Gaussian random vector of degree
4
. Unfortunately, the latest results on concentration of sub-Gaussian polynomials (see, for instance,
Schudy and Sviridenko 2012
;
Adamczak and Wolff 2015
;
Götze et al. 2021
) cannot recover the upper bound (
12
).

The final remark on Theorem
2.4
we want to make is that
\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}
should behave like a square of a sub-exponential random variable. However, in the right-hand side of (
12
), we have the standard sub-exponential tail
\sqrt{\log(2/\delta)}\vee\log(2/\delta)
, rather than
(\log(2/\delta))^{2}
. This is because the statement of Theorem
2.4
holds not for all
\delta
from the interval
(0,1)
but only for
\delta\gtrsim e^{-{\mathcal{O}}(n)}
, excluding the case of extremely high confidence. This is a consequence of our approach, relating the probability of large deviation of
\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}
with the analysis of the restricted exponential moment

\mathbb{E}\exp\left\{\frac{\lambda n}{2}\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}\right\}\mathbbm{1}\left(\sqrt{n}\left\|\Sigma^{1/2}(\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma)\Sigma^{1/2}\right\|_{{\rm F}}\leqslant\mathfrak{z}\right),

(13)

where
\mathfrak{z}
is an properly chosen constant (see Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
4.4
below). Since the expectation (
13
) may blow up as
\lambda>0
grows, we have introduce the technical requirements (
11
) to prevent this undesired event.

We proceed with a complementary lower bound on
\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}
. In contrast to the upper bound, we do not need the random vector
\boldsymbol{\xi}
to be sub-Gaussian anymore. The following will be sufficient for our purposes.

Assumption 2.5
.

There exists
\alpha>0
such that

\mathbb{E}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-\mathbb{E}\,\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}\right)^{4}\leqslant\alpha^{2}\;\|V\|_{{\rm F}}^{4}\quad\text{for all $V\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$.}

The reason for the significant relaxation of Assumption
2.1
is that in the proof of lower bounds we have to study the exponential moment
\mathbb{E}\exp\{-\lambda\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}\}
, which is finite for all
\lambda>0
whatever the distribution of
\boldsymbol{\xi}
is. As a result, the weaker Assumption
2.5
does not lead to worse dependence on the parameter
\delta
. A similar effect was observed in
(
Oliveira 2016
)
, where the author studied lower tails of the sample covariance.

Theorem 2.6
.

Grant Assumption
2.5
and let the sample size
n
be sufficiently large in a sense that

5\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{2})\left(3\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)^{2}+\frac{2\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{2})\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)^{2}}{\alpha}\right)\leqslant n\quad\text{and}\quad 96\left(1+\sqrt{\alpha}\right)^{2}\left(3\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)^{2}+\frac{2\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{2})\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)^{2}}{\alpha}\right)\leqslant\alpha n.

(14)

Then, for any
\delta\in(0,1)
, with probability at least
(1-\delta)
, it holds that

\left\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}-\mathbb{E}\left\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}>-\frac{4\|\Sigma\|^{2}}{n}\left(2\alpha\log(5/\delta)\vee\sqrt{2\mathfrak{R}(\Sigma)\log(5/\delta)}\right),

(15)

where

\mathfrak{R}(\Sigma)=\alpha\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{2})^{2}+\alpha^{2}\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{4})+\frac{5\alpha^{2}\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)^{2}}{2n}\left(3\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)^{2}+\frac{2\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{2})\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)^{2}}{\alpha}\right).

Theorems
2.4
and
2.6
imply that

\left|\left\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}-\mathbb{E}\left\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}\right|\lesssim\frac{\|\Sigma\|^{2}\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{2})}{n}

(16)

with high probability, provided that Assumption
2.1
is fulfilled. For instance, this is the case when
\boldsymbol{\xi}
is a Gaussian random vector. To ensure that the bound (
16
) is tight, it is enough to recall that the variance of
\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}
is of order
\|\Sigma\|^{4}\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{2})^{2}/n^{2}
in the Gaussian case. We can go even further examine the relative fluctuation of the squared Frobenius norm of
(\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma)
. Summing up the results of Theorem
2.4
and Theorem
2.6
and using the union bound, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 2.7
.

Grant Assumption
2.1
and let
\Sigma
,
\rho_{\max}
,
\tau\geqslant 2
, and
\delta\in(0,1)
(possibly depending on
n
) be such that

\frac{{\rm Tr}(\Sigma)^{2}\vee\log(8/\delta)}{n\rho_{\max}^{2}}\rightarrow 0,\quad\frac{\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)^{6}}{n}\rightarrow 0,\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)\log(8/\delta)}{\sqrt{n}}\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as $n\rightarrow\infty$.}

(17)

Then, with probability at least
(1-\delta)
, it holds that

\left|\frac{\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}}{\mathbb{E}\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}}-1\right|\lesssim\tau\max\left\{\frac{\sqrt{\log(8/\delta)}}{\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)-1},\frac{\log(8/\delta)}{\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)\big(\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)-1\big)}\right\},

(18)

where
\lesssim
stands for inequality up to an absolute constant.

Corollary
2.7
establishes that the ratio
\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}/\mathbb{E}\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}
is close to
1
, when the effective rank of
\Sigma
is large and
n/\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)^{6}\rightarrow\infty
as
n
tends to infinity. Though its proof is straightforward, we postpone it to Section
4.5
and focus on discussion of the result. First, we would like to emphasize that Assumption
2.1
plays a crucial role in this phenomenon. Our numerical experiments, reported in Section
3
, show the absence of concentration when we take the random vector
\boldsymbol{\xi}
as described in the proof of Proposition
2.3
. Second, one can apply Corollary
2.7
to bound the fluctuation of

\left\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}-\mathbb{E}\left\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}.

Theorem 2.8
.

Assume the conditions of Corollary
2.7
. In addition, suppose that
\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)/\sqrt{\log(8/\delta)}\rightarrow\infty
and
\tau\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)/n\rightarrow 0
as
n
approaches infinity. Then, with probability at least
(1-\delta)
, it holds that

\left|\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}-\mathbb{E}\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}\right|\lesssim\tau\|\Sigma\|\sqrt{\left(1\vee\frac{\tau}{\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)}\right)\cdot\frac{\log(8/\delta)}{n}},\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

As before,
\lesssim
denotes inequality up to an absolute constant.

The proof of Theorem
2.8
is moved to Section
4.6
. It is known that, if
\boldsymbol{\xi}\sim{\mathcal{N}}({\bf 0},I_{d})
is a standard Gaussian vector in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then the difference
\|\boldsymbol{\xi}\|^{2}-\mathbb{E}\|\boldsymbol{\xi}\|^{2}
is of order
\sqrt{d}
while
\big|\|\boldsymbol{\xi}\|-\mathbb{E}\|\boldsymbol{\xi}\|\big|\lesssim 1
. We observe a similar phenomenon for the Frobenius norm of
(\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma)
. Indeed, one can take
\tau={\mathcal{O}}(1)
in the Gaussian case. Then, according to Theorem
2.8
, it holds that

\left|\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}-\mathbb{E}\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}\right|\lesssim\|\Sigma\|\sqrt{\frac{\log(8/\delta)}{n}}

with probability at least
(1-\delta)
even if
\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)
is large. On the other hand, Theorems
2.4
and
2.6
only guarantee that

\left|\left\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}-\mathbb{E}\left\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}\right|\lesssim\frac{\|\Sigma\|^{2}}{n}\max\left\{\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{2})\sqrt{\log(8/\delta)},\log(8/\delta)\right\},

where the right-hand side is of order
d/n
in the worst-case scenario
\Sigma=I_{d}
.

3
Experiments

In this section, we illustrate the concentration phenomenon discussed in Section
2
with numerical simulations. Our goal is to observe that the ratio
\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}/\mathbb{E}\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}
concentrates around
1
when
\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)
tends to infinity, as guaranteed by (
18
).

Figure 1:
concentration of
\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}/\mathbb{E}\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}
around 1 as
{\tt r}(\Sigma)
tends to infinity.

Let us describe the process of population covariance generation.
We set the ambient dimension
d
equal to
50
. Then, for each
t\in[0,1]
, we define the covariance matrix
\Sigma_{t}=U\Lambda_{t}U^{\top},t\in[0,1]
, where
U
is a drawn from the uniform distribution on the orthogonal group
\mathbb{O}(d)
and
\Lambda_{t}
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\Lambda_{t}=\begin{cases}{\rm diag}\left(1,2t\left(1-\frac{1}{d}\right),2t\left(1-\frac{2}{d}\right),\ldots,\frac{2t}{d}\right)&\text{ if }t\in[0,0.5];\\
{\rm diag}\left(1,(1-1/d)^{2(1-t)},(1-2/d)^{2(1-t)},\dots,(1/d)^{2(1-t)}\right),&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

In our simulations, we take
t
from the grid
{\mathcal{T}}=\{0,1/69,2/69;\ldots,1\}
, so the effective ranks
\mathtt{r}(\Sigma_{t})
,
t\in{\mathcal{T}}
, are spread regularly over the segment
[1,d]
.

The simulation process goes as follows.
For each
\Sigma=U\Lambda U^{\top}\in\{\Sigma_{t}:t\in{\mathcal{T}}\}
, we consider two different distribution laws of
{\bf X}_{1},\ldots,{\bf X}_{n}
.

1.

The first variant is
{\bf X}=U\Lambda^{1/2}\boldsymbol{\xi}/\sigma
, where
\boldsymbol{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d})^{\top}
is a random vectors with independent entries drawn from the
truncated Laplace
distribution with the density

\displaystyle\mathsf{p}(x)=\frac{1}{Z}e^{-|x|}\mathbbm{1}(|x|\leqslant 6),

where
Z
is the normalizing constant. The constant
\sigma>0
is chosen is a way to ensure that
\mathbb{E}{\bf X}{\bf X}^{\top}=\Sigma
:

\sigma^{2}=\int\limits_{-6}^{6}x^{2}\mathsf{p}(x){\rm d}x.

2.

The second case we consider is
{\bf X}=\sqrt{d}U\Lambda^{1/2}\boldsymbol{\xi}
, where
\boldsymbol{\xi}
has the uniform distribution on the unit sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
.

Using numerical integration, we calculate
\mathbb{E}\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma_{t}\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}
for the truncated Laplace distribution with a reasonable accuracy:

\displaystyle n\mathbb{E}\left\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma_{t}\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}=\mathbb{E}\left\|{\bf X}{\bf X}^{\top}-\Sigma_{t}\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}=({\rm Tr}(\Lambda_{t}))^{2}+\left(K-2\right){\rm Tr}(\Lambda_{t}^{2}),

where
K
is the kurtosis of the truncated Laplace distribution, defined as

K=\frac{1}{\sigma^{4}}\int\limits_{-6}^{6}x^{4}\mathsf{p}(x){\rm d}x.

Similarly, we compute
\mathbb{E}\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma_{t}\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}
for the uniform distribution on the unit sphere:

\displaystyle n\mathbb{E}\left\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma_{t}\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}=\mathbb{E}\left\|{\bf X}{\bf X}^{\top}-\Sigma_{t}\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}=\frac{d}{d+2}\left({\rm Tr}(\Lambda_{t})\right)^{2}+\frac{d-2}{d+2}\,{\rm Tr}(\Lambda^{2}_{t}).

Note that both distributions fulfill the condition of Proposition
2.2
since in the former case
\boldsymbol{\xi}
is a vector of independent bounded random variables, and in the second case
\boldsymbol{\xi}
satisfies the logarithmic Sobolev inequality (see, for instance,
(
Bakry et al. 2014
, Theorem 5.7.4)
). Consequently, they admit concentration bounds provided in Theorem
2.4
and Theorem
2.6
.

For each
n\in\{10,50,100,1000\}
,
t\in{\mathcal{T}}=\{0,1/69,2/69;\ldots,1\}
and both distributions, we generate
5000
samples
({\bf X}_{1}^{j,t},\ldots,{\bf X}_{n}^{j,t})
,
j=1,\ldots,5000
, of size
n
. Next, we compute

\displaystyle a_{j,t}:=\frac{\|\widehat{\Sigma}_{j,t}-\Sigma_{t}\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}}{\mathbb{E}\|\widehat{\Sigma}_{j,t}-\Sigma_{t}\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}},

where
\widehat{\Sigma}_{j,t}
is the empirical covariance matrix based on
{\bf X}_{1}^{j,t},\ldots,{\bf X}^{j,t}_{n}
. For a fixed
t\in{\mathcal{T}}
, we calculate the width
w_{t}
of the empirical
0.95
-confidence interval for
\{a_{j,t}:1\leqslant j\leqslant 5000\}
.

Finally, we plot the dependence of
\log_{2}(w_{t})
on
\mathtt{r}(\Sigma_{t})
. The results are displayed in Figure
1
. We observe that the width of the interval goes to zero as
\mathtt{r}(\Sigma_{t})
increases.

Next, we support the necessity of Assumption
2.1
with numerical experiments. Proposition
2.3
states that there exists a random vector
\boldsymbol{\xi}
with unit covariance that satisfies
\psi_{2}
-
L_{2}
-equivalence but violates Assumption

. The desired random vector

is explicitly constructed in the proof of Proposition

. To show that it does not admit the studied concentration phenomenon, we apply the same framework as before in this section. For each

and the corresponding covariance matrix
\Sigma_{t}=U\Lambda_{t}U^{\top}

{\bf X}=U\Lambda_{t}^{1/2}\boldsymbol{\xi}
. Then, the sample covariance matrix satisfies

\displaystyle n\mathbb{E}\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma_{t}\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}=\mathbb{E}\|{\bf X}{\bf X}^{\top}-\Sigma_{t}\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}=2{\rm Tr}^{2}(\Lambda_{t})-{\rm Tr}(\Lambda_{t}^{2}).

As before, for each
n\in\{10,50,100,1000\}

0.95

4.1
Proof of Proposition
2.2

The key step of our proof is an upper bound the Orlicz norm of
(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-{\rm Tr}(V))
with respect to the measure
\mathsf{P}_{U}
, defined in (
8
) and (
9
). Similarly to the standard Orlicz
\psi_{1}
-norm, we define

\|f(\boldsymbol{\xi})\|_{\psi_{1}(\mathsf{P}_{U})}=\inf\left\{t>0:\mathsf{P}_{U}e^{|f(\boldsymbol{\xi})|/t}\leqslant 2\right\}

for any function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and any
U\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
such that
\mathbb{E}\exp\{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}U\boldsymbol{\xi}\}<\infty
. The
\psi_{1}(\mathsf{P}_{U})
-norm is just the Orlicz norm with respect to a different probability measure, and hence, it inherits all the properties of the usual
\psi_{1}
-norm. For instance, analogously to the bound on moments of sub-exponential random variables (see, e.g.,
(
Zhivotovskiy 2024
, proof of Lemma 2, eq. (10))
), we have

\mathsf{P}_{U}|f(\boldsymbol{\xi})|^{k}\leqslant 2\,\Gamma(k+1)\,\|f(\boldsymbol{\xi})\|_{\psi_{1}(\mathsf{P}_{U})}^{k}\quad\text{for all $k\in\mathbb{N}$.}

(19)

If we manage to obtain an upper bound on

\left\|\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-{\rm Tr}(V)\right\|_{\psi_{1}(\mathsf{P}_{U})},

then it is straightforward to confine

\mathsf{P}_{U}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-{\rm Tr}(V)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathsf{P}_{U}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-{\rm Tr}(V)\right)^{4}.

After that, the claim of the proposition follows immediately from the triangle inequality:

\left[\mathsf{P}_{U}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-\mathsf{P}_{U}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}\right)^{4}\right]^{1/4}\leqslant\left[\mathsf{P}_{U}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-{\rm Tr}(V)\right)^{4}\right]^{1/4}+\left|\mathsf{P}_{U}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-{\rm Tr}(V)\right|.

For convenience, we split the rest of the proof into several steps.

Step 1: a bound on the Orlicz norm.
We are going to show that

\left\|\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-{\rm Tr}(V)\right\|_{\psi_{1}(\mathsf{P}_{U})}\leqslant\frac{2\omega\|V\|_{{\rm F}}}{1-\omega\|U\|_{{\rm F}}}.

(20)

For this purpose, it is enough to prove that

\mathsf{P}_{U}\exp\left\{\frac{|\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-{\rm Tr}(V)|}{t}\right\}\leqslant 2,\quad\text{where}\quad t=\frac{2\omega\|V\|_{{\rm F}}}{1-\omega\|U\|_{{\rm F}}}.

(21)

According to Jensen’s inequality, we have

\mathbb{E}e^{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}U\boldsymbol{\xi}}\geqslant e^{\mathbb{E}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}U\boldsymbol{\xi}}=e^{{\rm Tr}(U)}.

Then it holds that

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}_{U}\exp\left\{\frac{|\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-{\rm Tr}(V)|}{t}\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\mathsf{P}_{U}\exp\left\{\frac{2|\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-{\rm Tr}(V)|}{t}\right\}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\mathbb{E}\exp\left\{\frac{2|\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-{\rm Tr}(V)|}{t}+\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}U\boldsymbol{\xi}-{\rm Tr}(U)\right\}\right)^{1/2}.

Since
|\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-{\rm Tr}(V)|=\max\{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-{\rm Tr}(V),{\rm Tr}(V)-\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}\}
, the expression in the right-hand side is not greater than

\left(\sum\limits_{\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}}\exp\left\{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}\left(U+2\varepsilon V/t\right)\boldsymbol{\xi}-{\rm Tr}(U+2\varepsilon V/t)\right\}\right)^{1/2}.

Note that, due to the triangle inequality and (
10
), for any
\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}\left(U+2\varepsilon V/t\right)\boldsymbol{\xi}-{\rm Tr}(U+2\varepsilon V/t)\right\|_{\psi_{1}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\|\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}U\boldsymbol{\xi}-{\rm Tr}(U)\right\|_{\psi_{1}}+\frac{2}{t}\left\|\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-{\rm Tr}(V)\right\|_{\psi_{1}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\omega\|U\|_{{\rm F}}+\frac{2\omega\|V\|_{{\rm F}}}{t}=1,

where
\|\cdot\|_{\psi_{1}}
stands for the standard
\psi_{1}
-norm (see the notations in Section
1
).
Then the Hölder inequality implies that

\mathbb{E}\exp\left\{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}\left(U+2\varepsilon V/t\right)\boldsymbol{\xi}-{\rm Tr}(U+2\varepsilon V/t)\right\}\leqslant 2^{\left\|\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}\left(U+2\varepsilon V/t\right)\boldsymbol{\xi}-{\rm Tr}(U+2\varepsilon V/t)\right\|_{\psi_{1}}}\leqslant 2.

Hence, we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}_{U}\exp\left\{\frac{|\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-{\rm Tr}(V)|}{t}\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\sum\limits_{\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}}\exp\left\{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}\left(U+2\varepsilon V/t\right)\boldsymbol{\xi}-{\rm Tr}(U+2\varepsilon V/t)\right\}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\sum\limits_{\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}}2\right)^{1/2}=2,

which yields (
20
).

Step 2: bounds on
\mathsf{P}_{U}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-{\rm Tr}(V)\right)^{4}
and
\left|\mathsf{P}_{U}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-{\rm Tr}(V)\right|
.
Applying the inequality (
19
), we obtain that

\mathsf{P}_{U}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-{\rm Tr}(V)\right)^{4}\leqslant 48\left\|\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-{\rm Tr}(V)\right\|_{\psi_{1}(\mathsf{P}_{U})}^{4}\leqslant 48\left(\frac{2\omega\|V\|_{{\rm F}}}{1-\omega\|U\|_{{\rm F}}}\right)^{4}.

(22)

Concerning
\left|\mathsf{P}_{U}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-{\rm Tr}(V)\right|
, we will prove a tighter bound than in (
19
). Let
t>0
be as defined in (
21
). Then it holds that

\left|\mathsf{P}_{U}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-{\rm Tr}(V)\right|\leqslant t\log\mathbb{E}\exp\left\{\frac{\left|\mathsf{P}_{U}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-{\rm Tr}(V)\right|}{t}\right\}\leqslant t\log 2=\frac{2\omega\|V\|_{{\rm F}}}{1-\omega\|U\|_{{\rm F}}}\cdot\log 2.

(23)

Step 3: final bound.
Summing up the inequalities (
22
) and (
23
) and using the triangle inequality, we obtain that

\left[\mathsf{P}_{U}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-\mathsf{P}_{U}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}\right)^{4}\right]^{1/4}\leqslant\frac{2\omega\|V\|_{{\rm F}}}{1-\omega\|U\|_{{\rm F}}}\left(\log 2+48^{1/4}\right).

If
\omega\|U\|_{{\rm F}}\leqslant 1/6
, then

\left[\mathsf{P}_{U}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-\mathsf{P}_{U}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}\right)^{4}\right]^{1/4}\leqslant\frac{12}{5}\left(\log 2+48^{1/4}\right)\omega\|V\|_{{\rm F}}\leqslant 8\omega\|V\|_{{\rm F}}.

This implies that, for any
U\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
satisfying the inequality
6\omega\|U\|_{{\rm F}}\leqslant 1
, it holds that

\mathsf{P}_{U}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-\mathsf{P}_{U}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}\right)^{4}\leqslant\left(64\omega^{2}\right)^{2}\|V\|_{{\rm F}}^{4}.

\square

4.2
Proof of Proposition
2.3

Let
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}=(\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{d})^{\top}
be a random vector with i.i.d. Rademacher entries and let
\eta\sim{\rm Be}(1/2)
be independent of
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}
. We are going to prove that a random vector
\boldsymbol{\xi}=\sqrt{2}\eta\,\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}
satisfies Proposition
2.3
. First, it is easy to observe that

\mathbb{E}\boldsymbol{\xi}=\sqrt{2}\mathbb{E}\eta\,\mathbb{E}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}={\bf 0}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{E}\boldsymbol{\xi}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}=2\mathbb{E}\eta^{2}\,\mathbb{E}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{\top}=I_{d}.

Second, let us fix any
{\bf u}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and show that

\mathbb{E}\exp\left\{\frac{3(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}{\bf u})^{2}}{16\|{\bf u}\|^{2}}\right\}\leqslant 2,

or equivalently, that the
\psi_{2}
-norm of
\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}{\bf u}
does not exceed
16\|{\bf u}\|^{2}/3=16\mathbb{E}(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}{\bf u})^{2}/3
. Indeed, introducing a random variable
\gamma\sim{\mathcal{N}}(0,1)
, which is independent of
\eta
and
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}
, we obtain that

\mathbb{E}\exp\left\{\frac{3(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}{\bf u})^{2}}{16\|{\bf u}\|^{2}}\right\}=\mathbb{E}\exp\left\{\frac{3\eta^{2}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{\top}{\bf u})^{2}}{8\|{\bf u}\|^{2}}\right\}\leqslant\mathbb{E}\exp\left\{\frac{3(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{\top}{\bf u})^{2}}{8\|{\bf u}\|^{2}}\right\}=\mathbb{E}\exp\left\{\frac{\sqrt{3}\gamma\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{\top}{\bf u}}{2\|{\bf u}\|}\right\}.

Hoeffding’s lemma, applied to independent Rademacher random variables
\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{d}
, implies that

\mathbb{E}\exp\left\{\frac{\sqrt{3}\gamma\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{\top}{\bf u}}{2\|{\bf u}\|}\right\}\leqslant\mathbb{E}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{d}\exp\left\{\frac{3\gamma^{2}u_{i}^{2}}{8\|{\bf u}\|^{2}}\right\}=\mathbb{E}\exp\left\{\frac{3\gamma^{2}}{8}\right\}=\left(1-\frac{3}{4}\right)^{-1/2}=2.

Thus, we proved that
\left\|\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}{\bf u}\right\|_{\psi_{2}}^{2}\leqslant 16\,\mathbb{E}\|\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}{\bf u}\|^{2}/3
. On the other hand, for
V=I_{d}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-\mathbb{E}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}\right)^{4}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\|\boldsymbol{\xi}\|^{2}-d\right)^{4}=\mathbb{E}\left(2\eta^{2}\|\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\|^{2}-d\right)^{4}=\mathbb{E}\left(2\eta^{2}d-d\right)^{4}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(2\eta^{2}-1\right)^{4}d^{4}=d^{4}=d^{2}\left\|V\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}.

The proof is finished.

4.3
Proof of Theorem
2.4

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
2.4
. Let us denote

H=\sqrt{n}\;\Sigma^{-1/2}\left(\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\right)\Sigma^{-1/2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left((\Sigma^{-1/2}{\bf X}_{i})(\Sigma^{-1/2}{\bf X}_{i})^{\top}-I\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{i}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{i}^{\top}-I\right).

Then

\left\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}=\frac{1}{n}\left\|\Sigma^{1/2}H\Sigma^{1/2}\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2},

and it is enough to show that

\displaystyle\left\|\Sigma^{1/2}H\Sigma^{1/2}\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}-\mathbb{E}\left\|\Sigma^{1/2}H\Sigma^{1/2}\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant 4\|\Sigma\|^{2}\max\left\{\sqrt{2\big(\tau\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{2})+\tau^{2}\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{4})\big)\log(2/\delta)},\;4e\tau\log(2/\delta)\right\}

with probability at least
(1-\delta)
. In order to do this, we first prove that

\left\|\Sigma^{1/2}H\Sigma^{1/2}\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}-\mathbb{E}\left\|\Sigma^{1/2}H\Sigma^{1/2}\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}\leqslant\max\left\{4\mathtt{v}\sqrt{2\log(2/\delta)},\;16e(\kappa\vee\|\Sigma\|^{2})\log(2/\delta)\right\}

(24)

with high probability, where

\displaystyle\mathtt{v}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\mathbb{E}{\rm\bf vec}({\bf X}{\bf X}^{\top}-\Sigma){\rm\bf vec}({\bf X}{\bf X}^{\top}-\Sigma)^{\top}\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2},

\displaystyle\kappa

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{\|U\|_{{\rm F}}=1}\mathbb{E}\left[{\bf X}^{\top}U{\bf X}-{\rm Tr}(U^{\top}\Sigma)\right]^{2},

(25)

and then use Lemma
4.6
to derive the final bound.
In the rest of the proof we study exponential moments of
\|\Sigma^{1/2}H\Sigma^{1/2}\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}
and derive the large deviation bound (
24
).
Since the proof is quite technical, we split it into several steps to improve readability of the paper. The proofs of some auxiliary results are moved to Appendix.

Step 1: linearization.
We start with the linearization trick. Let
\Gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
be a random matrix with i.i.d. standard Gaussian entries. Then it is straightforward to check that

\mathbb{E}\exp\left\{\lambda\|\Sigma^{1/2}H\Sigma^{1/2}\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}/2\right\}=\mathbb{E}\exp\left\{\sqrt{\lambda}{\rm Tr}(\Gamma^{\top}\Sigma^{1/2}H\Sigma^{1/2})\right\}.

(26)

The idea is that the power of the exponent in the right-hand side is linear in
H
, and the analysis of linear statistics is more convenient.
Unfortunately, the linearization trick in the form (
26
) will not bring us to the desired result, because the elements of
H
are sub-exponential, and hence, the conditional moment

\mathbb{E}_{H}\exp\left\{\sqrt{\lambda}{\rm Tr}(\Gamma^{\top}\Sigma^{1/2}H\Sigma^{1/2})\right\}\equiv\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left\{\sqrt{\lambda}{\rm Tr}(\Gamma^{\top}\Sigma^{1/2}H\Sigma^{1/2})\right\}\,\Big|\,\Gamma\right]

exists not for all
\Gamma
. Nevertheless, the idea of linearization is still useful, and we just have to tailor the right-hand side of (
26
) for our setup. This brings us to the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{z}
and
\lambda
be any positive numbers, and let
\Gamma
be a random matrix with i.i.d. standard Gaussian entries. Then, for any matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
satisfying the inequality

\|\Sigma A\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}\leqslant\mathfrak{z},

and for any
\beta>1
, it holds that

\displaystyle\exp\left\{\lambda\|\Sigma^{1/2}A\Sigma^{1/2}\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}/2\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}\mathbb{E}_{\Gamma}\,\exp\left\{\sqrt{\lambda}{\rm Tr}(\Gamma^{\top}\Sigma^{1/2}A\Sigma^{1/2})\right\}\mathbbm{1}\left(\|\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\|_{{\rm F}}\leqslant\mathfrak{z}\sqrt{\lambda}+\sqrt{\beta}{\rm Tr}(\Sigma)\right).

The proof of Lemma
4.1
is moved to Appendix
B
.
For any deterministic matrix
U\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, let us denote

\Phi(U)=\log\mathbb{E}e^{{\rm Tr}(H^{\top}U)}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\log\mathbb{E}e^{\boldsymbol{\xi}_{i}^{\top}U\boldsymbol{\xi}_{i}/\sqrt{n}}.

(27)

Applying Lemma
4.1
with

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda\|\Sigma^{1/2}H\Sigma^{1/2}\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}/2}\mathbbm{1}\left(\|\Sigma H\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}\leqslant\mathfrak{z}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\;\mathbb{E}e^{\sqrt{\lambda}{\rm Tr}(\Gamma^{\top}\Sigma^{1/2}H\Sigma^{1/2})}\mathbbm{1}\left(\|\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\|_{{\rm F}}\leqslant\mathfrak{z}\sqrt{\lambda}+\sqrt{2}\,{\rm Tr}(\Sigma)\right)

\displaystyle=2\;\mathbb{E}e^{\Phi(\sqrt{\lambda}\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2})}\mathbbm{1}\left(\|\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\|_{{\rm F}}\leqslant\mathfrak{z}\sqrt{\lambda}+\sqrt{2}\,{\rm Tr}(\Sigma)\right).

\Phi(U)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\log\mathbb{E}e^{\boldsymbol{\xi}_{i}^{\top}U\boldsymbol{\xi}_{i}/\sqrt{n}}=n\varphi(U/\sqrt{n}).

\mathbb{E}e^{\Phi(\sqrt{\lambda}\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2})}\mathbbm{1}\left(\|\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\|_{{\rm F}}\leqslant\mathfrak{z}\sqrt{\lambda}+\sqrt{2}\,{\rm Tr}(\Sigma)\right)

for a fixed
\mathfrak{z}>0
. The next lemma helps to bound the derivatives of

.

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose that the random vector
\boldsymbol{\xi}=\Sigma^{-1/2}{\bf X}
satisfies Assumption
2.1
. Then, for any
U\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, such that
\|U\|_{{\rm F}}\leqslant\rho_{\max}
, the derivatives of the cumulant generating function

\varphi(U)=\log\mathbb{E}\exp\left\{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}U\boldsymbol{\xi}\right\}

satisfy the inequalities

0\leqslant\left\langle\nabla^{2}\varphi(U),V^{\otimes 2}\right\rangle=\mathsf{P}_{U}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-\mathsf{P}_{U}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}\right)^{2}\leqslant\tau\|V\|_{{\rm F}}^{2},

-\tau^{3/2}\|V\|_{{\rm F}}^{3}\leqslant\left\langle\nabla^{3}\varphi(U),V^{\otimes 3}\right\rangle=\mathsf{P}_{U}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-\mathsf{P}_{U}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}\right)^{3}\leqslant\tau^{3/2}\|V\|_{{\rm F}}^{3},

and

-2\tau^{2}\|V\|_{{\rm F}}^{4}\leqslant\left\langle\nabla^{4}\varphi(U),V^{\otimes 4}\right\rangle\leqslant\tau^{2}\|V\|_{{\rm F}}^{4}.

Remark 4.3
.

Throughout the paper, we use the standard notation

\left\langle\nabla\varphi(U),V\right\rangle\equiv{\rm Tr}\left(\nabla\varphi(U)^{\top}V\right),

where
\nabla\varphi(U)
is the gradient of the scalar function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with respect to the matrix
U\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
.
For
k\geqslant 2
, the notation
\left\langle\nabla^{k}\varphi(U),V^{\otimes k}\right\rangle
is defined recursively:

\left\langle\nabla^{k}\varphi(U),V^{\otimes k}\right\rangle\equiv\Big\langle\nabla\left\langle\nabla^{k-1}\varphi(U),V^{\otimes(k-1)}\right\rangle,V\Big\rangle.

The proof of Lemma
4.2
is moved to Appendix
C
. Due to (

), we immediately obtain that

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla^{2}\Phi(O),U^{\otimes 2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\nabla^{2}\Phi(O),U^{\otimes 2}\right\rangle=\mathbb{E}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}U\boldsymbol{\xi}-\mathbb{E}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}U\boldsymbol{\xi}\right)=\mathbb{E}\left[{\rm Tr}\left((\boldsymbol{\xi}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}-I)U\right)\right]^{2},

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\nabla^{3}\Phi(O),U^{\otimes 3}\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\left|\left\langle\nabla^{3}\varphi(O),U^{\otimes 3}\right\rangle\right|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\mathbb{E}\left[{\rm Tr}\left((\boldsymbol{\xi}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}-I)U\right)\right]^{3}\leqslant\frac{\tau^{3/2}}{\sqrt{n}}\|U\|_{{\rm F}}^{3},

(29)

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\nabla^{4}\Phi(V),U^{\otimes 4}\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\left|\left\langle\nabla^{4}\varphi(V),U^{\otimes 4}\right\rangle\right|\leqslant\frac{\tau^{2}}{n}\|U\|_{{\rm F}}^{4}\quad\text{for all $\|V\|_{{\rm F}}\leqslant\rho_{\max}$.}

Here
O\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
denotes the matrix with zero entries.
Let us introduce

\mathtt{m}=n\;\mathbb{E}\left\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}=\mathbb{E}\left\|\Sigma^{1/2}H\Sigma^{1/2}\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}

(30)

for brevity.

Lemma 4.4
.

Step 3: peeling argument.
On this step, we transform the restricted exponential moment bound from Lemma
4.4
into a large deviation bound on
\|\Sigma^{1/2}H\Sigma^{1/2}\|_{{\rm F}}
.
Let
\mathfrak{z}
and
\lambda
be positive numbers to be specified later. Then it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\|\Sigma^{1/2}H\Sigma^{1/2}\|_{{\rm F}}\geqslant\mathfrak{z}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(e^{k-1}\mathfrak{z}\leqslant\|\Sigma^{1/2}H\Sigma^{1/2}\|_{{\rm F}}<e^{k}\mathfrak{z}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\|\Sigma^{1/2}H\Sigma^{1/2}\|_{{\rm F}}\geqslant e^{k-1}\mathfrak{z}\text{ and }\|\Sigma H\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}\leqslant e^{k}\mathfrak{z}\|\Sigma\|\right).

Obviously, for any
k\geqslant 1
, on the event
\{\|\Sigma^{1/2}H\Sigma^{1/2}\|_{{\rm F}}\geqslant e^{k-1}\mathfrak{z}\}
, we have

\mathbbm{1}\left(\|\Sigma^{1/2}H\Sigma^{1/2}\|_{{\rm F}}\geqslant e^{k-1}\mathfrak{z}\right)\leqslant\exp\left\{-\frac{e^{2k-2}\lambda\mathfrak{z}^{2}}{2\cdot e^{k}}+\frac{\lambda\|\Sigma^{1/2}H\Sigma^{1/2}\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}}{2\cdot e^{k}}\right\}

This yields the following version of the Markov inequality:

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\|\Sigma^{1/2}H\Sigma^{1/2}\|_{{\rm F}}\geqslant e^{k-1}\mathfrak{z}\text{ and }\|\Sigma H\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}\leqslant e^{k}\mathfrak{z}\|\Sigma\|\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\exp\left\{-\frac{e^{k-2}\lambda\mathfrak{z}^{2}}{2}+\frac{\lambda\|\Sigma^{1/2}H\Sigma^{1/2}\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}}{2\cdot e^{k}}\right\}\mathbbm{1}\big(\|\Sigma H\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}\leqslant e^{k}\mathfrak{z}\|\Sigma\|\big).

Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
4.4
imply that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\exp\left\{-\frac{e^{k-2}\lambda\mathfrak{z}^{2}}{2}+\frac{\lambda\|\Sigma^{1/2}H\Sigma^{1/2}\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}}{2\cdot e^{k}}\right\}\mathbbm{1}\big(\|\Sigma H\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}\leqslant e^{k}\mathfrak{z}\|\Sigma\|\big)

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\exp\left\{-\frac{e^{k-2}\lambda\mathfrak{z}^{2}}{2}+\frac{\lambda\mathtt{m}}{2\cdot e^{k}}+\frac{2\lambda^{2}\mathtt{v}^{2}}{e^{2k}}+\frac{1}{2}G\left(e^{-k}\lambda,e^{k}\mathfrak{z}\|\Sigma\|\right){\rm Tr}(\Sigma)^{2}+2G\left(e^{-k}\lambda,e^{k}\mathfrak{z}\|\Sigma\|\right)^{2}\left\|\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{4}\right\}.

It is straighforward to check that, by the definition of
G(\lambda,\mathfrak{z})
(see (
31
)), we have

G\left(e^{-k}\lambda,e^{k}\mathfrak{z}\|\Sigma\|\right)\leqslant G\left(\lambda,\mathfrak{z}\|\Sigma\|\right).

Hence,

\displaystyle 2\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\exp\left\{-\frac{e^{k-2}\lambda\mathfrak{z}^{2}}{2}+\frac{\lambda\mathtt{m}}{2\cdot e^{k}}+\frac{2\lambda^{2}\mathtt{v}^{2}}{e^{2k}}+\frac{1}{2}G\left(e^{-k}\lambda,e^{k}\mathfrak{z}\|\Sigma\|\right){\rm Tr}(\Sigma)^{2}+2G\left(e^{-k}\lambda,e^{k}\mathfrak{z}\|\Sigma\|\right)^{2}\left\|\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{4}\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\exp\left\{-\frac{\lambda(\mathfrak{z}^{2}-\mathtt{m})}{2e}+\frac{2\lambda^{2}\mathtt{v}^{2}}{e^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}G\left(\lambda,\mathfrak{z}\|\Sigma\|\right){\rm Tr}(\Sigma)^{2}+2G\left(\lambda,\mathfrak{z}\|\Sigma\|\right)^{2}\left\|\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{4}\right\}

\displaystyle\quad+2\sum\limits_{k=2}^{\infty}\exp\left\{-\frac{(e^{k-1}-1)\lambda\mathfrak{z}^{2}}{2e}-\frac{\lambda(\mathfrak{z}^{2}-\mathtt{m})}{2e}+\frac{2\lambda^{2}\mathtt{v}^{2}}{e^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}G\left(\lambda,\mathfrak{z}\|\Sigma\|\right){\rm Tr}(\Sigma)^{2}+2G\left(\lambda,\mathfrak{z}\|\Sigma\|\right)^{2}\left\|\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{4}\right\}.

Let us introduce a function
g:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
, defined by

g(u)=1+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\exp\left\{-\frac{(e^{k}-1)u}{2e}\right\}=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\exp\left\{-\frac{(e^{k}-1)u}{2e}\right\}.

(32)

Then

\displaystyle 2\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\exp\left\{-\frac{e^{k-2}\lambda\mathfrak{z}^{2}}{2}+\frac{\lambda\mathtt{m}}{2\cdot e^{k}}+\frac{2\lambda^{2}\mathtt{v}^{2}}{e^{2k}}+\frac{1}{2}G\left(e^{-k}\lambda,e^{k}\mathfrak{z}\|\Sigma\|\right){\rm Tr}(\Sigma)^{2}+2G\left(e^{-k}\lambda,e^{k}\mathfrak{z}\|\Sigma\|\right)^{2}\left\|\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{4}\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2g(\lambda\mathfrak{z}^{2})\exp\left\{-\frac{\lambda(\mathfrak{z}^{2}-\mathtt{m})}{2e}+\frac{2\lambda^{2}\mathtt{v}^{2}}{e^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}G\left(\lambda,\mathfrak{z}\|\Sigma\|\right){\rm Tr}(\Sigma)^{2}+2G\left(\lambda,\mathfrak{z}\|\Sigma\|\right)^{2}\left\|\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{4}\right\},

and thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\|\Sigma^{1/2}H\Sigma^{1/2}\|_{{\rm F}}\geqslant\mathfrak{z}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant 2g(\lambda\mathfrak{z}^{2})\exp\left\{-\frac{\lambda(\mathfrak{z}^{2}-\mathtt{m})}{2e}+\frac{2\lambda^{2}\mathtt{v}^{2}}{e^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}G\left(\lambda,\mathfrak{z}\|\Sigma\|\right){\rm Tr}(\Sigma)^{2}+2G\left(\lambda,\mathfrak{z}\|\Sigma\|\right)^{2}\left\|\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{4}\right\}.

(33)

, we prove the following result.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let Assumption
2.5
be satisfied with some
\alpha>0
. Then it holds that

\mathtt{v}^{2}=\left\|\mathbb{E}{\rm\bf vec}({\bf X}{\bf X}^{\top}-\Sigma){\rm\bf vec}({\bf X}{\bf X}^{\top}-\Sigma)^{\top}\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}\leqslant\alpha\left({\rm Tr}(\Sigma^{2})\right)^{2}+(\alpha^{2}-\alpha)\;{\rm Tr}(\Sigma^{4})

and

\kappa=\sup\limits_{\|U\|_{{\rm F}}=1}\mathbb{E}\left[{\bf X}^{\top}U{\bf X}-{\rm Tr}(U^{\top}\Sigma)\right]^{2}\leqslant\alpha\,\|\Sigma\|^{2}.

Let us recall that, under the conditions of Theorem
2.4
, Assumption
2.5
is fulfilled with
\alpha=\tau
. Hence, we have

\mathtt{v}^{2}\leqslant\tau\left({\rm Tr}(\Sigma^{2})\right)^{2}+(\tau^{2}-\tau)\;{\rm Tr}(\Sigma^{4})\quad\text{and}\quad\kappa\leqslant\tau\,\|\Sigma\|^{2}.

Applying Lemma
4.6
, we get the assertion of the theorem: with probability at least
(1-\delta)
it holds that

n\left\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}-n\;\mathbb{E}\left\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}<4\|\Sigma\|^{2}\max\left\{\sqrt{2\big(\tau\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{2})^{2}+\tau^{2}\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{4})\big)\log(2/\delta)},\;4e\tau\log(2/\delta)\right\}.

The proof is finished.

\square

4.4
Proof of Theorem
2.6

Since, for any
t>0
and any
\lambda>0
, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(n\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}-n\,\mathbb{E}\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}\leqslant-t\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp\left\{-\frac{\lambda t}{2}+\frac{\lambda n\,\mathbb{E}\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}}{2}\right\}\cdot\mathbb{E}\exp\left\{-\frac{\lambda n\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}}{2}\right\}

(42)

due to the Markov inequality, we are interested in upper bounds on the exponential moment

\mathbb{E}\exp\left\{-\frac{\lambda n\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}}{2}\right\}.

We would like to note that, in contrast to Theorem
2.4
, the exponential moment of interest exists for all
\lambda>0
in this case. This slightly simplifies the proof. In what follows, we will show that, under the conditions of Theorem
2.6
, for any
t>0
, it holds that

\mathbb{P}\left(n\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}-n\,\mathbb{E}\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}\leqslant-t\right)\leqslant 5\exp\left\{-\left(\frac{t^{2}}{8\mathtt{s}^{2}}\wedge\frac{t}{8\alpha\|\Sigma\|^{2}}\right)\right\}

with

\mathtt{s}^{2}=4\mathtt{v}^{2}+\frac{10\alpha^{2}{\rm Tr}(\Sigma)^{2}}{n}\left({\rm Tr}(\Sigma)^{2}+2\|\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}\left[\frac{\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)^{2}}{\alpha}+\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{2})\right]\right),

where
\mathtt{v}
is defined in (
4.3
). This will yield the desired high probability bound (
15
).
Similarly to the proof of Theorem
2.4
, we split our derivations in several steps for the ease of presentation.

Step 1: linearization.
As before, let us denote

H=\sqrt{n}\;\Sigma^{-1/2}\left(\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\right)\Sigma^{-1/2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{i}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{i}^{\top}-I\right),

where
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{i}=\Sigma^{-1/2}{\bf X}_{i}
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
are the standardized vectors.
Let
\Gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
be a random matrix with i.i.d. standard Gaussian entries. Applying the same linearization trick as in the proof of Theorem
2.4
(Step 1), we obtain that

\mathbb{E}\exp\left\{-\frac{\lambda n\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}}{2}\right\}=\mathbb{E}\exp\left\{-\frac{\lambda\|\Sigma^{1/2}H\Sigma^{1/2}\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}}{2}\right\}=\mathbb{E}\exp\left\{\mathtt{i}\sqrt{\lambda}\;{\rm Tr}(\Gamma^{\top}\Sigma^{1/2}H\Sigma^{1/2})\right\}.

Hence, we have to focus on the properties of the characteristic function of
H
. For any
U\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, let

\psi(U)=\log\mathbb{E}e^{\mathtt{i}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}U\boldsymbol{\xi}}

be the logarithm of the Fourier transform of
\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}U\boldsymbol{\xi}
. Applying the Fubini theorem, we get that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\exp\left\{\mathtt{i}\sqrt{\lambda}\;{\rm Tr}(\Gamma^{\top}\Sigma^{1/2}H\Sigma^{1/2})\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\exp\left\{\mathtt{i}\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{n}}\;\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{\rm Tr}\left(\Gamma^{\top}\Sigma^{1/2}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{j}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{j}^{\top}-I\right)\Sigma^{1/2}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\Gamma}\exp\left\{n\psi\left(\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{n}}\;\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\right)-\mathtt{i}\sqrt{n\lambda}\;{\rm Tr}(\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2})\right\}.

Thus, we proved that

\mathbb{E}\exp\left\{-\frac{\lambda n\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}}{2}\right\}=\mathbb{E}_{\Gamma}\exp\left\{n\psi\left(\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{n}}\;\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\right)-\mathtt{i}\sqrt{n\lambda}\;{\rm Tr}(\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2})\right\}.

On the other hand, the absolute value of
\mathbb{E}\exp\left\{-\lambda n\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}/2\right\}
is equal to the exponential moment itself. Then, due to the Jensen inequality, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\exp\left\{-\frac{\lambda n\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}}{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left|\mathbb{E}\exp\left\{-\frac{\lambda n\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}}{2}\right\}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\mathbb{E}_{\Gamma}\exp\left\{n\psi\left(\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{n}}\;\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\right)-\mathtt{i}\sqrt{n\lambda}\;{\rm Tr}(\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2})\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}_{\Gamma}\left|\exp\left\{n\psi\left(\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{n}}\;\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\right)-\mathtt{i}\sqrt{n\lambda}\;{\rm Tr}(\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2})\right\}\right|.

Let us introduce a positive number
\rho>0
such that

\rho^{2}={\rm Tr}(\Sigma)^{2}+2\|\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}\left(\frac{\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)^{2}}{\alpha}+\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{2})\right)

(43)

and define an event
{\mathcal{E}}_{\rho}
as follows:

{\mathcal{E}}_{\rho}=\left\{\left|{\rm Tr}(\Gamma\Sigma)\right|\leqslant\rho\text{ and }\left\|\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\right\|_{{\rm F}}\leqslant\rho\right\}.

Then it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\Gamma}\left|\exp\left\{n\psi\left(\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{n}}\;\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\right)-\mathtt{i}\sqrt{n\lambda}\;{\rm Tr}(\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2})\right\}\right|

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\Gamma}\left[\left|\exp\left\{n\psi\left(\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{n}}\;\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\right)-\mathtt{i}\sqrt{n\lambda}\;{\rm Tr}(\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2})\right\}\right|\mathbbm{1}\left({\mathcal{E}}_{\rho}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}_{\Gamma}\left[\left|\exp\left\{n\psi\left(\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{n}}\;\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\right)-\mathtt{i}\sqrt{n\lambda}\;{\rm Tr}(\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2})\right\}\right|\mathbbm{1}\left({\mathcal{E}}_{\rho}^{c}\right)\right],

where
{\mathcal{E}}_{\rho}^{c}
stands for the complement of
{\mathcal{E}}_{\rho}
. On
{\mathcal{E}}_{\rho}^{c}
, we apply the bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\Gamma}\left[\left|\exp\left\{n\psi\left(\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{n}}\;\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\right)-\mathtt{i}\sqrt{n\lambda}\;{\rm Tr}(\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2})\right\}\right|\mathbbm{1}\left({\mathcal{E}}_{\rho}^{c}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\Gamma}\left[\left|\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\exp\left\{\mathtt{i}\sqrt{\lambda}\;{\rm Tr}(\Gamma^{\top}\Sigma^{1/2}H\Sigma^{1/2})\right\}\right|\mathbbm{1}\left({\mathcal{E}}_{\rho}^{c}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}_{\Gamma}\left[1\cdot\mathbbm{1}\left({\mathcal{E}}_{\rho}^{c}\right)\right]=\mathbb{P}\left({\mathcal{E}}_{\rho}^{c}\right).

On the other hand, on
{\mathcal{E}}_{\rho}
we use the inequality

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\Gamma}\left[\left|\exp\left\{n\psi\left(\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{n}}\;\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\right)-\mathtt{i}\sqrt{n\lambda}\;{\rm Tr}(\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2})\right\}\right|\mathbbm{1}\left({\mathcal{E}}_{\rho}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}_{\Gamma}\left[\exp\left\{n\;{\rm Re}\left[\psi\left(\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{n}}\;\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\right)\right]\right\}\mathbbm{1}\left({\mathcal{E}}_{\rho}\right)\right].

Hence, we showed that

\mathbb{E}\exp\left\{-\frac{\lambda n\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}}{2}\right\}\leqslant\mathbb{E}_{\Gamma}\left[\exp\left\{n\;{\rm Re}\left[\psi\left(\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{n}}\;\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\right)\right]\right\}\mathbbm{1}\left({\mathcal{E}}_{\rho}\right)\right]+\mathbb{P}\left({\mathcal{E}}_{\rho}^{c}\right).

(44)

In what follows, we bound the terms in the right-hand side of (
44
) one by one, starting with the second summand.

Step 2: a bound on the remainder term.
According to the definition of
{\mathcal{E}}_{\rho}
, it is enough to bound the probabilities
\mathbb{P}\left(|{\rm Tr}(\Gamma\Sigma)|>\rho\right)
and
\mathbb{P}\left(\|\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\|_{{\rm F}}>\rho\right)
. Note that
{\rm Tr}(\Gamma\Sigma)
is a centered Gaussian random variable with variance
\|\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}
. Then, due to the Hoeffding inequality, it holds that

\mathbb{P}\left(|{\rm Tr}(\Gamma\Sigma)|>\rho\right)\leqslant 2\exp\left\{-\frac{\rho^{2}}{2\|\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}}\right\}.

(45)

The bound on
\mathbb{P}\left(\|\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\|_{{\rm F}}>\rho\right)
follows from the standard results on large deviations of Gaussian quadratic forms. Let us represent the squared Frobenius norm of
\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}
in the following form, using (
64
):

\|\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}={\rm Tr}\left(\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma^{\top}\Sigma\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\right)={\rm Tr}\left(\Gamma^{\top}\Sigma\Gamma\Sigma\right)={\rm\bf vec}(\Gamma)^{\top}\left(\Sigma\otimes\Sigma\right){\rm\bf vec}(\Gamma).

Then, according to
Laurent and Massart 2000
(see the proof of Lemma 1), for any
\mu\in(0,1/2)
, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\|\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\|_{{\rm F}}>\rho\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left({\rm\bf vec}(\Gamma)^{\top}\left(\Sigma\otimes\Sigma\right){\rm\bf vec}(\Gamma)>\rho^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp\left\{-\mu\rho^{2}+\mu{\rm Tr}(\Sigma\otimes\Sigma)+\frac{\|\Sigma\otimes\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}\;\mu^{2}}{1-2\|\Sigma\otimes\Sigma\|\,\mu}\right\}.

Due to the properties of the Kronecker product (see (
62
)), we have

{\rm Tr}(\Sigma\otimes\Sigma)=\left({\rm Tr}(\Sigma)\right)^{2},\quad\|\Sigma\otimes\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}=\|\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\|\Sigma\otimes\Sigma\|=\|\Sigma\|^{2}.

Hence,

\mathbb{P}\left(\|\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\|_{{\rm F}}>\rho\right)\leqslant\exp\left\{-\mu\left(\rho^{2}-\left[{\rm Tr}(\Sigma)\right]^{2}\right)+\frac{\|\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{4}\;\mu^{2}}{1-2\|\Sigma\|^{2}\mu}\right\}\quad\text{for all $\mu\in(0,1/2)$.}

(46)

Let us take
\mu\in(0,1/2)
satisfying the condition

1-2\|\Sigma\|^{2}\mu=\frac{\|\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{4}}{\|\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{4}+\|\Sigma\|^{2}(\rho^{2}-\left[{\rm Tr}(\Sigma)\right]^{2})},\quad\text{that is,}\quad\mu=\frac{(\rho^{2}-\left[{\rm Tr}(\Sigma)\right]^{2})/2}{\|\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{4}+\|\Sigma\|^{2}(\rho^{2}-\left[{\rm Tr}(\Sigma)\right]^{2})}.

Then it is straightforward to check that

\displaystyle-\mu\left(\rho^{2}-\left[{\rm Tr}(\Sigma)\right]^{2}\right)+\frac{\|\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{4}\;\mu^{2}}{1-2\|\Sigma\|^{2}\mu}

\displaystyle=-\mu\left(\rho^{2}-\left[{\rm Tr}(\Sigma)\right]^{2}\right)+\mu^{2}\left(\|\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{4}+\|\Sigma\|^{2}\rho^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{(\rho^{2}-\left[{\rm Tr}(\Sigma)\right]^{2})^{2}/4}{\|\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{4}+\|\Sigma\|^{2}(\rho^{2}-\left[{\rm Tr}(\Sigma)\right]^{2})}.

Substituting this equality into (
46
), we finally obtain that

\mathbb{P}\left(\|\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\|_{{\rm F}}>\rho\right)\leqslant\exp\left\{-\frac{(\rho^{2}-\left[{\rm Tr}(\Sigma)\right]^{2})^{2}/4}{\|\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{4}+\|\Sigma\|^{2}(\rho^{2}-\left[{\rm Tr}(\Sigma)\right]^{2})}\right\}.

This inequality and (
45
) immediately yield that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left({\mathcal{E}}_{\rho}^{c}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{P}\left(|{\rm Tr}(\Gamma\Sigma)|>\rho\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\|\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\|_{{\rm F}}>\rho\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\exp\left\{-\frac{\rho^{2}}{2\|\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}}\right\}+\exp\left\{-\frac{(\rho^{2}-\left[{\rm Tr}(\Sigma)\right]^{2})^{2}/4}{\|\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{4}+\|\Sigma\|^{2}(\rho^{2}-\left[{\rm Tr}(\Sigma)\right]^{2})}\right\}.

(47)

Step 3: Taylor’s expansion.
Next, we elaborate on the first term in the right-hand side of (
44
). For any
U\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, whenever
\psi(U)
is well-defined, the gradient
\nabla\psi(U)
is given by

\left\langle\nabla\psi(U),V\right\rangle=\frac{\mathtt{i}\mathbb{E}\left[\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}\;e^{\mathtt{i}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}U\boldsymbol{\xi}}\right]}{\mathbb{E}e^{\mathtt{i}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}U\boldsymbol{\xi}}}=\mathtt{i}\mathsf{P}_{\mathtt{i}U}\,\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}.

Here we extended the notation
\mathsf{P}_{U}f(\boldsymbol{\xi},U)
(see Section
2
) to the complex-valued matrices. Note that the extension is formal and
\mathsf{P}_{\mathtt{i}U}
is not a probability measure anymore. Nevertheless, we still can compute the higher-order derivatives of
\psi(U)
in similar way as in the proof Lemma
4.2
:

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla^{2}\psi(O),V^{\otimes 2}\right\rangle=-\mathbb{E}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-\mathbb{E}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}\right)^{2},

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla^{3}\psi(O),V^{\otimes 3}\right\rangle=-\mathtt{i}\;\mathbb{E}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-\mathbb{E}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}\right)^{3},

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla^{4}\psi(U),V^{\otimes 4}\right\rangle=\mathsf{P}_{\mathtt{i}U}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-\mathsf{P}_{\mathtt{i}U}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}\right)^{4}-3\left(\mathsf{P}_{\mathtt{i}U}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-\mathsf{P}_{\mathtt{i}U}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}\right)^{2}\right)^{2}.

As before,
O\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
stands the matrix with zero entries.
Applying Taylor’s formula with the Lagrange remainder term, we obtain that

\displaystyle\psi\left(\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{n}}\;\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{n}}\left\langle\nabla\psi(O),\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\right\rangle+\frac{\lambda}{2n}\left\langle\nabla^{2}\psi(O),\left(\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\right)^{\otimes 2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{6}\left(\frac{\lambda}{n}\right)^{3/2}\left\langle\nabla^{3}\psi(O),\left(\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\right)^{\otimes 3}\right\rangle+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{24n^{2}}\left\langle\nabla^{4}\psi(\Theta),\left(\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\right)^{\otimes 4}\right\rangle

for some
\Theta
such that

\|\Theta\|_{{\rm F}}\leqslant\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{n}}\;\left\|\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\right\|_{{\rm F}}\leqslant\rho\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{n}}\quad\text{on ${\mathcal{E}}_{\rho}$.}

Taking into account that
\langle\nabla\psi(O),\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\rangle
and
\langle\nabla^{3}\psi(O),(\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2})^{\otimes 3}\rangle
are imaginary, we immediately obtain

{\rm Re}\left[\psi\left(\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{n}}\;\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\right)\right]=\frac{\lambda}{2n}\left\langle\nabla^{2}\psi(O),\left(\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\right)^{\otimes 2}\right\rangle+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{24n^{2}}\left\langle\nabla^{4}\psi(\Theta),\left(\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\right)^{\otimes 4}\right\rangle.

Thus, our next goal is to bound the fourth derivative of
\psi
. To do so, we use the following lemma.

Lemma 4.7
.

Grant Assumption
2.5
and suppose that
|\mathbb{E}e^{\mathtt{i}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}U\boldsymbol{\xi}}|\geqslant 1/\beta
for some
\beta>1
. Then, for any
k\in[1,4]
, it holds that

\left|\mathsf{P}_{\mathtt{i}U}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-\mathsf{P}_{\mathtt{i}U}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}\right)^{k}\right|\leqslant 2^{k-1}\beta\left(1+\beta^{k}\right)\alpha^{k/2}\|V\|_{{\rm F}}^{k}\quad\text{for all $V\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$.}

The proof of Lemma
4.7
is deferred to Appendix
G
. Note that on the event
{\mathcal{E}}_{\rho}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}e^{\mathtt{i}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}\Theta\boldsymbol{\xi}}-1\right|

\displaystyle=2\left|\mathbb{E}\sin\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}\Theta\boldsymbol{\xi}/2\right)e^{\mathtt{i}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}\Theta\boldsymbol{\xi}/2}\right|\leqslant\mathbb{E}\left|\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}\Theta\boldsymbol{\xi}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant|{\rm Tr}(\Theta)|+\sqrt{\alpha}\|\Theta\|_{{\rm F}}\leqslant\rho\left(1+\sqrt{\alpha}\right)\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{n}}.

In what follows, we will choose
\lambda\leqslant 1/(2\alpha\|\Sigma\|^{2})
. Invoking the definition of
\rho
(see eq. (
43
)) and using the condition (
14
), we observe that

\displaystyle\rho^{2}\left(1+\sqrt{\alpha}\right)^{2}\frac{\lambda}{n}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{3\left(1+\sqrt{\alpha}\right)^{2}}{2\alpha\|\Sigma\|^{2}n}\left({\rm Tr}(\Sigma)^{2}+2\|\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}\left[\frac{\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)^{2}}{\alpha}+\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{2})\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{3\left(1+\sqrt{\alpha}\right)^{2}}{2\alpha n}\left(\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)^{2}+2\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{2})\left[\frac{\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)^{2}}{\alpha}+\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{2})\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{3\left(1+\sqrt{\alpha}\right)^{2}}{2\alpha n}\left(3\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)^{2}+\frac{2\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{2})\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)^{2}}{\alpha}\right)\leqslant\frac{1}{64}.

In the last line, we used the fact that
\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{2})\leqslant\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)
.
Hence, the conditions of Lemma
4.7
are fulfilled with

\beta=\left(1-\rho\left(1+\sqrt{\alpha}\right)\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{n}}\right)^{-1}\leqslant\left(1-1/8\right)^{-1}=\frac{8}{7}<2^{1/5},

and consequently, on
{\mathcal{E}}_{\rho}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla^{4}\psi(\Theta),\left(\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\right)^{\otimes 4}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(8\beta(1+\beta^{4})+12\beta^{2}\left(1+\beta^{2}\right)^{2}\right)\alpha^{2}\|\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\|_{{\rm F}}^{4}

\displaystyle<111\alpha^{2}\rho^{2}\|\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}.

This implies that

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\left[\psi\left(\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{n}}\;\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda}{2n}\left\langle\nabla^{2}\psi(O),\left(\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\right)^{\otimes 2}\right\rangle+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{24n^{2}}\left\langle\nabla^{4}\psi(\Theta),\left(\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\right)^{\otimes 4}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant-\frac{\lambda}{2n}\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\boldsymbol{\xi}-{\rm Tr}(\Gamma\Sigma)\right)^{2}+\frac{111\alpha^{2}\lambda^{2}}{24n^{2}}\left\|\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{4}

\displaystyle\leqslant-\frac{\lambda}{2n}\mathbb{E}_{{\bf X}}\left({\bf X}^{\top}\Gamma{\bf X}-{\rm Tr}(\Gamma\Sigma)\right)^{2}+\frac{5\alpha^{2}\lambda^{2}\rho^{2}}{n^{2}}\left\|\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}

on the same event. Hence, it holds that

\displaystyle\exp\left\{n\;{\rm Re}\left[\psi\left(\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{n}}\;\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\right)\right]\right\}\mathbbm{1}\left(\|\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\|_{{\rm F}}\leqslant\rho\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp\left\{-\frac{\lambda}{2}\;\mathbb{E}_{{\bf X}}\left({\bf X}^{\top}\Gamma{\bf X}-{\rm Tr}(\Gamma\Sigma)\right)^{2}+\frac{5\alpha^{2}\lambda^{2}\rho^{2}}{n}\left\|\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}\right\}.

The logarithm of the right-hand side is quadratic in
\Gamma
, and thus, we can easily bound its exponential moment. The precise statement is given in the following lemma.

Lemma 4.8
.

Assume that

\frac{10\alpha^{2}\lambda^{2}\rho^{2}\|\Sigma\|^{2}}{n}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}.

Then it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\Gamma}\exp\left\{-\frac{\lambda}{2}\;\mathbb{E}_{{\bf X}}\left({\bf X}^{\top}\Gamma{\bf X}-{\rm Tr}(\Gamma\Sigma)\right)^{2}+\frac{5\alpha^{2}\lambda^{2}\rho^{2}}{n}\left\|\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp\left\{-\frac{\lambda\mathtt{m}}{2}+2\lambda^{2}\mathtt{v}^{2}+\frac{5\alpha^{2}\lambda^{2}\rho^{2}}{n}\left({\rm Tr}(\Sigma)\right)^{2}+\frac{200\alpha^{4}\lambda^{4}\rho^{4}\|\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{4}}{n^{2}}\right\}.

We present the proof of Lemma
4.8
in Appendix
H
. Hence, on the third step, we proved that

{\rm Re}\left[\psi\left(\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{n}}\;\Sigma^{1/2}\Gamma\Sigma^{1/2}\right)\right]\leqslant\exp\left\{-\frac{\lambda\mathtt{m}}{2}+2\lambda^{2}\mathtt{v}^{2}+\frac{5\alpha^{2}\lambda^{2}\rho^{2}}{n}\left({\rm Tr}(\Sigma)\right)^{2}+\frac{200\alpha^{4}\lambda^{4}\rho^{4}\|\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{4}}{n^{2}}\right\}.

(48)

Step 4: final bound.

Summing up the inequalities (

), (
44
), (
4.4
) and (

:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(n\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}-n\,\mathbb{E}\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}\leqslant-t\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp\left\{-\frac{\lambda t}{2}+2\lambda^{2}\mathtt{v}^{2}+\frac{5\alpha^{2}\lambda^{2}\rho^{2}}{n}\left({\rm Tr}(\Sigma)\right)^{2}+\frac{200\alpha^{4}\lambda^{4}\rho^{4}\|\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{4}}{n^{2}}\right\}

(49)

\displaystyle\quad+e^{-\lambda t/2+\lambda\mathtt{m}/2}\left(2\exp\left\{-\frac{\rho^{2}}{2\|\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}}\right\}+\exp\left\{-\frac{(\rho^{2}-\left[{\rm Tr}(\Sigma)\right]^{2})^{2}/4}{\|\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{4}+\|\Sigma\|^{2}(\rho^{2}-\left[{\rm Tr}(\Sigma)\right]^{2})}\right\}\right).

In what follows, we will take
\lambda
from
\big[0,1/(2\alpha\|\Sigma\|^{2})\big]
. In view of Lemma
4.5
, this implies that

\frac{\lambda\mathtt{m}}{2}\leqslant\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)^{2}}{\alpha}+\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{2})\right).

The next lemma helps us to simplify the inequality (
4.4
) substantially.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
\rho>0
be as defined in (

Appendix A
Auxiliary results: Kronecker product and its properties

In this section, we present some facts about the Kronecker product, which are extensively used in our proofs.
Let us recall that, for any matrices
A\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times q}
and
B\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times s}
, their Kronecker product
A\otimes B
is a matrix of size
pr\times qs
, defined as

A\otimes B=\begin{pmatrix}a_{11}B&\dots&a_{1q}B\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
a_{p1}B&\dots&a_{pq}B\end{pmatrix}.

The basic property of the Kronecker product is the following identity (also referred to as the mixed product property):

(A\otimes B)(C\otimes D)=(AC)\otimes(BD).

(61)

This immediately implies that

(A\otimes B)^{-1}=A^{-1}\otimes B^{-1}\quad\text{and}\quad(A\otimes B)^{k}=(A^{k})\otimes(B^{k})\quad\text{for all $k\in\mathbb{N}$.}

Another corollary of (
61
) is that if
A\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times p}
and
B\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}
are symmetric matrices with eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\geqslant\lambda_{2}\geqslant\dots\geqslant\lambda_{p}
and
\mu_{1}\geqslant\mu_{2}\geqslant\dots\geqslant\mu_{r}
, respectively, then

\left\{\lambda_{i}\mu_{j}:1\leqslant i\leqslant p,\,1\leqslant j\leqslant r\right\}

are the eigenvalues of
(A\otimes B)
. Indeed, if
A=U^{\top}\Lambda U
and
B=V^{\top}MV
are the eigenvalue decompositions of
A
and
B
, respectively, then

A\otimes B=(U^{\top}\Lambda U)\otimes(V^{\top}MV)=(U^{\top}\otimes V^{\top})\big[(\Lambda U)\otimes(MV)\big]=(U\otimes V)^{\top}(\Lambda\otimes M)(U\otimes V)

is the eigenvalue decomposition of
(A\otimes B)
.
This fact yields that

{\rm Tr}(A\otimes B)={\rm Tr}(A){\rm Tr}(B),\quad\|A\otimes B\|=\|A\|\|B\|,\quad\text{and}\quad\|A\otimes B\|_{{\rm F}}=\|A\|_{{\rm F}}\|B\|_{{\rm F}}.

(62)

We also extensively exploit the properties of the Kronecker product related to interaction with the vectorization operator
{\rm\bf vec}
(see our notation in Section
1
). Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times q}
and
B\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times s}
be arbitrary matrices (not necessarily symmetric) and let
U\in\mathbb{R}^{s\times q}
be a matrix with columns
{\bf u}_{1},\dots,{\bf u}_{q}
, so we have
{\rm\bf vec}(U)=({\bf u}_{1}^{\top},\dots,{\bf u}_{q}^{\top})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{sq}
. Then it is straightforward to check that

(A\otimes B){\rm\bf vec}(U)={\rm\bf vec}(BUA^{\top}).

(63)

As a consequence, for any matrices
A\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times q}
,
B\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times s}
,
U\in\mathbb{R}^{s\times q}
, and
V\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times p}
, it holds that

{\rm Tr}(V^{\top}BUA^{\top})={\rm\bf vec}(V)^{\top}{\rm\bf vec}(BUA^{\top})={\rm\bf vec}(V)^{\top}(A\otimes B){\rm\bf vec}(U).

(64)

In our proofs, we use the representation (
64
) to compute expectation the bilinear (in
U
and
V
) form
{\rm Tr}(V^{\top}BUA^{\top})
when
U
and
V
are random matrices.

Proof of Lemma

Since all the elements of

are i.i.d. standard Gaussian random variables, the random vector

Appendix C
Proof of Lemma
4.2

Let us recall that, for any Borel function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times d}\rightarrow\mathbb{\mathbb{R}}
,
\mathsf{P}_{U}f(\boldsymbol{\xi},U)
denotes the expectation

\mathsf{P}_{U}f(\boldsymbol{\xi},U)=\frac{\mathbb{E}f(\boldsymbol{\xi},U)e^{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}U\boldsymbol{\xi}}}{\mathbb{E}e^{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}U\boldsymbol{\xi}}}.

One can compute the derivative of
\mathsf{P}_{U}f(\boldsymbol{\xi},U)
according to the formula

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla\mathsf{P}_{U}f(\boldsymbol{\xi},U),V\right\rangle

\displaystyle\equiv{\rm Tr}\left(\nabla\mathsf{P}_{U}f(\boldsymbol{\xi},U)^{\top}V\right)

\displaystyle=\mathsf{P}_{U}\left\langle\frac{\partial f(\boldsymbol{\xi},U)}{\partial U},V\right\rangle+\mathsf{P}_{U}f(\boldsymbol{\xi},U)\,\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-\mathsf{P}_{U}f(\boldsymbol{\xi},U)\;\mathsf{P}_{U}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}

(65)

\displaystyle=\mathsf{P}_{U}\left\langle\frac{\partial f(\boldsymbol{\xi},U)}{\partial U},V\right\rangle+\mathsf{P}_{U}\Big(f(\boldsymbol{\xi},U)-\mathsf{P}_{U}f(\boldsymbol{\xi},U)\Big)\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-\mathsf{P}_{U}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}\right),

which holds for all
U,V\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
whenever the expectations in the left- and the right-hand sides are well-defined. It is straightforward to check that

\left\langle\nabla\varphi(U),V\right\rangle={\rm Tr}\left(\nabla\varphi(U)^{\top}V\right)=\mathsf{P}_{U}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}\right).

Then, using (
C
), we can compute

\left\langle\nabla^{2}\varphi(U),V^{\otimes 2}\right\rangle\equiv\Big\langle\nabla\left\langle\nabla\varphi(U),V\right\rangle,V\Big\rangle=\mathsf{P}_{U}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-\mathsf{P}_{U}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}\right)^{2}.

For the higher order derivatives, we similarly have

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla^{3}\varphi(U),V^{\otimes 3}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-2\mathsf{P}_{U}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-\mathsf{P}_{U}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}\right)\left\langle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}U}\mathsf{P}_{U}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi},V\right\rangle+\mathsf{P}_{U}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-\mathsf{P}_{U}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}\right)^{3}

\displaystyle=0+\mathsf{P}_{U}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-\mathsf{P}_{U}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}\right)^{3}=\mathsf{P}_{U}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}-\mathsf{P}_{U}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\top}V\boldsymbol{\xi}\right)^{3}

and

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\log\det\left(I_{d^{2}}+\lambda\;\mathbb{E}_{{\bf X}}\left[({\bf X}{\bf X}^{\top}-\Sigma)\otimes({\bf X}{\bf X}^{\top}-\Sigma)\right]-\frac{10\alpha^{2}\lambda^{2}\rho^{2}}{n}\;\left(\Sigma\otimes\Sigma\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant-\frac{\lambda}{2}\mathbb{E}_{{\bf X}}{\rm Tr}\left[({\bf X}{\bf X}^{\top}-\Sigma)\otimes({\bf X}{\bf X}^{\top}-\Sigma)\right]+\frac{5\alpha^{2}\lambda^{2}\rho^{2}}{n}\;{\rm Tr}\left(\Sigma\otimes\Sigma\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\left\|\lambda\;\mathbb{E}_{{\bf X}}\left[({\bf X}{\bf X}^{\top}-\Sigma)\otimes({\bf X}{\bf X}^{\top}-\Sigma)\right]-\frac{10\alpha^{2}\lambda^{2}\rho^{2}}{n}\;\left(\Sigma\otimes\Sigma\right)\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}.

The last term in the right-hand side does not exceed

2\left\|\lambda\;\mathbb{E}_{{\bf X}}\left[({\bf X}{\bf X}^{\top}-\Sigma)\otimes({\bf X}{\bf X}^{\top}-\Sigma)\right]\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}+2\left\|\frac{10\alpha^{2}\lambda^{2}\rho^{2}}{n}\;\left(\Sigma\otimes\Sigma\right)\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}=2\lambda^{2}\mathtt{v}^{2}+\frac{200\alpha^{4}\lambda^{4}\rho^{4}\|\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{4}}{n^{2}}.

Taking into account that

\mathbb{E}_{{\bf X}}{\rm Tr}\left[({\bf X}{\bf X}^{\top}-\Sigma)\otimes({\bf X}{\bf X}^{\top}-\Sigma)\right]=\mathbb{E}_{{\bf X}}\left[{\rm Tr}({\bf X}{\bf X}^{\top}-\Sigma)\right]^{2}=\mathtt{m},

we obtain the inequality

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\log\det\left(I_{d^{2}}+\lambda\;\mathbb{E}_{{\bf X}}\left[({\bf X}{\bf X}^{\top}-\Sigma)\otimes({\bf X}{\bf X}^{\top}-\Sigma)\right]-\frac{10\alpha^{2}\lambda^{2}\rho^{2}}{n}\;\left(\Sigma\otimes\Sigma\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant-\frac{\lambda\mathtt{m}}{2}+2\lambda^{2}\mathtt{v}^{2}+\frac{5\alpha^{2}\lambda^{2}\rho^{2}}{n}\left({\rm Tr}(\Sigma)\right)^{2}+\frac{200\alpha^{4}\lambda^{4}\rho^{4}\|\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{4}}{n^{2}},

which yields the assertion of Lemma
4.8
in view of (
H
).

\square

Appendix I
Proof of Lemma
4.9

It is easy to observe that, according to the definition of
\rho
,

\frac{2\rho^{2}}{\|\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}}\geqslant 4\left(\frac{\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)^{2}}{\alpha}+\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{2})\right)>\frac{\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)^{2}}{\alpha}+\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{2}),

so it only remains to check the inequality

\frac{(\rho^{2}-\left[{\rm Tr}(\Sigma)\right]^{2})^{2}}{\|\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{4}+\|\Sigma\|^{2}(\rho^{2}-\left[{\rm Tr}(\Sigma)\right]^{2})}\geqslant\frac{\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)^{2}}{\alpha}+\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{2}).

Let us denote
\mathtt{a}=\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)^{2}/\alpha+\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{2})
for brevity and consider the inequality

\frac{\mathtt{x}^{2}}{\|\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{4}+\|\Sigma\|^{2}\mathtt{x}}\geqslant\mathtt{a},\quad\mathtt{x}\geqslant 0,

which is equivalent to

\mathtt{x}^{2}-\mathtt{a}\|\Sigma\|^{2}\mathtt{x}-\mathtt{a}\|\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{4}\geqslant 0,\quad\mathtt{x}\geqslant 0.

Its solution is given by

\mathtt{x}\geqslant\frac{\mathtt{a}\|\Sigma\|^{2}}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\mathtt{a}^{2}\|\Sigma\|^{4}}{4}+4\mathtt{a}\|\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{4}},

so we have to check that

\rho^{2}-\left[{\rm Tr}(\Sigma)\right]^{2}\geqslant\frac{\mathtt{a}\|\Sigma\|^{2}}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\mathtt{a}^{2}\|\Sigma\|^{4}}{4}+4\mathtt{a}\|\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{4}}.

We will show even a tighter bound, namely,

\rho^{2}-\left[{\rm Tr}(\Sigma)\right]^{2}\geqslant\mathtt{a}\|\Sigma\|^{2}+\|\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}\sqrt{\mathtt{a}}.

By the definition of
\rho
(see eq. (
43
)), it holds that

\frac{\rho^{2}-\left[{\rm Tr}(\Sigma)\right]^{2}}{\|\Sigma\|^{2}}=2\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{2})\left(\frac{\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)^{2}}{\alpha}+\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{2})\right).

Since the effective rank of any non-zero symmetric matrix is at least
1
, we have

\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{2})\geqslant 1\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)^{2}}{\alpha}+\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{2})\geqslant\left(\frac{\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)^{2}}{\alpha}+\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{2})\right)^{1/2}.

This implies that

\displaystyle\frac{\rho^{2}-\left[{\rm Tr}(\Sigma)\right]^{2}}{\|\Sigma\|^{2}}

\displaystyle=2\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{2})\left(\frac{\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)^{2}}{\alpha}+\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{2})\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant\left(\frac{\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)^{2}}{\alpha}+\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{2})\right)+\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{2})\left(\frac{\mathtt{r}(\Sigma)^{2}}{\alpha}+\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{2})\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\mathtt{a}+\mathtt{r}(\Sigma^{2})\sqrt{\mathtt{a}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathtt{a}\|\Sigma\|^{2}+\|\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}\sqrt{\mathtt{a}}}{\|\Sigma\|^{2}}.

\square

Appendix J
Proof of Lemma
4.10

We split the proof into several steps for the ease of exposure.

Step 1: the Efron-Stein inequality.

The key idea of the proof is to bound the variance, applying the Efron-Stein inequality to
\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}
. Let
\widetilde{\bf X}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{\bf X}_{n}
be i.i.d. copies of the random vector
{\bf X}
, which are independent of
{\bf X}_{1},\dots,{\bf X}_{n}
. For any
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, let us introduce

\widehat{\Sigma}_{-i}=\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{j\neq i}{\bf X}_{j}{\bf X}_{j}^{\top}\quad\text{and}\quad\widehat{\Sigma}_{i}=\frac{1}{n}\widetilde{\bf X}_{i}\widetilde{\bf X}_{i}^{\top}+\widehat{\Sigma}_{-i}.

According to the Efron-Stein inequality (see, for instance,
[
Boucheron et al. 2013
, Theorem 3.1]
), it holds that

{\rm Var}\left(\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}\right)\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left(\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}-\|\widehat{\Sigma}_{i}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}\right)^{2}.

For any
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}-\left\|\widehat{\Sigma}_{i}-\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{1}{n}{\bf X}_{i}{\bf X}_{i}^{\top}+\widehat{\Sigma}_{-i}-\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}-\left\|\frac{1}{n}\widetilde{\bf X}_{i}\widetilde{\bf X}_{i}^{\top}+\widehat{\Sigma}_{-i}-\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{1}{n}\big({\bf X}_{i}{\bf X}_{i}^{\top}-\Sigma\big)+\widehat{\Sigma}_{-i}-\frac{n-1}{n}\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}-\left\|\frac{1}{n}\big(\widetilde{\bf X}_{i}\widetilde{\bf X}_{i}^{\top}-\Sigma\big)+\widehat{\Sigma}_{-i}-\frac{n-1}{n}\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\left\|{\bf X}_{i}{\bf X}_{i}^{\top}-\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}-\frac{1}{n^{2}}\left\|\widetilde{\bf X}_{i}\widetilde{\bf X}_{i}^{\top}-\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2}{n}{\rm Tr}\left[({\bf X}_{i}{\bf X}_{i}^{\top}-\widetilde{\bf X}_{i}\widetilde{\bf X}_{i}^{\top})\left(\widehat{\Sigma}_{-i}-\frac{n-1}{n}\Sigma\right)\right].

Then, applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we obtain that

\displaystyle{\rm Var}\left(\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{n^{4}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|{\bf X}_{i}{\bf X}_{i}^{\top}-\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}-\left\|\widetilde{\bf X}_{i}\widetilde{\bf X}_{i}^{\top}-\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{4}{n^{2}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left({\rm Tr}\left[({\bf X}_{i}{\bf X}_{i}^{\top}-\widetilde{\bf X}_{i}\widetilde{\bf X}_{i}^{\top})\left(\widehat{\Sigma}_{-i}-\frac{n-1}{n}\Sigma\right)\right]\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{3}}\;\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|{\bf X}_{1}{\bf X}_{1}^{\top}-\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}-\left\|\widetilde{\bf X}_{1}\widetilde{\bf X}_{1}^{\top}-\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}\right)^{2}

(76)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{4}{n}\;\mathbb{E}\left({\rm Tr}\left[({\bf X}_{1}{\bf X}_{1}^{\top}-\widetilde{\bf X}_{1}\widetilde{\bf X}_{1}^{\top})\left(\widehat{\Sigma}_{-1}-\frac{n-1}{n}\Sigma\right)\right]\right)^{2}.

It remains to bound the two terms in the right-hand side.

Step 2: a bound on
\mathbb{E}\left(\|{\bf X}_{1}{\bf X}_{1}^{\top}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}-\|\widetilde{\bf X}_{1}\widetilde{\bf X}_{1}^{\top}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}\right)^{2}
.

Since the random variables
\|{\bf X}_{1}{\bf X}_{1}^{\top}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}
and
\|\widetilde{\bf X}_{1}\widetilde{\bf X}_{1}^{\top}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}
are independent and identically distributed, it holds that

\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|{\bf X}_{1}{\bf X}_{1}^{\top}-\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}-\left\|\widetilde{\bf X}_{1}\widetilde{\bf X}_{1}^{\top}-\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}\right)^{2}=2\;{\rm Var}\left(\left\|{\bf X}{\bf X}^{\top}-\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}\right).

(77)

We use the following lemma to bound the variance of
\|{\bf X}{\bf X}^{\top}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}
.

Lemma J.1
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\zeta}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a centered random vector with a nondegenerate covariance matrix
\Omega
. Suppose that
\boldsymbol{\zeta}
satisfies Assumption
2.5
, that is,

\mathbb{E}\left(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\top}\Omega^{-1/2}V\Omega^{-1/2}\boldsymbol{\zeta}-{\rm Tr}(V)\right)^{4}\leqslant\alpha^{2}\|V\|_{{\rm F}}^{4}\quad\text{for all $V\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$.}

Then it holds that

{\rm Var}\left(\left\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\top}-\Omega\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}\right)\leqslant 4\alpha^{2}{\rm Tr}(\Omega^{2})^{2}+24\alpha{\rm Tr}(\Omega)^{2}{\rm Tr}(\Omega^{2})

and

\mathbb{E}\left\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\top}-\Omega\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}\leqslant{\rm Tr}(\Omega)^{2}+(\alpha-1){\rm Tr}(\Omega^{2}).

We postpone the proof of Lemma
J.1
to Appendix
K
and proceed with the following inequality:

{\rm Var}\left(\left\|{\bf X}{\bf X}^{\top}-\Sigma\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}\right)\leqslant 4\alpha^{2}{\rm Tr}(\Sigma^{2})^{2}+24\alpha{\rm Tr}(\Sigma)^{2}{\rm Tr}(\Sigma^{2}).

(78)

It remains to bound the second term in the right-hand side of (
J
).

Step 3: a bound on
\mathbb{E}\left({\rm Tr}\left[({\bf X}_{1}{\bf X}_{1}^{\top}-\widetilde{\bf X}_{1}\widetilde{\bf X}_{1}^{\top})\left(\widehat{\Sigma}_{-1}-\frac{n-1}{n}\Sigma\right)\right]\right)^{2}
.

Note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left({\rm Tr}\left[({\bf X}_{1}{\bf X}_{1}^{\top}-\widetilde{\bf X}_{1}\widetilde{\bf X}_{1}^{\top})\left(\widehat{\Sigma}_{-1}-\frac{n-1}{n}\Sigma\right)\right]\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\left({\rm Tr}\left[({\bf X}_{1}{\bf X}_{1}^{\top}-\widetilde{\bf X}_{1}\widetilde{\bf X}_{1}^{\top})\left(\widehat{\Sigma}_{-1}-\frac{n-1}{n}\Sigma\right)\right]\right)^{2}\;\Big|\;{\bf X}_{2},\dots,{\bf X}_{n}\right]

\displaystyle=2\,\mathbb{E}\,{\rm Var}\left({\rm Tr}\left[{\bf X}_{1}{\bf X}_{1}^{\top}\left(\widehat{\Sigma}_{-1}-\frac{n-1}{n}\Sigma\right)\right]\;\Big|\;{\bf X}_{2},\dots,{\bf X}_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=2\,\mathbb{E}\,{\rm Var}\left({\bf X}_{1}^{\top}\left(\widehat{\Sigma}_{-1}-\frac{n-1}{n}\Sigma\right){\bf X}_{1}\;\Big|\;{\bf X}_{2},\dots,{\bf X}_{n}\right).

According to Assumption
2.5
, the expression in the right-hand side does not exceed

\displaystyle 2\,\mathbb{E}\,{\rm Var}\left({\bf X}_{1}^{\top}\left(\widehat{\Sigma}_{-1}-\frac{n-1}{n}\Sigma\right){\bf X}_{1}\;\Big|\;{\bf X}_{2},\dots,{\bf X}_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\alpha\,\mathbb{E}\left\|\Sigma^{1/2}\left(\widehat{\Sigma}_{-1}-\frac{n-1}{n}\Sigma\right)\Sigma^{1/2}\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}.

Representing
\Sigma^{1/2}\big(\widehat{\Sigma}_{-1}-\frac{n-1}{n}\Sigma\big)\Sigma^{1/2}
as a sum of independent centered random matrices, we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\|\Sigma^{1/2}\big(\widehat{\Sigma}_{-1}-\frac{n-1}{n}\Sigma\big)\Sigma^{1/2}\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum\limits_{i=2}^{n}\sum\limits_{j=2}^{n}\mathbb{E}\;{\rm Tr}\left(\Sigma({\bf X}_{i}{\bf X}_{i}^{\top}-\Sigma)\Sigma({\bf X}_{j}{\bf X}_{j}^{\top}-\Sigma)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum\limits_{i=2}^{n}\mathbb{E}\;{\rm Tr}\left(\Sigma({\bf X}_{i}{\bf X}_{i}^{\top}-\Sigma)\Sigma({\bf X}_{i}{\bf X}_{i}^{\top}-\Sigma)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{n-1}{n^{2}}\;\mathbb{E}\left\|\Sigma^{1/2}({\bf X}{\bf X}^{\top}-\Sigma)\Sigma^{1/2}\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}.

Thus, it holds that

\mathbb{E}\left({\rm Tr}\left[({\bf X}_{1}{\bf X}_{1}^{\top}-\widetilde{\bf X}_{1}\widetilde{\bf X}_{1}^{\top})\left(\widehat{\Sigma}_{-1}-\frac{n-1}{n}\Sigma\right)\right]\right)^{2}\leqslant\frac{2}{n}\;\mathbb{E}\left\|\Sigma^{1/2}({\bf X}{\bf X}^{\top}-\Sigma)\Sigma^{1/2}\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}.

Applying Lemma
J.1
to the random vector
\Sigma^{1/2}{\bf X}
, we obtain that

\mathbb{E}\left({\rm Tr}\left[({\bf X}_{1}{\bf X}_{1}^{\top}-\widetilde{\bf X}_{1}\widetilde{\bf X}_{1}^{\top})\left(\widehat{\Sigma}_{-1}-\frac{n-1}{n}\Sigma\right)\right]\right)^{2}\leqslant\frac{2{\rm Tr}(\Sigma^{2})^{2}+2(\alpha-1){\rm Tr}(\Sigma^{4})}{n}.

(79)

Step 4: final bound.

Due to the inequalities (
J
), (
77
), (
78
), and (
79
) the variance of
\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}
is not greater than

{\rm Var}\left(\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}\right)\leqslant\frac{8{\rm Tr}(\Sigma^{2})^{2}+8\alpha{\rm Tr}(\Sigma^{4})}{n^{2}}+\frac{8\alpha^{2}{\rm Tr}(\Sigma^{2})^{2}+48\alpha{\rm Tr}(\Sigma)^{2}{\rm Tr}(\Sigma^{2})}{n^{3}}.

The proof is finished.

\square

Appendix K
Proof of Lemma
J.1

Rewriting
\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\top}-\Omega\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}
in the form

\left\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\top}-\Omega\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}={\rm Tr}\left[(\boldsymbol{\zeta}\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\top}-\Omega)(\boldsymbol{\zeta}\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\top}-\Omega)\right]=\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\|^{4}-2\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\top}\Omega\boldsymbol{\zeta}+{\rm Tr}(\Omega^{2}),

we note that

{\rm Var}\left(\left\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\top}-\Omega\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}\right)={\rm Var}\left(\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\|^{4}-2\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\top}\Omega\boldsymbol{\zeta}\right)\leqslant 2{\rm Var}\left(\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\|^{4}\right)+8{\rm Var}\left(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\top}\Omega\boldsymbol{\zeta}\right).

(80)

Here we used the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to bound the variance of
\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\|^{4}-2\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\top}\Omega\boldsymbol{\zeta}
. Due to Assumption
2.5
, it holds that

\displaystyle{\rm Var}\left(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\top}\Omega\boldsymbol{\zeta}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\top}\Omega\boldsymbol{\zeta}-\mathbb{E}\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\top}\Omega\boldsymbol{\zeta}\right)^{2}=\mathbb{E}\left(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\top}\Omega\boldsymbol{\zeta}-{\rm Tr}(\Omega^{2})\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\top}\Omega\boldsymbol{\zeta}-{\rm Tr}(\Omega^{2})\right)^{4}}\leqslant\alpha\left\|\Omega^{2}\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}=\alpha{\rm Tr}(\Omega^{4}).

(81)

On the other hand,

\displaystyle{\rm Var}\left(\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\|^{4}\right)

\displaystyle={\rm Var}\left(\big(\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\|^{2}-{\rm Tr}(\Omega)+{\rm Tr}(\Omega)\big)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle={\rm Var}\left(\big(\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\|^{2}-{\rm Tr}(\Omega)\big)^{2}+2{\rm Tr}(\Omega)\big(\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\|^{2}-{\rm Tr}(\Omega)\big)+{\rm Tr}(\Omega)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle={\rm Var}\left(\big(\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\|^{2}-{\rm Tr}(\Omega)\big)^{2}+2{\rm Tr}(\Omega)\big(\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\|^{2}-{\rm Tr}(\Omega)\big)\right).

The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality implies that

\displaystyle{\rm Var}\left(\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\|^{4}\right)

\displaystyle={\rm Var}\left(\big(\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\|^{2}-{\rm Tr}(\Omega)\big)^{2}+2{\rm Tr}(\Omega)\big(\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\|^{2}-{\rm Tr}(\Omega)\big)\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant 2{\rm Var}\left(\big(\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\|^{2}-{\rm Tr}(\Omega)\big)^{2}\right)+8{\rm Tr}(\Omega)^{2}\,{\rm Var}\left(\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\|^{2}-{\rm Tr}(\Omega)\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\mathbb{E}\left(\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\|^{2}-{\rm Tr}(\Omega)\right)^{4}+8{\rm Tr}(\Omega)^{2}\,\mathbb{E}\left(\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\|^{2}-{\rm Tr}(\Omega)\right)^{2}.

Thus, according to the conditions of the lemma,

\displaystyle{\rm Var}\left(\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\|^{4}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\mathbb{E}\left(\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\|^{2}-{\rm Tr}(\Omega)\right)^{4}+8{\rm Tr}(\Omega)^{2}\mathbb{E}\left(\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\|^{2}-{\rm Tr}(\Omega)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\alpha^{2}\left\|\Omega\right\|_{\rm F}^{4}+8\alpha{\rm Tr}(\Omega)^{2}\left\|\Omega\right\|_{\rm F}^{2}

(82)

\displaystyle=2\alpha^{2}{\rm Tr}(\Omega^{2})^{2}+8\alpha{\rm Tr}(\Omega)^{2}{\rm Tr}(\Omega^{2}).

Hence, summing up the inequalities (
80
), (
K
), and (
K
), we obtain that

\displaystyle{\rm Var}\left(\left\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\top}-\Omega\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant 4\alpha^{2}{\rm Tr}(\Omega^{2})^{2}+16\alpha{\rm Tr}(\Omega)^{2}{\rm Tr}(\Omega^{2})+8\alpha{\rm Tr}(\Omega^{4})

\displaystyle\leqslant 4\alpha^{2}{\rm Tr}(\Omega^{2})^{2}+24\alpha{\rm Tr}(\Omega)^{2}{\rm Tr}(\Omega^{2}),

and the first part of the proof is finished. In order to get an upper bound on the expectation of
\left\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\top}-\Omega\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}
, we observe that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\top}-\Omega\right\|_{{\rm F}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\|^{4}-2\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\top}\Omega\boldsymbol{\zeta}+{\rm Tr}(\Omega^{2})\right)=\mathbb{E}\left(\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\|^{2}-{\rm Tr}(\Omega)+{\rm Tr}(\Omega)\right)^{2}-{\rm Tr}(\Omega^{2})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\|^{2}-{\rm Tr}(\Omega)\right)^{2}+{\rm Tr}(\Omega)^{2}-{\rm Tr}(\Omega^{2})\leqslant{\rm Tr}(\Omega)^{2}+(\alpha-1){\rm Tr}(\Omega^{2}),

where the last line follows from Assumption
2.5
and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.

\square

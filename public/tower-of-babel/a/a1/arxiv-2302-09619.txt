1.
Introduction

The pluricanonical maps of a surface with non-negative kodaira dimension play a important role in the calssification of algebraic surfaces. Let
S
be a surface, and let
K_{S}
be a canonical divisor. Suppose the
n
-genus
P_{n}(S)=h^{0}(S,\mathscr{O}_{S}(nK_{S}))\geq 2
, we can define a rational map
\phi_{n}\colon\thinspace S\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{P_{n}(S)-1}
, which is called the
n
th-pluricanonical map
of
S
. Bombieri
[
Bom73
]
showed that the
n
th-pluricanonical maps
\phi_{n}
are embeddings when
n\geq 5
and are birational when
n\geq 3
except for finite many cases; the bicanonical map
\phi_{2}
is birational if
K_{S}^{2}\geq 10
and
p_{g}(S)\geq 6
except for
S
admiting a genus 2 fiberation, in which case
\phi_{2}
is generically a double covering.

The image
\Sigma
of the canonical map
\phi_{1}\colon\thinspace S\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{p_{g}(S)-1}
is either a surface or a curve. If
\Sigma
is a curve, we say that the canonical linear system
|K_{S}|
is composed of a pencil. Let
f\colon\thinspace X\to B
be the fiberation induced by the rational map
\phi_{1}
, i.e.,
\mu\colon\thinspace X\to S
resolve the base locus of
|K_{S}|
and take
f
to be the Stein factorization of the composition morphism
\phi_{1}\circ\mu\colon\thinspace X\to\Sigma
.
Let
g,b
and
q
be the genus of a general fiber of
f
, the genus of
B
and the irregularity
q(S):=h^{0}(S,\mathscr{O}_{S})
of
S
. Beauville
[
Bea79
]
showed that
2\leq g\leq 5
if
\chi(S,\mathscr{O}_{S})\geq 21.
Later, Xiao
[
Xia85
]
showed that either
b=q=1
or
b=0,q\leq 2
.

If
\Sigma
is a surface. Beauville
[
Bea79
]
showed that either
p_{g}(\Sigma)=0
or
p_{g}(\Sigma)=p_{g}(S)
in which case
\Sigma
is of general type. In the first case, if
\chi(S,\mathscr{O}_{S})\geq 31
, then
\deg\phi_{1}\leq 9
; in the second case, if
\chi(S,\mathscr{O}_{S})\geq 14
, then
\deg\phi_{1}\leq 3
. More refiment results can be find in
[
Xia86
]
.

In this paper we will generalize the above result to open algebraic surfaces, for general references here are
[
Iit76
,
Iit77
]
. Let
S^{0}
be an open algebraic surface, by Nagata and Hironaka, we can embed it into a complete algebraic surface
S^{0}\hookrightarrow S
such that the complement
D=S\setminus S^{0}
is a divisor with simple normal corssing singularities. It is well known that cohomology groups of many vector bundles constructed using logarithmic forms with poles algong
D
depend only on
S^{0}
. Thus if we want to study properties of
S^{0}
, it is natural to consider the properties of the divisor
K_{S}+D
in the spirit of the theory of birational geometry. Thus it is convenient to consider a log pair
(S,D)
consists of a smooth projective surface
S
and a reduced effective divisor
D
on
S
with only simple normal crossing singularities, such a pair is also called
log smooth surface
. We will consider a
minimal
log smooth surface
(S,D)
, in the sence of Tsunoda which he called
almost minimal
(see for instance
[
Fuj79
,
Tsu83
]
).

Definition 1.1
.

We say that a projective log smooth surface
(S,D)
is
minimal
if
K_{S}+D
has the
Zariski-Fujita decomposition
K_{S}+D=(K_{S}+D^{\sharp})+\mathrm{Bk}(D)
satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

\mathrm{Bk}(D)
is an effecitve
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor such that
\mathrm{Bk}(D)\leq D
;

(2)

K_{S}+D^{\sharp}
is nef;

(3)

the intersection matrix
\mathrm{Bk}(D)
is negative defined;

(4)

(K_{S}+D^{\sharp})\cdot C=0
for any prime component
C
of
\mathrm{Bk}(D)
.

We can define analogous invariants of a log surface as usual surface:
p_{g}(S,D):=h^{0}(S,K_{S}+D)
the
log geometric genus
,
q(S,D)=h^{0}(S,\Omega_{S}^{1}(D))
the
log irregularity
,
\kappa(S,D)=\kappa(K_{S}+D)
the
log Kodaira dimension
. We say that a log surface
(S,D)
is of

if
\kappa(S,D)=2
or
(K_{S}+D^{\sharp})^{2}>0
.

The log canonical map
\phi\colon\thinspace S\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{p_{g}(S,D)-1}
has been study by many authors. Sakai
[
Sak80
]
considered the case when
D
is a stable curve and proved a Noether type inequality and a generalization of Bogomolov-Miyaoka-Yau inequality. Soon afterwards, Tsunoda and De-Qi Zhang
[
TZ92
]
generalized the result to the case when
(S,D)
is minimal log smooth and of log general type.

Our purpose is to consider minimal log surfaces
(S,D)
of log general type whose log canonical linear system
|K_{S}+D|
is composed of a penciel. We can assume, by blowing up at the base points of

if necessary, that

is base point free (see Lemma
3.2
). Let
\phi\colon\thinspace S\to\Sigma\subset\mathbb{P}^{p_{g}(S,D)-1}
be the correspongding morphism, then

is a curve. Let
f\colon\thinspace S\to B
be the Stein factorization of
\phi\colon\thinspace S\to\Sigma
. We will study the fiberation
f\colon\thinspace S\to B
induced by the linear system
|K_{S}+D|
. Let
g
and

denote the genus of a general fiber

of

and the base curve

respectively. It is of interest to know the possibilities of invariants

and the intersection numbers

.
The main results of this paper are the following.

Theorem 1.2

With the notations as above. Then we have

Preliminary

Throughout we work over an algebraically closed field of characteristic

A

We write
D\geq 0
for an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on a normal variety

. Let

2.1.
Nef vector bundles

In this subsection we recall some basic results on nef vector bundles that will be used throughtout the paper.

Definition 2.1
.

A vector bundle
\mathscr{E}
on a variety
X
is said to be
nef
if the tautological line bundle
\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{E})}(1)
is a nef line bundle on the projective bundle
\mathbb{P}_{X}(\mathscr{E})
over
X
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\mathscr{E}
be a vector bundle on a variety
X
.

(1)

If
\mathscr{E}
is nef, then so is any quotient bundle
\mathscr{Q}
of
\mathscr{E}
.

(2)

Let
f\colon\thinspace X\to Y
be a finite morphism of varieties. If
\mathscr{E}
is a nef vector bundle on
Y
, then
\mathscr{E}
is nef if and only if the pull-back
f^{*}\mathscr{E}
is a nef vector bundle on
X
.

(3)

Let
\mathscr{E}
be a vector bundle on a variety
X
, then
\mathscr{E}
is nef if and only if given any finite morphism
\nu\colon\thinspace C\to X
form a non-singular projective curve
C
to
X
, and given any quotient line bundle
\mathscr{L}
of
\nu^{*}\mathscr{E}
, one has
\deg\mathscr{L}\geq 0
.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
\mathscr{E}
be a vector bundle on a non-singular projective curve
C
of genus
g
. If
\mathscr{E}\otimes\omega_{C}^{-1}
is nef, then
\deg\mathscr{E}\geq 2(g-1)\mathrm{rank}\,\mathscr{E}
and thus
\chi(C,\mathscr{E})\geq(g-1)\mathrm{rank}\,{\mathscr{E}}
.

Proof.

By the Riemann-Roch theorem,
\chi(C,\mathscr{E})=\deg\mathscr{E}-\mathrm{rank}\,\mathscr{E}(g-1)
. If
\mathscr{E}\otimes\omega_{C}^{-1}
is nef, then we have
\deg\mathscr{E}\geq 2(g-1)\mathrm{rank}\,\mathscr{E}
and thus
\chi(C,\mathscr{E})\geq(g-1)\mathrm{rank}\,\mathscr{E}
.
∎

Let
f\colon\thinspace S\to B
be a fiberation from a smooth projective surface to a smooth curve. By Fujita’s theorem
[
Fuj78
]
,
f_{*}\omega_{S/B}
is a nef vector bundle on
B
. Let
D
be a reduced divisor on
S
which has only simple normal crossing singularities. To show that the sheaf
f_{*}\omega_{S/B}(D)
is also nef we need the following.

Proposition 2.4
.

[
CG17
]

Let
\mathscr{E}
be a vector bundle on a non-singular projective curve
C
, and
\mathscr{F}
be a subvector bundle of
\mathscr{E}
with the same rank of
\mathscr{E}
. If
\mathscr{F}
is nef, then so is
\mathscr{E}
.

2.2.
Divisors of surfaces

We review some of the standard facts on divisors of algebraic surfaces.

A reduced divisor
D=\sum C_{j}
on a surface
S
is also called a curve. The
arithmetic genus
of
D
is
p_{a}(D)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}1-\chi(D,\mathscr{O}_{D})
. By Riemann-Roch formula, we have

K_{S}\cdot D+D^{2}=2p_{a}(D)-2.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
D
be a reduced divisor on a surface
S
. If we write
D=D_{1}+D_{2}
into sum of two reduced divisors
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
, then

p_{a}(D)=p_{a}(D_{1})+p_{a}(D_{2})+D_{1}\cdot D_{2}-1.

In particular, if we write
D=\sum_{i=1}^{r}D_{i}
into sum of all its irreducible components, then we have

p_{a}(D)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}p_{a}(D_{i})+1+\sum_{i<j}D_{i}\cdot D_{j}-r.

The
weighted dual graph

\Gamma=\Gamma(D)
of
D
is the following: each curve
C_{j}
in
D
corresponds to a vertex
v_{j}
. Two vertices
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
are connected by an edge of weight
m
if the corresponding curves has intersection number
C_{i}\cdot C_{j}=m
. Each vertex
v_{j}
has a weight
n_{j}=-C_{j}^{2}
. The branching muber, denoted by
\beta(v_{j})
or
\beta_{D}(C_{j})
, of a vertex
v_{j}
is the muber of vertices which is connected to
v_{j}
.

A reduced divisor
D
is called
simple normal corssing
(SNC for short) if each irreducible component is non-singular and
D
has at most node. In this case we will say that the pair
(S,D)
is
log smooth
. The dual graph
\Gamma
of a SNC divisor
D
is a simple graph, i.e., it has no loops (an edge connected one vertex to itself) and multiple edges.

Remark 2.6
.

Let
D
be a reduced SNC divisor on
S
whose dual graph has
r
vertices and
l
edges. By Lemma
2.5
, its arithmetic genus
p_{a}(D)
equals
\sum_{i=1}^{r}p_{a}(D_{i})+1+l-r.
Note that, as
D
is SNC, the sum
\sum_{i<j}D_{i}\cdot D_{j}
of intersection numbers equals
l
.

A
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor is said to be
nef
(resp.
pseudo-effective
) if
D\cdot C\geq 0
for any irreducible curve
C
(resp.
D\cdot P
for any nef
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
P
) on
S
.

For a pseudo-effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D
on a smooth projective surface
S
, we have the
Zariski-Fujita decomposition

D=P+N
which satisfies and is determined by the following numerical properties

(1)

P
and
N
are
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors;

(2)

N\geq 0
and the intersection matrix
(C_{i}\cdot C_{j})
for the prime components
C_{i}
of
N
is negative defined.

(3)

P
is nef;

(4)

P\cdot C_{i}=0
for every prime componet
C_{i}
of
N
.

We call
P
(resp.
N
) the
nef part
(resp.
negative part
) of
D
. For the proofs we refer the reader to
[
Fuj79
]
.

2.3.
Theory of Peeling

A connected curve
E
in
D
is said to be
rational
(resp.
admissible
) if it consists only of rational curves (resp. if there are no
(-1)
-curves in
E
and the intersection matrix of
E
is negative defiend). Let
L
be an admissible rational linear chain in
D
. We write
L=\sum_{i=1}^{r}C_{i}
as the sum of its prime components. Suppose
L
satisfies the condition:
\beta_{D}(C_{1})=1
and
\beta_{D}(C_{i})=2
for
2\leq i\leq r-1
. If
\beta_{D}(C_{r})=1
, we call
L
a
rod
, i.e.,
L
is a connected component of
D
. If
\beta_{D}(C_{r})=2
and
C_{r}\cdot C_{r+1}=1
for a prime component
C_{r+1}
other than
C_{r-1}
, we call
L
a
twig
. An admissible rational twig is called maximal, if it is not extended to an admissible rational twig with more irreducible components of
D
, i.e.,
\beta_{D}(C_{r+1})\geq 3
. A connected curve
F
in
D
is called a
fork
if its dual graph is that of the exceptional curves of a minimal resolution of a log terminal singular point and is not a chain. The prime component
C
of a linear chain
L
in
D
with
\beta_{D}(C)=1
is called a
tip
.

Let
(S,D)
be a log smooth pair with
K_{S}+D
is pseudo-effective. In this subsection, we will consider the Zariski-Fujita decomposition

K_{S}+D=(K_{S}+D)^{+}+(K_{S}+D)^{-}.

Here and subseqently,
(K_{S}+D)^{+}
(resp.
(K_{S}+D)^{-}
) will denote the nef part (resp. negative part) of
K_{S}+D
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
S
be a smooth projective surface, and
D
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-boundary on
S
such that the log pair
(S,D)
is log smooth. If
E
is an irreducible curve on
S
with
E^{2}\leq-2
and
(K_{S}+D)\cdot E<0
, then
E\subset\mathrm{Supp}\,(D)
and
E
is a smooth rational curve.

Proof.

By assumption, we have

K_{S}\cdot E=2p_{a}(E)-2-E^{2}\geq 0

and thus
D\cdot E<0
,
E
is a prime component of
D
. Write
D=aE+D^{\prime}
with
0<a\leq 1
and
E\nsubseteq\mathrm{Supp}\,(D^{\prime})
, we have

2p_{a}(E)-2=(K_{S}+E)\cdot E\leq(K_{S}+aE+D^{\prime})\cdot=(K_{S}+D)\cdot E<0.

Thus
p_{a}(E)=0
, so that
E
is a smooth rational curve.
∎

Let
L
be an admissible rational linear chain in
D
. We write
L=\sum_{i=1}^{r}C_{i}
as the sum of its prime components. Suppose
\beta_{D}(C_{1})=1
, thus
C_{1}
is the tip of
L
. By the adjunction formula, for each
C_{j}
, we have

(K_{S}+D)\cdot C_{j}=-2+\beta_{D}(C_{j}).

Note that the intersection matrix
(C_{i}\cdot C_{j})
is negative defined, the following system of
r
linear equations in
r
variables has a unique solution

(2.1)

\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}C_{i}\cdot C_{j}=(K_{S}+D)\cdot C_{j}

Since
(K_{S}+D)\cdot C_{1}=-1<0
, it follows that
0<a_{i}\leq 1
for all
1\leq i\leq r
. The
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\mathrm{Bk}(L)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}C_{i}
is called
the bark
of
L
, and the process of substracting
\mathrm{Bk}(L)
from
L
is called
the peeling
of
L
.

Let
F
be an admissible rational fork, write
F=\sum_{i=1}^{r}C_{i}
as the sum of its prime components. In the same manner we can see that the system of linear equations
2.1
has a unique solution
\mathrm{Bk}(F)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}C_{i}
with
0<a_{i}\leq 1
for all
1\leq i\leq r
, which is also called the Bark of
F
.

Let
\{T_{\lambda}\}
,
\{R_{\mu}\}
and
\{F_{\nu}\}
be the sets of all admissible rational maximal twigs, all admissible rational rods and all admissible rational forks, respectively, where no prime coponent of
T_{\lambda}
’s belong to
R_{\mu}
’s and
F_{\nu}
’s. Since
T_{\lambda}
’s,
R_{\mu}
’s and
F_{\nu}
’s are disjoint from each other, we can peel the barks of
T_{\lambda}
’s,
R_{\mu}
’s and
F_{\nu}
’s independently. We set

D^{\sharp}=D-\sum_{\lambda}\mathrm{Bk}(T_{\lambda})-\sum_{\mu}\mathrm{Bk}(R_{\mu})-\sum_{\nu}\mathrm{Bk}(F_{\nu}).

The
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\mathrm{Bk}(D)=D-D^{\sharp}
is called
the bark
of
D
. By the above construction, we have

(1)

D^{\sharp}=D-\mathrm{Bk}(D)\geq 0
;

(2)

write
\mathrm{Bk}(D)=\sum_{i}a_{i}C_{i}
, the intersection matrix
(C_{i}\cdot C_{j})_{1\leq i,j\leq r}
is negative defined;

(3)

(K_{S}+D^{\sharp})\cdot C=0
for every prime component
C
of
T_{\lambda}
’s,
R_{\mu}
’s and
F_{\nu}
’s;

(4)

(K_{S}+D)^{+}=(K_{S}+D^{\sharp})^{+}
and
(K_{S}+D)^{-}=(K_{S}+D^{\sharp})^{-}+\mathrm{Bk}(D)
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
(S,D)
be a log smooth pair of log general type with
D
reduced. With the notations as above. If
E
is an irreducible curve on
S
such that
(K_{S}+D^{\sharp})\cdot E<0
, then
E
is an exceptional curve of the first kind.

Proof.

Consider the Zariski-Fujita decompositon
K_{S}+D^{\sharp}=P+N
, where
P
is the nef part and
N
is the negative part of
K_{S}+D^{\sharp}
. Since
(S,D)
is of log general type, we have
P^{2}>0
. By assumption,
(K_{S}+D^{\sharp})\cdot E<0
and note that
P\cdot E\geq 0
, it follows that
N\cdot E<0
and thus
E\subset\mathrm{Supp}\,(N)
. By the properties of Zariski-Fujita decompositon, we have
P\cdot E=0
. By the Hodge index theorem, we see that
E^{2}\leq 0
. Again by the properties of Zariski-Fujita decompositon, the intersection matrix of supports of
N
is negative defined, we infer that
E^{2}<0
. It remains to prove that the case
E^{2}\leq-2
can not occur.

Suppose
E^{2}\leq-2
, then by Lemma
2.7
, we have
E\subset\mathrm{Supp}\,(D^{\sharp})
and
E
is a smooth rational curve. Since
(K_{S}+D^{\sharp})\cdot C=0
every prime component
C
of
\mathrm{Bk}(D)
, it follows that
\mu_{D^{\sharp}}(E)=1
. Let
C_{1},\dots,C_{k}
be the prime components of
D
that meets
E
where
k=\beta_{D}(E)
. Set
a_{i}=\mu_{D^{\sharp}}(C_{i})
, then we have
(K_{S}+D^{\sharp})\cdot E=-2+a_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}
. Note that
(K_{S}+D)\cdot E=-2+k
, if
k\leq 1
, then
E
is either an isolated component of
D
or a tip of an admissible rational maximal twig or an admissible rational rod, in either case we have
\mu_{\mathrm{Bk}(D)}(E)>0
, which contradicts to
\mu_{D^{\sharp}}(E)=1
. Therefore we can assume that
k\geq 2
. By assumption, we have
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}<2
and
k\geq 2
. Thus at least one
i
with
a_{i}<1
, say
a_{1}<1
. We can conclude that there exists an admissible maximal rational twig
T
of
D
with
C_{1}\leq T
and
E\nsubseteq\mathrm{Supp}\,(T)
. If
k=2
, then
T+E
is also an admissible rational twig of
D
, which contradicts the maximality of
T
. By Lemma
[
Fuj82
, Lemma 6.16]
,
a_{i}=1-\frac{1}{d_{i}}
with
d_{i}\geq 2
or
a_{i}=1
, let
l
be the number of

with
a_{i}<1
, so we have
k-2<\frac{1}{d_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{d_{l}}
and
k\geq 3
. It follows that
l=k=3
. Therefore
a_{i}<1
for all
1\leq i\leq 3
, then there exists an admissible rational fork
F
such that
E+C_{1}+C_{2}+C_{3}\leq F
, in this case
\mu_{\mathrm{Bk}(F)}(E)>0
, which contradicts to
\mu_{\mathrm{Bk}(D)}(E)=0
and the proof is complete.
∎

After a succesion of several contractions of excetional curve

Write

(2.2)

Proposition 2.11

Let

By a theorem of Deligne (cf.

From this, we can see that

The Riemann-Roch theorem gives

\displaystyle\chi(S,\Omega_{S}^{1}(\log D))

a\geq 2
be an integer, and
\mathscr{K}=\{p_{0},p_{1},\dots,p_{4a-4}\}
be a cluster on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, where
p_{0},p_{1},\dots,p_{4a-4}
are points on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. Consider the linear system of plane curves of degree
3a
with assigned base points
\mathscr{K}
of type
(3a;3a-3,2^{4a-4})
. Let
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
be an irreducible member of this linear system. Let
\pi\colon\thinspace S\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
be the blowing-up at
\mathscr{K}
. Let
E_{i}
be the exceptional divisor over
p_{i}
,
\widetilde{C}
be the strict transform of
C
on
S
and
H
be the total transform of the class of line on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, we have

\widetilde{C}\sim 3aH-(3a-3)E_{0}-2\sum_{i=1}^{4a-4}E_{i}.

Take
D=\widetilde{C}
, consider the log surface
(S,D)
, we have

K_{S}+D\sim(3a-3)H-(3a-4)E_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{4a-4}E_{i}.

By Lemma
2.15
,
K_{S}+D
is big and the linear system
|(a-1)H-(a-2)E_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{4a-4}E_{i}|
is non-empty. Take
G\in|(a-1)H-(a-2)E_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{4a-4}E_{i}|
we have

(K_{S}+D)\cdot G<0,

thus
G
is contained in the fixed part of
|K_{S}+D|
. Sustracting
(a-1)H-(a-2)E_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{4a-4}E_{i}
from
K_{S}+D
, we get
(2a-2)(H-E_{0})
, which is a pencil. For the same reason as Example
4.2
, the induced fiberation
f\colon\thinspace S\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
has invariants
g=b=0
and
k=3a
.

The following example show that if we drop the assumption
p_{a}(D)\leq 2(l+q(S))+1-h^{1,1}(S)
of Theorem
3.4
, then the invariants
k
and
g
can be arbitrarily large.

Example 4.4
.

Let
0\leq e\leq g
be integers, consider the Hirzebruch surface
\Sigma_{e}=\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{O}\oplus\mathscr{O}(e))
of degree
e
. Let
\pi\colon\thinspace\Sigma_{e}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
be the
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-fiberation, let
\Delta_{0}
and
\Gamma
be its section with
\Delta_{0}^{2}=-e
and its general fiber. Set
\Delta_{\infty}=\Delta_{0}+e\Gamma
, then
\Delta_{\infty}
is the class of tautological line bundle of
\Sigma_{e}
and we have
\Delta_{\infty}^{2}=e
. Set
a=g+1-e>0
, then
2\Delta_{\infty}+a\Gamma
is very ample, and a general member
D
of the linear system
|2\Delta_{\infty}+a\Gamma|
is a smooth irreducible hyperelliptic curve of genus
g
. Choose
D_{0},D_{1}\in|2\Delta_{\infty}+a\Gamma|
, consider the Lefschetz pencil
\{\alpha D_{0}+\beta D_{1}\}
,
[\alpha\colon\thinspace\beta]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Since
D_{0}\cdot D_{1}=(2\Delta_{\infty}+a\Gamma)^{2}=4e+4a=4g+4.
Therefore we have
4g+4
distinct base points
p_{1},\dots,p_{4g+4}
of this pencil. Let
\nu\colon\thinspace S\to\Sigma_{e}
be the blowing-up at these base points, then we obtain a fiberation
f\colon\thinspace S\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
whose general fiber
F
with genus
g
. Note that

\displaystyle F\sim\nu^{*}(2\Delta_{\infty}+a\Gamma)-E_{1}-\cdots-E_{4g+4},

\displaystyle K_{S}\sim\nu^{*}(-2\Delta_{\infty}+(e-2)\Gamma)+E_{1}+\cdots+E_{4g+4},

where
E_{i}
is the exceptional divisor over
p_{i}
. Consider the linear system

|\nu^{*}(x\Delta_{\infty}+y\Gamma)-\sum_{i=1}^{4g+4}2E_{i}|.

If

(4.1)

(x+1)(y+\frac{ex}{2})+x-3(4g+4)>0

then it contains an irreducible member
D
, then we have

K_{S}+D\sim\nu^{*}((x-2)\Delta_{\infty}+(y+e-2)\Gamma)-\sum_{i=1}^{4g-g}E_{i}.

If

(4.2)

(x-2)(y+e-2+\frac{(x-2)e}{2})-\frac{1}{2}(4g+4)>0

then
K_{S}+D
is big.
Set
M=\nu^{*}((x-4)\Delta+(y+e-a-2)\Gamma)
, if

(4.3)

(x-3)(y+e-a-1+\frac{(x-4)e}{2})>0

then we can assume
M\geq 0
. If

(4.4)

(K_{S}+D)\cdot M=(x-4)(x-2)+(x-2)(y+e-a-2)+(x-4)(y+e-2)<0

then
M
is contained in the fixed part of
|K_{S}+D|
. Note that
K_{S}+D-M\sim F
. So
|K_{S}+D|
is composed of a pencil
|F|
. In this case the corresponding fiberation is
f\colon\thinspace X\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
, its general fiber has genus
g
and
D\cdot F=x(g+1+e)+2y-8g-8
.

We find that
x=8,y=1
, the inequalities (
4.1
), (
4.2
), (
4.3
) and (
4.4
) become

\displaystyle e>\frac{12g-5}{36}\quad(\ref{eq: D})

\displaystyle e>\frac{g+4}{12}\quad(\ref{eq: big})

\displaystyle e>\frac{g+1}{4}\quad(\ref{eq: effective})

\displaystyle e<\frac{3g-4}{8}\quad(\ref{eq: fixed part})

When
g\geq 8
, we have
\frac{3g-4}{8}>\frac{12g-13}{36}>\frac{g+1}{4}>\frac{g+4}{12}
. Since
e
is an integer, not every
g\geq 8
the interval
(\frac{12g-13}{36},\frac{3g-4}{8})
contains an integer, we find that
g=10,e=3
satisfies all the inequalities, and when
g\geq 27
we have
(\frac{12g-13}{36},\frac{3g-4}{8})\cap\mathbb{Z}\neq\varnothing
. We can see that
D\cdot F=8e+2
, therefore the genus
g
and the intersection number
k=D\cdot F
can be arbitrarily large.

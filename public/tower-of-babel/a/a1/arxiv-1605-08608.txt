On Free Field Realizations of 𝑾 ⁡ ( 𝟐 , 𝟐 ) \boldsymbol{W(2,2)} -Modules
Dražen ADAMOVIĆ † and Gordan RADOBOLJA ‡

Abstract
The aim of the paper is to study modules for the twisted Heisenberg–Virasoro algebra ℋ \mathcal{H} at level zero as modules for the W ⁡ ( 2 , 2 ) W(2,2) -algebra by using construction from [ J. Pure Appl. Algebra 219 (2015), 4322–4342, arXiv:1405.1707]. We prove that the irreducible highest weight ℋ {\mathcal{H}} -module is irreducible as W ⁡ ( 2 , 2 ) W(2,2) -module if and only if it has a typical highest weight. Finally, we construct a screening operator acting on the Heisenberg–Virasoro vertex algebra whose kernel is exactly W ⁡ ( 2 , 2 ) W(2,2) vertex algebra.

Lie algebra

Highest weight representation theory of the twisted Heisenberg–Virasoro Lie algebra has also been studied recently. Representations with nontrivial action of

have been developed in
[

[

Irreducible highest weight modules of highest weights
(0,0)
over these algebras carry the structure of simple vertex operator algebras. Let us denote these vertex operator algebras as
L^{W(2,2)}(c_{L},c_{W})
and

[

is also a

-module. In this paper we shall completely describe the structure of the irreducible highest weight
\mathcal{H}
-modules as

-modules. We show that in generic case the resulting

-module is irreducible. However, in case of a module of highest weight such that associated Verma module over
W(2,2)
contains cosingular vectors (we shall call this kind of weight atypical), irreducible
\mathcal{H}
-module is reducible over
W(2,2)

The twisted Heisenberg–Virasoro algebra
\mathcal{H}
is the universal central extension of the Lie algebra of differential operators on a circle of order at most one. It is the infinite-dimensional complex Lie algebra with a basis

\displaystyle\{L(n),I(n)\colon n\in\mathbb{Z}\}\cup\{C_{L},C_{LI},C_{I}\}

and commutation relations

\displaystyle[L(n),L(m)]=(n-m)L(n+m)+\delta_{n,-m}\frac{n^{3}-n}{12}C_{L},

\displaystyle[L(n),I(m)]=-mI(n+m)-\delta_{n,-m}\big(n^{2}+n\big)C_{LI},

\displaystyle[I(n),I(m)]=n\delta_{n,-m}C_{I},\qquad[{\mathcal{H}},C_{L}]=[{\mathcal{H}},C_{LI}]=[\mathcal{H},C_{I}]=0.

The Lie algebra
\mathcal{H}
admits the following triangular decomposition

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}^{-}\oplus\mathcal{H}^{0}\oplus\mathcal{H}^{+},

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\pm}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{I(\pm n),L(\pm n)\,|\,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}\},\qquad\mathcal{H}^{0}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{I(0),L(0),C_{L},C_{L,I},C_{I}\}.

Although they seem to be two similar extensions of the Virasoro algebra, representation theories of

are different. The main reason for that lies in the fact that

is a central element, while
W(0)
is not. However, applying free field realization, we shall see that highest weight modules over the two algebras are related.

3
Irreducible highest weight modules

In this section we present main results of the paper which completely describe the structure of (irreducible) highest weight modules for
\mathcal{H}
as
W(2,2)
-modules. The main tool is the homomorphism between
W(2,2)
and the Heisenberg–Virasoro vertex algebras from
[
4
]
.

L^{W(2,2)}(c_{L},c_{W},0,0)
is a simple universal vertex algebra associated to Lie algebra

(cf.
[

,
20
]

\displaystyle L(z)=Y(\omega,z)=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}L(n)z^{-n-2},\qquad W(z)=Y(W,z)=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}W(n)z^{-n-2},

\displaystyle L(z)=Y(\omega,z)=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}L(n)z^{-n-2},\qquad I(z)=Y(I,z)=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}I(n)z^{-n-1},

Note that

Throughout the rest of this section we assume that

we have:

\displaystyle[L(n),S_{i}(m)]=-mS_{i}(n+m),\qquad i=0,1,

\displaystyle[W(n),S_{0}(m)]=0,\qquad[W(n),S_{1}(m)]=2mc_{L,I}S_{0}(n+m).

In particular,
S_{0}(0)
and
S_{1}(0)
are screening operators. Moreover,

\displaystyle S_{1}=S_{1}(0)\colon\ L^{\mathcal{H}}(c_{L},c_{L,I})\rightarrow L^{\mathcal{H}}(1,0)

is nontrivial and
S_{1}(0)I(-1){\bf 1}=-v_{0}.

Proof.

Since
L(k)v^{i}=\delta_{k,0}v^{i}
for
k\geq 0
, commutator formula gives that

\displaystyle[L(n),S_{i}(m)]=-mS_{i}(n+m).

Next we calculate
[W(n),S_{1}(m)]
. We have

\displaystyle W(-1)v^{1}=2I(-1)v^{0}=-2c_{L,I}L(-1)v^{0},

\displaystyle W(0)v^{1}=-2c_{L,I}v^{0},\qquad W(n)v^{1}=0,\qquad n\geq 0.

This implies that

\displaystyle[W(n),S_{1}(m)]=2c_{L,I}mS_{0}(n+m).

Since
W(n)v^{0}=0
for
n\geq-1
we get

\displaystyle[W(n),S_{0}(m)]=0.

Therefore we have proved that
S_{i}(0)
,
i=0,1
are screening operators. Next we have

\displaystyle S_{1}(0)I(-1){\bf 1}=\operatorname{Res}_{z}Y_{\rm ext}\big(v^{1},z\big)I(-1)=\operatorname{Res}_{z}e^{zL(-1)}Y(I(-1){\bf 1},-z)v^{1}=-v_{0}.

The proof follows.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

S_{1}
is a derivation of the vertex algebra
\mathcal{V}^{\rm ext}
and we have

\displaystyle\Ker_{L^{\mathcal{H}}(c_{L},c_{L,I})}S_{1}\cong L^{W(2,2)}(c_{L},c_{W}).

Proof.

By construction
S_{1}=\operatorname{Res}_{z}Y_{\rm ext}(v^{1},z)
, so
S_{1}
is a derivation so
\overline{W}=\Ker_{L^{\mathcal{H}}(c_{L},c_{L,I})}S_{1}
is a vertex subalgebra of
L^{\mathcal{H}}(c_{L},c_{L,I})
. Since

\displaystyle S_{1}L(-2){\bf 1}=S_{1}W(-2){\bf 1}=0

we have that
L^{W(2,2)}(c_{L},c_{W})\subset\overline{W}
. Since
S_{1}I(-1){\bf 1}\neq 0
, we have that
I(-1){\bf 1}
does not belong to
\overline{W}
. By using the fact that
L^{\mathcal{H}}(c_{L},c_{L,I})
is as
W(2,2)
-module generated by singular vector
\bf{1}
and cosingular vector
I(-1){\bf 1}
(see Example
3.9
) we get that
\overline{W}=L^{W(2,2)}(c_{L},c_{W})
. The proof follows.
∎

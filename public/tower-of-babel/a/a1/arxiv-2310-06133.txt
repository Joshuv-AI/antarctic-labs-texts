Abstract
. This paper determines the full derived deformation theory of certain smooth rational curves C \mathrm{C} in Calabi-Yau 3-folds, by determining all higher A ∞ \mathrm{A}_{\infty} -products in its controlling DG-algebra. This geometric setup includes very general cases where C \mathrm{C} does not contract, cases where the curve neighbourhood is not rational, all known simple smooth 3 3 -fold flops, and all known divisorial contractions to curves. As a corollary, it is shown that the noncommutative deformation theory of C \mathrm{C} is described via a superpotential algebra derived from what we call free necklace polynomials, which are elements in the free algebra obtained via a closed formula from combinatorial gluing data. The description of these polynomials, together with the above results, establishes a suitably interpreted string theory prediction due to Ferrari [ F ] , Aspinwall–Katz [ AK ] and Curto–Morrison [ CM ] . Perhaps most significantly, the main results give both the language and evidence to finally formulate new contractibility conjectures for rational curves in CY 3-folds, which lift Artin’s celebrated results [ A ] from surfaces.



1.
Introduction

Rational curves in Calabi–Yau 3-folds
\mathrm{C}\subset\mathcal{X}
are fundamental building blocks of geometry, and yet some of even their most basic properties remain wide open. The main reason, in contrast to when
\mathcal{X}
is a surface, is that various key properties of
\mathrm{C}
in
\mathcal{X}
including its formal neighbourhood, its contractibility and its deformation theory, are structural and not combinatorial.
The controlling structure of all three properties now lies in homological and noncommutative algebra.

The purpose of this paper is to describe the full, derived, deformation theory of
\mathrm{C}
in
\mathcal{X}
, for the general curves described in §
1.1
below. This information is then used to (1) give the first general closed-formula description of the associated noncommutative deformation algebra of
\mathrm{C}
, (2) build, in the context of mirror symmetry, a B-side geometric model from the purely algebraic data of a given superpotential, and (3) use the evidence from these results, and others, to finally be in a position to formulate and conjecture contractibility criteria for formal neighbourhoods of curves in 3-folds. Noncommutative deformations are the language and framework needed in order to lift Artin’s 60-year old work
[
A
]
from surfaces to 3-folds.

1.1.
Geometric Setup

For many reasons, some explained in §
1.5
, the crucial open and key benchmarking case for all the problems listed above is when
\mathrm{C}\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
is a single curve, with normal bundle
\mathcal{O}(-3)\oplus\mathcal{O}(1)
. This is a vast set of curves, an uncharted zoo, known to contain an array of different and surprising geometric behaviour. It is thus important to understand such curves, and to establish their deformation properties.

Deformation theory is local, and so this problem at once reduces to a local model, patching together two copies of affine space (after completion, when necessary). In full generality, there is no known description of this model. However inside this general situation sits a very large subset, which is expected to be representative of general behaviour. This is provided by the string theory literature
[

,
K2
,

]
, where a smooth rational curve
\mathrm{C}
inside a 3-fold
\mathcal{X}
is built as follows.

Set
\uplambda_{00}=\uplambda_{10}=\uplambda_{01}=0

\uplambda_{jk}

and

defined by the two open patches

\mathcal{U}_{2}=\mathbb{A}^{3}_{b,w_{2},w_{1}}

The locus

Both the scheme

1.3.
Main Results

Given a curve
\mathrm{C}

(

.

Given a curve

locally modelled on (

(1)

For any

(2)

1.4.
Corollaries

The first main consequences of
1.1
are to deformation theory. Given a curve
\mathrm{C}
in
\mathcal{X}
locally described by (
1.A
), the sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{C}}\in\mathop{\rm coh}\nolimits\mathcal{X}
has an associated noncommutative (NC) deformation functor, recalled in §

. This functor always admits a prorepresenting hull,
\Lambda_{\mathrm{def}}
, called the NC deformation algebra (see §
6.1
).

Abstractly
\Lambda_{\mathrm{def}}
is always a superpotential algebra, however describing the superpotential has been a key open question. The following gives the first closed-formula description.

Corollary 1.2

(
6.4
)
.

The
NC
deformation algebra
\Lambda_{\mathrm{def}}
of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{C}}\in\mathop{\rm coh}\nolimits\mathcal{X}
is described by

\Lambda_{\mathrm{def}}\cong\mathcal{J}\mathrm{ac}(\mathsf{W})=\frac{\mathbb{C}\langle\kern-2.5pt\langle x,y\rangle\kern-2.5pt\rangle}{(\kern-2.5pt(\updelta_{x}\mathsf{W},\updelta_{y}\mathsf{W})\kern-2.5pt)}

where
\mathsf{W}=\sum\uplambda_{jk}\NeckPoly_{j,k}\in\mathbb{C}\langle\kern-2.5pt\langle x,y\rangle\kern-2.5pt\rangle
is the sum of free necklace polynomials, and the
\uplambda_{jk}
are the data in the glue (
1.A
).

The above then immediately implies a classical 1972 result of Namba
[
N
,
K2
]
, proved using complex analysis methods and the existence of Kuranishi spaces. As is standard, taking the abelianisation of
\Lambda_{\mathrm{def}}
, which just means formally commuting the variables, recovers commutative deformations of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{C}}
. After formally commuting variables, each
p_{m}
in
\NeckPoly_{j,k}(x,y)
becomes
x^{j}y^{k}
, and so
\NeckPoly_{j,k}(x,y)^{\mathop{\rm ab}\nolimits}=\frac{1}{j+k}{j+k\choose k}x^{j}y^{k}
.

Corollary 1.3

(
6.5
, Namba, Katz)
.

Classical commutative deformations of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{C}}\in\mathop{\rm coh}\nolimits\mathcal{X}
are prorepresented by

\Lambda_{\mathrm{def}}^{\mathop{\rm ab}\nolimits}\cong\mathcal{J}\mathrm{ac}(\mathsf{W})^{\mathop{\rm ab}\nolimits}=\frac{\mathbb{C}[\![x,y]\!]}{(\updelta_{x}\mathsf{V},\updelta_{y}\mathsf{V})}

where
\mathsf{V}=\sum\uplambda_{jk}\NeckPoly_{j,k}^{\,\mathop{\rm ab}\nolimits}=\sum\frac{\uplambda_{jk}}{j+k}{j+k\choose k}x^{j}y^{k}
.

The other consequence of
1.1
is categorical. Given a quiver with superpotential
(Q,\mathsf{W})
, Ginzburg associates a 3-CY category
\mathcal{D}_{\mathsf{W}}
. It is a basic question in mirror symmetry to find geometric models for such categories, on both the A- and B-sides.

Corollary 1.4

(
6.6
)
.

Let
\mathsf{W}\in\mathbb{C}\langle x,y\rangle
and consider the associated
3-CY
category
\mathcal{D}_{\mathsf{W}}
. If there exist scalars
\uplambda_{jk}
for which
\mathsf{W}=\sum\uplambda_{jk}\NeckPoly_{j,k}
, then there exists a

-fold
\mathcal{X}
and smooth rational curve

\mathop{\rm{D}^{b}}\nolimits(\mathop{\rm coh}\nolimits\mathcal{X})\supset\langle\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{C}}\rangle\cong\mathcal{D}_{\mathsf{W}}.

The above gives the first hint that perhaps not all superpotentials can be realised, whilst simultaneously not giving any hint on what general reasonable restrictions might be, given that necklace polynomials are not preserved under automorphisms. After imposing strong extra conditions such as

, 1.11]

1.5.
Contractibility

In 1962 Artin
[
A
]
established that the contractibility of curves in smooth surfaces is (suitably locally) a combinatorial problem. This is no longer true for 3-folds. It has been an open question as to what should replace this in higher dimension, and only special cases such as smooth curves currently have a criterion
[
J
]
.

Corollary
1.2
gives a presentation of
\Lambda_{\mathrm{def}}
. Based on empirical evidence from many computer algebra calculations
[
BCP
]
on these presentations, and from theoretical evidence in
[
BW
]
, we conjecture that in the 3-fold setting Artin’s combinatorics are replaced by numerical properties of
\Lambda_{\mathrm{def}}
. One of the advantages of the NC deformation theory technology is that it finally permits clean statements for multi-curves.

Conjecture 1.5

(‘3-fold Artin contractibility’)
.

Let
\mathrm{C}
be a connected union of
n
smooth rational curves in a
CY

3
-fold, and write
\Lambda_{\mathrm{def}}
for its multi-pointed
NC
deformation algebra. Then, in a formal neighbourhood of
\mathrm{C}
, the following are equivalent.

(1)

\mathrm{C}
contracts to a point, with an isomorphism elsewhere.

(2)

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\Lambda_{\mathrm{def}}<\infty
.

This conjecture is a reinterpretation of Artin’s results for surfaces, where crepant curves contract if and only if they are in ADE formation. The NC deformation algebra in this case is the preprojective algebra, which is finite dimensional if and only if the curve configuration is ADE. Note also for 3-folds that the conjecture is numerical, but not combinatorial: to illustrate this, whilst the superpotential determined in
1.1
is a combinatorial object, determining whether the numerical condition
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\Lambda_{\mathrm{def}}<\infty
holds is much more subtle.

The direction (
\Rightarrow
) of
1.5
is known, by
[
DW
]
. In the case of single curves,
[
BW
, 1.10]
shows that the only case of (
\Leftarrow
) that remains open is when the normal bundle is
\mathcal{O}(-3)\oplus\mathcal{O}(1)
. The results of this paper give strong evidence towards, but do not quite yet prove, that final case.

Conventions

We work over an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{K}
. Commas will be suppressed on the subscripts on scalars wherever possible, so e.g.

v_{1}r_{1}+v_{2}r_{2}+(a^{\mathsf{r}+1}v_{1}+v_{2}^{\mathsf{t}-1}A)r_{3}=0.

(r_{1}+a^{\mathsf{r}+1}r_{3})v_{1}=(-r_{2}-v_{2}^{\mathsf{t}-2}Ar_{3})v_{2}

shows that
r_{1}+a^{\mathsf{r}+1}r_{3}=Cv_{2}
and
-r_{2}-v_{2}^{\mathsf{t}-2}Ar_{3}=Cv_{1}
for some
C\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{U}_{1}}
, since
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{U}_{1}}
is a UFD.
Thus
(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})^{T}
is the image of
(-r_{3},C,0)^{T}
, as required.

Finally, it is clear that
\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{C}}
is the cokernel, since the equation
b^{2-\mathsf{r}}w_{1}=a^{\mathsf{r}+1}v_{1}+v_{2}^{\mathsf{t}-1}A
, together with
\mathsf{t}\geq 2
, shows that
b^{2-\mathsf{r}}w_{1}\in(v_{1},v_{2})=I_{C}
.
∎

3.2.

To immediately ease notation, write

(3.A)

By (

\updelta\colon\scrHom^{i}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})\to\scrHom^{i+1}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})

In the following picture, visually this is down-across
-(-1)^{i}
across-down.

\mathcal{E}_{n}
\mathcal{E}_{n-1}
\scriptstyle\mathsf{d}_{n}
\mathcal{E}_{n-i}
\mathcal{E}_{n-i-1}
\scriptstyle\mathsf{d}_{n-i}
\scriptstyle\mathsf{a}_{n}
\scriptstyle\mathsf{a}_{n-1}

Lemma 3.2
.

\updelta^{2}(\mathsf{a})=0

\updelta(\mathsf{a}_{|\mathcal{U}})=\updelta(\mathsf{a})_{|\mathcal{U}}

Proof.

The first claim is standard, and the second claim is the statement that
\mathsf{d}_{n}

and

(3.C)

The total complex

]

3.5.
The Čech enhancement

Using the well-known Čech enhancement described in e.g.
[
A+
, p587]
or
[
CS
, §1.2]
, the complex
(\mathcal{C},\mathds{D})
can be upgraded to a DG-algebra. The only slightly subtle point is the sign on the composition, which we explicitly recall here.

Definition 3.4
.

Given homogeneous
\mathsf{a}=(\mathsf{a}_{1},\mathsf{a}_{2},\mathsf{a}_{12})\in\mathcal{C}_{i}

and
\mathsf{b}=(\mathsf{b}_{1},\mathsf{b}_{2},\mathsf{b}_{12})\in\mathcal{C}_{j}
, define

\mathsf{a}\star\mathsf{b}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(\mathsf{a}_{1}\circ\mathsf{b}_{1},\,\,\,\mathsf{a}_{2}\circ\mathsf{b}_{2},\,\,\,\mathsf{a}_{12}\circ{\mathsf{b}_{2}}_{|\mathcal{U}_{12}}+(-1)^{i}{\mathsf{a}_{1}}_{|\mathcal{U}_{12}}\circ\mathsf{b}_{12})\in\mathcal{C}_{i+j}

(3.E)

and extend
\star
to all of
\mathcal{C}
by linearity.

To set notation for the next result, choose an injective resolution

0\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{C}}\to\mathcal{I}_{0}\to\mathcal{I}_{1}\to\ldots

of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{C}}
. Then consider the DG-algebra
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits^{\mathrm{DG}}_{\mathcal{X}}(\mathcal{I})=\bigoplus_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits^{\mathrm{DG}}_{\mathcal{X}}(\mathcal{I})_{t}
, where

\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits^{\mathrm{DG}}_{\mathcal{X}}(\mathcal{I})_{t}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{(f_{s})_{s\in\mathbb{Z}}\mid f_{s}\colon\mathcal{I}_{s}\to\mathcal{I}_{s+t}\}.

Multiplication is given by composition, and the differential is defined as in §
3.2
.

Proposition 3.5
.

(\mathcal{C},\star,\mathds{D})
is a
DG
-algebra, and this is quasi-isomorphic to the
DG
-algebra
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits^{\mathrm{DG}}_{\mathcal{X}}(\mathcal{I})
.

Proof.

The first statement can be checked manually. Alternatively, as in the proof of
3.3
,
(\mathcal{C},\mathds{D})
equals the complex
\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\infty}(\mathcal{X})}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})
. Now
\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\infty}(\mathcal{X})
is in fact a DG category, under the composition described in
[
A+
, p587]
. In our restricted setting, with only two open affine sets, this translates precisely into the operation
\star
defined above (see also
[
AK
]
). The second statement follows since
\mathcal{X}
is quasi-compact and separated by
2.2
, so the Čech DG enhancement of perfect complexes is quasi-equivalent to the injective DG enhancement of perfect complexes; see
[
CS
, §1.2]
or
[
LS
, 3.19]
.
∎

5.4.
Simple Maps and Consequences of Degree

As the collection of
\mathsf{a}_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes\mathsf{a}_{n}
with each
\mathsf{a}_{i}\in\{\mathsf{x},\mathsf{y},\mathsf{X},\mathsf{Y},\mathfrak{s}\}

is linearly independent in the tensor algebra
\mathrm{T}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathsf{A})
, the map
\mathsf{f}_{n}
may be defined by making
a choice for each
\mathsf{f}_{n}(\mathsf{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{a}_{n})
independently, compatible with (
5.B
). The following is clear, and is well-known.

Lemma 5.7
.

Suppose that
\mathsf{f}_{i}
is defined for
1\leq i\leq n-1
, and further that the defining equation
(
5.A
) gives
\mathsf{U}_{n}(\mathsf{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{a}_{n})=0_{\mathcal{C}}
where
\mathsf{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{a}_{n}\in\{\mathsf{x},\mathsf{y},\mathsf{X},\mathsf{Y},\mathfrak{s}\}
.
Then
\mathsf{m}_{n}(\mathsf{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{a}_{n})=0_{\mathsf{A}}
and we may choose the map
\mathsf{f}_{n}
so that

\mathsf{f}_{n}(\mathsf{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{a}_{n})=0_{\mathcal{C}}
.

Proof.

Since
\upiota
is injective, (
5.B
) is satisfied only if

\mathsf{m}_{n}(\mathsf{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{a}_{n})=[\mathsf{U}_{n}(\mathsf{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{a}_{n})]=0_{\mathsf{A}}
.
Since
\mathds{D}(0_{\mathcal{C}})=0_{\mathcal{C}}=(\upiota\mathsf{m}_{n}-\mathsf{U}_{n})(\mathsf{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{a}_{n})
,
the choice
\mathsf{f}_{n}(\mathsf{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{a}_{n})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}0_{\mathcal{C}}
is compatible with (
5.B
).
∎

Convention 5.8
.

If
\mathsf{U}_{n}(\mathsf{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{a}_{n})=0_{\mathcal{C}}
, we choose
\mathsf{f}_{n}
so that

\mathsf{f}_{n}(\mathsf{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{a}_{n})=0_{\mathcal{C}}
.

Usually controlling
\mathsf{U}_{n}
and
\mathsf{f}_{n}
is hard, but properties of both will be significantly simplified in our setting by the following.

Definition 5.9
.

We say that a collection of maps
\left\{\mathsf{f}_{\ell}\colon\mathsf{A}^{\otimes\ell}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}\right\}_{\ell=1}^{k}
is

simple
if
for all
1\leq\ell\leq k
, and for all inputs
\mathsf{a}_{i}\in\{\mathsf{x},\mathsf{y},\mathsf{X},\mathsf{Y},\mathfrak{s}\}
,

\mathsf{f}_{\ell}(\mathsf{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{a}_{\ell})=(\mathsf{S},\mathsf{T},0)\in\mathcal{C}_{d_{1}+\cdots+d_{\ell}+1-\ell}

for some chains
\mathsf{S},\mathsf{T}
that depend on
\ell
and the
\mathsf{a}_{i}
, where
d_{i}=|\mathsf{a}_{i}|
.

It will turn out, as part of our inductive process, that we may choose the
\mathsf{f}_{n}
to be simple to any degree. As such, later we will require the following two results.

Lemma 5.10
.

Fix
n\geq 2
. Suppose that the set of maps
\left\{\mathsf{f}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{f}_{n-1}\right\}

(1)

\mathsf{U}_{n}(\mathsf{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathsf{a}_{n})=0_{\mathcal{C}}

(1) Set

\mathsf{f}_{n}

Thus consider the graded vector space

.

The following defines an
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-structure on

, and furthermore the resulting

-algebra is quasi-isomorphic to the

(1)

where the

(2)

More generally,

with

(3)

The only other non-zero products are

This is now a direct consequence of

6.2.
Superpotentials and Necklaces

Following the conventions in e.g.
[
DWZ
,
BW
,
D
]
, consider the
\mathbb{C}
-linear map
\textnormal{\reflectbox{6}}_{x}\colon\mathbb{C}\langle\kern-2.5pt\langle x,y\rangle\kern-2.5pt\rangle\to\mathbb{C}\langle\kern-2.5pt\langle x,y\rangle\kern-2.5pt\rangle
which simply ‘strikes off’ the leftmost
x
of each monomial. Thus, on monomials

\textnormal{\reflectbox{6}}_{x}(m)=\begin{cases}n&\text{if }m=xn\\
0&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

with
\textnormal{\reflectbox{6}}_{y}
being defined similarly. In contrast, the cyclic derivative is the
\mathbb{C}
-linear map
\updelta_{x}\colon\mathbb{C}\langle\kern-2.5pt\langle x,y\rangle\kern-2.5pt\rangle\to\mathbb{C}\langle\kern-2.5pt\langle x,y\rangle\kern-2.5pt\rangle
which on monomials sends

x_{i_{1}}\ldots x_{i_{t}}\mapsto\sum_{j=1}^{t}\textnormal{\reflectbox{6}}_{x}(x_{i_{j}}x_{i_{j+1}}\ldots x_{i_{t}}\cdot x_{i_{1}}\ldots x_{i_{j-1}}),

with
\updelta_{y}
being defined similarly. For
f\in\mathbb{C}\langle\kern-2.5pt\langle x,y\rangle\kern-2.5pt\rangle
, the Jacobi algebra is defined to be

\mathcal{J}\mathrm{ac}(f)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{\mathbb{C}\langle\kern-2.5pt\langle x,y\rangle\kern-2.5pt\rangle}{(\kern-2.5pt(\updelta_{x}f,\updelta_{y}f)\kern-2.5pt)}

where
(\kern-2.5pt(\updelta_{x}f,\updelta_{y}f)\kern-2.5pt)
is the closure of the two-sided ideal
(\updelta_{x}f,\updelta_{y}f)
.

Recall from the introduction that the free necklace polynomial is defined to be

\NeckPoly_{j,k}(x,y)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{1}{j+k}\sum_{m\in\Orb_{j,k}}|m|\cdot p_{m}.

Remark 6.1
.

It is immediate from the definition that, if we instead work up to cyclic rotation, we may replace
|m|p_{m}
with the
m
distinct representatives of the orbit. In this way, up to cyclic permutation,
\NeckPoly_{j,k}
is clearly then equal to all terms with
j
occurrences of
x
in the free algebra expansion of
\frac{1}{j+k}(x+y)^{j+k}
.

Notation 6.2
.

Write
\Mono_{j,k}
for the sum of all monomials in
x
and
y
, inside the free algebra, where there are
j
occurrences of
x
, and
k
occurrences of
y
.

For calibration,
\Mono_{2,2}=xxyy+xyyx+yyxx+yxxy+xyxy+yxyx
. The following is elementary, where
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
is used to allow the denominators in the proof.

Lemma 6.3
.

\updelta_{x}(\NeckPoly_{j+1,k})=\Mono_{j,k}
and
\updelta_{y}(\NeckPoly_{j,k+1})=\Mono_{j,k}
.

Proof.

This follows at once from the definitions, since

\updelta_{x}(\NeckPoly_{j+1,k})=\sum_{m\in\Orb_{j+1,k}}\frac{|m|}{j+1+k}\cdot\updelta_{x}(p_{m})=\Mono_{j,k}.\qed

6.3.
Deformation Theory Corollaries

The following is now immediate, and is one of the main results.

Corollary 6.4
.

The
NC
deformation algebra
\Lambda_{\mathrm{def}}
of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{C}}\in\mathop{\rm coh}\nolimits\mathcal{X}
is described by

\Lambda_{\mathrm{def}}\cong\mathcal{J}\mathrm{ac}(\mathsf{W})=\frac{\mathbb{C}\langle\kern-2.5pt\langle x,y\rangle\kern-2.5pt\rangle}{(\kern-2.5pt(\updelta_{x}\mathsf{W},\updelta_{y}\mathsf{W})\kern-2.5pt)}

Proof.

As in

, and summarised in §

can be presented as the completed tensor algebra over

, subject to the relations induced by the map

\mathsf{A}_{2}^{*}\to\hat{\mathrm{T}}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathsf{A}_{1}^{*})

(6.A)

which is dual to

. Now, in

the term

appears precisely in those
\mathsf{m}_{n}
when there are

\mathsf{x}
and
k

\mathsf{y}

j

\mathsf{x}
and

6.4.
Mirror Models

The next application of
5.13
is categorical. Given a quiver with superpotential
(Q,\mathsf{W})
, Ginzburg
[
G
]
associates a 3-CY category
\mathcal{D}_{\mathsf{W}}
. It is a basic question to find geometric models for such categories, on both the A- and B-sides of mirror symmetry.

Corollary 6.6
.

Let
\mathsf{W}\in\mathbb{C}\langle x,y\rangle
and consider the associated
3-CY
category
\mathcal{D}_{\mathsf{W}}
. If there exist scalars
\uplambda_{jk}
for which
\mathsf{W}=\sum\uplambda_{jk}\NeckPoly_{j,k}
, then there exists a smooth
3
-fold
\mathcal{X}
and rational curve
\mathrm{C}\subset\mathcal{X}
such that

\mathop{\rm{D}^{b}}\nolimits(\mathop{\rm coh}\nolimits\mathcal{X})\supset\langle\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{C}}\rangle\cong\mathcal{D}_{\mathsf{W}}.

Proof.

On one hand, the category
\langle\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{C}}\rangle
can be described using the
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-structure on the DG-endomorphism ring of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{C}}
, which is computed in
5.13
above. On the other hand,
by definition
\mathcal{D}_{\mathsf{W}}=\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{fd}}(\Upgamma)
where
\Upgamma
is the Ginzburg DGA associated to the quiver with potential. This category has a canonical heart, generated by the simple modules indexed over the vertices of the quiver, which in our case (the two-loop quiver) consists of a unique simple
S
. Thus
\mathcal{D}_{\mathsf{W}}
is described using the
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-structure on the DG-endomorphism ring of
S
, which by
[
K4
, §A.15]
is entirely encoded by the pairing and the superpotential on degree one inputs. But this is precisely the
\mathrm{A}_{\infty}
-structure described in
5.13
above, and so the result follows.
∎

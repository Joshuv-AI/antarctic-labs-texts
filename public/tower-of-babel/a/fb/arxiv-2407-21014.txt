Asymptotics of the overlap distribution of branching Brownian motion at high temperature
Louis Chataignier, Michel Pain *

Abstract
At high temperature, the overlap of two particles chosen independently according to the Gibbs measure of the branching Brownian motion converges to zero as time goes to infinity. We investigate the precise decay rate of the probability to obtain an overlap greater than a a , for some a > 0 a>0 , in the whole subcritical phase of inverse temperatures β ∈ [ 0 , β c ) \beta\in[0,\beta_{c}) . Moreover, we study this probability both conditionally on the branching Brownian motion and non-conditionally. Two sub-phases of inverse temperatures appear, but surprisingly the threshold is not the same in both cases.



1
Introduction

The (binary) branching Brownian motion (BBM) is a continuous-time branching Markov process constructed as follows.
At time
t=0
, a single particle starts a standard Brownian motion in
\mathbb{R}
from the origin.
After a random time following an exponential distribution with parameter
1
, it splits in two, or equivalently, it dies while giving birth to two children.
These new particles then repeats the same process, independently of each other.
More precisely, the children perform independent Brownian motions from the position of their parent at its death and, after independent exponential times, they in turn split in two.
For a formal construction, see
[
Chauvin1991
]
.

Branching Brownian motion has been widely studied over the last fifty years, initially for its link with reaction-diffusion equations
[
McKean1975
]
which motivated a precise study of its extremes
[
Bramson1978
,
Bramson1983
,
LalleySellke1987
,
AidekonBerestyckiBrunetShi2013
,
ArguinBovierKistler2013
]
.
Other important motivations for this model come from physics.
In spin glass theory, the study of disordered systems with a hierarchical structure of correlation for the energies dates back to the generalized random energy model introduced by Derrida and Gardner
[
DerridaGardner1986
]
and includes the BBM (see e.g. Bovier’s book
[
Bovier2016
]
).
The BBM can also be seen as an infinite dimensional limit of directed polymers
[
DerridaSpohn1988
]
and is also related to diffractive scattering in high-energy particle physics
[
Munier2009
]
.
See Section
1.3
for more details.
In all these frameworks coming from physics, understanding the overlap distribution of particles chosen according to the Gibbs measure plays an important role, and this paper is set in this context, by investigating the probability to have an atypical overlap in the high temperature phase.

1.1
Definitions and previous results

From a statistical physics perspective, for some fixed time
t\geq 0
, one can identify the positions of particles alive at time
t
with the energy levels of a physical system.
Each particle can then be thought of as a configuration of the system.
Let
\mathcal{N}(t)
be the set of particles alive at time
t
and
X_{u}(t)
the position of particle
u
at time
t
.
The
Gibbs measure
of the BBM at time
t
and at inverse temperature
\beta\geq 0
is the (random) probability measure
\mathcal{G}_{\beta,t}
on
\mathcal{N}(t)
that assigns to each particle
u
a weight proportional to
\mathrm{e}^{\beta X_{u}(t)}
.
By rescaling the associated partition function, we obtain the so-called
additive martingale
of the BBM at inverse temperature
\beta
, introduced by McKean
[
McKean1975
]
,

W_{t}(\beta)=\mathrm{e}^{-\psi(\beta)t}\sum_{u\in\mathcal{N}(t)}\mathrm{e}^{\beta X_{u}(t)},

(1.1)

where
\psi(\beta)=1+\beta^{2}/2
.
Since it is a positive martingale, it converges a.s. to a random variable
W_{\infty}(\beta)
.
This limit plays a key role in the study of the long-term behavior of the BBM, as illustrated in Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
below.
A phase transition occurs: by
[
Neveu1988
, Proposition 1]
, if
\beta\geq\sqrt{2}
then
W_{\infty}(\beta)=0
a.s., if
\beta<\sqrt{2}
then
W_{\infty}(\beta)>0
a.s.
In the latter case, the additive martingale is uniformly integrable, or equivalently, it converges in
L^{1}
.
In the critical case
\beta=\sqrt{2}
, it is more suitable to consider the
derivative martingale
, introduced by Lalley and Sellke
[
LalleySellke1987
]
,

Z_{t}=\sum_{u\in\mathcal{N}(t)}(\sqrt{2}t-X_{u}(t))\mathrm{e}^{\sqrt{2}X_{u}(t)-2t}.

(1.2)

The authors showed that it converges a.s. to a random variable
Z_{\infty}
and that
Z_{\infty}>0
a.s. The critical additive martingale is related to the derivative martingale through the following convergence

\sqrt{t}W_{t}(\sqrt{2})\xrightarrow[t\to\infty]{}\sqrt{2\smash{/}\pi}Z_{\infty},\quad\text{in probability},

(1.3)

proved for the branching random walk (BRW) by Aïdékon and Shi
[
AidekonShi2014
]
.
For the BBM, this is a direct consequence of
[
MaillardPain2019
, Proposition 2.2]
.

In this paper, we are interested in the behavior of the overlap between particles chosen according to the Gibbs measure.
The
overlap
between two particles
u,v\in\mathcal{N}(t)
, denoted
q_{t}(u,v)\in[0,1]
, is defined as
the last time where
u
and
v
have had a common ancestor alive, rescaled by a factor of
t
.
In other words, it is the proportion of time where
u
and
v
have shared a common trajectory.
Then, the
overlap distribution
at time
t\geq 0
and inverse temperature
\beta
is the following random probability measure on
[0,1]
:

\nu_{\beta,t}=\mathcal{G}_{\beta,t}^{\otimes 2}(q_{t}(u,v)\in\cdot)=\frac{1}{W_{t}(\beta)^{2}}\sum_{u,v\in\mathcal{N}(t)}\mathrm{e}^{\beta(X_{u}(t)+X_{v}(t))-2\psi(\beta)t}\delta_{q_{t}(u,v)},

(1.4)

so that, for
a\in[0,1]
,
\nu_{\beta,t}([a,1])
is the conditional probability, given the BBM, that two particles chosen independently according to
\mathcal{G}_{\beta,t}
have an overlap at least
a
.
This quantity was introduced by Derrida and Spohn
[
DerridaSpohn1988
]
by analogy with the overlap between two configurations of spin glasses.
We are also interested in the
mean overlap distribution

\mathbb{E}[\nu_{\beta,t}]
, which is a deterministic probability measure on
[0,1]
such that
\mathbb{E}[\nu_{\beta,t}([a,1])]
is now the (non-conditional) probability that two particles chosen independently according to
\mathcal{G}_{\beta,t}
have an overlap at least
a
.

The overlap distribution satisfies the following phase transition:

\nu_{\beta,t}\xrightarrow[t\to\infty]{}\begin{cases}\delta_{0},&\text{if }\beta\leq\sqrt{2},\\
(1-\pi_{\beta})\delta_{0}+\pi_{\beta}\delta_{1},&\text{if }\beta>\sqrt{2},\end{cases}

(1.5)

in distribution for the weak topology, where
\pi_{\beta}
is a random variable with values in
(0,1)
, mean
1-\sqrt{2}/\beta
and a law which depends explicitely on
\beta
.
This result was conjectured by Derrida and Spohn
[
DerridaSpohn1988
]
.
In the subcritical case, it was first proved by Chauvin and Rouault
[
ChauvinRouault1997
]
for the BRW; more precisely, they proved the a.s. weak convergence of the rescaled measure
\nu_{\beta,t}(\cdot/t)
on
[0,\infty)
towards a non-trivial random measure.
In the critical case, this is a direct consequence of Bovier and Kurkova
[
BovierKurkova2004
]
in the case of the binary Gaussian BRW, and it has been established for general BRW by Madaule
[
Madaule2016
]
.
The supercritical case was treated by Mallein
[
Mallein2018
]
for the BRW (the convergence of the mean overlap distribution was proved earlier in
[
BovierKurkova2004
]
).
Finally, Bonnefont
[
Bonnefont2022
]
proved the convergence (
1.5
) for the BBM.
In this paper, we focus on the subcritical phase
\beta<\sqrt{2}
and obtain the precise decay rate of the overlap distribution on
(0,1]
, as well as its limit, once rescaled by this rate.
We investigate separately the typical overlap distribution in Theorem
1.1
and the mean overlap distribution in Theorem
1.2
.
Each theorem reveals two main regimes, though the threshold differs between them.

Theorem
1.2
involves a new probability measure
\mathbb{Q}_{\beta}
, introduced by Chauvin and Rouault
[
ChauvinRouault1988
]
.
If we denote by
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
the natural filtration of the BBM, we can define it through the Radon–Nikodym derivatives

\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathbb{Q}_{\beta}|_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}|_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}}=W_{t}(\beta),

(1.6)

for
t\geq 0
.
Under
\mathbb{Q}_{\beta}
, one of the particles, called
spine
, performs a Brownian motion with drift
\beta
and splits at rate
2
while the behavior of the other particles remains unchanged, see
for details.
We denote by
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}_{\beta}}
the associated expectation.

Throughout this paper, the letters
t
and
s
denote some points in time
[0,\infty)
.
The letters
C
and
c
denote constants, i.e. deterministic and time-invariant quantities, which may vary from one line to another.
We set
\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,2,\dots\}
and
\mathbb{N}^{*}=\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
.
We use the Bachmann–Landau notations
o
,
O
,
\sim
, and Hardy’s notation
\asymp
with their usual meaning.
More precisely, for
f\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
and
g\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}
, we say
that
f(t)=o(g(t))
as
t\to\infty
if
\lim_{t\to\infty}f(t)/g(t)=0
,
that
f(t)=O(g(t))
as
t\to\infty
if
\limsup_{t\to\infty}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lvert f(t)}}\right\rvert/g(t)<\infty
,
that
f(t)\sim g(t)
as
t\to\infty
if
\lim_{t\to\infty}f(t)/g(t)=1
,
and that
f(t)\asymp g(t)
as
t\to\infty
if
0<\liminf_{t\to\infty}f(t)/g(t)\leq\limsup_{t\to\infty}f(t)/g(t)<\infty
.

1.2
Main results

In the following theorems, we describe the asymptotic behavior at high temperature of the overlap distribution and its expectation.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
0\leq\beta<\sqrt{2}
and
0<a<1
.

(i)

If
0\leq\beta<\sqrt{2}/2
, then

\mathrm{e}^{(1-\beta^{2})at}\nu_{\beta,t}([a,1])\xrightarrow[t\to\infty]{}\frac{W_{\infty}(2\beta)}{W_{\infty}(\beta)^{2}}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[W_{\infty}(\beta)^{2}}}\right],\quad\text{a.s.{} }

(ii)

If
\beta=\sqrt{2}/2
, then

\sqrt{at}\mathrm{e}^{at/2}\nu_{\beta,t}([a,1])\xrightarrow[t\to\infty]{}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{Z_{\infty}}{W_{\infty}(\beta)^{2}}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[W_{\infty}(\beta)^{2}}}\right],\quad\text{in probability}.

(iii)

If
\sqrt{2}/2<\beta<\sqrt{2}
, then

(at)^{3\beta/\sqrt{2}}\mathrm{e}^{(\sqrt{2}-\beta)^{2}at}\nu_{\beta,t}([a,1])\xrightarrow[t\to\infty]{}\frac{C_{\beta}Z_{\infty}^{\sqrt{2}\beta}}{W_{\infty}(\beta)^{2}}S_{2\beta},\quad\text{in distribution},

where
S_{2\beta}
is a non-degenerate
(\sqrt{2}/2\beta)
-stable random variable independent of the BBM and
C_{\beta}>0
is a constant that depends only on
\beta
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
0\leq\beta<\sqrt{2}
and
0<a<1
.
As
t\to\infty
,

(i)

if
\beta=0
, then

\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\nu_{\beta,t}([a,1])}}\right]\sim 2at\mathrm{e}^{-at},

(ii)

if
0<\beta<\sqrt{2\smash{/}3}
, then
W_{t}(\beta)
converges
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. and
\mathbb{Q}_{2\beta}
-a.s. to some positive and finite variable
W_{\infty}(\beta)
, and

\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\nu_{\beta,t}([a,1])}}\right]\sim\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}_{2\beta}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\frac{1}{W_{\infty}(\beta)^{2}}}}\right]\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[W_{\infty}(\beta)^{2}}}\right]\mathrm{e}^{-(1-\beta^{2})at},

(iii)

if
\beta=\sqrt{2\smash{/}3}
, then

\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\nu_{\beta,t}([a,1])}}\right]\asymp t^{-1/2}\mathrm{e}^{-at/3},

(iv)

if
\sqrt{2\smash{/}3}<\beta<\sqrt{2}

\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\nu_{\beta,t}([a,1])}}\right]\asymp t^{-3/2}\mathrm{e}^{-(2-\beta^{2})^{2}at/8\beta^{2}}.

1.3
Comments, related literature and heuristics

Motivations.

As mentioned earlier, our motivation for studying the overlap comes from the physics literature.
In mean-field spin glass models such as the Sherrington–Kirkpatrick model, the overlap between two configurations is defined as an explicit function of the correlation between the energies of these configurations, and the overlap distribution plays a key role in the interpretation of Parisi’s formula for the free energy
[
Parisi1983
]
and of the ultrametric structure of the Gibbs measure in the infinite-volume limit
[
MezardParisiSourlasToulouseVirasoro1984
]
.
To understand the free energy and the overlap distribution on more tractable examples, Derrida and Gardner
[
DerridaGardner1986
]
introduced the Generalized Random Energy Model (GREM), where the ultrametric structure is directly implemented in the definition of the Hamiltonian at finite volume: more precisely, the energies have a hierarchical structure of correlations with a fixed number of levels, whereas the BBM corresponds to a number of levels growing with the number of particles.
As indicated above, the overlap distribution for the BBM has been first studied in physics by Derrida and Spohn
[
DerridaSpohn1988
]
, motivated by both spin glass and polymer theories.
Indeed, the BBM can also be seen as an infinite limit of the directed polymer model and, in this framework, the overlap distribution describes the proportion of time where two polymers chosen independently according to the Gibbs measure are overlapping.

More recently, Derrida and Mottishaw
[
DerridaMottishaw2016
]
investigated the finite-size corrections for the mean overlap distribution in the critical and supercritical phases
\beta\geq\sqrt{2}
: to be more precise than the known convergence
\mathbb{E}[\nu_{\beta,t}]\to\sqrt{2}/\beta\delta_{0}+(1-\sqrt{2}/\beta)\delta_{1}
as
t\to\infty
, they describe how the mass of
\mathbb{E}[\nu_{\beta,t}]
between 0 and 1 vanishes.
More precisely, they conjecture that, for any
0<a<b<1
,

\mathbb{E}[\nu_{\beta,t}([a,b])]\sim\frac{1}{\beta\sqrt{2\pi t}}\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x}{x^{3/2}(1-x)^{(1+2\cdot\mathds{1}_{\beta>\sqrt{2}})/2}}.

(1.8)

This prediction is based both on numerical simulations and on an approximation by the GREM, for which Derrida and Mottishaw
[
DerridaMottishaw2018
]
calculated the finite-size corrections of the overlap as well.
In the case
\beta>\sqrt{2}
, the asymptotic (
1.8
) also has an interpretation in high-energy particle physics, uncovered by Mueller and Munier
[
MuellerMunier2018a
,
MuellerMunier2018b
]
: using a link between the BBM and the scattering of high-energy hadrons dating back to
[
IancuMuellerMunier2005
]
, they show a link between the statistics of rapidity gaps in diffractive electron-nucleus scattering and statistics of the extremal particles of the BBM, including their overlap distribution, which is well-predicted by (
1.8
) because supercritical Gibbs measures are supported by extremal particles.

In this paper, we study this question in the subcritical phase
\beta<\sqrt{2}
instead, and investigate moreover the behavior of the typical overlap distribution.
Observe that the exponent appearing in the exponential decay of the mean overlap distribution is vanishing as
\beta
approaches
\sqrt{2}
(see Figure
1.1
), which is consistent with the fact that the decay is expected to be polynomial only for
\beta\geq\sqrt{2}
.
This is also the case for the typical overlap distribution, so we also expect a polynomial decay for
\beta\geq\sqrt{2}
in that case.
However, we expect the typical overlap distribution to decay faster than the mean overlap distribution when
\beta\geq\sqrt{2}
, as it is the case for
\beta\in[\sqrt{2}/2,\sqrt{2})
, which means that
\mathbb{E}[\nu_{\beta,t}([a,b])]
is governed by a rare event (called “fluctuation” in
[
MuellerMunier2018a
]
).

Typical overlap distribution.

Most heuristics can be deduced from the following rewriting of the overlap distribution (see (

\nu_{\beta,t}([a,1])=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{(\beta^{2}-1)at}}{W_{t}(\beta)^{2}}\sum_{w\in\mathcal{N}(at)}\mathrm{e}^{2\beta X_{w}(at)-\psi(2\beta)at}W_{t-at}^{(w,at)}(\beta)^{2},

(1.9)

where

.
Note that, in the sum of (

), these additive martingales are tilted by Gibbs weights at inverse temperature
2\beta

Consequently, the particles

The description of these particles’ behaviors follows from this.

There is a first regime
\beta\in[0,\sqrt{2}/2)
for which the Gibbs measure
\mathcal{G}_{2\beta,at}

These ones perform a Brownian motion with drift

So do the particles that contribute to the overlap distribution, up to time
at
, after which their trajectories relax to a drift
\beta
(see Figure

Stability of the nonlinear Milne Problem for radiative heat transfer system
Mohamed Ghattassi, Xiaokai Huo, Nader Masmoudi

Abstract
. This paper focuses on the nonlinear Milne problem of the radiative heat transfer system on the half-space. The nonlinear model is described by a second order ODE for temperature coupled to transport equation for radiative intensity. The nonlinearity of the fourth power Stefan-Boltzmann law of black body radiation, bring additional difficulty in mathematical analysis, compared to the well-developed theory for Milne problem of linear transport equation. With the help of the monotonicity property of the second order ODE, we prove the existence of the nonlinear Milne problem on a finite interval using monotonic convergence theorems. Then the solution is extended to the half-space using a uniform weighted estimate and the compactness method. Moreover, the solutions are proved to converge to constants as x → ∞ x\to\infty . Therefore, the linear stability analysis is used to study the uniqueness of the nonlinear Milne problem. The existence and uniqueness for the linearized system is established under a spectral assumption on the solution of the nonlinear problem. The spectral assumption is shown to be satisfied when the boundary data is close to the well-prepared case by using a generalized Hardy’s inequality. The uniqueness of the solution to the half-line nonlinear Milne problem is established in a neighborhood of solutions satisfying a spectral assumption and the energy estimate. The current work extends the study of Milne problem for linear transport equations and provides a comprehensive study on the nonlinear Milne problem of radiative heat transfer systems.

Introduction

We consider the following system

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{2}T+\langle\psi-T^{4}\rangle=~

\displaystyle 0,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mu\partial_{x}\psi+(\psi-T^{4})=~

\displaystyle 0,

(1.2)

in the space
(x,\mu)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times[-1,1]
,
where

The bracket

denotes the integral over
\mu\in[-1,1]

\langle f\rangle:=\int_{-1}^{1}f(\mu)d\mu

The boundary conditions are taken to be the non-homogeneous Dirichlet conditions

\displaystyle T(0)=~

\displaystyle T_{b},

(1.3)

\displaystyle\psi(0,\mu)=~

\displaystyle\psi_{b}(\mu),\text{ for any }\mu\in(0,1],

Here

In this paper, the above problem is called the
nonlinear Milne problem of radiative heat transfer
. The main objective of our work is to study the existence, uniqueness, long time behavior and stability of this nonlinear Milne problem.

System (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) was derived in the boundary layer problem for the diffusive limit of the following radiative heat transfer system

\displaystyle\partial_{t}T^{\varepsilon}=\Delta T^{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\langle\psi^{\varepsilon}-(T^{\varepsilon})^{4}\rangle,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\psi^{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\omega\cdot\nabla\psi^{\varepsilon}=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}(\psi^{\varepsilon}-(T^{\varepsilon})^{4}),

where
T^{\varepsilon}=T^{\varepsilon}(t,X)
,
\psi^{\varepsilon}=\psi(t,X,\omega)
with
X\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
. System (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) is the boundary layer problem of the corresponding steady state problem of the above radiative heat transfer system, for more details we refer the reader to
[
19
]
and references therein.
When the boundary data is well-prepared such that
\psi_{b}=T_{b}^{4}
, system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) has the constant solution
(T_{b},\psi_{b}1_{\mu\in[0,1]})
, where
1_{\mu\in[0,1]}
denotes the characteristic function on
[0,1]
, and
no boundary layer exists. In this case the diffusive limit for the radiative transfer system was rigorously justified in our previous work
[
13

based on the weak convergence method and the relative entropy method. In general, when
\psi_{b}\neq T_{b}^{4}
, it is crucial to study system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) because the boundary layer effects make the interior solution obtained by asymptotic analysis invalid near the boundary. The solution to (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) provides a better description of the radiative transfer system near the boundary. Moreover, the boundary condition for the interior solutions needs to be determined by the limit of the solutions to the nonlinear Milne problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) as

. Based on this work, we are able to provide a rigorous justification of the diffusive limit
[
11
,
12

. Hence it is important to study the nonlinear Milne problem and show the behavior of its solutions at infinity.

There has been a well-developed theory of the Milne problem for the linear transport equations. When the equation for temperature is not considered, system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) becomes

\displaystyle\mu\partial_{x}\psi+\psi-\frac{1}{2}\langle\psi\rangle=0.

(1.5)

This problem, referred to as the Milne problem for linear transport equation, dates back to the work
[
5
,
4
]
. It was derived in
[
5
]
as the boundary layer problem of the linear transport equation. The equation was shown to decay exponentially to a constant therein by using the theory of stochastic processes. Later, this was also proved by using compactness method and weighted estimates in
[
4
]
. Since
0
belongs to the spectral of the linear operator
\mathcal{L}\psi:=-\mu\partial_{x}\psi-\psi+\tfrac{1}{2}\langle\psi\rangle
, one cannot use semigroup theory to show the existence of solutions for the above problem. Instead, the maximum principle is used to show the existence of solutions in
L^{\infty}
. Moreover, using a weighted estimate, the exponential convergence and the uniqueness of solutions is shown in
[
4
]
. Recently, a geometric correction to the above Milne problem is proposed in
[
25
]
to better describe the behavior of solutions near the boundary when the boundary is not flat. A weighted estimate accounting for the geometric corrections was provided therein to show the exponential convergence of solutions.

Compared to the Milne problem for linear transport equations, there are only few works on the nonlinear Milne problem. For example, in
[
19
]
, the existence of weak solutions in
L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\times L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times[-1,1])
was shown under the assumption that
\gamma_{1}\leq T_{b}\leq\gamma_{2}
for some constants
\gamma_{1}>0
,
\gamma_{2}\geq 0
by using the Leray-Schauder fixed point theorem. However, whether the solutions converges to some finite constants is not known. Moreover, the uniqueness of weak solutions are not known. In
[
18
]
, existence and uniqueness are shown for system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) with Dirichlet boundary data in a bounded interval by using the maximum principle. For the half-space nonlinear Milne problem, the boundary condition at infinity is not known and such method cannot be adapted to our problem.

The major difficulty of the study of the nonlinear Milne problem (
1.1
)-(
1.4
) lies in two aspects. Firstly, the second order ODE (
1.1
) and the transport equation (
1.2
) are coupled and need to be studied together. Maximum principle holds for equation (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) separately, but is not known to hold for the system. Secondly, the
T^{4}
term is nonlinear, which is due to the Stefan-Boltzmann law of black body radiation. This physical term, makes the Milne problem nonlinear and the theory of linear operators not applicable. The lack of maximum principle and the nonlinearity nature of the problem, make the study of our problem challenging.

To overcome the above difficulty, several techniques are used. Firstly, the maximum principle of the second order ODE (
1.1
) and the transport equation (
1.2
) also implies the monotonicity of solutions with respect to source term and boundary data. Such monotonicity property could help to construct an approximate sequence converging to the solutions to (
1.1
)-(
1.4
). Such a technique was used in
[
9
]
to study the Milne problem for a similar system but without the second order derivative
\partial_{x}^{2}T
. Here we need to use the monotonicity property of the second order ODE to construct functions that converge to solutions of the nonlinear Milne problem. Secondly, to prove the exponential decay property of the solution, we cannot directly follow
[
4
]
since the property that
\partial_{x}\psi
satisfies the same equation as
\psi
is used to show the exponential decay of solution for the Milne problem (
1.5
). However, in our case, due to nonlinearity,
(\partial_{x}T,\partial_{x}\psi)
satisfies a different system.
Fortunately, we are able to derive a weighted estimate in a similar way as
[
4
]
that leads to the exponential decay of
T
as well as
\partial_{x}T
, which also implies the decay property of
\psi
by directly using the formulas for linear transport equation. Thirdly, unlike the linear Milne problem (
1.5
), here the uniqueness of nonlinear Milne problem (
1.1
)-(
1.4
) does not follow from the weighted estimates and decay property of solutions due to the nonlinearity. We solve this issue by using linear stability analysis and propose a spectral assumption that leads to weighted estimates and the uniqueness of the linearized system. Lastly, a sufficient condition is proposed for the spectral assumption by using the generalized Hardy’s inequality. This allows us to prove the uniqueness of the nonlinear Milne problem in the vicinity of solutions satisfying the sufficient condition or near well-prepared boundary data where
\psi_{b}-T_{b}^{4}
is small.

System (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) could be extended by considering the dependence on frequency of the radiative density. The corresponding frequency dependent model reads as

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{2}T+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{-1}^{1}(\psi-B_{\nu}(T))d\mu d\nu=0,

\displaystyle\mu\partial_{x}\psi+\psi-B_{\nu}(T)=0,

where
T=T(x),\psi=\psi(x,\mu,\nu)
and

is the frequency. The nonlinear function

is usually taken to be the Planck function

B_{\nu}(T)=\frac{2h\nu^{3}}{c^{2}}\frac{1}{e^{\frac{h\nu}{kT}}-1},

where

is the speed of light,
k
is the Boltzmann constant and
h
is the Planck constant. When the diffusive operator
\partial_{x}^{2}T
is neglected, the Milne problem is studied in
[
20
,
23
,
9
]
. The existence of the above frequency dependent half-space problem was proved in
[
23
]
, where more later a simplified demonstration was provided in
[
9
]
. However, the existence and uniqueness for the nonlinear Milne problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) and the above frequency dependent problem has not been proved in the literature. Our result of this paper could be extended to the frequency dependent case without any difficulty.

Our work could be treated as a development of the method of weighted estimates first developed in
[
4
]
to the nonlinear coupled Milne problem. The work
[
4
]
paves the way to use rigorous mathematical analysis to study the boundary layer problem of kinetic PDEs. The same technique was used in
[
25
]
to study the Milne problem of linear transport equation with geometric corrections. Such techniques have been also used and developed for the other kinetic equations, in particular Boltzmann equations. For example, existence, uniqueness and asymptotic behavior of the linearized Boltzmann equation for a gas of hard spheres is studied by using more complicated weighted estimates in
[
2
]
. The analysis is extended to gas mixtures in
[
1
]
and to the Vlasov-Boltzmann-Poisson equation in
[
7
]
. The nonlinear Milne problem for Boltzmann equation was considered in
[

, where existence and exponential decay property of solutions are proved for the Milne problem for the nonlinear Boltzmann equation in one-dimension with a slightly perturbed reflection boundary condition using weighted estimates and the Banach fixed-point theorem. For a boundary condition that the gas is in contact with its condensed phase, the existence and uniqueness of a uniformly decaying solution is shown in a neighborhood of Maxwellian equilibrium in
[
6
]
, where a penalized problem is studied in order to consider the phase transition at the interface. Besides these works, there are also some other ways to study the Milne problems of Boltzmann equations, for example
[

,

,

,

. Among them, In

Based on the macro-micro or hydrodynamics-kinetic decomposition of solutions, existence of solutions to the same nonlinear problem is proved in
[

and it is found that the Mach number of Maxwellian affects the existence. For the case of reflective boundary conditions, the existence is shown recently in

, where uniqueness is also established under some constraint conditions. For a review of the boundary layer problem for Boltzmann equation, we refer the reader to
[

. Since techniques and methods for the Milne problem of linear transport equations have been extended to study the Milne problem for the Boltzmann equation, for example from

Main results

The existence and exponential decay properties of solutions to the nonlinear Milne problem (

.

Given

1.1
)-(
1.2

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{2\alpha x}4T^{3}|\partial_{x}T|^{2}dx+(1-\alpha)\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{-1}^{1}e^{2\alpha x}(\psi-T^{4})^{2}d\mu dx

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{0}|\mu|(\psi(0,\mu)-T_{b}^{4})^{2}d\mu\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\mu(\psi_{b}-T_{b}^{4})^{2}d\mu,

(1.6)

for any
0\leq\alpha<1

such that

\displaystyle

\displaystyle|T(x)-T_{\infty}|\leq M_{\alpha}e^{-\alpha x},

\displaystyle|\psi(x,\mu)-T_{\infty}^{4}|\leq|\psi_{b}-T_{b}^{4}|e^{-\frac{x}{\mu}}+4(T_{b}+2M_{\alpha})^{3}M_{\alpha}\frac{1}{1-\mu\alpha}e^{-\alpha x},~\text{for }\mu>0,

\displaystyle T_{\infty}\leq T_{b}+M_{\alpha},\quad|T(x)|\leq T_{b}+2M_{\alpha}.

\displaystyle-\int_{x}^{B}\frac{1}{\mu}e^{-\frac{x-s}{\mu}}((T^{B})^{4}-(T_{1}^{B})^{4})(s)ds,\text{ for }\mu<0.

If
T^{B}(x)>T_{1}^{B}(x)
on some interval
(a,b)\subset[0,B]
, then the above equation implies that
\varphi^{b}(0,\mu)>0
for
\mu<0
. This, together with the boundary condition
\varphi^{B}(0,\mu)=0
for
\mu>0
, implies

\displaystyle\partial_{x}g^{B}(0)=\langle\mu\varphi^{B}(0,\cdot)\rangle=\int_{-1}^{0}\mu\varphi^{B}(0,\mu)d\mu<0.

(2.22)

This contradicts to the fact that
g^{B}(x)\geq 0
for any
x\in[0,B]
and
g^{B}(0)=0
. Hence
T^{B}(x)=T_{1}^{B}(x)
for
x\in[0,B]
almost everywhere.

Due to
T^{B}=T_{1}^{B}
, equation (
2.21
) becomes
\mu\partial_{x}\varphi^{B}+\varphi^{B}=0
, with boundary conditions
\varphi^{B}(0,\mu)=0,\varphi(B,\mu)=\varphi(B,-\mu)
for
\mu>0
. Therefore,
\psi^{B}(x,\mu)=\psi_{1}^{B}(x,\mu)
for
x\in[0,B],\mu\in[-1,1]
.
By the same argument we also obtain
T^{B}=T_{2}^{B},\psi^{B}=\psi_{2}^{B}
. Hence
T_{1}^{B}=T_{2}^{B}
and
\psi_{1}^{B}=\psi_{2}^{B}
, i.e. the solution to system (
2.1
)-(
2.4
) is unique.

∎

2.3.
Weighted estimate

Next we derive the weighted estimate (
4
).

Proof of Theorem
4
, weighted estimate.

We multiply (
2.1
) by
(T^{B})^{4}
, integrate over
[0,x]
and multiply (
2.2
) by
\psi^{B}
, integrate over
(z,\mu)\in[0,x]\times[-1,1]
, and combine the results to get

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{x}(T^{B})^{4}\partial_{z}^{2}T^{B}dz+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{x}\int_{-1}^{1}\mu\partial_{z}(\psi^{B})^{2}d\mu dz+\int_{0}^{x}\int_{-1}^{1}(\psi^{B}-(T^{B})^{4})^{2}d\mu dz=0.

(2.23)

Using integration-by-parts,
we obtain

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{x}(T^{B})^{4}\partial_{z}^{2}T^{B}dz

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{x}4(T^{B})^{3}|\partial_{z}T^{B}|^{2}dz-(T^{B})^{4}(z)\partial_{z}T^{B}(z)\bigg|_{z=0}^{x}.

(2.24)

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}\int_{-1}^{1}\mu\partial_{z}(\psi^{B})^{2}d\mu dz=\int_{-1}^{1}\mu(\psi^{B})^{2}(z,\cdot)d\mu\bigg|_{z=0}^{x}.

(2.25)

Comparing equation (
2.1
) to (
2.2
) gives

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{2}T^{B}=\partial_{x}\langle\mu\psi^{B}\rangle,

(2.26)

this together with
\partial_{x}T^{B}(B)=\langle\mu\psi^{B}(B,\cdot)\rangle=0
implies

\displaystyle\partial_{x}T^{B}(x)=\langle\mu\psi^{B}(x,\cdot)\rangle,\quad\text{for any }x\in[0,B].

(2.27)

Using this and taking (
2.24
) and (
2.25
) into (
2.23
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}4(T^{B})^{3}|\partial_{z}T^{B}|^{2}dz+\int_{0}^{x}\int_{-1}^{1}(\psi^{B}-(T^{B})^{4})^{2}d\mu dz

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}\mu(\psi^{B}(x,\cdot)-(T^{B}(x))^{4})^{2}d\mu-\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}\mu(\psi^{B}(0,\cdot)-T_{b}^{4})^{2}d\mu=0.

The boundary condition (
2.4
) implies

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\mu(\psi^{B}(0,\cdot)-T_{b}^{4})^{2}d\mu=\int_{-1}^{0}\mu(\psi^{B}(0,\cdot)-T_{b}^{4})^{2}d\mu+\int_{0}^{1}\mu(\psi_{b}-T_{b}^{4})^{2}d\mu.

(2.28)

Taking it into the previous equation leads to the estimate (
4
) with
\alpha=0
:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}4(T^{B})^{3}|\partial_{z}T^{B}|^{2}dx+\int_{0}^{x}\int_{-1}^{1}(\psi^{B}-(T^{B})^{4})^{2}d\mu dz

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}\mu(\psi^{B}(x,\cdot)-(T^{B}(x))^{4})^{2}d\mu+\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{0}|\mu|(\psi^{B}(0,\cdot)-T_{b}^{4})^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\mu(\psi_{b}-T_{b}^{4})^{2}d\mu.

(2.29)

Note here we have

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\mu(\psi^{B}(x,\cdot)-(T^{B}(x))^{4})^{2}d\mu\geq 0

for any
x\in[0,B]
, which is due to the fact that
\int_{-1}^{1}\mu(\psi^{B}(x,\cdot)-(T^{B}(x))^{4})^{2}d\mu
is a non-increasing function of
x
and

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\mu(\psi^{B}(B,\cdot)-(T^{B}(B))^{4})^{2}d\mu=0,

(2.30)

which is due to the boundary condition
\psi^{B}(B,\mu)=\psi^{B}(B,-\mu)
. The non-increasing fact of
\int_{-1}^{1}\mu(\psi^{B}(x,\cdot)-(T^{B}(x))^{4})^{2}d\mu
can be seen if we integrate over
[x_{1},x_{2}]
with
0\leq x_{1}<x_{2}\leq B
in the derivation above, the inequality

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}\mu(\psi^{B}(x,\cdot)-(T^{B}(x))^{4})^{2}d\mu\bigg|_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}

\displaystyle\quad=-\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}4(T^{B})^{3}|\partial_{x}T^{B}|^{2}dx-\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}\int_{-1}^{1}(\psi^{B}-(T^{B})^{4})^{2}d\mu dx\leq 0

is satisfied.

Next, let
\alpha\in(0,1)
be a constant.
We multiply (
2.1
) by
e^{2\alpha x}(T^{B})^{4}
, and (
2.2
) by
e^{2\alpha x}\psi^{B}
, and integrate to get

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{x}e^{2\alpha z}(T^{B})^{4}\partial_{z}^{2}T^{B}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{x}\int_{-1}^{1}\mu e^{2\alpha z}\partial_{z}(\psi^{B})^{2}d\mu dx

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{x}\int_{-1}^{1}e^{2\alpha z}(\psi^{B}-(T^{B})^{4})^{2}d\mu dx=0.

(2.31)

Using integration-by-parts, we get

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{x}e^{2\alpha z}(T^{B})^{4}\partial_{z}^{2}T^{B}dz

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{x}e^{2\alpha z}4(T^{B})^{3}|\partial_{x}T^{B}|^{2}dx+2\alpha\int_{0}^{x}e^{2\alpha z}(T^{B})^{4}\partial_{z}T^{B}dx

\displaystyle\quad-e^{2\alpha z}(T^{B})^{4}(z)\partial_{z}T^{B}(z)\bigg|_{z=0}^{x},

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}\int_{-1}^{1}\mu e^{2\alpha z}\partial_{z}(\psi^{B})^{2}d\mu dz

\displaystyle=-2\alpha\int_{0}^{x}\int_{-1}^{2}\mu e^{2\alpha z}(\psi^{B})^{2}d\mu dz+\int_{-1}^{1}\mu e^{2\alpha z}(\psi^{B})^{2}(z,\cdot)d\mu\bigg|_{0}^{x}.

Taking the above equations into (
2.3
) and using the relation (
2.27
) gives

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}e^{2\alpha z}4(T^{B})^{3}|\partial_{z}T^{B}|^{2}dx+\int_{0}^{x}\int_{-1}^{1}e^{2\alpha z}(\psi^{B}-(T^{B})^{4})^{2}d\mu dz

\displaystyle\quad-\alpha\int_{0}^{x}\int_{-1}^{1}\mu e^{2\alpha z}(\psi^{B}-(T^{B})^{4})^{2}d\mu dz=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}\mu e^{2\alpha z}(\psi^{B}(z,\cdot)-(T^{B}(z))^{4})^{2}d\mu\bigg|_{0}^{x}.

Using the relation (
2.28
) and
1-\alpha\mu\geq 1-\alpha
for
0\leq\alpha<1
in the above equation leads to

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}e^{2\alpha z}4(T^{B})^{3}|\partial_{z}T^{B}|^{2}dx+(1-\alpha)\int_{0}^{x}\int_{-1}^{1}e^{2\alpha z}(\psi^{B}-(T^{B})^{4})^{2}d\mu dz

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\mu\int_{-1}^{1}e^{2\alpha x}(\psi^{B}(x,\cdot)-(T^{B}(x))^{4})^{2}d\mu+\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{0}|\mu|(\psi^{B}(0,\cdot)-T_{b})^{4})^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\mu(\psi_{b}-T_{b}^{4})^{2}d\mu,

(2.32)

and finishes the proof of Theorem
4
.
∎

3.
Existence and decay results for the nonlinear Milne problem

In this section, we consider the existence of the nonlinear Milne problem and prove Theorem
1
.
We first pass to the limit
B\to\infty
in system (
2.1
)-(
2.2
) and show the existence of weak solutions to system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) as well as the weighted estimate (
1
). Then the exponentially decay of solutions is shown.

3.1.
Existence

We first pass to the limit
B\to\infty
on system (
2.1
)-(
2.2
) and show the existence of weak solutions for system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
).

Proof of Theorem
1
, existence.

First we show the convergence of
T^{B}(x)
as
B\to\infty
. To show this, we first prove that there exists a subsequence
\{T^{B_{k}}(B_{k})\}
converging to some constant
T_{\infty}
.
From the weighted estimate (
4
) (taking
x=B
in (
4
)), we can get

\displaystyle(T^{B})^{5/2}(B)-T_{b}^{5/2}=\int_{0}^{B}\partial_{x}(T^{B})^{5/2}dx=\int_{0}^{B}e^{-\alpha x}e^{\alpha x}\partial_{x}(T^{B})^{5/2}dx

\displaystyle\quad\leq\left(\int_{0}^{B}e^{2\alpha x}|\partial_{x}(T^{B})^{5/2}|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{0}^{B}e^{-2\alpha x}dx\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{1}{(2\alpha)^{1/2}}\sqrt{(1-e^{-2\alpha B})}\left(\frac{25}{16}\int_{0}^{B}e^{2\alpha x}4(T^{B})^{3}|\partial_{x}(T^{B})|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{5}{4(2\alpha)^{1/2}}\sqrt{(1-e^{-2\alpha B})}\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\mu(\psi_{b}-T_{b}^{4})^{2}d\mu\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{5}{4(2\alpha)^{1/2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\mu(\psi_{b}-T_{b}^{4})^{2}d\mu\right)^{1/2}.

Hence
(T^{B})^{5/2}(B)
is bounded for any
B>0
. By the Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem, there exists a subsequence
\{B_{k}\}
such that

\displaystyle T^{B_{k}}(B_{k})\to T_{\infty},\quad\text{as }k\to\infty,

where
T_{\infty}
is some constant. Since
0\leq T^{k}(B_{k})(B_{k})\leq\gamma
according to Theorem
4
,
0\leq T_{\infty}\leq\gamma
.

Next we show
T^{B}(x)-T^{B}(B)
is uniformly bounded in
L^{2}([0,B])
.
From estimate (
4
), it holds the inequality

\displaystyle(1-\alpha)\int_{0}^{B}\int_{-1}^{1}e^{2\alpha x}(\psi^{B}-(T^{B})^{4})^{2}d\mu dx\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\mu(\psi_{b}-T_{b}^{4})^{2}d\mu.

Using this estimate and the relation

\displaystyle\left(\int_{-1}^{1}\mu\psi^{B}d\mu\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{-1}^{1}\mu(\psi^{B}-(T^{B})^{4})d\mu\right)^{2}\leq\int_{-1}^{1}\mu^{2}d\mu\int_{-1}^{1}(\psi^{B}-(T^{B})^{4})^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{3}\int_{-1}^{1}(\psi^{B}-(T^{B})^{4})^{2}d\mu,

we can get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{B}e^{2\alpha x}|\langle\mu\psi^{B}\rangle|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{3}\int_{0}^{B}\int_{-1}^{1}e^{2\alpha x}(\psi^{B}-(T^{B})^{4})^{2}d\mu dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{3}\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\int_{0}^{1}\mu(\psi_{b}-T_{b}^{4})^{2}d\mu.

Due to (
2.27
), the above inequality implies

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{B}e^{2\alpha x}|\partial_{x}T^{B}|^{2}dx\leq\frac{1}{3}\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\int_{0}^{1}\mu(\psi_{b}-T_{b}^{4})^{2}d\mu.

(3.1)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{B}(T^{B}(x)-T^{B}(B))^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{B}\left(\int_{x}^{B}\partial_{z}T^{B}(z)dz\right)^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{B}\left(\int_{x}^{B}e^{-\alpha z}e^{\alpha z}\partial_{z}T^{B}(z)dz\right)^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{B}\left(\int_{x}^{B}e^{2\alpha z}|\partial_{z}T^{B}|^{2}(z)dz\cdot\int_{x}^{B}e^{-2\alpha z}dz\right)dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\alpha}\int_{0}^{B}e^{2\alpha x}|\partial_{x}T^{B}|^{2}dx\cdot\int_{0}^{B}(e^{-2\alpha x}-e^{-2\alpha B})dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{6}\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\frac{e^{2\alpha B}-2\alpha B-1}{2\alpha^{2}}e^{-2\alpha B}\int_{0}^{1}\mu(\psi_{b}-T_{b}^{4})^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{6}\frac{1}{2\alpha^{2}(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{1}\mu(\psi_{b}-T_{b}^{4})^{2}d\mu,

where
(e^{2\alpha B}-2\alpha B-1)e^{-2\alpha B}=1-2\alpha Be^{-2\alpha B}-e^{-2\alpha B}\leq 1
.
Hence
T^{B}(x)-T^{B}(B)\in L^{2}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
is uniformly bounded.
Moreover, due to the boundary condition (
2.3
),
\partial_{x}T^{B}(B)=0
and so the estimate (
3.1
) implies

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{B}|\partial_{x}T^{B}(x)-\partial_{x}T^{B}(B)|^{2}dx\leq\frac{1}{3(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{1}\mu(\psi_{b}-T_{b}^{4})^{2}d\mu,

(3.2)

thus
T^{B}(x)-T^{B}(B)
is uniformly bounded in
H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. Moreover,
\|\partial_{x}^{2}T^{B}\|_{L^{2}([0,B])}=\|\langle\psi^{B}-(T^{B})^{4}\rangle\|_{L^{2}([0,B])}\leq\sqrt{2}\|\psi^{B}-(T^{B})^{4}\|_{L^{2}([0,B]\times[-1,1])}
is uniformly bounded. Therefore, there exists a subsequence
B_{k}
such that

\displaystyle T^{B_{k}}(x)-T^{B_{k}}(B_{k})\rightharpoonup T(x)-T_{\infty},\quad\text{weakly in }H^{2}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+}).

(3.3)

By the continuous embedding
C_{\rm loc}^{1}\subset H^{2}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, we get from the above convergence

\displaystyle T^{B_{k}}(x)\to T(x),\quad\text{strongly in }C^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+}).

(3.4)

Next we show the convergence of
\psi^{B}(x)
. Due to the uniform estimate (
2.3
),
\|\psi^{B}-(T^{B})^{4}\|_{L^{2}([0,B]\times[-1,1])}
is uniformly bounded in
B
. This, together with (
3.4
) implies that there exists a subsequence
\{\psi^{B_{k}}\}
such that as
k\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\psi^{B_{k}}(x,\mu)-(T^{B_{k}})^{4}(x)\rightharpoonup\psi(x,\mu)-T^{4},\quad\text{ weakly in }L^{2}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times[-1,1]).

(3.5)

Next we show the limit
T,\psi
solves system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) with boundary conditions (
1.3
)-(
1.4
).
First we show the limit
T(x)
,
\psi(x)
satisfies the boundary condition (
1.3
)-(
1.4
). Due to the trace operator
u\mapsto u(0)
is continous (see
[
8
, Ex 8.18]
) for any
u\in H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, we can get from (
3.3
) that
T(0)=\lim_{k\to\infty}T^{B_{k}}(0)=T_{b}
. One can also check that
\varphi\to\varphi(0,\cdot)
is also continous for any
\varphi\in L^{2}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times[-1,1])\cap\{\varphi:\mu\partial_{x}\varphi+\varphi\in C_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\}
(one can use the formulas (
B.3
)-(
B.4
) to show this). Hence,
\psi^{B_{k}}(0,\mu)\rightharpoonup\psi^{k}(0,\mu)
as
B_{k}\to\infty
.
To show
(T,\psi)
satisfies (
1.1
)-(
1.2
), we apply a test function
h=h(x)\in C^{1}([0,x])
,
\varphi\in C^{1}([0,x]\times[-1,1])
to (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), integrate by parts, and obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}\partial_{z}T^{B}\partial_{z}hdz-\partial_{z}T^{B}(z)h(z)\bigg|_{0}^{x}+\int_{0}^{x}\int_{-1}^{1}(\psi^{B}-(T^{B})^{4})hd\mu dz=0,

(3.6)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}\int_{-1}^{1}\mu\psi^{B}\partial_{z}\varphi d\mu dz-\int_{-1}^{1}\mu\varphi(z,\cdot)\psi^{B}(z,\cdot)d\mu\bigg|_{0}^{x}+\int_{0}^{x}\int_{-1}^{1}(\psi^{B}-(T^{B})^{4})\varphi d\mu dz=0,

(3.7)

Due to (

), we can pass to the limit

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}\partial_{z}T^{B}\partial_{z}hdz\to\int_{0}^{x}\partial_{z}T\partial_{z}hdz,\quad\text{ as }B\to\infty.

(3.8)

Due to (

)-(

), we can pass to the limit

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}\int_{-1}^{1}\mu\psi^{B}\partial_{z}\varphi d\mu dz\to\int_{0}^{x}\int_{-1}^{1}\mu\psi\partial_{z}\varphi d\mu dz,\quad\text{as }B\to\infty,

(3.9)

Using (

,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}\int_{-1}^{1}(\psi^{B}-(T^{B})^{4})hd\mu dz\to\int_{0}^{x}\int_{-1}^{1}(\psi-T^{4})hd\mu dz,

(3.10)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}\int_{-1}^{1}(\psi^{B}-T^{4})\varphi d\mu dz\to\int_{0}^{x}\int_{-1}^{1}(\psi-T^{4})\varphi d\mu dz.

(3.11)

By the continuity of the trace operator, we get from the above facts,

(3.12)

Due to

Combining the above limits, we can pass to the limit

3.2.
Weighted estimate

Next we show the weighted estimate (
1
).

Proof of Theorem
1
, weighted estimate.

Due to the lower semi-continuous of the norm
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}([0,x])}
, the estimate (
2.3
) and the strong convergence (
3.4
) of
T
and the weak convergence (
3.3
) of
\partial_{x}T^{B}
imply

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}4T^{3}|\partial_{z}T|^{2}dz\leq\liminf_{B\to\infty}\int_{0}^{x}4(T^{B})^{3}|\partial_{z}T^{B}|^{2}dz.

(3.16)

Due to the weak convergence (
3.5
) of
\psi^{B}-(T^{B})^{4}
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}\int_{-1}^{1}(\psi-T^{4})^{2}d\mu dz\leq\liminf_{B\to\infty}\int_{0}^{x}\int_{-1}^{1}(\psi^{B}-(T^{B})^{4})^{2}d\mu dz.

(3.17)

From (
2.3
),
\langle\mu(\psi^{B}-(T^{B})^{2})^{2}\rangle
is non-negative and uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. Hence

\displaystyle\langle\mu(\psi^{B}-(T^{B})^{4})^{2}\rangle\rightharpoonup^{*}\langle\mu(\psi-T^{4})^{2}\rangle,\quad\text{weakly-* in }L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+}).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\limsup_{B\to\infty}\langle\mu(\psi^{B}-(T^{B})^{4})^{2}\rangle\geq\langle\mu(\psi-T^{4})^{2}\rangle\geq 0.

(3.18)

Combining the above inequality with (
3.16
)-(
3.17
), we can take
\limsup
on (
2.3
) and using
\limsup_{n\to\infty}(a_{n}+b_{n})\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}a_{n}+\liminf_{n\to\infty}b_{n}
to get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}4T^{3}|\partial_{z}T|^{2}dz+\int_{0}^{x}\int_{-1}^{1}(\psi-T^{4})^{2}d\mu dz+\frac{1}{2}\langle\mu(\psi(x,\cdot)-T^{4}(x))^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{0}|\mu|(\psi(0,\cdot)-T_{b}^{4})^{2}d\mu\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\mu(\psi_{b}-T_{b}^{4})^{2}d\mu,

(3.19)

holds for any
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Note in the above
\langle\mu(\psi(x,\cdot)-T^{4}(x))^{2}\rangle\geq 0
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

Similarly, we can pass to the limit
B\to\infty
in (
4
) and use the weak convergence up to a subsequence implied by (
4
):

\displaystyle e^{\alpha x}|\partial_{x}(T^{B})^{\frac{5}{2}}|\rightharpoonup e^{\alpha x}|\partial_{x}T^{\frac{5}{2}}|,\quad\text{weakly in }L^{2}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+}),

\displaystyle e^{\alpha x}(\psi^{B}-(T^{B})^{4})\rightharpoonup e^{\alpha x}(\psi-T^{4}),\quad\text{weakly in }L^{2}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times[-1,1]),

\displaystyle e^{2\alpha x}\langle\mu(\psi^{B}-(T^{B})^{4})^{2}\rangle\rightharpoonup^{*}e^{2\alpha x}\langle\mu(\psi-T^{4})^{2}\rangle,\quad\text{weakly-* in }L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+}),

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{0}|\mu|(\psi^{B}(0,\cdot)-T_{b}^{4})^{2}d\mu\to\int_{-1}^{0}|\mu|(\psi(0,\cdot)-T_{b}^{4})^{2}d\mu,\quad\text{weakly-* in }L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+}),

Consequently, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}e^{2\alpha z}4T^{3}|\partial_{z}T|^{2}dz\leq\liminf_{B\to\infty}\int_{0}^{x}e^{2\alpha}4(T^{B})^{3}|\partial_{z}T^{B}|^{2}dz,

∎

4.1.
The spectral assumption

We consider the spectrum of the nonlinear operator

Lemma 2

Before giving the proof of this lemma, we will show some inequalities related to the spectral assumption
(A)

Lemma 3

Assume

satisfies the spectral assumption
(A)
. Then the following inequality

\displaystyle M\int_{0}^{\infty}(2T^{\frac{3}{2}})^{2}|\partial_{x}g|^{2}dx\geq 4\int_{0}^{\infty}|\partial_{x}(2T^{\frac{3}{2}})|^{2}g^{2}dx,

(4.6)

holds for the same constant
M<1
given in
(A)
and any function

with

Proof.

Let
F\in C_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
be any function defined on the half-line and
F(x)\geq 0
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Taking
f=\int_{0}^{x}F(t)dt
in (
1.9
) leads to

\displaystyle M\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{2\beta x}(2T^{\frac{3}{2}})^{2}F^{2}(x)dx\geq 4\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{2\beta x}|\partial_{x}(2T^{\frac{3}{2}})|^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{x}F(t)dt\right)^{2}dx,

(4.7)

for the same constant
M<1
as in the spectral assumption
(A)
. Let
G(x)=e^{\beta x}F(x)\geq 0
, the above inequality implies

\displaystyle M\int_{0}^{\infty}(2T^{\frac{3}{2}})^{2}G^{2}(x)dx

\displaystyle\geq 4\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{2\beta x}|\partial_{x}(2T^{\frac{3}{2}})|^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{x}e^{-\beta t}G(t)dt\right)^{2}dx

\displaystyle=4\int_{0}^{\infty}|\partial_{x}(2T^{\frac{3}{2}})|^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{x}e^{\beta(x-t)}G(t)dt\right)^{2}dx

\displaystyle\geq 4\int_{0}^{\infty}|\partial_{x}(2T^{\frac{3}{2}})|^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{x}G(t)dt\right)^{2}dx,

since

Lemma 4

Assume the spectral assumption
(A)
is fulfilled, then

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}4T^{3}|\partial_{x}f|^{2}dx+\int_{0}^{\infty}\partial_{x}(4T^{3})f\partial_{x}fdx\geq 0

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{2\beta x}4T^{3}|\partial_{x}f|^{2}dx+\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{2\beta x}\partial_{x}(4T^{3})f\partial_{x}fdx\geq 0

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\partial_{x}(4T^{3})f\partial_{x}fdx\right|

Appendix B

Lemma 9
.

Given
\psi_{b}=\psi_{b}(\mu)
, for any
\mu\in(0,1]
satisfying

for some constant
\gamma>0
. Let

satisfy

for any

.
Then there exists a unique solution
\psi\in C^{1}([0,B]\times[-1,1])
to the equation

(B.1)

\displaystyle\psi(0,\mu)=\psi_{b}(\mu),\quad\psi(B,\mu)=\psi(B,-\mu),\quad\text{ for any }\mu\in(0,1],

(B.2)

and the solution verifies

for

.

\psi_{1},\psi_{2}
be two solutions corresponding to the source data

for any

and
\mu\in[-1,1]
.

The existence and uniqueness of (

) can be shown by the method of characteristic lines. Actually, we can compute the solution to (

) directly. For
\mu>0
, we integrate from
[0,x]
and get

\displaystyle\psi(x,\mu)=\psi_{b}(\mu)e^{-\frac{x}{\mu}}+\int_{0}^{x}\frac{1}{\mu}h(s)e^{-\frac{x-s}{\mu}}ds.

(B.3)

For
\mu<0
we integrate (

) over

\displaystyle\psi(x,\mu)=\psi_{b}(-\mu)e^{\frac{2B-x}{\mu}}-\int_{0}^{B}\frac{1}{\mu}e^{\frac{2B-x-s}{\mu}}h(s)ds-\int_{x}^{B}\frac{1}{\mu}e^{-\frac{x-s}{\mu}}h(s)ds,

\displaystyle\psi(x,\mu)\leq\gamma e^{-\frac{x}{\mu}}+\int_{0}^{x}\frac{\gamma}{\mu}e^{-\frac{x-s}{\mu}}ds=\gamma

from (

\displaystyle\psi(x,\mu)\leq\gamma e^{\frac{2B-x}{\mu}}-\int_{0}^{B}\frac{\gamma}{\mu}e^{\frac{2B-x-s}{\mu}}ds-\int_{x}^{B}\frac{\gamma}{\mu}e^{-\frac{x-s}{\mu}}ds=\gamma.

Suppose

is given by

\displaystyle\psi(x,\mu)=\psi_{b}(\mu)e^{-\frac{x}{\mu}}+\int_{0}^{x}\frac{1}{\mu}h(s)e^{-\frac{x-s}{\mu}}ds.

(C.3)

and

\displaystyle\psi(x,\mu)=-\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\mu}e^{-\frac{x-s}{\mu}}h(s)ds,

(C.4)

The formula (
C.3
) is the same as (
B.3
) and can be shown the same way. The formula (
C.4
) is obtained by sending
B\to\infty
in (
B.4
). The condition
h\in L^{\infty}
is used, since for example,

\displaystyle 0\leq\int_{0}^{B}\frac{1}{\mu}e^{\frac{2B-x-s}{\mu}}h(s)ds=e^{\frac{2B-x}{\mu}}\frac{1}{\mu}\int_{0}^{B}e^{-\frac{s}{\mu}}h(s)ds\leq e^{\frac{2B-x}{\mu}}\|h\|_{L^{\infty}([0,\infty))}(1-e^{-\frac{B}{\mu}})

(C.5)

By passing to the limit
B\to\infty
the above inequality becomes

\displaystyle 0\leq\lim_{B\to\infty}\int_{0}^{B}\frac{1}{\mu}e^{\frac{2B-x-s}{\mu}}h(s)ds\leq 0

(C.6)

and hence
\lim_{B\to\infty}\int_{0}^{B}\frac{1}{\mu}e^{\frac{2B-x-s}{\mu}}h(s)ds=0
.

Appendix D
Generalized Hardy’s inequality

First we recall the following generalized Hardy’s inequality.

Lemma 10

(Generalized Hardy’s inequality
[
22
]
)
.

Let
p>1
and
0<b\leq\infty
. The inequality

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{b}\left(\int_{0}^{x}F(t)dt\right)^{p}u(x)dx\leq C_{hd}\int_{0}^{b}F(x)^{p}v(x)dx

(D.1)

holds for all measurable function
F(x)\geq 0
on
(0,b)
if and only if

\displaystyle A=\sup_{r\in(0,b)}\left(\int_{r}^{b}u(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{0}^{r}(v(x))^{1-p^{\prime}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}<\infty,

and the best constant of
C_{hd}
satisfies
A\leq C_{hd}^{1/p}\leq p^{1/p}(p^{\prime})^{1/p^{\prime}}A
where
\tfrac{1}{p^{\prime}}+\tfrac{1}{p}=1
.

Theorem 6
.

[
14
, Theorem 11.3]

Let
\mathcal{N}
be a compact mapping of a Banach space
D

K

\|x\|_{D}<K

Abstract.

New explicit exponential stability conditions are presented for the non-autonomous scalar linear functional differential equation

\dot{x}(t)+\sum_{k=1}^{m}a_{k}(t)x(h_{k}(t))+\int_{g(t)}^{t}K(t,s)x(s)ds=0,

where
h_{k}(t)\leq t
,
g(t)\leq t
,
a_{k}(\cdot)
and the kernel
K(\cdot,\cdot)
are oscillatory and, generally, discontinuous functions.
The proofs are based on establishing boundedness of solutions and later using the exponential dichotomy for linear equations stating that either the homogeneous equation is exponentially stable or a non-homogeneous equation has an unbounded solution for some bounded right-hand side.
Explicit tests are applied to models of population dynamics, such as controlled Hutchinson and Mackey-Glass equations.
The results are illustrated with numerical examples, and connection to known tests is discussed.

The fundamental function

3.
Applications

Consider a generalized Hutchinson equation with the control-type term

\dot{N}(t)=N(t)\left.\left.\left.\left.\sum_{j=1}^{m}r_{j}(t)\right[K-N(h_{j}(t))\right]-u(t)\right[N(g(t))-K\right].

If the population is less than

5.
Discussion

Let us recall that a scalar ODE
\dot{x}(t)+a(t)x(t)=0
is UES if the coefficient
a(t)
is not necessarily positive,
but has to be positive in some integral sense. For linear FDEs, such as Eq. (
1.6
), a usual assumption in stability tests is
a(t)\geq 0
, which does not hold for many real-world models with seasonal fluctuations.
In the present paper we obtained explicit tests when linear FDEs without assumptions on signs of coefficients are UES and deduced local UES conditions for controlled Hutchinson and Mackey-Glass equations.
The results of the paper are illustrated with several examples.

Now proceed to comparison with known stability results for equations with oscillating coefficients.

Proposition
2
and Corollary
1
can be compared using Example
4
.
The proposition is applicable only for
b=\frac{\mu+\beta}{2}
. Other stability conditions in the proposition after some routine calculations give the following stability test for Eq. (
1.6
)

\frac{(\mu-\beta)(2\mu+\beta)}{\mu+\beta}<1,~~e\tau\frac{\mu+\beta}{2}<1.

(5.1)

To compare, let us fix
\mu=11\beta
. Then (
5.1
) becomes

\beta<\frac{6}{115},~~\tau\beta<\frac{1}{6e}\,,

while conditions (
4.5
) and (
4.6
) are

\tau\beta e^{6\beta}<\frac{5}{121}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\tau\beta e^{11\beta/6}<\frac{1}{66}\,,

respectively.
Thus, for
\beta\geq\frac{6}{115}\approx 0.05217
, the criterion in Proposition
2
cannot be applied. If we take
\beta=1
, the bound for the delay
\tau<\tau^{*}\approx 0.0024
given by (
4.6
) is sharper than
\tau<\tau^{*}\approx 0.0001
described in (
4.5
). For
\beta=0.1
, the bound for the delay
\tau<\tau^{*}\approx 0.22678
defined in (

) is sharper than
\tau<\tau^{*}\approx 0.1261
in (

). Further, for
\beta=0.05
, already (

) holds for
\tau<10/3
.
The bounds of

and
\tau^{*}\approx 0.276
are worse in the case when Proposition

Comparing Proposition
3
with Theorem
2
, we observe
that inequality (

and without the integral term)
the statement of Proposition

Application of Theorem

to Eq. (

) gives the stability test

\tau\|a\|_{[t_{0},\infty)}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{-\int_{s}^{t}a(\xi)d\xi}|a(s)|ds\leq\alpha<1

which is less sharp than Proposition

However, Theorems
1
and
2
are applicable
to more general non-autonomous equations than (

).

In addition, let us dwell on relevant areas and ideas
for future relevant research on equation and systems, whether with or without integral terms, including retarded arguments and oscillatory coefficients and/or kernels.

(1)

It would be natural to extend results in the obvious direction when the reference exponentially stable ODE includes a partial sum of
a_{k}
, or some other combination of concentrated-distributed terms, as a coefficient. Also, a FDE can be used as a reference equation rather than ODE, see, for example,
[
5
]
where this idea was implemented.

(2)

It is a challenging problem to investigate stability for systems of scalar linear delay differential equations with oscillating coefficients, as well as vector delay differential equations without any sign conditions on the entries of the matrices.

(3)

For applied nonlinear equations with oscillating coefficients considered in the present paper we obtained local stability conditions. It would be interesting to explore global stability.

(4)

For explicit UES conditions for all equations considered in the paper, we use only Part c) of Lemma
1
. It is also possible to obtain exponential stability tests applying other parts of the lemma.

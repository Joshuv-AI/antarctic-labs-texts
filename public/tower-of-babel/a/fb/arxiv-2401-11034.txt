Exact analytical solution for the Sakiadis boundary layer Preprint: AIP/123-QED
N. S. Barlow, W. C. Reinberger, S. J. Weinstein

Abstract
It has recently been established [Naghshineh et al., IMA J. of Appl. Math., 88 , 1 (2023)] that a convergent series solution may be obtained for the Sakiadis boundary layer problem once key parameters are determined iteratively using the series itself. Here, we provide exact and explicit analytical expressions for these parameters, including that associated with wall shear, thus completing the exact analytical solution. The complete exact analytical solution to the Sakiadis problem is summarized here for direct use.



The Sakiadis boundary layer along a moving wall is an important flow field in configurations where thin liquid films are coated onto moving substrates
Weinstein and Ruschak 2004
. From the time since
Sakiadis 1961
applied Blasius’s similarity transform to Prandtl’s boundary layer equations (with appropriate boundary conditions) to arrive at the similarity solution, the work has been cited nearly 3000 times (>500 times in 2023 alone). The similarity transformation reduces the nonlinear PDE system (the boundary layer equations) to a third-order nonlinear ODE for which no general analytical solution is known. Hence, it is not surprising that, even in the most recent studies
Hattori 2023
, the exact solution is obtained numerically. Asymptotically consistent approximant
Barlow et al. 2017
and convergent series
Naghshineh et al. 2023a
solutions may be obtained for the Sakiadis problem once key parameters are determined iteratively--using the approximant or series itself

Appendix: Recursive Formulae for Series Reversion

The implementation below follows the developments of
Henrici 1956

y=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}x^{n},a_{1}\neq 0,

(17)

x=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}\left(y-a_{0}\right)^{n},

(18)

series reversion
refers to the determination of the

coefficients. This process may be done recursively via the combination of Lagrange’s expansion formula (Equation 3.6.6 in

b_{n+1}=\frac{1}{\left(n+1\right)!}\left[\frac{d^{n}\left(\tilde{y}^{-n-1}\right)}{dx^{n}}\right]_{x=0},~\tilde{y}=\frac{y-a_{0}}{x},

(19)

with the formula (due to
Euler 1748
and
Hansted 1881
, a.k.a. the J.C.P. Miller Formula
Henrici 1956
) for raising a series to a power,

\tilde{y}^{-m}=\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n+1}x^{n}\right)^{-m}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbf{D}_{n,m}x^{n},

(20a)

\mathbf{D}_{n>0,m}=\frac{-1}{na_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(jm+n-j\right)a_{j+1}\mathbf{D}_{n-j,m},

(20b)

\mathbf{D}_{0,m}=a_{1}^{-m}.

(20c)

The Taylor coefficients of (
20a
) for
m=n+1
are exactly given by (
19
) multiplied by
(n+1)
, such that

b_{n+1}=\frac{\textbf{D}_{n,n+1}}{n+1}.

(21)

Substitution of (

n=0

and substitution of (

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\textbf{D}_{n,n+1}}{n+1},~n>0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-ma_{n+1}}{a_{1}^{m+1}}-\frac{1}{na_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(jm+n-j\right)a_{j+1}\textbf{D}_{n-j,m},

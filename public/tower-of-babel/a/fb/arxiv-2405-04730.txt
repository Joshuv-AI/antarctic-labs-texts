A rigidity property for a type of wave-Klein-Gordon system
Yan-Tao Li, Yue MA

Abstract
In this paper we investigate the rigidity property of a wave component coupled in a wave-Klein-Gordon system. We prove that when the radiation field of the wave component vanishes at the null infinity, the initial data of this component also vanish, therefor there is no wave in the whole spacetime.

In the present paper, we investigate the rigidity property of the wave component coupled in a wave-Klein-Gordon system. We have chosen (

The mechanism relies on an enforced energy estimate on wave component, which is carried out by applying the conformal-killing (with respect to the Minkowski metric) multiplier
Su=(t\partial_{t}+r\partial_{r})u
. This energy (denoted by
{\bf E}_{1}
in the coming sections) is stronger than the standard energy and provides strong decay rate on all components of
\nabla_{t,x}u
except one bad direction (this can be
\underline{L}=\partial_{t}-\partial_{r}
or
\partial_{r}=(x^{a}/r)\partial_{a}
. In the present paper we take
\partial_{t}
as the bad direction). Then combined with an ODE-based argument along a group of time-like hyperbolas together, one can establish an decay rate on
\nabla_{t,x}u
by integrating form the null infinity. This decay rate, as we call it “excessive decay”, is even faster than the generic linear wave decay. This leads to the fact that the standard energy (obtained by the multiplier
\partial_{t}u
) of the wave component tends to zero at a polynomial rate. Then an argument based on the energy identity and Gronwall’s inequality shows that the initial standard energy vanishes, and we conclude by the desired result.

2
Some basic facts of hyperboloidal framework

2.1
Frames and vector fields

Let
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{1+3}
with
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Denote by
r=\sqrt{|x^{1}|^{2}+|x^{2}|^{2}+|x^{3}|^{2}}
. We work in the light-cone
\mathcal{K}:=\{r<t-1\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{1+3}
.
Furthermore, we introduce

\mathcal{H}_{s}:=\{(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{1+3}|t=\sqrt{s^{2}+r^{2}}\},\quad\mathcal{H}_{[s_{0},s_{1}]}:=\mathcal{K}\cap\Big\{\sqrt{s_{0}^{2}+|x|^{2}}\leq t\leq\sqrt{s_{1}^{2}+|x|^{2}}\Big\},

\partial\mathcal{K}=\{t=|x|+1\},\quad\partial\mathcal{K}_{[s_{0},s_{1}]}=\partial\mathcal{K}\cap\Big\{\sqrt{s_{0}^{2}+|x|^{2}}\leq t\leq\sqrt{s_{1}^{2}+|x|^{2}}\Big\}.

and

\mathcal{H}^{\text{\bf init}}_{s_{0}}:=\big(\mathcal{H}_{s_{0}}\cap\mathcal{K}\big)\cup\partial\mathcal{K}_{[s_{0},s_{1}]}.

We recall the following nations introduced in
[
17
]
:

\uline{\partial}_{0}:=\partial_{t},\quad\uline{\partial}_{a}:=\bar{\partial}_{a}=(x^{a}/t)\partial_{t}+\partial_{a}.

The transition matrices between this frame and the natural frame
\{\partial_{\alpha}\}
are:

(2.1)

\uline{\Phi}_{\alpha}^{\beta}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}1&0&0&0\\
x^{1}/t&1&0&0\\
x^{2}/t&0&1&0\\
x^{3}/t&0&0&1\end{array}\right),\quad\uline{\Psi}_{\alpha}^{\beta}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}1&0&0&0\\
-x^{1}/t&1&0&0\\
-x^{2}/t&0&1&0\\
-x^{3}/t&0&0&1\end{array}\right)

with

\uline{\partial}_{\alpha}=\uline{\Phi}_{\alpha}^{\beta}\partial_{\beta},\quad\partial_{\alpha}=\uline{\Psi}_{\alpha}^{\beta}\uline{\partial}_{\beta}.

The vector field (derivatives)
\uline{\partial}_{a}
are tangent to the hyperboloid
\mathcal{H}_{s}
. We call them hyperbolic derivatives.

Let
T=T^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\otimes\partial_{\beta}
be a two tensor defined in
\mathcal{K}
or its subset. Then
T
can be written with
\{\uline{\partial}_{\alpha}\}
:

T=\uline{T}^{\alpha\beta}\uline{\partial}_{\alpha}\otimes\uline{\partial}_{\beta}\quad\text{with}\quad\uline{T}^{\alpha\beta}=T^{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}\uline{\Psi}_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{\alpha}\uline{\Psi}_{\beta^{\prime}}^{\beta}.

2.2
High-order derivatives

In the region
\mathcal{K}
, we introduce the following Lorentzian boosts:

L_{a}=x^{a}\partial_{t}+t\partial_{a},\quad a=1,2,3

and the following notation of high-order derivatives: let
I,J
be multi-indices taking values in
\{0,1,2,3\}
and
\{1,2,3\}
respectively,

I=(i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{m}),\quad J=(j_{1},j_{2},\cdots,j_{n}).

We define

\partial^{I}L^{J}=\partial_{i_{1}}\partial_{i_{2}}\cdots\partial_{i_{m}}L_{j_{1}}L_{j_{2}}\cdots L_{j_{n}}

to be an
(m+n)-
order derivative.

Let
\mathscr{Z}
be a family of vector fields.
\mathscr{Z}=\{Z_{i}|i=0,1,\cdots,6\}
with

Z_{0}=\partial_{t},\quad Z_{1}=\partial_{1},\quad Z_{2}=\partial_{2},\quad Z_{3}=\partial_{3},\quad Z_{4}=L_{1},\quad Z_{5}=L_{2},\quad Z_{6}=L_{3},

A high-order derivative of order
N
on
\mathscr{Z}
with milti-index
I=(i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{N})
,
i_{j}\in\{0,1,\cdots,6\}
is defined as

Z^{I}:=Z_{i_{1}}Z_{i_{2}}\cdots Z_{i_{N}}.

A high-order derivative
Z^{I}
is said to be of type
(a,b)
, if it contains at most
a
partial derivatives and
b
Lorentzian boosts. We then introduce the following notation:

\mathcal{I}_{p,k}=\{I|I\text{ is of type }(a,b)\text{ with }a+b\leq p,b\leq k\}

so
Z^{I}
with
I\in\mathcal{I}_{p,k}
stands for a high-order derivatives composed by boosts and partial derivative. Its order is smaller or equal to
p
and it contains at most
k
boosts. We define

(2.2)

\displaystyle|u|_{p,k}

\displaystyle:=\max_{K\in\mathcal{I}_{p,k}}|Z^{K}u|,\quad

\displaystyle|u|_{p}:=\max_{0\leq k\leq p}|u|_{p,k},

\displaystyle|\partial u|_{p,k}

\displaystyle:=\max_{\alpha}|\partial_{\alpha}u|_{p,k},

\displaystyle|\partial u|_{p}:=\max_{0\leq k\leq p}|\partial u|_{p,k},

\displaystyle|\partial^{m}u|_{p,k}

\displaystyle:=\max_{|I|=m}|\partial^{I}u|_{p,k},

\displaystyle|\partial^{m}u|_{p}:=\max_{0\leq k\leq p}|\partial^{I}u|_{p,k},

\displaystyle|\not{\partial}u|_{p,k}

\displaystyle:=\max\{|\uline{\partial}_{a}u|_{p,k}\},

\displaystyle|\not{\partial}u|_{p}:=\max_{0\leq k\leq p}|\not{\partial}u|_{p,k},

\displaystyle|\partial\not{\partial}u|_{p,k}

\displaystyle:=\max_{a,\alpha}\{|\uline{\partial}_{a}\partial_{\alpha}u|_{p,k},|\partial_{\alpha}\uline{\partial}_{a}u|_{p,k}\},

\displaystyle|\partial\not{\partial}u|_{p}:=\max_{0\leq k\leq p}|\partial\not{\partial}u|_{p,k},

\displaystyle|\not{\partial}\not{\partial}u|_{p,k}

\displaystyle:=\max_{a,b}\{|\uline{\partial}_{a}\uline{\partial}_{b}u|_{p,k}\},

\displaystyle|\not{\partial}\not{\partial}u|_{p}:=\max_{0\leq k\leq p}|\not{\partial}\not{\partial}u|_{p,k}.

In the above expressions the
\max
is taken for
\alpha\in\{0,1,2,3\}
and
a,b\in\{1,2,3\}
.
These quantities will be applied in order to control varies of high-order derivatives in the following discussion. Our first task is to bound them by energy densities. These results will be stated after the introduction of the standard and scaling energy inequalities in the following two sections.

3
The energy estimate with scaling multiplier on hyperboloids

3.1
Differential identities

We recall the following energy estimate (see for example the applications on Maxwell equations in
[
23
,
8
]
).Let
K_{1}:=t\partial_{t}+x^{a}\partial_{a}
. We firstly write the following differential identities in
\mathbb{R}^{1+n}
:

\displaystyle K_{1}u\Box u=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\Big(t\sum_{\alpha=0}^{n}|\partial_{\alpha}u|^{2}+2x^{b}\partial_{b}u\partial_{t}u\Big)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{a}\Big(2t\partial_{t}u\partial_{a}u+2x^{b}\partial_{b}u\partial_{a}u+x^{a}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}-x^{a}\sum_{b}|\partial_{b}u|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle+\frac{n-1}{2}\Big(|\partial_{t}u|^{2}-\sum_{b}|\partial_{b}u|^{2}\Big),

\displaystyle u\Box u=

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(u\partial_{t}u)-\partial_{a}(u\partial_{a}u)-\Big(|\partial_{t}u|^{2}-\sum_{b}|\partial_{b}u|^{2}\Big).

This leads to the divergence form:

(3.1)

\displaystyle\big(K_{1}u+(n-1)u/2\big)\Box u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\Big(t\sum_{\alpha=0}^{n}|\partial_{\alpha}u|^{2}+2x^{b}\partial_{b}u\partial_{t}u+(n-1)u\partial_{t}u\Big)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{a}\Big(2t\partial_{t}u\partial_{a}u+2x^{b}\partial_{b}u\partial_{a}u+x^{a}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}-x^{a}\sum_{b}|\partial_{b}u|^{2}+(n-1)u\partial_{a}u\Big).

3.2
Energy density

For simplicity of expression, we write (
3.1
) into the following form

\big(K_{1}u+(n-1)u/2\big)\Box u=\text{Div}\mathscr{V}_{1}[u]

with

\displaystyle 2\mathscr{V}_{1}^{0}[u]:=

\displaystyle t\sum_{\alpha=0}^{d}|\partial_{\alpha}u|^{2}+2x^{b}\partial_{b}u\partial_{t}u+(n-1)u\partial_{t}u,

\displaystyle-2\mathscr{V}_{1}^{a}[u]:=

\displaystyle 2t\partial_{t}u\partial_{a}u+2x^{b}\partial_{b}u\partial_{a}u+x^{a}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}-x^{a}\sum_{b}|\partial_{b}u|^{2}+(n-1)u\partial_{a}u.

In order to do energy estimate on hyperboloids, we need to regard firstly the energy density
\mathscr{V}_{1}[v]\cdot(1,-x^{a}/t)
, which is written as

\displaystyle 2{\bf e}_{1}[u]:=

\displaystyle t\big(1+(r/t)^{2}\big)|\partial_{t}u|^{2}+t\big(1-(r/t)^{2}\big)\sum_{a}|\partial_{a}u|^{2}+2t^{-1}|x^{a}\partial_{a}u|^{2}+4x^{a}\partial_{t}u\partial_{a}u

\displaystyle+\frac{n-1}{t}uK_{1}u.

It can be written as

(3.2)

{\bf e}_{1}[u]=\frac{1}{2t}|K_{1}u|^{2}+\frac{t}{2}|(x^{a}/r)\uline{\partial}_{a}u|^{2}+\frac{s^{2}}{2t}\sum_{a<b}|r^{-1}\Omega_{ab}u|^{2}+\frac{n-1}{2t}uK_{1}u

where
\Omega_{ab}:=x^{a}\partial_{b}-x^{b}\partial_{a}
. When
n=1
the last two terms disappear and when
n=2
we denote by
\Omega=\Omega_{12}
. Then in order to guarantee the positivity, we establish the following Hardy type inequality:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
u
be a compactly supported, sufficiently regular function defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then for
\alpha<n
,

(3.3)

\|r^{-\alpha/2}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\frac{2}{n-\alpha}\|r^{1-\alpha/2}\partial_{r}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

where
r=\sqrt{\sum_{a=1}^{n}|x^{a}|^{2}}
and
\partial_{r}:=(x^{a}/r)\partial_{a}
.

Remark 3.2
.

If we take
\alpha=2
, (
3.3
) leads to

\|r^{-1}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\frac{2}{n-2}\|\partial_{r}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

which is known as the classical Hardy’s inequality for
n\geq 3
. In the present work we will apply the case
\alpha=1
and
n\geq 2
.

Proof.

Remark the following identity

u^{2}r^{-\alpha}=\frac{u^{2}}{n-\alpha}\partial_{a}(x^{a}/r^{\alpha})=\frac{1}{n-\alpha}\partial_{a}\big(u^{2}x^{a}r^{-\alpha}\big)-\frac{2u}{n-\alpha}(x^{a}r^{-\alpha})\partial_{a}u

Integrate the above identity in the domain
\{r\geq\varepsilon\}
and apply Stokes’ formula (
u
is compactly supported), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{r\geq\varepsilon}u^{2}r^{-\alpha}dx=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n-\alpha}\int_{r\geq\varepsilon}\partial_{a}\big(u^{2}x^{a}r^{-\alpha}\big)dx-\frac{2}{n-\alpha}\int_{r\geq\varepsilon}u(x^{a}r^{-\alpha})\partial_{a}u\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n-\alpha}\int_{r=\varepsilon}u^{2}(x^{a}r^{-\alpha})\cdot(-x^{a}/r)d\sigma-\frac{2}{n-\alpha}\int_{r\geq\varepsilon}u(x^{a}r^{-\alpha})\partial_{a}u\,dx

where
d\sigma=\varepsilon^{n-1}d\omega_{n-1}
is the volume form of
\{r=\varepsilon\}
while
d\omega_{n-1}
is that of the unit
n-1
sphere. Then we obtain

\int_{r\geq\varepsilon}u^{2}r^{-\alpha}dx=\int_{r=\varepsilon}u^{2}r^{n-\alpha}d\omega_{n-1}-\frac{2}{n-\alpha}\int_{r\geq\varepsilon}u(x^{a}r^{-\alpha})\partial_{a}u\,dx.

Let
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, we obtain

\|r^{-\alpha/2}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\leq-\frac{2}{n-\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}r^{-\alpha/2}u\,x^{a}r^{-\alpha/2}\partial_{a}udx\leq\frac{2}{n-\alpha}\|r^{-\alpha/2}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|r^{1-\alpha/2}\partial_{r}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

This leads to the desired result.
∎

Return to our energy density. Let us fix

u_{s}(x):=u\big(\sqrt{s^{2}+r^{2}},x\big)

being the restriction of

on
\mathcal{H}_{s}

Then
\partial_{r}u_{s}(x)=(x^{a}/r)\uline{\partial}_{a}u(t,x)
with

(3.4)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}t|(x^{a}/r)\uline{\partial}_{a}u|^{2}dx=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}(t-r)|(x^{a}/r)\uline{\partial}_{a}u|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{n}}|r^{1-1/2}\partial_{r}u_{s}|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}(t-r)|(x^{a}/r)\uline{\partial}_{a}u|^{2}dx+\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}\|r^{-1/2}u_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}(t-r)|(x^{a}/r)\uline{\partial}_{a}u|^{2}dx+\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}\int_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}r^{-1}u^{2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}(t-r)|(x^{a}/r)\uline{\partial}_{a}u|^{2}dx+\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}\int_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}\big(r^{-1}-t^{-1}\big)u^{2}dx

\displaystyle+\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}\int_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}t^{-1}u^{2}dx.

Combine this bound with (

(3.5)

\displaystyle{\bf E}_{1}(s,u):=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}{\bf e}_{1}[u]dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{s^{2}}{2}\sum_{a<b}\|t^{-1/2}r^{-1}\Omega_{ab}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{s})}^{2}+\int_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}\frac{1}{2t}|K_{1}u|^{2}+\frac{t}{2}|(x^{a}/r)\uline{\partial}_{a}u|^{2}+\frac{n-1}{2t}uK_{1}u\,dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a<b}\|(s/t)t^{1/2}r^{-1}\Omega_{ab}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{s})}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|(t-r)^{1/2}(x^{a}/r)\uline{\partial}_{a}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{s})}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}t^{-1}(K_{1}u+(n-1)u/2)^{2}\,dx+\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}\Big\|\Big(\frac{t-r}{rt}\Big)^{1/2}u\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{s})}^{2}.

Then one has the following relations:

(3.11)

\|v(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=s^{-n/2}\|(s/t)^{n/2}w\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{s})},\quad\partial_{s}v(s,x)=s^{n/2-1}(t^{-n/2}K_{1}w)\Big|_{\big(s\sqrt{1+|x|^{2}},sx\big)}.

Then we calculate

\frac{d}{ds}\|v(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}=2\|v(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\frac{d}{ds}\|v(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

On the other hand

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}\|v(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}=\frac{d}{ds}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v^{2}(s,x)dx=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v(s,x)\partial_{s}v(s,x)dx

This leads to

2\|v(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\frac{d}{ds}\|v(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 2\|v(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\partial_{s}v(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

thus

\frac{d}{ds}\|v(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|\partial_{s}v(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

We concentrate on the right-hand-side:

\displaystyle\|\partial_{s}v(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}=

\displaystyle s^{n-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big|t^{-n/2}K_{1}w\big|^{2}_{\big(s\sqrt{1+|x|^{2}},sx\big)}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s^{-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big|t^{-n/2}K_{1}w\big|^{2}_{\big(\sqrt{s^{2}+|x^{2}|},x\big)}dx=s^{-2}\|t^{-n/2}K_{1}w\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{s})}^{2}.

Then we obtain

(3.12)

\|v(s_{1},\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|v(s_{0},\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\int_{s_{0}}^{s_{1}}s^{-1}\|t^{-n/2}K_{1}w\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{s})}ds

Now we take
w=t^{(n-1)/2}u
. Then
\|v(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{s})}=\|t^{-1/2}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{s})}
. Substitute these relations together with (
3.10
) into (
3.12
), we obtain the desired result.
∎

For the convenience of expression, we introduce

{\bf F}_{1}(s,u;s_{0}):={\bf E}_{1}(s,u)+{\bf E}_{1}(s_{0},u)+\int_{s_{0}}^{s}{s^{\prime}}^{-1}{\bf E}_{1}(s^{\prime},u)\,ds^{\prime}.

Here we remark that for a fixed
s_{0}

\|t^{-1/2}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{s_{0}})}\leq C(s_{0})\|(s/t)t^{-1/2}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{s_{0}})}

4
Sharp decay estimate on Klein-Gordon equation

Form this section we fix
n=3
. We also apply
A\lesssim B
for
A\leq CB
with
C
an irrelevant constant.

4.1
Objective

In this section we recall and reformulate the technique introduced in
[
15
]
and applied in
[
5
]
combined with normal form method. The main observation is that, based on the hyperbolic decomposition of wave operator, one can reduce Klein-Gordon equation into an ODE (more precisely, a harmonic oscillator). Then the techniques on ODE will be applied in order to obtain decay estimates which are more “robust” in the sens that it can undertake considerable perturbations.

4.2
Differential identity

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
v
be a sufficiently regular solution to

(4.1)

g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}v+c^{2}(1+P)v=f

in
\mathcal{H}_{[s_{0},s_{1}]}
and vanishing near
\partial\mathcal{K}
.
g^{\alpha\beta}=m^{\alpha\beta}+H^{\alpha\beta}
is a sufficiently regular metric defined in
\mathcal{H}_{[2,s_{1}]}
. We denote by

\bar{H}:=(s/t)^{-2}\uline{H}^{00}
and suppose that

(4.2)

|\bar{H}|\leq 1/2,\quad|H|_{p,k}\leq 1/2.

P,f
are sufficiently regular functions defined in
\mathcal{H}_{[2,s_{1}]}
with
|P|\leq 1/2
,
|P|_{p,k}\leq C
. Then

(4.3)

\mathcal{L}^{2}(s^{3/2}v)+c^{2}(1+P-\bar{H})s^{3/2}v=s^{3/2}S_{2}[v]+\frac{s^{3/2}f}{1+\bar{H}}

where
\mathcal{L}=(s/t)\partial_{t}+(x^{a}/s)\uline{\partial}_{a}
and

(4.4)

\displaystyle s^{3/2}|S_{2}[\partial^{I}L^{J}v]|\lesssim s^{-1/2}|v|_{p+2}+(s/t)^{-1}s^{1/2}|H||\partial v|_{p+1}+|\bar{H}||P||v|_{p},\quad|I|+|J|\leq p,\quad|J|\leq k,

\displaystyle s^{3/2}|(1+\bar{H})f|_{p,k}\lesssim|f|_{p,k}.

Proof.

We first recall the hyperbolic parameterization
(s,x)
of
\mathcal{K}
.

s=\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}},\quad x^{a}=x^{a}.

The associate the natural frame is

\bar{\partial}_{0}=\bar{\partial}_{s}=(s/t)\partial_{t},\quad\bar{\partial}_{a}=\uline{\partial}_{a}=(x^{a}/t)\partial_{t}+\partial_{a}.

It is evident that
[\bar{\partial}_{\alpha},\bar{\partial}_{\beta}]=0
.

Then we preform the following calculation:

(4.5)

\Box v+c^{2}(1+P)v+(s/t)^{-2}\uline{H}^{00}\bar{\partial}_{s}\bar{\partial}_{s}v=f-\uline{H}(\partial\partial v)+\uline{H}^{00}(s/t)^{-1}s^{-1}(r/t)^{2}\partial_{t}v

where

\uline{H}(\partial\partial v)=\sum_{(\alpha,\beta)\neq(0,0)}\uline{H}^{\alpha\beta}\uline{\partial}_{\alpha}\uline{\partial}_{\beta}u+H^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\big(\uline{\Psi}_{\beta}^{\beta^{\prime}}\big)\uline{\partial}_{\beta^{\prime}}v.

Then we recall the following hyperbolic decomposition of D’Alembert operator:

(4.6)

\displaystyle\Box v=

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}_{s}\bar{\partial}_{s}v+\frac{2x^{a}}{s}\bar{\partial}_{s}\bar{\partial}_{a}v-\sum_{a}\bar{\partial}_{a}\bar{\partial}_{a}v+\frac{3}{s}\bar{\partial}_{s}v.

Then combine (
4.5
) and (
4.6
), we obtain:

\displaystyle\big(1+(s/t)^{-2}\uline{H}^{00}\big)\bar{\partial}_{s}\bar{\partial}_{s}v

\displaystyle+\frac{2x^{a}}{s}\bar{\partial}_{s}\bar{\partial}_{a}v+\frac{3}{s}\bar{\partial}_{s}v+c^{2}(1+P)v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f-\uline{H}(\partial\partial v)+\uline{H}^{00}(s/t)^{-1}s^{-1}(r/t)^{2}\partial_{t}v+\sum_{a}\bar{\partial}_{a}\bar{\partial}_{a}v

then, denote by
\bar{H}=(s/t)^{-2}\uline{H}^{00}
,

(4.7)

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}_{s}\bar{\partial}_{s}v+\frac{2x^{a}}{s}\bar{\partial}_{s}\bar{\partial}_{a}v+\frac{3}{s}\bar{\partial}_{s}v+\frac{c^{2}(1+P)v}{1+\bar{H}}=S_{1}[H,P,v]+\frac{f}{1+\bar{H}}

where

\displaystyle S_{1}[H,P,v]=

\displaystyle\big(1-(1+\bar{H})^{-1}\big)\Big(\frac{2x^{a}}{s}\bar{\partial}_{s}\bar{\partial}_{a}v+\frac{2}{s}\bar{\partial}_{s}v\Big)

\displaystyle+(1+\bar{H})^{-1}\Big(\uline{H}^{00}(s/t)^{-1}s^{-1}(r/t)^{2}\partial_{t}v-\uline{H}(\partial\partial v)+\sum_{a}\bar{\partial}_{a}\bar{\partial}_{a}v\Big).

Now remark the following identity:

\bar{\partial}_{s}\bar{\partial}_{s}v+\frac{2x^{a}}{s}\bar{\partial}_{s}\bar{\partial}_{a}v+\frac{3}{s}\bar{\partial}_{s}v=s^{-3/2}\big(\bar{\partial}_{s}+(x^{a}/s)\bar{\partial}_{a}\big)^{2}(s^{3/2}v)-\frac{x^{a}x^{b}}{s^{2}}\bar{\partial}_{a}\bar{\partial}_{b}v-\frac{3x^{a}}{s^{2}}\bar{\partial}_{a}v-\frac{3}{4s^{2}}v.

Then combined with (
4.7
) and denote by
\mathcal{L}:=\bar{\partial}_{s}+(x^{a}/s)\bar{\partial}_{a}
, we obtain (
4.3
) with

\displaystyle S_{2}[H,P,v]=

\displaystyle s^{-2}(x^{a}x^{b}\bar{\partial}_{a}\bar{\partial}_{b}v+3x^{a}\uline{\partial}_{a}+3v/4)+S_{1}[H,P,v]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s^{-2}(x^{a}x^{b}\bar{\partial}_{a}\bar{\partial}_{b}v+3x^{a}\uline{\partial}_{a}+3v/4)+\big(1-(1+\bar{H})^{-1}-(1+P)^{-1}\bar{H}\big)c^{2}(1+P)v

\displaystyle+\big(1-(1+\bar{H})^{-1}\big)\Big(\frac{2x^{a}}{s}\bar{\partial}_{s}\bar{\partial}_{a}v+\frac{2}{s}\bar{\partial}_{s}v\Big)

\displaystyle+(1+\bar{H})^{-1}\Big(\uline{H}^{00}(s/t)^{-1}s^{-1}(r/t)^{2}\partial_{t}v-\uline{H}(\partial\partial v)+\sum_{a}\bar{\partial}_{a}\bar{\partial}_{a}v\Big).

We now concentrate on the bounds on the bound of
S_{2}
. Firstly we need to control
(1+\bar{H})^{-1},1-(1+\bar{H})^{-1}
and
1-\bar{H}-(1+\bar{H})^{-1}
. When
|\bar{H}|\leq 1/2
,

(1+\bar{H})^{-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-\bar{H})^{k},\quad 1-(1+\bar{H})^{-1}=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(-\bar{H})^{k},\quad 1-\bar{H}-(1+\bar{H})^{-1}=-\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}(-\bar{H})^{k}.

When
|\bar{H}|_{[p/2]}\leq 1/2
, one may differentiate the above identities term by term. Then we obtain

(4.8)

\displaystyle|(1+\bar{H})^{-1}|_{p}\lesssim

\displaystyle 1,\quad|(1+\bar{H})|_{p}\lesssim|\bar{H}|_{p},\quad\text{for }p\geq 1,

\displaystyle|1-(1+\bar{H})^{-1}|_{p}\lesssim

\displaystyle|\bar{H}|_{p},

\displaystyle|1-\bar{H}-(1+\bar{H})^{-1}|_{p}\lesssim

\displaystyle|\bar{H}|_{p}|\bar{H}|_{[p/2]}.

Equipped with the above bounds, it is direct to see the following bound on
S_{2}[H,P,v]
:

|S_{2}[H,P,\partial^{I}L^{J}v]|\lesssim s^{-2}|v|_{p+2}+(s/t)^{-1}s^{-1}|H||\partial v|_{p+1}+|\bar{H}||P||v|_{p},\quad|I|+|J|\leq p,\quad|J|\leq k.

In the same manner,

|s^{3/2}(1+\bar{H})^{-1}f|_{p,k}\lesssim s^{3/2}|f|_{p,k}.

The we established the desired bound.
∎

4.1

(5.1)

\displaystyle{\bf E}_{0,c}(s,u):=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}\Big(\big((s/t)\partial_{t}u\big)^{2}+\sum_{a}(\uline{\partial}_{a}u)^{2}+c^{2}u^{2}\Big)\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}\left((\uline{\partial}_{\perp}u)^{2}+\sum_{a}\left((s/t)\partial_{a}u\right)^{2}+\sum_{a<b}\left(t^{-1}\Omega_{ab}u\right)^{2}+c^{2}u^{2}\right)\,dx,

and, for
g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}u+c^{2}u=f

(5.2)

\displaystyle{\bf E}_{0,g,c}(s,u)=

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u(g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}u+c^{2}u)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\big(g^{00}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}-g^{ab}\partial_{a}u\partial_{b}u+c^{2}u^{2}\big)+\partial_{a}\big(g^{a\beta}\partial_{t}u\partial_{\beta}u\big)

UNEXPECTED UNCERTAINTY PRINCIPLE FOR DISC BANACH SPACES
K. MAHESH KRISHNA

Abstract




Abstract
: Let
(\{f_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty},\{\tau_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty})
and
(\{g_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty},\{\omega_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty})
be unbounded continuous p-Schauder frames (
0<p<1
) for a disc Banach space
\mathcal{X}
. Then for every
x\in(\mathcal{D}(\theta_{f})\cap\mathcal{D}(\theta_{g}))\setminus\{0\}
, we show that

(1)

\displaystyle\|\theta_{f}x\|_{0}\|\theta_{g}x\|_{0}\geq\frac{1}{\left(\displaystyle\sup_{n,m\in\mathbb{N}}|f_{n}(\omega_{m})|\right)^{p}\left(\displaystyle\sup_{n,m\in\mathbb{N}}|g_{m}(\tau_{n})|\right)^{p}},

where

\displaystyle\theta_{f}:\mathcal{D}(\theta_{f})\ni x\mapsto\theta_{f}x\coloneqq\{f_{n}(x)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N}),\quad\theta_{g}:\mathcal{D}(\theta_{g})\ni x\mapsto\theta_{g}x\coloneqq\{g_{n}(x)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N}).

Inequality (
1
) is unexpectedly different from both bounded uncertainty principle
[arXiv:2308.00312v1]
and unbounded uncertainty principle
[arXiv:2312.00366v1]
for Banach spaces.

Keywords
: Uncertainty Principle, Frame, Banach space.

Mathematics Subject Classification (2020)
: 42C15.

1.
Introduction

Given a finite collection
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
in a finite dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
over
\mathbb{K}
(
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
), define

\displaystyle\theta_{\tau}:\mathcal{H}\ni h\mapsto\theta_{\tau}h\coloneqq(\langle h,\tau_{j}\rangle)_{j=1}^{n}\in\mathbb{K}^{n}.

Recall that a collection
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
in
\mathcal{H}
is said to be a Parseval frame
[
6
]
for
\mathcal{H}
if

\displaystyle\|h\|^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\langle h,\tau_{j}\rangle|^{2},\quad\forall h\in\mathcal{H}.

Most general form of discrete uncertainty principle for finite dimensional Hilbert spaces is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

(
Donoho-Stark-Elad-Bruckstein-Ricaud-Torrésani Uncertainty Principle
)
[
8
,
9
,
19
]

Let
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
,
\{\omega_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
be two Parseval frames for a finite dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{\|\theta_{\tau}h\|_{0}^{2}+\|\theta_{\omega}h\|_{0}^{2}}{2}\geq\left(\frac{\|\theta_{\tau}h\|_{0}+\|\theta_{\omega}h\|_{0}}{2}\right)^{2}\geq\|\theta_{\tau}h\|_{0}\|\theta_{\omega}h\|_{0}\geq\frac{1}{\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n}|\langle\tau_{j},\omega_{k}\rangle|^{2}},\quad\forall h\in\mathcal{H}\setminus\{0\}.

Recently, Theorem
1.1
has been derived for Banach spaces using continuous p-Schauder frames.

Definition 1.2
.

[
13
]

Let
(\Omega,\mu)
be a measure space. Let
\{\tau_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega}
be a collection in a Banach space
\mathcal{X}
and
\{f_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega}
be a collection in
\mathcal{X}^{*}
. The pair
(\{f_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega},\{\tau_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega})
is said to be a
continuous p-Schauder frame
for
\mathcal{X}
(
1\leq p<\infty
) if the following holds.

(i)

For every
x\in\mathcal{X}
, the map
\Omega\ni\alpha\mapsto f_{\alpha}(x)\in\mathbb{K}
is measurable.

(ii)

For every
x\in\mathcal{X}
,

\displaystyle\|x\|^{p}=\int\limits_{\Omega}|f_{\alpha}(x)|^{p}\,d\mu(\alpha)\quad\text{if}\quad 1\leq p<\infty,

\displaystyle\|x\|=\operatorname{ess~sup}_{\alpha\in\Omega}|f_{\alpha}(x)|\quad\text{if}\quad p=\infty.

(iii)

For every
x\in\mathcal{X}
, the map

\Omega\ni\alpha\mapsto f_{\alpha}(x)\tau_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{X}

is weakly measurable.

(iv)

For every
x\in\mathcal{X}
,

\displaystyle x=\int\limits_{\Omega}f_{\alpha}(x)\tau_{\alpha}\,d\mu(\alpha),

where the integral is weak integral.

Given a continuous p-Schauder frame
(\{f_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega},\{\tau_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega})
for
\mathcal{X}
, define

\displaystyle\theta_{f}:\mathcal{X}\ni x\mapsto\theta_{f}x\in\mathcal{L}^{p}(\Omega,\mu);\quad\theta_{f}x:\Omega\ni\alpha\mapsto(\theta_{f}x)(\alpha)\coloneqq f_{\alpha}(x)\in\mathbb{K}

Theorem 1.3
.

(
Functional Continuous Donoho-Stark-Elad-Bruckstein-Ricaud-Torrésani Uncertainty Principle
)
[
13
,
15
,
12
]

Let
(\Omega,\mu)
,
(\Delta,\nu)
be measure spaces. Let
(\{f_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega}
,
\{\tau_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega})
and
(\{g_{\beta}\}_{\beta\in\Delta}
,
\{\omega_{\beta}\}_{\beta\in\Delta})
be continuous p-Schauder frames for a Banach space
\mathcal{X}
. Then

(i)

for
p>1
, we have

\displaystyle\mu(\operatorname{supp}(\theta_{f}x))^{\frac{1}{p}}\nu(\operatorname{supp}(\theta_{g}x))^{\frac{1}{q}}\geq\frac{1}{\displaystyle\sup_{\alpha\in\Omega,\beta\in\Delta}|f_{\alpha}(\omega_{\beta})|},\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\{0\};

\displaystyle\nu(\operatorname{supp}(\theta_{g}x))^{\frac{1}{p}}\mu(\operatorname{supp}(\theta_{f}x))^{\frac{1}{q}}\geq\frac{1}{\displaystyle\sup_{\alpha\in\Omega,\beta\in\Delta}|g_{\beta}(\tau_{\alpha})|},\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\{0\}.

where
q
is the conjugate index of
p
.

(ii)

for
p=1
, we have

\displaystyle\mu(\operatorname{supp}(\theta_{f}x))\geq\frac{1}{\displaystyle\sup_{\alpha\in\Omega,\beta\in\Delta}|f_{\alpha}(\omega_{\beta})|},\quad\nu(\operatorname{supp}(\theta_{g}x))\geq\frac{1}{\displaystyle\sup_{\alpha\in\Omega,\beta\in\Delta}|g_{\beta}(\tau_{\alpha})|},\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\{0\}.

(iii)

for
p=\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\nu(\operatorname{supp}(\theta_{g}x))\geq\frac{1}{\displaystyle\sup_{\alpha\in\Omega,\beta\in\Delta}|f_{\alpha}(\omega_{\beta})|},\quad\mu(\operatorname{supp}(\theta_{f}x))\geq\frac{1}{\displaystyle\sup_{\alpha\in\Omega,\beta\in\Delta}|g_{\beta}(\tau_{\alpha})|},\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\{0\}.

An unbounded version of
1.3
has been recently derived for unbounded frames.

Definition 1.4
.

[
16
]

Let
(\Omega,\mu)
be a measure space and
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Let
\{\tau_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega}
be a collection in a Banach space
\mathcal{X}
and
\{f_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega}
be a collection of linear functionals on
\mathcal{X}
(which may not be bounded). The pair
(\{f_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega},\{\tau_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega})
is called an
unbounded continuous p-Schauder frame
or
continuous semi p-Schauder frame
for
\mathcal{X}
if the following conditions holds.

(i)

For every
x\in\mathcal{X}
, the map
\Omega\ni\alpha\mapsto f_{\alpha}(x)\in\mathbb{K}
is measurable.

(ii)

The map

\displaystyle\theta_{f}:\mathcal{D}(\theta_{f})\ni x\mapsto\theta_{f}x\in\mathcal{L}^{p}(\Omega,\mu);\quad\theta_{f}x:\Omega\ni\alpha\mapsto(\theta_{f}x)(\alpha)\coloneqq f_{\alpha}(x)\in\mathbb{K}

is well-defined (need not be bounded).

(iii)

For every
x\in\mathcal{X}
, the map

\Omega\ni\alpha\mapsto f_{\alpha}(x)\tau_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{X}

is weakly measurable.

(iv)

For every
x\in\mathcal{D}(\theta_{f})
,

\displaystyle x=\int\limits_{\Omega}f_{\alpha}(x)\tau_{\alpha}\,d\mu(\alpha),

where the integral is weak integral.

Theorem 1.5
.

(
Unbounded Donoho-Stark-Elad-Bruckstein-Ricaud-Torrésani Uncertainty Principles
)
[
16
]

Let
(\Omega,\mu)
,
(\Delta,\nu)
be measure spaces and
p=1
or
p=\infty
. Let
(\{f_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega},\{\tau_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Omega})
and
(\{g_{\beta}\}_{\beta\in\Delta},\{\omega_{\beta}\}_{\beta\in\Delta})
be unbounded continuous p-Schauder frames for a Banach space
\mathcal{X}
. Then for every
x\in(\mathcal{D}(\theta_{f})\cap\mathcal{D}(\theta_{g}))\setminus\{0\}
, we have

\displaystyle\mu(\operatorname{supp}(\theta_{f}x))\nu(\operatorname{supp}(\theta_{g}x))\geq\frac{1}{\left(\displaystyle\sup_{\alpha\in\Omega,\beta\in\Delta}|f_{\alpha}(\omega_{\beta})|\right)\left(\displaystyle\sup_{\alpha\in\Omega,\beta\in\Delta}|g_{\beta}(\tau_{\alpha})|\right)}.

Above results cover the important Lebesgue spaces for
1\leq p\leq\infty
. The next natural class of spaces is the Lebesgue spaces for
0<p<1
. Recall that for
0<p<1
, we define

\displaystyle\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})\coloneqq\{\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}:a_{n}\in\mathbb{K},\forall n\in\mathbb{N},\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{p}<\infty\}

equipped with the inhomogeneous norm

\displaystyle\|\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\|_{p}\coloneqq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{p},\quad\forall\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N}).

In this paper, we derive a surprising result which is counter intuitive to the feeling we gain from Theorem
1.3
. This is why we called the uncertainty principle we obtained as unexpected uncertainty principle.

2.
Unexpected Uncertainty Principle

We start by recalling the following definition.

Definition 2.1
.

[
14
]

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a vector space over
\mathbb{K}
. We say that
\mathcal{X}
is a
disc Banach space
if there exists a map called as
disc norm

\|\cdot\|:\mathcal{X}\to[0,\infty)
satisfying the following conditions.

(i)

If
x\in\mathcal{X}
is such that
\|x\|=0
, then
x=0
.

(ii)

\|x+y\|\leq\|x\|+\|y\|
for all
x,y\in\mathcal{X}
.

(iii)

\|\lambda x\|\leq|\lambda|\|x\|
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
and for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{K}
with
|\lambda|\geq 1
.

(iv)

\|\lambda x\|\geq|\lambda|\|x\|
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
and for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{K}
with
|\lambda|\leq 1
.

(v)

\mathcal{X}
is complete w.r.t. the metric
d(x,y)\coloneqq\|x-y\|
for all
x,y\in\mathcal{X}
.

Banach space frame theory which is modeled on classical Lebesgue sequence spaces
[
17
]
and the theory of unbounded frames for Hilbert and Banach spaces
[
18
,
7
,
1
,
4
,
5
,
2
,
3
]
naturally gives the following definition.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a disc Banach space. Let
\{\tau_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a collection in
\mathcal{X}
and
\{f_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a collection of linear functionals on
\mathcal{X}
(which may not be bounded). The pair
(\{f_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty},\{\tau_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty})
is said to be a
unbounded p-Schauder frame
(
0<p<1
) or
semi p-Schauder frame
for
\mathcal{X}
if the following conditions holds.

(i)

The map

\displaystyle\theta_{f}:\mathcal{D}(\theta_{f})\ni x\mapsto\theta_{f}x\coloneqq\{f_{n}(x)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})

is well-defined (need not be bounded).

(ii)

For every
x\in\mathcal{D}(\theta_{f})
,

\displaystyle x=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_{n}(x)\tau_{n}

We are going to use the following important result.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
10
,
11
]

For every
0<p<1
,

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|\right)^{p}\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{p},\quad\forall\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N}).

Following is the main result of the paper.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
(\{f_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty},\{\tau_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty})
and
(\{g_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty},\{\omega_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty})
be unbounded p-Schauder frames for a disc Banach space
\mathcal{X}
. Then for every
x\in(\mathcal{D}(\theta_{f})\cap\mathcal{D}(\theta_{g}))\setminus\{0\}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\theta_{f}x\|_{0}\|\theta_{g}x\|_{0}\geq\frac{1}{\left(\displaystyle\sup_{n,m\in\mathbb{N}}|f_{n}(\omega_{m})|\right)^{p}\left(\displaystyle\sup_{n,m\in\mathbb{N}}|g_{m}(\tau_{n})|\right)^{p}}.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathcal{D}(\theta_{f})\setminus\{0\}
. Then using Theorem
2.3
,

\displaystyle\|\theta_{f}x\|

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|f_{n}(x)|^{p}=\sum_{n\in\operatorname{supp}(\theta_{f}x)}|f_{n}(x)|^{p}=\sum_{n\in\operatorname{supp}(\theta_{f}x)}\left|f_{n}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}g_{m}(x)\omega_{m}\right)\right|^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\operatorname{supp}(\theta_{f}x)}\left|\sum_{m\in\operatorname{supp}(\theta_{g}x)}g_{m}(x)f_{n}(\omega_{m})\right|^{p}\leq\sum_{n\in\operatorname{supp}(\theta_{f}x)}\left(\sum_{m\in\operatorname{supp}(\theta_{g}x)}|g_{m}(x)f_{n}(\omega_{m})|\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\displaystyle\sup_{n,m\in\mathbb{N}}|f_{n}(\omega_{m})|\right)^{p}\sum_{n\in\operatorname{supp}(\theta_{f}x)}\left(\sum_{m\in\operatorname{supp}(\theta_{g}x)}|g_{m}(x)|\right)^{p}

\displaystyle=\left(\displaystyle\sup_{n,m\in\mathbb{N}}|f_{n}(\omega_{m})|\right)^{p}\|\theta_{f}x\|_{0}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}|g_{m}(x)|\right)^{p}\leq\left(\displaystyle\sup_{n,m\in\mathbb{N}}|f_{n}(\omega_{m})|\right)^{p}\|\theta_{f}x\|_{0}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}|g_{m}(x)|^{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\displaystyle\sup_{n,m\in\mathbb{N}}|f_{n}(\omega_{m})|\right)^{p}\|\theta_{f}x\|_{0}\|\theta_{g}x\|.

Therefore

(2)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left(\displaystyle\sup_{n,m\in\mathbb{N}}|f_{n}(\omega_{m})|\right)^{p}}\|\theta_{f}x\|\leq\|\theta_{f}x\|_{0}\|\theta_{g}x\|.

On the other hand, let
x\in\mathcal{D}(\theta_{g})\setminus\{0\}
. Then again using Theorem
2.3
,

\displaystyle\|\theta_{g}x\|

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}|g_{m}(x)|^{p}=\sum_{m\in\operatorname{supp}(\theta_{g}x)}|g_{m}(x)|^{p}=\sum_{m\in\operatorname{supp}(\theta_{f}x)}\left|g_{m}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_{n}(x)\tau_{n}\right)\right|^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\operatorname{supp}(\theta_{g}x)}\left|\sum_{n\in\operatorname{supp}(\theta_{f}x)}f_{n}(x)g_{m}(\tau_{n})\right|^{p}\leq\sum_{m\in\operatorname{supp}(\theta_{g}x)}\left(\sum_{n\in\operatorname{supp}(\theta_{f}x)}|f_{n}(x)(x)g_{m}(\tau_{n})|\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\displaystyle\sup_{n,m\in\mathbb{N}}|g_{m}(\tau_{n})|\right)^{p}\sum_{m\in\operatorname{supp}(\theta_{g}x)}\left(\sum_{n\in\operatorname{supp}(\theta_{f}x)}|f_{n}(x)|\right)^{p}

\displaystyle=\left(\displaystyle\sup_{n,m\in\mathbb{N}}|g_{m}(\tau_{n})|\right)^{p}\|\theta_{g}x\|_{0}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|f_{n}(x)|\right)^{p}\leq\left(\displaystyle\sup_{n,m\in\mathbb{N}}|g_{m}(\tau_{n})|\right)^{p}\|\theta_{g}x\|_{0}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|f_{n}(x)|^{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\displaystyle\sup_{n,m\in\mathbb{N}}|g_{m}(\tau_{n})|\right)^{p}\|\theta_{g}x\|_{0}\|\theta_{f}x\|.

Therefore

(3)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left(\displaystyle\sup_{n,m\in\mathbb{N}}|g_{m}(\tau_{n})|\right)^{p}}\|\theta_{g}x\|\leq\|\theta_{g}x\|_{0}\|\theta_{f}x\|.

Multiplying Inequalities (
2
) and (
3
) we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left(\displaystyle\sup_{n,m\in\mathbb{N}}|f_{n}(\omega_{m})|\right)^{p}\left(\displaystyle\sup_{n,m\in\mathbb{N}}|g_{m}(\tau_{n})|\right)^{p}}\|\theta_{f}x\|\|\theta_{g}x\|\leq

\displaystyle\|\theta_{f}x\|_{0}\|\theta_{g}x\|_{0}\|\theta_{f}x\|\|\theta_{g}x\|,

\displaystyle\quad\forall x\in(\mathcal{D}(\theta_{f})\cap\mathcal{D}(\theta_{g}))\setminus\{0\}.

A cancellation of
\|\theta_{f}x\|\|\theta_{g}x\|
gives the inequality. ∎

As continuous version of Theorem
2.3
fails (even for finite measure spaces) it seems that continuous version of Theorem
2.4
fails.

In view of Tao’s uncertainty principle
[
20
]
we believe that Theorem
2.3
can be improved in prime dimensions.

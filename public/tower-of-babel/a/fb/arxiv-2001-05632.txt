1.
Introduction

In 1965, Erdős asked the following question
[
11
]
. Given an arbitrary sequence
A
of
N
different positive integers, what is the size of the largest sum-free subsequence of
A
? By
sum-free
we mean that if
x,y,z\in A
, then
x+y\neq z
. Let

\mathscr{M}_{(2,1)}(N)=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{>0}\\
|A|=N\end{subarray}}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq A\\
S\text{ is sum-free}\end{subarray}}|S|.

Using a beautiful probabilistic argument, Erdős showed that every
N
-element set
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{>0}
contains a sum-free subset of size at least
N/3
, in other words,
\mathscr{M}_{(2,1)}(N)\geq N/3
. It turns out that it is surprisingly hard to improve upon this bound. The result was later improved by Alon and Kleitman
[
2
]
, who showed that
\mathscr{M}_{(2,1)}(N)\geq(N+1)/3
. Bourgain
[
7
]
, using an entirely different Fourier analytic argument, showed that
\mathscr{M}_{(2,1)}(N)\geq(N+2)/3
, which is the best lower bound on
\mathscr{M}_{(2,1)}(N)
to date. In particular, the following conjecture has been made in a series of papers. See
[
11
,
7
,
10
,
28
]
for example.

Conjecture 1
.

There is a function
\omega(N)\to\infty
as
N\to\infty
, such that

\mathscr{M}_{(2,1)}(N)>\frac{N}{3}+\omega(N).

On the other hand, a recent breakthrough by Eberhard, Green, and Manners
[
10
]
proved that
\mathscr{M}_{(2,1)}(N)=(1/3+o(1))N
. More precisely, they showed that for every
\varepsilon>0
, when
N
is large enough, there is a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{>0}
of size
N
, such that every subset of
A
of size at least
(1/3+\varepsilon)N
contains
x,y,z
with
x+y=z
. This result is one of the first beautiful applications of the arithmetic regularity lemma. Later, using a completely different argument, the result is generalized by Eberhard
[
9
]
to
k
-sum-free
set. A set
A
is
k
-sum-free if for every
y,x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\in A
,
y\neq\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}
. Eberhard proved that for every
\varepsilon>0
, there is a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{>0}
of size
N
, such that every subset of
A
of size at least
(1/(k+1)+\varepsilon)N
contains a
k
-sum.
For more background we refer to the survey
[
28
]
.

In this paper, we study the analogue of the Erdős sum-free set problem for
(k,\ell)
-sum-free sets. Given two positive integers
k,\ell
with
k>\ell
, a set
A
is
(k,\ell)
-sum-free
if for every
x_{1},\dots,x_{k},y_{1},\dots,y_{\ell}\in A
,
\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{k}\neq\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}y_{j}
.
For example, using the notation of
(k,\ell)
-sum-free, sum-free is
(2,1)
-sum-free;
k
-sum-free is
(k,1)
-sum-free. Finding largest
(k,\ell)
-sum-free sets in some given structures is well-studied in the past fifty years, for example, the size of the maximum
(k,\ell)
-sum-free sets in finite cyclic groups was determined recently by Bajnok and Matzke
[
4
]
, and the size in compact abelian groups was determined by Kravitz
[
18
]
.

For every
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{>0}
, let

\mathscr{M}_{(k,\ell)}(A)=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq A\\
S\text{ is }(k,\ell)\text{-sum-free}\end{subarray}}|S|,\quad\text{and}\quad\mathscr{M}_{(k,\ell)}(N)=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{>0}\\
|A|=N\end{subarray}}\mathscr{M}_{(k,\ell)}(A).

The problem of determining
\mathscr{M}_{(k,\ell)}(N)
is suggested by Bajnok
[
3
, Problem G.41]
. In fact, we can also make the following conjecture for
(k,\ell)
-sum-free set, which is an analogue of Conjecture
1
.

Conjecture 2
.

Let
k>\ell>0
. There is a constant
c=c(k,\ell)>0
, and a function
\omega(N)\to\infty
as
N\to\infty
, such that

cN+\omega(N)<\mathscr{M}_{(k,\ell)}(N)<(c+\varepsilon)N,

for every
\varepsilon>0
.

As we mentioned above, the constant
c(k,\ell)
in Conjecture
2
for
(k,\ell)=(2,1)
is determined by Eberhard, Green, and Manners
[
10
]
, and for
(k,\ell)=(k,1)
is determined by Eberhard
[
9
]
. The conjecture for
(k,\ell)=(3,1)
is confirmed by Bourgain
[
7
]
.

Our first result determines the constant
c(k,\ell)
in Conjecture
2
for every
(k,\ell)
(see statements (i) and (iv) of Theorem
1.1
), which answers a question asked by Bajnok
[
3
]
when the ambient group is
\mathbb{Z}
. The statement (ii) of Theorem
1.1
also confirms Conjecture

for infinitely many
(k,\ell)

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
k,\ell
be two positive integers and
k>\ell
. Then the following hold:

(i)
for every
k,\ell
, we have
\mathscr{M}_{(k,\ell)}(N)\geq\frac{N}{k+\ell}
.

(ii)
suppose
k=5\ell
. Then

(1)

\mathscr{M}_{(k,\ell)}(N)\geq\frac{N}{k+\ell}+c\frac{\log N}{\log\log N},

where
c>0
is an absolute constant that only depends on
k,\ell
.

(iii)
for every set
A
of
N
positive integers, for every positive even integer
u
, there is an odd integer
v<u
such that if
k=(u+v)\ell/(u-v)
, then

(2)

\mathscr{M}_{(k,\ell)}(A)\geq\frac{N}{k+\ell}+c\frac{\log N}{\log\log N},

where
c>0
is an absolute constant that only depends on
k,\ell
.

(iv)
for every
k,\ell
, we have
\mathscr{M}_{(k,\ell)}(N)=\big(\frac{1}{k+\ell}+o(1)\big)N
.

We remark that Theorem
1.1
(iii) also implies estimate (
1
) when
k=3\ell
, which in particular covers the
(3,1)
-sum-free case obtained by Bourgain. This is because when
u=2
, the only possible value of

is
1
, and this gives us

. It follows that estimate (
2
) holds for every
N
-element set
A
when

. Hence, by the definition of
\mathscr{M}_{(k,\ell)}(N)
, we prove estimate (
1
) when
k=3\ell
.

The upper bound construction given by Eberhard, Green, and Manners
[
10
]
for
(2,1)
-sum-free set actually works in a more general setting: restricted
(2,1)
-sum-free set. A set
A
is
restricted
(k,\ell)
-sum-free
if for every
k
distinct elements
a_{1},\dots,a_{k}
in
A
, and
\ell
distinct elements
b_{1},\dots,b_{\ell}
in
A
, we have
\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}\neq\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}b_{j}
. Let

\widehat{\mathscr{M}}_{(k,\ell)}(N)=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{>0}\\
|A|=N\end{subarray}}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq A\\
S\text{ is restricted }(k,\ell)-\text{sum free}\end{subarray}}|S|.

Clearly, we have that
\mathscr{M}_{(k,\ell)}(N)\leq\widehat{\mathscr{M}}_{(k,\ell)}(N)
. Our next theorem gives us an upper bound on
\widehat{\mathscr{M}}_{(k,\ell)}(N)
when

.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
k,\ell
be positive integers, and

. Then

\widehat{\mathscr{M}}_{(k,\ell)}(N)=\Big(\frac{1}{k+\ell}+o(1)\Big)N.

Overview

The paper is organized as follows. In the next section, we provide some basic definitions and properties in additive combinatorics, harmonic analysis, and model theory (or more precisely, nonstandard analysis) used later in the proof. In Section 3, we prove a variant of the weak Littlewood conjecture, based on the ideas introduced by Bourgain
[
7
]
. Theorem
1.1
(i) is proved by using the probabilistic argument introduced by Erdős, and some structural results for the
(k,\ell)
-sum-free open set on the torus. This is included in Section 4. One of the main parts of the paper is to prove Theorem
1.1
(ii) and (iii). The special case for
(3,1)
-sum-free set is proved by Bourgain
[
7
]
, but his argument relies heavily on the fact that a certain term of the Fourier coefficient of the characteristic function is multiplicative, which is not true for the other
(k,\ell)
. Here we introduce a different sieve function, as well as a finer control on the functions we constructed. We will discuss it in detail in Section 5. In Sections 6 and 7, we prove Theorem
1.1
(iv). The proof goes by showing that the constructions given by Eberhard
[
9
]
for
(k,1)
-sum-free sets, the Følner sequence, is still the correct construction for the other
(k,\ell)
-sum-free sets. The new ingredients contain structural results for the large infinite
(k,\ell)
-sum-free sets, which can be viewed as a generalization of the Łuczak–Schoen Theorem
[
21
]
.
We will prove Theorem
1.2
in Section 8. In Section 9, we make some concluding remarks, and pose some open problems.

2.1.
Additive combinatorics

Throughout the paper, we use standard definitions and notation in additive combinatorics as given in
[
27
]
. Let
p
be a prime, and let
m,n,N
ranging over positive integers. Given
a,b,N\in\mathbb{N}
and
a<b
, let
[a,b]:=[a,b]\cap\mathbb{N}
, and let
[N]:=[1,N]
. We use the standard Vinogradov notation. That is,
f\ll g
means
f=O(g)
, and
f\asymp g
if
f\ll g
and
f\gg g
. Given
A,B\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
, we write

A+B:=\{a+b\mid a\in A,b\in B\},\quad\text{and}\quad AB:=\{ab\mid a\in A,b\in B\}.

When
A=\{x\}
, we simply write
x+B:=\{x\}+B
and
x\cdot B:=\{x\}B
. Given
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
, let

kA:=\{a_{1}+\dots+a_{k}\mid a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\in A\},

for integer
k\geq 2
. For example,
2\cdot\mathbb{N}
denotes the set of even natural numbers, while
2\mathbb{N}
denotes
\mathbb{N}+\mathbb{N}
which is still
\mathbb{N}
. Using this notation, a set
A
is
(k,\ell)
-sum-free
if
kA\cap\ell A=\varnothing
.

We also define the restricted sums. Let

\displaystyle A\widehat{+}B:=\{a+b\mid a\in A,b\in B,a\neq b\},

\displaystyle\widehat{kA}:=\{a_{1}+\dots+a_{k}\mid a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\in A,\text{ all of them are distinct}\}.

Thus a set
A
is
restricted
(k,\ell)
-sum-free
if
\widehat{kA}\cap\widehat{\ell A}=\varnothing
.

Let
f:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{C}
be a function. Define
\widehat{f}:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{C}
, where
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
is the 1-dimensional torus, and for every
r\in\mathbb{T}
,

\widehat{f}(r)=\sum_{x}f(x)e(-rx),

where
e(\theta)=e^{2\pi i\theta}
. By Fourier Inversion, for every
x\in\mathbb{Z}
,

f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\widehat{f}(r)e(rx)dr.

Let
\mu:\mathbb{N}^{>0}\to\mathbb{C}
be the
Möbius function
. Recall that
\mu
is supported on the square-free integers, and
\mu(n)=(-1)^{\omega(n)}
when
n
is square-free, where
\omega(n)
counts the number of distinct prime factors of
n
. By Inclusive-Exclusive Principle,

\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)=\begin{cases}0\quad&\text{ if }n>1,\\
1&\text{ if }n=1.\end{cases}

2.2.
Nonstandard analysis

We give some basic definitions in nonstandard analysis which will be used later in the proofs. For more systematic accounts we refer to
[
5
,
8
]
. Let
S
be a set with infinitely many elements. An
ultrafilter

\mathscr{U}
on
S
is a collection of subsets of
S
, such that the characteristic function
\mathbbm{1}_{\mathscr{U}}:2^{S}\to\{0,1\}
is a finitely additive
\{0,1\}
-valued probability measure on
S
. An ultrafilter is
principal
if it consists of all sets containing some element
s\in S
. Let
\beta S
denotes the collection of all ultrafilters. One can embed
S
into
\beta S
, by mapping
x\in S
to the principal ultrafilter generated by
x
. By a standard application of Zorn’s Lemma,
\beta S\setminus S
is non-empty.

Fix
\mathscr{U}\in\beta\mathbb{N}\setminus\mathbb{N}
, and let
M_{n}
be a structure for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The
ultraproduct

\prod_{n\to\mathscr{U}}M_{n}
is a space consists of all ultralimits
\lim_{n\to\mathscr{U}}x_{n}
of sequences
x_{n}
defined in
M_{n}
, with
\lim_{n\to\mathscr{U}}x_{n}=\lim_{n\to\mathscr{U}}y_{n}
if two sequences
\{x_{n}\}
and
\{y_{n}\}
agree on a set in
\mathscr{U}
. Let
{{}^{*}\mathbb{R}}:=\prod_{n\to\mathscr{U}}\mathbb{R}
be the hyperreal field. Every finite hyperreal number
\xi\in{{}^{*}\mathbb{R}}
is infinitely close to a unique real number
r\in\mathbb{R}
, called the
standard part
of
\xi
. In this case, we use the notation
r=\mathrm{st}(\xi)
.

Given a sequence of finite non-empty sets
F_{n}
, let
\mu_{n}(X)=|X\cap F_{n}|/|F_{n}|
be a uniform probability measure. Let
F=\prod_{n\to\mathscr{U}}F_{n}
be an ultraproduct. The
Loeb measure

[
19
]

\mu_{L}
on
F
is the unique probability measure on the
\sigma
-algebra generated by the Boolean algebra of internal subsets of
F
, such that when
X=\prod_{n\to\mathscr{U}}X_{n}
is an internal subset of
F
, we have

\mu_{L}(X)=\mathrm{st}\Big(\lim_{n\to\mathscr{U}}\mu_{n}(X_{n})\Big).

2.3.
Determinants of certain matrices

We make use of the following lemma several times in the later proofs, which records a fact about two special matrices.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
. Consider two matrices

A_{n}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\sin\theta_{1}&\sin\theta_{2}&\cdots&\sin\theta_{n}\\
\sin 2\theta_{1}&\sin 2\theta_{2}&\cdots&\sin 2\theta_{n}\\
\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots\\
\sin n\theta_{1}&\sin n\theta_{2}&\cdots&\sin n\theta_{n}\end{array}\right),

and

B_{n}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}1&1&\cdots&1\\
\cos\theta_{1}&\cos\theta_{2}&\cdots&\cos\theta_{n}\\
\cos 2\theta_{1}&\cos 2\theta_{2}&\cdots&\cos 2\theta_{n}\\
\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots\\
\cos(n\!-\!1)\theta_{1}&\cos(n\!-\!1)\theta_{2}&\cdots&\cos(n\!-\!1)\theta_{n}\end{array}\right).

Then we have the formula:

(3)

\det(A_{n})=2^{n-1}\big(\prod_{k=1}^{n}\sin\theta_{k}\big)\det(B_{n});

and

(4)

\det(B_{n})=2^{(n-1)(n-2)/2}\prod_{1\leq k<l\leq n}(\cos\theta_{l}-\cos\theta_{k}).

As a result,

\det(A_{n})=2^{n(n-1)/2}\big(\prod_{k=1}^{n}\sin\theta_{k}\big)\prod_{1\leq k<l\leq n}(\cos\theta_{l}-\cos\theta_{k}).

Proof.

For
k=1,2,\ldots n-1
, we subtract the
k
-th row from the
(k+1)
-th row in
A_{n}
, and use the basic trigonometric identities so that

\displaystyle\det(A_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\det\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}2\sin\frac{\theta_{1}}{2}\cos\frac{\theta_{1}}{2}&\cdots&2\sin\frac{\theta_{n}}{2}\cos\frac{\theta_{n}}{2}\\
\cdots&\cdots&\cdots\\
2\sin\frac{\theta_{1}}{2}\cos\frac{(2n\!-\!1)\theta_{1}}{2}&\cdots&2\sin\frac{\theta_{n}}{2}\cos\frac{(2n\!-\!1)\theta_{n}}{2}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{n}\big(\prod_{k=1}^{n}\sin\frac{\theta_{k}}{2}\big)\det\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\cos\frac{\theta_{1}}{2}&\cdots&\cos\frac{\theta_{n}}{2}\\
\cdots&\cdots&\cdots\\
\cos\frac{(2n\!-\!1)\theta_{1}}{2}&\cdots&\cos\frac{(2n\!-\!1)\theta_{n}}{2}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle C_{n}\det(B_{n}^{\prime}).

For
k=1,2,\ldots n-1
, we add the
k
-th row to the
k+1
-th row in
B_{n}^{\prime}
, and use the basic trigonometric identities again so that

\displaystyle\det(B_{n}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\det\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\cos\frac{\theta_{1}}{2}&\cdots&\cos\frac{\theta_{n}}{2}\\
2\cos\frac{\theta_{1}}{2}\cos\theta_{1}&\cdots&2\cos\frac{\theta_{n}}{2}\cos\theta_{n}\\
\cdots&\cdots&\cdots\\
2\cos\frac{\theta_{1}}{2}\cos(n\!-\!1)\theta_{1}&\cdots&2\cos\frac{\theta_{n}}{2}\cos(n\!-\!1)\theta_{n}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{n-1}\big(\prod_{k=1}^{n}\cos\frac{\theta_{k}}{2}\big)\det(B_{n})

Combining the calculations above we prove (
3
).

As for (
4
), we let
T_{n}
be the Chebyshev polynomial

T_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor{n/2}\rfloor}\binom{n}{2k}(x^{2}-1)^{k}x^{n-2k}.

Thus, we have
T_{n}(\cos x)=\cos nx
. The coefficient of the leading term,
x^{n}
in
T_{n}(x)
would be
a_{n}=2^{n-1}
. Combining this fact and several elementary row operations, we get

\displaystyle\det(B_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{(n-1)(n-2)/2}\det\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&\cdots&1\\
\cos\theta_{1}&\cdots&\cos\theta_{n}\\
\cdots&\cdots&\cdots\\
(\cos\theta_{1})^{n-1}&\cdots&(\cos\theta_{n})^{n-1}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{(n-1)(n-2)/2}\prod_{1\leq k<l\leq n}(\cos\theta_{l}-\cos\theta_{k}).

The last equation comes from the determinant formula for Vandermonde martix.

3.
A variant of the Littlewood conjecture

The Littlewood problem
[
15
]
is to ask that, what is

I(N):=\min_{A\subseteq\mathbb{Z},|A|=N}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Big|\sum_{n\in A}e^{inx}\Big|d\mu(x)?

The strong Littlewood conjecture asserts that the minimum occurs when
A
is an arithmetic progression. This conjecture is still widely open. However, the weak Littlewood conjecture,
I(N)\gg\log N
, is resolved by McGehee, Pigno, and Smith
[
22
]
, and independently by Konyagin
[
17
]
. The analogous question in discrete setting is also well studied, we refer to
[
13
,
24
,
25
]
for the interested readers. In this section, we will develop a variant of the weak Littlewood conjecture, based on the ideas given by Bourgain
[
7
]
.

Let
\mathcal{N}
be the set of natural numbers that only contains prime factors at least
P
, where
P\asymp(\log N)^{100}
is a prime. We will use the following lemma from
[
7
, Section 5]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
A
be a finite subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{+}
with
|A|=N
. For all
R\geq 1
, we define

A_{R}=\{m\in A:m<R\}.

Also, we use
{\rm Proj}_{R}\sum a_{k}e^{ikx}
to denote the truncated sum
\sum_{|k|\leq R}a_{k}e^{ikx}
. Assume
|a_{n}|\leq 1
and
P>(\log N)^{20}
. Then there is an absolute big constant
C
, such that

\Bigg\|{\rm Proj}_{R}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathcal{N},m\in A\end{subarray}}\frac{a_{n}}{n}e^{imnx}\Bigg\|_{2}<CP^{-1/15}|A_{R}|^{1/2}.

With Lemma
3.1
in hand, we are able to prove our technical lemma:

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
A
be a finite subset of
\mathbb{N}^{>0}
with
|A|=N
and let
P>(\log N)^{100}
. Assume
|a_{n}|\leq 1
. Then for any
r_{0}\in\mathbb{N}^{>0}
, there exists a function
\Phi(x)
with
\|\Phi\|_{\infty}<10
such that

(8)

\Big|\Big\langle\sum_{m\in A}(e^{imx}+e^{ir_{0}mx}),\Phi(x)\Big\rangle\Big|\geq c\log N;

while for any
\beta\in\mathbb{Z}
,

(9)

\Big|\Big\langle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathcal{N},m\in A\end{subarray}}\frac{a_{n}}{n}e^{i\beta mnx},\Phi(x)\Big\rangle\Big|\leq C(\log N)^{-2}.

Here
c,C
are two absolute constants.

Proof.

For convenience, we assume
A=\{m_{1},\ldots,m_{N}\}
, and define

G(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{im_{j}x}+e^{ir_{0}m_{j}x}.

Let
k_{0}
be the largest natural number that
10^{6k_{0}}<N
. We group
A
into disjoint subsets
\{B_{k}\}_{k=0}^{k_{0}}
such that for
0\leq k\leq k_{0}-1
,
|B_{k}|=10^{6k}
. Here
B_{0}=\{m_{1}\}
,
B_{1}=\{m_{2},\ldots,m_{10^{6}+1}\},\cdots
and
B_{k_{0}}=A\setminus(\bigcup_{k\leq k_{0}-1}B_{k})
. From the construction we know
|B_{k_{0}}|\asymp 10^{6k_{0}}
. For each
B_{k}
, we define

\widetilde{P}_{k}=\frac{1}{|B_{k}|}\sum_{m\in B_{k}}e^{imx}.

We also define, after setting
F_{M}=\sum_{|m|\leq M}\frac{M-|m|}{M}e^{imx}
to be the
M
-Féjer kernel,

P_{k}=\widetilde{P}_{k}\ast(e^{i\xi_{k}x}F_{|I_{k}|}).

Here
I_{k}=[a_{k},b_{k}]
is the interval with
a_{k}=\min\{m,m\in B_{k}\}
,
b_{k}=\max\{m,m\in B_{k}\}
, and
\xi_{k}
is the center of
I_{k}
. As a result, we have

(10)

{\rm supp}(\widehat{P}_{k})={\rm supp}(\widehat{\widetilde{P}_{k}})\subset I_{k},

and

Next, for each

Recall the inequality

if

. Then, observing that

4.
(k,\ell)
-sum-free open sets in the torus

In this section, we use
\mu_{H}
as the Haar probability measure on
\mathbb{T}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{T}
be a
(k,\ell)
-sum-free open set. Then
\mu_{H}(A)\leq\frac{1}{k+\ell}
.

Proof.

Since
A
is
(k,\ell)
-sum-free, we have
kA\cap\ell A=\varnothing
. In particular,
\mu_{H}(kA)+\mu_{H}(\ell A)\leq 1
. By Kneser’s inequality
[
16
]
,

(k+\ell)\mu_{H}(A)\leq\mu_{H}(kA)+\mu_{H}(\ell A)\leq 1,

which implies that
\mu_{H}(A)\leq 1/(k+\ell)
.
∎

Next, we construct some largest
(k,\ell)
-sum-free open sets in
\mathbb{T}
. When
k-\ell\geq 2
, our construction is asymmetric, which will help us get a better lower bound on
\mathscr{M}_{(k,\ell)}(N)
. We will discuss this in details in the next section.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
k,\ell
be two positive integers and
k>\ell
.
For every integer
t\in[k-\ell]
, set
\Omega_{t}=\big(\frac{t-1}{k-\ell}+\frac{\ell}{k^{2}-\ell^{2}},\frac{t-1}{k-\ell}+\frac{k}{k^{2}-\ell^{2}}\big)
.
Then
\Omega_{t}
is
(k,\ell)
-sum-free.

Lemma
4.2
is easy to verify, and we omit the details here.
When
k=\ell+1
, the following observation shows that all the possible
(k,\ell)
-sum-free open sets with maximum measure are symmetric. Thus one cannot apply the method used in the next section to improve the lower bound for the cases
k=\ell+1
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
k=\ell+1
. Suppose
A\subseteq\mathbb{T}
is a maximum
(k,\ell)
-sum-free open set. Then
A
is symmetric.

Proof.

Since
k=\ell+1
,
A
is
(k,\ell)
-sum-free implies that
(\ell A-\ell A)\cap A=\varnothing
. Hence
A\subseteq\mathbb{T}\setminus(\ell A-\ell A)
. By Kneser’s inequality,

\mu_{H}(\mathbb{T}\setminus(\ell A-\ell A))\leq 1-2\ell\mu_{H}(A).

By Proposition
4.1
,
\mu_{H}(A)=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}
. Thus
A=\mathbb{T}\setminus(\ell A-\ell A)
, and this implies that
A
is symmetric.
∎

Using the argument by Erdős
[
11
]
, Lemma
4.2
is able to give us the following lower bound on the maximum
(k,\ell)
-sum-free subsets of any set of
N
integers, which proves Theorem
1.1
(i).

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
k,\ell
be positive integers and
k>\ell
. Then for every
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{>0}
of size
N
,
A
contains a
(k,\ell)
-sum-free subsets of size at least
\frac{1}{k+\ell}N
.

Proof.

Let
\Omega_{t}
be as in Lemma
4.2
, and let
\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega}
be the characteristic function of
\Omega
in
\mathbb{T}
. Thus by Fubini’s Theorem,

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\sum_{n\in A}\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega}(nx)d\mu_{H}(x)=\sum_{n\in A}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega}(nx)d\mu_{H}(x)=\frac{N}{k+\ell}.

Therefore, by Pigeonhole principle, there exists
x\in\mathbb{T}
such that

|\{n\in A\mid nx\in\Omega\}|\geq\frac{N}{k+\ell},

finishes the proof.
∎

\displaystyle\,+\sum_{m\in A}\sum_{n\in\mathcal{N}\cup(2\cdot\mathcal{N})}\frac{1}{n}\Big(\psi^{\prime}(n)\cos(2\pi\ell mnx)+i\pi^{\prime}(n)\sin(2\pi\ell mnx)\Big)\Bigg\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\,\Bigg\|\sum_{t\in\mathcal{M}_{1}}\frac{\eta(t)}{t}\sum_{m\in A}F\Big(\frac{tmx}{4}\Big)\Bigg\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}+\Bigg\|\sum_{t\in\mathcal{M}}\frac{\mu(t)\pi(t)}{t}\sum_{m\in A}G\Big(\frac{tmx}{2}\Big)\Bigg\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\,+\Bigg\|\sum_{t\in\mathcal{M}}\frac{\mu(t)\pi(t)}{t}\sum_{m\in A}G(tmx)\Bigg\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\,\sum_{t\in\mathcal{M}_{1}}\bigg|\frac{\eta(t)}{t}\bigg|\bigg\|\sum_{m\in A}F(mx)\bigg\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}+2\sum_{t\in\mathcal{M}}\bigg|\frac{\mu(t)\pi(t)}{t}\bigg|\Bigg\|\sum_{m\in A}G(mx)\bigg\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\,\prod_{p\leq P}\Big(1+\frac{1}{p}\Big)\bigg(\sum_{t=1}^{k-\ell}\bigg\|\sum_{m\in A}f_{t}(mx)\bigg\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}+2\sum_{t\in I_{1}\cup I_{2}}\lambda_{t}\bigg\|\sum_{m\in A}f_{t}(mx)\bigg\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}\bigg).

By Mertens’ estimates we get

\prod_{p\leq P}\Big(1+\frac{1}{p}\Big)\ll\log P\asymp\log\log N.

Hence there is
t\in I
such that
\big\|\sum_{m\in A}f_{t}(mx)\big\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}\gg\frac{\log N}{\log\log N}
.

Note that,

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\sum_{n\in A}f_{t}(nx)dx=0.

Thus we have

\max_{x\in\mathbb{T}}\sum_{n\in A}f_{t}(nx)\geq\frac{1}{2}\bigg\|\sum_{n\in A}f_{t}(nx)\bigg\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}.

Together with (
22
) and (
23
), we get

\mathscr{M}(A)-\frac{N}{k+\ell}\gg\frac{\log N}{\log\log N},

and this proves Theorem
1.1
(ii).

Proof of Theorem
1.1
(iii)

Let
u
be an even integer, and let
t=u/2
in this subsection. Consider the following matrix

X=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\sin(\pi/u)&\sin(3\pi/u)&\cdots&\sin((2t-1)\pi/u)\\
\sin(2\pi/u)&\sin(6\pi/u)&\cdots&\sin(2(2t-1)\pi/u)\\
\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots\\
\sin(t\pi/u)&\sin(3t\pi/u)&\cdots&\sin(t(2t-1)\pi/u)\end{array}\right).

By Lemma
2.1
, there is
\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{t}
, with

, such that

.

For each odd integer
v
ranging from the interval
[1,u)
, define
\mathcal{P}_{v}
to be an infinite collection of pairs
(k_{v},\ell_{v})
of positive integers such that

. Let
F_{(k,\ell)}(x)
and

be the function constructed in (

) and (

) with respect to the pair
(k,\ell)
. Note that in the current constructions, for every
(k_{1},\ell_{1}),(k_{2},\ell_{2})\in\mathcal{P}_{v}
,

F_{(k_{1},\ell_{1})}\Big(\frac{x}{k_{1}-\ell_{1}}\Big)=F_{(k_{2},\ell_{2})}\Big(\frac{x}{k_{2}-\ell_{2}}\Big),

(k_{1}-\ell_{1})G_{(k_{1},\ell_{1})}\Big(\frac{x}{k_{1}-\ell_{1}}\Big)=(k_{2}-\ell_{2})G_{(k_{2},\ell_{2})}\Big(\frac{x}{k_{2}-\ell_{2}}\Big),

and we denote the above function by

.

Let
\bm{F}(x)=\left(F_{1}(x),F_{3}(x),\dots F_{2t-1}(x)\right),

since in this case

\mathcal{M}

\bm{\beta}\in\mathbb{R}^{t}

6.
Structure of infinite
(k,\ell)
-sum-free sets

Given
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{>0}
, the
upper density
of
A
is defined as

\overline{d}(A)=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{|A\cap[N]|}{N}.

We also define the
upper density on multiples
of
A
by

\widetilde{d}(A)=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{|A\cap(N!\cdot[n])|}{n}.

In this section, we will prove the following theorem, which will be used in the next section when constructing the upper bound estimate for Theorem 1 (iv).

Theorem 6.1
.

Suppose that
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{>0}
, and
A
is
(k,\ell)
-sum-free. Then
\widetilde{d}(A)\leq\frac{1}{k+\ell}
.

We break the proof of this theorem into three lemmas. The first lemma says that if a
(k,\ell)
-sum-free set
A
contains a certain long arithmetic progression, then the upper density of
A
is bounded.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{>0}
be a
(k,\ell)
-sum-free set. Let
x,s,d,m
be positive integers, such that
s\in\ell A-(k-1)A
,
x+d\cdot[m]\subseteq A
, and
s
is in the coset
x+d\cdot\mathbb{Z}
. Then

\overline{d}(A)\leq\frac{m+k+\ell-2}{(k+\ell)m+2(k+\ell-2)}.

Proof.

Since
s\in\ell A-(k-1)A
and
A
is
(k,\ell)
-sum-free, we have
s\notin A
. We will only consider
s\leq x
, and the case when
s\geq x+m
follows from the same proof.
Since
x+d\cdot[m]\subseteq A
, then
\big(x+d\cdot[m]\big)\cap\big(\ell A-(k-1)A\big)=\varnothing.
Thus, there is
s_{0}\in x+d\cdot\mathbb{Z}
, such that
s_{0}\in\ell A-(k-1)A
, and

(27)

\big(s_{0}+d\cdot[m]\big)\cap\big(\ell A-(k-1)A\big)=\varnothing.

Let
s_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}a_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}b_{j}
, where
a_{i},b_{j}\in A
for every
1\leq i\leq\ell
and
1\leq j\leq k-1
.

Let
B\subseteq A
such that

B:=\{b\in A\mid\big(b+d\cdot[m]\big)\cap A\neq\varnothing\}.

Set
a_{0}=b_{0}=0
. Given integers
1\leq u\leq k-1
and
2\leq v\leq\ell
, let

\mathcal{C}(u)=B+\sum_{j=1}^{k-u}b_{j}+(u-1)a_{1},\quad\quad\mathcal{D}(v)=B+\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-v}a_{j}+\sum_{i=0}^{v-1}b_{i},

and
\mathcal{C}(k)=A+(k-1)a_{1}
,
\mathcal{D}(1)=A+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}a_{j}
.
Let
\mathscr{F}=\{\mathcal{C}(u),\mathcal{D}(v)\mid u\in[k],v\in[\ell]\}
be the collection of all
\mathcal{C}(u)
and
\mathcal{D}(v)
.

Claim 1
.

Elements in
\mathscr{F}
are pairwise disjoint.

Proof of Claim
1
.

Observe that for every
u\in[k]
and
v\in[\ell]
,
\mathcal{C}(u)\cap\mathcal{D}(v)=\varnothing
. Otherwise, we will get
kA\cap\ell A\neq\varnothing
, contradicts that
A
is
(k,\ell)
-sum-free. Let
u_{1},u_{2}\in[k]
and
u_{1}<u_{2}
. Suppose that
\mathcal{C}(u_{1})\cap\mathcal{C}(u_{2})\neq\varnothing
. Then there exist
y_{1}\in B
and
y_{2}\in A
, such that

y_{1}+\sum_{j=k-u_{2}+1}^{k-u_{1}}b_{j}=y_{2}+(u_{2}-u_{1})a_{1}.

Then

\displaystyle s_{0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}a_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}b_{j}

\displaystyle=y_{1}+\sum_{i=2}^{\ell}a_{i}-y_{2}-(u_{2}-u_{1}-1)a_{1}-\sum_{j\in[1,k-u_{2}]\cup[k-u_{1}+1,k-1]}b_{j}.

Since
y_{1}\in B
, thus there is
r\in[m]
such that
y_{1}+rd\in A
. This implies
s_{0}+rd\in\ell A-(k-1)A
, contradicts (
27
).

Suppose
\mathcal{D}(v_{1})\cap\mathcal{D}(v_{2})\neq\varnothing
for some
v_{1},v_{2}\in[\ell]
and
v_{1}<v_{2}
. Similarly, there exist
y_{1}\in A
and
y_{2}\in B
, such that

y_{1}+\sum_{j=\ell-v_{2}+1}^{\ell-v_{1}}a_{j}=y_{2}+\sum_{i=v_{1}}^{v_{2}-1}b_{i}.

Let
c_{0}=0
, and let
c_{1},\dots,c_{v_{2}-v_{1}-1}\in A
if
v_{2}>v_{1}+1
. Therefore

\displaystyle s_{0}=y_{2}+\sum_{j\in[0,\ell-v_{2}]\cup[\ell-v_{1}+1,\ell]}a_{j}+\sum_{t=0}^{v_{2}-v_{1}-1}c_{t}-y_{1}-\sum_{i\in[0,v_{1}-1]\cup[v_{2},k-1]}b_{i}-\sum_{t=0}^{v_{2}-v_{1}-1}c_{t}.

Observe
y_{2}\in B
implies that there is
r\in[m]
, such that
y_{2}+rd\in A
. Hence
s_{0}+rd\in\ell A-(k-1)A
, which contradicts (
27
).
∎

By Claim
1
, we obtain

(28)

(k+\ell-2)\overline{d}(B)+2\overline{d}(A)\leq 1.

On the other hand, let
\mathcal{N}(t)=A\setminus B+td
for every
t\in[m]
, and let

\mathscr{G}=\Big\{A,A-(k-1)x+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-1}a_{i},\mathcal{N}(t)\ \Big|\ t\in[m]\Big\}.

Claim 2
.

Elements in
\mathscr{G}
are pairwise disjoint.

Proof of Claim
2
.

Suppose there are
u,v\in[m]
,
u<v
, such that
\mathcal{N}(u)\cap\mathcal{N}(v)\neq\varnothing
. Thus we have
c\in A\setminus B
such that
c_{1}+(u-v)d\in A
, and this contradicts the assumption of
B
. Same conclusion holds if
A\cap\mathcal{N}(u)\neq\varnothing
. Observe that if
A\cap(A-(k-1)x+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-1}a_{i})\neq\varnothing
, it will contradict that
A
is
(k,\ell)
-sum-free. Finally, we assume that there are
c_{1},c_{2}\in A
,
u\in[m]
such that

c_{1}+ud=c_{2}-(k-1)x+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-1}a_{i}.

Thus,
c_{1}+x+ud+(k-2)x=c_{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-1}a_{i}
. Since
x+d\cdot[m]\subseteq A
, this contradicts
A
is
(k,\ell)
-sum-free.
∎

Thus, by Claim
2
, we get

(m+2)\overline{d}(A)-m\overline{d}(B)\leq 1.

Together with (
28
), this finishes the proof.
∎

The next lemma is a finite version of the Szemerédi Theorem
[
26
]
, and we will use it to find the arithmetic progression in Lemma
6.2
.

Lemma 6.3

(
[
26
]
)
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
and
m\in\mathbb{N}^{>0}
, there is
L=L(\varepsilon,m)>0
such that every set
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{>0}
with
\overline{d}(A)>\varepsilon
, there exist
x\in\mathbb{N}
,
d<L
, and
x+d\cdot[m]\subseteq A
.

Our final lemma says that a
(k,\ell)
-sum-free set
A
with large upper density should be periodic. This structural result can be viewed as a generalization of the Łuczak–Schoen Theorem
[
21
]
.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. Then there is
D>0
such that the following holds. Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{>0}
be a
(k,\ell)
-sum-free set, and
\overline{d}(A)>\frac{1}{k+\ell}+\varepsilon
. Then
A
is contained in a periodic
(k,\ell)
-sum-free set with period
D
.

Proof.

We pick
m\in\mathbb{N}^{>0}
such that

(29)

\frac{m+k+\ell-2}{(k+\ell)m+2(k+\ell-2)}<\frac{1}{k+\ell}+\varepsilon.

Let
L=L(\varepsilon,m)
be as in Lemma
6.3
.
Let
D=L!
. Suppose the lemma fails. Let
C\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{>0}
be a periodic set with period
D
, consists of all positive integers in every coset
a+D\cdot\mathbb{Z}
for
a\in A
. Thus
C
is not
(k,\ell)
-sum-free. This means, there are
a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell}
and
b_{1},\dots,b_{k}
in
C
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}a_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}b_{j}
. Let
P
be the “
(k,\ell)
-sum-free” part of
C
. That is,

P=C\setminus\big(\ell C-(k-1)C\big).

Set
a_{0}=b_{0}=0
. For every
u\in[k]
and
v\in[\ell]
, let

\mathcal{M}(u)=P+\sum_{j=0}^{k-u}b_{j}+(u-1)a_{1},\qquad\mathcal{N}(v)=P+\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-v}a_{i}+(v-1)b_{1}.

Let
\mathscr{F}
be the collection of all
\mathcal{M}(u)
and
\mathcal{N}(v)
.

Claim 3
.

Elements in
\mathscr{F}
are pairwise disjoint.

Proof of Claim
3
.

Observe that for every
u\in[k]
and
v\in[\ell]
,
\mathcal{M}(u)\cap\mathcal{N}(v)=\varnothing
. Otherwise there are
p_{1},p_{2}\in P
, such that

p_{1}=p_{2}+\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-v}a_{1}+(v-1)b-\sum_{j=0}^{k-u}b_{j}-(u-1)a_{1}\in\ell C-(k-1)C,

contradicts the assumption of
P
. Now, suppose
u_{1},u_{2}\in[k]
,
u_{1}<u_{2}
, such that
\mathcal{M}(u_{1})\cap\mathcal{M}(u_{2})\neq\varnothing
. The case that
\mathcal{N}(v_{1})\cap\mathcal{N}(v_{2})\neq\varnothing
can be proved in the same way. Thus, there exist
p_{1},p_{2}\in P
, such that

p_{1}+\sum_{j=k-u_{2}+1}^{k-u_{1}}b_{j}=p_{2}+(u_{2}-u_{1})a_{1}.

This implies

0=\sum_{j=1}^{k}b_{j}-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}a_{i}=p_{2}+(u_{2}-u_{1}-1)a_{1}+\sum_{j\in[0,k-u_{2}]\cup[k-u_{1}+1,k]}b_{j}-\sum_{i=2}^{\ell}a_{i}-p_{1},

hence
P\cap(\ell C-(k-1)C)\neq\varnothing
, contradiction.
∎

By Claim
3
, we obtain that
\overline{d}(P)\leq\frac{1}{k+\ell}
. This means,
\overline{d}(A\setminus P)\geq\varepsilon
. By Lemma
6.3
,
A\setminus P
contains a progression
x+d\cdot[m]
, and
d<L
. By the way we construct
P
, there are
s_{1},\dots,s_{\ell}
and
t_{1},\dots,t_{k-1}
in
C
such that

x+dm=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}s_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}t_{j}.

Hence there are
e_{1},\dots,e_{\ell}
and
f_{1},\dots,f_{k-1}
in
A
, such that for every
i\in[\ell]
and
j\in[k-1]
, we have that
e_{i}\in s_{i}+D\cdot\mathbb{Z}
, and
f_{j}\in t_{j}+D\cdot\mathbb{Z}
. Let
s=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}e_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}f_{j}
, thus
s\in\ell A-(k-1)A
, and
s\in x+D\cdot\mathbb{Z}
. Since
d\mid D
, we have
s\in x+d\cdot\mathbb{Z}
. By Lemma
6.2
, we have that

\overline{d}(A)\leq\frac{m+k+\ell-2}{(k+\ell)m+2(k+\ell-2)},

and this contradicts (
29
).
∎

Now we can prove the main result of this section.

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

Let
A/N!:=\{a\mid aN!\in A\}
. Thus
\widetilde{d}(A)>0
implies that
A/N!
contains a multiple of every natural number. In particular,
A/N!
is not contained in a periodic
(k,\ell)
-sum-free set. By Lemma
6.4
,
\overline{d}(A/N!)\leq\frac{1}{k+\ell}
.
Observe that
\widetilde{d}(A)=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\overline{d}(A/N!)
, thus
\widetilde{d}(A)\leq\frac{1}{k+\ell}
.
∎

7.
Upper bound constructions

Recall a
Følner sequence
in
(\mathbb{N},\cdot)
is any sequence
\Phi:m\mapsto\Phi_{m}
of finite non-empty subsets of
\mathbb{N}
, such that for every
a\in\mathbb{N}
,

\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{|\Phi_{m}\triangle(a\cdot\Phi_{m})|}{|\Phi_{m}|}=0.

Følner sequence has been used as some good constructions in many additive combinatorics problems, see
[
23
,
6
]
for example. In this section, we will show that the sets in Følner sequence will never have large
(k,\ell)
-sum-free subsets. In fact, we will prove the following theorem.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
\Phi=\{\Phi_{m}\}
be a Følner sequence in
(\mathbb{N},\cdot)
. Suppose there are infinitely many
m
such that
\Phi_{m}
has a
(k,\ell)
-sum-free set of size at least
\delta|\Phi_{m}|
for some positive real number
\delta\leq 1
. Then there exists a
(k,\ell)
-sum-free set
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}
such that
\widetilde{d}(A)\geq\delta
.

Theorem
1.1
(iv) follows easily from Theorem
7.1
and Theorem
6.1
.

Proof of Theorem
7.1
.

By passing to a subsequence, we may assume for every
\Phi_{m}\in\Phi
, there is a
(k,\ell)
-sum-free set
\phi_{m}\subseteq\Phi_{m}
, such that
|\phi_{m}|/|\Phi_{m}|\geq\delta
.
Let
\beta\mathbb{N}
be the collection of ultrafilters, and let
\mathscr{U}\in\beta\mathbb{N}\setminus\mathbb{N}
be a non-principal ultrafilter. Let
{}^{*}\mathbb{Z}=\prod_{m\to\mathscr{U}}\mathbb{Z}
be the ultrapower of
\mathbb{Z}
. Let
\Sigma
be the Loeb
\sigma
-algebra on
{}^{*}\mathbb{Z}
. Let
\mu_{L}
be the Loeb measure induced by
\mu_{m}
, where
\mu_{m}(X)=|X\cap\Phi_{m}|/|\Phi_{m}|
for every
X\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
. Let
\phi=\prod_{m\to\mathscr{U}}\phi_{m}
be the internal set. Then by Łoś’s Theorem,
\phi
is
(k,\ell)
-sum-free, and

\mu_{L}(\phi)=\mathrm{st}\left(\lim_{m\to\mathscr{U}}\mu_{m}(\phi_{m})\right)\geq\delta.

Claim 4
.

For every
a\in\mathbb{N}
, the map
x\mapsto ax
is
\Sigma
-measurable and
\mu_{L}
-preserving.

Proof of Claim
4
.

Note that
x\mapsto ax
sends internal sets to internal sets, thus it is
\Sigma
-measurable. For every
X\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
, since

\mu_{m}(X)-\mu_{m}(a\cdot X)=\frac{|X\cap\Phi_{m}|-|(a\cdot X)\cap\Phi_{m}|}{|\Phi_{m}|}\leq\frac{|(a\cdot\Phi_{m})\triangle\Phi_{m}|}{|\Phi_{m}|}\to 0

as
m\to\infty
, it preserves the Loeb measure
\mu_{L}
.
∎

Now we are able to apply the probabilistic argument used in the proof of Proposition
4.1
on the set
\phi
. For every
x\in{{}^{*}\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}
, let
A_{x}:=\{a\in\mathbb{N}\mid ax\in\phi\}
. Thus
A_{x}
is
(k,\ell)
-sum-free. By Claim
4
,
\widetilde{d}(A_{x})
is
\Sigma
-measurable on
x
. Suppose
x
is chosen uniformly at random with respect to the measure
\mu_{L}
. By Fatou’s Lemma,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\widetilde{d}(A_{x}))

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{N\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{|A_{x}\cap(N!\cdot[n])|}{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}(jN!x\in\phi).

By Claim
4
, we have

\mathbb{E}(\widetilde{d}(A_{x}))\geq\limsup_{N\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}(x\in\phi)=\mu_{L}(\phi)\geq\delta.

Thus by Pigeonhole Principle, there exists a set
A_{x}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
for some
x\in{{}^{*}\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}
such that
\widetilde{d}(A_{x})\geq\delta
.
∎

8.
Restricted
(k,\ell)
-sum-free sets

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.2
. Since restricted
(k,\ell)
-sum-free can be expressed by first order formula, once we prove the conclusion in Theorem
6.1
also works for restricted
(k,\ell)
-sum-free sets, Theorem
1.2
follows by using the same proof in Theorem
7.1
. More precisely, in the proof of Theorem
7.1
, if
A_{x}=\{a\in\mathbb{N}\mid ax\in\phi\}
is not restricted
(k,\ell)
-sum-free for some
x\in{{}^{*}\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}
, since the map
a\mapsto ax
is injective, we also have that
\phi
is not restricted
(k,\ell)
-sum-free.

We first consider the analogue of Lemma
6.2
for restricted
(k,\ell)
-sum-free sets. The similar argument also works here, with a different and more involved constructions of sets
\mathcal{C}(u)
,
\mathcal{D}(v)
, and
\mathcal{N}(t)
, and a more careful analysis. These new constructions will lead a slightly different structure for the large infinite restricted
(k,\ell)
-sum-free sets in Lemma
8.2
, compared to the non-restricted setting.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
k,\ell
be positive integers, and
\ell<k\leq 2\ell+1
. Suppose
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{>0}
be a restricted
(k,\ell)
-sum-free set. Define
W\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{>0}
, satisfies that for every
w\in W
, there are
\ell
distinct elements
y_{1},\dots,y_{\ell}\in A
, and
k-1
distinct elements
z_{1},\dots,z_{k-1}\in A
, such that
w\neq z_{i}
for
i\in[k-1]
, and
w=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}y_{j}-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}z_{i}
. Let
x,s,d,m
be integers, such that
s\in W
,
m>k+\ell
,
x+d\cdot[m]\subseteq A
, and
s
is in the coset
x+d\cdot\mathbb{Z}
. Then

\overline{d}(A)\leq\frac{m-2}{(k+\ell)(m-k-\ell)+2(k+\ell-2)}.

Proof.

s\in W
implies that
s\notin A
since
A
is restricted
(k,\ell)
-sum-free. We only consider the case when
s<x
. Since
A\cap W=\varnothing
, there is
s_{0}\in x+d\cdot\mathbb{Z}
such that
s_{0}\in W
and
(s_{0}+d\cdot[m])\cap W=\varnothing
. Thus there are
\ell
distinct elements
a_{1},\dots,a_{l}\in A
, and
k-1
distinct elements
b_{1},\dots,b_{k-1}\in A
,
s_{0}\neq b_{j}
for every
j\in[k-1]
, and
s_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}a_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}b_{j}
. Let
\mathcal{E}
consists of
k-1
distinct elements
e_{1},\dots,e_{k-1}\in A
, and all of them are disjoint from
\{a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\ell}
,
\{b_{j}\}_{j=1}^{k-1}
,
\{s_{0}\}
and
s_{0}+d\cdot[m]
. Let

(30)

A^{\prime}=A\setminus\Big(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell}\{a_{i}\}\cup\bigcup_{j=1}^{k-1}\{b_{j}\}\cup\mathcal{E}\cup\{s_{0}\}\cup(s_{0}+d\cdot[m])\Big).

Observe that

(31)

(s_{0}+d\cdot[m])\cap\{b_{j}\}_{j=1}^{k-1}=\varnothing,

since
b_{j}\in W
for every
j\in[k-1]
. Let
m^{\prime}=m-k-\ell
, we claim that

(32)

(s_{0}+d\cdot[m^{\prime}])\cap\{a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\ell}=\varnothing.

Otherwise, suppose there is
r\in[m^{\prime}]
such that
s_{0}+rd=a_{t}
for some
t\in[\ell]
. Then

x^{\prime}+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}b_{j}=x^{\prime}+rd+\sum_{j=1,j\neq t}^{\ell}a_{j}.

By taking
x^{\prime}\in x+d\cdot[0,m-r]
, then both
x^{\prime}
and
x^{\prime}+rd
are in
A
. Since
m-r\geq k+\ell
, there is
\alpha\in[0,m-r]
such that
x+\alpha d\notin\{b_{j}\}_{j=1}^{k-1}
, and
x+(\alpha+r)d\notin\{a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\ell}
. This contradicts that
A
is restricted
(k,\ell)
-sum-free.

Let
B=\{b\in A^{\prime}\mid(b+d\cdot[m^{\prime}])\cap A\neq\varnothing\},

and let

B^{\prime}=B\setminus\left(\Big(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell}\{a_{i}\}\cup\mathcal{E}\Big)-d\cdot[m^{\prime}]\right).

Let
c_{0}=0
,
c_{i}=a_{i}
when
i\in[\ell]
, and
c_{j}=a_{j-\ell}
when
j\in[\ell+1,k-1]
. For
u\in[k-1]
and
v\in[2,\ell]
, let

\mathcal{C}(u)=B^{\prime}+\sum_{j=1}^{k-u}b_{j}+\sum_{i=0}^{u-1}c_{i},\quad\mathcal{D}(v)=B^{\prime}+\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-v}a_{i}+\sum_{j=0}^{v-1}b_{j},

and
\mathcal{C}(k)=A^{\prime}+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}c_{i}
,
\mathcal{D}(1)=A^{\prime}+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-1}a_{i}
. Let
\mathscr{F}
consists of all
\mathcal{C}(u)
and
\mathcal{D}(v)
, then Claim
1
still holds. In fact, suppose there are
u_{1},u_{2}\in[k]
,
u_{1}<u_{2}
such that
\mathcal{C}(u_{1})\cap\mathcal{C}(u_{2})\neq\varnothing
(the case when
\mathcal{D}(v_{1})\cap\mathcal{D}(v_{2})\neq\varnothing
is simpler). Then there exist
y_{1}\in B^{\prime}
,
y_{2}\in A^{\prime}
such that

y_{1}+\sum_{j=k-u_{2}+1}^{k-u_{1}}b_{j}=y_{2}+\sum_{i=u_{1}}^{u_{2}-1}c_{i}.

Let
e_{0}=0
, and
e_{1},\dots,e_{u_{2}-u_{1}-1}\in\mathcal{E}
if
u_{2}>u_{1}+1
. If
u_{2}\leq\ell
, we have

s_{0}=y_{1}+\sum_{i\in[0,u_{1}-1]\cup[u_{2},\ell]}a_{i}+\sum_{t=0}^{u_{2}-u_{1}-1}e_{t}-y_{2}-\sum_{j\in[0,k-u_{2}]\cup[k-u_{1}+1,k-1]}b_{j}-\sum_{t=0}^{u_{2}-u_{1}-1}e_{t}.

If
u_{1}\geq\ell+1
, we get

s_{0}=y_{1}+\sum_{i\in[0,u_{1}-1-\ell]\cup[u_{2}-\ell,\ell]}a_{i}+\sum_{t=0}^{u_{2}-u_{1}-1}e_{t}-y_{2}-\sum_{j\in[0,k-u_{2}]\cup[k-u_{1}+1,k-1]}b_{j}-\sum_{t=0}^{u_{2}-u_{1}-1}e_{t}.

If
u_{1}\leq\ell
,
u_{2}\geq\ell+1
, and
u_{2}-u_{1}+1\leq\ell
,

s_{0}=y_{1}+\sum_{i\in[u_{2}-\ell,u_{1}-1]}a_{i}+\sum_{t=0}^{u_{2}-u_{1}-1}e_{t}-y_{2}-\sum_{j\in[0,k-u_{2}]\cup[k-u_{1}+1,k-1]}b_{j}-\sum_{t=0}^{u_{2}-u_{1}-1}e_{t}.

If
u_{1}\leq\ell
,
u_{2}\geq\ell+1
, and
u_{2}-u_{1}\geq\ell
. Let
e_{0}=0
,
e_{1},\dots,e_{\ell-1}\in\mathcal{E}
if
\ell>1
. Thus

s_{0}=y_{1}+\sum_{t=0}^{\ell-1}e_{t}-y_{2}-\sum_{j\in[0,k-u_{2}]\cup[k-u_{1}+1,k-1]}b_{j}-\sum_{t=0}^{\ell-1}e_{t}-\sum_{i=u_{1}}^{u_{2}-1-\ell}a_{i}.

Note that
k\leq 2\ell+1
implies
u_{2}-1-\ell\leq\ell
.

In any case, since
y_{1}\in B
, by (
30
), (
31
), and (
32
), there is
r\in[m^{\prime}]
such that
s_{0}+rd\in W
, which contradicts the assumption of
s_{0}
. Therefore,

(33)

(k+\ell-2)\overline{d}(B)+2\overline{d}(A)\leq 1,

since
\overline{d}(A^{\prime})=\overline{d}(A)
and
\overline{d}(B^{\prime})=\overline{d}(B)
.

We also modify the construction of
\mathcal{N}(t)
in a similar way. For every
t\in[m^{\prime}]
, let
\mathcal{N}(t)=A^{\prime}\setminus B+td
. Let
e_{0}=0
, and
e_{1},\dots,e_{k-2}\in\mathcal{E}
if
k\geq 3
. Let
A^{\prime\prime}=A^{\prime}\setminus(x+d\cdot[m^{\prime}])
. Define

\mathscr{G}=\Big\{\mathcal{N}(t),A^{\prime},A^{\prime\prime}+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-1}a_{i}-x-\sum_{j=0}^{k-2}e_{j}\ \Big|\ t\in[m^{\prime}]\Big\}

Then by using the similar argument, it is easy to see that Claim
2
still holds. We omit the details here. We have

(m-k-\ell+2)\overline{d}(A)-(m-k-\ell)\overline{d}(B)\leq 1,

since
\overline{d}(A^{\prime\prime})=\overline{d}(A)
. Together with (
33
), finishes the proof.
∎

Next, we consider the analogue of Lemma
6.4
for restricted
(k,\ell)
-sum-free sets. The structure here is slightly different from the
(k,\ell)
-sum-free sets.

Lemma 8.2
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and let
k,\ell
be positive integers with
\ell<k\leq 2\ell+1
. Then there is
D>0
such that the following holds. Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{>0}
be a restricted
(k,\ell)
-sum-free set, and
\overline{d}(A)>\frac{1}{k+\ell}+\varepsilon
. Then after removing at most
D(2k+\ell)
elements from
A
, it is contained in a periodic restricted
(k,\ell)
-sum-free set with period
D
.

Proof.

We pick
m>k+\ell
such that

(34)

\frac{m-2}{(k+\ell)(m-k-\ell)+2(k+\ell-2)}<\frac{1}{k+\ell}+\varepsilon.

Let
L=L(\varepsilon,m)
be as in Lemma
6.3
, and let
D=L!
. We consider the partition of
\mathbb{N}
into cosets:

\mathbb{N}=\bigcup_{x\in[D]}x+D\cdot\mathbb{N}.

For every
x\in[D]
, let
\mathbb{N}_{x}=x+D\cdot\mathbb{N}
, and
A_{x}=A\cap\mathbb{N}_{x}
. Let
A^{\prime}
be a subset of
A
, obtained by removing
A_{x}
from
A
when
|A_{x}|<2k+\ell
. Hence
\overline{d}(A^{\prime})=\overline{d}(A)
. Next, we are going to show that
A^{\prime}
is contained in a periodic restricted
(k,\ell)
-sum-free set with period
D
. Suppose this is not the case. Let

C=\Big(\bigcup_{a\in A^{\prime}}a+D\cdot\mathbb{Z}\Big)\cap\mathbb{N}^{>0}.

Thus
C
is not restricted
(k,\ell)
-sum-free. This means, there are
\ell
distinct elements
a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell}\in C
and
k
distinct elements
b_{1},\dots,b_{k}\in C
, such that
\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}a_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}b_{j}
. Let
P
be the “
(k,\ell)
-sum-free” part of
C
, that for every
w\in P
, every
k-1
distinct elements
y_{1},\dots,y_{k-1}\in C\setminus\{w\}
, and every
\ell
distinct elements
z_{1},\dots,z_{\ell}\in C
, we have
w+\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}y_{i}\neq\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}z_{j}
. Let
e_{0}=0
, and let
\mathcal{E}
consists of
k-1
distinct elements
e_{1},\dots,e_{k-1}\in C
, such that
\mathcal{E}
is disjoint from
\{a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\ell}
and
\{b_{j}\}_{j=1}^{k}
.

P^{\prime}=P\setminus\Big(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell}\{a_{i}\}\cup\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}\{b_{j}\}\cup\mathcal{E}\Big).

Set
a_{0}=b_{0}=c_{0}=0
. Let
c_{t}=a_{t}
when
t\in[\ell]
, and
c_{t}=a_{t-\ell}
when
t\in[\ell+1,k-1]
. For every
u\in[k]
and
v\in[\ell]
, let

\mathcal{M}(u)=P^{\prime}+\sum_{j=0}^{k-u}b_{j}+\sum_{t=0}^{u-1}c_{t},\qquad\mathcal{N}(v)=P^{\prime}+\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-v}a_{i}+\sum_{t=0}^{v-1}b_{t}.

Let
\mathscr{F}
be the collection of all
\mathcal{M}(u)
and
\mathcal{N}(v)
. Then elements in
\mathscr{F}
are pairwise disjoint. Otherwise, suppose there are
u_{1},u_{2}\in[k]
,
u_{1}<u_{2}
such that
\mathcal{M}(u_{1})\cap\mathcal{M}(u_{2})\neq\varnothing
(the case when
\mathcal{N}(v_{1})\cap\mathcal{N}(v_{2})\neq\varnothing
is simpler). Thus, there are
y_{1},y_{2}\in P^{\prime}
, such that

y_{1}+\sum_{k-u_{2}+1}^{k-u_{1}}b_{j}=y_{2}+\sum_{t=u_{1}}^{u_{2}-1}c_{t}.

Let
e_{1},\dots,e_{u_{2}-u_{1}-1}\in\mathcal{E}
if
u_{2}>u_{1}+1
. If
u_{2}\leq\ell
, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}a_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}b_{j}

\displaystyle=y_{1}+\sum_{i\in[0,u_{1}-1]\cup[u_{2},\ell]}a_{i}+\sum_{t=0}^{u_{2}-u_{1}-1}e_{t}-y_{2}-\sum_{j\in[0,k-u_{2}]\cup[k-u_{1}+1,k]}b_{j}-\sum_{t=0}^{u_{2}-u_{1}-1}e_{t}.

If
u_{1}\geq\ell+1
, we have

0=y_{1}+\sum_{i\in[0,u_{1}-1-\ell]\cup[u_{2}-\ell,\ell]}a_{i}+\sum_{t=0}^{u_{2}-u_{1}-1}e_{t}-y_{2}-\sum_{j\in[0,k-u_{2}]\cup[k-u_{1}+1,k]}b_{j}-\sum_{t=0}^{u_{2}-u_{1}-1}e_{t}.

If
u_{1}\leq\ell
,
u_{2}\geq\ell+1
, and
\ell\geq u_{2}-u_{1}
, we get

0=y_{1}+\sum_{i=u_{2}-\ell}^{u_{1}-1}a_{i}+\sum_{t=0}^{u_{2}-u_{1}-1}e_{t}-y_{2}-\sum_{j\in[0,k-u_{2}]\cup[k-u_{1}+1,k]}b_{j}-\sum_{t=0}^{u_{2}-u_{1}-1}e_{t}.

If
u_{1}\leq\ell
,
u_{2}\geq\ell+1
, and
\ell<u_{2}-u_{1}
. Let
e_{1},\dots,e_{\ell-1}\in\mathcal{E}
if
\ell>1
, we get

0=y_{1}+\sum_{t=0}^{\ell-1}e_{t}-y_{2}-\sum_{j\in[0,k-u_{2}]\cup[k-u_{1}+1,k]}b_{j}-\sum_{i=u_{1}}^{u_{2}-1-\ell}a_{i}-\sum_{t=0}^{\ell-1}e_{t}.

In any case, we get a contradiction with the assumption of
P^{\prime}
and the fact that
y_{2}\in P^{\prime}
.
Therefore,

\overline{d}(P)\leq\frac{1}{k+\ell},

since
\overline{d}(P^{\prime})=\overline{d}(P)
. This means,
\overline{d}(A^{\prime}\setminus P)\geq\varepsilon
. By Lemma
6.3
,
A^{\prime}\setminus P
contains a progression
x+d\cdot[m]
, and
d<L
. By the way we construct
P
, there are
\ell
distinct elements
s_{1},\dots,s_{\ell}\in C
and
k-1
distinct elements
t_{1},\dots,t_{k-1}
in
C\setminus\{x+m\}
such that

x+m=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}s_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}t_{j}.

By the way we construct
A^{\prime}
, for every
r\in[D]
, if
|A^{\prime}\cap\mathbb{N}_{r}|>0
, then
|A^{\prime}\cap\mathbb{N}_{r}|\geq 2k+\ell
. Thus, there are
\ell
distinct elements
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{\ell}\in A^{\prime}
and
k-1
distinct elements
\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{k-1}\in A^{\prime}
, such that for every
i\in[\ell]
and
j\in[k-1]
, we have that
\alpha_{i}\in s_{i}+D\cdot\mathbb{Z}
, and
\beta_{j}\in t_{j}+D\cdot\mathbb{Z}
. Let
s=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\alpha_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\beta_{j}
. Note that
|A^{\prime}\cap\mathbb{N}_{r}|\geq 2k+\ell
also implies that there is
r^{\prime}\in[\ell]
, and
M\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{>0}
,
|M|\geq k
, such that

\alpha_{r^{\prime}}+D\cdot M\subseteq A^{\prime},\quad(\alpha_{r^{\prime}}+D\cdot M)\cap\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell}\{\alpha_{i}\}=\varnothing.

Thus if
s\cap\{\beta_{j}\}_{j=1}^{k-1}\neq\varnothing
, then by changing
\alpha_{r^{\prime}}
by
\alpha_{r^{\prime}}+nD
for some
n\in M
, one can make
s+nD\cap\{\beta_{j}\}_{j=1}^{k-1}=\varnothing
. Since
d\mid D
, we have
s\in x+d\cdot\mathbb{Z}
. By Lemma
6.2
, we have that

\overline{d}(A)\leq\frac{m-2}{(k+\ell)(m-k-\ell)+2(k+\ell-2)},

and this contradicts (
34
).
∎

Let
A
be a restricted
(k,\ell)
-sum-free set, and let
A^{\prime}
be a subset of
A
obtained by removing finitely many elements from
A
. Observe that, if
A^{\prime}
is contained in a periodic restricted
(k,\ell)
-sum-free set, then
A
cannot contain a multiple of every natural number. Thus, using the same proof in Theorem
6.1
, we conclude that
\widetilde{d}(A)\leq\frac{1}{k+\ell}
if
A
is restricted
(k,\ell)
-sum-free.

9.
Concluding Remarks

In this paper, we first study
\mathscr{M}_{(k,\ell)}(N)
. In particular, we prove that Conjecture
2
is true for infinitely many
(k,\ell)
. While solving Conjecture
2
might not be a realistic target at the moment, the following conjecture for the case when
k-\ell\geq 2
might be feasible. This is because in this case, Lemma
4.2
implies that we have two different asymmetric maximal
(k,\ell)
-sum-free open sets in
\mathbb{T}
, and the technique developed in this paper might be useful.

Conjecture 3
.

Let
k,\ell
be positive integers and
k\geq\ell+2
. Then there is a function
\omega(N)\to\infty
as
N\to\infty
, such that

\mathscr{M}_{(k,\ell)}(N)\geq\frac{N}{k+\ell}+\omega(N).

We also study
\widehat{\mathscr{M}}_{(k,\ell)}(N)
in Theorem
1.2
. As we can see in the proofs in Section 8, when
k>2\ell+1
, the current strategy failed to obtain disjoint sets
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
in the proof of Lemma
8.1
, as well as disjoint sets
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
in the proof of Lemma
8.2
. Although we think it is very likely that the conclusion in Theorem
1.2
holds for every
k
and
\ell
, the case
k>2\ell+1
may require some new ingredients.

Conjecture 4
.

For every positive integers
k,\ell
with
k>2\ell+1
,

\widehat{\mathscr{M}}_{(k,\ell)}(N)=\Big(\frac{1}{k+\ell}+o(1)\Big)N.

A
(k,\ell)
-sum-free set is a set forbidding a linear equation
\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}x_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}y_{j}
. Another interesting direction is to consider the analogue problem on sets forbidding a system of linear equations. One of the most interesting problems along this line might be forbidding the projective cubes. Given a multiset
S=\{s_{1},\dots,s_{d}\}
, a
d
-dimensional projective cube
generated by
S
is

\square^{d}(S):=\Big\{\sum_{i\in I}s_{i}\ \Big|\ \varnothing\neq I\subseteq[d]\Big\}.

A set is
\square^{d}
-free
if it does not contain any
d
-dimensional projective cubes as its subsets.
Extremal properties of projective cubes have a vast literature, see e.g.
[
1
,
12
,
14
,
20
]
. The problem on forbidding
d
-dimensional projective cubes can be viewed as a generalization of sum-free sets in another direction, since a sum-free set is also a
\square^{2}
-free set. Thus, the following problem is worthwhile to pursue.

Question 5
.

Let
d\geq 3
be an integer. Define

\mathscr{M}_{\square^{d}}(N):=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{>0}\\
|A|=N\end{subarray}}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}B\subseteq A\\
B\text{ \emph{is} }\square^{d}\text{\emph{-free}}\end{subarray}}|B|.

Determine
\mathscr{M}_{\square^{d}}(N)
.

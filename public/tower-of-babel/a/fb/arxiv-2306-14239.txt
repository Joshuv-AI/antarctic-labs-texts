Asymptotic analysis in multivariate worst case approximation with Gaussian kernels
A. A. Khartov 1,2,3 , I. A. Limar 2,3

Abstract
We consider a problem of approximation of d d -variate functions defined on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} which belong to the Hilbert space with tensor product-type reproducing Gaussian kernel with constant shape parameter. Within worst case setting, we investigate the growth of the information complexity as d → ∞ d\to\infty . The asymptotics are obtained for the case of fixed error threshold and for the case when it goes to zero as d → ∞ d\to\infty .

1
Introduction and problem setting

We consider multivariate approximation problems
in the worst case setting for functions of
d
variables from the Hilbert space with the squared-exponential
reproducing kernel as
d\to\infty
, when the error threshold
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
is fixed or
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{d}\to 0
.

For every
d\in\mathbb{N}
(set of positive integers) we consider the Hilbert space
H_{d,\gamma}
with the following squared-exponential reproducing kernel

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{d}(t,s)=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\exp\left\{-\gamma^{2}(t_{j}-s_{j})^{2}\right\},

(1)

where
t=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{d})
and
s=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{d})
are from
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
\mathbb{R}
is the set of real numbers),
\gamma>0
is a shape parameter. The space
H_{d,\gamma}
is well studied and it is widely used in numerical computations, statistical leaning and
engineering, see
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
19
]
,
[
20
]
.

We consider the multivariate approximation problem
\mathrm{APP}_{d}:H_{d,\gamma}\to L_{2,d}
with

\mathrm{APP}_{d}f=f
for all
f\in H_{d,\gamma}
, where
L_{2,d}
is the space of functions that have the finite norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L_{2,d}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Biggl(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f^{2}(x)\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}\dfrac{e^{-x_{j}^{2}}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\mathrm{d}x\Biggr)^{1/2},

which the space is equipped with. We set
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})
in the integral.
We approximate
\mathrm{APP}_{d}f
by algorithms that use finitely many, say
n\in\mathbb{N}
, values of linear functionals. It is well known, see
[
11
]
, that we can restrict ourselves to
n
-rank linear algorithms from the following class

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{d,n}=\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}\,\ell_{k}f:a_{k}\in L_{2,d},\ell_{k}\in H_{d,\gamma}^{*}\right\}.

The worst case error of the algorithm
A_{d,n}\in\mathcal{A}_{d,n}
is defined as

\displaystyle e(A_{d,n})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sup\limits_{\|f\|_{H_{d,\gamma}}\leqslant 1}\|\mathrm{APP}_{d}f-A_{d,n}f\|_{L_{2,d}},

where
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{H_{d,\gamma}}
is the norm of
H_{d,\gamma}
.
The
n^{th}
minimal worst case error has the following form

\displaystyle e(d,n)=\inf_{A_{d,n}\in\mathcal{A}_{d,n}}e(A_{d,n}).

We also deal with the initial error, i.e we consider the value

\displaystyle e(d,0)=\sup\limits_{\|f\|_{H_{d,\gamma}}\leqslant 1}\|f\|_{L_{2,d}}=\|\mathrm{APP}_{d}\|,

which can be thought as the error of the identical zero algorithm. We define the
worst case information complexity

n(d,\varepsilon)
for normalized criterion (
information complexity
for short) as

\displaystyle n(d,\varepsilon)=\min\bigl\{n\in\mathbb{N}:e(d,n)\leqslant\varepsilon e(d,0)\bigr\},\quad\varepsilon\in(0,1),\quad d\in\mathbb{N}.

(2)

The multivariate worst case setting and its modifications are comprehensively described in
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
.

The quantity
n(d,\varepsilon)
admits a representation via eigenvalues of the integral operator
I_{d}:H_{d,\gamma}\to H_{d,\gamma}
that acts as follows

\displaystyle(I_{d}f)(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(s)\prod_{j=1}^{d}\exp\left\{-\gamma^{2}(t_{j}-s_{j})^{2}\right\}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}\dfrac{e^{-s_{j}^{2}}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\,\mathrm{d}s,\quad f\in H_{d,\gamma},

where
t=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{d})
and
s=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{d})
are from
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Let
(\lambda_{d,m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
be the sequence of eigenvalues of
I_{d}
that is assumed to be ranked in the non-increasing order. Let
(\psi_{d,m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
be the corresponding sequence of
orthonormal eigenfunctions of
I_{d}
, i.e.
(I_{d}\psi_{d,m})(t)=\lambda_{d,m}\psi_{d,m}(t)
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
. It is well known (see
[
11
]
) that the following
n
-rank algorithm
S_{d,n}

\displaystyle S_{d,n}f=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(f,\psi_{d,m})_{H_{d,\gamma}}\psi_{d,m},\quad f\in H_{d,\gamma},

has the minimal worst case error
e(d,n)=\sqrt{\lambda_{d,n+1}}
. The initial error is also known:
e(d,0)=\sqrt{\lambda_{d,1}}
. Thus, representation (
2
) is written as the follows

\displaystyle n(d,\varepsilon)=\min\bigl\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\lambda_{d,n+1}\leqslant\varepsilon^{2}\lambda_{d,1}\bigr\},\quad\varepsilon\in(0,1),\quad d\in\mathbb{N}.

(3)

It is a nice fact that
(\lambda_{d,m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(\psi_{d,m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
are known for
I_{d}
with kernel (
1
). For
d=1
we introduce the following convenient notation:
\lambda_{k}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\lambda_{1,k}
and
\psi_{k}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\psi_{1,k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
. So for this case we have (see
[
13
]
p. 17 and
[
15
]
p. 97):

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}=(1-\omega)\,\omega^{k-1},\quad k\in\mathbb{N},\quad\text{with}\quad\omega\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\dfrac{2\gamma^{2}}{1+2\gamma^{2}+\sqrt{1+4\gamma^{2}}},

(4)

and

\displaystyle\psi_{k}(t)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sqrt{\tfrac{(1+4\gamma^{2})^{1/4}}{2^{k-1}(k-1)!}}\cdot\exp\Bigl\{-\tfrac{2\gamma^{2}t^{2}}{1+\sqrt{1+4\gamma^{2}}}\,\Bigr\}\cdot H_{k-1}\bigl((1+4\gamma^{2})^{1/4}t\bigr),\quad t\in\mathbb{R},\quad k\in\mathbb{N},

where
H_{k-1}
is the standard Chebyshev–Hermite polynomial of the degree
k-1
, see
[
18
]
, i.e.

\displaystyle H_{k-1}(x)=(-1)^{k-1}e^{x^{2}}\cdot\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^{k-1}}{\mathrm{d}x^{k-1}}\,e^{-x^{2}},\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\quad k\in\mathbb{N}.

For the general case,
d\in\mathbb{N}
, the sequence
(\lambda_{d,m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
is an array of the numbers

\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{d}\lambda_{k_{j}},\quad k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\in\mathbb{N},

that is indexed in the non-increasing order, and
(\psi_{d,m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
is correspondingly indexed sequence of the functions

\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{d}\psi_{k_{j}}(t_{j}),\quad k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\in\mathbb{N}.

Observe that
\lambda_{k}
and
\lambda_{d,m}
belong to the interval
(0,1)
for all
k,d,m\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover, it is not difficult to show that

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\lambda_{k}=1
and
\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\lambda_{d,m}=1
for every
d\in\mathbb{N}
.

For a given shape parameter
\gamma>0
we consider the information complexity
n(d,\varepsilon)
as a function depending on two variables

d\in\mathbb{N}
,
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, and we are interested in the character of growth of
n(d,\varepsilon)
for arbitrarily large
d
and small
\varepsilon
. There are a lot of results for this and more general problems concerning tractability (see
[
4
]
and
[
17
]
), where necessary and sufficient
conditions for the special upper bounds of
n(d,\varepsilon)
were obtained in terms of input parameters. Some of these results we will recall in the next section.
There exist results about tractabilty within average case setting (see
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
, and
[
8
]
). This setting is also studied in
[
9
]
with asymptotic approach that more accurately detect behaviour of the quantity
n(d,\varepsilon)
as
d\to\infty
. We are not aware any results with asymptotic approach for the worst case setting. In this paper, we do the first step in this direction. Namely, we will investigate asymptotics of
n(d,\varepsilon)
as
d\to\infty
for arbitrarily small fixed
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and for the case
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{d}\to 0
as
d\to\infty
. The latter setting seems to be new.

We will use the following notation in the paper. The set of non-negative integers will be denoted by
\mathbb{N}_{0}
. The indicator function
\mathds{1}(A)
equals one if
A
is true and it is zero if
A
is false. For a finite set
B
we denote by
\#B
the number of elements in the set
B
. By

distribution function
we mean a non-decreasing function
F
defined on
\mathbb{R}
that is right-continuous,
\lim\limits_{x\to+\infty}F(x)=1
, and

\lim\limits_{x\to-\infty}F(x)=0
. We denote by
\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}(C)
the probability of an event
C
, and we donote by
\mathrm{\mathbf{E}}Y
, and
\mathrm{\mathbf{Var}}Y
the expectation and the variance of a random variable
Y
, respectively.

2
Main results

In this section we formulate the main results of the paper. The proofs are provided in section 4, the necessary auxiliary tools are presented in section 3.

We consider the information complexity
n(d,\varepsilon)
defined in the previous section. We first recall existing results for this quatity that is corresponded to the kernel (
1
) with any fixed
\gamma>0
. From
[
4
]
it is known (see also review in
[
17
]
) that
quasi-polynomial tractability
(QPT) holds for
n(d,\varepsilon)
, i.e. there exist constants
C>0
and
\tau>0
such that

\displaystyle n(d,\varepsilon)\leqslant C\exp\bigl\{\tau(1+\ln d)(1+\ln\varepsilon^{-1})\bigr\},\quad\text{for all}\quad\varepsilon\in(0,1),\,\,d\in\mathbb{N}.

(5)

Here the constant
\tau
was determined as
\tau=2c_{\omega}
, where

\displaystyle c_{\omega}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{1}{|\ln\omega|}.

(6)

The constant
c_{\omega}
will be used here and throughout the paper.

Recall that there are two natural type of tractability that would be possible for
n(d,\varepsilon)
:

•

strong polynomial tractability
(SPT) holds iff there exist constant
C>0
and
\tau>0
such that

\displaystyle n(d,\varepsilon)\leqslant C\varepsilon^{-\tau},\quad\text{for all}\quad\varepsilon\in(0,1),\,\,d\in\mathbb{N};

•

polynomial tractability
(PT) holds iff there exist constants
C>0
,
\tau_{1}>0
and
\tau_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle n(d,\varepsilon)\leqslant C\varepsilon^{-\tau_{1}}d^{\tau_{2}},\quad\text{for all}\quad\varepsilon\in(0,1),\,\,d\in\mathbb{N}.

However, as it was shown in
[
4
]
, SPT and PT do not hold for the quantity
n(d,\varepsilon)
for the considered case. Morever,
exponential convergence and quasi-polynomial tractability
(EC-QPT) does not hold (see
[
17
]
), where EC-QPT, by its definition, holds iff there exist constants
C>0
and
\tau>0
such that

\displaystyle n(d,\varepsilon)\leqslant C\exp\bigl\{\tau(1+\ln d)(1+\ln(1+\ln\varepsilon^{-1}))\bigr\},\quad\varepsilon\in(0,1),\quad d\in\mathbb{N}.

We now formulate our asymptotical results that complement the estimate (
5
). We first consider the case when
d\to\infty
and
\varepsilon
is fixed.

Theorem 1

For any fixed
\varepsilon\in(0,1)

\displaystyle n(d,\varepsilon)=\dfrac{d^{N_{\omega}(\varepsilon)}}{N_{\omega}(\varepsilon)!}+O\bigl(d^{N_{\omega}(\varepsilon)-1}\bigr),\quad d\to\infty,

(7)

where
N_{\omega}(\varepsilon)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\max\bigl\{m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:m<c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|\bigr\}
.

We see that the main factor
d^{N_{\omega}(\varepsilon)}
in (
7
) is approximately equal to
\exp\bigl\{2c_{\omega}\cdot\ln d\cdot|\ln\varepsilon|\bigr\}
. This is consistent with (
5
) (with
\tau=2c_{\omega}
) for large
d
and small
\varepsilon
.

We turn to the case when
d\to\infty
and
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{d}\to 0
. Without loss of generality concerning the rate of convergence of
(\varepsilon_{d})_{d\in\mathbb{N}}
, we obtain the logarithmic asymptotics of
n(d,\varepsilon_{d})
as
d\to\infty
.

Theorem 2

Let
(\varepsilon_{d})_{d\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence from
(0,1)
such that
\varepsilon_{d}\to 0
,
d\to\infty
. Then

\displaystyle\ln n(d,\varepsilon_{d})=d\cdot\ln\Bigl(1+\tfrac{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}{d}\Bigr)+c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|\cdot\ln\Bigl(1+\tfrac{d}{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}\Bigr)+R_{d},\quad d\to\infty.

(8)

where
(R_{d})_{d\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence such that

\displaystyle\dfrac{R_{d}}{|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|\cdot\ln\Bigl(1+\tfrac{d}{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}\Bigr)}\to 0,\quad d\to\infty.

For large
d
the asymptotics (
8
) is consistent with (
5
) too. Indeed, if
d/|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|\to\infty
as
d\to\infty
, then for large
d
the quantity
\ln n(d,\varepsilon_{d})
is approximately equal to

\displaystyle c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|+c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|\ln d.

Here the second term is the main as in (
5
). If
|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|/d\to\infty
as
d\to\infty
, then
\ln n(d,\varepsilon_{d})
is approximately equal to

\displaystyle d\ln\bigl(c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|\bigr)+d

for sufficiently large
d
. So the first term is the main and for it we have

\displaystyle\dfrac{d\ln\bigl(c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|\bigr)}{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|\ln d}=\dfrac{d}{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}\cdot\dfrac{\ln\Bigl(\tfrac{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}{d}\Bigr)+\ln d}{\ln d}=\dfrac{\ln\Bigl(\tfrac{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}{d}\Bigr)}{\tfrac{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}{d}}\cdot\dfrac{1}{\ln d}+\dfrac{d}{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}.

It is seen that, by the assumption, this sequence goes to zero as
d\to\infty
. In particular,
d\ln\bigl(c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|\bigr)
is bounded by
c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|\ln d
for all sufficiently large
d
. Hence the same estimate holds for
\ln n(d,\varepsilon_{d})
similarly to (
5
) with
\tau=2c_{\omega}
.

3
Auxiliary results

In this section, we formulate and prove some statements that are needed to obtain the main results.

Lemma 1

Let
(U_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of independent random variables with the following distribution:

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}\bigl(U_{j}=k|\ln\omega|\bigr)=(1-\omega)\,\omega^{k},\quad k\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\quad j\in\mathbb{N}.

(9)

Then for any
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
the quantity
n(d,\varepsilon)
admits the following representation

\displaystyle n(d,\varepsilon)=e^{d|\ln(1-\omega)|}\,\mathrm{\mathbf{E}}\biggl[\exp\Bigl\{\sum_{j=1}^{d}U_{j}\Bigr\}\mathds{1}\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{d}U_{j}<|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|\Bigr)\biggr].

Proof of Lemma
1
.
Let us fix any
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Using (
3
), we have

\displaystyle n(d,\varepsilon)=\min\bigl\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\lambda_{d,n+1}\leqslant\varepsilon^{2}\lambda_{d,1}\bigr\}=\#\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\lambda_{d,n+1}>\varepsilon^{2}\lambda_{d,1}\}.

According to the known product structure of
\lambda_{d,m}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and due to the sharp expressions (
4
) for
\lambda_{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle n(d,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\#\Big\{(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}:\prod_{j=1}^{d}\lambda_{k_{j}}>\varepsilon^{2}\lambda_{d,1}\Big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\#\Big\{(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}:\prod_{j=1}^{d}\bigl((1-\omega)\,\omega^{k_{j}-1}\bigr)>\varepsilon^{2}(1-\omega)^{d}\Big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\#\Big\{(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}:\prod_{j=1}^{d}\omega^{k_{j}}>\varepsilon^{2}\Big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\#\Big\{(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}:\sum_{j=1}^{d}k_{j}|\ln\omega|<|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|\Big\}.

We write

\displaystyle n(d,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}\mathds{1}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{d}k_{j}|\ln\omega|<|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{d|\ln(1-\omega)|}\sum_{(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}\biggl[\exp\Bigl\{\sum_{j=1}^{d}k_{j}|\ln\omega|\Bigr\}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\bigl((1-\omega)\,\omega^{k_{j}}\bigr)\mathds{1}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{d}k_{j}|\ln\omega|<|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|\Bigr)\biggr].

By definition (
9
), we obtain

\displaystyle n(d,\varepsilon)=e^{d|\ln(1-\omega)|}\mathrm{\mathbf{E}}\biggl[\exp\Big\{\sum_{j=1}^{d}U_{j}\Big\}\mathds{1}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{d}U_{j}<|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|\Big)\biggr],

as required.
\Box

Let us define the moment generating function for random variables
U_{j}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
:

M(\nu)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathrm{\mathbf{E}}\exp\{\nu U_{j}\}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}e^{\nu|\ln\omega|k}(1-\omega)\,\omega^{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(1-\omega)\,\omega^{(1-\nu)k},\quad\nu\in\mathbb{R},

The next lemma states some properties of this function and its derivatives.

Lemma 2

1)
For any
\nu<1
the quantities
M(\nu)
,
M^{\prime}(\nu)
, and
M^{\prime\prime}(\nu)
are finite and admit representations

\displaystyle M(\nu)=\frac{1-\omega}{1-\omega^{1-\nu}},\qquad M^{\prime}(\nu)=\frac{(1-\omega)|\ln\omega|\,\omega^{1-\nu}}{(1-\omega^{1-\nu})^{2}},\qquad M^{\prime\prime}(\nu)=(1-\omega)|\ln\omega|^{2}\cdot\dfrac{(1+\omega^{1-\nu})\,\omega^{1-\nu}}{(1-\omega^{1-\nu})^{3}}.

2)
For any
\alpha>0
the equation
\alpha=M^{\prime}(\nu)/M(\nu)
has the solution
\nu_{\alpha}<1
expressed by the formula

\displaystyle\nu_{\alpha}=1-c_{\omega}\ln\bigl(1+\tfrac{1}{c_{\omega}\alpha}\bigr),

(10)

and then

\displaystyle M(\nu_{\alpha})=(1-\omega)(1+c_{\omega}\alpha),\qquad\dfrac{M^{\prime}(\nu_{\alpha})}{M(\nu_{\alpha})}=\alpha,\qquad\dfrac{M^{\prime\prime}(\nu_{\alpha})}{M(\nu_{\alpha})}-\biggl(\dfrac{M^{\prime}(\nu_{\alpha})}{M(\nu_{\alpha})}\biggr)^{2}=|\ln\omega|\cdot(1+c_{\omega}\alpha)\cdot\alpha.

(11)

Proof of Lemma
2
.

1)
Since
\omega\in(0,1)
, we get

\displaystyle M(\nu)=(1-\omega)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\omega^{(1-\nu)k}=\dfrac{1-\omega}{1-\omega^{1-\nu}}<\infty,\quad\nu<1.

This yields

\displaystyle M^{\prime}(\nu)=\biggl(\dfrac{1-\omega}{1-\omega^{1-\nu}}\biggr)^{\prime}=\dfrac{(1-\omega)|\ln\omega|\,\omega^{1-\nu}}{(1-\omega^{1-\nu})^{2}}<\infty,\quad\nu<1.

Next, we have

\displaystyle M^{\prime\prime}(\nu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\biggl(\dfrac{(1-\omega)|\ln\omega|\,\omega^{1-\nu}}{(1-\omega^{1-\nu})^{2}}\biggr)^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\omega)|\ln\omega|\cdot\dfrac{|\ln\omega|\,\omega^{1-\nu}\cdot(1-\omega^{1-\nu})^{2}+\omega^{1-\nu}\cdot 2(1-\omega^{1-\nu})\,\omega^{1-\nu}\,|\ln\omega|\,}{(1-\omega^{1-\nu})^{4}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\omega)|\ln\omega|\cdot\dfrac{|\ln\omega|\,\omega^{1-\nu}(1-\omega^{1-\nu})+2|\ln\omega|\,\omega^{2(1-\nu)}}{(1-\omega^{1-\nu})^{3}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\omega)|\ln\omega|^{2}\cdot\dfrac{(1+\omega^{1-\nu})\,\omega^{1-\nu}}{(1-\omega^{1-\nu})^{3}}<\infty,\quad\nu<1.

2)
We fix any
\alpha>0
and consider the equation
\alpha=M^{\prime}(\nu)/M(\nu)
with
\nu<1
. On account of the obtained formulas, we have

\displaystyle\dfrac{M^{\prime}(\nu)}{M(\nu)}=\frac{|\ln\omega|\cdot\omega^{1-\nu}}{1-\omega^{1-\nu}},\quad\nu<1.

So the equation gets the form

\displaystyle\alpha=\dfrac{|\ln\omega|\cdot\omega^{1-\nu}}{1-\omega^{1-\nu}},

that is equivalent to

\displaystyle\omega^{1-\nu}=\frac{\alpha}{|\ln\omega|+\alpha}\quad\text{and}\quad(1-\nu)|\ln\omega|=\ln\frac{|\ln\omega|+\alpha}{\alpha}.

The last logarithm equals
\ln\bigl(1+\tfrac{1}{c_{\omega}\alpha}\bigr)
. So we easily get the unique solution (
10
) that is less than
1
. Thus the second equality in (
11
) is obvious. We now check the first:

\displaystyle M(\nu_{\alpha})=\dfrac{1-\omega}{1-\omega^{1-\nu_{\alpha}}}=\dfrac{1-\omega}{1-\tfrac{\alpha}{|\ln\omega|+\alpha}}=\dfrac{1-\omega}{\tfrac{|\ln\omega|}{|\ln\omega|+\alpha}}=(1-\omega)\Bigl(1+\tfrac{\alpha}{|\ln\omega|}\Bigr)=(1-\omega)(1+c_{\omega}\alpha).

Next, we prove the third equality in (
11
). By the above, we have

\displaystyle\dfrac{M^{\prime\prime}(\nu_{\alpha})}{M(\nu_{\alpha})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{|\ln\omega|\,(1+\omega^{1-\nu_{\alpha}})}{1-\omega^{1-\nu_{\alpha}}}\cdot\dfrac{M^{\prime}(\nu_{\alpha})}{M(\nu_{\alpha})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\ln\omega|\cdot\Bigl(1+\frac{\alpha}{|\ln\omega|+\alpha}\Bigr)\cdot(1+c_{\omega}\alpha)\cdot\alpha

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigl(|\ln\omega|+\frac{\alpha}{1+c_{\omega}\alpha}\Bigr)\cdot(1+c_{\omega}\alpha)\cdot\alpha

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\ln\omega|\cdot(1+c_{\omega}\alpha)\cdot\alpha+\alpha^{2}.

Thus we obtain

\displaystyle\dfrac{M^{\prime\prime}(\nu_{\alpha})}{M(\nu_{\alpha})}-\biggl(\dfrac{M^{\prime}(\nu_{\alpha})}{M(\nu_{\alpha})}\biggr)^{2}=\dfrac{M^{\prime\prime}(\nu_{\alpha})}{M(\nu_{\alpha})}-\alpha^{2}=|\ln\omega|\cdot(1+c_{\omega}\alpha)\cdot\alpha.\quad\Box

Lemma 3

Let for every
\nu<1

(U_{j}^{(\nu)})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of independent random variables with the following distribution:

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}\bigl(U^{(\nu)}_{j}=k|\ln\omega|\bigr)=\dfrac{(1-\omega)\,\omega^{(1-\nu)k}}{M(\nu)},\quad k\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\quad j\in\mathbb{N}.

Then for any
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, and
\nu<1
the quantity
n(d,\varepsilon)
admits the following representation

\displaystyle n(d,\varepsilon)=e^{d|\ln(1-\omega)|}M(\nu)^{d}\,\mathrm{\mathbf{E}}\biggl[\exp\Big\{(1-\nu)\sum_{j=1}^{d}U_{j}^{(\nu)}\Big\}\mathds{1}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{d}U_{j}^{(\nu)}<|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|\Big)\biggr].

(12)

Proof of Lemma
3
.
Let us fix arbitrary
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, and
\nu<1
. Observe that

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}\bigl(U^{(\nu)}_{j}=k|\ln\omega|\bigr)=\dfrac{e^{k|\ln\omega|\nu}}{M(\nu)}\,(1-\omega)\,\omega^{k}=\dfrac{e^{k|\ln\omega|\nu}}{M(\nu)}\,\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}\bigl(U_{j}=k|\ln\omega|\bigr),\quad k\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\quad j\in\mathbb{N}.

Hence, due to the independence, for any
k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}\bigl(U^{(\nu)}_{1}=k_{1}|\ln\omega|,\ldots,U^{(\nu)}_{d}=k_{d}|\ln\omega|\bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}\bigl(U^{(\nu)}_{1}=k_{1}|\ln\omega|\bigr)\cdot\ldots\cdot\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}\bigl(U^{(\nu)}_{d}=k_{d}|\ln\omega|\bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{e^{k_{1}|\ln\omega|\nu}}{M(\nu)}\,\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}\bigl(U_{1}=k_{1}|\ln\omega|\bigr)\cdot\ldots\cdot\dfrac{e^{k_{d}|\ln\omega|\nu}}{M(\nu)}\,\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}\bigl(U_{d}=k_{d}|\ln\omega|\bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{e^{(k_{1}+\ldots+k_{d})|\ln\omega|\nu}}{M(\nu)^{d}}\,\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}\bigl(U_{1}=k_{1}|\ln\omega|,\ldots,U_{d}=k_{d}|\ln\omega|\bigr).

Let
F_{d}
and
F_{d}^{(\nu)}
be the distribution functions of the sums
\sum_{j=1}^{d}U_{j}
and
\sum_{j=1}^{d}U_{j}^{(\nu)}
, respectively.
Then for any
x\geqslant 0
we get

\displaystyle F_{d}^{(\nu)}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{d}U_{j}^{(\nu)}\leqslant x\Bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:\\
k_{1}+\ldots+k_{d}\leqslant x\end{subarray}}\dfrac{e^{(k_{1}+\ldots+k_{d})|\ln\omega|\nu}}{M(\nu)^{d}}\,\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}\bigl(U_{1}=k_{1}|\ln\omega|,\ldots,U_{d}=k_{d}|\ln\omega|\bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}\dfrac{e^{\nu u}}{M(\nu)^{d}}\,\mathrm{d}F_{d}(u).

Applying Lemma
1
together with this formula, we obtain the needed representation:

\displaystyle n(d,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{d|\ln(1-\omega)|}\,\mathrm{\mathbf{E}}\biggl[\exp\Bigl\{\sum_{j=1}^{d}U_{j}\Bigr\}\mathds{1}\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{d}U_{j}<|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|\Bigr)\biggr]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{d|\ln(1-\omega)|}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{x}\mathds{1}\bigl(x<|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}F_{d}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{d|\ln(1-\omega)|}M(\nu)^{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{(1-\nu)x}\mathds{1}\bigl(x<|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}F_{d}^{(\nu)}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{d|\ln(1-\omega)|}M(\nu)^{d}\,\mathrm{\mathbf{E}}\biggl[\exp\Big\{(1-\nu)\sum_{j=1}^{d}U_{j}^{(\nu)}\Big\}\mathds{1}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{d}U_{j}^{(\nu)}<|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|\Big)\biggr].\quad\Box

Note that formula (
12
) can be written in the following form:

\displaystyle n(d,\varepsilon)=e^{d|\ln(1-\omega)|}M(\nu)^{d}\,e^{(1-\nu)|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|}\mathrm{\mathbf{E}}\biggl[\exp\Big\{(1-\nu)\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{d}U_{j}^{(\nu)}-|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|\Bigr)\Bigr\}\mathds{1}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{d}U_{j}^{(\nu)}<|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|\Big)\biggr].

It is easily seen that the expectation less than
1
due to
\nu<1
. Hence the part before this dominates the growth of the quantity
n(d,\varepsilon)
. Let us choose
\nu<1
to minimize this part. It is equivalent to the minimization of the function
\nu\mapsto M(\nu)^{d}\,e^{-\nu|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|}
and, consequently, of the function
\nu\mapsto\ln M(\nu)-\nu\tfrac{|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|}{d}
. It is easily seen that for any
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
the latter function has the minimum at the point
\nu_{d,\varepsilon}
such that

\displaystyle\dfrac{M^{\prime}(\nu_{d,\varepsilon})}{M(\nu_{d,\varepsilon})}=\dfrac{|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|}{d}.

Applying Lemma
2
with
\alpha=\tfrac{|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|}{d}
(and
\nu_{\alpha}=\nu_{d,\varepsilon}
), we find

\displaystyle\nu_{d,\varepsilon}=1-c_{\omega}\ln\Bigl(1+\tfrac{d}{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|}\Bigr),\quad d\in\mathbb{N},\,\,\varepsilon\in(0,1).

(13)

Substituting this in (
12
) and using the first equality of (
11
), we have

\displaystyle e^{d|\ln(1-\omega)|}M(\nu_{d,\varepsilon})^{d}=e^{d|\ln(1-\omega)|}(1-\omega)^{d}\Bigl(1+\tfrac{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|}{d}\Bigr)^{d}=\Bigl(1+\tfrac{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|}{d}\Bigr)^{d},

(14)

and thus

\displaystyle n(d,\varepsilon)=\Bigl(1+\tfrac{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|}{d}\Bigr)^{d}\,\mathrm{\mathbf{E}}\biggl[\exp\Big\{c_{\omega}\ln\Bigl(1+\tfrac{d}{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|}\Bigr)S_{d,\varepsilon}\Big\}\mathds{1}\Big(S_{d,\varepsilon}<|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|\Big)\biggr],\quad d\in\mathbb{N},\,\,\varepsilon\in(0,1),

(15)

where we define

\displaystyle S_{d,\varepsilon}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}U_{j}^{(\nu_{d,\varepsilon})},\quad d\in\mathbb{N},\,\,\varepsilon\in(0,1).

Now we consider the asymptotic behaviour of distributions of
S_{d,\varepsilon}
as
d\to\infty
for the case of fixed
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and for the case, when
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{d}\to 0
as
d\to\infty
.

Proposition 1

For any fixed
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}\bigl(S_{d,\varepsilon}=m|\ln\omega|\bigr)=\exp\bigl\{-c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|\bigr\}\cdot\dfrac{(c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|)^{m}}{m!}+O\bigl(\tfrac{1}{d}\bigr),\quad d\to\infty.

Proof of Proposition
1
.
Let us fix
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Since
S_{d,\varepsilon}
is a sum of
d
independent and identically distributed random variables, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}\bigl(S_{d,\varepsilon}=m|\ln\omega|\bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:\\
k_{1}+\ldots+k_{d}=m\end{subarray}}\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}\Big(U_{1}^{(\nu_{d,\varepsilon})}=k_{1}|\ln\omega|,\ldots,U_{d}^{(\nu_{d,\varepsilon})}=k_{d}|\ln\omega|\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:\\
k_{1}+\ldots+k_{d}=m\end{subarray}}\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}\Big(U_{1}^{(\nu_{d,\varepsilon})}=k_{1}|\ln\omega|\Big)\cdot\ldots\cdot\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}\Big(U_{d}^{(\nu_{d,\varepsilon})}=k_{d}|\ln\omega|\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:\\
k_{1}+\ldots+k_{d}=m\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\dfrac{(1-\omega)\,\omega^{k_{j}(1-\nu_{d,\varepsilon})}}{M(\nu_{d,\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:\\
k_{1}+\ldots+k_{d}=m\end{subarray}}\frac{(1-\omega)^{d}\omega^{m(1-\nu_{d,\varepsilon})}}{M(\nu_{d,\varepsilon})^{d}}.

It is known that the equation
k_{1}+\ldots+k_{d}=m
with respect to non-negative integers
k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}
has

\binom{m+d-1}{m}
solutions (see,
[
5
]
, p. 38). Therefore

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}\bigl(S_{d,\varepsilon}=m|\ln\omega|\bigr)=\binom{m+d-1}{m}\frac{(1-\omega)^{d}\,\omega^{m(1-\nu_{d,\varepsilon})}}{M(\nu_{d,\varepsilon})^{d}},\quad d\in\mathbb{N}.

We represent

\displaystyle\binom{m+d-1}{m}=\frac{d\cdot\ldots\cdot(d+m-1)}{m!}=\frac{1\cdot\ldots\cdot\left(1+\tfrac{m-1}{d}\right)}{m!}\cdot d^{m}.

Note that the product in the fraction is assumed to be
1
for the case
m=0
. Next, on account of (
13
) and also (
6
), we have

\displaystyle\omega^{m(1-\nu_{d,\varepsilon})}=e^{m(1-\nu_{d,\varepsilon})\ln\omega}=\Bigl(1+\tfrac{d}{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|}\Bigr)^{mc_{\omega}\ln\omega}=\Bigl(1+\tfrac{d}{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|}\Bigr)^{-m}=\Bigl(\tfrac{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|}{d}\Bigr)^{m}\Bigl(1+\tfrac{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|}{d}\Bigr)^{-m}.

(16)

Due to (
14
), we get

\displaystyle\dfrac{(1-\omega)^{d}}{M(\nu_{d,\varepsilon})^{d}}=e^{-d|\ln(1-\omega)|}M(\nu_{d,\varepsilon})^{-d}=\Bigl(1+\tfrac{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|}{d}\Bigr)^{-d}.

(17)

Thus

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}\bigl(S_{d,\varepsilon}=m|\ln\omega|\bigr)=\dfrac{1\cdot\ldots\cdot\left(1+\tfrac{m-1}{d}\right)}{m!}\bigl(c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|\bigr)^{m}\Bigl(1+\tfrac{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|}{d}\Bigr)^{-m}\Bigl(1+\tfrac{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|}{d}\Bigr)^{-d},\quad d\in\mathbb{N}.

It is easily seen that for fixed
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)

\displaystyle 1\cdot\ldots\cdot\left(1+\tfrac{m-1}{d}\right)=1+O\bigl(\tfrac{1}{d}\bigr),\qquad\Bigl(1+\tfrac{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|}{d}\Bigr)^{-m}=1+O\bigl(\tfrac{1}{d}\bigr),\quad d\to\infty.

Next, observe that for
d\to\infty

\displaystyle\Bigl(1+\tfrac{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|}{d}\Bigr)^{-d}=\exp\Bigl\{-d\ln\Bigl(1+\tfrac{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|}{d}\Bigr)\Bigr\}=\exp\bigl\{-c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|+O\bigl(\tfrac{1}{d}\bigr)\bigr\}.

So we have

\displaystyle\Bigl(1+\tfrac{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|}{d}\Bigr)^{-d}=\exp\bigl\{-c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|\bigr\}\Bigl(1+O\bigl(\tfrac{1}{d}\bigr)\Bigr),\quad d\to\infty.

(18)

Therefore

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}\bigl(S_{d,\varepsilon}=m|\ln\omega|\bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1+O\bigl(\tfrac{1}{d}\bigr)}{m!}\bigl(c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|\bigr)^{m}\Bigl(1+O\bigl(\tfrac{1}{d}\bigr)\Bigr)\cdot\exp\bigl\{-c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|\bigr\}\Bigl(1+O\bigl(\tfrac{1}{d}\bigr)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\bigl\{-c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|\bigr\}\cdot\dfrac{(c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|)^{m}}{m!}\Bigl(1+O\bigl(\tfrac{1}{d}\bigr)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\bigl\{-c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|\bigr\}\cdot\dfrac{(c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|)^{m}}{m!}+O\bigl(\tfrac{1}{d}\bigr),\quad d\to\infty.\quad\Box

Proposition 2

Let
(\varepsilon_{d})_{d\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence from
(0,1)
such that
\varepsilon_{d}\to 0
,
d\to\infty
. Then

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\Biggl|\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}\Biggl(\dfrac{S_{d,\varepsilon_{d}}-\mathrm{\mathbf{E}}S_{d,\varepsilon_{d}}}{\sqrt{\mathrm{\mathbf{Var}}S_{d,\varepsilon_{d}}}}\leqslant x\Biggr)-\Phi(x)\Biggr|\to 0,\quad d\to\infty,

(19)

where
\Phi
is the distribution function of standard normal law:

\displaystyle\Phi(x)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{x}e^{-u^{2}/2}\mathrm{d}u,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

Proof of Proposition
2
.
Suppose that the sequence
(\varepsilon_{d})_{d\in\mathbb{N}}
is given. We define

\displaystyle a_{d}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathrm{\mathbf{E}}S_{d,\varepsilon_{d}},\quad\text{and}\quad b_{d}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sqrt{\mathrm{\mathbf{Var}}S_{d,\varepsilon_{d}}},\quad d\in\mathbb{N}.

(20)

We will prove the convergence (
19
) using the method of characteristic functions (see
[
10
]
). We introduce characteristic functions for the fractions in (
19
):

\displaystyle H_{d}(t)=\mathrm{\mathbf{E}}\exp\Bigl\{it\tfrac{S_{d,\varepsilon_{d}}-a_{d}}{b_{d}}\Bigr\},\quad t\in\mathbb{R},\quad d\in\mathbb{N}.

Since
S_{d,\varepsilon_{d}}
is the sum of
d
independent and identically distributed random variables
U_{j}^{(\nu_{d,\varepsilon_{d}})}
, we have

\displaystyle H_{d}(t)=h_{d}\bigl(\tfrac{t}{b_{d}}\bigr)^{d}\exp\bigl\{-it\tfrac{a_{d}}{b_{d}}\bigr\},\quad t\in\mathbb{R},\quad d\in\mathbb{N},

where
h_{d}
is the characteristic function of
U_{j}^{(\nu_{d,\varepsilon_{d}})}
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
, i.e.

\displaystyle h_{d}(t)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathrm{\mathbf{E}}\exp\bigl\{itU_{j}^{(\nu_{d,\varepsilon_{d}})}\bigr\},\quad t\in\mathbb{R},\quad d\in\mathbb{N}.

Let us find the convenient expression of
h_{d}(t)
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
d\in\mathbb{N}
:

\displaystyle h_{d}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(1-\omega)\omega^{k(1-\nu_{d,\varepsilon_{d}})}}{M(\nu_{d,\varepsilon_{d}})}\,e^{itk|\ln\omega|}=\dfrac{1-\omega}{M(\nu_{d,\varepsilon_{d}})}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigl(\omega^{1-\nu_{d,\varepsilon_{d}}}\cdot e^{it|\ln\omega|}\bigr)^{k}=\dfrac{\tfrac{1-\omega}{M(\nu_{d,\varepsilon_{d}})}}{1-\omega^{1-\nu_{d,\varepsilon_{d}}}\cdot e^{it|\ln\omega|}}.

From (
16
) (with
m=1
) and (
17
) (with
d=1
in the powers) we obtain

\displaystyle h_{d}(t)=\dfrac{\Bigl(1+\tfrac{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}_{d}|}{d}\Bigr)^{-1}}{1-\Bigl(1+\tfrac{d}{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}_{d}|}\Bigr)^{-1}e^{it|\ln\omega|}}=\dfrac{\tfrac{d}{d+c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}_{d}|}}{1-\tfrac{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}_{d}|}{d+c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}_{d}|}\,e^{it|\ln\omega|}}=\Bigl(1-\tfrac{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}_{d}|}{d}\,\bigl(e^{it|\ln\omega|}-1\bigr)\Bigr)^{-1}

for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
d\in\mathbb{N}
. We now return to
H_{d}
:

\displaystyle H_{d}(t)=\Bigl(1-\tfrac{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}_{d}|}{d}\,\bigl(\exp\bigl\{\tfrac{it}{b_{d}}|\ln\omega|\bigr\}-1\bigr)\Bigr)^{-d}\exp\bigl\{-it\tfrac{a_{d}}{b_{d}}\bigr\},\quad t\in\mathbb{R},\quad d\in\mathbb{N}.

Let us consider the sequences
a_{d}
and
b_{d}
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
. For
a_{d}
we have

\displaystyle a_{d}=\mathrm{\mathbf{E}}S_{d,\varepsilon_{d}}=\mathrm{\mathbf{E}}\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{d}U_{j}^{(\nu_{d,\varepsilon_{d}})}\Bigr)=d\,\mathrm{\mathbf{E}}U_{1}^{(\nu_{d,\varepsilon_{d}})},\quad d\in\mathbb{N},

due to the identical distributions of
U_{j}^{(\nu_{d,\varepsilon_{d}})}
. For
b_{d}
, due to the independence of
U_{j}^{(\nu_{d,\varepsilon_{d}})}
, we get

\displaystyle b_{d}^{2}=\mathrm{\mathbf{Var}}S_{d,\varepsilon_{d}}=\mathrm{\mathbf{Var}}\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{d}U_{j}^{(\nu_{d,\varepsilon_{d}})}\Bigr)=d\,\mathrm{\mathbf{Var}}U_{1}^{(\nu_{d,\varepsilon_{d}})},\quad d\in\mathbb{N}.

Recall that, by definition, the distribution of
U_{j}^{(\nu_{d,\varepsilon_{d}})}
can be considered as the Laplace transform of the distribution of
U_{j}
. From the theory of large deviations, it is known (see
[
1
]
, p. 243) that

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{E}}U_{1}^{(\nu_{d,\varepsilon_{d}})}=\frac{M^{\prime}(\nu_{d,\varepsilon_{d}})}{M(\nu_{d,\varepsilon_{d}})},\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{\mathbf{Var}}U_{1}^{(\nu_{d,\varepsilon_{d}})}=\frac{M^{\prime\prime}(\nu_{d,\varepsilon_{d}})}{M(\nu_{d,\varepsilon_{d}})}-\biggl(\frac{M^{\prime}(\nu_{d,\varepsilon_{d}})}{M(\nu_{d,\varepsilon_{d}})}\biggr)^{2},\quad d\in\mathbb{N}.

These equalities can be also checked directly using Lemma
2
and elementary notes about series (see
[
9
]
, Lemma 1). Next, according to (
11
) with
\alpha=\tfrac{|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}{d}
and
\nu_{\alpha}=\nu_{d,\varepsilon}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{E}}U_{1}^{(\nu_{d,\varepsilon_{d}})}=\tfrac{|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}{d},\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{\mathbf{Var}}U_{1}^{(\nu_{d,\varepsilon_{d}})}=|\ln\omega|\cdot\Bigl(1+\tfrac{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}{d}\Bigr)\cdot\tfrac{|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}{d},\quad d\in\mathbb{N}.

Thus we have

\displaystyle a_{d}=|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|,\quad\text{and}\quad b_{d}^{2}=|\ln\omega|\cdot\Bigl(1+\tfrac{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}{d}\Bigr)\cdot|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|,\quad d\in\mathbb{N}.

(21)

Since
\varepsilon_{d}\to 0
as
d\to\infty
, it is seen that
a_{d}\to\infty
and
b_{d}\to\infty
as
d\to\infty
. We also conclude that

\displaystyle\dfrac{a_{d}}{db_{d}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}{d\sqrt{|\ln\omega|\cdot\Bigl(1+\tfrac{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}{d}\Bigr)\cdot|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}}=\dfrac{\sqrt{|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}}{\sqrt{d}\cdot\sqrt{|\ln\omega|\cdot(d+c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|)}}

\displaystyle{}\quad\leqslant\dfrac{\sqrt{|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}}{\sqrt{d}\cdot\sqrt{|\ln\omega|\cdot c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\to 0,\quad d\to\infty.

We return to
H_{d}
:

\displaystyle H_{d}(t)=\Bigl(1-\tfrac{c_{\omega}a_{d}}{d}\,\bigl(\exp\bigl\{\tfrac{it}{b_{d}}|\ln\omega|\bigr\}-1\bigr)\Bigr)^{-d}\Bigl(\exp\bigl\{it\tfrac{a_{d}}{db_{d}}\bigr\}\Bigr)^{-d},\quad t\in\mathbb{R},\quad d\in\mathbb{N}.

We fix
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Due to comments above, the following expansions are valid:

\displaystyle\exp\bigl\{\tfrac{ita_{d}}{db_{d}}\bigr\}=1+it\tfrac{a_{d}}{db_{d}}-\tfrac{t^{2}}{2}\cdot\tfrac{a_{d}^{2}}{d^{2}b_{d}^{2}}+O\Bigl(\tfrac{a_{d}^{3}}{d^{3}b_{d}^{3}}\Bigr),\quad d\to\infty,

and

\displaystyle\exp\bigl\{\tfrac{it}{b_{d}}|\ln\omega|\bigr\}-1=\tfrac{it}{b_{d}}|\ln\omega|-\tfrac{t^{2}}{2b_{d}^{2}}|\ln\omega|^{2}+O\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{b_{d}^{3}}\Bigr),\quad d\to\infty.

From the latter we get

\displaystyle 1-\tfrac{c_{\omega}a_{d}}{d}\,\bigl(\exp\bigl\{\tfrac{it}{b_{d}}|\ln\omega|\bigr\}-1\bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\tfrac{c_{\omega}a_{d}}{d}\,\Bigl(\tfrac{it}{b_{d}}|\ln\omega|-\tfrac{t^{2}}{2b_{d}^{2}}|\ln\omega|^{2}+O\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{b_{d}^{3}}\Bigr)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-it\tfrac{a_{d}}{db_{d}}+\tfrac{t^{2}}{2}\cdot\tfrac{a_{d}}{db_{d}^{2}}\,|\ln\omega|+O\Bigl(\tfrac{a_{d}}{db_{d}^{3}}\Bigr),\quad d\to\infty.

Thus we obtain

\displaystyle H_{d}(t)=\Biggr[\biggl(1-it\tfrac{a_{d}}{db_{d}}+\tfrac{t^{2}}{2}\cdot\tfrac{a_{d}}{db_{d}^{2}}\,|\ln\omega|+O\Bigl(\tfrac{a_{d}}{db_{d}^{3}}\Bigr)\biggr)\cdot\biggl(1+it\tfrac{a_{d}}{db_{d}}-\tfrac{t^{2}}{2}\cdot\tfrac{a_{d}^{2}}{d^{2}b_{d}^{2}}+O\Bigl(\tfrac{a_{d}^{3}}{d^{3}b_{d}^{3}}\Bigr)\biggr)\Biggr]^{-d},\quad d\to\infty.

We next write

\displaystyle H_{d}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\biggl[1-it\tfrac{a_{d}}{db_{d}}+it\tfrac{a_{d}}{db_{d}}+\tfrac{t^{2}}{2}\cdot\tfrac{a_{d}}{db_{d}^{2}}\,|\ln\omega|-\tfrac{t^{2}}{2}\cdot\tfrac{a_{d}^{2}}{d^{2}b_{d}^{2}}-\bigl(it\tfrac{a_{d}}{db_{d}}\bigr)^{2}+\tfrac{t^{2}}{2}\cdot\tfrac{a_{d}}{db_{d}^{2}}\,|\ln\omega|\cdot it\tfrac{a_{d}}{db_{d}}+it\tfrac{a_{d}}{db_{d}}\cdot\tfrac{t^{2}}{2}\cdot\tfrac{a_{d}^{2}}{d^{2}b_{d}^{2}}

\displaystyle{}\quad-\tfrac{t^{2}}{2}\cdot\tfrac{a_{d}}{db_{d}^{2}}\,|\ln\omega|\cdot\tfrac{t^{2}}{2}\cdot\tfrac{a_{d}^{2}}{d^{2}b_{d}^{2}}+O\Bigl(\tfrac{a_{d}^{3}}{d^{3}b_{d}^{3}}\Bigr)+O\Bigl(\tfrac{a_{d}}{db_{d}^{3}}\Bigr)\biggr]^{-d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\biggl[1+\tfrac{t^{2}}{2}\cdot\Bigl(\tfrac{a_{d}}{db_{d}^{2}}\,|\ln\omega|+\tfrac{a_{d}^{2}}{d^{2}b_{d}^{2}}\Bigr)+\tfrac{it^{3}}{2}\cdot\Bigl(\tfrac{a_{d}^{2}}{d^{2}b_{d}^{3}}\,|\ln\omega|+\tfrac{a_{d}^{3}}{d^{3}b_{d}^{3}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle{}\quad-\tfrac{t^{4}}{4}\cdot\tfrac{a_{d}^{3}}{d^{3}b_{d}^{4}}\,|\ln\omega|+O\Bigl(\tfrac{a_{d}^{3}}{d^{3}b_{d}^{3}}\Bigr)+O\Bigl(\tfrac{a_{d}}{db_{d}^{3}}\Bigr)\biggr]^{-d},\quad d\to\infty.

Here

\displaystyle\dfrac{a_{d}}{db_{d}^{2}}\,|\ln\omega|+\dfrac{a_{d}^{2}}{d^{2}b_{d}^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\ln\omega|\cdot\dfrac{a_{d}^{2}}{d^{2}b_{d}^{2}}\cdot\dfrac{d+c_{\omega}a_{d}}{a_{d}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\ln\omega|\cdot\dfrac{|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}{d\cdot|\ln\omega|\cdot(d+c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|)}\cdot\dfrac{d+c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}{|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}=\dfrac{1}{d},\quad d\in\mathbb{N}.

Since

\displaystyle\dfrac{a_{d}}{db_{d}}=O(d^{-1/2}),\quad d\to\infty,

we have

\displaystyle\tfrac{it^{3}}{2}\cdot\Bigl(\tfrac{a_{d}^{2}}{d^{2}b_{d}^{3}}\,|\ln\omega|+\tfrac{a_{d}^{3}}{d^{3}b_{d}^{3}}\Bigr)=\tfrac{it^{3}}{2}\cdot\Bigl(O\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{db_{d}}\Bigr)+O\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{d\sqrt{d}}\Bigr)\Bigr)=o\bigl(\tfrac{1}{d}\bigr),\quad d\to\infty,

and

\displaystyle-\tfrac{t^{4}}{4}\cdot\dfrac{a_{d}^{3}}{d^{3}b_{d}^{4}}\,|\ln\omega|+O\Bigl(\tfrac{a_{d}^{3}}{d^{3}b_{d}^{3}}\Bigr)=O\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{d\sqrt{d}b_{d}}\Bigr)+O\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{d\sqrt{d}}\Bigr)=o\bigl(\tfrac{1}{d}\bigr),\quad d\to\infty.

Observe that

\displaystyle\dfrac{a_{d}}{db_{d}^{3}}=\dfrac{|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}{d|\ln\omega|^{3/2}\cdot\Bigl(1+\tfrac{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}{d}\Bigr)^{3/2}\cdot|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|^{3/2}}\leqslant\dfrac{1}{d\cdot|\ln\omega|^{3/2}\cdot|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|^{1/2}}=o\bigl(\tfrac{1}{d}\bigr),\quad d\to\infty.

Thus

\displaystyle H_{d}(t)=\left(1+\tfrac{t^{2}}{2d}+o\bigl(\tfrac{1}{d}\bigr)\right)^{-d}\to e^{-t^{2}/2},\quad d\to\infty.

Recall that
\exp\{-t^{2}/2\}
is the characteristic function corresponding to the standard normal law. By the well-known Lévy’s continuity theorem (see
[
10
]
p. 48–49), we have the pointwise convergence

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}\Biggl(\dfrac{S_{d,\varepsilon_{d}}-\mathrm{\mathbf{E}}S_{d,\varepsilon_{d}}}{\sqrt{\mathrm{\mathbf{Var}}S_{d,\varepsilon_{d}}}}\leqslant x\Biggr)\to\Phi(x),\quad d\to\infty,

(22)

for any
x
from the set
\mathcal{C}(\Phi)
of all continuity points of
\Phi
. Since
\Phi
is a continuous function on
\mathbb{R}
, the set
\mathcal{C}(\Phi)
is exactly
\mathbb{R}
and, moreover, the convergence (
22
) is uniform on
\mathbb{R}
(see
[
14
]
, p. 11). Thus we come to (
19
).
\Box

4
Proofs of the main results

Proof of Theorem
1
. We fix
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Let us use formula (
15
) and also (
6
):

\displaystyle n(d,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigl(1+\tfrac{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|}{d}\Bigr)^{d}\,\mathrm{\mathbf{E}}\biggl[\exp\Big\{c_{\omega}\ln\Bigl(1+\tfrac{d}{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|}\Bigr)S_{d,\varepsilon}\Big\}\mathds{1}\Big(S_{d,\varepsilon}<|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|\Big)\biggr]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigl(1+\tfrac{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|}{d}\Bigr)^{d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:\\
m|\ln\omega|<|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|\end{subarray}}\exp\Big\{c_{\omega}\ln\Bigl(1+\tfrac{d}{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|}\Bigr)m|\ln\omega|\Big\}\cdot\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}\bigl(S_{d,\varepsilon}=m|\ln\omega|\bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigl(1+\tfrac{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|}{d}\Bigr)^{d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:\\
m<c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|\end{subarray}}\exp\Big\{\ln\Bigl(1+\tfrac{d}{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|}\Bigr)m\Big\}\cdot\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}\bigl(S_{d,\varepsilon}=m|\ln\omega|\bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigl(1+\tfrac{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|}{d}\Bigr)^{d}\sum_{m=0}^{N_{\omega}(\varepsilon)}\Bigl(1+\tfrac{d}{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|}\Bigr)^{m}\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}\bigl(S_{d,\varepsilon}=m|\ln\omega|\bigr),

where we set
N_{\omega}(\varepsilon)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\max\bigl\{m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:m<c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|\bigr\}
as in the statement of the theorem. Due to (
18
), we have

\displaystyle\Bigl(1+\tfrac{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|}{d}\Bigr)^{d}=e^{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|}\cdot\Bigl(1+O\bigl(\tfrac{1}{d}\bigr)\Bigr),\quad d\to\infty.

Applying Proposition
1
to the sum above, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{N_{\omega}(\varepsilon)}\Bigl(1+\tfrac{d}{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|}\Bigr)^{m}\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}\bigl(S_{d,\varepsilon}=m|\ln\omega|\bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{N_{\omega}(\varepsilon)}\biggl[\Bigl(1+\tfrac{d}{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|}\Bigr)^{m}\Bigl(e^{-c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|}\cdot\tfrac{(c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|)^{m}}{m!}+O\bigl(\tfrac{1}{d}\bigr)\Bigr)\biggr]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigl(1+\tfrac{d}{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|}\Bigr)^{N_{\omega}(\varepsilon)}e^{-c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|}\cdot\dfrac{(c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|)^{N_{\omega}(\varepsilon)}}{N_{\omega}(\varepsilon)!}+O\bigl(d^{N_{\omega}(\varepsilon)-1}\bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigl(\tfrac{d}{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|}\Bigr)^{N_{\omega}(\varepsilon)}e^{-c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|}\cdot\dfrac{(c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|)^{N_{\omega}(\varepsilon)}}{N_{\omega}(\varepsilon)!}+O\bigl(d^{N_{\omega}(\varepsilon)-1}\bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|}\cdot\dfrac{d^{N_{\omega}(\varepsilon)}}{N_{\omega}(\varepsilon)!}+O\bigl(d^{N_{\omega}(\varepsilon)-1}\bigr),\quad d\to\infty.

Thus

\displaystyle n(d,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|}\cdot\Bigl(1+O\bigl(\tfrac{1}{d}\bigr)\Bigr)\cdot\biggl(e^{-c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon^{2}|}\cdot\dfrac{d^{N_{\omega}(\varepsilon)}}{N_{\omega}(\varepsilon)!}+O\bigl(d^{N_{\omega}(\varepsilon)-1}\bigr)\biggr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{d^{N_{\omega}(\varepsilon)}}{N_{\omega}(\varepsilon)!}+O\bigl(d^{N_{\omega}(\varepsilon)-1}\bigr),\quad d\to\infty,

as required.
\Box

Proof of Theorem
2
.
Suppose that the sequence
(\varepsilon_{d})_{d\in\mathbb{N}}
is given. Due to (
15
) we have

\displaystyle n(d,\varepsilon_{d})=\Bigl(1+\tfrac{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}{d}\Bigr)^{d}\,\mathrm{\mathbf{E}}\biggl[\exp\Big\{c_{\omega}\ln\Bigl(1+\tfrac{d}{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}\Bigr)S_{d,\varepsilon_{d}}\Big\}\mathds{1}\Big(S_{d,\varepsilon_{d}}<|\ln\varepsilon^{2}_{d}|\Big)\biggr],\quad d\in\mathbb{N}.

From this we easily obtain the upper bound for
n(d,\varepsilon_{d})
:

\displaystyle n(d,\varepsilon_{d})

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\Bigl(1+\tfrac{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}{d}\Bigr)^{d}\,\exp\Big\{c_{\omega}\ln\Bigl(1+\tfrac{d}{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}\Bigr)|\ln\varepsilon^{2}_{d}|\Bigl\}\mathrm{\mathbf{E}}\mathds{1}\bigl(S_{d,\varepsilon_{d}}<|\ln\varepsilon^{2}_{d}|\bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\biggl\{d\cdot\ln\Bigl(1+\tfrac{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}{d}\Bigr)+c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|\cdot\ln\Bigl(1+\tfrac{d}{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}\Bigr)\biggr\}\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}\bigl(S_{d,\varepsilon_{d}}<|\ln\varepsilon^{2}_{d}|\bigr)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\exp\biggl\{d\cdot\ln\Bigl(1+\tfrac{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}{d}\Bigr)+c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|\cdot\ln\Bigl(1+\tfrac{d}{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}\Bigr)\biggr\},\quad d\in\mathbb{N}.

For the lower bound we introduce
b_{d}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sqrt{\mathrm{\mathbf{Var}}S_{d,\varepsilon_{d}}}
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
, as in (
20
). So we have

\displaystyle n(d,\varepsilon_{d})

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\Bigl(1+\tfrac{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}{d}\Bigr)^{d}\,\mathrm{\mathbf{E}}\biggl[\exp\Big\{c_{\omega}\ln\Bigl(1+\tfrac{d}{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}\Bigr)S_{d,\varepsilon_{d}}\Big\}\mathds{1}\Big(|\ln\varepsilon^{2}_{d}|-b_{d}<S_{d,\varepsilon_{d}}<|\ln\varepsilon^{2}_{d}|\Big)\biggr]

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\Bigl(1+\tfrac{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}{d}\Bigr)^{d}\,\exp\Big\{c_{\omega}\ln\Bigl(1+\tfrac{d}{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}\Bigr)\bigl(|\ln\varepsilon^{2}_{d}|-b_{d}\bigr)\Big\}\mathrm{\mathbf{E}}\mathds{1}\Big(|\ln\varepsilon^{2}_{d}|-b_{d}<S_{d,\varepsilon_{d}}<|\ln\varepsilon^{2}_{d}|\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\biggl\{d\cdot\ln\Bigl(1+\tfrac{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}{d}\Bigr)+c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|\cdot\ln\Bigl(1+\tfrac{d}{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}\Bigr)\biggr\}

\displaystyle{}\cdot\exp\Big\{-c_{\omega}b_{d}\ln\Bigl(1+\tfrac{d}{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}\Bigr)\Big\}\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}\Big(|\ln\varepsilon^{2}_{d}|-b_{d}<S_{d,\varepsilon_{d}}<|\ln\varepsilon^{2}_{d}|\Big),\quad d\in\mathbb{N}.

According to (
21
), observe that

\displaystyle\dfrac{b_{d}}{|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}=\dfrac{1}{|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}\sqrt{|\ln\omega|\cdot\Bigl(1+\tfrac{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}{d}\Bigr)\cdot|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}=\sqrt{|\ln\omega|\cdot\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}+\tfrac{c_{\omega}}{d}\Bigr)}\to 0,\quad d\to\infty.

Therefore

\displaystyle-c_{\omega}b_{d}\ln\Bigl(1+\tfrac{d}{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}\Bigr)=-\dfrac{b_{d}}{|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}\cdot c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|\cdot\ln\Bigl(1+\tfrac{d}{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}\Bigr)=o\Bigl(|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|\cdot\ln\Bigl(1+\tfrac{d}{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}\Bigr)\Bigr),\quad d\to\infty.

Next, since
|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|=\mathrm{\mathbf{E}}S_{d,\varepsilon_{d}}
and
b_{d}=\sqrt{\mathrm{\mathbf{Var}}S_{d,\varepsilon_{d}}}
, by Proposition
2
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}\Big(|\ln\varepsilon^{2}_{d}|-b_{d}<S_{d,\varepsilon_{d}}<|\ln\varepsilon^{2}_{d}|\Big)=\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}\Biggl(-1<\dfrac{S_{d,\varepsilon_{d}}-\mathrm{\mathbf{E}}S_{d,\varepsilon_{d}}}{\sqrt{\mathrm{\mathbf{Var}}S_{d,\varepsilon_{d}}}}<0\Biggr)=\Phi(0)-\Phi(-1)+o(1),\quad d\to\infty.

Here
C\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Phi(0)-\Phi(-1)
is a positive constant:

\displaystyle C=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-1}^{0}e^{-x^{2}/2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-x^{2}/2}\,\mathrm{d}x>0.

In particular, this means that

\displaystyle\ln\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}\Big(|\ln\varepsilon^{2}_{d}|-b_{d}<S_{d,\varepsilon_{d}}<|\ln\varepsilon^{2}_{d}|\Big)=\ln\bigl(C+o(1)\bigr)=o\Bigl(|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|\cdot\ln\Bigl(1+\tfrac{d}{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}\Bigr)\Bigr),\quad d\to\infty.

Thus the lower bound admits the form

\displaystyle n(d,\varepsilon_{d})\geqslant\exp\biggl\{d\cdot\ln\Bigl(1+\tfrac{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}{d}\Bigr)+c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|\cdot\ln\Bigl(1+\tfrac{d}{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}\Bigr)+o\Bigl(|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|\cdot\ln\Bigl(1+\tfrac{d}{c_{\omega}|\ln\varepsilon_{d}^{2}|}\Bigr)\Bigr)\biggr\}.

The obtained bounds imply the required result.
\Box

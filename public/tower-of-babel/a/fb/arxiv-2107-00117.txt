A note on the K K -epigraph
Armand Gissler, Tim Hoheisel

Abstract
We study the question as to when the closed convex hull of a K K -convex map equals its K K -epigraph. In particular, we shed light onto the smallest cone K K such that a given map has convex and closed K K -epigraph, respectively. We apply our findings to several examples in matrix space as well as to convex composite functions.

Cone-induced ordering,
K
-convexity,
K
-epigraph, convex hull, Fenchel conjugate, horizon cone, spectral function, convex-composite function, matrix analysis.

Introduction

Motivation

In a recent paper, Burke et al.
[
8

show that the closed convex hull of the set
\mathcal{D}:=\left\{(X,\frac{1}{2}XX^{T})\,\left|\;X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}\right.\right\}

\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,\mathcal{D}=\left\{(X,Y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}\times\mathbb{S}^{n}\,\left|\;Y\succeq\frac{1}{2}XX^{T}\right.\right\}.

Here, ‘
\succeq

Löwner partial ordering

[
12
]
on the symmetric matrices

induced by the positive semidefinite cone
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
via ‘

if and only if
A-B\in\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
’.
At second glance, the set

is simply the graph of the matrix-valued map

in (

(induced by

(induced by
\mathbb{R}_{+}
) for scalar-valued functions.

More generally, given a map

\mathbb{E}_{2}
and a cone

2
Preliminaries

Throughout we make use of the
relative topology
for convex sets
[
18
, §6]
. The
relative interior

\mathrm{ri}\,C
of a convex set
C\subset\mathbb{E}
is its interior in the subspace topology induced by its
affine hull

\mathrm{aff}\,C:=\left\{\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y\,\left|\;\lambda\in\mathbb{R},\;x,y\in C\right.\right\}
. For a convex set
C\subset\mathbb{E}
and (any)
x_{0}\in C
, the
subspace parallel to

C
is
\mathrm{par}\,C:=\mathrm{aff}\,C-x_{0}
. For convex sets, we have a handy description of the affine hull.

Lemma 1
.

Let
C\subset\mathbb{E}
be a convex. Then

\mathrm{aff}\,C=\{\alpha x-\beta y\mid\alpha,\beta\geqslant 0,\alpha-\beta=1,x,y\in C\}.

Proof.

Set
A:=\{\alpha x-\beta y\mid\alpha,\beta\geqslant 0,\alpha-\beta=1,x,y\in C\}
. Thus
\mathrm{aff}\,C\supset A
.
Conversely, for
z\in\mathrm{aff}\,C
, there exist
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
x,y\in C
such that
z=\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y
. If
\lambda\in(0,1)
, then
z\in C
by convexity, and hence
z=1\cdot z-0\cdot z\in A
. If
\lambda>1
, set
\alpha:=\lambda\geqslant 0
,
\beta:=\lambda-1\geqslant 0
, and we get
\alpha-\beta=1
and
z=\alpha x-\beta y\in A
. Finally, if
\lambda<0
, then
1-\lambda\geqslant 0
, and thus (
\beta:=\lambda,\alpha:=1-\lambda
)
z\in A
.
∎

Let
f:\mathbb{E}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}:=\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
. We call
f

proper
if its
domain

\mathrm{dom}\,f:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{E}\,\left|\;f(x)<+\infty\right.\right\}
is nonempty and
f
doesn’t take the value
-\infty
. We say that
f
is
convex
if its
epigraph

\mathrm{epi}\,f:=\left\{(x,\alpha)\in\mathbb{E}\times\mathbb{R}\,\left|\;f(x)<\alpha\right.\right\}
is convex which coincides with the usual definition via a secant condition

f(\alpha x+(1-\alpha)y)\leq\alpha f(x)+(1-\alpha)f(y)\quad\forall x,y\in\mathrm{dom}\,f,\;\alpha\in(0,1),

if
f
does not take the value
-\infty
.
Although pointwise convergence is not a suitable for preservation of many variational properties, see e.g.
[
19
, Chapter 7]
, it still preserves convexity in the limit.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\{f_{k}:\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}\}
converge pointwise to
f:\mathbb{E}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
, i.e.
f_{k}(x)\to f(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{E}
. If
f_{k}
is convex for all
k\in\mathbb{E}
(sufficiently large), then so is
f
.

Proof.

For
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
x,y\in\mathbb{E}
, the convexity of
f_{k}
yields

\alpha f_{k}(x)+(1-\alpha)f_{k}(y)\geqslant f_{k}(\alpha x+(1-\alpha)y).

Passing to the limit
k\to\infty
on both sides gives

\alpha f+(1-\alpha)f(y)\geqslant f(\alpha x+(1-\alpha)y).

∎

We call
f:\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}

closed
or
lower semicontinuous (lsc)
if
\mathrm{epi}\,f
is closed.
We set

\displaystyle\Gamma(\mathbb{E})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left\{f:\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}\,\left|\;f\;\text{proper, convex}\right.\right\},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left\{f\in\Gamma(\mathbb{E})\,\left|\;f\;\text{closed}\right.\right\}.

For
f\in\Gamma(\mathbb{E})
, its
closure

\mathrm{cl}\;f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E})
is defined via
\mathrm{cl}\;(\mathrm{epi}\,f)=\mathrm{epi}\,(\mathrm{cl}\;f).

More generally, given a convex subset
D\subset\mathbb{E}
, we call
f

D
-closed
if
\mathrm{epi}\,f
is closed in the subspace topology induced by
D\times\mathbb{R}
.
We note that
D\times\mathbb{R}
is a metric space, hence closedness is sequential closedness and, in particular,
f
is
D
-closed if and only if

\liminf_{k\to\infty}f(x_{k})\geq f(\bar{x})\quad\forall\{x_{k}\in D\}\to\bar{x}\in D.

We define

\Gamma_{0}(D):=\left\{f\in\Gamma(\mathbb{E})\,\left|\;f\;\text{$D$-closed}\right.\right\}.

Lemma 3
.

Let
f\in\Gamma(\mathbb{E})
. Then the following are equivalent:

i)

f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathrm{dom}\,f)
;

ii)

f(x)=(\mathrm{cl}\;f)(x)
for all
x\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
.

Proof.

Since
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E})
, we have

\displaystyle f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathrm{dom}\,f)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\mathrm{epi}\,f\mbox{ is closed in }\mathrm{dom}\,f\times\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\mathrm{epi}\,f=\mathrm{cl}\;(\mathrm{epi}\,f)\cap\mathrm{dom}\,f\times\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\mathrm{epi}\,f=\mathrm{epi}\,(\mathrm{cl}\;f)\cap\mathrm{dom}\,f\times\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle f(x)=\mathrm{cl}\;f(x)\quad\forall x\in\mathrm{dom}\,f.

Here the first equivalence is simply the definition of
\Gamma_{0}(\mathrm{dom}\,f)
. The second is due to the fact that the closed sets in the
\mathrm{dom}\,f\times\mathbb{R}
subspace topology are exactly the intersections of closed sets (in
\mathbb{E}\times\mathbb{R}
) with
\mathrm{dom}\,f\times\mathbb{R}
. The third one is clear as
\mathrm{epi}\,(\mathrm{cl}\;f)=\mathrm{cl}\;(\mathrm{epi}\,f)
, and the fourth one follows from elementary considerations.
∎

Remark 4
.

We point out that
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E})
implies that
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathrm{dom}\,f)
, since the closed set
\mathrm{epi}\,f\subset\mathbb{E}\times\mathbb{R}
intersected with
\mathrm{dom}\,f\times\mathbb{R}
is (trivially) closed in the subspace topology induced by
\mathrm{dom}\,f\times\mathbb{R}
. However, the converse statement is not true. Consider for instance
\delta_{(0,1)}\in\Gamma_{0}((0,1))\setminus\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{R})
, as
(0,1)\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a closed set in the topology induced by
(0,1)\times\mathbb{R}
, but is not a closed set in
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
.

A nonempty subset
K\subset\mathbb{E}
is called a
cone
if
\lambda x\in K
for all
\lambda\geq 0
and
x\in K
. If the latter only holds for all
\lambda>0
, we call
K
a
pre-cone
. For instance if
K
is a cone, then
\mathrm{ri}\,K
is a pre-cone, use e.g.
[
18
, Corollary 6.6.1]
. Combining this with the
line segment principle

[
18
, Theorem 6.1]
and
[
18
, Theorem 6.3]
, we find the following result.

Lemma 5
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{E}
be a convex cone. Then
\mathrm{cl}\;K+\mathrm{ri}\,K\subset\mathrm{ri}\,K
.

The
polar cone
of a (pre-)cone
K
is given by
K^{\circ}:=\{v\in\mathbb{E}_{2}\mid\forall u\in K\colon\langle u,v\rangle\leqslant 0\},
and
-K^{\circ}
is referred to as the
dual cone
.
Recall that
\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,K=(K^{\circ})^{\circ}=:K^{\circ\circ}
by the bipolar theorem
[
19
, Corollary 6.21]
, and that the polarity operation is order reversing.
The
horizon cone
of
C\subset\mathbb{E}
is given by

C^{\infty}:=\{u\in\mathbb{E}\mid\exists\{t_{k}\}\downarrow 0,\{x_{k}\in C\}:\;\lim_{k\to\infty}t_{k}x_{k}=u\}.

If
C
is a nonempty convex set, then
C+C^{\infty}=\mathrm{cl}\;C
and for a cone
K
, we have
K^{\infty}=\mathrm{cl}\;K
.

A cone
K\subset\mathbb{E}
induces and ordering on
\mathbb{E}
via

y\geq_{K}x:\Longleftrightarrow y-x\in K\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{E}.

Lemma 6
.

If
K
is a closed and convex cone of
\mathbb{E}_{2}
, then

y\geq_{K}x\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\left\langle u,\,x\right\rangle\geqslant\left\langle u,\,y\right\rangle\quad\forall u\in-K^{\circ}.

Proof.

By the bipolar theorem
[
19
, Corollary 6.21]
, we have
K^{\circ\circ}=K
and hence

x\geqslant_{K}y\;\Longleftrightarrow\;x-y\in K=K^{\circ\circ}\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\left\langle u,\,x-y\right\rangle\geqslant 0\quad\forall u\in-K^{\circ}.

∎

A cone
K\subset\mathbb{E}
is said to be
pointed
if
K\cap(-K)=\{0\}
. Such a cone induces a partial ordering.

Lemma 7

(Ordering induced by a pointed cone)
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{E}
be pointed. Then

x=y\iff x\geq_{K}y\and y\geq_{K}x.

Proof.

Straightforward.
∎

K
-convexity and
K

We commence this section with the central definitions of this paper.

Definition 8

(
K
-epigraphs,
K
-convexity and
K
-closedness)
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{E}_{2}
be a cone and let
F\colon D\subset\mathbb{E}_{1}\to\mathbb{E}_{2}
. Then the
K
-epigraph of
F
is given by

{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}=\left\{(x,y)\in D\times\mathbb{E}_{2}\,\left|\;F(x)\leq_{K}y\right.\right\}\subset\mathbb{E}_{1}\times\mathbb{E}_{2}.

(2)

We say that
F
is

i)

proper if
{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}\neq\emptyset
(i.e.
D\neq\emptyset
);

ii)

K
-convex if
{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}
is convex;

iii)

K
-closed if
{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}
is closed.

For
D\subset\mathbb{E}_{1}
convex and
K\subset\mathbb{E}_{2}
a cone, we point out that
F:D\to\mathbb{E}_{2}
is
K
-convex if and only if
K
is convex and

\alpha F(x)+(1-\alpha)F(y)\geqslant_{K}F(\alpha x+(1-\alpha)y)\quad\forall x,y\in D,\alpha\in(0,1).

Moreover, we always have

{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}=\mathrm{gph}\,F+\{0\}\times K.

(3)

This has, in particular, the following immediate consequence.

Lemma 9
.

Let
F\colon D\subset\mathbb{E}_{1}\to\mathbb{E}_{2}
be proper, and
K_{1}\varsubsetneq K_{2}\subset\mathbb{E}_{2}
be cones. Then
{K_{1}}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}\varsubsetneq{K_{2}}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}
. In particular, there is at most one cone
K\subset\mathbb{E}_{2}
such that
{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}=\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,F)
.

In the convex case we can extract the following.

Lemma 10
.

Let
F\colon D\subset\mathbb{E}_{1}\to\mathbb{E}_{2}
be proper, and let
K_{1}\subset K_{2}\subset\mathbb{E}_{2}
be convex cones. Then

{K_{2}}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}={K_{1}}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}+\{0\}\times K_{2}
. In particular, if
F
is
K_{1}
-convex, then
F
is
K_{2}
-convex.

Proof.

This is due to (
3
) combined with the fact that
K_{1}+K_{2}=K_{2}
because
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
are convex cones.

Given a cone
K\subset\mathbb{E}
and its induced ordering, we attach to

a formal largest element
+\infty_{\bullet}
with respect to that ordering, and set
\mathbb{E}^{\bullet}:=\mathbb{E}\cup\{+\infty_{\bullet}\}
.
For
G\colon\mathbb{E}_{1}\to\mathbb{E}_{2}^{\bullet}
its
domain
is
\mathrm{dom}\,G:=\{x\in\mathbb{E}_{1}:G(x)\in\mathbb{E}_{2}\}
. The
graph
of

\mathrm{gph}\,G:=\{(x,F(x))\mid x\in\mathrm{dom}\,F\}.

We adopt the notions in Definition
8
via the restriction
F:=G_{|\mathrm{dom}\,G}
.
We record in the next result that
K
-closedness and
K
-convexity requires certain conditions about the underlying cone
K
.

Proposition 11
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{E}_{2}
be a cone, and let
F\colon\mathbb{E}_{1}\to\mathbb{E}_{2}^{\bullet}
be proper. Then the following hold:

a)

If
F
is
K
-closed, then
K
is closed.

b)

If
F
is
K
-convex, then
K
is convex.

Proof.

a) Let
\{y_{k}\in K\}\to y
and pick
x\in\mathrm{dom}\,F
. Then
(x,F(x)+y_{n})\in{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
(x,F(x)+y_{k})\to(x,F(x)+y)\in{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}
as
{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}
is closed. Thus,
y\in F(x)+y-F(x)=y\in K
.

b) Let
y_{1},y_{2}\in K,\alpha\in(0,1)
. For
x\in\mathrm{dom}\,F
we hence find
(x,F(x)+y_{1})\in{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}
, and
(x,F(x)+y_{2})\in{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}
. As
{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}
is a convex, we have
(x,F(x)+\alpha y_{1}+(1-\alpha)y_{2})\in{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}
, and consequently
\alpha y_{1}+(1-\alpha)y_{2}\in K
. Thus,
K
is convex.
∎

The following proposition shows that a function
F\colon\mathbb{E}_{1}\to\mathbb{E}_{2}^{\bullet}
is fully determined by its
K

is a pointed cone.

Proposition 12
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{E}_{2}
be a pointed cone, and let
F,G\colon\mathbb{E}_{1}\to\mathbb{E}_{2}^{\bullet}
be proper. Then

{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}={K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,G}\iff F=G.

Proof.

Suppose that
{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}={K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,G}
. In particular, for all
x\in\mathrm{dom}\,F
,
(x,F(x))\in{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}
, so
(x,F(x))\in{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,G}
, hence
x\in\mathrm{dom}\,G
and
F(x)\geqslant_{K}G(x)
. Likewise, for all
x\in\mathrm{dom}\,G
, we have
x\in\mathrm{dom}\,F
and
G(x)\geqslant_{K}F(x)
. Thus
\mathrm{dom}\,F=\mathrm{dom}\,G
and for any
x\in\mathrm{dom}\,F=\mathrm{dom}\,G
we have
F(x)=G(x)
by Lemma
7
.

Proposition 13

Let

be proper. Then:

a)

{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}=\bigcap_{u\in\mbox{-}K^{\circ}}(\mathrm{id},\left\langle u,\,\cdot\right\rangle)^{-1}\big(\mathrm{epi}\,\left\langle u,\,F\right\rangle\big)

b)

If

\mathrm{gph}\,F=\bigcap_{u\in\mbox{-}K^{\circ}}(\mathrm{id},\left\langle u,\,\cdot\right\rangle)^{-1}\big(\mathrm{gph}\,\left\langle u,\,F\right\rangle\big)

Proof.

We deduce from Lemma

\displaystyle{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}

\displaystyle=\left\{(x,v)\,\left|\;v\geq_{K}F(x)\right.\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{(x,v)\,\left|\;\left\langle u,\,v\right\rangle\geq\left\langle u,\,F(x)\right\rangle\;\forall u\in\mbox{-}K^{\circ}\right.\right\}

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{u\in\mbox{-}K^{\circ}}(\mathrm{id},\left\langle u,\,\cdot\right\rangle)^{-1}\big(\mathrm{epi}\,\left\langle u,\,F\right\rangle\big).

Similarly, if
K
is pointed, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{gph}\,F

\displaystyle=\left\{(x,v)\,\left|\;x\in\mathbb{E}_{1},\;v=F(x)\right.\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{(x,v)\,\left|\;x\in\mathbb{E}_{1},\;v-F(x)\in K\cap(-K)\right.\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{(x,v)\,\left|\;x\in\mathbb{E}_{1},\;v\geq_{K}F(x)\right.\right\}\bigcap\left\{(x,v)\,\left|\;x\in\mathbb{E}_{1},\;F(x)\geq_{K}v\right.\right\}

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{u\in\mbox{-}K^{\circ}}(\mathrm{id},\left\langle u,\,\cdot\right\rangle)^{-1}\big(\mathrm{gph}\,\left\langle u,\,F\right\rangle\big).

∎

As an immediate consequence of the latter theorem, one obtains Pennanen’s sufficient condition for
K
-closedness
[
17
, Lemma 6.2]
, which unfortunately excludes functions with domains that are not closed. We therefore provide the following stronger version whose proof is simply
a refinement of Pennanen’s in the next result’s part b). Part a) is a refinement of the scalarization characterization of
K
-convexity.

Proposition 14

Let

Proof.

\Rightarrow

2
.

’ii)
\Rightarrow

a).

’iii)
\Rightarrow

b) Assume that

We close out this preparatory paragraph with the following useful result.

Lemma 15
.

Let

be nonempty, let

, and let

be a family of cones of

{\left(\underset{i\in I}{\bigcap}K_{i}\right)}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}=\underset{i\in I}{\bigcap}\left({K_{i}}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}\right).

\displaystyle(x,y)\in{\left(\bigcap_{i\in I}K_{i}\right)}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}

3.1
Affine and
\{0\}
-convex functions

We extend the notion of affine functions to affine subsets of
\mathbb{E}
, see, e.g., Rockafellar
[
18
, §1]
for the standard case.

Definition 16
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{E}
be an affine set and let
x_{0}\in A
. Then a function
F\colon A\to\mathbb{E}_{2}
is said to be affine if there exists a linear map
L:\mathrm{par}\,A\mapsto\mathbb{E}_{2}
and a vector
b\in\mathbb{E}_{2}
such that, for all
x\in A
, we have
F(x)=L(x-x_{0})+b
for all
x\in A
.

Lemma 17
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{E}_{1}
be affine. Then
F\colon A\to\mathbb{E}_{2}
is affine if and only if

F(tx+(1-t)y)=tF(x)+(1-t)F(y)\quad\forall x,y\in A,\;t\in(0,1).

(6)

Proof.

Assume first that (
6
) holds. Discriminating the three cases
t\in[0,1]
,
t>1
and
t<0
, it is straightforward to show that, in fact, we have

F(tx+(1-t)y)=tF(x)+(1-t)F(y)\quad\forall x,y\in A,\;t\in\mathbb{R}.

(7)

Now let
x_{0}\in A
, i.e.
\mathrm{par}\,A=A-x_{0}
, and define
L:\mathrm{par}\,A\to\mathbb{E}_{2}
by
L(x):=F(x+x_{0})-F(x_{0})
.
Using (
7
), we find that
L(tx+(1-t)y)=tL(x)+(1-t)L(y)
for all
x,y\in\mathrm{par}\,A
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
Thus, taking
y=0
, as
L(0)=0
, gives
L(tx)=tL(x)
for all
x\in\mathrm{par}\,A
and all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Hence,
L(x+y)=L(\frac{1}{2}(2x)+\frac{1}{2}(2y))=\frac{1}{2}L(2x)+\frac{1}{2}L(2y)=L(x)+L(y)
, for all
x,y\in\mathrm{par}\,A
. This implies that
L
is linear. Hence, for all
x\in A
and
b:=F(x_{0})
, we have

F(x)=L(x-x_{0})+b.

Thus,
F
is affine.

Conversely, if
F
is affine, then we can write

F=L((\cdot)-x_{0})+b
for some linear map
L:\mathrm{par}\,A\to\mathbb{E}_{2}
,
x_{0}\in A
and
b\in\mathbb{E}_{2}
. Then

F(tx+(1-t)y)=t(L(x-x_{0})+b)+(1-t)(L(y-x_{0})+b)=tF(x)+(1-t)F(y),

for all
x,y\in A
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
. In particular, this is true for all
t\in(0,1)
.
∎

Proposition 18
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{E}_{1}
be nonempty convex. Then
F\colon D\to\mathbb{E}_{2}
is
\{0\}
-convex if and only if there exists an affine function
G\colon\mathrm{aff}\,D\to\mathbb{E}_{2}
such that
F=G_{|D}
.

Proof.

First, assume that
F\colon D\to\mathbb{E}_{2}
is
\{0\}
-convex and
z\in\mathrm{aff}\,D
.
By Lemma
1
, we can write
z=\alpha x-\beta y
, for some
\alpha,\beta\geqslant 0
,
\alpha-\beta=1
, and
x,y\in D
. Suppose
z
has two representations of this form, i.e.
z=\alpha x-\beta y=\alpha^{\prime}x^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime}y^{\prime}
, with
\alpha,\beta,\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}\geqslant 0
,
\alpha-\beta=\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime}=1
, and
x,y,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in D
.
Then
\Delta:=\alpha+\beta^{\prime}(=\alpha^{\prime}+\beta)=1+\beta+\beta^{\prime}>0
.
By convexity of
D
, we find that

\frac{\alpha}{\Delta}x+\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{\Delta}y^{\prime}=\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{\Delta}x^{\prime}+\frac{\beta}{\Delta}y\in D.

Using the
\{0\}
-convexity of
F
, we have

\frac{\alpha}{\Delta}F(x)+\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{\Delta}F(y^{\prime})=\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{\Delta}F(x^{\prime})+\frac{\beta}{\Delta}F(y).

Multiplying by
\Delta
and rearranging the above terms, we get

\alpha F(x)-\beta F(y)=\alpha^{\prime}F(x^{\prime})-\beta^{\prime}F(y^{\prime}).

Therefore, we the function
G:\mathrm{aff}\,D\to\mathbb{E}_{2},\;G(z)=\alpha F(x)-\beta F(y)
for
z\in\mathrm{aff}\,D
given by
z=\alpha x-\beta y
,
\alpha,\beta\geqslant 0
,
\alpha-\beta=1
,
x,y\in D
is well-defined.

Now, let
z
and
z^{\prime}
in
D
given by
z=\alpha x-\beta y
and
z^{\prime}=\alpha^{\prime}x^{\prime}-\beta y^{\prime}
, with
\alpha,\beta,\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}\geqslant 0
,
\alpha-\beta=1
,
\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime}=1
, and
x,y,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in D
. Let
t\in(0,1)
and set
p:=(1-t)z+tz^{\prime}
, as well as

a:=(1-t)\alpha+t\alpha^{\prime}
and
b:=(1-t)\beta+t\beta^{\prime}
. Then
a,b\geqslant 0
and
a-b=1
.

If
b=0
, then
\beta=\beta^{\prime}=0
and
\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}=1
, so
z=x\in D
and
z^{\prime}=x^{\prime}\in D
, and thus, using the
\{0\}
-convexity of
F
, we have
G(p)=F(p)=F((1-t)z+tz^{\prime})=(1-t)F(z)+tF(z^{\prime})=(1-t)G(z)+tG(z^{\prime})
.

If
b\neq 0
, then
\beta\neq 0
or
\beta^{\prime}\neq 0
, hence
a,b>0
. Then, we have

p=a\underbrace{\left[\frac{(1-t)\alpha}{a}x+\frac{t\alpha^{\prime}}{a}x^{\prime}\right]}_{\in D}-b\underbrace{\left[\frac{(1-t)\beta}{b}y+\frac{t\beta^{\prime}}{b}y^{\prime}\right]}_{\in D}.

Using this, and recalling the fact that
a,b\geqslant 0
and
a-b=1
, the definition of
G
yields

G(p)=aF\left(\frac{(1-t)\alpha}{a}x+\frac{t\alpha^{\prime}}{a}x^{\prime}\right)-bF\left(\frac{(1-t)\beta}{b}y+\frac{t\beta^{\prime}}{b}y^{\prime}\right).

As
F
is
\{0\}
-convex, we thus infer

\displaystyle G(p)

\displaystyle=a\frac{(1-t)\alpha}{a}F(x)+a\frac{t\alpha^{\prime}}{a}F(x^{\prime})-b\frac{(1-t)\beta}{b}F(y)-b\frac{t\beta^{\prime}}{b}F(y^{\prime})

\displaystyle=(1-t)\underbrace{[\alpha F(x)-\beta F(y)]}_{G(z)}+t\underbrace{[\alpha^{\prime}F(x^{\prime})-\beta^{\prime}F(y^{\prime})]}_{G(z^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=(1-t)G(z)+tG(z^{\prime}).

All in all, by Lemma
17
,
G
is affine.

Conversely, if there exists
G:\mathrm{aff}\,D\to\mathbb{E}_{2}
affine such that
F=G_{|D}
, then Lemma
17
yields

G(tx+(1-t)y)=tG(x)+(1-t)G(y)
for all
t\in(0,1)
and
x,y\in D
.
Hence

F(tx+(1-t)y)=tF(x)+(1-t)F(y)
for all
x,y\in D
,
t\in(0,1)
, and as
D
is a convex set,
F
is
\{0\}
-convex.

∎

As a simple corollary we get the following result.

Corollary 19
.

The
\{0\}
-convex functions
\mathbb{E}_{1}\to\mathbb{E}_{2}
are exactly the affine functions.

3.2
Convexity with respect to a (nontrivial) subspace

Using our study on
\{0\}
-convexity above, we are now in a position to investigate the functions which are convex w.r.t. a given (nontrivial) subspace. To this end, observe that for a subspace
U\subset\mathbb{E}
, the polar (and the dual) cone of

equal the orthogonal complement
U^{\perp}:=\left\{u\in\mathbb{E}\,\left|\;\left\langle u,\,y\right\rangle=0\;\forall y\in U\right.\right\}
.

Lemma 20
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{E}_{2}
be a nontrivial subspace and let
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{r}\}
be a basis of
U^{\perp}
.
Then for
F:D\subset\mathbb{E}_{1}\to\mathbb{E}_{2}
, with
D\subset\mathbb{E}_{1}
convex, the following are equivalent:

i)

F
is
U
-convex;

ii)

\left\langle u,\,F\right\rangle
is
\{0\}
-convex for all
u\in U^{\perp}
;

iii)

\left\langle e_{i},\,F\right\rangle
is
\{0\}
-convex for all
i=1,\dots,r
.

Proof.

‘i)
\Leftrightarrow
ii)’:
If
F
is
U
-convex and since
U^{\perp}
is a subspace, by Proposition
14
we find that both
\left\langle u,\,F\right\rangle
and
\left\langle-u,\,F\right\rangle
are convex for every
u\in U^{\perp}
. Therefore,
\left\langle u,\,F\right\rangle
is
\{0\}
-convex for all
u\in U^{\perp}
. In turn, if

\left\langle u,\,F\right\rangle
is
\{0\}
-convex for all
u\in U^{\perp}
, then in particular
\left\langle u,\,F\right\rangle
is (
\mathbb{R}_{+}
-)convex for all
u\in U^{\perp}
, so we can use Proposition
14
to conclude that
F
is
U
-convex.

‘ii)
\Leftrightarrow
iii)’: The implication ii)
\Rightarrow
iii) is trivial.
In turn, if iii) holds, let
u\in U^{\perp}
, i.e.
u=\sum_{i=1}^{r}u_{i}e_{i}
for some
u_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
. By assumption,
\left\langle e_{i},\,F\right\rangle\;(i=1,\dots,r)
are
\{0\}
-convex. Since
\left\langle u,\,F\right\rangle(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}u_{i}\left\langle e_{i},\,F\right\rangle(x)
for all
x\in\mathrm{dom}\,F
, we find that
\left\langle u,\,F\right\rangle
is
\{0\}
-convex.
∎

Proposition 21
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{E}_{2}
be a nontrivial subspace and let
F:D\subset\mathbb{E}_{1}\to\mathbb{E}_{2}
with
D
convex. Then
F
is
U
-convex if and only if there exists an affine map
G\colon\mathrm{aff}\,D\to\mathbb{E}_{2}
and a function
H\colon\mathrm{aff}\,D\to U
such that
G_{|D}+H_{|D}=F
.

Proof.

Suppose that
F
is
U
-convex and let
p:\mathbb{E}_{2}\to U
be the orthogonal projection onto
U
. Define

H:\mathrm{aff}\,D\to U,\quad H(x):=\begin{cases}p(F(x)),&x\in D,\\
0,&\text{else}.\end{cases}

Set
\hat{G}:=F-H_{|D}
. By Lemma
20
and Proposition
18
,
\left\langle u,\,F\right\rangle_{|D}
is the restriction of an affine function
f^{u}:\mathrm{aff}\,D\to\mathbb{R}
for all
u\in U^{\perp}
. However, as
H(x)\in U\;(x\in\mathbb{E}_{1})
, we have
\left\langle u,\,\hat{G}\right\rangle=\left\langle u,\,F\right\rangle
for all

. Now, let
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{r}\}
be an orthogonal basis of

. Then

. Then

is affine and

.

Conversely, suppose that there exists a function
H\colon\mathrm{aff}\,D\to U
and an affine map
G\colon\ \mathrm{aff}\,D\to\mathbb{E}_{2}
such that
F=G_{|D}+H_{|D}
. Then for
u\in U^{\perp}
we have
\left\langle u,\,F\right\rangle=\left\langle u,\,G\right\rangle_{|D}
, and thus
\left\langle u,\,F\right\rangle
is
\{0\}
-convex, as
\left\langle u,\,G\right\rangle
is affine on
\mathrm{aff}\,D\supset D
and
D
is convex. Thus, by Lemma
20
,
F
is
U
-convex.
∎

Lemma 28

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{|y_{i}|}{r}\right)(x+re_{1})+\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{|y_{i}|}{r}\right)(x-re_{1})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{|y_{i}|}{r}(x+\mbox{sgn}(y_{i})re_{i}).

4

This section is devoted to our main question as to when the closed convex hull of the graph of a function equals its
K

4.1
A characterization via the horizon cone

We commence this subsection with the central link between the graph and the
K
-epigraph of a function. To obtain an elegant proof we briefly tap into Fenchel conjugacy
[
18
]
. To this end, realize that every set
S\subset\mathbb{E}
is uniquely determined through its indicator function
\delta_{S}:\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
which is paired in duality with the
support function

\sigma_{S}:\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}

\sigma_{S}(x)=\sup_{y\in S}\left\langle x,\,y\right\rangle
via the conjugacy relations

\delta^{*}_{S}=\sigma_{S}=\sigma_{\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,S}
, hence
\sigma_{S}^{*}=\delta_{\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,S}
, and thus
\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,\delta_{S}=\delta_{\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,S}

Proposition 31
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{E}_{2}
be a closed, convex cone and let
F\colon\mathbb{E}_{1}\to\mathbb{E}_{2}^{\bullet}

K
-closed and
K

{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}=\mathrm{cl}\;\big(\overline{\mathrm{conv}\,}(\mathrm{gph}\,F)+\{0\}\times K\big).

Proof.

Using (
3
) and the set-additivity for support functions we have

\sigma_{{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}}=\sigma_{\mathrm{gph}\,F+\{0\}\times K}=\sigma_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}+\sigma_{\{0\}\times K}=\sigma_{\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,F)}+\delta_{\mathbb{E}_{1}\times K^{\circ}}.

Moreover, the assumptions on
F
imply that
{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}
is closed and convex, and hence

\delta_{{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}}=\big(\sigma_{\overline{\mathrm{conv}\,}(\mathrm{gph}\,F)}+\delta_{\mathbb{E}_{1}\times K^{\circ}}\big)^{*}=\mathrm{cl}\;\big(\delta_{\overline{\mathrm{conv}\,}(\mathrm{gph}\,F)}\mathbin{\mbox{\small$\square$}}\delta_{\{0\}\times K}\big)=\delta_{\mathrm{cl}\;\big(\overline{\mathrm{conv}\,}(\mathrm{gph}\,F)+\{0\}\times K\big)}.

Here the second identity uses
[
18

, while the third holds due to the identity
\delta_{A}\mathbin{\mbox{\small$\square$}}\delta_{B}=\delta_{A+B}
for any two sets.
∎

In turn, assume that
{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}=\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,F)
. Then by Theorem
34
we have
K\subset K^{F}
, and hence
\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,F)={K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}\subset{K^{F}}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}
. In addition,
\{0\}\times K^{F}\subset[\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,F)]^{\infty}
, by definition of
K^{F}
. Hence, using (
3
) and the horizon property of convex sets, we have

{K^{F}}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}=\mathrm{gph}\,F+\{0\}\times K^{F}\subset\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,F)+[\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,F)]^{\infty}=\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,F).

Thus,
{K^{F}}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}=\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,F)={K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}
and hence, by Lemma
9
, we already have
K^{F}=K
. Moreover, as
F
is
K
-convex and
K
-closed, we have
\hat{K}_{F}\subset K
, thus
{\hat{K}_{F}}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}\subset{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}=\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,F)
. Using the fact that
\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,F)\subset{\hat{K}_{F}}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}
(as
{\hat{K}_{F}}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}
is a closed convex set containing
\mathrm{gph}\,F
), we conclude that
{\hat{K}_{F}}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}={K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}
, hence, again by Lemma
9
,
K=\hat{K}_{F}
.
∎

4.2
Necessary conditions

In this subsection we discuss necessary conditions for
\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,F)={K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}
.

4.2.1
Necessary conditions on the dual cone

Proposition 36
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{E}_{2}
be a cone and
F:\mathbb{E}_{1}\to\mathbb{E}_{2}^{\bullet}
proper such that
{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}=\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,F)
. Then
K\subset-\hat{K}_{F}^{\circ}
.

Proof.

Let
u\in K\setminus\{0\}
.
By Theorem
34
, we have that
(0,u)\in[\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,F)]^{\infty}
. By Remark
33
there hence exist
\{(x_{k},y_{k})\in\mathrm{conv}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,F)\}
and
\{\lambda_{k}\}\downarrow 0
such that
\lambda_{k}(x_{k},y_{k})\to(0,u)
. With
\kappa:=\dim\mathbb{E}_{1}\times\mathbb{E}_{2}
and Carathéodory’s theorem
[
18
, Theorem 17.1]
, for
i=1,\dots,\kappa+1
, we find sequences
\{x_{k}^{i}\in\mathrm{dom}\,F\}_{k}
and
\{\alpha_{k}^{i}\}_{k}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{\kappa+1}\alpha_{k}^{i}=1
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
as well as

x_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{\kappa+1}\alpha_{k}^{i}x_{k}^{i}\and y_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{\kappa+1}\alpha_{k,j}^{i}F(x_{k,j}^{i})\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

Now let
t\geq 0
. Then
t_{k}:=t\frac{\lambda_{k}}{\|u\|^{2}}\downarrow 0
and

t_{k}x_{k}\to 0\and t_{k}\sum_{i=1}^{\kappa+1}\alpha_{k}^{i}\left\langle u,\,F\right\rangle(x_{k}^{i})=t\frac{\left\langle\lambda_{k}y_{k},\,u\right\rangle}{\|u\|^{2}}\to t.

Thus, for
t\geq 0
, we have
(0,t)\in[\mathrm{conv}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,\langle u,F\rangle)]^{\infty}=[\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,\langle u,F\rangle)]^{\infty}
. Hence
\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\subset[\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,\langle u,F\rangle)]^{\infty}
, so by Theorem
34
,
\mathrm{epi}\,\langle u,F\rangle=\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,\langle u,F\rangle)
. This means that
\mathrm{epi}\,\langle u,F\rangle
is closed and convex so that
\langle u,F\rangle\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E}_{1})
. Therefore
K\setminus\{0\}\in\{u\in\mathbb{E}_{2}\mid\langle u,F\rangle\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E}_{1})\}
, hence
K\subset\mathrm{cl}\;(\{u\in\mathbb{E}_{2}\mid\langle u,F\rangle\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E}_{1})\})=-\hat{K}_{F}^{\circ}
, by
Proposition
27
.
∎

We readily derive the following necessary condition on the dual cone.

Corollary 37
.

Under the assumptions of Proposition
36
, we have
K\subset-K^{\circ}
.

Proof.

By Corollary
35
,
K=\hat{K}_{F}
, and by Proposition
36
,
K\subset-\hat{K}_{F}^{\circ}=-K^{\circ}
.
∎

4.2.2
Affine majorization and minorization

For motivational purposes, we start this subsection with the scalar case (
K=\mathbb{R}_{+}
), where the question whether the closed convex hull of the graph of a function equals its
K
-epigraph can be fully answered via affine majorization. The proof relies, in essence, on a standard separation argument.

Theorem 38

(The scalar case)
.

Let
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E})
. Then
\mathrm{epi}\,f=\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,f)
if and only if
f
does not have an affine majorant on its domain.

Proof.

Suppose that there exists
(\bar{x},\bar{t})\in\mathrm{epi}\,f\backslash\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,f)
. In particular,
\bar{x}\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
, and by strong separation
[
18
, Corollary 11.4.2]
,
there exists
(s,\alpha)\in\mathbb{E}_{1}\times\mathbb{R}
such that

\left\langle s,\,\bar{x}\right\rangle+\alpha\bar{t}>\sup_{(x,t)\in\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,f)}\left\langle s,\,x\right\rangle+\alpha t.

(11)

Choosing
(x,t):=(\bar{x},f(\bar{x}))
, we find that

\alpha(\bar{t}-f(\bar{x}))>0,

and hence,
\alpha>0
. It then follows from (
11
) with
x\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
and
t=f(x)
that

\left\langle s/\alpha,\,\bar{x}-x\right\rangle+\bar{t}>f(x).

Thus
f
is majorized on its domain by the affine map
x\mapsto-\left\langle s/\alpha,\,x\right\rangle+\left\langle s/\alpha,\,\bar{x}\right\rangle+\bar{t}
, which proves one direction.

To prove the converse implication, suppose now that
f
has an affine majorant on its domain, i.e. there exists
(a,\beta)\in\mathbb{E}\times\mathbb{R}
, such that
f(x)\leq\left\langle a,\,x\right\rangle+\beta=:g(x)
for all
x\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
.
Now pick
\bar{x}\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
. Then
(\bar{x},g(\bar{x})+1)\in\mathrm{epi}\,f
, and it hence suffices to show that
(\bar{x},g(\bar{x})+1)\not\in\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,F)
. Assume, by contradiction, that
(\bar{x},g(\bar{x})+1)\in\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,F)
. Then with
\kappa:=\dim\mathbb{E}\times\mathbb{R}
, by Carathéodory’s theorem
[
18
, Theorem 17.1]
, for
i=1,\dots,\kappa+1
there exist
sequences
\{t_{i,k}\geq 0\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{x_{i}^{k}\in\mathrm{dom}\,f\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{\kappa+1}t_{i,k}=1
and

\sum_{i=1}^{\kappa+1}t_{i,k}(x_{i}^{k},f(x_{i}^{k}))\to_{k\to\infty}(\bar{x},g(\bar{x})+1)
. Consequently

g(\bar{x})+1=\lim_{k\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{\kappa+1}t_{i,k}f(x_{i}^{k})\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{\kappa+1}t_{i,k}g(x_{i}^{k})=\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\langle a,\,\sum_{i=1}^{\kappa+1}t_{i,k}x_{i}^{k}\right\rangle+\beta=g(\bar{x}),

which is the desired contradiction and thus concludes the proof.
∎

The questions as to what can be said when
f
in the above result is only proper and convex, but not necessarily closed is answered as the
opening to Section
4.3
.

To start our analysis of the vector-valued case we now formally introduce the notion of
K
-minorants and -majorants
, respectively.

Definition 39

(
K
-minorants/-majorants)
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{E}_{1}
be a cone, and let
F\colon\mathbb{E}_{1}\to\mathbb{E}_{2}^{\bullet}
be proper. A function
G:\mathbb{E}_{1}\to\mathbb{E}_{2}
is said to be:

•

a
K
-majorant of
F
on
S\subset\mathrm{dom}\,F
if

G(x)-F(x)\in K\quad\forall x\in S,

•

a
K
-minorant of
F
on
S\subset\mathrm{dom}\,F
if

F(x)-G(x)\in K\quad\forall x\in S.

For
S=\mathrm{dom}\,F
, we say that
G
is a
K
-minorant of
F
.

Naturally, in view of the scalar case from Theorem
38
, we are mainly interested in the case where
G
is an affine function.

For a function
F:\mathbb{E}_{1}\to\mathbb{E}_{2}^{\bullet}
, we record that, for a pointed, closed, convex cone
K
such that
{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}=\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,F)
, there cannot exist both an affine
K
-majorant on the domain and an affine
K
-minorant of
F
.

Proposition 40
.

Let
\{0\}\subsetneq K\subset\mathbb{E}_{2}
be a closed, convex, pointed cone and let
F\colon\mathbb{E}_{1}\to\mathbb{E}_{2}^{\bullet}
be proper. If
{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}=\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,F)
, then
F
cannot have both an affine
K
-majorant on its domain and an affine
K
-minorant.

Proof.

Assume, by contradiction, that there exist
T,L:\mathbb{E}_{1}\to\mathbb{E}_{2}
be linear and
u,w\in\mathbb{E}_{2}
be such that

T(x)+u\geqslant_{K}F(x)\geqslant_{K}L(x)+w,\quad\forall x\in\mathrm{dom}\,F.

We thus find that

\mathrm{gph}\,F\subset\left\{(x,y)\in\mathrm{dom}\,F\times\mathbb{E}_{2}\,\left|\;T(x)-y\in-u+K,\;y-L(x)\in w+K\right.\right\}:=C.

As
K
is closed and convex, so is
C
, and we hence deduce that
\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,F)\subset C
. Now pick
x\in\mathrm{dom}\,F
and
v\in K\setminus\{0\}
. Then

(x,F(x)+tv)\in{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}
for all
t>0
. Since
{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}=\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,F)\subset C
, it follows that

T(x)-F(x)-tv\in-u+K\and F(x)+tv-L(x)\in w+K\quad\forall t>0.

Dividing by
t
and letting
t\to+\infty
we get

v\in K\cap(-K)=\{0\},

which contradicts the choice of
v
, and therefore proves the statement.
∎

It is well known that a proper, convex function posseses an affine (
\mathbb{R}_{+}
-)minorant
[
1
, Theorem 9.20]
. In the vector-valued setting, we can fall back on this result to get affine
K
-minorants for proper
K
-convex functions when
K
is a particular polyhedral cone.

Proposition 41
.

Let
K=\left\{x\in\mathbb{E}_{2}\,\left|\;\left\langle b_{i},\,x\right\rangle\geq 0\quad\forall i=1,\dots,m\right.\right\}
with
b_{1},\dots,b_{m}
linearly independent.
If
F:\mathbb{E}_{1}\to\mathbb{E}_{2}^{\bullet}
is
K
-convex and proper, then
F
has an affine
K
-minorant.

Proof.

It holds that
-K^{\circ}=\mathrm{cone}\,\{b_{1},\dots,b_{m}\}
, see e.g.
[
19
, Lemma 6.45]
. This cone is pointed by linear independence of
\{b_{1},\dots,b_{m}\}
, hence
-K
has nonempty interior, see
[
19
, Exercise 6.22]
.
Now, in view of Proposition
14
a), for all
i=1,\dots,m
, the functions
\left\langle b_{i},\,F\right\rangle:\mathbb{E}_{1}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
are proper, convex and hence, see e.g.
[
1
, Theorem 9.20]
, there exist
(c_{i},\delta_{i})\in\mathbb{E}_{1}\times\mathbb{R}\;(i=1,\dots,m)
such that

\left\langle b_{i},\,F(x)\right\rangle\geq\left\langle c_{i},\,x\right\rangle+\delta_{i}\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{E}_{1},i=1,\dots,m.

(12)

Now, let
A:\mathbb{E}_{1}\to\mathbb{E}_{2}
be linear such that
A(b_{i})=c_{i}
for all
i=1,\dots,m
and let
w\in-\mathrm{int}\,K.
Then
\left\langle w,\,b_{i}\right\rangle<0
for all
i=1,\dots,m
, cf.
[
19
, Exercise 6.22]
. By positive homogeneity (and since
\mathrm{int}\,K
is a pre-cone), there hence exists
\bar{w}\in-\mathrm{int}\,K
with
\left\langle\bar{w},\,b_{i}\right\rangle<\delta_{i}
for all
i=1,\dots,m
. Finally, with
\bar{L}:=A^{*}
it hence follows

\left\langle b_{i},\,F(x)\right\rangle\geq\left\langle c_{i},\,x\right\rangle+\delta_{i}\geq\left\langle b_{i},\,\bar{L}(x)\right\rangle+\left\langle\bar{w},\,b_{i}\right\rangle\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{E}_{1},i=1,\dots,m.

Therefore, for all
x\in\mathrm{dom}\,F
, we have
F(x)-(\bar{L}(x)+\bar{w})\in K
, and
x\mapsto\bar{L}(x)+\bar{w}
is the desired affine
K
-minorant.
∎

4.3
Sufficient conditions

In this subsection we are primarily concerned with sufficient conditions. We start with some considerations in the scalar case.

Lemma 42
.

Let
f\in\Gamma(\mathbb{E})
. Then the following hold:

a)

\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,\mathrm{cl}\;f)=\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,f_{|{\mathrm{ri}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,f)}})
.

b)

\phi:\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{R}
is an affine majorant of
\mathrm{cl}\;f
on
\mathrm{dom}\,(\mathrm{cl}\;f)
if and only if
\phi
is affine majorant of
f
on
\mathrm{ri}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,f)
.

c)

If
f
is
(\mathrm{dom}\,f)
-closed (hence
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathrm{dom}\,f)
), then
\phi:\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{R}
is an affine majorant of
\mathrm{cl}\;f
on
\mathrm{dom}\,(\mathrm{cl}\;f)
if and only if
\phi
is affine majorant of
f
on
\mathrm{dom}\,f
.

Proof.

a) As
f(x)=\mathrm{cl}\;f(x)
for all
x\in\mathrm{ri}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,f)
(
[
18
, Theorem 7.4]
), we have
\mathrm{gph}\,f_{|{\mathrm{ri}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,f)}}\subset\mathrm{gph}\,\mathrm{cl}\;f
, and hence
\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,f_{|{\mathrm{ri}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,f)}})\subset\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,f)
. To prove the converse inclusion let
(x,\mathrm{cl}\;f(x))\in\mathrm{gph}\,\mathrm{cl}\;f
. Invoking
[
18
, Theorem 7.5]
(and
[
18
, Theorem 6.1]
), we find a sequence
\{x_{k}\in\mathrm{ri}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,f)\}\to x
with
f(x_{k})\to\mathrm{cl}\;f(x)
. Therefore,
\mathrm{gph}\,\mathrm{cl}\;f\subset\mathrm{cl}\;(\mathrm{gph}\,f_{|{\mathrm{ri}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,f)}})\subset\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,f_{|{\mathrm{ri}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,f)}})
, and hence, the desired inclusion follows by applying the
\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,
-operator on both sides.

b) If
\phi
is an affine majorant of
\mathrm{cl}\;f
on
\mathrm{dom}\,(\mathrm{cl}\;f)
, then
\phi
is an affine majorant of
\mathrm{cl}\;f
on
\mathrm{ri}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,f)\subset\mathrm{dom}\,(\mathrm{cl}\;f)
, and hence an affine majorant of
f
on
\mathrm{ri}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,f)
, since
f
and
\mathrm{cl}\;f
coincide on
\mathrm{ri}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,f)
. In turn, if
\phi
is an affine majorant of
f
on
\mathrm{ri}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,f)
, then for all
x\in\mathrm{dom}\,(\mathrm{cl}\;f)
, since
\mathrm{ri}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,(\mathrm{cl}\;f))=\mathrm{ri}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,f)
(see
[
18
, Corollary 7.4.1]
), by
[
18
, Theorem 7.5]
(and
[
18
, Theorem 6.1]
), there exists
\{x_{k}\in\mathrm{ri}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,(\mathrm{cl}\;f))\}\to x
with
\lim_{k}f(x_{k})=\mathrm{cl}\;f(x_{k})
. However
\phi(x_{k})\geqslant f(x_{k})
and
\phi
is continuous so
\phi(x)\geqslant\mathrm{cl}\;f(x)
, thus
\phi
is an affine majorant of
\mathrm{cl}\;f
on
\mathrm{dom}\,\mathrm{cl}\;f
.

c) By Lemma
3
,
f(x)=\mathrm{cl}\;f(x)
for all
x\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
. Therefore, if
\phi(x)\geq\mathrm{cl}\;f(x)
for all
x\in\mathrm{dom}\,(\mathrm{cl}\;f)\supset\mathrm{dom}\,f
, then
\phi(x)\geq f(x)
for all
x\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
. In turn, if
\phi
is an affine minorant of
f
on
\mathrm{dom}\,f\supset\mathrm{ri}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,f)
, then b) shows that
\phi
is an affine minorant of
\mathrm{cl}\;f
on
\mathrm{dom}\,(\mathrm{cl}\;f)
.

∎

We record some immediate consequences of the foregoing result.

Corollary 43
.

Let
f\in\Gamma(\mathbb{E})
. Then the following are equivalent:

i)

\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,f_{|{\mathrm{ri}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,f)}})=\mathrm{cl}\;(\mathrm{epi}\,f)
;

ii)

f
has no affine majorant on
\mathrm{ri}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,f)
;

iii)

\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\subset[\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,f_{|{\mathrm{ri}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,f)}})]^{\infty}
.

Proof.

Observe that
\mathrm{epi}\,(\mathrm{cl}\;f)=\mathrm{cl}\;(\mathrm{epi}\,f)
, hence by Lemma
42
a) we have

i)\iff\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,\mathrm{cl}\;f)=\mathrm{epi}\,(\mathrm{cl}\;f).

(13)

’i)
\Leftrightarrow
ii)’: By Lemma
42
b), we have that ii) is equivalent to saying that
\mathrm{cl}\;f
has no affine minorant on its domain. Therefore, the desired equivalence follows with (
13
) from Proposition
38
applied to
\mathrm{cl}\;f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E})
.

’i)
\Leftrightarrow
iii)’: Apply Theorem
34
to
\mathrm{cl}\;f
and use (
13
).
∎

Corollary 44
.

Let
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathrm{dom}\,f)
. Then the following are equivalent:

i)

\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,f)=\mathrm{cl}\;(\mathrm{epi}\,f)
;

ii)

f
has no affine majorants on its domain;

iii)

\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\subset[\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,f)]^{\infty}
.

Proof.

We observe that
f\in\Gamma(\mathbb{E})
(by definition of
\Gamma_{0}(\mathrm{dom}\,f)
), and that
f(x)=\mathrm{cl}\;f(x)
for all
x\in\mathrm{dom}\,f
, by Lemma
3
. In addition, by Lemma
42
, we have
\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,f|_{\mathrm{ri}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,f)})=\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,\mathrm{cl}\;f)
. Thus, we have

\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,f)\subset\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,\mathrm{cl}\;f)=\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,f|_{\mathrm{ri}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,f)})\subset\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,f),

and hence
\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,f)=\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,f|_{\mathrm{ri}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,f)})
. Consequently

i)\iff\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,f_{|{\mathrm{ri}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,f)}})=\mathrm{cl}\;(\mathrm{epi}\,f),

and

Moreover, with Lemma
42
we find that

ii)\iff f\;\text{has no affine majorant on}\;\mathrm{ri}\,(\mathrm{dom}\,f).

Therefore, the claimed equivalences follow from Corollary
43
.

∎

We now establish sufficient conditions in the vector-valued case, building on the results provided above. We start with the most general result, and then successively tighten the assumptions to obtain (weaker but) more handy conditions.

Lemma 45
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{E}_{2}
be a (nontrivial) closed, convex cone with
K\subset-K^{\circ}
, and let
F\colon\mathbb{E}_{1}\to\mathbb{E}_{2}^{\bullet}
be proper,
K
-closed and
K
-convex. Assume that the following hold:

i)

There is a nonempty set
L\subset K\cap\mathrm{rge}\,F
such that
\overline{\mathrm{cone}\,}L=K
;

ii)

For every
u\in L
, there exists a convex set
C^{u}\subset\mathrm{dom}\,F
such that
F(C^{u})\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}u
;

iii)

For all
u\in L
we have
f_{u}:=\langle u,F\rangle+\delta_{C^{u}}\in\Gamma_{0}(C^{u})
and it has no affine majorant on its domain.

Then
{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}=\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,F)
. In particular,
K=\hat{K}_{F}
.

Proof.

Let
u\in L\setminus\{0\}
, and let us prove that
(0,u)\in[\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,F)]^{\infty}
. As the latter is a cone, we may assume w.l.o.g that
\|u\|=1
. By iii),
f_{u}\in\Gamma_{0}(C^{u})
, hence Corollary
44
yields that
\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\subset[\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,f_{u})]^{\infty}
. As
\mathrm{conv}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,f_{u})
is convex, we hence have
(0,t)+\mathrm{conv}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,f_{u})\subset\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,f_{u})
for all
t\geq 0
.

Now, let
(x,r)\in\mathrm{conv}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,f_{u})
. Hence, with
\kappa:=\dim\mathbb{E}\times\mathbb{R}
, there there exist convex combinations
x=\sum_{i=1}^{\kappa+1}\alpha_{i}x^{i}
and
r=\sum_{u=1}^{\kappa+1}\alpha_{i}\left\langle u,\,F\right\rangle(x^{i})
with
x^{i}\in C^{u}\;(i=1,\dots,\kappa+1)
. By ii), there exists
\gamma\geqslant 0
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{\kappa+1}\alpha^{i}F(x^{i})=\gamma u,
and thus
\gamma=\left\langle u,\,\gamma u\right\rangle=r
. Consequently,
(x,ru)\in\mathrm{conv}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,F)
.

Moreover, we have
(x,r+t)\in\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,f_{u})
for all
t\geq 0
, hence

x=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{\kappa+1}\alpha^{i,n}_{t}x^{i,n}_{t}\and r+t=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{\kappa+1}\alpha^{i,n}_{t}\left\langle u,\,F\right\rangle(x^{i,n}_{t})

for certain
x^{i,n}_{t}\in C^{u}
and
\alpha^{i,n}_{t}\geq 0\;(i=1,\dots,\kappa+1)
with
\sum_{i=1}^{\kappa+1}\alpha^{i,n}=1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
However, by ii), there exist
\gamma_{t}^{n}\geqslant 0\;(n\in\mathbb{N})
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{\kappa+1}\alpha^{i,n}_{t}F(x^{i,n}_{t})=\gamma_{t}^{n}u\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

Thus

\gamma_{t}^{n}=\left\langle u,\,\sum_{i=1}^{\kappa+1}\alpha^{i,n}_{t}F(x^{i,n}_{t})\right\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{\kappa+1}\alpha^{i,n}_{t}\left\langle u,\,F\right\rangle(x^{i,n}_{t})\to r+t.

Thus,
(x,ru+tu)=(x,(r+t)u)\in\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,F)
for all
t\geqslant 0
. Now, for all
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathrm{conv}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,F)
, and for all
s\geqslant 0
, we have

(\bar{x},\bar{y}+su)=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}(1-\varepsilon)(\bar{x},\bar{y})+\varepsilon(x,ru+(s/\varepsilon)u).

However, by what was argued above,
(x,ru+(s/\varepsilon)u)\in\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,F)
for all
\varepsilon>0
, and we picked
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathrm{conv}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,F)\subset\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,F)
. By convexity and closedness , it follows that

(\bar{x},\bar{y}+su)\in\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,F)\quad\forall(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathrm{conv}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,F),\;s\geq 0.

Thus
(0,u)\in[\mathrm{conv}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,F)]^{\infty}
for all
u\in L\setminus\{0\}
as desired.
For
u=0
this is trivially true, hence, altogether we find that
\{0\}\times L\subset[\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,F)]^{\infty}
. But as
[\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,F)]^{\infty}
is a closed convex cone, we have then

\overline{\mathrm{cone}\,}(\{0\}\times L)=\{0\}\times K\subset[\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,F)]^{\infty},
cf. i). Thus, by Theorem
34
, we have
{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}=\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,F)
.
∎

To wrap up this section we want to provide a simplified version of Lemma
45
with more restrictive, but less arduous assumptions. To this end, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 47
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{E}_{2}
be a closed, convex cone with
K\subset-K^{\circ}
, and let
F\colon\mathbb{E}_{1}\to\mathbb{E}_{2}^{\bullet}
be proper,
K
-convex and
K
-closed. Then
\left\langle u,\,F\right\rangle\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E}_{1})
for all
u\in\mathrm{ri}\,K
.

Proof.

We observe from
[
17
, Corollary 7.4(ii)]
that
\left\langle u,\,F\right\rangle\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E}_{1})
for all
u\in\mathrm{ri}\,(-K^{\circ})
. Thus if
\mathrm{ri}\,K\subset\mathrm{ri}\,(-K^{\circ})
there is nothing to prove.

Hence, we only need to consider the case
\mathrm{ri}\,K\nsubseteq\mathrm{ri}\,(-K^{\circ})
. Since we assume that
K\subset-K^{\circ}
, by the definition of the relative topology, this can only hold, if
\mathrm{aff}\,K\varsubsetneq\mathrm{aff}\,(K^{\circ})
. We note that both of these sets contain
0
, and hence are subspaces of
\mathbb{E}_{2}
. In particular, the orthogonal projection
p:\mathbb{E}_{2}\to\mathbb{E}_{2}
onto
\mathrm{aff}\,K
(which is ordered by
K
) is a linear self-adjoint operator. We define
G:\mathbb{E}_{1}\to(\mathrm{aff}\,K)^{\bullet}
by

G(x):=\begin{cases}p(F(x)),&x\in\mathrm{dom}\,F,\\
+\infty_{\bullet},&\text{else.}\end{cases}

Note that for all
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
x,y\in\mathrm{dom}\,F
, we have

\alpha F(x)+(1-\alpha)F(y)-F(\alpha x+(1-\alpha)y)\in K.

Hence, as
K\subset\mathrm{aff}\,K
and by linearity of
p
, for all
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
x,y\in\mathrm{dom}\,F=\mathrm{dom}\,G
, we have

\alpha F(x)+(1-\alpha)F(y)-F(\alpha x+(1-\alpha)y)=\alpha G(x)+(1-\alpha)G(y)-G(\alpha x+(1-\alpha)y).

Therefore,
G
is
K
-convex. Moreover, if we denote
D:=\mathrm{dom}\,G
and
H:=F-G:D\to\mathrm{aff}\,(K)
, then

\alpha H(x)+(1-\alpha)H(y)-H(\alpha x+(1-\alpha)y)=0\quad\forall x,y\in D,\;\alpha\in(0,1).

Hence,
H
is
\{0\}
-convex, and by Proposition
18
, there exists an affine function
\hat{H}:\mathbb{E}_{1}\to\mathrm{aff}\,K
such that
\hat{H}_{|D}=H
.

Now, let
\{(x_{k},z_{k})\in{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,G}]\}\to(x,z)\in\mathbb{E}_{1}\times\mathrm{aff}\,K
. Then, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
x_{k}\in\mathrm{dom}\,G
, and there exists
v_{k}\in K
such that

z_{k}=G(x_{k})+v_{k}=F(x_{k})-H(x_{k})+v_{n}=F(x_{k})-\hat{H}(x_{k})+v_{k}.

As
\hat{H}:\mathbb{E}_{1}\to\mathrm{aff}\,K
is affine, it is continuous, so
\hat{H}(x_{k})\to\hat{H}(x)
, and thus
F(x_{k})+v_{k}\to z+\hat{H}(x)
. Therefore
\{(x_{k},F(x_{k})+v_{k})\in{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}\}\to(x,z+\hat{H}(x))
. As
F
is
K
-closed, we have
(x,z+\hat{H}(x))\in{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}
, thus
x\in\mathrm{dom}\,F=\mathrm{dom}\,G
,
\hat{H}(x)=H(x)
, and
z+H(x)-F(x)\in K
, so
z-G(x)\in K
and
(x,z)\in{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,G}
. This proves that
G
is
K
-closed.

Let
K^{\prime}
be the dual cone of
K
in
\mathrm{aff}\,K
. As
K\subset-K^{\circ}
by assumption, we consequently obtain

K\subset-K^{\circ}\cap\mathrm{aff}\,K=K^{\prime}\subset\mathrm{aff}\,K.

Hence,
\mathrm{ri}\,K\subset\mathrm{ri}\,K^{\prime}
. Moreover, as
G:\mathbb{E}_{1}\to(\mathrm{aff}\,K)^{\bullet}
is proper,
K
-closed and
K
-convex, by
[
17
, Corollary 7.4(ii)]
, we have
\left\langle u,\,G\right\rangle\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E}_{1})
for all
u\in\mathrm{ri}\,K^{\prime}
. But for any
u\in\mathrm{ri}\,K\subset\mathrm{aff}\,K
, as
p
is self-adjoint, we have
\left\langle u,\,G\right\rangle=\left\langle u,\,p(F)\right\rangle=\left\langle u,\,F\right\rangle
. Thus, for any
u\in\mathrm{ri}\,K
we have
\left\langle u,\,F\right\rangle\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E}_{1})
.
∎

Proposition 48
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{E}_{2}
be a proper, closed, convex cone such that

and let

4.4.3
The inverse matrix mapping

We consider the map

F:\mathbb{S}^{n}_{++}\to\mathbb{S}^{n},\quad F(X)=X^{-1}.

(16)

Proposition 51
.

Let
F
be given by (
16
). Then the following hold:

a)

\hat{K}_{F}=K_{F}=\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
.

b)

F
is
\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}
-convex and -closed.

c)

\mathrm{\overline{conv}}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,F)={\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}
.

Proof.

a) By Proposition
24
b), we know that
-K_{F}^{\circ}=\left\{V\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\,\left|\;\left\langle V,\,F\right\rangle\;\text{convex}\right.\right\}
. Now let
V\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
and observe that
\nabla\left\langle V,\,F\right\rangle(X)=-X^{-1}VX^{-1}
for all
X\succ 0
. Therefore, for all
X,Y\succ 0

\displaystyle\left\langle V,\,F\right\rangle(X)-\left\langle V,\,F\right\rangle(Y)+\left\langle\nabla\left\langle V,\,F\right\rangle(X),\,Y-X\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}\,(VX^{-1})-\mathrm{tr}\,(VY^{-1})-\mathrm{tr}\,(X^{-1}VX^{-1}(Y-X))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathrm{tr}\,(V(Y^{-1}+X^{-1}YX^{-1}))

For
\left\langle V,\,F\right\rangle
to be convex, by the gradient inequality, it is therefore necessary and sufficient that

\mathrm{tr}\,(V(Y^{-1}+X^{-1}YX^{-1})\geq 0\quad\forall X,Y\succ 0.

Since the trace of a product of two positive semidefinite matrices is nonnegative, it follows that
V\succeq 0
will be sufficient. On the other hand, let
V\not\succeq 0
and let

be the spectral decomposition with
q_{1},\dots,q_{n}
an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\lambda_{n}<0

Put

are orthonormal, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}\,(V(Y_{t}^{-1}+X_{t}^{-1}Y_{t}X_{t}^{-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\mathrm{tr}\,\left(V\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}q_{i}q_{i}^{T}+tq_{n}q_{n}^{T}+\frac{1}{t}I\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\left[\mathrm{tr}\,\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\lambda_{i}q_{i}q_{i}^{T}\right)+\mathrm{tr}\,(t\lambda_{n}q_{n}q_{n}^{T})+\mathrm{tr}\,\left(\frac{1}{t}V\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\lambda_{i}+t\lambda_{n}+\frac{\mathrm{tr}\,(V)}{t}\right]

\displaystyle\overset{t\to\infty}{\to}

\displaystyle-\infty.

Therefore
-K_{F}^{\circ}=\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}

b) Follows from a).

c) Firs note that
\mathrm{rge}\,F=\mathbb{S}^{n}_{++}=\mathrm{ri}\,\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}

0&-\alpha\end{array}\right]\mid\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\right\}
which is a subspace, hence nonempty and closed, and convex. Then
C^{b_{1}}\subset\lambda^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}b_{1})
and we have

\langle b_{1},\lambda\rangle\left(\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\alpha&0\\
0&-\alpha\end{array}\right]\right)=2|\alpha|
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
.
Therefore
\left\langle b_{1},\,\lambda\right\rangle+\delta_{C^{b_{1}}}\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
and has no affine majorant on its domain
C^{b_{1}}
. Therefore Proposition
46
yields the desired implication.

’iii)
\Rightarrow
i)’: Corollary
37
.
∎

5
Convex-convex composite functions

We start this section with the definition of
K
-increasing functions.

Definition 54

(
K
-increasing functions)
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{E}
be a cone. The function
g:\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is said to be
K
-increasing if

y\geq_{K}x\quad\Longrightarrow\quad g(y)\geq g(x)\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{E}.

It is well known and explored extensively in the literature
[
10
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
that, given
K\subset\mathbb{E}
, a
K
-increasing function
g\in\Gamma(\mathbb{E}_{2})
and a
K
-convex function
F:D\subset\mathbb{E}_{1}\to\mathbb{E}_{2}
, the composition

g\circ F:\mathbb{E}_{1}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\},\quad(g\circ F)(x):=\begin{cases}g(F(x)),&x\in D,\\
+\infty,&\text{else}\end{cases}

(18)

is convex (and proper if and only if
F(\mathrm{dom}\,F)\cap\mathrm{dom}\,g\neq\emptyset
). One of the questions we address in this section is the following: given
g\in\Gamma(\mathbb{E}_{2})
and
F:D\subset\mathbb{E}_{1}\to\mathbb{E}_{2}
such that
g\circ F
is convex, under which conditions does there exist a (closed) cone
K
such that
F
is
K
-convex and
g
is
K
-increasing?

5.1
The horizon cone of a closed, proper, convex function

For a proper function
f\colon\mathbb{E}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
, its
horizon function

f^{\infty}:\mathbb{E}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is defined via

\mathrm{epi}\,f^{\infty}=(\mathrm{epi}\,f)^{\infty}
.
The
horizon cone

\mathrm{hzn}\,f
of
f
is the level set

\mathrm{hzn}\,f:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{E}\,\left|\;f^{\infty}(x)\leq 0\right.\right\}.

For
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E})
the horizon function and horizon cone of
f
coincide with the respective
recession
objects
[
18
, Chapter 8]
.
We summarize some fundamental properties of the horizon cone of a closed, proper, convex function.

Proposition 55
.

Let
g\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E})
. Then the following hold:

a)

g^{\infty}
is closed, proper, convex and positively homogenous.

b)

We have

g^{\infty}(u)=\sup_{t>0}\frac{g(x+tu)-g(x)}{t}\quad\forall x\in\mathrm{dom}\,g.

In particular, we have

u\in\mathrm{hzn}\,g\iff g(x+tu)\leqslant g(x)\quad\forall x\in\mathrm{dom}\,g,t>0.

c)

\mathrm{hzn}\,g
is a closed convex cone.

d)

g
is
(-\mathrm{hzn}\,f)
-increasing.

e)

K
is a cone with respect to which
f
is increasing if and only if
K\subset-\mathrm{hzn}\,f
.

Proof.

a),b) See
[
19
, Theorem 3.12]
.

c) From a) and the definition of
\mathrm{hzn}\,f
.

d) See
[
10
, Lemma 7]
or
[
16
, Corollary 3.1]
.

e) See
[
16
, Proposition 3.2]
.
∎

The next example shows that the convexity in part b) is essential, which also shows that the horizon function is not the recession function (see
[
16
]
) without convexity.

Example 56
.

Consider
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

f(x)=\begin{cases}1+x,&x<-1,\\
0,&x\in[-1,1],\\
1-x,&x>1.\end{cases}

Then
f
is continuous (hence proper and lsc), but not convex, and it holds that
f^{\infty}(u)=-|u|
.
Moreover, for any
u\in\mathbb{R}
,
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R},t>0}\frac{f(x+tu)-f(x)}{t}=|u|
. Thus
f^{\infty}(u)\neq\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R},t>0}\frac{f(x+tu)-f(x)}{t}
.

5.2
The
K
-increasing case

The next proposition characterizes the situation where there exists a cone with respect which
F:D\to\mathbb{E}_{2}
is convex and
g\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E}_{2})
is increasing. At this, the cone
K_{F}
, the smallest closed cone with respect to which
F
is convex, comes in to play, which ties our study here to our results from Section

.

Proposition 57
.

Let
g\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E}_{2})
and
F:D\to\mathbb{E}_{2}^{\bullet}
with
D\subset\mathbb{E}_{1}
convex such that
g\circ F
is proper. Then the following statements are equivalent.

i)

There exists a cone
K
such that
g
is
K
-increasing and
F
is
K
-convex;

ii)

g
is
K_{F}
-increasing;

iii)

K_{F}\subset-\mathrm{hzn}\,g
;

iv)

(\mathrm{hzn}\,g)^{\circ}\subset-K_{F}^{\circ}
.

Proof.

We only (need to) show that i), ii) and iii) are equivalent. The equivalence of iii) and iv) follows from (bi)polarization and the fact that both cones in play are closed and convex.

i)
\Rightarrow
ii) : Let
K\subset\mathbb{E}_{2}
such that
F
is
K
-convex and
g
is
K
-increasing. In particular, by Proposition
55
e), we have
K\subset-\mathrm{hzn}\,g
, and thus

is
(-\mathrm{hzn}\,g)
-convex and
g
is
(-\mathrm{hzn}\,g)
-increasing, by Proposition
55
d). As
(-\mathrm{hzn}\,g)
is closed and convex, see Proposition
55
c), by definition of
K_{F}
we have

K_{F}\subset-\mathrm{hzn}\,g
. By Proposition
55
e) we find that
g
is
K_{F}
-increasing.

ii)
\Rightarrow
iii): From Proposition
55
e).

iii)
\Rightarrow
i): Let
K:=K_{F}
. Clearly,
F
is
K
-convex and, by Proposition
55
e),
g
is
K
-increasing.
∎

Proposition
57
yields the following concrete example.

Example 58
.

Consider
g\colon(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mapsto|x|
and
F\colon(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mapsto(x^{2},y)
. Hence

and

. Thus,

-increasing, and consequently, by Proposition

We close out by remarking that, if

\phi=g\circ F

5.3
Beyond
K
-monotonicity

It was already observed by Pennanen
[
17
]
and Burke et al.
[
10
]
that, in order to obtain convexity of the composition
g\circ F
in (
18
),
the assumption that
g
be
K
-increasing can be weakened to

g(F(x))\leq g(y)\quad\forall(x,y)\in{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F},

(19)

in which case we call
g
increasing w.r.t
{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}
.
Concretely, the following result holds.

Proposition 59

(
[
10
, Proposition 1]
)
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{E}_{2}
be a convex cone such that that
F:\mathbb{E}_{1}\to\mathbb{E}_{2}^{\bullet}
is
K
-convex and such that
g\in\Gamma(\mathbb{E}_{1})
is increasing w.r.t
{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}
in the sense of (
19
). Then
g\circ F
is convex.

The next proposition gives a characterization of the situation where there exists a closed convex cone
K
such that
g\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E}_{1})
is increasing w.r.t
{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}
and
F
is
K
-convex.

Proposition 60
.

Let
g\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathbb{E}_{2})
and
F:D\to\mathbb{E}_{2}
for
D\subset\mathbb{E}_{1}
(nonempty convex) such that
g\circ F
is proper. Then there exists a closed (convex) cone
K
such that
g
is increasing w.r.t
{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}
(in the sense of (
19
)) and
F
is
K
-convex if and only if
g
is increasing w.r.t
{K_{F}}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
g
is increasing w.r.t
{K_{F}}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}
and set
K:=K_{F}
. Then
K
is closed and convex and
F
is
K
-convex and
g
is increasing w.r.t
{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}
(by assumption).

Conversely, suppose now that there exists a closed (convex) cone
K
such that
g
is increasing w.r.t
{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}
and
F
is
K
-convex. As
K
is closed and
F
is
K
-convex, we have
K_{F}\subset K
, by definition of

. Therefore

{K_{F}}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}=\mathrm{gph}\,F+\{0\}\times K_{F}\subset\mathrm{gph}\,F+\{0\}\times K={K_{F}}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}
, and thus
g
is increasing w.r.t
{K_{F}}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}
.

∎

It turns out that, in Proposition
59
, the assumption that
g
be increasing w.r.t. to
{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}
can even be further weakened substituting
\mathrm{conv}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,F)
for
{K}\mbox{-}\mathrm{epi}{\,F}
by which, again, ties our considerations here to our previous study.

Proposition 61
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{E}_{1}
be (nonempty) convex,
F:D\to\mathbb{E}_{2}
, and let
g\in\Gamma(\mathbb{E}_{2})
be increasing w.r.t.
\mathrm{conv}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,F)
, i.e.

g(F(x))\leq g(y)\quad\forall(x,y)\in\mathrm{conv}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,F).

(20)

Then
g\circ F
is convex.

Proof.

Let
x,y\in\mathrm{dom}\,(g\circ F)
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Then
(\alpha x+(1-\alpha)y,\alpha F(x)+(1-\alpha)F(y))\in\mathrm{conv}\,(\mathrm{gph}\,F)
. By (
20
),and the convexity of
g
we find

g(F(\alpha x+(1-\alpha)y))\leq g(\alpha F(x)+(1-\alpha)F(y))\leq\alpha g(F(x))+(1-\alpha)g(F(y)).

Hence
g\circ F
is convex.
∎

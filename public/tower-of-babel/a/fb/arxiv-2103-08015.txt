Chebyshev-Fibonacci polynomial relations using generating functions


Abstract
The main object of the paper is to reveal connections between Chebyshev polynomials of the first and second kinds and Fibonacci polynomials introduced by Catalan. This is achieved by relating the respective (ordinary and exponential) generating functions to each other. As a consequence, we also establish new combinatorial identities for balancing polynomials and Fibonacci (Lucas) numbers.

1. Introduction

For any integer
n\geq 0
, the Chebyshev polynomials
\{T_{n}(x)\}_{n\geq 0}
of the first kind are defined by the second-order recurrence relation
[
14
]

T_{0}(x)=1,\quad T_{1}(x)=x,\quad T_{n+1}(x)=2xT_{n}(x)-T_{n-1}(x),

(1)

while the Chebyshev polynomials
\{U_{n}(x)\}_{n\geq 0}
of the second kind are defined by

U_{0}(x)=1,\quad U_{1}(x)=2x,\quad U_{n+1}(x)=2xU_{n}(x)-U_{n-1}(x).

(2)

If we denote
\alpha(x)=x+\sqrt{x^{2}-1}
and
\beta(x)=x-\sqrt{x^{2}-1}
, then we have

\displaystyle T_{n}(x)=\frac{\alpha^{n}(x)+\beta^{n}(x)}{2}=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor{n}/{2}\rfloor}{n\choose 2k}(x^{2}-1)^{k}x^{n-2k},

\displaystyle U_{n}(x)=\frac{\alpha^{n+1}(x)-\beta^{n+1}(x)}{2\sqrt{x^{2}-1}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor{n}/{2}\rfloor}{n+1\choose 2k+1}(x^{2}-1)^{k}x^{n-2k}.

Fibonacci polynomials are polynomials that can be defined by Fibonacci-like recursion relations. They were studied in 1883 by E. Catalan and E. Jacobsthal. For example, Catalan studied the polynomials
F_{n}(x)
defined by the recurrence

F_{n}(x)=xF_{n-1}(x)+F_{n-2}(x),\quad n\geq 2,

with
F_{0}(x)=0
and
F_{1}(x)=1
. A non-recursive expression for
F_{n}(x)
is

F_{n}(x)=\frac{\rho^{n}(x)-\sigma^{n}(x)}{\sqrt{x^{2}+4}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor}{n-k-1\choose k}x^{n-2k-1},\quad n\geq 0,

where
\rho(x)=\frac{x+\sqrt{x^{2}+4}}{2}
and
\sigma(x)=\frac{x-\sqrt{x^{2}+4}}{2}
.

Chebyshev and Fibonacci polynomials play an important role in applied mathematics. They possess many interesting and unique properties.
Excellent sources are the textbooks
[
11
,
14
,
16
]
, among others.

The most recent literature on Chebyshev and Fibonacci polynomials encompasses the following articles. Kilic et al.
[
9
]
computed various types of power sums for Chebyshev polynomials and deduced new connections between Chebyshev polynomials and Fibonacci numbers.
Kim et al.
[
10
]
recently derived new expressions for sums of finite products of Chebychev and Fibonacci polynomials. In
[
1
]
, Abd-Elhammed et al. established new connection formulas between Fibonacci polynomials and Chebyshev polynomials. These formulas are expressed in terms of certain values of hypergeometric functions. Li and Wenpeng
[
12
]
, using the definitions and properties of Chebyshev polynomials, studied the power sum problems involving Fibonacci polynomials and Lucas polynomials and obtained some interesting divisibility properties. Li
[
13
]
studied relationships between Chebyshev polynomials, Fibonacci polynomials, and their derivatives, and got the formula for derivatives of Chebyshev polynomials being represented by Chebyshev polynomials and Fibonacci polynomials. Finally, we mention the paper by Flórez, McAnally and Mukherjee
[
3
]
where many identities for generalized Fibonacci polynomials
are derived.

The purpose of this paper is to obtain some identities involving Chebyshev polynomials of the first and second kinds and Fibonacci polynomials. We achieve this in a conventional manner by relating the respective (ordinary and exponential) generating functions to each other, resulting in a range of interesting functional equations. Our approach is in the spirit of
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
. Also, using simple connections between Chebyshev polynomials and balancing polynomials we will be able to incorporate
the later polynomial class into our analysis. Some of the results of this paper were announced without proofs in
[
8
]
.

2. Some generating functions

This section contains the generating functions that will be used later in this article. We state the results without proofs
as they can be derived without much efforts. We recommend the article by Mező
[
15
]
for a comprehensive study of generating functions for second-order sequences.

From (
1
) and (
2
) it can be shown that the ordinary generating functions
for Chebyshev polynomials
T_{n}(x)
,
U_{n}(x)
and their odd and even indexed companions are given by

\displaystyle t(z,x)=\sum_{n\geq 0}T_{n}(x)z^{n}=\frac{1-xz}{1-2xz+z^{2}},

(3)

\displaystyle t_{1}(z,x)=\sum_{n\geq 0}T_{2n+1}(x)z^{n}=\frac{x(1-z)}{1-(4x^{2}-2)z+z^{2}},

(4)

\displaystyle t_{2}(z,x)=\sum_{n\geq 0}T_{2n}(x)z^{n}=\frac{1-(2x^{2}-1)z}{1-(4x^{2}-2)z+z^{2}},

(5)

and

\displaystyle u(z,x)=\sum_{n\geq 0}U_{n}(x)z^{n}=\frac{1}{1-2xz+z^{2}},

(6)

\displaystyle u_{1}(z,x)=\sum_{n\geq 0}U_{2n+1}(x)z^{n}=\frac{2x}{1-(4x^{2}-2)z+z^{2}},

(7)

\displaystyle u_{2}(z,x)=\sum_{n\geq 0}U_{2n}(x)z^{n}=\frac{1+z}{1-(4x^{2}-2)z+z^{2}}.

(8)

In addition, the corresponding exponential generating functions for these polynomial sequences are given by

\displaystyle\tau(z,x)=\sum_{n\geq 0}T_{n}(x)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=e^{xz}\cosh\bigl(\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\,z\bigr),

(9)

\displaystyle\tau_{1}(z,x)=\sum_{n\geq 0}T_{2n+1}(x)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=e^{(2x^{2}-1)\,z}\Bigl(x\cosh\bigl(2x\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\,z\bigr)+\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\sinh\bigl(2x\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\,z\bigr)\Bigr),

(10)

\displaystyle\tau_{2}(z,x)=\sum_{n\geq 0}T_{2n}(x)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=e^{(2x^{2}-1)z}\cosh\bigl(2x\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\,z\bigr),

(11)

and

\displaystyle\omega(z,x)=\sum_{n\geq 0}U_{n}(x)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{xz}}{\sqrt{x^{2}-1}}\Bigl(x\sinh\bigl(\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\,z\bigr)+\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\cosh\bigl(\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\,z\bigr)\Bigr),

(12)

\displaystyle\omega_{1}(z,x)=\sum_{n\geq 0}U_{2n+1}(x)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{(2x^{2}-1)z}}{\sqrt{x^{2}-1}}\Bigl((2x^{2}-1)\sinh\bigl(2x\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\,z\bigr)+2x\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\cosh\bigl(2x\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\,z\bigr)\Bigr),

(13)

\displaystyle\omega_{2}(z,x)=\sum_{n\geq 0}U_{2n}(x)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{(2x^{2}-1)z}}{\sqrt{x^{2}-1}}\Bigl(x\sinh\bigl(2x\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\,z\bigr)+\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\cosh\bigl(2x\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\,z\bigr)\Bigr).

Fibonacci polynomials
F_{n}(x)
,
F_{2n+1}(x)
and
F_{2n}(x)
have the following ordinary generating functions

\displaystyle f(z,x)=\sum_{n\geq 0}F_{n}(x)z^{n}=\frac{z}{1-xz-z^{2}},

(14)

\displaystyle f_{1}(z,x)=\sum_{n\geq 0}F_{2n+1}(x)z^{n}=\frac{1-z}{1-(x^{2}+2)z+z^{2}},

(15)

\displaystyle f_{2}(z,x)=\sum_{n\geq 0}F_{2n}(x)z^{n}=\frac{xz}{1-(x^{2}+2)z+z^{2}},

(16)

while the exponential generating functions are

\displaystyle\phi(z,x)=\sum_{n\geq 0}F_{n}(x)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=\frac{2e^{\frac{xz}{2}}}{\sqrt{x^{2}+4}}\sinh\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt{x^{2}+4}}{2}\,z\Bigr),

(17)

\displaystyle\phi_{1}(z,x)=\sum_{n\geq 0}F_{2n+1}(x)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{\frac{x^{2}+2}{2}z}}{\sqrt{x^{2}+4}}\left(x\sinh\Bigl(\frac{x\sqrt{x^{2}+4}}{2}\,z\Bigr)+\sqrt{x^{2}+4}\cosh\Bigl(\frac{x\sqrt{x^{2}+4}}{2}\,z\Bigr)\right),

(18)

\displaystyle\phi_{2}(z,x)=\sum_{n\geq 0}F_{2n}(x)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=\frac{2e^{\frac{x^{2}+2}{2}z}}{\sqrt{x^{2}+4}}\sinh\Bigl(\frac{x\sqrt{x^{2}+4}}{2}\,z\Bigr).

(19)

3. Chebyshev-Fibonacci polynomial identities using ordinary generating functions

In what follows, we will use the standard convention that
\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}=0
for
n<0
.

Theorem 1
.

For
n\geq 1
, the following polynomial identities hold:

\displaystyle F_{n}(x)=T_{n-1}(x)-\sum_{k=1}^{n-2}\bigl(xT_{n-1-k}(x)-2T_{n-2-k}(x)\bigr)F_{k}(x),

(20)

\displaystyle F_{n}(x)+xF_{n-1}(x)=U_{n-1}(x)-\sum_{k=1}^{n-2}\bigl(xU_{n-1-k}(x)-2U_{n-2-k}(x)\bigr)F_{k}(x).

(21)

Proof.

To prove formula (
20
), observe that by (
3
) and (
14
), we obtain, respectively,

\displaystyle 1-xz=\frac{1-xz+t(z,x)(xz-z^{2})}{t(z,x)},\qquad 1-xz=\frac{z+z^{2}f(z,x)}{f(z,x)},

and thus

(1-xz)f(z,x)-zt(z,x)=(2z^{2}-xz)t(z,x)f(z,x).

Expanding both sides of the last equation as a power series in
z
and using the Cauchy product of two power series, we then obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}F_{n}(x)z^{n}-x\sum_{n\geq 0}F_{n}(x)z^{n+1}-\sum_{n\geq 0}T_{n}(x)z^{n+1}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{n-k}(x)F_{k}(x)z^{n+2}-x\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{n-k}(x)F_{k}(x)z^{n+1}

or, equivalently,

\displaystyle F_{0}(x)+F_{1}(x)z+\sum_{n\geq 2}F_{n}(x)z^{n}-x\sum_{n\geq 2}F_{n-1}(x)z^{n}-T_{0}(x)z-\sum_{n\geq 2}T_{n-1}(x)z^{n}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{n\geq 2}\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}T_{n-2-k}(x)F_{k}(x)z^{n}-x\sum_{n\geq 2}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}T_{n-1-k}(x)F_{k}(x)z^{n},

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 2}\bigl(F_{n}(x)-xF_{n-1}(x)-T_{n-1}(x)\bigr)z^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 2}\Bigl(2\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}T_{n-2-k}(x)F_{k}(x)-x\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}T_{n-1-k}(x)F_{k}(x)\Bigr)z^{n}.

Comparing the coefficients on both sides, we have

\displaystyle F_{n}(x)-xF_{n-1}(x)-T_{n-1}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}\bigl(2T_{n-2-k}(x)-xT_{n-1-k}(x)\bigr)F_{k}(x)-xT_{0}(x)F_{n-1}(x),

as desired. The proof of (
21
) is very similar. From (
6
) and (
14
) the following functional equation follows:

\frac{1}{u(z,x)}=\frac{z}{f(z,x)}+z(2z-x)

or, equivalently,

f(z,x)-zu(z,x)=2z^{2}f(z,x)u(z,x)-xzf(z,x)u(z,x).

The remainder of the proof is the same as above.
∎

In a similar manner, we use the generating functions (
4
), (
7
), (
15
), and (
5
), (
8
), (
16
), respectively, to prove four additional relations between odd (even) indexed Chebyshev and Fibonacci polynomials. These relations are contained in the next theorem, those proofs we leave to the reader.

Theorem 2
.

The following identities hold for
n\geq 1

\displaystyle x\bigl(F_{2n+1}(x)-F_{2n-1}(x)\bigr)=T_{2n+1}(x)-T_{2n-1}(x)-(3x^{2}-4)\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}F_{2k+1}(x)T_{2(n-k)-1}(x),

\displaystyle 2xF_{2n+1}(x)=U_{2n+1}(x)-U_{2n-1}(x)-(3x^{2}-4)\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}F_{2k+1}(x)U_{2(n-k)-1}(x).

The even indexed counterparts are given by

\displaystyle F_{2n}(x)-(2x^{2}-1)F_{2n-2}(x)=xT_{2n-2}(x)-(3x^{2}-4)\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}F_{2k}(x)T_{2(n-k-1)}(x),

\displaystyle F_{2n}(x)+F_{2n-2}(x)=xU_{2n-2}(x)-(3x^{2}-4)\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}F_{2k}(x)U_{2(n-k-1)}(x).

Next, we present a range of Chebyshev-Fibonacci identities with mixed indices.

Theorem 3
.

For
n\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle xF_{n}(x)+(4x^{3}-x^{2}-3x)F_{n-1}(x)

\displaystyle=T_{2n-1}(x)-\sum_{k=1}^{n-2}\bigl((4x^{2}-x-2)T_{2(n-k)-1}(x)-2T_{2(n-k)-3}(x)\bigr)F_{k}(x),

\displaystyle 2xF_{n}(x)+(8x^{3}-2x^{2}-4x)F_{n-1}(x)

\displaystyle=U_{2n-1}(x)-\sum_{k=1}^{n-2}\bigl((4x^{2}-x-2)U_{2(n-k)-1}(x)-2U_{2(n-k)-3}(x)\bigr)F_{k}(x),

\displaystyle F_{n}(x)+(2x^{2}-x-1)F_{n-1}(x)

\displaystyle=T_{2n-2}(x)-\sum_{k=1}^{n-2}\bigl((4x^{2}-x-2)T_{2(n-k-1)}(x)-2T_{2(n-k-2)}(x)\bigr)F_{k}(x),

\displaystyle F_{n}(x)+(4x^{2}-x-1)F_{n-1}(x)

\displaystyle=U_{2n-2}(x)-\sum_{k=1}^{n-2}\bigl((4x^{2}-x-2)U_{2(n-k-1)}(x)-2U_{2(n-k-2)}(x)\bigr)F_{k}(x),

\displaystyle F_{2n+1}(x)-(2x^{2}-1)F_{2n-1}(x)

\displaystyle=T_{2n}(x)-T_{2n-2}(x)-(3x^{2}-4)\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}T_{2(n-k-1)}(x)F_{2k+1}(x),

\displaystyle F_{2n+1}(x)+F_{2n-1}(x)

\displaystyle=U_{2n}(x)-U_{2n-2}(x)-(3x^{2}-4)\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}U_{2(n-k-1)}(x)F_{2k+1}(x),

\displaystyle x^{2}F_{2n-1}(x)=xT_{2n-1}(x)-(3x^{2}-4)\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}T_{2(n-k)-1}(x)F_{2k}(x),

\displaystyle 2xF_{2n}(x)=xU_{2n-1}(x)-(3x^{2}-4)\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}U_{2(n-k)-1}(x)F_{2k}(x),

(22)

\displaystyle F_{2n+1}(x)-xF_{2n-1}=T_{n}(x)-T_{n-1}(x)+(x^{2}-2x+2)\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}T_{n-1-k}(x)F_{2k+1}(x),

\displaystyle F_{2n+1}(x)=U_{n}(x)-U_{n-1}(x)+(x^{2}-2x+2)\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}U_{n-1-k}(x)F_{2k+1}(x),

\displaystyle F_{2n}(x)-xF_{2n-2}=xT_{n-1}(x)+(x^{2}-2x+2)\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}T_{n-1-k}(x)F_{2k}(x),

\displaystyle F_{2n}(x)=xU_{n-1}(x)+(x^{2}-2x+2)\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}U_{n-1-k}(x)F_{2k}(x).

Proof.

We will prove only (
22
), the others can be proved in a similar way.
The formula is essentially a consequence of the functional equation

2xf_{2}(z,x)=xzu_{1}(z,x)-(3x^{2}-4)zu_{1}(z,x)f_{2}(z,x),

which can be derived from (
7
) and (
16
).
∎

B_{n}(x)

C_{n}(x)

w_{n}(x)=6xw_{n-1}(x)-w_{n-2}(x)
,

but with different initial values
B_{0}(x)=0
,
B_{1}(x)=1
and
C_{0}(x)=1
,
C_{1}(x)=3x
, respectively. From the definitions (
1
) and (
2
), the following connections are easily derived (see
[
4
]
)

\displaystyle B_{n}(x)=U_{n-1}(3x),\quad C_{n}=T_{n}(3x),\quad n\geq 1.

(23)

In view of (
23
) and Theorems
1
-
3
relations between Fibonacci and balancing (Lucas-balancing) polynomials
are obvious. In the next statement we present only a few of them.

Corollary 4
.

For
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle F_{n}(3x)=C_{n-1}(x)-\sum_{k=1}^{n-2}\bigl(3xC_{n-k-1}(x)-2C_{n-k-2}(x)\bigr)F_{k}(3x),

\displaystyle F_{n}(3x)+3xF_{n-1}(3x)=B_{n}(x)-\sum_{k=1}^{n-2}\bigl(3xB_{n-k}(x)-2B_{n-k-1}(x)\bigr)F_{k}(3x),

\displaystyle 9x^{2}F_{2n}(3x)=C_{2n+1}(x)-C_{2n-1}(x)-(27x^{2}-4)\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}C_{2(n-k)-1}(x)F_{2k+1}(3x),

\displaystyle 6xF_{2n+1}(3x)=B_{2(n+1)}(x)-B_{2n}(x)-(27x^{2}-4)\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}B_{2(n-k)}(x)F_{2k+1}(3x),

\displaystyle F_{2n}(3x)+F_{2n-2}(3x)=3xB_{2n-1}(x)-(27x^{2}-4)\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}B_{2(n-k)-1}(x)F_{2k}(3x),

\displaystyle 9x^{2}F_{2n-1}(3x)=3xC_{2n-1}(x)-(27x^{2}-4)\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}C_{2(n-k)-1}(x)F_{2k}(3x),

\displaystyle 6xF_{2n}(3x)=xB_{2n}(x)-(27x^{2}-4)\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}B_{2(n-k)}(x)F_{2k}(3x).

4. Chebyshev-Fibonacci polynomial identities via exponential generating functions

Functional equations for exponential generating functions will yield connections between
Chebyshev and Fibonacci polynomials involving binomial coefficients. Recall that we use the following notation

\displaystyle\alpha(x)=x+\sqrt{x^{2}-1},\quad\beta(x)=x-\sqrt{x^{2}-1},\quad\rho(x)=\frac{x+\sqrt{x^{2}+4}}{2},\quad\sigma(x)=\frac{x-\sqrt{x^{2}+4}}{2}.

Theorem 5
.

For
n\geq 0
, the following identities hold

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{n\choose k}\bigl(\sqrt{x^{2}+4}\bigr)^{n-1-k}\bigl(1-(-1)^{n-k}\bigr)T_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}{n\choose k}2^{k-1}\bigl(\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\bigr)^{n-k}\bigl(1+(-1)^{n-k}\bigr)F_{k}(x),

(24)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{n\choose k}\bigl(\sqrt{x^{2}+4}\bigr)^{n-1-k}\bigl(1-(-1)^{n-k}\bigr)U_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}{n\choose k}2^{k-1}\bigl(\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\bigr)^{n-1-k}\bigl(\alpha(x)-(-1)^{n-k}\beta(x)\bigr)F_{k}(x).

(25)

Proof.

To prove formula (
24
) we use the generating functions (
9
) and (
17
).
They give the functional equation

2\tau\left(\frac{z}{2},x\right)\sinh\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt{x^{2}+4}}{2}z\Bigr)=\phi(z,x)\sqrt{x^{2}+4}\,\cosh\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt{x^{2}-1}}{2}z\Bigr).

From this equation we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\frac{T_{k}(x)}{2^{k}}\left(\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt{x^{2}+4}}{2}\Bigr)^{n-k}-\Bigl(-\frac{\sqrt{x^{2}+4}}{2}\Bigr)^{n-k}\right)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{x^{2}+4}}{2}\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{k=1}^{n}{n\choose k}F_{k}(x)\left(\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt{x^{2}-1}}{2}\Bigr)^{n-k}+\Bigl(-\frac{\sqrt{x^{2}-1}}{2}\Bigr)^{n-k}\right)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}.

Comparing the coefficients of the both sides gives

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\frac{T_{k}(x)}{2^{k}}\left(\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt{x^{2}+4}}{2}\Bigr)^{n-k}-\Bigl(-\frac{\sqrt{x^{2}+4}}{2}\Bigr)^{n-k}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{x^{2}+4}}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}{n\choose k}F_{k}(x)\left(\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt{x^{2}-1}}{2}\Bigr)^{n-k}+\Bigl(-\frac{\sqrt{x^{2}-1}}{2}\Bigr)^{n-k}\right).

and after simplifications we get (
24
).
The proof of (
25
) follows in a similar way and is based on the functional equation

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\sinh\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt{x^{2}+4}}{2}z\Bigr)\omega\!\left(\frac{z}{2},x\right)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{x^{2}+4}\left(x\sinh\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt{x^{2}-1}}{2}z\Bigr)+\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\cosh\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt{x^{2}-1}}{2}z\Bigr)\right)\phi(z,x),

which we derive from generating functions (
12
) and (
17
).
∎

The next two theorems give us relations involving odd and even indexed Chebyshev and Fibonacci polynomial sequences.

Theorem 6
.

For
n\geq 0
, the following formulas hold

\displaystyle x\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt{x^{2}+4}(2x^{3}-x)}{x^{2}+2}\Bigr)^{n-1-k}\bigl(\rho(x)-(-1)^{n-k}\sigma(x)\bigr)T_{2k+1}(x)

\displaystyle=\bigl(2x\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\bigl)^{n-1}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\Bigl(\frac{2x^{2}-1}{(x^{3}+2x)\sqrt{x^{2}-1}}\Bigr)^{k-1}\bigl(\alpha(x)+(-1)^{n-k}\beta(x)\bigr)F_{2k+1}(x)

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt{x^{2}+4}(2x^{3}-x)}{x^{2}+2}\Bigr)^{n-1-k}\bigl(\rho(x)-(-1)^{n-k}\sigma(x)\bigr)U_{2k+1}(x)

\displaystyle=\bigl(2x\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\bigr)^{n-2}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\Bigl(\frac{2x^{2}-1}{(x^{3}+2x)\sqrt{x^{2}-1}}\Bigr)^{k-1}

\displaystyle\hskip-51.21504pt\times\bigl(\alpha^{2}(x)-(-1)^{n-k}\beta^{2}(x)\bigr)F_{2k+1}(x).

Proof.

The stated formulas follow from the functional equations

\displaystyle\left(x\sinh\Bigl(\frac{(2x^{3}-x)\sqrt{x^{2}+4}}{x^{2}+2}z\Bigr)+\sqrt{x^{2}+4}\cosh\Bigl(\frac{(2x^{3}-x)\sqrt{x^{2}+4}}{x^{2}+2}z\Bigr)\right)\tau_{1}(z,x)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{x^{2}+4}\left(x\cosh\bigl(2x\sqrt{x^{2}-1}z\bigr)+\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\sinh\bigl(2x\sqrt{x^{2}-1}z\bigr)\right)\phi_{1}\left(\frac{4x^{2}-2}{x^{2}+2}z,x\right)

and

\displaystyle 2\left(x\sinh\Bigl(\frac{(2x^{3}-x)\sqrt{x^{2}+4}}{x^{2}+2}z\Bigr)+\sqrt{x^{2}+4}\cosh\Bigl(\frac{(2x^{3}-x)\sqrt{x^{2}+4}}{x^{2}+2}z\Bigr)\right)\omega_{1}(z,x)

5. Concluding comments: Fibonacci and Lucas identities implied by Chebyshev-Fibonacci identities

The polynomial relations derived in this paper imply many Fibonacci and Lucas identities,
some of which are certainly known but some of which could turn out to be new.
These identities come from the various links between Chebyshev polynomials and Fibonacci (Lucas) numbers.
In
[
2
]
and
[

]
many such links are listed.
Among the various connections we have

\displaystyle T_{n}\Bigl(\frac{3}{2}\Bigr)=\frac{1}{2}L_{2n},\qquad U_{n}\Bigl(\frac{3}{2}\Bigr)=F_{2n+2},

(26)

\displaystyle T_{n}\Bigl(\frac{i}{2}\Bigr)=\frac{i^{n}}{2}L_{n},\qquad U_{n}\Bigl(\frac{i}{2}\Bigr)=i^{n}F_{n+1},

\displaystyle T_{2n}\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt{5}}{2}\Bigr)=\frac{1}{2}L_{2n},\qquad U_{2n}\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt{5}}{2}\Bigr)=L_{2n+1},

\displaystyle T_{2n+1}\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt{5}}{2}\Bigr)=\frac{\sqrt{5}}{2}F_{2n+1},\qquad U_{2n+1}\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt{5}}{2}\Bigr)=\sqrt{5}F_{2n+2}.

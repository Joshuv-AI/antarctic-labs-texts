On the light-ray algebra in conformal field theories Unité Mixte de Recherche 3681 du CNRS
Gregory P. Korchemsky a,b and Alexander Zhiboedov c

Abstract
We analyze the commutation relations of light-ray operators in conformal field theories. We first establish the algebra of light-ray operators built out of higher spin currents in free CFTs and find explicit expressions for the corresponding structure constants. The resulting algebras are remarkably similar to the generalized Zamolodchikov’s W ∞ W_{\infty} algebra in a two-dimensional conformal field theory. We then compute the commutator of generalized energy flow operators in a generic, interacting CFTs in d > 2 d>2 . We show that it receives contribution from the energy flow operator itself, as well as from the light-ray operators built out of scalar primary operators of dimension Δ ≤ d − 2 \Delta\leq d-2 , that are present in the OPE of two stress-energy tensors. Commutators of light-ray operators considered in the present paper lead to CFT sum rules which generalize the superconvergence relations and naturally connect to the dispersive sum rules, both of which have been studied recently.

2.1
Leading twist operators

We start with recalling the definition of higher spin conserved currents

\displaystyle O_{S}(x)\equiv O_{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{S}}(x)\bar{n}^{\mu_{1}}\dots\bar{n}^{\mu_{S}}

These operators are built out of two fundamental fields (scalars, fermions and gauge field strength tensor) and their derivatives. They carry the leading twist

\tau=\Delta-S=d-2

\displaystyle O^{(\phi)}_{S}\sim\bar{\phi}(i\partial_{-})^{S}\phi+\dots\,,\quad

\displaystyle O^{(\psi)}_{S}\sim\bar{\psi}\gamma_{-}(i\partial_{-})^{S-1}\psi+\dots\,,\quad

\displaystyle O^{(F)}_{S}\sim F_{-\boldsymbol{\mu}}(i\partial_{-})^{S-2}F_{-}{}^{\boldsymbol{\mu}}+\dots\,,

1\leq\alpha_{2}<\dots<\alpha_{d/2}\leq d-2
. In
d=2

\Phi

The fields (
23

\Delta_{\phi}=d/2-1
,
\Delta_{\psi}=(d-1)/2
and
\Delta_{F}=d/2
and diagonalize
the operator of angular momentum
i[M_{+-},\Phi(0)]=s_{\Phi}\Phi(0)
with

\displaystyle s_{\phi}=0\,,\qquad\qquad s_{\psi}=1/2\,,\qquad\qquad s_{F}=1\,.

(24)

As a consequence, the fields (
23
) have the same twist
\tau_{\Phi}=\Delta_{\Phi}-s_{\Phi}=(d-2)/2
and their conformal spins are given by

\displaystyle j_{\phi}={d-2\over 4}\,,\qquad\qquad j_{\psi}={d\over 4}\,,\qquad\qquad j_{F}={d+2\over 4}\,.

(25)

Note that
j_{\Phi}=(d-2)/4+s_{\Phi}
. The remaining field components that are not present in (
23

In general, the fields
\bar{\Phi}
and
\Phi
can be different. For the sake of simplicity, we shall take them to be conjugated
to each other,
\bar{\Phi}=\Phi^{\dagger}
.
Each derivative in (
22

\displaystyle S=\ell+2s_{\Phi}\,,\qquad\qquad\Delta=S+d-2\,,

(26)

where
\ell\geq 0
. The conformal spin of the operator (
22

\displaystyle j_{O}=S+{d\over 2}-1=\ell+2j_{\Phi}\,,\qquad\qquad\tau_{O}=d-2\,.

(27)

In what follows we use

and

interchangeably. The relation (

To define the expansion coefficients in (

\displaystyle P_{\ell}(p_{1},p_{2})=\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}c_{k\ell}\,p_{1}^{k}\,p_{2}^{\ell-k}\,.

Replacing
p_{i}
with light-cone derivatives
i\partial_{-}
, we can rewrite the operators (
22

\displaystyle O_{S}(x)=\bar{\Phi}(x)P_{\ell}(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rightarrow}}{{i\partial_{-}}},\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftarrow}}{{i\partial_{-}}})\Phi(x)\,,

(29)

where arrows indicate the fields the derivative acts on. The polynomials
P_{\ell}
define unambiguously the operators
O_{S}(x)
.
Properties of these polynomials play an important role in our analysis.

To determine the polynomials
P_{\ell}
, we use the well-known fact that a general form of two- and three-point correlation functions is fixed by the conformal symmetry. For the operators located at
x_{i}=(x_{i}^{+},x_{i}^{-},\mbox{\boldmath$0$})
the corresponding Wightman functions are

\displaystyle\langle{\Phi(x_{1})\bar{\Phi}(x_{2})}\rangle={i^{1-d/2-2j}\over(2\pi)^{d/2}}{\Gamma(2j)\over(x_{12}^{+}-i0)^{(d-2)/2}(x_{12}^{-}-i0)^{2j}}\,,

\displaystyle\langle{\Phi(x_{1})O_{S}(x_{2})\bar{\Phi}(x_{3})}\rangle\sim{1\over(x_{12}^{+}x_{23}^{+})^{t_{O}/2}}{1\over(x_{12}^{-}x_{23}^{-})^{j_{O}}(x_{13}^{-})^{2j-j_{O}}}\,,

(30)

where we used a shorthand notation for
x_{ij}^{\pm}=x_{i}^{\pm}-x_{j}^{\pm}
and

j=j_{\Phi}
is the conformal spin of fields
\Phi
and
\bar{\Phi}

\pm i0

Replacing

, its degree is

\ell+\ell^{\prime}+2(s-1)
.
Aside from the delta-function
\delta^{(d-2)}(n,n^{\prime})
, the relation (
2.2
) is remarkably similar to the expression for the three-point function (
2.2
). To match the two relations, we take advantage of the properties of Gegenbauer polynomials and expand (
2.2
) over the orthogonal basis of polynomials
P_{\ell^{\prime\prime}}(\alpha_{1},-\alpha_{1}+\omega^{\prime\prime})
defined in (
31
)

\displaystyle Q(\alpha_{1})=\sum_{\ell^{\prime\prime}=0}^{\ell+\ell^{\prime}+2(s-1)}C_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}{}^{\ell^{\prime\prime}}(\omega,\omega^{\prime})P_{\ell^{\prime\prime}}(\alpha_{1},-\alpha_{1}+\omega^{\prime\prime})\,,

(69)

where
s
is the spin of the field
\Phi
and the maximal value of
\ell^{\prime\prime}
coincides with the degree of
Q(\alpha_{1})
.

Substituting (
69
) into (
2.2
) and matching the resulting expression to (
2.2
), we arrive at the algebra

\displaystyle{[\mathcal{J}_{\omega,S}(n),\mathcal{J}_{\omega^{\prime},S^{\prime}}(n^{\prime})]}_{c}=\delta^{(d-2)}(n,n^{\prime})\sum_{S^{\prime\prime}=2s}^{S+S^{\prime}-2}C_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}{}^{\ell^{\prime\prime}}(\omega,\omega^{\prime})\mathcal{J}_{\omega+\omega^{\prime},S^{\prime\prime}}(n)\,,

(70)

where
\ell_{i}=S_{i}-2s
and the structure constants coincide with the expansion coefficients in (
69
).
The minimal value of
S^{\prime\prime}
in the sum (
70
) equals
2s_{\psi}=1
for fermion and
2s_{F}=2
for gauge field flow operators.

The relations (
2.2
) and (
69
) lead to nontrivial conditions for the structure constants

\displaystyle C_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}{}^{\ell^{\prime\prime}}(\omega,\omega^{\prime})=-C_{\ell^{\prime}\ell}{}^{\ell^{\prime\prime}}(\omega^{\prime},\omega)\,,

\displaystyle C_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}{}^{\ell^{\prime\prime}}(\lambda\omega,\lambda\omega^{\prime})=\lambda^{\ell+\ell^{\prime}-\ell^{\prime\prime}+2s-1}C_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}{}^{\ell^{\prime\prime}}(\omega,\omega^{\prime})\,,

\displaystyle C_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}{}^{\ell^{\prime\prime}}(-\omega,-\omega^{\prime})=-C_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}{}^{\ell^{\prime\prime}}(\omega,\omega^{\prime})\,.

(71)

Here the first relation follows from the antisymmetry of (
2.2
) under the exchange of
(\omega,\ell)
and
(\omega^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})
.
To check the second relation, it is sufficient to compare the properties of both sides of (
69
) under rescaling
\alpha\to\lambda\alpha
,
\omega\to\lambda\omega
and
\omega^{\prime}\to\lambda\omega^{\prime}
. Moreover, matching (
69
) to (
2.2
) we find that
C_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}{}^{\ell^{\prime\prime}}(\omega,\omega^{\prime})
are homogenous polynomials in
\omega
and
\omega^{\prime}
with real coefficients. It follows from the second relation in (
2.3
) that their degree is
\ell+\ell^{\prime}-\ell^{\prime\prime}+2s-1=S+S^{\prime}-S^{\prime\prime}-1
.
Finally, the last
relation in (
2.3
) can be derived by applying complex conjugation to both sides of (
70
) and by taking into account the relation
(\mathcal{J}_{\omega,S}(n))^{\dagger}=\mathcal{J}_{-\omega,S}(n)
.

Substituting
\lambda=-1
into the second relation in (
2.3
) and comparing it with the third one, we find that the structure constants
C_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}{}^{\ell^{\prime\prime}}(\omega,\omega^{\prime})
vanish for even
\ell+\ell^{\prime}-\ell^{\prime\prime}+2s-1
, or equivalently for odd

S+S^{\prime}-S^{\prime\prime}
. This means that the sum in (

S^{\prime\prime}=S+S^{\prime}-2p
with
p=1,2,\dots

To summarize, the structure constants take the following general form

\displaystyle C_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}{}^{\ell^{\prime\prime}}(\omega,\omega^{\prime})=\frac{1+(-1)^{S+S^{\prime}-S^{\prime\prime}}}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{S+S^{\prime}-S^{\prime\prime}-1}c_{k}(\ell,\ell^{\prime})\,\omega^{k}(\omega^{\prime})^{S+S^{\prime}-S^{\prime\prime}-1-k}\,,

where
S_{i}=\ell_{i}+2s
and real-valued expansion coefficients depend on spins and satisfy
c_{k}(\ell,\ell^{\prime})=-c_{S+S^{\prime}-S^{\prime\prime}-1-k}(\ell^{\prime},\ell)
.
Explicit expressions for the structure constants are derived in the next section. In what follows we do not display the signature factor and tacitly assume that
C_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}{}^{\ell^{\prime\prime}}(\omega,\omega^{\prime})
vanish for odd
S+S^{\prime}-S^{\prime\prime}
.

For the algebra (
70

2.2
) and (
69

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell^{\prime}}C_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}{}^{\ell^{\prime}}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})C_{\ell^{\prime}\ell_{3}}{}^{\ell^{\prime\prime}}(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2},\omega_{3})

\displaystyle+\sum_{\ell^{\prime}}C_{\ell_{2}\ell_{3}}{}^{\ell^{\prime}}(\omega_{2},\omega_{3})C_{\ell^{\prime}\ell_{1}}{}^{\ell^{\prime\prime}}(\omega_{2}+\omega_{3},\omega_{1})

\displaystyle+\sum_{\ell^{\prime}}C_{\ell_{3}\ell_{1}}{}^{\ell^{\prime}}(\omega_{3},\omega_{1})C_{\ell^{\prime}\ell_{2}}{}^{\ell^{\prime\prime}}(\omega_{3}+\omega_{1},\omega_{2})

\displaystyle=0\,.

(73)

The relation (
70
) takes into account the contribution to (
37
) of the connected part of the four-point function. We use (
65
) to obtain the disconnected contribution to (
37
) as

\displaystyle\langle{\Phi(x_{1})[\mathcal{J}_{\omega,S}(n),\mathcal{J}_{\omega^{\prime},S^{\prime}}(n^{\prime})]\bar{\Phi}(x_{2})}\rangle_{d}=-\delta_{SS^{\prime}}\delta(\omega+\omega^{\prime})\omega^{2S-1}\Omega_{d}{\left({n,n^{\prime}}\right)}\langle{\Phi(x_{1})\bar{\Phi}(x_{2})}\rangle\,.

It defines the central extension of the algebra (
70
). Namely,
combining together (
70
) and (

\displaystyle{[\mathcal{J}_{\omega,S}(n),\mathcal{J}_{\omega^{\prime},S^{\prime}}(n^{\prime})]}

\displaystyle=\delta^{(d-2)}(n,n^{\prime})\sum_{S^{\prime\prime}=2s}^{S+S^{\prime}-2}C_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}{}^{\ell^{\prime\prime}}(\omega,\omega^{\prime})\mathcal{J}_{\omega+\omega^{\prime},S^{\prime\prime}}(n)

\displaystyle-\delta_{SS^{\prime}}\delta(\omega+\omega^{\prime})\omega^{2S-1}\Omega_{S}{\left({n,n^{\prime}}\right)}\,,

(75)

where
\ell_{i}=S_{i}-2s
and the sum runs over the spins
S^{\prime\prime}
of the same parity as
S+S^{\prime}
. Here
the structure constants take the form (

) and
satisfy the relations (

\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{0}^{\infty}d\alpha_{1}\mathop{\rm e}\nolimits^{-i\alpha_{1}(nx_{1})+i\alpha_{2}(nx_{2})}\left[\Big({P_{S_{3}}(\alpha_{1},-\alpha_{1}+\omega_{3})P_{S_{2}}(\alpha_{1}-\omega_{3},-\alpha_{1}+\omega_{2}+\omega_{3})\over\alpha_{1}-\omega_{3}}\right.

where

3
Commutator of the energy flow operators in interacting CFTs

In the previous sections we analyzed the algebra of the light-ray operators
\mathcal{J}_{\omega,S}
built out of conserved currents in the free theory (and bilinear scalar operator of dimension
\Delta=d-2
and spin
S=0
). In a generic interacting CFT the only conserved current is the stress-energy tensor and, possibly, the spin one conserved current. The remaining operators develop anomalous dimensions. In this section we address the question: which operators contribute to the commutator of the energy flow operators
[{\cal E}_{\omega_{1}}(n_{1}),{\cal E}_{\omega_{2}}(n_{2})]
in an interacting CFT?

To answer this question, we consider the three-point function

\displaystyle\langle 0|[{\cal E}_{\omega_{1}}(n_{1}),{\cal E}_{\omega_{2}}(n_{2})]O_{\Delta,{\bf S}}(x)|0\rangle,

(121)

where
O_{\Delta,{\bf S}}(x)
is a conformal primary operator of scaling dimension
\Delta
belonging to an irreducible representation
{\bf S}
of the Lorentz group in
d
dimensions. Because the energy flow operator
{\cal E}_{\omega}(n)
is given by the integrated stress-energy tensor, the correlation function (
121
) is different from zero provided that
O_{\Delta,{\bf S}}(x)
can appear in the OPE of two stress-energy tensors.

To simplify the computation we choose
\omega_{1}<0
,
\omega_{2}>0
and
\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}<0
. In this case,
we use the fact that
\langle 0|{\cal E}_{\omega_{2}}(n_{2})=0
for
\omega_{2}>0
, see Ref.
Korchemsky:2021okt
, to get

\displaystyle\langle 0|[{\cal E}_{\omega_{1}}(n_{1}),{\cal E}_{\omega_{2}}(n_{2})]O_{\Delta,{\bf S}}(x)|0\rangle=\langle 0|{\cal E}_{\omega_{1}}(n_{1}){\cal E}_{\omega_{2}}(n_{2})O_{\Delta,{\bf S}}(x)|0\rangle.

(122)

Replacing the energy flow operators with (
88
), we can express the right-hand side of (
122
) as
the light-ray transform of the underlying three-point function

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dz_{1}^{-}dz_{2}^{-}\mathop{\rm e}\nolimits^{-iz_{1}^{-}\omega_{1}-iz_{2}^{-}\omega_{2}}\langle 0|T_{--}(\epsilon,z_{1}^{-},\mbox{\boldmath$z$}_{1})T_{--}(0,z_{2}^{-},\mbox{\boldmath$z$}_{2})O_{\Delta,{\bf S}}(x_{3})|0\rangle\,,

(123)

where we wrote the light-ray operators in the null plane representation, see (
16
).
The reason for introducing
\epsilon\to 0
limit will be clear in a moment.
The correlation function in (
123
) is fixed by conformal symmetry up to a few constants.

To correctly compute the commutator (
122
), it is important to regularize the product of the two stress-energy tensors in (
123
) by separating them in the ‘
+
’ direction by a non-zero
\epsilon
. Doing the light transform and taking the limit
\epsilon\to 0
we can identify the distributional terms localized at
\mbox{\boldmath$z$}_{1}=\mbox{\boldmath$z$}_{2}
.
Introducing non-zero
\epsilon
introduces the regions in the light transform where the detectors are null (and timelike) separated. In this region the two detectors can communicate with each other, and this cross-talk produces a nontrivial commutator in the
\epsilon\to 0
limit. By setting
\epsilon=0
before doing the light transform this communication region is pushed to infinity (in variable
z_{1}^{-}-z_{2}^{-}
) and is not properly resolved.

At the technical level, this can we understood as follows. The choice of the signs of
\omega_{i}
dictates the direction in which the integration contour in (
123
) can be deformed. For
\omega_{1}<0
we can deform the integration contour for
z_{1}^{-}
to the upper half-plane, for
\omega_{2}<0
we can deform the integration contour for
z_{2}^{-}
to the lower half-plane. The ordering of operators in (
123
) dictates that the correlation function depends on
z_{1}^{-}-z_{2}^{-}-i0
,
z_{1}^{-}-z_{3}^{-}-i0
, and
z_{2}^{-}-z_{3}^{-}-i0
. In particular, as we evaluate the integral over
z_{2}^{-}
by deforming the contour into the lower half-plane only the singularity coming from
x_{12}^{2}=2\epsilon(z_{1}^{-}-z_{2}^{-}-i0)-(\mbox{\boldmath$z$}_{1}-\mbox{\boldmath$z$}_{2})^{2}=0
produces a nontrivial contribution. To correctly capture it is necessary to keep
\epsilon\neq 0
when doing the light transform.

We first consider the case when
O_{\Delta,{\bf S}}(x)
is a traceless symmetric tensor in Lorentz indices of even spin
S
. We then show that the operators with twist
\tau=\Delta-S>d-2
do not contribute to (
122
). This implies that the spinning operators (with
S>0
) which are not conserved currents cannot appear in the commutator of the energy flow operators
[{\cal E}_{\omega_{1}}(n_{1}),{\cal E}_{\omega_{2}}(n_{2})]
. On the other hand, conformal operators with zero spin
S=0
and dimension
\Delta<d-2
produce an infinite contribution.

At
d=3
the three-point functions of conserved currents can contain additional conformal parity-odd structures involving the Levi-Civita tensor
Giombi:2011rz
;
Costa:2011mg

3.4
Commutation relations and the Jacobi identities

Let us summarize the results of this section so far. We have observed that the commutator of the energy flow operators in a generic, interacting CFT is given by

\displaystyle[{\cal E}_{\omega_{1}}(n_{1}),{\cal E}_{\omega_{2}}(n_{2})]=\delta^{(d-2)}(n_{1},n_{2})\Big[(\omega_{2}-\omega_{1}+\tilde{n}_{\phi}f_{\phi}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})){\cal E}_{\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}}(n_{1})-C\omega_{1}^{3}\delta(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2})+\dots\Big],

(149)

where
\tilde{n}_{\phi}f_{\phi}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})
arises from the free scalar structure in the expression (
132
) for the three-point function of the stress-energy tensors.
17
17

17

Recall that in an interacting CFT we expect such structure to be always present in the three-point function of stress-energy tensors
Zhiboedov:2013opa
;
Meltzer:2017rtf
.
The dots denote the contribution from light scalar primary operators of dimension
{d-2\over 2}<\Delta\leq d-2
.
The operators with twist
\tau>d-2
produce vanishing contribution to (
149
).

Given the expression (
149
) it is tempting to ask if the commutator satisfies the Jacobi identity

\displaystyle[[{\cal E}_{\omega_{1}}(n_{1}),{\cal E}_{\omega_{2}}(n_{2})],{\cal E}_{\omega_{3}}(n_{3})]+{\rm cyclic}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{=}}0.

(150)

As opposed to the free theories, one can easily see that in a generic, interacting CFT the Jacobi identities (
150
) are not satisfied. This sounds puzzling since we expect the Jacobi identity to hold as a consequence of crossing. Indeed, let us start with the four-point function
\langle TTTO_{i}\rangle
. We can ask if the operator algebra generated by the OPE satisfies the Jacobi identity at the level of the correlation function of local operators. This is of course nothing but the statement of crossing symmetry applied to the four-point function
\langle TTTO_{i}\rangle
. How is it possible then that the identity (
150
) fails?

The resolution of the puzzle is that the commutator
[{\cal E}_{\omega_{1}}(n_{1}),{\cal E}_{\omega_{2}}(n_{2})]
in
d>2
CFTs requires a regularization. In this section we defined the commutator by first separating the light-ray operators
in the ‘
+
’ direction by distance
\epsilon
, and then taking
\epsilon\to 0
. Let us denote the commutator at finite
\epsilon
as
[{\cal E}_{\omega_{1}}(n_{1}),{\cal E}_{\omega_{2}}(n_{2})]_{\epsilon}
.
We then expect that the following sum rule holds

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\Big([[{\cal E}_{\omega_{1}}(n_{1}),{\cal E}_{\omega_{2}}(n_{2})]_{\epsilon},{\cal E}_{\omega_{3}}(n_{3})]_{\epsilon}+{\rm cyclic}\Big)=0.

(151)

There is an important difference between (
150
) and (
151
).
According to (
131
),
the contribution of the operator
O_{\Delta,S}
with twist
\tau=\Delta-S
to the commutator
[{\cal E}_{\omega_{1}}(n_{1}),{\cal E}_{\omega_{2}}(n_{2})]_{\epsilon}
vanishes as
\epsilon^{{\tau-(d-2)\over 2}}{\cal J}_{\omega_{1}+\omega_{2},S}
for
\tau>d-2
.
Performing the light-ray transform of the three-point function
\langle TTO_{\Delta,S}\rangle
, one can show that

[{\cal J}_{\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}}(n_{1}),{\cal E}_{\omega_{3}}(n_{2})]_{\epsilon}
receives a singular contribution
\epsilon^{{(d-2)-\tau\over 2}}{\cal E}_{\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}+\omega_{3}}
which precisely compensates the factor
\epsilon^{{\tau-(d-2)\over 2}}
coming from the commutator of the energy flow operators. Therefore, we conclude that the operators with twist
\tau>d-2
do not contribute to (
150
), but they do contribute to (
151
).

The conclusion is that in a generic CFT we expect that the Jacobi identity (
150
) receives contributions from infinitely many operators when inserted inside the correlation function, e.g.

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\langle\Big([[{\cal E}_{\omega_{1}}(n_{1}),{\cal E}_{\omega_{2}}(n_{2})]_{\epsilon},{\cal E}_{\omega_{3}}(n_{3})]_{\epsilon}+{\rm cyclic}\Big)T\rangle=0\ .

One might wonder what kind of sum rules can be obtained in this way, but this goes beyond the scope of the present paper.

Instead, as we discuss in the concluding remarks, we can use (
149
) to write simpler sum rules which generalize the superconvergence sum rules
Kologlu:2019bco
and connect to the dispersive sum rules

4
Contact terms in Mellin space

In this section, we apply the obtained results for the algebra of light-ray operators to understand the properties of (generalized) energy-energy correlation in
{\cal N}=4
SYM

\displaystyle\text{EEC}(z,\omega_{1},\omega_{2})=\langle{0|\phi^{\dagger}\mathcal{E}_{\omega_{1}}(n_{1})\mathcal{E}_{\omega_{2}}(n_{2})\phi(q)|0}\rangle\,,

The commutation relations (
1
) imply that this property does not hold if the detectors are oriented along the same direction
n_{1}=n_{2}
, or equivalently
z=0
. It follows from (
1
) that
\text{EEC}(z,\omega_{1},\omega_{2})-\text{EEC}(z,\omega_{2},\omega_{1})\sim\delta(z)
.
18
18

18

The locus
z=0
corresponds to
n_{1}=n_{2}
, as such
\delta^{d-2}(n_{1},n_{2})
that we used in the previous sections becomes
\delta(z)
up to a trivial constant pre-factor.
It is natural to ask to what extent the analysis in Mellin space can be generalized to also cover such contact terms. This is the subject of the present section.

Let us start with an important subtlety which we already discussed at the beginning of Section
3
. In doing the computations in the papers mentioned above, the detector operators were first placed to future null infinity, and only then the integral over working time of the detector, or the light transform, was performed. It is possible to check that if we were to do this in the previous sections of the present paper we would miss important contact terms that produce nontrivial contributions to the commutator. As a result, if we want to correctly capture the commutator the analysis of
Belitsky:2013xxa
;
Korchemsky:2021okt
is not obviously applicable.

At the technical level, the subtlety arises from the treatment of the separation between detectors
(x_{12}^{2})^{-\delta_{12}}
in the definition of the Mellin amplitude. If we do the light transform first, we can potentially pick the poles from
x_{12}^{2}=0
which are sensitive to the light-ray commutators. If we, on the other hand, take the detectors to null infinity first, the
x_{12}^{2}=0
poles never contribute to the light transform.

There is another possibility for poles
x_{12}^{2}=0
not to contribute even if we want to discuss the locus
n_{1}=n_{2}
. If the light-ray operators are ordered as
{\cal E}_{\omega_{1}\geq 0}(n_{1}){\cal E}_{\omega_{2}\leq 0}(n_{2})
then by evaluating the light transform we will not get the contribution from the
x_{12}^{2}=0
poles due to the
i\epsilon
prescription, which takes the form
t_{1}-t_{2}-i\epsilon
, where we used the detector time from the definition (
1
). For
\omega_{1}\geq 0
we can deform the
t_{1}
contour to the lower half-plane, and given that
\omega_{2}\leq 0
, the
t_{2}
contour to the upper half-plane, so that the pole at
{\rm Im}[t_{1}-t_{2}]=\epsilon
never contributes.

In principle, it should be possible to generalize the analysis of
Belitsky:2013xxa
;
Korchemsky:2021okt
to include such contributions, but here for simplicity we only restrict our consideration to the particular ordering
{\cal E}_{\omega_{1}\geq 0}(n_{1}){\cal E}_{\omega_{2}\leq 0}(n_{1})
for which the contact terms in the event shapes can be simply computed using the formulas of
Belitsky:2013xxa
;
Korchemsky:2021okt
as we explain below.

4.3
Asymmetry

As was mentioned above, the commutator of the energy flow operator controls the asymmetry of the energy-energy correlation,
\text{EEC}(z,\omega_{1},\omega_{2})-\text{EEC}(z,\omega_{2},\omega_{1})\sim\delta(z)
.

Let us first consider the limit of zero coupling in
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM when the Mellin contribution to (
154
) is absent. It follows from the algebra (
9
) that

\displaystyle\text{EEC}^{\text{tree}}(z,\omega_{1},\omega_{2})-\text{EEC}^{\text{tree}}(z,\omega_{2},\omega_{1})={1\over 4}\delta(z)\langle(\omega_{2}-\omega_{1}){\cal E}_{\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}}(n_{1})+\omega_{1}^{2}\omega_{2}^{2}{\cal O}^{-}_{\omega_{1},\omega_{2}}(n_{1})\rangle_{\text{tree}}\,,

(170)

where an average on the right-hand side is taken with respect to the state
|{\phi(q)}\rangle
containing in the free theory two on-shell scalar particles with the total momentum
q
, in the representation
\mathbf{20^{\prime}}
of the
R-
symmetry group (see Ref.
Korchemsky:2021okt
for details).
The relevant one-point functions take the form

\displaystyle\langle{\cal E}_{\omega}(n)\rangle_{\text{tree}}

\displaystyle=1+\omega+{\omega^{2}\over 6}\,,

\displaystyle\langle{\cal O}^{-}_{\omega_{1},\omega_{2}}(n)\rangle_{\text{tree}}

\displaystyle={\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}\over 36(1+\omega_{1})(1+\omega_{2})}\,.

(171)

We can use (
4.2
) to check that the light-ray algebra identity (
170
) is indeed satisfied.
Notice that the first relation in (
4.3
) is protected from corrections in
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM.

The situation becomes more interesting at finite coupling. We show in Appendix
F
that the operator
{\cal O}^{(\phi)}_{\omega_{1},\omega_{2}}
is given by the sum of light-ray operators (
1
) of an arbitrary spin

. In
\mathcal{N}=4

acquire anomalous dimensions. Their twist satisfies

and, as a consequence, they do not contribute to the light-ray algebra. The only operator that contributes is the stress-energy tensor. This leads to the algebra (

\displaystyle\text{EEC}(z,\omega_{1},\omega_{2})-\text{EEC}(z,\omega_{2},\omega_{1})={1\over 4}\delta(z)(\omega_{2}-\omega_{1}+{1\over 10}f_{\phi}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2}))\langle{\cal E}_{\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}}(n_{1})\rangle\,.

5
Concluding remarks

In this paper we explored commutation relations of the light-ray operators (
1
) built out of local operators in a
d-
dimensional CFT.
The commutator of these operators
[\mathcal{J}_{\omega,S}(n),\mathcal{J}_{\omega^{\prime},S^{\prime}}(n^{\prime})]
is localized at
n=n^{\prime}
, when both operators are pointed in the same direction on the celestial sphere. We argued that it has different form in free and interacting CFTs.

We demonstrated that in a free theory in
d
dimensions, the flow operators form a closed algebra and derived the explicit expressions for the corresponding structure constants and the central charges.
In
d=2
the light-ray operators are closely related to generators of infinite-dimensional symmetries of CFT
2
. More precisely,
the light-ray operators of spin
S=2
, or equivalently the energy flow operators, are generating functions of the generators of Virasoro algebra. In the similar manner, for
d=2
, the light-ray operators of higher spin are related to the generators of the
W_{\infty}
algebra. We found that in the free theory of
complex scalar and fermion in
d=4
dimensions (and, more generally, for any even
d
), the structure constants of the algebra of the light-ray operators
are related to those of the Virasoro and
W_{\infty}
algebras by a linear finite-difference relation.

Defining the light-ray operators (
1
) in free CFTs, we have chosen the conformal operators to be built from two fundamental fields of the same type (scalars, fermions and gauge fields). In a similar manner, we can consider a free CFT describing one fermion, one complex scalar and gauge field, define “mixed” operators (built out of fields of different type) and construct the corresponding flow operators. In
d=2
dimensions, this theory respects
\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetry and, as a consequence, such flow operators form a
\mathcal{N}=2
supermultiplet. Their algebra coincides with super
-W_{\infty}
algebra
Bergshoeff:1988uc
;
Bergshoeff:1990cz
;
Bergshoeff:1991dz
. In
d=4
dimensions, all leading twist operators belong to the same
\mathcal{N}=4
supermultiplet
Belitsky:2003sh
. This suggests that the flow operators generate an
\mathcal{N}=4
superalgebra whose bosonic sector is given by (
8
) and (
9
). It would be interesting to compute the structure constants of this superalgebra and to establish their relation to those of super
-W_{\infty}
algebra.

We found that in an interacting CFT in
d>2
dimensions, the commutator of the energy flow operators only receives contribution from the stress-energy tensor itself and light scalar primary operators (which appear in the OPE of two stress-energy tensors) with dimension
\Delta\leq d-2
.
The latter contribution is divergent for
\Delta<d-2
.

As opposed to the free theories considered in Section
2
, the light-ray operators do not form an algebra in interacting CFTs.
As explained in Section
3.4
, this is related to the careful, regularized definitions of the commutators.
It would be interesting to understand if there is a sense in which the light-ray algebra that we found in the free CFTs is weakly broken at weak coupling, see e.g. Refs.
Maldacena:2012sf
;
Gerasimenko:2021sxj
.

We believe that the significance of the commutation relations considered in the present paper is two-fold. First, the algebra of the light-ray operators is connected to the symmetries of the theory, be it global conformal invariance in interacting CFTs or higher spin symmetry in the free CFTs. For example, the only spinning operators that contribute to the commutator of the energy flow operators are conserved currents out of which the symmetry generators are constructed. The result (
1
) contains a universal term

[\mathcal{E}_{\omega_{1}}(n_{1}),\mathcal{E}_{\omega_{2}}(n_{2})]=\delta^{(d-2)}(n_{1},n_{2})(\omega_{2}-\omega_{1}){\cal E}_{\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}}(n_{1})+\dots
which comes from the OPE
T\times T\sim T+\dots
and is closely related to the conformal Ward identities. In a free theory, in a close analogy with the
W
symmetry in CFT
2
, the algebra gets extended to include the light-ray operators built out of higher spin currents.

Second, the commutator of light-ray operators naturally leads to a family of sum rules

\displaystyle\int\prod_{i=1}^{2}d\omega_{i}d\vec{n}_{i}\,\delta(\vec{n}_{i}^{2}-1)g(n_{i},\omega_{i})\langle\phi_{i}|(\text{light-ray commutator})|\phi_{j}\rangle=0,

(174)

where
g(n_{i},\omega_{i})
is a priori an arbitrary function. The sum rules are obtained by inserting a complete set of states between the light-ray operators in the commutator.
For example, let us consider a generic interacting CFT, where the light-ray algebra takes the form (
1
). The presence of the divergent contributions in the algebra due to light scalar primary operators,
\Delta<d-2
,
signify the necessity of subtractions to get well-defined CFT sum rules. For the identity operator subtraction boils down to considering the connected correlator on the left-hand side of (
174
). For other light scalar primary operators
of dimension
{d-2\over 2}\leq\Delta<d-2
,
that appear in the OPE of stress-energy tensors, we can for example subtract the corresponding scalar exchange Witten diagram which effectively removes light scalars from the
[{\cal E}_{\omega_{1}}(n_{1}),{\cal E}_{\omega_{2}}(n_{2})]
. The sum rules (
174
) generalize the superconvergence relations of Ref.
Kologlu:2019bco
and we can refer to the sum rules that follow from the light-ray algebra as
generalized superconvergence relations
. We do not attempt studying the consequences of these sum rules for the OPE data of CFTs in the present paper.

Let us note in this context that the generalized superconvergence relations, analogous to (
174
), were shown to be equivalent to the dispersive sum rules in some interesting cases
Caron-Huot:2020adz
;
Caron-Huot:2021enk
. It would be very interesting to explore this connection in a greater depth. For example, by making
g(n_{i},\omega_{i})
vanishing in the limit
\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}\to 0
with
\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}
fixed, one damps the contribution of the Regge limit to the sum rule. Functionals with this property are related to subtractions in the CFT dispersion relations
Carmi:2019cub
;
Mazac:2019shk
;
Penedones:2019tng
.

The light-ray algebra has important application to the generalized event shapes introduced in Ref.
Korchemsky:2021okt
. It allows one to control the contribution to these observables of distributions localized at the coinciding points on the celestial sphere. Already for the conventional event shapes, for
\omega_{i}=0
, understanding of such distributional terms was important in order to verify the conformal Ward identities
Kologlu:2019mfz
;
Korchemsky:2019nzm
;
Dixon:2019uzg
, which serve as a nontrivial check of the computations. The same applies to the generalized event shapes for
\omega_{i}\neq 0
. In this context, we have explained how the distributional terms naturally emerge in Mellin space in
{\cal N}=4
SYM and presented expected
expression for the commutator (
4.3
). It would be interesting to derive this relation from the first principles.

Relatedly, generalized event shapes satisfy the following positivity condition

\displaystyle\int\prod_{i=1}^{2}d\omega_{i}d\vec{n}_{i}\,\delta(\vec{n}_{i}^{2}-1)\,g^{*}(n_{1},\omega_{1})g(n_{2},\omega_{2})\langle\Psi|\mathcal{E}_{-\omega_{1}}(n_{1})\mathcal{E}_{\omega_{2}}(n_{2})|\Psi\rangle\geq 0\,.

(175)

It follows from the fact that
\mathcal{E}_{\omega_{1}}^{\dagger}(n_{1})=\mathcal{E}_{-\omega_{1}}(n_{1})
and therefore (
175
) can be interpreted as a norm, or the total cross-section of the scattering
|{\Psi}\rangle
off the state created by the integrated energy flow operator. Computing (
175
) necessarily involves evaluation of the generalized event shapes at coincident point
n_{1}=n_{2}
, which we considered in Mellin space in Section
4
.

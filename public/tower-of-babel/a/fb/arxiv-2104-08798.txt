Near-miss Identities and Spinor Genus Classification of Ternary Quadratic Forms with Congruence Conditions
Kush Singhal

Abstract
In this paper, near-miss identities for the number of representations of some integral ternary quadratic forms with congruence conditions are found and proven. The genus and spinor genus of the corresponding lattice cosets are then classified. Finally, a complete genus and spinor genus classification for all conductor 2 lattice cosets of 2-adically unimodular lattices is given.



1
Introduction

For
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{N}^{3}
and
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
, we consider the ternary quadratic form

Q_{\mathbf{a}}(\mathbf{x})=a_{1}x_{1}^{2}+a_{2}x_{2}^{2}+a_{3}x_{3}^{2}

We may study the number of
representations

\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
of
n
by
Q_{\mathbf{a}}
, i.e. solutions to
Q_{\mathbf{a}}(\mathbf{x})=n
. The number of such representations is denoted by
r_{\mathbf{a}}(n)
. We may also consider representations of
n
by
Q_{\mathbf{a}}
under certain congruence conditions, i.e. given a modulus
N\in\mathbb{N}
and a vector
\mathbf{h}\in\mathbb{N}^{3}
, we can consider

r_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h},N}(n)=\#\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}:Q_{\mathbf{a}}(\mathbf{x})=n,\mathbf{x}\equiv\mathbf{h}\;(\mathrm{mod}\;N)\}.

Representations with congruence conditions are useful, for instance, in the study of quadratic polynomials, which are generalizations of quadratic forms with a non-zero linear contribution of
\mathbf{x}
. By completing squares, many quadratic polynomials may be transformed into quadratic forms under congruence conditions (see for instance
[
2
]
and
[
4
]
).

For certain special values of
\mathbf{a}
, formulae for
r_{\mathbf{a}}(n)
have been found and proven in
[
1
]
. Specifically, these special values of
\mathbf{a}
are those which have class number 1, i.e. where quadratic forms that are
p
-adically equivalent to
Q_{\mathbf{a}}
are integrally equivalent as well. Moreover, formulae for
r_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h},N}(n)
of the form

r_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h},N}(n)=d_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h},N}(n)r_{\mathbf{a}}(n)

(1.1)

are also given in
[
1
]
for certain values of
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h}
, and
N
, where
d_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h},N}(n)
only depends on the value of
n
modulo
M
for some fixed
M
depending on
\mathbf{a}
,
\mathbf{h}
, and
N
.

In this paper, we have found and proven a number of similar identities, where instead of holding for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, these identities hold for almost all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Explicitly, we have found values of
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h},N
, and
d_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h},N}(n)
for which an identity of the shape given in equation (
1.1
) holds for all positive integers
n
except those lying in a specific square class. This is the content of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For all 85 values of
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h}
, and
N
given in table
3
in appendix
A
, the identity

r_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h},N}(n)=d_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h},N}(n)r_{\mathbf{a}}(n)

holds for all
n\in\mathbb{N}\backslash\{tk^{2}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
for some positive integer
t
, where the value of
d_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h},N}(n)
depends only on
n\;(\mathrm{mod}\;M)
for some
M\in\mathbb{N}
. Here, the values of
t
,
M
, and
d_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h},N}(n)
are also given in table
3
(the value of
d_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h},N}(n)
is given under the column heading “
coeff.
”).

To explain the existence of such identities, we need to consider the lattice coset
L_{\mathbf{a}}+\mathbf{v}
where
\mathbf{v}=\frac{1}{N}\mathbf{h}
and
L_{\mathbf{a}}
is a lattice on a quadratic space defined by the quadratic form
Q_{\mathbf{a}}
(see Section
2.2
). Vectors in this lattice coset
L_{\mathbf{a}}+\mathbf{v}
correspond to
\mathbf{x}\in(\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{Z})^{3}
such that
\mathbf{x}-\frac{1}{N}\mathbf{h}\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
, which captures the congruence conditions stated above. We also consider the theta series
\Theta_{L^{\prime}+\mathbf{v}^{\prime}}
of an arbitrary lattice coset
L^{\prime}+\mathbf{v}^{\prime}
, which are generating functions of the value
\#\{\mathbf{x}\in L^{\prime}+v^{\prime}:Q_{L^{\prime}}(\mathbf{x})=n\}
. In the case of
L_{\mathbf{a}}+\mathbf{v}
(with
\mathbf{v}=\frac{1}{N}\mathbf{h}
and
\mathbf{h}\in L_{\mathbf{a}}
), the corresponding theta series is simply a generating function for
r_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h},N}(n)
. Identities of the form of equation (
1.1
) which hold for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
(given in
[
1
]
) can then be seen as resulting from the generalization of the Siegel-Weil Mass Formula to the case of lattice cosets (proven by Shimura in
[
8
]
), one version of which may be stated as:

\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{gen}^{+}(L_{\mathbf{a}}+\mathbf{v})}:=\frac{1}{\sum_{L^{\prime}+\mathbf{v}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}}|O^{+}(L^{\prime}+\mathbf{v}^{\prime})|^{-1}}\sum_{L^{\prime}+\mathbf{v}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}}\frac{\Theta_{L^{\prime}+\mathbf{v}^{\prime}}}{|O^{+}(L^{\prime}+\mathbf{v}^{\prime})|}

(1.2)

is an Eisenstein series, where
\mathcal{G}
is a set of representatives of the classes in the genus of
L_{\mathbf{a}}+\mathbf{v}
, and
O^{+}(L^{\prime}+\mathbf{v}^{\prime})
is the set of integral rotations of
L^{\prime}+\mathbf{v}^{\prime}
(see Section
2.2.2
). If
L_{\mathbf{a}}+\mathbf{v}
has class number 1, i.e. if there is only one class in the genus of
L_{\mathbf{a}}+\mathbf{v}
, then the average given on the right collapses to simply the theta series of
L_{\mathbf{a}}+\mathbf{v}
. Since Eisenstein series are generally well-known, comparing coefficients would then yield the desired identities involving
r_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h},N}(n)
and
r_{\mathbf{a}}(n)
.

A further generalization of equation (
1.2
) has also been conjectured in
[
4
]
, which may be stated as

\frac{1}{\sum_{L^{\prime}+\mathbf{v}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}|O^{+}(L^{\prime}+\mathbf{v}^{\prime})|^{-1}}\sum_{L^{\prime}+\mathbf{v}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\frac{\Theta_{L^{\prime}+\mathbf{v}^{\prime}}}{|O^{+}(L^{\prime}+\mathbf{v}^{\prime})|}=\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{gen}^{+}(L_{\mathbf{a}}+\mathbf{v})}+\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{spn}^{+}(L_{\mathbf{a}}+\mathbf{v})}

(1.3)

where
\mathcal{S}
is a set of class representatives in the spinor genus of
L_{\mathbf{a}}+\mathbf{v}
(see Section
2.2
),
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{gen}^{+}(L_{\mathbf{a}}+\mathbf{v})}
is as given in equation (
1.2
), and
\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{spn}^{+}(L_{\mathbf{a}}+\mathbf{v})}
is a linear combination of unary theta series (see Section
2.1.2
). The coefficients of a unary theta series are zero except for those corresponding to a specific square class, and so the coefficients of
\mathcal{U}
are zero except for some specific square classes. It is known that equation (
1.3
) holds in the case of lattices (i.e. when
\mathbf{v}\in L_{\mathbf{a}}
), and this conjecture is a generalization to lattice cosets. Again, if our lattice coset has only one class in its spinor genus, then the average on the left collapses as before, thus giving us an equation of the form

\Theta_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h},N}=\mathcal{E}+\mathcal{U}

(1.4)

where
\Theta_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h},N}
is the generating function of
r_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h},N}(n)
. This again yields an identity involving
r_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h},N}(n)
, but now we have some correction terms (given by coefficients of
\mathcal{U}
) corresponding to certain square classes of
n
. Of course, if the lattice coset has class number 1 as well, then
\mathcal{U}\equiv 0
. Since we want to consider examples with non-trivial
\mathcal{U}
, throughout the rest of the paper whenever a lattice coset is said to have spinor class number 1, what is really meant is that it has spinor class number 1 but not class number 1. Equation (
1.4
) leads to the following definition:

Definition
.

An identity involving
\Theta_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h},N}
of the form given in equation (
1.4
) to be a
pseudo spinor class 1 identity
.

Thus lattice cosets with spinor class number 1 (but not class number 1) are expected to satisfy pseudo spinor class 1 identities with non-zero
\mathcal{U}
. In view of the above discussion and definition, Theorem
1.1
may be reformulated and made more concrete. This is done in Theorem
3.1
, a proof of which is given in Section
3
. Theorem
1.1
follows immediately from Theorem
3.1
.

To illustrate, consider
\mathbf{a}=(1,1,1)^{T}
,
\mathbf{h}=(1,0,0)^{T}
, and
N=4
. Phrasing this in elementary terms, given
n
, we want to count the number of
(x,y,z)
such that
n=x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}
, where
x\equiv 1\;(\mathrm{mod}\;4)
, and
y
and
z
are divisible by 4. By Proposition
4.7
, the corresponding lattice coset has spinor class number 1. As expected, the theta series
\Theta_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h},4}
is found to satisfy a pseudo spinor class 1 identity, which is given in Appendix
A
. Comparing coefficients then yields the identity

r_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h},4}(n)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }n\not\equiv 1\;(\mathrm{mod}\;8),\\
\frac{1}{12}r_{\mathbf{a}}(n)&\text{if }n\equiv 1\;(\mathrm{mod}\;8)\text{ and }n\neq k^{2}\text{ for all }k\in\mathbb{N}\text{,}\\
\frac{1}{12}r_{\mathbf{a}}(n)+\frac{(-1)^{(k-1)/2}}{2}k&\text{if }n\equiv 1\;(\mathrm{mod}\;8)\text{ and }n=k^{2}\text{ for some odd }k\in\mathbb{N}.\end{cases}

In the notation of Theorem
1.1
, we have
t=1
,
M=8
, and
d_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h},4}(n)=\frac{1}{12}
for
n\equiv 1\;(\mathrm{mod}\;8)
and
d_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h},4}(n)=0
otherwise. Since
r_{\mathbf{a}}(n)
is well-known (see
[
1
]
), we now have a formula for
r_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h},4}(n)
. An example of a more complicated identity is given by

r_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h},6}(n)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }n\not\equiv 9\;(\mathrm{mod}\;36)\text{, or if }n\equiv 81\;(\mathrm{mod}\;108),\\
\frac{1}{8}r_{\mathbf{a}}(n)&\text{if }n\equiv 9,45\;(\mathrm{mod}\;216)\text{ and }n\neq 9k^{2}\text{ where }k\equiv\pm 1\;(\mathrm{mod}\;6),\\
\frac{1}{8}r_{\mathbf{a}}(n)+\frac{1}{2}k&\text{if }n\equiv 9\;(\mathrm{mod}\;216)\text{ and }n=9k^{2}\text{ for some }k\equiv 1\;(\mathrm{mod}\;6),\\
\frac{1}{8}r_{\mathbf{a}}(n)-\frac{1}{2}k&\text{if }n\equiv 9\;(\mathrm{mod}\;216)\text{ and }n=9k^{2}\text{ for some }k\equiv 5\;(\mathrm{mod}\;6),\\
\frac{1}{16}r_{\mathbf{a}}(n)&\text{if }n\equiv 117\;(\mathrm{mod}\;216),\\
\frac{1}{4}r_{\mathbf{a}}(n)&\text{if }n\equiv 153\;(\mathrm{mod}\;216),\end{cases}

where
\mathbf{a}=(1,3,9)^{T}
and
\mathbf{h}=(0,0,1)^{T}
. The value of
d_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h},4}(n)
can be determined from the above expression. As mentioned previously, formulae for
r_{\mathbf{a}}(n)
involving Hurwitz class numbers may be found in
[
1
]
.

For most of the values
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h},N
in Theorem
3.1
, a spinor classification has also been found to check whether these lattice cosets have spinor class number 1. While in most cases the lattice coset indeed had spinor class number 1, in certain cases there were multiple classes in the spinor genus (see the discussion after Proposition
4.10
in Section
4.1
). Thus the converse of equation (
1.3
), i.e. whether a lattice coset has only one class in its spinor genus if its theta series satisfies a pseudo spinor class 1 identity as given in equation (
1.4
), is false.

The method used to find the spinor classification of lattice cosets relies on the computation of the image of the group of
p
-adic rotations under the spinor norm map (see Sections
2.2
and
4
for details). Unlike in the case of lattices for which an algorithm is known, the 2-adic image under the spinor norm map in the case of lattice cosets cannot be computed very easily, and in some cases, it was not possible at all with currently known results. Assuming one can overcome this difficulty in computing the image under the spinor norm map, Section
4
gives a technique for computing the spinor genus.

On the other hand, the genus classification is quite simple to carry out, and Section
4
gives an algorithm which computes the genus of a lattice coset. This algorithm relies on Proposition
4.3
, which characterizes the group of
p
-adic rotations of a rank 3 lattice by a group of
3\times 3
matrices modulo a power of the prime
p
. This latter group is straightforward to compute by hand in small cases (see for example Section
4.2
), and by computer in larger cases. The action of the group of
p
-adic rotations on a lattice coset is equivalent to the action of this group of matrices on 3-dimensional vectors whose entries lie in
\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}
for some
k
(given by the proposition). Even though the algorithm given assumes the lattice
L_{\mathbf{a}}
is of class number 1, it may be generalized to lattices with higher class numbers by simply running the algorithm on lattice cosets of a class representative of all classes in the genus of
L_{\mathbf{a}}
.

The paper is structured as follows: In Section
2
, some important and useful results from the analytic and algebraic theory of quadratic forms have been recalled. Section
3
discusses pseudo spinor class 1 identities that were found, and also gives a proof of all 85 identities (see Theorem
3.1
). Section
4
then goes into detail regarding the classification of the found lattice cosets, along with some general discussion on the classification of lattice cosets. In particular, Section
4.1
gives a classification of many of the lattice cosets satisfying pseudo-spinor class 1 identities, and Section
4.2
classifies the genera and spinor genera of all 2-adically unimodular lattice cosets with conductor 2. Finally, Appendix
A
lists all 85 of the found identities, and Appendices
B
,
C
,
D
contain information about the genus, the image of the spinor norm map, and the action of (adelized) rotations on lattice cosets respectively, all of which are required in the genus and spinor genus classification given in Section
4.1
.

Preliminaries

2.1
Analytic Theory

2.1.1
Modular Forms

Some important definitions and preliminary results for modular forms are reproduced here; for further details one may refer to any book on modular forms (for instance
[
5
]
or
[
7
]
).

For
\gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, define the
slash
operator on a meromorphic function
f:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}
by

f\big|[\gamma]_{k/2}:=j(\gamma,z)^{-k}f\left(\frac{az+b}{cz+d}\right)

where
j(\gamma,z):=\left(\tfrac{c}{d}\right)\epsilon_{d}^{-1}\sqrt{cz+d}
, and where
\epsilon_{d}
is 1 if
d\equiv 1\;(\mathrm{mod}\;4)
and is
i
if
d\equiv-1\;(\mathrm{mod}\;4)
. Define the following
congruence subgroups
of
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}(N)

\displaystyle:=\left\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):N|c\right\},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}(N)

\displaystyle:=\left\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Gamma_{0}(N):a,d\equiv 1\;(\mathrm{mod}\;N)\right\}.

Definition

(Modular Forms)
.

Suppose
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\chi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
a Dirichlet character, and
\Gamma\leq\Gamma_{0}(4)
a (congruence) subgroup of finite index containing
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. Suppose
f:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}
is a meromorphic function satisfying

f\big|[\gamma]_{k/2}=\chi(d)f\qquad\qquad\forall\gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Gamma.

Such a meromorphic function
f
is said to satisfy
modularity of weight
k/2
and character
\chi
for
\Gamma
.

If
f:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}
is a holomorphic function satisfying modularity of weight
k/2
and character
\chi
for the congruence subgroup
\Gamma
, and if
f|[\gamma]_{k/2}
is bounded as
z
approaches
i\infty
for all
\gamma\in SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, then
f
is said to be a
modular form
of weight
k/2
and character
\chi
for
\Gamma
. The space of modular forms of weight
k/2
, character
\chi
, and congruence subgroup
\Gamma
, will be denoted by
\mathcal{M}_{k/2}(\Gamma,\chi)
.

If
f
is a modular form (of some weight and character) for the congruence subgroup
\Gamma
containing
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, then
f
admits a Fourier series expansion

f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}q^{n}.

Here,
q:=e^{2\pi iz}
.

It is well-known that the space of modular forms
\mathcal{M}_{k/2}(\Gamma,\chi)
is a finite-dimensional complex vector space. Moreover, as a result of the
valence formula
for modular forms, we have the following lemma (c.f.
[
1
, Lemma 2.1]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}q^{n}
is a modular form of weight
k/2
and character
\chi
for the congruence subgroup
\Gamma
, and if
a_{n}=0
for all
n\leq\frac{k}{12}[SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):\Gamma]
, then
f
is identically zero.

In particular, this means that to prove any identity involving modular forms, it suffices to show that the first few coefficients of the corresponding Fourier series expansions are equal.

2.1.2
Theta Series

For the quadratic form
Q_{\mathbf{a}}
with the congruence conditions
\mathbf{h}
and
N
, we may consider the generating function of
r_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h},N}(n)
, which is a
theta series
given by

\Theta_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h},N}(z):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}r_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h},N}(n)q^{n}

(where
q:=e^{2\pi iz}
). Without congruence conditions, we have

\Theta_{\mathbf{a}}(z)=\Theta_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{0},1}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}r_{\mathbf{a}}(n)q^{n}.

Let
\chi_{m}:=\left(\tfrac{m}{\cdot}\right)
denote the Kronecker symbol; this is a Dirichlet character if
m\neq 0
and
m\not\equiv 3\;(\mathrm{mod}\;4)
. Also, let
\Gamma_{N,M}
denote the congruence subgroup given by

\Gamma_{N,M}:=\Gamma_{0}(N)\cap\Gamma_{1}(M)=\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):a,d\equiv 1\;(\mathrm{mod}\;M),M|c,N|c\}.

It is easy to check that whenever
4|N
, the congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{N,M}
is a subgroup of
\Gamma_{0}(4)
containing
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
.

We have the following lemma (for a proof see, for instance,
[
1
]
).

Lemma 2.2
.

The theta series
\Theta_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h},N}
is a modular form of weight
3/2
and character
\chi_{4a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}}
for the congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{4N^{2}l,N}
, where
l=l_{\mathbf{a}}:=\mathrm{lcm}(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})
.

In particular, the theta series
\Theta_{\mathbf{a}}
is a modular form of weight
3/2
and character
\chi_{4a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}}
for the congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{4l,1}=\Gamma_{0}(4l)
.

In view of Lemma
2.1
, the following will be useful (for a proof see
[
1
, Lemma 2.2]
).

Lemma 2.3
.

If
N,M\in\mathbb{N}
with
M|N
, then
[SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):\Gamma_{N,M}]=NM\left(\prod_{p|N}\left(1+\tfrac{1}{p}\right)\right)\left(\prod_{p|M}\left(1-\tfrac{1}{p}\right)\right)
.

We will also consider
unary theta series
.

Definition

(Unary Theta Series)
.

Given a character
\chi
and an integer
t\in\mathbb{N}
, the
unary theta series

\theta_{\chi,t}
is given by the following Fourier series expansion

\theta_{\chi,t}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\chi(n)nq^{tn^{2}}.

It is a modular form of weight
3/2
and character
\chi\chi_{-4t}
for the congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(4tf_{\chi^{2}})
, where
f_{\chi}
is the conductor of the character
\chi
(this follows from
[
7
, Theorem 1.44]
and
[
7
, Proposition 2.22(1)]
).

2.1.3
Operators on Coefficients of Modular Forms

In this paper, we will need to consider the following sieving operator defined using the Fourier series expansion of a modular form:

f\big|S_{M,m}(z)=\sum_{n\equiv m\;(\mathrm{mod}\;M)}a_{n}q^{n}

where
M,m\in\mathbb{N}
are given, and
f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}q^{n}
(with
q:=e^{2\pi iz}
) is the Fourier expansion of the modular form
f
. The following lemma describes the action of the sieve
|S_{M,m}
on spaces of modular forms.

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose
f\in\mathcal{M}_{k/2}(\Gamma_{N,L},\chi)
for some
N,L\in\mathbb{N}
, some weight
k/2
, and some character
\chi
with conductor
f_{\chi}
. Then, for any
M,m\in\mathbb{N}
, the function
f|S_{M,m}
is a modular form. More precisely, we have
f|S_{M,m}\in\mathcal{M}_{k/2}(\Gamma_{N^{\prime},L^{\prime}},\chi)
, where

N^{\prime}=\begin{cases}\mathrm{lcm}(N,M^{2},f_{\chi}M,ML)&\text{if }M\not\equiv 2\;(\mathrm{mod}\;4),\\
\mathrm{lcm}(N,4M^{2},f_{\chi}M,ML)&\text{otherwise},\\
\end{cases}\quad\text{ and }\quad L^{\prime}=\begin{cases}L&\text{if }M|24,\\
\mathrm{lcm}(M,L)&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

A proof may be found, for instance, in
[
1
, Lemma 2.3]
.

2.2
Algebraic Theory

On the algebraic side of things, we will use the theory of quadratic spaces and lattices as described in O’Meara’s standard text
[
6
]
; of course, rather than working in an arbitrary algebraic number field, we will only work in
\mathbb{Q}
and the
p
-adic numbers
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. The ring of integers in
\mathbb{Q}
and
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
will be denoted as usual by
\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. We consider a
quadratic space

V=\mathbb{Q}^{k}
which is a vector space equipped with the quadratic form
Q
and associated symmetric bilinear form
B
. In this paper, we consider free
lattices
L
on
V
, which are essentially free
\mathbb{Z}
-modules of
V
generated by some basis
\{\mathbf{v}_{1},...,\mathbf{v}_{k}\}
for
V
. Given a basis
\{\mathbf{v}_{1},...,\mathbf{v}_{k}\}
for
L
, we may write
L\cong A
where
A
is the
k\times k
Gram matrix
A=(B(\mathbf{v}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{j}))
. The
discriminant

dL
of a lattice is simply the determinant of
A
, and is well-defined and independent of the choice of basis up to multiplication by a unit. The
localization
of a lattice
L
at the prime
p
is
L_{p}=L\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
; the localization
L_{p}
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-lattice in the localization
V_{p}=\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{k}
. The
rank
of a lattice
L
(resp.
L_{p}
) is the dimension of the quadratic space
\mathbb{Q}L
(resp.
\mathbb{Q}_{p}L_{p}
), or equivalently is the rank when considered as a free module over
\mathbb{Z}
(resp. over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
). For a lattice
L
and some
\mathbf{v}\in V
, we have the
lattice coset

L+\mathbf{v}
, which may be considered to be a coset in the quotient group
(L+\mathbb{Z}\mathbf{v})/L
. The smallest positive integer
N
such that
N\mathbf{v}\in L

of
\mathbf{v}
.

An

\sigma
of the quadratic space

equipped with the quadratic form

is a linear transformation

satisfying

for all

. The subgroup

of

of

are those isometries with determinant 1. The coordinate representation

, where

in some basis. We similarly define the groups

and

of

of (integral) rotations of a lattice

while the subgroup
O^{+}(L_{p})
of

As a consequence of

, an element of

belongs to

if its coordinate representation in a

(

In this paper, we will only be working with lattices and lattice cosets on quadratic spaces with rank 3. For any other unexplained notation please see

2.2.1

As we will be using the spinor norm map repeatedly in later sections, some important definitions and results are given here. For more details see

\displaystyle\mathrm{cls}^{+}(L+\mathbf{v})

]

where the product runs over all primes

, and

is the set of all idèles over

. This is extremely useful in classifying the classes in a genus into its various spinor genera.

3
Pseudo Spinor Class 1 Identities

Using a computer, lattice cosets were found whose corresponding theta series satisfied identities of the type

\Theta_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h},N}=\mathcal{E}+\mathcal{U}

(3.1)

where
\mathcal{E}
is an Eisenstein series and
\mathcal{U}
is a unary theta series. Such an identity was called a
pseudo spinor class 1 identity
since the theta series of lattice cosets with spinor class number 1 (are expected to) satisfy such identities. In this computer search, we have the following restrictions on
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{U}
.

•

\mathcal{E}
is a linear combination of
\Theta_{\mathbf{a}}\big|S_{M,m}
for
M,m\in\mathbb{N}
. Essentially, we are adding the extra restriction that all solutions
\mathbf{x}
to
Q_{\mathbf{a}}(\mathbf{x})=n
with
\mathbf{x}\equiv\mathbf{h}\;(\mathrm{mod}\;N)
are primitive solutions.

•

\mathcal{U}
consists of unary theta series for a fixed value of
t\in\mathbb{N}
, where the unary theta series are of the form
c\cdot\theta_{\chi,t}
or
c\cdot\theta_{\chi,t}\big|S_{M,m}
for some constant
c
, some character
\chi
, and some
M,m\in\mathbb{N}
.

These restrictions were placed only to simplify the search process. The results of the computer search are given in the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

For all 85 values of
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h}
, and
N
given in Table
3
in Appendix
A
, the theta series
\Theta_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h},N}
satisfies a pseudo-spinor class 1 identity. More concretely, we have the following identity

\Theta_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h},N}=\mathcal{E}+\mathcal{U}

where
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{U}
are given in Table
3
.

Proof.

For each given
\mathbf{a}
,
\mathbf{h}
, and
N
, we consider
f_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h},N}:=\Theta_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h},N}-\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{U}
. Using the results listed in Sections
2.1.2
and
2.1.3
, and noting that if
\Gamma^{\prime}\leq\Gamma
then
\mathcal{M}_{k/2}(\Gamma,\chi)\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{k/2}(\Gamma^{\prime},\chi)
, it can be seen that
f_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{h},N}
is a modular form of weight
3/2
and congruence subgroup
\Gamma
, where
\Gamma
is given in Table
3
. Lemma
2.3
is then used to compute
[SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):\Gamma]
. In view of Lemma
2.1
, it suffices to check that all coefficients

of

are zero for all positive integers

less than

\frac{1}{12}\cdot\frac{3}{2}[SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):\Gamma]=\frac{1}{8}[SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):\Gamma].

The value
\frac{1}{8}[SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):\Gamma]
is given in the last column of Table

. Using a computer, it can then be checked that

for all

, which then establishes the required identity. Such a computer verification up to the valence bound was carried out for all 85 identities.
∎

4
Classification of Lattice Cosets

The general strategy to classify the spinor genus of the lattice coset
NL_{\mathbf{a}}+\mathbf{h}
is to first find the genus of
NL_{\mathbf{a}}+\mathbf{h}
, and then to calculate the number of spinor genera in the genus (using equation (
2.4
)). Once we know the genus and the number of spinor genera, it is then a matter of finding which lattice cosets in the genus belong to the same orbit under the group action
O^{+}(V)O^{\prime}_{\mathbb{A}}(V)
. If we can find elements of
O^{+}(V)O^{\prime}_{\mathbb{A}}(V)
that map certain lattice cosets to other lattice cosets, then by knowing the number of orbits expected (i.e. the number of spinor genera), we can establish the decomposition into spinor genera. This general strategy is used in
[
9
]
as well as in
[
4
]
.

The classification of the genus is fairly straightforward, and makes use of the following results.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose
L\cong\mathrm{diag}\{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\}
in the basis
\mathbf{v}_{1},\mathbf{v}_{2},\mathbf{v}_{3}
for
V
. The group
O^{+}(L)
is then given by

O^{+}(L)=\{\sigma\in O^{+}(V):\sigma(\mathbf{v}_{i})=\pm\mathbf{v}_{j_{i}}\text{, where }a_{i}=a_{j_{i}}\text{, and }j_{1},j_{2},j_{3}\text{ is a permutation of }1,2,3\}.

Proof.

It is easy to see that
O^{+}(L)
contains all isometries given in the above set. If
\sigma\in O^{+}(L)
, then
L\cong\left<\sigma\mathbf{v}_{1}\right>\perp\left<\sigma\mathbf{v}_{2}\right>\perp\left<\sigma\mathbf{v}_{3}\right>\cong\left<\mathbf{v}_{1}\right>\perp\left<\mathbf{v}_{2}\right>\perp\left<\mathbf{v}_{3}\right>
, where
Q(\sigma\mathbf{v}_{i})=Q(\mathbf{v}_{i})
. However, such decompositions are unique by
[
6
, 105:1]
. The result follows.
∎

Thus the action of
O^{+}(L)
on
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})
is essentially to permute
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}
among themselves, along with some sign changes. Now, if
L_{\mathbf{a}}
has class number 1, then the proper genus of the lattice coset
NL_{\mathbf{a}}+\mathbf{h}
consists of classes of the form
\mathrm{cls}^{+}(NL_{\mathbf{a}}+\mathbf{h}^{\prime})
. Since
\mathrm{cls}^{+}(NL_{\mathbf{a}}+\mathbf{h}^{\prime})
is the orbit of
NL_{\mathbf{a}}+\mathbf{h}^{\prime}
under
O^{+}(L_{\mathbf{a}})
, and since
O^{+}(L_{\mathbf{a}})
is a finite group by the lemma, the class is easily enumerated once we have a class representative.

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose
L
is a definite lattice with class number 1, and consider the lattice coset
NL+\mathbf{h}
, where
\mathbf{h}\in L
. Suppose
\mathcal{G}
is a set of class representatives of all classes in the genus of
NL+\mathbf{h}
. Then,

\sum_{NL+\mathbf{h}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}}\frac{1}{|O^{+}(NL+\mathbf{h}^{\prime})|}=\frac{1}{|O^{+}(L)|}\prod_{p|N}[O^{+}(L_{p}):O^{+}(NL_{p}+\mathbf{h})].

(4.1)

Proof.

From the Mass Formula for lattices as well as for lattice cosets we have the following two equations (c.f.
[
9
, Corollary 2.5]
).

\displaystyle\sum_{NL+\mathbf{h}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}}\frac{1}{|O^{+}(NL+\mathbf{h}^{\prime})|}

\displaystyle=2\mu_{\infty_{\mathbb{Q}}}(O^{+}(V_{\infty_{\mathbb{Q}}}))^{-1}\prod_{p}\mu_{p}(O^{+}(NL_{p}+\mathbf{h}))^{-1}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|O^{+}(L)|}

\displaystyle=2\mu_{\infty_{\mathbb{Q}}}(O^{+}(V_{\infty_{\mathbb{Q}}}))^{-1}\prod_{p}\mu_{p}(O^{+}(L_{p}))^{-1}

where the product runs over all primes
p
, and
\mu
is the Tamagawa measure on
O^{+}_{\mathbb{A}}(V)
. Notice that there is only one term on the left of the second equation as
L
has class number 1. Dividing gives

\sum_{NL+\mathbf{h}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}}\frac{1}{|O^{+}(NL+\mathbf{h}^{\prime})|}=\frac{1}{|O^{+}(L)|}\prod_{p}\frac{\mu_{p}(O^{+}(L_{p}))}{\mu_{p}(O^{+}(NL_{p}+\mathbf{h}))}.

For
p\nmid N
, we have
NL_{p}+\mathbf{h}=L_{p}
, while for
p|N
we have

\mu_{p}(O^{+}(L_{p}))=\mu_{p}(O^{+}(NL_{p}+\mathbf{h}))[O^{+}(L_{p}):O^{+}(NL_{p}+\mathbf{h})].

Substituting back gives the required result.
∎

Thus, if we are given a list of distinct class representatives in a genus, we can use the above lemma to check whether these exhaust all classes in the genus.
Let us now try and compute
[O^{+}(L_{p}):O^{+}(NL_{p}+\mathbf{h})]
.

Suppose
L\cong\mathrm{diag}\{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\}=:A
in the standard basis
\mathbf{e}_{1},\mathbf{e}_{2},\mathbf{e}_{3}
for
\mathbb{Q}^{3}
, and consider the lattice coset
NL+\mathbf{h}
where
\mathbf{h}
has conductor
N
. For the statement (and proof) of Proposition
4.3
,
L
need not have class number 1. Suppose
p
is a prime dividing
N
. Let
k:=\mathrm{ord}_{p}N
,
\ell:=\mathrm{ord}_{p}(\mathrm{lcm}\{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\})
, and let
\delta
be given by

\delta=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }p\neq 2,\\
1&\text{if }p=2\text{ and }k\geq 2,\\
2&\text{if }p=2\text{ and }k=1.\end{cases}

Let
q=p^{k}
and
q^{\prime}=p^{k+\ell+\delta}
. Consider the following two groups:

G=G_{L,q^{\prime},q}:=\{X+qM_{3}(\mathbb{Z}/q^{\prime}\mathbb{Z}):X\in SL_{3}(\mathbb{Z}/q^{\prime}\mathbb{Z}),X^{T}AX\equiv A\;(\mathrm{mod}\;q^{\prime})\},

(4.2)

H=H_{L,\mathbf{h},q^{\prime},q}=\{X\in G:X\mathbf{h}\equiv\mathbf{h}\;(\mathrm{mod}\;q)\}.

(4.3)

Essentially,
G
is the reduction modulo

of the group of matrices in
M_{3}(\mathbb{Z}/q^{\prime}\mathbb{Z})
that preserve

and have determinant 1.

Proposition 4.3
.

Under the above assumptions and notation, there is a natural surjective homomorphism
\Psi
of groups from
O^{+}(L_{p})
onto
G
, where
\Psi
maps
\sigma\in O^{+}(L_{p})
to the coordinate matrix of
\sigma
in the basis
\{\mathbf{e}_{i}\}
, reduced modulo
q
. Moreover,
\Psi
maps
O^{+}(NL_{p}+\mathbf{h})
onto
H
.

Proof.

First consider the homomorphism
\Psi_{1}
mapping
\sigma\in O^{+}(L_{p})
to its coordinate matrix in the standard basis; this image is in
SL_{3}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
since
\sigma
preserves
L_{p}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathbf{e}_{1}+\mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathbf{e}_{2}+\mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathbf{e}_{3}
. The image in
SL_{3}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
of this map is

G^{\prime}=\{X\in SL_{3}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}):X^{T}AX=A,\det X=1\}

since
\sigma
is a rotation. Next, consider the homomorphism
\Psi_{2}
which takes a matrix in
G^{\prime}
and reduces modulo
q
to a matrix in
G
. Note that, as
\mathbb{Z}_{p}/q\mathbb{Z}_{p}\cong\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}
, reduction modulo

does indeed give an element of
\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}
. As
\Psi=\Psi_{2}\circ\Psi_{1}
, it follows that
\Psi
is a well-defined homomorphism from
O^{+}(L)
into
G
. The surjectivity of
\Psi
follows if
\Psi_{2}
is surjective; this we now show.

Without loss of generality, we may suppose that
a_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
, and
1=|a_{1}|_{p}\geq|a_{2}|_{p}\geq|a_{3}|_{p}
. Then,
\ell=\mathrm{ord}_{p}a_{3}
. Consider any
X\in G
, and we may as well suppose that
X\in M_{3}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, that

, and that
\det X\equiv 1\;(\mathrm{mod}\;q^{\prime})
. We will be done if we can find

such that
Y\equiv X\;(\mathrm{mod}\;q)
.

Let
X=(x_{ij})
. Comparing the
(1,2)

\sum_{i=1}^{3}a_{i}x_{i1}x_{i2}\equiv 0\;(\mathrm{mod}\;q^{\prime})

\eta:=-\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}\sum_{i=1}^{3}a_{i}x_{i1}x_{i2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}

\sum_{i=1}^{3}a_{i}x_{i1}^{2}\equiv a_{1}\;(\mathrm{mod}\;q^{\prime})
where
p|q^{\prime}
and
a_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
, it follows that the ideal
\left<a_{i}x_{i1}:i=1,2,3\right>
in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
contains a unit, and so is equal to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Thus, there exists
\xi_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{3}\xi_{i}\cdot a_{i}x_{i1}=\eta=-\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}\sum_{i=1}^{3}a_{i}x_{i1}x_{i2}

and so
\sum_{i=1}^{3}a_{i}x_{i1}(x_{i2}+q^{\prime}\xi_{i})=0
. Let
X_{1}
be the matrix obtained from
X
by adding
q^{\prime}\xi_{i}
to the
(i,2)
entry. Then,
X_{1}\equiv X\;(\mathrm{mod}\;q^{\prime})
, which implies
X_{1}^{T}AX_{1}\equiv A\;(\mathrm{mod}\;q^{\prime})
. Moreover, by construction, the
(1,2)
-entry (and hence the
(2,1)
-entry) of
X_{1}^{T}AX_{1}
is zero. Let
X_{1}=(x^{\prime}_{ij})
(so
x^{\prime}_{ij}=x_{ij}
for
j\neq 2
, and
x^{\prime}_{i2}=x_{i2}+\xi_{i}q^{\prime}
). Since the
(i,j)
-entry of
(X_{1}^{T})^{-1}
is
(-1)^{i+j}/\det X_{1}
times the determinant of the
2\times 2
matrix obtained from
X_{1}
by deleting the
i
’th row and
j
’th column, and since
\det X_{1}\equiv 1\;(\mathrm{mod}\;q^{\prime})
, the following three congruences then follow from
AX_{1}\equiv(X_{1}^{T})^{-1}A\;(\mathrm{mod}\;q^{\prime})

\displaystyle a_{1}x^{\prime}_{13}

\displaystyle\equiv a_{3}(x_{21}^{\prime}x_{32}^{\prime}-x_{31}^{\prime}x_{22}^{\prime})\;(\mathrm{mod}\;q^{\prime}),

\displaystyle a_{2}x^{\prime}_{23}

\displaystyle\equiv a_{3}(x_{12}^{\prime}x_{31}^{\prime}-x_{11}^{\prime}x_{32}^{\prime})\;(\mathrm{mod}\;q^{\prime}),

\displaystyle a_{3}x^{\prime}_{33}

\displaystyle\equiv a_{3}(x_{11}^{\prime}x_{22}^{\prime}-x_{21}^{\prime}x_{12}^{\prime})\;(\mathrm{mod}\;q^{\prime}).

Now, notice that
q^{\prime}\mathbb{Z}_{p}=p^{\delta}a_{3}q\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, and so there exists
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
such that

\displaystyle a_{1}x^{\prime}_{13}

\displaystyle=a_{3}(x_{21}^{\prime}x_{32}^{\prime}-x_{31}^{\prime}x_{22}^{\prime})+p^{\delta}a_{3}q\alpha_{1},

\displaystyle a_{2}x^{\prime}_{23}

\displaystyle=a_{3}(x_{12}^{\prime}x_{31}^{\prime}-x_{11}^{\prime}x_{32}^{\prime})+p^{\delta}a_{3}q\alpha_{2},

\displaystyle a_{3}x^{\prime}_{33}

\displaystyle=a_{3}(x_{11}^{\prime}x_{22}^{\prime}-x_{21}^{\prime}x_{12}^{\prime})+p^{\delta}a_{3}q\alpha_{3}.

A straightforward computation then yields

\sum_{i=1}^{3}a_{i}x^{\prime}_{ij}\left(x^{\prime}_{i3}-p^{\delta}q\frac{a_{3}}{a_{i}}\alpha_{i}\right)=0\qquad\text{for }j=1,2,

where
a_{3}/a_{1},a_{3}/a_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Consider
X_{2}=(x_{ij}^{\prime\prime})
given by
x_{ij}^{\prime\prime}=x_{ij}^{\prime}
for
j=1,2
, and
x_{i3}^{\prime\prime}=x^{\prime}_{i3}-p^{\delta}q\frac{a_{3}}{a_{i}}\alpha_{i}
; then
X_{2}\equiv X_{1}\equiv X\;(\mathrm{mod}\;p^{\delta}q)
and
X_{2}^{T}AX_{2}=\mathrm{diag}\{\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3}\}
where

\displaystyle\mu_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{3}a_{i}x_{i1}^{2}

\displaystyle\mu_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{3}a_{i}(x^{\prime}_{i2})^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}a_{i}(x_{i2}+q^{\prime}\xi_{i})^{2}

\displaystyle\mu_{3}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{3}a_{i}(x^{\prime\prime}_{i3})^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}a_{i}\left(x_{i3}-p^{\delta}q\frac{a_{3}}{a_{i}}\alpha_{i}\right)^{2}.

For
j=1,2
, it is clear that
\mu_{j}\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{3}a_{i}x_{ij}^{2}\equiv a_{j}\;(\mathrm{mod}\;q^{\prime})
. For
\mu_{3}
, a simple computation gives

\displaystyle\mu_{3}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{3}a_{i}x_{i3}^{2}+p^{\delta}a_{3}q\left(-2\alpha_{1}x_{13}+p^{\delta}q\left(\frac{a_{3}}{a_{1}}\right)\alpha_{1}^{2}-2\alpha_{2}x_{23}+p^{\delta}q\left(\frac{a_{3}}{a_{2}}\right)\alpha_{2}^{2}-2\alpha_{3}x_{33}+p^{\delta}q\alpha_{3}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\equiv a_{3}\;(\mathrm{mod}\;q^{\prime}).

Thus
\mu_{j}/a_{j}\equiv 1\;(\mathrm{mod}\;p^{\delta}q)
for
j=1,2,3
. The local square theorem (see
[
6
, 63:1]
) then implies that
\mu_{j}=a_{j}(\mu_{j}^{\prime})^{2}
for some
\mu_{j}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
satisfying
\mu_{j}^{\prime}\equiv 1\;(\mathrm{mod}\;q)
(the
p^{\delta}
term guarantees these congruences when
p=2
). Therefore, the matrix
Y:=(\mathrm{diag}\{\mu_{1}^{\prime},\mu_{2}^{\prime},\mu_{3}^{\prime}\})^{-1}X_{2}
satisfies
Y\equiv X\;(\mathrm{mod}\;q)
and

Y^{T}AY=(\mathrm{diag}\{(\mu_{1}^{\prime})^{-2},(\mu_{2}^{\prime})^{-2},(\mu_{3}^{\prime})^{-2}\})X^{T}AX=A.

Finally, as
\det Y\equiv\det X\equiv 1\;(\mathrm{mod}\;q)
and
(\det Y)^{2}=1
, it follows that
\det Y=1
if
q>2
. For
q=2
however, notice that we may flip the sign of any of the
\mu_{j}
’s if necessary so that
\det Y=1
in this case as well; as
-1\equiv 1\;(\mathrm{mod}\;q)
we still maintain
Y\equiv X\;(\mathrm{mod}\;q)
. Hence, we have found
Y\in G^{\prime}
such that
Y\equiv X\;(\mathrm{mod}\;q)
, and so
\Psi
is surjective.

Finally, it is clear that
\Psi
maps
O^{+}(NL_{p}+\mathbf{h})
into
H
, since any
\sigma\in O^{+}(NL_{p}+\mathbf{h})
preserves
\mathbf{h}
modulo
NL_{p}=N\mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathbf{e}_{1}+N\mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathbf{e}_{2}+N\mathbb{Z}_{p}\mathbf{e}_{3}
. On the other hand, for
X\in H
, by the surjectivity of
\Psi
we have
\sigma\in O^{+}(L_{p})
such that
\sigma\mathbf{h}\equiv X\mathbf{h}\equiv\mathbf{h}\;(\mathrm{mod}\;q)
(here,
\sigma\mathbf{h}=Y\mathbf{h}
where
Y=\Psi_{1}\sigma\equiv X\;(\mathrm{mod}\;q)
is the coordinate matrix of
\sigma
in the standard basis
\mathbf{e}_{i}
). Thus
\sigma\mathbf{h}\equiv\mathbf{h}\;(\mathrm{mod}\;NL_{p})
(noting that
N\mathbb{Z}_{p}=q\mathbb{Z}_{p}
), and so
\sigma(NL_{p}+\mathbf{h})=NL_{p}+\mathbf{h}
. Hence
\Psi
maps
O^{+}(NL_{p}+\mathbf{h})
onto
H
.
∎

Corollary 4.3.1
.

With the same conditions and notation of Proposition
4.3
, we have

\displaystyle[O^{+}(L_{p}):O^{+}(NL_{p}+\mathbf{h})]=[G_{L,q^{\prime},q}:H_{L,\mathbf{h},q^{\prime},q}]=\frac{|G_{L,q^{\prime},q}|}{|H_{L,\mathbf{h},q^{\prime},q}|}.

Proof.

This follows from elementary group theory, noting that
\Psi(O^{+}(L_{p}))=G
,
\Psi(O^{+}(NL_{p}+\mathbf{h}))=H
, and
\ker\Psi\subset O^{+}(NL_{p}+\mathbf{h})
.
∎

From a computational viewpoint Proposition
4.3
is useful. The orbit of a lattice coset
NL_{p}+\mathbf{h}
(
\mathbf{h}=h_{1}\mathbf{e}_{1}+h_{2}\mathbf{e}_{2}+h_{3}\mathbf{e}_{3}
) under the infinite group
O^{+}(L_{p})
is the same as the orbit of vectors
(h_{1},h_{2},h_{3})\in(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^{3}
under the finite group
G
(the latter group action is simply multiplication of a matrix with a vector modulo
q
). If
L
is a class 1 lattice, then the classes in the orbit of
NL+\mathbf{h}
under
O_{\mathbb{A}}(V)
are the same as the classes in the orbit of
NL+\mathbf{h}
under
O_{\mathbb{A}}(L)=\prod_{p}O^{+}(L_{p})
, the latter of which can be computed by the action of
\prod_{p}G_{L,q^{\prime},q}
on
\mathbf{h}\;(\mathrm{mod}\;q)
. Since
G_{L,q^{\prime},q}
is finite, and since we only need to study the action of
G_{L,q^{\prime},q}
on
NL_{p}+\mathbf{h}
for the finite number of primes
p|N
, it is possible for a computer to list the genus of
NL+\mathbf{h}
by listing out all elements in
G_{L,q^{\prime},q}
. Lemma
4.2
can then be used to prove that this list is complete, where
[O^{+}(L_{p}):O^{+}(NL_{p}+\mathbf{h})]
is computed using Corollary
4.3.1
(the computer should have already listed out all elements of
G
). Of course, for extremely large values of
N
and
dL
, this computation may not necessarily be feasible due to the large value of
q^{\prime}
. For reasonable values on the other hand, we now have a simple algorithm to find the genus of
NL+\mathbf{h}
whenever
L
has class number 1.

This leaves the problem of listing out elements of
G_{L,q^{\prime},q}=G
. In small cases, this is an elementary exercise in solving a system of congruences (an example of which is carried out in section
4.2
). In larger cases, a computer may be able to list out all matrices
X\in SL_{3}(\mathbb{Z}/q^{\prime}\mathbb{Z})
satisfying
X^{T}AX\equiv A\;(\mathrm{mod}\;q^{\prime})
and reducing modulo
q
. A simple straightforward method would be to first find all
\mathbf{x}_{j}=(x_{1j},x_{2j},x_{3j})^{T}\in(\mathbb{Z}/q^{\prime}\mathbb{Z})^{3}
satisfying
a_{1}x_{1j}^{2}+a_{2}x_{2j}^{2}+a_{3}x_{3j}^{2}\equiv a_{j}\;(\mathrm{mod}\;q^{\prime})
for each
j=1,2,3
, and then go through every choice of columns (one from each list) and check whether
X=[\mathbf{x}_{1}\,\,\mathbf{x}_{2}\,\,\mathbf{x}_{3}]
satisfies all conditions. This brute force approach can be quite slow for large values of
q^{\prime}
, but smarter, more efficient approaches to listing out all elements of
G_{L,q^{\prime},q}
can reduce computation time.

The next step in the classification of spinor genera would be to compute the number of spinor genera, using equation (
2.4
). The following two lemmas do this in some special cases.

Lemma 4.4
.

Fix a prime number
q
. Suppose
NL+\mathbf{h}
is a lattice coset such that the following conditions hold

•

\theta(O^{+}(NL_{p}+\mathbf{h}))=\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times})^{2}
for all primes
p\neq q

•

Either
\theta(O^{+}(NL_{q}+\mathbf{h}))\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{\times}(\mathbb{Q}_{q}^{\times})^{2}
, or
q(\mathbb{Q}_{q}^{\times})^{2}\in\theta(O^{+}(NL_{q}+\mathbf{h}))
.

Then the number of spinor genera in
\mathrm{gen}^{+}(NL+\mathbf{h})
is
[\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{\times}(\mathbb{Q}_{q}^{\times})^{2}:\theta(O^{+}(NL_{q}+\mathbf{h}))\cap\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{\times}(\mathbb{Q}_{q}^{\times})^{2}]
.

Proof.

Let
n=[\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{\times}(\mathbb{Q}_{q}^{\times})^{2}:\theta(O^{+}(NL_{q}+\mathbf{h}))\cap\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{\times}(\mathbb{Q}_{q}^{\times})^{2}]
and
P=\prod_{p}\theta(O^{+}(NL_{p}+\mathbf{h}))
. By equation (
2.4
), we just need to prove that
[J_{\mathbb{Q}}:\mathbb{Q}^{\times}P]=n
. Let
\mathfrak{i}^{(k)}
(
1\leq k\leq n
) be idèles in
J_{\mathbb{Q}}
such that
\mathfrak{i}_{p}^{(k)}=1
for and
p\neq q
all
1\leq k\leq n
, and such that
\{\mathfrak{i}_{q}^{(k)}:1\leq k\leq n\}
is a complete set of coset-representatives of the quotient group
\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{\times}(\mathbb{Q}_{q}^{\times})^{2}/\left(\theta(O^{+}(NL_{q}+\mathbf{h}))\cap\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{\times}(\mathbb{Q}_{q}^{\times})^{2}\right)
, where (without loss of generality), each of the
\mathfrak{i}_{q}^{(k)}
are
q
-adic units, and
\mathfrak{i}_{q}^{(1)}=1
. We will be done if we show that
\{\mathfrak{i}^{(k)}:k=1,...,n\}
is a complete set of coset-representatives of
J_{\mathbb{Q}}/(\mathbb{Q}^{\times}P)
.

To check that
\{\mathfrak{i}^{(k)}:1\leq k\leq n\}
is a set of
n
distinct coset representatives of
J_{\mathbb{Q}}
modulo
\mathbb{Q}^{\times}P
, notice that
\mathfrak{i}^{(k)}(\mathfrak{i}^{(k^{\prime})})^{-1}\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}P
is equivalent to
\mathfrak{i}^{(m)}\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}P
where
m=1,2,...,n
satisfies
(\mathfrak{i}^{(m)}_{q})\in\mathfrak{i}_{q}^{(k)}(\mathfrak{i}_{q}^{(k^{\prime})})^{-1}(\mathbb{Q}^{\times}P)
(such an
m
exists as the
\mathfrak{i}_{q}^{(k)}
are a a complete of set of representatives modulo
\mathbb{Q}^{\times}P
). Thus it suffices to check that each of the
\mathfrak{i}^{(k)}
are non-trivial for
k\geq 2
modulo
\mathbb{Q}^{\times}P
. So suppose that
\mathfrak{i}^{(k)}
(
k\geq 2
) is trivial, i.e. there exists
x\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
such that
x\mathfrak{i}^{(k)}\in P
. Then
x=x\mathfrak{i}_{p}^{(k)}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times})^{2}
for all
p\neq q
, and
x\mathfrak{i}_{q}^{(k)}\in\theta(O^{+}(NL_{q}+\mathbf{h}))
. The former implies that
\mathrm{ord}_{p}x
is even for all primes
p\neq q
, and so
x\in\mathbb{Q}^{2}\cup q\mathbb{Q}^{2}
. If
x=y^{2}
for some
y\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
, then
x\in(\mathbb{Q}_{q}^{\times})^{2}
and so
\mathfrak{i}_{q}^{(k)}\in\theta(O^{+}(NL_{q}+\mathbf{h}))
, contradicting the assumption that
\mathfrak{i}_{q}^{(k)}
is a
q
-adic unit not in
\theta(O^{+}(NL_{q}+\mathbf{h}))
. Now suppose
x=qy^{2}
for some
y\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
; then
q\mathfrak{i}_{q}^{(k)}\in\theta(O^{+}(NL_{q}+\mathbf{h}))
. By the second condition, this implies that
q\in\theta(O^{+}(NL_{q}+\mathbf{h}))
, and so
\mathfrak{i}_{q}^{(k)}\in\theta(O^{+}(NL_{q}+\mathbf{h}))
, which is again a contradiction.

Now, consider any idèle
\mathfrak{j}\in J_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Since
\mathfrak{j}_{p}
is a unit for almost all
p
, by multiplying by some suitable non-zero rational number
x
whose sign is the same as that of
\mathfrak{j}_{\infty}
, we may suppose that
x\mathfrak{j}_{p}
is a unit for all primes
p
. By construction
x\mathfrak{j}_{q}
is in
\mathfrak{i}^{(k)}_{q}\theta(O^{+}(NL_{q}+\mathbf{h}))
for some
k
, and thus
x\mathfrak{j}
is in
\mathfrak{i}^{(k)}P
, or equivalently
\mathfrak{j}\in\mathfrak{i}^{(k)}(\mathbb{Q}^{\times}P)
. Hence
\{\mathfrak{i}^{(k)}:1\leq k\leq n\}
is a complete set of coset representatives.
∎

In light of Corollary
2.5.1
, the above lemma is useful in the special case where
N
and
dL
are both powers of a prime. However, if this does not hold, the requirement that
\theta(O^{+}(NL_{p}+\mathbf{h}))=\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times})^{2}
for all primes

may be too strict, in which case the below lemma is useful as well.

Lemma 4.5
.

Fix two prime numbers
q
and
q^{\prime}
, where
q^{\prime}\neq 2
, and
q
is a quadratic nonresidue modulo
q^{\prime}
. Suppose
NL+\mathbf{h}
is a lattice coset such that

•

\theta(O^{+}(NL_{p}+\mathbf{h}))=\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times})^{2}
for all
p\neq q,q^{\prime}
.

•

\theta(O^{+}(NL_{q^{\prime}}+\mathbf{h}))=\{1,q^{\prime}\}(\mathbb{Q}_{q^{\prime}}^{\times})^{2}
.

•

\theta(O^{+}(NL_{q}+\mathbf{h}))
is a subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}_{q}(\mathbb{Q}_{q}^{\times})^{2}
with group index at most 2.

•

If
\theta(O^{+}(NL_{q}+\mathbf{h}))\neq\mathbb{Z}_{q}(\mathbb{Q}_{q}^{\times})^{2}
, then
q^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{q}^{\times})^{2}\not\in\theta(O^{+}(NL_{q}+\mathbf{h}))
.

Then, there are two spinor genera in the genus of
NL+\mathbf{h}
.

Proof.

If
\theta(O^{+}(NL_{q}+\mathbf{h}))
has group index 1 in
\mathbb{Z}_{q}(\mathbb{Q}_{q}^{\times})^{2}
, then
\theta(O^{+}(NL_{q}+\mathbf{h}))=\mathbb{Z}_{q}(\mathbb{Q}_{q}^{\times})^{2}
, and so the previous lemma is applicable. Noting that
\mathbb{Z}_{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{Q}_{q^{\prime}}^{\times})^{2}\cap\theta(O^{+}(NL_{q^{\prime}}+\mathbf{h}))
is the trivial subgroup of the order 2 group
\mathbb{Z}_{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{Q}_{q^{\prime}}^{\times})^{2}
, it follows that there are two spinor genera in the genus. From now onwards, suppose that
\theta(O^{+}(NL_{q}+\mathbf{h}))
has group index exactly 2 in
\mathbb{Z}_{q}(\mathbb{Q}_{q}^{\times})^{2}
, which also implies that
q^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{q}^{\times})^{2}\not\in\theta(O^{+}(NL_{q}+\mathbf{h}))
.

Let
P=\prod_{p}\theta(O^{+}(NL_{p}+\mathbf{h}))
; we want to show that
\mathbb{Q}^{\times}P
is an index 2 subgroup of
J_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Consider
\mathfrak{i}\in J_{\mathbb{Q}}
such that
\mathfrak{i}_{q^{\prime}}=q
, and
\mathfrak{i}_{p}=1
for all
p\neq q^{\prime}
. Note that
\mathfrak{i}_{q^{\prime}}
is a coset representative of the only non-trivial coset of
\mathbb{Q}_{q^{\prime}}^{\times}/(\mathbb{Q}_{q^{\prime}}^{\times})^{2}
modulo
\theta(O^{+}(NL_{q^{\prime}}+\mathbf{h}))
, since
q
is a quadratic non-residue modulo
q^{\prime}
. Suppose, to the contrary, that
\mathfrak{i}\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}P
. There must then exist
x\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
such that
x\mathfrak{i}\in P
. From
x=x\mathfrak{i}_{p}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times})^{2}
for
p\neq q,q^{\prime}
, it follows that
\mathrm{ord}_{p}x
is even for these primes. Hence, there exists
y\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
such that
x=ty^{2}
, where
t=1,q,q^{\prime}
, or
qq^{\prime}
. From
qx=x\mathfrak{i}_{q^{\prime}}\in\theta(O^{+}(NL_{q^{\prime}}+\mathbf{h}))
it follows that
qt\in\{1,q^{\prime}\}(\mathbb{Q}_{q^{\prime}}^{\times})^{2}
; as
q
is not in
(\mathbb{Q}_{q^{\prime}}^{\times})^{2}
and
t\in\{1,q,q^{\prime},qq^{\prime}\}
, we must have
t=q
or
t=qq^{\prime}
. Either way,
x=ty^{2}\not\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{\times}(\mathbb{Q}_{q}^{\times})^{2}
, contradicting the fact that
x=x\mathfrak{i}_{q}\in\theta(O^{+}(NL_{q}+\mathbf{h}))\subset\mathbb{Z}_{q}(\mathbb{Q}_{q}^{\times})^{2}
. Hence
\mathfrak{i}
is a non-trivial coset representative of
J_{\mathbb{Q}}
modulo
\mathbb{Q}^{\times}P
.

Next consider any
\mathfrak{j}\in J_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Since
\mathfrak{j}_{p}
is a unit for almost all
p
, there exists
x\in\mathbb{Q}
whose sign is the same as that of
\mathfrak{j}_{\infty}
such that
x\mathfrak{j}_{p}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times})^{2}
for all
p\neq q^{\prime}
. By the fourth assumption,
q^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{q}^{\times})^{2}
is a coset representative of the non-trivial coset of
\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{\times}(\mathbb{Q}_{q}^{\times})^{2}
modulo
\theta(O^{+}(NL_{q}+\mathbf{h}))
; hence
x\mathfrak{j}_{q}
is either in
\theta(O^{+}(NL_{q}+\mathbf{h}))
or in
q^{\prime}\theta(O^{+}(NL_{q}+\mathbf{h}))
. If the latter holds, then we may replace
x
by
q^{\prime}x
so that, without loss of generality, we can assume that
x\mathfrak{j}_{q}\in\theta(O^{+}(NL_{q}+\mathbf{h}))
(while
x\mathfrak{j}_{p}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times})^{2}
for all
p\neq q^{\prime}
continues to hold). It is now easy to see from
\theta(O^{+}(NL_{q^{\prime}}+\mathbf{h}))=\{1,q^{\prime}\}(\mathbb{Q}_{q^{\prime}}^{\times})^{2}
that either
x\mathfrak{j}\in P
, or
x\mathfrak{j}\in\mathfrak{i}P
. Hence
\mathfrak{j}
is in either
\mathbb{Q}^{\times}P
, or in
\mathfrak{i}\cdot\mathbb{Q}^{\times}P
, and so the group index given in equation (
2.4
) is exactly 2. Therefore, there are two spinor genera in the genus.
∎

As another example illustrating the use of Proposition

, where
\mathbf{h}
is one of the 7 distinct non-zero vectors modulo
2
, and

is a 2-adically unimodular lattice (i.e.

, we will always have

,

. Also, Lemma
4.2
along with Corollary

reduces to simply

\sum_{2L+\mathbf{h}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}}\frac{1}{|O^{+}(2L+\mathbf{h}^{\prime})|}=\frac{[O^{+}(L_{2}):O^{+}(2L_{2}+\mathbf{h})]}{|O^{+}(L)|}=\frac{|G_{L,q^{\prime},2}|}{|H_{L,\mathbf{h},q^{\prime},2}|\cdot|O^{+}(L)|}

(4.5)

where

The next lemma guarantees a symmetry between different, not necessarily
p
-adically equivalent, lattice cosets. The lemma is stated and proved in more generality with the 2-adic unimodularity condition removed.

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
\mathbf{h}=\mathbf{e}_{1}+\mathbf{e}_{2}+\mathbf{e}_{3}
, and consider
1\leq i<j\leq 3
. If
L
and
2L+\mathbf{h}
have class number 1, then
2L+\mathbf{e}_{j}
is in the same class (resp. genus, spinor genus) of
2L+\mathbf{e}_{i}
if and only if
2L+(\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{j})
is in the same class (resp. genus, spinor genus) of
2L+(\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{i})
.

Proof.

First let us show that, for any
\Sigma\in O_{\mathbb{A}}(V)
, we have
\Sigma(2L+\mathbf{e}_{i})=2L+\mathbf{e}_{j}
if and only if
\Sigma(2L+(\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{i}))=2L+\mathbf{(}\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{j})
. In either case however,
\Sigma
must satisfy
\Sigma L=L
. Now, if
\Sigma L=L
, then notice that
\Sigma(2L+\mathbf{h})=2L+\mathbf{v}
for some
\mathbf{v}\in L
. By definition, we have
2L+\mathbf{v}\in\mathrm{gen}^{+}(2L+\mathbf{h})
.
Since
2L+\mathbf{h}
is assumed to be of class number 1, it follows that
2L+\mathbf{v}\in\mathrm{cls}^{+}(2L+\mathbf{h})
. It can be checked, using Lemma
4.1
, that
2L+\mathbf{h}
is fixed by
O^{+}(L)
, which then implies that
\Sigma(2L+\mathbf{h})=2L+\mathbf{h}
. Thus, for all
p
there exists
\mathbf{u}_{p}\in L_{p}
such that
\Sigma_{p}\mathbf{h}=\mathbf{h}+2\mathbf{u}_{p}
.

Now, suppose that
\Sigma(2L+\mathbf{e}_{i})=2L+\mathbf{e}_{j}
; then
\Sigma_{p}\mathbf{e}_{i}=\mathbf{e}_{j}+2\mathbf{v}_{p}
for some
\mathbf{v}_{p}\in L_{p}
, for all
p
. This implies that
\Sigma_{p}(\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{i})=\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{j}+2(\mathbf{u}_{p}-\mathbf{v}_{p})\equiv\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{j}\;(\mathrm{mod}\;2L_{p})
by linearity, and so from
\Sigma L=L
it follows that
\Sigma(2L+(\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{i}))=2L+\mathbf{(}\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{j})
. The backward direction follows in exactly the same way.

Finally, notice that
2L+\mathbf{e}_{j}
is in the same class (resp. genus, spinor genus) of
2L+\mathbf{e}_{i}
if and only if there exists
\Sigma
in
O^{+}(V)
(resp.
O_{\mathbb{A}}(V)
,
O^{+}(V)O^{\prime}_{\mathbb{A}}(V)
) such that
\Sigma(2L+\mathbf{e}_{i})=2L+\mathbf{e}_{j}
. By the above result, this is equivalent to
\Sigma(2L+(\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{i}))=2L+\mathbf{(}\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{j})
for some
\Sigma
in
O^{+}(V)
(resp.
O_{\mathbb{A}}(V)
,
O^{+}(V)O^{\prime}_{\mathbb{A}}(V)
), which is itself equivalent to
2L+(\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{j})
being in the same class (resp. genus, spinor genus) of
2L+(\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{i})
. The result follows.
∎

To make use of Proposition

, let us completely characterize

Lemma 4.12
.

For a permutation
s
in the symmetric group

denote the matrix whose

’th column is the

’th standard basis vector of

Proof.

Consider any
X\in G_{L,8,2}
; then there exists
Y\in SL_{3}(\mathbb{Z}/8\mathbb{Z})
such that
Y\equiv X\;(\mathrm{mod}\;2)
and

. Let

. Since
\sum_{i=1}^{3}a_{i}y_{ij}^{2}\equiv a_{j}\;(\mathrm{mod}\;8)
for
j=1,2,3
, where each of the
a_{i}
are odd, it follows that an odd number of the

are odd for each
j

for

, it follows that at least one of

must be even for each
j
. Hence, exactly one of
y_{1j},y_{2j},y_{3j}
must be odd (and the others even) for each
j
, and so

for some permutation

. From
\sum_{i=1}^{3}a_{i}y_{ij}^{2}\equiv a_{j}\;(\mathrm{mod}\;8)
(where
y_{ij}^{2}\equiv 1\;(\mathrm{mod}\;8)
if
y_{ij}
is odd) it then follows that
a_{s(i)}\equiv a_{i}\;(\mathrm{mod}\;4)

is contained in the set on the right hand side.

For a rotation
\sigma

[\sigma]
denote its coordinate matrix in
M_{3}(\mathbb{Q}_{2})
. If

lies in the image of the homomorphism
\Psi
whenever

∎

Proposition 4.17
.

For
\mathbf{a}=(1,25,5)^{T},(1,9,21)^{T},(7,63,3)^{T}
, we have

\mathrm{gen}^{+}(2L+\mathbf{e}_{1})=\mathrm{spn}^{+}(2L+\mathbf{e}_{1})=\mathrm{cls}^{+}(2L+\mathbf{e}_{1})\sqcup\mathrm{cls}^{+}(2L+\mathbf{e}_{2})\sqcup\mathrm{cls}^{+}(2L+\mathbf{e}_{3})

Proof.

Note that
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}
are distinct,
a_{1}\equiv a_{2}\not\equiv a_{3}\;(\mathrm{mod}\;8)
but
a_{1}\equiv a_{2}\equiv a_{3}\;(\mathrm{mod}\;4)
. Thus,
|O^{+}(L)|=4
and
|G_{L,8,2}|=6
. It is easily seen that
2L+\mathbf{e}_{i}
for
i=1,2,3
are in distinct classes. Now, consider
\Sigma\in O_{\mathbb{A}}(V)
for which
\Sigma_{p}
is the identity for all
p\neq 2
, and

\Sigma_{2}=\begin{cases}\tau_{\mathbf{e}_{1}-\mathbf{e}_{2}}\tau_{\mathbf{e}_{1}-\mathbf{e}_{3}}\tau_{\mathbf{e}_{2}-\mathbf{e}_{3}}\tau_{2\mathbf{e}_{1}+\mathbf{e}_{2}+\mathbf{e}_{3}}&\text{if }\mathbf{a}=(1,25,5)^{T},(1,9,21)^{T},\\
\tau_{\mathbf{e}_{1}+\mathbf{e}_{3}}\tau_{2\mathbf{e}_{1}+\mathbf{e}_{2}+\mathbf{e}_{3}}&\text{if }\mathbf{a}=(7,63,3)^{T}.\end{cases}

It can be checked that
\Sigma_{2}\in O^{\prime}(L_{2})
, and is
E_{(1,3,2)}
under the homomorphism
\Psi
from Proposition
4.3
. Thus
\Sigma_{2}
maps
2L_{2}+\mathbf{e}_{1}
to
2L_{2}+\mathbf{e}_{3}
and
2L_{2}+\mathbf{e}_{3}
to
2L_{2}+\mathbf{e}_{2}
, and so
\Sigma\in O^{\prime}_{\mathbb{A}}(V)
maps
2L+\mathbf{e}_{1}
and
2L+\mathbf{e}_{3}
to
2L+\mathbf{e}_{3}
and
2L+\mathbf{e}_{2}
respectively. Hence
2L+\mathbf{e}_{i}
for
i=1,2,3
all lie in the same spinor genus. Finally, since
H_{L,\mathbf{e}_{1},8,2}=\{E_{\mathrm{id}},E_{(2,3)}\}
, and
O^{+}(2L+\mathbf{e}_{i})=O^{+}(L)
for
i=1,2,3
, equation (
4.5
) implies that these three classes exhaust the classes in the genus.
∎

Proposition 4.18
.

For
\mathbf{a}=(1,1,3)^{T},(3,3,1)^{T}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{gen}^{+}(2L+\mathbf{e}_{1})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{spn}^{+}(2L+\mathbf{e}_{1})=\mathrm{cls}^{+}(2L+\mathbf{e}_{1})=\mathrm{cls}^{+}(2L+\mathbf{e}_{2})

\displaystyle\mathrm{gen}^{+}(2L+\mathbf{e}_{3})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{spn}^{+}(2L+\mathbf{e}_{3})=\mathrm{cls}^{+}(2L+\mathbf{e}_{3})

and these two genera are distinct.

Proof.

Notice that, in either case,
\mathbf{a}=(a,a,b)^{T}
where
a\not\equiv b\;(\mathrm{mod}\;4)
. This implies that
|O^{+}(L)|=8
and
G_{L,8,2}=\{E_{\mathrm{id}},E_{(1,2)}\}
. It follows that
2L+\mathbf{e}_{1}
and
2L+\mathbf{e}_{3}
are in distinct genera, since both matrices in
G_{L,8,2}
fix
(0,0,1)^{T}
modulo 2. As
\tau_{\mathbf{e}_{3}}\tau_{\mathbf{e}_{1}-\mathbf{e}_{2}}\in O^{+}(L)
maps
\mathbf{e}_{1}
to
\mathbf{e}_{2}
, it follows that
2L+\mathbf{e}_{1}
and
2L+\mathbf{e}_{2}
lie in the same class. Finally, since
H_{L,\mathbf{e}_{1},8,2}=\{E_{\mathrm{id}}\}
,
H_{L,\mathbf{e}_{3},8,2}=G_{L,8,2}
,
|O^{+}(2L+\mathbf{e}_{1})|=4
and
O^{+}(2L+\mathbf{e}_{3})=O^{+}(L)
, equation (
4.5
) implies that both
2L+\mathbf{e}_{1}
and
2L+\mathbf{e}_{3}
have only one class in their respective genera.
∎

The distribution of braid indices of 2-bridge knots
Tobias Clark, Jeremy Frank, Adam M. Lowrance

Abstract
. In this article we study the braid indices of 2-bridge knots with a fixed crossing number c c . We show that the average braid index of the set of 2 2 -bridge knots of crossing number c c is asymptotically linear, approaching c 3 + 11 9 \frac{c}{3}+\frac{11}{9} . Additionally, we show that the variance of the braid indices of the set of 2 2 -bridge knots of crossing number c c is also asymptotically linear, approaching 2 ​ c 27 − 10 81 \frac{2c}{27}-\frac{10}{81} . Finally, we find a formula for the number k c , b k_{c,b} of 2 2 -bridge knots with crossing number c c and braid index b b , and show that for any fixed c c , the braid index where k c , b k_{c,b} achieves its maximum is b = ⌈ c 3 ⌉ + 1 b=\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil+1 .

Introduction

A
2-bridge knot
is a nontrivial knot with a representative such that projection to one coordinate yields only two maxima and two minima as critical points. Every
2
-bridge knot is the closure of a rational tangle, and Schubert
[
Sch56
]
classified
2
-bridge knots in terms of their rational tangle representations. Recent work
[
BKL+19
,
Coh23
,
ST22
,
RD22
,
CL22
,
CDL+23
]
has explored properties of the probability distribution of the genera of
2
-bridge knots with fixed crossing number
c
. In this article, we apply techniques from these sources to study the probability distribution of braid indices of
2
-bridge knots.

Alexander
[
Ale23
]
proved that every knot can be represented as the closure of a braid. The
braid index
of a knot is the minimum number of strands of any braid whose closure is the knot. Murasugi
[
Mur91
]
computed the braid index of a
2
-bridge knot; see Theorem
2.6
for a precise statement.

Define
\mathcal{K}_{c}
to be the set of
2
-bridge knots of crossing number
c
where only one of a knot and its mirror image is in
\mathcal{K}_{c}
. For example,
|\mathcal{K}_{3}|=1
because only one of the left-handed or right-handed trefoil is in
\mathcal{K}_{3}
. Define
\mathcal{K}_{c,b}
to be the subset of
\mathcal{K}_{c}
consisting of knots of braid index
b
, and let
k_{c,b}=|\mathcal{K}_{c,b}|
. Our first result gives a formula for the number
k_{c,b}
of
2
-bridge knots with crossing number
c
and braid index
b
.

Theorem 1.1
.

The number
k_{c,b}
of
2
-bridge knots with crossing number
c
and braid index
b
is

k_{c,b}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $3\leq c$, $c$ is odd, and $b=2$,}\\
2^{b-4}\binom{c-b}{b-2}&\text{if $3\leq c$, $3\leq b\leq\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil$, and $c+b$ is even,}\\
2^{b-4}\binom{c-b}{b-2}+2^{\frac{b-5}{2}}\binom{\frac{c-b-1}{2}}{\frac{b-3}{2}}&\text{if $3\leq c$, $3\leq b\leq\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil$, $c$ is even, and $b$ is odd,}\\
2^{b-4}\binom{c-b}{b-2}+2^{\frac{b-4}{2}}\binom{\frac{c-b-1}{2}}{\frac{b-2}{2}}&\text{if $3\leq c$, $3\leq b\leq\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil$, $c$ is odd, and $b$ is even,}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

The
mode
of a finite sequence
(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
of integers is an index
m
where
a_{m}\geq a_{i}
for all

i
with
1\leq i\leq n
. In our next result, we find the mode of the sequence
(k_{c,b})_{b=2}^{n}
where
n=\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil
for each fixed crossing number.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
c\geq 3
, and let
n=\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil
. The mode of the sequence
(k_{c,b})_{b=2}^{n}=(k_{c,2},k_{c,3},\dots,k_{c,n})
of the number of 2-bridge knots with crossing number
c
and braid index
b
is
b=\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil+1.

In Conjecture
4.3
, we conjecture that the median braid index is also
b=\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil+1.

We also find the average and the variance of the braid indices of
2
-bridge knots with a fixed crossing number
c
. Suzuki and Tran
[
ST23
]
independently computed the average braid index. Define the discrete random variable
\operatorname{Braid}_{c}:\mathcal{K}_{c}\to\mathbb{Z}
by setting
\operatorname{Braid}_{c}(K)
be the braid index of the
2
-bridge knot
K
. We call
\operatorname{Braid}_{c}
the
braid index of 2-bridge knots with crossing number
c
random variable
, or just the
braid index random variable
, for short. The average value of the braid indices of all
2
-bridge knots with crossing number
c
is the expected value
E(\operatorname{Braid}_{c})
of the braid index random variable.

Theorem 1.3
.

The average braid index
E(\operatorname{Braid}_{c})
of
2
-bridge knots with crossing number
c
is

E(\operatorname{Braid}_{c})=\frac{c}{3}+\frac{11}{9}+\begin{cases}\frac{2^{\frac{c}{2}}+9c-16}{9\left(2^{c-2}+2^{\frac{c-2}{2}}\right)}&\text{if $c\equiv 0$ mod $4$,}\\
\frac{19-9c-2^{\frac{c+3}{2}}}{9\left(2^{c-2}+2^{\frac{c-1}{2}}\right)}&\text{if $c\equiv 1$ mod $4$,}\\
\frac{2^{\frac{c}{2}}+3c-8}{9\left(2^{c-2}+2^{\frac{c-2}{2}}-2\right)}&\text{if $c\equiv 2$ mod $4$,}\\
\frac{5-3c-2^{\frac{c+3}{2}}}{9\left(2^{c-2}+2^{\frac{c-1}{2}}+2\right)}&\text{if $c\equiv 3$ mod $4$.}\end{cases}

Hence the average braid index

of
2
-bridge knots with crossing number
c

\frac{c}{3}+\frac{11}{9}
as

.

The variance of the braid indices of all
2
-bridge knots with crossing number

Theorem 1.4
.

The variance
\operatorname{Var}(\operatorname{Braid}_{c})
of the braid index random variable is

\operatorname{Var}(\operatorname{Braid}_{c})=\frac{2c}{27}-\frac{10}{81}+\varepsilon(c),

where

\varepsilon(c)=\begin{cases}\frac{(3c-13)2^{\frac{3c}{2}}+(21c-74)2^{c}-(42c-40)2^{\frac{c}{2}}-(324c^{2}-1152c+1024)}{81\left(2^{2c-2}+2^{\frac{3c}{2}}+2^{c}\right)}&\text{if $c\equiv 0$ mod $4$,}\\
\frac{(3c-1)2^{\frac{3c+1}{2}}+(15c-13)2^{c}-(69c-175)2^{\frac{c+3}{2}}-(324c^{2}-1368c+1444)}{81\left(2^{2c-2}+2^{\frac{3c+1}{2}}+2^{c+1}\right)}&\text{if $c\equiv 1$ mod $4$,}\\
\frac{(3c-13)2^{\frac{3c}{2}}-(18c^{2}-105c+142)2^{c}-(18c^{2}-75c+28)2^{\frac{c+2}{2}}+(108c^{2}-600c+640)}{81\left(2^{2c-2}+2^{\frac{3c}{2}}-3\cdot 2^{c}-2^{\frac{c+6}{2}}+16\right)}&\text{if $c\equiv 2$ mod $4$,}\\
\frac{(3c-1)2^{\frac{3c+1}{2}}+(18c^{2}-105c+97)2^{c}+(18c^{2}-129c+169)2^{\frac{c+3}{2}}+(108c^{2}-816c+964)}{81\left(2^{2c-2}+2^{\frac{3c+1}{2}}+6\cdot 2^{c}+4\cdot 2^{\frac{c+3}{2}}+16\right)}&\text{if $c\equiv 3$ mod $4$.}\end{cases}

Hence the variance
\operatorname{Var}(\operatorname{Braid}_{c})
approaches

as

Proof.

We first prove the recursive formula and then use the recursive formula to verify the closed formula. In order to prove the recursive formula, we partition
E(c,b)
into subsets and then show that the sizes of those subsets correspond to the terms in the recursive formula. For the remainder of the proof, let
\mathbf{a}=(2a_{1},2a_{2},\dots,2a_{2m})
. Define

\displaystyle E_{1}(c,b)=

\displaystyle\;\{\mathbf{a}\in E(c,b)~:~|a_{2m}|>1\},

\displaystyle E_{2}(c,b)=

\displaystyle\;\{\mathbf{a}\in E(c,b)~:~|a_{2m}|=1,|a_{2m-1}|>1\},

\displaystyle E_{3}(c,b)=

\displaystyle\;\{\mathbf{a}\in E(c,b)~:~|a_{2m}|=|a_{2m-1}|=1,\operatorname{sign}(a_{2m-2})=\operatorname{sign}(a_{2m-1})=\operatorname{sign}(a_{2m})\},

\displaystyle E_{4}(c,b)=

\displaystyle\;\{\mathbf{a}\in E(c,b)~:~|a_{2m}|=|a_{2m-1}|=1,-\operatorname{sign}(a_{2m-2})=\operatorname{sign}(a_{2m-1})=\operatorname{sign}(a_{2m})\},

\displaystyle E_{5}(c,b)=

\displaystyle\;\{\mathbf{a}\in E(c,b)~:~|a_{2m}|=|a_{2m-1}|=1,\operatorname{sign}(a_{2m-2})=-\operatorname{sign}(a_{2m-1})=\operatorname{sign}(a_{2m})\},

\displaystyle E_{6}(c,b)=

\displaystyle\;\{\mathbf{a}\in E(c,b)~:~|a_{2m}|=|a_{2m-1}|=1,\operatorname{sign}(a_{2m-2})=\operatorname{sign}(a_{2m-1})=-\operatorname{sign}(a_{2m})\}.

Also, define

Define

Define

Since
\mathbf{a}

4.
The mode of the braid indices of 2-bridge knots

In this section, we find the mode of the braid indices of 2-bridge knots of a fixed crossing number
c
, proving Theorem
1.2
. We begin by proving two properties of the finite sequence
(e(c,b))_{b=2}^{n}
where
n={\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil}
.

A finite sequence
(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
is
log concave
if
a_{i}^{2}\geq a_{i-1}a_{i+1}
for
1<i<n
and
unimodal
if there is an index
m
such that
a_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq\cdots\leq a_{m-1}\leq a_{m}\geq a_{m+1}\geq\cdots\geq a_{n-1}\geq a_{n}
. Log concave sequences are known to be unimodal. Our strategy to prove Theorem
1.2
is to first show that the sequence
(e(c,b))_{b=2}^{n}
where
n={\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil}
is log concave with mode
b=\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil+1
, and then show that
e_{p}(c,b)
is small enough in comparison to
e(c,b)
so that the mode of the finite sequence
(k_{c,b})_{b=2}^{n}=\left(\frac{1}{4}(e(c,b)+e_{p}(c,b))\right)_{b=2}^{n}
is also
b=\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil+1
.

Any sequence of length two or shorter is log concave. The first time the sequence of
e(c,b)
for a fixed
c
has length at least three is when
c=7
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For each
c\geq 7
, the sequence
(e(c,b))_{b=2}^{n}
where
n={\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil}
is log concave.

Proof.

If
b=3
and
c
is even, then
e(c,3)^{2}\geq 0=e(c,2)e(c,4)
. If
b=3
and
c
is odd, then

\displaystyle e(c,3)^{2}=

\displaystyle\;\left(2\binom{c-3}{1}\right)^{2}=4c^{2}-24c+36~\text{and}

\displaystyle e(c,2)e(c,4)=

\displaystyle\;2\cdot 2^{2}\binom{c-4}{2}=4c^{2}-36c+80.

Since
e(c,3)^{2}=4c^{2}-24c+36\geq 4c^{2}-36c+80
when
c\geq 4
, the result follows for
b=3
.

Let
b>3
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{e(c,b)^{2}}{e(c,b-1)e(c,b+1)}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{2^{2b-4}\binom{c-b}{b-2}^{2}}{2^{2b-4}\binom{c-b+1}{b-3}\binom{c-b-1}{b-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\frac{(c-b)!^{2}(b-3)!(c-2b+4)!(b-1)!(c-2b)!}{(c-b+1)!(c-b-1)!(b-2)!^{2}(c-2b+2)!^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\left(\frac{(c-b)(b-1)}{(c-b+1)(b-2)}\right)\left(\frac{(c-2b+4)(c-2b+3)}{(c-2b+2)(c-2b+1)}\right).

The second fraction in the above product is clearly greater than one. Next, we show that the first fraction in the above product is at least one. Since
b\leq n
, it follows that
b\leq\frac{c+2}{2}
or
2b-2\leq c
. Therefore
3b-2c-2\leq b-c
, and it follows that

(c-b+1)(b-2)=cb-2c-b^{2}+3b-2\leq cb-c-b^{2}+b=(c-b)(b-1).

Thus the quotient
\frac{e(c,b)^{2}}{e(c,b-1)e(c,b+1)}
is at least one, and therefore the sequence
(e(c,b))_{b=2}^{n}
is log concave.
∎

The next lemma gives the mode of the sequence
(e(c,b))_{b=2}^{n}
where
n={\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

For each
c\geq 7
, the largest term in the sequence
(e(c,b))_{b=2}^{n}
where
n={\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil}
is
e\left(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil+1\right)
.

Proof.

Since the sequence
(e(c,b))_{b=2}^{n}
where
n={\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil}
is unimodal, it suffices the show that

e\left(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil\right)\leq e\left(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil+1\right)\geq e\left(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil+2\right).

When
c\geq 8
, Proposition
3.3
implies the difference
e\left(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil+1\right)-e\left(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil\right)
is given by

(4.1)

e\left(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil+1\right)-e\left(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil\right)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{2^{r-1}}{r}\binom{2r+1}{r-1}&\text{if $c=3r$,}\\
\displaystyle\frac{2^{r-1}}{r}\binom{2r}{r-1}&\text{if $c=3r+1$,}\\
\displaystyle\frac{2^{r-1}(5r+4)}{(r+1)(r+2)}\binom{2r}{r}&\text{if $c=3r+2$.}\end{cases}

When
c\geq 8
, Proposition
3.3
also implies the difference
e\left(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil+1\right)-e\left(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil+2\right)
is given by

(4.2)

e\left(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil+1\right)-e\left(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil+2\right)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{2^{r-1}}{r}\binom{2r-2}{r-1}&\text{if $c=3r$,}\\
\displaystyle\frac{2^{r}(7r-5)}{r(r+1)}\binom{2r-2}{r-1}&\text{if $c=3r+1$,}\\
\displaystyle\frac{2^{r+2}}{r+1}\binom{2r-1}{r-1}&\text{if $c=3r+2$.}\end{cases}

Since both
e\left(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil+1\right)-e\left(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil\right)
and
e\left(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil+1\right)-e\left(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil+2\right)
are positive, the result follows.
∎

We use Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
to prove Theorem
1.2
. The inequality

(4.3)

\left(\frac{n}{k}\right)^{k}\leq\binom{n}{k}

for
0\leq k\leq n
will be useful in our proof.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
imply that the sequence
(e(c,b))_{b=2}^{n}
where
n={\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil}
is unimodal with mode
e\left(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil+1\right)
. Since
k_{c,b}=\frac{1}{4}(e(c,b)+e_{p}(c,b))
, to show that
k_{c,\left\lceil c/3\right\rceil+1}\geq k_{c,b}
for
2\leq b\leq\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil
, it suffices to show that
e(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil+1)+e_{p}(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil+1)\geq e(c,b)+e_{p}(c,b)
for
2\leq b\leq\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil.
Since

e\left(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil+1\right)-e(c,b)\geq\min\left\{e\left(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil+1\right)-e\left(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil\right),e\left(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil+1\right)-e\left(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil+2\right)\right\}

and
e_{p}(c,b)-e_{p}\left(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil+1\right)\leq e_{p}(c)
for
2\leq b\leq\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil
, it suffices to show that

(4.4)

\displaystyle e_{p}(c)\leq

\displaystyle\;e\left(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil+1\right)-e\left(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil\right)~\text{and}

(4.5)

\displaystyle e_{p}(c)\leq

\displaystyle\;e\left(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil+1\right)-e\left(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil+2\right).

We use Equations (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) to show that Inequalities (
4.4
) and (
4.5
) hold for
c\geq 34
. For
c\leq 34
, we use Theorem
1.1
to check that
k_{c,\left\lceil c/3\right\rceil+1}\geq k_{c,b}
for
2\leq b\leq\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil.

Suppose that
c=3r
. Corollary
3.6
implies that
e_{p}(c)\leq\frac{1}{3}\left(2^{\frac{c+2}{2}}\right)=\frac{1}{3}\left(2^{\frac{3r+2}{2}}\right)
. Equation (
4.1
) and Inequality (
4.3
) imply that

e\left(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil+1\right)-e\left(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil\right)=\frac{2^{r-1}}{r}\binom{2r+1}{r-1}\geq\left(\frac{2^{r-1}}{r}\right)\left(\frac{2r+1}{r-1}\right)^{r-1}\geq\frac{2^{2r-2}}{r}.

Since
\frac{2^{2r-2}}{r}\geq\frac{1}{3}\left(2^{\frac{3r+2}{2}}\right)
when
r\geq 10
, Inequality (
4.4
) holds with
c=3r\geq 30
. Equation (
4.2
) and Inequality (
4.3
) imply that

e\left(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil+1\right)-e\left(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil+2\right)=\frac{2^{r-1}}{r}\binom{2r-2}{r-1}\geq\left(\frac{2^{r-1}}{r}\right)\left(\frac{2r-2}{r-1}\right)^{r-1}=\frac{2^{2r-2}}{r}.

Since
\frac{2^{2r-2}}{r}\geq\frac{1}{3}\left(2^{\frac{3r+2}{2}}\right)
when
r\geq 10
, Inequality (
4.5
) holds with
c=3r\geq 30
.

Suppose that
c=3r+1
. Corollary
3.6
implies that
e_{p}(c)\leq\frac{1}{3}\left(2^{\frac{c+2}{2}}\right)=\frac{1}{3}\left(2^{\frac{3r+3}{2}}\right).
Equation (
4.1
) and Inequality (
4.3
) imply that

e\left(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil+1\right)-e\left(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil\right)=\frac{2^{r-1}}{r}\binom{2r}{r-1}\geq\left(\frac{2^{r-1}}{r}\right)\left(\frac{2r}{r-1}\right)^{r-1}\geq\frac{2^{2r-2}}{r}.

Since
\frac{2^{2r-2}}{r}\geq\frac{1}{3}\left(2^{\frac{3r+3}{2}}\right)
when
r\geq 11
, Inequality (
4.4
) holds with
c=3r+1\geq 34
. Equation (
4.2
) and Inequality (
4.3
) imply that

e\left(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil+1\right)-e\left(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil+2\right)=\frac{2^{r}(7r-5)}{r(r+1)}\binom{2r-2}{r-1}\geq\left(\frac{2^{r}(7r-5)}{r(r+1)}\right)\left(\frac{2r-2}{r-1}\right)^{r-1}=\frac{2^{2r-1}(7r-5)}{r(r+1)}.

Since
\frac{2^{2r-1}(7r-5)}{r(r+1)}\geq\frac{1}{3}\left(2^{\frac{3r+3}{2}}\right)
when
r\geq 4
, Inequality (
4.5
) holds with
c=3r+1\geq 13
.

Suppose that
c=3r+2
. Corollary
3.6
implies that
e_{p}(c)\leq\frac{1}{3}\left(2^{\frac{c+2}{2}}\right)=\frac{1}{3}\left(2^{\frac{3r+4}{2}}\right).
Equation (
4.1
) and Inequality (
4.3
) imply that

e\left(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil+1\right)-e\left(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil\right)=\frac{2^{r-1}(5r+4)}{(r+1)(r+2)}\binom{2r}{r}\geq\left(\frac{2^{r-1}(5r+4)}{(r+1)(r+2)}\right)\left(\frac{2r}{r}\right)^{r}=\frac{2^{2r-1}(5r+4)}{(r+1)(r+2)}.

Since
\frac{2^{2r-1}(5r+4)}{(r+1)(r+2)}\geq\frac{1}{3}\left(2^{\frac{3r+4}{2}}\right)
when
r\geq 3
. Inequality (
4.4
) holds with
c=3r+2\geq 11
. Equation (
4.2
) and Inequality (
4.3
) imply that

e\left(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil+1\right)-e\left(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil+2\right)=\frac{2^{r+2}}{r+1}\binom{2r-1}{r-1}\geq\left(\frac{2^{r+2}}{r+1}\right)\left(\frac{2r-1}{r-1}\right)^{r}\geq\frac{2^{2r+1}}{r+1}.

Since
\frac{2^{2r+1}}{r+1}\geq\frac{1}{3}\left(2^{\frac{3r+4}{2}}\right)
when
r\geq 2
. Inequality (
4.5
) holds with
c=3r+2\geq 8
.

Therefore if
c\geq 34
, both Inequality (
4.4
) and Inequality (
4.5
) hold, and the desired result follows. The result follows for
c<34
by direct computation of
k_{c,b}
using Theorem
1.1
.
∎

Using better bounds in the above proof can eliminate the need to use Theorem
1.1
and direct computation for many values of
c
. However, the proof using better bounds is significantly longer. The quantity
e_{p}(c)
will typically be much larger than
e_{p}(c,b)-e_{p}\left(c,\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil+1\right)
, and the upper bound
e_{p}(c)
could be replaced by
e_{p}(c,m_{c})
where
m_{c}
is the mode of the sequence
(e_{p}(c,b))_{b=2}^{n}
. For the sake of brevity, we forego this strategy.

The
median
of a finite sequence
(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
of non-negative integers is the index
m
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}
and
\sum_{i=m}^{n}a_{i}\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}
. If no such index
m
exists, then there is an index
m^{\prime}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{m^{\prime}}a_{i}=\sum_{i=m^{\prime}+1}^{n}a_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}
, and in this case, the median is defined as
m^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}
. We conjecture that the median of the sequence
(k_{c,b})_{b=2}^{n}
where
n=\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil
is the same as its mode. We have confirmed this conjecture for all crossing numbers
c
with
c\leq 10,000
.

Conjecture 4.3
.

Let
c\geq 3
, and let
n=\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil
. The median of the sequence
(k_{c,b})_{b=2}^{n}=(k_{c,2},k_{c,3},\dots,k_{c,n})
of the number of 2-bridge knots with crossing number
c
and braid index
b
is
b=\left\lceil\frac{c}{3}\right\rceil+1.

5.
The average braid index of 2-bridge knots

In this section, we compute the average braid index of 2-bridge knots with crossing number
c
. Define the
total braid index
\operatorname{tbi}(c)
by

\operatorname{tbi}(c)=\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in E(c)}\operatorname{Braid}_{c}(K(\mathbf{a}))=\sum_{b=2}^{\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil}b\;e(c,b),

that is,
\operatorname{tbi}(c)
is the sum of the braid indices of the knots coming from
E(c)
. Similarly, define the
total palindromic braid index
\operatorname{tbi}_{p}(c)
by

\operatorname{tbi}_{p}(c)=\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in E_{p}(c)}\operatorname{Braid}_{c}(K(\mathbf{a}))=\sum_{b=2}^{\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil}b\;e_{p}(c,b),

that is,
\operatorname{tbi}_{p}(c)
is the sum of the braid indices of the knots coming from
E_{p}(c)
. Theorem
2.3
implies that

4\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{c}}\operatorname{Braid}_{c}(K)=\operatorname{tbi}(c)+\operatorname{tbi}_{p}(c).

Therefore the average braid index of 2-bridge knots of crossing number
c
is

E(\operatorname{Braid}_{c})=\frac{\operatorname{tbi}(c)+\operatorname{tbi}_{p}(c)}{e(c)+e_{p}(c)}.

The following two propositions give recursive and closed formulas for
\operatorname{tbi}(c)
and
\operatorname{tbi}_{p}(c)
. The closed formulas can be verified from the recursive formula by straightforward but tedious induction arguments (similar to the proofs of Corollaries
3.4
and
3.6
) that we leave to the reader.

Proposition 5.1
.

If
c\geq 6
, then

(5.1)

\operatorname{tbi}(c)=3\operatorname{tbi}(c-2)+2\operatorname{tbi}(c-3)+2^{c-3}.

If
c\geq 3
, then

\operatorname{tbi}(c)=\frac{(6c+22)2^{c-2}+(6c-46)(-1)^{c}}{27}.

Proof.

The total braid index
\operatorname{tbi}(c)
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\operatorname{tbi}(c)=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{b=2}^{\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil}b\;e(c,b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{b=2}^{\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil}b\;e(c-2,b)+2\sum_{b=2}^{\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil}b\;e(c-2,b-1)+2\sum_{b=2}^{\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil}b\;e(c-3,b-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{b=2}^{\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil}b\;e(c-2,b)+2\left(\sum_{b=2}^{\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil}(b-1)\;e(c-2,b-1)+\sum_{b=2}^{\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil}e(c-2,b-1)\right)

\displaystyle\;+2\left(\sum_{b=2}^{\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil}(b-1)\;e(c-3,b-1)+\sum_{b=2}^{\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil}e(c-3,b-1)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\operatorname{tbi}(c-2)+2\operatorname{tbi}(c-2)+2e(c-2)+2\operatorname{tbi}(c-3)+2e(c-3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;3\operatorname{tbi}(c-2)+2\operatorname{tbi}(c-3)+2e(c-2)+2e(c-3).

Corollary
3.4
implies that

\displaystyle 2e(c-2)+2e(c-3)=

\displaystyle\;\frac{4\left(2^{c-4}-(-1)^{c-4}\right)}{3}+\frac{4\left(2^{c-5}-(-1)^{c-5}\right)}{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\frac{2^{c-2}+2^{c-3}}{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;2^{c-3}.

Therefore

\operatorname{tbi}(c)=3\operatorname{tbi}(c-2)+2\operatorname{tbi}(c-3)+2^{c-3},

verifying Equation
5.1
. The closed formula for
\operatorname{tbi}(c)
can be verified using Equation
5.1
and induction.
∎

We now give recursive and closed formulas for
\operatorname{tbi}_{p}(c)
.

Proposition 5.2
.

If
c\geq 7
, then

(5.2)

\operatorname{tbi}_{p}(c)=\operatorname{tbi}_{p}(c-2)+2\operatorname{tbi}_{p}(c-4)+4e_{p}(c-4).

If
c\geq 3
, then

\operatorname{tbi}_{p}(c)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{(3c+13)2^{\frac{c}{2}}+(12c+14)(-1)^{\frac{c}{2}}}{27}&\text{if $c$ is even,}\\
\displaystyle\frac{(6c+14)2^{\frac{c-1}{2}}-(12c+8)(-1)^{\frac{c-1}{2}}}{27}&\text{if $c$ is odd.}\end{cases}

Proof.

The total palindromic braid index can be expressed as

\displaystyle\operatorname{tbi}_{p}(c)=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{b=2}^{\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil}b\;e_{p}(c,b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{b=2}^{\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil}b\;e_{p}(c-2,b)+2\left(\sum_{b=2}^{\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil}b\;e_{p}(c-4,b-2)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{b=2}^{\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil}b\;e_{p}(c-2,b)+2\left(\sum_{b=2}^{\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil}(b-2)\;e_{p}(c-4,b-2)+2\sum_{b=2}^{\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil}e_{p}(c-4,b-2)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\operatorname{tbi}_{p}(c-2)+2\operatorname{tbi}_{p}(c-4)+4e_{p}(c-4),

verifying Equation
5.2
. The closed formula for
\operatorname{tbi}_{p}(c)
can be verified using Corollary
3.6
, Equation (
5.2
), and induction.
∎

Propositions
5.1
and
5.2
lead to our computation of the average braid index
E(\operatorname{Braid}_{c})
of 2-bridge knots with crossing number
c
, proving Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Theorem
2.3
implies that each
2
-bridge knot with crossing number
c
is represented by either two or four elements in
E(c)
depending on whether the corresponding elements are (anti-)palindromic or not, respectively. Thus it follows that the average braid index is

E(\operatorname{Braid}_{c})=\frac{\operatorname{tbi}(c)+\operatorname{tbi}_{p}(c)}{e(c)+e_{p}(c)}.

The result follows from Corollaries
3.4
and
3.6
and Propositions
5.1
and
5.2
.
∎

6.
The variance of the braid indices of 2-bridge knots

In this section, we compute the variance of the braid indices of
2
-bridge knots with crossing number
c
. The variance can be expressed as
\operatorname{Var}(\operatorname{Braid}_{c})=E(\operatorname{Braid}_{c}^{2})-E(\operatorname{Braid}_{c})^{2}
, and hence we make the following definitions. Define the
total square braid index

\operatorname{tbi}^{2}(c)
to be the sum of the squares of the braid indices of
K(\mathbf{a})
where
\mathbf{a}
ranges over
E(c)
, that is,

\operatorname{tbi}^{2}(c)=\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in E(c)}\operatorname{Braid}_{c}(K(\mathbf{a}))^{2}=\sum_{b=2}^{\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil}b^{2}\;e(c,b).

Similarly, define the
total palindromic square braid index

\operatorname{tbi}_{p}^{2}(c)
to be the sum of the squares of the braid indices of
K(\mathbf{a})
where
\mathbf{a}
ranges over
E_{p}(c)
, that is,

\operatorname{tbi}_{p}^{2}(c)=\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in E_{p}(c)}\operatorname{Braid}_{c}(K(\mathbf{a}))^{2}=\sum_{b=2}^{\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil}b^{2}\;e_{p}(c,b).

Theorem
2.3
implies that
E(\operatorname{Braid}_{c}^{2})=\frac{\operatorname{tbi}^{2}(c)+\operatorname{tbi}_{p}^{2}(c)}{e(c)+e_{p}(c)}
.

The following two propositions give recursive and closed formulas for
\operatorname{tbi}^{2}(c)
and
\operatorname{tbi}_{p}^{2}(c)
. As in Section
5
, the closed formulas can be verified from the recursive formula by straightforward but tedious induction arguments that we leave to the reader.

Proposition 6.1
.

If
c\geq 6
, then

(6.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\operatorname{tbi}^{2}(c)=&\;3\operatorname{tbi}^{2}(c-2)+2\operatorname{tbi}^{2}(c-3)+4\operatorname{tbi}(c-2)+4\operatorname{tbi}(c-3)+2e(c-2)+2e(c-3)\\
=&\;3\operatorname{tbi}^{2}(c-2)+2\operatorname{tbi}^{2}(c-3)+\frac{(6c+17)2^{c-3}+8(-1)^{c}}{9}.\end{split}

If
c\geq 3
, then

\operatorname{tbi}^{2}(c)=\frac{(3c^{2}+24c+37)2^{c-1}+(12c^{2}+30c-302)(-1)^{c}}{81}.

Proof.

If
c\geq 6
, then the total square braid index can be expressed as

\displaystyle\operatorname{tbi}^{2}(c)=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{b=2}^{\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil}b^{2}\;e(c,b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{b=2}^{\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil}b^{2}(e(c-2,b)+2e(c-2,b-1)+2e(c-3,b-1))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{b=2}^{\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil}b^{2}\;e(c-2,b)+2\sum_{b=2}^{\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil}b^{2}\;e(c-2,b-1)+2\sum_{b=2}^{\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil}b^{2}\;e(c-3,b-1).

Because
b^{2}=(b-1)^{2}+2(b-1)+1
, it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{b=2}^{\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil}b^{2}\;e(c-2,b-1)=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{b=2}^{\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil}(b-1)^{2}e(c-2,b-1)+2\sum_{b=2}^{\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil}(b-1)e(c-2,b-1)+\sum_{b=2}^{\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil}e(c-2,b-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\operatorname{tbi}^{2}(c-2)+2\operatorname{tbi}(c-2)+e(c-2).

Similarly,

\sum_{b=2}^{\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil}b^{2}\;e(c-3,b-1)=\operatorname{tbi}^{2}(c-3)+2\operatorname{tbi}(c-3)+e(c-3).

Therefore

\operatorname{tbi}^{2}(c)=3\operatorname{tbi}^{2}(c-2)+2\operatorname{tbi}^{2}(c-3)+4\operatorname{tbi}(c-2)+4\operatorname{tbi}(c-3)+2e(c-2)+2e(c-3).

Hence Corollary
3.4
and Proposition
5.1
imply that

\operatorname{tbi}^{2}(c)=3\operatorname{tbi}^{2}(c-2)+2\operatorname{tbi}^{2}(c-3)+\frac{(6c+17)2^{c-3}+8(-1)^{c}}{9},

proving Equation (
6.1
). The closed formula for
\operatorname{tbi}^{2}(c)
can be verified using Equation (
6.1
) and induction.
∎

The next proposition gives recursive and closed formulas for
\operatorname{tbi}_{p}^{2}(c)
.

Proposition 6.2
.

If
c\geq 7
, then

(6.2)

\operatorname{tbi}_{p}^{2}(c)=\operatorname{tbi}_{p}^{2}(c-2)+2\operatorname{tbi}_{p}^{2}(c-4)+8\operatorname{tbi}_{p}(c-4)+8e_{p}(c-4).

If
c\geq 3
, then

\operatorname{tbi}_{p}^{2}(c)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{(6c^{2}+36c+14)2^{\frac{c-1}{2}}-(24c^{2}+24c+8)(-1)^{\frac{c-1}{2}}}{81}&\text{if $c$ is odd,}\\
\displaystyle\frac{(3c^{2}+30c+43)2^{\frac{c}{2}}+(24c^{2}+48c+38)(-1)^{\frac{c}{2}}}{81}&\text{if $c$ is even.}\end{cases}

Proof.

If
c\geq 7
, then the total palindromic square braid index can be expressed as

\displaystyle\operatorname{tbi}_{p}^{2}(c)=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{b=2}^{\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil}b^{2}\;e_{p}(c,b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{b=2}^{\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil}b^{2}e_{p}(c-2,b)+2\sum_{b=2}^{\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil}b^{2}e_{p}(c-4,b-2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\operatorname{tbi}_{p}^{2}(c-2)+2\sum_{b=2}^{\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil}((b-2)^{2}+4(b-2)+4)e_{p}(c-4,b-2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\operatorname{tbi}_{p}^{2}(c-2)+2\sum_{b=2}^{\left\lceil\frac{c+1}{2}\right\rceil}(b-2)^{2}e_{p}(c-4,b-2)

Loops in AdS: From the Spectral Representation to Position Space III
Dean Carmi

Abstract
We study loop amplitudes in anti de-Sitter space via the spectral representation. We consider loops of spinning fields and in particular gauge fields, and derive various identities connecting different families of loop diagrams, at different number of loops, different spins, different masses. Such identities are useful for the computation of Witten diagrams. Considering the theory of large- N f N_{f} conformal scalar QED defined on AdS space, we derive an analytic expression for the exact 4-point correlation function at sub-leading order in 1 N f \frac{1}{N_{f}} . Additionally, we derive analytic expressions for bulk 2-point functions and boundary 4-point functions for various families of diagrams, which we denote as “blob diagrams”. Finally we study 4-point ladder diagrams with spinning fields, and we derive integral expressions for the spectral representation of a k k -loop ladder diagram.



1
Introduction

The importance of anti de-Sitter space-time in physics arises primarily due its role in the AdS/CFT correspondence. The latter is a duality that relates a gravitational theory in AdS with a (non-gravitational) quantum field theory (QFT) theory on the boundary of AdS. Deep insights into quantum gravity have been obtained by studying observables in the boundary conformal field theory (CFT). Vice versa, insights into strongly coupled QFT have been obtained by perturbatively studying the bulk AdS theory.

In fact, the bulk theory in AdS space does not have to be a gravitational theory, and one can consider a non-gravitational QFT defined on AdS. If there is no gravity in the bulk, the boundary theory is then a non-local conformal theory (i.e there is no stress tensor), and the boundary correlators are constrained by conformal symmetry. We define our theory in the bulk by specifying the matter content and Lagrangian for the bulk fields in AdS. Important physical observables in the theory are AdS scattering amplitudes, which can be obtained perturbatively via the computation of Feynman diagrams in AdS. Feynman diagrams in AdS are computed in a similar fashion to Feynman diagrams in Minkowski space, but the propagators are AdS propagators. Because there is no full translational invariance in AdS space, momentum-space techniques are less powerful compared to flat space.

The Feynman diagrams with all legs ending on the boundary of AdS define conformal correlators for the boundary conformal theory. AdS Feynman diagrams (often known as Witten diagrams) are generally harder to compute compared to their flat-space companions. Indeed, analytical results for loop diagrams in AdS are scarce. 1.2
QFT on AdS

Figure 1:
Anti de-Sitter space-time.
Left:
The AdS cylinder in global coordinates.
Right:
A time-slice of the AdS cylinder. The circle is the boundary of AdS, and the inside of the circle is the bulk of AdS.

Anti de-Sitter space-time is a maximally symmetric space-time, which is a solution of Einstein’s equations with negative cosmological constant.
AdS_{d+1}
is a hyperboloid inside flat space time:

\displaystyle X_{0}^{2}+X_{d+1}^{2}-\sum^{d}_{i=1}X_{i}^{2}=R_{AdS}^{2}

(1)

where
R_{AdS}
is the radius of AdS. The metric of AdS space-time in global coordinates is:

\displaystyle ds^{2}=\frac{1}{\cos^{2}\big(\frac{\rho}{R_{AdS}}\big)}\Big[dt^{2}-d\rho^{2}-\sin^{2}\big(\frac{\rho}{R_{AdS}}\big)d\Omega_{d-1}^{2}\Big]

(2)

Where
\rho
is an AdS radial coordinate, and the angular coordinates
\Omega_{d-1}
parametrise a
(d-1)
-sphere. The AdS cylinder is shown in the LHS of Fig.
1
, and a time-slice of this cylinder is shown in the RHS of Fig.
1
.

Now consider any quantum field theory defined via a Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}
. Instead of considering this QFT on flat-space
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
, we consider the theory on a fixed curved
AdS_{d+1}
background. The action is given by
S=\int_{AdS}\sqrt{g}\mathcal{L}
, and the AdS partition function is then
Z=\int\mathcal{D}\phi e^{-S[\phi]}
. For example, the Euclidean action of a scalar field of mass
m
and cubic coupling
\lambda
is given by:

\displaystyle S=\int_{AdS_{d+1}}d^{d+1}x\sqrt{g}\Big(\frac{1}{2}(\partial\phi)^{2}+m^{2}\phi^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{3!}\phi^{3}\Big)

(3)

This implies the equation of motion:

\displaystyle g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\phi-m^{2}\phi-\frac{\lambda}{3!}\phi^{3}=0

(4)

The bulk-to-bulk propagator between two points
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
in the bulk of AdS is the Green’s function, obeying the equation:

\displaystyle\big(\partial^{2}_{x_{1}}+m^{2}\big)G_{\Delta}(x_{1},x_{2})=(2\pi)^{d+1}\delta^{d+1}_{AdS}(x_{1}-x_{2})

(5)

where
\Delta
is related to the mass as
R_{AdS}^{2}m^{2}=\Delta(\Delta-d)
. The bulk-to-bulk propagator has the explicit expression:

\displaystyle G_{\Delta}(x_{1},x_{2})=\frac{\Gamma_{\Delta}}{2\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}\Gamma_{\Delta-\frac{d}{2}+1}}\zeta^{-\Delta}{}_{2}F_{1}(\Delta,\Delta-\frac{d-1}{2},2\Delta-d+1,-4\zeta^{-1})

(6)

Here
\zeta=\frac{(z_{1}-z_{2})^{2}+(\vec{x}_{1}-\vec{x}_{2})^{2}}{z_{1}z_{2}}
is the chordal distance squared in AdS,
\vec{x}_{i}
are boundary directions, and
z_{i}
are AdS radial coordinates. The bulk-to-boundary propagator is obtained by taking one of the points of the bulk-to-bulk propagator to the AdS boundary:

\displaystyle K_{\Delta}(P_{1},x_{2})=G_{\Delta}(x_{1},x_{2})\Big|_{x_{1}\to P_{1}}

(7)

where
P_{1}
is a point on the boundary of AdS.

Scattering amplitudes in AdS can be obtained perturbatively via the computation of Feynman diagrams in AdS. We show the tree-level 4-point Witten diagram in Fig.
2
, for the case of the
\phi^{3}
theory considered in Eq.
3
. The expression for this diagram is a product of four
K_{\Delta}
’s and one
G_{\Delta}
’s, which is then integrated in the bulk points
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
over the AdS space:

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{O}_{1}(P_{1})\ldots\mathcal{O}_{4}(P_{4})\rangle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}\int d^{d+1}x_{1}d^{d+1}x_{2}G_{\Delta}(x_{1},x_{2})K_{\Delta}(P_{1},x_{1})K_{\Delta}(P_{2},x_{1})K_{\Delta}(P_{3},x_{2})K_{\Delta}(P_{4},x_{2})

(8)

In Fig.
3
we show the perturbative expansion of the 4-point correlator for the case of
\phi^{3}
theory on AdS.

Figure 2:
A tree-level 4-point Witten diagram in the
\phi^{3}
theory of Eq.
3
. The points
x_{1,2}
are points in the bulk, and
P_{i}
are points on the boundary of AdS.

Figure 3:
The first few diagrams (1-loop) in the perturbative expansion of the 4-point correlator of
\phi^{3}

The boundary of AdS is obtained by taking the radial coordinate
\rho\to\frac{\pi}{2}
. The bulk field
\phi
is dual to the boundary operator
\mathcal{O}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(t,\Omega)\equiv\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{\phi(t,\rho(\epsilon),\Omega)}{\epsilon^{\Delta}}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ ,\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \rho(\epsilon)=\frac{\pi}{2}-\epsilon e^{-\tau}

(9)

The boundary operator
\mathcal{O}
inherits its conformal transformation properties from the AdS isometries of the bulk field
\phi
, and the correlation functions of
\mathcal{O}
’s will be governed by conformal symmetry. In particular, the space-time dependence of the 2-point and 3-point correlators is entirely fixed by the conformal symmetry. The 4-point correlators will have a non-trivial space-time dependence-

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{O}(x_{1})\mathcal{O}(x_{2})\mathcal{O}(x_{3})\mathcal{O}(x_{4})\sim\mathcal{G}(z,\bar{z})

(10)

where
z
and
\bar{z}
are cross-ratios built from the points
x_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,4
. We are interested in the function
\mathcal{G}(z,\bar{z})
, which obeys an expansion in conformal blocks
\mathcal{K}_{J,\Delta}(z\bar{z})
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(z,\bar{z})=\sum_{J,\Delta}c^{2}_{J,\Delta}\mathcal{K}_{J,\Delta}(z\bar{z})

(11)

where
\Delta
and
J
are the scaling dimensions and spin of the exchanged operators, and
c_{J,\Delta}
are called OPE coefficients. A conformal theory is entirely determined from knowledge of its “CFT data”, i.e all of the scaling dimensions and OPE coefficients in the theory.

The Spectral representation in AdS

In this section we discuss the spectral representation of bulk 2-point functions. A bulk 2-point correlator is such that both points are inside the bulk (as opposed to being on the boundary of AdS). We first review the spectral representation for a bulk scalar field, and then we discuss more generally the case of a spin-
J
field. In subsection
2.3
we compute the 1-loop bubble 2-point function of a spin-
J
field. In subsection
2.4
we attach bulk-to-boundary propagators in order to construct boundary 4-point “blob diagrams” (to be defined below). More details about spinning AdS propagators and spectral representations can be found in
Costa:2014kfa
.

2.1
Spin-0 (scalar) spectral representation

Consider a scalar field
\phi(x)
of mass
m
in
AdS_{d+1}
space-time. On the boundary of AdS, it corresponds to a scalar operator of scaling dimension
\Delta
, where
m^{2}=\Delta(\Delta-d)
, and we are setting the AdS radius to be
R_{AdS}=1
.
Consider the harmonic function in
AdS_{d+1}
, which is given by the difference of bulk propagators:

\displaystyle\Omega_{\nu}(x_{1},x_{2})=\frac{i\nu}{2\pi}\Big(G_{\frac{d}{2}+i\nu}(x_{1},x_{2})-G_{\frac{d}{2}-i\nu}(x_{1},x_{2})\Big)

(12)

The harmonic function is the solution of the eigenvalue equation

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{x_{1}}\Omega_{\nu}(x_{1},x_{2})=\Big(\frac{d^{2}}{4}+\nu^{2}\Big)\Omega_{\nu}(x_{1},x_{2})

(13)

The integral of
\Omega_{\nu}(x_{1},x_{2})
gives the delta function:

\displaystyle\delta^{d+1}(x_{1},x_{2})=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\nu\ \Omega_{\nu}(x_{1},x_{2})

(14)

Any bulk 2-point function
F(x_{1},x_{2})
in AdS, Fig.
4
-left, can be expanded in a basis of AdS harmonic functions
\Omega_{\nu}(x_{1},x_{2})
:

\displaystyle g^{(2)}(\zeta)=F(x_{1},x_{2})=\langle\phi(x_{1})\phi(x_{2})\rangle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\nu\tilde{F}_{\nu}\Omega_{\nu}(x_{1},x_{2})\,,

(15)

\tilde{F}_{\nu}
is a function of
\nu
, and is the spectral transform of
F(x_{1},x_{2})
. We wrote the LHS such as to explicitly show the dependence on the squared chordal distance. In Fig.
4
-left we denoted the general bulk 2-point function
F(x_{1},x_{2})
as a 2-point blob. The spectral representation (i.e expansion in terms of
\Omega_{\nu}(x_{1},x_{2})
) will be an important tool for us.

The simplest example of a bulk 2-point function is the bulk-to-bulk propagator
G_{\Delta}(x_{1},x_{2})=\langle\phi(x_{1})\phi(x_{2})\rangle\Big|_{tree}
. We write once more the expression for the scalar bulk-to-bulk propagator:

\displaystyle G_{\Delta}(x_{1},x_{2})=\frac{\Gamma_{\Delta}}{2\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}\Gamma_{\Delta-\frac{d}{2}+1}}\zeta^{-\Delta}{}_{2}F_{1}(\Delta,\Delta-\frac{d-1}{2},2\Delta-d+1,-4\zeta^{-1})

(16)

Here
\zeta=\frac{(z_{1}-z_{2})^{2}+(\vec{x}_{1}-\vec{x}_{2})^{2}}{z_{1}z_{2}}
is the chordal distance squared. This propagator can be written in the spectral representation as follows:

\displaystyle G_{\Delta}(x_{1},x_{2})=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\nu}{\nu^{2}+(\Delta-\frac{d}{2})^{2}}\Omega_{\nu}(x_{1},x_{2})

(17)

In other words, the spectral transform of the bulk-to-bulk propagator is just a simple pole
\tilde{F}_{\nu}=\frac{1}{\nu^{2}+(\Delta-\frac{d}{2})^{2}}
. This can easily be seen from Eq.
12
, by using the residue theorem.

Now that we wrote the bulk 2-point function, one can obtain CFT boundary correlators by attaching bulk-to-boundary propagators
K_{\Delta_{i}}
to the 2-point blob
F(x_{1},x_{2})
. As for Feynman diagrams in flat space, for each vertex one integrates over the whole AdS space. For example the 4-point correlation function (see Fig.
4
-right) is obtained by attaching 4 bulk-to-boundary propagators:

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{O}_{1}\ldots\mathcal{O}_{4}\rangle=\int d^{d+1}x_{1}d^{d+1}x_{2}F(x_{1},x_{2})K_{\Delta_{1}}(P_{1},x_{1})K_{\Delta_{2}}(P_{2},x_{1})K_{\Delta_{3}}(P_{3},x_{2})K_{\Delta_{4}}(P_{4},x_{2})

(18)

Here the
P_{i}
are boundary points, and
x_{i}
are bulk points. We denote the family of AdS Diagrams in Fig.
4
-right as “Blob diagrams”.

, and two attached to

We denote the bulk-to-bulk propagator of a spin-
J
field in AdS by
G_{\Delta,J}(x_{1},x_{2},W_{1},W_{2})
, where
W_{1,2}
are polarisation vectors. The bulk propagator of the spin-

field has a spectral representation

:

\displaystyle G_{\Delta,J}(x_{1},x_{2},W_{1},W_{2})=\sum_{l=0}^{J}((W_{1}\cdot\nabla_{1})(W_{2}\cdot\nabla_{2}))^{J-l}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\nu a_{l}(\nu)\Omega_{\nu,l}(x_{1},x_{2}:W_{1},W_{2})

where
\Omega_{\nu,l}
is the AdS harmonic function of spin-

:

\displaystyle\Omega_{\nu,l}(x_{1},x_{2},W_{1},W_{2})=\frac{i\nu}{2\pi}\Big(G_{\frac{d}{2}+i\nu,l}(x_{1},x_{2},W_{1},W_{2})-G_{\frac{d}{2}-i\nu,l}(x_{1},x_{2},W_{1},W_{2})\Big)

One can easily find the coefficients

, for example for

we have
a_{J}(\nu)=\frac{1}{\nu^{2}+(\Delta-\frac{d}{2})^{2}}
, see

.

For a general bulk 2-point function
F_{J}(x_{1},x_{2},W_{1},W_{2})
of spin-

\displaystyle F_{J}(x_{1},x_{2},W_{1},W_{2})=\sum_{l=0}^{J}((W_{1}\cdot\nabla_{1})(W_{2}\cdot\nabla_{2}))^{J-l}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\nu F_{\nu,l}\Omega_{\nu,l}(x_{1},x_{2}:W_{1},W_{2})

Where the harmonic function in AdS is:

\displaystyle\Omega_{\nu,l}(x_{1},x_{2},W_{1},W_{2})=\frac{i\nu}{2\pi}\Big(G_{\frac{d}{2}+i\nu,l}(x_{1},x_{2},W_{1},W_{2})-G_{\frac{d}{2}-i\nu,l}(x_{1},x_{2},W_{1},W_{2})\Big)

\displaystyle\Omega_{\nu,l}(x_{1},x_{2},W_{1},W_{2})=\frac{\nu^{2}}{\pi l!(\frac{d}{2}-1)_{l}}\int d^{d}PK_{\frac{d}{2}+i\nu,l}(x_{1},P,W_{1},D_{Z})K_{\frac{d}{2}-i\nu,l}(x_{2},P,W_{2},Z)

is the spin-

2.3
The 1-loop bubble spectral representation

2.3.1
Scalar field case

In the previous section we saw the spectral representation of the bulk propagator. Here we look at the spectral representation of 1-loop bubble, which is just a product of two bulk propagators
B(x,y)\equiv\langle\phi^{2}(x)\phi^{2}(y)\rangle=G^{2}_{\Delta}(x,y)
. We write the spectral representation
Carmi:2018qzm
:

\displaystyle B(x,y)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\nu\tilde{B}(\nu)\Omega_{\nu}(x,y)

(24)

In
Carmi:2018qzm
we computed the spectral transform of bubble
\tilde{B}(\nu)
. For the case of
AdS_{3}
, it takes the simple form:

\displaystyle\tilde{B}(\nu)=\frac{i}{8\pi\nu}\Big[\psi(\Delta-\frac{1+i\nu}{2})-\psi(\Delta-\frac{1-i\nu}{2})\Big]

(25)

where
\psi
is the digamma function
\psi(z)\equiv\frac{d}{dz}\log[\Gamma(z)]
, and
\Delta
is the scaling dimension of the scalar field. This expression further simplifies when
\Delta=1
:

\displaystyle B(\nu)\Big|_{\Delta=1}=-\frac{1}{8i\nu}\cot(\frac{\pi}{2}(1+i\nu))=\frac{1}{16}\frac{\Gamma_{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{i\nu}{2}}\Gamma_{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{i\nu}{2}}}{\Gamma_{1-\frac{i\nu}{2}}\Gamma_{1+\frac{i\nu}{2}}}

(26)

2.3.2
The
U(1)
current case

In
Ankur:2023lum
we studied large-
N_{f}
scalar quantum electrodynamics on
AdS_{d+1}
. The two-point function of the
U(1)
conserved currents has the spectral representation:

\displaystyle\langle j_{M}(x)j_{N}(y)\rangle=-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\nu B^{(1)}(\nu)\Omega^{(1)}_{\nu MN}(x,y)

(27)

where
B^{(1)}(\nu)
is the 1-loop bubble in the spectral representation, and
\Omega^{(1)}_{\nu MN}(x,y)
is the spin-1 AdS harmonic function.
For a massless gauge field in
AdS_{3}
, we derived
Ankur:2023lum
:

\displaystyle B^{(1)}(\nu)=\frac{\nu}{16\pi(\nu^{2}+1)}\Big(-2(2\Delta-3)\nu+(\nu^{2}+4(\Delta-1)^{2})(i\psi(\Delta-\frac{i\nu}{2})-i\psi(\Delta+\frac{i\nu}{2}))\Big)

where
\Delta
is the scaling dimension of the scalars. This expression simplifies further when
\Delta=1
:

\displaystyle B^{(1)}(\nu)\Big|_{\Delta=1}=\frac{\nu^{3}}{16(\nu^{2}+1)}\coth(\frac{\pi\nu}{2})=-\frac{i\nu^{3}}{16(\nu^{2}+1)}\frac{\Gamma_{1+\frac{i\nu}{2}}\Gamma_{-\frac{i\nu}{2}}}{\Gamma_{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{i\nu}{2}}\Gamma_{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{i\nu}{2}}}

(29)

We will use these results in the following to derive identities for AdS diagrams.

2.4
Boundary correlators: Blob diagrams

One constructs boundary correlators by attaching bulk-to-boundary propagators to the bulk 2-point blob, Fig.

5
2-point bulk correlators

In this work we are considering mainly 4-point boundary correlator functions (with the external legs ending on the boundary of AdS). The boundary theory has conformal symmetry, and therefore the space-time dependence of the 2-point and 3-point boundary correlators are completely fixed by the conformal symmetry. In this section we consider bulk 2-point correlators, e.g
\langle\phi(x_{1})\phi(x_{2})\rangle_{bulk}
, where the two external points
x_{1,2}
end in the bulk (see the RHS of Fig.
13
). The bulk correlators are not constrained by the full conformal symmetry, and they have non-trivial dependence on the chordal distance between the two points
x_{1,2}
. We wish to deduce this dependence in this section.

5.1
Bulk scalar 2-point correlators

The bulk-to-bulk propagator of a scalar field with scaling dimension
\Delta
is (Eq.
16
):

\displaystyle G_{\Delta}(x_{1},x_{2})=\frac{\Gamma_{\Delta}}{2\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}\Gamma_{\Delta-\frac{d}{2}+1}\sqrt{\zeta(\zeta+4)}}\zeta^{-\Delta}{}_{2}F_{1}(\Delta,\Delta-\frac{d-1}{2},2\Delta-d+1,-4\zeta^{-1})

Where
\zeta
is the chordal distance squared. It will be convenient to express the hypergeometric function above in terms of a Legendre-Q function. Then the propagator can be written as:

\displaystyle G_{\Delta}(x_{1},x_{2})=e^{-\pi i\frac{d-1}{2}}\pi^{-\frac{d+1}{2}}(\zeta(\zeta+4))^{-\frac{d+1}{2}}Q^{\frac{d-1}{2}}_{\Delta-\frac{d+1}{2}}(1+\frac{\zeta}{2})

(92)

where
Q_{\mu}^{\nu}(x)
is the associated Legendre function of the second kind. Thus the spectral representation of a general bulk 2-point function is (see Eq.
15
):

\displaystyle g^{(2)}(\zeta)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\nu F_{\nu}\Omega_{\nu}(x_{1},x_{2})=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\nu F_{\nu}i\nu G_{\frac{d}{2}+i\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}}\frac{\zeta^{-(\frac{d}{2})}}{\sqrt{\zeta(\zeta+4)}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\nu F_{\nu}\zeta^{-i\nu}{}_{2}F_{1}(\frac{d}{2}+i\nu,i\nu+\frac{1}{2},2i\nu+1,-4\zeta^{-1})

(93)

Or in terms of

\displaystyle g^{(2)}(\zeta)=e^{-\pi i\frac{d-1}{2}}\pi^{-\frac{d+1}{2}}(\zeta(\zeta+4))^{-\frac{d+1}{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\nu F_{\nu}i\nu Q^{\frac{d-1}{2}}_{i\nu-\frac{1}{2}}(1+\frac{\zeta}{2})

5.2

For

Ladder diagrams are an important class of diagrams in QFT, which occur in large-
N
theories such as Chern-Simons Matter theories and 2-dimensional QCD (i.e the ’t Hooft model). Ladder diagram are computationally more technically challenging than bubble diagrams (which can be reduced to the computation of the 1-loop bubble).

In this section we discuss the computation of ladder diagrams with spinning fields,
in particular for gauge theories. This extends the results of
Carmi:2021dsn

k
-loop ladder diagram, by ”gluing” together the rungs of the ladder. Each such gluing results in a factor of a 6j symbol, and the
k
-loop ladder is given by a convolution of
(k+1)
6J-symbol factors.

We consider a theory with a cubic interaction of scalar field with a spin-
L

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{(0)}=\mathcal{M}^{3214}_{5,exch}=\sum^{\infty}_{J=0}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\mu}{2\pi i}\ R^{1234}_{\mathcal{O}_{5},\mathcal{O}_{\mu,J}}\ \Psi^{1234}_{\mu,J}

(129)

This is derived in Eq.

8
Discussion

In this work we used the spectral representation as a tool to compute various families of loop diagrams in anti de-Sitter space, including blob diagrams and ladder diagrams. The spectral representation makes various identities explicit, enabling to related loop Witten diagrams to lower loop diagrams. We saw that it was often simpler to compute the double-discontinuity of a diagram. The conformal dispersion relation
Carmi:2019cub
, and it’s generalization to mixed correlators
carmi3
, can then be used to reconstruct the full 4-point correlator.

The spectral representation has recently been used for de-Sitter space, and it would be interesting to import our AdS methods to compute blob diagrams in deSitter. The
O(N)
model at large-
N
has recently been studied in de Sitter space
DiPietro:2023inn
, can one extend these computations for the case of the fermionic Gross-Neveu model and to large-
N_{f}
QED on deSitter. We also leave for future work the computation of loops of gluons and gravitons in AdS and dS.

In order to make progress on analytic loop computations of diagrams in AdS, it can be useful to consider some simplifying limits, such as the flat-space limit of AdS diagrams. Conformal blocks simplify in the large
d
limit, which might enable to perform analytical computations using the spectral representation. Another simplifying limit is the diagonal limit
\bar{z}=z
, which is essentially a 1d limit.

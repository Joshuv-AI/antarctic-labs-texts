Hamiltonian Formulations of Quasilinear Theory for Magnetized Plasmas
Alain J. Brizard 1,a and Anthony A. Chan 2

Abstract
Hamiltonian formulations of quasilinear theory are presented for the cases of uniform and nonuniform magnetized plasmas. First, the standard quasilinear theory of Kennel and Engelmann Kennel and Engelmann 1966 is reviewed and reinterpreted in terms of a general Hamiltonian formulation. Within this Hamiltonian representation, we present the transition from two-dimensional quasilinear diffusion in a spatially uniform magnetized background plasma to three-dimensional quasilinear diffusion in a spatially nonuniform magnetized background plasma based on our previous work Brizard and Chan 2001 ; Brizard and Chan 2004 . The resulting quasilinear theory for nonuniform magnetized plasmas yields a 3 × 3 3\times 3 diffusion tensor that naturally incorporates quasilinear radial diffusion as well as its synergistic connections to diffusion in two-dimensional invariant velocity space (e.g., energy and pitch angle).



1
Introduction

The complex interaction between charged particles and electromagnetic-field wave fluctuations in a magnetized plasma represents a formidable problem with crucial implications toward our understanding of magnetic confinement in laboratory and space plasmas
Kaufman and Cohen 2019
. These wave-particle interactions can be described either linearly, quasi-linearly, or nonlinearly, depending on how the background plasma is affected by the fluctuating wave fields and the level of plasma turbulence associated with them
Davidson 1972
.

In linear plasma wave theory
Stix 1992
, where the field fluctuations are arbitrarily small, the linearized perturbed Vlasov distribution of each charged-particle species describes the charged-particle response to the presence of small-amplitude electromagnetic waves which, when coupled to the linearized Maxwell wave equations, yields a wave spectrum that is supported by the uniform background magnetized plasma
Stix 1992
.

In weak plasma turbulence theory
Sagdeev and Galeev 1969
;
Galeev and Sagdeev 1983
, the background plasma is considered weakly unstable so that a (possibly discrete) spectrum of field perturbations grow to finite but small amplitudes. While these small-amplitude fluctuations interact weakly among themselves, they interact strongly with resonant particles, which satisfy a wave-particle resonance condition in particle phase space (described in terms of unperturbed particle orbits). These resonant wave-particle interactions, in turn, lead to a quasilinear modification of the background Vlasov distribution on a long time scale compared to the fluctuation time scale
Kaufman 1972a
;
Dewar 1973
.

Lastly, in strong plasma turbulence theory
Dupree 1966
, nonlinear wave-wave and wave-particle-wave interactions cannot be neglected, and wave-particle resonances include perturbed particle orbits
Galeev and Sagdeev 1983
. The reader is referred to a pedagogical review by Krommes
Krommes 2002
on the theoretical foundations of plasma turbulence as well as a recent study on the validity of quasilinear theory
Crews and Shumlak 2022
. In addition, the mathematical foundations of quasilinear theory for inhomogeneous plasma can be found in the recent work by Dodin
Dodin 2022
.

1.1
Motivation for this work

The primary purpose of the present paper is to present complementary views of two-dimensional quasilinear diffusion in a uniform magnetized plasma. First, we review the quasilinear theory derived by Kennel and Engelmann
Kennel and Engelmann 1966
, which represents the paradigm formulation upon which many subsequent quasilinear formulations are derived
Stix 1992
. (We mainly focus our attention on non-relativistic quasilinear theory in the text and summarize the extension to relativistic quasilinear theory in App. A.) As an alternative formulation of quasilinear theory, we present a Hamiltonian formulation that relies on the use of guiding-center theory for a uniform magnetic field
Cary and Brizard 2009
. In this Hamiltonian formulation, the quasilinear diffusion equation is described in terms of a diffusion tensor whose structure is naturally generalized to three-dimensional quasilinear diffusion in a nonuniform magnetized plasma, as shown in the works of Brizard and Chan
Brizard and Chan 2001
;
Brizard and Chan 2004
.

Next, two formulations of three-dimensional quasilinear theory are be presented. First, we present a generic quasilinear formulation based on the action-angle formalism
Kaufman 1972b
;
Mahajan and Chen 1985
, which applies to general magnetic-field geometries. This formulation is useful in highlighting the modular features of the quasilinear diffusion tensor. Our second three-dimensional quasilinear formulation is developed for the case of an axisymmetric magnetic field
{\bf B}_{0}=\nabla\psi\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla\varphi
, for which the drift action
{\sf J}_{\rm d}=q\psi/c
is expressed simply in terms of the magnetic flux
\psi
. The presentation of this case is based on a summary of the non-relativistic limit of our previous work
Brizard and Chan 2004
.

1.2
Notation for quasilinear theory in a uniform magnetized plasma

In a homogeneous magnetic field
{\bf B}_{0}=B_{0}\,\widehat{\sf z}
, the unperturbed Vlasov distribution
f_{0}({\bf v})
(for a charged-particle species with charge
q
and mass
M
) is a function of velocity
{\bf v}
alone and the perturbed Vlasov-Maxwell fields

(\delta f,\delta{\bf E},\delta{\bf B})
can be decomposed in terms of Fourier components:
\delta f=\delta\widetilde{f}({\bf v})\,\exp(i\vartheta)+{\rm c.c.}
and
(\delta{\bf E},\delta{\bf B})=(\delta\widetilde{\bf E},\delta\widetilde{\bf B})\,\exp(i\vartheta)+{\rm c.c.}
, where the wave phase is
\vartheta({\bf x},t)={\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf x}-\omega\,t
and the dependence of the eikonal (Fourier) amplitudes
(\delta\widetilde{f},\delta\widetilde{\bf E},\delta\widetilde{\bf B})
on
({\bf k},\omega)
, which is denoted by a tilde, is hidden. According to Faraday’s law, we find
\delta\widetilde{\bf B}=({\bf k}c/\omega)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\delta\widetilde{\bf E}
, which implies
{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\delta\widetilde{\bf B}=0
. For the time being, however, we will keep the perturbed electric and magnetic fields separate, and assume that the uniform background plasma is perturbed by a monochromatic wave with definite wave vector
{\bf k}
and wave frequency
\omega
.

Following the notation used by Kennel and Engelmann
Kennel and Engelmann 1966
, the velocity
{\bf v}
and wave vector
{\bf k}
are decomposed in terms of cylindrical components

\left.\begin{array}[]{rcl}{\bf v}&=&v_{\|}\,\widehat{\sf z}\;+\;v_{\bot}\left(\cos\phi\,\widehat{\sf x}+\sin\phi\,\widehat{\sf y}\right)\\
{\bf k}&=&k_{\|}\,\widehat{\sf z}\;+\;k_{\bot}\left(\cos\psi\,\widehat{\sf x}+\sin\psi\,\widehat{\sf y}\right)\end{array}\right\},

(1.1)

so that
{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v}=k_{\|}v_{\|}+k_{\bot}v_{\bot}\cos(\phi-\psi)
, where
\phi
is the gyroangle phase and
\psi
is the wave-vector phase. We note that the unperturbed Vlasov equation
\partial f_{0}/\partial\phi=0
implies that
f_{0}({\bf v})
is independent of the gyroangle
\phi
, i.e.,
f_{0}(v_{\|},v_{\bot})
. In what follows, we will use the definition

\frac{{\bf k}_{\bot}}{k_{\bot}}\;=\;\cos\psi\,\widehat{\sf x}+\sin\psi\,\widehat{\sf y}\;=\;\frac{1}{2}\,e^{i\psi}(\widehat{\sf x}-i\,\widehat{\sf y})\;+\;\frac{1}{2}\,e^{-i\psi}(\widehat{\sf x}+i\,\widehat{\sf y})\;\equiv\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\widehat{\sf K}\;+\frac{}{}\widehat{\sf K}^{*}\right),

(1.2)

and the identity

\frac{{\bf v}_{\bot}}{v_{\bot}}\;\equiv\;\widehat{\bot}\;=\;\cos\phi\,\widehat{\sf x}+\sin\phi\,\widehat{\sf y}\;\equiv\;e^{i(\phi-\psi)}\widehat{\sf K}/\sqrt{2}\;+\;e^{-i(\phi-\psi)}\widehat{\sf K}^{*}/\sqrt{2}.

(1.3)

We note that, in the work of Kennel and Engelmann
Kennel and Engelmann 1966
, the right-handed polarized electric field is
\delta\widetilde{E}_{R}\equiv\delta\widetilde{\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{\sf K}\,e^{-i\psi}
and the left-handed polarized electric field is
\delta\widetilde{E}_{L}\equiv\delta\widetilde{\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{\sf K}^{*}\,e^{i\psi}
; we will refrain from using these components in the present work.

2
Kennel-Engelmann Quasilinear Diffusion Equation

In this Section, we review the quasilinear theory presented by Kennel and Engelmann
Kennel and Engelmann 1966
for the case of a uniform magnetized plasma. Here, we make several changes in notation from Kennel and Engelmann’s work in preparation for an alternative formulation presented in Sec.
3
.

2.1
First-order perturbed Vlasov equation

The linearized perturbed Vlasov equation is expressed in terms of the first-order differential equation for the eikonal amplitude
\delta\widetilde{f}({\bf v})
:

-i\left(\omega-{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v}\right)\delta\widetilde{f}-\Omega\,\frac{\partial\delta\widetilde{f}}{\partial\phi}\equiv-\Omega\;e^{i\Theta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}\left(e^{-i\Theta}\;\delta\widetilde{f}\right)\;=\;-\,\frac{q}{M}\left(\delta\widetilde{\bf E}+\frac{\bf v}{c}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\delta\widetilde{\bf B}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\bf v}

(2.1)

where
\Omega=qB_{0}/(Mc)
denotes the (signed) gyrofrequency and the solution of the integrating factor
\partial\Theta/\partial\phi\equiv\Omega^{-1}d\vartheta/dt=({\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v}-\omega)/\Omega
yields

\displaystyle\Theta(\phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{k_{\|}v_{\|}-\omega}{\Omega}\right)\phi\;+\;\frac{k_{\bot}v_{\bot}}{\Omega}\;\sin(\phi-\psi)\;\equiv\;\varphi(\phi)\;+\;\lambda\;\sin(\phi-\psi),

(2.2)

where
\lambda=k_{\bot}v_{\bot}/\Omega
. The perturbed Vlasov equation (
2.1
) is easily solved as

\delta\widetilde{f}({\bf v})\;=\;\frac{q\,e^{i\Theta}}{M\Omega}\int^{\phi}e^{-i\Theta^{\prime}}\left(\delta\widetilde{\bf E}+\frac{{\bf v}^{\prime}}{c}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\delta\widetilde{\bf B}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial{\bf v}^{\prime}}\;d\phi^{\prime},

(2.3)

where a prime denotes a dependence on the integration gyroangle
\phi^{\prime}
. Here, we can write the perturbed evolution operator

\frac{q}{M\Omega}\left(\delta\widetilde{\bf E}+\frac{\bf v}{c}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\delta\widetilde{\bf B}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial\bf v}\;\equiv\;\delta\widetilde{V}_{\|}\;\frac{\partial}{\partial v_{\|}}\;+\;\delta\widetilde{V}_{\bot}\;\frac{\partial}{\partial v_{\bot}}\;+\;\delta\widetilde{\phi}\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi},

(2.4)

which is expressed in terms of the velocity-space eikonal amplitudes

\displaystyle\delta\widetilde{V}_{\|}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q}{M\Omega}\left(\delta\widetilde{\bf E}\;+\;\frac{{\bf v}_{\bot}}{c}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\delta\widetilde{\bf B}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{\sf z},

(2.5)

\displaystyle\delta\widetilde{V}_{\bot}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q}{M\Omega}\left(\delta\widetilde{\bf E}\;+\;\frac{v_{\|}\widehat{\sf z}}{c}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\delta\widetilde{\bf B}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{\bot},

(2.6)

\displaystyle\delta\widetilde{\phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q}{M\Omega}\left(\delta\widetilde{\bf E}+\frac{v_{\|}\widehat{\sf z}}{c}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\delta\widetilde{\bf B}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\widehat{\phi}}{v_{\bot}}-\frac{\delta\widetilde{B}_{\|}}{B_{0}},

(2.7)

where
\widehat{\phi}=\partial\widehat{\bot}/\partial\phi=\widehat{\sf z}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{\bot}
. Whenever direct comparison with the work of Kennel and Engelmann
Kennel and Engelmann 1966
is needed, we will use Faraday’s law to express
\delta\widetilde{\bf B}=({\bf k}c/\omega)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\delta\widetilde{\bf E}
. With this substitution (see App.
A
for details), for example, we note that Eqs. (
2.4
)-(
2.7
) agree exactly with Eq. (2.12) of Kennel and Engelmann
Kennel and Engelmann 1966
.

We now remark that, since
\partial f_{0}(v_{\|},v_{\bot})/\partial\phi
vanishes, only the first two terms in Eq. (
2.4
) are non-vanishing when applied to
f_{0}
. Hence, Eq. (
2.3
) contains the integrals

\displaystyle e^{i\Theta}\int^{\phi}e^{-i\Theta^{\prime}}d\phi^{\prime},

(2.8)

\displaystyle e^{i\Theta}\int^{\phi}e^{-i\Theta^{\prime}}\widehat{\bot}^{\prime}d\phi^{\prime}.

(2.9)

In order to evaluate these integrals, we use the Bessel-Fourier decomposition
e^{i\Theta}=e^{i\varphi}\;\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}J_{\ell}(\lambda)\;e^{i\ell(\phi-\psi)}
,
so that the scalar integral (
2.8
) becomes

e^{i\Theta}\int^{\phi}e^{-i\Theta^{\prime}}d\phi^{\prime}=\sum_{m,\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}i\,\Delta_{\ell}\;J_{m}(\lambda)\,J_{\ell}(\lambda)\;e^{i(m-\ell)(\phi-\psi)},

(2.10)

where the resonant denominator is

\Delta_{\ell}\;\equiv\;\frac{\Omega}{k_{\|}v_{\|}+\ell\,\Omega-\omega},

(2.11)

while, using the identity (
1.3
), the vector integral (
2.9
) becomes

e^{i\Theta}\int^{\phi}e^{-i\Theta^{\prime}}\widehat{\bot}^{\prime}\;d\phi^{\prime}\;=\;\sum_{m,\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}i\,\Delta_{\ell}\;J_{m}(\lambda)\;\mathbb{J}_{\bot\ell}(\lambda)\;e^{i(m-\ell)(\phi-\psi)},

(2.12)

where we introduced the vector-valued Bessel function

\mathbb{J}_{\bot\ell}(\lambda)\;\equiv\;\frac{\widehat{\sf K}}{\sqrt{2}}\,J_{\ell+1}(\lambda)\;+\;\frac{\widehat{\sf K}^{*}}{\sqrt{2}}\,J_{\ell-1}(\lambda),

(2.13)

with the identity

{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mathbb{J}_{\bot\ell}\;=\;(J_{\ell+1}+J_{\ell-1})\;k_{\bot}/2\;=\;(\ell\Omega/v_{\bot})\;J_{\ell},

(2.14)

which follows from a standard recurrence relation for Bessel functions. The perturbed Vlasov distribution (
2.3
) is thus expressed as

\delta\widetilde{f}\;=\;\sum_{m,\ell}i\,\Delta_{\ell}\;J_{m}(\lambda)\;e^{i(m-\ell)(\phi-\psi)}\left(\delta\widetilde{V}_{\|\ell}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial v_{\|}}\;+\;\delta\widetilde{V}_{\bot\ell}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial v_{\bot}}\right),

(2.15)

where the Bessel-Fourier components are

\displaystyle\delta\widetilde{V}_{\|\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q}{M\Omega}\,\delta\widetilde{E}_{\|}\;J_{\ell}(\lambda)\;-\;v_{\bot}\,\widehat{\sf z}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\delta\widetilde{\bf B}}{B_{0}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mathbb{J}_{\bot\ell}(\lambda),

(2.16)

\displaystyle\delta\widetilde{V}_{\bot\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q}{M\Omega}\left(\delta\widetilde{\bf E}+\frac{v_{\|}\widehat{\sf z}}{c}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\delta\widetilde{\bf B}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mathbb{J}_{\bot\ell}(\lambda).

(2.17)

Once again, Eqs. (
2.15
)-(
2.17
) agree exactly with Eq. (2.19) of Kennel and Engelmann
Kennel and Engelmann 1966
when Faraday’s law is inserted in Eqs. (
2.16
)-(
2.17
); see App.
A
for details. Th relativistic version of Eqs. (
2.15
)-(
2.17
), which was first derived by Lerche
Lerche 1968
, is also shown in App.
A
.

2.2
Quasilinear diffusion in velocity space

We are now ready to calculate the expression for the quasilinear diffusion equation for the slow evolution (
\tau=\epsilon^{2}t
) of the background Vlasov distribution

\frac{1}{\Omega}\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\tau}\;=\;-\;{\rm Re}\left[\left\langle\frac{q}{M\Omega}\left(\delta\widetilde{\bf E}^{*}+\frac{\bf v}{c}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\delta\widetilde{\bf B}^{*}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial\delta\widetilde{f}}{\partial\bf v}\right\rangle\right]\;=\;-\,{\rm Re}\left[\left\langle\left(\delta\widetilde{V}_{\|}^{*}\;\frac{\partial}{\partial v_{\|}}\;+\;\delta\widetilde{V}_{\bot}^{*}\;\frac{\partial}{\partial v_{\bot}}\;+\;\delta\widetilde{\phi}^{*}\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}\right)\delta\widetilde{f}\right\rangle\right],

(2.18)

where
\epsilon
denotes the amplitude of the perturbation fields,
\langle\;\rangle

denotes a gyroangle average, and
(\delta\widetilde{V}_{\|}^{*},\delta\widetilde{V}_{\bot}^{*},\delta\widetilde{\phi}^{*})
are the complex conjugates of
Eqs. (
2.5
)-(
2.7
). In addition, the real part appears on the
right side of Eq. (
2.18
) as a result of averaging with respect to the wave phase

\vartheta
. We note that Kennel and Engelmann
Kennel and Engelmann 1966
ignore the term
\partial f_{2}/\partial t
on the left side of Eq. (
2.18
), which is associated with the
second-order perturbed Vlasov distribution
f_{2}
generated by non-resonant particles
Kaufman 1972a
;
Dewar 1973
. While this term was shown by Kaufman
Kaufman 1972a
to be essential in demonstrating the energy-momentum conservation laws of quasilinear theory, it is also omitted here and the right side of Eq. (
2.18
) only contains resonant-particle contributions.

First, since Eqs. (
2.5
)-(
2.6
) are independent of
v_{\|}
and
v_{\bot}
, respectively, we find

\displaystyle\left\langle\delta\widetilde{V}_{\|}^{*}\;\frac{\partial\delta\widetilde{f}}{\partial v_{\|}}\;+\;\delta\widetilde{V}_{\bot}^{*}\;\frac{\partial\delta\widetilde{f}}{\partial v_{\bot}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial v_{\|}}\left\langle\delta\widetilde{V}_{\|}^{*}\frac{}{}\delta\widetilde{f}\right\rangle\;+\;\frac{\partial}{\partial v_{\bot}}\left\langle\delta\widetilde{V}_{\bot}^{*}\frac{}{}\delta\widetilde{f}\right\rangle

(2.19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial v_{\|}}\left\langle\delta\widetilde{V}_{\|}^{*}\frac{}{}\delta\widetilde{f}\right\rangle\;+\;\frac{1}{v_{\bot}}\frac{\partial}{\partial v_{\bot}}\left(v_{\bot}\;\left\langle\delta\widetilde{V}_{\bot}^{*}\frac{}{}\delta\widetilde{f}\right\rangle\right)\;-\;\left\langle\frac{\delta\widetilde{V}_{\bot}^{*}}{v_{\bot}}\;\delta\widetilde{f}\right\rangle,

where we took into account the proper Jacobian
(v_{\bot})
in cylindrical velocity space
(v_{\|},v_{\bot},\phi)
. On the other hand, the third term in Eq. (
2.18
) can be written as

\left\langle\delta\widetilde{\phi}^{*}\;\frac{\partial\delta\widetilde{f}}{\partial\phi}\right\rangle\;=\;-\;\left\langle\left(\frac{\partial\delta\widetilde{\phi}^{*}}{\partial\phi}\right)\;\delta\widetilde{f}\right\rangle\;=\;\frac{q}{M\Omega}\left(\delta\widetilde{\bf E}^{*}+\frac{v_{\|}\widehat{\sf z}}{c}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\delta\widetilde{\bf B}^{*}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\langle\frac{\widehat{\bot}}{v_{\bot}}\;\delta\widetilde{f}\right\rangle\;\equiv\;\left\langle\frac{\delta\widetilde{V}_{\bot}^{*}}{v_{\bot}}\;\delta\widetilde{f}\right\rangle,

where the last term in Eq. (
2.7
) is independent of the gyroangle
\phi
. Since this term cancels the last term in Eq. (
2.19
), the quasilinear diffusion equation (
2.18
) becomes

\frac{1}{\Omega}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\tau}\;=\;-\;\frac{\partial}{\partial v_{\|}}\left({\rm Re}\left\langle\delta\widetilde{V}_{\|}^{*}\frac{}{}\delta\widetilde{f}\right\rangle\right)\;-\;\frac{1}{v_{\bot}}\frac{\partial}{\partial v_{\bot}}\left(v_{\bot}\;{\rm Re}\left\langle\delta\widetilde{V}_{\bot}^{*}\frac{}{}\delta\widetilde{f}\right\rangle\right).

(2.20)

Next, using the identity (
1.3
), we find

\sum_{m}J_{m}(\lambda)\left\langle\left(1,\frac{}{}\widehat{\bot}\right)\;e^{i(m-\ell)(\phi-\psi)}\right\rangle\;=\;\left(J_{\ell}(\lambda),\frac{}{}\mathbb{J}_{\bot\ell}(\lambda)\right),

so that, from Eq. (
2.15
), we find

\displaystyle\sum_{m}J_{m}(\lambda)\left\langle\delta\widetilde{V}_{\|}^{*}\frac{}{}e^{i(m-\ell)(\phi-\psi)}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q}{M\Omega}\,\delta\widetilde{E}_{\|}^{*}\;J_{\ell}(\lambda)\;-\;v_{\bot}\,\widehat{\sf z}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\delta\widetilde{\bf B}^{*}}{B_{0}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mathbb{J}_{\bot\ell}(\lambda)\;\equiv\;\delta\widetilde{V}_{\|\ell}^{*},

(2.21)

\displaystyle\sum_{m}J_{m}(\lambda)\left\langle\delta\widetilde{V}_{\bot}^{*}\frac{}{}e^{i(m-\ell)(\phi-\psi)}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q}{M\Omega}\left(\delta\widetilde{\bf E}^{*}+\frac{v_{\|}\widehat{\sf z}}{c}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\delta\widetilde{\bf B}^{*}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mathbb{J}_{\bot\ell}(\lambda)\;\equiv\;\delta\widetilde{V}_{\bot\ell}^{*}.

(2.22)

Hence, the quasilinear diffusion equation (
2.20
) can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Omega}\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\;\frac{\partial}{\partial v_{\|}}\left\{{\rm Re}\left[\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}i\,\Delta_{\ell}\,\delta\widetilde{V}_{\|\ell}^{*}\left(\delta\widetilde{V}_{\|\ell}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial v_{\|}}\;+\;\delta\widetilde{V}_{\bot\ell}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial v_{\bot}}\right)\right]\right\}

(2.23)

\displaystyle-\;\frac{1}{v_{\bot}}\frac{\partial}{\partial v_{\bot}}\left\{v_{\bot}\;{\rm Re}\left[\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}i\,\Delta_{\ell}\,\delta\widetilde{V}_{\bot\ell}^{*}\left(\delta\widetilde{V}_{\|\ell}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial v_{\|}}\;+\;\delta\widetilde{V}_{\bot\ell}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial v_{\bot}}\right)\right]\right\}\;\equiv\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\bf v}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\sf D$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\bf v}\right),

where the diagonal diffusion coefficients are

\displaystyle{\sf D}^{\|\|}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\widehat{\sf z}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\sf D$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{\sf z}\;=\;\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}\;{\rm Re}\left(-\,i\,\Delta_{\ell}\right)\;|\delta\widetilde{V}_{\|\ell}|^{2},

(2.24)

\displaystyle{\sf D}^{\bot\bot}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\widehat{\bot}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\sf D$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{\bot}\;=\;\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}\;{\rm Re}\left(-\;i\,\Delta_{\ell}\right)\;|\delta\widetilde{V}_{\bot\ell}|^{2},

(2.25)

while the off-diagonal diffusion coefficients are

\displaystyle{\sf D}^{\|\bot}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\widehat{\sf z}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\sf D$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{\bot}\;=\;\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm Re}\left(-\;i\,\Delta_{\ell}\right)\;{\rm Re}(\delta\widetilde{V}_{\|\ell}^{*}\,\delta\widetilde{V}_{\bot\ell}),

(2.26)

\displaystyle{\sf D}^{\bot\|}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\widehat{\bot}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\sf D$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{\sf z}\;=\;\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm Re}\left(-\;i\,\Delta_{\ell}\right)\;{\rm Re}(\delta\widetilde{V}_{\bot\ell}^{*}\,\delta\widetilde{V}_{\|\ell}),

(2.27)

which are defined to be explicitly symmetric (i.e.,
{\sf D}^{\|\bot}={\sf D}^{\bot\|}
). Here, using the Plemelj formula

Stix 1992
, we find

{\rm Re}\left(-\;i\,\Delta_{\ell}\right)\;=\;{\rm Re}\left[\frac{i\,\Omega}{(\omega-k_{\|}v_{\|}-\ell\Omega)}\right]\;=\;\pi\,\Omega\;\delta(\omega_{r}\;-\;k_{\|}v_{\|}-\ell\Omega),

(2.28)

where we assumed
\omega=\omega_{r}+i\,\gamma
and took the weakly unstable limit
\gamma\rightarrow 0^{+}
. Hence, the quasilinear diffusion coefficients (
2.24
)-(
2.27
) are driven by resonant particles, which satisfy the resonance condition
k_{\|}v_{\|{\rm res}}\equiv\omega-\ell\Omega
. The reader is referred to the early references by Kaufman
Kaufman 1972a
and Dewar
Dewar 1973
concerning the role of non-resonant particles in demonstrating the energy-momentum conservation laws of quasilinear theory.

Equation (2.25) from Kennel and Engelmann
Kennel and Engelmann 1966
(see App.
A
) can be expressed as the dyadic diffusion tensor

\mbox{\boldmath$\sf D$}\;\equiv\;\sum_{\ell}{\rm Re}(-\,i\,\Delta_{\ell})\;\widetilde{\bf v}_{\ell}^{*}\,\widetilde{\bf v}_{\ell}\;=\;\sum_{\ell}{\rm Re}(-\,i\,\Delta_{\ell})\left[\left(\delta\widetilde{V}^{*}_{\|\ell}\;\widehat{\sf z}\;+\;\delta\widetilde{V}^{*}_{\bot\ell}\;\widehat{\bot}\right)\frac{}{}\left(\delta\widetilde{V}_{\|\ell}\;\widehat{\sf z}\;+\;\delta\widetilde{V}_{\bot\ell}\;\widehat{\bot}\right)\right],

(2.29)

which is Hermitian since the term
-i\,\Delta_{\ell}
is replaced with
{\rm Re}(-\,i\,\Delta_{\ell})
. Here, the perturbed velocity

\displaystyle\widetilde{\bf v}_{\ell}=\delta\widetilde{V}_{\|\ell}\;\widehat{\sf z}\;+\;\delta\widetilde{V}_{\bot\ell}\;\widehat{\bot}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q\,\delta\widetilde{\bf E}}{M\Omega}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left[J_{\ell}(\lambda)\;\widehat{\sf z}\,\widehat{\sf z}\;+\;\mathbb{J}_{\bot\ell}(\lambda)\;\widehat{\bot}\right]\;+\;\widehat{\sf z}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\delta\widetilde{\bf B}}{B_{0}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mathbb{J}_{\bot\ell}(\lambda)\;\left(v_{\|}\,\widehat{\bot}\;-\frac{}{}v_{\bot}\,\widehat{\sf z}\right)

(2.30)

explicitly separates the electric and magnetic contributions to the quasilinear diffusion tensor (
2.29
). In particular, the role of the perturbed perpendicular magnetic field is clearly seen in the process of pitch-angle diffusion because of the presence of the terms
(v_{\|}\,\widehat{\bot}-v_{\bot}\,\widehat{\sf z})
associated with it. We also note that the parallel component of the perturbed magnetic field,
\delta\widetilde{B}_{\|}=\widehat{\sf z}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\delta\widetilde{\bf B}
, does not contribute to quasilinear diffusion in a uniform magnetized plasma. The components of the perturbed electric field, on the other hand, involve the parallel component,
\delta\widetilde{E}_{\|}=\widehat{\sf z}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\delta\widetilde{\bf E}
, as well as the right and left polarized components,
\delta\widetilde{E}_{R}=\delta\widetilde{\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,(\widehat{\sf x}-i\,\widehat{\sf y})/\sqrt{2}
and
\delta\widetilde{E}_{L}=\delta\widetilde{\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,(\widehat{\sf x}+i\,\widehat{\sf y})/\sqrt{2}
, respectively, appearing through the definition (
2.13
).

Lastly, we note that the dyadic form (
2.29
) of the quasilinear diffusion tensor in the quasilinear diffusion equation (
2.23
) can be used to easily verify that the unperturbed entropy
{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\equiv-\,\int f_{0}\;\ln f_{0}\;d^{3}v
satisfies the H Theorem:

\frac{d{\mathcal{S}}_{0}}{dt}\;=\;-\,\epsilon^{2}\int\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\tau}\;(\ln f_{0}+1)\;d^{3}v\;=\;\epsilon^{2}\sum_{\ell}\int{\rm Re}(-i\Delta_{\ell})\;f_{0}\;\left|\widetilde{\bf v}_{\ell}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial\ln f_{0}}{\partial\bf v}\right|^{2}\;d^{3}v\;>\;0.

(2.31)

Once again, the energy-momentum conservation laws in quasilinear theory will not be discussed here. Instead the interested reader can consult earlier references
Kaufman 1972a
;
Dewar 1973
, as well as Chapters 16-18 in the standard textbook by Stix
Stix 1992
.

2.3
Quasilinear diffusion in invariant velocity space

In preparation for Sec.
3
, we note that a natural choice of velocity-space coordinates, suggested by guiding-center theory, involves replacing the parallel velocity
v_{\|}
with the parallel momentum
p_{\|}=M\,v_{\|}
and the perpendicular speed
v_{\bot}
with the magnetic moment
\mu=Mv_{\bot}^{2}/(2B_{0})
. We note that these two coordinates are independent dynamical invariants of the particle motion in a uniform magnetic field.

With this change of coordinates, the quasilinear diffusion equation (
2.23
) becomes

\frac{1}{\Omega}\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\tau}\;\equiv\;\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{\|}}\left(D^{pp}\;\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial p_{\|}}\;+\;D^{p\mu}\;\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\mu}\right)\;+\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}\left(D^{\mu p}\;\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial p_{\|}}\;+\;D^{\mu\mu}\;\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\mu}\right),

(2.32)

where the quasilinear diffusion coefficients are

\left.\begin{array}[]{rcl}D^{pp}&=&M^{2}\,{\sf D}^{\|\|}\;=\;\sum_{\ell}{\rm Re}(-i\,\Delta_{\ell})\;|\delta\widetilde{P}_{\|\ell}|^{2}\\
D^{p\mu}&=&(M^{2}v_{\bot}/B_{0})\,{\sf D}^{\|\bot}\;=\;\sum_{\ell}{\rm Re}(-i\,\Delta_{\ell})\;{\rm Re}\left(\delta\widetilde{P}_{\|\ell}^{*}\frac{}{}\delta\widetilde{\mu}_{\ell}\right)\\
D^{\mu\mu}&=&(Mv_{\bot}/B_{0})^{2}\,{\sf D}^{\bot\bot}\;=\;\sum_{\ell}{\rm Re}(-i\,\Delta_{\ell})\;|\delta\widetilde{\mu}_{\ell}|^{2}\end{array}\right\},

(2.33)

with the eikonal amplitudes

\displaystyle\delta\widetilde{P}_{\|\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q}{\Omega}\left(\delta\widetilde{E}_{\|}\;J_{\ell}\;+\;\frac{v_{\bot}}{c}\,\mathbb{J}_{\bot\ell}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\delta\widetilde{\bf B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{\sf z}\right),

(2.34)

\displaystyle\delta\widetilde{\mu}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q}{B_{0}\Omega}\left(\delta\widetilde{\bf E}\;+\;\frac{v_{\|}\,\widehat{\sf z}}{c}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\delta\widetilde{\bf B}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,v_{\bot}\,\mathbb{J}_{\bot\ell},

(2.35)

and the symmetry
D^{\mu p}=D^{p\mu}
follows from the assumption of a Hermitian diffusion tensor. Lastly, as expected, we note that the eikonal amplitude for the perturbed kinetic energy

\delta\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\ell}\;\equiv\;M{\bf v}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widetilde{\bf v}_{\ell}\;=\;v_{\|}\,\delta\widetilde{P}_{\|\ell}+\delta\widetilde{\mu}_{\ell}\,B_{0}\;=\;\frac{q}{\Omega}\;\delta\widetilde{\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(v_{\|}\;J_{\ell}\;+\frac{}{}v_{\bot}\;\mathbb{J}_{\bot\ell}\right),

(2.36)

only involves the perturbed electric field. Hence, another useful representation of quasilinear diffusion in invariant velocity
({\mathcal{E}},\mu)
space is given by the quasilinear diffusion equation

\frac{1}{\Omega}\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\tau}\;\equiv\;v_{\|}\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathcal{E}}\left[\frac{1}{v_{\|}}\left(D^{{\mathcal{E}}{\mathcal{E}}}\;\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\mathcal{E}}\;+\;D^{{\mathcal{E}}\mu}\;\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\mu}\right)\right]\;+\;v_{\|}\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}\left[\frac{1}{v_{\|}}\left(D^{\mu{\mathcal{E}}}\;\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\mathcal{E}}\;+\;D^{\mu\mu}\;\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\mu}\right)\right],

(2.37)

where the quasilinear diffusion coefficients are

\left.\begin{array}[]{rcl}D^{{\mathcal{E}}{\mathcal{E}}}&=&\sum_{\ell}{\rm Re}(-i\,\Delta_{\ell})\;|\delta\widetilde{{\mathcal{E}}}_{\ell}|^{2}\\
D^{{\mathcal{E}}\mu}&=&\sum_{\ell}{\rm Re}(-i\,\Delta_{\ell})\;{\rm Re}\left(\delta\widetilde{{\mathcal{E}}}_{\ell}^{*}\frac{}{}\delta\widetilde{\mu}_{\ell}\right)\\
D^{\mu\mu}&=&\sum_{\ell}{\rm Re}(-i\,\Delta_{\ell})\;|\delta\widetilde{\mu}_{\ell}|^{2}\end{array}\right\},

(2.38)

and the Jacobian
1/v_{\|}
is a function of
({\mathcal{E}},\mu)
:
|v_{\|}|=\sqrt{(2/M)({\mathcal{E}}-\mu\,B_{0})}
, while the sign of
v_{\|}
is a constant of the motion in a uniform
magnetic field.

3
Hamiltonian Quasilinear Diffusion Equation

In Sec.
2
, we reviewed the standard formulation of quasilinear theory in a uniform magnetized plasma
Kennel and Engelmann 1966
. In this Section, we introduce the Hamiltonian formulation of the Vlasov equation from which we will derive the Hamiltonian quasilinear diffusion equation, which will then be compared with the Kennel-Engelmann quasilinear diffusion equation (
2.23
).

In order to proceed with a Hamiltonian formulation, however, we will be required to express the perturbed electric and magnetic fields in terms of perturbed electric and magnetic potentials. We note that, despite the use of these potentials, the gauge invariance of the Hamiltonian quasilinear diffusion equation will be guaranteed in the formulation adopted here.

3.1
Non-adiabatic decomposition of the perturbed Vlasov distribution

The Hamiltonian formulation of quasilinear diffusion begins with the representation of the perturbed electric and magnetic fields in terms of the perturbed electric scalar potential
\delta\Phi
and the perturbed magnetic vector potential
\delta{\bf A}
, where

\delta{\bf E}=-\nabla\delta\Phi-c^{-1}\partial\delta{\bf A}/\partial t
and

\delta{\bf B}=\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\delta{\bf A}
. Hence, we find the identity

\delta{\bf E}\;+\;\frac{\bf v}{c}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\delta{\bf B}\;=\;-\;\nabla\left(\delta\Phi\;-\;\frac{\bf v}{c}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\delta{\bf A}\right)\;-\;\frac{1}{c}\,\frac{d\delta{\bf A}}{dt}\;\equiv\;-\;\nabla\delta\Psi\;-\;\frac{1}{c}\,\frac{d\delta{\bf A}}{dt},

(3.1)

where
d/dt
denotes the unperturbed time derivative. We note that the gauge transformation

(\delta\Phi,\delta{\bf A},\delta\Psi)\;\rightarrow\;\left(\delta\Phi-\frac{1}{c}\frac{\partial\delta\chi}{\partial t},\;\delta{\bf A}+\nabla\delta\chi,\;\delta\Psi\;-\;\frac{1}{c}\,\frac{d\delta\chi}{dt}\right)

(3.2)

guarantees the gauge invariance of the right side of Eq. (
3.1
).

Next, by removing the perturbed magnetic vector potential
\delta{\bf A}
from the canonical momentum

{\bf P}\;=\;m{\bf v}+q\,({\bf A}_{0}+\epsilon\,\delta{\bf A})/c\;\rightarrow\;{\bf P}_{0}\;=\;m{\bf v}+q{\bf A}_{0}/c,

the noncanonical Poisson bracket (which can also be expressed in divergence form)

\displaystyle\{f,\;g\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M}\left(\nabla f\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial g}{\partial\bf v}\;-\;\frac{\partial f}{\partial\bf v}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla g\right)\;+\;\frac{q{\bf B}_{0}}{M^{2}c}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial f}{\partial\bf v}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\partial g}{\partial\bf v}

(3.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\bf v}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left[\frac{1}{M}\left(\nabla f\;+\;\frac{q{\bf B}_{0}}{Mc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\partial f}{\partial\bf v}\right)\;g\right]\;-\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial\bf v}\;\frac{g}{M}\right)

(3.4)

only contains the unperturbed magnetic field
{\bf B}_{0}
., where
f
and
g
are arbitrary functions of
({\bf x},{\bf v})
.

The removal of the perturbed magnetic vector potential
\delta{\bf A}
from the noncanonical Poisson bracket (
3.3
), however, implies that the perturbed Vlasov distribution can be written as

\delta f\;=\;\frac{q}{c}\,\delta{\bf A}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\bf v}\;+\;\delta g\;\equiv\;\frac{q}{c}\,\delta{\bf A}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf x},f_{0}\}\;+\;\{\delta s,\;f_{0}\},

(3.5)

where the
non-adiabatic
contribution
\delta g
is said to be generated by the perturbation scalar field
\delta s

Brizard 1994
;
Brizard 2018
;
Brizard and Chandre 2020
, which satisfies the first-order eikonal equation

i\;\left({\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v}\;-\frac{}{}\omega\right)\delta\widetilde{s}\;-\;\Omega\;\frac{\partial\delta\widetilde{s}}{\partial\phi}\;=\;q\,\left(\delta\widetilde{\Phi}\;-\;\frac{\bf v}{c}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\delta\widetilde{\bf A}\right)\;\equiv\;q\,\delta\widetilde{\Psi}.

(3.6)

Hence, the eikonal solution for
\delta\widetilde{s}
is expressed with the same integrating factor used in Eq. (
2.3
):

\delta\widetilde{s}({\bf v})\;=\;-\,\frac{q}{\Omega}\;e^{i\Theta}\int^{\phi}\delta\widetilde{\Psi}^{\prime}\,e^{-i\Theta^{\prime}}\,d\phi^{\prime}\;=\;-\frac{q}{\Omega}\sum_{m,\ell}i\,\Delta_{\ell}\;J_{m}(\lambda)\;e^{i(m-\ell)(\phi-\psi)}\;\delta\widetilde{\Psi}_{\ell},

(3.7)

where the gyroangle Fourier component of the effective perturbed potential is

\delta\widetilde{\Psi}_{\ell}\;\equiv\;\left(\delta\widetilde{\Phi}-\frac{v_{\|}}{c}\,\delta\widetilde{A}_{\|}\right)J_{\ell}(\lambda)\;-\;\frac{v_{\bot}}{c}\,\delta\widetilde{\bf A}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mathbb{J}_{\bot\ell}(\lambda),

(3.8)

and the eikonal amplitude of the non-adiabatic perturbed Vlasov distribution is

\delta\widetilde{g}\;=\;e^{-i\vartheta}\left\{\delta\widetilde{s}\,e^{i\vartheta},\frac{}{}f_{0}\right\}\;=\;\frac{1}{M}\left(i{\bf k}\,\delta\widetilde{s}\;+\;\Omega\,\widehat{\sf z}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\partial\delta\widetilde{s}}{\partial\bf v}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\bf v}\;=\;\frac{i{\bf k}}{M}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\bf v}\;\delta\widetilde{s}\;-\;\frac{\Omega}{B_{0}}\,\frac{\partial\delta\widetilde{s}}{\partial\phi}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\mu},

(3.9)

where
\mu\equiv M|{\bf v}_{\bot}|^{2}/(2B_{0})
denotes the magnetic moment. We note that, under the gauge transformations (
3.2
), the scalar field
\delta s
transforms as
\delta s\rightarrow\delta s-(q/c)\,\delta\chi

Brizard 1994
;
Brizard 2018
;
Brizard and Chandre 2020
, and the expression (
3.5
) for the perturbed Vlasov distribution is gauge-invariant. Moreover, under the gauge transformation (
3.2
), the eikonal Fourier amplitude (
3.8
) transforms as

\delta\widetilde{\Psi}_{\ell}\;\rightarrow\;\delta\widetilde{\Psi}_{\ell}\;+\;\frac{i}{c}\left(\omega-k_{\|}v_{\|}-\ell\,\Omega\right)\;J_{\ell}\;\delta\widetilde{\chi},

(3.10)

which is consistent with Eq. (
3.2
).

Next, since the components of the Poisson bracket (
3.3
) are constant, the unperturbed time derivative of
\delta f
yields the linearized perturbed Vlasov equation

\displaystyle\frac{d\delta f}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q}{c}\,\frac{d\delta{\bf A}}{dt}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf x},f_{0}\}\;+\;\frac{q}{c}\,\delta{\bf A}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf v},f_{0}\}\;+\;\left\{\frac{d\delta s}{dt},\;f_{0}\right\}

(3.11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q}{c}\,\frac{d\delta{\bf A}}{dt}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf x},f_{0}\}\;+\;\frac{q}{c}\,\delta{\bf A}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf v},f_{0}\}\;+\;\left\{q\,\delta\Psi,\;f_{0}\right\}\;=\;\left(\frac{q}{c}\,\frac{d\delta{\bf A}}{dt}\;+\;q\,\nabla\delta\Psi\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf x},f_{0}\}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\,\frac{q}{M}\left(\delta{\bf E}\;+\;\frac{\bf v}{c}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\delta{\bf B}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\bf v},

which implies that the non-adiabatic decomposition (
3.5
) is a valid representation of the perturbed Vlasov distribution.

3.2
Second-order perturbed Vlasov equation

In order to derive an alternate formulation of quasilinear theory for uniform magnetized plasmas, we begin with second-order evolution of the background Vlasov distribution

\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\tau}\;=\;-\frac{q}{M}\left(\delta{\bf E}+\frac{\bf v}{c}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\delta{\bf B}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial\delta f}{\partial\bf v}\;=\;\left(q\,\nabla\delta\Psi\;+\;\frac{q}{c}\,\frac{d\delta{\bf A}}{dt}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf x},\;\delta f\},

(3.12)

where, once again,
\tau=\epsilon^{2}t
denotes the slow quasilinear diffusion time scale, we have ignored the second-order perturbed Vlasov distribution
f_{2}
, and we have inserted Eqs. (
3.1
) and (
3.3
). The first term on the right side of Eq. (
3.12
) can be written as

\displaystyle q\nabla\delta\Psi\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf x},\;\delta f\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{q\,\delta\Psi,\;\delta f\}\;-\;q\,\frac{\partial\delta\Psi}{\partial\bf v}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf v},\;\delta f\}\;=\;\{q\,\delta\Psi,\;\delta f\}\;+\;\frac{q}{c}\,\delta{\bf A}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf v},\;\delta f\}

(3.13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{q\,\delta\Psi,\;\left(\frac{q}{c}\,\delta{\bf A}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf x},f_{0}\}\;+\;\delta g\right)\right\}\;+\;\frac{q}{c}\,\delta{\bf A}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\{{\bf v},\;\delta f\right\},

where we have inserted the non-adiabatic decomposition (
3.5
), so that the first term can be written as

\displaystyle\left\{q\,\delta\Psi,\;\frac{q}{c}\,\delta{\bf A}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf x},f_{0}\}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q^{2}}{c}\left(\{\delta\Psi,\;\delta{\bf A}\}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf x},\;f_{0}\}\;+\;\delta{\bf A}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\{\delta\Psi,\frac{}{}\{{\bf x},\;f_{0}\}\right\}\right)

(3.14)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q^{2}}{Mc^{2}}\,\delta{\bf A}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\delta{\bf A}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf x},\;f_{0}\}\;+\;\frac{q}{c}\delta{\bf A}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\{q\,\delta\Psi,\frac{}{}\{{\bf x},\;f_{0}\}\right\},

where we used
\{\delta\Psi,\delta{\bf A}\}=(\delta{\bf A}/Mc)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\delta{\bf A}
. The second term on the right side of Eq. (
3.12
), on the other hand, can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{q}{c}\,\frac{d\delta{\bf A}}{dt}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf x},\;\delta f\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q^{2}}{c^{2}}\,\frac{d\delta{\bf A}}{dt}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\{{\bf x},\frac{}{}\{{\bf x},f_{0}\}\right\}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\delta{\bf A}\;+\;\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{q}{c}\delta{\bf A}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf x},\;\delta g\}\right)\;-\;\frac{q}{c}\delta{\bf A}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf v},\delta g\}

(3.15)

\displaystyle+\;\frac{q}{c}\delta{\bf A}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\{{\bf x},\frac{}{}\{f_{0},\;q\,\delta\Psi\}\right\}.

Next, by using the Jacobi identity for the Poisson bracket (
3.3
):

\left\{f,\frac{}{}\{g,\;h\}\right\}+\left\{g,\frac{}{}\{h,\;f\}\right\}+\left\{h,\frac{}{}\{f,\;g\}\right\}\;=\;0,

(3.16)

which holds for arbitrary functions
(f,g,h)
, we obtain

\frac{q^{2}}{c}\,\delta{\bf A}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\left\{\delta\Psi,\frac{}{}\{{\bf x},\;f_{0}\}\right\}\;+\;\left\{{\bf x},\frac{}{}\{f_{0},\;\delta\Psi\}\right\}\right)\;=\;\frac{q^{2}}{c}\,\delta{\bf A}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\{f_{0},\frac{}{}\{{\bf x},\;\delta\Psi\}\right\}\;\equiv\;-\;\{f_{0},\;\delta H_{2}\},

(3.17)

where
\delta H_{2}=q^{2}|\delta{\bf A}|^{2}/(2Mc^{2})
is the second-order perturbed Hamiltonian. We now look at the first term on the right side of Eq. (
3.15
), which we write as

\displaystyle\frac{q^{2}}{c^{2}}\,\frac{d\delta{\bf A}}{dt}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\{{\bf x},\frac{}{}\{{\bf x},f_{0}\}\right\}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\delta{\bf A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{q^{2}}{c^{2}}\,\delta{\bf A}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\{{\bf x},\frac{}{}\{{\bf x},f_{0}\}\right\}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\delta{\bf A}\right)\;-\;\frac{q^{2}}{c^{2}}\,\delta{\bf A}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\left\{{\bf v},\frac{}{}\{{\bf x},f_{0}\}\right\}+\left\{{\bf x},\frac{}{}\{{\bf v},f_{0}\}\right\}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\delta{\bf A}

(3.18)

\displaystyle-\;\frac{q^{2}}{c^{2}}\,\delta{\bf A}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\{{\bf x},\frac{}{}\{{\bf x},f_{0}\}\right\}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{d\delta{\bf A}}{dt}.

Because of the symmetry of the tensor
\left\{{\bf x},\frac{}{}\{{\bf x},\;f_{0}\}\right\}
, the last term on the right side (omitting the minus sign) is equal to the left side, so that we obtain

\frac{q^{2}}{c^{2}}\,\frac{d\delta{\bf A}}{dt}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\{{\bf x},\frac{}{}\{{\bf x},f_{0}\}\right\}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\delta{\bf A}=\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{q^{2}}{2c^{2}}\,\delta{\bf A}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\{{\bf x},\frac{}{}\{{\bf x},f_{0}\}\right\}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\delta{\bf A}\right)\;-\;\frac{q^{2}}{c^{2}}\,\delta{\bf A}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\{{\bf v},\frac{}{}\{{\bf x},f_{0}\}\right\}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\delta{\bf A},

where we used the Jacobi identity (
3.16
) to find
\{{\bf x},\;\{{\bf v},f_{0}\}\}=\{{\bf v},\;\{{\bf x},f_{0}\}\}
, since
\{f_{0},\;\{{\bf x},{\bf v}\}\}=0
.

When these equations are combined into Eq. (
3.12
), we obtain the final Hamiltonian form of the second-order perturbed Vlasov equation

\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\tau}\;=\;\left\{\delta H,\frac{}{}\delta g\right\}\;+\;\left\{\delta H_{2},\frac{}{}f_{0}\right\}\;+\;\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{q}{c}\,\delta{\bf A}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf x},\;\delta g\}\;+\;\frac{q^{2}}{2c^{2}}\;\delta{\bf A}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\{{\bf x},\frac{}{}\{{\bf x},\;f_{0}\}\right\}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\delta{\bf A}\right),

(3.19)

where
\delta H=q\,\delta\Psi=q\,\delta\Phi-q\,\delta{\bf A}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v}/c
and
\delta g=\{\delta s,\;f_{0}\}
.

3.3
Hamiltonian quasilinear diffusion equation

We now perform two separate averages of the second-order perturbed Vlasov equation (
3.19
): we first perform an average with respect to the wave phase
\vartheta
, which will be denoted by an overbar, and, second, we perform an average with respect to the gyroangle
\phi
. We begin by noting that the averaged second-order perturbed Hamiltonian
\delta\overline{H}_{2}=q^{2}|\delta\widetilde{\bf A}|^{2}/(2Mc^{2})
is a constant and, therefore, its contribution in Eq. (
3.19
) vanishes upon eikonal-phase averaging. Likewise, the total time derivative in Eq. (
3.19
) vanishes upon eikonal-phase averaging.

The Hamiltonian quasilinear diffusion equation is, therefore, defined as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\tau}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\;\left\langle\overline{\left\{\delta H,\frac{}{}\delta g\right\}}\right\rangle\;=\;\frac{1}{2}\overline{\left[\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\frac{q}{Mc}\,\delta{\bf A}\;\langle\delta g\rangle\right)\right]}\;+\;\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial\bf v}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\overline{\left[\left(\nabla\delta H\;+\;\Omega\,\frac{q}{c}\,\delta{\bf A}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{\sf z}\right)\;\frac{\delta g}{M}\right]}\right\rangle

(3.20)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial\bf v}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\overline{\left[\left(\nabla\delta H\;+\;\Omega\,\frac{q}{c}\,\delta{\bf A}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{\sf z}\right)\;\frac{\delta g}{M}\right]}\right\rangle,

where we used the divergence form (
3.4
) of the Poisson bracket and the eikonal average of the spatial divergence vanishes. Next, the eikonal average of the first term on the last line of the right side of Eq. (
3.20
) yields

\overline{\left(\nabla\delta H\frac{}{}\delta g\right)}\;=\;i\,{\bf k}\;\left(\delta\widetilde{H}\;\delta\widetilde{g}^{*}\;-\frac{}{}\delta\widetilde{H}^{*}\;\delta\widetilde{g}\right),

so that

\displaystyle\left\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial\bf v}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\overline{\left(\nabla\delta H\;\frac{\delta g}{M}\right)}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{\|}}\left(ik_{\|}\frac{}{}\left\langle\delta\widetilde{H}\;\delta\widetilde{g}^{*}\;-\frac{}{}\delta\widetilde{H}^{*}\;\delta\widetilde{g}\right\rangle\right)\;+\;\frac{1}{B_{0}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}\left[i{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\langle{\bf v}_{\bot}\left(\delta\widetilde{H}\;\delta\widetilde{g}^{*}\;-\frac{}{}\delta\widetilde{H}^{*}\;\delta\widetilde{g}\right)\right\rangle\right],

(3.21)

where
p_{\|}=M\,v_{\|}
and
\mu=M|{\bf v}_{\bot}|^{2}/2B_{0}
.
The eikonal average of the second term on the last line of the right side of Eq. (
3.20
), on the other hand, yields

\frac{\Omega}{2\,v_{\bot}}\frac{\partial}{\partial v_{\bot}}\left[\frac{v_{\bot}}{M}\left(\frac{q}{c}\delta\widetilde{\bf A}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\langle\widehat{\phi}\,\delta\widetilde{g}^{*}\right\rangle\;+\;\frac{q}{c}\delta\widetilde{\bf A}^{*}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\langle\widehat{\phi}\,\delta\widetilde{g}\right\rangle\right)\right]\;\equiv\;\frac{\Omega}{B_{0}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}\left[{\rm Re}\left\langle\left(\frac{q}{c}\delta\widetilde{\bf A}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial{\bf v}_{\bot}}{\partial\phi}\right)\delta\widetilde{g}^{*}\right\rangle\right],

(3.22)

so that by combining Eqs. (
3.21
)-(
3.22
) into Eq. (
3.20
), we find

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Omega}\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{\|}}\left(\frac{k_{\|}}{\Omega}\;{\rm Re}\left\langle i\,\delta\widetilde{H}\;\delta\widetilde{g}^{*}\right\rangle\right)\;+\;\frac{1}{B_{0}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}\left[{\rm Re}\left\langle\left(\frac{q}{c}\delta\widetilde{\bf A}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial{\bf v}_{\bot}}{\partial\phi}\;+\;i\,\frac{{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v}_{\bot}}{\Omega}\;\delta\widetilde{H}\right)\delta\widetilde{g}^{*}\right\rangle\right],

(3.23)

In order to evaluate the gyroangle averages in Eq. (
3.23
), we need to proceed with a transformation from particle phase space to guiding-center phase space, which is presented in the next Section.

4
Guiding-center Hamiltonian Quasilinear Diffusion Equation

In this Section, we use the guiding-center transformation
Northrop 1963
in order to simplify the calculations involved in obtaining an explicit expression for the Hamiltonian quasilinear diffusion equation (
3.23
) that can compared with the standard quasilinear diffusion equation (
2.32
) obtained from Kennel-Engelmann’s work
Kennel and Engelmann 1966
.

4.1
Guiding-center transformation

In a uniform background magnetic field, the transformation from particle phase space to guiding-center phase space is simply given as
{\bf x}={\bf X}+\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}
, where the particle position
{\bf x}
is expressed as the sum of the guiding-center position
{\bf X}
and the gyroradius vector
\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}\equiv\widehat{\sf z}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf v}_{\bot}/\Omega
, while the velocity-space coordinates
(p_{\|},\mu,\phi)
remain unchanged
Cary and Brizard 2009
. Hence, the eikonal wave phase
\vartheta={\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf x}-\omega\,t
becomes

\vartheta\;=\;{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,({\bf X}+\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$})-\omega\,t\;=\;\theta\;+\;{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}\;\equiv\;\theta\;+\;\Lambda,

(4.1)

where
\theta
denotes the guiding-center eikonal wave phase and
\Lambda\equiv\lambda\;\sin(\phi-\psi)
. Next, the particle Poisson bracket (
3.3
) is transformed into the guiding-center Poisson bracket
Cary and Brizard 2009

\{F,\;G\}_{\rm gc}\;=\;\widehat{\sf z}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\nabla F\;\frac{\partial G}{\partial p_{\|}}\;-\;\frac{\partial F}{\partial p_{\|}}\;\nabla G\right)\;-\;\frac{\Omega}{B_{0}}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\phi}\;\frac{\partial G}{\partial\mu}\;-\;\frac{\partial F}{\partial\mu}\;\frac{\partial G}{\partial\phi}\right)\;-\;\frac{c\,\widehat{\sf z}}{qB_{0}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla F\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla G,

(4.2)

where the last term vanishes in the case of a uniform background plasma since the guiding-center functions
F
and
G
depend on the guiding-center position only through the guiding-center wave phase
\theta
(with
\nabla\theta={\bf k}
).

4.2
First-order perturbed guiding-center Vlasov equation

The guiding-center transformation induces a transformation on particle phase-space functions
f
to a guiding-center phase-space function
F
through the guiding-center push-forward
{\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{-1}:F\equiv{\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{-1}f
. For a perturbed particle phase-space function
\delta g\equiv\delta\widetilde{g}\,\exp(i\vartheta)+{\rm c.c.}
, we find the perturbed guiding-center phase-space function
\delta G\equiv\delta\widetilde{G}\,\exp(i\theta)+{\rm c.c.}
, where the eikonal amplitude
\delta\widetilde{G}
is given by the push-forward expression as

\delta\widetilde{G}\;=\;\delta\widetilde{g}\;e^{-i\Lambda}\;=\;e^{-i\theta}\;\left\{\delta\widetilde{S}\,e^{i\theta},\frac{}{}f_{0}\right\}_{\rm gc}\;=\;i\,k_{\|}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial p_{\|}}\;\delta\widetilde{S}\;-\;\frac{\Omega}{B_{0}}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\mu}\;\frac{\partial\delta\widetilde{S}}{\partial\phi}.

(4.3)

The eikonal amplitude of the guiding-center generating function
\delta\widetilde{S}=\delta\widetilde{s}\,\exp(-i\Lambda)
satisfies an equation derived from the first-order eikonal equation (
3.6
):

i\,(k_{\|}v_{\|}-\omega)\;\delta\widetilde{S}\;-\;\Omega\,\frac{\partial\delta\widetilde{S}}{\partial\phi}\;=\;\delta\widetilde{H}\;e^{-i\Lambda}\;\equiv\;\delta\widetilde{H}_{\rm gc}.

(4.4)

The solution of the first-order guiding-center eikonal equation (
4.4
) makes use of the gyroangle expansion
\delta\widetilde{S}=\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}\delta\widetilde{S}_{\ell}\exp[-i\ell(\phi-\psi)]
, which yields the Fourier component

\delta\widetilde{S}_{\ell}\;=\;-\,\frac{i\,\Delta_{\ell}}{\Omega}\;\left\langle\delta\widetilde{H}\frac{}{}e^{-i\Lambda+i\ell(\phi-\psi)}\right\rangle\;=\;-\,\frac{i\,\Delta_{\ell}}{\Omega}\;q\,\delta\widetilde{\Psi}_{\ell}.

(4.5)

Inserting this solution into Eq. (
4.3
), with the gyroangle expansion
\delta\widetilde{G}=\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}\delta\widetilde{G}_{\ell}\exp[-i\ell(\phi-\psi)]
, yields

\delta\widetilde{G}_{\ell}\;=\;i\,\left(k_{\|}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial p_{\|}}\;+\;\frac{\ell\,\Omega}{B_{0}}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\mu}\right)\delta\widetilde{S}_{\ell}\;=\;\frac{q}{\Omega}\,\delta\widetilde{\Psi}_{\ell}\;\Delta_{\ell}\;\left(k_{\|}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial p_{\|}}\;+\;\frac{\ell\,\Omega}{B_{0}}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\mu}\right).

(4.6)

Hence, the solution for the eikonal amplitude
\delta\widetilde{g}
appearing in Eq. (
3.23
) can be obtained from the guiding-center pull-back expression
\delta\widetilde{g}=\delta\widetilde{G}\,\exp(i\,\Lambda)
.

4.3
Guiding-center Hamiltonian quasilinear diffusion equation

Using the solution (
4.6
) for
\delta\widetilde{G}_{\ell}
, we are now ready to calculate the quasilinear diffusion equation (
3.23
) and obtaina simple dyadic form for the quasilinear diffusion tensor.

a
Quasilinear diffusion in guiding-center
(p_{\|},\mu)
-space

Now that the solution for the eikonal amplitude
\delta g
is obtained in terms of the guiding-center phase-space function
\delta\widetilde{g}=\delta\widetilde{G}\,\exp(i\,\Lambda)
, we are now able to evaluate the gyroangle-averaged expressions in Eq. (
3.23
).
We begin with the gyroangle-averaged quadratic term

\displaystyle\left\langle\delta\widetilde{H}\;\delta\widetilde{g}^{*}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\delta\widetilde{H}\;\left(\delta\widetilde{G}\frac{}{}e^{i\Lambda}\right)^{*}\right\rangle\;=\;\left\langle\left(\delta\widetilde{H}\frac{}{}e^{-i\Lambda}\right)\;\delta\widetilde{G}^{*}\right\rangle\;=\;\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}\delta\widetilde{G}_{\ell}^{*}\left\langle\delta\widetilde{H}\frac{}{}e^{-i\Lambda+i\,\ell(\phi-\psi)}\right\rangle

(4.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}q\,\delta\widetilde{\Psi}_{\ell}\;\delta\widetilde{G}_{\ell}^{*}\;=\;\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{q^{2}}{\Omega}\,|\delta\widetilde{\Psi}_{\ell}|^{2}\;\Delta_{\ell}^{*}\left(k_{\|}\;\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial p_{\|}}\;+\;\frac{\ell\,\Omega}{B_{0}}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\mu}\right),

\frac{k_{\|}}{\Omega}\;{\rm Re}\left\langle i\,\delta\widetilde{H}\frac{}{}\delta\widetilde{g}^{*}\right\rangle\;=\;\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}k_{\|}\;{\mathcal{D}}_{\ell}\left(k_{\|}\;\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial p_{\|}}\;+\;\frac{\ell\,\Omega}{B_{0}}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\mu}\right),

(4.8)

where we introduced the quasilinear perturbation potential

{\mathcal{D}}_{\ell}\;=\;{\rm Re}\left(-\,i\,\Delta_{\ell}\right)\;|(q/\Omega)\,\delta\widetilde{\Psi}_{\ell}|^{2}\;\equiv\;{\rm Re}\left(-\,i\,\Delta_{\ell}\right)\;|\delta\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}_{\ell}|^{2},

(4.9)

and

\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{\|}}\left(\frac{k_{\|}}{\Omega}\;{\rm Re}\left\langle i\,\delta\widetilde{H}\frac{}{}\delta\widetilde{g}^{*}\right\rangle\right)\;\equiv\;\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{\|}}\left(D_{\rm H}^{pp}\;\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial p_{\|}}\;+\;D_{\rm H}^{p\mu}\;\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\mu}\right),

(4.10)

where
D_{\rm H}^{pp}=\sum_{\ell}\,k_{\|}^{2}\,{\mathcal{D}}_{\ell}
and

D_{\rm H}^{p\mu}=\sum_{\ell}k_{\|}\,(\ell\,\Omega/B_{0})\,{\mathcal{D}}_{\ell}
.

Next, we find

\displaystyle\left\langle\left(\frac{q}{c}\delta\widetilde{\bf A}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial{\bf v}_{\bot}}{\partial\phi}\;+\;i\,\frac{{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v}_{\bot}}{\Omega}\;\delta\widetilde{H}\right)\delta\widetilde{g}^{*}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\left(\frac{q}{c}\delta\widetilde{\bf A}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial{\bf v}_{\bot}}{\partial\phi}\;+\;i\,\frac{{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v}_{\bot}}{\Omega}\;\delta\widetilde{H}\right)\left(\delta\widetilde{G}\frac{}{}e^{i\Lambda}\right)^{*}\right\rangle

(4.11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\left[\frac{q}{c}\delta\widetilde{\bf A}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial{\bf v}_{\bot}}{\partial\phi}\,e^{-i\Lambda}\;-\;i\,q\frac{\partial\Lambda}{\partial\phi}\;\left(\delta\widetilde{\Phi}-\frac{v_{\|}}{c}\delta\widetilde{A}_{\|}-\frac{{\bf v}_{\bot}}{c}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\delta\widetilde{\bf A}\right)\,e^{-i\Lambda}\right]\delta\widetilde{G}^{*}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\;\left\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}\left(\delta\widetilde{H}\frac{}{}e^{-i\Lambda}\right)\delta\widetilde{G}^{*}\right\rangle\;=\;\left\langle\delta\widetilde{H}\;e^{-i\Lambda}\;\frac{\partial\delta\widetilde{G}^{*}}{\partial\phi}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}i\ell\;\delta\widetilde{G}_{\ell}^{*}\left\langle\delta\widetilde{H}\frac{}{}e^{-i\Lambda+i\,\ell(\phi-\psi)}\right\rangle\;=\;\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}i\ell\;q\,\delta\widetilde{\Psi}_{\ell}\;\delta\widetilde{G}_{\ell}^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}i\,\Delta_{\ell}^{*}\;\ell\Omega\;(q/\Omega)^{2}\,|\delta\widetilde{\Psi}_{\ell}|^{2}\;\left(k_{\|}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial p_{\|}}\;+\;\frac{\ell\,\Omega}{B_{0}}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\mu}\right),

so that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{B_{0}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}\left[{\rm Re}\left\langle\left(\frac{q}{c}\delta\widetilde{\bf A}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial{\bf v}_{\bot}}{\partial\phi}\;+\;i\,\frac{{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v}_{\bot}}{\Omega}\;\delta\widetilde{H}\right)\delta\widetilde{g}^{*}\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}\left[\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\ell\,\Omega}{B_{0}}\;{\mathcal{D}}_{\ell}\;\left(k_{\|}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial p_{\|}}\;+\;\frac{\ell\,\Omega}{B_{0}}\,\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\mu}\right)\right]

(4.12)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}\left(D_{\rm H}^{\mu p}\;\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial p_{\|}}\;+\;D_{\rm H}^{\mu\mu}\;\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\mu}\right),

where
D_{\rm H}^{\mu p}=\sum_{\ell}\,(\ell\,\Omega/B_{0})\,k_{\|}\,{\mathcal{D}}_{\ell}
and

D_{\rm H}^{\mu\mu}=\sum_{\ell}(\ell\,\Omega/B_{0})^{2}\,{\mathcal{D}}_{\ell}
.
We can now write the Hamiltonian quasilinear diffusion equation (
3.23
) as

\frac{1}{\Omega}\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\tau}\;=\;\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{\|}}\left(D_{\rm H}^{pp}\;\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial p_{\|}}\;+\;D_{\rm H}^{p\mu}\;\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\mu}\right)\;+\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}\left(D_{\rm H}^{\mu p}\;\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial p_{\|}}\;+\;D_{\rm H}^{\mu\mu}\;\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\mu}\right).

(4.13)

This quasilinear diffusion equation will later be compared with the standard quasilinear diffusion equation (
2.32
) derived by Kennel and Engelmann
Kennel and Engelmann 1966
.

b

({\sf J}_{\rm g},{\mathcal{E}})
-space

Before proceeding with this comparison, however, we consider an alternate representation for the Hamiltonian quasilinear diffusion equation (
4.13
), which will be useful in the derivation of a quasilinear diffusion equation for nonuniform magnetized plasmas. If we replace the guiding-center parallel momentum
p_{\|}
with the guiding-center kinetic energy
{\mathcal{E}}=p_{\|}^{2}/2m+\mu\,B_{0}
, and the guiding-center magnetic moment
\mu

{\sf J}_{\rm g}=\mu B_{0}/\Omega

\delta\widetilde{G}_{\ell}\;=\;q\,\delta\widetilde{\Psi}_{\ell}\;\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\mathcal{E}}\;+\;\frac{q}{\Omega}\,\delta\widetilde{\Psi}_{\ell}\;\Delta_{\ell}\left(\omega\;\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\mathcal{E}}\;+\;\ell\;\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial{\sf J}_{\rm g}}\right),

(4.14)

where the first term on the right side is interpreted as a guiding-center adiabatic contribution to the perturbed Vlasov distribution
Brizard 1994
, while the remaining terms (proportional to the resonant denominator
\Delta_{\ell}
) are non-adiabatic contributions.

By substituting this new solution in Eq. (

), we find

\frac{k_{\|}}{\Omega}\;{\rm Re}\left\langle i\,\delta\widetilde{H}\frac{}{}\delta\widetilde{g}^{*}\right\rangle\;=\;\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}k_{\|}\;{\mathcal{D}}_{\ell}\left(\omega\;\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\mathcal{E}}\;+\;\ell\;\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial{\sf J}_{\rm g}}\right),

(4.15)

while

{\rm Re}\left\langle\left(\frac{q}{c}\delta\widetilde{\bf A}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial{\bf v}_{\bot}}{\partial\phi}\;+\;i\,\frac{{\bf k}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v}_{\bot}}{\Omega}\;\delta\widetilde{H}\right)\delta\widetilde{g}^{*}\right\rangle\;=\;\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}\ell\Omega\;{\mathcal{D}}_{\ell}\left(\omega\;\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\mathcal{E}}\;+\;\ell\;\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial{\sf J}_{\rm g}}\right),

where the guiding-center adiabatic contribution has cancelled out. The guiding-center quasilinear diffusion equation (

\frac{1}{\Omega}\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\tau}\;=\;v_{\|}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathcal{E}}\left[\frac{1}{v_{\|}}\left(D_{\rm H}^{{\mathcal{E}}{\mathcal{E}}}\;\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\mathcal{E}}\;+\;D_{\rm H}^{{\mathcal{E}}{\sf J}}\;\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial{\sf J}_{\rm g}}\right)\right]\;+\;v_{\|}\frac{\partial}{\partial{\sf J}_{\rm g}}\left[\frac{1}{v_{\|}}\left(D_{\rm H}^{{\sf J}{\mathcal{E}}}\;\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\mathcal{E}}\;+\;D_{\rm H}^{{\sf J}{\sf J}}\;\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial{\sf J}_{\rm g}}\right)\right],

(4.17)

where the guiding-center quasilinear diffusion tensor is represented in
2\times 2
matrix form as

\mbox{\boldmath$\sf D$}_{\rm H}\;\equiv\;\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\ell^{2}&\ell\omega\\
&\\
\omega\,\ell&\omega^{2}\end{array}\right){\mathcal{D}}_{\ell}.

(4.18)

We note that, because of the simple dyadic form of Eq. (

), other representations for the guiding-center quasilinear diffusion tensor

\mbox{\boldmath$\sf D$}_{\rm H}
can be easily obtained, e.g., by replacing the guiding-center gyroaction

with the pitch-angle coordinate

. We also note that the dyadic quasilinear tensor (

) has a simple modular form compared to the dyadic form (

).

) with the guiding-center Hamiltonian quasilinear diffusion equation (

). First, we express the perturbed fields (

)-(

) in terms of the perturbed potentials

\displaystyle\delta\widetilde{P}_{\|\ell}\;=\;M\,\delta\widetilde{V}_{\|\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q}{\Omega}\left[J_{\ell}\;\left(-i\,k_{\|}\;\delta\widetilde{\Phi}\;+\;i\,\frac{\omega}{c}\;\delta\widetilde{A}_{\|}\right)\;-\;\frac{v_{\bot}}{c}\left(i{\bf k}\;\delta\widetilde{A}_{\|}\;-\;i\,k_{\|}\;\delta\widetilde{\bf A}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mathbb{J}_{\bot\ell}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\,k_{\|}\;\delta\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}_{\ell}\;+\;i\left(\omega\;-\;k_{\|}v_{\|}\;-\frac{}{}\ell\,\Omega\right)\frac{q\delta\widetilde{A}_{\|}}{\Omega\,c}\;J_{\ell},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\,\frac{\ell\,\Omega}{B_{0}}\;\delta\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}_{\ell}\;+\;i\left(\omega\;-\;k_{\|}v_{\|}\;-\frac{}{}\ell\,\Omega\right)\frac{q\delta\widetilde{\bf A}}{cB_{0}\Omega}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,v_{\bot}\mathbb{J}_{\bot\ell},

which are both gauge invariant according to the transformation (

). Hence, these perturbed fields are expressed in terms of a contribution from the perturbed action

and a contribution that vanishes for resonant particles (i.e.,

). We note that, in the resonant-particle limit (

(4.21)

which yields

in the resonant-particle limit (

).

In summary, we have shown that, in the resonant-particle limit (

), the Hamiltonian quasilinear diffusion equation (

) is identical to the standard quasilinear diffusion equation (

) derived by Kennel and Engelmann

5
Hamiltonian Quasilinear Formulations for Nonuniform Magnetized Plasma

In this Section, we briefly review the Hamiltonian formulation for quasilinear diffusion in a nonuniform magnetized background plasma. In an axisymmetric magnetic-field geometry, the
2\times 2
quasilinear diffusion tensor in velocity space is generalized to a
3\times 3
quasilinear diffusion tensor that includes radial quasilinear diffusion. In a spatially magnetically-confined plasma, the process of radial diffusion is a crucial element in determining whether charged particles leave the plasma. A prime example is provided by the case of radial diffusion in Earth’s radiation belt, which was recently reviewed by Lejosne and Kollmann
Lejosne and Kollmann 2020
.

We present two non-relativistic Hamiltonian formulations of quasilinear diffusion in a nonuniform magnetized plasmas. The first one based on the canonical action-angle formalism
Kaufman 1972b
;
Mahajan and Chen 1985
;
Mynick and Duvall 1989
;
Schulz 1996
and the second one based on a summary of our previous work
Brizard and Chan 2004
.

5.1
Canonical action-angle formalism

When a plasma is confined by a nonuniform magnetic field, the charged-particle orbits can be described in terms of 3 orbital angle coordinates
\theta
(generically referred to as the gyration, bounce, and precession-drift angles) and their canonically-conjugate 3 action coordinates
{\bf J}
(generically referred to as the gyromotion, bounce-motion, and drift-motion actions). In principle, these action coordinates are adiabatic invariants of the particle motion and they are calculated according to standard methods of guiding-center theory
Tao et al. 2007
;
Cary and Brizard 2009
, which are expressed in terms of asymptotic expansions in powers of a small dimensionless parameter
\epsilon_{B}=\rho/L_{B}\ll 1
defined as the ratio of a characteristic gyroradius (for a given particle species) and the gradient length scale
L_{B}
associated with the background magnetic field
{\bf B}_{0}
. When an asymptotic expansion for an adiabatic invariant
{\sf J}={\sf J}_{0}+\epsilon_{B}{\sf J}_{1}
is truncated at first order, for example, we find
d{\sf J}/dt\sim\epsilon_{B}^{2}
and the orbital angular average

\langle d{\sf J}/dt\rangle=0
is the necessary condition for the adiabatic invariance of
{\sf J}
. The reader is referred to Refs.
Cary and Brizard 2009
and
Tao et al. 2007
where explicit expansions for all three guiding-center adiabatic invariants are derived in the non-relativistic and relativistic limits, respectively, for arbitrary background magnetic geometry.

The canonical action-angle formulation of quasilinear theory assumes that, in the absence of wave-field perturbations, the action coordinates
{\bf J}
are constants of motion

d{\bf J}/dt=-\,\partial H_{0}/\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}=0
, which follows from the
invariance of the unperturbed Hamiltonian
H_{0}({\bf J})
on the canonical orbital angles

\theta
. In this case, the unperturbed Vlasov distribution
F_{0}({\bf J})
is a function of action coordinates only. We note that the action coordinates considered here are either exact invariants or adiabatic invariants
Kaufman 1972b
;
Mynick and Duvall 1989
of the particle motion, and it is implicitly assumed that any adiabatic action invariant used in this canonical action-angle formulation of quasilinear theory can be calculated to sufficiently high order in
\epsilon_{B}
within a region of particle phase space that excludes non-adiabatic diffusion in action space
Bernstein and Rowlands 1976
. For example, see Ref.
Brizard and Markowski 2022
for a brief discussion of the breakdown of the adiabatic invariance of the magnetic moment (on the bounce time scale) for charged particles trapped by an axisymmetric dipole magnetic field.

In the presence of wave-field perturbations, the perturbed Hamiltonian can be represented in terms of a Fourier decomposition in terms of a discrete wave spectrum
\omega_{k}
and orbital angles (with Fourier-index vector
{\bf m}
):

\delta{\mathcal{H}}({\bf J},\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$},t)\;=\;\sum_{{\bf m},k}\;\delta\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}({\bf J})\;\exp\left(i\frac{}{}{\bf m}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}-i\,\omega_{k}t\right)\;+\;{\rm c.c.},

(5.1)

where the parametric dependence of
\delta\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
on the Fourier indices
({\bf m},k)
is hidden. The perturbed Vlasov distribution
\delta F
is obtained from the perturbed Vlasov equation

\frac{\partial\delta F}{\partial t}\;+\;\frac{\partial\delta F}{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial H_{0}}{\partial\bf J}\;=\;\frac{\partial\delta{\mathcal{H}}}{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial\bf J},

(5.2)

from which we obtain the solution for the Fourier component
\delta\widetilde{f}
:

\delta\widetilde{F}\;=\;-\;\left(\frac{\delta\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}{\omega_{k}-{\bf m}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\Omega$}}\right)\;{\bf m}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial\bf J},

(5.3)

where
\mbox{\boldmath$\Omega$}({\bf J})\equiv\partial H_{0}/\partial{\bf J}
denotes the unperturbed orbital-frequency vector.

The quasilinear wave-particle interactions cause the Vlasov distribution

F_{0}({\bf J},\tau)
to evolve on a slow time scale
\tau=\epsilon^{2}t
, represented by the quasilinear diffusion equation

\displaystyle\frac{\partial F_{0}({\bf J},\tau)}{\partial\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\left\{\delta{\mathcal{H}},\frac{}{}\delta F\right\}\right\rangle\;=\;\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bf J}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\langle\frac{\partial\delta{\mathcal{H}}}{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}\;\delta F\right\rangle\;=\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\bf J}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\sum_{{\bf m},k}{\bf m}\;{\rm Im}\left\langle\delta\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{*}\frac{}{}\delta\widetilde{F}\right\rangle\right)

(5.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\bf J}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left[{\rm Im}\left(\sum_{{\bf m},k}\frac{-\,{\bf m}{\bf m}\;|\delta\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}|^{2}}{\omega_{k}-{\bf m}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\Omega$}}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial\bf J}\right]\;\equiv\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\bf J}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\sf D$}_{\rm QL}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial\bf J}\right),

where
\langle\;\rangle
includes orbital-angle averaging and wave time-scale averaging, and
the canonical quasilinear diffusion tensor

\mbox{\boldmath$\sf D$}_{\rm QL}\;\equiv\;\sum_{{\bf m},k}{\bf m}{\bf m}\left[\pi\frac{}{}\delta(\omega_{k}-{\bf m}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\Omega$})\right]\;|\delta\widetilde{H}|^{2}

(5.5)

is expressed in terms of a dyadic Fourier tensor
{\bf m}{\bf m}
, a wave-particle resonance condition obtained from the Plemelj formula

{\rm Im}\left(\frac{-\,1}{\omega_{k}-{\bf m}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\Omega$}}\right)\;=\;{\rm Re}\left(\frac{i}{\omega_{k}-{\bf m}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\Omega$}}\right)\;=\;\pi\;\delta(\omega_{k}-{\bf m}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\Omega$}),

and the magnitude squared of the perturbed Hamiltonian Fourier component

\delta\widetilde{H}({\bf J})
, which is an explicit function of the action coordinates
{\bf J}
and the perturbation fields (see Eq. (63) of Ref.
Brizard and Chan 2004
, for example). We note that the perturbed Hamiltonian
\delta\widetilde{H}({\bf J})
will, therefore, include terms that contain a product of an adiabatic action coordinate (such as the gyro action
{\sf J}_{\rm g}
) and a wave perturbation factor (such as
\delta B/B_{0}\sim\epsilon_{\delta}
). This means that an expansion of an adiabatic action coordinate (e.g.,
{\sf J}_{\rm g}={\sf J}_{\rm g}^{(0)}+\epsilon_{B}\,{\sf J}_{\rm g}^{(1)}+\cdots
) in the factor
|\delta\widetilde{H}|^{2}
in Eq. (
5.5
) results in a leading term of order
\epsilon_{\delta}^{2}
, followed by negligible terms of order
\epsilon_{B}\,\epsilon_{\delta}^{2}\ll\epsilon_{\delta}^{2}
. Hence, only a low-order expansion (in
\epsilon_{B}
) of the adiabatic action coordinates
{\bf J}\simeq{\bf J}_{0}
is needed in an explicit evaluation of Eq. (
5.5
). In addition, we note that the form (
5.4
), with Eq. (
5.5
), guarantees that the Vlasov entropy
S_{0}=-\,\int F_{0}\;\ln F_{0}\;d^{3}J

\frac{dS_{0}}{dt}\;=\;-\;\epsilon^{2}\int\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial\tau}\;(\ln F_{0}+1)\;d^{3}J\;=\;\epsilon^{2}\sum_{{\bf m},k}\int F_{0}\left({\bf m}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial\ln F_{0}}{\partial\bf J}\right)^{2}\pi\,\delta(\omega_{k}-{\bf m}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\Omega$})\;|\delta\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}|^{2}\;d^{3}J\;>\;0

(5.6)

satisfies the H Theorem. Lastly, we note that collisional transport in a magnetized plasma can also be described in terms of drag and diffusion in action space
Bernstein and Molvig 1983
.

5.2
Local and bounce-averaged wave-particle resonances in quasilinear theory

The canonical action-angle formalism presented in Sec.
5.1
unfortunately makes use of the bounce action
{\sf J}_{\rm b}=\oint p_{\|}(s)\,ds
, which is a nonlocal quantity
Northrop 1963
, while the drift action
{\sf J}_{\rm d}\equiv(q/2\pi c)\,\oint\psi\,d\varphi=q\psi/c
is a local coordinate in an axisymmetric magnetic field
{\bf B}=\nabla\psi\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla\varphi
, where the drift action is canonically conjugate to the toroidal angle
\varphi
. In our previous work
Brizard and Chan 2001
;
Brizard and Chan 2004
, we replaced the bounce action with the guiding-center kinetic energy
{\mathcal{E}}
in order to obtain a local quasilinear diffusion equation in three-dimensional
{\sf J}^{i}=({\sf J}_{\rm b},{\mathcal{E}},{\sf J}_{\rm d})
guiding-center invariant space:

\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial\tau}\;=\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\sf J$}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\sf D$}_{\rm QL}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\sf J$}}\right)\;=\;\frac{1}{\tau_{\rm b}}\frac{\partial}{\partial{\sf J}^{i}}\left(\tau_{\rm b}\;D_{\rm QL}^{ij}\;\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial{\sf J}^{j}}\right),

(5.7)

where the bounce period
\tau_{\rm b}\equiv\oint ds/v_{\|}
is the Jacobian. In addition, the
3\times 3
quasilinear diffusion tensor

\mbox{\boldmath$\sf D$}_{\rm QL}\;=\;\sum_{\ell,k,m}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\ell^{2}&\ell\,\omega_{k}&\ell\,m\\
\omega_{k}\ell&\omega_{k}^{2}&\omega_{k}m\\
m\ell&m\omega_{k}&m^{2}\end{array}\right)\Gamma_{\ell km}

(5.8)

is defined in terms of the Fourier indices
\ell
(associated with the gyroangle
\zeta
) and
m
(associated with the toroidal angle
\varphi
) and the wave frequency
\omega_{k}
, while the scalar
\Gamma_{\ell km}
was shown in Ref.
Brizard and Chan 2004
to include the bounce-averaged wave-particle resonance condition

\omega_{k}\;=\;\ell\,\langle\omega_{\rm c}\rangle_{\rm b}\;+\;n\,\omega_{\rm b}\;+\;m\,\langle\omega_{\rm d}\rangle_{\rm b},

(5.9)

where
\langle\omega_{\rm c}\rangle_{\rm b}=(q/Mc)\,\langle B\rangle_{\rm b}
and
\langle\omega_{\rm d}\rangle_{\rm b}
are the bounce-averaged cyclotron and drift frequencies, respectively, and
\omega_{\rm b}=2\pi/\tau_{\rm b}
is the bounce frequency. Here, the bounce-average operation is defined as

\langle\;\cdots\;\rangle_{\rm b}\;\equiv\;\frac{1}{\tau_{\rm b}}\;\sum_{\sigma}\;\int_{s_{L}}^{s_{U}}\;\frac{ds}{|v_{\|}|}\;(\cdots),

(5.10)

where
\sigma\equiv v_{\|}/|v_{\|}|
denotes the sign of the parallel guiding-center velocity, and the points
s_{L,U}(\mbox{\boldmath$\sf J$})
along a magnetic field line are the bounce (turning) points where
v_{\|}
changes sign (for simplicity, we assume all particles are magnetically trapped). In this Section, we present a brief derivation of the quasilinear diffusion equation (
5.7
), with the
3\times 3
quasilinear diffusion tensor (
5.8
) and the wave-particle resonance condition (
5.9
), based on our previous work
Brizard and Chan 2004
, which is presented here in the non-relativistic limit.

We begin with the linear guiding-center Vlasov equation in guiding-center phase space
(s,\varphi,\zeta;\mbox{\boldmath$\sf J$})
:

\frac{d_{0}\delta F}{dt}\;=\;\frac{\partial\delta F}{\partial t}\;+\;\{\delta F,\;{\mathcal{E}}\}_{\rm gc}\;=\;-\;\left\{F_{0},\;\delta H\right\}_{\rm gc},

(5.11)

where the perturbed Hamiltonian is a function of the guiding-center invariants
({\sf J}_{\rm b},{\mathcal{E}},{\sf J}_{\rm d})
as well as the angle-like coordinates
(s,\varphi,\zeta)
. The unperturbed guiding-center Poisson bracket, on the other hand, is

\displaystyle\left\{F,\frac{}{}G\right\}_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial F}{\partial\zeta}\;\frac{\partial G}{\partial{\sf J}_{\rm g}}\;-\;\frac{\partial F}{\partial{\sf J}_{\rm g}}\;\frac{\partial G}{\partial\zeta}\;+\;\frac{\partial F}{\partial\varphi}\;\frac{\partial G}{\partial{\sf J}_{\rm d}}\;-\;\frac{\partial F}{\partial{\sf J}_{\rm d}}\;\frac{\partial G}{\partial\varphi}\;+\;\left(\frac{d_{0}F}{dt}-\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}\right)\frac{\partial G}{\partial\mathcal{E}}\;-\;\frac{\partial F}{\partial\mathcal{E}}\left(\frac{d_{0}G}{dt}-\frac{\partial G}{\partial t}\right),

(5.12)

and
d_{0}/dt=\partial/\partial t+v_{\|}\,\partial/\partial s+\omega_{\rm d}\,\partial/\partial\varphi+\omega_{\rm c}\,\partial/\partial\zeta
denotes the unperturbed Vlasov operator (
s
denotes the local spatial coordinate along an unperturbed magnetic-field line). Since the right side of Eq. (
5.11
) is

-\;\left\{F_{0},\;\delta H\right\}_{\rm gc}\;=\;\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial{\sf J}_{\rm g}}\;\frac{\partial\delta H}{\partial\zeta}\;+\;\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial{\sf J}_{\rm d}}\;\frac{\partial\delta H}{\partial\varphi}\;+\;\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial\mathcal{E}}\left(\frac{d_{0}\delta H}{dt}-\frac{\partial\delta H}{\partial t}\right),

(5.13)

we can introduce the non-adiabatic decomposition
Chen and Tsai 1983

\delta F\;\equiv\;\delta H\;\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial\mathcal{E}}\;+\;\delta G,

(5.14)

where the non-adiabatic contribution
\delta G
satisfies the perturbed non-adiabatic Vlasov equation

\frac{d_{0}\delta G}{dt}\;=\;\left(\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial{\sf J}_{\rm g}}\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\zeta}\;+\;\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial{\sf J}_{\rm d}}\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\varphi}\;-\;\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial\mathcal{E}}\;\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)\delta H\;\equiv\;\widehat{\mathcal{F}}\,\delta H.

(5.15)

Next, since the background plasma is time independent and axisymmetric, and the unperturbed guiding-center Vlasov distribution is independent of the gyroangle, we perform Fourier transforms in
(\varphi,\zeta,t)
so that Eq. (
5.15
) becomes

\left[v_{\|}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\;-\;i\,\left(\omega_{k}\;-\frac{}{}\ell\,\omega_{\rm c}\;-\;m\,\omega_{\rm d}\right)\right]\delta\widetilde{G}(s,\sigma)\;\equiv\;\widehat{\mathcal{L}}\,\delta\widetilde{G}(s,\sigma)\;=\;i\,{\mathcal{F}}\,\delta\widetilde{H}(s,\sigma),

(5.16)

where the amplitudes
(\delta\widetilde{G},\delta\widetilde{H})
depend on the spatial parallel coordinate
s
and the sign
\sigma=v_{\|}/|v_{\|}|=\pm 1
, as well as the invariants
\sf J
, while the operator
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
becomes
i\,{\mathcal{F}}
, with

{\mathcal{F}}\;\equiv\;\omega_{k}\;\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial\mathcal{E}}\;+\;\ell\;\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial{\sf J}_{\rm g}}\;+\;m\;\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial{\sf J}_{\rm d}}.

(5.17)

In order to remove the dependence of the perturbed Hamiltonian
\delta\widetilde{H}
on
\sigma
(which appears through the combination
v_{\|}\delta\widetilde{A}_{\|}
), we follow our previous work
Brizard and Chan 2004
and introduce the gauge
\delta\widetilde{A}_{\|}\equiv\partial\delta\widetilde{\alpha}/\partial s
and the transformation
(\delta\widetilde{G},\delta\widetilde{H})\rightarrow(\delta\widetilde{G}^{\prime},\delta\widetilde{K})
, where
\delta\widetilde{G}^{\prime}=\delta\widetilde{G}+i\,(q/c){\mathcal{F}}\,\delta\widetilde{\alpha}
and
\delta\widetilde{K}=\delta\widetilde{H}+(q/c)\,\widehat{\mathcal{L}}\,\delta\widetilde{\alpha}
, so that Eq. (
5.16
) becomes
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}\,\delta\widetilde{G}^{\prime}(s,\sigma)=i\,{\mathcal{F}}\,\delta\widetilde{K}(s)
.

In order to obtain an integral solution for
\delta\widetilde{G}^{\prime}
, we now introduce the integrating factor

\left[v_{\|}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\;-\;i\,\left(\omega_{k}\;-\frac{}{}\ell\,\omega_{\rm c}\;-\;m\,\omega_{\rm d}\right)\right]\delta\widetilde{G}^{\prime}(s,\sigma)\;\equiv\;e^{i\sigma\theta}v_{\|}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\left[e^{-i\sigma\theta}\frac{}{}\delta\widetilde{G}^{\prime}(s,\sigma)\right]\;=\;i\,{\mathcal{F}}\,\delta\widetilde{K}(s),

(5.18)

where

\theta(s)\;\equiv\;\int_{s_{L}}^{s}\left(\omega_{k}\;-\frac{}{}\ell\,\omega_{\rm c}(s^{\prime})\;-\;m\,\omega_{\rm d}(s^{\prime})\right)\;\frac{ds^{\prime}}{|v_{\|}|}

(5.19)

is defined in terms of the lower (L) turning point
s_{L}(\mbox{\boldmath$\sf J$})
. The solution of Eq. (
5.18
) is, therefore, expressed as

\delta\widetilde{G}^{\prime}(s,\sigma)\;=\;\delta\overline{G}^{\prime}\;e^{i\sigma\theta}\;+\;i\,\sigma\;e^{i\sigma\theta}\left(\int_{s_{L}}^{s}\delta\widetilde{K}(s^{\prime})\;e^{-i\sigma\theta(s^{\prime})}\frac{ds^{\prime}}{|v_{\|}|}\right){\mathcal{F}}

(5.20)

where the constant amplitude
\delta\overline{G}^{\prime}
is determined from the matching conditions
\delta\widetilde{G}^{\prime}(s_{L},+1)=\delta\widetilde{G}^{\prime}(s_{L},-1)
and
\delta\widetilde{G}^{\prime}(s_{U},+1)=\delta\widetilde{G}^{\prime}(s_{U},-1)
at the two turning points. At the lower turning point, the matching condition implies that
\delta\overline{G}^{\prime}
is independent of
\sigma
. The matching condition at the upper turning point, on the other hand, is expressed as

e^{i\Theta}\;\delta\overline{G}^{\prime}\;+\;\frac{i\tau_{\rm b}}{2}\;\left\langle\delta\widetilde{K}\frac{}{}e^{-i\theta}\right\rangle_{\rm b}\,e^{i\Theta}\;{\mathcal{F}}\;=\;e^{-i\Theta}\;\delta\overline{G}^{\prime}\;-\;\frac{i\tau_{\rm b}}{2}\;\left\langle\delta\widetilde{K}\frac{}{}e^{i\theta}\right\rangle_{\rm b}\,\,e^{-i\Theta}\;{\mathcal{F}},

which yields

\delta\overline{G}^{\prime}\;=\;-\;\frac{\tau_{\rm b}}{2}\left(\cot{\Theta}\;\left\langle\delta\widetilde{K}\frac{}{}\cos\theta\right\rangle_{\rm b}\;+\frac{}{}\left\langle\delta\widetilde{K}\frac{}{}\sin\theta\right\rangle_{\rm b}\right)\;{\mathcal{F}},

(5.21)

where

\Theta\;\equiv\;\theta(s_{U})\;=\;\frac{\tau_{\rm b}}{2}\left(\omega_{k}\;-\;\ell\;\langle\omega_{\rm c}\rangle_{\rm b}\;-\frac{}{}m\;\langle\omega_{\rm d}\rangle_{\rm b}\right).

(5.22)

We note that
\cot\Theta
in Eq. (
5.21
) has singularities at
n\pi
, which immediately leads to the resonance condition (
5.9
).

Now that the solution
\delta\widetilde{G}^{\prime}
has been determined, we can proceed with the derivation of the quasilinear diffusion equation, which has been shown by Brizard and Chan
Brizard and Chan 2004
to be expressed as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau_{\rm b}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathcal{E}}\left[\tau_{\rm b}\left(\sum_{\ell,k,m}\omega_{k}\,\Gamma_{\ell km}\;{\mathcal{F}}\right)\right]+\frac{1}{\tau_{\rm b}}\frac{\partial}{\partial{\sf J}_{\rm g}}\left[\tau_{\rm b}\left(\sum_{\ell,k,m}\ell\,\Gamma_{\ell km}\;{\mathcal{F}}\right)\right]+\frac{1}{\tau_{\rm b}}\frac{\partial}{\partial{\sf J}_{\rm d}}\left[\tau_{\rm b}\left(\sum_{\ell,k,m}m\,\Gamma_{\ell km}\;{\mathcal{F}}\right)\right]

(5.23)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau_{\rm b}}\frac{\partial}{\partial{\sf J}^{i}}\left(\tau_{\rm b}\;D_{\rm QL}^{ij}\;\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial{\sf J}^{j}}\right),

which requires us to evaluate
\Gamma_{\ell km}\equiv{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}{\rm Im}\langle\delta\widetilde{H}^{*}\delta\widetilde{G}\rangle_{\rm b}={\mathcal{F}}^{-1}{\rm Im}\langle\delta\widetilde{K}^{*}\delta\widetilde{G}^{\prime}\rangle_{\rm b}
, which is found to be expressed as

\Gamma_{\ell km}\;=\;\frac{\tau_{\rm b}}{2}\frac{}{}{\rm Im}(-\,\cot\Theta)\;\left|\left\langle\delta\widetilde{K}\frac{}{}\cos\theta\right\rangle_{\rm b}\right|^{2},

(5.24)

where, using the Plemelj formula with the identity
\cot z=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}(z-n\pi)^{-1}
, we finally obtain

\Gamma_{\ell km}\;=\;\left|\left\langle\delta\widetilde{K}\frac{}{}\cos\theta\right\rangle_{\rm b}\right|^{2}\;\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\pi\;\delta\left(\omega_{k}\;-\frac{}{}\ell\,\langle\omega_{\rm c}\rangle_{\rm b}\;-\;n\,\omega_{\rm b}\;-\;m\,\langle\omega_{\rm d}\rangle_{\rm b}\right).

(5.25)

This expression completes the derivation of the quasilinear diffusion tensor (
5.8
) and the perturbed Hamiltonian
\delta\widetilde{K}
is fully defined in Ref.
Brizard and Chan 2004
. We note that, in the limit of low-frequencies electromagnetic fluctuations, we also recover our previous work
Brizard and Chan 2001
from Eq. (
5.8
).

We now make a few remarks concerning the bounce-averaged wave-particle resonance condition (
5.9
). First, in the case of a uniform magnetized plasma (with the drift frequency
\omega_{\rm d}\equiv 0
), we substitute the eikonal representations
\delta\widetilde{G}=\delta\overline{G}\,\exp(ik_{\|}s)
and
\delta\widetilde{H}=\delta\overline{H}\,\exp(ik_{\|}s)
in Eq. (
5.16
) and we recover the uniform quasilinear diffusion equation (
4.17
). Second, the bounce-averaged wave-particle resonance condition (
5.9
) assumes that the waves are coherent on the bounce-time scale, which is not realistic for high-frequency (VLF), short-wavelength whistler waves
Stenzel 1999
;
Allanson et al. 2021
. We recover a local wave-particle resonance condition by introducing the bounce-angle coordinate
\xi(s)

Brizard 2000
, which is defined by the equation
d\xi/ds=\omega_{\rm b}/v_{\|}
, so that
v_{\|}\,\partial/\partial s
in Eq. (
5.16
) is replaced with
\omega_{\rm b}\,\partial/\partial\xi
. Next, by introducing the bounce-angle Fourier series
\delta\widetilde{G}=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\delta\overline{G}\,\exp(in\xi)
and
\delta\widetilde{H}=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\delta\overline{H}\,\exp(in\xi)
in Eq. (
5.16
), the integral phase (
5.19
) is replaced by the new integral phase

\sigma\,\chi(s)\;=\;\sigma\,\theta(s)\;-\;n\,\xi(s)\;=\;\sigma\int_{s_{L}}^{s}\left(\omega_{k}\;-\frac{}{}\ell\,\omega_{c}(s^{\prime})\;-\;m\,\omega_{\rm d}(s^{\prime})\;-\;n\,\omega_{\rm b}\right)\frac{ds^{\prime}}{|v_{\|}|}.

(5.26)

If we now evaluate this integral by stationary-phase methods
Stix 1992
, the dominant contribution comes from points
s_{0}
along a magnetic-field line where

0\;=\;\chi^{\prime}(s_{0})\;=\;|v_{\|}(s_{0})|^{-1}\left(\omega_{k}\;-\frac{}{}\ell\,\omega_{c}(s_{0})\;-\;m\,\omega_{\rm d}(s_{0})\;-\;n\,\omega_{\rm b}\right),

(5.27)

which yields the local wave-particle resonance condition, provided
v_{\|}(s_{0})\neq 0
(i.e., the local resonance does not occur at a turning point).

6
Summary

In the present paper, we have established a direct connection between the standard reference of quasilinear theory for a uniform magnetized plasma by Kennel and Engelmann
Kennel and Engelmann 1966
and its Hamiltonian formulation in guiding-center phase space. We have also shown that the transition to a quasilinear theory for a nonuniform magnetized plasma is greatly facilitated within a Hamiltonian formulation. The main features of a Hamiltonian formulation of quasilinear theory is that the quasilinear diffusion tensor has a simple modular dyadic form in which a matrix of Fourier indices is multiplied by a single quasilinear scalar potential, which includes the resonant wave-particle delta function. This simple modular is observed in the case of a uniform magnetized plasma, as seen in Eq. (
4.18
), as well as in the case of a nonuniform magnetized plasma, as seen in Eq. (
5.8
). In particular, we note that the quasilinear diffusion tensor (
5.8
) naturally incorporates quasilinear radial diffusion as well as its synergistic connections to diffusion in two-dimensional invariant velocity space. These features are easily extended to the quasilinear diffusion of relativistic charged particles that are magnetically confined by nonuniform magnetic fields.

\displaystyle\delta\widetilde{V}_{\|\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q}{M\Omega}\left[\delta\widetilde{E}_{\|}\;J_{\ell}\;-\;v_{\bot}\widehat{\sf z}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\left(\frac{\bf k}{\omega}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\delta\widetilde{\bf E}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mathbb{J}_{\bot\ell}\right]\;=\;\frac{q}{M\Omega}\left[\delta\widetilde{E}_{\|}\left(J_{\ell}\;-\;\frac{v_{\bot}{\bf k}}{\omega}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mathbb{J}_{\bot\ell}\right)\;+\;\frac{k_{\|}v_{\bot}}{\omega}\;\delta\widetilde{\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mathbb{J}_{\bot\ell}\right]

(A.12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q}{M\Omega}\left[\delta\widetilde{E}_{\|}\left(1\;-\;\frac{\ell\Omega}{\omega}\right)\;J_{\ell}\;+\;\frac{k_{\|}v_{\bot}}{\omega}\;\delta\widetilde{\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mathbb{J}_{\bot\ell}\right],

which is identical to Eq. (
A.11
), where we used the identity

(v_{\bot}{\bf k}/\omega)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mathbb{J}_{\bot\ell}=(\ell\Omega/\omega)\,J_{\ell}
.

Next,
\delta\widetilde{V}_{\bot\ell}
is given by Kennel and Engelmann as

\delta\widetilde{V}_{\bot\ell}\;=\;\frac{q\delta\widetilde{\bf E}}{M\Omega}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\frac{\widehat{\sf K}}{\sqrt{2}}\;J_{\ell+1}+\frac{\widehat{\sf K}^{*}}{\sqrt{2}}\;J_{\ell-1}\right)\left(1-\frac{k_{\|}v_{\|}}{\omega}\right)\;+\;\frac{q\delta\widetilde{E}_{\|}}{M\Omega}\frac{v_{\|}}{v_{\bot}}\;\left(\frac{\ell\Omega}{\omega}\right)J_{\ell}

(A.13)

If we insert Faraday’s Law into Eq. (
2.17
), on the other hand, we obtain

\displaystyle\delta\widetilde{V}_{\bot\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q}{M\Omega}\left[\delta\widetilde{\bf E}\;+\;v_{\|}\widehat{\sf z}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\left(\frac{\bf k}{\omega}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\delta\widetilde{\bf E}\right)\right]\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mathbb{J}_{\bot\ell}\;=\;\frac{q}{M\Omega}\left[\delta\widetilde{E}_{\|}\;\frac{v_{\|}{\bf k}}{\omega}\;+\;\left(1-\frac{k_{\|}v_{\|}}{\omega}\right)\;\delta\widetilde{\bf E}\right]\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mathbb{J}_{\bot\ell},

(A.14)

which is identical to Eq. (
A.13
), where we used the identity

(v_{\bot}{\bf k}/\omega)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mathbb{J}_{\bot\ell}=(\ell\Omega/\omega)\,J_{\ell}
.

A.3
Equations (2.26)-(2.27) of Kennel and Engelmann

Equation (2.26) of Kennel and Engelmann can be expressed as
{\sf D}=\sum_{\ell}\;(-i\,\Delta_{\ell})\;\widetilde{\bf v}_{\ell}^{*}\,\widetilde{\bf v}_{\ell}
, where the perturbed velocity Fourier component
\widetilde{\bf v}_{\ell}\equiv q\,\widetilde{\bf a}_{\ell}/(M\Omega)
is given by Eq. (2.27) as

\displaystyle\widetilde{\bf v}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q\,\delta\widetilde{\bf E}}{M\Omega}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\frac{\widehat{\sf K}}{\sqrt{2}}\;J_{\ell+1}\;+\;\frac{\widehat{\sf K}^{*}}{\sqrt{2}}\;J_{\ell+1}\right)\left[\left(1-\frac{k_{\|}v_{\|}}{\omega}\right)\;\widehat{\bot}\;+\;\frac{k_{\|}v_{\bot}}{\omega}\;\widehat{\sf z}\right]\;+\;\frac{q\,\delta\widetilde{E}_{\|}}{M\Omega}\,J_{\ell}\;\left[\left(1-\frac{\ell\Omega}{\omega}\right)\;\widehat{\sf z}\;+\;\frac{v_{\|}}{v_{\bot}}\left(\frac{\ell\Omega}{\omega}\right)\;\widehat{\bot}\right]

(A.15)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q}{M\Omega}\left[\delta\widetilde{E}_{\|}\,J_{\ell}\;\left(1-\frac{\ell\Omega}{\omega}\right)\;+\;\frac{k_{\|}v_{\bot}}{\omega}\;\delta\widetilde{\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mathbb{J}_{\bot\ell}\right]\;\widehat{\sf z}\;+\;\frac{q}{M\Omega}\left[\frac{v_{\|}}{v_{\bot}}\left(\frac{\ell\Omega}{\omega}\right)\;\delta\widetilde{E}_{\|}\;J_{\ell}\;+\;\delta\widetilde{\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mathbb{J}_{\bot\ell}\left(1-\frac{k_{\|}v_{\|}}{\omega}\right)\right]\widehat{\bot}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\delta\widetilde{V}_{\|\ell}\;\widehat{\sf z}\;+\;\delta\widetilde{V}_{\bot\ell}\;\widehat{\bot},

where the Fourier components
\delta\widetilde{V}_{\|\ell}
and
\delta\widetilde{V}_{\bot\ell}
are given by Eqs. (
A.11
) and (
A.13
), respectively.

A.4
Relativistic Extension of Kennel and Engelmann

Lerche
Lerche 1968
extended the work of Kennel and Engelmann
Kennel and Engelmann 1966
by deriving a relativistic quasilinear theory for a uniform magnetized plasma, for which the background Vlasov distribution
f_{0}({\bf p};\tau)
is an arbitrary function of the relativistic momentum
{\bf p}=m\gamma\,{\bf v}
, where
\gamma=(1-|{\bf v}|^{2}/c^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}=(1+|{\bf p}/(mc)|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
. In Eq. (11) of Ref.
Lerche 1968
, the perturbed Vlasov distribution (
A.10
) is replaced with the relativistic expression

\delta\widetilde{f}\;=\;\sum_{\ell,m}i\Delta_{\ell}\;J_{m}(\lambda)\;e^{i(m-\ell)(\phi-\psi)}\left(\delta\widetilde{P}_{\|\ell}\;\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial p_{\|}}\;+\;\delta\widetilde{P}_{\bot\ell}\;\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial p_{\bot}}\right),

(A.16)

where
\Delta_{\ell}=\Omega/[(k_{\|}v_{\|}-\omega)+\ell\Omega/\gamma]
, and the relativistic Fourier components
\delta\widetilde{P}_{\|\ell}
and
\delta\widetilde{P}_{\bot\ell}
are

\displaystyle\delta\widetilde{P}_{\|\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q}{\Omega}\left[\delta\widetilde{E}_{\|}\left(1\;-\;\frac{\ell\Omega}{\gamma\,\omega}\right)\;J_{\ell}(\lambda)\;+\;\frac{k_{\|}v_{\bot}}{\omega}\;\delta\widetilde{\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mathbb{J}_{\bot\ell}(\lambda)\right],

(A.17)

\displaystyle\delta\widetilde{P}_{\bot\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q}{\Omega}\left[\delta\widetilde{E}_{\|}\;\frac{v_{\|}{\bf k}}{\omega}\;+\;\left(1-\frac{k_{\|}v_{\|}}{\omega}\right)\;\delta\widetilde{\bf E}\right]\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mathbb{J}_{\bot\ell}(\lambda),

(A.18)

where the argument of the Bessel functions in Eqs. (
A.16
)-(
A.18
) is
\lambda=k_{\bot}p_{\bot}/(M\Omega)=\gamma\,k_{\bot}v_{\bot}/\Omega
, so that

(v_{\bot}{\bf k}/\omega)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mathbb{J}_{\bot\ell}=(\ell\Omega/\gamma\omega)\,J_{\ell}
.

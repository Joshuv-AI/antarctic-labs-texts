On ruin probabilities with risky investments in a stock with stochastic volatility
Anastasiya Ellanskaya, Yuri Kabanov

Abstract
We investigate the asymptotic of ruin probabilities when the company combines the life- and non-life insurance businesses and invests its reserve into a risky asset with stochastic volatility and drift driven by a two-state Markov process. Using the technique of the implicit renewal theory we obtain the rate of convergence to zero of the ruin probabilities.

Introduction

In the classical collective risk theory initiated by Filip Lundberg in 1903 and further developed by Harald Cramér in the thirties it was usually assumed that an insurance company does not invest its reserve. This assumption leads to a good news: if claims
are not heavy-tailed, then the ruin probabilities are exponentially decreasing when the
initial capital tends to infinity.
For quite a long period of time studies of ruin probabilities did not suppose investments in spite that such an assumption is clearly non realistic: in
the modern world insurance companies operate in a financial environment and invests their reserves into risky assets. Studies of mathematical models with risky investments started in the nineties. To the date there is an ample literature dedicated to the asymptotic of ruin probabilities under various assumptions on the price processes on the business activity.
The fundamental discovery is that the risky investments change radically the asymptotic behavior of the ruin probabilities. For example,
in the papers Frolova, Kabanov and Pergamenshchikov
FrKP
, Kabanov and Pergamenshchikov
KP
, and Kabanov and Pukhlyakov
KPukh
there were studied Lundberg type models with investments having, respectively, the downward jumps (non-life insurance), downward jumps (annuity model), and two-side jumps.
In the cited paper it was supposed that an insurance company invests in a asset whose price process
S=(S_{t})
is a geometric Brownian motion (gBm) given by the equality
dS_{t}/S_{t}=adt+\sigma W_{t}
.
It was shown that if the parameter
\beta:=2a/\sigma^{2}-1
is strictly positive then the ruin probability
has the asymptotic behavior
Cu^{-\beta}
as the initial capital
u
tends to infinity. For the large volatility, when
\beta\leq 0
, the ruin is imminent.

A much more general setting, that of the generalized Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process, covering the case of investments with the price following a geometric Lévy process, was introduced by Paulsen in 1993,
Paul-93
, and studied, using the techniques of implicit renewal theory, in the series of his papers,
Paul-98
;
Paul-02
;
PG-97
. For the recent results
in this direction and further references see
KP2020
.

To our knowledge, the ruin problem with risky investments in the asset with price with
stochastic volatility was never studied.
In the present note we obtain the asymptotic of ruin probabilities for the Lundberg–Cramér type model with investments and two-side jumps. We assume that the price process
is a conditional geometric Brownian motion with volatility and drift modulated by a telegraph signal, that is, by a
two-state Markov process, see the paper by Di Masi, Kabanov, and Runggaldier
DKR
.
Such models are usually referred to as the hidden Markov models or models with regime switching.
In economics two-state Markov processes are used to model business cycles and we believe that it is relevant to the situation with long term investments typical in insurance.

As in the paper
KP2020
, in our study we use the approach based on the recent progress in the implicit
renewal theory.

For the classical models with a positive average trend and

For the exponentially distributed claims the ruin

For the models with exponentially distributed jumps with the investment in a risky asset with price following a geometric Brownian motion with the drift coefficient

(6)

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{10}}{\lambda^{10}+q(a-\sigma_{1}^{2}/2)-q^{2}\sigma_{1}^{2}/2}=\frac{\lambda^{10}}{\lambda^{10}+(1/2)\sigma_{1}^{2}q(\beta_{1}-q)}

.

(ii)
Rewriting (
12
) in the form

Y_{\tau_{2n}}=Q_{1}+M_{1}(Q_{2}+M_{2}Q_{3}+...+M_{2}...M_{n-1}Q_{n})

and observing that the sequence of random variables in the parentheses converges
as to a random variable with same law as
Y_{\infty}
and independent on
(Q_{1},M_{1})
we get the needed assertion.

(iii)
In virtue of
(ii)
it is sufficient to check that the set

\{Q_{1}\geq N,\ M_{1}\leq 1/N\}
is non-null whatever is
N\geq 0
. We consider several
cases.

1)

c<0
. On the set
\{T_{1}>\tau_{2}\}
we have

Q_{1}=-c\int_{0}^{\tau_{2}}e^{-V_{s}}ds
. For every
t>0
the set

B_{N}(t):=\Big\{-c\int_{0}^{t}e^{-V_{s}}ds\geq N,\ e^{-V_{t}}\leq 1/N\Big\}

is non-null and so is
B_{N}(\tau_{2})
.
Note that

\Gamma_{N}:=\{Q_{1}\geq N,\ M_{1}\leq 1/N,\ T_{1}>\tau_{2}\}=B_{N}(\tau_{2})\cap\{T_{1}>\tau_{2}\}.

Thus,

{\bf P}(\Gamma_{N}):={\bf E}I_{B_{N}(\tau_{2})}I_{\{T_{1}>\tau_{2}\}}={\bf E}I_{B_{N}(\tau_{2})}e^{-(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})\tau_{2}}>0.

2)

c\geq 0
. Since
P
is not increasing,
\Pi(]-\infty,0[)>0
. Let
r_{N}:=\delta+\ln N
. We consider the set

\Delta_{N}:=\{-\delta+\gamma<V_{s}<\delta+\gamma s,\ \forall\,s\leq r_{N}+1\}\cap\{r_{N}\leq\tau_{2}\leq r_{N}+1\}

The set
\Delta_{N}
is non-null and on this set
e^{-V_{\tau_{2}}}\leq e^{\delta-r_{N}}\leq 1/N
and

c\int_{0}^{\tau_{2}}e^{-V_{s}}ds\geq\int_{0}^{\tau_{2}}e^{-V_{s}}ds

Note that the set

\Big\{e^{-V}*x_{\{x<0\}}\pi_{r_{N}}\geq C_{N}+N,\ x_{\{x>0\}}*\pi_{\tau_{2}}=0\Big\}

is non-null and so non-null is its intersection with
\Delta_{N}
. But this intersection is a subset of the set
\{Q_{1}\geq N,\ M_{1}\leq 1/N\}
.
\Box

Lemma 4

For all
u>0

\bar{G}_{i}(u)\leq\,\Psi_{i}(u)=\frac{\bar{G}_{i}(u)}{{\bf E}\big(\bar{G}_{\theta_{\tau^{u,i}}}(0)|\tau^{u,i}<\infty\big)}\leq\frac{\bar{G}_{i}(u)}{\bar{G}_{0}(0)\wedge\bar{G}_{1}(0)},

(14)

where
\bar{G}_{i}(u):={\bf P}(Y_{\infty}^{i}>u)
.

Proof.

Let
\tau
be an arbitrary stopping time with respect to the filtration
({\cal F}^{P,R,\theta}_{t})
.
As the finite limit
Y^{i}_{\infty}
exists, the random variable

Y_{\tau,\infty}^{i}:=\begin{cases}-\lim_{N\to\infty}\int_{]\tau,\tau+N]}\,e^{-(V_{s}-V_{\tau})}dP_{s},&\tau<\infty,\\
0,&\tau=\infty,\end{cases}

is well defined. On the set
\{\tau<\infty\}

Y_{\tau,\infty}^{i}=e^{V_{\tau}}(Y^{i}_{\infty}-Y^{i}_{\tau})=X_{\tau}^{u}+e^{V_{\tau}}(Y^{i}_{\infty}-u).

(15)

Let
\xi
be a
{\cal F}_{\tau}^{P,R,\theta}
-measurable random variable. Note that the conditional distribution of
Y_{\tau,\infty}^{i}
given
(\tau,\xi,\theta_{\tau})=(t,x,j)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}\times\{0,1\}
is the same as the distribution of
Y_{\infty}^{j}
. It follows that

{\bf P}\big(Y_{\tau,\infty}^{i}>\xi,\ \tau<\infty,\ \theta_{\tau}=j\big)={\bf E}\bar{G}_{j}(\xi)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\tau<\infty,\;\theta_{\tau}=j\}}.

Thus, if
{\bf P}(\tau<\infty)>0
, then

{\bf P}\big(Y_{\tau,\infty}^{i}>\xi,\ \tau<\infty\big)={\bf E}\big(\bar{G}_{\theta_{\tau}}(\xi)\,|\,\tau<\infty\big)\,{\bf P}\big(\tau<\infty\big)\,.

Noting that
\Psi_{i}(u):={\bf P}\big(\tau^{u,i}<\infty\big)\geq{\bf P}\big(Y^{i}_{\infty}>u\big)=\bar{G}_{i}(u)>0
, we deduce from here using (
15
) that

\displaystyle\bar{G}_{i}(u)

\displaystyle={\bf P}\big(Y^{i}_{\infty}>u,\ \tau^{u,i}<\infty\big)={\bf P}\big(Y_{\tau^{u,i},\infty}^{i}>X_{\tau^{u,i}}^{u,i},\ \tau^{u,i}<\infty\big)

\displaystyle={\bf E}\big(\bar{G}_{\theta_{\tau^{u,i}}}(X_{\tau^{u,i}}^{u,i})\,|\,\tau^{u,i}<\infty\big)\,{\bf P}\big(\tau^{u,i}<\infty\big)

implying the equality in (
14
). Also,

{\bf E}\big(\bar{G}_{\theta_{\tau^{u,i}}}(0)|\tau^{u,i}<\infty\big)=\bar{G}_{0}(0){\bf P}\big(\theta_{\tau^{u,i}}=0|\tau^{u,i}<\infty\big)+\bar{G}_{1}(0){\bf P}\big(\theta_{\tau^{u,i}}=1|\tau^{u,i}<\infty\big).

The result follows since

X_{\tau^{u,i}}^{u,i}\leq 0
on
\{\tau^{u,i}<\infty\}
and, in the case with only upward jumps that is when

\Pi(]-\infty,0[)=0
, the process
X^{u,i}
crosses zero in a continuous way, i.e.
X_{\tau^{u,i}}^{u,i}=0
on this set.
\Box

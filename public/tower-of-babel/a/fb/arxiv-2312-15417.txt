Knot cobordism, torsion order and framed instanton homology
Sudipta Ghosh, Zhenkun Li

Abstract
. We construct cobordism maps for the minus version of instanton knot homology associated to a specially decorated knot cobordisms of arbitrary genus between two null-homologous knots in closed oriented 3 3 -manifolds. As an application of our construction, we recover an inequality between the torsion order of knots in instanton theory, which was originally established in Heegaard Floer theory by work of Juhász, Miller, and Zemke. We further use this inequality to compute the framed instanton Floer homology of any non-zero Dehn surgeries along an alternating knot of bridge index at most 3 3 .



1.
Introductions

In Heegaard Floer homology, link cobordism maps associated to decorated link cobordisms were constructed by Juhász
[
11
]
for hat version and by Zemke
[
26
]
for general version. The construction of link cobordism maps has led to many interesting topological applications, such as the stabilization distance between minimal-genus surfaces in
B^{4}
bounding knots in
S^{3}=\partial B^{3}
by Juhász and Zemke
[
13
]
and a bound on the bridge index of a knot from the torsion order of the knot by Juhász, Miller, and Zemke
[
10
]
. It is natural to ask whether similar constructions can be made in other branches of Floer theory. In instanton theory, the instanton knot homology
KHI
was constructed by Kronheimer and Mrowka
[
15
]
, which corresponds to the hat version of knot Floer homology. The minus version of instanton knot homology
KHI^{-}
was constructed for knots by the authors of the current paper in
[
18
]
and
[
7
]
, and was recently revisited by the first author and Zemke
[
9
]
. As for link cobordism maps, the construction for
KHI
follows from the work of the second author in
[
17
]
, while the construction for
KHI^{-}
was previously unknown.

Suppose we have a link cobordism
(W,\Sigma):(Y_{0},K_{0})\to(Y_{1},K_{1})
. In Zemke’s construction,
(W,\Sigma)
was equipped with some extra data called decorations and is decomposed into a few fundamental types: the birth, death, and saddle. Then the link cobordism map associated to the decorated cobordism is the composition of ones associated to the fundamental types. In this paper we develop a different construction. Instead of decomposing, we use the surface
\Sigma
and a special decoration
\mathcal{D}
on
\Sigma
to construct a map

\hat{\rho}:KHI^{-}(Y_{0},K_{0})\to KHI^{-}(Y_{0}\#Z_{2g},K_{1}\#B_{g}),

where
B_{g}\subset Z_{2g}
is the borromean knot as in Section
2.4
, with
g=g(\Sigma)
. Then the
4
-dimensional cobordism
W
induces a map

F^{-}_{W-N(\Sigma)}:KHI^{-}(Y_{0}\#Z_{2g},K_{0}\#B_{g})\to KHI^{-}(Y_{1},K_{1}).

Thus the composition gives rise to the desired cobordism map

F_{W,\Sigma_{g},\mathcal{D}}=F^{-}_{W-N(\Sigma)}\circ\hat{\rho}:KHI^{-}(Y_{0},K_{0})\to KHI^{-}(Y_{1},K_{1}).

Theorem 1.1
.

If
(W,\Sigma_{g},\mathcal{D}):(Y_{0},K_{0})\to(Y_{1},K_{1})
is a specially decorated knot cobordism, then the map
F_{W,\Sigma_{g},\mathcal{D}}
constructed as above is
\mathbb{C}[U]
-equivariant and is functorial under the composition of such cobordisms.

Using the same strategy we also extend our construction from knots to 2-component links
L=K\cup U
in a disconnected manifold where
K\subset Y
is an arbitrary knot and
U\subset Y^{\prime}
is the unknot. We prove that the knot cobordism maps and these special classes of link cobordism maps are functorial with respect to compositions.

Remark 1.2
.

In order to define the map
\hat{\rho}
and hence
F_{W,\Sigma_{g},\mathcal{D}}
, one needs to choose an element in
KHI^{-}(Z_{2g},B_{2g})
. This homology group has been computed explicitly, and in this paper, in order to establish our applications, we choose a special element in
KHI^{-}(Z_{2g},B_{2g})
and omit it from the data. However, there are many other elements that could potentially lead to different link cobordisms. So one could potentially choose different elements to define different link cobordism maps.

Remark 1.3
.

Due to the naturality issue (
c.f.

[
2
]
), all instanton Floer homology groups and maps between them in this paper are only well defined up to the multiplication of a non-zero element in
\mathbb{C}
.

Instanton Floer homology was constructed based on a set of partial differential equations and is known to be very hard to compute. In recent years, there have been several works towards computing the framed instanton Floer homology of various families of 3-manifolds, especially those coming from Dehn surgeries on knots. See, for example
[
19
,
4
,
5
,
1
,
20
,
22
]
. As an application of our construction for the cobordism maps for
KHI^{-}
, we include a large family of 3-manifolds into the known list.

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose
K\subset S^{3}
is an alternating knot with a of bridge index at most 3 and
r\in\mathbb{Q}
is non-zero. Then we have

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}I^{\sharp}(S^{3}_{r}(K))={\rm rk}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\widehat{HF}(S^{3}_{r}(K)).

Remark 1.5
.

Although we state the theorem as an identity between the dimensions in instanton Floer theory and Heegaard Floer theory, given an (alternating) diagram of any such knot, there is an algorithm to compute the precise dimension from the diagram. Also, it should be noted that 2-bridge knots are automatically alternating.

The proof of Theorem
1.4
relies on a recent progress in developing the surgery formula in instanton Floer homology by the second author and Ye. In particular, in
[
22
]
, we have the following.

Proposition 1.6
.

Suppose
K\subset S^{3}
is an alternating knot of torsion-order-one, and
r\in\mathbb{Q}
is non-zero. Then we have

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}I^{\sharp}(S^{3}_{r}(K))={\rm rk}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\widehat{HF}(S^{3}_{r}(K)).

Regarding the torsion order, the second author constructed in
[
18
]
a minus version of instanton knot homology, which we denote by
KHI^{-}(S^{3},K)
for a knot
K\subset S^{3}
. The minus version
KHI^{-}(S^{3},K)
admits an action of
U
and the torsion order of
K
is defined as the maximal
U
-order of any
U
-torsion element in
KHI^{-}(S^{3},K)
. We denote the torsion order of
K
by
{\rm ord}_{U}(K)

Utilizing Proposition
1.6
and the fact that the torsion order of alternating knots are odd (
c.f.
Lemma
2.4
), in order to prove Theorem
1.4
, it remains to prove the following.

Theorem 1.7
.

Suppose
K\subset S^{3}
is a knot. Then

{\rm ord}_{U}(K)\leq{\rm br}(K)-1.

Here
{\rm br}(\cdot)
denotes the bridge index of
K
.

A parallel result in Heegaard Floer theory has been established by Juhász, Miller, and Zemke
[
10
]
. Previously three ingredients were missing to establish Theorem
1.7
in instanton theory:

(1)

The cobordism map as in Theorem
1.1
.

(2)

The tube attachment lemma as in Proposition
4.1
: If
\Sigma^{\prime}
is obtained from
\Sigma
by attaching a tube to
\Sigma
, then (with suitable choices of auxiliary data), we have

F_{\Sigma^{\prime},A^{\prime}}=U\circ F_{\Sigma,A}:KHI^{-}(S^{3},K_{0})\rightarrow KHI^{-}(S^{3},K_{1}).

(3)

A connected sum formula for
KHI^{-}
.

Item (3) was recently established by the first author and Zemke
[
9
]
, while the first two items will be presented herein.

1.4
assuming some technical results that we establish in latter sections. In particular, we construct maps associated to knot cobordisms in Section

.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
The minus version of instanton knot homology

In this section, we review the minus version of instanton knot homology constructed in
[
18
]
. Suppose
Y
is a connected, closed, oriented
3
-manifold, and let
K\subset Y
is a null-homologous knot. Let
(\lambda,\mu)
represent the Seifert framing on
\partial(Y-N(K))
, where
\lambda
is the longitude and
\mu
is the meridian. Let
\Gamma_{n}
be the suture on
\partial(Y-N(K))
consisting of two curves of class
\pm(\lambda+n\mu)
.
(Y-N(K),\Gamma_{n})
forms a balanced sutured manifold as in
[
12
]
, and Kronheimer and Mrowka
[
15
]
constructed the sutured instanton Floer homology of the pair, denoted by
SHI(Y-N(K),\Gamma_{n})
.

A Seifert surface of
K
induces a grading on
SHI(Y-N(K),\Gamma_{n})
. This construction was pioneered by the work of Kronheimer and Mrowka in
[
14
]
and Baldwin and Sivek in
[
6
]
, and further developed by the second author and his collaborators in
[
18
,
7
,
21
]
.

Assuming we fix a Seifert surface
S
and choose the sutures
\Gamma_{n}
to have
2n
intersections with
S
for all
n
. In
[
18
]
, if
n
is odd, then
S
can be used to construct a
\mathbb{Z}
-grading, while if
n
is even, one needs to isotope
S
in one of two ways (called stabilizations in
[
18
]
) to construct a
\mathbb{Z}
-grading. The gradings associated with the two stabilizations always differ by
1
. Therefore, in
[
21
]
, the authors avoid choosing one stabilization by defining a
(\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2})
-grading for
S
when
n
is even. We will denote the graded part as:

SHI(Y-N(K),\Gamma_{n},i)~{\rm for~}i\in\mathbb{Z}~{\rm or}~\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}.

As in
[
3
]
, we have two bypass maps

\psi^{n}_{\pm,n+1}:SHI(Y-N(K),\Gamma_{n})\rightarrow SHI(Y-N(K),\Gamma_{n+1})

The original construction involved the orientation reversal of
3
-manifolds and sutures, but we can initiate the construction on
-Y
to avoid the sign complexity. The two bypass maps are symmetric in some sense, and their grading-shifting behavior was understood in
[
18
]
. In this paper, we adopt the convention that:

{\rm deg}(\psi^{n}_{\pm,n+1})=\mp\frac{1}{2}.

To simplify the notations, we write

\mathbf{\Gamma^{K}_{n}}=SHI(Y-N(K),\Gamma_{n}),~{\rm and~}(\mathbf{\Gamma^{K}_{n}},i)=SHI(Y-N(K),\Gamma_{n},i).

Also we define a grading shift

(\mathbf{\Gamma^{K}_{n}}[k],i)=(\mathbf{\Gamma^{K}_{n}},i-k).

Under this notation, we know that the map

\psi^{n}_{-,n+1}:\mathbf{\Gamma^{K}_{n}}[\frac{1-n}{2}]\to\mathbf{\Gamma^{K}_{n+1}}[\frac{-n}{2}]

is grading-preserving and
\psi^{n}_{+,n+1}
drops the grading by
-1
.

Additionally, we can attach a contact
2
-handle (cf.
[
3
]
, and again, we omit the negative orientation of
3
-manifolds) to
(Y-N(K),\Gamma_{n})
along the meridian of the knot, thereby obtaining a map:

F_{n}:\mathbf{\Gamma^{K}_{n}}\rightarrow I^{\sharp}(Y).

Definition 2.1

(
[
18
,
8
]
)
.

We define
KHI^{-}(Y,K)
to be the direct limit of the sequence

\psi^{n}_{-,n+1}:\mathbf{\Gamma^{K}_{n}}[\frac{1-n}{2}]\to\mathbf{\Gamma^{K}_{n+1}}[\frac{-n}{2}].

It inherits a
\mathbb{Z}
-grading. The collection of maps

\psi^{n}_{+,n+1}:\mathbf{\Gamma^{K}_{n}}[\frac{1-n}{2}]\to\mathbf{\Gamma^{K}_{n+1}}[\frac{-n}{2}]

induce a map

U:KHI^{-}(Y,K,i)\rightarrow KHI^{-}(Y,K,i-1).

The maps

F_{n}:\mathbf{\Gamma^{K}_{n}}[\frac{1-n}{2}]\rightarrow I^{\sharp}(Y)

induce a map

F^{-}_{K}:KHI^{-}(Y,K)\rightarrow I^{\sharp}(Y).

Lemma 2.2

(
[
18
,
8
]
)
.

For the unknot
\mathbb{U}_{1}\subset Y
, we have an isomorphism (of
\mathbb{C}[U]
-modules)

KHI^{-}(Y,\mathbb{U}_{1})\cong I^{\sharp}(Y)\mathop{\otimes}_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}[U],

where
I^{\#}(Y)
is supported at grading
0
and the element
1\in\mathbb{C}[U]
lies in grading
0
as well. Furthermore, the map
F^{-}_{\mathbb{U}_{1}}
restricts to an isomorphism on
KHI^{-}(S^{3},\mathbb{U}_{1},i)
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}
.

Definition 2.3
.

For a null homologous knot
K\subset S^{3}
, we define

{\rm ord}_{U}(K)=\max\{k~|~\exists~x\in KHI^{-}(S^{3},K)~s.t.~U^{k}\cdot x\neq 0,~U^{k+1}\cdot x=0.\}

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose
K\subset S^{3}
is an alternating knot. Then
{\rm ord}_{U}(K)
is odd.

Proof.

Suppose
K
is an alternating knot. In
[
20
]
, the author constructed a set of differentials
{d_{k}}
on Kronheimer and Mrowka’s version of instanton knot homology
KHI(S^{3},K)
. In
[
22
]
, it is shown that:

{\rm ord}_{U}(K)=\max\{k~|~d_{k}\neq 0\}.

In
[
20
]
, it is demonstrated that
d_{k}
has degree
-1
with respect to the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading and degree
-k
with respect to the Alexander grading on
KHI(S^{3},K)
. Furthermore, the
KHI
of alternating knots is computed through the work of Kronheimer and Mrowka in
[
14
,
16
]
. Specifically,
KHI(S^{3},K,i)
and
KHI(S^{3},K,j)
are supported in the same
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading if
i-j
is even. Hence,
d_{k}=0
if
k
is even, and we are done.
∎

. Later it will be used in the construction of the cobordism map. Suppose we have two null-homologous knots

C_{h}:\mathbf{\Gamma^{K_{1}}_{n}}\otimes\mathbf{\Gamma^{K_{2}}_{n}}\to\mathbf{\Gamma^{K_{1}\#K_{2}}_{m+n}}

Furthermore, the grading shifting behavior and the commutativity mentioned above also lead to the following two properties of the map
\rho
.

Lemma 2.5
.

The map
\rho
is homogeneous with respect to the Alexander grading. In particular,

\rho\bigg(KHI^{-}(Y_{1},K_{1},i)\otimes KHI^{-}(Y_{2},K_{2},j)\bigg)\subset KHI^{-}(Y_{1}\#Y_{2},K_{1}\#K_{2},i+j).

Lemma 2.6
.

The map
\rho
intertwines with the
U
-action. More precisely, we have

\rho\bigg((U\cdot x)\otimes y\bigg)=\rho\bigg(x\otimes(U\cdot y)\bigg)=U\cdot\rho(x\otimes y).

With the help of the above two lemmas, we can derive the following.

Lemma 2.7
.

Suppose
Y_{2}=S^{3}
and
K_{2}=\mathbb{U}_{1}
is the unknot. Let
\nu_{0}\in KHI^{-}(Z_{0}=S^{3},B_{0}=\mathbb{U}_{1},0)
be the generator. Then the map

\iota:KHI^{-}(Y_{1},K_{1})\to KHI^{-}(Y_{1}\cong Y_{1}\#S^{3},K_{1}\cong K_{1}\#\mathbb{U}_{1})

defined as

\iota(x)=\rho(x\otimes\nu_{0})

coincides with the identity map.

Proof.

In
[
18
]
, the author demonstrated that the direct system in Definition
2.1
stabilizes when
n
is large. Transitioning to sutured instanton Floer homology, to validate Lemma
2.7
, it is sufficient to establish the following: let
\nu^{m}_{0}\in(\mathbf{\Gamma}^{\mathbb{U}_{1}}_{m},\frac{m-1}{2})
be the generator of the highest non-vanishing grading. Then, the map:

\iota_{h,n+m}^{n}:\mathbf{\Gamma^{K_{1}}_{n}}\to\mathbf{\Gamma^{K_{1}}_{n+m}}\cong\mathbf{\Gamma^{K_{1}\#\mathbb{U}_{1}}_{m+n}}

defined as

\iota_{h,n+m}^{n}(x)=C_{h}(x\otimes\nu^{\prime}_{0})

coincide with the composition of bypass maps:

\iota_{h,n+m}^{n}=\psi^{n+m-1}_{-,m+n}\circ\dots\circ\psi^{n}_{-,n+1}.

The sutured instanton Floer homology of the sutured solid tori and the bypass maps on them have been computed in
[
18
]
. In particular, we know that

\mathbf{\Gamma_{1}^{\mathbb{U}_{1}}}\cong\mathbb{C},

and let
\nu_{0}^{1}\in\mathbf{\Gamma_{1}^{\mathbb{U}_{1}}}
be the generator, then we have

\nu_{0}^{m}=\psi^{m-1}_{-,m}\circ\dots\circ\psi^{1}_{-,2}(\nu_{0}^{1}).

Since
C_{h}
commutes with the bypass maps, it suffices to prove

\iota_{h,n+1}^{n}=\psi^{n}_{-,n+1}:\mathbf{\Gamma^{K_{1}}_{n}}\to\mathbf{\Gamma^{K_{1}}_{n+m}}.

To do this, note that
(-(S^{3}-N(K)),-\Gamma_{1})
admits a unique tight contact structure whose contact element is

\nu_{0}^{1}\in\mathbf{\Gamma_{1}^{\mathbb{U}_{1}}}\cong\mathbb{C}.

Note that
(-(S^{3}-N(K)),-\Gamma_{1})
can be decomposed into a contact
0

1
-handle. Additionally,
C_{h}
inherently is the map associated with a contact
1
-handle and a contact
2
-handle. In total, we have four contact handles; however, the
0
-handle and one of the
1

1
-handle and
2
-handle coincide with the construction of the bypass map as outlined in

]

\iota_{h,n+1}^{n}=\psi^{n}_{-,n+1}:\mathbf{\Gamma^{K_{1}}_{n}}\to\mathbf{\Gamma^{K_{1}}_{n+m}}.

∎

2.4.
Borromean knots and the

5.
Torsion order and connected sum

In this section, we prove the following result.

Proposition 5.1
.

Suppose
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
are two knots in
S^{3}
. Then we have the following equality

{\rm ord}_{U}(K_{1}\#K_{2})=\max\{{\rm ord}_{U}(K_{1}),{\rm ord}_{U}(K_{2})\}.

Lemma 5.2
.

Suppose
A
and
B
are two
\mathbb{Z}
-graded finitely generated rank-one
\mathbb{C}[U]
-modules so that
U
has degree
-1
. Then

{\rm ord}_{U}(A\widetilde{\otimes}B)=\max\{{\rm ord}_{U}(A),{\rm ord}_{U}(B)\}.

Proof.

We denote the
U
-action on
A
as
U_{1}
, and the action on
B
as
U_{2}
. Choose
a\in A
as a
U_{1}
-free element at the highest grading in
A
, and
b\in B
as a
U_{2}
-free element at the highest grading in
B
. We can treat
A
and
B
as complex vector spaces and select a set of homogeneous bases for each, ensuring that the following conditions are met.

(1)

The elements
U_{1}^{j}a
for
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
are all in the basis for
A
, and any other element in the basis of
A
is
U
-torsion.

(2)

The elements
U_{2}^{j}b
for
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
are all in the basis for
B
, and any other element in the basis of
B
is
U
-torsion.

Assume, without loss of generality, we have

r={\rm ord}_{U}(A)\geq{\rm ord}_{U}(B).

Step 1.
We show that
{\rm ord}_{U}(A\widetilde{\otimes}B)\geq\max\{{\rm ord}_{U}(A),{\rm ord}_{U}(B)\}.

Take
a^{\prime}\in A
a homogeneous element so that
U^{r}_{1}a^{\prime}\neq 0
but
U^{r+1}_{1}a^{\prime}=0
. Now take the element
x=a^{\prime}\otimes b\in A\otimes B
. We would like to show that
U_{1}^{r}x\notin\im(U_{1}+U_{2})
.

Assume the contrary,
i.e.
, we have

(U_{1}+U_{2})\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\cdot(a_{i}\otimes b_{i})=a^{\prime}\otimes b.

where all
a_{i}
and
b_{i}
are included in the chosen bases for
A
and
B
, respectively. Since
b
is a free element in the basis, there must be at least one free element among the
b_{i}
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
b_{1}
is the free element with the lowest grading, that
b_{2}
through
b_{m}
are equal to
b_{1}
, and that
b_{i}
differs from
b_{1}
for
i
ranging from
m+1
to
n
. It is important to note that
B
has a rank of one as a
\mathbb{C}[U]
-module, which means
b_{1}
is the sole free element in the basis at its particular grading. Consequently, we observe the following:

(5.1)

a^{\prime}\otimes b=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\cdot a_{i}\otimes(U_{2}b_{1})+{\rm(other~higher~grading~or~}U_{2}\textrm{-}{\rm torsion~terms)}.

Thus, we conclude that
m=1
,
\lambda_{1}=1
,
a^{\prime}=a_{1}
, and
b=U_{2}b_{1}
. However, this conclusion contradicts the selection of
b
as a
U
-free element of maximal grading.

Consequently,
U^{r}_{1}x
does not fall within the image of
U_{1}-U_{2}
, indicating that
[U_{1}^{r}x]
is non-zero in
{\rm coker}(U_{1}-U_{2})
and subsequently embeds into
A\widetilde{\otimes}B
. Nonetheless,
U_{1}^{r+1}x=(U^{r+1}_{1}a^{\prime}\otimes b)=0
. Therefore, we deduce that
[x]
corresponds to a
U
-torsion element in
A\widetilde{\otimes}B
with order
r
, leading us to the following conclusion

{\rm ord}_{U}(A\widetilde{\otimes}B)\geq\max\{{\rm ord}_{U}(A),{\rm ord}_{U}(B)\}.

Step 2.
We show that
{\rm ord}_{U}(A\widetilde{\otimes}B)\leq\max\{{\rm ord}_{U}(A),{\rm ord}_{U}(B)\}.

We can regard
A\widetilde{\otimes}B
as
\ker(U_{1}-U_{2})\oplus{\rm coker}(U_{1}-U_{2})
. As a result, we treat the kernel and co-kernal parts separately. First, suppose

x=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\cdot(a_{i}\otimes b_{i})\in\ker(U_{1}-U_{2}).

If we take
a^{\prime}\otimes b=0
in Equation (
5.1
), we conclude that no
b_{i}
could be
U_{2}
-free. As a result, we conclude that any such element has torsion order at most
{\rm}ord_{U}(B)
.

Second, suppose

x=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\cdot(a_{i}\otimes b_{i})\notin\im(U_{1}-U_{2})

is an element so that
[x]\in{\rm coker}(U_{1}+U_{2})
is
U
-torsion. We may assume that for
i=1,...,m
, both
a_{i}
and
b_{i}
are free, and for
i=m+1,...,n
, either
a_{i}
or
b_{i}
is torsion. Take
k
large enough, we conclude that

U_{1}^{k}U_{2}^{k}x=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\cdot(U_{1}^{k}a_{i})\otimes(U_{2}^{k}b_{i}).

Note that at sufficiently low gradings, the torsion components of both
A
and
B
do not contribute to
A\widetilde{\otimes}B
. Therefore, the low-grade portions of
A\widetilde{\otimes}B
correspond to the standard model of
\mathbb{C}[U]\widetilde{\otimes}\mathbb{C}[U]
. Consequently, it is evident that if all
a_{i}
and
b_{i}
are free, and
k
is sufficiently large, then:

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\cdot(U_{1}^{k}a_{i})\otimes(U_{2}^{k}b_{i})\in\im(U_{1}+U_{2})\Leftrightarrow\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\cdot(a_{i})\otimes(b_{i})\in\im(U_{1}+U_{2}).

As a result, we conclude that

[x]=[y=\sum_{i=m+1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\cdot(a_{i}\otimes b_{i})]\in{\rm coker}(U_{1}+U_{2}).

Now for each
i=m+1,...,n
, either
a_{i}
or
b_{i}
is torsion, so we conclude that
[x]
has torsion order at most
r={\rm ord}_{U}(A)\geq{\rm ord}_{U}(B)
.
∎

Hardware-Impaired Rician-Faded Cell-Free Massive MIMO Systems With Channel Aging
Venkatesh Tentu, Dheeraj N Amudala, Anish Chattopadhyay, Rohit Budhiraja

Abstract
We study the impact of channel aging on the uplink of a cell-free (CF) massive multiple-input multiple-output (mMIMO) system by considering i) spatially-correlated Rician-faded channels; ii) hardware impairments at the access points and user equipments (UEs); and iii) two-layer large-scale fading decoding (LSFD). We first derive a closed-form spectral efficiency (SE) expression for this system, and later propose two novel optimization techniques to optimize the non-convex SE metric by exploiting the minorization-maximization (MM) method. The first one requires a numerical optimization solver, and has a high computation complexity. The second one with closed-form transmit power updates, has a trivial computation complexity. We numerically show that i) the two-layer LSFD scheme effectively mitigates the interference due to channel aging for both low- and high-velocity UEs; and ii) increasing the number of AP antennas does not mitigate the SE deterioration due to channel aging. We numerically characterize the optimal pilot length required to maximize the SE for various UE speeds. We also numerically show that the proposed closed-form MM optimization yields the same SE as that of the first technique, which requires numerical solver, and that too with a much reduced time-complexity.

1

A CF mMIMO system consists of a large number of access points (APs) which are randomly deployed over a large coverage area, and are connected to a central processing unit (CPU) via high-speed fronthaul links. The APs cooperate via these fronthaul links to mitigate the inter-user interference (IUI) caused due to multiple user equipments (UEs) being served on the same time-frequency resource

The
main contributions
of the current work address these gaps as follows:

•

We consider a spatially-correlated Rician-faded CF mMIMO system with channels aging, and investigate the impact of dynamic ADC architecture and low-cost hardware-impaired RF chains. We also consider the two-layer LSFD, and derive a closed-form SE expression by addressing the derivation difficulties caused by the combined modelling of channel aging, RF impairments, dynamic ADC architecture and spatially-correlated Rician channels.

•

We use the derived closed-form SE expression to maximize the non-convex SE metric by optimizing the UE transmit powers and the LSFD coefficients, which is the second contribution of this work. We propose two optimal power allocation schemes using minorization-maximization (MM) technique, which iteratively maximizes the convex surrogate of the non-convex objective
[
29
]
. We propose a novel convex surrogate function, and analytically show that it satisfies the desirable surrogacy properties
[
29
]
. This approach, however, has a high computation complexity as it requires off-the-shelf optimization solvers
[
29
]
. We next design a low-complexity practically-implementable power allocation technique by using the Lagrangian dual transform technique
[
30
]
. This enables us in designing the closed-form transmit power update, which has a trivial computational complexity. It is extremely useful for designing optimal power for practical CF mMIMO systems with channel aging.

•

We show that LSFD can effectively mitigate the detrimental effects of i) channel aging for both low and high UE velocities; and ii) IUI for low-velocity UEs but not for high-velocity UEs. We also show that the increased number of AP antennas cannot mitigate the SE degradation due to channel aging.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section
II
discusses the channel model, uplink channel estimation and data transmission, and LSFD for the proposed hardware-impaired CF mMIMO system with channel aging. Section
III
derives and analyzes the closed-form SE expression for the aforementioned system. Section
IV
proposes two optimal power allocation schemes to optimize SE. Section
V
first numerically validates the efficacy of the derived closed-form SE. It then investigates the effect of channel aging and LSFD on the SE. It then compares the complexity of the proposed optimization techniques. Section
VI
finally concludes the paper.

II
System Model

We consider the uplink of a CF mMIMO system with
M
multi-antenna APs and
K
single-antenna UEs. Each AP has
N
antennas. We assume, similar to
[
5
,
24
]
, that the APs are randomly distributed over a large geographical area, and are connected to a CPU via high-speed fronthaul links. To reduce the system hardware cost and power consumption, APs and UEs are equipped with low-cost hardware-impaired RF chains. Further, the APs have a dynamic-resolution ADC architecture, wherein each AP antenna can be connected to a different resolution ADC. This is unlike
[
22
,
25
,
26
]
, which assume that the ADCs either have a low or a mixed resolution. The UEs are designed using low-resolution DACs.
In
correlated
Rician-faded cell-free mMIMO systems, the channel does not harden. For high speed UEs, the channel will age. To investigate channel aging effect, we consider, as shown in Fig.

, a resource block of length
\tau_{c}
time instants.

II-A

Channel model

The channel from the
k
th UE to the
m
th AP at the
\lambda
th time instant is denoted as
\mathbf{h}_{mk}[\lambda]
. Due to dense AP deployment, the channel
\mathbf{h}_{mk}[\lambda]
contains both LoS and NLoS paths.
It, therefore, has a Rician probability density function (pdf), and is modeled as follows
[
4
]
:

\displaystyle\!\!\!\mathbf{h}_{mk}[\lambda]=\bar{\mathbf{h}}_{mk}e^{j\phi_{mk}^{\lambda}}\!+\!\mathbf{R}_{mk}^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{mk}[\lambda].

(1)

Here
\bar{\mathbf{h}}_{mk}=\sqrt{\frac{\!K_{mk}\beta_{mk}}{\!K_{mk}+1}}\breve{\mathbf{h}}_{mk}
,
\mathbf{R}_{mk}\!=\frac{\beta_{mk}}{\!K_{mk}\!+\!1}\breve{\mathbf{R}}_{mk}
. The term
K_{mk}
is the Rician factor, and
\beta_{mk}
is the large-scale fading coefficient.
The LoS term
\breve{\mathbf{h}}_{mk}
is modeled as
\breve{\mathbf{h}}_{mk}=[1,e^{j\psi_{mk}},\cdots,e^{j(M-1)\psi_{mk}}]^{T}
, where
\psi_{mk}
is the angle of arrival from the
k
th UE to the
m
th AP. The term,
\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{mk}[\lambda]
, with pdf
\mathcal{CN}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I}_{N})
models the small-scale NLoS fading. The term
\breve{\mathbf{R}}_{mk}
is the spatial correlation matrix. The LoS phase-shift
\phi_{mk}^{\lambda}
at the
\lambda
th instant is uniformly distributed between
[-\pi,\pi]
.
The long-term channel statistics i.e.,
\bar{\mathbf{h}}_{mk}
,
\beta_{mk}
and
\mathbf{R}_{mk}
, remain constant over a resource block and, similar to
[
4
]
, are assumed to be perfectly known at the AP. Most of the Rician CF works e.g.,
[
31
,
32
,
19
]
, assume that the LoS phase-shift
\phi_{mk}^{\lambda}
is static. A slight change in UEs position, however, can radically modify the phase. It is, thus, crucial to consider a dynamic phase-shift
\phi_{mk}^{\lambda}
in the Rician channel to practically model it
[
4
]
. We accordingly, similar to
[
4
]
, assume that
\phi_{mk}^{\lambda}
varies as frequently as small-scale fading, and that the AP is unaware of it. As an AP does not have the prior knowledge of
\phi_{mk}^{\lambda}
, it estimates the channel
\mathbf{h}_{mk}
, without its knowledge.

As discussed earlier, due to UEs mobility, the AP-UE channel in a resource block varies across time instants, which
causes channel aging
[
15
]
. To analyze its effect, we model the channel
\mathbf{h}_{mk}[n]
at the
n
th time instant
1\leq n\leq\tau_{c}
, as a combination of the channel
\mathbf{h}_{mk}[\lambda]
at the time instant
\lambda
, and the innovation component as follows
[
14
,
15
]
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{h}_{mk}[n]=\rho_{k}[\lambda-n]\mathbf{h}_{mk}[\lambda]+\sqrt{1-\rho_{k}^{2}[\lambda-n]}\Big(\bar{\mathbf{h}}_{mk}e^{j\phi_{mk}^{n}}+\mathbf{f}_{mk}[n]\Big).

(2)

The temporal correlation
\rho_{k}[\lambda-n]
, based on the Jake’s model
[
15
]
, is given as
\rho_{k}[\lambda-n]=J_{0}(2\pi f_{d,i}T_{s}(\lambda-n))
. Here
J_{0}(\cdot)
is the zeroth-order Bessel function, and
T_{s}
is the sampling time. The term
f_{d,i}=(v_{k}f_{c})/c
is the Doppler spread, with
v_{k}
,
f_{c}
and
c
being the user velocity, carrier frequency and the velocity of light, respectively. The innovation component
\mathbf{f}_{mk}[n]
is independent of the channel at the time instant
\lambda
i.e.,
{\mathbf{h}}_{mk}[\lambda]
, and has a pdf
\mathcal{CN}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{R}_{mk})

[
15
]
.

II-B

Uplink training

Recall that the uplink training phase consists of
\tau_{p}
time instants. The
k
th UE, similar to
[
15
]
, transmits its pilot signal
\sqrt{\tilde{p}_{k}}\phi_{k}[t_{k}]
at the time instant
t_{k}\subset\{1,\dots,\tau_{p}\}
. Here
|\phi_{k}[t_{k}]|^{2}=1
.
We assume that the uplink training duration
\tau_{p}<K
. The number of UEs transmitting pilot at a particular time instant is more than one, which causes pilot contamination
[
4
]
. The set of UEs that transmit pilots at the time instant
t_{k}
is denoted as
\mathcal{P}_{k}
. The
k
th UE feeds its pilot signal to the low-resolution DAC, which distorts it. This distortion is commonly analyzed using Bussgang model
[
33
]
. The distorted DAC output, based on the Bussgang model, is:

\displaystyle s_{\text{DAC},k}^{p}[t_{k}]=Q(\sqrt{\tilde{p}_{k}}\phi_{k}[t_{k}])=\alpha_{d,k}\sqrt{\tilde{p}_{k}}\phi_{k}[t_{k}]+\upsilon^{p}_{\text{DAC},k}[t_{k}].

(3)

Here
\alpha_{d,k}=1\!-\iota_{d,k}
with
\iota_{d,k}
being the DAC distortion factor. The term
\upsilon^{p}_{\text{DAC},k}[t_{k}]
is zero-mean DAC quantization noise, which is uncorrelated with the input pilot signal, and has a variance
\alpha_{d,k}(1\!-\!\alpha_{d,k})\tilde{p}_{k}\mathbb{E}\big\{|\phi_{k}[t_{k}]|^{2}\big\}

[
4
]
. The
k
th UE DAC output signal
s_{\text{DAC},k}^{p}[t_{k}]
is fed to its hardware- impaired RF chain, which adds a distortion term

\xi_{\text{RF},k}^{p}[t_{k}]

[
34
]
. The effective uplink pilot signal is, therefore, given as
s_{\text{RF},k}^{p}[t_{k}]=s_{\text{DAC},k}^{p}[t_{k}]+\xi_{\text{RF},k}^{p}[t_{k}]
. The distortion
\xi_{\text{RF},k}^{p}[t_{k}]
is independent of the input signal, and has a pdf
\mathcal{CN}\big(0,\kappa_{t,k}^{2}\big(\mathbb{E}\{s^{p}_{\text{DAC},k}[t_{k}](s_{\text{DAC},k}^{p}[t_{k}])^{H}\}\big)\big)

[
34
]
. The term
\kappa_{t,k}
in the pdf models the UE transmit EVM
[
34
]
.
At the
t_{k}
time instant, the
m
th AP receives at its antennas, the sum of pilot signals transmitted by UEs in the set
\mathcal{P}_{k}
i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}_{m}^{p}[t_{k}]=\sum\limits_{i\in\mathcal{P}_{k}}\mathbf{h}_{mi}[t_{k}]s_{\text{RF},i}^{p}[t_{k}].

(4)

To reduce the system cost, the APs are designed using hardware-impaired RF chains and a dynamic-ADC architecture, which enables us to vary the resolution of each ADC from
1
to a maximum of
D
bits. The
m
th AP feeds the above pilots signal received at its antenna to the hardware-impaired RF chains, whose distorted output, based on the EVM model, is
[
34
]
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}_{\text{RF},m}^{p}[t_{k}]=\mathbf{y}_{m}^{p}[t_{k}]+\boldsymbol{\eta}_{\text{RF},m}^{p}[t_{k}]+\mathbf{z}_{m}^{p}[t_{k}].

(5)

The term
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{\text{RF},m}^{p}[t_{k}]
, with pdf
\mathcal{CN}(\boldsymbol{0},\kappa_{r,m}^{2}\mathbf{W}_{m}[t_{k}])
, models the RF hardware distortion. The term
\kappa_{r,m}^{2}
is the AP receiver EVM and
\mathbf{W}_{m}[t_{k}]=\text{diag}\big(\mathbb{E}\big\{\mathbf{y}_{m}^{p}[t_{k}](\mathbf{y}_{m}^{p}[t_{k}])^{H}\big|\mathbf{h}_{mk}[t_{k}]\big\}\big)
. Note that the receive hardware impairment is proportional to the power of UEs transmit signals
[
34
]
. The vector
\mathbf{z}_{m}[t_{k}]
, with pdf
\mathcal{CN}(\boldsymbol{0},\mathbf{I}_{N})
, is the additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) at the
m
th AP. The RF chain output is then fed to the dynamic-resolution ADCs, which distort it by adding quantization noise. The distorted ADCs output, based on the Bussgang model
[
33
]
, is given as

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}_{\text{ADC},m}^{p}[t_{k}]=\mathbf{A}_{m}\mathbf{y}_{\text{RF},m}^{p}[t_{k}]+{\mathbf{n}^{p}_{\text{ADC},m}}[t_{k}]=\mathbf{A}_{m}\big(\mathbf{y}_{m}^{p}[t_{k}]+\boldsymbol{\eta}^{p}_{\text{RF},m}[t_{k}]+\mathbf{z}^{p}_{m}[t_{k}]\big)+{\mathbf{n}^{p}_{\text{ADC,m}}}[t_{k}].

(6)

The matrix
\mathbf{A}_{m}=\text{diag}(1-\iota_{m,n},\cdots,1-\iota_{m,n})
, where
\iota_{m,n}
is the ADC distortion factor
[
33
]
. The zero-mean additive ADC quantization noise
{\mathbf{n}^{p}_{\text{ADC},m}}[t_{k}]
is uncorrelated with
\mathbf{y}_{\text{RF},m}^{p}[t_{k}]
. It has a covariance of
\mathbf{B}_{m}\text{diag}\big(\mathbb{E}\big\{\mathbf{y}_{\text{RF},m}^{p}[t_{k}](\mathbf{y}_{\text{RF},m}^{p}[t_{k}])^{H}\big|\mathbf{h}_{mk}[t_{k}]\big\}\big)
with
\mathbf{B}_{m}=\mathbf{A}_{m}(\mathbf{I}_{N}-\mathbf{A}_{m})

[
33
]
. The existing CF mMIMO literature
[

,

,

,

,

,

,

]
, except

.
It significantly complicates the SE analysis and derivation, when compared with

Theorem 1

For a hardware-impaired CF mMIMO system with spatially-correlated Rician fading and phase-shifts, the linear minimum mean square error (LMMSE) estimate of

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{mk}[\lambda]=\sqrt{\tilde{p}_{k}}\alpha_{d,k}\rho_{k}[\lambda-t_{k}]\bar{\mathbf{R}}_{mk}\mathbf{A}_{m}\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{mk}\mathbf{y}_{\text{ADC},m}^{p}[t_{k}]\;,\text{where }\overline{\mathbf{R}}_{mk}=\big(\bar{\mathbf{h}}_{mk}\bar{\mathbf{h}}_{mk}^{H}+\mathbf{R}_{mk}\big).

The matrix

Remark 1
.

We note that the LMMSE channel estimation method does not fully utilize the temporal correlation among different time sample. Its major advantage, however, is its closed-form solution which crucially helps us in deriving a closed form SE expression in Section
III
, which is a function only of the long-term channel statistics. This closed-form SE expression is then crucially used to derive a low-complexity solution for its optimization, which we do in Section
IV
. In contrast, the
iterative
Sparse Bayesian Learning (SBL) method in
[
10
]
exploits the channel temporal correlation, but does not provide a closed-form solution. This will radically complicate the SE closed form derivation and its optimization. Further, the SBL method proposed in
[
10
]
cannot be trivially extended to our CF mMIMO system with RF impairments, ADC/DAC quantization noise and Rician phase-shifts. Reference
[
10
]
did not consider these impairments. A closed-form SBL method for the system considered herein, which can help in deriving closed form SE expression, however, is an interesting direction of future work.

II-C

Uplink Transmission

Let
s_{k}[n]
, with
\mathbb{E}\{|s_{k}[n]|^{2}\}=1

k
th UE wants to transmit at the
n
th time instant. The symbol

[

, is given as

. The term

is the DAC quantization noise
[
33
]
, and has a zero mean and variance
\!(1\!-\alpha_{d,k})\alpha_{d,k}p_{k}
. It is uncorrelated with the information signal
s_{k}[n]
. The DAC output
s_{\text{DAC},k}[n]
is fed to the low-cost hardware-impaired RF chain, whose distorted output, based on the EVM model
[
34
]
, is given as

\displaystyle s_{\text{RF},k}[n]=s_{\text{DAC},k}[n]+\xi_{\text{RF},k}[n]=\alpha_{d,k}\sqrt{p_{k}}s_{k}[n]+\upsilon_{\text{DAC},k}[n]+\xi_{\text{RF},k}[n].

(10)

The distortion term
\xi_{\text{RF},k}[n]
is the transmit RF hardware impairment of the
k
th UE. It has a pdf
\mathcal{CN}(0,\kappa_{t,k}^{2}(\mathbb{E}\{s_{\text{DAC},k}[n](s_{\text{DAC},k}[n])^{H}\}))
, with
\kappa_{t,k}
being its transmit EVM
[
4
]
.

The
m
th AP receives the following sum signal at its antennas:
\mathbf{y}_{m}[n]=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\mathbf{h}_{mk}[n]s_{\text{RF},k}[n]
. The AP feeds this receive signal to its hardware-impaired RF chains, which distorts it as
[
34
]
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}_{\text{RF},m}[n]=\mathbf{y}_{m}[n]+\boldsymbol{\eta}_{\text{RF},m}[n]+{\mathbf{z}_{m}[n]}.

(11)

Here

, with pdf
\mathcal{CN}(\mathbf{0},\kappa_{r,m}^{2}\mathbf{W}^{m}[n])
, is the receiver hardware distortion, and
\mathbf{W}^{m}[n]=\text{diag}(\mathbb{E}\{\mathbf{y}_{m}[n]\mathbf{y}_{m}[n]^{H}|\mathbf{h}_{mk}[n]\})
. The term
\mathbf{z}_{m}[n]
, with pdf
\mathcal{CN}(\boldsymbol{0},\mathbf{I}_{N})
, is the AWGN at the
m
th AP. The RF chain output is then fed to the dynamic-resolution ADCs, whose quantized and

noisy output, based on the Bussgang model
[
33
]

The matrix
\mathbf{A}_{m}=\text{diag}\{\iota_{m,1},\cdots,\iota_{m,N}\}
, with
\iota_{m,i}
being the ADC distortion factor for the
i

m
th AP. The vector

\mathbf{S}^{m}[n]=\text{diag}(\mathbb{E}\{{\mathbf{y}_{\text{RF},m}[n]}{\mathbf{y}^{H}_{\text{RF},m}[n]}|\mathbf{h}_{mk}[n]\})

[
33
]

Principles of two-layer decoding:
The CF system considered herein, as shown in Fig.
2
, employs two-layer decoding to mitigate IUI. In the first-stage, each AP combines its received signal by using local channel estimates, which only partially mitigate the IUI. To mitigate the residual IUI, all APs sends their locally-combined received signal to the CPU, which performs the second-layer LSFD. The CPU computes the LSFD weights based on the large-scale fading coefficients, which remains constant for
100
s of coherence intervals
[
5
,
36
]
. These coefficients, can thus, be easily computed and stored at the CPU
[
5
]
. We now explain the two-step decoding in detail.

The

\displaystyle\breve{s}_{km}[n]=\hat{\mathbf{h}}^{H}_{mk}[\lambda]{\mathbf{y}_{\text{ADC},m}[n]}.

(12)

It sends the combined signal

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\overline{\text{SINR}}_{k,n}\!=\!\frac{\alpha_{d,k}^{2}p_{k}|\mathbf{a}_{k}^{H}[n]\boldsymbol{\delta}_{k,n}|^{2}}{\begin{Bmatrix}\!\mathbf{a}_{k}^{H}[n]\Big(\mathbf{B}_{k,n}\!+\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k,n}\!+\sum\limits_{i\neq k}^{K}\alpha_{d,i}^{2}p_{i}\mathbf{C}_{ki,n}\!+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{K}\big(1\!-\alpha_{d,i}\!+\!\kappa_{t,i}^{2}\big)\alpha_{d,i}p_{i}\mathbf{C}_{ki,n}\\[-3.0pt]

(17)

Remark 2
.

In CF mMIMO systems, each AP may have a small number of two to four antennas. The system designer, may not connect each AP antenna to a different resolution ADC, as it will increase the system complexity. The current framework, however, also allows a designer to analytically evaluate the SE of such CF mMIMO system by varying the ADC resolution across different APs, and by keeping them same within an AP. This design is practical as each AP is designed as a separate subsystem. For CF mMIMO systems, if AP has greater than four antennas, the dynamic ADC architecture could be implemented within each AP itself.

IV
SE Optimization of Hardware-Impaired CF System With Channel Aging

We now optimize the SE for a given time instant
n
. The SE optimization problem, by using the SE expression in Theorem
2
, can be cast as follows:

\displaystyle\textbf{P1:}\;\underset{\mathbf{p}}{\text{Max}}\;R_{n}(\mathbf{p})=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\log_{2}\bigg(1+\frac{{\Delta}_{k,n}(\mathbf{p})}{{\Omega}_{k,n}(\mathbf{p})}\bigg),\;\text{subject to }0\leq p_{k}\leq P_{max}\;\forall k.

(21)

The term
P_{max}
is the maximum allowed UE transmit power, and the vector
\mathbf{p}=[p_{1},\cdots,p_{K}]^{T}
.
For a given time-instant
n
, the objective of
P1
is not a concave function.
To optimize
P1
, we now use MM technique, which first finds a surrogate function that locally approximates the objective function with their difference minimized at the current point, and then iteratively maximizes it. The surrogate functions should be designed to have the following desirable features:
i) convexity and smoothness; and ii) existence of a closed-form minimizer
[
29
]
.
The first property helps in easily optimizing the surrogate problem, which can be easily shown to converge to a stationary point of the original non-convex problem
[
29
]
. The second property helps in designing a low-complexity optimization solution. We use MM framework to propose two solutions. The surrogate function in the first case only has the first property but does not have the second one. The first solution thus has a high complexity and requires a numerical optimization solver e.g., CVX
[
37
]
. The second solution, builds upon the first one, and combines the MM approach with Lagrangian dual transform from
[
30
]
to design another surrogate function, which satisfies the second property also. This leads to its highly reduced complexity.

We now briefly explain the MM technique from
[
29
]
. Consider the following non-concave maximization problem with a non-concave objective
f(\mathbf{x})
:

\displaystyle{\text{Maximize}}\;\;f(\mathbf{x})\text{ subject to }\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X},\text{ with $\mathcal{X}$ being a convex set}.

The MM technique solves it by first constructing a convex surrogate
g(\mathbf{x}|\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})
of
f(\mathbf{x})
at a feasible point
\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}

[
29
]
. It then iteratively generates a sequence of feasible points
\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t+1)}
by maximizing
g(\cdot|\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})
.
The surrogate function
g(\mathbf{x}|\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})
, along with two aforementioned desirable properties, should be continuous in
(\mathbf{x},\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})
. It should also lower bound the objective function
f(\mathbf{x})
.
The surrogate function has to additionally satisfy the following two technical conditions
[
29
]
:

\displaystyle\text{C1}:g(\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}|\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})=f(\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}),\text{ and }\text{C2}:\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}g(\mathbf{x}|\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})|_{\mathbf{x}=\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}}=\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}f(\mathbf{x})|_{\mathbf{x}=\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}}.

(22)

Each MM iteration generates a feasible point
\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}
. The function value at each iteration
f(\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})
increases, and then finally converges to a stationary point of the original problem
[
29
]
.

IV-A

Minorization-maximization approach

We begin by constructing a novel surrogate function for the non-concave objective
R_{n}(\mathbf{p})
. We achieve this aim by first proposing a lower bound on
R_{n}(\mathbf{p})
, and then show that it satisfies C1 and C2. This will make it a valid surrogate function. We now state a Lemma, which is then used to construct a lower bound on
R_{n}(\mathbf{p})
.

Lemma 3
.

For
N_{k}(\mathbf{x}):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
D_{k}(\mathbf{x}):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{++}
and an increasing function
f(\cdot)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{2K}f\Big(\frac{N_{k}(\mathbf{x})^{2}}{D_{k}(\mathbf{x})}\Big)\geq\sum_{k=1}^{2K}f(2N_{k}(\mathbf{x})-D_{k}(\mathbf{x})).

(23)

By substituting
N_{k}(\mathbf{x})=y_{k,n}\sqrt{{\Delta}_{k,n}(\mathbf{p})}
,
D_{k}(\mathbf{x})=y_{k,n}^{2}{\Omega}_{k,n}(\mathbf{p})
, and
f(\cdot)=\text{log}_{2}(1+\cdot)
in Lemma
3
, the objective function
R_{n}(\mathbf{p})
is lower-bounded as follows:

\displaystyle R_{n}(\mathbf{p})\geq\sum_{k=1}^{2K}\text{log}_{2}\Big(1+2y_{k,n}\sqrt{{\Delta}_{k,n}(\mathbf{p})}-y_{k,n}^{2}{\Omega}_{k,n}(\mathbf{p})\Big)=\widetilde{R}_{n}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{y}_{n}).

(24)

Here
y_{k,n}
is a function of the feasible point
\mathbf{p}^{(t)}
as
y_{k,n}=\sqrt{{\Delta}_{k,n}(\mathbf{p}^{(t)})}/{\Omega}_{k,n}(\mathbf{p}^{(t)})
, and
\mathbf{y}_{n}=[y_{1,n},\cdots,y_{K,n}]^{T}
.
We now show that
\widetilde{R}_{n}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{y}_{n})
in (
24
) satisfies conditions
C1
and
C2
, and is a valid surrogate function.

•

Condition
C1
can be proved by substituting the variable update of
y_{k,n}
and
\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{p}^{(t)}
in
R_{n}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{y}_{n})
.

•

Condition
C2
can be verified by differentiating both
\widetilde{R}_{n}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{y}_{n})
and
R_{n}(\mathbf{p})
at
\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{p}^{(t)}
.

By replacing
R_{n}(\mathbf{p})
with the surrogate function
\widetilde{R}_{n}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{y}_{n})
, problem
P1
is recast as follows:

P2:

\displaystyle\underset{\mathbf{p}}{\text{Maximize}}\;\;\;\;\sum_{k=1}^{2K}\text{log}_{2}\Big(1+2y_{k,n}\sqrt{{\Delta}_{k,n}(\mathbf{p})}-y_{k,n}^{2}{\Omega}_{k,n}(\mathbf{p})\Big)

(25a)

\displaystyle\text{subject to }0\leq p_{k}\leq P_{max}\;\forall k.

(25b)

We note that the
{\Delta}_{k,n}(\mathbf{p})
and
{\Omega}_{k,n}(\mathbf{p})
in the objective of
P2
are concave and convex in
\mathbf{p}
, respectively. Problem
P2
now becomes concave which can be solved using CVX
[
37
]
. The procedure to calculate optimal power coefficients begins by first constructing the surrogate function
R_{n}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{y}_{n})
, with
y_{k,n}=\sqrt{{\Delta}_{k,n}(\mathbf{p}^{(t)})}/{\Omega}_{k,n}(\mathbf{p}^{(t)})
, and then by solving
P2
using CVX.

IV-B

Closed-form MM approach

The MM-based SE optimization has a high complexity. We now propose a practically-implementable SE optimization algorithm by combining MM approach with the Lagrangian dual transform
[
30
]
,
which provides an iterative closed-form solution for the UE transmit powers
\mathbf{p}
.
To use this approach, problem
P1
is equivalently expressed as

\displaystyle\textbf{P3:}\;\underset{\mathbf{p},\gamma_{k,n}}{\text{Maximize}}\;\;\sum_{k=1}^{K}\log_{2}\big(1+\gamma_{k,n}\big)\;\text{ subject to }\gamma_{k,n}\leq\frac{{\Delta}_{k,n}(\mathbf{p})}{{\Omega}_{k,n}(\mathbf{p})},\;0\leq p_{k}\leq P_{max}\;\forall k.

(26)

The epigraph variable
\gamma_{k,n}
moves the ratio out of the logarithm
[
37
]
. Problem
P3
can be decomposed into outer and inner optimizations over
p_{k}
and
\gamma_{k,n}
, respectively. The inner optimization in
\gamma_{k,n}
is convex. The strong, duality therefore, holds. Its equivalent Lagrangian function is
[
37
]
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\gamma_{k,n},\lambda_{k,n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\log_{2}\big(1+\gamma_{k,n}\big)-\sum_{k=1}^{K}\lambda_{k,n}\left(\gamma_{k,n}-\frac{{\Delta}_{k,n}(\mathbf{p})}{{\Omega}_{k,n}(\mathbf{p})}\right).

(27)

Here
\lambda_{k,n}
is the Lagrangian dual variable. Let
(\gamma_{k,n}^{\ast},\lambda_{k,n}^{\ast})
be the saddle point which satisfies the first-order condition i.e.,
{\partial\mathcal{L}(\gamma_{k,n},\lambda_{k,n})}/{\partial\gamma_{k,n}}\big|_{\gamma_{k,n}^{*}}=0
, then

\displaystyle\lambda_{k,n}^{*}=\frac{1}{(1+\gamma_{k,n}^{*})\ln{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{(a)}}}{{=}}\frac{\Omega_{k,n}(\mathbf{p})}{(\Delta_{k,n}(\mathbf{p})+\Omega_{k,n}(\mathbf{p}))\ln{2}}.

(28)

Equality
(a)
is because
\gamma_{k,n}^{\ast}\!=\!{\Delta_{k,n}(\mathbf{p})}/{\Omega_{k,n}(\mathbf{p})}
, for a fixed
\mathbf{p}
. Substituting (
28
) in (
27
), the problem
P3
is rewritten as:

\displaystyle\hskip-5.0pt\textbf{P4}:\;

\displaystyle\underset{\mathbf{p},\gamma_{k,n}}{\text{Maximize}}\quad\mathcal{L}(\gamma_{k,n},\lambda_{k,n}^{*})=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left[\!\log_{2}\left(1+\gamma_{k,n}\right)-\frac{1}{\ln{2}}\left(\gamma_{k,n}-{\frac{\Delta_{k,n}(\mathbf{p})(1+\gamma_{k,n})}{\Delta_{k,n}(\mathbf{p})+\Omega_{k,n}(\mathbf{p})}}\right)\!\right]

(29)

\displaystyle\text{subject to }\quad 0\leq p_{k}\leq P_{max}.

The objective function of problem
P4
contains non-convex fractional terms. We now aim to solve
P4
by proposing a surrogate function that lower bounds the objective. The relevant surrogate function is obtained by substituting
N_{k}(\mathbf{x})=y_{k,n}\sqrt{\Delta_{k,n}(\mathbf{p})(1+\gamma_{k,n})}
and
D_{k}(\mathbf{x})=y_{k,n}^{2}\big(\Delta_{k,n}(\mathbf{p})+\Omega_{k,n}(\mathbf{p})\big)
in Lemma
3
, and is given as

\displaystyle{\overline{R}}_{n}(\mathbf{p},\gamma_{k,n},\mathbf{y}_{n})=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\log_{2}(1+\gamma_{k,n})-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\ln{2}}\Big(\gamma_{k,n}-\big(2y_{k,n}\sqrt{\Delta_{k,n}(\mathbf{p})(1+\gamma_{k,n})}\Big.

\displaystyle\Big.-y_{k,n}^{2}(\Delta_{k,n}(\mathbf{p})+\Omega_{k,n}(\mathbf{p}))\big)\Big).

(30)

Here
\mathbf{y}_{n}=[y_{1,n},\cdots,y_{K,n}]^{T}
with
y_{k,n}
being a function of the feasible point
\big(\mathbf{p}^{(t)},\gamma_{k,n}^{(t)}\big)
:
y_{k,n}^{(t)}=\frac{\sqrt{\Delta_{k,n}(\mathbf{p}^{(t)})(1+\gamma_{k,n}^{(t)})}}{\Delta_{k,n}(\mathbf{p}^{(t)})+\Omega_{k,n}(\mathbf{p}^{(t)})}
. The surrogate function
{\overline{R}}_{n}(\mathbf{p},\gamma_{k,n},\mathbf{y}_{n})
can be shown to follow conditions C1 and C2, on the lines similar to that of
\widetilde{R}_{n}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{y}_{n})
. The resultant problem is, therefore, given as

\displaystyle\textbf{P5:}\;\;\underset{\mathbf{p},\gamma_{k,n}}{\text{Maximize}}\quad

\displaystyle{\overline{R}}_{n}(\mathbf{p},\gamma_{k,n},\mathbf{y}_{n})\quad\text{subject to}\quad 0\leq p_{k}\leq P_{max}.

(31)

Problem
P5
, for a fixed
\mathbf{p}
, is convex in
\gamma_{k,n}
and its optimal value is given as
\gamma_{k,n}^{\ast}=\Delta_{k,n}(\mathbf{p})/\Omega_{k,n}(\mathbf{p})
. We now provide the closed-form expression to calculate optimal power in the following lemma.

Lemma 4
.

For a fixed
\gamma_{k,n}
, problem
P5
is concave in
p_{k}
, and the optimal value of
p_{k}
, obtained using the first-order optimality condition, is given as

\displaystyle p_{k}

\displaystyle=\min\left(P_{{max}},\frac{y_{k,n}^{2}\left(1+\gamma_{k,n}\right)\alpha_{d,k}^{2}|\mathbf{a}_{k}^{H}[n]\boldsymbol{\delta}_{k,n}|^{2}}{\left(\alpha_{d,k}^{2}y_{k,n}^{2}|\mathbf{a}_{k}^{H}[n]\boldsymbol{\delta}_{k,n}|^{2}+l_{k}^{d}\right)^{2}}\right).

(32)

Here

l_{k}^{d}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{K}\mathbf{a}_{i}^{H}[n]\Big(y_{i,n}^{2}\alpha_{d,i}(1+\kappa_{t,i}^{2})\mathbf{C}_{ik,n}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{K}y_{i,n}^{2}(\kappa^{2}_{r,m}\mathbf{D}_{ik,n}+\overline{\mathbf{D}}_{ik,n})\Big)\mathbf{a}_{i}[n]+\mathbf{a}_{k}^{H}[n]y_{k,n}^{2}(\mathbf{B}_{k,n}+\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k,n}-\alpha_{d,k}^{2}\mathbf{C}_{kk,n})\mathbf{a}_{k}[n]

Proof.

VI
Conclusion

We derived a closed-form SE expression for a hardware-impaired spatially-correlated Rician-faded CF mMIMO system with channel aging and two-layer LSFD. We verified this expression for different transmit power and hardware impairment values. We optimized the non-convex SE metric by proposing two novel optimization techniques. The first one has a high complexity, while the second provides a closed-form solution with a trivial complexity. We numerically showed that the two-layer LSFD effectively mitigates the interference due to channel aging for both low- and high-velocity UEs. It, however, mitigates inter-user interference only for the low-velocity UEs, and not the fast ones. We also showed that the SE loss due to hardware impairments can be compensated by the proposed dynamic ADC architecture.

Balanced blow-ups and cliques in
G_{R}(k)

Balanced blow-ups and cliques

For any graph
G
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
, the
(balanced) blow-up of order
m
of
G
, denoted
G^{(m)}
, is the graph obtained by replacing each vertex
x
of
G
by a set
V_{x}
of
m
independent vertices and every edge
\{x,y\}
of
G
by a complete bipartite graph
K_{m,m}
with parts
V_{x}
and
V_{y}
(of course
G^{(1)}=G
).
Notice that we have the natural isomorphism

(2.1)

G\otimes\mathring{K}_{m}\simeq G^{(m)},

where
\mathring{\Gamma}
denotes a graph
\Gamma
with a loop added at each vertex.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a simple undirected graph and let
m,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. Then,

\mathcal{K}_{\ell}(G^{(m)})=\mathcal{K}_{\ell}(G)\cdot m^{\ell}.

Proof.

Notice that any clique of size
\ell
of
G^{(m)}
induces a clique of the same size in
G
. Indeed the vertices of this clique in
G^{(m)}
must belong to different
independent sets of the blow-up, since if two of them belong to the same independent set, so they are not neighbors.
Conversely, given a clique
K_{\ell}
in
G
, by blowing up of its vertices we can obtain

|m|^{\ell}
distinct cliques in
G^{(m)}
.
The above argument shows that all of the cliques in
G^{(m)}
can be obtained in this way.
Hence, by the so called multiplication principle of combinatorics we have that

\mathcal{K}_{\ell}(G^{(m)})=\mathcal{K}_{\ell}(G)\cdot m^{\ell},

as asserted.
∎

Example 2.2
.

For
\ell,m,n\in\mathbb{N}
we have that

\mathcal{K}_{\ell}(K_{n}^{(m)})=\tbinom{n}{\ell}m^{\ell}.

Indeed, by taking into account that any choice of
\ell
vertices in
K_{n}
determines a clique in
K_{n}

and all of the cliques in
K_{n}
are determined uniquely in this way,
we have that
\mathcal{K}_{\ell}(K_{n})=\binom{n}{\ell}
.
The assertion follows immediately from the above proposition.

As a consequence of the previous proposition we obtain the following.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
G
be a simple undirected graph.
Then,
\omega(G^{(m)})=\omega(G)
for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.1
we have that
\mathcal{K}_{\ell}(G^{(m)})=0
if and only if
\mathcal{K}_{\ell}(G)=0
.
The corollary follows directly from (
1.3
).
∎

(

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
p
be a prime with
p\equiv 1\pmod{3}
.
If there is a minimal
t\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(3.1)

p^{t}=X^{2}+27Y^{2}

has integral solutions

with
(x,p)=1
,
then
\mathcal{K}_{3}(\Gamma(3,p^{t\ell+s}))
, with
\ell\geq 1
and

,
is determined by the numbers
\mathcal{K}_{3}(\Gamma(3,p^{t}))
and
\mathcal{K}_{3}(\Gamma(3,p^{s}))
.

Proof.

Let
t
be minimal in
\mathbb{N}
such that (
3.1
) has an integral solution
x,y
with
(x,p)=1
. Notice that if
(x,y)
is a solution of (
3.1
) then
(x,-y)
and

are also solutions. Also, from (

) we have that

since

and hence
x\equiv\pm 1\pmod{3}
. Thus, we will choose one solution
(x_{0},y_{0})
, with
x_{0}\in\{\pm x\}
and
y_{0}\in\{\pm y\}
, such that
x_{0}\equiv 1\pmod{3}
.

Considering the complex number

(3.2)

we have that
\|z_{x,y}\|^{2}=x^{2}+27y^{2}=p^{t}
and hence

(3.3)

for any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. Now, we will express

in the form given in (

).
For any

where

and

,

.
For instance,

Abstract
. We consider quasilinear wave equations in ( 1 + 3 ) (1+3) -dimensions where the nonlinearity F ⁡ ( u , u ′ , u ′′ ) F(u,u^{\prime},u^{\prime\prime}) is permitted to depend on the solution rather than just its derivatives. For scalar equations, if ( ∂ u 2 F ) ​ ( 0 , 0 , 0 ) = 0 (\partial_{u}^{2}F)(0,0,0)=0 , almost global existence was established by Lindblad. We seek to show a related almost global existence result for coupled systems of such equations. To do so, we will rely upon a variant of the r p r^{p} -weighted local energy estimate of Dafermos and Rodnianski that includes a ghost weight akin to those used by Alinhac. The decay that is needed to close the argument comes from space-time Klainerman-Sobolev type estimates from the work of Metcalfe, Tataru, and Tohaneanu.



1.
Introduction

In this article, we shall examine long-time existence for
systems of
(1+3)
-dimensional quasilinear wave equations with small
initial data where the nonlinearity is
permitted to depend on the solution rather than just its derivatives.
In particular, for
\Box=\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta
, we shall examine

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\Box u^{I}=F^{I}(u,u^{\prime},u^{\prime\prime}),\quad(t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3},\quad I=1,2,\dots,M,\\
u(0,{\,\cdot\,})=f,\quad\partial_{t}u(0,{\,\cdot\,})=g.\end{cases}

Here
u=(u^{1},\dots,u^{M})
.
We use the notation
u^{\prime}=\partial u=(\partial_{t}u,\nabla u)
for the
space-time gradient.
The smooth function
F
vanishes to second order at the
origin, and it is linear in the
u^{\prime\prime}
components. Moreover, we shall
assume that

(1.2)

(\partial_{u^{J}}\partial_{u^{K}}F^{I})(0,0,0)=0,\quad\forall\,I,J,K=1,2,\dots,M,

which has the effect of disallowing
u^{2}
-type terms at the quadratic
level for
F
. In
[
11
]
, almost global existence,
which shows that the lifespan grows exponentially as the size of the
data shrinks, was proved for scalar equations. In the current
article, we seek similar lower bounds on the lifespan for systems.

In the sequel, we use Einstein’s summation convention. Repeated Greek
letters
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
are understood to sum from
0
to
3

where
x_{0}=t
. Repeated lower case Roman letters
i,j,k
are summed
from
1
to
3
, and repeated upper case letters
I,J,K
will be
summed from
1
to
M
.

For simplicity, we shall truncate the nonlinearity at the quadratic
level:

(1.3)

F^{I}(u,u^{\prime},u^{\prime\prime})=a^{I,\alpha}_{JK}u^{J}\partial_{\alpha}u^{K}+b^{I,\alpha\beta}_{JK}\partial_{\alpha}u^{J}\partial_{\beta}u^{K}+A^{I,\alpha\beta}_{JK}u^{K}\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}u^{J}+B^{I,\alpha\beta\gamma}_{JK}\partial_{\gamma}u^{K}\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}u^{J}.

In the small data regime, higher order terms are better behaved.
The constants will be assumed to satisfy the symmetry conditions

(1.4)

A^{I,\alpha\beta}_{JK}=A^{I,\beta\alpha}_{JK}=A^{J,\alpha\beta}_{IK},\quad B^{I,\alpha\beta\gamma}_{JK}=B^{I,\beta\alpha\gamma}_{JK}=B^{J,\alpha\beta\gamma}_{IK}.

Our main result is the following statement of almost global existence.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
f,g\in(C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))^{M}
. Moreover, assume that the smooth function
F
vanishes to second order
at the origin, satisfies (
1.2
), and is subject to the
symmetry conditions (
1.4
). Then, for
N\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently
large, there are constants
c,\varepsilon_{0}>0
so that if

(1.5)

\sum_{|\mu|\leq N+1}\|\partial^{\mu}f\|_{L^{2}}+\sum_{|\mu|\leq N}\|\partial^{\mu}g\|_{L^{2}}\leq\varepsilon

with
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
,
then (
1.1
) has a unique solution with
u\in(C^{\infty}([0,T_{\varepsilon}]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}))^{M}
where

(1.6)

T_{\varepsilon}=\exp(c/\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}).

To aid the exposition, we have restricted to the case of compactly
supported initial data. Without loss of generality, we shall take

(1.7)

\text{supp}\,f^{I},\text{supp}\,g^{I}\subset\{|x|\leq 2\},\quad\forall\,I=1,2,\dots,M.

Small data in a sufficiently weighted space
would also suffice. In
[
11
]
, the lower bound of the lifespan was

\exp(c/\varepsilon)
. The difference in the lifespan between

[
11
]
and Theorem
1.1
is primarily
due to a logarithmic loss that occurs as a part of an endpoint Hardy
inequality. See Lemma
2.5
. While refinements of our argument to improve
the power in (
1.6
) are likely
possible, it is not clear
what the sharp power is.

Equations such as (
1.1
) with nonlinearity that depends
on the solution rather than just its derivatives do not mesh as simply
with the energy methods that are typically employed to prove long-time
existence. In
[
7
]
, without the hypothesis (
1.2
),
a lower bound of
\exp(c/\varepsilon)
was established in

(1+4)
-dimensions. For scalar equations, the additional hypothesis
(
1.2
) was, moreover, found to be sufficient to guarantee global
existence for sufficiently small data. The analogous results in

(1+3)
-dimensions appeared in
[
11
]
where the lifespan was
shown to exceed
c/\varepsilon^{2}
without (
1.2
) and almost
global existence was provided for scalar equations.

In both
[
7
]
and
[
11
]
, the restriction to
scalar equations is necessitated by the use of the chain rule to write

u\cdot\partial u=\frac{1}{2}\partial u^{2}
interactions in
divergence form. This special form, in turn, allows for easier
estimation of the solution rather than only its derivatives. See,
e.g.,
[
11
, Proposition 1.8]
.
The article

[
13
]
extended the result of
[
7
]
by establishing small data global existence for systems
(
1.1
) subject to (
1.2
) in
(1+4)
-dimensions.
We establish the
(1+3)
-dimensional analog here.

The principle source of decay in our proof is obtained from space-time
Klainerman-Sobolev estimates as were proved in
[
20
]
. This will
be paired with variants of the integrated local energy decay
estimates. In
[
2
]
,
r^{p}
-weighted local energy
estimates, which provide improved bounds on the “good” components of
the gradient:
\partial_{t}+\partial_{r}
and
{\not\negmedspace\nabla}:=\nabla-\frac{x}{r}\partial_{r}
, were proved. These will be combined with a ghost weight,
which originates from
[
1
]
. This permits a
further improvement of the bounds in the vicinity of the light cone and
meshes particularly well with the space-time Klainerman-Sobolev
estimates of
[
20
]
.

While the proof uses the method of invariant vector fields, it will
not rely on the Lorentz boosts. While Lorentz boosts are perfectly
acceptable for systems such as (
1.1
), they can limit
further extensions to, e.g., multiple speed settings, equations in
exterior domains, or equations on stationary background geometries.
Our proof is readily adaptable to Dirichlet wave equations exterior
to, say, star-shaped obstacles. We do not include these extraneous
details here. When combined with
[
4
]
,
[
3
]
, and

[
16
]
, it (largely) completes the extension of the long-time
existence results of
[
7
]
and
[
11
]
to exterior
domains. The work on systems in
[
13
]
was also completed
exterior to star-shaped obstacles. The restriction to star-shaped
obstacles is likely a convenience. It is anticipated that any geometry
that permits a sufficiently rapid decay of local energy would
suffice. See, e.g.,
[
5
,
6
]
.

The key aspects to the proof are to effectively bound the solution
u

when typical energy methods estimate
\partial u
and to obtain
additional decay from the derivative that must be present in at least
one factor of every nonlinear term. In particular, it was the former
that restricted the analysis to scalar equations in preceding results.
The
r^{p}
-weights and ghost weights help with both aspects. In
particular, when combined with rather standard Hardy-type estimates,
improved bounds on the local energy of the solution without
derivatives, when compared to e.g. (
2.2
), result. When
attempting to gain additional decay from the derivative that must
appear in at least one factor of every term of the nonlinearity, one
often relies on the scaling vector field, which near the light cone
gives additional decay in
t-|x|
. The ghost weight allows us to take
advantage of this additional decay off of the light cone.

Since derivatives can be exchanged for extra decay using the scaling
vector field and since the
r^{p}
-weighted and ghost weighted estimates
allow for much larger weights for the good derivatives, which in
essence provides additional decay that can be used on other factors,
one can quickly become convinced that the worst possible nonlinear
terms are of the form
u(\partial_{t}-\partial_{r})u
. Moreover when all
of the vector fields land on the differentiated factor, one cannot
afford to lose the additional vector field that would result from
using the scaling vector field to get additional decay. In this case,
we move the
\partial_{t}-\partial_{r}
using integration by parts.
Within the local energy estimates, it could land on the weights or the
lower order factor, which allows us to gain additional decay, in the
latter case by using the scaling vector field. Additionally it could
land on the multiplier, which has the basic form

\partial_{t}+\partial_{r}
and modulo better behaved terms results in

\Box
effectively turning these quadratic interactions into better cubic
interactions.

This article is organized as follows. In the next subsection, we shall
gather some notation and preliminary results that will be used
frequently throughout the paper. In Section 2, the integrated local
energy estimates will be proved. Section 3 contains our sources of
decay, which are primarily space-time versions of the
Klainerman-Sobolev inequality. Finally, the main theorem is proved in
Section 4.

1.1.
Notation

The vector fields that we rely on are

Z=(\partial_{t},\partial_{1},\partial_{2},\partial_{3},\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2},\Omega_{3},S)

where

S=t\partial_{t}+r\partial_{r},\quad\Omega=x\times\nabla

represent the scaling vector field and generators of (spatial)
rotations, respectively.
We will frequently rely on the orthogonal decomposition

\nabla=\frac{x}{r}\partial_{r}+{\not\negmedspace\nabla},

and we shall use
{\not\negmedspace\partial}=(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r},{\not\negmedspace\nabla})
as an
abbreviation for the “good” derivatives. We note that

{\not\negmedspace\nabla}=-\frac{x}{r^{2}}\times\Omega,

and as such,

(1.8)

|{\not\negmedspace\nabla}u|\leq\frac{1}{r}|Zu|.

Moreover, the following commutator will be used frequently in the
proofs of local energy estimates, as it was in the seminal work

[
21
]
:

(1.9)

[\nabla,\partial_{r}]=[{\not\negmedspace\nabla},\partial_{r}]=\frac{1}{r}{\not\negmedspace\nabla}.

The admissible vector fields are well-known to satisfy

(1.10)

[\Box,\partial]=[\Box,\Omega_{j}]=0,\quad[\Box,S]=2\Box.

Moreover, we have

(1.11)

[Z,\partial]\in\text{span}(\partial),\quad|[Z,{\not\negmedspace\partial}]u|\leq\frac{1}{r}|Zu|+|{\not\negmedspace\partial}u|.

We shall abbreviate

|Z^{\leq N}u|=\sum_{|\mu|\leq N}|Z^{\mu}u|,\quad|\partial^{\leq N}u|=\sum_{|\mu|\leq N}|\partial^{\mu}u|.

We use
L^{p}L^{q}
as an abbreviation for
L^{p}_{t}L^{q}_{x}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3})=L^{p}([0,T];L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))
. In several circumstances, it will be
convenient to do an inhomogeneous dyadic decomposition of
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, and
for this purpose we denote

A_{R}=\{R<|x|<2R\},\quad\tilde{A}_{R}=\Bigl\{\frac{7}{8}R<|x|<\frac{17}{8}R\Bigr\}\quad\text{if }R>1,

and
A_{1}=\{|x|<2\}
,
\tilde{A}_{1}=\{|x|<17/4\}
.
For the standard integrated local energy estimates,
we shall employ the following notations from

[
24
]
,
[
20
]
:

\|u\|_{LE}=\sup_{j\geq 0}2^{-j/2}\|u\|_{L^{2}L^{2}([0,T]\times A_{2^{j}})},\quad\|u\|_{LE^{1}}=\|(\partial u,|x|^{-1}u)\|_{LE}.

In the proof of the local energy estimates, we will often desire a
C^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
,
bounded, nondecreasing function and for these purposes set

\sigma_{U}(z)=\frac{z}{U+|z|},\quad U>0.

In the sequel, we shall also need dyadic decompositions in
t-r
, so
we set

X_{U}=\{(t,x)\in[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\,:\,U<t-r<2U\}\>\text{for $U>1$},\quad X_{1}=\{(t,x)\in[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\,:\,|t-r|<2\},

with a similar enlargement being denoted by
\tilde{X}_{U}
.

The estimates from
[
20
]
rely on a mixed decomposition where the cone is
divided in
|x|
away from the light cone and in
t-|x|
near the
cone. We set
C=\{r\leq t+2\}
.
Due to the simplifying assumption that the data are

[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
in a straightforward fashion.

We first divide into dyadic intervals in time

C_{\tau}=\{t\in[\tau,2\tau]\cap[0,T],r\leq t+2\}
. Next, we shall decompose

or

, we set

C^{R}_{\tau}=C_{\tau}\cap\{R<r<2R\},\quad C_{\tau}^{U}=C_{\tau}\cap\{U<t-r<2U\}

and

C^{R=1}_{\tau}=C_{\tau}\cap\{r<2\},\quad C^{U=1}_{\tau}=C_{\tau}\cap\{|t-r|<2\}.

We note that

C_{\tau}=\Bigl(\bigcup_{1\leq R\leq\tau/4}C_{\tau}^{R}\Bigr)\cup\Bigl(\bigcup_{1\leq U\leq\tau/4}C^{U}_{\tau}\Bigr)\cup C^{\frac{\tau}{2}}_{\tau},

where

C^{\frac{\tau}{2}}_{\tau}=C_{\tau}\cap\{t-r\geq\frac{\tau}{2}\}\cap\{r\geq\frac{\tau}{2}\}.

We use
\tilde{C}^{R}_{\tau}
,
\tilde{C}_{\tau}^{U}
to

\tau

[(7/8)\tau,2\tau]\cap[0,T]

\tilde{\tilde{C}}^{R}_{\tau}
and

\langle r\rangle\approx R,\quad t-r\approx\tau,\quad\text{ on }C^{R}_{\tau},\tilde{C}^{R}_{\tau},\tilde{\tilde{C}}^{R}_{\tau},\quad\text{with }1\leq R\leq\tau/4

In this sense, the
C^{\frac{\tau}{2}}_{\tau}
region can be thought of as either a

C^{R}_{\tau}
or a
C^{U}_{\tau}
region.
Here and throughout,

Here we have set

to be a smooth function so that
\langle r\rangle\geq 3
and

\langle r\rangle=(3+r)
and

In the sequel, we shall need cutoffs to localize to certain regions.
We fix

\beta\equiv 1

\beta_{<R}(z)=\beta(z/R),\quad\beta_{>R}(z)=1-\beta_{<R}(z).

2.
Local energy estimate

In this section, we shall collect some integrated local energy
estimates, which will serve as the main linear estimate for our proof
of almost global existence. The first of these is a now standard version of the
original estimates of
[
21
]
. We proceed to explore a
variant of this that only bounds the “good” derivatives but with
much better weights. What results is a mixture of the ghost weight
method of
[
1
]
(see also the related estimates
in
[
12
]
) and the
r^{p}
-weighted local
energy estimates of
[
2
]
.

In this examination of a quasilinear problem, it will be helpful to
have estimates on small, time-dependent perturbations of the flat
operator
\Box
. To this end, we define

(\Box_{h}u)^{I}=(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)u^{I}-h^{I,\alpha\beta}_{J}(t,x)\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}u^{J}.

Here we shall assume that

(2.1)

h^{I,\alpha\beta}_{J}=h^{I,\beta\alpha}_{J}=h^{J,\alpha\beta}_{I},\qquad h^{I,\alpha\beta}_{J}\in C^{1}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}).

We shall also abbreviate

|h|=\sum_{I,J=1}^{M}\sum_{\alpha,\beta=0}^{3}|h^{I,\alpha\beta}_{J}|,\quad|\partial h|=\sum_{I,J=1}^{M}\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma=0}^{3}|\partial_{\gamma}h^{I,\alpha\beta}_{J}|.

We begin by recalling the local energy estimate for perturbations of

\Box
that was proved in
[
15
]
. See, also,

[
23
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
19
,
18
]
, and
[
17
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose that
h
satisfies (
2.1
), that
|h|

is sufficiently small,
that
u\in(C^{2}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}))^{M}
, and that for all
t\in[0,T]
,
|\partial^{\leq 1}u(t,x)|\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
. Then,

(2.2)

\|\partial u\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}+\|u\|^{2}_{LE^{1}}\lesssim\|\partial u(0,{\,\cdot\,})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\int_{0}^{T}\int|\Box_{h}u|\Bigl(|\partial u|+\frac{|u|}{r}\Bigr)\,dx\,dt\\
+\int_{0}^{T}\int\Bigl(|\partial h|+\frac{|h|}{\langle r\rangle}\Bigr)|\partial u|\Bigl(|\partial u|+\frac{|u|}{r}\Bigr)\,dx\,dt.

The proof of the theorem follows by pairing
\Box_{h}u
with the
multiplier
C\partial_{t}u+\sigma_{2^{j}}(r)\partial_{r}u+\frac{\sigma_{2^{j}}(r)}{r}u
, integrating over a space-time slab, and
integrating by parts.

We next consider the following variant of Theorem
2.1
.
It represents a combination of the ideas of
[
2
]

and
[
1
]
. The former considered multipliers
with principal part of the form
r^{p}(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r})u
.
Rather than considering associated flux terms to bound terms similar
to the third term in the left side below, we shall instead modify the
multiplier using a ghost weight, which originates in

[
1
]
. This more readily allows us to perform
necessary manipulations to prevent a loss of regularity due to the
quasilinear nature of the problem. It will also allow us to
subsequently integrate by parts to control a term using ideas akin to
normal forms.

Theorem 2.2
.

Fix
0<p<2
. Suppose that
h
satisfies (
2.1
),
that
u\in(C^{2}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}))^{M}
, and that for
all
t\in[0,T]
,
|r^{\frac{p+2}{2}}\partial^{\leq 1}u(t,x)|\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
. Then for any
U>0
,

(2.3)

\|r^{\frac{p-2}{2}}{\not\negmedspace\partial}(ru)\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}+\|r^{\frac{p-3}{2}}{\not\negmedspace\partial}(ru)\|^{2}_{L^{2}L^{2}}+\|r^{\frac{p-2}{2}}(\sigma^{\prime}_{U}(t-r))^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r})(ru)\|^{2}_{L^{2}L^{2}}\\
\lesssim\|r^{\frac{p-2}{2}}{\not\negmedspace\partial}(ru)(0,{\,\cdot\,})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int r^{p}|h||\partial u|\Bigl(|\partial u|+\frac{|u|}{r}\Bigr)\,dx\\
+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Bigl|\int_{0}^{t}\int r^{p-1}e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}\Box_{h}u\cdot\Bigl(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r}\Bigr)(ru)\,dx\,dt\Bigr|+\int_{0}^{T}\int r^{p-1}\Bigl(|\partial h|+\frac{|h|}{r}\Bigr)|\partial u||(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r})(ru)|\,dx\,dt\\
+\int_{0}^{T}\int r^{p-1}|h||\partial u|\Bigl(|{\not\negmedspace\nabla}u|+\frac{|u|}{r}\Bigr)\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\int|h|r^{p-1}\sigma_{U}^{\prime}(t-r)|\partial u||(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r})(ru)|\,dx\,dt\\
+\int_{0}^{T}\int r^{p}\Bigl(|(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r})h|+\frac{|h|}{r}\Bigr)|\partial u|^{2}\,dx\,dt.

The implicit constant is independent of both
T
and
U
.

Proof.

We first note that

\int_{0}^{t}\int(\Box u)^{I}r^{p}e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}\Bigl(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r}+\frac{1}{r}\Bigr)u^{I}\,dx\,dt\\
=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\Bigl(\partial_{t}^{2}-\partial_{r}^{2}-{\not\negmedspace\nabla}\cdot{\not\negmedspace\nabla}\Bigr)(ru)^{I}\cdot r^{p}e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}\Bigl(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r}\Bigr)(ru)^{I}\,d\omega\,dr\,dt.

Using integration by parts, we see that the right side is equal to

\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}r^{p}e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}\Bigl(\partial_{t}-\partial_{r}\Bigr)\Bigl|\Bigl(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r}\Bigr)(ru)\Bigr|^{2}\,d\omega\,dr\,dt\\
+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}r^{p}e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}{\not\negmedspace\nabla}(ru)^{I}\cdot{\not\negmedspace\nabla}\Bigl(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r}\Bigr)(ru)^{I}\,d\omega\,dr\,dt.

Relying on (
1.9
), we
further see that this is the same as

\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}r^{p}e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}\Bigl|\Bigl(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r}\Bigr)(ru)\Bigr|^{2}\,d\omega\,dr\Bigl|_{t=0}^{t}+\frac{p}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}r^{p-1}e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}\Bigl|\Bigl(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r}\Bigr)(ru)\Bigr|^{2}\,d\omega\,dr\,dt\\
+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}r^{p}\sigma_{U}^{\prime}(t-r)e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}\Bigl|\Bigl(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r}\Bigr)(ru)\Bigr|^{2}\,d\omega\,dr\,dt+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}r^{p-1}e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}|{\not\negmedspace\nabla}(ru)|^{2}\,d\omega\,dr\,dt\\
+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}r^{p}e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}\Bigl(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r}\Bigr)|{\not\negmedspace\nabla}(ru)|^{2}\,d\omega\,dr\,dt.

A final integration by parts then gives that

(2.4)

\int_{0}^{t}\int(\Box u)^{I}r^{p}e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}\Bigl(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r}+\frac{1}{r}\Bigr)u^{I}\,dx\,dt\\
=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}r^{p}e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}\Bigl(\Bigl|\Bigl(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r}\Bigr)(ru)\Bigr|^{2}+|{\not\negmedspace\nabla}(ru)|^{2}\Bigr)\,d\omega\,dr\Bigl|_{t=0}^{t}\\
+\frac{p}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}r^{p-1}e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}\Bigl|\Bigl(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r}\Bigr)(ru)\Bigr|^{2}\,d\omega\,dr\,dt\\
+\Bigl(1-\frac{p}{2}\Bigr)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}r^{p-1}e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}|{\not\negmedspace\nabla}(ru)|^{2}\,d\omega\,dr\,dt\\
+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}r^{p}\sigma_{U}^{\prime}(t-r)e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}\Bigl|\Bigl(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r}\Bigr)(ru)\Bigr|^{2}\,d\omega\,dr\,dt.

This string of equalities proves the desired estimate when
h\equiv 0
.

We now consider the perturbation. Using integration by parts, we note that

-\int_{0}^{t}\int h^{I,\alpha\beta}_{J}\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}u^{J}\cdot r^{p}e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}\Bigl(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r}+\frac{1}{r}\Bigr)u^{I}\,dx\,dt=-\int h^{I,0\beta}_{J}r^{p-1}e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}\partial_{\beta}u^{J}\Bigl(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r}\Bigr)(ru^{I})\,dx\Bigl|_{t=0}^{t}\\
+\int_{0}^{t}\int(\partial_{\alpha}h^{I,\alpha\beta}_{J})r^{p-1}e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}\partial_{\beta}u^{J}\Bigl(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r}\Bigr)(ru^{I})\,dx\,dt\\
+\int_{0}^{t}\int h^{I,\alpha\beta}_{J}r^{-1}\partial_{\alpha}\Bigl(r^{p}e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}\Bigr)\partial_{\beta}u^{J}\Bigl(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r}\Bigr)(ru^{I})\,dx\,dt\\
+\int_{0}^{t}\int h^{I,\alpha\beta}_{J}r^{p}e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}\partial_{\beta}u^{J}\partial_{\alpha}\Bigl(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r}+\frac{1}{r}\Bigr)u^{I}\,dx\,dt.

Commuting the
\partial_{\alpha}
using (
1.9
) and using the symmetries (
2.1
), we see that

\int_{0}^{t}\int h^{I,\alpha\beta}_{J}r^{p}e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}\partial_{\beta}u^{J}\partial_{\alpha}\Bigl(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r}+\frac{1}{r}\Bigr)u^{I}\,dx\,dt=\int_{0}^{t}\int h^{I,k\beta}_{J}r^{p-1}e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}\partial_{\beta}u^{J}{\not\negmedspace\nabla}_{k}u^{I}\,dx\,dt\\
-\int_{0}^{t}\int h^{I,k\beta}_{J}r^{p-2}e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}\frac{x_{k}}{r}u^{I}\partial_{\beta}u^{J}\,dx\,dt+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int h^{I,\alpha\beta}_{J}r^{p}e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}\Bigl(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r}+\frac{2}{r}\Bigr)\Bigl[\partial_{\beta}u^{J}\partial_{\alpha}u^{I}\Bigr]\,dx\,dt.

Combining the above two identities and integrating by parts, we obtain

(2.5)

-\int_{0}^{t}\int h^{I,\alpha\beta}_{J}\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}u^{J}\cdot r^{p}e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}\Bigl(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r}+\frac{1}{r}\Bigr)u^{I}\,dx\,dt\\
=-\int h^{I,0\beta}_{J}r^{p-1}e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}\partial_{\beta}u^{J}\Bigl(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r}\Bigr)(ru^{I})\,dx\Bigl|_{t=0}^{t}+\frac{1}{2}\int h^{I,\alpha\beta}_{J}r^{p}e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}\partial_{\beta}u^{J}\partial_{\alpha}u^{I}\,dx\Bigl|_{t=0}^{t}\\
+\int_{0}^{t}\int(\partial_{\alpha}h^{I,\alpha\beta}_{J})r^{p-1}e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}\partial_{\beta}u^{J}\Bigl(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r}\Bigr)(ru^{I})\,dx\,dt\\
+p\int_{0}^{t}\int h^{I,k\beta}_{J}\frac{x_{k}}{r}r^{p-2}e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}\partial_{\beta}u^{J}\Bigl(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r}\Bigr)(ru^{I})\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{t}\int h^{I,k\beta}_{J}r^{p-1}e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}\partial_{\beta}u^{J}{\not\negmedspace\nabla}_{k}u^{I}\,dx\,dt\\
-\int_{0}^{t}\int h^{I,k\beta}_{J}r^{p-2}e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}\frac{x_{k}}{r}u^{I}\partial_{\beta}u^{J}\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{t}\int h^{I,\alpha\beta}_{J}\omega_{\alpha}r^{p-1}\sigma_{U}^{\prime}(t-r)e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}\partial_{\beta}u^{J}(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r})(ru^{I})\,dx\,dt\\
-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int\Bigl(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r}\Bigr)(h^{I,\alpha\beta}_{J})r^{p}e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}\partial_{\beta}u^{J}\partial_{\alpha}u^{I}\,dx\,dt-\frac{p}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int h^{I,\alpha\beta}_{J}r^{p-1}e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}\partial_{\beta}u^{J}\partial_{\alpha}u^{I}\,dx\,dt.

Here
\omega=(-1,x/r)
.
Our estimate (
2.3
) is an immediate consequence of (
2.4
)
and (
2.5
) and taking the supremum over
t\in[0,T]
.
∎

We next consider a Hardy-type inequality to obtain associated bounds
on the solution, which are analogous to the bounds on

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
0<p<2
. Suppose
u\in C^{1}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and that for
every
t\in[0,T]
,
|r^{p/2}u(t,x)|\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
. Then

(2.6)

\|r^{\frac{p-3}{2}}u\|_{L^{2}L^{2}}+\|r^{\frac{p-2}{2}}u\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\lesssim\|r^{\frac{p-2}{2}}u(0,{\,\cdot\,})\|_{L^{2}}+\|r^{\frac{p-3}{2}}{\not\negmedspace\partial}(ru)\|_{L^{2}L^{2}}.

Proof.

By integrating by parts, for any
t\in[0,T]
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2-p}\int r^{p-2}u^{2}(t,x)\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int r^{p-3}u^{2}(\tau,x)\,dx\,d\tau

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2-p}\int r^{p-2}u^{2}(t,x)\,dx+\frac{1}{p-2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}(\partial_{\tau}+\partial_{r})(r^{p-2})(ru)^{2}\,dr\,d\omega\,d\tau

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2-p}\int r^{p-2}u^{2}(0,x)\,dx-\frac{2}{p-2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{p-2}(ru)(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r})(ru)\,dr\,d\omega\,d\tau.

As the Schwarz inequality allows us to bound the last term by

C\Bigl(\int_{0}^{t}\int r^{p-3}u^{2}\,dx\,d\tau\Bigr)^{1/2}\Bigl(\int_{0}^{t}\int r^{p-3}\Bigl[(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r})(ru)\Bigr]^{2}\,dx\,d\tau\Bigr)^{1/2}

and as the first factor can be bootstrapped, (
2.6
) follows
upon taking a supremum in
t\in[0,T]
.
∎

While we will not directly use the next lemma, which indicates the
form of the lower order bound with decay in
t-r
and
0<p<1
,
we include it for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
0\leq p<1
.
Suppose
u\in C^{1}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and that for every
t\in[0,T]
,

|u(t,x)|\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
. Then

(2.7)

\int_{0}^{T}\int r^{p-2}\sigma_{U}^{\prime}(t-r)e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}u^{2}\,dx\,dt+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int r^{p-1}\sigma_{U}^{\prime}(t-r)e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}u^{2}(t,x)\,dx\\
\lesssim\int r^{p-1}\sigma_{U}^{\prime}(-r)e^{-\sigma_{U}(-r)}u^{2}(0,x)\,dx+\int_{0}^{T}\int r^{p-2}\sigma_{U}^{\prime}(t-r)e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}\Bigl[(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r})(ru)\Bigr]^{2}\,dx\,dt.

Proof.

We argue similarly to the preceding lemma and apply integration by parts and
the Schwarz inequality to observe that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}

\displaystyle\int r^{p-2}\sigma_{U}^{\prime}(\tau-r)e^{-\sigma_{U}(\tau-r)}u^{2}\,dx\,d\tau+\frac{1}{1-p}\int r^{p-1}\sigma_{U}^{\prime}(t-r)e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}u^{2}(t,x)\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p-1}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}(\partial_{\tau}+\partial_{r})[r^{p-1}\sigma_{U}^{\prime}(\tau-r)e^{-\sigma_{U}(\tau-r)}](ru)^{2}\,dr\,d\omega\,d\tau

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\frac{1}{1-p}\int r^{p-1}\sigma_{U}^{\prime}(t-r)e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}u^{2}(t,x)\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-p}\int r^{p-1}\sigma_{U}^{\prime}(-r)e^{-\sigma_{U}(-r)}u^{2}(0,x)\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-\frac{2}{p-1}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{p-1}\sigma_{U}^{\prime}(\tau-r)e^{-\sigma_{U}(\tau-r)}ru(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r})(ru)\,dr\,d\omega\,d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\int r^{p-1}\sigma_{U}^{\prime}(-r)e^{-\sigma_{U}(-r)}u^{2}(0,x)\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+\Bigl(\int_{0}^{t}\int r^{p-2}\sigma_{U}^{\prime}(\tau-r)e^{-\sigma_{U}(\tau-r)}u^{2}\,dx\,d\tau\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigl(\int_{0}^{t}\int r^{p-2}\sigma_{U}^{\prime}(\tau-r)e^{-\sigma_{U}(\tau-r)}\Bigl[(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r})(ru)\Bigr]^{2}\,dx\,d\tau\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We may then bootstrap the first factor of the last term and take a
supremum in
t
to complete the argument.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose
u\in C^{1}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3})

and that for every
t\in[0,T]
,

|u(t,x)|\to 0
as

. Then

(2.8)

\int_{0}^{T}\int\beta_{>2}(r)\frac{1}{r(\log\langle r\rangle)^{2}}\sigma_{U}^{\prime}(t-r)e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}u^{2}\,dx\,dt\\
+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int\beta_{>2}(r)\frac{1}{\log\langle r\rangle}\sigma_{U}^{\prime}(t-r)e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}u^{2}(t,x)\,dx\\
\lesssim\int\frac{1}{\log\langle r\rangle}\sigma_{U}^{\prime}(-r)e^{-\sigma_{U}(-r)}u^{2}(0,x)\,dx\\
+\int_{0}^{T}\int r^{-1}\sigma_{U}^{\prime}(t-r)e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}\Bigl[\Bigl(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r}\Bigr)(ru)\Bigr]^{2}\,dx\,dt+\|u\|^{2}_{LE^{1}}.

Proof.

We observe that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int

\displaystyle\beta_{>2}(r)\frac{1}{r(\log r)^{2}}\sigma_{U}^{\prime}(\tau-r)e^{-\sigma_{U}(\tau-r)}u^{2}\,dx\,d\tau+\int\beta_{>2}(r)\frac{1}{\log r}\sigma_{U}^{\prime}(t-r)e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}u^{2}(t,x)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\beta_{>2}(r)\Bigl(\partial_{\tau}+\partial_{r}\Bigr)\Bigl[-\frac{1}{\log r}\sigma_{U}^{\prime}(\tau-r)e^{-\sigma_{U}(\tau-r)}\Bigr](ru)^{2}\,dr\,d\omega\,d\tau

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\int\beta_{>2}(r)\frac{1}{\log r}\sigma_{U}^{\prime}(t-r)e^{-\sigma_{U}(t-r)}u^{2}(t,x)\,dx

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\beta_{>2}(r)\frac{1}{\log r}\sigma_{U}^{\prime}(\tau-r)e^{-\sigma_{U}(\tau-r)}(ru)\Bigl(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r}\Bigr)(ru)\,dr\,d\omega\,d\tau

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int\beta_{>2}(r)\frac{1}{\log r}\sigma_{U}^{\prime}(-r)e^{-\sigma_{U}(-r)}u^{2}(0,x)\,dx+\int_{0}^{t}\int\beta_{>2}^{\prime}(r)\frac{1}{\log r}\sigma_{U}^{\prime}(\tau-r)e^{-\sigma_{U}(\tau-r)}u^{2}\,dx\,d\tau.

As
\text{supp}\,\beta^{\prime}_{>2}\subset[2,4]
, the last term is bounded by
\|u\|_{LE^{1}}^{2}
.
The Schwarz inequality shows that the first term in the right side is

\lesssim\Bigl(\int_{0}^{t}\int\beta_{>2}(r)\frac{1}{r(\log r)^{2}}\sigma_{U}^{\prime}(\tau-r)e^{-\sigma_{U}(\tau-r)}u^{2}\,dx\,d\tau\Bigr)^{1/2}\\
\times\Bigl(\int_{0}^{t}\int\beta_{>2}(r)r^{-1}\sigma_{U}^{\prime}(\tau-r)e^{-\sigma_{U}(\tau-r)}\Big[\Bigl(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r}\Bigr)(ru)\Bigr]^{2}\,dx\,d\tau\Bigr)^{1/2}.

The first factor may be bootstrapped, and upon taking a supremum over

t\in[0,T]
, the proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Suppose that

is supported where

(2.11)

\int\frac{1}{(1+r)(t-r+3)^{2}}u^{2}\,dx\lesssim\int\frac{1}{(1+r)r^{2}}u^{2}\,dx+\int\frac{1}{(1+r)}(\partial_{r}u)^{2}\,dx.

Proof.

For

\displaystyle\int\frac{1}{(1+r)(t-r+3)^{2}}

The main decay estimate that we shall rely upon is a space-time variant of the

[

]
that was established in

]

As is described in Section

Lemma 3.1
.

For any

and

\displaystyle\|w\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}(C^{R}_{\tau})}

(3.2)

\displaystyle\|w\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}(C_{\tau}^{U})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\tau^{\frac{3}{2}}U^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|Z^{\leq 4}w\|_{L^{2}L^{2}(\tilde{C}^{U}_{\tau})}+\frac{1}{\tau^{\frac{3}{2}}U^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r})(rZ^{\leq 3}w)\|_{L^{2}L^{2}(\tilde{C}^{U}_{\tau})},

(3.3)

\displaystyle\|w\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}(C^{\frac{\tau}{2}}_{\tau})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}\|Z^{\leq 4}w\|_{L^{2}L^{2}(\tilde{C}^{\frac{\tau}{2}}_{\tau})}+\frac{1}{\tau}\|(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r})Z^{\leq 3}w\|_{L^{2}L^{2}(\tilde{C}^{\frac{\tau}{2}}_{\tau})}

See
[

. The proof of (

\|w\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}(C^{R}_{\tau})}\lesssim\frac{1}{\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}R^{\frac{3}{2}}}\|Z^{\leq 3}w\|_{L^{2}L^{2}(\tilde{C}^{R}_{\tau})}+\frac{1}{\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}R}\|Z^{\leq 3}w\|^{1/2}_{L^{2}L^{2}(\tilde{C}^{R}_{\tau})}\|\partial_{r}Z^{\leq 3}w\|^{1/2}_{L^{2}L^{2}(\tilde{C}^{R}_{\tau})}

\begin{cases}\Box u_{k}^{I}=a^{I,\alpha}_{JK}u_{k-1}^{J}\partial_{\alpha}u_{k-1}^{K}+b^{I,\alpha\beta}_{JK}\partial_{\alpha}u_{k-1}^{J}\partial_{\beta}u_{k-1}^{K}+A^{I,\alpha\beta}_{JK}u_{k-1}^{K}\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}u_{k}^{J}+B^{I,\alpha\beta\gamma}_{JK}\partial_{\gamma}u_{k-1}^{K}\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}u_{k}^{J},\\

Note that the right side of the equation is

converges. The limit

We shall work with

To show that the sequence is convergent, we first show a

4.1.
Boundedness

For any fixed

, we set

+\sup_{U}\|r^{-\frac{1}{2}}\langle t-r\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial_{t}+\partial_{r})(rZ^{\leq 60}u_{k})\|_{L^{2}L^{2}(X_{U})}+\sup_{U}\|r^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\log\langle r\rangle)^{-1}\langle t-r\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}Z^{\leq 60}u_{k}\|_{L^{2}L^{2}(X_{U})}\\
+\Bigl(\sum_{\tau\leq T}\sum_{R\leq\tau/2}\|\partial Z^{\leq 50}u_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}L^{2}(\tilde{C}^{R}_{\tau})}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\Bigl[\sum_{\tau\leq T}\sum_{R\leq\tau/2}\Bigl(R\|{\not\negmedspace\partial}\partial Z^{\leq 40}u_{k}\|_{L^{2}L^{2}(\tilde{C}^{R}_{\tau})}\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr]^{\frac{1}{2}}\\

,

respectively. We shall argue inductively to show that

4.2.
Convergence

We now conclude the proof by showing that the sequence is Cauchy.
This is somewhat simplified by the extremely high regularity that was

We use that

\Box u_{k}-\Box u_{k-1}=a^{I,\alpha}_{JK}(u_{k-1}^{J}-u_{k-2}^{J})\partial_{\alpha}u_{k-1}^{K}+a^{I,\alpha}_{JK}u_{k-2}^{J}\partial_{\alpha}(u_{k-1}^{K}-u_{k-2}^{K})\\

+A^{I,\alpha\beta}_{JK}u_{k-1}^{K}\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}(u_{k}^{J}-u_{k-1}^{J})+A^{I,\alpha\beta}_{JK}(u_{k-1}^{K}-u_{k-2}^{K})\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}u_{k-1}^{J}\\
+B^{I,\alpha\beta\gamma}_{JK}\partial_{\gamma}u_{k-1}^{K}\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}(u^{J}_{k}-u^{J}_{k-1})+B^{I,\alpha\beta\gamma}_{JK}\partial_{\gamma}(u_{k-1}^{K}-u_{k-2}^{K})\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}u_{k-1}^{J}.

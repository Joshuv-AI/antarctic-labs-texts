On joint properties of vertices with a given degree or label in the random recursive tree
Bas Lodewijks

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the joint behaviour of the degree, depth and label of and graph distance between high-degree vertices in the random recursive tree. We generalise the results obtained by Eslava [ 12 ] and extend these to include the labels of and graph distance between high-degree vertices. The analysis of both these two properties of high-degree vertices is novel, in particular in relation to the behaviour of the depth of such vertices. In passing, we also obtain results for the joint behaviour of the degree and depth of and graph distance between any fixed number of vertices with a prescribed label. This combines several isolated results on the degree [ 22 ] , depth [ 7 , 24 ] and graph distance [ 9 , 15 ] of vertices with a prescribed label already present in the literature. Furthermore, we extend these results to hold jointly for any number of fixed vertices and improve these results by providing more detailed descriptions of the distributional limits. Our analysis is based on a correspondence between the random recursive tree and a representation of the Kingman n n -coalescent.

Second, we study the joint behaviour of the degree and depth of and graph distance between any fixed number of vertices with a prescribed label. This combines, extends, improves and recovers a range of results on the degree

Our extensions of the aforementioned results arise mainly due to two contributions. First, we are able to analyse the joint behaviour of multiple statistics beyond what was known already in the literature. Second, we obtain these results for any finite number of vertices, whereas only a single vertex or single pair of vertices is considered in most results available to date. It is exactly the correlations that arise due to considering several statistics and many vertices at once that prove to be the most challenging aspects of the analysis. The improvement of the existing results is mostly due to the fact that considering the joint behaviour of several statistics allows us, in certain cases, to obtain more detailed descriptions of their limiting laws beyond what was known previously.

The analysis in this paper is based on the Kingman
n
-coalescent construction of the random recursive tree. This construction was first observed by Pittel in
[
32
]
and later recovered and used by Addario-Berry and Eslava
[
1
]
, and Eslava
[
12
,
13
]
. This construction provides several advantages compared to the more common recursive construction of the random recursive tree. First, rather than in the recursive construction in which distinct vertices have different arrival times (which influence their degree, depth, label, and graph distance), the coalescent construction allows for a perspective in which all vertices are exchangeable. Second, the coalescent construction enables a more natural decoupling of the statistics of distinct vertices, which provides us with tools to tackle the correlations between these statistics in a more refined manner. Finally, in particular the degree, label and depth of a vertex can be expressed in terms of random numbers of coin flips, simplifying the analysis of these statistics. The degree of a vertex equals the length of the first streak of heads, the label equals the step at which the first tails occurs and the depth equals the total number of tails thrown.

Definitions and main results

The random recursive tree model is defined as follows:

Definition 2.1

(Random recursive tree model)
.

Let
(T_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of trees. Initialise
T_{1}
by a root with label
1
. For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, construct
T_{n+1}
from
T_{n}
by adding a vertex with label
n+1
to
T_{n}
and connecting it by a directed edge to a vertex

Due to the temporal nature of the random recursive tree model, it is natural to think of the edges as directed towards the root. Throughout, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and

, we write

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{T_{n}}(u)

\displaystyle:=\text{in-degree of vertex $u$ in }T_{n},

\displaystyle{\rm dist}_{T_{n}}(u,v)

\displaystyle:=\text{graph distance between vertices }u,v\text{ in }T_{n},

\displaystyle h_{T_{n}}(u)

\displaystyle:=\text{depth of vertex $u$ in }T_{n}={\rm dist}_{T_{n}}(u,1).

The graph distance between vertices
u
and
v
denotes the number of edge on the unique path between vertices. Here we do not take the direction of the edges into account. This only matters for the in-degree.

The main approach to proving Theorems

,
2.4
, and
2.6
is to use a ‘reversed-time’ construction or coalescent construction of the random recursive tree, known as the Kingman

. First, the depth, degree, and label of vertices in the Kingman

-coalescent are exchangeable, which simplifies the analysis of their joint behaviour. Second, the coalescent construction simplifies dealing with correlations that appear when considering the depth, degree, and label of multiple vertices at once. In particular, it provides an elegant way to decouple the degree, depth, and label of distinct vertices. Finally, the size of the depth, degree, and label of a vertex can be understood in terms of sums of independent indicator random variables and independent fair coin flips. As a result, standard central limit theorem results can be applied to obtain the desired results.

The variant of the Kingman

-coalescent we use here is a process which starts with

Though the theorems presented in Section

\displaystyle(i)_{(b)}+(iii)

where we remark that we can omit the conditioning due to the fact that the coin flips are independent of everything else.

We now prove (
4.7
). Let us set
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{n}:=\{h_{n}(1)\leq h,\ell_{n}(1)=\ell,\mathrm{d}_{n}(1)\leq d\}
. Again, we express the occurrence and the probability of the event
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{n}
in terms of
J
:

(i)

\ell_{n}(1)=\ell
and
\mathrm{d}_{n}(1)\leq d
can only occur together if the following two things occur:

(a)

Vertex
1
is selected at most
d
times in steps
n
through
\ell+1
, and all associated coin flips favour vertex
1
. The latter occurs with probability
2^{-|J\cap[\ell+1,n]|}
.

(b)

Vertex
1
is selected at step
\ell
and is not favoured by the associated coin flip. The latter occurs with probability
1/2
.

Indeed, if
(a)
does not occur then either the degree or the label of vertex
1
(in the random recursive tree) is too large. If
(b)
does not occur, then the label of vertex
1
(in the random recursive tree) is not equal to
\ell

(ii)

In steps
\ell-1
through
2

J

(since the height of

\ell

X_{n,\ell,2}\leq h-1
.

Combining this, we can write

(4.10)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{n}\,|\,\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)=J)=

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}_{\{|[\ell+1,n]\cap J|\leq d\}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\ell\in J\}}\mathbb{P}(\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{n}\,|\,\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)=J)

\displaystyle(i)_{(a)}+(i)_{(b)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}_{\{|[\ell+1,n]\cap J|\leq d\}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\ell\in J\}}2^{-(|[\ell+1,n]\cap J|+1)}

\displaystyle(i)_{(a)}+(i)_{(b)}

\displaystyle\times\mathbb{P}\!\left(h_{n}(1)\leq h-1\,|\,\ell_{n}(1)=\ell,\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)=J\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}_{\{|[\ell+1,n]\cap J|\leq d\}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\ell\in J\}}2^{-(|[\ell+1,n]\cap J|+1)}\mathbb{P}\!\left(X_{n,\ell,2}\leq h-1\right).

\displaystyle(ii)

We remark that in the last step, as in the proof of (

\{\ell_{n}(1)=\ell,\mathcal{S}_{n}(1)=J\}

\{h_{n}(1)\leq h-1\}

∎

We now extend this result to multiple vertices, which we can do with relative ease as long as the truncated selection sets of the vertices
1,\ldots,k
are disjoint. For ease of writing, we define
\overline{S}_{n,1}:=(\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(i))_{i\in[k]}

\bar{J}:=(J_{i})_{i\in[k]}

Lemma 4.4
.

Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
and consider a truncation sequence
(t_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
t_{n}\leq n
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
h_{i},d_{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{0},i\in[k],(J_{i})_{i\in[k]}\in\Omega_{1}^{k}
such that the

are pairwise disjoint. Then,

(4.11)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}{}

\displaystyle(h_{n,1}(i)\leq h_{i},\mathrm{d}_{n}(i)\geq d_{i},i\in[k]\,|\,\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}=\bar{J})=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{P}(h_{n,1}(i)\leq h_{i},\mathrm{d}_{n}(i)\geq d_{i}\,|\,\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(i)=J_{i}).

If, additionally, we let
\ell_{i}\in\Omega_{1}
for all

,

(4.12)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}{}

\displaystyle(h_{n,1}(i)\leq h_{i},\ell_{n}(i)\geq\ell_{i},\mathrm{d}_{n}(i)\geq d_{i},i\in[k]\,|\,\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}=\bar{J})

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{P}(h_{n,1}(i)\leq h_{i},\ell_{n}(i)\geq\ell_{i},\mathrm{d}_{n}(i)\geq d_{i}\,|\,\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(i)=J_{i}),

and

(4.13)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}{}

\displaystyle(h_{n,1}(i)\leq h_{i},\ell_{n}(i)=\ell_{i},\mathrm{d}_{n}(i)\geq d_{i},i\in[k]\,|\,\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}=\bar{J})

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{P}((h_{n,1}(i)\leq h_{i},\ell_{n}(i)=\ell_{i},\mathrm{d}_{n}(i)\geq d_{i},i\in[k]\,|\,\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(i)=J_{i}).

Proof.

The first result follows from
[
12
, Lemma
3.1
]
. We prove (
4.12
), the proof of the last result follows an analogous approach.

The proof is similar to that of
[
12
, Lemma
3.1
]
. Let us rewrite
J_{i}=\{j_{i,1},\ldots,j_{i,|J_{i}|}\}
, where
j_{i,1}>\ldots>j_{i,J_{i}}
for each
i\in[k]
. Conditionally on
\{\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}=\bar{J}\}
, we have that for each
i\in[k]
,

(4.14)

h_{n,1}(i)=\sum_{m=1}^{|J_{i}|}h_{i,j_{i,m}}.

Also, the event
\{\mathrm{d}_{n}(i)\geq d_{i}\}
holds if and only if
|J_{i}|\geq d_{i}
and
h_{i,j_{i,m}}=0
for all
m\in[d_{i}]
, and the event
\{\ell_{n}(i)\geq\ell_{i}\}
holds if and only if
\max\{m\in J_{i}:h_{i,m}=0\}\geq\ell_{i}
. As

, it follows that the event
\{h_{n,1}(i)\leq h_{i},\ell_{n}(i)\geq\ell_{i},\mathrm{d}_{n}(i)\geq d_{i},i\in[k]\}
, conditionally on

, depends solely on

and

. Since the sets

are pairwise disjoint, the occurrence of the events

, for each

, depend on disjoint sets of random variables. Moreover, since the random variables

for different values of

are determined by independent coin flips, we have that the events

, for each

, depend on disjoint sets of independent random variables, from which (

∎

4.2.

Recall

Lemma 4.6

(Lemma

,
[

]
)
.

Let

\mathbb{P}\!\left(\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}\in\mathcal{B}_{n,\delta}\right)=1-o(1).

We also know that the elements of

The following lemma provides bounds for the decay of the tail distribution of
\tau_{k}
, conditionally on certain events.

Lemma 4.9
.

Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
and recall
\tau_{k}
from (
4.3
). We have that
(\tau_{k})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a tight sequence of random variables. Furthermore, fix
c\in(0,2)
and let
(d_{i})_{i\in[k]}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{k}
such that
d_{i}\leq c\log n
for all
i\in[k]
. Then,

(4.19)

\mathbb{P}\!\left(\tau_{k}<\lceil(\log n)^{2}\rceil\,\Big|\,\mathrm{d}_{n}(i)\geq d_{i},i\in[k]\right)\geq 1-\mathcal{O}\Big(\frac{1}{\log n}\Big).

Furthermore, let
(\ell_{i})_{i\in[k]}\in[n]^{k}
be distinct such that
\ell_{i}
diverges as
n\to\infty
for all
i\in[k]
. Then,

(4.20)

\mathbb{P}\!\left(\tau_{k}<\min_{i\in[k]}\log\ell_{i}\,\Big|\,\ell_{n}(i)=\ell_{i},i\in[k]\right)\geq 1-\mathcal{O}\Big(\frac{1}{\min_{i\in[k]}\log\ell_{i}}\Big).

Proof.

We first prove the tightness of
(\tau_{k})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Fix
\varepsilon>0
and set
K_{\varepsilon}:=\lceil 2+k^{2}/\varepsilon\rceil
. We recall that in Definition
3.2
,
\{a_{j},b_{j}\}
denotes the two trees selected at step
j
in the Kingman
n
-coalescent, for
2\leq j\leq n
. Also, the trees are ordered by their smallest-labelled vertex, so that
\tau_{k}<K_{\varepsilon}
is implied by
\{a_{j},b_{j}\}\not\subseteq[k]
for all
K_{\varepsilon}\leq j\leq n
. Since the selection of these roots is independent at each step, we obtain

(4.21)

\mathbb{P}\!\left(\tau_{k}<K_{\varepsilon}\right)\geq\mathbb{P}\bigg(\bigcap_{j=K_{\varepsilon}}^{n}\big\{\{a_{j},b_{j}\}\not\subseteq[k]\big\}\bigg)=\prod_{j=K_{\varepsilon}}^{n}\mathbb{P}\!\left(\{a_{j},b_{j}\}\not\subseteq[k]\right)=\prod_{j=K_{\varepsilon}}^{n}\Big(1-\frac{k(k-1)}{j(j-1)}\Big).

We then bound the product from below to obtain the lower bound

(4.22)

\mathbb{P}\!\left(\tau_{k}<K_{\varepsilon}\right)\geq 1-\sum_{j=K_{\varepsilon}}^{n}\frac{k^{2}}{(j-1)^{2}}\geq 1-k^{2}\int_{K_{\varepsilon}-2}^{\infty}x^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}x=1-\frac{k^{2}}{K_{\varepsilon}-2}\geq 1-\varepsilon.

As a result,
\mathbb{P}\!\left(\tau_{k}\geq K_{\varepsilon}\right)\leq\varepsilon
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, from which the tightness follows.

We then prove (
4.19
) and set
s_{n}:=\lceil(\log n)^{2}\rceil
for ease of writing. Using Bayes’ theorem, the bound in (
4.22
) and that
\mathbb{P}\!\left(\mathrm{d}_{n}(i)\geq d_{i},i\in[k]\right)=2^{-\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}}(1+o(1))
by Proposition
4.5
, we obtain

(4.23)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\!\left(\tau_{k}<s_{n}\,|\,\mathrm{d}_{n}(i)\geq d_{i},i\in[k]\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{P}\!\left(\tau_{k}<s_{n}\right)}{\mathbb{P}\!\left(\mathrm{d}_{n}(i)\geq d_{i},i\in[k]\right)}\mathbb{P}\!\left(\mathrm{d}_{n}(i)\geq d_{i},i\in[k]\,|\,\tau_{k}<s_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\big(1-\mathcal{O}\big(s_{n}^{-1}\big)\big)2^{\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}}\mathbb{P}\!\left(\mathrm{d}_{n}(i)\geq d_{i},i\in[k]\,|\,\tau_{k}<s_{n}\right).

As in the proof of Lemma
4.4
, the event
\{\mathrm{d}_{n}(i)\geq d_{i},i\in[k]\}
occurs when both

|\mathcal{S}_{n}(i)\cap[s_{n},n]|\geq d_{i}
holds and when the first
d_{i}
associated coin flips favour vertex
i
, for all
i\in[k]
. Conditionally on
\{\tau_{k}<s_{n}\}
, we know that all these coin flips occurs at different steps for
all
vertices
1,\ldots,k
, so that they are independent. Moreover, they are independent of the selection sets, so that we obtain the lower bound

(4.24)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\!\left(\tau_{k}<s_{n}\,|\,\mathrm{d}_{n}(i)\geq d_{i},i\in[k]\right)

\displaystyle\geq\big(1-\mathcal{O}\big(s_{n}^{-1}\big)\big)\mathbb{P}\!\left(|\mathcal{S}_{n}(i)\cap[s_{n},n]|\geq d_{i},i\in[k]\,|\,\tau_{k}<s_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\big(1-\mathcal{O}\big(s_{n}^{-1}\big)\big)\mathbb{P}\bigg(\Big|\cup_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{S}_{n}(i)\cap[s_{n},n]\Big|\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}\bigg|\tau_{k}<s_{n}\bigg).

Again, the last step uses the conditional event, on which we have that all
\mathcal{S}_{n}(i)\cap[s_{n},n]
are disjoint, so that
|\mathcal{S}_{n}(i)\cap[s_{n},n]|\geq d_{i}
for all
i\in[k]
is equivalent to the cardinality of the union of all these sets being greater than the sum of the
d_{i}
. We also know, conditionally on
\{\tau_{k}<s_{n}\}
, that for every
j\in[s_{n},n]
, at most one
s_{i,j}
can equal one among all
i\in[k]
. So, for every
j\in[s_{n},n]
,

(4.25)

\mathbb{P}\!\left(\cup_{i=1}^{k}\{s_{i,j}=1\}\,|\,\tau_{k}<s_{n}\right)=k\mathbb{P}\!\left(s_{1,j}=1\,|\,\tau_{k}<s_{n}\right)=2k\frac{j-k}{j(j-1)-k(k-1)}=\frac{2k}{j+k-1}.

Hence, if we let
(\widetilde{s}_{j})_{j=s_{n}}^{n}
be independent indicator random variables such that

\mathbb{P}\!\left(\widetilde{s}_{j}=1\right)=2k/(j+k-1)
, we can write, conditionally on
\{\tau_{k}<s_{n}\}
.

(4.26)

\Big|\big(\cup_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{S}_{n}(i)\big)\cap[s_{n},n]\Big|\overset{d}{=}\sum_{j=s_{n}}^{n}\widetilde{s}_{j}.

Since
\log s_{n}=o(\log n)
, it is readily checked that

(4.27)

\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j=s_{n}}^{n}\widetilde{s}_{j}\right]=2k(1+o(1))\log n,\qquad{\rm Var}\Big(\sum_{j=s_{n}}^{n}\widetilde{s}_{j}\Big)=2k(1+o(1))\log n.

Again using that
d_{i}\leq c\log n
for each
i\in[k]
and all
n
sufficiently large, where
c<2
, we obtain for some
\widetilde{c}\in(0,2-c)
by using Chebychev’s inequality,

(4.28)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}{}

\displaystyle\bigg(\Big|\big(\cup_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{S}_{n}(i)\big)\cap[s_{n},n]\Big|\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}\,\bigg|\,\tau_{k}<s_{n}\bigg)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\Bigg(\sum_{j=s_{n}}^{n}\widetilde{s}_{j}\geq ck\log n\Bigg)

\displaystyle\geq 1-\mathbb{P}\Bigg(\bigg(\sum_{j=s_{n}}^{n}\widetilde{s}_{j}-\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{j=s_{n}}^{n}\widetilde{s}_{j}\bigg]\bigg)^{2}\geq(\widetilde{c}k\log n)^{2}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\geq 1-\frac{1}{(\widetilde{c}k\log n)^{2}}{\rm Var}\Big(\sum_{j=s_{n}}^{n}\widetilde{s}_{j}\Big)=1-\mathcal{O}\Big(\frac{1}{\log n}\Big),

which, combined with (
4.24
), completes the proof of (
4.19
).

We now prove (
4.20
) and we set
t_{n}=\min_{i\in[k]}\log\ell_{i}
and note that
t_{n}
diverges with
n
. As in the proof of (
4.19
),

(4.29)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big({}

\displaystyle\tau_{k}<t_{n}\,\Big|\,\ell_{n}(i)=\ell_{i},i\in[k]\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\bigg(\bigcap_{j=t_{n}}^{n}\big\{\{a_{j},b_{j}\}\not\subseteq[k]\big\}\,\Big|\,\ell_{n}(i)=\ell_{i},i\in[k]\bigg)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=t_{n}}^{n}\mathbb{P}\!\left(\{a_{j},b_{j}\}\not\subseteq[k]\,|\,\ell_{n}(i)=\ell_{i},i\in[k]\right)

\displaystyle\geq\!\!\!\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=t_{n}\\
j\neq\ell_{i},i\in[k]\end{subarray}}^{n}\!\!\!\!\mathbb{P}\!\left(\{a_{j},b_{j}\}\not\subseteq[k]\right)\prod_{j=1}^{k}\mathbb{P}\!\left(\{a_{\ell_{j}},b_{\ell_{j}}\}\not\subseteq[k]\,|\,\ell_{n}(i)=\ell_{i},i\in[k]\right).

Here, omitting the conditional event for
j\neq\ell_{i}
for any
i\in[k]
yields a lower bound. Indeed, for any two distinct
i,i^{\prime}\in[k]
, if
j>\max\{\ell_{i},\ell_{i^{\prime}}\}
then
\{a_{j},b_{j}\}=\{i,i^{\prime}\}
cannot occur conditionally on
\ell_{n}(i)=\ell_{i}
. Furthermore, we isolate the steps
\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{k}
, since the conditional event prescribes that vertex
i
is selected at step
\ell_{i}
. For any
j\in[k]
,

(4.30)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\!\left(\{a_{\ell_{j}},b_{\ell_{j}}\}\not\subseteq[k]\,|\,\ell_{n}(i)=\ell_{i},i\in[k]\right)

\displaystyle=1-\mathbb{P}\!\left(\{a_{\ell_{j}},b_{\ell_{j}}\}\subseteq[k]\,|\,\{a_{\ell_{j}}=j\}\cup\{b_{\ell_{j}}=j\}\right)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{k-1}{\ell_{j}-1}.

As a result, we obtain

(4.31)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\!\left(\tau_{k}<t_{n}\,\Big|\,\ell_{n}(i)=\ell_{i},i\in[k]\right)

\displaystyle\geq\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=t_{n}\\
j\neq\ell_{i},i\in[k]\end{subarray}}^{m}\Big(1-\frac{k(k-1)}{j(j-1)}\Big)\prod_{j=1}^{k}\Big(1-\frac{k-1}{\ell_{j}-1}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=t_{n}\\
j\neq\ell_{i},i\in[k]\end{subarray}}^{m}\Big(1-\frac{k(k-1)}{(j-1)^{2}}\Big)\Big(1-\frac{k-1}{t_{n}}\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=t_{n}}^{n}\Big(1-\frac{k(k-1)}{(j-1)^{2}}\Big)\prod_{j=1}^{k}\Big(1-\frac{k(k-1)}{(\ell_{j}-1)^{2}}\Big)^{-1}\Big(1-\frac{k-1}{t_{n}}\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle\geq\prod_{j=t_{n}}^{n}\Big(1-\frac{k(k-1)}{(j-1)^{2}}\Big)\Big(1-\frac{k(k-1)}{(n-1)^{2}}\Big)^{-k}\Big(1-\frac{k-1}{t_{n}}\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle\geq 1-\mathcal{O}\big(t_{n}^{-1}\big),

where the last step follows from (
4.22
), and which concludes the proof.
∎

Beyond the sets
\mathcal{A}_{\bar{d}}
and
\mathcal{B}_{n,\delta}
and the random variable
\tau_{k}
, we also want to control of the probability of the events

and

conditionally on the truncated selection sets
\overline{S}_{n,1}
. To this end, we define, for
\bar{\ell}:=(\ell_{i})_{\in[k]}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
,

(4.32)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\overline{\ell}}

\displaystyle:=\{\bar{J}\in\Omega_{1}^{k}:\mathbb{P}\!\left(\ell_{n}(i)=\ell_{i},i\in[k],\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}=\bar{J}\right)>0\},

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{n}

\displaystyle:=\{\bar{J}\in\Omega_{1}^{k}:(J_{1},\ldots,J_{k})\text{ are pairwise disjoint}\}.

We then have the following lemma, which is partially covered by
[
12
, Lemma
3.1
]
.

Lemma 4.10
.

Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
and let
(\ell_{i})_{i\in[k]}\in[n]^{k}
such that
\ell_{i}\neq\ell_{j}
when
i\neq j
. Then,

(4.33)

\mathbb{P}\!\left(\ell_{n}(i)=\ell_{i},i\in[k]\right)=\frac{1}{(n)_{k}}.

Also, when the truncation sequence
t_{n}
diverges with
n
,

(4.34)

\mathbb{P}\!\left(\ell_{n}(i)=\ell_{i},i\in[k],\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{n}^{c}\right)=o(n^{-k}).

Finally, let
(d_{i})_{i\in[k]},\mathbb{N}_{0}^{k}
and let
t_{n}=\lceil(\log n)^{2}\rceil
. If
\bar{J}\in\mathcal{A}_{\bar{d}}
,

(4.35)

\mathbb{P}\!\left(\mathrm{d}_{n}(i)\geq d_{i},i\in[k]\,|\,\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}=\bar{J}\right)=2^{-\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}}.

Proof.

The first result in (
4.33
) follows from Corollary
3.4
, as each vertex obtains a uniform label from
[n]
after the relabelling of the final tree
F_{1}
in the Kingman
n
-coalescent and all
\ell_{i}
are distinct.

To prove (
4.34
), we write

(4.36)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\!\left(\ell_{n}(i)=\ell_{i},i\in[k],\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{n}^{c}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\!\left(\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{n}^{c}\,\Big|\,\ell_{n}(i)=\ell_{i},i\in[k]\right)\mathbb{P}\!\left(\ell_{n}(i)=\ell_{i},i\in[k]\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\!\left(\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{n}^{c}\,\Big|\,\ell_{n}(i)=\ell_{i},i\in[k]\right)\frac{1}{(n)_{k}},

where the last step follows from (
4.33
). It thus remains to argue to that probability on the right-hand side is
o(1)
. For
\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{n}^{c}
to hold, the truncated selection sets should overlap at some step
t_{n}\leq j\leq n
, i.e.
\tau_{k}\geq t_{n}
should hold. Conditionally on the event
\{\ell_{n}(i)=\ell_{i},i\in[k]\}
, however, the truncated selection sets in
\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}
cannot overlap at certain steps. Namely, for
j>\max_{i\in[k]}\ell_{i}
,
j\in\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(i)
can hold for at most one
i\in[k]
. Indeed, if the converse would be the case, i.e.
j\in\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(i)
and
j\in\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(i^{\prime})
for some distinct
i,i^{\prime}\in[k]
, then one of the vertices
i,i^{\prime}
, let us assume this is vertex
i
, would lose the associated coin flip at step
j
and hence its label in the random recursive tree would be
j>\ell_{i}
. This clearly contradicts the conditional event. As a result, on the conditional event
\{\ell_{n}(i)=\ell_{i},i\in[k]\}
, the event
\{\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{n}^{c}\}
implies that
\{t_{n}\leq\tau_{k}\leq\max_{i\in[k]}\ell_{i}\}
holds. Hence, by Lemma
4.9
and since
t_{n}
diverges with
n
,

(4.37)

\mathbb{P}\!\left(\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{n}^{c}\,\Big|\,\ell_{n}(i)=\ell_{i},i\in[k]\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\Big(t_{n}\leq\tau_{k}\leq\max_{i\in[k]}\ell_{i}\Big)\leq\mathbb{P}\!\left(\tau_{k}\geq t_{n}\right)=o(1),

as desired.

The final result in (
4.35
) is proved in
[
12
, Lemma
3.2
]
.
∎

In Lemma
4.4
we saw that, as long as the truncated selection sets
(\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(i))_{i\in[k]}
are pairwise disjoint, then the events
\{h_{n,1}(i)\leq h_{i},\ell_{n}(i)\geq\ell_{i},\mathrm{d}_{n}(i)\geq d_{i}\}
,
i\in[k]
, are independent, conditionally on
\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}
. Furthermore, when the truncation sequence
t_{n}
diverges as
n\to\infty
, we already observed that the event
\{\tau_{k}<t_{n}\}
holds with high probability by Lemma
4.9
, which implies that the
(\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(i))_{i\in[k]}
are disjoint.

On the other hand, we use the truncated depths
(h_{n,1}(i))_{i\in[k]}
merely for technical reasons, and are really interested in the depths
(h_{n}(i))_{i\in[k]}
. As a result, choosing a truncation sequence
(t_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
that diverges with
n
‘too quickly’, may lead to different behaviour of
h_{n,1}(1)
compared to
h_{n}(1)
. In other words, if
t_{n}
grows ‘too quickly’, then
h_{n,2}(1)=h_{n}(1)-h_{n,1}(1)
might become ‘too large’. In the following lemma we make this informal statement more precise and provide constraints on
t_{n}
to avoid such discrepancies between
h_{n}(1)
and
h_{n,1}(1)
.

Lemma 4.11

(Partially from Lemma
2.7
,
[
12
]
)
.

Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
c\in(0,2)
. If
d_{i}\leq c\log n
for all
i\in[k]
and
t_{n}=\lceil(\log n)^{2}\rceil
, then for any
j\in[k]
and any
\varepsilon>0
,

(4.38)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\!\left(h_{n,2}(j)\geq\varepsilon\sqrt{\log n}\,\Big|\,\mathrm{d}_{n}(i)\geq d_{i},i\in[k]\right)=0.

Furthermore, let
(\ell_{i})_{i\in[k]}\in[n]^{k}
be
k
distinct integers that diverge as
n\to\infty
. If
\log t_{n}=o(\min_{i\in[k]}\sqrt{\log\ell_{i}})
, then for any
j\in[k]
and any
\varepsilon>0
,

(4.39)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\!\left(h_{n,2}(j)\geq\varepsilon\sqrt{\log\ell_{j}}\,\Big|\,\ell_{n}(i)=\ell_{i},i\in[k]\right)=0.

Remark 4.12
.

Assume the truncation sequence
(t_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies the assumptions of Lemma
4.11
. As a direct consequence of Lemma
4.11
, the limiting distributions of

(4.40)

\frac{h_{n}(j)-(\log n-d_{j}/2)}{\sqrt{\log n-d_{j}/4}}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{h_{n,1}(j)-(\log n-d_{j}/2)}{\sqrt{\log n-d_{j}/4}}

conditionally on the event
\mathrm{d}_{n}(i)\geq d_{i}
for al
i\in[k]
, with
d_{i}\leq c\log n
for all
i\in[k]
, are identical (assuming they both exist), for any
j\in[k]
. This follows from Slutsky’s theorem
[
35
, Lemma
2.8
]
and since
\sqrt{\log n-d_{j}/4}=\Theta(\sqrt{\log n})
. Similarly, conditionally on
\ell_{n}(i)=\ell_{i},i\in[k]
(where the
\ell_{i}
diverge as
n\to\infty
), the limiting distributions of

(4.41)

\frac{h_{n}(j)-\log\ell_{j}}{\sqrt{\log\ell_{j}}}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{h_{n,1}(j)-\log\ell_{j}}{\sqrt{\log\ell_{j}}}

are identical (assuming they exist), for any
j\in[k]
.

Proof.

The result in (
4.38
) follows from
[
12
, Lemma
2.7
]
.

To prove (
4.39
), we consider
j=1
only by the exchangeability of the vertices. We first note that
t_{n}\leq\min_{i\in[k]}\ell_{i}
by the assumption on
t_{n}
and since the
\ell_{i}
diverge with
n
. As a result, the event
\{\ell_{n}(i)=\ell_{i},i\in[k]\}
is solely dependent on the truncated selection sets
\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}
and the associated coin flips of the truncated selection sets, whereas
h_{n,2}(1)
is determined by the set
\mathcal{S}_{n}(1)\cap[2,t_{n}-1]
and its associated coin flips. It thus follows that
h_{n,2}(1)
is independent of the event
\{\ell_{n}(i)=\ell_{i},i\in[k]\}
. The result then follows from the Markov inequality and by the assumption on
t_{n}
, as

(4.42)

\mathbb{P}(h_{n,2}(1)\geq\varepsilon\sqrt{\log\ell_{1}})\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[h_{n,2}(1)\right]}{\varepsilon\sqrt{\log\ell_{1}}}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon\sqrt{\log\ell_{1}}}\sum_{j=2}^{t_{n}-1}\frac{1}{j}=\mathcal{O}\Big(\frac{\log t_{n}}{\sqrt{\log\ell_{1}}}\Big)=o(1),

by the assumptions on
t_{n}
, which concludes the proof.
∎

5.
Joint properties of high-degree vertices

In this section we use the preliminary results proved in Section
4
to study the joint behaviour of the depth and label of high-degree vertices.

We set

(5.1)

h:=(\log n-d/2)+y\sqrt{\log n-d/4},\quad\ell:=n\exp(-d/2+x\sqrt{d/4}),\quad t_{n}:=\lceil(\log n)^{2}\rceil,

with
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
. We then have the following result.

Proposition 5.1
.

Fix
a\in[0,2)
, let
h,\ell
and
t_{n}
be as in (
5.1
), with
d\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and let
M
and
N
be two independent standard normal random variables. When
\limsup_{n\to\infty}d<\infty
,

(5.2)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\!\left(h_{n,1}(1)\leq h\,|\,\mathrm{d}_{n}(1)\geq d\right)=\Phi(y).

When, instead,
d
diverges as
n\to\infty
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}d/\log n=a
,

(5.3)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\!\left(h_{n,1}(1)\leq h,\ell_{n}(1)\geq\ell\,|\,\mathrm{d}_{n}(1)\geq d\right)=\mathbb{P}\!\left(M\sqrt{\frac{a}{4-a}}+N\sqrt{1-\frac{a}{4-a}}\leq y,M>x\right).

Remark 5.2
.

(i)
As
M\sqrt{a/(4-a)}+N\sqrt{1-a/(4-a)}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
, the result in (
5.3
), when omitting the event
\ell_{n}(1)\geq\ell
(or, equivalently, letting
x\to-\infty
and setting
a=0
), yields

(5.4)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\!\left(h_{n,1}(1)\leq h\,|\,\mathrm{d}_{n}(1)\geq d\right)=\Phi(y),

and hence complements the result in (
5.2
) in the case that
d
diverges with
n
such that
d\leq c\log n
for some
c\in(0,2)
. Together, they are a generalisation of
[
12
, Lemma
2.5
]
, where only a parametrised version with
d=\lfloor a\log n\rfloor+b
and
a\in[0,2),b\in\mathbb{Z}
is considered.

(ii)
Combined with Lemma
4.11
and Remark
4.12
, we obtain that the results in Proposition
5.1
hold when we substitute
h_{n}(1)
for
h_{n,1}(1)
as well.

Proof.

We first prove (
5.3
) and briefly discuss how to prove (
5.2
) using
[
12
, Lemma
2.5
]
at the end. In the setting of (
5.3
), we recall that we assume that
d
diverges as
n\to\infty
and
h,\ell
and
t_{n}
are as in (
5.1
).

Take
c\in(a,2)
. Recall that, conditionally on
\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)
,
X_{n,\ell,1}\sim\text{Bin}(|[\ell,n]\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)|-d,1/2)
and
X_{n,\ell,2}\sim\text{Bin}(|[t_{n},\ell-1]\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)|,1/2)
(where we set
X_{n,\ell,1}=0,X_{n,\ell,2}=0
when

|[\ell,n]\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)|-d\leq 0
and
|[t_{n},\ell-1]\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)|=0
, respectively). By Lemma
4.1
,

(5.5)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}{}

\displaystyle(h_{n,1}\leq h,\ell_{n}(1)\geq\ell\,|\,\mathrm{d}_{n}(1)\geq d)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{P}\!\left(h_{n,1}\leq h,\ell_{n}(1)\geq\ell,\mathrm{d}_{n}(1)\geq d\right)}{\mathbb{P}\!\left(\mathrm{d}_{n}(1)\geq d\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}\!\left(\mathrm{d}_{n}(1)\geq d\right)2^{d}}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{\{|[\ell,n]\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)|\geq d+1\}}\mathbb{P}\!\left(X_{n,\ell,1}+X_{n,\ell,2}\leq h,X_{n,\ell,1}\geq 1\,|\,\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)\right)\right]

Since
d
diverges with
n
and
d\leq c\log n
(with
c\in(a,2)
),

(5.6)

\ell=n\exp(-d/2+x\sqrt{d/4})>\lceil(\log n)^{2}\rceil=t_{n}

for all
n
large. As a result,
X_{n,\ell,2}
is non-zero with positive probability.

Since
\mathbb{P}\!\left(\mathrm{d}_{n}(1)\geq d\right)2^{d}=1+o(1)
by Proposition
4.5
, we obtain

(5.7)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}({}

\displaystyle h_{n,1}\leq h,\ell_{n}(1)\geq\ell\,|\,\mathrm{d}_{n}(1)\geq d)

\displaystyle=(1+o(1))\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{\{|[\ell,n]\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)|\geq d+1\}}\mathbb{P}\!\left(X_{n,\ell,1}+X_{n,\ell,2}\leq h,X_{n,\ell,1}\geq 1\,|\,\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)\right)\right].

To prove the expected value has the desired limit, we start by rewriting the binomial random variables
X_{n,\ell,1}
and
X_{n,\ell,2}
. Let
(I_{j}^{n})_{j\in[n],n\in\mathbb{N}}
,
(\widetilde{I}_{j}^{n})_{j\in[n],n\in\mathbb{N}}
be two i.i.d. sequences of independent Bernoulli
(1/2)
random variables. Finally, let
Q_{n}:=|[\ell,n]\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)|
,
\widetilde{Q}_{n}:=|[t_{n},\ell-1]\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)|
, independent of the
I_{j}^{n},\widetilde{I}_{j}^{n}
. Then,

(5.8)

X_{n,\ell,1}:=\sum_{j=1}^{Q_{n}-d}I_{j}^{Q_{n}-d},\qquad X_{n,\ell,2}:=\sum_{j=1}^{\widetilde{Q}_{n}}\widetilde{I}_{j}^{\widetilde{Q}_{n}}.

Here, we set
X_{n,\ell,1}=0,X_{n,\ell,2}=0
if
Q_{n}-d\leq 0,\widetilde{Q}_{n}=0
, respectively. Notice that
Q_{n}
and
\widetilde{Q}_{n}
are independent, that they can be determined from
\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)
and that the values of the
I_{j}^{n},\widetilde{I}_{j}^{n}
are independent of
\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)
, so that conditioning on
\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)
is equivalent to conditioning on
Q_{n},\widetilde{Q}_{n}
. We can then write the expected value in the statement of the proposition as

(5.9)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}{}

\displaystyle\left[\mathbbm{1}_{\{Q_{n}\geq d+1\}}\mathbb{P}\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\widetilde{Q}_{n}}\widetilde{I}_{j}^{\widetilde{Q}_{n}}+\sum_{j=1}^{Q_{n}-d}I_{j}^{Q_{n}-d}\leq h,\sum_{j=1}^{Q_{n}-d}I_{i}^{Q_{n}-d}\geq 1\,\bigg|\,Q_{n},\widetilde{Q}_{n}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\widetilde{Q}_{n}}\widetilde{I}_{j}^{\widetilde{Q}_{n}}+\sum_{j=1}^{(Q_{n}-d)\mathbbm{1}_{\{Q_{n}-d\geq 1\}}}I_{j}^{Q_{n}-d}\leq h,\sum_{j=1}^{(Q_{n}-d)\mathbbm{1}_{\{Q_{n}-d\geq 1\}}}I_{j}^{Q_{n}-d}\geq 1\right).

The second line follows from the fact that, by changing the upper limits of the second and third sum in the probability on the first line from
Q_{n}-d
to
(Q_{n}-d)\mathbbm{1}_{\{Q_{n}-d\geq 1\}}
, we can remove the indicator in the expected value. Indeed, if
Q_{n}\leq d
, then
\mathbbm{1}_{\{Q_{n}-d\geq 1\}}=0
, and hence the second event in the probability cannot occur almost surely, so that the probability is zero. As a result, the indicator in the expected value is redundant. We thus obtain

(5.10)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Bigg({}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\widetilde{Q}_{n}}\widetilde{I}_{j}^{\widetilde{Q}_{n}}+\sum_{j=1}^{(Q_{n}-d)\mathbbm{1}_{\{Q_{n}-d\geq 1\}}}I_{j}^{Q_{n}-d}\leq h\Bigg)-\mathbb{P}\Bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{\widetilde{Q}_{n}}\widetilde{I}_{j}^{\widetilde{Q}_{n}}\leq h,\sum_{j=1}^{(Q_{n}-d)\mathbbm{1}_{\{Q_{n}-d\geq 1\}}}I_{j}^{Q_{n}-d}=0\Bigg)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{\widetilde{Q}_{n}}\widetilde{I}_{j}^{\widetilde{Q}_{n}}+\sum_{j=1}^{(Q_{n}-d)\mathbbm{1}_{\{Q_{n}-d\geq 1\}}}I_{j}^{Q_{n}-d}\leq h\Bigg)

\displaystyle-\mathbb{P}\Bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{\widetilde{Q}_{n}}\widetilde{I}_{j}^{\widetilde{Q}_{n}}\leq h\Bigg)\mathbb{P}\Bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{(Q_{n}-d)\mathbbm{1}_{\{Q_{n}-d\geq 1\}}}I_{j}^{Q_{n}-d}=0\Bigg),

where the second step follows from the independence of the two sums in the second probability on the first line. The event

(5.11)

\Bigg\{\sum_{j=1}^{(Q_{n}-d)\mathbbm{1}_{\{Q_{n}-d\geq 1\}}}I_{j}^{Q_{n}-d}=0\Bigg\}

occurs either when
Q_{n}\leq d
or when, given
Q_{n}\geq d+1
,
I_{1}^{Q_{n}-d}=\ldots=I_{Q_{n}-d}^{Q_{n}-d}=0
. Hence,

(5.12)

\mathbb{P}\Bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{(Q_{n}-d)\mathbbm{1}_{\{Q_{n}-d\geq 1\}}}I_{j}^{Q_{n}-d}=0\Bigg)=\mathbb{P}\!\left(Q_{n}\leq d\right)+\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{\{Q_{n}\geq d+1\}}2^{-(Q_{n}-d)}\right].

Combining this with (
5.10
) yields

(5.13)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Bigg({}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\widetilde{Q}_{n}}\widetilde{I}_{j}^{\widetilde{Q}_{n}}+\sum_{j=1}^{(Q_{n}-d)\mathbbm{1}_{\{Q_{n}-d\geq 1\}}}I_{j}^{Q_{n}-d}\leq h\Bigg)-\mathbb{P}\Bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{\widetilde{Q}_{n}}\widetilde{I}_{j}^{\widetilde{Q}_{n}}\leq h\Bigg)\mathbb{P}\!\left(Q_{n}\leq d\right)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{O}\Big(\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{\{Q_{n}\geq d+1\}}2^{-(Q_{n}-d)}\right]\Big).

What remains is to show that the first two terms yield the desired limit and that the last term is negligible compared to the first two. Let us start with the former and tackle the product of two probabilities on the first line. It follows from Lindeberg’s conditions
[
11
, Theorem
3.4.5
]
that

(5.14)

\frac{Q_{n}-\mathbb{E}\left[Q_{n}\right]}{\sqrt{{\rm Var}(Q_{n})}}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{d}}N,\qquad\frac{\widetilde{Q}_{n}-\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{Q}_{n}]}{\sqrt{{\rm Var}(\widetilde{Q}_{n})}}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{d}}\widetilde{N},

with
N,\widetilde{N}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
independent standard normal random variables, as we recall that
Q_{n}
and
\widetilde{Q}_{n}
are sums of independent Bernoulli random variables. It is readily checked that by the choice of
\ell
in (
5.1
) and since
d
diverges with
n
,

(5.15)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Q_{n}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=\ell}^{n}\frac{2}{j}=2\log(n/\ell)+\mathcal{O}(1)=d-x\sqrt{d}(1+o(1)),

\displaystyle{\rm Var}(Q_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=\ell}^{n}\frac{2}{j}\Big(1-\frac{2}{j}\Big)=d-x\sqrt{d}(1+o(1)),

and, by the choice of
\ell,d
and
t_{n}
,

(5.16)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{Q}_{n}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=t_{n}}^{\ell-1}\frac{2}{j}=2\log n-d+x\sqrt{d}-(1+o(1))\log\log n,

\displaystyle{\rm Var}(\widetilde{Q}_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=t_{n}}^{\ell-1}\frac{2}{j}\Big(1-\frac{2}{j}\Big)=2\log n-d+x\sqrt{d}-(1+o(1))\log\log n.

By (
5.14
) and (
5.15
) we thus obtain that

(5.17)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\!\left(Q_{n}\leq d\right)=\mathbb{P}\!\left(\frac{Q_{n}-\mathbb{E}\left[Q_{n}\right]}{\sqrt{{\rm Var}(Q_{n})}}\leq\frac{d-\mathbb{E}\left[Q_{n}\right]}{\sqrt{{\rm Var}(Q_{n})}}\right)=\mathbb{P}\!\left(\frac{Q_{n}-\mathbb{E}\left[Q_{n}\right]}{\sqrt{{\rm Var}(Q_{n})}}\leq\frac{x\sqrt{d}(1+o(1))}{\sqrt{d}(1+o(1))}\right),

which converges to
\Phi(x)
, where we recall that
\Phi:\mathbb{R}\to(0,1)
denotes the cumulative density function of a standard normal distribution. By Skorokhod’s representation theorem
[
5
, Theorem
6.7
]
there exists a probability space and a coupling of
(Q_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}},(\widetilde{Q}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(I_{j}^{n})_{j\in[n],n\in\mathbb{N}},(\widetilde{I}_{j}^{n})_{j\in[n],n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that the collections
(I_{j}^{n})_{j\in\mathbb{N}},(\widetilde{I}_{j}^{n})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
are independent of
Q_{n}
and
\widetilde{Q}_{n}
and the convergence in (
5.14
) is almost sure rather than in distribution. In particular,
Q_{n}/d\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{a.s.}}1
,
\widetilde{Q}_{n}/(2\log n-d)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{a.s.}}1
and
Q_{n},\widetilde{Q}_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{a.s.}}\infty
. Moreover, it also follows from this representation that

(5.18)

\frac{2\sum_{j=1}^{n}I_{j}^{n}-n}{\sqrt{n}}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{a.s.}}N^{\prime},\qquad\frac{2\sum_{j=1}^{n}\widetilde{I}_{j}^{n}-n}{\sqrt{n}}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{a.s.}}N^{\prime\prime},

as
n\to\infty
as well, where
N^{\prime},N^{\prime\prime}
are independent standard normal random variables, also independent of
N,\widetilde{N}
in (
5.14
). We then rewrite

(5.19)

\displaystyle\frac{2\sum_{j=1}^{\widetilde{Q}_{n}}\widetilde{I}_{j}^{\widetilde{Q}_{n}}-(2\log n-d)}{\sqrt{4\log n-d}}={}

\displaystyle\frac{2\sum_{j=1}^{\widetilde{Q}_{n}}\widetilde{I}_{j}^{\widetilde{Q}_{n}}-\widetilde{Q}_{n}}{\sqrt{\widetilde{Q}_{n}}}\sqrt{\frac{\widetilde{Q}_{n}}{2\log n-d}}\sqrt{\frac{2\log n-d}{4\log n-d}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\widetilde{Q}_{n}-\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{Q}_{n}]}{\sqrt{{\rm Var}(\widetilde{Q}_{n})}}\sqrt{\frac{{\rm Var}(\widetilde{Q}_{n})}{2\log n-d}}\sqrt{\frac{2\log n-d}{4\log n-d}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{Q}_{n}]-(2\log n-d)}{\sqrt{d}}\sqrt{\frac{d}{4\log n-d}}.

Combining this with the Skorokhod representation, the fact that
d/\log n\to a
and (
5.16
), yields

(5.20)

\frac{2\sum_{j=1}^{\widetilde{Q}_{n}}\widetilde{I}_{j}^{\widetilde{Q}_{n}}-(2\log n-d)}{\sqrt{4\log n-d}}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{d}}N_{1}\sqrt{\frac{2-a}{4-a}}+N_{2}\sqrt{\frac{2-a}{4-a}}+x\sqrt{\frac{a}{4-a}},

where
N_{1},N_{2}
are independent standard normal random variables. Combining this with (
5.17
) and using that
h=\log n-d/2+y\sqrt{\log n-d/4}
, we obtain

(5.21)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\!\left(Q_{n}\leq d\right)\mathbb{P}\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\widetilde{Q}_{n}}\widetilde{I}_{j}^{\widetilde{Q}_{n}}\leq h\right)

\displaystyle=\Phi(x)\mathbb{P}\!\left(N_{1}\sqrt{\frac{2-a}{4-a}}+N_{2}\sqrt{\frac{2-a}{4-a}}+x\sqrt{\frac{a}{4-a}}\leq y\right)

\displaystyle=\Phi(x)\mathbb{P}\!\left(N\sqrt{1-\frac{a}{4-a}}+x\sqrt{\frac{a}{4-a}}\leq y\right),

where
N
is again a standard normal random variable. This deals with the second term of (
5.13
). For the first term, we observe that

(5.22)

\mathbb{P}\!\left(\frac{(Q_{n}-d)\mathbbm{1}_{\{Q_{n}-d\geq 1\}}}{\sqrt{d}}=0\right)=\mathbb{P}\!\left(Q_{n}\leq d\right)\to\Phi(x),

as
n\to\infty
by (
5.17
), and similarly for
z\geq 0
,

(5.23)

\mathbb{P}\!\left(\frac{(Q_{n}-d)\mathbbm{1}_{\{Q_{n}-d\geq 1\}}}{\sqrt{d}}>z\right)=\mathbb{P}\!\left(\frac{Q_{n}-\mathbb{E}\left[Q_{n}\right]}{\sqrt{{\rm Var}(Q_{n})}}>\frac{d-\mathbb{E}\left[Q_{n}\right]+z\sqrt{d}}{\sqrt{{\rm Var}(Q_{n})}}\right)\to 1-\Phi(x+z),

as
n\to\infty
. Hence, for
x\in\mathbb{R}
fixed, let us define a random variable
M_{x}:=\mathbbm{1}_{\{M>x\}}(M-x)
, where
M
is a standard normal random variable. It then follows that
\mathbb{P}\!\left(M_{x}=0\right)=\Phi(x)
and
\mathbb{P}\!\left(M_{x}>z\right)=\mathbb{P}\!\left(M>x+z\right)=1-\Phi(x+z),z>0
, so that

(5.24)

\frac{(Q_{n}-d)\mathbbm{1}_{\{Q_{n}-d\geq 1\}}}{\sqrt{d}}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{d}}M_{x}.

By the independence of the Bernoulli random variables
I_{j}^{n},\widetilde{I}_{j}^{n}
, we can relabel them as a sequence of i.i.d. random variables. If we set
O_{n}:=\widetilde{Q}_{n}+(Q_{n}-d)\mathbbm{1}_{\{Q_{n}-d\geq 1\}}
, then we can write them as
(\hat{I}_{j}^{O_{n}})_{j\in[O_{n}]}
, with
\hat{I}^{O_{n}}_{j}:=\widetilde{I}_{j}^{\widetilde{Q}_{n}}
if
1\leq j\leq\widetilde{Q}_{n}
and
\hat{I}^{O_{n}}_{j}:=I_{j-\widetilde{Q}_{n}}^{Q_{n}-d}
if
\widetilde{Q}_{n}+1\leq j\leq\widetilde{Q}_{n}+(Q_{n}-d)\mathbbm{1}_{\{Q_{n}-d\geq 1\}}
. Again following Lindeberg’s conditions, we find that

(5.25)

\frac{2\sum_{j=1}^{n}\hat{I}^{n}_{j}-n}{\sqrt{n}}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{d}}N^{\prime},

where
N^{\prime}
is a standard normal random variable. Moreover,
O_{n}/(2\log n-d)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{\mathbb{P}}}1
by combining (
5.14
), (
5.16
) and (
5.24
). We can then write

(5.26)

\displaystyle\frac{2\sum_{j=1}^{\widetilde{Q}_{n}}\widetilde{I}_{j}^{\widetilde{Q}_{n}}+2\sum_{j=1}^{(Q_{n}-d)\mathbbm{1}_{\{Q_{n}-d\geq 1\}}}I_{j}^{Q_{n}-d}-(2\log n-d)}{\sqrt{4\log n-d}}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\frac{2\sum_{j=1}^{O_{n}}\hat{I}^{O_{n}}_{j}-O_{n}}{\sqrt{O_{n}}}\sqrt{\frac{O_{n}}{2\log n-d}}\sqrt{\frac{2\log n-d}{4\log n-d}}+\frac{\widetilde{Q}_{n}-\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{Q}_{n}]}{\sqrt{{\rm Var}(\widetilde{Q}_{n})}}\sqrt{\frac{{\rm Var}(\widetilde{Q}_{n})}{2\log n-d}}\sqrt{\frac{2\log n-d}{4\log n-d}}

\displaystyle+\frac{(Q_{n}-d)\mathbbm{1}_{\{Q_{n}-d\geq 1\}}}{\sqrt{d}}\sqrt{\frac{d}{4\log n-d}}+\frac{\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{Q}_{n}]-(2\log n-d)}{\sqrt{d}}\sqrt{\frac{d}{4\log n-d}}.

If we let
N,N^{\prime},N^{\prime\prime}
be i.i.d. standard normal random variables, independent of
M_{x}
, and use similar steps as in (
5.24
) and (
5.19
) (in particular using the Skorokhod representation for the random variables
(\hat{I}_{j}^{n})_{j\in[n]},O_{n},(Q_{n}-d)\mathbbm{1}_{\{Q_{n}-d\geq 1\}}
and that
d/\log n\to a
), this converges in distribution to

(5.27)

\displaystyle N^{\prime}{}

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{2-a}{4-a}}+N^{\prime\prime}\sqrt{\frac{2-a}{4-a}}+M_{x}\sqrt{\frac{a}{4-a}}+x\sqrt{\frac{a}{4-a}}

\displaystyle\overset{d}{=}N\sqrt{1-\frac{a}{4-a}}+M_{x}\sqrt{\frac{a}{4-a}}+x\sqrt{\frac{a}{4-a}},

Combining this with (
5.21
) in (
5.13
) yields

(5.28)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}{}

\displaystyle\Bigg[\mathbb{P}\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\widetilde{Q}_{n}}\widetilde{I}_{j}^{\widetilde{Q}_{n}}+\sum_{j=1}^{(Q_{n}-d)\mathbbm{1}_{\{Q_{n}-d\geq 1\}}}I_{j}^{Q_{n}-d}\leq h\right)-\mathbb{P}\!\left(Q_{n}\leq d\right)\mathbb{P}\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\widetilde{Q}_{n}}\widetilde{I}_{j}^{\widetilde{Q}_{n}}\leq h\right)\Bigg]

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\!\left(M_{x}\sqrt{\frac{a}{4-a}}+x\sqrt{\frac{a}{4-a}}+N\sqrt{1-\frac{a}{4-a}}\leq y\right)

\displaystyle-\Phi(x)\mathbb{P}\!\left(N\sqrt{1-\frac{a}{4-a}}+x\sqrt{\frac{a}{4-a}}\leq y\right).

By intersecting the event in the first probability on the right-hand side with the complementary events
\{M_{x}=0\},\{M_{x}>0\},
and using that
M_{x}
is independent of
N
, we arrive at

(5.29)

\mathbb{P}\!\left(M_{x}\sqrt{\frac{a}{4-a}}+x\sqrt{\frac{a}{4-a}}+N\sqrt{1-\frac{a}{4-a}}\leq y,M_{x}>0\right).

By the definition of
M_{x}
, it follows that the event
\{M_{x}>0\}
is equivalent to
\{M>x\}
, where we recall that
M
is a standard normal random variable. Moreover, on the event
\{M_{x}>0\}=\{M>x\}
,
M_{x}+x=\mathbbm{1}_{\{M>x\}}(M-x)+x=M
. Thus, we obtain

(5.30)

\mathbb{P}\!\left(M\sqrt{\frac{a}{4-a}}+N\sqrt{1-\frac{a}{4-a}}\leq y,M>x\right),

as desired. Finally, we show that

(5.31)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{\{Q_{n}\geq d+1\}}2^{-(Q_{n}-d)}\right]=0.

By splitting the expected value into the cases where
Q_{n}
is at most
d+1+\lfloor d^{1/2-\eta}\rfloor
and at least
d+1+\lceil d^{1/2-\eta}\rceil
, respectively, for some
\eta\in(0,1/2)
, we obtain

(5.32)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathbbm{1}_{\{Q_{n}\geq d+1\}}2^{-(Q_{n}-d)}\Big]={}

\displaystyle\sum_{m=d+1}^{d+1+\lfloor d^{1/2-\eta}\rfloor}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\mathbb{P}\!\left(Q_{n}=m\right)2^{-(m-d)}+\!\!\!\!\sum_{m\geq d+1+\lceil d^{1/2-\eta}\rceil}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\mathbb{P}\!\left(Q_{n}=m\right)2^{-(m-d)}

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\sum_{m=d+1}^{d+1+\lfloor d^{1/2-\eta}\rfloor}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\mathbb{P}\!\left(Q_{n}=m\right)+\!\!\!\!\sum_{m\geq d+1+\lceil d^{1/2-\eta}\rceil}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\mathbb{P}\!\left(Q_{n}=m\right)2^{-d^{1/2-\eta}}

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\!\left(d+1\leq Q_{n}\leq d+1+\lfloor d^{1/2-\eta}\rfloor\right)+2^{-d^{1/2-\eta}}.

Since
d^{1/2-\eta}=o\big(\sqrt{{\rm Var}(Q_{n})}\big)
(see (
5.15
)), it follows from (
5.14
) that the probability in the last line converges to zero. This proves (
5.31
), and combining this with the limit (
5.30
) of the left-hand side of (
5.28
) in (
5.13
) yields the desired result and concludes the proof of (
5.3
).

We now discuss the the proof of (
5.2
). We recall that now
L:=\limsup_{n\to\infty}d<\infty
. Also, conditionally on
\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)
, let
X_{n}=X_{n}(d)\sim\text{Bin}(|\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)|-d,1/2)
(where we set
X_{n}=0
when
|\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)|-d\leq 0
) and let us define
h^{\prime}:=\log n+y\sqrt{\log n}
. Note that
(h-h^{\prime})/\sqrt{\log n}=o(1)
since
L<\infty
, so that using
h^{\prime}
instead of
h
yields the same result. Again using Lemma
4.1
and Proposition
4.5
, we obtain

(5.33)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\!\left(h_{n,1}(1)>h^{\prime}\,|\,\mathrm{d}_{n}(1)\geq d\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{P}\!\left(h_{n,1}(1)>h^{\prime},\mathrm{d}_{n}(1)\geq d\right)}{\mathbb{P}\!\left(\mathrm{d}_{n}(1)\geq d\right)}

\displaystyle=(1+o(1))\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbbm{1}_{\{|\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)|\geq d\}}\mathbb{P}\!\left(X_{n}>h^{\prime}\,|\,S_{n,1}(1)\right)\big].

Notice that, for any
\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)\subseteq\Omega_{1}
, both the indicator as well as the probability are decreasing functions of
d
. As a result, we can bound the expected value from above by setting
d=0
in the indicator and using
X_{n}(0)
in the probability. The upper bound has the desired limit by
[
12
, Lemma
2.5
]
with
a=b=0
. Similarly, we can bound the expected value from below by setting
d=L
in the indicator and using
X_{n}(L)
in the probability. The result then follows from
[
12
, Lemma
2.5
]
with
a=0,b=L
, which yields a matching lower bound.
∎

To finish this section, we use the results related to the truncated selection sets developed in Section
4
to extend Proposition
5.1
to the case of multiple vertices.

Proposition 5.3
.

Fix
k\in\mathbb{N},(a_{i})_{i\in[k]}\in[0,2)^{k}
. Let
(d_{i})_{i\in[k]}
be
k
integer-valued sequences such that, for all
i\in[k]
,
\lim_{n\to\infty}d_{i}/\log n=a_{i}
. Let
\ell_{i}:=n\exp(-d_{i}/2+x_{i}\sqrt{d_{i}/4})
and
h_{i}:=(\log n-d_{i}/2)+y_{i}\sqrt{\log n-d_{i}/4}
with
(x_{i})_{i\in[k]},(y_{i})_{i\in[k]}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
, and set
t_{n}=\lceil(\log n)^{2}\rceil
. Then,

(5.34)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(h_{n,1}(i)\leq h_{i},i\in[k]\,|\,\mathrm{d}_{n}(i)\geq d_{i},i\in[k])=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\Phi(y_{i}).

If, additionally,
d_{i}
diverges as
n\to\infty
for all
i\in[k]
, let
M
and
N
be independent standard normal random variables. Then,

(5.35)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}{}

\displaystyle(h_{n,1}(i)\leq h_{i},\ell_{n}(i)\geq\ell_{i},i\in[k]\,|\,\mathrm{d}_{n}(i)\geq d_{i},i\in[k])

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{P}\!\left(M\sqrt{\frac{a_{i}}{4-a_{i}}}+N\sqrt{1-\frac{a_{i}}{4-a_{i}}}\leq y_{i},M>x_{i}\right).

Remark 5.4
.

As is the case in Remark
5.2
, it follows from Lemma
4.11
and Remark
4.12
that the result in Proposition
5.3
holds when substituting
h_{n}(i)
for
h_{n,1}(i)
as well.

Proof.

We provide a proof for (
5.35
), the proof of (
5.34
) uses the same steps.

It suffices to prove that

(5.36)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}{}

\displaystyle(h_{n,1}(i)\leq h_{i},\ell_{n}(i)\geq\ell_{i},\mathrm{d}_{n}(i)\geq d_{i},i\in[k])

\displaystyle=(1+o(1))2^{-\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{P}\!\left(M\sqrt{\frac{a_{i}}{4-a_{i}}}+N\sqrt{1-\frac{a_{i}}{4-a_{i}}}\leq y_{i},M>x_{i}\right),

since then, by Proposition
4.5
,

(5.37)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}{}

\displaystyle(h_{n,1}(i)\leq h_{i},\ell_{n}(i)\geq\ell_{i},i\in[k]\,|\,\mathrm{d}_{n}(i)\geq d_{i},i\in[k])

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{(1+o(1))2^{-\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{P}\!\left(M\sqrt{\frac{a_{i}}{4-a_{i}}}+N\sqrt{1-\frac{a_{i}}{4-a_{i}}}\leq y_{i},M>x_{i}\right)}{\mathbb{P}\!\left(\mathrm{d}_{n}(i)\geq d_{i},i\in[k]\right)}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{P}\!\left(M\sqrt{\frac{a_{i}}{4-a_{i}}}+N\sqrt{1-\frac{a_{i}}{4-a_{i}}}\leq y_{i},M>x_{i}\right).

Let us define

(5.38)

\displaystyle f_{n}(\bar{J}):={}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\!\left(h_{n,1}(i)\leq h_{i},\ell_{n}(i)\geq\ell_{i},\mathrm{d}_{n}(i)\geq d_{i},i\in[k]\,|\,\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}=\bar{J}\right),

\displaystyle g_{n}(\bar{J}):={}

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{P}\!\left(h_{n,1}(i)\leq h_{i},\ell_{n}(i)\geq\ell_{i},\mathrm{d}_{n}(i)\geq d_{i}\,|\,\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(i)=J_{i}\right).

Then, take
c\in(\max_{i\in[k]}a_{i},2)
and set
\delta:=2-c
so that
\mathcal{B}_{n,\delta}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{\bar{d}}
by Lemma
4.6
. We write,

(5.39)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}{}

\displaystyle(h_{n,1}(i)\leq h_{i},\ell_{n}(i)\geq\ell_{i},d_{n}(i)\geq d_{i},i\in[k])

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[f_{n}(\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[f_{n}(\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1})\mathbbm{1}_{\{\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}\in\mathcal{B}_{n,\delta}\}}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[f_{n}(\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1})\mathbbm{1}_{\{\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}\in\mathcal{A}_{\bar{d}}\backslash\mathcal{B}_{n,\delta}\}}\right].

For the first term on the right-hand side, we use that the truncated selection sets are pairwise disjoint by the definition of
\mathcal{B}_{n,\delta}
in (
4.16
) and that by Lemma
4.4
,
f_{n}(\bar{J})=g_{n}(\bar{J})
for all
\bar{J}\in\mathcal{B}_{n,\delta}
and
n
sufficiently large as a result. Together with Lemma
4.8
, recalling that
\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{n,1}
is a tuple of
k
independent copies of
\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)
, this yields

(5.40)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[f_{n}(\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1})\mathbbm{1}_{\{\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}\in\mathcal{B}_{n,\delta}\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bar{J}\in\mathcal{B}_{n,\delta}}f_{n}(\bar{J})\mathbb{P}\!\left(\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}=\bar{J}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bar{J}\in\mathcal{B}_{n,\delta}}g_{n}(\bar{J})\mathbb{P}\!\left(\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{n,1}=\bar{J}\right)(1+o(1))

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[g_{n}(\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{n,1})\mathbbm{1}_{\{\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{n,1}\in\mathcal{B}_{n,\delta}\}}\right](1+o(1)).

Moreover, since
f_{n}(\bar{J}),g_{n}(\bar{J})\leq 2^{-\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}}
when
\bar{J}\in\mathcal{A}_{\bar{d}}
by (
4.35
) in Lemma
4.10
, and using Lemmas
4.7
and
4.8
,

(5.41)

\displaystyle\Big|{}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[f_{n}(\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1})\mathbbm{1}_{\{\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}\in\mathcal{A}_{\bar{d}}\backslash\mathcal{B}_{n,\delta}\}}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[g_{n}(\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{n,1})\mathbbm{1}_{\{\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{n,1}\in\mathcal{A}_{\bar{d}}\backslash\mathcal{B}_{n,\delta}\}}\right]\Big|

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}}(\mathbb{P}\!\left(\overline{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}\in\mathcal{A}_{\bar{d}}\backslash\mathcal{B}_{n,\delta}\right)+\mathbb{P}\!\left(\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{n,1}\in\mathcal{A}_{\bar{d}}\backslash\mathcal{B}_{n,\delta}\right))

\displaystyle=o\Big(2^{-\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}}\Big).

Thus, combining (
5.39
), (
5.40
) and (
5.41
), we arrive at

(5.42)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(h_{n,1}(i)\leq h_{i},\ell_{n}(i)\geq\ell_{i},d_{n}(i)\geq d_{i},i\in[k])=\mathbb{E}\left[g_{n}(\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{n,1})\right](1+o(1))+o\Big(2^{-\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}}\Big).

As the elements of
\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{n,1}
are i.i.d., we obtain

(5.43)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[g_{n}(\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{n,1})]

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{P}\!\left(h_{n,1}(i)\leq h_{i},\ell_{n}(i)\geq\ell_{i},\mathrm{d}_{n}(i)\geq d_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{P}\!\left(h_{n,1}(i)\leq h_{i},\ell_{n}(i)\geq\ell_{i}\,|\,\mathrm{d}_{n}(i)\geq d_{i}\right)\mathbb{P}\!\left(\mathrm{d}_{n}(i)\geq d_{i}\right).

By combining Proposition
4.5
, Proposition
5.1
and (
5.42
), we thus have

(5.44)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}{}

\displaystyle(h_{n,1}(i)\leq h_{i},\ell_{n}(i)\geq\ell_{i},d_{n}(i)\geq d_{i},i\in[k])

\displaystyle=(1+o(1))2^{-\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{P}\!\left(M\sqrt{\frac{a_{i}}{4-a_{i}}}+N\sqrt{1-\frac{a_{i}}{4-a_{i}}}\leq y_{i},M>x_{i}\right),

as desired, which concludes the proof.
∎

6.
Joint properties of vertices with a given label

This section is devoted to studying the joint behaviour of the degree and depth of vertices with a given label. We use the preliminary results proved in Section
4
to obtain the required results. The section is structured in the same way as Section
5
.

We let
\ell\in[n]
be increasing in
n
such that
\ell
diverges as
n\to\infty
, and set

(6.1)

h:=\log\ell+x\sqrt{\log\ell},\quad\begin{cases}d:=\log(n/\ell)+y\sqrt{\log(n/\ell)}&\mbox{if }\ell=o(n),\\
d\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\text{ fixed}&\mbox{otherwise,}\end{cases}\quad t_{n}:=\lceil\log\ell\rceil,

with
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, we define, for the same
y\in\mathbb{R}
used in the definition of
d
,
\rho\in(0,1)
and with
P(\rho)\sim\text{Poi}(\log(1/\rho))
,

(6.2)

\text{Pr}=\text{Pr}(y,\rho,\ell):=\begin{cases}\Phi(y)&\mbox{if }\ell=o(n),\\
\mathbb{P}\!\left(P(\rho)\leq d\right)&\mbox{if }\ell=(1+o(1))\rho n,\\
1&\mbox{if }\ell=n-o(n).\end{cases}

We then have the following result.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
d,h,\ell
and
t_{n}
be as in (
6.1
) with
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
, and recall
\mathrm{Pr}
from (
6.2
), with
y\in\mathbb{R},\rho\in(0,1)
. Then,

(6.3)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\!\left(h_{n,1}(1)\leq h,\mathrm{d}_{n}(1)\leq d\,|\,\ell_{n}(1)=\ell\right)=\Phi(x)\mathrm{Pr}.

Remark 6.2
.

As is the case in Remark
5.2
, it follows from Lemma
4.11
and Remark
4.12
that the result in Proposition
6.1
holds when substituting
h_{n}(i)
for
h_{n,1}(i)
as well.

Proof.

We start by using Lemmas
4.1
and
4.10
to obtain

(6.4)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}{}

\displaystyle(h_{n,1}(1)\leq h,\mathrm{d}_{n}(1)\leq d\,|\,\ell_{n}(1)=\ell)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{P}\!\left(h_{n,1}(1)\leq h,\mathrm{d}_{n}(1)\leq d,\ell_{n}(1)=\ell\right)}{\mathbb{P}\!\left(\ell_{n}(1)=\ell\right)}

\displaystyle=n\mathbb{E}\Big[2^{-([\ell+1,n]\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)|+1)}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|[\ell+1,n]\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)|\leq d\}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\ell\in\mathcal{S}_{n,1}\}}\mathbb{P}\big(X_{n,\ell,2}(1)\leq h-1\big|\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)\big)\Big].

We observe that we can divide the terms in the expected value into three parts which are pairwise independent. Namely, the exponent and the first indicator, the second indicator, and finally the conditional probability, respectively. Indeed, the exponent and first indicator only depend on
[\ell+1,n]\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)
, the second indicator only on the event
\{\ell\in\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)\}
, and the conditional probability depends only on
[t_{n},\ell-1]\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)
. Since
\ell>\lceil\log\ell\rceil=t_{n}
for all
n
sufficiently large, these three parts depend on disjoint sets of independent random variables and are hence independent. As a result, we obtain

(6.5)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[{}

\displaystyle 2^{-([\ell+1,n]\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)|+1)}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|[\ell+1,n]\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)|\leq d\}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\ell\in\mathcal{S}_{n,1}\}}\mathbb{P}\!\left(X_{n,\ell,2}(1)\leq h-1\,|\,\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)\right)\big]

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{P}\!\left(\ell\in\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)\right)\mathbb{E}\left[2^{-|[\ell+1,n]\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)|}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|[\ell+1,n]\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)|\leq d\}}\right]\mathbb{P}\!\left(X_{n,\ell,2}(1)\leq h-1\right).

The first probability on the right-hand side equals
2/\ell
. The expected value on the right-hand side can be rewritten as follows. First, by summing over all possible truncated selection sets
\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)
,

(6.6)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[{}

\displaystyle 2^{-|[\ell+1,n]\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)|}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|[\ell+1,n]\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)|\leq d\}}\Big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq[\ell+1,n]\\
|S|=m\end{subarray}}2^{-m}\prod_{j\in S}\frac{2}{j}\prod_{j\not\in S}\Big(1-\frac{2}{j}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq\{\ell+1,\ldots,n\}\\
|S|=m\end{subarray}}\prod_{j\in S}\frac{1}{j}\prod_{j\not\in S}\Big(1-\frac{1}{j}\Big)\prod_{j\not\in S}\frac{j-2}{j-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\ell-1}{n-1}\sum_{m=0}^{d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq\{\ell+1,\ldots,n\}\\
|S|=m\end{subarray}}\prod_{j\in S}\frac{j-1}{j-2}\prod_{j\in S}\frac{1}{j}\prod_{j\not\in S}\Big(1-\frac{1}{j}\Big).

As
\ell
diverges with
n
,
(\ell-1)/(n-1)=(1+o(1))\ell/n
. Defining
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}(1)
as a random subset of
\{\ell+1,\ldots,n\}
which includes each integer
j\in\{\ell+1,\ldots,n\}
independently with probability
1/j
, the double sum and triple product can be interpreted as

(6.7)

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{\{|\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}(1)|\leq d\}}\prod_{j=\ell+1}^{n}\Big(1+\mathbbm{1}_{\{j\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}(1)\}}\frac{1}{j-2}\Big)\right].

Combining both observations we obtain

(6.8)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[{}

\displaystyle 2^{-|[\ell+1,n]\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)|}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|[\ell+1,n]\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)|\leq d\}}\Big]

\displaystyle=(1+o(1))\frac{\ell}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{\{|\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}(1)|\leq d\}}\prod_{j=\ell+1}^{n}\Big(1+\mathbbm{1}_{\{j\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}(1)\}}\frac{1}{j-2}\Big)\right].

By bounding the product from below by one and using (
6.5
), we obtain the lower bound

(6.9)

\displaystyle n\mathbb{E}\big[{}

\displaystyle 2^{-([\ell+1,n]\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)|+1)}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|[\ell+1,n]\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)|\leq d\}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\ell\in\mathcal{S}_{n,1}\}}\mathbb{P}\!\left(X_{n,\ell,2}(1)\leq h-1\,|\,\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)\right)\big]

\displaystyle\geq(1+o(1))\mathbb{P}\!\left(|\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}(1)|\leq d\right)\mathbb{P}\!\left(X_{n,\ell,2}(1)\leq h-1\right)

\displaystyle=\Phi(x)\text{Pr}+o(1),

where the last step follows if we assume that the two probabilities in the first step are asymptotically equal to
Pr
and
\Phi(x)
, respectively. For an upper bound, we first expand the product in the expected value of (
6.8
) to obtain

(6.10)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[{}

\displaystyle 2^{-|[\ell+1,n]\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)|}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|[\ell+1,n]\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)|\leq d\}}\Big]

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle(1+o(1))\frac{\ell}{n}\Bigg(\mathbb{P}\!\left(|\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}(1)|\leq d\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{m=1}^{n-\ell}\sum_{\ell+1\leq j_{1}<\ldots<j_{m}\leq n}\Big(\prod_{t=1}^{m}\frac{1}{j_{t}-2}\Big)\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{\{|\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}(1)|\leq d\}}\prod_{t=1}^{m}\mathbbm{1}_{\{j_{t}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}(1)\}}\right]\Bigg).

We then use the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to bound

(6.11)

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n-\ell}{}

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell+1\leq j_{1}<\ldots<j_{m}\leq n}\Big(\prod_{t=1}^{m}\frac{1}{j_{t}-2}\Big)\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{\{|\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}(1)|\leq d\}}\prod_{t=1}^{m}\mathbbm{1}_{\{j_{t}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}(1)\}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\!\left(|\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}(1)|\leq d\right)^{1/2}\sum_{m=1}^{n-\ell}\sum_{\ell+1\leq j_{1}<\ldots<j_{m}\leq n}\prod_{t=1}^{m}\bigg(\frac{1}{j_{t}-2}\mathbb{P}\!\left(j_{t}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}(1)\right)^{1/2}\bigg).

As
\mathbb{P}(j_{t}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}(1))=1/j_{t}\leq 1/(j_{t}-2)
, we arrive at the upper bound

(6.12)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\!\left(|\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}(1)|\leq d\right)^{1/2}\sum_{m=1}^{n-\ell}{}

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell-1\leq j_{1}<\ldots<j_{m}\leq n-2}\prod_{t=1}^{m}\frac{1}{j_{t}^{3/2}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\!\left(|\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}(1)|\leq d\right)^{1/2}{}

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n-\ell}\big(2(\ell-2)^{-1/2}\big)^{m}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\!\left(|\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}(1)|\leq d\right)^{1/2}{}

\displaystyle\frac{2}{(\ell-2)^{1/2}-2}.

Combining this with (
6.10
) and (
6.5
) and since
\ell
diverges with
n
, we thus obtain the upper bound

(6.13)

\displaystyle n\mathbb{E}\big[{}

\displaystyle 2^{-([\ell+1,n]\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)|+1)}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|[\ell+1,n]\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)|\leq d\}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\ell\in\mathcal{S}_{n,1}\}}\mathbb{P}\!\left(X_{n,\ell,2}(1)\leq h-1\,|\,\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)\right)\big]

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle(1+o(1))\Big(\mathbb{P}\!\left(|\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}(1)|\leq d\right)+\mathbb{P}\!\left(|\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}(1)|\leq d\right)^{1/2}\frac{2}{(\ell-2)^{1/2}+2}\Big)\mathbb{P}\!\left(X_{n,\ell,2}(1)\leq h-1\right)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\Phi(x)\text{Pr}+o(1),

when we (again) assume that the first and last probability on the second line are asymptotically equal to
Pr
and
\Phi(x)
, respectively. As this upper bound matches the lower bound in (
6.9
), we arrive at the desired result.

It remains to prove that

(6.14)

\mathbb{P}\!\left(|\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}(1)|\leq d\right)=\text{Pr}+o(1),\qquad\mathbb{P}\!\left(X_{n,\ell,2}(1)\leq h-1\right)=\Phi(x)+o(1).

For the first result, let us start by considering
\ell=o(n)
, so that
d:=\log(n/\ell)+y\sqrt{\log(n/\ell)}
for
y\in\mathbb{R}
fixed. It then follows from Lindeberg’s conditions
[
11
, Theorem
3.4.5
]
that

(6.15)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\!\left(|\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}(1)|\leq d\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\!\left(\frac{|\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}(1)|-\mathbb{E}[|\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}(1)|]}{\sqrt{{\rm Var}(|\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}(1)|)}}\leq\frac{\log(n/\ell)+y\sqrt{\log(n/\ell)}-\mathbb{E}[|\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}(1)|]}{\sqrt{{\rm Var}(|\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}(1)|)}}\right)

\displaystyle=\Phi(y)+o(1),

since

(6.16)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}(1)|]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=\ell+1}^{n}\frac{1}{j}=\log(n/\ell)+\mathcal{O}(1),\qquad

\displaystyle{\rm Var}(|\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}(1)|)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=\ell+1}^{n}\frac{1}{j}\Big(1-\frac{1}{j}\Big)=\log(n/\ell)+\mathcal{O}(1),

when
\ell=o(n)
. When
\ell=(1+o(1))\rho n
for some
\rho\in(0,1)
, we recall that
d\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
is fixed, and instead use that for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

(6.17)

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{t|\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}(1)|}\right]=\prod_{j=\ell+1}^{n}\Big(1-\frac{1}{j}+\mathrm{e}^{t}\frac{1}{j}\Big)=\prod_{j=\ell+1}^{n}\Big(1+\big(\mathrm{e}^{t}-1\big)\frac{1}{j}\Big).

Using that
x-x^{2}\leq\log(1+x)\leq x
for all
x>0
and that

(6.18)

\sum_{j=\ell+1}^{n}\frac{1}{j}=(1+o(1))\int_{\rho}^{1}x^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}x=(1+o(1))\log(1/\rho),

yields

(6.19)

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{t|\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}(1)|}\right]=\mathrm{e}^{(\mathrm{e}^{t}-1)\log(1/\rho)}+o(1).

Since, for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
, the moment generating function (MGF) of
|\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}(1)|
converges to the MGF of
P(\rho)
,

(6.20)

\mathbb{P}\!\left(|\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}(1)|\leq d\right)=\mathbb{P}\!\left(P(\rho)\leq d\right)+o(1).

Finally, when
\ell=n-o(n)
, using Markov’s inequality yields

(6.21)

\mathbb{P}\!\left(|\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}(1)|=0\right)\geq 1-\mathbb{E}[|\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}(1)|]=1-\sum_{j=\ell+1}^{n}\frac{1}{j}=1-(1+o(1))\log(n/\ell)=1-o(1),

as desired.

For the latter result in (
6.14
) we set
\widetilde{Q}_{n}:=|[t_{n},\ell-1]\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,1}(1)|
, let
(I^{n}_{j})_{j\in[n]}
denote independent Bernoulli random variables with success probability
1/2
, also independent of
\widetilde{Q}_{n}
, and write

(6.22)

\frac{2X_{n,\ell,2}(1)-2\log\ell}{2\sqrt{\log\ell}}=\frac{2\sum_{j=1}^{\widetilde{Q}_{n}}I^{\widetilde{Q}_{n}}_{j}-\widetilde{Q}_{n}}{\sqrt{\widetilde{Q}_{n}}}\sqrt{\frac{\widetilde{Q}_{n}}{4\log\ell}}+\frac{\widetilde{Q}_{n}-\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{Q}_{n}]}{\sqrt{{\rm Var}(\widetilde{Q}_{n})}}\sqrt{\frac{{\rm Var}(\widetilde{Q}_{n})}{4\log\ell}}+\frac{\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{Q}_{n}]-2\log\ell}{2\sqrt{\log\ell}}.

We then use a similar approach as (
5.19
). In particular, we use the Skorokhod embedding which provides us with a coupling of the random variables
\widetilde{Q}_{n}
and
(I_{i}^{n})_{i\in[n]}
such that

(6.23)

\frac{2\sum_{j=1}^{n}I^{n}_{j}-n}{\sqrt{n}}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{a.s.}}N_{1},\qquad\frac{\widetilde{Q}_{n}-\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{Q}_{n}]}{\sqrt{{\rm Var}(\widetilde{Q}_{n})}}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{a.s.}}N_{2},

where
N_{1},N_{2}
are two independent standard normal random variables. Moreover, a straightforward computation of
\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{Q}_{n}]
and
{\rm Var}(\widetilde{Q}_{n})
shows that
\widetilde{Q}_{n}/(2\log\ell)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{a.s.}}1
(and hence
\widetilde{Q}_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{a.s.}}\infty
), that
{\rm Var}(\widetilde{Q}_{n})/(2\log\ell)\to 1
and that
\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{Q}_{n}]-2\log\ell=o(\sqrt{\log\ell})
as
n\to\infty
. As a result, it follows that

(6.24)

\frac{2X_{n,\ell,2}(1)-2\log\ell}{2\sqrt{\log\ell}}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{d}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}N_{1}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}N_{2}\overset{d}{=}N,

where
N
is a standard normal random variable. As a result, recalling that
h:=\log\ell+x\sqrt{\log\ell}
,

(6.25)

\mathbb{P}\!\left(X_{n,\ell,2}(1)\leq h-1\right)=\mathbb{P}\!\left(\frac{2X_{n,\ell,2}(1)-2\log\ell}{2\sqrt{\log\ell}}\leq\frac{h-1-\log\ell}{\sqrt{\log\ell}}\right)=\Phi(x)+o(1),

as required, which concludes the proof.
∎

This section is devoted to proving Theorem

. We first provide some additional theory on top of what is introduced in Section

7.1.
Convergence of marked point processes via finite dimensional distributions

We recall that, as discussed in Section
3
, Theorem
2.2
can be understood as the weak convergence of the marked point process
\mathcal{M}\mathcal{P}^{(n_{t})}
to
\mathcal{M}\mathcal{P}^{\varepsilon}
, as defined in (
3.3
) and (
3.2
), respectively. The approach to prove this is via the convergence of its finite dimensional distributions (FDDs) along suitable subsequences. The FDDs of a random measure
\mathcal{P}
on
\mathcal{X}
are defined as the joint distributions, for all finite families of bounded Borel sets
(B_{1},\ldots,B_{k})\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})^{k}
, of the random variables
(\mathcal{P}(B_{1}),\ldots,\mathcal{P}(B_{k}))
, see
[
6
, Definition
9.2.
II]
. Moreover, by
[
6
, Proposition
9.2.
III]
, the distribution of a random measure
\mathcal{P}
on
\mathcal{X}
is completely determined by the FDDs for all finite families
(B_{1},\ldots,B_{k})
of
disjoint
sets from a semiring
\mathcal{A}
that generates
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})
. In our case, we consider the marked point process
\mathcal{M}\mathcal{P}^{(n)}
on
\mathcal{X}:=\mathbb{Z}^{*}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
, see (
3.2
). Here, we let

(7.1)

\mathcal{A}:=\{\{s\}\times(a,b]\times(c,d]:s\in\mathbb{Z},a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{R}\}\cup\{[s,\infty]\times(a,b]\times(c,d]:s\in\mathbb{Z},a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{R}\}

be the semiring that generates
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{Z}^{*}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

Recall the counting measures
X_{s}^{(n)}(B),X_{\geq s}^{(n)}(B),\widetilde{X}_{s}^{(n)}(B),\widetilde{X}_{\geq s}^{(n)}(B)
defined in (
3.17
) (in terms of the Kingman
n
-coalescent) and
X_{s}(B),X_{\geq s}(B)
defined in (
3.4
). We observe that

\widetilde{X}_{s}^{(n)}(B)=\mathcal{M}\mathcal{P}^{(n)}(\{s\}\times B),\widetilde{X}_{\geq s}^{(n)}(B)=\mathcal{M}\mathcal{P}^{(n)}([s,\infty]\times B),X_{s}(B)=\mathcal{M}\mathcal{P}^{\varepsilon}(\{s\}\times B)
and
X_{\geq s}(B)=\mathcal{M}\mathcal{P}^{\varepsilon}([s,\infty]\times B)
. As a result, the convergence of the FDDs of
\mathcal{M}\mathcal{P}^{(n_{t})}
to the FDDs of
\mathcal{M}\mathcal{P}^{\varepsilon}
can be obtained via the convergence of any finite collection of these counting measures.

For any
K\in\mathbb{N}
, take any (fixed) increasing integer sequence
(s_{m})_{m\in[K]}
and let
0\leq K^{\prime}:=\min\{m:s_{m+1}=s_{K}\}
. Also, fix any sequence
(B_{m})_{m\in[K]}
with
B_{m}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that
B_{m}\cap B_{\ell}=\varnothing
when
s_{m}=s_{\ell}
and
m\neq\ell
. The conditions on the sets
B_{m}
ensure that the elements
\{s_{1}\}\times B_{1},\ldots,\{s_{K}^{\prime}\}\times B_{K^{\prime}},\{s_{K^{\prime}+1},\ldots\}\times B_{K^{\prime}+1},\ldots,\{s_{K},\ldots\}\times B_{K}
of
\mathcal{A}
are disjoint. We are thus required to prove the joint distributional convergence of the random variables

(7.2)

(\widetilde{X}_{s_{1}}^{(n)}(B_{1}),\ldots,\widetilde{X}^{(n)}_{s_{K^{\prime}}}(B_{K^{\prime}}),\widetilde{X}^{(n)}_{\geq s_{K^{\prime}+1}}(B_{K^{\prime}+1}),\ldots,\widetilde{X}_{\geq s_{K}}^{(n)}(B_{K})),

to prove Theorem
2.2
. We use the method of moments combined with Proposition
3.1
to achieve this:

Proof of Theorem
2.2
subject to Proposition
3.1
.

As discussed, it suffices to prove the weak convergence of
\mathcal{M}\mathcal{P}^{(n_{t})}
to
\mathcal{M}\mathcal{P}^{\varepsilon}
along subsequences
(n_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\varepsilon_{n_{t}}\to\varepsilon
(where
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
) as
t\to\infty
. In turn, this is implied by the convergence of the FDDs, i.e., by the joint convergence of the counting measures
\widetilde{X}_{s}^{(n)}(B),\widetilde{X}_{\geq s}^{(n)}(B)
of finite collections of disjoint subsets of
\mathcal{A}
(see (
7.1
)).

We recall that the points
P_{i}
in the definition of the variables
X_{s}(B),X_{\geq s}(B)
in (
3.4
) are the points of the Poisson point process
\mathcal{P}
with intensity measure

\mathcal{P}
,

(7.3)

We also recall that
(n_{\ell})_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}

\varepsilon_{n_{\ell}}\to\varepsilon
as
\ell\to\infty
. We now take

and for any
K\in\mathbb{N}
consider any fixed non-decreasing integer sequence
(s_{m})_{m\in[K]}
. By the choice of

In this section we provide the final steps that build on Propositions

and
6.1
to prove Theorems

and
3.7
. In particular, we show how to include the graph distance between vertices
1,\ldots,k
in the Kingman
n
-coalescent. As mentioned at the end of Section

and
3.7

and

Intuitively, the graph distance between vertices can be related to their (truncated) depth. By the definition of
\tau_{k}
, the largest common ancestor of any two distinct vertices
i,j\in[k]
in the random recursive tree has label at most
\tau_{k}
and hence the sum of the depths and truncated depths of vertices
i
and
j
form an upper and lower bound for the graph distance between these vertices in the Kingman
n
-coalescent, respectively. Since the depth and the truncated depth are asymptotically equal under certain constraints on the truncation sequence
t_{n}
(see Lemma
4.11
and Remark
4.12
), and since
(\tau_{k})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
forms a tight sequence of random variables by Lemma
4.9
, these bounds on the graph distance are sufficiently sharp. Using the largest common ancestor to provide a lower bound on the graph distance has been used by Munsonius and Rüschendorf for
b
-are recursive trees
[
29
]
and by Ryvkina for random split trees
[
33
]
, previously.

We formalise the above intuition in the remainder of the section, in which we prove Theorems
3.5
and
3.7
.

Proof of Theorem

.

We prove (

). The proof of (

t_{n}=\min_{i\in[k]}\log\ell_{i}
. Proposition
6.1
provides that the tuple

(8.10)

\Big(d^{*}_{n}(i),\frac{h_{n,1}(i)-\log\ell_{i}}{\sqrt{\log\ell_{i}}}\Big)_{i\in[k]},

conditionally on the event
\mathcal{L}_{k}:=\{\ell_{n}(i)=\ell_{i},i\in[k]\}
, converges in distribution to
(Z_{i},N_{i})_{i\in[k]}
, where the
N_{i}
are i.i.d. standard normal random variables, also independent of the
Z_{i}
. By our choice of
t_{n}
, Lemma
4.11
and Remark
4.12
yield that the result holds when
h_{n,1}(i)
is substituted by
h_{n}(i)
as well. As in (
8.3
), we can use the trivial upper bound
{\rm dist}_{n}(i,j)\leq h_{n}(i)+h_{n}(i),i,j\in[n]
. We can thus write, similar to (
8.4
),

(8.11)

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm dist}_{n}(i,j)-(\log\ell_{i}+\log\ell_{j})}{\sqrt{\log\ell_{i}+\log\ell_{j}}}\leq{}

\displaystyle\frac{h_{n}(i)-\log\ell_{i}}{\sqrt{\ell_{i}}}\sqrt{\frac{\log\ell_{i}}{\log\ell_{i}+\log\ell_{j}}}

\displaystyle+\frac{h_{n}(j)-\log\ell_{j}}{\sqrt{\log\ell_{j}}}\sqrt{\frac{\log\ell_{j}}{\log\ell_{i}+\log\ell_{j}}}.

Define, for
(y_{i})_{i\in[k]}\in\mathbb{R}^{k},(z_{i,j})_{1\leq i<j\leq k}\in\mathbb{R}^{k(k-1)/2}
fixed,

(8.12)

h_{i}:=\log\ell_{i}+y_{i}\sqrt{\log\ell_{i}},\qquad L_{i,j}:=(\log\ell_{i}+\log\ell_{j})+z_{i,j}\sqrt{\log\ell_{i}+\log\ell_{j}},\quad 1\leq i<j\leq k.

Recall the limits
c_{i,j},c_{j,i}
of the two square-root terms on the right-hand side of (
8.11
) from (
3.18
). We thus obtain, for
(x_{i})_{i\in[k]}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
fixed, by (
8.11
) and (
8.10
) (and the remark on the
h_{n}(i)
below (
8.10
)) together with the continuous mapping theorem
[
5
]
,

(8.13)

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}{}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\!\left(d^{*}_{n}(i)\leq x_{i},h_{n}(i)\leq h_{i},i\in[k],{\rm dist}_{n}(i,j)\leq L_{i,j},1\leq i<j\leq k\,|\,\mathcal{L}_{k}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\!\left(Z_{i}\leq x_{i},N_{i}\leq y_{i},i\in[k],c_{i,j}N_{i}+c_{j,i}N_{j}\leq z_{i,j},1\leq i<j\leq k\right).

We now use the same observation made after (
8.7
). That is, on the event
\{\tau_{k}<t_{n}\}
, it holds that
{\rm dist}_{n}(i,j)\geq h_{n,1}(i)+h_{n,1}(j)
for any two distinct vertices
i,j\in[k]
. We hence have

(8.14)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}({}

\displaystyle d^{*}_{n}(i)\leq x_{i},h_{n}(i)\leq h_{i},i\in[k],{\rm dist}_{n}(i,j)\leq L_{i,j},1\leq i<j\leq k\,|\,\mathcal{L}_{k})

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(d^{*}_{n}(i)\leq x_{i},h_{n}(i)\leq h_{i},i\in[k],h_{n,1}(i)+h_{n,1}(j)\leq L_{i,j},1\leq i<j\leq k,\tau_{k}<t_{n}\,|\,\mathcal{L}_{k})

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}\!\left(\tau_{k}\geq t_{n}\,|\,\mathcal{L}_{k}\right)

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(d^{*}_{n}(i)\leq x_{i},h_{n}(i)\leq h_{i},i\in[k],h_{n,1}(i)+h_{n,1}(j)\leq L_{i,j},1\leq i<j\leq k\,|\,\mathcal{L}_{k})

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}\!\left(\tau_{k}\geq t_{n}\,|\,\mathcal{L}_{k}\right).

The last term on the right-hand side tends to zero by Lemma
4.10
. Using the right-hand side of (
8.11
) to rewrite the event
\{h_{n,1}(i)+h_{n,1}(j)\leq L_{i,j}\}
, we thus obtain

(8.15)

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}{}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(d^{*}_{n}(i)\leq x_{i},h_{n}(i)\leq h_{i},i\in[k],{\rm dist}_{n}(i,j)\leq L_{i,j},1\leq i<j\leq k\,|\,\mathcal{L}_{k})

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\!\left(Z_{i}\leq x_{i},N_{i}\leq y_{i},i\in[k],c_{i,j}N_{i}+c_{j,i}N_{j}\leq z_{i,j},1\leq i<j\leq k\right),

which matches the lower bound in (
8.13
) and concludes the proof.
∎

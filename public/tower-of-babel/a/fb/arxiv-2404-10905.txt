Intermediate Domains for Scalar Conservation Laws
Fabio Ancona, Alberto Bressan, Elio Marconi, Luca Talamini

Abstract
For a scalar conservation law with strictly convex flux, by Oleinik’s estimates the total variation of a solution with initial data u ¯ ∈ 𝐋 ∞ ​ ( ℝ ) \overline{u}\in{\bf L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}) decays like t − 1 t^{-1} . This paper introduces a class of intermediate domains 𝒫 α \cal P_{\alpha} , 0 < α < 1 0<\alpha<1 , such that for u ¯ ∈ 𝒫 α \overline{u}\in\cal P_{\alpha} a faster decay rate is achieved: Tot.Var. ​ { u ⁡ ( t , ⋅ ) } ∼ t α − 1 \hbox{Tot.Var.}\bigl\{u(t,\cdot)\bigr\}\sim t^{\alpha-1} . A key ingredient of the analysis is a “Fourier-type” decomposition of u ¯ \overline{u} into components which oscillate more and more rapidly. The results aim at extending the theory of fractional domains for analytic semigroups to an entirely nonlinear setting.



Key words:
Scalar conservation law, total variation decay, intermediate domain.

1
Introduction

Consider a scalar conservation law

u_{t}+f(u)_{x}~=~0,

(1.1)

with strictly convex flux.
By a classical result
[
9
,
19
]
, there exists a contractive semigroup
S:{\bf L}^{1}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\mapsto{\bf L}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
such that, for every initial datum

u(0,\cdot)~=~\overline{u}\in{\bf L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}),

(1.2)

the
trajectory
t\mapsto u(t)=S_{t}\overline{u}
is the unique entropy weak solution of the Cauchy problem.

It is well known that, even for smooth initial data, the solution can develop shocks in finite time.
Taking an abstract point of view, consider the operator
Au\doteq\frac{\partial}{\partial x}f(u)

which generates the semigroup.
Then there exists data

\overline{u}\in Dom(A)
in the domain of the generator, such that
S_{\tau}\overline{u}\notin Dom(A)

for some
\tau>0
. In other words, the domain of the generator is not positively invariant.

To address this issue,
the paper
[
10
]
introduced a definition of “generalized domain”
{\cal D}
for the operator. This consists of all initial data
\overline{u}
for which the map
t\mapsto S_{t}\overline{u}

is globally Lipschitz continuous. Notice that for the conservation law (
1.1
)
one has

{\bf L}^{1}\cap BV~\subseteq~{\cal D}.

Using the fact that the semigroup is contractive, it is easy to show that this generalized domain is
positively invariant. Indeed, the quantity

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0+}~{\bigl\|S_{t+\varepsilon}\overline{u}-S_{t}\overline{u}\bigr\|_{{\bf L}^{1}}\over\varepsilon}

is a non-increasing function of
t
.

Our present aim is to study intermediate domains

{\cal D}_{\alpha}~\subset~{\bf L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}),\qquad\qquad 0<\alpha<1,

(1.3)

related to the decay properties of the corresponding trajectories of (
1.1
).
As in
[
1
]
we define

{\cal D}_{\alpha}~\doteq~\Big\{\overline{u}\in{\bf L}^{1}(\mathbb{R})\,;~~\sup_{0<t<1}t^{-\alpha}\bigl\|S_{t}\overline{u}-\overline{u}\|_{{\bf L}^{1}}<+\infty\Big\}.

(1.4)

We also consider the domains (slightly different from the ones considered in
[
1
]
)

\widetilde{\cal D}_{\alpha}~\doteq~\Big\{\overline{u}\in{\bf L}^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap{\bf L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\,;~~\sup_{0<t<1}t^{1-\alpha}\cdot\hbox{Tot.Var.}\bigl\{S_{t}\overline{u}\}<+\infty\Big\}.

(1.5)

The domains
{\cal D}_{\alpha}
arise naturally in connection with balance laws:

u_{t}+f(u)_{x}~=~g(t,x).

(1.6)

Indeed, as shown in
[
1
]
, one has

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
f\in{\cal C}^{2}
with
f^{\prime\prime}(u)\geq c>0
for all
u\in\mathbb{R}
.
Consider a compactly supported solution
u=u(t,x)
of (
1.6
), and assume that
the source term
satisfies

\bigl\|g(t,\cdot)\bigr\|_{{\bf L}^{1}}~\leq~C\qquad\qquad\hbox{for all}~t\in[0,T].

(1.7)

Then for every
0<t\leq T
, one has
u(t,\cdot)\in{\cal D}_{1/2}
.

We remark, however, that some of the functions
u(t,\cdot)
can be unbounded.
In particular, they can have infinite total variation.

In the theory of linear analytic semigroups
[
16
,
20
]
, intermediate domains
arise naturally as domains of fractional powers of sectorial operators.
The faster decay of solutions is usually related to higher
Sobolev regularity of the initial data. In particular, this theory applies to
semilinear equations of the form

u_{t}-\Delta u~=~F(x,u,\nabla u),\qquad\qquad u(0,\cdot)=\overline{u}.

(1.8)

Under natural assumptions (see
[
16
]
for details), this Cauchy problem is well posed provided that
the initial datum
\overline{u}
lies in the domain of some fractional power

(-\Delta)^{\alpha}
of the generator.

Our eventual goal is to develop a similar theory of intermediate domains
for nonlinear semigroups generated by conservation laws.
In particular, we conjecture that the Cauchy problem for a genuinely nonlinear

2\times 2
hyperbolic system with
{\bf L}^{\infty}
initial data
[
4
,
15
]

is well posed within an intermediate domain such as (
1.4
) or (
1.5
).

As a first step in this research program, here we focus our attention
on the scalar conservation law (
1.1
), seeking conditions on the initial data

\overline{u}\in{\bf L}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
that will imply
\overline{u}\in{\cal D}_{\alpha}
or
\overline{u}\in\widetilde{\cal D}_{\alpha}
,
respectively.

Assumptions that imply
\overline{u}\in{\cal D}_{\alpha}
can be readily
formulated in terms of fractional Sobolev regularity.
On the other hand, conditions that guarantee a faster decay rate
of the total variation are more subtle. Here we consider the assumption

(P
α
)

For every
\lambda\in\,]0,1]
, there exists an open set
V(\lambda)\subset\mathbb{R}
such that the following holds.

\hbox{meas}\bigl(V(\lambda)\bigr)~\leq~C\,\lambda^{\alpha},

(1.9)

\hbox{Tot.Var.}\bigl\{\overline{u}\,;~\mathbb{R}\setminus V(\lambda)\bigr\}~\leq~C\,\lambda^{\alpha-1},

(1.10)

for some constant
C
independent of
\lambda
.

Roughly speaking,
\overline{u}
can have unbounded variation, but most of its variation
should be concentrated on a set with small Lebesgue measure.
Our main result establishes the implication

\overline{u}~~\hbox{satisfies}~{\bf(P_{\alpha})}\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\overline{u}\in\widetilde{\cal D}_{\alpha}

(1.11)

when
1/2<\alpha<1
. On the other hand, a counterexample shows that the above implication
fails for
\alpha\leq 1/2
.
The proof of (
1.11
)
relies on a structural result
for functions satisfying

{\bf(P_{\alpha})}
, which is of independent interest.
Indeed, Theorem
5.1

provides a nonlinear “Fourier-type” decomposition of such functions,
in components which oscillate more and more rapidly.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2

we describe a general class of metric interpolation spaces,
for functions defined on a set
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
. This yields a
natural way to formulate conditions such as
(
{\bf P}_{\alpha}
)
, in a general setting.

Section
3
contains some examples. The first one (Fig.
1
)
shows how to construct an initial datum
\overline{u}
with unbounded variation, such that
\overline{u}\in\widetilde{\cal D}_{\alpha}
, for any given
0<\alpha<1
. We then consider initial data
consisting of a packet of triangular waves (Fig.
3
). By suitably
choosing the size and distance of these triangular blocks we show that,
if
0<\alpha\leq 1/2
, then there
exists an initial datum

\overline{u}
that satisfies
(
{\bf P}_{\alpha}
)
and yet
\overline{u}\notin\widetilde{\cal D}_{\beta}

for any
\beta\in\,]0,1[\,
.
As stated in Proposition
3.2
, the implication (
1.11
) thus cannot hold for

\alpha\leq 1/2
.

Section
4
is concerned with the intermediate domain
{\cal D}_{\alpha}
. For
0<\alpha<1
we
prove that any one of the conditions: (i)
\overline{u}
lies in the fractional Sobolev space
W^{\alpha,1}(\mathbb{R})
, or
(ii)
\overline{u}
satisfies
(
{\bf P}_{\alpha}
)
,
implies
\overline{u}\in{\cal D}_{\alpha}
. These results are valid for any flux
f\in{\cal C}^{1}
, not necessarily convex.

Section
5
establishes further properties of functions
which satisfy
(
{\bf P}_{\alpha}
)
, proving a useful decomposition result, stated in
Theorem
5.1
.
Finally, in Section
6
we prove our main theorem,
showing that for
1/2<\alpha<1

the property
(
{\bf P}_{\alpha}
)
implies that
\overline{u}\in\widetilde{\cal D}_{\alpha}
.
To simplify the exposition, the proofs will first be given for Burgers’ equation.
In Remark
6.2
we observe that all results remain valid for a conservation law with
uniformly convex flux.

For an introduction to the theory of
conservation laws we refer to
[
6
,
7
,
12
,
17
]
. Results on the decay of solutions to conservation laws in generalized BV spaces can be found in
[

]
.
In addition to genuinely nonlinear conservation laws, several other examples

see in particular

2
A family of metric interpolation spaces

Consider an open set
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
and let
X
be a Banach space contained in the set
L^{0}(\Omega)
of Lebesgue measurable functions
f:\Omega\mapsto\mathbb{R}
. Let
0<\alpha<1
be given.
A distance function
d(\cdot,\cdot):L^{0}(\Omega)\times L^{0}(\Omega)\to[0,+\infty]

can be defined as follows. For any
\lambda\in\,]0,1]
, we begin by
setting

d^{\lambda}(f,g)~\doteq~d^{\lambda}(f-g,0),

(2.1)

\begin{array}[]{l}d^{\lambda}(f,0)~\doteq~\inf\Big\{C\geq 0\,;~~\hbox{there exists $\widetilde{f}\in X$ such that}\\[8.53581pt]
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\|\widetilde{f}\|_{X}\,\leq C\,\lambda^{\alpha-1}\,,\qquad\hbox{meas}\bigl\{x\in\Omega\,;~f(x)\not=\widetilde{f}(x)\bigr\}\,\leq\,C\,\lambda^{\alpha}\Big\}.\end{array}

(2.2)

Finally, we define

d(f,g)~\doteq~\sup_{0<\lambda\leq 1}~d^{\lambda}(f,g).

(2.3)

By possibly identifying couples of functions
f,g,
such that
d(f,g)=0
, we claim that (
2.3
)
yields a distance on the set of Lebesgue measurable functions
f\in L^{0}(\Omega)
for
which
d(f,0)<\infty
. This is usually
a strictly larger set than
X
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
0<\alpha<1
be given, and let
d(\cdot,\cdot)
be as in (
2.1
)–(
2.3
).
Then the following properties hold.

(i)

d(f,g)=d(g,f)\geq 0
.

(ii)

If
f\in X
, then
d(f,0)\leq\|f\|_{X}
.

(iii)

d(f,h)~\leq~d(f,g)+d(g,h)
.

Proof.

1.
Part (i) is trivial. If
f\in X
, choosing
\widetilde{f}=f
in (
2.2
), we see that
d^{\lambda}(f,0)\leq\|f\|_{X}
for every
\lambda>0
. This yields (ii).

2.
We now check that each
d^{\lambda}(\cdot,\cdot)
,
0<\lambda\leq 1
, satisfies the triangle inequality.
Toward this goal,
let
f_{1},f_{2}
be measurable functions such that

d^{\lambda}(f_{i},0)=C_{i},\qquad i=1,2.

We then need to show that

d^{\lambda}(f_{1},f_{2})~\doteq~d^{\lambda}(f_{1}-f_{2},0)~\leq~C_{1}+C_{2}\,.

(2.4)

Given
\varepsilon>0
, by assumption there exist functions
\widetilde{f}_{1},\widetilde{f}_{2}\in X
such that

\|\widetilde{f}_{i}\|_{X}\,\leq\,C_{i}\lambda^{\alpha-1}+\varepsilon,\qquad\hbox{meas}\bigl\{x\in\Omega\,;~\widetilde{f}_{i}(x)\not=f_{i}(x)\bigr\}\,\leq\,C_{i}\lambda^{\alpha}+\varepsilon,\qquad\qquad i=1,2.

(2.5)

Then the function
\widetilde{f}_{1}-\widetilde{f}_{2}
satisfies

\|\widetilde{f}_{1}-\widetilde{f}_{2}\|_{X}~\leq~(C_{1}+C_{2})\lambda^{\alpha-1}+2\varepsilon,

\hbox{meas}\Big\{x\in\Omega\,;~\widetilde{f}_{1}(x)-\widetilde{f}_{2}(x)\not=f_{1}(x)-f_{2}(x)\Big\}~\leq~(C_{1}+C_{2})\lambda^{\alpha}+2\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, this proves (
2.4
).

3.

In turn, the triangle inequality (iii) follows from

\begin{array}[]{rl}d(f-g,0)&=~\displaystyle\sup_{\lambda\in]0,1]}~d^{\lambda}(f-g,0)~\leq~\sup_{\lambda\in]0,1]}~\Big(d^{\lambda}(f,0)+d^{\lambda}(g,0)\Big)\\[11.38109pt]
&\leq~\displaystyle\sup_{\lambda\in]0,1]}~d^{\lambda}(f,0)+\sup_{\lambda\in]0,1]}~d^{\lambda}(g,0)~=~d(f,0)+d(g,0).\end{array}

MM

Remark 2.2
.

One should keep in mind that, in general, the balls
\{g\in{L^{0}(\Omega)}\,;~~d(g,f)\leq r\}
are not convex. Moreover, the function
f\mapsto d(f,0)
is not a norm.

In connection with
(
{\bf P}_{\alpha}
)
, for
0<\alpha<1

we consider the distances
d^{\lambda}(f,g)
as in (
2.1
), where now

\begin{array}[]{l}d^{\lambda}(f,0)~\doteq~\inf\Big\{C\geq 0\,;~~\hbox{there exists $\widetilde{f}\in{\bf L}^{1}(\mathbb{R})$ such that}\\[8.53581pt]
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\hbox{Tot.Var.}\{\widetilde{f}\}\leq C\,\lambda^{\alpha-1},\quad\hbox{meas}\{x\in\mathbb{R}\,;~f(x)\neq\widetilde{f}(x)\}\leq C\,\lambda^{\alpha}\Big\}.\end{array}

(2.6)

Finally, given
\overline{u}\in\mathbf{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap\mathbf{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, we
define

\|\overline{u}\|_{{\cal P}_{\alpha}}~\,\doteq~\sup_{0<\lambda\leq 1}\,d^{\lambda}(\overline{u},0)

(2.7)

and write
\overline{u}\in{\cal P}_{\alpha}
if

\|\overline{u}\|_{{\cal P}_{\alpha}}<+\infty
. Notice that this holds provided that

\overline{u}
satisfies the
condition
(
{\bf P}_{\alpha}
)
.
Throughout the following, we shall use
\|\overline{u}\|_{{\cal P}_{\alpha}}
as a convenient notation. However, as already pointed out
in Remark
2.2
, one should be aware that
\|\cdot\|_{{\cal P}_{\alpha}}
is not a norm.

3
Examples

We present here some examples, to motivate the results proved in later sections.
We consider Burgers’ equation

u_{t}+\left(u^{2}\over 2\right)_{x}~=~0.

(3.1)

Throughout the following, we use the semigroup notation
t\mapsto S_{t}\overline{u}

to denote the solution of (
3.1
) with initial data (
1.2
).

Example 3.1
.

Fix
\beta>0
and consider the decreasing sequence of points
x_{n}=n^{-\beta}
,
n\geq 1
.
As shown in Fig.
1
, define the piecewise affine function

\overline{u}(x)~=~\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}0&\hbox{if}\quad x\notin\,[0,1]\,,\\[11.38109pt]
\displaystyle{x-x_{n+1}\over x_{n}-x_{n+1}}&\hbox{if}\quad x_{n}<x<x_{n-1}\,.\end{array}\right.

(3.2)

We claim that this initial data lies in some of the subdomains
\widetilde{\cal D}_{\alpha}
,
depending on the exponent
\beta
. Indeed, fix a time
t\in\,]0,1]
.
Consider the position
x_{k}(t)
of the shock which is initially located at
x_{k}
.
By Oleinik’s inequality, the total variation of the solution
u(t,\cdot)
can be estimated by

\hbox{Tot.Var.}\bigl\{u(t,\cdot)\,;~[0,x_{k}(t)]\bigr\}~\leq~2{x_{k}(t)\over t}\,.

(3.3)

On the other hand, for
x>x_{k}(t)
, still by Oleinik’s estimates we have

\hbox{Tot.Var.}\bigl\{u(t,\cdot)\,;~[x_{k}(t),x_{1}(t)]\bigr\}~\leq~2{x_{1}(t)-x_{k}(t)\over(x_{k-1}-x_{k})+t}\,.

(3.4)

Observing that

x_{k}(t)\,\leq\,x_{k}+t,\qquad\qquad x_{1}(t)-x_{k}(t)~\leq~1+t,

from (
3.3
)-(
3.4
) we deduce

\hbox{Tot.Var.}\bigl\{u(t,\cdot)\bigr\}~\leq~2{x_{k}+t\over t}+2{1+t\over(x_{k-1}-x_{k})+t}~\leq~4+2{x_{k}\over t}+{2\over(x_{k-1}-x_{k})+t}\,.

(3.5)

Since we are assuming
x_{k}=k^{-\beta}
,
the previous estimate yields

\hbox{Tot.Var.}\bigl\{u(t,\cdot)\bigr\}~\leq~4+{2k^{-\beta}\over t}+{2\over\beta k^{-\beta-1}+t}\,.

Here
k\geq 1
is arbitrary.
Choosing
k\approx t^{-\gamma}
, we obtain

\hbox{Tot.Var.}\bigl\{u(t,\cdot)\bigr\}~\leq~{\cal O}(1)\cdot\left({t^{\beta\gamma}\over t}+{1\over t^{\gamma(\beta+1)}+t}\right)~=~{\cal O}(1)\cdot\Big(t^{\beta\gamma-1}+t^{-\gamma(\beta+1)}\Big).

Here and throughout the sequel, the Landau symbol
{\cal O}(1)
denotes a uniformly bounded quantity.
The two terms on the right hand side have similar magnitude if
\gamma=(1+2\beta)^{-1}
.
With this choice, we obtain

\hbox{Tot.Var.}\bigl\{u(t,\cdot)\bigr\}~\leq~{\cal O}(1)\cdot t^{-{\beta+1\over 2\beta+1}},

hence

\overline{u}\in\widetilde{\cal D}_{\alpha},\qquad\hbox{with}\qquad\alpha~=~1-{\beta+1\over 2\beta+1}~=~{\beta\over 2\beta+1}.

In the next examples we consider initial data consisting of one or more triangular blocks.
As shown in Fig.
2

w(0,x)~=~\overline{w}(x)~=~\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}h-\frac{2h}{\ell}\cdot|x-\ell/2|&\hbox{if}~~x\in[0,\ell],\\[2.84526pt]

(3.6)

At time
t=\ell/(2h)
, a shock is created in the solution at the point
\ell>0
. Characteristics originating from points
0<x<\ell/2
start impinging on the shock,

w(t,x)~=~\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\frac{2hx}{2ht+\ell}&\hbox{if}~~x\in[0,L(t)],\\[2.84526pt]

(3.7)

Conservation of mass implies that the shock at time

L(t)\,=\,\sqrt{\frac{\ell}{2}\,(2\,h\,t+\ell)}.

(3.8)

Always for
t\geq\ell/(2h)
, we thus have

\hbox{Tot.Var.}\{S_{t}\overline{w}\}\,=\,2\,p(t),\qquad\quad p(t)\doteq h\sqrt{\frac{2\ell}{2ht+\ell}}.

We notice for later use that the (decreasing) function

p(t)\,\geq\,\sqrt{h\,\ell\over 2t}\qquad\text{for all \; $t\geq\ell/2h$.}

(3.10)

and that the (increasing) function
L(t)
satisfies the upper bound

L(t)\,\leq\,\sqrt{2\,\ell\,h\,t}\qquad\text{for all \; $t\geq\ell/2h$.}

(3.11)

In the following proposition we denote by
{\cal C}^{0,\sigma}(\mathbb{R})
the space of Holder functions
\overline{u}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
with exponent
0<\sigma<1
, equipped with the norm

\|\overline{u}\|_{{\cal C}^{0,\sigma}}\doteq\|\overline{u}\|_{{\cal C}^{0}}+|\overline{u}|_{{\cal C}^{0,\sigma}}

|\overline{u}|_{{\cal C}^{0,\sigma}}\doteq\sup_{x<y}\frac{|\overline{u}(y)-\overline{u}(x)|}{|y-x|^{\sigma}}.

Proposition 3.2
.

There exists a compactly supported function
\overline{u}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
with the following properties:

1.

\overline{u}\in{\cal P}_{\alpha}
for every
0<\alpha\leq 1/2
;

2.

\overline{u}\in{\cal C}^{0,\sigma}(\mathbb{R})
for every
0<\sigma<1
;

3.

\overline{u}\notin\widetilde{{\cal D}}_{\beta}
for any
0<\beta<1
. Namely:

\limsup_{t\to 0+}\;t^{1-\beta}\cdot\hbox{Tot.Var.}\{S_{t}\overline{u}\}\,=\,+\infty\qquad\hbox{for all}~\;0<\beta<1.

(3.12)

Remark 3.3
.

The function
\overline{u}
constructed in Proposition
3.2
does not belong to any
{\cal P}_{\alpha}
if
1/2<\alpha<1
. This suggests that
\alpha=1/2
is a critical exponent for the decay of solution with initial data in
{\cal P}_{\alpha}
. In fact, this surprising behavior will be later confirmed by Theorem
6.1
.

Remark 3.4
.

By part 2. of Proposition
3.2
and by the embedding
{\cal C}^{0,\sigma}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow W^{s,p}_{loc}(\mathbb{R})
for every
0<s<\sigma<1
,
p\geq 1
, we obtain that

W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R})\not\subset\widetilde{{\cal D}}_{\beta}\qquad\text{for every $0<s<1$, ~$1\leq p<\infty$ and $0<\beta<1$.}

On the other hand, the inclusion
W^{\alpha,1}(\mathbb{R})\subset{\cal D}_{\alpha}
does hold for every

, as proved in Proposition
4.2
.

The proof of Proposition
3.2
is based on the following lemma.

Lemma 3.5
.

For every fixed
t\in(0,1)
, there exists a function
\widehat{u}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\widehat{u}(x)\in[0,1]&\hbox{if}~~x\in[0,1],\\[2.84526pt]
\widehat{u}(x)=0&\hbox{if}~~x\notin[0,1],\end{array}\right.

(3.13)

\|\widehat{u}\|_{{\cal P}_{\alpha}}~\leq~C_{0}\,,\qquad\hbox{for all}~\;0<\alpha\leq 1/2,\qquad\qquad\hbox{Tot.Var.}\{S_{t}\widehat{u}\}~\geq~\frac{1}{C_{0}\,t}\,.

(3.14)

\|\widehat{u}\|_{{\cal C}^{0,\sigma}}~\leq~C_{\sigma}\,\doteq\,\left(e^{2^{\left(\frac{1}{1-\sigma}\right)}}\right)^{(1-\sigma)e^{-1}}\qquad\text{for all }\sigma\in\,]0,1[.

(3.15)

Here
C_{0}
is a constant independent of
t
.

We postpone the proof of the lemma, and begin by showing that it implies
the previous proposition.

Proof of Proposition
3.2
.

Let
(t_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be a sequence decreasing to
0
sufficiently fast (to be specified later). Let

x_{0}=0,\quad x_{j}\doteq\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}2\cdot 2^{-k},\qquad I_{j}\doteq[x_{j},x_{j+1}],\qquad j=0,1,2\ldots

Let
\widehat{u}_{j}
be a function satisfying the properties (
3.13
)-(
3.14
) in Lemma
3.5
for
t=t_{j}
. Consider the rescaled functions

\overline{u}_{j}(x)~\doteq~2^{-j}\widehat{u}_{j}\big(2^{j}(x-x_{j})\big),\qquad j=0,1,2\ldots

By Lemma
3.5
, the corresponding rescaled solution of Burgers’ equation
satisfies

\hbox{Tot.Var.}\{S_{t_{j}}\overline{u}_{j}\}~=~2^{-j}\,\hbox{Tot.Var.}\{S_{t_{j}}\widehat{u}_{j}\}~\geq~\frac{1}{C_{0}\,2^{j}\,t_{j}}\,.

Define the initial data

\overline{u}~=~\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\overline{u}_{j}.

(3.16)

Notice that

{\rm supp}\;\overline{u}_{j}(t,\cdot)~\subseteq~[x_{j},x_{j}+2^{-j}(1+t)]~\subset~I_{j}\qquad\qquad\hbox{for }~t\in[0,1].

In particular for every
i\neq j
and
t<1
the supports of
S_{t}\overline{u}_{i}

The goal of this section is to study the subdomains

(4.1)

Let

(4.3)

As it is well known, functions in Sobolev spaces can be approximated by smooth functions by taking mollifications.
Let
\eta:\mathbb{R}\mapsto[0,1]

{\cal C}^{\infty}

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\eta(s)=\eta(-s)\,\in\,[0,1]&\hbox{if}\quad s\in[-1,1],\\[5.69054pt]

(4.4)

Here and in the sequel, the prime
′
denotes a derivative.
For
h>0

\eta_{h}(s)~=~{1\over h}\eta\left({s\over h}\right).

(4.5)

For

u_{h}=u\star\eta_{h}
.
The rate of

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume

\|u-u_{h}\|_{{\bf L}^{1}}~\leq~\|u\|_{W^{\alpha,1}}\cdot h^{\alpha},\qquad\qquad\|u_{h}^{\prime}\|_{{\bf L}^{1}}~\leq~C\|u\|_{W^{\alpha,1}}\cdot\frac{1}{h^{1-\alpha}},

Proof.

A direct computation yields

\begin{array}[]{rl}\displaystyle\int|u(x)-u_{h}(x)|\,dx&\displaystyle\leq~\int\!\!\!\!\int|u(x)-u(y)|\eta_{h}(y-x)\,dxdy\\[11.38109pt]
&=~\displaystyle\int\frac{1}{h}\eta(s/h)\int|u(x+s)-u(x)|\,dxds\\[11.38109pt]
&\displaystyle\leq~\int_{{-h}}^{h}\left(\frac{1}{|s|}\int\bigl|u(x+s)-u(x)\bigr|\,dx\right)ds\\[11.38109pt]

\begin{array}[]{rl}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u_{h}^{\prime}(x)|\,dx&\displaystyle=~\int\left|\int u(y)\eta^{\prime}_{h}(y-x)dy\right|\,dx\\[11.38109pt]

Theorem 6.1
.

Proof.

1.

Let

\begin{array}[]{rl}\displaystyle\sum_{i\in{\cal J}}\delta_{i}&\displaystyle\leq~2^{3/2}\cdot 2^{k/2}\cdot\sum_{q=k}^{\infty}\,\sum_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\,2^{q/2}\ell_{q}^{p}~~\displaystyle\leq~2^{3/2}\cdot C\cdot 2^{k/2}\cdot\sum_{q=k}^{\infty}\,2^{q/2}2^{-\alpha q}\\[11.38109pt]

(6.14)

where
c_{2}
is another absolute constant.
Combining (
6.11
) with (

), we obtain the desired decay rate,
under the additional assumptions (H1)-(H2).

Remark 6.2
.

If Burgers’ equation is replaced by a general scalar conservation law with a
{\cal C}^{2}
,
uniformly convex flux
f
, so that
f^{\prime\prime}\geq c>0
, from the Hopf-Lax formula we obtain that
(x_{0},u_{0})\in{\mathcal{Q}}(t)
if and only if

\int_{x_{0}}^{y}\left[\overline{u}(z)-(f^{*})^{\prime}\Big(f^{\prime}(u_{0})-\frac{z-x_{0}}{t}\Big)\right]^{+}\,dz~\geq~\int_{x_{0}}^{y}\left[\overline{u}(z)-(f^{*})^{\prime}\Big(f^{\prime}(u_{0})-\frac{z-x_{0}}{t}\Big)\right]^{-}dz

(6.24)

for all
y\geq x_{0}\,
, and

\int_{y}^{x_{0}}\left[\overline{u}(z)-(f^{*})^{\prime}\Big(f^{\prime}(u_{0})-\frac{z-x_{0}}{t}\Big)\right]^{+}dz~\leq~\int_{y}^{x_{0}}\left[\overline{u}(z)-(f^{*})^{\prime}\Big(f^{\prime}(u_{0})-\frac{z-x_{0}}{t}\Big)\right]^{-}dz

(6.25)

for all
y\leq x_{0}\,
.
Here

f^{*}(u)~\doteq~\sup_{v\in\mathbb{R}}~\bigl\{uv-f(v)\bigr\}

denotes
the Legendre transform of
f
.
By a well known property of the Legendre transform (see e.g.
[
8
,
14
]
) we have

(f^{*})^{\prime}\bigl(f^{\prime}(u_{0})\bigr)~=~u_{0}\,.

By uniform convexity it thus follows

u_{0}-\frac{1}{ct}(z-x_{0})~\leq~(f^{*})^{\prime}\Big(f^{\prime}(u_{0})-\frac{z-x_{0}}{t}\Big)\qquad\hbox{for all}~\;z>x_{0}\,,

(f^{*})^{\prime}\Big(f^{\prime}(u_{0})-\frac{z-x_{0}}{t}\Big)~\leq~u_{0}-\frac{1}{ct}(z-x_{0})\qquad\hbox{for all}~\;z<x_{0}.

Using the inequalities above, the above proof remains valid up to minor modifications. Therefore, Theorem
5.1
remains valid for any uniformly convex flux
f
.

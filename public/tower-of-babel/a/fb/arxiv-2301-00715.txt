On a convexity property of the space of almost fuchsian immersions
Samuel Bronstein, Graham Andrew Smith

Abstract
. We study the space of Hopf differentials of almost fuchsian minimal immersions of compact Riemann surfaces. We show that the extrinsic curvature of the immersion at any given point is a concave function of the Hopf differential. As a consequence, we show that the set of all such Hopf differentials is a convex subset of the space of holomorphic quadratic differentials of the surface. In addition, we address the non-equivariant case, and obtain lower and upper bounds for the size of this set.



1.
Background and main result

Convexity is always a noteworthy property whenever it is encountered. In this note, we describe a new convexity property of the space of almost fuchsian homomorphisms.

Let
\mathbb{H}^{3}
denote
3
-dimensional hyperbolic space, let
\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{3}
denote its ideal boundary, which we recall identifies with the Riemann sphere
\hat{\mathbb{C}}
, and let
{\text{\rm PSL}}(2,\mathbb{C})
denote its group of orientation-preserving isometries. Let
\rho
be a discrete, injective homomorphism from a compact surface group
\Gamma
into
{\text{\rm PSL}}(2,\mathbb{C})
. This homomorphism is said to be
fuchsian
whenever it preserves a circle in
\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{3}
, and
quasi-fuchsian
whenever it preserves a Jordan curve, in which case the preserved Jordan curve is known to be unique.

The set of almost fuchsian homomorphisms was introduced by Uhlenbeck in
[
9
]
. The homomorphism
\rho
is said to be
almost fuchsian
whenever, in addition to being quasi-fuchsian, its fixed Jordan curve bounds an embedded minimal disk in
\mathbb{H}^{3}
with sectional curvatures of absolute values everywhere less than
1
. When this holds, the minimal disk is also unique. Almost fuchsian homomorphisms form a neighbourhood of the set of fuchsian homomorphisms with various interesting properties.

In order to state our result, we now alter our perspective slightly. Let
\mathbb{D}
denote the Poincaré disk, and let
{\text{\rm PSL}}(2,\mathbb{R})
denote its group of orientation-preserving isometries. By Riemann’s uniformization theorem, we may associate to every almost fuchsian homomorphism
\rho
a pair
(\sigma,f)
, where
\sigma:\Gamma\rightarrow{\text{\rm PSL}}(2,\mathbb{R})
is a fuchsian homomorphism, and
f:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{3}
is a
(\rho,\sigma)
-equivariant conformal minimal embedding. Furthermore
\sigma
is unique up to conjugation and, once
\sigma
is fixed,
f
is unique. Conversely, the pair
(\sigma,f)
uniquely determines
\rho
.

We call
\sigma
the
conformal type
of the almost fuchsian homomorphism, and henceforth suppose that it is fixed. Recall now that the Hopf differential of
f
is defined to be the
(2,0)
-component of its shape operator. This is a
\sigma
-invariant holomorphic quadratic differential which determines
f
up to postcomposition by isometries of
\mathbb{H}^{3}
, and which in turn determines
\rho
up to conjugation.

Let
\mathcal{Q}(\mathbb{D},\sigma)
denote the set of
\sigma
-invariant holomorphic quadratic differentials over
\mathbb{D}
, and let
\mathcal{Q}_{\text{{\rm af}}}(\mathbb{D},\sigma)
denote the subset consisting of Hopf differentials of equivariant, almost fuchsian conformal minimal embeddings. By the preceding discussion,
\mathcal{Q}_{\text{{\rm af}}}(\mathbb{D},\sigma)
parametrizes the space of conjugacy classes of almost fuchsian homomorphisms with conformal type
\sigma
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any fuchsian homomorphism
\sigma:\Gamma\rightarrow{\text{\rm PSL}}(2,\mathbb{R})
,
\mathcal{Q}_{\text{{\rm af}}}(\mathbb{D},\sigma)
is a convex subset of
\mathcal{Q}(\mathbb{D},\sigma)
.

Remark
.

In
[
8
]
, it is shown that this subset is star-shaped about the origin.

In fact, we prove a stronger result, from which Theorem
1.1
will immediately follow. In Section
3
, we provide a natural extension of the concept of almost fuchsian to the case of non-equivariant minimal embeddings. We denote by
\mathcal{Q}_{\text{{\rm bdd}}}(\mathbb{D})
the space of holomorphic quadratic differentials over
\mathbb{D}
which are bounded with respect to the Poincaré metric. We furnish this space with the
C^{0}
norm, and note that this makes it into a Banach space. We will see that the Hopf differential of every almost fuchsian conformal minimal embedding is an element of
\mathcal{Q}_{\text{{\rm bdd}}}(\mathbb{D})
and, furthermore, that every element of
\mathcal{Q}_{\text{{\rm bdd}}}(\mathbb{D})
is the Hopf differential of at most one such embedding.

Let
\mathcal{Q}_{\text{{\rm af}}}(\mathbb{D})\subseteq\mathcal{Q}_{\text{{\rm bdd}}}(\mathbb{D})
denote the set of Hopf differentials of almost fuchsian conformal minimal embeddings. For all
\phi\in\mathcal{Q}_{\text{{\rm af}}}(\mathbb{D})
, let
\kappa(\phi):\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
denote the extrinsic curvature function of its corresponding almost fuchsian conformal minimal embedding.

Theorem 1.2
.

For all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, the function

(1.1)

\kappa_{z}:\mathcal{Q}_{\text{{\rm af}}}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow\mathbb{R};\phi\mapsto\kappa(\phi)(z)

is concave.

Remark
.

Theorem
1.2
is proven in Theorem
4.1
.

As a consequence of Theorem
1.2
, we obtain the following result.

Theorem 1.3
.

\mathcal{Q}_{\text{{\rm af}}}(\mathbb{D})
is a convex open subset of
\mathcal{Q}_{\text{{\rm bdd}}}(\mathbb{D})
. Furthermore

(1.2)

B(0,1/2)\subseteq\mathcal{Q}_{\text{{\rm af}}}(\mathbb{D})\subseteq B(0,1),

where, for all
r>0
,
B(0,r)
denotes the open ball of radius
r
in
\mathcal{Q}_{\text{{\rm bdd}}}(\mathbb{D})
.

Remark
.

A stronger version of Theorem
1.3
is proven in Theorem
4.2
.

Our work also has the following interesting application to the study of Finsler metrics over Teichmüller space. Let
\mathcal{T}(\Gamma)
denote the Teichmüller space of conjugacy classes of Fuchsian representations
\rho:\Gamma\rightarrow{\text{\rm PSL}}(2,\mathbb{R})
. Recall that the cotangent space of
\mathcal{T}(\Gamma)
at any point
\sigma
identifies with the space
\mathcal{Q}(\mathbb{D},\sigma)
of
\sigma
-invariant holomorphic quadratic differentials over
\mathbb{D}
. Since
\mathcal{Q}_{\text{{\rm af}}}(\mathbb{D},\sigma)
is a convex neighbourhood of the origin in
\mathcal{Q}(\mathbb{D},\sigma)
, it thus defines a Finsler metric over
\mathcal{T}(\Gamma)
. More generally, in Sections
3
and
4
, we construct a family
(\mathcal{U}_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in(0,1]}
of convex neighbourhoods of the origin in
\mathcal{Q}(\mathbb{D},\sigma)
which, suitably rescaled, interpolates between the unit
L^{\infty}
ball and
\mathcal{Q}_{\text{{\rm af}}}(\mathbb{D},\sigma)
. Bearing in mind the estimates derived in Theorem
4.2
, we thus have the following result.

Theorem 1.4
.

There exists a continuous family
(p_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in[0,1]}
of uniformly equivalent Finsler metrics
p
on the Teichmüller space
\mathcal{T}_{g}
such that
p_{0}
coincides with the Finsler metric dual to the unit
L^{\infty}
balls under the identification of the cotangent bundle with the space of holomorphic quadratic differentials.

The authors thank Nicolas Tholozan for his insights and discussions on this topic.

2.
Preliminaries

We first recall some elementary results of Gauss-Codazzi theory that will be used in the sequel. Let
h
denote the Poincaré metric of
\mathbb{D}
, and let
f:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{3}
be a smooth immersion which is conformal in the sense that its induced metric
g
satisfies

(2.1)

g=e^{2u}h\ ,

for some smooth function
u
, which we call its
conformal factor
. Let
\kappa
denote the curvature of
g
, and recall that

(2.2)

\Delta^{h}u=-1-e^{2u}\kappa\ ,

or, equivalently

(2.3)

\Delta^{g}u=-\kappa-e^{-2u}\ .

Let
II
denote the second fundamental form of
f
, let
A
denote its shape operator, and let
H:=\text{{\rm Tr}}(A)/2
denote its mean curvature. Recall that the
Hopf differential

\phi
of
f
is defined to be the
(2,0)
-component of
II
with respect to
g
. In other words, it is the symmetric bilinear form given by

(2.4)

\phi(\xi,\nu):=\frac{1}{4}\bigg(\text{I\!I}(\xi,\nu)-i\text{I\!I}(J\xi,\nu)-i\text{I\!I}(\xi,J\nu)-\text{I\!I}(J\xi,J\nu)\bigg)\ ,

where
J
here denotes the complex structure of
\mathbb{D}
. In particular,

(2.5)

\left|\phi\right|_{g}^{2}=H^{2}-\text{{\rm Det}}(A).

By Gauss’ equation,

(2.6)

\text{{\rm Det}}(A)=\kappa+1\ .

Combined with (
2.1
), (
2.2
) and (
2.5
), this yields

(2.7)

\Delta^{h}u=-1+e^{2u}(1-H^{2})+e^{-2u}\left|\phi\right|_{h}^{2}\ .

We call the triple
(u,H,\phi)
the
Gauss–Codazzi
data of the immersion
f
. We recall the fundamental theorem of surface theory, as it applies in this context.

Theorem 2.1

(Fundamental theorem of surface theory)
.

For any triple
(u,H,\phi)
satisfying (
2.7
), there exists a conformal immersion
f:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{3}
with this Gauss–Codazzi data. Furthermore,
f
is unique up to post-composition by isometries of
\mathbb{H}^{3}
.

Finally, we will make use of the following result of Li–Mochizuki
[
4
]
.

Theorem 2.2

(Sub-supersolution method)
.

Let
(\Sigma,g)
be a riemannian surface, let
F:\Sigma\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function, and consider the equation

(2.8)

\Delta^{g}u=F(x,u).

If there exist
C^{2}
functions
u_{-},u_{+}:\Sigma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying

(2.9)

u_{-}<u_{+}\ ,\Delta^{g}u_{-}\geq F(x,u_{-})\ ,\ \text{and}\ \Delta^{g}u_{+}\leq F(x,u_{+})\ ,

then (
2.8
) admits at least one solution
u
satisfying
u_{-}\leq u\leq u_{+}
.

We will also require the following version of the Omori-Yau maximum principle (see
[
6
,
10
,
4
]
).

Theorem 2.3

(Omori-Yau maximum principle)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a complete riemannian manifold with Ricci curvature bounded below, and let
u:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a
C^{2}
function bounded above. For all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
x\in M
such that

(2.10)

u(x)\geq\sup_{x\in M}u(x)-\varepsilon\ ,\ |(\nabla^{g}u)(x)|\leq\varepsilon\ ,\ \text{and}\ (\Delta^{g}u)(x)\leq\varepsilon\ .

3.
Almost fuchsian conformal minimal immersions

We will be concerned with conformal immersions that are minimal in the sense that their mean curvature
H
vanishes. In this case, the Hopf differential
\phi
is known to be holomorphic (see [Hop50],§2), and (
2.7
) clearly reduces to the fundamental identity

(3.1)

\Delta^{h}u=-1+e^{2u}+e^{-2u}\left|\phi\right|^{2}_{h}\ .

It is this equation that will be the object of our study.

For all
(k,\alpha)
, and for every
k
-times differentiable function
f:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, let
\|f\|_{C^{k,\alpha}}
denote the
C^{k,\alpha}
-norm of
f
with respect to the Poincaré metric, and let
C^{k,\alpha}(\mathbb{D})
denote the Banach space of
k
-times differentiable functions over
\mathbb{D}
with bounded
C^{k,\alpha}
-norm. Let
\mathcal{Q}_{\text{{\rm bdd}}}(\mathbb{D})
denote the space of bounded holomorphic quadratic differentials over
\mathbb{D}
furnished with the
C^{0}
norm, and note that this is also a Banach space. Consider the function

(3.2)

\mathcal{F}:C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{D})\times\mathcal{Q}_{\text{{\rm bdd}}}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{D})\ ;(u,\phi)\mapsto\Delta^{h}u+1-e^{2u}-e^{-2u}\left|\phi\right|_{h}^{2}.

Note that, since this function is constructed as a finite combination of differentiations and compositions by smooth functions, it defines a smooth function between Banach spaces.

Let
\mathcal{Z}:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}
denote the solution space of
\mathcal{F}
, and let
\pi:\mathcal{Z}\rightarrow\mathcal{Q}_{\text{{\rm bdd}}}(\mathbb{D})
denote projection onto the second factor. The following result tells us that
\mathcal{Z}
identifies with the space of complete conformal minimal immersions of
\mathbb{D}
in
\mathbb{H}^{3}
up to post-composition by isometries.

Lemma 3.1
.

\mathcal{Z}
is the set of all Gauss–Codazzi data of complete conformal minimal immersions.

Proof.

Indeed, let
f:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}
be a conformal minimal immersion with conformal factor
u
, induced metric
g
, and Hopf differential
\phi
. Note that
g
is complete, and let
\kappa
denote its curvature. Consider now the equation

\Delta^{g}v=-\kappa-e^{-2v}.

Applying Theorem
2.2
with subsolution
v_{-}:=0
and supersolution
v_{+}:=\text{{\rm Ln}}(2)/2
yields a smooth solution
v
of this equation satisfying

0\leq v\leq\frac{\text{{\rm Ln}}(2)}{2}.

Since
g
is complete, and since
v
is bounded,
e^{2v}g
is also complete. Since this metric has constant sectional curvature equal to
-1
, it follows by uniqueness of the Poincaré metric over
\mathbb{D}
that

e^{2v}g=h=e^{-2u}g,

so that
u=-v
is also bounded. It now follows by quasi-linearity of (
2.2
) and the classical theory of elliptic operators that
u\in C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{D})
, so that
(u,\phi)\in\mathcal{Z}
, as desired. Conversely, if
(u,\phi)\in\mathcal{Z}
, then, by the fundamental theorem of surface theory, there exists a conformal minimal immersion
f:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{3}
with this Gauss–Codazzi data. Since
u
is bounded, this immersion is complete, and this completes the proof.
∎

The study of existence and uniqueness of solutions of
\mathcal{F}
is equivalent to the study of the geometry of the pair
(\mathcal{Z},\pi)
. Indeed, the standard PDE problems of determining a priori estimates, existence and uniqueness correspond to the respective geometric problems of verifying properness, surjectivity and injectivity. In what follows, we study the extent to which
\mathcal{Z}
is a graph over the image of
\pi
. To this end, we introduce, for all
\lambda>0
, the subsets
\Omega_{\lambda}\subseteq C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{D})\times\mathcal{Q}_{\text{{\rm bdd}}}(\mathbb{D})
and
\mathcal{U}_{\lambda}\subseteq\mathcal{Q}_{\text{{\rm bdd}}}(\mathbb{D})
given by

(3.3)

\displaystyle{{}\Omega_{\lambda}}

\displaystyle{{}:=\big\{(u,\phi)\ \big|\ \sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}e^{-4u(z)}\left|\phi(z)\right|^{2}_{h}<\lambda^{2}\big\}\ ,\ \text{and}}

\displaystyle{{}\mathcal{U}_{\lambda}}

\displaystyle{{}:=\pi(\mathcal{Z}\cap\Omega_{\lambda})\ .\vphantom{\big(}}

The sets
\Omega_{1}
and
\mathcal{U}_{1}
are closely related to the space of almost fuchsian embeddings. We first extend the definition given in the introduction (c.f.
[
3
,
7
]
). We say that a conformal minimal immersion
f:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{3}
, with conformal factor
u
, induced metric
g
, shape operator
A
, and Hopf differential
\phi
, is
almost fuchsian
whenever

(1)

f
is properly embedded,

(2)

\partial_{\infty}f(\mathbb{D})
is a Jordan curve in
\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{3}
, and

(3)

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\|A(z)\|_{g}<1
.

We call
\partial_{\infty}f(\mathbb{D})
the
boundary curve
of
f
. Note that, by (
2.1
) and (
2.5
),

(3.4)

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}e^{-2u(z)}\left|\phi(z)\right|_{h}=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\left|\phi(z)\right|_{g}=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\|A(z)\|\ ,

so that the third condition given above has various equivalent formulations.

Theorem 3.2
.

\mathcal{Z}\cap\Omega_{1}
is the set of all Gauss–Codazzi data of almost fuchsian conformal minimal embeddings. Furthermore, if
f
is an almost fuchsian conformal minimal embedding with Gauss–Codazzi data
(u,\phi)
, then its boundary curve is a
K
-quasicircle, where

(3.5)

K\leq\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\frac{1+\left|\phi\right|_{g}}{1-\left|\phi\right|_{g}}\ .

Remark
.

This result follows readily form the original work
[
9
]
of Uhlenbeck as well as the argument provided in the proof of Theorem
1.3
of
[
2
]
. We provide here the details for completeness.

Proof.

By (
3.4
) and Lemma
3.1
, the Gauss–Codazzi data of any almost fuchsian conformal minimal embedding is an element of
\mathcal{Z}\cap\Omega_{1}
. We now prove the converse. Choose
(u,\phi)\in\mathcal{Z}\cap\Omega_{1}
, and let
f:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{3}
be a conformal minimal immersion given by the fundamental theorem of surface theory. We will show that
f
is almost fuchsian. Indeed, let
g:=e^{2u}h
denote its induced metric, let
\nu
denote its unit normal vector field, let
A
denote its shape operator, and note that, by (
3.4
),

(3.6)

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\|A(z)\|_{g}<1\ .

Now define
\widetilde{f}:\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{3}
by

\widetilde{f}(x,t):=\text{{\rm Exp}}_{f(x)}(t\nu(x))\ .

where
Exp
here denotes the exponential map of
\mathbb{H}^{3}
. As in Lemma
2.1
of
[
2
]
, we see that the metric induced by
\widetilde{f}
over
\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{R}
is

\widetilde{g}(x,t):=g_{t}(x)\oplus dt^{2}\ ,

where, for all
t
,

g_{t}(\cdot,\cdot)=g((\cosh(t)I_{0}+\sinh(t)A)\cdot,(\cosh(t)I_{0}+\sinh(t)A)\cdot)\ .

By (
3.6
),
\widetilde{g}
is everywhere non-degenerate and, since
g
is complete, so too is
\widetilde{g}
. In particular, since
\widetilde{f}
is a local isometry with respect to this metric, it satisfies the path-lifting property. Since
\mathbb{H}^{3}
is simply-connected, it follows that
\widetilde{f}
is a diffeomorphism, so that
f
is indeed properly embedded.

We now show that
\partial_{\infty}f(\mathbb{D})
is a Jordan curve in
\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{3}
. To see this, let
f^{\prime}:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{3}
be an isometric parametrization of a totally geodesic plane, let
\nu^{\prime}
denote its unit normal vector field, and define
\widetilde{f}^{\prime}:\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{3}
by

\widetilde{f}^{\prime}(x,t)=\text{{\rm Exp}}_{f^{\prime}(x)}(t\nu^{\prime}(x))\ .

The metric induced by
\widetilde{f}^{\prime}
is

\widetilde{g}^{\prime}(x,t)=\cosh^{2}(t)g\oplus dt^{2}\ .

By (
3.6
),
\alpha:=e\circ f^{-1}
is bilipschitz. It therefore extends (see, for example, Gehring
[
1
]
or Mostow
[
5
]
) to a quasiconformal map
\widetilde{\alpha}
from
\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{3}
to itself, from which it follows that
\partial_{\infty}f(\mathbb{D})
is a quasicircle. In particular, it is a Jordan curve, as asserted.

Finally, for all
t
, the restrictions of
\widetilde{g}^{\prime}
and
\widetilde{g}
to the slice
\mathbb{D}\times\{t\}
are
K
-quasiconformal to one-another, where

K=\frac{1+\left|\phi\right|_{g}}{1-\left|\phi\right|_{g}}\ .

Letting
t
tend to
+\infty
, we see that
\widetilde{\alpha}
is
K
-quasiconformal. Since
\partial_{\infty}f(\mathbb{D})
is the image under
\widetilde{\alpha}
of an equator in
\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{3}
, it follows that this curve is a
K
-quasicircle, and this completes the proof.
∎

4.
Proofs of main results

We now prove the main results of this note. For all
\phi\in\mathcal{U}_{1}
, let
\kappa(\phi)
denote the extrinsic curvature function of its corresponding almost fuchsian conformal minimal embedding.

Theorem 4.1
.

For all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, the function

(4.1)

\kappa_{z}:\mathcal{U}_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R};\phi\mapsto\kappa(\phi)(z)

is concave.

Theorem 4.2
.

The solution space
\mathcal{Z}
has the following properties.

(1)

\mathcal{Z}\cap\Omega_{1}
is a smooth graph over its image,

(2)

for all
0<\lambda\leq 1
,
\mathcal{U}_{\lambda}
is convex, and

(3)

for all
0<\lambda\leq 1
,

(4.2)

B(0,\lambda/(1+\lambda^{2}))\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{\lambda}\subseteq B(0,\lambda),

where, for all
r>0
,
B(0,r)
denotes the open ball of radius

about the origin in
\mathcal{Q}(\mathbb{D})
with respect to the

In order to prove these results, we first consider the more general function

(4.3)

\hat{\mathcal{F}}:C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{D})\times C^{0,1}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{D})\ ;(u,f)\mapsto\Delta^{h}u+1-e^{2u}-e^{-2u}f.

As before,
\hat{\mathcal{F}}
is a smooth function between Banach spaces. Let
\hat{\mathcal{Z}}:=\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}
denote its zero set, let
\pi:Z\rightarrow C^{0,1}(\mathbb{D})
denote the projection onto the first factor, and, for all
\lambda>0
, define

(4.4)

\displaystyle{{}\hat{\Omega}_{\lambda}}

\displaystyle{{}:=\big\{(u,f)\ \big|\ \sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}e^{-4u(z)}f(z)<\lambda^{2}\big\}\ ,\ \text{and}}

\displaystyle{{}\hat{\mathcal{U}}_{\lambda}}

\displaystyle{{}:=\pi(\mathcal{Z}\cap\hat{\Omega}_{\lambda})\ .\vphantom{\big(}}

Lemma 4.3
.

For all
\lambda>0
, and for all
(u,f)\in\hat{\mathcal{Z}}\cap\hat{\Omega}_{\lambda}
,

(4.5)

-\frac{1}{2}\text{{\rm Ln}}(1+\lambda^{2})\leq u\leq 0\ .

Proof.

Suppose first that

c^{+}:=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}u(z)>0\ .

By the Omori-Yau maximum principle, there exists
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}
such that

(\Delta^{h}u)(z_{0})<e^{c^{+}}-1\ ,\ \text{and}\ u(z_{0})>c^{+}/2\ .

At this point,

\hat{\mathcal{F}}(u,f)<(e^{c^{+}}-1)+1-e^{c^{+}}=0\ ,

which is absurd, and it follows that

.

Suppose now that

c^{-}:=\inf_{z\in\mathbb{D}}u(z)<-\frac{1}{2}\text{{\rm Ln}}(1+\lambda^{2})\ .

Choose
\delta>0
such that

\displaystyle{{}c^{-}+\delta}

\displaystyle{{}<-\frac{1}{2}\text{{\rm Ln}}(1+\lambda^{2})}

\displaystyle{\Leftrightarrow}

\displaystyle{{}e^{2(c^{-}+\delta)}(1+\lambda^{2})}

\displaystyle{{}<1\ .}

By the Omori-Yau maximum principle, there exists
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}
such that

(\Delta^{h}u)(z_{0})>-(1-e^{2(c^{-}+\delta)}(1+\lambda^{2}))\ ,\ \text{and}\ u(z_{0})<c^{-}+\delta\ .

At this point,

e^{-2u(z_{0})}f(z_{0})=e^{2u(z_{0})}(e^{-4u(z_{0})}f(z_{0}))\leq\lambda^{2}e^{2u(z_{0})}\ .

Consequently,

\hat{\mathcal{F}}(u,f)>-(1-e^{2(c^{-}+\delta)}(1+\lambda^{2}))+1-e^{2u(z_{0})}(1+\lambda^{2})>0\ ,

which is absurd. It follows that

, and this completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

The restriction of
\pi
to
\hat{Z}\cap\hat{\Omega}_{1}
is injective.

Proof.

Suppose the contrary, so that there exist
u,u\in C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{D})
and

such that
(u,f),(u^{\prime},f)\in\hat{\mathcal{Z}}\cap\hat{\Omega}_{1}
. Denote

, and without loss of generality, suppose that

c:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{D}}w(z)>0\ .

Choose
0<\delta<1
be such that

\delta<\inf_{z\in\mathbb{D}}2e^{2u^{\prime}(z)}(1-e^{-4u^{\prime}(z)}f(z))\ .

By hypothesis,

\hat{\mathcal{F}}(u,f)=\hat{\mathcal{F}}(u^{\prime},f)=0\ .

By convexity, for all

,

e^{y}-e^{x}\geq e^{x}(y-x)\ .

Bearing in mind (

Lemma 4.5

\hat{\mathcal{Z}}\cap\hat{\Omega}_{1}

Proof.

The partial derivative of
\mathcal{F}

vanishes.

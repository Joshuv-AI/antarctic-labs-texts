Stability of planar exterior stationary flows with suction
Mitsuo Higaki

Abstract




Abstract.
We consider the two-dimensional Navier-Stokes system in a domain exterior to a disk. The system admits a stationary solution with critical decay
O(|x|^{-1})
written as a linear combination of the pure rotating flow and the flux carrier. We prove its nonlinear stability in large time for initial disturbances in
L^{2}
under smallness conditions, assuming that there is suction across the boundary, namely that the sign of coefficients of the flux carrier is negative. This result partially solves an open problem in the literature.

Keywords.
Navier-Stokes system,
Two-dimensional exterior domains,
Stability of stationary solutions,
Scale-critical decay.

2020 MSC.
35Q30, 35B35, 76D05, 76D17.

Introduction

We consider the two-dimensional Navier-Stokes system in an exterior disk

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u-\Delta u+\nabla p=-u\cdot\nabla u&\mbox{in}\ (0,\infty)\times\Omega\\
\operatorname{div}u=0&\mbox{in}\ [0,\infty)\times\Omega\\
u(x)=\alpha x^{\bot}-\delta x&\mbox{on}\ (0,\infty)\times\partial\Omega\\
u|_{t=0}=u_{0}&\mbox{in}\ \Omega.\end{array}\right.

(NS)

The unknown functions
u=(u_{1}(t,x),u_{2}(t,x))
and
p=p(t,x)
are respectively the velocity field of the fluid and the pressure field. The function
u_{0}=(u_{0,1}(x),u_{0,2}(x))
is a given initial data. The set
\Omega
denotes the exterior unit disk
\{x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}~|~|x|>1\}
where
|x|=\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}
. We assume that both
\alpha
and
\delta
are real number constants. The vector
x^{\bot}
refers to
(-x_{2},x_{1})
. The system (
NS
) describes the time evolution of viscous incompressible fluids around the disk rotating at angular velocity
\alpha
on whose surface there is suction in the orthogonal direction when
\delta>0
and injection when
\delta<0
.

The system (
NS
) admits an explicit stationary solution
(\alpha U-\delta W,\nabla P_{\alpha,\delta})
where

\displaystyle\begin{split}U(x)=\frac{x^{\bot}}{|x|^{2}},\qquad W(x)=\frac{x}{|x|^{2}},\end{split}

(1.1)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}\nabla P_{\alpha,\delta}(x)=-\nabla\Big(\frac{|\alpha U(x)-\delta W(x)|^{2}}{2}\Big).\end{split}

(1.2)

This velocity is a linear combination of the vector field
U
denoting the pure rotating flow in
\Omega
and
W
the flux carrier. To lighten notation, in the following, we write

\displaystyle V=V(\alpha,\delta)=\alpha U-\delta W.

(1.3)

The solution
V
is invariant under the scaling of the Navier-Stokes equations. A (non-trivial) solution having this property is said to be scale-critical and it represents the balance between the nonlinear and linear parts of the equations. Therefore, investigating the properties of the scale-critical solutions is a fundamental and important issue in understanding the typical behavior of the Navier-Stokes flows. Let us mention that
V
is an element of the family of stationary solutions of (
NS
) found by Hamel
[
20
]
. This family is known to be an example showing the non-uniqueness of the
D
-solutions; see Galdi
[
15
, Section XII.2]
. The Hamel solutions are generalized by Guillod and Wittwer
[
19
]
in view of rotation symmetries.

In this paper, we study the nonlinear stability of
V
in large time. More precisely, assuming that an initial disturbance around
V
belongs to the Lebesgue spaces, we consider the time evolution of the disturbance in the nonlinear system (
NS
). Particularly, we are interested in the large-time decay estimate. By using the relation

\displaystyle u\cdot\nabla v+v\cdot\nabla u=u^{\bot}{\rm rot}\,v+v^{\bot}{\rm rot}\,u+\nabla\Big(\frac{|u+v|^{2}-|u|^{2}-|v|^{2}}{2}\Big)

(1.4)

and
\operatorname{rot}V=0
, we see that the pair of new unknown functions

v=u-V\qquad\text{and}\qquad\nabla q=\nabla\Big(p+\frac{|u|^{2}}{2}\Big)

formally solves the nonlinear problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}v-\Delta v+V^{\bot}\operatorname{rot}v+\nabla q=-v^{\bot}\operatorname{rot}v&\mbox{in}\ (0,\infty)\times\Omega\\
\operatorname{div}v=0&\mbox{in}\ [0,\infty)\times\Omega\\
v=0&\mbox{on}\ (0,\infty)\times\partial\Omega\\
v|_{t=0}=v_{0}:=u|_{t=0}-V&\mbox{in}\ \Omega.\end{array}\right.

(NP)

The linearized problem of (
NP
) is given by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}v-\Delta v+V^{\bot}\operatorname{rot}v+\nabla q=0&\mbox{in}\ (0,\infty)\times\Omega\\
\operatorname{div}v=0&\mbox{in}\ [0,\infty)\times\Omega\\
v=0&\mbox{on}\ (0,\infty)\times\partial\Omega\\
v|_{t=0}=v_{0}&\mbox{in}\ \Omega.\end{array}\right.

(LP)

Our main aim in this paper is to obtain large-time decay estimates of the solutions of (
LP
), by studying the operators associated with (
LP
). We will provide the
L^{p}
-
L^{q}
estimates sufficient to prove the nonlinear stability of the stationary solution
V
in large time.

In order to make the framework clearer, we recall some notations and basic facts about the linear system (
LP
). We let
C^{\infty}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega)
denote
\{\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)^{2}~|~\operatorname{div}\varphi=0\}
,
L^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
the closure of
C^{\infty}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega)
in
L^{2}(\Omega)^{2}
, and
{\mathbb{P}}:L^{2}(\Omega)^{2}\to L^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
the orthogonal projection. The operator
{\mathbb{P}}
is called the Helmholtz projection and satisfies
{\mathbb{P}}\nabla p=0
for
p\in L^{2}_{{\rm loc}}(\overline{\Omega})
with
\nabla p\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{2}
. The operator, called the Stokes operator, is defined by

{\mathbb{A}}=-{\mathbb{P}}\Delta,\qquad D({\mathbb{A}})=L^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{2}\cap W^{2,2}(\Omega)^{2}.

It is well known that
{\mathbb{A}}
is nonnegative and self-adjoint in
L^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
and that
-{\mathbb{A}}
generates the
C_{0}
-analytic semigroup; see Sohr
[
39
]
. Moreover, the spectrum of
-{\mathbb{A}}
is the set of nonpositive real numbers
\sigma(-{\mathbb{A}})=\mathbb{R}_{\leq 0}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}~|~x\leq 0\}
; see Section
3
for the references. With these notations, we define the operator associated with (
LP
) by

{\mathbb{A}}_{V}v={\mathbb{A}}v+{\mathbb{P}}V^{\bot}\operatorname{rot}v,\qquad D({\mathbb{A}}_{V})=D({\mathbb{A}}),

and write (
LP
) equivalently with the evolution system

\frac{\,{\rm d}v}{\,{\rm d}t}+{\mathbb{A}}_{V}v=0\mkern 9.0mu\mbox{in}\ (0,\infty),\qquad v|_{t=0}=v_{0}.

(1.5)

We aim at proving the properties of solutions of (
1.5
) by studying the operator
-{\mathbb{A}}_{V}
.

One basic way to study the properties of
-{\mathbb{A}}_{V}
is to consider the equation

(\lambda+{\mathbb{A}}_{V})v=f

(R)

for given
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
and
f\in L^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
. This equation can be obtained by formal application of the Laplace transform to (
1.5
). From the general theory of functional analysis, we find the following two facts. First, as the operator
{\mathbb{P}}V^{\bot}\operatorname{rot}
is lower order with respect to
{\mathbb{A}}
, from theory for sectorial operators, we see that
-{\mathbb{A}}_{V}
is also sectorial in
L^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
and generates the
C_{0}
-analytic semigroup, denoted by
\{e^{-t{\mathbb{A}}_{V}}\}_{t\geq 0}
; see Lunardi
[
32
, Proposition 2.4.3]
. Second, as
{\mathbb{P}}V^{\bot}\operatorname{rot}
is relatively compact with respect to
{\mathbb{A}}
, from the perturbation theory of operators, we see that
\sigma(-{\mathbb{A}}_{V})=\mathbb{R}_{\leq 0}\cup\sigma_{{\rm disc}}(-{\mathbb{A}}_{V})
where
\sigma_{{\rm disc}}(-{\mathbb{A}}_{V})
denotes the discrete spectrum of
-{\mathbb{A}}_{V}
; see Section
3
for details. These two facts, however, are not sufficient to obtain the large-time estimate of
\{e^{-t{\mathbb{A}}_{V}}\}_{t\geq 0}
since
\sigma(-{\mathbb{A}}_{V})
contains
\mathbb{R}_{\leq 0}
. We need a precise estimate of the resolvent
(\lambda+{\mathbb{A}}_{V})^{-1}
when
\lambda
is close to the origin.

The fundamental difficulty in analyzing (
R
) when
|\lambda|\ll 1
is that the Hardy inequality

\displaystyle\Big\|x\mapsto\frac{f(x)}{|x|}\Big\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}},\quad f\in\dot{W}^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{d}=\Big(\overline{C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)}^{\|\nabla\,\cdot\,\|_{L^{2}}}\Big)^{d}

(1.6)

does not hold in exterior domains
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
when
d=2
. If (
1.6
) holds when
d=2
, the term
{\mathbb{P}}V^{\bot}\operatorname{rot}v
in (
R
) can be controlled by the dissipation from
-\Delta v
if
|\alpha|+|\delta|
is small. Nevertheless, one needs a logarithmic correction in the left-hand side of (
1.6
) to obtain the correspondence; see
[
15
, Theorem II.6.1]
. This implies that energy method does not work well in general in deriving estimates for (
R
) when
|\lambda|\ll 1
. One way to recover inequalities of the type (
1.6
) when
d=2
is to assume symmetries both on
\Omega
and
f
; see Galdi and Yamazaki
[
16
]
, Yamazaki
[
41
]
, and Guillod
[
18
]
for the stability results of symmetric flows under symmetries. As we do not assume any symmetries on initial data in (
NP
), unlike
[
16
,
41
,
18
]
, such inequalities are not applicable to (
LP
) nor (
R
). This is in stark contrast to the three-dimensional stability results by Heywood
[
21
]
and by Borchers and Miyakawa
[
4
,
5
]
in which the Hardy inequality (
1.6
) with
d=3
is an essential tool. As a recent monograph of the three-dimensional results, we refer to Brandolese and Schonbek
[
7
]
.

Therefore, even for the flow
V=V(\alpha,\delta)
explicitly given in (
1.3
), the stability analysis in two-dimensional exterior domains requires specific considerations depending on the parameters
\alpha
and
\delta
. The known results are summarized as follows.

•

The case
\alpha=0
and
\delta\neq 0
is treated in Guillod
[
18
]
. This case is tractable and similar to the three-dimensional cases if
|\delta|
is sufficiently small. In fact, for general exterior domains
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, Russo
[
38
, Lemma 3]
proves the Hardy-type inequality

\displaystyle|\langle u\cdot\nabla u,W\rangle|\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\quad u\in\dot{W}^{1,2}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega)=\overline{C^{\infty}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega)}^{\|\nabla\,\cdot\,\|_{L^{2}}}.

(1.7)

The reader is referred to
[
15
, Remark X.4.2]
and
[
18
, Lemma 3]
for further discussions. Combining (
1.7
) with the relation (
1.4
), we obtain the control

\displaystyle|\langle{\mathbb{P}}(\delta W)^{\bot}\operatorname{rot}v,v\rangle|\leq C|\delta|\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\quad v\in D({\mathbb{A}}_{V}).

(1.8)

This observation implies that, by a simple energy estimate applied to (
R
), we can obtain the
L^{p}
-
L^{q}
estimates for the system (
LP
) and prove the nonlinear stability of
V=\delta W
. Alternatively, as is done in
[
18
]
, one can prove the stability by considering
L^{2}
-estimates of the semigroup generated by the adjoint of the operator
-{\mathbb{A}}_{\delta W}
. A similar idea is also used in Karch and Pilarczyk
[
27
]
.

•

The case
\alpha\neq 0
and
\delta=0
is treated in Maekawa
[
33
]
. In this case, energy method is not useful for (
R
). Indeed,
[
18
, Lemma 4]
points out that the Hardy-type inequality (
1.7
) does not hold if
W
is replaced by
U
. To relax the situation,
[
33
]
considers the problem in an exterior disk and performs explicit computations. The
L^{p}
-
L^{q}
estimates for (
LP
) are obtained when
|\alpha|
is sufficiently small by an explicit formula for the resolvent
(\lambda+{\mathbb{A}}_{\alpha U})^{-1}
. Also, the nonlinear stability of
\alpha U
is proved when both
|\alpha|
and the
L^{2}
-norm of initial data in (
NP
) are sufficiently small. This stability result is extended by the author in
[
22
]
to a certain class of non-symmetric domains where the domains are assumed to be small perturbations of the exterior unit disk, and in
[
24
]
for three-dimensional initial disturbances around an infinite cylinder.

•

The case
\alpha\neq 0
and
\delta\neq 0
is treated in Maekawa
[
34
]
. The problem is considered on an exterior disk as in
[
33
]
. The idea of the proof is to regard the term
{\mathbb{P}}(\delta W)^{\bot}\operatorname{rot}v
in (
R
) as an external force and to utilize the estimate of
(\lambda+{\mathbb{A}}_{\alpha U})^{-1}
in
[
33
]
. The
L^{p}
-
L^{q}
estimates for (
LP
) are obtained when
|\alpha|+|\delta|
is sufficiently small, under the restriction that initial data belong to a subcritical space
L^{2}\cap L^{q}
for some
1<q<2
. Also, the nonlinear stability of
V
is proved when both
|\alpha|+|\delta|
and the (
L^{2}\cap L^{q}
)-norm of initial data in (

) are sufficiently small. This restriction on exponents is essentially needed when estimating
(\lambda+{\mathbb{A}}_{\alpha U})^{-1}{\mathbb{P}}(\delta W)^{\bot}\operatorname{rot}v
. As is mentioned in
[

, it is not clear if the condition

can be removed in this method.

1.1
Main results

This paper addresses large-time estimates for the system (
LP
), namely, of the semigroup
\{e^{-t{\mathbb{A}}_{V}}\}_{t\geq 0}
, when
\alpha\neq 0
and
\delta\neq 0
as in
[
34
]
. Our particular interest is the
L^{p}
-
L^{2}
estimates left as open problems in
[
34
]
. The following theorem solves it affirmatively under a condition on the sign of
\delta
. This condition is discussed in Remark
1.2
(
iii
) below.

Theorem 1.1

Let
\alpha,\delta\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy
\alpha\neq 0
and
\delta\geq 0
and let
|\alpha|+\delta
be sufficiently small. For
f\in L^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|e^{-t{\mathbb{A}}_{V}}f\|_{L^{2}}&\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}},\quad t>0,\\
\|\nabla e^{-t{\mathbb{A}}_{V}}f\|_{L^{2}}&\leq Ct^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{2}},\quad t>0.\end{split}

(1.9)

The constant
C
depends on
\alpha,\delta,p
.

Remark 1.2

(i)

By combining Theorem
1.1
with the
L^{p}
-
L^{q}
estimates in
[
34
]
and by applying the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|e^{-t{\mathbb{A}}_{V}}f\|_{L^{p}}&\leq Ct^{-\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{q}},\quad t>0,\\
\|\nabla e^{-t{\mathbb{A}}_{V}}f\|_{L^{2}}&\leq Ct^{-\frac{1}{q}}\|f\|_{L^{q}},\quad t>0\end{split}

(1.10)

for
1<q\leq 2\leq p<\infty
and
f\in L^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)\cap L^{q}(\Omega)^{2}
with a constant
C=C(\alpha,\delta,q,p)
.

(ii)

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is based on an analysis of the operator
-{\mathbb{A}}_{V}
. The estimate (
1.9
) for
\{e^{-t{\mathbb{A}}_{V}}\}_{t\geq 0}
is deduced by the Dunford integral of the resolvent
(\lambda+{\mathbb{A}}_{V})^{-1}
. Inspired by
[
33
]
, we determine the spectrum of
-{\mathbb{A}}_{V}
and estimate
(\lambda+{\mathbb{A}}_{V})^{-1}
by explicit computations. It is shown in Section
3
that the function characterizing the discrete spectrum of
-{\mathbb{A}}_{V}
crucially depends on both
\alpha
and
\delta
. Therefore, it is suggested that, when estimating
(\lambda+{\mathbb{A}}_{V})^{-1}
for
|\lambda|\ll 1
, one cannot regard
{\mathbb{P}}(\delta W)^{\bot}\operatorname{rot}v
in (
R
) as an external force even if
|\delta|
is small, in spite of the control (
1.8
).

(iii)

It is an open problem whether the same estimate as in (
1.9
) can be obtained for the case
\delta<0
. Actually, by following the argument in Section
4
, one can prove (
1.9
) if
\delta
is chosen to depend on a given
\alpha
, but the general case is still open. It might be meaningful to recall here that the case
\delta<0
corresponds to the situation where there is injection into fluids at the boundary. We mention Drazin and Reid
[
11
, Problem 3.7]
and Drazin and Riley
[
12
, Section 3.1]
as the references related to this topic.

(iv)

It is important to extend the
L^{p}
-
L^{q}
estimates in (
1.10
) to general exterior domains. However, this is a difficult problem because of the dependence of constants on
\alpha,\delta
. The problem when
\delta=0
is tackled in
[
22
]
and it is shown that, if the domain
\Omega
is a perturbation from the exterior unit disk in algebraic order of

, then the

-

estimates can be obtained by energy method combined with explicit formulas. The restriction to a class of domains is due to singularity in the operator norm of the resolvent
(\lambda+{\mathbb{A}}_{\alpha U})^{-1}
for small
|\alpha|
. It is observed that, in explicit computations, cancellation of the effects from the two terms
\lambda v
and
\alpha U^{\bot}\operatorname{rot}v
in (
R
) (with
\delta=0
) occurs for
\lambda
in a certain domain, dubbed the “nearly-resonance regime” in
[
22
]
. This cancellation causes the singularity at algebraic order of
|\alpha|
, which in energy method restricts the shape of domains, more precisely the lengths between domains and the exterior unit disk. Such singularity also appears in the operator norm of
(\lambda+{\mathbb{A}}_{V})^{-1}
for small

By using Theorem

\displaystyle v(t)=e^{-t{\mathbb{A}}_{V}}v_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s){\mathbb{A}}_{V}}\mathbb{P}(v^{\bot}\operatorname{rot}v)(s)\,{\rm d}s,\quad t>0.

Related results

Let us refer to the results that are closely related to the present study.

Analysis of (
NP
) and (
LP
) when
V\equiv 0
.
For (
NP
), the estimate (
1.12
) for
V\equiv 0
, which can be viewed as the nonlinear stability of the trivial solution, is classical; see Masuda
[
37
]
for the proof when

and Kozono and Ogawa
[

]
when

. These results do not require smallness on the initial data in

. For (

), the

-

estimates of the Stokes semigroup

are established by Maremonti and Solonnikov
[
36
]
and by Dan and Shibata
[
9
,
10
]
. We note that all of the results above hold in general exterior domains

. It is pointed out in
[

, Remark 1.4]
that the logarithmic singularity of the resolvent
(\lambda+{\mathbb{A}})^{-1}
for small

, observed in
[
9
, §3]
, disappears in
(\lambda+{\mathbb{A}_{\alpha U}})^{-1}
if
\alpha\neq 0
. As compensation, however, singularity appears in the operator norm of
(\lambda+{\mathbb{A}_{\alpha U}})^{-1}
for small
|\alpha|
. Such singularity, as discussed in Remark
1.2
(
iv
), also appears in the operator norm of
(\lambda+{\mathbb{A}}_{V})^{-1}
, and is an obstacle when generalizing the
L^{p}
-
L^{q}
estimates in (
1.10
). Let us mention the study of the boundedness of
\{e^{-t{\mathbb{A}}}\}_{t\geq 0}
in spaces
L^{p}_{\sigma}(\Omega)=\overline{C^{\infty}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega)}^{\|\,\cdot\,\|_{L^{p}}}
pioneered by Borchers and Varnhorn
[
6
]
. See Abe
[
1
,
2
]
for the recent progress.

Non-symmetric stationary solutions around
V
.

We consider the stationary problem of (
NS
), which also admits the explicit solution
V
. It is known that, for suitably chosen
\alpha,\delta
, the fundamental solution for the linearized problem around
V
, namely for the stationary problem of (
LP
), has a better spatial decay compared to the one for the problem linearized around the trivial solution
V\equiv 0
. This improvement is due to the vorticity transport by
V
and implies the resolution of the famous Stokes paradox; see
[
8
,
14
,
15
,
30
,
26
]
for descriptions. Furthermore, these new fundamental solutions allow us to construct non-symmetric solutions for the nonlinear problem decaying in the order
O(|x|^{-1})
. This is done in Hillairet and Wittwer
[
25
]
when
|\alpha|>\sqrt{48}
and
\delta=0
for given zero-flux boundary data in a suitable class, and in
[
23
]
when
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
\delta>2
for given external forces with suitable spatial decay. The solutions in
[
23
]
are compatible with the Liouville-type theorem in Guillod
[
17
, Proposition 4.6]
. We emphasize that the results in
[
25
,
23
]
do not require any symmetries on the given data. Interestingly, such improvement in the fundamental solutions occurs even for small
\alpha,\delta
. Indeed, Maekawa and Tsurumi
[
35
]
constructs non-symmetric solutions for the nonlinear problem in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, whose principal part at spatial infinity is
cU
with a small but nonzero constant
c
. This result is contrasting with
[
25
]
in view of the size of coefficients, and the reason is that, as there are no boundaries in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the terms needed to match the no-slip boundary condition in exterior domains do not appear in the problem.

1.3
Outlined proof

We describe the proof of Theorem
1.1
. However, the estimate (
1.9
) is almost a direct consequence of the estimate of the resolvent
(\lambda+{\mathbb{A}}_{V})^{-1}
in Proposition
5.1
. Hence we give in Appendix
C
the proof that derives Theorem
1.1
from Proposition
5.1
, and outline here the proof of Proposition
5.1
. As noted in Remark
1.2
(
ii
), it consists of two steps:

(I) Spectral analysis of
-{\mathbb{A}}_{V}
.

Recall that
\sigma(-{\mathbb{A}}_{V})=\mathbb{R}_{\leq 0}\cup\sigma_{{\rm disc}}(-{\mathbb{A}}_{V})
. Thus we identify the location of the discrete spectrum
\sigma_{{\rm disc}}(-{\mathbb{A}}_{V})
to obtain the large-time decay of
\{e^{-t{\mathbb{A}}_{V}}\}_{t\geq 0}
. For this purpose, we consider the homogeneous equation of (
R
) and its general solutions, by using the streamfunction-vorticity equations. We see that the no-slip boundary condition imposes that
\lambda
belongs to
\sigma_{{\rm disc}}(-{\mathbb{A}}_{V})
if and only if
\lambda
belongs to

\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}_{\leq 0}~|~F_{n}(\sqrt{\lambda})=0\}.

Here
F_{n}=F_{n}(z)
is the analytic function defined in (
3.7
) in Section
3
. For
|n|\neq 1
, one can show that
F_{n}(\sqrt{\cdot})
has no zeros in the sector
\Sigma_{\frac{3}{4}\pi}
by energy method if
|\alpha|+|\delta|
is sufficiently small; see Propositions
3.3
and
3.4
. However, for
|n|=1

3

In this section, we study the spectrum of the operator

as zeros of certain analytic functions. We are aware that the presentation in Subsections

3.1
Notation

Let us recall the standard notation in the perturbation theory. Our main reference is Kato
[
28

. Let
X
be a Banach space and
\mathbb{L}:D(\mathbb{L})\subset X\to X
be a closed linear operator. We let

denote the null space of

,

its range, and

the quotient space of

by

. Moreover,

denotes the resolvent set of
\mathbb{L}
,
\sigma(\mathbb{L})
its spectrum, and
\sigma_{{\rm disc}}(\mathbb{L})
its discrete spectrum, namely, the set of isolated eigenvalues of

with finite multiplicity. The operator
\mathbb{L}
is said to be semi-Fredholm if

is closed and at least one of
\operatorname{dim}N(\mathbb{L})
or
\operatorname{dim}X/R(\mathbb{L})
is finite. If
\mathbb{L}
is semi-Fredholm, the index of

\operatorname{ind}(\mathbb{L})=\operatorname{dim}N(\mathbb{L})-\operatorname{dim}X/R(\mathbb{L})

is well-defined, taking values in

. Finally, let us set

\rho_{{\rm sf}}(\mathbb{L})=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}~|~\text{$\lambda-\mathbb{L}$ is semi-Fredholm}\},\qquad\sigma_{{\rm ess}}(\mathbb{L})=\mathbb{C}\setminus\rho_{{\rm sf}}(\mathbb{L})

and call the semi-Fredholm domain of
\mathbb{L}
and the essential spectrum of
\mathbb{L}
, respectively.

Generally,
\rho_{{\rm sf}}(\mathbb{L})
is the union of a countable (at most) family of connected open sets. From the argument in
[

, Chapter
\mathrm{I}\hskip-1.2pt\mathrm{V}
§5 6]
, we see that

is a constant function of

in each component

. Moreover, both

and
\operatorname{dim}X/R(\lambda-\mathbb{L})
are constants in each

except for an isolated set of values of
\lambda
. Therefore, if these constants are zero in particular, then

is contained in
\rho(\mathbb{L})
with possible exception of isolated points of

, which are, isolated eigenvalues of finite algebraic multiplicity.

for

in the second line and the Young inequality in the third line. Hence we obtain

|\Im\langle-\mathbb{A}_{V}v,v\rangle|+\Re\langle-\mathbb{A}_{V}v,v\rangle-(|\alpha|+|\delta|)^{2}\leq 0,

which leads to the inclusion

\overline{\Theta(-{\mathbb{A}}_{V})}\subset\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}~|~|\Im\lambda|+\Re\lambda-(|\alpha|+|\delta|)^{2}\leq 0\}.

From

, Chapter
\mathrm{V}
, Theorem 3.2]
, we know that

for any

belonging to the complement of the right-hand side

This set is obviously a subset of

and thus the second statement follows.

(2) The fact that

can be proved in a similar manner as in

, Lemma 3.3]
, and
\sigma_{{\rm ess}}(-{\mathbb{A}}_{n})=\sigma(-{\mathbb{A}}_{n})
follows by the property of

. Hence the first statement

follows from the relative compactness of

with respect to
-{\mathbb{A}}_{n}
. The second statement
\sigma_{{\rm disc}}(-{\mathbb{A}}_{V,n})\sqcup\rho(-\mathbb{A}_{V,n})=\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}_{\leq 0}
can be deduced from the same discussion as above with
\mathbb{A}_{V}
replaced by
\mathbb{A}_{V,n}
.

(3) It suffices to prove the first statement
\sigma_{{\rm disc}}(-{\mathbb{A}}_{V})=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sigma_{{\rm disc}}(-{\mathbb{A}}_{V,n})
. If

such that

. Choosing

such that

and

Lemma 3.2

Let
\alpha,\delta\in\mathbb{R}
. For
v\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{2}\cap L^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
, we have

\displaystyle|\langle V^{\bot}\operatorname{rot}v,v\rangle|\leq|\alpha|\Big|\sum_{|n|=1}\langle U^{\bot}\operatorname{rot}v_{n},v_{n}\rangle\Big|+|\alpha|\|\nabla v_{\neq}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+|\delta|\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(3.2)

Moreover, for any
T>0
,

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{|n|=1}\langle U^{\bot}\operatorname{rot}v_{n},v_{n}\rangle\Big|\leq 2h(T)\Big(\sum_{|n|=1}\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)+\frac{1}{4T^{2}h(T)}\Big(\sum_{|n|=1}\|v_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big).

(3.3)

Here the function
h=h(T)
is defined by

h(T)=\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{\tau}e^{-\frac{1}{\tau}}\,{\rm d}\tau,\quad T>0,

which satisfies

\displaystyle e^{-1}\log T\leq h(T)\leq\log T,\quad T>e.

(3.4)

Proof.

By the definition of
V
, we see that

\langle V^{\bot}\operatorname{rot}v,v\rangle=\alpha\langle U^{\bot}\operatorname{rot}v,v\rangle-\delta\langle W^{\bot}\operatorname{rot}v,v\rangle.

The Fourier series expansion leads to

\langle U^{\bot}\operatorname{rot}v,v\rangle=\sum_{|n|=1}\langle U^{\bot}\operatorname{rot}v_{n},v_{n}\rangle+\langle U^{\bot}\operatorname{rot}v_{\neq},v_{\neq}\rangle.

We have

\displaystyle|\langle U^{\bot}\operatorname{rot}v_{\neq},v_{\neq}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|(r,\theta)\mapsto\frac{v_{\neq}(r,\theta)}{r}\Big\|_{L^{2}}\|\operatorname{rot}v_{\neq}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla v_{\neq}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where the Hardy-type inequality (
2.7
) and
\|\operatorname{rot}u\|_{L^{2}}=\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}
for
u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)^{2}\cap L^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
are applied. Also, from (
1.4
) and (
1.7
) in the introduction,

|\langle W^{\bot}\operatorname{rot}v,v\rangle|\leq\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Combining all the estimates so far, we obtain (
3.2
).

Next let
|n|=1
. We compute

|\langle U^{\bot}\operatorname{rot}v_{n},v_{n}\rangle|\leq\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r}|(\operatorname{rot}v_{n})_{n}(r)||v_{r,n}(r)|r\,{\rm d}r\,{\rm d}\theta.

As is shown in
[
33
, Proof of Lemma 3.26]
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r}|(\operatorname{rot}v_{n})_{n}(r)||v_{r,n}(r)|r\,{\rm d}r\,{\rm d}\theta

\displaystyle\leq h(T)\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{T}\|v_{n}\|_{L^{2}}\ \|\nabla v_{n}\|_{L^{2}},\quad T>0.

The Young inequality yields

\frac{1}{T}\|v_{n}\|_{L^{2}}\ \|\nabla v_{n}\|_{L^{2}}\leq h(T)\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{4T^{2}h(T)}\|v_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

These estimates imply (
3.3
) after summation. The proof is complete.

Proposition 3.3

The same statement with

(3)

Proof.

Let us consider the numerical range as in the proof of Proposition
3.1
.

(1) We first estimate

. Let

\displaystyle 2|\langle V^{\bot}\operatorname{rot}v,v\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq 2(2|\alpha|h(T)+|\alpha|+|\delta|)\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{|\alpha|}{2T^{2}h(T)}\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(

4.1
Expansion of the order

When
|n|=1
, we denote

\displaystyle 1_{\delta}=\Big\{1+\Big(\frac{\delta}{2}\Big)^{2}\Big\}^{\frac{1}{2}},\qquad\eta_{n}=\xi_{n}-1.

(4.1)

Here

is defined in (

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Re(\xi_{n})&=\frac{1_{\delta}}{\sqrt{2}}\bigg[\Big\{1+\Big(\frac{\alpha}{1_{\delta}^{2}}\Big)^{2}\Big\}^{\frac{1}{2}}+1\bigg]^{\frac{1}{2}},\\
\Im(\xi_{n})&=\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha n)\frac{1_{\delta}}{\sqrt{2}}\bigg[\Big\{1+\Big(\frac{\alpha}{1_{\delta}^{2}}\Big)^{2}\Big\}^{\frac{1}{2}}-1\bigg]^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

We need the following expansion of

in the next subsection.

Lemma 4.1

Let
|n|=1
. For sufficiently small
\alpha,\delta\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\Re(\eta_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{2}+\delta^{2}}{8}+O(\alpha^{4}+\delta^{4}),

(4.2)

\displaystyle\Im(\eta_{n})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha n)\frac{|\alpha|}{2}+O\big(|\alpha|(\alpha^{2}+\delta^{2})\big).

(4.3)

All the implicit constants in

are independent of
\alpha,\delta
.

Proof.

The proof is done by the Taylor theorem. For (
4.2
), from

\Re(\eta_{n})=\Re(\xi_{n})-1=1_{\delta}\bigg\{1+\frac{1}{8}\Big(\frac{\alpha}{1_{\delta}^{2}}\Big)^{2}-\frac{5}{128}\Big(\frac{\alpha}{1_{\delta}^{2}}\Big)^{4}+O(\alpha^{6})\bigg\}-1,

we see that

\Re(\eta_{n})=1_{\delta}-1+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{8}+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{8}\Big(\frac{1}{1_{\delta}^{3}}-1\Big)-\frac{5\alpha^{4}}{128}-\frac{5\alpha^{4}}{128}\Big(\frac{1}{1_{\delta}^{7}}-1\Big)+O(\alpha^{6}).

Hence (
4.2
) is obtained by

\displaystyle 1_{\delta}-1=\frac{\delta^{2}}{8}-\frac{\delta^{4}}{128}+O(\delta^{6})

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1_{\delta}^{3}}-1=-\frac{3\delta^{2}}{8}+O(\delta^{4}),\qquad\frac{1}{1_{\delta}^{7}}-1=-\frac{7\delta^{2}}{8}+O(\delta^{4}).

For (

), from

\Im(\eta_{n})=\Im(\xi_{n})=\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha n)1_{\delta}\bigg\{\frac{1}{2}\Big|\frac{\alpha}{1_{\delta}^{2}}\Big|-\frac{1}{16}\Big|\frac{\alpha}{1_{\delta}^{2}}\Big|^{3}+O(|\alpha|^{5})\bigg\},

we see that

\Im(\eta_{n})=\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha n)\bigg\{\frac{|\alpha|}{2}+\frac{|\alpha|}{2}\Big(\frac{1}{1_{\delta}}-1\Big)-\frac{|\alpha|^{3}}{16}-\frac{|\alpha|^{3}}{16}\Big(\frac{1}{1_{\delta}^{5}}-1\Big)+O(|\alpha|^{5})\bigg\}.

Hence (
4.3
) is obtained by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1_{\delta}}-1=-\frac{\delta^{2}}{8}+O(\delta^{4}),\qquad\frac{1}{1_{\delta}^{5}}-1=-\frac{5\delta^{2}}{8}+O(\delta^{4}).

This completes the proof.

Lemma 4.2

) and by the Fubini theorem, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&z\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{1-\frac{\delta}{2}}K_{\eta_{n}}(zs)\,{\rm d}s\\
&=z\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{1-\frac{\delta}{2}}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{zs}{2}(t+\frac{1}{t})}t^{-\eta_{n}-1}\,{\rm d}t\bigg)\,{\rm d}s\\
&=\frac{z}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-1-\eta_{n}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{1-\frac{\delta}{2}}e^{-\frac{z}{2}(t+\frac{1}{t})s}\,{\rm d}s\bigg)\,{\rm d}t.\end{split}

Observing that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{1-\frac{\delta}{2}}e^{-as}\,{\rm d}s=\Gamma\Big(2-\frac{\delta}{2}\Big)a^{-2+\frac{\delta}{2}},\quad a\in\Sigma_{\frac{\pi}{2}},

we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{z}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-1-\eta_{n}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{1-\frac{\delta}{2}}e^{-\frac{z}{2}(t+\frac{1}{t})s}\,{\rm d}s\bigg)\,{\rm d}t\\
&=\Gamma\Big(2-\frac{\delta}{2}\Big)\Big(\frac{z}{2}\Big)^{-1+\frac{\delta}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-1-\eta_{n}}\Big(t+\frac{1}{t}\Big)^{-2+\frac{\delta}{2}}\,{\rm d}t\\
&=\Gamma\Big(2-\frac{\delta}{2}\Big)\Big(\frac{z}{2}\Big)^{-1+\frac{\delta}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{1-\frac{\delta}{2}-\eta_{n}}}{(t^{2}+1)^{2-\frac{\delta}{2}}}\,{\rm d}t.\end{split}

The change of variable
t=\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}
leads to

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{1-\frac{\delta}{2}-\eta_{n}}}{(t^{2}+1)^{2-\frac{\delta}{2}}}\,{\rm d}t&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\tau^{-\frac{\delta}{4}-\frac{\eta_{n}}{2}}}{(\tau+1)^{2-\frac{\delta}{2}}}\,{\rm d}\tau\\
&=\frac{1}{2}B\Big(1-\frac{\delta}{4}-\frac{\eta_{n}}{2},1-\frac{\delta}{4}+\frac{\eta_{n}}{2}\Big),\end{split}

where
B(p,q)
is the Beta function. Then the well-known formulas

\displaystyle z\Gamma(z)=\Gamma(z+1),\qquad B(p,q)=\frac{\Gamma(p)\Gamma(q)}{\Gamma(p+q)}

imply (
4.9
). This completes the proof.
∎

.

Proof.

This is a consequence of the previous proposition and Lemma

(

Proposition
4.7
below, giving a lower bound of
|F_{n}(z)|
, is proved based on the expansion (

Lemma 4.5

Let
|n|=1
. For sufficiently small
\alpha,\delta\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Log}\Gamma(1+\eta_{n})

\displaystyle=-\gamma\eta_{n}+O(|\eta_{n}|^{2}),

(4.12)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Log}\Delta(\delta,\eta_{n})

\displaystyle=\gamma\Big(\frac{\delta}{2}\Big)+O\Big(\Big(\frac{\delta}{2}\Big)^{2}+|\eta_{n}|^{2}\Big),

(4.13)

where
\gamma=0.5772\ldots
is the Euler constant. Moreover, if
\delta\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Log}\Delta(\delta,\eta_{n})-\operatorname{Log}\Gamma(1+\eta_{n})

\displaystyle=\gamma\Big(\frac{\delta}{2}+\eta_{n}\Big)+O\Big(\Big|\frac{\delta}{2}+\eta_{n}\Big|^{2}\Big).

(4.14)

All the implicit constants in
O(\cdot)
are independent of
\alpha,\delta
.

Proof.

We may apply the Taylor series expansion of
\operatorname{Log}\Gamma(1+z)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Log}\Gamma(1+z)=\gamma(-z)+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta(k)}{k}(-z)^{k},\quad\{|z|<1\}.

(4.15)

Here
\zeta(k)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}m^{-k}
is the Riemann zeta function. One can prove (
4.15
) using

\displaystyle z\Gamma(z)=\Gamma(z+1),\qquad\frac{1}{\Gamma(z)}=ze^{\gamma z}\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}\Big(1+\frac{z}{m}\Big)e^{-\frac{z}{m}}.

Indeed, from

\displaystyle\operatorname{Log}\Gamma(1+z)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Log}z-\operatorname{Log}\frac{1}{\Gamma(z)}

\displaystyle=\gamma(-z)-\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\operatorname{Log}\Big(1+\frac{z}{m}\Big)-\frac{z}{m}\bigg)

and the Taylor series expansion

\displaystyle\operatorname{Log}(1+z)=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k}(-z)^{k},\quad\{|z|<1\},

we see that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Log}\Gamma(1+z)=\gamma(-z)+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k}\Big(-\frac{z}{m}\Big)^{k},

which leads to (
4.15
) after change of order of summations.

The expansion (
4.12
) is a direct consequence of (
4.15
). Also, by

\displaystyle\operatorname{Log}\Gamma\Big(1-\frac{\delta}{4}-\frac{\eta_{n}}{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma}{2}\Big(\frac{\delta}{2}+\eta_{n}\Big)+O\Big(\Big|\frac{\delta}{2}+\eta_{n}\Big|^{2}\Big),

\displaystyle\operatorname{Log}\Gamma\Big(1-\frac{\delta}{4}+\frac{\eta_{n}}{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma}{2}\Big(\frac{\delta}{2}-\eta_{n}\Big)+O\Big(\Big|\frac{\delta}{2}-\eta_{n}\Big|^{2}\Big)

and the definition of
\Delta(\delta,\eta_{n})
in (
4.7
), we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Log}\Delta(\delta,\eta_{n})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Log}\Gamma\Big(1-\frac{\delta}{4}-\frac{\eta_{n}}{2}\Big)+\operatorname{Log}\Gamma\Big(1-\frac{\delta}{4}+\frac{\eta_{n}}{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=\gamma\Big(\frac{\delta}{2}\Big)+O\Big(\Big|\frac{\delta}{2}+\eta_{n}\Big|^{2}+\Big|\frac{\delta}{2}-\eta_{n}\Big|^{2}\Big),

which implies (
4.13
). If
\delta\geq 0
, we see from Lemma
4.1
that, for sufficiently small
\alpha,\delta
,

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{\delta}{2}\Big)^{2}+|\eta_{n}|^{2}=\Big|\frac{\delta}{2}+\eta_{n}\Big|^{2}-\delta\Re\eta_{n}\leq\Big|\frac{\delta}{2}+\eta_{n}\Big|^{2},

which implies (
4.14
). This completes the proof.
∎

The following is the key technical lemma in the proof of Proposition
4.7
below.

Lemma 4.6

Let
\epsilon\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
. Suppose that
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\zeta|\ll 1
satisfies

\displaystyle\Re\zeta>0,\qquad|\Im\zeta|>0

(4.16)

and

\displaystyle\Big\{\Re\zeta+(1+\kappa)\frac{(\Im\zeta)^{2}}{\Re\zeta}\Big\}\Big(\frac{\pi}{2}-\epsilon\Big)<\pi

(4.17)

with some constant
\kappa=\kappa(\epsilon)\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
independent of
\zeta
. Then, by defining

\displaystyle K(\zeta)=\min\bigg\{\Big\{\frac{(\Re\zeta)^{2}}{|\Im\zeta|}+|\Im\zeta|\Big\},\Re\zeta\bigg\},

(4.18)

one has

\displaystyle|1-w^{\zeta}|\geq C\min\Big\{1,K(\zeta)\big|\log|w|\big|\Big\},\quad w\in\Sigma_{\frac{\pi}{2}-\epsilon}\cap\{|z|<1\}.

(4.19)

The constant
C
depends only on
\epsilon
and
\kappa
.

Proof.

By setting

\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle=(\Re\zeta)\log|w|-(\Im\zeta)\operatorname{arg}w,

(4.20)

\displaystyle\theta

\displaystyle=(\Im\zeta)\log|w|+(\Re\zeta)\operatorname{arg}w,

(4.21)

we denote

1-w^{\zeta}=1-e^{\mu}e^{i\theta}.

From

\log|w|=\frac{\Im\zeta}{\Re\zeta}\operatorname{arg}w+\frac{1}{\Re\zeta}\mu,

we compute

\displaystyle\theta=\Big\{\Re\zeta+\frac{(\Im\zeta)^{2}}{\Re\zeta}\Big\}\operatorname{arg}w+\frac{\Im\zeta}{\Re\zeta}\mu.

(4.22)

Before going into details, let us explain the difficulties. When
\mu
is close to zero, one essentially needs to provide lower bounds of
|1-e^{i\theta}|
. However, such bounds require good control of
\theta
, since
1-e^{i\theta}
vanishes when
\theta=2m\pi
with
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. The reason why the conditions (
4.16
)–(
4.17
) are needed is to control the range of
\theta
when
\mu
is close to zero.

We will consider two cases:

(i) Case
|\mu|\leq\kappa|\Im\zeta||\operatorname{arg}w|
. In this case, we have

\frac{1}{2}\leq e^{\mu}\leq\frac{3}{2}.

In addition, by (
4.22
) and the assumption (
4.17
),

|\theta|\leq\Big\{\Re\zeta+(1+\kappa)\frac{(\Im\zeta)^{2}}{\Re\zeta}\Big\}|\operatorname{arg}w|<\pi,\quad w\in\Sigma_{\frac{\pi}{2}-\epsilon}\cap\{|z|<1\}.

Thus
e^{i\theta}
is equal to
1
if and only if
\theta=0
. If
0\leq|\theta|<\frac{\pi}{2}
, the imaginary part gives

\displaystyle|1-e^{\mu}e^{i\theta}|\geq e^{\mu}|\sin\theta|\geq e^{\mu}\frac{2}{\pi}|\theta|\geq\frac{1}{\pi}|\theta|.

(4.23)

If
\frac{\pi}{2}\leq|\theta|<\pi
, the real part gives

\displaystyle|1-e^{\mu}e^{i\theta}|\geq|1-e^{\mu}\cos\theta|\geq 1>\frac{1}{\pi}|\theta|.

(4.24)

Hence we estimate
|\theta|
. Combining
|\mu|\leq\kappa|\Im\zeta||\operatorname{arg}w|
with (
4.22
), we have

|\theta|\geq\Big\{\Re\zeta+(1-\kappa)\frac{(\Im\zeta)^{2}}{\Re\zeta}\Big\}|\operatorname{arg}w|.

Combining with (
4.20
),

\displaystyle\Re\zeta\big|\log|w|\big|\leq|\Im\zeta||\operatorname{arg}w|+|\mu|\leq(1+\kappa)|\Im\zeta||\operatorname{arg}w|.

By these two estimates, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}|\theta|&\geq\Big\{\Re\zeta+(1-\kappa)\frac{(\Im\zeta)^{2}}{\Re\zeta}\Big\}\frac{\Re\zeta\big|\log|w|\big|}{(1+\kappa)|\Im\zeta|}\\
&\geq\frac{1-\kappa}{1+\kappa}\Big\{\frac{(\Re\zeta)^{2}}{|\Im\zeta|}+|\Im\zeta|\Big\}\big|\log|w|\big|.\end{split}

Therefore, from (
4.23
) and (
4.24
), we see that

\displaystyle|1-e^{\mu}e^{i\theta}|\geq\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{1-\kappa}{1+\kappa}\Big\{\frac{(\Re\zeta)^{2}}{|\Im\zeta|}+|\Im\zeta|\Big\}\big|\log|w|\big|.

(4.25)

(ii) Case
|\mu|>\kappa|\Im\zeta||\operatorname{arg}w|
. In this case, we may rely on

\displaystyle|1-e^{\mu}e^{i\theta}|\geq|1-e^{\mu}|\geq e^{-1}\min\{1,|\mu|\},\quad\mu,\theta\in\mathbb{R}.

(4.26)

We deduce that if
|\operatorname{arg}w|>\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Re\zeta}{|\Im\zeta|}\big|\log|w|\big|
,

|\mu|>\frac{\kappa}{2}\Re\zeta\big|\log|w|\big|

by
|\mu|>\kappa|\Im\zeta||\operatorname{arg}w|
, and that if
|\operatorname{arg}w|\leq\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Re\zeta}{|\Im\zeta|}\big|\log|w|\big|
,

|\mu|\geq\Re\zeta\big|\log|w|\big|-|\Im\zeta||\operatorname{arg}w|\geq\frac{1}{2}\Re\zeta\big|\log|w|\big|.

by (
4.20
). Combining these two with (
4.26
), we obtain

\displaystyle|1-e^{\mu}e^{i\theta}|\geq e^{-1}\min\Big\{1,\frac{\kappa}{2}\Re\zeta\big|\log|w|\big|\Big\}.

(4.27)

The assertion follows from (
4.25
) and (
4.27
). The proof is complete.
∎

Proposition 4.7

Let
|n|=1
and
\epsilon\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
. Let
K(\zeta)
be defined in (
4.18
).
For sufficiently small

(4.28)

The constant

\epsilon
.

Remark 4.8

One observes a sort of stabilizing effect by the flow
\delta W
from this proposition. By the definition (
4.18
) and Lemma
4.1
, we have a simple (but rough) estimate from below

\displaystyle\begin{split}K\Big(\frac{\delta}{2}+\eta_{n}\Big)&\geq\min\Big\{\Big|\Im\Big(\frac{\delta}{2}+\eta_{n}\Big)\Big|,\Re\Big(\frac{\delta}{2}+\eta_{n}\Big)\Big\}\\
&\geq\frac{1}{8}\min\{|\alpha|,\delta+\alpha^{2}\}.\end{split}

(4.29)

The second inequality is valid for sufficiently small
\alpha,\delta
. Therefore, the radius of the disks on which
F_{n}(z)
has no zeros is greater than that for
\delta=0
. This is interpreted as a stabilizing effect by
\delta W
in time frequency near zero related to large-time behavior of flows.

Proof.

Let
z\in\Sigma_{\frac{\pi}{2}-\epsilon}\cap\{|z|<\frac{1}{2}\}
first. Using Corollary
4.4
, we write

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{\delta}{2}+\eta_{n}\Big)F_{n}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(1+\eta_{n})}{2}\Big(\frac{z}{2}\Big)^{-1-\eta_{n}}\Big\{1-\frac{\Delta(\delta,\eta_{n})}{\Gamma(1+\eta_{n})}\Big(\frac{z}{2}\Big)^{\frac{\delta}{2}+\eta_{n}}+R_{n}(z)\Big\}.

(4.30)

Here

R_{n}(z)=\frac{2}{\Gamma(1+\eta_{n})}\Big(\frac{z}{2}\Big)^{1+\eta_{n}}R^{(3)}_{n}(z)

\displaystyle|R_{n}(z)|\leq C|z|^{2}\big|\log|z|\big|,\quad z\in\Sigma_{\frac{\pi}{2}}\cap\Big\{|z|<\frac{1}{2}\Big\}.

(4.31)

The condition
\delta\geq 0
and Lemma

imply

\frac{\Delta(\delta,\eta_{n})}{\Gamma(1+\eta_{n})}=e^{\operatorname{Log}\Delta(\delta,\eta_{n})-\operatorname{Log}\Gamma(1+\eta_{n})}=e^{\gamma_{n}(\frac{\delta}{2}+\eta_{n})},

where

is the function satisfying

\gamma_{n}=\gamma+O\Big(\Big|\frac{\delta}{2}+\eta_{n}\Big|\Big).

Setting

\zeta_{n}=\frac{\delta}{2}+\eta_{n},\qquad w_{n}=\frac{e^{\gamma_{n}}}{2}z,

we will derive a lower bound of

1-\frac{\Delta(\delta,\eta_{n})}{\Gamma(1+\eta_{n})}\Big(\frac{z}{2}\Big)^{\frac{\delta}{2}+\eta_{n}}=1-w_{n}^{\zeta_{n}}.

To apply Lemma
4.6
, we check that all the conditions are fulfilled by

. We have

|w_{n}|\leq 1,\qquad|\operatorname{arg}w_{n}|\leq\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{\epsilon}{2}

\alpha,\delta

4.1
. By the same lemma, there are constants

independent of

\frac{(\Im\zeta_{n})^{2}}{\Re\zeta_{n}}\leq\Big\{\frac{|\alpha|}{2}+C_{1}|\alpha|(\alpha^{2}+\delta^{2})\Big\}^{2}\Big\{\frac{\alpha^{2}+\delta^{2}}{8}-C_{2}(\alpha^{4}+\delta^{4})\Big\}^{-1}.

Thus, for sufficiently small

\frac{(\Im\zeta_{n})^{2}}{\Re\zeta_{n}}=2+O(\alpha^{2}+\delta^{2})

and, with a constant
\kappa_{n}=\kappa_{n}(\epsilon)\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
independent of
\alpha,\delta
,

\Big\{\Re\zeta_{n}+(1+\kappa_{n})\frac{(\Im\zeta_{n})^{2}}{\Re\zeta_{n}}\Big\}\Big(\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{\epsilon}{2}\Big)<\pi,

which is (
4.17
) with
\epsilon

\frac{\epsilon}{2}
. Now, applying Lemma
4.6
, we see that

\displaystyle|1-w_{n}^{\zeta_{n}}|

\displaystyle\geq C\min\Big\{1,K(\zeta_{n})\big|\log|w_{n}^{\zeta_{n}}|\big|\Big\}

\displaystyle\geq C\min\Big\{1,K(\zeta_{n})\big|\log|z|\big|\Big\},\quad z\in\Sigma_{\frac{\pi}{2}-\epsilon}\cap\Big\{|z|<\frac{1}{2}\Big\},

for sufficiently small
\alpha,\delta
. The constant

\epsilon
.

Therefore, combining this estimate with (
4.30
) and (
4.31
), we obtain a lower bound

\displaystyle\begin{split}|\zeta_{n}F_{n}(z)|\geq C|z|^{-1-\Re\eta_{n}}\Big(\min\Big\{1,K(\zeta_{n})\big|\log|z|\big|\Big\}-|z|^{2}\big|\log|z|\big|\Big),\end{split}

5
Resolvent estimate

In this section, we estimate the solutions of

(R)

for given
\lambda\in\rho(-{\mathbb{A}}_{V})
and

. The main result is the following.

Proposition 5.1

Let
\epsilon\in(0,\frac{\pi}{4})
and let
(\alpha,\delta)\in\mathbb{R}^{\ast}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be sufficiently small. We have, for
q\in(1.2]
and

\begin{split}\|(\lambda+{\mathbb{A}}_{V})^{-1}f\|_{L^{2}}\leq C|\lambda|^{-\frac{3}{2}+\frac{1}{q}}\|f\|_{L^{q}},\quad\lambda\in\Sigma_{\frac{3}{4}\pi-\epsilon}\end{split}

and, for

\begin{split}\|\nabla(\lambda+{\mathbb{A}}_{V})^{-1}f\|_{L^{2}}\leq C|\lambda|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{2}},\quad\lambda\in\Sigma_{\frac{3}{4}\pi-\epsilon}.\end{split}

(5.2)

The constant
C
depends only on
\alpha,\delta,\epsilon,q
.

Once Proposition
5.1
is proved, it is routine to prove Theorem
1.1
by representing
\{e^{-t{\mathbb{A}}_{V}}\}_{t\geq 0}
in the Dunford integral of the resolvent. Thus the detail will be given in Appendix

.

We prove Proposition

; see Proposition

for details. Therefore, all that remains is to prove the estimate when

5.1
Energy method

We start with a priori estimates for (

) using energy method.

Lemma 5.2

Let
\alpha,\delta\in\mathbb{R}
. For
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
,
q\in(1,2]
and
f\in L^{2}_{\sigma}(\Omega)\cap L^{q}(\Omega)^{2}
, suppose that there is a solution
v\in D(\mathbb{A}_{V})
of (
R
). Then we have the following.

(1)

For
v_{n}
with
|n|=1
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Big(|\Im\lambda|+\Re\lambda-4e\alpha^{2}e^{-\frac{1}{4|\alpha|}}\Big)\|v_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\Big\{\frac{1}{4}-2(|\alpha|+|\delta|)\Big\}\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&\leq C\|f\|_{L^{q}}^{\frac{2q}{3q-2}}\|v_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{4(q-1)}{3q-2}}.\end{split}

(2)

For
v_{\neq}=v-\sum_{|n|=1}v_{n}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&(|\Im\lambda|+\Re\lambda)\|v_{\neq}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\Big\{\frac{3}{4}-2(|\alpha|+|\delta|)\Big\}\|\nabla v_{\neq}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&\leq C\|f\|_{L^{q}}^{\frac{2q}{3q-2}}\|v_{\neq}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{4(q-1)}{3q-2}}.\end{split}

The constant
C
depends only on
q
.

Proof.

(1) Taking the inner product of (
R
) with
v_{n}
, we see that

\lambda\|v_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\langle-\mathbb{A}_{V}v_{n},v_{n}\rangle=\langle f,v_{n}\rangle

and hence that

\displaystyle\begin{split}(|\Im\lambda|+\Re\lambda)\|v_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq|\Im\langle-\mathbb{A}_{V}v_{n},v_{n}\rangle|+\Re\langle-\mathbb{A}_{V}v_{n},v_{n}\rangle+2|\langle f,v_{n}\rangle|.\end{split}

(5.3)

From (
3.1
) and (
3.5
) in Section
3
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&|\Im\langle-\mathbb{A}_{V}v_{n},v_{n}\rangle|+\Re\langle-\mathbb{A}_{V}v_{n},v_{n}\rangle\\
&\leq-\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2|\langle V^{\bot}\operatorname{rot}v_{n},v_{n}\rangle|\\
&\leq\Big\{-\frac{1}{2}+2(|\alpha|+|\delta|)\Big\}\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+4e\alpha^{2}e^{-\frac{1}{4|\alpha|}}\|v_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.\end{split}

(5.4)

One has, by the Hölder and the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities,

Appendix A
Modified Bessel function

We summarize the facts about the modified Bessel functions. Our main references are
[
40
,
3
]
. The modified Bessel function of the first kind
I_{\mu}(z)
of order
\mu
is defined by

\displaystyle I_{\mu}(z)=\Big(\frac{z}{2}\Big)^{\mu}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m!\Gamma(\mu+m+1)}\Big(\frac{z}{2}\Big)^{2m},\quad z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}_{\leq 0},

(A.1)

where
\Gamma(z)
is the Gamma function, the second kind
K_{\mu}(z)
of order
\mu\notin\mathbb{Z}
is by

\displaystyle K_{\mu}(z)=\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{I_{-\mu}(z)-I_{\mu}(z)}{\sin(\mu\pi)},\quad z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}_{\leq 0},

(A.2)

and
K_{n}(z)
of order
n\in\mathbb{Z}
is by the limit of
K_{\mu}(z)
in (
A.2
) as
\mu\to n
. In this paper, we exclusively consider the case where the order
\mu
satisfies
\mu\notin\mathbb{Z}
and
\Re\mu>0
.

The functions
K_{\mu}(z)
and
I_{\mu}(z)
are linearly independent solutions of

-\frac{\,{\rm d}^{2}\omega}{\,{\rm d}z^{2}}-\frac{1}{z}\frac{\,{\rm d}\omega}{\,{\rm d}z}+\Big(1+\frac{\mu^{2}}{z^{2}}\Big)\omega=0,\quad z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}_{\leq 0},

with the Wronskian

\displaystyle\det\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}K_{\mu}(z)&I_{\mu}(z)\\
\displaystyle{\frac{\,{\rm d}K_{\mu}}{\,{\rm d}z}(z)}&\displaystyle{\frac{\,{\rm d}I_{\mu}}{\,{\rm d}z}(z)}\end{array}\right)=\frac{1}{z}.

It is well known that
I_{\mu}(z)
grows exponentially and
K_{\mu}(z)
decays exponentially as
|z|\to\infty
in
\Sigma_{\frac{\pi}{2}}
; see
[
3
, Section 4.12]
. As an integral representation useful in Section
4
, we have

\displaystyle K_{\mu}(z)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{z}{2}(t+\frac{1}{t})}t^{-\mu-1}\,{\rm d}t,\quad z\in\Sigma_{\frac{\pi}{2}},

(A.5)

which can be deduced by the formula
[
40
, Chapter
\mathrm{V}\hskip-1.2pt\mathrm{I}
6
\cdot
22 (5)]
and change of variables.

Collected below are the estimates involving
K_{\mu}(z)
and
I_{\mu}(z)
used in this paper. Each of them can be found in the references
[
3
,
40
]
or follows from a simple calculation using Lemma
A.1
. and hence we omit the proof. For the details when
\delta=0
, we refer to
[
33
, Lemma 3.31 and Appendix A]
and to
[
22
]
studying the dependence on
\alpha
in the estimates.

We recall that the constants
\eta_{n}
and
\xi_{n}
are defined in (
4.1
) and (
3.6
), respectively.

Lemma A.1

Let
\Re\mu>0
,
\epsilon\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
and
M>0
. We have

\displaystyle|K_{\mu}(z)|

\displaystyle\leq C|z|^{-\Re\mu},\quad z\in\Sigma_{\frac{\pi}{2}}\cap\{|z|<M\},

\displaystyle|K_{\mu}(z)|

\displaystyle\leq C|z|^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\Re z},\quad z\in\Sigma_{\frac{\pi}{2}}\cap\{|z|\geq M\},

Appendix B
Homogeneous equation for vorticity

For
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}_{\leq 0}
, we consider the homogeneous equation of (
2.13
)

-\frac{\,{\rm d}^{2}\omega_{n}}{\,{\rm d}r^{2}}-\frac{1+\delta}{r}\frac{\,{\rm d}\omega_{n}}{\,{\rm d}r}+\Big(\lambda+\frac{n^{2}+i\alpha n}{r^{2}}\Big)\omega_{n}=0,\quad r>1.

We will prove that its linearly independent solutions are, with
\xi_{n}
defined in (
3.6
),

\displaystyle r^{-\frac{\delta}{2}}K_{\xi_{n}}(\sqrt{\lambda}r)\quad\text{and}\quad r^{-\frac{\delta}{2}}I_{\xi_{n}}(\sqrt{\lambda}r),

(B.1)

and the Wronskian is
r^{-1-\delta}
. The proof is as follows. Applying the transformation

\displaystyle\omega_{n}(r)=r^{-\frac{\delta}{2}}\tilde{\omega}_{n}(r),

(B.2)

we find that
\tilde{\omega}_{n}
solves

-\frac{\,{\rm d}^{2}\tilde{\omega}_{n}}{\,{\rm d}r^{2}}-\frac{1}{r}\frac{\,{\rm d}\tilde{\omega}_{n}}{\,{\rm d}r}+\Big(\lambda+\frac{\xi_{n}^{2}}{r^{2}}\Big)\tilde{\omega}_{n}=0,\quad r>1.

By Appendix
A
, its linearly independent solutions are

K_{\xi_{n}}(\sqrt{\lambda}r)\quad\text{and}\quad I_{\xi_{n}}(\sqrt{\lambda}r).

Hence, by the inverse transformation of (
B.2
), we see that the desired solutions are (
B.1
). One can easily compute the Wronskian using (
A
). The proof is complete.

1.
Introduction

The aim of this article is to study the spectral type of the Landau levels of the singularly perturbed Landau Hamiltonian

(1.1)

H_{\upsilon}=(-i\nabla-A)^{2}+\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma},

where
A(x):=\tfrac{b}{2}(-x_{2},x_{1})
,
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, is a magnetic potential which generates a constant scalar magnetic field
b>0
, and the singular perturbation
is supported on a
C^{1,1}
-smooth Jordan curve
\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and has strength
\upsilon\in L^{p}(\Gamma;{\mathbb{R}})
,
p>1
.
The expression (
1.1
) is of formal nature here and the self-adjoint operator
H_{\upsilon}
will be defined rigorously via the corresponding quadratic form in
Section
2
.
If the singular perturbation is absent, that is,

\upsilon=0
in (
1.1
), then the operator reduces to the usual self-adjoint Landau Hamiltonian
H_{0}=(-i\nabla-A)^{2}
.
It is well known that

\sigma(H_{0})=\sigma_{\rm ess}(H_{0})=\bigcup_{q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}\left\{\Lambda_{q}\right\},

where the
Landau levels

\Lambda_{q}:=b(2q+1)
,
q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}=\{0,1,2,\dots\}
, are eigenvalues of
H_{0}
of infinite multiplicity. Under our assumption on
\Gamma
and

\upsilon
it turns out that
H_{\upsilon}
is a compact perturbation of
H_{0}
in the resolvent sense and hence the essential spectrum remains invariant, that is,

\sigma_{\rm ess}(H_{\upsilon})=\sigma_{\rm ess}(H_{0})=\bigcup_{q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}\left\{\Lambda_{q}\right\}.

In the spectral gaps
(\Lambda_{q-1},\Lambda_{q})
, where
q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
and
\Lambda_{-1}:=-\infty
, of
H_{0}
there may appear discrete eigenvalues of
H_{\upsilon}
which
can only accumulate at the Landau levels
\Lambda_{q}
,
q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
. Some results on the asymptotic distribution near any fixed
\Lambda_{q}
of these discrete eigenvalues were obtained in
[
7
]
.
In particular, it was shown that if either
\upsilon\geq 0
or
\upsilon\leq 0
on
\Gamma
,
v\not\equiv 0
, and
certain additional regularity assumptions hold, then in a neighbourhood of any
\Lambda_{q}
there are infinitely many discrete eigenvalues of
H_{\upsilon}
and their accumulation rate to the Landau levels is described in terms of the logarithmic capacity of the interaction support; cf.
[
10
,
20
,
35
,
37
]
for similar results on the clustering of eigenvalues of Landau Hamiltonians on unbounded domains with Dirichlet, Neumann, and Robin boundary conditions.

Our main objective in this article is to obtain a deeper understanding of the spectral points
\Lambda_{q}
,
q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
, of the perturbed operator
H_{\upsilon}
;
in other words, we are interested in the fate of the Landau levels
\Lambda_{q}
under
\delta
-potentials of strength
\upsilon
.
In particular, we would like to know what part of the infinite-dimensional eigenspace
{\rm ker}\,(H_{0}-\Lambda_{q})
is transformed into an eigenspace
{\rm ker}\,(H_{\upsilon}-\Lambda_{q})

under the singular perturbation
\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma}
.

The analogous problem on the fate of Landau levels under
regular
perturbations of the Landau Hamiltonian
H_{0}
was investigated earlier in
[
28
]
.
Roughly speaking, it was shown that for any non-negative potential
V\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2};{\mathbb{R}})
,
V\not\equiv 0
,
with
\|V\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}<2b

one has

(1.2)

{\rm ker}\,(H_{0}\pm V-\Lambda_{q})=\left\{0\right\}.

The assumption that
V
is sign-definite is essential here. In fact, in
[
28
]
it was also shown that for every
q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
,
there exists a compactly supported
V\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2};{\mathbb{R}})
with
\|V\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}<b
of non-constant sign, such that

{\rm dim\,ker}\,(H_{0}+V-\Lambda_{q})=\infty.

The key idea in the proof of (
1.2
) is to show
{\rm ker}\,(H_{0}\pm V-\Lambda_{q})\subset{\rm ker}\,(\hat{T}_{q}(V))
and
{\rm ker}\,(\hat{T}_{q}(V))=\{0\}
if
\|V\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}<2b
, where
\hat{T}_{q}(V):=p_{q}Vp_{q}

is a Berezin-Toeplitz type operator and
p_{q}
denotes the orthogonal projection onto the eigenspace
{\rm ker}\,(H_{0}-\Lambda_{q})
.

In our treatment of the perturbed Landau Hamiltonian with a
\delta
-potential in (
1.1
) a singular analogue of the Berezin-Toeplitz operator plays a key role; cf. the discussion below (
2.11
) for more details and references.
More precisely, if
\tau
is the restriction operator onto
\Gamma
we consider the operator

T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma}):=(\tau p_{q})^{*}\upsilon(\tau p_{q})
and in our main results we show that the analysis of

{\rm ker}\,(H_{\pm\upsilon}-\Lambda_{q})
can be reduced to that of
{\rm ker}\,(T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma}))
. Namely, under the definiteness assumption

\upsilon\geq 0
we prove in Theorem
3.1
that

{\rm ker}\,(T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma}))\subset{\rm ker}\,(H_{\pm\upsilon}-\Lambda_{q}),\quad q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+},

and
0\leq{\rm dim\,ker}\,(H_{\pm\upsilon}-\Lambda_{q})-{\rm dim\,ker}\,(T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma}))<\infty
for all
q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
. Furthermore,
if
\|\upsilon\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}
is not too large it turns out that

{\rm ker}\,(H_{\pm\upsilon}-\Lambda_{q})={\rm ker}\,(T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma})),\quad q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}.

As we will see, for
\upsilon\geq 0
the kernel of
T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma})
consists of eigenfunctions of
H_{0}
for
\Lambda_{q}
which vanish on the support of
\upsilon
. Intuitively, it is clear that such functions
u\in{\rm ker}\,(T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma}))
are also eigenfunctions of
H_{\upsilon}
, as in this case one formally has
\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma}u=0
, i.e. the singular interaction does not have an effect on
u
, and hence
H_{\upsilon}u=H_{0}u=\Lambda_{q}u
. This allows one to show with the help of
[
7
, Lemma 3.7]
that the kernel of
T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma})
is finite-dimensional under the assumption that
\upsilon
is strictly positive.
Moreover, this connection provides a direct link to nodal sets for eigenfunctions of
H_{0}
and the non-emptiness of
{\rm ker}\,(H_{\upsilon}-\Lambda_{q})
. The above observation means, in particular, that for all
\upsilon\in L^{p}(\Gamma)
one has
{\rm ker}\,(H_{\pm\upsilon}-\Lambda_{q})\neq\{0\}
, whenever
\Gamma
is contained in a nodal set of an eigenfunction of
H_{0}
. This is in strong contrast to the case of regular potentials; cf. (
1.2
).
Additionally, for the first Landau level
\Lambda_{0}
we find

{\rm ker}\,(T_{0}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma}))=\{0\}
in Theorem
3.6
, which leads to

{\rm ker}\,(H_{\upsilon}-\Lambda_{0})=\{0\}.

At present it is not clear if
{\rm dim\,ker}\,(T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma}))
can be further estimated for higher Landau levels and general curves
\Gamma
.
However, we find it worthwhile to discuss the special case that

\Gamma={\mathcal{C}}_{r}
is a circle of radius
r\in(0,\infty)
. In this situation we find that

{\rm dim\,ker}\,(T_{q}(\delta_{{\mathcal{C}}_{r}}))\leq q,\quad q=1,2\ldots,

and the radii
r\in(0,\infty)
for which
{\rm dim\,ker}(T_{q}(\delta_{{\mathcal{C}}_{r}}))\geq 1
form an infinite and discrete set; cf. Theorem
3.9
. The idea to prove these results is again to study when a circle is a nodal set for an eigenfunction of
H_{0}
and use the fact, that this can be characterized explicitly in terms of zeros of Laguerre polynomials.
Translating this observation to the spectral points
\Lambda_{q}
of the perturbed Landau operator (
1.1
) leads to a precise understanding of the fate of
Landau levels under
\delta
-perturbations supported on circles. For example, if
\upsilon\not\equiv 0
and
\upsilon\geq 0
on some non-empty open subset of
\Gamma
, then
\Lambda_{q}
can only be an eigenvalue of finite multiplicity; cf. Section
3.2
for details.

The article is organized as follows. In the next section we introduce the Landau Hamiltonian perturbed by singular
\delta
-interactions. In Section
3
we formulate our main results. Section
4
contains some auxiliary facts from the spectral theory of the Landau Hamiltonian.
Finally, in Section
5
we prove our main theorems.

2.
Landau Hamiltonians with
\delta
-interactions supported on curves

Let
b>0
be a constant scalar magnetic field. Then

A(x):=\frac{b}{2}(-x_{2},x_{1}),\quad x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2},

is a magnetic potential which generates
b
, i.e.

b=\frac{\partial A_{2}}{\partial x_{1}}-\frac{\partial A_{1}}{\partial x_{2}}.

Denote by

\Pi(A)=(\Pi_{1}(A),\Pi_{2}(A)):=-i\nabla-A

the magnetic gradient. In the following, for
\ell=(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{2}
with
{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}=\{0,1,2,\dots\}
the notations
|\ell|:=\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}
and
\Pi(A)^{\ell}:=\Pi_{1}(A)^{\ell_{1}}\Pi_{2}(A)^{\ell_{2}}
are used. For an open non-empty set
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and an index
s\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
introduce the magnetic Sobolev spaces

{\rm H}_{A}^{s}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(\Omega)\,|\,\Pi(A)^{\ell}u\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}),\;\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{2},\;0\leq|\ell|\leq s\right\}

with a norm defined by

\|u\|^{2}_{{\rm H}_{A}^{s}(\Omega)}:=\sum_{\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{2}:0\leq|\ell|\leq s}\int_{\Omega}|\Pi(A)^{\ell}u|^{2}dx.

Throughout this paper it is assumed that
\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
is a
C^{1,1}
-smooth Jordan curve, i.e. a closed simple curve which is mapped onto the unit circle by a
C^{1,1}
-smooth diffeomorphism. Let
{\rm H}^{1/2}(\Gamma)
be the
L^{2}
-based Sobolev space of order
1/2
on
\Gamma
.

The Dirichlet trace operator
\tau:{\rm H}^{1}_{A}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\to{\rm H}^{1/2}(\Gamma)
is the continuous extension of the restriction map

{\rm H}^{1}_{A}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\cap C({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\ni u\mapsto u_{|\Gamma}.

Assume that
\upsilon\in L^{p}(\Gamma;{\mathbb{R}})
with
p>1
. Denote by
H_{\upsilon}
the self-adjoint operator generated in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
by the
symmetric, densely defined, lower-bounded, and closed quadratic form

(2.1)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|\Pi(A)u|^{2}dx+\int_{\Gamma}\upsilon|\tau u|^{2}\,ds,\quad u\in{\rm H}_{A}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2});

cf. Appendix
A
.
In particular, for
\upsilon=0
one obtains

H_{0}=\Pi_{1}(A)^{2}+\Pi_{2}(A)^{2}=(-i\nabla-A)^{2},

which is the
Landau Hamiltonian
, self-adjoint on
{\rm H}_{A}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
(see, e.g.,
[
18
, Appendix A]
), and essentially self-adjoint on
C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
(see
[
30
, Theorem 2]
). As mentioned in the introduction one has

\sigma(H_{0})=\sigma_{\rm ess}(H_{0})=\bigcup_{q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}\left\{\Lambda_{q}\right\},

where
\Lambda_{q}:=b(2q+1)
,
q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
, are the
Landau levels
which are eigenvalues of
H_{0}
of infinite multiplicity (see
[
4
,
17
,
29
]
). In particular,

\inf\sigma(H_{0})=\Lambda_{0}=b>0.

Note that integration by parts allows to find an explicit characterization of
H_{\upsilon}
. Denote by
\Omega_{\rm in}
and
\Omega_{\rm ex}
the interior and the exterior of
\Gamma
, respectively, and by
\nu
the unit normal vector on
\Gamma
pointing outwards of
\Omega_{\rm in}
. Then one can show in the same way as in
[
7
, Section 4]

that

(2.2)

\begin{split}(H_{\upsilon}u)_{\natural}&=(-i\nabla-A)^{2}u_{\natural}\quad\text{ for }\natural={\rm in},{\rm ex}\\
{\mathfrak{D}}(H_{\upsilon})&=\bigg\{u\in{\rm H}_{A}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})|(-i\nabla-A)^{2}u_{\natural}\in L^{2}(\Omega_{\natural})\text{ for }\natural={\rm in},{\rm ex},\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\frac{\partial u_{\rm ex}}{\partial\nu}-\frac{\partial u_{\rm in}}{\partial\nu}=\upsilon u\,\,\text{on}\,\,\Gamma\bigg\}.\end{split}

In the next lemma it is shown that the difference of the resolvents of
H_{0}
and
H_{\upsilon}
is compact, which implies that the essential spectra of
H_{0}
and
H_{\upsilon}
coincide. In order to formulate the lemma, define for
\lambda>-b
the operator

(2.3)

G_{\upsilon}(\lambda):=|\upsilon|^{1/2}\tau(H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1/2}:L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\to L^{2}(\Gamma).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\lambda>-b
and set
J_{\upsilon}:={\rm sign}\,\upsilon
. Then
G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)
is compact and there exists
\lambda_{0}>-b
such that for all
\lambda>\lambda_{0}
the resolvent difference of
H_{0}
and
H_{\upsilon}
is a compact
operator in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
and admits the
factorization

\begin{split}&(H_{\upsilon}+\lambda)^{-1}-(H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1}\\
&\quad=-(H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1/2}G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)^{*}J_{\upsilon}G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)(I+G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)^{*}J_{\upsilon}G_{\upsilon}(\lambda))^{-1}(H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1/2}.\end{split}

In particular, one has

(2.4)

\sigma_{\rm ess}(H_{0})=\sigma_{\rm ess}(H_{\upsilon})=\bigcup_{q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}\left\{\Lambda_{q}\right\}.

Proof.

Note first that the operator
G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)
in (
2.3
) depends only on
|\upsilon|
but not on the sign of
\upsilon
.
The assumption
\upsilon\in L^{p}(\Gamma;{\mathbb{R}})
with
p>1
, and the compactness of the trace

{\rm H}^{1}_{A}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\rightarrow L^{r}(\Gamma)
for any
r>1
(see
[
33
, Section 2.6, Theorem 6.2]
), easily imply that

G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)
is compact, and

\begin{split}\|G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)\|^{2}&=\sup_{0\neq w\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\frac{\||\upsilon|^{1/2}\tau(H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1/2}w\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}}{\|w\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}}\\
&=\sup_{0\neq u\in{\rm H}_{A}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\frac{\||\upsilon|^{1/2}\tau u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}}{\|(H_{0}+\lambda)^{1/2}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}}\\
&=\sup_{0\neq u\in{\rm H}_{A}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\frac{\int_{\Gamma}|\upsilon||\tau u|^{2}ds}{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(|\Pi(A)u|^{2}+\lambda|u|^{2})\,dx},\end{split}

so that the Hölder inequality leads to the estimate

(2.5)

\|G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)\|^{2}\leq C_{p}(\lambda)\|\upsilon\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}

with

(2.6)

C_{p}(\lambda):=\sup_{0\neq u\in{\rm H}_{A}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\frac{\left(\int_{\Gamma}|\tau u|^{2p^{\prime}}ds\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}}{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(|\Pi(A)u|^{2}+\lambda|u|^{2})\,dx},\quad p^{\prime}:=\frac{p}{p-1},\quad\lambda>-b.

Set

J_{\upsilon}={\rm sign}\,{\upsilon}:=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\upsilon|\upsilon|^{-1}&&\text{if }\;\upsilon\neq 0,\\
&0&&\text{if }\;\upsilon=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Let
\lambda>-b
. Then one has for
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
,
w:=(H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1/2}u\in{\rm H}^{1}_{A}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
, and the self-adjoint operator
G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)^{*}J_{\upsilon}G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)

(2.7)

\big\langle(I+G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)^{*}J_{\upsilon}G_{\upsilon}(\lambda))u,u\big\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\big(|\Pi(A)w|^{2}+\lambda|w|^{2}\big)dx+\int_{\Gamma}\upsilon|\tau w|^{2}\,ds

and hence, with (
2.1
) one concludes that

(2.8)

\lambda>-\inf\sigma(H_{\upsilon})

is equivalent to

(2.9)

I+G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)^{*}J_{\upsilon}G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)>0.

Assume in the following that
\lambda>-\inf\sigma(H_{\upsilon})
is fixed. Due to the compactness of
G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)^{*}J_{\upsilon}G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)
it follows from (
2.9
) that the operator

I+G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)^{*}J_{\upsilon}G_{\upsilon}(\lambda):L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\to L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})

is boundedly invertible. Thus, we have

(2.10)

H_{\upsilon}+\lambda=M_{\upsilon}(\lambda)^{*}M_{\upsilon}(\lambda)

where the operator

M_{\upsilon}(\lambda):=(I+G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)^{*}J_{\upsilon}G_{\upsilon}(\lambda))^{1/2}(H_{0}+\lambda)^{1/2},\quad{\mathfrak{D}}(M_{\upsilon}(\lambda))={\rm H}^{1}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),

is closed in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
, as it is a product of a bijective operator in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
(H_{0}+\lambda)^{1/2}
, which is bijective from
{\rm H}^{1}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. The representation in (
2.10
) can be seen with the help of the quadratic form in (
2.1
) associated with
H_{\upsilon}
and a similar calculation as in (
2.7
).
Therefore, the operators
H_{\upsilon}+\lambda
and, hence,
H_{\upsilon}
are self-adjoint on

{\mathfrak{D}}(H_{\upsilon}):=\left\{u\in{\rm H}^{1}_{A}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\,|\,M_{\upsilon}(\lambda)u\in{\mathfrak{D}}(M_{\upsilon}(\lambda)^{*})\right\}

(see
[
40
, Theorem X.25]
). In the above construction we have obtained an alternative characterisation of the operator domain of
H_{\upsilon}
; cf. (
2.2
). Moreover,

\begin{split}(H_{\upsilon}&+\lambda)^{-1}-(H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1}\\
&=(H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1/2}\big(I+G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)^{*}J_{\upsilon}G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)\big)^{-1}(H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1/2}-(H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1}\\
&=-(H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1/2}G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)^{*}J_{\upsilon}G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)(I+G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)^{*}J_{\upsilon}G_{\upsilon}(\lambda))^{-1}(H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1/2}.\end{split}

Bearing in mind the compactness of the operator
G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)^{*}J_{\upsilon}G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)
, and applying a suitable version of the Weyl theorem on the invariance of the essential spectrum
(see, e.g.,
[
9
, Chapter 9, Section 1, Theorem 4]
), we obtain (
2.4
).

Consider the operator

(2.11)

T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma}):=(\tau p_{q})^{*}\upsilon(\tau p_{q}),\quad q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+},

which can be viewed as a singular analogue of a Berezin-Toeplitz operator. The relation of Landau Hamiltonians coupled with regular potentials
V
and the Berezin-Toeplitz type operators
\hat{T}_{q}(V)=p_{q}Vp_{q}
was discovered in
[
38
]
and further studied in many publications. Singular Toeplitz operators as in (
2.11
) play an important role in modern operator theory and are also of independent interest. They were already considered in
[
2
]
, and in connection with magnetic Laplacians with different types of boundary conditions these types of operators appear in

[
20
,
21
,
37
]
; we also refer the reader
to
[
3
,
10
,
11
,
13
,
16
,
32
,
36
,
41
,
42
]
for some other
recent related works in this context.
Note that the operator
T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma})
corresponds to the quadratic form

(2.12)

t_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma})[u]:=\int_{\Gamma}\upsilon(x)|u(x)|^{2}ds,\quad u\in p_{q}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).

Lemma 2.2
.

The operator
T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma})
,
q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
, is a compact self-adjoint operator in
p_{q}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.
For
\lambda>-b
and
G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)
in (
2.3
) one has

(2.13)

T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma})=(\Lambda_{q}+\lambda)p_{q}G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)^{*}J_{\upsilon}G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)p_{q}.

Moreover, if
\upsilon
has a constant sign, then there exists
\lambda_{0}>-b
such that for all
\lambda>\lambda_{0}

(2.14)

{\rm ker}\,(p_{q}Q_{\upsilon}(\lambda)p_{q})={\rm ker}\,(T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma})),

where
Q_{\upsilon}(\lambda):=(H_{\upsilon}+\lambda)^{-1}-(H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1}
.

Proof.

Recall that
J_{\upsilon}
denotes the sign of
\upsilon
. A simple calculation involving the form
t_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma})
shows for any
u\in p_{q}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
\lambda>-b
that

\begin{split}t_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma})[u]&=\int_{\Gamma}\upsilon(x)|u(x)|^{2}ds\\
&=(\Lambda_{q}+\lambda)\int_{\Gamma}\upsilon(x)|(\tau(H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1/2}u)(x)|^{2}ds\\
&=(\Lambda_{q}+\lambda)\big\langle J_{\upsilon}G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)p_{q}u,G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)p_{q}u\big\rangle_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}.\end{split}

Thus, we get the representation (
2.13
), which also shows with Lemma
2.1
that
T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma})
is compact and self-adjoint. Moreover, (
2.13
) immediately implies

(2.15)

{\rm ker}\,(T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma}))=\ker(p_{q}G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)^{*}J_{\upsilon}G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)p_{q}).

It remains to prove equation (
2.14
). For this, assume that
\upsilon
has a constant sign, i.e.
\pm\upsilon\geq 0
, and fix
\lambda>-b
sufficiently large such that
H_{\upsilon}+\lambda
is strictly positive.
Then the equivalence of (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) shows that
I+G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)^{*}J_{\upsilon}G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)
is strictly positive and bounded. Thus, we get for
K_{\upsilon}(\lambda):=G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)^{*}G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)\geq 0
by Lemma
2.1
that

\begin{split}\big\langle p_{q}&Q_{\upsilon}(\lambda)p_{q}u,u\big\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=-J_{\upsilon}(\Lambda_{q}+\lambda)^{-1}\big\langle K_{\upsilon}(\lambda)(I+J_{\upsilon}K_{\upsilon}(\lambda))^{-1}p_{q}u,p_{q}u\big\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\\
&=-J_{\upsilon}(\Lambda_{q}+\lambda)^{-1}\big\langle(I+J_{\upsilon}K_{\upsilon}(\lambda))^{-1}(K_{\upsilon}(\lambda))^{1/2}p_{q}u,(K_{\upsilon}(\lambda))^{1/2}p_{q}u\big\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.\end{split}

Taking the bijectivity of
I+G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)^{*}J_{\upsilon}G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)
and (
2.15
) into account, this leads to (
2.14
).
∎

Via the form
t_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma})
one gets also another interesting characterization of
{\rm ker}\,(T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma}))
in the case that
\upsilon\geq 0
almost everywhere on
\Gamma
, as then it follows from (
2.12
) that
T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma})
is a non-negative operator and that
{\rm ker}\,(T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma}))\neq\{0\}
if and only if there exists a
u\in p_{q}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that
\upsilon u=0
on
\Gamma
. This yields

(2.16)

{\rm ker}\,(T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma}))=\{u\in p_{q}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}):u=0\text{ on }\rm{supp}\,\upsilon\},

where
\rm{supp}\,\upsilon
denotes the essential support of
\upsilon
. In other words, this means that
{\rm ker}\;(T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma}))\neq\{0\}
if and only if the essential support of
\upsilon
is contained in a nodal set of an eigenfunction of
H_{0}
for
\Lambda_{q}
. Furthermore, the dimension of
{\rm ker}\,(T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma}))
is equal to the number of linearly independent eigenfunctions
u
of
H_{0}
for
\Lambda_{q}
such that
u=0
on
\rm{supp}\,\upsilon
. For studies on nodal sets of eigenfunctions we refer the reader to, e.g.,
[
24
,
25
,
26
,
34
]
. Clearly, a similar consideration is true if
\upsilon\leq 0
almost everywhere on
\Gamma
.

3.
Main results

In this section we formulate our main results on the fate of Landau levels under
\delta
-perturbations supported on curves.
The case of general
C^{1,1}
-smooth Jordan curves is treated first and, roughly speaking, we show that the analysis of the eigenspaces

{\rm ker}\,(H_{\pm\upsilon}-\Lambda_{q})
of the perturbed Landau Hamiltonian can be reduced to the analysis of the kernels
{\rm ker}\,(T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma}))
of the Berezin-Toeplitz type operators defined in (
2.11
).
This connection is of independent interest, but also turns out to be useful for a more explicit analysis of the Landau levels. We illustrate this
for the special case of
\delta
-perturbations supported on circles.

3.1.
Singular interactions supported on
C^{1,1}
-smooth Jordan curves

Throughout this subsection let
\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
be a
C^{1,1}
-smooth Jordan curve
and assume that
\upsilon\in L^{p}(\Gamma;{\mathbb{R}})
for some
p>1
is such that
\upsilon\geq 0
on
\Gamma
and
\upsilon\not\equiv 0
.
Our first theorem contains two independent statements which concern the operators
H_{\upsilon}
and
H_{-\upsilon}
respectively.
The proof of Theorem
3.1
can be found in Subsection
5.1
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
and let
T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma})
be the operator of Berezin-Toeplitz type in
(
2.11
)
.

(i)

There holds

{\rm ker}\,(T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma}))\subset{\rm ker}\,(H_{\pm\upsilon}-\Lambda_{q}).

(ii)

There exist
n_{q}^{\pm}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
depending on
\upsilon
such that

(3.1)

{\rm dim\,ker}\,(H_{\pm\upsilon}-\Lambda_{q})\leq{\rm dim\,ker}\,(T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma}))+n_{q}^{\pm}

and for
q=0
one can choose
n_{0}^{+}=0
.

(iii)

There exist
\upsilon^{\pm}_{q}>0
such that
\|\upsilon\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}<\upsilon^{\pm}_{q}
implies

(3.2)

{\rm ker}\,(H_{\pm\upsilon}-\Lambda_{q})={\rm ker}\,(T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma}))

and for
q=0
one can choose
\upsilon_{0}^{+}=\infty
. Moreover, there is a constant
c>0
independent of
b
and
q
such that

(3.3)

\upsilon_{q}^{+}\geq\frac{2bc}{(\Lambda_{q}+1)(\Lambda_{q-1}+1)}\quad\text{and}\quad\upsilon_{q}^{-}\geq\frac{2bc}{2b+(\Lambda_{q}+1)(\Lambda_{q+1}+1)}.

Remark 3.2
.

Writing (
3.2
), we mean that
u\in{\rm ker}\,(H_{\pm\upsilon}-\Lambda_{q})
implies
u=p_{q}u
, and

u\in{\rm ker}\,(T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma}))\subset p_{q}L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}),

and vice versa.
A similar remark applies to all further inclusions of the same kind.
We also mention that the inequality (
3.1
) will be obtained by showing that
{\rm ker}\,(H_{\pm\upsilon}-\Lambda_{q})
is the sum of
{\rm ker}\,(T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma}))

and a finite dimensional space.

Remark 3.3
.

The definitions of the numbers
\upsilon^{\pm}_{q}
in Theorem
3.1
(iii) are given in (
5.36
) and (
5.45
), respectively. Their precise value is not obvious. However, equation (
3.2
) is also true, if one replaces
\upsilon_{q}^{\pm}
by the lower bounds in (
3.3
). Since
\Lambda_{q}=b(2q+1)
, we see that these lower bounds are decreasing in
q
; the same is true for
\upsilon^{\pm}_{q}
defined in (
5.36
) and (
5.45
).

Remark 3.4
.

The operator
H_{\upsilon}
can be introduced as a self-adjoint extension of the symmetric operator
S
given by

(3.4)

Su=(-i\nabla-A)^{2}u,\qquad{\mathfrak{D}}(S)=\big\{u\in{\rm H}^{2}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\,|\,u_{|\Gamma}=0\big\},

i.e.
S
is the restriction of
H_{0}
onto functions in
{\rm H}^{2}_{A}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
that vanish on
\Gamma
; cf.
[
7
]
for the case
\upsilon\in L^{\infty}(\Gamma;\mathbb{R})
. In view of (
2.16
), if
u\in{\rm ker}\,(T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma}))
for
\upsilon>0
or
\upsilon<0
, then
u\in{\rm ker}\,(S-\Lambda_{q})
and thus, as
H_{\upsilon}
was defined as an extension of
S
,
u\in{\rm ker}(H_{\upsilon}-\Lambda_{q})
. Thus, the result of Theorem
3.1
(i) can also be interpreted from an
extension theoretic point of view.

Remark 3.5
.

Considering (
3.1
) the question arises, if
{\rm dim\,ker}\,(T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma}))
is finite, as then
{\rm dim\,ker}\,(H_{\pm\upsilon}-\Lambda_{q})<\infty
. If
\upsilon
is strictly positive, i.e. if
\upsilon\geq c>0
everywhere on
\Gamma
, then by
[
7
, Lemma 3.7]
and (
2.16
) one indeed has
{\rm dim\,ker}\,(T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma}))<\infty
.

Theorem
3.1
reduces the analysis of
{\rm ker}\,(H_{\upsilon}-\Lambda_{q})
to that of
{\rm ker}\,(T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma}))
. That is why our further results concern
{\rm ker}\,(T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma}))
.
The situation is particularly simple for the Berezin-Toeplitz operator
T_{0}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma})
as the next theorem shows. Its proof can be found in Subsection
5.2
.

Theorem 3.6
.

For
q=0
we have

(3.5)

{\rm ker}\,(T_{0}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma}))=\{0\}.

Combining (
3.5
) and (
3.1
)–(
3.2
) with
q=0
we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 3.7
.

We have

{\rm ker}\,(H_{\upsilon}-\Lambda_{0})=\{0\}\quad\text{and}\quad{\rm dim\,ker}\,(H_{-\upsilon}-\Lambda_{0})<\infty.

Moreover, if
\|\upsilon\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}
is sufficiently small, then

{\rm ker}\,(H_{-\upsilon}-\Lambda_{0})=\{0\}.

The next remark concerns regular perturbations of the Landau Hamiltonian and the fate of the Landau levels as investigated earlier in
[
28
]
.

Remark 3.8
.

Assume that
V\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2};\mathbb{R})
satisfies
V\not\equiv 0
,
V\geq 0
, and
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}V(x)=0
.
Then, applying the general scheme of the proof of Theorem
3.1
below, one can verify that

(3.6)

{\rm dim\,ker}\,(H_{0}\pm V-\Lambda_{q})<\infty

and

(3.7)

{\rm ker}\,(H_{0}\pm V-\Lambda_{q})={\rm ker}\,(\hat{T}_{q}(V)),

if
\|V\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}<V_{q}^{\pm}
for some constants
V_{q}^{\pm}>0
; here

\hat{T}_{q}(V)=p_{q}Vp_{q},

and
p_{q}
is the orthogonal projection onto
{\rm ker}\,(H_{0}-\Lambda_{q})
.

The main idea of the proof of
[
28
, Theorem 1]
is to show that, under the assumptions
\|V\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}<2b
and
V\geq 0
, one has

(3.8)

{\rm ker}\,(H_{0}\pm V-\Lambda_{q})\subset{\rm ker}\,(\hat{T}_{q}(V)),\quad q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}.

On the other hand, if additionally
V\not\equiv 0
, then the results of
[
28
,
39
]
imply

(3.9)

{\rm ker}\,(\hat{T}_{q}(V))=\left\{0\right\},\quad q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+},

and (
1.2
) follows from (
3.8
) and (
3.9
).

If one compares (
3.6
)–(
3.7
) (following our approach) with (
3.8
)–(
3.9
) (following the approach in
[
28
]
), then the upper bounds
V_{q}^{\pm}
for
\|V\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}
in (
3.7
) are not as explicit as the upper bound
2b
in (
3.8
), but one gets in (
3.6
) also results for potentials with
\|V\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\geq 2b
.

3.2.
Singular interactions supported on circles

Now we illustrate the above results for the special case that
\Gamma={\mathcal{C}}_{r}
is a circle of radius
r\in(0,\infty)
. In this situation more explicit results on the structure
of
{\rm ker}\,(T_{q}(\delta_{{\mathcal{C}}_{r}}))
are obtained, as one can use an explicit basis of
{\rm ker}(H_{0}-\Lambda_{q})
in polar coordinates. Using this and the simple characterization of
{\rm ker}(T_{q}(\delta_{{\mathcal{C}}_{r}}))
from (
2.16
), it will turn out that
{\rm ker}(T_{q}(\delta_{{\mathcal{C}}_{r}}))\neq\{0\}
is equivalent to the fact
that
br^{2}/2
is a zero of a suitable Laguerre polynomial.
Since (
2.16
) implies
{\rm ker}\,(T_{q}(\delta_{{\mathcal{C}}_{r}}))={\rm ker}(T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\mathcal{C}_{r}}))
, if
\upsilon
is strictly positive or negative everywhere on
\Gamma
, this yields further results on
{\rm ker}\,(H_{\pm\upsilon}-\Lambda_{q})
.
The obtained result on
{\rm ker}\,(T_{q}(\delta_{{\mathcal{C}}_{r}}))
is the following:

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
q\in{\mathbb{N}}
, assume that
\Gamma={\mathcal{C}}_{r}
is a circle of radius
r\in(0,\infty)
,
and let
T_{q}(\delta_{{\mathcal{C}}_{r}})
be the corresponding operator of Berezin-Toeplitz type in
(
2.11
)
.

(i)

For any
r\in(0,\infty)
we have

(3.10)

{\rm dim\,ker}\,(T_{q}(\delta_{{\mathcal{C}}_{r}}))\leq q.

(ii)

The set

{\mathcal{D}}_{q}:=\left\{r\in(0,\infty)\,|\,{\rm dim\,ker}\,(T_{q}(\delta_{{\mathcal{C}}_{r}}))\geq 1\right\}

is infinite and discrete.

Theorem
3.9
will be proved in Subsection
5.3
. Since (
2.16
) implies the relation
{\rm ker}\,(T_{q}(\delta_{{\mathcal{C}}_{r}}))={\rm ker}(T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\mathcal{C}_{r}}))
, if
\upsilon
is strictly positive, a combination of (
3.1
) and (
3.10
) leads to the following corollary.

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
q\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
\Gamma={\mathcal{C}}_{r}
be a circle of radius
r\in(0,\infty)
, and assume that

\upsilon\in L^{p}({\mathcal{C}}_{r};{\mathbb{R}})
with
p>1
satisfies
\upsilon\geq c
on
{\mathcal{C}}_{r}
with some constant
c>0
. Then

{\rm dim\,ker}\,(H_{\pm\upsilon}-\Lambda_{q})<\infty.

Remark 3.11
.

For
q\in{\mathbb{N}}
set

{\mathcal{D}}_{q,j}:=\left\{r\in(0,\infty)\,|\,{\rm dim\,ker}\,(T_{q}(\delta_{{\mathcal{C}}_{r}}))=j\right\},\quad j=1,\ldots,q,

so that
{\mathcal{D}}_{q}=\cup_{j=1}^{q}{\mathcal{D}}_{q,j}
; note that the union stops at
j=q
by Theorem
3.9
(i).
In the proof of Theorem
3.9
, we will describe the dimension of
{\rm ker}\,(T_{q}(\delta_{{\mathcal{C}}_{r}}))
in terms of the zeros of Laguerre polynomials of
q
-th degree (see (
5.50
)–(
5.51
) below). If
q=1,2
, these zeros can be easily calculated, and we obtain explicitly the sets
{\mathcal{D}}_{q}
and their components
{\mathcal{D}}_{q,j}
, namely

(3.11)

{\mathcal{D}}_{1}={\mathcal{D}}_{1,1}=\ \sqrt{(2/b){\mathbb{N}}},

(3.12)

{\mathcal{D}}_{2}=\sqrt{(2/b)(({\mathbb{N}}+1)-\sqrt{({\mathbb{N}}+1)})}\cup\sqrt{(2/b)({\mathbb{N}}+\sqrt{{\mathbb{N}}})},

(3.13)

{\mathcal{D}}_{2,2}=\sqrt{(2/b)({\mathbb{N}}^{2}+{\mathbb{N}})},\quad{\mathcal{D}}_{2,1}={\mathcal{D}}_{2}\setminus{\mathcal{D}}_{2,2},

where
b>0
is the magnetic field.

Remark 3.12
.

Taking the explicit representation (
4.6
) of the orthonormal basis of
{\rm ran}\,(p_{q})={\rm ker}\,(H_{0}-\Lambda_{q})
,
q\in\mathbb{N}
,
into account, we conclude that
\Lambda_{q}
is still an eigenvalue of the symmetric operator
S
defined in (
3.4
)
provided that
br^{2}/2
is a root of a Laguerre polynomial of
q
-th degree. Since
H_{\upsilon}
is a self-adjoint extension of
S
for all
\upsilon\in L^{p}(\Gamma;\mathbb{R})
,
\Lambda_{q}
remains an eigenvalue of
H_{\upsilon}
under the same assumption on
r>0
.

4.
Auxiliary results from the spectral theory of
H_{0}

In this section we recall several known facts about
H_{0}
that are necessary for our considerations; the first part follows
[
37
, Section 4.2]
, while the second part on the basis of
{\rm ker}\,(H_{0}-\Lambda_{q})
can be found in
[
39
, Section 3.1]
, see also
[
23
]
. In the following, we describe a suitable spectral representation of
H_{0}
.
Set

\phi(x):=\frac{b|x|^{2}}{4},\quad x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}.

Introduce the magnetic creation operator

(4.1)

a^{*}=\Pi_{1}(A)-i\Pi_{2}(A)=-2ie^{\phi}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}e^{-\phi},\quad z=x_{1}+ix_{2},

and the magnetic annihilation operator

(4.2)

a=\Pi_{1}(A)+i\Pi_{2}(A)=-2ie^{-\phi}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{z}}e^{\phi},\quad\bar{z}=x_{1}-ix_{2}.

The operators
a
and
a^{*}
are closed on
{\mathfrak{D}}(a)={\mathfrak{D}}(a^{*})={\rm H}_{A}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
, and are mutually adjoint in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
. Moreover,

(4.3)

[a,a^{*}]=2b,

and

H_{0}=a^{*}a+b=aa^{*}-b.

Further,

(4.4)

{\rm ker}\,(H_{0}-\Lambda_{q})=(a^{*})^{q}\,{\rm ker}\,(a),\quad q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}.

By (
4.2
), we have

(4.5)

{\rm ker}\;(a)=\left\{u\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\,|\,u=e^{-\phi}\,g,\quad\frac{\partial g}{\partial\bar{z}}=0\right\}.

Thus,
e^{\phi}\,{\rm ker}\,(H_{0}-\Lambda_{0})=e^{\phi}\,{\rm ker}\,(a)
coincides with the
Fock-Segal-Bargmann space
of entire functions
g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2};e^{-2\phi}dx)

(see, e.g.,
[
23
, Section 3.2]
).
Assume now that

u\in{\rm ker}\,(H_{0}-\Lambda_{q}),\quad q\in{\mathbb{N}}.

By (
4.4
) and (
4.1
), there exists an entire function
g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2};e^{-2\phi}dx)
such that

\begin{split}(e^{\phi}u)(x)&=:f(x)=((e^{\phi}(a^{*})^{q}\,e^{-\phi})g)(x)=(-2i)^{q}\left(\left(e^{2\phi}\frac{\partial^{q}}{\partial z^{q}}\,e^{-2\phi}\right)g\right)(x)\\
&=(-2i)^{q}\sum_{\ell=0}^{q}\binom{q}{\ell}\left(-\frac{b\bar{z}}{2}\right)^{\ell}g^{(q-\ell)}(z),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2},\,\,z=x_{1}+ix_{2}.\end{split}

Evidently,
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2};e^{-2\phi}dx)
is a polyanalytic function of order
q+1
, i.e.
f
is a solution of the equation

\frac{\partial^{q+1}f}{\partial\bar{z}^{q+1}}(z)=0,\quad z\in{\mathbb{C}},

(see
[
1
,
5
,
6
]
and also
[
42
, Section 2.2]
).
We have

\left\{h\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2};e^{-2\phi}dx)\,\big|\,\frac{\partial^{q+1}h}{\partial\bar{z}^{q+1}}=0\right\}=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{q}e^{\phi}\,{\rm ker}\,(H_{0}-\Lambda_{j})

and the spaces
e^{\phi}\,{\rm ker}\,(H_{0}-\Lambda_{j})
,
j=0,\ldots,q
, are called sometimes
true poly-Fock spaces
of order
j
(see
[
1
,
44
]
).

Next, we introduce an explicit orthonormal basis of every
{\rm ker}\,(H_{0}-\Lambda_{q})
,
q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
, called sometimes the
angular-momentum basis
.
Let at first
q=0
. Then the functions

\widetilde{\varphi}_{k,0}(x)=z^{k}e^{-\phi(x)},\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2},\quad z=x_{1}+ix_{2},\quad k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+},

form an orthogonal basis of
{\rm ker}\;(a)={\rm ran}\,(p_{0})
(see, e.g.,
[
23
, Sections 3.1–3.2]
). Normalizing, we obtain an orthonormal basis of
{\rm ran}\,(p_{0})
, consisting of the functions

{\varphi}_{k,0}(x):=\frac{\widetilde{\varphi}_{k,0}(x)}{\|\widetilde{\varphi}_{k,0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}}=\sqrt{\frac{b}{2\pi}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{k!}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{b}{2}}\,z\right)^{k}\,e^{-\phi(x)},\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2},\quad k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}.

Let now
q\geq 1
. Set

\widetilde{{\varphi}}_{k,q}=(a^{*})^{q}\,\varphi_{k,0},\quad k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}.

The commutation relation (
4.3
) easily implies

\langle\widetilde{{\varphi}}_{k,q},\widetilde{{\varphi}}_{\ell,q}\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}=(2b)^{q}q!\delta_{k\ell},\quad k,\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}.

Therefore, the functions

{\varphi}_{k,q}:=\frac{\widetilde{\varphi}_{k,q}}{\|\widetilde{\varphi}_{k,q}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}}=\frac{\widetilde{\varphi}_{k,q}}{\sqrt{(2b)^{q}q!}},\quad k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+},

form an orthonormal basis of
{\rm ran}\,(p_{q})
,
q\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
They admit a more explicit representation
(see
[
39
, Subsection 3.1]
), namely

(4.6)

\begin{split}\varphi_{k,q}(x)=\frac{1}{i^{q}}\sqrt{\frac{b}{2\pi}}\sqrt{\frac{q!}{k!}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{b}{2}}\,z\right)^{k-q}&\,{\rm L}_{q}^{(k-q)}\left(\frac{b|x|^{2}}{2}\right)e^{-\phi(x)},\\
&x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2},\quad z=x_{1}+ix_{2},\quad k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+},\end{split}

where

{\rm L}_{q}^{(\alpha)}(t):=\frac{t^{-\alpha}e^{t}}{q!}\frac{d^{q}}{dt^{q}}\left(t^{q+\alpha}e^{-t}\right),\quad t>0,\quad\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}},\quad q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+},

are the (generalized) Laguerre polynomials (see
[
22
, Eq. 8.970 (1)]
). In particular,

(4.7)

{\rm L}_{1}^{(\alpha)}(t)=-t+\alpha+1,

(4.8)

{\rm L}_{2}^{(\alpha)}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\left(t^{2}-2(\alpha+2)t+(\alpha+2)(\alpha+1)\right).

The Laguerre polynomials
{\rm L}_{q}^{(\alpha)}
with
q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
and
\alpha>-1
satisfy

(4.9)

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}t^{\alpha}{\rm L}_{q}^{(\alpha)}(t){\rm L}_{p}^{(\alpha)}(t)dt=\Gamma(\alpha+1)\binom{q+\alpha}{q}\delta_{qp},\quad q,p\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+},

(see
[
43
, Eq. (5.1.1)]
).

Finally, for
y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, introduce the
magnetic translations

(4.10)

({\mathcal{T}}_{y}u)(x):=e^{-i\frac{b}{2}(x\wedge y)}\,u(x-y),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2},

where

x\wedge y:=x_{1}y_{2}-x_{2}y_{1}.

Evidently, for each
y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, the operator
{\mathcal{T}}_{y}
is
unitary in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
. A direct calculation yields

{\mathcal{T}}_{y}^{*}\,\Pi_{j}(A)\,{\mathcal{T}}_{y}=\Pi_{j}(A),\quad j=1,2,

and, therefore,

{\mathcal{T}}_{y}^{*}\,H_{0}\,{\mathcal{T}}_{y}=H_{0},\quad y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}.

Then the spectral theorem implies

(4.11)

{\mathcal{T}}_{y}^{*}\,p_{q}\,{\mathcal{T}}_{y}=p_{q},\quad y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2},\quad q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}.

5.
Proofs of the main results

5.1.
Proof of Theorem
3.1

We start with the proof of item (i). Denote by
\Omega_{\rm in}
and
\Omega_{\rm ex}
the interior and the exterior of
\Gamma
respectively, and by
\nu
the unit normal vector on
\Gamma
pointing
outwards of
\Omega_{\rm in}
.
For
w\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
set

w_{\natural}:=w_{|\Omega_{\natural}},\quad\natural={\rm in},{\rm ex}.

In view of (
2.2
) we have that
w\in{\mathfrak{D}}(H_{\pm\upsilon})
is equivalent to the following conditions:

(a)
w\in{\rm H}_{A}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
;

(b)
(-i\nabla-A)^{2}w_{\natural}\in L^{2}(\Omega_{\natural})
,
\natural={\rm in},{\rm ex}
;

(c)

\left(\frac{\partial w_{\rm ex}}{\partial\nu}-\frac{\partial w_{\rm in}}{\partial\nu}\mp\upsilon w\right)_{\Gamma}=0
.

Moreover, if
w\in{\mathfrak{D}}(H_{\pm\upsilon})
, then

(5.1)

(H_{\pm\upsilon}w)_{\natural}=(-i\nabla-A)^{2}w_{\natural},\quad\quad\natural={\rm in},{\rm ex}.

Assume
u\in{\rm ker}\,(T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma}))
,
q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
. Since
\upsilon\geq 0
, by (
2.16
) this is equivalent to

(5.2)

u\in{\rm ker}\,(H_{0}-\Lambda_{q})\subset{\mathfrak{D}}(H_{0})={\rm H}_{A}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})

and

(5.3)

\upsilon u=0\quad\mbox{on}\quad\Gamma.

By
u\in{\rm H}_{A}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
conditions (a)–(b) are fulfilled and, moreover,

(5.4)

\frac{\partial u_{\rm ex}}{\partial\nu}=\frac{\partial u_{\rm in}}{\partial\nu}\quad\mbox{on}\quad\Gamma.

Combining (
5.3
) with (
5.4
) we find that also (c) also holds, i.e.

u\in{\mathfrak{D}}(H_{\pm\upsilon})
.
By (
5.2
) we have

H_{0}u=(-i\nabla-A)^{2}u=\Lambda_{q}u\quad\mbox{in}\quad{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

and, hence,

(5.5)

(-i\nabla-A)^{2}u_{\natural}=\Lambda_{q}u_{\natural}\quad\mbox{in}\quad\Omega_{\natural},\quad\natural={\rm in},{\rm ex}.

Bearing in mind (
5.1
), we now find that (
5.5
) implies

H_{\pm\upsilon}u=\Lambda_{q}u
,
i.e.

u\in{\rm ker}\,(H_{\pm\upsilon}-\Lambda_{q}).

The remaining items (ii) and (iii) will be proved together. To make the proof accessible in an easier way, we have split it into several steps. First, the case
\upsilon\geq 0
is treated. In
Step 1
the claims for the first Landau level
\Lambda_{0}
are shown. In
Step 2–5
the claims for
\Lambda_{q}
,
q\in\mathbb{N}
, are verified. More precisely, in
Step 2
the eigenvalue equation is reduced to equations for operators which are easier accessible for our purposes. Then, in
Step 3
a representation of
{\rm ker}\,(T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma}))
involving these new operators is proved. In
Step 4
we are putting all this together to verify assertion (ii) for
\upsilon\geq 0
, while in
Step 5
the proof of item (iii) in this case is concluded. Finally, in
Step 6
the case
\upsilon\leq 0
is treated.

First let us introduce the notations which will be used throughout the proof. Assume, as usual, that
\upsilon\in L^{p}(\Gamma;{\mathbb{R}})
with
p>1
,
\upsilon\geq 0
,
\upsilon\neq 0
, and (
2.9
) holds true. Set

Q^{+}_{\upsilon}(\lambda):=(H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1}-(H_{\upsilon}+\lambda)^{-1},\quad Q^{-}_{\upsilon}(\lambda):=-(H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1}+(H_{-\upsilon}+\lambda)^{-1}.

By Lemma
2.1
, we have
Q^{\pm}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)\geq 0
, and the operators
Q^{\pm}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)
are compact in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
. Note that Lemma
2.1
also implies

(5.6)

Q^{\pm}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)=(H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1/2}G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)^{*}(I\pm G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)^{*})^{-1}G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)(H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1/2}.

Further, put

P_{q}^{+}:=\sum_{j=q}^{\infty}p_{j}\quad\text{and}\quad P_{q}^{-}:=I-P_{q}^{+},

so that
P_{0}^{+}=I
, and
P_{0}^{-}=0
. For
q\geq 1
the projections
P_{q}^{\pm}
have infinite rank. Finally, set

\mu_{q}(\lambda):=\left(\Lambda_{q}+\lambda\right)^{-1},\quad q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+},\quad\lambda>-b.

Step 1:
We first prove the part of Theorem
3.1
(ii) and (iii) concerning positive perturbations
H_{\upsilon}
and start with the case
q=0
. Assume that

u\in{\rm ker}(H_{\upsilon}-\Lambda_{0}).

Then
u
satisfies

(H_{\upsilon}+\lambda)^{-1}u=\mu_{0}(\lambda)u

or, equivalently,

(5.7)

\left((H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1}-\mu_{0}(\lambda)\right)u-Q^{+}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)u=0,\quad\lambda>-b.

Thus,

(5.8)

\big\langle\left((H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1}-\mu_{0}(\lambda)\right)u,u\big\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}-\big\langle Q^{+}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)u,u\big\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}=0.

Both terms on the left-hand side of (
5.8
) are non-positive, and hence they both should vanish. Since
(H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1}-\mu_{0}(\lambda)
is non-positive, the equality

\big\langle\left((H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1}-\mu_{0}(\lambda)\right)u,u\big\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}=0

and the min-max principle imply
u=p_{0}u
. Then,

\big\langle Q^{+}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)u,u\big\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}=\big\langle p_{0}Q^{+}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)p_{0}u,u\big\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}=0,

where the operator
p_{0}Q^{+}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)p_{0}
is self-adjoint and non-negative in the space
p_{0}L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})={\rm ran}\,(p_{0})
. Hence, by Lemma
2.2

(5.9)

u\in{\rm ker}\,(p_{0}Q^{+}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)p_{0})={\rm ker}\,(T_{0}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma})).

Thus, we obtain the inclusion
\subset
in (
3.2
) for
H_{\upsilon}
and
q=0
. The remaining inclusion

\supset
in (
3.2
) is clear by (i). Therefore, item (iii) is shown in the case of positive perturbations and
q=0
, which implies also assertion (ii) in the same case.

Step 2:
Assume now

u\in{\rm ker}\,(H_{\upsilon}-\Lambda_{q}),\quad q\in{\mathbb{N}}.

In this step we reduce the eigenvalue equation for
u
to equations for operators which are easier accessible for our purposes.
Similarly to (
5.7
) we have

(5.10)

\left((H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1}-\mu_{q}(\lambda)\right)u-Q^{+}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)u=0,\quad\lambda>-b.

Set

(5.11)

u^{+}:=P_{q}^{+}u\quad\text{and}\quad u^{-}:=P_{q}^{-}u,

so that

(5.12)

u=u^{+}+u^{-}.

Since
P_{q}^{\pm}
are functions of
H_{0}
, they commute with
(H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1}
and thus, their application to (
5.10
) implies

(5.13)

\left((H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1}-\mu_{q}(\lambda)\right)u^{+}=P_{q}^{+}Q^{+}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)u^{+}+P_{q}^{+}Q^{+}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)u^{-},

(5.14)

\left((H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1}-\mu_{q}(\lambda)\right)u^{-}=P_{q}^{-}Q^{+}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)u^{+}+P_{q}^{-}Q^{+}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)u^{-}.

Let

(5.15)

S_{q}^{+}(\lambda):=P_{q}^{-}Q^{+}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)P_{q}^{-}

and observe that by Lemma
2.1
the operator
S_{q}^{+}(\lambda)
is compact, self-adjoint, and non-negative in
P_{q}^{-}L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
. Set

(5.16)

m_{q}^{+}(\lambda):=\inf\sigma\left(\left((H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1}-\mu_{q}(\lambda)\right)_{|P_{q}^{-}L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\right)=\frac{2b}{(\Lambda_{q}+\lambda)(\Lambda_{q-1}+\lambda)},

and

S_{q,>}^{+}(\lambda):=S_{q}^{+}(\lambda)\,\mathds{1}_{[m_{q}^{+}(\lambda),\infty)}\left(S_{q}^{+}(\lambda)\right),\quad S_{q,<}^{+}(\lambda):=S_{q}^{+}(\lambda)-S_{q,>}^{+}(\lambda);

here and in the sequel
\mathds{1}_{{\mathcal{J}}}(T)
denotes the spectral projection of the operator
T=T^{*}
associated with the Borel set
{\mathcal{J}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
. Note that

{\rm rank}\,(S_{q,>}^{+}(\lambda))<\infty.

Moreover, if

\|S_{q}^{+}(\lambda)\|<m_{q}^{+}(\lambda),

then
S_{q,>}^{+}(\lambda)=0
.
Now, (
5.14
) is equivalent to

(5.17)

\left((H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1}-\mu_{q}(\lambda)-S_{q,<}^{+}(\lambda)\right)u^{-}=P_{q}^{-}Q^{+}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)u^{+}+S_{q,>}^{+}(\lambda)u_{>}^{-},

where

(5.18)

u_{>}^{-}:=P_{q,>}^{-}u

and

(5.19)

P_{q,>}^{-}=P_{q,>}^{-}(\lambda):=\mathds{1}_{[m_{q}^{+}(\lambda),\infty)}(S_{q}^{+}(\lambda))\,P_{q}^{-}.\\

Note that

(5.20)

{\rm rank}\,(P_{q,>}^{-}(\lambda))={\rm rank}\,(S_{q,>}^{+}(\lambda))<\infty.

Since
S_{q}^{+}(\lambda)
is compact and
m_{q}^{+}(\lambda)>0
, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

\sigma(S_{q}^{+}(\lambda))\cap(m_{q}^{+}(\lambda)-\varepsilon,m_{q}^{+}(\lambda))=\emptyset

and hence
\|S_{q,<}^{+}(\lambda)\|\leq m_{q}^{+}(\lambda)-\varepsilon<m_{q}^{+}(\lambda)
. By definition of
m_{q}^{+}(\lambda)
, we have

\big((H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1}-\mu_{q}(\lambda)\big)_{|P_{q}^{-}L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\geq m_{q}^{+}(\lambda)

and thus, the operator

\left((H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1}-\mu_{q}(\lambda)\right)_{|P_{q}^{-}L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}-S_{q,<}^{+}(\lambda)\\

is positive and boundedly invertible on
P_{q}^{-}L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
. Set

R_{q}^{+}(\lambda):=\left(\left((H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1}-\mu_{q}(\lambda)\right)_{|P_{q}^{-}L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}-S_{q,<}^{+}(\lambda)\right)^{-1}.

Then (
5.17
) implies

(5.21)

u^{-}=R_{q}^{+}(\lambda)P_{q}^{-}Q^{+}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)u^{+}+R_{q}^{+}(\lambda)S_{q,>}^{+}(\lambda)u_{>}^{-}.

Inserting (
5.21
) into (
5.13
) we get

(5.22)

P_{q}^{+}X_{q}^{+}(\lambda)P_{q}^{+}u^{+}=-P_{q}^{+}Y_{q}^{+}(\lambda)u_{>}^{-},

where

X_{q}^{+}(\lambda):=-(H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1}+\mu_{q}(\lambda)+Q^{+}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)+Q^{+}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)R_{q}^{+}(\lambda)P_{q}^{-}Q^{+}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)

and

Y_{q}^{+}(\lambda):=Q^{+}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)R_{q}^{+}(\lambda)\,S_{q,>}^{+}(\lambda).

Step 3:

Define the operator

(5.23)

K_{q}^{+}:=P_{q}^{+}X_{q}^{+}(\lambda)P_{q}^{+},

which is self-adjoint and non-negative in
P_{q}^{+}L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
. In this step we show

(5.24)

{\rm ker}\,(K_{q}^{+})={\rm ker}\,(T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma})).

In order to check (
5.24
), assume first that
w\in{\rm ker}\,(T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma}))
. Then
w=p_{q}w
, see Remark
3.2
, and

(5.25)

\begin{split}\langle K_{q}^{+}&w,w\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}=\big\langle(-(H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1}+\mu_{q}(\lambda))p_{q}w,p_{q}w\big\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\\
&+\big\langle Q^{+}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)p_{q}w,p_{q}w\big\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}+\big\langle Q^{+}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)R_{q}^{+}(\lambda)P_{q}^{-}Q^{+}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)p_{q}w,p_{q}w\big\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}.\end{split}

Using Lemma
2.2
and
{\rm ker}\,(A^{*}A)={\rm ker}\,(A)
, one has

(5.26)

{\rm ker}\,(T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma}))={\rm ker}\,(p_{q}Q^{+}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)p_{q})={\rm ker}\,(Q^{+}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)^{1/2}p_{q}).

Moreover, the definitions of
\mu_{q}(\lambda)
and
p_{q}
yield

(5.27)

\quad\big((H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1}-\mu_{q}(\lambda)\big)p_{q}=0.

Thus, (
5.25
)–(
5.27
) imply

\langle K_{q}^{+}w,w\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}=0.

Since
K_{q}^{+}\geq 0
we conclude that
w\in{\rm ker}\,(K_{q}^{+})
, i.e.

(5.28)

{\rm ker}\,(T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma}))\subset{\rm ker}\,(K_{q}^{+}).

Let now
w\in{\rm ker}\,(K_{q}^{+})
. Then

\big\langle(-(H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1}+\mu_{q}(\lambda))P_{q}^{+}w,P_{q}^{+}w\big\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}+\big\langle Q^{+}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)P_{q}^{+}w,P_{q}^{+}w\big\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}

(5.29)

+\big\langle Q^{+}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)R_{q}^{+}(\lambda)P_{q}^{-}Q^{+}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)P_{q}^{+}w,P_{q}^{+}w\big\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}=0.

The three terms on the left-hand side of (
5.29
) are non-negative and therefore all of them vanish. The equality

\big\langle((H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1}-\mu_{q}(\lambda))P_{q}^{+}w,P_{q}^{+}w\big\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}=0

implies that
P_{q}^{+}w=p_{q}w
. Inserting this into

\langle Q^{+}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)P_{q}^{+}w,P_{q}^{+}w\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}=0

we obtain

\langle Q^{+}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)p_{q}w,p_{q}w\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}=0,

i.e. with (
5.26
)

w\in{\rm ker}\,(p_{q}Q^{+}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)p_{q})={\rm ker}\,(T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma})).

Therefore,

{\rm ker}\,(K_{q}^{+})\subset{\rm ker}\,(T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma})),

which combined with (
5.28
) yields (
5.24
).

Step 4:
Now we have everything in hands to finish the proof of assertion (ii) for
\upsilon\geq 0
. For this purpose we show that the element
u\in{\rm ker}\,(H_{\upsilon}-\Lambda_{q})
can be written as
u=u_{0}^{+}+W_{q}^{+}u_{>}^{-}
, where
u_{0}^{+}\in{\rm ker}\,(T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma}))
,
u_{>}^{-}\in{\rm ran}\,(P_{q,>}^{-}(\lambda))
, and
W_{q}^{+}
is a suitable operator; this implies (
3.1
) as
{\rm rank}\,(P_{q,>}^{-}(\lambda))<\infty
by (
5.20
).
Let
\pi_{0}^{+}
be the orthogonal projection onto
{\rm ker}\,(K_{q}^{+})
and
\pi_{\perp}^{+}:=P_{q}^{+}-\pi_{0}^{+}
. Set

u_{0}^{+}:=\pi_{0}^{+}u^{+}\quad\text{and}\quad u_{\perp}^{+}:=\pi_{\perp}^{+}u^{+},

where
u^{+}
is the function defined in (
5.11
).
Thus,

(5.30)

u^{+}=u_{0}^{+}+u_{\perp}^{+}.

Denote by
K_{q,\perp}^{+}
the operator
\pi_{\perp}^{+}K_{q}^{+}\pi_{\perp}^{+}
, which is self-adjoint and positive on
{\rm ran}\,(\pi_{\perp}^{+})
. Then, as
u_{0}^{+}\in{\rm ker}\,(K_{q}^{+})
, (
5.22
) and (
5.23
) imply

(5.31)

K_{q,\perp}^{+}u_{\perp}^{+}=-P_{q}^{+}Y_{q}^{+}(\lambda)u^{-}_{>}.

Since we started with an arbitrary
u\in{\rm ker}\,(H_{\upsilon}-\Lambda_{q})
we have by (
5.18
)

u_{>}^{-}\in P_{q,>}^{-}{\rm ker}\,(H_{\upsilon}-\Lambda_{q}).

Then (
5.31
) implies

(5.32)

P_{q}^{+}Y_{q}^{+}(\lambda)P_{q,>}^{-}{\rm ker}\,(H_{\upsilon}-\Lambda_{q})\subset{\rm ran}\,(K_{q,\perp}^{+}).

Recall that
K_{q,\perp}^{+}
is positive and hence invertible. Therefore, we can define on
P_{q,>}^{-}{\rm ker}\,(H_{\upsilon}-\Lambda_{q})
the operator

L_{q}^{+}:=-(K_{q,\perp}^{+})^{-1}P_{q}^{+}Y_{q}^{+}(\lambda).

By (
5.31
) we have

(5.33)

u_{\perp}^{+}=L_{q}^{+}u_{>}^{-}.

Putting together (
5.12
), (
5.21
), (
5.30
), and (
5.33
), we find that for any
u\in{\rm ker}\,(H_{\upsilon}-\Lambda_{q})
we have

(5.34)

u=u_{0}^{+}+W_{q}^{+}u_{>}^{-},

where

u_{0}^{+}\in{\rm ker}\,(K_{q}^{+})={\rm ker}\,(T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma})),\quad u_{>}^{-}\in P_{q,>}^{-}{\rm ker}\,(H_{\upsilon}-\Lambda_{q}),

and

W_{q}^{+}=R_{q}^{+}(\lambda)S_{q,>}^{+}(\lambda)+(I+R_{q}^{+}(\lambda)P_{q}^{-}Q^{+}_{\upsilon}(\lambda))L_{q}^{+}.

Deriving (
5.34
) we have also taken into account that

R_{q}^{+}(\lambda)P_{q}^{-}Q^{+}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)u_{0}^{+}=0

due to
u_{0}^{+}\in{\rm ker}\,(K_{q}^{+})
, (
5.24
), and (
5.26
).
Now, (
5.34
) and (
5.32
) entail (
3.1
) for the case of
H_{\upsilon}
with

n_{q}^{+}:=\inf_{\lambda\in(-b,\infty)}{\rm rank}\,(P_{q,>}^{-}(\lambda)),\quad q\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Step 5:
Let us prove item (iii) for
H_{\upsilon}
and
q\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Note that we have already shown assertion (i) and hence
it suffices to verify the inclusion
\subset
in (
3.2
). Since
\|(I+G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)^{*}G_{\upsilon}(\lambda))^{-1}\|\leq 1
we get by (
5.15
) and (
2.6
),

(5.35)

\|S_{q}^{+}(\lambda)\|\leq\|G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)\|^{2}\leq\|\upsilon\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}C_{p}(\lambda),\quad\lambda>-b+1.

Let

(5.36)

\|\upsilon\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}<\upsilon^{+}_{q}:=\sup_{\lambda\in(-b+1,\infty)}\frac{m_{q}^{+}(\lambda)}{C_{p}(\lambda)},

where
m_{q}^{+}(\lambda)
is the quantity defined in (
5.16
).
Let
R>0
be such that
\Gamma
is contained in the open ball
B_{R}
with radius
R
.
Observe that by the diamagnetic inequality

(5.37)

\displaystyle C_{p}(1)

\displaystyle=\sup_{0\neq u\in{\rm H}^{1}_{A}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\frac{\left(\int_{\Gamma}|\tau u|^{2p^{\prime}}ds\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}}{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\big(|\Pi(A)u|^{2}+|u|^{2}\big)dx}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{0\neq u\in{\rm H}^{1}_{A}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\frac{\left(\int_{\Gamma}|\tau u|^{2p^{\prime}}ds\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}}{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\big(\big|\nabla|u|\big|^{2}+|u|^{2}\big)dx}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{0\neq u\in{\rm H}^{1}_{A}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\frac{\left(\int_{\Gamma}|\tau u|^{2p^{\prime}}ds\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}}{\int_{B_{R}}\big(\big|\nabla|u|\big|^{2}+|u|^{2}\big)dx}

\displaystyle=\sup_{0\neq u\in{\rm H}^{1}(B_{R})}\frac{\left(\int_{\Gamma}|\tau u|^{2p^{\prime}}ds\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}}{\int_{B_{R}}\big(\big|\nabla|u|\big|^{2}+|u|^{2}\big)dx}=:c^{-1},\qquad p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1},

where the constant
c>0
does not depend on the magnetic field.
Hence, the constant
\upsilon^{+}_{q}
can be estimated from below as

\upsilon^{+}_{q}\geq\frac{m_{q}^{+}(1)}{C_{p}(1)}\geq\frac{2bc}{(\Lambda_{q}+1)(\Lambda_{q-1}+1)},

which is the bound in (
3.3
).
Furthermore, (
5.35
) implies that there exist
\lambda>-b+1
such that

\|S_{q}^{+}(\lambda)\|<m_{q}^{+}(\lambda),

so that
P_{q,>}^{-}(\lambda)=0
by (
5.19
). By (
5.34
), we conclude that if
u\in{\rm ker}\,(H_{\upsilon}-\Lambda_{q})
, then
u\in{\rm ker}\,(T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma}))
, as
u_{>}^{-}\in{\rm ran}\,(P_{q,>}^{-}(\lambda))=\{0\}
.
Therefore, together with (i) we conclude that (
3.2
) holds.

Step 6:

Let us now consider
H_{-\upsilon}
, i.e. the Landau Hamiltonian perturbed by a negative
\delta
-potential. The proof of Theorem
3.1
(ii) and (iii) in this case is quite similar to the one concerning
H_{\upsilon}
, so that we omit certain details. Assume

u\in{\rm ker}\,(H_{-\upsilon}-\Lambda_{q}),\quad q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}.

Then, similarly to (
5.10
), we have

(5.38)

\left((H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1}-\mu_{q}(\lambda)\right)u+Q^{-}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)u=0,\quad\lambda>-\inf\sigma(H_{-\upsilon}).

Put

u^{+}:=P_{q+1}^{+}u\quad\text{and}\quad\quad u^{-}:=P_{q+1}^{-}u,

so that again

u=u^{+}+u^{-}.

Then (
5.38
) implies

(5.39)

\left(\mu_{q}(\lambda)-(H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1}\right)u^{-}=P_{q+1}^{-}Q^{-}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)u^{-}+P_{q+1}^{-}Q^{-}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)u^{+},

(5.40)

\left(\mu_{q}(\lambda)-(H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1}\right)u^{+}=P_{q+1}^{+}Q^{-}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)u^{-}+P_{q+1}^{+}Q^{-}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)u^{+}.

Let

S_{q}^{-}(\lambda):=P_{q+1}^{+}Q^{-}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)P_{q+1}^{+}

and observe that by Lemma
2.1
the operator

S_{q}^{-}(\lambda)
is compact, self-adjoint, and non-negative in
P_{q+1}^{+}L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
. Set

(5.41)

m_{q}^{-}(\lambda):=\inf\sigma\left(\left(\mu_{q}(\lambda)-(H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1}\right)_{|P_{q+1}^{+}L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\right)=\frac{2b}{(\Lambda_{q}+\lambda)(\Lambda_{q+1}+\lambda)}

and

S_{q,>}^{-}(\lambda):=S_{q}^{-}(\lambda)\,\mathds{1}_{[m_{q}^{-}(\lambda),\infty)}\left(S_{q}^{-}(\lambda)\right),\quad S_{q,<}^{-}(\lambda):=S_{q}^{-}(\lambda)-S_{q,>}^{-}(\lambda).

Now (
5.40
) is equivalent to

(5.42)

\left(\left(\mu_{q}(\lambda)-(H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1}\right)-S_{q,<}^{-}(\lambda)\right)u^{+}=P_{q+1}^{+}Q^{-}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)u_{-}+S_{q,>}^{-}(\lambda)u^{+}_{>},

where

u_{>}^{+}:=P_{q+1,>}^{+}u

and

P_{q+1,>}^{+}=P_{q+1,>}^{+}(\lambda):=\mathds{1}_{[m_{q}^{-}(\lambda),\infty)}(S_{q}^{-}(\lambda))\,P_{q+1}^{+}.\\

Note that

{\rm rank}\,(P_{q+1,>}^{+}(\lambda))={\rm rank}\,(S_{q,>}^{-}(\lambda))<\infty.

The operator

\left(\mu_{q}(\lambda)-(H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1}\right)_{|P_{q+1}^{+}L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}-S_{q,<}^{-}(\lambda)\\

is positive and boundedly invertible in
P_{q+1}^{+}L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
. Set

R_{q}^{-}(\lambda):=\left(\left(\mu_{q}(\lambda)-(H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1}\right)_{|P_{q+1}^{+}L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}-S_{q,<}^{-}(\lambda)\right)^{-1}.

Then (
5.42
) implies

(5.43)

u^{+}=R_{q}^{-}(\lambda)\,P_{q+1}^{+}\,Q^{-}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)\,u^{-}+R_{q}^{-}(\lambda)\,S_{q,>}^{-}(\lambda)\,u_{>}^{+}.

Inserting (
5.43
) into (
5.39
), we obtain

P_{q+1}^{-}X_{q}^{-}(\lambda)P_{q+1}^{-}u^{-}=-P_{q+1}^{-}Y_{q}^{-}(\lambda)u_{>}^{+},

with

X_{q}^{-}(\lambda):=(H_{0}+\lambda)^{-1}-\mu_{q}(\lambda)+Q^{-}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)+Q^{-}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)R_{q}^{-}(\lambda)P_{q+1}^{+}Q^{-}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)

and

Y_{q}^{-}(\lambda):=Q^{-}_{\upsilon}(\lambda)R_{q}^{-}(\lambda)\,S_{q,>}^{-}(\lambda).

Then, similarly to (
5.34
), we find that for any
u\in{\rm ker}\,(H_{\upsilon}-\Lambda_{q})
we have

(5.44)

u=u_{0}^{-}+W_{q}^{-}u_{>}^{+},

with

u_{0}^{-}\in{\rm ker}\,(T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma})),\quad u_{>}^{+}\in P_{q+1,>}^{+}{\rm ker}\,(H_{-\upsilon}-\Lambda_{q}),

and an appropriate operator

W_{q}^{-}:P_{q+1,>}^{+}{\rm ker}\,(H_{-\upsilon}-\Lambda_{q})\to L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}).

Now, (
5.44
) entails (
3.1
) for
H_{-\upsilon}
with

n_{q}^{-}:=\inf_{\lambda\in(-\inf\sigma(H_{\upsilon}),\infty)}{\rm rank}\,(P_{q+1,>}^{+}(\lambda))<\infty.

Finally, we prove (
3.2
) for
H_{-\upsilon}
. Assume that

(5.45)

\|\upsilon\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}<\upsilon_{q}^{-}:=\sup_{\lambda\in(-b+1,\infty)}\frac{m_{q}^{-}(\lambda)}{C_{p}(\lambda)(1+m_{q}^{-}(\lambda))},

where
C_{p}(\lambda)
and
m_{q}^{-}(\lambda)
are the quantities defined in (
2.6
) and (
5.41
), respectively.
Note that using (
5.37
) we can estimate
\upsilon_{q}^{-}
from below as

\upsilon_{q}^{-}\geq\frac{m_{q}^{-}(1)}{C_{p}(1)(1+m_{q}^{-}(1))}\geq\frac{2bc}{2b+(\Lambda_{q}+1)(\Lambda_{q+1}+1)}.

Furthermore, there exists
\lambda\in(-b+1,\infty)
such that

\|\upsilon\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}\,C_{p}(\lambda)<\frac{m_{q}^{-}(\lambda)}{1+m_{q}^{-}(\lambda)}<1.

By (
2.5
)

(5.46)

\|G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)\|^{2}\leq C_{p}(\lambda)\|\upsilon\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}\leq\frac{m_{q}^{-}(\lambda)}{1+m_{q}^{-}(\lambda)}<1.

On the other hand, since
\|(I-G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)^{*}G_{\upsilon}(\lambda))^{-1}\|\leq(1-\|G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)\|^{2})^{-1}
, similarly to (
5.35
) we have

(5.47)

\|S_{q}^{-}(\lambda)\|\leq\frac{\|G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)\|^{2}}{1-\|G_{\upsilon}(\lambda)\|^{2}}.

Putting together (
5.47
) and (
5.46
) we get

\|S_{q}^{-}(\lambda)\|<m_{q}^{-}(\lambda),

so that
P_{q+1,>}^{+}(\lambda)=0
, and (
5.44
) implies that for
u\in{\rm ker}\,(H_{-\upsilon}-\Lambda_{q})
we have
u\in{\rm ker}\,(T_{q}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma}))
, i.e. (
3.2
) holds
for
H_{-\upsilon}
.

5.2.
Proof of Theorem
3.6

Assume that

u=p_{0}u\in{\rm ker}\,(T_{0}(\upsilon\delta_{\Gamma})),

which is equivalent to
\upsilon^{1/2}(u_{|\Gamma})=0
as an element of

\upsilon\neq 0
as an element of

5.3.
Proof of Theorem
3.9

Due to the invariance of
p_{q}
under the magnetic translations (see (
4.10
) and (
4.11
)) we may assume without loss of generality that
{\mathcal{C}}_{r}
is centered at the origin.

Let
u\in{\rm ker}(H_{0}-\Lambda_{q})={\rm ran}\,(p_{q})
,
q\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then we have

(5.48)

u(x)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}c_{k}\varphi_{k,q}(x),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2},

with
{\bf c}:=\left\{c_{k}\right\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{+})
, where
\left\{\varphi_{k,q}\right\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}
is the orthonormal basis of
{\rm ker}(H_{0}-\Lambda_{q})
defined in (
4.6
).
Hence, the representation in (
5.48
) generates a unitary operator
{\mathcal{U}}_{q}:{\rm ker}(H_{0}-\Lambda_{q})\to\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{+})
which maps
u
to
{\bf c}
.
On the other hand we have

\langle T_{q}(\delta_{{\mathcal{C}}_{r}})\varphi_{k,q},\varphi_{\ell,q}\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}=\lambda_{k,q}(r)\delta_{k\ell},

where

(5.49)

\begin{split}\lambda_{k,q}(r):&=\langle T_{q}(\delta_{{\mathcal{C}}_{r}})\varphi_{k,q},\varphi_{k,q}\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\\
&=b\frac{q!}{k!}\left(br^{2}/2\right)^{k-q}\,{\rm L}_{q}^{(k-q)}\left(br^{2}/2\right)^{2}e^{-br^{2}/2},\quad r\in(0,\infty).\end{split}

Then we have

{\mathcal{U}}_{q}T_{q}(\delta_{{\mathcal{C}}_{r}}){\mathcal{U}}_{q}^{*}=\tau_{q,r},

where
\tau_{q,r}:\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{+})\to\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{+})
is a compact self-adjoint operator defined by

(\tau_{q,r}{\bf c})_{k}=\lambda_{k,q}(r)c_{k},\quad k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+},

with
{\bf c}=\left\{c_{k}\right\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{+})
. In particular, the functions
\varphi_{k,q}
are eigenfunctions of
T_{q}(\delta_{{\mathcal{C}}_{r}})
with eigenvalues equal to
\lambda_{k,q}(r)
.
For
r\in(0,\infty)
set

(5.50)

m_{q}(r):=\#\left\{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}\,|\,{\rm L}_{q}^{(k-q)}\left(br^{2}/2\right)=0\right\}.

Then (
5.49
) implies

(5.51)

{\rm dim\,ker}\,(T_{q}(\delta_{{\mathcal{C}}_{r}}))=\#\left\{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}\,|\,\lambda_{k,q}(r)=0\right\}=m_{q}(r),\quad r\in(0,\infty),

and

(5.52)

{\mathcal{D}}_{q}=\left\{r\in(0,\infty)\,|\,{\rm dim\,ker}\,(T_{q}(\delta_{{\mathcal{C}}_{r}}))\geq 1\right\}=\left\{r\in(0,\infty)\,|\,m_{q}(r)\geq 1\right\}.

Bearing in mind the expressions for the Laguerre polynomials
{\rm L}_{q}^{(k-q)}
with
q=1,2

)–(
4.8
), we find that the zero of
{\rm L}_{1}^{(k-1)}
is equal to

, while the zeros of

,

. Thus, (

)–(
5.52
) easily entail the explicit description of the sets
{\mathcal{D}}_{q}
,

, and their components

, available in (
3.11
)–(
3.13
).

Let us estimate
m_{q}(r)
and describe
{\mathcal{D}}_{q}
in the general case. Note that the polynomial
{\rm L}_{q}^{(\alpha)}
with
\alpha>-1
has exactly
q
simple strictly positive zeros
(see
[
43
, Theorem 3.3.1]
and (
4.9
)). Denote by
\left\{\zeta_{\ell}(\alpha)\right\}_{\ell=1}^{q}
,
\alpha\in[0,\infty)
, the set of the zeros of
{\rm L}_{q}^{(\alpha)}
,
enumerated in decreasing order. The functions
\zeta_{\ell}
,
\ell=1,\ldots,q
, are smooth strictly increasing functions (see
[
43
, Subsection 6.21 (4)]
) which tend to infinity as
\alpha\to\infty
(see
[
12
]
). Thus we can classify the zeros of
{\rm L}_{q}^{(k-q)}
with
k\geq q
.
In order to handle the polynomials
{\rm L}_{q}^{(k-q)}
with
0\leq k<q
we note that

(5.53)

{\rm L}_{q}^{(k-q)}(t)=\frac{k!}{q!}(-t)^{q-k}{\rm L}_{k}^{(q-k)}(t),\quad t\in{\mathbb{R}},

(see
[

), so that if
k=0

{\rm L}_{q}^{(-q)}(t)

, while if
q\geq 2

{\rm L}_{q}^{(k-q)}

k

q-k

\left\{z_{m,k}\right\}_{m=1}^{k}
the set of the positive zeros of

Moreover, we have

(5.54)

\frac{d}{dt}{\rm L}_{k}^{(q-k)}(t)=-{\rm L}_{k-1}^{(q-k+1)}(t),\quad t\in{\mathbb{R}},

(see
[

5.54

interlace, i.e.

15

and interpolate by linear functions on the intervals

Finally,

Appendix A
Closedness and semiboundedness of the quadratic form in (
2.1
)

Recall that we consider for
\upsilon\in L^{p}(\Gamma;\mathbb{R})
the following quadratic form

(A.1)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|\Pi(A)u|^{2}dx+\int_{\Gamma}\upsilon|\tau u|^{2}\,ds,\quad u\in{\rm H}_{A}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}).

The function
\upsilon
can be decomposed as
\upsilon=\upsilon_{1}+\upsilon_{2}
, where
\upsilon_{1}\in L^{\infty}(\Gamma)
and where
\|\upsilon_{2}\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}\leq\delta
for arbitrarily small
\delta>0
.
First, we get the following elementary estimate

(A.2)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Gamma}\upsilon|\tau u|^{2}\,ds\right|\leq\int_{\Gamma}|\upsilon_{1}||\tau u|^{2}\,ds+\int_{\Gamma}|\upsilon_{2}||\tau u|^{2}\,ds.

Next, we estimate the two terms on the right hand side separately.
Combining
[
8
, Lemma 2.6]
, the diamagnetic inequality
[
31
, Theorem 7.21]
, and that
\upsilon_{1}
is a bounded function we obtain that for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
C(\varepsilon)>0
such that

(A.3)

\int_{\Gamma}|\upsilon_{1}||\tau u|^{2}\,ds\leq\varepsilon\|\Pi(A)u\|^{2}_{{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}+C(\varepsilon)\|u\|^{2}_{{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}.

By
[
19
, Lemma 5.3]
the operator of multiplication
\mathcal{M}_{|\upsilon_{2}|}
with
|\upsilon_{2}|
is bounded from
{\rm H}^{1/2}(\Gamma)
into
{\rm H}^{-1/2}(\Gamma)
and, moreover, its norm between these two spaces is estimated from above by

\|\mathcal{M}_{|\upsilon_{2}|}\|_{{\rm H}^{1/2}(\Gamma)\rightarrow{\rm H}^{-1/2}(\Gamma)}\leq c\|\upsilon_{2}\|_{{L}^{p}(\Gamma)}=c\delta,

where
c=c(\Gamma,p)>0
.
Using the above estimate of the norm of
\mathcal{M}_{|\upsilon_{2}|}
and that the mapping
\tau
is bounded from
{\rm H}_{A}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
into
{\rm H}^{1/2}(\Gamma)
we get

(A.4)

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}|\upsilon_{2}||\tau u|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\|{\mathcal{M}}_{|\upsilon_{2}|}\tau u\|_{\rm H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)}\|\tau u\|_{\rm H^{1/2}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\leq c\delta\|\tau\|_{{\rm H}_{A}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\rightarrow{\rm H}^{1/2}(\Gamma)}^{2}\|u\|^{2}_{{\rm H}_{A}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}.

Combining the estimates (
A.2
), (
A.3
), (
A.4
), and taking into account that the decomposition of
\upsilon
can be chosen such that the parameter
\delta
is arbitrarily small, we conclude that for any
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
there exists
C^{\prime}(\varepsilon^{\prime})>0
so that

\left|\int_{\Gamma}\upsilon|\tau u|^{2}\,ds\right|\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}\|\Pi(A)u\|^{2}_{{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}+C^{\prime}(\varepsilon^{\prime})\|u\|^{2}_{{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}.

Hence, it follows from the perturbation result
[
27
, Theorem VI.1.33]
that the quadratic form in (
A.1
) is closed and semibounded.

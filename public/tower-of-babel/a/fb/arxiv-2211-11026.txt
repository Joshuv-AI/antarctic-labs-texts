Linear resolutions of t t –spread lexsegment ideals via Betti splittings
Marilena Crupi, Antonino Ficarra

Abstract
. Let S = K ⁡ [ x 1 , … , x n ] S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}] be a polynomial ring in n n variables with coefficients over a field K K . A t t –spread lexsegment ideal I I of S S is a monomial ideal generated by a t t –spread lexsegment set. We determine all t t –spread lexsegment ideals with linear resolution by means of Betti splittings. As applications we provide formulas for the Betti numbers of such a class of ideals and furthermore we characterize all incompletely t t –spread lexsegment ideals with linear quotients.

Introduction

The classification of
ideals with linear resolution is of great importance in commutative algebra. In general, proving
that a graded ideal of a standard graded polynomial ring has linear resolution can be difficult. However,
some classes of graded ideals with linear resolution may be found in
[
2
,
3
,
8
,
9
,
11
,
14
,
21
,
24
,
25
]
and the reference therein.
Let
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be the polynomial ring in
n
variables with coefficients over a fixed field
K
. One method for computing Betti numbers of a monomial ideal
I
of
S
is to decompose
I
into smaller monomial ideals
P
and
Q
such that
I=P+Q
and the minimal set of monomial generators
G(I)
of
I
is the disjoint union of the minimal sets of monomial generators of
P
and
Q
. By taking this approach in
[
12
]
, Eliahou and Kervaire introduced the notion of
splittable monomial ideal
and proved that for such an ideal
I
there exists a formula for computing its Betti numbers in terms of the Betti numbers of two suitable proper non–zero ideals
P,Q
contained in
I
, and the Betti numbers of
P\cap Q
. More in detail,
\beta_{q}(I)=\beta_{q}(P)+\beta_{q}(Q)+\beta_{q-1}(P\cap Q)
, for all
q
. Verifying that an ideal
I
is splittable is not very simple, however, there are many ways of manipulating the minimum set of monomial generators of a monomial ideal
I
to construct smaller ideals
P
and
Q
such that the formula above still holds. Motivated overall by this fact afterwards Francisco, Ha and Van Tuyl have introduced the concept of
Betti splitting

[
15
]
: let
I
,
P
and
Q
be monomial ideals of
S
such that
G(I)
is the disjoint union of
G(P)
and
G(Q)
. The ideal
I=P+Q
is said to be a
Betti splitting
if

\beta_{i,j}(I)=\beta_{i,j}(P)+\beta_{i,j}(Q)+\beta_{i-1,j}(P\cap Q)
, for all
i,j
. This strategy implies that one knows some information about the minimal
resolutions of
P
,
Q
and
P\cap Q
, and thus it is natural to analyze conditions on the Betti
numbers of those ideals that force
I=P+Q
to be a Betti splitting. This notion will be crucial for the development of the article. Furthermore, if
I=P+Q
is a Betti splitting and
P\cap Q=PQ
, to compute the (graded) Betti numbers of
I
, one can take also into account the formula for the (graded) Betti numbers of the product of two ideals, see
[
21
, Proposition 3.1]
.

In
[
1
]
, the authors introduced the notion of
t
–spread lexsegment ideals. For
t\geq 1
, this new class of monomial ideals generalizes the notion of squarefree lexsegment ideal
[
2
]
. A squarefree monomial ideal
I
of
S
is a squarefree lexsegment ideal if for all squarefree monomials
u\in I
and all squarefree monomials
v\in S
with
\deg(u)=\deg(v)
and such that
v\geq_{\textup{slex}}u
, then
v\in I
. Such a notion can be generalized as follows
[
1
]
: let
t\geq 1
. A
t
–spread ideal
I
of
S
is said to be a
t
–spread lexsegment ideal
if for all
t
–spread monomials
u\in I
and all
t
–spread monomials
v\in S
with
\deg(u)=\deg(v)
and such that
v\geq_{\textup{slex}}u
, then
v\in I
. If
t\geq 0
is an integer, a monomial
u=x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{d}}
of
S
is
t
–spread
if
i_{j+1}-i_{j}\geq t
, for all
j=1,\dots,d-1
and a monomial ideal of
S
is
t
–spread if it is generated by
t
–spread monomials
[
7
]
.

In
[
23
]
, a general lexsegment ideal was introduced. Such a definition has been recently generalized in
[
14
]
by the notion of an arbitrary
t
–spread lexsegment ideal.
Let
t\geq 1
and let
M_{n,d,t}
be the set of all
t
–spread monomials of
S
of degree
d
. Assume that
M_{n,d,t}
is endowed with the squarefree lexicographic order
\geq_{\textup{slex}}
. Let
u,v\in M_{n,d,t}
with

, then the set
\mathcal{L}_{t}(u,v)=\{w\in M_{n,d,t}:u\geq_{\textup{slex}}w\geq_{\textup{slex}}v\}
is called

–spread lexsegment set
.
If
u
is the maximum monomial of
M_{n,d,t}
, or
v
is the minimum monomial of
M_{n,d,t}
,
\mathcal{L}_{t}(u,v)
is said to be an
initial
or a
final
t
–spread lexsegment set
, respectively, and the ideal
(\mathcal{L}_{t}(u,v))
is said to be an
initial
or a
final
t
–spread lexsegment ideal
. One can observe that the
initial
t
–spread lexsegment ideal
, defined above, is the
t
–spread lexsegment ideal introduced in
[
1
]
. Thus, in this article, we will refer to such an ideal as the
initial

–spread lexsegment ideal
, and

t
–spread lexsegment ideal
an ideal generated by a
t
–spread lexsegment
\mathcal{L}_{t}(u,v)
. For

, one obtains the definition in
[
23
]
. A
t
–spread lexsegment ideal
I=(\mathcal{L}_{t}(u,v))
is said to be a
completely

–spread lexsegment ideal
if

is the intersection of an initial and a final
t
–spread lexsegment ideal. Otherwise,

is said to be an
incompletely
t
–spread lexsegment ideal
.

The aim of this article is to classify all
t
–spread lexsegment ideals with a linear resolution
via
Betti splittings, where
t
is a positive integer. The classification is achieved by analyzing the behaviour of the incompletely
t
–spread lexsegment ideals. Such a study began in
[
14

using

techniques and all completely

[

,
11

[
4
]
. On one point of view, the article provides a further contribution to the more general problem of determining all monomial ideals with linear resolutions.

The article is organized as follows. Section

–spread lexsegment ideal

is a completely

. Furthermore, we characterize all incompletely

1.
Preliminaries

Let
K
be a field and
S=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be the standard graded polynomial ring with coefficients in
K
. For a graded ideal
I
of
S
, we denote by
\beta_{i,j}(I)=\dim_{K}\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{S}(I,k)_{j}
the graded Betti numbers of
I
and by
\beta_{i}(I)=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\beta_{i,j}(I)
the Betti numbers of
I
. Given a monomial ideal
I
of
S
, let
G(I)
be the minimal system of monomial generators of
I
and denote by
G(I)_{d}
the set of all
u\in G(I)
with
\deg(u)=d
,
d>0
. The lowest degree of a monomial of
G(I)
is denoted by
\textup{indeg}(I)
.

If
I
is generated in degree
d
, we say that
I
has a
d
–linear resolution if
\beta_{i,i+j}(I)=0
for every
i,j\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
j\neq d
. Usually, when the context is clear, we simply write that
I
has a linear resolution.

The
Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity
of a graded ideal
I
is defined as follows:

\operatorname{reg}(I)=\max\{j-i:\beta_{i,j}(I)\neq 0\}.

A graded ideal
I
has a
d
–linear resolution if and only if
\operatorname{reg}(I)=d
and
I
is generated in degree
d
. Moreover, if
I
is a graded ideal of
S
with homogeneous generators
m_{1},\ldots,m_{r}
, then
\operatorname{reg}(I)\geq\deg(m_{i})
, for all
i
and, in particular,
\operatorname{reg}(I)\geq\textup{indeg}(I)
.

Every monomial
w\in S
can be written as
w=x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{d}}
with sorted indices
1\leq i_{1}\leq i_{2}\leq\dots\leq i_{d}\leq n
. We define the
support
of
w
as follows:

\operatorname{supp}(w)=\{j:x_{j}\,\mbox{divides $w$}\}=\{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{d}\}.

With the notation above,
\min(w)=\min\big\{i:i\in\operatorname{supp}(u)\big\}=i_{1}
,
\max(w)=\max\big\{i:i\in\operatorname{supp}(u)\big\}=i_{d}
. Moreover, we set
\max(1)=\min(1)=0
.
If
i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{d}
, the monomial
w
is said to be
squarefree
.

1.1.
t
–spread lexsegment ideals

This subsection is an overview on
t
–spread lexsegment ideals. We refer to
[
1
,
7
,
14
]
for more details on the subject.

Definition 1.1
.

Given
n\geq 1,t\geq 0
and
u=x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{d}}\in S
, with
1\leq i_{1}\leq i_{2}\leq\ldots\leq i_{d}\leq n
, we say that
u
is
t
–spread
if
i_{j+1}-i_{j}\geq t
, for all
j=1,\dots,d-1
. We say that a monomial ideal
I
of
S
is
t
–spread
if it is generated by
t
–spread monomials.

We note that any monomial ideal of
S
is a
0
–spread monomial ideal and any squarefree monomial ideal of
S
is a
1
–spread monomial ideal.

Let
n,d,t\geq 1
. We denote by
M_{n,d,t}
the set of all
t
–spread monomials of
S
of degree
d
. Throughout the article we tacitly assume that
n\geq 1+(d-1)t
, otherwise
M_{n,d,t}=\emptyset
. Furthermore, we assume that
M_{n,d,t}
is endowed with the
squarefree lexicographic order
,
\geq_{\textup{slex}}

[
2
]
. Recall that given
u=x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{d}},v=x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{2}}\cdots x_{j_{d}}\in M_{n,d,t}
, with
1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\dots<i_{d}\leq n
,
1\leq j_{1}<j_{2}<\dots<j_{d}\leq n
, then
u>_{\textup{slex}}v
if

i_{1}=j_{1},\ \dots,\ i_{s-1}=j_{s-1}\ \ \text{and}\ \ i_{s}<j_{s}
,
for some
1\leq s\leq d
.

Let
T
be a not empty subset of
M_{n,d,t}
. We denote by
\max(T)
(
\min(T)
, respectively) the maximum (minimum, respectively) monomial
w\in T
with respect to
\geq_{\textup{slex}}
. It is
\max(M_{n,d,t})=x_{1}x_{1+t}x_{1+2t}\cdots x_{1+(d-1)t}
and
\min(M_{n,d,t})=x_{n-(d-1)t}x_{n-(d-2)t}\cdots x_{n-t}x_{n}
.

In
[
14
]
, the following definitions have been introduced.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
u,v\in M_{n,d,t}
,
u\geq_{\textup{slex}}v
.
The set

\mathcal{L}_{t}(u,v)=\big\{w\in M_{n,d,t}:u\geq_{\textup{slex}}w\geq_{\textup{slex}}v\big\}

is called
t
–spread lexsegment set
. The set

\mathcal{L}_{t}^{i}(v)=\big\{w\in M_{n,d,t}:w\geq_{\textup{slex}}v\big\}=\mathcal{L}_{t}(\max(M_{n,d,t}),v)

is said to be an
initial
t
–spread lexsegment set
and the set

\mathcal{L}_{t}^{f}(u)=\big\{w\in M_{n,d,t}:w\leq_{\textup{slex}}u\big\}=\mathcal{L}_{t}(u,\min(M_{n,d,t}))

is said to be a
final
t
–spread lexsegment set
.

It is clear that for
u,v\in M_{n,d,t}
with
u\geq_{\textup{slex}}v
, then
\mathcal{L}_{t}(u,v)=\mathcal{L}_{t}^{i}(v)\cap\mathcal{L}_{t}^{f}(u)
.

Definition 1.3
.

A
t
–spread monomial ideal
I
of
S
is said to be a
t
–spread lexsegment ideal
if it is generated by a
t
–spread lexsegment.

A
t
–spread lexsegment ideal
I=(\mathcal{L}_{t}(u,v))
is a
completely
t
–spread lexsegment ideal
if

I=J\cap T,

where
J=(\mathcal{L}_{t}^{i}(v))
and
T=(\mathcal{L}_{t}^{f}(u))
. Otherwise,
I
is said to be an
incompletely
t
–spread lexsegment ideal
.

One can easily observe that every initial and every final
t
–spread lexsegment ideal is completely
t
–spread lexsegment, but not all
t
–spread lexsegment ideals are completely
t
–spread lexsegment ideals (see, for instance,
[
14
, Example 2.5]
).
In
[
14
]
, all completely
t
–spread lexsegment ideals were characterized.

Now, we recall some notions that will be useful in the sequel.

A
t
–spread monomial ideal
I
is said to be
t
–spread strongly stable
if for all
t
–spread monomial
u\in I
, all
j\in\operatorname{supp}(u)
and all
i<j
such that
x_{i}(u/x_{j})
is
t
–spread, it follows that
x_{i}(u/x_{j})\in I
.

Since every initial
t
–spread lexsegment ideal
I
of
S
is a
t
–spread strongly stable ideal, in order to compute the graded Betti numbers of
I
one can use the Ene, Herzog, Qureshi’s formula (
[
7
, Corollary 1.12]
):

(1)

\beta_{i,i+j}(I)\ =\ \sum_{u\in G(I)_{j}}\binom{\max(u)-t(j-1)-1}{i}.

A
t
–spread final lexsegment ideal
I
of
S
is also a
t
–spread strongly stable ideal, but with the order of the variables reversed (
x_{n}>x_{n-1}>\cdots>x_{1}
). Thus to compute the graded Betti numbers of
I
one can use the following
modified
Ene, Herzog, Qureshi’s formula
[
14
]
:

(2)

\beta_{i,i+j}(I)\ =\ \sum_{u\in G(I)_{j}}\binom{n-\min(u)-t(j-1)}{i}.

Let
>_{\operatorname{lex}}
be the usual
lexicographic order
on
S
with
x_{1}>x_{2}>\cdots>x_{n}

[
20
]
. The next theorem collects some results from
[
14
]
that will be fundamental for the aim of the article.

Theorem 1.4
.

[
14
]
.
Given
n,d,t\geq 1
, let
u=x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{d}}
and
v=x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{2}}\cdots x_{j_{d}}
be
t
–spread monomials of degree
d
of
S
such that
u\geq_{\textup{slex}}v
. Let
I=(\mathcal{L}_{t}(u,v))
.

(i)

The following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

I
is a completely
t
–spread lexsegment ideal;

(b)

for every
w\in M_{n,d,t}
with
w<_{\textup{slex}}v
there exists an integer
s>i_{1}
such that
x_{s}
divides
w
and
x_{i_{1}}(w/x_{s})\leq_{\operatorname{lex}}u
.

(ii)

Suppose
I
is a completely
t
–spread lexsegment ideal with
\min(v)>\min(u)=1
.
I
has a linear resolution if and only if one of the following conditions holds:

(a)

i_{2}=1+t
;

(b)

i_{2}>1+t
and for the largest
w\in M_{n,d,t}
,
w<_{\textup{slex}}v
, we have
x_{1}(w/x_{\max(w)})

\leq_{\operatorname{lex}}x_{1}x_{i_{2}-t}x_{i_{3}-t}\cdots x_{i_{d}-t}
.

(iii)

Suppose
I
is a completely
t
–spread lexsegment ideal with a linear resolution. Then, for all
i\geq 0
,

\beta_{i}(I)=\sum_{w\in\mathcal{L}_{t}^{f}(u)}\binom{n-\min(w)-(d-1)t}{i}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in\mathcal{L}_{t}^{f}(v)\\
w\neq v\end{subarray}}\binom{\max(w)-(d-1)t-1}{i}.

1.2.
Betti numbers and Betti splittings

In this subsection we discuss the notions of splittable monomial ideal
[
12
]
and Betti splitting
[
15
]
.
In
[
12
]
, Eliahou and Kervaire introduced the notion of
splittable monomial ideal
.

Definition 1.5
.

A monomial ideal
I
of
S
is
splittable
if
I
is the sum of two non–zero monomial ideals
P
and
Q
of
S
such that
G(I)
is the disjoint union of
G(P)
and
G(Q)
and there is a
splitting function

\displaystyle G(P\cap Q)\

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\ G(P)\times G(Q)

\displaystyle w\qquad\

\displaystyle\longmapsto\ (\phi(w),\psi(w))

satisfying the following properties:

(S1)

w=\operatorname{lcm}(\phi(w),\psi(w))
, for all
w\in G(P\cap Q)
,

(S2)

for every subset
G^{\prime}
of
G(P\cap Q)
, both
\operatorname{lcm}(\phi(G^{\prime}))
and
\operatorname{lcm}(\psi(G^{\prime}))
strictly divide
\operatorname{lcm}(G^{\prime})
.

The pair
P,Q
satisfying the above conditions is called a
splitting of
I
.
Moreover, in
[
12
, Proposition 3.1]
(see, also,
[
18
, Theorem 3.2]
) it was proved that if
I
is a splittable ideal with splitting
P,Q
, then for all
i,j\geq 0
,

(3)

\beta_{i,j}(I)=\beta_{i,j}(P)+\beta_{i,j}(Q)+\beta_{i-1,j}(P\cap Q),

where
\beta_{-1,0}=0
. Hence, for all
q\geq 0
,

(4)

\beta_{q}(I)=\beta_{q}(P)+\beta_{q}(Q)+\beta_{q-1}(P\cap Q),

with
\beta_{-1}=0
.

From now on, if
I
is a monomial ideal which is splittable in the sense of Eliahou and Kervaire, we will say that
I
is
Eliahou–Kervaire splittable
(E–K splittable, for short) and if
P,Q
is a
splitting of
I
, the ideal
I=P+Q
will be called
Eliahou–Kervaire splitting
(E–K splitting, for short).
In
[
12
]
, it was proved that every stable ideal is E–K splittable. Now, we want to verify that a
t
–spread strongly stable ideal
I
is E–K splittable.

Let
I
be a
t
–spread strongly stable ideal of
S=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
. Let
P
be the ideal of
S
whose minimal set of monomial generators consists of all
u\in G(I)
such that
x_{1}
divides
u
. It is clear that
P
is a
t
–spread strongly stable ideal of
S
.
Consider the monomial ideal
Q
such that
G(Q)=G(I)\setminus G(P)
. One can quickly verify that
Q
is a
t
–spread strongly stable ideal of
K[x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}]
.

Next, we prove that
W=P\cap Q
is equal to
x_{1}Q
. The inclusion
W\subseteq x_{1}Q
is clear. Let
v\in G(Q)
, then
x_{1}v\in I
. Consider the monomial
\tilde{v}=x_{1}v/x_{\min(v)}
.
Since
v
is a
t
-spread monomial and
1<\min(v)
, it yields that
\tilde{v}
is
t
–spread and
\tilde{v}\in I
because
I
is
t
–spread strongly stable. Hence, from
[
7
, Lemma 1.3]
,
\tilde{v}=uy
, with
u\in G(I)
,
\max(u)<\min(y)
. It follows that
x_{1}
divides
u
and
u\in G(P)
. Thus
x_{1}v\in P\cap Q
and the claim follows. Note that
G(P\cap Q)=x_{1}G(Q)
.

Define the following map

\displaystyle G(P\cap Q)\

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\ G(P)\times G(Q)

\displaystyle w\ \qquad

\displaystyle\longmapsto\ (\phi(w)=u,\psi(w)=w/x_{1}),

where
u
is determined as follows: write
w=x_{1}v
,
v\in G(Q)
and consider the canonical decomposition of the
t
–spread monomial
w/x_{\min(v)}=x_{1}v/x_{\min(v)}
,
i.e.
,
w/x_{\min(v)}=uy
, with
u\in G(I)
,
\max(u)<\min(y)

[
7
, Lemma 1.3]
. It is easy to verify that the above map is a splitting function of
I
and thus
I=P+Q
is an E–K splitting. On the other hand, since the ideal
Q
in
S
has the same (graded) Betti numbers of
Q
in
K[x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}]
and
P\cap Q=x_{1}Q
, it follows that

\beta_{i,j}(I)=\beta_{i,j}(P)+\beta_{i,j}(Q)+\beta_{i-1,j}(P\cap Q)=\beta_{i,j}(P)+\beta_{i,j}(Q)+\beta_{i-1,j-1}(Q),

for all
i,j
, and

\beta_{q}(I)=\beta_{q}(P)+\beta_{q}(Q)+\beta_{q-1}(Q),\,\,\textrm{for all $q$}.

Finally, in order to compute the (graded) Betti numbers of the
t
–spread strongly stable ideal
I=P+Q
, we need only to compute the (graded) Betti numbers of
P
and
Q
.

The notion of splittable ideal has been used in many contexts, see
[
15
]
and the reference therein. Nevertheless, the construction of the required splitting function or even to know whether such a function does exist is not easy. In
[
15
]
, Francisco, Ha and Van Tuyl pointed out that there are other conditions on
P
and
Q
beyond the criterion of Eliahou and Kervaire that imply that formula (
3
) holds. Such situations motivated them to introduce the next definition.

Definition 1.6
.

Let
I
,
P
,
Q
be monomial ideals of
S
such that
G(I)
is the disjoint union of
G(P)
and
G(Q)
. We say that

(\mathcal{L}_{t}(u,x_{1}x_{n-(d-2)t}\cdots x_{n-t}x_{n}))=x_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{t}^{f}(u/x_{1}))

.

Let

.
First, we prove that

t

Conversely, if

.

Let

. Since

as an ideal of

. Hence, we can consider the natural short exact sequence of

\cdots\rightarrow\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{S}(K,\widetilde{I}\cap J)_{\bf a}\rightarrow\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{S}(K,\widetilde{I})_{\bf a}\oplus\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{S}(K,J)_{\bf a}\rightarrow\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{S}(K,I)_{\bf a}\rightarrow\cdots,

3.
Some applications

This section contains some applications of the results obtained in the previous sections. More precisely, we compute the Betti numbers of incompletely
t
–spread lexsegment ideals with linear resolutions and, furthermore, we characterize all incompletely
t
–spread lexsegment ideal with linear quotients.

In order to describe the Betti numbers of the class of
t
–spread lexsegment ideals, we have included
[
14
, Proposition 4.5]
as statement (a) in the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
u,v\in M_{n,d,t}
with
\min(u)=1
,
\min(v)>1
and let
I=(\mathcal{L}_{t}(u,v))
. Assume
I
has a linear resolution.

(a)

If
I
is a completely
t
–spread lexsegment ideal, then for all
i\geq 0

\beta_{i}(I)=\sum_{w\in\mathcal{L}_{t}^{f}(u)}\binom{n-\min(w)-(d-1)t}{i}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in\mathcal{L}_{t}^{f}(v)\\
w\neq v\end{subarray}}\binom{\max(w)-(d-1)t-1}{i}.

(b)

If
I
is an incompletely
t
–spread lexsegment ideal, then for all
i\geq 0

\displaystyle\beta_{i}(I)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in\mathcal{L}_{t}(u,v)\\
\min(w)\geq 2\end{subarray}}\binom{\max(w)-(d-1)t-2}{i}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=0}^{i}\sum_{w\in\mathcal{L}_{t}^{f}(u/x_{1})}\binom{\min(v)-1}{j}\binom{n-\min(w)-(d-2)t}{i-j}.

Proof.

(a) See Theorem
1.4
(iii).

(b) As shown in Corollary
2.1
,
I=P+Q
is a Betti splitting with
P=(\mathcal{L}_{t}(u,x_{1}x_{n-(d-2)t}

\cdots x_{n-t}x_{n}))

=x_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{t}^{f}(u/x_{1}))
and
Q=(\mathcal{L}_{t}(x_{2}x_{2+t}\cdots x_{2+(d-1)t},v))
. Moreover, it follows from Corollary
2.3
and the proof of Corollary
2.6
that

P\cap Q=x_{1}Y
has a
(d+1)
–linear resolution, where
Y=(\mathcal{L}_{t}^{f}(u/x_{1}))\cap Q=\textstyle\sum\limits_{i=2}^{\min(v)}x_{i}(\mathcal{L}_{t}^{f}(u/x_{1}))
.

Furthermore, from Corollary
2.6
and (
8
), we have

(10)

P\cap Q=x_{1}(x_{2},\dots,x_{\min(v)})(\mathcal{L}_{t}^{f}(u/x_{1}))=(x_{2},\dots,x_{\min(v)})P.

Indeed,
\operatorname{supp}(P)\cap\{2,\dots,{\min(v)}\}=\emptyset
,
(x_{2},\dots,x_{\min(v)})\cap P=(x_{2},\dots,x_{\min(v)})P
, where
\operatorname{supp}(P)=\bigcup_{w\in G(P)}\operatorname{supp}(w)
. Since
I=P+Q
is a Betti splitting, then

\beta_{i}(I)=\beta_{i}(P)+\beta_{i}(Q)+\beta_{i-1}(P\cap Q),

for every
i\geq 0
. On the other hand,
P=x_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{t}^{f}(u/x_{1}))
and so
\beta_{i}(P)=\beta_{i}((\mathcal{L}_{t}^{f}(u/x_{1})))
. Moreover,
(\mathcal{L}_{t}^{f}(u/x_{1}))
is a
t
–spread strongly stable ideal of the polynomial ring
S
with the order on the variables reversed (
x_{n}>x_{n-1}>\dots>x_{1}
), whereas
Q
is a
t
–spread strongly stable ideal of the polynomial ring
K[x_{2},\dots,x_{n}]
in
n-1
variables with the usual order on the variables
x_{2}>x_{3}>\dots>x_{n}
. Hence, in order to compute
\beta_{i}(Q)
, we must substitute in (
1
)
\max(w)
with
\max(w)-1
, for every
w\in G(Q)
; whereas when we compute
\beta_{i}((\mathcal{L}_{t}^{f}(u/x_{1})))
by formula (
2
), we must take into account that
(\mathcal{L}_{t}^{f}(u/x_{1}))
is equigenerated in degree
d-1
.

Finally, setting
\ell=\min(v)
and applying
[
22
, Proposition 3.1]
(see, also,
[
16
, Corollary 3.5]
), from (
10
), we have:

\displaystyle\beta_{i-1}(P\cap Q)\

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{k=0}^{i-1}\beta_{j}((x_{2},\dots,x_{\ell}))\cdot\beta_{i-1-k}(P)

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{k=0}^{i-1}\binom{\ell-1}{k+1}\beta_{i-k-1}(P).

If we set

, then

and we can write

\displaystyle\beta_{i}(P)+\beta_{i-1}(P\cap Q)\

\displaystyle=\ \beta_{i}(P)+\sum_{j=1}^{i}\binom{\ell-1}{j}\beta_{i-j}(P)

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{j=0}^{i}\sum_{w\in\mathcal{L}_{t}^{f}(u/x_{1})}\binom{\min(v)-1}{j}\binom{n-\min(w)-(d-2)t}{i-j}.

∎

We conclude the section with our last application which is related to the determination of incompletely
t
–spread lexsegment ideals which have linear quotients.

Recall that a monomial ideal
I\subset S
has linear quotients if there exists an ordering
u_{1}>u_{2}>\dots>u_{m}
of its minimal generating set
G(I)
such that the colon ideals
(u_{1},\dots,u_{k-1}):u_{k}
are generated by a subset of the set of the variables
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
, for all
k=2,\dots,m
. If
I
is a monomial ideal equigenerated in degree
d
and
I
has linear quotients, then
I
has a
d
–linear resolution
[
20
, Proposition 8.2.1]
. The converse is not true. However, if
I
is a monomial ideal generated in degree
2
, then
I
has linear quotients if and only if
I
has a linear resolution
[
20
, Theorem 10.2.6]
. Thus, one can ask for what other classes of monomial ideals these properties are equivalent.

In the following, we show that the aforementioned properties are equivalent for all incompletely
t
–spread lexsegment ideals.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
I
be an incompletely
t
–spread lexsegment ideal generated in degree
d
. Then
I
has linear quotients if and only if
I
has a linear resolution.

Proof.

Since
I
is generated in degree
d
, then if
I
has linear quotients, it follows that
I
has a linear resolution. Conversely, suppose
I=(\mathcal{L}_{t}(u,v))
has a linear resolution,
\min(u)=1
and
\min(v)>1
. By Theorem
2.8
, we have

u<x_{1}x_{\ell+1+t}\cdots x_{\ell+1+(d-1)t}
and
v=x_{\ell}x_{n-(d-2)t}\cdots x_{n-t}x_{n},

for some
2\leq\ell<n-(d-1)t
. Moreover, from the proof of Corollary
2.6
, if we set
P=(\mathcal{L}_{t}(u,x_{1}x_{n-(d-2)t}\cdots x_{n-t}x_{n}))=x_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{t}^{f}(u/x_{1}))
and
Q=(\mathcal{L}_{t}(x_{2}x_{2+t}\cdots x_{2+(d-1)t},v))
, then
P\cap Q=(x_{2},\dots,x_{\ell})P
.

Following
[
8
]
, we order the generators of
I
in a suitable way. Firstly, note that
G(I)
is the disjoint union of
G(P)
and
G(Q)
. Let
>_{\operatorname{lex}}
be the lexicographic order corresponding to
x_{1}>x_{2}>\dots>x_{n}
and let
>_{\overline{\operatorname{lex}}}
be the lexicographic order corresponding to
x_{n}>x_{n-1}>\dots>x_{1}
. We order
G(Q)=\{w_{1}>_{\operatorname{lex}}w_{2}>_{\operatorname{lex}}\dots>_{\operatorname{lex}}w_{p}\}
decreasingly with respect to
>_{\operatorname{lex}}
and
G(P)=\{z_{1}>_{\overline{\operatorname{lex}}}z_{2}>_{\overline{\operatorname{lex}}}\cdots>_{\overline{\operatorname{lex}}}z_{q}\}
decreasingly with respect to
>_{\overline{\operatorname{lex}}}
. Then, we order
G(I)
as follows:

w_{1}>_{\operatorname{lex}}w_{2}>_{\operatorname{lex}}\cdots>_{\operatorname{lex}}w_{p}>_{\overline{\operatorname{lex}}}z_{1}>_{\overline{\operatorname{lex}}}z_{2}>_{\overline{\operatorname{lex}}}\cdots>_{\overline{\operatorname{lex}}}z_{q}.

In order to simplify the notation, we will denote such an order by
\succ
. Thus

(11)

w_{1}\succ w_{2}\succ\dots\succ w_{p}\succ z_{1}\succ z_{2}\succ\cdots\succ z_{q}.

The colon ideal
(w_{1},\dots,w_{k-1}):w_{k}
is generated by variables, for all
k=2,\dots,p
. Indeed,
Q
is an initial
t
–spread lexsegment ideal in
K[x_{2},\dots,x_{n}]
. Thus, it is also a
t
–spread strongly stable ideal and so it has linear quotients with respect to the ordering
w_{1}\succ w_{2}\succ\dots\succ w_{p}

[
7
, Theorem 1.4]
. If one take
k\in\{1,\dots,q\}
, then

\displaystyle(w_{1},\dots,w_{p},z_{1},\dots,z_{k-1}):z_{k}\

\displaystyle=\ (w_{1},\dots,w_{p}):z_{k}+(z_{1},\dots,z_{k-1}):z_{k}

\displaystyle=\ Q:z_{k}+(z_{1},\dots,z_{k-1}):z_{k}.

Since
P
can be seen as a
t
–spread strongly stable ideal but with the order on the variables reversed (
x_{n}>x_{n-1}>\dots>x_{1}
),
P
has linear quotients with respect to the ordering
z_{1}\succ z_{2}\succ\cdots\succ z_{q}

[
7
, Theorem 1.4]
. Thus,
(z_{1},\dots,z_{k-1}):z_{k}
is generated by variables. Thus, if we show that
Q:z_{k}
is generated by variables too, we can conclude that also
(w_{1},\dots,w_{p},z_{1},\dots,z_{k-1}):z_{k}
is generated by variables, as desired.

Let us prove that
Q:z_{k}=(x_{2},\ldots,x_{\ell})
. Firstly, one can observe that

z_{k}(Q:z_{k})=Q\cap(z_{k})\subseteq Q\cap P=(x_{2},\dots,x_{\ell})P.

Let
y\in Q:z_{k}
. Then,
yz_{k}\in(x_{2},\dots,x_{\ell})P
and
x_{j}z_{s}
divides
yz_{k}
, for some
j\in\{2,\dots,\ell\}
and some
s\in\{1,\dots,q\}
. Since
z_{k}\leq_{\textup{slex}}u<_{\textup{slex}}x_{1}x_{\ell+1+t}\cdots x_{\ell+1+(d-1)t}
, then
x_{j}
does not divide
z_{k}
. Hence,
x_{j}
divides
y
. This shows that
Q:z_{k}\subseteq(x_{2},\dots,x_{\ell})
. Conversely, we prove that
(x_{2},\dots,x_{\ell})\subseteq Q:z_{k}
. Indeed, if one picks
x_{j}\in(x_{2},\dots,x_{\ell})
, then
x_{j}z_{k}\in Q
. In fact, since
z_{k}\leq_{\operatorname{lex}}u<_{\textup{slex}}x_{1}x_{\ell+1+t}\cdots x_{\ell+1+(d-1)t}
and
\min(z_{k})=1
, we have that
\min(z_{k}/x_{1})\geq\ell+1+t
. Thus
\min(z_{k}/x_{1})-j>t
and
x_{j}(z_{k}/x_{1})
is
t
–spread. Furthermore,
x_{j}(z_{k}/x_{1})\in\mathcal{L}_{t}(x_{2}x_{2+t}\cdots x_{2+(d-1)t},v)=G(Q)
. Thus
x_{1}x_{j}(z_{k}/x_{1})=x_{j}z_{k}\in Q
. The assertion follows.
∎

as in (
11
), then
\operatorname{set}_{I}(w_{k})=\operatorname{set}_{Q}(w_{k})
and
\operatorname{set}_{I}(z_{k})=\{x_{2},\dots,x_{\ell}\}\cup\operatorname{set}_{P}(z_{k})
, where the union is disjoint.

By
[
20
, Corollary 8.2.2]
,

(12)

\beta_{i}(I)=\sum_{y\in G(I)}\binom{|\operatorname{set}_{I}(y)|}{i}.

We claim that we can recover the formula given in Theorem
3.1
(b).
Indeed, from formulas (
1
) and (
2
), we have that
|\operatorname{set}_{Q}(w_{k})|=\max(w_{k})-2-(d-1)t
and
|\operatorname{set}_{P}(z_{k})|=n-\min(z_{k})-(d-2)t
. Hence,

\displaystyle\beta_{i}(I)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in G(I)}\binom{|\operatorname{set}_{I}(y)|}{i}=\sum_{w\in G(Q)}\binom{|\operatorname{set}_{Q}(w)|}{i}+\sum_{z\in G(P)}\binom{|\{x_{2},\dots,x_{\ell}\}\cup\operatorname{set}_{P}(z)|}{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in\mathcal{L}_{t}(u,v)\\
\min(w)\geq 2\end{subarray}}\!\!\binom{\max(w)-(d-1)t-2}{i}+\sum_{z\in G(P)}\sum_{j=0}^{i}\binom{|\{x_{2},\dots,x_{\ell}\}|}{j}\binom{|\operatorname{set}_{P}(z)|}{i-j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in\mathcal{L}_{t}(u,v)\\
\min(w)\geq 2\end{subarray}}\!\!\binom{\max(w)-(d-1)t-2}{i}\!+\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=0,\ldots,i\\
w\in\mathcal{L}_{t}^{f}(u/x_{1})\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\!\binom{\ell-1}{j}\binom{n-\min(w)-(d-2)t}{i-j},

which coincides with the formula given in Theorem
3.1
(b).

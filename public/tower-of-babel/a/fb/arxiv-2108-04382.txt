The projection onto the cross
Heinz H. Bauschke, Manish Krishan Lal, Xianfu Wang

Abstract
We consider the set of pairs of orthogonal vectors in Hilbert space, which is also called the cross because it is the union of the horizontal and vertical axes in the Euclidean plane when the underlying space is the real line. Crosses, which are nonconvex sets, play a significant role in various branches of nonsmooth analysis such as feasibility problems and optimization problems. In this work, we study crosses and show that in infinite-dimensional settings, they are never weakly (sequentially) closed. Nonetheless, crosses do turn out to be proximinal (i.e., they always admit projections) and we provide explicit formulas for the projection onto the cross in all cases.

Lemma 1.1

(

We have
\operatorname{conv}\,C=X\times X

Proof
. Clearly,

Unfortunately, when

Lemma 1.2

(

The set

If

If

.

(u_{i})_{i\in I}

Next, let

\displaystyle=\frac{\|x_{0}\|^{2}+\|y_{0}\|^{2}}{1+\lambda^{2}}\big(1+\lambda^{4}-2\lambda^{2}\big)

(33e)

\displaystyle=\frac{\|x_{0}\|^{2}+\|y_{0}\|^{2}}{1+\lambda^{2}}\big(1-\lambda^{2}\big)^{2}.

(33f)

\displaystyle f(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2(1-\lambda^{2})^{2}}\frac{\|x_{0}\|^{2}+\|y_{0}\|^{2}}{1+\lambda^{2}}\big(1-\lambda^{2}\big)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{1+\lambda^{2}}\frac{\|x_{0}\|^{2}+\|y_{0}\|^{2}}{2}

Proposition 3.10
.

Consider the equation

\left\langle{x_{0}},{y_{0}}\right\rangle\lambda^{2}-(\|x_{0}\|^{2}+\|y_{0}\|^{2})\lambda+\left\langle{x_{0}},{y_{0}}\right\rangle=0,

\lambda
.
Then

\displaystyle\lambda_{\pm}

\displaystyle:=\frac{\|x_{0}\|^{2}+\|y_{0}\|^{2}\pm\sqrt{(\|x_{0}\|^{2}+\|y_{0}\|^{2})^{2}-4\left\langle{x_{0}},{y_{0}}\right\rangle^{2}}}{2\left\langle{x_{0}},{y_{0}}\right\rangle}

(36a)

\displaystyle=\frac{\|x_{0}\|^{2}+\|y_{0}\|^{2}\pm\|x_{0}+y_{0}\|\|x_{0}-y_{0}\|}{2\left\langle{x_{0}},{y_{0}}\right\rangle}

(36b)

which satisfy

\lambda_{+}\lambda_{-}=1
.
Moreover,

\lambda_{+}\neq\lambda_{-}

\Leftrightarrow

x_{0}\neq\pm y_{0}
,

\lambda_{+}\left\langle{x_{0}},{y_{0}}\right\rangle\geq\lambda_{-}\left\langle{x_{0}},{y_{0}}\right\rangle>0,

(37)

and the left inequality is strict if and only if
x_{0}\neq\pm y_{0}
.

Proof
. First note that

\|x_{0}\mp y_{0}\|^{2}\geq 0

\Leftrightarrow

\|x_{0}\|^{2}+\|y_{0}\|^{2}\geq\pm 2\left\langle{x_{0}},{y_{0}}\right\rangle

\Leftrightarrow

\|x_{0}\|^{2}+\|y_{0}\|^{2}\geq 2|\negthinspace\left\langle{x_{0}},{y_{0}}\right\rangle\negthinspace|

which makes the discriminant of

Eq.

35
nonnegative, and equal to
0

\Leftrightarrow

x_{0}=\pm y_{0}
.
Hence, the roots of
Eq.

35
are real,
and distinct if and only if
x_{0}\neq\pm y_{0}
.
Second, Vieta’s formulas give
\lambda_{+}\lambda_{-}=\left\langle{x_{0}},{y_{0}}\right\rangle/\left\langle{x_{0}},{y_{0}}\right\rangle=1
,
so both
\lambda_{+}
and
\lambda_{-}
are nonzero and they have the same sign.
Next, the quadratic formula yields

\displaystyle\lambda_{\pm}

\displaystyle=\frac{\|x_{0}\|^{2}+\|y_{0}\|^{2}\pm\sqrt{(\|x_{0}\|^{2}+\|y_{0}\|^{2})^{2}-4\left\langle{x_{0}},{y_{0}}\right\rangle^{2}}}{2\left\langle{x_{0}},{y_{0}}\right\rangle}

(38a)

\displaystyle=\frac{\|x_{0}\|^{2}+\|y_{0}\|^{2}\pm\sqrt{\big(\|x_{0}\|^{2}+\|y_{0}\|^{2}+2\left\langle{x_{0}},{y_{0}}\right\rangle\big)\big(\|x_{0}\|^{2}+\|y_{0}\|^{2}-2\left\langle{x_{0}},{y_{0}}\right\rangle\big)}}{2\left\langle{x_{0}},{y_{0}}\right\rangle}

(38b)

\displaystyle=\frac{\|x_{0}\|^{2}+\|y_{0}\|^{2}\pm\|x_{0}+y_{0}\|\|x_{0}-y_{0}\|}{2\left\langle{x_{0}},{y_{0}}\right\rangle},

(38c)

which yields
Eq.

36
.
Clearly,
\lambda_{+}\left\langle{x_{0}},{y_{0}}\right\rangle>0
and

\lambda_{+}\left\langle{x_{0}},{y_{0}}\right\rangle\geq\lambda_{-}\left\langle{x_{0}},{y_{0}}\right\rangle
.
Finally, as observed above,
\lambda_{+}
and
\lambda_{-}
have the same sign; hence,

\lambda_{-}\left\langle{x_{0}},{y_{0}}\right\rangle

\hfill\quad\blacksquare

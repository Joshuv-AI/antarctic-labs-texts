2.
Cofiniteness

The following definition is a generalization of the concept
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite modules which was introduced by Hartshorne.

Definition 2.1

(
[
4
]
)
.

Let
\Phi
be a system of ideals of
R
and
X
an
R
-module. The general local cohomology
module
\lc^{j}_{\Phi}(X)
is called to be
\Phi
-cofinite if there exists an ideal
\mathfrak{a}\in\Phi
such that

\Ext^{i}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{a},\lc^{j}_{\Phi}(X))
is finitely generated for all
i.

In the case of module
\lc^{i}_{\Phi}(M,X),
where
M
is a finitely generated
R
-module, we have a similar definition.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\Phi
be a system of ideals of
R
. The module
\lc^{j}_{\Phi}(M,X)
is called to be
\Phi
-cofinite if there exists an ideal
\mathfrak{a}\in\Phi
such that

\Ext^{i}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{a},\lc^{j}_{\Phi}(M,X))
is finitely generated for all
i.

The cohomological dimension with respect to ideal
\mathfrak{a}
is denoted by
\mathrm{cd}(\mathfrak{a})
and defined as:

\mathrm{cd}(\mathfrak{a})=\sup\{i\mid\lc^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(X)\neq 0\text{ for any $R$-module }X\}.

Definition 2.3
.

(
[
2
]
)

For any system of ideals
\Phi
of
R
and any
R
-module
X,
we
denote the cohomological dimension of
X
with respect to
\Phi
by
\mathrm{cd}(\Phi,X)
and
define as

\mathrm{cd}(\Phi,X)=\sup\{i\mid\lc^{i}_{\Phi}(X)\neq 0\},

if this supremum exists, otherwise, we define it as
-\infty.

We denote
\mathrm{cd}(\Phi,R)
by
\mathrm{cd}(\Phi)
, therefore
\mathrm{cd}(\Phi)\leq\sup\{\mathrm{cd}(\mathfrak{a})\mid\mathfrak{a}\in\Phi\}.

The first result deals with the cohomological dimension with respect to an ideal. It is also an extension of
[
25
, 2.2]
and
[
14
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
M,X
be two finitely generated
R
-modules.
Assume that
\mathrm{cd}(\mathfrak{a})\leq 1
for all
\mathfrak{a}\in\Phi

(
or
\cd(\Phi)\leq 1
)
. Then
\lc^{i}_{\Phi}(M,X)
is
\Phi
-cofinite for all
i\geq 0.

Proof.

Let
F=\Gamma_{\Phi}(-)
and
G=\Hom_{R}(M,-)
be functors from the category of
R
-modules to itself.
For any
R
-module
K,
by
[
20
, 2.8]

FG(K)=\Gamma_{\Phi}(\Hom_{R}(M,K))=\Hom_{R}(M,\Gamma_{\Phi}(K))=\Gamma_{\Phi}(M,K).

If
E
is an injective
R
-module, then so is

\Gamma_{\Phi}(E)
by
[
4
, 4.4]
. Let
\textbf{F}_{\bullet}
be a free resolution of
M

\textbf{F}_{\bullet}:\cdots\to F_{2}\to F_{1}\to F_{0}\to M\to 0,

where
F_{i}
is finitely generated free for all
i\geq 0.

By applying the functor
\Hom_{R}(-,\Gamma_{\Phi}(E))
to the free resolution of
M
, there is an exact sequence

0\to\Hom_{R}(M,\Gamma_{\Phi}(E))\to\Hom_{R}(F_{0},\Gamma_{\Phi}(E))\to\Hom_{R}(F_{1},\Gamma_{\Phi}(E))\to\cdots

and
\Hom_{R}(F_{i},\Gamma_{\Phi}(E))
is injective for all
i\geq 0.

Since
M
is finitely generated, the exact sequence can be rewritten

0\to\Gamma_{\Phi}(\Hom_{R}(M,E))\to\Gamma_{\Phi}(\Hom_{R}(F_{0},E))\to\Gamma_{\Phi}(\Hom_{R}(F_{1},E))\to\cdots.

Note that
\Hom_{R}(\textbf{F}_{\bullet},E)
is an injective resolution of

\Hom_{R}(M,E).
Then

R^{i}F(G(E))=\lc^{i}_{\Phi}(\Hom_{R}(M,E))=0
for all
i>0.
By
[
28
, 10.47]
there is a Grothendieck spectral sequence

E_{2}^{p,q}=\lc^{p}_{\Phi}(\Ext^{q}_{R}(M,X))\underset{p}{\Rightarrow}\lc^{p+q}_{\Phi}(M,X).

Since
\mathrm{cd}(\mathfrak{a})\leq 1
for all
\mathfrak{a}\in\Phi
(or
\cd(\Phi)\leq 1
), we see that
E_{2}^{p,q}=0
for all
q\geq 0,~p>1.
Let
n
be a non-negative integer, there is a filtration
\phi
of submodules of
\lc^{n}=\lc^{n}_{\Phi}(M,X)

0=\phi^{n+1}\lc^{n}\subseteq\phi^{n}\lc^{n}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq\phi^{1}\lc^{n}\subseteq\phi^{0}\lc^{n}=\lc^{n}

such that

E_{\infty}^{i,n-i}\cong\phi^{i}\lc^{n}/\phi^{i+1}\lc^{n}

for all
i\leq n.
Since
E_{\infty}^{p,q}

is a sub-quotient of
E_{2}^{p,q},
it follows that
E_{\infty}^{p,q}=0
for all
p>1.
Consequently,

\phi^{2}\lc^{n}=\phi^{3}\lc^{n}=\ldots=\phi^{n+1}\lc^{n}=0.

Furthermore,
E_{2}^{1,n-1}=E_{\infty}^{1,n-1}\cong\phi^{1}\lc^{n}/\phi^{2}\lc^{n}=\phi^{1}\lc^{n}
and
E_{2}^{0,n}=E_{\infty}^{0,n}\cong\phi^{0}\lc^{n}/\phi^{1}\lc^{n}.
Hence, there is a short exact sequence

0\to\lc^{1}_{\Phi}(\Ext_{R}^{n-1}(M,X))\to\lc^{n}_{\Phi}(M,X)\to\lc^{0}_{\Phi}(\Ext_{R}^{n}(M,X))\to 0.

By
[
2
, 2.15]
, the module
\lc^{1}_{\Phi}(\Ext_{R}^{n-1}(M,X))
is
\Phi
-cofinite. Moreover,
\lc^{0}_{\Phi}(\Ext_{R}^{n}(M,X))
is finitely generated, this implies that
\lc^{n}_{\Phi}(M,X)
is
\Phi
-cofinite.
∎

3.
Artinianess and cofiniteness

It is well-known that
\lc^{\dim(X)}_{\mathfrak{a}}(X)
is an
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite Artinian (see
[
27
, Proposition 5.1]
)

-module and
\lc^{\mathrm{pd}(M)+\dim(X)}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M,N)
is an
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite Artinian
R
-module (see
[
15
, Proposition 2.7 and Theorem 2.8]
). Now, we consider the top generalized local cohomology module with respect to a system of ideals.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a local ring and
M
a finitely generated
R
-module with
\mathrm{pd}(M)<\infty.
Then
\lc^{\mathrm{pd}(M)+\dim(R)}_{\Phi}(M,R)
is
\Phi
-cofinite Artinian.

Proof.

Let
p=\mathrm{pd}(M)
and
d=\dim(R).
We consider the spectral sequence

E_{2}^{p,q}=\Ext_{R}^{p}(M,\lc^{q}_{\Phi}(R))\underset{p}{\Rightarrow}\lc^{p+q}_{\Phi}(M,R).

There is a filtration
\phi
of submodules of
H^{p+d}=H^{p+d}_{\Phi}(M,R)
such that

E_{\infty}^{i,p+d-i}\cong\phi^{i}\lc^{p+d}/\phi^{i+1}\lc^{p+d}

for all
i\leq p+d.
By
[
9
, 2.7]
,
E_{2}^{i,j}=0
for all
i>p
or
j>d.
Since
E_{\infty}^{i,p+d-i}
is a sub-quotient of
E_{2}^{i,p+d-i}
, we can assert that
E_{\infty}^{i,p+d-i}=0
for all
i\neq p.
Consequently,

\phi^{0}\lc^{p+d}=\ldots=\phi^{p}\lc^{p+d}\text{ and }\phi^{p+1}\lc^{p+d}=\ldots=\phi^{p+d+1}\lc^{p+d}=0.

The homomorphisms of the spectral sequence

0=E_{2}^{p-2,d+1}\to E_{2}^{p,d}\to E_{2}^{p+2,d-1}=0

induces that
E_{2}^{p,d}=E_{\infty}^{p,d}.
Hence, there is an isomorphism

\Ext_{R}^{p}(M,\lc^{d}_{\Phi}(R))\cong\phi^{p}\lc^{p+d}/\phi^{p+1}\lc^{p+d}=\lc^{p+d}_{\Phi}(M,R).

The Artianness of
\lc^{p+d}_{\Phi}(M,R)
is followed from
[
10
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Our next goal is to prove the cofiniteness by induction on
d.
When
d=0,

R
is an Artinian
R
-module and
\Supp(R)\subseteq\{\mathfrak{m}\}\subseteq\bigcup_{\mathfrak{a}\in\Phi}V(\mathfrak{a}).
Hence
R
is
\Phi
-torsion by
[
23
, Lemma 2.4]
. It follows from
[
20
, Lemma 2.8]
that

H^{p}_{\Phi}(M,R)\cong\Ext_{R}^{p}(M,R).

Therefore,
\lc^{p}_{\Phi}(M,R)
is finitely generated and then it is
\Phi
-cofinite.

Assume that
d>0.
Let
\overline{R}=R/\Gamma_{\Phi}(R)
which is
\Phi
-torsion-free by
[
20
, Lemma 2.4]
. Using again
[
20
, Lemma 2.4]
,
\overline{R}
is
\mathfrak{a}
-torsion free for all
\mathfrak{a}\in\Phi.
We can take an element
x\in\mathfrak{a}
which is regular on
\overline{R}.
The short exact sequence

0\to\overline{R}\to\overline{R}\to\overline{R}/x\overline{R}\to 0

deduces the following exact sequence

\cdots\overset{f}{\to}\lc^{p+d-1}_{\Phi}(M,\overline{R}/x\overline{R})\overset{g}{\to}\lc^{p+d}_{\Phi}(M,\overline{R})\overset{.x}{\to}\lc^{p+d}_{\Phi}(M,\overline{R})\to 0.

Since
\dim(\overline{R}/x\overline{R})\leq d-1,
by the inductive hypothesis
\lc^{p+d-1}_{\Phi}(M,\overline{R}/x\overline{R})
is
\Phi
-cofinite Artinian. Consequently,
\Hom_{R}(R/\mathfrak{a},\mathrm{Im}f)
is finitely generated as it is a submodule of

\Hom_{R}(R/\mathfrak{a},H^{p+d-1}_{\Phi}(M,\overline{R}/x\overline{R})).

Moreover,
\mathrm{Im}f
is an Artinian
R
-module. It follows from
[
2
, Lemma 2.5 and Definition 2.1]
that
\Ext_{R}^{i}(R/\mathfrak{a},\mathrm{Im}f)
is finitely generated for all
i\geq 0.
Therefore,
\Ext_{R}^{i}(R/\mathfrak{a},\mathrm{Im}g)
is finitely generated for all
i\geq 0.
In particularly,
\Hom_{R}(R/\mathfrak{a},\Image g)
is finitely generated and

\displaystyle\Hom_{R}(R/\mathfrak{a},\Image g)

\displaystyle=\Hom_{R}(R/\mathfrak{a},0:_{\lc^{p+d}_{\Phi}(M,\overline{R})}x)

\displaystyle\cong\Hom_{R}(R/\mathfrak{a},\lc^{p+d}_{\Phi}(M,\overline{R})).

The proof above gives that
\lc^{p+d}_{\Phi}(M,\overline{R})
is Artinian as
\dim{\overline{R}}\leq d.
By
[
2
, Lemma 2.5 and Definition 2.1]
,
\lc^{p+d}_{\Phi}(M,\overline{R})
is
\Phi
-cofinite.
The short exact sequence

0\to\Gamma_{\Phi}(R)\to R\to\overline{R}\to 0

induces a long exact sequence

\cdots\to\lc^{p+d}_{\Phi}(M,\Gamma_{\Phi}(R))\to\lc^{p+d}_{\Phi}(M,R)\to\lc^{p+d}_{\Phi}(M,\overline{R})\to 0.

We get by
[
20
, Lemma 2.8]
that
\lc^{i}_{\Phi}(M,\Gamma_{\Phi}(R))\cong\Ext_{R}^{i}(M,\Gamma_{\Phi}(R))
for all
i\geq 0
and then
\lc^{i}_{\Phi}(M,\Gamma_{\Phi}(R))
is finitely generated for all
i\geq 0.

Therefore
\lc^{p+d}_{\Phi}(M,R)
is
\Phi
-cofinite and the proof is complete.
∎

\mathrm{pd}(M)<\infty.
If
\dim(X)\leq 2
or
\dim(M)\leq 2,
then
\lc^{i}_{\Phi}(M,X)
is
\Phi
-cofinite for all
i\geq 0.

Proof.

There is a Grothendieck spectral sequence

E_{2}^{p,q}=\lc^{p}_{\Phi}(\Ext^{q}_{R}(M,X))\underset{p}{\Rightarrow}\lc^{p+q}_{\Phi}(M,X).

The assumption shows that
\dim(\Ext_{R}^{i}(M,X))\leq 2
and
\Ext_{R}^{i}(M,X)
is finitely generated for all
i\geq 0.

By Corollary
3.2
,
\lc^{2}_{\Phi}(\Ext^{q}_{R}(M,X))
is
\Phi
-cofinite for all
q\geq 0.
On the other hand
\lc^{p}_{\Phi}(\Ext^{q}_{R}(M,X))=0
for all
p>2,q\geq 0
by

[
9
, 2.7]
.
Let
q\geq 0,
the homomorphisms of spectral sequence

0=E_{2}^{i-2,q-i+1}\to E_{2}^{i,q-i}\to E_{2}^{i+2,q-i-1}=0

induce

E_{2}^{i,q-i}=E_{\infty}^{i,q-i}

where
i=0,1.

There is a filtration
\phi
of submodules of
\lc^{q}=\lc^{q}_{\Phi}(M,X)
such that

E_{\infty}^{i,q-i}\cong\phi^{i}\lc^{q}/\phi^{i+1}\lc^{q}

for all
0\leq i\leq q.
Since
E_{\infty}^{i,q-i}
is a sub-quotient of
E_{2}^{i,q-i},
we claim that
E_{\infty}^{i,q-i}=0
for all
i>2.
Thus,

\phi^{3}\lc^{q}=\phi^{4}\lc^{q}=\ldots=\phi^{q+1}\lc^{q}=0.

The homomorphisms of spectral sequence

0\to E_{2}^{0,q-1}\overset{d_{2}^{0,q-1}}{\to}E_{2}^{2,q-2}\overset{d_{2}^{2,q-2}}{\to}E_{2}^{4,q-3}=0

turn out

E_{\infty}^{2,q-2}=E_{3}^{2,q-2}=\Ker d_{2}^{2,q-2}/\Image d_{2}^{0,q-1}=E_{2}^{2,q-2}/\Image d_{2}^{0,q-1}.

The short exact sequence

0\to\Image d_{2}^{0,q-1}\to E_{2}^{2,q-2}\to E_{\infty}^{2,q-2}\to 0

induces the following exact sequence

\Hom_{R}(R/\mathfrak{a},E_{2}^{2,q-2})\longrightarrow\Hom_{R}(R/\mathfrak{a},E_{\infty}^{2,q-2})\longrightarrow\Ext_{R}^{1}(R/\mathfrak{a},\Image d_{2}^{0,q-1}).

Since
E_{2}^{2,q-2}
is
\Phi
-cofinite Artinian by Corollary
3.2
,
\Hom_{R}(R/\mathfrak{a},E_{2}^{2,q-2})
is finitely generated. Furthermore,
\Image d_{2}^{0,q-1}
is finitely generated. Therefore,
\Hom_{R}(R/\mathfrak{a},E_{\infty}^{2,q-2})
is finitely generated. Note that
E_{\infty}^{2,q-2}
is Artinian as it is a sub-quotient of the Artinian
R
-module
E_{2}^{2,q-2}=\lc^{2}_{\Phi}(\Ext_{R}^{q-2}(M,X))
by Corollary
3.2
. It follows from
[
2
, Lemma 2.5 and Definition 2.1]
that
\Ext_{R}^{i}(R/\mathfrak{a},E_{\infty}^{2,q-2})
is finitely generated for all
i\geq 0.

The equality
E_{\infty}^{1,q-1}=E_{2}^{1,q-1}
shows that
\phi^{1}\lc^{q}/\phi^{2}\lc^{q}
is
\Phi
-cofinite by
[
2
, Corolary 2.15]
. On the other hand, we have
\phi^{2}\lc^{q}\cong E_{\infty}^{2,q-2}
and then

\Ext_{R}^{i}(R/\mathfrak{a},\phi^{1}\lc^{q})
is finitely generated for all
i\geq 0.

Note that
\phi^{0}\lc^{q}/\phi^{1}\lc^{q}\cong E_{\infty}^{0,q}
is finitely generated since
E_{\infty}^{0,q}
is a sub-quotient of
E_{2}^{0,q}
which is finitely generated. Thus
\Ext_{R}^{i}(R/\mathfrak{a},\phi^{0}\lc^{q})
is finitely generated for all
i\geq 0.
This means that the module

\phi^{0}\lc^{q}=\lc^{q}_{\Phi}(M,X)
is
\Phi
-cofinite.
∎

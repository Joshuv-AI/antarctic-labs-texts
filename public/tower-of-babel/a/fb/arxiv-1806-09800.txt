Another class of warped product skew CR-submanifolds of Kenmotsu manifolds
Shyamal Kumar Hui ∗ , Tanumoy Pal and Joydeb Roy

Abstract
. Recently, Naghi et al. [ 32 ] studied warped product skew CR-submanifold of the form M 1 × f M ⊥ M_{1}\times_{f}M_{\bot} of order 1 1 of a Kenmotsu manifold M ¯ \bar{M} such that M 1 = M T × M θ M_{1}=M_{T}\times M_{\theta} , where M T M_{T} , M ⊥ M_{\bot} and M θ M_{\theta} are invariant, anti-invariant and proper slant submanifolds of M ¯ \bar{M} . The present paper deals with the study of warped product submanifolds by interchanging the two factors M T M_{T} and M ⊥ M_{\bot} , i.e, the warped products of the form M 2 × f M T M_{2}\times_{f}M_{T} such that M 2 = M ⊥ × M θ M_{2}=M_{\bot}\times M_{\theta} . The existence of such warped product is ensured by an example and then we characterize such warped product submanifold. A lower bounds of the square norm of second fundamental form is derived with sharp relation, whose equality case is also considered.

2.
Preliminaries

In
[
36
]
Tanno classified connected almost contact metric manifolds whose automorphism groups possess the maximum dimension.
For such a manifold, the sectional curvature of plane sections containing
\xi
is a constant, say
c
. He proved that they could
be divided into three classes:
(i) homogeneous normal contact Riemannian manifolds with
c>0
,
(ii) global Riemannian products of a line or a circle with a Kähler manifold of constant holomorphic sectional curvature
if
c=0
and
(iii) a warped product space
\mathbb{R}\times_{f}\mathbb{C}^{n}
if
c<0
.

Kenmotsu
[
23
]
characterized the differential geometric properties of the manifolds of class (iii) which are nowadays called Kenmotsu
manifolds and later studied by several authors (
[
16
]
-
[
18
]
) etc.

An odd dimensional smooth manifold
\bar{M}^{2m+1}
is said to be an almost contact metric manifold
[
5
]
if it admits a
(1,1)

tensor field
\phi
, a vector field
\xi
, an
1
-form
\eta
and a Riemannian metric
g
which satisfy

(2.1)

\phi\xi=0,\ \ \ \eta(\phi X)=0,\ \ \ \phi^{2}X=-X+\eta(X)\xi,

(2.2)

g(\phi X,Y)=-g(X,\phi Y),\ \ \ \eta(X)=g(X,\xi),\ \ \ \eta(\xi)=1,

(2.3)

g(\phi X,\phi Y)=g(X,Y)-\eta(X)\eta(Y)

for all vector fields
X,Y
on
\bar{M}
.

An almost contact metric manifold
\bar{M}^{2m+1}(\phi,\xi,\eta,g)
is said to be Kenmotsu manifold if the following conditions hold
[
23
]
:

(2.4)

\bar{\nabla}_{X}\xi=X-\eta(X)\xi,

(2.5)

(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi)(Y)=g(\phi X,Y)\xi-\eta(Y)\phi X,

where
\bar{\nabla}
denotes the Riemannian connection of
g
.

Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional submanifold of a Kenmotsu manifold
\bar{M}
. Throughout the paper we assume that
the submanifold
M
of
\bar{M}
is tangent to the structure vector field
\xi
. Let
\nabla
and
\nabla^{\bot}
be the induced connections on the tangent bundle
TM
and the normal bundle
T^{\bot}M
of
M
respectively.
Then the Gauss and Weingarten formulae are given by

(2.6)

\bar{\nabla}_{X}Y=\nabla_{X}Y+h(X,Y)

and

(2.7)

\bar{\nabla}_{X}N=-A_{N}X+\nabla_{X}^{\bot}N

for all
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)
and
N\in\Gamma(T^{\bot}M)
, where
h
and
A_{N}
are second fundamental form and the shape operator
(corresponding to the normal vector field
N
) respectively for the immersion of
M
into
\bar{M}
and they are related by
g(h(X,Y),N)=g(A_{N}X,Y)

for any
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)
and
N\in\Gamma(T^{\bot}M)
, where g is the Riemannian metric on
\bar{M}
as well as on
M
.

The mean curvature
H
of
M
is given by
H=\frac{1}{n}\text{trace}\ h
. A submanifold
M
of a Kenmotsu manifold
\bar{M}
is said to be totally umbilical if
h(X,Y)=g(X,Y)H
for any
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)
. If
h(X,Y)=0
for all
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)
, then
M
is totally geodesic and if
H=0
then
M
is minimal in
\bar{M}
.

Let
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}\}
be an orthonormal basis of the tangent bundle
TM
and
\{e_{n+1},\cdots,e_{2m+1}\}
be that of the normal bundle

T^{\bot}M
. Set

(2.8)

h_{ij}^{r}=g(h(e_{i},e_{j}),e_{r})\ \text{and}\ \ \|h\|^{2}=g(h(e_{i},e_{j}),h(e_{i},e_{j})),

for
i,j\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
and
r\in\{n+1,\cdots,2m+1\}
. For a differentiable function
f
on
M
, the gradient
\boldsymbol{\nabla}f
is defined by

(2.9)

g(\boldsymbol{\nabla}f,X)=Xf

for any
X\in\Gamma(TM)
. As a consequence, we get

(2.10)

\|\boldsymbol{\nabla}f\|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(e_{i}(f))^{2}.

For any
X\in\Gamma(TM)
and
N\in\Gamma(T^{\bot}M)
, we can write

(2.11)

\displaystyle\text{(a)}\ \phi X=PX+QX,\ \ \text{(b)}\ \phi N=bN+cN

where
PX,\ bN
are the tangential components and
QX,\ cN
are the normal components.

A submanifold
M
of an almost contact metric manifold
\bar{M}
is said to be invariant if
\phi(T_{p}M)\subseteq T_{p}M
and anti-invariant if
\phi(T_{p}M)\subseteq T^{\bot}_{p}M
for every
p\in M
.

A submanifold
M
of an almost contact metric manifold
\bar{M}
is said to be slant if for each non-zero vector
X\in T_{p}M
, the angle
\theta
between
\phi X

and
T_{p}M
is a constant, i.e. it does not depend on the choice of
p\in M
. Invariant and anti-invariant submanifolds are particular cases of slant submanifolds with slant angles
\theta=0
and
\frac{\pi}{2}
respectively.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
8
]

Let
M
be a submanifold of an almost contact metric manifold
\bar{M}
such that
\xi\in\Gamma(TM)
. Then,
M
is slant if and
only if there exists a constant
\lambda\in[0,1]
such that

(2.12)

P^{2}=\lambda(-I+\eta\otimes\xi),

furthermore if
\theta
is slant angle then
\lambda=\cos^{2}\theta
.

If
M
is a slant submanifold of an almost contact metric manifold
\bar{M}
, the following relation holds
[
38
]
:

(2.13)

bQX=\sin^{2}\theta\{-X+\eta(X)\xi\},\ \ \ cQX=-QPX.

Definition 2.1
.

[
6
]
Let
(N_{1},g_{1})
and
(N_{2},g_{2})
be two Riemannian manifolds with Riemannian metric
g_{1}

and
g_{2}
respectively and
f
be a positive definite smooth function on
N_{1}
. The warped product
of
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
is the Riemannian manifold
N_{1}\times_{f}N_{2}=(N_{1}\times N_{2},g)
, where

(2.14)

g=g_{1}+f^{2}g_{2}.

A warped product manifold
N_{1}\times_{f}N_{2}
is said to be trivial if the warping function
f
is constant. For a warped product manifold
M=N_{1}\times_{f}N_{2}
, we have
[
6
]

(2.15)

\displaystyle\nabla_{U}X=\nabla_{X}U=(X\ln f)U

for any
X
,
Y\in\Gamma(TN_{1})
and
U\in\Gamma(TN_{2})
.

We now recall the following:

Theorem 2.2
.

(Hiepko’s Theorem, see
[
15
]
)
.
Let
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
be two orthogonal distribution on a Riemannian manifold
M
. Suppose that
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
both are involutive such that
\mathcal{D}_{1}
is a totally geodesic foliation and
\mathcal{D}_{2}

M_{2}\times_{f}M_{2}

M_{2}
and
M_{2}

\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
, respectively.

3.
Skew
CR
-submanifolds of Kenmotsu manifolds

Let
M
be a submanifold of a Kenmotsu manifold
\bar{M}
. First from
[
34
]
, we recall the definition of skew
CR
-submanifolds. Throughout the paper we consider the structure vector field
\xi
is tangent to the submanifold otherwise the submanifold is
C
-totally real
[
14
]
.

For any
X
and
Y
in
T_{p}M
, we have
g(PX,Y)=-g(X,PY)
. Hence it follows that
P^{2}
is symmetric operator on the tangent space
TM
, for all
p\in M
. Therefore the eigen values are real and it is diagonalizable. Moreover its eigen values are bounded by
-1
and
0
. For each
p\in M
, we may set

\mathcal{D}^{\lambda}_{p}=ker\{P^{2}+\lambda^{2}(p)I\}_{p},

where
I
is the identity transformation and
\lambda(p)\in[0,1]
such that
\-\lambda^{2}(p)
is an eigen value of
P^{2}_{p}
. We note that
\mathcal{D}^{1}_{p}=kerQ
and
\mathcal{D}^{0}_{p}=kerP
.
\mathcal{D}^{1}_{p}
is the maximal
\phi
-invariant subspace of
T_{p}M
and
\mathcal{D}^{0}_{p}
is the maximal
\phi
-anti-invariant subspace of
T_{p}M
. From now on, we denote the distributions
\mathcal{D}^{1}
and
\mathcal{D}^{0}
by
\mathcal{D}^{T}\oplus<\xi>
and
\mathcal{D}^{\bot}
, respectively. Since
P^{2}_{p}
is symmetric and diagonalizable, if
-\lambda^{2}_{1}(p),\cdots,-\lambda^{2}_{k}(p)
are the eigenvalues of
P^{2}
at
p\in M
, then
T_{p}M
can be decomposed as direct sum of mutually orthogonal eigen spaces, i.e.

T_{p}M=\mathcal{D}^{\lambda_{1}}_{p}\oplus\mathcal{D}^{\lambda_{2}}_{p}\cdots\oplus\mathcal{D}^{\lambda_{k}}_{p}.

Each
\mathcal{D}^{\lambda_{i}}_{p}
,
1\leq i\leq k
defined on
M
with values in
(0,1)
such that

(i) Each
-\lambda^{2}_{i}(p)
,
1\leq i\leq k
is a distinct eigen value of
P^{2}
with

T_{p}M=\mathcal{D}^{T}_{p}\oplus\mathcal{D}^{\bot}_{p}\oplus\mathcal{D}^{\lambda_{1}}_{p}\oplus\mathcal{D}^{\lambda_{2}}_{p}\cdots\oplus\mathcal{D}^{\lambda_{k}}_{p}\oplus<\xi>_{p}

for any
p\in M
.

(ii) The dimensions of
\mathcal{D}^{T}_{p},

\mathcal{D}_{p}^{\bot}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\lambda_{i}},1\leq i\leq k
are independent on
p\in M
.

Moreover, if each
\lambda_{i}
is constant on
M
, then
M
is called a skew CR-submanifold. Thus, we observe that CR-submanifolds are a particular class of skew CR-submanifolds with
k=0
,
\mathcal{D}^{T}\neq\{0\}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\bot}\neq\{0\}
. And slant submanifolds are also a particular class of skew CR-submanifold with
k=1
,
\mathcal{D}^{T}=\{0\}
,
\mathcal{D}^{\bot}=\{0\}
and
\lambda_{1}
is constant. Moreover, if
\mathcal{D}^{\bot}=\{0\}
,
\mathcal{D}^{T}\neq 0
and
k=1
, then
M
is semi-slant submanifold. Furthermore, if
\mathcal{D}^{T}=\{0\}
,
\mathcal{D}^{\bot}\neq\{0\}
and
k=1
, then
M
is a pseudo-slant (or hemi-slant) submanifold.

A submanifold
M
of
\bar{M}
is said to be proper skew CR-submanifold of order 1 if
M
is a skew CR-submanifold with
k=1
and
\lambda_{1}
is constant. In that case, the tangent bundle of
M
is decomposed as

TM=\mathcal{D}^{T}\oplus\mathcal{D}^{\bot}\oplus\mathcal{D}^{\theta}\oplus<\xi>.

The normal bundle
T^{\bot}M
of a skew CR-submanifold
M
is decomposed as

T^{\bot}M=\phi\mathcal{D}^{\bot}\oplus Q\mathcal{D}^{\theta}\oplus\nu,

where
\nu
is a
\phi
-invariant normal subbundle of
T^{\bot}M
.

Now for the sake of further study we give the following useful results.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
M
be a proper skew
CR
-submanifold of order
1
of a Kenmotsu manifold
\bar{M}
such that
\xi\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{\bot}\oplus\mathcal{D}^{\theta})
, then we have

(3.1)

g(\nabla_{X}Y,Z)=g(A_{\phi Z}X,\phi Y)-\eta(Z)g(X,Y),

(3.2)

g(\nabla_{X}Y,U)=\csc^{2}\theta[g(A_{QU}X,\phi Y)-g(A_{QPU}X,Y)]-\eta(U)g(X,Y)

for every
X,Y\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{T})
,
Z\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{\bot})
and
U\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{\theta})
.

Proof.

For any
X,Y\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{T})
,
Z\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{\bot})
, we have

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{X}Y,Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\phi\bar{\nabla}_{X}Y,\phi Z)+\eta(Z)g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}Y,\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi Y,\phi Z)-g((\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi)Y,\phi Z)-\eta(Z)g(Y,\bar{\nabla}_{X}\xi).

Using (2.4), (2.5) and (2.6) in the above equation, we get (
3.1
).
Also, for
X,Y\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{T})
,
U\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{\theta})
, we have

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{X}Y,U)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\phi\bar{\nabla}_{X}Y,\phi U)+\eta(U)g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}Y,\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi Y,\phi U)-g((\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi)Y,\phi U)-\eta(U)g(Y,\bar{\nabla}_{X}\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi Y,PU)+g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi Y,QU)-\eta(U)g(X,Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g(\phi Y,\bar{\nabla}_{X}PU)-g(\phi Y,\bar{\nabla}_{X}QU)-\eta(U)g(X,Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi PU,Y)-g((\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi)PU,Y)-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}QU,\phi Y)-\eta(U)g(X,Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}P^{2}U,Y)+g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}QPU,Y)-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}QU,\phi Y)-\eta(U)g(X,Y).

By virtue of (
2.4
), (
2.7
) and (
2.12
) the above equation yields

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{X}Y,U)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\cos^{2}\theta g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}U,Y)+\cos^{2}\theta\eta(U)g(X,Y)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle g(A_{QPU}X,Y)+g(A_{QU}X,\phi Y)-\eta(U)g(X,Y),

Thus we get

\displaystyle\sin^{2}\theta g(\nabla_{X}Y,U)=g(A_{QU}X,\phi Y)-g(A_{QPU}X,Y)-\sin^{2}\theta\eta(U)g(X,Y).

From which the relation (3.2) follows.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
M
be a proper skew
CR
-submanifold of order
1
of a Kenmotsu manifold
\bar{M}
such that
\xi\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{\bot}\oplus\mathcal{D}^{\theta})

g([X,Y],Z)=g(A_{\phi Z}X,\phi Y)-g(A_{\phi Z}Y,\phi X)

(3.4)

for every

,

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
M
be a proper skew
CR
-submanifold of order
1
of a Kenmotsu manifold
\bar{M}
such that
\xi\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{\bot}\oplus\mathcal{D}^{\theta})
, then we have

(3.5)

g(\nabla_{Z}W,X)=-g(A_{\phi W}\phi X,Z),

(3.6)

g(\nabla_{Z}U,X)=\csc^{2}\theta\{g(A_{QPU}X,Z)-g(A_{QU}\phi X,Z)\},

(3.7)

g(\nabla_{U}Z,X)=-g(A_{\phi Z}X,U),

(3.8)

g(\nabla_{U}V,X)=\csc^{2}\theta\{g(A_{QPV}X,U)-g(A_{QV}\phi X,U)\},

(3.9)

g(\nabla_{X}Z,U)=\sec^{2}\theta\{g(A_{QPU}Z,X)-g(A_{\phi Z}PU,X)

for
X\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{T})
,
Z,W\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{\bot})
and
U,V\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{\theta})
.

Proof.

For every
X\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{T})
and
Z,W\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{\bot})
, we have

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{Z}W,X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\phi\bar{\nabla}_{Z}W,\phi X),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}\phi W,\phi X)-g((\bar{\nabla}_{Z}\phi)W,\phi X).

Using (
2.5
), (
2.7
) and orthogonality of vector fields in the above equation, we get (
3.5
). Also, for
X\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{T})
,
Z\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{\bot})
and
U\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{\theta})
, we have

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{Z}U,X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\phi\bar{\nabla}_{Z}U,\phi X),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}\phi U,\phi X)-g((\bar{\nabla}_{Z}\phi)U,\phi X),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}PU,\phi X)+g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}QU,\phi X),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}P^{2}U,X)-g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}QPU,X)+g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}QU,\phi X).

Using (
2.7
), (
2.12
) and the symmetry of shape operator in the above equation, we obtain

g(\nabla_{Z}U,X)=\cos^{2}\theta g(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}U,X)+g(A_{QPU}X,Z)-g(A_{QU}\phi X,Z),

from which the relation (
3.6
) follows.

Again, for
X\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{T})
,
Z\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{\bot})
and
U\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{\theta})
, we have

g(\nabla_{U}Z,X)=g(\phi\bar{\nabla}_{U}Z,\phi X)=g(\bar{\nabla}_{U}\phi Z,\phi X)-g((\bar{\nabla}_{U}\phi)Z,X).

By virtue of (
2.5
) and (
2.7
), the above equation yields

g(\nabla_{U}Z,X)=-g(A_{\phi Z}U,\phi X)-g(\phi U,X)\eta(Z),

from which the relation (
3.7
) follows. Again we have

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{U}V,X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\phi\bar{\nabla}_{U}V,\phi X),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\bar{\nabla}_{U}\phi V,\phi X)-g((\bar{\nabla}_{U}\phi)V,\phi X),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\bar{\nabla}_{U}PV,\phi X)+g(\bar{\nabla}_{U}QV,\phi X),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g(\bar{\nabla}_{U}P^{2}V,X)-g(\bar{\nabla}_{U}QPV,X)+g(\bar{\nabla}_{U}QV,\phi X).

Using (
2.7
), (
2.12
) and the symmetry of shape operator in the above equation, we get

g(\nabla_{U}V,X)=\cos^{2}\theta g(\bar{\nabla}_{U}V,X)+g(A_{QPV}X,U)-g(A_{QV}\phi X,U),

from which we get (
3.8
).

For every
X\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{T})
,
Z\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{\bot})
and
U\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}^{\theta})
, we have

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{X}Z,U)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\phi\bar{\nabla}_{X}Z,\phi U)+\eta(U)g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}Z,\xi),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi Z,\phi U-g((\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi)Z,\phi U)-\eta(U)g(Z,\bar{\nabla}_{X}\xi),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi Z,PU)+g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi Z,QU)+\eta(Z)g(\phi X,\phi U)-\eta(U)g(X,Z),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi Z,PU)-g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}QU,\phi Z),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}\phi Z,PU)+g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}bQU,Z)+g(\bar{\nabla}_{X}cQU,Z).

In view of (
2.7
), (

,
D^{\bot}
and

Then the local orthonormal frames for

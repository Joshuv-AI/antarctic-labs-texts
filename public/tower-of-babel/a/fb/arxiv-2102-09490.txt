Mean-Based Trace Reconstruction over Oblivious Synchronization Channels
Mahdi Cheraghchi, Joseph Downs, João Ribeiro, Alexandra Veliche

Abstract
Mean-based reconstruction is a fundamental, natural approach to worst-case trace reconstruction over channels with synchronization errors. It is known that exp ⁡ ( Θ ⁡ ( n 1 / 3 ) ) \exp(\Theta(n^{1/3})) traces are necessary and sufficient for mean-based worst-case trace reconstruction over the deletion channel, and this result was also extended to certain channels combining deletions and geometric insertions of uniformly random bits. In this work, we use a simple extension of the original complex-analytic approach to show that these results are examples of a much more general phenomenon. We introduce oblivious synchronization channels , which map each input bit to an arbitrarily distributed sequence of replications and insertions of random bits. This general class captures all previously considered synchronization channels. We show that for any oblivious synchronization channel whose output length follows a sub-exponential distribution either mean-based trace reconstruction is impossible or exp ⁡ ( O ⁡ ( n 1 / 3 ) ) \exp(O(n^{1/3})) traces suffice for this task.

Introduction

When any length-
n

The goal of worst-case trace reconstruction over

This problem was first introduced by Levenshtein
[
2
,
3

, who studied it over combinatorial channels causing synchronization errors, such as worst-case deletions and insertions of symbols and certain discrete memoryless channels.
Trace reconstruction over the

, which independently deletes each input symbol with some probability, was first considered by Batu, Kannan, Khanna, and McGregor
[
4
]
.
Some of their results were quickly generalized to what we call the

[
5

6

Both the deletion and geometric insertion-deletion channels are examples of discrete memoryless synchronization channels
[
7
,
8
]
.

Holenstein, Mitzenmacher, Panigrahy, and Wieder
[
9

were the first to obtain non-trivial worst-case trace reconstruction algorithms for the deletion channel with constant deletion probability.
They showed that

traces suffice for

reconstruction of any input string with high probability, where
\widetilde{O}(\cdot)
hides polylogarithmic factors.
By mean-based reconstruction, we mean that the reconstruction algorithm only requires knowledge of the expected value of each trace coordinate.
In general, this procedure works as follows:
Let
Y_{x}=(Y_{x,1},Y_{x,2},\dots)
denote the
trace distribution
on input
x\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
and
Y^{\prime}_{x}
denote the infinite string obtained by padding

with zeros on the right.
The

is given by

\mu_{x}=(\mathds{E}[Y^{\prime}_{x,1}],\mathds{E}[Y^{\prime}_{x,2}],\dots).

As the first step, the algorithm estimates
\mu_{x}
from
t

sampled i.i.d. according to
Y_{x}^{\prime}
via the empirical means

\widehat{\mu}_{i}=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{j=1}^{t}T^{(j)}_{i},\quad i=1,2,\dots.

(1)

Subsequently, it outputs the string
\widehat{x}\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{-1,1}}\right\}^{n}
that minimizes
\|\mu_{\widehat{x}}-\widehat{\mu}\|_{1}
.
If

is large enough, we have

with high probability over the randomness of the traces.
Because of their structure, pinpointing the number of traces required for mean-based reconstruction over any channel reduces to bounding
\|\mu_{x}-\mu_{x^{\prime}}\|_{1}
for any pair of distinct strings
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{-1,1}}\right\}^{n}
.
Overall, mean-based reconstruction is a natural paradigm, and it is not only useful over channels with synchronization errors.
For example,

traces suffice for mean-based reconstruction over the binary symmetric channel, which is optimal.

More recently, an elegant complex-analytic approach was employed concurrently by De, O’Donnell, and Servedio
[
10

and by Nazarov and Peres
[
11

to show that

1.3
Channel Model

We introduce and study a general model of discrete memoryless synchronization channels that, in particular, captures the models studied in
[
5
,
6
,
10
,
11
,
1
]

An
oblivious synchronization channel

\mathsf{Ch}_{M}
is characterized by a random variable
M
and a corresponding collection of randomized functions
F_{M}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{-1,1}}\right\}\to\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{-1,1}}\right\}^{M}

To avoid trivial settings where trace reconstruction is impossible, we require that

.
On each input
x_{i}\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{-1,1}}\right\}
, the channel samples
m

and decides which positions of the output are

replicated, flipped, have a value of -1, or have a value of 1.
The sets corresponding to these positions are denoted by
\mathsf{Rep}
,
\mathsf{Flip}
,

, and
C_{-}
, respectively.
These sets are so named because
\mathsf{Rep}
replicates the input bit,
\mathsf{Flip}
flips it,
C_{+}
is constantly
+1
, and
C_{-}
is constantly
-1
.
Note that these sets partition the output length
[m]
according to an arbitrary distribution independent of the input

.
This sampling determines a function
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{-1,1}}\right\}\to\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{-1,1}}\right\}^{m}
.
In other words,

\displaystyle\mathsf{Rep}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{j\in[m]\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}f(-1)_{j}=-1\text{ and }f(1)_{j}=1\},

\displaystyle\mathsf{Flip}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{j\in[m]\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}f(-1)_{j}=1\text{ and }f(1)_{j}=-1\},

\displaystyle C_{+}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{j\in[m]\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}f(-1)_{j}=1=f(1)_{j}\},

\displaystyle C_{-}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{j\in[m]\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}f(-1)_{j}=-1=f(1)_{j}\},

where
f(y)_{j}
denotes the
j^{\text{th}}
coordinate of
f(y)
.
The channel evaluates this function at
x_{i}
to obtain
f(x_{i})
.
From here onward, we use
\mathsf{Rep}
and
\mathsf{Flip}
to denote the sets as well as their corresponding distributions.

As an example, we now show how the replication-insertion channel from
[
1
]
is an instance of the oblivious synchronization channel. Note that the deletion and geometric insertion-deletion channels are particular examples of the replication-insertion channel.
A replication-insertion channel
\mathsf{Ch}_{(M,\mathcal{R},p_{\mathsf{flip}})}
is determined by three parameters a flip probability
p_{\mathsf{flip}}\in[0,\frac{1}{2})
, a sub-exponential output length distribution
\mathcal{M}
over the non-negative integers, and a replication distribution
\mathcal{R}
over subsets of
[M]
.
For any given bit
x\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{-1,1}}\right\}
, the channel samples
(M_{i},R_{i})
according to the joint distribution
(M,R)
.
It then produces the output string
Y_{x}\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{-1,1}}\right\}^{M_{i}}
bit-wise by defining

with probability

and
Y_{x,j}=x
with probability
1-p_{\mathsf{flip}}
if

and sample
Y_{x,j}
uniformly from
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{-1,1}}\right\}
otherwise.
Let
m=M_{i}
and
F_{m}
be the collection of randomized functions, where the randomness comes from sampling
p_{\mathsf{flip}}\leftarrow[0,\frac{1}{2})
and
R_{i}\leftarrow 2^{[m]}
.
We sample the sets
\mathsf{Rep},\mathsf{Flip},C_{+},
and
C_{-}
in the following way:
For each position
j\in[m]
, if
j\in R_{i}
we put
j
in
\mathsf{Rep}
with probability
1-p_{\mathsf{flip}}
and in
\mathsf{Flip}
with probability
p_{\mathsf{flip}}
.
Otherwise, we choose a bit uniformly at random.
If the random bit is
1
, we put
j
in
C_{+}
, and if it is
-1
, we put
j
in
C_{-}
.

Our Contributions

Our main theorem shows that previous results on mean-based trace reconstruction over the deletion and geometric insertion-deletion channels are examples of a much more general phenomenon.

Then, we have

\mathinner{\!\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lvert\mu_{x,i}-\mu_{x^{\prime},i}}}\right\rvert}\leq\Pr[M^{(n)}\geq i]

for every
i
.
Since
M
is sub-exponential, a direct application of Bernstein’s inequality
[
44
, Theorem 2.8.1]
guarantees the existence of constants
c_{4},c_{6}>0
such that for
N=c_{4}n
and any
j\geq 1
we have

\Pr[M^{(n)}\geq N+j]\leq 2e^{-c_{6}(N+j)}.

Combining these observations with the assumption that
|z|\leq e^{c_{3}n^{-2/3}}
yields

\displaystyle\sum_{i=N+1}^{\infty}\mathinner{\!\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lvert\mu_{x,i}-\mu_{x^{\prime},i}}}\right\rvert}|z|^{i-1}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2e^{-c_{6}(N+j)}\cdot e^{c_{3}n^{-2/3}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-c_{5}n}

for some constant
c_{5}>0
and
n
large enough.

5
The Conditions in Theorem
1
are Necessary

Our main result applies to oblivious synchronization channels that meet two conditions:
M
must be a sub-exponential random variable and distributions formed from
\mathsf{Rep}
and
\mathsf{Flip}
must not satisfy a certain equality. It is natural to ask whether these conditions are necessary. We do not know whether the sub-exponentiality is necessary, but we now demonstrate that the requirements on
\mathsf{Rep}
and
\mathsf{Flip}
are.

Corollary 9
.

Let
\mathsf{Ch}_{M}
be an oblivious synchronization channel where
M
is a sub-exponential random variable. If
\mathds{E}[|\mathsf{Rep}|]\cdot g_{W_{R}}(z)=\mathds{E}[|\mathsf{Flip}|]\cdot g_{W_{F}}(z)
, then mean-based trace reconstruction is impossible.

Proof.

Let
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{-1,1}}\right\}^{n}
. We show that their mean traces are identical. By combining our assumption that
\mathds{E}[|\mathsf{Rep}|]\cdot g_{W_{R}}(z)=\mathds{E}[|\mathsf{Flip}|]\cdot g_{W_{F}}(z)
with corollary
4
, we have that

\displaystyle\mathinner{\!\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lvert\overline{P}_{x}(z)-\overline{P}_{x^{\prime}}(z)}}\right\rvert}

\displaystyle=\mathinner{\!\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lvert P_{x}(g_{M}(z))-P_{x^{\prime}}(g_{M}(z))}}\right\rvert}\cdot\mathinner{\!\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lvert\mathds{E}[|\mathsf{Rep}|]\cdot g_{w_{R}}(z)-\mathds{E}[|\mathsf{Flip}|]\cdot g_{w_{F}}(z)}}\right\rvert}

\displaystyle=0

which implies that the power series
\overline{P}_{x}(z)-\overline{P}_{x^{\prime}}(z)
has all zero coefficients. This is the same as saying that for all
i
,
\mu_{x,i}=\mu_{x^{\prime},i}
. This holds for any pair of messages
x,x^{\prime}
, so all possible messages result in the same mean trace. Thus, mean-based trace reconstruction is impossible.
∎

This corollary by itself does not show that we have to state the assumption
\mathds{E}[|\mathsf{Rep}|]\cdot g_{W_{R}}(z)\neq\mathds{E}[|\mathsf{Flip}|]\cdot g_{W_{F}}(z)
in our theorem.
If all the channels we are concerned with had this property, then the theorem’s assumption would be redundant and the above corollary would be vacuously true.

It turns out that there are channels where this assumption does not hold. We prove that such channels exist by describing one. Specifically, we describe an oblivious synchronization channel where
\mathds{E}[|\mathsf{Rep}|]\cdot g_{W_{R}}(z)=\mathds{E}[|\mathsf{Flip}|]\cdot g_{W_{F}}(z)
and mean-based trace reconstruction is impossible, but (non-mean-based) trace reconstruction is easy.

5.1
A Channel Where Mean-based Trace Reconstruction is Impossible

Let
M=2
be a constant, i.e. for each input bit the channel always outputs two bits.
C_{-}=\emptyset
always.
\textsf{Rep},\textsf{Flip},
and
C_{+}
are jointly distributed among three equally likely outcomes.

Rep

Flip

C_{+}

Output 1

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{1,2}}\right\}

\emptyset

\emptyset

Output 2

\emptyset

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{1}}\right\}

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{1}}\right\}

Output 3

\emptyset

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{2}}\right\}

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{1}}\right\}

Equivalently, for a single input bit, the channel outputs the following three strings with equal probability.

Input

-1

+1

Output 1

(-1,-1)

(+1,+1)

Output 2

(+1,+1)

(-1,+1)

Output 3

(+1,+1)

(+1,-1)

Regardless of input bit, both output bits have expected value
1/3
. Thus, mean-based trace reconstruction is impossible.

However, trace reconstruction is easy. On input
-1
, the channel outputs
-1,-1
with probability
1/3
. On input
+1
, the channel never outputs
-1,-1
. This can be used to distinguish any pair of strings. Let
x,y\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{-1,1}}\right\}^{n}
be two distinct strings.
Without loss of generality, suppose
x_{i}=-1
and
y_{i}=+1
for an arbitrary index
i
. Let
\textsf{Tr}(x)
and
\textsf{Tr}(y)
denote traces of
x
and
y
. Then

\Pr\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\textsf{Tr}(x)_{2i-1}=\textsf{Tr}(x)_{2i}=-1}}\right]=1/3

and

\Pr\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\textsf{Tr}(y)_{2i-1}=\textsf{Tr}(y)_{2i}=-1}}\right]=0.

This leads to a simple reconstruction algorithm, which outputs a string
z\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{-1,1}}\right\}^{n}
after looking at the traces of a string
z_{\text{true}}\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{-1,1}}\right\}^{n}
.

1.

Look at
t
independent traces of
z_{\text{true}}
.

2.

If any of the traces have
(-1,-1)
in the
(2i-1)^{\text{th}}
and
(2i)^{\text{th}}
coordinates, set
z_{i}=-1
. Otherwise, set
z_{i}=+1
.

3.

Output
z
.

To analyze this algorithm’s correctness, we consider the probability that a fixed coordinate is correct, then union bound over all coordinates. Let
j\in[n]
be a fixed coordinate. If
(z_{\text{true}})_{j}=+1
, then
z_{j}=+1
with probability
1
. If
(z_{\text{true}})_{j}=-1
, then
z_{j}=+1
with probability
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(2/3}}\right)^{t}
. Combining this with a union bound over the
n
coordinates, we get

\displaystyle\Pr\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[z_{\text{guess}}\neq z_{\text{true}}}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq n\cdot\Pr\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(z_{\text{guess}}}}\right)_{j}\neq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(z_{\text{true}}}}\right)_{j}}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq n\cdot\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(2/3}}\right)^{t}.

Hence,
O(\log(n/\delta))
traces are enough to reconstruct the string with probability at least
1-\delta
.

6
Future Work

We have shown that
\exp(O(n^{1/3}))
traces suffice for mean-based worst-case trace reconstruction over a broad class of oblivious synchronization channels.
Because
\exp(\Omega(n^{1/3}))
traces are required for mean-based worst-case trace reconstuction over the deletion channel, this means that our result cannot be improved in general.
However, our channel model does not cover all discrete memoryless synchronization channels as defined by Dobrushin
[
7
,
8
]
.
It would be interesting to extend our result in some form to all such non-trivial channels.
On the other hand, to complement the above, it would be interesting to prove trace complexity lower bounds for mean-based reconstruction over all these channels.
Furthermore, it is unclear whether the assumption that
M
is sub-exponential is necessary for our result.
A clear extension of this work would be to either remove this condition or prove that it is necessary for mean-based trace reconstruction from
\exp(O(n^{1/3}))
traces.

On the connected blocks polytope
Justus Bruckamp, Markus Chimani, Martina Juhnke

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the connected blocks polytope, which, apart from its own merits, can be seen as the generalization of certain connectivity based or Eulerian subgraph polytopes. We provide a complete facet description of this polytope, characterize its edges and show that it is Hirsch. We also show that connected blocks polytopes admit a regular unimodular triangulation by constructing a squarefree Gröbner basis. In addition, we prove that the polytope is Gorenstein of index 2 2 and that its h ∗ h^{\ast} -vector is unimodal.



1.
Introduction

We study the
connected blocks polytope

\mathrm{CBP}(G)
of a connected graph
G
, which is closely related to several polytopes from literature. Our study generalizes some known results about these polytopes and also considers new aspects that have not been studied so far at all. Our aim is to understand connectivity in a polyhedral way and we are interested in finding sets of blocks of a graph that induce a connected subgraph. This generalizes the problem of finding a maximum weighted subtree of a weighted tree. For the exact definitions of the following mentioned related polytopes, as well as of the connected blocks polytope and their connections to each other, see
Section

3
. All of theses polytopes are defined over a graph or, more general, a matroid, and most of them coincide if the underlying graph is a bridgeless
cactus graph
, i.e., a graph in which two cycles have at most one vertex in common.

In
[
DBKN15
]
, the
connected subgraph polytope

\mathrm{CSP}(G)
of a graph
G
is studied, where a complete inequality description for the connected subgraph polytope of a tree is provided. As we will see in
Proposition

3.1
, the connected subgraph polytope and the connected blocks polytope coincide if the underlying graph is a tree. In
Theorem

5.11
, we provide a complete facet description for the connected blocks polytope, generalizing the aforementioned result in
[
DBKN15
]
.

In literature, the
Eulerian subgraph polytope
mostly appears as a special case of the
cycle polytope
of a binary matroid, e.g., see
[
BG86
]
. In this case the considered subgraphs have components that are Eulerian but these subgraphs are not necessarily connected. To the best of our knowledge, the actual Eulerian subgraph polytope – where only Eulerian subgraphs are considered – has not been studied so far. This can be of interest, e.g., for optimization purposes such as finding a minimum/maximum weighted Eulerian subgraph in a weighted graph
G
. Our research covers the case that the underlying graph is a Eulerian cactus graph. In this case the cycle polytope would be given by the unit cube of some dimension, but the connected blocks polytope is more complicated and is affinely isomorphic to the actual Eulerian subgraph polytope in the considered case, see
Proposition

3.1
. In
[
BG86
]
, a complete facet description of the cycle polytope of a binary matroid without some forbidden minors is given. Furthermore, the polyhedral edges are characterized and it is shown that the cycle polytope is Hirsch if the binary matroid does not have a special minor. We obtain similar results for the connected blocks polytope which directly implies that same results for the actual Eulerian subgraph polytope hold if the underlying graph is a Eulerian cactus graph.

The
2
-edge-connected subgraph polytope
has mostly been studied with the additional condition that the considered subgraphs are spanning subgraphs
[
Mah94
,
BM95
]
. We want to look at the case where all
2
-edge-connected subgraphs are allowed. Therefore consider the case where the underlying graph is a
2
-edge-connected cactus graph. In this case the spanning
2
-edge-connected subgraph polytope has dimension zero since the only spanning
2
-edge-connected subgraph is the graph
G
itself. The
2
-edge-connected subgraph polytope, as we will consider it, however, is more complex and is affinely isomorphic to the connected blocks polytope, if the underlying graph is a
2
-edge-connected cactus graph, see
Proposition

3.1
. In
[
Mah94
,
BM95
]
, several facet-defining inequalities for the spanning
2
-edge-connected subgraph polytope are studied. Furthermore, a complete inequality description is provided for some classes of graphs such as series-parallel graphs and Halin graphs. Since we also provide a complete inequality description of the connected blocks polytope, see
Theorem

5.11
, this directly implies the same result for the
2
-edge-connected subgraph polytope, if the underlying graph is a
2
-edge-connected cactus graph.

There exists no study of Ehrhart theoretical aspects for any of the above mentioned polytopes so far.

Our Contribution and Organization

In
Section

2
, we give all the needed preliminaries on graph theory and introduce some notations. In
Section

3
, we formally define the connected blocks polytope and explain its relation to other polytopes of interest. We then start with some basic results on the connected blocks polytope in
Section

4

\mathrm{CBP}(G)
and characterize when connected blocks polytopes are simple and simplicial, respectively. Afterwards we study the facets in
Section

5
, where we give a complete facet description of
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
in
Theorem

5.11
. This also yields a complete facet description for the Eulerian (
2
-edge-connected) subgraph polytope of Eulerian (
2
-edge-connected, respectively) cactus graphs and in particular generalizes the mentioned main result of
[
DBKN15
]
. In
Section

6
, motivated by
[
BG86
]
, we characterize the polyhedral edges of connected blocks polytopes and show that the polytopes are Hirsch. In
Section

7
, we initiate the study of Ehrhart theoretical aspects of connected blocks polytopes. We show that connected blocks polytopes admit a regular unimodular triangulation and are Gorenstein, which implies unimodality of its
h^{\ast}
-vector. Thus, these polytopes are not only interesting from an optimization point of view but also from a discrete geometry and Ehrhart theoretical point of view. We give the Ehrhart theoretical preliminaries in the beginning of that section since there is no knowledge on Ehrhart theory needed in the rest of the paper.

3.
Considered polytopes

3.1.
Connected Blocks Polytope

Let
G
be a connected graph and
\mathcal{B}
its set of blocks. For a subset of the blocks
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{B}
we define the incidence vector
\chi^{\mathcal{A}}\in\{0,1\}^{\mathcal{B}}
by

\displaystyle\chi_{B}^{\mathcal{A}}\coloneqq\begin{cases}1,\text{ if }B\in\mathcal{A},\\
0,\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

We are interested in finding subsets of blocks that induce a connected graph. We define the
connected blocks polytope
of
G
by

\displaystyle\mathrm{CBP}(G)\coloneqq\mathop{\rm conv}\nolimits\{\chi^{\mathcal{A}}\mid\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{B},\ G[\mathcal{A}]\text{ is connected}\}.

We also consider the empty graph to be connected and hence,
\chi^{\emptyset}=\mathbf{0}\in\mathrm{CBP}(G)
. Given the block-cut tree
\mathcal{T}
of
G
and
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{B}\subseteq V(\mathcal{T})
, we let
\mathcal{T}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle\subseteq\mathcal{T}
denote the smallest subtree containing
\mathcal{A}
. Then
G[\mathcal{A}]
is connected if and only if
V(\mathcal{T}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle)\cap\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{A}
. Thus, we get

\displaystyle\mathrm{CBP}(G)=\mathop{\rm conv}\nolimits\{\chi^{\mathcal{A}}\mid\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{B},\ V(\mathcal{T}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle)\cap\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{A}\}.

In the following, we will show that
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
is closely related to several polytopes from literature.

3.2.
Connected Subgraph Polytope

The
connected subgraph polytope

\mathrm{CSP}(G)
of a graph
G
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathrm{CSP}(G)\coloneqq\mathop{\rm conv}\nolimits\{\chi^{E(H)}\mid H\subseteq G\text{ is connected}\},

where
\chi^{E(H)}\in\{0,1\}^{E(G)}
for a subgraph
H\subseteq G
is defined as

\displaystyle\chi^{E(H)}_{e}\coloneqq\begin{cases}1,\text{ if }e\in E(H),\\
0,\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

If
T
is a tree, every block of
T
consists of a single edge
e\in E(T)
together with its incident vertices. We thus have a one-to-one correspondence between the blocks of
T
and the edges of
T
and it directly follows that

\displaystyle\mathrm{CBP}(T)=\mathrm{CSP}(T)

for every tree
T
. Hence, every connected subgraph polytope of a tree is a connected blocks polytope. The converse is not true since in a tree every block contains at most two cut vertices, while this does not hold for arbitrary graphs.

In
[
DBKN15
]
, a complete inequality description is provided for
\mathrm{CSP}(G)
in the case that
G
is a tree, a cycle, or has no matching of cardinality three. In
Theorem

5.11
, we generalize the first case (
[
DBKN15
, Theorem 6]
) by giving a complete facet description for
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
for general graphs, i.e., the connected blocks polytopes that are not necessarily given by the connected subgraph polytope of a tree.

3.3.
Eulerian Subgraph Polytope

For a graph
G
the
Eulerian subgraph polytope
is defined as

(1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{ESP}(G)\coloneqq\mathop{\rm conv}\nolimits\{\chi^{E(H)}\mid H\subseteq G\text{ is Eulerian}\}.

In the literature, the term Eulerian subgraph polytope is also used for the cycle polytope of a graphic matroid; see for example
[
BG86
]
for a definition and more details. However, that definition also allows subgraphs that have multiple non-trivial components (each of which is Eulerian). Thus those graphs are not really Eulerian and the naming is misleading. Our definition in (
1
) is thus different but arguably more sound in terms of graph theory.

Suppose now that
G
is a connected Eulerian cactus graph. Then the blocks of
G
are exactly the cycles of
G
. Given a cycle
C
of
G
, every Eulerian subgraph
H
of
G
either contains all edges of
C
or none, since otherwise there would exist a bridge in
H
. Hence,
x_{e}=x_{f}
for all
x\in\mathrm{ESP}(G)
and all
e,f\in E(C)
. Thus it is not necessary to have one variable per edge in
G
, but it suffices to have one variable for each cycle in
G
. For a Eulerian cactus graph
G
, let
\mathcal{C}\coloneqq\mathcal{C}(G)
denote the set of cycles of
G
. The previous discussion gives rise to what we call the
projected Eulerian subgraph polytope
of a Eulerian cactus graph
G
. Namely,

\displaystyle\mathrm{ESP}_{p}(G)\coloneqq\mathop{\rm conv}\nolimits\{\chi^{\mathcal{C}(H)}\mid H\subseteq G\text{ Eulerian}\},

where
\chi^{\mathcal{C}(H)}\in\{0,1\}^{\mathcal{C}}
is defined by

\displaystyle\chi^{\mathcal{C}(H)}_{C}\coloneqq\begin{cases}1,\text{ if }C\in\mathcal{C}(H),\\
0,\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

By the above discussion, the two polytopes
\mathrm{ESP}_{p}(G)
and
\mathrm{ESP}(G)
are unimodular equivalent. Since every block of a Eulerian cactus graph is a cycle and every Eulerian subgraph of a Eulerian cactus graph is precisely a subset of cycles whose union is connected, it directly follows that

\displaystyle\mathrm{CBP}(G)=\mathrm{ESP}_{p}(G)

for every Eulerian cactus graph
G
.

As already mentioned, the cycle polytope of a graphic matroid has been studied in literature.
[
BG86
]
provides a nonredundant linear system of inequalities describing the cycle polytope of a graphic matroid. If
G
is a graph such that every subgraph whose components are Eulerian already is connected except possibly some isolated vertices, the cycle polytope and the Eulerian subgraph polytope defined in (
1
) coincide and Theorem 4.23 of
[
BG86
]
is true in this case. Note that this is only the case if and only if all two edge-disjoint cycles of
G
share a common vertex. To the best of our knowledge, there is no work on the Eulerian subgraph polytope as defined in (
1
). We start to close this gap by considering the case that the underlying graph is a Eulerian cactus graph. Note that the cycle polytope of a Eulerian cactus graph would be unimodular equivalent to the trivial unit cube
Q_{n}\coloneqq[0,1]^{n}
of dimension
n
, where
n
is given by the number of cycles of
G
. In our case, however, we typically deal with strict subpolytopes of
Q_{n}
.

3.4.
2-edge-connected Subgraph Polytope

We say that
G
is
2
-edge-connected
if
G
is connected and contains no bridges. We define the
2
-edge-connected subgraph polytope
by

(2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{TECSP}(G)\coloneqq\mathop{\rm conv}\nolimits\{\chi^{E(H)}\mid H\subseteq G\text{ is }2\text{-edge-connected}\}.

In the literature, the
2
-edge-connected subgraph polytope is typically only considered with the additional restriction that the subgraphs need to be spanning, see
[
BM95
]
, i.e., the subgraphs must contain all vertices of the underlying graph. As there is no work on the
2
-edge-connected subgraph polytope as defined in (
2
), we start to close this gap by considering the case that the underlying graph is a
2
-edge-connected cactus graph. Note that the spanning
2
-edge-connected subgraph polytope of a
2
-edge-connected cactus graph
G
would be
\{\chi^{E(G)}\}=\{\mathbf{1}\}
, since
G
is the only spanning
2
-edge-connected subgraph of
G
. In our case, however, the polytope is much more interesting.

Suppose that
G
is a
2
-edge-connected cactus graph. By definition a connected cactus graph is
2
-edge-connected if it contains no bridges, i.e., if and only if it is Eulerian. Analogously to the Eulerian subgraph polytope of a Eulerian cactus graph we observe that for
2
-edge-connected cactus graphs the
2
-edge-connected subgraph polytope is unimodular equivalent to the connected blocks polytope.

Summing up, we obtain the following relations, where
\mathcal{P}_{1}\cong\mathcal{P}_{2}
denotes that the two polytopes
\mathcal{P}_{1}
and
\mathcal{P}_{2}
are unimodular equivalent:

Proposition 3.1
.

(i)

Let
T
be a tree. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{CBP}(T)=\mathrm{CSP}(T).

(ii)

Let
G
be a connected Eulerian cactus graph. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{ESP}_{p}(G)=\mathrm{CBP}(G)\cong\mathrm{TECSP}(G)=\mathrm{ESP}(G).

Thus, in the following, it will suffice to consider the general connected blocks polytope, to directly obtain results on the other above mentioned polytopes.

4.
Polyhedral Warm-up

In this section we determine the dimension of the connected blocks polytope and characterize when those polytopes are simple and simplicial, respectively.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
G
be a connected graph and
\mathcal{B}
its set of blocks. Then we have

\displaystyle\dim(\mathrm{CBP}(G))=|\mathcal{B}|.

In particular,
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
is full-dimensional.

Proof.

The
|\mathcal{B}|+1
vectors
\{\chi^{\{B\}}\}_{B\in\mathcal{B}}\cup\{\mathbf{0}\}
are affinely independent vertices of
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
G
be a connected graph and
\mathcal{B}
its set of blocks.

(i)

\mathrm{CBP}(G)
is simple if and only if
G
has at most one cut vertex.

(ii)

\mathrm{CBP}(G)
is simplicial if and only if
|\mathcal{B}|\leq 2
.

Proof.

(i)

The polytope is trivial if
G
has no cut vertex. Suppose
G
has precisely one cut vertex
v\in V(G)
. Then
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
is given by the
|\mathcal{B}|
-dimensional unit cube since every subset
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{B}
induces a connected subgraph of
G
. In particular,
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
is simple.

Suppose now that there are at least two distinct cut vertices
v,w\in V(G)
. Since these two vertices are connected in
G
by a block path of length
\geq 1
, there must exist a block path
P_{3}=B_{1}\cup B_{2}\cup B_{3}\subseteq G
of length
3
. Consider the face
\mathcal{F}
of
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\coloneqq\{x\in\mathrm{CBP}(G)\mid x_{B}=0\text{ for all }B\in\mathcal{B}\backslash\{B_{1},B_{2},B_{3}\}\}.

Then
\mathcal{F}
is unimodular equivalent to the
3
-dimensional connected blocks polytope of
P_{3}
. It is easy to verify that the vertex
\chi^{\{B_{1}\}}
of
\mathrm{CBP}(P_{3})
lies in an edge with each of the vertices
\chi^{\emptyset},\chi^{\{B_{3}\}},\chi^{\{B_{1},B_{2}\}}
and
\chi^{\{B_{1},B_{2},B_{3}\}}
, see also
Section

6
. Thus, by Proposition 2.4 of
[
Zie94
]
the vertex figure of
\chi^{\{B_{1}\}}
is not a simplex,
\mathcal{F}
is not simple, and thus
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
is not simple.

(ii)

For
|\mathcal{B}|\in\{1,2\}
, we have
\mathrm{CBP}(G)=Q_{1}
or
\mathrm{CBP}(G)=Q_{2}
, respectively, which are simplicial.

Suppose
|\mathcal{B}|\geq 3
. Let
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
be blocks sharing a common vertex. Since
|\mathcal{B}|\geq 3
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\coloneqq\{x\in\mathrm{CBP}(G)\mid x_{B}=0\text{ for all }B\in\mathcal{B}\backslash\{B_{1},B_{2}\}\}

defines a proper face of
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
. Since
\mathcal{F}
is affinely isomorphic to
Q_{2}
, it follows that
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
is not simplicial. ∎

5.
Facets

In this section we will provide a complete and irredundant facet description for the connected blocks polytope.

Box inequalities

We first show that the box inequalities
0\leq x_{B}\leq 1
for
B\in\mathcal{B}
define facets of
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
G
be a connected graph,
\mathcal{B}
its set of blocks and
B\in\mathcal{B}
. Then the inequalities

\displaystyle x_{B}\geq 0\text{ and }x_{B}\leq 1

define facets of
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
.

Proof.

Since
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
is a
0
-
1
-polytope, it is clear that the inequalities define supporting hyperplanes of
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be the face of
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
defined by
x_{B}=0
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\coloneqq\{x\in\mathrm{CBP}(G)\mid x_{B}=0\}.

Then the
|\mathcal{B}|
many vertices
\{\chi^{\{B^{\prime}\}}\}_{B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}\setminus\{B\}}\cup\{\textbf{0}\}
lie in
\mathcal{F}
and are affinely independent. It follows that
\mathcal{F}
is a facet, since
\dim(\mathrm{CBP}(G))=|\mathcal{B}|
.

Now consider
x_{B}\leq 1
and let
\mathcal{B}=\{B_{1},\ldots,B_{n}\}
. After possibly reordering the blocks we can assume that
B=B_{1}
and
G[\{B_{1},\ldots,B_{i}\}]
is connected for all
i\in[n]
. Then the incidence vectors
\chi^{\{B_{1},\ldots,B_{i}\}}
are vertices of the face defined by
x_{B}=1
for all
i\in[n]
. Since these
|\mathcal{B}|
many vectors are affinely independent, the inequality
x_{B}\leq 1
defines a facet of
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
.
∎

Independent blocks inequalities

We will motivate the next type of inequalities by a few examples. Consider a block path
P_{3}=B_{1}\cup B_{2}\cup B_{3}
of length
3
, which can also be seen as a subgraph of the graph in
Figure

1
. Since the blocks
B_{1}
and
B_{3}
do not share a common vertex, we have to find an inequality that is feasible for
\mathrm{CBP}(P_{3})
, but not for
\chi^{\{B_{1},B_{3}\}}
. This can be achieved by imposing the inequality

\displaystyle x_{B_{1}}-x_{B_{2}}+x_{B_{3}}\leq 1.

For an arbitrary connected graph
G
with set of blocks
\mathcal{B}
this can be generalized to

(3)

\displaystyle x_{B_{1}}-x_{B_{2}}+x_{B_{3}}\leq 1,

for any
B_{1},B_{2},B_{3}\in\mathcal{B}
, with
V(B_{1})\cap V(B_{3})=\emptyset
and
B_{2}\in\mathcal{B}\langle\{B_{1},B_{3}\}\rangle\setminus\{B_{1},B_{3}\}
. It is easy to check that together with the box inequalities this already describes all feasible lattice points

\displaystyle\mathrm{CBP}(G)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{\mathcal{B}}.

However, these inequalities do not suffice to describe
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
, i.e., the convex hull of these points. For example consider a block path
P_{5}=B_{1}\cup B_{2}\cup B_{3}\cup B_{4}\cup B_{5}
of length
5
, which again can be seen as a subgraph of the graph in
Figure

1
. Then the inequality

\displaystyle x_{B_{1}}-x_{B_{2}}+x_{B_{3}}-x_{B_{4}}+x_{B_{5}}\leq 1

is valid for
\mathrm{CBP}(P_{5})
and forbids the point
(\frac{1}{2},0,\frac{1}{2},0,\frac{1}{2})^{T}
, which would remain feasible if only the inequalities in (
3
) and the box inequalities were imposed. Similarly, if
G
is a graph with blocks
\mathcal{B}=\{B_{1},B_{2},B_{3},B\}
such that
V(B)\cap V(B_{i})\neq\emptyset
for all

\alpha_{A_{i}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{<0}
, such that
G[\mathcal{B}\setminus\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{t}\}]
has exactly
m
connected components
K_{1},\ldots,K_{m}
, with
\mathcal{B}\langle\mathcal{I}^{(i)}\rangle\subseteq K_{i}
for all
i\in[m]
, and
(\mathcal{I},\beta)
, with

\displaystyle\beta_{B}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\alpha^{(i)}_{B},&\text{if }B\in\mathcal{B}\langle\mathcal{I}^{(i)}\rangle,\\
\alpha_{A_{i}},&\text{if }B=A_{i},\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

defines an independent blocks inequality. Moreover, we have
\sum_{i=1}^{t}\alpha_{A_{i}}=-(m-1)
.

Proof.

We show this by induction on
m
, where the statement is clear for
m=1
. Hence, let
m>1
, and suppose that the above lemma is true for all
m^{\prime}<m
. By the assumption, there exists an
A\in\mathcal{B}\langle\mathcal{I}\rangle\cap\mathcal{A}
, and we have
A\notin\mathcal{B}\langle\mathcal{I}^{(i)}\rangle
for all
i\in[m]
by the definition of
\mathcal{A}
. Consider the connected components
K_{1},\ldots,K_{\ell}
of the graph
G[\mathcal{B}\setminus A]
. By the choice of
A
, we have
\ell\geq 2
, and for all
i\in[m]
, we have
\mathcal{B}\langle\mathcal{I}^{(i)}\rangle\subseteq\mathcal{B}[K_{s}]
for some
s\in[\ell]
. In particular,
(\mathcal{I}^{(i)},\alpha^{(i)})
defines an independent blocks inequality for
\mathrm{CBP}(K_{s})
. Moreover, all pairs
(\mathcal{I}^{(i)},\alpha^{(i)})
belonging to the same connected component
K_{s}
together with the set
\mathcal{A}_{s}\coloneqq\mathcal{A}\cap\mathcal{B}[K_{s}]
trivially fulfill the assumptions of this lemma and by the choice of
A
, for each connected component there are at most
m-1
pairs
(\mathcal{I}^{(i)},\alpha^{(i)})
belonging to the same connected component. Hence, we can apply the induction hypothesis to
K_{s}
, and get independent blocks inequalities
(\mathcal{I}^{\prime(s)},\beta^{(s)})
for
K_{s}
for all
s\in[\ell]
with the required properties. Then it is easy to check that
(\mathcal{I},\beta)
, where

\displaystyle\beta_{B}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\beta^{(s)}_{B},&\text{if }B\in\mathcal{B}[K_{s}],\\
-(\ell-1),&\text{if }B=A,\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

defines an independent blocks inequality with the required properties.
∎

We can now state the main result of this section.

Theorem 5.11
.

Let
G
be a connected graph. Then
\mathrm{CBP}(G)=\mathcal{P}(G)
. In particular the independent blocks inequalities, together with nonnegativity constraints, yield the complete and irredundant facet description of
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
.

Proof.

It is clear that
\mathrm{CBP}(G)\subseteq\mathcal{P}(G)
. Let
x\in\mathcal{P}(G)
. We show that there exists a
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, with
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\leq 1
, and vertices
v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}
of
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
, such that

\displaystyle x=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}v_{i}.

This yields that
x
can be written as a convex combination of the vertices of
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
since the zero vector can be added to the sum with coefficient
1-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}
. We proceed by induction on the number of blocks in
G
and show the stronger statement that for every
x\in\mathcal{P}(G)
there exist
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, vertices
v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}
of
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
, and an independent blocks inequality
(\mathcal{I},\alpha)
, such that

\displaystyle x=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}v_{i}

and

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\leq\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}}\alpha_{B}x_{B}.

Since
x\in\mathcal{P}(G)
, this implies
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\leq 1
. Let
\mathcal{B}
be the set of blocks of
G
. If
|\mathcal{B}|=1
, the statement is obviously true. Suppose now that
|\mathcal{B}|=n
and by induction the statement is true for all connected graphs with less than
n
blocks. Let
x=(x_{B_{1}},\ldots,x_{B_{n}})\in\mathcal{P}(G)
and
\mu=\min_{i\in[n]}x_{B_{i}}
. Then we can write
x
as

\displaystyle x=\mu\cdot\mathbf{1}+y,

with
y\coloneqq(x_{B_{1}}-\mu,\ldots,x_{B_{n}}-\mu)
. Observe that all entries of
y
are between
0
and
1
and there is at least one entry in
y
that is
0
. Let
G_{y}
be the graph that is given by the union of the blocks of
\supp(y)\coloneqq\{B\in\mathcal{B}\mid y_{B}>0\}
and let
K_{1},\ldots,K_{m}
be the connected components of
G_{y}
. For
i\in[m]
we denote by
y^{(i)}
the projection of
y
onto the coordinates
\{B\in\mathcal{B}\mid B\subseteq K_{i}\}
. We show that
y^{(i)}\in\mathcal{P}(K_{i})
for all
i\in[m]
. Every independent blocks inequality
(\mathcal{I},\alpha)
of any
\mathrm{CBP}(K_{i})
induces an independent blocks inequality
(\mathcal{I},\alpha^{\prime})
of
G
, where
\alpha^{\prime}
is defined by

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}_{B}=\begin{cases}\alpha_{B},&\text{if }B\in\mathcal{B}\langle\mathcal{I}\rangle,\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

With this it follows

\displaystyle\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}[K_{i}]}\alpha_{B}y_{B}^{(i)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}}\alpha^{\prime}_{B}x_{B}-\mu\underbrace{\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}}\alpha^{\prime}_{B}}_{=1}\leq 1-\mu\leq 1,

which shows that
y^{(i)}\in\mathcal{P}(K_{i})
. Hence, by induction, there exist
\lambda_{1}^{(i)},\ldots,\lambda_{k_{i}}^{(i)}>0
and vertices
v_{1}^{(i)},\ldots,v_{k_{i}}^{(i)}\in\mathrm{CBP}(K_{i})
and an independent blocks inequality
(\mathcal{I}^{(i)},\alpha^{(i)})
of
\mathrm{CBP}(K_{i})
such that

\displaystyle y^{(i)}=\sum_{j=1}^{k_{i}}\lambda_{j}^{(i)}v_{j}^{(i)},

and

(6)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k_{i}}\lambda_{j}^{(i)}\leq\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}[K_{i}]}\alpha^{(i)}_{B}y^{(i)}_{B},

Let
w_{j}^{(i)}\coloneqq v_{j}^{(i)}\times\{0\}_{B\in\mathcal{B}\setminus\mathcal{B}[K_{i}]}
. We note that the
w_{j}^{(i)}
are vertices of
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
. We can hence write
x
as

\displaystyle x=\mu\cdot\mathbf{1}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{k_{i}}\lambda_{j}^{(i)}w_{j}^{(i)}.

For the sum of coordinates we have

\displaystyle\mu+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{k_{i}}\lambda_{j}^{(i)}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{lambda}}{\leq}\mu+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}[K_{i}]}\alpha^{(i)}_{B}y_{B}^{(i)}

\displaystyle=\mu+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}[K_{i}]}\alpha^{(i)}_{B}(x_{B}-\mu)\right).

Let
\mathcal{B}(\mu)\coloneqq\{B\in\mathcal{B}\mid x_{B}=\mu\}
. Hence,
G[\mathcal{B}\setminus\mathcal{B}(\mu)]=G_{y}
. By
Lemma

5.10
, there exists a set of blocks
\mathcal{A}\coloneqq\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{t}\}\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\mu)
,
t\leq m-1
, and weights
\alpha_{A_{i}}
such that
(\mathcal{I},\beta)
, with
\mathcal{I}\coloneqq\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{I}^{(i)}
, and

\displaystyle\beta_{B}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\alpha^{(i)}_{B},&\text{if }B\in\mathcal{B}[K_{i}],\\
\alpha_{A_{s}},&\text{if }B=A_{s},s\in[t],\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

defines an independent blocks inequality, such that
\sum_{i=1}^{t}\beta_{A_{i}}=-(m-1)
. With this we have

\displaystyle\mu+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}[K_{i}]}\alpha^{(i)}_{B}(x_{B}-\mu)\right)

\displaystyle=-(m-1)\mu+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}[K_{i}]}\alpha^{(i)}_{B}x_{B}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\beta_{A_{i}}x_{A_{i}}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}[K_{i}]}\beta_{B}x_{B}

\displaystyle=\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}}\beta_{B}x_{B},

which finishes the proof.
∎

6.
Edges

In this section we fully characterize the edges of the connected blocks polytope and show as an application that connected blocks polytopes are Hirsch. The following theorem considers the polyhedral edges incident to the origin, i.e., the empty subset of blocks.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
G
be a connected graph and
\mathcal{B}
its set of blocks. Consider
\emptyset\neq\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{B}
such that
G[\mathcal{A}]
is connected. Then
\{\textbf{0},\chi^{\mathcal{A}}\}
is an edge of
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
if and only if
|\mathcal{A}|=1
.

Proof.

Due to
Theorem

5.11
, the only homogeneous facets are the faces given by
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{B}}\mid x_{B}=0\text{ for a }B\in\mathcal{B}\}
. Hence, the vertex figure of the origin is the same as the vertex figure of the origin in the unit cube
Q_{|\mathcal{B}|}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{B}}
.
∎

The next theorem characterizes the edges of
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
in the remaining cases.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
G
be a connected graph and
\mathcal{B}
its set of blocks. Let
\emptyset\neq\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}\subseteq\mathcal{B}
,
\mathcal{A}_{1}\neq\mathcal{A}_{2}
,
G[\mathcal{A}_{1}]
and
G[\mathcal{A}_{2}]
connected, and
|\mathcal{A}_{1}|\leq|\mathcal{A}_{2}|
. Then
\{\chi^{\mathcal{A}_{1}},\chi^{\mathcal{A}_{2}}\}
is an edge of
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
if and only if one of the following conditions holds:

(i)

G[\mathcal{A}_{1}\cup\mathcal{A}_{2}]
is disconnected, or

(ii)

\mathcal{A}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{2}
and there is a unique block in
\mathcal{A}_{2}\setminus\mathcal{A}_{1}
that shares a common vertex with a block of
\mathcal{A}_{1}
.

Proof.

First suppose that
G[\mathcal{A}_{1}\cup\mathcal{A}_{2}]
is disconnected. Let
|\mathcal{A}_{1}|=k
and
|\mathcal{A}_{2}|=\ell
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}\coloneqq\Big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{B}}\Big|\textstyle{\frac{1}{k}}\sum_{B\in\mathcal{A}_{1}}x_{B}+\textstyle{\frac{1}{\ell}}\sum_{B\in\mathcal{A}_{2}}x_{B}-2\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}\setminus(\mathcal{A}_{1}\cup\mathcal{A}_{2})}x_{B}=1\Big\}

is a supporting hyperplane for
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
and the only vertices contained in this hyperplane are
\chi^{\mathcal{A}_{1}}
and
\chi^{\mathcal{A}_{2}}
. Thus
\{\chi^{\mathcal{A}_{1}},\chi^{\mathcal{A}_{2}}\}
is an edge of
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
.

Suppose now that
\mathcal{A}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{2}
and that there is a unique block
B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{2}\backslash\mathcal{A}_{1}
that shares a vertex with a block of
\mathcal{A}_{1}
. Let
m=|\mathcal{A}_{2}\backslash\mathcal{A}_{1}|
. We consider the face
\mathcal{F}
of
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\coloneqq

\displaystyle\{x\in\mathrm{CBP}(G)\mid x_{A}=1\text{ for }A\in\mathcal{A}_{1},x_{A}=0\text{ for }A\notin\mathcal{A}_{2}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm conv}\nolimits\{\chi^{\mathcal{A}}\mid\mathcal{A}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{2},G[\mathcal{A}]\text{ is connected}\}.

Then the hyperplane

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}\coloneqq\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{B}}\mid(m-1)x_{B^{\prime}}-\textstyle{\sum_{B\in\mathcal{A}_{2}\backslash\left(\mathcal{A}_{1}\cup\{B^{\prime}\}\right)}x_{B}}=0\}

is a supporting hyperplane for
\mathcal{F}
and
\chi^{\mathcal{A}_{1}}
and
\chi^{\mathcal{A}_{2}}
are the only vertices of
\mathcal{F}
in
\mathcal{H}
.This shows that
\{\chi^{\mathcal{A}_{1}},\chi^{\mathcal{A}_{2}}\}
is an edge of
\mathcal{F}
and hence of
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
.

It remains to show that in all other cases
\{\chi^{\mathcal{A}_{1}},\chi^{\mathcal{A}_{2}}\}
is not an edge of
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
. We first consider the case that
G[\mathcal{A}_{1}\cup\mathcal{A}_{2}]
is connected but
\mathcal{A}_{1}\nsubseteq\mathcal{A}_{2}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}\nsubseteq\mathcal{A}_{1}
. Then
\mathcal{A}_{1}\cap\mathcal{A}_{2}
and
\mathcal{A}_{1}\cup\mathcal{A}_{2}
induce connected subgraphs of
G
different from
\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}
. For their incidence vectors we obtain the relation

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Big(\chi^{\mathcal{A}_{1}}+\chi^{\mathcal{A}_{2}}\Big)=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\chi^{\mathcal{A}_{1}\cap\mathcal{A}_{2}}+\chi^{\mathcal{A}_{1}\cup\mathcal{A}_{2}}\Big),

which shows that there is a second pair of vertices of
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
that has the same midpoint as
\chi^{\mathcal{A}_{1}}
and
\chi^{\mathcal{A}_{2}}
. Hence,
\{\chi^{\mathcal{A}_{1}},\chi^{\mathcal{A}_{2}}\}
is not an edge of
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
.

The second case to consider is that
\mathcal{A}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{2}
and there are at least two blocks
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
in
\mathcal{A}_{2}\backslash\mathcal{A}_{1}
that each shares a vertex with some block of
\mathcal{A}_{1}
(possibly distinct). Thus, the two graphs
G[\mathcal{A}_{1}\cup\{B_{1}\}]
and
G[\mathcal{A}_{1}\cup\{B_{2}\}]
are connected subgraphs of
G
. Now we join the blocks of
\mathcal{A}_{2}\backslash(\mathcal{A}_{1}\cup\{B_{1},B_{2}\})
with
\mathcal{A}_{1}\cup\{B_{1}\}
or
\mathcal{A}_{1}\cup\{B_{2}\}
iteratively in the following way. We set
\mathcal{A}_{1}^{(1)}\coloneqq\mathcal{A}_{1}\cup\{B_{1}\}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}^{(1)}\coloneqq\mathcal{A}_{1}\cup\{B_{2}\}
and
\mathcal{B}^{(1)}\coloneqq\{B_{1},B_{2}\}
. For increasing
\ell
, starting with
\ell=1
, we pick a block
B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{2}\backslash(\mathcal{A}_{1}\cup\mathcal{B}^{(\ell)})
that shares a common vertex with a block of
\mathcal{A}_{1}^{(\ell)}
or
\mathcal{A}_{2}^{(\ell)}
(or both). We set
\mathcal{A}_{1}^{(\ell+1)}\coloneqq\mathcal{A}_{1}^{(\ell)}\cup\{B^{\prime}\}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}^{(\ell+1)}\coloneqq\mathcal{A}_{2}^{(\ell)}
, if
B^{\prime}
shares a common vertex with a block of
\mathcal{A}_{1}^{(\ell)}
, and else, we set
\mathcal{A}_{1}^{(\ell+1)}\coloneqq\mathcal{A}_{1}^{(\ell)}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}^{(\ell+1)}\coloneqq\mathcal{A}_{2}^{(\ell)}\cup\{B^{\prime}\}
. In both cases we set
\mathcal{B}^{(\ell+1)}\coloneqq\mathcal{B}^{(\ell)}\cup\{B^{\prime}\}
. By construction it is clear that such a block
B^{\prime}
always exists as long as
\mathcal{A}_{2}\backslash(\mathcal{A}_{1}\cup\mathcal{B}^{(\ell)})\neq\emptyset
. In the end, we obtain two sets of blocks
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{1}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{2}
that induce connected subgraphs of
G
and whose incidence vectors have the same midpoint as
\chi^{\mathcal{A}_{1}}
and
\chi^{\mathcal{A}_{2}}
in the polytope
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
. Hence,
\{\chi^{\mathcal{A}_{1}},\chi^{\mathcal{A}_{2}}\}
is not an edge of
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
.
∎

We recall that a
d
-dimensional polytope
\mathcal{P}
is Hirsch, if its diameter is at most
k-d
, where
k
denotes the number of facets of
\mathcal{P}
. In
[
Dan98
]
, Dantzig stated the Hirsch conjecture, first formulated by Hirsch in 1957, which claims that every polytope is Hirsch. More than fifty years later, Santos found the first counterexample to the Hirsch conjecture, see
[
San12
]
. To show that connected blocks polytopes are Hirsch, we first give an upper bound on their diameter.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
G
be a connected graph. Then
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
has diameter at most
\dim(\mathrm{CBP}(G))
, i.e., for each pair of vertices there exists a path along the edges of the polytope between those vertices traversing at most
\dim(\mathrm{CBP}(G))
many edges.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be the set of blocks and let
\delta(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
denote the distance of the vertices
\chi_{1}
and
\chi_{2}
in the graph of the polytope
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
. At first we remark that if
\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}\subseteq\mathcal{B}
, such that
G[\mathcal{A}_{1}]
and
G[\mathcal{A}_{2}]
are connected, with
\mathcal{A}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{2}
, we have
\delta(\chi^{\mathcal{A}_{1}},\chi^{\mathcal{A}_{2}})\leq|\mathcal{A}_{2}\setminus\mathcal{A}_{1}|
: Let
\mathcal{A}_{2}=\mathcal{A}_{1}\cup\{B_{1},\ldots,B_{k}\}
. After possibly reordering we can assume that
\mathcal{A}^{(i)}\coloneqq\mathcal{A}_{1}\cup\{B_{1},\ldots,B_{i}\}
induces a connected subgraph for all
i\in\{0,\ldots,k\}
. Due to
Theorems

6.1
and
6.2
, respectively, we see that
\{\chi^{\mathcal{A}^{(i-1)}},\chi^{\mathcal{A}^{(i)}}\}
is an edge of
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
for all
i\in[k]
. This implies
\delta(\chi^{\mathcal{A}_{1}},\chi^{\mathcal{A}_{2}})\leq|\mathcal{A}_{2}\setminus\mathcal{A}_{1}|
.

Now let
\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}\subseteq\mathcal{B}
be arbitrary sets of blocks each inducing a connected subgraph. Clearly,
G[\mathcal{A}_{1}\cap\mathcal{A}_{2}]
also induces a connected subgraph. Then by the previous case, we have

\displaystyle\delta(\chi^{\mathcal{A}_{1}},\chi^{\mathcal{A}_{2}})

\displaystyle\leq\delta(\chi^{\mathcal{A}_{1}},\chi^{\mathcal{A}_{1}\cap\mathcal{A}_{2}})+\delta(\chi^{\mathcal{A}_{2}},\chi^{\mathcal{A}_{1}\cap\mathcal{A}_{2}})

\displaystyle\leq|\mathcal{A}_{1}\setminus(\mathcal{A}_{1}\cap\mathcal{A}_{2})|+|\mathcal{A}_{2}\setminus(\mathcal{A}_{1}\cap\mathcal{A}_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq|\mathcal{B}|=\dim(\mathrm{CBP}(G)),

where the last inequality follows because the two sets are disjoint.
∎

From this it easily follows:

Corollary 6.4
.

The connected blocks polytopes are Hirsch.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{B}
the set of blocks of a connected graph
G
. The dimension of
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
is
|\mathcal{B}|
by
Proposition

4.1
. In
Theorem

5.1
, we showed that the box inequalitites are facets of
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
. Hence,
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
has at least
2\cdot|\mathcal{B}|
many facets. By
Lemma

6.3
, the diameter of
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
is bounded by

\displaystyle\dim(\mathrm{CBP}(G))=|\mathcal{B}|=2\cdot|\mathcal{B}|-\dim(\mathrm{CBP}(G))\leq k-\dim(\mathrm{CBP}(G)),

where
k
denotes the number of facets of
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
.
∎

7.
Unimodular Triangulations

In this section, using our above obtained knowledge of the polytope’s facets, we study the
h^{\ast}
-vector of the connected blocks polytope. We start by providing the needed preliminaries from Ehrhart theory. Afterwards, we show that connected blocks polytopes admit a regular unimodular triangulation by giving a squarefree Gröbner basis. Continuing, we show that connected blocks polytopes are Gorenstein of index
2
. Both results together imply unimodality of the
h^{\ast}
-vector. Furthermore, we will determine the
h^{\ast}
-polynomial of connected blocks polytopes explicitly, in the case that the underlying graph is a block path. We close this section by giving a lower bound on
h^{\ast}_{1}
.

Lattice polytopes and Ehrhart theory

Let
\mathcal{P}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a lattice polytope. We say that
\mathcal{P}
is
reflexive
if its dual polytope
\mathcal{P}^{\Delta}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is also a lattice polytope. If
\mathcal{P}
can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid A\cdot x\leq\mathbf{1}\},

where
A
is an integer matrix, it already follows that
\mathcal{P}
is reflexive. Furthermore, we say that
\mathcal{P}
is
Gorenstein of index
k
if the
k
-th dilation
k\mathcal{P}
of
\mathcal{P}
is reflexive.

Ehrhart
[
Ehr62
]
proved that
|n\mathcal{P}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|
– i.e., the number of lattice points in the
n
-th dilation of
\mathcal{P}
– is given by a polynomial
E(\mathcal{P},n)
in
n
of degree
\dim P
. Similarly, the
h^{\ast}
-polynomial

h^{\ast}(\mathcal{P},t)=h_{0}^{\ast}(\mathcal{P})+h_{1}^{\ast}(\mathcal{P})t+\cdots+h_{d}^{\ast}(\mathcal{P})t^{d}
of a
d
-dimensional lattice polytope
\mathcal{P}
is obtained by applying a particular change of basis to
E(\mathcal{P},n)
; namely, we have

E(\mathcal{P},n)=h_{0}^{\ast}(\mathcal{P}){n+d\choose d}+h_{1}^{\ast}(\mathcal{P}){n+d-1\choose d}+\cdots+h_{d}^{\ast}(\mathcal{P}){n\choose d}\,.

All coefficients of
h^{\ast}(\mathcal{P},t)
are nonnegative
[
Sta80
]
. For
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathcal{P}
is Gorenstein of index
k
if
h^{\ast}(\mathcal{P},t)=t^{d+1-k}h^{\ast}\left(\mathcal{P},\frac{1}{t}\right)

[
Hib92
]
.
A
triangulation

\mathcal{S}
of a
d
-dimensional lattice polytope
\mathcal{P}
is a subdivision into simplices of dimension at most
d
.
Such a triangulation is
unimodular
if all its simplices are, i.e., they have normalized volume
1
. The
f
-polynomial
of
\mathcal{S}
is defined as

f(\mathcal{S},t)=f_{-1}+f_{0}t+\cdots+f_{d}t^{d+1},

where
f_{i}=|\{F\in\mathcal{S}\mid\dim(F)=i\}|
. The
h
-polynomial

h(\mathcal{S},t)
of
\mathcal{S}
is defined via the relation

f(\mathcal{S},t)=\sum_{i=0}^{d+1}h_{i}t^{i}(t+1)^{d+1-i}.

If
\mathcal{P}
admits a unimodular triangulation
\mathcal{S}
, then
h^{\ast}(\mathcal{P},t)=h(\mathcal{S},t)
.

To calculate a regular, possibly unimodular, triangulation of
\mathcal{P}
, one often computes a Gröbner basis. Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field and
\mathbb{K}[y_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,y_{d}^{\pm},s]
be the Laurent polynomial ring. For a lattice polytope
\mathcal{P}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
and any
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d})\in\mathcal{P}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we set
y^{\alpha}=y_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots y_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}\in\mathbb{K}[y_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,y_{d}^{\pm},s]
. The ring
\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{P}]\coloneqq\mathbb{K}[y^{\alpha}s~|~\alpha\in\mathcal{P}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}]
is the
toric ring
of
\mathcal{P}
, i.e.,
\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{P}]
is the subring of
\mathbb{K}[y_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,y_{d}^{\pm},s]
generated by the monomials
y^{\alpha}s
for
\alpha\in\mathcal{P}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Let
S=\mathbb{K}[x_{\alpha}~|~\alpha\in\mathcal{P}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}]
, where
\deg(x_{\alpha})=1
. The
toric ideal

I_{\mathcal{P}}
of
\mathcal{P}
is the kernel of the surjective ring homomorphism

\pi\colon S\to\mathbb{K}[P]\text{, with }\pi(x_{\alpha})\coloneqq y^{\alpha}s.

A
term order
on a polynomial ring, here
R=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, is a total order
<
on the set of monomials, such that for all monomials
a,b,c
we have
1<a
, and
a<b
implies
ac<bc
. A prototypical example is given by the
degree reverse lexicographic ordering

<_{\mathrm{rev}}
(degrevlex), induced by
x_{1}<\cdots<x_{n}
; we have
x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}<_{\mathrm{rev}}x_{1}^{\beta_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\beta_{n}}
if and only if
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}<\sum_{i=1}^{n}\beta_{i}
, or
\sum\alpha_{i}=\sum\beta_{i}
and
\alpha_{\ell}>\beta_{\ell}
for
\ell=\min\{j~|~\alpha_{j}\neq\beta_{j}\}
. For a polyomial
f\in R
, its
initial term

\mathrm{in}_{<}(f)
is the largest monomial appearing in
f
. The
initial ideal

\mathrm{in}_{<}(I)
of an ideal
I\subseteq R
is the ideal generated by all initial terms in
I
. We call a set of generators
g_{1},\ldots,g_{r}
of
I
a
Gröbner basis
(w.r.t.
<
) if
\mathrm{in}_{<}(g_{1}),\ldots,\mathrm{in}_{<}(g_{r})
generate
\mathrm{in}_{<}(I)
.

Any Gröbner basis of the toric ideal
I_{\mathcal{P}}
of a lattice polytope
\mathcal{P}
gives rise to a regular triangulation
\Delta_{\mathcal{P}}
of
\mathcal{P}
on the vertex set
\mathcal{P}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
as follows: A set
M\subseteq\mathcal{P}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is the vertex set of a simplex in
\Delta_{\mathcal{P}}
if and only if
\prod_{\alpha\in M}x_{\alpha}\notin\sqrt{\mathrm{in}_{<}(I_{\mathcal{P}})}
. The triangulation
\Delta_{\mathcal{P}}
is a regular unimodular triangulation if and only if
\mathrm{in}_{<}(I_{\mathcal{P}})=\sqrt{\mathrm{in}_{<}(I_{\mathcal{P}})}
. This is the case if and only if
I_{\mathcal{P}}
has a squarefree Gröbner basis (see e.g.,
[
Stu96
, Corollary 8.9]
), i.e., there exists a Gröbner basis whose initial terms are squarefree.

Results for the connected blocks polytopes

We now focus on Gröbner bases for connected blocks polytopes. For a connected graph
G
and its set of blocks
\mathcal{B}
, we consider the polynomial ring
\mathbb{K}[x_{\mathcal{A}}\mid\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{B},\,G[\mathcal{A}]\text{ connected}]
. We consider the partial order where
x_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}<x_{\mathcal{A}_{2}}
if and only if
\mathcal{A}_{1}\supset\mathcal{A}_{2}
. We use the degree reverse lexicographic ordering
<_{\mathrm{rev}}
w.r.t. any linear extension of this partial order.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
G
be a connected graph and
\mathcal{B}
its set of blocks. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}\coloneqq\{\underline{x_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}x_{\mathcal{A}_{2}}}-x_{\mathcal{A}_{1}\cap\mathcal{A}_{2}}x_{\mathcal{A}_{1}\cup\mathcal{A}_{2}}\mid

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}\subseteq\mathcal{B},\ \mathcal{A}_{1}\nsubseteq\mathcal{A}_{2},\ \mathcal{A}_{2}\nsubseteq\mathcal{A}_{1},

\displaystyle G[\mathcal{A}_{1}],\ G[\mathcal{A}_{2}],\ G[\mathcal{A}_{1}\cup\mathcal{A}_{2}]\text{ connected}\}

is a Gröbner basis of the toric ideal
I_{\mathrm{CBP}(G)}
of
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
with respect to the above defined order. In particular,
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
has a regular unimodular triangulation. The leading monomials are the underlined terms.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{\mathcal{B}}=\{\mathcal{B}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{B}_{m}\}
be the set of all subsets of blocks inducing a connected subgraph. Let
x^{a}-x^{b}\in I_{\mathrm{CBP}(G)}
,
a,b\in\mathbb{N}^{\tilde{\mathcal{B}}}
, with
\mathrm{in}_{<_{\mathrm{rev}}}(x^{a}-x^{b})=x^{a}
. We have to show that there exists
\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}\in\tilde{\mathcal{B}}
,
x_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}x_{\mathcal{A}_{2}}-x_{\mathcal{A}_{1}\cap\mathcal{A}_{2}}x_{\mathcal{A}_{1}\cup\mathcal{A}_{2}}\in\mathcal{G}
such that
x_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}x_{\mathcal{A}_{2}}
divides
x^{a}
, since
\mathrm{in}_{<}(x_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}x_{\mathcal{A}_{2}}-x_{\mathcal{A}_{1}\cap\mathcal{A}_{2}}x_{\mathcal{A}_{1}\cup\mathcal{A}_{2}})=x_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}x_{\mathcal{A}_{2}}
. Without loss of generality we can assume that
\gcd(x^{a},x^{b})=1
. Let
\mathcal{B}[x^{a}]\coloneqq\bigcup_{a_{\mathcal{B}_{i}}\neq 0}\mathcal{B}_{i}
. Since
x^{a}-x^{b}\in I_{\mathrm{CBP}(G)}
, we have
\deg(x^{a})=\deg(x^{b})
and
\mathcal{B}[x^{a}]=\mathcal{B}[x^{b}]
. In particular,
G[\mathcal{B}[x^{a}]]
and
G[\mathcal{B}[x^{b}]]
have the same connected components. Suppose that there is no element in
\mathcal{G}
whose initial term divides
x^{a}
. If there were two sets of blocks
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{1},\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{2}\in\tilde{\mathcal{B}}
with
a_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{1}},a_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{2}}\neq 0
such that
G[\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{1}\cup\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{2}]
is connected, and
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{1}\nsubseteq\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{2}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{2}\nsubseteq\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{1}
, then
x_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{1}}x_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{2}}-x_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{1}\cap\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{2}}x_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{1}\cup\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{2}}\in\mathcal{G}
and
\mathrm{in}_{<_{\mathrm{rev}}}(x_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{1}}x_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{2}}-x_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{1}\cap\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{2}}x_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{1}\cup\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{2}})=x_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{1}}x_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{2}}
divides
x^{a}
. Hence, assume that for all
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{1},\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{2}\in\tilde{\mathcal{B}}
with
a_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{1}},a_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{2}}\neq 0
and
G[\mathcal{A}_{1}\cup\mathcal{A}_{2}]
connected, we have
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{2}
or
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{2}\subseteq\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{1}
. Then we have
a_{\mathcal{B}[K]}\neq 0
for every connected component
K
of
G[\mathcal{B}[x^{a}]]=G[\mathcal{B}[x^{a}]]
for the following reason. Suppose
a_{\mathcal{B}[K]}=0
and let
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{B}[K]
be an inclusion-maximal set with
a_{\mathcal{A}}\neq 0
. By definition of
K
, for every block
B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}[K]
, there has to be an
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\in\tilde{\mathcal{B}}
, with
a_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime}}\neq 0
, that contains
B^{\prime}
. In particular there is an
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
with
a_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime}}\neq 0
, that contains a block of
\mathcal{B}[K]\setminus\mathcal{A}
that shares a common vertex with a block of
\mathcal{A}
. Hence,
G[\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{A}^{\prime}]
is connected and this implies
\mathcal{A}\subsetneq\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
which is a contradiction to
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{B}[K]
being inclusion-maximal such that
a_{\mathcal{A}}\neq 0
. Since
\gcd(x^{a},x^{b})=1
, we have
b_{\mathcal{B}[K]}=0
and since
G[\mathcal{B}[x^{b}]]
has the same components, for every
\mathcal{A}\in\tilde{\mathcal{B}}
with
b_{\mathcal{A}}\neq 0
there exists an
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\in\tilde{\mathcal{B}}
with
\mathcal{A}\subsetneq\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
and
a_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime}}\neq 0
. But because of
\deg(x^{a})=\deg(x^{b})
this means that
\mathrm{in}_{<}(x^{a}-x^{b})=x^{b}
, which is a contradiction to the assumption that
x^{a}
is the initial term of
x^{a}-x^{b}
.

Since
\mathcal{G}
is square free, it follows from
[
Stu96
, Corollary 8.9]
that
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
has a regular unimodular triangulation.
∎

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
G
be a connected graph and
\mathcal{B}
its set of blocks. Then the connected blocks polytope
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
is Gorenstein of index
2
.

Proof.

We have to show that the dual polytope of
2\cdot\mathrm{CBP}(G)-\mathbf{1}
is a lattice polytope. By
Theorem

5.11
we know that all inequalities defining
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
are either of the form
x_{B}\geq 0
for some
B\in\mathcal{B}
or independent blocks inequalities. Then the inequalities defining
2\cdot\mathrm{CBP}(G)
are either of the form
x_{B}\geq 0
or
\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}}\alpha_{B}x_{B}\leq 2
, where there exists an independent set of blocks
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{B}
such that
(\mathcal{I},\alpha)
defines an independent blocks inequality. Thus the inequalities describing
2\cdot\mathrm{CBP}(G)-\mathbf{1}
are of the form
x_{B}+1\geq 0
and
\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}}\alpha_{B}(x_{B}+1)\leq 2
.
The first inequality is equivalent to
-x_{B}\leq 1
and the second inequality is equivalent to
\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}}\alpha_{B}x_{B}\leq 1
, since
\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}}\alpha_{B}=1
. Since all coefficients are integral, this shows that
2\cdot\mathrm{CBP}(G)
is a reflexive polytope by
[
Zie94
, Theorem 2.11]
.
∎

It is well known due to Stanley that a result like
Theorem

7.2
is equivalent to the following statement.

Corollary 7.3
.

Let
G
be a connected graph and
\mathcal{B}
its set of blocks. The
h^{\ast}
-vector of
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
is of the form
(h_{0}^{\ast},h_{1}^{\ast},h_{2}^{\ast},\ldots,h_{n-1}^{\ast},h_{n}^{\ast})
, where
n=|\mathcal{B}|
,

\displaystyle h_{n}^{\ast}

\displaystyle=0,\text{ and}

\displaystyle h^{*}_{i}

\displaystyle=h^{\ast}_{n-i-1}\text{ for }i=0,\ldots,\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor.

Furthermore, Theorem 1 of
[
BR07
]
implies that the
h^{\ast}
-vector of a Gorenstein lattice polytope with a regular unimodular triangulation is unimodal. Hence, from
Theorems

7.1
and
7.2
we get:

Corollary 7.4
.

Let
G
be a connected graph. The
h^{\ast}
-vector of
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
is unimodal.

Theorem

7.6
shows the explicit description of the
h^{\ast}
-vector for the connected blocks polytope of a block path of length
n
, which is given by the
n
-th Narayana polynomial. To this end we define:

Definition 7.5
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
and
1\leq k\leq n
the Narayana number
N(n,k)
is defined by

\displaystyle N(n,k)\coloneqq\frac{1}{n}\binom{n}{k}\binom{n}{k-1}.

The
n
-th Narayana polynomial is given by

\displaystyle N_{n}(x)\coloneqq\sum_{k=1}^{n}N(n,k)x^{k-1}.

Theorem 7.6
.

Let
G^{\prime}
be a block path of length
n
. Then the
h^{\ast}
-polynomial of
\mathrm{CBP}(G^{\prime})
is given by the
n
-th Narayana polynomial.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{B}=\{B_{1},\ldots,B_{n}\}
be the set of blocks of
G^{\prime}
, where
B_{i}
and
B_{i+1}
have a common vertex for
i\in[n-1]
. In
[
Bra15
]
, Braun showed that the
h^{\ast}
-polynomial of

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{n}\coloneqq\mathop{\rm conv}\nolimits\{\mathbf{0},\textbf{e}_{i}-\textbf{e}_{j}\mid n\geq i>j\geq 1\},

where
\textbf{e}_{i}
denotes the
i
-th standard unit vector, is given by the
n-1
-st Narayana polynomial (in
[
Bra15
]
it is claimed that
h^{\ast}(\mathcal{P}_{n},z)=N_{n}(z)
, however, the correct statement is
h^{\ast}(\mathcal{P},z)=N_{n-1}(z)
). We will show that
\mathrm{CBP}(G^{\prime})
and
\mathcal{P}_{n+1}
are unimodular equivalent. Our connected blocks polytope is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{CBP}(G^{\prime})=\mathop{\rm conv}\nolimits\{\mathbf{0},\chi^{\{B_{k},\ldots,B_{\ell}\}}\mid 1\leq k\leq\ell\leq n\}.

\mathcal{P}_{n+1}
lies in the
n
-dimensional subspace
V\coloneqq\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\mid\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}=0\}
. We define the linear map
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{B}}\to V
by

\displaystyle\varphi(\chi^{\{B_{i}\}})\coloneqq\textbf{e}_{i+1}-\textbf{e}_{i},

for all
i\in[n]
. Then
\varphi
is a unimodular transformation that maps
\mathrm{CBP}(G^{\prime})
to
\mathcal{P}_{n+1}
. Thus,
\mathrm{CBP}(G^{\prime})
and
\mathcal{P}_{n+1}
have the same
h^{\ast}
-polynomial.
∎

We finish by giving a lower bound for
h_{1}^{\ast}
:

Theorem 7.7
.

Let
G
be a connected graph and
\mathcal{B}
its set of blocks. Then,

\displaystyle h_{1}^{\ast}\geq\dim\mathrm{CBP}(G)-1

and
h^{\ast}_{1}=\dim\mathrm{CBP}(G)-1
if and only if
|\mathcal{B}|\leq 2
.

Proof.

Let
V(\mathrm{CBP}(G))
denote the set of vertices of
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
. We have

\displaystyle h^{\ast}_{1}=|V(\mathrm{CBP}(G))|-(\dim(\mathrm{CBP}(G))+1).

Hence, we count the vertices of
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
. Let
\mathcal{B}=\{B_{1},\ldots,B_{n}\}
. Without loss of generality we can assume that
G[\{B_{1},\ldots,B_{i}\}]
is connected for all
i\in[n]
. Then the incidence vectors
\chi^{\{B_{1},\ldots,B_{i}\}}
yield
|\mathcal{B}|
many vertices of
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
. Additionally, there are the vertices
\chi^{\emptyset}
and
\chi^{B}
for all
B\in\mathcal{B}\backslash\{B_{1}\}
. Due to
Proposition

4.1
,
|V(\mathrm{CBP}(G))|\geq 2\cdot\dim(\mathrm{CBP}(G))
and it follows
h_{1}^{\ast}\geq\dim\mathrm{CBP}(G)-1
. For
|\mathcal{B}|\leq 2
it is clear that these are all the vertices of
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
, which shows
h_{1}^{\ast}=\dim\mathrm{CBP}(G)-1
if
|\mathcal{B}|\leq 2
. For
|\mathcal{B}|\geq 3
we know that
B_{3}
shares a common node with
B_{1}
or
B_{2}
by choice of the numbering of
B_{1},\ldots,B_{n}
. Hence,
\chi^{\{B_{1}\cup B_{3}\}}
or
\chi^{\{B_{2}\cup B_{3}\}}
is another vertex of
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
. Hence,
h_{1}^{\ast}>\dim\mathrm{CBP}(G)-1
.
∎

For a palindromic
h^{\ast}
-polynomial the so-called
\gamma
-vector is defined by the coefficients of the
h^{\ast}
-polynomial after a specific change of basis in the polynomial ring
\mathbb{R}[x]
, and we have
\gamma_{1}=h^{\ast}_{1}-d
, where
d
denotes the degree of the
h^{\ast}
-polynomial. For a connected graph
G
, it directly follows from
Theorem

7.7
that the
\gamma
-vector of
\mathrm{CBP}(G)
has the property that
\gamma_{1}\geq 0
and
\gamma_{1}=0
if and only if
|\mathcal{B}|\leq 2
, where
\mathcal{B}
denotes the set of blocks of
G
.

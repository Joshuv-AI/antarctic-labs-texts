On the conservation laws and the structure of the nonlinearity for SQG and its generalizations
Philip Isett, Andrew Ma

Abstract
Using a new definition for the nonlinear term, we prove that all weak solutions to the SQG equation (and mSQG) conserve the angular momentum. This result is new for the weak solutions of [Resnick, ’95] and rules out the possibility of anomalous dissipation of angular momentum. We also prove conservation of the Hamiltonian under conjecturally optimal assumptions, sharpening a well-known criterion of [Cheskidov-Constantin-Friedlander-Shvydkoy, ’08]. Moreover, we show that our new estimate for the nonlinearity is optimal and that it characterizes the mSQG nonlinearity uniquely among active scalar nonlinearities with a scaling symmetry.

Many important models in fluid dynamics are active scalar equations, which

Of equal importance is the Hamiltonian of the flow, which exists in 2 dimensions when the multiplier is angular and odd. For the mSQG equations, the Hamiltonian is given up to a constant by

Theorem 1
.

Every weak solution to the mSQG equation (

There appears to be no precedent for this type of criterion for angular momentum conservation in the literature, and the proof is quite different from the better-studied conservation of energy. Optimality of the definition

With these definitions in hand, the final question we wish to address in this work is:
Is our definition of the nonlinearity the best possible definition?
To compare different definitions, there are four criteria one can consider:

1.

Which definition applies at the lowest possible regularity for
\theta
?

2.

Which definition yields the best possible estimate for the nonlinearity?

3.

Which definition yields the best possible continuity properties for the nonlinearity?

4.

Which definition yields a continuous extension to the largest possible space of test functions?

Regarding the first point, it will be shown in the course of proving Theorem
6
that the nonlinearity does not make sense for
\theta

\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}

5

6

require at least

\Psi(x)=|x|^{2}\log|x|^{2}=2|x|^{2}\log|x|\in\mathring{W}^{2,\infty}
.

Two open questions related to this theorem are the following: First, is the space of
C_{c}^{\infty}
functions dense in
\mathring{W}^{2,\infty}
in the weak-* topology? (If so, the weak-* continuity and boundedness properties would uniquely characterize the continuous extension of the nonlinearity.) Likewise, is the space
\mathring{W}^{2,\infty}
complete in the sense of a non-Hausdorff space? That is, given
\Psi_{n}
Cauchy in the semi-norm, does there exist
\Psi\in\mathring{W}^{2,\infty}
that is the (non-unique) limit of
\Psi_{n}
? Completeness is true for the space
\dot{W}^{2,\infty}
(see the Appendix), but it is not obvious whether completeness holds in
\mathring{W}^{2,\infty}
.

1.2
Definitions of basic function spaces

For
X
a Banach space of distributions on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
or
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we denote by
L_{t}^{p}X
to be the space of distributions
u
such that
u
is strongly measurable in time with values in
X
and the norm
\|\|u(t)\|_{X}\|_{L^{p}}
is finite.

The Banach space
\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}
is defined and its properties are studied in detail in the Appendix.

We define Littlewood-Paley projections with the conventions that
P_{\leq 0}
has a Fourier multiplier
\hat{\chi}_{\leq 0}(\xi)
that equals
1
in
|\xi|\leq 1/2
and has compact support in
|\xi|\leq 1
. With this convention
P_{\leq q}
has Fourier multiplier
\hat{\chi}_{\leq q}\equiv\hat{\chi}_{\leq 0}(2^{-q}\xi)
and we define
P_{q}\equiv P_{\leq q+1}-P_{\leq q}
, which has a Fourier multiplier
\hat{\chi}_{q}
localized to
2^{q-1}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{q+1}
.

We say that a distribution
u
belongs to the homogeneous Besov space
\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{p,c(\mathbb{N})}
if there exists a sequence of
C_{c}^{\infty}
functions
u_{n}
such that
\|u-u_{n}\|_{\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{p,c(\mathbb{N})}}\to 0
, where

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{p,c(\mathbb{N})}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{q}2^{\alpha q}\|P_{q}u\|_{L^{p}}.

Distributions in this class inherit properties from test functions such as
\|P_{q}u\|_{L^{p}}=o(2^{-\alpha q})
as
q\to\infty
.

2
Hamiltonian conservation: The test function step

The first step in the proof of Hamiltonian conservation is to justify multiplying the equation by a test function that is not necessarily smooth with compact support. This step is less technical on the torus as opposed to the full Euclidean space, so we concentrate on the
\mathbb{T}^{2}
case first. We explain the additional technicalities for the Euclidean case in Section
3
.

Let
\theta
be an mSQG solution with
\delta\in[0,1/2]
fixed and
\theta\in L_{t}^{3}\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}
with
\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\theta\in L^{3}_{t}B^{\alpha}_{3,\infty}
for
\alpha\geq(1+\delta)/3
. Note that on the torus the assumption
\theta\in L_{t}^{3}\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}
follows from our other assumption on
\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\theta
. Indeed, if
\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\theta\in L^{3}_{t}B^{\alpha}_{3,\infty}
for
\alpha>0
then
\theta\in L^{3}_{t}\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}_{x}
due to the Littlewood-Paley inequality

\|\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}\sim\sum_{q\geq 0}\|2^{(-1+\delta)q}P_{q}\theta\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\lesssim\sum_{q\geq 0}\left(2^{-\alpha q}\|\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\theta\|_{B^{\alpha}_{3,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\right)^{2}\lesssim\|\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\theta\|_{B^{\alpha}_{3,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}

We refer to Section
1.2
for the definitions of the operators
P_{q}
and the Besov space
B^{\alpha}_{3,\infty}
.

Define
\phi(t)\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
to be a radial bump function of unit integral and let
\phi_{\tau}(t)=\tau^{-1}\phi(\tau^{-1}t)
be a mollifying kernel in time. In addition, let
\eta(t)\in C_{c}^{\infty}(I)
be a test function in time. Define the test function

\psi_{\hat{q},\tau}\coloneqq\left(\eta(t)\left(P_{\leq\hat{q}}^{2}\Lambda^{2(-1+\delta)}\theta\right)_{\tau}\right)_{\tau}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})

where
(\cdot)_{\tau}
denotes mollification in time with
\phi_{\tau}
.

We may safely apply
\psi_{\hat{q},\tau}
as a test function to mSQG in order to obtain

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\theta\partial_{t}\psi_{\hat{q},\tau}\,dxdt-\int B[\psi_{\hat{q},\tau}(t),\theta(t),\theta(t)]dt=0

(7)

where
B[\psi_{\hat{q},\tau},\theta,\theta]\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}u^{l}\theta\nabla_{l}\psi_{\hat{q},\tau}\,\textrm{`}dx\textrm{'}
is the nonlinear term defined in Definition
1.3
. Using the self-adjointness of
P_{\leq\hat{q}}^{2}
and
(\cdot)_{\tau}
, we have that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}}\eta^{\prime}(t)\cdot\left|\left(\Lambda^{-1+\delta}P_{\leq\hat{q}}\theta\right)_{\tau}\right|^{2}\,dxdt

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\theta\partial_{t}\psi_{\hat{q},\tau}\,dxdt

By the definition of a weak solution, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}}\eta^{\prime}(t)\cdot\left|\left(\Lambda^{-1+\delta}P_{\leq\hat{q}}\theta\right)_{\tau}\right|^{2}\,dxdt=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}}u^{l}\theta\nabla_{l}\left(\eta\left(P_{\leq\hat{q}}^{2}\Lambda^{2(-1+\delta)}\theta\right)_{\tau}\right)_{\tau}\,\textrm{`}dxdt\textrm{'}

(8)

where the formal integral on the right hand side is interpreted in terms of the linear-quadratic form for mSQG as

\displaystyle\int B\left[\left(\eta\left(P_{\leq\hat{q}}^{2}\Lambda^{2(-1+\delta)}\theta\right)_{\tau}\right)_{\tau},\theta(t),\theta(t)\right]dt

We would like to take a limit of
\tau\rightarrow 0
of both sides of (
8
). To do this we use the next Lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

\psi_{\hat{q},\tau}\longrightarrow\eta(t)\Lambda^{2(-1+\delta)}P_{\leq\hat{q}}^{2}\theta\eqqcolon\psi_{\hat{q}}\text{ in }L^{3}_{t}\dot{W}^{2,\infty}\text{ as }\tau\rightarrow 0

Proof.

For each fixed
\hat{q}
we note that
\Lambda^{2(-1+\delta)}P^{2}_{\leq\hat{q}}:L^{3}_{t}\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}_{x}\longrightarrow L^{3}_{t}\dot{W}^{2,\infty}
is a bounded linear operator with an operator norm that depends on
\hat{q}
. So

\|\psi_{\hat{q},\tau}-\psi_{\hat{q}}\|_{L^{3}_{t}\dot{W}^{2,\infty}}=\left\|\Lambda^{2(-1+\delta)}P_{\leq\hat{q}}^{2}\left((\eta(t)\theta_{\tau})_{\tau}-\eta(t)\theta\right)\right\|_{L^{3}_{t}\dot{W}^{2,\infty}}\lesssim_{\hat{q}}\left\|(\eta(t)\theta_{\tau})_{\tau}-\eta(t)\theta\right\|_{L^{3}_{t}\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}_{x}}

Hence, it is enough to show

\lim_{\tau\rightarrow 0}\left\|(\eta(t)\theta_{\tau})_{\tau}-\eta(t)\theta\right\|_{L^{3}_{t}\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}_{x}}=0

We proceed via an approximation argument. Define
\psi\coloneqq\eta(t)\theta
. Banach valued simple functions are dense in
L^{3}_{t}\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}_{x}

[
21
]
and
[
38
]
. Using this face, we can approximate
\psi
in the
L^{3}_{t}\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}_{x}
-norm by a smooth in time approximation
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))

[
38
, Chapter 4, Theorem 4.11]
. Given an
\epsilon>0
, take
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))
such that

\left\|\theta-f\right\|_{L^{3}_{t}\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}_{x}}<\epsilon
. Define
\psi_{\tau,\tau}\coloneqq\left(\eta(t)\theta_{\tau}\right)_{\tau}
and
f_{\tau,\tau}\coloneqq\left(\eta(t)f_{\tau}\right)_{\tau}
and consider the inequality

\displaystyle\left\|\psi_{\tau,\tau}-\psi\right\|_{L^{3}_{t}\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\psi_{\tau,\tau}-f_{\tau,\tau}\right\|_{L^{3}_{t}\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}_{x}}+\left\|f_{\tau,\tau}-\eta f\right\|_{L^{3}_{t}\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}_{x}}+\left\|\eta f-\psi\right\|_{L^{3}_{t}\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}_{x}}

(9)

\displaystyle\coloneqq I+II+III

We will discuss how to estimate each term on the RHS of (
9
). Beginning with
I
,
\left\|\psi_{\tau,\tau}-f_{\tau,\tau}\right\|_{L^{3}_{t}\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}_{x}}<\epsilon

due to a modified argument of the proof that convolutions are bounded linear operators in
L^{p}
-norms.

For
II
,
\lim_{\tau\rightarrow 0}\left\|f_{\tau,\tau}-\eta f\right\|_{L^{3}_{t}\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}_{x}}=0

by a dominated convergence argument and observing that for sufficiently small
\tau
, the family of functions
\{f_{\tau,\tau}\}
can yield a sequence of uniformly bounded functions converging to
\eta f
in
\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}
pointwise uniformly in time.

Finally for
III
,
\left\|\eta f-\psi\right\|_{L^{3}_{t}\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}_{x}}\leq\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\left\|f-\theta\right\|_{L^{3}_{t}\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}_{x}}<\epsilon

by Young’s inequality and our assumption
\left\|f-\theta\right\|_{L^{3}_{t}\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}_{x}}<\epsilon
. By combining the estimates of
I,II
, and
III
and taking a limit of (
9
) we get

\limsup_{\tau\rightarrow 0}\left\|\psi_{\tau,\tau}-\psi\right\|_{L^{3}_{t}\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}_{x}}\leq\limsup_{\tau\rightarrow 0}\left(2\epsilon+\left\|f_{\tau,\tau}-\eta f\right\|_{L^{3}_{t}\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}_{x}}\right)\leq 2\epsilon
. Since this inequality holds for any
\epsilon>0
we conclude that

\lim_{\tau\rightarrow 0}\left\|\psi_{\tau,\tau}-\psi\right\|_{L^{3}_{t}\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}_{x}}=0

∎

Next, by the assumption
\theta\in L^{3}_{t}B^{\alpha}_{3,\infty}\subseteq L^{3}_{t}\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}_{x}
, Lemma
2.1
, and the bound on the nonlinear term given
in Theorem
3
we may safely take a limit of
\tau\rightarrow 0
of line (
8
). Formally, the identity we have established is

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\eta^{\prime}\left(P_{\leq\hat{q}}\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\theta\right)^{2}dxdt=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}u^{l}\theta\nabla_{l}\left(\eta P_{\leq\hat{q}}^{2}\Lambda^{2(-1+\delta)}\theta\right)\textrm{`dxdt'}

(10)

where in the line above we define

\displaystyle\psi_{\hat{q}}\coloneqq\eta(t)P_{\leq\hat{q}}^{2}\Lambda^{2(-1+\delta)}\theta.

(11)

Rigorously the right hand side of (
10
) must be interpreted as

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}u^{l}\theta\nabla_{l}\left(\eta P_{\leq\hat{q}}^{2}\Lambda^{2(-1+\delta)}\theta\right)\textrm{`dxdt'}\coloneqq\int B(\psi_{\hat{q}}(t),\theta(t),\theta(t))dt

where
B
is the linear-quadratic form that is bounded on
\dot{W}^{2,\infty}\times\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}\times\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}
.

We observe that the left hand side of (
10
) is an approximation of the weak derivative of the Hamiltonian,
\frac{d}{dt}H(t)
, against the smooth bump function in time
-\eta(t)
and we observe that, by dominated convergence,

\lim_{\hat{q}\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\eta^{\prime}\left(P_{\leq\hat{q}}\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\theta\right)^{2}dxdt=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\eta^{\prime}\left(\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\theta\right)^{2}dxdt

Our goal is thus to show

\lim_{\hat{q}\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\eta^{\prime}(t)\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\left(P_{\leq\hat{q}}\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\theta\right)^{2}dxdt=0
. In other words, we would like to prove that the RHS of (
10
) obeys the limit

\displaystyle\lim_{\hat{q}\rightarrow\infty}\int B(\psi_{\hat{q}}(t),\theta(t),\theta(t))dt=0

(12)

To investigate this limit we will make use of an approximating sequence.

3
The test function step in Euclidean space

In this section we show how to generalize the analysis of Section
2
from the torus to the case of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The reader interested mainly in the periodic case may wish to skip to Section
4
upon first reading.

We assume
\theta\in L_{t}^{3}\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}
. We start by observing that, as in the periodic case

\displaystyle\int\eta^{\prime}(t)\int\left|\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\theta(t,x)\right|^{2}dxdt

\displaystyle=\lim_{\hat{q}\to\infty}\lim_{\tau\to 0}\int\eta^{\prime}(t)\int\left|P_{\leq\hat{q}}^{2}(\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\theta(t,x))_{\tau}\right|^{2}dxdt

(13)

by a simple approximation argument using density of
\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}
-valued simple functions of time.

We now deviate from the periodic case to write

(
13
)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\hat{q}\to\infty}\lim_{\tau\to 0}\lim_{m\to\infty}\int\eta^{\prime}(t)\int\left|P_{(-m,\hat{q}]}(\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\theta(t,x))_{\tau}\right|^{2}dxdt

(14)

where
P_{(-m,\hat{q}]}\coloneqq P_{\leq\hat{q}}^{2}-P_{\leq-m}
is a truncation of
P_{\leq\hat{q}}^{2}
. Again we have applied dominated convergence using that
\theta\in L_{t}^{2}\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}
.

Our next step is to introduce a physical space cutoff
\chi_{R}(x)=\chi(x/R)
, where
\chi(x)=1
on the unit ball and
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}
. We claim that

(
14
)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\hat{q}\to\infty}\lim_{\tau\to 0}\lim_{m\to\infty}\lim_{R\to\infty}\int\eta^{\prime}(t)\int\left|P_{(-m,\hat{q}]}\Lambda^{-1+\delta}[\chi_{R}\theta(t,x)_{\tau}]\right|^{2}dxdt

(15)

To prove this claim, we decompose the function on the right hand side as

\displaystyle P_{(-m,\hat{q}]}\Lambda^{2(-1+\delta)}[\chi_{R}\theta_{\tau}]

\displaystyle=I+II+III+IV

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=P_{(-m,\hat{q}]}\Lambda^{2(-1+\delta)}[P_{\leq-m-2}\chi_{R}P_{\leq-m-2}\theta_{\tau}]

\displaystyle II

\displaystyle=P_{(-m,\hat{q}]}\Lambda^{2(-1+\delta)}[P_{(-m-2,\hat{q}+2]}\chi_{R}P_{\leq-m-2}\theta_{\tau}]

\displaystyle III

\displaystyle=P_{(-m,\hat{q}]}\Lambda^{2(-1+\delta)}[P_{\leq\hat{q}+2}\chi_{R}P_{(-m-2,\hat{q}+2]}\theta_{\tau}]

\displaystyle IV

\displaystyle=P_{(-m,\hat{q}]}\Lambda^{2(-1+\delta)}\sum_{k\geq\hat{q}+1}[P_{k+1}\chi_{R}P_{k}\theta_{\tau}+P_{k}\chi_{R}P_{k+1}\theta_{\tau}+P_{k+1}\chi_{R}P_{k+1}\theta_{\tau}]

Note that
IV
contains all the terms involving high frequencies since, if
k\geq\hat{q}+1
, we have that
P_{k+1}\chi_{R}P_{n}\theta_{\tau}
and
P_{k+1}\theta_{\tau}P_{n}\theta_{\tau}
have frequency support disjoint from that of
P_{(-m,\hat{q}]}^{2}
for
n<k-1
.

By frequency support considerations
I=0
, so it suffices to take the limit in
L^{2}
of the other terms.

We start by estimating
II
. All estimates are taken at a fixed time

\displaystyle\|II(t)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{m,\hat{q}}\|P_{(-m-2,\hat{q}+2]}\chi_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|P_{\leq-m-2}\theta_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{m,\hat{q}}\|P_{(-m-2,\hat{q}+2]}\chi_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\theta_{\tau}(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}}

Furthermore, as
R\to\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\|P_{(-m-2,\hat{q}+2]}\chi_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{m,\hat{q}}\|\nabla\chi_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}}\to 0\qquad\mbox{ as }R\to\infty

and we conclude
II
converges to
0
in
L^{2}_{x}
at any time
t
, with
L^{2}
norms bounded uniformly by a square-integrable function of
t
.

We now consider the limit of
III
, which is the only term that does not vanish in the limit. As
\hat{q}
is fixed, the functions

\displaystyle P_{\leq\hat{q}+2}\chi_{R}(x)

\displaystyle=\int\chi_{R}(y)\eta_{\leq\hat{q}+2}(x-y)dy

are uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}
and converge pointwise (but not uniformly) in
x
to
1=\int\eta_{\leq\hat{q}+2}dy
as
R\to\infty
. Using dominted convergence and the
L^{2}
boundedness of
P_{(-m,\hat{q}]}\Lambda^{2(-1+\delta)}
, it follows that

\displaystyle III(t)

\displaystyle\to P_{(-m,\hat{q}]}\Lambda^{2(-1+\delta)}P_{(-m-2,\hat{q}+2]}\theta_{\tau}=P_{(-m,\hat{q}]}\Lambda^{2(-1+\delta)}\theta_{\tau}

in
L^{2}_{x}
for any time
t
, with
L^{2}
norms bounded uniformly by a square-integrable function of
t
.

Finally we estimate
IV
as follows. Using the fact that
P_{(-m,\hat{q}]}\Lambda^{2(-1+\delta)}
is bounded on
L^{2}
we have

\displaystyle\|IV\|_{L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{m,\hat{q}}\sum_{k\geq\hat{q}}\|P_{\approx k}\chi_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|P_{\approx k}\theta_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{m,\hat{q}}\sum_{k\geq\hat{q}}\|P_{\approx k}\chi_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}}2^{(1-\delta)k}\|\Lambda^{-1+\delta}P_{\approx k}\theta_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{m,\hat{q}}\sum_{k\geq\hat{q}}2^{-3k}\|\nabla^{3}\chi_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}}2^{(1-\delta)k}\|\theta_{\tau}(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}}

Since
\nabla^{3}\chi_{R}\to 0
uniformly, we have that
IV
converges to
0
in
L_{x}^{2}
at any time
t
, with
L^{2}
norms uniformly bounded by a square-integrable function of
t
.

This analysis together with the dominated convergence theorem justifies the claim (
15
). We now observe that, for each fixed value of
\hat{q},\tau,m
and
R
, we have

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int\eta^{\prime}(t)\int

\displaystyle\left|P_{(-m,\hat{q}]}(\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\chi_{R}\theta(t,x))_{\tau}\right|^{2}dxdt=-\int\theta\partial_{t}\psi_{\hat{q},\tau,m,R}dxdt

(16)

\displaystyle\psi_{\hat{q},\tau,m,R}

\displaystyle=\chi_{R}\left(\eta P_{(-m,\hat{q}]}^{2}\Lambda^{2(-1+\delta)}(\chi_{R}\theta_{\tau})\right)_{\tau}.

(17)

using the self-adjointness of both the mollification operators involved and of multiplication by
\chi_{R}
. Note that
\psi_{\hat{q},\tau,m,R}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
is a smooth test function with compact support, which enables us to apply the definition of a weak solution to write the right hand side as

(
17
)

\displaystyle=\int B(\psi_{\hat{q},\tau,m,R}(t),\theta(t),\theta(t))dt.

We now aim to take the limits as
R\to\infty,m\to\infty
and
\tau\to\infty
in that order. We first claim that

\displaystyle\psi_{\hat{q},\tau,m,R}\to\psi_{\hat{q},\tau,m}\coloneqq\left(\eta P_{(-m,\hat{q}]}\Lambda^{2(-1+\delta)}(\theta_{\tau})\right)_{\tau}\qquad\mbox{ in }L_{t}^{3}\dot{W}^{2,\infty},

(18)

which is sufficient given that
\theta(t)\in L_{t}^{3}\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}
and the
\dot{W}^{2,\infty}\times\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}\times\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}

B
.

To take this limit, first observe that the convergence

\displaystyle\eta P_{(-m,\hat{q}]}\Lambda^{2(-1+\delta)}(\chi_{R}\theta_{\tau})\to\eta P_{(-m,\hat{q}]}\Lambda^{2(-1+\delta)}(\theta_{\tau})

(19)

holds in
L_{t}^{3}L_{x}^{2}
by the same argument used to take the

limit in (
15
). The convergence also holds in the

norm by the boundedness of the frequency localization operator
P_{(-m,\hat{q}]}=P_{(-m-2,\hat{q}+2]}P_{(-m,\hat{q}]}
.

Recalling that the
\dot{W}^{2,\infty}
semi-norm is
\|\nabla^{2}f\|_{L^{\infty}}
, it is not hard to check that the second mollification in time preserves the
L_{t}^{3}\dot{W}^{2,\infty}
convergence, as does multiplying by
\chi_{R}
and letting
R\to\infty
. This observation suffices to take the
R\to\infty
limit in (
18
).

We now study the
m\to\infty
limit where we re-insert the low frequencies. Observe that, by the Berenstein inequality,

\displaystyle\|\psi_{\hat{q},\tau,m}(t)-\psi_{\hat{q},\tau}(t)\|_{\dot{W}^{2,\infty}}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla^{2}P_{\leq-m}\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\theta_{\tau}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-2m/2}\|\nabla^{2}P_{\leq-m}\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\theta_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-m}\|\nabla^{2}P_{\leq-m}\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\theta_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-m}2^{-(1+\delta)m}\|\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\theta_{\tau}(t)\|_{L^{2}}

The bound for
\|\nabla^{2}P_{\leq-m}\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}
used in the last line can be shown by a dyadic decomposition and summing a geometric series. This estimate and an application of the dominated convergence theorem suffice for the
L_{t}^{3}\dot{W}^{2,\infty}
convergence. (In the case
\delta=0
of
2D
Euler, we employ the bound
\|\Lambda^{-1}\theta_{\tau}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|v^{i}\|_{L^{2}}\approx\|\theta_{\tau}(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}
, where

is the Helmholtz solution to
\nabla_{i}v^{i}=\theta_{\tau}
, and we use that
\Lambda^{-1}\nabla_{i}
is
L^{2}
bounded. See Appendix Section
A.2
.)

Next we claim that the convergence

\displaystyle\psi_{\hat{q},\tau}=\left(\eta P_{\leq\hat{q}}^{2}\Lambda^{2(-1+\delta)}(\theta_{\tau})\right)_{\tau}\to\psi_{\hat{q}}=\eta P_{\leq\hat{q}}^{2}\Lambda^{2(-1+\delta)}\theta\quad\mbox{in }L_{t}^{3}\dot{W}^{2,\infty}\mbox{ as }\tau\to 0

for
\theta\in L_{t}^{3}\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}
. The proof follows a similar line of reasoning as in Section
2
, but we now use the fact that
\nabla^{2}P_{\leq\hat{q}}^{2}\Lambda^{-1+\delta}
is bounded from
L_{t}^{3}L^{2}_{x}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
to
L_{t}^{3}L^{\infty}_{x}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
, which again can be proved by a dyadic decomposition.

We now proceed with the proof of Hamiltonian conservation, which is primarily a high frequency analysis.

4
Conservation of the Hamiltonian

We now return to the setting of
\mathbb{T}^{d}
periodic solutions and explain the modifications needed in the nonperiodic setting in Section
4.1
.

By the convention laid out in Section
1.2
,
P_{\leq q}
has a multiplier that equals
1
for
|\xi|\leq 2^{q-1}
and is supported in
|\xi|<2^{q}
. Consequently,
P_{q}\equiv P_{\leq q+1}-P_{\leq q}
has a multiplier supported in
2^{q-1}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{q+1}
.

Let
\theta_{N}=P_{\leq N}\theta
and
\psi_{\hat{q}}\equiv\Delta^{-1+\delta}P_{\leq\hat{q}}^{2}\theta
(recall that
\Delta=\Lambda^{2}
). By the boundedness of
B
on
\dot{W}^{2,\infty}\times\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}\times\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}
, and by dominated convergence, we have that

\displaystyle\int B(\psi_{\hat{q}}(t),\theta(t),\theta(t))dt=\lim_{N\to\infty}\int B(\psi_{\hat{q}}(t),\theta_{N}(t),\theta_{N}(t))dt

(20)

However, the right hand side (at least in the case of the torus) is a classical integral. Specifically, we are looking at

\displaystyle B_{\hat{q}N}=B(\psi_{\hat{q}};\theta_{N},\theta_{N})

\displaystyle\equiv\int\eta(t)\int T^{\ell}(\theta_{N})\theta_{N}\nabla_{\ell}\psi_{\hat{q}}dxdt

(21)

\displaystyle=\int\eta(t)\int\nabla_{\ell}(-\Delta)^{-1+\delta}P_{\leq\hat{q}}^{2}\theta P_{\leq N}\theta\epsilon^{\ell a}\nabla_{a}(-\Delta)^{-1+\delta}P_{\leq N}\theta dxdt

(22)

We can assume that the mean value
\Pi_{0}\theta
of
\theta_{N}
is zero, for in general we may write
\theta=\Pi_{0}\theta+\Pi_{\neq 0}\theta
, where
T^{\ell}(\Pi_{0}\theta)=0
(since the multiplier is odd), and

\displaystyle B(\psi_{\hat{q}};\theta_{N},\theta_{N})

\displaystyle=\int\eta(t)\int T^{\ell}(\Pi_{\neq 0}\theta_{N})\Pi_{0}\theta_{N}\nabla_{\ell}\psi_{\hat{q}}dxdt+\int\eta(t)\int T^{\ell}(\Pi_{\neq 0}\theta_{N})\Pi_{\neq 0}\theta_{N}\nabla_{\ell}\psi_{\hat{q}}dxdt.

(23)

The first integral vanishes as
\Pi_{0}\theta_{N}
is constant,
T^{\ell}(\Pi_{\neq 0}\theta_{N})
is divergence free, and there is no boundary.

The part of (
22
) from
\epsilon^{\ell a}\nabla_{\ell}(-\Delta)^{-1+\delta}P_{\leq\hat{q}}^{2}\theta\nabla_{a}(-\Delta)^{-1+\delta}P_{\leq\hat{q}}^{2}\theta
also vanishes, leaving us with

\displaystyle B(\psi_{\hat{q}};\theta_{N},\theta_{N})

\displaystyle=\int\eta(t)\int\nabla_{\ell}(-\Delta)^{-1+\delta}P_{\leq\hat{q}}^{2}\theta P_{\leq N}\theta T_{>\hat{q}}^{\ell}(P_{\leq N}\theta)dxdt

(24)

\displaystyle T_{>\hat{q}}^{\ell}(f)

\displaystyle\equiv(1-P_{\leq\hat{q}}^{2})T^{\ell}f=(1-P_{\leq\hat{q}}^{2})\epsilon^{\ell a}\nabla_{a}(-\Delta)^{-1+\delta}f

We remark here that there is an additional complication related to low frequencies in the case of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. We address this complication in Section
4.1
.

Since
T_{>\hat{q}}
restricts to frequencies above
2^{\hat{q}-1}
, we have

\displaystyle B_{\hat{q}N}=\sum_{q=\hat{q}-2}^{N}\delta_{q}B_{\hat{q}N},\quad\delta_{q}B_{\hat{q}N}=\int\eta(t)\int\nabla_{\ell}\psi_{\hat{q}}(P_{\leq q+1}\theta T_{>\hat{q}}^{\ell}P_{\leq q+1}\theta-P_{\leq q}\theta T_{>\hat{q}}^{\ell}P_{\leq q}\theta)dxdt.

Indeed,
P_{\leq q}\theta T_{>\hat{q}}^{\ell}P_{\leq q}\theta=0
for
q<\hat{q}-2
. Now decompose
\delta_{q}B_{\hat{q}N}=\delta_{qH1}B_{\hat{q}N}+\delta_{qH2}B_{\hat{q}N}+\delta_{qL}B_{\hat{q}N}
,

\displaystyle\delta_{qH1}B_{\hat{q}N}

\displaystyle=\int\eta(t)\int\nabla_{\ell}\psi_{\hat{q}}\left(P_{q+1}\theta T_{>\hat{q}}^{\ell}P_{q+1}\theta\right)dxdt

(25)

\displaystyle\delta_{qH2}B_{\hat{q}N}

\displaystyle=\int\eta(t)\int\nabla_{\ell}\psi_{\hat{q}}\left(P_{q+1}\theta T_{>\hat{q}}^{\ell}P_{q}\theta+P_{q}\theta T_{>\hat{q}}^{\ell}P_{q+1}\theta\right)dxdt

(26)

\displaystyle\delta_{qL}B_{\hat{q}N}

\displaystyle=\int\eta(t)\int\nabla_{\ell}\psi_{\hat{q}}\underbrace{\left(P_{q+1}\theta T_{>\hat{q}}^{\ell}P_{\leq q-1}\theta+P_{\leq q-1}\theta T_{>\hat{q}}^{\ell}P_{q+1}\theta\right)}_{Q_{qL}(\theta)}dxdt

(27)

Observe that, since
{\mathcal{F}}\mbox{supp}\,P_{q+1}\theta\subseteq\{2^{q}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{q+2}\},{\mathcal{F}}\mbox{supp}\,P_{\leq q-1}\theta\subseteq\{|\xi|<2^{q-1}\}
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}\mbox{supp}\,\,Q_{qL}(\theta)

\displaystyle\subseteq\{2^{q-1}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{q+3}\}

(28)

In particular,
\delta_{qL}B_{\hat{q}N}=0
for
q>\hat{q}+3
since
{\mathcal{F}}\mbox{supp}\,\psi_{\hat{q}}\subseteq\{|\xi|<2^{\hat{q}+1}\}
.

To estimate
\delta_{qL}B_{\hat{q}N}
, write
Q_{qL}(\theta)=\nabla_{j}\nabla^{j}\Delta^{-1}Q_{qL}(\theta)
and integrate by parts

\displaystyle\delta_{qL}B_{\hat{q}N}

\displaystyle=-\int\eta(t)\int\nabla_{j}\nabla_{\ell}\psi_{\hat{q}}\Delta^{-1}\nabla^{j}Q_{qL}(\theta)dxdt.

(29)

Let
t
be a time for which
\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\theta
belongs to
B_{3,c(\mathbb{N})}^{\alpha}
. Then

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{j}\nabla_{\ell}\psi_{\hat{q}}(t)\|_{L^{3}_{x}}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla_{j}\nabla_{\ell}(-\Delta)^{-1+\delta}P_{\leq\hat{q}}^{2}\theta(t)\|_{L_{x}^{3}}\lesssim\sum_{q=-\infty}^{\hat{q}+1}\|\nabla_{j}\nabla_{\ell}\Lambda^{-1+\delta}P_{q}P_{\leq\hat{q}}^{2}\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\theta\|_{L_{x}^{3}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{q=-\infty}^{\hat{q}+1}\|\nabla_{j}\nabla_{\ell}\Lambda^{-1+\delta}P_{\approx q}\|\cdot\|P_{q}\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\theta\|_{L_{x}^{3}}

Here we recall that we write
\|C\ast\|\equiv\|C\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
for a convolution operator
C\ast
, and that
P_{\approx q}
has a Fourier multiplier that is a smooth bump function adapted to
|\xi|\sim 2^{q}
. Thus

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{j}\nabla_{\ell}\psi_{\hat{q}}(t)\|_{L^{3}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{q=-\infty}^{\hat{q}+1}2^{(2-1+\delta)q}2^{-\alpha q}\|\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\theta(t)\|_{\dot{B}_{3,\infty}^{\alpha}}\lesssim 2^{(1+\delta-\alpha)\hat{q}}\|\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\theta(t)\|_{\dot{B}_{3,\infty}^{\alpha}}.

(30)

since
1+\delta-\alpha>0
for
\alpha\leq(1+\delta)/3
. Furthermore, for any
t
such that
\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\theta\in B_{3,c(\mathbb{N})}^{\alpha}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{j}\nabla_{\ell}\psi_{\hat{q}}(t)\|_{L^{3}_{x}}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla_{j}\nabla_{\ell}(-\Delta)^{-1+\delta}P_{\leq\hat{q}}^{2}\theta(t)\|_{L_{x}^{3}}=o(2^{(1+\delta-\alpha)\hat{q}})\qquad\mbox{ as }\hat{q}\to\infty

(31)

by a simple approximation argument. The key points are the uniform in
\hat{q}
boundedness of the operators
2^{-(1+\delta-\alpha)\hat{q}}\nabla_{j}\nabla_{\ell}\Lambda^{-1+\delta}
on
B_{3,\infty}^{\alpha}
, and the fact that
\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\theta(t)
is the strong limit in
B_{3,\infty}^{\alpha}
of
\Lambda^{-1+\delta}f_{(k)}
, where the
f_{(k)}
are smooth and satisfy an
O(1)
estimate as
\hat{q}\to\infty
.

Meanwhile, since we can take
\theta
to have zero mean, we have
P_{\leq q}\theta=\sum_{r<q}\Lambda^{1-\delta}P_{r}\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\theta
. We obtain using
1-\delta-\alpha\geq 2/3-4\delta/3>0
for
\delta<1/2

\displaystyle\|P_{\leq q}\theta(t)\|_{L^{3}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{r<q}\|\Lambda^{1-\delta}P_{\approx r}\|2^{-\alpha r}\|\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\theta(t)\|_{\dot{B}_{3,\infty}^{\alpha}}\lesssim 2^{(1-\delta-\alpha)q}\|\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\theta(t)\|_{\dot{B}_{3,\infty}^{\alpha}}.

In the case of SQG where
\delta=1/2
,
\alpha=(1+\delta)/3=1/2
at the critical exponent, we have
1-\delta-\alpha=0
and the above estimate breaks down. Instead, in this case, we require
\theta(t)\in L_{x}^{3}
to bound
P_{\leq\hat{q}}\theta(t)
uniformly in
L^{3}
by
\lesssim\|\theta(t)\|_{L^{3}_{x}}
.

We now use the Fourier support of
Q_{qL}
in (
28
), the fact that
\delta_{qL}B_{\hat{q}N}=0
unless
\hat{q}-2\leq q\leq\hat{q}+3
, and Hölder to obtain

\displaystyle\|(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla^{j}Q_{qL}(\theta)\|_{L^{3/2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-q}\|Q_{qL}\|_{L^{3/2}_{x}}\lesssim 2^{-q}\|P_{q}\theta\|_{L_{x}^{3}}\|P_{\approx q}T_{>\hat{q}}\|\|P_{\leq q-1}\theta(t)\|_{L_{x}^{3}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-q}\|P_{\approx q}\Lambda^{1-\delta}\|\,\|P_{q}\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\theta(t)\|\,\|P_{\approx q}\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1+\delta}\|\,\|P_{\leq q-1}\theta(t)\|_{L_{x}^{3}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{(-1+(1-\delta)-\alpha+(2\delta-1)+(1-\delta-\alpha))q}\|\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\theta(t)\|_{\dot{B}_{3,\infty}^{\alpha}}^{2}

\displaystyle\|(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla^{j}Q_{qL}(\theta)\|_{L^{3/2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-2\alpha q}\|\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\theta(t)\|_{\dot{B}_{3,\infty}^{\alpha}}^{2}.

(32)

Substituting into (
29
) leaves us with

\displaystyle\sup_{N}\sum_{q=\hat{q}-2}^{N}|\delta_{qL}B_{\hat{q}N}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int\eta(t)\|\nabla_{j}\nabla_{\ell}\psi_{\hat{q}}(t)\|_{L_{x}^{3}}2^{-2\alpha\hat{q}}\|\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\theta(t)\|_{\dot{B}_{3,\infty}^{\alpha}}^{2}dt.

(33)

(In the SQG case, we simply bound
\|P_{q}\theta(t)\|_{L_{x}^{3}},\|P_{\leq q-1}\theta(t)\|_{L_{x}^{3}}\lesssim\|\theta(t)\|_{L_{x}^{3}}
in deriving (
32
) to obtain
\|\theta(t)\|_{L_{x}^{3}}^{2}
in place of
\|\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\theta(t)\|_{\dot{B}_{3,\infty}^{\alpha}}^{2}
on the right hand side of (
33
).)

We now proceed to establish the same bound for (
25
) and (
26
). In fact we will only consider (
26
) since (
25
) can be treated identically. Consider the bilinear form appearing in (
26
)

\displaystyle Q_{q}^{\ell}[\theta,\theta]=P_{q+1}\theta T_{>\hat{q}}^{\ell}P_{q}\theta+P_{q}\theta T_{>\hat{q}}^{\ell}P_{q+1}\theta=Q_{q}^{\ell}[P_{\approx q}\theta,P_{\approx q}\theta]

We suppress the dependence of
Q_{q}
on
\hat{q}
to ease notation. In Section
5
below we derive the following divergence form for
Q_{q}

\displaystyle Q_{q}^{\ell}[\theta,\theta]

\displaystyle=\nabla_{j}B_{q}^{j\ell}[P_{\approx q}\theta,P_{\approx q}\theta]

(34)

\displaystyle B_{q}^{j\ell}[f,f]

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(t,x-h_{1})f(t,x-h_{2})\widetilde{K}_{q}^{j\ell}(h_{1},h_{2})dh_{1}dh_{2}

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{K}_{q}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{2(-1+\delta)q},\quad\|B_{q}^{j\ell}[f,f]\|_{L_{x}^{3/2}}\leq\|\widetilde{K}_{q}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|f\|_{L_{x}^{3}}^{2},

(35)

where again we suppress the dependence of
B_{q}
and
K_{q}
on
\hat{q}
. The last estimate follows by Hölder and Fubini using the duality between
L^{3/2}
and
L^{3}
together with the translation invariance of the
L^{3}
norm.

Substituting (
34
) into (
26
), integrating by parts and using (
35
), we have

\displaystyle\delta_{qH2}B_{\hat{q}N}

\displaystyle=-\int\eta(t)\int\nabla_{j}\nabla_{\ell}\psi_{\hat{q}}B_{q}^{j\ell}[P_{\approx q}\theta,P_{\approx q}\theta]dxdt

\displaystyle|\delta_{qH2}B_{\hat{q}N}|

\displaystyle\leq\int\eta(t)\,\|\nabla_{j}\nabla_{\ell}\psi_{\hat{q}}(t)\|_{L_{x}^{3}}\|B_{q}^{j\ell}[P_{\approx q}\theta,P_{\approx q}\theta]\|_{L_{x}^{3/2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int\eta(t)\,\|\nabla_{j}\nabla_{\ell}\psi_{\hat{q}}(t)\|_{L_{x}^{3}}2^{2(-1+\delta)q}\|P_{\approx q}\theta(t)\|_{L_{x}^{3}}^{2}dt

(36)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int\eta(t)\,\|\nabla_{j}\nabla_{\ell}\psi_{\hat{q}}(t)\|_{L_{x}^{3}}\|P_{\approx q}\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\theta(t)\|_{L_{x}^{3}}^{2}dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\int\eta(t)\,\|\nabla_{j}\nabla_{\ell}\psi_{\hat{q}}(t)\|_{L_{x}^{3}}2^{-2\alpha q}\|\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\theta(t)\|_{\dot{B}_{3,\infty}^{\alpha}}^{2}dt

\displaystyle\sup_{N}\sum_{q=\hat{q}-2}^{N}|\delta_{qH2}B_{\hat{q}N}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int\eta(t)\|\nabla_{j}\nabla_{\ell}\psi_{\hat{q}}(t)\|_{L_{x}^{3}}2^{-2\alpha\hat{q}}\|\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\theta(t)\|_{\dot{B}_{3,\infty}^{\alpha}}^{2}dt

(37)

In the SQG case (
\delta=\alpha=1/2
), we replace
\|\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\theta(t)\|_{\dot{B}_{3,\infty}^{\alpha}}
with
\|\theta(t)\|_{L_{x}^{3}}
, in which case the bound follows from (
36
).

We similarly obtain
\sup_{N}\sum_{q=\hat{q}-2}^{N}|\delta_{qH1}B_{\hat{q}N}|
bounded by the right hand side of (
37
), which, in view of (
33
), means that our bound for (
22
) is

\displaystyle|B(\psi_{\hat{q}};\theta,\theta)|=\lim_{N}|B(\psi_{\hat{q}};\theta_{N},\theta_{N})|\leq\int\eta(t)\|\nabla_{j}\nabla_{\ell}\psi_{\hat{q}}(t)\|_{L_{x}^{3}}2^{-2\alpha\hat{q}}\|\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\theta(t)\|_{\dot{B}_{3,\infty}^{\alpha}}^{2}dt.

Using (
30
), the
dt
integrand on the right hand side is bounded by
\eta(t)\|\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\theta(t)\|_{\dot{B}_{3,\infty}^{\alpha}}^{3}
(or bounded by
\eta(t)\|\theta(t)\|_{L_{x}^{3}}^{3}
in the SQG case), which is
L^{1}
in time since
\Lambda^{-1+\delta}\theta(t)\in L_{t}^{3}\dot{B}_{3,c(\mathbb{N})}^{\alpha}
. Moreover, the little-Oh bound (
31
) shows that the
dt
integrand goes to
0
for almost every
t
as
\hat{q}\to\infty
. By the dominated convergence theorem, we conclude that
|B(\psi_{\hat{q}};\theta,\theta)|\to 0
as
\hat{q}\to\infty
.

4.1
Hamiltonian conservation in the Euclidean case

In the Euclidean case we replace
\theta_{N}=P_{\leq N}\theta
by
\theta_{N}=P_{[-N,N]}\theta\equiv(P_{\leq N}-P_{\leq-N})\theta
. Then equation (
20
) still holds, but it is not quite a classical integral. Rather, for
P_{[-N,\hat{q}]}\theta\equiv(P_{\leq\hat{q}}^{2}-P_{\leq-N})\theta
we have

\displaystyle B_{\hat{q}N}=B(\psi_{\hat{q}};\theta_{N},\theta_{N})

\displaystyle\equiv\int\eta(t)\int T^{\ell}(P_{[-N,N]}\theta)P_{[-N,N]}\theta\nabla_{\ell}\Delta^{-1+\delta}P_{[-N,\hat{q}]}\theta dxdt

(38)

\displaystyle+\int\eta(t)B(P_{\leq-N}\Delta^{-1+\delta}\theta(t),P_{[-N,N]}\theta(t))dt

(39)

The main point is that the term (
39
) is an error term, for
\theta\in L_{t}^{3}\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}
. In fact,

\displaystyle|B(P_{\leq-N}\Delta^{-1+\delta}\theta,P_{[-N,N]}\theta)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla^{2}P_{\leq-N}\Delta^{-1+\delta}\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}\|P_{[-N,N]}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|P_{\leq-N}\nabla\Lambda^{\delta}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\Lambda^{-2+\delta}\theta\|_{L^{2}}\|\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim o(1)\cdot\|\theta(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}}^{3}

The remainder of the proof in the Euclidean case, which involves estimating (
38
), follows along the lines of the torus case but with
-N
as the lower bound for the frequency sums. Specifically, after observing the key cancellation, the quantity that must be estimated is

\displaystyle\int\eta(t)\int\nabla_{\ell}(-\Delta)^{-1+\delta}P_{[-N,\hat{q}]}\theta P_{[-N,N]}\theta T_{>\hat{q}}^{\ell}(P_{[-N,N]}\theta)dxdt,

and one takes
P_{[-N,q]}\theta\equiv(P_{\leq q}-P_{\leq-N})\theta
in place of
P_{\leq q}\theta
during the analysis.

5
Deriving the bilinear form

In this section, we include the derivation of the bilinear form (
34
) and the bound (
35
). We follow
[
26
]
, which in turn generalizes a computation of
[
3
]
. We only consider the case where the domain is the torus since the nonperiodic case is simpler.

Consider the bilinear form appearing in (
26
)

\displaystyle Q_{q}^{\ell}[\theta,\theta]

\displaystyle=P_{q+1}\theta T_{>\hat{q}}^{\ell}P_{q}\theta+P_{q}\theta T_{>\hat{q}}^{\ell}P_{q+1}\theta=Q_{q}^{\ell}[P_{\approx q}\theta,P_{\approx q}\theta]

(40)

\displaystyle\widehat{T_{>\hat{q}}^{\ell}f}(\xi)

\displaystyle=m_{\hat{q}}^{\ell}(\xi)\hat{f}(\xi)=(1-\hat{\eta}_{\leq\hat{q}}^{2}(\xi))\epsilon^{\ell a}(i\xi_{a})|\xi|^{2(-1+\delta)}\hat{f}(\xi).

The most crucial point is the anti-symmetry of the operator
T
, reflected in the fact that
m_{\hat{q}}^{\ell}
is odd. Without this feature, (
40
) may not have integral
0
and an anti-divergence would not exist. The idea to obtain the divergence form will be to Taylor expand in frequency space. Another example of Taylor expansion in frequency space to derive an anti-divergence operator can be found in
[
28
]
.

We start by approximating the periodic function
\theta
by Schwartz functions. By mollifying in frequency space, we may assume that the Schwartz approximations to
P_{q}\theta
and
P_{q+1}\theta
have frequency support in
\{2^{q-2}\leq|\xi|<2^{q+2}\}
, and that they converge pointwise in physical space while also remaining uniformly bounded there. By abuse of notation, assume now that
P_{q}\theta
and
P_{q+1}\theta
are the Schwartz approximations. For these approximations we construct a bilinear anti-divergence as follows.

\displaystyle\hat{Q}_{q}^{\ell}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\hat{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}m_{\hat{q}}^{\ell}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{P_{q+1}\theta}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{P_{q}\theta}(\eta)+\widehat{P_{q+1}\theta}(\xi-\eta)m_{\hat{q}}^{\ell}(\eta)\widehat{P_{q}\theta}(\eta)\frac{d\eta}{(2\pi)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\hat{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}m_{\hat{q}}^{\ell}(\xi-\eta)\hat{\chi}_{q}\widehat{P_{q+1}\theta}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{P_{q}\theta}(\eta)+\widehat{P_{q+1}\theta}(\xi-\eta)m_{\hat{q}}^{\ell}(\eta)\hat{\chi}_{q}\widehat{P_{q}\theta}(\eta)\frac{d\eta}{(2\pi)^{2}}

\displaystyle\text{Define }m_{q}(\cdot)\coloneqq m_{\hat{q}}(\cdot)\hat{\chi}_{q}(\cdot)

\displaystyle=\int_{\hat{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\left[m^{\ell}_{q}(\xi-\eta)+m^{\ell}_{q}(\eta)\right]\widehat{P_{q+1}\theta}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{P_{q}\theta}(\eta)\frac{d\eta}{(2\pi)^{2}}

\displaystyle\text{By the oddness of the multiplier }m^{\ell}_{q}

\displaystyle=\int_{\hat{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\left[m^{\ell}_{q}(\xi-\eta)-m^{\ell}_{q}(-\eta)\right]\widehat{P_{q+1}\theta}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{P_{q}\theta}(\eta)\frac{d\eta}{(2\pi)^{2}}

\displaystyle=i\xi_{j}\int_{\hat{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}-i\nabla^{j}m^{\ell}_{q}(u_{\sigma})d\sigma\widehat{P_{q+1}\theta}(\zeta)\widehat{P_{q}\theta}(\eta)\frac{d\eta}{(2\pi)^{2}}

\displaystyle\hat{Q}_{q}^{\ell}(\xi)

\displaystyle\eqqcolon i\xi_{j}\int_{\hat{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\hat{K}^{j\ell}_{q}(\zeta,\eta)\widehat{P_{q+1}\theta}(\zeta)\widehat{P_{q}\theta}(\eta)\frac{d\eta}{(2\pi)^{2}},

(41)

where we have set

\zeta\coloneqq\xi-\eta,\quad u_{\sigma}\coloneqq\sigma(\xi-\eta)-(1-\sigma)\eta=\sigma\zeta-(1-\sigma)\eta,

and we have used Taylor’s Remainder Theorem in the last equality. In the last line we defined

\hat{K}^{j\ell}_{q}(\zeta,\eta)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{1}-i\nabla^{j}m^{\ell}_{q}(u_{\sigma})\hat{\chi}_{q}(\zeta)\hat{\chi}_{q}(\eta)d\sigma.

(42)

Here we suppress the dependence of
K_{q}^{j\ell}
on
\hat{q}
. For
q>\hat{q}+1
, the cutoff
(1-\hat{\eta}_{\leq\hat{q}}^{2})
in
m_{q}
is
1
on the support of
\hat{\chi}_{q}
, and the homogeneity of
m^{\ell}
leads to the scaling
\widehat{K}_{q}^{j\ell}(\zeta,\eta)=2^{2(-1+\delta)q}f^{j\ell}(2^{-q}\zeta,2^{-q}\eta)
, where
f^{j\ell}
is Schwartz, which in turn implies the bound

\displaystyle\|K_{q}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{2(-1+\delta)q}

(43)

for
q>\hat{q}+1
. The same bound also holds for
\hat{q}-1\leq q\leq\hat{q}+1
by scaling; for example
K_{\hat{q}-1}^{j\ell}(\zeta,\eta)=2^{2(-1+\delta)\hat{q}}f_{0}^{j\ell}(2^{-\hat{q}}\zeta,2^{-\hat{q}}\eta)
, for some Schwartz function
f_{0}
. Finally,
K_{q}=0
for
q<\hat{q}-1
.

Take the inverse Fourier Transform of (
41
) to obtain

\displaystyle Q_{q}^{\ell}(x)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{j}\int_{\hat{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{i\xi\cdot x}\int_{\hat{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\hat{K}^{j\ell}_{q}(\zeta,\eta)\widehat{P_{q+1}\theta}(\zeta)\widehat{P_{q}\theta}(\eta)\frac{d\eta}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{d\xi}{(2\pi)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{j}\int_{\hat{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times\hat{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{i(\zeta+\eta)\cdot x}\hat{K}^{j\ell}_{q}(\zeta,\eta)\widehat{P_{q+1}\theta}(\zeta)\widehat{P_{q}\theta}(\eta)\frac{d\eta}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{d\zeta}{(2\pi)^{2}}.

Observe that
\hat{K}_{q}^{j\ell}
is compactly supported in frequency and smooth due to the factors of
\hat{\chi}_{\approx\lambda}
, making
\hat{K}_{q}^{j\ell}
a Schwartz function. Thus we can define the corresponding physical space kernel
K^{j\ell}_{q}(h_{1},h_{2})
as the inverse Fourier Transform of
\hat{K}^{j\ell}_{q}(\zeta,\eta)
so that

\hat{K}^{j\ell}_{q}(\zeta,\eta)=\int_{\hat{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times\hat{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{-i(\zeta,\eta)\cdot(h_{1},h_{2})}K^{j\ell}_{q}(h_{1},h_{2})dh_{1}dh_{2}.

Then

\displaystyle Q_{q}^{\ell}(x)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{i(\zeta+\eta)\cdot x}\int_{\hat{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times\hat{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{-i(\zeta,\eta)\cdot(h_{1},h_{2})}K^{j\ell}_{q}(h_{1},h_{2})dh_{1}dh_{2}\widehat{P_{q+1}\theta}(\zeta)\widehat{P_{q}\theta}(\eta)\frac{d\eta}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{d\zeta}{(2\pi)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}K^{j\ell}_{q}(h_{1},h_{2})\int_{\hat{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{i\zeta\cdot(x-h_{1})}\widehat{P_{q+1}\theta}(\zeta)\frac{d\zeta}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\hat{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{i\eta\cdot(x-h_{2})}\widehat{P_{q}\theta}(\eta)\frac{d\eta}{(2\pi)^{2}}dh_{1}dh_{2}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}K^{j\ell}_{q}(h_{1},h_{2})P_{q+1}\theta(x-h_{1})P_{q}\theta(x-h_{2})dh_{1}dh_{2}

\displaystyle\eqqcolon\nabla_{j}B^{j\ell}_{q}[\theta,\theta](x)

Here we define the bilinear anti-divergence operator as

\displaystyle B^{j\ell}_{q}[F_{1},F_{2}](x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}K^{j\ell}_{q}(h_{1},h_{2})P_{q+1}F_{1}(x-h_{1})P_{q}F_{2}(x-h_{2})dh_{1}dh_{2},

\displaystyle B^{j\ell}_{q}[F_{1},F_{2}](x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}\widetilde{K}^{j\ell}_{q}(h_{1},h_{2})F_{1}(x-h_{1})F_{2}(x-h_{2})dh_{1}dh_{2}

(44)

for scalar fields
F_{1},F_{2}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
. Here we obtain
\widetilde{K}_{q}=(P_{q+1}\otimes P_{q})K_{q}
from
K_{q}
by writing
P_{q+1}F_{1}
(and
P_{q}F_{2}
) as a convolution in the variable
h_{1}
(
h_{2}
in the latter case) and we pass
P_{q}
and
P_{q+1}
onto the kernel
K_{q}
by a change of variables. This bi-convolution operator satisfies the equality

\displaystyle T^{\ell}[P_{q+1}\theta]\nabla_{\ell}P_{q}\theta+T^{\ell}[P_{q}\theta]\nabla_{\ell}P_{q+1}\theta=\nabla_{j}B^{j\ell}_{q}[P_{\approx q}\theta,P_{\approx q}\theta](x)

(45)

for the Schwartz approximations to
P_{q+1}\theta,P_{q}\theta
whose Fourier supports lie in
\{|\xi|\sim 2^{q}\}
. Since
\|\widetilde{K}_{q}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
is finite and the Schwartz approximations to
P_{\approx q}\theta
are bounded and converge pointwise, we may apply dominated convergence to pass to the limit in (
44
) with
F_{1}=F_{2}\to P_{\approx q}\theta
to conclude that (
45
) holds (both in
{\cal D}^{\prime}
and classically) for the periodic functions
P_{q+1}\theta,P_{q}\theta
. Thus we have shown (
34
). Finally, note that the bound (
43
) for
K_{q}
holds as well for
\widetilde{K}_{q}
, which establishes (
35
).

Remark:
For future reference, we note here the important property that
K_{q}
is trace-free in the special case of the mSQG multiplier. To check that
K_{q}^{j\ell}
is trace-free, we use the fact that the frequency cutoff
\chi_{q}(\xi)=\tilde{\chi}_{q}(|\xi|^{2})
is radial and it suffices check that
\nabla^{j}m_{q}^{\ell}(\xi)
is trace-free:

\displaystyle m_{q}^{\ell}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{\ell a}i\xi_{a}|\xi|^{2(-1+\delta)}\chi_{q}(\xi)=\epsilon^{\ell a}i\xi_{a}|\xi|^{2(-1+\delta)}\tilde{\chi}_{q}(|\xi|^{2})

\displaystyle\nabla^{j}m_{q}^{\ell}(\xi)

\displaystyle=i\epsilon^{\ell j}|\xi|^{2(-1+\delta)}\chi_{q}(\xi)+\epsilon^{\ell a}i\xi_{a}\xi^{j}F_{q,\delta}(|\xi|^{2}).

(46)

Here
F_{q,\delta}
is some radial smooth function adapted to
|\xi|^{2}\sim(2^{q})^{2}
. As the above expression is trace-free (
\epsilon^{\ell j}
is antisymmetric and
\epsilon^{\ell a}\xi_{a}
is perpendicular to
\xi
), this calculation confirms that
K_{q}^{j\ell}
is trace-free.

6
Defining the nonlinearity

For scalar functions
\psi
,
\theta\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
, we define the form

\displaystyle B[\psi,\theta]

\displaystyle=-\int\psi(x)T^{\ell}\theta(x)\nabla_{\ell}\theta(x)dx=\int\nabla_{\ell}\psi\theta T^{\ell}\theta dx

(47)

The main result in this section is the estimate

\displaystyle|B[\psi,\theta]|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\delta}\|\nabla^{2}\psi\|_{C^{0}}\|\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}}^{2},\qquad 0<\delta<\infty,

(48)

where the constant depends on
\delta>0
. The same bound also holds for the case of Euler (
\delta=0
), in which case the nonlinearity is given by
B[\psi,\theta]=-\int\psi\cdot\epsilon_{a_{\ell}}\nabla^{a}\nabla_{j}(T^{j}\theta T^{\ell}\theta)dx
,
\epsilon
the Levi-Civita symbol and
T^{\ell}\theta=-\epsilon^{\ell b}\nabla_{b}\Delta^{-1}\theta
.

The associated trilinear form
T[\psi,\theta,\phi]\coloneqq\int\nabla_{\ell}\psi(\theta T^{\ell}\phi+\phi T^{\ell}\theta)dx
, for which
B[\psi,\theta]=\frac{1}{2}T[\psi,\theta,\theta]
and
T[\psi,\theta,\phi]=\frac{1}{4}(B[\psi,\theta+\phi]-B[\psi,\theta-\phi])
, will consequently satisfy a bound

\displaystyle|T[\psi,\theta,\phi]|\lesssim\|\nabla^{2}\psi\|_{C^{0}}\|\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}}\|\phi\|_{\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}}.

(49)

This bound on
T
guarantees a unique continuous extension to
\theta\in L_{t}^{2}\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}
for the nonlinearity defining the SQG equation by a Cauchy sequence argument. We now begin the proof of (
48
).

Note that when
\psi
is a constant,
B[\psi,\theta]=0
. Thus we obtain the following decomposition of paraproduct type.

\displaystyle B[\psi,\theta]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\int P_{k}\nabla_{\ell}\psi\theta\,T^{\ell}\theta dx=LL+HH+HL+LH

(50)

\displaystyle LL

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\int P_{k}\nabla_{\ell}\psi P_{\leq k-3}\theta\,T^{\ell}P_{\leq k-3}\theta dx=0

(51)

\displaystyle HL

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\sum_{q\geq k-3}\int P_{k}\nabla_{\ell}\psi P_{q+1}\theta\,T^{\ell}P_{\leq q-1}\theta dx

(52)

\displaystyle LH

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\sum_{q\geq k-3}\int P_{k}\nabla_{\ell}\psi P_{\leq q-1}\theta\,T^{\ell}P_{q+1}\theta dx

(53)

\displaystyle HH

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\sum_{q\geq k-3}\int P_{k}\nabla_{\ell}\psi\underbrace{\left(P_{q}\theta\,T^{\ell}P_{q+1}\theta+P_{q+1}\theta\,T^{\ell}P_{q}\theta+P_{q+1}\theta\,T^{\ell}P_{q+1}\theta\right)}_{Q_{q}^{\ell}(\theta)}dx

(54)

That
LL=0
follows from Plancharel and the bound on the frequency support of
P_{\leq k-3}\theta T^{\ell}P_{\leq k-3}
.

We now estimate
HH
. From Section
5
, we have a divergence form for
Q_{q}^{\ell}(\theta)=\nabla_{j}B_{q}^{j\ell}[P_{\approx q}\theta]
, where the multiplier for
P_{\approx q}
is a bump function adapted to
\{\xi\in\widehat{\mathbb{R}}^{2}~:~2^{q-4}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{q+4}\}
,

\displaystyle B_{q}^{j\ell}[P_{\approx q}\theta](x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}K_{q}^{j\ell}(h_{1},h_{2})P_{\approx q}\theta(x-h_{1})P_{\approx q}\theta(x-h_{2})dh_{1}dh_{2},

(55)

and
\|K_{q}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim 2^{2(-1+\delta)q}
. (The
K_{q}
is the sum of the kernels coming from the interaction of
P_{q+1}\theta
with itself, and the other coming from the symmetric interaction of
P_{q+1}\theta
with
P_{q}\theta
.) Plugging in the anti-divergence, interchanging the order of summation (which is easily justified for smooth
\psi
and
\theta
), and integrating by parts yields

\displaystyle-HH

\displaystyle=\sum_{q}\int\left(\sum_{k\leq q-3}P_{k}\nabla_{j}\nabla_{\ell}\psi\right)B_{q}^{j\ell}[P_{\approx q}\theta]dx

(56)

\displaystyle|HH|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{q}\|P_{\leq q-3}\nabla^{2}\psi\|_{C^{0}}\|B_{q}[P_{\approx q}\theta]\|_{L^{1}}dx

\displaystyle|HH|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\ell}\|P_{\leq\ell}\nabla^{2}\psi\|_{C^{0}}\sum_{q}\|K_{q}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|P_{\approx q}\theta\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla^{2}\psi\|_{C^{0}}\sum_{q}\left(2^{(-1+\delta)q}\|P_{\approx q}\theta\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle|HH|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla^{2}\psi\|_{C^{0}}\|\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}}^{2}

(57)

Now we consider
HL
and
LH
. Notice that
{\mathcal{F}}\mbox{supp}\,(P_{q+1}\theta\,T^{\ell}P_{\leq q-1}\theta)\subseteq\{|\xi|\geq 2^{q-1}\}
and similarly for the quadratic term in
LH
. Thus the sum over
q
extends only from
k-3
to
k+2
. To simplify the presentation, we treat the sum as though there is only one term with
q=k
.

\displaystyle HL

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{k}\int P_{k}\nabla_{\ell}\psi P_{k+1}\theta\,T^{\ell}P_{\leq k-1}\theta dx,\qquad LH\sim\sum_{k}\int P_{k}\nabla_{\ell}\psi P_{\leq k-1}\theta\,T^{\ell}P_{k+1}\theta dx

We first treat
HL
. Recall from the remarks surrounding (
23
) that we may assume that the zero Fourier mode of
\theta
vanishes. Thus we decompose

\displaystyle HL

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{k}\sum_{j\leq k-1}\int P_{k}\nabla_{\ell}\psi P_{k+1}\theta T^{\ell}P_{j}\theta dx

(58)

Using
\|T^{\ell}P_{j}\theta\|_{L^{2}}=\|\nabla^{\ell}(-\Delta)^{-1+\delta}P_{j}\theta\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 2^{(-1+2\delta)j}\|P_{j}\theta\|_{L^{2}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle|HL|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k}\sum_{j\leq k-1}\|P_{k}\nabla\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|P_{k+1}\theta\|_{L^{2}}2^{\delta j}2^{(-1+\delta)j}\|P_{j}\theta\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j}\sum_{k\geq j+1}\|P_{k}\nabla\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}2^{(1-\delta)k}\|\Lambda^{-1+\delta}P_{k+1}\theta\|_{L^{2}}2^{\delta j}\|\Lambda^{-1+\delta}P_{j}\theta\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j}\sum_{k}\|P_{k}\nabla^{2}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}2^{-\delta|j-k|}\|\Lambda^{-1+\delta}P_{k+1}\theta\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{-1+\delta}P_{j}\theta\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{\ell}\|P_{\ell}\nabla^{2}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\sum_{j,k}2^{-\delta|j-k|}\|\Lambda^{-1+\delta}P_{k+1}\theta\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{-1+\delta}P_{j}\theta\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle|HL|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{\ell}\|P_{\ell}\nabla^{2}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}}^{2}

(59)

upon applying the Schur test to the last term. Here we have used the standard bound

\displaystyle 2^{k}\|P_{k}\nabla_{\ell}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle=2^{k}\|\nabla^{j}\Delta^{-1}P_{\approx k}P_{k}\nabla_{j}\nabla_{\ell}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|P_{k}\nabla^{2}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Finally, the bound for
LH
follows without interchanging the order of summation

\displaystyle LH

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{k}\sum_{j\leq k-1}\int P_{k}\nabla_{\ell}\psi\,P_{j}\theta\,T^{\ell}P_{k+1}\theta dx

(60)

\displaystyle|LH|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k}\sum_{j\leq k-1}\|P_{k}\nabla_{\ell}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|P_{j}\theta\|_{L^{2}}2^{(-1+2\delta)k}\|P_{k+1}\theta\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k}\sum_{j\leq k-1}\|P_{k}\nabla_{\ell}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}2^{(1-\delta)j}\|\Lambda^{-1+\delta}P_{j}\theta\|_{L^{2}}2^{\delta k}\|\Lambda^{-1+\delta}P_{k+1}\theta\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k}\sum_{j\leq k-1}2^{k}\|P_{k}\nabla_{\ell}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}2^{-\delta|k-j|}\|\Lambda^{-1+\delta}P_{j}\theta\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{-1+\delta}P_{k+1}\theta\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle|LH|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{\ell}\|P_{\ell}\nabla^{2}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\sum_{j,k}2^{-\delta|k-j|}\|\Lambda^{-1+\delta}P_{j}\theta\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{-1+\delta}P_{k+1}\theta\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle|LH|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{\ell}\|P_{\ell}\nabla^{2}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}}^{2}.

(61)

Again we have applied the Schur test. Combining (
57
), (
59
) and (
61
), we have proven (
48
).

6.1
The nonperiodic case

We now explain how to extend the estimate (
48
) where
\mathbb{T}^{2}
is replaced by
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. In this case, we define

\displaystyle B[\psi,\theta]

\displaystyle=-\int\psi(x)\nabla_{\ell}[T^{\ell}\theta(x)\theta(x)]dx=\int\nabla_{\ell}\psi\theta T^{\ell}\theta dx

for
\psi
in the Schwartz class and for
\theta\in{\cal S}_{0}
in the class of Schwartz functions with compact frequency support away from
0
. The additional details involve handling the low frequencies.

For any integer
m\geq 0
, we have
\psi(x)=P_{\leq-m}\psi+\sum_{k=-m+1}^{\infty}P_{k}\psi
, and the series converges uniformly. Furthermore,
\|P_{\leq-m}\nabla\psi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim 2^{-2m}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
,
\|P_{k}\nabla\psi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim 2^{2k}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
, and
\|P_{k}\nabla\psi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim 2^{-k}\|\nabla^{2}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
together with
\theta T^{\ell}\theta\in L^{1}
for
\theta\in{\cal S}_{0}
allow us to obtain the expansion (
50
) by letting
m\to\infty
and applying dominated convergence and Fubini-Tonelli to interchange the sum and the integral. Equations (
51
) through (
54
) then follow routinely from
\int P_{k}\nabla_{\ell}\psi\theta T^{\ell}\theta dx=\lim_{N\to\infty}\int P_{k}\nabla_{\ell}\psi P_{\leq N}\theta T^{\ell}P_{\leq N}\theta dx
once the absolute convergence of the series has been obtained.

Bounding each term in (
51
) through (
54
) requires the decompositions (
58
) and (
60
). In the case of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
, the summation over
j
includes only finitely terms, but it extends to all
j>-\infty
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Since
P_{\leq k-1}=P_{\leq-m}+\sum_{j=-m+1}^{k-1}P_{j}
for any
m
, the key point is to verify the limit

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\int P_{k}\nabla_{\ell}\psi(P_{k+1}\theta\,T^{\ell}P_{\leq-m}\theta dx+P_{\leq-m}\theta\,T^{\ell}P_{k+1}\theta)dx=0

(62)

for each fixed
k
, every Schwartz function
\psi
and every
\theta\in{\cal S}_{0}
. This limit is clear by the compact frequency support of
\theta
away from the origin, since the left hand side is
0
for sufficiently large
m
.

6.2
Remark on generalized estimates of this type

Our technique for estimating the nonlinearity can be applied to a more general class of nonlinearities that have the form
\int\psi\theta T[\theta]dx
. The only properties of the bilinear form
B(\psi,\theta)=\int\nabla_{\ell}\psi\theta T^{\ell}\theta dx
that we have used in the proof are the scaling and anti-symmetry of
T^{\ell}
, reflected in the oddness and the homogeneity of the multiplier. For example, the bound

\displaystyle\left|\int\psi_{\ell}\theta\nabla^{\ell}\Delta\theta dx\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\qquad\psi_{\ell},\theta\in C_{c}^{\infty}

(63)

can be proven by a similar (nonlocal) argument based on the fact that the symbol of
\nabla\Delta
is odd and degree
3
homogeneous.

It is natural to wonder whether the estimate (
63
) can be proven by an integration by parts argument, but it appears that a nonlocal proof is necessary. To see that an integration by parts argument would not apply, note that the bound is false if we replace the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
norms with local
L^{2}(\mathrm{supp}\,\psi)
norms. To see this failure of the localized version of the estimate, consider a
\theta_{0}
with
\nabla\Delta\theta_{0}
nonzero, and choose
\psi_{\ell}=\chi\nabla_{\ell}\Delta\theta_{0}
where
\chi
is a bump function supported where
\nabla\Delta\theta_{0}
is nonzero. Now set
\theta_{R}=\theta_{0}+R\tilde{\chi}
, where
R
is large and
\tilde{\chi}=1
on the support of
\chi
. Then
\|\nabla\theta_{R}\|_{L^{2}(\mathrm{supp}\,\psi)}=\|\nabla\theta_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathrm{supp}\,\psi)}
does not depend on
R
, but the left hand side of (
63
) becomes unbounded as
R
gets large.

These considerations also motivate why our definition of the space
\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}
requires that we exclude polynomials. After all the estimate
|\int\psi_{\ell}\theta\nabla^{\ell}\Delta\theta dx|\lesssim\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}
would be false for high degree polynomials for which the right hand side would vanish.

7
Continuous extension of the operator

We now have the homogeneous estimate

\displaystyle|B[\psi,\theta]|\lesssim_{\delta}\|\nabla^{2}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}}^{2}

(64)

for
\psi,\theta
Schwartz, and this bound extends by continuity for any Schwartz
\psi
and any
\theta\in\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}
using the corresponding bound (
49
) for the trilinear form and a density and Cauchy sequence argument. The completion of the Schwartz space under the semi-norm
\|\nabla^{2}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}
is the space
\dot{C}_{0}^{2}
of
C^{2}
functions with bounded second derivatives that satisfy
|\nabla^{2}\psi(x)|=o(1)
as
|x|\to\infty
(see Appendix Section
A
). The more delicate matter is proving the continuous extension with respect to weak-* convergence, which is the subject of the next section.

7.1
Extension to
\dot{W}^{2,\infty}
: Definition, boundedness and uniqueness

Now let
\dot{W}^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
\dot{W}^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
denote the space of distributions
\Psi
such that
\nabla^{2}\Psi\in L^{\infty}
. This space includes functions (such as quadratic polynomials) for which there is no sequence
\psi_{n}\in\dot{C}_{0}^{2}
satisfying
\nabla^{2}\psi_{n}\to\nabla^{2}\Psi
in
L^{\infty}
, as the latter convergence would imply
\Psi\in\dot{C}_{0}^{2}
. Consequently, our estimate (
64
) on
|B[\psi,\theta]|
does not on its own provide a unique continuous extension to allow for
\Psi\in\dot{W}^{2,\infty}
.

Nonetheless we can uniquely extend the bilinear form
B(\psi,\theta)
to the space of
(\Psi,\theta)\in\dot{W}^{2,\infty}\times\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}
such that the extension
B(\Psi,\theta)
also satisfies the homogeneous estimate (
64
), and such that whenever
(\Psi_{n},\theta_{n})
is a sequence in
\dot{W}^{2,\infty}\times\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}
satisfying
\nabla^{2}\Psi_{n}\rightharpoonup\nabla^{2}\Psi
in the
L^{\infty}
weak-* topology, and
\theta_{n}\to\theta
in the
\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}
norm, we have that
B(\psi_{n},\theta_{n})\to B(\Psi,\theta)
.

To define this extension, first let
\theta\in\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}
be fixed and
\Psi\in\dot{W}^{2,\infty}
be given. Choose a sequence
\psi_{n}\in C_{c}^{\infty}
such that
\nabla^{2}\psi_{n}\hookrightarrow\nabla^{2}\Psi
in
L^{\infty}
weak-*. (A construction can be found in Section
A
.) In particular, there is a uniform bound on
\|\nabla^{2}\psi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}
.
For the sequence
\psi_{n}
, we use the formulas

\displaystyle B(\psi_{n},\theta)

\displaystyle=HL(\psi_{n},\theta)+LH(\psi_{n},\theta)+HH(\psi_{n},\theta)

(65)

\displaystyle HL(\psi_{n},\theta)+LH(\psi_{n},\theta)

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{k}\sum_{j\leq k-1}\left\{\int P_{k}\nabla_{\ell}\psi_{n}(P_{k+1}\theta T^{\ell}P_{j}\theta+P_{j}\theta\,T^{\ell}P_{k+1}\theta)dx\right\}

(66)

\displaystyle HH(\psi_{n},\theta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{q}\left\{\int P_{\leq q-3}\nabla_{j}\nabla_{\ell}\psi_{n}B_{q}^{j\ell}[P_{\approx q}\theta]dx\right\}

(67)

For
\Psi\in\dot{W}^{2,\infty}
,
\theta\in\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}
, we define
B(\Psi,\theta)
to be the right hand side with
\psi_{n}
replaced by
\Psi
. For each fixed
k,q\in\mathbb{Z}
, the functions
P_{\leq q-3}\nabla_{j}\nabla_{\ell}\psi_{n}
and
2^{k}P_{k}\nabla_{\ell}\psi_{n}=[2^{k}\Delta^{-1}\nabla^{i}P_{\approx k}]P_{k}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\ell}\psi_{n}
(along with their derivatives) are uniformly bounded and they converge
uniformly
to
P_{\leq q-3}\nabla_{j}\nabla_{\ell}\Psi
and
2^{k}P_{k}\nabla_{\ell}\Psi
by a compactness and contradiction argument. The terms in the braces then converge as
n\to\infty
for any fixed
k,j\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
or
q\in\mathbb{Z}
, and they are bounded by an absolutely summable series in
(k,j)
or
q
by repeating the proof of the bounds for
LH
,
HL
and
HH
in Section
6
. By dominated convergence, we obtain
B(\psi_{n},\theta)\to B(\Psi,\theta)
for any fixed
\theta\in\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}
.

If we desire
B(\Psi,\theta)
to be sequentially continuous in
\Psi
with respect to weak-* convergence, this argument proves that
B(\Psi,\theta)
is uniquely determined and has the desired continuity property. The continuous extension maintains the homogeneous bound (
64
), though the optimal constant may in principle be different.

We also desire the property that
B(\Psi_{n},\theta_{n})\to B(\Psi,\theta)
whenever
\theta_{n}\to\theta
in the
\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}
seminorm, and
\nabla^{2}\Psi_{n}\rightharpoonup\nabla^{2}\Psi
in
L^{\infty}
weak-*. This last statement is a corollary of the continuity proven in Section
7.2
below, but it is desirable to have a direct proof. If
\theta_{n}
were constant in
n
, the convergence follows as in the previous argument after noting that
\sup_{n}\|\nabla^{2}\Psi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty
by the uniform boundedness principle. Now let
T[\Psi,\theta,\phi]
be the trilinear form, symmetric in
\theta,\phi
for which
B(\Psi,\theta)=T[\Psi,\theta,\theta]/2
. Then
T[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]
is bounded on
\dot{W}^{2,\infty}\times\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}\times\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}
as in (
49
). We then conclude that

\displaystyle B(\Psi_{n},\theta_{n})-B(\Psi,\theta)=T[\Psi_{n},\theta_{n}-\theta,\theta_{n}]/2+T[\Psi_{n},\theta,\theta_{n}-\theta]/2+B(\Psi_{n}-\Psi,\theta)

converges to
0
as
n\to\infty
.

7.2
Continuity property of the extended operator

In this section, we prove another continuity property of the extended operator as the proof will be useful to us later on.

Let
X\subseteq\dot{W}^{2,\infty}
be bounded in the semi-norm. We want to show that
B
is continuous on
X\times\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}
with the appropriate (weak-*
\times
strong) topologies. We obtain this continuity by showing that the trilinear form
T[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]
is continuous as a map on the product
X\times\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}\times\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}
into
\mathbb{R}
when we endow
\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}
with the strong topology induced by the homogeneous norm, and
X
with the (non-Hausdorff) weak-* topology obtained as the pullback of the map
\Psi\mapsto\nabla^{2}\Psi
into
L^{\infty}
weak-*. The continuity of
B(\cdot,\cdot)
follows then by restricting to the diagonal in
\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}\times\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}

7.4
Special properties and conservation laws of mSQG

In this Section, we now prove the estimates that are unique to SQG, 2D Euler and the mSQG family, which stem from the following Theorem:

Theorem 11

Let
m^{\ell}=\epsilon^{\ell a}\nabla_{a}(-\Delta)^{-1+\delta}
be the mSQG multiplier,
\delta\geq 0
, and

denote the corresponding operator. Then there is a bounded bilinear operator
\widetilde{T}^{j\ell}

E_{\lambda}
converges to
0
in
L^{1}
. To see this, we apply Fubini-Tonelli and apply Cauchy-Schwartz, using the translation invariance of the
L^{2}
norm, to obtain

\displaystyle\int|E_{\lambda}(x)|dx\lesssim\lambda^{2-2\delta+1+\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\left[\int|\nabla\varphi(\lambda^{1/2}(x-\sigma h_{1}))||\varphi(\lambda^{1/2}(x-\sigma h_{2}))|dx\right]|h||\tilde{K}_{\lambda}(h_{1},h_{2})|dh_{1}dh_{2}d\sigma

\displaystyle\lesssim(\lambda^{1/2}\|\nabla\varphi(\lambda^{1/2}\cdot)\|_{L^{2}})\cdot(\lambda^{1/2}\|\varphi(\lambda^{1/2}\cdot)\|_{L^{2}})\cdot\lambda^{2-2\delta+1/2}\int|h||\tilde{K}_{\lambda}(h_{1},h_{2})|dh_{1}dh_{2}\lesssim(1)\cdot(1)\cdot\lambda^{-1/2}.

The last bound follows due to the scaling of the kernel
\tilde{K}_{\lambda}(h_{1},h_{2})=\lambda^{-2+2\delta}\lambda^{2d}\tilde{K}_{1}(\lambda h_{1},\lambda h_{2})
, which is Schwartz. Note that the estimate relies crucially on the gain of
\lambda^{-1}
coming from the small factor of
|h|
and scaling. Thus
\|E_{\lambda}\|_{L^{1}}=O(\lambda^{-1/2})
goes to
0
as
\lambda\to\infty
.

9
Uniqueness of the main estimate for the mSQG multiplier

In this section we show that the special estimate for the mSQG nonlinearity in Theorem
3
is not satisfied by any other active scalar nonlinearities with the same translation and scaling symmetries other than the constant multiples of the mSQG nonlinearity.

Consider an active scalar nonlinearity on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, which is a nonlinearity of the form

\displaystyle B(\psi,\theta)\equiv\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\psi T^{\ell}\theta\nabla_{\ell}\theta dx

initially defined for
\theta
in the space
{\cal S}_{0}
of Schwartz functions with compact Fourier support away from the origin and for
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}
. We restate here the theorem we are aiming to prove for the convenience of the reader.

Theorem 12
.

Assume that the operator
T^{\ell}
has a Fourier multiplier
m^{\ell}
that is homogeneous of degree
-1+2\delta
,
\delta\geq 0
,
\delta\neq 1
. If the nonlinearity
B(\psi,\theta)
satisfies the bound

\displaystyle|B(\psi,\theta)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathring{\nabla}^{2}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\theta\|_{H^{-1+\delta}}^{2}

(S)

for
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
\theta\in{\cal S}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, then
T^{\ell}
is a constant multiple of the mSQG Biot Savart law.

9.1
Reducing to the case of a smooth multiplier

Let us reduce to the case where the multiplier is smooth. We can argue by contradiction. Suppose
m^{\ell}
is a homogeneous multiplier that is not a constant multiple of the mSQG multiplier, but for which the corresponding nonlinearity nonetheless satisfies (
S
). We first reduce to the case where
m^{\ell}
is smooth away from the origin by exploiting a natural action of the group of rotations.

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, let
R_{\alpha}
denote the one parameter group of linear operators that act on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
by rotation by the angle
\alpha
, and let
[R_{\alpha}]_{k}^{\ell}
denote the corresponding matrix entries. The multiplier
m^{\ell}(\xi)
can be regarded as a vector field on
\widehat{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, and the group
R_{\alpha}
acts on this space of vector fields by pushforward:
(R_{\alpha}m)^{\ell}(\xi)\equiv[R_{\alpha}]_{k}^{\ell}m^{k}(R_{-\alpha}\xi)
. The mSQG multiplier, which we denote by
M^{\ell}(\xi)
, is up to a constant the unique vector field invariant under this action with the
-1+2\delta
homogeneity.

Letting
f\circ R_{\alpha}(x)=f(R_{\alpha}x)
, since
R_{\alpha}
is orthogonal, the formula
\widehat{f\circ R_{\alpha}}(\xi)=\hat{f}(R_{\alpha}\xi)
yields that the rotation group correspondingly acts on the operator
T^{\ell}
associated with
m^{\ell}
by
(R_{\alpha}T)^{\ell}[\theta](x)\equiv[R_{\alpha}]^{\ell}_{k}T^{k}[\theta\circ R_{\alpha}](R_{-\alpha}x)
. From this identity, we can check that the action of the rotation group respects the estimate (
S
), for if (
S
) holds for
T^{\ell}
then for the rotated form
B_{\alpha}
, we have

\displaystyle B_{\alpha}[\psi,\theta]

\displaystyle\equiv\int\psi(x)(R_{\alpha}T)^{\ell}[\theta]\nabla_{\ell}\theta(x)dx=\int\psi(x)[R_{\alpha}]_{k}^{\ell}T^{k}[\theta\circ R_{\alpha}](R_{-\alpha}x)\nabla_{\ell}\theta(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int\psi(R_{\alpha}x)T^{k}[\theta\circ R_{\alpha}](x)[R_{\alpha}]_{k}^{\ell}\nabla_{\ell}\theta(R_{\alpha}x)=\int\psi(R_{\alpha}x)T^{k}[\theta\circ R_{\alpha}](x)\nabla_{k}[\theta\circ R_{\alpha}](x)

\displaystyle B_{\alpha}[\psi,\theta]

\displaystyle=B_{0}[\psi\circ R_{\alpha},\theta\circ R_{\alpha}]

Thus the bilinear form
B_{\alpha}
associated to
R_{\alpha}T
maintains the bound (
S
), since both
\|\mathring{\nabla}^{2}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}
and
\|\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}
are invariant under rotations.

Now consider again the operator
T
that we have assumed is not the mSQG operator but still satisfies (
S
). Define the twisted mollification of
T
to be

\displaystyle(T_{\epsilon})^{\ell}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(R_{\alpha}T)^{\ell}\eta_{\epsilon}(\alpha)d\alpha

(93)

where
\eta_{\epsilon}
is a standard mollifier supported in
|\alpha|\leq\epsilon
. Then
T_{\epsilon}
inherits the estimate (
S
) since we are simply averaging over rotations that preserve the estimate. Moreover, we claim the multiplier of
T_{\epsilon}
is smooth and distinct from the mSQG multiplier for
\epsilon
small enough.

Indeed, since
m^{\ell}
is a homogeneous distribution on
\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{2}}
, away from the origin
m
has the form
m^{\ell}(\xi)=h^{\ell}(\omega)|\xi|^{-1+2\delta}
, where
\omega\in\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
is the polar angle on
\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{2}}
and
h^{\ell}
is an
\mathbb{R}^{2}
-valued distribution on the
1D
torus
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
(see e.g.
[
23
]
). The operation of twisted mollification forming the operator
T_{\epsilon}
simply replaces the distribution
h
by the distribution
h_{\epsilon}^{\ell}(\omega)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}h^{k}(\omega-\alpha)\eta_{\epsilon}(\alpha)[R_{\alpha}]_{k}^{\ell}d\alpha
, where the integral and the translation of
h
are interpreted in the sense of distributions using duality.

In the same way that a usual convolution produces a smooth function from a distribution, the distribution
h_{\epsilon}
can be shown to be smooth. Furthermore, since
R_{\alpha}
is smooth and
R_{0}=\mbox{Id}
, one can check that
h_{\epsilon}
converges to
h
in
{\cal D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})
as
\epsilon\to 0
by calculating the dual operator that acts on test functions, and finally showing that the dual twisted mollification converges in
C^{k}(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})
for any test function and any
k\geq 0
. We omit the details, which are similar to the usual process of convolution for distributions (see e.g.
[
23
]
).

As a result,
T_{\epsilon}
must be distinct from the mSQG operator for
\epsilon
sufficiently small, for we have assumed that the limiting multiplier
m^{\ell}(\xi)=h^{\ell}(\omega)|\xi|^{-1+2\delta}
is distinct from the mSQG multiplier. Also, as pointed out
T_{\epsilon}
inherits the estimate (
S
). Thus, if we can show
T_{\epsilon}
is the mSQG Biot-Savart Law for every
\epsilon>0
, the limiting operator
T
is also.

We now assume
T
has a smooth multiplier satisfying (
S
) and
-1+2\delta
homogeneity, and proceed to show
T
is a multiple of the Biot-Savart law for mSQG.

9.2
The case of an odd multiplier

By the analysis of the previous section, if the bound (
S
) holds for an operator
T^{\ell}
with multiplier
m^{\ell}
, the same bound holds for the odd part of the operator
T_{o}^{\ell}=(T^{\ell}+(R_{\pi}T)^{\ell})/2
whose multiplier is
m_{o}^{\ell}(\xi)=(m^{\ell}(\xi)-m^{\ell}(-\xi))/2
, and also for the even part of the operator
T_{e}^{\ell}=(T^{\ell}-(R_{\pi}T)^{\ell})/2
. We will show that
T_{o}^{\ell}
is a multiple of the mSQG Biot-Savart law and that
T_{e}^{\ell}
is
0
(except possibly in the case where the multiplier is degree
1
homogeneous).

Let us now consider the case where the multiplier
m^{\ell}=m_{o}^{\ell}
is odd. We first establish that, assuming oddness of the multiplier, the velocity field
T^{\ell}
must be incompressible in the sense that
m(\xi)\cdot\xi=0
.

Proposition 9.1
.

If the velocity field
T^{\ell}
is not incompressible, then there is a test function
\psi
and a sequence
\theta_{\lambda}
with Fourier support in an annulus
\{|\xi|\sim\lambda\}
such that
\|\theta_{\lambda}\|_{\dot{H}^{\delta}}=O(1)
and such that
B(\psi,\theta_{\lambda})
has a nontrivial limit. As a consequence, there cannot exist any estimate of the form

\displaystyle|B(\psi,\theta)|\lesssim\|\psi\|_{X}\|\theta\|_{H^{s}}^{2},\qquad\psi\in{\cal C}_{c}^{\infty},\theta\in H^{s}

(94)

for any
s<\delta
and any norm
X
on
{\cal C}_{c}^{\infty}
.

Indeed, if an estimate of the type (
94
) were to exist, it would force
B(\psi,\theta_{\lambda})\to 0
for the example we now construct in view of the estimate
\|\theta_{\lambda}\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\lambda^{s-\delta}\|\theta_{\lambda}\|_{\dot{H}^{\delta}}
for functions with Fourier support in a annulus of radius
\sim\lambda
.

Proof.

As
T
is not incompressible-valued, there exists a frequency
\bar{\xi}
,
|\bar{\xi}|=1
such that
m(\bar{\xi})\cdot\bar{\xi}\neq 0
. Let
\eta_{\lambda}
be a Schwartz function that is a bump function in frequency space adapted to
|\lambda\bar{\xi}-\xi|\leq\lambda/2
. Define the operator
P_{\lambda}=\eta_{\lambda}\ast
and define a bump function
\chi_{\lambda}(x)=\lambda^{-\delta}\lambda^{d/4}\chi(\lambda^{1/2}x)
(with
d=2
)
and define

\displaystyle\theta_{\lambda}=(\Theta_{\lambda}+\overline{\Theta}_{\lambda})=(\Theta_{\lambda}+\Theta_{-\lambda}),\qquad\Theta_{\lambda}=P_{\lambda}[e^{i\lambda\bar{\xi}\cdot x}\chi_{\lambda}(x)].\qquad

We need the following geometric optics Lemma:

Lemma 9.1
.

There is a leading order expansion of the form

\displaystyle T^{\ell}\Theta_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=e^{i\lambda\bar{\xi}\cdot x}\lambda^{-1+2\delta}\chi_{\lambda}m^{\ell}(\bar{\xi})+\mathrm{ErrV}^{\ell}

(95)

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{ErrV}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=O(\lambda^{-1/2}\lambda^{-1+2\delta}\lambda^{-\delta})=O(\lambda^{-3/2+\delta})

(96)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\ell}\Theta_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=(i\lambda)\bar{\xi}_{\ell}e^{i\lambda\bar{\xi}\cdot x}\chi_{\lambda}+\mathrm{ErrS}_{\ell}

(97)

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{ErrS}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=O(\lambda^{1-\delta-1/2})

(98)

We prove the expansion for the velocity field. The proof for the scalar gradient is similar. Let
K_{\lambda}^{\ell}
denote the convolution kernel representing
T^{\ell}P_{\lambda}
. Observe that

\displaystyle T^{\ell}\Theta_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\int e^{i\lambda\bar{\xi}\cdot(x-h)}\chi_{\lambda}(x-h)K_{\lambda}^{\ell}(h)dh

(99)

\displaystyle=e^{i\lambda\bar{\xi}\cdot x}\int e^{-i\lambda\bar{\xi}\cdot h}\chi_{\lambda}(x-h)K_{\lambda}^{\ell}(h)dh

(100)

\displaystyle=e^{i\lambda\bar{\xi}\cdot x}\int e^{-i\lambda\bar{\xi}\cdot h}\chi_{\lambda}(x)K_{\lambda}^{\ell}(h)dh+\mathrm{ErrV}

(101)

Notice that the leading order term is equal to the one in (
95
). We estimate the error term by writing

\displaystyle\mathrm{ErrV}

\displaystyle=e^{i\lambda\bar{\xi}\cdot x}\int\int_{0}^{1}e^{-i\lambda\bar{\xi}\cdot h}\nabla_{a}\chi_{\lambda}(x-\sigma h)h^{a}K_{\lambda}^{\ell}(h)d\sigma dh

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{ErrV}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{\sigma,h}\|\nabla\chi_{\lambda}(\cdot-\sigma h)\|_{L^{2}}\cdot\|h|K_{\lambda}|\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{1/2-\delta}\cdot\lambda^{-1-1+2\delta}=O(\lambda^{-3/2+\delta})

Note that the reason we gain by a factor of
\lambda^{-1/2}
compared to the main term is that differentiating
\chi
costs only
\lambda^{1/2}
, but gains a factor of
\lambda^{-1}
coming from the factor of
h
. The same observations lead to the other equations (
97
) and (
98
).

We now let
\psi
be any test function with
\psi(0)=1
. We claim that

\displaystyle B(\psi,\theta_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\int\psi T^{\ell}\theta_{\lambda}\nabla_{\ell}\theta_{\lambda}dx=\int\psi(T^{\ell}\Theta_{\lambda}\nabla_{\ell}\Theta_{-\lambda}+T^{\ell}\Theta_{\lambda}\nabla_{\ell}\Theta_{-\lambda})dx+o(1)

To see that the other terms are
o(1)
, consider
\int\psi T^{\ell}\Theta_{\lambda}\nabla_{\ell}\Theta_{\lambda}
. We can write this as an integral in frequency space by Plancharel, and note that the Fourier transform of
T^{\ell}\Theta_{\lambda}\nabla_{\ell}\Theta_{\lambda}
, which is localized to frequencies of the order
\lambda
, has
L^{2}
norm bounded by a polynomial in
\lambda
. By applying dominated convergence in frequency space, the integral tends to zero as claimed.

We now consider the main term, which we expand as

\displaystyle B(\psi,\theta_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=(-i\lambda)\lambda^{-1+2\delta}\int\psi\chi_{\lambda}^{2}(m^{\ell}(\bar{\xi})\bar{\xi}_{\ell}-m^{\ell}(-\bar{\xi})\bar{\xi}_{\ell})dx

\displaystyle+\lambda^{-1+2\delta}\int\psi\chi_{\lambda}m^{\ell}(\bar{\xi})\mathrm{ErrS}_{\ell}-(i\lambda)\int\psi\chi_{\lambda}\bar{\xi}_{\ell}\mathrm{ErrV}^{\ell}

\displaystyle+\int\psi\mathrm{ErrS}_{\ell}\mathrm{ErrV}^{\ell}dx

By bounding
\|\psi\|
in
L^{\infty}
and taking the other factors in
L^{2}
using
\|\chi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\lambda^{-\delta}
and (
96
),(
98
), we see that the terms on the second line are
O(\lambda^{-1/2})
while the error term in the last line is
O(\lambda^{-1})
.

The term on the first line is therefore the main term. The cutoff
\chi_{\lambda}
is normalized so that
\lambda^{2\delta}\chi_{\lambda}^{2}~\rightharpoonup~A\delta_{0}
for some
A\neq 0
. Since we have assumed a choice of
\bar{\xi}
such that
m^{\ell}(\bar{\xi})\bar{\xi}_{\ell}-m^{\ell}(-\bar{\xi})\bar{\xi}_{\ell}=2m^{\ell}(\bar{\xi})\bar{\xi}_{\ell}
is not zero (recall that
m
is odd) and a choice of
\psi
that does not vanish at the origin, we see as desired that
B(\psi,\theta_{\lambda})
has a nontrivial limit as
\lambda\to\infty
.
∎

We have now established that the velocity field is incompressible; that is, the multiplier satisfies
m(\xi)\cdot\xi=0
. Our next task is to show that the mSQG nonlinearity is the only one consistent with the special bound (
S
). This fact follows from the following proposition.

Proposition 9.2
.

Let
T^{\ell}
be incompressible with odd multiplier
m
that is homogeneous of degree
-1+2\delta
. If
m
is not a scalar multiple of the mSQG multiplier, then there cannot exist a bound of the form (
S
) for the corresponding nonlinearity.

Proof.

Let
M^{\ell}
be the mSQG multiplier that is homogeneous of degree
-1+2\delta
, which is a vector field tangent to the unit circle of constant length when restricted to the unit circle. Since we work in two dimensions and
m(\xi)
satisfies
m(\xi)\cdot\xi=0
(i.e.
m
is also tangent to the unit circle), we can deduce that
m
has the form
m^{\ell}(\xi)=H(\xi)M^{\ell}(\xi)
for some function
H
that is homogeneous of degree zero and smooth away from the origin.

Suppose now that
m
is not a constant multiple of the mSQG multiplier. Then
H
is not constant on the unit circle, and thus there exists a
\bar{\xi}\in S^{1}
such that
\nabla_{\ell}H(\bar{\xi})M^{\ell}(\bar{\xi})\neq 0
.

We now construct a family of scalars
\Theta_{\lambda}
with frequency support in
\xi\sim\lambda
that oscillate in the
\bar{\xi}
direction. Set

\displaystyle\Theta_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{1-\delta}P_{\approx\lambda}[e^{i\lambda\bar{\xi}\cdot x}\varphi_{\lambda}(x)]

where the multiplier for
P_{\approx\lambda}
is a bump function adapted to
|\xi-\lambda\bar{\xi}|\leq\lambda/4
, and where
\varphi_{\lambda}(x)=\lambda^{d/2}\varphi(\lambda x)
is a rescaled bump function normalized so that
\int\varphi_{\lambda}^{2}dx=\int\varphi(x)dx=1
. Define
\theta_{\lambda}=\Theta_{\lambda}+\bar{\Theta}_{\lambda}
, so that
\theta_{\lambda}
is real-valued. Note that the family
\theta_{\lambda}
is uniformly bounded in
\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}
. Also note the direct analogy with the construction of
\Theta_{\lambda}
in line (
79
).

Now let
\psi
be an arbitrary test function. By incompressibility we have

\displaystyle B(\psi,\theta_{\lambda})=\int\psi T^{\ell}\theta_{\lambda}\nabla_{\ell}\theta_{\lambda}dx=-\int\nabla_{\ell}\psi\theta_{\lambda}T^{\ell}\theta_{\lambda}dx

We also have
\int\nabla_{\ell}\psi\,T^{\ell}\Theta_{\lambda}\,\Theta_{\lambda}dx=o(1)
as
\lambda\to\infty
and similarly for
\Theta_{\lambda}
replaced by
\bar{\Theta}_{\lambda}
since
\nabla_{\ell}\psi
is Schwartz and
T^{\ell}\Theta_{\lambda}\Theta_{\lambda}
has frequency support in
|\xi|\sim\lambda
and has
L^{2}
norm bounded by a polynomial in
\lambda
.

This vanishing leaves us with

\displaystyle-B(\psi,\theta_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\int\nabla_{\ell}\psi(\underbrace{T^{\ell}\Theta_{\lambda}\bar{\Theta}_{\lambda}+T^{\ell}\bar{\Theta}_{\lambda}\Theta_{\lambda}}_{\equiv N^{\ell}_{\lambda}})dx+o(1).

In other words the main term is the high-high to low frequency cascade once we establish it is nonzero.

Now recall that the machinery of Section
5
gives us a divergence form representation of the form

\displaystyle N^{\ell}_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{j}B_{\lambda}^{j\ell}[\Theta_{\lambda},\bar{\Theta}_{\lambda}]

\displaystyle B_{\lambda}^{j\ell}[\Theta_{\lambda},\bar{\Theta}_{\lambda}]

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}K_{\lambda}^{j\ell}(h_{1},h_{2})\Theta_{\lambda}(x-h_{1})\bar{\Theta}_{\lambda}(x-h_{2})dh_{1}dh_{2}

\displaystyle\widehat{K}_{\lambda}^{j\ell}(\zeta,\eta)

\displaystyle=-i\int_{0}^{1}\nabla^{j}m_{\lambda}^{\ell}(\sigma\zeta-(1-\sigma)\eta)d\sigma\hat{\chi}_{\lambda}(\zeta)\hat{\chi}_{\lambda}(\eta)

where
m_{\lambda}
is a cutoff version of
m
adapted to frequency
\lambda
and where
\hat{\chi}_{\lambda}
is a cutoff adapted to frequencies of size
\lambda
. We can also absorb the factors of
P_{\lambda}
into the kernel
K
to get

\displaystyle N^{\ell}_{\lambda}=\nabla_{j}\tilde{B}_{\lambda}^{j\ell}[e^{i\lambda\bar{\xi}\cdot x}\varphi_{\lambda},e^{-i\lambda\bar{\xi}\cdot x}\varphi_{\lambda}]

(102)

\displaystyle\tilde{B}_{\lambda}^{j\ell}

\displaystyle[\Theta_{\lambda},\bar{\Theta}_{\lambda}]=\lambda^{2-2\delta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}\tilde{K}_{\lambda}^{j\ell}(h_{1},h_{2})e^{i\lambda\bar{\xi}\cdot(x-h_{1})}\varphi_{\lambda}(x-h_{1})e^{-i\lambda\bar{\xi}\cdot(x-h_{2})}\varphi_{\lambda}(x-h_{2})dh_{1}dh_{2}

(103)

\displaystyle\eqref{eq:bilinFormToExpand}=\lambda^{2-2\delta}\int e^{i\lambda\bar{\xi}\cdot h_{1}}e^{-i\lambda\bar{\xi}\cdot h_{2}}\lambda\varphi^{2}(\lambda^{1/2}x)\tilde{K}_{\lambda}^{j\ell}(h_{1},h_{2})dh_{1}dh_{2}

(104)

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\lambda^{2-2\delta}\int e^{i\lambda\bar{\xi}\cdot(h_{1}-h_{2})}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{d\sigma}\lambda\varphi(\lambda^{1/2}(x-\sigma h_{1}))\varphi(\lambda^{1/2}(x-\sigma h_{2}))\tilde{K}_{\lambda}^{j\ell}(h_{1},h_{2})dh_{1}dh_{2}d\sigma}_{\equiv E_{\lambda}}

(105)

We then have

\displaystyle B(\psi,\theta_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\int\nabla_{j}\nabla_{\ell}\psi\eqref{eq:leadingTerm2}^{j\ell}dx+\int\nabla_{j}\nabla_{\ell}\psi E_{\lambda}^{j\ell}dx+o(1)

(106)

By the computation following (
92
), we have that
\int\nabla_{j}\nabla_{\ell}\psi E_{\lambda}^{j\ell}dx=o(1)
. The leading term is then derived using the degree
-1+2\delta
homogeneity of the multiplier as

\displaystyle\eqref{eq:leadingTerm2}^{j\ell}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2-2\delta}\lambda\varphi^{2}(\lambda^{1/2}x)\widehat{\tilde{K}_{\lambda}^{j\ell}}(\lambda\bar{\xi},-\lambda\bar{\xi})

\displaystyle=\lambda\varphi^{2}(\lambda^{1/2}x)\nabla^{j}m^{\ell}(\bar{\xi})

\displaystyle=\lambda\varphi^{2}(\lambda^{1/2}x)(\nabla^{j}H(\bar{\xi})M^{\ell}(\bar{\xi})+H\nabla^{j}M^{\ell}(\bar{\xi}))

where we recall that
m^{\ell}=H(\xi)M^{\ell}
, where
M^{\ell}
is the mSQG Biot-Savart law. We can thus write

\displaystyle\eqref{eq:leadingTerm2}^{j\ell}

\displaystyle=\lambda\varphi^{2}(\lambda^{1/2}x)\left(\frac{1}{d}\nabla_{i}H(\bar{\xi})M^{i}(\bar{\xi})\delta^{j\ell}+\mathring{A}^{j\ell}\right)

where
\mathring{A}^{j\ell}
is a (fixed, constant) trace-free matrix, since we know
\nabla^{j}M^{\ell}
is trace-free.

Assuming the special bound (
S
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\nabla_{i}H(\bar{\xi})M^{i}(\bar{\xi})}{d}\Delta\psi(0)+\mathring{A}^{j\ell}\nabla_{j}\nabla_{\ell}\psi(0)\right|=\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}|B(\psi,\theta_{\lambda})|\lesssim\|\mathring{\nabla}^{2}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}

since
\limsup\|\theta_{\lambda}\|_{\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}}^{2}
is bounded. This bound, however, implies the estimate
\|\Delta\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|\mathring{\nabla}^{2}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}
for all test functions, which is a false estimate (as can be seen by considering test functions that approximate
\chi(x)|x|^{2}\log|x|^{2}
,
\chi
a smooth bump function).

Our conclusion is then that the function
H
in the decomposition
m^{\ell}(\xi)=H(\xi)M^{\ell}(\xi)
must be a constant, so that there is no point on the unit circle where
M(\xi)\cdot\nabla H(\xi)\neq 0
. Thus,
m
is a constant multiple of the mSQG Biot-Savart law under the assumption that the multiplier is odd.
∎

9.3
The case of an incompressible velocity field with even multiplier

We want to show that, outside a certain exceptional case, the active scalar nonlinearities that satisfy the exceptional bound (
S
) must have vanishing even part. We first eliminate the case of an incompressible even multiplier (that is,
\nabla_{\ell}T^{\ell}[\theta]=0
). In this case the nonlinearity has the form

\displaystyle B(\psi,\theta)

\displaystyle=-\int\nabla_{\ell}\psi\,\theta\,T^{\ell}\theta dx

(107)

after an integration by parts.

Proposition 9.3
.

If the velocity field
T^{\ell}
is incompressible and the multiplier
m
has nonzero even part, then there is a test function
\psi
and a sequence
\theta_{\lambda}
with Fourier support in an annulus
\{|\xi|\sim\lambda\}
such that
\|\theta_{\lambda}\|_{\dot{H}^{-1/2+\delta}}=O(1)
and such that
B(\psi,\theta_{\lambda})
has a nontrivial limit. As a consequence, there cannot exist any estimate of the form

\displaystyle|B(\psi,\theta)|\lesssim\|\psi\|_{X}\|\theta\|_{H^{s}}^{2},\qquad\psi\in{\cal C}_{c}^{\infty},\theta\in H^{s}

(108)

for any
s<-1/2+\delta
and any norm
X
on
{\cal C}_{c}^{\infty}
.

Proof.

Indeed, suppose the even part of
m
takes on a nonzero value at some
\bar{\xi}
on the unit circle. We construct

\displaystyle\Theta_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{1/2-\delta}P_{\lambda}[e^{i\lambda\bar{\xi}\cdot x}\lambda^{d/4}\varphi(\lambda^{1/2}x)],\qquad\theta_{\lambda}=\Theta_{\lambda}+\bar{\Theta}_{\lambda}

with the properties that
\varphi
is a bump function with
\int|\varphi|^{2}dx=1
,
P_{\lambda}
is a Fourier multiplier adapted to
|\xi-\lambda\bar{\xi}|<\lambda/5
.

Similar to Lemma
9.1
, we have a leading order expansion where the
L^{2}
norm of the errors improves on the main term by a factor of
\lambda^{-1/2}

Lemma 9.2
.

There is a leading order expansion of the form

\displaystyle T^{\ell}\Theta_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-1/2+\delta}e^{i\lambda\bar{\xi}\cdot x}\lambda^{d/4}\varphi(\lambda^{1/2}x)m^{\ell}(\bar{\xi})+\mathrm{ErrV}^{\ell}

(109)

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{ErrV}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=O(\lambda^{-1/2}\lambda^{-1/2+\delta})=O(\lambda^{-1+\delta})

(110)

\displaystyle\Theta_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{1/2-\delta}e^{i\lambda\bar{\xi}\cdot x}\lambda^{d/4}\varphi(\lambda^{1/2}x)+\mathrm{ErrS}

(111)

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{ErrS}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=O(\lambda^{-1/2}\lambda^{1/2-\delta})=O(\lambda^{-\delta})

(112)

We omit the proof, which is essentially the same as that of Lemma
9.1
.

Arguing as in the proof of Proposition
9.1
and using (
107
) we obtain

\displaystyle-B(\psi,\theta_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\int\nabla_{\ell}\psi(\bar{\Theta}_{\lambda}T^{\ell}\Theta_{\lambda}+\Theta_{\lambda}T^{\ell}\bar{\Theta}_{\lambda})dx+o(1)

\displaystyle=\int\nabla_{\ell}\psi\lambda^{d/2}\varphi^{2}(\lambda^{1/2}x)(m^{\ell}(\bar{\xi})+m^{\ell}(-\bar{\xi}))dx+o(1)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\ell}\psi(0)(m^{\ell}(\bar{\xi})+m^{\ell}(-\bar{\xi}))+o(1).

Since
\bar{\xi}
was chosen so that
m^{\ell}(\bar{\xi})+m^{\ell}(-\bar{\xi})\neq 0
, this limit is nontrivial if
\psi
is chosen appropriately, which concludes the proof of Proposition
9.3
.
∎

9.4
The case of a more general even multiplier

It is more difficult to rule out an estimate of the form (
S
) when we consider compressible velocity fields with an even multiplier. In this case we must do a more careful analysis of the nonlinearity

\displaystyle Q[\theta]

\displaystyle=T^{\ell}\theta\nabla_{\ell}\theta.

There is a cancellation in this nonlinearity because the multiplier
m^{\ell}
of
T^{\ell}
is even while the multiplier
\iota_{\ell}=i\xi_{\ell}
for
\nabla_{\ell}
, has the opposite parity, being odd. In what follows we will skip several details since they are similar to what has already been presented.

Suppose that
\theta=\Theta_{\lambda}
has frequency support in
|\xi_{\lambda}|
and let
m_{\lambda}
be a localized version of
m
with frequency support in
|\xi|\sim\lambda
. We can perform frequency space calculations that are analogous to those of Section
5
(in particular the derivation of (
41
)) to obtain

\displaystyle m_{\lambda}^{\ell}(\xi-\eta)\iota_{\ell}(\eta)

\displaystyle+m_{\lambda}^{\ell}(\eta)\iota_{\ell}(\xi-\eta)=-m_{\lambda}^{\ell}(\xi-\eta)\iota_{\ell}(-\eta)+m_{\lambda}^{\ell}(-\eta)\iota_{\ell}(\xi-\eta)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{d\sigma}\left(\iota_{\ell}(\sigma\xi-\eta)m_{\lambda}^{\ell}((1-\sigma)\xi-\eta)\right)d\sigma

\displaystyle=\xi_{a}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\nabla^{a}\iota_{\ell}(\sigma\xi-\eta)m_{\lambda}^{\ell}((1-\sigma)\xi-\eta)-\iota_{\ell}(\sigma\xi-\eta)\nabla^{a}m_{\lambda}^{\ell}((1-\sigma)\xi-\eta)\right)d\sigma

\displaystyle=i\xi_{a}\hat{K}^{a}_{\lambda}(\xi-\eta,\eta).

The definition of
\iota_{\ell}=i\xi_{\ell}
gives
\nabla^{a}\iota_{\ell}=i\delta^{a}_{\ell}
. We set

\displaystyle\widehat{\tilde{K}^{a}_{\lambda}}(\zeta,\eta)

\displaystyle\equiv i\int_{0}^{1}\left(m^{a}(\sigma\zeta-(1-\sigma)\eta)-(\sigma\zeta_{\ell}-(1-\sigma)\eta_{\ell})\nabla^{a}m^{\ell}(\sigma\zeta-(1-\sigma)\eta)\right)d\sigma\chi_{\lambda}(\zeta)\chi_{\lambda}(-\eta),

where
\chi_{\lambda}
is the frequency cutoff adapted to
|\xi-\lambda\bar{\xi}|\leq\lambda/5
that is the symbol for the
P_{\lambda}
in our usual definition of

\displaystyle\Theta_{\lambda}=\lambda^{1/2-\delta}P_{\lambda}[e^{i\lambda\bar{\xi}\cdot x}\lambda^{d/4}\phi(\lambda^{1/2}x)].

We then have

\displaystyle\widehat{\tilde{K}^{a}_{\lambda}}(\lambda\bar{\xi},-\lambda\bar{\xi})=\hat{K}^{a}_{\lambda}(\lambda\bar{\xi},-\lambda\bar{\xi})

\displaystyle=im^{a}(\lambda\bar{\xi})-i\lambda\bar{\xi}_{\ell}\nabla^{a}m^{\ell}(\lambda\bar{\xi})=i\lambda^{-1+2\delta}(m^{a}(\bar{\xi})-\bar{\xi}_{\ell}\nabla^{a}m^{\ell}(\bar{\xi}))

\displaystyle\widehat{\tilde{K}^{a}_{\lambda}}(\lambda\bar{\xi},-\lambda\bar{\xi})

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-1+2\delta}(m^{a}(\bar{\xi})-\bar{\xi}_{\ell}\nabla^{a}m^{\ell}(\bar{\xi})),

and using this expression we obtain for any test function
\psi

\displaystyle B(\psi,\Theta_{\lambda}+\bar{\Theta}_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\int\psi(T^{\ell}\Theta_{\lambda}\,\bar{\Theta}_{\lambda}+T^{\ell}\bar{\Theta}_{\lambda}\,\Theta_{\lambda})dx+o(1)

\displaystyle=\int\psi\lambda^{1-2\delta}\lambda^{d/2}\nabla_{a}\int\tilde{K}_{\lambda}^{a}(h_{1},h_{2})e^{i\lambda\bar{\xi}\cdot(h_{1}-h_{2})}\varphi(x-h_{1})\varphi(x-h_{2})dh_{1}dh_{2}dx+o(1)

\displaystyle=-\int\nabla_{a}\psi\lambda^{1-2\delta}\widehat{\tilde{K}^{a}_{\lambda}}(\lambda\bar{\xi},-\lambda\bar{\xi})\lambda^{d/2}\varphi^{2}(\lambda^{1/2}(x))dx+o(1)

\displaystyle=-\nabla_{a}\psi(0)(m^{a}(\bar{\xi})-\bar{\xi}_{\ell}\nabla^{a}m^{\ell}(\bar{\xi}))+o(1).

As in the previous arguments, we can
conclude that the nonlinearity has no bounded extension to
C_{c}^{\infty}\times\dot{H}^{s}\times\dot{H}^{s}
for
s<-1/2+\delta
if there exists any
\bar{\xi}\in S^{1}
that makes the above expression nonzero. Thus (
S
), which implies boundedness on
\dot{W}^{2,\infty}\times\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}\times\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}
, here implies

\displaystyle m^{a}(\bar{\xi})-\bar{\xi}_{\ell}\nabla^{a}m^{\ell}(\bar{\xi})=2m^{a}(\bar{\xi})-\nabla^{a}(\bar{\xi}_{\ell}m^{\ell}(\bar{\xi}))=0,\qquad\mbox{ for all }\bar{\xi}\in S^{1}.

This equality implies that
m
is the gradient of
F(\xi)=\frac{1}{2}\xi\cdot m(\xi)
. In particular,
m=\nabla F
implies
F(\xi)=\frac{1}{2}\xi\cdot\nabla F
, which tells us that
F
must be homogeneous of degree two by Euler’s theorem on homogeneous functions, and hence
m=\nabla F
must be homogeneous of degree one.

At this point we are able to conclude that the special estimate (
S
) implies that the velocity field
T^{\ell}
must be a scalar multiple of the mSQG Biot-Savart law unless the multiplier is degree
1
homogeneous, the velocity field is compressible, and the even part of the multiplier is the gradient of an (odd) homogeneous function of degree
2
. We do not know if the latter class contains further examples of nonlinearities satisfying (
S
). However, our methods can be extended to show that every such multiplier is bounded on
\dot{W}^{2,\infty}\times\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}\times\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}
by deriving a double-divergence form for the nonlinearity using a second order Taylor expansion in frequency.

Appendix A
Homogeneous function spaces

In this section we gather relevant facts about the homogeneous function spaces employed in this work. Since multiple definitions of homogeneous Sobolev and Besov spaces exist in the literature, the precise definitions are important. We focus more on the case of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
since the case of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
is simpler but similar.

A.1
The space
\dot{W}^{2,\infty}

We define the spaces

\displaystyle\dot{C}_{0}^{2}

\displaystyle=\{\psi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})~:~\|\nabla^{2}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty,|\nabla^{2}\psi(x)|=o(1)\mbox{ as }|x|\to\infty\}

(113)

\displaystyle\dot{W}_{0}^{2,\infty}

\displaystyle=\{\Psi\in{\cal D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})~:~\|\nabla^{2}\Psi\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty,|\nabla^{2}\psi(x)|=o(1)\mbox{ as }|x|\to\infty\}

(114)

\displaystyle\dot{W}^{2,\infty}

\displaystyle=\{\Psi\in{\cal D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})~:~\|\nabla^{2}\Psi\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty\}

(115)

We equip them with the semi-norm
[\Psi]_{\dot{W}^{2,\infty}}=\|\nabla^{2}\Psi\|_{L^{\infty}}
. Each space fails to be Hausdorff (unless one considers the Hausdorff quotient), since linear polynomials have semi-norm equal to
0
.

The space
\dot{C}_{0}^{2}
is the closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}
in the semi-norm
\|\nabla^{2}\Psi\|_{L^{\infty}}
.
That is, if
\nabla^{2}\psi_{n}\to\nabla^{2}\Psi
uniformly, then
\nabla^{2}\Psi
is continuous, bounded, and satisfies
|\nabla^{2}\Psi(x)|=o(1)
as
|x|\to\infty
. Conversely, let
\Psi\in\dot{C}_{0}^{2}
and define
\psi_{n}(x)=\phi_{n}(x)\eta_{1/n}\ast\Psi
, where
\phi_{n}(x)=\phi(x/n)
is a bump function equal to
1
on
|x|\leq n
, and
\eta_{\epsilon}(x)=\epsilon^{-2}\eta(x/\epsilon)
is a standard mollifying kernel that is even, non-negative, and supported in
|x|<\epsilon
. Since
|\nabla^{k}\phi_{n}(x)|\lesssim_{k}|x|^{-k}
, to check
\|\nabla^{2}(\Psi-\psi_{n})\|_{L^{\infty}}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
it suffices to show

\displaystyle|\nabla^{2}\Psi(x)|=o(1),\qquad|\nabla\Psi(x)|=o(|x|),\qquad|\Psi(x)|=o(|x|^{2}),\qquad\mbox{as }|x|\to\infty.

(116)

The first of these is our assumption
\Psi\in\dot{C}^{2}_{0}
, and it implies the latter two. For example, to prove the last one, use Taylor’s theorem with remainder to write

\displaystyle\Psi(x)=\Psi(0)+x^{a}\nabla_{a}\Psi(0)+x^{a}x^{b}\int_{0}^{1}\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}\Psi(\sigma x)(1-\sigma)d\sigma

The first two terms are clearly
o(|x|^{2})
. To check that the last one is, let
\epsilon>0
,
0<\delta<1
be given, and choose
R_{0}>0
so that
|\nabla^{2}\Psi(y)|<\epsilon
for all
|y|>\delta R_{0}
. Then for all
|x|\geq R_{0}
, the integrand is bounded by
\max\{\|\nabla^{2}\Psi\|_{L^{\infty}},\epsilon\cdot 1_{\sigma\geq\delta}\}
, which means that the integral itself is
o(1)
as
|x|\to\infty
and
\Psi
itself is
o(|x|^{2})
as desired. The proof that
|\nabla\Psi(x)|=o(|x|)
is similar.

Now suppose
\Psi\in\dot{W}^{2,\infty}
. Some basic properties of
\Psi
are that
\Psi(x)
and
\nabla\Psi(x)
are Lipschitz continuous and satisfy the bounds
|\Psi(x)|=O(|x|^{2}),|\nabla\Psi(x)|=O(|x|)
as
|x|\to\infty
.
Indeed, let
\Psi_{\epsilon}=\eta_{\epsilon}\ast\Psi
be a regularization of
\Psi
with a non-negative, even mollifying kernel
\eta_{\epsilon}
. Non-negativity of the mollifying kernel implies that
|\nabla^{2}\Psi_{\epsilon}(x)|\leq|\nabla^{2}\Psi(x)|
pointwise. Then the Taylor expansion formula
\nabla\Psi_{\epsilon}(y)-\nabla\Psi_{\epsilon}(x)=(y^{a}-x^{a})\int_{0}^{1}\nabla_{a}\nabla\Psi_{\epsilon}((1-\sigma)x+\sigma y)d\sigma
shows that
\nabla\Psi_{\epsilon}
are uniformly Lipschitz.
For any particular
\epsilon>0
we can subtract a linear polynomial
L_{\epsilon}(x)
from
\Psi_{\epsilon}(x)
so that
\widetilde{\Psi}_{\epsilon}(x)\coloneqq\Psi_{\epsilon}(x)-L_{\epsilon}(x)
satisfies
\widetilde{\Psi}_{\epsilon}(0)=0
,
\nabla\widetilde{\Psi}_{\epsilon}(0)=0
. Then the sequence
\nabla\widetilde{\Psi}_{\epsilon}
is locally uniformly bounded and uniformly Lipschitz.
The integral formulas
\widetilde{\Psi}_{\epsilon}(x)=x^{a}x^{b}\int_{0}^{1}\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}\Psi_{\epsilon}(\sigma x)(1-\sigma)d\sigma
and
\nabla\widetilde{\Psi}_{\epsilon}(x)=x^{a}\int_{0}^{1}\nabla_{a}\nabla\Psi_{\epsilon}(\sigma x)d\sigma
then guarantee that

\displaystyle|\widetilde{\Psi}_{\epsilon}(x)|\leq\|\nabla^{2}\Psi\|_{L^{\infty}}|x|^{2},\qquad|\nabla\widetilde{\Psi}_{\epsilon}(x)|\leq\|\nabla^{2}\Psi\|_{L^{\infty}}|x|

(117)

hold uniformly in
\epsilon
. Since
\widetilde{\Psi}_{\epsilon}
and
\nabla\widetilde{\Psi}_{\epsilon}
are equi-bounded and Lipschitz locally uniformly in
\epsilon
and they converge uniformly on compact sets by Arzelà-Ascoli along a subsequence to some
\widetilde{\Psi}
that satisfies the growth bounds in (
117
) and
\nabla^{2}\widetilde{\Psi}=\nabla^{2}\Psi
as a distribution. Since
\Psi
and
\widetilde{\Psi}
differ by a linear polynomial, we have that
|\Psi(x)|=O(|x|^{2})
and
|\nabla\Psi(x)|=O(|x|)
, and that
\nabla\Psi=\nabla\widetilde{\Psi}+C
is Lipschitz continuous as desired.

There exists a sequence
\psi_{n}
in
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that
\nabla^{2}\psi_{n}\rightharpoonup\nabla^{2}\Psi
in
L^{\infty}
weak-*.
It suffices to take
\psi_{n}(x)=\phi(x/n)\widetilde{\Psi}_{1/n}(x)
. Then for any test function
\phi^{j\ell}(x)
supported in
|x|<n
, we have that

\displaystyle\int\phi^{j\ell}(x)\nabla_{j}\nabla_{\ell}\psi_{n}(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int\phi^{j\ell}(x)\nabla_{j}\nabla_{\ell}\widetilde{\Psi}_{1/n}(x)dx=\int\eta_{1/n}\ast\phi^{j\ell}(x)\nabla_{j}\nabla_{\ell}\Psi(x)dx

converges to
\int\phi^{j\ell}(x)\nabla_{j}\nabla_{\ell}\Psi(x)dx
as
n\to\infty
. The same convergence holds for any
\phi^{j\ell}\in L^{1}
by approximation using the bound
\sup_{n}\|\nabla^{2}\psi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty
, which is a consequence of (
117
).

When
\Psi\in\dot{W}_{0}^{2,\infty}
, this sequence has the additional property that
\|\nabla^{2}\psi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\to\|\nabla^{2}\Psi\|_{L^{\infty}}
as
n\to\infty
.
To see this fact, note that
|\nabla^{2}\widetilde{\Psi}_{\epsilon}(x)|\leq\|\nabla^{2}\Psi\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\epsilon}(x))}
by the support and non-negativity of
\eta_{\epsilon}
. We then obtain
\sup_{\epsilon\leq 1}|\nabla\widetilde{\Psi}_{\epsilon}(x)|=o(|x|)
and
\sup_{\epsilon\leq 1}|\widetilde{\Psi}_{\epsilon}(x)|=o(|x|^{2})
by the same integral formulas used to prove (
116
). With these properties,
|\nabla^{k}\phi_{n}(x)|\lesssim|x|^{-k}
and
\mathrm{supp}\,\nabla^{k}\phi_{n}\subseteq\{|x|\geq n\}
for
k=1,2
, we can see that
\|\nabla^{2}(\phi_{n}(x)\widetilde{\Psi}_{1/n})\|_{L^{\infty}}
converges to
\|\nabla^{2}\Psi\|_{L^{\infty}}
as
n\to\infty
as desired.

Finally,
the space
\dot{W}^{2,\infty}
is complete in that every Cauchy sequence converges,
and as a consequence so are
\dot{C}_{0}^{2}
and
\dot{W}_{0}^{2,\infty}
since they are closed subspaces. Namely suppose
\Phi_{n}
is Cauchy in
\dot{W}^{2,\infty}
, and let
A_{j\ell}\in L^{\infty}
be the strong limit of
\nabla_{j}\nabla_{\ell}\Phi_{n}
. The functions
\widetilde{\Phi}_{n}
normalized so that
\widetilde{\Phi}_{n}(0)=0,\nabla\widetilde{\Phi}_{n}(0)=0
, and
\nabla^{2}\widetilde{\Phi}_{n}=\nabla^{2}\Phi_{n}
are also Cauchy. Then by the growth bounds similar to (
117
),
\widetilde{\Phi}_{n}
and its first derivatives are continuous, uniformly bounded on compact sets, and uniformly Lipschitz, implying the existence of a subsequence
n_{k}
such that
\widetilde{\Phi}_{n_{k}}
converges in
C^{1}
on compact sets to a some
\widetilde{\Phi}_{\infty}
. This
\widetilde{\Phi}_{\infty}
does not depend on the subsequence; in fact, its weak second derivative is given by
\nabla_{j}\nabla_{\ell}\widetilde{\Phi}_{\infty}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\nabla_{j}\nabla_{\ell}\widetilde{\Phi}_{n_{k}}=A_{j\ell}
. Now the full sequence must converge to
\widetilde{\Phi}_{\infty}
, for otherwise we would have a
\delta>0
such that
\|\nabla_{j}\nabla_{\ell}\Phi_{n}-A_{j\ell}\|_{L^{\infty}}=\|\nabla_{j}\nabla_{\ell}\Phi_{n}-\nabla_{j}\nabla_{\ell}\widetilde{\Phi}_{\infty}\|_{L^{\infty}}\geq\delta
for infinitely many
n
, contradicting the definition of
A_{j\ell}
.

A.2
The space
\dot{H}^{s}

We employ a definition based on Littlewood-Paley theory, since it connects directly to the analysis used here. For clarity, we focus on Euclidean space and generalize the dimension to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We will establish among other things density results for
\dot{H}^{-1+\delta}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
,
\delta\geq 0
.

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
\theta
a tempered distribution, define

\displaystyle\|\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}\coloneqq\sum_{k}\left(2^{sk}\|P_{k}\theta\|_{L^{2}}\right)^{2}=\|2^{sk}\|P_{k}\theta\|_{L^{2}}\|_{\ell_{k}^{2}}^{2}

This norm is equivalent to the norm
\|\|\Lambda^{sk}P_{k}\theta\|_{L^{2}}\|_{\ell_{k}^{2}}
with
\Lambda=\sqrt{-\Delta}
.

We define the space
\dot{H}^{s}
to be the space of tempered distributions with
\|\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}
finite, and with the vanishing zero mode property that
\lim_{m\to\infty}P_{\leq-m}\theta=0
weakly in
{\cal S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. (For periodic
\theta
,
\lim_{m\to\infty}P_{\leq-m}\theta=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\theta(x)dx
is the mean value of
\theta
, so we are assuming
\theta
has mean zero in that case.) This additional condition makes
\|\cdot\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}
a norm rather than a semi-norm, since the series expansion
\theta=P_{\leq-m}\theta+\sum_{k\geq-m}P_{k}\theta
always converges weakly in
{\cal S}^{\prime}
for
m
fixed. It also guarantees that
{\cal S}_{0}
, the space of Schwartz functions with compact Fourier support away from
0
, is dense in
\dot{H}^{s}
.

In particular, in contrast to other definitions (e.g.
[
20
]
) we exclude constants and polynomials, whose Fourier transforms are linear cominations of the delta function at the origin and its derivatives, as they would have zero norm.

A common alternative definition (e.g.
[
1
]
) is to require that
\widehat{\theta}\in L_{loc}^{1}(\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{d}})
and
\int|\xi|^{2s}|\widehat{\theta}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi<\infty
, which always implies membership in the space
\dot{H}^{s}
defined above. Conversely, when
s<d/2
and
\theta\in\dot{H}^{s}
, it is true that
\widehat{\theta}\in L_{loc}^{1}(\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{d}})
, and the two definitions of
\dot{H}^{s}
are equivalent. Namely, local integrability of
\widehat{\theta}
away from
0
is true for any
s
by Plancharel, while for
s<d/2
the sequence
\widehat{P_{\leq 0}\theta}-\widehat{P_{\leq-m}\theta}
is Cauchy in
L^{1}(\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{d}})
as
m\to\infty
, and converges weakly in
{\cal S}^{\prime}(\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{d}})
to
\widehat{P_{\leq 0}\theta}
, implying local integrability of
\hat{\theta}
near
0
. In fact, for
s<d/2
, the
\dot{H}^{s}
defined by either condition is complete (as explained below).

We will use often the following inequality, valid for Schwartz
\psi
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

\displaystyle\|P_{\approx k}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{L}\min\{2^{(d/2)k}\|\psi\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},2^{-Lk}\|\nabla^{L}P_{\approx k}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\}.

(118)

The
L^{1}
control is useful at low frequencies, while the control of
\nabla^{L}\psi
in
L^{2}
will be used at high frequency. (On the torus, we use instead that
P_{k}\psi=0
for sufficiently large, negative
k
and the analysis is simpler, though the properties of
\dot{H}^{s}
are slightly different.)

The regime
s>-d/2

We now consider
s>-d/2
and consider the special case of
\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
at the end. The following properties hold for
s=-d/2+\delta
where
\delta>0
. Writing
\delta=z+\bar{\delta}
with
z\geq 0
an integer and
0<\bar{\delta}\leq 1
, we have the interpolation estimate

\displaystyle\|\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{-d/2+z+\bar{\delta}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla^{z+1}\theta\|_{L^{2}}^{\alpha}\|\nabla^{z}\theta\|_{L^{1}}^{1-\alpha},\qquad\alpha=2\bar{\delta}/(2+d),\qquad\theta\in{\cal S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(119)

A standard approach to proving bounds such as (
119
) is to bound

\displaystyle\|\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{-d/2+z+\bar{\delta}}}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{k\leq\bar{k}}\left(2^{(-d/2+\bar{\delta})k}\|\nabla^{z}P_{k}\theta\|_{L^{2}}\right)^{2}+\sum_{k\geq\bar{k}}\left(2^{(-d/2+\bar{\delta}-1)\bar{k}}\|\nabla^{z+1}P_{k}\theta\|_{L^{2}}\right)^{2}.

(120)

Applying (
118
) for
\psi=\nabla^{z}\theta
at low frequency, using the almost orthogonality of Littlewood-Paley projections for the high frequency sum, and optimizing over
\bar{k}
yields the result.

Every Schwartz function satisfies the vanishing condition
P_{\leq-m}\psi\rightharpoonup 0
in
{\cal S}^{\prime}
and belongs to
\dot{H}^{-d/2+\delta}
by (
119
) or (
120
). From this fact and (
119
) or (
120
) we obtain the density of
C_{c}^{\infty}
in
\dot{H}^{-d/2+\delta}
.

The regime
s<d/2

Now assuming
s<d/2
, two important properties of
\dot{H}^{s}
are that it is complete, and that
\dot{H}^{s}
convergence implies weak convergence in
{\cal S}^{\prime}
(for our specific definition of
\dot{H}^{s}
). To see the latter claim, let
\psi
be Schwartz and
\theta\in\dot{H}^{s}
. We use the vanishing zero mode condition to obtain

\displaystyle\langle\theta,\psi\rangle=\lim_{m\to\infty}\left[\langle\theta,P_{\leq-m}\psi\rangle+\sum_{k=-m}^{\infty}\langle P_{k}\theta,\psi\rangle\right]=\lim_{m\to\infty}\sum_{k=-m}^{\infty}\langle P_{k}\theta,P_{\approx k}\psi\rangle_{L^{2}}

Using Cauchy-Schwartz, we bound this sum by
\sum_{k}(2^{sk}\|P_{k}\theta\|_{L^{2}})(2^{-sk}\|P_{\approx k}\psi\|_{L^{2}})\lesssim\|\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\|\psi\|_{\dot{H}^{-s}}
. But
-s>-d/2
, so (
119
) with
\theta
replaced by
\psi
implies
\|\psi\|_{\dot{H}^{-s}}<\infty
. Thus the series converges absolutely and the functional
\theta\mapsto\langle\theta,\psi\rangle
is bounded on
\dot{H}^{s}
, so that
\dot{H}^{s}
convergence implies
{\cal S}^{\prime}
convergence.

We now sketch a proof of completeness. If
\theta_{n}
is Cauchy in
\dot{H}^{s}
, then
H\equiv\sup_{n}\|\theta_{n}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}
is bounded. Also, the sequences
P_{k}\theta_{n}
are Cauchy in
L^{2}
for every fixed
k
and therefore converge strongly in
L^{2}
to functions
\Theta_{k}
that satisfy
P_{\approx k}\Theta_{k}=\Theta_{k}
and
\|2^{sk}\|\Theta_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\|_{\ell_{k}^{2}}\leq H<\infty
by Fatou’s Lemma.

We can define
\langle\Theta,\psi\rangle\coloneqq\lim_{M\to\infty}\int\theta^{(M)}(x)\psi(x)dx
as a linear function on the Schwartz space by checking that the latter sequence is Cauchy in
M
. Apply Cauchy-Schwartz to bound

\displaystyle\Big|\int(\theta^{(M+\ell)}-\theta^{(M)})\psi(x)dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{M<|k|\leq M+\ell}\left|\int\Theta_{k}P_{\approx k}\psi dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{M<|k|\leq M+\ell}\left(2^{-sk}\|P_{\approx k}\psi\|_{L^{2}}\right)\left(2^{sk}\|\Theta_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\sum_{M-1<|k|}(2^{-sk}\|P_{k}\psi\|_{L^{2}})^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\|2^{sk}\|\Theta_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\|_{\ell_{k}^{2}}.

We have already noted that
\|2^{sk}\|\Theta_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\|_{\ell_{k}^{2}}\leq H<\infty
. Since
-s>-d/2
, we have that
\|\psi\|_{\dot{H}^{-s}}<\infty
. By dominated convergence, the final bound goes to
0
as
M
gets large, and hence the sequence in
M
defining
\langle\Theta,\psi\rangle
is Cauchy. Similarly one obtains
|\langle\Theta,\psi\rangle|\lesssim\|\psi\|_{\dot{H}^{-s}}H
,
-s>-d/2
, making
\Theta
a tempered distribution that is the weak limit of the
\theta^{(M)}
by (
119
).

We likewise obtain using (
118
) again that

\displaystyle|\langle P_{\leq-m}\Theta,\psi\rangle|=\lim_{M}|\langle\theta^{(M)},P_{\leq-m}\psi\rangle|\lesssim 2^{-(d/2-s)m}\|\psi\|_{L^{1}}H,

which implies the vanishing zero mode condition for
s<d/2
. Furthermore,
P_{k}\Theta=\Theta_{k}=\lim_{n\to\infty}P_{k}\theta_{n}
(strong
L^{2}
limit), since
P_{k}\theta^{(M)}
is independent of
M
for
M>|k|
. In particular,
\Theta\in\dot{H}^{s}
with
\|\Theta\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\leq H
. Finally, the inequality

\displaystyle\left|2^{sk}\|P_{k}\theta_{n}-P_{k}\Theta\|_{L^{2}}-2^{sk}\|P_{k}\theta_{m}-P_{k}\Theta\|_{L^{2}}\right|\leq 2^{sk}\|P_{k}(\theta_{n}-\theta_{m})\|_{L^{2}}

implies that
2^{sk}\|P_{k}\theta_{n}-P_{k}\Theta\|_{L^{2}}
, regarded as a sequence with values in
\ell^{2}_{k}
, is Cauchy in
n
, so it converges as
n\to\infty
in
\ell_{k}^{2}
to its pointwise limit, which is the
0
element of
\ell_{k}^{2}
. That is,
\|2^{sk}\|P_{k}\theta_{n}-P_{k}\Theta\|_{L^{2}}\|_{\ell_{k}^{2}}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, and hence
\theta_{n}\to\Theta
in
\dot{H}^{s}
.

The space
\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})

Finally, we address the case of
\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
that is of relevance to 2D Euler. In general, a distribution
\theta\in{\cal D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
belongs to
\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if and only if
\theta=\nabla_{i}u^{i}
is the divergence (in the weak sense) of a vector field
u\in L^{2}
. That is, if
\theta=\nabla_{i}u^{i}
, the vanishing zero mode condition follows from
\|P_{\leq-m}\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|\nabla\eta_{\leq-m}\|_{L^{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 2^{-(d/2+1)m}\|u\|_{L^{2}}
, while the bound

\displaystyle\|\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k}\left(2^{-k}\|\nabla_{i}P_{k}u^{i}\|_{L^{2}}\right)^{2}\lesssim\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

(121)

implies
\|\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}<\infty
. Conversely, for
\theta\in\dot{H}^{-1}
, the sequence of vector fields
u_{(m)}^{i}=\Delta^{-1}\nabla^{i}(1-P_{\leq-m})\theta
is Cauchy in
L^{2}
as
m\to\infty
, and its limit
u
satisfies
\nabla_{i}u^{i}=\theta
by the vanishing
0
mode condition.

In dimension
2
, it is no longer true that every
\theta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
belongs to
\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Rather, if
\theta\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
has a nonzero integral, then
\theta
cannot belong to
\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
since then
\int\theta(x)dx=\widehat{\theta}(0)\neq 0
implies that
\|\widehat{P_{k}\theta}\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{2}})}^{2}\sim 2^{2k}
for large
k<0
, and
\|\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}
is infinite.

For Schwartz functions
\theta
, the vanishing of
\int\theta(x)dx=0
then provides a necessary and sufficient condition to belong to
\dot{H}^{-1}
, for if this condition holds we can represent
\theta=\nabla_{a}u^{a}
as the divergence of a Schwartz vector field
u
. The existence of a Schwartz anti-divergence is simple when the support of
\widehat{\theta}
is bounded away from the origin, for in that case the Helmholtz solution
u_{\infty}^{a}=\Delta^{-1}\nabla^{a}\theta
is Schwartz and provides a suitable anti-divergence. For more general Schwartz functions
\theta
with
\int\theta(x)dx=\widehat{\theta}(0)=0
, we can Taylor expand at the origin to obtain
\widehat{\theta}(\xi)=i\xi_{a}\hat{u}_{0}^{a}
for a smooth
\hat{u}_{0}
that may have poor decay at high frequency. Gluing
\hat{u}_{0}
and
\hat{u}_{\infty}
with a smooth cutoff in frequency yields a Schwartz vector field
u^{a}
that solves
\widehat{\theta}(\xi)=(i\xi_{a})\hat{u}^{a}=\widehat{\nabla_{a}u^{a}}(\xi)
and is thus a Schwartz anti-divergence for
\theta
.

As a corollary, we have that the space of compactly supported test functions
\theta\in C_{c}^{\infty}
with
\int\theta(x)dx=0
is a dense subspace of
\dot{H}^{-1}
, for the space of Schwartz functions with frequency support away from the origin is already dense in
\dot{H}^{-1}
, and we can obtain any such
\theta=\nabla_{a}u^{a}
,
u\in L^{2}
, as the limit of the compactly supported sequence
\theta_{n}(x)=\nabla_{a}(\phi(x/n)\eta_{n}\ast u^{a})
, where
\phi
as before is a bump function and
\eta_{n}
is a suitable mollifier, which converges in
\dot{H}^{-1}
by (
121
).

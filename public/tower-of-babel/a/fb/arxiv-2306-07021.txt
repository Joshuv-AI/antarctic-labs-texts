Gradient Hölder regularity in mixed local and nonlocal linear parabolic problem
Stuti Das

Abstract
We prove the local Hölder regularity of weak solutions to the mixed local nonlocal parabolic equation of the form u t − Δ ​ u + P.V. ​ ∫ ℝ n u ⁡ ( x , t ) − u ⁡ ( y , t ) | x − y | n + 2 ​ s ​ 𝑑 y = 0 , u_{t}-\Delta u+\text{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\frac{u(x,t)-u(y,t)}{{\left|x-y\right|}^{n+2s}}}dy=0, where 0 < s < 1 \displaystyle 0<s<1 ; for every exponent α 0 ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \displaystyle\alpha_{0}\in(0,1) . Here, Δ \displaystyle\Delta is the usual Laplace operator. Next, we show that the gradients of weak solutions are also α \displaystyle\alpha -Hölder continuous for some α ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \displaystyle\alpha\in(0,1) . Our approach is purely analytic and it is based on perturbation techniques.

Introduction

Mixed local nonlocal problems are emerging areas of many recent engineering works. More specifically, these kinds of problems combine two operators with different orders of differentiation, the simplest model of this case being
\displaystyle-\Delta+(-\Delta)^{s}
, for
\displaystyle s\in(0,1)
. In this article, our main concern is the parabolic case of such operators given by
\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta+(-\Delta)^{s},
for
\displaystyle s\in(0,1)
. Due to the presence of a leading local operator together with a nonlocal lower order fractional term, it is expected that the same kind of regularity results as that of the local case should also hold here. We aim to show these regularity results using a perturbation technique, where we take the solution of the heat equation as the reference one and will use their regularity. Before going into the details, we give a brief history.

Regularity and higher regularity results for mixed local nonlocal operators have been obtained in the past few years, mainly using iteration techniques. Using the De Giorgi-Nash-Moser theory, Garain and Kinnunen
[
10
]
have obtained the local boundedness, local Hölder regularity, Harnack inequality results for the weak solutions of quasilinear elliptic equations of the type

-\Delta_{p}u+(-\Delta_{p})^{s}u=0\text{ in }\Omega,\quad 1<p<\infty,

where
\displaystyle\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
The higher Hölder regularity for the solution to the above equation for
\displaystyle 2\leq p<\infty
is obtained in
[
13
]
. The article is based on the iteration ideas of Moser. Moreover, in
[
13
]
, the authors have shown that the solution is also locally Hölder continuous for all exponents
\displaystyle\alpha\in(0,1)
.

In
[
18
]
, Xifeng Su et al. have obtained the gradient Hölder regularity of the weak solution to the mixed local nonlocal semilinear elliptic problem of form

\begin{array}[]{rcl}-\Delta u+(-\Delta)^{s}u=g(x,u)&\text{ in }&\Omega,\\
u=0&\text{ in }&\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\Omega,\end{array}

where
\displaystyle 0<s\leq 1/2
and for some
\displaystyle c>0
,
\displaystyle q\in[2,2^{*}_{s}]
, the function
\displaystyle g
satisfies
\displaystyle|g(x,t)|\leq c(1+|t|^{q-1})\text{ for a.e. }x\in\Omega,t\in\mathbb{R}
. Here
\displaystyle 2^{*}_{s}
is the critical fractional Sobolev exponent given by
\displaystyle 2^{*}_{s}=\frac{2n}{n-2s}
. Under these conditions, they have shown that the solution is in
\displaystyle C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
for all
\displaystyle\alpha\in(0,1)
. The
\displaystyle L^{\infty}
-boundedness of weak solutions (for all
\displaystyle 0<s<1
) was also obtained.

Whereas in
[
5
]
, the authors combined two different orders of differentiation and have shown that weak solutions to the equation of the type

-\Delta_{p}u+(-\Delta_{q})^{s}u=f,

where
\displaystyle p,q>1
; are locally Hölder continuous for all
\displaystyle\alpha\in(0,1)
, and their gradients are Hölder continuous for some exponent
\displaystyle\beta\in(0,1)
, by using perturbation techniques. Along with this, they have obtained some results on the boundary regularity of weak solutions. We refer [
5
, Section 1.1] for details on the assumptions for the problem.

Coming to our problem, here we deal with the regularity theory for the equation

{}\partial_{t}u-\Delta u+\mathcal{L}u=0\text{ in }\Omega_{T},

(1.1)

where
\displaystyle\Omega_{T}=\Omega\times(0,T)
for
\displaystyle T>0
, and
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}
is the operator formally defined by

\mathcal{L}u=c_{n,s}\text{ P.V. }\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{u(x,t)-u(y,t)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dy,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
\displaystyle c_{n,s}
is a suitable normalization constant, and P.V. denotes the Cauchy Principal Value.

Regarding our problem, we list the works done in the areas of regularity and boundedness. The weak Harnack inequality for solutions to the equation
Eq.

1.1
have been obtained by Garain and Kinnunen in
[
12
]
. Whereas for the quasilinear case, using De Giorgi-Nash-Moser techniques, Fang et al.
[
9
]
, have obtained that weak solutions to the equation

\partial_{t}u-\Delta_{p}u+(-\Delta_{p})^{s}u=0\text{ in }\Omega_{T},

are locally bounded for all
\displaystyle 1<p<\infty
under suitable assumptions, and are locally Hölder continuous for
\displaystyle p>2
. Garain and Kinnunen have obtained local boundedness results and continuity results for a more general quasilinear operator
[
11
]
. In
[
16
]
, Shang et al. unified the Hölder continuity results and extended it for the case
\displaystyle p<2
. The authors again obtained the Harnack inequality and, as a corollary, the local Hölder continuity for
\displaystyle p>2
case in
[
17
]
.

Organization of the article:

In this article, we will follow the perturbation ideas developed by Giuseppe Mingione and Cristiana De Filippis
[
5
]
. For this, we use the regularity properties of the heat equations, which are taken from
[
15
]
. In the next section, we fix some notations and describe the primary function spaces we need. Moreover, the definition of weak solutions for our context, as well as some auxiliary results, are provided.
We use the Steklov averages to define weak solutions and will eventually employ their convergence properties to estimate our results. In section 3, we prove the fundamental Caccioppoli inequality for our solution and an energy estimate in order to transfer the regularity of the reference solution (of the heat equation) to our case. We will end up getting some nonlocal integral that we also estimate there. Then, the following two sections will contain the proofs of our main results
Theorems

2.6
and
2.7
.

2.2
Function spaces

Fractional Sobolev Spaces:
Let
\displaystyle 0<s<1
and
\displaystyle\Omega
be a open connected subset of
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The fractional Sobolev space
\displaystyle W^{s,2}(\Omega)
is defined by

W^{s,2}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in L^{2}(\Omega):\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{\frac{n}{2}+s}}\in L^{2}(\Omega\times\Omega)\right\},

and endowed with the norm

{}\|u\|_{W^{s,2}(\Omega)}=\left(\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{2}dx+\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dxdy\right)^{1/2},

it becomes a reflexive Banach space.

The fractional Sobolev space with zero boundary values, denoted by
\displaystyle W_{0}^{s,2}(\Omega)
, is the subspace of
\displaystyle W^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, consisting of functions vanishing outside
\displaystyle\Omega
, i.e.

W_{0}^{s,2}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in W^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):u=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\Omega\right\}.

Indeed the space
\displaystyle W_{0}^{s,2}(\Omega)
is also reflexive. Readers can find the details regarding fractional Sobolev spaces in [
7
, Section 2]. The classical Sobolev space is denoted by
\displaystyle W^{1,2}(\Omega)
. The parabolic Sobolev space
\displaystyle L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\Omega)),
for
\displaystyle T>0
, consists of measurable functions
\displaystyle u
on
\displaystyle\Omega\times(0,T)
such that

\|u\|_{L^{2}\left(0,T;W^{1,2}(\Omega)\right)}=\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}^{2}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}<\infty.

The space
\displaystyle L_{\textit{loc}}^{2}(0,T;W_{\textit{loc}}^{1,2}(\Omega))
is defined by requiring the conditions above for every
\displaystyle\Omega^{\prime}\times[t_{1},t_{2}]\Subset\Omega\times(0,T)
. Here
\displaystyle\Omega^{\prime}\times[t_{1},t_{2}]\Subset\Omega\times(0,T)
denotes that
\displaystyle\overline{\Omega^{\prime}}\times\left[t_{1},t_{2}\right]
is a compact subset of
\displaystyle\Omega\times(0,T)
.

Tail Spaces:
The regularity results require some finiteness conditions on the nonlocal tails. To this end,
we recall a tail space as

L_{\alpha}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\left\{v\in L_{\textit{loc}}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|v(x)|^{q}}{1+|x|^{n+\alpha}}dx<+\infty\right\},\quad q>0\text{ and }\alpha>0.

Then, a nonlocal tail of the supremum version is defined by

\displaystyle\displaystyle{}{\operatorname{Tail}_{\infty}(v;x_{0},r,I)}{=\operatorname{Tail}_{\infty}(v;x_{0},r,t_{0}-T_{1},t_{0}+T_{2})}{:=\underset{t\in I}{\operatorname{ess}\text{sup}}\left(r^{2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B_{r}(x_{0})}\frac{|v(x,t)|}{\left|x-x_{0}\right|^{n+2s}}dx\right),}

(2.1)

where
\displaystyle(x_{0},t_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,T)
and the interval
\displaystyle I=\left[t_{0}-T_{1},t_{0}+T_{2}\right]\subseteq(0,T)
. From these definitions, one can easily deduce that
\displaystyle\operatorname{Tail}_{\infty}(v;x_{0},r,I)
is well-defined for any
\displaystyle v\in L^{\infty}(I,L_{2s}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
. Also we note that for any
\displaystyle v\in L^{\infty}(I,L_{2s}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
and for any
\displaystyle k\in\mathbb{R}
, the quantity
\displaystyle\underset{t\in I}{\operatorname{ess}\text{sup}}\left(r^{2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B_{r}(x_{0})}\frac{|v(x,t)-k|}{\left|x-x_{0}\right|^{n+2s}}dx\right)
is finite.

2.3
Weak solutions

We denote

\mathcal{E}(u,v,t):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u(x,t)-u(y,t))(v(x,t)-v(y,t))\times K(x,y,t)\,dxdy,

where
\displaystyle K(x,y,t)=\frac{1}{{{\left|x-y\right|}^{n+2s}}}
; and define our weak solution as:

Definition 2.1
.

A function
\displaystyle u\in L^{2}(I;W_{\textit{loc}}^{1,2}(\Omega))\cap C^{0}(I;L_{\textit{loc}}^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}(I;L_{2s}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
is a local weak solution to
Eq.

1.1
if for any closed interval
\displaystyle I:=\left[t_{1},t_{2}\right]\subseteq(0,T)
, there holds that

{}\begin{array}[]{c}\int_{\Omega}u(x,t_{2})\varphi(x,t_{2})\,dx-\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Omega}u(x,t)\partial_{t}\varphi(x,t)\,dxdt+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dxdt+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\mathcal{E}(u,\varphi,t)\,dt\\
=\int_{\Omega}u(x,t_{1})\varphi(x,t_{1})\,dx,\end{array}

(2.2)

for every function
\displaystyle\varphi\in L^{2}(I;W^{1,2}(\Omega))\cap W^{1,2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))
with the property that
\displaystyle\varphi
has spatial support compactly contained in
\displaystyle\Omega
.

2.4
Steklov averages

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\displaystyle v
be a function in
\displaystyle L^{1}(\Omega_{T})
. For
\displaystyle 0<h<T
, we define the Steklov averages
\displaystyle v_{h}(\cdot,t)
by

\begin{array}[]{l}v_{h}(\cdot,t):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{1}{h}\int_{t}^{t+h}v(\cdot,\tau)d\tau,\quad t\in(0,T-h],\\
0,\quad t>T-h;\end{array}\right.\end{array}

and

\begin{array}[]{l}v_{\bar{h}}(\cdot,t):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{1}{h}\int_{t-h}^{t}v(\cdot,\tau)d\tau,\quad t\in(h,T],\\
0,\quad t<h.\end{array}\right.\end{array}

We state the following lemma without proof.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\displaystyle v\in L^{r}(0,T;L^{q}(\Omega))
. Then, as
\displaystyle h\to 0
,
\displaystyle v_{h}
converges to
\displaystyle v
in
\displaystyle L^{r}(0,T-\varepsilon;L^{q}(\Omega))
for every
\displaystyle\varepsilon\in(0,T)
. If
\displaystyle v\in C(0,T;L^{q}(\Omega))
, then as
\displaystyle h\to 0
,
\displaystyle v_{h}(.,t)
converges to
\displaystyle v(\cdot,t)
in
\displaystyle L^{q}(\Omega)
for every
\displaystyle t\in(0,T-\varepsilon),\forall\varepsilon\in(0,T).

A similar statement holds for
\displaystyle v_{\bar{h}}
.

We refer to [
4
, Lemma
\displaystyle 2.2,2.5
] for proofs. The same proof can be done for the spaces
\displaystyle L^{2}(I;W_{\textit{loc}}^{s,2}(\Omega))
,
\displaystyle L^{2}(I;W_{\textit{loc}}^{1,2}(\Omega)).

In view of the above lemma, we give another equivalent definition of a weak solution.

Definition 2.4
.

A function
\displaystyle u\in L^{2}_{\textit{loc}}(0,T;W_{\textit{loc}}^{1,2}(\Omega))\cap C^{0}_{\textit{loc}}(0,T;L_{\textit{loc}}^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}_{\textit{loc}}(0,T;L_{2s}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
is a weak solution of
Eq.

1.1

if
\displaystyle u
satisfies

{}\int_{\mathcal{K}\times\{t\}}\left\{u_{h,t}\varphi+(\nabla u)_{h}\cdot\nabla\varphi\right\}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(u(x,t)-u(y,t)\right)_{h}\left(\varphi(x,t)-\varphi(y,t)\right)K(x,y,t)\,dxdy=0,

(2.3)

for all
\displaystyle 0<t<T-h
and for all
\displaystyle\varphi\in W_{0}^{1,2}(\mathcal{K})\cap L_{\textit{loc}}^{\infty}(\Omega)
with
\displaystyle\mathcal{K}\Subset\Omega
.

Now we take
\displaystyle I=[a,b]\subset\mathbb{R}
to be any interval and
\displaystyle h>0
. Given
\displaystyle v\in L^{2}(I,L^{2}_{\textit{loc}}(\Omega))
we define
\displaystyle v_{h}\in C^{0}(I,L^{2}_{\textit{loc}}(\Omega))
by

v_{h}(\cdot,t)=\frac{1}{h}\int_{t}^{t+h}\tilde{v}(\cdot,s)ds,\quad\text{ for each }a\leq t\leq b,

where
\displaystyle\tilde{v}(\cdot,t)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},L^{2}_{\textit{loc}}(\Omega))
is defined by

\tilde{v}(\cdot,t)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &v(\cdot,t),\quad\text{ when }t\in I,\\
&0,\quad\text{ when }t\in\mathbb{R}\backslash I.\end{aligned}\right.

With these definitions, we state the following important result [
4
, Lemma 2.4] regarding Steklov averages:

Lemma 2.5
.

\displaystyle v_{h}(\cdot,t)
as defined above satisfies for all
\displaystyle E\Subset\Omega
:

v_{h}(\cdot,t)\in L^{2}(E),\quad\forall t\in I

with

\begin{gathered}\left\|v_{h}(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{2}(E)}\leq\frac{1}{h^{1/r}}\|v(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}\left(I,L^{2}(E)\right)};\\
v_{h}\in C^{0}(I,L^{2}(E))\cap L^{\infty}(I,L^{2}(E))\text{ and }v_{h}(\cdot,t):I\rightarrow L^{2}(E)\text{ is uniformly continuous on }I.\\
v_{h}\in L^{2}(I,L^{2}(E))\text{ and }||v_{h}||_{L^{2}(I,L^{2}(E))}\leq||v||_{L^{2}(I,L^{2}(E))}.\end{gathered}

The same result as of
Lemma

2.5
can be done for the space
\displaystyle L^{2}(I;W_{\textit{loc}}^{1,2}(\Omega))
and
\displaystyle L^{2}(I;W_{\textit{loc}}^{s,2}(\Omega))
.

2.6
Auxiliary results

We begin with the basic Poincaré inequality, which can be found in [
8

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\displaystyle\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain with
\displaystyle C^{1}
boundary. Then there exist a positive constant
\displaystyle C>0
depending only on
\displaystyle n
and
\displaystyle\Omega

\int_{\Omega}\left|u\right|^{2}dx\leq C\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}dx,\qquad\forall u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega).

Specifically, if we take
\displaystyle\Omega=B_{\varrho}
, then we will get for all
\displaystyle u\in W^{1,2}(B_{\varrho})

\fint_{B_{\varrho}}\left|u-(u)_{B_{\varrho}}\right|^{2}dx\leq c\varrho^{2}\fint_{B_{\varrho}}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}dx,

where
\displaystyle c
is a constant depending only on
\displaystyle n
and
\displaystyle(u)_{B_{\varrho}}
denotes the average of
\displaystyle u
in
\displaystyle B_{\varrho}
.

The following version of Poincaré inequality holds for fractional Sobolev spaces.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
\displaystyle s\in(0,1),B_{\varrho}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a ball. If
\displaystyle u\in W^{s,2}(B_{\varrho})

\left(\fint_{B_{\varrho}}\left|u-(u)_{B_{\varrho}}\right|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}\leq c\varrho^{s}\left(\int_{B_{\varrho}}\fint_{B_{\varrho}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dxdy\right)^{1/2}

holds with
\displaystyle c\equiv c(n,s)

The following result asserts that the classical Sobolev space is continuously embedded in the fractional Sobolev space; for details, one can see [
7

\displaystyle\Omega
so that we can extend functions from
\displaystyle W^{1,2}(\Omega)
to
\displaystyle W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\displaystyle\Omega
be a smooth bounded domain in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\displaystyle 0<s<1
. There exists a positive constant
\displaystyle C=C(\Omega,n,s)
such that

\|u\|_{W^{s,2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)},

for every
\displaystyle u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
.

The next embedding result for the fractional Sobolev spaces with zero boundary value follows from [
3

Lemma

2.10

Lemma 2.11

Let
\displaystyle\Omega

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\displaystyle 0<s<1

\displaystyle C=C(n,s,\Omega)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dxdy\leq C\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx,

for every
\displaystyle u\in W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\Omega

\displaystyle\varepsilon>0

|a|^{p}\leq|b|^{p}+C_{p}\varepsilon|b|^{p}+\left(1+C_{p}\varepsilon\right)\varepsilon^{1-p}|a-b|^{p},

with
\displaystyle C_{p}:=(p-1)\Gamma(\max\{1,p-2\})
. Here
\displaystyle\Gamma
is the standard Gamma function.

The following version of parabolic Poincaré is from [

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
\displaystyle f\in L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\Omega))
and suppose that
\displaystyle{B}_{r}\Subset\Omega
be a compactly contained ball of radius
\displaystyle r>0
. Let
\displaystyle I\subset(0,T)
be a time interval and
\displaystyle\rho(x,t)\in L^{1}({B}_{r}\times I)
be a positive function satisfying
\displaystyle\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}({B}_{r}\times I)}\lesssim_{n}\frac{\|\rho\|_{L^{1}({B}_{r}\times I)}}{\left|{B}_{r}\times I\right|}
and
\displaystyle\mu(x)\in C_{c}^{\infty}({B}_{r})
such that
\displaystyle\int_{{B}_{r}}\mu(x)dx=1
with
\displaystyle|\mu|\leq\frac{C(n)}{r^{n}}
and
\displaystyle|\nabla\mu|\leq\frac{C(n)}{r^{n+1}}
. Then there holds

\begin{array}[]{l}\qquad\fint\fint_{{B}_{r}\times I}\left|\frac{f-(f)_{\rho}}{r}\right|^{2}dz\quad\lesssim_{n}\fint\fint_{{B}_{r}\times I}|\nabla f|^{2}dz+\sup_{t_{1},t_{2}\in I}\left|\frac{(f)_{\mu}(t_{2})-(f)_{\mu}(t_{1})}{r}\right|^{2},\\
\text{ where }(f)_{\rho}:=\iint_{{B}_{r}\times I}f(z)\frac{\rho(z)}{\|\rho\|_{L^{1}({B}_{r}\times I)}}dz\text{ and }(f)_{\mu}(t_{i}):=\int_{{B}_{r}}f(x,t_{i})\mu(x)dx\text{ for }i=1,2.\end{array}

3
Fundamental Estimates

3.1
Caccioppoli estimate

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\displaystyle u
be a local solution to our problem. Let
\displaystyle x_{0}\in\Omega,r>0,B_{r}\equiv B_{r}(x_{0})
satisfies
\displaystyle\bar{B}_{r}\subseteq\Omega
and
\displaystyle 0<\tau_{1}<\tau_{2},
satisfies
\displaystyle\left[\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\right]\subseteq(0,T)
. Then for all functions
\displaystyle\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{r})
and
\displaystyle\eta\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\displaystyle\eta(t)\equiv 0
if
\displaystyle t\leq\tau_{1}
,
there exists a constant
\displaystyle C>0
only depending on
\displaystyle s
and
\displaystyle n
such that

\begin{array}[]{l}\quad\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}\int_{B_{r}}\int_{B_{r}}\left|v(x,t)\psi(x)-v(y,t)\psi(y)\right|^{2}\eta^{2}(t)d\mu dt+\underset{\tau_{1}<t<\tau_{2}}{\operatorname{ess}\sup}\int_{B_{r}}v^{2}(x,t)\psi^{2}(x)\eta^{2}(t)dx\\
\quad+\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}\int_{B_{r}}\left|\nabla u(x,t)\right|^{2}\psi^{2}(x)\eta^{2}(t)dxdt\\
\leq C\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}\int_{B_{r}}\int_{B_{r}}\operatorname{max}\left\{v(x,t),v(y,t)\right\}^{2}|\psi(x)-\psi(y)|^{2}\eta^{2}(t)d\mu dt\\
\quad+C\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}\int_{B_{r}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B_{r}}\frac{|v(y,t)|}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dy\right)|v(x,t)|\psi^{2}(x)\eta^{2}(t)dxdt\\
\quad+C\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}\int_{B_{r}}v^{2}(x,t)\psi^{2}(x)\eta(t)\left|\partial_{t}\eta(t)\right|dxdt+C\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}\int_{B_{r}}v^{2}(x,t)\left|\nabla\psi(x)\right|^{2}\eta^{2}(t)dxdt,\end{array}

where
\displaystyle d\mu:=K(x,y,t)dxdy
and
\displaystyle v:=u-k
with a level
\displaystyle k\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Take

\displaystyle 0<h<T-\tau_{2}
and
\displaystyle I=[\tau_{1},\tau_{2}].
We choose arbitrary
\displaystyle\varphi\in L^{2}(I;W^{1,2}(\Omega))\cap W^{1,2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))
such that
\displaystyle\varphi
has spatial support compactly contained in
\displaystyle B_{r}
. So from
Eq.

2.3
, integrating over
\displaystyle(t_{1},t_{2}]\subset(\tau_{1},\tau_{2}]
we obtain

\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{B_{r}}u_{h,t}\varphi dxdt+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{B_{r}}(\nabla u)_{h}\cdot\nabla\varphi dxdt+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\mathcal{E}(u_{h},\varphi,t)dt=0.

Let us fix here
\displaystyle t_{1}=\tau_{1}
and take
\displaystyle t_{2}\in\left(\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\right]
which will be specified later.

Now as
\displaystyle v(x,t):=(u-k)(x,t)
, we choose
\displaystyle\varphi(x,t)=v_{h}(x,t)\psi^{2}(x)\eta^{2}(t)
in above to get

{}\begin{array}[]{c}\underbrace{\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{B_{r}}\partial_{t}u_{h}(x,t)(v_{h}\psi^{2}\eta^{2})(x,t)dxdt}_{I_{1}^{h}}\\
+\underbrace{\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{B_{r}}\int_{B_{r}}(v(x,t)-v(y,t))_{h}((v_{h}\psi^{2}\eta^{2})(x,t)-(v_{h}\psi^{2}\eta^{2})(y,t))d\mu dt}_{I_{2}^{h}}\\
+\underbrace{\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B_{r}}\int_{B_{r}}(v(x,t)-v(y,t))_{h}\times(v_{h}\psi^{2}\eta^{2})(x,t)d\mu dt}_{I_{3}^{h}}+\underbrace{\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{B_{r}}(\nabla u)_{h}\cdot\nabla(v_{h}\psi^{2}\eta^{2})dxdt}_{I_{4}^{h}}=0.\end{array}

(3.1)

Now we need to take
\displaystyle h\rightarrow 0
and find the limits of
\displaystyle I_{1}^{h},I_{2}^{h},I_{3}^{h},I_{4}^{h}
before we proceed for our required estimate.

Estimate for
\displaystyle I_{1}^{h}
:
Using integrating by parts, we obtain that

\begin{array}[]{rcl}I_{1}^{h}&=&\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{B_{r}}\partial_{t}(v_{h}(x,t))v_{h}(x,t)\psi^{2}(x)\eta^{2}(t)dxdt\\
&=&\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{B_{r}}\partial_{t}(v_{h}(x,t))^{2}\psi^{2}(x)\eta^{2}(t)dxdt\\
&=&\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{r}}\left(v_{h}(x,t_{2})\right)^{2}\psi^{2}(x)\eta^{2}(t_{2})dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{r}}\left(v_{h}(x,t_{1})\right)^{2}\psi^{2}(x)\eta^{2}(t_{1})dx\\
&&-\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{B_{r}}\left(v_{h}(x,t)\right)^{2}\psi^{2}(x)\eta(t)\partial_{t}\eta(t)dxdt.\end{array}

Since our weak solution
\displaystyle u\in C^{0}(I;L_{\textit{loc}}^{2}(\Omega))
,
\displaystyle\psi
is bounded, and
\displaystyle\eta
is also bounded over
\displaystyle I
, therefore using
Lemma

2.3
,
we get the following convergence

{}\begin{array}[]{c}I_{1}^{h}\longrightarrow\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{r}}v^{2}(x,t_{2})\psi^{2}(x)\eta^{2}(t_{2})dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{r}}v^{2}(x,t_{1})\psi^{2}(x)\eta^{2}(t_{1})dx\\
-\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{B_{r}}\eta(t)\partial_{t}\eta(t)\psi^{2}(x)v^{2}(x,t)dxdt=:I_{1}\quad\text{ as }h\rightarrow 0.\end{array}

(3.2)

Estimate for
\displaystyle I_{2}^{h}
:
Using the form of
\displaystyle K(x,y,t)
, we get

I_{2}^{h}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{B_{r}}\int_{B_{r}}(v(x,t)-v(y,t))_{h}K^{\frac{1}{2}}(x,y,t)\times((v_{h}\psi^{2}\eta^{2})(x,t)-(v_{h}\psi^{2}\eta^{2})(y,t))K^{\frac{1}{2}}(x,y,t)\,dxdydt.

Using
Lemma

2.5
we see that the norm of
\displaystyle\left\{v_{h}\right\}_{h\in\left(0,T-\tau_{2}\right)}
in
\displaystyle L^{2}(t_{1},t_{2};W^{s,2}(B_{r}))
is bounded by
\displaystyle||v||_{L^{2}(t_{1},t_{2};W^{s,2}(B_{r}))}
, which is independent of
\displaystyle h
; also since
\displaystyle\psi
and
\displaystyle\eta
are bounded, we will get

\begin{array}[]{rcl}&\left\|\frac{(v_{h}\psi^{2}\eta^{2})(x,t)}{|x-y|^{\frac{n}{2}+s}}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(t_{1},t_{2};L^{2}\left(B_{r}\times B_{r}\right)\right)}&\leq C\\
\implies&\left\|K^{\frac{1}{2}}(x,y,t)(v_{h}\psi^{2}\eta^{2})(x,t)\right\|_{L^{2}\left(t_{1},t_{2};L^{2}\left(B_{r}\times B_{r}\right)\right)}&\leq C.\end{array}

Now as
\displaystyle(v_{h}\psi^{2}\eta^{2})\rightarrow v\psi^{2}\eta^{2}
a.e. in
\displaystyle B_{r}\times(t_{1},t_{2})
we derive that as
\displaystyle h\rightarrow 0
,

K^{\frac{1}{2}}(x,y,t)(v_{h}\psi^{2}\eta^{2})(x,t)\rightharpoonup K^{\frac{1}{2}}(x,y,t)v(x,t)\psi^{2}(x)\eta^{2}(t)\text{ in }L^{2}(t_{1},t_{2};L^{2}(B_{r}\times B_{r})).

By utilizing form of
\displaystyle K(x,y,t)
, we can check

(v(x,t)-v(y,t))_{h}K^{\frac{1}{2}}(x,y,t)\in L^{2}(t_{1},t_{2};L^{2}(B_{r}\times B_{r})).

Thus, combining the above three, we finally get the convergence that

\displaystyle I_{2}^{h}\rightarrow I_{2}\text{ as }h\rightarrow 0\text{,}

where

{}I_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{B_{r}}\int_{B_{r}}(v(x,t)-v(y,t))\times(v(x,t)\psi^{2}(x)-v(y,t)\psi^{2}(y))\eta^{2}(t)d\mu dt.

(3.3)

Estimate for
\displaystyle I_{3}^{h}
:
For the term
\displaystyle I_{3}^{h}
, using similar arguments as of the case

{}I_{3}^{h}\rightarrow\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B_{r}}\int_{B_{r}}(v(x,t)-v(y,t))\times(v\psi^{2}\eta^{2})(x,t)d\mu dt=:I_{3}\quad\text{ as }h\rightarrow 0.

(3.4)

The explicit reasoning for proving the above can be found in [

Since the Steklov averages have nothing to do with the variable

{}I_{4}^{h}\rightarrow\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{B_{r}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla(v\psi^{2}\eta^{2})dxdt=:I_{4}\quad\text{ as }h\rightarrow 0.

(3.5)

\displaystyle I_{4}

For

Now we get using the fact that

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathcal{T}_{1}(x,y)&=&\mathcal{T}(x,y)-\mathcal{T}_{2}(x,y)\\

&&-C\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{B_{r}}\int_{B_{r}}\left(\max\left\{v(x,t),v(y,t)\right\}\right)^{2}|\psi(x)-\psi(y)|^{2}\eta^{2}(t)d\mu dt.\end{array}

\quad+\frac{C}{r^{2}}\fint_{t_{0}-r^{2}}^{t_{0}}\fint_{B_{r}}v^{2}(x,t)\psi^{2}(x)\eta(t)dxdt+\frac{C}{r^{2}}\fint_{t_{0}-r^{2}}^{t_{0}}\fint_{B_{r}}v^{2}(x,t)\eta^{2}(t)dxdt.\end{array}

We write the parabolic cylinder
\displaystyle Q_{r}=B_{r}\times(t_{0}-r^{2},t_{0})
for convenience. Now we choose

\quad+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\underset{t_{0}-r^{2}<t<t_{0}}{\operatorname{ess}\sup}\fint_{B_{r}}\left|u(x,t)-(u)_{Q_{r}}\right|^{2}\psi^{2}(x)dx&&\\
\leq C\fint_{t_{0}-r^{2}}^{t_{0}}\int_{B_{r}}\fint_{B_{r}}\frac{\max\left\{v(x,t),v(y,t)\right\}^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}|\psi(x)-\psi(y)|^{2}dxdydt&&\\
\quad+C\fint_{t_{0}-r^{2}}^{t_{0}}\fint_{B_{r}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B_{r}}\frac{\left|u(y,t)-(u)_{Q_{r}}\right|}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dy\right)\left|u(x,t)-(u)_{Q_{r}}\right|\psi^{2}(x)dxdt&&\\
\quad+\frac{C}{r^{2}}\fint_{t_{0}-r^{2}}^{t_{0}}\fint_{B_{r}}\left|u(x,t)-(u)_{Q_{r}}\right|^{2}dxdt.\end{array}

\leq C\int_{t_{0}-r^{2}}^{t_{0}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B_{r}}\frac{\left|u(y,t)-(u)_{Q_{r}}\right|}{|y-x_{0}|^{n+2s}}dy\right)^{2}dt+\frac{C}{r^{2}}\fint_{t_{0}-r^{2}}^{t_{0}}\fint_{B_{r}}\left|u(x,t)-(u)_{Q_{r}}\right|^{2}dxdt.\end{array}

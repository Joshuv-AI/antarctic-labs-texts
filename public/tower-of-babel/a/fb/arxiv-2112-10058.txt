Abstract




Abstract
Let
\vec{p}\in(0,1]^{n}
be a
n
-dimensional vector and
A
a dilation.
Let
H_{A}^{\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denote the anisotropic
mixed-norm Hardy space defined via the radial maximal function.
Using the known atomic characterization of

H_{A}^{\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and establishing a uniform estimate
for corresponding atoms, the authors prove that the Fourier transform
of
f\in H_{A}^{\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
coincides with a
continuous function
F
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in the sense of tempered distributions.
Moreover, the function
F
can be controlled pointwisely by the product of
the Hardy space norm of
f
and a step function with respect to the transpose
matrix of
A
. As applications, the authors obtain a higher order of convergence for the function

F
at the origin, and an analogue of Hardy–Littlewood inequalities in the present setting
of
H_{A}^{\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Introduction

Let
\vec{p}:=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})\in(0,\infty)^{n}
be a
n
-dimensional vector and
A
a dilation (see Definition
2.1
below).
The anisotropic mixed-norm Hardy space
{H_{A}^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}
was introduced in
[
18
]
.
The main purpose of this paper is to study the Fourier transform
on
{H_{A}^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}
associated with
\vec{p}\in(0,1]^{n}
.
The question of the Fourier transform on classical Hardy spaces
H^{p}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
was put forward originally by Fefferman and Stein
[
12
]
, which is an important topic in the real-variable theory of

H^{p}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. Applying entire functions of exponential type,
Coifman
[
10
]
first characterized the Fourier transform
\widehat{f}
of

f\in H^{p}({\mathbb{R}})
. The related conclusions in higher dimensions were studied in
[
2
,
11
,
13
,
25
]
. Particularly, the following estimate was given by
Taibleson and Weiss
[
25
]
: for any given
p\in(0,1]
,
the Fourier transform of

f\in H^{p}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
coincides with a continuous
function
F
on
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
, which satisfies that there exists a positive constant
C_{(n,p)}
such that,
for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
,

\displaystyle\left|F(x)\right|\leq C_{(n,p)}\|f\|_{H^{p}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}|x|^{n(1/p-1)}.

(1.1)

Moreover, the estimate (
1.1
) illustrates the following inequality as a generalization of the well-konwn Hardy–Littlewood
inequality for Hardy spaces, that is, for any fixed
p\in(0,1]
, there
exists a positive constant
K
such that, for each
f\in H^{p}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
,

\displaystyle\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}|x|^{n(p-2)}\left|F(x)\right|^{p}\,dx\right]^{1/p}\leq K\|f\|_{H^{p}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})},

(1.2)

where
F
is as in (
1.1
); see
[
23
, p. 128]
.

On the other hand, the theory of classic Hardy spaces
H^{p}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
has a wide range of applications
in many mathematical fields such as harmonic analysis and partial differential equations;
see, for instance,
[
12
,
21
,
23
,
24
]
. Inspired by the notable work of Calderón and Torchinsky
[
3
]

on parabolic Hardy spaces, there were various generalizations of classic Hardy spaces; see,
for instance,
[
1
,
8
,
14
,
18
,
26
,
27
,
28
]
. In particular, Bownik
[
1
]
introduced the anisotropic Hardy space
H^{p}_{A}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, where
p\in(0,\infty)
and
A
is a dilation, which is actually a generalization of both the isotropic Hardy space and the parabolic Hardy space. In addition, via the atomic characterization of
H^{p}_{A}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, Bownik and Wang
[
2
]
extended both inequalities (
1.1
) and
(
1.2
) to the anisotropic Hardy space
H^{p}_{A}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
.
Recently, the analogous results were proved in the new setting of Hardy spaces associated with ball quasi-Banach function spaces and
the anisotropic mixed-norm Hardy space
{H_{\vec{a}}^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}
,
where

\vec{a}:=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in[1,\infty)^{n}\quad{\rm and}\quad\vec{p}:=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})\in(0,1]^{n};

see, respectively,
[
15
,
16
]
. In addition, motivated by the previous work of
[
8
,
12
,
17
]
,
Huang et al.
[
18
]
introduced the anisotropic mixed-norm Hardy space
{H_{A}^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}
with respect to

\vec{p}\in(0,\infty)^{n}
and a dilation
A
, and investigated its various real-variable characterizations.
For more information on mixed-norm function spaces, we refer the
reader to
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
9
,
19
,
20
,
22
]
.

Inspired by the known results about the Fourier transform of the aforementioned Hardy-type spaces [namely,
H^{p}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
,
H^{p}_{A}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
and
{H_{\vec{a}}^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}
], using the real-variable theory of the anisotropic mixed-normed Hardy space

{H_{A}^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}

) to the setting of anisotropic mixed-norm Hardy spaces

As a preliminary, in Section

Section

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we give the definitions of dilations, mixed-norm Lebesgue spaces and anisotropic mixed-norm Hardy spaces.
The following definition is originally from
[
1
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

We call
A
a
dilation
if
A
is a real
n\times n
matrix
A
and satisfies the following condition:

\min_{\lambda\in\sigma(A)}|\lambda|>1,

where
\sigma(A)
denotes the
set of all eigenvalues of
A
. We denote the eigenvalues of
A
by
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}
, which satisfies
1<|\lambda_{1}|\leq\cdots\leq|\lambda_{n}|
. Here and thereafter, let
\lambda_{-}
and
\lambda_{+}
be two numbers such that

1<\lambda_{-}<|\lambda_{1}|\leq\cdots\leq|\lambda_{n}|<\lambda_{+}
.

By
[
1
, p. 5, Lemma 2.2]
, for a given dilation
A
, there exists an open set in
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
which is called an
ellipsoid
,
denoted by
\Delta
, and has the following property:
|\Delta|=1
, and we can find a constant
r\in(1,\infty)
such that
\Delta\subset r\Delta\subset A\Delta
. For any given
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we denote
A^{i}\Delta
by
B_{i}
. It is easy to check that
\{B_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
is a family of open sets around the origin,
B_{i}\subset rB_{i}\subset B_{i+1}
and
|B_{i}|=b^{i}
with
b:=|\det A|
.
For any given dilation
A
, the notation
\mathfrak{B}
is the set of all
dilated balls
, namely,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}:=\left\{x+B_{i}:\ x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}},\ i\in{\mathbb{Z}}\right\}.

(2.1)

The next two definitions were introduced by Bownik
[
1
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

A measurable mapping
\rho:\ {\mathbb{R}^{n}}\to[0,\infty)

is called a
homogeneous quasi-norm
, with respect to a dilation
A
, if

(i)

\rho(x)\geq 0
, and
\rho(x)=0\Rightarrow x=0
;

(ii)

for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
,
\rho(Ax)=b\rho(x)
;

(iii)

for any
x
,
y\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
,
\rho(x+y)\leq c[\rho(x)+\rho(y)]
, where
c
is a positive constant independent of
x
and
y
.

It is easy to verify that the following
step homogeneous quasi-norm
is a homogeneous quasi-norm.

Definition 2.3
.

A
step homogeneous quasi-norm

\rho
with respect to a dilation
A
, is defined by setting, for each
x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
,

\rho(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}b^{i}&\hskip 17.07182pt{\rm when}\hskip 14.22636ptx\in B_{i+1}\backslash B_{i}\\
0&\hskip 17.07182pt{\rm when}\hskip 14.22636ptx=\mathbf{0}\end{array}\right..

In
[
1
, p. 5, Lemma 2.4]
, it was proved that any two homogeneous
quasi-norms associated with a fixed dilation
A
are equivalent. For convenience,
in what follows, we always use the step homogeneous quasi-norm.

A
C^{\infty}
complex-valued function
\phi
on
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
is called a
Schwartz function
if, for
every pair of
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
and multi-index
\gamma\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{n}
, the following inequality

\|\phi\|_{\gamma,k}:=\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}[\rho(x)]^{k}\left|\partial^{\gamma}\phi(x)\right|<\infty

holds true.
The set of all Schwartz functions on
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
is denoted by
{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. Indeed,
\{\|\cdot\|_{\gamma,k}\}_{\gamma\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{n},\,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}
is a family of semi-norms, which induces a topology and makes
{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
to be a topological vector space. We denote the dual space of
{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})

by
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, equipped with the weak-
\ast
topology.

For a
n
-dimensional vector
\vec{p}:=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})\in{(0,\infty]}^{n}
, let

\displaystyle p_{-}:=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{min}_{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}}\{p_{i}\},\hskip 9.95863ptp_{+}:=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{max}_{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}}\{p_{i}\},\hskip 9.95863pt{\rm and}\hskip 9.95863pt\underline{p}:=\min\{p_{-},1\}.

(2.2)

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\vec{p}:=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})\in{(0,\infty]}^{n}
. The
mixed-norm Lebesgue space

{L}^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
is defined to be the set of all measurable functions
f
such that

{\Arrowvert f\Arrowvert}_{{L}^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\cdots\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})|^{p_{1}}dx_{1}\right)^{\frac{p_{2}}{p_{1}}}\cdots dx_{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{n}}}<\infty

with the usual modifications made when
p_{i}=\infty
for some
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
satisfy
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi(x)\,dx\neq 0
. The

radial maximal function

M_{\varphi}(f)
of
f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, with respect to
\varphi
, is defined by

\displaystyle M_{\varphi}(f)(x):=\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|f\ast\varphi_{k}(x)|,\quad\forall\,x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}},

here and thereafter, for any
\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
and
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
\varphi_{k}(\cdot):=b^{k}\varphi(A^{k}\cdot)
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\vec{p}\in{(0,\infty)}^{n}
and
\varphi
be as in Definition
2.5
.
The
anisotropic mixed-norm Hardy space

{H_{A}^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}
is defined by setting

{H_{A}^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}:=\left\{f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}^{n}}):\ M_{\varphi}(f)\in{L^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}\right\}.

Moreover, for any
f\in{H_{A}^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}
, let

\|f\|_{{H_{A}^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}:=\|M_{\varphi}(f)\|_{{L^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}
.

3
Fourier transforms of
{H_{A}^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}

In this section, we study the Fourier transform
\widehat{f}
of
f\in{H_{A}^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}
. We first present
the notion of Fourier transforms.

For a given schwartz function
\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, we define its
Fourier transform
as follows:

\mathscr{F}\varphi(x)=\widehat{\varphi}(x):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\varphi(t)e^{-2\pi\imath t\cdot x}\,dt,\quad\forall\,x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}},

where
\imath:=\sqrt{-1}
and
t\cdot x:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}t_{k}x_{k}

for any
t:=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})
,
x:=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
.
Futhermore, we can also define the
Fourier transform
of
f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, also denoted by
\mathscr{F}f
or
\widehat{f}
,
that is, for each
\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
,

\langle\mathscr{F}f,\varphi\rangle=\left\langle\widehat{f},\varphi\right\rangle:=\left\langle f,\widehat{\varphi}\right\rangle.

We now give the main result of this paper.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\vec{p}\in{(0,1]}^{n}
. Then, for any

f\in{H_{A}^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}
, there exists a continuous function
F
on
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
such that

\widehat{f}=F\quad in\quad{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}^{n}}),

and there exists a positive constant
C
, depending only on
A
and
\vec{p}
, such that, for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
,

\displaystyle|F(x)|\leq C\|f\|_{{H_{A}^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}\max\left\{[\rho_{\ast}(x)]^{\frac{1}{p_{-}}-1},[\rho_{\ast}(x)]^{\frac{1}{p_{+}}-1}\right\},

(3.1)

here and thereafter,
\rho_{*}
is as in Definition
2.2
with
A
replaced by its
transposed matrix
A^{*}
.

Recall that, for a given measurable set
E\subset{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
, the
Lebesgue space

L^{p}(E)
,
0<p<\infty
, is the set of all the measurable functions satisfying that

\|f\|_{L^{p}(E)}:=\left[\int_{E}|f(x)|^{p}\,dx\right]^{1/p}<\infty,

and
L^{\infty}(E)
is the set of all the measurable functions satisfying that

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(E)}:=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,ess\,sup\,}_{x\in E}|f(x)|<\infty.

The dilation operator
D_{A}
is defined by setting, for any measurable function
f
on
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
,

D_{A}(f)(\cdot):=f(A\cdot).

Then, for any
f\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
,
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
, the following identity

\widehat{f}(x)=b^{k}\left(D_{A^{*}}^{k}\mathscr{F}D_{A}^{k}f\right)(x)

can be easily verified.

Next, we present some notions used in the real-variable characterizations of anisotropic mixed-norm Hardy spaces; see
[
18
]
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\vec{p}\in{(0,\infty)}^{n}
,
r\in(1,\infty]
and

\displaystyle s\in\left[\left\lfloor\left(\dfrac{1}{p_{-}}-1\right)\dfrac{\ln b}{\ln\lambda_{-}}\right\rfloor,\infty\right)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}_{+},

(3.2)

where
p_{-}
is as in (
2.2
).

(I)

A measurable function
a
on
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
is called an
anisotropic
(\vec{p},r,s)
-atom

(simply, a
(\vec{p},r,s)
-
atom
) if

(i)

\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,supp\,}a\subset B
, where

B\in\mathfrak{B}
with
\mathfrak{B}
as in (
2.1
);

(ii)

\|a\|_{L^{r}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}\leq\frac{|B|^{1/r}}{\|\mathbf{1}_{B}\|_{{L^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}}
;

(iii)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}a(x)x^{\gamma}\,dx=0
for any
\gamma\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{n}

with
|\gamma|\leq s
.

(II)

The
anisotropic mixed-norm atomic Hardy space

{H_{A}^{\vec{p},r,s}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}
is defined to be the
set of all
f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
satisfying that there exist a sequence

\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\mathbb{C}
and a sequence of
(\vec{p},r,s)
-atoms

\{a_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
, supported respectively in

\{B^{(i)}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\mathfrak{B}
such that

\displaystyle f=\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\lambda_{i}a_{i}\quad\mathrm{in}\quad{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}^{n}}).

Furthermore, for any
f\in{H_{A}^{\vec{p},r,s}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}
, let

\displaystyle\|f\|_{{H_{A}^{\vec{p},r,s}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}:={\inf}\left\|\left\{\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left[\frac{|\lambda_{i}|\mathbf{1}_{B^{(i)}}}{\|\mathbf{1}_{B^{(i)}}\|_{{L^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}}\right]^{\underline{p}}\right\}^{1/\underline{p}}\right\|_{{L^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}},

where the infimum is taken over all the decompositions of
f
as above.

The next Lemma
3.3
will be used to prove Lemma
3.4
below.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\vec{p}
,
r
and
s
be as in Definition
3.2
.
Assume that
a
is a
(\vec{p},r,s)
-atom supported in
x_{0}+B_{i_{0}}

with some
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
and
i_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Then there exists a positive
constant
C
, depending only on
A
and
s
, such that,
for any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{n}
with
|\alpha|\leq s
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
,

\displaystyle\left|\partial^{\alpha}\left(\mathscr{F}D_{A}^{i_{0}}a\right)(x)\right|\leq Cb^{-i_{0}/r}\|a\|_{L^{r}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}\min\left\{1,|x|^{s-|\alpha|+1}\right\}{}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may take
x_{0}=\mathbf{0}
. This implies that
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,supp\,}(D_{A}^{i_{0}}a)\subset B_{0}
.
Then, for any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{n}
with
|\alpha|\leq s
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
, we easily obtain

\partial^{\alpha}\left(\mathscr{F}D_{A}^{i_{0}}a\right)(x)=\int_{B_{0}}(-2\pi\imath\xi)^{\alpha}\left(D_{A}^{i_{0}}a\right)(\xi)e^{-2\pi\imath\xi\cdot x}\,d\xi.

Let
P
be the degree
s-|\alpha|
Taylor expansion polynomial of the function

e^{-2\pi\imath\xi\cdot x}
centered at the origin
\mathbf{0}
respect to
\xi
.
Applying Definition
3.2
(iii), the Taylor remainder theorem and the Hölder
inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left|\partial^{\alpha}\left(\mathscr{F}D_{A}^{i_{0}}a\right)(x)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{B_{0}}(-2\pi\imath\xi)^{\alpha}\left(D_{A}^{i_{0}}a\right)(\xi)e^{-2\pi\imath\xi\cdot x}\,d\xi\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{B_{0}}(-2\pi\imath\xi)^{\alpha}\left(D_{A}^{i_{0}}a\right)(\xi)\left[e^{-2\pi\imath\xi\cdot x}-P(\xi)\right]\,d\xi\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{B_{0}}|\xi^{\alpha}|\left|a\left(A^{i_{0}}\xi\right)\right||x|^{s-|\alpha|+1}|\xi|^{s-|\alpha|+1}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim|x|^{s-|\alpha|+1}\int_{B_{0}}\left|a\left(A^{i_{0}}\xi\right)\right||\xi|^{s+1}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim|x|^{s-|\alpha|+1}b^{-i_{0}}\int_{B_{i_{0}}}|a(\xi)|\,d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim|x|^{s-|\alpha|+1}b^{-i_{0}/r}\|a\|_{L^{r}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}.

The other estimate can be directly deduced from the Hölder inequality, namely,

\displaystyle\left|\partial^{\alpha}\left(\mathscr{F}D_{A}^{i_{0}}a\right)(x)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{B_{0}}(-2\pi\imath\xi)^{\alpha}\left(D_{A}^{i_{0}}a\right)(\xi)e^{-2\pi\imath\xi\cdot x}\,d\xi\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{B_{0}}|\xi|^{|\alpha|}\left|a\left(A^{i_{0}}\xi\right)\right|\,d\xi\lesssim b^{-i_{0}}\int_{B_{i_{0}}}|a(\xi)|\,d\xi\lesssim b^{-i_{0}/r}\|a\|_{L^{r}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}.

This finishes the proof of Lemma
3.3
.
∎

Applying Lemma
3.3
, we obtain a uniform estimate for
(\vec{p},r,s)
-atoms as follows, which plays a key role in the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\vec{p}\in{(0,1]}^{n}
and let
r
and
s
be as
in Definition
3.2
. Then there exists a positive constant
C
such that,
for any
(\vec{p},r,s)
-atom
a
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
,

\displaystyle\left|\widehat{a}(x)\right|\leq C\max\left\{\left[\rho_{*}(x)\right]^{\frac{1}{p_{-}}-1},\,\left[\rho_{*}(x)\right]^{\frac{1}{p_{+}}-1}\right\},

(3.3)

where
\rho_{*}
is as in Theorem
3.1
.

The following inequalities will be used to prove Lemma
3.4
, which are just
[
1
, p. 11, Lemma 3.2]
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
A
be a given dilation. There exists a positive constant
C
such that, for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
,

\frac{1}{C}[\rho(x)]^{\ln\lambda_{-}/\ln b}\leq\left|x\right|\leq C[\rho(x)]^{\ln\lambda_{+}/\ln b}\qquad{when}\ \rho(x)\in(1,\infty),

and

\frac{1}{C}[\rho(x)]^{\ln\lambda_{+}/\ln b}\leq\left|x\right|\leq C[\rho(x)]^{\ln\lambda_{-}/\ln b}\qquad{when}\ \rho(x)\in[0,1],

where
\lambda_{-}
and
\lambda_{+}
are as in Definition
2.1
.

We now give the proof of Lemma
3.4
.

Proof of Lemma
3.4
.

Let
a
be a
(\vec{p},r,s)
-atom supported in
x_{0}+B_{i_{0}}
with some
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}

and
i_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Without loss of generality, we may assume
x_{0}=\mathbf{0}
.
By Lemma
3.3
with
\alpha=(\overbrace{0,\ldots,0}^{n\ \rm times})
, we find that,
for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
,

\displaystyle\left|\widehat{a}(x)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|b^{i_{0}}\left(D_{A^{*}}^{i_{0}}\mathscr{F}D_{A}^{i_{0}}a\right)(x)\right|=\left|b^{i_{0}}\left(\mathscr{F}D_{A}^{i_{0}}a\right)\left((A^{*})^{i_{0}}x\right)\right|

(3.4)

\displaystyle\lesssim b^{i_{0}}b^{-i_{0}/r}\|a\|_{L^{r}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}\min\left\{1,\left|(A^{*})^{i_{0}}x\right|^{s+1}\right\}

\displaystyle\lesssim b^{i_{0}}\left\|\mathbf{1}_{B_{i_{0}}}\right\|_{{L^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}^{-1}\min\left\{1,\left|(A^{*})^{i_{0}}x\right|^{s+1}\right\}.

Next, we show that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{1}_{B_{i_{0}}}\right\|_{{L^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}^{-1}\lesssim\max\left\{b^{-\frac{i_{0}}{p_{-}}},\,b^{-\frac{i_{0}}{p_{+}}}\right\}.

(3.5)

Indeed, there exists a
K\in{\mathbb{Z}}
large enough such that, if
i_{0}\in(K,\infty)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}
, then

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{1}_{B_{i_{0}}}\right\|_{{L^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\cdots\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathbf{1}_{B_{i_{0}}}|^{p_{1}}dx_{1}\right)^{\frac{p_{2}}{p_{1}}}\cdots dx_{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{n}}}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\cdots\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathbf{1}_{B_{i_{0}}}|^{p_{+}}dx_{1}\right)^{\frac{p_{+}}{p_{+}}}\cdots dx_{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{+}}}=b^{\frac{i_{0}}{p_{+}}}.

On the other hand, if
i_{0}\in(-\infty,K]
, by
[
18
, Lemma 6.8]
, we conclude that, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,

\frac{\|\mathbf{1}_{B_{K}}\|_{{L^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}}{\|\mathbf{1}_{B_{i_{0}}}\|_{{L^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}}\lesssim b^{(K-i_{0})\frac{1+\varepsilon}{p_{-}}}.

Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
, we have

\left\|\mathbf{1}_{B_{i_{0}}}\right\|_{{L^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}^{-1}\lesssim\frac{b^{\frac{K}{p_{-}}}}{\|\mathbf{1}_{B_{K}}\|_{{L^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}}b^{-\frac{i_{0}}{p_{-}}}.

Thus, (
3.5
) holds true. From this and (
3.4
), it follows that, for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
,

\displaystyle\left|\widehat{a}(x)\right|\lesssim b^{i_{0}}\max\left\{b^{-\frac{i_{0}}{p_{-}}},\,b^{-\frac{i_{0}}{p_{+}}}\right\}\min\left\{1,\left|(A^{*})^{i_{0}}x\right|^{s+1}\right\}.

(3.6)

We next prove (
3.3
) by considering two cases:

\rho_{*}(x)\leq b^{-i_{0}}
and
\rho_{*}(x)>b^{-i_{0}}
.

Case 1).

\rho_{*}(x)\leq b^{-i_{0}}
. In this case, note that

\rho_{*}((A^{*})^{i_{0}}x)\leq 1
. From (
3.6
), Lemma
3.5
and the fact that

1-\frac{1}{p_{+}}+(s+1)\frac{\ln\lambda_{-}}{\ln b}\geq 1-\frac{1}{p_{-}}+(s+1)\frac{\ln\lambda_{-}}{\ln b}>0,

we deduce that, for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
satisfying
\rho_{*}(x)\leq b^{-i_{0}}
,

\displaystyle\left|\widehat{a}(x)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim b^{i_{0}}\max\left\{b^{-\frac{i_{0}}{p_{-}}},\,b^{-\frac{i_{0}}{p_{+}}}\right\}\left[\rho_{*}\left((A^{*})^{i_{0}}x\right)\right]^{(s+1)\frac{\ln\lambda_{-}}{\ln b}}

(3.7)

\displaystyle\sim\max\left\{b^{i_{0}[1-\frac{1}{p_{-}}+(s+1)\frac{\ln\lambda_{-}}{\ln b}]},\,b^{i_{0}[1-\frac{1}{p_{+}}+(s+1)\frac{\ln\lambda_{-}}{\ln b}]}\right\}\left[\rho_{*}(x)\right]^{(s+1)\frac{\ln\lambda_{-}}{\ln b}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\max\left\{\left[\rho_{*}(x)\right]^{\frac{1}{p_{-}}-1},\,\left[\rho_{*}(x)\right]^{\frac{1}{p_{+}}-1}\right\}.

This shows (
3.3
) for Case 1).

Case 2).

\rho_{*}(x)>b^{-i_{0}}
. In this case, note that
\rho_{*}((A^{*})^{i_{0}}x)>1
.
Using (
3.6
), Lemma
3.5
again and the fact that

\frac{1}{p_{-}}-1\geq\frac{1}{p_{+}}-1\geq 0,

it is easy to see that, for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
satisfying
\rho_{*}(x)>b^{-i_{0}}
,

\displaystyle\left|\widehat{a}(x)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim b^{i_{0}}\max\left\{b^{-\frac{i_{0}}{p_{-}}},\,b^{-\frac{i_{0}}{p_{+}}}\right\}\sim\max\left\{b^{(1-\frac{1}{p_{-}})i_{0}},\,b^{(1-\frac{1}{p_{+}})i_{0}}\right\}

\displaystyle\lesssim\max\left\{\left[\rho_{*}(x)\right]^{\frac{1}{p_{-}}-1},\,\left[\rho_{*}(x)\right]^{\frac{1}{p_{+}}-1}\right\},

which completes the proof of (
3.3
) and hence of Lemma
3.4
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\vec{p}\in(0,1]^{n}
. Then, for any
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\mathbb{C}
and
\{B^{(i)}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\mathfrak{B}
,

\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}|\lambda_{i}|\leq\left\|\left\{\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left[\frac{|\lambda_{i}|\mathbf{1}_{B^{(i)}}}{\|\mathbf{1}_{B^{(i)}}\|_{{L^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}}\right]^{\underline{p}}\right\}^{1/{\underline{p}}}\right\|_{{L^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}},

where
\underline{p}
is as in (
2.2
).

Proof.

Observe that, for any
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\mathbb{C}
and
\gamma\in(0,1]
,

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{i}|\right)^{\gamma}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{i}|^{\gamma}.

(3.8)

By this and the inverse Minkovski inequality, we know that

\displaystyle\left\|\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left[\frac{|\lambda_{i}|\mathbf{1}_{B^{(i)}}}{\|\mathbf{1}_{B^{(i)}}\|_{{L^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}}\right]^{\underline{p}}\right\}^{1/{\underline{p}}}\right\|_{{L^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}

\displaystyle\geq\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{|\lambda_{i}|\mathbf{1}_{B^{(i)}}}{\|\mathbf{1}_{B^{(i)}}\|_{{L^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}}\right\|_{{L^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}

\displaystyle\geq\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{|\lambda_{i}|\mathbf{1}_{B^{(i)}}}{\|\mathbf{1}_{B^{(i)}}\|_{{L^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}}\right\|_{{L^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\lambda_{i}|.

Letting
N\to\infty
, we obtain the desired inequality as in Lemma
3.6
.
∎

To show Theorem
3.1
, we also need the following atomic characterizations
of
{H_{A}^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}
, which is just
[
18
, Theorem 4.7]
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\vec{p}\in{(0,\infty)}^{n}
,
r\in(\max\{p_{+},1\},\infty]
with
p_{+}

as in (
2.2
),
s
be as in (
3.2
) and

N\in\mathbb{N}\cap\left[\left\lfloor\left(\frac{1}{{{\rm min}\{p_{-},1\}}}-1\right)\frac{\ln b}{\ln\lambda_{-}}\right\rfloor+2,\infty\right)

with
p_{-}
as in (
2.2
).
Then
{H_{A}^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}={H_{A}^{\vec{p},r,s}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}
with equivalent quasi-norms.

We now prove Theorem
3.1
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Let
\vec{p}\in{(0,1]}^{n}
,
r\in(\max\{p_{+},1\},\infty]
,
s
be as in (
3.2
)
and
f\in{H_{A}^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\|f\|_{{H_{A}^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}>0
. Then, by Lemma
3.7
and Definition
3.2
(II), we find that
there exist a sequence

\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\mathbb{C}
and a sequence of
(\vec{p},r,s)
-atoms
\{a_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
, supported respectively in

\{B^{(i)}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\mathfrak{B}
, such that

\displaystyle f=\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\lambda_{i}a_{i}\quad\mathrm{in}\quad{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}^{n}}),

(3.9)

and

\displaystyle\|f\|_{{H_{A}^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}\sim\left\|\left\{\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left[\frac{|\lambda_{i}|\mathbf{1}_{B^{(i)}}}{\|\mathbf{1}_{B^{(i)}}\|_{{L^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}}\right]^{\underline{p}}\right\}^{1/\underline{p}}\right\|_{{L^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}.

(3.10)

Taking the Fourier transform on the both sides of (
3.9
), we have

\displaystyle\widehat{f}=\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\lambda_{i}\widehat{a_{i}}\quad\mathrm{in}\quad{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}^{n}}).

(3.11)

Note that a function
f\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
implies that

\widehat{f}
is well defined in
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
, so does
\widehat{a_{i}}
for any
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
. From
Lemmas
3.4
and
3.6
, and (
3.10
), it follows that, for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}|\lambda_{i}||\widehat{a_{i}}(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}|\lambda_{i}|\max\left\{\left[\rho_{*}(x)\right]^{\frac{1}{p_{-}}-1},\,\left[\rho_{*}(x)\right]^{\frac{1}{p_{+}}-1}\right\}

(3.12)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{{H_{A}^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}\max\left\{\left[\rho_{*}(x)\right]^{\frac{1}{p_{-}}-1},\,\left[\rho_{*}(x)\right]^{\frac{1}{p_{+}}-1}\right\}<\infty.

Therefore, for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
, the function

\displaystyle F(x):=\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\lambda_{i}\widehat{a_{i}}(x)

(3.13)

is well defined pointwisely and

\displaystyle|F(x)|\lesssim\|f\|_{{H_{A}^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}\max\left\{\left[\rho_{*}(x)\right]^{\frac{1}{p_{-}}-1},\,\left[\rho_{*}(x)\right]^{\frac{1}{p_{+}}-1}\right\}.

We next show the continuity of the function
F
on
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
. If we can prove that
F
is continuous on any compact subset of
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
, then
the continuity on
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
is obvious. Indeed, for any compact subset
E
, there exists a positive constant
K
, depending only on
A
and

E
, such that
\rho_{*}(x)\leq K
holds for every
x\in E
. By this and (
3.12
), we conclude that, for any
x\in E
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}|\lambda_{i}||\widehat{a_{i}}(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\max\left\{K^{\frac{1}{p_{-}}-1},\,K^{\frac{1}{p_{+}}-1}\right\}\|f\|_{{H_{A}^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}<\infty.

Thus, the summation
\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\lambda_{i}\widehat{a_{i}}(\cdot)
converges uniformly
on
E
. This, together with the fact that, for any
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\widehat{a_{i}}(x)
is continuous,
implies that
F
is also continuous on any compact subset
E
and hence on
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
.

Finally, to complete the proof of Theorem
3.1
, by (
3.11
) and (
3.13
),
we only need to show that

\displaystyle F=\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\lambda_{i}\widehat{a_{i}}\quad\mathrm{in}\quad{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}^{n}}).

(3.14)

For this purpose, from Lemma
3.4
and the definition of Schwartz functions, we deduce that,
for any
\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
and
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\widehat{a_{i}}(x)\varphi(x)\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{(A^{*})^{k+1}B^{*}_{0}\setminus(A^{*})^{k}B^{*}_{0}}\max\left\{\left[\rho_{*}(x)\right]^{\frac{1}{p_{-}}-1},\,\left[\rho_{*}(x)\right]^{\frac{1}{p_{+}}-1}\right\}|\varphi(x)|\,dx+\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b^{k}b^{k(\frac{1}{p_{-}}-1)}b^{-k(\lceil\frac{1}{p_{-}}-1\rceil+2)}+\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}

\displaystyle\quad\sim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b^{-k}+\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})},

where
B^{*}_{0}
is the unit dilated ball with respect to
A^{*}
.
This implies that there exists a positive constant
C
such that
\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\widehat{a_{i}}(x)\varphi(x)\,dx\right|\leq C
holds true
uniformly for any
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Combining this, Lemma
3.6
and (
3.10
), we have

\displaystyle\lim_{I\to\infty}\sum_{i=I+1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{i}|\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\widehat{a_{i}}(x)\varphi(x)\,dx\right|\lesssim\lim_{I\to\infty}\sum_{i=I+1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{i}|=0.

Therefore, for any
\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
,

\langle F,\varphi\rangle=\lim_{I\to\infty}\left\langle\sum_{i=1}^{I}\lambda_{i}\widehat{a_{i}},\,\varphi\right\rangle.

This finishes the proof of (
3.14
) and hence of Theorem
3.1
.
∎

)

As applications of Theorem

, we first prove the function

We embark on the proof of the first desired result.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\vec{p}\in{(0,1]}^{n}
. Then, for any

f\in{H_{A}^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}
, there exists a continuous function
F
on
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
such that

\widehat{f}=F
in
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})

and

\displaystyle\lim_{|x|\to 0^{+}}\frac{F(x)}{[\rho_{*}(x)]^{\frac{1}{p_{+}}{-1}}}=0.

Proof.

Let
\vec{p}\in(0,1]^{n}
,
r\in(\max\{p_{+},1\},\infty]
,
s
be as in (
3.2
)
and
f\in{H_{A}^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}
. Then, by Lemma
3.7
and
Definition
3.2
(II), we find that there exists a sequence

\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\mathbb{C}
and a sequence of
(\vec{p},r,s)
-atoms,

\{a_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
, supported, respectively, in

\{B^{(i)}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\mathfrak{B}
such that

\displaystyle f=\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\lambda_{i}a_{i}\quad\mathrm{in}\quad{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}^{n}}),

and

\displaystyle\|f\|_{{H_{A}^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}\sim\left\|\left\{\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left[\frac{|\lambda_{i}|\mathbf{1}_{B^{(i)}}}{\|\mathbf{1}_{B^{(i)}}\|_{{L^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}}\right]^{\underline{p}}\right\}^{1/\underline{p}}\right\|_{{L^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}.

(4.2)

Furthermore, from the proof of Theorem
3.1
, it follows that the function

\displaystyle F(x)=\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\lambda_{i}\widehat{a_{i}}(x),\quad\forall x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}},

(4.3)

is continuous and satisfies that
\widehat{f}=F
in
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
.

Thus, to show Theorem
4.1
, we only need to prove (
4.1
) holds true for the function
F

as in (
4.3
). To do this, observe that, for any
(\vec{p},r,s)
-atom

a
supported in
x_{0}+B_{k_{0}}
with some
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
and
k_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}

\rho_{*}(x)\leq b^{-k_{0}}

(
3.7
) holds true. This, together with the fact that

\displaystyle\lim_{|x|\to 0^{+}}\frac{|\widehat{a}(x)|}{[\rho_{*}(x)]^{\frac{1}{p_{+}}{-1}}}=0.

For any

, we get the following inequality by (

\displaystyle\frac{|F(x)|}{[\rho_{*}(x)]^{\frac{1}{p_{+}}{-1}}}\leq\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}|\lambda_{i}|\frac{|\widehat{a_{i}}(x)|}{[\rho_{*}(x)]^{\frac{1}{p_{+}}{-1}}}.

(4.5)

Moreover, by (

) and the fact

).
Combining this and (

\displaystyle\lim_{|x|\to 0^{+}}\frac{F(x)}{[\rho_{*}(x)]^{\frac{1}{p_{+}}{-1}}}=0,

which completes the proof of Theorem
4.1
.

{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})

and

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|F(x)|^{p_{+}}\min\left\{\left[\rho_{*}(x)\right]^{p_{+}-\frac{p_{+}}{p_{-}}-1},\,\left[\rho_{*}(x)\right]^{p_{+}-2}\right\}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{+}}}\leq C\|f\|_{{H_{A}^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}},

(4.7)

where
C
is a positive constant depending only on
A
and
\vec{p}
.

Proof.

Let
\vec{p}\in(0,1]^{n}
and
f\in{H_{A}^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}
. Then, by Lemma
3.7

and Definition
3.2
(II), we find that
there exist a sequence

\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\mathbb{C}
and a sequence of
(\vec{p},2,s)
-atoms

\{a_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
, supported respectively, in

\{B^{(i)}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\mathfrak{B}
such that

\displaystyle f=\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\lambda_{i}a_{i}\quad\mathrm{in}\quad{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}^{n}}),

and

\displaystyle\left\|\left\{\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left[\frac{|\lambda_{i}|\mathbf{1}_{B^{(i)}}}{\|\mathbf{1}_{B^{(i)}}\|_{{L^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}}\right]^{\underline{p}}\right\}^{1/\underline{p}}\right\|_{{L^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}\lesssim\|f\|_{{H_{A}^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}<\infty.

(4.8)

To prove Theorem
4.3
, it suffices to show that (
4.7
)
holds true for the function
F
as in (
4.3
). For this purpose,
by the fact that
\underline{p}\leq p_{+}\leq 1
, the inverse Minkovski inequality and (
4.8
), we have

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}|\lambda_{i}|^{p_{+}}\right)^{1/p_{+}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left\|\frac{|\lambda_{i}|{\mathbf{1}}_{B^{(i)}}}{\|{\mathbf{1}}_{B^{(i)}}\|_{L^{\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}\right\|_{{L^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}^{p_{+}}\right)^{1/p_{+}}

(4.9)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left\|\frac{|\lambda_{i}|^{p_{+}}{\mathbf{1}}_{B^{(i)}}}{\|{\mathbf{1}}_{B^{(i)}}\|_{{L^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}^{p_{+}}}\right\|_{L^{\vec{p}/p_{+}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}\right)^{1/p_{+}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left[\frac{|\lambda_{i}|{\mathbf{1}}_{B^{(i)}}}{\|{\mathbf{1}}_{B^{(i)}}\|_{{L^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}}\right]^{p_{+}}\right\|_{L^{\vec{p}/p_{+}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}^{1/p_{+}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\left\{\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left[\frac{|\lambda_{i}|{\mathbf{1}}_{B^{(i)}}}{\|{\mathbf{1}}_{B^{(i)}}\|_{{L^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}}\right]^{p_{+}}\right\}^{1/p_{+}}\right\|_{L^{\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\left\{\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left[\frac{|\lambda_{i}|{\mathbf{1}}_{B^{(i)}}}{\|{\mathbf{1}}_{B^{(i)}}\|_{{L^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}}\right]^{\underline{p}}\right\}^{1/\underline{p}}\right\|_{{L^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{{H_{A}^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}.

On another hand, from (
4.3
), the fact that
p_{+}\in(0,1]
, (
3.8
)
and the Fatou lemma, it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}|F(x)|^{p_{+}}\min\left\{\left[\rho_{*}(x)\right]^{p_{+}-\frac{p_{+}}{p_{-}}-1},\,\left[\rho_{*}(x)\right]^{p_{+}-2}\right\}\,dx

(4.10)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}|\lambda_{i}|^{p_{+}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\left[\left|\widehat{a_{i}}(x)\right|\min\left\{\left[\rho_{*}(x)\right]^{1-\frac{1}{p_{-}}-\frac{1}{p_{+}}},\,\left[\rho_{*}(x)\right]^{1-\frac{2}{p_{+}}}\right\}\right]^{p_{+}}\,dx.

Next, we devote to proving the following uniform estimate for all
(\vec{p},2,s)
-atoms, namely,

\displaystyle\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\left[\left|\widehat{a}(x)\right|\min\left\{\left[\rho_{*}(x)\right]^{1-\frac{1}{p_{-}}-\frac{1}{p_{+}}},\,\left[\rho_{*}(x)\right]^{1-\frac{2}{p_{+}}}\right\}\right]^{p_{+}}\,dx\right)^{1/p_{+}}\leq M,

(4.11)

where
M
is a positive constant independent of
a
. Assume that (
4.11
) holds true for the moment. Combining this, (
4.9
) and (
4.10
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}|F(x)|^{p_{+}}\min\left\{\left[\rho_{*}(x)\right]^{p_{+}-\frac{p_{+}}{p_{-}}-1},\,\left[\rho_{*}(x)\right]^{p_{+}-2}\right\}\,dx\right)^{1/{p_{+}}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq M\left(\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}|\lambda_{i}|^{p_{+}}\right)^{1/{p_{+}}}\lesssim\|f\|_{{H_{A}^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}.

This is the desired conclusion (
4.7
).

Thus, the rest of the whole proof is to show the assertion
(
4.11
). Indeed, for any
(\vec{p},2,s)
-atom
a
supported in
a dilated ball
x_{0}+B_{i_{0}}
with some
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
and
i_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\left[\left|\widehat{a}(x)\right|\min\left\{\left[\rho_{*}(x)\right]^{1-\frac{1}{p_{-}}-\frac{1}{p_{+}}},\,\left[\rho_{*}(x)\right]^{1-\frac{2}{p_{+}}}\right\}\right]^{p_{+}}\,dx\right)^{1/{p_{+}}}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\left(\int_{(A^{*})^{-i_{0}+1}B_{0}^{*}}\left[\left|\widehat{a}(x)\right|\min\left\{\left[\rho_{*}(x)\right]^{1-\frac{1}{p_{-}}-\frac{1}{p_{+}}},\,\left[\rho_{*}(x)\right]^{1-\frac{2}{p_{+}}}\right\}\right]^{p_{+}}\,dx\right)^{1/{p_{+}}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(\int_{((A^{*})^{-i_{0}+1}B_{0}^{*})^{\complement}}\left[\left|\widehat{a}(x)\right|\min\left\{\left[\rho_{*}(x)\right]^{1-\frac{1}{p_{-}}-\frac{1}{p_{+}}},\,\left[\rho_{*}(x)\right]^{1-\frac{2}{p_{+}}}\right\}\right]^{p_{+}}\,dx\right)^{1/{p_{+}}}

\displaystyle\quad=:{\rm I}_{1}+{\rm I}_{2},

where
B^{*}_{0}
is the unit dilated ball with respect to
A^{*}
.

Let
\theta
be a fixed positive constant such that

1-\frac{1}{p_{+}}+(s+1)\frac{\ln\lambda_{-}}{\ln b}-\theta\geq 1-\frac{1}{p_{-}}+(s+1)\frac{\ln\lambda_{-}}{\ln b}-\theta>0.

Then, to deal with
{\rm I}_{1}
, by (
3.7
), we know that

\displaystyle{\rm I}_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim b^{i_{0}[1+(s+1)\frac{\ln\lambda_{-}}{\ln b}]}\max\left\{b^{-\frac{i_{0}}{p_{-}}},\,b^{-\frac{i_{0}}{p_{+}}}\right\}

\displaystyle\hskip 7.11317pt\times\left(\int_{(A^{*})^{-i_{0}+1}B_{0}^{*}}\left[\min\left\{\left[\rho_{*}(x)\right]^{1-\frac{1}{p_{-}}-\frac{1}{p_{+}}+(s+1)\frac{\ln\lambda_{-}}{\ln b}},\,\left[\rho_{*}(x)\right]^{1-\frac{2}{p_{+}}+(s+1)\frac{\ln\lambda_{-}}{\ln b}}\right\}\right]^{p_{+}}\,dx\right)^{1/{p_{+}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim b^{i_{0}[1+(s+1)\frac{\ln\lambda_{-}}{\ln b}]}\max\left\{b^{-\frac{i_{0}}{p_{-}}},\,b^{-\frac{i_{0}}{p_{+}}}\right\}

\displaystyle\hskip 7.11317pt\times\min\left\{b^{-i_{0}[1-\frac{1}{p_{-}}+(s+1)\frac{\ln\lambda_{-}}{\ln b}-\theta]},\,b^{-i_{0}[1-\frac{1}{p_{+}}+(s+1)\frac{\ln\lambda_{-}}{\ln b}-\theta]}\right\}\left(\int_{(A^{*})^{-i_{0}+1}B_{0}^{*}}\left[\rho_{*}(x)\right]^{\theta p_{+}-1}\,dx\right)^{1/{p_{+}}}

\displaystyle\sim b^{i_{0}\theta}\left[\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus{\mathbb{N}}}b^{-i_{0}+k}(b-1)b^{(-i_{0}+k)(\theta p_{+}-1)}\right]^{1/{p_{+}}}\sim\left(\frac{b-1}{1-b^{-\theta p_{+}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{+}}}.

As for the estimate of
{\rm I}_{2}
, by the Hölder inequality, the Plancherel theorem,
the fact that
0<p_{-}\leq p_{+}\leq 1
and the size condition of
a
, we obtain

\displaystyle{\rm I}_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\{\int_{((A^{*})^{-i_{0}+1}B_{0}^{*})^{\complement}}\left|\widehat{a}(x)\right|^{2}\,dx\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 7.11317pt\hskip 7.11317pt\times\left\{\int_{((A^{*})^{-i_{0}+1}B_{0}^{*})^{\complement}}\left[\min\left\{\left[\rho_{*}(x)\right]^{1-\frac{1}{p_{-}}-\frac{1}{p_{+}}},\,\left[\rho_{*}(x)\right]^{1-\frac{2}{p_{+}}}\right\}\right]^{\frac{2p_{+}}{2-p_{+}}}\,dx\right\}^{\frac{2-p_{+}}{2p_{+}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|a\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}\left\{\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}b^{-i_{0}+k}(b-1)\left[\min\left\{b^{(-i_{0}+k)(1-\frac{1}{p_{-}}-\frac{1}{p_{+}})},\,b^{(-i_{0}+k)(1-\frac{2}{p_{+}})}\right\}\right]^{\frac{2p_{+}}{2-p_{+}}}\right\}^{\frac{2-p_{+}}{2p_{+}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|a\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}\left\{b^{-i_{0}}\left[\min\left\{b^{-i_{0}(1-\frac{1}{p_{-}}-\frac{1}{p_{+}})},\,b^{-i_{0}(1-\frac{2}{p_{+}})}\right\}\right]^{\frac{2p_{+}}{2-p_{+}}}\right\}^{\frac{2-p_{+}}{2p_{+}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\max\left\{b^{i_{0}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p_{-}})},\,b^{i_{0}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p_{+}})}\right\}\min\left\{b^{-i_{0}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p_{-}})},\,b^{-i_{0}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p_{+}})}\right\}

\displaystyle\sim 1.

This finishes the proof of (
4.11
) and hence of Theorem
4.3
.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

Actually, when
\vec{p}=(p,\ldots,p)\in(0,1]^{n}
, the Hardy space
{H_{A}^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}
in Theorem
4.3
is just
the anisotropic Hardy space
H^{p}_{A}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
from
[
1
]
in the sense of equivalent quasi-norms. Thus, we point out that Theorem
4.3
covers
[
2
, Corollary 8]
.
Moreover, if
A=d\,{\rm I}_{n\times n}
for some
d\in{\mathbb{R}}
with
|d|\in(1,\infty)
,
then the anisotropic mixed-norm Hardy space
H^{\vec{p}}_{A}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, with

\vec{p}=(p,\ldots,p)\in(0,1]^{n}
, coincides with the classical Hardy space
H^{p}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
of
Fefferman and Stein
[
12
]
. In this case,
\rho_{*}(x)\sim|x|^{n}
for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
, and hence (
4.5
) is just the classic Hardy–Littlewood inequality as in (
1.2
).

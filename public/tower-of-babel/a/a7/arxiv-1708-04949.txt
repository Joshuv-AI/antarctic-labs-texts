On the p p -essential normality of principal submodules of the Bergman module on strongly pseudoconvex domains
Ronald G. Douglas, Kunyu Guo, Yi Wang

Abstract
In this paper, we show that under a mild condition, a principal submodule of the Bergman module on a bounded strongly pseudoconvex domain with smooth boundary in ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} is p p -essentially normal for all p > n p>n . This improves a previous result by the first author and K. Wang, in which it was shown that any polynomial-generated principal submodule of the Bergman module on the unit ball 𝔹 n \mathbb{B}_{n} is p p -essentially normal for all p > n p>n . As a consequence, we show that the submodule of L a 2 ​ ( 𝔹 n ) L_{a}^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n}) consisting of functions vanishing on an analytic subset of pure codimension 1 1 is p p -essentially normal for all p > n p>n .

Introduction

Let
\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}]
be the ring of analytic polynomials of
n
variables. For a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, a homomorphism

\Phi:~\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}]\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})

defines a
\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}]
-module structure on
\mathcal{H}
. In this case we say
\mathcal{H}
is a Hilbert module (over
\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}]
). A closed subspace
\mathcal{P}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
invariant under the module actions is called a submodule. It naturally inherits a Hilbert module structure by restriction:

\Phi^{\prime}:\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}]\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}),\quad p\mapsto\Phi(p)|_{\mathcal{P}}.

The quotient space
\mathcal{Q}=\mathcal{H}\ominus\mathcal{P}\cong\mathcal{H}/\mathcal{P}
, inherits a module structure by compression:

\Phi^{\prime\prime}:\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}]\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{Q}),\quad p\mapsto Q\Phi(p)|_{\mathcal{Q}}.

Here
Q
denotes the projection operator onto
\mathcal{Q}
.

The Hilbert module
\mathcal{H}
is said to be
essentially normal
if
the cross commutator
[\Phi(z_{i}),\Phi(z_{j})^{*}]
is compact for all
i,j=1,\ldots,n.
If moreover the corss commutators are in the Schatten-
p
class
\mathcal{S}^{p}
for some
p>0
, then we say that
\mathcal{H}
is
p
-essentially normal
.

A well known example of essentially normal Hilbert module is the Bergman module
L_{a}^{2}(\Omega)
on a bounded strongly pseudoconvex domain
\Omega
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with smooth boundary. In fact it is
p
-essentially normal for all
p>n
. The module action on
L_{a}^{2}(\Omega)
is given by pointwise multiplications

\Phi(z_{i})=M_{z_{i}},\quad M_{z_{i}}f=z_{i}f,\quad f\in L_{a}^{2}(\Omega),~i=1,\ldots,n.

For an ideal
I
in
\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}]
, denote
[I]
its closure in
L_{a}^{2}(\Omega)
. Then
[I]
is a Hilbert submodule of
L_{a}^{2}(\Omega)
. The operators
R_{i}=M_{z_{i}}|_{[I]},~i=1,\ldots,n
give the module action on
[I]
.

In a sequence of papers
[
1
]
[
2
]
, Arveson conjectured that all submodules obtained by taking closure of a homogenous polynomial ideal in the Drury-Arveson module
H_{n}^{2}
on the unit ball
\mathbb{B}_{n}
are
p
-essentially normal for all
p>n
. Using the graded structure, one can show that the conjecture is equivalent to its analogous version on the Bergman space
L_{a}^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n})
. It was then pointed out by the first author in
[
5
]
that, more generally, an essentially normal Hilbert module may carry important geometric information. Suppose
\mathcal{H}
is an irreducible essentially normal Hilbert module. Let
\mathcal{T}
be the
C^{*}
-algebra generated by its module actions
\{\Phi(p):p\in\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}]\}
and the identity operator
I
. Then the operators in
\mathcal{T}
are essentially commutative, giving a short exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{T}\to C(X)\to 0.

Here
X
is the joint essential Taylor spectrum of the tuple
\big(\Phi(z_{1}),\ldots,\Phi(z_{n})\big)
. By the BDF-theory (cf.
[
3
]
), the exact sequence gives an element
[\mathcal{H}]
in the K-homology group
K_{1}(X)
. For the Bergman module
L_{a}^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n})
it can be shown that
X=\mathbb{S}_{n}=\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}
, the unit sphere, and
[L_{a}^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n})]
is the fundamental class in
K_{1}(\mathbb{S}_{n})
. For a homogenous ideal
I
, if the submodule
[I]
is essentially normal, then
[I]
defines the same
K
-homology element as
L_{a}^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n})
. Things are more interesting on the quotient module
[I]^{\perp}
, in which case the
K
-homology element lies in
K_{1}\big(Z(I)\cap\mathbb{S}_{n}\big)
. Several generalizations of Arveson’s conjecture were listed in
[
5
]
. In particular, for a quotient module
[I]^{\perp}
the range of
p
where
[I]^{\perp}
is
p
-essentially normal is conjectured to be
p>\dim_{\mathbb{C}}Z(I)
. Also, questions on the
p
-essential normality of submodules/quotient modules over strongly pseudoconvex domains was raised. These are generally refereed to as the Arveson-Douglas Conjecture. Various effort have been invested into the study of this conjecture,
[

]
[

]
[

]

]

]

]
, to list just a few. See

In
[
7
]
, the first author and K. Wang proved the surprising result that a principal submodule generated by any polynomial in the Bergman space on the unit ball
\mathbb{B}_{n}
is
p
-essentially normal for all

The main goal of this paper is to prove the following result.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
4.1
)
.

Suppose
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a bounded strongly pseudoconvex domain with smooth boundary,
h
is a holomorphic function defined in an open neighborhood of
\overline{\mathbb{B}_{n}}
. Then the principal submodule generated by
h
,

[h]=\overline{\{hf:~f\in L_{a}^{2}(\Omega)\}},

is
p
-essentially normal for all
p>n
.

In
[
7
]
, the authors took an approach which involves some global estimate in which the controlling constant increases to infinity as the degree of the generating polynomial increases to infinity. In this paper, we take a different approach, which involves a sub-mean-type inequality (see Theorem
3.1
) that can be proved using local estimates. Once the inequality is proved, the main result follows from a relatively standard argument.

For a complex zero variety
V
, we define the submodule
\mathcal{P}_{V}
consisting of functions in
L_{a}^{2}(\Omega)
that vanish on
V
. A geometric version of the Arveson-Douglas Conjecture was raised by Engliš and Eschmeier in
[
9
]
. For a nice variety
V
it was conjectured that the quotient module
\mathcal{Q}_{V}=\mathcal{P}_{V}^{\perp}
is
p
-essential normality for
p>\dim_{\mathbb{C}}V
.

Suppose
V
is of pure dimension
n-1
in an open neighborhood of
\overline{\Omega}
, and assume that
\Omega
satisfies an additional topological assumption that ensures solvability of the second Cousin problem, then (see Lemma
5.6
)
V
has a defining function
h
so that

\mathcal{P}_{V}=\{hf:f\in\mathrm{Hol}(\Omega),~hf\in L_{a}^{2}(\Omega)\}.

As a byproduct of our main result, we show the following.

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
5.7
)
.

Suppose
V
is a pure
(n-1)
-dimensional analytic subset of an open neighborhood of
\overline{\mathbb{B}_{n}}
, then
V
has a minimal defining function
h
on an open neighborhood of
\overline{\mathbb{B}_{n}}
. Moreover, we have

[h]=\mathcal{P}_{V}=\{hf:f\in\mathrm{Hol}(\mathbb{B}_{n}),hf\in L_{a}^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n})\}.

In particular, the submodule
\mathcal{P}_{V}
and the quotient module
\mathcal{P}_{V}^{\perp}
are
p
-essentially normal for all
p>n
.

This paper is organized as follows. In section 2 we introduce tools and notations involving strongly pseudoconvex domains. In section 3 we prove the sub-mean-type inequality mentioned above. This will contain most of the technicality in this paper. In section 4 we prove our main result using the inequality. In section 5 we study the submodule

and prove Theorem

Definition 2.1
.

1.

For
\Omega
a bounded domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with smooth boundary, we call
\rho(z)
a
defining function
for
\Omega
provided

(i)

\Omega=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:~\rho(z)<0\}
and
\rho(z)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
.

(ii)

|\mathrm{grad}\rho(z)|\neq 0
for all
z\in\partial\Omega
.

2.

For
\Omega
a
bounded strongly pseudoconvex domain with smooth boundary
we mean that there are a defining function
\rho
of
\Omega
and a constant
k>0
such that

\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}\rho(\zeta)}{\partial z_{i}\partial\bar{z}_{j}}\xi_{i}\bar{\xi}_{j}\geq k|\xi|^{2},\quad\forall\zeta\in\partial\Omega,~\forall\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

(2.1)

In the sequel, when we say that
\rho
is a defining function for a bounded strongly pseudoconvex domain
\Omega
with smooth boundary, we mean that
\rho
is a defining function of
\Omega
satisfying (
2.1
).

3.

Suppose
\Omega
is a bounded strongly pseudoconvex domain with smooth boundary and
\rho
a defining function. For a point
\zeta\in\partial\Omega
, the vector

\bar{\partial}\rho(\zeta)=\big(\frac{\partial\rho(\zeta)}{\partial\bar{z}_{1}},\frac{\partial\rho(\zeta)}{\partial\bar{z}_{2}},\ldots,\frac{\partial\rho(\zeta)}{\partial\bar{z}_{n}}\big)^{T}

(2.2)

is non-zero and represents the
complex normal direction
at
\zeta
.
The
complex tangent space

T^{\mathbb{C}}_{\zeta}(\partial\Omega)
(cf.
[
23
]
) at
\zeta
is defined to be the orthogonal complement of
\bar{\partial}\rho(\zeta)
, i.e.,

T_{\zeta}^{\mathbb{C}}(\partial\Omega)=\bigg\{\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:~\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial\rho(\zeta)}{\partial z_{j}}\xi_{j}=0\bigg\}.

The definition does not depend on the choice of a defining function.
Since
\partial\Omega
is smooth, for
z
close enough to
\partial\Omega
there exists a unique point
\pi(z)\in\partial\Omega
with
d(z,\pi(z))=d(z,\partial\Omega)
,
d
being the Euclidean distance. Moreover, the map
z\mapsto\pi(z)
is smooth. (cf.
[
12
, Theorem 4.8 (5) and Lemma 4.11]
). For such
z
, define the
complex normal (tangent) direction
at
z
to be the corresponding complex normal (tangent) direction at
\pi(z)
.

Remark 2.2
.

In classic literature, for example,
[
21
]
and
[
25
]
, strongly pseudoconvex domains, also called strictly Levi pseudoconvex domains, are defined by the weaker condition that (
2.1
) holds only for
\xi\in T^{\mathbb{C}}_{\zeta}(\partial\Omega),\zeta\in\partial\Omega
. There is a related type of domain called strictly pseudoconvex domain, for which one requires (
2.1
) but drops the requirement (ii) in Definition
2.1
. However, in the case when
\Omega
is bounded with smooth boundary, these definitions coincide and can be strengthened into the one we give in Definition
2.1
(cf.
[
25
, Page 60]
). This allows us to cite references on both strongly pseudoconvex domains and strictly pseudoconvex domains.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a bounded strongly pseudoconvex domain with smooth boundary. The
Bergman space

L_{a}^{2}(\Omega)
consists of all holomorphic functions on
\Omega
which are square integrable with respect to the Lebesgue measure
v_{n}
.

L_{a}^{2}(\Omega)=\{f\in\mathrm{Hol}(\Omega):~\int_{\Omega}|f(z)|^{2}dv_{n}(z)<\infty\}.

For
l\geq 0
, one defines the
weighted Bergman space

L_{a,l}^{2}(\Omega)
in a similar way.

L_{a,l}^{2}(\Omega)=\{f\in\mathrm{Hol}(\Omega):~\int_{\Omega}|f(z)|^{2}|\rho(z)|^{l}dv_{n}(z)<\infty\}.

Standard arguments show that the Bergman and weighted Bergman spaces are reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces. We use
K(z,w)
and
K_{l}(z,w)
to denote their reproducing kernels, i.e.,

f(z)=\int_{\Omega}f(w)K(z,w)dv_{n}(w),\quad\forall f\in L_{a}^{2}(\Omega),~z\in\Omega,

f(z)=\int_{\Omega}f(w)K_{l}(z,w)|\rho(w)|^{l}dv_{n}(w),\quad\forall f\in L_{a,l}^{2}(\Omega),~z\in\Omega.

\big|F^{\prime}(\Phi(z),\Phi(w))\big|\\

3
An Inequality

The goal of this section is to prove the following estimate, which plays a key role in the proof of our main theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a bounded strongly pseudoconvex domain with smooth boundary and
h
is a holomorphic function defined in an open neighborhood of
\overline{\Omega}
. Then there exist constants
N>0,C>0
such that for any
w,z\in\Omega
and
f\in\mathrm{Hol}(\Omega)
,

|h(z)f(w)|\leq C\frac{F(z,w)^{N}}{|\rho(w)|^{N+n+1}}\int_{E(w,1)}|h(\lambda)f(\lambda)|\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\lambda).

(3.1)

Here the function
F
is defined as in Definition
2.7
.

Let us first give an outline of the proof. By Lemma
2.6
, for
w
close to the boundary,
E(w,1)
is comparable with some
P_{w}(a\delta(w),b\delta(w)^{1/2})
. A large part of this section is to show that the following inequality holds for
(z,w)\in R_{\delta}
, for some
\delta>0
.

\displaystyle\log|h(z)|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|P_{w}(a\delta(w),b\delta(w)^{1/2})|}\int_{P_{w}(a\delta(w),b\delta(w)^{1/2})}\log|h(\lambda)|\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\lambda)

(3.2)

\displaystyle~~~+N\log\frac{F(z,w)}{|\rho(w)|}+\text{some constant}.

For any
f\in\mathrm{Hol}(\Omega)
, the function
\log|f|
is pluri-subharmonic. So

\log|f(w)|\leq\frac{1}{|P_{w}(a\delta(w),b\delta(w)^{1/2})|}\int_{P_{w}(a\delta(w),b\delta(w)^{1/2})}\log|f(\lambda)|\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\lambda)

(3.3)

Adding up (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), and then applying Jensen’s inequality yields

|h(z)f(w)|\lesssim\bigg(\frac{F(z,w)}{|\rho(w)|}\bigg)^{N}\cdot\frac{1}{|P_{w}(a\delta(w),b\delta(w)^{1/2})|}\int_{P_{w}(a\delta(w),b\delta(w)^{1/2})}|h(\lambda)f(\lambda)|\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\lambda).

From this we get the following local version of Theorem
3.1
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a bounded strongly pseudoconvex domain with smooth boundary and
h
is a holomorphic function defined in an open neighborhood of
\overline{\Omega}
. Then there exist
\delta>0
,
N>0,C>0
such that (
3.1
) holds for any
(z,w)\in R_{\delta}\cap\Omega\times\Omega
and
f\in\mathrm{Hol}(\Omega)
.

Once Lemma
3.2
is established, Theorem
3.1
follows from a relatively easy argument.

We start our proof. The following estimate holds by direct computation.

Lemma 3.3
.

For any
z,a\in\mathbb{C}
and
r>0
,

\log|z-a|<\frac{1}{|\Delta(0,r)|}\int_{\Delta(0,r)}\log|\lambda-a|\mathrm{d}v_{1}(\lambda)+\log\frac{r+|z|}{r}+1.

Proof.

We split the proof into two cases.

Case 1:
|a|\geq r
.

In this case the function
\log|\lambda-a|
is harmonic in
\Delta(0,r)
. Therefore

\frac{1}{|\Delta(0,r)|}\int_{\Delta(0,r)}\log|\lambda-a|\mathrm{d}v_{1}(\lambda)=\log|a|.

It follows that

\log|z-a|-\frac{1}{|\Delta(0,r)|}\int_{\Delta(0,r)}\log|\lambda-a|\mathrm{d}v_{1}(\lambda)-\log\frac{r+|z|}{r}=\log\frac{|z-a|r}{|a|(r+|z|)}.

Set
M=\max\{r+|z|,|a|\}
and
m=\min\{r+|z|,|a|\}
. Then

|z-a|\leq|z|+|a|\leq 2\max\{|z|,|a|\}\leq 2\max\{r+|z|,|a|\}=2M,

and

r\leq\min\{r+|z|,|a|\}=m.

Therefore

\frac{|z-a|r}{|a|(r+|z|)}=\frac{|z-a|r}{Mm}\leq 2.

This shows that

\log|z-a|-\frac{1}{|\Delta(0,r)|}\int_{\Delta(0,r)}\log|\lambda-a|\mathrm{d}v_{1}(\lambda)-\log\frac{r+|z|}{r}=\log\frac{|z-a|r}{|a|(r+|z|)}\leq\log 2<1.

This proves the inequality in the case 1.

Case 2:
|a|<r
.

\displaystyle\int_{\Delta(0,r)}\log|\lambda-a|\mathrm{d}v_{1}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Delta(a,r)}\log|\lambda-a|\mathrm{d}v_{1}(\lambda)-\int_{\Delta(a,r)\backslash\Delta(0,r)}\log|\lambda-a|\mathrm{d}v_{1}(\lambda)+\int_{\Delta(0,r)\backslash\Delta(a,r)}\log|\lambda-a|\mathrm{d}v_{1}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\Delta(a,r)}\log|\lambda-a|\mathrm{d}v_{1}(\lambda)-\int_{\Delta(a,r)\backslash\Delta(0,r)}\log r\mathrm{d}v_{1}(\lambda)+\int_{\Delta(0,r)\backslash\Delta(a,r)}\log r\mathrm{d}v_{1}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Delta(a,r)}\log|\lambda-a|\mathrm{d}v_{1}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}2\pi s\log s\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi r^{2}\big(\log r-\frac{1}{2}\big).

It follows that

\displaystyle\log|z-a|-\frac{1}{|\Delta(0,r)|}\int_{\Delta(0,r)}\log|\lambda-a|\mathrm{d}v_{1}(\lambda)-\log\frac{r+|z|}{r}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\log|z-a|-\big(\log r-\frac{1}{2}\big)-\log\frac{r+|z|}{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log\frac{|z-a|}{r+|z|}+\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\log\frac{|a|+|z|}{r+|z|}+\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\log 1+\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle 1.

This proves the inequality in case 2.
The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose
p
is a one-variable polynomial of degree less than or equal to
d
. Then for any
r>0
and
z\in\mathbb{C}
,

\log|p(z)|\leq\frac{1}{|\Delta(0,r)|}\int_{\Delta(0,r)}\log|p(\lambda)|\mathrm{d}v_{1}(\lambda)+d\log\frac{r+|z|}{r}+d.

Proof.

The case
p\equiv 0
is trivial. Suppose
p\nequiv 0
. Then by assumption,

p(\lambda)=c\prod_{i=1}^{k}(\lambda-a_{i}),\quad k\leq d,~c\neq 0.

Therefore by Lemma
3.3
,

\displaystyle\log|p(z)|=

\displaystyle\log|c|+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\log|z-a_{i}|

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\log|c|+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\bigg[\frac{1}{|\Delta(0,r)|}\int_{\Delta(0,r)}\log|\lambda-a_{i}|\mathrm{d}v_{1}(\lambda)+\log\frac{r+|z|}{r}+1\bigg]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\Delta(0,r)|}\int_{\Delta(0,r)}\log|p(\lambda)|\mathrm{d}v_{1}(\lambda)+k\log\frac{r+|z|}{r}+k

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\Delta(0,r)|}\int_{\Delta(0,r)}\log|p(\lambda)|\mathrm{d}v_{1}(\lambda)+d\log\frac{r+|z|}{r}+d.

This completes the proof.
∎

For a
n
-variable polynomial
p\in\mathbb{C}[z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n}]
, the degree of

is the highest degree of the monomials contained in

, i.e., for
p(z)=\sum a_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}

\mathrm{degree}p=\{|\alpha|:~a_{\alpha}\neq 0\}.

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose
p
is a
n
-variable polynomial, i.e.,
p\in\mathbb{C}[z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n}]

is of degree less than or equal to

. Then for any

\log|p(z)|\leq\frac{1}{|P_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}}|}\int_{P_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}}}\log|p(\lambda)|\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\lambda)+d\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log\frac{r_{i}+|z_{i}|}{r_{i}}+nd.

Proof.

The inequality follows from Lemma
3.4
by induction.

\displaystyle\log|p(z)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\Delta(0,r_{1})|}\int_{\Delta(0,r_{1})}\log|p(\lambda_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})|\mathrm{d}v_{1}(\lambda_{1})+d\log\frac{r_{1}+|z_{1}|}{r_{1}}+d

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\Delta(0,r_{1})|}\int_{\Delta(0,r_{1})}\bigg\{\frac{1}{|\Delta(0,r_{2})|}\int_{\Delta(0,r_{2})}\log|p(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},z_{3},\ldots,z_{n})|\mathrm{d}v_{1}(\lambda_{2})+d\log\frac{r_{2}+|z_{2}|}{r_{2}}+d\bigg\}\mathrm{d}v_{1}(\lambda_{1})

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi^{2}r_{1}^{2}r_{2}^{2}}\int_{\Delta(0,r_{1})\times\Delta(0,r_{2})}\log|p(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},z_{3},\ldots,z_{n})|\mathrm{d}v_{2}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})+d\frac{r_{2}+|z_{2}|}{r_{2}}+d\log\frac{r_{1}+|z_{1}|}{r_{1}}+2d

\displaystyle\ldots

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi^{n}r_{1}^{2}\ldots r_{n}^{2}}\int_{P_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}}}\log|p(\lambda)|\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\lambda)+d\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log\frac{r_{i}+|z_{i}|}{r_{i}}+nd

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|P_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}}|}\int_{P_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}}}\log|p(\lambda)|\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\lambda)+d\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log\frac{r_{i}+|z_{i}|}{r_{i}}+nd.

.

(3.7)

|\varphi_{1}(\xi,w)|\leq\frac{M}{(s_{1}/2)^{d_{1}}},\quad\frac{1}{|\varphi_{1}(\xi,w)|}\leq\frac{(2s_{1})^{d_{1}}}{m}

for

Take

and

is defined on

For

Lemma 3.9
.

Suppose
h\in\mathrm{Hol}(U)
, where
U
is an open neighborhood of
0\in\mathbb{C}^{k}
,
h
is not identically zero. Then there exists an orthonormal basis
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}\}
of
\mathbb{C}^{k}
, such that for every
i\in\{1,\cdots,k\}
, the one-variable function
h(\lambda e_{i})
is not identically zero.

Lemma
3.9
can be implied by
[
4
, Lemma 2, Page 33]
. To avoid employing more terminologies we give a straightforward proof. We thank Dr. Hui Dan for suggesting this proof to us.

Proof.

In the case
k=1
, the conclusion is obvious. In the case
k=2
, notice that
\langle(z_{1},z_{2}),(\bar{z}_{2},-\bar{z}_{1})\rangle=0
for all pairs
(z_{1},z_{2})
. Let

f(z_{1},z_{2})=h(z_{1},z_{2})\overline{h(\bar{z}_{2},-\bar{z}_{1})}.

The function
f
is a holomorphic function defined in an open neighborhood of
0
and
f
is not identically zero. Pick any
(z_{1},z_{2})\neq 0
so that
f(z)\neq 0
and normalize
\{(z_{1},z_{2}),(\bar{z}_{2},-\bar{z}_{1})\}
into an orthonormal basis. This will satisfy our condition.

We prove the general case by induction, suppose we have proved the result for
U\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{k-1}
. Now for
U\subset\mathbb{C}^{k}
, pick
z\neq 0
so that
h(z)\neq 0
. Pick a two dimensional subspace
L\subset\mathbb{C}^{k}
containing
z
, then
h|_{L\cap U}\nequiv 0
. Since
\dim L=2
, by the previous argument we have orthonormal
v_{1}
and
v_{2}\in L\cap U
so that
h
is not identically
0
on
\mathbb{C}v_{1}\cap U
and
\mathbb{C}v_{2}\cap U
. Now consider
L^{\prime}=v_{1}^{\perp}
, since
v_{2}\in L^{\prime}\cap U
,
h|_{L^{\prime}\cap U}\nequiv 0
. By induction, we have orthonormal
\{e_{2},\ldots,e_{k}\}\subseteq L^{\prime}
such that
h
is not identically
0
on
\mathbb{C}e_{i}\cap U
,
i=2,\ldots,k
. The set
\{v_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{k}\}
is the desired basis. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.10
.

\Omega

Without loss of generality, assume

e_{0,1}=\frac{\bar{\partial}\rho(0)}{|\bar{\partial}\rho(0)|},

.
Recall that the complex tangent space at

Below let us work under the basis

We split the proof into two cases.

Case 1:

h|_{\mathbb{C}e_{0,1}\cap U}
is not identically zero.

If

, then there exists a positive integer

If

. Thus in either case we have

h(z)=z_{1}^{d}h_{1}(z),\quad\text{ where }h_{1}|_{\mathbb{C}e_{0,1}\cap U}\nequiv 0,\quad h_{1}|_{T^{\mathbb{C}}_{0}(\partial\Omega)\cap U}\nequiv 0.

(3.10)

By Lemma
3.6
, we can modify our choice of

(3.11)

For
w
close to

By Definition

Apply the Gram-Schmidt method to

, and the vectors in

vary smoothly with respect to

Define the map

For
s,t>0

P_{w}(s,t)=\{w+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}e_{w,i}:|\xi_{1}|<s,|\xi_{j}|<t,j=2,\ldots,n\}=U_{w}(P_{s,t,\ldots,t}).

This is consistent with our notation in the preliminary.
By Lemma
2.6

\big(\Delta(0,R)\big)^{n}\times\big(\Delta(0,R)\big)^{n}

),

\displaystyle C\frac{F^{\prime}(\Phi(z),\Phi(w))^{N}}{|\rho(w)|^{N+n+1}}\int_{E(w,1)}|h(\lambda)f(\lambda)|J(\lambda)\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{F(z,w)^{N}}{|\rho(w)|^{N+n+1}}\int_{E(w,1)}|h(\lambda)f(\lambda)|\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\lambda).

Here
J(\lambda)
is the real Jacobian of
\Phi
. The last inequality comes from Lemma
2.14
and the fact that
J(\lambda)
is bounded on
\overline{\Omega}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

Since
\partial\Omega
is compact, by Lemma
3.10
, there is a finite open cover
\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
of
\partial\Omega
, and constants
C_{i},N_{i}>0,i=1,\ldots,k
such that for any
f\in\mathrm{Hol}(\Omega)
,
i=1,\ldots,k,
and
z,w\in V_{i}\cap\Omega,

|h(z)f(w)|\leq C_{i}\frac{F(z,w)^{N_{i}}}{|\rho(w)|^{N_{i}+n+1}}\int_{E(w,1)}|h(\lambda)f(\lambda)|\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\lambda).

By Definition
2.7
,
\frac{F(z,w)}{|\rho(w)|}\geq 1
. Take
N=\max\{N_{1},\ldots,N_{k}\}
. Then the above inequality implies

|h(z)f(w)|\lesssim\frac{F(z,w)^{N}}{|\rho(w)|^{N+n+1}}\int_{E(w,1)}|h(\lambda)f(\lambda)|\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\lambda)

whenever
(z,w)\in\big(\cup_{i}V_{i}\times V_{i}\big)\cap\big(\Omega\times\Omega\big)
.
It is easy to see that
R_{\delta}\subset\cup_{i}V_{i}\times V_{i}
for some
\delta>0
. So the inequality holds for
R_{\delta}\cap\big(\Omega\times\Omega\big)\subset\big(\cup_{i}V_{i}\times V_{i}\big)\cap\big(\Omega\times\Omega\big)
. This completes the proof.
∎

We are ready to prove the key inequality.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Let
\delta,N,C
be as in Lemma
3.2
. It suffices to prove (
3.1
) for
(z,w)\notin R_{\delta}
. We first prove

|f(w)|\lesssim\frac{1}{|\rho(w)|^{N+n+1}}\int_{E(w,1)}|h(\lambda)f(\lambda)|\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\lambda),\quad\forall w\in\Omega.

(3.16)

Denote
\mathbb{B}_{n}(z,r)
the Euclidean open ball in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with center
z
and radius
r
.
Take a finite set

\{z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{k}\}\subset\Omega_{\delta/4}

such that

h(z_{i})\neq 0,\quad\forall i=1,2,\ldots,k,

and

\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{B}_{n}(z_{i},\frac{\delta}{3})\supset\Omega_{\delta/4}.

Then for any
w\in\Omega_{\delta/4}
, there is some
z_{i}
such that
|z_{i}-w|<\delta/3
. So

\delta(z_{i})+\delta(w)+|z-w|<\delta/4+\delta/4+\delta/3<\delta,

i.e.,
(z_{i},w)\in R_{\delta}
. Thus by Lemma
3.2
,

|h(z_{i})f(w)|\leq C\frac{F(z_{i},w)^{N}}{|\rho(w)|^{N+n+1}}\int_{E(w,1)}|h(\lambda)f(\lambda)|\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\lambda).

Denote

\delta^{\prime}=\min\{|\rho(z_{j})|:j=1,\ldots,k\},\quad m=\min\{|h(z_{j})|:j=1,\ldots,k\}.

Then
\delta^{\prime}>0,m>0
by construction.
By Definition
2.7
,

F(z_{i},w)\geq\delta^{\prime}.

So we have

\displaystyle|f(w)|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{|h(z_{i})f(w)|}{m}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}C\frac{F(z_{i},w)^{N}}{|\rho(w)|^{N+n+1}}\int_{E(w,1)}|h(\lambda)f(\lambda)|\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\delta^{\prime N}}{m}\frac{1}{|\rho(w)|^{N+n+1}}\int_{E(w,1)}|h(\lambda)f(\lambda)|\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\lambda).

This proves (
3.16
) when
w\in\Omega_{\delta/4}
.
On the other hand, there is
\epsilon>0
such that

|\rho(w)|>\epsilon,\quad E(w,1)\supset\mathbb{B}_{n}(w,\epsilon),\quad\forall w\in\Omega\backslash\Omega_{\delta/4}.

By
[
21
, Proposition 2.1.10]
, the subharmonic function
\log|h|
is locally integrable, and therefore the function
w\mapsto\frac{1}{|\mathbb{B}_{n}(w,\epsilon)|}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}(w,\epsilon)}\log|h(\lambda)|\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\lambda)
is continuous. This proves that there is some
c\in\mathbb{R}
such that

c\leq\frac{1}{|\mathbb{B}_{n}(w,\epsilon)|}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}(w,\epsilon)}\log|h(\lambda)|\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\lambda),\quad\forall w\in\Omega\backslash\Omega_{\delta/4}.

Also,

\log|f(w)|\leq\frac{1}{|\mathbb{B}_{n}(w,\epsilon)|}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}(w,\epsilon)}\log|f(\lambda)|\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\lambda).

Adding up the two inequalities above we get

c+\log|f(w)|\leq\frac{1}{|\mathbb{B}_{n}(w,\epsilon)|}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}(w,\epsilon)}\log|f(\lambda)h(\lambda)|\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\lambda),\quad\forall w\in\Omega\backslash\Omega_{\delta/4}.

By Jensen’s inequality,

\displaystyle e^{c}|f(w)|\leq

\displaystyle\exp\bigg\{\frac{1}{|\mathbb{B}_{n}(w,\epsilon)|}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}(w,\epsilon)}\log|f(\lambda)h(\lambda)|\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\lambda)\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\mathbb{B}_{n}(w,\epsilon)|}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}(w,\epsilon)}|f(\lambda)h(\lambda)|\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}(w,\epsilon)}|f(\lambda)h(\lambda)|\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{E(w,1)}|f(\lambda)h(\lambda)|\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\rho(w)|^{N+n+1}}\int_{E(w,1)}|f(\lambda)h(\lambda)|\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\lambda),\quad\forall w\in\Omega\backslash\Omega_{\delta/4}.

This proves (
3.16
) for
w\in\Omega\backslash\Omega_{\delta/4}
, and hence (
3.16
) holds for any
w\in\Omega
.
If
(z,w)\notin R_{\delta}
, then
F(z,w)
is bounded away from
0
. Also,
|h|
has an upper bounded. So

\displaystyle|h(z)f(w)|\lesssim

\displaystyle|f(w)|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\rho(w)|^{N+n+1}}\int_{E(w,1)}|h(\lambda)f(\lambda)|\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{F(z,w)^{N}}{|\rho(w)|^{N+n+1}}\int_{E(w,1)}|h(\lambda)f(\lambda)|\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\lambda).

Combing Lemma
3.2
we see that the inequality above holds for any
(z,w)\in\Omega\times\Omega
. This completes the proof.
∎

4
Main Result

In this section, we prove the following theorem, which is the main result of this paper.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a bounded strongly pseudoconvex domain with smooth boundary and
h\in\mathrm{Hol}(\overline{\Omega})
. Then the principal submodule of the Bergman module
L_{a}^{2}(\Omega)
generated by
h
is
p
-essentially normal for all
p>n
.

The main ingredient of the proof of Theorem
4.1
is Theorem
3.1
. Besides that, some other preparations are needed.

For a measurable function
G
on
\Omega\times\Omega
, formally define the integral operator

A_{G}f(z)=\int_{\Omega}\frac{G(z,w)}{F(z,w)^{n+1}}f(w)\mathrm{d}v_{n}(w).

(4.1)

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose
G(z,w)\in L^{\infty}(\Omega\times\Omega)
. Then
A_{G}
defines a bounded operator on
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.15
,

\int_{\Omega}\bigg|\frac{G(z,w)}{F(z,w)^{n+1}}\bigg||\rho(w)|^{-1/2}\mathrm{d}v_{n}(w)\lesssim\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\rho(w)|^{-1/2}}{F(z,w)^{n+1}}\mathrm{d}v_{n}(w)\lesssim|\rho(z)|^{-1/2},

and

\int_{\Omega}\bigg|\frac{G(z,w)}{F(z,w)^{n+1}}\bigg||\rho(z)|^{-1/2}\mathrm{d}v_{n}(z)\lesssim\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\rho(z)|^{-1/2}}{F(z,w)^{n+1}}\mathrm{d}v_{n}(z)\lesssim|\rho(w)|^{-1/2}.

By Schur’s test,
A_{G}
is bounded.
∎

As in
[
8
, Lemma 5.1]
, by Lemma
4.2
and interpolation, we have the following Schatten-class membership criterion.

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose
2\leq p<\infty
and
G(z,w)
is a measurable function in
\Omega\times\Omega
. Let
A_{G}
be as in (
4.1
).
If

\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|G(z,w)|^{p}}{F(z,w)^{2(n+1)}}dv_{n}(z)dv_{n}(w)<\infty,

then the operator
A_{G}
is in the Schatten-
p
class
\mathcal{S}^{p}
.

An immediate consequence of Lemma
4.3
is the following.

Lemma 4.4
.

Suppose
c>0
. Denote
P
the orthogonal projection from
L^{2}(\Omega)
onto
L_{a}^{2}(\Omega)
. Then for
p>\frac{n}{c},p\geq 2
it hods that

M_{|\rho|^{c}}P\in\mathcal{S}^{p}.

Proof.

For any
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,

M_{|\rho|^{c}}Pf(z)=\int_{\Omega}|\rho(z)|^{c}K(z,w)f(w)\mathrm{d}v_{n}(w)=\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\rho(z)|^{c}K(z,w)F(z,w)^{n+1}}{F(z,w)^{n+1}}f(w)\mathrm{d}v_{n}(w).

For
p>\frac{n}{c}
,
p\geq 2
, by (
2.10
) and Lemma
2.15
,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\big||\rho(z)|^{c}K(z,w)F(z,w)^{n+1}\big|^{p}}{F(z,w)^{2(n+1)}}\mathrm{d}v_{n}(z)\mathrm{d}v_{n}(w)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\rho(z)|^{cp}}{F(z,w)^{2(n+1)}}\mathrm{d}v_{n}(z)\mathrm{d}v_{n}(w)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\rho(w)|^{-(n+1-cp)}\mathrm{d}v_{n}(w).

Since
-(n+1-cp)>-n-1+n=-1
, by Lemma
2.15
again, the integral above is finite. Therefore by Lemma
4.3
,
M_{|\rho|^{c}}P\in\mathcal{S}^{p}
. This completes the proof.
∎

For
l\geq 0
, let
G_{l}(z,w)
be as in Lemma
2.9
. For
i=1,\ldots,n
, formally define the integral operator,

G_{i}^{(l)}f(z)=\int_{\Omega}\bar{w}_{i}f(w)G_{l}(z,w)|\rho(w)|^{l}\mathrm{d}v_{n}(w).

(4.2)

Lemma 4.5
.

For
l\geq 0,i=1,\ldots,n
,
G_{i}^{(l)}
extends to a bounded operator from
L^{2}(\Omega)
to
L_{a}^{2}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

By Definition
2.7
and (
2.11
),

\big|\bar{w}_{i}G_{l}(z,w)\big||\rho(w)|^{l}\lesssim\frac{|\rho(w)|^{l}}{F(z,w)^{n+1+l}}\leq\frac{1}{F(z,w)^{n+1}}.

By Lemma
4.2
,
G_{i}^{(l)}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Since
G_{l}(z,w)
is holomorphic in
z
, for every
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, its image
G_{i}^{(l)}f
is a holomorphic function. Therefore
G_{i}^{(l)}
has range in
L_{a}^{2}(\Omega)
. This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Denote
[h]
the principal submodule of
L_{a}^{2}(\Omega)
generated by
h
.

[h]=\overline{\{hf:f\in L_{a}^{2}(\Omega)\}}\subset L^{2}(\Omega).

In this proof we consider
[h]
as a Hilbert subspace of
L^{2}(\Omega)
. All operators on
[h]
can be identified with its extension to
L^{2}(\Omega)
that take zero on
L^{2}(\Omega)\ominus[h]
.
Denote
P_{h}
the orthogonal projection from
L^{2}(\Omega)
onto
[h]
. For
i=1,\ldots,n
, the module action on
[h]
is defined by the operators

R_{i}=P_{h}M_{z_{i}}P_{h},\quad i=1,2,\ldots,n,

where
M_{z_{i}}
is the multiplication operator on
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Since

\displaystyle[R_{i},R_{j}^{*}]=

\displaystyle P_{h}M_{z_{i}}P_{h}M_{\bar{z}_{j}}P_{h}-P_{h}M_{\bar{z}_{j}}P_{h}M_{z_{i}}P_{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{h}M_{\bar{z}_{j}}(I-P_{h})M_{z_{i}}P_{h}-P_{h}M_{z_{i}}(I-P_{h})M_{\bar{z}_{j}}P_{h}

(4.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\bigg[(I-P_{h})M_{\bar{z}_{i}}P_{h}\bigg]^{*}\bigg[(I-P_{h})M_{\bar{z}_{j}}P_{h}\bigg],

it suffices to prove that for any
i=1,2,\ldots,n
,

(I-P_{h})M_{\bar{z}_{i}}P_{h}\in\mathcal{S}^{p},\quad\forall p>2n.

(4.4)

Let
N>0
be as in Theorem
3.1
. Take a positive integer
l>N+1
. For
i=1,\ldots,n
, let
G_{i}^{(l)}
be as in (
4.2
). Define

X_{i}(hf)=M_{\bar{z}_{i}}(hf)-hG_{i}^{(l)}(f),\quad\forall f\in L_{a}^{2}(\Omega).

(4.5)

By Lemma
4.5
,
G_{i}^{(l)}(f)\in L_{a}^{2}(\Omega)
. So
hG_{i}^{(l)}(f)\in[h]
. Therefore

(I-P_{h})M_{\bar{z}_{i}}(hf)=(I-P_{h})\bigg(M_{\bar{z}_{i}}(hf)-hG_{i}^{(l)}(f)\bigg)=(I-P_{h})X_{i}(hf).

(4.6)

For any
f\in L_{a}^{2}(\Omega),z\in\Omega
, since
f\in L_{a}^{2}(\Omega)\subset L_{a,l}^{2}(\Omega)
,

\displaystyle X_{i}(hf)(z)=

\displaystyle\bar{z}_{i}h(z)\int_{\Omega}f(w)G_{l}(z,w)|\rho(w)|^{l}\mathrm{d}v_{n}(w)-h(z)\int_{\Omega}\bar{w}_{i}f(w)G_{l}(z,w)|\rho(w)|^{l}\mathrm{d}v_{n}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\overline{(z_{i}-w_{i})}h(z)f(w)G_{l}(z,w)|\rho(w)|^{l}\mathrm{d}v_{n}(w).

By Definition
2.7
,

|z-w|\leq F(z,w)^{1/2},\quad\forall z,w\in\Omega.

By (
2.11
),

|G_{l}(z,w)|\lesssim F(z,w)^{-n-1-l},\quad\forall z,w\in\Omega.

So by Theorem
3.1
and the above,

\displaystyle\big|X_{i}(hf)(z)\big|\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|z-w|~|h(z)f(w)|~|G_{l}(z,w)|~|\rho(w)|^{l}\mathrm{d}v_{n}(w)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|h(z)f(w)|\frac{|\rho(w)|^{l}}{F(z,w)^{n+1/2+l}}\mathrm{d}v_{n}(w)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\bigg(\frac{F(z,w)^{N}}{|\rho(w)|^{N+n+1}}\int_{E(w,1)}|h(\lambda)f(\lambda)|\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\lambda)\bigg)\frac{|\rho(w)|^{l}}{F(z,w)^{n+1/2+l}}\mathrm{d}v_{n}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\int_{E(w,1)}|h(\lambda)f(\lambda)|\frac{|\rho(w)|^{l-N-(n+1)}}{F(z,w)^{n+1/2+(l-N)}}\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\lambda)\mathrm{d}v_{n}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\bigg(\int_{E(\lambda,1)}\frac{|\rho(w)|^{l-N-(n+1)}}{F(z,w)^{n+1/2+(l-N)}}\mathrm{d}v_{n}(w)\bigg)|h(\lambda)f(\lambda)|\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\lambda).

By Lemma
2.6
, Lemma
2.12
and Lemma
2.13
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{E(\lambda,1)}\frac{|\rho(w)|^{l-N-(n+1)}}{F(z,w)^{n+1/2+(l-N)}}\mathrm{d}v_{n}(w)\approx\int_{E(\lambda,1)}\frac{|\rho(\lambda)|^{l-N-(n+1)}}{F(z,\lambda)^{n+1/2+(l-N)}}\mathrm{d}v_{n}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|\rho(\lambda)|^{l-N-(n+1)}}{F(z,\lambda)^{n+1/2+(l-N)}}|E(\lambda,1)|\approx\frac{|\rho(\lambda)|^{l-N}}{F(z,\lambda)^{n+1/2+(l-N)}}.

Combining the above shows that

|X_{i}(hf)(z)|\lesssim\int_{\Omega}|h(\lambda)f(\lambda)|\frac{|\rho(\lambda)|^{a}}{F(z,\lambda)^{n+\frac{1}{2}+a}}\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\lambda),

(4.7)

where
a=l-N>1
. Define
Y_{i}
on the linear subspace
L=\{|\rho|^{1/2}hf:f\in L_{a}^{2}(\Omega)\}\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
,

Y_{i}(|\rho|^{1/2}hf)=X_{i}(hf).

Then by (
4.7
), for
f\in L_{a}^{2}(\Omega)
,

\displaystyle|Y_{i}(|\rho|^{1/2}hf)(z)|\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\big||\rho(\lambda)|^{1/2}h(\lambda)f(\lambda)\big|\frac{|\rho(\lambda)|^{a-1/2}}{F(z,\lambda)^{n+\frac{1}{2}+a}}\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\big|\big(|\rho|^{1/2}hf\big)(\lambda)\big|\frac{1}{F(z,\lambda)^{n+1}}\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\lambda).

By Lemma
4.2
,
Y_{i}
extends to a bounded operator on
L^{2}(\Omega)
. By (
4.6
),

(I-P_{h})M_{\bar{z}_{i}}P_{h}(hf)=(I-P_{h})X_{i}(hf)=(I-P_{h})Y_{i}M_{|\rho|^{1/2}}PP_{h}(hf).

Since
\{hf:f\in L_{a}^{2}(\Omega)\}
is dense in
[h]
, we have

(I-P_{h})M_{\bar{z}_{i}}P_{h}=(I-P_{h})Y_{i}M_{|\rho|^{1/2}}PP_{h}.

By Lemma
4.4
,
M_{|\rho|^{1/2}}P\in\mathcal{S}^{p},\forall p>2n
. So
(I-P_{h})M_{\bar{z}_{i}}P_{h}\in\mathcal{S}^{p},\forall p>2n
, for any
i=1,\ldots,n
. By (
4
),

[R_{i},R_{j}^{*}]\in\mathcal{S}^{p},\quad\forall p>n.

This completes the proof.
∎

5
The Geometric Arveson-Douglas Conjecture

Suppose
V
is a complex variety in
\Omega
. Define

\mathcal{P}_{V}=\{f\in L_{a}^{2}(\Omega):f|_{V}=0\}.

Since evaluations are bounded on
L_{a}^{2}(\Omega)
,
\mathcal{P}_{V}
is a closed subspace of
L_{a}^{2}(\Omega)
. Clearly
\mathcal{P}_{V}
is closed under each
M_{z_{i}}
. So
\mathcal{P}_{V}
is a submodule of
L_{a}^{2}(\Omega)
.

In
[
20
]
, Kennedy and Shalit introduced a geometric version of the Arveson-Douglas Conjecture, which states that if
\Omega
is
\mathbb{B}_{n}
and
V
is homogeneous, then the quotient module
\mathcal{Q}_{V}:=\mathcal{P}_{V}^{\perp}
is
p
-essentially normal for any
p>\dim_{\mathbb{C}}V
. It quickly drew attentions of many researchers, see for example,
[
6
]
and
[
9
]
. This geometric conjecture naturally extends to the setting of strongly pseudoconvex domains. As a byproduct of our Theorem
4.1
we give some result on the Geometric Arveson-Douglas Conjecture.

Let us begin with a few definitions. See
[
4
]
for more details.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a complex manifold. A set
A\subseteq\Omega
is called a
(complex) analytic subset
of
\Omega
if for each point
a\in\Omega
there are a neighborhood
U
of
a
and functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{N}
holomorphic in this neighborhood such that

A\cap U=\{z\in U:~f_{1}(z)=\cdots=f_{N}(z)=0\}.

In particular,
A
is said to be
principal
if there is a holomorphic function
f
on
\Omega
, not identically vanishing on any component of
\Omega
, such that
A=Z(f):=\{z\in\Omega:~f(z)=0\}
. The function
f
is called a defining function of
A
(not to be confused with the defining function of a pseudoconvex domain).

For an analytic subset
A
of
\Omega
, a point
a\in A
is called a
regular point
if there is a neighborhood
U
of
a
in
\Omega
such that
A\cap U
is a complex submanifold of
\Omega
. Otherwise
a
is called a
singular point
. The complex dimension of
A
at a regular point
z
, denoted by
\dim_{z}A
, is simply the dimension of the complex manifold
A\cap U
, where
U
is a sufficiently small neighborhood of
z
. The set of regular points is dense in
A
(
[
4
]
) and this leads to a definition of dimension at any point.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
A
be an analytic subset of
\Omega
. The
dimension
of
A
at an arbitrary point
a\in A
is the number

\dim_{a}A:=\limsup_{z\to a,z\text{ regular}}\dim_{z}A.

The dimension of
A
is, by definition, the maximum of its dimensions at all points:

\dim A:=\max_{z\in A}\dim_{z}A.

A
is said to be
pure
if its dimensions at all points coincide.

Pure analytic subsets of codimension
1
has some very important properties.

Lemma 5.3
.

[
4
, Corollary 1, Page 26]

Every pure
(n-1)
dimensional analytic subset on an
n
-dimensional complex manifold is locally principal, i.e., for any
a\in A
there exist open neighborhood
U
of
a
in
\Omega
and holomorphic function
f
on
U
such that
A\cap U=\{z\in U:~f(z)=0\}
.

Let
A
be a principal analytic subset of
\Omega
, i.e.,
A=\{z\in\Omega:~f(z)=0\}
for a ceratin holomorphic function
f
. The function
f
is called a
minimal defining function
of
A
if for every open set
U\subseteq\Omega
and every
g\in\mathrm{Hol}(U)
such that
g|_{A\cap U}=0
, there exists an
h\in\mathrm{Hol}(U)
such that
g=fh
in
U
.

Lemma 5.4
.

[
4
, Proposition 1, Page 27]

Every pure
(n-1)
-dimensional analytic subset on an
n
-dimensional complex manifold locally has a minimal defining function.

Now suppose
\Omega
is a bounded strongly pseudoconvex domain with smooth boundary,
\rho
is a defining function and
V
is a pure
(n-1)
-dimensional analytic subset of an open neighborhood of
\overline{\Omega}
. For
\epsilon>0
, denote

\Omega^{\epsilon}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\rho(z)<\epsilon\}.

Choose
\epsilon
sufficiently small so that
\Omega^{\epsilon}
is also a bounded strongly pseudoconvex domain with smooth boundary, and
V
is an analytic subset of
\Omega^{\epsilon}
. Since the gradient of
\rho
is non-vanishing on
\partial\Omega
, we can take
\epsilon
small enough so that
\overline{\Omega^{\epsilon/2}}
is a compact subset of
\Omega^{\epsilon}
. By Lemma
5.4
, there is a finite open cover
\{U_{i}\}
of
\overline{\Omega^{\epsilon/2}}\subset\Omega^{\epsilon}
and a minimal defining function
h_{i}
on each
U_{i}
. By definition, if
U_{i}\cap U_{j}\neq\emptyset
, the function
g_{ij}=h_{i}/h_{j}
is holomorphic and non-vanishing on
U_{i}\cap U_{j}
. They satisfy

g_{ij}\cdot g_{ji}=1\text{ on }U_{i}\cap U_{j},

and

g_{ij}\cdot g_{jk}\cdot g_{ki}=1\text{ on }U_{i}\cap U_{j}\cap U_{k}.

Such a set of functions is called a second Cousin data. The second Cousin problem asks for non-vanishing holomorphic functions
\{g_{i}:U_{i}\to\mathbb{C}\}
, such that
g_{ij}=g_{i}/g_{j}
on each
U_{i}\cap U_{j}
. In the famous paper
[
14
]
, Oka proved that the second Cousin problem on a domain of holomorphy can be solved by holomorphic functions if it can be solved by continuous functions. More generally, the following holds.

Lemma 5.5

(
[
21
]
Corollary 6.5.15)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a domain. If
H^{1}(\Omega,\mathscr{O})=H^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{Z})=0
then the second Cousin problem is always solvable on
\Omega
.

If
\Omega
is a bounded strongly pseudoconvex domain with smooth boundary then
H^{1}(\Omega,\mathscr{O})=0
. Therefore the second Cousin problem is solvable on a bounded strongly pseudoconvex domain
\Omega
with smooth boundary satisfying the topological condition
H^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{Z})=0
.

Lemma 5.6
.

Suppose
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a bounded strongly pseudoconvex domain with smooth boundary and
H^{2}(\Omega^{\epsilon},\mathbb{Z})=0
for sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
. Suppose
V
is a complex analytic subset of an open neighborhood of
\overline{\Omega}
of pure dimension
n-1
. Then there is a holomorphic function
h
defined in an open neighborhood of
\overline{\Omega}
such that

\mathcal{P}_{V}=\{hf:f\in\mathrm{Hol}(\Omega),hf\in L_{a}^{2}(\Omega)\}.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.5
and the analysis above it, there exist non-vanishing holomorphic functions
g_{i}\in\mathrm{Hol}(U_{i}\cap\Omega^{\epsilon})
such that
g_{ij}=g_{i}/g_{j}
. Thus
h_{i}/h_{j}=g_{i}/g_{j},\forall i,j.
If we define
h=h_{i}/g_{i}
on
U_{i}\cap\Omega^{\epsilon}
, then
h
is well defined and becomes a global minimal defining function for
V
in
\Omega^{\epsilon/2}
.
Suppose
f\in\mathcal{P}_{V}
. Then
f=gh
for some
g\in\mathrm{Hol}(\Omega)
. Thus

\mathcal{P}_{V}\subset\{gh:g\in\mathrm{Hol}(\Omega),gh\in L_{a}^{2}(\Omega)\}.

The other side of inclusion is trivial by the definition of
\mathcal{P}_{V}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Let
h
be as in Lemma
5.6
. Then clearly

[h]\subset\mathcal{P}_{V}=\{hf:f\in\mathrm{Hol}(\Omega),hf\in L_{a}^{2}(\Omega)\}.

If one can show that
[h]=\mathcal{P}_{V}
, then Theorem
4.1
actually implies the
p
-essential normality of
\mathcal{P}_{V}
(and hence
\mathcal{P}_{V}^{\perp}
) for
p>n
. In the following subsection, we show that this is indeed the case when
\Omega=\mathbb{B}_{n}
.

5.1
Characterization of
[h]
on the Unit Ball

As explained above, the main goal of this subsection is to prove the following.

Theorem 5.7
.

Suppose
V
is a pure
(n-1)
-dimensional analytic subset of an open neighborhood of
\overline{\mathbb{B}_{n}}
, then
V
has a minimal defining function
h
on an open neighborhood of
\overline{\mathbb{B}_{n}}
. Moreover,

[h]=\mathcal{P}_{V}=\{hf:f\in\mathrm{Hol}(\mathbb{B}_{n}),hf\in L_{a}^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n})\}.

(5.1)

In particular, the submodule
\mathcal{P}_{V}
and the quotient module
\mathcal{P}_{V}^{\perp}
are
p
-essentially normal for all
p>n
.

In the case
\Omega=\mathbb{B}_{n}
, one can take the defining function
\rho(z)=|z|^{2}-1
. Moreover,

F(z,w)\approx|1-\langle z,w\rangle|,\quad\forall z,w\in\mathbb{B}_{n}.

The Kobayashi metric on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
is exactly the Bergman metric, given by

\beta(z,w)=\tanh^{-1}|\varphi_{z}(w)|,

where
\varphi_{z}(w)
is the Möbius transform, defined as in (
3.15
).

By the above, when
\Omega=\mathbb{B}_{n}
, Theorem
3.1
translates into the following.

Theorem 5.8
.

Suppose
h
is a holomorphic function defined on a neighborhood of the closed unit ball
\overline{\mathbb{B}_{n}}
. Then there exists a constant
N
such that for any function
f\in\mathrm{Hol}(\mathbb{B}_{n})
and any
z,w\in\mathbb{B}_{n}
,

|h(z)f(w)|\lesssim\frac{|1-\langle w,z\rangle|^{N}}{(1-|w|^{2})^{N+n+1}}\int_{E(w,1)}|h(\lambda)f(\lambda)|dv_{n}(\lambda),

where
E(w,1)
is the unit ball centered at
w
with radius
1
in the Bergman metric.

Also recall inequality (
3.16
) in the proof of Theorem
3.1
. For
\Omega=\mathbb{B}_{n}
, it translates into

|f(w)|\lesssim\frac{1}{(1-|w|^{2})^{N+n+1}}\int_{E(w,1)}|f(\lambda)h(\lambda)|\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\lambda),\quad\forall w\in\mathbb{B}_{n}.

(5.2)

Definition 5.9
.

Let
h
be a holomorphic function in an open neighborhood of
\overline{\mathbb{B}_{n}}
. Take the measure
d\mu_{h}=|h|^{2}dv_{n}
. Let
L^{2}(\mu_{h})
be the space of functions that are square integrable under this measure. Let
L_{a}^{2}(\mu_{h})
be the weighted Bergman space consisting of holomorphic functions in
L^{2}(\mu_{h})
, that is,

L_{a}^{2}(\mu_{h})=\big\{f\in\mathrm{Hol}(\mathbb{B}_{n}):\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}|f|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu_{h}<\infty\big\}.

Lemma 5.10
.

The weighted Bergman space
L_{a}^{2}(\mu_{h})
is a complete reproducing kernel Hilbert space.

Proof.

By (
5.2
), for any
f\in L_{a}^{2}(\mu_{h})
and
w\in\mathbb{B}_{n}
,

\displaystyle|f(w)|\lesssim

\displaystyle(1-|w|^{2})^{-(N+n+1)}\int_{E(w,1)}|fh|\mathrm{d}v_{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-|w|^{2})^{-(N+n+1)}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}|fh|\mathrm{d}v_{n}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle(1-|w|^{2})^{-(N+n+1)}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}|fh|^{2}\mathrm{d}v_{n}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-|w|^{2})^{-(N+n+1)}\|f\|_{L_{a}^{2}(\mu_{h})}.

Thus the evaluations functionals are bounded on
L_{a}^{2}(\mu_{h})
. Given a compact subset
K\subset\mathbb{B}_{n}
, the above also shows that the evaluation functionals at points in
K
are uniformly bounded.

Next we show that
L_{a}^{2}(\mu_{h})
is complete, or equivalently,
L_{a}^{2}(\mu_{h})
is closed in
L^{2}(\mu_{h})
. Suppose
\{f_{n}\}\subseteq L_{a}^{2}(\mu_{h})
and
f_{n}
converge to
f\in L^{2}(\mu_{h})
. By the above,
f_{n}
converge to
f
uniformly on any compact subset of
\mathbb{B}_{n}
.
This shows that
f
is holomorphic. Therefore
f\in L_{a}^{2}(\mu_{h})
. So
L_{a}^{2}(\mu_{h})
is complete. This completes the proof.
∎

For
f\in\mathrm{Hol}(\mathbb{B}_{n})
and
0<r<1
, write

f_{r}(z)=f(rz),\forall z\in\mathbb{C}^{n},|z|<\frac{1}{r}.

Lemma 5.11
.

Suppose
h
is a holomorphic function defined in an open neighborhood of
\overline{\mathbb{B}_{n}}
. Then there exists
C>0
such that for any
f\in L_{a}^{2}(\mu_{h})
and
\frac{1}{2}<r<1
,

\|f_{r}\|_{L_{a}^{2}(\mu_{h})}\leq C\|f\|_{L_{a}^{2}(\mu_{h})}.

As a consequence, the set of holomorphic functions defined in a neighborhood of
\overline{\mathbb{B}_{n}}
is dense in
L_{a}^{2}(\mu_{h})
.

Proof.

Applying Theorem
5.8
for
w=rz
, we get

\displaystyle|h(z)f(rz)|\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{(1-r|z|^{2})^{N}}{(1-r^{2}|z|^{2})^{N+n+1}}\int_{E(rz,1)}|h(\lambda)f(\lambda)|\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-r^{2}|z|^{2})^{n+1}}\int_{E(rz,1)}|h(\lambda)f(\lambda)|\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\lambda).

Therefore

\displaystyle\|f_{r}\|^{2}_{L_{a}^{2}(\mu_{h})}=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}|h(z)f_{r}(z)|^{2}\mathrm{d}v_{n}(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\frac{1}{(1-r^{2}|z|^{2})^{2(n+1)}}\bigg|\int_{E(rz,1)}|h(\lambda)f(\lambda)|\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\lambda)\bigg|^{2}\mathrm{d}v_{n}(z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\bigg(\frac{1}{(1-r^{2}|z|^{2})^{2(n+1)}}v_{n}(E(rz,1))\cdot\int_{E(rz,1)}|h(\lambda)f(\lambda)|^{2}\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\lambda)\bigg)\mathrm{d}v_{n}(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\bigg(\frac{1}{(1-r^{2}|z|^{2})^{n+1}}\int_{E(rz,1)}|h(\lambda)f(\lambda)|^{2}\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\lambda)\bigg)\mathrm{d}v_{n}(z).

By Fubini’s Theorem, the last integral is equal to

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\bigg(\int_{\{z:~rz\in E(\lambda,1)\}}\frac{1}{(1-r^{2}|z|^{2})^{n+1}}\mathrm{d}v_{n}(z)\bigg)|h(\lambda)f(\lambda)|^{2}\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\bigg(\int_{E(\lambda,1)}\frac{1}{(1-|\eta|^{2})^{n+1}}\frac{1}{r^{2n}}\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\eta)\bigg)|h(\lambda)f(\lambda)|^{2}\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}|h(\lambda)f(\lambda)|^{2}\mathrm{d}v_{n}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|f\|^{2}_{L_{a}^{2}(\mu_{h})}.

Here we used the fact that
v_{n}(E(w,1))\approx(1-|w|^{2})^{n+1}
, and
1-|\eta|^{2}\approx 1-|\lambda|^{2}
whenever
\eta\in E(\lambda,1)
(cf.
[
27
]
).

We have proved the inequality. It remains to show that functions defined in a neighborhood of
\overline{\mathbb{B}_{n}}
are dense. For any
f\in L_{a}^{2}(\mu_{h})
, let
f_{m}:=f_{1-\frac{1}{m+1}}
. Then the sequence of functions
\{f_{m}\}
are defined in a neighborhood of
\overline{\mathbb{B}_{n}}
. By the previous argument, this is a bounded sequence in
L_{a}^{2}(\mu_{h})
. Therefore there exists a subsequence that converges weakly. Since
f_{m}\to f
pointwise, the weak limit must be
f
. Thus
f
lies in the weak closure of the subspace of function defined in a neighborhood of
\overline{\mathbb{B}_{n}}
. By the Hahn-Banach Theorem,
f
also belongs to the norm closure. This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.7
.

Let
V
be as in Theorem
5.7
. Since
\mathbb{B}_{n}
is contractible, clearly
H^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathbb{Z})=0
. Thus by Lemma
5.6
, there is a holomorphic function
h
in an open neighborhood of
\overline{\mathbb{B}_{n}}
such that

\mathcal{P}_{V}=\big\{hf:f\in\mathrm{Hol}(\mathbb{B}_{n}),hf\in L_{a}^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n})\big\}=\big\{hf:f\in L_{a}^{2}(\mu_{h})\big\}.

(5.3)

The last equality follows easily from Definition
5.9
.
Define the operator

\mathcal{I}:L_{a}^{2}(\mu_{h})\to L_{a}^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n}),\quad f\mapsto fh.

Then
\mathcal{I}
is an isometric isomorphism, and by the above,
\mathrm{Ran}(\mathcal{I})=\mathcal{P}_{V}
. By Lemma
5.11
, the subspace
\mathrm{Hol}(\overline{\mathbb{B}_{n}})
consisting of
functions that are holomorphic in a neighborhood of
\overline{\mathbb{B}_{n}}
are dense in
L_{a}^{2}(\mu_{h})
. Therefore

\mathcal{P}_{V}=\mathrm{Ran}(\mathcal{I})=\mathcal{I}\big(L_{a}^{2}(\mu_{h})\big)=\mathcal{I}\bigg(\overline{\mathrm{Hol}(\overline{\mathbb{B}_{n}})}\bigg)=\overline{\mathcal{I}(\mathrm{Hol}(\overline{\mathbb{B}_{n}}))}=[h].

(5.4)

Combining (
5.3
) and (
5.4
) gives (
5.1
). Finally, by Theorem
4.1
, the submodule
\mathcal{P}_{V}=[h]
is
p
-essentially normal for all
p>n
. It is well-known that for
p>n
, the
p
-essential normality of a submodule in
L_{a}^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n})
is equivalent to the corresponding quotient module. Thus
\mathcal{P}_{V}^{\perp}=L_{a}^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n})\ominus\mathcal{P}_{V}
is also
p
-essentially normal for
p>n
.
This completes the proof.
∎

We end this paper with several remarks.

Remark 5.12
.

In Lemma
5.6
, we showed that if
\Omega
satisfies the topological condition
H^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{Z})=0
then for a pure codimension
1
variety
V
in a neighborhood of
\overline{\Omega}
,

\mathcal{P}_{V}=\{hf:f\in\mathrm{Hol}(\Omega),hf\in L_{a}^{2}(\Omega)\}

for some generating function
h
. The gap between
\mathcal{P}_{V}
and
[h]
is then filled for
\Omega=\mathbb{B}_{n}
. The technical difficulty of extending this result to any bounded strongly pseudoconvex domain
\Omega
with smooth boundary, satisfying
H^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{Z})=0
, lies in Lemma
5.11
. We believe that this difficulty can be overcome by a detailed estimate (perhaps using the Narasimhan lemma
[
21
]
).

Remark 5.13
.

Proving equalities like
\mathcal{P}_{V}=[h]
in Theorem
5.7
allows us to study the Geometric Arveson-Douglas Conjecture by studying submodules. There is, however, a drawback in this approach: for
n\geq p>\dim_{\mathbb{C}}V
, the
p
-essential normality of a quotient module is no longer equivalent to that of its corresponding submodule. In fact a general submodule is not expected to be
p
-essentially normal for
p\leq n
. Nonetheless, the essential normality (the cross commutator being compact) of a quotient module in
L_{a}^{2}(\Omega)
has already some important applications. For example, by the BDF-theory, an essentially normal quotient module
\mathcal{Q}
defines an element in a K-homology
K_{1}(X)
, where
X
a topology space depending on
\mathcal{Q}
. For nice varieties
V
, the
X
space for
\mathcal{Q}_{V}=\mathcal{P}_{V}^{\perp}
is
V\cap\partial\Omega
. As observed by the first author in
[
5
]
, this gives a new kind of index theory.

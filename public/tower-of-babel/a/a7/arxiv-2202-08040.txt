1-jettiness with jet axis at O ⁡ ( α s ) O(\alpha_{s}) in deep inelastic scattering
Zexuan Chu, Yunlu Wang, June-Haak Ee, Jinhui Chen, Daekyoung Kang

Abstract
We present O ⁡ ( α s ) O(\alpha_{s}) analytic predictions for event shape 1-jettiness τ 1 \tau_{1} distribution aiming measurements in deep inelastic scattering process at future Electron Ion Colliders. The result depends on conventional variables x x and Q Q as well as on τ 1 \tau_{1} and is relatively compact and easy to implement for numerical calculation. Three different choices of axis, with respect to which τ 1 \tau_{1} is measured are considered in the Breit frame. The first is the one optimally adjusted to minimize τ 1 \tau_{1} and the second and third are taken from anti- k T k_{T} and Centauro jet algorithms defined with a jet radius parameter R R , respectively. We find that the first and second give the same result at this order and are independent of R R , while the third depends on the radius. This fixed-order result provides a nonsingular contribution to be combined with a singular log-resummed contribution to give the full spectrum in τ 1 \tau_{1} space and also shows how fixed-order and resummation regions change as a function of x x and Q Q .

2.3
Centauro algorithm
\tau^{\text{ct}}

For the third jet axis we would like to adopt the Centauro algorithm

The Centauro algorithm defines the following distance measure

\displaystyle d_{ij}

\displaystyle=\left[(\Delta f_{ij})^{2}+2f_{i}f_{j}(1-\cos\Delta\phi_{ij})\right]/R^{2},\quad d_{iB}=1\,,

\displaystyle=f(\bar{\eta}_{i})\,,\qquad\qquad\bar{\eta}_{i}=2\frac{|\mathbf{p}_{iT}|}{\bar{n}_{z}\!\cdot\!p_{i}}\,,

(18)

where

diverges in forward region as

and thus prevents jets from enclosing the proton beam direction, while it decreases as particles become closer in backward region
{\bar{n}_{z}\!\cdot\!p_{i}}\gg|\mathbf{p}_{iT}|
.
For the function

, we take the simplest form in

(19)

Then,

and

are given by

\displaystyle f_{1}

\displaystyle=2\sqrt{\frac{1-z}{z}}\sqrt{\frac{v}{1-v}}\,,\qquad\qquad f_{2}=2\sqrt{\frac{1-z}{z}}\sqrt{\frac{1-v}{v}}\,,

(20)

\displaystyle d_{12}

\displaystyle=\frac{(f_{1}+f_{2})^{2}}{R^{2}}=\frac{4}{R^{2}}\frac{1-z}{z}\frac{1}{v(1-v)}\,,

(21)

where

is a radius parameter of order

is a

\alpha_{s}
. Details of the calculations are given in App.

\sigma^{\rm c}(\tau,x,Q)=\int_{0}^{\tau}d\tau^{\prime}\frac{d\sigma}{dx\,dQ^{2}\,d\tau^{\prime}}\,.

(27)

The DIS cross section is conventionally decomposed into the structure functions
F_{i}

\displaystyle\sigma^{\rm c}(\tau,x,Q)

\displaystyle=\frac{4\pi\alpha^{2}}{Q^{4}}\left[(1+(1-y)^{2})F_{1}+\frac{1-y}{x}F_{L}\right]\,.

(28)

This can be expressed in terms of

by using the relation between structure functions
F_{2}=F_{L}+2xF_{1}
.
Note that the structure functions are the functions of
\tau
as well as
x
and
Q
.
At the point
\tau=\tau_{\text{max}}
, the cumulative functions
\sigma^{\rm c}
and
F_{i}
reduce to the inclusive cross section and inclusive structure functions, respectively.
The
F_{i}
are coefficients of basis tensors that decompose a current-current correlator called the hadronic tensor
W^{\mu\nu}
defined in Eq. (
A
) and one can read off the individual coefficients by projecting with orthogonal tensors like
P_{\mu}P_{\nu}W^{\mu\nu}
and

as shown in Eq. (

).

The structure functions are written in terms of these projected correlators

\displaystyle F_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=q,\bar{q},g}(A_{i}+B_{i})\,,

(29)

\displaystyle F_{L}

5
Conclusions

In this paper we study the event shape 1-jettiness in DIS at the first order in
\alpha_{s}
that can be
measured in future experiments. We considered three different jet axes, onto which a particle momentum is projected to compute the value of 1-jettiness.
\tau^{\text{jt}}
is a version with the jettiness axis that is optimally adjusted to minimize the value of 1-jettiness. The other two versions are
\tau^{\text{kt}}
and
\tau^{\text{ct}}
that take their axes from exclusive jet algorithms such as anti-
k_{T}
and the Centauro algorithms in the Breit frame, respectively. We find that
\tau^{\text{jt}}
and
\tau^{\text{kt}}
are equivalent for the two-body final state,
\textit{i}.\textit{e}.,
at the order
\alpha_{s}
.

Our main result is the predictions for
\tau^{\text{jt}}
and
\tau^{\text{ct}}
distributions at the first order in
\alpha_{s}
analytically expressed in Eqs. (
32
) and (
34
). They are expressed in the form of the cumulative distribution and in the Appendix the differential distribution is also given. The results are expressed such that one can easily write the structure functions such as
F_{1}(x,Q^{2},\tau)
and
F_{L}(x,Q^{2},\tau)
as well as the cross section. The results of
\tau^{\text{ct}}
share many terms in common with those of
\tau^{\text{jt}}
and their difference depending on
R
is given so that
\tau^{\text{ct}}
is obtained by adding the difference on the top of
\tau^{\text{jt}}
result. Comparison to the analytic result for
\tau^{b}
using Breit frame axis are also given in App.
C
.

Numerical results of our predictions are presented at different values of
x
and
Q
. Singular and nonsingular parts are compared in
\tau^{\text{jt}}
distributions. For
\tau^{\text{ct}}
, we studied the difference from
\tau^{\text{jt}}
and found that it is sensitive to the value of radius
R
and the magnitude is in the range of
5\sim 25\%
of
\tau^{\text{jt}}
distribution when the value of
R
is from
1
to
1.8
. We also studied the singular-nonsingular crossing point
\tau_{\text{cross}}
as a function of
x
and
Q
. The crossing implies a boundary between resummation and fixed-order regions and the value can be used as a reference point for turning off the resummation. The value of
\tau_{\text{cross}}
hence, the resummation region increases with the decreasing value of
x
, and the region gets close to
\tau_{\text{max}}=1/2
at
x=0.01
, while the value is less sensitive to
Q
. We found that the
x
dependence is well explained by the singular part and obtained an approximate expression for
\tau_{\text{cross}}
, which can be used in the absence of nonsingular part.

Our results provide an important piece of information toward precision predictions of
event shapes in DIS. Our prediction for nonsingular part combined with resummed singular
part can be measured in the future EIC and
EicC

and can be used to determine the strong coupling constant and a universal hadronic nonpertubative parameter.

In this section we calculate 1-jettiness QCD cross section.
In derivations of the cross section, we follow the steps done in
Kang:2013nha
.
The cross section is expressed in the product of lepton and hadronic tensors.

\frac{d\sigma}{dx\,dQ^{2}\,d\tau}=L^{II^{\prime}}_{\mu\nu}(x,Q^{2})W^{II^{\prime}\mu\nu}(x,Q^{2},\tau)\,,

(45)

where
L^{II^{\prime}}_{\mu\nu}
is the lepton tensor given in
Kang:2013nha

and the index
I={V,A}
implies vector and axial currents. Although, we consider
II^{\prime}=VV

in this paper but we keep these index to maintain generality for a moment.
For vector currents, we have

L^{VV}_{\mu\nu}(x,Q^{2})=-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2x^{2}s^{2}}g_{\mu\nu}^{T}\,,

(46)

where
-g_{\mu\nu}^{T}=-g_{\mu\nu}+2/Q^{2}(k^{\mu}k^{\prime\nu}+k^{\prime\mu}k^{\nu})
,
with
k^{\mu}
and
k^{\prime\mu}
being the momenta of incoming
and outgoing leptons and the photon momentum is given by

q^{\mu}=k^{\mu}-k^{\prime\mu}

One can show that the lepton tensor is transverse to virtual photon as implied by the Ward identity

q^{\mu}L^{VV}_{\mu\nu}\propto q^{\mu}g_{\mu\nu}^{T}=0
.
The hadronic tensor
W^{\mu\nu}
that measures the 1-jettiness from final hadronic state
X
can be expressed as

\displaystyle W_{II^{\prime}}^{\mu\nu}(x,Q^{2},\tau)

\displaystyle=\sum_{X}\left\langle P\right\rvert J_{I}^{\mu{\dagger}}(x)\left\lvert X\right\rangle\left\langle X\right\rvert J_{I^{\prime}}^{\nu}(x)\left\lvert P\right\rangle(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{4}(P+q-p_{X})\delta(\tau-\tau(X))\,,

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}\int\frac{d\Phi_{n}}{d\tau}\left\langle P\right\rvert J_{I}^{\mu{\dagger}}(x)\left\lvert p_{1},\cdots,p_{n}\right\rangle\left\langle p_{1},\cdots,p_{n}\right\rvert J_{I^{\prime}}^{\nu}(0)\left\lvert P\right\rangle\,,

(47)

where there is an average over incoming spins implicit. In the second line we specify the number of final particles and the corresponding phase-space integral.

The hadronic tensor is decomposed into several tensors

\displaystyle W_{II^{\prime}}^{\mu\nu}(x,Q^{2},\tau)

\displaystyle=(4\pi)\bigg[T^{\mu\nu}_{1}F_{1}(x,Q^{2},\tau)+T^{\mu\nu}_{2}\frac{F_{2}(x,Q^{2},\tau)}{P\cdot q}+T^{\mu\nu}_{3}\frac{F_{3}(x,Q^{2},\tau)}{2P\cdot q}\bigg]\,.

over

\displaystyle T_{1\,\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=-g_{\mu\nu}+\frac{q_{\mu}q_{\nu}}{q^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle T_{2\,\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\bigg(P_{\mu}-q_{\mu}\frac{P\cdot q}{q^{2}}\bigg)\bigg(P_{\nu}-q_{\nu}\frac{P\cdot q}{q^{2}}\bigg)\,,

\displaystyle T_{3\,\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=-i\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}q^{\alpha}P^{\beta}\,.

(49)

Multiplying
g_{\mu\nu}
and
P_{\mu}P_{\nu}
by Eq. (
A

and
F_{2}
.
Solving for
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
, they are expressed in terms of the hadronic tensor.
Similarly,

can be obtained by multiplying

\displaystyle F_{1}(x,Q^{2},\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8\pi(1-\epsilon)}\bigg(-g_{\mu\nu}+\frac{4x^{2}}{Q^{2}}P_{\mu}P_{\nu}\bigg)W^{\mu\nu}_{II^{\prime}}\,,

\displaystyle F_{2}(x,Q^{2},\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{x}{4\pi(1-\epsilon)}\bigg(-g_{\mu\nu}+(3-2\epsilon)\frac{4x^{2}}{Q^{2}}P_{\mu}P_{\nu}\bigg)W^{\mu\nu}_{II^{\prime}}\,,

\displaystyle F_{L}(x,Q^{2},\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{2x^{3}}{\pi Q^{2}}\frac{-q^{2}}{(P\cdot q)^{2}}P_{\mu}P_{\nu}W^{\mu\nu}_{II^{\prime}}=F_{2}-2x\,F_{1}\,,

\displaystyle F_{3}(x,Q^{2},\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{x}{2\pi(1-\epsilon)(1-2\epsilon)}\frac{q^{\alpha}P^{\beta}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}}{Q^{2}}W^{\mu\nu}_{II^{\prime}}\,.

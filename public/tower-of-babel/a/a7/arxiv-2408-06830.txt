A functional central limit theorem for the general Brownian motion on the half-line
Dirk Erhard, Tertuliano Franco, Milton Jara, Eduardo Pimenta

Abstract
In this work, we establish a Trotter-Kato type theorem. More precisely, we characterize the convergence in distribution of Feller processes by examining the convergence of their generators. The main novelty lies in providing quantitative estimates in the vague topology at any fixed time. As important applications, we deduce functional central limit theorems for random walks on the positive integers with boundary conditions, which converge to Brownian motions on the positive half-line with boundary conditions at zero.



Keywords:
Absorbed Brownian motion; Berry-Esseen estimates; elastic Brownian motion; exponential holding Brownian motion; functional central limit; general Brownian motion; killed Brownian motion; mixed Brownian motion; sticky Brownian motion.

1
Introduction

The subject of functional central limit theorems (functional CLTs) originated from the now standard Donsker Theorem and Invariance Principles for Brownian motion. Since then, a substantial body of literature has emerged, focusing on invariance principles for various types of random walks (including those on random media) and their convergence to standard Brownian motion.
However, the convergence to Brownian-type processes, such as the skew Brownian motion, sticky Brownian motion, elastic Brownian motion, and others, has received much less attention up to the present day.

As some rare examples,
Harrison and Lemoine (1981)
showed the convergence of a specific queue to sticky Brownian motion.
Amir (1991)
established the convergence of rescaled discrete-time random walks with deterministic waiting times, also to sticky Brownian motion. More recently,
Erhard et al. (2021)
proved a functional central limit for the slow bond random walk. The limit of this process is given by the
snapping out Brownian motion
, a Brownian-type process created by
Lejay (2016)
. Somewhat related to the subject,
Kosygina et al. (2022)
have shown the convergence of the one-dimensional cookie random walk (a walk that takes a decision based on the local time of the present position) towards what they called a
Brownian motion perturbed at extrema
, which is a stochastic process
W(t)
solving a functional equation relating
W(t)
, its maxima and minima, and a standard Brownian motion.

In this work, we present a criterion to ensure a functional central limit theorem for Feller processes, based on the rate of convergence of their generators. This criterion comes with a corresponding Berry-Esseen type estimate. Convergence in distribution via the convergence of generators is not a new topic. A classical result in the book
Ethier and Kurtz (1986, Theorem 6.1 on page 28)
can be paraphrased as follows: Consider a sequence
(T_{n})
of strongly continuous contraction semigroups, where each
T_{n}
is defined on a Banach space
\text{X}_{n}
and has generator
L_{n}
. Denote by
\pi_{n}
the projection of another Banach space
X
onto
\text{X}_{n}
where a strongly continuous semigroup
T
with generator
L
is defined. Then
T_{n}(t)\pi_{n}f
converges to
T(t)f
for each
t
if and only if for every
f
in a core for
L
there exists
f_{n}
in a core for
L_{n}
such that
f_{n}\to f
and
L_{n}f_{n}\to Lf
. Our main result complements the above result and shows that a rate of convergence in the convergence of the generators implies a rate of convergence of the corresponding semigroups. This in turn will imply a rate of convergence in the vague topology of the law induced by
T_{n}
to the one induced by
T
. Our result therefore establishes a sort of
weak Berry-Esseen estimate
. The primary applications we have in mind are boundary problems, where we show that, in many cases, it is crucial that the function
f_{n}
does not necessarily coincide with
\pi_{n}f
but can be chosen to address issues arising from the boundary conditions. As an interesting application of this general framework, we establish a functional central limit theorem for a wide class of random walks on the positive integers, which converge to the most general Brownian motion on the positive half-line.

The most general Brownian motion on the positive half-line was studied by Feller, and a comprehensive overview on it can be found in
Knight (1981, Theorem 6.2, p. 157)
. To put it simply, it is defined as a class of Feller processes on the positive half line such that its excursion to zero are the same as those of a standard Brownian motion, which can be shown to be a mixture of the reflected Brownian motion, absorbed Brownian motion, and killed Brownian motion. Its generator is given by one half of the continuous Laplacian acting on the domain of
{\mathcal{C}}^{2}
-functions decaying at infinity and satisfying
c_{1}f(0)-c_{2}f^{\prime}(0)+\frac{c_{3}}{2}f^{\prime\prime}(0)=0
with
c_{1}+c_{2}+c_{3}=1
,
c_{i}\geq 0
. Given three non-negative parameters
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}
that sum to one, we also occasionally denote by
B(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3})
the corresponding general Brownian motion.

The discrete class of models considered here involves continuous-time random walks on
(\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{N})\cup\{\Delta\}
, where
\mathbb{N}={0,1,\ldots}
and the state
\Delta
is usually referred to as the
cemetery
. The walk follows the usual symmetric walk on
{1,2,\ldots}
with jump rates to nearest neighbors everywhere equal to
1/2
. However, at state
0
, we introduce the following: the rate to jump to state
1
is
A/n^{\alpha}
, and the rate to jump to the cemetery is
B/n^{\beta}
, where
\alpha,A,\beta,B\geq 0
, and
n
is the scaling parameter. Additionally, if the walk reaches the cemetery, it remains there indefinitely.

The chosen values of parameters
\alpha,A,\beta,B
then determine the limiting Brownian-type process of the random walk.
We show here that for any choice of
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}\geq 0
such that
c_{1}+c_{2}+c_{3}=1
and
c_{1}\neq 1
there are classes of choices of
\alpha,A,\beta,B
such that the above random walk converges to
B(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3})
. We additionally show that by making a small shift to the right of the random walk, we have convergence to the killed Brownian motion, which corresponds to
c_{1}=1
.

Each type of Brownian motion has different properties and behaviors, which makes them useful in different applications. For example, reflected Brownian motion can model a financial asset that cannot have negative values, while absorbed Brownian motion can model the extinction of a biological population. Elastic Brownian motion can model the behavior of a particle that is attracted to
0
but has long-range repulsion from some boundary point and finally, the sticky Brownian motion can model a particle that sticks to a point, the killed Brownian motion corresponds to a continuous walk that jumps directly to the cemetery when it gets arbitrarily close to the origin. All these processes are particular cases of the above-mentioned general Brownian motion on the half-line, which illustrates the importance of the functional central limit theorem we prove in this paper. Moreover, the speed of convergence here obtained can come in handy when simulating those processes. Indeed, the random walks considered can be easily simulated as we will show in Section
4
.

The outline of the paper is the following: in Section
2
we state results and mention related literature. Proofs are given in Section
3
. Finally, Section
4
is dedicated to computational simulations of the simple random walk with boundary conditions which, by the presented results, are good approximations of the general BM on the half-line.

|{\mathsf{L}}e^{-x^{2}}|\;\lesssim\;(1+x^{2})e^{-x^{2}}\quad\text{ and }\quad|{\mathsf{L}}^{2}e^{-x^{2}}|\;\lesssim\;(1+x^{4})e^{-x^{2}}\,.

(13)

Now, since
b_{j}\equiv 0
outside the compact set
[-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{j},\frac{\sqrt{2}}{j}]
and
{\mathsf{P}}
is a polynomial, for all
i\geq 1
one has that
\lVert\frac{d^{(i)}}{dx^{(i)}}(b_{j}{\mathsf{P}})\rVert\lesssim j^{i}
.
Hence, using equation (
13
), the product rule and the bound above, we can obtain upper bounds for the generator
{\mathsf{L}}
and
{\mathsf{L}}^{2}
norm

\lVert{\mathsf{L}}f_{0,j}\rVert\lesssim\lVert\tilde{f}_{0}\rVert+\lVert\tilde{f}_{2}\rVert+j^{2}

and

\lVert{\mathsf{L}}^{2}f_{0,j}\rVert\lesssim\lVert\tilde{f}_{0}\rVert+\lVert\tilde{f}_{4}\rVert+j^{4}
.
Hence, we ensured that conditions
(G2)
and
(G3)
are met.

It is missing to verify
(G4)
, which will rely on some knowledge about the semigroup of the limiting process.

The limiting processes mentioned in Theorem
2.11
are the reflected, absorbed, mixed, sticky, elastic, exponential holding and killed Brownian motion. Except the sticky BM and the exponential holding BM, all corresponding semigroup’s formulas can be found in
Borodin and Salminen (2002, Appendix 1)
. However, the semigroup for the sticky BM on
[0,\infty)
can be deduced by symmetry from the formula for the sticky BM on
{\mathbb{R}}

(
Borodin and Salminen, 2002
, page 123)
. We could not find the semigroup for the exponential holding BM in the literature, so we provide below its transition probability:

\begin{cases}p_{t}(\Delta,\{\Delta\})=1&\\
p_{t}(0,\{\Delta\})=1-e^{-\lambda t}&\\
p_{t}(0,\{0\})=e^{-\lambda t}&\\
p_{t}(x,A)=p_{t}^{\text{absorbed BM}}(x,A)&\forall\,A\not\ni 0,\Delta\quad\forall\,x>0\\
p_{t}(x,\{\Delta\})=\int_{0}^{t}f_{\tau_{0}}(s,x)\big(1-e^{-\lambda(t-s)}\big)ds&\forall\,x>0\\
p_{t}(x,\{0\})=\int_{0}^{t}f_{\tau_{0}}(s,x)e^{-\lambda(t-s)}ds&\forall\,x>0\\
\end{cases}

for all
t\geq 0
, where
p_{t}^{\text{absorbed BM}}
is the transition probability function of the absorbed BM and

f_{\tau_{0}}(t,x)\;=\;\frac{x}{\sqrt{2\pi t^{3}}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2t}}\mathds{1}_{[0,\infty)}(t)

is the density of the hitting time of zero for the absorbed BM.
In the supplementary material of this paper, see
Erhard et al. (2026b)
, we provide details on the claim that
(G4)
holds for all semigroups dealt with in this paper.

Remark 3.3
.

Hypothesis
(G4)
has been used just once, in the proof of Theorem
2.7
, and its importance relies on the fact that the random walk and its limiting process may not have the same starting point. For instance, in the setup of Theorem
2.11
, the boundary random walk starts from
\lfloor un\rfloor/n
, whereas its Brownian counterpart starts from
u>0
. If we assume that the discrete process and the limiting process start from the same point
u\in{\mathsf{S}}
, hypothesis
(G4)
can be dropped from Theorem
2.7
. This is possible in the setup of Theorem
2.11
, for instance, if we assume that the scaling parameter is given by
n=n(k)=2^{k}
and the initial point
u
is a positive integer.

We therefore must verify the remaining assumptions.
Those involve the correction operator
\Xi_{n}
, which is very model dependent. Before we study each model separately, we start with some generalities. We will always assume that
(\Xi_{n}f)(\Delta)=0
, and moreover all functions considered here satisfy
f(\Delta)=0
and they have continuous derivatives up to order four. Thus,
the generator of the random walk with boundary conditions applied to the function
\Phi_{n}f
at zero is given by

{\mathsf{L}}_{n}\Phi_{n}f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{0}{n}\big)=-\frac{A}{n^{\alpha-2}}f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{0}{n}\big)+\frac{B}{n^{\beta-2}}\left[f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{1}{n}\big)-f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{0}{n}\big)\right]-\frac{A}{n^{\alpha-2}}\Xi_{n}f(0)+\frac{B}{n^{\beta-2}}\big[\Xi_{n}f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{1}{n}\big)-\Xi_{n}f(0)\big].

(14)

Outside of zero, a Taylor expansion yields

\displaystyle{\mathsf{L}}_{n}\Phi_{n}f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{x}{n}\big)\;=\;

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime\prime}\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{x}{n}\big)+\frac{1}{2\cdot 4!n^{2}}\left[f^{{}^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}}(\theta)+f^{{}^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}}(\eta)\right]+\frac{n^{2}}{2}\Big[\Xi_{n}f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{x+1}{n}\big)+\Xi_{n}f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{x-1}{n}\big)-2\Xi_{n}f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{x}{n}\big)\Big]

\displaystyle\;=\;

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\pi_{n}f^{\prime\prime}\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{x}{n}\big)+\|f^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}\|\cdot O(n^{-2})+n^{2}{\mathsf{L}}_{n}\Xi_{n}f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{x}{n}\big)\,,

(15)

for some
\theta\in(x/n,(x+1)/n)
and
\xi\in((x-1)/n,x/n)
.

3.2.1
The elastic BM: the case
\beta\in[0,1),\alpha=\beta+1

Recall that in this case we set

c_{1}=\frac{B}{A+B}
,
c_{2}=\frac{A}{A+B}
and
c_{3}=0
.
Denote the generator of the elastic Brownian motion by
{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{EBM}}
, whose domain is given by

{\mathfrak{D}}\left({\mathsf{L}}_{\text{EBM}}\right)=\left\{f\,\in\,{\mathcal{C}}_{0}^{2}({\mathbb{G}}):\frac{A}{A+B}f(0)-\frac{B}{A+B}f^{\prime}(0)\;=\;0\right\}
.
Let
f\in{\mathfrak{D}}({\mathsf{L}}^{2}_{\text{EBM}})
, which implies the boundary conditions
Af(0)=Bf^{\prime}(0)
and
Af^{\prime\prime}(0)=Bf^{\prime\prime\prime}(0)
.
Using this together with
\alpha=1+\beta
in Equation (
14
) and a Taylor expansion, we get that

\displaystyle{\mathsf{L}}_{n}\Phi_{n}f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{0}{n}\big)\;=\;

\displaystyle-\frac{A}{n^{\alpha-2}}f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{0}{n}\big)+\frac{B}{n^{\beta-2}}\left[\frac{f^{\prime}(0)}{n}+\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(0)}{2!n^{2}}+\frac{f^{\prime\prime\prime}(0)}{3!n^{3}}+\frac{f^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}(\eta)}{4!n^{4}}\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{A}{n^{\alpha-2}}\Xi_{n}f(0)+\frac{B}{n^{\beta-2}}\Big[\Xi_{n}f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{1}{n}\big)-\Xi_{n}f(0)\Big]

\displaystyle\;=\;

\displaystyle\frac{B}{n^{\beta}}\left[\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(0)}{2!}+\frac{A}{B}\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(0)}{3!n}+\frac{f^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}(\eta)}{4!n^{2}}\right]

(16)

\displaystyle-\frac{A}{n^{\beta-1}}\Xi_{n}f(0)+\frac{B}{n^{\beta-2}}\Big[\Xi_{n}f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{1}{n}\big)-\Xi_{n}f(0)\Big]

(17)

for some
0\leq\eta\leq 1/n
. Note that for
\beta>0
the parcel (
16
) is vanishing, and it is at this point where the correction operator enters the game.
Assume first that
\beta\neq 0
; we will discuss the situation
\beta=0
at the ending of this subsection.
Define the correction operator in this case by

\Xi_{n}f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{x}{n}\big)\;:=\;-\frac{\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime\prime}(0)}{An^{1-\beta}\left(1+\frac{1}{n}g\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)\right)}\,,

(18)

where
g
is some arbitrary non-negative Lipschitz function of constant
K>0
satisfying
g(0)=0
and
g(u)\to\infty
as
u\to\infty
. Note that the condition on the growth of
g
is only necessary to assure that
\Xi_{n}f
belongs to the domain of
{\mathsf{L}}_{n}
which is given by

{\mathfrak{D}}({\mathsf{L}}_{n})=\big\{f:\{\Delta\}\cup\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{1}{n}{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{R}}\text{ such that }\lim_{\frac{x}{n}\to\infty}f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{x}{n}\big)=0\text{ and }f(\Delta)=0\big\}
.

By Remark
2.1
, in order to verify
(H4)
, we only need to check that
s\mapsto\Xi_{n}T(t-s)f\in{\mathsf{B}}_{n}
has derivative given by
-\Xi_{n}{\mathsf{L}}T_{n}(t-s)f
. This is true because by (
18
) one has that for all
x

\Xi_{n}T_{n}(t-s)f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{x}{n}\big)\;=\;-\frac{\frac{1}{2}(T(t-s)f)^{\prime\prime}(0)}{An^{1-\beta}\left(1+\frac{1}{n}g\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)\right)}\,,

and
\frac{1}{2}(T(t-s)f)^{\prime\prime}(0)={\mathsf{L}}T(t-s)f(0)=T(t-s){\mathsf{L}}f(0)
. Here we are allowed to differentiate since
f\in{\mathfrak{D}}({\mathsf{L}}^{2})
. The same argument holds for all subsequent cases, except for the killed BM, which will be treated separately. Nevertheless, in that specific case we do not apply the Theorem
2.2
, so
(H4)
is not necessary. From now on we do not mention
(H4)
anymore, being understood that it holds whenever it is needed.

We then have that

\displaystyle-\frac{A}{n^{\beta-1}}\Xi_{n}f(0)+\frac{B}{n^{\beta-2}}\Big[\Xi_{n}f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{1}{n}\big)-\Xi_{n}f(0)\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime\prime}(0)+\frac{Bn}{A}\left[\frac{\frac{1}{n}g\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)}{1+\frac{1}{n}g\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)}\right]\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime\prime}(0)

\displaystyle=\;{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{EBM}}f(0)+\frac{BK}{A}\|{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{EBM}}f\|\cdot O(n^{-1})

because
g(0)=0
and
g
is
K
-Lipschitz. Plugging it into (
16
) – (
17
) yields

\left|\left(\pi_{n}{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{EBM}}f-{\mathsf{L}}_{n}\Phi_{n}f\right)(0)\right|\,\lesssim\,\left(\frac{1}{n^{\beta}}+\frac{1}{n}+\frac{1}{n^{1+\beta}}\right)\lVert{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{EBM}}f\rVert+\frac{1}{n^{2+\beta}}\lVert{\mathsf{L}}^{2}_{\text{EBM}}f\rVert\,.

(19)

For
\frac{x}{n}\in{\mathbb{G}}_{n}\backslash\{0\}
, equation (
15
) indicates that we need to estimate

\displaystyle{\mathsf{L}}_{n}\Xi_{n}f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{x}{n}\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{EBM}}f(0)n^{2}}{An^{1-\beta}}\left[\frac{\frac{1}{n}\left(g\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{x+1}{n}\big)-g\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{x}{n}\big)\right)}{\left(1+\frac{1}{n}g\left(\frac{x+1}{n}\right)\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{n}g\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)\right)}+\frac{\frac{1}{n}\left(g\left(\frac{x-1}{n}\right)-g\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)\right)}{\left(1+\frac{1}{n}g\left(\frac{x-1}{n}\right)\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{n}g\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)\right)}\right]

\displaystyle=K\|{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{EBM}}f\|O(n^{\beta-1})\,,

where we used again that
g
is
K
-Lipschitz. Plugging it into (
15
), we conclude that

\left|\left(\pi_{n}{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{EBM}}f-{\mathsf{L}}_{n}\Phi_{n}f\right)\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{x}{n}\big)\right|\;\lesssim\;\frac{1}{n^{1-\beta}}\lVert{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{EBM}}f\rVert+\frac{1}{n^{2}}\lVert{\mathsf{L}}^{2}_{\text{EBM}}f\rVert

(20)

uniformly in
\frac{x}{n}\in{\mathbb{G}}_{n}\backslash\{0\}
.
Putting together (
19
) and (
20
), we infer that

\begin{split}\lVert\pi_{n}{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{EBM}}f-{\mathsf{L}}_{n}\Phi_{n}f\rVert\;\lesssim\;&\max\Big\{\frac{1}{n^{\beta}},\frac{1}{n},\frac{1}{n^{1-\beta}}\Big\}\lVert{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{EBM}}f\rVert+\max\Big\{\frac{1}{n^{2}},\frac{1}{n^{2+\beta}}\Big\}\lVert{\mathsf{L}}^{2}_{\text{EBM}}f\rVert\\
=\;&\max\Big\{\frac{1}{n^{\beta}},\frac{1}{n^{1-\beta}}\Big\}\lVert{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{EBM}}f\rVert+\frac{1}{n^{2}}\lVert{\mathsf{L}}^{2}_{\text{EBM}}f\rVert\,.\end{split}

(21)

In view of (
21
), we have assured hypothesis
(H2)
and it is only missing to check
(H3)
. From (
18
), we immediately get that

\lVert\Xi_{n}f\rVert\lesssim\frac{1}{n^{1-\beta}}\lVert{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{EBM}}f\rVert
,
showing that
(H3)
holds. Hence, Theorem
2.7
yields

{\bf d}(\mu_{n},\mu)\;\lesssim\;\max\left\{\frac{1}{n^{\beta}},\frac{1}{n^{1-\beta}},\frac{1}{n},\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right\}\;=\;\max\left\{\frac{1}{n^{\beta}},\frac{1}{n^{1-\beta}}\right\}

and that
\{X_{n}(t):t\geq 0\}
weakly converges to
\{X^{\text{EBM}}(t):t\geq 0\}
under the Skorohod topology of
{\mathsf{D}}_{\mathbb{G}}[0,\infty)
. We thus can conclude this case.
We come to the case
\beta=0
. In this case we define

\Xi_{n}f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{x}{n}\big)\;:=\;-\frac{(1-B)\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime\prime}(0)}{An\left(1+\frac{1}{n}g\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{x}{n}\big)\right)}\,.

Analogous arguments as above yield that
{\bf d}(\mu_{n},\mu)\lesssim\frac{1}{n}
in this case. We omit the details.

3.2.2
The sticky BM: the case
\beta=1,\alpha\in(2,\infty)

Recall that in this case we set

c_{1}=0
,
c_{2}=\frac{B}{B+1}
and
c_{3}=\frac{1}{B+1}
.
We denote the generator of the sticky BM by
{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{SBM}}
, whose domain is

{\mathfrak{D}}\left({\mathsf{L}}_{\text{SBM}}\right)=\left\{f\,\in\,{\mathcal{C}}_{0}^{2}({\mathbb{G}}):-\frac{B}{B+1}f^{\prime}(0)+\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{B+1}f^{\prime\prime}(0)=0\right\}
.
As we shall see, no correction will be necessary here, being enough to choose
\Xi_{n}\equiv 0
.
Let
f\in{\mathfrak{D}}({\mathsf{L}}^{2}_{\text{SBM}})
, which yields the boundary conditions
Bf^{\prime}(0)=\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime\prime}(0)
and
Bf^{\prime\prime\prime}(0)=\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}(0)
.
Keeping this in mind and also that
\beta=1
and
\alpha\in(2,\infty)
, we obtain from Equation (
14
) that

\displaystyle{\mathsf{L}}_{n}\Phi_{n}f(0)

\displaystyle\;=\;-\frac{A}{n^{\alpha-2}}f(0)+Bf^{\prime}(0)\,+\,\frac{B}{2n}f^{\prime\prime}(0)+\frac{B}{3!n^{2}}f^{\prime\prime\prime}(0)+\frac{B}{4!n^{3}}f^{{}^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}}(\eta)

\displaystyle\;=\;-\frac{A}{n^{\alpha-2}}f(0)+\Big(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{B}{2n}\Big)f^{\prime\prime}(0)+\frac{1}{2\cdot 3!n^{2}}f^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}(0)+\frac{B}{4!n^{3}}f^{{}^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}}(\eta)

for some
0\leq\eta\leq 1/n
.
Thus,

|\left(\pi_{n}{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{SBM}}f-{\mathsf{L}}_{n}\Phi_{n}f\right)(0)|\leq\frac{A}{n^{\alpha-2}}\lVert f\rVert+\frac{B}{n}\lVert{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{SBM}}f\rVert+\left(\frac{B}{6n^{2}}+\frac{B}{6n^{3}}\right)\lVert{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{SBM}}^{2}f\rVert\,.

Recalling (
15
) and our choice of
\Xi_{n}
yields

\lVert\pi_{n}{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{SBM}}f-{\mathsf{L}}_{n}\Phi_{n}f\rVert\;\lesssim\;\frac{1}{n^{\alpha-2}}\lVert f\rVert+\frac{1}{n}\lVert{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{SBM}}f\rVert+\frac{1}{n^{2}}\lVert{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{SBM}}^{2}f\rVert\,.

(22)

Thus, Theorem
2.7
shows that

{\bf d}(\mu_{n},\mu)\;\lesssim\;\max\left\{\frac{1}{n^{\alpha-2}},\frac{1}{n^{2}},\frac{1}{n}\right\}\;=\;\max\left\{\frac{1}{n^{\alpha-2}},\frac{1}{n}\right\}\,,

and also that
\{X_{n}(t):t\geq 0\}
converges weakly to
\{X^{\text{SBM}}(t):t\geq 0\}
under the Skorohod topology of
{\mathsf{D}}_{{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}}[0,\infty)
. Hence, we can conclude this case.

3.2.3
The exponential holding BM: the case
\alpha=2
and
\beta\in(1,\infty]

Here we set

c_{1}=\frac{A}{A+1}
,
c_{2}=0
and
c_{3}=\frac{1}{A+1}
.
Denote by
{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{EHBM}}
the generator of the exponential holding BM, whose domain is
{\mathfrak{D}}\left({\mathsf{L}}_{\text{EHBM}}\right)=\left\{f\in{\mathcal{C}}_{0}^{2}({\mathbb{G}}):\frac{A}{A+1}f(0)+\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{A+1}f^{\prime\prime}(0)=0\right\}
.
Additionally to
\alpha=2
and
\beta\in(1,\infty)
, assume for now that
\beta>2
. The case
\beta\in(1,2]
will be analyzed later.
Consider the correction operator identically null, that is,
\Xi_{n}\equiv 0
. Let
f\in{\mathfrak{D}}({\mathsf{L}}^{2}_{\text{EHBM}})
, which yields the boundary conditions
Af(0)=-\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime\prime}(0)
and
Af^{\prime\prime}(0)=-\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}(0)
. Then,

\begin{split}{\mathsf{L}}_{n}\Phi_{n}f(0)&\;=\;-Af(0)+\frac{B}{n^{\beta-2}}\Big[f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{1}{n}\big)-f(0)\Big]\;=\;\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime\prime}(0)+\frac{B}{n^{\beta-2}}\Big[f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{1}{n}\big)-f(0)\Big]\,,\end{split}

(23)

which implies
\left|\left(\pi_{n}{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{EHBM}}f-{\mathsf{L}}_{n}\Phi_{n}f\right)(0)\right|\;\leq\;\frac{2B}{n^{\beta-2}}\lVert f\rVert
.
Consequently, taking (
15
) into account,

\lVert\pi_{n}{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{EHBM}}f-{\mathsf{L}}_{n}\Phi_{n}f\rVert\;\lesssim\;\frac{1}{n^{\beta-2}}\lVert f\rVert+\frac{1}{n^{2}}\lVert{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{EHBM}}^{2}f\rVert\,.

(24)

Thus, Theorem
2.7
implies that

{\bf d}(\mu,\mu_{n})\;\lesssim\;\max\left\{\frac{1}{n^{\beta-2}},\frac{1}{n^{2}},\frac{1}{n}\right\}\;=\;\max\left\{\frac{1}{n^{\beta-2}},\frac{1}{n}\right\},

and also that
\{X_{n}(t):t\geq 0\}
converges weakly to
\{X^{\text{EHBM}}(t):t\geq 0\}
in the Skorohod topology of
{\mathsf{D}}_{{\mathbb{G}}}[0,\infty)
.

As a consequence of (
23
) and using that

n^{2-\beta}[f(1/n)-f(0)]\sim n^{1-\beta}f^{\prime}(0)
we get that
\lVert\pi_{n}{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{EHBM}}f-{\mathsf{L}}_{n}\pi_{n}f\rVert\to 0
. By
Engel and Nagel (2006, Proposition 1.8, page 53)
for any generator
{\mathsf{L}}
associated to a strongly continuous semigroup, the set
{\mathfrak{D}}({\mathsf{L}}^{2})
is a core for
{\mathsf{L}}
. Thus, in particular
{\mathfrak{D}}({\mathsf{L}}_{\text{EHBM}}^{2})
is a core for
{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{EHBM}}
. Applying

Ethier and Kurtz (1986, Theorem 6.1, page 28 and Theorem 2.11, page 172)
one can conclude the convergence towards the exponential holding BM. However, since the rate of convergence relies on the first derivative of
f
, we are not allowed to apply Theorem
2.7
, and no speed of convergence could be provided in this case.

3.2.4
The reflected BM: the case
\beta\in[0,1),\alpha>\beta+1

Denote by
{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{RBM}}
the generator of the reflected Brownian motion, whose domain is

{\mathfrak{D}}({\mathsf{L}}_{\text{RBM}}):=\left\{f\in{\mathcal{C}}_{0}^{2}({\mathbb{G}}):f^{\prime}(0)=0\right\}
.
Let
f\in{\mathfrak{D}}({\mathsf{L}}_{\text{RBM}}^{2})
. Thus
f^{\prime}(0)=f^{\prime\prime\prime}(0)=0
, which leads to

\begin{split}{\mathsf{L}}_{n}\Phi_{n}f(0)&=-An^{2-\alpha}f(0)+\frac{B}{2n^{\beta}}f^{\prime\prime}(0)+\frac{B}{4!n^{2+\beta}}f^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}(\eta)\\
&\qquad-\frac{A}{n^{\alpha-2}}\Xi_{n}f(0)+\frac{B}{n^{\beta-2}}\left[\Xi_{n}f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{1}{n}\big)-\Xi_{n}f(0)\right]\,,\end{split}

(25)

for some
0\leq\eta\leq 1/n
.
Let
\Xi_{n}f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{x}{n}\big):=\widehat{\Xi}_{n}f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{x}{n}\big)+\widetilde{\Xi}_{n}f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{x}{n}\big)
,
where

\displaystyle\widehat{\Xi}_{n}f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{x}{n}\big)\;=\;\frac{Af(0)}{Bn^{\alpha-\beta-1}}h\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{x}{n}\big)\quad\text{ and }\quad\widetilde{\Xi}_{n}f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{x}{n}\big)\;=\;\Big(-\frac{1}{n}+\frac{1}{Bn^{1-\beta}}\Big)\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(0)}{2}h\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{x}{n}\big)\,,

and we assume that
h
is a fixed smooth compactly supported function satisfying
h(0)=h^{\prime\prime}(0)=0
,
h^{\prime}(0)=1
.
First, note that

\lVert\Xi_{n}f\rVert\;\lesssim\;\frac{\lVert f\rVert}{n^{\alpha-\beta-1}}+\frac{\lVert f^{\prime\prime}\rVert}{n^{1-\beta}}

(26)

which converges to zero since
\beta+1<\alpha<2
and
\beta<1
, verifying hypothesis
(H3)
. Our goal now is to check
(H2)
.
Since
h(0)=0
,

\displaystyle-An^{2-\alpha}\widehat{\Xi}_{n}f(0)+Bn^{2-\beta}\left[\widehat{\Xi}_{n}f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{1}{n}\big)-\widehat{\Xi}_{n}f(0)\right]

\displaystyle=\;Bn^{2-\beta}\widehat{\Xi}_{n}f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{1}{n}\big)\;=\;Af(0)n^{3-\alpha}\left[h(0)+h^{\prime}(0)\frac{1}{n}+\frac{h^{\prime\prime}(0)}{2!}\frac{1}{n^{2}}+\frac{h^{\prime\prime\prime}(\theta)}{3!}\frac{1}{n^{3}}\right]\,,

for some
\theta\in[0,1/n]
. Since
h(0)=h^{\prime\prime}(0)=0
and
h^{\prime}(0)=1
, we conclude that

\displaystyle-An^{2-\alpha}\widehat{\Xi}_{n}f(0)+Bn^{2-\beta}\left[\widehat{\Xi}_{n}f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{1}{n}\big)-\widehat{\Xi}_{n}f(0)\right]\;=\;Af(0)n^{2-\alpha}+\lVert f\rVert\cdot O(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{1}{n^\alpha})\,.

On the other hand, using that
h(0)=h^{\prime\prime}(0)=0
and
h^{\prime}(0)=1
,

\displaystyle-An^{2-\alpha}\widetilde{\Xi}_{n}f(0)+Bn^{2-\beta}\left[\widetilde{\Xi}_{n}f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{1}{n}\big)-\widetilde{\Xi}_{n}f(0)\right]\;=\;(-Bn^{1-\beta}+n)\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(0)}{2}h\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{1}{n}\big)

\displaystyle=\;(-Bn^{1-\beta}+n)\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(0)}{2}\left[h^{\prime}(0)\frac{1}{n}+\frac{h^{\prime\prime}(0)}{2!}\frac{1}{n^{2}}+\frac{h^{\prime\prime\prime}(\theta)}{3!}\frac{1}{n^{3}}\right]\;=\;(-Bn^{1-\beta}+n)\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(0)}{2}\left[\frac{1}{n}+\frac{h^{\prime\prime\prime}(\theta)}{3!}\frac{1}{n^{3}}\right]

for some
\theta\in[0,1/n]
.
Therefore, recalling (
25
),

\displaystyle|\pi_{n}{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{RBM}}f(0)-{\mathsf{L}}_{n}\Phi_{n}f(0)|\;\lesssim\;\frac{1}{n^{\alpha}}\|f\|+\frac{1}{n^{2}}\|{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{RBM}}f\|+\frac{1}{n^{2+\beta}}\|{\mathsf{L}}^{2}_{\text{RBM}}f\|\,.

Let us deal with the convergence outside zero. Using a Taylor expansion on
h
similarly as in (
15
) it is easy to check that, for
\frac{x}{n}\in{\mathbb{G}}_{n}\backslash\{0\}
,

\displaystyle\big|{\mathsf{L}}_{n}\widehat{\Xi}_{n}f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{x}{n}\big)\big|\;\lesssim\;\frac{|f(0)|}{n^{\alpha-\beta-1}}\Big[\lVert h^{\prime\prime}\rVert+\frac{\lVert h^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}\rVert}{n^{2}}\Big]\;\lesssim\;\frac{\|f\|}{n^{\alpha-\beta-1}}\,.

On the other hand, also for
\frac{x}{n}\in{\mathbb{G}}_{n}\backslash\{0\}
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathsf{L}}_{n}\widetilde{\Xi}_{n}f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{x}{n}\big)=\lVert f^{\prime\prime}\rVert O\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{1}{n^{1-\beta}}\big)\,.

Putting together all those bounds with (
25
) and (
15
), we finally get

\displaystyle\lVert\pi_{n}{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{RBM}}f-{\mathsf{L}}_{n}\Phi_{n}f\rVert\;\lesssim\;\max\left\{\frac{1}{n^{\alpha}},\frac{1}{n^{\alpha-\beta-1}}\right\}\lVert f\rVert+\frac{1}{n^{1-\beta}}\lVert{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{RBM}}f\rVert+\max\left\{\frac{1}{n^{2+\beta}},\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right\}\|{\mathsf{L}}^{2}_{\text{RBM}}f\|\,.

(27)

In view of (
27
) and (
26
), we can apply Theorem
2.7
, hence giving us that

\displaystyle{\bf d}(\mu_{n},\mu)

\displaystyle\lesssim\;\max\left\{\frac{1}{n},\frac{1}{n^{\alpha}},\frac{1}{n^{\alpha-\beta-1}},\frac{1}{n^{1-\beta}},\frac{1}{n^{2+\beta}},\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right\}\;=\;\max\left\{\frac{1}{n^{\alpha-\beta-1}},\frac{1}{n^{1-\beta}}\right\}

and that
\{X_{n}(t):t\geq 0\}
weakly converges to
\{X^{\text{RBM}}(t):t\geq 0\}
under the Skorohod topology on
{\mathsf{D}}_{{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}}[0,\infty)
, finishing the proof.

3.2.5
The absorbed BM: the case
\beta>1,\alpha>2

Denote by
{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{ABM}}
the generator of the absorbed Brownian motion, whose domain is given by

{\mathfrak{D}}\left({\mathsf{L}}_{\text{ABM}}\right):=\{f\in{\mathcal{C}}_{0}^{2}({\mathbb{G}}):f^{\prime\prime}(0)=0\}
.

Subcase
\alpha>2
and
\beta>2

Consider a null correction
\Xi_{n}\equiv 0
. Then

\displaystyle{\mathsf{L}}_{n}\Phi_{n}f(0)

\displaystyle=-\frac{Af(0)}{n^{\alpha-2}}+\frac{B}{n^{\beta-2}}\left[f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{1}{n}\big)-f(0)\right]\,,

thus combining this with (
15
)

\lVert\pi_{n}{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{ABM}}f-{\mathsf{L}}_{n}\Phi_{n}f\rVert\;\lesssim\;\max\left\{\frac{1}{n^{\alpha-2}},\frac{1}{n^{\beta-2}}\right\}\lVert f\rVert+\frac{1}{n^{2}}\|{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{ABM}}^{2}f\|\,.

(28)

Denote by
\mu
the probability measure of the absorbed BM at time
t>0
. In view of (
28
), we can apply Theorem
2.7
, which gives us

{\bf d}(\mu_{n},\mu)\;\lesssim\;\max\left\{\frac{1}{n},\frac{1}{n^{\alpha-2}},\frac{1}{n^{\beta-2}},\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right\}\;=\;\max\left\{\frac{1}{n},\frac{1}{n^{\alpha-2}},\frac{1}{n^{\beta-2}}\right\}

and
\{X_{n}(t):t\geq 0\}
weakly converges to
\{X^{\text{ABM}}(t):t\geq 0\}
under the Skorohod topology of
{\mathsf{D}}_{{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}}[0,\infty)
.

Subcase
\alpha>2
and
\beta\in(1,2]

Similarly to what happened in the exponential holding BM in the strip
1<\beta<2
, we can check that
\lVert\pi_{n}{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{ABM}}f-{\mathsf{L}}_{n}\pi_{n}f\rVert\to 0
for every
f\in{\mathfrak{D}}({\mathsf{L}}_{\text{ABM}}^{2})
, so we can apply
Ethier and Kurtz (1986, Theorem 6.1, page 28 and Theorem 2.11, page 172)
to deduce the convergence towards the Absorbed BM. However, since the rate of convergence relies on the first derivative of
f
, we cannot apply Theorem
2.7
, hence no speed of convergence could be provided in this case.

3.2.6
The mixed BM: the case
\alpha=2
and
\beta=1

Consider

c_{1}=\frac{A}{1+A+B}
,
c_{2}=\frac{B}{1+A+B}
and
c_{3}=\frac{1}{1+A+B}
.
Let
{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{MBM}}
be the generator of the mixed Brownian motion, whose domain is given by

{\mathfrak{D}}\left({\mathsf{L}}_{\text{MBM}}\right)\;:=\;\Big\{f\in{\mathcal{C}}_{0}^{2}({\mathbb{G}}):\frac{Af(0)}{1+A+B}-\frac{Bf^{\prime}(0)}{1+A+B}+\frac{\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime\prime}(0)}{(1+A+B)}=0\Big\}.

In this case, also no correction is needed, so define
\Xi_{n}\equiv 0
to be the null operator.
If
f\in{\mathfrak{D}}({\mathsf{L}}_{\text{MBM}}^{2})
, then we get the boundary conditions
Bf^{\prime}(0)=Af(0)+\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime\prime}(0)
and
Bf^{\prime\prime\prime}(0)=Af^{\prime\prime}(0)+\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}(0)
. Since
\alpha=2
and
\beta=1
, we have that using (
14
)

\displaystyle{\mathsf{L}}_{n}\Phi_{n}f(0)

\displaystyle\;=\;\left(Bf^{\prime}(0)-Af(0)\right)+\frac{B}{2!n}f^{\prime\prime}(0)+\frac{B}{3!n^{2}}f^{\prime\prime\prime}(0)+\frac{B}{4!n^{3}}f^{{}^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}}(\eta)

for some
0\leq\eta\leq 1/n
. Applying the boundary conditions, it implies that

\Big|\big(\pi_{n}{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{MBM}}f-{\mathsf{L}}_{n}\Phi_{n}f\big)(0)\Big|\;\leq\;\left(\frac{B}{n}+\frac{A}{3n^{2}}\right)\lVert{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{MBM}}f\rVert+\left(\frac{1}{3n^{2}}+\frac{B}{6n^{3}}\right)\|{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{MBM}}^{2}f\|\,.

By the above bound and (
15
)

\lVert\pi_{n}{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{MBM}}f-{\mathsf{L}}_{n}\Phi_{n}f\rVert\;\lesssim\;\frac{1}{n}\lVert{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{MBM}}f\rVert+\frac{1}{n^{2}}\|{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{MBM}}^{2}f\|\,.

Denote by
\mu
the probability measure of the mixed BM at time
t>0
.
Thus, by the previous inequality we can invoke Theorem
2.7
, concluding that

{\bf d}(\mu_{n},\mu)\;\lesssim\;\max\left\{\frac{1}{n},\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right\}\;=\;\frac{1}{n}

and that
\{X_{n}(t):t\geq 0\}
weakly converges to
\{X^{\text{MBM}}(t):t\geq 0\}
in the Skorohod topology on
{\mathsf{D}}_{\mathbb{G}}[0,\infty)
.

3.3
Proof of Theorem
2.12

In this section we prove Theorem
2.12
. Before delving into the details we recall that the topology is different here, because
{\mathsf{S}}=(0,\infty)
. In this scenario, see Remark
2.4
, the functions in the space
{\mathcal{C}}_{0}({\mathsf{S}}_{\Delta})
must converge to zero at zero, see Definition
2.3
.
Since the topology is different from the previous setup, we need to check again hypothesis
(G2)
. Recall the definition of the functions
f_{k}
in (
11
). We note that, for all
j,k\geq 1
, we have that
f_{k,j}(0)=f_{k,j}^{\prime\prime}(0)=0
, which implies
f_{k,j}\in{\mathfrak{D}}({\mathsf{L}}_{\text{KBM}})
for all
j,k\geq 1
. In the proposition below we do not consider the function
f_{0}
since
f_{0}(0)\neq 0
, so it does not belong to the
space
{\mathcal{C}}_{0}\big((0,\infty)\big)
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{B}}=\{f_{k}:k\geq 1\}
. Then
\spann({\mathcal{B}})
is dense in
{\mathcal{C}}_{0}\big((0,\infty)\big)
.

Proof.

Immediate from Corollary
3.2
and the fact that
f_{k}(0)=0
for any
k\geq 1
.
∎

Proposition
3.4
together with its preceding comment assure
(G2)
. To ease arguments, note that
\tau_{n}X_{n}
is the same as considering the boundary random walk
X_{n}
on the state space in Figure
4
.
Denote by
X^{\text{KBM}}
the killed Brownian motion of coefficients
c_{1}=1
,
c_{2}=0
,
c_{3}=0
and let
{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{KBM}}
be its generator, whose domain is given by

{\mathfrak{D}}\left({\mathsf{L}}_{\text{KBM}}\right):=\{f\in{\mathcal{C}}_{0}^{2}({\mathbb{G}}):f(0)=0\}
.
Let
f\in{\mathfrak{D}}({\mathsf{L}}_{\text{KBM}}^{2})
. By the definition of
{\mathcal{C}}_{0}({\mathbb{G}})
and since
{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{KBM}}f\in{\mathcal{C}}_{0}({\mathbb{G}})
, we infer that
f^{\prime\prime}(0)=0
.
By doing Taylor expansions around zero and using that
f(0)=f^{\prime\prime}(0)=0
, we get

\displaystyle{\mathsf{L}}_{n}f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{1}{n}\big)

\displaystyle=\;\frac{An^{2}}{n^{\alpha}}\Big[f(\Delta)-f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{1}{n}\big)\Big]+\frac{Bn^{2}}{n^{\beta}}\Big[f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{2}{n}\big)-f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{1}{n}\big)\Big]

\displaystyle=\;-An^{2-\alpha}\Big[f(0)+f^{\prime}(0)\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{1}{n}+\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(0)}{2!}\frac{1}{n^{2}}+\frac{f^{\prime\prime\prime}(\theta_{1})}{3!}\frac{1}{n^{3}}\Big]

\displaystyle+Bn^{2-\beta}\Big[f^{\prime}(0)\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{2}{n}+\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{f''(0)}{2!}\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{2}{n}\big)^{2}+\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{f'''(\theta_2)}{3!}\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{2}{n}\big)^{3}-f^{\prime}(0)\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{1}{n}-\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{f''(0)}{2!}\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{1}{n^2}-\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{f'''(\theta_3)}{3!}\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{1}{n^3}\Big]

\displaystyle=\;f^{\prime}\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{0}{n}\big)\Big[Bn^{1-\beta}-An^{1-\alpha}\Big]+O(\max\{\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{1}{n^{1+\beta}},\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{1}{n^{1+\alpha}}\})\,,

(29)

where
\theta_{1},\theta_{3}\in[0,\frac{1}{n}]

.
On the other hand, we have that assuming that our correction
\Xi_{n}
is such that
\Xi_{n}f(\Delta)=0
,

\displaystyle{\mathsf{L}}_{n}\Xi_{n}f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{1}{n}\big)

\displaystyle\;=\;\frac{An^{2}}{n^{\alpha}}\Big[\Xi_{n}f(\Delta)-\Xi_{n}f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{1}{n}\big)\Big]+\frac{Bn^{2}}{n^{\beta}}\Big[\Xi_{n}f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{2}{n}\big)-\Xi_{n}f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{1}{n}\big)\Big]

\displaystyle\;=\;-An^{2-\alpha}\Xi_{n}f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{1}{n}\big)+Bn^{2-\beta}\Big[\Xi_{n}f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{2}{n}\big)-\Xi_{n}f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{1}{n}\big)\Big]\,.

(30)

Since
{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{KBM}}f(0)=\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime\prime}(0)=0
, our goal is to find a correction
\Xi_{n}
such that (
30
) cancels the non-vanishing terms in (

).

3.3.1
Subcase
\beta>1

In this case, the parcel
Bn^{1-\beta}
in (
29
) vanishes as
n\to\infty
, and we only need to deal with
An^{1-\alpha}
.
Define

\displaystyle\Xi_{n}f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{x}{n}\big)\;:=\;-\frac{f^{\prime}(0)\cdot n^{-1}}{1+\frac{g\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{x-1}{n}\big)}{n}}

\frac{x}{n}\geq\frac{1}{n}
, where

is a non-negative compact supported smooth function such that

. It is now straightforward to check that
\|\pi_{n}{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{KBM}}f-{\mathsf{L}}_{n}\Phi_{n}f\|\to 0
.
Thus, applying

, we conclude that
\{X_{n}(t):t\geq 0\}
weakly converges to
\{X^{\text{KBM}}(t):t\geq 0\}

2.7

3.3.2
Subcase
\beta\in[0,1],\alpha<1+\beta

In this scenario, the parcel
Bn^{1-\beta}
does not vanish as

, and we need to define an extra correction to deal with it.
Define

\displaystyle\Xi_{n}f\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{x}{n}\big)\;:=\;-\frac{f^{\prime}(0)\cdot n^{-1}}{1+\frac{g\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{x-1}{n}\big)}{n}}+\frac{\frac{B}{A}f^{\prime}(0)\cdot n^{\alpha-\beta-1}}{1+\frac{g\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{x-1}{n}\big)}{n}}

for
\frac{x}{n}\geq\frac{1}{n}
and
g
is the same function as in the previous case. Note that the condition
\alpha<1+\beta
guarantees that the correction above goes to zero as
n\to\infty
. It is now straightforward to check that
\|\pi_{n}{\mathsf{L}}_{\text{KBM}}f-{\mathsf{L}}_{n}\Phi_{n}f\|\to 0
, leading us to conclude that
\{X_{n}(t):t\geq 0\}
weakly converges to
\{X^{\text{KBM}}(t):t\geq 0\}
under the Skorohod topology on
{\mathsf{D}}_{\mathbb{G}}[0,\infty)
.

Appendix A
Local Lipschitz property of semigroups

This appendix provides a verification of the local Lipschitz property of the semigroups of the absorbed Brownian motion, killed Brownian motion,
reflected Brownian motion, sticky Brownian motion, elastic Brownian motion, exponential holding Brownian motion and mixed Brownian motion. This is needed to verify condition
(G4)
, which is an ingredient in the proof of Theorem
2.7
.
Namely, it says that
\{T(t):t\geq 0\}
is locally Lipschitz in the sense that, for each
t>0
and each compact set
K\subseteq{\mathsf{S}}
, there exists a constant
M=M(t,K)>0
such that, for all
f\in{\mathcal{C}}_{0}({\mathsf{S}}_{\Delta})
,

|{\mathsf{T}}(t)f(u)-{\mathsf{T}}(t)f(\tilde{u})|\;\leq\;M\cdot\|f\|_{\infty}\cdot d_{{\mathsf{S}}}(u,\tilde{u})\,,\quad\forall\,u,\tilde{u}\in K\,.

For the processes considered in this paper, we have
{\mathsf{S}}=[0,\infty)
, except in the case of the killed BM, where
{\mathsf{S}}=(0,\infty)
. In all cases, the distance on
{\mathsf{S}}
is
d(u,\tilde{u})=|u-\tilde{u}|
. Let us postpone the analysis of the killed BM to the end. The semigroups of absorbed BM,
reflected BM, sticky BM, elastic BM, exponential holding BM and mixed BM all have transition probability densities of the form

p_{t}(u,dv)\;=\;q_{t}(u,v)dv+r_{t}(u)\delta_{0}(dv)+s_{t}(u)\delta_{\Delta}(dv)\,.

Since the test functions are assumed to satisfy
f(\Delta)=0
, we can estimate

\displaystyle|{\mathsf{T}}(t)f(u)-{\mathsf{T}}(t)f(\tilde{u})|\;\leq\;

\displaystyle\int_{[0,\infty)}|q_{t}(u,v)-q_{t}(\tilde{u},v)|\cdot|f(v)|\,dv+|r_{t}(u)-r_{t}(\tilde{u})|\cdot|f(0)|

\displaystyle\;\leq\;

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\infty}\Big\{|r_{t}(u)-r_{t}(\tilde{u})|+\int_{[0,\infty)}|q_{t}(u,v)-q_{t}(\tilde{u},v)|\,dv\Big\}\,.

Assume for a moment that we have checked the following two conditions:

(A1)

The function
r_{t}(u)
is
C^{1}
in
u\in[0,\infty)
.

(A2)

The function
q_{t}(u,v)
is
C^{1}
in
u\in[0,\infty)
and, for all
K\subset{\mathsf{S}}
compact, it holds that

\int_{[0,\infty)}\Big[\sup_{u\in K}|\partial_{u}q_{t}(u,v)|\Big]dv\;<\;\infty\,.

Then, under
(A1)
and
(A2)
, we have

\displaystyle|{\mathsf{T}}(t)f(u)-{\mathsf{T}}(t)f(\tilde{u})|\;\leq\;

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\infty}\cdot\bigg\{\sup_{u\in K}|\partial_{u}r_{t}(u,v)|+\int_{[0,\infty)}\Big[\sup_{u\in K}|\partial_{u}q_{t}(u,v)|\Big]\,dv\bigg\}\cdot|u-\tilde{u}|

\displaystyle\;=\;

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\infty}\cdot M(t,K)\cdot|u-\tilde{u}|\,,

assuring the local Lipschitz property. So our task is to check
(A1)
and
(A2)
for each of the seven semigroups we dealt with in this paper. Except for exponential holding BM, all formulas below are adapted from
(
Borodin and Salminen, 2002
, Appendix 1)
. Note that there is missing a factor of
1/2
everywhere in comparison with the formulas of
Borodin and Salminen (2002)
because there the authors take twice the Lebesgue measure as reference measure. Denote by

\Gamma(z)\;=\;\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{z}^{\infty}e^{-u^{2}}\text{\rm d}u\qquad\text{ and }\qquad G_{t}(u,v)\;=\;\frac{e^{-\frac{(u-v)^{2}}{2t}}}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}

the error function and the Gaussian kernel, respectively.
Note that these two functions satisfy the following properties.

(i)

\Gamma
and
G_{t}(\cdot,v)
are smooth for all times
t>0
and all
v
.

(ii)

There exists a
c>0
such that for all
z>0
one has that
\Gamma(z)\leq\tfrac{c}{z}e^{-z^{2}}
. This is a known Gaussian tail bound.

(iii)

A direct computation yields
\Gamma^{\prime}(z)=-\tfrac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}e^{-z^{2}}
.

(iv)

For all compact sets
K\subset{\mathsf{S}}
and all times
t>0
the functions

v\mapsto\sup_{u\in K}|\partial_{u}G_{t}(u,v)|\qquad\text{and}\qquad v\mapsto\sup_{u\in K}|\partial_{u}G_{t}(u,-v)|

are integrable over
v\in[0,\infty)
. This follows from a direct computation.

Absorbed BM

(
Borodin and Salminen, 2002
, Page 121)
. In this case,

\displaystyle{q}_{t}(u,v)

\displaystyle\;=\;G_{t}(u,v)-G_{t}(u,-v)\qquad\text{ for }u,v\in[0,\infty),

\displaystyle{r}_{t}(u)

\displaystyle\;=\;1-\int_{0}^{\infty}q_{t}(u,v)dv,\quad\text{ for }u\in[0,\infty),

\displaystyle{s}_{t}(u)

\displaystyle\;=\;0.

Reflected BM

(
Borodin and Salminen, 2002
, Page 120)
. In this case,

\displaystyle{q}_{t}(u,v)

\displaystyle\;=\;G_{t}(u,v)+G_{t}(u,-v),\qquad\text{ for }u,v\in[0,\infty),

\displaystyle{r}_{t}(u)

\displaystyle\;=\;0,

\displaystyle{s}_{t}(u)

\displaystyle\;=\;0.

Elastic BM

(
Borodin and Salminen, 2002
, Page 125)
. In this case, denoting
\gamma:=\frac{c_{1}}{c_{2}}
, where
c_{1},c_{2}
are as in
Erhard et al., 2026a
, Theorem 2.10
, we have that

\displaystyle{q}_{t}(u,v)

\displaystyle\;=\;G_{t}(u,v)+G_{t}(u,-v)-\gamma e^{\Big(\gamma(u+v)+\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{\gamma^2t}{2}\Big)}\Gamma\Bigl(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{u + v + \gamma t}{\sqrt{2t}}\Bigr),\qquad\text{ for }u,v\in[0,\infty),

\displaystyle{r}_{t}(u)

\displaystyle\;=\;0,

\displaystyle{s}_{t}(u)

\displaystyle\;=\;1-\int_{0}^{\infty}q_{t}(u,v)dv,\quad\text{ for }u\in[0,\infty).

Note that the transition kernel in
Borodin and Salminen (2002)

Sticky BM

(
Borodin and Salminen, 2002
, Pages 123-124)
. In this case,

\displaystyle{q}_{t}(u,v)

\displaystyle\;=\;G_{t}(u,v)-G_{t}(u,-v)+\frac{1}{\gamma}e^{\Big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{2u+ 2v}{\gamma}+\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{2t}{\gamma^2}\Big)}\Gamma\Bigl(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{u+ v}{\sqrt{2t}}+\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{\sqrt{2t}}{\gamma}\Bigr)\,,\;\text{ for }u,v\in[0,\infty),

\displaystyle\;=\;1-\int_{0}^{\infty}q_{t}(u,v)dv,\quad\text{ for }u\in[0,\infty),

\displaystyle{s}_{t}(u)

\displaystyle\;=\;0.

Note that in

the sticky Brownian motion is defined on the full line. Nevertheless, identifying

Exponential Holding BM
. In this case, the transition probability density is

\displaystyle{q}_{t}(u,v)

\displaystyle\;=\;G_{t}(u,v)-G_{t}(u,-v),\qquad\text{ for }u,v\in[0,\infty),

\displaystyle{r}_{t}(u)

\displaystyle\;=\;\begin{cases}\int_{0}^{t}f_{\tau_{0}}(s,u)e^{-\lambda(t-s)}ds&\text{ for }u\in(0,\infty),\\
e^{-\lambda t}&\text{ for }u=0,\end{cases}

(31)

\displaystyle{s}_{t}(u)

\displaystyle\;=\;\begin{cases}\int_{0}^{t}f_{\tau_{0}}(s,u)\big(1-e^{-\lambda(t-s)}\big)ds,&\text{ for }u\in(0,\infty),\\
1-e^{-\lambda t}&\text{ for }u=0,\end{cases}

where

f_{\tau_{0}}(s,u)=\frac{u}{\sqrt{2\pi s^{3}}}e^{-\frac{u^{2}}{2s}}\mathds{1}_{[0,\infty)}(s)
is the density of
\tau_{0}
, the hitting time of zero for the absorbed BM.
We were not able to locate the above case in the literature. However one can deduce it as follows. The exponential holding Brownian motion is defined as a Brownian motion that upon hitting zero cannot transition to the half-line anymore (so it gets absorbed), and then jumps from zero to the cemetery after an exponentially distributed amount of time. The formula for
q_{t}
coincides with the corresponding expression for the absorbed Brownian motion. The first line in the definition of
r_{t}
integrates the probability density of the process to go from
u
to zero at time
s
and then staying there for the remaining
t-s
time units. The second line is the probability of an exponentially distributed random variable to be bigger than
t
, which means that the process starts in zero and stays there. An analogous reasoning can be applied to deduce the formula for
s_{t}
.

(

\displaystyle+\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{\Upsilon- 1}{2 c \Upsilon}e^{\big(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{(u + v)2\gamma}{1 + \Upsilon}+\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{(\Upsilon- 1)^2 t}{8 c^2}\big)}\Gamma\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{u + v}{\sqrt{2t}}+\genfrac{}{}{}{1}{(1 - \Upsilon)\sqrt{t}}{2c\sqrt{2}}\bigg),\qquad\text{ for }u,v\in[0,\infty),

Note that we need to analyse the derivative of

Using the substitution

\sqrt{2}\int_{\tfrac{u^{2}}{2t}}^{\infty}v^{-1/2}e^{-v}e^{\lambda\tfrac{u^{2}}{2v}}\,dv\,.

To continue, define for

and note that

\partial_{u}\big(v^{-1/2}e^{-v}e^{\lambda\tfrac{u^{2}}{2v}}\big)\;=\;v^{-1/2}e^{-v}\tfrac{u}{v}e^{\lambda\tfrac{u^{2}}{2v}}\,.

Hence, dominated convergence implies that we can interchange integration and differentiation with respect to

Using the chain rule we thus conclude that

\partial_{u}h(\tfrac{u^{2}}{2t},u)\;=\;\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{t}}e^{-\tfrac{u}{2t}}e^{\lambda t}+\int_{\tfrac{u^{2}}{2t}}^{\infty}\frac{u}{v^{3/2}}e^{-\lambda v}e^{\lambda\tfrac{u^{2}}{2v}}\,dv\,.

Estimating

on the domain of integration we see that, for all

\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{u}{v^{3/2}}e^{-\lambda v}e^{\lambda\tfrac{u^{2}}{2v}}\,dv\;\leq\;Cu\,,

This concludes the prove that

A priori estimates in terms of the maximum norm for the solution of the Navier-Stokes equations with periodic initial data Thanks: Received May 31, 2016
Santosh Pathak

Abstract




The Nepali Math. Sc. Report

Vol. 36, No.1, 2019

Abstract:
In this paper, we consider the Cauchy problem for the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
n\geq 3
with smooth periodic initial data and derive a priori estimtes of the maximum norm of all derivatives of the solution in terms of the maximum norm of the initial data. This paper is a special case of a paper by H-O Kreiss and J. Lorenz which also generalizes the main result of their paper to higher dimension.

Key Words
: Incompressible Navier-Stokes equation; Maximum norm estimates, Periodic initial data

AMS (MOS) Subject Classification.
Classification here.

1.
Introduction

We consider the Cauchy problem of the Navier-Stokes equations in
\mathbb{R}^{n},n\geq 3
:

(1.1)

\displaystyle u_{t}+u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla p=\triangle u,\hskip 15.0pt\nabla\cdot u=0,

with initial condition

(1.2)

\displaystyle u(x,0)=f(x),\hskip 15.0ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
u=u(x,t)=(u_{1}(x,t),\cdots u_{n}(x,t))
and
p=p(x,t)
stand for the unknown velocity vector field of the fluid and its pressure, while
f=f(x)=(f_{1}(x),\cdots f_{n}(x))
is the given initial velocity vector field. In what follows, we will use the same notations for the space of vector valued and scalar functions for convenience in writing.

There is a large literature on the existence and uniqueness of solution of the Navier-Stokes equations in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For given initial data, solutions of (1.1) and (1.2) have been constructed in various function spaces. For example, if
f\in L^{r}
for some
r
with
3\leq r<\infty
, then it is well known that there is a unique classical solution in some maximum interval of time
0\leq t<T_{f}
where
0<T_{f}\leq\infty
. But for the uniqueness of the pressure one requires
|p(x,t)|\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
. (See
[
6
]
and
[
11
]
for
r=3
and
[
1
]
for
3<r<\infty
.)

If
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
then existence of a regular solution follows from
[
2
]
. The solution is only unique if one puts some growth restrictions on the pressure as
|x|\to\infty
. A simple example of non-uniqueness is demonstrated in
[
7
]
where the velocity
u
is bounded but
|p(x,t)|\leq C|x|
. In addition, an estimate
|p(x,t)|\leq C(1+|x|^{\sigma})
with
\sigma<1
( see
[
3
]
) implies uniqueness. Also the assumption
p\in L^{1}_{loc}(0,T;BMO)
(see
[
5
]
) implies uniqueness.

In this paper we consider the initial function
f\in C^{\infty}_{per}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
which is the space of smooth
2\pi
periodic functions. Since
C^{\infty}_{per}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a closed subspace of the Banach space
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the existence of a regular solution of the Navier-Stokes equations (1.1) and (1.2) can be guaranteed by
[
2
]
. In addition, in a paper by Giga and others
[
5
]
they consider
f\in BUC(\mathbb{R}^{n})
where
BUC(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the space of bounded uniformly continuous functions. In the paper they construct a regular solution of the Navier-Stokes equations in some maximum interval of time
0\leq t<T_{f}
where
T_{f}\leq\infty
. Clearly, our case is also a special case of their paper where we put extra assumption of “smooth periodic” on their initial function
f\in BUC(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover, for smooth periodic initial data, the existence of smooth periodic solution is proved by H-O Kreiss and J. Lorenz in their book
[
9
]
for
n=3
where they use successive iteration using the the vorticity formulation. On the other hand, Giga and others use iteration on the integral equation of the transformed abstract ordinary differential equations to construct a mild solution of the Navier-Stokes equations and later prove such mild solution is indeed a regular solution (local in time) of the Navier-Stokes equations (1.1) and (1.2) for
f\in BUC(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Readers are referred to the paper by Giga and others
[
5
]
for details on existence of the smooth periodic solution of the Navier-Stokes equations of (1.1) and (1.2) for
f\in C^{\infty}_{per}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with necessary alternations in their proofs of case
f\in BUC(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

The work in this paper reproves Theorem 4.1 of the Kreiss and Lorenz paper
[
8
]
in periodic case assuming smooth periodic solution exists for some maximum interval of time
0\leq t<T_{f}
. Since we are in a special case of their paper, result of Theorem 4.1 must be true for smooth periodic initial data as well, but what makes our work interesting and different is the approach taken to handle the pressure term of the Navier-Stokes equations while deriving the result of Theorem 4.1 of the Kreiss and Lorenz paper as I have adopted in my first paper
[
10
]
Notice, pressure term of the Navier-Stokes equations can be determined from the Poisson equation

(1.3)

\displaystyle\triangle p=-\nabla\cdot(u\cdot\nabla)u

which is given by

(1.4)

\displaystyle p=\sum_{i,j}R_{i}R_{j}(u_{i}u_{j}),

where
R_{i}=(-\triangle)^{-1/2}D_{i}
is the
i
-th Riesz transform. Since the Riesz transforms are not bounded in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the pressure term
p\in L^{1}_{loc}(0,T;BMO)
where
BMO
is the space of functions of bounded mean oscillation. Because of the non-local nature of the pressure, the proof of Theorem 4.1 of the Kreiss and Lorenz paper is complicated, however.

The main objective of this paper is to derive a priori estimates of the maximum norm of the derivatives of
u
in terms of the maximum norm of the initial function,
u(x,0)=f(x)
,
assuming
the solution to exist and to be
C^{\infty}_{per}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),n\geq 3
for
0\leq t<T_{f}
. Before we start formulating the problem, we introduce the following notations

\displaystyle|f|_{\infty}=\mathop{\sup}_{x}{|f(x)|}\hskip 15.0pt\text{with}\hskip 15.0pt|f(x)|^{2}=\sum_{i}f_{i}^{2}(x),

and
D^{\alpha}=D_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots D_{n}^{\alpha_{n}},D_{i}=\partial/{\partial x_{i}}\hskip 5.0pt\text{for a multiindex}\hskip 5.0pt\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n})
.
In what follows, for any
j=0,1,\cdots
, if
|\alpha|=j
then we will denote
D^{\alpha}
by
D^{j}
. We also set

\displaystyle|\mathcal{D}^{j}u(t)|_{\infty}:=|\mathcal{D}^{j}u(\cdot,t)|_{\infty}=\mathop{\max}_{|\alpha|=j}|D^{\alpha}u(\cdot,t)|_{\infty}.

Clearly,
|\mathcal{D}^{j}u(t)|_{\infty}
measures all space derivatives of order
j
in maximum norm.

Proving the following theorem is the main goal of this paper whereas Kreiss and Lorenz in their paper
[
8
]
prove the same theorem for
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
n=3
from rather difficult approach while dealing with the pressure term
p(x,t)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Consider the Cauchy problem for the Navier-Stokes equations (1.1), (1.2), where
f\in C^{\infty}_{per}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
n\geq 3
with
\nabla\cdot f=0
. There is a constant
c_{0}>0
and for every
j=0,1,\cdots
there is a constant
K_{j}
so that

(1.5)

\displaystyle t^{j/2}|\mathcal{D}^{j}u(t)|_{\infty}\leq K_{j}|f|_{\infty}\hskip 15.0pt\text{for}\hskip 15.0pt0<t\leq\frac{c_{0}}{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}.

The constants
c_{0}
and
K_{j}
are independent of
t
and
f
.

For the purpose of proving Theorem 1.1, we start by transforming the momentum equation (1.1) of the Navier-Stokes equations into the abstract ordinary differential equation for
u

(1.6)

\displaystyle u_{t}=\triangle u-\mathbb{P}(u\cdot\nabla)u

by eliminating the pressure, where
\mathbb{P}
is the Leray projector defined by

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}=(\mathbb{P}_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq n},\hskip 15.0pt\mathbb{P}_{ij}=\delta_{ij}+R_{i}R_{j};

where
R_{i}
is same as in (1.4) and
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker delta function. Note that the equation (1.6) is obtained from (1.1 ) by applying the Leray projector with the properties
\mathbb{P}(\nabla p)=0,\mathbb{P}(\triangle u)=\triangle u
, since
\nabla\cdot u=0
.

Since
\mathbb{P}(u\cdot\nabla u)=\sum_{i}D_{i}\mathbb{P}(u_{i}u)
, therefore it is very appropriate to consider an analogous system of (1.6) as below:

(1.7)

\displaystyle u_{t}=\triangle u+D_{i}\mathbb{P}g(u)\hskip 15.0ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\hskip 15.0ptt>0

with initial condition

(1.8)

\displaystyle u(x,0)=f(x)\hskip 15.0pt\text{where}\hskip 15.0ptf\in C^{\infty}_{per}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Here
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is assumed to be quadratic in
u
. The maximal interval of existence is again
0\leq t<T_{f}
. We would like to prove the estimates of the maximum norm of the derivatives of the solution of (1.7) and (1.8) in terms of the maximum norm of the initial data.

Theorem 1.2
.

Under the above assumptions on
f
and
g
the solution of (1.7) and (1.8) satisfies the following

(a) There is a constant
c_{0}>0
with

(1.9)

\displaystyle T(f)>\frac{c_{0}}{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}

and

(1.10)

\displaystyle|u(t)|_{\infty}\leq 2|f|_{\infty}\hskip 15.0pt\text{for}\hskip 15.0pt0\leq t\leq\frac{c_{0}}{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}.

(b) For every
j=1,2,\cdots
, there is a constant
K_{j}>0
with

(1.11)

\displaystyle t^{j/2}|\mathcal{D}^{j}u(.,t)|_{\infty}\leq K_{j}|f|_{\infty}\hskip 15.0pt\text{for}\hskip 15.0pt0<t\leq\frac{c_{0}}{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}.

The constant
c_{0}
and
K_{j}
are independent of
t
and
f
.

In section 2, we will introduce some auxiliary results for the solution of the heat equation and few other important estimates which are used later in section 3 and 4. Proof of Theorem 1.2 will be provided in section 3. Then we prove Theorem 1.1 in section 4. Finally, in section 5 we outline some remarks on the use of the result obtained in Theorem 1.1.

2.
Some Auxiliary results

Let us consider
f\in C^{\infty}_{per}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The solution of

\displaystyle u_{t}=\triangle u,\hskip 15.0ptu=f\hskip 15.0pt\text{at}\hskip 15.0ptt=0,

is denoted by

\displaystyle u(t):=u(\cdot,t)=e^{\triangle t}f=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\theta(x-y,t)f(y)dy

where

(2.1)

\displaystyle\theta(x,t)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}e^{-|k|^{2}t}e^{ik\cdot x},\hskip 15.0ptt>0

is the periodic heat kernel in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Using the Poisson summation formula, (2.1) can be written as

(2.2)

\displaystyle\theta(x,t)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\bigg(\frac{\pi}{t}\bigg)^{n}\exp\bigg[\frac{-|x+2\pi k|^{2}}{4t}\bigg],\hskip 15.0ptt>0.

With the use of (2.2), it is well known that

(2.3)

\displaystyle|e^{t\triangle}f|_{\infty}\leq|f|_{\infty},\hskip 15.0ptt\geq 0

and

(2.4)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{D}^{j}e^{t\triangle}f|_{\infty}\leq C_{j}t^{-j/2}|f|_{\infty}

for some
C_{j}>0
independent of
t
and
f
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f\in C^{\infty}_{per}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
then for any
j\geq 1

(2.5)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{D}^{j}e^{\triangle t}\mathbb{P}f|_{\infty}\leq C_{j}t^{-j/2}|f|_{\infty}\hskip 15.0pt\text{for}\hskip 15.0ptt>0

for some constant
C_{j}>0
independent of
t
and
f
.

Proof.

Let us first denote
e^{\triangle t}f=\theta*f
where
\theta(x,t)
is given by (2.2). Let us denote the Fourier coefficient of a function by
\mathcal{F}
. For
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, notice
\mathcal{F}({\theta(x,t)})(\xi)=Ce^{-t|\xi|^{2}},t>0
, where
C
depends on the normalizing constant in the definition of the Fourier coefficient. In the proof of this lemma, we will allow the constant
C
to change line to line as per the need. Now, for any
t>0
, any choice of
k,l\in\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
; and for any multiindex
\alpha
such that
|\alpha|=j
, the operator
D^{j}e^{\triangle t}\mathbb{P}_{kl}
on the Fourier side is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(D^{j}e^{\triangle t}\mathbb{P}_{kl}f_{l})(\xi)

\displaystyle=(-i\xi)^{\alpha}\mathcal{F}(e^{\triangle t}\mathbb{P}_{kl}f_{l})(\xi)

\displaystyle=(-i\xi)^{\alpha}\mathcal{F}(\theta*\mathbb{P}_{kl}f_{l})(\xi)

\displaystyle=C(-i\xi)^{\alpha}\mathcal{F}(\theta(x,t))(\xi)\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{P}_{kl}f_{l})(\xi)

\displaystyle=C(-i\xi)^{\alpha}e^{-t|\xi|^{2}}\bigg(\delta_{kl}-\frac{\xi_{k}\xi_{l}}{|\xi|^{2}}\bigg)\mathcal{F}(f_{l})(\xi)

\displaystyle=C(-i\xi)^{\alpha}e^{-t|\xi|^{2}}\delta_{kl}\mathcal{F}(f_{l})(\xi)

\displaystyle-C(-i\xi)^{\alpha}\xi_{k}\xi_{l}\mathcal{F}(f_{l})(\xi)\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{-\tau|\xi|^{2}}d\tau.

Using Fourier expansion we can write

\displaystyle D^{j}(e^{\triangle t}\mathbb{P}_{kl}f_{l})(x)

\displaystyle=C\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(-i\xi)^{\alpha}\delta_{kl}e^{-t|\xi|^{2}}\mathcal{F}(f_{l})(\xi)e^{i\xi\cdot x}

\displaystyle+C\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(-i\xi)^{\alpha}(i\xi_{k})(i\xi_{l})\mathcal{F}(f_{l})(\xi)e^{i\xi\cdot x}\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{-\tau|\xi|^{2}}d\tau

\displaystyle=(-1)^{j}C\delta_{kl}D^{\alpha}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}e^{-t|\xi|^{2}}\mathcal{F}(f_{l})(\xi)e^{i\xi\cdot x}

\displaystyle+C(-1)^{j}\int_{t}^{\infty}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}e^{-\tau|\xi|^{2}}(i\xi)^{\alpha}(i\xi_{k})(i\xi_{l})\mathcal{F}(f_{l})(\xi)e^{i\xi\cdot x}d\tau

\displaystyle=(-1)^{j}C\delta_{kl}D^{\alpha}e^{\triangle t}f_{l}+(-1)^{j}C\int_{t}^{\infty}D^{\alpha}D_{k}D_{l}e^{\triangle\tau}f_{l}d\tau

\displaystyle=I_{1}+I_{2}.

From (2.4) we have
|I_{1}|_{\infty}\leq C_{j}t^{-j/2}|f_{l}|_{\infty}
.

By the use of (2.4) one more time we obtain

\displaystyle|I_{2}|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq C_{j}|f_{l}|_{\infty}\int_{t}^{\infty}\tau^{-(j+2)/2}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq C_{j}t^{-j/2}|f_{l}|_{\infty}.

Therefore

\displaystyle|D^{j}e^{\triangle t}\mathbb{P}_{kl}f_{l}|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq|I_{1}|_{\infty}+|I_{2}|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq C_{j}t^{-j/2}|f_{l}|_{\infty}.

Hence Lemma 2.1 is proved.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
g\in C^{\infty}_{per}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,T])
for some
T>0
, then the solution of

(2.6)

\displaystyle u_{t}=\triangle u+D_{i}\mathbb{P}g,\hskip 15.0ptu=0\hskip 15.0pt\text{at}\hskip 15.0ptt=0

satisfies

(2.7)

\displaystyle|u(t)|_{\infty}\leq Ct^{1/2}\mathop{\max}_{0\leq s\leq t}|g(s)|_{\infty}.

Proof.

The solution of (2.6) is given by

\displaystyle u(t)=\int_{0}^{t}e^{\triangle(t-s)}D_{i}\mathbb{P}g(u))(s)ds

and

\displaystyle|u(t)|_{\infty}\leq\int_{0}^{t}|e^{\triangle(t-s)}D_{i}\mathbb{P}g(u)(s)|_{\infty}ds.

After commuting
D_{i}
with the heat semi-group, we can use Lemma 2.1 to obtain

\displaystyle|u(t)|_{\infty}\leq\mathop{\max}_{0\leq s\leq t}|g(s)|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-1/2}ds.

Hence we obtain

\displaystyle|u(t)|_{\infty}\leq Ct^{1/2}\mathop{\max}_{0\leq s\leq t}|g(s)|_{\infty}.

∎

3.
Estimates for
u_{t}=\triangle u+D_{i}\mathbb{P}g(u)
: proof of Theorem 1.2

In this section we consider the system
u_{t}=\triangle u+D_{i}\mathbb{P}g(u)
with the initial condition
u=f
at
t=0
where
f\in C^{\infty}_{per}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. It is well-known that the solution is smooth
2\pi
periodic in a maximal interval
0\leq t<T_{f}
where
0<T_{f}\leq\infty
.

Let us consider
u
is the solution of the inhomogeneous equation

u_{t}=\triangle u+D_{i}\mathbb{P}(g(u(x,t)))
and recall
g(u)
is quadratic in
u
. Thus, there is a constant
C_{g}
such that we have the following:

(3.1)

\displaystyle|g(u)|\leq C_{g}|u|^{2},\hskip 15.0pt|g_{u}(u)|\leq C_{g}|u|,\hskip 15.0pt\text{for all}\hskip 15.0ptu\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

We first estimate the maximum norm of
u
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
C_{g}
denote the constant in (3.1) and let
C
denote the constant in (2.7); set
c_{0}=\frac{1}{16C^{2}C_{g}^{2}}
. Then we have
T_{f}>c_{0}/{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}
and

(3.2)

\displaystyle|u(t)|_{\infty}<2|f|_{\infty}\hskip 15.0pt\text{ for }\hskip 15.0pt0\leq t<\frac{c_{0}}{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}.

Proof.

Suppose (3.2) does not hold, then we can find the smallest time
t_{0}
such that
|u(t_{0})|_{\infty}=2|f|_{\infty}
. Since
t_{0}
is the smallest time so we have
t_{0}<c_{0}/|f|_{\infty}^{2}
. Now by (2.3) and (2.7) we have

\displaystyle 2|f|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=|u(t_{0})|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq|f|_{\infty}+Ct_{0}^{1/2}\mathop{\max}_{0\leq s\leq t_{0}}|g(s)|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq|f|_{\infty}+CC_{g}t_{0}^{1/2}\mathop{\max}_{0\leq s\leq t_{0}}|u(s)|_{\infty}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq|f|_{\infty}+CC_{g}t_{0}^{1/2}4|f|_{\infty}^{2}.

This gives

\displaystyle 1\leq 4CC_{g}t_{0}^{1/2}|f|_{\infty},

therefore
t_{0}\geq 1/{(16C^{2}C_{g}^{2}|f|_{\infty}^{2})}={c_{0}}/{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}
which is a contradiction. There (3.2) must hold. The estimate
T_{f}>c_{0}/{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}
is valid since
\limsup_{t\to T_{f}}|u(t)|_{\infty}=\infty
if
T_{f}
is finite.
∎

Now we prove estimate (1.11) of Theorem 1.2 by induction on
j
. Let
j\geq 1
, and assume

(3.3)

\displaystyle t^{k/2}|\mathcal{D}^{k}u(t)|_{\infty}\leq K_{k}|f|_{\infty},\hskip 15.0pt\text{for}\hskip 15.0pt0\leq t\leq\frac{c_{0}}{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}\hskip 15.0pt\text{and}\hskip 15.0pt0\leq k\leq j-1.

Let us apply
D^{j}
to the equation
u_{t}=\triangle u+D_{i}\mathbb{P}g(u)
to obtain

\displaystyle v_{t}=\triangle v+D^{j+1}\mathbb{P}g(u),\hskip 15.0ptv:=D^{j}u,

\displaystyle v(t)=D^{j}e^{\triangle t}f+\int_{0}^{t}e^{\triangle(t-s)}D^{j+1}(\mathbb{P}g(u))(s)ds.

Using (2.4) we get

(3.4)

\displaystyle t^{j/2}|v(t)|_{\infty}\leq C|f|_{\infty}+t^{j/2}\biggl|\int_{0}^{t}e^{\triangle(t-s)}D^{j+1}(\mathbb{P}g(u))(s)ds\biggr|_{\infty}.

We split the integral into

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t/2}+\int_{t/2}^{t}=:I_{1}+I_{2}

and obtain

\displaystyle|I_{1}(t)|

\displaystyle=\biggl|\int_{0}^{t/2}D^{j+1}e^{\triangle(t-s)}(\mathbb{P}g(u))(s)ds\biggr|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t/2}|D^{j+1}e^{\triangle(t-s)}(\mathbb{P}g(u))(s)ds|_{\infty}ds.

Using the inequality (2.5) in Lemma 2.1, we get

\displaystyle|I_{1}(t)|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t/2}(t-s)^{-(j+1)/2}|g(u(s))|_{\infty}ds

\displaystyle\leq C|f|_{\infty}^{2}t^{(1-j)/2}.

The integrand in
I_{2}
has singularity at
s=t
. Therefore, we can move only one derivative from
D^{j+1}\mathbb{P}g(u)
to the heat semigroup.( If we move two or more derivatives then the singularity becomes non-integrable.) Thus, we have

\displaystyle|I_{2}(t)|_{\infty}=\biggl|-\int_{t/2}^{t}De^{\triangle(t-s)}(D^{j}\mathbb{P}g(u))(s)ds\biggr|_{\infty}.

Since the Leray projector commutes with any order derivatives, therefore

\displaystyle|I_{2}(t)|_{\infty}=\bigg|-\int_{t/2}^{t}De^{\triangle(t-s)}(\mathbb{P}D^{j}g(u))(s)ds\bigg|_{\infty}.

If we use Lemma 2.1 for
j=1
, we obtain

(3.5)

\displaystyle|I_{2}(t)|_{\infty}\leq C\int_{t/2}^{t}(t-s)^{-1/2}|D^{j}g(u)(s)|_{\infty}ds.

Since
g(u)
is quadratic in
u
, therefore

\displaystyle|D^{j}g(u)|_{\infty}\leq C|u|_{\infty}|\mathcal{D}^{j}u|_{\infty}+\sum_{k=1}^{j-1}|\mathcal{D}^{k}u|_{\infty}|\mathcal{D}^{j-k}u|_{\infty}.

By induction hypothesis (3.3) we obtain

(3.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{j-1}|\mathcal{D}^{k}u(s)|_{\infty}|\mathcal{D}^{j-k}u(s)|_{\infty}\leq Cs^{-j/2}|f|_{\infty}^{2}.

Expression in (3.5) can be estimated as below:

\displaystyle|I_{2}(t)|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{t/2}^{t}(t-s)^{-1/2}\bigg(C|u(s)|_{\infty}|\mathcal{D}^{j}u(s)|_{\infty}+\sum_{k=1}^{j-1}|\mathcal{D}^{k}u(s)|_{\infty}|\mathcal{D}^{j-k}u(s)|_{\infty}\bigg)ds

\displaystyle=J_{1}+J_{2}.

Using (3.6), and since
\int_{t/2}^{t}(t-s)^{-1/2}s^{-j/2}ds=Ct^{(1-j)/2}
, where
C
is independent of
t
, we obtain
|J_{2}(t)|_{\infty}\leq C|f|_{\infty}^{2}t^{(1-j)/2}
.

For
J_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle|J_{1}(t)|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=C\int_{t/2}^{t}(t-s)^{-1/2}|u(s)|_{\infty}|\mathcal{D}^{j}u(s)|_{\infty}ds

\displaystyle\leq C|f|_{\infty}\int_{t/2}^{t}(t-s)^{-1/2}s^{-j/2}s^{j/2}|\mathcal{D}^{j}u(s)|_{\infty}ds

\displaystyle\leq C|f|_{\infty}t^{(1-j)/2}\mathop{\max}_{0\leq s\leq t}\{s^{j/2}\mathcal{D}^{j}u(s)|_{\infty}\}.

We use these bounds to bound the integral in (3.4). We have
v=D^{j}u
. Then maximizing the resulting estimate for
t^{j/2}|D^{j}u(t)|_{\infty}
over all derivatives
D^{j}
of order
j
and setting

\displaystyle\phi(t):=t^{j/2}|\mathcal{D}^{j}u(t)|_{\infty}

and from (3.4), we obtain the following estimate

\displaystyle\phi(t)\leq C|f|_{\infty}+Ct^{1/2}|f|_{\infty}^{2}+C|f|_{\infty}t^{1/2}\mathop{\max}_{0\leq s\leq t}\phi(s)\hskip 15.0pt\text{for}\hskip 15.0pt0\leq t\leq\frac{c_{0}}{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}.

Since
t^{1/2}|f|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{c_{0}}
then
Ct^{1/2}|f|_{\infty}^{2}\leq C\sqrt{c_{0}}|f|_{\infty}
. Therefore

(3.7)

\displaystyle\phi(t)\leq C_{j}|f|_{\infty}+C_{j}|f|_{\infty}t^{1/2}\mathop{\max}_{0\leq s\leq t}\phi(s)\hskip 15.0pt\text{for}\hskip 15.0pt0\leq t\leq c_{0}/{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}.

Let us fix
C_{j}
so that the above estimate holds, and set

\displaystyle c_{j}=\min\bigg\{c_{0},\frac{1}{4C_{j}^{2}}\bigg\}.

First, let us prove the following

\displaystyle\phi(t)<2C_{j}|f|_{\infty}\hskip 15.0pt\text{for}\hskip 15.0pt0\leq t<\frac{c_{j}}{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}.

Suppose there is a smallest time
t_{0}
such that
0<t_{0}<c_{j}/{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}
with
\phi(t_{0})=2C_{j}|f|_{\infty}
. Then using (3.7) we obtain

\displaystyle 2C_{j}|f|_{\infty}=\phi(t_{0})\leq C_{j}|f|_{\infty}+2C_{j}^{2}|f|_{\infty}^{2}t_{0}^{1/2},

thus

\displaystyle 1\leq 2C_{j}|f|_{\infty}t_{0}^{1/2}\hskip 15.0pt\text{gives}\hskip 15.0ptt_{0}\geq c_{j}/{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}

which contradicts the assertion. Therefore, we proved the estimate

(3.8)

\displaystyle t^{j/2}|\mathcal{D}^{j}u(t)|_{\infty}\leq 2C_{j}|f|_{\infty}\hskip 15.0pt\text{for}\hskip 15.0pt0\leq t\leq c_{j}/{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}.

If

(3.9)

\displaystyle T_{j}:=\frac{c_{j}}{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}<t\leq\frac{c_{0}}{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}=:T_{0}

then we start the corresponding estimate at
t-T_{j}
. Using Lemma 3.1, we have
|u(t-T_{j})|_{\infty}\leq 2|f|_{\infty}
and obtain

(3.10)

\displaystyle T_{j}^{j/2}|\mathcal{D}^{j}u(t)|_{\infty}\leq 4C_{j}|f|_{\infty}.

Finally, for any
t
satisfying (3.9)

\displaystyle t^{j/2}\leq T_{0}^{j/2}=\bigg(\frac{c_{0}}{c_{j}}\bigg)^{j/2}T_{j}^{j/2}

and (3.10) yield

\displaystyle t^{j/2}|\mathcal{D}^{j}u(t)|_{\infty}\leq 4C_{j}\bigg(\frac{c_{0}}{c_{j}}\bigg)^{j/2}|f|_{\infty}.

This completes the proof of Theorem 1.2.

4.
Estimates For the Navier-Stokes Equations

Recall the transformed abstract ordinary differential equation (1.6)

(4.1)

\displaystyle u_{t}=\triangle u-\mathbb{P}(u\cdot\nabla u),\hskip 15.0pt\nabla\cdot u=0

with

(4.2)

\displaystyle u(x,0)=f(x).

Solution of (4.1) and (4.2) is given by

(4.3)

\displaystyle u(t)=e^{\triangle t}f-\int_{0}^{t}e^{\triangle(t-s)}\mathbb{P}(u\cdot\nabla u)(s)ds.

Using (4.3) with previous estimates (2.3), (2.4) and (2.5), we prove the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Set

(4.4)

\displaystyle V(t)=|u(t)|_{\infty}+t^{1/2}|\mathcal{D}u(t)|_{\infty},\hskip 15.0pt0<t<T(f).

There is a constant
C>0
, independent of
t
and
f
, so that

(4.5)

\displaystyle V(t)\leq C|f|_{\infty}+Ct^{1/2}\mathop{\max}_{0\leq s\leq t}{V^{2}(s)},\hskip 15.0pt0<t<T(f).

Proof.

Using estimate (2.3) of the heat equation in (4.3), we obtain

\displaystyle|u(t)|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq|f|_{\infty}+\bigg|\int_{0}^{t}e^{\triangle(t-s)}\mathbb{P}(u\cdot\nabla u)(s)ds\bigg|_{\infty}.

Apply identity
\mathbb{P}(u\cdot\nabla u)=\sum_{i}D_{i}\mathbb{P}(u_{i}u)
with the fact, heat semi-group commutes with
D_{i}
, then use of inequality (2.5) in Lemma 2.1 for
j=1
to proceed

\displaystyle|u(t)|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq|f|_{\infty}+C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-1/2}|u(s)|_{\infty}^{2}ds

\displaystyle=|f|_{\infty}+C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-1/2}s^{-1/2}s^{1/2}|u(s)|_{\infty}^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq|f|_{\infty}+C\mathop{\max}_{0\leq s\leq t}\{s^{1/2}|u(s)|_{\infty}^{2}\}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-1/2}s^{-1/2}ds.

Since
\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-1/2}s^{-1/2}ds=C>0
, which is independent of
t
, we have the following estimate

\displaystyle|u(t)|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq|f|_{\infty}+C\mathop{\max}_{0\leq s\leq t}\{s^{1/2}|u(s)|_{\infty}^{2}\}

(4.6)

\displaystyle|u(t)|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq|f|_{\infty}+Ct^{1/2}\mathop{\max}_{0\leq s\leq t}{V^{2}(s)}.

Apply
D_{i}
to (4.1), and the
Duhamel’s principle
to obtain

(4.7)

\displaystyle v(t)=D_{i}e^{\triangle t}f-\int_{0}^{t}e^{\triangle(t-s)}D_{i}\mathbb{P}(u\cdot\nabla)u(s)ds.

We can estimate the integral in (4.7) using Lemma 2.1 for
j=1
in the following way:

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{0}^{t}D_{i}e^{\triangle(t-s)}\mathbb{P}(u\cdot\nabla u)(s)ds\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}|D_{i}e^{\triangle(t-s)}\mathbb{P}(u\cdot\nabla u)(s)|ds

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-1/2}|u(s)|_{\infty}|\mathcal{D}u(s)|_{\infty}ds

\displaystyle=C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-1/2}s^{-1/2}s^{1/2}|u(s)|_{\infty}|\mathcal{D}u(s)|_{\infty}ds

\displaystyle\leq C\mathop{\max}_{0\leq s\leq t}{\{s^{1/2}|u(s)|_{\infty}|\mathcal{D}u(s)|_{\infty}\}}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-1/2}s^{-1/2}ds

\displaystyle\leq C\mathop{\max}_{0\leq s\leq t}{\{|u(s)|_{\infty}^{2}+s|\mathcal{D}u(s)|_{\infty}^{2}\}}.

Therefore, using (2.4)
j=1
in expression (4.7), we arrive at

\displaystyle|v(t)|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-1/2}|f|_{\infty}+C\mathop{\max}_{0\leq s\leq t}{\{|u(s)|_{\infty}^{2}+s|\mathcal{D}u(s)|_{\infty}^{2}\}}

(4.8)

\displaystyle t^{1/2}|\mathcal{D}u(t)|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq C|f|_{\infty}+Ct^{1/2}\mathop{\max}_{0\leq s\leq t}{V^{2}(t)}.

Using (4.6) and (4.8), we have proved Lemma 4.1.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
C>0
denote the constant in estimate (4.5) and set

\displaystyle c_{0}=\frac{1}{16C^{4}}.

Then
T_{f}>c_{0}/{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}
and

(4.9)

\displaystyle|u(t)|_{\infty}+t^{1/2}|\mathcal{D}u(t)|_{\infty}<2C|f|_{\infty}\hskip 15.0pt\text{for}\hskip 15.0pt0\leq t<\frac{c_{0}}{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}.

Proof.

We prove this lemma by contradiction after recalling the definition of
V(t)
in (4.4). Suppose that (4.9) does not hold, then denote by
t_{0}
the smallest time with
V(t_{0})=2C|f|_{\infty}
. Use (4.5) to obtain

\displaystyle 2C|f|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=V(t_{0})

\displaystyle\leq C|f|_{\infty}+Ct_{0}^{1/2}4C^{2}|f|_{\infty}^{2},

thus

\displaystyle 1\leq 4C^{2}t_{0}^{1/2}|f|_{\infty}^{2},

therefore
t_{0}\geq c_{0}/|f|_{\infty}^{2}
. This contradiction proves (4.9) and
T_{f}>c_{0}/{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}
.
∎

j=0
and
j=1

j=0,1,\cdots

Remarks

We can apply estimate (1.5) of Theorem 1.1 for

(5.1)

\displaystyle\frac{c_{0}}{2|f|_{\infty}^{2}}\leq t\leq\frac{c_{0}}{|f|_{\infty}^{2}}

and obtain

(5.2)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{D}^{j}u(t)|_{\infty}\leq C_{j}|f|_{\infty}^{j+1}

in interval (5.1). Starting the estimate at
t_{0}\in[0,T_{f})
we have

(5.3)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{D}^{j}u(t_{0}+t)|_{\infty}\leq C_{j}|u(t_{0})|_{\infty}^{j+1}

for

(5.4)

\displaystyle\frac{c_{0}}{2|u(t_{0})|_{\infty}^{2}}\leq t\leq\frac{c_{0}}{|u(t_{0})|_{\infty}^{2}}.

Then, if
t_{1}
is fixed with

(5.5)

\displaystyle\frac{c_{0}}{2|f|_{\infty}^{2}}\leq t_{1}<T_{f},

we can maximize both sides of (5.3) over
0\leq t_{0}\leq t_{1}
and obtain

(5.6)

\displaystyle\max\bigg\{|\mathcal{D}^{j}u(t)|_{\infty}:\frac{c_{0}}{2|f|_{\infty}^{2}}\leq t\leq t_{1}+\tau\bigg\}\leq C_{j}\max\{|u(t)|_{\infty}^{j+1}:0\leq t\leq t_{1}\}

with

\displaystyle\tau=\frac{c_{0}}{|u(t_{1})|_{\infty}^{2}}

Estimate (5.6) says, essentially, that the maximum of the
j
-th derivatives of
u
measured by
|\mathcal{D}^{j}u|_{\infty}
, can be bounded in terms of
|u|_{\infty}^{j+1}
.
The positive value of
\tau
on the left-hand side of (5.6) shows that
|u|_{\infty}^{j+1}
controls
|\mathcal{D}^{j}u|_{\infty}
for some time into the future.

As is well known, if
(u,p)
solves the Navier-Stokes equations and
\lambda>0
is any scaling parameter, then the functions
u_{\lambda},p_{\lambda}
defined by

\displaystyle u_{\lambda}(x,t)=\lambda u(\lambda x,\lambda^{2}t),\hskip 15.0ptp_{\lambda}(x,t)=\lambda^{2}p(\lambda x,\lambda^{2}t)

also solve the Navier-Stokes equations. Clearly,

\displaystyle|u_{\lambda}(t)|_{\infty}=\lambda|u(\lambda^{2}t)|_{\infty},\hskip 15.0pt|\mathcal{D}^{j}u_{\lambda}(t)|_{\infty}=\lambda^{j+1}|\mathcal{D}^{j}u(\lambda^{2}t)|_{\infty}.

Therefore,
|\mathcal{D}^{j}u|_{\infty}
and
|u|_{\infty}^{j+1}
both scale like
\lambda^{j+1}
, which is, of course, consistent with the estimate (5.6). We do not know under what assumptions
|u|_{\infty}^{j+1}
can conversely be estimated in terms of
|\mathcal{D}^{j}u|_{\infty}
.

Abstract
. We study Hecke operators associated with curves over a non-archimedean local field K K and over the rings O / 𝔪 N O/{\mathfrak{m}}^{N} , where O ⊂ K O\subset K is the ring of integers. Our main result is commutativity of a certain “small” local Hecke algebra over O / 𝔪 N O/{\mathfrak{m}}^{N} , associated with a connected split reductive group G G such that [ G , G ] [G,G] is simple and simply connected. The proof uses a Hecke algebra associated with G ⁡ ( K ⁡ ( ( t ) ) ) G(K(\!(t)\!)) and a global argument involving G G -bundles on curves.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let

\int_{X(K)}E_{n,|\omega_{X}|}(\phi)=\frac{1}{|O/{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}|^{\dim X}}\sum_{x\in X(O/{\mathfrak{m}}^{n})}\phi(x).

[

Then we have the corresponding compact open neighborhood

and our statement reduces to the statement that

obtained from the canonical trivialization of
|\omega_{X}|

We can choose near

3.4.
Local Hecke algebras over

and

The analogs of the constructions and results of Sections
3.1
and
3.2
also hold if we replace

Namely, we consider the group

Similarly to Definition

with the algebra

of

-biinvariant distributions with compact support on

G(O/{\mathfrak{m}}^{N}(\!(t)\!))
. Indeed,
the group

acts transitively on the fibers of the map

are in bijection with the double

Since the delta-functions of the former orbits form a basis in

, while the delta-functions of the latter double cosets form a basis in

we get an identification

\nu:{\mathcal{H}}({\mathbb{G}}_{N},{\mathbb{H}}_{N})\rTo{\sim}{\mathcal{H}}_{G,O/{\mathfrak{m}}^{N}}.

More explicitly, given a
G(O/{\mathfrak{m}}^{N}[\![t]\!])
-invariant functional
\delta
on

, we construct a

-biinvariant distribution on

as follows: starting with a smooth function on
G(O/{\mathfrak{m}}^{N}(\!(t)\!))
we restrict it to

G(O/{\mathfrak{m}}^{N}(\!(t)\!))_{\leq\lambda}
, the preimage of
\overline{\operatorname{Gr}}_{\lambda}(O/{\mathfrak{m}}^{N})
, then integrate along the fibers of the map

\delta
to the resulting function.

Proposition 3.6
.

The map
\nu
is an isomorphism of algebras.

Proof.

To understand multiplication on
{\mathcal{H}}({\mathbb{G}}_{N},{\mathbb{H}}_{N})
let us look again at the isomorphism

{\operatorname{End}}_{{\operatorname{Rep}}({\mathbb{G}}_{N})}(i_{{\mathbb{H}}_{N}}^{{\mathbb{G}}_{N}}(\C))\rTo{\sim}\varinjlim\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathbb{H}}_{N}}({\mathcal{S}}_{\lambda,N},\C),

where

(these are finite dimensional

-representations).
We have
i_{{\mathbb{H}}_{N}}^{{\mathbb{G}}_{N}}(\C)=``\varprojlim"{\mathcal{S}}_{\lambda,N}
,
and an endomorphism

of this pro-vector space is given by a compatible collection

The fact that

commutes with the
{\mathbb{G}}_{N}
-action allows us to recover
(h_{\lambda})
from

.
Namely, for each
\lambda,\mu

, induces a map

\alpha:\overline{\operatorname{Gr}}_{\lambda}(O/{\mathfrak{m}}^{N})\times{\mathcal{S}}_{\psi(\lambda,\mu),N}\to({\mathcal{S}}_{\mu,N})_{{\mathbb{H}}_{N}},

Now, for

Thus,

The latter definition is compatible with the usual convolution of distributions on
{\mathbb{G}}_{N}

∎

A.2.2.
Lifting

-bundles to
B
-bundles

The idea of our construction of a nice

T
-bundle, choose its lifting to a
B
-bundle
(with sufficiently generic behavior) and then take the induced
G
-bundle.
In this subsection we fix some notation about Cech representatives of various principal bundles that will repeatedly appear in the rest of this section and explain how a twisted unipotent bundle encodes the lifts of a
T
-bundle to
B
-bundles.

The conjugation action of
T
on
N
induces a map
H^{1}(C,T)\to H^{1}(C,\text{Aut}(N))
. Thus, with every class
\gamma\in H^{1}(C,T)
we can associate a sheaf of groups
N^{\gamma}

over
C
, which is a form of
N
(i.e., locally isomorphic to
N
).

Let
\mathcal{F}_{T}
be a
T-
bundle on
C
given by the Cech
1
-cocycle
(\gamma_{ij}\in T(U_{ij}))
with respect to some open covering
(U_{i})
.
To lift the
T
-bundle
\mathcal{F}_{T}
to a
B
-bundle, it suffices to choose an
N^{\gamma}
-bundle. Indeed, using the decomposition
B=T\cdot N
, we can define a
B
-bundle by a Cech
1
-cocycle

\sigma_{ij}=\gamma_{ij}\beta_{ij}
, where
\beta_{ij}\in N(U_{ij})
. The cocycle condition
\sigma_{ij}|_{U_{ijk}}\sigma_{jk}|_{U_{ijk}}=\sigma_{ik}|_{U_{ijk}}
is equivalent to

(\gamma_{jk}^{-1}|_{U_{ijk}}\beta_{ij}|_{U_{ijk}}\gamma_{jk}|_{U_{ijk}})\beta_{jk}|_{U_{ijk}}=\beta_{ik}|_{U_{ijk}}.

In other words,
(\beta_{ij})
is a
\gamma
-twisted
1
-cocycle, so it corresponds to an element in
H^{1}(C,N^{\gamma})
.

Lemma A.4

For

and

\pi_{\alpha}(\operatorname{Ad}(n)(t)-t)=d\alpha(t)\pi_{\alpha}(n),

where

Proof.

We will repeatedly use the fact (see

shows that the left-hand side indeed depends only
on

.
Also, since the adjoint action of

is trivial,

\operatorname{Ad}(n_{1}n_{2})(t)-t=\operatorname{Ad}(n_{1})(\operatorname{Ad}(n_{2})(t)-t)+\operatorname{Ad}(n_{1})(t)-t

Thus, we can start with

, where
\alpha^{\prime}\in\Delta

depend on

, so we are reduce to the rank

{\mathbb{P}}^{1}

Let

We keep the notation of Sec.
A.2.1

the coweight lattice of
T

Given a
B
-bundle
F
on a curve
C
, for every
\alpha\in X^{\bullet}(T)
we define the degree
\deg_{\alpha}(F)\in\Z
as degree of the line bundle associated with
F
and
the homomorphism
B\to T\to{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
given by
\alpha
.

Proposition A.6
.

For any positive integer
m
, there exists a
B
-bundle
F
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
such that the induced
G
-bundle is trivial and
\deg_{\alpha}(F)<-m
for all
\alpha\in\Delta
.

Proof.

We use the standard open cover
(U_{i})_{i=0,1}
of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, where
U_{0}
(resp.
U_{1}
) the affine line
\operatorname{Spec}k[t]
(resp.
\operatorname{Spec}k[t^{-1}]
), and
U_{01}=\operatorname{Spec}k[t,t^{-1}]
the intersection. Let us consider the
B
-bundle
F
on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, trivial over
U_{0}
and
U_{1}
, with a transition function
\gamma\cdot\beta\in B(k[t,t^{-1}])
,
where
\gamma\in T(k[t,t^{-1}])
,
\beta\in N(k[t,t^{-1}])
. We will choose
\gamma
to be a coweight, i.e., a homomorphism
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\to T
.
Then the requirements on
\gamma
and
\beta
are that
\langle\gamma,\alpha\rangle<-m
for every
\alpha\in\Delta
, and that there exist
\phi_{0}\in G(k[t])
and
\phi_{1}\in G(k[t^{-1}])
, such that

\gamma\beta=\phi_{0}\phi_{1}

(i.e., the induced

-bundle is trivial).
The existence of such data follows from a more precise Lemma

below: in the notations of this lemma we take
\beta=n_{+}^{-1}
,
\phi_{0}=n_{-}
,
\phi_{1}=g
.
∎

Lemma A.7
.

Let
G
be a connected split reductive group. For any
m>0
, there exists
\gamma\in X_{\bullet}(T)\subset T(k[t^{\pm}])
such that
\langle\gamma,\alpha\rangle<-m
for every
\alpha\in\Delta
and admits a decomposition
\gamma=n_{-}gn_{+}
for some
n_{-}\in N_{-}(k[t])
,
g\in G(k[t^{-1}])
and
n_{+}\in N(k[t])
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the assertion for
[G,G]
instead of
G
, so we can assume
G
to be semisimple. Furthermore, by considering (commuting) simple subgroups of
G
corresponding to the simple factors of
{\mathfrak{g}}
, we reduce to the case of a simple
G
.

We will use induction on the rank with the following induction step (we will prove the base case later).
Let
I\subset\Delta
denote the set of simple roots (identified with the nodes in the Dynkin diagram) to which the affine root attaches on the extended Dynkin diagram
(note that
|I|=1
unless
G
is of type
A
).
Let
P
be the standard parabolic subgroup of
G
that corresponds to the diagram with the subset
I
removed. Also, let
L
denote the derived subgroup of the Levi subgroup corresponding to
P
, and let
T_{L}
denote a maximal torus of
L
. Assuming that the assertion holds for smaller rank, we have
\gamma_{L}\in X_{\bullet}(T_{L})
such that
\langle\gamma_{L},\alpha\rangle<-m
for all
\alpha\in\Delta(L)=\Delta\setminus i
, and

\gamma_{L}=n_{-}gn_{+}

for some
n_{-}\in(N_{-})_{L}(k[t])
,
n_{+}\in N_{L}(k[t])
and
g\in L(k[t^{-1}])
, where
N_{L}
(resp.
(N_{-})_{L}
) denote the corresponding unipotent subgroup (resp. opposite unipotent subgroup).

Let
\lambda
be the highest positive root of
G
,
\lambda^{\vee}
the corresponding coroot.
Then we have

\langle\lambda^{\vee},\alpha\rangle=\begin{cases}p&\alpha\in I\\
0&\alpha\in\Delta-I\end{cases},

where
p
is equal to
1
or
2
.
Hence, there exists a positive integer
c
such that

\langle\gamma_{L}-c\lambda^{\vee},\alpha\rangle<-m,

for all
\alpha\in\Delta
.

Let us denote
G_{\lambda}
be the subgroup of
G
generated by the root groups
U_{\lambda}
and
U_{-\lambda}
.
It follows from the rank one case that there exists a decomposition in
G_{\lambda}(k[t,t^{-1}]
,

-c\lambda^{\vee}=n^{\prime}_{-}g^{\prime}n^{\prime}_{+}

for some
n^{\prime}_{-}\in U_{-\lambda}(k[t])
,
n^{\prime}_{+}\in U_{\lambda}(k[t])

and
g^{\prime}\in G_{\lambda}(k[t^{-1}])
.

Finally, we claim that the coweight
\gamma_{L}-c\lambda^{\vee}\in X_{\bullet}(T)
has the desired decomposition.
Indeed, since
\lambda
is orthogonal to
\alpha

\alpha\in\Delta-I

[

\gamma_{L}-c\lambda^{\vee}=(n_{-}n^{\prime}_{-})(gg^{\prime})(n_{+}n_{+}^{\prime})

Since in our induction step we have
|I|=1

A

and
G={\operatorname{SL}}_{3}

These cases follow from the identities

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}t^{-m}&0\\
0&t^{m}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
-t^{m}+1&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}t^{-m}&-t^{-m}-1\\
-t^{-m}+1&t^{-m}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&t^{m}+1\\
0&1\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}t^{-m}&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&t^{m}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
-t^{m}+1&0&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}t^{-m}&0&-t^{-m}-1\\
0&1&0\\
-t^{-m}+1&0&t^{-m}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&0&t^{m}+1\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix}.

∎

Abelian category of cominimax modules and local cohomology
Moharram Aghapournahr

Abstract
. Let R R be a commutative Noetherian ring, 𝔞 \mathfrak{a} an ideal of R R , M M an arbitrary R R -module and X X a finite R R -module. We prove that the category of 𝔞 \mathfrak{a} -cominimax modules is a Melkersson subcategory of R R -modules whenever dim R ≤ 1 \dim R\leq 1 and is an Abelian subcategory whenever dim R ≤ 2 \dim R\leq 2 . We prove a characterization theorem for H 𝔞 i ⁡ ( M ) \lc_{\mathfrak{a}}^{i}(M) and H 𝔞 i ⁡ ( X , M ) \lc_{\mathfrak{a}}^{i}(X,M) to be 𝔞 \mathfrak{a} -cominimax for all i i , whenever one of the following cases holds: (a) ara ⁡ ( 𝔞 ) ≤ 1 \ara(\mathfrak{a})\leq 1 , (b) dim R / 𝔞 ≤ 1 \dim R/\mathfrak{a}\leq 1 or (c) dim R ≤ 2 \dim R\leq 2 .

1.
Introduction

Throughout this paper
R
is a commutative Noetherian ring with non-zero
identity and
\mathfrak{a}
an ideal of
R
. For an

-module

i^{th}

local cohomology module

\lc^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)\cong\underset{n}{\varinjlim}\Ext^{i}_{R}(R/{\mathfrak{a}}^{n},M).

Also the generalized local
cohomology module

\lc^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(X,M)\cong\underset{n}{\varinjlim}\Ext^{i}_{R}(X/{\mathfrak{a}}^{n}X,M).

for all
R
–modules

and

was introduced by Herzog in
[
19
]
.
Clearly it is a generalization of ordinary local cohomology module.

We refer the reader to
[
11
]
for more details about the local cohomology.

In
[
16
]
, Grothendieck conjectured that for any ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
R
and any finitely generated
R
-module

,

\Hom_{R}(R/\mathfrak{a},\lc^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))
is a finitely generated

-module for all

. Hartshorne

provided a
counterexample to Grothendieck’s conjecture. He defined an

-module

to be

(i)
For which rings

and ideals
\mathfrak{a}
are the modules
\lc^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)

\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite for all

and all finitely generated
modules

?

(ii)
Whether the category
\mathscr{C}(R,\mathfrak{a})_{cof}

of
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite modules forms an Abelian subcategory of the category of all
R
-modules?
That is, if

is an
R
-homomorphism of

\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite modules, are
\Ker f
and
{\Coker}f

\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite?

With respect to the question (i), there are several papers devoted to this question; for example see
[

,

,
25
,
9
,
8
,

With respect to the question (ii), Hartshorne with an example showed that this is

Recall that a module

[

[

Since the class of minimax modules is a generalization of Matlis reflexive modules, thus the study of minimax modules is as important as the study of Matlis reflexive modules. As a generalization of

-cofinite modules

-cominimax

Since the concept of minimax modules is a natural generalization of the finitely generated modules,
many authors studied the minimaxness and cominimaxness of local cohomology modules and answered the Hartshorne’s
question in the class of minimax modules (see for example
[
1
,
2
,
3
,

,

,

,

,

Recall also that a class of

-modules is said to be a

-torsion

2.
Prelaminaries

We begin this section with some preliminaries which are needed in the proof of main results of the paper. The following remark is some elementary properties of the class of minimax
R
-modules which we shall use.

Remark 2.1
.

The following statement holds:

(i)

The class of minimax modules contains all finitely generated and all Artinian modules;

(ii)

Let
0\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow 0
be an exact sequence of

R
-modules. Then
M
is minimax if and only if
L
and
N
are both minimax
(
see
[
9
, Lemma 2.1]
)
. Thus any
submodule and quotient of a minimax module is minimax;

(iii)

The set of associated primes of any minimax
R
-module is finite;

(iv)

Every zero-dimensional minimax
R
-module is Artinian;

(v)

If
M
is a minimax
R
-module and
\mathfrak{p}
is a non-maximal prime ideal of
R
, then
M_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a finitely
generated
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
-module.

The following well-known lemma which is true even for an arbitrary Serre subcategory is needed frequently in this paper.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
M
is an
R
-module such that
(0:_{M}\mathfrak{a})
is minimax, then so is
(0:_{M}\mathfrak{a}^{n})
for each
n
.

Proof.

For each
n\geq 2
, there exists an exact sequence of modules

0\rightarrow(0:_{M}\mathfrak{a})\rightarrow(0:_{M}\mathfrak{a}^{n})\overset{f}{\rightarrow}a_{1}(0:_{M}\mathfrak{a}^{n})\oplus...\oplus a_{s}(0:_{M}\mathfrak{a}^{n})

where
\mathfrak{a}=(a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{s})
and
f(x)=(a_{1}x,...,a_{s}x)
. Since
a_{i}(0:_{M}\mathfrak{a}^{n})
is a submodule of the
minimax module
(0:_{M}\mathfrak{a}^{n-1})
for each
i
, the result is obtained by an easy induction on
n
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose
x\in\mathfrak{a}
and
\Supp_{R}(M)\subseteq\V(\mathfrak{a})
. If
0:_{M}x
and
M/xM
are both
\mathfrak{a}
-cominimax, then

must also be
\mathfrak{a}
-cominimax.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the proof of

, Corollary 3.4]
. ∎

Lemma 2.4

Let

be a finite

–module,

be an arbitrary

–module. Then the following statements hold true.

(a)

\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(X,M)\cong\Hom_{R}(X,\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)).

(b)

If

, then
\lc^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(X,M)\cong\Ext^{i}_{R}(X,M)
for all

.

Proof.

See
[
28

.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian ring with
\dim R\leq 2
. Let

\mathfrak{a}
be an ideal of
R
and
M
a non-zero minimax

-module. Then
\lc^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cominimax for

.

Proof.

It follows from Grothendieck’s Vanishing Theorem

3.
An Abelian category of cominimax modules

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}
be an ideal of
R
,
n
be a non-negative integer and
M
an
R
-module. We say that
R
admits
P^{\prime}_{n}(\mathfrak{a})

if for any
R
-module
M
, the following implication holds:

If
Ext_{R}^{i}(R/\mathfrak{a},M)
is minimax for all
i\leq n
and
\Supp(M)\subseteq\V(\mathfrak{a})
,

then
M
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cominimax.

We now present the first main theorem of this section which is generalization of
[
27
, Theorem 2.3]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian ring of dimension
d\geq 1
admitting the condition
P^{\prime}_{d-1}(\mathfrak{a})
for all ideals
\mathfrak{a}
of dimension
\leq d-1
(i.e.
\dim R/\mathfrak{a}\leq d-1
), then
R
admits the condition
P^{\prime}_{d-1}(\mathfrak{a})
for all ideals
\mathfrak{a}
of
R
.

Proof.

Assume that
M
is an
R
-module and
\mathfrak{a}
is an arbitrary ideal such that
\Supp(M)\subseteq\V(\mathfrak{a})
and
Ext_{R}^{i}(R/\mathfrak{a},M)

is minimax for all
i\leq d-1
. We show that
M
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cominimax. In the case where
\mathfrak{a}
is nilpotent, say
\mathfrak{a}^{n}=0

for some integer
n
, we have
M=(0:_{M}\mathfrak{a}^{n})
. Now, since
(0:_{M}\mathfrak{a})
is minimax, Lemma
2.2
implies that

M
is minimax and so
\V(\mathfrak{a})=\Spec R
forces that
M
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cominimax. Now suppose that
\mathfrak{a}
is not nilpotent. In
this case, we can choose a positive integer
n
such that
(0:_{R}\mathfrak{a}^{n})=\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)
. Put
\overline{R}=R/\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)
and
\overline{M}=M/(0:_{M}\mathfrak{a}^{n})
which is an
\overline{R}
-module. Taking
\overline{\mathfrak{a}}
as an image of
\mathfrak{a}
in

\overline{R}
, we have
\Gamma_{\overline{\mathfrak{a}}}(\overline{R})=0
. Thus
\overline{\mathfrak{a}}
contains an
\overline{R}
-regular element
so that
\dim R/\mathfrak{a}+\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)=\dim\overline{R}/\overline{\mathfrak{a}}\leq d-1
. The assumption on
M
together with the fact that

\Supp_{R}(R/\mathfrak{a}+\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(R))\subset\Supp_{R}(R/\mathfrak{a})
and
[
3
, Lemma 2.1]
imply that

\Ext_{R}^{i}(R/\mathfrak{a}+\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(R),M)
is minimax for all
i\leq d-1
. In view of Lemma
2.2
, the module

(0:_{M}\mathfrak{a}^{n})
is minimax and thus
\Ext_{R}^{i}(R/\mathfrak{a}+\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(R),\overline{M})
is minimax for all

i\leq d-1
. On the other hand, it is clear that
\Supp(\overline{M})\subseteq\V(\mathfrak{a}+\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(R))
and since by the assumption

R
admits
P^{\prime}_{d-1}(\mathfrak{a}+\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(R))
, the module
\overline{M}
is
\mathfrak{a}+\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)
-cominimax. Now using
the change of ring principle
[
6
, Theorem 4.8]
, the module
M
is an
\mathfrak{a}
-cominimax
R
-module; and finally the minimaxness of
(0:_{M}\mathfrak{a}^{n})
forces that
M
is an
\mathfrak{a}
-cominimax
R
-module.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian ring of dimension
2
. Then
R
admits the condition
P^{\prime}_{1}(\mathfrak{a})
for all ideals
\mathfrak{a}
of
R
.

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
[
22
, Lemma 2.4]
that
R
admits the condition
P^{\prime}_{1}(\mathfrak{a})
for all ideals
\mathfrak{a}
with
\dim R/\mathfrak{a}=1
. Thus the result follows by Theorem
3.2
.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring of dimension
3
. Then
R
admits the condition
P^{\prime}_{2}(\mathfrak{a})
for all ideals
\mathfrak{a}
of
R
.

Proof.

It follows from Theorem
[
22
, Theorem 2.6]
that
R
admits the condition
P^{\prime}_{2}(\mathfrak{a})
for all ideals
\mathfrak{a}
with
\dim R/\mathfrak{a}=2
. Thus the result follows by Theorem

.
∎

The following proposition shows that the class of minimax
R
-modules is a Melkersson subcategory of
R
-modules whenever
\dim R\leq 1
. Therefore, in this case, submodules and quotients modules of an
\mathfrak{a}
-cominimax modules are always
\mathfrak{a}
-cominimax.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian ring with
\dim R\leq 1
. Let
M
be an
R
-module such that
\Supp_{R}(M)\subseteq\V(\mathfrak{a})
and
(0:_{M}\mathfrak{a})
is a minimax
R
-modules. Then
M

is minimax. In this case the class of
\mathfrak{a}
-cominimax
R
-modules is a
Serre subcategory of
R
-modules.

Proof.

If
\mathfrak{a}
is nilpotent, then there is
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathfrak{a}^{n}=0
, so
M=(0:_{M}\mathfrak{a}^{n})
. By Lemma
2.2
, it follows
that
M
is minimax. Therefore suppose that
\mathfrak{a}
is not nilpotent. Since
R
is a Noetherian ring, thus there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such
that
(0:_{R}\mathfrak{a}^{n})=\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)
. Then it is easy to see that
\overline{M}=\dfrac{M}{(0:_{M}\mathfrak{a}^{n})}
is a module over the
ring
\overline{R}=\dfrac{R}{\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)}
. Let
\overline{\mathfrak{a}}
be the image of
\mathfrak{a}
in
\overline{R}
. Then
\overline{\mathfrak{a}}
contains
an
\overline{R}
-regular element and therefore
\dim\dfrac{\overline{R}}{\overline{\mathfrak{a}}}=0
. Suppose
(0:_{M}\mathfrak{a})
is minimax.
Then, in view of Lemma
2.2
, it follows that
(0:_{\overline{M}}\overline{\mathfrak{a}})=(\dfrac{0:_{{M}}{\mathfrak{a}}^{n+1}}{0:_{{M}}{\mathfrak{a}}^{n}})
is a minimax
R
-module. Since
(0:_{\overline{M}}\overline{\mathfrak{a}})
is an
\overline{R}
-module and

\overline{\mathfrak{a}}(0:_{\overline{M}}\overline{\mathfrak{a}})=0
, hence
(0:_{\overline{M}}\overline{\mathfrak{a}})
has
\dfrac{\overline{R}}{\overline{\mathfrak{a}}}
-module structure. Since
\dfrac{\overline{R}}{\overline{\mathfrak{a}}}
is an Artinian ring, so

\Supp_{{\overline{R}}/{\overline{\mathfrak{a}}}}(0:_{\overline{M}}\overline{\mathfrak{a}})\subseteq\Spec(\dfrac{\overline{R}}{\overline{\mathfrak{a}}})=\Max(\dfrac{\overline{R}}{\overline{\mathfrak{a}}})
.

Thus by Remark
2.1
(iv), it follows that
(0:_{\overline{M}}\overline{\mathfrak{a}})
is a Artinian
\dfrac{\overline{R}}{\overline{\mathfrak{a}}}
-module and therefore Artinian
\overline{R}
-module. Since
\Supp_{R}(\overline{M})\subseteq\Supp_{R}(M)\subseteq\V(\mathfrak{a})
, it is easy to see that
\Supp_{\overline{R}}(\overline{M})\subseteq\V(\overline{\mathfrak{a}})
, then by Melkersson’s theorem
[
11
, Theorem 7.1.2]
,
\overline{M}

is a Artinian
\overline{R}
-module. So
\overline{M}
is an Artinian
R
-module. Now Lemma
2.2
, Remark
2.1
(ii) and the exact sequence

0\rightarrow(0:_{M}\mathfrak{a}^{n})\rightarrow M\rightarrow\overline{M}\rightarrow 0

implies that
M
is minimax as required.
∎

In the following corollary, we look at the case
\dim R=2
. In this case we give a criterion for a quotient of an
\mathfrak{a}
-cominimax module to be
\mathfrak{a}
-cominimax.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
R
be a ring of dimension
2
. Let
M
be an
\mathfrak{a}
-cominimax module and
N
be a homomorphic image of
M
. Then
N
is

\mathfrak{a}
-cominimax if and only if
(0:_{N}\mathfrak{a})
is minimax.

Proof.

Let
f:M\rightarrow N
be a surjective
R
-linear map with
K=\Ker f
and let
0:_{N}\mathfrak{a}
be minimax. By the assumption

\Hom_{R}(A/\mathfrak{a},K)
and
\Ext_{R}^{1}(A/\mathfrak{a},K)
are minimax; and hence Corollary
3.3
implies that
K
is

\mathfrak{a}
-cominimax. Therefore
N
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cominimax.
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian ring with
\dim R\leq 2
. Then for any ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
R
, the full subcategory of
\mathfrak{a}
-cominimax
R
-modules of the category of
R
-modules
(
\mathscr{C}(R,\mathfrak{a})_{comin}
)
is Abelian.

Proof.

In view of Proposition
3.5
, we may assume that
\dim R=2
. Now, let
f:M\rightarrow N
be any
homomorphism between
\mathfrak{a}
-cominimax modules such that
K=\Ker f,I=\Image f
and
C=\Coker f
. According to the Corollary
3.6
, since
0:_{I}\mathfrak{a}
is minimax,
I
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cominimax; and therefore so is
K
by the exact sequence

0\rightarrow K\rightarrow M\rightarrow I\rightarrow 0
. on the other hand, the exact sequence
0\rightarrow I\rightarrow N\rightarrow C\rightarrow 0
force that
C
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cominimax. ∎

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian ring with
\dim R\leq 2
. Let

\mathfrak{a}
be an ideal of

\lc^{i}(X^{\bullet})
is in
X^{i}\in\mathscr{C}(R,\mathfrak{a})_{comin}
.

Proof.

The assertion follows from Corollary
3.7
.
∎

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian ring with
\dim R\leq 2
. Let

\mathfrak{a}
be an ideal of
R
and

is a non-zero
\mathfrak{a}
-cominimax

Since

,

and

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
M
be an
R
-module and suppose one of the following cases holds:

(a)

\ara(\mathfrak{a})\leq 1
;

(b)

\dim R/\mathfrak{a}\leq 1
;

(c)

\dim R\leq 2
.

Then,
\lc^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cominimax for all
i
if and only if
\Ext^{i}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{a},M)
is a minimax
R
-module for all
i
.

Proof.

The case (c) follows by Theorem
4.1
.

In the cases (a) and (b), suppose
\lc^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cominimax for all
i
. It follows from
[
7
, Proposition 3.7]
that
\Ext^{i}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{a},M)
is a minimax
R
-module for all
i
.

To prove the converse we use induction on
i
. Let
i=0
. From the exact

0\longrightarrow\G_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow M/\G_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)\longrightarrow 0,

we obtain that
{\Hom}_{R}(R/{\mathfrak{a}},\G_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))
and
{\Ext}^{1}_{R}(R/{\mathfrak{a}},\G_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))
are minimax. Now, it follows by
[
3
, Theorem 2.8]
In the case (a) and
[
22
, Lemma 2.4]
In the case (b) that
\G_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cominimax. It follows also that for all
i
the
R
-modules
{\Ext}^{i}_{R}(R/{\mathfrak{a}},M/\G_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))
are minimax. Let
i>0
and the case
i-1
is settled. Consider the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow M/\G_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow 0,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,(\star)

in which
E
is an injective
\mathfrak{a}
-torsion free module. It is easy to see that
\lc_{\mathfrak{a}}^{i}(E)=0={\Ext}^{i}_{R}(R/{\mathfrak{a}},E)
for all
i\geq 0
. Now, using the exact sequence
(\star)
, we easily get the isomorphisms

\lc_{\mathfrak{a}}^{i}(L)\cong\lc_{\mathfrak{a}}^{i+1}(M/\G_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))\cong\lc_{\mathfrak{a}}^{i+1}(M)
.

and

{\Ext}^{i}_{R}(R/{\mathfrak{a}},L)\cong{\Ext}^{i+1}_{R}(R/{\mathfrak{a}},M/\G_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))
.

Hence the hypothesis is satisfied by
L
. This completes the inductive step.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
M
be an
R
-module and suppose one of the following cases holds:

(a)

\ara(\mathfrak{a})\leq 1
;

(b)

\dim R/\mathfrak{a}\leq 1
;

(c)

\dim R\leq 2
.

Then, for any finite
R
-module
X
,
\lc^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(X,M)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cominimax for all
i\geq 0
if and only if
\Ext^{i}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{a},M)
is minimax
R
-module for all
i\geq 0
.

Proof.

First suppose for any finite
R
-module
X
,
\lc^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(X,M)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cominimax for all
i\geq 0
. Let
X=R
, then it follows by Theorem
4.2
, that
\Ext^{i}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{a},M)
is minimax
R
-module for all
i\geq 0
.

To prove the converse we use the induction on
i
. Let
i=0
, then it follows by Lemma
2.4
(a) that

\lc^{0}_{\mathfrak{a}}(X,M)=\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(X,M)\cong\Hom_{R}(X,\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)).

Since
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cominimax by Theorem
4.2
,
so the assertion follows by Corollary
3.10
,
[
3
, Corollary 2.11]
and
[
21
, Corollary 2.8]
. Now assume that
i>0
and that the claim holds for
i-1
. Since
\G_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cominimax and
\Ext^{i}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{a},M)
is minimax
R
-module for all
i\geq 0
. Using the short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\G_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow M/\G_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)\longrightarrow 0,

it is easy to see that the
R
-modules
{\Ext}^{i}_{R}(R/{\mathfrak{a}},M/\G_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))
are minimax for all
i\geq 0
. Now, by applying the derived functor
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(X,-)
to the same short exact sequence and using Lemma
2.4
(b), we obtain the long exact sequence

\dots\longrightarrow\Ext_{R}^{i}(X,\G_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))\overset{f_{i}}{\longrightarrow}\lc^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(X,M)\overset{g_{i}}{\longrightarrow}\lc^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(X,M/\G_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))\overset{h_{i}}{\longrightarrow}\Ext_{R}^{i}(X,\G_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))\overset{f_{i+1}}{\longrightarrow}\lc^{i+1}_{\mathfrak{a}}(X,M)\longrightarrow\dots

which yields short exact sequences

0\longrightarrow\Ker{f_{i}}\longrightarrow\Ext_{R}^{i}(X,\G_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))\longrightarrow\Image{f_{i}}\longrightarrow 0,

0\longrightarrow\Image{f_{i}}\longrightarrow\lc^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(X,M)\longrightarrow\Image{g_{i}}\longrightarrow 0

and

0\longrightarrow\Image{g_{i}}\longrightarrow\lc^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(X,M/\G_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))\longrightarrow\Ker{f_{i+1}}\longrightarrow 0.

Since
\Ext_{R}^{i}(X,\G_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cominimax
R
-module for all
i\geq 0
by Corollary
3.10
,
[
3
, Corollary 2.11]
and
[
21
, Corollary 2.8]
, it follows by definition that,
\lc^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(X,M)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cominimax
R
-module if and only if
\lc^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(X,M/\G_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cominimax
R
-module for all
i\geq 0
. Therefore it suffices to show that
\lc^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(X,M/\G_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cominimax
R
-module for all
i\geq 0
. To this end consider the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow M/\G_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow 0,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,(\dagger)

in which
E
is an injective
\mathfrak{a}
-torsion free module. Since

\G_{\mathfrak{a}}(M/\G_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))=0=\G_{\mathfrak{a}}(E)
, thus
\Hom_{R}(R/\mathfrak{a},E)=0
and
\G_{\mathfrak{a}}(X,M/\G_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))=0=\G_{\mathfrak{a}}(X,E)
by Lemma
2.4
(a). Applying the derived functors of
\Hom_{R}(R/\mathfrak{a},-)
and
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(X,-)
to the short exact sequence
(\dagger)

we obtain, for all
i>0
, the isomorphisms

\lc^{i-1}_{\mathfrak{a}}(X,L)\cong\lc^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(X,M/\G_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))
.

and

{\Ext}^{i-1}_{R}(R/{\mathfrak{a}},L)\cong{\Ext}^{i}_{R}(R/{\mathfrak{a}},M/\G_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))
.

From what has already been proved, we conclude that
{\Ext}^{i-1}_{R}(R/{\mathfrak{a}},L)
is minimax for all
i>0
. Hence
\lc^{i-1}_{\mathfrak{a}}(X,L)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cominimax by induction hypothesis for all
i>0
, which yields that
\lc^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(X,M/\G_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cominimax for all
i\geq 0
, this completes the inductive step.
∎

.

Let

be an
R
-module and suppose one of the following cases holds:

(a)

\ara(\mathfrak{a})\leq 1
;

(b)

\dim R/\mathfrak{a}\leq 1
;

(c)

\dim R\leq 2
.

Then the following conditions
are equivalent:

(i)

\Ext^{i}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{a},M)
is minimax for all
i
.

(ii)

\Ext^{i}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{a},M)

in the cases
(
a
)
and
(
b
)

(
resp. for
i=0,1,2
in the case
(
c
))
;

\lc^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cominimax for all
i
;

(iv)

\lc^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(X,M)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cominimax
for all
i
and for any finite
R
-module
X
;

(v)

\Ext^{i}_{R}(X,M)
is minimax for all
i
and for any finite
R
-module
X
with
\Supp_{R}(X)\subseteq\V(\mathfrak{a})
;

(vi)

\Ext^{i}_{R}(X,M)
is minimax for all
i
and for some finite
R
-module
X
with
\Supp_{R}(X)=\V(\mathfrak{a})
;

(vii)

\Ext^{i}_{R}(X,M)
is minimax for
i=0,1
in the cases
(
a
)
and
(
b
)

(
resp. for
i=0,1,2
in the case
(
c
))
and for any finite
R
-module
X
with
\Supp_{R}(X)\subseteq\V(\mathfrak{a})
;

(viii)

\Ext^{i}_{R}(X,M)
is minimax for
i=0,1
in the cases
(
a
)
and
(
b
)

(
resp. for
i=0,1,2
in the case
(
c
))
and for some finite
R
-module
X
with
\Supp_{R}(X)=\V(\mathfrak{a})

In order to prove (i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii), use
[
3
, Theorem 2.5]
and

, Lemma 2.4]

4.1

(i)
\Leftrightarrow
(iii) follows by Theorem
4.2
.

(i)
\Leftrightarrow
(iv) follows by Theorem

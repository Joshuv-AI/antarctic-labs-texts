Learned transform compression with optimized entropy encoding.
Magda Gregorová & Marc Desaules & Alexandros Kalousis

Abstract
We consider the problem of learned transform compression where we learn both, the transform as well as the probability distribution over the discrete codes. We utilize a soft relaxation of the quantization operation to allow for back-propagation of gradients and employ vector (rather than scalar) quantization of the latent codes. Furthermore, we apply similar relaxation in the code probability assignments enabling direct optimization of the code entropy. To the best of our knowledge, this approach is completely novel. We conduct a set of proof-of concept experiments confirming the potency of our approaches.

Introduction

We consider the problem of compressing data
{\bm{x}}\in{\mathbb{X}}

We take the standard transform coding

We then quantize the transformed data

\overline{{\mathcal{Q}}_{\bm{E}}}:{\mathbb{C}}\to\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}\subset{\mathbb{Z}}

\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}\subset{\mathbb{Z}}
corresponding to the quantization embeddings.
The dequantized data

to obtain the reconstructions
\hat{{\bm{x}}}={\mathcal{D}}_{\phi}(\hat{{\bm{z}}})

Our aim is to learn the transform (encoder/decoder) as well as the quantization so as to minimize the expected distortion

Quantization

We employ the soft relaxation approach to quantization proposed in
Agustsson et al. (2017)
simplified similarly to
Mentzer et al. (2018)
.
However, instead of the scalar version
Mentzer et al. (2018)
;
Habibian et al. (2019)

k

\{{\bm{e}}^{(j)}\}_{j=1}^{k},\ {\bm{e}}_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}

{\bm{E}}=[{\bm{e}}^{(1)},\ldots,{\bm{e}}^{(k)}]

The quantizer
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\bm{E}}

5

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{\bm{E}}:\quad\hat{{\bm{z}}}^{(i)}=\argmin_{{\bm{e}}^{(j)}}\|{\bm{z}}^{(i)}-{\bm{e}}^{(j)}\|,\qquad{c}^{(i)}=\{j:\hat{{\bm{z}}}^{(i)}={\bm{e}}^{(j)}\},\quad i=1,\ldots,d/m\kern 5.0pt.

Minimizing the code cross-entropy

Though the optimal lossless encoding is decided by the self-information of the code
-\log p_{c}({\bm{c}})
, it cannot be used directly since
p_{c}
is unknown.
Instead we replace the unknown
p_{c}
by its estimated approximation

q_{c}
, derive the code length
\hat{l}({\bm{c}})
from
\hat{i}_{c}=-\log q_{c}({\bm{c}})
, and therefore minimize the expected approximate self-information, the cross-entropy
{\mathbb{H}}_{\mu_{c}|q_{c}}(\mathtt{c})=-\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{c}}\log q_{c}(\mathtt{c})
.
This, however, yields inefficiencies as
{\mathbb{H}}_{\mu_{c}|q_{c}}(\mathtt{c})\geq{\mathbb{H}}_{\mu_{c}}(\mathtt{c})
due to the decomposition

\displaystyle{\mathbb{H}}_{\mu_{c}|q_{c}}(\mathtt{c})=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{c}\|q_{c})+{\mathbb{H}}_{\mu_{c}}(\mathtt{c})\kern 5.0pt,

(5)

where
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\geq 0
is the Kullback-Leibler divergence between
p_{c}
and
q_{c}
which can be interpreted as the expected additional bits over the optimal rate
{\mathbb{H}}_{\mu_{c}}({\bm{c}})
caused by using
q_{c}
instead of the true
p_{c}
.
In addition to
{\mathcal{E}}_{\theta}
,
{\mathcal{D}}_{\phi}
and
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\bm{E}}
we shall now therefore train also a probability estimator
{\mathcal{P}}_{\psi}:\{{\bm{c}}\}_{i}^{n}\to q_{c}
by minimizing the cross-entropy
{\mathbb{H}}_{\mu_{c}|q_{c}}(\mathtt{c})
so that the estimated
q_{c}
is as close as possible to the true
p_{c}
, the
D_{\mathrm{KL}}
is small, and the above mentioned inefficiencies disappear.

As we cannot evaluate the cross-entropy over the unknown
\mu_{c}
, we instead learn
{\mathcal{P}}_{\psi}
by its empirical estimate over the sample data which is equivalent to minimizing the negative log likelihood (NLL)

\displaystyle\argmin_{\psi}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}^{n}\log q_{c}(\mathtt{c}={\bm{c}}_{i}),\quad{\bm{c}}_{i}\sim\mu_{c}\kern 5.0pt.

(6)

Similar strategy has been used for example in
Theis et al. (2017)
and
Ballé et al. (2017)
both using some form of continuous relaxation of
q_{c}
as well as in
Mentzer et al. (2018)
using an autoregressive PixelCNN as
{\mathcal{P}}_{\psi}
to model
q_{c}({\bm{c}})=\prod_{i}q_{c}({c}_{i}|{c}_{i-1},\ldots.{c}_{1})
.

There is one caveat to the above approach.
In the minimization in equation
6
the sampling distribution
\mu_{c}
is treated as fixed.
Minimizing the cross-entropy in such a regime minimizes the
D_{\mathrm{KL}}
and hence the additional bits due to
q_{c}\neq p_{c}
but not the entropy
{\mathbb{H}}_{\mu_{c}}(\mathtt{c})
(see equation
5
) which is treated as fixed and therefore not optimized for low rate as explained in section
2
.

This may seem natural and even inevitable since the samples
{\bm{c}}
are the result of sampling the data
{\bm{x}}
from the unknown yet fixed distribution
\mu_{x}
.
Yet,
the distribution
\mu_{c}
is not fixed
.
It is determined by the learned transformation
{\mathcal{T}}_{{\bm{E}},\theta}={\mathcal{Q}}_{\bm{E}}\circ{\mathcal{E}}_{\theta}
as the push-forward measure of
\mu_{x}

\displaystyle\mu_{c}[\mathtt{c}\in{\bm{A}}]=\mu_{c}[{\mathcal{T}}_{{\bm{E}},\theta}(\mathtt{x})\in{\bm{A}}]=\mu_{x}[\mathtt{x}\in{\mathcal{T}}_{{\bm{E}},\theta}^{-1}({\bm{A}})]\kern 5.0pt,

(7)

where
{\bm{A}}\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
{\mathcal{T}}^{-1}
is the inverse image
6
6

6

The notation
{\mathcal{T}}^{-1}

as
{\mathcal{T}}
is generally not invertible.
defined as
{\mathcal{T}}^{-1}({\bm{A}}):=\{\mathtt{x}\in{\mathbb{X}}:{\mathcal{T}}(\mathtt{x})\in{\bm{A}}\}
.
Changing the parameters of the encoder
{\mathcal{E}}_{\theta}
and the embeddings
{\bm{E}}
will change the measure
\mu_{c}
and hence the entropy
{\mathbb{H}}_{\mu_{c}}(\mathtt{c})
and the cross-entropy
{\mathbb{H}}_{\mu_{c}|q_{c}}(\mathtt{c})
even with the approximation
q_{c}
fixed.

We therefore propose to optimise the encoder and the embeddings so as to minimize the cross-entropy not only through learning better approximation
q_{c}
but also through changing
\mu_{c}
to achieve overall lower rate.
Since the discrete sampling operation from
\mu_{c}
is non-differentiable, we propose to use a simple continuous soft relaxation similar the one described in section
2
.
Instead of using the deterministic non-differentiable code assignments

\displaystyle p_{c}(\mathtt{c}^{(i)}=j)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if}\ \hat{{\bm{z}}}^{(i)}={\bm{e}}^{(j)}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases},\quad i=1,\ldots,d/m

(8)

we use the differentiable soft relaxation

\displaystyle\hat{p}_{c}(\mathtt{c}^{(i)}=j)=\mathrm{softmax}(-\sigma\|{\bm{z}}^{(i)}-{\bm{e}}^{(j)}\|)=\frac{\exp(-\sigma\|{\bm{z}}^{(i)}-{\bm{e}}^{(j)}\|)}{\sum_{j}^{k}\exp(-\sigma\|{\bm{z}}^{(i)}-{\bm{e}}^{(j)}\|)}\kern 5.0pt.

(9)

Our final objective is the minimization of the empirical loss composed of three terms: the distortion, the
soft
cross-entropy, and the
hard
cross-entropy

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{L}}(\theta,{\bm{E}},\phi,\psi):=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}^{n}d({\bm{x}}_{i},\hat{{\bm{x}}}_{i})+\alpha\,s({\bm{c}}_{i})+\beta\,h({\bm{c}}_{i})\kern 5.0pt.

(10)

The distortion may be the squared error
d({\bm{x}},\hat{{\bm{x}}})=\|{\bm{x}}-\hat{{\bm{x}}}\|^{2}
or other application-dependent metric (e.g. multi-scale structural similarity index for visual perception in images).
Through the distortion we optimize the parameters of the decoder
{\mathcal{D}}_{\phi}
and using the relaxation described in section
2
for the backward pass also the parameters of the encoder
{\mathcal{E}}_{\theta}
and the quantization embeddings
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\bm{E}}

The

\displaystyle h({\bm{c}})=-\frac{m}{d}\sum_{j}^{d/m}\sum_{{\bm{c}}^{(j)}=1}^{k}p_{c}({\bm{c}}^{(j)})\log q_{c}({\bm{c}}^{(j)})=-\frac{m}{d}\sum_{j}^{d/m}\log q_{c}({\bm{c}}^{(j)})\kern 5.0pt,

can be used directly as the entropy model for the lossless arithmetic coding (expects the elements of the messages to be sampled i.i.d. from a single distribution).
The loss simplifies to the final form due to the 0/1 probabilities of the deterministic code-assignments in equation
8
.
Through the hard cross-entropy loss we learn the parameters of the probability model
{\mathcal{P}}_{\psi}
outputting the

The
soft

\displaystyle s({\bm{c}})=-\frac{m}{d}\sum_{j}^{d/m}\sum_{{\bm{c}}^{(j)}=1}^{k}\hat{p}_{c}({\bm{c}}^{(j)})\log\textrm{sg}[q_{c}({\bm{c}}^{(j)})]\kern 5.0pt,

(12)

which uses the differentiable soft relaxation

of equation
9

This allows for back-propagating the gradients to the encoder
{\mathcal{E}}_{\theta}
and the quantizer
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\bm{E}}

We use the

q_{c}
as fixed in this part of the loss preventing further updating of the parameters of the probability model
{\mathcal{P}}_{\psi}
.

Upper deviation probabilities for level sets of a supercritical branching random walk
Shuxiong Zhang, Lianghui Luo

Abstract




Abstract:

Given a supercritical branching random walk
\{Z_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{R}
, let
Z_{n}([y,\infty))
be the number of particles located in
[y,\infty)\subset\mathbb{R}
at generation
n
. Let
m
be the mean of the offspring law of
\{Z_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
and
I(x)
be the large deviation rate function of the underlying random walk of
\{Z_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
. It is known from
[
6
]
that under some mild conditions, for
x\in(0,x^{*})
,
n^{-1}\log Z_{n}([xn,\infty))
converges almost surely to
\log m-I(x)
on the event of nonextinction as
n\to\infty
, where
x^{*}
is the speed of maximal position of the branching random walk. In this work, we investigate its upper deviation probabilities, in other words, the convergence rates of

\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}([xn,\infty))\geq e^{an}\right)

as
n\to\infty
, where
x>0
and
a>(\log m-I(x))^{+}
. This paper is a counterpart work of the lower deviation probabilities
[
28
]
and also completes those results in
[
1
]
for the branching Brownian motion.

Mathematics Subject Classifications (2020): 60F10, 60J80, 60G50.

Keywords
: Branching random walk; Upper deviation probability; Level set.

1
Introduction and Main results

1.1
Introduction

Generally speaking, a branching random walk (BRW) is governed by a point process on
\mathbb{R}
. Every particle moves and splits
according to this point process (see
[
27
]
). Nevertheless, in this article, we study a simplified version by assuming that the branch and the motion are independent. Given a probability distribution
\{p_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{N}
and a real-valued random variable
X
, a branching random walk
\{Z_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
with offspring law
\{p_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
and step size
X
is defined as follows. At time
0
, there is one particle located at the origin (i.e.,
Z_{0}=\delta_{0}
). The particle dies and produces offsprings according to the offspring distribution
\{p_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
. Afterwards, the offspring particles move independently according to the law of
X
. This forms a point process at time
1
, denoted by
Z_{1}
. For any point process
Z_{n}
,
n\geq 2
, we define it by the following iteration

Z_{n}=\sum_{x\in Z_{n-1}}\tilde{Z}_{1}^{x},

where
\tilde{Z}_{1}^{x}
has the same distribution as
Z_{1}(\cdot-S_{x})
and
\tilde{Z}_{1}^{x},\ x\in Z_{n-1}
(conditioned on
Z_{n-1}
) are independent. Here and later, for a point process
\xi
,
x\in\xi
means
x
is an atom of
\xi
, and
S_{x}
is the position of
x
(i.e.,
\xi=\sum_{x\in\xi}\delta_{S_{x}}
).

For
A\subset\mathbb{R}
, let

Z_{n}(A):=\#\left\{u\in Z_{n}:S_{u}\in A\right\},

i.e. the number of particles located in the set
A
. Write
|Z_{n}|:=Z_{n}(\mathbb{R})
,
n\geq 0
and
m:=\mathbb{E}[|Z_{1}|]
.

Usually, we call
\{|Z_{n}|\}_{n\geq 0}
the branching process or the Galton-Watson process.
\{|Z_{n}|\}_{n\geq 0}
is called a supercritical (critical, subcritical) branching process if
m>1
(
=1
,
<1
). In supercritical case, the branching process will survive with positive probability, otherwise it will die out almost surely (provided
p_{1}\neq 1
). For a more detailed discussion, one can refer to
[
3
]
. In this paper, we always deal with the supercritical case.

According to Biggins
[
6
, Theorem 2]
, if
1<m<\infty
,
\mathbb{E}[X]=0
and
\mathbb{E}[e^{\kappa X}]<\infty
for some
\kappa>0
, then for
x\in[0,x^{*})
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log Z_{n}([xn,\infty))=\log m-I(x),~\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.~on nonextinction},

(1.1)

where
I(x):=\sup_{t\geq 0}\{tx-\log\mathbb{E}[e^{tX}]\}
is the rate function (see
[
6
]
) and

x^{*}:=\sup\{y\geq 0:I(y)\leq\log m\}

is the speed of maximal position of a branching random walk (see
[
18
]
). In this work, we are going to study the corresponding upper deviation probabilities, i.e. the decay rate of

\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}([xn,\infty))\geq e^{an})

as
n\rightarrow\infty
, where
x>0
and
a>(\log m-I(x))^{+}
.

In fact, this question was first studied by Aïdekon, Hu and Shi
[
1
, Theorem 1.1]
for the binary branching Brownian motion
\{Z_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
. To be specific, they proved that if
x>0
and
(1-\frac{x^{2}}{2})^{+}<a<1
, then

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log{\mathbb{P}}(Z_{t}([xt,\infty))\geq e^{at})=-\left[\frac{x^{2}}{2(1-a)}-1\right].

A branching random walk can be viewed as a discrete but somewhat generalized version of the classical branching Brownian motion. Thus, our work is a generalization of
[
1
, Theorem 1.1]
. However, since our assumptions about the offspring law and step size are much weaker than
[
1
]
, this makes the proof delicate. For example, in our setting, the rate function
I(x)
and the distribution of
|Z_{n}|
do not have explicit expression, some equations and deviation probabilities involved can not be dealt with as those in
[
1
]
. The difficulty related to
I(x)
is overcome by a careful analysis of several optimization problems using the convexity and asymptotics of
I(x)
. For
|Z_{n}|
, we use the uniform boundedness of the exponential moment of
|Z_{n}|m^{-n}
(see
[
2
, Theorem 4]
) to obtain upper deviation probabilities of it. Furthermore, we find the following new decay scales. If the essential supremum of the step size is bounded, then
\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}([xn,\infty))\geq e^{an})
may decay double-exponentially. We also consider the case
a\geq\log m
, which has not been studied in
[
1
]
, and the decay scale also turns out to be double-exponential. Moreover, we show that heavy-tailed offspring law may lead phase transition for the upper deviation probability.

For the corresponding lower deviation probabilities of level sets, namely the decay rate of

\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}([xn,\infty))<e^{an}),

where
x\in(0,x^{*})
and
0\leq a<\log m-I(x)
, one can refer to Zhang
[
28
]
; see also Öz
[
25
]
for the lower deviation probability of local mass of a branching Brownian motion. The lower deviation probability and upper deviation probability are essentially two different questions. Firstly, the lower deviation aims at keeping a small population in
[xn,\infty)
at time
n
. So, the strategy is to force particles before some intermediate time
t_{n}
to produce as less descendants as possible, and in the meanwhile, one should control individuals at time
t_{n}
locate at a low position. However, for the upper deviation, the strategy is to force the maximum of the branching random walk at time
t_{n}
become large enough such that the sub-branching random walk emanating from the maximum at time
t_{n}
can normally produce
e^{an}
descendants in
(nx,\infty)
at time
n
.
Secondly, in the lower deviation probability, the decay scales mainly depend on the tail probability of the step size and whether
p_{1}
is 0 or not. But, in the upper deviation probability, the decay scales depend on the tail probability of the offspring law and the size of
a
and
\log m
.
Therefore, both in terms of method and conclusion, to study the the upper deviation will make some progress of the large deviation theory of the branching random walk.

We mention here that, since the last few decades, the branching random walk has been extensively studied due to its connection to many fields, such as Gaussian multiplicative chaos, random walk in random environment, random polymer, random algorithms and discrete Gaussian free field etc; see
[
20
]
,
[
21
]
,
[
7
]
and
[
1
]
references therein. One can refer to Shi
[
27
]
for a more detailed overview. Especially, the large deviation probability (LDP) for branching random walks and branching Brownian motions on real line have attracted many researchers’ attention. For example, Hu
[
19
]
, Gantert and Höfelsauer
[
17
]
and Chen and He
[
11
]
considered the LDP and the moderate deviation probability of the branching random walk’s maximum (for the maximum of branching Brownian motion, see Chauvin and Rouault
[
9
]
and Derrida and Shi
[
14
,
15
,
16
]
). For the LDP of empirical distribution, see Louidor and Perkins
[
22
]
, Chen and He
[
10
]
and Zhang
[
29
]
. Some other related works include Rouault
[
26
]
, Buraczewski and Maślanka
[
8
]
and Bhattacharya
[
5
]
.

1.2
Main Results

Let
L\in(-\infty,\infty]
be the essential supremum of the step size
X
, i.e.
L=\sup\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}:\mathbb{P}(X>x)>0\}
. In the
sequel of this work, we always need the following assumptions.

Assumption 1.1
.

(i)
p_{0}=0
,
1<m<\infty
;

(ii)
\mathbb{E}[X]=0
,
{\mathbb{P}}(X=0)<1
and
\mathbb{E}[e^{\kappa X}]<\infty
for some
\kappa>0
.

Remark 1.1
.

If
p_{0}>0
, using similar arguments of this paper, one can also obtain the following theorems under the probability
\mathbb{P}(\ \cdot\ |\ |Z_{n}|>0,\ \forall\ n\geq 0)
.
\mathbb{E}[X]=0
is only made to simplify the statement.
{\mathbb{P}}(X=0)<1
is made to avoid trivial case. The other assumptions are necessary for the almost sure convergence (
1.1
).

Now, we are ready to show our main results. Recall that
I(x)=\sup_{t\geq 0}\{tx-\log\mathbb{E}[e^{tX}]\}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
\mathbb{E}[e^{\theta|Z_{1}|}]<\infty
for some
\theta>0
,
x\in(0,L)
and
a\in((\log m-I(x))^{+},\log m)
. If

L=\infty
, then

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}([xn,\infty))\geq e^{an})=-I(a,x)\in(-\infty,0),

where

I(a,x):=\inf_{s\in\left(0,1-\frac{a}{\log m}\right],y\in(0,x]:\atop~\log m-I\left(\frac{x-y}{1-s}\right)=\frac{a}{1-s}}\left\{sI\left(\frac{y}{s}\right)-s\log m\right\}.

Remark 1.2
.

According to the local survival probabilities studied in
[
26
]
and truncation arguments used in
[
4
]
, one can obtain the upper deviation probabilities of the maximum of a general branching random walk (i.e. defined by a point process). Then, making use of the upper deviation probabilities of the maximum, one can generalize Theorem
1.1
to the general branching random walk setting.

Remark 1.3
.

Denote by
y_{s}
the unique solution of the equation (w.r.t.
y
)

\log m-I\left(\frac{x-y}{1-s}\right)=\frac{a}{1-s}

(1.2)

on
(0,x]
, where
s\in(0,1-\frac{a}{\log m}]
; see Lemma
2.4
for the proof. Theorem
1.1
still holds if we replace the condition
L=\infty
with one of the following conditions:

(i)
L<\infty
,
x^{*}<L
,
\mathbb{P}(X=L)=0
and there exists some
s\in\big(0,1-\frac{a}{\log m}\big]
such that

y_{s}/s<L;

(ii)
L<\infty
,
x^{*}<L
,
\mathbb{P}(X=L)>0
and there exists some
s\in\big(0,1-\frac{a}{\log m}\big]
such that

y_{s}/s\leq L.

Remark 1.4
.

We give several examples to illustrate Remark
1.3
.

(i) If
X
follows uniform distribution on
(-1,1)
,
a\in\left((\log m-I(x))^{+},(1-\frac{x}{L})\log m\right)
and
s^{*}:=1-\frac{a}{\log m}
, then
y_{s^{*}}=x
. This implies
X
satisfies Remark
1.3
(i).

(ii) Let
X
satisfy
\mathbb{P}(X=-1)=\mathbb{P}(X=1)=0.5
,
a\in\left((\log m-I(x))^{+},(1-\frac{x}{L})\log m\right]
and
m=1.5
. Then, similarly to (i), it is simple to see that
X
satisfies Remark
1.3
(ii).

(iii) Now, we give an example such that none of the Remark
1.3
(i) and (ii) holds. Since
\lim_{s\to 0}\frac{y_{s}}{s}=\infty
(see (
2.23
) below), there exists
\delta\in(0,1)
such that for any
s\in(0,\delta)
,
\frac{y_{s}}{s}>2L
and
(1-\delta)\log m>\log m-I(x)
. Finally, one can see that if
a\in((1-\delta)\log m,\log m)
, then
\frac{y_{s}}{s}>2L
for
s\in\big(0,1-\frac{a}{\log m}\big]
.

In the next theorem, we will show that if none of Remark
1.3
(i) and (ii) holds, then
{\mathbb{P}}(Z_{n}([xn,\infty))\geq e^{an})
may decay double-exponentially. Put
b:=\inf\{k\geq m:p_{k}>0\}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume
\mathbb{E}[e^{\theta|Z_{1}|}]<\infty
for some
\theta>0
,
x\in(0,L)
and
a\in((\log m-I(x))^{+},\log m)
. Suppose
L<\infty
and one of the following two conditions holds.

(i)
x^{*}=L;

(ii)
x^{*}<L
and

y_{s}/s>L,\forall s\in\Big(0,1-\frac{a}{\log m}\Big].

Then

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle<\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left[-\log\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}([xn,\infty))\geq e^{an})\right]

(1.3)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left[-\log\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}([xn,\infty))\geq e^{an})\right]\leq c^{*}\log b,

where
c^{*}\in(0,\frac{x}{L})
is the unique solution of the equation (w.r.t.
c
)

\log m-I\left(L+\frac{x-L}{1-c}\right)-\frac{a-\log b}{1-c}=\log b.

Though we get Remark
1.3
and Theorem
1.2
, we are unable to obatin the decay scale of
{\mathbb{P}}(Z_{n}([xn,\infty))\geq e^{an})
if

L<\infty
,
{\mathbb{P}}(X=L)=0
and

\inf_{s\in(0,1-\frac{a}{\log m}]}\frac{y_{s}}{s}=L.

In the next, we consider the case
a\geq\log m
. Let
R_{n}
be the rightmost position of a branching random walk with offspring law
p_{k}
and step size
-X
at generation
n
. If there exists some
k\geq 2
such that
p_{k}=1
, then it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}([xn,\infty))\geq e^{an})=\begin{cases}\mathbb{P}(R_{n}\leq-xn),&~a=\log m;\cr 0,&~a>\log m,\end{cases}

where
{\mathbb{P}}(R_{n}\leq-xn)
has been well studied in
[
10
]
. Thus, we only need to consider the case that the branch is random. Define
\mu:=\sup\{k:p_{k}>0,~k\geq 1\}\in(0,\infty]
and
\alpha:=\inf\{k:p_{k}>0,k\geq e^{a}\}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume
\mathbb{E}[e^{\theta|Z_{1}|}]<\infty
for some
\theta>0
. Suppose
p_{k}<1
for any
k\geq 2
,
x\in(0,L)
and
a\in[\log m,\log\mu]\cap\mathbb{R}
. Then

\displaystyle a-\log m

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log[-\log{\mathbb{P}}(Z_{n}([xn,\infty))\geq e^{an})]

(1.4)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log[-\log{\mathbb{P}}(Z_{n}([xn,\infty))\geq e^{an})]\leq\log\alpha.

(1.5)

Remark 1.5
.

Although the lower bound of (
1.4
) is degenerate in the case
a=\log m
, one can use Theorem
1.2
to show that the lower bound is still positive if
L<\infty
; see Section
4
for the proof.

Remark 1.6
.

Theorem
1.1
still holds, if we replace the condition
\mathbb{E}[e^{\theta|Z_{1}|}]<\infty
for some
\theta>0
with
\lim_{x\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(|Z_{1}|>x)e^{-\lambda x^{\alpha}}=1
for some
\lambda>0
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
; see Subsection
2.3
for the proof.

Though Remark
1.6
tells us that the assumption
\mathbb{E}[e^{\theta|Z_{1}|}]<\infty
can be weaken, the following theorem shows that if the offspring law has a Pareto tail, then the limit will change. To demonstrate this, we consider the standard normal step size. Hence,
I(x)=x^{2}/2
for
x\geq 0
. We write
f(y)=\Theta(1)g(y)
if
0<\liminf_{y\to+\infty}f(y)/g(y)\leq\limsup_{y\to+\infty}f(y)/g(y)<\infty
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Assume
\mathbb{P}(|Z_{1}|>y)=\Theta(1)y^{-\beta}
for some
\beta>1
. Suppose
X
is a standard normal random variable,
x>0
and
a\in((\log m-\frac{x^{2}}{2})^{+},\infty)
. Then

\displaystyle-\left[(\beta-1)a+a-\left(\log m-\frac{x^{2}}{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}([xn,\infty))\geq e^{an})

(1.6)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}([xn,\infty))\geq e^{an})\leq-\left[a-\left(\log m-\frac{x^{2}}{2}\right)\right].

Remark 1.7
.

In Theorem
1.1
, if
X
is a standard normal random variable, then

I(a,x)=\frac{x^{2}\log m}{2(\log m-a)}-\log m.

Thus, if
a\in(\log m-\frac{x^{2}}{2\beta},\log m)
, then the lower bound in Theorem
1.4
is larger than the limit of Theorem
1.1
.

The rest of this paper is organised as follows. In Section
2
, we consider the case
a\in((\log m-I(x))^{+},\log m)
and

L=\infty
, where Theorem
1.1
is proved. This section is divided into three subsections. In the first subsection, we present several lemmas related to rate functions
I(x)
and
I(a,x)
. In the second and third subsection, we prove the lower bound and upper bound of Theorem
1.1
, respectively. Section
3
is devoted to study the case
a\in((\log m-I(x))^{+},\log m)
and

L<\infty
, where Theorem
1.2
is proved. We deal with the case
a\geq\log m
in Section
4
, and Theorem
1.3
is proved. We prove Theorem
1.4
in Section
5
, where the offspring law is assumed to be a Pareto tail.

2
Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we are going to prove Theorem
1.1
, and the proofs also carry through for Remark
1.3
. This section is divided into three subsections. In subsection
2.1
, we present several lemmas, which concern the asymptotic behaviour of level sets and properties of the rate function
I(a,x)
. In subsection
2.2
, we use the upper deviation probability of the maximum for the branching random walk (see
[
17
]
) to obtain the lower bound of Theorem
1.1
. We prove the upper bound of Theorem
1.1
in subsection
2.3
. The proof mainly uses the path partition technique in
[
1
]
. But since we consider more general step size and offspring law, the details are much more involved.

2.1
Preliminaries

In the remainder of the paper, for ease of notation, we write
Z_{n}A=Z_{n}(A)
for a Borel set
A\subset\mathbb{R}
. According to
[
6
, Theorem 2]
, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

The following holds,
\mathbb{P}
almost surely:

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log Z_{n}[xn,\infty)}{n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}~~~~0~~~~,&&{x>x^{*}};\\
\log m-I(x),&&{0<x<x^{*}};\\
~~~~\log m~~~~,&&{x\leq 0}.\end{array}\right.

(2.1)

The next lemma provides several properties of the rate function
I(x)=\sup_{t\geq 0}\{tx-\log\mathbb{E}[e^{tX}]\}
, which are borrowed from
[
28
, Lemma 2.3]
.
Let
\lambda^{*}:=\sup\left\{\lambda\geq 0:\mathbb{E}[e^{\lambda X}]<\infty\right\}
and
\Lambda(\lambda):=\log\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\lambda X}\right]
. Note that by Assumption
1.1
(i), we have
\lambda^{*}>0
. Furthermore, by
[
12
, Exercise 2.2.24]
,
\Lambda(\lambda)
is smooth and convex on
(0,\lambda^{*})
.

Lemma 2.2
.

The rate function
I(x)
can be classified into the following three cases.

(i) If
\Lambda^{\prime}(\lambda)\uparrow+\infty
as
\lambda\rightarrow\lambda^{*}
, then for any
x>0
, there exists some
\lambda\in(0,\lambda^{*})
such that

x=\Lambda^{\prime}(\lambda),~I(x)=\lambda\Lambda^{\prime}(\lambda)-\Lambda(\lambda).

Furthermore,
\lim_{x\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{I(x)}{x}=\lambda^{*}
.

(ii) If
\lambda^{*}=+\infty
,
\Lambda^{\prime}(\lambda)
converges to some finite limit as
\lambda\rightarrow\infty
, then
\Lambda^{\prime}(\lambda)\uparrow L
and

I(x)=\begin{cases}\text{positive finite},&x\in(0,L);\cr-\log\mathbb{P}(X=L),&x=L;\cr+\infty,&x\in(L,\infty).\end{cases}

Furthermore, as
x\uparrow L
,
I(x)\uparrow-\log\mathbb{P}(X=L).

(iii) If
0<\lambda^{*}<+\infty
,
\Lambda^{\prime}(\lambda)
converges to some finite limit
T
as
\lambda\rightarrow\lambda^{*}
, then
\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\lambda^{*}X}\right]<\infty
and

I(x)=\begin{cases}\text{positive finite},&x\in(0,T];\cr\lambda^{*}x-\log\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\lambda^{*}X}\right],&x\in[T,\infty).\end{cases}

Remark 2.1
.

For instance, the following three examples satisfy Lemma
2.2
(i), (ii) and (iii), respectively:
(i) If
X
is a standard normal random variable, then
\Lambda(\lambda)=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}
and
\lambda^{*}=\infty
;
(ii) If
\mathbb{P}(X=1)=\mathbb{P}(X=-1)=0.5
, then
\Lambda(\lambda)=\log(e^{\lambda}+e^{-\lambda})-\log 2
and
\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\Lambda^{\prime}(\lambda)=1
;
(iii) Let
Y
be a random variable with density function

f(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{Ce^{-x}}{x^{3}},&x\geq 1;\cr 0,&x<1,\end{cases}

where
C
is chosen such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\mathrm{d}x=1
. Then we can define
X:=Y-\mathbb{E}[Y]
, and obviously
\lambda^{*}=1
.

The following lemma can be inferred from
[
12
, Exercise 2.2.24]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

I(x)
is infinitely differentiable and strictly increasing on
[0,L)
.

In the proof of lower and upper bound of Theorem
1.1
, we will encounter different optimization problems. The following lemma shows the equivalency of these optimization problems.

Lemma 2.4
.

Assume
x^{*}<L
,
x\in(0,L)
and
a\in((\log m-I(x))^{+},\log m)
.
Suppose that one of the following three conditions holds:

(i)
L=\infty
;

(ii)
L<\infty
,
\mathbb{P}(X=L)=0
and there exists some
s\in\left(0,1-\frac{a}{\log m}\right]
such that

\frac{y_{s}}{s}<L;

(iii)
L<\infty
,
\mathbb{P}(X=L)>0
and there exists some
s\in\left(0,1-\frac{a}{\log m}\right]
such that

\frac{y_{s}}{s}\leq L.

Then,

\displaystyle\inf_{s\in(0,1),y\in(0,x):\atop~\frac{y}{s}>x^{*},~\log m-I\left(\frac{x-y}{1-s}\right)\geq\frac{a}{1-s}}\left\{sI\left(\frac{y}{s}\right)-s\log m\right\}

(2.2)

2.2

By Lemma

The idea to the proof is as follows. Denote by
M_{n}:=\max\{S_{u}:u\in Z_{n}\}
the maximum of the branching random walk at time
n
. Let
t_{n}=\lceil sn\rceil
,

and
u
be the rightmost particle at time

. We let

large enough so that the sub-BRW emanating from

can normally produce

descendants in

at time
n
. By this means, this problem transforms into the upper deviation probability of the maximum, which has been well studied in
[
17
]
. Finally, the desired lower bound follows by an optimization for
t_{n}
. Here, we note that
although, in
[
17
]
, the step size is assumed to satisfy
\mathbb{E}[e^{\lambda X}]<\infty

[

, Theorem 3.2]
, one only need to assume
\mathbb{E}[e^{\lambda X}]<\infty
for some
\lambda>0
. Hence, in Theorem
1.1
, we just assume the step size has positive exponential moment. As usual, we use
\lfloor x\rfloor
(

) to stand for the largest integer not greater than

(the smallest integer not less than

Proof.

Let

,

. Let

. By the Markov property,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}[xn,\infty)\geq e^{an})

(2.30)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\left(M_{t_{n}}\geq(1+\varepsilon)yn\right)\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n-t_{n}}(nx-(1+\varepsilon)yn,\infty)\geq e^{an}\right).

, by the proof of
[

, Theorem 3.2]
, we have for

(2.32)

On the other hand, (

\frac{(x-(1+\varepsilon)y)}{1-s}<x^{*}~\text{and}~\log m-I\left(\frac{x-(1+\varepsilon)y}{1-s}\right)>\frac{a}{1-s}.

(2.33)

Letting

This completes the proof of the lower bound.

2.3
Proof of Theorem
1.1
: upper bound

In this subsection, we are going to prove the upper bound:

\limsup\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}[xn,\infty)\geq e^{an})\leq-I(a,x)

The idea to the proof is as follows. We divide the space-time into some paths. These paths can be classified into two categories: good path and bad path. Then, to estimate
\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}[xn,\infty)\geq e^{an})
is reduced to consider the probability of the number of particles to follow a fixed path is large than
e^{an}
. For good path, the probability can be well-estimated by the Markov inequality, which exhibits an exponential decay. For bad path, the probability is essentially the upper deviation probability of an inhomogeneous Galton-Watson process, which has been well-handled in
[
1
, Proposition 2.1]
, and the probability turns out to be a double-exponential decay. Hence, the desired upper bound comes from an optimization for the good path.

Proof.

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
. We discretize time by splitting time interval
[0,n]
into intervals of length
\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor
. Let
s_{0}=0,s_{i}=n-(M-i)\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor
,
i=1,...,M
, where
M:=\lfloor n/\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor\rfloor+1
. Let

be the constant in Theorem
1.1
and write
C=(2\log m+\log\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\theta X}\right]+I(a,x))/\theta
.

Denote by
\text{supp}Z_{n}
the support of
Z_{n}
. Set

E_{1}(n):=\left\{\text{supp}Z_{i}\subset(-\infty,Cn]~\text{for~every}~1\leq i\leq n\right\}.

(2.35)

Recall that
S_{u}
is the position of particle
u
. Note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(E_{1}(n)^{c}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\exists 1\leq i\leq n~\text{and}~u\in Z_{i}~\text{s.t.}~S_{u}\geq Cn\right)

(2.36)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\sum^{n}_{i=1}\sum_{u\in Z_{i}}{\bf 1}_{\{S_{u}\geq Cn\}}\right]

(2.37)

\displaystyle=\sum^{n}_{i=1}m^{i}\mathbb{P}(S_{i}\geq Cn),

(2.38)

where
S_{i}:=\sum_{\ell=1}^{i}X_{\ell}
and
X_{\ell}
is independent and identically distributed as the step size of the branching random walk.
Since
\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\theta X}\right]\geq e^{\mathbb{E}\left[\theta X\right]}=1
, by Markov inequality, for any
1\leq i\leq n
,

\mathbb{P}(S_{i}\geq Cn)\leq\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\theta(S_{i}-Cn)}\right]=\mathbb{E}^{i}\left[e^{\theta X}\right]e^{-\theta Cn}\leq\mathbb{E}^{n}\left[e^{\theta X}\right]e^{-\theta Cn}.

(2.39)

Plugging (
2.39
) into (
2.36
) yields

\mathbb{P}\left(E_{1}(n)^{c}\right)\leq nm^{n}\mathbb{E}^{n}\left[e^{\theta X}\right]e^{-\theta Cn}=o\left(e^{-I(a,x)n}\right).

Let
Z^{u}_{m}
be the
m
-th generation of the sub-BRW emanating from particle
u
. Set

E_{2}(n):=\left\{|Z^{u}_{s_{i+1}-s_{i}}|\leq n^{2}m^{\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor}~\text{for~all}~u\in Z_{s_{i}},~0\leq i\leq M-1\right\}.

Write
v\succ u
if
v
is a descendant of
u
. Put

D_{n}=\left\{v\succ u:u\in Z_{s_{i}},|Z^{u}_{s_{i+1}-s_{i}}|>n^{2}m^{\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor}~\text{for some}~0\leq i\leq M-1\right\}.

Let
\bar{Z}_{s_{i}}
,
0\leq i\leq M
be the branching random walk by cutting all the individuals in
D_{n}
. In other words,
\{\bar{Z}_{s_{i}}\}_{0\leq i\leq M}
is an inhomogeneous branching random walk with point processes
Z_{s_{1}}(\cdot){\bf 1}_{\left\{|Z_{s_{1}}|\leq n^{2}m^{\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor}\right\}}
and
Z_{\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor}{\bf 1}_{\left\{|Z_{\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor}|\leq n^{2}m^{\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor}\right\}}
as its evolutionary mechanism at time
0
and after time
s_{1}
, respectively. It is simple to see that

\displaystyle E_{2}(n)=\{\bar{Z}_{s_{i}}=Z_{s_{i}},~0\leq i\leq M\}.

(2.40)

According to
[
2
, Theorem 4]
, if
\mathbb{E}[e^{\theta|Z_{1}|}]<\infty
for some
\theta>0
, then there exists some
\theta_{1}>0
such that

\sup_{i\geq 1}\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\theta_{1}W_{i}}\right]<\infty,

where
W_{i}:=|Z_{i}|m^{-i}
,
i\geq 1
. Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(E_{2}(n)^{c}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\exists 0\leq i\leq M-1~\text{and}~u\in Z_{s_{i}}~\text{s.t.}~|Z^{u}_{s_{i+1}-s_{i}}|>n^{2}m^{\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor}\right)

(2.41)

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{M-1}_{i=0}m^{s_{i}}\mathbb{P}\left(|Z_{\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor}|\geq n^{2}m^{\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor}\right)

(2.42)

\displaystyle\leq Mm^{s_{M}}\sup_{i\geq 1}\mathbb{P}\left(W_{i}\geq n^{2}\right)

(2.43)

\displaystyle\leq nm^{n}\sup_{i\geq 1}\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\theta_{1}W_{i}-\theta_{1}n^{2}}\right]

(2.44)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{i\geq 1}\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\theta_{1}W_{i}}\right]nm^{n}e^{-\theta_{1}n^{2}}=o\left(e^{-I(a,x)n}\right),

(2.45)

where the third inequality follows by Markov inequality.

We write
v\prec u
if
v
is an ancestor of
u
. Define an event

E_{3}(n):=\left\{\forall~u\in Z_{n},S_{u}\geq xn,~\text{and}~v\prec u,~v\in Z_{i},~1\leq i<n~\text{are~located~in}~[-Cn,\infty)\right\}.

(2.46)

Then,

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}(E_{3}(n)^{c})&=\mathbb{P}\left(\exists u\in Z_{n}~\text{and}~v\prec u~\text{satisfying}~S_{u}\geq xn~\text{and}~S_{v}<-Cn\right)\cr&\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{u\in Z_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\sum_{v\in Z_{i}\atop v\prec u}{\bf 1}_{\{S_{v}<-Cn,\ S_{u}\geq xn\}}\right]\\
&\leq m^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\mathbb{P}(S_{n-i}>(x+C)n)\\
&\leq m^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\mathbb{E}^{n-i}\left[e^{\theta X}\right]e^{-\theta(x+C)n}\\
&\leq nm^{n}\mathbb{E}^{n}\left[e^{\theta X}\right]e^{-\theta(x+C)n}=o\left(e^{-I(a,x)n}\right).\\
\end{split}

Consequently,

\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}[xn,\infty)\geq e^{an}\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}[xn,\infty)\geq e^{an},E_{1}(n),E_{2}(n),E_{3}(n)\right)+o\left(e^{-I(a,x)n}\right).

Fix
\delta^{\prime}\in(0,\delta)
and
\varepsilon\in(0,(x/3)\wedge a\wedge\varepsilon^{*})
, where
\varepsilon^{*}
is the unique positive root of

a=\log m+\varepsilon^{*}-I(x-3\varepsilon^{*}).

So,

\displaystyle a>\log m+\varepsilon-I(x-3\varepsilon).

(2.47)

We discretize space by splitting space interval
[-Cn,Cn]
into intervals of length
n^{\delta^{\prime}}
. Let
x_{k}=kn^{\delta^{\prime}}
,
-\lceil Cn^{1-\delta^{\prime}}\rceil\leq k\leq\lceil Cn^{1-\delta^{\prime}}\rceil
. We call

f:\{s_{i}:0\leq i\leq M\}\rightarrow\left\{x_{k}=kn^{\delta^{\prime}}:-\lceil Cn^{1-\delta^{\prime}}\rceil\leq k\leq\lceil Cn^{1-\delta^{\prime}}\rceil\right\}

a path if

f(0)=0~\text{and}~f(s_{M})\geq(x-\varepsilon)n.

For a branching random walk, a particle at time
s_{i}
is said to follow a path
f
until time
s_{i}
if for all
0\leq j\leq i
the ancestor of the particle at time
s_{j}
lies in
\left[f(s_{j})-n^{\delta^{\prime}},f(s_{j})+n^{\delta^{\prime}}\right]
. Let

\displaystyle Z_{s_{i}}(f):=\text{the number of particles following a path}~f~\text{until time}~s_{i}.

(2.48)

On the event
E_{1}(n)\cap E_{3}(n)
, we have

Z_{n}[xn,\infty)\leq\sum_{f}Z_{s_{M}}(f)\leq\#(\text{paths})\max_{f}Z_{s_{M}}(f),

(2.49)

where
\sum_{f}
and
\max_{f}
, respectively, denote the sum and maximum over all possible paths
f
, and

\#
(paths) stands for the total number of paths. Let
a^{\prime}\in(0,a)
. Since
\#(\text{paths})\leq(2Cn^{1-\delta^{\prime}}+2)^{n^{1-\delta}+1}=e^{o(n)}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, by (
2.49
), it follows that for
n
large enough, on the event
\{Z_{n}[xn,\infty)\geq e^{an}\}\cap E_{1}(n)\cap E_{3}(n)
, there exists a path
f
such that
Z_{s_{M}}(f)\geq e^{a^{\prime}n}
. Accordingly, for
n
large enough,

\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}[xn,\infty)\geq e^{an},E_{1}(n),E_{2}(n),E_{3}(n)\right)\leq e^{o(n)}\max_{f}\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{s_{M}}(f)\geq e^{a^{\prime}n},E_{2}(n)\right).

(2.50)

Since
I(a,x)
is left continuous at
a
(see Lemma
2.4
), the proof of the upper bound is reduced to show the following:

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\max_{f}\log\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{s_{M}}(f)\geq e^{an},E_{2}(n)\right)\leq-I(a,x).

To bound
\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{s_{M}}(f)\geq e^{an},E_{2}(n)\right)
, we distinguish two situations. A path
f
is said to be good if
there exists
1\leq i\leq M
such that

(n-s_{i})\log m-I\left(\frac{f(s_{M})-f(s_{i})}{n-s_{i}}-\varepsilon\right)(n-s_{i})\geq(a-\varepsilon)n.

(2.51)

Otherwise, we say
f
is a bad path.

We claim that (
2.51
) implies
f(s_{i})>\varepsilon n
. In fact, if
f(s_{i})\leq\varepsilon n
, then we have

\displaystyle I\left(\frac{f(s_{M})-f(s_{i})}{n-s_{i}}-\varepsilon\right)(n-s_{i})

\displaystyle\geq I\left(\frac{f(s_{M})-\varepsilon n}{n-s_{i}}-\varepsilon\right)(n-s_{i})

(2.52)

\displaystyle\geq I\left(\frac{(x-3\varepsilon)n}{n-s_{i}}\right)(n-s_{i})

(2.53)

\displaystyle\geq I\left(x-3\varepsilon\right)\frac{n}{n-s_{i}}(n-s_{i})

(2.54)

\displaystyle=I\left(x-3\varepsilon\right)n,

(2.55)

where the second inequality follows from the fact that
f(s_{M})>(x-\varepsilon)n
and the third inequality follows by the convexity of
I(\cdot)
. Since
a>\log m+\varepsilon-I(x-3\varepsilon)
, by (
2.52
),

\displaystyle I\left(\frac{f(s_{M})-f(s_{i})}{n-s_{i}}-\varepsilon\right)(n-s_{i})+(a-\varepsilon)(n-s_{i})

\displaystyle\geq I\left(x-3\varepsilon\right)n+(a-\varepsilon)(n-s_{i})

(2.56)

\displaystyle>(n-s_{i})\log m,

which contradicts with (
2.51
). Thus,
f(s_{i})>\varepsilon n
.

We first consider the situation that
f
is a good path. Observe that there exists some
N>0
such that for
n>N
and any good path
f
with
i
defined in (
2.51
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{s_{M}}(f)\geq e^{an},E_{2}(n)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{s_{i}}(f)\geq 1\right)

(2.57)

\displaystyle\leq m^{s_{i}}\mathbb{P}\left(|S_{s_{i}}-f(s_{i})|\leq n^{\delta^{\prime}}\right)

(2.58)

\displaystyle\leq m^{s_{i}}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{S_{s_{i}}}{s_{i}}>\frac{f(s_{i})}{s_{i}}-\varepsilon\right)

(2.59)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left\{s_{i}\log m-I\left(\frac{f(s_{i})}{s_{i}}-\varepsilon\right)s_{i}\right\}.

Thus, for any good path
f
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{s_{M}}(f)\geq e^{an},E_{2}(n)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\bigg\{\sup_{s\in(0,n),y>\varepsilon n,z\geq(x-\varepsilon)n:\atop(n-s)\log m-I\left(\frac{z-y}{n-s}-\varepsilon\right)(n-s)\geq(a-\varepsilon)n}\left\{s\log m-I\left(\frac{y}{s}-\varepsilon\right)s\right\}\bigg\}

(2.60)

\displaystyle=\exp\bigg\{n\sup_{s\in(0,1),y>\varepsilon,z\geq(x-\varepsilon):\atop(1-s)\log m-I\left(\frac{z-y}{1-s}-\varepsilon\right)(1-s)\geq(a-\varepsilon)}\left\{s\log m-I\left(\frac{y}{s}-\varepsilon\right)s\right\}\bigg\}.

(2.61)

Thus, by Lemma
2.5
and Lemma
2.4
, uniformly in all good paths
f
, we have

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{s_{M}}(f)\geq e^{an},E_{2}(n)\right)\leq-I(a,x).

Hence, to obatin the desired upper bound it suffices to show that for all bad paths
f
uniformly,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(Z_{s_{M}}(f)\geq e^{an},\ E_{2}(n))=-\infty.

Let
\varepsilon^{\prime}\in(0,\varepsilon/2)
. For any path
f
, define

\tau=\tau(f,n):=\inf\{i:\ 1\leq i\leq M,\ Z_{s_{i}}(f)\geq e^{\varepsilon^{\prime}n}\},\ \inf\varnothing:=\infty.

On the event
\{Z_{s_{M}}(f)\geq e^{an}\}\cap E_{2}(n)
, we have
1\leq\tau\leq M-1
and
Z_{s_{\tau}}(f)\leq n^{2}m^{\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor}e^{\varepsilon^{\prime}n}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(Z_{s_{M}}(f)\geq e^{an},\ E_{2}(n))

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}(Z_{s_{M}}(f)\geq e^{an},\ Z_{s_{\tau}}(f)\leq n^{2}m^{\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor}e^{\varepsilon^{\prime}n},\ E_{2}(n))

(2.62)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{M-1}\mathbb{P}(Z_{s_{M}}(f)\geq e^{an},\ e^{\varepsilon^{\prime}n}\leq Z_{s_{i}}(f)\leq n^{2}m^{\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor}e^{\varepsilon^{\prime}n},\ E_{2}(n))

(2.63)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{M-1}\sum_{\ell=\lfloor e^{\varepsilon^{\prime}n}\rfloor}^{n^{2}m^{\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor}e^{\varepsilon^{\prime}n}}\mathbb{P}(Z_{s_{M}}(f)\geq e^{an},~Z_{s_{i}}(f)=\ell,\ E_{2}(n))

(2.64)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{M-1}\sum_{\ell=\lfloor e^{\varepsilon^{\prime}n}\rfloor}^{n^{2}m^{\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor}e^{\varepsilon^{\prime}n}}\mathbb{P}(\bar{Z}_{s_{M}}(f)\geq e^{an},~\bar{Z}_{s_{i}}(f)=\ell),

(2.65)

where
\bar{Z}_{s_{i}}(f)
is defined similar to (
2.48
) (thus
\bar{Z}_{s_{i}}(f)=Z_{s_{i}}(f)
on
E_{2}(n)
) and the last inequality follows from the following (see (
2.40
)):

\displaystyle E_{2}(n)=\{\bar{Z}_{s_{i}}=Z_{s_{i}},~1\leq i\leq M\}.

Next, we deal with the probability
\mathbb{P}(\bar{Z}_{s_{M}}(f)\geq e^{an},\ \bar{Z}_{s_{i}}(f)=\ell,\ E_{2}(n))
. Fix
1\leq i\leq M-1
. The sequence
(\bar{Z}_{s_{i+j}}(f),\ 0\leq j\leq M-i)
can be written as

\bar{Z}_{s_{i+j+1}}(f)=\sum_{k=1}^{\bar{Z}_{s_{i+j}}(f)}\nu_{k}^{(j)}~\text{for}~0\leq j\leq M-i-1,

where
\nu_{k}^{(j)}
is the number of particles in
[f(s_{i+j+1})-n^{\delta^{\prime}},f(s_{i+j+1})+n^{\delta^{\prime}}]
at time
s_{i+j+1}
generated by the
k
-th particle of
\bar{Z}_{s_{i+j}}(f)
. We define an inhomogeneous Galton-Watson process
(\tilde{Z}_{s_{i+j}}(f),0\leq j\leq M-i)
as follows. For
j=0
,
\tilde{Z}_{s_{i}}(f):=\bar{Z}_{s_{i}}(f)
. For
1\leq j\leq M-i
, we define it by induction. Let
u
be the
k
th particle of
\tilde{Z}_{s_{i+j}}(f)
with its position
S_{u}
. Let
\tilde{\nu}_{k}^{(j)}
be the number of descendants of
u
which lie in

H:=\Big[f(s_{i+j+1})-n^{\delta^{\prime}}+S_{u}-(f(s_{i+j})+n^{\delta^{\prime}}),f(s_{i+j+1})+n^{\delta^{\prime}}+S_{u}-(f(s_{i+j})-n^{\delta^{\prime}})\Big]

at time
s_{i+j+1}
. For
0\leq j\leq M-i-1
, write

\tilde{Z}_{s_{i+j+1}}(f):=\sum_{k=1}^{\tilde{Z}_{s_{i+j}}(f)}\tilde{\nu}_{k}^{(j)}.

Note that if
S_{u}\in[f(s_{i+j})-n^{\delta^{\prime}},f(s_{i+j})+n^{\delta^{\prime}}]
, then

H\supset\Big[f(s_{i+j+1})-n^{\delta^{\prime}},f(s_{i+j+1})+n^{\delta^{\prime}}\Big].

So,
\bar{Z}_{s_{i+j}}(f)\leq\tilde{Z}_{s_{i+j}}(f)
,
0\leq j\leq M-i
.
Write
\Delta f(s_{i+j}):=f(s_{i+j+1})-f(s_{i+j})
. Note that
s_{k+1}-s_{k}=\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor
for
k=1,...,M-1
. Thus, for
n
large enough,

\begin{split}m_{j}&:=\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{\nu}_{k}^{(j)}\right]\\
&=\mathbb{E}\left[|\bar{Z}^{u}_{\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor}|\right]\mathbb{P}\left(|S_{\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor}-\Delta f(s_{i+j})|\leq 2n^{\delta^{\prime}}\right)\\
&\leq m^{\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor}\mathbb{P}\left(|S_{\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor}-\Delta f(s_{i+j})|\leq 2n^{\delta^{\prime}}\right)\\
&\leq m^{\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{S_{\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor}}{\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor}>\frac{\Delta f(s_{i+j})-2n^{\delta^{\prime}}}{\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor}\right)\\
&\leq\exp\left\{\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor\log m-\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor I\left(\frac{\Delta f(s_{i+j})}{\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor}-\varepsilon\right)\right\},\end{split}

where the first inequality follows from the fact that

\mathbb{E}\left[|\bar{Z}^{u}_{\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor}|\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[|Z_{\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor}|{\bf 1}_{\{|Z_{\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor}|\leq n^{2}m^{\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor}\}}\right]\leq m^{\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor}.

Thus, for
0\leq k\leq M-i-1
, above yields

\prod_{j=k}^{M-i-1}m_{j}\leq\exp\left\{(M-i-k)\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor\log m-\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor\sum_{j=k}^{M-i-1}I\left(\frac{\Delta f(s_{i+j})}{\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor}-\varepsilon\right)\right\}.

(2.66)

Since
I(\cdot)
is convex, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M-i-k}\sum_{j=k}^{M-i-1}I\left(\frac{\Delta f(s_{i+j})}{\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor}-\varepsilon\right)

(2.67)

\displaystyle\geq I\left(\sum_{j=k}^{M-i-1}\frac{\Delta f(s_{i+j})-\varepsilon\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor}{(M-i-k)\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor}\right)=I\left(\frac{f(s_{M})-f(s_{i+k})}{(M-i-k)\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor}-\varepsilon\right).

Using above inequality, (
2.66
) yields

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq k\leq M-i-1}\prod_{j=k}^{M-i-1}m_{j}

(2.68)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left\{(M-i-k)\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor\log m-(M-i-k)\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor I\left(\frac{f(s_{M})-f(s_{i+k})}{(M-i-k)\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor}-\varepsilon\right)\right\}

(2.69)

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{(n-s_{i+k})\log m-(n-s_{i+k})I\left(\frac{f(s_{M})-f(s_{i+k})}{n-s_{i+k}}-\varepsilon\right)\right\}

(2.70)

\displaystyle\leq e^{(a-\varepsilon)n},

(2.71)

where the last inequality comes from the definition of bad path.

Let
\alpha>1
and
h>0
be some constants satisfying
\alpha+h<e^{\varepsilon-2\varepsilon^{\prime}}
. There exists a positive constant
r
depending only on
\alpha
such that for every
y\in[0,r]
, we have
e^{y}-1\leq\alpha y
. Set

\displaystyle\lambda_{j}:=m^{-2n^{\delta}},

(2.72)

which implies
\lambda_{j}\tilde{\nu}^{(j)}\leq n^{2}m^{-n^{\delta}}
. Thus, for
n
large enough,

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\lambda_{j}\tilde{\nu}^{(j)}}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}[1+\alpha\lambda_{j}\tilde{\nu}^{(j)}]\leq 1+\alpha\lambda_{j}m_{j}\leq e^{\alpha\lambda_{j}m_{j}}.\end{split}

Thus, by
[
1
, Proposition 2.1]
, there exists some
N^{\prime}>0
such that for
n>N^{\prime}
,
i=1,2,...,M-1
and
\lfloor e^{\varepsilon^{\prime}n}\rfloor\leq\ell\leq n^{2}m^{\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor}e^{\varepsilon^{\prime}n}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\bar{Z}_{s_{M}}(f)\geq e^{an},\ \bar{Z}_{s_{i}}(f)=\ell)

(2.73)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}(\tilde{Z}_{s_{M}}(f)\geq e^{an},\ \tilde{Z}_{s_{i}}(f)=\ell)

(2.74)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\left.\tilde{Z}_{s_{M}}(f)\geq\max\bigg\{\ell,\ (\alpha+h)^{n}\ell\max_{0\leq k<M-i}\prod_{j=k}^{M-i-1}m_{j}\bigg\}\right|\tilde{Z}_{s_{i}}(f)=\ell\right)

(2.75)

\displaystyle\leq n\exp\left(-\frac{\ell h}{\alpha+h}m^{-2n^{\delta}}+m^{-2n^{\delta}}\right),

(2.76)

where the second inequality follows by the fact that from (
2.68
), we have

(\alpha+h)^{n}\ell\max_{0\leq k<M-i}\prod_{j=k}^{M-i-1}m_{j}\leq e^{(\varepsilon-2\varepsilon^{\prime})n}n^{2}e^{\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor\log m+\varepsilon^{\prime}n}e^{n(a-\varepsilon)}\leq e^{an}.

Plugging (
2.73
) into (
2.62
) yields that for
n>N^{\prime}
and every bad path
f
,

\mathbb{P}(Z_{s_{M}}(f)\geq e^{an},\ E_{2}(n))\leq n^{4}e^{\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor\log m+\varepsilon^{\prime}n}\exp\left(-\frac{h\lfloor e^{\varepsilon^{\prime}n}\rfloor}{\alpha+h}m^{-2n^{\delta}}+m^{-2n^{\delta}}\right).

Thus,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(Z_{s_{M}}(f)\geq e^{an},\ E_{2}(n))=-\infty

uniformly in all bad paths
f
. Therefore, the desired upper bound follows.

3
Proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section, we are going to prove

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle<\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left[-\log\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}([xn,\infty))\geq e^{an})\right]

(3.1)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left[-\log\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}([xn,\infty))\geq e^{an})\right]\leq c^{*}\log b.

For the lower bound, the strategy is to force every particle to produce exactly
b
offsprings and have displacement near
L
before time
\lfloor c^{*}n\rfloor
.
c^{*}
is chosen such that particles at time
\lfloor c^{*}n\rfloor

can naturally produce
e^{an}/b^{\lfloor c^{*}n\rfloor}
particles in
[xn,\infty)
at time
n
. The proof of upper bound is basically the same as Theorem
1.1
. However, since under the assumptions of Theorem
1.2
, the probability that the branching random walk follows a good path is
0
(see (
3.18
) below), the decay rate comes from the bad path.

Proof.

Lower bound.
Recall that
b=\min\{k\geq m:p_{k}>0\}
. Let

u(c):=\log m-I\left(L+\frac{x-L}{1-c}\right)-\frac{a-\log b}{1-c},~c\in\left[0,\frac{x}{L}\right].

It is easy to see that
u(c)
is strictly increasing and continuous on
[0,\frac{x}{L}]
. Moreover,

\displaystyle u(0)

\displaystyle=\log m-I(x)-a+\log b<\log b,

(3.2)

\displaystyle u\left(\frac{x}{L}\right)

\displaystyle=\log m-\frac{a-\log b}{1-\frac{x}{L}}>\log m-(a-\log b)>\log b.

Thus, the equation
u(c)=\log b
has an unique solution on
(0,\frac{x}{L})
, denoted as
c^{*}
. Fix
\epsilon\in(0,\frac{x}{L}-c^{*}).

Let
t_{n}:=\lfloor(c^{*}+\epsilon)n\rfloor.
Since
u(c^{*}+\epsilon)>0
, there exists some
\eta\in(0,L)
such that

\log m-I\left(\frac{x-(L-\eta)(c^{*}+\epsilon)}{1-(c^{*}+\epsilon)}\right)>\frac{a-(c^{*}+\epsilon)\log b}{1-(c^{*}+\epsilon)}.

Thus, by Lemma

Proof of Theorem

In this section, we deal with the case of

. We are going to prove

\begin{split}a-\log m&\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log[-\log{\mathbb{P}}(Z_{n}([xn,\infty))\geq e^{an})]\\
&\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log[-\log{\mathbb{P}}(Z_{n}([xn,\infty))\geq e^{an})]\leq\log\alpha.\end{split}

For the lower bound, the strategy is to force all particles in the first

\alpha

Proof.

Lower bound.

X_{v}
stands for the displacement of
v
. Since
a\in[\log m,\log\mu]\cap\mathbb{R}
,
\alpha=\inf\{k:p_{k}>0,k\geq e^{a}\}\in[m,\infty)

\begin{split}{\mathbb{P}}(Z_{n}[xn,\infty)\geq e^{an})&\geq{\mathbb{P}}(|Z_{n}|={\alpha}^{n},\forall u\in Z_{n},S_{u}\geq xn)\\

&\geq p_{\alpha}^{\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}{\alpha}^{i}}{\mathbb{P}}(X\geq x)^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\alpha}^{i}}\\

which implies

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log[-\log{\mathbb{P}}(Z_{n}[xn,\infty)\geq e^{an})]\leq\log\alpha.

Upper bound.

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(Z_{n}[xn,\infty)\geq e^{an})

\displaystyle={\mathbb{P}}\left(W_{n}\geq e^{(a-\log m)n}\right)

(4.2)

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}\left[e^{\theta_{1}W_{n}}\right]e^{-\theta_{1}e^{(a-\log m)n}}.

Taking limits yields that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log[-\log{\mathbb{P}}(Z_{n}[xn,\infty)\geq e^{an})]\geq a-\log m.

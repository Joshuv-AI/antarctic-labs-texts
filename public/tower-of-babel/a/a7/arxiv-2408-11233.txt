On the Gaussian Kinematic Formula of R. Adler and J. Taylor
Joseph H.G. Fu

Abstract
. We apply methods of algebraic integral geometry to prove a special case of the Gaussian kinematic formula of Adler-Taylor. The idea, suggested already by Adler and Taylor, is to view the GKF as the limit of spherical kinematic formulas for spheres of large dimension N N and curvature 1 N \frac{1}{N} .



1.
Introduction

In this paper we formulate and prove a theorem of R. Adler and J. Taylor about excursion
sets of random functions on the sphere, in terms of the theory of smooth valuations in the sense of
S. Alesker. The valuations that arise are of two types. For simplicity we consider valuations
to be defined on sets with positive reach (PR sets) throughout.

1.1.
Two families of valuations

The first type consists of canonical valuations associated to any smooth Riemannian manifold
M
. Referring to
[
7
]
,
there exists an algebra
\mathcal{L}\mathcal{K}(M)
of smooth valuations, generated
by a single canonical element
t
such that
t^{\dim M}
is (up to scale) the volume measure and
t^{\dim M+1}=0
. The various powers
t^{k}

are the total integrals of the
Lipschitz-Killing curvatures
, in the sense that if
A\subset M

is a compact submanifold with boundary then

t^{k}(A)=\int_{A}\phi_{k}+\int_{\partial A}\psi_{k}

for suitable differential forms
\phi_{k}
,
\psi_{k}
that are polynomials in the curvature and connection forms (cf. e.g. (7), (8) of
[
fu_wan
]
).
In particular, the multiplicative unit element
t^{0}=\chi
, the Euler characteristic. These valuations are homogeneous:
t^{k}(\{rx:x\in A\})=r^{k}t^{k}(A)
.

The second type consists of the
Gaussian intrinsic volumes

\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1},\dots
on a
euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For measurable
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
put
\gamma_{0}(D)
to be the volume of
D
with respect to the standard unit covariance
Gaussian measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

\gamma_{0}(D):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi d}}\int_{D}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{2}}\,dx.

To define
\gamma_{i}(D)
for
i>0
we restrict to
D\in\PR
. Let
\tube(D,r)
be the set of points within distance
r
of
D
. Then

(1)

\gamma_{i}(D):=\left.\frac{d^{i}}{dr^{i}}\right|_{r=0}\gamma_{0}(\tube(D,r)).

1.2.
Statement of the theorem

The
canonical process
on the unit sphere
\{(x_{0},\dots,x_{n}):\sum x_{i}^{2}=1\}=:\mathbb{S}_{1}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is the random function

(2)

f(x)=f_{\omega}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}x_{i}\xi_{i}(\omega)

where
\xi_{0},\dots,\xi_{n}
are independent Gaussian random variables with mean zero and
variance 1. Let
f_{1},\dots,f_{d}
be independent copies of the canonical process on
\mathbb{S}_{1}^{n}
, and put

F:=(f_{1},\dots,f_{d}):\mathbb{S}_{1}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

(3)

\mathbb{E}[t^{m}(A\cap F^{-1}D])]=\sum_{k}\frac{(\frac{\pi}{2})^{\frac{k}{2}}}{k!\omega_{k}}t^{k+m}(A)\gamma_{k}(D)

for any compact sets
A\in\PR(\mathbb{S}_{1}^{n}),D\in\PR(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

In fact this theorem is a special case of a much broader theorem
[
1
]
of Adler-Taylor. Our proof of the special case follows their outline
[
2
]
, which is based on a beautiful
connection with spherical integral geometry via “Poincaré’s limit” (cf. (
52
) below).
Our contribution is to apply methods of algebraic
integral geometry to execute their proof scheme.

The scheme

3.1.
Another Poincaré limit

It will be helpful to view the matter in terms of probability densities on the space

of linear maps
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}

are restrictions of such maps to

with the euclidean space of

and set

are independent centered Gaussian random variables with unit variance.

The analytic core of the proof scheme of
[

rests on the following approximation to

.
For each

, consider the density
\Pi_{N}
on

g\mapsto\sqrt{N}\left(\begin{matrix}g_{10}&g_{11}&\dots&g_{1n}\\
g_{20}&g_{21}&\dots&g_{2n}\\
\dots&\dots&\dots&\dots\\
g_{d0}&g_{d1}&\dots&g_{dn}\\
\end{matrix}\right)

Then
\Pi_{N}\to\Pi_{\infty}
in a sense that we will make precise in Section

below. Denote by

the expectation with respect to the distribution

, and by
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
the expectation with respect to

. The first step in proving Theorem

is to show

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{N}[t^{m}(A\cap F^{-1}(D))]=\mathbb{E}_{\infty}[t^{m}(A\cap F^{-1}(D))].

3.2.
Enter the spherical kinematic formula

Consider the embedding
\iota_{N}:\mathbb{S}^{n}_{1}\hookrightarrow\Sigma^{N}
given by

\iota_{N}(y_{0},\dots,y_{n}):=(\sqrt{N}y_{0},\dots,\sqrt{N}y_{n},0\dots,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}.

Put
\Sigma^{N}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
for the sphere of curvature

(i.e. radius
\sqrt{N}
) and set

(10)

D_{N}:=\Sigma^{N}\cap\pi_{N}^{-1}D

where
\pi_{N}
is the orthogonal projection
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}

Then the restriction to
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{1}
of the random map

F=\pi_{N}\circ g\circ\iota_{N}

so if

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{N}[t^{m}(A\cap F^{-1}D)]]

\displaystyle=\int_{O(N+1)}t^{m}(A\cap\iota_{N}^{-1}g^{-1}\pi_{N}^{-1}D)\,dg

\displaystyle=\int_{O(N+1)}t^{m}(\iota_{N}^{-1}(\iota_{N}A\cap g^{-1}\pi_{N}^{-1}D))\,dg

(11)

\displaystyle=N^{-\frac{m}{2}}\int_{O(N+1)}t^{m}(\iota_{N}A\cap g^{-1}D_{N})\,dg

\displaystyle=2^{m}\int_{O(N+1)}\left(\frac{t}{\sqrt{4N}}\right)^{m}(\iota_{N}A\cap g^{-1}D_{N})\,dg.

Definition 3.1
.

It will be convenient to set

Denote by

The kinematic operators

Thus we may rewrite (

Theorem 5.3

Remark.
The functionals
|\tau_{i}|
may be regarded as

7.1.

Even though the natural domain of classical integral geometry remains mysterious, we know

S. Alesker and A. Bernig

The first observation is that

7.3.
The full Adler-Taylor theorem

Adler and Taylor framed the theorem differently,
in terms of an isometric embedding
\Phi=(\phi_{0},\dots,\phi_{n})
of a Riemannian manifold

M
into
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{1}
, and the resulting random function
\sum_{i=0}^{n}\xi_{i}\phi_{i}
on
M
(our framing
is equivalent). The full extent of their theorem goes well beyond this however, giving
similar conclusions for random functions
\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\xi_{i}\phi_{i}
corresponding to an
isometric embedding
(\phi_{0},\phi_{1},\dots)
of
M
into the unit sphere of
\ell^{2}
. Such random
functions may be characterized axiomatically as the
Gaussian random fields
described
in
[
2
]
and
[
1
]
. As Adler and Taylor emphasized, the resulting Lipschitz-Killing valuations
t^{k}
on

M
are Riemannian invariants, a fact that we call the Weyl principle.

One of the main points of the present paper is to emphasize the essentially valuation-theoretic
nature of Adler-Taylor theory. It would be desirable to give a proof also of the broader Adler-Taylor
theorem in these terms.

Another intriguing prospect of this subject is a possible avenue to prove the
Weyl principle for PR sets
A
– that is, that the restrictions to
A
of the Lipschitz-Killing valuations to any
such
A
depend only on its intrinsic metric structure. This is known if
A
is suitably stratified, but this is not the case for general PR sets.

Abstract
We show a flow-augmentation algorithm in directed graphs: There exists a randomized polynomial-time algorithm that, given a directed graph G G , two vertices s , t ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) s,t\in V(G) , and an integer k k , adds (randomly) to G G a number of arcs such that for every minimal s ​ t st -cut Z Z in G G of size at most k k , with probability 2 − poly ⁡ ( k ) 2^{-\mathrm{poly}(k)} the set Z Z becomes a minimum s ​ t st -cut in the resulting graph. We also provide a deterministic counterpart of this procedure. The directed flow-augmentation tool allows us to prove fixed-parameter tractability of a number of problems parameterized by the cardinality of the deletion set, whose parameterized complexity status was repeatedly posed as open problems: 1. Chain SAT , defined by Chitnis, Egri, and Marx [ESA’13, Algorithmica’17], 2. a number of weighted variants of classic directed cut problems, such as Weighted s ​ t st -Cut or Weighted Directed Feedback Vertex Set . By proving that Chain SAT is FPT, we confirm a conjecture of Chitnis, Egri, and Marx that, for any graph H H , if the List H H -Coloring problem is polynomial-time solvable, then the corresponding vertex-deletion problem is fixed-parameter tractable.

Introduction

The study of graph separation problems has been one of the more vivid areas of parameterized
complexity in the recent 10–15 years.
The term “graph separation problems” is here used widely, and captures a number of classic graph problems where,
given a (undirected or directed) graph

To illustrate the applicability of the directed flow-augmentation, let us first

Subsequent and accompanying work

This paper is the first part in a series that explores variants and
applicability of the new technique.
In this part, the main emphasis is on Theorem
1.1
itself, with aforementioned corollaries
stated as motivation.

In the second part
[

The proof is also arguably simpler.

In the
Min SAT
(\Gamma)

Here, we restrict only to binary alphabet.
Note that if

-cut

In the third part of the series

Here, flow-augmentation turned out to be pivotal for two new

In
[

, Sharma and a subset of the current authors explored further

Prior to

2.2
Color-coding and its derandomization

A standard color-coding step is the following randomized step: given two sets
A,B
,
we randomly sample a function
f:A\to B
. The goal is that for some unknown subset
A^{\prime}\subseteq A

and an unknown function
f^{\prime}:A^{\prime}\to B
,
we aim at
f
extending
f^{\prime}
. Obviously, this happens with probability
|B|^{-|A|^{\prime}}
.
In applications, usually
A^{\prime}
and
f^{\prime}
are some objects associated with the unknown sought solution.
For the deterministic version of flow-augmentation, we will need the following derandomization
statement (cf.
[
CFK+15
]
for a wider discussion).

Theorem 2.3
.

Given two finite sets
A
and
B
and an integer
k
, one can in

|A|^{\mathcal{O}(1)}\cdot 2^{\mathcal{O}(k\log|B|)}
time output a family
\mathcal{F}

of functions
A\to B
of size
2^{\mathcal{O}(k\log|B|)}\cdot\mathcal{O}(\log|A|)
such that
for every
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
of size at most
k
and every
f^{\prime}:A^{\prime}\to B
, there
exists
f\in\mathcal{F}
that extends
f^{\prime}
.

We will also need the following random separation version; this exact statement
is taken from
[
CCH+16
,
CKL+21
]
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Given a set
U
of size
n
and integers
0\leq a,b\leq n
, one
can in time
2^{\mathcal{O}(\min(a,b)\log(a+b))}n\log n

construct a family
\mathcal{F}
of at most
2^{\mathcal{O}(\min(a,b)\log(a+b))}\log n

subsets of
U
such that the following holds:
for any sets
A,B\subseteq U
with
A\cap B=\emptyset
,
|A|\leq a
, and
|B|\leq b
,
there exists a set
S\in\mathcal{F}
with
A\subseteq S
and
B\cap S=\emptyset
.

3
Directed Flow-Augmentation

An
instance
is a tuple
\mathcal{I}=(G,s,t,k)
where
G
is a directed graph,
s,t\in V(G)
, and
k
is a nonnegative integer.
An
instance with a flow
is a pair
(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{P})
where
\mathcal{I}=(G,s,t,k)
is an instance and

\mathcal{P}=\{P_{1},P_{2},\ldots,P_{\lambda}\}
is an

-flow.
In an

is a maximum
st
-flow.
With an instance with a flow

we associate the residual graph

.

In this section we prove the following results that generalize Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.

Theorem 3.1
.

There exists a randomized polynomial-time algorithm that, given an instance
\mathcal{I}=(G,s,t,k)
, returns a set
A\subseteq V(G)\times V(G)

and an
st
-maxflow
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}
in

such that
for every star
st
-cut

of size at most

,
with probability

the pair

is compatible with

.

Theorem 3.2
.

There exists an algorithm that, given an instance
\mathcal{I}=(G,s,t,k)
,
runs in time

and returns a set

\mathcal{A}
of size

such that every element of
\mathcal{A}
is a pair
(A,\widehat{\mathcal{P}})
where

and
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}
is an
st
-maxflow in
G+A
.
Furthermore, we have the following guarantee:
for every star
st
-cut

of size at most

there exists a pair
(A,\widehat{\mathcal{P}})\in\mathcal{A}
that is compatible with
Z
.

A few remarks are in place.
First, Lemma
2.2
ensures that if

is compatible with

, then

remains a star
st
-cut in

.
Second, we not only guarantee that

is compatible with

with good probability and
that

is an

-mincut in

, but also that

is a witnessing flow; in particular, that a witnessing flow exists at all.
Third, a usage of Theorem

makes sense even for cuts

with

being an

-mincut
for the sake of the witnessing flow: we may need to add some edges to

for a witnessing flow to exist, let alone being returned by the algorithm.
Finally, due to the convention that for

, any flow containing an

is an

-maxflow,
the algorithm is allowed to return a pair

contains an

(but clearly such a pair

).

We will prove Theorems

and
3.2

in parallel, as the proofs are mostly the same. The leading narration will be for the randomized case, as we find it more natural.
In most cases, the difference is that in the randomized case we perform a random guess, and in the
deterministic case we branch. This becomes a bit more tricky when the random step is
of color-coding type (cf. Section

), in particular when one combines

If

, then for every star

-cut

in

, so we can return

and

in Theorem

and

.
If

of size at most

being any

Henceforth we assume

We set a threshold

for later use. Note that while the algorithm is recursive and the value of

Lemma 3.3

Let

-maxflow in instance

3.2
Recursion structure and initial steps

The algorithms of Theorem
3.1

and of Theorem
3.2
are recursive.
The input is an instance with a flow
(\mathcal{I}=(G,s,t,k),\mathcal{P}=\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{\lambda}\})
and an integer
\kappa
.
In the randomized case, the goal is to return a set
A\subseteq V(G)\times V(G)
and an
st
-maxflow
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}
in
G+A

such that
\lambda_{G+A}(s,t)\geq\kappa

and
for every star
st
-cut
Z
with
|Z|\leq k
and
|\mathrm{core}_{G}(Z)|\geq\kappa
,

(A,\widehat{\mathcal{P}})
is compatible with
Z

(i.e.,
A
is compatible with
Z
,
\mathrm{core}_{G+A}(Z)
is an
st
-mincut in
G+A
, and
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}
is a witnessing flow for
Z
in
G+A
)
with good probability.
In the deterministic case, we want a small family
\mathcal{A}
of pairs
(A,\widehat{\mathcal{P}})

with
\lambda_{G+A}(s,t)\geq\kappa
and
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}
being an
st
-maxflow in
G+A
,
such that for every star
st
-cut
Z
with
|Z|\leq k
and
|\mathrm{core}_{G}(Z)|\geq\kappa
,
at least one element of
\mathcal{A}
is compatible with
Z
.

For an input
(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{P},\kappa)
, the algorithm may perform some randomized choices / branching steps and a number of recursive calls.
In each case, we will argue that the returned output (one of the returned outputs)
(A,\widehat{\mathcal{P}})
is compatible with
Z
, usually using the fact that a similar property holds for outputs of recursive subcalls.
While performing random choices or branching steps, we will always say what to aim for (e.g., for a vertex
v
we may randomly guess if it is on the
s
-side or
t
-side of the hypothethical cut
Z
)
and bound the probability of a correct choice.
Furthermore, for some recursive calls
(\mathcal{I}^{\prime},\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime})
we will define a star
st
-cut
Z^{\prime}
(depending usually on
Z
)
and prove that if the returned output
(A^{\prime},\widehat{\mathcal{P}}^{\prime})
is compatible with
Z^{\prime}
, then the final output
(A,\widehat{\mathcal{P}}
) is compatible with
Z
(if the returned family
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
contains
an element compatible with
Z^{\prime}
, then the final output
\mathcal{A}
contains an element
compatible with
Z
respectively in the deterministic case).

However, for sake of clarity in the description of the algorithm we will not perform a formal probability analysis.
(We perform a formal probability analysis for Theorem
3.1

in Section
3.6

and an analysis of the size of the output family

for Theorem
3.2

Base case: only self-loops in

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{P})
be an instance with maximum flow with proper boundaries and let
H
be its reachability pattern.
Assume
|V(H)|=|E(H)|
.
For every
i\in[\lambda]
,
v\in V(P_{i})\setminus\{t\}
, and a bottleneck edge

on

, if

lies before

on
P_{i}
, then any path from

to

visits

Consequently, any set consisting of one bottleneck edge from each path

is an
st
-mincut in

Proof.

For the first claim, let
Q
be a path from
s
to
v
in
G
avoiding

.
By the assumption on proper boundaries,

. If
Q
contains a vertex of
V(P_{j})\setminus\{s,t\}
for some

, then
a minimal subpath of

from a vertex of

to a vertex of

is a path also in

and thus
contradicts the assumption that

contains only self-loops.

Let

be an
st
-mincut witnessing that

is a bottleneck edge. Since

is before

on

, the concatenation of

and a subpath of

from

to

is

For the second claim, pick a bottleneck edge

on every path

and let

.
Assume

is not an
st
-mincut.
This implies that there exists a path

in

that starts on a path

in a vertex

before

, ends on a path

in a vertex

after

and does not contain any edge of

nor any internal vertex on paths of
\mathcal{P}
.
In particular,

does not contain any edge of

.

Note that if

and

Hence, either

, or

, or

∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{P})
be an instance with maximum flow with proper boundaries and let
H
be its reachability pattern.
Assume
|V(H)|=|E(H)|
and let

be a star
st
-cut in

.
Then exactly one of the following cases hold.

•

There exists
i\in[\lambda]
such that no edge of

is a bottleneck edge and
\mathrm{core}(Z)
is not an
st
-mincut, that is,

•

\mathrm{core}(Z)
is an
st
-mincut, that is,

, and no edge of

is a bottleneck edge of

Proof.

Assume first there is
i\in[\lambda]
such that no edge of

is a bottleneck edge. Then,

contains no

-mincut, in particular,

is not an
st
-mincut, and the first option holds.

In the other case, for every
i\in[\lambda]
, pick the first (closest to

) bottleneck edge

and denote

.
By Lemma

,

is an
st
-mincut.
Furthermore, for every

, every edge

on

is in the

. Since

We infer that for every

We proceed as follows with the randomized algorithm. If
\kappa=\lambda
, with probability 0.5 guess that

is an

-mincut in

.
In this case, for every

Denote

,

to be the path consisting of edges

,
(u_{i,1},v_{i,1})
,
(v_{i,1},u_{i,2})
, …,

. We return

By Lemma

,

is compatible with

.

If
\kappa>\lambda
, or
\kappa=\lambda
but (with the remaining probability 0.5) we guessed that

is not an

-mincut in

,
we randomly guess

, aiming at

We set

to be the copies of all bottleneck edges of

, recurse on

, obtaining a pair

, and return

.
Clearly, if the guess is correct,

is compatible with

. Furthermore, if
(A^{\prime},\widehat{\mathcal{P}}^{\prime})

, then

Since we added a copy of every bottleneck edge on one flow path,

The deterministic counterpart of the above process is the natural one.
First, we insert to the constructed family

the pair

from the first case above. Then, we branch into

,

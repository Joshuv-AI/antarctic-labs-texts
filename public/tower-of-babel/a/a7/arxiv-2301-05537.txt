Almost Surely T \sqrt{T} Regret for Adaptive LQR
Yiwen Lu, Yilin Mo

Abstract
The Linear-Quadratic Regulation (LQR) problem with unknown system parameters has been widely studied, but it has remained unclear whether 𝒪 ~ ​ ( T ) \tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{T}) regret, which is the best known dependence on time, can be achieved almost surely. In this paper, we propose an adaptive LQR controller with almost surely 𝒪 ~ ​ ( T ) \tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{T}) regret upper bound. The controller features a circuit-breaking mechanism, which falls back to a known stabilizing controller when the input signal or control gain is large, thereby circumventing potential safety breach and ensuring the convergence of the system parameter estimate. Meanwhile the circuit-breaking is shown to be triggered only finitely often, and hence has a negligible effect on the asymptotic performance of the controller. The proposed controller is also validated via simulation on Tennessee Eastman Process (TEP), a commonly used industrial process example.

From a theoretical perspective, we argue that it is highly difficult to extend these algorithms to provide stronger performance guarantees. For these algorithms, the probability of such catastrophic event is bounded by a positive

In this paper, we present an adaptive LQR controller with
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{T})
regret almost surely, only assuming the availability of a known stabilizing feedback gain, which is a common assumption in the literature.

II
Problem formulation

This paper considers a fully observed discrete-time linear system subject to Gaussian process noise, defined by the following equation:

x_{k+1}=Ax_{k}+Bu_{k}+w_{k},\quad k\in\mathbb{N}^{*},\quad x_{1}=0,

(1)

where
x_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the state,
u_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is the control input, and
w_{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{ i.i.d. }}}{{\sim}}\mathcal{N}(0,W)
is the process noise, where
W\succeq 0
.
It is assumed without loss of generality that
W=I_{n}
, but all the conclusions apply to general positive semidefinite
W
up to the scaling of constants.
It is also assumed that the system and input matrices
A,B
are unknown to the controller, but
(A,B)
is controllable.

We evaluate the system performance using an average infinite-horizon quadratic cost, which reflects the long-term cost effectiveness of control strategies:

J=\limsup_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=1}^{T}x_{k}^{\top}Qx_{k}+u_{k}^{\top}Ru_{k}\right],

(2)

where
Q\succ 0,R\succ 0
are fixed weighting matrices specified by the system operator.

It is well known that the optimal control law is the linear feedback control law of the form
u(x)=K^{*}x
, where the optimal feedback gain
K^{*}
can be specified as:

K^{*}=-\left(R+B^{\top}P^{*}B\right)^{-1}B^{\top}P^{*}A,

(3)

and
P^{*}
is the positive definite solution to the discrete algebraic Riccati equation:

P^{*}=A^{\top}P^{*}A-A^{\top}P^{*}B\left(R+B^{\top}P^{*}B\right)^{-1}B^{\top}P^{*}A+Q.

(4)

The corresponding optimal cost is

J^{*}=\tr\left(\mathbb{E}\left[w_{k}w_{k}^{\top}\right]P^{*}\right)=\tr(WP^{*})=\tr(P^{*}).

(5)

The matrix
P^{*}
also satisfies the discrete Lyapunov equation

(A+BK^{*})^{\top}P^{*}(A+BK^{*})-P^{*}+Q+K^{\top}RK=0,

(6)

which implies that there exists a scalar
0<\rho^{*}<1
such that

(A+BK^{*})^{\top}P^{*}(A+BK^{*})\prec\rho^{*}P^{*}.

(7)

We assume that the system is open-loop stable, i.e.,
\rho(A)<1
.
Consequently, there exists
P_{0}\succ 0
that satisfies the discrete Lyapunov equation

A^{\top}P_{0}A-P_{0}+Q=0,

(8)

and there exists a scalar
0<\rho_{0}<1
such that

A^{\top}P_{0}A\prec\rho_{0}P_{0}.

(9)

Remark 1
.

It has been commonly assumed in the literature
[
11
,
13
]
that
(A,B)
is stabilizable by a known feedback gain
K_{0}
, i.e.,
\rho(A+BK_{0})<1
.
We assume without loss of generality that the system is open-loop stable, i.e.,
K_{0}=0
, for the simplicity of notations.
However, all the results in this paper can be extended to
K_{0}\neq 0
, which we discuss in Section
IV-B
.

Since
A,B
are unknown to the controller in the considered setting, it is not possible to directly compute the optimal control law from (
3
) and (
4
). Instead, the controller learns the optimal control law online, whose performance is measured via the
regret
defined as follows:

\mathcal{R}(T)=\sum_{k=1}^{T}(x_{k}^{\top}Qx_{k}+u_{k}^{\top}Ru_{k})-TJ^{*}.

(10)

The goal of the controller is to minimize the asymptotic growth of
\mathcal{R}(T)
.

The threshold

The time

,
13
]
.

6.

On one hand, when the identification error in item

is sufficiently small, the certainty equivalent gain

is sufficiently close to the optimal gain

, and is therefore stabilizing. On the other hand, when both the modes under switching

are stable, a common Lyapunov function exists when the dwell time

is sufficiently large

, which can be reached since

by our design.

7.

After

is reached, the switched system is stable, and hence
\|x_{k}\|\lesssim\sqrt{\log(k/\delta)}
in light of item

). Meanwhile, the circuit-breaking threshold

grows at the order of
\log(k)
. The quantity

characterizes the time after which the upper bound on the state norm (and hence the upper bound on

) drops below the threshold

, and hence circuit-breaking is no longer triggered. ∎

Remark 5
.

Theorem

offers several key observations:

Items

) and

Item

Item

Finally, item

Remark 6
.

The convergence of system identification results (item

) relies on two factors:

1.

The persistence of excitation provided by the probing noise

, which decays at a rate of

.

2.

The bounded magnitude of the feedback control signal

These conditions are sufficient to ensure the convergence of parameter estimates, regardless of the stabilizability of individual

The following corollary characterizes the tail probabilities of

and

For

\mathbb{P}(T_{\text{stab}}\geq T)=\mathcal{O}(\exp(-c\sqrt{T})),

(25)

where

2.

For any

,

The bounds (

∎

Building upon the properties stated in Theorem
4
, a high-probability bound on the regret under the proposed controller can be ensured:

Theorem 8

(Non-asymptotic regret bound)
.

Given a probability
\delta
, there exists a constant
T_{0}\lesssim(1/\delta)^{1/4}
, such that for any fixed
T>T_{0}
, it holds with probability at least
1-\delta
that the regret defined in (
10
) satisfies

\mathcal{R}(T)\lesssim(1/\delta)^{1/4}+\sqrt{T}(\log(T/\delta))^{5}.

(28)

Proof Sketch.

We provide a brief and informal sketch of the proof here, and defer the rigorous proof to Appendix
B
.

With some algebra, the regret can be decomposed into (see Proposition
91
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(T)\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{=}}}\sum_{k=1}^{T}\|K_{k}-K^{*}\|^{2}\|x_{k}\|^{2}+

\displaystyle\quad\sum(\text{cross terms in~\eqref{eq:Ecross}})+\sum(\text{quadratic terms of noise}),

where the quadratic dependence on
\|K_{k}-K^{*}\|
arises from a perturbation result on the Riccati equation (see Lemma
22
).
Since the latter two terms can be bounded by
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{T})
according to Theorem
4
, items
2
) and
4
), the main task is to bound the first term.

We can further split the summation into two parts according to the time
T_{\text{stab}}
:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{T}\|K_{k}-K^{*}\|^{2}\|x_{k}\|^{2}=

\displaystyle\quad\underbrace{\sum_{k=1}^{T_{\text{stab}}}\|K_{k}-K^{*}\|^{2}\|x_{k}\|^{2}}_{\text{Term I}}+\underbrace{\sum_{k=T_{\text{stab}}+1}^{T}\|K_{k}-K^{*}\|^{2}\|x_{k}\|^{2}}_{\text{Term II}}.

To bound Term I, note that
\|K_{k}\|
is bounded by a constant by design, and that
\|x_{k}\|\sim\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(1)
according to Theorem
4
, item
3
), Term I can be bounded by
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(T_{\text{stab}})
. Invoking Theorem
4
, item
23
) with
\alpha=1/4
leads to the
(1/\delta)^{1/4}
dependence in the regret bound. The reason for the choice
\alpha=1/4
will be evident from the proofs of Corollary
9
and Theorem
10
: it ensures that
\delta(T)
for different
T
’s are summable while keeping
(1/\delta)^{\alpha}
at the order of
\sqrt{T}
, contributing to the final almost surely
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{T})
regret bound.

To bound Term II, note that
K_{k}=\hat{K}_{k}
for all
k>T_{\text{nocb}}
by definition of
T_{\text{nocb}}
, i.e., the certainty equivalent gain is always used. The error of the certainty equivalent gain is bounded by
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/\sqrt{T})
according to Theorem
4
, item
5
), summing up to
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{T})
.

∎

As corollaries of Theorem
28
, one can obtain a bound on the tail probability of
\mathcal{R}(T)
(Corollary
9
), and the main conclusion of this work, i.e., an almost sure bound on
\mathcal{R}(T)
(Theorem
10
):

Corollary 9
.

For sufficiently large
T
, it holds

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{R}(T)\geq C_{R}\sqrt{T}(\log(T))^{5}\right)\leq\frac{1}{T^{2}},

where
C_{R}
is a system-dependent constant.

Proof.

The conclusion follows from invoking Theorem
28
with
\delta=1/T^{2}
.
∎

Theorem 10

(Asymptotic, almost sure regret bound)
.

It holds almost surely that

\mathcal{R}(T)=\tilde{\mathcal{O}}\left(\sqrt{T}\right).

Proof.

By Theorem
9
, we have

\sum_{T=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{R}(T)\geq C_{R}\sqrt{T}(\log(T))^{5}\right)<+\infty,

i.e., the sum of probabilities of the events
\left\{\mathcal{R}(T)\geq C_{R}\sqrt{T}(\log(T))^{5}\right\}
is finite.
By Borel-Cantelli lemma, it holds almost surely that the aforementioned event occurs finitely often,
i.e.,

\mathcal{R}(T)=\tilde{\mathcal{O}}\left(\sqrt{T}\right),\quad\text{a.s.}\qed

Remark 11
.

The controllability of estimated system matrices
(\hat{A}_{k},\hat{B}_{k})
does not affect the almost sure regret bound. The set of uncontrollable
(A,B)
pairs forms a proper algebraic variety, which has Lebesgue measure zero
[
26
]
. According to (
11
) and the presence of Gaussian noise, the random matrices
(\hat{A}_{k},\hat{B}_{k})
have a distribution that is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure on the space of all matrix pairs. Therefore, for any fixed
k
, the probability of
(\hat{A}_{k},\hat{B}_{k})
being uncontrollable is zero. By the countable additivity of probability measures, this extends to all
k
simultaneously, ensuring that uncontrollability events do not affect our almost sure results.
Furthermore, as shown in the proof of Proposition
24
, on the high-probability event
\mathcal{E}_{\text{est}}
,
(\hat{A}_{k},\hat{B}_{k})
converges to
(A,B)
, which implies that
(\hat{A}_{k},\hat{B}_{k})
is controllable for sufficiently large
k
. This provides a more formal alternative to the Lebesgue measure argument, demonstrating that the potential uncontrollability of
(\hat{A}_{k},\hat{B}_{k})
for some
k
does not affect our almost sure regret bound.

IV-B

Extension to open-loop unstable systems

This subsection extends the main results to the case where the system is open-loop unstable, but pre-stabilized by a known feedback gain
K_{0}
, i.e.,
\rho(A)\geq 1
but
\rho(A+BK_{0})<1
, which is a common assumption in the literature
[
11
,
13
]
. Such
K_{0}
can be classical controllers commonly used in engineering practice
[
27
,
28
]
, or can result from adaptive or learning-based stabilization
[
29
,
14
,
30
]
.

In this case, the system can be rewritten as:

x_{k+1}=A^{\prime}x_{k}+Bu_{k}^{\prime}+w_{k},

(29)

where
A^{\prime}=A+BK_{0},u_{k}^{\prime}=u_{k}-K_{0}x_{k}
.
It follows that the optimal control law for the system (
29
) is
u_{k}^{\prime}=(K^{*}-K_{0})x_{k}
, where
K^{*}
is the optimal control gain (
3
) determined by
(A,B,Q,R)
.
Therefore, Algorithm
1
applied to the system (
29
) will lead to the control law

u_{k}^{\prime}=\begin{cases}(\hat{K}_{k}-K_{0})x_{k}+u_{k}^{pr},&\text{if circuit breaking is not active},\\
u_{k}^{pr},&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where
\hat{K}_{k}
is certainty equivalent gain determined by
(\hat{A},\hat{B},Q,R)
,
u_{k}^{pr}
is the probing noise, and similarly to Lines
10
-
18
in Algorithm
1
, circuit breaking is triggered when
\max\{\|(\hat{K}_{k}-K_{0})x_{k}\|,\|\hat{K}_{k}-K_{0}\|\}>M_{k}
and lasts for
t_{k}
steps.

To show that the proposed regret bound still holds, define
X_{k}=[x_{k}^{\top}\;x_{k-1}^{\top}\;u_{k-1}^{\prime\top}]^{\top}
. It follows that

X_{k+1}=\begin{bmatrix}A^{\prime}&0&0\\
I&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{bmatrix}X_{k}+\begin{bmatrix}B\\
0\\
I\end{bmatrix}u_{k}^{\prime}+\begin{bmatrix}I\\
0\\
0\end{bmatrix}w_{k},

and the stepwise cost can be equivalently written as

\displaystyle x_{k}^{\top}Qx_{k}+u_{k}^{\top}Ru_{k}=

\displaystyle\qquad X_{k+1}^{\top}\begin{bmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&Q+K_{0}^{\top}RK_{0}&K_{0}^{\top}R\\
0&RK_{0}&R\end{bmatrix}X_{k+1}+u_{k}^{\prime\top}0_{m\times m}u_{k}^{\prime}.

The problem is then reduced to the case of open-loop stable system with standard LQR cost. Therefore, the proposed almost surely
\mathcal{\tilde{O}}(\sqrt{T})
regret still holds.

VI
Conclusion

This paper proposes an adaptive LQR controller capable of achieving
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{T})
regret almost surely.
A key aspect of the controller design is a circuit-breaking mechanism, which ensures the convergence of the parameter estimate while being triggered only finitely often, thus having negligible effect on asymptotic performance.

Future research directions include: i) extending the circuit-breaking mechanism to the partially observed LQG setting, and ii) considering more general process noise distributions, such as sub-Weibull distributions
[
34
]
. This analysis could build on identification bounds in
[
35
]
and switching costs in
[
36
]
.

So far, we have proved that switching between the two stable modes in (
103
) without a dwell time may lead to the destabilization of the system. By contrast, it is evident from the main results that the introduction of a dwell time
t_{k}=\lfloor\log(k)\rfloor
can prevent the destabilization almost surely. This comparison is also illustrated by numerical simulation: in Fig.

w_{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{ i.i.d. }}}{{\sim}}\mathcal{N}(0,0.01I)

104
), has the state norm oscillating around the threshold
M_{k}
, and hence diverging to infinity. This diverging pattern is consistent with the proof of Proposition
25

t_{k}=\lfloor\log(k)\rfloor

A_{0}

\lfloor\log(k)\rfloor

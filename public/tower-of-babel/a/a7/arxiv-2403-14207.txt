Some Observations on projective Stiefel Manifolds
Bikramjit Kundu, Sudeep Podder

Abstract
. We compute the upper characteristic rank of the projective Stiefel manifolds over ℝ , ℂ \mathbb{R},\mathbb{C} and ℍ \mathbb{H} and of the flip Stiefel manifolds. We provide bounds for the cup \mathrm{cup} lengths of these spaces. We also provide necessary conditions for existence of S 3 S^{3} -map between quaternionic Stiefel manifolds using Fadell-Husseini index.

Introduction

This article concerns two independent problems of computing the
upper characteristic rank
of the projective Stiefel manifolds and that of flip Stiefel manifolds, and determining the existence of
S^{3}
-equivariant maps between quaternionic Stiefel manifolds. Recall that the cohomology algebra of the real projective space
\mathbb{R}P^{n}
is generated by the Stiefel-Whitney class of the tautological line bundles over
\mathbb{R}P^{n}
. In fact, for
n
odd, the cohomology algebra of the unoriented Grassmannian
G_{n,k}
of
k
-planes in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is generated by the Stiefel-Whitney classes of its tangent bundle (See
[
21
, 2.1. Lemma]
). It is, therefore, natural to ask whether the cohomology algebra of a CW-complex
Y
can be generated by the Stiefel-Whitney classes of some vector bundle over
Y
, or, at least, what is the largest integer
r\leq\dim(Y)
such that all the cohomology groups up to dimension
r
is generated by Stiefel-Whitney classes of some vector bundle over
Y
.

The characteristic rank
\mathrm{charrank}(\xi)
of a vector bundle
\xi
over a CW-complex
Y
is the largest integer
r\leq\dim(Y)
such that all the cohomology classes in
H^{i}(Y;\mathbb{Z}_{2}),i\leq r
can be expressed as a polynomial in the Stiefel-Whitney classes of
\xi
. The
upper characteristic rank
of
Y
is defined to be
\max\{\mathrm{charrank}(\xi)

|\penalty\ \xi
is a bundle over
Y\}
.

The upper characteristic rank of a CW-complex is an important invariant and has implications to the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-cup length, Betti number and LS-category (see
[
13
]
,
[
3
]
). In
[
14
]
, Korbaš showed that if
M
is a connected smooth manifold, unoriented cobordant to zero, and
\tilde{H}^{k}(M;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
is the first nontrivial reduced cohomology group, then

\mathrm{cup}(M)\leq 1+\frac{d-1+\mathrm{charrank(TM)}}{k},

provided
\mathrm{charrank(TM)}\leq d-2
. He introduced the term upper characteristic rank
charrank(M)
of a closed smooth manifold
M
and defined it to be the upper characteristic rank of the tangent bundle
TM
of
M
, and asked what is the
\mathrm{charrank}(M)
for all closed connected manifolds. The definition was further generalized by Naolekar and Thakur in
[
16
]
to all vector bundles over CW-complexes and introduced the upper characteristic rank of a CW-complex. They also generalized the above result of Korbsš (See
[
16
, Theorem 1.2]
). In
[
2
]
, the authors have derived upper bounds
for the characteristic rank of the oriented Grassmann manifold
\tilde{G}_{n,k}
if
n
is odd, with a sharp upper bound for
\mathrm{charrank}(\tilde{G}_{n,2})
, by computing the characteristic rank and provided a general upper bound for the characteristic rank of smooth null-cobordant manifolds. Although the computation of upper characteristic rank is in general a complex problem and not much seems to be known, it is an interesting problem in it’s own right. In
[
15
]
,
[
19
]
, the authors determined the characteristic rank of the canonical vector bundle over the oriented Grassmann manifold
\tilde{G}_{k,n}
of
k
-dimensional subspaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for specific values of
k
and
n
and determined the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-cup-length for these spaces. The characteristic rank of smooth null-cobordant manifolds were computed by Balko and Korbas in
[
2
]
. In
[
1
]
, they have also computed bounds for the characteristic rank of null cobordant manifolds and of total spaces of smooth fiber bundles whose fibers are totally non-homologous to zero. For
n\geq 8
, in
[
4
]
, Basu and Chakraborty have calculated the upper characteristic rank of
\tilde{G}_{n,3}
to be the characteristic rank of the oriented tautological bundle over
\tilde{G}_{n,3}
. For the product of spheres, real and complex projective spaces, the Dold manifolds and the stunted projective spaces, the characteristic rank of vector bundles was computed in
[
16
]
. In
[
13
]
the authors have given a complete description of the upper characteristic rank of the Stiefel manifolds
\mathbb{F}V_{n,k}
where
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}
or
\mathbb{H}
, except for few cases where they could provide lower bound for upper characteristic rank (see Theorem
1.1.3
).

Recall that the Stiefel manifold
\mathbb{F}V_{n,k}
is the space of orthonormal-
k
frames in
\mathbb{F}^{n}
. Note that
\mathbb{F}V_{n,k}\subset\prod_{k}\mathbb{F}S^{n-1}
where

is the underlying sphere inside

. The Stiefel manifold inherits the subspace topology from the product of spheres in
\mathbb{F}^{n}
. The projective Stiefel manifolds

by the free left action, given by

, of the group of absolute value 1 elements of
\mathbb{F}
. On the other hand, the flip Stiefel manifolds were recently introduced by Basu
et al.

[

be the cyclic group of order

and

\mbox{Index}_{G}(X;R)=\ker p^{*}:H^{*}(BG;R)\to H^{*}(X_{hG};R).

-spaces

]

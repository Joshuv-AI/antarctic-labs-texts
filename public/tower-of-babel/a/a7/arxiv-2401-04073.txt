The number of preimages of iterates of ϕ \phi and σ \sigma
Agbolade Akande

Abstract




1
Introduction

This paper explores the preimages of iterates of Euler’s totient function
\phi
and the sum-of-divisors function
\sigma
. Define
N_{a}^{k}(n)=\#\{m:a_{k}(m)=n\}
, where
a_{k}
is the
k
-th iterate of the arithmetic function
a
. Our main theorem is an upper bound, for each fixed
k
, on the functions
N_{\phi}^{k}(n)
and
N_{\sigma}^{k}(n)
. This confirms a conjecture of de Koninck and Katai
[
1
]
.

We first recall what is known about
N_{\sigma}^{1}(n)
and
N_{\phi}^{1}(n)
. Define

L(n)=\exp(\log n\cdot\log\log\log n/\log\log n).

Erdős showed that for a certain absolute constant
c>0
, and all large enough
n
,

N_{\phi}^{1}(n)\leq n/L(n)^{c}

[
2
]
. Carl Pomerance found a more optimal upper bound for the number of preimages of
\phi
and
\sigma

[
3
]
:

Theorem A

(Pomerance)
.

As
n\rightarrow\infty
,

N^{1}_{\phi}(n)\leq\frac{n}{L(n)^{1+o(1)}},

N^{1}_{\sigma}(n)\leq\frac{n}{L(n)^{1+o(1)}}.

The proof for
N^{1}_{\sigma}(n)
is not given explicitly in
[
3
]
but it follows the same reasoning for
N_{\phi}^{1}(n)
. (This observation is made explicitly in
[
4
]
). Erdős’ and Pomerance’s proofs require bounds on the number of smooth shifted primes (shifted primes
p-1
without large prime factors). Under a plausible conjecture on the distribution of these primes, Pomerance shows in
[
3
]
that the bounds in Theorem A are optimal, in the sense that the exponent
1+o(1)
cannot be replaced with
1-\epsilon
for any fixed
\epsilon>0
. We will later use this idea to formulate a conjecture for a lower bound.

Our upper bounds on
N_{\phi}^{k}(n)
and
N_{\sigma}^{k}(n)
borrow ideas from work of Pollack and Vandehey
[
5
]
and Pollack
[
6
]
. The proof goes by induction on
k
, the input for the case
k=1
being the results of Pomerance (though the weaker results of Erdős would also suffice). Let
a\in\{\phi,\sigma\}
. Observe that if
a_{k}(m)=n
, then
a(m)
belongs to the set
S=\{\ell:a_{k-1}(\ell)=n\}
counted by
N_{a}^{k-1}(n)
. We can assume by our the induction hypothesis that we have a usable upper bound on
|S|
. We split
S
into three separate pieces. In the first piece, we put those
m\in S
with many large prime factors. In the second piece, we place those
m
which do not have many large prime factors, but have many prime factors in total. The final piece contains the leftovers. We then estimate the size, under
a^{-1}
, of each of these parts; this is carried out in sections
3
and
4
after establishing some technical preliminary estimates in Section
2
.

For each positive integer
k
and each real number
\beta
, define

L_{k,\beta}(n)=\exp\left(\log n\cdot(\log\log\log n)^{\beta}/(\log\log n)^{k}\right).

The end result is as follows:

Theorem 1
.

Fix
a\in\{\phi,\sigma\}
. Fix a positive integer
k
and
\beta<k-1
. As
n\rightarrow\infty
,

N_{a}^{k}(n)\leq\frac{n}{L_{k,\beta+1}(n)^{1+o(1)}}.

Theorem
1
establishes a strong form of a conjecture of de Koninck and Katai
[
1
, Conjecture 2]
, who had
c_{k}n/(\log{n})^{9}
in place of
n/L_{k,\beta+1}(n)^{1+o(1)}
. As a consequence, we derive the following theorem, which was proved conditionally on their conjecture in
[
1
]
:

Theorem B
.

Let
e(y)=e^{2\pi iy}
, and let
\mathcal{M}_{1}
be the set of all multiplicative arithmetic functions
f
such that
\lvert f(n)\rvert=1
for all integers
n
. Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
, then for almost all real numbers
\alpha\in(0,1)

\sup\limits_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{1}}\frac{1}{x}\left\lvert\sum\limits_{n\leq x}f(n)e(\alpha\phi_{k}(n))\right\rvert\rightarrow 0

\sup\limits_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{1}}\frac{1}{x}\left\lvert\sum\limits_{n\leq x}f(n)e(\alpha\sigma_{k}(n))\right\rvert\rightarrow 0

as
x\rightarrow\infty
.

Notation and conventions.
The letter
p
is reserved throughout for primes. We use
\Omega(n)
for the number of prime factors of
n
counted with multiplicity and
\Omega_{>z}(n)
for the number of prime factors of
n
exceeding
z
, counted with multiplicity.

The
z
-rough and
z
-smooth parts of a positive integer
m
denotes the largest divisors of
m
composed of primes exceeding
z
, and not exceeding
z
respectively.

We will use
\Gamma(z)
to represent the gamma function defined by the improper integral:

\Gamma(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{z-1}e^{-t}dt.

2
Bounds on some moment generating functions

In this section, we will find an upper bound on some moment generating functions involving the total number of prime factors and the number of large prime factors of
a(n)
.

The next lemma gives our estimate for large prime factors of
a(n)
.

Fix
\eta\in(0,1)
and let

z=\exp((\log\log x)^{1/2}),

A=(\log\log x)^{1-\eta}.

Lemma 1
.

Fix
a\in\{\phi,\sigma\}
. As
x\rightarrow\infty

\sum\limits_{n\leq x}A^{\Omega_{>z}(a(n))}\leq x\exp\left(O\left(\frac{\log x}{\exp((\log\log x)^{\eta/2})}\right)\right).

Proof.

Let
c\in(1,2)
. By Rankin’s trick
[
7
]
,

\sum\limits_{n\leq x}A^{\Omega_{>z}(a(n))}\leq x^{c}\sum\limits_{n\leq x}\frac{A^{\Omega_{>z}(a(n))}}{n^{c}}\leq x^{c}\prod\limits_{p\leq x}\left(1+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{A^{\Omega_{>z}(a(p^{k}))}}{p^{kc}}\right)

\leq x^{c}\exp\left(\sum\limits_{p\leq x}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{A^{\Omega_{>z}(a(p^{k}))}}{p^{kc}}\right).

Notice that

\Omega_{>z}(a(p^{k}))\leq\frac{\log(a(p^{k}))}{\log z}\leq\frac{\log(\sigma(p^{k}))}{\log z}=\frac{\log\left(\frac{p^{k+1}-1}{p-1}\right)}{\log z}\leq\frac{(k+1)\log p-\log(p-1)}{\log z}.

Applying this back to the original sum,

\sum\limits_{n\leq x}A^{\Omega_{>z}(a(n))}\leq x^{c}\exp\left(\sum\limits_{2\leq p\leq x}\left[\frac{A^{\Omega_{>z}(a(p))}}{p^{c}}+\frac{1}{A^{\frac{\log(p-1)}{\log z}}}\sum\limits_{k\geq 2}\frac{A^{\frac{(k+1)\log p}{\log z}}}{p^{kc}}\right]\right).

For
x
large,
A<z^{1/3}
so that

A^{\frac{\log p}{\log z}}/p^{c}\leq z^{\frac{\log p}{3\log z}}/p^{c}\leq p^{1/3-c}<p^{-2/3}\leq 2^{-2/3}.

Therefore,

\sum\limits_{k\geq 2}\frac{A^{\frac{(k+1)\log p}{\log z}}}{p^{kc}}\ll\frac{A^{\frac{3\log p}{\log z}}}{p^{2c}}.

Thus,

\sum\limits_{n\leq x}A^{\Omega_{>z}(a(n))}\leq x\exp\left((c-1)\log x+O\left(\sum\limits_{2\leq p\leq x}\frac{A^{\Omega_{>z}(a(p))}}{p^{c}}+\sum\limits_{2\leq p\leq x}\frac{A^{\frac{3\log p-\log(p-1)}{\log z}}}{p^{2c}}\right)\right).

Notice that
\frac{A^{\frac{3\log p-\log(p-1)}{\log z}}}{p^{2c}}=\frac{\left(\frac{p^{3}}{p-1}\right)^{\frac{\log A}{\log z}}}{p^{2c}}\ll\frac{p^{\frac{2\log A}{\log z}}}{p^{2c}}\leq p^{-4/3}
. Hence,
\sum\limits_{2\leq p\leq x}\frac{A^{\frac{3\log p-\log(p-1)}{\log z}}}{p^{2c}}\leq\sum\limits_{2\leq p\leq x}p^{-4/3}=O(1)
.

Thus, we need to bound
\sum\limits_{p\leq x}\frac{A^{\Omega_{>z}(a(p))}}{p^{c}}
. Let
g
be the arithmetic function defined implicitly by
A^{\Omega_{>z}(d)}=\sum\limits_{r\mid d}g(r)
. Then
g
is multiplicative and
g(p^{e})=A^{e}-A^{e-1}
when
p>z
and
g(p^{e})=0
otherwise. Hence,

\sum\limits_{p\leq T}A^{\Omega_{>z}(a(p))}=\sum\limits_{r\leq T}g(r)\sum\limits_{\underset{r\mid a(p)}{p\leq T}}1.

Since
a\in\left\{\phi,\sigma\right\}
,
\sum\limits_{\underset{r\mid a(p)}{p\leq T}}1\leq\frac{(T+1)}{r}
. Hence,

\sum\limits_{p\leq T}A^{\Omega_{>z}(a(p))}\leq(T+1)\sum\limits_{r\leq T}\frac{g(r)}{r}=(T+1)\prod\limits_{z<p\leq T}\left(1+\frac{A-1}{p}+\frac{A^{2}-A}{p^{2}}+\cdots\right)

\leq(T+1)\exp\left((A-1)\sum\limits_{z<p\leq T}\frac{1}{p}\right)\exp\left(\sum\limits_{z<p\leq T}\frac{A^{2}-A}{p^{2}}+\frac{A^{3}-A^{2}}{p^{3}}+\cdots\right).

The geometric ratio of successive terms in the second summation is
A/p\leq A/z<\frac{1}{2}
. Hence,

\sum\limits_{z<p\leq T}\left(\frac{A^{2}-A}{p^{2}}+\frac{A^{3}-A^{2}}{p^{3}}+\cdots\right)<2\sum\limits_{z<p\leq T}\frac{A^{2}-A}{p^{2}}\leq 2A^{2}\sum\limits_{z<p\leq T}\frac{1}{p^{2}}\ll A^{2}/z\ll 1.

By the prime number theorem,

\sum\limits_{z<p\leq T}\frac{1}{p}=\log\frac{\log T}{\log z}+O\left(\frac{1}{(\log z)^{2}}\right)

so that

(A-1)\sum\limits_{z<p\leq T}\frac{1}{p}=(A-1)\log\frac{\log T}{\log z}+O(1).

Hence,

S(T)=\sum\limits_{p\leq T}A^{\Omega_{>z}(a(p))}\ll\begin{cases}T&\text{if $T\leq z$,}\\
T(\log T/\log z)^{A-1}&\text{if $T>z$}.\end{cases}

Using Abel’s summation and the fact that
1<c<2
,

\sum\limits_{p\leq T}\frac{A^{\Omega_{>z}(p-1)}}{p^{c}}=\int_{1}^{\infty}t^{-c}dS(t)\leq c\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{S(t)}{t^{c+1}}dt\ll\int_{1}^{z}\frac{S(t)}{t^{c+1}}dt+\int_{z}^{\infty}\frac{S(t)}{t^{c+1}}dt

\ll\int_{1}^{z}\frac{1}{t}dt+\frac{1}{(\log z)^{A-1}}\int_{z}^{\infty}\frac{(\log t)^{A-1}}{t^{c}}dt.

We know
\int_{1}^{z}\frac{1}{t}dt=\log z
and

\int_{z}^{\infty}\frac{(\log t)^{A-1}}{t^{c}}dt\leq\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{(\log t)^{A-1}}{t^{c}}dt.

Let
t=e^{u/(c-1)}
and
dt=\frac{1}{(c-1)}e^{u/(c-1)}du
. Then,

\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{(\log t)^{A-1}}{t^{c}}dt=\frac{1}{(c-1)^{A}}\int_{1}^{\infty}u^{A-1}e^{-u}du=\frac{\Gamma(A)}{(c-1)^{A}}.

Combining all the inequalities

\sum\limits_{n\leq x}A^{\Omega_{>z}(a(n))}\leq x\cdot z^{O(1)}\cdot\exp\left((c-1)\log x+O\left(\frac{\Gamma(A)}{(c-1)^{A}(\log z)^{A-1}}\right)\right).

Let

c=1+\frac{A}{(\log x)^{1/(A+1)}(\log z)^{(A-1)/(A+1)}}.

For this value of
c
,

(c-1)\log x+O\left(\frac{\Gamma(A)}{(c-1)^{A}(\log z)^{A-1}}\right)\ll{A}(\log x/\log z)^{1-\frac{1}{A+1}}(\log z)^{\frac{1}{A+1}}\ll\frac{\log x}{\exp((\log\log x)^{\eta/2})}.

∎

Now we turn attention to the total number of prime factors of
a(n)
,

Lemma 2
.

Fix
1\leq B<\sqrt[3]{2}
and fix
a\in\{\phi,\sigma\}
. As
x\rightarrow\infty
,

\sum\limits_{n\leq x}B^{\Omega(a(n))}\leq x\exp(O_{B}((\log x)^{3/4})).

Proof.

Let
c\in(1,2)
. By Rankin’s trick,

\sum\limits_{n\leq x}B^{\Omega(a(n))}\leq x^{c}\sum\limits_{n\leq x}\frac{B^{\Omega(a(n))}}{n^{c}}\leq x^{c}\prod\limits_{p\leq x}\left(1+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{B^{\Omega(a(p^{k}))}}{p^{kc}}\right)

\leq x^{c}\exp\left(\sum\limits_{p\leq x}\left[\frac{B^{\Omega(a(p))}}{p^{c}}+\sum\limits_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{B^{\Omega(a(p^{k}))}}{p^{kc}}\right]\right).

Notice that
\Omega(a(p^{k}))\leq\frac{(k+1)\log p-\log(p-1)}{\log 2}
. Hence,

\sum\limits_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{B^{\Omega(a(p^{k}))}}{p^{kc}}\leq\frac{B^{\log(p)/\log(2)}}{B^{\log(p-1)/\log(2)}}\left(\sum\limits_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{B^{k\log(p)/\log(2)}}{p^{kc}}\right).

Since
B<\sqrt[3]{2}<2
,

B^{\log(p)/\log(2)}=p^{\log B/\log 2}=p^{1-(1-\log B/\log 2)}<p.

This implies

\sum\limits_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{B^{k\log(p)/\log(2)}}{p^{kc}}\ll_{B}\frac{B^{2\log(p)/\log(2)}}{p^{2c}}

and,

x^{c}\exp\left(\sum\limits_{p\leq x}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{B^{\Omega(a(p^{k}))}}{p^{kc}}\right)\leq x\exp\left((c-1)\log x+O_{B}\left(\sum\limits_{p\leq x}\left[\frac{B^{\Omega(a(p))}}{p^{c}}+\frac{B^{\frac{3\log(p)-\log(p-1)}{\log(2)}}}{p^{2c}}\right]\right)\right).

From our choices of
B
and
c
,

3\log(B)/\log(2)-2c<3\log(\sqrt[3]{2})/\log(2)-2=-1.

Therefore,

\sum\limits_{p\leq x}\frac{B^{\frac{3\log(p)-\log(p-1)}{\log(2)}}}{p^{2c}}\leq\sum\limits_{p\leq x}\frac{B^{\frac{3\log(p)}{\log(2)}}}{p^{2c}}=\sum\limits_{p\leq x}p^{3\log(B)/\log(2)-2c}=O(1).

Since
a(p)\in\{p-1,p+1\}
,
\sum\limits_{p\leq T}{B^{\Omega(a(p))}}=\sum\limits_{p\leq T}{B^{\Omega(a(p))}}\leq\sum\limits_{d\leq T+1}{2^{\Omega(d)}}\ll T(\log T)^{2}
for all
T\geq 2
(see
[
8
, Exercise 57, p. 59]
).

Using partial summation as in the proof of Lemma
1
,

\sum\limits_{p\leq x}\frac{B^{\Omega(a(p))}}{p^{c}}\ll\frac{1}{(c-1)^{3}}

so that

\sum\limits_{n\leq x}B^{\Omega(a(n))}\leq x\exp\left((c-1)\log x+O_{B}\left(\frac{1}{(c-1)^{3}}\right)\right).

Taking
c=1+\frac{1}{(\log x)^{1/4}}
completes the proof.
∎

Similar to the proof in Lemma
1
, it can be shown that
\sum\limits_{d\leq T+1}B^{\Omega(d)}\ll T(\log T)^{B-1}
, which would allow
c=1+\frac{1}{(\log x)^{1/3}}
. Hence, Lemma
2
can be improved as follows:

\sum\limits_{n\leq x}B^{\Omega(a(n))}\leq x\exp(O_{B}((\log x)^{2/3})).

3
Proof of Theorem
1

This section will cover the proof of Theorem
1
by induction on
k
. For
k=1
, Theorem
1
follows from the sharper Theorem A. Now we assume the inductive hypothesis:

As
n\rightarrow\infty
, for any
\beta<k-1
,
N_{a}^{k}(n)\leq\frac{n}{L_{k,\beta+1}(n)^{1+o(1)}}

and we prove

As
n\rightarrow\infty
, for any
\alpha<k
,
N_{a}^{k+1}(n)\leq\frac{n}{L_{k+1,\alpha+1}(n)^{1+o(1)}}
.

To find an upper bound for the size of the preimages for the proof, we use that
\phi(n)
and
\sigma(n)
are within a small factor of
n
(see
[
9
, pg 266-267]
):

Lemma 3
.

There are positive constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
such that for all positive integers n,

\sigma(n)\leq c_{1}n\log\log(3n)
and
\phi(n)\geq c_{2}n/\log\log(3n)
.

Also, an estimate is needed from
[
5
]
to bound the number of
m
for which
a(m)
is a multiple of a given integer
d
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
a(n)
be any of the functions
\phi(n)
or
\sigma(n)
. Let
d
be a positive integer and let
\ell=\Omega(d)
. For each
x\geq 1
, the number of
n\leq x
where
d\mid a(n)
is at most

\frac{x}{d}(8\ell\log^{2}(ex))^{\ell}.

It follows from Lemma 3 that for each fixed
k
and all large enough positive integers
n
, any solution
m
of the equation
a_{k}(m)=n
satisfies
m\leq n\log(2n)
.

Thus, we set
x=n\log(2n)
.

Fix
a\in\{\phi,\sigma\}
and define
S=a_{k}^{-1}(n)=\{\ell:a_{k}(\ell)=n\}
. For each
\ell\in S
, let
a(m)=\ell
and write
\ell=\ell^{\prime}\ell^{\prime\prime}
, where
\ell^{\prime}
and
\ell^{\prime\prime}
are the
z
-rough and
z
-smooth parts of
\ell
respectively and
z=\exp((\log\log x)^{1/2})
as defined in Section
2
. Below,
S
is partitioned into 3 sets (
P
,
Q
and
R
) by the number of
z
-rough prime factors and number of prime factors in total. Hence, if
a_{k+1}(m)=n
, then
a(m)\in S
, so that
m\in a^{-1}(P)\cup a^{-1}(Q)\cup a^{-1}(R)
. We note, as will be important below, that every element of
a^{-1}(P)\cup a^{-1}(Q)\cup a^{-1}(R)
is at most
x
.

Our first set will contain the values of
\ell
with many large prime factors. Let
\alpha<k
and choose
\beta
with
\alpha<\beta+1<k
. Define
P=\{\ell\in S:\Omega_{>z}(\ell)\geq\log L_{k+1,\alpha}(x)
}. If
a(m)\in P
,
\Omega_{>z}(a(m))\geq\log L_{k+1,\alpha}(x)
. Moreover, by Lemma 1,

\sum\limits_{\overset{m\leq x}{\Omega_{>z}(a(m))\geq\log L_{k+1,\alpha}(x)}}1\leq\frac{1}{A^{\log L_{k+1,\alpha}(x)}}\sum\limits_{m\leq x}A^{\Omega_{>z}(a(m))}

\leq x\exp\left(\frac{\log x}{\exp(\log\log x)^{\eta/2}}-(1-\eta)\log L_{k+1,\alpha+1}(x)\right)=\frac{x}{L_{k+1,\alpha+1}(x)^{(1-\eta)+o(1)}}.

Since
\eta
can be taken arbitrarily close to 0, we conclude that
\#a^{-1}(P)\leq\frac{x}{L_{k+1,\alpha+1}(x)^{1+o(1)}}
.

Next, we focus on the second set containing the values of
\ell\notin P
with a large number of total prime factors. Let
Q=\left\{\ell\in S\backslash P:\Omega(\ell)\geq{\log x}/{(\log\log x)^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\right\}
. If
a(m)\in Q
,
\Omega(a(m))\geq{\log x}/{(\log\log x)^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
. By Lemma 2 setting
B=\frac{\sqrt[3]{2}+1}{2}
,

\sum\limits_{\overset{m\leq x}{\Omega(a(m))\geq\frac{\log x}{(\log\log x)^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}}}1\leq\frac{1}{B^{\frac{\log x}{(\log\log x)^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}}}\sum\limits_{m\leq x}B^{\Omega(a(m))}

\leq x\exp\left(C_{B}(\log x)^{3/4}-\frac{\log x}{(\log\log x)^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\log(B)\right)\leq x\exp\left((-D+o(1))\frac{\log x}{(\log\log x)^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\right)

for some positive constant
D
.

Hence, an upper bound for
\#a^{-1}(Q)
is
x\exp\left((-D+o(1))\frac{\log x}{(\log\log x)^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\right)
.

Our final set
R
will contain everything else in
S
:
R=S\backslash(P\cup Q)
. Given
\ell
, fix
\ell^{\prime}
. Let
j=\Omega(\ell^{\prime})=\Omega_{>z}(\ell)<\log L_{k+1,\alpha}(x)
. Since
\ell^{\prime}\mid\ell=a(m)
, we can use Lemma 4 to find an upper bound on the number of
m
corresponding to a fixed
\ell^{\prime}
. The upper bound is given as follows:

\frac{x}{\ell^{\prime}}(8j\log^{2}(ex))^{j}\leq\frac{x}{\ell^{\prime}}\exp\left((3+o(1))\log L_{k,\alpha}(x)\right)=\frac{xL_{k,\alpha}(x)^{3+o(1)}}{\ell^{\prime}}.

Since
\Omega(\ell)<\frac{\log x}{(\log\log x)^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
,
\ell^{\prime\prime}\leq z^{\Omega(\ell)}<\exp(\log x/(\log\log x)^{k})=L_{k,\alpha}(x)^{o(1)}
. Summing over all
\ell^{\prime}
to obtain an upper bound on
\#a^{-1}(R)
, we get

xL_{k,\alpha}(x)^{3+o(1)}\sum\limits_{\ell\in R}\frac{1}{\ell^{\prime}}=xL_{k,\alpha}(x)^{3+o(1)}\sum\limits_{\ell\in R}\frac{\ell^{\prime\prime}}{\ell}\leq xL_{k,\alpha}(x)^{3+o(1)}\sum\limits_{\ell\in S}\frac{1}{\ell}.

Since
a(\ell)=n
, by Lemma 3,
\ell\geq c\frac{n}{\log\log 3n}
for a certain absolute positive constant
c
, and so by our inductive hypothesis,
\sum\limits_{\ell\in S}\frac{1}{\ell}\leq\frac{c\log\log 3n}{n}\sum\limits_{\ell\in S}1\leq L_{k,\beta+1}(n)^{-1+o(1)}=L_{k,\beta+1}(x)^{-1+o(1)}
.
Therefore, from our choice of
\alpha
,

\#a^{-1}(R)\leq x\exp\bigg((3+o(1))\log L_{k,\alpha}(x)-(1+o(1))\log L_{k,\beta+1}(x)\bigg)=\frac{x}{L_{k,\beta+1}(x)^{1+o(1)}}.

We add all the upper bounds together to get an upper bound for
N_{a}^{k+1}(n)=\#a^{-1}(S)
. We will get

N_{a}^{k+1}(n)=\#a^{-1}(S)=\#a^{-1}(P)+\#a^{-1}(Q)+\#a^{-1}(R)

\leq\frac{x}{L_{k+1,\alpha+1}(x)^{1+o(1)}}+x\exp\left((-D+o(1))\frac{\log x}{(\log\log x)^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\right)+\frac{x}{L_{k,\beta+1}(x)^{1+o(1)}}

\leq\frac{x}{L_{k+1,\alpha+1}(x)^{1+o(1)}}=\frac{n}{L_{k+1,\alpha+1}(n)^{1+o(1)}},

thus completing the proof of Theorem
1
.

4
Remarks

The proof can be extended to any order of composition of
\phi
and
\sigma
:

Let
a^{(1)},\ldots,a^{(k)}\in\{\phi,\sigma\}
for a fixed
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and define
A=a^{(1)}\circ\cdots\circ a^{(k)}
. Then for fixed
\beta<k-1
, as
n\rightarrow\infty
,
we have

\#\{m:A(m)=n\}\leq\frac{n}{L_{k,\beta+1}(n)^{1+o(1)}}.

It is interesting to speculate about the sharp maximal order of
\#\phi_{k}^{-1}(m)
. When
k=1
, we have noted already Pomerance’s conjecture that there is a sequence of
m
tending to
\infty
along which
\#\phi^{-1}(m)=m/L(m)^{1+o(1)}
. One reason for believing this conjecture is that it becomes a theorem under the following plausible hypothesis on smooth shifted primes
p-1
:

Hypothesis
.

Let
\Psi(x,y)
denote the number of integers
n\leq x
free of prime factors exceeding
y
and
\Pi(x,y)
denote the number of primes
p\leq x
such that
p-1
is free of prime factors exceeding
y
. If
x\geq y
and
y\rightarrow\infty
,

\frac{1}{x}\Psi(x,y)\sim\frac{1}{\pi(x)}\Pi(x,y).

Under this hypothesis, Pomerance showed
[
10
]
that there is a set of
n\leq x
of size
x/L(x)^{1+o(1)}
(as
x\rightarrow\infty
) for which all the values
\phi(n)
are
\log x
smooth. Since the cardinality of
\log x
smooth numbers up to
x
is only
L(x)^{o(1)}
, it follows that some
(\log x)
-smooth number
m\leq x
has at least
x/L(x)^{1+o(1)}
preimages
n\leq x
. Hence,
\#\phi^{-1}(m)\geq x/L(x)^{1+o(1)}\geq m/L(m)^{1+o(1)}
.

Lamzouri has studied smooth values of the iterates of
\phi
. He proves in
[
11
]
, conditional on the Elliott–Halberstam conjecture, that:

Theorem 2
.

Define
\Phi_{k}(x,y)=\lvert\{m\leq x:p\mid\phi_{k}(m)\Rightarrow p\leq y\}\rvert
. Fix
U>1
. If
y=x^{1/u}
where
1\leq u\leq U
, then as
x\rightarrow\infty
,

\Phi_{k}(x,y)\sim x\rho_{k}(u)

where
\rho_{0}(u)=\left(\frac{e+o(1)}{u\log u}\right)^{u}
and when
k\geq 1
,

\rho_{k}(u)=\left(\frac{1+o(1)}{\log_{k}(u)\log_{k+1}(u)}\right)^{u}

as
u\rightarrow\infty
, where
\log_{k}(u)=\underbrace{\log\log\cdots\log}_{\text{$k$ times}}u
.

In Lamzouri’s conjecture, the smoothness parameter
y
exceeds a fixed positive power of
x
. It is tempting to conjecture, optimistically, that Lamzouri’s conjecture remains valid down to
y=\log x
. If so, we would derive (analogously to Pomerance)
\#\phi_{k}^{-1}(m)\geq m\exp\left((-1+o(1))\frac{\log m\log_{k+2}m}{\log_{2}m}\right)
on a sequence of
m
tending to infinity. If true, it is also tempting to conjecture that this is sharp.

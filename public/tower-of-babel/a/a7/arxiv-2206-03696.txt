Divisibility and distribution of 5-regular partitions
QI-YANG ZHENG

Abstract
. In this paper we study b 5 ​ ( n ) b_{5}(n) , the 5 5 -regular partitions of n n . Using the theory of modular forms, we prove several theorems on the divisibility and distribution properties of b 5 ​ ( n ) b_{5}(n) modulo prime m ≥ 5 m\geq 5 . In particular, we prove that there are infinitely many Ramanujan-type congruences modulo prime m ≥ 5 m\geq 5 .



1.
Introduction and statement of results

1.1.
Introduction

In number theory, we usually denote
p(n)
as the number of the partitions of
n
. Ramanujan found the three remarkable congruences as follows:

\displaystyle p(5n+4)

\displaystyle\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 5),

\displaystyle p(7n+5)

\displaystyle\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 7),

\displaystyle p(11n+6)

\displaystyle\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 11).

Such congruences are called Ramanujan-type congruences.

For
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>1}
, we define the
k
-regular partitions
b_{k}(n)
by

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{k}(n)q^{n}=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1-q^{kn}}{1-q^{n}}.

We study the arithmetical properties of
b_{5}(n)
in this paper. Hirschhorn and Sellers
[
3
]
prove that there are infinitely many Ramanujan-type congruences of
b_{5}(n)
modulo
2
. Gordon and Ono
[
2
]
prove that

b_{5}(5n+4)\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 5).

Up to now, Ramanujan-type congruence of
b_{5}(n)
modulo prime
m\geq 7
has not been found. Our main result is that for each prime
m\geq 5
, there exist infinitely many Ramanujan-type congruences modulo
m
. For example, we obtain

b_{5}(2023n+99)\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 7)

satisfied for each nonnegative integer
n
. We will give more examples in
Section

5
.

1.2.
Statement of results

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
m\geq 5
be a prime. Then a positive density of primes
l
have the property that

(1.1)

b_{5}\left(\frac{mln-1}{6}\right)\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ m)

satisfied for each integer
n
with
(n,l)=1
.

The theorem immediately implies that there are infinitely many Ramanujan-type congruences of
b_{5}(n)
modulo
m
. Moreover, together with the Chinese Remainder Theorem, we obtain that if
m
is a squarefree integer coprime to
3
, then there are infinitely many Ramanujan-type congruences of
b_{5}(n)
modulo
m
.

Surprisingly, we do not know whether there is Ramanujan-type congruence of
b_{5}(n)
modulo
3
.

Theorem

also implies that

\#\{0\leq n\leq X\ |\ b_{5}(n)\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ m)\}\gg X,

where
m\geq 5
is a prime. For other residue classes
i\not\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ m)

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
m\geq 5
be a prime. If there exists one
k\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

b_{5}\left(mk+\frac{m^{2}-1}{6}\right)\equiv e\not\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ m),

then for each

The congruence of Gordon and Ono show that our criterion is inapplicable for the case

2.
Notation and definitions

Our proof is depending on the theory of modular forms. First recall that the Dedekind’s eta function is defined by:

\eta(z)=q^{\frac{1}{24}}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{n}),

where
q=e^{2\pi iz}
. It is well-known that
\eta(z)
is holomorphic and never vanishes in the upper half plane.

Now we introduce the
U
operator. If
j
is a positive integer, we define
U(j)
as follows:

(2.1)

\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a(n)q^{n}\right)\ |\ U(j)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a(jn)q^{n}.

Sometimes the following expression of
U(j)
operator is more convenient for computation.

(2.2)

\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a(n)q^{n}\right)\ |\ U(j)=\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n=0}{j|n}}^{\infty}a(n)q^{\frac{n}{j}}.

We define
M_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)_{m}
as the reduction mod
m
of the
q
-expansions of modular forms in
M_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
with integral coefficients. Moreover, we define
S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)_{m}
in a similar way.

Theorem 4.2

(J. Sturm)
.

Suppose

such that

.

Let

, where

Let

We also have the following conjecture analogous to Newman’s Conjecture for the usual partition function

Let

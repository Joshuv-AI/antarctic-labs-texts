Rational Solutions of the Fifth Painlevé Equation. Generalised Laguerre Polynomials
Peter A. Clarkson, Clare Dunning

Abstract
In this paper rational solutions of the fifth Painlevé equation are discussed. There are two classes of rational solutions of the fifth Painlevé equation, one expressed in terms of the generalised Laguerre polynomials, which are the main subject of this paper, and the other in terms of the generalised Umemura polynomials. Both the generalised Laguerre polynomials and the generalised Umemura polynomials can be expressed as Wronskians of Laguerre polynomials specified in terms of specific families of partitions. The properties of the generalised Laguerre polynomials are determined and various differential-difference and discrete equations found. The rational solutions of the fifth Painlevé equation, the associated σ \sigma -equation and the symmetric fifth Painlevé system are expressed in terms of generalised Laguerre polynomials. Non-uniqueness of the solutions in special cases is established and some applications are considered. In the second part of the paper, the structure of the roots of the polynomials are investigated for all values of the parameter. Interesting transitions between root structures through coalescences at the origin are discovered, with the allowed behaviours controlled by hook data associated with the partition. The discriminants of the generalised Laguerre polynomials are found and also shown to be expressible in terms of partition data. Explicit expressions for the coefficients of a general Wronskian Laguerre polynomial defined in terms of a single partition are given.



Keywords
: Painlevé equation, rational solutions, Laguerre polynomials, discriminant, partition, Wronskian.

Dedicated to Athanassios S. Fokas on the occasion of his 70th anniversary for his many contributions to studies of integrable nonlinear differential equations, including Painlevé equations.

The six Painlevé equations (

Subsequently the Painlevé transcendents are a chapter in the NIST

The general solutions of the Painlevé equations are transcendental in the sense that they cannot be expressed in terms of known elementary functions and so require the introduction of a new transcendental function to describe their solution. However, it is well known that all the Painlevé equations, except

Partitions

Partitions will appear throughout this article. We give a brief description of the key ideas. Useful references include

A partition
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{r})

is a sequence of non-increasing integers
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\ldots\geq\lambda_{r}
.
We sometimes set
r=\ell(\boldsymbol{\lambda})
. The partition
\boldsymbol{\emptyset}
represents the unique partition of zero.
We define
|\boldsymbol{\lambda}|=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\dots+\lambda_{r}
.
The associated
degree vector

\boldsymbol{h}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}=(h_{1},h_{2},\ldots,h_{r})
is a sequence of distinct integers

h_{1}>h_{2}>\ldots>h_{r}>0
related to partition elements via

\lambda_{j}=h_{j}-r+j,\qquad j=1,2,\ldots,r.

(2.1)

We often write
\boldsymbol{h}
rather than
\boldsymbol{h}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
.
Define the Vandermonde determinant

\Delta(\boldsymbol{h})
as

\Delta(\boldsymbol{h})=\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq r}(h_{k}-h_{j}).

(2.2)

Partitions are usefully
represented as Young diagrams by stacking
r
rows of boxes of decreasing length

\lambda_{j}
for
j=1,2,\dots,r
on top of each other.
Reflecting a Young diagram in the main diagonal gives the
diagram corresponding to the conjugate partition
\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{*}
.
Young’s lattice is the lattice of all partitions partially ordered by inclusion of the corresponding Young diagrams. That is,
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\leq\boldsymbol{\lambda}
if
\widetilde{\lambda}_{i}\leq\lambda_{i}
for
i=1,2,\dots,\ell(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})
.
We write
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}<_{j}\boldsymbol{\lambda}
if
|\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}|+j=|\boldsymbol{\lambda}|.

Let
F_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
denote the number of paths in the Young lattice from
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
to
\boldsymbol{\emptyset}
, and
F_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}/\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}
the number of paths from
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
to

\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
. Explicitly

F_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}/\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}=(|\boldsymbol{\lambda}|-|\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}|)!\text{ det}\,\left[\frac{1}{(\lambda_{j}-\widetilde{\lambda}_{k}-j+k)!}\right]_{j,k=1}^{\ell(\boldsymbol{\lambda})}.

A hook length
h_{jk}
is assigned
to box
(j,k)
in the Young diagram via

h_{j,k}=\lambda_{j}+{\lambda}^{*}_{k}-j-k+1.

(2.3)

The hook length counts the number of boxes to the right of and below box
(j,k)
plus one. Thus

F_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}=\frac{|\boldsymbol{\lambda}|!}{\prod_{h\in\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}h},

where
\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is the set of all hook lengths.
The entries of the degree vector
\boldsymbol{h}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
are
the hooks in the first column of the Young diagram. Examples of Young diagrams
and the corresponding hook lengths are given in Figure
2.1
.

A partition can be represented as

and

-quotient
(\boldsymbol{\nu}_{1},\dots,\boldsymbol{\nu}_{p})

-core and

6
- and
7

We only consider

The hooks of size
2
are vertical or horizontal dominoes.
We note that all
2
-cores are staircase partitions
\overline{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}=(k,k-1,\ldots,1)
.

The
2
-core of a partition is found by sequentially removing all hooks of size
2
from the Young diagram such that at each step the diagram represents a partition.

Definition 3.1

The

(3.2)

\displaystyle T_{m,n}^{(\mu)}(z)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}\Wr\left(L_{m+n}^{(n+\mu)}(z),L_{m+n-1}^{(n+\mu)}(z),\ldots,L_{m+1}^{(n+\mu)}(z)\right)

\displaystyle=\Wr\left(L_{m+1}^{(n+\mu)}(z),L_{m+2}^{(n+\mu)}(z),\ldots,L_{m+n}^{(n+\mu)}(z)\right).

Proof.

We use

\frac{{\rm d}^{k}}{{\rm d}z^{k}}L_{n}^{(\alpha)}(z)=\begin{cases}(-1)^{k}L_{n-k}^{(\alpha+k)}(z),\quad&k\leq n,\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

Using the result

\displaystyle m\geq 0,\quad n\geq 1,

(3.21c)

\displaystyle T_{m,n}^{(\mu)}(z)

\displaystyle m\geq 0,\quad n\geq 1,

(3.21d)

\displaystyle m\geq 0,\quad n\geq 1,

(3.21e)

where

Proof.

Lemma 3.10
.

The generalised Laguerre polynomial
T_{m,n}^{(\mu)}(z)
satisfies the second-order, differential-difference equation

T_{m,n}^{(\mu)}\frac{{\rm d}^{2}{T_{m,n}^{(\mu)}}}{{{\rm d}{z}}^{2}}-\left(\frac{{\rm d}{T_{m,n}^{(\mu)}}}{{{\rm d}{z}}}\right)^{\!2}=T_{m+1,n-1}^{(\mu)}T_{m-1,n+1}^{(\mu)}.

(3.22)

Proof.

According to Sylvester
[
65
]
, see also
[
48
]
,
if
\mathcal{A}_{n}(\varphi)
is the double Wronskian given by

\mathcal{A}_{n}(\varphi)=\det\left[\frac{{\rm d}^{j+k}{\varphi}}{{{\rm d}{z}}^{j+k}}\right]_{j,k=0}^{n-1}=\Wr\left(\varphi,\frac{{\rm d}{\varphi}}{{{\rm d}{z}}},\ldots,\frac{{\rm d}^{n-1}{\varphi}}{{{\rm d}{z}}^{n-1}}\right),

then
\mathcal{A}_{n}(\varphi)
satisfies the

\mathcal{A}_{n}\frac{{\rm d}^{2}{\mathcal{A}_{n}}}{{{\rm d}{z}}^{2}}-\left(\frac{{\rm d}{\mathcal{A}_{n}}}{{{\rm d}{z}}}\right)^{\!2}=\mathcal{A}_{n+1}\mathcal{A}_{n-1},

(3.23)

which is now known as the Toda equation.
From (
3.1
)

T_{m,n}^{(\mu)}=\det\left[\frac{{\rm d}^{j+k}{L_{m+n}^{(\mu)}}}{{{\rm d}{z}}^{j+k}}\right]_{j,k=0}^{n-1}=\Wr\left(L_{m+n}^{(\mu)},\frac{{\rm d}{L_{m+n}^{(\mu)}}}{{{\rm d}{z}}},\ldots,\frac{{\rm d}^{n-1}{L_{m+n}^{(\mu)}}}{{{\rm d}{z}}^{n-1}}\right).

If we let
\varphi=L_{m+n}^{(\mu)}
and
\mathcal{A}_{n}\left(L_{m+n}^{(\mu)}\right)=T_{m,n}^{(\mu)}
, then we need to show that

\mathcal{A}_{n+1}\left(L_{m+n}^{(\mu)}\right)=T_{m-1,n+1}^{(\mu)},\qquad\mathcal{A}_{n-1}\left(L_{m+n}^{(\mu)}\right)=T_{m+1,n-1}^{(\mu)}.

By definition

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{n+1}\left(L_{m+n}^{(\mu)}\right)

\displaystyle=\Wr\left(L_{m+n}^{(\mu)},\frac{{\rm d}{L_{m+n}^{(\mu)}}}{{{\rm d}{z}}},\ldots,\frac{{\rm d}^{n}{L_{m+n}^{(\mu)}}}{{{\rm d}{z}}^{n}}\right)=T_{m-1,n+1}^{(\mu)},

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{n-1}\left(L_{m+n}^{(\mu)}\right)

\displaystyle=\Wr\left(L_{m+n}^{(\mu)},\frac{{\rm d}{L_{m+n}^{(\mu)}}}{{{\rm d}{z}}},\ldots,\frac{{\rm d}^{n-2}{L_{m+n}^{(\mu)}}}{{{\rm d}{z}}^{n-2}}\right)=T_{m+1,n-1}^{(\mu)},

which proves the result.
∎

1\end{subarray}\Big]&=\Wr\left(L_{m+2}^{(n+\mu-1)},L_{m+3}^{(n+\mu-1)},\ldots,L_{m+n+1}^{(n+\mu-1)}\right)=T_{m+1,n}^{(\mu-1)},\\
\mathcal{A}_{n+1}\Big[\begin{subarray}{c}1,n\\[2.0pt]
1,n+1\end{subarray}\Big]&=\Wr\left(\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}{}}{{{\rm d}{z}}}L_{m+2}^{(n+\mu-1)},\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}{}}{{{\rm d}{z}}}L_{m+3}^{(n+\mu-1)},\ldots,\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}{}}{{{\rm d}{z}}}L_{m+n}^{(n+\mu-1)}\right),\\
&=(-1)^{n-1}\Wr\left(L_{m+1}^{(n+\mu)},L_{m+2}^{(n+\mu)},\ldots,L_{m+n-1}^{(n+\mu)}\right)=(-1)^{n-1}\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}{}}{{{\rm d}{z}}}T_{m,n-1}^{(\mu+1)},\end{split}

and so

T_{m,n-1}^{(\mu+1)}\,\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}{}}{{{\rm d}{z}}}T_{m+1,n}^{(\mu-1)}-T_{m+1,n-1}^{(\mu)}\,T_{m,n}^{(\mu)}=T_{m+1,n}^{(\mu-1)}\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}{}}{{{\rm d}{z}}}T_{m,n-1}^{(\mu+1)},

which proves the result. The other results (
3.27c
)–(
3.27f
) are proved in a similar way.

Remark 4.2

Rational solutions in case (i) of Theorem

Rational solutions in cases (ii) and (iii) of Theorem

As mentioned above, Umemura

Remark 4.4

The polynomial

Lemma 4.5
.

The polynomials
\tau_{m,n}^{(\mu)}(z)
and

are related as follows

\tau_{m,n}^{(\mu)}(z)=a_{m,n}z^{n(n-1)/2}T_{m,n}^{(\mu)}(z),\qquad a_{m,n}=\prod^{n}_{j=1}(m+n+j+\mu)^{j-1}.

Proof.

From (
4.4

\tau_{m,n}^{(\mu)}(z)=\det\left[\left(z\frac{{\rm d}{}}{{{\rm d}{z}}}\right)^{\!(j+k)}L_{m+n}^{(n+\mu)}(z)\right]_{j,k=0}^{n-1}.

Now we use the identity

\det\left[\left(z\frac{{\rm d}{}}{{{\rm d}{z}}}\right)^{\!j}f_{k}(z)\right]_{j,k=0}^{n-1}=z^{n(n-1)/2}\Wr\big(f_{0}(z),f_{1}(z),\ldots,f_{n-1}(z)\big),

(4.7a)

(4.7b)

Using the recurrence relation

z\frac{{\rm d}{}}{{{\rm d}{z}}}L_{n}^{(\alpha)}(z)=nL_{n}^{(\alpha)}(z)-(n+\mu)L_{n-1}^{(\alpha)}(z),

cf.
[

it is straightforward to show by induction that

\left(z\frac{{\rm d}{}}{{{\rm d}{z}}}\right)^{\!k}L_{n}^{(\alpha)}(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}b_{j,k}^{(n,\mu)}L_{n-j}^{(\alpha)}(z)+(-1)^{k}b_{k,k}^{(n,\mu)}L_{n-k}^{(\alpha)}(z),

(4.8)

where

b_{k,k}^{(n,\mu)}=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(n-j+\mu).

(4.9)

(It is not necessary to know what

,

4.7
) and (
4.8
), we have

\displaystyle\tau_{m,n}^{(\mu)}(z)

\displaystyle=z^{n(n-1)/2}\Wr\left(L_{m+n}^{(n+\mu)}(z),z\frac{{\rm d}{}}{{{\rm d}{z}}}L_{m+n}^{(n+\mu)}(z),\ldots,\left(z\frac{{\rm d}{}}{{{\rm d}{z}}}\right)^{n-1}L_{m+n}^{(n+\mu)}(z)\right)

\displaystyle=z^{n(n-1)/2}\Wr\left(L_{m+n}^{(n+\mu)}(z),-(m+2n+\mu)L_{m+n-1}^{(n+\mu)}(z),\ldots,(-1)^{(n-1)}b_{n-1,n-1}^{(m+n,n+\mu)}L_{m+1}^{(n+\mu)}(z)\right),

since, as in the proof of Lemma

\displaystyle\tau_{m,n}^{(\mu)}(z)

\displaystyle=z^{n(n-1)/2}\left(\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}b_{k,k}^{(m+n,n+\mu)}\right)\Wr\left(L_{m+1}^{(n+\mu)}(z),L_{m+2}^{(n+\mu)}(z),\ldots,L_{m+n}^{(n+\mu)}(z)\right)

5.2

There are two classes of rational solutions of

Theorem 5.2

The rational solution of

(

) in terms of the generalised Laguerre polynomial

is

\sigma_{m,n}(z;\boldsymbol{\nu})=z\frac{{\rm d}{}}{{{\rm d}{z}}}\ln\left\{T_{m,n}^{(\mu)}(z)\right\}-(m+1)n,\qquad m\geq 0,\quad n\geq 1,

(5.8)

for the parameters

\boldsymbol{\nu}=(m+1,-n,m+n+\mu+1).

(5.9)

Proof.

This result can be inferred from the work of Forrester and Witte
[
25
]
and Okamoto
[
58
]
on special function solutions of

V
, together with the relationship between Kummer functions and associated Laguerre polynomials (
4.3
). We have used Lemma
5.1
as a normalisation.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

The rational solution of
S
V
(
5.4
) in terms of the generalised Laguerre polynomial
\widehat{T}_{m,n}^{(\mu)}(z)
is

\widehat{\sigma}_{m,n}(z;\boldsymbol{\nu})=z\frac{{\rm d}{}}{{{\rm d}{z}}}\ln\left\{\widehat{T}_{m,n}^{(\mu)}(z)\right\}-(m+1)n,\qquad m\geq 0,\quad n\geq 1,

(5.10)

for the parameters

\boldsymbol{\nu}=(-m-1,n,-m-n-\mu-1).

(5.11)

Proof.

Since
\widehat{T}_{m,n}^{(\mu)}(z)=T_{m,n}^{(\mu)}(-z)
then

\widehat{\sigma}_{m,n}(z;\boldsymbol{\nu})={\sigma}_{m,n}(-z;-\boldsymbol{\nu})
.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

We note that

\displaystyle\sigma_{m,n}(z;m+1,-n,m+1-j)=\sigma_{m-j,n}(z;m+1-j,-n,m+1),\quad

\displaystyle j

\displaystyle=1,\ldots,m,

\displaystyle\sigma_{m,n}(z;m+1,-n,0)=0,

\displaystyle\sigma_{m,n}(z;m,-n,1-j)=\sigma_{m,j-1}(z;m+1,1-j,-n),\quad

\displaystyle j

\displaystyle=2,\ldots,n.

This result follow from the factorisation
given in Lemma
7.2

of the
T_{m,n}^{(\mu)}(z)
at certain negative integer values of
\mu
.
The third case also follows from the invariance of the Hamiltonian
\mbox{$\mathcal{H}_{\rm V}$}(q,p,z)
under
the interchange of
\nu_{2}
and
\nu_{3}

7
Properties of generalised Laguerre polynomials

Remark 7.1
.

The generalised Laguerre polynomial
T_{m,n}^{(\mu)}(z)
is such that

\displaystyle T_{m,n}^{(\mu)}(z)=c_{m,n}

\displaystyle\left\{z^{(m+1)n}-n\bigl(m{+}1\bigr)(m{+}n{+}1{+}\mu)z^{(m+1)n-1}\right.

\displaystyle\quad+\tfrac{1}{2}n(m+1)(m+n+1+\mu)[(m+1)(mn+n^{2}+n-2)+(mn+n-1)\mu]z^{(m+1)n-2}

\displaystyle\qquad\left.+\ldots+(-1)^{n(m+n)}d_{m,n}\right\}

(7.1)

where

c_{m,n}=(-1)^{n(2m+1+n)/2}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{(j-1)!}{(m+j)!},

(7.2)

which follows from Lemma 1 in
[
9
]
, and

d_{m,n}=\prod_{j=1}^{{\rm min}(m+1,n)-1}(\mu+n+j)^{j}\prod_{{\rm min}(m+1,n)}^{{\rm max}(m+1,n)}(\mu+n+j)^{{\rm min}(m+1,n)}\prod_{{\rm max}(m+1,n)+1}^{m+n}(\mu+n+j)^{m+n+1-j}.

(7.3)

Therefore

T_{m,n}^{(-n-j)}(0)=0,\qquad j=1,2,\ldots,m+n.

(7.4)

Lemma 7.2
.

The generalised Laguerre polynomials have multiple
roots at the origin when

\mu=-n-j,\qquad j=1,2,\ldots,m+n.

(7.5)

Moreover at such values of
\mu
the polynomials
T_{m,n}^{(\mu)}(z)
factorise
as

\displaystyle T_{m,n}^{(-n-j)}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{m,n}}{c_{m-j,n}}z^{nj}\,T_{m-j,n}^{(j-n)}(z),

\displaystyle j

\displaystyle=1,2,\ldots,m,

(7.6)

\displaystyle T_{m,n}^{(-m-n-1)}(z)

\displaystyle=c_{m,n}\,z^{n(m+1)},

(7.7)

\displaystyle T_{m,n}^{(-m-n-j)}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{m,n}}{c_{m,j-1}}\,z^{(m+1)(n+1-j)}\,T_{m,j-1}^{(-m-n-j)}(z),

\displaystyle j

\displaystyle=2,\ldots,n,

(7.8)

where

T_{m-j,n}^{(j-n)}(0)\neq 0,\qquad T_{m,j-1}^{(-m-n-j)}(0)\neq 0.

Proof.

The fact that the generalised Laguerre polynomials have
multiple roots at the points (
7.5
) follows from
the discriminant, and
that these roots are always at the origin is a consequence of (
7.4
).
We use the standard property of Wronskians

\Wr\big(c_{1}g(x)f_{1}(x),\ldots,c_{r}g(x)f_{r}(x)\big)=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{r}c_{i}\right)[g(x)]^{r}\Wr\big(f_{1}(x),\ldots,f_{r}(x)\big),\qquad c_{1},\ldots,c_{r}\in\mathbb{C},

(7.9)

and the property (see, for example,
[
43
]
)

L_{n}^{(\alpha)}(z)=\frac{(n+\alpha)!}{n!}(-z)^{-\alpha}L_{n+\alpha}^{(-\alpha)}(z),\qquad\alpha\in\{-n,-n+1,\ldots,-1\},

(7.10)

to rewrite

T_{m,n}^{(-m-n-1)}(z)=\Wr\left(L_{m+1}^{(-m-1)}(z),L_{m+2}^{(-m-1)}(z),\ldots,L_{m+n}^{(-m-1)}(z)\right),

(7.11)

as

T_{m,n}^{(-m-n-1)}(z)=(-z)^{n(m+1)}\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{j!}{(m+j+1)!}\Wr\left(L_{0}^{(m+1)}(z),L_{1}^{(m+1)}(z),\ldots,L_{n-1}^{(m+1)}(z)\right).

(7.12)

Since
L_{0}^{(m+1)}(z)=1
and

\Wr\big(1,f_{1}(x),f_{2}(x),\ldots,f_{r}(x)\big)=\Wr\big(f_{1}^{\prime}(x),f_{2}^{\prime}(x),\ldots,f_{r}^{\prime}(x)\big),

(7.13)

we repeatedly use (
3.4
) and (
7.13
) to show that

\Wr\left(L_{0}^{(m+1)}(z),L_{1}^{(m+1)}(z),\ldots,L_{n-1}^{(m+1)}(z)\right)=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{j}.

(7.14)

Hence we obtain

T_{n,m}^{(-m-n-1)}(z)=(-z)^{n(m+1)}\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{(-1)^{j}j!}{(m+j+1)!}=c_{m,n}\,z^{n(m+1)}.

(7.15)

When
\alpha=-n-j
for
j=1,2,\ldots,m
, we
again use (
7.10
) and (
7.9
)
to obtain

\displaystyle T_{m,n}^{(-n-j)}(z)

\displaystyle=\Wr\left(L_{m+1}^{(-j)}(z),L_{m+2}^{(-j)}(z),\ldots,L_{m+n}^{(-j)}(z)\right)

\displaystyle=z^{nj}\,(-1)^{nj}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{(m-j+i)!}{(m+i)!}\Wr\left(L_{m+1-j}^{(j)}(z),L_{m+2-j}^{(j)}(z),\ldots,L_{m+n-j}^{(j)}(z)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{m,n}}{c_{m-j,n}}z^{nj}\,T_{m-j,n}^{(j-n)}(z).

(7.16)

The final case of
\alpha=-m-n-j

for
j=2,3,\ldots,n
follows similarly, except that we first apply
the symmetry (
3.19
) in order to use (
7.10
).
Specifically, we have

\displaystyle T_{m,n}^{(-m-n-j)}(z)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\lfloor(m+n+1)/2\rfloor}\,\widehat{T}_{n-1,m+1}^{(-m-n+j-2)}(z)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\lfloor(m+n+1)/2\rfloor}z^{(m+1)(n-j+1)}\prod_{i=0}^{m}\frac{(j+i-1)!}{(n+i)!}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\Wr\left(L_{j-1}^{(n+1-j)}(-z),L_{j}^{(n+1-j)}(-z),\ldots,L_{j+m-1}^{(n+1-j)}(-z)\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\lfloor(m+n+1)/2\rfloor}z^{(m+1)(n-j+1)}\prod_{i=0}^{m}\frac{(j+i-1)!}{(n+i)!}\,\widehat{T}_{j-2,m+1}^{(n-m-j)}(z).

Applying the symmetry (
3.19

7.8
).
Finally,

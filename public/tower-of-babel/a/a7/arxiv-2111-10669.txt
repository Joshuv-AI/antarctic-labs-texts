Finite Coxeter Groups and Generalized Elnitsky Tilings
Robert Nicolaides, Peter Rowley

Abstract
In [ 4 ] , Elnitsky constructed three elegant bijections between classes of reduced words for Type A \mathrm{A} , B \mathrm{B} and D \mathrm{D} families of Coxeter groups and certain tilings of polygons. This paper presents a particular generalization of this concept to all finite Coxeter groups in terms of embeddings into the symmetric group, which combined with [ 9 ] and [ 10 ] enables tilings to be constructed for all finite Coxeter groups.

Introduction

Reduced words for elements of a Coxeter group have been extensively investigated, as they are implicated in a host of problems and questions in many diverse areas. For example, fully commutative elements, which are elements such that any reduced word can be obtained from another by commutation of generators, illustrate this vividly. They arise in applications to symmetric functions associated with the Weyl groups of type
\mathrm{B}
and
\mathrm{D}
(see
[
12
]
and references therein). Further they also give a natural indexing of a basis for the generalized Temperley-Lieb algebras (see
[
1
]

\mathrm{Sym}(n)
have connections to permutations which avoid the pattern
321
(in one-line notation) and the Catalan numbers. As to the number of reduced words for an element of
\mathrm{Sym}(n)

[

demonstrated that this can be calculated in terms of particular shaped Young tableaux. We note that this description does not, in general, allow easy enumeration. However, for commutation classes of the longest element in
\mathrm{Sym}(n)
see
[
3
]
.

A different perspective on reduced words, expounded in Elnitsky’s PhD dissertation

rhombic tilings of

In this paper we introduce generalized Elinitsky tilings, which extend the work in

The weak order has a succinct interpretation in the context of Elnitsky’s tiling for
W=\mathrm{Sym}({n})
. Namely for all
w\in W
,

Theorem 1.5
.

For all

implies
\mathrm{B}^{\pi/4}(u)\prec\mathrm{B}^{\pi/4}(v)

W

W=\langle S\;|\;(rs)^{m_{r,s}}=1\;r,s\in S\rangle,

where
m_{r,r}=1
and

for all

in

Let

if
w_{2}=w_{1}s
for some

\ell(w_{2})=\ell(w_{1})+1
. While the Bruhat order on

is the transitive extension of
w_{1}<_{B}w_{2}
if

. The Bruhat order has been studied extensively, but here we only have need of a characterization of this order in the symmetric group.

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1.5
which by way of contrast demonstrates that any two Bruhat comparable elements of
\mathrm{Sym}({n})
forms an
\mathrm{E}
-polygon which is a tile. We emphasise that the proof is not dependent on consecutive edges of
\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}

Proof.

It’s enough to observe this statement for a covering set of relations of the Bruhat order. That is, for all
w\in\mathrm{Sym}({n})
and
t\in T
, if
w<_{B}wt
and
\ell(wt)=\ell(w)+1
, then

\mathrm{B}_{n}(w)\prec\mathrm{B}_{n}(wt)
. But, by Theorem
2.1
, this is equivalent to the condition that
t=(a,b)
with
a<b
and
(a)w^{-1}<(b)w^{-1}
and no such intermediate
k
such that
a<k<b
and
(a)w^{-1}<(k)w^{-1}<(b)w^{-1}
. Let us suppose this is the case. Then the images of
\mathcal{B}_{n}(w)
and
\mathcal{B}_{n}(wt)
must be identical apart from the transpositions of the vectors,
\beta_{n}^{(a)w^{-1}}
and
\beta_{n}^{(b)w^{-1}}
. Since
a<b
,
\beta_{n}^{(a)w^{-1}}
appears in a lower position to
\beta_{n}^{(b)w^{-1}}
as line segment in
\mathrm{B}^{\alpha}_{n}(w)
. From
(a)w^{-1}<(b)w^{-1}
,
\beta_{n}^{(a)w^{-1}}
’s
x
coordinate is more negative than that of
\beta_{n}^{(b)w^{-1}}
- intuitively meaning that

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\varphi:W\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Sym}(n)

We define the

Definition 5.2
.

We now prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof.

Suppose

is a reduced word for some element
w\in W
. Necessarily, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{id}<_{R}s_{i_{1}}<_{R}\ldots<_{R}s_{i_{1}}\ldots s_{i_{k}}.

Since
\varphi
is an
E
-embedding, we know

\displaystyle\varphi(\mathrm{id})<_{B}\varphi(s_{i_{1}})<_{B}\ldots<_{B}\varphi(s_{i_{1}}\ldots s_{i_{k}})

and then Theorem
1.5
implies

\displaystyle\mathrm{B}_{\varphi}(\mathrm{id})\prec\mathrm{B}_{\varphi}(s_{i_{1}})\prec\ldots\prec\mathrm{B}_{\varphi}(s_{i_{1}}\ldots s_{i_{k}}).

∎

The significance of this theorem is that what we call tilings are indeed deserving of their name. Since
\mathrm{B}_{\varphi}(\mathrm{id})\prec\mathrm{B}_{\varphi}(s_{i_{1}})\prec\ldots\prec\mathrm{B}_{\varphi}(s_{i_{1}}\ldots s_{i_{k}})
for each reduced word, each of
\mathrm{P}_{\varphi}(\mathrm{id},s_{i_{1}}),\mathrm{P}_{\varphi}(s_{i_{1}},s_{i_{1}}s_{i_{2}}),\ldots,\mathrm{P}_{\varphi}(s_{i_{1}}\ldots s_{i_{k-1}},s_{i_{1}}\ldots s_{i_{k}})
forms a tile and set of the interiors of these tiles partition the interior of the polygon
\mathrm{P}_{\varphi}(s_{i_{1}}\ldots s_{i_{k}})
.

Lemma 5.5

\varphi:W\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Sym}(n)

\sum_{i=1}^{a^{\prime}_{m}-1}\sin\left(\dfrac{((i-1)\varphi(w))(\pi-2\alpha)}{n-1}+\alpha\right)<y<\sum_{i=1}^{b^{\prime}_{m}}\sin\left(\dfrac{((i-1)\varphi(w))(\pi-2\alpha)}{n-1}+\alpha\right)

Since

for

Proof.

.

Write

. Lemma
5.5

\mathrm{B}_{\varphi}(vs))
and
\mathrm{B}_{\varphi}(vr))

\mathrm{B}_{\varphi}(v))

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}_{\varphi}(s)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{m=1}^{k^{\prime}}\{a^{\prime}_{m},a^{\prime}_{m}+1,\ldots,b^{\prime}_{m}\},

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}_{\varphi}(r)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{j=1}^{h^{\prime}}\{c^{\prime}_{j},c^{\prime}_{j}+1,\ldots,d^{\prime}_{j}\}

\mathrm{I}_{\varphi}(s)

It is now evident that the necessary bijection for Theorem

Quantum Mpemba effect in a quantum dot with reservoirs
Amit Kumar Chatterjee, Satoshi Takada, Hisao Hayakawa

Abstract
We demonstrate the quantum Mpemba effect in a quantum dot coupled to two reservoirs, described by the Anderson model. We show that the system temperatures starting from two different initial values (hot and cold), cross each other at finite time (and thereby reverse their identities i.e. hot becomes cold and vice versa) to generate thermal quantum Mpemba effect. The slowest relaxation mode believed to play the dominating role in Mpemba effect in Markovian systems, does not contribute to such anomalous relaxation in the present model. In this connection, our analytical result provides necessary condition for producing quantum Mpemba effect in the density matrix elements of the quantum dot, as a combined effect of the remaining relaxation modes.

Protocol.-

and
\mathrm{II}

\hat{\rho}^{\mathrm{I}}(\tau=0)
and

\hat{\rho}^{\mathrm{II}}(\tau=0)

[Eq. (

\mathrm{I}
, we choose
\mu_{\mathrm{L}}^{\mathrm{I}}\neq\mu_{\mathrm{R}}^{\mathrm{I}}

\mathrm{II}

\mu_{\mathrm{L}}^{\mathrm{II}}=\mu_{\mathrm{R}}^{\mathrm{II}}=\mu^{\mathrm{II}}

is quenched to

. The parameters after quench can be higher or lower than their values before quench. We follow the time evolution of an entity for
\mathrm{I}
and
\mathrm{II}

QMPE in density matrix elements.-
The time evolution of
\rho_{\alpha}(\tau)
(
\alpha=1,2,3,4

[Eq. (
4

\displaystyle\rho^{\mathrm{I}}_{\alpha}(\tau)\,

\displaystyle=

(

\hat{K}
, respectively

;

;

This naturally raises the question: what are the roles of the coefficients

QMPE in system temperature.-
Next we investigate the possibility of QMPE in the system temperature, i.e., quantum counterpart of the original MPE. The concept of temperature has to be

Summary.-
We have demonstrated QMPE in a single level quantum dot coupled to two reservoirs, described by the Anderson model. Interestingly, the slowest relaxation mode which has been by far the only focus for producing MPE in Markovian systems, has no contribution to QMPE for the Anderson model. Rather, we have presented the necessary criterion, involving the combination of remaining relaxation modes, to obtain QMPE in the density matrix elements. We have achieved the thermal QMPE in the temperature where an initially hotter system can cool faster than an initially colder system and they reverse their identities (hotter becomes colder and vice versa) after some finite time.

It would be important to investigate the thermal QMPE in other quantum systems and systematically establish the general framework for it. We wish to explore the connection of symmetry breaking to QMPE
Ares23
in quantum dots. There have been several experiments on QD

;

I
Eigenvalues and eigenvectors of the transition rate matrix
\hat{K}

We start from the quantum Master equation given by Eqs. (2) and (3) of the main text, describing the time evolution of the density matrix for the quantum dot. The four eigenvalues of
\hat{K}
are given by

\lambda_{1}=0,\hskip 28.45274pt\lambda_{2}=-2+f_{+}^{(0)}-f_{+}^{(1)},\hskip 28.45274pt\lambda_{3}=-2-f_{+}^{(0)}+f_{+}^{(1)},\hskip 28.45274pt\lambda_{4}=-4.

(S1)

Keeping in mind that
f_{+}^{(0)}>f_{+}^{(1)}
for all possible choices of chemical potentials and temperatures, we understand that

\displaystyle\mathrm{steady}\,\,\mathrm{state}\,\,:\,\,

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}=0

(S2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{slowest}\,\,\mathrm{relaxation}\,\,\mathrm{mode}:\,\,

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}=-2+f_{+}^{(0)}-f_{+}^{(1)}.

(S3)

The steady state
|\hat{\rho}_{\lambda_{1}}\rangle=|\hat{\rho}^{ss}(f_{+}^{(0)},f_{+}^{(1)})\rangle
is given by the right eigenvector (normalized) corresponding to the eigenvalue zero

|\hat{\rho}^{ss}(f_{+}^{(0)},f_{+}^{(1)})\rangle=\frac{1}{4+2(f_{+}^{(0)}-f_{+}^{(1)})}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}f_{+}^{(0)}f_{+}^{(1)}\\
f_{+}^{(0)}(2-f_{+}^{(1)})\\
f_{+}^{(0)}(2-f_{+}^{(1)})\\
(2-f_{+}^{(0)})(2-f_{+}^{(1)})\\
\end{array}\right).

(S4)

Note that
\rho^{ss}_{\uparrow}=\rho^{ss}_{\downarrow}
, i.e. the singly occupied up-spin and down-spin state occur with same probability, there is no external field to differentiate between these two states.

The matrix
\hat{R}
containing the (normalized) right eigenvectors of the Lindbladian as columns, is given by

\hat{R}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\frac{f_{+}^{(0)}f_{+}^{(1)}}{4+2(f_{+}^{(0)}-f_{+}^{(1)})}&0&\frac{2f_{+}^{(0)}f_{+}^{(1)}}{-4+\left(f_{+}^{(0)}-f_{+}^{(1)}\right)^{2}}&\frac{f_{+}^{(0)}f_{+}^{(1)}}{4-2(f_{+}^{(0)}-f_{+}^{(1)})}\\
\frac{f_{+}^{(0)}(2-f_{+}^{(1)})}{4+2(f_{+}^{(0)}-f_{+}^{(1)})}&-\frac{1}{2}&-\frac{f_{+}^{(0)}(2-f_{+}^{(0)}-f_{+}^{(1)})}{-4+\left(f_{+}^{(0)}-f_{+}^{(1)}\right)^{2}}&-\frac{f_{+}^{(0)}f_{+}^{(1)}}{4-2(f_{+}^{(0)}-f_{+}^{(1)})}\\
\frac{f_{+}^{(0)}(2-f_{+}^{(1)})}{4+2(f_{+}^{(0)}-f_{+}^{(1)})}&\frac{1}{2}&-\frac{f_{+}^{(0)}(2-f_{+}^{(0)}-f_{+}^{(1)})}{-4+\left(f_{+}^{(0)}-f_{+}^{(1)}\right)^{2}}&-\frac{f_{+}^{(0)}f_{+}^{(1)}}{4-2(f_{+}^{(0)}-f_{+}^{(1)})}\\
\frac{(2-f_{+}^{(0)})(2-f_{+}^{(1)})}{4+2(f_{+}^{(0)}-f_{+}^{(1)})}&0&-\frac{2f_{+}^{(0)}(2-f_{+}^{(0)})}{-4+\left(f_{+}^{(0)}-f_{+}^{(1)}\right)^{2}}&\frac{f_{+}^{(0)}f_{+}^{(1)}}{4-2(f_{+}^{(0)}-f_{+}^{(1)})}\\
\end{array}\right).

(S5)

The matrix
\hat{L}
containing the left eigenvectors of the Lindbladian as rows, is as follows

\hat{L}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}1&1&1&1\\
0&-1&1&0\\
-\frac{2-f_{+}^{(1)}}{f_{+}^{(0)}}&-\frac{2-f_{+}^{(0)}-f_{+}^{(1)}}{2f_{+}^{(0)}}&-\frac{2-f_{+}^{(0)}-f_{+}^{(1)}}{2f_{+}^{(0)}}&1\\
\frac{(2-f_{+}^{(0)})(2-f_{+}^{(1)})}{f_{+}^{(0)}f_{+}^{(1)}}&-\frac{2-f_{+}^{(0)}}{f_{+}^{(0)}}&-\frac{2-f_{+}^{(0)}}{f_{+}^{(0)}}&1\\
\end{array}\right)

(S6)

The elements of these matrices
\hat{L}
and
\hat{R}
alongside the eigenvalues in Eq. (
S1
) are used to calculate the explicit expressions of the time evolved density matrix elements in Eq. (5) of the main text.

(S7)

with
g_{1}(\eta_{\mathrm{aq}})=-2
,
g_{2}(\eta_{\mathrm{aq}})=g_{3}(\eta_{\mathrm{aq}})=\eta_{\mathrm{aq}}-1

g_{4}(\eta_{\mathrm{aq}})=2\eta_{\mathrm{aq}}

and
\eta_{\mathrm{bq}}

after quench

before quench

f_{.}^{(.)}
of the transition matrix
\hat{K}
as

\displaystyle\eta_{\mathrm{aq}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2-f_{+}^{(0)}}{f_{+}^{(1)}},

(S8)

\displaystyle\eta_{\mathrm{bq}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{f_{+}^{(0),\mathrm{I}}f_{+}^{(1),\mathrm{I}}\left(4+2(f_{+}^{(0),\mathrm{II}}-f_{+}^{(1),\mathrm{II}})\right)-f_{+}^{(0),\mathrm{II}}f_{+}^{(1),\mathrm{II}}(4+2(f_{+}^{(0),\mathrm{I}}-f_{+}^{(1),\mathrm{I}}))}{f_{+}^{(0),\mathrm{I}}(2-f_{+}^{(1),\mathrm{I}})(4+2(f_{+}^{(0),\mathrm{II}}-f_{+}^{(1),\mathrm{II}}))-f_{+}^{(0),\mathrm{II}}(2-f_{+}^{(1),\mathrm{II}})\left(4+2(f_{+}^{(0),\mathrm{I}}-f_{+}^{(1),\mathrm{I}})\right)},

(S9)

where the superscripts
\mathrm{I}
and
\mathrm{II}
correspond to two different initial conditions discussed in the main text. Note that when one employs the criterion in Eq. (9) [main text] to explore QMPE, in principle the parameter space under consideration is six dimensional, either in terms of the physically controllable parameters
(\mu_{\mathrm{L}}^{\mathrm{I}},\mu_{\mathrm{R}}^{\mathrm{I}},\mu^{\mathrm{II}},T_{\mathrm{i}};\mu,T)
or equivalently in terms of the transition rates
(f_{+}^{(0),\mathrm{I}},f_{+}^{(1),\mathrm{I}},f_{+}^{(0),\mathrm{II}},f_{+}^{(1),\mathrm{II}};f_{+}^{(0)},f_{+}^{(1)})

S7
)-(
S9
) imply that instead of concentrating on the six dimensional parameter spaces, it would be sufficient to focus on the two-dimensional plane of
(\eta_{\mathrm{aq}},\eta_{\mathrm{bq}})
that captures all the possibilities to get QMPE in
\rho_{\alpha}
-s.

Let us now proceed to classify different regions of the
\eta_{\mathrm{aq}}-\eta_{\mathrm{bq}}
plane with distinct values of
\nu(\hat{\rho})
(defined in the main text) that can take one of the four possible values
0,1,2,3
. From Eq. (
S9
), we see that
\eta_{\mathrm{aq}}
can only be positive since

whereas
\eta_{\mathrm{bq}}
can take both positive and negative values.

Case-1:
\eta_{\mathrm{aq}}<1
and
\eta_{\mathrm{bq}}>0

This case guarantees that
(\eta_{\mathrm{bq}}+1)/((1-\eta_{\mathrm{aq}})\eta_{\mathrm{bq}}+2)>0
. We must have
g_{\alpha}(\eta_{\mathrm{aq}})<0
to satisfy the first criterion of Eq. (9) [main text]. Clearly,
g_{4}(\eta_{\mathrm{aq}})
and consequently
S_{4}
does not satisfy this condition. The other three
g_{1}(\eta_{\mathrm{aq}}),g_{2}(\eta_{\mathrm{aq}})
and
g_{3}(\eta_{\mathrm{aq}})
satisfy it. Thus, the next step is to check if the corresponding magnitudes of
S_{1},S_{2},S_{3}
are less than
1

If we proceed to find out the condition under which
|S_{1}|<1
, that leads us to the following

\eta_{\mathrm{bq}}(1+\eta_{\mathrm{aq}})<0,

(S10)

which is impossible for this case. Thus
S_{1}
cannot exhibit QMPE under this case.

The condition
|S_{2}|<1
gives rise to the condition

1+\eta_{\mathrm{aq}}>0,

(S11)

which is valid under this case. We do not have to perform the similar procedure for
S_{3}
since
S_{3}=S_{2}
.

Therefore, for Case-1, the singly occupied upspin state (
\uparrow

\downarrow

\nu(\hat{\rho})=2
.

Case-2:
\eta_{\mathrm{aq}}>1
and
\eta_{\mathrm{bq}}>0

In this case, the denominator
((1-\eta_{\mathrm{aq}})\eta_{\mathrm{bq}}+2)
in Eq. (
S7

which means
g_{\alpha}(\eta_{\mathrm{aq}})
must be negative. This in turn means,
g_{2}(\eta_{\mathrm{aq}}),g_{3}(\eta_{\mathrm{aq}})
and
g_{4}(\eta_{\mathrm{aq}})
do not satisfy this condition and consequently
S_{2},S_{3}
and
S_{4}
cannot exhibit QMPE under this case. Only chance remains for
S_{1}
. However, when we test if
|S_{1}|<1
that gives

\eta_{\mathrm{bq}}(1+\eta_{\mathrm{aq}})<0,

(S12)

which is impossible under this case. For

Let us consider the sub-case
\eta_{\mathrm{bq}}>2/(\eta_{\mathrm{aq}}-1)
. In this scenario,
g_{\alpha}(\eta_{\mathrm{aq}})
must be positive. Thus,
S_{1}
cannot show QMPE. The condition
|S_{2}|<1
leads us to

1+\eta_{\mathrm{aq}}<0,

(S13)

which is again impossible for the case under consideration. For
|S_{4}|<1
, one has to obey

(\eta_{\mathrm{bq}}+2)(1+\eta_{\mathrm{aq}})<0,

(S14)

which cannot be satisfied under this case.

\nu(\hat{\rho})=0
.

Case-3:
\eta_{\mathrm{aq}}<1
and
\eta_{\mathrm{bq}}<0

For this case, we rewrite the definition of
S_{\alpha}

, where

selects the smaller one between
a
and
b
. In this sub-case,

must be negative. This implies
S_{4}
cannot show QMPE. If we consider

, that leads us to

(1+\eta_{\mathrm{aq}})|\eta_{\mathrm{bq}}|>0,

(S16)

which is true. So,
S_{1}
shows QMPE. For
|S_{2}|<1,
we have to satisfy

1+\eta_{\mathrm{aq}}>0

(S17)

which is valid. As a consequence, both

and
S_{3}

Therefore, for the sub-case
\eta_{\mathrm{aq}}<1
and
\eta_{\mathrm{bq}}<0
and
|\eta_{\mathrm{bq}}|<1
, the doubly occupied state (
d
), the singly occupied upspin (

\downarrow
) state exhibit QMPE and this parameter regime is characterized by

.

Next we consider the sub-case where

. In this sub-case the numerator of the fraction in Eq. (
S15
) is negative while its denominator is positive. So,

has to be positive. This means
S_{1},S_{2}
and
S_{3}

, we arrive at the condition below

|\eta_{\mathrm{bq}}|<2.

(S18)

For

and
\eta_{\mathrm{bq}}<0
and

e
) exhibits QMPE and this region is characterized by

.

and

IV
Retrieving classical limit of the system temperature

The validity of the system temperature (Eq. (11) [main text]) used to demonstrate QMPE in the QD, should be carefully checked. One way to investigate this is to see if the temperatures starting from different initial conditions converge to the same steady state temperature and thereby assure the thermalization of the system. Indeed, we have shown explicitly in the inset of Fig. 3 [main text], inset of Fig.
S2
(a) and Fig.
S2
(b) that temperatures starting from distinct initial conditions lead to same steady state value. In this appendix, we examine the reliability of the temperature in another way by checking the classical limit of the temperature.

Figure S3:
The figure (a) shows how the classical limit can be set up at
\tau=0
by increasing
\beta\epsilon_{0}
. The figure (b) shows that the steady state (
\tau\rightarrow\infty
) value of temperature converges to the initial reservoir temperature (dotted line) with increasing
\beta\epsilon_{0}
. Such equilibration between system-reservoir indicates the recovery of classical behavior at large
\beta\epsilon_{0}
and points towards the correctness of the definition of system temperature. Parameters used are
\beta U=1.25,\beta\mu_{\mathrm{L}}^{\mathrm{I}}=4.5,\beta\mu_{\mathrm{R}}^{\mathrm{I}}=1.0,\beta\mu^{\mathrm{II}}=2.43,\beta T_{\mathrm{i}}=5.0,\beta\mu=2.0
.

To do so, we first revisit the definitions of the parameters
f_{+}^{(j)}
with
j=0,1
from Eq. (4) [main text]. These basically consist of the Fermi-Dirac (FD) distributions of the form
\frac{1}{1+e^{X}}
where
X:=\beta(\epsilon_{0}+jU-\mu)
where
j=0,1
and
\beta=1/T
. Note that one can retrieve the classical Maxwell-Boltzmann (MD) distribution of the form
e^{-X}
by making
e^{X}\gg 1
. In our case, one possible way of getting close to the classical MB distribution is to increase
\beta\epsilon_{0}
so that the difference
\beta(\epsilon_{0}-\mu)
increases. We would like to check this prescription by defining the following measure of difference between FD and MB distributions,

\Delta(\mathrm{MB},\mathrm{FD})=2e^{-X}-2/(1+e^{X}),

(S26)

where the factor
2
in
f_{+}^{(j)}
comes from the simple choice
\mu_{\mathrm{L}}=\mu_{\mathrm{R}}
(like initial condition II used in the main text, similar analysis can be straightforwardly extended to
\mu_{\mathrm{L}}\neq\mu_{\mathrm{R}}
case). In Fig.
S3
(a), we observe that
\Delta(\mathrm{MB},\mathrm{FD})
goes to zero monotonically as we increase
\beta\epsilon_{0}
and for considerably large values of
\beta\epsilon_{0}
we recover the classical behavior of the reservoirs and the system initially at
\tau=0
. To understand what it means for the system temperature
\beta T_{\mathrm{s}}(\tau)
, we present the temporal variations of
\beta T_{\mathrm{s}}(\tau)
in Fig.
S3
(b) for different values of
\beta\epsilon_{0}
. Indeed we observe that the steady state system temperature (at
\tau\rightarrow\infty
) gets more and more closer to the initial (
\tau=0
) reservoir temperature
\beta T
(i.e. unity) as we increase
\beta\epsilon_{0}
i.e. move towards the classical equilibration between system and reservoir. Thus, the temperature we have used for the quantum mechanical system, indeed recovers the classically expected steady state behavior in the classical limit when the FD distributions can be approximated as MB distributions. This assures the validity of the system temperature we utilize to study thermal QMPE.

QMPE in energy, von Neumann entropy and Kullback-Leibler divergence

In this appendix we discuss that apart from the system temperature, there are other observables like average energy, von-Neumann entropy, Kullback-Leibler divergence of the system that also exhibit QMPE. The average energy
E_{\mathrm{s}}(\tau)
and the von-Neumann entropy
S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\tau)
are calculated as

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{s}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}[\hat{\rho}(\tau)\hat{H}_{\mathrm{s}}],

(S27)

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{\alpha=1}^{4}\rho_{\alpha}(\tau)\mathrm{ln}(\rho_{\alpha}(\tau)).

(S28)

S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\tau)

One of the most commonly used tool to analyze MPE or QMPE in the existing literature is the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence. It is a dynamic measure of distance-from-steady state defined below,

D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\tau)=\mathrm{Tr}[\hat{\rho}(\tau)\,\left(\mathrm{ln}\hat{\rho}(\tau)-\mathrm{ln}\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{ss}}\right)],

(S29)

where
\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{ss}}
corresponds to the steady state density matrix. Since the density matrix in our case does not contain any off diagonal elements we can rewrite the expression for
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\tau)
in terms of density matrix elements as

\displaystyle D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\tau)=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{4}\rho_{\alpha}(\tau)\,\mathrm{ln}\left(\frac{\rho_{\alpha}(\tau)}{\rho_{\mathrm{ss},\alpha}}\right)=-\sum_{\alpha=1}^{4}\rho_{\alpha}(\tau)\,\mathrm{ln}(\rho_{\mathrm{ss},\alpha})-S_{\mathrm{vN}},

(S30)

where
S_{\mathrm{vN}}
is the von-Neumann entropy defined in Eq. (
S28
).

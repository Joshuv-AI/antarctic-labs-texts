A Categorical Archive of ChatGPT Failures
Ali Borji

Abstract
Large language models have been demonstrated to be valuable in different fields. ChatGPT, developed by OpenAI, has been trained using massive amounts of data and simulates human conversation by comprehending context and generating appropriate responses. It has garnered significant attention due to its ability to effectively answer a broad range of human inquiries, with fluent and comprehensive answers surpassing prior public chatbots in both security and usefulness. However, a comprehensive analysis of ChatGPT’s failures is lacking, which is the focus of this study. Eleven categories of failures, including reasoning, factual errors, math, coding, and bias, are presented and discussed. The risks, limitations, and societal implications of ChatGPT are also highlighted. The goal of this study is to assist researchers and developers in enhancing future language models and chatbots. 2.1
Reasoning

The NLP community typically assesses LLMs based on different types of reasoning skills, such as arithmetic reasoning, commonsense reasoning, logical reasoning, symbolic reasoning, and multimodal reasoning. This section focuses on forms of reasoning that require familiarity with real-world knowledge and excludes mathematical and logical reasoning, which will be covered in the following sections. It should be noted that there is some overlap between these concepts.

Critical thinking, decision making, and problem solving are all crucial activities that rely heavily on the fundamental aspect of human intelligence known as reasoning.
Models like ChatGPT lack a “world model”, meaning they do not possess a complete understanding of the physical and social world, or the capability to reason about the connections between concepts and entities. They can only generate text based on the patterns they have learned during training.

Spatial reasoning
refers to the ability to understand and manipulate the relationships between objects, people, and places in the physical space around us. It involves visualizing and mentally transforming objects in 2D or 3D space and recognizing patterns, transformations, and relationships between objects. Spatial reasoning skills are important for many tasks and activities, such as navigation, problem-solving, and visualizing objects and structures in your mind. Fig.
1
(left panel) displays an instance where ChatGPT struggles to complete a spatial navigation task. Despite this setback, ChatGPT does possess some level of spatial understanding, as evidenced by its ability to translate the relative positions of grid boxes into language. For the question “A, P, R, X, S and Z are sitting in a row. S and Z are in the centre. A and P are at the ends. R is sitting to the left of A. Who is to the right of P?”, ChatGPT was not able to find the correct answer ‘X’ (right panel in Fig.
1
).

Temporal reasoning
is the ability to reason about and make predictions about events and their ordering in time. It involves understanding the temporal relationships between events, the duration of events, and the timing of events relative to each other. Temporal reasoning is an important aspect of human intelligence, and it is used in various domains, such as scheduling, planning, and prediction. In artificial intelligence, temporal reasoning is a challenging task that requires specialized models and algorithms that can learn and make predictions about sequences of events and their timing. Fig.

Joe arrived before me. John arrived after David. Who arrived first?”, ChatGPT was unable to provide the correct answer.

2.2
Logic

Reasoning refers to the process of thinking through a problem or situation and coming to a conclusion. It involves weighing different options, making connections between ideas, and considering the evidence to support a particular conclusion. Logic, on the other hand, is a branch of mathematics and philosophy that studies the principles of reasoning. It deals with the rules and methods for correct reasoning, such as syllogisms, induction, and deduction. Logic is concerned with ensuring that arguments are valid, consistent, and free from contradictions. While reasoning is a natural and intuitive process, logic provides a systematic and formal approach to reasoning that can help avoid errors and improve the quality of decision-making. Common forms of logical
reasoning include deductive reasoning and inductive reasoning. g. Deductive reasoning is performed
by going from general information to specific conclusions. As opposed to deductive reasoning,
inductive reasoning aims to draw conclusions by
going from the specific to the general.

2.4
Factual Errors

Factual errors refer to inaccuracies in information or statements that are not in accordance with reality or the truth. Factual errors are often unintentional but can result in incorrect or misleading information. In the context of chatbots, it is often referred to as “hallucination.”

ChatGPT’s output lacks accuracy in regards to scientific facts. The information it provides may seem credible, making it challenging for a knowledgeable expert to detect its inaccuracies, but it ultimately proves to be incorrect. It sometimes lacks knowledge of basic facts, which can be quickly obtained through a Google search.
Models like ChatGPT lack the ability to access information from an external memory or database, leading to potential inaccuracies in recall of facts. However, because these programs generate images and text based on statistical patterns, rather than gaining a true understanding of the world, they are prone to fabricating facts and repeating hateful and biased statements (
e.g.

[
24
]
). Figs.

here
).

ChatGPT struggles to differentiate between factual information and fiction and creates imaginary information (Fig.

). While this is a challenge that humans face as well, they at least comprehend the distinction between the two.

It is important to exercise caution when using language models like LLMs as they have only acquired a limited and superficial understanding of human knowledge and may not provide accurate factual information.

I asked ChatGPT whether it is aware of its biases. Its response was “As a language model, I have been trained on a large dataset of text that includes many examples of biases and stereotypes. 2.6
Wit and Humor

“Humor is reason gone mad.”

Groucho Marx

Humor is the quality of being amusing or comical, often expressed through words or actions that entertain or make someone laugh. It can take many forms, such as jokes, satire, irony, or playful behavior, and its meaning can vary greatly depending on cultural context and personal taste. Humor often involves the use of humor techniques such as puns, wordplay, and absurdity to evoke a response from the audience. It is a complex and delicate form of human communication that poses a significant challenge for bots, even the most advanced ones.

How well does ChatGPT handle joke creation? While ChatGPT has some understanding of humor, there have been relatively few publicly documented failures in this regard. An example is depicted in Fig.

ChatGPT can assist with tasks such as producing generic functions or repetitive code, but the need for programmers will persist.

ChatGPT and GitHub CoPilot are distinct in several ways. CoPilot, released by OpenAI in October 2021, has a feature allowing users to suggest code through code comments and outlines. It also acts as a highly advanced auto-complete in an IDE, offering code optimizations and suggestions. CoPilot focuses mainly on code completion, while ChatGPT prioritizes providing ideas, explanations, and then generating code. Syntactic structure refers to the arrangement of words, phrases, and clauses in a sentence to form a well-defined and meaningful structure according to the rules of a particular language. Discussion

In addition to the previously mentioned points, there are several other potential adverse impacts that large language models (LLMs) like ChatGPT may have on society, which will be discussed below.

3.1
Transparency and Trustworthiness

The size and complexity of deep learning models, particularly language models, have increased to the point where even the creators have difficulty comprehending why their models make specific predictions. This lack of interpretability is a major concern, particularly in situations where individuals want to understand the reasoning behind a model’s output. The absence of transparency regarding the data sources used for generating output makes it challenging to properly cite the answers provided by ChatGPT. Further, LLMs lack the ability to provide uncertainty estimates
[
51
]
. This makes it difficult for users to verify or have confidence in its output. As a result, answers produced by ChatGPT have been banned on forums such as Stack Overflow
16
16

16

https://stackoverflow.com/help/gpt-policy
. For instance, when someone utilizes ChatGPT to obtain first aid guidance, it is crucial for them to ensure the response they receive is dependable, precise, and sourced from credible sources.

Lack of transparency and reliability is a major concern in today’s era of fake news and misinformation. AI language models, if manipulated, can spread false information and destabilize politics. OpenAI is working on detecting its model’s output and adding cryptographic markers, but these responsible AI measures are not happening fast enough and may not be sufficient.

The security of LLMs is a growing concern due to their parent model’s general nature prior to fine tuning. Conclusion and Future Work

In this study, I delved into several shortcomings of ChatGPT and highlighted its limitations. Despite its impressive capabilities in certain tasks, further improvement is necessary for it to excel in areas such as reasoning, mathematical problem-solving, reducing bias, etc. At present, ChatGPT remains susceptible to these faults. It is uncertain whether these limitations can be addressed due to the unclear capabilities of current technology. The reliability and trustworthiness of ChatGPT and future models is also in question.

While this study thoroughly evaluates ChatGPT, it has some limitations that need to be addressed in future studies, as outlined below.

To address this, it is crucial to have a diverse and inclusive dataset that can mitigate biases and ensure equitable and accurate outcomes.

ChatGPT’s capacity to comprehend and address intricate inquiries is restricted. Although it can produce articulate and fluid responses, it may not have the ability to comprehend the intricacies of a question and offer a comprehensive and precise answer. This is particularly relevant to inquiries that necessitate reasoning, abstract thought, and an appreciation of multiple perspectives.

•

Language models like LLMs have the potential to be effectively utilized for both few-shot and zero-shot learning (

). This advantage enables them to learn from limited labeled data and generalize well to new data, leading to numerous possibilities for practical application in the real world.

•

The integration of LLMs with search engines and the combination of answers from various chatbots, such as ChatGPT and Google’s Bard, presents a non-trivial challenge. This is an issue that must be addressed in the future.

•

Making large language models open source can aid in gaining a deeper comprehension of these models and addressing their deficiencies. In a recent effort in this direction, Meta company has recently released the LLaMA model. See

.

•

LLMs can require significant computational resources for both training and run, which may incur high costs and consume significant amounts of energy. This has consequences for nature and environment. A further issue is that few institutions have the ability to train LLMs using large-scale data, which could result in research in this field being less accessible to a wider range of people.

•

The fair evaluation and comparison of LLMs remains an ongoing issue, despite the availability of datasets designed for this purpose. The primary obstacle lies in gathering questions that are unlikely to be present in the training set of these LLMs, given that these models continually crawl the web for data. Additionally, it is difficult to ascertain whether ChatGPT has come across a particular question before due to the inaccessible nature of the training set. One possible method for evaluating ChatGPT is to employ examples that are highly improbable to have been previously encountered by the model. To accomplish this, I suggest constructing held-out test sets that are not publicly available, akin to how some image recognition competitions are administered.

•

Finally, the collection of failures outlined here can serve as a foundation for creating a comprehensive dataset of typical questions to assess future LLM and ChatGPT iterations, as well as generate simulated data for model training and evaluating the performance of models.

Any language model used publicly must be monitored, transparently communicated, and regularly checked for biases. Even though the current technology is far a way from algorithms and hardware in the brain, it is still astonishing how well it works. Whether or not it can reach human level intelligence or beat it in wide array of problems remains to be seen.

The double contravariant powerset monad in the Goguen category of fuzzy sets
Sijia Lu, Dexue Zhang

Abstract
A monad is constructed in the Goguen category of fuzzy sets valued in a unital quantale, which is an analog of the double contravariant powerset monad in the category of sets. With help of this monad it is proved that the Goguen category of fuzzy sets is dually monadic over itself. Keywords: Fuzzy set, Quantale, Monad MSC(2020): 03E72, 18C15, 18C20

A

p\with\Big(\bigvee_{i\in I}q_{i}\Big)=\bigvee_{i\in I}p\with q_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad\Big(\bigvee_{i\in I}p_{i}\Big)\with q=\bigvee_{i\in I}p_{i}\with q

p,q,p_{i},q_{i}\in{\sf Q}

.

Quantales abound in mathematics; numerous examples are presented in

27
]
. As advocated in Goguen

8
,
9
,
11
]
(where quantales are called complete lattice ordered semigroup), quantales are natural candidates for truth-value tables in the theory of fuzzy sets.

Proposition 2.1
.

(
[
7
,
27
]
)
Let
({\sf Q},\with,k)
be a unital quantale.

(i)

k\leq y\ldd x\iff x\leq y\iff k\leq x\rdd y
.

(ii)

x\ldd k=x=k\rdd x
.

(iii)

(y\rdd z)\ldd x=y\rdd(z\ldd x)
.

(iv)

(y\ldd x)\with x\leq y,\ x\with(x\rdd y)\leq y
.

(v)

(z\ldd y)\ldd x=z\ldd(x\with y)
,

x\rdd(y\rdd z)=(y\with x)\rdd z
.

(vi)

(z\ldd y)\with(y\ldd x)\leq z\ldd x
,
(x\rdd y)\with(y\rdd z)\leq x\rdd z
.

The triple

and

are natural transformations and satisfy the monad requirements, we show that it is a lifting of a monad in

Assigning to each

For each set

Let

A

Lemma 4.5

For each set

is a natural transformation; that is, for each map

\textstyle{\exp_{\sf Q}^{-2}Y}
\scriptstyle{j_{Y}}
\textstyle{\exp_{\sf Q}X}
\textstyle{\exp_{\sf Q}Y}
\scriptstyle{\exp_{\sf Q}f}
\textstyle{\exp_{\sf Q}X}
\textstyle{\exp_{\sf Q}^{-2}X}
\scriptstyle{j_{X}}
\textstyle{\exp_{\sf Q}^{-2}X}
\textstyle{\exp_{\sf Q}^{-2}Y}
\scriptstyle{\exp_{\sf Q}^{-2}f}

is commutative. This is easy since for all

and

) we have

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
{\sf Q}
be a commutative quantale. Then the powerset monad
(\mathscr{U},{\sf m},{\sf e})
is a submonad of the double contravariant powerset monad
(\mathfrak{P},\mu,\eta)

Proof.

We prove the conclusion in two steps.

Step 1
. For each object
(X,\alpha)
of
{\sf Set}({\sf Q})
and each
\lambda\in{\sf Q}^{X}
,
\alpha^{\downarrow}(\lambda)=(\alpha_{\dagger}^{\uparrow})^{\uparrow}(j_{X}(\lambda)).

Since

\alpha^{\downarrow}(\lambda)={\rm sub}_{X}(\lambda,\alpha)=j_{X}(\lambda)(\alpha)

and

{\rm sub}_{X}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\leq j_{X}(\lambda)(\gamma_{1})\rightarrow j_{X}(\lambda)(\gamma_{2})

for all
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in{\sf Q}^{X}
, it suffices to show that

(\alpha_{\dagger}^{\uparrow})^{\uparrow}(\Lambda)=\Lambda(\alpha)

whenever
\Lambda\colon{\sf Q}^{X}\>\lx@xy@svg{\hbox{\raise 2.5pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\hbox{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces{}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern 4.49588pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{{}\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 3.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{}$}}}\kern 3.0pt}}}}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern 4.49588pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{{}\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 3.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{}$}}}\kern 3.0pt}}}}}}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern 14.99176pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\lx@xy@tip{1}\lx@xy@tip{-1}}}}}}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}\ignorespaces}}}}\>{\sf Q}
satisfies

{\rm sub}_{X}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\leq\Lambda(\gamma_{1})\rightarrow\Lambda(\gamma_{2}).

Since
{\sf Q}
is commutative, by definition we have

\displaystyle(\alpha_{\dagger}^{\uparrow})^{\uparrow}(\Lambda)

\displaystyle=\bigwedge_{\gamma\in{\sf Q}^{X}}{\rm sub}_{X}(\alpha,\gamma)\rightarrow\Lambda(\gamma).

Since
{\rm sub}_{X}(\alpha,\alpha)\geq k
, then

(\alpha_{\dagger}^{\uparrow})^{\uparrow}(\Lambda)\leq k\rightarrow\Lambda(\alpha)=\Lambda(\alpha).

Conversely, since

{\rm sub}_{X}(\alpha,\gamma)\rightarrow\Lambda(\gamma)\geq(\Lambda(\alpha)\rightarrow\Lambda(\gamma))\rightarrow\Lambda(\gamma)\geq\Lambda(\alpha)

for all
\gamma\in{\sf Q}^{X}
, then

\displaystyle(\alpha_{\dagger}^{\uparrow})^{\uparrow}(\Lambda)

\displaystyle=\bigwedge_{\gamma\in{\sf Q}^{X}}{\rm sub}_{X}(\alpha,\gamma)\rightarrow\Lambda(\gamma)\geq\Lambda(\alpha).

Step 2
.
(\mathscr{U},{\sf m},{\sf e})
is a submonad of
(\mathfrak{P},\mu,\eta)
.

By
Step 1
one sees that for each
(X,\alpha)
of
{\sf Set}({\sf Q})
, the map

\kappa_{(X,\alpha)}\colon\mathscr{U}(X,\alpha)\>\lx@xy@svg{\hbox{\raise 2.5pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\hbox{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces{}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern 4.49588pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{{}\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 3.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{}$}}}\kern 3.0pt}}}}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern 4.49588pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{{}\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 3.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{}$}}}\kern 3.0pt}}}}}}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern 14.99176pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\lx@xy@tip{1}\lx@xy@tip{-1}}}}}}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}\ignorespaces}}}}\>\mathfrak{P}(X,\alpha),\quad\lambda\mapsto{\rm sub}_{X}(\lambda,-)

satisfies the Goguen condition, hence
\kappa=\{\kappa_{(X,\alpha)}\}
is a natural transformation from
\mathscr{U}
to
\mathfrak{P}
, and it is a lifting of the natural transformation

in the above lemma.

It is clear that, as a morphism between functors,

is a monomorphism and
\eta=\kappa\circ{\sf e}
. So, to see that
(\mathscr{U},{\sf m},{\sf e})
is a submonad of
(\mathfrak{P},\mu,\eta)
, we only need to show that the square

\textstyle{\mathscr{U}}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{P}}
\scriptstyle{\kappa}
\textstyle{\mathscr{U}^{2}}
\textstyle{\mathscr{U}}
\scriptstyle{{\sf m}}
\textstyle{\mathscr{U}^{2}}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{P}^{2}}
\scriptstyle{\kappa*\kappa}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{P}^{2}}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{P}}
\scriptstyle{\mu}

is commutative. Since
\kappa
is a lifting of
j
, it suffices to show that for each set
X
, the following square is commutative:

\textstyle{\exp_{\sf Q}X}
\textstyle{\exp_{\sf Q}^{-2}X}
\scriptstyle{j_{X}}
\textstyle{\exp_{\sf Q}^{2}X}
\textstyle{\exp_{\sf Q}X}
\scriptstyle{{\sf m}_{X}}
\textstyle{\exp_{\sf Q}^{2}X}
\textstyle{\exp_{\sf Q}^{-4}X}
\scriptstyle{(j*j)_{X}}
\textstyle{\exp_{\sf Q}^{-4}X}
\textstyle{\exp_{\sf Q}^{-2}X}
\scriptstyle{\mu_{X}}

For this we calculate: for all

\displaystyle\mu_{X}\circ(j*j)_{X}(\Lambda)(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\mu_{X}\circ j_{{\sf Q}^{{\sf Q}^{X}}}\circ\exp_{\sf Q}j_{X}(\Lambda)(\lambda)

\displaystyle=j_{{\sf Q}^{{\sf Q}^{X}}}(j_{X}(\Lambda))(\widehat{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\bigwedge_{\Xi\in{\sf Q}^{{\sf Q}^{X}}}j_{X}(\Lambda)(\Xi)\rightarrow\Xi(\lambda)

\displaystyle={\rm sub}_{X}\Big(\bigvee_{\gamma\in{\sf Q}^{X}}\Lambda(\gamma)\with\gamma,\lambda\Big)

-filter monad

Proposition 4.9

The triple

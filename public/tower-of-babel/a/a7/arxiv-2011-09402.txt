1
Introduction

Given a collection
\mathcal{A}
of subsets of an
n
element set,
\mathcal{A}
follows
oddtown rules
if the sizes of all sets in
\mathcal{A}
are odd and distinct pairs of sets from
\mathcal{A}
have even sized intersections. Berlekemp
[
4
]
proved that the size of a family which satisfies oddtown rules is at most
n
which is easily seen to be best possible. One generalization of this classical result is the
skew oddtown theorem
which appears in Babai and Frankl
[
3
]
:

Theorem 1

(Babai, Frankl
[
3
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{1},A_{2},\dots,A_{m}\}
and
\mathcal{B}=\{B_{1},B_{2},\dots,B_{m}\}
be families of subsets of an
n
element set where
|A_{i}\cap B_{i}|
is odd for all
i\in[m]
and
|A_{i}\cap B_{j}|
is even for all
i<j\in[m]
. Then
m\leq n
.

In this paper, we give analogs of Theorem
1
for
k\geq 3
families. Our main result is as follows:

Theorem 2
.

Let
(\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{k})
be set families of an
n
element set with
\mathcal{A}_{j}=\{A_{j,i}:1\leq i\leq m\}
where

|\bigcap_{j=1}^{k}A_{j,i_{j}}|
is even if and only if
i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\in[m]
are all distinct. Then
m=O(n^{1/\lfloor k/2\rfloor})
.

By employing a connection to a hypergraph covering problem and a hypergraph extension (see Alon
[
2
]
, Cioabă-Kündgen-Verstraëte
[
5
]
, and Leader-Milićević-Tan
[
11
]
) of the Graham-Pollak Theorem
[
10
]
, we also show that Theorem
2
is best possible. Moreover, we prove the following:

Theorem 3
.

Let
t,k
be integers with
t\geq 2
and
2t-2\leq k
. If
(\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{k})
are set families of an
n
element set with
\mathcal{A}_{j}=\{A_{j,i}:1\leq i\leq m\}
where
|\bigcap_{j=1}^{k}A_{j,i_{j}}|
is even if and only if at least
t
of the
i_{j}
are distinct, then
m=O(n^{1/(t-1)})
.

Theorem
3
is seen to be best possible by again using a connection to a hypergraph covering problem. In the case where
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{1}=\cdots=\mathcal{A}_{k}
, we recover a variation of the classical oddtown problem. For integers

, a family
\mathcal{A}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
is said to follow
(k,t)
-oddtown rules
if the intersection of any

\mathcal{A}
is odd if

and even if

.

Corollary 1
.

Let

be so that
2\leq t\leq k
and
2t-2\leq k
. If
\mathcal{A}\subset 2^{[n]}
follows
(k,t)
-oddtown rules, then
|\mathcal{A}|=O(n^{1/(t-1)})
.

Corollary
1
is best possible by considering
\mathcal{A}_{i}=\{A\in\binom{[n]}{t-1}:i\in A\}
for
i\in[n]
for
n
so that the binomial coefficients corresponding to the size of the
d
-wise intersections are odd for
d<t
. The first open case is
(3,3)
-oddtown for which the above construction has size
\Theta(n^{1/2})
. If
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}\}
satisfies
(3,3)
-oddtown rules, then
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=\{A_{1}\cap A_{i}:2\leq i\leq m\}
satisfies oddtown rules and thus
|\mathcal{A}|\leq n+1
. This is unlikely to be best possible as this holds replacing
A_{1}
with
any

A_{j}\in\mathcal{A}
.

When
t<k
and
2t-2>k
, we are able to show that

(see Section
4
), and conjecture that a stronger bound holds:

Conjecture 1
.

Let

be integers with

and
2t-2>k
. If

The bound

Many results in extremal set theory regarding even/odd sized intersections have been extended to arbitrary primes

For more on

[

[

]

[

]
consider

-wise oddtown problems wherein the size of the sets are odd and the sizes of intersections of

[

study

-intersecting set systems where the size of distinct

.

Conjecture 2
.

Let
p

(\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{k})
be set families of an

(1)

It follows that (

In this section, we relate the problem in Theorem
2
and Theorem
3
to that of a covering problem of a particular hypergraph. We first define the corresponding collection of set families.

Definition 1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{j}=\{A_{j,i}:1\leq i\leq m\}
for

. Then
(\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{k})

Let

(k,t)
-tuple modulo

.

In the setting where

(2,2)
-tuple modulo

2

and Theorem

, we consider the following related hypergraph covering problem:

Definition 2
.

Let

2

is a collection of complete
k
-partite
k
-graphs which cover each edge of

t

k
-partite
k

(2)

The problem in Definition

[

]

\frac{2\binom{n-1}{k}}{\binom{2k}{k}}\leq f_{2k}(n)\leq\binom{n-k}{k}.

(3)

Currently Leader, Milićević, and Tan

]
have the best general bounds. A key ingredient in our proof of Theorem

[

]
. To this end, define the

\displaystyle V(OK_{n:k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k}):\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}\in\binom{[n]}{k}\}

\displaystyle E(OK_{n:k})

\displaystyle\{((i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}),(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})):\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}\cap\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}\}=\emptyset\}.

Let

For each complete

\mathcal{C}_{i}:=\prod_{j=1}^{2k}X_{i,j}

\Box

Let

(11)

Since

4.2  Upper bound on

We first prove the following lemma which relates

to

.

Lemma 6
.

Given

, let
\alpha=\alpha(k,t)=2t-k+2
. Then letting

,

b_{2,2}(n)\geq\begin{cases}{m\choose t-1}\hskip 15.0pt\text{if}\hskip 5.0pt2t-2\leq k\vskip 2.84526pt\\
{m-\alpha\choose k-t+1}\hskip 15.0pt\text{if}\hskip 5.0pt2t-2>k\\
\end{cases}

In particular, both quantities are less than or equal to
n+1
by (
1
).

Proof.

Let
m=b_{k,t}(n)
for
2t-2\leq k
and let
(\mathcal{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{k})
be a Bollobás set
(k,t)
-tuple modulo
2
of size
m
where
\mathcal{A}_{i}=\{A_{j,i}\subseteq[n]:i\in[m]\}
for all
j\in[k]
. For each
I=\{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{t-1}\}\in\binom{[m]}{t-1}
, define the families
\mathcal{A}:=\{A_{I}\}
and
\mathcal{B}:=\{B_{I}\}
where:

\displaystyle A_{I}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle A_{1,i_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap A_{t-1,i_{t-1}}

\displaystyle B_{I}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle A_{t,i_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap A_{2t-2,i_{t-1}}

Note that
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
is a Bollobás set pair modulo

and hence the result follows.

Similarly, for
2t-2>k

(\mathcal{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{k})

\mathcal{A}_{i}:=\{A_{j,i}\subseteq[n]:i\in[m]\}

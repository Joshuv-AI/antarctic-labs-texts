Core States of Neutron Stars from Anatomizing their Scaled Structure Equations
Bao-Jun Cai, Bao-An Li, Zhen Zhang

Abstract
Given an Equation of State (EOS) for neutron star (NS) matter, there is a unique mass-radius sequence characterized by a maximum mass M NS max M_{\textrm{NS}}^{\max} at radius R max R_{\max} . We first show analytically that the M NS max M_{\textrm{NS}}^{\max} and R max R_{\max} scale linearly with two different combinations of NS central pressure P c P_{\textrm{c}} and energy density ε c \varepsilon_{\textrm{c}} by dissecting perturbatively the dimensionless Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff (TOV) equations governing NS internal variables. The scaling relations are then verified via 87 widely used and rather diverse phenomenological as well as 17 microscopic NS EOSs with/without considering hadron-quark phase transitions and hyperons by solving numerically the original TOV equations. The EOS of densest NS matter allowed before it collapses into a black hole (BH) is then obtained. Using the universal M NS max M_{\textrm{NS}}^{\max} and R max R_{\max} scalings and NICER (Neutron Star Interior Composition Explorer) and XMM-Newton mass-radius observational data for PSR J0740+6620, a very narrow constraining band on the NS central EOS is extracted directly from the data for the first time without using any specific input EOS model.

2. NS Radius/Mass Scalings by Perturbatively Solving the Scaled Structural Equations

Information about the EOS in the entire NS is normally obtained by comparing observations with predictions of a given EOS model by integrating the Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff (TOV) equations
(
Tolman 1939
;
Oppenheimer & Volkoff 1939
)
from the NS center to its surface.
In the following, we present a novel approach to access directly the NS core EOS without the hinderance of its still unconstrained EOSs in other regions especially the crust.

Using the energy density scale
W=G^{-1}(4\pi G\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}})^{-1/2}
and length scale
Q=(4\pi G\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}})^{-1/2}
, the reduced mass
\widehat{M}\equiv M/W
, radius
\widehat{r}\equiv r/Q
, pressure
\widehat{P}\equiv P/\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}}
and energy density
\widehat{\varepsilon}\equiv\varepsilon/\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}}
(with
\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}}
being the central energy density) satisfy the dimensionless TOV equations (adopting
c=1
),

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\widehat{r}}\widehat{P}=-\frac{(\widehat{P}+\widehat{\varepsilon})(\widehat{r}^{3}\widehat{P}+\widehat{M})}{\widehat{r}^{2}-2\widehat{M}\widehat{r}},~~\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\widehat{r}}\widehat{M}=\widehat{r}^{2}\widehat{\varepsilon}.

(1)

The smallness of
\widehat{P}
enables solving perturbatively the above coupled evolution equations of
\widehat{P}
and
\widehat{M}
given an EOS
P(\varepsilon)
. Starting from the NS center at
\widehat{r}=0
, the (reduced) energy density
\widehat{\varepsilon}
, pressure
\widehat{P}
and mass
\widehat{M}
can be expanded as
\widehat{\varepsilon}\approx 1+a_{1}\widehat{r}+a_{2}\widehat{r}^{2}+\cdots
,
\widehat{P}\approx\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}+b_{1}\widehat{r}+b_{2}\widehat{r}^{2}+\cdots
, and
\widehat{M}\approx c_{1}\widehat{r}+c_{2}\widehat{r}^{2}+\cdots
, respectively. Moreover, the reduced EOS
\widehat{P}(\widehat{\varepsilon})
can be written as
\widehat{P}=\sum_{k=1}d_{k}\widehat{\varepsilon}^{k}
with coefficients
\{d_{k}\}
.
The inner-relations among the coefficients
\{a_{k}\}
,
\{b_{k}\}
and
\{c_{k}\}
are determined by the NS EOS and the TOV equations.
Particularly, as shown in the APPENDIX, all
a_{k}
’s and
b_{k}
’s with odd
k
are zero and the first nonzero coefficient is
b_{2}=-6^{-1}(1+3\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}^{2}+4\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}})
,

c_{1}=c_{2}=0
and
c_{k}=a_{k-3}/k
for
k\geq 3
.

Within the perturbative approach outlined above, the reduced radius
\widehat{R}
defined via the vanishing of pressure
\widehat{P}
is determined to order
b_{2}
by
\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}+b_{2}\widehat{R}^{2}=0
. The resulting
\widehat{R}=[6\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}/(1+3\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}^{2}+4\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}})]^{1/2}
multiplied by
Q\sim 1/\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}}}
makes the physical radius
R=Q\widehat{R}
to scale as

R\sim\nu_{\textrm{c}}\equiv\frac{\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}^{1/2}}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}}}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{1+3\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}^{2}+4\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}}\right)^{1/2}.

(2)

Since the reduced NS mass is
\widehat{M}(\widehat{R})\approx c_{3}\widehat{R}^{3}=\widehat{R}^{3}/3
, its physical mass
M_{\textrm{NS}}\equiv M\sim\widehat{R}^{3}/\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}}}
then scales as

M_{\textrm{NS}}\sim\Gamma_{\textrm{c}}\equiv\frac{\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}^{3/2}}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}}}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{1+3\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}^{2}+4\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}}\right)^{3/2}.

(3)

The above scalings relate directly the radius and mass observables of a NS with its central EOS
{P}_{\textrm{c}}(\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}})=P(\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}})
. These scalings are general since they are direct consequences of the TOV equations themselves without assuming any particular structure, composition and EOS for the NS. Once the
\nu_{\textrm{c}}
or
\Gamma_{\textrm{c}}
is constrained within a certain range by observational data, it can be used to determine the NS central EOS.
Moreover, the scaling for NS mass is expected to be more accurate than that for the radius which is affected more by the uncertain low-density EOS for the NS crust
(
Baym et al. 1971
)
, see FIG.
1
.
Actually the low-density EOS may affect the crust thickness and consequently the NS radius sizably while has little influence on its mass
(
Xu et al. 2009
)
.

Extensive studies on scaling relations among NS observables exist in the literature, see, e.g., Refs.
Rhoades & Ruffini 1974
;
Hartle 1978
;
Kalogera & Baym 1996
;
Lattimer & Prakash 2005
;
Tsui & Leung 2005
;
Yagi & Yunes 2013
;
Wen et al. 2019
;
Jiang & Yagi 2020
and references therein. These EOS-independent universial scalings are mostly for NS bulk properties, such as the moment of inertia (
I
), tidal Love number (
L
), (spin-induced) quadrupole moment (
{Q}
), compactness (
C
), and frequencies (
f
or
\omega
) of various oscillation modes of NSs. Knowing one or more observables, these scalings (e.g.,
I
-
L
-
{Q}
,
I
-
L
-
C
or
M\omega
-
C
) enable the finding of other observables or bulk properties that have not been or hard to be measured.
They are completely different from the EOS-independent scalings between the radius and mass of most massive NSs and the combinations of the reduced core pressure and energy density that we have found and used here for the first time. Among the earlier scalings closest to ours is the one
showing
M_{\textrm{NS}}^{\max}\sim D_{\textrm{M}}\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}}^{-1/2}
(see, e.g., Refs.
Hartle 1978
;
Kalogera & Baym 1996
;
Lattimer & Prakash 2005
) with the coefficient
D_{\textrm{M}}
estimated using either unrealistic or certain selected microscopic dense matter EOSs.
In addition, a numerical power law was given as
R\sim D_{\textrm{R}}P^{\delta}
with
\delta\approx 0.23\mbox{$\sim$}0.26

(
Lattimer & Prakash 2001
)
, where
P
is the pressure at about 1-2 times the nuclear saturation density
\rho_{\textrm{sat}}
.
Since the coefficients
D_{\textrm{M}}
and
D_{\textrm{R}}
are obtained quite empirically, the corresponding predictions either have sizable EOS model dependence or lack solid/clear physical origins.
Considering our method and most importantly,
it is the scaling of the observables
M_{\textrm{NS}}^{\max}
and radius
R_{\max}
with the internal variables (different combinations of the core pressure and energy density via
\nu_{\textrm{c}}
and
\Gamma_{\textrm{c}}
) that enable us to extract the relationship (core EOS)
{P}_{\textrm{c}}(\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}})=P(\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}})
once either one of the observables is constrained observationally, instead of just the individual value of
\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}}
or the pressure around
(1\mbox{$\sim$}2)\rho_{\textrm{sat}}
in the previous studies of scalings. Thus, as outlined in the previous section and discussed in detail in the APPENDIX, our method and results are completely novel with respect to the exisiting work in the literature.

3. Validations of NS Radius/mass Scalings by Solving the Original Structural Equations

The maximum mass
M_{\textrm{NS}}^{\max}
and radius
R_{\max}
characterize the densest stable NS configuration
on the mass-radius curve before it collapses to a BH for a given NS EOS. The
M_{\textrm{NS}}^{\max}
is required to be at least as large as the currently observed most massive NS.
Using the signals from GW170817
(
Abbott et al. 2018
)

\sim
2.16

M_{\odot}

(
Rezzolla et al. 2018
)
, similarly
Margalit & Metzger 2017
showed it should be
\lesssim 2.17M_{\odot}
by including the electromagnetic information). In addition, a consistent prediction of
M_{\textrm{NS}}^{\max}\lesssim 2.16\mbox{$\sim$}2.28M_{\odot}
through general relativistic magnetohydrodynamics simulations was also given
(
Ruiz et al. 2018
)

4. Central EOS via NICER’s Mass/radius Measurements and Limits for Speed of Sound

As discussed earlier, once the
\Gamma_{\textrm{c}}
or
\nu_{\textrm{c}}
is determined within certain range by astrophysical observations, the NS central EOS
P_{\textrm{c}}(\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}})
is subsequently constrained. The
\Gamma_{\textrm{c}}
and
\nu_{\textrm{c}}
extracted above from NICER’s observation of PSR J0740+6620 provide two independent constraints on the NS central EOS as shown in FIG.
2
by the blue and chocolate bands, respectively.
Since the uncertainty of the radius is currently about 3 times larger than that of the mass measurement, the resulting constraining band of the central EOS from the radius measurement is wider.
Consequently, the central EOS is essentially given by the mass measurement via
\Gamma_{\textrm{c}}
while the radius effectively sets the upper- and lower-limits for
\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}}
. In the overlapping area of the two constraining bands, the most probable NS central EOS can be written as
P_{\textrm{c}}(\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}})\approx 0.012\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}}^{4/3}\cdot[1+0.047\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}}^{1/3}+0.0026\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}}^{2/3}+\cdots]
where
\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}}
varies within about 640
\sim
1210
\,\textrm{MeV}/\textrm{fm}^{3}
.

2
), respectively.
This value of central energy density is consistent with that (about 900
\sim
1200
\textrm{MeV}/\textrm{fm}^{3}
)
from a joint Bayesian inference using NS mass and radius constraints from GW170817 and several other astronomical observations
(
Al-Mamun et al. 2021
)
. Using similar observational data,
Brandes et al. 2023
found very recently for a
2.1M_{\odot}
NS (with the inferred radius about 11.6 km) that
P_{\textrm{c}}\approx 312_{-169}^{+226}\,\textrm{MeV}/\textrm{fm}^{3}
and
\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}}\approx 904_{-327}^{+329}\,\textrm{MeV}/\textrm{fm}^{3}
. Our results are consistent with theirs but have much smaller uncertainties.

The stiffness of NS EOS can be measured effectively by the speed of sound squared
s^{2}\equiv\mathrm{d}P/\mathrm{d}\varepsilon

(
Bedaque & Steiner 2015
;
Fujimoto et al. 2022
)
. Using the heuristic relation
M_{\textrm{NS}}\sim\widehat{R}^{3}/\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}}}
and the correlation in panel (b) of FIG.
1
, we obtain the central speed of sound squared for the maximum mass configuration,

s_{\textrm{c}}^{2}=\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}\left(1+\frac{1}{3}\frac{1+3\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}^{2}+4\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}}{1-3\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}^{2}}\right).

(4)

Essentially,
s_{\textrm{c}}^{2}
grows (minishes) as
\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}
increases (decreases).
For PSR J0740+6620, using the constraints for
\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}
given above we obtain
s_{\textrm{c}}^{2}\approx 0.45_{-0.18}^{+0.14}
.
The condition
s_{\textrm{c}}^{2}\leq 1
leads in addition to an upper bound
\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}\lesssim 0.374
. Therefore although the causality condition requires apparently
\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}\leq 1
, the superdense nature of core NS matter (indicated by the nonlinear dependence of
s_{\textrm{c}}^{2}
on
\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}
) renders it to be much smaller.

Finally, we notice here that slightly different core EOSs are extracted by using the radii extracted by
Miller et al. 2021
and
Salmi et al. 2022
compared to the results presented above using the radius from
Riley et al. 2021
. This is what one expects and it verifies the sensitivity of our approach. Interestingly, as shown numerically in the APPENDIX, the constraining bands on the core EOSs in PSR J0740+6620 from using the three radii
(
Riley et al. 2021
;
Miller et al. 2021
;
Salmi et al. 2022
)
are all very narrow and they largely overlap. The remaining small widths of the constraining bands reflect mainly the observational uncertainties as no model EOS is used in extracting the NS core EOS.
As the radius uncertainty becomes smaller with the ongoing and planned new measurements using more advanced
high-precision observatories
(
Sathyaprakash et al. 2019
;
Watts 2019
)
, our method can be further applied to refine the core EOS of massive NSs.

5. Conclusion

The mass and radius of the maximum mass configuration of any dense matter EOS scale linearly with two different combinations of their central pressure and energy density.
The EOS of densest NS matter allowed before it collapses into a BH is obtained without using any specific model EOS.
Using NICER’s mass-radius observational data for PSR J0740+6620, a very narrow constraining band on its central EOS is extracted directly from the observational data itself without using any model EOS for the first time.

A. Perturbative Treatments of the Dimensionless TOV Equations

The dimensionless TOV equations are given in Eq. (
1
), where the reduced pressure
\widehat{P}
, reduced energy density
\widehat{\varepsilon}
and the reduced mass
\widehat{M}
are all functions of the reduced radius
\widehat{r}
. Without loosing generality, we expand them in polynomials,

\displaystyle\widehat{\varepsilon}=

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{k=1}a_{k}\widehat{r}^{k}\approx 1+a_{1}\widehat{r}+a_{2}\widehat{r}^{2}+a_{3}\widehat{r}^{3}+\cdots,

(a5)

\displaystyle\widehat{P}=

\displaystyle\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}+\sum_{k=1}b_{k}\widehat{r}^{k}\approx\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}+b_{1}\widehat{r}+b_{2}\widehat{r}^{2}+b_{3}\widehat{r}^{3}+\cdots,

(a6)

\displaystyle\widehat{M}=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}c_{k}\widehat{r}^{k}\approx c_{1}\widehat{r}+c_{2}\widehat{r}^{2}+c_{3}\widehat{r}^{3}+\cdots,

(a7)

here
\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}={P}_{\textrm{c}}/{\varepsilon}_{\textrm{c}}
and
\widehat{\varepsilon}_{\textrm{c}}=\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}}/\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}}=1
.
By putting these polynomials into the dimensionless TOV equations, and expanding them to the same order of
\widehat{r}
, we obtain,

b_{1}=0,

(a8)

and

c_{1}=c_{2}=0,~~c_{3}=\frac{1}{3},~~c_{k}=\frac{a_{k-3}}{k},~~\textrm{for}\;k\geq 4.

(a9)

Moreover,

\displaystyle b_{2}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{6}\left(1+3\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}^{2}+4\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}\right),

(a10)

\displaystyle b_{3}=

\displaystyle-\frac{a_{1}}{36}\left(7+15\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}\right),

(a11)

\displaystyle b_{4}=

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{3a_{2}}{10}-\frac{5}{36}+\frac{1}{4}\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}^{2}+\frac{1}{3}\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}\right)\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}-\frac{2a_{2}}{15}+\frac{2}{9}\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}-\frac{a_{1}^{2}}{16}.

(a12)

Next, the pressure could be expanded in terms of the energy density as,

\widehat{P}=\sum_{k=1}d_{k}\widehat{\varepsilon}^{k}\approx d_{1}\widehat{\varepsilon}+d_{2}\widehat{\varepsilon}^{2}+d_{3}\widehat{\varepsilon}^{3}+\cdots

(a13)

which could be rewritten as,

\displaystyle\widehat{P}\approx

\displaystyle d_{1}+d_{2}+d_{3}+\cdots+a_{1}(d_{1}+2d_{2}+3d_{3}+\cdots)\widehat{r}

\displaystyle+\left[(d_{2}+3d_{3}+\cdots)a_{1}^{2}+(d_{1}+2d_{2}+3d_{3}+\cdots)a_{2}\right]\widehat{r}^{2}

\displaystyle+\left[\left(a_{1}^{2}d_{3}+2a_{2}d_{2}+6a_{2}d_{3}+\cdots\right)a_{1}+\left(d_{1}+2d_{2}+3d_{3}+\cdots\right)a_{3}\right]\widehat{r}^{3}.

(a14)

The zeroth-order term gives the sum rule for
\{d_{k}\}
as,

\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}=d_{1}+d_{2}+d_{3}+\cdots=\sum_{k=1}d_{k}.

(a15)

Moreover, since
b_{1}=0
, we obtain

a_{1}(d_{1}+2d_{2}+3d_{3}+\cdots)=0.

(a16)

The speed of sound squared at the center gives another sum rule for
\{d_{k}\}
,

s_{\textrm{c}}^{2}=\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}\widehat{P}}{\mathrm{d}\widehat{\varepsilon}}\right|_{\widehat{r}=0\leftrightarrow\widehat{\varepsilon}_{\textrm{c}}=1}=d_{1}+2d_{2}+3d_{3}+\cdots=\sum_{k=1}kd_{k},

(a17)

which is generally nonzero, thus

a_{1}=0.

(a18)

Another argument is since
\widehat{P}
and
\widehat{\varepsilon}
are both even functions of
\widehat{r}
, only even terms of
\widehat{r}
survive, one then naturally has
b_{1}=0
and
a_{1}=0
.
According to the expression for
b_{3}\propto a_{1}
, one then obtains
b_{3}=0
(and immediately
a_{3}=0
could be obtained considering the coefficients in front of
\widehat{r}^{3}
of Eq. (
A. Perturbative Treatments of the Dimensionless TOV Equations
)).

The next-order term is
b_{4}
, i.e.,

b_{4}=\frac{\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}}{12}\left(1+3\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}^{2}+4\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}\right)-\frac{a_{2}}{30}\left(4+9\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}\right).

(a19)

Here the coefficient
a_{2}
could be obtained as follows: the speed of sound squared
s^{2}
is

s^{2}=\frac{\mathrm{d}\widehat{P}}{\mathrm{d}\widehat{\varepsilon}}=\frac{\mathrm{d}\widehat{P}}{\mathrm{d}\widehat{r}}\cdot\frac{\mathrm{d}\widehat{r}}{\mathrm{d}\widehat{\varepsilon}}=\frac{2b_{2}\widehat{r}+4b_{4}\widehat{r}^{3}+\cdots}{2a_{2}\widehat{r}+4a_{4}\widehat{r}^{3}+\cdots}=\frac{b_{2}+2b_{4}\widehat{r}^{2}+\cdots}{a_{2}+2a_{4}\widehat{r}^{2}+\cdots}.

(a20)

Taking it at the center gives
s_{\textrm{c}}^{2}=b_{2}/a_{2}
, see Eq. (
4
) for the expression of
s_{\textrm{c}}^{2}
(for the maximum-mass configuration).
Consequently,
a_{2}=b_{2}/s_{\textrm{c}}^{2}
.
The
b_{6}
coefficient is given by,

b_{6}=-\frac{1}{216}\left(1+9\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}^{2}\right)\left(1+3\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}^{2}+4\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}\right)-\frac{a_{2}^{2}}{30}+\left(\frac{2}{15}\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}^{2}+\frac{1}{45}\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}-\frac{1}{54}\right)a_{2}-\frac{5+12\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}}{63}a_{4},

(a21)

here besides the coupling to
a_{2}
, it also couples to
a_{4}
(which has no simple relation of
\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}
as
a_{2}
does).

Correction
Y
as a function of
\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}

b_{2}
, then only the combination
\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}
comes into play since
b_{2}=-6^{-1}(1+3\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}^{2}+4\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}})

B. Derivations of the Core Speed of Sound
s_{\textrm{c}}^{2}

Using the radius scaling,

\widehat{R}\sim\left(\frac{\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}}{1+3\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}^{2}+4\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}}\right)^{1/2},

(a25)

we can write the NS mass
M_{\textrm{NS}}
in the heuristic form
M_{\textrm{NS}}\sim\widehat{R}^{3}/\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}}}\sim F(\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}})
as,

F(\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}})=\frac{\Phi}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}}}}\left(\frac{\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}}{1+3\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}^{2}+4\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}}\right)^{3/2},

(a26)

where
\Phi
is a positive coefficient.
Taking derivative of
F
with respect to
\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}}
gives,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}F}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}}}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{F(\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}})}{\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}}}\left[3\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}}}{P_{\textrm{c}}}\frac{\mathrm{d}{P}_{\textrm{c}}}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}}}-1\right)\frac{1-3\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}^{2}}{1+3\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}^{2}+4\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}}-1\right],

(a27)

where one uses the expression for the derivative of
\widehat{R}
with respect to
\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}
,

\frac{\mathrm{d}\widehat{R}}{\mathrm{d}\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}}=\widehat{R}\left(\frac{1-3\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}^{2}}{2\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}+8\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}^{2}+6\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}^{3}}\right),

(a28)

and the basic relation,

\frac{\mathrm{d}\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}}}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}}}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}{P}_{\textrm{c}}}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}}}-\frac{P_{\textrm{c}}}{\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}}}\right).

(a29)

Making
\mathrm{d}F/\mathrm{d}\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}}=0
gives the speed of sound
s_{\textrm{c}}^{2}\equiv\mathrm{d}P_{\textrm{c}}/\mathrm{d}\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}}
at the center of a NS of Eq. (
4
) in the main text.
After obtaining the
s_{\textrm{c}}^{2}
, we have,

\frac{\partial R}{\partial\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}}}\sim\frac{\partial}{\partial\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}}}\left(\frac{\widehat{R}}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}}}}\right)=\left(\frac{s_{\textrm{c}}^{2}}{\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}}-1\right)\frac{1-3\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}^{2}}{1+3\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}^{2}+4\widehat{P}_{\textrm{c}}}-1=-\frac{2}{3},

(a30)

i.e., as
\varepsilon_{\textrm{c}}
increases, the radius
R
decreases, as expected.

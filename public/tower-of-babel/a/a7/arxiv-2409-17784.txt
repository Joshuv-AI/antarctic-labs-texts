Two problems in the representation theory of reduced enveloping algebras
Matthew Westaway

Abstract
. In this paper we consider two problems relating to the representation theory of Lie algebras 𝔤 {\mathfrak{g}} of reductive algebraic groups G G over algebraically closed fields 𝕂 {\mathbb{K}} of positive characteristic p > 0 p>0 . First, we consider the tensor product of two baby Verma modules Z χ ​ ( λ ) ⊗ Z χ ′ ​ ( μ ) Z_{\chi}(\lambda)\otimes Z_{\chi^{\prime}}(\mu) and show that it has a filtration of baby Verma modules of a particular form. Secondly, we consider the minimal-dimension representations of a reduced enveloping algebra U χ ​ ( 𝔤 ) U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}}) for a nilpotent χ ∈ 𝔤 ∗ \chi\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*} . We show that under certain assumptions in type A A we can obtain the minimal-dimensional modules as quotients of certain modules obtained by base change from simple highest weight modules over ℂ {\mathbb{C}} .

1.
Introduction

This paper tackles two questions relating to the representation theory of Lie algebras of reductive algebraic groups in positive characteristic. In this introduction we begin by setting up some general notation, before discussing the two questions in more detail.

Let
G
be a reductive algebraic group over an algebraically closed field
{\mathbb{K}}
of characteristic
p>0
satisfying the standard hypotheses (see
[
Ja2
, §6.3–6.4]
), and let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be its Lie algebra. We pick a maximal torus
T
of
G
and a Borel subgroup
B
of
G
containing
T
, and denote their Lie algebras by
{\mathfrak{t}}
and
{\mathfrak{b}}
respectively. We then denote by
X(T)
the character group of
T
, by
\Phi\subseteq X(T)
the corresponding root system, and by
\Phi^{+}
and
\Pi
the sets of positive and simple roots in
\Phi
corresponding to
B
. Given
\alpha\in\Phi
, we write
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha}
for the associated root space in
{\mathfrak{g}}
and we write
{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha}
and
{\mathfrak{n}}^{-}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}{\mathfrak{g}}_{-\alpha}
.

Given
\chi\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
we may define the reduced enveloping algebra
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})
; since every simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-module is a simple
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})
-module for some
\chi\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
, we may reduce many questions about
{\mathfrak{g}}
-modules to questions about
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})
-modules. Assume now that
\chi({\mathfrak{n}}^{+})=0
(which we are usually permitted to do without loss of generality) and denote

\Lambda_{\chi}=\{\lambda\in{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}\mid\lambda(h)^{p}-\lambda(h^{[p]})=\chi(h)^{p}\mbox{ for all }h\in{\mathfrak{t}}\}

(here,
x\mapsto x^{[p]}
denotes the restricted structure on
{\mathfrak{g}}
and
{\mathfrak{t}}
). Given
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\chi}
we may then define the baby Verma module
Z_{\chi}(\lambda)=U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes_{U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{b}})}{\mathbb{K}}_{\lambda}
, where
{\mathbb{K}}_{\lambda}
is the one-dimensional
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{b}})
-module on which
{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}
acts as zero and
{\mathfrak{t}}
acts via
\lambda
. Each simple
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})
-module is then a quotient of a baby Verma module.

In the important case when
\chi
is in standard Levi form (which we don’t define in this introduction, but can be found in Subsection
2.1
), each baby Verma module
Z_{\chi}(\lambda)
in fact has a
unique
simple quotient, which we denote
L_{\chi}(\lambda)
. Since the baby Verma modules are finite-dimensional they all have composition series; one broad question in this area is then to determine the composition multiplicities

[Z_{\chi}(\lambda):L_{\chi}(\mu)]

for
\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda_{\chi}
. More generally, we wish to understand the structure of the simple
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})
-modules.

Let us turn now to the first problem we tackle. Given
\chi,\chi^{\prime}\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
, we may take the tensor product of a
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})
-module
M
and a
U_{\chi^{\prime}}({\mathfrak{g}})
-module
N
to get a
U_{\chi+\chi^{\prime}}({\mathfrak{g}})
-module
M\otimes N
. In particular, given
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\chi}
and
\mu\in\Lambda_{\chi^{\prime}}
we may form the
U_{\chi+\chi^{\prime}}({\mathfrak{g}})
-module

Z_{\chi}(\lambda)\otimes Z_{\chi^{\prime}}(\mu).

The first goal of this paper is then to understand a little about the structure of this module. The main result (Theorem
2.6
) we prove is the following; in this statement, we enumerate
\Phi^{+}=\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{D}\}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\chi,\chi^{\prime}\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
with
\chi({\mathfrak{n}}^{+})=\chi^{\prime}({\mathfrak{n}}^{+})=0
, and let
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\chi}
and
\mu\in\Lambda_{\chi^{\prime}}
. Then
Z_{\chi}(\lambda)\otimes Z_{\chi^{\prime}}(\mu)
has a
U_{\chi+\chi^{\prime}}({\mathfrak{g}})
-module filtration in which the successive quotients are precisely the modules

Z_{\chi+\chi^{\prime}}(\lambda+\mu-b_{D}\gamma_{D}-\cdots-b_{1}\gamma_{1})\quad\mbox{for}\quad 0\leq b_{1},\ldots,b_{D}<p.

Each such module appears precisely once in the filtration for each tuple
(b_{1},\ldots,b_{D})\in[0,p)^{D}
.

Before moving on to the second topic of this paper, let us discuss a little bit of the motivation for this question. Firstly, we note that in the case where
\chi
is regular nilpotent and
\chi^{\prime}=-\chi
the module
Z_{\chi}(\lambda)\otimes Z_{-\chi}(\mu)
was studied briefly by Bezrukavnikov and Riche in
[
BR
, Proposition 4.4]
, where it played in a key role in their proof that the affine Hecke category acts on the principal block of
\operatorname{Rep}(G)
when
p
is greater than the Coxeter number. We also note that analogous questions regarding composition multiplicities in tensor products of modules have been studied in a great many settings, and it is sensible to explore these questions for reduced enveloping algebras as well. For example, the tensor product of two finite-dimensional simple
\mbox{\rm GL}_{N}({\mathbb{C}})
-modules (equivalently, finite-dimensional simple
{\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{N}({\mathbb{C}})
-modules) decomposes into simple constituents according to the Littlewood-Richardson rule. For a general symmetrisable Kac-Moody algebra over
{\mathbb{C}}
(for example, a complex semisimple Lie algebra), this was generalised by Littelmann through the use of Littelmann paths
[
Li1
,
Li2
]
. The tensor product of certain polynomial
\mbox{\rm GL}_{N}({\mathbb{K}})
-modules has also been studied by Brundan and Kleshchev in
[
BK1
,
BK2
]
.

Let us turn now to the second subject of this paper. Note that the reductive algebraic group
G
acts on
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
via the coadjoint action; in particular, we may consider coadjoint
G
-orbits in
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
. One of the first major conjectures in the representation theory of Lie algebras in positive characteristic was due to Kac and Weisfeiler in
[
KW
]
, and it posited that

p^{\dim(G\cdot\chi)/2}\mid\dim M

for all
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})
-modules
M
. This was proved by Premet in
[
Pr1
]
. A related conjecture was made by Humphreys in
[
Hu2
]
(see also Kac’s comment in
[
Ka
]
), which states that there always exists a
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})
-module with dimension exactly
p^{\dim(G\cdot\chi)/2}
. This is proved under the standard hypotheses by Premet and Topley in
[
PT
]
(although can be deduced more easily in type
A
using parabolic induction). Combining these two results yields the fact that the minimal dimension of a
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})
-module is
p^{\dim(G\cdot\chi)/2}
; we therefore refer to
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})
-modules of this dimension as
minimal-dimensional modules
.

Many questions remain open about the minimal-dimensional
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})
-modules. For
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{N}({\mathbb{K}})
a classification of such modules was obtained in
[
GT
]
(which we make significant use of in this paper), but a classification in other types remains open. The conjecture regarding minimal-dimensional modules that we focus on was made in
[
PT
, Remark 5.9]
, but requires a little bit of set-up. We go through this in more detail in Section
3
, so we only give the basic idea here.

Suppose that
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}}
is a reductive Lie algebra over
{\mathbb{C}}
with suitable (Chevalley) basis
{\mathcal{B}}
and let
R
be a localisation of
{\mathbb{Z}}
at a finite number of primes such that
p
is not invertible in
R
. We then set
{\mathfrak{g}}_{R}
to be the
R
-span of
{\mathcal{B}}
and define the
{\mathbb{K}}
-Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{K}}={\mathfrak{g}}_{R}\otimes_{R}{\mathbb{K}}
(that this is a Lie algebra obviously requires that our
{\mathcal{B}}
be chosen appropriately). We now assume that
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
are chosen so that
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{K}}={\mathfrak{g}}
. Given
\lambda:{\mathfrak{t}}_{R}\to R
(where
{\mathfrak{t}}_{R}={\mathfrak{t}}_{\mathbb{C}}\cap{\mathfrak{g}}_{R}
for a Cartan subalgebra
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\mathbb{C}}
of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}}
) we may form the Verma module
M_{\mathbb{C}}(\lambda)
and its simple quotient
L_{\mathbb{C}}(\lambda)
. These are modules for the universal enveloping algebra
U({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}})
, and thus for its
R
-form
U({\mathfrak{g}}_{R})
. Letting
\overline{v}_{\lambda}
be the natural
generator of
L_{\mathbb{C}}(\lambda)
, we define
L_{R}(\lambda)=U({\mathfrak{g}}_{R})\overline{v}_{\lambda}
and
L_{p}(\lambda)=L_{R}(\lambda)\otimes_{R}{\mathbb{K}}
. This is a
U({\mathfrak{g}})
-module. Given
\chi\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
, we can define the ideal
J_{\chi}
of
U({\mathfrak{g}})
generated by the elements
x^{p}-x^{[p]}-\chi(x)^{p}
for
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
. Then
L_{p}^{\chi}(\lambda):=L_{p}(\lambda)/J_{\chi}L_{p}(\lambda)
is a (possibly trivial)
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})
-module.

A weak version of the conjecture of
[

]

-orbits in

(where

is defined to be a complex reductive algebraic group such that

{\mathbb{O}}_{\mathbb{C}}
in
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
we may choose a representative

which maps

into
R
and which has the property that
\overline{\chi}:=\chi\otimes 1\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
lies in the corresponding nilpotent orbit

in
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
. We recall furthermore that a primitive ideal
I
in

is called

if
U({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}})/I
is a domain, and that the associated variety
{{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{A}}}^{\prime}(I)
of

is a subvariety of

(defined in Subsection

Jo1

.

Let
R={\mathbb{Z}}[1/q\mid q\mbox{ is a bad prime for }\Phi]

such that
\operatorname{Ann}_{U({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}})}(L_{\mathbb{C}}(\lambda))

2.
Tensor products of baby Verma modules

In this section, we discuss the structure of the tensor product of two baby Verma modules. Subsection
2.1
begins by setting up some conventions and notation for this section. Subsection
2.2
then considers the structure of baby Verma modules over a particular subalgebra of
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})
, which is then applied in Subsection
2.3
to determine a filtration of the tensor product of baby Verma modules. Finally, we conclude in Subsection
2.4
by considering the graded version of these results (in the sense of
[
Ja2
]
).

2.1.
Preliminaries

Let
G
be a reductive algebraic group over an algebraically closed field
{\mathbb{K}}
of characteristic
p>0
, and let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be its Lie algebra. Assume that
G
satisfies the standard hypotheses, i.e. that (A) the derived subgroup of
G
is simply connected, (B) the prime
p
is good for
G
, and (C) there exists a non-degenerate
G
-invariant symmetric bilinear form on
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Let
T
be a maximal torus of
G
and let
B
be a Borel subgroup of
G
containing
T
, with corresponding Lie algebras
{\mathfrak{t}}
and
{\mathfrak{b}}
. We set
X(T)
to be the character group of
T
. Denote by
\Phi\subseteq X(T)
the root system of
G
corresponding to
T
, by
\Phi^{+}
the set of positive roots corresponding to
B
, and by
\Pi
the associated set of simple roots. Each root
\alpha\in\Phi
is a homomorphism of algebraic groups
T\to{\mathbb{K}}^{*}
, and it differentiates to a homomorphism of Lie algebras
{\mathfrak{t}}\to{\mathbb{K}}
which we also denote by
\alpha
. For each root
\alpha\in\Phi
, we denote by
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha}
the corresponding root space in
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Define
{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha}
and
{\mathfrak{n}}^{-}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}{\mathfrak{g}}_{-\alpha}
. We thus have
{\mathfrak{b}}={\mathfrak{t}}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}
and
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{n}}^{-}\oplus{\mathfrak{t}}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}
. For each
\alpha\in\Phi
we fix a root vector
e_{\alpha}\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha}
, and we fix a basis
h_{1}\ldots,h_{d}
of
{\mathfrak{t}}
; we assume that these are chosen in such a way as to satisfy the Chevalley basis relations (adapted to the reductive case). We also define
h_{\alpha}=[e_{\alpha},e_{-\alpha}]\in{\mathfrak{t}}
for
\alpha\in\Phi
.

Enumerate
\Pi=\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d}\}
. Given
\gamma\in\Phi
, there exist
a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
(either all positive or all negative) such that

\gamma=a_{1}\alpha_{1}+\cdots+a_{d}\alpha_{d}.

We then set

{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(\gamma)=a_{1}+\cdots+a_{d};

clearly
\gamma\in\Phi^{+}
if and only if
{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(\gamma)>0
. We label the positive roots
\Phi^{+}=\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{D}\}
such that
r<l
implies
{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(\gamma_{r})\leq{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(\gamma_{l})
. Set
\rho=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{D}\gamma_{i}
; this naturally lies in
X(T)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{Q}}
but under our assumptions it in fact lies in
X(T)
itself.

Let
Y(T)
be the cocharacter group of
T
and let
\langle-,-\rangle:X(T)\times Y(T)\to{\mathbb{Z}}
be the perfect pairing such that
(\lambda\circ\sigma)(t)=t^{\langle\lambda,\sigma\rangle}
for all
\lambda\in X(T)
,
\sigma\in Y(T)
and
t\in{\mathbb{K}}^{*}
. The coroot of a root
\alpha\in\Phi
is denoted
\alpha^{\vee}
and we write
\Phi^{\vee}=\{\alpha^{\vee}\mid\alpha\in\Phi\}\subseteq Y(T)
. Given
\alpha\in\Phi
we define
s_{\alpha}:X(T)\to X(T)
by
s_{\alpha}(\lambda)=\lambda-\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle\alpha
and
t_{\alpha}:X(T)\to X(T)
by
t_{\alpha}(\lambda)=\lambda+p\alpha
. The Weyl group
W
is then defined to be the subgroup of
\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{Z}}(X(T))
generated by all
s_{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in\Phi
and the affine Weyl group
W_{p}
is defined to be the subgroup of
\operatorname{Aff}_{\mathbb{Z}}(X(T))
generated by the elements
s_{\alpha}
and
t_{\beta}
for
\alpha,\beta\in\Phi
. Both
W
and
W_{p}
act on
X(T)
by construction; we also define a dot-action of
W
and
W_{p}
on
X(T)
by
w\cdot\lambda=w(\lambda+\rho)-\rho
.

The Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
is equipped with a natural restricted structure
{\mathfrak{g}}\to{\mathfrak{g}}
written as
x\mapsto x^{[p]}
. The universal enveloping algebra
U({\mathfrak{g}})
has a large central subalgebra generated by the elements
x^{p}-x^{[p]}
for
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, which is called the
p
-centre and is denoted
Z_{p}({\mathfrak{g}})
. Given
\chi\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
, we set the
reduced enveloping algebra

U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})
to be the central quotient

U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})=\frac{U({\mathfrak{g}})}{\langle x^{p}-x^{[p]}-\chi(x)^{p}\mid x\in{\mathfrak{g}}\rangle}.

It is classical that every simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-module is a
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})
-module for some
\chi\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
. The restricted structure on
{\mathfrak{g}}
restricts to restricted structures on
{\mathfrak{b}}
,
{\mathfrak{n}}^{-}
and
{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}
; we may therefore also define reduced enveloping algebras
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{b}})
,
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{n}}^{-})
and
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{n}}^{+})
. Each of these is a subalgebra of
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})
, and
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})
is free as a module over each of them by the PBW theorem.

Denote

\Lambda_{\chi}:=\{\lambda\in{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}\mid\lambda(h)^{p}-\lambda(h^{[p]})=\chi(h)^{p}\mbox{ for all }h\in{\mathfrak{t}}\}.

For any
\lambda\in{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
we may define a one-dimensional
{\mathfrak{b}}
-module
{\mathbb{K}}_{\lambda}
on which
{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}
acts as zero and
{\mathfrak{t}}
acts via
\lambda
. Assuming that
\chi({\mathfrak{n}}^{+})=0
, this
{\mathfrak{b}}
-module extends to a
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{b}})
-module if and only if
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\chi}
. Given such
\lambda
, we may then define the
baby Verma module
corresponding to
\lambda
as
Z_{\chi}(\lambda):=U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes_{U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{b}})}{\mathbb{K}}_{\lambda}
. This is a
p^{D}
-dimensional
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})
-module, and every simple
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})
-module arises as a quotient of a baby Verma module.

We often assume
\chi({\mathfrak{b}})=0
, in which case

\Lambda_{\chi}=\Lambda_{0}=X(T)/pX(T).

At times, we make the further assumption that
\chi
has (weak) standard Levi form, i.e. that there exists
I\subseteq\Pi
such that
\chi({\mathfrak{b}})=0
and

\chi(e_{-\alpha})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}=0&\mbox{if }\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\setminus I,\\
\neq 0&\mbox{if }\alpha\in I.\end{array}\right.

(We omit the “weak” if
\chi(e_{-\alpha})=1
for all
\alpha\in I
, though such property will not have a meaningful effect on the representation theory.) When
\chi
has (weak) standard Levi form, each baby Verma module
Z_{\chi}(\lambda)
has a unique simple quotient, which we denote by
L_{\chi}(\lambda)
. As already observed, each simple
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})
-module is of the form
L_{\chi}(\lambda)
for some
\lambda\in\Lambda_{0}
.

Define by
W_{I}
the subgroup of
W
generated by those
s_{\alpha}
with
\alpha\in\Phi\cap{\mathbb{Z}}I
and by
W_{I,p}
the subgroup of
W_{p}
generated by those
s_{\alpha}
and
t_{\beta}
with
\alpha,\beta\in\Phi\cap{\mathbb{Z}}I

2.2.
Baby Verma modules as
U_{0}({\mathfrak{n}}^{+})
-modules

Assume that
\chi({\mathfrak{n}}^{+})=0
. The baby Verma modules
Z_{\chi}(\lambda)
, for
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\chi}
, are
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})
-modules and thus may be restricted to
U_{0}({\mathfrak{n}}^{+})
-modules. In this subsection, we explore the structure of baby Verma modules when viewed as
U_{0}({\mathfrak{n}}^{+})
-modules in this way.

Recall that
Z_{\chi}(\lambda)
has a basis consisting of elements

e_{-\gamma_{D}}^{a_{D}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda}\qquad\mbox{for}\qquad 0\leq a_{1},\ldots,a_{D}<p,

where
z_{\lambda}:=1\otimes 1\in Z_{\chi}(\lambda)
. We call this the “monomial basis” of
Z_{\chi}(\lambda)
, and we define

{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(e_{-\gamma_{D}}^{a_{D}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda})=-\sum_{i=1}^{D}a_{i}{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(\gamma_{i}).

In particular, we have
{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(z_{\lambda})=0
. For ease of notation, set
V=Z_{\chi}(\lambda)
. For
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we define

V_{\geq-m}={\mathbb{K}}\mbox{--span}\{e_{-\gamma_{D}}^{a_{D}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda}\mid{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(e_{-\gamma_{D}}^{a_{D}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda})\geq-m\}\subseteq V.

Lemma 2.1
.

Fix
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Let
\gamma_{r}\in\Phi^{+}
and let
e_{-\gamma_{D}}^{a_{D}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda}\in V_{\geq-m}
. Then

e_{-\gamma_{r}}e_{-\gamma_{D}}^{a_{D}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda}\in V_{\geq-m-\tiny{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(\gamma_{r})}.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
m
.

The base case of
m=0
is straightforward: since
V_{\geq 0}={\mathbb{K}}z_{\lambda}
, the result follows from the definitional fact that
e_{-\gamma_{r}}v_{\lambda}\in V_{\geq-\tiny{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(\gamma_{r})}
.

For the induction step, let us assume that the result holds for all
k\leq m
. We prove the inductive step by reverse induction on
r
. The base case is
r=D
, in which case we have for
e_{-\gamma_{D}}^{a_{D}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda}\in V_{\geq-(m+1)}
that

e_{\gamma_{r}}e_{-\gamma_{D}}^{a_{D}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}e_{-\gamma_{D}}^{a_{D}+1}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda}&\mbox{if }a_{D}<p-1,\\
\chi(e_{-\gamma_{D}})^{p}e_{-\gamma_{D-1}}^{a_{D-1}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda}&\mbox{if }a_{D}={p-1}.\end{array}\right.

In the former of these cases, we have
{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(e_{-\gamma_{D}}^{a_{D}+1}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda})={{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(e_{-\gamma_{D}}^{a_{D}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda})-{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(\gamma_{D})\geq-(m+1)-{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(\gamma_{r})
. In the latter, either
\chi(e_{-\gamma_{D}})=0
– in which case the result is trivial – or
\chi(e_{-\gamma_{D}})\neq 0
and
{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(e_{-\gamma_{D-1}}^{a_{D-1}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda})={{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(e_{-\gamma_{D}}^{a_{D}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda})+(p-1){{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(\gamma_{D})\geq-(m+1)+(p-1){{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(\gamma_{D})\geq-(m+1)-{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(\gamma_{r})
. Either way, the base case for the induction on
r
thus holds.

For the inductive step, suppose that
e_{-\gamma_{i}}V_{\geq-(m+1)}\subseteq V_{-(m+1)-\tiny{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(\gamma_{i})}
for all
i>r
. Let
e_{-\gamma_{D}}^{a_{D}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda}\in V_{\geq-(m+1)}
;
i.e., let
{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(e_{-\gamma_{D}}^{a_{D}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda})\geq-(m+1)
. If
{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(e_{-\gamma_{D}}^{a_{D}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda})\geq-m
then the result follows by the inductive assumption; we thus assume
{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(e_{-\gamma_{D}}^{a_{D}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda})=-(m+1)
.

We need to show
e_{-\gamma_{r}}e_{-\gamma_{D}}^{a_{D}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda}\in V_{\geq-(m+1)-\tiny{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(\gamma_{r})}
. Let
l
be maximal such that
a_{l}\neq 0
, noting that
l\geq 1
.

This follows since
{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(e_{-\gamma_{D}}^{a_{D}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda})=-(m+1)<0
.
There are three cases to consider.

Case 1:

r>l
.

We have
{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(e_{-\gamma_{r}}e_{-\gamma_{l}}^{a_{l}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda})=-(m+1)-{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(\gamma_{r})
by definition. The result thus holds in this case.

Case 2:

r=l
.

In this case we have

e_{-\gamma_{r}}e_{-\gamma_{D}}^{a_{D}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}e_{-\gamma_{l}}^{a_{l}+1}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda}&\mbox{if }a_{l}<p-1,\\
\chi(e_{-\gamma_{l}})^{p}e_{-\gamma_{l-1}}^{a_{l-1}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda}&\mbox{if }a_{l}=p-1.\end{array}\right.

As in the base case for the induction on
r
, we get
e_{-\gamma_{l}}e_{-\gamma_{D}}^{a_{D}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda}\in V_{\geq-(m+1)-\tiny{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(\gamma_{l})}

Case 3:

r<l

Case 3(a):

\gamma_{l}+\gamma_{r}\notin\Phi^{+}

In this case we have
[e_{-\gamma_{r}},e_{-\gamma_{l}}]=0

e_{-\gamma_{r}}e_{-\gamma_{l}}^{a_{l}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda}=e_{-\gamma_{l}}e_{-\gamma_{r}}e_{-\gamma_{l}}^{a_{l}-1}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda}.

Note that
e_{-\gamma_{l}}^{a_{l}-1}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda}\in V_{\geq-(m+1)+\tiny{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(\gamma_{l})}

by the inductive assumption on
r
, as required.

Case 3(b):

\gamma_{l}+\gamma_{r}\in\Phi^{+}

In this case, there exists

e_{-\gamma_{r}}e_{-\gamma_{l}}^{a_{l}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda}=e_{-\gamma_{l}}e_{-\gamma_{r}}e_{-\gamma_{l}}^{a_{l}-1}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda}+C_{r,l}e_{-\gamma_{l}-\gamma_{r}}e_{-\gamma_{l}}^{a_{l}-1}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda}.

The first part of this sum lies in

{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(\gamma_{l}+\gamma_{r})={{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(\gamma_{l})+{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(\gamma_{r})

{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(e_{-\gamma_{l}-\gamma_{r}}e_{-\gamma_{l}}^{a_{l}-1}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda})=-{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(\gamma_{l})-{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(\gamma_{r})+{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(e_{-\gamma_{l}}^{a_{l}-1}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda})=-(m+1)-{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(\gamma_{r})
, as required.

The inductive step therefore holds for the induction on
r
, and thus also for the induction on

The associative algebra
U_{0}({\mathfrak{n}}^{+})
is a Hopf algebra; the augmentation ideal of
U_{0}({\mathfrak{n}}^{+})
is then defined to be the kernel of the counit
\varepsilon
. We denote it by
I^{+}
; note that it is precisely the ideal generated by the elements
e_{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then
V_{\geq-m}
is a
U_{0}({\mathfrak{n}}^{+})
-module and the
U_{0}({\mathfrak{n}}^{+})
-action on
V_{\geq-m}/V_{\geq-m+1}
is trivial. In other words,
I^{+}V_{\geq-m}\subseteq V_{\geq-m+1}
.

Proof.

Under our assumptions,
I^{+}
is generated by the root vectors
e_{\alpha_{i}}
for
i=1,\ldots,d
. It is therefore sufficient to show that
e_{\alpha_{i}}V_{\geq-m}\subseteq V_{\geq-m+1}
for all
i=1,\ldots,d
.

We proceed by induction on
m
. For the base case of
m=0
, we note that
V_{\geq 0}={\mathbb{K}}z_{\lambda}
. This means in particular that
e_{\alpha_{i}}z_{\lambda}=0
for all
i=1,\ldots,d
, and thus we have
e_{\alpha_{i}}V_{\geq 0}\subseteq V_{\geq 1}=\{0\}
.

For the inductive step, suppose that the result holds for all
k\leq m
. We must prove that
e_{\alpha_{i}}e_{-\gamma_{D}}^{a_{D}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda}\in V_{\geq-m}
whenever
{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(e_{-\gamma_{D}}^{a_{D}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda})\geq-(m+1)
. The result follows from the inductive assumption if
{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(e_{-\gamma_{D}}^{a_{D}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda})\geq-m
; we therefore assume that
{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(e_{-\gamma_{D}}^{a_{D}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda})=-(m+1)
.

Let
l
be maximal such that
a_{l}\neq 0
(so
l\geq 1
). There are three cases to consider.

Case 1:

\gamma_{l}-\alpha_{i}\notin\Phi^{+}\cup\{0\}
.

In this case we have
[e_{\alpha_{i}},e_{-\gamma_{l}}]=0
, and so

e_{\alpha_{i}}e_{-\gamma_{l}}^{a_{l}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda}=e_{-\gamma_{l}}e_{\alpha_{i}}e_{-\gamma_{l}}^{a_{l}-1}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda}.

Since
{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(e_{-\gamma_{l}}^{a_{l}-1}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda})=-(m+1)+{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(\gamma_{l})\geq-m
, by induction we have that

e_{\alpha_{i}}e_{-\gamma_{l}}^{a_{l}-1}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda}\in V_{\geq-(m+1)+\tiny{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(\gamma_{l})+\tiny{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(\alpha_{i})}=V_{\geq-m+\tiny{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(\gamma_{l})}.

By Lemma
2.1
we thus have
e_{-\gamma_{l}}e_{\alpha_{i}}e_{-\gamma_{l}}^{a_{l}-1}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda}\in V_{\geq-m+\tiny{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(\gamma_{l})-\tiny{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(\gamma_{l})}=V_{\geq-m},
as required.

Case 2:

\gamma_{l}=\alpha_{i}
.

In this setting, we have
[e_{\alpha_{i}},e_{-\gamma_{l}}]=h_{\alpha_{i}}=:h_{i}
, and therefore

\begin{split}e_{\alpha_{i}}e_{-\gamma_{l}}^{a_{l}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda}=&e_{-\gamma_{l}}e_{\alpha_{i}}e_{-\gamma_{l}}^{a_{l}-1}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda}+h_{i}e_{-\gamma_{l}}^{a_{l}-1}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda}\\
=&e_{-\gamma_{l}}e_{\alpha_{i}}e_{-\gamma_{l}}^{a_{l}-1}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda}\\
&+(\lambda-(a_{l}-1)\gamma_{l}-\cdots-a_{1}\gamma_{1})(h_{i})e_{-\gamma_{l}}^{a_{l}-1}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda}.\end{split}

The former summand lies in
V_{\geq-m}
as in Case 1, while the latter summand is either zero or has height
{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(e_{-\gamma_{l}}^{a_{l}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{2}}^{a_{2}}e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda})+{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(\gamma_{l})=-(m+1)+{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(\gamma_{l})\geq-m
as required.

Case 3:

\gamma_{l}-\alpha_{i}\in\Phi^{+}
.

In this case, there exists
C_{l,i}\in{\mathbb{K}}
such that
[e_{\alpha_{i}},e_{-\gamma_{l}}]=C_{l,i}e_{-\gamma_{l}+\alpha_{i}}
. We then have

e_{\alpha_{i}}e_{-\gamma_{l}}^{a_{l}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda}=e_{-\gamma_{l}}e_{\alpha_{i}}e_{-\gamma_{l}}^{a_{l}-1}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda}+C_{l,i}e_{-\gamma_{l}+\alpha_{i}}e_{-\gamma_{l}}^{a_{l}-1}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda}.

As in Case 1, the former of the terms in this sum lies in
V_{\geq-m}
. For the latter, note that
{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(e_{-\gamma_{l}}^{a_{l}-1}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda})=-(m+1)+{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(\gamma_{l})
; by Lemma
2.1
we therefore have

e_{-\gamma_{l}+\alpha_{i}}e_{-\gamma_{l}}^{a_{l}-1}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda}\in V_{\geq-(m+1)+\tiny{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(\gamma_{l})-\tiny{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(\gamma_{l}-\alpha_{i})}=V_{\geq-m}.

This proves the induction step and thus the result.

{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}

By Proposition
2.2
,
Z_{\chi}(\lambda)
has a
U_{0}({\mathfrak{n}}^{+})

(2.1)

such that

2.3.
Tensor products

Since
U({\mathfrak{g}})
is a Hopf algebra, the tensor product
M\otimes N
of two
U({\mathfrak{g}})
-modules
M
and
N
can be given the structure of a
U({\mathfrak{g}})
-module. Specifically, given
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
,
m\in M
and
n\in N
, we have

x\cdot(m\otimes n)=(x\cdot m)\otimes n+m\otimes(x\cdot n).

It is well-known (and straightforward to check) that when
M
is a
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}})
-module and
N
is a
U_{\chi^{\prime}}({\mathfrak{g}})
-module (for
\chi,\chi^{\prime}\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
) the resulting tensor product
M\otimes N
is in fact a
U_{\chi+\chi^{\prime}}(g)
-module.

Fixing
\chi,\chi^{\prime}\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
with
\chi({\mathfrak{n}}^{+})=\chi^{\prime}({\mathfrak{n}}^{+})=0
, and letting
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\chi}
and
\mu\in\Lambda_{\chi^{\prime}}
, we may therefore form the
U_{\chi+\chi^{\prime}}({\mathfrak{g}})
-module
Z_{\chi}(\lambda)\otimes Z_{\chi^{\prime}}(\mu)
. Since we are now considering two baby Verma modules, we modify our notation from above a little bit in order to differentiate them. Specifically, we still set
z_{\lambda}
to be the generator
1\otimes 1
of
Z_{\chi}(\lambda)
, but we now denote by
u_{\mu}
the generator
1\otimes 1
of
Z_{\chi^{\prime}}(\mu)
. The module
Z_{\chi}(\lambda)\otimes Z_{\chi^{\prime}}(\mu)
then naturally has a basis consisting of the elements

(e_{-\gamma_{D}}^{a_{D}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda})\otimes(e_{-\gamma_{D}}^{b_{D}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{b_{1}}u_{\mu})\quad\mbox{for}\quad 0\leq a_{1},\ldots,a_{D},b_{1},\ldots,b_{D}<p.

(2.2)

It will be useful, however, to work with a slightly different basis of
Z_{\chi}(\lambda)\otimes Z_{\chi^{\prime}}(\mu)
.

Lemma 2.4
.

The
U_{\chi+\chi^{\prime}}({\mathfrak{g}})
-module
Z_{\chi}(\lambda)\otimes Z_{\chi^{\prime}}(\mu)
has a basis consisting of the elements

e_{-\gamma_{D}}^{c_{D}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{c_{1}}(z_{\lambda}\otimes e_{-\gamma_{D}}^{d_{D}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{d_{1}}u_{\mu})\quad\mbox{for}\quad 0\leq c_{1},\ldots,c_{D},d_{1},\ldots,d_{D}<p.

(2.3)

Proof.

Since
Z_{\chi}(\lambda)\otimes Z_{\chi^{\prime}}(\mu)
has dimension
p^{2\dim{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}}
, it is enough to show that the elements described in (
2.3
) span
Z_{\chi}(\lambda)\otimes Z_{\chi^{\prime}}(\mu)
.
To do so, it is sufficient to show that each element in the basis described in (
2.2
) can be written as a linear combination of elements of the form described in (
2.3
). Using the notation from (
2.2
), we prove this by induction on
-{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(e_{-\gamma_{D}}^{a_{D}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda})

-{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(e_{-\gamma_{D}}^{a_{D}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda})=0
; suppose that it is true whenever
-{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(e_{-\gamma_{D}}^{a_{D}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda})<m
. If
-{{\mbox{\rm ht}}}(e_{-\gamma_{D}}^{a_{D}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda})=m
and
l
is maximal such that
a_{l}\neq 0
then

\begin{split}(e_{-\gamma_{l}}^{a_{l}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda})\otimes(e_{-\gamma_{D}}^{b_{D}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{b_{1}}u_{\mu})=&e_{-\gamma_{l}}((e_{-\gamma_{l}}^{a_{l}-1}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda})\otimes(e_{-\gamma_{D}}^{b_{D}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{b_{1}}u_{\mu}))\\
&-(e_{-\gamma_{l}}^{a_{l}-1}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{a_{1}}z_{\lambda})\otimes(e_{-\gamma_{l}}e_{-\gamma_{D}}^{b_{D}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{b_{1}}u_{\mu}).\end{split}

By induction, and using the fact that
U_{\chi+\chi^{\prime}}({\mathfrak{n}}^{-})
and
U_{\chi^{\prime}}({\mathfrak{n}}^{-})
are algebras with bases consisting of the elements
e_{-\gamma_{D}}^{c_{D}}\cdots e_{-\gamma_{1}}^{c_{1}}
for
0\leq c_{i}<p
, each part of this sum can be rewritten in terms of elements of the form (
2.3
). This completes the induction step and thus proves the result.

∎

Since
{\mathbb{K}}z_{\lambda}
is a
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{b}})
-module and since we may restrict
Z_{\chi^{\prime}}(\mu)
to a
U_{\chi^{\prime}}({\mathfrak{b}})
-module, we may take the tensor product of these modules to form the
U_{\chi+\chi^{\prime}}({\mathfrak{b}})
-module
{\mathbb{K}}z_{\lambda}\otimes Z_{\chi^{\prime}}(\mu)

Furthermore, Frobenius reciprocity implies that for all
U_{\chi+\chi^{\prime}}({\mathfrak{g}})
-modules

\operatorname{Hom}_{U_{\chi+\chi^{\prime}}({\mathfrak{g}})}(V_{\chi,\chi^{\prime}}(\lambda,\mu),M)=\operatorname{Hom}_{U_{\chi+\chi^{\prime}}({\mathfrak{b}})}({\mathbb{K}}z_{\lambda}\otimes Z_{\chi^{\prime}}(\mu),M).

In particular,

\operatorname{Hom}_{U_{\chi+\chi^{\prime}}({\mathfrak{g}})}(V_{\chi,\chi^{\prime}}(\lambda,\mu),Z_{\chi}(\lambda)\otimes Z_{\chi^{\prime}}(\mu))=\operatorname{Hom}_{U_{\chi+\chi^{\prime}}({\mathfrak{b}})}({\mathbb{K}}z_{\lambda}\otimes Z_{\chi^{\prime}}(\mu),Z_{\chi}(\lambda)\otimes Z_{\chi^{\prime}}(\mu)).

The natural embedding
{\mathbb{K}}z_{\lambda}\otimes Z_{\chi^{\prime}}(\mu)\hookrightarrow Z_{\chi}(\lambda)\otimes Z_{\chi^{\prime}}(\mu)

for the image of

\alpha\in\Phi
, we continue to denote it by
\alpha
whether we view it as an element of
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
,
{\mathfrak{t}}_{R}^{*}
or
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\mathbb{K}}^{*}
.

X(T_{\mathbb{C}})

In the same way, we write

{\mathfrak{g}}_{R}^{*}

3.2.
Representation theory

We introduce here some of the representation-theoretic objects we use further below. The constructions here largely follow
[
Pr3
]
.

Given
\lambda\in{\mathfrak{t}}_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
, the Verma module
M_{\mathbb{C}}(\lambda)
corresponding to
\lambda
is the
U({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}})
-module defined as
M_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\lambda):=U({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}})\otimes_{U({\mathfrak{b}}_{\mathbb{C}})}{\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda}
, where
{\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda}
is the one-dimensional
U({\mathfrak{b}}_{\mathbb{C}})
-module on which
{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}_{\mathbb{C}}
acts trivially and
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\mathbb{C}}
acts via
\lambda
. This has a unique simple quotient
L_{\mathbb{C}}(\lambda)
, which is finite-dimensional if and only if
\lambda
is integral and dominant, i.e.
\lambda(h_{\alpha})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
for all
\alpha\in\Pi
. Let us denote
v_{\lambda}:=1\otimes 1\in M_{\mathbb{C}}(\lambda)
, a highest weight vector in
M_{\mathbb{C}}(\lambda)
, and let us denote by
\overline{v}_{\lambda}
the image of
v_{\lambda}
in
L_{\mathbb{C}}(\lambda)
. We further denote by
M^{\operatorname{max}}_{\mathbb{C}}(\lambda)
the unique maximal submodule of
M_{\mathbb{C}}(\lambda)
, so that
M_{\mathbb{C}}(\lambda)/M^{\operatorname{max}}_{\mathbb{C}}(\lambda)=L_{\mathbb{C}}(\lambda)
.

The universal enveloping algebra
U({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}})
has a Poincaré-Birkhoff-Witt
{\mathbb{C}}
-basis consisting of ordered monomials in
{\mathcal{B}}
(with respect to the chosen total order on
{\mathcal{B}}
). Note further that
{\mathfrak{g}}_{R}
is a Lie ring and has a universal enveloping
R
-algebra
U({\mathfrak{g}}_{R})
. Moreover,
U({\mathfrak{g}}_{R})
is a free
R
-algebra which also has an
R
-basis of ordered monomials in
{\mathcal{B}}
. In particular, this implies that
U({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}})\cong U({\mathfrak{g}}_{R})\otimes_{R}{\mathbb{C}}
.

Given
\lambda\in{\mathfrak{t}}_{R}^{*}
, define
M_{R}(\lambda)=U({\mathfrak{g}}_{R})v_{\lambda}
, which is an
R
-form of
M_{\mathbb{C}}(\lambda)
by construction. We then define the
U({\mathfrak{g}}_{R})
-modules
M_{R}^{\operatorname{max}}(\lambda):=M_{R}(\lambda)\cap M^{\operatorname{max}}(\lambda)
and
L_{R}(\lambda):=M_{R}(\lambda)/M_{R}^{\operatorname{max}}(\lambda)
. We may alternately define
L_{R}(\lambda)=U({\mathfrak{g}}_{R})\overline{v}_{\lambda}
; it is straightforward to see that these two definitions coincide. The important point for present purposes is that there is a surjective homomorphism of
U({\mathfrak{g}}_{R})
-modules
M_{R}(\lambda)\twoheadrightarrow L_{R}(\lambda)
.

Note now that
U({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{K}})
also has a Poincaré-Birkhoff-Witt basis of monomials in
{\mathcal{B}}
, and we thus have
U({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{K}})\cong U({\mathfrak{g}}_{R})\otimes_{R}{\mathbb{K}}
. In particular we may define the
U({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{K}})
-modules
M_{p}(\lambda):=M_{R}(\lambda)\otimes_{R}{\mathbb{K}}
and
L_{p}(\lambda):=L_{R}(\lambda)\otimes_{R}{\mathbb{K}}
. It is straightforward to check that we may alternatively define
M_{p}(\lambda)=U({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{K}})\otimes_{U({\mathfrak{b}}_{{\mathbb{K}}})}{\mathbb{K}}_{\lambda}
, where
{\mathbb{K}}_{\lambda}
is the one-dimensional
U({\mathfrak{b}}_{\mathbb{K}})
-module on which
{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}_{{\mathbb{K}}}
acts via zero and
{\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathbb{K}}}
acts via
\widetilde{\lambda}
. Letting
w_{\lambda}:=v_{\lambda}\otimes 1\in M_{p}(\lambda)
and
\overline{w}_{\lambda}:=\overline{v}_{\lambda}\otimes 1\in L_{p}(\lambda)
, the surjection
M_{R}(\lambda)\twoheadrightarrow L_{R}(\lambda)
induces a surjective homomorphism of
U({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{K}})
-modules
M_{p}(\lambda)\twoheadrightarrow L_{p}(\lambda)
sending
w_{\lambda}
to
\overline{w}_{\lambda}
.

Given
\chi\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{K}}^{*}
, define
J_{\chi}:=\langle x^{p}-x^{[p]}-\chi(x)^{p}\mid x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{K}}\rangle\subseteq Z_{p}({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{K}})
. Then we may form the submodule
J_{\chi}M_{p}(\lambda)\subseteq M_{p}(\lambda)
and the quotient module
M_{p}^{\chi}(\lambda):=M_{p}(\lambda)/J_{\chi}M_{p}(\lambda)
. By the same token,
J_{\chi}L_{p}(\lambda)
is a
U({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{K}})
-submodule of
L_{p}(\lambda)
and we may form the quotient module
L_{p}^{\chi}(\lambda):=L_{p}(\lambda)/J_{\chi}L_{p}(\lambda)
. Both
M_{p}^{\chi}(\lambda)
and
L_{p}^{\chi}(\lambda)
are then (possibly trivial)
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{K}}})
-modules, and the surjection
M_{p}(\lambda)\twoheadrightarrow L_{p}(\lambda)
induces a surjection
M_{p}^{\chi}(\lambda)\twoheadrightarrow L_{p}^{\chi}(\lambda)
of
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{K}}})
-modules.

The next lemma shows that the
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{K}})
-modules
M_{p}^{\chi}(\lambda)
in fact coincide with certain modules we discussed extensively in Section
2
of this paper: baby Verma modules.

Proposition 3.1
.

The
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{K}}})
-modules
M_{p}^{\chi}(\lambda)
and
Z_{\chi}(\widetilde{\lambda})
are isomorphic.

Proof.

Since
{\mathfrak{n}}_{{\mathbb{K}}}^{+}w_{\lambda}=0
and
hw_{\lambda}=\widetilde{\lambda}(h)w_{\lambda}
for all
h\in{\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathbb{K}}}
, Frobenius reciprocity for
Z_{\chi}(\widetilde{\lambda})
implies that there exists a homomorphism
Z_{\chi}(\widetilde{\lambda})\to M_{p}^{\chi}(\lambda)
. Similarly, as
{\mathfrak{n}}_{{\mathbb{K}}}^{+}z_{\lambda}=0
and
hz_{\lambda}=\widetilde{\lambda}(h)z_{\lambda}
, Frobenius reciprocity for
M_{p}(\lambda)
induces a homomorphism of
U({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{K}}})
-modules
M_{p}(\lambda)\to Z_{\chi}(\widetilde{\lambda})
. Since
J_{\chi}Z_{\chi}(\widetilde{\lambda})=0
, this induces a homomorphism
M_{p}^{\chi}(\lambda)\to Z_{\chi}(\widetilde{\lambda})
. It is straightforward to see that the maps
Z_{\chi}(\widetilde{\lambda})\to M_{p}^{\chi}(\lambda)
and
M_{p}^{\chi}(\lambda)\to Z_{\chi}(\widetilde{\lambda})
are inverse to each other.
∎

This proposition allows us to prove the following corollary. To state it, recall that when
\chi
is in (weak) standard Levi form each baby Verma module
Z_{\chi}(\widetilde{\lambda})
has a unique simple quotient which we denote
L_{\chi}(\widetilde{\lambda})
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Suppose that
\chi
is in standard Levi form and that
L_{p}^{\chi}(\lambda)\neq 0
. Then there is a surjective homomorphism of
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{K}}})
-modules
L_{p}^{\chi}(\lambda)\twoheadrightarrow L_{\chi}(\widetilde{\lambda})
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.1
,
M_{p}^{\chi}(\lambda)=Z_{\chi}(\widetilde{\lambda})
. Therefore, it has unique simple quotient
L_{\chi}(\widetilde{\lambda})
. We have already observed that
M_{p}^{\chi}(\lambda)
surjects onto
L_{p}^{\chi}(\lambda)
(and thus, in particular,
L_{p}^{\chi}(\lambda)
is finite-dimensional). If
L_{p}^{\chi}(\lambda)\neq 0
then it must have a simple quotient, which must also be a simple quotient of
M_{p}^{\chi}(\lambda)
. The result follows.
∎

Consider now only the case where
G_{\mathbb{C}}=\mbox{\rm GL}_{N}({\mathbb{C}})
. Then
G_{\mathbb{C}}^{\prime}:=\mbox{\rm SL}_{N}({\mathbb{C}})
is a simple simply connected complex algebraic group, with Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\prime}={\mathfrak{sl}}_{N}({\mathbb{C}})
. Writing
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\prime}={\mathfrak{t}}_{\mathbb{C}}\cap{\mathfrak{sl}}_{N}({\mathbb{C}})
, there is a natural surjection
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\twoheadrightarrow({\mathfrak{t}}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\prime})^{*}
which we write as
\lambda\mapsto\lambda^{\prime}
. One can easily see that restricting the simple
U({\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{N}({\mathbb{C}}))
-module
L_{\mathbb{C}}(\lambda)
to
U({\mathfrak{sl}}_{N}({\mathbb{C}}))
gives the simple
U({\mathfrak{sl}}_{N}({\mathbb{C}}))
-module
L_{\mathbb{C}}(\lambda^{\prime})
. Furthermore, it is a straightforward exercise to check that, based on our choices of bases
{\mathcal{B}}
, restriction of the
U({\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{N}({\mathbb{K}}))
-module
L_{p}(\lambda)
to
U({\mathfrak{sl}}_{N}({\mathbb{K}}))
gives precisely the module
L_{p}(\lambda^{\prime})
which we obtain by directly applying in the setting of
G_{\mathbb{C}}=\mbox{\rm SL}_{N}({\mathbb{C}})
the constructions of Subsection
3.1
and this subsection.

As with the Cartan subalgebras, we may restrict an element
\chi\in{\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{N}({\mathbb{K}})^{*}
to an element
\chi^{\prime}\in{\mathfrak{sl}}_{N}({\mathbb{K}})^{*}
. Define then
J_{\chi}=\langle x^{p}-x^{[p]}-\chi(x)\mid x\in{\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{N}({\mathbb{K}})\rangle\subseteq Z_{p}({\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{N}({\mathbb{K}}))
and
J^{\prime}_{\chi^{\prime}}=\langle x^{p}-x^{[p]}-\chi(x)\mid x\in{\mathfrak{sl}}_{N}({\mathbb{K}})\rangle\subseteq Z_{p}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{N}({\mathbb{K}}))
, and consider the
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{N}({\mathbb{K}}))
-module
L_{p}^{\chi}(\lambda)=L_{p}(\lambda)/J_{\chi}L_{p}(\lambda)
and the
U_{\chi^{\prime}}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{N}({\mathbb{K}}))
-module
L_{p}^{\chi^{\prime}}(\lambda^{\prime})=L_{p}(\lambda^{\prime})/J_{\chi^{\prime}}^{\prime}L_{p}(\lambda^{\prime})
. These two modules are then related as follows – note that
U_{\chi^{\prime}}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{N}({\mathbb{K}}))
is a subalgebra of
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{N}({\mathbb{K}}))
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
y\in{\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{N}({\mathbb{K}})
denote the identity matrix. If
\chi(y)=0
then restriction of the
U_{\chi}({\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{N}({\mathbb{K}}))
-module
L_{p}^{\chi}(\lambda)
to
U_{\chi^{\prime}}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{N}({\mathbb{K}}))
precisely gives the
U_{\chi^{\prime}}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{N}({\mathbb{K}}))
-module
L_{p}^{\chi^{\prime}}(\lambda^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Since
L_{p}(\lambda)
and
L_{p}(\lambda^{\prime})
coincide as
U({\mathfrak{sl}}_{N}({\mathbb{C}}))
-modules, it suffices to show that there is equality
J_{\chi}L_{p}(\lambda)=J_{\chi^{\prime}}^{\prime}L_{p}(\lambda^{\prime})
.
The
p
-centre
Z_{p}({\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{N}({\mathbb{C}}))
coincides with the polynomial subalgebra
{\mathbb{K}}[e_{11}^{p}-e_{11},\ldots,e_{NN}^{p}-e_{NN}]
of
U({\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{N}({\mathbb{C}}))
. Setting
x_{ii}=e_{i-1,i-1}-e_{i,i}
for
i=2,\ldots,N
, and recalling that
y=e_{11}+\cdots+e_{NN}
, we may rewrite this subalgebra as the polynomial algebra
{\mathbb{K}}[x_{22}^{p}-x_{22},\ldots,x_{NN}^{p}-x_{NN},y^{p}-y]
so long as
p\nmid N
(which for us is imposed by our assumptions on
R
). Since
Z_{p}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{N}({\mathbb{K}}))={\mathbb{K}}[x_{22}^{p}-x_{22},\ldots,x_{NN}^{p}-x_{NN}]
, we may rewrite this as
Z_{p}({\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{N}({\mathbb{K}}))=Z_{p}({\mathfrak{sl}}_{N}({\mathbb{K}}))+\langle y^{p}-y\rangle
.

From this perspective, it is straightforward to see that
J_{\chi}=J_{\chi^{\prime}}^{\prime}+\langle y^{p}\rangle
. Since
y

via scalar multiplication by
\widetilde{\lambda}(y)\in R
(which follows from the easy-to-see analogous statement for
M_{\mathbb{C}}(\lambda)
and

(y^{p}-y)v=(\widetilde{\lambda}(y)^{p}-\widetilde{\lambda}(y))v=0
for all

. Thus,
J_{\chi}L_{p}(\lambda)=J_{\chi^{\prime}}^{\prime}L_{p}(\lambda^{\prime})

∎

By definition,
\chi\in{\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{N}({\mathbb{C}})^{*}
is in standard Levi form if and only if
\chi\in{\mathfrak{sl}}_{N}({\mathbb{C}})^{*}
is in standard Levi form.

Corollary 3.4
.

Suppose that
\chi\in{\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{N}({\mathbb{C}})^{*}
is in standard Levi form. Then
L_{p}^{\chi}(\lambda)\neq 0
if and only if
L_{p}^{\chi^{\prime}}(\lambda^{\prime})\neq 0
.

In particular, this corollary means that to determine when

for

(and thus to apply Corollary

), we need only consider the situation over

G_{\mathbb{C}}
simple.

3.3.
Associated Varieties

In light of Corollary
3.2
, we want to determine when
L_{p}^{\chi}(\lambda)
is non-zero. The main tool to do so is
[
Pr3
, Lemma 3.1]
; to explain this result, we first need to introduce various subvarieties of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
and
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{K}}^{*}
. We proceed to do so in this subsection. As mentioned above, we (largely) only consider
G_{\mathbb{C}}
which are simple simply connected complex algebraic groups here, for ease of exposition. Our explanations follow
[
Ja4
,
Pr3
]
.

As we have already observed, the universal enveloping algebra
U({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}})
has a Poincaré-Birkhoff-Witt basis, which induces a PBW-filtration on
U({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}})
. More specifically, if we temporarily write the basis of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
as
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}
, then
U({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}})
has a basis consisting of the elements

x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{k}^{a_{k}}\qquad\mbox{for}\qquad a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\geq 0.

The PBW-filtration of
U({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}})
is then given by

{\mathbb{C}}=U_{0}({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}})\subseteq U_{1}({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}})\subseteq U_{2}({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}})\subseteq\cdots\subseteq U({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}})

where

U_{n}({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}):={\mathbb{C}}\mbox{-span}\{x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{k}^{a_{k}}\mid a_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}\leq n\}.

It is well-known that the associated graded algebra
\operatorname{gr}U({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}):=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}U_{n}({\mathfrak{g}})/U_{n-1}({\mathfrak{g}})
of
U({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}})
with respect to this filtration (setting
U_{-1}({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}})=0
) is isomorphic to the symmetric algebra
S({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}})
. Given an ideal
I
of
U({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}})
, we may form an ideal of
S({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}})
as

\operatorname{gr}I=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\left((I\cap U_{n}({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}))+U_{n-1}({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}})\right)/U_{n-1}({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}})\subseteq S({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}})\cong{\mathbb{C}}[{\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{*}].

The
associated variety

{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{A}}(I)
of
I
is then defined to be the zero set of
\operatorname{gr}I
in
{\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{*}
. When
I
is a primitive ideal (i.e. the annihilator of a simple
U({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}})
-module), Joseph’s Irreducibility Theorem says that

{{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{A}}}(I)=\overline{{\mathbb{O}}}

for some nilpotent coadjoint
G_{{\mathbb{C}}}
-orbit
{\mathbb{O}}\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{*}
(see
[
Jo1
, Corollary 3.3]
for type
A
,
[
Jo3
, Theorem 3.10]
for other types). Using the Killing form, we can identify
{\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}
and
{\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{*}
and thus identify
{{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{A}}}(I)=\overline{{\mathbb{O}}}
with a subvariety of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}}
(we abuse notation and use
{{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{A}}}(I)
to denote this subvariety as well).

Remark
.

When
G_{\mathbb{C}}=\mbox{\rm GL}_{N}({\mathbb{C}})
and
I
is a primitive ideal in
U({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}})
, we may apply the same construction to get the associated variety
{{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{A}}}(I)\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{N}({\mathbb{C}})^{*}
. Using the trace form (instead of the Killing form, which is no longer non-degenerate), we may also identify this with a subvariety of
{\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{N}({\mathbb{C}})
which, again, we still denote by
{{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{A}}}(I)
. We then denote by
{{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{A}}}^{\prime}(I)
the image of
{{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{A}}}(I)
under the surjection
{\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{N}({\mathbb{C}})\twoheadrightarrow{\mathfrak{pgl}}_{N}({\mathbb{C}})\xrightarrow{\sim}{\mathfrak{sl}}_{N}({\mathbb{C}})
. Joseph’s Irreducibility Theorem again gives that
{{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{A}}}^{\prime}(I)
is the closure of a nilpotent orbit. (Although we don’t use it in this paper, a similar definition may be used whenever
G_{\mathbb{C}}
is reductive.)

Given
\lambda\in{\mathfrak{t}}_{R}^{*}
, the
U({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}})
-module
L_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\lambda)
is simple and thus the annihilator
I(\lambda):=\operatorname{Ann}_{U({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}})}L_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\lambda)
is a primitive ideal of
U({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}})
. There thus exists a nilpotent coadjoint
G_{{\mathbb{C}}}
-orbit, which we henceforth denote
{\mathbb{O}}_{\lambda}
, such that
{{\mathcal{V}}{\mathcal{A}}}(I(\lambda))=\overline{{\mathbb{O}}}_{\lambda}
.

The PBW-filtration on
U({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}})
induces a filtration on
L_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\lambda)
by setting
L_{{\mathbb{C}},n}(\lambda)=U_{n}({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}})\overline{v}_{\lambda}
. This filtration is compatible with the filtration of
U({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}})
in the sense that
U_{n}({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}})L_{{\mathbb{C}},m}(\lambda)\subseteq L_{{\mathbb{C}},m+n}(\lambda)
. From this, we may form the associated graded module

\operatorname{gr}L_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\lambda):=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}L_{{\mathbb{C}},n}(\lambda)/L_{{\mathbb{C}},n-1}(\lambda),

which is an
S({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}})
-module. Note that
\overline{v}_{\lambda}\in L_{{\mathbb{C}},0}(\lambda)
; we abuse notation to write
\overline{v}_{\lambda}=\operatorname{gr}_{0}(\overline{v}_{\lambda})\in\operatorname{gr}L_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\lambda)
. It remains true that
\overline{v}_{\lambda}
generates
\operatorname{gr}L_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\lambda)
as an
S({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}})
-module, i.e.
\operatorname{gr}L_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\lambda)=S({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}})\overline{v}_{\lambda}
. Since
S({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}})
is commutative, we have

J(\lambda):=\operatorname{Ann}_{S({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}})}\operatorname{gr}L_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\lambda)=\operatorname{Ann}_{S({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}})}\overline{v}_{\lambda}\subseteq S({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}).

The associated variety
{\mathcal{V}}_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}}L_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\lambda)
is then defined to be the zero set of
J(\lambda)
in
{\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{*}
, i.e. the set of maximal ideals in
S({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}})
containing
J(\lambda)
.

The universal enveloping
R
-algebra
U({\mathfrak{g}}_{R})
corresponding to the Lie ring
{\mathfrak{g}}_{R}
also has a PBW-filtration

R=U_{0}({\mathfrak{g}}_{R})\subseteq U_{1}({\mathfrak{g}}_{R})\subseteq U_{2}({\mathfrak{g}}_{R})\subseteq\cdots\subseteq U({\mathfrak{g}}_{R}),

with associated graded algebra isomorphic to
S({\mathfrak{g}}_{R})
. Using this filtration, we may define a corresponding filtration of
L_{R}(\lambda)
by setting

L_{R,n}(\lambda)=U_{n}({\mathfrak{g}}_{R})\overline{v}_{\lambda}.

We form as before the associated graded module
\operatorname{gr}L_{R}(\lambda)
and the annihilator

J_{R}(\lambda):=\operatorname{Ann}_{S({\mathfrak{g}}_{R})}\operatorname{gr}L_{R}(\lambda)=\operatorname{Ann}_{S({\mathfrak{g}}_{R})}\overline{v}_{\lambda}.

We may do similar over
{\mathbb{K}}
: as before,
U({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{K}}})
has a PBW basis and a PBW-filtration, allowing us define a filtration

0=L_{p,-1}(\lambda)\subseteq L_{p,0}(\lambda)\subseteq L_{p,1}(\lambda)\subseteq\cdots\subseteq L_{p}(\lambda)

of
L_{p}(\lambda)
. We may define the associated graded module
\operatorname{gr}L_{p}(\lambda):=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}L_{p,n}(\lambda)/L_{p,n-1}(\lambda)
, an
S({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{K}}})
-module. We then define

J_{p}(\lambda):=\operatorname{Ann}_{S({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{K}}})}\operatorname{gr}L_{p}(\lambda)=\operatorname{Ann}_{S({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{K}}})}\overline{w}_{\lambda}\subseteq S({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{K}}}).

The associated variety
{\mathcal{V}}_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{K}}}}L_{p}(\lambda)\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{K}}}^{*}
of
L_{p}(\lambda)
is defined to be the zero set of
J_{p}(\lambda)
, i.e. the set of maximal ideals of
S({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{K}})
containing
J_{p}(\lambda)
.

Finally, since
L_{p}(\lambda)
is a
U({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{K}}})
-module it restricts to a module over the
p
-centre
Z_{p}({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{K}}})
. We then define

{\mathcal{I}}_{p}(\lambda):=\operatorname{Ann}_{Z_{p}({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{K}}})}L_{p}(\lambda)=\operatorname{Ann}_{Z_{p}({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{K}}})}\overline{w}_{\lambda}\subseteq Z_{p}({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{K}}}).

The
p
-centre
Z_{p}({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{K}})
identifies with
S({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{K}}^{(1)})
, where
\cdot\,^{(1)}
denotes the Frobenius twist.
5
5

5

More explicitly,
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{K}}^{(1)}
is precisely the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{K}}
except with scalar multiplication now given by
a\cdot x=a^{1/p}x
.
Since the Frobenius morphism on
{\mathbb{K}}
is bijective, there is a
{\mathbb{K}}
-algebra isomorphism
S({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{K}}^{(1)})\cong S({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{K}})
; the maximal spectrum of
Z_{p}({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{K}})
can thus be identified with
{\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{K}}}^{*}
, and hence the vanishing set of
{\mathcal{I}}_{p}(\lambda)
can be identified with a subvariety of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{K}}^{*}
. We denote the latter variety by
V_{p}(\lambda)\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{K}}^{*}
.

As discussed earlier, the following result of
[
Pr3

Lemma 3.5

Let
\chi\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{K}}^{*}

In light of Corollary

(and therefore some further restrictions on

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\lambda\in{\mathfrak{t}}_{R}^{*}
and make assumption (R2(
\lambda)
)). If
\chi:{\mathfrak{g}}_{R}\to R
is an

-linear map such that
\chi\in{\mathcal{V}}_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}}L_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\lambda)
(when

is viewed as an element of

Proof.

For this proof, as above, let us enumerate the Chevalley basis
{\mathcal{B}}
of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}}
as

and

,

(x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{k}^{a_{k}}\overline{v}_{\lambda})\otimes 1

.

That the maps labelled (1) and (6) are isomorphisms follows easily from the fact that
\kappa
is an isomorphism and clearly sends
\operatorname{Ann}_{S({\mathfrak{g}}_{R})\otimes_{R}{\mathbb{K}}}(\overline{v}_{\lambda}\otimes 1)
to
\operatorname{Ann}_{S({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{K}})}(\overline{w}_{\lambda})
(here
\overline{v}_{\lambda}\otimes 1
is an element of
\operatorname{gr}(L_{R}(\lambda)\otimes_{R}{\mathbb{K}})
, not an element of
\operatorname{gr}(L_{R}(\lambda))\otimes_{R}{\mathbb{K}}
) and sends
\iota(\operatorname{Ann}_{S({\mathfrak{g}}_{R})}(\overline{v}_{\lambda})\otimes_{R}{\mathbb{K}})
to
\kappa\iota(\operatorname{Ann}_{S({\mathfrak{g}}_{R})}(\overline{v}_{\lambda})\otimes_{R}{\mathbb{K}})
. Furthermore, that the maps labelled (2) and (4) are isomorphisms follows immediately from the description of these maps and the first isomorphism theorem (recalling that
\operatorname{gr}(L_{R}(\lambda)\otimes_{R}{\mathbb{K}})
and
\operatorname{gr}(L_{R}(\lambda))
are generated by
\overline{v}_{\lambda}\otimes 1
and
\overline{v}_{\lambda}
respectively). And the map (5) is an isomorphism due to the right-exactness of
-\otimes_{R}{\mathbb{K}}
and the exactness of the sequence of
R
-modules

0\to\operatorname{Ann}_{S({\mathfrak{g}}_{R})}(\overline{v}_{\lambda})\to S({\mathfrak{g}}_{R})\to S({\mathfrak{g}}_{R})/\operatorname{Ann}_{S({\mathfrak{g}}_{R})}(\overline{v}_{\lambda})\to 0.

What remains is therefore to show that the map labelled (3) is an isomorphism. As in
[
Pr3
]
, this follows from our assumption that each
L_{R,n}(\lambda)/L_{R,n-1}(\lambda)
is torsion-free over
R
, and thus free of finite rank. Indeed, this assumption is enough to show that
L_{p,n}(\lambda)\cong L_{R,n}(\lambda)\otimes_{R}{\mathbb{K}}
and that the inclusion
L_{R,n-1}(\lambda)\hookrightarrow L_{R,n}(\lambda)
induces an injection
L_{R,n-1}(\lambda)\otimes_{R}{\mathbb{K}}\to L_{R,n}(\lambda)\otimes_{R}{\mathbb{K}}
, which together imply the result. The map obtained via this argument sends

x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{k}^{a_{k}}\overline{v}_{\lambda}\otimes 1\mapsto x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{k}^{a_{k}}\overline{v}_{\lambda}\otimes 1.

Putting all this together yields an isomorphism
\frac{S({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{K}})}{\operatorname{Ann}_{S({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{K}})}(\overline{w}_{\lambda})}\xrightarrow{\sim}\frac{S({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{K}})}{\kappa\iota(\operatorname{Ann}_{S({\mathfrak{g}}_{R})}(\overline{v}_{\lambda})\otimes_{R}{\mathbb{K}})}
induced from the identity map
S({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{K}})\to S({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{K}})
. Therefore,

\operatorname{Ann}_{S({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{K}}})}(\overline{w}_{\lambda})=\kappa\iota(\operatorname{Ann}_{S({\mathfrak{g}}_{R})}(\overline{v}_{\lambda})\otimes_{R}{\mathbb{K}}).

Now, given an
R
-linear map
\chi:{\mathfrak{g}}_{R}\to R
, we define

{\mathfrak{m}}_{\chi}^{{\mathbb{C}}}=\langle x-\chi(x)\mid x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\rangle\subseteq S({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}),

{\mathfrak{m}}_{\chi}^{R}=\langle x-\chi(x)\mid x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{R}\rangle\subseteq S({\mathfrak{g}}_{R}),\quad\mbox{and}

{\mathfrak{m}}_{\chi}^{{\mathbb{K}}}=\langle x-(\chi\otimes 1)(x)\mid x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{K}}}\rangle\subseteq S({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{K}}}).

The first and last of these are maximal ideals, which correspond to the points
\chi\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{*}
and
\chi\otimes 1\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{K}}}^{*}
. The statement that
\chi\in{\mathcal{V}}_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}}}L_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\lambda)
is therefore equivalent to the statement that

\operatorname{Ann}_{S({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}})}(\overline{v}_{\lambda})\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}_{\chi}^{{\mathbb{C}}}.

This then implies that

\operatorname{Ann}_{S({\mathfrak{g}}_{R})}(\overline{v}_{\lambda})=\operatorname{Ann}_{S({\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{C}}})}(\overline{v}_{\lambda})\cap S({\mathfrak{g}}_{R})\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}_{\chi}^{{\mathbb{C}}}\cap S({\mathfrak{g}}_{R})={\mathfrak{m}}_{\chi}^{R},

and therefore that

\iota(\operatorname{Ann}_{S({\mathfrak{g}}_{R})}(\overline{v}_{\lambda})\otimes_{R}{\mathbb{K}})\subseteq\overline{\iota}({\mathfrak{m}}_{\chi}^{R}\otimes_{R}{\mathbb{K}}),

where
\overline{\iota}
is induced from the inclusion
{\mathfrak{m}}_{\chi}^{R}\subseteq S({\mathfrak{g}}_{R})
. Applying
\kappa
to both sides gives

\operatorname{Ann}_{S({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{K}})}(\overline{w}_{\lambda})=\kappa\iota(\operatorname{Ann}_{S({\mathfrak{g}}_{R})}(\overline{v}_{\lambda})\otimes_{R}{\mathbb{K}})\subseteq\kappa\overline{\iota}({\mathfrak{m}}_{\chi}^{R}\otimes_{R}{\mathbb{K}})={\mathfrak{m}}_{\chi}^{\mathbb{K}}.

This precisely means that
\chi\otimes 1\in{\mathcal{V}}_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{K}}}}L_{p}(\lambda)
.

∎

For the next result we also need to assume
that there exist algebraically independent homogeneous elements
y_{1},\ldots,y_{s}\in S({\mathfrak{g}}_{R})/J_{R}(\lambda)
and elements
v_{1},\ldots,v_{S}\in S({\mathfrak{g}}_{R})/J_{R}(\lambda)
such that

S({\mathfrak{g}}_{R})/J_{R}(\lambda)=R[y_{1},\ldots,y_{s}]v_{1}+\cdots+R[y_{1},\ldots,y_{s}]v_{S}.

As in
[
Pr3
, §2.3]
, we may make this assumption for a given

by expanding
{\mathcal{S}}
as necessary. We label this assumption “Assumption (R3(
\lambda
))”, noting that it depends on
\lambda\in{\mathfrak{t}}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}
(and therefore part of the assumption is that
\lambda
may be defined as element of
{\mathfrak{t}}_{R}^{*}
).

. Note that, although our current assumptions are not enough to satisfy all the assumptions of that theorem, they are sufficient for the result we need. This result says as follows.

Proposition 3.7
.

Assume that
\lambda\in{\mathfrak{t}}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}
and that
R
satisfies assumptions (R1), (R2(
\lambda
)) and (R3(
\lambda
)) (so in fact

. Then every irreducible component of
{\mathcal{V}}_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{{\mathbb{K}}}}L_{p}(\lambda)

of maximal dimension.

Type

From now on, we only consider the case where

Proposition 3.12
.

The subvariety
\widetilde{B}_{\mathbb{K}}({\mathbb{K}})\cdot(\chi_{\pi}\otimes 1)
has dimension
\frac{1}{2}\dim{\mathbb{O}}_{\pi}
.

Proof.

Using the isomorphism
{\mathfrak{sl}}_{N}({\mathbb{K}})\cong{\mathfrak{sl}}_{N}({\mathbb{K}})^{*}
, it will be enough for us to compute
\dim(\widetilde{B}_{\mathbb{K}}({\mathbb{K}})\cdot(e_{\pi}\otimes 1))
. (For ease of notation, we write
e_{\pi}
in place of
e_{\pi}\otimes 1
for the remainder of this proof.) Recalling that

B_{{\mathbb{K}}}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a_{11}&a_{12}&\cdots&a_{1N}\\
0&a_{22}&\cdots&a_{2N}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&a_{NN}\\
\end{pmatrix}\mid a_{ij}\in{\mathbb{K}}\mbox{ and }a_{11}\cdots a_{NN}\neq 0\right\}\subseteq\mbox{\rm GL}_{N}({\mathbb{K}}),

it is elementary that
\dim(\widetilde{B}_{\mathbb{K}}({\mathbb{K}})\cdot e_{\pi})=\dim(B_{\mathbb{K}}\cdot e_{\pi})
, where the latter is a subvariety of
{\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{N}({\mathbb{K}})
. We compute the latter dimension, since it is easier. In what follows, we write
B
for
B_{\mathbb{K}}
for ease of notation.

Note that
\dim(B\cdot e_{\pi})=\dim B-\dim B_{e_{\pi}}
and that

B_{e_{\pi}}=B\cap{\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{N}({\mathbb{K}})_{e_{\pi}}\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{N}({\mathbb{K}}),

where
B_{e_{\pi}}
denotes the stabiliser of
e_{\pi}
in
B
and
{\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{N}({\mathbb{K}})_{e_{\pi}}
denotes the centraliser of
e_{\pi}
in
{\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{N}({\mathbb{K}})
. For
i=1,\ldots,r
set
v_{i}
to be the standard basis element of
{\mathbb{K}}^{N}
with a
1
in the
(p_{1}+\cdots+p_{i})
-th entry and zeroes elsewhere. The standard basis of
{\mathbb{K}}^{N}
can then be alternately viewed as

\{e_{\pi}^{k}(v_{i})\mid 1\leq i\leq r,\,\,0\leq k<p_{i}\}.

As in
[
Ja4
, §3.1]
, an element
Z\in{\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{N}({\mathbb{K}})_{e_{\pi}}
is uniquely determined by its effect on
v_{i}
for each
i=1,\ldots,r
, since
Z(e_{\pi}^{k}(v_{i}))=e_{\pi}^{k}(Z(v_{i}))
for all
k
. Furthermore, each
Z(v_{i})
can be written as

Z(v_{i})=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\sum_{k=\operatorname{max}(0,p_{j}-p_{i})}^{p_{j}-1}a_{k,j;i}e_{\pi}^{k}(v_{j})

with the
a_{k,j;i}
chosen arbitrarily.

Writing this in matrix form, it is easy to see that such
Z
lies in
B
if and only if
a_{0,1;1}a_{0,2;2}\cdots a_{0,r;r}\neq 0
and
a_{k,j;i}=0
whenever
j>i
. In particular, this means that

\dim B_{e_{\pi}}=\sum_{1\leq j\leq i\leq r}\min(p_{i},p_{j}).

Note that, as in
[
Ja4
, §3.1]
,

\dim{\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{N}({\mathbb{K}})_{e_{\pi}}=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq r}\min(p_{i},p_{j}).

Therefore

\dim{\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{N}({\mathbb{K}})_{e_{\pi}}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}p_{i}+2\sum_{1\leq j<i\leq r}\min(p_{i},p_{j})=N+2\sum_{1\leq j<i\leq r}\min(p_{i},p_{j}).

This implies

\sum_{1\leq j<i\leq r}\min(p_{i},p_{j})=\frac{1}{2}\left(\dim{\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{N}({\mathbb{K}})_{e_{\pi}}-N\right)

and thus

\dim B_{e_{\pi}}=\sum_{1\leq j\leq i\leq r}\min(p_{i},p_{j})=\frac{1}{2}\left(\dim{\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{N}({\mathbb{K}})_{e_{\pi}}+N\right).

Hence

\dim B-\dim B_{e_{\pi}}=\frac{1}{2}(2\dim B-N-\dim{\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{N}({\mathbb{K}})_{e_{\pi}})=\frac{1}{2}(\dim{\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{N}({\mathbb{K}})-\dim{\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{N}({\mathbb{K}})_{e_{\pi}})

and so

\dim(B\cdot e_{\pi})=\frac{1}{2}\dim{\mathbb{O}}_{\pi}

as required.
∎

We may put all this together into the following corollary. To state it, recall the definition of
V_{p}(\lambda_{\widehat{A}}-\rho)\subseteq{\mathfrak{sl}}_{N}({\mathbb{K}})^{*}
from Subsection
3.3

)) for

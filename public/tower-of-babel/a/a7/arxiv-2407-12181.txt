Non-semisimple topological field theory and Z ^ \widehat{Z} -invariants from 𝔬 ​ 𝔰 ​ 𝔭 ​ ( 1 | 2 ) \mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
Francesco Costantino, Matthew Harper, Adam Robertson, Matthew B. Young

Abstract
. We construct three-dimensional non-semisimple topological field theories from the unrolled quantum group of the Lie superalgebra 𝔬 ​ 𝔰 ​ 𝔭 ​ ( 1 | 2 ) \mathfrak{osp}(1|2) . More precisely, the quantum group depends on a root of unity q = e 2 ​ π ​ − 1 r q=e^{\frac{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}{r}} , where r r is a positive integer greater than 2 2 , and the construction applies when r r is not congruent to 4 4 modulo 8 8 . The algebraic result which underlies the construction is the existence of a relative modular structure on the non-finite, non-semisimple category of weight modules for the quantum group. We prove a Verlinde formula which allows for the computation of dimensions and Euler characteristics of topological field theory state spaces of unmarked surfaces. When r r is congruent to ± 1 \pm 1 or ± 2 \pm 2 modulo 8 8 , we relate the resulting 3 3 -manifold invariants with physicists’ Z ^ \widehat{Z} -invariants associated to 𝔬 ​ 𝔰 ​ 𝔭 ​ ( 1 | 2 ) \mathfrak{osp}(1|2) . Finally, we establish a relation between Z ^ \widehat{Z} -invariants associated to 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 2 ) \mathfrak{sl}(2) and 𝔬 ​ 𝔰 ​ 𝔭 ​ ( 1 | 2 ) \mathfrak{osp}(1|2) which was conjectured in the physics literature.



Introduction

The first goal of this paper is to develop the representation theory of an unrolled quantization of the orthosymplectic Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
. The second goal is to connect this representation theory to the non-semisimple quantum topology of
3
-manifolds. We achieve the second goal in two ways. First, we construct a three-dimensional non-semisimple topological field theory (TFT)
\mathcal{Z}
using the framework of relative modular categories. Second, we study the
\widehat{Z}^{\mathfrak{g}}
-invariants of
3
-manifolds, which were recently introduced by Gukov, Pei, Putrov and Vafa. Here
\mathfrak{g}
is a complex semisimple Lie superalgebra. More precisely, we establish direct relations between
\widehat{Z}^{\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)}
and
\widehat{Z}^{\mathfrak{sl}(2)}
and between
\widehat{Z}^{\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)}
and the
3
-manifold invariants defined by
\mathcal{Z}
. In the remainder of the introduction, we explain our results in more detail and explain the general context into which they fit.

The constructions of this paper are fundamentally different than those of the previous paragraph. The key new feature is the consideration of a much larger class of representations of quantum

Our first main result, which is the culmination of our study of the representation theory of

Theorem A

(Theorem
2.31
)
.

If
r\not\equiv 4\mod 8
, then the category
\mathcal{C}
admits a relative modular structure.

The details of the relative modular structure, which includes a grading
\mathcal{C}=\bigoplus_{g\in\mathsf{G}}\mathcal{C}_{g}
by an abelian group
\mathsf{G}
and an abelian group
\mathsf{Z}\simeq\mathsf{Z}_{0}\times\mathbb{Z}/\penalty 2\mathbb{Z}
together with a ribbon functor

modulo
8
. As explained below, the cases

is a distinct family. By general results of De Renzi

, a relative modular structure on

gives rise to a symmetric monoidal functor

\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{C}}:\mathsf{C}\mathsf{ob}^{\textnormal{ad}}_{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow\mathsf{V}\mathsf{ect}^{\mathsf{Z}\textnormal{-gr}}_{\mathbb{C}},

the three-dimensional oriented non-semisimple TFT associated to
\mathcal{C}
. The domain
\mathsf{C}\mathsf{ob}^{\textnormal{ad}}_{\mathcal{C}}
is a category of decorated surfaces and their admissible bordisms. For example, morphisms include the data of a cohomology class
\omega
on the underlying
3
-manifold with coefficients in
\mathsf{G}
. The codomain
\mathsf{V}\mathsf{ect}^{\mathsf{Z}\textnormal{-gr}}_{\mathbb{C}}
is the monoidal category of
\mathsf{Z}
-graded vector spaces with a symmetric braiding which, upon restricting to underlying

-graded vector spaces, recovers that of the category of super vector spaces. The

are, up to normalization, the CGP invariants introduced by first author, Geer and Patureau-Mirand
[
14

In Section
3
we perform a number of calculations for
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{C}}
. We begin by proving a Verlinde formula, Theorem
3.2
, which relates the partition function of a trivial circle bundle over a surface
\Sigma_{g}
of genus
g\geq 1
, with
\omega
having holonomy
\beta\in\mathsf{G}
along the circle fibre, to a
\beta
-dependent specialization of the generating function of
\mathsf{Z}
-graded dimensions of
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{C}}(\Sigma_{g})
. Our derivation of the Verlinde formula follows previous derivations
[
4
,
34
,
29
]
, relying on an explicit surgery presentation of the
3
-manifold in question and the computation of modified quantum dimensions of projective
\overline{U}_{q}^{H}(\mathfrak{osp}(1|2))
-modules. The Verlinde formula is a key tool to establish the following result, which we state for
g\geq 2
.

Theorem B

(Corollaries
3.3
and
3.6
)
.

The Euler characteristic with respect to the parity subgroup
\mathbb{Z}/\penalty 2\mathbb{Z}
of
\mathsf{Z}
and total dimension of the state space
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{C}}(\Sigma_{g})
are given by

\chi(\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{C}}(\Sigma_{g}))=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{if }r\equiv 1\mod 2,\\
0&\mbox{if }r\equiv 2\mod 4,\\
\frac{r^{3g-3}}{2^{2g-3}}&\mbox{if }r\equiv 0\mod 8\end{cases}

and

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{C}}(\Sigma_{g})=r^{3g-3}\cdot\begin{cases}2^{2g-2}&\mbox{if }r\equiv 1\mod 2,\\
\frac{1}{2^{g-1}}&\mbox{if }r\equiv 2\mod 4,\\
\frac{1}{2^{2g-3}}&\mbox{if }r\equiv 0\mod 8.\end{cases}

The computation of the Euler characteristic follows by taking the limit
\beta\rightarrow 0
in the Verlinde formula. The total dimension is more difficult to access since it does not obviously arise as a limit of the Verlinde formula. To resolve this, we provide in Theorem
3.5
a combinatorially defined spanning set of
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{C}}(\Sigma_{g})
which, in particular, constrains the
\mathsf{Z}
-support of
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{C}}(\Sigma_{g})
. Using these support conditions, the total dimension is seen to arise as a limit of the Verlinde formula, leading to the above result.

Theorem D

(Theorem

Let

c^{\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)}_{\omega,\sigma(b,s)}=\frac{e^{\pi{\sqrt{-1}}\mu(M,s)}\mathcal{T}(M,[4\omega])}{|H_{1}(M;\mathbb{Z})|}\sum_{a,f}e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}\left(-\frac{r-\delta}{8}\ell k(a,a)-\ell k(a,b+f)+2\ell k(f,f)-\frac{1}{2}\omega(a)\right)}

•

and

Preliminary material

The ground field is

1.1.
Superalgebra

Our superalgebra conventions match
[
20
, I-Supersymmetry]
. The parity of a homogeneous element
v
of a super vector space
V=V_{\bar{0}}\oplus V_{\bar{1}}
is denoted
\bar{v}\in\mathbb{Z}/\penalty 2\mathbb{Z}
. Morphisms of super vector spaces are parity preserving linear maps. The tensor product of super vector spaces
V
and
W
is the tensor product of the underlying vector spaces with
\mathbb{Z}/\penalty 2\mathbb{Z}
-grading given by

(V\otimes W)_{\bar{p}}=\bigoplus_{\bar{a}+\bar{b}=\bar{p}}V_{\bar{a}}\otimes W_{\bar{b}}.

A (left) module over a (associative) superalgebra
A
is a super vector space
M
with an
A
-module structure for which the action map
A\otimes M\rightarrow M
is a morphism of super vector spaces. Given homogeneous elements
a,b\in A
, set
[a,b]=ab-(-1)^{\bar{a}\bar{b}}ba
.

1.2.
Monoidal categories

Our conventions for monoidal categories match
[
26
]
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-linear abelian monoidal category. We assume that the functor
\otimes:\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
is
\mathbb{C}
-bilinear, the monoidal unit
\mathbb{I}\in\mathcal{C}
is simple and the
\mathbb{C}
-algebra map
\mathbb{C}\to\textup{\text{End}}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbb{I}),k\mapsto k\cdot\textup{\text{id}}_{\mathbb{I}}
, is an isomorphism. If
\mathcal{C}
is in addition rigid, braided and has a compatible twist, then
\mathcal{C}
is called a
\mathbb{C}
-linear ribbon category
. The left and right duality structure maps are

\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longleftarrow}}{{\operatorname{ev}}}_{V}:V^{\vee}\otimes V\to\mathbb{I},\qquad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longleftarrow}}{{\operatorname{coev}}}_{V}:\mathbb{I}\to V\otimes V^{\vee}

and

\qquad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longrightarrow}}{{\operatorname{ev}}}_{V}:V\otimes V^{\vee}\to\mathbb{I},\qquad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longrightarrow}}{{\operatorname{coev}}}_{V}:\mathbb{I}\to V^{\vee}\otimes V,

respectively, the braiding is
c=\{c_{V,W}:V\otimes W\rightarrow W\otimes V\}_{V,W\in\mathcal{C}}
and the twist is
\theta=\{\theta_{V}:V\rightarrow V\}_{V\in\mathcal{C}}
. An object
V\in\mathcal{C}
is
regular
if
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longleftarrow}}{{\operatorname{ev}}}_{V}
is an epimorphism.

Relative modular categories

We recall a number of definitions from
[

14
,

. Let
\mathcal{C}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-linear abelian ribbon category.

Let
V,W\in\mathcal{C}
. Recall that right partial trace along
W
is the map

\displaystyle\operatorname{ptr}_{W}:\textup{\text{End}}_{\mathcal{C}}(V\otimes W)

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle\textup{\text{End}}_{\mathcal{C}}(V)

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle(\textup{\text{id}}_{V}\otimes\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longrightarrow}}{{\operatorname{ev}}}_{W})\circ(f\otimes\textup{\text{id}}_{W^{\vee}})\circ(\textup{\text{id}}_{V}\otimes\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longleftarrow}}{{\operatorname{coev}}}_{W}).

Definition 1.1
.

A full subcategory
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{C}
is an ideal if it has the following properties:

(1)

If
U\in\mathcal{I}
and
V\in\mathcal{C}
, then
U\otimes V\in\mathcal{I}
.

(2)

If
U\in\mathcal{I}
and
V\in\mathcal{C}
and there exist morphisms
f:V\to U
and
g:U\to V
satisfying
g\circ f=\textup{\text{id}}_{V}
, then
V\in\mathcal{I}
.

Definition 1.2
.

(1)

A
modified trace
on an ideal
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{C}
is a family of
\mathbb{C}
-linear functions

\mathsf{tr}=\{\mathsf{tr}_{V}:\textup{\text{End}}_{\mathcal{C}}(V)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\mid V\in\mathcal{I}\}
with the following properties:

(a)

Cyclicity
: For all
V,W\in\mathcal{I}
and
f\in\textup{\text{Hom}}_{\mathcal{C}}(W,V)
and
g\in\textup{\text{Hom}}_{\mathcal{C}}(V,W)
, there is an equality
\mathsf{tr}_{V}(f\circ g)=\mathsf{tr}_{W}(g\circ f)
.

(b)

Partial trace property
: For all
V\in\mathcal{I}
,
W\in\mathcal{C}
and
f\in\textup{\text{End}}_{\mathcal{C}}(V\otimes W)
, there is an equality
\mathsf{tr}_{V\otimes W}(f)=\mathsf{tr}_{V}(\operatorname{ptr}_{W}(f))
.

(2)

The
modified dimension
of
V\in\mathcal{I}
is
\mathsf{d}(V)=\mathsf{tr}_{V}(\textup{\text{id}}_{V})
.

Definition 1.3
.

(1)

A set
\mathcal{E}=\{V_{i}\mid i\in J\}
of objects of
\mathcal{C}
is
dominating
if for any
V\in\mathcal{C}
there exist indices
\{i_{1},\dots,i_{m}\}\subseteq J
and morphisms
\iota_{k}\in\textup{\text{Hom}}_{\mathcal{C}}(V_{i_{k}},V)
,
s_{k}\in\textup{\text{Hom}}_{\mathcal{C}}(V,V_{i_{k}})
such that
\textup{\text{id}}_{V}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\iota_{k}\circ s_{k}
.

(2)

A dominating set
\mathcal{E}
is
completely reduced
if
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\textup{\text{Hom}}_{\mathcal{C}}(V_{i},V_{j})=\delta_{ij}
for all
i,j\in J
.

Let
(\mathsf{Z},+)
be an abelian group. We often view
\mathsf{Z}
as a discrete monoidal category with object set
\mathsf{Z}
.

Definition 1.4
.

A
free realization of
\mathsf{Z}
in
\mathcal{C}
is a monoidal functor
\sigma:\mathsf{Z}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
,
k\mapsto\sigma_{k}
, such that

(1)

\sigma_{0}=\mathbb{I}
,

(2)

\theta_{\sigma_{k}}=\textup{\text{id}}_{\sigma_{k}}\text{ for all }k\in\mathsf{Z}
, and

(3)

if

for a simple object

, then

.

We often identify
\sigma:\mathsf{Z}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
with the set of objects
\sigma_{\mathsf{Z}}:=\{\sigma_{k}\mid k\in\mathsf{Z}\}
, omitting from the notation the monoidal coherence data.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
(\mathsf{G},+)
be an abelian group.

(1)

A subset
\mathsf{X}\subset\mathsf{G}
is
symmetric
if
\mathsf{X}=-\mathsf{X}
and

if

(2)

-linear abelian categories

(a)

.

(b)

(c)

(3)

A
\mathsf{G}

1.4.
Spin
and
\textnormal{Spin}^{\textnormal{c}}
structures on
3
-manifolds

Following
[
21
, §2.2]
, we recall basic topology of
3
-manifolds obtained by surgery on links in
S^{3}
.

Let
L\subset S^{3}
be a framed oriented link with connected components
V
and
|V|\times|V|
linking matrix
B
. Let
M
be the closed oriented
3
-manifold obtained by performing integral surgery on
L
. For
\mathsf{G}
an abelian group, there are canonical isomorphisms

(2)

H_{1}(M;\mathbb{Z})\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{V}/\penalty B\mathbb{Z}^{V}

and

(3)

H^{1}(M;\mathsf{G})\simeq\{\phi\in\mathsf{G}^{V}|B\phi=0\}.

Here we write
\mathbb{Z}^{V}
for the free abelian group on the set
V
and view its elements as column vectors. Throughout the paper we assume that
B
is non-singular, so that
M
is a rational homology sphere. In this case,
|\det B|=|H_{1}(M;\mathbb{Z})|
and, with respect to the isomorphism (
2
), the linking pairing is

\ell k:H_{1}(M;\mathbb{Z})\otimes H_{1}(M;\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}/\penalty\mathbb{Z},\qquad a\otimes b\mapsto a^{t}B^{-1}b\mod 1

where
(-)^{t}
is transposition.

Denote by
\textnormal{Spin}(M)
and
\textnormal{Spin}^{\textnormal{c}}(M)
the sets of equivalence classes of
Spin
and
\textnormal{Spin}^{\textnormal{c}}
structures on
M
, respectively. There are identifications

\textnormal{Spin}(M)=\{s\in\left(\mathbb{Z}/\penalty 2\mathbb{Z}\right)^{V}\mid\sum_{j\in V}B_{ij}s_{j}\equiv B_{ii}\mod 2\mbox{ for all }i\in V\}

and

\textnormal{Spin}^{\textnormal{c}}(M)=\{K\in\mathbb{Z}^{V}/\penalty 2B\mathbb{Z}^{V}\mid K_{i}\equiv B_{ii}\mod 2\mbox{ for all }i\in V\}.

There is a canonical surjective map

\sigma:H_{1}(M;\mathbb{Z})\times\textnormal{Spin}(M)\rightarrow\textnormal{Spin}^{\textnormal{c}}(M),\qquad\sigma(b,s)=2b+i(s),

where
i(s)=B\tilde{s}
for any lift
\tilde{s}\in\mathbb{Z}^{V}
of
s\in\left(\mathbb{Z}/\penalty 2\mathbb{Z}\right)^{V}
. Finally, given
s\in\textnormal{Spin}(M)
, the Rokhlin invariant
\mu(M,s)\in\mathbb{Z}/\penalty 16\mathbb{Z}
of
M
with spin structure
s
satisfies

(4)

\mu(M,s)\equiv\sigma-s^{t}Bs\mod 4,

where
\sigma
is the signature of
B
.

2.1.
Super quantum integers

We introduce super variants of the standard quantum integers. Our conventions differ slightly from those in the literature (
cf.

[
42
, §2]
,
[
12
, §2.5]
).

Let
v
be an indeterminate and
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{Z}[v,v^{-1}]
. For an integer
n\geq 0
, set

\llbracket n\rrbracket=\frac{v^{-n}-(-v)^{n}}{v+v^{-1}}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{n+1+i}v^{n-1-2i}\in\mathcal{A}

and

\llbracket n\rrbracket!=\begin{cases}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\llbracket i\rrbracket&\mbox{if }n\geq 1,\\
1&\mbox{if }n=0\end{cases},\qquad\left\llbracket\begin{matrix}n\\
k\end{matrix}\right\rrbracket=\begin{cases}\frac{\llbracket n\rrbracket!}{\llbracket n-k\rrbracket!\llbracket k\rrbracket!}&\mbox{if }0\leq k\leq n,\\
0&\mbox{if }k<0.\end{cases}

Lemma 2.1
.

For integers
1\leq k\leq n
, there are equalities

\left\llbracket\begin{matrix}n+1\\
k\end{matrix}\right\rrbracket=(-v)^{n-k+1}\left\llbracket\begin{matrix}n\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right\rrbracket+v^{-k}\left\llbracket\begin{matrix}n\\
k\end{matrix}\right\rrbracket,

\left\llbracket\begin{matrix}n+1\\
k\end{matrix}\right\rrbracket=v^{k-n-1}\left\llbracket\begin{matrix}n\\
k-1\end{matrix}\right\rrbracket+(-v)^{k}\left\llbracket\begin{matrix}n\\
k\end{matrix}\right\rrbracket.

Proof.

This is a direct verification.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

For any integer
n\geq 0
, there is an equality

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{\frac{k(k+1)}{2}}v^{k(n-1)}\left\llbracket\begin{matrix}n\\
k\end{matrix}\right\rrbracket=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if }n=0,\\
0&\mbox{if }n>0.\end{cases}

Proof.

The equality is proved using Lemma
2.1
and induction on
n
.
∎

2.2.
The quantum group of
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)

Definition 2.3
.

Let
U_{v}(\mathfrak{osp}(1|2))
be the unital superalgebra over
\mathbb{Q}(v)
with generators
K
,
K^{-1}
of parity
\bar{0}
and
E
,
F
of parity
\bar{1}
and relations

KK^{-1}=K^{-1}K=1,\qquad KE=v^{2}EK,\qquad KF=v^{-2}FK,\qquad[E,F]=\frac{K-K^{-1}}{v-v^{-1}}.

There is a unique Hopf superalgebra structure on
U_{v}(\mathfrak{osp}(1|2))
with counit
\epsilon
, coproduct
\Delta
and antipode
S
defined on generators by

\epsilon(K)=1,\qquad\epsilon(E)=\epsilon(F)=0,

\Delta(K)=K\otimes K,\qquad\Delta(E)=1\otimes E+E\otimes K,\qquad\Delta(F)=K^{-1}\otimes F+F\otimes 1,

S(K)=K^{-1},\qquad S(E)=-EK^{-1},\qquad S(F)=-KF.

For
i,n\in\mathbb{Z}
with
i\geq 1
, define elements of
U_{v}(\mathfrak{osp}(1|2))
by

\llbracket K;n\rrbracket=\frac{Kv^{n}-K^{-1}(-v)^{-n}}{v-v^{-1}},\qquad\left\llbracket\begin{matrix}K;n\\
i\end{matrix}\right\rrbracket=\prod_{j=1}^{i}\dfrac{\llbracket K;n+j-i\rrbracket}{\llbracket j\rrbracket}.

Set
\left\llbracket\begin{matrix}K;n\\
0\end{matrix}\right\rrbracket=1
. For an invertible scalar
z
, we define
\llbracket z;n\rrbracket
and
\left\llbracket\begin{matrix}z;n\\
i\end{matrix}\right\rrbracket
analogously. A direct computation shows that

(5)

\displaystyle(-1)^{k}\llbracket j\rrbracket\llbracket K;i+k\rrbracket+\llbracket k\rrbracket\llbracket K;i-j\rrbracket=\llbracket j+k\rrbracket\llbracket K;i\rrbracket

for all
i,j,k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Compare with
[
12
, Eqn. (2.8)]
.

Lemma 2.4

(
cf
.
[
12
, Lemma 2.8]
)
.

For each integer
n\geq 1
, there are equalities

(6)

[E,F^{n}]=\llbracket n\rrbracket F^{n-1}\llbracket K;-n+1\rrbracket,

(7)

[F,E^{n}]=(-1)^{n}\llbracket n\rrbracket E^{n-1}\llbracket K;n-1\rrbracket.

Proof.

We prove only equation (
6
). We proceed by induction on
n
. The case
n=1
is a defining relation of
U_{v}(\mathfrak{osp}(1|2))
. Assuming equation (
6
) holds for
n
, we compute

\displaystyle EF^{n+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n}F^{n}EF+\llbracket n\rrbracket F^{n-1}\llbracket K;-n+1\rrbracket F

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n+1}F^{n+1}E+(-1)^{n}F^{n}\llbracket K;0\rrbracket+\llbracket n\rrbracket F^{n}\llbracket v^{-2}K;-n+1\rrbracket

so that

[E,F^{n+1}]=F^{n}\left((-1)^{n}\frac{K-K^{-1}}{v-v^{-1}}+\frac{v^{-n}-(-1)^{n}v^{n}}{v+v^{-1}}\cdot\frac{Kv^{-n-1}+(-1)^{n}K^{-1}v^{n+1}}{v-v^{-1}}\right),

which is seen to equal
F^{n}\llbracket n+1\rrbracket\llbracket K;-(n+1)+1\rrbracket
by a direct calculation.
∎

Lemma 2.5

(
cf.

[
12
, Lemma 4.6]
)
.

For each integer
n\geq 1
, there are equalities

(8)

\Delta(E^{n})=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\left\llbracket\begin{matrix}n\\
j\end{matrix}\right\rrbracket(-v)^{j(n-j)}E^{j}\otimes E^{n-j}K^{j},

\Delta(F^{n})=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\left\llbracket\begin{matrix}n\\

Proof.

We prove equation (
8
) by induction on
n
. The case
n=1
holds by the definition of
\Delta
. Assuming equation (

) holds for
n
, we compute

\displaystyle\Delta(E^{n+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1\otimes E+E\otimes K)\sum_{j=0}^{n}\left\llbracket\begin{matrix}n\\
j\end{matrix}\right\rrbracket(-v)^{j(n-j)}E^{j}\otimes E^{n-j}K^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}\left\llbracket\begin{matrix}n\\
j\end{matrix}\right\rrbracket(-v)^{j(n-j)}(-1)^{j}E^{j}\otimes E^{n+1-j}K^{j}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=0}^{n}\left\llbracket\begin{matrix}n\\

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1\otimes E^{n+1}+E^{n+1}\otimes K^{n+1}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-v)^{j(n+1-j)}\left(\left\llbracket\begin{matrix}n\\

Definition 2.6
.

The

U_{q}^{H}(\mathfrak{osp}(1|2))
is the unital superalgebra over
\mathbb{C}
with generators
H
,
K
,
K^{-1}
of parity
\bar{0}

,

\bar{1}

Remark 2.7
.

The Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
is generated by vectors
J,X^{+},X^{-}
of parity
\bar{0}
and
\psi^{+},\psi^{-}
of parity
\bar{1}

[J,X^{\pm}]=\pm X^{\pm},\qquad[J,\psi^{\pm}]=\pm\frac{1}{2}\psi^{\pm},\qquad[X^{+},X^{-}]=2J,

[X^{\pm},\psi^{\pm}]=0,\qquad[X^{\pm},\psi^{\mp}]=-\psi^{\pm},\qquad[\psi^{\pm},\psi^{\pm}]=\pm 2X^{\pm},\qquad[\psi^{+},\psi^{-}]=2J.

In fact, the relations involving only
J
,
\psi^{\pm}
together with the super Jacobi identify already determine
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
. Setting
H=4J
,
E=\psi^{+}
and
F=2\psi^{-}
, this smaller set of relations becomes

[H,E]=2E,\qquad[H,F]=-2F,\qquad[E,F]=H.

The superalgebra
U_{q}^{H}(\mathfrak{osp}(1|2))
is the unrolled quantization of this presentation of
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
. This also explains Definition
2.3
, which agrees with
[
41
, Example 2.1]
but differs from
[
42
, §2]
and
[

, Definition 2.1]
. Our normalizations are chosen to ease comparison with the representation theory of the unrolled quantum group of
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
.

Give

, given in Section

Lemma 2.8
.

If
r=4
, then
\llbracket n\rrbracket\neq 0
for all
n\geq 1
. If
r\neq 4
, then the minimal positive integer
{{r}_{0}}
which satisfies
\llbracket{{r}_{0}}\rrbracket=0
is

{{r}_{0}}=\begin{cases}2r&\mbox{if }r\equiv 1\mod 2,\\
r&\mbox{if }r\equiv 2\mod 4,\\
\frac{r}{2}&\mbox{if }r\equiv 0\mod 8,\\
\frac{r}{4}&\mbox{if }r\equiv 4\mod 8.\end{cases}

Proof.

If
r=4
, then
\llbracket n\rrbracket=(-\sqrt{-1})^{n-1}n
. If
r\neq 4
, then the denominator of
\frac{q^{-n}-(-q)^{n}}{q+q^{-1}}
is non-zero so that
\llbracket n\rrbracket=0
if and only if the numerator vanishes. This is equivalent to setting
q^{2n}=(-1)^{n}
, which holds if and only if
n\in\frac{r}{4}(2\mathbb{Z}+p_{n})
where

is the parity of
n
. Note that if
n
is odd, then
r\equiv 0\mod 4
. We proceed in cases:

•

If
r\equiv 1\mod 2
, then
n
is even, so that
n\in\frac{r}{2}\mathbb{Z}
. It follows that
n=2r
is the minimal solution.

•

If
r\equiv 2\mod 4
, then
n
is even, so that
n\in\frac{r}{2}\mathbb{Z}
. It follows that
n=r
is the minimal solution.

•

If
r\equiv 0\mod 8
, then
r=8k
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}

. It follows that

(18)

f_{\bar{\gamma};\alpha,\beta}=d_{\alpha,\beta}\textup{\text{id}}_{\mathbb{C}^{H}_{(\frac{kr}{2},\bar{0})}}\otimes\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longleftarrow}}{{\operatorname{coev}}}_{V_{(\alpha,\bar{0})}}\circ\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longrightarrow}}{{\operatorname{ev}}}_{V_{(\alpha,\bar{0})}}

for some
d_{\alpha,\beta}\in\mathbb{C}
. The modified trace of the right hand side of equation (
18
) is
\pm d_{\alpha,\beta}\mathsf{d}(V_{(\alpha,\bar{0})})
and so vanishes precisely when
d_{\alpha,\beta}=0
. Computing as in the previous paragraph, we find the modified trace of the left hand side of equation (
18
) to be

\sum_{\delta\in\gamma+I_{r}}\mathsf{d}(V_{(\delta,\bar{0})})\mathsf{tr}_{V_{(\alpha+k,\bar{0})}\otimes V_{(\alpha,\bar{0})}^{\vee}}(f_{\delta;\alpha+k,\alpha})=-\mathsf{d}(V_{(\alpha,\bar{0})})q^{k\gamma}\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}q^{\frac{kr}{2}i}.

When
k=0
the sum over
i
is equal to
r
. When
k=1
we have
q^{\frac{r}{2}}=-1
and the sum over
i
vanishes. The constant
d_{\alpha,\beta}
therefore vanishes unless
\alpha=\beta
, in which case it is equal to
-r
. It follows that
\zeta=-r
.
∎

3.1.
Non-semisimple TFTs from relative modular categories

Fix a relative modular category
\mathcal{C}
. Let

be the category of decorated surfaces and their diffeomorphism classes of admissible decorated bordisms, as defined in
[

{\mathcal{S}}=(\Sigma,\{x_{i}\},\omega,\mathcal{L})
consisting of

•

a closed surface
\Sigma
with a choice
*
of basepoint for each connected component,

•

a finite set
\{x_{i}\}\subset\Sigma\setminus*
of oriented framed
\mathcal{C}
-coloured points,

•

a cohomology class

such that

is the degree of the colour of
x_{i}
, where
\mathfrak{m}_{i}
is the oriented boundary of a regular neighbourhood of
x_{i}
, and

•

a Lagrangian subspace
{\mathcal{L}}\subset H_{1}(\Sigma;\mathbb{R})
.

A morphism in
\mathsf{C}\mathsf{ob}^{\textnormal{ad}}_{\mathcal{C}}
is a tuple
\mathcal{M}=(M,T,\omega,m):{\mathcal{S}}_{1}\rightarrow{\mathcal{S}}_{2}
consisting of

•

a bordism
M:\Sigma_{1}\rightarrow\Sigma_{2}
,

•

a
\mathcal{C}
-coloured ribbon graph

whose colouring is compatible with those of the marked points of
{\mathcal{S}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{S}}_{2}
,

•

a cohomology class

which restricts to
\omega_{j}
on
\Sigma_{j}
,

of

has degree

, where

is an oriented meridian of
T_{c}
, and

•

an integer

, the

.

Moreover, it is required that

is

of

which is disjoint from
\Sigma_{1}
, at least one edge of

is coloured by a projective object of
\mathcal{C}
or there exists an embedded closed oriented curve

such that

. Disjoint union gives
\mathsf{C}\mathsf{ob}^{\textnormal{ad}}_{\mathcal{C}}

The unique pairing
\mathopen{\hbox{${\langle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\langle}$}}-,-\mathclose{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}:\mathsf{Z}\times\mathsf{Z}\rightarrow\{\pm 1\}
which makes the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma_{k_{1}}\otimes\sigma_{k_{2}}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma_{k_{2}+k_{1}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces c_{\sigma_{k_{1}},\sigma_{k_{2}}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\wr}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\wr}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathopen{\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\langle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\langle}$}}k_{1},k_{2}\mathclose{\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\rangle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\rangle}$}}\cdot\textup{\text{id}}_{\sigma_{k_{1}+k_{2}}}}

of

-graded vector spaces and their degree preserving linear maps.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
19
, Theorem 6.2]
)
.

A modular
\mathsf{G}
-category
\mathcal{C}
relative to
(\mathsf{Z},\mathsf{X})
together with a choice

of square root of the relative modularity parameter

defines a symmetric monoidal functor

\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{C}}:\mathsf{C}\mathsf{ob}^{\textnormal{ad}}_{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow\mathsf{V}\mathsf{ect}^{\mathsf{Z}\textnormal{-gr}}_{\mathbb{C}}.

We refer to
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{C}}
as the TFT associated to
\mathcal{C}
. The values of
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{C}}
on closed bordisms

coincide with a (renormalization of the)

-manifold invariants

of
[

]
; see Definition

below. The former values are defined as follows. Let

be a
\mathcal{C}
-coloured ribbon graph in

. Suppose that an edge of

is coloured by a generic simple object

. Let

be the
(1,1)
-ribbon graph obtained from

by cutting an edge labelled by

. Then

is an isotopy invariant of

[

. With this notation, the partition function of a closed admissible bordism
\mathcal{M}=(M,T,\omega,m)
with computable surgery presentation

is

(19)

\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{M})={\mathcal{D}}^{-1-l}\big(\frac{{\mathcal{D}}}{\Delta_{-}}\big)^{m-\sigma(L)}F^{\prime}_{\mathcal{C}}(L\cup T)\in\mathbb{C}.

Here

has

is the signature of the linking matrix of

and each component

of

We note for later use that the construction of

requires the choice of an element

and a simple projective

In the following sections we take
\mathcal{C}
to be one of the relative modular categories of Theorem

. The pairing
\mathopen{\hbox{${\langle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\langle}$}}-,-\mathclose{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}:\mathsf{Z}\times\mathsf{Z}\rightarrow\{\pm 1\}
is

\mathopen{\hbox{${\langle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\langle}$}}(k_{1},\bar{p}_{1}),(k_{2},\bar{p}_{2})\mathclose{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}=(-1)^{\bar{p}_{1}\bar{p}_{2}}.

The category
\mathcal{C}
is TFT finite in the sense of
[

, Definition 1.15]

is a Krull–Schmidt category whose isomorphism classes of indecomposable projectives are in bijection with isomorphism classes of simple objects. It follows from

3.2.
Verlinde formulae

Let
\mathcal{C}
be one of relative modular categories constructed in Theorem
2.31
. In this section we compute the value of
\mathcal{Z}:=\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{C}}
on trivial circle bundles over surfaces. Together with a combinatorial description of spanning sets of state spaces of
\mathcal{Z}
, this allows for the computation of Euler characteristics and total dimensions of state spaces. These results can be seen as Verlinde formulae for
\mathcal{Z}
.

Let
4
4

4

We omit the required Lagrangian subspace of

since it is not used in what follows. Similarly, we omit the signature defect required to define morphisms in
\mathsf{C}\mathsf{ob}^{\textnormal{ad}}_{\mathcal{C}}
.

{\mathcal{S}}=(\Sigma,\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}\},\omega)
be a decorated admissible connected surface of genus

. For each
\bar{\lambda}\in\mathsf{G}
, define the decorated

{\mathcal{S}}\times S^{1}_{\bar{\lambda}}=(\Sigma\times S^{1},T=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{s}\}\times S^{1},\omega\oplus\bar{\lambda}),

where

The partition function of
{\mathcal{S}}\times S^{1}_{\bar{\lambda}}
can be computed via an explicit surgery presentation, as in
[
4
, §5.3]
,
[

, §3.2]
. Writing

\mathcal{Z}({\mathcal{S}}\times S^{1}_{\bar{\lambda}})=(-1)^{\bar{p}}\zeta^{g-1}\sum_{k\in I_{r}}q^{\mu(\lambda+k)}\left(\frac{q^{{{r}_{0}}(\lambda+k)}-q^{-{{r}_{0}}(\lambda-k)}}{q^{\lambda+k}+q^{-\lambda-k}}\right)^{2g-2-s}.

\mathsf{Z}
-degree

Theorem 3.2

(Verlinde formula)
.

There is an equality

\mathcal{Z}({\mathcal{S}}\times S^{1}_{\bar{\lambda}})=\dim_{(q^{\varepsilon r\bar{\lambda}},1)}\mathcal{Z}({\mathcal{S}}).

Proof.

The equality can be proved by a direct modification of the proofs of
[
4
, Theorem 5.9]
and
[
34
, Theorem 3.5]
. The only change is the explicit form of the bicharacter
\psi

2.28
and
2.29
, which determines the appropriate specialization of the variables

and

.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

When
{\mathcal{S}}
has no marked points, that is,
s=0
, the Euler characteristic of
\mathcal{Z}({\mathcal{S}})
with respect to the parity subgroup is

\chi(\mathcal{Z}({\mathcal{S}}))=\begin{cases}{{r}^{\prime}}&\mbox{if }r\equiv 1\mod 2,\\
{{r}^{\prime}}&\mbox{if }r\equiv 2\mod 4,\\
r&\mbox{if }r\equiv 0\mod 8.\end{cases}

if
g=1
and

\chi(\mathcal{Z}({\mathcal{S}}))=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{if }r\equiv 1\mod 2,\\
0&\mbox{if }r\equiv 2\mod 4,\\
\frac{r^{3g-3}}{2^{2g-3}}&\mbox{if }r\equiv 0\mod 8\end{cases}

if
g\geq 2
.

Proof.

Theorem
3.2
gives

\chi(\mathcal{Z}({\mathcal{S}}))=\lim_{\bar{\lambda}\rightarrow 0}\mathcal{Z}({\mathcal{S}}\times S^{1}_{\bar{\lambda}}).

Since
q^{{{r}_{0}}}
is a sign (see equation (
9
)), when
s=0
equation (
20
) becomes

\mathcal{Z}({\mathcal{S}}\times S^{1}_{\bar{\lambda}})=\zeta^{g-1}\sum_{k\in I_{r}}\left(\frac{q^{{{r}_{0}}\lambda}-q^{-{{r}_{0}}\lambda}}{q^{\lambda+k}+q^{-\lambda-k}}\right)^{2g-2}.

When
g=1
, we obtain

\chi(\mathcal{Z}({\mathcal{S}}))=|I_{r}|=\begin{cases}{{r}^{\prime}}&\mbox{if }r\equiv 1\mod 2\mbox{ or }r\equiv 2\mod 4,\\
r&\mbox{if }r\equiv 0\mod 8.\end{cases}

Consider then the case
g\geq 2
. If
r\equiv 1\mod 2
or
r\equiv 2\mod 4
, then

(22)

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\frac{q^{{{r}_{0}}\lambda}-q^{-{{r}_{0}}\lambda}}{q^{\lambda+k}+q^{-\lambda-k}}

\chi(\mathcal{Z}({\mathcal{S}}))=0
. If instead

.
∎

Definition 3.4
.

A
colouring of
\widetilde{\Gamma}
of degree
k\in\mathsf{Z}
is a function
\mathsf{Col}:\textnormal{Edge}(\widetilde{\Gamma})\rightarrow\textnormal{Ob}(\mathcal{C})
such that

\mathsf{Col}(e_{-1}^{1})=V_{g_{0}}=\mathsf{Col}(e_{-1}^{-1}),\qquad\mathsf{Col}(e_{-1}^{0})=\sigma_{k}

and assigning to each of the remaining edges
e
a simple module
V_{(\alpha_{e}-{{r}_{0}}+1,\bar{p}_{e})}
, where
(\alpha_{e},\bar{p}_{e})\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{Z}/\penalty 2\mathbb{Z}
is congruent to
\omega(\mathfrak{m}_{e})\in\mathsf{G}
, such that the following
Balancing Condition
holds: at each trivalent vertex, the algebraic sum of the colours of incident edges is consistent with the isomorphism (
11
).

Fix a lift
\tilde{\omega}\in H^{1}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{Z}/\penalty 2\mathbb{Z})
of
\omega\in H^{1}(\Sigma;\mathsf{G})
and let

\mathfrak{C}_{k}=\{\mbox{degree $k$ colourings of $\widetilde{\Gamma}$}\mid\mathsf{Col}(e_{i})=V_{\tilde{\omega}(\mathfrak{m}_{e_{i}})+j}\mbox{ for some }j\in I_{r},\;i=1,\dots,g\}.

Set
\mathfrak{C}=\bigsqcup_{k\in\mathsf{Z}}\mathfrak{C}_{k}
. As explained in
[
34
, §1.6]
, each colouring
c\in\mathfrak{C}_{k}
defines a vector
v_{c}=(\tilde{\eta},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{c},\omega,0)\in\mathcal{Z}_{-k}({\mathcal{S}})
. The construction is based off the fact that
\widetilde{\Gamma}
is a modification of an oriented spine of a genus
g
handlebody with a open ball removed; the modification is required to construct vectors of non-zero degree.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}=(\Sigma,\omega)
be a decorated connected surface of genus
g\geq 1
without marked points. Assume that
2\omega
is not in the image of the canonical map
H^{1}(\Sigma;\mathsf{X})\rightarrow H^{1}(\Sigma;\mathsf{G})
and
\omega(\mathfrak{m}_{e_{1}})+g_{0}\not\in\mathsf{X}
. Then
\{v_{c}\mid c\in\mathfrak{C}_{k}\}
spans
\mathcal{Z}_{-k}({\mathcal{S}})
. Moreover,
\mathcal{Z}_{-k}({\mathcal{S}})
is trivial unless
k=(d,\bar{d})
for some
d\in\mathbb{Z}
which, if
r\equiv 0\mod 8
, must be even.

Proof.

A direct generalization of the argument from the proof of
[
34
, Theorems 3.3 and 4.1]
establishes the spanning statement; this uses the stated assumptions on
\omega
.

Next, we study the set
\mathfrak{C}
. Assume that
g\geq 2
; the slightly degenerate case
g=1
, where
e_{1}=f_{0}
, is dealt with similarly. An element of
\mathfrak{C}
can be constructed as follows. For each
i=1,\dots,g
, colour
e_{i}
by a simple module
V_{(\alpha_{i}-{{r}_{0}}+1,\bar{p}_{i})}
satisfying the constraint in the definition of
\mathfrak{C}_{k}
. Next, colour
f_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{2g-3}
recursively so as to satisfy the Balancing Conditions. The colours
\{V_{(\beta_{i}-{{r}_{0}}+1,\bar{q}_{i})}\}
of
\{f_{i}\}
are determined by
(\epsilon_{1},\dots,\epsilon_{2g-3})\in\{-i\mid 0\leq i\leq{{r}_{0}}-1\}^{\times 2g-3}
through the initial condition
\beta_{1}=\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}-2\epsilon_{1}
and the recursive system

\begin{cases}\beta_{2i}=\beta_{2i-1}+\alpha_{i+1}+2\epsilon_{2i}&\mbox{for }1\leq i\leq g-2,\\
\beta_{2i+1}+\alpha_{i+2}+2\epsilon_{2i+1}=\beta_{2i}&\mbox{for }1\leq i\leq g-2.\end{cases}

The solution is

\begin{cases}\beta_{2i}=\alpha_{1}+2\sum_{j=1}^{2i}(-1)^{j}2\epsilon_{j},&\mbox{for }1\leq i\leq g-2,\\
\beta_{2i+1}=\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{i+2}+2\sum_{j=1}^{2i+1}(-1)^{j}\epsilon_{j}&\mbox{for }1\leq i\leq g-2.\end{cases}

Similarly, we find for the parities

\begin{cases}\bar{q}_{2i}=\bar{p}_{1}+\sum_{j=1}^{2i}\bar{\epsilon}_{j}&\mbox{for }1\leq i\leq g-2,\\
\bar{q}_{2i+1}=\bar{p}_{1}+\bar{p}_{i+2}+\sum_{j=1}^{2i+1}\bar{\epsilon}_{j}&\mbox{for }1\leq i\leq g-2.\end{cases}

Writing
\sigma_{k}=\mathbb{C}^{H}_{(\varepsilon kr,\bar{p})}
, the colours of the edges
e_{g+1}
,
e_{g+1}^{\prime}
and
e_{1}^{\prime}
are subject only to the Balancing Conditions associated to the four top right vertices of
\widetilde{\Gamma}
:

(\alpha_{1},\bar{p}_{1})+g_{0}+2\epsilon^{\prime}=(\alpha_{1}^{\prime},\bar{p}_{1}^{\prime}),

(\alpha_{g+1}^{\prime},\bar{p}_{g+1}^{\prime})+(\varepsilon kr,\bar{p})=(\alpha_{1}^{\prime},\bar{p}_{1}^{\prime}),

(\alpha_{g+1}^{\prime},\bar{p}_{g+1}^{\prime})=(\alpha_{g+1},\bar{p}_{g+1})+g_{0}+2\epsilon^{\prime\prime},

(\alpha_{g+1},\bar{p}_{g+1})=(\alpha_{g},\bar{p}_{g})+(\beta_{2g-3},\bar{q}_{2g-3})+2\epsilon^{\prime\prime\prime}.

Here
\epsilon^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime\prime},\epsilon^{\prime\prime\prime}\in\{-i\mid 0\leq i\leq{{r}_{0}}-1\}
. These equations hold if and only if

\varepsilon kr=2\epsilon^{\prime}-2\epsilon^{\prime\prime}-2\epsilon^{\prime\prime\prime}-2\sum_{j=1}^{2g-3}(-1)^{j}\epsilon_{j},\qquad\bar{p}=\bar{\epsilon}^{\prime}+\bar{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime}+\bar{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime\prime}+\sum_{j=1}^{2g-3}\bar{\epsilon}_{j}.

and

\alpha_{g+1}=\alpha_{1}+2\epsilon^{\prime\prime\prime}+2\sum_{j=1}^{2g-3}(-1)^{j}\epsilon_{j},\qquad\bar{p}_{g+1}=\bar{p}_{1}+\bar{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime\prime}+\sum_{j=1}^{2g-3}\bar{\epsilon}_{j}.

Setting
d=\epsilon^{\prime}-\epsilon^{\prime\prime}-\epsilon^{\prime\prime\prime}-\sum_{j=1}^{2g-3}(-1)^{j}\epsilon_{j}
gives
(\varepsilon kr,\bar{p})=(2d,\bar{d})
. If
r\equiv 1\mod 2
or
r\equiv 2\mod 4
, then
\frac{\varepsilon r}{2}
is odd and we conclude that this colouring is of degree
(d,\bar{d})\in\mathsf{Z}
for some
d\in\mathbb{Z}
. If
r\equiv 0\mod 8
, then
\frac{r}{2}
is even so that
d
is even. It is immediate that the above construction recovers each element of
\mathfrak{C}
.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

Assume that
g\geq 2
. When
s=0
, the total dimension of
\mathcal{Z}({\mathcal{S}})
is

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{Z}({\mathcal{S}})=r^{3g-3}\cdot\begin{cases}2^{2g-2}&\mbox{if }r\equiv 1\mod 2,\\
\frac{1}{2^{g-1}}&\mbox{if }r\equiv 2\mod 4,\\
\frac{1}{2^{2g-3}}&\mbox{if }r\equiv 0\mod 8.\end{cases}

Proof.

When
r\equiv 0\mod 8
, Theorem
3.5
implies that
\mathcal{Z}({\mathcal{S}})
has even parity, whence
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{Z}({\mathcal{S}})
is equal to the Euler characteristic computed in Corollary
3.3
.

When
r\equiv 1\mod 2
or
r\equiv 2\mod 4
, Theorem
3.5
implies that the generating function of graded dimensions simplifies to

\dim_{(x,y)}\mathcal{Z}({\mathcal{S}})=\sum_{d\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{\bar{d}}\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{Z}_{(d,\bar{d})}({\mathcal{S}})x^{d}y^{\bar{d}}.

Since
\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}}q^{\varepsilon\lambda r}=-1
, Theorem
3.2
and equation (
21
) lead to the equality

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{Z}({\mathcal{S}})=\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}}\zeta^{g-1}\sum_{k\in I_{r}}\left(\frac{q^{{{r}_{0}}\lambda}-q^{-{{r}_{0}}\lambda}}{q^{\lambda+k}+q^{-\lambda-k}}\right)^{2g-2}.

We have
\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}}(q^{{{r}_{0}}\lambda}-q^{-{{r}_{0}}\lambda})=0
. Writing
k=-{{r}_{0}}+1+2n
and noting that
q^{{{r}_{0}}}=1
, we see that
\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}}(q^{\lambda+1+2n}+q^{-\lambda-1-2n})=0
if and only if
q^{4n+2+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}=-1
. The latter holds if and only if
4n+2+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\equiv\frac{r}{2}\mod r
. The unique element
-{{r}_{0}}+1+2n_{*}\in I_{r}
which satisfies the latter equation has

n_{*}=\begin{cases}\frac{5r-5}{8}&\mbox{if }r\equiv 1\mod 8,\\
\frac{3r+2}{8}&\mbox{if }r\equiv 2\mod 8,\\
\frac{7r-5}{8}&\mbox{if }r\equiv 3\mod 8,\\
\frac{r-5}{8}&\mbox{if }r\equiv 5\mod 8,\\
\frac{r-6}{8}&\mbox{if }r\equiv 6\mod 8,\\
\frac{3r-5}{8}&\mbox{if }r\equiv 7\mod 8.\end{cases}

An application of l’Hôpital’s rule now gives the claimed total dimension.
∎

4.
Comparison between
N_{r}^{\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)}
and
\widehat{Z}^{\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)}
-invariants

The goal of this section is to prove Theorems
C
and
D
from the Introduction.

4.1.
Plumbed
3
-manifolds

Let
\Gamma=(\Gamma,f)
be a plumbing graph, that is,
\Gamma
is a tree with (finite) sets
V(\Gamma)
and
E(\Gamma)
of vertices and edges, respectively, and vertex weighting
f:V(\Gamma)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
. We view
E(\Gamma)
as a collection of unordered pairs of distinct elements of
V(\Gamma)
. Let
B
be the
|V(\Gamma)|\times|V(\Gamma)|
-matrix with entries

B_{vv^{\prime}}=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if }\{v,v^{\prime}\}\in E(\Gamma),\\
f_{v}&\mbox{if }v=v^{\prime},\\
0&\mbox{else}.\end{cases}

We assume that
B
is invertible. Let
b_{+}
(resp.
b_{-}
) be the number of positive (resp. negative) eigenvalues of
B
and
\sigma=b_{+}-b_{-}
the signature of
B
. Note that
b_{+}+b_{-}=|V(\Gamma)|
. The number of edges incident to a vertex
v
is its degree
\deg(v)
. The plumbing graph
(\Gamma,f)
is called
weakly negative definite
if the restriction of
B^{-1}
to vertices of degree greater than two is negative definite
[
36
, §4.3]
.

Let
L\subset S^{3}
be the framed link with an unknot component for each vertex
v\in V(\Gamma)
with framing
f_{v}
and with distinct components
v
and
v^{\prime}
Hopf linked whenever
B_{vv^{\prime}}=1
. The linking matrix of
L
is therefore
B
. Let
M
be the closed oriented
3
-manifold obtained by integral surgery along
L
.

4.2.
\widehat{Z}
-invariants for
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)

Let
\Gamma
be a plumbing graph. Define formal power series

(23)

F^{\pm}(\{x_{v}\}_{v\in V(\Gamma)})=\prod_{v\in V(\Gamma)}\left(x_{v}\pm\frac{1}{x_{v}}\right)^{2-\deg v}\in\mathbb{C}\llbracket\{x_{v},x_{v}^{-1}\}_{v\in V(\Gamma)}\rrbracket.

Lemma 4.1
.

There is an equality
F^{-}(\{\sqrt{-1}x_{v}\}_{v\in V(\Gamma)})=-F^{+}(\{x_{v}\}_{v\in V(\Gamma)})
.

Proof.

We have

F^{-}(\{\sqrt{-1}x_{v}\}_{v\in V(\Gamma)})=\sqrt{-1}^{2|V(\Gamma)|-\sum_{v}\deg v}F^{+}(\{x_{v}\}_{v\in V(\Gamma)}).

Now use that, since
\Gamma
is a tree,
2|V(\Gamma)|-\sum_{v}\deg v=2
.
∎

Define complex numbers
\{F_{l}^{\pm}\}_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{V(\Gamma)}}
through the equality

F^{\pm}(\{x_{v}\}_{v\in V(\Gamma)})=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{V(\Gamma)}}F^{\pm}_{l}\prod_{v\in V(\Gamma)}x_{v}^{l_{v}}.

It is immediate that
F^{+}_{l}=0
unless
l\equiv\deg v\mod 2
, and similarly for
F^{-}_{l}
. Moreover, Lemma
4.1
gives

(24)

F^{+}_{l}=-\sqrt{-1}^{l^{t}\mathbf{1}}F^{-}_{l},\qquad l\in\mathbb{Z}^{V(\Gamma)}.

where
\mathbf{1}
denotes the column vector
(1,\dots,1)\in\mathbb{Z}^{V(\Gamma)}
.

Let
\mathbf{q}
be an indeterminate.

Definition 4.2

(
[
7
, §4.2]
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a weakly negative definite plumbing graph with linking matrix
B
and associated closed oriented
3
-manifold
M
. The
\widehat{Z}^{\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)}
-invariant of
M
with
\textnormal{Spin}^{\textnormal{c}}
structure
\mathfrak{s}
is

(25)

\widehat{Z}^{\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)}_{\mathfrak{s}}(M;\mathbf{q})=\mathbf{q}^{\frac{3\sigma-\operatorname{tr}B}{4}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\in\mathbb{Z}^{V(\Gamma)}\\
l\equiv\mathfrak{s}\mod 2B\mathbb{Z}^{V(\Gamma)}\end{subarray}}F^{+}_{l}\mathbf{q}^{-\frac{1}{4}l^{t}B^{-1}l}\in\mathbf{q}^{\Delta^{\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)}_{\mathfrak{s}}}\mathbb{Q}\llbracket\mathbf{q}\rrbracket[\mathbf{q}^{-1}]

for some scalar
\Delta^{\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)}_{\mathfrak{s}}\in\mathbb{Q}
.

Keeping the notation of Definition
4.2
, we recall from
[
38
, Appendix A]
that the
\widehat{Z}
-invariant for
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
is

(26)

\widehat{Z}^{\mathfrak{sl}(2)}_{\mathfrak{s}}(M;\mathbf{q})=(-1)^{b_{+}}\mathbf{q}^{\frac{3\sigma-\operatorname{tr}B}{4}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\in\mathbb{Z}^{V(\Gamma)}\\
l\equiv\mathfrak{s}\mod 2B\mathbb{Z}^{V(\Gamma)}\end{subarray}}F^{-}_{l}\mathbf{q}^{-\frac{1}{4}l^{t}B^{-1}l}\in\mathbf{q}^{\Delta^{\mathfrak{sl}(2)}_{\mathfrak{s}}}\mathbb{Q}\llbracket\mathbf{q}\rrbracket[\mathbf{q}^{-1}]

for some
\Delta^{\mathfrak{sl}(2)}_{\mathfrak{s}}\in\mathbb{Q}
.

Remark 4.3
.

(1)

Definition
4.2
generalizes the
\widehat{Z}^{\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)}
-invariants of
[
7
, Eqn. (79)]
from negative definite to weakly negative definite plumbing graphs. The additional overall factor of
2^{-|V(\Gamma)|}
which appears in
[
7
, Eqn. (79)]
is absorbed into the coefficients
F_{l}^{-}
used in this paper; compare equations (
23
) and
[
7
, Eqn. (80)]
. In contrast to
\widehat{Z}^{\mathfrak{sl}(2)}
, the series
\widehat{Z}^{\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)}
does not have a global factor of
(-1)^{b_{+}}
. Quite generally,
\widehat{Z}^{\mathfrak{g}}
for a simple (say) Lie superalgebra is expected to have a global factor of
(-1)^{|R_{+}|b_{+}}
, where
|R_{+}|
is the number of positive roots of
\mathfrak{g}
; see
[
51
, Def. 2.2]
and
[
27
, Eqn. (2.10)]
. In particular,
|R_{+}|
is equal to
1
and
2
for
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
and
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
, respectively, explaining the difference in global signs.

(2)

Weak negative definiteness of
\Gamma
ensures convergence of the
\mathbf{q}
-series
\widehat{Z}^{\mathfrak{sl}(2)}_{\mathfrak{s}}(M;\mathbf{q})
in the unit disk
\{\mathbf{q}\mid|\mathbf{q}|<1\}

[
36
]
. A direct modification of this argument applies to
\widehat{Z}^{\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)}_{\mathfrak{s}}(M;\mathbf{q})
.

(3)

That the
\mathbf{q}
-series (
25
) is independent of the plumbing presentation of
M
, and hence an invariant of the pair
(M,\mathfrak{s})
, can be verified by a direct modification of the calculation for
\widehat{Z}^{\mathfrak{sl}(2)}
given in
[
36
, Proposition 4.6]
. For example, the global factor
(-1)^{b_{+}}
appearing in equation (
26
) is required to cancel a sign which appears due to the oddness of the function
z-\frac{1}{z}
(seen as a factor of
F^{-}
) under the substitution
z\mapsto z^{-1}
. In the case of
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
we consider instead the function
z+\frac{1}{z}
(seen as a factor of
F^{+}
), which is even under the same substitution, and hence do not need to cancel a factor of
(-1)^{b_{+}}
.

4.3.
\widehat{Z}^{\mathfrak{sl}(2)}
versus
\widehat{Z}^{\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)}

The following result is motivated by physical calculations of Chauhan and Ramadevi
[
7
, §§4-5]
.

Theorem 4.4
.

For each
\textnormal{Spin}^{\textnormal{c}}
structure
\mathfrak{s}
on
M
, there exists a root of unity
c_{\mathfrak{s}}\in\mathbb{C}
whose order is divisible by
8|H_{1}(M;\mathbb{Z})|
such that

\widehat{Z}^{\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)}_{\mathfrak{s}}(M;-\mathbf{q})=c_{\mathfrak{s}}\widehat{Z}_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathfrak{sl}(2)}(M;\mathbf{q}).

Proof.

In the notation of Section
1.4
, write
\mathfrak{s}=\sigma(b,s)
for some
b\in H_{1}(M;\mathbb{Z})
and
s\in\textnormal{Spin}(M)
. Writing the summation index
l
in the definition of
\widehat{Z}^{\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)}_{\mathfrak{s}}
as
2b+B(s-\mathbf{1})+2Bk
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{V(\Gamma)}
and using equation (
24
) to replace
F_{l}^{+}
with
F_{l}^{-}
, we find

\widehat{Z}^{\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)}_{\sigma(b,s)}(M;\mathbf{q})=-\mathbf{q}^{\frac{3\sigma-\operatorname{tr}B}{4}-\frac{(2b+B(s-\mathbf{1}))^{t}B^{-1}(2b+B(s-\mathbf{1}))}{4}}\sqrt{-1}^{(2b+B(s-\mathbf{1}))^{t}\mathbf{1}}\\
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{V(\Gamma)}}(-1)^{k^{t}B\mathbf{1}}F^{-}_{l(k)}\mathbf{q}^{-k^{t}Bk-k^{t}(2b+B(s-\mathbf{1}))}.

The definitions give

k^{t}Bk+k^{t}Bs=2\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq|V(\Gamma)|}B_{ij}k_{i}k_{j}+\sum_{i=1}^{|V(\Gamma)|}B_{ii}k^{2}_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{|V(\Gamma)|}\big(\sum_{j=1}^{|V(\Gamma)|}B_{ij}s_{j}\big)k_{i}.

Since
\sum_{j=1}^{|V(\Gamma)|}B_{ij}s_{j}\equiv B_{ii}\mod 2
, we have
k^{t}Bk+k^{t}Bs\equiv 0\mod 2
, whence

-k^{t}Bk-k^{t}(2b+B(s-\mathbf{1}))\equiv k^{t}B\mathbf{1}\mod 2.

We conclude that

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{V(\Gamma)}}(-1)^{k^{t}B\mathbf{1}}F^{-}_{l(k)}\mathbf{q}^{-k^{t}Bk-k^{t}(2b+B(s-\mathbf{1}))}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{V(\Gamma)}}F^{-}_{l(k)}(-\mathbf{q})^{-k^{t}Bk-k^{t}(2b+B(s-\mathbf{1}))}.

Hence, we have

\widehat{Z}^{\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)}_{\sigma(b,s)}(M;\mathbf{q})=-\mathbf{q}^{\frac{3\sigma-\operatorname{tr}B}{4}-\frac{(2b+B(s-\mathbf{1}))^{t}B^{-1}(2b+B(s-\mathbf{1}))}{4}}\sqrt{-1}^{(2b+B(s-\mathbf{1}))^{t}\mathbf{1}}\\
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{V(\Gamma)}}F^{-}_{l(k)}(-\mathbf{q})^{-k^{t}Bk-k^{t}(2b+B(s-\mathbf{1}))}.

Making the same substitution as above for
l
in
\widehat{Z}^{\mathfrak{sl}(2)}_{\sigma(b,s)}(M;\mathbf{q})
, we see that we may take
c_{\sigma(b,s)}=e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}\tilde{c}_{\sigma(b,s)}}
, where

\tilde{c}_{\sigma(b,s)}=\frac{1+b_{+}}{2}+\frac{(2b+B(s-\mathbf{1}))^{t}\mathbf{1}}{4}+\frac{\delta}{2}\in\mathbb{Q}

with

\delta=\frac{3\sigma-\operatorname{tr}B}{4}-\frac{(2b+B(s-\mathbf{1}))^{t}B^{-1}(2b+B(s-\mathbf{1}))}{4}.

Explicitly, we have

\tilde{c}_{\sigma(b,s)}=\frac{4(1+b_{+})+3\sigma-\operatorname{tr}B-4b^{t}(s-\mathbf{1})-(s-\mathbf{1})^{t}B(s-\mathbf{1})}{8}-\frac{b^{t}B^{-1}b}{2}

Since the numerator of the first term is an integer
b^{t}B^{-1}b\in\frac{1}{\det B}\mathbb{Z}
and
|\det B|=|H_{1}(M;\mathbb{Z})|
, we conclude that
\tilde{c}_{\sigma(b,s)}\in\frac{1}{8|H_{1}(M;\mathbb{Z})|}\mathbb{Z}
.
∎

to

4.5.
Factorization when

We begin with the case

N_{r}^{\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)}(M,\omega)=\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B}\mathcal{C},

where

The existence of the factorization (

\frac{{{r}_{0}}}{r}=2
(resp.
\frac{{{r}_{0}}}{r}=1
) when

(resp.

by

Fix

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in I_{r}^{V(\Gamma)}}q^{\frac{1}{2}(\alpha+k)^{t}B(\alpha+k)+l^{t}(\alpha+k)}.

Defining

gives

\mathcal{C}_{l}=e^{\frac{\pi\sqrt{-1}}{r}\tilde{\alpha}^{t}B\tilde{\alpha}+\frac{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}{r}l^{t}\tilde{\alpha}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{V(\Gamma)}/\penalty{{r}^{\prime}}\mathbb{Z}^{V(\Gamma)}}e^{\frac{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}{{{r}^{\prime}}}n^{t}(\frac{2}{t}B)n+\frac{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}{{{r}^{\prime}}}\frac{2}{t}(l+B\tilde{\alpha})^{t}n}.

Recall that

and
{{r}^{\prime}}=\frac{r}{t}
. Applying Proposition
4.9
, we obtain

\mathcal{C}_{l}=e^{-\frac{\pi\sqrt{-1}}{r}l^{t}B^{-1}l}\frac{e^{\frac{\pi\sqrt{-1}\sigma}{4}}(\frac{r}{4})^{\frac{|V(\Gamma)|}{2}}}{|\det B|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{l},

where we have introduced

\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{l}=\sum_{\tilde{a}\in\mathbb{Z}^{V(\Gamma)}/\penalty\frac{4}{t}B\mathbb{Z}^{V(\Gamma)}}e^{-\frac{\pi\sqrt{-1}r}{4}\tilde{a}^{t}B^{-1}\tilde{a}-\pi\sqrt{-1}\tilde{a}^{t}B^{-1}(l+B\tilde{\alpha})}.

Setting
\tilde{a}=BA+a
for
A\in\mathbb{Z}^{V(\Gamma)}/\penalty\frac{4}{t}\mathbb{Z}^{V(\Gamma)}
and

, we have

\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{l}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in\mathbb{Z}^{V(\Gamma)}/\penalty B\mathbb{Z}^{V(\Gamma)}\\
A\in\mathbb{Z}^{V(\Gamma)}/\penalty\frac{4}{t}\mathbb{Z}^{V(\Gamma)}\end{subarray}}e^{-\frac{\pi\sqrt{-1}r}{4}A^{t}BA-\frac{\pi\sqrt{-1}r}{4}a^{t}B^{-1}a-\frac{\pi\sqrt{-1}r}{2}A^{t}a-\pi\sqrt{-1}A^{t}(l+B\tilde{\alpha})-\pi\sqrt{-1}a^{t}B^{-1}(l+B\tilde{\alpha})}.

Write

\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{l}=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}^{V(\Gamma)}/\penalty B\mathbb{Z}^{V(\Gamma)}}e^{-\frac{\pi\sqrt{-1}r}{4}a^{t}B^{-1}a-2\pi\sqrt{-1}a^{t}B^{-1}b-\pi\sqrt{-1}a^{t}(s+\alpha)}\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}_{l},

where we have set

\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}_{l}=\sum_{A\in\mathbb{Z}^{V(\Gamma)}/\penalty\frac{4}{t}\mathbb{Z}^{V(\Gamma)}}e^{-\frac{\pi\sqrt{-1}r}{4}A^{t}BA-\frac{\pi\sqrt{-1}r}{2}A^{t}a-\pi\sqrt{-1}A^{t}Bs}.

4.5.1.
The case

Suppose that

\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}_{l}=\sum_{A\in\mathbb{Z}^{V(\Gamma)}/\penalty 4\mathbb{Z}^{V(\Gamma)}}e^{\frac{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}{4}A^{t}(-\frac{\delta}{2}B)A+\frac{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}{4}(-\delta a-2Bs)^{t}A}.

Applying Proposition

\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{l}=\frac{e^{-\delta\frac{\pi\sqrt{-1}\sigma}{4}}2^{\frac{|V(\Gamma)|}{2}}}{|\det B|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\sum_{a,f\in\mathbb{Z}^{V(\Gamma)}/\penalty B\mathbb{Z}^{V(\Gamma)}}e^{-\frac{\pi\sqrt{-1}r}{4}a^{t}B^{-1}a-2\pi\sqrt{-1}a^{t}B^{-1}b-\pi\sqrt{-1}a^{t}\alpha}\cdot\\

4.9
, we obtain

\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{l}=\frac{e^{-\delta\frac{\pi\sqrt{-1}\sigma}{4}}2^{\frac{|V(\Gamma)|}{2}}}{|\det B|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\sum_{a,f\in\mathbb{Z}^{V(\Gamma)}/\penalty B\mathbb{Z}^{V(\Gamma)}}\\

On the canonical bundle formula in positive characteristic
Marta Benozzo

Abstract
. Let f : X → Z f\colon X\rightarrow Z be a fibration from a normal projective variety X X of dimension n n onto a normal curve Z Z over a perfect field of characteristic p > 0 p>0 . Let ( X , B ) (X,B) be a dlt pair such that the induced pair on a general fibre is log canonical. Assuming the LMMP and the existence of log resolutions in dimension ≤ n \leq n , we prove that, when K X + B K_{X}+B is f f -nef, the moduli part is nef up to a birational map Y ⇢ X Y\dashrightarrow X . As a corollary, we prove positivity of the moduli part in the K K -trivial case, i.e. when K X + B ∼ ℚ f ∗ L K_{X}+B\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}f^{*}L for some ℚ \mathbb{Q} -Cartier ℚ \mathbb{Q} -divisor L L on Z Z . In particular, consider a dlt pair ( X , B ) (X,B) of dimension 3 3 over a perfect field of characteristic p > 5 p>5 such that the induced pair on a general fibre is log canonical, then the canonical bundle formula holds unconditionally.

Introduction

The classification of varieties is one of the guiding problems in algebraic geometry.
An important tool that birational geometers use to this end is the study of positivity properties of the canonical divisor.
A natural question in the field is whether we can meaningfully relate the canonical divisors of the source and the target of a fibration.
The canonical bundle formula tackles this problem.
Kodaira’s result on elliptic fibrations is the first instance of a formula in this direction (see for example
[
21
, Theorem 8.2.1, Chapter 8]
).
It states that, given an elliptic fibration
f\colon X\rightarrow Z
from a normal projective surface over an algebraically closed field of characteristic
0
such that
K_{X}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}f^{*}L
for some
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor
L
on
Z
, then

K_{X}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}f^{*}(K_{Z}+B_{Z}+M_{Z}),

where
B_{Z}
is an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor which has an explicit description in terms of the singularities of the fibres, while
M_{Z}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor defined via the
j
-invariant of the fibres, a number that classifies elliptic curves.
More precisely, if
j\colon Z\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
is the map that generically sends
z\in Z
to the
j
-invariant of the elliptic curve
f^{-1}(z)
, then
M_{Z}
is a positive rational multiple of
j^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)
.
Over fields of positive characteristic, a similar formula holds with an additional term that takes into account the possible presence of
wild fibres
(
[
14
, Theorem 2]
).

Later, a similar formula was proven for
K
-trivial
fibrations in characteristic
0
.
If
(X,B)
is a log canonical pair and
f\colon X\rightarrow Z
is a fibration such that
K_{X}+B\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}f^{*}L
, for some
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
L
on
Z
, then we can write
L=K_{Z}+B_{Z}+M_{Z}
.
The divisor
B_{Z}
is defined according to the singularities of
f
, whereas
M_{Z}
measures how far the fibration is from being isotrivial, namely its
variation
.
In general, it is difficult to construct a moduli space for the fibres, so
M_{Z}
does not have an explicit description as in the elliptic curves case.
However, we can at least study whether it defines a meaningful map from
Z
.
The first step is looking at the positivity properties of
M_{Z}
.
These are essential to understand especially for inductive purposes: if we control
B_{Z}
and
M_{Z}
, we can infer properties of
X
from the study of
Z
, which is lower dimensional.
In this spirit, this formula has had many important applications in birational geometry, ranging from adjunction on log canonical centres (
[
40
]
,
[
25
]
), to finite generation of the log canonical ring (
[
30
, §5]
).
Using variation of Hodge structures, it is possible to show that, under further smoothness assumptions,
f_{*}\omega_{X/Z}
is
semipositive
(see
[
21
, §8.9]
for an account of the details).
Given this, in
[
40
]
,
[
30
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
21
]
, the authors proved that, up to a birational base change,
M_{Z}
is nef. Moreover, we can control the singularities of the induced pair on
Z
(
[
6
, Theorem 3.1]
).
Unfortunately, these techniques cannot be used over fields of positive characteristics. In fact, over any algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
, it is possible to construct fibrations
f\colon X\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
, where
X
is a smooth surface, called Moret–Bailly families, which give counterexamples to the positivity of
f_{*}\omega_{X/Z}
(
[
49
]
,
[
53
, Proposition 3.16]
).

With the development of the theory of
F
-singularities
, which take into account both the geometry and the arithmetic of the varieties, it was possible to prove a canonical bundle formula using more algebraic techniques.
Indeed, if we ask the pair induced on the generic fibre to be
globally
F
-split
(
[
25
, Definition 2.16]
), the splitting allows to descend effectiveness on the base. More precisely,
K_{X}+B\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}f^{*}(K_{Z}+\Delta_{Z})
, where
\Delta_{Z}
is an effective, canonically defined
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor (
[
25
, Theorem 5.2]
,
[
28
, Theorem 3.17]
).
Moreover, since moduli spaces of curves are well understood in any characteristic, when the fibration has relative dimension one we can exploit them to get the desired positivity (
[
18
, Lemma 6.6, Lemma 6.7]
,
[
60
, Theorem 3.2]
).
For this result we need to assume that the geometric generic fibre is smooth and the pair induced on it is log canonical, whereas in general the statement is known to be false (
[
60
, Example 3.5]
).
If only the generic fibre is log canonical, it is still possible to prove a weaker statement for fibrations of relative dimension one by considering purely inseparable covers of the base (
[
60
, Theorem 1]
,
[
20
, Theorem 6.2]
).

Recently, a new approach has been taken in characteristic
0
in the paper
[
5
]
.
The advantage is that this approach does not make use of variation of Hodge structures, but rather techniques coming from the minimal model program (MMP).
In order to have the necessary flexibility, the authors of
[
5
]
consider more general fibrations
f\colon X\to Z
, not only
K
-trivial ones. In this context, it is still possible to define a discriminant part
B_{Z}
on
Z
measuring the singularities of
f
and then the moduli part
M_{X}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor defined on
X
.
The problem is again to prove that the moduli part has some geometric meaning linked to the variation in moduli of the fibres. More specifically, the main result of the paper is that, given
f\colon X\to Z
a fibration between normal projective varieties over an algebraically closed field and
B
an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor such that the pair
(X,B)
is log canonical and
K_{X}+B
is
f
-nef, the moduli part
M_{X}
is nef, up to passing to a different birational model of the fibration (
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
).

Recently, there has been a lot of developments on the MMP in positive characteristic.
In particular, it has been proven that we can run the MMP in dimension at most
3
over fields of characteristic
p>5
(
[
36
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
38
]
), and we have partial results in low characteristics (
[
33
]
and
[
34
]
). Moreover, log resolutions have been constructed in dimension at most
3
.
In view of this, it is natural to ask whether the techniques in
[
5
]
could be adapted to the positive characteristic setting.

One of the main problems to follow the strategy in characteristic
0
is that, due to the failure of generic smoothness, it is not even possible to define a discriminant part of the fibration as all the fibres may be very singular.
The main result of this paper is that, assuming that the fibration has log canonical general fibres, a canonical bundle formula holds over perfect fields of positive characteristic when the base is a curve, conjecturally to the MMP.

Let

For fibrations satisfying Property
(\ast)
, over fields of characteristic
0
, we can compare the moduli part with the canonical divisor of the
foliation
induced by the fibration.
Over fields of characteristic
0
, to each fibration
f\colon X\to Z
between normal varieties, we can associate a foliation
\mathcal{F}
, defined as the saturation of the kernel of the differential map
df\colon T_{X}\to f^{*}T_{Z}
. If
f
is equidimensional, the canonical divisor of the foliation,
K_{\mathcal{F}}
, can be explicitly computed as
{K_{X}-f^{*}K_{Z}-R(f)}
, where
R(f)
is the

, supported on fibres that have multiplicity at least
2
.
The moduli part, under Property
(\ast)
assumptions, has a similar description.
On the other hand, over fields of positive characteristic, the foliation
\mathcal{F}
could behave very differently. For example, if
f
is not
separable
, the rank of
\mathcal{F}
is bigger than expected.
Moreover, even if we require the fibration

to be separable and to have normal general fibres, the divisor
K_{\mathcal{F}}
has a different description due to the presence of possible
wild components
, i.e. vertical divisors whose multiplicity is divisible by the characteristic.
All in all, we prove that, over perfect fields of positive characteristic,

K_{\mathcal{F}}=K_{X}-f^{*}K_{Z}-R(f)-W(f),

where
R(f)
is the ramification divisor defined as in the characteristic
0

W(f)
is an effective divisor supported on the wild components (see
Theorem 4.6
).
Therefore, even if the moduli part does not always coincide with the canonical divisor of the foliation, under Property
(\ast)
assumptions, we have a good control of the difference between the two.

In

5

[

Over fields of positive characteristic this latter result is known to fail (see
[

To overcome this issue, we exploit a correspondence between foliations and purely inseparable maps in positive characteristic (
[
50

2.

Singularities arise naturally in birational geometry because the class of smooth varieties is not stable under the birational operations of the Minimal Model Program.

3.
Singularities of fibrations

In characteristic
0
, given a fibration, it is automatic that the generic fibre is geometrically reduced.
In positive characteristic, this is no longer true and it is equivalent to asking that the fibration is
separable
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let

be a field extension. It is called

if there exists a transcendence basis

such that

is a finite separable extension of

Let

We say that

is

if the field extension

is separable; otherwise,

is called

The next lemma is well-known, we state it for completeness.

Lemma 3.2

(i)

be a field extension, then there is an intermediate field

such that

is a separable extension and

is a purely inseparable finite extension.

(ii)

Let

be a finite morphism of curves. Then there exists

such that
\varphi=F^{e}\circ\sigma=\sigma\circ F^{e}
. In this case

is said to be the

of

.

(iii)

be a normal projective variety with function field

and

.

Proof.

Point (i) follows from
[
56
, Tag 030K]
.
Point (ii) follows from

, Tag 0CD2]
and
[

.
As for point (iii): by (i) we can decompose the extension

(
[

, Proposition 2.15, Chapter 3]
)

In particular, a fibration

When

(

)

4.
Foliations and purely inseparable morphisms

4.1.
Foliations

Every separable fibration
f\colon X\to Z
defines a
foliation

\mathcal{F}
whose general leaves consist of the fibres of
f
and of some subvarieties on which
f
is inseparable.
Here, we give a formula for the canonical divisor of
\mathcal{F}
assuming that the general fibres are normal.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
X
be a normal variety.
A subsheaf
\mathcal{F}\subseteq T_{X}
is said to be
saturated
if the quotient
T_{X}/\mathcal{F}
is torsion free.
In characteristic
p>0
, if
\partial
is a derivation, then the composition of
p
copies of
\partial
as a function is also a derivation and it is called the
p
-power
of
\partial
.
A
foliation
on
X
is a subsheaf of the tangent sheaf,
\mathcal{F}\subseteq T_{X}
, which is saturated, closed under Lie brackets and under
p
-powers.

Let
\omega_{\mathcal{F}}:=\det(\mathcal{F})^{*}
be the dual of the reflexified top exterior power of
\mathcal{F}
.
The canonical divisor of a foliation
\mathcal{F}
is any Weil divisor
K_{\mathcal{F}}
such that
\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{\mathcal{F}})=\omega_{\mathcal{F}}
.

Separable fibrations naturally induce foliations by considering the relative tangent bundle.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
f\colon X\rightarrow Z
be a separable fibration between normal varieties. It induces a foliation
\mathcal{F}
as the saturation of the kernel of
df\colon T_{X}\to f^{*}T_{Z}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
f\colon X\to Z
be a separable flat fibration between normal varieties. Then, the kernel of
{df\colon T_{X}\to f^{*}T_{Z}}
is saturated in
T_{X}
.

Proof.

Note that
T_{X}/\ker(df)\subseteq f^{*}T_{Z}
. Since
Z
is normal,
T_{Z}
is torsion free and since
f
is flat, by
[
56
, Tag 0AXV]

f^{*}T_{Z}
is torsion free. Therefore,
T_{X}/\ker(df)
is torsion free as well.
∎

Definition 4.4
.

Let
f\colon X\rightarrow Z
be a separable equidimensional fibration between normal varieties.
Let
\delta\subset Z
be a prime divisor. If
f^{*}\delta=\sum\ell_{D}D
, with the sum running over the prime divisors

over
\delta
, then
\ell_{D}
is called the

with respect to
f
.
We define the

to be

where the first sum is taken over all prime divisors

of

, while the second sum is taken over all vertical prime divisors

on

and
\ell_{D}
is their multiplicity with respect to

.
If
p
divides

, we call

a

.
If

.

Remark 4.5
.

If

is an equidimensional morphism between normal varieties and

4.2.
Frobenius base change

Note that the differential of the Frobenius morphism is
0
. This easy observation plays a key role in the study of fibrations.
In fact, to each finite purely inseparable morphism corresponds a foliation and vice-versa.
In particular, given a fibration
f
, its induced foliation corresponds to the foliation induced by any power of the relative Frobenius.
Using this correspondence, we provide formulas to relate the canonical divisors obtained from a Frobenius base change of a fibration.
Later, we perform this base change to overcome two issues: the fact that in positive characteristic we do not have a Cone theorem for foliations (
Section 7
) and to deal with fibrations with normal, but non-log canonical fibres (
Section 6.4
).

Definition 4.10
.

Let

power. For all

we denote by

the
e^{\text{th}}
power of the absolute Frobenius.

Definition 4.11
.

Let
X
and

be schemes over a field of characteristic

.
A purely inseparable morphism

is called of

.

6.1.

M_{Z}:=L-(K_{Z}+B_{Z})
, where
B_{Z}
is the discriminant part of
(X/Z,B)
.
Then,
M_{Z}
is nef.

Proof.

Let
M_{X}=f^{*}M_{Z}
be the moduli part of
(X/Z,B)
.
Let
\mu\colon X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
be a
(\ast)
-modification as in
Theorem 6.6
.
Let
{f^{\prime}\colon X^{\prime}\rightarrow Z}
be the induced morphism and
B^{\prime}\geq 0
defined so that
K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{\prime}=\mu^{*}(K_{X}+B)
.
Let
B^{\prime}_{Z}
and
M^{\prime}
be respectively the discriminant and the moduli parts of
(X^{\prime}/Z,B^{\prime})
.
Then
M^{\prime}=\mu^{*}M_{X}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}f^{\prime*}L^{\prime}
, for some
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
L^{\prime}
on
Z
, so it is enough to show that
M^{\prime}
is nef.
There exists an effective vertical
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
G
such that, if
B^{*}:=B^{\prime}+G
,
(X^{\prime}/Z,B^{*})
satisfies Property
(\ast)
. Let
\Sigma_{Z}
be the discriminant part of
(X^{\prime}/Z,B^{*})
and
M^{*}
its moduli part.
Let
\gamma^{*}_{z}
be the log canonical threshold of
f^{\prime*}(z)
with respect to
(X^{\prime}/Z,B^{*})
and
\gamma^{\prime}_{z}
the one with respect to
(X^{\prime}/Z,B^{\prime})
, for
z\in Z
.
Note that
\gamma^{*}_{z}\leq\gamma^{\prime}_{z}
. In particular,
\Sigma_{Z}\geq B^{\prime}_{Z}
.

Now, define
B^{\prime\prime}:=B^{\prime}+f^{\prime*}(\Sigma_{Z}-B^{\prime}_{Z})
, so that
K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{\prime\prime}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}f^{\prime*}L^{\prime\prime}
, for some
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
L^{\prime\prime}
on
Z
. The discriminant part of
(X^{\prime}/Z,B^{\prime\prime})
is
\Sigma_{Z}
and its moduli part is
M^{\prime}
.
Note that
B^{\prime\prime}\leq B^{*}
and the difference is vertical.

It is possible that
K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{*}
is not
f^{\prime}
-nef, in which case we perform a
(K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{*})
-MMP over
Z
.
Let
\varphi\colon X^{\prime}\dashrightarrow Y
be the result of this MMP and
(Y,C)
the resulting pair, where
C:=\varphi_{*}B^{*}
. Note that this MMP cannot end with a Mori fibre space since
K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{\prime}
is
f^{\prime}
-nef.
Call
g\colon Y\rightarrow Z
the induced fibration and
M_{Y}
the moduli part of
(Y/Z,C)
.
Since
(X,B^{*})
is log canonical, so is
(Y,C)
and, by
Section 6.1
,
(Y/Z,C)
satisfies Property
(\ast)
and its discriminant part is
\Sigma_{Z}
.
Let
C^{\prime\prime}:=\varphi_{*}B^{\prime\prime}

K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{\prime\prime}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}f^{\prime*}L^{\prime\prime}
, the divisor
(K_{Y}+C)-(K_{Y}+C^{\prime\prime})

g
-nef. Moreover, by construction, it is effective and supported on a vertical
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\Phi
which does not contain any fibre. Let
\Psi
be an effective vertical
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor such that
\Phi+\Psi\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}g^{*}D
, for some
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D
on
Z
.
Then,
(K_{Y}+C)-(K_{Y}+C^{\prime\prime})\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}g^{*}D-\Psi

g
-nef, whence
\Phi=0
and
C=C^{\prime\prime}
.
The pair
(Y/Z,C)
satisfies Property
(\ast)
and
M_{Y}
is

-nef by construction.
Therefore,
M_{Y}
is nef by
Theorem 8.4
.

Now, we want to compare
M_{Y}
and
M^{\prime}
.
Let
\widetilde{X}
be a common resolution of
X^{\prime}\dashrightarrow Y
with
p\colon\widetilde{X}\rightarrow X^{\prime}
,
q\colon\widetilde{X}\rightarrow Y
the induced projections.
We have that both
p^{*}M^{\prime}-q^{*}M_{Y}
and
q^{*}M_{Y}-p^{*}M^{\prime}
are
q
-nef since

is
f^{\prime}
-trivial.
Moreover,
q_{*}(p^{*}M^{\prime}-q^{*}M_{Y})=\varphi_{*}B^{\prime\prime}-C=0=q_{*}(q^{*}M_{Y}-p^{*}M^{\prime})
.
Therefore, by the Negativity lemma (see
[
42

),
p^{*}M^{\prime}=q^{*}M_{Y}
, and it is nef, whence the conclusion.
∎

Abstract
. The large time behavior of the solutions to a multi-dimensional viscous conservation law is considered in this paper. It is shown that the solution time-asymptotically tends to the planar rarefaction wave if the initial perturbations are multi-dimensional periodic. The time-decay rate is also obtained. Moreover, a Gagliardo-Nirenberg type inequality is established in the domain ℝ × 𝕋 n − 1 ​ ( n ≥ 2 ) {\mathbb{R}}\times\mathbb{T}^{n-1}(n\geq 2) , where 𝕋 n − 1 \mathbb{T}^{n-1} is the n − 1 n-1 -dimensional torus.

1.
introduction

We are concerned with a scalar viscous conservation law, which reads in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
as

(1.1)

{}\partial_{t}u(x,t)+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{i}\left(f_{i}(u(x,t))\right)=\triangle u(x,t),\quad t>0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
u(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}},

\partial_{i}:=\partial_{x_{i}}~(i=1,2,..,n)
and
\triangle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{i}^{2};
the fluxes
f_{i}(u)~(i=1,2,..,n)
are smooth and
f_{1}^{\prime\prime}(u)\geq a_{0}
for some constant
a_{0}>0
.

It is well known
[
11
,
21
]
that the one-dimensional (1-d) conservation law, i.e. the right hand side of (
1.1
) is zero and
n=1,
has rich wave phenomena including shocks and rarefaction waves.
A centered rarefaction wave
u^{R}(x_{1},t)
is an entropy solution of the following Riemann problem

(1.2)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u^{R}(x_{1},t)+\partial_{1}f_{1}(u^{R}(x_{1},t))=0,\\
u^{R}(x_{1},0)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\bar{u}_{l},~x_{1}<0,\\
\bar{u}_{r},~x_{1}>0,\end{array}\right.\end{cases}

with
\bar{u}_{l}<\bar{u}_{r}
, and has an explicit formula as

(1.3)

u^{R}(x_{1},t)=\begin{cases}\bar{u}_{l},&x_{1}\leq f^{\prime}_{1}(\bar{u}_{l})t,\\
(f_{1}^{\prime})^{-1}(\frac{x_{1}}{t}),\quad&f^{\prime}_{1}(\bar{u}_{l})t<x_{1}\leq f^{\prime}_{1}(\bar{u}_{r})t,\\
\bar{u}_{r},&x_{1}>f^{\prime}_{1}(\bar{u}_{r})t.\end{cases}

It is shown in
[
7
,
12
,
24
,
10
]
that the rarefaction wave given in (
1.3
) is asymptotically stable for both the inviscid and viscous conservation laws in
L^{2}
framework provided that the initial perturbations are
L^{2}
integrable.
The analysis for the solutions of conservation laws with periodic initial data is important, cf.
[
11
,
3
,
1
,
2
,
25
,
26
,
27
]
.
In particular, the large time behavior of the nonlinear waves under periodic perturbations for viscous conservation laws is firstly
investigated in
[
25
]
with the aid of maximum principle.
The 1-d rarefaction wave
u^{R}(x_{1},t)
becomes the planar rarefaction wave in the multi-dimensional (m-d) case. The main purpose of this paper is to use the energy method to extend
the work of
[
25
]
to the m-d case, i.e.,
to show whether the planar rarefaction wave
is stable or not under m-d periodic perturbations.
Besides, we refer to

and the references therein for the other interesting works about the stability of shocks, rarefaction waves and contact discontinuities.

Now we formulate the main result. Since the centered rarefaction wave given in (
1.3
) is a weak solution and only Lipschitz continuous with respect to
x_{1},
we need to construct a viscous version of rarefaction wave
\tilde{u}^{R}(x_{1},t)
to replace the original one. Following
[
17
,
24
]
,
\tilde{u}^{R}(x_{1},t)
can be constructed as a smooth solution of the viscous conservation law (
1.1
), i.e.,

(1.4)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\tilde{u}^{R}+\partial_{1}(f_{1}(\tilde{u}^{R}))=\partial_{1}^{2}\tilde{u}^{R},&\\
\tilde{u}^{R}(x_{1},0)=\frac{\overline{u}_{l}+\overline{u}_{r}}{2}+\frac{\overline{u}_{r}-\overline{u}_{l}}{2}\frac{e^{x_{1}}-e^{-x_{1}}}{e^{x_{1}}+e^{-x_{1}}}.&\end{cases}

It is straightforward to check that

\lim_{x_{1}\to-\infty}\tilde{u}^{R}(x_{1},0)=\overline{u}_{l}<\overline{u}_{r}=\lim_{x_{1}\to+\infty}\tilde{u}^{R}(x_{1},0).

Consider the scalar equation (
1.1
) with the following initial data

(1.5)

u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)=\tilde{u}^{R}(x_{1},0)+w_{0}(x),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
w_{0}(x)
is a m-d periodic function defined on the n-d torus
\mathbb{T}^{n}=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}[0,1].
Without loss of generality (by adding the average constant onto
\overline{u}_{l}
and
\overline{u}_{r},
respectively), one can assume that

(1.6)

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}w_{0}(x)dx=0.

We are ready to state the main theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that the periodic perturbation
w_{0}(x)\in H^{\left[\frac{n}{2}\right]+2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
and satisfies
Equation

1.6
.

Then there exists a unique global smooth solution
u
of
Equation

1.1
,
Equation

1.5
satisfying

(1.7)

\left\lVert u(x,t)-\tilde{u}^{R}(x_{1},t)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{2}},\quad t>0,

where the constant
C>0
is independent of
t.

Remark 1.2
.

It is shown in

[
10
]
that any viscous rarefaction waves connecting same end states are time-asymptotically equivalent in the
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
space with the rate
t^{-\frac{1}{2}}.
We remark that the stability result
Equation

1.7
also holds true for more general initial values
\tilde{u}^{R}(x_{1},0)+v_{0}(x_{1})+w_{0}(x)
instead of
Equation

1.5
, where
v_{0}(x_{1})
is any 1-d function in
L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})\cap H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}).

Remark 1.3
.

Theorem
1.1
is the first work concerning the m-d periodic perturbations around the nonlinear waves for conservation laws, which shows that the oscillations in all directions around the planar rarefaction wave decay to zero with the rate
t^{-\frac{1}{2}},
even though the initial perturbations keep oscillating at infinity
\left\lvert x\right\rvert\rightarrow+\infty
. In other words, the oscillations are eliminated due to the genuine nonlinearity of the equation.

Let us outline the proof of
1.1
. Motivated by
[
24
]
and
[
10
]
in which the planar rarefaction waves are shown to be stable under m-d perturbations for the scalar equation
Equation

1.1
, we want to use the energy method to prove
1.1
.
However, the initial periodic perturbation
w_{0}(x)
is not integrable on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n},
and has no any limit at far fields. Thus, the effective energy method developed in the previous articles can not be applied here directly.
To overcome this difficulty, we construct a suitable ansatz
\tilde{u}(x,t)
which contains the oscillations in the
x_{1}
direction so that the oscillations in the difference between the solution
u
and the ansatz
\tilde{u}
is eliminated in the
x_{1}
direction, i.e.,
(u-\tilde{u})(x,t)\in L^{1}_{x_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}),
This is the key point in our proof. Then the stability with time-decay rates
Equation

1.7
can be obtained by the
L^{p}
energy method developed in
[
10
]
.

To prove
1.1
, we establish a Gagliardo-Nirenberg type inequality in the domain
\Omega:={\mathbb{R}}\times\mathbb{T}^{n-1}
, which is the second novelty in this paper. The Gagliardo-Nirenberg (GN) inequality
is very useful in the field of PDEs and usually holds in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
or in the domain with zero Dirichlet boundary condition. Let us recall the GN inequality which reads as, for any
1\leq p\leq+\infty
and integer
0\leq j<m
,

(1.8)

\left\lVert\nabla_{x}^{j}u\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\left\lVert\nabla_{x}^{m}u\right\rVert_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\theta}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{1-\theta},

where
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{j}{n}+\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{m}{n}\right)\theta+\frac{1}{q}\left(1-\theta\right)
,
\frac{j}{m}\leq\theta\leq 1
and
C
is a constant independent of
u
. However, the
n
-d GN inequality
Equation

1.8
does not hold in the domain
\Omega
generally owing to the following counterexample.

Counterexample:

It is noted that any 1-d function
0\neq f(x_{1})\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
is periodic in the
x_{i}
direction for
i=2,\cdots,n
(one cannot exclude the 1-d case since the initial perturbations satisfying
Equation

1.6
include the 1-d periodic functions). Then corresponding to the case
j=0,p=\frac{n}{n-1},\theta=m=r=1
in
Equation

1.8
, which is exactly the
n
-d Sobolev inequality, one can let
\zeta_{d}(x_{1})=f(d^{-1}x_{1}),

d>0,
and a direct computation implies that

(1.9)

\left\lVert\zeta_{d}\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{n}{n-1}}(\Omega)}=C_{n,d}\left\lVert\nabla_{x}\zeta_{d}\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega)},

where
C_{n,d}:=d^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{n}{n-1}}({\mathbb{R}})}/\left\lVert f^{\prime}\right\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}
. Since
C_{n,d}\to+\infty
as
d\rightarrow+\infty
, we conclude that in general the
n
-d GN inequality (
1.8
) is not true in
\Omega
without additional conditions.

Instead, based on a function-decomposition in
3.3
below, we establish a Gagliardo-Nirenberg type inequality on the domain
\Omega
.

Theorem 1.4

(GN type inequality on
\Omega={\mathbb{R}}\times\mathbb{T}^{n-1}
)
.

Let
u\in L^{q}(\Omega)
and
\nabla^{m}u\in L^{r}(\Omega)
where
1\leq q,r\leq+\infty
and
m\geq 1,
and
u
is periodic in the
x_{i}
direction for
i=2,\cdots,n.
Then there exists a decomposition
u(x)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}u^{(k)}(x)
such that each
u^{(k)}
satisfies the
k+1
-dimensional GN inequality
Equation

1.8
, i.e.,

(1.10)

\left\lVert\nabla^{j}u^{(k)}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\left\lVert\nabla^{m}u\right\rVert_{L^{r}(\Omega)}^{\theta_{k}}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{1-\theta_{k}},

for any
0\leq j<m
and
1\leq p\leq+\infty
satisfying
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{j}{k+1}+\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{m}{k+1}\right)\theta_{k}+\frac{1}{q}\left(1-\theta_{k}\right)
and
\frac{j}{m}\leq\theta_{k}\leq 1,

with the following exceptional cases,

1)

if
j=0,rm<k+1
and
q=+\infty,
additional assumption that
u\to 0
as
\left\lvert x_{1}\right\rvert\to+\infty
is required;

2)

if
1<r<+\infty
and
m-j-\frac{k+1}{r}
is a non-negative integer, additional assumption that
\theta_{k}<1
is required.

Hence, it holds that

(1.11)

\left\lVert\nabla^{j}u\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left\lVert\nabla^{m}u\right\rVert_{L^{r}(\Omega)}^{\theta_{k}}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{1-\theta_{k}},

where the constant
C>0
is independent of
u.

Remark 1.5
.

It is noted that the first term for
k=0
on the right-hand side (RHS) of
Equation

1.11
is necessary since
u
can be a 1-d function defined on
{\mathbb{R}}.

For example, considering the same function
\zeta_{d}(x_{1})
as in the counterexample, when corresponding to the case
j=0,p=r=2
and
m=q=1
in (
1.11
), a direct computation gives that

(1.12)

\left\lVert\zeta_{d}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=Cd^{\frac{1}{2}\left(3\theta-1\right)}\left\lVert\nabla\zeta_{d}\right\rVert^{\theta}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left\lVert\zeta_{d}\right\rVert^{1-\theta}_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\quad\text{for any }0\leq\theta\leq 1,

where
C>0
is independent of
d.
The estimate
Equation

1.11
can be true only for
\theta=\theta_{0}=\frac{1}{3}
. Otherwise, one can let
d\to 0+
if
\theta<\frac{1}{3}
and
d\to+\infty
if
\theta>\frac{1}{3},
respectively to get the contradiction.

An interesting interpolation inequality involving
\nabla^{2}u
in the domain
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}
was established in

23
]

2.
Preliminaries and Ansatz

Some properties of the viscous rarefaction wave are listed as follows.

Lemma 2.1
.

The viscous rarefaction wave
\tilde{u}^{R}(x_{1},t)
solving
Equation

1.4
satisfies

(2.1)

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\left\lVert\left(\tilde{u}^{R}-u^{R}\right)(\cdot,t)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})}=0.

Moreover, for any
t>0
it holds that

(2.2)

\displaystyle 0<\partial_{1}\tilde{u}^{R}(x_{1},t)\leq\min\left\{\frac{C}{t},~\max_{x_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}}\left(u_{0}^{R}\right)^{\prime}\right\}\qquad\forall x_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}},

(2.3)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left(\tilde{u}^{R}(\cdot,t)-\overline{u}_{l}\right)\left(\tilde{u}^{R}(\cdot,t)-\overline{u}_{r}\right)\right\rVert_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq C(1+t),

(2.4)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\partial_{1}\tilde{u}^{R}(\cdot,t)\right\rVert_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq Ct^{-1+\frac{1}{p}},\qquad p\in[1,+\infty),

where the constant
C>0
is independent of
t.

Proof.

The
L^{\infty}
time-asymptotically equivalence
Equation

2.1
has been proved in
[
7
,
10
]
.
By the maximum principles for
Equation

1.4
and the equation of the derivative
v=\partial_{1}\tilde{u}^{R},

(2.5)

\partial_{t}v-\partial_{1}^{2}v+f_{1}^{\prime\prime}(\tilde{u}^{R})v^{2}+f_{1}^{\prime}(\tilde{u}^{R})\partial_{1}v=0,

one can get that
\sup\limits_{x_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}},t\geq 0}\left\lvert\tilde{u}^{R}(x_{1},t)\right\rvert\leq\left\lVert u_{0}^{R}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})}
and
0<\partial_{1}\tilde{u}^{R}(x_{1},t)\leq\max\limits_{x_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}}\left(u_{0}^{R}\right)^{\prime}(x_{1}).

Besides, it was shown in
[
19
]
that the solution
\tilde{u}^{R}
of the 1-d convex conservation law
Equation

1.4
satisfies the well-known Oleǐnik entropy condition, i.e.,
\partial_{1}\tilde{u}^{R}(x_{1},t)\leq\frac{C}{t}
for all
x_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}},
where
C>0
is independent of
t.

Since
\overline{u}_{l}<\tilde{u}^{R}<\overline{u}_{r}
and both
\tilde{u}^{R}
and
\partial_{1}\tilde{u}^{R}
are uniformly bounded, it follows from
Equation

1.4
that

\frac{d}{dt}\left[\int_{-\infty}^{0}\left(\tilde{u}^{R}(x_{1},t)-\overline{u}_{l}\right)dx_{1}+\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(\overline{u}_{r}-\tilde{u}^{R}(x_{1},t)\right)dx_{1}\right]\leq C,\quad t>0,

which implies that

\int_{-\infty}^{0}\left(\tilde{u}^{R}(x_{1},t)-\overline{u}_{l}\right)dx_{1}+\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(\overline{u}_{r}-\tilde{u}^{R}(x_{1},t)\right)dx_{1}\leq C(1+t),\quad t>0,

and thus
Equation

2.3
holds true. And
Equation

2.4
can follow from

\left\lVert\partial_{1}\tilde{u}^{R}\right\rVert_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})}^{p}\leq\left\lVert\partial_{1}\tilde{u}^{R}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})}^{p-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{1}\tilde{u}^{R}(x_{1},t)dx_{1}\leq Ct^{-p+1}.

∎

Now we construct the ansatz. Set

(2.6)

g(x_{1},t):=\frac{\tilde{u}^{R}(x_{1},t)-\overline{u}_{l}}{\overline{u}_{r}-\overline{u}_{l}},\quad x_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}},~t\geq 0,

which is smooth, belongs to the interval
(0,1)
and satisfies
\partial_{1}g(x_{1},t)>0
for any
x_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}},t\geq 0.

Let
u_{l}(x,t)
and
u_{r}(x,t)
denote the two periodic solutions of (
1.1
) (see
[
20
]
for the global existence) with the respective periodic data

(2.7)

u_{l}(x,0)=\overline{u}_{l}+w_{0}(x)\quad\text{ and}\quad u_{r}(x,0)=\overline{u}_{r}+w_{0}(x),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

And define

(2.8)

w_{i}(x,t):=u_{i}(x,t)-\overline{u}_{i},\quad i=l\text{ or }r,

which is a periodic function with zero average for any
t\geq 0.
Then we have

Lemma 2.2
.

If
w_{0}(x)\in H^{\left[\frac{n}{2}\right]+2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
satisfies
Equation

1.6
, then for
i=l
or
r,
it holds that

(2.9)

\left\lVert w_{i}(\cdot,t)\right\rVert_{W^{1,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C\left\lVert w_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{\left[\frac{n}{2}\right]+2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}e^{-2\alpha t},\quad t\geq 0,

where the constants
\alpha>0
and
C>0
are independent of
t.

Proof.

The proof of
2.2
is based on basic
L^{2}
energy estimates and the Poincáre inequality on
\mathbb{T}^{n},
which is standard and thus omitted.
∎

We are ready to construct the ansatz as follows:

(2.10)

\tilde{u}(x,t):=u_{l}(x,t)\left(1-g(x_{1},t)\right)+u_{r}(x,t)g(x_{1},t),

which is periodic in the
x_{i}
direction for
i=2,\cdots,n.

By direct calculations, the source term induced by the ansatz
\tilde{u}(x,t)
is given as follows.

\displaystyle h(x,t):=~

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{u}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{i}\left(f_{i}(\tilde{u}(x,t))\right)-\triangle\tilde{u}(x,t)

(2.11)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\left(u_{r}-u_{l}\right)g(1-g)\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left[\sigma_{i}(u_{l},\tilde{u})\partial_{i}w_{l}-\sigma_{i}(u_{r},\tilde{u})\partial_{i}w_{r}\right]

\displaystyle+\left(u_{r}-u_{l}\right)\sigma_{1}(\tilde{u}^{R},\tilde{u})\left(\tilde{u}-\tilde{u}^{R}\right)\partial_{1}g-2\partial_{1}\left(w_{r}-w_{l}\right)\partial_{1}g,

where
\sigma_{i}(u,v):=\int_{0}^{1}f_{i}^{\prime\prime}(v+\theta(u-v))d\theta,~i=1,\cdots,n.
Thus, the source term
h(x,t)
is also periodic in the
x_{i}
direction for
i=2,\cdots,n
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Under the assumptions of
2.2
, it holds that for any
p\in[1,+\infty],

(2.12)

\left\lVert h(\cdot,t)\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\left\lVert w_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{\left[\frac{n}{2}\right]+2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}e^{-\alpha t},\quad t\geq 0,

where
\alpha>0
is the constant in
2.2
, and
C>0
is independent of
t.

Proof.

By
Equation

2.3
,
Equation

2.4
and
2.2
, it is straightforward to check that
Equation

2.12
holds for
p=1
and
+\infty
. And for
p\in(1,+\infty),

Equation

2.12
follows from the interpolation
\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\leq\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{p}}.

∎

3.
Interpolation inequality on
\Omega=\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{n-1}

Although the
n
-d GN inequality (
1.8
) does not hold in the domain
\Omega={\mathbb{R}}\times\mathbb{T}^{n-1}
generally, it is true with an additional condition, that is,

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
u\in L^{q}(\Omega),
and its derivatives of order
m,

\nabla^{m}u\in L^{r}(\Omega),
where
m\geq 1
and
1\leq r,q\leq+\infty.
And assume that
u
is periodic in the
x_{i}
direction and satisfies

(3.1)

\int_{\mathbb{T}}u(x)dx_{i}=0\quad\text{ for all }i=2,3,\cdots,n.

Then the
n
-d GN inequality (
1.8
) holds true for
u
.

Proof.

Following
[
18
, Lecture II]
, it suffices to prove the following two extreme cases.

1)

For a.e.
x\in\Omega,
it holds that

(3.2)

\left\lvert u(x)\right\rvert^{n}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\lvert\partial_{1}u\right\rvert dx_{1}\prod_{i=2}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left\lvert\partial_{i}u\right\rvert dx_{i}.

2)

For any
1\leq i\leq n,
and
1\leq q<+\infty,1<r<+\infty,
it holds that

(3.3)

\int\left\lvert\partial_{i}u\right\rvert^{p}dx_{i}\leq C\left(\int\left\lvert\partial_{i}^{2}u\right\rvert^{r}dx_{i}\right)^{\frac{p}{2r}}\left(\int\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{q}dx_{i}\right)^{\frac{p}{2q}},

where
\frac{2}{p}=\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{q},
and
``\int"
indicates the integral over
{\mathbb{R}}
if
i=1
or
\mathbb{T}
if
i>1.

Thanks to
Equation

3.1
, it is straightforward to show
Equation

3.2
. And
Equation

3.3
for
i=1
has been verified in
[
18
]
. It remains to show this inequality on
\mathbb{T}=[0,1]
for
i>1.

In fact, for any bounded interval
I\subset{\mathbb{R}},
it follows from
[
18
, (2.7)]
that

(3.4)

\int_{I}\left\lvert\partial_{i}u\right\rvert^{p}dx_{i}\leq C\left\lvert I\right\rvert^{1+p-\frac{p}{r}}\left(\int_{I}\left\lvert\partial_{i}^{2}u\right\rvert^{r}dx_{i}\right)^{\frac{p}{r}}+C\left\lvert I\right\rvert^{-\left(1+p-\frac{p}{r}\right)}\left(\int_{I}\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{q}dx_{i}\right)^{\frac{p}{q}},

where
C>0
is independent of
I.
Since
u
is periodic in the
x_{i}
direction with period
1,
the first term on the RHS of
Equation

3.4
must be greater than the second one, as long as the length of
I
is equal to a large integer
N.

Similar to
[
18
, (2.8)]

Corollary 3.5

(Interpolation Inequality in
\Omega
)
.

For any
2\leq p<\infty
and
1\leq q\leq p,
it holds that

(3.14)

\displaystyle\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\left\lVert\nabla(\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{\frac{p}{2}})\right\rVert^{\frac{2\gamma_{k}}{1+\gamma_{k}p}}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left\lVert u\right\rVert^{\frac{1}{1+\gamma_{k}p}}_{L^{q}(\Omega)},

where
\gamma_{k}=\frac{k+1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}\right)
and the constant
C=C(p,q,n)>0
is independent of
u.

Proof.

For the function
v(x)=\left\lvert u(x)\right\rvert^{\frac{p}{2}},
it follows from
1.4
that

\displaystyle\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\left\lVert\nabla v\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\theta_{k}}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2r_{k}}(\Omega)}^{1-\theta_{k}},

where
\frac{1}{2}=\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{k+1}\right)\theta_{k}+\frac{1}{2r_{k}}\left(1-\theta_{k}\right)
with
\frac{1}{2}\leq r_{k}\leq 1
and
0\leq\theta_{k}\leq 1
for
k=0,1,\cdots,n-1.
This yields that

(3.15)

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\left\lVert\nabla\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{\frac{p}{2}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{2}{p}\theta_{k}}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{pr_{k}}(\Omega)}^{1-\theta_{k}}.

If
2\leq p\leq 2q,
choosing
r_{k}=\frac{q}{p}\geq\frac{1}{2}
for
k=0,\cdots,n-1
can finish the proof.
If
p>2q,
let
\frac{1}{2}<r_{k}<1,
which will be determined later. Since
q<pr_{k}<p,
it follows from interpolation that

(3.16)

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{pr_{k}}(\Omega)}\leq\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{1-\rho_{k}}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{\rho_{k}},

where
\rho_{k}=\frac{pr_{k}-q}{(p-q)r_{k}}\in(0,1).
Plugging
Equation

3.16
into
Equation

3.15
yields that

(3.17)

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\left\lVert\nabla\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{\frac{p}{2}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{2}{p}\theta_{k}}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{(1-\rho_{k})(1-\theta_{k})}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{\rho_{k}(1-\theta_{k})}.

For
k=0,1,\cdots,n-1
with
n\geq 2
and
r_{k}\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right),
one can obtain by simple calculations that

(3.18)

\rho_{k}(1-\theta_{k})=\frac{p}{p-q}\times\frac{r_{k}-\frac{q}{p}}{\frac{k+1}{2}-\frac{k-1}{2}r_{k}}\quad\in\left(\frac{2(p-2q)}{(k+3)(p-q)},1\right).

Thus, for any
\delta\in\left(\frac{2(p-2q)}{3(p-q)},1\right),
there exist
r_{0},r_{1},\cdots,r_{n-1}\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right)
such that
\rho_{k}(1-\theta_{k})=\delta
for any
k=0,1,\cdots,n-1.
Then
Equation

3.17
yields that

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\left\lVert\nabla\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{\frac{p}{2}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{2\theta_{k}}{p(1-\delta)}}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{\frac{(1-\rho_{k})(1-\theta_{k})}{1-\delta}}.

A direct calculation implies that
\frac{2\theta_{k}}{p(1-\delta)}=\frac{2\gamma_{k}}{1+\gamma_{k}p}
and
\frac{(1-\rho_{k})(1-\theta_{k})}{1-\delta}=\frac{1}{1+\gamma_{k}p}
for all
k=0,\cdots,n-1,
where
\gamma_{k}=\frac{k+1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}\right)
, and the proof is completed.

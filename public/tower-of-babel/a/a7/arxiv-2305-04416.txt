Threshold resummation for computing large- x x parton distribution through large-momentum effective theory
Xiangdong Ji, Yizhuang Liu, Yushan Su

Abstract
Parton distribution functions (PDFs) at large x x are poorly constrained by high-energy experimental data, but extremely important for probing physics beyond standard model at colliders. We study the calculation of PDFs at large- x x through large-momentum P z P^{z} expansion of the lattice quasi PDFs. Similar to deep-inelastic scattering, there are two distinct perturbative scales in the threshold limit where the matching coefficient can be factorized into a space-like jet function at scale P z ​ | 1 − y | P^{z}|1-y| and a pair of heavy-light Sudakov form factors at scale P z P^{z} . The matching formula allows us to derive a full renormalization group resummation of large threshold logarithms, and the result is consistent with the known calculation to the next-to-next to leading order (NNLO). This paves the way for direct large- x x PDFs calculations in lattice QCD. As by-products, we find that the space-like jet function is related to a time-like version calculated previously through analytic continuation, and the heavy-light Sudakov form factor, calculated here to NNLO, is a universal object appearing as well in the large momentum expansion of quasi transverse-momentum-dependent PDFs and quasi wave-function amplitudes.

Space-time picture of the threshold limit and the space-like heavy-quark jet function

In this section we study the space-like jet function, a crucial object in order to resum the large threshold logarithm. Our convention for the light-cone plus vector is
n=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1,1,0,0)
and the space-like vector is
n_{z}=(0,1,0,0)
. In order to be pedagogical we first provide a brief introduction to the threshold factorization of DIS induced by an incoming quark with four-momenta
p=(p^{+},0,0,0)
and a virtual photon with four-momenta
q=(-x_{B}p^{+},x_{B}p^{+},0,0)
. As usual,
-q^{2}=Q^{2}=2x_{B}^{2}(p^{+})^{2}
is large and
x_{B}=-\frac{q^{2}}{2P\cdot q}
is the standard Bjorken
x
.

Unlike the collinear factorization of DIS at a generic

Sterman 1987
;
Catani and Trentadue 1989
;
Bauer et al. 2002a
;
Manohar 2003
;

;

;

On the other hand, the final state jet, consisting of the anti-collinear quark and all the real “soft gluon emmisions” has a new perturbative scale

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\rm QCD}^{2}\ll\mu_{i,{\rm DIS}}^{2}=(p+Q)^{2}=(1-x_{B})Q^{2}\ll Q^{2}\ ,

(11)

;
Korchemsky and Marchesini 1993
;

;
Becher et al. 2007
;
Becher and Neubert 2006

Sterman 1987
;
Korchemsky and Marchesini 1993
;
Becher et al. 2007
;
Becher and Neubert 2006

;
Ji et al. 2005
;

;

\displaystyle W_{n,-}(x)=P\exp\bigg[ig\int_{-\infty}^{0}dsn\cdot A(x+sn)\bigg]\ ,

\displaystyle W_{z}(x)=P\exp\bigg[ig\int_{0}^{\infty}dsn_{z}\cdot A(x+sn_{z})\bigg]\ ,

Notice that the space-like jet function depends only on

and is real. This can be argued to all orders in the following way. In the time-ordered perturbation theory, the incoming and outgoing light-like gauge links are represented as heavy quarks travelling at speed of light with dispersion relation
E=k^{z}
, as a result, all the energy denominators are of the same sign and can not produce any imaginary part. The only source for imaginary part is therefore the gauge link in
z
direction, represented as a static operator at
t=0
. One can see immediately that flipping the direction of gauge link simply changes the sign of the imaginary part. However, for
z>0
the Wilson-loop in Eq. (
17
) contains only space-like and light-like separations and is below threshold, therefore real. This implies that for
z<0
the result is also real. This argument is similar to that given in Appendix A of Ref.
Ji and Liu 2022
for imaginary part of quasi-LFWF amplitudes.

Similar to the time-like heavy-quark jet function in Ref.
Jain et al. 2008
, the space-like jet function in coordinate space satisfies a simple renormalization group equation

\displaystyle\frac{d\ln\tilde{J}(\mu^{2}z^{2})}{d\ln\mu}=\Gamma_{\rm cusp}(\alpha_{s})l_{z}-\tilde{\gamma}_{J}(\alpha_{s})\ .

(21)

The
\Gamma_{\rm cusp}(\alpha_{s})
is the standard light-like cusp-anomalous dimension
Korchemsky and Radyushkin 1987
and is known up to four-loops

;
von Manteuffel et al. 2020

is the same as the anomalous dimension for the “heavy-quark jet function” in Ref.
Jain et al. 2008
. In fact, it can be expressed in terms of the UV anomalous dimension
-2\gamma_{HL}
of a heavy-light Wilson-line cusp originally calculated in Ref.
Korchemsky and Marchesini 1993
and the “soft-anomalous dimension”
\gamma_{s}
for a light-light Wilson-line cusp
Korchemskaya and Korchemsky 1992

One can check that

\displaystyle\tilde{\gamma}_{J}=2\gamma_{HL}-2\gamma_{s}\ .

(22)

Notice that the soft anomalous dimension
\gamma_{s}
comes from the implicit “zero-bin subtraction”
Becher et al. 2007
;
Manohar and Stewart 2007
;
Jain et al. 2008
when calculating the jet function. Given these, it is easy to show that

\displaystyle\tilde{\gamma}_{J}(\alpha_{s})=-\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{\pi}+\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\right)^{2}\bigg[C_{F}C_{A}\left(-\frac{1396}{27}+\frac{23\pi^{2}}{9}+20\zeta_{3}\right)+C_{F}n_{f}\left(\frac{232}{27}-\frac{2\pi^{2}}{9}\right)\bigg]\ .

(23)

As expected, the above reproduces the same anomalous dimension for the heavy-quark jet function defined through time-like Wilson lines in Ref.
Jain et al. 2008
.

\displaystyle=\delta(1-y)+\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{2\pi}\bigg[{\cal P}\bigg(\frac{\ln(1-y)^{2}}{|1-y|}\bigg)+(L_{z}-1){\cal P}\left(\frac{1}{|1-y|}\right)+\delta(y-1)\bigg(2+\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}+\frac{1}{2}L_{z}^{2}-L_{z}\bigg)\bigg]\ ,

4
Universal heavy-light Sudakov form factor and redefinition of the jet function

In the previous section, we have introduced the most crucial object in the threshold re-summation: the space-like jet function resuming all the semi-hard exchanges at scale
\mu_{i}=|1-y|p^{z}\sim\frac{1}{z}
in the large
z
or large
y
limit, fluctuating between the collinear quark and the static gauge link. On the other hand, the incoming collinear quark, when hit by the quark-link vertex, transits into a space-like heavy quark with low virtuality
|1-y|p^{z}\sim|z|^{-1}
. This leads to strong Sudakov effect due to virtual fluctuations nearby, similar to the case where a collinear quark becomes another anti-collinear quark in the case of DIS.

As usual, the Sudakov effect can be resummed to all orders using a universal object, the heavy-light Sudakov form factor, which is similar to the standard Sudakov form factor
Sudakov 1956
;
Collins 1989
;
Moch et al. 2005
;
Baikov et al. 2009
involving only light-quarks. For quasi-PDF since there are two quark-link vertices, one needs a pair of Sudakov form factors, each one with a light external on-shell quark with
p^{2}=0
and a external heavy gauge-link extending to infinity. The natural scale of the form factor is
\zeta_{z}=\frac{4|p\cdot n_{z}|^{2}}{n_{z}^{2}}=4p_{z}^{2}
,
which is the only scalable quantity that can be formed in terms of Lorentz invariant combinations of
p
and
n_{z}
. We have already encountered such object in factorization of quasi-TMDPDF
Ebert et al. 2019
;
Ji et al. 2020
;
Ji et al. 2021
;
Vladimirov and Schäfer 2020
;
Ebert et al. 2020
;
Ebert et al. 2022
and quasi-LFWF
Ji and Liu 2022
;
Ji et al. 2021
. In the current case the hard kernel simply agrees with the quasi-LFWF one

\displaystyle H_{\rm HL}\left(\frac{\zeta_{z}}{\mu^{2}},{\rm sign}(z)\right)\equiv H_{\rm LFWF}^{-{\rm sign}(z)}\left(\frac{\zeta_{z}}{\mu^{2}}\right)\ .

(31)

Notice that there are imaginary part of each Sudakov form factor, as demonstrated in Ref.
Ji and Liu 2022
. In case of quasi-TMDPDF they cancel with each other, while for the threshold limit and for the quasi-LFWF the imaginary parts of the two Sudakov kernels add together and depend on the sign of
z

Ji and Liu 2022
. At one-loop, one has
Ji and Liu 2022

\displaystyle H^{\pm}_{\rm LFWF}=1+\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{4\pi}\bigg(-4-\frac{5\pi^{2}}{6}+2L_{\pm}-L_{\pm}^{2}\bigg)\ ,

(32)

\displaystyle L_{\pm}\equiv\ln\frac{-\zeta_{z}+i0}{\mu^{2}}=L_{z}\pm i\pi\ .

(33)

More generally, the RGE for the above heavy-light Sudakov form factor reads
Ji et al. 2020
;
Ji and Liu 2022

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\ln\mu}\ln H_{\rm HL}\left(\frac{\zeta_{z}}{\mu^{2}},\alpha(\mu),{\rm sign}(z)\right)=\Gamma_{\rm cusp}(\alpha)\ln\frac{\zeta_{z}}{\mu^{2}}+\tilde{\gamma}_{H}(\alpha)-i\pi{\rm sign}(z)\Gamma_{\rm cusp}(\alpha)\ .

(34)

Here
\tilde{\gamma}_{H}\equiv\gamma_{C}
is the same anomalous dimension for the hard kernel of the quasi-TMDPDF factorization and is known to two-loop
Ji et al. 2020
;
Ji and Liu 2022
. We will discuss more on anomalous dimensions in Appendix
A
.

Given all above, it is much more convenient to redefine the hard kernel to be purely real, while assign the phase to the jet function:

\displaystyle H(L_{z})\equiv|H|_{\rm HL}\left(L_{z}\right)\ ,

(35)

\displaystyle J_{f}(z\mu)\equiv\tilde{J}(z^{2}\mu^{2})\exp\bigg[i{\rm sign}(z)A(L_{z})\bigg]\ ,

(36)

where we define the phase angle as

\displaystyle{\rm sign}(z)A(L_{z})={\rm Arg}\bigg(H_{\rm HL}\left(\frac{\zeta_{z}}{\mu^{2}},{\rm sign}(z)\right)\bigg).

(37)

Notice that the phase angle is proportional to
{\rm sign}(z)
to all orders.
After this redefinition, one can see that the real part of
J_{f}
at one-loop remains the same as
\tilde{J}
, while the imaginary part becomes

\displaystyle{\rm Im}J_{f}(z\mu)=\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{2\pi}(L_{z}-1)i\pi{\rm sign}(z)\ .

(38)

After Fourier transform, one simply has

\displaystyle{\cal F}\left(i\pi{\rm sign}(\lambda)\right)(1-y)={\cal P}\bigg(\frac{1}{1-y}\bigg)\ .

(39)

Adding this to Eq. (
3.3
), one has the final NLO jet function

\displaystyle p^{z}J_{f}\left(\frac{(1-y)p^{z}}{\mu},\frac{4p_{z}^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\right)=\delta(1-y)\bigg[1+\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{2\pi}\left(2+\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}+\frac{L_{z}^{2}}{2}-L_{z}\right)\bigg]

\displaystyle+\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{2\pi}\bigg[{\cal P}\left(\frac{\ln(1-y)^{2}}{|1-y|}\right)+(L_{z}-1)\bigg({\cal P}\left(\frac{1}{1-y}\right)+{\cal P}\left(\frac{1}{|1-y|}\right)\bigg)\bigg]\ ,

(40)

and the NLO hard kernel

\displaystyle H(L_{z})=1+\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{2\pi}\bigg(-2+\frac{\pi^{2}}{12}-\frac{L_{z}^{2}}{2}+L_{z}\bigg)\ .

(41)

It is easy to check that the above hard kernel together with the jet function

completely reproduces the one-loop quasi-PDF in
\overline{\text{M}S}
scheme

;
Izubuchi et al. 2018

(1-y)^{-1}
through the factorization formula Eq. (
3

D

;
Ji et al. 2020

Vladimirov and Schäfer 2020
;
Ebert et al. 2020
;
Ji and Liu 2022

6.1
Evolution equation and matching to DGLAP

As a reminder, for the absolute value of the hard kernel, the renormalization group equation reads

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\ln\mu}\ln H\left(\frac{\zeta_{z}}{\mu^{2}},\alpha(\mu)\right)=\Gamma_{\rm cusp}(\alpha)\ln\frac{\zeta_{z}}{\mu^{2}}+\tilde{\gamma}_{H}(\alpha)\ .

(55)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\ln\mu}J_{f}\left(\frac{(1-\xi)xP^{z}}{\mu}\right)=-\left(\tilde{\gamma}_{J}(\alpha_{s})+\Gamma_{\rm cusp}(\alpha_{s})\ln\frac{4x^{2}P_{z}^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\right)J_{f}\left(\frac{(1-\xi)xP^{z}}{\mu},\alpha(\mu)\right)

(56)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\ln\mu}{\cal C}\left(\xi,\frac{xP^{z}}{\mu}\right)=(\tilde{\gamma}_{H}-\tilde{\gamma}_{J}){\cal C}\left(\xi,\frac{xP^{z}}{\mu}\right)-2\Gamma_{\rm cusp}{\cal P}\int_{\xi^{\prime}>\xi}\frac{{\cal C}\left(\xi^{\prime},\frac{xP^{z}}{\mu}\right)}{\xi^{\prime}-\xi}d\xi^{\prime}\ ,

\displaystyle\tilde{\gamma}_{H}=2\gamma_{H}+2\gamma_{F}+(2\gamma_{HL}-\gamma_{s})\ .

7
Summary and outlook

In this paper we have presented all the necessary results on the threshold limit of quark quasi-PDF. We have shown that in the threshold limit, the quasi-PDF factorizes through a space-like jet function and a pair of heavy-light Sudakov form factors. By comparing the prediction based on our factorization formula with the exact calculation in Ref.
Li et al. 2021
;
Chen et al. 2021
, we are able to confirm the correctness of our formalism explicitly up to NNLO, and extract the universal heavy-light Sudakov form factor at NNLO.
Using the RGE of the individual pieces, we obtain the resummed form of the matching kernel in momentum space that will be applied to numerical calculations. Finally, the results reveal a high level of universality across the threshold limit of quasi-PDF and the large momentum expansion of quasi-TMDPDFs and LFWFs.

In a future work, we will study further the RGE re-summation of the threshold region of matching kernel based on our current formalism and apply it to numerical determination of pion PDF.

Note: During the writing-up stage of this paper, we noticed that Ref.
del Río and Vladimirov 2023
explicitly computed two-loop heavy-light Sudakov form factor
in the context of quasi-TMDPDF large-momentum expansion, which
agrees with the result we found here.

Appendix D
Fourier transform of logarithms

In this appendix we provide a pedagogical introduction to Fourier transforms of logarithm, emphasizing the singular contribution at
x=0
. We first introduce for
\varphi\in{\cal S}(R)
(the Schwartz class),

\displaystyle\langle|z|^{\alpha},\varphi(z)\rangle\equiv\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|z|^{\alpha}\varphi(z)dz\ .

(125)

Clearly, this defines for any
-1<\alpha<\infty
a tempered distribution acting on smooth functions
\varphi\in{\cal S}(R)
with fast decrease at large
z
. Furthermore, it is easy to see that it is infinitely smooth near
\alpha=0
, with

\displaystyle\frac{d^{n}}{d^{n}\alpha}\langle|z|^{\alpha},\varphi(z)\rangle_{\alpha=0}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\ln^{n}|z|\varphi(z)dz\ .

(126)

We would like to express the distribution Eq. (
125
) in terms of
\hat{\varphi}(x)
, the Fourier transform of
\varphi(z)
, through absolute convergent integral representations. The key point is, for
0<\alpha<-1
, Fourier transform of
|z|^{\alpha}
is of the form
|x|^{-1+|\alpha|}
, which is integrable at
|x|=0
and requires no subtraction at all. Therefore one needs to partial integrate to decrease the power of
|z|^{\alpha}
down to
|z|^{\alpha-1}
. This can be performed as below

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dz|z|^{\alpha}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{ixz}\hat{\varphi}(x)dx=2\int_{0}^{\infty}dzz^{\alpha-1}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}z\cos xz\hat{\varphi}(x)dx

\displaystyle=-2\int_{0}^{\infty}dzz^{\alpha-1}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\sin xz\hat{\varphi}^{\prime}(x)dx=-2\Gamma(\alpha)\sin\frac{\alpha\pi}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\frac{\hat{\varphi}^{\prime}(x){\rm sign}(x)}{|x|^{\alpha}}\ .

(127)

Clearly, for
0<\alpha<1
this integral is convergent absolutely near
x=0
. Now, we simply partial integrate

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\frac{\hat{\varphi}^{\prime}(x){\rm sign}(x)}{|x|^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}dx\frac{(\hat{\varphi}(x)-\hat{\varphi}(0))^{\prime}}{|x|^{\alpha}}+\int_{1}^{\infty}dx\frac{\hat{\varphi}^{\prime}(x)}{|x|^{\alpha}}-\int_{-1}^{0}dx\frac{(\hat{\varphi}(x)-\hat{\varphi}(0))^{\prime}}{|x|^{\alpha}}-\int_{-\infty}^{-1}dx\frac{\hat{\varphi}^{\prime}(x)}{|x|^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\alpha\int_{-1}^{1}dx\frac{\hat{\varphi}(x)-\hat{\varphi}(0)}{|x|^{1+\alpha}}+\alpha\int_{|x|>1}dx\frac{\hat{\varphi}(x)}{|x|^{1+\alpha}}-2\hat{\varphi}(0)\ .

(128)

Therefore, one finally has the master formula

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|z|^{\alpha}\varphi(z)dz=2\Gamma(\alpha)\sin\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\bigg(2\hat{\varphi}(0)-\alpha\int_{-1}^{1}dx\frac{\hat{\varphi}(x)-\hat{\varphi}(0)}{|x|^{1+\alpha}}-\alpha\int_{|x|>1}dx\frac{\hat{\varphi}(x)}{|x|^{1+\alpha}}\bigg)\ .

(129)

Now, all the integral converges absolutely and defines a smooth functions of
\alpha
in a neighborhood of
\alpha=0
, therefore by taking derivative at
\alpha=0
and equating two sides of the equation, one generates Fourier transform of all the
\ln^{n}|z|
. For example

\displaystyle{\cal F}(\ln|z|)=-\gamma_{E}\delta(x)-{\cal P}\frac{1}{2|x|}\ ,

(130)

\displaystyle{\cal F}(\ln^{2}|z|)=\bigg(\gamma_{E}^{2}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{12}\bigg)\delta(x)+{\cal P}\frac{\ln e^{\gamma_{E}}|x|}{|x|}\ ,

(131)

\displaystyle{\cal F}(\ln^{3}|z|)=-\bigg(\gamma_{E}^{3}+\frac{1}{4}\gamma_{E}\pi^{2}+2\zeta_{3}\bigg)\delta(x)-{\cal P}\frac{12\ln^{2}e^{\gamma_{E}}|x|+\pi^{2}}{8|x|}\ ,

(132)

\displaystyle{\cal F}(\ln^{4}|z|)=\bigg(\gamma_{E}^{4}+\frac{\gamma_{E}^{2}\pi^{2}}{2}+8\gamma_{E}\zeta_{3}+\frac{19\pi^{4}}{240}\bigg)\delta(x)+{\cal P}\frac{4\ln^{3}e^{\gamma_{E}}|x|+\pi^{2}\ln e^{\gamma_{E}}|x|+8\zeta_{3}}{2|x|}\ ,

(133)

and so on. Clearly, the method above can be generalized to Fourier transform of
{\rm sign}(z)\ln^{n}|z|
with minor modifications. In fact, the only formula one needs is

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}dzz^{\alpha-1}\cos zx=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha)\cos\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}}{|x|^{\alpha}}\ ,

(134)

when
0<\alpha<1
, which leads to

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm sign}(z)|z|^{\alpha}\varphi(z)dz=2i\Gamma(\alpha)\cos\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\frac{\hat{\varphi}^{\prime}(x)}{|x|^{\alpha}}\ ,

(135)

and one then proceeds exactly the same way by partial integrating. The results up to
{\rm sign(z)}\ln^{2}|z|
reads

\displaystyle{\cal F}(i\pi{\rm sign}(z))={\cal P}\frac{1}{x}\ ,

(136)

\displaystyle{\cal F}(i\pi{\rm sign}(z)\ln|z|)=-{\cal P}\frac{\ln e^{\gamma_{E}}|x|}{x}\ ,

(137)

\displaystyle{\cal F}(i\pi{\rm sign}(z)\ln^{2}|z|)={\cal P}\frac{\ln^{2}e^{\gamma_{E}}|x|-\pi^{2}}{12x}\ .

(138)

Notice the absence of any subtraction term at
x=0
, which is expected for old distributions.

Appendix E
Converting the threshold “plus function” to principal value and numerical extraction of
c_{H}

Like many other quantities in QFT, the quasi-PDF in massless perturbation theory has a high level of regularity in coordinate space: it is not only a tempered distribution
1
1

1

This is the level of regularity for a generic correlator in local quantum field theory
Streater and Wightman 1989
.
, but also an analytic and local-integrable function with logarithmic small and large
z
asymptotics. In particular, the large
z
asymptotics for non-singlet quark quasi-distributions has the form
e^{-iP^{z}z}\ln^{n}z
and corresponds exactly to the threshold limit. As a result, when transformed to the momentum space in the sense of tempered distribution, the level of singularity at
x=1
is enhanced, requiring additional subtraction terms. The spirit of such subtraction is the same as Fourier transform of logarithms and is normally presented in the “plus-function” notation in literature
Li et al. 2021
. Since in the threshold limit the natural prescription is perhaps the principal value, which is adopted in this paper, we need to convert the “plus-functions” into principal value, defined in Eq. (
27
). This is crucial to extract the coefficient of
\delta(1-y)
term. In this appendix we present the details of this conversion.

We first consider the region
y>1
. A plus function of the following form

\displaystyle\bigg\langle\bigg[\theta(y-1)f(y)\bigg]^{[\infty]}_{\oplus(1)},\varphi(y)\bigg\rangle=\int_{1}^{\infty}f(y)\bigg(\varphi(y)-\varphi(1)\bigg)dy\ ,

(139)

can be converted to principal value in the threshold limit in the following way. First, one expand
f(y)
at
y=1
and separate the
{\cal O}(1-y)^{-1}
singular term and non-singular term

\displaystyle f(y)=f_{\rm sing}(y)+\bigg(f(y)-f_{\rm sing}(y)\bigg)\ ,

(140)

the first term survives the threshold limit in the region
1<y<2

\delta(1-y)

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}f(y)\bigg(\varphi(y)-\varphi(1)\bigg)dy=\int_{1}^{2}f_{\rm sing}(y)\bigg(\varphi(y)-\varphi(1)\bigg)dy+\int_{2}^{\infty}f_{\rm sing}(y)\varphi(y)dy

\displaystyle-\bigg[\int_{1}^{2}\bigg(f(y)-f_{\rm sing}(y)\bigg)dy+\int_{2}^{\infty}f(y)dy\bigg]\varphi(1)+\int_{1}^{\infty}\bigg(f(y)-f_{\rm sing}(y)\bigg)\varphi(y)dy\ ,

clearly, the first line is just our convention of the principal value, while the last terms is power-suppressed. As a result, one has in the threshold limit

\displaystyle\bigg[\theta(y-1)f(y)\bigg]^{[\infty]}_{\oplus(1)}

\displaystyle\rightarrow{\cal P}\bigg(\theta(y-1)f_{\rm sing}(y)\bigg)-\bigg[\int_{1}^{2}\bigg(f(y)-f_{\rm sing}(y)\bigg)dy+\int_{2}^{\infty}f(y)dy\bigg]\delta(y-1)\ .

(142)

Similarly one has

\displaystyle\bigg[\theta(1-y)\theta(y)f(y)\bigg]^{[-\infty]}_{+(1)}

\displaystyle\rightarrow{\cal P}\bigg(\theta(1-y)\theta(y)f_{\rm sing}(y)\bigg)-\bigg[\int_{0}^{1}\bigg(f(y)-f_{\rm sing}(y)\bigg)dy\bigg]\delta(y-1)\ ,

(143)

\displaystyle\bigg[\theta(-y)\theta(y+1)f(y)\bigg]^{[-\infty]}_{+(1)}\rightarrow-\int_{-1}^{0}f(y)dy\delta(1-y)\ ,

(144)

\displaystyle\bigg[\theta(-y-1)f(y)\bigg]^{[-\infty]}_{+(1)}\rightarrow-\int_{-\infty}^{-1}f(y)dy\delta(1-y)\ .

(145)

In this appendix, we present calculation details for a sample two-loop diagram with triple gluon vertex and with six propagators, shown in Fig.

. The purpose of this appendix is three fold. First, it provides a check on the non-trivial fact claimed in Ref.

+\infty n

t\rightarrow-i|z|
without encountering any singularities. Finally, it serves as a pedagogical introduction to a sample two-loop calculations with gauge-link propagators and light-cone singularities, which is hard to find in the literature.

The starting point is Fig.
6
. Using the standard Feynman rule, it is easy to check that the in momentum space (
t

q^{0}

\displaystyle F(111;110;100)\equiv\int\frac{d^{D}kd^{D}l}{(2\pi)^{2D}}\frac{1}{D_{l}D_{k+l}D_{k}}\frac{1}{(l\cdot v+q^{0})((k+l)\cdot v+q^{0})(l\cdot n)}\ .

\displaystyle\frac{1}{D-4}\bigg(F(100;100;100)F(002;001;000)-F(021;110;100)\bigg)

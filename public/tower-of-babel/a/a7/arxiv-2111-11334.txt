1.
Introduction

1.1.
Setting problem

In this paper, we consider the initial-boundary problem of semilinear wave equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{tt}-\Delta u=f(u),\,\,\,&x\in\Omega,\,\,t>0,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\,\,\,u_{t}(x,0)=u_{1}(x)&x\in\overline{\Omega},\\
u(x,t)=0\,\,\,&x\in\partial\Omega,t\geq 0,\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. A total energy of problem (
1.1
) is

\displaystyle E(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}u^{2}_{t}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx-\int_{\Omega}[F(u)-\sigma]dx,

where
F(u)=\int_{0}^{u}f(s)ds
and
\sigma
is some positive constant. Then a law of conservation of energy states that

E(t)=E(0):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}u^{2}_{1}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{0}|^{2}dx-\int_{\Omega}[F(u_{0})-\sigma]dx.

(1.2)

A simple computation gives

\displaystyle\frac{dE(t)}{dt}

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\left[\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}u^{2}_{t}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx-\int_{\Omega}[F(u)-\sigma]dx\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}u_{t}u_{tt}dx+\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla u_{t}dx-\int_{\Omega}f(u)u_{t}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}[\Delta u+f(u)]u_{t}dx-\int_{\Omega}\Delta uu_{t}dx-\int_{\Omega}f(u)u_{t}dx

\displaystyle=0.

In this paper we use the following notations:

\displaystyle||u||^{2}:=||u||^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\int_{\Omega}|u(x,t)|^{2}dx,\,\,\text{ and }\,\,||u||^{\gamma}:=||u||^{\gamma}_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}=\int_{\Omega}|u(x,t)|^{\gamma}dx.

1.2.
A new condition on nonlinearity

Let
f(u)
satisfy the following assumptions:

(i)

f\in C^{1}
,
f(0)=f^{\prime}(0)=0
.

(ii)

(a)

is monotone and convex for

, concave for
u<0

(b)

is convex for
-\infty<u<+\infty
.

(iii)

Let
2<\alpha\leq\gamma<2n/(n-2)
, then

|uf(u)|\leq\gamma|F(u)-\sigma|,

(1.3)

and

\alpha F(u)\leq uf(u)+\beta u^{2}+\alpha\sigma,

(1.4)

where

,

, and

In the case (b), (

For our convenience, these assumptions on the nonlinearity

.

Let

(H)-(a)

(H)-(b)

(v)

Here we omit the proof.

Note that new condition (

includes the following cases:

\displaystyle(2+\epsilon)F(u)

\displaystyle\leq uf(u),

(1.5)

\displaystyle(2+\epsilon)F(u)

\displaystyle\leq uf(u)+\sigma,

(1.6)

\displaystyle(2+\epsilon)F(u)

\displaystyle\leq uf(u)+\beta u^{2}+\sigma,

,

. Condition (

[

]
to prove the blow-up solutions for the semilinear heat equation. It is a special case of an abstract condition by Levine and Payne
[

. Later, Bandle and Brunner
[

]
relaxed this condition with (

Note that condition (

Condition (

\frac{d}{du}\left(\frac{F(u)}{u^{2+\epsilon}}-\left(\frac{\sigma}{2+\epsilon}\right)\frac{1}{u^{2+\epsilon}}-\frac{\beta}{\epsilon}\frac{1}{u^{\epsilon}}\right)\geq 0,\,\,\,u>0.

Then it is easy to see that

\displaystyle(\ref{eq-0.1})\,\,\,\text{ holds if and only if }\,\,F(u)=u^{2+\epsilon}h_{1}(u),

\displaystyle(\ref{eq-0.2})\,\,\,\text{ holds if and only if }\,\,F(u)=u^{2+\epsilon}h_{2}(u)+b,

\displaystyle(\ref{eq-0.3})\,\,\,\text{ holds if and only if }\,\,F(u)=u^{2+\epsilon}h_{3}(u)+au^{2}+b,

for some positive constants

,

, and

, where

, and

Lemma 1.2
.

Let

be a function satisfying
(\ref{eq-0.3})
and

for

. Then condition

implies that there exists

such that

for

. In this case, it is possible to find

such that

Proof of Lemma
1.2
.

Recall the facts
0<a<\lambda_{1}/2
and
\lambda>\lambda_{1}
that allows to obtain

F(u)\geq\frac{\lambda}{2}u^{2}>\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}u^{2},

then

u^{2+\epsilon}h_{3}(u)=F(u)-au^{2}-b\geq\frac{\lambda-\lambda_{1}}{2}u^{2}-b,

which goes to

as
u\rightarrow+\infty
. So there is
m>1
such that
h_{3}(m)>0
, which implies that

F(u)\geq u^{2+\epsilon}h_{3}(u),\,\,\,u\geq m.

Applying it into condition (

), we get

\displaystyle u^{2+\epsilon}h_{3}(m)

\displaystyle\leq uf(u)+\beta u^{2}+\sigma

\displaystyle\leq(1+\epsilon/2)uf(u)+\sigma,\,\,\,u\geq m>1.

That gives

for some constant
\mu>0

Next we show that (
1.6
) and (

) are equivalent. Since
0<\beta\leq\epsilon\lambda_{1}/2
and

\epsilon_{1}F(u)+(2+\epsilon_{2})F(u)\leq uf(u)+\frac{\epsilon\lambda_{1}}{2}u^{2}+\sigma,

\epsilon_{1}=\frac{\epsilon\lambda_{1}}{\lambda}>0

\epsilon_{2}=\epsilon-\epsilon_{1}>0

\displaystyle uf(u)+\sigma

\displaystyle\geq(2+\epsilon_{2})F(u)+\epsilon_{1}\int_{0}^{u}[f(s)-\lambda s]ds

\displaystyle\geq(2+\epsilon_{2})F(u),

which is (

∎

1.4.
Main goal of the paper

A main aim of this work is to study a global existence and blow-up of solutions to initial-boundary problem (
1.1
) with a new condition on a nonlinearity
f(u)
as follows

\alpha\int_{0}^{u}f(s)ds\leq uf(u)+\beta u^{2}+\alpha\sigma,

for some positive constants
\alpha
,
\beta
, and

being the first eigenvalue of Laplacian. The new condition was introduced by Chung-Choi

2.
Potential Wells Theory

In this section, we introduce a family of potential wells. First, we would like to illustrate idea of potential wells by giving a simple example such as one-dimensional mechanical analogue of problem (
1.1
)

\ddot{x}=-x+f(x),

(2.1)

where
x
is a real number. This example was given by Payne-Sattinger
[
31
]
and it describes a mechanical system with one degree of freedom, while problem (
1.1
) may be considered as a system with an infinite number of degree of freedom.
The potential energy of equation (
2.1
) is defined by

V(x)=\frac{x^{2}}{2}-F(x),

where
F(x)=\int_{0}^{x}f(s)ds
. Imagine that potential energy
V(x)
has the qualitative shape with a local minimum at
x=0
and a local maximum at
x=x_{*}
.

The set

W=\{x:V(x)<d,\,\,x<x_{*}\}

describes a potential well. The total energy of equation (
2.1
) is

E(t)=\frac{\dot{x}^{2}}{2}+\frac{x^{2}}{2}-F(x)=\frac{\dot{x}^{2}}{2}+V(x).

As we know that the total energy of (
2.1
) is conserved under the motion. Then by the conservation of energy we have

•

If
E(0)<d
and
x(0)\in W
, then
x(t)\in W
for all
t>0
.

•

If
E(0)<d
and
x(0)>x_{*}
, then
x(t)>x_{*}
for all
t>0
.

In order to cross into potential well
W
, the total energy would have to be greater than the depth of potential well
d
.

This idea can be extended to the infinite dimensional case (
1.1
). The
potential energy
associated with (
1.1
) is the functional

\displaystyle J(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}||\nabla u||^{2}-\int_{\Omega}[F(u)-\sigma]dx.

We define the
Nehari functional
by

I(u)=||\nabla u||^{2}-\int_{\Omega}uf(u)dx,\,\,\,u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),

where
I(u)
can be considered as the extension of point
x_{*}
in one dimension analogue.

The depth of potential wells is defined by

d:=\inf\{J(u):u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\backslash\{0\},I(u)=0\}.

Also the quantity
d
can be understood as a mountain pass energy. For instance, there is the valley. If we pour water into the valley containing a local minimum at the origin, as the water level rises in the direction of the potential energy and passes at a local maximum
d
.

Note that in all proofs, we should treat slightly differently the cases
(a)
and
(b)
for assumption
(H)
for nonlinearity
f(u)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f(u)
satisfy
(H)
, then we have

(a)

uf^{\prime}(u)-f(u)\geq 0
holds in the case
(H)-(a)
with equality holding only for
u=0
, and in the case
(H)-(b)
for
u\geq 0
.

(b)

|F(u)-\sigma|\leq A|u|^{\gamma}
for some
A>0
and all
u\in R
.

(c)

F(u)-\sigma\geq B|u|^{\lambda}
for
|u|\geq 1
with
B=\frac{F(\widetilde{u})}{\widetilde{u}^{\lambda}}>0
and
\lambda=\frac{\alpha}{1+\beta}>2
.

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

(a)
If
f(u)
satisfies
(H)-(a)
, then the proof is based on the second variation of functional
J
at a critical point
w
. Let

i(\tau):=J(w+\tau v)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(w+\tau v)|^{2}dx-\int_{\Omega}F(w+\tau v)dx,

where
v\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. The second variation of
J
is

i^{\prime\prime}(0)=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2}dx-\int_{\Omega}f^{\prime}(w)v^{2}dx.

If
w
is a local minimum of functional
J
, then
i^{\prime\prime}(0)\geq 0
. Then
w=0
gives
i^{\prime\prime}(0)=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2}dx\geq 0
. On other hand,
w
is a non-trivial critical point, then
i^{\prime\prime}(0)\leq 0
. Let
w
be an admissible test function, then we compute

\displaystyle i^{\prime\prime}(0)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{2}dx-\int_{\Omega}f^{\prime}(w)w^{2}dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}w(\Delta w+f^{\prime}(w)w)dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}w(f^{\prime}(w)w-f(w))dx,

it follows that
w(f^{\prime}(w)w-f(w))\geq 0
.

In the case
(H)-(b)

,

f
is convex for
-\infty<u<+\infty
, this gives
f(0)\geq f(u)+f^{\prime}(u)(0-u)
that gives
uf^{\prime}(u)\geq f(u)
.

(b)
From
(H)
, the growth condition
|uf(u)|\leq\gamma|F(u)-\sigma|
implies

\displaystyle\frac{|f(u)|}{|F(u)-\sigma|}\leq\frac{\gamma}{|u|}\,\,\,\text{ for }\,\,\,u\neq 0.

Taking any known fixed
\widetilde{u}\neq 0
, we integrate above inequality from
\widetilde{u}
to
u
, which gives

\displaystyle\int_{\widetilde{u}}^{u}\frac{|f(s)|}{|F(s)-\sigma|}ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\widetilde{u}}^{u}\frac{\gamma}{|s|}ds,

and by using
F^{\prime}(u)=f(u)
, then we have

\displaystyle\ln\frac{|F(u)-\sigma|}{|F(\widetilde{u})-\sigma|}\leq\ln\frac{|u|^{\gamma}}{|\widetilde{u}|^{\gamma}},

that is
|F(u)-\sigma|\leq A|u|^{\gamma}
where
A=\frac{|F(\widetilde{u})-\sigma|}{|\widetilde{u}|^{\gamma}}
.

(c)
From
(H)
we have
\alpha F(u)\leq uf(u)+\beta u^{2}+\alpha\sigma
. For
|u|\geq 1
, we write

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{|u|}\leq\frac{|f(u)|}{F(u)-\sigma}+\frac{\beta|u|}{F(u)-\sigma}\leq(1+\beta)\frac{|f(u)|}{F(u)-\sigma},

then we get

\frac{|f(u)|}{F(u)-\sigma}\geq\frac{\lambda}{|u|},

(2.2)

where
\lambda=\frac{\alpha}{1+\beta}
. As the previous approach on
(b)
, we get
F(u)-\sigma\geq B|u|^{\lambda}
where
B=\frac{F(\widetilde{u})}{\widetilde{u}^{\lambda}}
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let

satisfy
(H)
. Let

\displaystyle\phi(\epsilon)=\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int_{\Omega}uf(\epsilon u)dx.

•

Proof of Lemma

Let

\displaystyle\frac{d\phi(\epsilon)}{d\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\int_{\Omega}[\epsilon u^{2}f^{\prime}(\epsilon u)-uf(\epsilon u)]dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\epsilon^{3}}\int_{\Omega}\epsilon u[\epsilon uf^{\prime}(\epsilon u)-f(\epsilon u)]dx>0.

By

The property

\displaystyle|\phi(\epsilon)|\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\int_{\Omega}|\epsilon uf(\epsilon u)|dx\leq\frac{\gamma A}{\epsilon^{2}}\int_{\Omega}|\epsilon u|^{\gamma}dx=\gamma\epsilon^{\gamma-2}A||u||^{\gamma}_{\gamma}.

Proof of Lemma

Remark 2.5
.

In this remark, we would like to show how the notation of

is adopted.

Note that for a unique

we have

can be considered as the highest level attained when leaving the

In order to introduce the family of potential wells

4.
Global existence of solutions for semilinear wave equation

In this section, we prove a global existence of solutions of semilinear wave equation (
1.1
) by using the family of potential wells.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
u\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))
and
u_{t}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
. Then a function
u(x,t)
is called a
weak solution
of problem (
1.1
) on
\Omega\times[0,T)
if

(u_{t},v)+\int_{0}^{t}(\nabla u,\nabla v)d\tau=\int_{0}^{t}(f(u),v)d\tau+(u_{1},v),

for every
v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
,
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, and
t\in(0,T)
.

4.1.
Initial condition
0<E(0)<d
and
I(u_{0})>0

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
f(u)
satisfy
(H)
. Let
u_{0}(x)\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
,
u_{1}(x)\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
2\beta<\lambda_{1}(\alpha-2)
. Suppose that
0<E(0)<d
and
I(u_{0})>0
or
||\nabla u_{0}||=0
. Then problem (
1.1
) admits a global weak solution
u(t)\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))
,
u_{t}(t)\in(0,\infty;L^{2}(\Omega))
and
u(t)\in W
for
t\in[0,\infty)
.

Lemma 4.3

(Lemma 1.3 in
[
21
]
)
.

Let
D
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{t}
,
g_{\mu}
and
g
are functions from
L^{q}(D)
,
1<q<\infty
, so

||g_{\mu}||_{L^{q}(D)}\leq C,\,\,\,\,g_{\mu}\rightarrow g\,\,\text{ in }D.

Then
g_{\mu}\rightarrow g
weakly star in
L^{q}(D)
.

Proof of Theorem
4.2
.

Here we follow the proof from Liu-Zhao
[
23
]
. Let
w_{j}(x)
be a system of base functions in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
. Then we are able to construct the approximate solutions
u_{m}(x,t)
of problem (
1.1
) as follows

u_{m}(x,t)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}g_{jm}(t)w_{j}(x)\,\,\text{ for }\,\,m=1,2,\ldots

satisfying

(u_{m}^{\prime\prime},w_{s})+(\nabla u_{m},\nabla w_{s})=(f(u_{m}),w_{s})\,\,\text{ for }\,\,s=1,2,\ldots,m,

(4.1)

u_{m}(x,0)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{jm}w_{j}(x)\rightarrow u_{0}(x)\,\,\text{ in }\,\,H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),

(4.2)

u_{m}^{\prime}(x,0)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}b_{jm}w_{j}(x)\rightarrow u_{1}(x)\,\,\text{ in }\,\,L^{2}(\Omega).

(4.3)

Note that
u_{t}=u^{\prime}
. If we multiply (
4.1
) by
g^{\prime}_{sm}(t)
and sum over
s
, then this gives

(u_{m}^{\prime\prime},u_{m}^{\prime})+(\nabla u_{m},\nabla u_{m}^{\prime})=(f(u_{m}),u_{m}^{\prime})\,\,\text{ for }\,\,m=1,2,\ldots.

This implies conservation of energy
E_{m}(t)
, which allows to write for
0\leq t<\infty

\displaystyle E(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}||u_{m}^{\prime}||^{2}+\frac{1}{2}||\nabla u_{m}||^{2}-\int_{\Omega}[F(u_{m})-\sigma]dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}||u_{m}^{\prime}||^{2}+J(u_{m})=E_{m}(0)<d,

(4.4)

and for
I(u_{m}(x,0))>0
we have
u_{m}\in W
for sufficiently large
m
and
0\leq t<\infty
.

Now we obtain a priori estimates. Let

\displaystyle J(u_{m})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}||\nabla u_{m}||^{2}-\int_{\Omega}[F(u_{m})-\sigma]dx

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}||\nabla u_{m}||^{2}-\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{\Omega}u_{m}f(u_{m})dx-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}||u_{m}||^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{\beta}{\alpha\lambda_{1}}\right)||\nabla u_{m}||^{2}+\frac{1}{\alpha}I(u_{m})

\displaystyle\geq a||\nabla u_{m}||^{2},

where
a=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{\beta}{\alpha\lambda_{1}}
. Then from (
4.1
) it follows that

\frac{1}{2}||u_{m}^{\prime}||^{2}+a||\nabla u_{m}||^{2}<d,\,\,\,0\leq t<\infty,

(4.5)

for sufficiently large
m
. Then for
0\leq t<\infty
(
4.5
) implies the following estimates

\displaystyle||\nabla u_{m}||^{2}<\frac{d}{a},

(4.6)

\displaystyle||u_{m}||^{2}_{\gamma}\leq C_{*}^{2}||\nabla u_{m}||^{2}<C_{*}^{2}\frac{d}{a},

(4.7)

\displaystyle||u_{m}^{\prime}||^{2}<2d,

(4.8)

and Lemma
2.1
and (
4.7
) give

\displaystyle||f(u_{m})||^{q}_{q}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}[\gamma A|u_{m}|^{\gamma-1}]^{q}dx=\gamma^{q}A^{q}||u_{m}||^{\gamma}_{\gamma}

\displaystyle<\gamma^{q}A^{q}C_{*}^{\gamma}\left(\frac{d}{a}\right)^{\gamma/2},

(4.9)

where
q=\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}
.

Since a priori estimates (
4.6
)-(
4.8
), we may extract the subsequence
\{u_{l}\}
from sequence
\{u_{m}\}
such that

\displaystyle u_{l}

\displaystyle\rightarrow u\,\,\text{ weakly star in }L^{\infty}(0,\infty;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))\,\,\text{ and a.e. }Q=\Omega\times[0,\infty)

\displaystyle u_{l}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\rightarrow u_{t}\,\,\text{ weakly star in }L^{\infty}(0,\infty;L^{2}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle f(u_{l})

\displaystyle\rightarrow\chi\,\,\text{ in }L^{\infty}(0,\infty;L^{q}(\Omega))\,\,\,\text{ and a.e. }Q=\Omega\times[0,\infty).

From Lemma
4.3
we have
\chi=f(u)
. We integrate equation (
4.1
) with respect to time from
0
to
t
as follows

(u_{m}^{\prime},w_{s})-(u_{m}^{\prime}(0),w_{s})+\int_{0}^{t}(\nabla u_{m},\nabla w_{s})d\tau=\int_{0}^{t}(f(u_{m}),w_{s})d\tau.

(4.10)

For fixed
s
in (
4.10
) and taking
m=l\rightarrow\infty
we get

(u_{t},w_{s})-(u_{1},w_{s})+\int_{0}^{t}(\nabla u,\nabla w_{s})d\tau=\int_{0}^{t}(f(u),w_{s})d\tau,\,\,\,\forall s,

then

(u_{t},v)-(u_{1},v)+\int_{0}^{t}(\nabla u,\nabla v)d\tau=\int_{0}^{t}(f(u),v)d\tau,\,\,\,\forall v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\,\,t>0.

Also (
4.2
) implies that
u_{0}(x)
in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
. Therefore
u(x,t)
is a global weak solution of problem (
1.1
).
∎

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
f(u)
satisfy
(H)
. Let

and

Proof of Theorem
4.5
.

The proof is divided for two cases
||\nabla u_{0}||\neq 0
and
||\nabla u_{0}||=0
.

In the case
||\nabla u_{0}||\neq 0
, we take
\epsilon_{m}=1-1/m
and
u_{0m}=\epsilon_{m}u_{0}
for

. Then we consider problem (
1.1
) with the following initial conditions

u(x,0)=u_{0m}(x)\,\,\text{ and }\,\,u_{t}(x,0)=u_{1}(x).

(4.11)

The fact
\epsilon^{*}=\epsilon^{*}(u_{0})\geq 1
follows from
I(u_{0})\geq 0
and Lemma
2.4
. Then for
I(u_{0m})>0
we have

J(u_{0m})\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{\beta}{\alpha\lambda_{1}}\right)||\nabla u_{0m}||^{2}+\frac{1}{\alpha}I(u_{0m})>0,

(4.12)

and
J(u_{m0})<J(u_{0})
. Then

0<E_{m}(0)=\frac{1}{2}||u_{1}||^{2}+J(u_{0m})<\frac{1}{2}||u_{1}||^{2}+J(u_{0})=E(0)=d.

Now it is easy to see from Theorem
4.2
that for each
m
problem (
1.1
) with initial conditions (
4.11
) admits a global weak solution
u_{m}(t)\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))
with

and

for

(4.13)

for every

, and

\frac{1}{2}||u_{mt}||^{2}+J(u_{m})=E_{m}(0)<d.

(4.14)

Then we obtain the following estimates

\displaystyle||\nabla u_{m}||^{2}<\frac{d}{a},

\displaystyle||u_{m}||^{2}_{\gamma}\leq C_{*}^{2}||\nabla u_{m}||^{2}<C_{*}^{2}\left(\frac{d}{a}\right),

\displaystyle||u_{m}^{\prime}||^{2}<2d,

and

\displaystyle||f(u_{m})||^{q}_{q}<\gamma^{q}A^{q}C_{*}^{\gamma}\left(\frac{d}{a}\right)^{\gamma/2},

and

From above priori estimates, we extract the subsequence

\displaystyle\rightarrow u\,\,\text{ weakly star in }L^{\infty}(0,\infty;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))\,\,\text{ and a.e. }Q=\Omega\times[0,\infty)

(4.15)

5.
Blow-up solutions of semilinear wave equation

5.1.
Initial condition
E(0)<d
and
I(u_{0})<0

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
f
satisfy
(H)
with
2\beta<\lambda_{1}(\alpha-2)
and
\lambda_{1}
is a first eigenvalue of Laplacian. Let
u_{0}(x)\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and
u_{1}(x)\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. Suppose that energy
E(0)<d
and
I(u_{0})<0
. Then the solution of problem (
1.1
) blows up such that

\lim_{t\rightarrow T}\int_{\Omega}u^{2}dx=+\infty,

(5.1)

in a finite time

T=\frac{\int_{\Omega}u_{0}^{2}(x)dx}{(\alpha+2)\int_{\Omega}u_{0}(x)u_{1}(x)dx}.

(5.2)

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Define

\displaystyle M(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}u^{2}dx.

Then we have

\displaystyle M^{\prime}(t)=2\int_{\Omega}uu_{t}dx\,\,\text{ and }\,\,M^{\prime\prime}(t)=2\int_{\Omega}u_{t}^{2}dx+2\int_{\Omega}uu_{tt}dx.

(5.3)

Then by using
u_{tt}=\Delta u+f(u)
and (
1.4
) we have

\displaystyle M^{\prime\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=2\int_{\Omega}u_{t}^{2}dx+2\int_{\Omega}[\Delta u+f(u)]udx

\displaystyle=2\int_{\Omega}u_{t}^{2}dx-2I(u)

\displaystyle\geq 2\int_{\Omega}u_{t}^{2}dx+2\alpha\int_{\Omega}[F(u)-\sigma]dx-2\beta\int_{\Omega}u^{2}dx-2\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx.

Then from the conservation of energy

E(0)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}u_{t}^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx-\int_{\Omega}[F(u)-\sigma]dx,

and the Poincaré inequality, we get

M^{\prime\prime}(t)\geq(\alpha+2)\int_{\Omega}u_{t}^{2}dx+\left(\lambda_{1}(\alpha-2)-2\beta\right)M(t)-2\alpha E(0),

where
\lambda_{1}
is the first eigenvalue of Laplacian and
2\beta>\lambda_{1}(\alpha-2)
.

In the case
E(0)<0
and
2\beta>\lambda_{1}(\alpha-2)
, then

M^{\prime\prime}(t)\geq(\alpha+2)\int_{\Omega}u_{t}^{2}dx.

(5.4)

In the case
0<E(0)<d
and
2\beta<\lambda_{1}(\alpha-2)
, there exists sufficient large time
t_{1}
such that

\left(\lambda_{1}(\alpha-2)-2\beta\right)M(t)-2\alpha E(0)>0.

Since
M(t)
is a convex function of
t
, then we can find a time
t_{1}
such that
M^{\prime}(t_{1})>0
, then
M(t)
is increasing for all
t>t_{1}
. Also this part of proof can be shown by using the family of potential wells
V_{\delta}
and Lemma
2.6
(see
[
23
]
).

We are able to prove that (
5.4
) for two cases
E(0)<0
and
0<E(0)<d
. Next we obtain the blow-up solutions by using concavity argument and (
5.4
) as follows

M^{\prime\prime}(t)M(t)-(\alpha+3)[M^{\prime}(t)]^{2}\geq(\alpha+3)\left[\left(\int_{\Omega}u^{2}dx\right)\left(\int_{\Omega}u_{t}^{2}dx\right)-\left(\int_{\Omega}uu_{t}dx\right)^{2}\right]\geq 0,

which gives for
t\geq 0

\frac{d}{dt}\left[\frac{M^{\prime}(t)}{M^{\alpha+3}(t)}\right]>0\Rightarrow\begin{cases}M^{\prime}(t)\geq\left[\frac{M^{\prime}(0)}{M^{\alpha+3}(0)}\right]M^{\alpha+3}(t),\\
M(0)=\int_{\Omega}u_{0}^{2}dx.\end{cases}

Then we have

M(t)\geq M(0)\left(1-\frac{(\alpha+2)M^{\prime}(0)}{M(0)}t\right)^{-\frac{1}{\alpha+2}}.

Then the blow-up time
T
satisfies

0<T\leq\frac{M(0)}{(\alpha+2)M^{\prime}(0)}.

This completes the proof.
∎

5.2.
Critical initial condition
E(0)=d
and
I(u_{0})<0

Next we prove the blow-up solution of problem (
1.1
) with the critical initial conditions
E(0)=d
and
I(u_{0})<0
. First we need the following lemma.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
u_{0}(x)\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and
u_{1}(x)\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. Suppose that
E(0)=d
and
(u_{0},u_{1})>0
. Then set

V^{\prime}=\{u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\,:\,I(u)<0\}

is invariant under the flow of problem (
1.1
).

Proof of Lemma
5.2
.

Let
u(t)
be any weak solution of problem (
1.1
) with
E(0)=d
,
I(u_{0})<0
and
\int_{\Omega}u_{0}u_{1}dx\geq 0
, and
T
is the maximal existence time of
u(t)
.
I(u(t))<0
for
t\in(0,T)
is established by a contradiction. Let it be false, then there exists
t_{0}\in(0,T)
such that
I(u(t_{0}))=0
and
I(u(t))
for
t\in[0,t_{0})
. By Lemma
2.6
, we have
||\nabla u(t)||>r(1)
for
t\in[0,t_{0})
and
||\nabla u(t_{0})||\geq r(1)>0
. Next we have
J(u(t_{0}))=d
and
\int_{\Omega}u_{t}^{2}(t_{0})dx=0
from the definition of
d
which implies
J(u(t_{0}))\geq d
and

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}u_{t}^{2}(t_{0})dx+J(u(t_{0}))\leq E(0)=d.

Let

M(t)=\int_{\Omega}u^{2}(t)dx\,\,\text{ and }\,\,M^{\prime}(t)=2\int_{\Omega}u_{t}(x,t)dx,

with

\displaystyle M^{\prime}(0)

\displaystyle=2\int_{\Omega}u_{0}(x)u_{1}(x)dx,

\displaystyle M^{\prime\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=2\int_{\Omega}u_{t}^{2}(t)dx-2I(u(t))>0\,\,\text{ for }\,\,0\leq t<t_{0}.

Hence
M^{\prime}(t)
is strictly increasing with respect to
t\in[0,t_{0}]
along with
M^{\prime}(0)\geq 0
gives

M^{\prime}(t_{0})=2\int_{\Omega}u(t_{0})u_{t}(t_{0})dx>0.

This clearly contradicts
\int_{\Omega}u_{t}^{2}(t_{0})dx=0
. This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
f
satisfy
(H)
with
2\beta<\lambda_{1}(\alpha-2)
and
\lambda_{1}
is a first eigenvalue of Laplacian. Let
u_{0}(x)\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and
u_{1}(x)\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. Suppose that

\displaystyle E(0)=d,\,\,I(u_{0})<0,\,\,\text{ and }\int_{\Omega}u_{0}(x)u_{1}(x)dx\geq 0.

Then the weak solution of problem (
1.1
) blows up in a finite time.

Proof of Theorem
5.3
.

Let

\displaystyle M(t)=\int_{\Omega}u^{2}dx,\,\,\text{ and }\,\,M^{\prime}(t)=2\int_{\Omega}uu_{t}dx.

As in previous case we have

M^{\prime\prime}(t)\geq(\alpha+2)\int_{\Omega}u_{t}^{2}dx+\left(\lambda_{1}(\alpha-2)-2\beta\right)M(t)-2\alpha d,

and

M^{\prime\prime}(t)=2\int_{\Omega}u^{2}_{t}dx-2I(u)>0,

(5.5)

for
t\in[0,\infty)
because of
\int_{\Omega}u_{0}(x)u_{1}(x)dx\geq 0
and Lemma
5.2
.
M^{\prime\prime}(t)>0
means that
M^{\prime}(t)
is strictly increasing for
t\in[0,\infty)
. Hence for any
t_{0}>0
we have
M^{\prime}(t)\geq M^{\prime}(t_{0})>0
for
t\geq t_{0}
,

M(t)\geq M^{\prime}(t_{0})(t-t_{0})+M(t_{0})\geq M^{\prime}(t_{0})(t-t_{0}).

Therefore there exists sufficiently large time
t
such that

\left(\lambda_{1}(\alpha-2)-2\beta\right)M(t)>2\alpha d,

for
2\beta<\lambda_{1}(\alpha-2)
. Then we have

M^{\prime\prime}(t)\geq(\alpha+2)\int_{\Omega}u^{2}_{t}dx.

The rest of proof repeats the same computation as in Theorem
5.1
.
∎

6.
Conclusion

In conclusion, we arrive at the following results of global existence and blow-up of solutions for initial-boundary problem (
1.1
):

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
f(u)
satisfy
(H)
,
u_{0}(x)\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
,
u_{1}(x)\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Then problem (
1.1
) admits a global weak solution if the following assumptions hold:

•

E(0)<d
and
I(u_{0})\geq 0
;

•

E(0)=d
,
(u_{0}(x),u_{1}(x))\geq 0
, and
I(u_{0})\geq 0
.

The solution of problem (
1.1
) blows up in finite time if the following assumption hold:

•

E(0)<d
and
I(u_{0})<0
;

•

E(0)=d
,
(u_{0}(x),u_{1}(x))\geq 0
, and
I(u_{0})<0
.

The finite sequences and the partitions whose members are finite of a set
Palagorn Phansamdaeng, Pimpen Vejjajiva

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate relationships between | seq ​ ( A ) | |\text{{seq}}(A)| and | Part fin ​ ( A ) | |\text{{Part}}_{\text{{fin}}}(A)| in the absence of the Axiom of Choice, where seq ​ ( A ) \text{{seq}}(A) is the set of finite sequences of elements in a set A A and Part fin ​ ( A ) \text{{Part}}_{\text{{fin}}}(A) is the set of partitions of A A whose members are finite. We show that | seq ​ ( A ) | < | Part fin ​ ( A ) | |\text{{seq}}(A)|<|\text{{Part}}_{\text{{fin}}}(A)| if A A is Dedekind-infinite and the condition cannot be removed. Moreover, this relationship holds for an arbitrary infinite set A A if we restrict seq ​ ( A ) \text{{seq}}(A) to the set of finite sequences with a bounded length.

Introduction

[
4

and

A

[

[

and

In this paper, we investigate relationships between

RESULTS IN ZF

In this section, we shall work in ZF. For any sets
A
and
B
, we say that
|A|=|B|
if there is an explicit bijection between
A
and
B
,
|A|\leq|B|
if there is an explicit injection from

into

, and

|A|\leq|B|

|A|\neq|B|

A set

\aleph_{0}\leq|A|
, otherwise
A
is
Dedekind finite
.

Apart from the notation introduced in the previous section, we list some that will be used in our work below.

Notation
.

1.

For any set
A

.

2.

For a partition
\Pi

.

For a sequence

.

We start with the relationship between
|\text{{seq}}^{1-1}(A)|
and
|\text{{Part}}(A)|
when
A
is a finite set.

Theorem 2.1
.

For any natural number
n\neq 0
,
|\text{{seq}}^{1-1}(n)|>|\text{{Part}}(n)|
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
n
. Suppose the result holds for all
k\leq n
. By recurrences of arrangement number and Bell number,

\displaystyle|\text{{seq}}^{1-1}(n+1)|

\displaystyle=(n+1)|\text{{seq}}^{1-1}(n)|+1

\displaystyle>(n+1)|\text{{Part}}(n)|

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k\leq n}\binom{n}{k}|\text{{Part}}(k)|=|\text{{Part}}(n+1)|.\qed

Corollary 2.2
.

For any nonempty finite set
A
,
|\text{{seq}}^{1-1}(A)|>|\text{{Part}}(A)|
.

For infinite sets, things turn out to be different. The facts below together with Cantor’s theorem show that
|\text{{seq}}(A)|<|\text{{Part}}_{\text{{fin}}}(A)|
for all infinite well-ordered sets
A
.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
2
, Theorem 5.19]

For any infinite ordinal
\alpha
,
|\alpha|=|\text{{seq}}(\alpha)|
.

Theorem 2.4
.

[
6
, Theorem 3.5]

For any infinite ordinal
\alpha
,
|\text{{Part}}_{\text{{fin}}}(\alpha)|=2^{|\alpha|}
.

Since AC is equivalent to “every set can be well-ordered”, we obtain that
|\text{{seq}}(A)|<|\text{{Part}}_{\text{{fin}}}(A)|
for all infinite sets
A
if AC is assumed.

What happens in the absence of AC?

We shall show that the relationship holds for Dedekind infinite sets and shall show later in the next section that this assumption cannot be removed. First, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 2.5
.

For any sets
A
and
B\subseteq A
, if
|A|=2^{|\alpha|}
and
|B|\leq|\alpha|
, where
\alpha
is an infinite ordinal, then
|A|=|A\setminus B|
.

Proof.

Let
A
be a set such that
|A|=2^{|\alpha|}
, where
\alpha
is an infinite ordinal. Since
|\alpha|<2^{|\alpha|}=|A|
, there is
C\subseteq A
such that
|C|=|\alpha|
. First, we show that
|A|=|A\setminus C|
.

Let
f_{A}\colon A\rightarrow\mathcal{P}(\alpha)
and
f_{C}\colon C\rightarrow\alpha
be bijections and fix a canonical bijection
F\colon\alpha\rightarrow\alpha+\alpha
.

Define
g\colon\alpha+\alpha\rightarrow\mathcal{P}(\alpha)
recursively as follows:

g(\beta)=\begin{cases}f_{A}\circ f^{-1}_{C}(\beta)&\text{if }\beta<\alpha,\\
\{\xi<\alpha:F(\xi)<\beta\rightarrow\xi\not\in(g\upharpoonright\beta\circ F)(\xi)\}&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Clearly,
g\upharpoonright\alpha
is injective and for each
\alpha\leq\beta<\alpha+\alpha
, if
g(\beta)=(g\upharpoonright\beta\circ F)(\xi)
for some
\xi<\alpha
, then
F(\xi)<\beta
and so
\xi\in g(\beta)=(g\upharpoonright\beta\circ F)(\xi)\leftrightarrow\xi\not\in g(\beta)
, a contradiction. Hence
g(\beta)\not\in\text{{ran}}(g\upharpoonright\beta\circ F)=\text{{ran}}(g\upharpoonright\beta)
for all
\alpha\leq\beta<\alpha+\alpha
. Thus
g
is injective.

Define
G\colon\alpha\rightarrow\mathcal{P}(\alpha)
by
G(\beta)=g(\alpha+\beta)
for each
\beta<\alpha
. Clearly,
G
is injective.
Since
\text{{ran}}(g\restriction_{\alpha})=f_{A}\circ f_{C}^{-1}[\alpha]=f_{A}[C]
,
\text{{ran}}(G)\subseteq\mathcal{P}(\alpha)\setminus f_{A}[C]
. Hence
f_{A}^{-1}[\text{{ran}}(G)]\subseteq A\setminus C
, so

f_{A}^{-1}\circ G\circ f_{C}\colon C\rightarrow A\setminus C
is injective. Since
|C|
is an aleph and
|C|\leq|A\setminus C|
,
|A|=|A\setminus C|+|C|=|A\setminus C|
.

Now, let
B\subseteq A
such that
|B|\leq|\alpha|
. Let
B^{\prime}=B\cup f_{A}^{-1}[\alpha]
. Since
|B|\leq|\alpha|=|f_{A}^{-1}[\alpha]|
,
|B^{\prime}|=|\alpha|
. Hence, by the above result,
|A|=|A\setminus B^{\prime}|\leq|A\setminus B|\leq|A|
, and so
|A|=|A\setminus B|
by the Cantor-Bernstein theorem.
∎

Theorem 2.6
.

For any Dedekind infinite set
A
,
|\text{{seq}}(A)|<|\text{{Part}}_{\text{{fin}}}(A)|
.

Proof.

Let
A
be a Dedekind infinite set. Then
|A|=|A\cup\omega|
. We may assume that
A\cap\omega=\emptyset
.
First, we show that
|\text{{seq}}(A)|\leq|\text{{Part}}_{\text{{fin}}}(A\cup\omega)|
.

Define
f\colon\text{{seq}}(A)\rightarrow\text{{Part}}_{\text{{fin}}}(A\cup\omega)
by stipulating

a=\langle a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{n-1}\rangle\mapsto\{\{a_{i}\}\cup\{j:a_{i}=a_{j}\}:i<n\}\cup[A\setminus\underline{a}]^{1}\cup[\omega\setminus n]^{1}.

To show that
f
is injective, let
a=\langle a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{m-1}\rangle
,
b=\langle b_{0},b_{1},\dots,b_{n-1}\rangle\in\text{{seq}}(A)
such that
a\neq b
.
If
m=n
, then there is
i<n
such that
a_{i}\neq b_{i}
, so
a_{i}\in[i]_{f(a)}\setminus[i]_{f(b)}
.
Otherwise, if
m<n
, we have
\{m\}\in f(a)\setminus f(b)
. Hence
f(a)\neq f(b)
and so
f
is injective. Thus
|\text{{seq}}(A)|\leq|\text{{Part}}_{\text{{fin}}}(A\cup\omega)|=|\text{{Part}}_{\text{{fin}}}(A)|
.

To show that
|\text{{seq}}(A)|<|\text{{Part}}_{\text{{fin}}}(A\cup\omega)|
, suppose to the contrary that there is an injection
g\colon\text{{Part}}_{\text{{fin}}}(A\cup\omega)\rightarrow\text{{seq}}(A)
. We shall construct a one-to-one
\alpha
-sequence of elements in
\text{{seq}}(A)
for every ordinal
\alpha
. This contradicts Hartogs’ theorem.

First, we construct a one-to-one
\omega
-sequence of elements in
\text{{seq}}(A)
recursively by

\displaystyle s_{0}

\displaystyle=g([A]^{1}\cup\{\{2i,2i+1\}:i<\omega\}),

\displaystyle s_{n+1}

\displaystyle=g(f(s_{n})).

Since
[A]^{1}\cup\{\{2i,2i+1\}:i<\omega\}\not\in\text{{ran}}(f)
where
f
and
g
are injective,
\langle s_{0},s_{1},\dots\rangle_{\omega}
is one-to-one.

Suppose we have already constructed a one-to-one
\alpha
-sequence
\langle s_{0},s_{1},\dots\rangle_{\alpha}
of elements in
\text{{seq}}(A)
for an ordinal
\alpha\geq\omega
. We shall define
s_{\alpha}\in\text{{seq}}(A)
which is distinct from
s_{i}
for all
i<\alpha
.

Let
S=\bigcup_{\iota<\alpha}\underline{s_{\iota}}
. If
S
is finite, then let
a
be the first member of a fixed denumerable subset of
A
which is not in
S
and let
s_{\alpha}=\langle a\rangle
. Suppose
S
is infinite. Define a well-ordering
<_{S}
on
S
by

x<_{S}y\text{ if and only if }i_{x}<i_{y}\text{ or }(i_{x}=i_{y}\text{ and }j_{x}<j_{y}),

where
i_{z}
is the first
i
such that
z\in\underline{s_{i}}
and
j_{z}
is the first
j
such that
s_{i_{z}}(j)=z
for
z\in S
.

Define an injection
F\colon\text{{Part}}_{\text{{fin}}}(S)\rightarrow\text{{seq}}(A)
by

F(\Pi)=g(\Pi\cup[(A\cup\omega)\setminus S]^{1}).

Let
Y=\text{{Part}}_{\text{{fin}}}(S)\setminus F^{-1}[\{s_{\iota}:\iota<\alpha\}]
. Since
|F^{-1}[\{s_{\iota}:\iota<\alpha\}]|\leq|\{s_{\iota}:\iota<\alpha\}|=|\alpha|\leq|\text{{seq}}(S)|=|S|
and
|\text{{Part}}_{\text{{fin}}}(S)|=2^{|S|}
, by Lemma
2.5
,
|Y|=|\text{{Part}}_{\text{{fin}}}(S)|
. Let
p\colon\text{{Part}}_{\text{{fin}}}(S)\rightarrow Y
be the canonical bijection. Then
F\circ p\colon\text{{Part}}_{\text{{fin}}}(S)\to\text{{seq}}(A)\setminus\{s_{\iota}:\iota<\alpha\}
is injective. Let
s_{\alpha}=F\circ p([S]^{1})
. Then
s_{\alpha}\not\in\{s_{\iota}:\iota<\alpha\}
.

We can see that the sequence constructed by the above process is an extension of the sequences previously constructed. Thus we can construct a one-to-one sequence whose length is a limit ordinal as the union of the sequences constructed earlier.
∎

Next, we shall show that if we restrict the set of all finite sequences of a set to the set of all finite sequences with bounded lengths, then the relationship holds for arbitrary infinite sets.

Theorem 2.7
.

For any infinite set
A
and any natural number
n
,

|\text{{seq}}_{\leq n}(A)|<|\text{{Part}}_{\text{{fin}}}(A)|
.

Proof.

Let
A
be an infinite set and
n
be a natural number.
First, we construct an injection from
\text{{seq}}_{\leq n}(A)
to
\text{{Part}}_{\text{{fin}}}(A)
.

Pick
(n+2)^{2}
elements in
A
, say
a^{j}_{i}
where
i,j\leq n+1
and let
A_{j}=\{a^{j}_{i}:i\leq n+1\}
for all
j\leq n+1
. For each
c=\langle c_{0},c_{1},\dots,c_{k-1}\rangle\in\text{{seq}}_{\leq n}(A)
, let
j_{c}
be the first
j
such that
\underline{c}\cap A_{j}=\emptyset
and let

\pi_{c}=\{[c_{\iota}]:\iota<k\}\cup\{\{a^{j_{c}}_{i}:k\leq i\leq n+1\}\}\cup[A\setminus(\underline{c}\cup A_{j_{c}})]^{1},

where
[c_{\iota}]=\{c_{\iota}\}\cup\{a^{j_{c}}_{m}:m<k\text{ and }c_{m}=c_{\iota}\}
for
\iota<k
.

Define
f\colon\text{{seq}}_{\leq n}(A)\to\text{{Part}}_{\text{{fin}}}(A)
by
f(c)=\pi_{c}
. To show that
f
is injective, let
b=\langle b_{0},b_{1},\dots,b_{k-1}\rangle,c=\langle c_{0},c_{1},\dots,c_{\ell-1}\rangle\in\text{{seq}}_{\leq n}(A)
such that
b\neq c
.

Case 1.

j_{b}=j_{c}
.

If there is
i<\min\{k,\ell\}
such that
b_{i}\neq c_{i}
, then
a^{j_{b}}_{i}\in[b_{i}]\cap[c_{i}]
but
[b_{i}]\neq[c_{i}]
, so
\pi_{b}\neq\pi_{c}
. Otherwise,
b
and
c
must be of different lengths. We may assume that
b
is a proper sub-sequence of
c
, so
k<\ell
. Then
c_{k}\not\in\{a^{j_{b}}_{i}:k\leq i\leq n+1\}=[a^{j_{b}}_{k}]_{\pi_{b}}
but
c_{k}\in[a^{j_{b}}_{k}]_{\pi_{c}}
, so
\pi_{b}\neq\pi_{c}
.

Case 2.

j_{b}\neq j_{c}
.

Then
[a^{j_{b}}_{n+1}]_{\pi_{c}}
is a singleton or contains some
a\in A_{j_{c}}
(when
a^{j_{b}}_{n+1}\in\underline{c}
). Since
[a^{j_{b}}_{n+1}]_{\pi_{b}}
is a non-singleton subset of
A_{j_{b}}
and
A_{j_{b}}\cap A_{j_{c}}=\emptyset
,
[a^{j_{b}}_{n+1}]_{\pi_{c}}\neq[a^{j_{b}}_{n+1}]_{\pi_{b}}
. Thus
\pi_{b}\neq\pi_{c}
.

Hence
f
is injective, so
|\text{{seq}}_{\leq n}(A)|\leq|\text{{Part}}_{\text{{fin}}}(A)|
.

Next, suppose to the contrary that
|\text{{seq}}_{\leq n}(A)|=|\text{{Part}}_{\text{{fin}}}(A)|
. Then there is an injection
g\colon\text{{Part}}_{\text{{fin}}}(A)\rightarrow\text{{seq}}_{\leq n}(A)
.

We shall construct a one-to-one
\omega
-sequence of elements in
\text{{seq}}_{\leq n}(A)
.

Let
s_{0}=g(\{A_{j}:j\leq n+1\}\cup[A\setminus\bigcup_{j\leq n+1}A_{j}]^{1})
and
s_{k+1}=g(f(s_{k}))
for all
k<\omega
. Since
\{A_{j}:j\leq n+1\}\cup[A\setminus\bigcup_{j\leq n+1}A_{j}]^{1}\not\in\text{{ran}}(f)
where
g
and
f
are injective,
\langle s_{0},s_{1},\dots\rangle_{\omega}
is a one-to-one
\omega
-sequence of elements in
\text{{seq}}_{\leq n}(A)
. Define
a_{0}=s_{0}(0)
(or
a_{0}=s_{1}(0)
if
s_{0}
is the empty sequence) and
a_{k+1}=s_{i}(j)
where
i
is the first
l
such that
\underline{s_{l}}\setminus\{a_{m}:m\leq k\}\neq\emptyset
and
j
is the first
p
such that
s_{i}(p)\not\in\{a_{m}:m\leq k\}
. Thus
\langle a_{0},a_{1},\dots\rangle_{\omega}
is a one-to-one
\omega
-sequence of elements in
A
, so
A
is Dedekind infinite. This contradicts Theorem
2.6
.
Hence, we can conclude that
|\text{{seq}}_{\leq n}(A)|<|\text{{Part}}_{\text{{fin}}}(A)|
.
∎

3
CONSISTENCY RESULT AND SUMMARY

From a result in ZF, we know that “
|\text{{seq}}(X)|<|\text{{Part}}_{\text{{fin}}}(X)|
for any Dedekind infinite set
X
”.
Now, we show that the assumption that “
X
is Dedekind infinite”  cannot be removed.

We use
permutation models which are models of ZFA, the Zermelo-Fraenkel set theory with atoms. Our relative consistency result can be transferred to ZF by the
Jech-Sochor First Embedding Theorem (cf.
[
5
, Theorem 6.1]
). First, we give a brief explanation of permutation models.

Let
A
be a set of atoms and
\mathcal{G}
be a group of permutations on
A
. Each
\pi\in\mathcal{G}
is extended so that
\pi x=x
for all pure sets
x
, i.e. sets whose transitive closure contain no atoms.
For a normal ideal
I
of subsets of
A
, a set
E\in I
is a
support
of
x
if and only if
\text{{fix}}(E)\subseteq\text{{sym}}(x)
, where
\text{{fix}}(E)=\{\pi\in\mathcal{G}:\pi e=e\text{\ for all\ }e\in E\}
and
\text{{sym}}(x)=\{\pi\in\mathcal{G}:\pi x=x\}
. Then we have a permutation model
\mathcal{V}
induced by a normal ideal, defined hereditarily by

\mathcal{V}=\{x:x\text{\ has a support and\ }x\subseteq\mathcal{V}\}.

The model we use here is the basic Fraenkel model
\mathcal{V}_{F_{0}}
which is a permutation model induced by the normal ideal
\text{{fin}}(A)
where
A
is a denumerable set of atoms and
\mathcal{G}
is the group of all permutations on
A
(see
[
5
, Chapter 4]
for more details). First, we list some notation used in our work.

Notation
.

1.

For any set
X
and
E\in\text{{fin}}(A)
, let
X_{E}=\{x\in X:\text{ $E$ is a support of $x$}\}
.

2.

Let
(a;b)
denote the cyclic permutation of
A
such that
a\mapsto b\mapsto a
and fixes all elements in
A\setminus\{a,b\}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

\mathcal{V}_{F_{0}}\vDash|\text{{seq}}^{1-1}(A)|\not\leq|\text{{Part}}_{\text{{fin}}}(A)|
.

Proof.

Suppose to the contrary that there is an injection
f\colon\text{{seq}}^{1-1}(A)\to\text{{Part}}_{\text{{fin}}}(A)
with a support
E
.
We may assume that
E\neq\emptyset
.

For any
\pi\in\text{{fix}}(E)
and any
x\in\text{{seq}}^{1-1}(A)
, since
\pi(f(x))=(\pi f)(\pi x)=f(\pi x)
where
f
is injective,

\pi(f(x))=f(x)\leftrightarrow f(\pi x)=f(x)\leftrightarrow\pi x=x.

Thus
f[\text{{seq}}^{1-1}(A)_{E}]\subseteq\text{{Part}}_{\text{{fin}}}(A)_{E}
, so
|\text{{seq}}^{1-1}(A)_{E}|\leq|\text{{Part}}_{\text{{fin}}}(A)_{E}|
.
Since
\text{{seq}}^{1-1}(E)\subseteq\text{{seq}}^{1-1}(A)_{E}
,
|\text{{seq}}^{1-1}(E)|\leq|\text{{Part}}_{\text{{fin}}}(A)_{E}|
.
By Corollary
2.2
,
|\text{{seq}}^{1-1}(E)|>|\text{{Part}}(E)|
. Thus, in order to get a contradiction, it remains to show that

|\text{{Part}}(E)|\geq|\text{{Part}}_{\text{{fin}}}(A)_{E}|
.

To show that
\text{{Part}}_{\text{{fin}}}(A)_{E}\subseteq\{Y\cup[A\setminus E]^{1}:Y\in\text{{Part}}(E)\}
, let
\Pi\in\text{{Part}}_{\text{{fin}}}(A)_{E}
. Suppose
[a]_{\Pi}
is not a singleton for some
a\in A\setminus E
. Then pick
b\in A\setminus(E\cup[a]_{\Pi})
and let
\pi=(a;b)
. Thus
\pi
fixes
E
but
\pi(\Pi)\neq\Pi
where as
E
is a support of
\Pi
, a contradiction. Hence
[x]_{\Pi}=\{x\}
for all
x\in A\setminus E
, i.e.
[A\setminus E]^{1}\subseteq\Pi
as desired. Thus
|\text{{Part}}_{\text{{fin}}}(A)_{E}|\leq|\{Y\cup[A\setminus E]^{1}:Y\in\text{{Part}}(E)\}|=|\text{{Part}}(E)|
.
∎

In conclusion, we have shown in ZF that

1.

for any Dedekind infinite set
X
,
|\text{{seq}}(X)|<|\text{{Part}}_{\text{{fin}}}(X)|
(Theorem
2.6
).

2.

for any infinite set
X
and any natural number
n
,
|\text{{seq}}_{\leq n}(X)|<|\text{{Part}}_{\text{{fin}}}(X)|
(Theorem
2.7
).

It follows from the above consistency result that the condition that “
X
is Dedekind infinite set”  in Theorem
2.6
cannot be removed. Actually, Theorem 3.1 tells us more than that. It implies that even the statement “
|\text{{seq}}^{1-1}(X)|\leq|\text{{Part}}_{\text{{fin}}}(X)|
for any infinite set
X
”  is not provable in ZF. However, we are still wondering whether the statement “
|\text{{seq}}^{1-1}(X)|\neq|\text{{Part}}_{\text{{fin}}}(X)|
for any infinite set
X
”  is provable in ZF or not? This is left open for future research.

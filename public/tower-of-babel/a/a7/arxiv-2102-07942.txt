Dirac index of some unitary representations of S ​ p ​ ( 2 ​ n , ℝ ) Sp(2n,\mathbb{R}) and S ​ O ∗ ​ ( 2 ​ n ) SO^{*}(2n)
Chao-Ping Dong, Kayue Daniel Wong

Abstract
. Let G G be S ​ p ​ ( 2 ​ n , ℝ ) Sp(2n,\mathbb{R}) or S ​ O ∗ ​ ( 2 ​ n ) SO^{*}(2n) . We compute the Dirac index of a large class of unitary representations considered by Vogan in Section 8 of [ 32 ] , which include all weakly fair A 𝔮 ​ ( λ ) A_{\mathfrak{q}}(\lambda) modules and (weakly) unipotent representations of G G as two extreme cases. We conjecture that these representations exhaust all unitary representations of G G with nonzero Dirac cohomology. In general, for certain irreducible unitary module of an equal rank group, we clarify the link between the possible cancellations in its Dirac index, and the parities of its spin-lowest K K -types.

1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a connected linear Lie group with Cartan involution
\theta
. Denote by
\widehat{G}
the unitary dual of
G
, which stands for the set of all the equivalence classes of irreducible unitary representations of
G
.
Classification of
\widehat{G}
is a fundamental problem in representation theory of Lie groups. An algorithm answer has been given by Adams, van Leeuwen, Trapa and Vogan
[
1
]
. Including unitarity detecting, the corresponding software implementation
atlas

[
34
]
now computes many aspects of problems in Lie theory.

To get a better understanding of
\widehat{G}
, Vogan introduced Dirac cohomology in 1997
[
33
]
. Let us build up necessary notation to recall this notion. Assume that
K:=G^{\theta}
is a maximal compact subgroup of
G
. Choose a maximal torus
T
of
K
. Write the Lie algebra of
G
,
K
and
T
as
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
,
\mathfrak{k}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{t}_{0}
, respectively. Let

\mathfrak{g}_{0}=\mathfrak{k}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{p}_{0}

be the Cartan decomposition on the Lie algebra level.
Let
\mathfrak{a}_{0}
be the centralizer of
\mathfrak{t}_{0}
in
\mathfrak{p}_{0}
, and put
A=\exp(\mathfrak{a}_{0})
. Then
H=TA
is the unique maximally compact
\theta
-stable Cartan subgroup of
G
, and

\mathfrak{h}_{0}:=\mathfrak{t}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{a}_{0}
is the fundamental Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
. We will write
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{0}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
and so on.

Fix a non-degenerate invariant symmetric bilinear form
B
on
\mathfrak{g}
. We may also write
B
as
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
. Then
\mathfrak{k}
and
\mathfrak{p}
are orthogonal to each other under
B
. Fix an orthonormal basis
\{Z_{1},\dots,Z_{m}\}
of
\mathfrak{p}_{0}
with respect to the inner product on
\mathfrak{p}_{0}
induced by
B
. Let
U(\mathfrak{g})
be the universal enveloping algebra, and let
C(\mathfrak{p})
be the Clifford algebra. As introduced by Parthasarathy
[
24
]
,
the
Dirac operator
is defined as

(1)

D:=\sum_{i=1}^{m}Z_{i}\otimes Z_{i}\in U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes C(\mathfrak{p}),

which is independent of the choice of the orthonormal basis
\{Z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
.
Let
{\rm Ad}:K\to SO(\mathfrak{p}_{0})
be the adjoint map, and
p:{\rm Spin}(\mathfrak{p}_{0})\to SO(\mathfrak{p}_{0})
be the universal covering map. Then

\widetilde{K}:=\{(k,s)\in K\times{\rm Spin}(\mathfrak{p}_{0})\mid{\rm Ad}(k)=p(s)\}

is the spin double cover of
K
. Let
\pi
be any
(\mathfrak{g},K)
module.
The Dirac operator
D
acts on
\pi\otimes{\rm Spin}_{G}
, where
{\rm Spin}_{G}
is a spin module for the Clifford algebra
C(\mathfrak{p})
. The
Dirac cohomology
is defined
[
33
]
as the following
\widetilde{K}
-module:

(2)

H_{D}(\pi):=\mathop{\hbox{Ker}}\nolimits D/(\mathop{\hbox{Ker}}\nolimits D\cap\mathop{\hbox{Im}}\nolimits D).

Fix a positive root system
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t})
once for all, and denote the half sum of roots in
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t})
by
\rho_{c}
. We will use
E_{\mu}
to denote the
\mathfrak{k}
-type (that is, an irreducible representation of
\mathfrak{k}
) with highest weight
\mu
. Abuse the notation a bit,
E_{\mu}
will also stand for the
K
-type as well as the
\widetilde{K}
-type with highest weight
\mu
.

The following Vogan conjecture, proved by Huang and Pandžić
[
17
]
, is foundational for computing
H_{D}(\pi)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

(
Huang-Pandžić

[
17
]
)

Let
\pi
be any irreducible
(\mathfrak{g},K)
module with infinitesimal character
\Lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
. Assume that
H_{D}(\pi)
is non-zero, and that
E_{\gamma}
is contained in
H_{D}(\pi)
. Then
\Lambda
is conjugate to
\gamma+\rho_{c}
by some element in the Weyl group

.

Let
\widehat{G}^{d}
be the
Dirac series
of
G
. That is, the members of
\widehat{G}
with non-zero Dirac cohomology. Classification of Dirac series is a smaller project than the classification of the unitary dual. Yet it is still worthy of pursuing since Dirac series contains many interesting unitary representations such as the discrete series
[
17
]
, certain
A_{\mathfrak{q}}(\lambda)
modules
[
16
]
and beyond. Moreover, due to the research announcement of Barbasch and Pandžić
[
4
]
, Dirac series should have applications in the theory of automorphic forms.

Recently, Dirac series has been classified for complex classical Lie groups
[
5
,
9
,
10
]
and
GL(n,\mathbb{R})

[
11
]
.
For other classical groups such as

,

and

whose unitary dual is unknown, it is still very hard to achieve the complete classification of their Dirac series. In this case, the

can offer some help: it is much easier to compute and whenever it is non-zero, the Dirac cohomology must be non-zero.

Let us build up a bit more notation for introducing Dirac index. Unless stated otherwise, we further assume that
G
is equal rank henceforth. Then
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{t}
and
\mathfrak{a}=0
. Put
\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{R}}=i\mathfrak{t}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}=i\mathfrak{t}_{0}^{*}
. Let
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}
be the dominant Weyl chamber for
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t})
. Choose a positive root system

\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})=\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t})\cup\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{t}).

Let
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{g}}\subseteq\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}
be the dominant Weyl chamber for
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})
.
Let

(3)

W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})^{1}=\{w\in W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})\mid w\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{g}}\subseteq\mathcal{C}\}.

The set
W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})^{1}
has cardinality
s:=|W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})|/|W(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t})|
. Any positive root system
(\Delta^{+})^{\prime}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})
of
\Delta(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})
containing
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t})
has the form

(\Delta^{+})^{\prime}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})=\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t})\cup(\Delta^{+})^{\prime}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{t})

with
(\Delta^{+})^{\prime}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{t})=w\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{t})
for some

.
Denote the half sum of roots in
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})
(resp.,
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{t}))
by
\rho
(resp.,
\rho_{n}
). The notation
\rho^{\prime}
and
\rho_{n}^{\prime}
will be interpreted similarly. Then

\rho=\rho_{c}+\rho_{n},\quad\rho^{\prime}=\rho_{c}+\rho_{n}^{\prime}.

Let

\mathfrak{p}^{+}=\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{t})}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha},\quad\mathfrak{p}^{-}=\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{t})}\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}.

Then
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{p}^{+}\oplus\mathfrak{p}^{-}
and

{\rm Spin}_{G}\cong\bigwedge\mathfrak{p}^{+}\otimes\mathbb{C}_{-\rho_{n}}.

Any weight in
{\rm Spin}_{G}
has the form
-\rho_{n}+\langle\Phi\rangle
, where
\Phi
is a subset of
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{t})
and
\langle\Phi\rangle
stands for the sum of the roots in
\Phi
.
Now put

(4)

{\rm Spin}_{G}^{+}=\bigwedge^{\rm even}\mathfrak{p}^{+}\otimes\mathbb{C}_{-\rho_{n}},\quad{\rm Spin}_{G}^{-}=\bigwedge^{\rm odd}\mathfrak{p}^{+}\otimes\mathbb{C}_{-\rho_{n}}.

The Dirac operator
D
interchanges
\pi\otimes{\rm Spin}^{+}_{G}
and
\pi\otimes{\rm Spin}^{-}_{G}
. Thus the Dirac cohomology
H_{D}(\pi)
breaks up into the even part
H_{D}^{+}(\pi)
and the odd part
H_{D}^{-}(\pi)
. The
Dirac index
of
\pi
is defined as
the virtual
\widetilde{K}
-module

(5)

{\rm DI}(\pi):=H_{D}^{+}(\pi)-H_{D}^{-}(\pi).

By Remark 3.8 of
[
23
]
, if
(\Delta^{+})^{\prime}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})
is chosen instead of
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})
, one has

{\rm DI}^{\prime}(\pi)=(-1)^{\#((\Delta^{+})^{\prime}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{t})\setminus\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{t}))}{\rm DI}(\pi).

Therefore, the Dirac index is well-defined up to a sign. Moreover, by Proposition 3.12 of
[
22
]
,

{\rm DI}(\pi)=\pi\otimes{\rm Spin}^{+}_{G}-\pi\otimes{\rm Spin}^{-}_{G}.

It turns out that the Dirac index preserves short exact sequences and has nice behavior with respect to coherent continuation
[
22
,
23
]
. This idea is pursued in

to compute the Dirac index of all weakly fair

-modules for

In this paper, we study a larger class of unitary representations

2.1.
Dirac index

In this section, we choose
the
Vogan diagram for
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
as Appendix C of Knapp
[
18
]
. Then we have actually chosen a

\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})=\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t})\cup\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{t}).

The Vogan diagram for
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
has a unique black dot, which stands for a simple root
\gamma
. We denote the fundamental weight corresponding to
\gamma
by
\widetilde{\zeta}
. Put

(6)

\zeta=\frac{2}{\|\gamma\|^{2}}\widetilde{\zeta}.

The following result should be well-known, and it can be obtained by going through the classification of real simple Lie algebras.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\beta
be the highest root in
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})
. Then
G/K
is Hermitian symmetric if and only if the unique black dot simple root has coefficient
1
in
\beta
. Otherwise, the unique black dot simple root must have coefficient
2
in
\beta
.
As a consequence, we always have that for any
\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})
,

(7)

\langle\alpha,\zeta\rangle\mbox{ is even}\Leftrightarrow\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t}),\quad\langle\alpha,\zeta\rangle\mbox{ is odd}\Leftrightarrow\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{t}).

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
w\in W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})^{1}
, the set
\Phi_{w}:=w\Delta^{-}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})\cap\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})
must be contained in
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{t})
.

Proof.

Suppose there exists a
w\in W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})^{1}
such that
\Phi_{w}
is not contained in
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{t})
. Then we can find a root
\alpha\in\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t})
such that
\alpha\in\Phi_{w}
. By the definition of
\Phi_{w}
, we can further find a root
\beta\in\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})
such that
\alpha=-w\beta
. That is,
-\alpha=w\beta
. Now,

\langle-\alpha,w\rho\rangle=\langle w\beta,w\rho\rangle=\langle\beta,\rho\rangle>0.

Therefore,
\langle w\rho,\alpha\rangle<0
. This contradicts to the assumption that
w\in W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})^{1}
since
w\rho
should be dominant for
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t})
.
∎

The first named author learned the following result from Pandžić. It should be well-known to the experts.

Lemma 2.3
.

We have the following decompositions of the spin module into
\mathfrak{k}
-types:

(8)

{\rm Spin}^{+}_{G}=\bigoplus_{\scriptsize l(w)\,\mbox{even}}E_{w\rho-\rho_{c}},\quad{\rm Spin}^{-}_{G}=\bigoplus_{\scriptsize l(w)\,\mbox{odd}}E_{w\rho-\rho_{c}},

where
w
runs over the set
W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})^{1}
.

Proof.

As we know,

{\rm Spin}_{G}=\bigwedge\mathfrak{p}^{+}\bigotimes\mathbb{C}_{-\rho_{n}}\cong\bigoplus_{w\in W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})^{1}}V_{w\rho-\rho_{c}}.

It remains to separate the even part and the odd part of the spin module.
Take an arbitrary
w\in W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})^{1}
. Then there exists a subset
\Phi
of
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{t})
such that

w\rho-\rho_{c}=\rho_{n}-\langle\Phi\rangle,

where
\langle\Phi\rangle
stands for the sum of roots in
\Phi
. One deduces from the above equality that

\langle\Phi_{w}\rangle=\langle\Phi\rangle.

Taking inner products of the two sides of the above equality with
\zeta
, we have that

(9)

l(w)\equiv|\Phi|\quad({\rm mod}\ 2)

by recalling (
7
) and Lemma
2.2
.
Note that any weight of
E_{w\rho-\rho_{c}}
can be obtained by subtracting some roots of
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t})
from
w\rho-\rho_{c}
. Now the desired result follows from (
7
).
∎

Let
\pi
be an irreducible unitary
(\mathfrak{g},K)
module. Assume that

is non-zero. Then any
\widetilde{K}
-type

of

lives in either
\pi\otimes{\rm Spin}_{G}^{+}
or
\pi\otimes{\rm Spin}_{G}^{-}
. We assign a sign to

as follows: the sign is
+1
in the first case, it is
-1
otherwise.
We will refer to this sign as the

of
E_{\gamma}
in
H_{D}(\pi)
.

2.2.
Cohomologically induced modules

Firstly, let us fix an element
H\in\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{R}}
which is dominant for
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t})
, and define the
\theta
-stable parabolic subalgebra

(10)

\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{l}\oplus\mathfrak{u}

as the nonnegative eigenspaces of
{\rm ad}(H)
. The Levi subalgebra
\mathfrak{l}
of
\mathfrak{q}
is the zero eigenspace of
{\rm ad}(H)
, while the nilradical
\mathfrak{u}
of
\mathfrak{q}
is the sum of positive eigenspaces of
{\rm ad}(H)
. If we denote by
\overline{\mathfrak{u}}
the sum of negative eigenspaces of
{\rm ad}(H)
, then

\mathfrak{g}=\overline{\mathfrak{u}}\oplus\mathfrak{l}\oplus\mathfrak{u}.

Let
L
be the normalizer of
\mathfrak{q}
in
G
. Then
L\cap K
is a maximal compact subgroup of
L
. Let
\mathfrak{z}
be the center of
\mathfrak{l}
.

We choose a positive root system
(\Delta^{+})^{\prime}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})
containing
\Delta(\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{t})
and
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t})
. Set

\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{t})=\Delta(\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{t})\cap(\Delta^{+})^{\prime}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t}).

Denote by
\rho^{L}
(resp.,
\rho_{c}^{L}
) the half sum of positive roots in
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{t})
(resp.,
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{l}\cap\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t})
). Let
\rho_{n}^{L}=\rho^{L}-\rho_{c}^{L}
. Denote by
\rho(\mathfrak{u})
(resp.,
\rho(\mathfrak{u}\cap\mathfrak{p})
,
\rho(\mathfrak{u}\cap\mathfrak{k})
) the half sum of roots in
\Delta(\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{t})
(resp.,
\Delta(\mathfrak{u}\cap\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{t})
,
\Delta(\mathfrak{u}\cap\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t})
).
The following relations hold

(11)

\rho^{(j)}=\rho^{L}+\rho(\mathfrak{u}),\quad\rho_{c}=\rho_{c}^{L}+\rho(\mathfrak{u}\cap\mathfrak{k}),\quad\rho_{n}^{(j)}=\rho_{n}^{L}+\rho(\mathfrak{u}\cap\mathfrak{k}).

The cohomological induction functors
\mathcal{L}_{j}(\cdot)
and
\mathcal{R}^{j}(\cdot)
lift an admissible
(\mathfrak{l},L\cap K)
module
Z
to
(\mathfrak{g},K)
modules, and the most interesting case happens at the middle degree
S:=\mathop{\hbox{dim}}\nolimits(\mathfrak{u}\cap\mathfrak{k})
. Assume that
Z
has real infinitesimal character
\Lambda_{Z}\in\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}
.
After
[
19
]
, we say that
Z
is in the
good range
(relative to
\mathfrak{q}
and
\mathfrak{g}
) if

(12)

\langle\Lambda_{Z}+\rho(\mathfrak{u}),\alpha\rangle>0,\quad\forall\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{t}).

We say that
Z
is in the
weakly good range
if

(13)

\langle\Lambda_{Z}+\rho(\mathfrak{u}),\alpha\rangle\geq 0,\quad\forall\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{t}).

Moreover,
Z
is said to be in the
fair range
if

(14)

\langle\Lambda_{Z}+\rho(\mathfrak{u}),\alpha|_{\mathfrak{z}}\rangle>0,\quad\forall\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{t}).

We say that
Z
is in the
weakly fair range
if

(15)

\langle\Lambda_{Z}+\rho(\mathfrak{u}),\alpha|_{\mathfrak{z}}\rangle\geq 0,\quad\forall\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{t}).

When the inducing module
Z
is a one-dimensional unitary character
\mathbb{C}_{\lambda}
, we denote the corresponding
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-module
\mathcal{L}_{S}(Z)
by
A_{\mathfrak{q}}(\lambda)
, which has infinitesimal character
\lambda+\rho^{\prime}
.
Good range
A_{\mathfrak{q}}(\lambda)
modules must be non-zero, irreducible, and unitary
[
19
]
.
They play an important role in the unitary dual. Indeed, as shown by Salamanca-Riba
[
28
]
, any irreducible unitary
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-module with a real, integral, and strongly regular infinitesimal character
\Lambda
must be isomorphic to an
A_{\mathfrak{q}}(\lambda)
module in the good range. Here
\Lambda
being
strongly regular
means that

\langle\Lambda-\rho^{\prime},\alpha\rangle\geq 0,\quad\forall\alpha\in(\Delta^{+})^{\prime}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t}).

Note that the module
A_{\mathfrak{q}}(\lambda)
is weakly fair if

(16)

\langle\lambda+\rho(\mathfrak{u}),\alpha\rangle\geq 0,\quad\forall\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{t}).

A weakly fair
A_{\mathfrak{q}}(\lambda)
module can be zero or reducible. However, whenever it is non-zero, it must be unitary
[
19
]
. More importantly, many singular unitary representations can be realized as (a composition factor of) a weakly fair
A_{\mathfrak{q}}(\lambda)
module.

Based on
[
22
,
23
]
, the following result computes the Dirac index of weakly fair
A_{\mathfrak{q}}(\lambda)
.

Theorem 2.4
.

(Theorem 4.3 of
[
12
]
)

The Dirac index of weakly fair
A_{\mathfrak{q}}(\lambda)
is equal to

{\rm DI}(A_{\mathfrak{q}}(\lambda))=\sum_{w\in W(\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{t})^{1}}{\rm det}(w)\widetilde{E}_{w(\lambda+\rho)},

where

(17)

\widetilde{E}_{\mu}=\begin{cases}0&\text{if}\ \mu\ \text{is}\ \Delta(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t})\text{-singular}\\
\mathrm{det}(w)E_{w\mu-\rho_{c}}&\text{if }\exists w\in W(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t})\ \text{s.t. }w\mu\ \text{is dominant regular for }\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t})\end{cases}.

3.1.
Dirac index of
\pi_{u}=X(r,s;\epsilon,\eta)

Let
k
be a positive integer such that
r+s=2k\leq n
, where
r
and

are non-negative integers. Let
\epsilon,\eta
be

or

if

, and set

if

. The irreducible unipotent representation
X(r,s;\epsilon,\eta)
is constructed from the character
\mathbb{C}_{\epsilon,\eta}
of

via theta lifting. Its

-types are as follows:

(18)

(\frac{r-s}{2},\dots,\frac{r-s}{2})+(2a_{1}+\epsilon,\dots,2a_{r}+\epsilon,0,\dots,0,-2b_{s}-\eta,\dots,-2b_{1}-\eta),

where

The infinitesimal character of

As in

2k
coordinates

the

of
\Lambda
, and call the first
n-2k

the

of
\Lambda
. For

,
w\Lambda-\rho_{c}
is the highest weight of a
\widetilde{K}
-type if and only if the entries of

are strictly decreasing. In such a case, we must have that either

(19)

w\Lambda=(i_{1},\dots,i_{u},k,k-1,\dots,-k+1,-j_{v},\dots,-j_{1}),

or

w\Lambda=(i_{1},\dots,i_{u},k-1,\dots,-k+1,-k,-j_{v},\dots,-j_{1}).

Here

and

are two non-negative integers such that

. Moreover,

,

Let

\widetilde{K}
-type. We call
\tau

special
if (
19

\tau

non-special
if (

be the longest element of

is special if and only if
-w_{0}^{K}\tau

Let
\tau:=w\Lambda-\rho_{c}
be a
\widetilde{K}
-type with

given by (

). In particular,

is special. Let

Put

and

Theorem 3.2
.

Each of the following virtual
(\mathfrak{g},K)
module has zero Dirac index:

a)

\displaystyle\sum_{\epsilon+\eta\equiv\delta\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 2)}X(2j-1,2k+1-2j;\epsilon,\eta)
, for any
1\leq j\leq k-1
and
\delta\in\{0,1\}
;

b)

\displaystyle\sum_{\epsilon}X(2k,0;\epsilon,0)+\sum_{\eta}X(0,2k;0,\eta)+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\sum_{\epsilon+\eta\equiv\delta\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 2)}X(2j,2k-2j;\epsilon,\eta)
for
\delta\in\{0,1\}
.

In each case above, both
\epsilon
and
\eta
run over
\{0,1\}
.

Proof.

Fix any special
\widetilde{K}
-type
E_{\tau}:=w\Lambda-\rho_{c}
with
w\Lambda
given by (
19
). We will show that
E_{\tau}
has coefficient zero in the Dirac index of each of the above virtual
(\mathfrak{g},K)
module. One can draw the same conclusion for each non-special
E_{\tau}
. Thus the desired conclusion follows.

(a) By Theorem 3.7(2II) of
[
3
]
, when
\delta\equiv n+1\ ({\rm mod}\ 2)
, each summand has zero Dirac cohomology. Thus the conclusion is trivial. Now assume that
\delta\equiv n\ ({\rm mod}\ 2)
. By Theorem 3.7(2I) of
[
3
]
, the coefficient of
E_{\tau}
in
{\rm DI}(X(2j-1,2k+1-2j;\epsilon,\eta))
is

(-1)^{M(\tau)}{k-1\choose j-1}=(-1)^{j_{1}^{\prime}+\cdots+j_{v}^{\prime}+v+j-1+\epsilon}{k-1\choose j-1}.

The total sum is zero when
\epsilon
and
\eta
run over
\{0,1\}
such that
\epsilon+\eta\equiv\delta\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 2)
.

(b) By Proposition 3.4 of
[
3
]
, the coefficient of
E_{\tau}
in
{\rm DI}(X(2k,0;0,0)+X(2k,0;1,0))
is

(-1)^{j_{1}^{\prime}+\cdots+j_{v}^{\prime}+k(2k-1)}=(-1)^{j_{1}^{\prime}+\cdots+j_{v}^{\prime}+k}.

Similarly, the coefficient of
E_{\tau}
in
{\rm DI}(X(0,2k;0,0)+X(0,2k;0,1))
is

(-1)^{j_{1}^{\prime}+\cdots+j_{v}^{\prime}}.

Fix
1\leq j\leq k-1
, by Theorem 3.7(1) of
[
3
]
, the coefficient of
E_{\tau}
in

{\rm DI}(\sum_{\epsilon+\eta\equiv\delta\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 2)}X(2j,2k-2j;\epsilon,\eta))

is equal to

(-1)^{j_{1}^{\prime}+\cdots+j_{v}^{\prime}+j(2j-1)}{k\choose j}=(-1)^{j_{1}^{\prime}+\cdots+j_{v}^{\prime}+j}{k\choose j}.

Note that
{k-1\choose j}+{k-1\choose j-1}={k\choose j}
.
Therefore, the total sum is

(-1)^{j_{1}^{\prime}+\cdots+j_{v}^{\prime}}\left[1+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}(-1)^{j}{k\choose j}+(-1)^{k}\right]=(-1)^{j_{1}^{\prime}+\cdots+j_{v}^{\prime}}(1-1)^{k}=0.

∎

.

Since a necessary condition for a linear combination for some (non-trivial) special unipotent representations

(a), and the linear combinations in Theorem

(if

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
G=Sp(2n,\mathbb{R})
with
n
odd. The following virtual
(\mathfrak{g},K)
module has zero Dirac index:

X^{\prime}(0,2k;0,0)+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}X^{\prime}(2j,2k-2j;0,0)+X^{\prime}(2k,0;0,0).

Proof.

Let us figure out the coefficient of
E_{0}
in the Dirac index of each

using Theorem 3.8 of
[

]
. Indeed,

\bullet

For

(-1)^{0}{k-1\choose 0}=(-1)^{0}{k\choose 0}
.

\bullet

For

,

, the coefficient is
(-1)^{\frac{2j(2j-1)}{2}}{k\choose j}=(-1)^{j}{k\choose j}
.

\bullet

For

(-1)^{\frac{2k(2k-1)}{2}}{k-1\choose k-1}=(-1)^{k}{k\choose k}

Therefore, the total sum is

(-1)^{0}{k\choose 0}+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}(-1)^{j}{k\choose j}+(-1)^{k}{k\choose k}=(1-1)^{k}=0.

∎

Theorem 4.1
.

Let

. Then the unipotent representations of

whose infinitesimal characters satisfy Theorem

are obtained from theta lifts of the trivial representation in

with

Proof.

By the condition of the infinitesimal character
\Lambda
of
\pi_{u}
given by Theorem
1.1
,
the coordinates of
\Lambda
must be integers. Therefore, the associated variety of

must be
a
special
nilpotent orbit, and
\pi_{u}
must be a
special unipotent representation
.
Therefore,
\Lambda=h^{\vee}/2
, where
h^{\vee}
is the semisimple element of a Jacobson-Morozov triple of a special
(more precisely, even) nilpotent orbit.

Putting in the extra condition that
w\Lambda
is
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t})
-dominant,
w\Lambda

can only be of the form

\Lambda_{k}^{\prime}=(n-k-1,\dots,k,\dots,1,0,-1,\dots,-k)

for

Suppose now the infinitesimal character of
\pi_{u}
is equal to
\Lambda=\Lambda_{k}^{\prime}
. Then

{\rm AV}(Ann_{U(\mathfrak{g})}(\pi_{u}))=\overline{\mathcal{O}_{k}},

where

is the nilpotent orbit in
\mathfrak{so}(2n,\mathbb{C})
corresponding to the partition

[2^{2k}1^{n-2k}]
. By
[
21
]
, the number of such special unipotent representations is equal to
k+1
.

For each
r+s=k
, the theta lift of the trivial representation of
Sp(r,s)

to
SO^{*}(2n)
has infinitesimal character
\Lambda_{k}

[
27
]
. Moreover, its associated variety is equal
to the closure of the
K
-nilpotent orbit
\mathcal{O}_{r,s}
with partition
[(+-)^{2r}(-+)^{2s}+^{n-k}-^{n-k}]
.
See
[
20
]
. Therefore, the

representations constructed in this way are distinct, and
satisfy
{\rm AV}(Ann_{U(\mathfrak{g})}(\pi_{u}))=\overline{\mathcal{O}_{k}}
. This exhausts all
possibilities of unipotent representations.
∎

We denote the representation obtained by theta lift of the trivial representation
of

. In the next subsection, we will study the Dirac cohomology and Dirac index
of

Lemma 5.7

,

∎

Theorem 5.19
.

Let

).

(resp.,

). Then the multiplicity

\left|\widetilde{E}_{(\mathcal{A}^{+};\mathcal{R}_{l};\mathcal{B}_{l}^{+};\dots;\mathcal{R}_{1};\mathcal{B}_{1}^{+};\ \mathcal{K};\ \underline{\mathcal{A}^{-};\mathcal{R}_{l};\mathcal{B}_{l}^{-};\dots;\mathcal{R}_{1};\mathcal{B}_{1}^{-}})}:\mathrm{DI}(\mathcal{L}_{S}(Z(\lambda_{l},\dots,\lambda_{1},X(r,s))))\right|

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{M}_{t}\,\amalg\,\mathcal{N}_{t}=\mathcal{R}_{t};\\

6.
Parity of spin-lowest
K
-types and cancellation in the Dirac index

Let
\pi
be an irreducible unitary
(\mathfrak{g},K)
module. Fix a
K
-type
\mu_{0}
of
\pi
. Let
\mu
be any
K
-type of
\pi
. We assume that

(43)

\langle\mu-\mu_{0},\zeta\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}

We call the parity of this integer the
parity of
\mu
, and denote it by
p(\mu)
.

Definition 6.1
.

We say that the
K
-type
E_{\mu}
of
\pi
is
related to
the
\widetilde{K}
-type
E_{\nu}
of
H_{D}(\pi)
if
E_{\nu}
is a PRV-component
[
25
]
of
E_{\mu}\otimes{\rm Spin}_{G}
.

The following result aims to clarify the link between the possible cancellations of
\widetilde{K}
-types in
H_{D}(\pi)
and the parities of the spin-lowest
K
-types of
\pi
.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible unitary
(\mathfrak{g},K)
module such that (
43
) holds. Then
{\rm Hom}_{\widetilde{K}}(H_{D}^{+}(\pi),H_{D}^{-}(\pi))=0
if and only if for each
\widetilde{K}
-type
E_{\nu}
of
H_{D}(\pi)
(if exists), all the spin lowest
K
-types of
\pi
which are related to
E_{\nu}
have the same parity.

Proof.

It suffices to consider the case that
H_{D}(\pi)\neq 0
. Then
{\rm Hom}_{\widetilde{K}}(H_{D}^{+}(\pi),H_{D}^{-}(\pi))=0
if and only if the occurrences of
E_{\nu}
either
all
live in
H_{D}^{+}(\pi)
, or
all
live in
H_{D}^{-}(\pi)
. Here
E_{\nu}
runs over all the
distinct

\widetilde{K}
-components of
H_{D}(\pi)
.

Take two arbitrary
K
-types
E_{\mu_{1}}
and
E_{\mu_{2}}
of
\pi
which are related to the
\widetilde{K}
-type
E_{\nu}
. Then there exist
w_{1},w_{2}\in W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})^{1}
such that

\{(w_{1}\rho-\rho_{c})+w_{0}^{K}\mu_{1}\}=\{(w_{2}\rho-\rho_{c})+w_{0}^{K}\mu_{2}\}=\nu.

Here
w_{0}^{K}
stands for the unique longest element of
W(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t})
. Removing the two brackets, we have

(44)

(w_{1}\rho-\rho_{c})+\mu_{1}-(\mu_{1}-w_{0}^{K}\mu_{1})+\gamma_{1}=(w_{2}\rho-\rho_{c})+\mu_{2}-(\mu_{2}-w_{0}^{K}\mu_{2})+\gamma_{2},

where each
\gamma_{i}
is a non-negative integer combination of roots in
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t})
, and so is each
\mu_{i}-w_{0}^{K}\mu_{i}
.
Therefore,

\mu_{1}-(\rho-w_{1}\rho)-(\mu_{1}-w_{0}^{K}\mu_{1})+\gamma_{1}=\mu_{2}-(\rho-w_{2}\rho)-(\mu_{2}-w_{0}^{K}\mu_{2})+\gamma_{2}.

In other words,

\mu_{1}-\langle\Phi_{w_{1}}\rangle-(\mu_{1}-w_{0}^{K}\mu_{1})+\gamma_{1}=\mu_{2}-\langle\Phi_{w_{2}}\rangle-(\mu_{2}-w_{0}^{K}\mu_{2})+\gamma_{2}.

Taking inner products with
\zeta
and passing to
{\rm mod}\ 2
, we have that

(45)

p(\mu_{1})+l(w_{1})\equiv p(\mu_{2})+l(w_{2})\quad({\rm mod}\ 2)

by using (
7
). Now the desired conclusion follows from (
45
) and Lemma
2.3
.
∎

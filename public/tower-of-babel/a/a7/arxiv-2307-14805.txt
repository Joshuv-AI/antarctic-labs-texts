Linear Termination over ℕ \boldsymbol{\mathbb{N}} is Undecidable
Fabian Mitterwallner, Aart Middeldorp, René Thiemann

Abstract
Recently it was shown that it is undecidable whether a term rewrite system can be proved terminating by a polynomial interpretation in the natural numbers. In this paper we show that this is also the case when restricting the interpretations to linear polynomials, as is often done in tools, and when only considering single-rule rewrite systems. What is more, the new undecidability proof is simpler than the previous one. We further show that polynomial termination over the rationals/reals is undecidable.

Introduction

In a recent paper
[
3
]
the problem of whether
a finite term rewrite system (TRS) can be proved terminating by a polynomial
interpretation in the naturals numbers was shown to be undecidable. The
result was strengthened by restricting the instance to incremental
polynomially
terminating TRSs. Moreover, incremental polynomial termination over
\mathbb{N}
is
an undecidable property of terminating TRSs.

In this paper we complement these results by proving the somewhat surprising
result that the problem remains undecidable if we restrict the allowed
interpretation functions to linear ones, even for single-rule polynomially
terminating TRSs. A second contribution is that the termination problem is
undecidable if we consider polynomial interpretations over the rationals
and reals. Our undecidability proofs are surprisingly simple.

The results in this paper are obtained by a reduction
from a variant of Hilbert’s tenth problem. Hilbert’s tenth is one of 23
problems published by David Hilbert in 1900 which where all unsolved at
the time. To
solve the tenth problem one should find an algorithm that given a
diophantine equation with integer coefficients determines if the equation
has a solution in the integers
[
1
]
. As is turns out this is
impossible and the underlying decision problem was proved undecidable by
Matijasevic in 1970
[
2
]
.

To simplify the encoding of Hilbert’s tenth problem, we first reduce it to
a slightly modified decision problem. Instead of using an arbitrary integer
polynomial, we consider two polynomials
P
and
Q
with only positive
coefficients and ask if
P(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})\geqslant Q(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})
for some arguments

x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
. This is also undecidable and is
more easily applicable in the proofs related to polynomial termination.

Lemma 1
.

The following decision problem is undecidable:

instance:

two polynomials
P
and
Q
with positive integer
coefficients

question:

P(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})\geqslant Q(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})
for some
x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
?

Proof.

We proceed by a reduction from Hilbert’s 10th problem.
Assume the decision problem is decidable and let
R\in\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}]

be a polynomial. We can modify Hilbert’s 10th problem for
R
as follows:

\displaystyle\exists\,x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}~R(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})=0

\displaystyle\iff~

\displaystyle\exists\,x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}~R(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})^{2}\leqslant 0

\displaystyle\iff~

\displaystyle\exists\,a_{1},\dotsc,a_{n}\in\{\hskip 0.85358pt-1,0,1\hskip 0.85358pt\}~\exists\,x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}~R(a_{1}x_{1},\dots,a_{n}x_{n})^{2}\leqslant 0

(1)

We can now split
R(a_{1}x_{1},\dots,a_{n}x_{n})^{2}
into two polynomials

P_{\vec{a}}
and
Q_{\vec{a}}
containing only positive coefficients, such
that

R(a_{1}x_{1},\dots,a_{n}x_{n})^{2}=Q_{\vec{a}}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})-P_{\vec{a}}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})
.
Hence (1) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\exists\,a_{1},\dotsc,a_{n}\in\{\hskip 0.85358pt-1,0,1\hskip 0.85358pt\}~\exists\,x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}~P_{\vec{a}}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})\geqslant Q_{\vec{a}}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})

The final problem is decidable by our assumption, since it
consists of
3^{n}
instances of the decision problem. This contradicts
the undecidability of Hilbert’s 10th problem, thereby proving the lemma.
∎

2
Undecidability of Linear Termination over
\mathbb{N}

To prove undecidability of linear termination we define a
TRS
\mathcal{R}
which is parameterized by two polynomials
P
and
Q

containing only positive coefficients. We then prove that
\mathcal{R}
can be
shown to be terminating using a linear interpretation if and
only of
P(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})\geqslant Q(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})
for some
x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
.
For polynomials containing the indeterminates
v_{1},\dotsc,v_{n}
, the signature of

\mathcal{R}
is
\mathcal{F}=\{\hskip 0.85358pt\mathsf{z},\mathsf{o},\mathsf{a},\mathsf{f},\mathsf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathsf{v}_{n}\hskip 0.85358pt\}
,
where
\mathsf{z}
and
\mathsf{o}
are constants,

\mathsf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathsf{v}_{n}
are unary symbols,

\mathsf{a}
is a binary symbol and
\mathsf{f}
has arity four.

To this end we first define an encoding
{\ulcorner\cdot\urcorner}^{x}
, which maps
polynomials with positive coefficients to terms containing the variable

x
.

Definition 2
.

Let
P
be a polynomial containing only positive coefficients, and the
indeterminates
v_{1},\dotsc,v_{n}
. We can then encode natural numbers as

\displaystyle{\ulcorner 0\urcorner}^{x}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{z}

\displaystyle{\ulcorner m+1\urcorner}^{x}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{a}(x,{\ulcorner m\urcorner}^{x})

A monomial
M=c\cdot v_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdot v_{2}^{m_{2}}\cdots v_{n}^{m_{n}}

with
c\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and
m_{1},\dotsc,m_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
is encoded as

\displaystyle{\ulcorner M\urcorner}^{x}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{v}_{\mathsf{1}}^{m_{1}}(\mathsf{v}_{\mathsf{2}}^{m_{2}}(\cdots(\mathsf{v}_{\mathsf{n}}^{m_{n}}({\ulcorner c\urcorner}^{x}))\cdots))

where
v^{0}(t)=t
and
v^{i+1}(t)=v(v^{i}(t))

for
v\in\{\hskip 0.85358pt\mathsf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathsf{v}_{n}\hskip 0.85358pt\}
. Finally the polynomial

P=M_{1}+M_{2}+\cdots+M_{k}
is encoded as

{\ulcorner P\urcorner}^{x}=\mathsf{a}({\ulcorner M_{1}\urcorner}^{x},\mathsf{a}({\ulcorner M_{2}\urcorner}^{x},\cdots\mathsf{a}({\ulcorner M_{k}\urcorner}^{x},\mathsf{z})\cdots))

Example 3
.

For the polynomial
P=X^{3}+2X+2
we obtain the term

{\ulcorner P\urcorner}^{y}=\mathsf{a}(\,\underbrace{\mathsf{X}(\mathsf{X}(\mathsf{X}(\mathsf{a}(y,\mathsf{z}))))}_{{\ulcorner X^{3}\urcorner}}\,,\mathsf{a}(\,\underbrace{\mathsf{X}(\mathsf{a}(y,\mathsf{a}(y,\mathsf{z})))}_{{\ulcorner 2X\urcorner}}\,,\mathsf{a}(\,\underbrace{\mathsf{a}(y,\mathsf{a}(y,\mathsf{z}))}_{{\ulcorner 2\urcorner}}\,,\mathsf{z})))

The TRS
\mathcal{R}
can then be defined via this encoding.

Definition 4
.

For polynomials
P
and
Q
containing only positive coefficients we
obtain the
TRS

\mathcal{R}
consisting of the single rule

\mathsf{f}(y_{1},y_{2},\mathsf{a}({\ulcorner P\urcorner}^{y_{3}},y_{3}),\mathsf{o})\to\mathsf{f}(\mathsf{a}(y_{1},\mathsf{z}),\mathsf{a}(\mathsf{z},y_{2}),\mathsf{a}({\ulcorner Q\urcorner}^{y_{3}},y_{3}),\mathsf{z})

The rule serves two purposes. First it constrains any linear
interpretation proving its termination to conform to a very limited
shape. Secondly it uses these restricted shapes
to encode the inequality
P\geqslant Q
in the orientation of the rule

[\ell]_{\mathbb{N}}>[r]_{\mathbb{N}}
. This leads to the following result.

Theorem 5
.

Termination of
\mathcal{R}
can be shown by a linear interpretation
if and only if
P(v_{1},\dotsc,v_{n})\geqslant Q(v_{1},\dotsc,v_{n})
for some

v_{1},\dotsc,v_{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
.

Proof.

For the if direction assume
P(v_{1},\dotsc,v_{n})\geqslant Q(v_{1},\dotsc,v_{n})

for some
v_{1},\dotsc,v_{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
. We then choose the monotone interpretations

\displaystyle\mathsf{z}_{\mathbb{N}}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\mathsf{a}_{\mathbb{N}}(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=x_{1}+x_{2}

\displaystyle\mathsf{v_{\mathit{i}}}_{\mathbb{N}}(x)

\displaystyle=v_{i}\cdot x\quad\text{for all $i\in\{\hskip 0.85358pt1,\dotsc,n\hskip 0.85358pt\}$}

\displaystyle\mathsf{o}_{\mathbb{N}}

\displaystyle=1

\displaystyle\mathsf{f}_{\mathbb{N}}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})

\displaystyle=x_{1}+x_{2}+\mathrlap{x_{3}+x_{4}}

Note that using this interpretation we have

[{\ulcorner P\urcorner}^{y_{3}}]_{\mathbb{N}}=P(v_{1},\dotsc,v_{n})\cdot y_{3}
,
and the same holds for
Q
. Hence we orient the rule in
\mathcal{R}
, as
seen in

[\ell]_{\mathbb{N}}=y_{1}+y_{2}+(P(v_{1},\dotsc,v_{n})+1)y_{3}+1>y_{1}+y_{2}+(Q(v_{1},\dotsc,v_{n})+1)y_{3}=[r]_{\mathbb{N}}

For the only-if direction we assume a linear interpretation for all

f\in\mathcal{F}
, such that
[\ell]_{\mathbb{N}}>[r]_{\mathbb{N}}
.
Hence we know that all interpretations have the shape

{f}_{\mathbb{N}}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k})=f_{0}+f_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+f_{k}x_{k}

where
f_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
and
f_{1},\dotsc,f_{k}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
due to monotonicity.
For any term
t
we write
[t]_{\mathbb{N}}^{y_{i}}
for the coefficient of the
indeterminate
y_{i}
of the linear polynomial
[t]_{\mathbb{N}}
. Using this
notation,
[\ell]_{\mathbb{N}}>[r]_{\mathbb{N}}
implies

[\ell]_{\mathbb{N}}^{y_{i}}\geqslant[r]_{\mathbb{N}}^{y_{i}}
for
i\in\{\hskip 0.85358pt1,2,3\hskip 0.85358pt\}
.
By the shape of the rule we deduce

\mathsf{f_{1}}=[\ell]_{\mathbb{N}}^{y_{1}}\geqslant[r]_{\mathbb{N}}^{y_{1}}=\mathsf{f_{1}}\mathsf{a_{1}}

and in combination with
\mathsf{f_{1}}>0
and
\mathsf{a_{1}}>0
we conclude

\mathsf{a_{1}}=1
.
Similarly, from
[\ell]_{\mathbb{N}}^{y_{2}}\geqslant[r]_{\mathbb{N}}^{y_{2}}
we infer

\mathsf{a_{2}}=1
, and in turn

\mathsf{a}_{\mathbb{N}}(x_{1},x_{2})=x_{1}+x_{2}+\mathsf{a_{0}}
for some
\mathsf{a_{0}}\in\mathbb{N}
.
Due to the shape
\mathsf{a}_{\mathbb{N}}
it is easy to see that

[{\ulcorner m\urcorner}^{y_{3}}]_{\mathbb{N}}^{y_{3}}=m
for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

[c\cdot{\ulcorner v_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots v_{n}^{m_{n}}\urcorner}^{y_{3}}]_{\mathbb{N}}^{y_{3}}=c\cdot v_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots v_{n}^{m_{n}}
and further

[{\ulcorner P\urcorner}^{y_{3}}]_{\mathbb{N}}^{y_{3}}=P(v_{1},\dotsc,v_{n})
for any polynomial
P
. Hence

\mathsf{f_{3}}\cdot(P(v_{1},\dotsc,v_{n})+1)=[\ell]_{\mathbb{N}}^{y_{3}}\geqslant[r]_{\mathbb{N}}^{y_{3}}=\mathsf{f_{3}}\cdot(Q(v_{1},\dotsc,v_{n})+1)

Since
\mathsf{f_{3}}>0
, division by
\mathsf{f_{3}}
is possible, resulting in the
desired inequality
P(v_{1},\dotsc,v_{n})\geqslant Q(v_{1},\dotsc,v_{n})
for

v_{1},\dotsc,v_{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
.
∎

Corollary 6
.

Linear termination is undecidable, even for single-rule
TRS
s.

Proof.

This follows directly from Theorem
5
and Lemma
1
.
∎

Interestingly the TRS
\mathcal{R}
is always terminating, independent of the
polynomials
P
and
Q
. This can be shown using a (non-linear)
polynomial interpretation.

Lemma 7
.

The
TRS

\mathcal{R}
is polynomially terminating.

Proof.

Use the following monotone interpretation over
\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\mathsf{o}_{\mathbb{N}}

\displaystyle=Q(1,\dots\mathrlap{,1)+1}

\displaystyle\mathsf{a}_{\mathbb{N}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=x+y

\displaystyle\mathsf{v_{\mathit{i}}}_{\mathbb{N}}(x)

\displaystyle=x\quad\text{for all $i\in\{\hskip 0.85358pt1,\dotsc,n\hskip 0.85358pt\}$}

\displaystyle\mathsf{z}_{\mathbb{N}}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\mathsf{f}_{\mathbb{N}}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})

\displaystyle=x_{3}x_{4}+x_{1}\mathrlap{{}+x_{2}+x_{3}+x_{4}}

Note that due to
[\mathsf{v_{\mathit{i}}}(x)]_{\mathbb{N}}^{x}=1
we have

[{\ulcorner P\urcorner}^{y_{3}}]_{\mathbb{N}}^{y_{3}}=P(1,\dots,1)
and

[{\ulcorner Q\urcorner}^{y_{3}}]_{\mathbb{N}}^{y_{3}}=Q(1,\dots,1)
.
Hence, we can orient the rule as seen in

\displaystyle[\ell]_{\mathbb{N}}

\displaystyle=(Q(1,\dots,1)+1)(P(1,\dots,1)+1)y_{3}+{}

\displaystyle\phantom{{}={}}y_{1}+y_{2}+(P(1,\dots,1)+1)y_{3}+(Q(1,\dots,1)+1)

\displaystyle>y_{1}+y_{2}+(Q(1,\dots,1)+1)y_{3}=[r]_{\mathbb{N}}\qed

Corollary 8
.

Linear termination is undecidable, even for polynomially
terminating single-rule systems.

3
Polynomial Termination over
\mathbb{Q}
and
\mathbb{R}

When considering polynomial interpretations over
\mathbb{Q}
and
\mathbb{R}
, we
restrict the domain to all non-negative values. Moreover, when comparing the
polynomials associated with the left- and right-hand side of a rewrite rule,
we demand that the difference is at least
\delta
, for some fixed positive
value
\delta
of the domain. This ensures termination. We refer to
[
4
]

for formal definitions as well as the relationship
between the notions of polynomial termination over
\mathbb{N}
,
\mathbb{Q}
and
\mathbb{R}
.

In the previous section we encoded polynomials as terms such that
indeterminates of the polynomials correspond to coefficients of some
interpretation.
When dealing with polynomial termination over
\mathbb{Q}
and
\mathbb{R}

a new approach for proving undecidability is required,
since coefficients take values in
\mathbb{Q}
or
\mathbb{R}
.
However, what does not change is that the exponents of our
interpretations must still be natural numbers. We can make use of this by
encoding the polynomials and the order on polynomials in the degrees of
our interpretations. As long as we can represent multiplication in the
interpretations we can use basic arithmetic to encode the polynomials in
the degrees.

Lemma 9
.

If
P
and
Q
are univariate polynomials containing only positive
coefficients then

1.

\deg(P+Q)=\max(\deg(P),\deg(Q))
,

2.

\deg(P\cdot Q)=\deg(P)+\deg(Q)
, and

3.

\deg(P\circ Q)=\deg(P)\cdot\deg(Q)
. ∎

For encoding polynomials with positive coefficients
as terms we use
\Cref
def:encoding, so using function symbols from

\{\hskip 0.85358pt\mathsf{z},\mathsf{a}\hskip 0.85358pt\}\cup\{\hskip 0.85358pt\mathsf{v}_{i}\mid 1\leqslant i\leqslant n\hskip 0.85358pt\}
.
Moreover, we write
{\ulcorner P\urcorner}
for
{\ulcorner P\urcorner}^{x}
with some fixed variable
x
.
The polynomial is then encoded in the degree of
{\ulcorner P\urcorner}
, as seen in the
following lemma, which can be proved using a simple induction over

\Cref
def:encoding.

Lemma 10
.

Let
\mathcal{D}\in\{\hskip 0.85358pt\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{R}\hskip 0.85358pt\}
and suppose
\mathsf{z}_{\mathcal{D}}=z_{0}
and

\mathsf{a}_{\mathcal{D}}=a_{3}xy+a_{2}x+a_{1}y+a_{0}
for some

z_{0},a_{0}\in\mathcal{D}_{\geqslant 0}
and
a_{3},a_{2},a_{1}\in\mathcal{D}_{>0}
.
If
P\in\mathbb{Z}[v_{1},\dotsc,v_{n}]
with positive coefficients then

\deg([{\ulcorner P\urcorner}]_{\mathcal{D}})=P(\deg([\mathsf{v}_{1}]_{\mathcal{D}}),\dots,\deg([\mathsf{v}_{n}]_{\mathcal{D}}))
.

Proof.

We use induction on the definition of
{\ulcorner P\urcorner}
.
If
P=0
then
[{\ulcorner P\urcorner}]_{\mathcal{D}}=\mathsf{z}
and thus

\deg([{\ulcorner P\urcorner}]_{\mathcal{D}})=0=P
. For
P=m+1
we obtain

{\ulcorner P\urcorner}=\mathsf{a}(x,{\ulcorner m\urcorner})
and thus

[{\ulcorner P\urcorner}]_{\mathcal{D}}=a_{3}\cdot[{\ulcorner m\urcorner}]_{\mathcal{D}}\cdot x+a_{2}\cdot x+a_{1}\cdot[{\ulcorner m\urcorner}]_{\mathcal{D}}+a_{0}

Hence
\deg([{\ulcorner P\urcorner}]_{\mathcal{D}})=\deg([{\ulcorner m\urcorner}]_{\mathcal{D}})+1=m+1

by the induction hypothesis. For a monomial

P=c\cdot v_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots v_{n}^{m_{n}}
with

c\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and
m_{1},\dotsc,m_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
we obtain

\displaystyle\deg([{\ulcorner P\urcorner}]_{\mathcal{D}})

\displaystyle=\deg([{\ulcorner c\urcorner}]_{\mathcal{D}})\cdot\deg([\mathsf{v}_{1}]_{\mathcal{D}})^{m_{1}}\,\cdots\,\deg([\mathsf{v}_{n}]_{\mathcal{D}})^{m_{n}}

\displaystyle=c\cdot\deg([\mathsf{v}_{1}]_{\mathcal{D}})^{m_{1}}\,\cdots\,\deg([\mathsf{v}_{n}]_{\mathcal{D}})^{m_{n}}

\displaystyle=P(\deg([\mathsf{v}_{1}]_{\mathcal{D}}),\dots,\deg([\mathsf{v}_{n}]_{\mathcal{D}}))

Finally, if
P=M_{1}+\cdots+M_{k}
then

{\ulcorner P\urcorner}=\mathsf{a}({\ulcorner M_{1}\urcorner},\cdots\mathsf{a}({\ulcorner M_{k}\urcorner},\mathsf{z})\cdots)

and thus

\displaystyle\deg{[{\ulcorner P\urcorner}]_{\mathcal{D}}}

\displaystyle=\deg([{\ulcorner M_{1}\urcorner}]_{\mathcal{D}})+\cdots+\deg([{\ulcorner M_{k}\urcorner}]_{\mathcal{D}})

\displaystyle=M_{1}(\deg([\mathsf{v}_{1}]_{\mathcal{D}}),\dots,\deg([\mathsf{v}_{n}]_{\mathcal{D}}))+\cdots+M_{k}(\deg([\mathsf{v}_{1}]_{\mathcal{D}}),\dots,\deg(\mathrlap{[\mathsf{v}_{n}]_{\mathcal{D}}))}

\displaystyle=P(\deg([\mathsf{v}_{1}]_{\mathcal{D}}),\dots,\deg([\mathsf{v}_{k}]_{\mathcal{D}}))\qed

Definition 11
.

Given polynomials
P
and
Q
containing only positive coefficients and
containing the indeterminates
v_{1},\dotsc,v_{n}
, the
TRS

\mathcal{Q}
is defined
over the signature

\mathcal{F}=\{\hskip 0.85358pt\mathsf{z},\mathsf{a},\mathsf{h},\mathsf{q},\mathsf{g}\hskip 0.85358pt\}\cup\{\hskip 0.85358pt\mathsf{v}_{i}\mid i\in\{\hskip 0.85358pt1,\dotsc,n\hskip 0.85358pt\}\hskip 0.85358pt\}
and
consists of the rules

\displaystyle\mathsf{q}(\mathsf{h}(x))

\displaystyle\,\xrightarrow{\,\makebox[5.69054pt]{$\scriptstyle 1$}\,}\,\mathsf{h}(\mathsf{h}(\mathsf{q}(x)))

\displaystyle\mathsf{a}(x,x)

\displaystyle\,\xrightarrow{\,\makebox[5.69054pt]{$\scriptstyle 5$}\,}\,\mathsf{q}(x)

\displaystyle\mathsf{h}(x)

\displaystyle\,\xrightarrow{\,\makebox[5.69054pt]{$\scriptstyle 2$}\,}\,\mathsf{g}(x,x)

\displaystyle\mathsf{h}(x)

\displaystyle\,\xrightarrow{\,\makebox[5.69054pt]{$\scriptstyle 6$}\,}\,\mathsf{a}(\mathsf{z},x)

\displaystyle\mathsf{g}(\mathsf{q}(x),\mathsf{h}(\mathsf{h}(\mathsf{h}(x))))

\displaystyle\,\xrightarrow{\,\makebox[5.69054pt]{$\scriptstyle 3$}\,}\,\mathsf{q}(\mathsf{g}(x,\mathsf{h}(\mathsf{z})))

\displaystyle\mathsf{h}(x)

\displaystyle\,\xrightarrow{\,\makebox[5.69054pt]{$\scriptstyle 7$}\,}\,\mathsf{a}(x,\mathsf{z})

\displaystyle\mathsf{h}(\mathsf{q}(x))

\displaystyle\,\xrightarrow{\,\makebox[5.69054pt]{$\scriptstyle 4$}\,}\,\mathsf{a}(x,x)

\displaystyle\mathsf{h}(\mathsf{a}({\ulcorner P\urcorner},x))

\displaystyle\,\xrightarrow{\,\makebox[5.69054pt]{$\scriptstyle 8$}\,}\,\mathsf{a}({\ulcorner Q\urcorner},x)

The main idea behind this system is that rules (1) through (7)
restrict the possible interpretations of
\mathsf{a}_{\mathcal{D}}
and
\mathsf{h}_{\mathcal{D}}

such that Lemma
10
is applicable, and that compatibility with
rule (8) implies
P(v_{1},\dotsc,v_{n})\geqslant Q(v_{1},\dotsc,v_{n})
.
The rules (1)–(7) are similar to ones already used in
[
5
]
,
where they restrict possible interpretations over
\mathbb{N}
,
and in
[
4
]
, where they are also applied to interpretations over

\mathbb{Q}
and
\mathbb{R}
.

Theorem 12
.

For
\mathcal{D}\in\{\hskip 0.85358pt\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{R}\hskip 0.85358pt\}
and polynomials
P,Q\in\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}]

with positive integer coefficients the
TRS

\mathcal{Q}
can be
proved terminating
using a polynomial interpretation over
\mathcal{D}
if and only if

P(v_{1},\dotsc,v_{n})\geqslant Q(v_{1},\dotsc,v_{n})
for some
v_{1},\dotsc,v_{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
.

Proof.

For the if direction assume
P(v_{1},\dotsc,v_{n})\geqslant Q(v_{1},\dotsc,v_{n})
for some

v_{1},\dotsc,v_{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
. Take the interpretations

\displaystyle\mathsf{z}_{\mathcal{D}}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\mathsf{g}_{\mathcal{D}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=x+y

\displaystyle\mathsf{a}_{\mathcal{D}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=xy+x+y+1

\displaystyle\mathsf{q}_{\mathcal{D}}(x)

\displaystyle=x^{2}+2x

\displaystyle\mathsf{h}_{\mathcal{D}}(x)

\displaystyle=hx+h

\displaystyle\mathsf{v_{\mathit{i}}}_{\mathcal{D}}(x)

\displaystyle=x^{v_{i}}\quad\text{for all $i\in\{\hskip 0.85358pt1,\dotsc,n\hskip 0.85358pt\}$}

where
h>2
. With
\delta=1
these orient the rules
(1) – (7). Lemma
10
yields

\deg([{\ulcorner P\urcorner}]_{\mathcal{D}})=P(\deg([\mathsf{v}_{1}]_{\mathcal{D}}),\dots,\deg([\mathsf{v}_{n}]_{\mathcal{D}}))=P(v_{1},\dotsc,v_{n})
and similarly
\deg([{\ulcorner Q\urcorner}]_{\mathcal{D}})=Q(v_{1},\dotsc,v_{n})
.
From the assumption
P(v_{1},\dotsc,v_{n})\geqslant Q(v_{1},\dotsc,v_{n})
we therefore
obtain
\deg([{\ulcorner P\urcorner}]_{\mathcal{D}})\geqslant\deg([{\ulcorner Q\urcorner}]_{\mathcal{D}})
.
Consequently,

\deg([\mathsf{h}(\mathsf{a}({\ulcorner P\urcorner},x))]_{\mathcal{D}})=\deg([{\ulcorner P\urcorner}]_{\mathcal{D}})+1\geqslant\deg([{\ulcorner Q\urcorner}]_{\mathcal{D}})+1=[\mathsf{a}({\ulcorner Q\urcorner},x)]_{\mathcal{D}}

It follows that by choosing the coefficient
h
large enough,
the remaining rule (8) is oriented.

For the only-if direction assume the TRS
\mathcal{Q}
is
polynomially terminating over
\mathcal{D}
. From compatibility
with rule (1) we infer

\deg([\mathsf{q}(\mathsf{h}(x))]_{\mathcal{D}})=\deg(\mathsf{q}_{\mathcal{D}})\cdot\deg(\mathsf{h}_{\mathcal{D}})\geqslant\deg(\mathsf{q}_{\mathcal{D}})\cdot\deg(\mathsf{h}_{\mathcal{D}})^{2}=\deg{[\mathsf{h}(\mathsf{h}(\mathsf{q}(x)))]_{\mathcal{D}}}

Hence
\deg(\mathsf{h}_{\mathcal{D}})=1
and thus
\mathsf{h}_{\mathcal{D}}(x)=h_{1}x+h_{0}

for some
h_{1}\geqslant 1
and
h_{0}\geqslant 0
. From compatibility
with rule (2) we infer
\deg(\mathsf{g}_{\mathcal{D}})=1
and thus

\mathsf{g}_{\mathcal{D}}(x,y)=g_{2}x+g_{1}y+g_{0}
with
g_{2},g_{1}\geqslant 1
.
Moreover,
h_{1}\geqslant g_{1}+g_{2}\geqslant 2
and

h_{0}>_{\delta}g_{0}\geqslant 0
. Looking back at rule (1) we now
can infer that
\mathsf{q}_{\mathcal{D}}
is at least quadratic, since if it were
linear we would obtain the inequality

q_{1}h_{1}\cdot x+q_{1}h_{0}+q_{0}>_{\delta}q_{1}h_{1}^{2}\cdot x+h_{1}^{2}q_{0}+h_{1}h_{0}+h_{0}

for all
x\in\mathcal{D}_{\geqslant 0}
. This can only hold if

q_{1}h_{1}\geqslant q_{1}h_{1}^{2}
, which in turn implies
h_{1}\leqslant 1
,
contradicting
h_{1}\geqslant 2
. Next we show

\deg(\mathsf{q}_{\mathcal{D}})=2
.
Compatibility with rule (3) induces the constraint

g_{2}\cdot[\mathsf{q}(x)]_{\mathcal{D}}+g_{1}\cdot[\mathsf{h}(\mathsf{h}(\mathsf{h}(x)))]_{\mathcal{D}}+g_{0}>_{\delta}[\mathsf{q}(\mathsf{g}(x,\mathsf{h}(\mathsf{z})))]_{\mathcal{D}}
, which implies

1=\deg([\mathsf{h}(\mathsf{h}(\mathsf{h}(x)))]_{\mathcal{D}}\geqslant\deg([\mathsf{q}(\mathsf{g}(x,\mathsf{h}(\mathsf{z})))]_{\mathcal{D}}-g_{2}\cdot[\mathsf{q}(x)]_{\mathbb{N}})

Since
h_{0}>0
and
[\mathsf{h}(\mathsf{z})]_{\mathcal{D}}>0
this can only be the
case if
\deg(\mathsf{q}_{\mathcal{D}})=2
. Using this fact together with
compatibility with the rules (4) and (5) we
infer
\deg(\mathsf{a}_{\mathcal{D}})=2
and hence

\mathsf{a}_{\mathcal{D}}(x,y)=a_{5}x^{2}+a_{4}y^{2}+a_{3}xy+a_{2}x+a_{1}y+a_{0}
.
Compatibility with rules (6) and (7) implies

a_{5}=a_{4}=0
resulting in

\mathsf{a}_{\mathcal{D}}(x,y)=a_{3}xy+a_{2}x+a_{1}y+a_{0}
with

a_{3},a_{2},a_{1}\in\mathcal{D}_{>0}
. Compatibility with (8) implies

\deg([{\ulcorner P\urcorner}]_{\mathcal{D}})+1\geqslant\deg([{\ulcorner Q\urcorner}]_{\mathcal{D}})+1
.
With the help of Lemma
10
we obtain

P(\deg([\mathsf{v}_{1}]_{\mathcal{D}}),\dots,\deg([\mathsf{v}_{n}]_{\mathcal{D}}))\geqslant Q(\deg([\mathsf{v}_{1}]_{\mathcal{D}}),\dots,\deg([\mathsf{v}_{n}]_{\mathcal{D}}))
.
∎

Corollary 13
.

Polynomial termination over
\mathbb{Q}
and
\mathbb{R}
is undecidable. ∎

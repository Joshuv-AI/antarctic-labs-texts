Spherically symmetric Einstein-scalar-field equations for slowly particle-like decaying null infinity
Chuxiao Liu 1,4, Xiao Zhang 2,3,4

Abstract
. We show that the spherically symmetric Einstein-scalar-field equations for small slowly particle-like decaying initial data at null infinity have unique global solutions.

1.
Introduction

Spherically symmetric spacetime metrics can be written as

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-gqdu^{2}-2gdudr+r^{2}(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\psi^{2})

(1.1)

in Bondi coordinates, see, e.g.
[
5
,
11
,
12
]
, where
g(u,r)
and
q(u,r)
are
C^{2}
nonnegative functions over
(0,\infty)
. The null frames are

\displaystyle\vec{n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{gq}}D,\hskip 10.00002pt\vec{l}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{gq^{-1}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r},\hskip 10.00002pte_{1}=\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta},\hskip 10.00002pte_{2}=\frac{1}{r\sin\theta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi},

where

\displaystyle D=\frac{\partial}{\partial u}-\frac{q}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}

is the derivative along the incoming light rays.

Throughout the paper, we denote by
\bar{f}(r)
the integral average of integrable function
f(r)
over
[0,r]

\displaystyle\bar{f}(r)=\frac{1}{r}\int_{0}^{r}f(r^{\prime})dr^{\prime}.

For a real
C^{2}
scalar field
\phi(u,r)
on
(0,\infty)\times(0,\infty)
,
the Einstein-scalar field equations are

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu}=8\pi\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi,\hskip 10.00002pt\Box{\phi}=0.

(1.2)

Under the following regularity conditions at
r=0
and boundary condition at
r=\infty
,

Regularity Condition I
: For each
u
,

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}\left(-\frac{rq}{g}\frac{\partial g}{\partial u}+r\frac{\partial q}{\partial u}+\frac{rq^{2}}{2g}\frac{\partial g}{\partial r}-8\pi r^{2}\Big(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial u}-\frac{q}{2}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial r}\Big)^{2}\right)=0.

Regularity Condition II
: For each
u
,

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}\big(r\phi\big)=\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}\big(rq\big)=0.

Boundary Condition
: For each
u
,

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}g=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}q=1,

the Einstein-scalar field equations (
1.2
) are equivalent to the following systems, see, e.g.
[
12
]

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}\left\{\begin{aligned} h&=\frac{\partial(r\phi)}{\partial r},\\
g&=\exp{\left\{-4\pi\int_{r}^{\infty}{\frac{(h-\bar{h})^{2}}{r^{\prime}}dr^{\prime}}\right\}},\\
q&=\bar{g}=\frac{1}{r}\int_{0}^{r}{gdr^{\prime}},\\
Dh&=\frac{g-\bar{g}}{2r}(h-\bar{h}).\end{aligned}\right.\end{matrix}

(1.3)

The Bondi mass
M_{B}(u)
for each
u
and the final Bondi mass
M_{B1}
are given by
[
2
,
11
]

\displaystyle M_{B}(u)=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{r}{2}\Big(1-q\Big),\hskip 10.00002ptM_{B1}=\lim_{u\rightarrow\infty}M_{B}(u).

The Bondi-Christodoulou mass
M(u)
for each
u
and the final Bondi-Christodoulou mass
M_{1}
are given by
[
5
,
8
,
11
]

\displaystyle M(u)=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{r}{2}\Big(1-\frac{q}{g}\Big),\hskip 10.00002ptM_{1}=\lim_{u\rightarrow\infty}M(u).

In
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
, Christodoulou studied global existence and uniqueness of classical solutions with small initial data, and of generalized solutions with large initial data, when the initial data satisfies the following decaying property

\displaystyle h(0,r)=O(r^{-3}),\hskip 10.00002pt\frac{\partial h}{\partial r}(0,r)=O(r^{-4}),\hskip 10.00002ptas\,\,r\rightarrow\infty.

In particular, he proved that the solution satisfies the following uniformly decaying estimates

\displaystyle\big|h(u,r)\big|\leq\frac{C}{(1+u+r)^{3}},\hskip 10.00002pt\Big|\frac{\partial h}{\partial r}(u,r)\Big|\leq\frac{C}{(1+u+r)^{4}},

and the corresponding spacetime is future causally geodesically complete with vanishing final Bondi-Christodoulou mass.

For the Einstein-scalar field equations in double null coordinates

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-\Omega^{2}dudv+r^{2}\big(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2}\big),

(1.4)

Christodoulou also showed the unique spherically symmetric global solution exists for the characteristic initial-value problem with small bounded variation norms
[
9
]
. Later, his result was extended to the more general situation by Luk, Oh and Yang, and they showed the unique spherically symmetric global solution exists and the resulting spacetime is future causally geodesically complete if the initial data satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{u}^{v}\big|\Phi(v^{\prime})\big|dv^{\prime}\leq\epsilon\big(v-u\big)^{1-\gamma},\quad|\Phi(v)|+\Big|\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial v}(v)\Big|\leq\delta\end{aligned}

for certain positive constants
\gamma
,
\delta
, where

\displaystyle\Phi(v)=\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\big(r\phi\big)(u_{0},v)

and

. The solution satisfies a priori estimates

\displaystyle\partial_{v}r>\frac{1}{3},\hskip 10.00002pt-\frac{1}{6}

\displaystyle>\partial_{u}r>-\frac{2}{3},\hskip 10.00002pt\frac{2m}{r}<\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle|\phi|\leq C\delta\min\{1,r^{-\gamma}\},\hskip 10.00002pt|\partial_{v}

\displaystyle(r\phi)|\leq C\big(|\Phi(v)|+\delta\min\{1,r^{-\gamma}\}\big),

\displaystyle|\partial_{u}(r\phi)|\leq C\delta,\hskip 10.00002pt|\partial^{2}_{v}(r\phi

\displaystyle)|+|\partial^{2}_{v}r|+|\partial^{2}_{u}(r\phi)|+|\partial^{2}_{u}r|\leq C

where
C>0
is some constant depending on
\gamma
. However, the solution does not have uniformly decaying estimates in

unless the initial data satisfies

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{v\in[u_{0},\infty)}{\bigg\{(1+v)^{1+\epsilon}|\Phi(v)|+(1+v)^{2+\epsilon}|\partial_{v}\Phi(v)|\bigg\}}\leq A_{0}

further for some
A_{0}>0
and

[

,

[

In this paper, we prove global existence and uniqueness of classical solutions for the following small initial data sets, which we refer as slowly particle-like decaying null infinity.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
0<\epsilon<1
. Given initial data
\breve{h}(r)\in C^{1}[0,\infty)
, denote

\displaystyle d_{0}=\inf\limits_{b>0}\sup\limits_{r\geq 0}{\left\{\left(1+\frac{r}{b}\right)^{\epsilon}\left|\breve{h}(r)\right|+\left(1+\frac{r}{b}\right)^{1+\epsilon}\left|b\frac{\partial\breve{h}}{\partial r}(r)\right|\right\}}.

Then there exists
\delta>0
such that if
d_{0}<\delta
, there exists unique global classical solution

\displaystyle h(u,r)\in C^{1}\Big([0,\infty)\times[0,\infty)\Big)

of (
1.3
) which satisfies the initial condition
h(0,r)=\breve{h}(r)
and the decay property

\displaystyle|h(u,r)|\leq\frac{C}{\left(1+u+r\right)^{\epsilon}},\hskip 10.00002pt\left|\frac{\partial h}{\partial r}(u,r)\right|\leq\frac{C}{\left(1+u+r\right)^{1+\epsilon}}

for some constant
C
depending on
\epsilon
only. Moreover, the corresponding spacetime is future causally geodesically complete. If we further assume

\frac{1}{2}<\epsilon<1
, then the final Bondi mass vanishes.

We adopt the main arguments in
[
5
,
11
]
to obtain the main estimates so as to prove the theorem. However, due to the slower decaying, we need to derive the estimates case by case much more carefully.

The paper is organized as follow: In Section 2, we derive the main estimates. In Section 3, we prove the main theorem.

Main estimates

r
. Denote by

\displaystyle\|h\|_{X}=\sup\limits_{u\geq 0,r\geq 0}{\left\{(1+u+r)^{\epsilon}|h(u,r)|+(1+u+r)^{1+\epsilon}\Big|\frac{\partial h}{\partial r}(u,r)\Big|\right\}}

is finte. As in

,

, let

be the solution of the equation

\displaystyle D_{n}h_{n+1}-\frac{g_{n}-\bar{g}_{n}}{2r}h_{n+1}=-\frac{g_{n}-\bar{g}_{n}}{2r}\bar{h}_{n}

with the initial data

\displaystyle h_{n+1}(0,r)=h(0,r),

where

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
0<\epsilon<1
. Given the initial data
h(0,r)
and the nth-iteration solution
h_{n}(u,r)
which are
C^{1}
and satisfy

\displaystyle\|h(0,r)\|_{X}=d,\hskip 10.00002pt\|h_{n}(u,r)\|_{X}=x.

Then the solution of (
2.1
) satisfies

\displaystyle\|h_{n+1}\|_{X}\leq C(d+x^{3})(2+x^{2})\exp{(Cx^{2})}

for some positive constant
C
independent of
n
.

Proof:
By the assumption, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |\bar{h}_{n}(u,r)|&\leq\frac{1}{r}\int_{0}^{r}{|h_{n}(u,s)|ds}\\
&\leq\frac{x}{r}\int_{0}^{r}{\frac{ds}{(1+u+s)^{\epsilon}}}\\
&=\frac{x}{(1-\epsilon)r}\left[(1+u+r)^{1-\epsilon}-(1+u)^{1-\epsilon}\right]\\
&=\frac{x}{(1-\epsilon)r}\frac{(1+u+r)-(1+u)^{1-\epsilon}(1+u+r)^{\epsilon}}{(1+u+r)^{\epsilon}}\\
&\leq\frac{x}{(1-\epsilon)(1+u+r)^{\epsilon}}.\end{aligned}

(2.2)

Using (
2.2
), we can estimate
|(h_{n}-\bar{h}_{n})(u,r)|
as follows.

(1) For
0\leq r\leq 1+u
, same as
[
11
, (2.11)]
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |(h_{n}-\bar{h}_{n})(u,r)|\leq&\frac{1}{r}\int_{0}^{r}{\int_{r^{\prime}}^{r}{\left|\frac{\partial h_{n}}{\partial s}\right|ds}dr^{\prime}}\\
\leq&\frac{x}{\epsilon r}\int_{0}^{r}{\left[\frac{1}{\left(1+u+r^{\prime}\right)^{\epsilon}}-\frac{1}{\left(1+u+r\right)^{\epsilon}}\right]dr^{\prime}}\\
\leq&\frac{x}{\epsilon(1-\epsilon)r}\bigg[\left(1+u+r\right)^{1-\epsilon}-\left(1+u\right)^{1-\epsilon}\\
&-\frac{(1-\epsilon)r}{\left(1+u+r\right)^{\epsilon}}\bigg]\\
\leq&\frac{5xr(1+u)^{1-\epsilon}}{\epsilon(1-\epsilon)(1+u+r)^{2}}.\\
\end{aligned}

(2) For
r\geq 1+u
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |(h_{n}-\bar{h}_{n})(u,r)|&\leq|h_{n}(u,r)|+|\bar{h}_{n}(u,r)|\\
&\leq\frac{x}{\left(1+u+r\right)^{\epsilon}}+\frac{x}{1-\epsilon}\frac{1}{\left(1+u+r\right)^{\epsilon}}\\
&\leq\frac{2x}{1-\epsilon}\frac{(1+u+r)}{(1+u+r)^{1+\epsilon}}\\
&\leq\frac{4xr}{(1-\epsilon)\left(1+u+r\right)^{1+\epsilon}}.\\
\end{aligned}

Let
c=\frac{5}{\epsilon(1-\epsilon)}
. We obtain, for
r\geq 0
,

\displaystyle|(h_{n}-\bar{h}_{n})(u,r)|\leq\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{cxr\left(1+u\right)^{1-\epsilon}}{\left(1+u+r\right)^{2}},\quad&0\leq r\leq 1+u,\\
&\frac{cxr}{\left(1+u+r\right)^{1+\epsilon}},\quad&r\geq 1+u.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.3)

Thus

\displaystyle|(h_{n}-\bar{h}_{n})(u,r)|\leq\frac{cxr}{\left(1+u+r\right)^{1+\epsilon}}.

(2.4)

Let
k=\exp{\left(-\frac{2\pi c^{2}x^{2}}{\epsilon}\right)}
,
0<k<1
. Since
g(u,r)
is monotonically increasing with respect to
r
, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \bar{g}_{n}(u,r)&\geq g_{n}(u,0)\\
&\geq\exp{\left[-4\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}{\frac{(h_{n}-\bar{h}_{n})^{2}}{s}ds}\right]}\\
&\geq\exp{\left[-4\pi c^{2}x^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\frac{ds}{(1+u+s)^{1+2\epsilon}}}\right]}\\
&\geq\exp{\left[-\frac{2\pi c^{2}x^{2}}{\epsilon(1+u)^{2\epsilon}}\right]}\geq k.\\
\end{aligned}

(2.5)

Claim
: Let
c_{1}=\frac{4\pi c^{2}}{\epsilon}
. We have

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}(g_{n}-\bar{g}_{n})(u,r)\leq\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{c_{1}x^{2}r^{2}}{(1+u)^{2\epsilon-1}\left(1+u+r\right)^{3}},\,\,&0\leq r\leq 1+u,\\
&\frac{c_{1}x^{2}r}{\left(1+u\right)^{2\epsilon}\left(1+u+r\right)},\,\,&r\geq 1+u.\\
\end{aligned}\right.\end{matrix}

(2.6)

Indeed, for
0\leq r\leq 1+u
, (
2.6
) is a direct consequence of (
2.4
) and
[
11
, (2.14)]
.
For
r\geq 1+u
, using (
2.4
) and

\displaystyle\frac{\partial g_{n}}{\partial r}=\frac{4\pi(h_{n}-\bar{h}_{n})^{2}g_{n}}{r},

we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} (g_{n}-\bar{g}_{n})(u,r)&=\frac{1}{r}\int_{0}^{r}{\int_{r^{\prime}}^{r}{\frac{\partial g_{n}}{\partial s}ds}dr^{\prime}}\\
&\leq\frac{2\pi c^{2}x^{2}}{\epsilon r}\int_{0}^{r}{\left[\frac{1}{(1+u+r^{\prime})^{2\epsilon}}-\frac{1}{\left(1+u+r\right)^{2\epsilon}}\right]dr^{\prime}}\\
&\leq\frac{2\pi c^{2}x^{2}}{\epsilon r}\left[\frac{r}{\left(1+u\right)^{2\epsilon}}-\frac{r}{\left(1+u+r\right)^{2\epsilon}}\right]\\
&=\frac{2\pi c^{2}x^{2}}{\epsilon}\frac{(1+u+r)^{2}-\left(1+u\right)^{2\epsilon}\left(1+u+r\right)^{2-2\epsilon}}{\left(1+u\right)^{2\epsilon}\left(1+u+r\right)^{2}}\\
&\leq\frac{c_{1}x^{2}r}{\left(1+u\right)^{2\epsilon}\left(1+u+r\right)}.\end{aligned}

Thus, the claim follows and it also implies that

\displaystyle(g_{n}-\bar{g}_{n})(u,r)\leq\frac{c_{1}x^{2}r}{\left(1+u\right)^{2\epsilon+1}}.

(2.7)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left|-\frac{g_{n}-\bar{g}_{n}}{2r}\bar{h}_{n}\right|&\leq\frac{1}{2r}\frac{c_{1}x^{2}r}{\left(1+u\right)^{2\epsilon+1}}\frac{x}{1-\epsilon}\frac{1}{\left(1+u+r\right)^{\epsilon}}\\

The characteristic

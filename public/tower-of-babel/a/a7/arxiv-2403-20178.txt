Abstract
. Let S S be a minimal compact complex surface with Betti numbers b 1 ​ ( S ) = 1 b_{1}(S)=1 and b 2 ​ ( S ) ≥ 1 b_{2}(S)\geq 1 i.e. a compact surface in class VII + 0 {}_{0}^{+} . We show that if there exists a twisted logarithmic 1-form θ ∈ H 0 ​ ( S , Ω 1 ​ ( log ⁡ D ) ⊗ ℒ λ ) \theta\in H^{0}(S,\Omega^{1}(\log D)\otimes\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}) , where D D is a non zero divisor and ℒ ∈ H 1 ​ ( S , ℂ ⋆ ) \mathcal{L}\in H^{1}(S,\mathbb{C}^{\star}) , then S S is a Kato surface. It is known that λ \lambda is in fact real and we show that λ ≥ 1 \lambda\geq 1 and unique if S S is not a Inoue-Hirzebruch surface. Moreover λ = 1 \lambda=1 if and only if S S is a Enoki surface. 2.
Negativity of Camacho-Sad coefficients

We start with an explicit example and after we use a family of polynomials, already used in

Algebraic part of the proof

Lemma 2.2
.

We consider the family of polynomials

P_{0}=1,\ P_{1}(X_{1})=X_{1},\ P_{i}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{i})=X_{1}P_{i-1}(X_{2},\ldots,X_{i})-P_{i-2}(X_{3},\ldots,X_{i}),\ i\geq 2

Then for any

,
P_{n}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})=P_{n}(X_{n},\ldots,X_{1})
.

Proof.

Remark that the definition gives

P_{i}(X_{i},\ldots,X_{1})=X_{i}P_{i-1}(X_{i-1},\ldots,X_{1})-P_{i-2}(X_{i-2},\ldots,X_{1}).

Therefore we have to show by induction on

that

P_{i}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{i})=X_{i}P_{i-1}(X_{i-1},\ldots,X_{1})-P_{i-2}(X_{i-2},\ldots,X_{1}),

It is easy to check that for

the two expressions give the same polynomials.
Suppose that it is true for
i
and
i-1
; then for
i+1
we have by the induction hypothesis

\begin{array}[]{ccl}P_{i+1}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{i+1})&=&X_{1}P_{i}(X_{2},\ldots,X_{i+1})-P_{i-1}(X_{3},\ldots,X_{i+1})\\
\\
&=&X_{1}\bigl[X_{i+1}P_{i-1}(X_{i},\ldots,X_{2})-P_{i-2}(X_{i-1},\ldots,X_{2})\bigr]\\
\\
&&-\bigl[X_{i+1}P_{i-2}(X_{i},\ldots,X_{3})-P_{i-3}(X_{i-1},\ldots,X_{3})\bigr]\\
\\
&=&X_{i+1}\bigl[X_{1}P_{i-1}(X_{i},\ldots,X_{2})-P_{i-2}(X_{i},\ldots,X_{3})\bigr]\\
\\
&&-\bigl[X_{1}P_{i-2}(X_{i-1},\ldots,X_{2})-P_{i-3}(X_{i-1},\ldots,X_{3})\bigr]\\
\\
&=&X_{i+1}P_{i}(X_{i},\ldots,X_{1})-P_{i-1}(X_{i-1},\ldots,X_{1})\par\end{array}

∎

Lemma 2.5
.

If

Remark 2.7

Lemma 2.8

Lemma 2.13
.

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
\delta=(\delta_{0},\ldots,\delta_{p-1})
be the second member of a system
(E_{p})
which corresponds to a surface

with a cycle of rational curves, in particular admits real solutions.

Then for any index

,

P_{p}(\delta_{i},\ldots,\delta_{i-1})-P_{p-2}(\delta_{i+1},\ldots,\delta_{i-2})>0,

Proof.

By lemma
2.11
,
\delta_{i}>1
for
i=0,\ldots,p-1
. The highest possible power of an indeterminacy in a monomial is one. Therefore the only non vanishing partial derivatives

\frac{\partial^{|q|}\bigl(P_{p}(X_{0},\ldots,X_{p-1})-P_{p-2}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{p-2})\bigr)}{\partial^{q_{0}}X_{0}\cdots\partial^{q_{p-1}}X_{p-1}}

are those for which

, or
q_{i}=1
,
i=0,\ldots,p-1
, where

. Besides, for

and fixed
|q|
,

2.5

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{|q|}Q_{p}(X_{0},\ldots,X_{p-1})}{\partial^{q_{0}}X_{0}\cdots\partial^{q_{p-1}}X_{p-1}}(2,\ldots,2)=P_{p-|q|}(2,\ldots,2)-P_{p-2-|q|}(2,\ldots,2)=2.

\begin{array}[]{l}Q_{p}(\delta_{0},\ldots,\delta_{p-1})\\
\\
\hskip 28.45274pt=\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq j\leq p}\ \sum_{q,|q|=j}(\delta_{0}-2)^{q_{0}}\cdots(\delta_{p-1}-2)^{q_{p-1}}\frac{\partial^{|q|}Q_{p}(X_{0},\ldots,X_{p-1})}{\partial^{q_{0}}X_{0}\cdots\partial^{q_{p-1}}X_{p-1}}(2,\ldots,2)\\
\\
\hskip 28.45274pt=\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq j\leq p}\ \sum_{q,|q|=j}(\delta_{0}-2)^{q_{0}}\cdots(\delta_{p-1}-2)^{q_{p-1}}Q_{p-|q|}(2,\ldots,2)\\

\hskip 28.45274pt=\displaystyle\ 2\sum_{0\leq j\leq p}\ \sum_{q,|q|=j}(\delta_{0}-2)^{q_{0}}\cdots(\delta_{p-1}-2)^{q_{p-1}}\\
\\
\hskip 28.45274pt=2\displaystyle\prod_{j=0}^{p-1}\bigl(1+(\delta_{j}-2)\bigr)=2\prod_{j=0}^{p-1}(\delta_{j}-1)>0.\end{array}

P_{p}(\delta_{i},\ldots,\delta_{i-1})-P_{p-2}(\delta_{i+1},\ldots,\delta_{i-2})>0.

In a similar way, for
\delta=(\delta_{0},\ldots,\delta_{p-1})
,
\delta_{i}>1
,

,

\begin{array}[]{lcl}P_{p-1}(\delta_{0},\ldots,\delta_{p-2})&=&\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq j\leq{p-1}}\ \sum_{q,|q|=j}(\delta_{0}-2)^{q_{0}}\cdots(\delta_{p-1}-2)^{q_{p-1}}\frac{\partial^{|q|}P_{p-1}(X_{0},\ldots,X_{p-2})}{\partial^{q_{0}}X_{0}\cdots\partial^{q_{p-1}}X_{p-1}}(2,\ldots,2)\\
&&\\
&=&\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq j\leq p-1}(p-j)\sum_{|q|=j}(\delta_{0}-2)^{q_{0}}\cdots(\delta_{p-2}-2)^{q_{p-2}}\\
$$\\
&=&\displaystyle\prod_{i=0}^{p-2}(\delta_{i}-1)+\sum_{k=0}^{p-2}\ \prod_{i\neq k}(\delta_{i}-1)>0\end{array}

We have the result by circular permutation.

∎

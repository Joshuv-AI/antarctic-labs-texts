Free curves in Fano hypersurfaces must have high degree
Raymond Cheng

Abstract
. The purpose of this note is to show that the minimal e e for which every smooth Fano hypersurface of dimension n n contains a free rational curve of degree at most e e cannot be bounded by a linear function in n n when the base field has positive characteristic. This is done by providing a super-linear bound on the minimal possible degree of a free curve in certain Fermat hypersurfaces.

Introduction

The geometry of smooth projective Fano varieties is controlled by the rational
curves they contain. Seminal work
[
KMM92
,
Cam92
]
of
Kollár–Miyaoka–Mori and Campana show that, over a field of characteristic

0
, every smooth projective Fano variety
X
contains a rational curve

\varphi\colon\mathbf{P}^{1}\to X
that, informally speaking, can be deformed to
pass through
r+1
general points of
X
for any chosen
r\geq 0
: in
other words,
X
is
separably rationally connected
. The precise
condition on the curve
\varphi
is that

\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\varphi^{*}\mathcal{T}_{X}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(-r-1))=0
;
by way of terminology,
\varphi
is said to be
free
or
very
free
when
r=0
or
r=1
, respectively. See
[
Kol96
,
Deb01
]
for a presentation of this theory.

Whether smooth projective Fano varieties over a field of positive
characteristic are separably rationally connected is a long-standing open
question. Results are fragmentary even for smooth Fano hypersurfaces in
projective space: The general Fano hypersurface is separably rationally
connected by
[
Zhu24
,
CZ14
,
Tia15
,
CR19
]
; notably, the work of Tian reduces
the problem of separable rational connectedness to
separable
uniruledness
—that is, the existence of a free rational curve—a problem
that often is simpler because free curves typically have significantly lower
degree than very free curves. More recently,
[
STZ22
, Theorems
3.10 and 3.24]
and
[
ST19
, Corollary 9]
shows that all smooth Fano
hypersurfaces with degree less than the characteristic are separably rationally
connected and even that, up to a minor condition, such hypersurfaces always
contain either free lines or conics. See also
[
LP21
, Theorem 34]
,

[
BS23
, Theorem 1.5]
, and
[
BLL+24
, Theorem 1.12]
for related results.

The main result of this note is that, nevertheless and contrary to experience,
the minimal
e
such that there exists a free rational curve of degree

\leq e
on every smooth Fano hypersurface cannot be bounded by a linear
function in the dimension (or degree); contrast this with the fact that every
smooth Fano hypersurface in characteristic
0
contains either a free line or
conic.

Theorem
. —

For any algebraically closed field
\mathbf{k}
of characteristic
p>0
,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\inf\Set{e\in\mathbf{Z}}{\begin{array}[]{c}\text{for every smooth Fano hypersurface of dimension $n$ over $\mathbf{k}$}\\
\text{there exists a free rational curve of degree $\leq e$}\end{array}}=\infty.

It suffices to describe one sequence of increasingly high-dimensional
hypersurfaces without low degree free curves, and this is given by the Fermat
hypersurface
X
of degree
q+1
in
\mathbf{P}^{q+1}
with

q\coloneqq p^{\nu}
for
\nu\geq 1
. Shen has studied very free curves in

X
and showed in
[
She12
]
that many integers below
q^{2}
cannot be the
degree of such a curve; a reformulation of this for free curves is given in

2
. The main technical result
5
excludes a
complementary set of degrees, and they are combined in

6
to show that there are no gaps below
q^{3/2}-q
.
The method is to exploit a certain tension arising from the curious
differential geometry of these hypersurfaces: the structure of the equation
implies that, on the one hand, free curves span either the ambient projective
space or else a hyperplane and, on the other hand, there are unexpected
constraints on the coordinate functions of the curve.

These hypersurfaces are well-known to be exceptional and exemplify many
positive characteristic phenomena: see
[
Che22
, pp.7–11]
for a general
survey regarding these hypersurfaces. What is fascinating is that these
hypersurfaces contain many, many rational curves—they are unirational!—and
so the challenge is to develop techniques to study their spaces of rational
curves: see
[
Che25
,
Che24
]
for work in this direction.

Free curves in the Fermat hypersurface

In what follows, let

X\coloneqq\mathrm{V}(T_{0}^{q+1}+\cdots+T_{q+1}^{q+1})
be the Fermat
hypersurface of degree
q+1
in
\mathbf{P}^{q+1}
. The
embedded tangent
bundle

\mathcal{E}_{X}
is the vector bundle on
X
whose fibre at a point

x
is the linear space underlying the embedded tangent space of
X
at

x
; it fits into a short exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{E}_{X}\to\mathcal{T}_{X}\to 0.

The extension class is the pullback via the tangent map

\mathcal{T}_{X}\to\mathcal{T}_{\mathbf{P}^{q+1}}\rvert_{X}
of the class of the Euler
sequence, and so there is another short exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{E}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)^{\oplus q+2}\to\mathcal{N}_{X/\mathbf{P}^{q+1}}\to 0

where the second map is

({}_{0},\ldots,{}_{q+1})\mapsto\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{q+1}T_{i}^{q}\cdot{}_{i}
.
Remarkably, as already observed by
[
She12
, Equation (2)]
, this sequence
twisted down by
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-1)
is isomorphic to the pullback of the dual Euler
sequence by the
q
-power Frobenius morphism
\Fr
. In particular, there is
an isomorphism

\mathcal{E}_{X}(-1)\cong\Fr^{*}({}^{1}_{\mathbf{P}^{q+1}}(1)\rvert_{X}).

Let
\varphi\colon\mathbf{P}^{1}\to X
be a nonconstant morphism of degree

e=mq+r
, where
m,r\in\mathbf{Z}
and
0\leq r\leq q-1
; to
simplify notation,
\varphi
will sometimes be viewed as a morphism into

\mathbf{P}^{q+1}
. Viewing
\mathcal{E}_{X}
as an extension of
\mathcal{T}_{X}
,
it follows that
\varphi
is free if and only if

\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\varphi^{*}\mathcal{E}_{X}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(-1))=0
.
Combined with the isomorphism above and the projection formula, this implies
that, if
\varphi
is free,

\displaystyle 0=\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\varphi^{*}\mathcal{E}_{X}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(-1))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\varphi^{*}\Fr^{*}({}^{1}_{\mathbf{P}^{q+1}}(1)\rvert_{X})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(e-1))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\varphi^{*}({}^{1}_{\mathbf{P}^{q+1}}(1))\otimes\Fr_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(e-1)).

Since

\Fr_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(e-1)\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(m)^{\oplus r}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(m-1)^{\oplus q-r}
,
this shows that:

1 Lemma
. —

If
\varphi\colon\mathbf{P}^{1}\to X
is free, then

\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\varphi^{*}{}^{1}_{\mathbf{P}^{q+1}}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(e+m-1))=0
.
∎

The converse also holds. Since

\chi(\mathbf{P}^{1},\varphi^{*}{}^{1}_{\mathbf{P}^{q+1}}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(e+m-1))=(q+2)m-e-m=m-r
,
this gives a relation between
m
and
r
when
\varphi
is free,
recovering
[
She12
, Theorem 1.7]
:

2 Lemma
. —

If
\varphi\colon\mathbf{P}^{1}\to X
is free, then
r\leq m
. ∎

The following gives a geometric restriction on free curves in
X
, and stands
in stark contrast to the fact, see
[
Kol96
, V.4.4]
, that a general
smooth Fano hypersurface contains either a free line or conic:

3 Lemma
. —

If
\varphi\colon\mathbf{P}^{1}\to X
is free, then
\varphi(\mathbf{P}^{1})
either
spans
\mathbf{P}^{q+1}
or a hyperplane. Moreover, in the latter case,
e=mq

for positive
m
, and

\varphi^{*}({}^{1}_{\mathbf{P}^{q+1}}(1))\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(-m)^{\oplus q}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}
.

Proof.

Identifying
\mathcal{E}_{X}=\ker(\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)^{\oplus q+2}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(q+1))
and
using that
\varphi
is free shows that

\dim\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\varphi^{*}\mathcal{E}_{X}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(-1))=e.

If
\varphi
were contained in a hyperplane
\mathbf{P}^{q}\subset\mathbf{P}^{q+1}
, then
juxtaposing the two Euler sequences shows that

\varphi^{*}({}^{1}_{\mathbf{P}^{q+1}}(1))
would split as

\varphi^{*}({}^{1}_{\mathbf{P}^{q}}(1))\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}
. Combined with the fact
that
\mathcal{E}_{X}(-1)\cong\Fr^{*}({}^{1}_{\mathbf{P}^{q+1}}(1)\rvert_{X})
, this
would imply that

e=\dim\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\varphi^{*}\mathcal{E}_{X}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(-1))=\dim\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\Fr^{*}\varphi^{*}({}^{1}_{\mathbf{P}^{q}}(1))\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(e-1))+e.

Thus
\Fr^{*}\varphi^{*}({}^{1}_{\mathbf{P}^{q}}(1))\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(e-1)

cannot have global sections. Freeness of
\varphi
also implies that it has
no higher cohomology, and so it must be isomorphic to

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(-1)^{\oplus q}
. However, since

\varphi^{*}({}^{1}_{\mathbf{P}^{q}}(1))
is a vector bundle of degree
-e
, there
are integers
a_{i}\geq 0
with
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{q}=e
such that

\Fr^{*}\varphi^{*}({}^{1}_{\mathbf{P}^{q}}(1))\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(e-1)\cong\bigoplus\nolimits_{i=1}^{q}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(-a_{i}q+e-1).

Therefore
e=a_{i}q
for each
i
, and so

a_{1}=\cdots=a_{q}=m
,
e=mq
, and

\varphi^{*}({}^{1}_{\mathbf{P}^{q}}(1))\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(-m)^{\oplus q}
.
Since
\varphi
is nonconstant,
m
is positive and
\varphi(\mathbf{P}^{1})

spans the hyperplane
\mathbf{P}^{q}
.
∎

4
. —

Viewing
\varphi
as a morphism into
\mathbf{P}^{q+1}
and letting

(\varphi_{0}:\cdots:\varphi_{q+1})
be its components,

3
means that the
\varphi_{i}
enjoy at most
1
linear
relation in
\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(e))
. Already, this implies

e\geq q
. That
\varphi
factors through
X
means that

\sum_{i=0}^{q+1}\varphi_{i}^{q}\cdot\varphi_{i}=0
. Upon choosing
homogeneous coordinates
(S_{0}:S_{1})
for
\mathbf{P}^{1}
, there is a unique
decomposition

\varphi_{i}=\sum\nolimits_{j=0}^{r}\zeta_{ij}^{q}\cdot S_{0}^{j}S_{1}^{r-j}+\sum\nolimits_{k=1}^{q-r-1}\eta_{ik}^{q}\cdot S_{0}^{r+k}S_{1}^{q-k}

where
\zeta_{ij}\in\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(m))
and

\eta_{ik}\in\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(m-1))
. Note that this
decomposition provides a specific choice of isomorphism

\Fr_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(e)\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(m)^{\oplus r+1}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(m-1)^{\oplus q-r-1}
,
and which shall be used below. Substituting this into
the equation of
X
and using the fact that
q
is a power of the ground
field characteristic shows that

0=\sum\nolimits_{j=0}^{r}\Big(\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{q+1}\varphi_{i}\zeta_{ij}\Big)^{q}\cdot S_{0}^{j}S_{1}^{r-j}+\sum\nolimits_{k=1}^{q-r-1}\Big(\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{q+1}\varphi_{i}\eta_{ik}\Big)^{q}\cdot S_{0}^{r+k}S_{1}^{q-k}

and so, upon looking at exponents modulo
q
,

\sum_{i=0}^{q+1}\varphi_{i}\zeta_{ij}=\sum_{i=0}^{q+1}\varphi_{i}\eta_{ik}=0
for all
j
and
k
. These
relations may impose further linear relations on the
\varphi_{i}
, so give
strong restrictions on the degree
e
:

5 Theorem
. —

If
\varphi\colon\mathbf{P}^{1}\to X
is free, then

q+1\leq m^{2}+m+r
if
r>0
and

q\leq m^{2}+m
if
r=0
.

Proof.

Consider the linear map

\Phi\colon\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{q+1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{q+1}}(1))\to\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(e))
defining
\varphi\colon\mathbf{P}^{1}\to\mathbf{P}^{q+1}
, so that
the
i
-th coordinate
T_{i}
maps to
\varphi_{i}
. Identify the
target of
\Phi
as

\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(e))\cong\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\Fr_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(e))\cong\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(m))^{\oplus r+1}\oplus\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(m-1))^{\oplus q-r-1}

where
\Fr\colon\mathbf{P}^{1}\to\mathbf{P}^{1}
is the
q
-power Frobenius morphism.
Let
\Phi_{1}
and
\Phi_{2}
be the linear maps obtained by post-composing

\Phi
with projection to
\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(m))^{\oplus r+1}

and
\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(m-1))^{\oplus q-r-1}
, respectively.
Elementary linear algebra gives

\rank\Phi\leq\rank\Phi_{1}+\rank\Phi_{2}\leq(r+1)(m+1)+\rank\Phi_{2}.

To bound the rank of
\Phi_{2}
, let

\Phi_{2,k}\colon\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{q+1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{q+1}}(1))\to\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(m-1))

be the further projection to the
k
-th component of its
target, so that
\Phi_{2,k}(T_{i})=\eta_{ik}
, notation as in

4
. The discussion of

4
means that the
\Phi_{2,k}
lie in the
kernel of the linear map

\displaystyle\Hom(\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{q+1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{q+1}}(1)),\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(m-1)))

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(e+m-1))

\displaystyle\longmapsto\sum\nolimits_{i=0}^{q+1}\varphi_{i}\cdot\Psi(T_{i}).

The kernel of this map is isomorphic to

\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\varphi^{*}{}^{1}_{\mathbf{P}^{q+1}}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(e+m-1))
:
Indeed, pull back the Euler sequence via
\varphi
and twist up by

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(e+m-1)
to obtain the exact sequence

0\to\varphi^{*}{}_{\mathbf{P}^{q+1}}^{1}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(e+m-1)\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(m-1)\otimes\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{q+1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{q+1}}(1))\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(e+m-1)\to 0,

then use the choice of coordinates
T_{i}
to make the identification

\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(m-1))\otimes\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{q+1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{q+1}}(1))\cong\Hom(\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{q+1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{q+1}}(1)),\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(m-1))).

On the one hand,
1
and the Euler characteristic computation
following it shows that this kernel has dimension
m-r
, and so at most

m-r
of the
q-r-1

components
\Phi_{2,k}
of
\Phi_{2}
are linearly
independent. Since
\rank\Phi_{2,k}\leq m
for each
k
,

\rank\Phi_{2}\leq(m-r)m
. Therefore

\rank\Phi\leq(r+1)(m+1)+(m-r)m=m^{2}+m+r+1.

On the other hand,
3
means that
\Phi
has rank
q+2

if
r>0
and rank at least
q+1
if
r=0
. Put together, these give
the inequalities of the statement.
∎
